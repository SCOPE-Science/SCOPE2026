# The three anticanonical Bertini spectra for all-smooth quadratic pencils over \(\mathbf F_2\)

## Finding
For a pencil \(W\) of quadrics in \(\mathbf P^4_{\mathbf F_2}\), assume its three members defined over \(\mathbf F_2\) are geometrically smooth. Then the common zero surface \(X=V(W)\) is a smooth degree-four del Pezzo surface. For each rational hyperplane \(H\), write \(n_H=\#(X\cap H)(\mathbf F_2)\), and \(e_H=1\) if \(X\cap H\) is geometrically smooth, otherwise \(e_H=0\). The complete profile \(\{(e_H,n_H):H\in(\mathbf P^4)^\vee(\mathbf F_2)\}\) has exactly three possibilities:

| Profile | Rational lines on \(X\) | Smooth \(n_H=1,2,3,4,5\) | Singular \(n_H=0,2,3,4,5,6\) | Pencil count |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| A | 1 | \((2,2,3,4,1)\) | \((0,3,6,6,3,1)\) | 9,999,360 |
| B | 2 | \((0,9,6,0,0)\) | \((0,0,3,6,6,1)\) | 1,666,560 |
| C | 0 | \((0,0,0,3,6)\) | \((1,6,9,6,0,0)\) | 1,666,560 |

Thus precisely \(12\), \(15\), or \(9\) rational hyperplanes cut a geometrically smooth genus-one curve, respectively. Every surface has seven rational points. Particularly, the two coefficient families of half-discriminant factorization \((2,3)\) have disjoint smooth-section rational-point ranges: only \(2,3\) points versus only \(4,5\) points.

## Assumptions and scope
All counts are over \(\mathbf F_2\), for embedded pencils as two-dimensional subspaces of the 15-dimensional space of quadratic forms in five coordinates. No quotienting by projective automorphisms is used in the pencil totals. A geometrically smooth quadric in \(\mathbf P^4\) has polar matrix rank \(4\) and its one-dimensional polar radical has nonzero quadratic value. The three nonzero members of a two-dimensional subspace must satisfy this condition. A smooth anticanonical hyperplane section of the common zero surface is a genus-one degree-four complete-intersection curve in \(\mathbf P^3\). Distinct rational hyperplanes are the 31 nonzero linear equations, because \(\mathbf F_2^\times\) is trivial.

For compact and unambiguous explicit representatives, enumerate the coordinates as \(x_0,\ldots,x_4\). Encode a quadratic equation by a 15-bit integer: bit \(i\) is the coefficient of \(x_i^2\), and bits \(5,\ldots,14\) are the coefficients of \(x_ix_j\), for \(i<j\), in lexicographic order. Set \(Q_0=4144\), which means \(Q_0=x_0x_1+x_2x_3+x_4^2\). Representatives for profiles A, B, C are \((Q_0,Q_1)=(4144,778),(4144,792),(4144,793)\), respectively. The original source code implements and documents the encoding.

## Proof
**Exhaustive smooth-quadric inventory.** The space of nonzero quadratic equations has \(2^{15}-1=32,767\) elements. The polar rank/radical criterion identifies exactly \(13,888\) geometrically smooth equations. Each is linearly equivalent to \(Q_0\). Enumerating unordered generator pairs whose third sum is also smooth gives \(39,997,440\) such pairs. Every two-dimensional pencil contributes exactly three pairs, hence there are \(13,332,480\) pencils. Their half-discriminants are all squarefree of degree five, with factorization only \((5)\) or \((2,3)\), verifying that each surface intersection is smooth; see Dolgachev--Duncan's squarefree half-discriminant criterion.

**Orbit reduction without claiming a new pencil-classification theorem.** The orthogonal stabilizer of \(Q_0\), of order \(720\), acts on the \(2,880\) pencils containing that fixed quadric. Using the invariant symplectic form on the first four coordinates, all \(720\) group elements are enumerated explicitly. There are five orbits, with sizes \(720,360,360,720,720\), represented by second equations \(778,792,793,808,938\). The three size-720 orbits all have the same hyperplane profile A. The two size-360 orbits have the distinct profiles B and C. No assertion about the number of full projective equivalence classes is necessary for this classification of profiles. Double-counting incidences between a smooth quadric and a pencil (three quadrics per pencil) yields global profile counts \(13,888\cdot2160/3=9,999,360\), and \(13,888\cdot360/3=1,666,560\) twice.

**Exact hyperplane tests.** For each of the five orbit representatives, the 31 rational hyperplanes are exhaustively enumerated. Eliminate one coordinate using its linear equation; on each of the four standard affine charts of that hyperplane, substitute the chart coordinate equal to one in the two remaining quadrics. The section is geometrically singular if and only if the two equations together with their three \(2\times2\) Jacobian minors admit a common zero over the algebraic closure. An exact Gröbner-basis computation over \(\mathbf F_2\) decides whether the ideal is the unit ideal. Separately, count the seven surface rational points satisfying the hyperplane equation. This gives the displayed full joint distributions, including all singular hyperplanes. The orbit action permutes rational hyperplanes and preserves smoothness and rational-point count, so the five representatives suffice. Direct projective-chart Jacobian tests also confirm that the three named surface representatives are geometrically smooth.

## Verification
Run `g++ -O3 -std=c++17 quadric_p4.cpp -o quadric_p4 && ./quadric_p4` to regenerate the 13,888 smooth quadrics, 13,332,480 eligible pencils and half-discriminant totals. Run `g++ -O3 -std=c++17 orbits.cpp -o orbits && ./orbits` to regenerate the five fixed-quadric orbits. Run `python hyperplanes.py` (requires SymPy) for the complete five-orbit hyperplane data and `python independent_geometry.py` for the separate surface smoothness and rational-line tests. `EXPECTED.txt` contains the audited numerical assertions, and `REPRODUCE.md` contains detailed instructions. The C++ census is exhaustive over coefficient bit vectors; the Gröbner tests decide existence of geometric singularities exactly, not only singularities at rational points.

## Relationship to prior work
Asgarli--Ghioca (2022, arXiv:2203.07169) study pencils over finite fields with all rational members smooth, supplying the motivating geometric question; their full manuscript does not give this degree-four del Pezzo hyperplane-profile classification. Dolgachev--Duncan (2015 preprint, published 2018, arXiv:1510.06803) give a much stronger general classification and the half-discriminant criterion for regular pencils in characteristic two. Therefore the enumeration of quadratic-pencil classes, the existence of three underlying types, and the general smoothness criterion are **not** claimed as original. The new assertion is the joint geometrically smooth anticanonical hyperplane-section census, its rational-point stratification, and its exact pencil weighting. Bergvall (2023) studies anticanonical sections and their singularities in moduli of degree-three and degree-four del Pezzo surfaces, but the inspected text does not calculate these characteristic-two, 31-hyperplane spectra. Searches for finite-field Bertini spectra and the numerical values did not locate an equivalent table.

## Limitations
The result applies exclusively to \(\mathbf F_2\), all-smooth quadratic pencils in \(\mathbf P^4\), and their 31 rational hyperplane sections. It does not classify arbitrary degree-four del Pezzo surfaces, determine geometric singularity types of singular hyperplane sections, or claim mathematical novelty for existing quadratic-pencil classification. An unindexed finite-field census or an implicit table from a wider arithmetic classification could predate the present refinement. No independent external audit has been performed.
