# The three-atom Crofton section law and affine parity rigidity of the cuboctahedron

## Finding
Let \(C\subset\mathbb R^3\) be the regular cuboctahedron
\[
C=\operatorname{conv}\{(\pm1,\pm1,0),(\pm1,0,\pm1),(0,\pm1,\pm1)\}.
\]
Select an unoriented affine plane by the Euclidean-motion-invariant measure on planes, conditioned on meeting the interior of \(C\). Let \(N\) be the number of vertices of the polygonal section. Set \(\gamma=\arctan\sqrt2\). Then the entire distribution is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(N=4)&=3-\frac{\pi(2+\sqrt2)}{4\gamma}
                 =0.193059337124398\ldots,\\
\Pr(N=6)&=\frac{\pi\sqrt2}{2\gamma}-2
                 =0.325345782679200\ldots,\\
\Pr(N=8)&=\frac{\pi(2-\sqrt2)}{4\gamma}
                 =0.481594880196402\ldots.
\end{aligned}
\]
All other section-vertex counts have probability zero, and
\[
\mathbb E N=\frac{2\pi}{\gamma}=6.577071086144008\ldots.
\]
In particular, almost every nonempty plane section has an even number of sides despite the triangular facets of \(C\). The generic even-sidedness conclusion also holds for every invertible affine image of \(C\), although the three numerical probabilities need not be preserved by such a deformation.

A stronger direction-resolved assertion holds. For a generic unit normal \(u\), write its absolute coordinates in descending order as \(x>y>z>0\), and parameterize parallel planes as \(u\cdot X=t\). Their section-vertex counts, for \(|t|<x+y\), are exactly
\[
N(u,t)=\begin{cases}
8,&0\le |t|<x-z,\\
6,&x-z<|t|<x+z,\\
4,&x+z<|t|<x+y.
\end{cases}
\]
The exceptional equality planes form a set of motion-invariant measure zero and are excluded from this generic formula.

## Assumptions and scope
A plane is represented by \((u,t)\), with \(u\in S^2\), \(t\in\mathbb R\), and the identification \((u,t)\sim(-u,-t)\). The invariant measure is proportional to \(d\sigma(u)\,dt\). This differs from first choosing a uniform orientation and then choosing an independently uniform offset conditional on intersection: under the invariant hitting-plane distribution, normal directions are weighted in proportion to the body's width. For \(C=-C\), the supporting offset is \(h_C(u)=\max_{v\in C}u\cdot v\), so intersection occurs for \(-h_C(u)<t<h_C(u)\). No nonregular affine realization is assigned the displayed probabilities.

## Proof
**1. Exact sweep of the twenty-four edges.** The twelve vertices are precisely the signed permutations of \((1,1,0)\). Two distinct vertices are joined by an edge exactly when their squared Euclidean distance is \(2\), giving twenty-four edges. Every vertex has degree four. Coordinate sign changes and coordinate permutations reduce generic normal directions to \(x>y>z>0\). In that cone the six vertices of positive height, their heights, and their numbers of neighboring vertices of greater height are:

| Vertex | Height | Higher neighbors |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| \((0,1,-1)\) | \(y-z\) | 2 |
| \((0,1,1)\) | \(y+z\) | 2 |
| \((1,-1,0)\) | \(x-y\) | 2 |
| \((1,0,-1)\) | \(x-z\) | 1 |
| \((1,0,1)\) | \(x+z\) | 1 |
| \((1,1,0)\) | \(x+y\) | 0 |

This table follows directly by comparing the heights of the four distance-\(\sqrt2\) neighbors of each vertex. For example, all neighbor-height differences have a fixed sign on the cone generated by \((1,0,0),(1,1,0),(1,1,1)\); the signs can be checked on those three generating rays, as in the accompanying standalone checker. There are eight edges connecting vertices of opposite height, so the central section has eight vertices.

As \(t\) rises past a vertex with \(r\) higher neighbors, the number of edges crossing the plane changes by \(r-(4-r)=2r-4\). The three vertices with two higher neighbors cause no change. Crossing height \(x-z\) removes two section vertices; crossing \(x+z\) removes two; and crossing the top height \(x+y\) removes the final four. As \(z<y\), these thresholds occur in that order. Central symmetry handles negative offsets. This proves the complete direction-resolved assertion and the parity conclusion. It also shows that the lengths of the positive-offset intervals of types \(8,6,4\) are, respectively,
\[
\ell_8(u)=x-z,\qquad \ell_6(u)=2z,\qquad \ell_4(u)=y-z,
\]
whose sum is the support height \(x+y\).

**2. Kinematic integration.** The full octahedral symmetry group maps forty-eight copies of the spherical triangle
\[
F=\{u\in S^2:u_1\ge u_2\ge u_3\ge0\}
\]
onto the sphere, apart from null boundaries. Write
\[
I_j=\int_F u_j\,d\sigma(u),\qquad j=1,2,3.
\]
The triangle corners in positively oriented boundary order are
\[
a=(1,0,0),\quad b=(1,1,0)/\sqrt2,\quad c=(1,1,1)/\sqrt3.
\]
For a spherical region with successive great-circle arc endpoints \(v,w\), Stokes' theorem gives the vector area
\[
\int_F u\,d\sigma(u)=\frac12\sum_{v\to w}
\frac{\arccos(v\cdot w)}{\lVert v\times w\rVert}(v\times w).
\]
For this triangle let \(\beta=\arctan(1/\sqrt2)=\pi/2-\gamma\). The three arcs have angular lengths \(\pi/4,\beta,\gamma\), and direct evaluation yields
\[
(I_1,I_2,I_3)=\left(\frac{\beta}{2\sqrt2},
\frac{\gamma-\beta}{2\sqrt2},
\frac\pi8-\frac\gamma{2\sqrt2}\right).
\]
The total positive-offset invariant mass per copy of \(F\) is
\[
Z=\int_F h_C(u)\,d\sigma(u)=I_1+I_2=\frac\gamma{2\sqrt2}.
\]
Consequently, after cancellation of the common forty-eightfold symmetry factor and the positive/negative offset factor,
\[
\Pr(N=8)=\frac{I_1-I_3}{Z},\quad
\Pr(N=6)=\frac{2I_3}{Z},\quad
\Pr(N=4)=\frac{I_2-I_3}{Z}.
\]
Substitution gives exactly the three asserted arctangent formulas. Their sum is one and their weighted sum is \(2\pi/\gamma\).

**3. Affine invariance of the section-side support.** An invertible affine map takes a generic plane and a section polygon to a generic plane and a combinatorially equivalent section polygon. Therefore it preserves the possible generic side counts, though it does not preserve the Euclidean-motion-invariant distribution on planes. This proves the claimed extension to affine cuboctahedra.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library checker `verify_crofton.py` reconstructs all twelve vertices and twenty-four edges; verifies all twenty-four fixed-sign neighbor comparisons on the three extreme rays of the fundamental normal cone; verifies the central crossing count and all six vertex-event increments; and independently evaluates the spherical triangle vector-area integral by oriented great-circle boundary arcs. It then compares the evaluated probabilities to the closed formulas and checks normalization and the expectation. Running `python3 verify_crofton.py` gives `VERIFY_OK` with the three probabilities stated above. Separate random-direction/offset numerical experiments agreed with the exact probabilities to sampling precision but are not part of the proof. The proof itself is analytic and finite-combinatorial.

## Relationship to prior work
The cuboctahedron is classically the rectified cube and octahedron. Matteo's 2014 classification proves its special two-orbit symmetry and gives its vertex/edge incidence data but does not determine the combinatorial law of invariantly sampled affine-plane cuts. Rajpoot's geometric analysis of the cuboctahedron treats face geometry, radii, area and volume, not the random affine-section law. Chakerian and Logothetti examine cube slices, which concern a different polyhedron; their full article was not accessible for comparison, and remains a recorded priority risk. Prior exact laws for **central** sections of cubes and for unrelated Archimedean solids do not imply this **offset-integrated** cuboctahedral distribution. General integral-geometry sampling identities provide the measure used here but do not supply the evaluated three-atom law or its parity rigidity without the new edge-event classification.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns generic plane sections: planes containing vertices or edges may have degenerate combinatorics not described by the probability table, but comprise a null set. It does not determine section areas, perimeters, conditional metric distributions, or the numerical probabilities for arbitrary affine stretches. The geometric and historical novelty assessment is based on inspected accessible sources and targeted overlap searches; unindexed historical material, especially the unavailable full text of the 1991 cube-slice paper, remains a residual risk. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
