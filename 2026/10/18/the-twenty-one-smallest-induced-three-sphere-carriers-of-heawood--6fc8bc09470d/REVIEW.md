# Scientific review: induced Heawood homology carriers

## Correctness — PASS
The statement uses the point–covector definition of the Fano incidence graph, all fourteen labeled vertices, all 2002 nine-vertex and 1001 ten-vertex induced subcomplexes, and inclusion-induced degree-three maps. The reproducible exact calculation verifies mod-two third-homology ranks for every one, the incidence classification of precisely twenty-one complements, integral sphere homotopy via 69-pair acyclic matchings for each, and an acyclic 225-pair ambient matching leaving one vertex and six three-cells. The nonstandard inclusion assertion is separately tested: 21 local three-cycles are not ambient four-boundaries, and adjoining them increases ambient boundary rank from 44 to 50. Vanishing mod-two degree-three homology gives a rational upper bound by rank comparison; nonvanishing ambient mod-two images of free integral sphere generators yields nonzero rational maps. The 21 images span rationally but are not claimed to form an integral saturated basis.

## Originality — PASS with bibliographic risk
The published Heawood homotopy calculation identifies the ambient wedge of six three-spheres and a separate published analysis identifies its six-dimensional collineation module. Both RESULTS were inspected, and neither computes ten-vertex induced complexes, inclusion homomorphisms, or the 21-support list. Barmak's general star-cluster methods and Tsukuda's incidence-graph/Alexander-duality reduction have different stated conclusions. The exact formulation of Hochster's formula in Dochtermann–Engström transfers the newly determined carrier enumeration to the graded Betti number; it does not give the numerical answer itself. Searches also tested aliases including Fano incidence graph, Heawood Stanley–Reisner ring, induced third-homology carriers, and multigraded Betti numbers. A specialized published table using the Hochster viewpoint might contain the 21 count and remains a concrete residual risk; search absence is not uniqueness proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The Heawood graph is the canonical point–line incidence graph of the Fano plane; its previously determined six-dimensional third homology does not answer how many vertices are needed to realize any nonzero class by an induced subcomplex, which carriers suffice, or which maps generate the ambient group. The theorem provides a sharp support threshold, a complete homogeneous geometric classification of 21 minimal carriers, a strong integral homotopy description of each carrier, and a six-dimensional generation test. These data also produce the natural squarefree Betti invariant \(\beta_{6,10}(\mathbb Q[K])=21\), but do not assert a general theorem for larger projective planes.
