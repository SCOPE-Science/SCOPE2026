# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The claim concerns the uniform-start coarsest equitable partition inside each graph, including full side-swapping automorphisms for balanced connected bipartitions. The frozen mask enumeration covers every graph by uniqueness of its unordered bipartition. Its candidate filter is safe: a stable cell all of whose vertices are open twins is a single orbit by transposing any two twins, and different stable cells cannot meet the same orbit. I independently enumerated all doubly sorted nonzero incidence matrices, retaining connected ones and testing exact orbits without that filter. Every graph admits a doubly sorted labeling: choose the largest row-major binary code under independent row and column permutations; an inversion of either numerical row or numerical column masks could be swapped to increase it. For every left permutation, the right bijections are exactly the matches of the induced column types; for equal sides the same test against the transpose exhausts side-exchanging maps. On each matrix I compared counted refinement with the orbits generated by all such maps. Order one is immediate. Among 2,251 candidate-free connected matrix representations at nine vertices none failed; among 26,648 at ten vertices five failed, collapsing to exactly two under full isomorphism. Their masks, edge counts, stable and orbit sizes, and group orders (16, 48) agree with the frozen finding. No larger-order extrapolation is made.

## value

PASS

The exact boundary of when uniform colour refinement suffices to recover vertex orbits in connected bipartite graphs is a natural structural question. The two types are useful small test cases for distinct reasons: one failure hides two kinds of edge subdivisions in one semiregular cell, and the other hides two orbits in a completely regular cubic graph. Exhausting all smaller graphs and both exception types gives a benchmark not supplied by total graph counts or cross-graph identification tests.
