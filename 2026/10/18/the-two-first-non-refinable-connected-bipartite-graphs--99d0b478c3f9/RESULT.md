# The two first non-refinable connected bipartite graphs

## Finding
For every finite connected simple bipartite graph with at most nine vertices, ordinary synchronous one-dimensional counting colour refinement, started with one colour on every vertex, has stable cells equal to the full automorphism orbits. At ten vertices exactly two nonisomorphic connected bipartite graphs fail this equality. They admit the following incidence-row encodings: with left-side size \(a\), right-side size \(b\), the integer mask \(r_i\) has bit \(j\) set exactly when left vertex \(i\) is adjacent to right vertex \(j\), numbering from zero.

* \(a=4,b=6\), masks \((25,38,7,56)\). There are 12 edges. The stable cell sizes are \((4,6)\), whereas orbit sizes are \((4,2,4)\).
* \(a=5,b=5\), masks \((28,7,11,19,28)\). There are 15 edges and the graph is cubic. Its single stable cell has size 10, but its orbit sizes are \((4,6)\).

The minimum order of a connected bipartite graph whose stable one-dimensional counting-refinement partition is not its orbit partition is therefore exactly ten, with exactly two isomorphism types at that order.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple, undirected and connected; bipartite includes the one-vertex graph. The initial vertex colour is uniform. The next colour is the ordered pair of the old colour and the multiset of old neighbour colours. Stabilization means the resulting vertex partition does not refine further. An orbit is under the full automorphism group of the uncoloured graph, including possible interchange of the two sides of a balanced bipartition. The assertion is an exhaustive finite cutoff through order ten; it is not an all-order classification or a graph-identification theorem against arbitrary competitors.

## Proof
The following finite enumeration is exhaustive by a direct parametrization, rather than an unverified sample. Every connected bipartite graph with at least two vertices has a unique unordered bipartition \((L,R)\), because parity of distance from one vertex fixes the sides. Order them so \(a=|L|\le b=|R|\). For any fixed \(a+b=n\), label \(R\) by \(0,\ldots,b-1\); its incidence with each \(i\in L\) is represented by a nonzero \(b\)-bit mask. Sorting the \(a\) masks weakly increasingly loses no isomorphism type. Conversely, every such list with each right vertex used and with the associated graph connected represents a connected simple bipartite graph, possibly with duplicates. Therefore looping over every weakly increasing list of masks in \(\{1,\ldots,2^b-1\}\), testing column coverage and connectivity, covers every graph of every requested order.

For each incidence matrix the included enumerator computes exact stable colours by iterating the old-colour and neighbour-count signature. There are at most \(n\) strict refinements, so the algorithm's bound of \(n\) steps is sufficient. Automorphisms always preserve those stable colours. A stable cell contained in one bipartition side whose vertices have identical open neighbourhoods is a single automorphism orbit: every permutation of those vertices alone preserves adjacency. Hence any graph in which every stable cell has that property is necessarily refinable. The enumerator retains all remaining cases: a same-colour pair of different open neighbourhoods on one side, or a same-colour pair on different sides. This *necessary* test is deliberately permissive; no possible failure is discarded.

To remove only bipartition-preserving duplicates, the program permutes all \(a!\) left rows, sorts the resulting \(b\) column signatures, and chooses the least resulting bit code. Equality of this canonical code is equivalent to isomorphism preserving the designated two sides. For \(a=b\), the same uncoloured graph can additionally admit a side-interchanging isomorphism; the final classification uses full graph isomorphism, not only the side-preserving code.

The exhaustive side-preserving candidate totals are 224 on nine vertices and 1,810 on ten vertices; smaller orders are checked separately by a second full incidence-row enumeration. Exact automorphism enumeration for every candidate yields no failing graph through nine vertices and exactly two full isomorphism types at ten, with incidence masks as displayed above. On nine vertices there are 730 unlabelled connected bipartite graphs. At ten vertices the complete connected bipartite census is 4,032 types: the enumerator has 5,664 side-preserving types, including 3,529 with equal sides, and collapsing those 3,529 under side exchange gives 1,897 balanced types, hence \(5664-3529+1897=4032\). The independent known sequence A005142 confirms 730 and 4,032, verifying the completeness scale but not supplying the orbit calculations.

The witnesses also have direct structural interpretations. The \((4,6)\) example subdivides each edge of an alternating-multiplicity four-cycle with edge multiplicities \(1,2,1,2\); its four original vertices form one orbit, while the two subdividers of the multiplicity-one edges form a second orbit and the four subdividers of the multiplicity-two edges a third. Thus the three orbit sizes are \((4,2,4)\), whereas counting refinement only sees the bidegrees three and two. The \((5,5)\) example is formed from two disjoint copies of \(K_{2,3}\) by matching their three-vertex sides pairwise. Its four vertices on the two-vertex sides form one orbit and the six remaining vertices another. Every vertex has degree three, so uniform-start counting refinement never splits the one colour. The complete automorphism enumerations give respectively 16 and 48 automorphisms.

## Verification
Run the standard-library C++17 source `enumerate_candidate_graphs.cpp` with a C++17 compiler. Execute `./enumerate_candidate_graphs 9 candidates9.txt all_graphs9.txt` and then `./enumerate_candidate_graphs 10 candidates10.txt all_graphs10.txt`. The generated candidate and full-census data included here can be compared bytewise with the embedded files. With Python 3 and NetworkX as stated in `requirements.txt`, run `python verify_candidates.py` and `python check_census.py`. The first independently evaluates stable colours and enumerates all automorphisms on the side-preserving candidate lists; it additionally enumerates every connected bipartite incidence-row representation through nine vertices without using the C++ candidate filter. The second independently reduces balanced-side representatives under side exchange and checks the published graph count. Recorded outputs `verification_output_final.txt` and `census_output.txt` both end in PASS. Every supplied script was executed against the supplied data.

## Relationship to prior work
Arvind, Köbler, Rattan, and Verbitsky (arXiv:1502.01255, publicly available 2015-02-04), give the foundational definition of refinability, the hierarchy of colour-refinement properties, and general structural theory, but the inspected relevant statements do not assert this two-exception connected-bipartite cutoff. Kiefer, Schweitzer, and Selman (arXiv:1503.08792, publicly available 2015-03-30) characterize identification by counting logic and connect it with stable refinement. Identification across different graphs does not coincide with recovery of automorphism orbits within a fixed graph: in particular, an earlier connected-bipartite nine-vertex count-logic equivalence census concerns *different nonisomorphic graphs*, and does not imply the present within-graph cutoff. OEIS A005142 records the unlabelled connected-bipartite counts, but not the orbit-refinement classification. No covering assertion was located in the inspected primary material and mathematical-index searches; a specialized prior classification is a residual priority risk.

## Limitations
The cutoff and the uniqueness claim are proved only for connected bipartite graphs with at most ten vertices. A graph on ten vertices can fail refinement for reasons unrelated to identification of other graphs. Computational exhaustion is justified by the complete finite incidence parametrization and checked by independently implemented orbit calculations; it is not a dimension-free theorem. No independent scientific audit or priority certification has been performed.
