The claim is finite, exactly quantified over connected simple interval graphs of order at most eight. Every such graph admits a simplicial vertex with connected interval deletion. Every possible clique-neighbour extension of all relevant smaller isomorphism types is enumerated. Interval membership is tested by the proved chordal/asteroidal-triple criterion; candidates are grouped by isomorphism invariants but deduplicated only by exact isomorphism. The graph atlas is complete through seven vertices. Exact self-isomorphism enumeration supplies complete automorphism orbits and synchronous counting refinement supplies the stable partition. The full census produces 1,328 eight-vertex classes and exactly one exception. Independently, a second script begins with all 272 seven-vertex connected chordal types, recognizes interval graphs by maximal-clique consecutive ordering, and obtains exactly the same exception. The explicit witness has clique-path representation, stable equitable quotient, and fully determined automorphism orbits. Results are reproducible from the included source files and recorded full outputs, not merely logs or truncated experiments.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The 2015 refinability hierarchy of Arvind, Köbler, Rattan and Verbitsky does not enumerate minimal interval-graph exceptions. The 2015 classification of graphs identified by two-variable counting logic concerns a stronger cross-graph property. The chordal/asteroidal-triple characterization and Hanlon's interval graph counts do not give refinability membership. Earlier mathematical findings already identify the eight-vertex *witness* as a double-broom-tree line graph and classify it within that narrow infinite family, which is explicitly credited rather than represented as new. Neither that statement nor the previous eight-vertex cross-graph chordal identification boundary gives uniqueness among all connected interval graphs. The value asserted here is the exact all-interval cutoff; searches did not reveal an equivalent earlier census. A specialized older enumeration may nevertheless exist. ## Value — PASS

This is a sharp natural class-wide cutoff, not a randomly chosen graph. Interval graphs are a standard hereditary class admitting a well-structured clique path, and refinability tests whether a canonical local symmetry invariant agrees with actual automorphism orbits. The theorem shows exactly when the class first departs from orbit recoverability, and not merely that the familiar eight-vertex witness fails. Exhaustive uniqueness at the natural first obstruction is a usable boundary fact for colour refinement on intersection graphs.
