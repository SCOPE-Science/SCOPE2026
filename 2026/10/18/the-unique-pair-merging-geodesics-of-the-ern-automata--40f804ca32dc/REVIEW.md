# Scientific review: unique shortest pair-merging words

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem has an all-parameter proof, not a computer-extrapolated pattern. The gap-and-rotation coordinates \((d,j)\) are a bijection with unordered state pairs, including the even-state antipodal boundary. The explicit integer potential \(R(P_{d,j})=n(d-1)+j+1\) never decreases by more than one under either letter; exactly one letter decreases it by one. The resulting forced descent constructs the claimed word, proves its minimal length, and proves **uniqueness**. The consecutive-block counting establishes the full spectrum and bivariate enumerator. Independently built reverse-BFS distance and shortest-path counts agree for \(3\le n\le40\), covering \(10{,}659\) unordered pairs. The all-\(n\) conclusion rests on the proof.

## Originality — PASS within examined evidence
The leading same-object precursor is V. V. Gusev, *Synchronizing Automata with Random Inputs*, arXiv:1404.6731v1, Section 3 and Lemma 1. Gusev's representation of the **odd-state** pair automaton uses swapped letter names and is explicit enough that the present distance formula can be derived from it; this is a substantial priority consideration. The paper's actual results optimize **expected random-input hitting time**, not unit-cost shortest-word uniqueness, complete pairwise distance multiplicities, or the optimal-word Parikh enumerator. Its even-state discussion also concerns random-input expectations. The 2026 survey of M. Szykuła (arXiv:2608.24245v1) discusses Černý extremality, pair compression, and subset synchronization but does not state this exact all-pair result in accessible material. F. Gonze and collaborators (arXiv:1704.04047v2) study a distinct pair-digraph problem and distinct automata. Explicit-index and literature searches yielded no statement subsuming the complete claimed optimization classification. Relevant public preprints can contain missed material; the 2026 survey could not be read in full and is a residual risk, not represented as fully excluded.

## Value — PASS
Determining the exact shortest control word for **every** unordered pair in the paradigmatic slowly synchronizing automata, including uniqueness and its complete word-composition distribution, is a natural classification of pair-compression difficulty. It shows the classical worst-case \(\binom n2\) pair bound is realized by one pair, while every smaller time is also realized by exactly one pair. The paired-word formula supplies all optimal controls, not merely minimum costs. These features illuminate the gap between pair merging and full-automaton reset behavior and between optimal and random-input merging. The result does not claim a solution of the general reset conjecture.

## Literature and limits
Primary comparisons: Szykuła, arXiv:2608.24245v1 (2026); Gusev, arXiv:1404.6731v1 (2014); Gonze et al., arXiv:1704.04047v2 (2017). Strongest overlap is Gusev's explicit odd-state pair graph, which substantially reduces the effort needed to derive the odd case. The even antipodal case, unique optimal words, the full length spectrum and compositions are included in the present explicit classification. Independent scientific evaluation and a comprehensive historical priority proof are not claimed.
