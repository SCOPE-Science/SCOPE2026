# Scientific review: directionally uniform circular cones

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is a strict, all-aspect-ratio minimum of the squared relative variance of Euclidean random widths of a **solid right circular cone**, and a strict separation from the previously known worst-direction range optimum. The proof starts from the exact support formula for an arbitrary Haar-uniform direction; its one-dimensional direction coordinate is truly uniform on the entire interval. Its first and second moments are integrated over all directions, with the correct cone radius, and give the stated dimensionless functional. The derivative identity (4) reduces every stationary point to \(T(t)=\pi\), with a strictly positive prefactor. The critical nonstandard step is the strict sign analysis of \(T'(t)\) for \(0<t\le3\): the two cases for the polynomial \(P\) exhaust the interval, and both yield explicitly negative bounds. The separate \(t\ge3\) bound rules out any additional crossing. The small-parameter limit and continuity guarantee exactly one crossing. The comparison with \(t=\sqrt3\) uses an exact special value of \(T\), not a numerical coincidence. The finite checker passed `VERIFY_OK 1533`, but the infinite inequalities and uniqueness rest on the proof, not its sampled tests. The theorem does not assume or establish a minimum over other convex-body families.

## Originality — PASS, with remaining bibliographic risk

A material reading of the published *Unlabeled directional widths determine a right circular cone* includes its entire support-function reduction, piecewise density and inverse-law proof. This is **stronger prior coverage of the forward width distribution** and implies the possibility of computing moments; the moment calculations alone are not presented as the new contribution. Its stated results and proof do not contain the optimum of mean-normalized width variance, the global derivative sign theorem, or its strict mismatch with the already available sharp endpoint-range optimum. Omri Abas's 2026 manuscript *Random Width and Brightness* supplies general recent motivation, variance formulas, and polyhedral/zonotopal methods but, in the materially inspected pertinent sections, does not optimize the anisotropy of solid circular cones. The published *Sharp width-variance extremizers among tree graphical zonotopes* has a similar type of objective but a different object and classification. Searches for the exact aspect-ratio parameter, alias terminology (width dispersion, mean-square near-constant width, coefficient of variation, randomized directional widths), and prior cones did not identify a dominating theorem. These are affirmative mathematical implication comparisons, not a proof of historical uniqueness. An older unindexed computation might still contain the optimal parameter; that risk remains disclosed.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Variance normalized by squared mean width is a natural scale-free mean-square measure of departure from constant width and is meaningful for the entire cone similarity class. Near-constant directional widths matter for geometric anisotropy benchmarks and support-based shape design; the optimum has only about \(4.11\%\) relative standard deviation. The exact uniqueness theorem is more than a plug-in moment evaluation: the derivative changes sign precisely once because of a nontrivial global radical-polynomial inequality. Moreover, minimizing the worst-direction width ratio selects \(\sqrt3\), while mean-square dispersion selects the **strictly different** ratio \(1.7090130666\ldots\). This establishes a substantive boundary between two canonical notions of isotropy, rather than an arbitrary finite numerical slice. Neither finite-sample statistical guarantees nor a universal best convex body is claimed.
