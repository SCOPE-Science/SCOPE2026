# Thinness versus monomiality at the first truncated-polynomial non-thin strata

## Finding

Let \(k\) be an arbitrary field, \(R=k[t]/(t^{14})\), and \(S\subseteq R\) a unital \(k\)-subalgebra. For nonzero \(f\in R\), let \(\nu(f)\) be its least nonzero power of \(t\). Consider either of the valuation sets
\[
E_7=\{0,4,6,8,10,12,13\},\qquad
E_8=\{0,4,6,8,10,11,12,13\}.
\]
For every such \(S\) whose valuation set is \(E_7\) or \(E_8\), choose monic elements \(u,v\in S\) with \(\nu(u)=4\) and \(\nu(v)=6\), and write the coefficients of \(t^5\) in \(u\) and of \(t^7\) in \(v\) as \(\alpha,\beta\). Define \(\delta=3\alpha-2\beta\in k\).

The abstract \(k\)-algebra structure of \(S\) depends only on whether \(\delta=0\). When \(\delta\ne0\), \(S\) is a monomial algebra. When \(\delta=0\), \(S\) is **not isomorphic to any finite-dimensional local monomial algebra**, over any field. This reverses, in both valuation sets, the usual *valuation thinness* status: \(\delta=0\) is thin and \(\delta\ne0\) is non-thin. The four resulting abstract isomorphism types (two dimensions, two statuses) are precisely the following:

\[
B_M=k[u,v]/(u^4,u^2v,uv^2,v^3),
\]
\[
B_T=k[u,v,w]/(u^4,u^2v,uv^2,v^3,u^3-v^2,uw,vw,w^2).
\]
For \(E_7\), the non-thin algebra is \(B_M\) and the thin algebra is \(B_T\). For \(E_8\), respectively, they are \(B_M\oplus kw\) and \(B_T\oplus kh\), in which the added one-dimensional summand annihilates each maximal ideal, has square zero, and has scalar multiplication through the common residue field \(k\). The symbols in these direct-socle extensions are new independent generators (not an additional identity).

Moreover the full automorphism functors of the seven-dimensional types are represented, **as affine varieties (not asserted as direct-product algebraic groups)**, by
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(B_M)\simeq\mathbb G_m^2\times\mathbb A^9,
\qquad
\operatorname{Aut}(B_T)\simeq\mathbb G_m^2\times\mathbb A^{11}.
\]
Both automorphism schemes are smooth in every characteristic. Consequently, for every finite field \(\mathbb F_q\),
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb F_q}(B_M)|=(q-1)^2q^9,\qquad
|\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb F_q}(B_T)|=(q-1)^2q^{11}.
\]
Thus valuation-thin and valuation-non-thin embeddings of identical prescribed valuation set give different intrinsic monomiality and an exact two-dimensional jump in automorphism-group dimension.

## Assumptions and scope

All algebras are commutative, associative and unital over \(k\). A finite-dimensional *local monomial algebra* means a finite-dimensional local quotient of a polynomial algebra by an ideal generated by monomials, up to abstract \(k\)-algebra isomorphism. Its standard grading agrees with its maximal-ideal associated grading. Valuation thinness means that \(\dim_k(\mathfrak m_S/\mathfrak m_S^2)\) equals the number of minimal positive additive generators of the truncated valuation set, not the unrelated notion of monomiality. No restriction on the characteristic is imposed. The valuation sets \(E_7,E_8\) are fixed; the statement does not classify all subalgebras of \(R\).

## Proof

**1. Invariant parameter and multiplication.** Since \(u\) and \(v\) begin at orders \(4\) and \(6\), direct multiplication modulo \(t^{14}\) gives
\[
u^3-v^2=\delta t^{13}\pmod{t^{14}}.
\]
The coefficient \(\delta\) is independent of the choice of monic \(u,v\): the difference between two monic valuation-four elements in \(S\) has valuation at least \(6\) (as \(5\notin E_j\)), and similarly two monic valuation-six elements differ by valuation at least \(8\) (as \(7\notin E_j\)); thus neither indicated coefficient changes. The only products of positive-valuation elements that can be nonzero at these valuation sets have initial orders \(8,10,12\); they are \(u^2,uv,u^3,v^2\). All products \(u^2v,uv^2,u^4,v^3\) vanish by order at least \(14\). Elements of orders \(11\) and \(13\) annihilate the positive-valuation ideal. These assertions do not use division by \(2\) or \(3\).

**2. Presentations and completeness.** If \(\delta\ne0\), \(u^3-v^2=\delta t^{13}\ne0\); thus the seven monomials \(1,u,v,u^2,uv,v^2,u^3\) are linearly independent, their valuations giving the six distinct orders \(0,4,6,8,10,12\) and a last independent vector at order \(13\) by subtraction. They exhaust \(S\) for \(E_7\), proving \(S\cong B_M\). If \(\delta=0\), the monic order-thirteen element \(w=t^{13}\) is independent of \(1,u,v,u^2,uv,u^3\), and \(v^2=u^3\). Hence these seven elements are a basis and give the displayed presentation of \(B_T\). Adding the independent monic order-eleven element \(h\) for \(E_8\) adds one square-zero socle generator and yields the two eight-dimensional types. No other coefficients affect the multiplication tables. Conversely, each type occurs over every field: \(u=t^4+t^5,v=t^6+t^7\) gives \(\delta=1\), and \(u=t^4,v=t^6\) together with \(w=t^{13}\) gives \(\delta=0\). Adjoin \(h=t^{11}\) for the eight-dimensional cases.

**3. Valuation thinness.** In \(E_7\), the minimal positive additive generators are \(4,6,13\), so the number is \(3\). In \(E_8\), they are \(4,6,11,13\), so the number is \(4\). When \(\delta\ne0\), the order-thirteen generator lies in \(\mathfrak m^2\), making the embedding dimensions \(2\) and \(3\), respectively. When \(\delta=0\), it does not, giving embedding dimensions \(3\) and \(4\). Thus precisely the \(\delta=0\) types are valuation-thin. The Hilbert functions \(\dim_k\mathfrak m^j/\mathfrak m^{j+1}\) for \(j=0,1,2,3\) are \((1,2,3,1)\) and \((1,3,2,1)\) for \(B_M,B_T\), respectively; the socle extensions add one to the second entry. Therefore the four types are pairwise nonisomorphic.

**4. Intrinsic monomiality obstruction.** The presentations of \(B_M\) and its socle extension are monomial. For \(B_T\), write \(z=u^3=v^2\). Then \(\mathfrak m^2=\langle u^2,uv,z\rangle\) and \(\mathfrak m^3=\langle z\rangle\). Any element \(x=a u+b v+c w+P\), \(P\in\mathfrak m^2\), has
\[
x^2=a^2u^2+2ab\,uv+(b^2+2a\,[u^2]P)z.
\]
Since \(u^2,uv,z\) are independent over every field, \(x^2=0\) forces \(a=b=0\). Consequently the image in \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\) of the square-zero locus is exactly the one-dimensional space \(kw\). In the associated graded algebra \(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m}(B_T)\), the relation \(v^2=z\in\mathfrak m^3\) becomes \(v^2=0\) in degree two. Hence the images of square-zero elements contain the two-dimensional space \(\langle v,w\rangle\) in degree one (and precisely this space). These dimensions are preserved by any algebra isomorphism, so \(B_T\not\cong\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m}(B_T)\). Every finite-dimensional local monomial quotient is canonically isomorphic to its radical-associated graded algebra because its defining monomial ideal is homogeneous. Therefore \(B_T\) is not monomializable. With the additional annihilator generator \(h\), the corresponding square-zero-lift image dimensions are \(2\) versus \(3\), establishing the same conclusion for the eight-dimensional thin type.

**5. Automorphism schemes.** In \(B_M\), the maximal ideal has basis \(u,v,u^2,uv,v^2,u^3\), and the cubic ideal is \(ku^3\). Preservation of this ideal forces the coefficient of \(u\) in the image of \(u\) to be invertible. The relation \(u^2v=0\) then forces the coefficient of \(u\) in the image of \(v\) to vanish. Conversely every substitution
\[
u\mapsto a u+b v+P,\qquad v\mapsto d v+Q,
\quad a,d\in k^\times,\ b\in k,\ P,Q\in\mathfrak m^2
\]
preserves all defining relations and has invertible induced maps on the successive radical layers. Since \(\dim\mathfrak m^2=4\), this provides \(\mathbb G_m^2\times\mathbb A^9\) as a parameter variety.

In \(B_T\), put \(z=u^3=v^2\). Preservation of the third radical power, the relations \(u^2v=0\), and the annihilation of \(w\) force the general automorphism to have the following—and only the following—normal form:
\[
\begin{aligned}
u&\mapsto a u+b v+c w+P,\\
v&\mapsto e v+f w+Q,\\
w&\mapsto i w+Huv+Iz,
\end{aligned}
\qquad P,Q\in\langle u^2,uv,z\rangle,
\]
where \(a,e,i\in k^\times\) and \(e^2=a^3\). These conditions are sufficient because all defining products vanish or reduce to \(u^3=v^2=z\). There are \(11\) free affine coordinates: \(b,c,f,H,I\) and three coefficients in each of \(P,Q\). The equation \(e^2=a^3\) is parametrized without taking roots by \(a=s^2,e=s^3\), with inverse \(s=e/a\). Together with \(i\), this gives \(\mathbb G_m^2\times\mathbb A^{11}\). The coordinate derivations use only equations whose leading coefficients are invertible, so the normal forms remain valid for commutative base rings, proving the asserted smooth automorphism schemes. Their finite-field point counts follow immediately.

## Verification

The standalone \(\texttt{verify.py}\) constructs both seven-dimensional multiplication tables, exhaustively enumerates all automorphisms over \(\mathbb F_2\), and obtains \(512\) for \(B_M\) and \(2048\) for \(B_T\), agreeing with the formulas. For each of characteristics \(2,3,5\), it constructs both types inside \(k[t]/(t^{14})\), checks seven or eight independent basis elements and closure, and tests the square-zero-lift distinction for the seven-dimensional thin type in characteristics \(2\) and \(3\). The program prints \(\texttt{VERIFY_OK}\). The all-field conclusion follows from the proof, not these finite tests.

## Relationship to prior work

Assi–García-Sánchez–Micale (first public 2014) develops value-semigroup and reduced-basis methods for one-variable subalgebras and analyzes collisions such as orders \(4\) and \(6\), but not the present abstract monomiality/automorphism-scheme classification. Franco Muñoz (2018), Theorem 6 and Propositions 7–8, establishes the minimal \(14\)-truncation non-thin example, the two valuation strata and their embedded finite-field counts; these are **prior facts**, not contributions claimed here. The new claim begins with the intrinsic four-type collapse, the reversed monomiality correspondence, and the two complete automorphism-scheme parameterizations. Existing classification of Artinian Gorenstein algebras treats a distinct socle-one class: both seven-dimensional examples here have socle dimension \(3\). A recent study of almost-monomial subalgebras of \(k[x]\) uses a different notion of thinness and does not directly state the truncated-ring automorphism results.

## Limitations

This is a classification only for the two explicitly identified valuation strata in the \(14\)-truncation, not for all subalgebras or all lengths. The published precursor already enumerates their embeddings; those enumeration results are not new. The nonmonomiality claim concerns abstract finite-dimensional local monomial presentations, not weighted homogeneity. No independent mathematical audit has been performed; an unlocated classification of seven-dimensional commutative local algebras could contain an equivalent structural treatment.
