Franco Muñoz (2018), arXiv:1812.03576, Theorem 6 and Propositions 7–8, already establishes the first non-thin truncation at exponent fourteen and the relevant valuation sets and embedded point counts, which are expressly excluded from novelty. The inspected primary text does not give the abstract monomial/nonmonomial comparison or automorphism schemes. Jelisiejew's Artin Gorenstein work concerns socle-one algebras, unlike these socle-three types. A 2025 study of almost-monomial polynomial subalgebras uses a distinct notion of thinness. Semantic mathematical-record searches for the valuation spectra, presentations and automorphism parameters found related but non-dominating theorems. Specialized local Artinian algebra classifications might already contain equivalent families; this is an explicit residual risk.

## Value

**PASS.** The first possible failure of valuation-semigroup thinness does not signal intrinsic failure of monomiality; the correspondence actually reverses at two distinct minimal valuation sets. The four-type classification separates an embedding invariant from an intrinsic algebra invariant and gives smooth, precisely enumerated automorphism schemes for the seven-dimensional pair. This supplies a motivated sharp structural boundary rather than a routine numerical recount of known embedded subalgebras.

## Limitations and closest antecedents

No claim is made about universal subalgebra classification or a general equivalence between valuation thinness and abstract monomiality. Exact finite-field embedded counts belong to Franco Muñoz's precursor theorem; all-field automorphism point-count formulas here follow from parametrized schemes. An unlocated complete classification of small commutative local algebras could cover the four examples. Mathematical verification is not independent.
