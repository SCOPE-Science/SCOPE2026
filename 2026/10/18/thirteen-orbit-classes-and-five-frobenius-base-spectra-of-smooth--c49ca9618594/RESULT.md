# Thirteen orbit classes and five Frobenius base spectra of smooth \((2,2)\) pencils over \(\mathbf F_2\)

## Finding

Let \(S=\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf F_2}\times\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf F_2}\), and put \(V=H^0(S,\mathcal O_S(2,2))\). Call a two-dimensional \(\mathbf F_2\)-subspace \(W\subset V\) **all-smooth** if all its three nonzero forms cut out geometrically smooth divisors on \(S\). Then:

1. Exactly **804** subspaces \(W\) are all-smooth; under the entire \(\mathbf F_2\)-automorphism group of \(S\), they form exactly **13 orbits** (ten of size 72, two of size 36, one of size 12).
2. No three-dimensional subspace of \(V\) has every nonzero member geometrically smooth. Thus projective dimension one is maximal for this linear-system problem on \(S\).
3. The base scheme \(B_W=V(F,G)\subset S\) of every such pencil is reduced over the algebraic closure and consists of exactly eight geometric points. The following are precisely its possible Frobenius-cycle partitions, with their equation-coordinate pencil counts and orbit counts:

| Frobenius cycle partition on eight base points | Pencils | \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbf F_2}(S)\)-orbits |
|---|---:|---:|
| \((8)\) | 72 | 1 |
| \((2,3,3)\) | 12 | 1 |
| \((1,7)\) | 288 | 4 |
| \((1,2,5)\) | 216 | 3 |
| \((1,1,6)\) | 216 | 4 |
| **Total** | **804** | **13** |

In particular, exactly 72 pencils have a single closed base point of degree eight. The rational base-point count can only be zero, one or two, occurring respectively for 84, 504 and 216 pencils.

## Assumptions and scope

Every coefficient belongs to \(\mathbf F_2\). The term *smooth* means smooth after base change to \(\overline{\mathbf F}_2\), not merely nonsingular at the nine rational points of \(S\). Only the three \(\mathbf F_2\)-rational members of a pencil must be smooth; members over extension fields may be singular. A smooth divisor of bidegree \((2,2)\) on \(S\) is geometrically integral of genus one, by the intersection form and adjunction. Different coefficient subspaces are counted separately, not modulo automorphisms, unless an orbit count is explicitly stated.

The full group is \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbf F_2}(S)\cong (\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_2)\times\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_2))\rtimes C_2\) of order 72, where the final involution swaps the two factors. It is not the automorphism group of an abstract genus-one curve.

## Proof

Write a general bidegree-\((2,2)\) form as
\[
F=\sum_{0\le i,j\le2}a_{ij}u^{2-i}v^i s^{2-j}t^j.
\]
The coefficient tuple is encoded by the nonzero integer \(m=\sum_{i,j} a_{ij}2^{3i+j}\) in the range 1 through 511. This lists every form exactly once; in \(\mathbf F_2\), no nontrivial scalar rescaling occurs.

**Exact geometric smoothness test.** On each of the four standard product affine charts, write the dehomogenization as \(f(x,y)\). The curve is geometrically smooth if and only if the ideal \((f,f_x,f_y)\subset\mathbf F_2[x,y]\) is the unit ideal on all four charts. This equivalence follows from the affine Jacobian criterion and the fact that the charts cover the base-changed surface. Reduced Gröbner bases over \(\mathbf F_2\) test the unit ideal exactly: they return the single basis element 1 if and only if the ideal contains 1. Enumerating the 511 nonzero coefficient masks yields precisely 144 smooth forms. An independent check over \(\mathbf F_{16}\) gives the same 144 (the proof of extension-field completeness appears in Verification).

**Pencil and net enumeration.** Any all-smooth pencil has the three distinct nonzero elements \(F,G,F+G\). Enumerating all unordered triples closed under addition among the 144 smooth masks gives 804 different two-dimensional subspaces. To extend a pencil to a three-dimensional all-smooth subspace would require a fourth smooth mask \(H\) such that \(H+F,H+G,H+F+G\) are also smooth. Exhaustive testing of each of the 804 pencils against each smooth mask finds no such \(H\). This proves the maximality assertion without relying on a timeout or sampling.

**Orbits and arithmetic strata.** The 72 automorphisms are explicitly generated by six invertible \(2\times2\) binary matrices in each factor and the factor swap. Substituting the induced transformations of the bidegree-two basis \((u^2,uv,v^2)\) into all 804 pencils yields 13 disjoint orbits of sizes as claimed. Independently, evaluation on the \(3\times3\) rational grid is a linear isomorphism \(V\to\mathbf F_2^9\): for each factor, evaluation of \((u^2,uv,v^2)\) at the three rational projective points has invertible binary matrix. The full automorphism group therefore acts by all row permutations, column permutations, and transposition of the grid. Recomputing orbits using this second model reproduces the same 13 orbits.

**Reduced base schemes.** A smooth bidegree-\((2,2)\) divisor is irreducible, so two different smooth members share no component. Their intersection number on \(S\) is \((2,2)\cdot(2,2)=8\). The scheme \(B_W\) is reduced over the algebraic closure if and only if no common zero of \(F,G\) has dependent two-coordinate gradients. On each of four charts this is the exact unit-ideal condition \((f,g,f_xg_y+f_yg_x)=(1)\). It holds for all 804 pencils by a complete Gröbner-basis test. Thus every base consists of eight distinct geometric points.

For each of the 13 orbit representatives, direct evaluation over \(\mathbf F_{2^d}\), for each \(1\le d\le8\), gives the number \(N_d\) of geometric base points defined over \(\mathbf F_{2^d}\). The chosen eight irreducible binary moduli and field operations are explicit in the verifier. If \(c_e\) is the number of degree-\(e\) closed base points, the identities
\[
N_d=\sum_{e\mid d}e c_e,\qquad
c_d=\frac{N_d-\sum_{e\mid d,\ e<d}e c_e}{d}
\]
recover every cycle length since the base has length eight. The five partitions in the first table result, and their multiplicities follow by orbit invariance. In particular, the orbit of the pencil represented by \((98,415)\) has one degree-eight base point and no smaller closed base point.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_groebner.py` with SymPy, and independently run `python3 verify_f16.py` with the Python standard library. Both programs enumerate all 511 nonzero forms and exactly reproduce 144 smooth forms, 804 smooth pencils, no all-smooth nets, 13 orbits and all rational point strata. The first checks all 804 base loci over the algebraic closure and computes Frobenius partitions using exact extension-field arithmetic. Their saved outputs are included. In the delivered package, both sources were re-executed after extraction and their outputs compared byte-for-byte with these saved records.

## Relationship to prior work

Asgarli and Ghioca posed and studied the existence of smooth rational members of finite-field pencils of hypersurfaces (arXiv:2203.07169, first public March 14, 2022, primary MSC 14N05). Their plane-conic and quadric-surface examples are not all-smooth pencils of genus-one divisors *on a fixed split quadric*, and do not imply any of the 804 counts, 13 orbits, five base-point partitions or maximality result here. Asgarli, Ghioca and Reichstein (arXiv:2207.00918) treat existence and nonexistence of linear families of projective hypersurfaces, again not this fixed-surface bidegree classification. Hamada and Hayano (arXiv:2008.02925) classify genus-one *holomorphic Lefschetz pencils over the complex numbers up to smooth isomorphism*, not the arithmetic coordinate orbits or Frobenius structures over \(\mathbf F_2\).

This is a complete finite-field classification of a natural extremal genus-one linear system, rather than a new general existence theorem or a classification of abstract elliptic curves. No equivalent published exact census was identified in the sources reviewed; undocumented or unindexed finite-geometry tables remain a possible priority risk.

## Limitations

The theorem is restricted to the split surface \(\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\), bidegree \((2,2)\), and the base field \(\mathbf F_2\); it makes no statement for nonsplit quadric forms, higher fields or larger bidegrees. The base loci are geometrically reduced, but not generally geometrically irreducible as zero-dimensional schemes. Arithmetic point counts and orbits concern fixed embeddings and coordinate subspaces, not abstract genus-one isomorphism classes. A complete contemporary and historical literature census is not guaranteed; independent mathematical audit remains outstanding.
