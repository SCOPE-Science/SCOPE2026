# Thirty conifold strata on the graphic-matroid toric fivefold of \(K_4\)

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, let \(E\) be the six edges of the complete graph \(K_4\), and put
\[
P=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf 1_T:T\subset E\text{ is a spanning tree}\}
 \subset \{x\in\mathbb R^E:\sum_{e\in E}x_e=3\}.
\]
For the five-dimensional normal projective toric variety \(X_P\) of this lattice polytope, the reduced singular locus has exactly thirty irreducible components. Twenty-four are isomorphic to \(\mathbb P^2\), and six are isomorphic to \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\). All thirty have dimension two and codimension three in \(X_P\), and their general transverse germ is the ordinary threefold node
\[
\operatorname{Spec} k[a,b,c,d]/(ad-bc).
\]
Every singular torus orbit lies in one of these thirty orbit closures. Equivalently, the reduced singular locus equals the non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial locus. There are exactly ninety-four singular torus orbits, distributed by orbit dimension as sixteen of dimension zero, forty-eight of dimension one, and thirty of dimension two.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground set is \(E=\{01,02,03,12,13,23\}\), in this order. A basis of the graphic matroid \(M(K_4)\) is a three-edge spanning tree of \(K_4\). There are sixteen bases. The lattice of differences of integral points of \(P\) is \(L=\{z\in\mathbb Z^6:\sum z_e=0\}\). Coordinates \(x_{01},x_{02},x_{03},x_{12},x_{13}\) identify \(L\) with \(\mathbb Z^5\), with \(x_{23}=3-\sum_{e\ne23}x_e\) on the affine span. By \(X_P\) we mean the normal toric variety with fan the normal fan of \(P\); equivalently it is the projective torus orbit closure of a realization of this matroid in \(\operatorname{Gr}(3,6)\).

## Proof
**Facets and exhaustive face certificate.** The base-polytope rank-inequality description gives \(\sum_{e\in S}x_e\le r_M(S)\) for each subset \(S\subset E\), in addition to \(\sum_e x_e=3\). Direct determination of the rank function of \(M(K_4)\) shows that the sixteen distinct facets are the six inequalities \(x_e\ge0\), six inequalities \(x_e\le1\), and four inequalities \(\sum_{e\in E(C)}x_e\le2\), one for each triangle \(C\) of \(K_4\). There are no others: the certificate tests all sixty-two nonempty proper subsets, and retains a rank inequality precisely when the affine rank of its equality vertices is four.

For a facet, let its *mask* denote the set of spanning-tree vertices satisfying its defining equality. Every nonempty face of a full-dimensional polytope is the intersection of facets, so the iterated intersections of the sixteen masks exhaust the face lattice. The self-contained exact-arithmetic certificate verifies 219 nonempty faces, with face numbers \((16,54,78,54,16,1)\) in dimensions zero through five.

**Smoothness test.** The cone normal to a face \(F\) is generated by the primitive outward normals of the facets containing \(F\). The torus orbit \(O_F\) has dimension \(\dim F\). By the smoothness criterion for normal toric varieties, it is smooth exactly when its normal cone is generated by part of an integral basis of the dual lattice. The certificate checks this without approximations: if \(c=5-\dim F\), the cone is nonsimplicial when its number of incident facets differs from \(c\); when the number is \(c\), the gcd of all \(c\)-minors of its integral normal-ray matrix must equal one. The output is sixteen singular vertices, forty-eight singular edges, thirty singular two-faces, and no higher-dimensional singular faces.

The thirty singular two-faces are inclusion-maximal among singular faces. Indeed, the exhaustive mask check verifies that each singular face belongs to one of them. Their orbit closures are therefore precisely the irreducible components of the reduced singular locus. Twenty-four of these two-faces are triangles and six are parallelograms. The certificate checks their edge lattices are primitive, so the induced toric surfaces are respectively the standard \(\mathbb P^2\) and \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\). The graph-automorphism group \(S_4\) splits the thirty faces into orbits of sizes twelve, twelve, and six.

**Transverse node and nonfactoriality.** For each of the thirty singular two-faces, the normal cone is three-dimensional with exactly four primitive rays \(v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4\). Exact lattice calculations show that any three rays generate a saturated rank-three lattice and, after relabeling, their unique primitive relation is \(v_1+v_2=v_3+v_4\). Thus each cone is unimodularly equivalent to the cone over a unit lattice square. Its affine toric variety is the rank-one \(2\times2\) determinantal cone \(ad-bc=0\), and the general transverse singularity along the associated orbit closure is the indicated ordinary node. These thirty cones are nonsimplicial, so their orbits are non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial. Conversely, every simplicial cone in the fan passed the integral basis test, hence is smooth and \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial. This proves equality of the two reduced loci.

## Relationship to prior work
Howard (2005) established projective normality of torus-orbit closures in flag varieties, including the Grassmannian setting. Feichtner and Sturmfels (2004) developed the matroid-polytope and Bergman-fan dictionary and treated the graphic matroid of \(K_4\), identifying its Bergman complex with the Petersen graph. These general descriptions do not state the thirty-component singular-locus or local-conifold classification. Later graph-theoretic work of Hibi and collaborators classifies the Gorenstein property for graphic-matroid base rings, with \(K_4\) a central example; that property is distinct from the singular-component problem. General face-enumeration results also do not by themselves determine the normal cones' transverse analytic types. The finding is a specific geometric and local-singularity computation, not a claim of new basic toric criteria or new normality results.

## Limitations
The classification concerns this specific five-dimensional toric variety and does not assert any formula for larger complete graphs, for other graph-derived toric varieties such as cut varieties, or for nonnormal monomial projections. The component census and local isomorphisms are obtained from finite exact enumeration backed by the included executable certificate. The originality conclusion is subject to possible differently formulated prior computations, especially unindexed tables of small matroid polytopes, and requires independent human literature review before public novelty claims.
