# Scientific review: graphic-matroid toric fivefold of \(K_4\)

## Correctness
The polytope is specified by all graphic matroid bases. The proof uses the known complete rank-inequality description, and an exact certificate enumerating the full finite list of facets and all their intersections, so the face lattice is exhaustive. The lattice test for toric smoothness is exact. The component count is the number of maximal singular faces; the local conifold types follow from the primitive four-ray relation and unimodularity of every three rays. The program was executed successfully from its standalone source. Finite enumeration is a complete proof only because the object is fixed and exhaustively represented, not by extrapolation.

## Originality
Howard's work on flag-torus orbit closures establishes projective normality, not the singular-component classification. Feichtner and Sturmfels describe the \(K_4\) matroid's Bergman complex but do not state the 30 irreducible singular strata or their conifold transversals. Hibi and coauthors classify Gorenstein graphic-matroid polytopes, a different invariant; later face-enumeration literature does not compute this local toric geometry. The claim remains vulnerable to an unindexed computation or table giving the same singular fan strata; no proof of global uniqueness can be inferred from searches.

## Value
The complete singular stratification of the distinguished \(K_4\) torus orbit closure gives 24 projective planes, 6 Segre surfaces, and uniform generic conifold types; the equality with the non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial locus links a natural graph matroid to precise birational-geometric obstruction strata. The numeric finite result is motivated by the central role of this matroid in Grassmannian torus-orbit geometry and does not amount to an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
Feichtner–Sturmfels (2004), Howard (2005), and Hibi et al. (2019) are closest in objects and methods. The result does not classify larger \(K_n\), other graph toric models, or nonsimplicial degenerations in unrelated matroids. Mathematical correctness and scientific novelty still warrant an independent assessment.
