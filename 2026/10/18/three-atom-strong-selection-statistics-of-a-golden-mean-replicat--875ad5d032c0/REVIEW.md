# Review of the strong-selection replicator-horseshoe theorem

## Correctness

**PASS.** The log-odds conjugacy is exact. For fixed asymmetric \(b\), two monotone interval branches exist at sufficiently large \(a\); their endpoint equations yield a bounded central interval and a second interval at log-odds \(ab+O(\log a)\). Every admissible two-symbol word visits the central branch, giving uniform two-step expansion. The transition-coded physical coordinate therefore approaches three explicit values uniformly, and stationarity fixes transition frequencies \(1-2q,q,q\). Exact mean telescoping, the derivative conjugacy and the conditional-entropy inequality establish the remaining formulas. The code tests finite examples but is not the infinite proof.

## Originality

**PASS under the documented comparisons, with remaining bibliographic risk.** Kryzhevich–Zhang, arXiv:2603.20685v1, Sections 4–6 and Appendix A, prove the horseshoe and fixed mean; the inspected statements and calculations do not give the all-measure three-atom law, joint variance–Lyapunov spectrum or entropy envelope. The closest identified published replicator result concerns the loss of stability of an individual two-cycle near symmetry, not these global statistics across all invariant laws. Searches for alternate formulations, large-selection symbolic limits, invariant marginals and Lyapunov spectra returned no decisive covering theorem. An unpublished or unindexed equivalent in the older replicator literature remains possible. This is not an independence certification.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The motivating fixed-mean property masks a continuum of higher-moment behaviors. The finding identifies the complete limiting physical marginal family, every attainable variance–expansion pair and the sharp dynamical entropy available at each symbolic frequency. Uniformity over every invariant law and the explicit second Lyapunov term go beyond merely exhibiting a single periodic orbit or applying a general shift theorem.

## Scope and limitations

The theorem is confined to the canonical large-parameter expanding subsystem, excludes the symmetric parameter, and makes no assertion about full-interval invariant laws or global statistical attraction. The entropy formula is exactly parameterized by symbolic frequency; physical variance is only asymptotically equivalent. Residual risk is equivalence with a less readily indexed singular-selection theorem.
