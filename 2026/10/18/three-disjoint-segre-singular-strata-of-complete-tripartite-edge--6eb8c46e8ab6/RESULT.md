# Three disjoint Segre singular strata of complete-tripartite edge toric varieties

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. For positive integers \(a,b,c\), let \(G=K_{a,b,c}\) be the complete tripartite graph, and form the standard graded edge ring \(R=k[z_u z_v:\{u,v\}\in E(G)]\subset k[z_v:v\in V(G)]\), with every edge monomial assigned degree one. Put \(X=\operatorname{Proj}R\).

For any two distinct parts \(U,V\), with omitted third part \(W\) of size \(w\), let \(Z_{UV}\subset X\) be the coordinate subvariety on which all edge coordinates outside \(U\times V\) vanish. Then \(Z_{UV}\cong\mathbb P^{|U|-1}\times\mathbb P^{|V|-1}\) under its Segre embedding, and
\[
\operatorname{Sing}(X)_{\mathrm{red}}=\coprod_{\{U,V\}:|W|\ge2} Z_{UV}.
\]
In particular, the components are **pairwise disjoint**, their number is the number of parts with size at least two, and \(\dim Z_{UV}=|U|+|V|-2\), \(\operatorname{codim}_X Z_{UV}=w+1\). Their projective degrees are \(\binom{|U|+|V|-2}{|U|-1}\).

More strongly, for every edge \(\{i,j\}\) joining the first and second parts, the entire standard affine chart is
\[
D_+(z_i z_j)\cong\mathbb A^{a+b-2}\times Y_c,\qquad
Y_c=\{M\in\operatorname{Mat}_{2\times c}(k):\operatorname{rank}M\le1\}.
\]
The same formula holds after permuting parts. The factor \(Y_1\) is smooth affine two-space. For \(w\ge2\), the singularity along \(Z_{UV}\) has this rank-one \(2\times w\) transverse determinantal model at **every point**, not merely generically; its transverse Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity is \(w\) and its local Weil divisor class group is \(\mathbb Z\). Consequently the singular locus is exactly the non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial locus. The variety \(X\) is normal and Cohen–Macaulay; it is \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein (equivalently Gorenstein) exactly when \(a,b,c\le2\), in which case it has terminal singularities.

## Assumptions and scope
All three parts are nonempty, \(a,b,c\ge1\), and the field has characteristic zero. The dimension is \(\dim X=a+b+c-1\). The displayed description is for the reduced singular locus and the indicated standard projective toric model, not the affine cone \(\operatorname{Spec}R\). All coordinate charts and geometric singularity identifications are scheme-theoretic isomorphisms. The statements about Gorensteinness and terminality are global local-ring properties of \(X\), not of an arbitrarily chosen projective embedding's cone.

## Proof
**1. Every edge chart is a product with a rank-one cone.** Pick \(i\) in the first part \(A\) and \(j\) in the second part \(B\). The coordinate ring of \(D_+(z_i z_j)\) is generated by Laurent monomials obtained by dividing every edge monomial by \(z_i z_j\). For \(p\in A\setminus\{i\}\), let \(u_p=z_p/z_i\); for \(q\in B\setminus\{j\}\), let \(v_q=z_q/z_j\). For \(r\in C\), introduce \(s_r=z_r/z_j\) and \(t_r=z_r/z_i\). The ratios for other edges are respectively \(u_pv_q\), \(u_ps_r\) and \(v_qt_r\), so these displayed monomials generate the chart. Their relations are exactly
\[
 s_rt_{r'}-s_{r'}t_r=0\quad(r,r'\in C).
\]
Indeed, the exponents of each \(u_p\) and \(v_q\) can be read uniquely from the coordinates indexed by \(p\) and \(q\). Among the remaining \(s,t\) monomials, equality of exponent vectors is equivalent to matching each column total (the total exponent on each \(z_r\)) and matching the number of \(s\)-factors (equivalently \(t\)-factors). These are precisely the fibers of the Segre monomial map \(s_r\mapsto Xz_r\), \(t_r\mapsto Yz_r\), whose kernel is generated by the \(2\times2\) minors, by repeated exchanges of a column between the two rows. This proves the chart isomorphism without assuming a saturation or normalization result.

**2. Singular locus and its components.** The cone \(Y_w\) is the cone over the Segre \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{w-1}\) when \(w\ge2\). It has dimension \(w+1\), and is smooth at every nonzero rank-one matrix (where a nonzero entry permits row/column elimination). At the origin the Jacobian of its quadratic minors vanishes and its tangent dimension is \(2w>w+1\). Thus \(\operatorname{Sing}(Y_w)=\{0\}\) for \(w\ge2\), whereas \(Y_1\cong\mathbb A^2\) is smooth. In an \(A\)-\(B\) chart, setting every \(s_r,t_r\) to zero imposes precisely that all edge coordinates involving \(C\) vanish; its intersection is the standard affine chart of the Segre variety \(Z_{AB}\). Every point of \(X\) is in at least one edge chart, so the charts exhaust the entire singular locus. Coordinate linear spaces supported on the three disjoint edge types \(A\!\times\!B\), \(A\!\times\!C\), \(B\!\times\!C\) are pairwise disjoint in projective space, so the \(Z\)'s are disjoint, irreducible, smooth, and are exactly the singular components that occur. The dimensions and codimensions follow from \(\dim Y_w=w+1\); the Segre degree is the coefficient of the product of top hyperplane powers \((H_U+H_V)^{|U|+|V|-2}\).

**3. Local invariants.** The Segre cone \(Y_w\) is a normal Cohen–Macaulay determinantal variety. Its graded coordinate ring has Hilbert function \((d+1)\binom{d+w-1}{w-1}\) in degree \(d\), hence Hilbert series \((1+(w-1)t)/(1-t)^{w+1}\) and vertex multiplicity \(w\). For \(w\ge2\), the divisor class group of the vertex local ring is \(\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^{w-1})/\mathbb Z[\mathcal O(1,1)]\cong\mathbb Z\); the cone is not \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial at its vertex. The canonical bundle of the Segre base is \(\mathcal O(-2,-w)\), so its cone's canonical class is torsion (indeed zero) exactly when \(w=2\). For \(w=1\) the local ring is regular. Accordingly \(X\) is normal and Cohen–Macaulay (both are local properties), and is \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein exactly when each part size is at most two. In that case all nonsmooth charts are products with the threefold ordinary double point \(Y_2=\{s_1t_2-s_2t_1=0\}\), which is Gorenstein and terminal in characteristic zero; products with smooth affine space preserve terminality.

As a non-equidimensional example, \((a,b,c)=(2,3,5)\) gives three disjoint components of dimensions \(3,5,6\), with transverse multiplicities \(5,3,2\), respectively.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify.py` checks all \(192\) edge-chart generator decompositions and lattice ranks for \(1\le a,b,c\le4\); the rank-one-cone degree-by-degree Hilbert counts for \(1\le w\le6\), \(0\le d\le4\); and the exact determinantal Jacobian ranks at nonzero rank-one matrices for \(2\le w\le6\). It also checks the indicated four sample singular-component counts. It uses exact integer and rational arithmetic only and prints `VERIFY_OK` on success. These finite checks are supporting evidence, not an all-parameter proof: the latter is the semigroup argument above.

## Relationship to prior work
Ohsugi and Hibi's 2000 investigation established fundamental combinatorial, toric-ideal and Hilbert-series information for edge rings of complete multipartite graphs, including their classical Gorenstein criterion. The numerical Gorenstein cutoff \(a,b,c\le2\) is therefore **not claimed as a standalone novelty**. Higashitani and Matsushita's 2020/2022 work studies affine edge-ring divisor class groups, conic ideals, and noncommutative resolutions. Here the asserted additional information concerns the **projective** variety's complete reduced singular locus as disjoint embedded Segre strata and the explicit \(2\times w\) transverse model **everywhere along each stratum**. General determinantal results supply the local invariant calculations once the precise projective chart is established; they are not independently claimed as new formulas. A complete published treatment of the same projective local-chart decomposition, if it exists, would subsume the result.

## Limitations
The classification concerns exactly three nonempty graph parts; it does not establish any four-or-more-part formula. The nonreduced Jacobian singular scheme is not analyzed. The 2000 original article was identifiable and its abstract examined, but full text was inaccessible during the comparison; possible previously unrecognized coverage there remains an originality risk. Independently verified novelty has not been established.
