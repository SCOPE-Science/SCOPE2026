# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof decomposes the denumerant by the total number of coins and uses monotonicity modulo the smallest denomination. In each of the three ranges the candidate has exactly \(p\) representations and every one of its next \(a\) successors has more than \(p\). The finite integer checker reproduces all \(14{,}632\) parameter cases in \(3\le a\le120\), \(0\le p\le2a\); an independently coded direct-count implementation checks \(3{,}374\) further small entries. The infinite result is proved by the displayed inequalities, not inferred from the experiments.

**Originality — PASS within inspected evidence.** Komatsu–Ying (arXiv:2206.13052) already prove the first range \(p\le\lfloor a/2\rfloor\) and explicitly ask for the regime beyond it. The second and third ranges, which cover all \(p\le2a\), are the new claim. Subwattanachai's 2023 divisibility theorem (arXiv:2309.09149) does not apply to consecutive triples. Searches for synonymous formulas and relevant prior claim indexes found no equivalent result; a broader unindexed derivation remains possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A complete explicit solution to a motivated published continuation problem for an infinite and canonical progression family supplies two structurally different regimes and a sharp parity-dependent final phase. The formulas are not extrapolated from small examples.

**Limitations.** No assertion is made beyond \(p=2a\) or for nonunit progression step; independent mathematical audit has not occurred. Novelty remains qualified by literature coverage.
