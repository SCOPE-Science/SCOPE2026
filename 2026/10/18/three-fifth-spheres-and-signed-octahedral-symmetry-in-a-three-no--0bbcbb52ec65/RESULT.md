# Three fifth-spheres and signed octahedral symmetry in a three-nonmatching complex

## Finding

Let \(O=K_{2,2,2}\) be the octahedral graph, with three parts of two vertices each. Its three-nonmatching complex \(K=\operatorname{NM}_3(O)\) consists of edge subsets whose matching number is at most two. Then

\[
K\simeq S^5\vee S^5\vee S^5.
\]

Moreover, the full automorphism group \(B_3=(C_2)^3\rtimes S_3\) acts on \(\widetilde H_5(K;\mathbb Q)\) as its three-dimensional signed coordinate representation \(U\). Writing an automorphism as a permutation \(p\in S_3\) of the three opposite-vertex pairs and a flip vector \(f=(f_1,f_2,f_3)\in\{0,1\}^3\), the character is

\[
\chi(g)=\sum_{j:p(j)=j}(-1)^{f_j}.
\]

Thus the fifth integral homology is free of rank three, all other reduced integral homology vanishes, and no nonzero rational class is fixed by every automorphism.

## Assumptions and scope

For a finite graph \(G\), \(\nu(G)\) denotes its largest matching size. On the vertex set \(E(G)\), define \(\operatorname{NM}_3(G)=\{F\subseteq E(G):\nu((V(G),F))\leq 2\}\). Homotopy equivalences use geometric realizations. Homology is reduced unless specified otherwise. The automorphism group acts simplicially by permuting the graph edges. The statement concerns exactly the six-vertex octahedral graph; it does not assert a classification for all multipartite graphs or all forbidden matching sizes.

## Proof

**1. The eight perfect matchings form a cube incidence structure.** Label the three parts \(\{A_0,A_1\}\), \(\{B_0,B_1\}\), and \(\{C_0,C_1\}\). Every size-three matching is a perfect matching, and all eight are given by

\[
M_{abc}=\{\{A_a,B_b\},\{B_{1-b},C_c\},\{C_{1-c},A_{1-a}\}\},\qquad(a,b,c)\in\{0,1\}^3.
\]

For each original edge, exactly two perfect matchings contain it. Their indices differ in exactly one coordinate. Consequently the twelve original edges correspond bijectively to the twelve edges of the cube \(Q_3\), with incidence to perfect matchings represented by the cube's edge endpoints. These statements also follow directly by considering the three possible part-pairs of an edge.

**2. Alexander duality converts the problem into the cube independence complex.** Let \(K^*\) be the combinatorial Alexander dual on its twelve graph-edge vertices. Since the eight perfect matchings are exactly the minimal nonfaces of \(K\), the complex \(K^*\) is the union of eight simplices \(E(O)\setminus M_{abc}\). All their nonempty intersections are simplices. Their nerve \(N\) has a face indexed by \(J\subseteq V(Q_3)\) precisely when some original edge avoids every matching in \(J\). By the incidence correspondence, this means an edge of \(Q_3\) has both endpoints outside \(J\), or equivalently that \(V(Q_3)\setminus J\) is not independent. Thus

\[
K^*\simeq N=\operatorname{Ind}(Q_3)^*,
\]

where the homotopy equivalence is the nerve lemma and the last dual is on the eight cube vertices. Combinatorial Alexander duality with integral coefficients, applied twice, now gives for every integer \(i\)

\[
\widetilde H_i(K;\mathbb Z)
\cong\widetilde H^{12-i-3}(K^*;\mathbb Z)
\cong\widetilde H^{9-i}(N;\mathbb Z)
\cong\widetilde H_{8-(9-i)-3}(\operatorname{Ind}(Q_3);\mathbb Z)
=\widetilde H_{i-4}(\operatorname{Ind}(Q_3);\mathbb Z).
\]

The last equality is an isomorphism of abelian groups; the integer Alexander-duality formula is valid in all dimensions with the usual reduced conventions.

**3. The cube independence complex is a three-circle wedge.** The cube has four even-parity vertices and four odd-parity vertices. Each parity class spans a full tetrahedron in the independence complex. An even vertex and odd vertex are nonadjacent in the cube only when they are antipodal; these give four isolated cross-edges and no mixed faces of dimension at least two. Collapsing the two contractible tetrahedra to points leaves two vertices joined by four arcs. It follows that

\[
\operatorname{Ind}(Q_3)\simeq\bigvee^3S^1.
\]

The duality calculation therefore gives \(\widetilde H_5(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^3\) and zero in every other reduced integral degree.

**4. The homology calculation determines the homotopy type.** The complex \(K\) has a complete one-skeleton: no set of two graph edges contains a three-edge matching. Its only missing triangles are the eight perfect matchings. If \(\{e_1,e_2,e_3\}\) is a perfect matching and \(d\) is any other graph edge, the three triangles \(\{d,e_1,e_2\}\), \(\{d,e_1,e_3\}\), and \(\{d,e_2,e_3\}\) lie in \(K\). Indeed, for two edges of a perfect matching, the unique original graph edge disjoint from both and joining their remaining endpoints is the third matching edge. These three triangles fill the boundary of each missing triangle. As every simplicial loop in a complete one-skeleton is generated by triangular loops, \(\pi_1(K)=0\). Since its first nonzero reduced integral homology is free in degree five, Hurewicz gives 4-connectivity and \(\pi_5(K)\cong\mathbb Z^3\). Choose three representatives for a homology basis. Their wedge map \(\bigvee^3S^5\to K\) is an integral homology equivalence between simply-connected CW spaces, hence a homotopy equivalence by Whitehead's theorem.

**5. Exact group action.** Let \(g\in B_3\) induce a permutation of the twelve edge vertices of \(K\), with cycles \(C_1,\ldots,C_r\). A simplex fixed setwise by \(g\) is a union of whole cycles. Its signed simplicial contribution to the reduced Lefschetz number is \((-1)^{|J|+1}\) for a union of cycle indices \(J\). Since only reduced degree-five homology survives, the fifth-homology trace is

\[
\chi_{\widetilde H_5}(g)=\sum_{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}:\,\bigcup_{j\in J}C_j\in K}(-1)^{|J|},
\]

including the empty face. The accompanying dependency-free checker enumerates all 48 automorphisms, computes these signed integer sums, and compares them with \(\sum_{j:p(j)=j}(-1)^{f_j}\); all 48 agree. Equality of finite-group characters over characteristic zero determines the rational representation. The signed coordinate representation is irreducible because the three distinct coordinate characters of \((C_2)^3\) are permuted transitively by \(S_3\). Their direct sum has no trivial summand.

## Relationship to prior work

Linusson–Shareshian–Welker established complete-graph and complete-bipartite nonmatching-complex wedge theorems and explicitly asked in their Question 1.16 for the homotopy type of general complete multipartite nonmatching complexes. The balanced tripartite octahedral graph has no singleton part and is not covered by their two-part theorem. Holmsen–Lee proved broad homological-vanishing results and discussed a singleton-part multipartite case, but not this exact homotopy classification. Shan–Wang's 2026 edge-addition homology theorem requires bipartiteness, which the octahedral graph fails because it contains triangles. The present calculation gives a small natural multipartite instance and its complete rational automorphism representation; it does not solve the general multipartite question.

## Limitations

This theorem applies to the single graph \(K_{2,2,2}\) and matching threshold three. It does not identify integral equivariant module extensions, characterize larger multipartite families, or resolve the general 2008 problem. Literature comparison cannot exclude unindexed or unpublished equivalent results. The included finite checker is a reproducibility aid, not an independent third-party audit.
