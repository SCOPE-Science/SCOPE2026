# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The forbidden triples of edges of \(K_{2,2,2}\) are exactly its eight perfect matchings. Each edge lies in exactly two of them, and their incidence graph is the three-cube. Integral Alexander duality and a simplicial nerve lemma reduce the reduced homology to that of the cube independence complex, two tetrahedra connected by four antipodal bridges, homotopy equivalent to three circles. Correct degree shifts yield only free reduced fifth homology of rank three. The complex has a complete one-skeleton; every missing triangular boundary has a three-triangle filling using any other graph edge, so it is simply connected. Hurewicz and Whitehead promote the integral homology calculation to a wedge of three fifth-spheres. An exhaustive, fully replayable certificate verifies all eight perfect matchings, all 256 dual-nerve incidences, 1,699 augmented faces, 847 acyclic discrete-Morse face pairs, independent binary boundary ranks, and signed character sums for all 48 automorphisms.

## Originality — PASS

The full 2008 Linusson–Shareshian–Welker text proves complete and bipartite cases but poses the complete-multipartite homotopy case as Question 1.16. The fully inspected Shan–Wang manuscript, publicly posted 2026-09-02, requires bipartiteness for its edge-addition homology theorem, whereas the octahedral graph has triangles. Holmsen–Lee's broad Leray estimates and singleton-part discussion do not supply the present exact homotopy and automorphism representation. Indexed literature searches for nonmatching aliases, three-part balanced parameters, the cube dual, octahedral symmetry, and higher matching reveal no equivalent statement; the nearest records are for ordinary matching complexes, graph Morse complexes, or different graphs. Exact tables and implication-based coverage were compared, not only paper titles. The caveat is the normal risk of unindexed or unpublished duplicate results.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a natural balanced three-partite instance of an explicitly documented complete-multipartite topology question, lying outside the known complete and bipartite theorems. The nontrivial integral sphere-wedge calculation and full irreducible symmetry character distinguish it mathematically from a numerical face-count or elementary special case. It is not claimed to settle the general multipartite question.
