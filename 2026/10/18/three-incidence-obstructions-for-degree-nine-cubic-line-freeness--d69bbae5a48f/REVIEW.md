# Review: three incidence obstructions for degree-nine cubic–line freeness

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem assumes smooth distinct cubic components, distinct lines, precisely the three specified singularity types, and freeness over the complex numbers. Talar's published necessary inequality, the minimal-Jacobian-degree bound, Bézout, the free Tjurina formula, and branch counts reduce the possibilities to exactly four numerical cases. Two one-cubic cases violate independently proved line-only/mixed-triple capacities; the two-cubic case with two triples contradicts the cubic–cubic Bézout count modulo three. Each inference and its domain is explicit in RESULT.md. CHECK.py reproduces the necessary integer arithmetic, but does not purport to decide geometric existence.

## Originality — PASS
Talar, *On cubic-line arrangements with simple singularities*, arXiv:2305.01530, Section 3, explicitly leaves the geometric realization of all four degree-nine types unresolved. This result excludes three via additional incidence arguments; the printed necessary numerical and orbifold inequalities alone do not imply these geometric contradictions. Dimca–Ilardi–Pokora–Sticlaru, arXiv:2301.01117, construct free examples of different degrees or singularity patterns, not this exclusion. Targeted searches of published research records and comparisons with accessible prior claims yielded no stronger covering result. An undiscovered equivalent nonrealizability theorem remains a bibliographic risk.

## Value — PASS
The theorem settles three specific natural open realizability cases in a small-degree classification, provides a precise structural obstruction for one-cubic arrangements, and isolates exactly one remaining numerical case without claiming its existence. It is a substantive strengthening of numerical weak-combinatorics bounds rather than a parameter substitution or a numerical micro-increment.

## Scientific limitations
Only necessary freeness conditions are proved. In particular, the two-cubic three-line case with three nodes and nine order-three contacts remains open as an existence question; there is no assertion of a complete classification of all arrangements or geometric realizations. Literature searches cannot guarantee absolute novelty.
