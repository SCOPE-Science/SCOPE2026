# Three isomorphism classes of maximal one-nodal elliptic curves over \(\mathbb F_{25}\)

## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbb F_{25}\). Among geometrically integral projective \(k\)-curves of geometric genus \(1\), arithmetic genus \(2\), and exactly \(37\) rational points, there are exactly **three \(k\)-isomorphism classes**. All have the same normalization
\[
E: y^2=x^3+1,
\]
and are obtained by identifying the two geometric points of a degree-two closed point of \(E\) into one rational non-split node. The \(270\) degree-two closed points of \(E\) form three orbits under \(\operatorname{Aut}_k(E)\), of sizes \(54,108,108\). The corresponding singular curves have automorphism groups \(C_2\times C_2\), \(C_2\), and \(C_2\), respectively.

Equivalently, fix either geometric branch \(R\in E(k_2)\setminus E(k)\), where \(k_2=\mathbb F_{625}\), of the chosen node and set \(\Delta=F(R)-R\), with \(F\) the \(25\)-power Frobenius. The three isomorphism types correspond bijectively to the three \(\mu_6\)-orbits in \(E[4]\setminus\{O\}\), under the action of origin-preserving automorphisms of \(E\). One orbit comprises all three nonzero points of \(E[2]\); the other two each comprise six order-four points. The automorphism-group order is \(4\) in the first orbit and \(2\) in either primitive orbit. The two primitive orbits remain distinct as isomorphism classes, despite the same automorphism-group order.

## Assumptions and scope
A curve means a reduced, projective, geometrically integral curve over \(k\). Isomorphisms and automorphisms are over \(k\), and no plane embedding is marked. Geometric genus is the genus of the smooth normalization, arithmetic genus is \(h^1(X,\mathcal O_X)\), and points are \(k\)-rational points. The number \(37=25+1+10+1\) is the Aubry--Perret upper bound for geometric genus one and arithmetic genus two. The theorem concerns **all** curves attaining it, not just a particular displayed projective model. A non-split node is rational over \(k\), with two geometric branches conjugate over \(k_2\).

## Proof
**1. Maximality forces a unique elliptic normalization and one gluing.** The equality case in Aubry--Iezzi's structural theorem (as reproduced in Hilario--Salomão--Martins, Proposition 2.2) says that a curve attaining the bound has maximal normalization and that every singular point is rational, of singularity degree one, with exactly one degree-two closed branch on the normalization. Since arithmetic minus geometric genus equals one, there is exactly one such singular point. Its local ring is \(k+\mathfrak m_P\subset\mathcal O_{E,P}\), for a degree-two closed point \(P\). Conversely, the Ferrand-style pinching specified by this local ring produces a geometrically integral projective nodal curve with arithmetic genus two and exactly \(\#E(k)+1\) rational points. The normalization and closed branch are intrinsically recoverable, so these pinched curves are isomorphic precisely when the degree-two points lie in the same \(k\)-automorphism orbit on their normalizations.

**2. There is only one maximal elliptic normalization up to \(k\)-isomorphism.** For the model \(E:y^2=x^3+1\) over \(\mathbb F_5\), direct counting gives six rational points. Thus its Frobenius \(\pi_5\) has trace zero and satisfies \(\pi_5^2=[-5]\); base change to \(k\) gives \(F=[-5]\) and \(\#E(k)=36\). Any maximal elliptic curve over \(k\) has Frobenius trace \(-10\), hence is supersingular. In characteristic five, the coefficient of \(x^4\) in \((x^3+ax+b)^2\), the Hasse invariant of a short Weierstrass equation, is \(2a\); supersingularity forces \(a=0\). Consequently every maximal elliptic curve has \(j=0\) and is a \(k\)-form \(y^2=x^3+b\). Because \(k^\times\) contains all sixth roots of unity, the six forms are twists indexed by \(\mu_6\). Their Frobenius endomorphisms are \([-5]\zeta\) with \(\zeta\in\mu_6\). Only \(\zeta=1\) has trace \(-10\) (as \(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}=2\) only there). Thus the displayed \(E\) is the unique maximal elliptic curve over \(k\), up to \(k\)-isomorphism.

**3. Degree-two points reduce to nonzero four-torsion.** Since \(F=[-5]\), its square is \([25]\). Therefore
\[
E(k)=\ker(F-1)=E[6],\qquad E(k_2)=\ker(F^2-1)=E[24].
\]
These have respectively \(36\) and \(576\) points, all prime-to-five torsion. There are \((576-36)/2=270\) closed degree-two points. A geometric branch \(R\) of such a point has difference \(\Delta=F(R)-R=[-6]R\in E[4]\setminus\{O\}\). The homomorphism \([-6]:E[24]\to E[4]\) is onto with kernel \(E[6]\) of size \(36\); hence every nonzero four-torsion difference occurs. Changing the branch replaces \(\Delta\) by \(-\Delta\).

**4. The orbit invariant is complete.** Every \(k\)-automorphism of \(E\) has the form \(R\mapsto uR+t\), with \(u\in\mu_6\) and \(t\in E[6]\). It transforms the difference \(\Delta\) into \(u\Delta\). If two differences become equal, or negatives, after such multiplication, select the appropriate conjugate branches \(R,S\). Then \(t=S-uR\) satisfies \(F(t)-t=(F(S)-S)-u(F(R)-R)=0\), so \(t\in E(k)\), giving a \(k\)-automorphism carrying the two closed points to each other. As \(-1\in\mu_6\), the three orbit types are exactly those of \(\mu_6\) acting on \(E[4]\setminus\{O\}\).

**5. Exactly three orbits.** The three nonidentity points of \(E[2]\) have the form \((r,0)\), \(r^3=-1\); multiplication by a primitive cube root of unity cyclically permutes them, so they form one orbit of size three. There are twelve points of exact order four. The \(\mu_6\)-action on them is free: \(-1\) fixes only two-torsion, primitive cube roots fix no nonzero four-torsion because \((\omega-1)(\omega^2-1)=[3]\), and primitive sixth roots fix no nonzero four-torsion because their difference from \(1\) is a unit in \(\mathbb Z[\omega]\). Thus the twelve primitive points form two orbits of size six. Every \(\Delta\) has \(36\) branch-point lifts; quotienting by the two possible choices of branch gives closed-point orbit sizes \(36\cdot3/2=54\) and \(36\cdot6/2=108\), twice.

**6. Full automorphism groups.** Since normalization is canonical, an automorphism of each nodal curve lifts uniquely to an automorphism of \(E\) preserving the associated closed degree-two point, and conversely every such stabilizer descends through the local subring \(k+\mathfrak m_P\). As \(|\operatorname{Aut}_k(E)|=36\cdot6=216\), orbit--stabilizer gives orders \(4,2,2\). In the order-four case \(\Delta\in E[2]\setminus\{O\}\); translation by \(\Delta\) and the reflection \(R\mapsto(R_0+F(R_0))-R\) are distinct commuting involutions preserving the pair \(\{R_0,F(R_0)\}\). Hence the stabilizer is \(C_2\times C_2\); the order-two stabilizers are necessarily \(C_2\).

## Verification
A standalone pure-Python script `verify.py` exhaustively checks the finite algebraic orbit computations on \((\mathbb Z/24\mathbb Z)^2\), with multiplication by a primitive cube root represented by \((a,b)\mapsto(-b,a-b)\). It computes six points on \(E(\mathbb F_5)\), \(270\) degree-two closed points, a \(36\)-element translation subgroup, all \(216\) affine automorphisms, the orbit sizes \((54,108,108)\), and stabilizer orders \((4,2,2)\). Its terminal output is `VERIFY_OK`. The reconstruction of Frobenius and the geometric classification are proved above, not inferred from those finite checks.

## Relationship to prior work
The singular-point structure, local pinching ring and maximality criterion are established in Aubry--Iezzi, *On the maximum number of rational points on singular curves over finite fields* (2015), Proposition 5.2, as recalled and used in Hilario--Salomão--Martins (2026), Proposition 2.2 and Lemma 2.6. The recent paper's Theorem 3.4 and Example 3.7 explicitly construct a maximal arithmetic-genus-two nodal curve over \(\mathbb F_{25}\) normalized by the very elliptic curve \(y^2=x^3+1\). The complete count of three isomorphism classes, the global classification of all \(270\) possible degree-two gluings and the complete stabilizer group types are not supplied by those statements. The latter computations use standard elliptic torsion and automorphism theory as inputs rather than claiming these general tools as new.

## Limitations
This is a classification over \(\mathbb F_{25}\) only, restricted to geometric genus one and arithmetic genus two; it neither classifies smooth genus-two curves nor maximal curves of geometric genus zero. The two size-\(108\) orbits are distinguished by the four-torsion orbit invariant, not by automorphism-group order alone. It provides abstract isomorphism types, not explicit equations for representatives in projective space. The structural input on singular maximal curves and elliptic twist classification are imported established theorems. An equivalent orbit computation could exist in literature not located by the searches, and independent audit has not been conducted.
