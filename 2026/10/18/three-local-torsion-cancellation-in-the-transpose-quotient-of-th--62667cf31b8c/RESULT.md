# Three-local torsion cancellation in the transpose quotient of the five-by-five chessboard complex

## Finding
Let \(X=\mathcal M(K_{5,5})\) be the ordinary chessboard complex: its vertices are the 25 board cells \((i,j)\), for \(0\le i,j<5\), and a set of cells is a simplex precisely when no two have a row or column in common. Let \(\tau(i,j)=(j,i)\). Let \(Y=|X|/\langle\tau\rangle\) denote the **ordinary topological orbit space**, not a homotopy-orbit space. Then:

1. \(\tau_*\) acts by \(-1\) on the nonzero integral torsion group \(\widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/3\).
2. \(\tau_*\) has trace zero on the free rank-56 group \(\widetilde H_3(X;\mathbb Z)\), hence its \(+1\) and \(-1\) eigenspaces over \(\mathbb Z_{(3)}\) each have rank 28.
3. The ordinary quotient has \(\pi_1(Y)=0\) and \(\widetilde H_q(Y;\mathbb Z_{(3)})\cong\mathbb Z_{(3)}^{28}\) for \(q=3\), and zero for every other degree. Consequently its Bousfield–Kan localization at the prime three satisfies
\[
Y_{(3)}\simeq\bigvee^{28}(S^3)_{(3)}.
\]

The induced character of the full board symmetry group \((S_5\times S_5)\rtimes C_2\) on \(\widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\) is \(\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)(-1)^e\), where \(e=0\) for a side-preserving operation \((\sigma,\pi)\) and \(e=1\) when followed by transposition. The side-preserving factor was established in earlier work; the new input is the transpose factor and the resulting quotient topology.

## Assumptions and scope
All homology is simplicial or singular homology with the indicated coefficients. The symbol \(\mathbb Z_{(3)}\) denotes integers localized at three, in which two is invertible; space localization is the simply connected three-local homotopy localization. The result concerns precisely this five-by-five chessboard and the order-two transposition quotient. No claim is made for other board sizes or for integral homotopy type of \(Y\).

We use the established chessboard homology and connectivity results of Shareshian–Wachs: \(X\) is simply connected and \(\widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/3\), generated by the explicit cycle below. The existence and nontriviality of this torsion class are **prior results**, not new deductions. Jonsson independently supplied a computer-free proof of the same torsion calculation.

## Proof
**The integral chain and its transpose.** Write \(v_{ij}\) for the oriented zero-simplex at board cell \((i,j)\). Define zero-cycles \(\alpha_{i;jk}=v_{ij}-v_{ik}\) and \(\beta_{ij;k}=v_{ik}-v_{jk}\). Wedge means the oriented simplicial join-product of chains supported on disjoint rows and columns. The explicit generator given in Shareshian–Wachs, Theorem 5.4, specializes (with indices shifted to start at zero) to
\[
z=\alpha_{0;01}\wedge\alpha_{1;23}\wedge\beta_{23;4}\in C_2(X;\mathbb Z).
\]
Each factor is a reduced zero-cycle; therefore \(\partial z=0\). The cited theorem proves that its integral class generates \(\mathbb Z/3\).

There is an explicit 60-term oriented three-chain \(w\), with coefficients in \(\mathbb F_3\), satisfying
\[
\partial w=z+\tau_*(z)\pmod3.
\]
Its coefficients, indexed by lexicographically ordered four-rook simplices, are in `certificate.json`, under `boundary_chain`. The companion 184-term two-cocycle \(\varphi\), indexed by lexicographically ordered three-rook simplices, satisfies \(\delta\varphi=0\) and \(\langle\varphi,z\rangle=1\pmod3\). All identities are checked from the actual simplex boundaries by `verify.py`, not inferred from a log. The exact modular boundary ranks \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_3}\partial_2=176\) and \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_3}\partial_3=423\) independently give \(\dim H_2(X;\mathbb F_3)=600-176-423=1\).

The universal coefficient exact sequence injects \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\otimes\mathbb F_3\) into \(H_2(X;\mathbb F_3)\); it is an isomorphism because \(H_1(X;\mathbb Z)=0\). Thus the displayed modular boundary identity implies \(\tau_*[z]=-[z]\) already **integrally**, on the unique three-torsion summand. The source's row and column sign relations, also checked on generators by the cocycle pairing, determine the whole \((S_5\times S_5)\rtimes C_2\) torsion character.

**Integral degree-three freeness.** The simplex counts by size one through five are \((25,200,600,600,120)\). Every four-rook placement has exactly one completion to a five-rook placement, so each three-simplex belongs to exactly one four-simplex. The matrix \(\partial_4\) has a distinguished signed identity minor of order 120. This makes \(\partial_4\) injective and its image a direct summand of the free group \(C_3(X;\mathbb Z)\); hence \(H_3(X;\mathbb Z)\) is free. The reduced Euler characteristic is \(-56\), and by the cited connectivity and torsion the only nonzero rational reduced homology is \(H_3(X;\mathbb Q)\). Consequently \(H_3(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{56}\) and all higher reduced homology vanishes.

**Trace of the transpose.** A \(\tau\)-stable matching of board cells is a union of fixed diagonal cells and paired off-diagonal cells \(\{(i,j),(j,i)\}\), with pairwise disjoint labels. Signed simplicial Lefschetz enumeration weights each chosen orbit by \(-1\). Denote the resulting signed sum over configurations on \(n\) labels by \(F_n\). Optional inclusion or exclusion of a diagonal cell cancels all configurations in which that label is not paired; the remaining configurations give \(F_n=-(n-1)F_{n-2}\), with \(F_0=1,F_1=0\). Therefore \(F_5=0\). The reduced Lefschetz number is \(-F_5=0\); since rational reduced homology is concentrated in odd degree three, its \(\tau\)-trace is zero. Since \(\tau^2=1\), the two rational eigenspaces have dimensions \((56\pm0)/2=28\). The same statement holds for rank over \(\mathbb Z_{(3)}\) by the idempotents \((1\pm\tau)/2\).

**The ordinary orbit space.** Passing to barycentric subdivision makes the order-two simplicial action without inversions, so the cellular chain complex of \(Y\) over \(\mathbb Z_{(3)}\) is the coinvariant complex of the subdivided chains of \(X\). As two is invertible, taking coinvariants is exact. Thus \(H_q(Y;\mathbb Z_{(3)})\cong H_q(X;\mathbb Z_{(3)})_{\langle\tau\rangle}\). The degree-two torsion disappears because transposition acts as \(-1\); degree three contributes precisely its 28-dimensional \(+1\)-eigensummand. There are no other positive-degree three-local homology groups.

The chessboard connectivity theorem makes \(X\) simply connected. Transposition fixes every diagonal vertex \((i,i)\); therefore Armstrong's orbit-space fundamental group theorem gives \(\pi_1(Y)=0\). For a simply connected finite CW complex with three-local reduced homology free and concentrated in degree three, the three-local Hurewicz and homological Whitehead theorems identify the three-localization with the wedge of 28 three-local three-spheres. This proves the claim.

Its output must begin `VERIFIED faces=(25,200,600,600,120)`.

The proof's noncomputational dependencies are Shareshian–Wachs's integral torsion generator and connectivity theorem, Armstrong's orbit fundamental-group theorem, and standard localization/Whitehead results. These are mathematical premises and are not claimed to be re-proved by `verify.py`.

## Relationship to prior work
Shareshian and Wachs (first publicly circulated in 2004) prove the integral three-torsion and its generator, and derive the separate signs for row and column permutations (Theorem 5.4, equations 5.8–5.10). Those signs alone do not determine the order-two **interchange** character, because the side-preserving sign module has two possible extensions to the full board automorphism group. Jonsson's proof of the order-three torsion uses a non-transpose-invariant auxiliary subcomplex; it likewise does not settle the interchange action. Friedman and Hanlon determine ordinary Betti numbers and rational representation information, which cannot determine this torsion action. The distinguishing new fact here is that diagonal transposition reverses the torsion generator, and, together with Lefschetz and orbit-space arguments, removes its three-primary obstruction in the ordinary quotient.

## Limitations
The homotopy conclusion is three-local, not integral. The quotient may possess two-primary phenomena not addressed here. The explicit 60-term boundary is a finite computational certificate for a specific equality, not a general formula for larger chessboards. We make no independence or external-certification claim; the novelty comparison is based on the specified inspected sources and targeted indexed searches, with possible unindexed equivalents remaining a risk.
