# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The integral generator and simple connectivity are precisely the premises of Shareshian–Wachs, Theorems 5.4 and 1.1 (arXiv:math/0409054). The standalone, executable mod-three boundary and cocycle witnesses establish transpose sign on that generator; the universal coefficient injection transports the sign to integral homology. A signed fixed-simplex argument proves rational Lefschetz trace zero. The 120 distinct codimension-one free faces prove degree-three freeness. Exactness of coinvariants away from two, Armstrong's quotient fundamental-group theorem, and the local Whitehead theorem give the 28-sphere three-local conclusion. No finite check is used as a surrogate for the cited general theorems.

**Originality — PASS (best-knowledge comparison).** Shareshian–Wachs already provide integral three-torsion and separate row/column sign actions; these prior facts are not relabeled as new. Their full theorem does not determine the action of interchanging the board parts. Jonsson gives another torsion proof through an asymmetric subcomplex. Friedman–Hanlon's rational representations cannot decide an automorphism's sign on integral three-torsion. Indexed searches did not identify a theorem concerning the transpose quotient or its three-local homotopy type. A hidden equivalent treatment remains a bibliographic risk, not a demonstrated implication.

**Value — PASS.** The ordinary quotient of a standard torsion-bearing complex has three-local homotopy equivalent to a **torsion-free wedge of 28 three-spheres**. This is a natural topological effect of a geometric duality; it distinguishes actions identical on rational homology and explains how three-primary torsion can disappear under a quotient whose order is prime to three. The computation is specific to this canonical square board, not an arbitrary parametrized example. Neither the known ordinary Betti numbers nor the published side-preserving sign formula forces its transpose character.

Closest literature: Shareshian–Wachs (2004 arXiv), Theorems 1.1 and 5.4; Jonsson (2012 arXiv), Theorem 3.1; Friedman–Hanlon (1998); Armstrong (1965). Limits: the result is three-local, not an integral quotient classification; unindexed equivalent work may exist, and a separate scientific audit remains outstanding.
