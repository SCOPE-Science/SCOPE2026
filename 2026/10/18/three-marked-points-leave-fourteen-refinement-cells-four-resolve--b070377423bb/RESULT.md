# Three marked points leave fourteen refinement cells; four resolve prime projective planes

## Finding
Let \(\Pi\) be a finite projective plane of order \(q\ge 2\), and let \(G(\Pi)\) be its point-line incidence graph, with the point and line sides initially distinguished. Give three noncollinear points distinct individual colours; all other points retain one common point colour and all lines retain one common line colour. Ordinary one-dimensional colour refinement has **exactly fourteen stable colour classes, reached after two refinement rounds**, with the following seven classes on each side:

* point side: the three marked points (one each), the unmarked points on each of the three joining sides (\(q-1\) each), and the points off all three sides (\((q-1)^2\));
* line side: the three joining lines (one each), the other lines through each marked point (\(q-1\) each), and the lines through none of the marked points (\((q-1)^2\)).

The quotient colour-multiplicity profile and every interclass neighbour count depend **only on \(q\)**, not on the isomorphism type of \(\Pi\). Consequently any two order-\(q\) projective planes with named noncollinear point triples have \(C^2\)-equivalent coloured incidence graphs. In particular, an order-nine Desarguesian plane and a non-Desarguesian plane are still indistinguishable by \(C^2\) after independently naming three noncollinear points.

For a prime \(p\), the **minimum number of individual point colours needed to make colour refinement discrete** on the point-line incidence graph of \(\operatorname{PG}(2,p)\) (with the point/line sorts retained) is
\[
\boxed{3\text{ if }p=2,\qquad 4\text{ if }p\ge3.}
\]
For every prime \(p\), four points in general position (no three collinear) suffice; when \(p=2\), three noncollinear points already suffice. The assertion concerns a *discrete refinement partition of the fixed coloured graph*, not identification of an unmarked plane or a metric resolving set.

## Assumptions and scope
A finite projective plane of order \(q\) has \(q^2+q+1\) points and as many lines, \(q+1\) incidences at each point and line, a unique joining line for two distinct points, and a unique intersection point for two distinct lines. Its incidence graph is bipartite, with one vertex for each point and line and an edge exactly for incidence. The graph is initially two-coloured by its point/line sorts. Individually naming a point assigns it a colour not used at any other vertex, distinct from all other named colours.

Refinement starts from these initial colours and replaces each vertex colour simultaneously by its preceding colour together with the multiset of its neighbours' preceding colours. A class is *stable* if no further round splits it. A discrete partition consists of singleton classes. The prime-field statement only concerns primes (not arbitrary prime powers). The first statement needs no Desarguesian assumption.

## Proof
**1. The fourteen classes.** Fix noncollinear marked points \(A,B,C\), and call their joining lines \(a=BC\), \(b=CA\), \(c=AB\). At the first refinement round, lines are distinguished by which of \(A,B,C\) they contain. There are exactly seven possibilities: \(a,b,c\), the three distinct sets of lines through exactly one of \(A,B,C\), and the lines through none. The corresponding sizes are respectively \(1,1,1,q-1,q-1,q-1,(q-1)^2\). At the second round, unmarked points split according to which of \(a,b,c\) they lie on, and whether they lie on none. Since the three sides meet only at their named vertices, the seven point cells have sizes \(1,1,1,q-1,q-1,q-1,(q-1)^2\).

These fourteen classes are equitable. Every marked point lies on two side lines and \(q-1\) further lines through it. A point in the interior of side \(BC\) lies on \(a\), on exactly one nonside line through \(A\), and on exactly \(q-1\) lines through no marked point; the corresponding statements hold cyclically. An off-side point lies on exactly one line through each of \(A,B,C\), plus \(q-2\) lines through none. Dually, each side line contains its two endpoints and \(q-1\) side-interior points; a nonside line through \(A\) contains \(A\), precisely one point in the interior of \(BC\), and \(q-1\) off-side points. A line with no marked point meets each side in one distinct side-interior point and contains \(q-2\) off-side points. All neighbour counts depend only on \(q\). Thus no further split occurs. The descriptions are genuinely distinct after the indicated rounds (side-line incidence colours distinguish the seven point cells), proving exactness, including \(q=2\), when all fourteen cells are singletons. The uniform equitable partition on disjoint unions of any two such marked planes, and the standard equivalence of colour refinement with \(C^2\), prove the logical indistinguishability assertion.

**2. Why three points cannot suffice for prime \(p\ge3\).** Three noncollinear marks leave cells of size \(p-1\ge2\), by Part 1. If at most three marked points are collinear, take that line as the line at infinity, with homogeneous coordinates \(z=0\). The nonidentity projectivity \((x:y:z)\mapsto(x+z:y:z)\) fixes the entire line pointwise but moves affine points. Therefore the marked incidence graph has a nontrivial colour-preserving automorphism, preventing a discrete refinement partition. For fewer than three marks, extend them to three collinear marks; the same argument applies. Hence four marks are necessary when \(p\ge3\). For \(p=2\), at most two marks similarly leave a nontrivial elation, while three noncollinear marks give fourteen singleton classes.

**3. Four points suffice over a prime field.** In \(\operatorname{PG}(2,p)\), the projective collineation group sends any ordered quadrangle to the standard frame
\[
Q=(1:0:0),\quad R=(0:1:0),\quad O=(0:0:1),\quad U=(1:1:1).
\]
Whenever two point vertices are uniquely coloured, their unique common neighbouring line is uniquely coloured after the next refinement round. Dually, two uniquely coloured lines determine their unique common neighbouring point after the next round. Hence all vertices produced by finitely many successive *joins* of distinct known points and *meets* of distinct known lines eventually receive singleton colours.

For completeness, the frame generates the whole prime projective plane by these operations. Use affine coordinates \((x,y)=(x:y:1)\); the line at infinity \(QR\), horizontal direction \(Q\), vertical direction \(R\), and slope-one direction \(S=OU\cap QR\) are constructible. The lines \(RU\), \(QU\), \(QO\), \(RO\) construct \(X=(1,0)\) and \(Y=(0,1)\). Starting with \((a,0)\), join it to \(S\) and meet that line with the known horizontal line through \(U\) at \((a+1,1)\); then join this point to \(R\) and meet with the known horizontal axis \(QO\) to construct \((a+1,0)\). Repetition constructs \((a,0)\) for every \(a\in\mathbf F_p\), because \(1\) additively generates the prime field. The point \(D=XY\cap QR\) represents direction slope minus one. The line through \((a,0)\) in direction \(D\) meets the vertical axis at \((0,a)\). Thus every \((a,b)\) is the intersection of the vertical through \((a,0)\) with the horizontal through \((0,b)\). Joining affine points yields every projective line, and intersecting their directions with \(QR\) yields all points at infinity. Therefore every point and line becomes uniquely coloured after finitely many refinement rounds; the stable partition is discrete.

## Verification
`verify_planes.py` constructs the complete point-line incidence graph over each prime \(p\in\{2,3,5,7,11,13\}\) and carries out exact, deterministic multiset refinement. It checks the exact fourteen-class, two-round profile for three noncollinear marks and the discreteness of four-frame refinement. `verify_closure.py`, independently coded around normalized projective cross products, repeatedly joins and meets every known pair and verifies generation of all points and lines from a standard quadrangle over the same six prime fields. Run both programs with Python 3 from this directory. These are finite computational checks supporting, but not replacing, the all-prime and arbitrary-projective-plane proofs above.

## Relationship to prior work
Héger and Takáts (2012) determine the metric dimension of finite projective-plane incidence graphs for sufficiently large order, namely \(4q-4\) when \(q\ge23\). Metric resolving sets use only distance vectors and are not the same as iterative colour-refinement individualization: the present exact value four for prime \(p\ge23\) is consistent with, and contrasts with, their growing metric bound. Arvind and collaborators (2015, 2016) give the general framework for colour refinement and vertex individualization, but not the fourteen-cell triangle profile or this prime-plane point-individualization threshold in the inspected sections. Chen, Ren, and Ponomarenko (2023) prove a stronger *unmarked* three-dimensional Weisfeiler–Leman equivalence for incidence graphs of planes of the same order and study one-point coherent extensions; their results do not explicitly give the present exact three-named-point one-dimensional partition or the sharp four-point prime-field threshold. Earlier projective-coordinate constructions and non-Desarguesian planes are classical. The present claim is their specific refinement-theoretic synthesis and quantitative landmark boundary, not novelty of those constituent facts.

## Limitations
The four-point sufficiency is proved only for Desarguesian planes over a prime field. A quadrangle in \(\operatorname{PG}(2,p^r)\) with \(r>1\) first generates the prime subplane, and no universal four-point conclusion for general prime powers or non-Desarguesian planes is claimed. The fourteen-cell result assumes three pairwise distinct **noncollinear** marked point vertices and a precoloured point/line bipartition. The equivalence assertion concerns structures with corresponding named noncollinear triples, not arbitrary inconsistent marker placements. The historical priority comparison is not a proof that no equivalent specialized theorem appears elsewhere. Independent audit has not been performed.
