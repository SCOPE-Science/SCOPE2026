# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The six vertices have projected heights \(\pm U_i\), and their twelve edges are exactly pairs from distinct axes. For \(M\) positive projected heights above an offset plane, exactly \(M(5-M)\) edges cross it; the three generic outcomes are thereby complete. Coordinate conditioning on circles verifies the first- and second-order overlap derivatives and the three-way overlap, with exact boundary values. Direct differentiation verifies the strict one-maximum result. The included reproducible edge enumerator agrees at eight offsets, but finite testing is not used as proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Liu–Tkocz (2019) optimize cross-polytope noncentral section volumes; Brandenburg–De Loera–Meroni (2023) classify hyperplane-section types. A published exact *width* distribution already supplies the empty-section probability and certain spherical-lens evaluations, so those facts are not asserted new. Width is a largest-coordinate statistic and does not itself contain the full threshold-exceedance count. The newly combined three-way overlap, complete offset-dependent probability distribution of polygon types, and unique radical maximizing offset go beyond the checked statements. The nearby literature on random planar *shadows* is inapplicable to plane *sections*. An earlier unlocated equivalent stereological result remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The family parametrizes a canonical geometric sampling problem by normalized distance. Its full distribution has two sharp offset transitions, and the probability of the natural quadrilateral outcome reaches an exact, unexpected interior maximum with a unique radical location and value exceeding 90%. This is a complete natural distribution/optimization, not an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Limitations
The claim is restricted to the three-dimensional regular octahedron. Existing cap and width formulas are credited, and overlap cannot be excluded exhaustively from all classical integral-geometry sources. The deterministic numerical tests support but do not prove the theorem, whose proof is analytic.
