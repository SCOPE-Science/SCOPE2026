# Review of the exact three-run prefix-normal enumerator

## Correctness

**PASS.** The all-length claim is proved from an elementary exact gap-window equivalence. The two exceptional gaps occur after the first and second one-runs; the proof treats all possible window sizes and boundary equalities. Disjoint enumeration of the two admissible parameter regions produces the bivariate rational function, while pole orders give the asymptotic constant. The packaged integer-coefficient checker covers the first 26 total lengths with all one-counts and independently checks direct word-level prefix normality on the specified finite ranges. These computations are corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Originality

**PASS against inspected evidence; residual search risk.** The closest direct precedent is Burcsi et al. (arXiv:1611.09017, 2016), whose Lemma 8 already establishes the general membership inequalities for the distances between ones. This known result is explicitly not claimed as new. Their Proposition 16 enumerates words of fixed one-count (up to six) and states rationality with that parameter fixed; it does not give a generating function with all one-counts for fixed three-run shape or the \(7/24\) limiting proportion. Their 2014 generation and exposition papers describe algorithms and membership tests rather than this census. Searches using the exact three-run criterion, generating function, run-count aliases and coefficient sequence found no equivalent published enumeration. Such search cannot rule out an unindexed formula.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The result gives a natural complete classification for the first three-run regime where the two inter-run zero gaps interact, with an explicit joint length/composition count across unbounded one-counts. The limiting fraction \(7/24\) is a nontrivial sharp asymptotic invariant relative to the natural run-conditioned sample space, not a recomputation of published fixed-density tables. Both the exact algebraic count and its volume-asymptotic interpretation are useful run-sensitive benchmarks in binary jumbled matching and prefix-normal enumeration.

## Closest literature and limitations

The general gap criterion is not novel; the enumeration requires additional two-case run analysis and rational summation. The finding does not address arbitrary numbers of one-runs and has not received independent expert examination. No absolute novelty guarantee is asserted.
