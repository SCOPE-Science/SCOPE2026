**Correctness — PASS.** The Dirichlet concentration score is exactly the centered log-product, so differentiation gives the covariance. The beta score's strictly concave single-hump shape and zero integral prove the one-crossing property. A justified endpoint-subtracted incomplete-beta expansion, a digamma maximum squeeze, and a beta-to-gamma score limit prove the three distinct asymptotic regimes. The attached standalone test independently compares quadrature, incomplete-beta derivatives, root signs, and limit formulas at 139 finite points; the proof does not rely on numerical enumeration.

**Originality — PASS for the combined three-scale theorem, subject to implicit-priority risk.** The central uniqueness of a finite-parameter beta CDF crossing is covered in broader form by Portnoy–Torrado–Veerman (2023, Theorem 4.3) and is **not** claimed alone as a new result. The assessed claim is the *derivative-covariance* amplitude and its sharp sparse-, concentrated-, and large-dimensional scaling limits. Bose–Dasgupta–Maulik (2008) cover Dirichlet/gamma maxima; Griffiths–Spanò (2010) analyze Dirichlet canonical correlations; Arab–Oliveira–Wiklund (2020) analyze beta distribution orders. Hjelle (2025) studies threshold expectations for a different, fixed-total-mass asymptotic. None of the inspected material directly states the complete claimed sensitivity interpolation. It may still be implicit in broader beta/gamma concentration and threshold-count formulas, so historical priority remains uncertain.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The result computes the response of a natural sparsity measure—the expected number of Dirichlet categories above a common threshold—to a prior's concentration. The same explicitly characterized sign boundary moves from half-mass to the uniform mean-mass scale and to a distinct gamma-scale constant when dimension increases. In contrast to the previously known pairwise CDF bunching statement, it gives nontrivial limiting covariance amplitudes and a fixed-shape gamma score asymptotic directly relevant to prior-sensitivity comparisons.

**Scope and remaining risks.** The shape coordinate is common across categories; monotonic movement of the crossover with shape is not established. The distinction between fixed shape and fixed total mass is essential. Some earlier general concentration-order or sensitivity framework could yield the reported limits implicitly.
