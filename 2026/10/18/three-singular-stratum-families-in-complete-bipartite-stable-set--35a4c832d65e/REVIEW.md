# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The projective normal fan is specified in the full standard lattice. The complete facet list is proved from the stable-set cube join, and all active facet normals identify with a rooted graph-incidence submatrix after a sign change. Forest subsets are unimodular; every cyclic active subgraph forces containment in one of exactly three explicit face families. This establishes completeness, not merely example counts. Generic transverse semigroups are computed directly; the binary-slice semigroup yields a Segre cone of \((\mathbb P^1)^q\) with Hilbert function \((a+1)^q\), not a rank-one \(2\times q\) determinantal cone when \(q>2\). The square cone is an ordinary double point. Exhaustive exact face and semigroup checks for small instances corroborate the proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The general projective Hibi smoothness criterion of Wagner (1996) covers the setting and is explicitly treated as prior art. The affine Hibi singular-locus framework of Lakshmibai--Mukherjee (arXiv:0706.2686) and the order-polytope identification of Friedl (arXiv:1506.02407) are also prior art. The claim here is a separate worked all-parameter classification: an exact number of maximal singular orbit closures in three non-equidimensional families together with two Segre-cone transverse types and a conifold type. Accessible primary full texts did not contain this explicit specialization. The direct full article of Wagner was not available, leaving a material risk that it already includes an equivalent worked example. Searches and comparisons are recorded in AUDIT.json; failure to locate wording is not used as proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Complete-bipartite stable-set polytopes are a natural, symmetric family, simultaneously interpretable as order polytopes of two-level posets. The explicit singular-component count and generic multiplicity jump from conifolds to high-dimensional Segre cones resolve the singular geometry of the entire two-parameter family rather than an arbitrary sample or minor normalization exercise. The generic multiplicity \(q!\) is a meaningful invariant of the transverse Segre cone, and the component dimensions exhibit precisely when singular support is nonpure.
