# Three-sphere homotopy types of rook-graph dominance complexes

## Finding
For every pair of integers \(p,q\ge 2\), let \(R_{p,q}=K_p\mathbin{\square}K_q\) be the rook graph of a rectangular board, and let \(\mathcal D(R_{p,q})\) be the simplicial complex of vertex subsets whose complements dominate the graph. There is an integral homotopy equivalence
\[
\boxed{\mathcal D(R_{p,q})\simeq S^{pq-p-1}\vee S^{pq-q-1}\vee S^{pq-p-q+1}.}
\]
Repeated dimensions mean distinct wedge summands. In particular, \(\mathcal D(R_{4,4})\simeq S^9\vee S^{11}\vee S^{11}\); for every permitted board the reduced integral homology is free, with one generator per displayed sphere.

## Assumptions and scope
The vertex set is \(V=[p]\times[q]\). Distinct vertices are adjacent exactly when they share a row or a column. A set \(T\subseteq V\) dominates if every vertex is in \(T\) or shares a row or a column with a vertex of \(T\). Thus \(\sigma\in\mathcal D(R_{p,q})\) precisely when \(V\setminus\sigma\) dominates. All complexes are finite abstract simplicial complexes, and homotopy equivalences refer to their geometric realizations. The theorem covers all integers \(p,q\ge2\), including the two-row and square boundaries, but makes no assertion for a one-row board.

## Proof
**1. A row–column cover.** A subset \(T\) dominates the rook graph if and only if every row meets \(T\), or every column meets \(T\). Indeed, if an empty row and an empty column both exist, their intersection is not dominated. Conversely, if every row meets \(T\), any board square is dominated by a square in its own row, and similarly for columns. Let \(A\) be the complex of \(\sigma\) containing no entire row and \(B\) the complex containing no entire column. Then
\[\mathcal D(R_{p,q})=A\cup B.\]
As joins of simplex boundaries,
\[
A=(\partial\Delta^{q-1})^{*p}\cong S^{p(q-1)-1}=S^{pq-p-1},\qquad
B=(\partial\Delta^{p-1})^{*q}\cong S^{q(p-1)-1}=S^{pq-q-1}.
\]

**2. The intersection sphere.** Put \(K=A\cap B\) and \(N=pq\). For a complex on the fixed set \(V\), define its combinatorial Alexander dual by
\[K^\vee=\{\tau\subseteq V:V\setminus\tau\notin K\}.\]
Here \(K^\vee\) is the union of the \(p+q\) simplices on the complements of individual rows or individual columns. An intersection indexed by rows \(I\subseteq[p]\) and columns \(J\subseteq[q]\) is a nonempty simplex exactly when \(I\ne[p]\) and \(J\ne[q]\). The simplicial nerve of this good cover is therefore
\[\partial\Delta^{p-1}*\partial\Delta^{q-1}\cong S^{p+q-3}.\]
By the nerve lemma, \(K^\vee\simeq S^{p+q-3}\). Integral combinatorial Alexander duality gives
\[\widetilde H_i(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H^{N-i-3}(K^\vee;\mathbb Z),\]
so \(K\) has the reduced integral homology of exactly one sphere of dimension
\[d=N-p-q=(p-1)(q-1)-1.\]

We upgrade this homology calculation to homotopy. If \(p,q\ge3\), \(K\) has the complete one-skeleton: every forbidden row or column has at least three vertices. Any triangle absent from \(K\) must be a whole row (only possible if \(q=3\)) or a whole column (only possible if \(p=3\)). The boundary of such a triangle can be filled by coning to a vertex in another row, respectively another column: all three new triangles avoid containing a whole row or column. Every loop in the complete one-skeleton is generated by triangle boundaries; consequently \(\pi_1(K)=0\). Since \(d\ge3\) and the integral reduced homology is that of \(S^d\), the Hurewicz theorem and the homological Whitehead theorem imply \(K\simeq S^d\).

If \(p=2\), then \(B=(S^0)^{*q}\cong S^{q-1}\), and imposing the no-full-row condition removes precisely the interiors of its two disjoint opposite facets. The resulting complex \(K\) has realization a sphere with two disjoint open top-dimensional simplices removed, hence deforms onto \(S^{q-2}=S^d\). The case \(q=2\) is symmetric. These arguments also cover \(p=q=2\), when \(K\simeq S^0\). Thus for all permitted parameters
\[K\simeq S^{pq-p-q}.\]

**3. Homotopy pushout.** The inclusions \(K\hookrightarrow A\) and \(K\hookrightarrow B\) are null-homotopic: each target is a sphere of strictly larger dimension than \(d=pq-p-q\), so every map from \(S^d\) into it is null; for \(d=0\), both targets are connected. Simplicial inclusions are cofibrations, and the homotopy pushout of two null maps is \(A\vee B\vee\Sigma K\). Therefore
\[A\cup B\simeq S^{pq-p-1}\vee S^{pq-q-1}\vee S^{pq-p-q+1},\]
as claimed. This is a proof for every integer \(p,q\ge2\), not an inference from finite enumeration.

## Verification
The included standalone Python program `rook_dominance_verifier.py` tests every vertex subset for boards of sizes \((2,2),(2,3),(2,4),(2,5),(3,3),(3,4),(4,4)\). It independently compares the closed-neighborhood domination predicate against the row–column union predicate for each subset. It verifies the predicted reduced Euler characteristic for all seven boards and directly computes all reduced homology ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\) for the six boards with at most twelve vertices. The largest board has \(59{,}746\) nonempty faces and passes the Euler test; its mod-two boundary ranks are deliberately not computed. The accompanying `CHECK_OUTPUT.txt` records actual checks. These finite checks support, but do not replace, the all-parameter proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Matsushita's 2020 work establishes general connections between dominance-complex connectivity and vertex-cover numbers. Matsushita and Wakatsuki (first posted 2023) establish Alexander-dual and neighborhood-complex methods and determine several path-product examples. Mertens (first posted 2024) determines the domination polynomial of rectangular rook graphs, exploiting the fact that a dominating placement meets every row or every column. The present conclusion is a topological classification of their entire dominance complexes, obtained through an intersection-sphere argument and a homotopy pushout; a generating function for dominating sets alone does not establish these homotopy types. Other complexes associated with rook graphs, such as open-neighborhood and ordinary cut complexes, have distinct definitions and do not imply this claim.

## Limitations
The result is nonequivariant: it does not determine the actions of row and column permutations on homology. It does not classify generalized chess-piece attack graphs, and it does not assert that the complexes are PL wedges or that the stated equivalences are simplicial collapses. Literature searches cannot rule out an unindexed equivalent result. The finite checker computes mod-two ranks, not an independent integral homotopy certificate; the integral assertion follows from the proof above.
