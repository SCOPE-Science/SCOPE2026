# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The cost graph has only two zero-cost recurrent cycles, the constant configurations. The finite-temperature equilibrium is the unique Perron Markov measure of an explicit strictly positive transfer matrix. Its characteristic polynomial after the change \(\lambda=1+\varepsilon z\) has simple golden-ratio limiting roots; componentwise right and left Perron eigenvectors establish the irrational stationary weights. Direct transition asymptotics give a rescaled two-state Markov limit. Equal diagonal entries of every relevant two-state transfer matrix force exact stationary equibarycentricity, proving the minimality assertion. The exact algebra was independently checked using standard-library finite computations.

## Originality
PASS on currently inspected published statements, with residual risk. Chazottes–Gambaudo–Ugalde already solve general convergence and give an algorithm, as well as a four-symbol example with four irrationally weighted ground states; none of those is a published three-symbol/two-fixed-ground-state minimum-alphabet classification or kinetic statement. Baraviera–Leplaideur–Lopes already give the identical golden weights in a **non-locally-constant** three-symbol example; those coefficients are not claimed as new. Hedges addresses finite-temperature attainability, not the present minimal locally constant zero-temperature model. No directly equivalent statement was found in the targeted index and literature searches. A general theorem could implicitly yield the specific calculation, and unindexed examples might already contain it.

## Value
PASS. The theorem settles a sharp natural alphabet-size boundary for asymmetric selection between two constant minimizing phases under nearest-neighbor dynamics. It separates genuine finite-range asymmetry from a universal binary equibarycenter constraint, and supplies the exact rare-transition clock and asymptotic rates. The compact matrix illustrates a substantive phenomenon whose individual numerical golden coefficient is known in a different model, rather than claiming the matrix algebra alone to be a general theory.

## Closest literature and limitations
Hedges, arXiv:2604.10225, full current preprint: existence of potentials realizing prescribed ergodic states. Chazottes–Gambaudo–Ugalde, arXiv:0903.1212, Sections 1, 4, 6.1–6.3: general convergence/renormalization and four-symbol irrational example. Baraviera–Leplaideur–Lopes, arXiv:1004.2848, Sections 1–2: an identical golden-ratio selection weight via a nonlocal potential. The classification covers exactly the stated one-step setting; it does not establish priority against every possible symbolic recoding or implicit specialization of general matrix theory.
