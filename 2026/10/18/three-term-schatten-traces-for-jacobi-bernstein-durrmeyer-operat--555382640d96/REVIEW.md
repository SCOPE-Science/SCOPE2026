# Three-term Schatten traces for Jacobi Bernstein–Durrmeyer operators

## Correctness — PASS
The operator is positive and selfadjoint, and its degree-preserving polynomial action yields the exact product eigenvalues. The three-term trace formula follows from a lattice-centered logarithmic expansion, an exponentially decaying high-degree tail, Gaussian moments, and a shifted Euler–Maclaurin formula. Explicitly, the index shift \(\delta=(a+b+1)/2\) creates the exponent-independent constant \(-(a+b)/2\); the next coefficient is obtained from \(\delta^2+(a+b+2)t^2/2-t^4/6\). The proof controls the remainder uniformly over compact Jacobi-parameter and positive-power sets. A standalone checker reproduced 28 exact polynomial spectral identities, 19 exact trace identities, and 24 finite-asymptotic comparisons. The computations do not certify the infinite asymptotic.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Berdysheva–Jetter (2010), Section 3.2.2, establishes the Jacobi eigenvalue formula; Lara-Velasco–Pérez (2024), Section 2, gives the same eigenvalues and fixed-degree expansions. Those are prior results, not new discoveries. The distinct claim here is summation of the entire degree-dependent spectrum on its \(k\asymp\sqrt n\) scale to obtain a compact-uniform, three-term trace expansion for **all positive real powers and all Jacobi weight parameters**. Targeted published-literature and mathematical-research-record searches did not identify a stronger statement that contains it, nor an exact table of these coefficients. The indexed 2010 material was not obtained as a complete local full-text copy, and an unindexed equivalent asymptotic cannot be excluded. This is not a claim of a complete bibliographic census.

## Value — PASS
The power traces quantify the effective spectral dimension of the classical Durrmeyer approximation operator (ordinary trace, Hilbert–Schmidt energy, and other spectral moments). The parameter-independent leading Gaussian term is sharpened by a Jacobi-weight boundary correction that does not depend on the Schatten exponent and a fully explicit next coefficient. The result is a natural complete asymptotic classification across the full real Jacobi-parameter family; it is not a single arbitrary parameter example or a recomputation of the classical eigenvalue list. Its compact-uniform remainder supports quantitative comparisons across operator families.

## Source comparisons and limitations
The closest directly applicable result is the known Jacobi eigenvalue formula in Berdysheva–Jetter, *Journal of Approximation Theory* 162 (2010), 576–598, doi:10.1016/j.jat.2009.11.005. The accessible full HTML of Lara-Velasco–Pérez, *Computational and Applied Mathematics* 43 (2024), article 277, doi:10.1007/s40314-024-02796-2, states the eigenvalues and degree-fixed limits, but not the all-spectrum power-trace coefficients. Derriennic's foundational 1981 paper describes the original operator; its complete text was not reviewed. These access limitations and possible independent derivations remain bibliographic risks. The theorem does not cover Jacobi weights or Schatten exponents moving with degree, and the remainder constant is not optimized.
