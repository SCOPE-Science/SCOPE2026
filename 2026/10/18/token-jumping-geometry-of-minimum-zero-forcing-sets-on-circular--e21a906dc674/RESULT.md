# Token-jumping geometry of minimum zero-forcing sets on circular ladders

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge6\), the token-jumping reconfiguration graph \(\mathcal R_n\) of minimum ordinary zero-forcing sets of \(C_n\square K_2\) is **connected**. It has exactly \(13n\) vertices and \(48n\) edges. Its vertex-degree multiset is
\[
\{12^{\langle n\rangle},\ 8^{\langle 8n\rangle},\ 5^{\langle 4n\rangle}\},
\]
where the bracketed exponent indicates multiplicity, not ordinary exponentiation. More strongly, the symmetry-orbit quotient matrix is given below. Exactly \(4n\) vertices have odd degree.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite, simple and undirected. Put \(G_n=C_n\square K_2\) and write its two layers as \(u_i,v_i\) for \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), with rim edges \(u_i u_{i+1}\), \(v_i v_{i+1}\), and rungs \(u_i v_i\). A blue vertex with exactly one white neighbor may force that neighbor blue. An initial set is *zero forcing* if repeated legal forces color the entire graph blue; its minimum cardinality is \(Z(G_n)\). A vertex of \(\mathcal R_n\) is a minimum-cardinality zero-forcing set. Two such sets are adjacent exactly if their intersection has cardinality \(Z(G_n)-1\), i.e., one initially blue token may jump to an unoccupied vertex while maintaining a minimum zero-forcing set. All conclusions in this note assume \(n\ge6\). In particular the claim is about token jumping, not token sliding or skew forcing.

## Proof

**1. Minimum-set shape (premise independently established here).** Every vertex has degree three. Three initially blue vertices cannot force the graph: if an initial force occurs, the blue vertices comprise one center \(z\) and two neighbors. Its only white neighbor becomes blue, leaving exactly the closed neighborhood \(N[z]\) blue. Since \(G_n\) is triangle-free and cubic for \(n\ge6\), none of the three blue neighbors of \(z\) can then force, and \(z\) cannot force; no further force is possible. If there is no initial force the process is already stuck. On the other hand the four corners of two consecutive rungs force all of \(G_n\) by propagating along both rims. Hence \(Z(G_n)=4\).

Choose the center of a first force from a four-blue set, send it to \(u_0\) by symmetry, and use reflection to reduce the two other initially blue neighbors of the center to either \(\{u_{-1},u_1\}\) or \(\{u_1,v_0\}\). The fourth initially blue vertex is outside \(N[u_0]\), as the third neighbor must be initially white. Once \(u_0\) has forced, its closed neighborhood is blue. The only possible next force requires the fourth initially blue vertex to meet at least one of the other three neighbors of \(u_0\); otherwise each such neighbor has two white neighbors and the fourth blue vertex itself has too few blue neighbors. Thus the fourth vertex must be among \(u_{-2},u_2,v_{-1},v_1\). Directly substituting the eight possibilities and using layer swap, reflection and rotation yields exactly these five representative sets, each of which forces a pair of adjacent entire rungs in at most two steps:

| Type | Representative | Orbit order | Immediate construction |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| \(Q\) | \(\{u_0,u_1,v_0,v_1\}\) | \(n\) | Already contains two rungs. |
| \(R\) | \(\{u_0,u_1,u_2,u_3\}\) | \(2n\) | \(u_1\to v_1\), \(u_2\to v_2\). |
| \(T\) | \(\{u_0,u_1,v_0,v_{-1}\}\) | \(2n\) | \(v_0\to v_1\). |
| \(E\) | \(\{u_0,u_1,u_2,v_0\}\) | \(4n\) | \(u_1\to v_1\). |
| \(J\) | \(\{u_0,u_1,u_3,v_1\}\) | \(4n\) | \(u_1\to u_2\), \(u_2\to v_2\). |

The orbits are distinct by layer occupancy and induced adjacencies. Their sizes follow by orbit–stabilizer under the order-\(4n\) group generated by rotations, reflections and layer interchange; stabilizer orders in table order are \(4,2,2,1,1\). This reproduces the previously established five-orbit classification only as a premise, not as the assessed new result.

**2. Exact adjacency quotient.** Let \(\mathcal O_Q,\mathcal O_R,\mathcal O_T,\mathcal O_E,\mathcal O_J\) denote those five orbits. Every orbit is transitive under the graph automorphism group, and thus every vertex in a fixed orbit has the same number of neighbors in each orbit. Two four-element sets are adjacent in \(\mathcal R_n\) if and only if they have *exactly three* common elements. For each displayed representative \(S\), compare it with every image of each of the five representatives under the maps
\[
u_i\longmapsto \begin{cases}u_{\sigma i+t},&\varepsilon=0,\\v_{\sigma i+t},&\varepsilon=1,\end{cases}
\qquad
v_i\longmapsto \begin{cases}v_{\sigma i+t},&\varepsilon=0,\\u_{\sigma i+t},&\varepsilon=1,\end{cases}
\]
where \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\), \(t\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) and \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}\). Retain a candidate if and only if it shares three of the four vertices of \(S\), identifying duplicate candidates. All five patterns use column indices between \(-1\) and \(3\). Any common vertex between a representative and a transformed copy therefore forces its relative translation to satisfy \(t\equiv i-\sigma j\pmod n\) for some such indices \(i,j\). Thus \(t\) has an integer representative in \(\{-4,-3,\ldots,6\}\), a fixed list independent of \(n\). For \(n\ge11\), these candidate translations are distinct modulo \(n\), and the intersections follow from the same finite comparison; the cases \(6\le n\le10\) are evaluated directly with reductions modulo \(n\). Counting each *distinct* four-set once gives the **exact equitable quotient**
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrr|r}
 &Q&R&T&E&J&\deg\\\hline
Q&0&0&4&4&4&12\\
R&0&2&0&4&2&8\\
T&2&0&0&4&2&8\\
E&1&2&2&1&2&8\\
J&1&1&1&2&0&5
\end{array}.
\]
This finite comparison is reproducible with `verify_prism_reconfig.py`, whose explicit `orbit` transformations and `reconfiguration_graph` function compute the same intersection condition without using this quotient matrix as an input to graph construction. The entries can also be checked by hand; for example a complete-rung square has four one-token neighbors of each of types \(T,E,J\), and none of types \(Q,R\). All five rows obey detailed balance with the corresponding orbit orders (e.g., \(n\cdot4=(4n)\cdot1\)). Therefore the degrees have multiplicities \(n,8n,4n\), and the handshaking lemma gives
\[
|E(\mathcal R_n)|=\frac{12n+8(8n)+5(4n)}2=48n.
\]

**3. Connectedness.** Let \(Q_i=\{u_i,u_{i+1},v_i,v_{i+1}\}\). Consecutive square configurations are joined by the two token-jumps
\[
Q_i\longleftrightarrow \{u_i,u_{i+1},v_{i+1},v_{i+2}\}\longleftrightarrow Q_{i+1},
\]
where the intermediate set is of type \(T\) (after a symmetry). Therefore all \(Q_i\) belong to one reconfiguration component. Relative to \(Q_0\), the representatives of types \(T,E,J\) differ by one vertex and are adjacent; \(R\) differs by one vertex from \(E\), so its representative also reaches \(Q_0\) in two moves. Applying any rotation, reflection or layer interchange carries these paths to a path from every representative in that orbit to some square \(Q_i\). Hence every one of the \(13n\) optimum configurations belongs to the single component containing the \(Q_i\). This proves connectedness and completes the theorem.

## Verification

The standalone Python 3 verifier `verify_prism_reconfig.py` independently builds each underlying prism, simulates ordinary zero forcing, constructs the five symmetry orbits, and forms the reconfiguration graph by exact three-element intersections. Across \(6\le n\le15\), it compares **every** four-element initial subset against the five-orbit prescription, tests all three-element initial subsets, and checks the entire adjacency quotient, degree distribution, number of edges and graph connectivity. It inspects \(91{,}992\) four-element initial subsets. Larger sizes \(n=24,40,80\) independently verify connectivity and the edge formula on the symmetry-generated optimum sets. The recorded output is `prism_reconfig_check_output.txt`. The mathematical proof, not any finite test, establishes the formulas for all \(n\ge6\).

## Relationship to prior work

Premodkmuar, Dominic and Chacko (2020, Proposition 22) establish the *connected* zero-forcing **number** \(4\) for circular ladders and exhibit one optimal configuration, but do not analyze the token-jumping configuration graph. Geneson, Haas and Hogben (2020) introduce reconfiguration graphs of minimum zero-forcing sets and prove connectedness for forests, not this cyclic prism family. Cameron and collaborators (2023 preprint, with later revisions) study fort-based zero forcing in Cartesian products, including scalar parameter bounds, not the prism optimum reconfiguration graph. The five orbit types and their \(13n\) total are also available in earlier work and are used here only as the configuration classification required to define and analyze \(\mathcal R_n\). The **new assessed assertion** concerns its connectedness, full orbit adjacency quotient, degree multiset and exact edge count.

## Limitations

The theorem addresses ordinary minimum zero forcing with unrestricted single-token replacement, and only \(n\ge6\). It does not determine reconfiguration diameter, token-sliding reachability, or the reconfiguration of nonminimum sets. The bibliographic comparison cannot exclude unnoticed equivalent research, and no theorem about reconfiguration diameter or token sliding is asserted.
