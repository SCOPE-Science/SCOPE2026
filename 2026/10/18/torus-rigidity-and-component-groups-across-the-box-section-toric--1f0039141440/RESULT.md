# Torus rigidity and component groups across the box-section toric Fano alpha spectrum

## Finding

Let \(n\ge2\), and let \(A,B\) be coprime positive integers with \(B\le A\le nB\). In the rank-\(n\) lattice
\[
N=\left\{d=(d_0,\ldots,d_n)\in\mathbb Z^{n+1}:\sum_{i=0}^{n}d_i=0\right\}
\]
let \(Q=\{d\in N_{\mathbb R}:-A\le d_i\le B\}\). Let \(X\) be the complete complex toric variety whose fan consists of the cones over the faces of \(Q\). Then
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb C}(X)\cong (\mathbb C^*)^n\rtimes
\begin{cases}
\mathfrak S_{n+1}\times C_2,&(A,B)=(1,1),\\
\mathfrak S_{n+1},&A>B.
\end{cases}
\]
The \(\mathfrak S_{n+1}\)-factor permutes the coordinates of \(N\); in the first case \(C_2\) is generated by inversion \(d\mapsto-d\). In particular, \(\operatorname{Aut}^{0}(X)=(\mathbb C^*)^n\) for all parameters and its number of components is \(2(n+1)!\) when \((A,B)=(1,1)\), and \((n+1)!\) otherwise.

## Assumptions and scope

The toric variety is taken over \(\mathbb C\), with **precisely the sum-zero integer lattice** displayed above; the fan and its lattice are part of the definition. No smoothness assumption is imposed. The bounding inequalities describe a full-dimensional lattice polytope with the origin in its interior. This is the box-section presentation arising in the recent toric alpha-spectrum constructions; no statement about varieties defined with a different finite-index lattice is made.

The dual character lattice is \(M=\mathbb Z^{n+1}/\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1)\), paired with \(N\) by the ordinary dot product. The vertices of \(Q\) are primitive rays: when there is an interior free coordinate, each has both \(-A\) and \(B\), and \(\gcd(A,B)=1\); if all coordinates lie on bounds, the same gcd argument applies. The endpoint \((A,B)=(n,1)\) is covered.

## Proof

**Step 1: vertex structure and orbit tight frame.** A vertex of \(Q\) has at least \(n\) active coordinate bounds. Thus either it has the multiset
\[
(\underbrace{-A,\ldots,-A}_{k},\,C,\underbrace{B,\ldots,B}_{n-k}),\qquad C=kA-(n-k)B,
\]
where \(-A<C<B\) and \(1\le k\le n-1\), or it has \(p\) copies of \(-A\) and \(n+1-p\) copies of \(B\), with \(p(A+B)=(n+1)B\). Every vertex set is a single coordinate-permutation orbit, so its sum is zero. Let \(R\) be its cardinality. In the first case \(R=(n+1)\binom nk>2n\). In the second, \(R=\binom{n+1}{p}\). If \(A<nB\), then \(2\le p\le(n+1)/2\); hence \(R>2n\), except when \((n,A,B)=(3,1,1)\), for which \(R=6=2n\).

Write \(W=N\otimes\mathbb R\) with the Euclidean inner product induced from \(\mathbb R^{n+1}\). All vertices have the same squared norm \(s>0\). The standard permutation representation on \(W\) is irreducible, and averaging rank-one tensors over the vertex orbit gives
\[
\sum_{v\in\operatorname{Vert}(Q)}v\otimes v=\frac{Rs}{n}I_W.
\]
For any nonzero linear functional \(m\) identified with a vector of \(W\), its nonzero pairings with vertices therefore have support size at least \(R/n\): compare the displayed sum of squares with the bound \(|\langle m,v\rangle|^2\le s\|m\|^2\). In particular, if \(R>2n\), **every nonzero functional pairs nontrivially with at least three vertices**.

**Step 2: exclude Demazure roots.** A Demazure root is an element \(m\in M\) having pairing \(-1\) with one primitive ray, and nonnegative integral pairings with every other primitive ray. Because the sum of all ray generators is zero, the nonnegative values must sum to \(1\). Thus its pairings would be \(-1,+1,0,\ldots,0\), with exactly two nonzero entries. This contradicts Step 1 whenever \(R>2n\).

For the simplex endpoint \((A,B)=(n,1)\), the rays are \(v_i=(1,\ldots,1)-(n+1)e_i\). For any character represented by \((m_0,\ldots,m_n)\in\mathbb Z^{n+1}\), all \(\langle m,v_i\rangle=\sum_jm_j-(n+1)m_i\) are congruent modulo \(n+1\). Since \(n+1\ge3\), the required mixture of \(-1,+1,0\) is impossible.

For the only remaining case \((n,A,B)=(3,1,1)\), the six rays consist of three opposite pairs generated by
\[
v_1=(-1,-1,1,1),\quad v_2=(-1,1,-1,1),\quad v_3=(-1,1,1,-1).
\]
A two-entry support must be one opposite pair. If \(\langle m,v_2\rangle=\langle m,v_3\rangle=0\), then \(m_0=m_1\) and \(m_2=m_3\) for every integral representative of the character; consequently \(\langle m,v_1\rangle=2(m_2-m_0)\) is even and cannot equal \(\pm1\). The other pairs are symmetric. Hence no Demazure roots occur anywhere in the family. By the complete toric automorphism theorem, \(\operatorname{Aut}^{0}(X)\) equals its dense torus.

**Step 3: classify lattice fan automorphisms.** A fan automorphism permutes primitive ray generators, and because these are exactly the vertices of \(Q\), it preserves \(Q\). Conversely, any lattice symmetry of \(Q\) preserves the face fan. Let \(u_i\) denote the class of \(e_i\) in the dual space \(M_{\mathbb R}\); its unique linear dependence is \(\sum_i u_i=0\).

For \(A<nB\), the facets of \(Q\) are precisely the \(2(n+1)\) sets \(d_i=-A\) and \(d_i=B\), with outward normals on the paired lines \(\mathbb Ru_i\). Hence any polytope symmetry permutes the \(n+1\) paired lines. Its dual linear map has the form \(u_i\mapsto\lambda_i\epsilon_i u_{\pi(i)}\) for positive real \(\lambda_i\) and signs \(\epsilon_i\). Applying the unique linear relation forces all signed scalars \(\lambda_i\epsilon_i\) equal. Thus all signs are equal, and the map is a common scalar multiple of either a coordinate permutation or its negative. Lattice unimodularity forces the absolute scalar to be \(1\). Negative coordinate permutation preserves \(Q\) exactly when \(A=B\), which coprimality makes \((A,B)=(1,1)\).

At \(A=nB\), only the \(n+1\) upper coordinate facets survive; the same unique-relation argument forces coordinate permutations, with no global sign option. Therefore the lattice fan symmetry group is \(\mathfrak S_{n+1}\) except at \((A,B)=(1,1)\), where it is \(\mathfrak S_{n+1}\times C_2\).

**Step 4: assemble the group.** Since all Demazure roots are absent, the identity component is precisely the torus; the fan symmetries give a finite complement by toric automorphisms. The asserted semidirect products follow.

## Verification

The accompanying dependency-free `VERIFY.py` independently builds each primitive vertex orbit from the box inequalities. It checks the exact orbit tight-frame identities and the required cardinality inequalities for 65 parameter triples in dimensions \(2\) through \(6\). It also exhaustively checks characters with a fixed representative and coefficients between \(-2\) and \(2\) for 27 smaller triples. These finite checks corroborate Step 1 and the absence of small roots; neither finite sweep proves the universal statements. The proof of all dimensions is the preceding argument, including both exceptional integer-lattice cases.

## Relationship to prior work

Wu's 2026 alpha-spectrum construction supplies the motivating toric polytope family and arithmetic parameter range. Its inspected construction and properties concern K-polystability, alpha values, singularities, and anticanonical geometry, not the complete algebraic automorphism groups. Nill's general results connect symmetry, reductivity and Demazure roots; Sancho and collaborators describe full automorphism groups of complete toric varieties. Those general theorems are the structural inputs, not the family-specific root exclusion or finite-component classification.

A previously established automorphism calculation for the specialization \((A,B)=(2n-1,2)\) already gives the \(\mathfrak S_{n+1}\) answer there. **No priority is claimed for that subfamily.** The present statement covers all coprime parameters in the entire interval, notably the centrally symmetric endpoint with an additional involution and the simplex endpoint with root-free automorphism identity component. A broader unindexed earlier classification or an implicit application of classical toric theory remains a possible overlap.

## Limitations

The group statement is over \(\mathbb C\), for the explicitly fixed lattice only. It does not classify arbitrary toric K-polystable Fano varieties or their moduli, and it does not prove a new alpha-spectrum theorem. Bibliographic priority cannot be certified by unsuccessful searches; independent review is outstanding.
