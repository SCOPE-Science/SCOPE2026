# Translation homology characters of group-Latin-square clique complexes

## Finding

Let \(A\) be **any finite abelian group** of order \(n\geq5\), let \(A[2]=\{u\in A:2u=0\}\), and put \(t_2=|A[2]|\). The graph \(G_A\) has vertex set \(A\times A\), with distinct \((x,y)\) and \((x',y')\) adjacent precisely when \(x=x'\), \(y=y'\), or \(x+y=x'+y'\). Write \(X_A=\operatorname{Cl}(G_A)\). The translation group \(T=A\times A\) acts by \((a,b):(x,y)\mapsto(x+a,y+b)\).

Define three subgroups of \(T\):
\[
R=\{(0,b):b\in A\},\quad C=\{(a,0):a\in A\},\quad D=\{(a,-a):a\in A\},\qquad L=R\cup C\cup D.
\]
All reduced rational homology of \(X_A\) is concentrated in degree \(2\). The character \(\chi(t)=\operatorname{tr}(t_*\mid\widetilde H_2(X_A;\mathbb Q))\) **on every translation** is
\[
\chi(t)=
\begin{cases}
(n-1)^3-\dfrac{(t_2-1)n^2}{4},&t=0,\\[4pt]
n-1-\dfrac{n^2}{4},&t\in L\setminus\{0\},\ 2t=0,\\[4pt]
n-1,&t\in L\setminus\{0\},\ 2t\ne0,\\[4pt]
-1,&t\notin L.
\end{cases}
\]
(The second case can occur only for even \(n\), so its displayed quantity is integral.)

This determines the entire rational \(T\)-module, since rational representations of finite groups are semisimple and determined by their characters. It also determines the reduced rational homology of **every subgroup quotient**. For \(H\leq T\), let \(L_1=R,L_2=C,L_3=D\), and define
\[
h_i=|H\cap L_i|,\qquad e_i=|\{t\in H\cap L_i:2t=0\}|\quad (i=1,2,3).
\]
Then \(\widetilde H_j(|X_A|/H;\mathbb Q)=0\) for \(j\ne2\), and
\[
\dim_{\mathbb Q}\widetilde H_2(|X_A|/H;\mathbb Q)
=\frac{(n-1)^3-\frac{(t_2-1)n^2}{4}+n\sum_{i=1}^3(h_i-1)-\frac{n^2}{4}\sum_{i=1}^3(e_i-1)-(|H|-1)}{|H|}.
\]
In particular, for the **full translation quotient**,
\[
\boxed{\dim_{\mathbb Q}\widetilde H_2(|X_A|/T;\mathbb Q)=n-|A[2]|.}
\]
Thus this quotient is rationally acyclic if and only if \(A\) has exponent \(2\). For example, at group order \(8\) its second rational Betti number is \(6\) for \(A\cong\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z\), \(4\) for \(A\cong\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\), and \(0\) for \(A\cong(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^3\).

## Assumptions and scope

All groups are finite and abelian, and \(n\geq5\). All graph cliques are ordinary simple-graph cliques, all complexes are their full flag complexes, and the quotient is the **geometric realization quotient**, not the naively identified abstract simplicial complex. Homology and characters are over \(\mathbb Q\), except for the finite mod-two checks described under verification. The only homological premise imported from the literature is the established vanishing \(\widetilde H_j(X_A;\mathbb Q)=0\) for \(j\ne2\) for Latin-square graphs of order at least five; all character and quotient formulas are independently derived below.

## Proof

**1. Classification of cliques.** Regard a row, column, or constant-sum set of \(A^2\) as a *line*. Each pair of distinct adjacent vertices lies in exactly one such line. In a clique that is not itself contained in one line, choose an edge on a line and a third vertex outside it: the resulting triangle uses all three distinct adjacency types, since two edges of the same type in a triangle force the third to have that type too. The affine symmetries generated by translations, swapping the two coordinates, and \((x,y)\mapsto(x,-x-y)\) permute these three types. Thus every triangle not contained in a line can be normalized to
\[
(0,0),\ (0,u),\ (u,0),\qquad u\ne0.
\]
A fourth point adjacent to all three must have both coordinates nonzero, have coordinate sum zero, have second coordinate \(u\) to meet \((0,u)\), and have first coordinate \(u\) to meet \((u,0)\). Therefore this point exists precisely when \(2u=0\), and is uniquely \((u,u)\). There cannot be a fifth vertex adjacent to the initial triangle. Consequently, every clique with at least five vertices lies in a unique line; all four-vertex cliques not in lines are exactly the *intercalates*
\[
\{(x,y),(x+u,y),(x,y+u),(x+u,y+u)\},\qquad 0\ne u\in A[2].
\]
For each fixed \(u\), there are \((n/2)^2\) such rectangles, so their total number is
\[
I_A=(t_2-1)n^2/4.
\]
Every non-line triangle has a unique vertex at which its row and column edges meet, giving exactly \(n^2(n-1)\) non-line triangles. The face counts are therefore \(n^2\) vertices; for \(k\ge2\), line \((k-1)\)-simplices number \(3n\binom nk\); and additionally there are \(n^2(n-1)\) non-line triangles and \(I_A\) intercalate tetrahedra. (The expression \(\binom nk\) is taken as zero if \(k>n\).)

Using \(\sum_{k=2}^n(-1)^{k-1}\binom nk=1-n\), we obtain
\[
\widetilde\chi(X_A)=n^2+3n(1-n)+n^2(n-1)-I_A-1=(n-1)^3-I_A.
\]
The Latin-square-graph homology theorem of Cioabă--Mim [1, Section 3, especially Theorem 3.12] asserts vanishing of reduced rational homology outside degree two for \(n\ge5\). Hence \(\dim\widetilde H_2=(n-1)^3-I_A\), proving the identity character case. In particular \(X_A\) is connected, also immediate from its row and column edges.

**2. Signed Lefschetz enumeration of a nonidentity translation.** Let \(t=(a,b)\ne0\) have order \(d\ge2\). Every vertex orbit has exactly \(d\) members. For a \(t\)-invariant simplex that is a union of \(q\ge1\) whole vertex orbits, its signed contribution to the ordinary simplicial Lefschetz number is
\[
(-1)^{dq-1}\,(-1)^{q(d-1)}=(-1)^{q-1}.
\]
The reduced Lefschetz number subtracts \(1\) for the augmentation. A vertex orbit is a clique precisely if at least one of \(a=0\), \(b=0\), or \(a+b=0\) holds; this follows by testing adjacency between a vertex and its translate. Thus if \(t\notin L\), no nonempty invariant simplex exists, and the reduced Lefschetz number is \(-1\).

If \(t\in L\setminus\{0\}\), exactly one of those relations holds, so \(t\) preserves exactly \(n\) of the corresponding lines (and preserves no lines in the other two families). On each invariant line, the vertex permutation consists of \(n/d\) disjoint cycles. The signed sum over all nonempty unions of those cycles is
\[
\sum_{q=1}^{n/d}(-1)^{q-1}\binom{n/d}{q}=1.
\]
No two distinct lines share an invariant simplex with at least two vertices. Hence all line-supported invariant simplices contribute exactly \(n\), before subtracting the reduced augmentation.

A non-line invariant simplex must be an intercalate: a four-vertex clique. Translation invariance of a rectangle forces \(2a=2b=0\), hence \(d=2\). Conversely, if \(t\in L\setminus\{0\}\) and \(2t=0\), the invariant intercalates are exactly the \(n^2/4\) rectangles of step given by its nonzero coordinate (or by the nonzero common value \(a=-b\)). The translation acts as two disjoint swaps on each such four-vertex set, so each intercalate contributes \((-1)^3=-1\). There are no other non-line invariant simplices. Combining the line sum, the intercalate correction, and the reduced augmentation gives \(n-1\) for line translations of order above two and \(n-1-n^2/4\) for line translations of order two. This proves the displayed character formula, since all reduced homology is in even degree two and the reduced Lefschetz number is its trace.

**3. All subgroup quotients.** For any finite subgroup \(H\le T\), barycentric subdivision makes the action without inversions. The rational chain complex of the realization quotient identifies with the \(H\)-coinvariants of the subdivided chains, which equal the invariant chains by rational averaging; taking homology commutes with averaging. Thus
\[
\widetilde H_*(|X_A|/H;\mathbb Q)\cong\widetilde H_*(X_A;\mathbb Q)^H.
\]
The dimension of its degree-two part is \(|H|^{-1}\sum_{t\in H}\chi(t)\). The three line subgroups meet pairwise only in the identity. Consequently, \(H\) contains \(\sum_i(h_i-1)\) nonzero line translations, including \(\sum_i(e_i-1)\) involutions, with \(|H|-1-\sum_i(h_i-1)\) remaining nonzero translations. Substitution gives the stated subgroup formula.

For \(H=T\), each \(h_i=n\) and \(e_i=t_2\), while \(|T|=n^2\). The numerator simplifies exactly to \(n^2(n-t_2)\). Therefore the full quotient has second rational Betti number \(n-t_2\). It vanishes precisely when all elements of \(A\) have order at most two, i.e. \(A\) has exponent two.

## Verification

The included dependency-free `verify.py` reconstructs group tables and every clique independently of the classification argument. It explicitly applies translations to oriented simplices, tests setwise invariance, computes permutation signs by inversion counts, and sums signed traces; it also verifies all subgroup quotient ranks for cyclic and two-generated subgroups, as well as the full group. It checks seven group types: cyclic orders \(5,6,7,8\), \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\), \((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^3\), and \((\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z)^2\). Across them it checks \(7\) full translation characters and \(1149\) distinct subgroup cases (the counts are listed separately in `verify_output.txt`). It additionally computes complete simplicial boundary ranks mod two for cyclic groups of orders \(5\) and \(6\). These are reproducibility cross-checks of the all-parameter proof, not substitutes for it.

Run from a directory containing the files using `python3 verify.py`. The reference output is `verify_output.txt`.

## Relationship to prior work

Cioabă--Mim [1], publicly available June 25, 2026, proves the nonequivariant homology concentration and ranks for Latin-square-graph clique complexes of all orders at least five and discusses intercalates and their top-dimensional faces. Its Section 3 contains no translation-character theorem and no formula for rational subgroup-quotient homology. We use its homology concentration as an established input, and independently rederive the necessary combinatorial clique enumeration before computing new equivariant Lefschetz traces.

Generic simplicial Lefschetz theory and rational finite-group averaging are classical tools; their general statements are not claimed as new. The contribution is the explicit all-translation character, its involution/intercalate correction, and the resulting subgroup-quotient classification for the group-table family. General adjacency-spectrum work on Latin-square graphs addresses a different invariant and does not determine this homology character. Amarra [3] studies the regular action by group-table translations but classifies **normal quotient graphs** and arc-transitivity, rather than the homology of geometric quotients of the clique complex. Its graph-theoretic quotient does not generally commute with the clique-complex quotient.

## Limitations

The theorem is restricted to finite abelian groups of order at least five, their indicated translation actions, and rational (co)homology of geometric orbit spaces. It does **not** classify the full graph-automorphism representation, integral torsion of the orbit spaces, their fundamental groups, or their homotopy types. No equivalence to sphere wedges is asserted. The finite computational cases do not establish the infinite formulas; the preceding combinatorial proof and credited nonequivariant theorem do.
