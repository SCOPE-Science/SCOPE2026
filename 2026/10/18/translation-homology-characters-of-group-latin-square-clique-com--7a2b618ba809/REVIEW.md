# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For every finite abelian group \(A\) of order \(n\ge5\), the proof explicitly classifies all clique types by a normalized three-direction triangle and its unique possible fourth vertex. It counts \((|A[2]|-1)n^2/4\) intercalates, derives the reduced Euler characteristic, and uses the precisely scoped published Latin-square-graph theorem to place all reduced rational homology in degree two. The signed Lefschetz contribution of a union of translation orbits is calculated with orientation signs, and non-line invariant faces occur exactly as the stated order-two intercalates. Averaging gives every subgroup-quotient rank. Complete direct face-trace computations for seven group types and independent mod-two chain ranks for two types agree. There is no extrapolation from finite sampling.

## Originality — PASS with residual risk

The closely matching primary source, Cioabă–Mim, arXiv:2606.27328, Section 3, establishes nonequivariant homology groups of these exact clique complexes and counts intercalates, but does not calculate the action of translations or quotient homology. Full relevant primary-source text was inspected, and mathematical implication was checked: dimensions alone do not determine the character, and the involution correction requires a new invariant-simplex comparison. Targeted exact-topic literature and published-record searches did not identify a theorem covering the displayed character or all subgroup quotients. Classical Lefschetz and finite-group averaging are acknowledged, not represented as new. Possible unindexed equivalent equivariant results remain a residual risk; absence from search is not proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The character and full subgroup-quotient ranks give structural information for an infinite natural class of clique complexes that ordinary Betti numbers do not provide. The dependence on the group’s two-torsion gives a sharp vanishing criterion: the full translation quotient is rationally acyclic exactly when every element has order at most two. It distinguishes the full-quotient ranks \(6\), \(4\), and \(0\) for the three abelian groups of order eight. The result is not a one-parameter numerical increment, a rephrasing of the prior rank formula, or a finite table recomputation.

## Closest literature and limits

Also inspected: Amarra, *Quotient-complete arc-transitive latin square graphs from groups*, arXiv:1709.05760, Sections 2.1–2.2, where normal quotient graphs are constructed rather than geometric quotients of clique complexes. Nearby adjacency-only literature: Bailey–Cameron–Gavrilyuk–Goryainov, *Equitable partitions of Latin-square graphs*, J. Combinatorial Designs 27 (2019), 142–160; the available abstract addresses graph adjacency partition spectra and was not used to exclude unseen full-text results. The present conclusion does not extend to nonabelian group tables or integral subgroup quotient homology, and independent validation has not occurred.
