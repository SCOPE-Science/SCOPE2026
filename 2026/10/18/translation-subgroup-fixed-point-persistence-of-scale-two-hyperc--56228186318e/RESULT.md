# Translation-subgroup fixed-point persistence of scale-two hypercube Vietoris–Rips complexes

## Finding

For each integer \(n\geq3\), identify the binary hypercube with \(T=(\mathbb F_2)^n\), equipped with Hamming distance, and let \(K_r=\operatorname{VR}(Q_n;r)\) be the inclusive Vietoris–Rips complex. The group \(T\) acts by coordinate-flip translations. For every **nontrivial subgroup** \(H\leq T\), the geometric fixed-point spaces \(|K_r|^H\) for all \(0\leq r\leq2\), together with their filtration inclusions, have the following complete classification:

* For \(0\leq r<1\), every \(|K_r|^H\) is empty.
* For \(1\leq r<2\), \(|K_r|^H\) is a discrete space of exactly \(2^{n-1}\) points if \(H=\langle e_i\rangle\) is generated by a weight-one coordinate vector. It is empty for every other nontrivial \(H\).
* For \(r=2\), if \(H=\langle v\rangle\) is generated by a vector \(v\) of Hamming weight one **or two**, then \(|K_2|^H\) is **homeomorphic to the geometric realization of the \((n-1)\)-dimensional cube graph** \(Q_{n-1}\), hence
  \[
  |K_2|^H\simeq\bigvee^{\,1+(n-3)2^{n-2}}S^1.
  \]
* For \(r=2\), if \(H\) is any two-dimensional binary subspace whose nonzero vectors all have Hamming weight at most two, then \(|K_2|^H\) is a discrete space of exactly \(2^{n-2}\) points, hence a \((2^{n-2}-1)\)-fold wedge of zero-spheres. Such \(H\) are precisely the \(\binom n2\) coordinate-square subgroups \(\langle e_i,e_j\rangle\) and the \(\binom n3\) even-parity subgroups \(\langle e_i+e_j,e_i+e_k\rangle\) supported on three distinct coordinates.
* For \(r=2\), every other nontrivial subgroup has an **empty** fixed-point space. In particular, all subgroups of rank at least three have empty fixed sets.

For the rank-one weight-one subgroups, the inclusion \(|K_1|^H\hookrightarrow |K_2|^H\) is, under the displayed homeomorphism, the inclusion of the \(2^{n-1}\) vertices as the zero-skeleton of \(Q_{n-1}\). This determines the full fixed-space filtration and its integral persistent homology through scale two. The ordinary nonequivariant classification of \(K_2\) as a wedge of three-spheres is established in earlier work and **is not a new claim**.

## Assumptions and scope

Here \(e_i\) denotes the standard basis of \(T\), the weight \(\operatorname{wt}(v)\) is the number of nonzero coordinates of \(v\), and \(Q_m\) denotes the one-skeleton of the \(m\)-cube, with vertices \((\mathbb F_2)^m\) and edges between strings differing in one coordinate. A face of \(K_r\) is any finite subset of binary vertices with all pairwise Hamming distances at most \(r\); the threshold is **inclusive**. Translation \(t\in T\) sends the vertex \(x\) to \(x+t\) and acts simplicially on \(K_r\). The notation \(|K_r|^H\) means points of its **geometric realization fixed simultaneously by all members of \(H\)**; it does not mean the induced subcomplex on pointwise fixed vertices (which would always be empty for nontrivial translations).

All spaces and homology statements are integral. The theorem covers **all** nontrivial subgroups of the entire translation group and every scale in the interval \([0,2]\), not just involutions, isolated parameter values, or rational homology. Empty spaces are not assigned a nonempty wedge model. The parent complex \(K_r\) and the trivial subgroup are outside the *new* fixed-point assertion.

## Proof

**1. A fixed-orbit description valid for any subgroup.** Let \(K\) be a finite abstract simplicial complex on which a finite group \(H\) acts simplicially. A geometric point \(p=\sum_{x}t_xx\), expressed in barycentric coordinates, is fixed by \(H\) exactly when \(t_x=t_{hx}\) for every \(h\in H\). Thus its support is a union of vertex orbits, each endowed with constant barycentric weight. It follows that \(|K|^H\) is canonically homeomorphic to the realization of the simplicial complex \(F_H(K)\) whose vertices are the \(H\)-orbits that themselves form a face of \(K\), and whose faces are collections of orbits whose union is a face of \(K\). The homeomorphism sends each orbit-vertex to the barycenter of that orbit. For translation actions, all vertex orbits are cosets \(x+H\).

For \(K_r\), a coset \(x+H\) is an admissible orbit if and only if \(\operatorname{wt}(h)\leq r\) for every \(h\in H\). This is because its pairwise differences are precisely the elements of \(H\). If even one nonzero \(h\) has weight exceeding \(r\), there are no admissible orbits and \(|K_r|^H=\varnothing\). This already settles \(0\leq r<1\), and rules out all but weight-one cyclic subgroups for \(1\leq r<2\).

**2. The fixed space at scale one.** If \(H=\langle e_i\rangle\), its \(2^{n-1}\) cosets are the length-one edges parallel to coordinate \(i\). Two distinct cosets, indexed by different values \(y,z\in(\mathbb F_2)^{n-1}\), contain points at maximum mutual distance \(1+d_H(y,z)\geq2\). Therefore no two cosets form a simplex in \(K_1\), and \(F_H(K_1)\) is exactly \(2^{n-1}\) isolated vertices.

**3. Weight-one involutions at scale two.** For \(H=\langle e_i\rangle\), use the same coset labels \(y\in(\mathbb F_2)^{n-1}\). Two orbits have union of Hamming diameter at most two if and only if \(d_H(y,z)\leq1\). For distinct labels this means Hamming distance exactly one. Every clique in this orbit-compatibility graph has at most two vertices, since the ordinary hypercube graph is triangle-free. Hence \(F_H(K_2)\) is the clique complex of \(Q_{n-1}\), which is \(Q_{n-1}\) itself as a one-dimensional simplicial complex. The original orbit set at scale one is unchanged, so its fixed-space inclusion is exactly the inclusion of the cube's zero-skeleton.

**4. Weight-two involutions at scale two.** After permuting coordinates, write the generator as \(e_1+e_2\), and let \(m=n-2\). Its orbits are
\[
 A_y=\{(0,0,y),(1,1,y)\},\qquad
 B_y=\{(0,1,y),(1,0,y)\},\qquad y\in(\mathbb F_2)^m.
\]
All \(2^{m+1}\) orbits are admissible. Distinct \(A\)-orbits cannot be paired in a face, because the union of \(A_y,A_z\) has diameter \(2+d_H(y,z)>2\); similarly for two distinct \(B\)-orbits. The union of \(A_y\) and \(B_z\) has diameter \(1+d_H(y,z)\), so they are compatible precisely when \(d_H(y,z)\leq1\). There are no triangles. Label \(A_y\) as \((0,y)\) and \(B_y\) as \((1,y)\). The compatible edges are differences \((1,0)\) and \((1,e_j)\) for \(1\leq j\leq m\). These \(m+1\) vectors form an \(\mathbb F_2\)-basis. More explicitly, the invertible linear relabeling \((t,y)\mapsto (t+\sum_jy_j,y)\) sends them to the standard basis. Hence the orbit complex is **isomorphic** to \(Q_{m+1}=Q_{n-1}\).

For \(n\geq3\), the connected cube graph \(Q_{n-1}\) has \(V=2^{n-1}\) vertices and \(E=(n-1)2^{n-2}\) edges. Its integral homotopy type is a wedge of \(E-V+1=1+(n-3)2^{n-2}\) circles. This establishes the rank-one cases, with torsion-free integral homology.

**5. Classification of admissible rank-two subgroups.** Let \(H\) be generated by independent nonzero vectors \(u,v\), and suppose that all three of \(u,v,u+v\) have weight at most two. If \(u\) and \(v\) are both weight one, they are distinct coordinate vectors and \(H\) is a coordinate square. If exactly one generator has weight one and the other weight two, the weight-one coordinate must belong to the support of the other (otherwise their sum has weight three), so \(H\) is again a coordinate square. If both generators have weight two, their supports must intersect in exactly one coordinate (disjoint supports give weight four), and \(H\) is the even-parity plane on the union of the three coordinates. This gives exactly \(\binom n2+\binom n3\) rank-two admissible subgroups, without overcounting.

For a coordinate square \(H=\langle e_1,e_2\rangle\), the \(2^{n-2}\) cosets are entire squares \(\{0,1\}^2\times\{y\}\). Distinct cosets have union diameter \(2+d_H(y,z)>2\), so the fixed space is exactly the discrete set of these coset barycenters. For a three-coordinate even-parity plane \(H=\langle e_1+e_2,e_1+e_3\rangle\), the cosets are indexed by parity \(p\in\mathbb F_2\) of the three supported coordinates and the outside string \(y\in(\mathbb F_2)^{n-3}\). Same-parity cosets with different outside strings have mutual diameter \(2+d_H(y,z)>2\). Opposite-parity cosets contain a pair differing in all three supported coordinates, also excluding their union from \(K_2\). Thus every admissible rank-two subgroup again has \(2^{n-2}\) isolated orbit-vertices and no edges.

**6. No admissible subgroup of rank three or higher.** Every admissible rank-two subgroup is one of the two types just classified. For the coordinate square on \(\{i,j\}\), an additional independent vector \(w\) of weight at most two either has an outside coordinate, in which case some member of \(w+H\) has weight at least three, or has support inside \(\{i,j\}\), in which case \(w\in H\). For the three-coordinate even-parity plane, an additional \(w\) with any outside coordinate makes one of \(w,w+e_i+e_j,w+e_i+e_k,w+e_j+e_k\) have weight at least three; if \(w\) lies entirely on the three coordinates, any odd-parity \(w\notin H\) also has a translate of weight three. Therefore no admissible rank-two plane admits an independent third vector. Every subgroup of rank at least three contains a rank-three subgroup, and hence some nonzero vector of weight at least three. Step 1 then forces its fixed-point space to be empty at scale two.

Steps 1–6 prove the complete space and filtration classifications. The small-scale persistence assertion follows because all complexes remain constant between consecutive integer thresholds, while the only nonempty inclusion is the weight-one rank-one case in Step 3. \(\square\)

## Verification

The included independent, dependency-free `verify.py` enumerates the translation subgroups of ranks one, two and three for every dimension \(3\leq n\leq6\). It reconstructs all cosets and all compatible orbit pairs directly from Hamming distances at scales zero, one and two. For every rank-one admissible subgroup it checks the cube graph's vertex count, degree, edge count, connectedness, full graph-distance profile and Euler characteristic; for rank-two admissible subgroups it checks the exact isolated orbit count; for rank-three subgroups it checks the weight obstruction. The rank-one scale-one inclusion is checked to identify exactly the same coset-vertex set at scale two. All finite checks passed; see `verification_output.txt`.

The verification is **not** the infinite proof. The all-parameter assertions follow from the explicit orbit-homeomorphism, the two linear graph identifications, and the exhaustive rank-two subgroup classification in the proof. Neither the script nor the output claims to verify all dimensions computationally.

## Relationship to prior work

Adamaszek and Adams prove that \(\operatorname{VR}(Q_n;2)\) is a wedge of three-spheres and provide its nonequivariant Betti numbers; this already covers the trivial-subgroup homotopy type. Bendersky, Elia and Grbić analyze connectivity, cohomology and explicit cohomology classes of the same hypercube Rips complexes, but their results do not determine geometric fixed subspaces. Galetto, Montaño and Wellner determine the **characteristic-zero homology representations** of the full cube automorphism group at scales including two. These are actions on the homology of the entire parent complex; they are not a homotopy classification of \(\bigl|\operatorname{VR}(Q_n;r)\bigr|^H\) for every subgroup, nor do they specify the fixed-point filtration maps. In particular, the rational representation of the parent space does not alone determine its subgroup fixed-point homotopy types. The present theorem concerns the latter, finer equivariant invariant.

The original group's geometric isotropy is elementary to describe after the key observation, but deriving the complete fixed-space diagram requires accounting for **all** binary subspaces of diameter at most two, the subtle three-coordinate parity planes and the nontrivial cube graph arising from weight-two translations. General equivariant Rips methods could yield related orbit descriptions; this possibility is a residual originality risk and is not presented as a proved absence of previous work.

## Limitations

Only the translation subgroup \(T\) of the full hyperoctahedral automorphism group is classified, not the arbitrary coordinate-permutation subgroups. Only scales at most two are covered. The theorem does not yield an equivariant homotopy decomposition of the parent Rips complex, nor does it provide its new nonequivariant homology. It determines subgroup-fixed spaces and their inclusions, not the maps between fixed-point spaces associated with inclusions of **different** subgroups. No generalization to nonbinary Hamming cubes is claimed. Earlier or unindexed treatments of these fixed loci may exist.
