# Scientific review: translation-subgroup fixed-point persistence of hypercube Rips complexes

## Correctness — PASS

The assertion concerns the **geometric** fixed locus of each nontrivial binary translation subgroup and includes all scales from zero through two. The key barycentric-orbit lemma is proved directly, so invariant simplices with vertices permuted by a subgroup are not overlooked. Hamming-distance checks classify weight-one and weight-two cyclic orbit complexes as the graph \(Q_{n-1}\), with an explicit invertible binary linear relabeling in the latter case. The two families of rank-two diameter-two binary subspaces are classified without omissions, and the impossibility of extending them to rank three is shown. The incidence counts give the claimed integral wedge types and all filtration inclusions. Complete finite subgroup enumeration for \(3\leq n\leq6\) corroborates every case, but the symbolic proof—not the finite tests—supplies the all-dimensional conclusion.

## Originality — PASS (subject to specified search risk)

The primary nonequivariant classification of \(\operatorname{VR}(Q_n;2)\) as a three-sphere wedge was already established by Adamaszek and Adams and is explicitly excluded from the new claim. Bendersky–Elia–Grbić's 2026 work derives connectivity/cohomology results for the same parent complexes, while Galetto–Montaño–Wellner's 2026 Theorem 5.4 determines the characteristic-zero representation of their **ordinary homology** under the hyperoctahedral group. These statements do not determine the topology of the **geometric fixed spaces** for all binary translation subgroups, their rank-two isotropy, or the fixed-space filtration inclusions. The original manuscripts' relevant introduction, theorems, scale-two calculation and main cohomological sections were inspected. Database and literature queries for translations, fixed loci, involutions, Burnside/Bredon data and equivalent subgroup formulations did not locate that classification.

The most relevant separately indexed scale-two hypercube **Čech** calculations concern a different simplicial construction and characteristic-zero homology representations, not scale-two **Rips subgroup-fixed spaces**. A previously indexed reduction of **penultimate-scale** hypercube Rips complexes concerns a different radius and object. Published-result summaries were available for these latter comparisons; their full documents were not accessible, so they are not characterized as globally checked. General equivariant Rips methods and unindexed work remain residual originality risks; absence from a database is not taken as proof of unique discovery.

## Mathematical value — PASS

At scale two the parent \(\operatorname{VR}(Q_n;2)\) is known to be a wedge of three-spheres, but its fixed locus for every weight-one or weight-two translation is an exponentially growing wedge of circles, and precisely two natural classes of rank-two subgroups have disconnected fixed loci. The result exhausts **every subgroup** of the full translation group and records both isotropy thresholds and persistence inclusions, giving a complete nontrivial part of an equivariant fixed-point diagram, not an arbitrary isolated Betti-number calculation. Geometric subgroup-fixed-point data is finer than the known nonequivariant homotopy or characteristic-zero global representation. These distinctions are relevant to equivariant topology and to symmetry-sensitive persistent homology.

## Limits and nearest literature

The result stops at scale two and at the normal translation subgroup; no arbitrary coordinate-permutation action or higher-scale fixed locus is classified. The source checks cannot exclude an unindexed general equivariant theorem with this instance. All computations are finite corroboration. Relevant checked references: Adamaszek–Adams (arXiv:2103.01040, Theorem 1), Bendersky–Elia–Grbić (arXiv:2605.00705, Sections 2 and 5–6), and Galetto–Montaño–Wellner (arXiv:2606.20784, Theorem 5.4 and Section 5).
