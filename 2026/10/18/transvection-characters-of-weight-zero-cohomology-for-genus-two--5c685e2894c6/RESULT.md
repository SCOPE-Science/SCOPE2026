# Transvection characters of weight-zero cohomology for genus-two Siegel level covers

## Finding
For every odd prime \(p\), the natural change-of-level action of \(\mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb F_p)\) on the weight-zero compactly supported cohomology of \(\mathcal A_2[p]\) has a completely explicit character on every nonidentity symplectic transvection. Write
\[
W_i=W_0H_c^i(\mathcal A_2[p];\mathbb Q),\quad c=\frac{p^2-1}{2},\quad \pi=\frac{p^4-1}{2},\quad h=1+\frac{(p-6)(p^2-1)}{24}.
\]
For any nonzero \(v\in\mathbb F_p^4\), let \(T_v(w)=w+\langle w,v\rangle v\). Every nonidentity power \(T_v^j\), \(1\le j<p\), has character values
\[
\boxed{t_2=\operatorname{tr}(T_v^j\mid W_2)=1-\frac{p^3-1}{2}-c+c^2+2hc},
\qquad
\boxed{t_3=\operatorname{tr}(T_v^j\mid W_3)=c}.
\]
In particular the graph part of the first trace equals
\[
1-\frac{p^3-1}{2}-c+c^2=\frac{(p-3)(p^3+p^2-p-3)}4.
\]
The published nonequivariant dimensions become
\[
d_2=\dim W_2=1+\frac{p(p^2-1)(p^4-1)}{24},\qquad d_3=\dim W_3=\pi.
\]
Thus the entire restriction to the cyclic subgroup generated by \(T_v\) is given, for \(i=2,3\), by
\[
\boxed{W_i\big|_{\langle T_v\rangle}\cong\mathbb Q^{a_i}\oplus\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)^{b_i},\quad a_i=\frac{d_i+(p-1)t_i}{p},\quad b_i=\frac{d_i-t_i}{p}}.
\]
Here \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\) denotes the standard \((p-1)\)-dimensional irreducible rational representation of \(C_p\). One may simplify \(b_2=p(p-1)(p^4+p^3-p^2-p+12)/24\), \(a_3=(p-1)(p+1)^2/2\), and \(b_3=p(p^2-1)/2\). All other \(W_i\) vanish.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\), \(p\ge3\) is prime, and \(\mathcal A_2[p]\) is the moduli variety of principally polarized abelian surfaces with full symplectic level \(p\) structure. The finite deck group acts by changing level structures; its central sign acts trivially in this moduli description. We use \(\mathbb Q\)-cohomology, the weight-zero part of compactly supported mixed Hodge cohomology, and the standard symplectic form \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\) on \(\mathbb F_p^4\). The maps \(T_v\) are order-\(p\) symplectic transvections. Their scalar multiples and sign conventions do not affect the character formulas for nonidentity powers.

The primary geometric input is the actual gluing of compact modular-curve surfaces along vertices in Assaf–Brandt–Bruce–Chan–Vlad [1], not an assertion that the paper's non-equivariant wedge decomposition is equivariant.

## Proof
**1. Equivariant gluing description.** By [1, Theorem 4.5, Corollary 5.8, Theorem 5.10], the boundary link \(L_p\) is formed by gluing \(\pi\) oriented closed surfaces of genus \(h\), each labeled by an isotropic plane \(P\subset\mathbb F_p^4\) together with a generator \([\omega]\in(\bigwedge^2P\setminus\{0\})/\{\pm1\}\). Their marked vertices correspond to nonzero \(w\in P\) modulo sign. Distinct surface components meet only at their marked vertices. The associated connected bipartite graph \(G_p\) has \(\pi\) vector vertices, \(\pi\) surface vertices, and \(\pi c\) incidence edges. Hence \(\dim H^1(G_p;\mathbb Q)=1+\pi(c-2)\). The finite symplectic action preserves this geometric diagram of surfaces and gluing points.

Gluing connected spaces along finite sets of points gives natural, equivariant exact sequences whose traces yield
\[
\operatorname{tr}(T\mid H^1(L_p))=\operatorname{tr}(T\mid H^1(G_p))+\sum_{\text{fixed surfaces }S}\operatorname{tr}(T\mid H^1(S)),
\]
\[
\operatorname{tr}(T\mid H^2(L_p))=\sum_{\text{fixed surfaces }S}\operatorname{tr}(T\mid H^2(S)).
\]
To justify the first identity without an equivariant wedge-equivalence, use the natural exact sequence \(0\to H^1(G_p)\to H^1(L_p)\to\bigoplus_SH^1(S)\to0\), obtained from the Mayer–Vietoris complex of the graph of surfaces and intersection points. The second identity is its degree-two analogue. Finite-group representation traces are additive in exact sequences.

**2. Fixed vector vertices.** If \(T_v^j[w]=[w]\) for \(1\le j<p\), then \(T_v^jw=\pm w\). An odd-characteristic unipotent map cannot have eigenvalue \(-1\), so the sign is positive. Therefore \(\langle w,v\rangle=0\). The fixed signed-vector count is
\[
F_V=\frac{p^3-1}{2}.
\]

**3. Fixed decorated planes.** A two-dimensional totally isotropic subspace \(P\) is invariant under a nontrivial power of \(T_v\) if and only if it contains \(v\). Indeed, if some \(w\in P\) has nonzero pairing with \(v\), then \(T_v(w)-w\in P\) forces \(v\in P\). Otherwise \(P\subset v^\perp\); the alternating form on \(v^\perp\) has radical \(\langle v\rangle\), so a two-dimensional isotropic subspace must contain \(v\). The planes containing \(v\) correspond to the \(p+1\) lines in the symplectic plane \(v^\perp/\langle v\rangle\). Each carries \((p-1)/2\) generator classes of its determinant line. Consequently
\[
F_P=\frac{(p+1)(p-1)}2=c.
\]
On each such plane, \(T_v\) restricts to the identity because \(P\) is isotropic and \(v\in P\). Thus it fixes all its \(c\) signed vectors and all its triangles pointwise, and the total fixed incidence edges are \(F_E=c^2\). Furthermore it acts as the identity on the entire corresponding modular-curve surface, so the fixed-surface contributions to \(H^1\) and \(H^2\) are respectively \(2hc\) and \(c\). Nonfixed surfaces are cyclically permuted; their trace contributions vanish.

**4. Character formula.** Since \(G_p\) is connected, its augmented cellular chain exact sequence is
\[
0\to H_1(G_p;\mathbb Q)\to\mathbb Q^{E(G_p)}\to\mathbb Q^{V(G_p)}\to\mathbb Q\to0.
\]
Every fixed incidence edge has fixed endpoints and hence contributes \(+1\) to the edge-permutation trace. It follows that
\[
\operatorname{tr}(T_v^j\mid H^1(G_p))=1-F_V-F_P+F_E
=1-\frac{p^3-1}{2}-c+c^2.
\]
Adding the fixed-surface contributions establishes \(t_2\) and \(t_3\) above. The natural comparison between weight-zero compactly supported cohomology and reduced cohomology of the tropical boundary link in [1, Equation (9) and Theorem 5.12], obtained from the canonical boundary complex, is compatible with the change-of-level action. This yields the stated traces on \(W_2\) and \(W_3\).

**5. Rational cyclic decomposition.** Every nonidentity power \(T_v^j\) has the same fixed strata and thus the same trace \(t_i\). Since the only rational irreducible representations of \(C_p\) are \(\mathbb Q\) and \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\), if their multiplicities are \(a_i,b_i\), then \(d_i=a_i+(p-1)b_i\) and \(t_i=a_i-b_i\). Solving gives the displayed formula, including exact multiplicities in every odd prime level. This is an all-\(p\) argument.

## Verification
The independent exact finite-field checker `verify.py` constructs signed-vector classes and decorated isotropic planes by wedge classes in \(\bigwedge^2\mathbb F_p^4\). It computes fixed vertices, fixed surfaces, fixed incidence edges, dimensions, characters and cyclic irreducible multiplicities for \(p=3,5,7\). Its final output is `VERIFY_OK`. Exact examples are:

| \(p\) | \((d_2,t_2,a_2,b_2)\) | \((d_3,t_3,a_3,b_3)\) |
|---:|---|---|
|3|\((81,0,27,27)\)|\((40,4,16,12)\)|
|5|\((3121,71,681,610)\)|\((312,12,72,60)\)|

Run `python3 verify.py` with a standard Python 3 installation. No third-party packages or externally generated data are required. These finite checks are corroboration only; the arbitrary-prime conclusion follows from the fixed-stratum and cohomology arguments.

## Relationship to prior work
Assaf–Brandt–Bruce–Chan–Vlad [1] establish the explicit simplicial link, decorated-plane gluing, and nonequivariant homology dimensions (especially Corollary 5.8, Theorems 5.10 and 5.12). Their result supplies essential structure but does not state these transvection traces or their complete cyclic eigenmultiplicities. Assaf and coauthors [2] supply the general tropical comparison and local symmetric-space framework. Capovilla-Searle [3] studies the symplectic \(\pm\)-oriented Steinberg complex and rank bounds, explicitly noting limitations of representation-level splitting; it does not state these character values. The result here is a numerical equivariant refinement, obtained by counting fixed signed vectors, fixed decorated planes, fixed incidences, and the surface cohomology action; previously known dimensions alone cannot determine these traces. The possibility of implicit recovery from general representation formulas remains open as a literature risk.

## Limitations
The formulas hold over \(\mathbb Q\) for prime odd levels and order-\(p\) symplectic transvections only. They give no character values for other conjugacy classes, no decomposition into irreducible \(\mathrm{Sp}_4(\mathbb F_p)\)-representations, no integral torsion description, and no higher-genus analogue. The homotopy wedge splitting in [1] is not assumed equivariant. Existing general representation-theoretic theorems may implicitly imply some or all of these character formulas; no global claim of uniqueness or independent validation is made.
