# Trivial dihedral orientation in five squared-cycle total-cut seven-spheres

## Finding
For each integer \(k\in\{4,5,6,7,8\}\), put \(n=3k+3\) and let \(W_n=C_n^2\) be the square of the \(n\)-cycle. Let \(X_k=\Delta_k^t(W_n)\), the total \(k\)-cut complex whose facets are complements of independent \(k\)-subsets. The graph's natural dihedral action on \(X_k\) is **trivial on the integral orientation class**:
\[
\widetilde H_7(X_k;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z_{\mathrm{triv}}
\quad\text{as a }\mathbb Z[D_n]\text{-module}.
\]
More explicitly, for every rotation \(r^b:i\mapsto i+b\) and every reflection \(r^bs:i\mapsto b-i\), the action on the rank-one \(\widetilde H_7\) is multiplication by \(+1\). Accordingly, for the ordinary topological orbit space,
\[
\widetilde H_j(X_k/D_n;\mathbb Q)\cong
\begin{cases}\mathbb Q,&j=7,\\0,&j\ne7.\end{cases}
\]
This determines new *equivariant* information about five complexes whose underlying nonequivariant homotopy types \(X_k\simeq S^7\) were previously established; no new sphere classification is claimed.

## Assumptions and scope
Vertices are \(0,\ldots,n-1\) modulo \(n\). The edges of \(W_n\) connect vertices at circular distance one or two. A subset \(F\) is a simplex of \(X_k\) if and only if \(V(W_n)\setminus F\) contains \(k\) pairwise nonadjacent vertices. The dihedral action is induced by the permutations \(i\mapsto i+b\) and \(i\mapsto b-i\) for all \(0\le b<n\). The entire assertion is restricted to precisely \((k,n)=(4,15),(5,18),(6,21),(7,24),(8,27)\), and uses the ordinary integral simplicial homology of these finite complexes.

## Proof
**1. Complete, independent face construction.** Let \(M(F)=\sum_{i\in F}2^i\) encode a vertex subset. The standalone `verify_dihedral_cut.cpp` generates all cardinality-\(k\) subsets by increasing index recursion, checks their pairwise circular separation, and retains exactly the independent sets. For each retained set it marks all submasks of its complement. By the definition of a simplicial complex generated by facets, the resulting Boolean face array is exactly the whole face poset, including its empty face. The independent-set numbers equal the independently derived cyclic-gap counts
\[
\frac{n(k+1)(k+2)}6=75,126,196,288,405.
\]
The code scans every one of the \(2^n\) possible subset masks, not a sample.

**2. Integral homology concentration.** On this full augmented face poset apply sequential element matchings at the vertices \(0,1,\ldots,n-1\): at the current vertex, match any as-yet-unmatched face not containing it to its one-vertex enlargement whenever both are still unmatched. The sequential element-matching theorem (Bayer et al., Theorem 7.5) gives an acyclic matching even though unmatched intermediate collections need not be subcomplexes. The independent verifier confirms that the empty face is matched and that the **sole** critical face is the eight-vertex mask `21846`, hence a 7-simplex, for every stated \(k\). An acyclic augmented matching with these critical cells gives a CW model with one zero-cell and one seven-cell and therefore \(X_k\simeq S^7\); in particular \(\widetilde H_7(X_k;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), all other reduced groups zero. This agrees with the earlier published nonequivariant classification, which is not the novelty here.

**3. A signed fixed-face trace formula.** Let \(g\) be any dihedral vertex permutation. Its vertex orbits form a partition \(\mathcal O_g\). A simplex fixed setwise by \(g\) is exactly a union of vertex orbits. Define the computable alternating sum
\[
S(g)=\sum_{J\subseteq\mathcal O_g\, :\,\bigcup J\in X_k}(-1)^{|J|},
\]
including \(J=\varnothing\). On an invariant nonempty simplex \(F\), the sign of the induced permutation of its vertices is \((-1)^{|F|-|J|}\), since its cycles are exactly the selected vertex orbits. Its contribution to the reduced augmented simplicial Lefschetz number is therefore \((-1)^{|F|-1}(-1)^{|F|-|J|}=-(-1)^{|J|}\). The augmented empty face contributes \(-1\), corresponding to the empty selection. Thus
\[
\widetilde L(g)=-S(g).
\]
The integral Lefschetz trace formula and the homology concentration in Step 2 give \(\widetilde L(g)=(-1)^7\operatorname{tr}(g_*\mid\widetilde H_7)=-\operatorname{tr}(g_*)\). Hence **the integer \(S(g)\) equals the sought orientation trace**.

**4. Exhaustive orbit-sum calculation.** For every stated \(k\), the verifier constructs orbit masks for all \(2n\) group elements \(g:i\mapsto \epsilon i+b\), with \(\epsilon\in\{+1,-1\}\) and \(0\le b<n\). It enumerates all subsets of the orbit collection, joins their masks and includes \((-1)^{|J|}\) exactly when the joined mask is a face. Across the five graph sizes, it verifies \(S(g)=1\) for all **210** elements (respectively 30, 36, 42, 48 and 54). Every sum is an exact signed-integer calculation on the complete vertex-orbit partitions of the corresponding finite graphs. Since an automorphism of an integral rank-one homology group is \(+1\) or \(-1\), each is \(+1\), proving the equivariant claim.

**5. Rational homology of the quotient.** Barycentric subdivision makes the simplicial action admissible for the quotient chain description. Over \(\mathbb Q\), averaging over the finite dihedral group identifies rational homology of the quotient with invariant rational homology of the complex. In Step 4 all of \(\widetilde H_7\cong\mathbb Q\) is invariant, and all other reduced groups vanish. This proves the quotient conclusion. \(\square\)

## Verification
Compile `verify_dihedral_cut.cpp` with a C++17 compiler and execute it:
```sh
g++ -std=c++17 -O3 -Wall -Wextra -pedantic verify_dihedral_cut.cpp -o verify_dihedral_cut
./verify_dihedral_cut
```
It prints independent-set, complete-face and matching counts, the unique unmatched 7-face, the number of dihedral elements, and the all-ones character result; successful termination prints `ALL_CHECKS_PASSED`. The largest complete face construction has \(27,332,161\) faces (empty included), on a universe of \(2^{27}\) possible masks. The accompanying `verification_output.txt` records the run that was actually executed. A separately written verifier `verify_signed_15.py` evaluates **signed fixed simplices directly** (rather than orbit sums) for all 30 elements in the smallest case; this independently gives the reduced Lefschetz value \(-1\) and prints `INDEPENDENT_SIGNED_CHECK_OK`. Both verifiers include their own full face construction logic. Their correctness depends on the inspectable code and the standard finite matching/Lefschetz theorems, not on a sampling inference.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer et al. (arXiv:2209.13503, first public 2022-09-27) introduce total cut complexes and Conjecture 5.1 predicting the nonequivariant homotopy types for squared cycles. Shen et al. (arXiv:2510.22574, first public 2025-10-26) prove particular parameter families and compute some homology, without describing the dihedral character here. The published finite result *Five consecutive squared-cycle total-cut complexes are seven-spheres* proves precisely these five underlying sphere homotopy types, but it does not determine whether a reflection acts by \(+1\) or \(-1\), or the rational homology of the orbit space. The present work is a logically distinct equivariant refinement.

A related theorem, *Dihedral orientation characters of bounded-independence spheres on cycles*, identifies characters for \(B_d(C_n^p)\) only in the power-cycle range \(n\ge 2pd\). The present square-cycle parameters have \(n=3k+3<4k=2\cdot2k\) for every \(k\in\{4,5,6,7,8\}\); moreover the present complex is the Alexander dual of the bounded-independence complex, not the same simplicial complex. Therefore neither that theorem nor a nonequivariant sphere classification implies this character without a new argument. Results about **ordinary** cut complexes or three-cut complexes also do not determine this total-cut dihedral module.

## Limitations
The result covers the five explicitly named finite parameter pairs only; it proves no character formula for \(k\ge9\) or other values of \(n\). The quotient claim is about rational homology, not the homotopy type or integral homology of the orbit space. The homotopy classification is independently reproduced but previously known and not claimed as new. The existence of unindexed equivalent equivariant computations cannot be excluded. These computations and comparisons have not undergone an external independent mathematical audit.
