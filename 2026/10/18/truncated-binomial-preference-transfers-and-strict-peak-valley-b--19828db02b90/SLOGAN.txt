In uniform classical parking functions, shifting occupancy between any earlier and later preferred space follows an exact truncated-binomial law, forcing earlier occupancy loads to dominate in stochastic order. For every \(n\ge3\), a strict peak among the first three preferences is more likely than under independent uniform values on \(\{1,\ldots,n+1\}\), while a strict valley is less likely, and the two probabilities sum to exactly twice the independent-uniform benchmark.
