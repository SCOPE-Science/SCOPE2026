**Correctness — PASS.** The odd-part definition gives an exact recursion for the number of ones in every dyadic-length factor. Odd-indexed symbols alternate, so the edge-transition count is determined by the endpoint pair; these identities reconstruct every bigram count from the ones count and the two endpoints. The three specified adjacent-bit pairs and four offsets produce all twelve classes for every dyadic length at least sixteen, while twelve explicit starts settle length eight. The packaged verifier reconstructs independent finite instances and tests every witness. No finite extrapolation is used in the proof.

**Originality — PASS relative to inspected literature.** The closest paperfolding primary text (Madill–Rampersad, arXiv:1208.2856) gives one-abelian rather than two-abelian complexity. The inspected Parreau–Rigo–Rowland–Vandomme text (arXiv:1405.3532) proves two-abelian statements for Thue–Morse and period-doubling, not paperfolding; Greinecker (arXiv:1404.3906) likewise treats Thue–Morse. Searches for the paperfolding dyadic two-abelian value did not locate an equivalent formula. Absence of an indexed match is not a uniqueness proof, and an unindexed or implicitly derivable specialization is the remaining risk.

**Value — PASS.** The paperfolding word is a canonical automatic sequence, and refining abelian classes by ordered adjacent pairs is a natural reconstruction invariant. Its one-abelian complexity has exactly three classes at dyadic lengths, whereas the theorem completely resolves the finer, nontrivially constrained two-abelian profiles, showing a persistent four-to-one refinement along every dyadic scale. This is an exact infinite-family structural fact, not an isolated numerical increment.

**Limitations.** Nondyadic factor lengths and generalized paperfolding words are not classified. No independent audit has been performed.
