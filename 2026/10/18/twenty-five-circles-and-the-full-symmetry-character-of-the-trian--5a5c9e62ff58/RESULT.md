# Twenty-five circles and the full symmetry character of the triangular-prism strong-Morse complex

## Finding
Let \(P=C_3\square K_2\) denote the **one-dimensional** triangular-prism graph, with both triangular cycles unfilled. Its full strong discrete-Morse matching complex in the sense of Knudson and Owens-White has integral homotopy type
\[
\mathcal{SM}(P)\simeq \bigvee^{25}S^1.
\]
Consequently \(\pi_1(\mathcal{SM}(P))\cong F_{25}\), \(\widetilde H_1(\mathcal{SM}(P);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{25}\), and all other reduced integral homology groups vanish.

The full graph automorphism group is \(G\cong S_3\times C_2\), acting by permuting the two triangular layers simultaneously and optionally interchanging them. Its rational first-homology module is
\[
H_1(\mathcal{SM}(P);\mathbb Q)\cong 2\,\mathbb Q[G]\oplus\varepsilon,
\]
where \(\varepsilon\) is the one-dimensional sign character of \(S_3\), with the layer-interchange factor acting trivially. Thus the \(G\)-fixed subspace is two-dimensional. This representation statement is over \(\mathbb Q\); no integral splitting into these summands is asserted.

## Assumptions and scope
The vertices of \(P\) are \(\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\), with the nine edges of the triangles \(012\), \(345\) and the connecting edges \(03,14,25\). The object is the full strong-Morse complex, **not** the full ordinary Morse complex and **not** the pure strong-Morse subcomplex.

For a total order \(\prec\) of the six graph vertices, a vertex \(v\) whose set of earlier neighbors is the singleton \(\{u\}\) is paired with the incident edge \(\{u,v\}\); no pair is contributed by \(v\) when it has zero or at least two earlier neighbors. Because the prism is one-dimensional, the descending link is a cone precisely when it consists of one vertex. Each total order therefore yields a strong discrete-Morse matching. The simplicial complex \(\mathcal{SM}(P)\) is generated by these matchings: its simplicial vertices are directed incidences \((v,u)\) with \(\{u,v\}\) an edge of \(P\), and its faces are all subsets of the matching sets realized by at least one total order.

There are exactly \(6!=720\) vertex orderings. All indexing and certificates use increasing lexicographic order on the eighteen incidences, with an incidence encoded as \((v,u)\), meaning the vertex \(v\) is paired with its edge \(\{u,v\}\). For a face encoded by an integer, bit \(i\) indicates the presence of incidence \(i\). The empty face is represented by integer zero and is excluded from the discrete-Morse matching on the nonempty face poset.

## Proof
**Exhaustive face enumeration.** Enumerate the 720 orders. For each order and each vertex, compute its earlier-neighbor set, include exactly the incidences described above, and then take all subsets of the resulting matching. The 720 generated matchings have exactly 36 inclusion-maximal members: 12 are one-dimensional simplices, and 24 are two-dimensional simplices. The resulting full nonempty face vector is exactly
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(18,66,24),\qquad \sum_i f_i=108.
\]
No simplices of dimension three or higher occur. The verifier reconstructs every face from the defining orders, without treating the recorded facet list as an axiom.

**Acyclic finite discrete-Morse matching.** Iterate over incidence bits \(i=0,\ldots,17\). At each bit, process currently unmatched nonempty faces not containing \(i\), in increasing order of face cardinality and then bit-mask value, pairing \(\sigma\) with \(\sigma\cup\{i\}\) whenever both remain unmatched. The resulting list is explicitly stored in `prism_morse_certificate.json`; there are 41 disjoint covering pairs. The 108-element nonempty face poset has exactly 204 Hasse covering relations. Orient each unmatched covering relation downward and each matched relation upward. A complete topological sort exhausts **all 108 vertices** of this directed Hasse graph, proving it has no directed cycle. The unmatched critical faces are exactly one vertex and 25 edges, with no critical triangle.

By the discrete-Morse CW-equivalence theorem, the complex has the homotopy type of a CW complex with one zero-cell, 25 one-cells and no cells in higher dimensions. Every one-cell attaches its two endpoints to the unique zero-cell. The CW complex is therefore the wedge of 25 circles. This supplies an **integral homotopy proof**, not an extrapolation from homology ranks.

**Full rational symmetry character.** Every graph automorphism preserves the set of strong matchings generated by total orders, so it acts simplicially on the enumerated complex. Write elements of \(G\) as a simultaneous permutation \(\sigma\in S_3\) of the two layers, followed optionally by the layer swap. The automorphism acts on incidence \((v,u)\) as \((g(v),g(u))\). For each degree, a fixed oriented simplex contributes the sign of its induced incidence permutation to the corresponding chain trace. Alternating these traces and subtracting from the one-dimensional trivial degree-zero homology yields the trace on \(H_1\), since higher rational homology vanishes by the preceding homotopy proof.

The six conjugacy-class traces are: identity without swap 25; identity with swap 1; either nonidentity three-cycle class 1; either transposition class \(-1\). Thus, for every \((\sigma,t)\in S_3\times C_2\),
\[
\operatorname{tr}\bigl((\sigma,t)\mid H_1\bigr)
=24\,\mathbf 1_{(\sigma,t)=e}+\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma).
\]
The character on the right is precisely the character of \(2\,\mathbb Q[G]\oplus\varepsilon\). Rational representations of finite groups are semisimple, and their characters determine their isomorphism classes. Averaging the character over the twelve group elements gives exactly two invariant dimensions.

## Verification
The self-contained `verify_topology.py` reconstructs all 720 vertex-order matchings and all 108 nonempty faces, independently regenerates the 41 incidence-toggle covering pairs, compares them bit-for-bit with `prism_morse_certificate.json`, checks every one of the 204 Hasse covers, and proves acyclicity by a full topological sort. Output: `topology_output.txt`.

The independent `verify_representation.py` reconstructs the faces without using the first verifier or the matching certificate. It computes the simplicial boundary ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\) as 17 and 24 in degrees one and two, giving the reduced Betti vector \((0,25,0)\); it also enumerates all twelve graph automorphisms and calculates the signed Lefschetz trace for each. Its exact output is `representation_output.txt`.

Both scripts require only Python 3.10 or later, have no external dependencies and may be run from the directory containing their files with `python verify_topology.py` and `python verify_representation.py`. The proof of the integral homotopy type uses acyclic discrete Morse matching, **not** the mod-two computation.

## Relationship to prior work
Knudson and Owens-White, *Complexes of strong discrete Morse matchings* (first public September 6, 2026), introduce the strong matching complex, classify the path/star/simplex examples, study the pure cycle complex and tabulate full cycle-complex homology. Their discussion and results do not give this prism computation.

The published result *The triangular-prism Morse complex is a wedge of sixty-four four-spheres* treats the **ordinary** Morse complex \(\mathcal M(P)\), with 1,727 nonempty simplices and a wedge of 64 four-spheres. Ordinary and strong Morse matching complexes are distinct: the latter has only 108 nonempty simplices here and is a 25-circle wedge. Neither ordinary Morse homology nor its homotopy type determines the restricted strong-Morse subcomplex or its representation. A general equivalence between the two constructions does not hold. Searches using strong discrete-Morse, rooted forest, triangular prism, graph prism, and symmetry terminology produced no equivalent or dominating prism strong-Morse theorem in the inspected sources.

## Limitations
The theorem is for the indicated **one-dimensional graph** and its full strong-Morse complex only; it does not classify every prism graph, every cubic graph, or the pure strong-Morse complex. The equivariant decomposition is rational, not an asserted direct sum over the integers. Enumeration is exhaustive **only** for the finite six-vertex prism; there is no claim about an infinite graph family. No independent external scientific audit has been performed. Other unindexed computations or differently named equivalent constructions remain a literature-overlap risk.
