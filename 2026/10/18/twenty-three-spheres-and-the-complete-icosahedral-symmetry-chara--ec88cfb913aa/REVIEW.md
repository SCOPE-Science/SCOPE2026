# Review of the icosahedral two-independence homotopy and symmetry result

## Correctness

**PASS.** The graph is given by an explicit 30-edge list and independently by a two-pentagon construction. The complete 323-face complex has face vector \((12,66,140,105)\). A deterministic 151-pair partial matching leaves precisely one zero-cell and twenty three-cells; an exact topological sort on all 972 oriented Hasse covers certifies acyclicity. Standard discrete Morse theory then gives \(\bigvee^{20}S^3\) over the integers. The complete 120-element automorphism group and the signed Hopf-trace sums are exhaustively checked. Standard \(A_5\) character reduction gives the rational representation. The mod-two chain-rank calculation is independent corroboration rather than a substitute for the integral homotopy proof.

## Originality

**PASS with bibliographic uncertainty.** Deshpande–Singh (2020) provide exact higher-independence classifications for graph families not including the icosahedron. Sawant (2026) treats trees and Ghosh–Selvaraja (2025) treats block graphs and constrained constructions. Previously reported results for the icosahedron's three-clique-free, three-cut, matching, and neighborhood complexes concern different face predicates. A two-independence homotopy result for the Petersen graph cannot imply the icosahedron case. Searches also considered dissociation, three-vertex-path-free, connected-triple-hypergraph and equivalent terminology, together with domination by broader theorems. No equal or dominating prior result was located; indexing incompleteness remains possible. The full \(A_5\times C_2\) rational symmetry character is not entailed by a nonequivariant homotopy classification.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The precise geometric object is the regular icosahedral graph, and the invariant is its natural two-independence homotopy and full automorphism homology character. A 323-face, pure three-dimensional complex has an integral twenty-sphere wedge, while the icosahedral antipode splits its rational homology into unequal 15- and 5-dimensional eigenspaces and a one-dimensional full-group invariant subspace. These features are independent of face enumeration and furnish a useful benchmark for equivariant topology of nonchordal graph complexes. No statement for a general graph family or integral lattice decomposition is claimed.
