# Twist-dependent Cartan matrices in modular split metacyclic group algebras

## Finding
Let \(p\) be an odd prime, \(m>1\) a divisor of \(p-1\), \(r\in\mathbb F_p^\times\) an element of order \(m\), \(q=p^f\) with \(f\ge1\), and \(a\in\mathbb F_q^\times\). Define the twisted algebra
\[
A(a)=\mathbb F_q\langle x,y\rangle/
(x^p-1,\ y^m-a,\ yx-x^r y),
\]
where the intended algebra has the normal-form basis \(\{x^i y^j:0\le i<p,\ 0\le j<m\}\). Every normalized scalar-twisted group algebra of the faithful split metacyclic group \(C_p\rtimes_r C_m\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) is isomorphic to some \(A(a)\), with the twist parameter defined modulo \((\mathbb F_q^\times)^m\).

Put
\[
c=a^{(q-1)/m}\in\mu_m(\mathbb F_q),\qquad d=\operatorname{ord}(c),\qquad h=m/d.
\]
Then the following are simultaneously exact:

1. The Jacobson radical is \(J=(x-1)A(a)=A(a)(x-1)\), and \(J^k=(x-1)^k A(a)\) for \(0\le k\le p\). Its nilpotency index is exactly \(p\), and \(\dim_{\mathbb F_q}J^k=m(p-k)\).
2. The semisimple quotient is \(A(a)/J\cong\mathbb F_{q^d}^{\,h}\). Thus the algebra has exactly \(h\) isomorphism classes of simple left modules, each of \(\mathbb F_q\)-dimension \(d\).
3. The indecomposable projective covers \(P_0,\ldots,P_{h-1}\) are uniserial of composition length \(p\). They can be indexed cyclically such that their successive radical factors are \(S_j,S_{j+1},\ldots,S_{j+p-1}\), subscripts modulo \(h\).
4. Consequently, with the convention \(C_{ij}=[P_j:S_i]\), the Cartan matrix is
\[
C=I_h+\frac{p-1}{h}\,\mathbf1_h\mathbf1_h^{\mathsf T}.
\]
It has determinant \(p\) and eigenvalues \(p\) (once) and \(1\) (\(h-1\) times), including the one-by-one case \(h=1\). In particular the twist changes the number of simple types and the Cartan matrix, though its Cartan determinant stays fixed.

Every divisor \(h\mid m\) occurs for an appropriate twist class \(a\), so this family realizes exactly \(\tau(m)\) different numbers of simples (and therefore at least \(\tau(m)\) distinct Morita-equivalence types), where \(\tau(m)\) is the positive-divisor count.

## Assumptions and scope
All statements concern finite fields of characteristic exactly \(p\), including nonsplit twist classes and arbitrary extension degree \(f\). The case \(m=1\) is excluded from the faithful nonabelian family but would give the evident local cyclic group algebra. No statement addresses arbitrary metacyclic groups or coefficient characteristic different from \(p\). The Cartan matrix records composition-factor multiplicities over \(\mathbb F_q\), not dimensions of Hom-spaces or matrices after extending scalars.

## Proof
**Normal form and cocycles.** Let \(G=C_p\rtimes_r C_m\). As \(p\nmid |\mathbb F_q^\times|\), group cohomology of \(C_p\) with these trivial coefficients vanishes in positive degrees. Inflation in the Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence identifies \(H^2(G,\mathbb F_q^\times)\) with \(H^2(C_m,\mathbb F_q^\times)\cong\mathbb F_q^\times/(\mathbb F_q^\times)^m\). Representatives may therefore be chosen with the displayed relations \(x^p=1\), \(y^m=a\), and \(yxy^{-1}=x^r\); see the specific cohomology theorem in Bhowmick–de la Cruz–Martínez-Moro. The quotient is the skew-crossed product of \(\mathbb F_q[C_p]\) by \(C_m\); its stated \(pm\) normal forms are linearly independent by construction and its multiplication is associative. The parameter \(a\) changes by an \(m\)-th power when \(y\) is rescaled.

**Radical and its powers.** Write \(t=x-1\). In characteristic \(p\), \(t^p=x^p-1=0\). Conjugating by \(y\) gives \(y t y^{-1}=(1+t)^r-1=t\,u(t)\), where \(u(0)=r\ne0\). Hence \(u(t)\) is a unit of \(\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^p)\); conjugation preserves each ideal \((t^k)\). Therefore \(J_0=tA=At\) is two-sided and \(J_0^k=t^kA\), of vector-space dimension \(m(p-k)\) for \(0\le k\le p\). The quotient by \(J_0\) is \(B=\mathbb F_q[y]/(y^m-a)\). Its defining polynomial is squarefree since \(p\nmid m\) and \(a\ne0\); hence \(B\) is semisimple. The ideal \(J_0\) is nilpotent, so \(J_0\subseteq\operatorname{Jac}(A)\), while semisimplicity of \(A/J_0\) forces the reverse inclusion. Thus \(J=J_0\) exactly.

**Twist arithmetic and simple factors.** Because \(m\mid p-1\mid q-1\), every \(m\)-th root of unity belongs to \(\mathbb F_q\). For any root \(\beta\) of \(Y^m-a\) in an algebraic closure,
\[
\beta^q=\beta\,(\beta^m)^{(q-1)/m}=c\beta.
\]
The Frobenius orbit of \(\beta\) consequently has length precisely \(d=\operatorname{ord}(c)\). The \(m\) roots form \(\beta\mu_m\), and Frobenius partitions them into \(h=m/d\) orbits of length \(d\). Therefore \(B\cong\mathbb F_{q^d}^{\,h}\), giving precisely \(h\) simple modules \(S_j\), each of dimension \(d\).

**Projective radical layers.** The subalgebra generated by \(y\) is an embedded copy of \(B\). Its primitive idempotents \(e_0,\dots,e_{h-1}\) sum to \(1\) and remain primitive in \(A\) because their reductions are primitive in \(A/J\). Set \(P_j=Ae_j\). Since \(A=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{p-1}t^k B\) as an \(\mathbb F_q\)-space, each \(J^kP_j/J^{k+1}P_j\) has dimension \(d\), represented by \(t^kB e_j\). Modulo \(J^{k+1}P_j\), the conjugation formula yields \(y t^k\equiv r^k t^k y\). Thus the action of \(y\) on this layer is obtained from its action on \(Be_j\) by multiplying the root parameter by \(r^k\). Multiplication by \(r\), a generator of \(\mu_m\), acts transitively on the \(h\) Frobenius orbits, with cycle length \(h\). Labeling the corresponding simples cyclically, the layer at depth \(k\) is \(S_{j+k\bmod h}\). It is simple, and there are exactly \(p\) nonzero layers. Each \(J^kP_j\) is cyclic as a left \(A\)-module, with its unique maximal submodule \(J^{k+1}P_j\), so these projectives are uniserial.

**Cartan matrix and attainability.** Because \(h\mid m\mid p-1\), the residues \(j,j+1,\dots,j+p-1\) modulo \(h\) consist of \((p-1)/h\) complete cycles and one extra occurrence of \(j\). Hence \(C_{ij}=(p-1)/h+\delta_{ij}\). The vector \(\mathbf1_h\) has eigenvalue \(1+h(p-1)/h=p\), and its orthogonal complement has eigenvalue \(1\), proving the claimed spectrum and determinant. Finally, as \(a\) runs through \(\mathbb F_q^\times\), the map \(a\mapsto a^{(q-1)/m}\) surjects onto the cyclic group \(\mu_m\). Every order \(d\mid m\) occurs, hence so does every \(h=m/d\).

## Verification
The included standalone `verify.py` uses exact arithmetic over the prime fields \(\mathbb F_3\), \(\mathbb F_5\), and \(\mathbb F_7\). It factors \(Y^m-a\) by trial division; independently recovers \(d\), \(h\), the permutation of irreducible factors under root scaling, and the full layer-count Cartan matrix. For every tested algebra of dimension at most \(20\), it also checks associativity on all ordered triples of basis elements using explicit multiplication in the \(t^k y^j\) basis. Twelve parameter cases, including all four values \(h=6,3,2,1\) for \((p,m)=(7,6)\), pass. Run `python3 verify.py` to reproduce them. These finite checks supplement, rather than replace, the all-field proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Bhowmick, de la Cruz, and Martínez-Moro (2026, arXiv:2603.22185v1) calculate scalar cocycle classes and the Wedderburn decomposition in coefficient characteristic *different* from \(p\). Their Theorem 2.3 supplies the cocycle parametrization used here; their semisimple decomposition does not determine a Jacobson radical or Cartan matrix when the characteristic equals \(p\). Margolis and Schnabel (2019/2020, arXiv:1902.04281) develop twisted group-ring isomorphism methods and Yamazaki covers, rather than this modular, twist-dependent radical series. Wallace (1970, Theorem 3.4), following Passman, already gives a general radical-theoretic mechanism for twisted group rings of polycyclic groups; the identification of \(J\) in this special finite group is therefore not asserted to be new. Standard cyclic-defect and Brauer-tree theory provides important context for the untwisted split-field case; here the explicit nonsplit finite-field descent, uniserial layer permutation, and cocycle-order-dependent Cartan matrices are computed directly. The formula is a specialized structural result, not a claim that the general representation theory of cyclic-defect algebras is new.

## Limitations
The result does not classify all algebra isomorphism classes of \(A(a)\), all indecomposable modules, or arbitrary crossed products. Although the displayed matrix is derived without classification tables, its split specializations are compatible with classical cyclic-defect/Brauer-star structure. An unindexed explicit treatment of the same twisted modular family could subsume the formula; searches and source comparisons reduce but cannot eliminate this originality risk.
