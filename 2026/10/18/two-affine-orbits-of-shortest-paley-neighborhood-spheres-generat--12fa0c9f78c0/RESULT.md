# Two affine orbits of shortest Paley neighborhood spheres generate integral second homology

## Finding
Let \(P_{13}\) be the Paley graph on \(\mathbb F_{13}\), with adjacency differences \(Q=\{1,3,4,9,10,12\}\), and let \(K=\mathcal N(P_{13})\) be its ordinary **open** Lovász neighborhood complex. A four-vertex subset \(W\) is a minimal induced carrier of nonzero ambient integral second homology if and only if \(K[W]\) is the boundary of a tetrahedron. Exactly **104** subsets have this property, and each of their fundamental classes has nonzero image in \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\).

The natural order-\(78\) affine group \(G=\mathbb F_{13}\rtimes Q\) partitions these carriers into three orbits of sizes \(26,39,39\), represented by \(\{0,1,3,9\}\), \(\{0,1,3,11\}\), and \(\{0,1,6,8\}\), respectively. Most notably, **each** of the two different \(39\)-element orbits, by itself, generates the entire integral group
\[
H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{25}.
\]
The \(26\)-element orbit alone does not generate this group: its images span dimension \(24\) modulo \(2\).

## Assumptions and scope
All simplicial chains use the increasing vertex orientation on the labels \(0,1,\ldots,12\), with the usual alternating boundary. The neighborhood complex contains exactly those nonempty subsets \(\sigma\) for which \(\sigma\subseteq v+Q\) for some \(v\in\mathbb F_{13}\); the vertex itself is not included in its open neighborhood. For a four-set \(W\), the chain \(z_W=\partial[W]\) is the alternating boundary of the abstract tetrahedron with vertex set \(W\), considered as a two-chain in \(K\) when its four triangular faces are present. An induced carrier means an inclusion-induced nonzero class in ambient homology. The theorem is for the order-thirteen graph, integral coefficients, and the indicated affine subgroup; no assertion of a family-wide formula or of the full graph automorphism group is needed.

## Proof
**Enumeration and the sharp support threshold.** The thirteen six-vertex facets \(v+Q\) have exactly the face vector
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4,f_5)=(13,78,221,195,78,13).
\]
For every four-subset \(W\), test whether all four three-subsets are faces but \(W\) itself is not. Precisely \(104\) subsets pass, and their induced complexes are tetrahedral boundaries, each isomorphic to \(S^2\). A complex on at most three vertices cannot have nonzero second reduced homology. On four vertices, a nonzero second cycle requires all four triangles, and it survives in induced homology exactly when the four-vertex simplex is absent. This proves the complete four-vertex classification and minimality, once essentiality is checked.

**Essentiality of every carrier.** Integer simplex boundaries reduce modulo \(2\). The boundary \(\partial_3:C_3(K;\mathbb F_2)\to C_2(K;\mathbb F_2)\) has rank \(130\), and adjoining the column \(z_W\) increases that rank to \(131\) for each of the \(104\) four-sets above. Since \(z_W\) is a cycle, it is not an ambient boundary even modulo \(2\), hence it represents a nonzero integral class. The complete calculation is regenerated by the attached standalone verifier.

**Orbits.** The maps \(x\mapsto ax+b\), with \(a\in Q\) and \(b\in\mathbb F_{13}\), preserve quadratic-residue adjacency and the neighborhood complex. Applying all \(78\) maps to the lexicographically first remaining carrier yields, in order, the three disjoint orbits with representatives \(\{0,1,3,9\}\), \(\{0,1,3,11\}\), \(\{0,1,6,8\}\) and sizes \(26,39,39\). Their total size is \(104\), so the classification is exhaustive.

**Characteristic-zero dimensions.** In degree two, the mod-two boundary rank is \(66\). Because the complete one-skeleton has \(78\) edges and its first boundary has rank \(12\), the characteristic-zero rank cannot exceed \(66\), and it therefore equals \(66\). The mod-two ranks of the next two boundaries are \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_3=130\) and \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_4=65\). Characteristic-zero ranks are at least the corresponding mod-two ranks. Moreover \(\partial_3\partial_4=0\), so \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\partial_3\le195-65=130\). It follows that \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\partial_3=130\), giving
\[
\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_2(K;\mathbb Q)=221-66-130=25.
\]

**Integral, orbitwise generation.** Choose the star at vertex \(0\) as a spanning tree of the complete one-skeleton. Projection of integral one-cycles to its \(66\) non-tree edge coordinates is a lattice isomorphism. In this basis, the triangle-boundary matrix has a deterministically selected \(66\)-column minor with exact determinant \(-1\). Consequently, these \(66\) triangle columns form an integral basis of the one-cycle lattice, and the other \(155\) triangle coordinates give an integral basis for \(\ker\partial_2\).

In that \(155\)-coordinate basis, independently for either \(39\)-element orbit, take all tetrahedral boundary columns belonging to \(K\), followed by the \(39\) missing-tetrahedron boundary columns of that orbit. Greedy elimination modulo \(2\) selects exactly \(155\) linearly independent integer columns: \(130\) genuine three-boundaries and \(25\) missing-tetrahedron cycles. Direct fraction-free integer elimination gives determinant \(-1\) for **each** selected square matrix. Thus each such column set is an integral basis of \(\ker\partial_2\). Because the rational rank of \(\partial_3\) is exactly \(130\), every remaining genuine three-boundary is an integral combination of the chosen \(130\) boundary basis vectors (its coordinates in the full unimodular basis are integers and the last \(25\) rational coordinates vanish). Hence \(\operatorname{im}\partial_3\) is saturated of rank \(130\), and its quotient is \(\mathbb Z^{25}\), generated by the selected spheres from either orbit.

Finally, the \(26\)-element orbit, together with all genuine three-boundaries, has rank only \(154\) over \(\mathbb F_2\), rather than \(155\); its images therefore span just \(24\) of the \(25\) ambient mod-two dimensions, so it cannot generate integral second homology.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with Python 3, using only the standard library. The program reconstructs all \(598\) nonempty faces; all \(104\) tetrahedral-boundary carriers; their affine orbits; the boundary ranks \((66,130,65)\) in dimensions \((2,3,4)\); and all \(104\) nonzero images in mod-two second homology. It checks the three orbitwise mod-two spans \((24,25,25)\), constructs the star-tree coordinate lattice, and calculates three exact determinants, each \(-1\): one \(66\)-column pivot minor and one \(155\)-column generator minor for each \(39\)-orbit. All matrices and columns are reconstructed from the simplicial definition and orientation convention. No stored computer-algebra status or external certificate is required.

## Relationship to prior work
The global homotopy equivalence \(\mathcal N(P_{13})\simeq\bigvee^{25}S^2\) was obtained in two earlier public computational analyses, *The neighborhood complex of the order-thirteen Paley graph is a wedge of twenty-five two-spheres* (record `3f8cbbf12bfc`) and *The thirteen-vertex Paley neighborhood complex is a wedge of twenty-five two-spheres* (record `0bd4f768ad7e`). This global homotopy result is **not** claimed as new. Those inspected proofs give discrete-Morse matchings and homology dimensions, but not the present \(104\)-carrier classification, three affine orbits, or unimodular proof that either \(39\)-orbit separately generates the integral homology.

Matsushita develops fundamental-group methods for neighborhood complexes, while Shukla classifies neighborhood complexes of graphs of maximum degree at most three and four-regular circulants. Neither covers the local integral generator lattice for this six-regular Paley graph. The cyclic-arc nerve classification of Adamaszek and collaborators treats consecutive-interval facets rather than the Paley quadratic-residue neighborhoods, and it does not imply this carrier classification.

## Limitations
No description of the relations among all \(104\) sphere generators, of the full automorphism representation, or of analogous support patterns in other Paley graphs is claimed. The determinant certificates prove exact integral generation for the two specified affine orbits, not an arbitrary orbit or complex. Targeted prior-work comparisons cannot exclude an equivalent unpublished or poorly indexed computation. Scientific review here is not independent validation.
