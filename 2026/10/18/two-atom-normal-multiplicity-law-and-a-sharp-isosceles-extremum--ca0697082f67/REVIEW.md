## Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources, with explicit historical uncertainty

Domokos–Lángi (arXiv:1406.0813, 2014) poses/reviews the average-normal problem but does not give the precise distribution for obtuse triangles. Nasonov–Panina–Siersma (arXiv:2406.01773, Section 6, Proposition 9) contains the highly relevant qualitative result: mean six iff nonobtuse, between four and six if obtuse. Their statement does not provide the exact two-atom probabilities, the complete two-parameter shape formula, or the sharp equality and stability law at fixed obtuse angle. Related published normal-multiplicity computations for regular polygons and ellipses cannot logically imply an obtuse-triangle formula without additional geometry. Relevant work of Hann (1993) and Hug (1995) could not be checked in full, so historical priority is not certified.

The family is the entire moduli space of Euclidean triangles rather than an arbitrary special specimen. The exact probabilities refine a specific qualitative gap in the modern literature on Santaló's average-normal invariant and give a precise geometric location for the high-multiplicity region. The fixed-angle maximizer is unique and has a quantified nonnegative deficit, identifying a natural extremizer. The result is not an algorithmic numerical estimate or a restatement of the earlier six-versus-less-than-six bound.

## Limits

The equality and limiting claims are for nondegenerate triangles and fixed strictly obtuse angle, not degenerating boundaries themselves. The rational tests do not establish the continuum statement independently of the supplied proof. Uninspected historical sources remain a priority risk.
