# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
Projection along any nonhorizontal viewing direction into the base plane is linearly equivalent to the orthogonal shadow. The two bases reduce to a polygon and a positive homothetic copy centered at an explicitly distributed random point. For generic center locations inside, containment forces \(n\) shadow vertices; for exterior centers, the two support rays and two bridge edges force exactly \(n+2\). The ratio of spherical coordinates is planar Cauchy and integrates to the solid-angle probability. Its derivative is strictly negative for every positive aspect, and triangulating a regular base yields the explicit arctangent expression. All quantifiers, exceptional measure-zero directions, and limiting regimes were considered. A self-contained checker supports but does not substitute for the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to accessible evidence
The 2022 homothetic-hull article gives the crucial containment equivalence as established prior art, not as a new contribution. Its volume and rigidity theorems do not state the polygon-silhouette dichotomy, solid-angle law, or one-frequency identification. The 2012 polyhedral-shadow treatment concerns different polyhedra and statistics; the random Poisson-polytope silhouette paper addresses random bodies. Relevant indexed frustum and polyhedral shadow results did not directly cover the all-polygon deterministic dichotomy and distribution. Historical priority remains provisional: elementary homothety properties could appear in older geometric optics or computational geometry references.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The output is a complete random-silhouette classification for every polygonal homothetic frustum, including asymmetrical bases, not a fit to an arbitrary special case. It rules out every intermediate silhouette complexity, supplies an exactly computable stochastic-geometric law (and closed regular-base formula), and reveals a uniquely identifiable geometric combination and a precise two-parameter nonidentifiability. These are useful structural benchmarks for random-view polyhedral geometry and silhouette-based shape inference.

## Limits and risks
No claim of newness for planar containment equivalence or elementary solid-angle triangle formulas. The result is for uniform orthogonal directions and strict \(0<t<1\), not the collapsed pyramid or nonhomothetic frusta. No finite-sample precision bounds or reconstruction of arbitrary unknown bases. Unindexed historical literature might cover the dichotomy in another language.
