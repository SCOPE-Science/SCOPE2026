# Two base-scheme orbits of all-smooth plane-filling quintic pencils over \(\mathbf F_3\)

## Finding
Let \(V\) be the \(8\)-dimensional \(\mathbf F_3\)-vector space of ternary quintics vanishing at all \(13\) rational points of \(\mathbf P^2_{\mathbf F_3}\). A *rationally all-smooth pencil* is a \(2\)-dimensional vector subspace \(W\subset V\) for which each of the four curves represented by \(\mathbf P(W)(\mathbf F_3)\) is geometrically smooth.

There are **exactly \(2016\) all-smooth pencils** among the \(896260\) two-dimensional subspaces of \(V\). The natural action of \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_3)\), of order \(5616\), has exactly **two orbits** of these pencils, of sizes \(1872\) and \(144\). Their stabilizer orders are respectively \(3\) and \(39\).

Each pencil has \(13\) simple rational base points, namely every point of \(\mathbf P^2(\mathbf F_3)\). Its base scheme has degree \(25\), with the additional degree-\(12\) portion distributed as follows:

| Pencil orbit | Pencils | Remaining base scheme over \(\mathbf F_3\) |
|---|---:|---|
| Reduced | 1872 | One geometrically reduced closed point of degree \(12\) |
| Nonreduced | 144 | One closed point of degree \(3\), with local intersection multiplicity \(4\) at each of its three geometric conjugates |

There is **no** all-smooth projective linear system of dimension at least \(2\) contained in \(\mathbf P(V)\). Thus these pencils have the maximal possible projective dimension among all-smooth linear systems within the plane-filling quintics over \(\mathbf F_3\).

## Assumptions and scope
Work only over \(\mathbf F_3\) and its algebraic closure. All-smooth means *all four* \(\mathbf F_3\)-rational members are geometrically smooth; it does **not** mean every member over every field extension is smooth. Count vector subspaces, not ordered generator pairs. Projective equivalence is equivalence under the complete \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_3)\) action. A closed point of degree \(d\) is one Frobenius orbit of \(d\) geometric points.

With \(\mathbf X=(X,Y,Z)^T\), write
\[
F_A(\mathbf X)=\det\bigl(\mathbf X,\mathbf X^{[3]},A\mathbf X\bigr),\qquad A\in M_3(\mathbf F_3).
\]
Here \(\mathbf X^{[3]}=(X^3,Y^3,Z^3)^T\). The kernel of the map \(A\mapsto F_A\) is the scalar matrices, so \(V\cong M_3(\mathbf F_3)/(\mathbf F_3 I)\), of dimension \(8\). In the quotient gauge \(a_{00}=0\), two representatives are:
\[
A_{r}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&1\\1&1&0\\2&1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
B_{r}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\0&1&1\\1&1&0\end{pmatrix};
\]
\[
A_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\0&1&1\\1&1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
B_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&1\\1&2&2\\1&2&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The pencils \(\langle F_{A_r},F_{B_r}\rangle\) and \(\langle F_{A_n},F_{B_n}\rangle\) represent the reduced and nonreduced orbits, respectively. Matrices in the second pair commute; matrices in the first pair do not.

## Proof
**Linear system and singularity criterion.** The homogeneous ideal of the \(13\) rational points is generated by the three Frobenius bilinears \(X^3Y-XY^3\), \(Y^3Z-YZ^3\), \(Z^3X-ZX^3\). Multiplying by linear forms gives the \(8\)-dimensional quintic component with one linear syzygy. These are precisely the determinants \(F_A\), with scalar matrices in the kernel. For \(p\in\mathbf P^2(\mathbf F_3)\) one has \(p^{[3]}=p\), and the differential is
\[
dF_A(p)(v)=\det(v,p,Ap).
\]
Consequently, the curve \(F_A=0\) is singular at \(p\) exactly when \(Ap\) is proportional to \(p\). Thus a geometrically smooth member necessarily has no rational eigenvector. Conversely, a direct finite orbit computation finds \(576\) projective classes of forms with no rational eigenvector in two \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_3)\) orbits (sizes \(432,144\)); explicit Jacobian Gröbner calculations on all three standard affine charts prove geometric smoothness of both representatives. Invariance then establishes sufficiency for every one of the \(576\) forms.

**Pencil enumeration.** Represent each nonzero coset of \(M_3(\mathbf F_3)/(\mathbf F_3 I)\) by its eight residual entries, normalized so the first nonzero entry equals \(1\). There are \(3280\) projective quintic forms, of which \(576\) are smooth. For linearly independent representatives \(a,b\), the four rational pencil members are \(a,b,a+b,a-b\), each re-normalized. An exhaustive exact count gives \(2016\) distinct four-element pencils. Independently, a C++17 computation visits *every* \(8\)-coordinate \(2\)-dimensional RREF subspace, totaling \(896260\), and obtains exactly \(2016\) with all four members satisfying the no-rational-eigenvector criterion. Because the preceding two-orbit Gröbner test proves this criterion equivalent to absolute geometric smoothness, both are exact enumerations, not sampling.

**Projective orbits.** Matrix conjugation by \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_3)\) acts on quintics via coordinate change. Four explicitly defined projective matrices in the supplied checker generate all \(5616\) projective transformations (proved by group closure); orbit traversal partitions the \(2016\) pencils into orbits of \(1872\) and \(144\). The displayed matrices are representatives.

**Base schemes.** Two quintics in a qualifying pencil are smooth plane curves of degree \(5\) and cannot share an irreducible component: if they did, smoothness would force both entire degree-five curves to agree geometrically, contradicting independent coefficients. Bézout gives intersection length \(25\). At any rational point \(p\), the two gradients are independent: if they were dependent, some nonzero pencil member would have vanishing gradient at \(p\), contradicting smoothness. Hence the \(13\) rational base points are transverse and contribute total length \(13\).

For each orbit representative, compute the scheme ideal \((F_A,F_B)\) on the three standard affine charts. On \(X=1\), its monic elimination polynomial in \(Z\) is
\[
Z(Z-1)(Z+1)P_{12}(Z)\quad\text{for the 1872-orbit},
\]
where
\[
P_{12}(Z)=Z^{12}-Z^9+Z^8-Z^7+Z^6-Z^5+Z^4-Z^3+Z^2-Z+1,
\]
and
\[
Z(Z-1)(Z+1)P_3(Z)^4\quad\text{for the 144-orbit},\qquad P_3(Z)=Z^3-Z^2+Z+1.
\]
Both \(P_{12}\) and \(P_3\) are irreducible over \(\mathbf F_3\) (checked by exact factorization). On the residual components the other affine coordinate is an algebraic function of \(Z\), since \(Z^3-Z\) is invertible there. For the first representative, Gröbner bases of \((F_A,F_B,\det(\partial(F_A,F_B)/\partial(y,z)))\) are the unit ideal on all three charts, proving reducedness. For the second representative they are not the unit ideal; the residual quotient is locally \(\mathbf F_3[Z]/(P_3^4)\), so its three geometric points each have length four. The lines at infinity introduce no extra nonrational base points: the degree-\(25\) total and the \(13\) rational points exhaust the stated contributions. Projective invariance proves both configurations throughout their orbits.

**No higher-dimensional all-smooth systems.** Fix any rational point \(p\). For a vector subspace \(W\subset V\) the differential at \(p\) defines a linear map \(W\to T_p^*\mathbf P^2\), a space of dimension \(2\). If \(\dim W\ge3\), some nonzero form in \(W\) has zero differential at \(p\). Every form in \(V\) vanishes at \(p\), so this form defines a singular rational member. Thus \(\dim W\le2\) for all-smooth systems.

## Verification
The distributed sources `enumerate_pencils.py`, `verify_geometry.py`, `verify_rref.cpp` and `EXPECTED.txt` contain exact complete finite checkers and expected results. Run `python3 verify_geometry.py` in a directory containing `enumerate_pencils.py` (requires SymPy). Separately compile `g++ -std=c++17 -O2 verify_rref.cpp -o rref_checker` and run `./rref_checker`. The first checker verifies projective-group generation, smooth quintic orbit representatives by geometric Gröbner bases, every rational tangent pair, pencil orbits and residual scheme factors. The second independently checks all RREF two-spaces and the pencil count. On the full tested finite parameter space the programs agree.

## Relationship to prior work
Homma and Kim (2009, 2013) classify individual nonsingular minimum-degree plane-filling curves via matrices. Duran Cunha (2018) studies their automorphism groups and other individual-curve invariants. Homma (2019) studies individual curves and degenerations. Asgarli and Ghioca (2022) formulate and study the distinct problem of pencils all of whose rational members are smooth. Those established results supply the geometry and motivation; the assertion here is specifically the **joint pencil enumeration, two projective pencil-orbits and reduced-versus-nonreduced degree-\(12\) base-scheme dichotomy**, not the known individual-curve classification, degree minimum, or general existence of all-smooth pencils.

## Limitations
The enumeration is restricted to characteristic \(3\), base field \(\mathbf F_3\), and the minimum-degree plane-filling quintic subspace. No formula for arbitrary finite fields or degrees is claimed. Smoothness is imposed only on rational pencil members. Literature coverage cannot rule out unindexed equivalent tables; full texts of every historical matrix-classification source were not accessible for exhaustive priority checking. The result has not received an independent scientific audit.
