# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The result has one precise domain: contexts generated by first drawing a symmetric \((m+1)\)-category Dirichlet vector, freezing it, and then taking independent uniform-within-row-size objects until all \(2^m\) attribute subsets become intents. The proof establishes Boolean completion iff every coatom row is sampled, independently derives the beta marginal for their shared total probability, and uses conditional coupon waiting time to derive the exact inverse-first-moment criterion. It separately proves the standard-prior inverse-exponential limit by negative covariance of unseen categories and the fixed-precision logarithmic exponential limit by a beta small-shape tail and conditional uniform bounds. It treats repeated rows, the empty row case, and all positive integer attribute counts. It does **not** infer asymptotic statements from finite enumeration. The attached executable exact-rational checker agrees with independently computed closure systems on 15,159 finite contexts in 45 parameter cases.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Felde and Hanika (2018, arXiv:1809.11160), and Felde, Hanika and Stumme (2020, DOI:10.3390/info11030135), defined the Dirichlet context generator and expressly reduced collecting coatom rows *conditional on the coatom category* to the classical coupon collector. Those familiar ingredients, and the deterministic Booleanity criterion, are **not** asserted as original. The reviewed claim instead concerns the *unconditional shared-random-environment* stopping time: its sharp integrability boundary, standard-prior inverse-exponential completion law, and fixed-precision exponential-log law. The cited works' inspected model, probability, and discussion sections do not state these conclusions. Sakurai (2021, arXiv:2101.11023) studies expected concept counts under independent Bernoulli incidences, not the same shared Dirichlet environment. An existing exact Boolean-saturation threshold for iid Bernoulli contexts does not imply the present annealed limiting laws, because it lacks the latent beta-distributed common row-category mass. Searches of related research records under Dirichlet, contranominal, coupon-collector, inverse-exponential, and random-context aliases identified no equivalent. This is a best-knowledge self-review, **not** a claim of priority proof. The full text of Yang et al. (ICMLC 2024), *Counting Formal Concepts of A Random Formal Context*, was not obtained and remains a documented comparison risk; its known metadata does not settle the question.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The model is a previously motivated null model for formal-concept data, and its full Boolean concept lattice is a distinguished maximal structural outcome, not an arbitrary statistic. The theorem says that the usual symmetric Dirichlet generator has an **infinite average waiting time at every attribute size**, even though its typical completion time has a concrete polynomial scale and inverse-exponential law. Holding total precision fixed changes that scale to exponential, with a nondegenerate law for its logarithm. This resolves a natural quantitative aspect of the cited Dirichlet model and explains why reporting only the conditional coupon-collector mean or an average row-size probability can be badly misleading. The value comes from the coupled integrability and phase-scale classification, not the preexisting coupon criterion or code validation alone.

## Literature and limitations

Primary literature compared: Felde–Hanika 2018, full arXiv text (including model and discussion); Felde–Hanika–Stumme 2020, substantive accessible sections on Dirichlet design and coupon concentration; Sakurai 2021, full arXiv text of the independent-Bernoulli average-count result. Bibliographic evidence only was available for Yang–Tsang–Zhang–Wu 2024; its full text was unavailable. Other classical structured coupon-collector work assumes independent draws from a fixed distribution, rather than the unconditional dependence created by freezing a Dirichlet draw. No result for arbitrary asymmetric Dirichlet parameters or a finite random object-count cap is claimed.
