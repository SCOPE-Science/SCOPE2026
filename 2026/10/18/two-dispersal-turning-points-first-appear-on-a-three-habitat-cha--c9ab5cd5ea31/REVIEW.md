# Scientific review

**Correctness.** The OU covariance exists uniquely because all local returns are positive and the graph Laplacian is positive semidefinite. Direct exact symbolic solution and differentiation reproduce the three-patch variance and numerator. Its polynomial has at most two positive roots by Descartes and at least two by rational sign tests, yielding the asserted local minimum and maximum. The two-patch derivative has only one possible positive root due to strict ordering of three independent sign thresholds; the equal-rate and one-node cases are constant. The open-neighborhood assertion uses strict sign persistence. Risks: the model is a linear Gaussian fluctuation approximation, not an extinction model; there is no global optimum assertion.

**Originality.** Relevant prior studies address ecological spatial synchrony, regional metapopulation variability, network \(H_2\) performance, and heterogeneous local dynamics. Wang, Haegeman and Loreau (2015) explicitly discuss qualitative heterogeneous two-patch variability, but this does not imply the exact three-patch two-turning certificate. A related two-patch stationary-variance classification does not dominate a three-patch reentrant derivative sign pattern. The closest full texts of some control-theoretic studies and the complete Wang appendices were unavailable. Thus exact equivalent coverage elsewhere remains possible; the claim is limited to the proved mathematical statement and does not claim ownership of general stochastic covariance theory.

**Value.** In spatial population-variance control, a single increase in corridor strength can change from stabilizing to destabilizing and back to stabilizing without changing environmental forcing or patch qualities. An explicit three-habitat minimum, exact rational certificate, and open robustness distinguish this nonmonotonicity from a numerical curiosity. It matters when interpreting fluctuation outcomes of one-parameter corridor policies, though no empirical or global optimization conclusion follows.
