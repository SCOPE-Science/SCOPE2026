# Two homological recolouring components for the Chvátal graph

## Finding

Let \(G\) be the twelve-vertex Chvátal graph given by the complete edge list below. Let \(\mathcal R_4(G)\) have as its vertices **all labeled proper colorings** \(c:V(G)\to\{0,1,2,3\}\), joining two colorings exactly when they differ at one vertex and both remain proper. Then

\[
|V(\mathcal R_4(G))|=18024,\qquad |E(\mathcal R_4(G))|=64320,
\]

and \(\mathcal R_4(G)\) has **exactly two connected components, each of size \(9012\)**. The components are precisely the two fibers of an explicitly computable homological degree \(\deg_Z(c)\in\{-1,+1\}\) arising from the simplicial coloring map from the open-neighborhood complex of \(G\) to the boundary of a tetrahedron. The two degrees occur \(9012\) times each; any odd permutation of the four color labels swaps the components, and any even permutation preserves each component.

This is a classification at the minimum admissible palette \(\chi(G)=4\), **not** a claim about larger palettes. The known theorem that all four-colorings of a connected noncomplete four-regular graph are Kempe-equivalent applies to \(G\); Kempe-switch connectivity is not claimed as new.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite, simple and undirected. Number vertices \(0,1,\ldots,11\); the twenty-four unordered edges are

```text
(0,1) (0,4) (0,6) (0,9) (1,2) (1,5) (1,7) (2,3)
(2,6) (2,8) (3,4) (3,7) (3,9) (4,5) (4,8) (5,10)
(5,11) (6,10) (6,11) (7,8) (7,11) (8,10) (9,10) (9,11)
```

A *single-vertex recoloring move* changes the value of \(c\) on exactly one vertex, with properness required before and after the change. Palette names are **not** quotiented by their permutations. All four colors are necessarily used since the graph is four-chromatic; the attached complete backtracking verifier separately checks that there are no three-colorings.

For a graph \(F\), its ordinary **open-neighborhood complex** \(\mathcal N(F)\) consists of vertex sets contained in \(N_F(v)\) for at least one vertex \(v\). This definition excludes closed-neighborhood faces. Here \(K=\mathcal N(G)\), and \(L=\mathcal N(K_4)\) is the two-dimensional simplicial sphere \(\partial\Delta^3\) on color vertices \(0,1,2,3\). For a proper coloring \(c:G\to K_4\), the vertex map \(c\) induces a simplicial map \(\phi_c:K\to L\): a common neighbor \(w\) of a simplex forces all its image colors to avoid \(c(w)\).

## Proof

**1. Explicit integral two-cycle.** For \(a<b<c\), write \([a,b,c]\) for the ordered oriented triangle. Define \(Z\in C_2(K;\mathbb Z)\) by the following twenty nonzero coefficients (all unlisted coefficients vanish):

```text
+ (0,2,7)    - (0,2,10)   - (0,3,8)    + (0,3,11)
- (0,5,7)    + (0,5,8)    - (0,10,11)  + (1,3,8)
- (1,3,11)   - (1,4,9)    + (1,4,10)   - (1,6,8)
+ (1,6,9)    + (1,10,11)  + (2,4,9)    - (2,4,10)
- (2,7,9)    + (5,6,8)    - (5,6,9)    + (5,7,9)
```

All twenty triangles are faces of \(K\). Using \(\partial[a,b,c]=[b,c]-[a,c]+[a,b]\), the coefficient on each oriented edge in \(\partial Z\) is zero. The first verifier checks the complete integer boundary, not just its parity; thus \(\partial Z=0\). It also reconstructs the \(K\) face vector \((12,42,44,12)\) from all twelve open neighborhoods. A previously published topological calculation already determines \(K\simeq S^2\); that fact and this face vector are **not** new contributions.

**2. A recoloring-invariant homological sign.** Fix the standard orientation of the tetrahedron boundary

\[
T=\partial[0,1,2,3]=[1,2,3]-[0,2,3]+[0,1,3]-[0,1,2].
\]

For every proper coloring \(c\), the induced integral two-chain satisfies \(\phi_{c\#}(Z)=d(c)T\) for a unique integer \(d(c)\). To see this independently of computation, \(\phi_{c\#}(Z)\) is an integral cycle in \(\partial\Delta^3\), whose degree-two integral cycle group is freely generated by \(T\). A direct formula is the signed coefficient of \([1,2,3]\): for each term \(\epsilon[a,b,c]\) in \(Z\), add \(\epsilon\operatorname{sgn}(c(a),c(b),c(c))\) when the three colors are a permutation of \((1,2,3)\), and zero otherwise. The verifier also checks **all four** oriented triangle coefficients agree with \(d(c)T\).

Suppose \(c,c'\) differ only at vertex \(w\). The simplicial maps \(\phi_c\) and \(\phi_{c'}\) are **contiguous**. Indeed, any simplex \(\sigma\in K\) has a common neighbor \(v\). If \(v\ne w\), both image color sets \(c(\sigma)\) and \(c'(\sigma)\) avoid the unchanged color \(c(v)=c'(v)\). If \(v=w\), the simplex lies in the neighbors of \(w\), and its color images are identical and avoid the color of \(w\). In either case their union is a proper face of \(\Delta^3\), hence a simplex of \(L\). Contiguous maps induce equal maps on homology, so **\(d(c)=d(c')\)** for every legal single-site move.

Consider the proper coloring, in vertex order,

```text
c0 = (0,1,0,1,2,0,1,0,1,2,3,3)
```

(the existence of this coloring is previously recorded in the literature). Directly substituting its twelve colors into the twenty terms gives \(\phi_{c_0\#}(Z)=T\), so \(d(c_0)=+1\). Transpose palette names \(0\) and \(1\) to obtain \(c_1\). Since an odd permutation of tetrahedron vertices reverses its integral orientation, \(d(c_1)=-1\). Therefore these colorings are in **different components**. More generally color permutations multiply the integer degree by their sign.

**3. Exhaustive connectivity and completeness.** The verifier `verify_coloring.py` recursively assigns colors \(0,1,2,3\) to vertices \(0,\ldots,11\), rejecting each assignment as soon as it duplicates the color of any already colored neighbor. Its recursion visits every proper labeled four-coloring **exactly once**, giving \(18024\), and independently finds no labeled three-coloring. From each coloring it generates every legal single-vertex recoloring by examining all twelve vertices and all four color names; hence these are the complete edges of \(\mathcal R_4(G)\). Breadth-first searches from \(c_0\) and \(c_1\) reach disjoint sets of exactly \(9012\) colorings each, whose union is the enumerated set of all \(18024\). Therefore exactly two connected components exist. Evaluating the cycle degree for **every** coloring yields \(9012\) instances of \(+1\) and \(9012\) of \(-1\), and agrees pointwise with the two BFS components. No higher-degree cases are omitted.

**4. Separately implemented count and move graph.** The verifier `verify_partition.py` starts instead with the twelve *neighbor bitmasks* and enumerates every independent nonempty subset by a mask recurrence. It constructs every partition of all twelve vertices into **four nonempty independent blocks**, ordering blocks by their least elements. Exactly \(751\) partitions occur. Each admits all \(4!=24\) color-label bijections; hence \(751\cdot24=18024\) colorings. Independent union-find exploration of all one-site recoloring edges yields exactly \(64320\) undirected edges and two components of \(9012\) each. Thus the counts, adjacency and connectivity are corroborated using different graph encodings and different enumeration/connectivity algorithms.

## Verification

Place `verify_coloring.py`, `verify_partition.py` in one directory and run each with Python 3 using only the standard library. Their expected stdout transcripts are `verify_coloring.out` and `verify_partition.out`. The first confirms that the displayed \(Z\) is a closed 2-chain, recovers the image orientation sign for all \(18024\) colorings and exhausts both components; the second independently reconstructs the combinatorial state space from independent-set partitions and the recoloring graph from neighbor masks. Both finish with explicit success markers. Full code, rather than output logs alone, is part of the evidence. The claim is finite, exact and exhaustively established; neither stochastic sampling nor asymptotic extrapolation is involved.

## Relationship to prior work

Chvátal's 1970 construction supplies the underlying smallest triangle-free, four-regular, four-chromatic graph. Cereceda, van den Heuvel and Johnson (research report 2005; article 2008) **define exactly this one-vertex recoloring graph**, prove general mixing/nonmixing theorems, and show four-chromatic graphs can be either connected or disconnected at the minimum palette; their inspected paper does not compute this named graph's number of colorings, components, sizes, edges or homological sign. Bonamy, Bousquet, Feghali and Johnson (2015) prove that every connected noncomplete \(k\)-regular graph, \(k\ge4\), has a single Kempe-equivalence class of proper \(k\)-colorings. That general Kempe connectivity already applies here and **does not** imply one-site connectivity: a Kempe move may recolor multiple vertices simultaneously.

Two published exact studies of this same Chvátal graph compute its independence and neighborhood complexes and their symmetry actions, including \(K\simeq S^2\), but do **not** classify the one-site coloring graph or compute the coloring-induced degree function above. The new contribution is the **specific two-component classification together with a chain-level homological obstruction** on this canonical chromatic-extremal graph; neither general four-chromatic disconnection nor the known Kempe theorem is claimed as new.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns only the explicit twelve-vertex Chvátal graph and the palette of exactly four named colors. It does not claim that every four-regular four-chromatic graph has two components or that every proper four-coloring of an arbitrary graph has degree \(\pm1\). The original two-page 1970 note was available in text transcription but its original figure was not legible in that source; the full exact edge list was cross-checked against independently published same-graph mathematics, and both checkers reconstruct it explicitly. A 2022 thesis containing Chvátal colorings and general recoloring ideas was identified but the full text was not retrievable for a decisive appendix search. An unindexed equivalent computation or unnamed stronger theorem is a residual originality risk. No external independent audit or publication priority is asserted.
