In Brownian particles sharing a randomly switching harmonic trap, the probability that the stationary maximum lies below the nominal compact-support edge is proportional to \(\log\log N/\log N\)^{R_1}, whereas genuine exceedance above the upper edge decays as \((\log N)^{-R_2-1/2}\). Sharper Gumbel-centered crossover laws identify the boundary profiles and the \(1/\log N\)-scale trap environments behind these rare events.
