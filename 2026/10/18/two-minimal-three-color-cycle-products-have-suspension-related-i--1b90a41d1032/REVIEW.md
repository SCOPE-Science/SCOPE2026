# Scientific review: two minimal three-color cycle products

## Correctness

PASS. Exact finite enumeration yields 5,281 and 18,721 nonempty faces. The stated toggle matchings contain respectively 2,638 and 9,358 pairs; reversing their matched Hasse edges gives acyclic directed graphs, confirmed by complete topological sorts. The only positive-dimensional Morse boundary matrices are both exactly zero over the integers, computed by signed recursive gradient-path projection. The resulting CW models have one zero-cell, one critical cell in degree three/four, and three in degree four/five. Vanishing integral attaching degrees yield the precise wedges because \(\pi_d(S^d)\cong\mathbb Z\) for the applicable \(d\ge3\). A second independent signed-chain elimination reproduces the homological ranks, without reliance on finite-field inference.

## Originality

PASS, with specified residual risk. Antolín Camarena–Carnero Bravo (2023), the most direct published source, name both examples and report nonzero free homology in both allowed degrees but do not state their exact torsion-free homotopy classifications or a suspension equivalence. Their Theorem 23 allows torsion and hence does not imply the full results. The closest accessible published research finding treats \(C_7\times K_4\), a different graph with no proven reduction to three colors. The 2025 related paper handles path-factor products; searches for tensor/direct-product aliases and both exact graph parameters found no covering statement. An equivalent unindexed result or authors' unpublished computational detail remains possible.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The two three-color cases are explicitly singled out in the foundational paper's open torsion-freeness discussion. They are the shortest currently unclassified cycle lengths beyond the previously solved short-cycle and divisible-by-three families, and the exact integral homotopy computations remove the Moore-space ambiguity in two concrete named examples. The additional suspension-type coincidence links these first two unresolved cases, though no natural map is claimed. This is a finite exact clarification, not a general torsion-free theorem.

## Limits

The computations certify only the two 21- and 24-vertex graph complexes; no inference is made for all color counts, larger cycle lengths, or natural equivariance of a suspension map. Source searches cannot exclude unindexed equivalent work. Both programs are reproducible directly from the embedded sources; independent external mathematical validation has not occurred.
