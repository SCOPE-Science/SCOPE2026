# Scientific review: two mobility conventions on finite stars

**Correctness — PASS.** A full three-orbit first-jump system for the finite product walk yields both rational means, including the static-trap boundary. Subtraction gives the two positive exact slowdown thresholds and the interprotocol comparison. Derivative numerators are strictly decreasing quartics with exactly one positive root, proving unique maxima. Positive-coefficient identities bound the maxima uniformly and evaluation at the explicit parameter ratio \(m^{-1/2}\) proves their sharp distinct limits. An independent exact-rational labeled-state calculation confirms both formulas for 40 parameter/protocol settings and all 880 checked first-step equations. Numerical checks do not establish the all-degree theorem; the algebraic proof does.

**Originality — PASS within inspected sources, with residual risk.** Slow encounters between mobile walkers on inhomogeneous networks were already established by Campari–Cassi (2012) and Agliari–Blumen–Cassi (2014); a continuum mobility threshold was established by Tzou–Xie–Kolokolnikov (2014). The full four-page Campari–Cassi text, the relevant finite-comb passages of Agliari et al. (2014, 2016), and the mobile-trap section of Tzou et al. were compared against the claim. None supplied the simultaneous star-specific edge-clock and vertex-clock formulas, the sharp degree-dependent crossing, and the unbounded-versus-bounded maximal slowdown. General discrete-time product-chain meeting methods can in principle evaluate finite chains but do not directly state the sharp classification. The absence of a hidden equivalent finite-star calculation cannot be guaranteed by these searches; independent bibliographic review remains necessary.

**Value — PASS.** Degree-normalized versus per-edge random walks are standard distinct physical conventions on nonregular graphs. The theorem shows that keeping the searcher's leaf departure clock fixed changes the strongest possible capture delay from asymptotically half the star degree to a universal factor two. This is a structural comparison and a sharp boundary for a natural two-particle encounter observable, not merely a table of sample rates.

**Limits and prior work.** Primary finite-branched encounter source: E. Agliari, A. Blumen and D. Cassi, *Slow Encounters of Particle Pairs in Branched Structures*, arXiv:1405.5554 (first posted 2014-05-21). Closest related sources include R. Campari and D. Cassi (2012), DOI:10.1103/PhysRevE.86.021110; J. C. Tzou, S. Xie and T. Kolokolnikov (2014), arXiv:1410.1391; and E. Agliari et al. (2016), arXiv:1604.05874. The result is restricted to independently walking particles initially at one leaf and the hub of a star; it does not prove a general network or full-distribution claim.
