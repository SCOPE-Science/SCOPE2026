# Two-part localization of strong graph-Morse complexes for all complete multipartite graphs

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(r\ge3\), let \(n_1,\ldots,n_r\) be arbitrary positive integers, and let \(G=K_{n_1,\ldots,n_r}\) be a graph regarded as a one-dimensional simplicial complex (not its clique complex). Write \(\mathcal{SM}(G)\) for the full, not necessarily pure, complex of strong discrete-Morse matchings generated by injective vertex filtrations in the sense of Knudson and Owens–White. If \(A_i\) are the canonical independent parts, there is an **equality of subcomplexes on pairwise disjoint vertex sets**:

\[
\mathcal{SM}(G)=\bigsqcup_{1\le i<j\le r}\mathcal{SM}(K_{A_i,A_j}).
\]

Every two-part summand admits an explicit simplicial collapse to a graph. For \(a,b\ge2\), the summand \(\mathcal{SM}(K_{a,b})\) is connected and collapses to a graph with cycle rank \(2ab-a-b+1\). For \(\min(a,b)=1<\max(a,b)\), it is collapsible to a point; for \(a=b=1\), it is exactly two isolated vertices.

Let \(s=\#\{i:n_i=1\}\), \(c=\binom r2+\binom s2\), and

\[
 b=\sum_{\substack{1\le i<j\le r\\ n_i,n_j\ge2}}(2n_in_j-n_i-n_j+1).
\]

Then the entire complex collapses simplicially to a disjoint union of graphs with exactly \(c\) connected components and total first Betti number \(b\). In particular,

\[
\widetilde H_k(\mathcal{SM}(G);\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z^{c-1},&k=0,\\
\mathbb Z^b,&k=1,\\
0,&k\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]

These descriptions are **integral and natural under all graph automorphisms**. For every pair with both sizes at least two, form the doubled undirected bipartite multigraph \(D_{ij}\) having the original vertices of \(A_i\cup A_j\), with one edge labeled by each directed incidence \((v,w)\) across the bipartition. Then

\[
H_1(\mathcal{SM}(G);\mathbb Z)
\cong\bigoplus_{\substack{i<j\\n_i,n_j\ge2}}\ker\left(\partial_{ij}:\mathbb Z[E(D_{ij})]\longrightarrow\mathbb Z[V(D_{ij})]\right),
\quad \partial_{ij}(v,w)=w-v,
\]

where automorphisms act by transporting labels and permuting the part pairs. If \(\Omega\) consists of one symbol for each unordered pair of parts except that a pair of singleton parts contributes the two directed incidences separately, then \(\widetilde H_0\cong\ker(\varepsilon:\mathbb Z[\Omega]\to\mathbb Z)\) as an integral automorphism module.

In particular, for the octahedral graph \(K_{2,2,2}\) the strong matching complex has three connected components, each homotopy equivalent to a wedge of five circles: \(\mathcal{SM}(K_{2,2,2})\simeq\coprod^3\bigvee^5S^1\). The disconnectedness is forced by the filtration, not by disconnectedness of the underlying graph.

## Assumptions and scope

The input is precisely the one-dimensional simplicial complex consisting of the vertices and edges of a finite complete multipartite graph; triangles in its graph are **not filled**. A vertex of its strong-matching complex is an oriented incidence \((v,w)\), standing for the Hasse pair \(\{v\}\subset\{v,w\}\). A face is any nonempty subset of the pairs produced by some injective vertex filtration; its ordinary empty face is understood in the usual simplicial convention. This is the full strong matching complex, not the pure subcomplex and not the unrestricted complex of all acyclic discrete vector fields.

The theorem concerns every \(r\ge3\) and every positive vector of part sizes, including singleton parts and all-singleton graphs. It gives an actual simplicial collapse to a one-dimensional complex but does not claim that the original complex is itself one-dimensional or that it is a manifold.

## Proof

**1. Order localization.** For any ordering of the vertices of a graph viewed as a one-dimensional complex, the descending link at \(v\) is a discrete set of its earlier graph-neighbors. Such a link is a simplicial cone exactly when it has one vertex. Hence \(v\) contributes a strong Hasse pair \((v,w)\) precisely when \(w\) is its unique earlier neighbor. There are no higher-dimensional Hasse pairs.

For any vertex ordering of \(G\), let \(A_i\) and \(A_j\) be the first two distinct independent parts encountered. After the first vertex from any third part has been encountered, every subsequently considered vertex has at least two earlier neighbors, lying in two distinct other parts: one from each of two parts already encountered and distinct from its own. This vertex is therefore not descending dominated. Before that event, every strong Hasse pair has both ends in \(A_i\cup A_j\). Restricting the ordering to these two parts produces exactly the same strong pairs, because subsequent vertices cannot contribute. Conversely, an arbitrary ordering of \(A_i\cup A_j\), extended by the vertices of all other parts, reproduces its bipartite strong pairs and introduces no new pairs. Therefore the collection of generated simplices equals the union of the two-part collections. Their nonempty face vertex sets are disjoint because a directed edge of \(G\) belongs to exactly one unordered pair of parts. This proves the claimed disjoint union of simplicial complexes.

**2. Exact bipartite facet cover.** Fix disjoint nonempty parts \(A,B\) of sizes \(a,b\). For \(y\in B\) put \(X_y=\{(x,y):x\in A\}\), and for \(x\in A\) put \(Y_x=\{(y,x):y\in B\}\). With \(\Delta(S)\) denoting the full simplex on a vertex set \(S\), define

\[
 U_y=\Delta(X_y)\ \cup\ \bigcup_{x\in A}\Delta\bigl((X_y\setminus\{(x,y)\})\cup\{(y,x)\}\bigr),
\]
\[
 V_x=\Delta(Y_x)\ \cup\ \bigcup_{y\in B}\Delta\bigl((Y_x\setminus\{(y,x)\})\cup\{(x,y)\}\bigr).
\]

Inspection of which side of a bipartite ordering first supplies a unique earlier neighbor shows that every generated matching belongs to some \(U_y\) or \(V_x\). Conversely, each displayed simplex is contained in a generated matching from a vertex ordering: for the base \(\Delta(X_y)\), place \(y\) first, then all of \(A\), then the remaining vertices of \(B\); for the flap \(\{(y,x)\}\cup(X_y\setminus\{(x,y)\})\), place \(x\) first, then \(y\), then \(A\setminus\{x\}\), then \(B\setminus\{y\}\). The constructions for \(V_x\) are symmetric. Consequently,
\[
\mathcal{SM}(K_{A,B})=\bigcup_{y\in B}U_y\ \cup\ \bigcup_{x\in A}V_x.
\]

**3. Explicit collapse when both parts have size at least two.** Each \(U_y\) is a simplex on \(X_y\), with \(a\) extra simplices (flaps). The flap indexed by \(x\) has a new tip \((y,x)\) and meets the base along the nonempty proper face \(X_y\setminus\{(x,y)\}\). Choose any \(z_x\in X_y\setminus\{(x,y)\}\). By pairing each face that contains its tip and a nonempty subset of the other base vertices, but not \(z_x\), with the face obtained by adjoining \(z_x\), in descending cardinality, this flap collapses relative to the base and the edge \(\{(y,x),z_x\}\). Distinct flaps intersect only in base faces, so these are elementary free-face collapses in the union. Collapse the base simplex to the tree of edges joining a chosen root of \(X_y\) to every other base vertex, by the analogous descending-cardinality elementary pairs. All its vertices remain. The result is a tree having \(2a\) vertices and \(2a-1\) edges. Apply the symmetric process to every \(V_x\), producing a tree on \(2b\) vertices and \(2b-1\) edges.

For \(a,b\ge2\), pieces within the same family are disjoint, and \(U_y\cap V_x\) consists of exactly the two isolated orientation vertices \((x,y)\) and \((y,x)\), with no edge between them; there are no triple intersections. Therefore the relative collapses inside these pieces remain valid globally. The resulting complex is a connected graph with \(2ab\) vertices and
\[
 b(2a-1)+a(2b-1)=4ab-a-b
\]
(the total number of edges in the collapsed graph). Its cycle rank is \((4ab-a-b)-(2ab)+1=2ab-a-b+1\). A connected graph of that cycle rank is homotopy equivalent to the indicated circle wedge.

**4. Boundary cases.** When \(a=1<b\), the complete cover reduces to the single contractible patch \(V_x\). Its nontrivial flaps collapse relative to the central base simplex by the same pairing, and that simplex collapses to a tree; deleting leaves reduces it to one point. The case \(b=1<a\) is symmetric. When \(a=b=1\), the possible orderings give exactly the two isolated orientation vertices, with no edge. No other boundary cases exist for positive part sizes.

**5. Homology and symmetry.** By Steps 1–4 the number of components is one per unordered part pair, except a singleton–singleton pair contributes two, giving \(c\). The first Betti number sums over those pairs having both sizes at least two, giving \(b\), and all higher homology vanishes integrally. The component set \(\Omega\) is permuted canonically by graph automorphisms, giving the augmentation-kernel formula for reduced degree-zero homology. In the \(a,b\ge2\) case, the contractible-piece cover of Step 2 has a natural degree-one Mayer–Vietoris description: one abstract vertex for each \(U_y\) and each \(V_x\), and **two distinct overlap connectors** for every \(x,y\), labeled by the two oriented incidences. All pieces have vanishing higher homology, so the Mayer–Vietoris boundary identifies integral first homology naturally with the cycle lattice of the doubled bipartite graph. Since graph automorphisms transport every piece and both distinct intersection labels, this isomorphism is equivariant, including automorphisms that exchange equally sized parts. Direct sums across all unordered part pairs finish the proof.

## Verification

The standalone scripts `verify_faces.py` and `verify_collapses.py` (Python 3 standard library only) reconstruct all vertex-order-generated simplices, independently enumerate the proposed facets and verify equality, calculate mod-two simplicial boundary ranks, and verify each claimed elementary collapse by a free-face condition. Results are recorded in `verification_faces.txt` and `verification_collapses.txt`. Tested part-size vectors are \((2,2,2)\), \((1,2,2)\), \((1,1,2)\), \((1,1,1,1)\), \((2,3,2)\), \((2,2,1,1)\), \((3,3)\), \((1,4)\), \((2,3)\), and \((1,1)\). The first case has 60 nonempty faces, three connected components, and total first Betti number fifteen. These bounded computational checks corroborate, but do not replace, the general proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Knudson and Owens–White introduced the full strong discrete-Morse matching complex and computed homotopy types for paths, stars, simplices and simplex boundaries, together with a pure-cycle classification. Fernández developed the underlying filtration and descending-domination theory. The bipartite two-part classification is not asserted as a distinct novelty of the present theorem: its graph rank also arises in earlier bipartite calculations. The all-singleton case is already contained in the published calculation of strong matching complexes for complete graphs. The new conclusion is that **every** complete multipartite graph with at least three nonempty parts splits exactly into disconnected two-part matching complexes, with a uniform treatment of singleton-part changes, a global explicit one-dimensional simple-homotopy model and natural integral automorphism modules.

These strong matching complexes differ fundamentally from ordinary Chari–Joswig discrete Morse complexes, which admit all acyclic graph-rooted-forest matchings and need not have the same homotopy type; they also differ from the standard matching and higher-matching complexes of graph edges.

## Limitations

The theorem requires each part to be nonempty and the input to be the one-dimensional graph simplicial complex. It does not extend without alteration to the clique complex of a multipartite graph, arbitrary subgraphs, the pure strong matching complex, or the unrestricted discrete Morse complex. Neither external review nor an independent audit is implied by the included computations; no exhaustive claim about unindexed literature is made.
