# Review of two-part localization for complete multipartite strong graph-Morse complexes

## Correctness — PASS

The graph is taken as a one-dimensional simplicial complex, making the descending link discrete. Its domination criterion is exactly one earlier graph-neighbor. The first two distinct multipartite parts in any filtration therefore determine all matching pairs; after the third part appears, every later vertex has two earlier neighbors in distinct foreign parts. Both directions of the exact simplicial localization follow by restriction and extension of permutations. For each bipartite pair, every facet is explicitly generated by either a central simplex or a flap of the displayed \(U_y,V_x\) cover; relative elementary collapse pairs reduce every nondegenerate contractible patch to a tree while retaining its two-point intersections. Graph Euler accounting proves the rank formula. The singleton cases are checked separately. Mayer–Vietoris for the natural piece cover gives an integral, automorphism-equivariant cycle lattice. The runnable scripts independently verify facet equality, mod-two homology, and genuine free-face elementary collapses on ten distinct part-size vectors. The infinite quantifiers rest on the argument, not the finite tests.

## Originality — PASS with bibliographic limitations

The closest primary source is Knudson–Owens–White, *Complexes of strong discrete Morse matchings*, arXiv:2609.06844v1, Sections 3–4, 7, 10 (full HTML inspected); it introduces this exact invariant, handles stars and complete graphs as well as other families, and does not give the all-multipartite localization statement. Fernández, arXiv:2504.15729, supplies its filtration framework. The bipartite summands have already been classified and are acknowledged here, not claimed as new. The all-singleton complete-graph case is also covered by the original paper. The mathematical difference is the exact decomposition for three or more parts, together with singleton transitions and full global equivariant integral homology. Ordinary discrete Morse complexes of complete bipartite and octahedral graphs differ in admissible faces and thus do not imply the theorem. Searches also tested aliases such as strong graph-Morse, full strong matching, Turán graphs, complete tripartite, and equality-of-complexes formulations. No exact equivalent or stronger implication was found in the accessible indexed sources. Unindexed overlap remains possible; originality is not a uniqueness certificate.

## Value — PASS

This is a natural, complete, unbounded family classification of an emerging topological construction, with a structural explanation of the surprising disconnection of the strong matching complex of a connected complete multipartite graph. Explicit collapses to dimension one and functorial integral symmetry modules are stronger than enumeration or isolated graph cases. For example, the octahedral graph has three components each a wedge of five circles, and singleton parts create a sharp doubling of individual components.

## Scope and evidential limits

The proof is mathematical and self-contained given the filtration definition. Bounded enumerations do not establish arbitrary-size statements. The result does not concern the clique complex, the pure strong-matching subcomplex or the ordinary discrete-Morse complex. Overlap with inaccessible or unindexed papers is a residual risk. Independent scientific validation has not been performed.
