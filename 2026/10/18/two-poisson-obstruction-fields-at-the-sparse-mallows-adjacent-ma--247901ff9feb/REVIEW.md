# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The independent truncated-geometric insertion code follows by factorizing the Mallows inversion weight. Its characterization of disjoint adjacent swaps was cross-checked by a separate permutation-class test through size eight; the inductive argument supplies the proof for all sizes. The weighted matching enumeration yields an exact finite-size identity. The characteristic-root expression has an analytic logarithmic expansion uniform in the sample size, and factorial-moment cluster bounds show asymptotic independence of the two rare defect fields at the square-root threshold. The standalone checker reports 138780 finite structural/algebraic/numerical assertion events. Numerical and finite checks are not substituted for the uniform expansion or Poisson limit proof.

## Originality

**PASS on reviewed evidence, with residual historical-priority risk.** Pinsky's paper proves fixed-parameter probabilities for single classical length-three patterns and uses the same classical insertion representation; its Theorems 1–4 do not assert a two-type rare-defect field for the class of products of disjoint adjacent transpositions. Crane, DeSalvo and Elizalde prove fixed-parameter consecutive-pattern growth and Gaussian pattern counts rather than this triangular-array critical regime. The two cycle-count central limit theorem papers by He and by He–Müller–Verstraaten assume fixed nonunit parameter. These works imply the model's standard input representation, not the stated marked Poisson limit or uniform matching-probability transition without further mathematical arguments. Generic Poisson approximation under local dependence could provide an alternate route and is not claimed as original. An equivalent result could be present under layered-permutation, matching-polynomial, or joint pattern-avoidance terminology; independent priority confirmation remains outstanding.

## Value

**PASS.** The model has a natural near-identity regime in which the typical number of single adjacent swaps grows like \(nq_n\), yet the failure of a global adjacent-matching structure occurs at the different scale \(nq_n^2\). The theorem quantifies that structural transition exactly and identifies two independently scattered failure mechanisms. The joint spatial limit has information unavailable from a one-dimensional bound and can serve as a benchmark for structural testing of weakly disordered rankings.

## Limitations

The matching event excludes distant swaps and general involutions. The Poisson law requires \(q_n\to0\) and finite \(nq_n^2\) limit; the exact probability and logarithmic expansion have broader but explicitly stated domains. The work does not assert an independently audited result or a novelty guarantee beyond the inspected sources.
