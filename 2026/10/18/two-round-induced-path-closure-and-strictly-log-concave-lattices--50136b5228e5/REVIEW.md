# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The induced-path rules are exactly completion of the four cross edges of a disjoint edge pair. The four-vertex clique core and its two-round propagation establish the full closure; missing first-round pairs are characterized precisely by degree-one vertices with a common neighbor. The closed-set classification follows from the elementary structure of pairwise intersecting two-subsets; this classification supports a graded rank assignment and exact enumeration. The rank-sequence boundary ratios and the positive binomial-times-linear formula establish strict log-concavity. The atomic crosscut identity and full-support inclusion-exclusion give the Möbius value. Join behavior on atoms identifies the triangular graph automorphism group. Independent direct finite enumerations corroborate the intermediate statements.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The definitions and general theory of induced-path convexity are known, notably Araújo–Sampaio–Szwarcfiter (2013) and Gutierrez–Protti–Tondato (2022). Their accessible primary material studies properties such as convex geometries and computational questions rather than the exact triangular-graph closure lattice, graded rank polynomial, Möbius and automorphism assertions. Dourado–Penso–Rautenbach (2022) studies the hull-number problem. Search comparisons against existing mathematical records and earlier related results found no implication establishing the stated combined theorem. A terminological equivalent remains possible; inaccessible full text on hull numbers leaves a bounded priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a uniform complete classification for an infinite natural family of closure lattices. It turns a local logical rule into a sharp two-round global process; identifies a geometric failure mode; derives rank, cardinality, and Möbius invariants; and isolates a genuine automorphism-group exception at the smallest dimension. The rank sequence's strict log-concavity adds a nontrivial, all-dimensional structural constraint. The result is more than restatement of the generic convexity definition or a finite enumeration.

## Scope and residual risks

The theorem assumes a complete-graph line graph and parallel Horn rounds. It is not asserted for arbitrary line graphs, nor is an exhaustive mathematical priority search possible. Computational checks cover only stated finite ranges and cannot establish infinite claims by themselves.
