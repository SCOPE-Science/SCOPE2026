# Scientific review: two sharp asymmetry thresholds for hyperbolic drums

## Correctness — PASS
The construction intersects one radius-\(r\) hyperbolic ball with two disjoint totally geodesic halfspaces. Splitting the spherical belt, flat disks, and circular curvature ridges into north and south pieces gives exact additive one-cap area and total-mean-curvature formulas; their balanced specialization reproduces Hong's equations (4.1)--(4.2). The one-cap area is strictly decreasing with cut angle, validating the inverse function and the exactly area-preserving perturbation. Independent differentiation establishes the second derivative in the area coordinate. Sixth-order shallow-cut expansions and second-order deep-cut expansions give the two displayed coefficients. Both threshold equations have the stated unique positive radius. Analytic strict endpoint signs follow for every fixed radius away from the critical cases. The symbolic checker and independent high-precision fixed-area perturbations passed. Neither finite calculation is used as proof of a continuum claim.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
Hong, *On Santaló's Problem*, arXiv:2606.01771, section 4, studies a **symmetric** two-plane truncation and its total area and mean curvature, and establishes a counterexample to a proposed fixed-area inequality. It does not determine the fixed-area second variation when the two cuts differ, either endpoint asymptotic, or the two distinct stability thresholds. The present argument acknowledges and uses its geometric one-cap contributions instead of claiming to discover those formulas. Ghomi–Spruck, arXiv:2206.06554, and Hong, arXiv:2603.19646, establish more general curvature inequalities but do not compare balancing of two plane cuts at a fixed parent radius. Search comparisons with local-stability work on distinct geometric functionals did not reveal a dominating theorem. The primary source and relevant general sources were read in their open-access HTML representations. Residual risk: an older or differently named analysis of asymmetrically clipped spheres could already contain this exact local criterion; bibliographic searches cannot prove uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
In the hyperbolic fixed-surface-area total-mean-curvature problem, symmetric truncated balls are a prominent new test family. The finding shows that merely balancing two clipping planes is not uniformly favorable even in the sharply constrained subclass with the parent ball fixed. The two *different exact radii* mark where infinitesimal area-balanced asymmetry reverses its effect at the two canonical geometric ends: shallow ball truncations and deep near-double-disk truncations. In the intermediate radius band the same parent ball supports both strict local-minimum and strict local-maximum balanced configurations at different fixed areas. This is a motivated structural obstruction to a natural symmetrization strategy rather than an arbitrary numerical example.

## Limits and closest sources
The claim is restricted to the antisymmetric cutting variation with fixed parent radius and total surface area. It does not classify all finite cut angles or unrestricted convex minimizers. It makes no sign claim at the critical radii. The motivating public source was first posted June 1, 2026; the current open-access HTML revision was inspected for the explicit formulas. Other relevant sources include Ghomi–Spruck's global Minkowski inequality and Hong's strengthened curvature inequality, whose global bounds do not imply local two-cut signs.
