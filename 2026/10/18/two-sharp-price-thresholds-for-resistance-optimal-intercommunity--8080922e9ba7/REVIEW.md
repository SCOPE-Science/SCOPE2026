# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** A direct three-eigenspace decomposition yields the exact Kirchhoff objective. The trace-inverse Hessian proves global strict convexity even when optimal edges vanish, giving unique unrestricted-edge allocation and justifying symmetry reduction. Three per-price derivatives and complementary slackness establish every support regime, both equality endpoints, closed-form conductances and objective values. An independently constructed full-Laplacian exact-arithmetic program checked 752 assertions including KKT conditions at inactive edges. These finite checks corroborate but do not prove the unbounded-size statement.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The MTNS 2006 and SIAM Review 2008 Ghosh–Boyd–Saberi sources establish the general convex objective, necessary and sufficient KKT conditions, and solve distinct tree, wheel and bridge-barbell settings. The full text of Tovar et al., arXiv:2610.03627, supplies a general linear-cost marginal balance while focusing on resource-exponent and topology effects, not these two-community thresholds. The full text of Bhoite et al., arXiv:2609.29790, optimizes exact-cardinality augmentation of a fixed weighted base network, rather than unconstrained reallocation of every edge. Searches for paired-clique, biclique augmentation, community-priced Kirchhoff minimization and equivalent threshold formulas found no dominating theorem. Nonetheless general convex programming specializes to this setting, so independent verification against additional scattered exact examples remains advisable; absence from searches does not establish novelty.

**Value — PASS.** The result completely classifies the optimal support and conductances for an infinite, naturally parameterized two-community transport-design family. The unequal-group intermediate interval is a genuine structural regime: capacity is purchased inside only the larger group, before the smaller group's internal edges become worth buying. The two exact thresholds are budget independent, merge precisely for balanced groups, and converge to separate limits \(1\) and \(2\) under large imbalance. This distinguishes the result from a routine derivative restatement and gives a directly applicable sizing rule. It does not address physical implementation cost nonlinearities or arbitrary network topology.
