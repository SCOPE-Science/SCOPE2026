# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** The argument gives a complete four-endpoint classification of exposed edges using strict one-Lipschitz potentials. It handles antipodes, common heads/tails, opposite consecutive edges, and disjoint endpoints separately, then constructs a signed two-cover and derives degrees by exact inclusion-exclusion. The regularity boundary uses the strictly positive fourth-part remainder, and the spectral corollary is the invariant fiber-constant Laplacian block. Finite pairwise linear programs and independently generated convex-hull facets were checked on nine and five graph instances respectively. No infinite assertion rests on finite sampling.

**Originality: PASS, with residual bibliographic risk.** The 2018 general extreme-molecule criterion, 2018 complete-multipartite *facet* classification, 2019 *cycle* face-poset classification, and 2023 matroid invariance are not the asserted adjacency result. The closest published mathematical record treats the full two-lift and spectrum only for complete *bipartite* graphs with at least two vertices per part. Our complete tripartite theorem includes unavoidable triangles, so it does not follow from that bipartite instance. The exact multipartite covering, degree formula, and regularity transition were not identified in the inspected references or research-record search. Other unindexed face-lattice evaluations remain possible. Some specialized examples, including complete graphs and four-cycles, are already known and are not independently claimed.

**Value: PASS.** The result determines an entire infinite family's one-skeleton, not merely its vertex or facet count, and reveals a non-obvious regularity phenomenon: arbitrary imbalanced tripartite metrics have degree \(M-1\), while adding a fourth part destroys regularity unless the part sizes balance. The signed-cover description gives a natural structural explanation and a forced Laplacian eigenspace of dimension \(M-1\) in every tripartite case. These facts motivate studying the transport ball's graph rather than recomputing a known facet census.

**Limits.** The theorem concerns unweighted complete multipartite graph metrics with at least three parts. It does not classify the full Laplacian spectrum, higher face lattices, or arbitrary metric weights. The published complete-bipartite two-cover is an explicit predecessor; uniqueness against all mathematical literature is not certified.
