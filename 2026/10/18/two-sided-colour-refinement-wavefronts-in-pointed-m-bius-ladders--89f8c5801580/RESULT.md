# Two-sided colour-refinement wavefronts in pointed Möbius ladders

## Finding
For each integer \(m\ge4\), let \(M_{2m}\) have vertex set \(\mathbb Z/(2m)\), with edges joining residues that differ by \(1\), \(-1\), or \(m\). At round zero, vertex \(0\) has a unique colour and all other vertices share a second colour. Perform synchronous one-dimensional counting colour refinement: a vertex's next colour records its current colour and the multiset of its neighbours' current colours. Let \(P_j\) denote the reflection orbit \(\{j,-j\}\), with \(P_0=\{0\}\), \(P_m=\{m\}\) and \(1\le j\le m-1\).

The partition at round zero is \(P_0\) and \(P_1\cup\cdots\cup P_m\). At round one it is \(P_0\), \(P_1\cup P_m\), and \(P_2\cup\cdots\cup P_{m-1}\). At round two it consists of \(P_0\), \(P_1\cup P_m\), \(P_2\), \(P_{m-1}\), and \(P_3\cup\cdots\cup P_{m-2}\), omitting empty blocks. For every integer \(r\ge3\), until stabilization, the partition comprises each \(P_j\) separately for \(0\le j\le r\) and \(m-r+1\le j\le m\), together with the single block \(P_{r+1}\cup\cdots\cup P_{m-r}\) if the indicated interval is nonempty. In particular the precise number of strict refinement rounds before stabilization is
\[
R(m)=\max\bigl\{3,\lfloor m/2\rfloor\bigr\}.
\]
The final partition is exactly the pointwise stabilizer-orbit partition of the marked graph, with \(m+1\) blocks of sizes \(1,2,\ldots,2,1\). The small boundary \(m=3\), where \(M_6=K_{3,3}\), instead stabilizes after one strict round in three classes matching its larger stabilizer orbits.

## Assumptions and scope
The graphs are finite, undirected, simple, unweighted, and connected. Only vertex \(0\) is individually marked. A *strict refinement round* is one that increases the number of colour classes; the first subsequent update that leaves the partition unchanged is not counted. Colour names are irrelevant. Reflection \(x\mapsto-x\) preserves both the graph and the initial colouring. Thus every refinement class is a union of the sets \(P_j\). The claims refer to refinement inside each given marked graph, not graph identification against an arbitrary second graph.

## Proof
**Automorphism orbits.** For \(m\ge4\), every opposite chord \(\{i,i+m\}\) is contained in exactly two 4-cycles and every circumference edge \(\{i,i+1\}\) is contained in exactly one 4-cycle. To check this, list the three neighbours of each endpoint: the two 4-cycles of a chord use its two cycle directions; a circumference edge belongs to the rectangle made with the corresponding opposite circumference edge. Any 4-cycle with three or four circumference edges would require a cycle of length at most four in the circumference of length \(2m\ge8\), and mixed 4-cycles must have two opposite chords. Therefore an automorphism must preserve the circumference-edge set. Its restriction lies in the dihedral group of the \(2m\)-cycle. Conversely all rotations and reflections preserve opposite chords, hence the full automorphism group is dihedral. The subgroup fixing \(0\) consists of the identity and the reflection, with orbits precisely \(P_0,\ldots,P_m\).

**Multiplicity-weighted quotient.** In the quotient by reflection, let the symbols \(j=0,\ldots,m\) stand for the classes \(P_j\). The multiset of quotient neighbours of \(0\) is \(\{1,1,m\}\); of \(m\) it is \(\{m-1,m-1,0\}\); and of each \(1\le j\le m-1\) it is \(\{j-1,j+1,m-j\}\), with multiplicity retained even if two entries coincide. Every actual vertex in \(P_j\) has exactly these neighbours' class indices, so refinement on this quotient is exactly refinement on the original graph at orbit-class level. This identity also handles a chord fixed by reflection at \(j=m/2\).

**First two rounds.** Initially only \(0\) is unique. Exactly indices \(1,m\) have one neighbour in its class, proving round one. At the next update, \(1,m\) have identical counts to each round-one class; index \(2\) has one neighbour in their joint class; index \(m-1\) has two; all \(3,\ldots,m-2\) have none. This proves the round-two partition. For \(m=4,5\), direct substitution in the displayed quotient neighbourhoods gives all singletons after round three.

**Third round and induction.** For \(m\ge6\), indices \(1,m\) separate at round three: both see index \(0\), but \(1\) additionally sees index \(2\) and index \(m-1\), while \(m\) sees index \(m-1\) twice. Indices \(3,m-2\) split from the round-two middle class: index \(3\) has a neighbour in singleton class \(2\), whereas index \(m-2\) sees both singleton classes \(2\) and \(m-1\); the interior indices \(4,\ldots,m-3\) see neither. This gives the stated round-three partition, including empty or singleton middle intervals.

Suppose the asserted partition holds after round \(r\ge3\), with the remaining middle interval \([r+1,m-r]\) containing at least two indices. Its left endpoint \(r+1\) has an edge to singleton index \(r\); its right endpoint \(m-r\) has an edge to that same singleton via its opposite chord and another edge to singleton index \(m-r+1\) along the circumference. Every strictly interior index has no neighbour in an already singleton class (the other neighbours remain in the middle interval). Consequently the two endpoints acquire two distinct new colours, each different from the common colour of all strictly interior indices. There is no additional split in that interior because each such index has three neighbours in the old middle class. This proves the wavefront assertion inductively. If one or zero middle indices remain, the partition is already discrete and stable.

**Exact first discrete round.** For \(m\ge6\), at rounds \(r\ge3\) the remaining middle indices, if any, form \([r+1,m-r]\), whose size is \(m-2r\). If it has size two or more, refinement must still split. When its size is at most one, all orbit classes are already singletons. Hence the exact first discrete round is \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) for \(m\ge6\), except that for \(m=6,7\) it is round three as already calculated. For \(m=4,5\) it is also round three because round two still merges \(P_1\) and \(P_m\). The formula follows. The automorphism orbit partition is equitable and refinement cannot split it, so the reached discrete quotient partition is stable. Finally, for \(m=3\), the graph is \(K_{3,3}\), and the one-marked partition consists exactly of the marked point, its three neighbours, and the two other vertices on its side; round one gives the three stabilizer orbits.

## Verification
The complete program `verify_direct.py` explicitly builds each graph and checks every predicted partition for \(4\le m\le120\) using synchronous neighbour-multiset refinement. The independently written `verify_quotient.py` implements the multiplicity-weighted quotient and checks all rounds for \(4\le m\le150\), including 32,779,756 pair comparisons. The latter also enumerates all graph automorphisms with NetworkX for \(4\le m\le10\) and checks the rooted reflection orbits. Run using `python verify_direct.py` and `python verify_quotient.py` with Python 3 and NetworkX installed; expected outputs appear in `direct_output.txt` and `quotient_output.txt`. The proof, not finite testing, supplies the all-integer assertion.

## Relationship to prior work
Guy and Harary (1967) introduce the Möbius-ladder graph family. Arvind, Köbler, Rattan and Verbitsky (first public arXiv version February 4, 2015; 2015 manuscript version 3 inspected) formalize stable colour refinement, refinability, orbit partitions and the hierarchy of related graph classes. Their result about arbitrary individualizations of Johnson graphs \(J(n,2)\) concerns a different line-graph family. The exact one-marked two-sided wavefront and sharp iteration count above are not implied by those framework results. The entire four-page Guy–Harary article was inspected: its theorem is about crossing number one, not stable equitable partitions, individualized vertex orbits, or refinement depth. Specialized later literature might still contain the exact assertion, so independent publication-priority review remains outstanding. Other work on Möbius-ladder homology studies a distinct invariant, so sharing the graph family does not yield a refinement-depth implication.

## Limitations
This theorem is restricted to a single distinguished vertex and ordinary synchronous one-dimensional counting refinement. It does not establish arbitrary-individualization refinability, identify the graph against arbitrary competitors, or determine higher-dimensional Weisfeiler–Leman behaviour. The comparison covers the primary 1967 article and the 2015 refinement framework, but is not an exhaustive survey of all later specialized articles. An independent scientific audit has not been performed.
