# Two singularity types and a cubic crossover in the Laplace Gabor spectral distribution

## Finding
For each \(\lambda>0\), the canonical Zak spectral distribution of the twofold-redundant Gabor frame generated by \(g_\lambda(t)=e^{-\lambda|t|}\) has two different interior singularities. A nondegenerate smooth saddle produces a logarithmic density divergence at
\[
E_{\mathrm{s}}(\lambda)=\frac{2}{\cosh\lambda-1},
\]
whereas the nonsmooth translation seam produces a one-sided square-root density cusp at
\[
E_{\mathrm{c}}(\lambda)=2\tanh^2\lambda.
\]
Their energy ordering reverses exactly once, at
\[
\lambda_*=\operatorname{arcosh}\bigl(1+2\cos(2\pi/7)\bigr)=1.4490747226775864\ldots.
\]
The transition is the unique positive solution of \((\cosh\lambda)^3-2(\cosh\lambda)^2-\cosh\lambda+1=0\). The normalized spectral density is also given below by an exact one-dimensional integral, not merely by its singular asymptotics.

Set \(r=e^{-2\lambda}\), \(h_0=1/\sinh\lambda\), and
\[
P(\theta)=\frac{1-r^2}{1-2r\cos\theta+r^2},\qquad
Q(\theta)=\frac{r((1+r^2)\cos\theta-2r)}{(1-2r\cos\theta+r^2)^2}.
\]
Writing \(H(x)=\cosh(\lambda(1-2x))/\sinh\lambda\), the exact Zak multiplier is
\[
F_\lambda(x,\theta)=2\bigl[H(x)P(\theta)+4Q(\theta)\bigr].
\]
For \(h_E(\theta)=[E/2-4Q(\theta)]/P(\theta)\), its spectral density is
\[
\rho_\lambda(E)=\frac1{4\pi\lambda}\int_0^{2\pi}
\frac{\mathbf1_{\{h_0<h_E(\theta)<\coth\lambda\}}}
{P(\theta)\sqrt{h_E(\theta)^2-h_0^2}}\,d\theta.
\]
The density is understood almost everywhere, with singular-point values immaterial.

Define positive coefficients
\[
A_\lambda=\frac{8\lambda^2}{\sinh\lambda}\frac{1+r}{1-r},\quad
B_\lambda=4r\left[\frac{1+r}{\sinh\lambda(1-r)^3}+\frac{2(1+4r+r^2)}{(1-r)^4}\right],
\]
\[
a_\lambda=4\lambda\tanh\lambda,\qquad
b_\lambda=\frac32\tanh^2\lambda\,\operatorname{sech}^2\lambda.
\]
Then for each fixed \(\lambda>0\), as \(E\to E_{\mathrm{s}}(\lambda)\),
\[
\rho_\lambda(E)=\frac1{\pi\sqrt{A_\lambda B_\lambda}}
\log\frac1{|E-E_{\mathrm{s}}(\lambda)|}+O_\lambda(1).
\]
For each fixed \(\lambda>0\) with \(\lambda\ne\lambda_*\), as \(\delta\downarrow0\),
\[
\rho_\lambda(E_{\mathrm{c}}-\delta)-\rho_\lambda(E_{\mathrm{c}}+\delta)
=\frac{2}{\pi a_\lambda\sqrt{b_\lambda}}\sqrt\delta+o_\lambda(\sqrt\delta)
=\frac{\cosh\lambda}{\pi\sqrt6\,\lambda\tanh^2\lambda}\sqrt\delta+o_\lambda(\sqrt\delta).
\]
At \(\lambda=\lambda_*\) the two critical energy levels coincide; the logarithmic leading term remains valid, but the separate cusp difference statement is deliberately not asserted there.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(\mathcal G_\lambda=\{e^{\pi i m t}g_\lambda(t-n):m,n\in\mathbb Z\}\) act on \(L^2(\mathbb R)\), with the unnormalized window \(g_\lambda(t)=e^{-\lambda|t|}\). Its bounded positive frame operator is \(S_\lambda\). For \(Zf(x,\omega)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}f(x+n)e^{-2\pi i n\omega}\), \(0\le x,\omega<1\), define the probability measure \(\nu_\lambda\) as the pushforward of normalized Lebesgue measure on the Zak cell by the real multiplier of \(ZS_\lambda Z^{-1}\). Equivalently, for every bounded continuous \(\Phi\),
\[
\int\Phi(E)\,d\nu_\lambda(E)=\int_0^1\int_0^1\Phi(F_\lambda(x,4\pi\omega))\,d\omega\,dx.
\]
The theorems concern this canonical normalized trace-per-cell spectral law, **not** a discrete list of eigenvalues of the infinite-dimensional operator. Angles are modulo \(2\pi\). The parameters \(\lambda\) are fixed for each asymptotic; no uniform assertion at the collision is made.

## Proof
**1. Zak diagonalization and exact multiplier.** Standard Fourier-series orthogonality of modulation with frequency step \(1/2\) gives the Walnut/Zak identity
\[
(ZS_\lambda f)(x,\omega)=\bigl(|Zg_\lambda(x,\omega)|^2+|Zg_\lambda(x,\omega+1/2)|^2\bigr)(Zf)(x,\omega).
\]
This identity also follows directly for compactly supported test functions by Poisson summation and extends by density, since the exponential window has absolutely summable translates. For every integer \(j\ge0\) and \(0\le x\le1\), direct separation of the integer summation into the intervals before, between, and after the two shifted centers yields
\[
R_j(x):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}g_\lambda(x+k)g_\lambda(x+k+j)
=e^{-\lambda j}\left(\frac{\cosh(\lambda(1-2x))}{\sinh\lambda}+j\right).
\]
The formula also holds for negative \(j\) after replacing \(j\) by \(|j|\). Cancelling odd Fourier correlations in the sum of the two squared Zak transforms gives
\[
F_\lambda(x,\theta)=2\biggl[H(x)+2\sum_{j\ge1}r^j(H(x)+2j)\cos(j\theta)\biggr]
=2\bigl[H(x)P(\theta)+4Q(\theta)\bigr].
\]
Both series converge absolutely with every angular derivative for \(\lambda>0\).

**2. Spectral density and critical points.** With uniform \(x\in[0,1]\), \(H(x)\) ranges from \(h_0\) to \(h_1=\coth\lambda\) and has exact density
\[
p_H(h)=\frac{\mathbf1_{(h_0,h_1)}(h)}{\lambda\sqrt{h^2-h_0^2}}.
\]
Indeed, on the two monotone half-intervals the Jacobian equals \((2\lambda\sqrt{h^2-h_0^2})^{-1}\). Since \(P(\theta)>0\), conditioning on \(\theta\) and using \(\partial F/\partial h=2P\) proves the one-dimensional density formula and absolute continuity of \(\nu_\lambda\).

The function \(H(x)\) decreases on \((0,1/2)\), increases on \((1/2,1)\), and has a strictly positive quadratic minimum at \(x=1/2\), whereas it has a downward cusp on the circle at \(x=0\). To exclude hidden critical energies, put \(u=\cos\theta\). The numerator of \(\partial F/\partial u\), after multiplication by the positive quantity \((1-2ru+r^2)^3/(4r)\), is the affine function
\[
T(u)=H(x)(1-r^2)(1-2ru+r^2)
+2\bigl[(1+r^2)(1-2ru+r^2)+4r((1+r^2)u-2r)\bigr].
\]
For the least admissible \(H=h_0\), \(T(-1)>0\) and \(T(1)>0\): writing \(s=e^{-\lambda}\), the factor \(h_0(1-r^2)+2(1-4r+r^2)\) controlling \(T(-1)\) simplifies to \(2(1-s)^2(s^2+3s+1)>0\); at \(u=1\) all remaining terms are positive. Increasing \(H\) increases \(T\). Thus \(\partial F/\partial u>0\) for all \(x,u\). The only smooth stationary point with mixed Hessian signs is \((x,\theta)=(1/2,0)\); the other smooth stationary point \((1/2,\pi)\) is a strict minimum. The only seam stationary angular values are \((0,0)\), a global maximum, and \((0,\pi)\), the cusp energy. Direct substitution yields the two claimed energy formulas.

**3. Logarithmic density divergence.** The Hessian at the smooth saddle is diagonal; direct differentiation gives
\[
\partial_x^2F_\lambda(1/2,0)=A_\lambda>0,
\qquad -\partial_\theta^2F_\lambda(1/2,0)=B_\lambda>0.
\]
The ordinary analytic Morse lemma, or a direct smooth change to the two quadratic normal coordinates, therefore yields locally
\[
F_\lambda-E_{\mathrm{s}}=\frac12A_\lambda X^2-\frac12B_\lambda Y^2
\]
up to a smooth Jacobian with value one at the critical point. Integrating the resulting delta function over a bounded rectangle, against measure \(dX\,dY/(2\pi)\), gives precisely the logarithmic coefficient \(1/(\pi\sqrt{A_\lambda B_\lambda})\). The difference between the exact Jacobian and its value at the saddle contributes a bounded term. Off this critical neighborhood, the pushforward density is bounded near this energy by the implicit function theorem; even at the coincident cusp its local density is bounded. This proves the stated \(O_\lambda(1)\) remainder.

**4. Nonsmooth corner law.** At \(x=0\) modulo integer translations and \(\theta=\pi\), choose local signed coordinates \(s\) across the seam and \(v=\theta-\pi\). The exact formula gives
\[
F_\lambda=E_{\mathrm{c}}-a_\lambda|s|+b_\lambda v^2
+O_\lambda(s^2+|s|v^2+v^4),
\]
where \(a_\lambda=4\lambda\tanh\lambda\), and
\[
b_\lambda=\frac{6r(1-r)^2}{(1+r)^4}
=\frac32\tanh^2\lambda\operatorname{sech}^2\lambda>0.
\]
For the leading local model, integrating first in \(s\) gives a density proportional to the set of admissible \(v\) for which \(b_\lambda v^2>E-E_{\mathrm{c}}\). Crossing from \(E_{\mathrm{c}}-\delta\) to \(E_{\mathrm{c}}+\delta\) therefore removes an interval of angular length \(2\sqrt{\delta/b_\lambda}\). There are two sides of the seam, each contributing inverse Jacobian \(1/a_\lambda\). The normalized angular measure \(dv/(2\pi)\) gives the coefficient \(2/(\pi a_\lambda\sqrt{b_\lambda})\). The smooth higher-order corrections to the model contribute \(o_\lambda(\sqrt\delta)\) by rescaling \(v=\sqrt\delta\,w\) near the moving boundary. Away from the seam, the density is locally continuously differentiable at \(E_{\mathrm{c}}\) provided \(E_{\mathrm{c}}\ne E_{\mathrm{s}}\); its symmetric difference is \(O_\lambda(\delta)\). This proves the asserted square-root difference for \(\lambda\ne\lambda_*\).

**5. Exact collision and ordering.** Put \(c=\cosh\lambda>1\). The identity \(2/(c-1)=2(c^2-1)/c^2\) rearranges to \(c^3-2c^2-c+1=0\). This cubic is negative for \(1<c\le2\), then strictly increasing after its unique positive turning point, and has one root greater than \(1\). The trigonometric triple-angle identity shows that the root is \(1+2\cos(2\pi/7)\). The two energies are ordered \(E_{\mathrm{s}}>E_{\mathrm{c}}\) below the collision and \(E_{\mathrm{s}}<E_{\mathrm{c}}\) above it. These statements are independent of the numerical approximations.

## Relationship to prior work
The August 5, 2026 theorem of de Dios Pont, Gröchenig, Liehr, Shafkulovska and Taylor establishes the qualitative frame set for totally positive windows, including this Laplace family. Faulhuber and Shafkulovska (2023), especially their Section 8, already determine sharp extreme Gabor frame bounds for two-sided exponential windows at integer oversampling; the present finding **does not claim those extrema as new**. Kloos and Stöckler (2013) establish general Zak-transform methods for totally positive windows. The distinct content here is the complete spectral *distribution* and two inequivalent interior singular mechanisms, together with their exact nontrivial crossing. These do not follow from knowledge of the extremal bounds alone. General Zak diagonalization and Morse singularity methods are standard ingredients; historical equivalence via an unindexed calculation remains a residual priority risk.

## Limitations
The result is confined to the unnormalized symmetric two-sided exponential window and lattice parameters \(\alpha=1\), \(\beta=1/2\). It does not claim a corresponding classification for other Gabor windows, noninteger redundancy, arbitrary lattice shapes, a discrete eigenvalue spectrum, or a uniform two-parameter crossover expansion near \(\lambda_*\). The logarithmic statement remains valid at the crossing; the separate square-root-difference asymptotic was intentionally restricted to \(\lambda\ne\lambda_*\).
