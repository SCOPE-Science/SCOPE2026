# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The complete proof uses Walsh-character diagonalization of \(H(n,q)\), establishes every per-eigenvalue two-sink Gram rank, and proves that no eigenmode outside the orthogonal spectral complement can survive local positive loss. The Fourier-character phase argument handles all boundary cases \(n\ge1\), \(q\ge2\), and \(1\le d\le n\). A Gram-pseudoinverse derivation proves the localized Krawtchouk formula. Independent full adjacency calculations reproduced 174 rank cases and 6366 source projector values; 12 finite no-jump propagations confirmed the claimed long-time limit. Computations alone do not imply generality; the analytic proof does.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The known single-sink spectral reduction and binary antipodal redundancy are explicit precedents, not a claimed new theorem. Moore–Russell provides coherent hypercube spectral theory but no two-trap capture law. Schuld–Sinayskiy–Petruccione uses directed Lindblad sink routing after deleting coherent sink links; Makmal–Tiersch–Ganahl–Briegel uses a discrete coined measured walk on nonlocally modified hypercubes, and Le–Donati–Severini–Caruso develops general dark-subspace methods without the two-trap Hamming rank classification. Closest accessible earlier and later literature was examined against mathematical model, quantifiers, and implication rather than words in titles. Search results and one prior-deduplication comparison do not prove global novelty. The main remaining risk is an equivalent unindexed theorem in spectral graph controllability or coherent trapping of Hamming association schemes.

## Value — PASS
This is a complete, natural all-size classification of a loss-protected subspace in a standard quantum-network family: it settles how two detector placements affect eventual capture, identifies binary-only antipodal redundancy, and resolves exact survival for each localized source. The nontrivial distinction between alphabet sizes and the explicit geometrical survival deficit make it a useful calibration case for multi-absorber coherent transport, rather than a single graph computation or arbitrary parameter slice.

## Sources and scientific limitations
Moore–Russell (2001), Schuld–Sinayskiy–Petruccione (2014), Makmal–Tiersch–Ganahl–Briegel (2015/2016), Le–Donati–Severini–Caruso (2017/2018), and Angeles-Canul and colleagues (2009) were examined in relation to the claim. Applicability is restricted to exact unweighted Hamming adjacency with two strictly positive local loss rates. Spectral ranks do not assert fast absorption or robustness to coherent disorder.
