# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The result is a complete equivalence between strong subresiduated expansions of a **fixed finite upset lattice** and idempotent regressive isotone maps on its dual poset. Preservation of union and intersection and idempotence are derived directly from the subresiduation definition; finite distributive lattice duality gives the map representation. The unique greatest-fixed-predecessor characterization is proved, and the singleton-layer condition yields disjoint recurrences. The claimed orbit action is precisely independent within-level permutations. The actual standalone verifier exhaustively matched both counting recurrences on 127 level-size profiles of total size at most seven and checked further large profiles, direct bounded-sublattice enumeration, and literal group orbits. The proof, not finite enumeration, establishes all positive profiles.

## Originality — PASS with specified residual risks

The general poset fixed-image lemma and arbitrary-poset branching algorithm are already known from Denton (2011) and Hivert–Schilling–Thiéry (2013); these are explicitly credited. Celani and San Martín (2023) formulate the strong subresiduated variety but do not supply these exact weak-order labeled and isomorphism-class transfer recurrences in inspected sections. Related public research summaries address all monotone maps on weak orders or generic (not necessarily regressive) retract images, without specifying the enumerated strong-lattice expansions and their two-state orbit formulas. Targeted prior-record and literature searches did not identify an exact matching recurrence or stronger formula that directly implies it. Two close research summaries were accessible only as abstracts; equivalent specialized enumeration in inaccessible full text remains a risk. A failed or empty search is not treated as proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The problem is an exact enumeration of a natural algebraic-logical variety over an infinite and highly symmetric family of finite distributive lattices, with arbitrary layer widths and lengths rather than a single small table. The two-state description replaces exponential enumeration of image subsets with a profile-linear algorithm, gives a complete orbit classification under algebra isomorphism, and includes closed all-height uniform-width formulas. The result does not claim a new generic image-set theorem, a new integer sequence, or a paper-scale advance; its value is the exact natural family census and compressed orbit calculation.
