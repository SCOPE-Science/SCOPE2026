# Three-axis scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The claim uses the standard reflection-containment definition of Minkowski asymmetry. The two endpoints of the Cayley hull suffice for containment, and their support inequalities are derived with every scaling and height factor explicit. For symmetric bases, the horizontal zero translation achieves the exact lower bound of a two-variable linear program, and the optimizer is unique. Three candidate LP vertices give all cases including boundaries. The full fiber inequality follows by substitution, and the orthotope classification follows by comparing sums of absolute coordinate supports. The strict upper asymmetry bound and crossed-box sharpness family are analytical. The standard-library checker verifies 1,320 integer box profiles and 46,200 directional inequalities with exact rational arithmetic; these finite checks are corroborative, not a universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, with recorded risk

The inspected Cayley-sum literature studies integer decomposition and related algebraic properties, whereas the inspected prismatoid geometry literature studies unfoldings. Neither establishes the present reflection-factor optimizer. Homothetic-frustum identities are subsumed as a special case, and free-join formulas assume independent base coordinates rather than a shared spatial block; neither gives the nonhomothetic two-ratio law or all its center fibers. Exact database and literature alias searches found other asymmetry families but no decisive covering implication. This is best-knowledge originality, not a guarantee against unindexed older equivalent work. Some indexed related items were inspectable only as summaries; no negative whole-document conclusion rests on those snippets.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result treats arbitrary symmetric convex bodies, not a constructed numerical specimen. It translates continuous geometric support constraints into two body-to-body comparison ratios and a complete three-phase classification, with all minimizing centers. The orthotope corollary resolves center-locus dimension transitions exactly, and the sharp dimension-independent asymmetry cap is attained as a limit through crossed boxes. These give structural geometric information beyond the scalar value for homothetic frusta.

## Literature and limitations

The directly inspected papers were Hibi–Ohsugi–Tsuchiya, *Integer decomposition property for Cayley sums of order and stable set polytopes* (arXiv:1807.05989v1), and Bian–Demaine–Madhukara, *Edge-Unfolding Prismatoids: Tall or Rectangular Base* (arXiv:2106.14262v1). Neither main result implies the claimed reflection-containment law. The theorem requires centrally symmetric bases; arbitrary-body center-fiber dimension, bibliography completeness and independent mathematical validation remain unresolved.
