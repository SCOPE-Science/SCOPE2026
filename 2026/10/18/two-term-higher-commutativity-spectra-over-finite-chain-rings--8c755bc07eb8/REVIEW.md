# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The argument reduces group commutation to zero \(2\times2\) minors of a \(2\times r\) matrix. Each matrix lies either in the automatically vanishing high-valuation stratum or in a unique primitive stratum. The count of primitive outer products is obtained from a free unit-group action, and all lifting factors are explicit. The parity-dependent geometric sums give the same two-term expression. The maximal-abelian order bound uses Smith normal form on the \(R\)-span, not an unjustified assumption that the additive projection is automatically an \(R\)-submodule. Finite checks independently confirm selected cases; they are not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with risk
Torres-Giese establishes the general commuting-tuple invariant and finite Dirichlet-series framework. Levit–Shwartz establish a general spectrum/leading-coefficient theorem and evaluate cyclic split quotients. The latter covers the cyclic additive-quotient subfamily indirectly, so that subfamily alone is not asserted as novel. Their field-valued Heisenberg theorem addresses commutator values in a field, not arbitrary length-\(m\) chain rings. The evaluated uniform two-level spectrum for every ring type, including the noncyclic quotient cases, was not found in the inspected relevant sections or recorded claims. A more specialized determinantal local-density formula may have appeared elsewhere and remains a priority risk.

## Value — PASS
The hierarchy counts every ordered pairwise-commuting tuple, rather than a fixed small tuple length. The two-term collapse gives an exact minimal order-two recurrence and an explicit count of maximal-order abelian subgroups, making the general spectral picture concrete in a nilpotent group family with non-field coefficient rings. Independence from mixed/equal characteristic is a structural assertion not forced by the field case.

## Literature and limits
Primary comparisons: Torres-Giese, arXiv:1005.3876 (Sections 1–3 and its stated section structure); Levit–Shwartz, arXiv:2605.02071 (introduction, Theorems A, 4.9, 4.18 and conclusion); and Levit–Shwartz, arXiv:2605.17171 (Section 7, Definitions 7.12 and Theorems 7.13–7.14). Searches also tested zero-minor, rank-one, additive-quotient and finite-chain-ring aliases. Limits: no higher unitriangular ranks, no orbit classes, no non-chain rings, and no complete literature-priority guarantee.
