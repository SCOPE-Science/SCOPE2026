# Review: Type-independent commuting-idempotent counts in finite Coxeter zero-Hecke monoids

## Correctness — PASS
In a finite zero-Hecke monoid, commuting parabolic-longest idempotents multiply to the parabolic longest idempotent of their union. The Bruhat subword property and a directed Coxeter-element precedence path prohibit mixed exclusive support in one diagram component; conversely, a component fully contained in either support is absorbed by its parabolic longest idempotent. Counting the permissible subset-pair labels gives the component-weight formula. A three-message rooted-tree recurrence and verified branch-addition identities prove shape independence for every subcubic tree. All finite irreducible Coxeter diagrams belong to that graph class. Direct multiplication tests cover selected Coxeter systems, and a separate graph enumeration covers every labeled subcubic tree of size at most six. Tests corroborate, but do not replace, the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with limitations
The type-A count and its sequence were previously known, including a Catalan-monoid quotient explanation; the recurrence itself appears as OEIS A007070. They are **not** claimed to be new. In the inspected sections of Ganyushkin–Mazorchuk, arXiv:1006.0316, the classification of 0-Hecke idempotents is established, not the Coxeter-general commutation criterion or all-type count. Hivert–Schilling–Thiéry, arXiv:1012.1361, provide the general Coxeter 0-Hecke formalism in connection with a different biHecke monoid. Searches for equivalent all-type and branching-tree statements did not reveal a covering theorem. Closest published research records found via semantic search concern different graph-component weights and different algebraic objects. An unlocated equivalent theorem remains a residual originality risk; this is not a claim of exhaustive priority.

## Value — PASS
Pairwise commuting idempotents measure a natural internal structure of a finite monoid. The equality of this invariant in the same rank across types A, B, D, E, F, G, H and the noncrystallographic families is not a rank-counting tautology; it requires a structural criterion and a branching-tree addition identity. The theorem determines the exact invariant in every rank, including products of irreducible components. The degree-four star provides a sharp obstruction to unrestricted tree-shape invariance.

## Boundaries
The result counts ordered idempotent pairs within a monoid and does not address idempotents of its monoid algebra. The numerical type-A recurrence predates the present comparison. The finite tests use Coxeter types A, B, D only, but the general proof uses the graph classification. No exhaustive literature-uniqueness determination or independent audit was performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
