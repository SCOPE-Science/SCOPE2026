# Uniform codimension-three singular strata of the isotropic Plücker quadric

## Finding
For integers \(2\leq k\leq n-2\), equip \(W=\mathbb C^n\) with the standard nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form \(\langle v,w\rangle=v^Tw\). Write \(p_I\) for the Plücker coordinates of a \(k\)-plane \(U\). The isotropic Plücker quadric section is
\[
Q_{k,n}=\bigl\{U\in\operatorname{Gr}(k,W):\sum_{|I|=k}p_I^2=0\bigr\}.
\]
Its scheme-theoretic singular locus is the determinantal locus
\[
\operatorname{Sing}(Q_{k,n})=\{U:\dim(U\cap U^\perp)\geq2\}
\]
with the ideal generated in each Grassmann chart by all \((k-1)\)-minors of the Gram matrix of the restricted bilinear form. For every possible \(2\leq r\leq\min(k,n-k)\), the exact-radical-\(r\) stratum is smooth and has codimension \(r(r+1)/2\) in \(\operatorname{Gr}(k,n)\). Therefore
\[
\operatorname{codim}_{\operatorname{Gr}(k,n)}\operatorname{Sing}(Q_{k,n})=3,
\]
independent of \(k,n\) in the stated range. Every point of radical dimension two has a transverse ordinary double point of type \(A_1\), with transverse local equation \(ac-b^2=0\). The singular locus is irreducible for \(n\geq5\); for \((k,n)=(2,4)\) it consists of two copies of \(\mathbb P^1\), embedded as the two families of maximal isotropic planes.

## Assumptions and scope
All varieties and orthogonality are over \(\mathbb C\). The standard bilinear form is symmetric, not Hermitian. A \(k\)-plane is nondegenerate if its restricted Gram matrix is invertible. Its radical is \(R=U\cap U^\perp\). The adjective *singular* refers to the scheme-theoretic singular locus of the **quadric section of the Plücker Grassmannian**, not to the ordinary Grassmannian, which is smooth. The range \(2\leq k\leq n-2\) is essential: for \(k=1\) or \(n-k=1\), radical dimension two cannot occur.

## Proof
**1. The quadratic section is the determinant of the Gram form.** Represent \(U\) by a full-rank \(k\times n\) matrix \(A\). Cauchy–Binet gives
\[
\sum_{|I|=k}\det(A_I)^2=\det(AA^T).
\]
Rescaling \(A\) multiplies both sides by the square of its determinant, so this defines the Plücker quadric globally. Write \(G=AA^T\).

**2. Scheme-theoretic critical ideal.** In any standard Grassmann chart take \(A=[I_k\ B]\), where \(B\) is \(k\times(n-k)\). Then \(G=I_k+BB^T\), and the Jacobian formula gives
\[
\frac{\partial\det G}{\partial b_{ij}}=2(\operatorname{adj}(G)B)_{ij}.
\]
Since \(\operatorname{adj}(G)G=(\det G)I_k\), we also have
\[
\operatorname{adj}(G)= (\det G)I_k-\operatorname{adj}(G)BB^T.
\]
Thus every entry of \(\operatorname{adj}(G)\) belongs to the ideal generated by \(\det G\) and its first partials. Conversely every partial belongs to the ideal of entries of \(\operatorname{adj}(G)\), and \(\det G\) is in that ideal by expanding along a row. Consequently the two ideals are **equal**, without taking radicals. The cofactors of \(G\) vanish exactly when \(\operatorname{rank}G\leq k-2\), or equivalently \(\dim R\geq2\). This proves the singular scheme identification. It also independently recovers the cofactor form of the local singular ideal appearing in Friedman–Hoşten–Rosana, Proposition 2.4.

**3. Exact dimension of every radical stratum.** Fix an integer \(r\) with \(1\leq r\leq\min(k,n-k)\). First choose the totally isotropic radical \(R\) in the orthogonal Grassmannian \(\operatorname{OGr}(r,n)\). The usual isotropic-subspace deformation calculation gives
\[
\dim\operatorname{OGr}(r,n)
=r(n-r)-\frac{r(r+1)}2.
\]
Now \(U/R\) is a \((k-r)\)-plane in the nondegenerate quotient \(R^\perp/R\), of dimension \(n-2r\). Requiring the restriction to \(U/R\) to be nondegenerate is an open, nonempty condition in \(\operatorname{Gr}(k-r,n-2r)\), with dimension \((k-r)(n-k-r)\). The exact-radical-\(r\) stratum is therefore a smooth locally trivial open Grassmann bundle over \(\operatorname{OGr}(r,n)\), of dimension
\[
r(n-r)-\frac{r(r+1)}2+(k-r)(n-k-r).
\]
Subtracting from \(k(n-k)\) gives \(r(r+1)/2\). The stratum \(r=2\) exists in every stated pair \((k,n)\), so the full singular locus has codimension precisely three; the higher-radical strata have strictly larger codimension.

**4. Local transverse singularity and closure.** At a plane with radical \(R\) of dimension \(r\), the tangent map from \(\operatorname{Hom}(U,W/U)\) to \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(R^*)\), obtained by differentiating the Gram form on \(R\), is surjective. Indeed the nondegenerate ambient form induces a surjection \(W/U\longrightarrow R^*\), and a prescribed symmetric form is obtained by choosing a half of it as the pairings of a deformation on \(R\). Using local coordinates and a nondegenerate complement to \(R\) in \(U\), Schur complementation reduces the defining determinant, up to a local unit, to the determinant of a symmetric \(r\times r\) matrix of independent transverse local coordinates. For \(r=2\) this is exactly \(ac-b^2\); hence all radical-two points have transverse \(A_1\) type. The same submersion permits a nearby deformation of a radical-\(r\) plane, for \(r>2\), into one with radical precisely two, so every higher-radical stratum lies in the closure of the radical-two stratum.

**5. Components.** The exact-radical-two stratum is an open Grassmann bundle over \(\operatorname{OGr}(2,n)\). The latter is irreducible for \(n\geq5\), so its bundle and its closure are irreducible. By Step 4 the closure is the entire singular locus. For \(n=4\), the only allowed \(k\) is two, and \(\operatorname{OGr}(2,4)\) has its two classical components of maximal isotropic planes, each isomorphic to \(\mathbb P^1\). Their union is exactly the singular locus.

## Relationship to prior work
Friedman, Hoşten and Rosana (arXiv:2610.02018v1, Section 2, Proposition 2.4) identify the singular ideal through cocircuit-matrix pairings and prove an upper bound \(\binom{k+1}{2}\) for its codimension (Theorem 2.3); they state exact codimension three for \(k=2\) only (Corollary 2.5). The present geometric radical stratification supplies exact codimension **three in every interior Grassmannian**, determines component structure including the exceptional split case, and identifies the transverse \(A_1\) singularities. Classical orthogonal Grassmannian dimension formulae underpin Step 3. A separate, earlier squared-Grassmannian construction concerns a quotient under coordinate sign changes, not this quadratic section.

## Limitations
The statement concerns the specific standard isotropic Plücker quadric, not all quadratic sections or arbitrary subvarieties. Its exact singular codimension does not, by itself, assert that every projective three-dimensional model meets the singular locus or settle ED-generality thresholds. Higher-radical transversal singularities are not classified beyond the indicated symmetric-determinant local model. Established general orthogonal-degeneracy methods may give another derivation of these properties; absence of an independently identified identical theorem is not proof of historical priority.
