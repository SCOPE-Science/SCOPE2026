# Review of uniform critical-window long-range relaxation

**Correctness — PASS.** Fourier characters diagonalize the precisely normalized periodization of the infinite jump *rates*. Positivity of the Laplace-integral sine kernel rigorously locates the gap at the first wave number. Two integrations of the standard logarithmic cosine series give the marginal constant. A uniform Taylor/tail estimate and a uniform integral-test estimate yield the crossover for bounded moving exponent. Independently rerun `gap_check.py`; its 125 checks supplement rather than establish the asymptotic proof. All assumptions, signs, rates, finite-size indices, and log-domain restrictions were reviewed.

**Originality — PASS, subject to independent verification.** The Catalano et al. motivating source gives fixed exponents and a marginal-time assertion, while Gonçalves–Scotta already identifies the marginal \(N^2/\log N\) time scale in a *different* boundary-driven process. The originality claim is therefore restricted to the compact-uniform moving-exponent spectral crossover \(\Phi(z)=(1-e^{-z})/z\) for the precisely periodized single-particle rate generator, together with its exact marginal normalization. Related primary texts and semantic mathematical-record searches did not identify that formulation or a stronger result implying it. General regular-variation and Abelian theorems may imply the crossover implicitly and remain a residual risk. No blanket novelty is asserted for the logarithmic exponent alone.

**Scientific value — PASS.** The \(p-3\asymp1/\log N\) window is the unique scale on which a diverging truncated second moment and a slightly summable second moment compete. Its explicit \(z\)-profile tells how to tune an exponent near the normal/superdiffusive boundary, and fixes the sign and normalization of the relaxation-time logarithm. It resolves a physically motivated marginal finite-size ambiguity. The bound is uniform and infinitely many finite sizes/exponents are covered, rather than a routine one-parameter substitution.

**Closest prior works and limits.** Catalano et al., arXiv:2212.07744 (rate mapping and fixed-exponent discussion); Gonçalves–Scotta, arXiv:2004.03978 (known critical time scale, different boundary-driven model); Bernardin et al., arXiv:2007.01621 (noncritical superdiffusive regime with reservoirs). This is a theorem for periodized effective rates, not for all finite open-boundary quantum models or nonlinear many-body relaxation. Relevant broader Tauberian implications remain to be checked independently.
