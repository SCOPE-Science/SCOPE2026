# Review of the uniform Hilbert–Schmidt crossover

**Correctness (C): PASS.** Expansion against orthonormal monomials gives the stated exact matrix. Uniform Stirling estimates on the compact shift interval and the beta integral give squared row masses \(\Gamma(\alpha+4+\delta)\Gamma(-\alpha)n^{-1-\delta}+O_\alpha(n^{-2-\delta})\). Because \(-1<\alpha<0\) and \(\delta_0<-\alpha\), the columns excluded by the finite square section contribute only \(O_\alpha(1)\). Integral comparison then proves the crossover and the zeta pole. The included independently evaluated finite matrices check normalizations but are not treated as a proof of a limit.

**Originality (O): PASS, subject to a historical-priority risk.** Li–Zhou's differential Hilbert operator study establishes norm, essential norm, compactness and Schatten-class membership, not the explicit two-parameter Hilbert–Schmidt residue and cutoff crossover. Tang–Zhang's generalized Hilbert operator study considers Schatten criteria in the unweighted-source-and-target setting, but does not imply a sharp near-critical two-weight coefficient. Pushnitski's general Hardy-kernel spectral-density theorem assumes Hermitian homogeneous kernels and does not state the present uniformly moving two-weight exponent law. The closest available research-index records concern distinct logarithmic Bergman endpoints and other generalized Hilbert norms. The mathematics uses standard gamma and beta identities, but the complete uniform two-limit normalization is the assessed claim. A possible unindexed two-weight sum asymptotic is an unresolved priority risk.

**Value (V): PASS.** At a natural bounded/noncompact transition, the Hilbert–Schmidt mass of degree-truncated operators changes from logarithmic growth to a simple pole under arbitrarily small supercritical shifts. Identifying the **exact** coefficient and universal mixed-scale function explains how the truncation degree must grow to observe the singular critical regime. The result provides more information than compactness or Schatten membership and is an exact benchmark for regularizing these analytic operators.
