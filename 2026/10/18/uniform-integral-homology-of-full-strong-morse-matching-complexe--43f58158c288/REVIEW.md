# Scientific review: integral homology of full strong matching complexes of cycles

## Correctness

**PASS.** The claimed domain is the full strong discrete Morse matching complex of the one-dimensional cycle for every \(n\ge6\), with integral reduced homology, not a pure or ordinary Morse complex. The independent cyclic-word characterization was reconstructed directly from strict vertex heights. The auxiliary bipartite independence complex contains precisely those faces satisfying the local sign-consistency rule. The relative chains omitted by the global acyclicity rule split into the two monochromatic simplex-relative-Alexander-dual pairs. A cover of the bipartite independence complex by a simplex and a contractible union of closed stars has intersection the Alexander dual of \(\operatorname{Ind}_2(C_n)\). These yield the stated exact integer homology sequence. The established integral sphere-wedge results for \(\operatorname{Ind}_1(C_n)\) and \(\operatorname{Ind}_2(C_n)\) determine all terms. Their respective nonzero degrees do not coincide in the same long-exact-sequence position for \(n\ge6\); the right-hand terms are free, so all group extensions split. The bounds, degree shifts, and coincidence cases have been checked. The standalone checker confirms strict-filtration face membership, auxiliary models, small-chain boundary ranks, and higher-independence acyclic matchings. None of these finite tests is incorrectly presented as an infinite or integral proof.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to literature-coverage risk.** The closest direct source, Knudson and Owens-White, *Complexes of strong discrete Morse matchings* (arXiv:2609.06844v1; Sections 4 and 8, Table 1), explicitly computes full-cycle homology only through \(n=21\) and reports the \(n=22\) calculation as out of memory. The previously published all-cycle face enumeration (public result `7ecee706974b`) derives cyclic words and a periodic Euler characteristic but expressly says it does not determine individual homology groups. The published full-strong-cycle dihedral calculation (public result `4819a9d67705`) gives alternating Lefschetz traces and explicitly disclaims graded or integral homology. The pure strong-cycle equivariant calculation (public result `0dacd6b82f58`) treats a different complex. Deshpande and Singh (arXiv:2001.05448, Proposition 4.1 and Theorem 4.6) compute the higher-independence ingredients but do not identify them with the homology of full strong cycle matching complexes. The all-\(n\) integral conclusion is not an implication of any of these isolated claims without the new relative pair, Alexander-dual, and nerve decomposition. Searches for integral full strong cycle homology, the \(n=22\) case, duality, and equivalent cycle formulations found no dominating statement in the inspected accessible records. Other indexed or unindexed equivalent reductions may exist.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** A 2026 primary paper explicitly left the first \(n=22\) case uncomputed and described the full-cycle homology pattern as non-obvious. The result gives every integral Betti number for **all** \(n\ge6\), proves no torsion can occur, and resolves that first open finite instance as \(\widetilde H_{10}\cong\mathbb Z\) and \(\widetilde H_{13}\cong\mathbb Z^2\). It replaces a rapidly growing face-level computation by two established higher-independence complexes and a short exact sequence. Its uniform classification, topology-sensitive degree shifts, and first inaccessible case are substantive beyond known Euler or face counts.

## Closest literature and limitations

The strongest relevant full-complex literature is the 2026 defining paper and the preceding public face/Euler and equivariant Euler results. The stronger 2020 higher-independence theorem concerns different complexes and provides an essential ingredient, not the asserted full strong matching classification. The proof establishes integral reduced **groups** only: it does not determine full-complex homotopy type, cohomology products, integral dihedral representations, or canonical generators. The source screens cannot certify that no unpublished or unindexed equivalent proof exists. An independent scientific audit remains necessary.
