# Uniform isolated conductor defects in tangent-pinched Veronese varieties

## Finding
Let \(n\ge3\), \(d\ge5\), \(r=n-1\), and let \(k\) be algebraically closed of arbitrary characteristic. Consider the monomial linear series given by **all** degree-\(d\) monomials except \(x_i^{d-1}x_j\) for ordered pairs \(i\ne j\). Its projective image \(Y_{n,d}\) in \(\mathbb P^{\binom{n+d-1}{d}-n(n-1)-1}\) has normalization \(\mathbb P^r\) and precisely \(n\) reduced singular points, the images \(P_i\) of the coordinate points. For each \(i\), a distinguished affine chart centered at \(P_i\) is
\[
 B_r=k+\mathfrak m^2\ \subset\ S_r=k[z_1,\ldots,z_r],\qquad \mathfrak m=(z_1,\ldots,z_r).
\]
The normalization conductor on this chart is \(\mathfrak m^2\), the normalization-cokernel at \(P_i\) has length \(r\), and
\[
0\to\mathcal O_Y\to\nu_*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^r}\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^n k_{P_i}^{\oplus r}\to0.
\]
In particular, every singular local ring has depth \(1\), embedding dimension \(\binom{r+1}{2}+\binom{r+2}{3}\) and Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity \(2^r\). Furthermore,
\[
\deg Y=d^r,\quad h^1(Y,\mathcal O_Y)=nr,\quad h^j(Y,\mathcal O_Y)=0\ (j\ge2),\quad
\chi(Y,\mathcal O_Y(t))=\binom{dt+r}{r}-nr\ (t\ge0).
\]

## Assumptions and scope
All statements hold for integers \(n\ge3\), \(d\ge5\) and algebraically closed \(k\), including positive characteristic. We define \(A_{n,d}=k[M]\), the standard-graded subalgebra generated by the indicated degree-\(d\) monomials; \(Y=\operatorname{Proj} A_{n,d}\), and \(\nu\) is induced by their globally generated basepoint-free linear system. Its pullback of \(\mathcal O_Y(1)\) is \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^r}(d)\). The singularity and conductor statements describe the intrinsic local rings, not merely their reduced sets or the affine cone at its vertex.

## Proof
**1. Affine charts and their semigroups.** The pure monomials \(x_i^d\) all belong to the system; their standard opens cover \(Y\) because every omitted-independent generator \(M\) obeys \(M^d=\prod_j (x_j^d)^{\deg_{x_j}M}\). On the open \(D_+(x_i^d)\), let \(z_j=x_j/x_i\) for \(j\ne i\). The degree-zero coordinate ring is generated by the nonconstant ratios \(M/x_i^d\). Every such ratio has total \(z\)-degree at least \(2\): the only monomials giving degree \(1\) were deliberately deleted. Conversely, for \(d\ge5\), every monomial of total \(z\)-degree \(2\) or \(3\) occurs among these ratios: its preimage has \(x_i\)-exponent \(d-2\) or \(d-3\), and no exponent equals \(d-1\). Since every monomial of total degree at least \(2\) is a product of monomials of total degrees \(2\) and \(3\), the chart ring is exactly \(B_r=k+\mathfrak m^2\). This argument also proves compatibility of the charts across their overlaps.

**2. Normalization and singular support.** The ring \(S_r=k[z_1,\ldots,z_r]\) is finite over \(B_r\), since it is generated as a \(B_r\)-module by \(1,z_1,\ldots,z_r\), and it is integral because \(z_j\) satisfies \(T^2-z_j^2=0\) over \(B_r\). Their fraction fields agree, since \(z_j=z_j^3/z_j^2\). As \(S_r\) is normal, it is the normalization of \(B_r\). These chart normalizations glue to the affine charts of \(\mathbb P^r\), hence \(\nu\) is finite birational and its source is the normalization. Moreover \(B_r[1/z_j^2]=S_r[1/z_j^2]\): the element \(z_j\) is a ratio of its cube and square, and each other \(z_\ell\) is \((z_jz_\ell)/z_j\). Thus the normalization is an isomorphism away from the origin in every chart. The only possible singular points are \(P_i\); these are actually singular because their local rings have embedding dimension greater than \(r\) (as computed below). They are distinct by their pure-coordinate sections.

**3. Defect and conductor.** The \(B_r\)-module \(S_r/B_r\) is the \(k\)-vector space spanned by the images of \(z_1,\ldots,z_r\). Multiplication by \(\mathfrak m^2\) annihilates it. The conductor \((B_r:S_r)\) equals \(\mathfrak m^2\): a polynomial is in the conductor precisely when it and its products by all \(z_j\) have no linear term, hence it has no constant or linear part. The quotient is supported only at the chart origin and has length \(r\). The local quotients glue to the stated skyscraper direct sum.

**4. Local depth, tangent dimension and multiplicity.** At the origin let \(\mathfrak n=\mathfrak m^2\subset B_r\). The exact local sequence \(0\to B_r\to S_r\to k^r\to0\), with \(r\ge2\), gives \(H^0_{\mathfrak n}(B_r)=0\), \(H^1_{\mathfrak n}(B_r)\cong k^r\), because the regular local normalization has no local cohomology below degree \(r\). Hence depth equals \(1\). Since \(\mathfrak n^2=\mathfrak m^4\), the tangent space \(\mathfrak n/\mathfrak n^2\) consists of monomials of total degrees \(2\) and \(3\), proving the embedding-dimension formula. For each integer \(q\ge0\),
\[
\operatorname{length}_{B_r}(B_r/\mathfrak n^{q+1})
 =\binom{r+2q+1}{r}-r.
\]
Its leading term is \(2^rq^r/r!\), so the Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity is \(2^r\).

**5. Projective invariants.** The finite birational map has \(\nu^*\mathcal O_Y(1)=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^r}(d)\); intersecting hyperplanes on the normalization gives \(\deg Y=d^r\). Apply coherent cohomology to the sequence in the finding. Since \(H^0(\mathbb P^r,\mathcal O)=k\) and its higher structure-sheaf cohomology vanishes, the map on constants is the identity and \(H^1(Y,\mathcal O_Y)\simeq k^{nr}\), with all cohomology in degrees at least \(2\) zero. Tensor the exact sequence by the invertible \(\mathcal O_Y(t)\) and use the projection formula for finite \(\nu\), so its Euler characteristic is \(\chi(\mathbb P^r,\mathcal O(dt))-nr\). For \(t\ge0\), this is the displayed binomial formula. \(\square\)

For \((n,d)=(3,5),(3,6),(4,5),(4,6),(5,5)\), it also checks the chart semigroup through total degree \(9\) and local Hilbert--Samuel lengths for \(0\le q\le4\). Run `python3 CHECKER.py`; expected terminal marker: `VERIFY_OK`. ## Relationship to prior work
Caviglia studies the classical *single*-pinch cubic Veronese and its Koszul property [1]. Caviglia--Conca classify projections of the Veronese **cubic surface from one point**, not the simultaneous all-coordinate-tangent deletion in arbitrary degree and dimension [2]. Maddox--Pandey study single pinches and a multi-pinch family in which every deleted exponent vector has maximum **strictly smaller than** \(d-1\) (their Section 5); our deleted vectors have maximum exactly \(d-1\), so that multi-pinch theorem does not imply the present conductor calculation [3]. These works motivate the local normalization gap; no novelty assertion about their pre-existing single-pinch or Koszul results is made.

## Limitations
The assertion concerns only \(n\ge3\), \(d\ge5\) and the exact symmetric deletion of all tangent monomials; no theorem is asserted for arbitrary sets of deleted monomials or for \(d\le4\). The global homogeneous affine-cone singular scheme, graded Betti numbers and integral cohomology are not computed. Prior related monomial-projection literature could contain an equivalent geometric statement under different terminology; originality is conditional on the inspected comparisons, not certified by an independent audit.
