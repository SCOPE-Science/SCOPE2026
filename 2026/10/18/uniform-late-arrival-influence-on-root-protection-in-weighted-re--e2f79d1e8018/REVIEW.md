# Review

## Correctness

PASS. Independent root-attachment Bernoulli decisions and uniform potential nonroot parents give a coupling in which a new root attachment deletes an edge from the induced nonroot forest. Deleting an edge can create but not remove singleton components, and an explicit positive-probability configuration makes the influence strict. Ewens singleton deletion consistency gives the sharp leading influence term; a single further singleton-event probability bounds its remainder. The factorial moments of singleton counts converge to those of a Poisson law, and the aggregate remainder is bounded by a harmonic sum. An exact rational enumeration checks all parent assignments through eight nonroot vertices at three rational weights. The proof, not the experiment, covers all parameters.

## Originality

PASS on inspected evidence, with limitations. The closest fully inspected 2020 Ewens-derangements treatment explicitly supplies the probability of zero singleton cycles and the distribution and mean of total cycles; those aggregate data are credited. It does not state an indexed influence law for each arrival or its uniform late-arrival leading coefficient. The 2022 recursive-tree protected-node profile treatment is related through its root protection concept, but available content concerns level profiles. Full text for that article was unavailable, making an unrecognized implication a residual risk. An independent priority audit has not been performed.

## Value

PASS. Root protection is the natural no-leaf-child resilience event in growing trees, while root degree is the primary local growth statistic. Unlike an aggregate correlation alone, the theorem shows that *every* individual arrival choice changes the event in the same negative direction, quantifies finite errors, and identifies a nonvanishing universal covariance as the sum of asymptotically equal late-arrival shares. The parameter-general result explains how this behavior persists under a nonuniform root-attachment bias.

## Limits and relation to existing results

The ordinary Ewens/Chinese-restaurant identities and the aggregate derangement cycle moments are not new. The mathematical contribution is local finite influence plus the uniform bulk asymptotic, with the global limit as a consequence. A complete reading of one protected-profile source was blocked by access restrictions; the checked abstract is not misrepresented as full-text noncoverage.
