# Unique fastest mixing on cones over crown graphs

## Finding
For every integer \(m\ge4\), let \(G_m=K_1\vee\operatorname{Cr}_m\), where \(\operatorname{Cr}_m\) is the crown graph obtained from \(K_{m,m}\) by deleting a perfect matching. Among **all** real symmetric, entrywise nonnegative, row-stochastic matrices supported on the edges and loops of \(G_m\), the matrix minimizing the second-largest eigenvalue modulus is unique. Its nonloop transition probabilities are
\[
 a_m=\frac{1}{2m}\quad\text{on each hub--peripheral edge},\qquad
 b_m=\frac{2m-1}{m(3m-4)}\quad\text{on each surviving crown edge}.
\]
Its optimal second-largest eigenvalue modulus is
\[
 \mu_m^*=\frac{2m-1}{2(3m-4)}.
\]
The hub has zero holding probability and every peripheral vertex has holding probability \((2m-1)(m-2)/(2m(3m-4))>0\). Both edge orbits carry strictly positive probabilities. In particular, \(\mu_m^*\to1/3\) as \(m\to\infty\). The uniqueness statement makes no symmetry assumption on the competing matrix.

## Assumptions and scope
The vertex set is \(\{h,L_1,\ldots,L_m,R_1,\ldots,R_m\}\). Edges are \(\{h,L_i\}\), \(\{h,R_i\}\) for all \(i\), and \(\{L_i,R_j\}\) for \(i\ne j\); the pairs \(L_iR_i\) are absent, as are edges within either side. Loops represent nonnegative holding probabilities rather than graph edges. A feasible matrix \(P\) is real, symmetric, entrywise nonnegative, satisfies \(P\mathbf1=\mathbf1\), and has \(P_{uv}=0\) for distinct nonadjacent vertices. Thus it has stationary distribution uniform on the \(2m+1\) vertices. Write \(J=\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/(2m+1)\) and \(\mu(P)=\lVert P-J\rVert_2\). Only this discrete-time uniform-stationary reversible fastest-mixing criterion is considered. Zero transition probabilities are allowed in the feasible set.

## Proof
**1. Spectral reduction.** Averaging any feasible \(P\) over the group generated by simultaneous permutations of the indices \(1,\ldots,m\) and interchange of the two sides preserves feasibility and cannot increase \(\lVert P-J\rVert_2\). Every invariant matrix has weight \(a\) on all \(2m\) hub edges and weight \(b\) on all \(m(m-1)\) surviving crown edges. Feasibility is exactly \(a,b\ge0\), \(2ma\le1\), and \(a+(m-1)b\le1\).

Decompose \(\mathbb R^{2m+1}\) into constants; the hub-versus-periphery contrast; the constant difference between left and right sides; the antisymmetric standard vectors \((0,u,-u)\) with \(\sum_i u_i=0\); and the symmetric standard vectors \((0,u,u)\) with \(\sum_i u_i=0\). Their respective eigenvalues and multiplicities are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
1&1\\
1-(2m+1)a&1\\
1-a-2(m-1)b&1\\
1-a-(m-2)b&m-1\\
1-a-mb&m-1.
\end{array}
\]
This decomposition accounts for all \(2m+1\) eigenvalues.

**2. Sharp lower bound and attainment.** Put \(u=1-a-(m-2)b\), \(v=1-a-2(m-1)b\). If \(\mu=\mu(P)\) for an invariant candidate, then \(u\le\mu\) and \(-v\le\mu\). The identity
\[
2(m-1)u-(m-2)v=m(1-a)
\]
gives
\[
\mu\ge\frac{m(1-a)}{3m-4}\ge\frac{2m-1}{2(3m-4)}=:t.
\]
Equality requires \(a=1/(2m)\), \(u=t\), and \(v=-t\), which uniquely force \(b=(2m-1)/(m(3m-4))\). These weights satisfy every nonnegativity constraint: the peripheral holding probability is \((2m-1)(m-2)/(2m(3m-4))\). The other nontrivial eigenvalues at these weights are \(-1/(2m)\) and \(\frac{m-4}{m}t\), both of modulus at most \(t\) for \(m\ge4\). Thus the lower bound is attained, and the invariant optimum is unique. Convexity of the spectral norm and the averaging step establish the global optimal **value**.

**3. Rigidity of every global optimizer.** Let \(P\) be any global optimizer, without an invariance assumption, and \(P_g\) its conjugates by the finite symmetry group. Their average is the unique invariant optimum \(P_*\). Every \(P_g\) acts on \(\mathbf1^\perp\) with spectrum in \([-t,t]\). On the \((m-1)\)-dimensional subspace \(E_+=\{(0,u,-u):\sum_i u_i=0\}\), one has \(P_*z=tz\). Since each \(tI-P_g\) is positive semidefinite on \(\mathbf1^\perp\), equality after averaging yields \(P_gz=tz\) for every \(z\in E_+\), in particular \(Pz=tz\). Likewise, the vector \(c=(0,\mathbf1_m,-\mathbf1_m)\) satisfies \(P_*c=-tc\); positivity of \(P_g+tI\) on \(\mathbf1^\perp\) yields \(Pc=-tc\).

Write the arbitrary off-diagonal weights of \(P\) as \(\alpha_i=P_{h,L_i}\), \(\beta_i=P_{h,R_i}\), and \(\gamma_{ij}=P_{L_i,R_j}\) for \(i\ne j\). From \(P(0,u,-u)=t(0,u,-u)\) for every zero-sum \(u\), the \(L_i\)-row has coefficient \(-\gamma_{ij}\) at each \(u_j\), \(j\ne i\), and coefficient \(1-\alpha_i-\sum_{j\ne i}\gamma_{ij}-t\) at \(u_i\); because this row vanishes on the zero-sum subspace, all its coefficients coincide. Thus the off-diagonal \(\gamma_{ij}\) in each row are equal, and \(\alpha_i+(m-2)\gamma_{ij}=1-t\). Applying the same argument to the \(R_j\)-rows shows that the off-diagonal \(\gamma_{ij}\) in each column are equal, with \(\beta_j+(m-2)\gamma_{ij}=1-t\). For \(m\ge3\), any two distinct rows have a common allowed column, so all \(\gamma_{ij}\) equal one \(b\). Consequently all \(\alpha_i\) and \(\beta_j\) equal one \(a=1-t-(m-2)b\). The identity \(Pc=-tc\) gives \(a+2(m-1)b=1+t\). Subtraction yields \(mb=2t\), whence \(b=(2m-1)/(m(3m-4))\) and \(a=1/(2m)\). All off-diagonal entries of \(P\) are now fixed and row-stochasticity determines the diagonal. Hence \(P=P_*\), proving global uniqueness.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone Python program `verify_cone_crown.py` checks 324 rational identities across orders \(4\le m\le30\), 72 independently constructed full-matrix spectral comparisons across \(4\le m<40\), five exact characteristic-polynomial identities for \(4\le m\le8\), and 840 comparisons with randomly selected feasible matrices. It requires NumPy and SymPy. All reported tests passed. These finite checks are corroboration; the proof above establishes the infinite-family result without relying on enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Boyd, Diaconis, and Xiao [1] formulate fastest symmetric stochastic mixing as convex eigenvalue optimization. Boyd, Diaconis, Parrilo, and Xiao [2] establish automorphism averaging and spectral block methods, and explicitly solve wheels, complete multipartite graphs, and two cliques joined by a bridge. The exceptional case \(m=3\) is excluded here because \(\operatorname{Cr}_3=C_6\), so its cone is the six-rim wheel already covered by [2, §5.4.3]. For \(m\ge4\), the base is the non-cycle crown graph, not a complete multipartite graph or the bridge-connected clique graph in [2]. The graph-specific all-parameter weights and, especially, the rigidity argument proving uniqueness among *non-invariant* kernels are not stated in the inspected sections of [1,2]. Generic optimization and symmetry methods alone do not imply uniqueness of all optimizers. An equivalent unindexed graph-specific result remains possible.

## Limitations
This result is restricted to the specific crown-cone family and to finite, discrete-time, symmetric nonnegative stochastic kernels with uniform stationary measure. It does not classify nonreversible walks, arbitrary stationary measures, continuous-time chains, or faster mixing by graph modification. The minimum is for the second-largest eigenvalue modulus, not the total-variation mixing time at a fixed finite time. The exact theorem is analytically proved, but independent validation and exhaustive coverage of unpublished or unindexed literature have not been performed.
