# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** Definitions, domains and all boundary cases are explicit. The independent-arrival proof reconstructs the published mixed-spider mass function, and the centre mass follows by normalization. A signed-deviation identity transforms total variation into the maximum of a monotone increasing and a monotone decreasing term. Binomial symmetry leaves exactly two candidates, whose comparison is settled by a positive paired rational expression for every \(k\ge7\) and explicit fractions for smaller \(k\). The optimal-bias asymptotic follows by a uniform denominator expansion and the central-binomial estimate. Exact-fraction graph recurrences and bounded parameter tests independently check the key identities, without being substituted for infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with bibliographic uncertainty.** The closest source, Fairfax-Ball's arXiv:2610.02276v1, supplies the output-law formula and the particular near-uniform tuning as an upper bound, but does not state or imply optimality of that tuning across all positive direct-leaf counts, its exceptional small values, or the exact asymptotic leading constant. The new monotone-deviation argument is necessary to establish that global within-family minimizer. Nearby work on random greedy independent sets and asymptotically uniform iterative constructions has different statements. Searches of mathematical records and recent literature found no equivalent optimization claim; indexing and terminology limitations remain.

**Value — PASS.** The initiating work supplies a particular near-uniform tuning without identifying the optimal direct-leaf count at each fixed number of length-two arms. This theorem gives the unique optimizer throughout the full two-parameter family, records the surprising finite exceptions, and upgrades the order bound to a sharp constant. It is a motivated exact stability/optimization result, not a change of notation or a sample computation. Scope is restricted to mixed spiders and should not be confused with the harder all-tree fixed-order extremum.
