# Scientific review: optimal resetting frequency with refractory delays

**Correctness (C): PASS.** The one-cycle endpoint is an explicit bilateral exponential law; its two geometric integer-jump tail sums give the renewal-reward drift. The logarithmic derivative \(G\) is strictly decreasing because \(\sinh(\delta y)<\delta\sinh y\), and its endpoints have opposite signs. Its unique root is the global maximum. Exact implicit differentiation proves both parameter monotonicities. Small-root and diverging-root dominant balances prove the two singular scaling regimes. The standalone program independently sums the tail law and checks 116 numeric assertions; the infinite-domain and all-parameter statements rest on the analytic proof, not these samples.

**Originality (O): PASS under source-reviewed, non-independent priority assessment.** Crucially, the zero-delay current itself is already Eq. (24) of Roberts, Sezik and Lardet (2024); Ghosh et al. (2023) numerically found optimal resetting scales. Neither result is recast as new. The assessed additional statement is a sharp unique-optimum theorem for all nondegenerate asymmetries and nonnegative deterministic refractory delays, exact comparative statics, and the change from \(\varepsilon^{-2}\) to \(\varepsilon^{-2/3}\) in optimal rate for fixed positive delay. Close-paper Section IV and Appendix C and literature on symmetric local resetting were compared. Priority risk: a general renewal-reward optimization treatment might imply the delayed extension, and a stricter interpretation could treat some derivative inequalities as immediate corollaries of the prior current law. Full independent literature audit remains outstanding.

**Scientific value (V): PASS.** The stationary-current literature motivates the best reset frequency, but finite immobilization delays change the singular asymptotic optimal-frequency exponent and move the optimum strictly. This gives a nontrivial complete design classification for a precisely specified model, not a parameter-table recomputation. The result is confined to ideal instantaneous capture and fixed refractory periods; it is not a theorem for finite-height ratchet wells.
