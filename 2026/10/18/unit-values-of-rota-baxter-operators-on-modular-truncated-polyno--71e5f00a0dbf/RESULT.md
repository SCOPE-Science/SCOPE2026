# Unit values of Rota–Baxter operators on modular truncated polynomial algebras

## Finding

Let \(k\) be **any** field of prime characteristic \(p>0\), let \(N\geq2\) be an integer, and write \(A=k[t]/(t^N)\). A weight-zero Rota–Baxter operator is a \(k\)-linear map \(P:A\to A\) satisfying
\[
P(x)P(y)=P\big(P(x)y+xP(y)\big)\quad(x,y\in A).
\]
Consider the evaluation-at-the-unit map \(\mathrm{ev}_1:P\mapsto P(1)\) on the set of these operators.

**Theorem.** For every such \(k,N\), the image of \(\mathrm{ev}_1\) is exactly the Frobenius-nilpotent ideal:
\[
\boxed{\{P(1):P\in\mathrm{RB}_0(A)\}=\{a\in A:a^p=0\}=(t^{\lceil N/p\rceil}).}
\]
Moreover, for every integer \(r\) with \(\lceil N/p\rceil\le r<N\), the restriction of evaluation admits an **algebraic section** over the locally closed valuation stratum \(\mathcal V_r\) of polynomials whose lowest nonzero coefficient is that of \(t^r\). The zero stratum has the zero-operator section. These are statements about the corresponding reduced parameter strata and the operator scheme; no assertion about the scheme-theoretic image of evaluation is made.

For any finite field \(k=\mathbb F_q\) of characteristic \(p\), the exact number of attainable values of \(P(1)\) is
\[
\boxed{q^{N-\lceil N/p\rceil}.}
\]
This counts **distinct values**, not the number of Rota–Baxter operators.

## Assumptions and scope

Only \(\mathrm{char}(k)=p>0\) and \(N\ge2\) are required. The field need not be perfect or finite. The algebra is the commutative associative unital truncated polynomial algebra \(A=k[t]/(t^N)\); operators are \(k\)-linear, and their weight is zero. For a nonzero polynomial \(a\), \(\operatorname{ord}_t(a)\) denotes its least nonzero exponent; \(\mathcal V_r\) is given by vanishing coefficients below \(r\) and a nonzero coefficient at \(r\). Thus \(\mathcal V_r\cong\mathbb G_m\times\mathbb A^{N-r-1}\).

## Proof

**1. Universal necessary obstruction.** Put \(b_0=1\), \(b_j=P^j(1)\) for \(j\ge1\). Iterated application of the Rota–Baxter identity, together with Pascal's recurrence, gives by induction the divided-power multiplication law
\[
b_m b_n=\binom{m+n}{m}b_{m+n}\qquad(m,n\ge0).
\]
Indeed, for positive \(m,n\), apply the operator identity to \(b_{m-1},b_{n-1}\) and insert the previously established formulas for \(b_{m-1}b_n\) and \(b_mb_{n-1}\); the two coefficients sum to \(\binom{m+n}{m}\). Therefore \(b_1^p=p!b_p=0\), so every possible \(a=P(1)\) satisfies \(a^p=0\).

**2. Nilpotent values are precisely the stated ideal.** Suppose \(a\ne0\), and write \(a=a_rt^r+\cdots\) with \(a_r\in k^\times\). Since \(A\) is truncated at degree \(N\), the leading term of \(a^p\) is \(a_r^pt^{pr}\) whenever \(pr<N\). Thus \(a^p=0\) if and only if \(pr\ge N\), equivalently \(r\ge\lceil N/p\rceil\). The zero element is included separately.

**3. A split module over the subalgebra generated by the value.** Fix nonzero \(a\) with \(a^p=0\), put \(r=\operatorname{ord}_t(a)\), and let \(L=\lceil N/r\rceil\), so \(2\le L\le p\). The powers \(1,a,\ldots,a^{L-1}\) are linearly independent by their distinct leading degrees \(0,r,\ldots,(L-1)r\), and \(a^L=0\). Consequently \(B=k[a]\cong k[z]/(z^L)\).

For every \(0\le i<r\), form the vectors \(t^i a^j\) with \(j\ge0\) and \(i+rj<N\). Their leading terms have mutually distinct degrees \(i+rj\), and these degrees exhaust \(0,\ldots,N-1\). The change-of-basis matrix from these \(N\) vectors to \(1,t,\ldots,t^{N-1}\) is triangular with invertible diagonal entries \(a_r^j\); hence they form a basis of \(A\). Grouping them by \(i\) yields a direct sum of \(B\)-submodules
\[
A=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r-1} t^iB.
\]
In particular, the projection \(\pi:A\to B\) onto the \(i=0\) summand is \(B\)-linear and satisfies \(\pi(1)=1\).

**4. Integration on the short subalgebra and extension.** Define \(I:B\to B\) by
\[
I(a^j)=\begin{cases}a^{j+1}/(j+1),&0\le j<L-1,\\0,&j=L-1.\end{cases}
\]
All denominators used are nonzero in \(k\), since \(L\le p\). For monomials \(a^i,a^j\) with \(i+j+2<L\), the Rota–Baxter identity reduces to the rational identity
\[
\frac{1}{(i+1)(j+1)}=\frac{1/(i+1)+1/(j+1)}{i+j+2},
\]
where all three denominators are invertible. When \(i+j+2\ge L\), both sides of the operator identity vanish in \(B\) after checking the truncated exponents (including either top-degree input). Bilinearity therefore proves that \(I\) is a weight-zero Rota–Baxter operator on \(B\), with \(I(1)=a\).

Set \(P=I\circ\pi:A\to A\). Since \(\pi\) is \(B\)-linear and \(I(B)\subseteq B\), for arbitrary \(x,y\in A\) one obtains
\[
\begin{aligned}
P(x)P(y)&=I(\pi x)I(\pi y)\\
&=I\big(I(\pi x)\pi y+\pi xI(\pi y)\big)\\
&=P\big(P(x)y+xP(y)\big).
\end{aligned}
\]
Moreover \(P(1)=I(1)=a\). For \(a=0\), take \(P=0\). This proves surjectivity onto the Frobenius-nilpotent ideal.

**5. Sections on valuation strata.** On \(\mathcal V_r\), the leading coefficient \(a_r\) is an invertible coordinate. All entries of the triangular basis matrix in Step 3 are polynomial functions of the coordinates of \(a\); its inverse has entries in the localized polynomial ring \(\mathbb F_p[a_r,a_r^{-1},a_{r+1},\ldots,a_{N-1}]\). The formula for \(I\) also has regular coefficients (its denominators are units in \(\mathbb F_p\)). Therefore the matrix entries of \(P=I\circ\pi\) are regular functions on \(\mathcal V_r\). The Rota–Baxter identities and \(P(1)=a\) hold as polynomial identities over this coordinate ring, giving the asserted algebraic section.

**6. Exact count.** The ideal \((t^{\lceil N/p\rceil})\) is a \(k\)-vector space of dimension \(N-\lceil N/p\rceil\). Over \(\mathbb F_q\) it has exactly \(q^{N-\lceil N/p\rceil}\) elements.

## Verification

The independent finite-field checker `verify_rb.py` constructs the operator \(I\circ\pi\) through a triangular elimination on truncated polynomials and checks the defining identity on every pair of standard basis monomials. It reports `VERIFICATION_OK 1204` after 1,204 construction tests in 24 field/truncation configurations, including \(\mathbb F_4\) and \(\mathbb F_9\). Five small configurations additionally enumerate **every** linear map and test whether its unit value is precisely in the predicted ideal. Run `python verify_rb.py` with ordinary Python 3. These calculations corroborate but do not establish the theorem's universal quantifiers.

## Relationship to prior work

The divided-power product law and the consequent prime-characteristic obstruction to arbitrary \(P(1)\) are classical aspects of the free Rota–Baxter construction, studied for instance by Guo and Yu in *Characteristics of Rota-Baxter Algebras*. They are **not** new contributions here. Guo, Rosenkranz and Zheng previously classified Rota–Baxter operators on semigroup algebras of orders two and three; their separate work on polynomial algebras emphasizes characteristic-zero integration. More recent work by Farooq treats square-zero truncated multivariable polynomial algebras in characteristic zero. The present result instead identifies the **complete attainable unit-value locus and regular sections** for arbitrary truncation depth in positive characteristic. The explicit splitting construction is the substantive converse to the known obstruction.

## Limitations

This theorem does not classify all Rota–Baxter operators, compute dimensions or components of their full operator scheme, count operators over finite fields, or assert that evaluation has a global regular section. It describes the set-theoretic image and the reduced valuation-stratum sections only. The literature comparison is bounded, so stronger coverage in unlocated specialized sources cannot be excluded without further independent review.
