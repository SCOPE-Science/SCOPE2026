# Scientific review: fixed-degree Hardy boundary layers

## Correctness — PASS
The defining residual identity is exact. The concentrating-boundary-measure calculation gives the normalization \(C_0(p)\varepsilon\); the pointwise identity \(\operatorname{Re}(1+B_a)=|1+B_a|^2/2\) identifies the first variation. A uniform-convexity lower inequality bounds polynomial minimizers at the correct \(\varepsilon^{1/(p-1)}\) scale. The Taylor remainder bound, integrated separately for \(2<p<3\), \(p=3\), and \(p>3\), proves finite-dimensional variational convergence, hence coefficient convergence and the exact-baseline correction. Degree-one symmetry and strict convexity supply uniqueness of the explicit profile. Independent exact quartic and sixth-power baseline calculations in the embedded checker support, but do not substitute for, the infinite proof.

The newer Bénéteau et al. 2025 result solves the unrestricted Hardy subspace benchmark but not the finite-degree gap or Puiseux profile. published-finding corpus searches covering aliases, boundary layers, low-degree approximation, and moving Blaschke factors returned related but non-dominating Dirichlet bidisk or Hardy geometric results. ## Mathematical value — PASS
Fixed-degree reciprocal approximation near a moving zero is a natural filter-design and Hardy-space approximation regime. The exact universal finite-vs-infinite gap, fractional coefficient scaling, and explicit next-order degree-one loss distinguish it from mere root estimates or routine Hilbert-space projection. The value is the boundary transition across all real exponents \(p>2\) and all fixed finite degrees, not merely a recomputation of a fourth-power example.
