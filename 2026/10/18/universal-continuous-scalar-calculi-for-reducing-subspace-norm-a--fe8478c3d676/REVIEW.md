# Review of universal reducing-subspace attainment calculi

## Correctness — PASS

The proof uses the exact published positive pure-point order criteria [1, Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.17]. It checks all quantifiers over bounded positive operators on the fixed separable infinite Hilbert space and over continuous nonnegative scalar functions on \([0,R]\). The construction of monotone image subsequences from a strict scalar crossing gives exact infinite-dimensional diagonal witnesses for every necessity direction. Flat scalar intervals give distinct witnesses showing that weak monotonicity cannot guarantee reflection. Strict scalar monotonicity supplies a continuous inverse on the compact spectral image. No convergence limit is treated as an eigenvalue without verification.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

The principal prior article [1] already proves both operator spectral characterizations and one affine order-reversing duality. These are credited and used, not claimed. The classified set of **all** continuous scalar preservers/exchangers, all four iff converse directions, and explicit crossing/flatness obstructions were not stated in the inspected full texts of [1] or [2]. The 2023 functional-calculus article [3] concerns the smaller absolutely norm-attaining class; its full text was inaccessible, leaving a genuine priority risk. Public mathematical-record comparisons concerning positive absolute norm attainment, perturbations, and fixed-polar density did not imply this eight-way universal characterization. Absence of a retrieved equivalent is not a proof of worldwide priority.

## Value — PASS

The result identifies exactly which scalar functional calculi retain or interchange two recently characterized operator classes and precisely when spectral information loss precludes reversing the conclusion. The simultaneous classification is natural and exhaustive, and its infinite diagonal obstructions show why finite-dimensional tests cannot reveal the boundary. It supplies a useful universal transformation calculus rather than an isolated example, and it distinguishes preservation from reflection without imposing artificial smoothness assumptions.

## Scope and independent validation

This is a theorem about positive Hilbert-space operators and continuous scalar functions. Nonpositive normal operators, Borel functional calculus, and the stronger absolutely norm-attaining class are not classified. Publication priority is subject to review of previously inaccessible related literature.
