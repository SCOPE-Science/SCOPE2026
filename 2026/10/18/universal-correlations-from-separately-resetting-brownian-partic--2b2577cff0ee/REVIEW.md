# Scientific review — universal correlations in a fluctuating resetting environment

## Correctness (PASS)

The stated stationary model has a common Poisson-redrawn intensity and *independent* reset events for each particle conditional on the intensity path. The generator independently kills each particle's square at its own instantaneous hazard; its mixed fourth moment is therefore killed at twice that hazard. Stationary conditional moment equations yield the exact values of \(M=\mathbb EX_i^2\), \(P=\mathbb EX_1^2X_2^2\) and \(H=\mathbb EX_i^4\) and hence the correlation formula. Conditional independence given the full environmental path explains strict positive squared-displacement covariance and zero ordinary positional covariance. Conditional Jensen bounds the correlation strictly below \(1/3\). An exponential-moment renewal bound establishes stationary moment finiteness for every positive refresh rate. Rescaling the small-rate integrals shows a near-zero density \(f(\lambda)\sim c\lambda^{\alpha}\) forces a universal limit for \(-1<\alpha<1\); an exact integral factorization evaluates its exponent dependence. Independent finite-state balance calculations, exact rational checks and high-precision quadrature passed 49 assertions in the packaged checker. These tests are supporting evidence, not an infinite proof.

## Originality (PASS, qualified)

Arutkin and Reuveni, arXiv:2509.25365 (2025), establish the environmental redraw model and its one-particle steady-state distribution, not the present multi-particle joint correlations. De Mauro et al., arXiv:2509.06658 (2025), and Boyer–Majumdar, arXiv:2510.21972 (2025), calculate particle correlations under **simultaneous** resetting, which gives a different mixed-moment loss term and different common-age structure. Mesquita–Majumdar–Sabhapandit, arXiv:2506.20859 (2025), consider a **common stochastic diffusivity** rather than a stochastic reset intensity. Relevant published finding searches and prior accessible results did not identify the specific separate-reset joint-moment identity or the universal near-zero-rate exponent law. The standard conditional-independence mechanism is prior work and is not presented as novel. A general theorem on Markov-modulated Cox reset systems could imply part of the calculation; the available literature does not rule out implicit coverage. The checked materials support originality under best knowledge, but this is not an independent literature certification.

## Value (PASS)

The source's stochastic rate already creates an experimentally motivated one-particle crossover between self-averaged and slowly changing environments. The central missing question is whether different particles sharing that environment become correlated without **ever resetting simultaneously**. The exact stationary two-particle correlation both answers that question quantitatively and identifies a nontrivial family of universal constants indexed by the rate density's low-intensity exponent. The result distinguishes correlated squared displacements from zero positional correlation and identifies the physically meaningful slow-refresh order of limits. It goes beyond a textbook moment computation or a change of variables: the mixed reset generator, dependence mechanism, and classification of divergent fourth-order moment ratios all matter.

The closest directly motivating source is Arutkin–Reuveni's complete open-access 2025 text, especially equations (23)–(24). Its independent rate redraws and one-body Fourier formula supply the baseline model, not the new pairwise conclusion. The strongest related multi-particle studies focus on simultaneous events or shared diffusivity and cannot be substituted for separate conditional reset clocks. The proof covers a stationary common Poisson-redrawn rate, independent conditional reset events, and rate densities with asymptotic exponent strictly between \(-1\) and \(1\) for its universal limit. A nonstationary or simultaneous-reset variant is outside scope. A general Cox-modulated resetting theorem, or less accessible mathematical literature, may already cover the claim; independent expert checking remains necessary.
