# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The coatom-witness equivalence is proved in both directions, including the empty-family and single-object cases. Disjoint column-pattern probabilities yield exact inclusion–exclusion and factorial moments. The universal asymptotic uses the inequality \(r t_r\le1\), which justifies the second-order logarithmic error for every allowed sequence of incidence probabilities; the supercritical and subcritical limits follow from first- and second-moment bounds. An exhaustive independent enumeration of small matrices reproduces the exact integer counts. No computational sample is substituted for the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with caveats
Howlader and Liau (2023) develop two-sorted modal representations of formal concepts but not random saturation; Sakurai (2021) calculates the expectation of the concept count for a random context and a lower bound but does not give this maximal-lattice threshold; Felde, Hanika and Stumme (2020) discuss random-context generators and contranominal scales but the accessible portions do not identify this precise universal Poisson window; Albano and Chornomaz (2017) investigate extremal size without this random threshold. Published-research database searches for exact probability/contranominal/Boolean/Gumbel formulations found no dominating claim. The underlying Boolean criterion and coupon-collector principle are classical and are not claimed separately as new. Risk: an equivalent occupancy formula may be documented in other formal-context literature; the full text of the 2020 study was not retrievable in the present source session.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Saturation to the maximal \(2^r\) concepts is a natural exact invariant and a central pathological case for formal-concept algorithms. The theorem quantifies an entire sharp probability transition, with explicit finite probabilities, a limiting Poisson missing-pattern law and uniformity even when the incidence density varies arbitrarily with the dimension. It therefore informs the regime in which random-context generators produce Boolean concept lattices; it is not merely a list of small examples.
