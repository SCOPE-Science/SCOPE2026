# Scientific review: universal critical aging limit in persistent walks

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is genuinely a functional limit for a discrete walk with arbitrary finite early probabilities and \(k p_k\to c\), not a finite-time identity. Independent rare-event estimates on each compact interval bounded away from zero show convergence of reversal points to the Poisson intensity \(c\,du/u\). The product \(\prod (1-2p_k)\) erases the initial sign, and the Riemann-sum bound near zero together with deterministic Lipschitz tightness proves the full path limit. The limiting filter has an explicit invariant joint density checked directly against its generator, with vanishing boundary flux and uniqueness by exponential contraction after coupling signs. The covariance follows by Fubini from the known stationary two-state correlation, and moment convergence is permitted by bounded path values. The singular coefficient \(c=1/2\) and its three limiting-covariance regimes have been checked algebraically. The standalone pure-Python program passed 53 finite-step and integral consistency assertions, including fourth moments and unequal-time covariances. Finite checks are supplementary, not infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS, with material residual uncertainty

The source arXiv:2605.20116v1 establishes the original time-dependent reversal model, the critical MSD prefactor, and finite-time Binder scaling; these are not re-claimed. **More importantly, Garra and Orsingher arXiv:1411.0648 explicitly give exactly the same beta density for the continuum Poisson \(c/t\)-rate flight. The density, its arcsine/uniform/bell-shaped cases, and that continuous-time model are unambiguously PRIOR, not new.** The surviving claim is the universal discrete functional convergence under only \(k p_k\to c\) together with the derived two-time displacement aging kernel and its \(c=1/2\) transition. These are not stated in inspected source sections. Angelani–Garra provide a related telegraph-rate PDE; Cénac et al. analyze run-length-dependent persistence, not the absolute calendar clock. Published-index and scientific database searches for discrete rare-switch beta limits, Euler–Poisson–Darboux reductions, and aging covariances yielded no direct matching implication. A broad generic point-process invariance theorem coupled to the known continuous random-flight process could imply much of the result; this is a substantive residual originality risk. No independent validation is represented.

## Value — PASS

The central 2026 random-walk article leaves the critical displacement law broad but implicit and its Binder crossing numerical, while the continuous solvable density appears in another modeling tradition. Rigorous discrete-to-continuous identification for **all** sequences satisfying a single leading-order condition is a nontrivial scaling assertion. It yields a checkable, parameter-dependent critical Binder value and distinguishes stationary velocity on logarithmic time from aging displacement on physical time. The exact two-time covariance yields a separate and physically meaningful \(c=1/2\) boundary separating increasing, logarithmically increasing, and saturating cross-correlations at widely separated times in the limiting process. The output is an analytically useful joint scaling benchmark, not merely a rediscovery of a known beta table.
