# Scientific review: exact adaptive-reward critical relaxation

## Correctness — PASS
The proof derives an exact Lyapunov identity and verifies compactness of its sublevels at each critical reward face; the only invariant zero-dissipation state is the selected critical equilibrium. An oriented slow reward deviation is eventually monotone and its cooperation-to-reward ratio obeys an asymptotically stable scalar equation. The resulting inverse-time coefficients follow from a reciprocal derivative limit. A second-order tracking expansion with exponentially damped error of order \(O(t^{-2})\) gives an integrable remainder after the explicit logarithmic term is subtracted. Exact rational checks verify the coefficients in both parameter examples and numerical trajectories corroborate the rates. Numeric integration is not used as an infinite-time proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
Hua and Liu establish the public-goods/reward model and several equilibrium stability regimes; the original arXiv full text and its appendix were compared. Earlier related analysis of the same model establishes qualitative convergence at reward equalities. Qualitative boundary selection and the general occurrence of algebraic relaxation are therefore excluded from the novelty claim. The result newly evaluated here is the all-interior-data pair of exact critical amplitudes at both reward faces, with the next explicit logarithmic term. The broader feedback-game literature, including Weitz et al., uses different systems and does not provide these constants. General center-manifold asymptotic theory, other uninspected papers or future independent priority review could affect novelty.

## Value — PASS
At either natural equality between an institution's necessary reward and its permitted reward interval, local linearization loses a stability eigenvalue. Exact decay amplitudes supply a quantitative time-to-equilibrium law beyond the previously established stability classification. The leading cooperation coefficient is universal across game payoff and strategy-speed parameters, whereas the logarithmic inverse-reward correction distinguishes the relative speed. This identifies a meaningful second-order observable for critical adaptive incentive dynamics.
