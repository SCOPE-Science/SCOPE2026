# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The complete proof is a two-level decimation induction. Even-index letters alternate; this rules out alternating four-letter factors and forces any putative even border of length \(2h\) in a length-\(2^k\) factor to have \(h\) even. Odd-index decimation then produces a forbidden even border in a shorter dyadic factor. Parity-opposed offsets of an odd border of length at least seven force an impossible alternating factor in a decimated word. A border of length five forces equality of the extreme symbols, hence border one. The regular fold example starting at index three realizes shortest border three. Finite tests are supporting checks, not infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS against inspected evidence
Allouche's generalized paperfolding factor count \(4n\) is an aggregate invariant and does not imply shortest-border lengths. Goč–Mousavi–Shallit's unbordered-factor treatment gives counts and examples for other automatic words but not the dyadic per-factor paperfolding criterion. Holub–Müller's conjugacy results quantify over rotations rather than generalized paperfolding factor positions. Search comparisons identified no broader theorem implying the exact statement. Unindexed equivalent work, including results not exposed by the examined literature, remains a risk.

## Value — PASS
The theorem gives a sharp universal boundary certificate for factors across all generalized fold-instruction streams. It makes unborderedness at natural substitution-scale lengths decidable from at most six boundary symbols, regardless of length, and proves a separate impossible-even-border structural lemma. This classification answers a natural question raised by existing unbordered-factor studies, rather than merely reproducing a finite table.

## Scope and limitations
Only lengths \(2^k\) with \(k\ge2\) are covered. The result does not count how many distinct factors satisfy the criterion or settle nondyadic lengths. Novelty is conditional on the inspected source coverage.
