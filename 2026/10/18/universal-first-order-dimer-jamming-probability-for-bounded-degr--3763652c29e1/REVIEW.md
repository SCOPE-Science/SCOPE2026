# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof derives the exact allowed-edge transition counts at each vacancy level, obtains uniform marked-vertex survival products, separates single-defect survival from multiple-defect interactions, and bounds terminal forbidden bicliques. The finite calculation independently verifies 31 exact-rational facts, including three all-order edge enumeration comparisons. The uniform \(O_\Delta(n^{-1/3})\) bound comes from explicit asymptotic estimates, not numerical extrapolation.

**Originality — PASS within the inspected corpus, with bibliographic uncertainty.** Published-record comparisons and mathematical research searches did not disclose an equivalent sparse-defect, arbitrary-arrangement coefficient theorem. The accessible full arXiv text of Bennett–Bohman (arXiv:1210.3581) provides a high-degree regular hypergraph bound on unmatched proportion, not the present probability of any failure. The Dyer–Frieze (1991) abstract instead emphasizes general worst-case and sparse graph approximation ratios, and Pippenger (1989) discusses graph adsorption with locally tree-like structure. Complete texts of the two older papers were not obtained; the absence of direct coverage cannot be certified from abstracts alone. An earlier crown-graph special case is subsumed but was excluded from the novelty claim.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The count-only first-order coefficient is a structural universality statement for irreversible adsorption on dense but imperfect bipartite substrates. The underlying graph has perfect matchings for large \(n\), yet irreversible deposition can jam; the theorem quantifies the small failure probability rather than merely noting that final coverage tends to one. Controlling overlapping forbidden edges without exchangeability is a substantive extension beyond the single matching-deletion case.

**Risks and limits.** The complete earlier articles could conceal overlap, the maximum forbidden degree must remain fixed, and no independent scholarly audit has been conducted.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
