# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS

The proof covers every positive pair of run lengths and every positive factor length. Its negative direction uses the complete six-subgroup lattice of \(S_3\) and explicitly accounts for the optional constant length-three factors. Its positive direction identifies factors with one-hot phase indices, and gives concrete diagonal coordinate-permutation groups with any prescribed number of orbits within each weight class. The ambient-group orbit definition is respected. The standalone checker replayed 1,662 positive realizations and 49 complete length-three subgroup comparisons; these finite checks corroborate but do not replace the all-parameter proof.

## Originality: PASS within inspected evidence

The closest recent source, Launer–Puzynina–Voloshinova (arXiv:2607.07620v1, especially Section 6 and the concluding questions), introduces universality and an algorithmic finite criterion for eventually periodic words, but does not provide a uniform two-run binary classification or the all-length diagonal group construction. The earlier Charlier–Puzynina–Zamboni paper (arXiv:1505.03848) supplies the group-complexity framework and an example involving one-hot words, but not this universality equivalence. Searches of related open research records yielded no equivalent or dominating formula. The possibility of an unindexed or differently expressed earlier classification remains a genuine limitation; failure to find one is not a proof of priority.

## Value: PASS

This is a complete, natural infinite two-parameter classification within a newly studied property. The distinction between one isolated symbol per period and two long monochromatic runs is proved on both sides: all target complexities have explicit witnesses in the first regime, while in the second an unavoidable missing value occurs already at length three. It replaces case-by-case finite group enumeration for this family with a uniform structural criterion; it neither claims nor requires a general classification of periodic words.
