# Review: quantum-exterior unit-group and commuting-scheme nonreconstruction

## Correctness
PASS. The four basis normal forms and the radical multiplication table are explicit. The polynomial substitution \(w=(c-\lambda ab)/(1-\lambda)\) conjugates the unit multiplication to \(U_3\) over every base algebra. Commuting tuples satisfy exactly the minors of a two-row matrix. Radical congruence and cosquare eigenvalues give the nonisomorphism classification; swapping generators supplies the converse. The dimension lower bound uses the radical filtration of split local algebras, and the finite-field formula follows by partitioning rank-one matrices by their image line. Direct checks from the packaged program corroborate the critical formulas without replacing the general proofs.

## Originality
PASS under inspected-source and accessible-record comparisons, with residual risk. Bergh–Oppermann (arXiv:0710.2606) introduce and investigate the relevant quantum complete intersections but study representation dimension; Fialowski–Penkava (arXiv:1309.5770) already handle bilinear-form congruence and parameter interchange in the corresponding nonunital family, so that classification is acknowledged as **prior**. Grimley (arXiv:1606.01727) studies parameter-sensitive Hochschild invariants. None of these inspected full texts states the combined universal unit-group scheme collapse, all-arity commuting determinantal scheme, and sharp local dimension-four nonreconstruction threshold. The nearest research-database and historical results treat other classes (incidence algebras or almost-abelian Lie algebras) with no implication establishing this theorem. Specialized unlocated unit-group literature could nonetheless cover the collapse; absence from search results is not proof of uniqueness.

## Value
PASS. The problem is whether easily measured multiplicative-group and commuting-configuration invariants can reconstruct a small local associative algebra. For this natural two-generator quantum family, both entire algebraic-group schemes and all tuple-commuting schemes discard a parameter that distinguishes \(\lceil q/2\rceil\) algebra isomorphism classes. The first possible noncommutative split-local dimension is exactly four, giving a sharp structural obstruction rather than an arbitrary small finite enumeration. The explicit functorial group coordinate change and determinantal model make the obstruction reusable.
