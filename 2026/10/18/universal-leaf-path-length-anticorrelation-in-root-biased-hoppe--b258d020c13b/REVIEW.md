# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** Conditioning on the full tree, the precise updates for leaf count, total internal depth and leaf-depth sum yield the coupled moment recurrence. A strictly positive auxiliary cross-moment defect forces negative covariance for all positive root weights and all sizes at least three; a closed recurrence solution gives the formula and finite harmonic bound. The leading variance results used for the correlation constant are established in Leckey–Neininger (2012), Theorems 2.5–2.6. Exact-rational verification enumerated 231165 weighted trees across 40 parameter-size pairs and completed 208 assertions. These are finite checks, not an infinite proof.

**Originality: PASS (root-weight-dependent claim).** Leckey–Neininger (2012) provide separate leaf and path-length marginals, without their covariance. Hiesmayr–Işlak study weighted recursive trees but not the stated cross-statistic. Lundblad–Wagner (2026) give an exact multivariate generating-function equation for **uniform** recursive trees, so the \(\theta=1\) case is *implicitly covered*, even if its covariance simplification is not printed. The assessed new result is the explicit mixed covariance in the exceptional-root model for varying fixed \(\theta>0\), including the full-family sign and sharp correlation behavior. The older Dobrow–Fill total-path paper could only be reviewed at abstract/bibliographic level. Previous overlapping claims were excluded; neither search failure nor a partition warrants absolute uniqueness.

**Value: PASS.** Root bias is a canonical perturbation of uniform recursive trees. The proof reveals a universal negative slope \(-1/4\) independent of the root weight despite weight-dependent marginal path-length variance. The result quantifies dependence between a branching statistic and total search depth, including strict finite-size behavior and a parameter-dependent sharp decorrelation constant; this information is not recoverable from the known marginals.

**Limitations and residual risks.** No claim of full joint distribution, asymptotic independence, or growing root weights. The uniform model is already implicitly covered by a stronger 2026 joint generating function. Some earlier original full texts were inaccessible, so independent priority verification remains necessary. The tests are finite and independent scientific validation has not occurred.
