# Scientific review — universal near-activation law

**Correctness: PASS.** Strict convexity of the effective-resistance objective makes symmetrization globally definitive; the weighted-join eigenspaces reduce the problem to a scalar ratio. At the critical price the second derivative is \(2(N+1)d(N-d)>0\), ensuring a unique analytic interior branch. Its coefficient comparison uses the first three exact Laplacian spectral moments, including the triangle count, and gives the stated ratio and minimized objective. The exact-rational program checks 120 finite identities but is not relied upon for the infinite-family theorem.

**Originality: PASS, with limits.** Earlier general convex optimality conditions and explicit optional tree-chord screening already cover the sharp activation price; a wheel-specific first-order activation coefficient is also known. Neither is submitted as new. The assessed claim is the *general* degree-only linear activation and quadratic optimal-value gain, with a precise graph-specific second coefficient determined by triangle count, for every transitive peripheral graph. Relevant primary comparisons are Ghosh–Boyd–Saberi (2006; 2008) and Johnson–Chertkov (2010). A 2023 fixed-cardinality augmentation paper was available only in excerpt, and analogous specialized expansions may remain unindexed.

**Value: PASS.** The onset laws quantify the amount of newly justified peripheral conductance and the sharp resistance return on a small price reduction, something the binary known threshold alone cannot do. The finding reveals exactly when topology first matters beyond leaf count and degree, distinguishing same-order same-degree networks through triangles.

**Limits.** No claim is made about a full closed-form optimum far below the threshold, nontransitive peripheral graphs, or independent confirmation. This is a local asymptotic theorem for the Kirchhoff index, not a mixing-rate theorem.
