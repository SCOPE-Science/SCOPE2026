# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Origin-centered cyclic symmetry and constant width leave exactly the nonconstant Fourier modes k=(2j+1)n. The support-area identity extends to nonsmooth convex supports by smoothing in W¹,²; averaging a rotated copy multiplies the squared k-amplitude by cos²(kθ/2). At the three stated angles these factors are uniform. Positive-curvature single-mode supports with k=n and k=3n are admissible for sufficiently small nonzero amplitude. Setting x=nθ/2, their factors agree precisely when sin(4x)sin(2x)=0 in [0,π/2]. At the half-period rotation the support average is constant w/2. For the regular Reuleaux body, the displayed support pieces meet in C¹, the circular arc has curvature density w, and the vertex block has zero density. Splitting the mixed-area integral at θ−δ/2 gives nw/2 times [w(δ−θ)−2R sin(δ/2−θ)]. The midpoint area is one half the sum of the original and mixed areas, yielding the profile. Its derivative is positive in the interior since |δ/2−θ|<δ/2; reflecting θ about δ/2 cancels the sine terms and gives the complement. For θ=tδ the cubic coefficient in the deficit bracket is 1/24+(1/2−t)/8−(1/2−t)³/6=(1−t)²(1+2t)/12. Taylor's remainder is O(δ⁵) uniformly for t∈[0,1], giving the stated area remainder. The single 3n-mode verifies that the monotonicity assertion cannot be extended to the full class.

## originality

PASS

The n=3 area profile, opposite-body endpoint, regular minimizing shapes, and general support-Fourier machinery are not new. What survives comparison is the all-odd-order exact intermediate-angle profile for n≥5 with its uniform large-order law, together with the precise admissible-mode rigidity calculation for one-step area contraction. The latter is a short application of Fourier area, not a new general averaging theorem; the explicit Reuleaux profile does not follow from that universal identity. The assessed contribution is distinct to the best of the available statement-level evidence, not an absolute priority certification.
