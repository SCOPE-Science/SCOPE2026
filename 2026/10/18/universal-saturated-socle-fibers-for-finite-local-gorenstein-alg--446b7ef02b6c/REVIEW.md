# Scientific review: universal saturated socle fibers

**Correctness (PASS).** A maximal-length radical-filtration argument proves that every nonzero submodule of a finite free module over a local Artinian algebra intersects its socle; thus independent socle images give an embedded free summand. The extremal dimension forces freeness. The orbit of the regular representation has stabilizer \(\operatorname{GL}_r(R)\); the prescribed square-zero operator has the explicit block-triangular centralizer computed in the proof. Their exact group orders yield the stated fiber cardinality in all characteristics. Finite exhaustive enumerations in the included verification program agree, but are not the general proof.

**Originality (PASS with residual bibliographic uncertainty).** Relevant comparisons: Levy's arXiv:math/0609180 treats module varieties for the two-variable square-zero algebra; Kokol Bukovšek et al., doi:10.13001/1081-3810.1199, treat products of commuting nilpotent operators and rank bounds; Hua, doi:10.1017/S0004972700032603, counts finite-field polynomial-matrix solutions, with only abstract-level material accessible in this check. A historical fixed-rank enumeration for a commuting square-zero pair gives one length-four specialization; that specialization is not a new claim here. None of the inspected primary texts supplies the stated all-Gorenstein-algebras fixed-socle-fiber universality. Equivalence to an unlocated orbit calculation remains possible. An inaccessible full text alone is not evidence of noncoverage.

**Value (PASS).** The naturally defined saturated-socle representation fiber is a concrete algebraic invariant of finite-field representation spaces; the theorem proves it is universal across nonisomorphic local Gorenstein algebras and gives the exact multiplicity for every prescribed target. It links a sharp module-freeness criterion to finite-field geometry, including higher embedding dimensions and arbitrary lengths, rather than a small ad hoc numerical slice. No classification of lower-rank fibers or independent certification is claimed.
