# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim specifies the \(3\times3\) Hermitian normalization, ordered gaps, equilibrium renewal observation, and finite \(\mathbb EW^2\). Integrating out the center coordinate gives an exact two-gap density, and an explicit gamma-integral reduction produces exact normalizing, first, and mixed moments. Matrix Brownian self-similarity supplies the fixed-age scaling. The stationary renewal age is length-biased-uniform; Cauchy–Schwarz supplies the sharp \(8/9\) moment-ratio bound and identifies precisely when it is attained. Rational bounds give both strict signs. Numerical quadrature and 234 checks corroborate but cannot replace the analytic identities. Risk: normalization mismatches would rescale numerical constants; the matrix variance convention is therefore stated explicitly.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The complete eigenvalue distribution and Poisson renewal mixture are established by Biroli–Majumdar–Schehr (2025), and are not claimed as new. The 2026 Akemann–Majumdar–Päßler matrix construction retains that prior joint law; the 2025 Shir–Martinez-Azcona–Chenu work treats marginal spacing surmises. Material comparison of their relevant results did not identify the exact adjacent-two-gap covariance or a universal, equality-sharp sign reversal over all stationary renewal clocks. Known three-level Gaussian joint-spacing moments and general covariance-under-mixtures theorems may implicitly recover some steps, so originality cannot be guaranteed by search alone.

## Value — PASS
Neighboring levels in a repulsive finite log-gas have negative fixed-age gap covariance, yet the stationary elapsed-time mixture reverses its sign under **every** independent renewal clock of finite second moment. The normalized correlation has a strict positive floor independent of the waiting-time law, and the extremal protocol is identified exactly. This separates clock-induced collective scale fluctuations from intrinsic eigenvalue repulsion, an interpretable observable for stochastic-reset random matrices. The result does not extrapolate to large matrices or arbitrary resets.
