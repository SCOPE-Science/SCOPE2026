# Universal square-root Tjurina decay under iterated Thom–Sebastiani joins

## Finding

Let \(h\in\mathbb C\{x_1,\ldots,x_d\}\) define an isolated critical point at the origin and let \(A_h=\mathbb C\{\mathbf x\}/J_h\) be its Milnor algebra, where \(J_h\) is generated by all first partial derivatives. Assume multiplication by the class \([h]\) satisfies \([h]^2=0\) in \(A_h\). Write
\[
\mu=\dim_{\mathbb C}A_h,\quad \tau=\dim_{\mathbb C}A_h/([h]),\quad c=\mu-\tau,\quad s=2\tau-\mu.
\]
For every integer \(r\ge1\), let \(F_r\) be the sum of \(r\) copies of \(h\), each in disjoint variables. Then
\[
\boxed{\mu(F_r)=\mu^r,\qquad \tau(F_r)=\sum_{k=0}^r\binom{r}{k}\binom{k}{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}c^ks^{r-k}.}
\]
If \(c>0\), then
\[
\boxed{\frac{\tau(F_r)}{\mu(F_r)}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\pi c r}}\bigl(1+O_h(r^{-1})\bigr)\quad\text{as }r\to\infty.}
\]
If \(c=0\), all \(F_r\) satisfy \(\tau(F_r)=\mu(F_r)\). In particular, the theorem applies to **every isolated complex plane-curve singularity**: the two-variable Briançon–Skoda bound supplies \([h]^2=0\). The normalized Tjurina number tends to zero at the stated universal rate for every nonquasihomogeneous plane-curve germ.

## Assumptions and scope

All local rings are convergent power-series rings over \(\mathbb C\); the germs have isolated critical points. The integer \(r\) varies independently of the fixed initial germ. The Tjurina algebra is \(A_h/([h])\), not a filtration-graded variant; \(\mu\) and \(\tau\) are the ordinary dimensions. Because multiplication by \([h]\) is square-zero, its rank is \(c\), its number of one-dimensional Jordan blocks is \(s=\mu-2c\ge0\), and its number of two-dimensional Jordan blocks is \(c\). The assumption is automatic for \(d=2\) by the Briançon–Skoda theorem. The analytic quasihomogeneity criterion \(\mu=\tau\) is classical. For higher ambient dimension the square-zero hypothesis is required, not automatic.

## Proof

**1. Separated Jacobians.** For disjoint variables, the partial derivatives of \(F_r\) are exactly the partial derivatives of the \(r\) individual summands. Consequently its finite-dimensional Milnor algebra is canonically \(A_h^{\otimes r}\), with dimension \(\mu^r\). The class of \(F_r\) acts by the Kronecker sum
\[
N_r=\sum_{i=1}^r I^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes N\otimes I^{\otimes(r-i)},\qquad N(a)=[h]a.
\]
By definition, \(\tau(F_r)=\dim\operatorname{coker}N_r=\dim\ker N_r\).

**2. Square-zero Jordan reduction.** The operator \(N\) is a direct sum of \(c\) copies of the length-two nilpotent Jordan block \(J_2\) and \(s\) copies of the zero one-dimensional block \(J_1\). Expand the \(r\)-fold tensor product by choosing \(k\) places occupied by \(J_2\). Their multiplicity is \(\binom rk c^ks^{r-k}\). Each such term carries the operator \(J_2\otimes I+\cdots+I\otimes J_2\) on \((\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes k}\).

**3. Central-binomial kernel identity.** View \(\mathbb C^2\) as the standard representation of \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\), with its raising operator conjugate to \(J_2\). On the \(k\)-fold tensor power the operator in Step 2 is the diagonal raising operator. By the decomposition of finite-dimensional \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\)-modules, every irreducible summand contributes precisely one kernel vector. The number of summands equals the dimension of the weight-zero space when \(k\) is even, or the weight-one space when \(k\) is odd. The character \((z+z^{-1})^k\) gives either dimension as \(\binom{k}{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\). This also applies for \(k=0\), when the kernel dimension is one. Summing all tensor summands yields the exact formula.

**4. Integral representation and sharp asymptotic.** Write \(\operatorname{CT}\) for the constant term. The middle-coefficient formula is
\[
\tau(F_r)=\operatorname{CT}\bigl[(1+z)(s+c(z+z^{-1}))^r\bigr]
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(s+2c\cos\theta)^r(1+\cos\theta)\,d\theta.
\]
Indeed the integrand selects the sum of the constant and the coefficient of \(z^1\); for a symmetric Laurent polynomial the latter equals the coefficient of \(z^{-1}\). If \(c>0\), the expansion near zero is
\[
s+2c\cos\theta=\mu-c\theta^2+O(\theta^4),\qquad 1+\cos\theta=2+O(\theta^2).
\]
The maximum of the absolute value of the first factor is \(\mu\) at \(\theta=0\). When \(s>0\) it is strictly smaller everywhere else. When \(s=0\), the sole other equal-magnitude point, \(\theta=\pi\), is suppressed by the quadratic zero of \(1+\cos\theta\), producing a lower-order contribution. Standard Gaussian Laplace expansion around zero therefore gives
\[
\tau(F_r)=\mu^r\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\pi c r}}\bigl(1+O_h(r^{-1})\bigr).
\]
For \(s=0\), the secondary saddle is at most relative order \(O(r^{-1})\), so the same error bound holds. If \(c=0\), the operator is zero on every tensor power, proving the remaining case.

**5. Plane curves.** The two-variable Briançon–Skoda theorem ensures \([h]^2=0\) for any isolated plane-curve germ; \(c>0\) is equivalent to \(\mu>\tau\), i.e. nonquasihomogeneity. This proves the stated dichotomy without restrictions on the degrees or Newton polygons of the plane-curve germ.

## Verification

A self-contained ordinary Python 3 checker (`verify.py`, standard library only) builds the actual Kronecker-sum matrices over the prime field with 101 elements for several small Jordan configurations, computes their ranks by elimination, and compares the resulting kernel dimensions with the closed formula. It checks the published example \((\mu,\tau)=(16,15)\), for which \(\tau(F_2)=226\), and the threefold example \((12,11)\), for which \(\tau(F_3)=1363\). Further numeric evaluations compare the exact \(r\)-fold ratios with the derived leading asymptotic constant. These computations are independent checks of finite instances, not substitutes for Steps 1–5.

## Relationship to prior work

Zheng–Zuo (2026) develop filtered Hodge–Tjurina invariants and two-factor Thom–Sebastiani formulas, including a twofold example with Milnor/Tjurina pair \((16,15)\). Almirón (2023) proves a general two-summand formula and bounds for ordinary Tjurina numbers, and explicitly computes a threefold example with \((\mu,\tau)=(12,11)\). The Milnor tensor product, Tjurina-as-cokernel description and two-variable Briançon–Skoda bound are established foundations. The claim here is the **uniform any-number-of-summands exact expression and its sharp universal decay law**; none of the materially inspected primary discussions supplies that closed law. A general unstated Jordan-block formulation elsewhere could subsume it; this remains a literature risk rather than a claim of exhaustive novelty.

## Limitations

The theorem is about ordinary local Milnor/Tjurina algebras in characteristic zero, not Hodge-filtered Tjurina numbers, global singular hypersurfaces or characteristic-positive analogues. For higher-dimensional initial germs only the explicit square-zero class is covered. The asymptotic is for repeated sums of one fixed germ while the dimension of the ambient space increases. The finite verifier checks only small instances; the general assertion requires the algebraic and analytic argument above. No independent scientific audit has been performed.
