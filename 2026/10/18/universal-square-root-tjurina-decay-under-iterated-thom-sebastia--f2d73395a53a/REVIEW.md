# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** The differential Jacobian ideals in disjoint variables yield the Milnor tensor power, and multiplication by the defining sum yields the Kronecker sum. The square-zero hypothesis gives precisely \(c\) length-two and \(s\) length-one Jordan blocks. The central-binomial kernel identity follows from the diagonal \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) representation; the Fourier integral and Laplace expansion verify the constant \(\sqrt{\mu/(\pi c)}\) including the secondary saddle when \(s=0\). Briançon–Skoda covers all plane-curve germs. A separate exact matrix checker matches the closed expression in several finite cases and reproduces two independently published special-case numbers. The argument proves all \(r\ge1\); numerical tests alone do not.

**Originality — PASS, subject to residual risk.** The 2026 Zheng–Zuo paper develops two-factor filtered formulas and demonstrates the twofold \((16,15)\) example. Almirón (2023) establishes the generic two-factor Tjurina expression/bounds and a threefold concrete computation, but neither inspected treatment provides an arbitrary \(r\)-fold exact formula or universal sharp square-root decay law. Semantic searches of mathematical findings and targeted broader literature terms did not identify a stronger statement; a result phrased as a general nilpotent tensor-operator theorem remains a plausible unindexed source of coverage. Nonappearance in searches is not a proof of uniqueness.

**Value — PASS.** Iterated joins are natural constructions that change the ambient dimension while preserving separated singularity structure. The theorem completely determines all Tjurina numbers of their infinite tower from just \(\mu\) and \(\tau\), shows an exact quasihomogeneous/nonquasihomogeneous asymptotic dichotomy, and computes the sharp decay exponent and constant. This directly complements the classical Milnor–Tjurina ratio problem and extends explicit two- and threefold examples without claiming a general filtered formula. The work is restricted to square-zero Milnor multiplication, which includes every isolated complex plane-curve germ.

**Closest sources and limitations.** Zheng–Zuo, arXiv:2610.08290v1, two-factor Hodge–Tjurina theory and explicit example; Almirón, doi:10.1007/s00009-023-02458-3, Theorem 1, Corollary 2, Example 1, Section 3. Possible implicit coverage via classic \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) tensor identities is the principal novelty risk. No global or characteristic-positive conclusions are asserted.
