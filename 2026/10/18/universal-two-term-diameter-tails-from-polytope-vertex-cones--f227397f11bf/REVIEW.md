# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact squared-distance identity, uniform radial perturbation of product tangent cones and beta integration prove the two-term expansion with its remainder for any fixed polytope. An independent difference-density integral validates square and nonsquare rectangle specializations numerically; numerical checks are not asserted as an infinite-dimensional proof.

**Originality — PASS relative to inspected material.** Mayer–Molchanov's limiting sample-diameter analysis supplies relevant leading extreme-value theory, while Tong–Pan's 2016 and Pure–Durrani–Tong–Pan's 2019 planar polygon-distance methods cover the full planar distribution in principle. The contribution assessed here is the all-dimensional, explicit second-order cone-moment coefficient; isolated planar cases are not presented as novel. Ruben (1978) and other older polygon work pose unresolved historical-priority risks because their complete text was inaccessible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The second-order term captures transverse cone moments absent from first-order pointed-cap asymptotics, giving a natural computable shape-sensitive correction for general polytopes, including multiple diametral pairs and nonsimplicial cones.

The result is restricted to Euclidean distances between two independent uniform points of a fixed convex polytope; there is no uniform remainder in shape, no direct many-point maximum law, no smooth-body extension, and no independent validation.
