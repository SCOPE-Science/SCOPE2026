# Scientific assessment: mirror-protected quantum-path escape

## Correctness — PASS

The result specifies a finite \((2L+1)\)-site open path, a central population absorber, independent site dephasing with off-diagonal rate \(\gamma\), and the survival-decay exponent as the negative spectral abscissa. Reflection makes the antisymmetric sector dark at zero noise, and strict stability of the bright amplitude matrix excludes peripheral leakage from elsewhere. Projected dephasing has Hilbert–Schmidt real part no larger than \(-1/2\); equality inside a Hamiltonian-frequency subspace forces the unique scalar antisymmetric identity, so that a simple dominant real eigenbranch emerges. The exact second-order coefficient comes from a bright Lyapunov equation solved entrywise for arbitrary length. The dissipativity identity establishes eventual absorption for every positive dephasing rate. The packaged finite-size checks compare the displayed Lyapunov solution with an independent numerical solver and compare the quadratic prediction to directly assembled finite Lindbladians. Numerical agreement is corroboration, not the infinite-family proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to publication-priority uncertainty

Caruso, Chin, Datta, Huelga and Plenio, arXiv:0901.4454, give an explicit sink-plus-site-dephasing master equation for fully connected networks, analyze noise-induced loss of dark subspaces, and state a weak-noise lifetime scale of order \(1/\gamma\). Their full-text appendix treats complete-graph dynamics, not the central odd path and its exact quadratic spectral correction. Le, Donati, Severini and Caruso, arXiv:1707.07482, directly discuss dark states at symmetry points on paths (PDF page 3), uniform site dephasing as a way of suppressing dark populations (PDF pages 2–4), and illustrative noise-assisted transport on networks. They do **not** derive the central-sink all-length small-\(\gamma\) spectral-abscissa expansion nor its explicit quadratic term. Marais et al., DOI `10.1088/1367-2630/15/1/013038`, establish analytic environmental-spin-assisted transfer on fully connected networks; Jeske, Vogt and Cole, arXiv:1308.3042, analyze spin-chain noise correlations. Their claims concern different environments/observables and do not imply this exact homogeneous central-path coefficient. General degenerate spectral perturbation theory can frame the argument but does not provide the bright-block inverse explicitly. Targeted mathematical-record and published-literature searches produced no exact stronger coverage; failed searches alone are not a novelty certificate. Priority remains subject to specialist literature review, particularly possible generic dark-state leakage theorems or less easily indexed treatments of first-passage Lindbladians.

## Value — PASS

Weak noise turns a strictly protected sector into a metastable absorber: the leading rate \(\gamma/2\) is independent of hopping, capture rate, and size, while the exact next term grows with the network half-length and distinguishes inverse-capture delay from coherent propagation. Both the exact *spectral* statement and the finite-dimensional closed Lyapunov solution provide analytical calibration for noise-assisted transport and finite-size extrapolation. This goes beyond qualitative noise-assisted absorption and beyond a routine plug-in exercise. It does not claim a uniform large-system expansion, finite-time optimality, or a multiwalker extension.

The foundational 2009 complete-graph paper was inspected including its main sections and master-equation appendix; the other fully-connected paper and the correlated-chain paper were inspected in lawful complete-text versions. Unidentified stronger analytical coverage cannot be excluded without independent bibliographic review.
