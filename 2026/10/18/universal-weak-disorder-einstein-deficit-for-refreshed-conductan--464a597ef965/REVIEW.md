# Scientific review: Universal weak-disorder Einstein deficit for refreshed conductances

**Correctness — PASS.** The explicit generator has a refresh spectral gap for each positive rate; the bounded perturbation admits an analytic stationary density and corrector. The proof independently derives field response and martingale variance—including the environment refresh contribution—and exactly evaluates the Fourier integrals. Numerical quadrature and finite-state generator checks support, but do not replace, these analytical steps. The error bound is an unspecified fixed-rate \(O_\mu(\varepsilon^4)\), so positivity is local in disorder.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Couillard and Scali (arXiv:2610.04598v1) establish qualitative Einstein-relation failure; the present claim gives both explicit second-order responses and a universal strict sign across all positive refresh rates. Avena, Blondel and Faggionato (arXiv:1602.06322) provide prior spectral-gap perturbation techniques and analytic speed expansions. Their general machinery is acknowledged; no exact evaluated result for this joint variance–mobility quantity was identified in inspected material or the searched mathematical records. Earlier static Einstein results do not apply. A broader unlocated response identity or unexamined parts of Komorowski–Olla (2005) could reduce originality and merit independent review.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem resolves a natural quantitative question in dynamically refreshed conductances beyond isolated counterexamples and distinguishes the positive-refresh regime from the singular static limit. The exact rate-dependent coefficient provides a benchmark for future response theory; no global large-disorder or joint-refresh scaling theorem is asserted.

**Limitations.** The coefficient is proved for bounded symmetric iid marks and fixed positive refresh rate, with perturbation amplitude tending to zero. A finite-state computation is not an infinite-volume certificate. All source comparisons are same-authority scientific checks, not a claim of definitive world-wide priority.
