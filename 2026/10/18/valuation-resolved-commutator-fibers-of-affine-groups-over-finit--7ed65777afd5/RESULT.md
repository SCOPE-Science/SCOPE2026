# Valuation-resolved commutator fibers of affine groups over finite chain rings

## Finding

Let \(R\) be any finite commutative chain ring of length \(n\ge2\), with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m=(\pi)\) and residue field of cardinality \(q\). Put \(G=\operatorname{AGL}_1(R)=R^\times\ltimes(R,+)\), where
\[
(a,x)(b,y)=(ab,x+ay).
\]
For \(t\in R\), let \(F(t)\) count ordered pairs \((g,h)\in G^2\) with group commutator \(ghg^{-1}h^{-1}=(1,t)\). Write \(v(t)\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\) for the \(\pi\)-adic valuation, with \(v(0)=n\).

Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
N_0&=q^n-q^{n-1},\\
N_s&=q^{n-s}\quad(1\le s\le n),\\
M_s&=N_s^2-N_{s+1}^2\quad(0\le s<n),\\
M_n&=1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Then **every prescribed commutator fiber** is given exactly by
\[
\boxed{F(t)=q^n\sum_{s=0}^{v(t)}q^sM_s.}
\]
No element with nonidentity multiplicative coordinate can be a commutator. Explicitly,
\[
M_0=q^{2n-1}(q-2),\qquad
M_s=q^{2(n-s)}-q^{2(n-s-1)}\quad(1\le s<n).
\]
In particular, the commutator image is \(\{1\}\times R\) when \(q>2\), but precisely \(\{1\}\times\mathfrak m\) when \(q=2\).

For attainable nonzero targets, \(F(t)\) depends only on \(v(t)\) and **strictly increases with that valuation**. Thus there are exactly \(n\) positive fiber sizes for nonzero commutators if \(q>2\), and exactly \(n-1\) if \(q=2\). For each \(0\le r<n\), exactly \(q^{n-r}-q^{n-r-1}\) central targets have valuation \(r\); the identity is the unique valuation-\(n\) target. This law depends only on \((q,n)\), regardless of the isomorphism type or characteristic of \(R\).

## Assumptions and scope

A finite commutative chain ring is a finite commutative local principal ideal ring with one chain of ideals \(R\supset\pi R\supset\cdots\supset\pi^nR=0\), where \(\pi^{n-1}R\ne0\). Its residue field has \(q\ge2\) elements; the ideal \(\pi^sR\) has \(q^{n-s}\) elements. Every element of \(R^\times\) is a unit. The theorem holds for mixed-characteristic rings such as \(\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z\) and equal-characteristic rings such as \(\mathbb F_q[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^n)\).

The convention is \([g,h]=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}\). The result counts **ordered** pairs, not conjugacy classes of pairs. No field-characteristic, odd-prime, or cyclic-additive-group assumption is made.

## Proof

Write \(g=(a,x)\) and \(h=(b,y)\), where \(a,b\in R^\times\) and \(x,y\in R\). Since \(R\) is commutative, the multiplicative part of every commutator is \(1\). Comparing \(gh=(ab,x+ay)\) with \(hg=(ab,y+bx)\) gives
\[
[g,h]=(1,(a-1)y-(b-1)x).
\]
For fixed \(a,b\), let \(s=\min\{v(a-1),v(b-1)\}\). The image of the \(R\)-linear map
\[
L_{a,b}:R^2\longrightarrow R,\qquad
(x,y)\longmapsto(a-1)y-(b-1)x
\]
is exactly the ideal \(I=(a-1,b-1)=\pi^sR\), of size \(q^{n-s}\). Every fiber over \(I\) has the common size
\[
\frac{|R|^2}{|I|}=q^{n+s}.
\]
There are no solutions for \(t\notin I\).

It remains to count unit pairs with this minimum valuation. Set
\[
U_s=\{a\in R^\times:v(a-1)\ge s\}.
\]
For \(s\ge1\), \(U_s=1+\pi^sR\), so \(|U_s|=q^{n-s}\), including \(|U_n|=1\). For \(s=0\), \(U_0=R^\times\) has \(q^n-q^{n-1}\) elements. Consequently the number of pairs \((a,b)\in U_s^2\setminus U_{s+1}^2\) with minimum valuation exactly \(s<n\) is \(M_s=N_s^2-N_{s+1}^2\), while the sole pair at level \(n\) is \((1,1)\). Thus a target with valuation \(r\) lies in the image exactly for levels \(s\le r\), and
\[
F(t)=\sum_{s=0}^{r}M_s q^{n+s}=q^n\sum_{s=0}^{r}q^sM_s.
\]
The claimed expressions for \(M_s\) follow from substitution. Notably \(M_0=q^{2n-1}(q-2)\), which vanishes exactly when \(q=2\). For every \(1\le s<n\), \(M_s>0\). Therefore the attainable target valuations begin at \(0\) for \(q>2\) and at \(1\) for \(q=2\); all greater nonzero valuations occur. For \(1\le r<n\),
\[
F(\pi^r)-F(\pi^{r-1})=q^{n+r}M_r>0,
\]
where the valuation notation denotes an arbitrary element of the stated valuation; when \(q=2\) the level-\(0\) fiber is zero. The number of elements of valuation \(r\) is \(|\pi^rR|-|\pi^{r+1}R|=q^{n-r}-q^{n-r-1}\). All formulas use only the ideal-chain cardinalities, proving uniformity across chain rings.

As an independent consistency check, the zero fiber has size \(|G|\,k(G)\), where \(k(G)\) denotes the conjugacy-class count. Substituting \(v(0)=n\) and telescoping gives
\[
F(0)=|G|\left(q^n-q^{n-1}+\frac{q^n-1}{q-1}\right).
\]
This identity is a check, not a separate novelty claim. It also follows by classifying conjugacy with fixed multiplicative component using unit orbits modulo the ideal generated by \(a-1\).

## Verification

Run `python3 checker.py` in the same directory as this document. The standalone checker constructs group multiplication and inverses, computes \(ghg^{-1}h^{-1}\) **directly** for every ordered pair, and compares each target fiber against the formula. Its ring models include \(\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z\) for \(p\in\{2,3,5\}\) and admissible small lengths, and three distinct dual-number models with residue fields of orders \(2,3,4\). It checks total pair counts, support of the commutator image, and the standard identity \(F(0)=|G|k(G)\). The independent run returns `VERIFY_OK`.

For \(R=\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\), the identity has \(40\) ordered-pair commutators, the nonzero element of \(2R\) has \(24\), and odd targets have zero. For \(R=\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z\), the three valuation layers have \(243\), \(459\), and \(540\) representations. Over \(\mathbb F_4[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\), the corresponding layers have \(2048\), \(3008\), and \(3264\) representations.

The finite enumeration is a check on the algebraic proof, not a substitute for an infinite-family argument.

## Relationship to prior work

Juráš and Ursul study commuting probabilities of finite groups and rings, including general equalities and transformations of commuting probabilities. Their 2020 preprint concerns the **identity** commutator fiber and general comparisons, not the valuation of a prescribed target in \(\operatorname{AGL}_1(R)\).

Pradhan and Sury examine rational representations of holomorphs of cyclic prime-power groups, which include \(\operatorname{AGL}_1(\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z)\) as a special case. Their inspected article does not supply this target-by-target commutator distribution; the present result also covers noncyclic additive chain rings.

Kasai's 2026 article treats the same affine groups over finite commutative principal ideal rings. It explicitly uses the commutation identity \((a-1)d=(c-1)b\) to study a **cross-commutation** condition on quadruples. That elementary identity is prior work; the contribution established here is the exact prescribed, potentially nonzero, group-commutator fiber law, its valuation multiplicities, and the residue-field-two support dichotomy. The later paper supplies relevant mathematical comparison but not the dating anchor of this finding.

Publicly indexed work on scalar affine Frobenius groups and on additive commutators of Kronecker path algebras was also compared. The former studies character-codegree pseudo-algebras of field-based groups; the latter studies additive commutators in noncommutative associative algebras. Neither statement implies the formula for prescribed group commutators in chain-ring affine groups.

## Limitations

The ring must be commutative and a chain ring; general finite principal ideal rings can be handled through products but are not asserted here. The result is an exact word-map count, not a classification of the groups, representations, or commuting graphs. Commuting probabilities and conjugacy-class counts for several cyclic special cases are independently known; no originality is asserted for those zero-fiber identities. Related semidirect-product character formulas may provide another route to the same distribution; bibliographic completeness beyond the inspected literature is not certified.
