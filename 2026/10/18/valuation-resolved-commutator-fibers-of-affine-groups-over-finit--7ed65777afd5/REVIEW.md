# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

For any pair of units the group commutator reduces exactly to an \(R\)-linear map from \(R^2\) onto the ideal generated by two unit differences. The kernel has size \(q^{n+s}\), where \(s\) is the minimum of their valuations. The nested unit sets have sizes \(N_0=q^n-q^{n-1}\) and \(N_s=q^{n-s}\) for \(s\ge1\), giving the difference-of-squares coefficients and the full formula. The exceptional \(q=2\) case follows because \(M_0=0\), not from a finite test. Independently enumerated group multiplication and inverses over modular and dual-number rings match every target fiber. The standalone proof covers all finite commutative chain rings; experiments are illustrative only.

## Originality — PASS with residual risk

The 2020 Juráš–Ursul analysis concerns commuting probability and not all prescribed targets. The 2021 Pradhan–Sury article concerns representations of cyclic-group holomorphs. Kasai's 2026 full text gives the relevant affine commutation equation and a different cross-commutation classification, without the valuation-stratified prescribed group-commutator count. An inspected published scalar-affine group paper studies character codegrees, and an inspected Kronecker-algebra result concerns additive, not group, commutators. Searches under the word-map, holomorph, semidirect-product, modular-ring, and valuation formulations did not find direct coverage. Residual risk: a stronger existing character-theoretic treatment of affine semidirect-product word maps may imply this distribution. The elementary commutation formula and zero-fiber/class-number deductions are not claimed as original.

## Value — PASS

The result computes a natural finite-group word-map invariant at **every** target, not just the commuting probability, for all residue fields and chain lengths; it gives exact multiplicities of fiber sizes and separates the residue-field-two support geometry from the odd/larger-field case. Ring-independence across mixed- and equal-characteristic chain rings is a structural consequence. The target-by-target classification is a reusable exact distribution for finite affine groups and their nilpotent-residue deformations.
