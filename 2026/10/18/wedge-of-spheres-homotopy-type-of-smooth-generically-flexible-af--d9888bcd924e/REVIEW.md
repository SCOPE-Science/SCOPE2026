# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
For every integer \(n\ge1\), the two invariant quotient charts are computed explicitly and cover the variety. The principal open chart is affine \(\mathbb A^{n+2}\). The boundary has the algebraic parametrization \((b,s,U_2,\ldots,U_n)\), giving \(\mathbb A^1\times(\mathbb G_m)^n\), and the invariant parameter \(q=xu_1\) trivializes its normal bundle. The tubular-neighborhood homotopy-pushout argument identifies the quotient with the Thom space of this line bundle, and the iterated suspension splitting proves the integral wedge decomposition. The included independent arithmetic checker verifies monomial weights and exponents, not an infinite-family theorem. Scope: complex topology for these quotients only.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The originating work is A. Nikitina, *Generically Flexible Varieties with an Invariant Divisor*, arXiv:2610.05344v1, especially the introductory construction and its explicit invariant ring. It presents the same smooth generically flexible but not flexible family, and does not state its affine-space complement, torus-cylinder divisor, trivial normal line, full classical homotopy type, or integral cohomology ring. S. Gaifullin, arXiv:2507.14745v1, studies a different, generally singular family with invariant divisors and likewise does not imply the stated result. Targeted searches for equivalent quotient topology, homotopy, Thom-space, Betti and torus-divisor descriptions found no covering computation. The cited topological theorems themselves are standard; the new claim is their concrete geometric application to the identified family. Unindexed equivalent calculations or stronger generally phrased applications remain possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result computes the **entire unstable complex homotopy type** in every dimension for a natural recently constructed class of smooth non-flexible affine varieties. It exhibits nontrivial, torsion-free homology in an explicitly controlled family despite the affine-space complement, and gives the full integral cup-product structure, rather than only an Euler characteristic or partial Betti count. The explanation through a normal-trivial torus boundary may also be useful for comparing flexibility and topology in related quotient constructions.

## Limits
The result does not address algebraic \(\mathbb A^1\)-homotopy, the whole automorphism group, or arbitrary generically flexible affine varieties. Independent external mathematical assessment has not occurred. The originality check cannot exclude unidentified equivalent work.
