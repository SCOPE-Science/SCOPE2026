# Scientific review of the wedge-sparse cosmological-polytope width theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** The polytope vertices all have coordinate sum one and span the correct affine hyperplane. Their score covariance census is exact: variance three, correlation minus one-third within edge, and five plus-one-third versus four minus-one-third correlations across each pair of incident edges. With the wedge count as the exact dependency-pair parameter, the bivariate Gaussian orthant estimate supplies a \(N^{-3/2}(\log N)^{-1/4}\) upper bound per dependent signed pair. The local-dependence process theorem yields two *independent* Poisson limits and therefore two independent Gumbels. The Gaussian-to-intrinsic-sphere denominator is negligible at the specified scale. A matching of linear size supports moment convergence when degrees are uniformly bounded, but not for general dense graphs; the written claim respects that boundary. A checker verifies the finite geometric and covariance premises and the exact support identity, without treating simulations as proof.

**Originality — PASS, with a methodological qualification.** Cosmological-polytope vertex coordinates and face structure are credited to the foundational and combinatorial literature. The sum-of-two-Gumbels limit has already been established for regular simplex widths, and the Poisson approximation mechanism is classical; neither is claimed to be new. The analyzed geometric claim identifies precisely how a graph's incident-edge wedge count controls the full *cosmological-polytope* Gaussian incidence process and spherical width, with a logarithmic-quarter density allowance sufficient even for complete graphs. Relevant primary sources and mathematical-record searches did not reveal this graph-indexed geometric statement. General Gaussian-array approximation results are substantial prior tools and remain the closest possible route to an equivalent reformulation; specialist literature may still contain one.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The theorem explains why a wide range of combinatorially disparate cosmological polytopes—including path/cycle sparse families and complete-graph dense families—share one nontrivial intrinsic directional-width fluctuation law, while a hub-dominated star lies outside its geometric criterion. The precise wedge condition, orientation-independent covariance census, rate of rare-event approximation, and bounded-degree leading variance constant are natural structural invariants, not a chosen finite sample or arbitrary parameter slice. No converse or exact sharp threshold is claimed.

**Closest references and limits.** Kühne–Monin, arXiv:2209.08069, supplies the polytope structure; Benjes–Ferry–Schröter, arXiv:2503.13393, studies triangulations, not directional extremes; Kabluchko–Litvak–Zaporozhets, arXiv:1511.08479, proves a superficially identical Gumbel-sum law for *regular simplices*, but not the graph-polytope wedge criterion; Arratia–Goldstein–Gordon, DOI 10.1214/ss/1177012015, gives the general Poisson machinery, not the specific Euclidean graph construction. Sparse and dense families can have markedly different finite-size convergence rates; the general-degree moment limit is deliberately left unproved. Bibliographic equivalence risk and unperformed independent mathematical validation remain explicit.
