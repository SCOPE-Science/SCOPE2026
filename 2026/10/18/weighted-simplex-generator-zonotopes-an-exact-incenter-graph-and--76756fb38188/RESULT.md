# Weighted simplex-generator zonotopes: an exact incenter graph and discrepancy-controlled outer contacts

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge 3\), let \(w_1,\ldots,w_n>0\), let \(H=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\sum_i x_i=0\}\) carry its inherited Euclidean metric, and put \(v_i=e_i-\mathbf1/n\). Define the centered, full-dimensional zonotope
\[
Z(w)=\sum_{i=1}^n[-w_iv_i/2,w_iv_i/2]\subset H.
\]
Write \(s=\min_{i<j}(w_i+w_j)\), and let \(G_s\) be the graph on \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\) whose edges are exactly the pairs satisfying \(w_i+w_j=s\). Then:

1. The complete irredundant facet description is
\[
Z(w)=\{x\in H:|x_i-x_j|\le (w_i+w_j)/2\ \text{for all }i<j\}.
\]
There are exactly \(n(n-1)\) oriented facets.

2. Its Euclidean inradius is \(r=s/(2\sqrt2)\). The **entire** set of centers of radius-\(r\) balls contained in \(Z(w)\) is
\[
\mathcal I(w)=\{z\in H:|z_i-z_j|\le (w_i+w_j-s)/2\ \text{for all }i<j\}.
\]
If \(c(G_s)\) denotes the number of connected components of \(G_s\), then
\[
\dim\operatorname{aff}\mathcal I(w)=c(G_s)-1.
\]
The centered maximal ball touches exactly \(2|E(G_s)|\) facets, with contact unit normals \(\pm(e_i-e_j)/\sqrt2\) for \(\{i,j\}\in E(G_s)\).

3. If the smallest weight occurs \(k\ge2\) times, then \(\dim\operatorname{aff}\mathcal I(w)=n-k\) and the centered ball has \(k(k-1)\) facet contacts. If instead the smallest weight is unique and the second-smallest value occurs \(q\ge1\) times, these values are \(n-q-1\) and \(2q\). Thus the incenter is unique precisely when all weights agree, or one weight is strictly smaller than all the other mutually equal weights. In contrast, generic strictly increasing weights give an \((n-2)\)-dimensional incenter locus with only two centered-ball facet contacts.

4. Put \(\delta(w)=\min_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^n}|\sum_i\varepsilon_iw_i|\). The circumradius and its complete outer-contact vertex set are
\[
R(w)^2=\frac14\left(\sum_iw_i^2-\frac{\delta(w)^2}{n}\right),\qquad
\left\{\frac12\sum_i\varepsilon_iw_iv_i:\left|\sum_i\varepsilon_iw_i\right|=\delta(w)\right\}.
\]
The origin is the unique circumcenter. For positive integer weights, deciding whether \(4R(w)^2=\sum_iw_i^2\) is weakly NP-complete, being exactly the PARTITION decision problem. The formulas separate a two-smallest-weight inradius and a graph-controlled center locus from a partition-discrepancy-controlled circumradius.

The novel structural emphasis is the **complete incenter-locus and contact classification**. General formulas for zonotope radii and the unweighted simplex-generated case predate this work; the numerical radius expressions should not be interpreted as independently novel general radius theorems.

## Assumptions and scope
All weights are strictly positive real numbers. Every dimension and all centers, contacts and facets refer to the Euclidean metric of the sum-zero subspace \(H\), **not** the metric of arbitrary oblique coordinates. Equality cases include all weight ties. The PARTITION complexity statement applies to positive integer weights encoded in binary; it is a weak, not strong, NP-completeness assertion. A zero weight is excluded, because facet combinatorics and full-dimensionality can change.

## Proof
**Facet structure.** The vectors \(v_i\) satisfy the single dependence \(\sum_i v_i=0\), and any \(n-1\) of them are independent in \(H\). For a nonzero outer normal \(u\in H\), the exposed face of \(Z(w)\) is a translate of the zonotope generated by the \(v_i\) for which \(\langle u,v_i\rangle=u_i=0\). It is a facet precisely when these zero-index generators span dimension \(n-2\), equivalently when \(u\) has exactly two nonzero coordinates. Because \(u\in H\), such a normal is proportional to \(e_i-e_j\). Its support number is
\[
h_{Z(w)}(e_i-e_j)=\tfrac12\sum_kw_k|\langle e_i-e_j,v_k\rangle|=(w_i+w_j)/2.
\]
All such faces contain \(n-2\) independent generators and really are facets. A bounded full-dimensional polytope equals the intersection of its facet halfspaces, proving part 1.

**Optimal radius and every incenter.** The distance from the origin to the facet with normal \(e_i-e_j\) is \((w_i+w_j)/(2\sqrt2)\), so the origin-centered maximal radius is \(r=s/(2\sqrt2)\). Since \(Z(w)=-Z(w)\), reflecting any contained ball of radius \(\rho\) and taking the midpoint of the two balls produces an origin-centered ball of radius \(\rho\). Hence no translated ball has larger radius. A ball of radius \(r\) centered at \(z\in H\) satisfies every facet inequality exactly when
\[
|z_i-z_j|+\sqrt2r\le (w_i+w_j)/2.
\]
This is the displayed formula for \(\mathcal I(w)\). Every edge of \(G_s\) forces \(z_i=z_j\). Conversely, restricting to the linear space on which these edge equalities hold, all inequalities corresponding to nonedges are strict at \(z=0\). An open relative neighborhood of zero therefore lies in \(\mathcal I(w)\), so its affine hull is exactly the subspace of vectors constant on components of \(G_s\) and with coordinate sum zero. It has dimension \(c(G_s)-1\). The claimed contact normals and counts follow from equality in the facet distances. If \(k\ge2\) weights share the minimum, \(G_s\) is their complete graph plus isolated other vertices; otherwise \(G_s\) is a star joining the unique least-weight vertex to every vertex at the second-smallest weight. This gives every stated dimension, contact count, and center-uniqueness case.

**Farthest vertices, discrepancy, and complexity.** The zonotope is the image under orthogonal projection onto \(H\) of the weighted box \(\prod_i[-w_i/2,w_i/2]\). Its signed cube corners project to
\[
x_\varepsilon=\tfrac12\left((\varepsilon_iw_i)_{i=1}^n-\frac1n\sum_i\varepsilon_iw_i\,\mathbf1\right),
\quad
4\|x_\varepsilon\|^2=\sum_iw_i^2-\frac1n\left(\sum_i\varepsilon_iw_i\right)^2.
\]
A convex quadratic reaches its maximum on a polytope at a vertex; thus maximizing over signed corners gives the stated radius. Any sign vector containing both signs exposes a distinct zonotope vertex: choose a normal \(u\in H\) with \(\operatorname{sgn}(u_i)=\varepsilon_i\), possible by positive scaling the two nonempty sign classes until their sums cancel. All-equal sign patterns never minimize the discrepancy, because reversing the sign of a single positive weight strictly decreases the absolute sum. Hence the displayed set classifies **every** farthest vertex. Symmetry makes the origin a circumcenter; for any nonzero competing center \(z\), an antipodal farthest pair \(\pm x\) satisfies \(\max\{\|x-z\|^2,\|-x-z\|^2\}\ge\|x\|^2+\|z\|^2>R(w)^2\), proving uniqueness. Finally, with positive integral weights, the equality \(4R(w)^2=\sum_iw_i^2\) holds if and only if \(\delta(w)=0\), exactly a yes-instance of PARTITION. A sign vector is a polynomial-time certificate, and the classical PARTITION problem is weakly NP-complete. This proves part 4.

## Verification
An exact-rational standalone checker, `CHECK.py`, independently enumerates all \(2^n-2\) mixed-sign vertices for \(3\le n\le8\) on 48 distinct positive-integer weight profiles. It tests every proposed facet halfspace, proves each candidate facet has affine dimension \(n-2\) by rational Gaussian elimination, confirms the squared circumradius by exhaustive signed vertices, and reconstructs the incenter equality graph. It also exhibits a full-dimensional relative simplex of feasible nonzero centers wherever the predicted center dimension is positive. The execution completed 1,328 independent facet-rank checks and 3,936 exact vertex checks. The computations test finite cases only; the all-dimensional result follows from the proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Joós and Lángi, *Isoperimetric problems for zonotopes* (first arXiv version June 7, 2022), study precisely the general class of zonotopes with dimension plus one generators, establish generic signed-vector and support-function formulas for circumradius and inradius (their equations (2) and (3)), and prove volume/mean-width extremal inequalities. Those general scalar formulas already imply the existence of radius descriptions; the present signed-discrepancy identity is an explicit specialization, not an independent general radius theorem. The **newly assessed distinction** is a complete formula and affine-dimension classification for every optimal inball center, the exact tangent-facet graph for arbitrary positive weights, and a simultaneous identification of the outer contacts. Živaljević's *Illumination complexes, Δ-zonotopes, and the polyhedral curtain theorem* (2013) gives the regular/unweighted simplex-generator construction and its root-system facet description, but does not classify weighted optimal-center loci. Heck, Reis and Rothvoss, *The Vector Balancing Constant for Zonotopes* (2022), study vector balancing in general zonotopes, a different optimization invariant. A bibliographic search did not identify a covering weighted incenter-locus theorem; non-detection is not proof of historical novelty.

## Limitations
The asserted scientific increment is the equality/center structure, not the routine substitution of generator weights into existing general radius formulas. The complexity assertion concerns the exact outer-radius threshold, not approximation algorithms or strong NP-hardness. The center locus formula determines all centers but does not enumerate their vertices or compute their volume. The theorem has not undergone independent mathematical audit; an equivalent older incenter result under metric-graph or polytope-erosion terminology remains possible.
