# Wheel perfect-matching circles and a sharp change in their inclusion into matching complexes

## Finding
For every integer \(r\ge2\), put \(N=2r+1\), and let \(W_{2r+2}=K_1*C_N\) be the wheel with hub \(h\) and odd cycle rim. Let \(\mathcal P_r\) be the simplicial complex on graph edges whose facets are perfect matchings, and let \(\mathcal M_r\) be the ordinary matching complex. Then
\[
\mathcal P_r\simeq S^1,\qquad
\widetilde H_j(\mathcal P_r;\mathbb Z)=
\begin{cases}\mathbb Z,&j=1,\\0,&j\ne1.\end{cases}
\]
Its natural \(D_N\)-action on \(\widetilde H_1\) is the one-dimensional orientation character: every rim rotation acts by \(+1\) and every rim reflection by \(-1\).

More strongly, the facet-intersection nerve has an explicit simple-homotopy collapse to the \(N\)-cycle, using exactly \(N(2^{r-2}-1)\) elementary face pairs. The natural inclusion \(j_r:\mathcal P_r\hookrightarrow\mathcal M_r\) has a sharp transition: \(j_2\) is non-nullhomotopic and a primitive split injection on integral first homology, whereas \(j_r\) is nullhomotopic for every \(r\ge3\). In the smallest case, \(\mathcal M_2\simeq\bigvee^6S^1\), with \(\mathcal M_2\) obtained from \(\mathcal P_2\) by adding five one-cells. All \(\mathcal P_r\) are pure \(r\)-dimensional and are neither shellable nor Cohen--Macaulay over any field.

## Assumptions and scope
Graph indices are as follows. The rim vertices are \(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\), with rim edges \(e_i=\{i,i+1\}\) and spoke edges \(s_i=\{h,i\}\), indices modulo \(N\). A perfect matching is a set of pairwise disjoint graph edges covering all \(N+1\) vertices. Its subsets form the faces of \(\mathcal P_r\). A matching is an arbitrary set of pairwise disjoint graph edges; its subsets form \(\mathcal M_r\). The wheel-automorphism group is \(D_N\), generated by \(i\mapsto i+1\) and \(i\mapsto-i\) on rim vertices with \(h\) fixed. The theorem is asserted for every integer \(r\ge2\), not for the exceptional four-vertex wheel.

For integers \(N>k\ge1\), denote by \(B(N,k)\) the simplicial complex with facets all cyclic intervals of length \(k\): \(\{a,a+1,\dots,a+k-1\}\subset\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\). This is \(\mathcal N(N,k-1)\) in the notation of Adamaszek et al. [3], and must not be confused with the clique/flag complex on the same vertices.

## Proof
**Unique perfect matchings.** Every perfect matching contains exactly one spoke \(s_i\), as all spokes touch \(h\). Removing \(h\) and rim vertex \(i\) leaves a path on \(2r\) rim vertices, whose unique perfect matching uses consecutive alternating rim edges. Consequently the complete list of facets is
\[
M_i=\{s_i,e_{i+1},e_{i+3},\dots,e_{i+2r-1}\},\qquad i\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z.
\]
These are \(N\) distinct maximal simplices with \(r+1\) vertices, proving purity and dimension \(r\).

**Identify the facet nerve.** Cover \(\mathcal P_r\) by the full simplices \(\Delta(M_i)\). Every nonempty intersection of these covering simplices is a simplex and hence contractible. The simplicial nerve theorem therefore identifies \(\mathcal P_r\) with their nerve \(L_r\). A spoke occurs in only one facet. For each rim edge \(e_j\), the indices of facets containing it form precisely
\[
S_j=\{j+2,j+4,\dots,j+2r\}\subset\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z.
\]
Since \(r\ge2\), every face of the nerve is contained in some \(S_j\), and the \(S_j\) are all its maximal faces. Multiplication by the inverse of \(2\) modulo odd \(N\) sends these to all cyclic length-\(r\) intervals, giving an explicit simplicial isomorphism \(L_r\cong B(N,r)\).

**Uniform elementary collapses.** For every integer \(t\) with \(3\le t\le r\), the facet \(F_a=\{a,a+1,\dots,a+t-1\}\) of \(B(N,t)\) contains the endpoint edge \(E_a=\{a,a+t-1\}\). Because \(2(t-1)<N\), no other length-\(t\) cyclic interval contains both endpoints. Thus \(E_a\) is a free face with unique maximal coface \(F_a\). Deleting all faces containing \(E_a\) in \(F_a\) is a composite of elementary simplicial collapses: pair the faces in the Boolean interval \([E_a,F_a]\) by toggling one fixed vertex of \(F_a\setminus E_a\). The deleted intervals for different \(a\) are disjoint because each \(E_a\) belongs to a unique maximal facet. After deleting them all, exactly the faces supported in length-\((t-1)\) cyclic intervals remain, since a face of \(F_a\) not containing both endpoints lies entirely in one of its two length-\((t-1)\) end subintervals. Therefore \(B(N,t)\searrow B(N,t-1)\), with \(N2^{t-3}\) elementary pairs. Iterating gives
\[
L_r\cong B(N,r)\searrow B(N,2)=C_N,
\qquad\sum_{t=3}^rN2^{t-3}=N(2^{r-2}-1).
\]
For \(r=2\), the nerve is already \(C_5\). By the nerve theorem, \(\mathcal P_r\simeq C_N\simeq S^1\) integrally for every \(r\ge2\). The collapse certificate applies to the facet nerve; an elementary collapse of the original larger complex is not asserted.

**Dihedral representation.** The facet cover is permuted by the rim dihedral group and every nonempty intersection is a simplex whose setwise stabilizer has the barycenter as a fixed point. The standard equivariant nerve construction thus gives an isomorphism on integral homology representations between \(\mathcal P_r\) and \(L_r\). In the cyclic-interval model, a rim rotation is an orientation-preserving rotation of the indexing circle (possibly by a step other than one), while a rim reflection reverses its orientation. To calculate the induced action without assuming the non-equivariant elementary collapses are equivariant, represent \(B(N,r)\) as the nerve of equally spaced closed arcs of length \((r-1)/N<1/2\) on a circle, with each arc centered at the corresponding polygon vertex. All intersections are empty or contractible, and the circle-arc nerve equivalence is dihedrally natural. It follows that rotations act on \(H_1\cong\mathbb Z\) by \(+1\) and reflections by \(-1\), in agreement with Proposition 7.3 of [3] in the one-circle regime.

**The inclusion threshold.** For \(r=2\), the wheel has six graph vertices. Every three-edge matching covers all six vertices, so the ordinary matching complex and perfect-matching complex have exactly the same two-simplices. All ten wheel edges already belong to perfect matchings. Direct pair enumeration gives 15 one-simplices in \(\mathcal P_2\) and 20 in \(\mathcal M_2\). Hence \(\mathcal M_2\) is obtained from the connected complex \(\mathcal P_2\simeq S^1\) by attaching five one-cells with endpoints already connected. Each such cell adds one free circle and splits the induced map on first integral homology. Therefore \(\mathcal M_2\simeq\bigvee^6 S^1\) and \((j_2)_*:\mathbb Z\hookrightarrow\mathbb Z^6\) has primitive direct-summand image.

For \(r\ge3\), Theorem 5.2 of Vega [1] proves that the ordinary matching complex of any wheel on \(n\) vertices is a wedge of spheres of dimension \(\lceil(n-4)/3\rceil\), with positive multiplicities depending on \(n\bmod3\). At \(n=2r+2\ge8\), this dimension is at least two. Thus \(\mathcal M_r\) is simply connected; every map \(\mathcal P_r\simeq S^1\to\mathcal M_r\) is nullhomotopic. Finally, purity in dimension \(r\ge2\) combined with nonzero \(\widetilde H_1\) below top dimension obstructs Cohen--Macaulayness over every field and pure shellability.

## Verification
The standalone file `checks/check_wheel.py` constructs the wheel graph directly, enumerates its perfect matchings independently, compares the facet nerve with its cyclic-interval model, verifies the uniquely supported endpoint-edge collapse at every stage, and checks all rotation/reflection Lefschetz numbers on the original perfect-matching complex for each \(2\le r\le8\). It also verifies the precise exceptional six-vertex inclusion face counts. The recorded output is `checks/check_output.txt`; the program uses only the Python standard library. For each tested \(r\), all rotations have unreduced Lefschetz number zero and reflections have Lefschetz number two, consistent with the one-dimensional orientation character. These finite tests corroborate, but do not replace, the uniform proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Vega [1, Theorem 5.2] classifies **ordinary** matching complexes of wheels, not the subcomplex generated by perfect matchings, and does not determine the induced inclusion map between them. Bayer, Jelić Milutinović and Vega [2, Sections 1--2] define perfect-matching complexes and use a facet-nerve strategy for honeycomb graphs, a structurally different family. Adamaszek, Adams, Frick, Peterson and Previte-Johnson [3, Sections 3 and 7] already prove the circle homotopy type and dihedral action of **abstract cyclic-interval nerves**; that fact is not claimed new here. The present wheel-specific contribution is the exact identification of perfect-matching facet intersections with one of those nerves, the explicit all-parameter collapse certificate in wheel notation, and the sharp inclusion threshold obtained by comparison with ordinary wheel matching complexes. None of the inspected sources states that combined wheel result.

## Limitations
The theorem treats odd rims of length at least five; four-vertex wheels behave differently. The \(D_N\)-representation statement concerns integral first homology, not a claim that the original perfect-matching complex equivariantly collapses simplicially to a polygon. The ordinary-complex inclusion vanishing in the stable range uses the published ordinary wheel matching-complex classification. Equivalent unindexed or inaccessible prior work could alter the originality assessment; no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
