# Wild-characteristic thickening of the rank-one circulant determinantal scheme

## Finding
For an integer \(n\ge3\), let \(C_n\) be the \(n\)-by-\(n\) circulant matrix \((x_{i-j\bmod n})_{0\le i,j<n}\), and let \(I_n\subseteq\mathbb Z[x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1}]\) be its ideal of all \(2\)-by-\(2\) minors. As a projective scheme over \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z\),
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Proj}(\mathbb Z[x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1}]/I_n)\ \simeq\ \mu_n=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z[z]/(z^n-1).}
\]
The isomorphism is induced by \(z\mapsto[1:z:\cdots:z^{n-1}]\), interpreted scheme-theoretically. In particular this projective family is finite flat of degree \(n\) over \(\mathbb Z\), not merely a collection of points on geometric fibers.

If \(k\) is algebraically closed of characteristic \(p>0\), write \(n=p^a m\) with \(p\nmid m\), allowing \(a=0\). The special fiber has exactly \(m\) distinct geometric support points; at each, its completed local ring is
\[
\boxed{k[[u]]/(u^{p^a}).}
\]
Thus the rank-one circulant locus degenerates from \(n\) reduced points when \(p\nmid n\) to precisely \(m\) curvilinear fat points, each of length \(p^a\), when \(p\mid n\). There is no hidden embedded contribution beyond these local Artin rings.

The homogeneous coordinate ring has \(\mathbb Z\)-free degree-\(d\) part of rank \(n\) for every \(d\ge1\), and degree-zero part \(\mathbb Z\). For every field its Hilbert series is \((1+(n-1)t)/(1-t)\), and its minimal graded resolution has the characteristic-independent ranks \(\beta_{i,i+1}=i\binom{n}{i+1}\) for \(1\le i\le n-1\), with all other positive Betti numbers zero. These homological formulas are provided as derived context rather than claimed independent novelty.

## Assumptions and scope
The integral ideal is generated by *all* \(2\)-by-\(2\) minors, not only consecutive or principal minors. No roots-of-unity hypothesis is placed on the base; the projective family is considered with scheme structure. In the geometric-fiber statement \(k\) is algebraically closed, and the local rings are the completed local rings at the distinct geometric support points. The characteristic-zero fiber is reduced of length \(n\).

## Proof
Write indices in \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). A minor with rows \(r,s\) and columns \(c,d\) has the form \(x_{r-c}x_{s-d}-x_{r-d}x_{s-c}\), so both degree-two monomials have the same cyclic sum of subscripts. Conversely, if \(i,j\not\equiv0\pmod n\), the minor with rows \(i+j,j\) and columns \(j,0\) gives
\[
 x_ix_j-x_0x_{i+j}=0.
\]
When one index is zero the same identity is trivial. Repeatedly applying these identities to any degree-\(d\ge1\) monomial expresses its class as \(x_0^{d-1}x_s\), where \(s\) is the cyclic sum of all subscripts. Therefore the homogeneous quotient has at most \(n\) spanning monomials in each positive degree.

Define a graded map into \(\mathbb Z[t,z]/(z^n-1)\) by \(x_i\mapsto tz^i\). Every minor maps to zero. The \(n\) candidate normal monomials map to the \(\mathbb Z\)-linearly independent elements \(t^dz^s\), \(0\le s<n\). Hence these normal monomials form an integral basis in every positive degree, and the stated Hilbert function and flatness of graded pieces hold without any restriction on residue characteristic.

Taking \(d=n\) and summing equal subscripts shows \(x_i^n=x_0^n\) for all \(i\). Consequently every point of the projective scheme lies in \(D_+(x_0)\): the quotient by \(x_0\) has nilpotent irrelevant ideal. On this chart, write \(z=x_1/x_0\). The quadratic identities give successively \(x_i/x_0=z^i\) and then \(z^n=1\), with no other relations by the preceding normal-form independence. Thus the entire projective scheme is \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z[z]/(z^n-1)\). Its coordinate algebra is free of rank \(n\) over \(\mathbb Z\), proving finite flatness of the actual projective family.

Over characteristic \(p>0\), the Frobenius identity gives \(z^n-1=(z^m-1)^{p^a}\). Since \(p\nmid m\), \(z^m-1\) has precisely \(m\) simple roots in \(k\). The Chinese remainder theorem gives the indicated product of rings \(k[u]/(u^{p^a})\), where \(u=z-\zeta\), and proves that no other associated points or nilpotent structure occur.

For the homological context, multiplication by \(x_0\) is injective on the graded normal-form basis in every degree. Over a field this makes the one-dimensional standard graded ring Cohen–Macaulay with \(h\)-polynomial \(1+(n-1)t\). The standard Cohen–Macaulay regularity formula yields \(\operatorname{reg}(S/I_n)=1\); as there are no linear generators, its minimal resolution is \(2\)-linear. Coefficient comparison in \((1+(n-1)t)(1-t)^{n-1}\) gives exactly \(\beta_{i,i+1}=i\binom n{i+1}\). The computation holds over every field; it is not an appeal to diagonalizing a circulant matrix, which fails when \(p\mid n\).

## Verification
A standalone exact checker `verify.py` checks, for \(3\le n\le8\), all quadratic circulant-minor relations, their cyclic normal-form classes, graded residue classes through degree six, and the claimed Betti-polynomial identity. It also verifies exact Frobenius factorizations in the wild fibers \((n,p)=(3,3),(4,2),(6,2),(6,3),(8,2),(9,3),(10,2),(12,3)\). It reports `VERIFY_OK` when all assertions pass. These checks are corroborating finite instances; the preceding integral normal-form argument proves the result for every \(n\).

## Relationship to prior work
Generic rank-one determinantal varieties, and their linear-code applications, are studied by Beelen–Ghorpade–Hasan (2015). Zaare-Nahandi (2002) studied ideals of minors of generic pluri-circulant matrices and proved Cohen–Macaulayness; Zaare-Nahandi (2004) gave related free resolutions. Zaare-Nahandi's 2008 preprint discusses Fourier diagonalization only when the characteristic does not divide the circulant order and explains the obstruction in the bad-characteristic regime. The present statement isolates the **integral rank-one projective scheme and its nonreduced geometric fibers when the characteristic divides the order**. The Hilbert-series and Betti identities are included for self-contained context, not offered as a novel replacement for existing homological literature.

## Limitations
The result concerns \(2\)-minors of one square circulant block; it does not classify higher rank loci, arbitrary pluri-circulant blocks, arbitrary determinantal ideals, or singularities of their higher rank strata. It gives completed local rings of the finite projective rank-one locus, not a detailed embedded deformation theory. The 2002 and 2004 primary articles were accessible through abstracts and downstream descriptions, but not in verified full text during this literature comparison; an explicit equivalent treatment of the wild fibers in those articles remains a residual originality risk.
