# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof reconstructs the torus function field by the valuation-one Newton polygon at \(t=0\), then uses the complete affine Jacobian equations to identify all singular points. In bad characteristic \(m=p^e r\), Frobenius gives the separable tame Kummer normalization \(v^r=(-1)^n(1+\tau)^n/\tau\). Riemann–Hurwitz computes genus, and the genus–delta formula, transitive \(\mu_r\)-symmetry, and nonzero second derivative give every formal cusp \(U^2-V^{p^e}\). The irreducibility proof prevents an omitted component. The small-field script corroborates, rather than substitutes for, these arguments.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The closest paper on this exact plane family, Aguglia–Korchmáros–Torres (2001), explicitly restricts to characteristics prime to \(m\) in its introduction and §3. Badr–Bars (2015) concerns smooth characteristic-zero Klein curves, while Elkies (1998) studies the distinct arithmetic and reduction geometry of the specific Klein quartic. The later Bisogno et al. work studies supersingularity in the smooth setting. Actual mathematical-index searches using the exponent determinant, Kummer normalization, cusps, and characteristic-seven Klein model did not surface a claim implying the full family theorem. Search absence is not uniqueness proof; unindexed or differently formulated trinomial-curve literature and the known characteristic-seven special case remain risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A standard family used for maximal and highly symmetric curves explicitly excludes prime divisors of its characteristic determinant. The present structural classification fills that motivated bad-characteristic boundary: exact number and local types of all wild cusps, change of geometric genus, and explicit normalization as a Kummer cover. The sharp degeneration \(m\mapsto m/p^{v_p(m)}\) is not a routine change of variables from the previously studied smooth regime.

## Scientific limits
Results pertain to the fixed singular plane equation, not potentially good reductions or stable models. The computational tests are over finite prime fields and do not prove the infinite theorem. Independent review and an exhaustive literature search remain outstanding.
