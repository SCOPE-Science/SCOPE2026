# Wild cusps on two-part coincident-root surfaces in positive characteristic

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of odd characteristic \(p\). Choose integers \(0<a<b\) with \(p\nmid a\) and \(p\mid(b-a)\). Put \(d=a+b\), \(e=v_p(b-a)\geq1\), and \(h=p^e\). Let \(X_{a,b}\subset\mathbb P^d_k\) be the reduced projective image of the map \((L,M)\mapsto L^aM^b\) from \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\).

For every point of the pure-power locus \(\{[L^d]\}\), an affine neighborhood in \(X_{a,b}\) is isomorphic to
\[
\operatorname{Spec}k[v,x,y]/(y^2-x^h),
\]
with the pure-power locus given by \(x=y=0\). Every point outside the pure-power locus is smooth. The parameter surface \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) is the normalization of \(X_{a,b}\); in the stated chart its map is \((v,w)\mapsto(v,w^2,w^h)\).

The transverse normalization defect has length \((h-1)/2\); its conductor in \(k[v,w]\) is \(w^{h-1}k[v,w]\), and its conductor in \(k[v,w^2,w^h]\) is the ideal generated by \(w^{h-1}\) and \(w^h\). The local Jacobian singular scheme is \(\operatorname{Spec}k[v,x]/(x^h)\), of generic transverse length \(h\) along the reduced pure-power curve. These lengths are unbounded with \(v_p(b-a)\).

## Assumptions and scope
All assertions concern the reduced scheme-theoretic image over an algebraically closed field of odd characteristic \(p\), with \(p\nmid a\) and \(p\mid(b-a)\). Since \(b\equiv a\pmod p\) and \(p\ne2\), also \(p\nmid d\). No assertion is made for characteristic two, for \(p\mid a\), or for equal weights. The local model is a complete affine coordinate-ring isomorphism on each monic-polynomial chart, not merely a formal or tangent-cone equivalence. A generic transverse section here means fixing the smooth coordinate \(v\).

## Proof
1. Work in the chart of monic polynomials \(F(T)=(T-\alpha)^a(T-\beta)^b\); both roots are finite here. The coefficient of \(T^{d-1}\) is \(-a\alpha-b\beta\). Because \(d\) is invertible, the weighted center \(v=(a\alpha+b\beta)/d\) is an affine coordinate recovered from the polynomial coefficients. Put \(w=(\alpha-\beta)/d\); then \(\alpha=v+bw\), \(\beta=v-aw\). Writing \(U=T-v\), expand
\[
F(T)=(U-bw)^a(U+aw)^b=\sum_{j=0}^{d}c_jw^jU^{d-j},\quad c_j=[z^j](1-bz)^a(1+az)^b.
\]
The coefficient transformation between powers of \(T\) and powers of \(U\) is polynomial in \(v\), so the coordinate ring of the image is exactly \(k[v,c_2w^2,\ldots,c_dw^d]\). The linear coefficient vanishes by construction.

2. Write \(b-a=p^e c\), where \(p\nmid c\). In characteristic \(p\), the binomial Frobenius identity gives the polynomial identity
\[
(1-bz)^a(1+az)^b=(1-a^2z^2)^a(1+az^{p^e})^c.
\]
Every nonzero coefficient exponent belongs to the numerical semigroup \(\langle2,p^e\rangle\). The quadratic coefficient is \(-a^3\ne0\), and the coefficient of \(z^{p^e}\) is \(ac\ne0\), since \(p^e\) is odd. Hence those two coefficients recover \(w^2\) and \(w^{p^e}\), while every remaining coefficient lies in their subalgebra. It follows, without truncation or genericity qualification, that the image coordinate algebra is
\[
k[v,w^2,w^{p^e}]\cong k[v,x,y]/(y^2-x^{p^e}).
\]
Injectivity of the last presentation follows from the unique decomposition into even and odd powers of \(w\), equivalently the irreducibility of \(y^2-x^{p^e}\) in \(k[x,y]\).

3. The normalization is \(k[v,w]\): the element \(w\) is integral through the monic equation \(w^2=x\), and it belongs to the fraction field since \(w=w^{p^e}/(w^2)^{(p^e-1)/2}\). The normalization is smooth, and the morphism from \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) is proper and finite (its geometric fibers are finite by uniqueness of the factorization into two specified, unequal root multiplicities); it is birational by the displayed equality of fraction fields and therefore is the normalization globally.

4. In the displayed chart the Jacobian criterion gives \(\partial(y^2-x^h)/\partial y=2y\) and \(\partial(y^2-x^h)/\partial x=-hx^{h-1}=0\), because \(h\) is divisible by \(p\). Therefore the singular scheme is defined by \((y,x^h)\), its reduction is \((x,y)\), and the transverse scheme has length \(h\). Since every binary form admits a projective coordinate in which its (at most two) roots are finite, these monic charts cover the image after invertible linear coordinate changes; the reduced singular support is globally exactly \(\{[L^d]\}\).

5. The numerical semigroup \(\langle2,h\rangle\) has exactly the odd gaps \(1,3,\ldots,h-2\), so the finite-length quotient \(k[v,w]/k[v,w^2,w^h]\), considered over \(k[v]\), is free of rank \((h-1)/2\). Every exponent at least \(h-1\) belongs to the semigroup; the least exponent whose addition by all nonnegative integers stays in it is \(h-1\). This gives the stated conductor in both rings. The local multiplicity is two, since the lowest-degree term of \(y^2-x^h\) is \(y^2\).

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` checks the exact modular binomial coefficients of \((1-bz)^a(1+az)^b\) for 413 triples \((p,a,b)\) covering \(p\in\{3,5,7,11,13,17\}\), including the nonzero quadratic and \(p\)-power coefficients and the absence of exponents outside \(\langle2,p^e\rangle\). It also checks six individually displayed examples including \((p,a,b)=(5,2,27)\), for which \(h=25\) and transverse normalization defect has length \(12\). These finite tests corroborate the symbolic identity; the unbounded theorem follows from the algebraic proof above, not from extrapolation.

## Relationship to prior work
Chipalkatti, *On equations defining coincident root loci* (arXiv:math/0110224v1, 19 October 2001), studies these surfaces over \(\mathbb C\). In particular Theorem 4.1 and its local proof determine the conductor quotient supported on the pure-power curve, yielding the ordinary two-generator cusp behavior in characteristic zero. Kurmann, *Some remarks on equations defining coincident root loci* (arXiv:1108.4532v1, 23 August 2011), explicitly assumes characteristic zero and studies the singular support and normalization morphism. Their singular-support framework is acknowledged; neither characteristic-zero theorem implies the positive-characteristic growth of the cusp exponent \(p^{v_p(b-a)}\), the resulting unbounded normalization defect, or the characteristic-dependent Jacobian thickening. The Macaulay2 CoincidentRootLoci documentation summarizes the established singular-support classification without giving these local positive-characteristic exponents.

## Limitations
The theorem assumes \(p\ne2\), \(p\nmid a\), unequal multiplicities, and \(p\mid(b-a)\). It establishes affine coordinate rings along the indicated charts, global singular support, and local invariants; it does not claim a complete homogeneous ideal, minimal resolution, or global cohomological description. The literature overlap check includes the identified principal characteristic-zero sources and targeted search, but an unindexed treatment of the positive-characteristic special case is a residual possibility.
