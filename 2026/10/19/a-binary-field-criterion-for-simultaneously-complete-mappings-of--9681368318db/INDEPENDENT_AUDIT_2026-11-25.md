# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For any permutation carrying each element into its principal ideal, inclusions along a permutation cycle force ideal equality. Apply this separately to theta(x)=x alpha(x) and delta=theta composed with alpha^{-1}; at x and alpha(x) this gives (x)=(x alpha(x))=(alpha(x))=(x^2). Thus x=x^2 b for some b and no nonzero nilpotent can exist. The finite commutative Artinian decomposition is therefore into fields. Principal ideals distinguish supports, so alpha and theta permute each individual one-coordinate nonzero stratum, even if alpha was not assumed coordinatewise. In an odd field the product over this stratum yields -1=1, and in F_2 the lone nonzero point and zero both map to zero under x+alpha(x). Conversely scalar multiplication by a not in {0,1} gives invertible alpha and x+alpha(x), and x alpha(x)=a x^2 is invertible by Frobenius. Both separate witnesses on F_2 x F_4 work, the additive witness after an F_2-linear identification with F_8. The excluded zero ring would be a separate trivial case.
