# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The cut-vertex-forcing lemma follows from disconnecting components of the graph after deletion of each cut vertex. The block-cut tree makes the noncut portions of different cycle blocks independent. In each cycle block, selected vertices must induce one connected arc, and domination limits the omitted interval to at most two adjacent vertices. Deletion-minimality leaves exactly adjacent omitted noncut pairs or an isolated omitted noncut singleton bracketed by cut vertices; with no available omission, all cycle vertices are forced. This establishes the complete size polynomial. One-for-one exchange of local omission pairs corresponds exactly to sliding an omitted pair within a run, while isolated singleton omissions form a clique. Cross-block moves are excluded by symmetric-difference size. Bare cycles and two tiny graphs are handled separately. The standard-library exhaustive verifier reproduces these claims on all 103 unlabeled connected cacti through seven vertices, 180 deterministically generated larger cacti, and two examples with different exchange connectivity. Finite tests are sanity checks, not a proof for unbounded size.

## Originality — PASS, with qualified scope
Golovach–Heggernes–Kratsch (2015), *Enumerating Minimal Connected Dominating Sets in Graphs of Bounded Chordality*, develops systematic minimal CDS enumeration and general class bounds; accessible full-text passages do not state the cactus cut-block run classification, minimal-CDS size polynomial, or Cartesian exchange factorization. Lokshtanov–Mouawad–Panolan–Siebertz (2020) studies TAR reconfiguration with a cardinality cap rather than one-for-one exchange restricted to inclusion-minimal states. Abu-Khzam et al. (2022) provides general enumeration algorithms for bounded degeneracy, not the concrete closed-form local exchange graph. Gupta–Acharya–Jain (2016) treats two-point set domination of cactus graphs, which is a distinct definition. Brimkov–Mikesell–Smith (2017) provides a cactus block algorithm for connected power domination, a weaker observation predicate, not all minimal ordinary CDSs. Full-text searches, published-finding corpus claim/alias searches, and accessible earlier ledger claims did not provide an implication covering this theorem. These negative findings cannot exclude equivalent unindexed work, and general algorithmic enumeration could implement the formula without stating it. Such residual risk is material but not a decisive covered theorem on currently examined evidence.

## Value — PASS
Inclusion-minimal connected dominating sets are a natural family in graph algorithms, and cactus graphs are a canonical block-decomposable test family. The result gives every state, its cardinality distribution, complete component decomposition and exact diameters of the optimality-independent minimal-state exchange space for all cactus sizes. The coexistence of path-like and clique-like exchange factors gives a structural connectivity criterion rather than merely one more scalar domination number. Its scope is natural and infinite; the result is not an arbitrary induced subgraph slice or a known-table recomputation.
