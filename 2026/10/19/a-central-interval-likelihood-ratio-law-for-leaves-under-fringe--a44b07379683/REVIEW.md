# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** Occurrence marking and exact plane-tree leaf contraction give a reversible bijection. The Narayana law and its first two moments give the exact Palm mass and covariance. The adjacent-likelihood-ratio formula is an explicit product of two strictly decreasing positive sequences, handling all admissible sizes and small-support boundaries. Exact enumeration over rooted ordered trees and rational arithmetic checks corroborate, but do not establish, the general proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The generic marked-fringe contraction, Narayana leaf counts, and large-size covariance are already covered by the 1984 pattern-enumeration report, classical leaf enumeration, Janson (2013), and the 2025 fixed-degree fringe-tree article. They are credited, not claimed new. The assessed additional claim is the universal **strict finite-sample likelihood-ratio log-concavity**, with its interval-superlevel testing consequence and finite sign boundary; the inspected results did not state this inequality. The plausibly relevant 2009 Dershowitz–Zaks paper could be read only at abstract level, so an implicit equivalent theorem remains possible. Search and inspection cannot certify novelty independently.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The result solves the distributional-ordering problem for leaf counts under a natural occurrence-weighted alternative, showing an unexpected middle-of-distribution preference and a shape-invariant exact sign classification. It is an all-size structural inequality, not simply a small covariance computation.

**Scientific limitations.** The biased model is occurrence-weighted rather than conditioning on at least one occurrence. Results concern ordered rooted plane trees; no extension to arbitrary Galton–Watson weights is claimed. The 2009 full text and potentially implicit older coverage remain outstanding originality risks.
