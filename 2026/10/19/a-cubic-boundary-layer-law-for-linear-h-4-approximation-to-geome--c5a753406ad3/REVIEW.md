# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The global complex optimizer exists and is unique because the finite-dimensional span of the two polynomial multiples is closed in strictly convex \(H^p\). Conjugation and reversal symmetry force the real one-parameter form, not merely a real-constrained local extremum. The residual has a reversible coefficient vector, so the exact fourth-moment polynomial follows from the complete finite autocorrelation sum. Strict convexity and opposite derivative signs at \(-1\) and \(0\) locate the unique minimizer and show the zero is outside the closed unit disk. Uniform expansions of both the objective and its derivative, and strict positivity of \(Q''\), prove the stated sharp \(m^{-1}\) constants. The companion checker reproduces the algebraic quartic by an independent direct sum and boundary quadrature, but numerical runs are not relied on for universal quantifiers.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The first nonlinear even Hardy norm already loses the Hilbert-space orthogonal-projection formula; the geometric sums are canonical moving-average/Dirichlet-kernel input generators, with singular unit-circle zero geometry. Here reciprocal invariance reduces the *complex* nonlinear problem to one real parameter for every Hardy exponent, while the fourth-norm case yields exact minimizers for every length and identifies the first nontrivial boundary-layer coefficient as the filter length tends to infinity. The non-Hilbert \(H^4\) zero displacement \(2c/m\) differs from the \(H^2\) coefficient \(1/m\), so the rate has intrinsic norm-sensitive content beyond a routine scalar minimum. Scope is deliberately narrow: no conclusions on degree two or higher, variable exponent asymptotics, or general stability.
