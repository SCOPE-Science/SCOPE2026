# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The derivation identifies all subalgebras as internal subspaces or external affine lifts of invariant subspaces of \(T\). The two-eigenspace decomposition makes the classification exhaustive in every finite characteristic. The two disjoint nonpermutability contributions are counted with their finite-field fiber multiplicities, and their sum equals the claimed polynomial. The pairwise nonisomorphism assertion follows from the characteristic derived ideal and the distinguished repeated eigenvalue. Direct enumeration over \(\mathbb F_3\), \(\mathbb F_4\), and \(\mathbb F_5\) checks all admissible parameters. The symbolic argument, rather than any bounded computation, proves the theorem.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-access risk
The source by Muhie–Otera–Russo (arXiv:2609.19086v1, 2026-09-16) introduces the invariant and emphasizes Heisenberg examples. Its full text was not accessible for direct inspection; this is explicitly an unresolved historical comparison risk. The inspected full public three-dimensional classification applies only in dimension three. The inspected four-dimensional central-Heisenberg result concerns nilpotent algebras with nonzero center and gives a different rational function; it cannot imply a center-free nonnilpotent family without an independent theorem. Searches for spectral aliases and the exact polynomial found no dominating published statement. Failure to locate a result is not a proof of historical uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The invariant has just been motivated in finite-field Lie-subalgebra probability. This gives a complete exact expression for a natural first four-dimensional nonnilpotent split family, including all \(q-2\) pairwise nonisomorphic parameters at each admissible field order. Independence from the distinct eigenvalue and the nonzero limit \(1/4\) show a substantive structural effect not explained by the three-dimensional formulas. It is not a mere specialization of the existing Heisenberg count.

## Remaining limits
The result is restricted to the stated two-eigenspace multiplicity pattern. The primary paper's full text could not be retrieved, so older or same-paper equivalent formulations cannot be categorically excluded. There is no independent validation.
