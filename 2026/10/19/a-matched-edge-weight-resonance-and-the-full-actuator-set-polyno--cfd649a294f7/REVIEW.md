# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The block Laplacian is explicitly diagonalized into constant, antisymmetric-constant, symmetric zero-sum and antisymmetric zero-sum modules, with all eigenvalue coincidences exhaustively located at the one allowed resonance. The symmetric PBH theorem converts each eigenspace into an exact index-support condition, and at resonance treats the *merged* eigenspace instead of checking the former summands separately. The two criteria yield the whole generating polynomial by an exhaustive pair/side case division. Bernoulli formulas and the two Poisson limits are deduced from the same exact counts. The standalone checker passed 2,167 finite assertions; it is corroboration, not proof for arbitrary real weights or all orders.

## Originality — PASS (self-review, residual search risk)
The 2011 path/cycle study and the 2011 distance-regular study use standard spectral and partition methods, but inspected sections do not state this two-weight bipartite theorem. The 2011/2014 zero-forcing works distinguish robust graph-class controllability from the fixed-weight Laplacian considered here. Those works imply neither the exact nonresonant pair-selection rule nor its generation polynomial. The familiar homogeneous \(K_{n,n}\) minimum at \(b=a\) and the general PBH and zero-forcing tools are prior facts; they are not the novel part. A crown-graph edge-metric basis enumeration shares the same combinatorial coefficient \(n2^{n-1}\) when matched edges are absent, but the invariant and positive-weight claims differ. Specialized unindexed treatments of balanced bipartite systems are a residual risk; no database search establishes absolute novelty.

## Value — PASS
Matched-edge heterogeneity is a natural way of changing intercommunity coupling while preserving the graph support. The full all-actuator-set classification is nontrivial design information beyond a single optimal placement. The minimum count has an arbitrarily sharp, factor-two resonance on the *same* positive support graph, and the exact reliability polynomial translates this into a change of independent-node omission scale from \(n^{-1/2}\) to \(n^{-1}\). These are interpretable structural boundaries rather than an arbitrary finite parameter lookup.
