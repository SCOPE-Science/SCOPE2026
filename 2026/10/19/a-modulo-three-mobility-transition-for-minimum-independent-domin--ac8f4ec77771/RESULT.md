# A modulo-three mobility transition for minimum independent domination in complementary cycle prisms

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge5\), consider the complementary prism \(Q_n=C_n\overline{C_n}\). Its minimum independent-dominating sets have two canonical families and exhibit a sharp exchange transition:
\[
i(Q_n)=\lfloor n/3\rfloor+2,\qquad
N_n=\begin{cases}nk^2,&n=3k,\\n(2k+1),&n=3k+1,\\2n,&n=3k+2,\end{cases}
\]
where \(N_n\) is the number of optimal sets, and \(k\) is the integer quotient. Under **token sliding** (replace one selected vertex by an adjacent unselected one) the graph of all optimal sets is connected exactly when \(n\not\equiv2\pmod3\). If \(n\equiv2\pmod3\), **even token jumping** (a replacement without requiring adjacency) has no edges. Thus the stronger token-jumping reconfiguration graph is connected in the other two residue classes and is edgeless in the third.

## Assumptions and scope
Write vertices of the cycle as \(a_j\), and their corresponding complementary-layer vertices as \(b_j\), for \(j\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). The \(a\)-layer has edges \(a_ja_{j\pm1}\), the \(b\)-layer has edges \(b_jb_k\) when \(k\notin\{j,j\pm1\}\), and the layers are joined only by \(a_jb_j\). An *independent dominating set* is an independent set meeting every closed vertex neighborhood. The two exchange graphs have as vertices **all minimum-cardinality** independent dominating sets; an edge represents exactly one removal and one insertion preserving minimum independent domination at both endpoints. No intermediate nonoptimal states are permitted.

For a path on \(m\ge0\) vertices, denote its family of minimum independent dominating sets by \(\mathcal P_m\), with \(\mathcal P_0=\{\varnothing\}\). The following two canonical families consist of sets of distinct original/complementary vertices, with indices modulo \(n\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F^1_j&=\bigl\{\{b_j,a_{j-1},a_{j+1}\}\cup\{a_{j+3+t}:t\in I\}:I\in\mathcal P_{n-5}\bigr\},\\
\mathcal F^2_j&=\bigl\{\{b_j,b_{j+1}\}\cup\{a_{j+2+t}:t\in I\}:I\in\mathcal P_{n-2}\bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
In the first formula, path positions \(t\) range from \(0\) to \(n-6\); in the second from \(0\) to \(n-3\). The two families, over all \(j\), are pairwise disjoint and partition the minimum sets.

## Proof
**Complementary-layer restriction and completeness.** If an independent set has no \(b\)-vertex, domination of every \(b_j\) forces every \(a_j\) to be selected, which is impossible for a cycle. The selected \(b\)-indices must form a clique in \(C_n\), so there are exactly one or two, and two must be consecutive. With precisely \(b_j\), the complementary vertices \(b_{j-1}\) and \(b_{j+1}\) are not adjacent to it, and can be dominated only by their matched \(a\)-vertices. Hence \(a_{j-1},a_{j+1}\) are forced. These three vertices dominate original vertices at the five consecutive indices \(j-2,\ldots,j+2\), leaving exactly the induced path on \(j+3,\ldots,j+n-3\) to be independently dominated. Since no selected vertex outside that path can dominate its vertices, the path selection must be an independent dominating set. Conversely, any such selection yields an independent dominating set of \(Q_n\). With precisely \(b_j,b_{j+1}\), the entire complementary layer is dominated and the matched original indices \(j,j+1\) are covered; the remaining original layer is exactly an induced path on \(j+2,\ldots,j+n-1\), so the second family is necessary and sufficient. Distinct complementary-layer selections make these classes disjoint.

**Cardinality and counting.** For \(m\ge0\), minimum independent domination of \(P_m\) has size \(r=\lceil m/3\rceil\): one selected path vertex covers at most three consecutive vertices, and selecting vertices at offsets three apart attains the bound. Its minimum-set count \(A(m)\) is
\[
A(3r)=1,\qquad A(3r+1)=\binom{r+2}{2},\qquad A(3r+2)=r+2.
\]
For completeness, sort selected positions \(p_1<\cdots<p_s\). Independent domination is equivalent to first offset \(p_1-1\in\{0,1\}\), final offset \(m-p_s\in\{0,1\}\), and gaps \(p_{h+1}-p_h\in\{2,3\}\). For minimum \(s=\lceil m/3\rceil\), count the solutions to \(m=3s-(e_L+e_R+h)\), where \(e_L,e_R\in\{0,1\}\) describe uncovered end slack and \(h\) is the number of gaps shortened from three to two. The distributions give precisely the displayed coefficients (for \(m=0\), \(A(0)=1\)). The two canonical families therefore each have size \(2+\lceil(n-5)/3\rceil=2+\lceil(n-2)/3\rceil=\lfloor n/3\rfloor+2\), and together number \(n(A(n-5)+A(n-2))\), which simplifies to the three formulas for \(N_n\).

**Connectivity inside a fixed complementary-layer fibre.** Minimum independent-dominating sets on \(P_m\) are connected by adjacent-vertex slides along the path. For sorted positions put \(y_h=p_h-2(h-1)\), obtaining weakly increasing integral sequences whose consecutive gaps are zero or one, with two-value endpoint constraints. The pointwise minimum of two feasible sequences remains feasible. Given feasible \(w\le y\) coordinatewise with \(w\ne y\), decrement the *rightmost differing coordinate* of \(y\) that is at the left edge of its constant block; equivalently, take the leftmost coordinate in the last constant block containing a difference. Its preceding coordinate, when present, is strictly smaller, and its following coordinate, if present, is at most one larger. The decrement preserves monotonicity and the gap bound; the endpoint constraints are maintained because a decrement at the final coordinate can only occur when the target endpoint allows it. Repeating reaches \(w\), and joining two sequences via their pointwise minimum proves connectedness. Each such slide of path vertices is also a legal slide in \(Q_n\).

**Global connectivity for \(n=3k\) or \(3k+1\).** Fix any \(j\). For \(n=3k\), take cycle indices \(I_j=\{j+3,j+6,\ldots,j+3k-3,j+3k-1\}\); for \(n=3k+1\), take \(I_j=\{j+3,j+6,\ldots,j+3k\}\), interpreted modulo \(n\). Define \(A_j=\{b_j,a_{j+1}\}\cup\{a_i:i\in I_j\}\), \(B_j=\{b_j,b_{j+1}\}\cup\{a_i:i\in I_j\}\), and \(C_j=\{b_{j+1},b_{j+2}\}\cup\{a_i:i\in I_j\}\). Directly checking consecutive gaps shows that these are minimum independent-dominating sets in, respectively, \(\mathcal F^1_j\), \(\mathcal F^2_j\), and \(\mathcal F^2_{j+1}\). The transition \(A_j\to B_j\) slides \(a_{j+1}\) along its matching edge to \(b_{j+1}\). The transition \(B_j\to C_j\) slides \(b_j\) to \(b_{j+2}\), an edge of the complement of \(C_n\) since \(j\) and \(j+2\) are nonadjacent on \(C_n\) for \(n\ge5\). Hence every type-one fibre attaches to a type-two fibre, and all successive type-two fibres form one cycle of inter-fibre links. Since each fibre is connected by the preceding lemma, the full token-sliding graph is connected.

**Complete freezing for \(n=3k+2\).** Both residual paths have lengths divisible by three, so each fibre contains just one optimal set. In the type-one configuration, the cycle-layer indices are \(j-1,j+1,j+4,j+7,\ldots,j+3k-2\), and among their cyclic successive gaps exactly one has length two (between \(j-1\) and \(j+1\)), all others length three. Thus these original-layer vertices determine \(j\) uniquely. In type two, the original indices are \(j+3,j+6,\ldots,j+3k\), with exactly one cyclic gap of length five, all others length three (for \(k=1\) its sole vertex still determines \(j\)). So there is no one-vertex replacement preserving the same type and same number of complementary-layer vertices: a replacement in the original layer cannot stay in the same singleton fibre, while a replacement in the complementary layer would leave an original-layer fingerprint that determines the same fibre. A cross-type replacement from one to two complementary vertices would have to add \(b_{j+1}\) while removing \(a_{j+1}\), or add \(b_{j-1}\) while removing \(a_{j-1}\); the remaining original-layer set then includes, respectively, \(a_{j-1}\) or \(a_{j+1}\), which is forbidden by the target type-two pattern. The reverse operation is equally impossible. Hence token jumping has no edges, and token sliding, its subgraph, has none. This concludes the proof.

## Verification
Two separate standalone programs accompany this result. `verify_bruteforce.cpp` independently enumerates all vertex subsets for each \(5\le n\le12\), checks both predicates directly, enumerates every minimum set, and forms token-slide and token-jump exchange graphs. `verify_structural.py` uses the canonical path families without reusing the brute-force predicate, builds both exchange graphs for \(5\le n\le21\), and independently verifies the same cardinality, count, and connectivity dichotomy. The two full minimum-set certificates for \(5\le n\le12\) are byte-for-byte identical; each lists all sets as canonical integer bitmasks, exchange edge totals, and component-size multisets. These finite checks confirm the implementations and do not replace the infinite proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Góngora, Haynes, and Jum (2013) established foundational independent-domination bounds and exact results for several other complementary-prism families, including paths, complete bipartite graphs and subdivided stars. Their full author-uploaded text was inspected, including statements of Theorems 4, 5, 8 and 9, the introduction and the classes treated; it does not state this full cycle-prism optimal-set count or its reconfiguration transition. In particular, their general domination bounds do not imply the exchange-graph connectivity assertion. The 2015 work on identifying codes in complementary prisms of cycles concerns a different (uniqueness-of-neighborhood-signatures) covering requirement and does not imply independent-domination exchange connectivity. Brewster, Mynhardt and Teshima's reconfiguration framework establishes the relevant independent-domination graph operation in general, not this family-specific structure. A 2018 work titled *Maximal independent sets in complementary prism graphs* is related to the same maximal-independent-set objects; available bibliographic material does not establish whether it covers the precise optimum count, and its full text was not obtained. Our claim of distinct contribution therefore principally concerns the sharp modulo-three token-slide/token-jump dichotomy and constructive links. Prior published bounds and terminology are credited, rather than treated as new.

## Limitations
The result assumes simple finite cycles of length at least five, exactly one removed and inserted vertex per move, and minimum-set states only; it does not address arbitrary graph complementary prisms, nonminimum maximal independent sets, multi-vertex operations, or the diameters of connected reconfiguration graphs. The initial 2009 thesis on independent domination in complementary prisms is known bibliographically, but its original public-release day and readable full text were not established. A later directly relevant 2018 maximal-independent-set publication lacked accessible full text; possible overlap of its counting statements remains an explicit priority risk, as do unindexed exact exchange results. No claim of independent external audit, priority certification or universal uniqueness is made.
