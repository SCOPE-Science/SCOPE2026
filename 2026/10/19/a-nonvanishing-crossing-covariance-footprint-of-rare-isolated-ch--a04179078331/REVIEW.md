# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** For each designated isolated chord, separate internal pairings of the two complementary arcs give the exact event probability and two independent uniform matchings. The three equiprobable matchings of four endpoints give a one-third crossing probability, leading to the exact conditional crossing deficit. The multiplicity symmetry proves the full covariance formulas for every \(n\ge2\). Geometric tail control of the double-factorial weights yields the rare-event asymptotics. Exhaustive independent rational enumeration checks all \(146598\) matchings through \(n=7\); such tests are not a substitute for the general argument.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Flajolet–Noy (2000) give marginal component and crossing moments; Acan (2015) proves marginal random-chord degree and monolith results; Courtiel–Yeats–Zeilberger (2016/2019) gives a bivariate crossing and top-chord refinement for indecomposable and connected diagrams; these top chords are not isolated vertices. Paguyo (2021/2023 version) gives marginal normal and Poisson approximation bounds. The relevant examined sections do not present the exact mixed crossing–isolated-chord covariance or identify the surviving covariance of nonsimple isolated chords. Other related mathematical identities for endpoint-span/crossing covariance and long-chord point processes do not cover zero-crossing chords. A latent multivariate-generating-function identity could imply the same result, so absolute originality is not established.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** This is a natural mixed second-order characteristic of an intersection graph at the junction of a macroscopic crossing count and a Poisson-scale isolation statistic. More importantly, it exposes a singular rare-event effect: a class whose expected count is only \(\Theta(n^{-1})\) retains a nonzero limiting covariance \(-1/3\) with crossings. Consequently, replacing isolated chords with simple chords loses a precise constant-order term in finite-size dependence calculations, even though the two counts agree with probability approaching one. The exact formula and sharp asymptotic explain why a high-probability approximation of counts does not control mixed moments with a growing observable.

**Limitations and closest work.** The result is restricted to uniform random matchings. Primary papers inspected were Flajolet and Noy (2000), Acan (arXiv:1501.01489), Courtiel–Yeats–Zeilberger (arXiv:1611.04611), and Paguyo (arXiv:2104.01134). The papers' principal statements are marginal, and stronger unpublished or differently indexed mixed-enumeration results may exist. No independent audit has been performed.
