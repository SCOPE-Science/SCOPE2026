# Scientific review: a prime-field zero-mode transition in three-tap affine convolution

## Correctness: PASS
The claim concerns exactly the support \(\{(1,0),(1,b),(a,c)\}\), with primitive \(a\) and nonzero \(b\), on \(\operatorname{AGL}(1,q)\) for \(q=p^r\ge3\). The Fourier decomposition is complete: \(q-1\) linear representations and one degree-\(q-1\) irreducible account for the group order in the sum-of-squares identity. The latter block is the sum of one cyclic permutation matrix and one additive-character diagonal. Its determinant has only the diagonal and full-cycle terms. Counting additive characters proves the cyclotomic product, including the characteristic-two case; scalar blocks are never zero. In the singular odd-prime-field case, all diagonal entries remain nonzero, making the nonlinear kernel exactly one-dimensional. Regular multiplicity establishes nullity \(q-1\). Finite exact checks confirm the computed formula for seven fields, but do not substitute for this proof.

The closest mathematical records for three-tap singularity concern generalized dihedral and dicyclic groups, not full affine multiplier complements. A record on affine-group Cayley spectra addresses reconstruction from multiplicities rather than this singular-support filter. ## Mathematical value: PASS
This is an exact infinite-family spectral singularity transition for a natural canonical filter in finite nonabelian harmonic analysis. The same sparse support is singular for every odd prime field but invertible over every proper extension, and the explicit nonlinear nullity is maximal at one dimension per regular copy. Infinitely many examples have group orders indivisible by three, so the phenomenon is not reducible to subgroup-of-order-three coset cancellation. The exact integer determinant also distinguishes every extension degree. The scope is a canonical support family rather than a full census of all three-tap supports, a material limitation but not a failure of the stated structural result's value.
