# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

For every integer \(n\ge3\), the theorem concerns symmetric weighted-cycle Laplacians under an exact, strictly positive monetary budget. The centered affine-coordinate Rayleigh quotient bounds every feasible spectral gap by \([B-(t-(n-1)^2)c]/S_n\). The weighted path eigenvalues are exactly \(k(k+1)/2\), proved through invariant polynomial flags, so the path optimum is both attained and rigid. Simplicity of the path Fiedler eigenvalue makes the price-below-boundary derivative strictly positive. At equality, every putative optimizer must retain the affine vector as an eigenvector, forcing the one-parameter conductance formula; the Brownian-bridge covariance identity converts the remaining spectral condition to an exact auxiliary complete-graph Laplacian threshold. The proof controls all dimensions and all boundary cases. The standalone exact-rational checker reports 200 assertion groups; it is supporting evidence, not a substitute for the theorem.

## Originality — PASS, with qualifications

Sun, Boyd, Xiao and Diaconis (SIAM Review 2006, DOI 10.1137/S0036144504443821, Result 5 and Section 5.3) already give the parabolic weighted-path optimum and formulate cycle optimization for arbitrary edge prices. Their general semidefinite and geometric principles can recover some features of the result; no novelty is asserted for the framework, path allocation, Rayleigh upper bound, or optimizer nonuniqueness in general. The claimed distinction is the combined explicit exceptional-price threshold \((n-1)^2\) and the **entire transition-price optimizer face**, including its sharp Brownian-bridge complete-graph spectral endpoint. Checked published records on cycle Fourier invariants and a separate two-price even-cycle diffusion theorem concern different parameters and do not imply this edge-space cutoff. The full text of Fiedler's 1990 tree antecedent was unavailable; specialization or a stronger older cycle-price theorem remains a residual literature risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

An optional link can be rigorously classified as mandatory, forbidden, or part of a continuum of rate-optimal allocations depending on its price. At the knife-edge price the complete optimizer geometry is measured by a different natural Laplacian—a Brownian-bridge covariance complete graph—and the exact spectral endpoint detects a multiplicity increase. This is a motivated, full critical-boundary classification for diffusion-rate network design; it is not a purely numerical refinement or an arbitrary isolated coefficient. No complete characterization of the cheaper-link phase is claimed.

## Limitations

There is no formula for subcritical optimal conductances or an asymptotic limit for the auxiliary cutoff. Originality remains conditional on literature visibility; independent mathematical validation is outstanding.
