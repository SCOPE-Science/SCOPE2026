# A quadratic-cone compactification of abelian planes in a Takiff Lie algebra

## Finding

Let \(k\) be any field with \(\operatorname{char}k\ne2\). Write \(\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_2(k)\), \(D=k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\), and \(\mathfrak t=\mathfrak g\otimes_kD=\mathfrak g\oplus\epsilon\mathfrak g\). Define \(X\) as the **closed scheme** of two-dimensional Lie-abelian linear subspaces of \(\mathfrak t\): inside \(\operatorname{Gr}(2,\mathfrak t)\) it is the zero scheme of the bracket homomorphism \(\bigwedge^2\mathcal S\to\mathfrak t\otimes\mathcal O\) for the tautological bundle \(\mathcal S\).

**Theorem.** \(X\) is a geometrically integral, projective, normal fourfold. Let \(\pi:\mathfrak t\to\mathfrak g\) be projection. The rank-one projection locus \(X^\circ\) is isomorphic, as a scheme, to \(\operatorname{Tot}(T\mathbb P(\mathfrak g))\cong\operatorname{Tot}(T\mathbb P^2)\). The remaining scheme is \(B=\operatorname{Gr}(2,\epsilon\mathfrak g)\cong\mathbb P^2\), which is the exact scheme-theoretic singular locus. At every point of \(B\), a Grassmannian affine chart identifies \(X\) with the product of an affine plane and the quadratic cone \(\operatorname{Spec}k[u,v,w]/(u^2+vw)\). The morphism
\[
\rho:\mathbb P_{\mathbb P^2}(\mathcal O\oplus T\mathbb P^2)\longrightarrow X
\]
constructed in the proof is a projective resolution, with exceptional divisor \(\mathbb P(T\mathbb P^2)\) and fibers \(\mathbb P^1\) over \(B\). For every odd prime power \(q\) and integer \(m\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{\#X(\mathbb F_{q^m})=(q^{2m}+q^m+1)(q^{2m}+1).}
\]
In particular, its Hasse–Weil zeta function is
\[
Z(X/\mathbb F_q,T)=\frac1{(1-T)(1-qT)(1-q^2T)^2(1-q^3T)(1-q^4T)}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The characteristic restriction is necessary for the map \([\ ,\ ]:\bigwedge^2\mathfrak{sl}_2\to\mathfrak{sl}_2\) to be a vector-space isomorphism; in characteristic \(2\), the standard simple structure degenerates. The scheme is defined by **all** Lie brackets of rank-two subspaces, not by a set-theoretic or reduced replacement. The affine plane appearing in each boundary chart has base coordinates along \(B\). Assertions about normality and singular loci remain valid after arbitrary extensions of the ground field. All counts refer to rational two-dimensional subspaces, not ordered generating pairs.

## Proof

**Step 1: convert the bracket to exterior algebra.** Choose the basis \(e,h,f\) of \(\mathfrak g\). The brackets \([e,f]=h\), \([h,e]=2e\), \([h,f]=-2f\) show that \(\beta:\bigwedge^2\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g\) is invertible when \(2\ne0\). For vectors \(a+\epsilon b\) and \(c+\epsilon d\), the vanishing of their bracket is therefore equivalent, **scheme-theoretically over every \(k\)-algebra**, to
\[
a\wedge c=0,\qquad a\wedge d+b\wedge c=0.
\]
This equivalence is a linear change of equations, without any division by tuple coordinates.

**Step 2: classify the open locus.** The projected rank-two possibility is excluded by \(a\wedge c=0\). For a two-plane with projected rank one, write the projected line as \(\ell=ka\subset\mathfrak g\); choose a generator \(a+\epsilon b\) above \(a\) and a vector \(\epsilon c\) spanning its kernel. The remaining equation says \(a\wedge c=0\), hence \(kc=\ell\) even at the scheme level in rank-one charts (after selecting a unit coordinate of \(a\)). Thus
\[\nW=\operatorname{span}\{a+\epsilon b,\epsilon a\},\qquad [b]\in\mathfrak g/\ell.
\]
Changing the lift of \(b\) by a multiple of \(a\) leaves \(W\) unchanged. The corresponding vector-bundle fiber is \(\operatorname{Hom}(\ell,\mathfrak g/\ell)\), precisely the tangent bundle of \(\mathbb P(\mathfrak g)\). Consequently \(X^\circ\cong\operatorname{Tot}(T\mathbb P^2)\), smooth, irreducible and four-dimensional.

**Step 3: analyze the complementary boundary as a scheme.** The rank-zero projection locus consists of arbitrary two-planes in the square-zero ideal \(\epsilon\mathfrak g\), giving \(B\cong\operatorname{Gr}(2,3)\cong\mathbb P^2\). To compute the full local scheme near any boundary point, extend a basis \(e_1,e_2\) of its underlying two-plane to \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) of \(\mathfrak g\). The standard Grassmannian chart has basis vectors
\[
w_1=(a_1,a_2,a_3)+\epsilon(1,0,b_1),\qquad
w_2=(c_1,c_2,c_3)+\epsilon(0,1,b_2).
\]
Applying Step 1, the three \(\epsilon\)-coefficient equations solve uniquely for
\[
c_2=-a_1,\qquad a_3=a_2b_2+b_1a_1,\qquad c_3=b_1c_1-a_1b_2.
\]
Substitution into \(a\wedge c=0\) leaves three equations
\[
b_1(a_1^2+a_2c_1)=0,\quad b_2(a_1^2+a_2c_1)=0,\quad -(a_1^2+a_2c_1)=0.
\]
Thus the **entire** ideal is generated by \(Q=a_1^2+a_2c_1\), proving an isomorphism of the chart to \(\mathbb A^2\times\operatorname{Spec}k[a_1,a_2,c_1]/(Q)\). This works for every boundary point because the basis was arbitrary and \(\beta\) is invertible.

**Step 4: geometric structure.** The polynomial \(Q\) is geometrically irreducible in odd characteristic. Its gradient is \((2a_1,c_1,a_2)\), so the singular locus is precisely \(a_1=a_2=c_1=0\), with free coordinates \(b_1,b_2\), agreeing scheme-theoretically with \(B\). This chart is reduced, an integral hypersurface, Cohen–Macaulay and regular in codimension one; it is normal by Serre's criterion. The open locus from Step 2 is smooth. Moreover every boundary plane \(\epsilon\operatorname{span}(a,b)\) is a specialization of \(\operatorname{span}(t a+\epsilon b,\epsilon a)\) as \(t\to0\). Hence no additional component is supported on \(B\), and \(X\) is geometrically integral and normal with singular locus exactly \(B\).

**Step 5: resolution.** Over \(\mathbb P(\mathfrak g)\) consider the smooth projective bundle \(Y=\mathbb P(\mathcal O\oplus T\mathbb P(\mathfrak g))\). A point consists of a line \(\ell=ka\) and a nonzero projective pair \([\tau,F]\), where \(F:\ell\to\mathfrak g/\ell\) is linear. Choose a lift \(b\) of \(F(a)\) and send it to
\[
\rho(\ell,[\tau,F])=\operatorname{span}\{\tau a+\epsilon b,\epsilon a\}.
\]
Rescaling \(a\), rescaling \((\tau,F)\), or changing the lift \(b\) does not affect the subspace. Thus \(\rho\) is a regular morphism to \(X\). On \(\tau\ne0\), normalize \(\tau=1\) and recover the inverse from Step 2. When \(\tau=0\), one has \(F\ne0\), and the image is \(\epsilon\operatorname{span}(a,b)\). Such pairs comprise \(\mathbb P(T\mathbb P^2)\); over a fixed two-plane \(\operatorname{span}(a,b)\), the choices are exactly lines \(\ell\) in that plane, a \(\mathbb P^1\). The source is smooth, the map is projective and birational, and it is an isomorphism away from \(B\), as claimed.

**Step 6: arithmetic.** A rational point of \(X^\circ\) lies over a unique \(\mathbb F_Q\)-line \(\ell\subset\mathfrak g\), with fiber \(\operatorname{Hom}(\ell,\mathfrak g/\ell)\cong\mathbb A^2\) over \(\mathbb F_Q\). The boundary contributes \(\#\operatorname{Gr}(2,3)(\mathbb F_Q)=Q^2+Q+1\). Therefore
\[
\#X(\mathbb F_Q)=(Q^2+Q+1)Q^2+(Q^2+Q+1)=(Q^2+Q+1)(Q^2+1).
\]
Putting \(Q=q^m\) yields the stated point counts. Expanding the polynomial and applying the defining exponential series gives the zeta function.

## Verification

The accompanying `check_takiff_ab2.py` uses exact symbolic elimination (SymPy) to reproduce the complete local equations, and direct finite-field row-reduced-matrix enumeration of all \(2\)-planes in a \(6\)-space. Over \(\mathbb F_3\) it examines \(11,011\) planes and obtains \(130=13(9+1)\) abelian planes, with \(117\) open and \(13\) boundary. Over \(\mathbb F_5\) it examines \(508,431\) planes and obtains \(806=31(25+1)\), with \(775\) open and \(31\) boundary. The script prints `VERIFY_OK`. These are finite checks of the formulas, not substitutes for the arguments over arbitrary fields.

## Relationship to prior work

Takiff algebras and their adjoint invariant theory were systematically studied well before this calculation. In particular, Panyushev's 2005 work on semidirect products addresses generic stabilizers, invariant polynomial rings, quotient morphisms, null cones and their irreducibility; its Takiff results include adjoint representation theorems and a separate investigation of singularities of **null cones**. The 2007 treatment by Panyushev studies periodic automorphisms of generalized Takiff algebras, their contractions, and invariant rings. Neither inspected article states a description of the scheme of **all abelian two-dimensional Lie subalgebras** inside a Grassmannian, nor its local quadratic-cone chart, exceptional divisor or rational-point formula. In particular, a null cone for the adjoint quotient is not the abelian-plane Grassmannian defined here. Standard facts on the tangent bundle of projective space and on quadratic cone singularities are used without novelty claims. Literature overlap may persist in work not located or accessible in these searches.

## Limitations

No result in characteristic \(2\) is asserted. The theorem concerns two-dimensional abelian Lie subalgebras only, not maximal subalgebras of every dimension, commuting-tuples of arbitrary arity, or infinitesimal deformation functors beyond this defining Grassmannian scheme. The resolution need not be canonical or minimal. The literature search is not an exhaustive novelty certificate, and no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
