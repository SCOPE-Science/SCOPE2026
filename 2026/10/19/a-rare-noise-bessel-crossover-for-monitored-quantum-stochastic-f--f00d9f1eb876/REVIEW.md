# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim uses the explicit state/position update in the originating full text, Appendix E, equations (67)–(75). The full scalar first-return and survival generating functions are reconstructed by the renewal identity, without treating their already known derivation as a new result. At zero noise the return is at exactly two steps. For each fixed positive noise, square-root singularity transfer in the even-time variable proves the survival and return-density constants and divergence of the mean. For joint \(p\to0\), \(2np\to t\), the coefficient identity has a uniform absolutely summable majorant on every bounded time interval; its limit is identified with the Bessel survival by Laplace transforms. The conditioning normalization is \(S_2=p-p^2/2\) and the limiting tail is a proper probability law. Potential proof risk: the source formulas depend specifically on the monitored convention and exchanging coin; neither is extended silently. The 215 finite checks independently compare a finite-state Markov evolution and Fourier-resolvent evaluation, and cannot certify the infinite limits by themselves.

## Originality — PASS, subject to independent assessment

The closest and strongest source is Štefaňák et al., arXiv:2501.08674, Appendix E, which **already contains the complete first-return generating matrix** at the same coin and all-\(p\) recurrence. It determines the finite-time distribution implicitly. The present added content is an explicit, rigorously uniform two-parameter singular limit, a conditional Bessel survival law on the noise-dependent time scale, and the positive-noise infinite-mean transition. These statements are not in its displayed results, figures, or Appendix E; the paper itself notes nonuniform behavior near zero noise. The proof makes the extra joint-asymptotic argument, not a new generating-function discovery. Konno (2009) treats a genuine persistent-direction model and reports Bessel position-scaling limits as well as absorption probabilities, but its transition rule ties internal direction to each spatial step, unlike the classical-shift component here; it does not state the claimed conditional monitored return profile. Kiumi–Konno–Tamura treat unmonitored position-return probabilities. Yoshino et al. consider a different interpolation and unmonitored position weak limits, including a distinct \(p\sim1/n\) regime. Indexed mathematical findings concerning quantum-walk Riccati burn-in, fully connected first-detection scheduling, and elephant cover times are different claims. The important residual originality risk is implicit implication by general persistent random walk/telegraph first-passage analyses or by standard two-parameter coefficient transfer applied directly to the existing exact generating function. No absence of search results proves uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

The first-return mean changes from exactly two to infinity for arbitrarily small classical noise, while every process still returns almost surely. The nontrivial conditional law quantifies what happens to the probability-order-\(p\) trajectories that escape the immediate return, their inverse-noise delay, and a nonexponential heavy-tailed crossover. This resolves a physically motivated singular finite-time-versus-infinite-time distinction specifically emphasized in the source, and supplies a concrete diagnostic for rare trajectories in monitored stochastic walks. Value does not depend on relabeling the source's recurrence result.

## Scope and risks

The coin is fixed at \(\theta=\pi/2\), measurements occur after each step, and the initial position is zero. The conditional rare-noise limit is not a claim about ordinary unmonitored positions, arbitrary coins, or robustness to imperfections. The initial generating function is fully acknowledged prior mathematics. The asymptotic regime could be implicit in a more general correlated-walk first-passage theorem; independent external assessment has not been performed.
