# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The assertion is an expectation limit for positive fixed chord rate and diverging distances in both cyclic directions. The proof explicitly defines the physical first-clock process, obtains exact directed-branch probabilities using tree independence, evaluates all four ray corrections and the bridge, and reduces the result to a single beta integral. It then supplies a factorial decreasing-priority path bound so that the local tree computation legitimately yields the full order-one difference on finite cycles. Strict stochastic monotonicity and endpoint limits establish the unique sign reversal. A separate exact first-clock recursion on finite graphs agrees numerically; finite computations are not substituted for the all-size proof. No theorem of finite-order sign monotonicity is asserted.

**Originality — PASS, with literature-access risks.** Actual claim-level comparisons included published-results searches for random greedy matchings, dimer adsorption with an added chord, and a Braess reversal; the most relevant known published-finding corpus matches concern the average over maximal matchings of cyclic graphs or greedy independent sets of chain graphs, not the exponential edge-clock process on a chorded cycle. Accessible full text of Gamarnik–Goldberg analyzes regular-graph mean performance and locally tree-like corrections, not the signed order-one chord defect. Accessible full text of Krivelevich et al. analyzes normalized greedy-independent-set densities, which necessarily discard a single-edge order-one correction. Dyer–Frieze and Miller–Pritikin discuss fixed-graph randomized greedy matching and bounds, respectively; their accessible abstracts do not establish the formula but are not enough to exclude hidden specializations. Pippenger's graph-RSA full text was inaccessible, and the possibly relevant general results there remain a residual risk. The result's distinctive evaluated constant, strict rate threshold, and weak-rate discontinuity are not established by the verified source statements.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A single extra adsorption opportunity need not improve a random greedy matching: at a standard unit rate the infinite-size mean drops by the exact, nonvanishing constant \(e^{-4}\), while slowly arriving chords improve it. The complete signed rate profile and unique threshold identify a physically interpretable kinetic competition between bridging occupancy and four local depletion rays. The answer provides a finite-defect benchmark that cannot be obtained by bulk-density calculations alone and is distinct from a routine restatement of the base one-dimensional jamming density.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Classical RSA and greedy matching methods, the regular-graph weighted analysis of Gamarnik–Goldberg, and the local-limit theory of Krivelevich et al. are relevant foundations. The proof does not control the distribution of the count difference or convergence uniformly in chord rates; one foundational graph-RSA article and older weighted-matching articles were not fully inspected. Independent review remains necessary to resolve residual priority claims.
