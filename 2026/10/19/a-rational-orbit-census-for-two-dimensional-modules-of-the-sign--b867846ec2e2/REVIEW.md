# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** Seven disjoint and exhaustive rational-canonical types for the first matrix reduce the module classification to invertible-centralizer orbit problems. For trace-zero split matrices, orbit invariants are the product of the off-diagonal entries together with the two distinct axis types. For trace-zero nonsplit matrices, the Frobenius-semilinear model and norm-one torus action prove the \(q\)-orbit count uniformly for every odd prime power. The remaining centralizers and the trace-obstructed zero solutions are elementary. The seven contributions sum exactly to \(4q^2+q-2\). Exhaustive direct orbit enumeration for \(q=3,5\) checks all classes; it is supplementary, not an infinite proof.

## Originality

**PASS, with literature risk.** The published primary results by Chen--Wang and Chen--Lu classify geometric components/GIT quotients over algebraically closed fields; these do not enumerate all rational orbits. Huang counts labeled matrices and provides an inverse-automorphism-weighted sum, explicitly stating that the result does not classify the modules. Lopes--Lourenço impose a non-root-of-unity hypothesis, excluding the sign parameter. A closely related earlier anticommutator result provides the previously known zero-target **labeled** count and other prescribed-value fibers but not simultaneous conjugacy orbits. Searches of relevant research records and direct exact-word/alias literature searches produced no equivalent unweighted count. A stronger unlocated specialization of general root-of-unity quantum-plane module theory remains possible.

## Value

**PASS.** Isomorphism classes, rather than labeled representation points, are the natural discrete objects in representation theory. The theorem gives a complete finite-field classification at the first nontrivial root-of-unity parameter and isolates split/nonsplit orbit behavior controlled by a quadratic norm. The exact polynomial, all seven orbit strata, and treatment of nonsemisimple objects add information inaccessible from a labeled point count or closed-orbit quotient alone.

## Scientific limits

The theorem is valid for all finite fields of odd characteristic and module dimension two only. Nothing is established concerning scheme multiplicities or higher dimensions; small-field experiments are not a substitute for the general argument. An independent mathematical audit has not been carried out.
