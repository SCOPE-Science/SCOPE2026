Since each scalar multiplier is strictly positive, every diagonal entry is positive; the off-diagonal sign is a quadratic in the timestep. For weights at most \((a+b)/n\) every coefficient is nonnegative. For larger weights the quadratic has one and only one positive root, and the maximal edge gives the smallest root. The strongly regular graph parameters are applied using a standard adjacency identity. The supplied exact-rational verifier independently checks 45,967 finite identities and inequalities in 17 weighted graph instances. No finite run is used as proof of a claim quantified over infinitely many graphs.

Brouwer–Haemers supplies the strongly regular spectral identity, not the Padé positivity formula. Existing results for unweighted complete multipartite graphs and for three-vertex weighted graphs overlap as special cases and are not independently novel. The compared published records concerning reversible semigroups and an eight-state directed Padé counterexample do not imply the stated sharp graph-wide quadratic threshold. A mathematically equivalent specialized source outside the accessed and indexed literature remains a residual risk; this is not a claim that a negative search establishes uniqueness.

**Value — PASS.** The exact answer concerns a natural fourth-order time integrator and a structurally characterized infinite class of diffusion networks. It replaces a generic sufficient step-size bound with a necessary-and-sufficient design rule determined by just the largest conductance, two eigenvalues and network size. The strongly regular classification supplies readily interpretable infinite-family contrasts: Petersen has a finite cutoff, Paley graphs do not, and the triangular family has a sharp order-six versus order-seven transition. The single-interval property rules out a distinct positivity phenomenon known for richer spectra. These are substantive qualitative and quantitative conclusions, not a merely relabeled example.
