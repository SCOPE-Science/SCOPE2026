# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The complete-MUB orthogonality follows from the nondegenerate finite-field quadratic Gauss sum. The full ten-basis second-moment identity is derived from ten mutually orthogonal eight-dimensional traceless diagonal spaces. Two Cauchy--Schwarz steps establish \(S\ge50\) for every nonzero state. For the explicit five-point state, the nine-element finite-field trace calculation proves the complete coefficient pattern: four zeros and five squared amplitudes \(1/5\) per basis. The independent exact cyclotomic-arithmetic checker exhausts all 81 finite-basis amplitudes and corroborates those identities. There is no asymptotic or unverified infinite extrapolation.

## Originality
**PASS, with residual historical-priority risk.** The nearest 2026 result (Li, arXiv:2609.16030) fixes a *prime* dimension; its sharp bound and classification do not entail a saturating construction over \(\mathbb F_9\). Fiorentino--Weigert likewise restrict their multi-basis support analysis to prime dimensions. The 2014 balanced-state constructions treat odd prime-power dimensions congruent to three modulo four, while the displayed state has dimension nine congruent to one modulo four. Relevant searches of existing indexed results found single-Fourier-transform support tables over \(\mathbb F_3^2\), cyclic-group order-nine incidence classifications, and finite-field fusion frames, but not this all-ten-basis support theorem or balanced state. A differently phrased historical construction could still exist. The generic lower bound and general MUB construction are acknowledged as prior tools rather than novelty.

## Value
**PASS.** This gives an exact, non-implied sharp invariant in the first odd composite prime-power dimension for the standard complete-MUB total-support problem, together with explicit attainment and a balanced pure state in the previously open \(1\pmod4\) regime. Attainment is substantially stronger than the previously known lower bound alone and supplies a concrete edge-case test for extension of prime-dimensional zero-count theorems. The five-point construction is natural (zero and the four square roots of unity in \(\mathbb F_9\)); the finite result has a clear mathematical object, direct proof and independent exact reproducibility.

The theorem does not say all complete ten-basis families attain fifty, does not classify equality states, and does not claim independent verification or exhaustive historical priority.
