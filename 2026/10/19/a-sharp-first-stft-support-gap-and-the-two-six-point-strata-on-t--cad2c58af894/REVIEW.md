# Review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof isolates all possible signal/window support-size configurations. The only sub-eight sparse case consists of two opposite two-site supports, where exact sign tests give the two frequency-row counts. Full-support pairs with two adjacent pure Fourier rows have all four rows pure; two separated pure rows force a \(\pm1\) half-period ratio and the displayed parameterization. A direct two-character calculation gives the sharp resonance condition \((v/u)^4=(-1)^r\). Each implication has an explicit converse. Exact Gaussian-integer enumeration over 65,025 pairs independently tests the derived conditions; no finite enumeration is treated as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, best-knowledge
The primary full text of Nicola (arXiv:2204.14176) classifies the minimum-support cases, not the first gap or six-point stratum. Krahmer–Pfander–Rashkov (arXiv:math/0611493) treats support bounds and generic-window diagrams but does not enumerate all exceptional-window six-point fibers in cyclic order four. The two most proximate published mathematical notes respectively fix one equal two-tap window and assume odd prime cyclic order; both fail to cover a theorem quantifying over all complex windows on the four-cycle. Relevant results were compared as implications, not just titles. Remaining risk: older specialist finite diagrams or unindexed algebraic classifications may be equivalent.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The four-cycle is the first nonprime cyclic example where subgroup and full-support chirp-type minimizers coexist. The result identifies the exact first nonminimum support threshold *and every pair attaining it*, exhibiting two genuinely distinct mechanisms (opposite sparse pairs and nonvanishing reciprocal chirps). This supplies a natural sharp boundary case that generic-window results and equality-only theorems do not distinguish. It is a small finite classification, not a general-order uncertainty theorem.
