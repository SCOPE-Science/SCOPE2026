# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The universal dimension bound follows from a nonsingular \(M\)-matrix denominator and the degree bound \(\deg P_{jk}\le n-2\) on pairwise multiplier-comparison polynomials. For \(n\le3\), the intersection of affine half-intervals cannot be disconnected. The four-sector nilpotent witness is reconstructed exactly from its first four matrix powers, and its two rational crossings follow from a complete sign factorization. An independently solved strictly positive perturbation gives three exact positive winner margins; it does not assert exact crossover locations for the perturbation. The embedded standard-library rational verifier executes successfully.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The Leontief inverse and parameter-dependent Katz/Bonacich centrality are prior work, and changing centrality ranks as attenuation changes is explicitly acknowledged. The assessed new claim is the sharp four-sector minimum for **re-entrance of a global winner** and a concrete two-switch witness surviving strictly positive coefficients. The closest inspected full texts are Lerman–Ghosh (arXiv:0911.1095), Ghosh–Lerman (arXiv:1010.4247), Arratia–Marijuán (doi:10.1016/j.laa.2016.04.011), and Contreras–Fagiolo (arXiv:1401.4704); the Zhan et al. open-access article provides additional comparison. None of the material examined states the sharp classification or the four-sector witness. A broader, unlocated theorem in attenuation-dependent Katz ranking may cover it; searches and inspections cannot rule out such a theorem.

## Value — PASS
Which sector maximizes total production from a standardized sectoral demand impulse is a natural Leontief multiplier-selection problem. Proving that winner identity cannot re-enter in three-sector systems, while it can in four-sector systems even with every input coefficient strictly positive, distinguishes a structural dimension threshold from simple parameter-dependent ranking changes. The narrow result is a rigorous benchmark for economic-network sensitivity; it does not imply greater gross production maximizes welfare, nor does it establish empirical frequencies.
