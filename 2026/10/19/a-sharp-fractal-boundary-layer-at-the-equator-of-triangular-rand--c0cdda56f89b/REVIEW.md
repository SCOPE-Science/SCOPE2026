## Originality (O): PASS with residual bibliographic risk

It provides quantitative growth limits for each **fixed** nonequatorial direction, not uniform limits along sequences of directions approaching the equator exponentially. A published example concerning ordered-product spectral growth under a periodic cocycle uses a different spectral observable and fixed dynamics, not this finite-time input scaling. There is residual risk of an elementary equivalent calculation in the broad random-affine-recursion literature; no exhaustive uniqueness claim is made.

The moving-initial-direction problem is motivated by the explicit equator exclusion in the recent fixed-direction limit theorem. It is not an arbitrary numerical specialization: the Lyapunov-gap rate is the natural boundary-layer scale where competing expanding and contracting modes have comparable magnitude. The theorem gives a complete parameterized crossover, sharp piecewise Lyapunov growth law, exponential distributional rates, and a continuum of exact fractal dimensions for pathwise finite-size oscillations. The fair-sign Cantor measure and its Hausdorff dimension are classical ingredients; the value resides in their quantified interaction with boundary-layer norm growth, not in renaming them.
