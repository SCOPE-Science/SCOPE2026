# Review of the exact invariant-measure dichotomy

## Correctness (C): PASS
Each level word has even parity, but its parent inserts two ones. Consequently, precisely one of its \(q_k\) starting phases is flipped; this gives the symmetric rational incidence matrix. The level-tower partitions generate modulo invariant probability because the Toeplitz skeleton has exactly one unresolved residue per period, of density \(p_k^{-1}\). Invariant measures are therefore exactly the inverse limit of the computed two-dimensional stochastic simplices. The odd eigenvector is multiplied by \(1-2/q_k\), leading to the claimed product, convergent/divergent dichotomy and two extreme measures. The statement does not require independent numerical evidence for the infinite classification; the exact finite verifier cross-checks the delicate parity calculation.

## Originality (O): PASS, with residual risk
Kang–Jäger arXiv:2609.01140v1, Theorem 6.13, supplies the recursion and proves only existence of non-uniquely-ergodic choices. The exact if-and-only-if threshold and every invariant measure's tower-base masses are not stated there. The general Bratteli theory in arXiv:1709.00055v3 supplies the overarching incidence-method framework but does not identify this lifted family or its parameter threshold. Searches against published mathematical records and nearby literature revealed no equivalent explicit classification. General methods could yield a shorter alternate derivation; this is a bibliographic originality risk, not evidence of stated coverage.

## Value (V): PASS
The result closes a natural parameter gap in a newly presented counterexample family: it shows exactly when the *same minimal two-fold lift* switches between strict ergodicity and two ergodic invariant probabilities, without changing the base's regular Toeplitz unique ergodicity. It also computes the full measure simplex and the intensity of phase separation at every hierarchical scale. Both outcomes occur for natural sequences (constant or linearly growing ratios versus quadratically growing ratios). The classification is structural rather than a numerical census or an arbitrary finite special case.

The theorem excludes ratios below four and other Toeplitz recursions; no independence or expert certification is claimed.
