Finite-offset illustration only; not a mathematical proof. For the dimensionless system in RESULT.md, integrate a small attracting cycle, measure its least period T from successive upward crossings of x=1, and compute the first Fourier amplitude r of x by quadrature. Compare \( (T\omega_{\rm lin}/(2\pi)-1)/r^2 \) with the limiting coefficient \(C(\gamma)\). DOP853 integration rtol=2e-9, atol=2e-11.

gamma=1.5, \(\alpha-\alpha_H\)=0.02: measured coefficient 0.12855711; limiting value 0.12500000.
gamma=2.0, \(\alpha-\alpha_H\)=0.02: measured coefficient 0.11104840; limiting value 0.10416667.
gamma=2.0, \(\alpha-\alpha_H\)=0.01: measured coefficient 0.10757409; limiting value 0.10416667.
gamma=3.0, \(\alpha-\alpha_H\)=0.02: measured coefficient 0.14679917; limiting value 0.12500000.
gamma=3.0, \(\alpha-\alpha_H\)=0.01: measured coefficient 0.13553167; limiting value 0.12500000.

Convergence is nonuniform as gamma changes; the theorem holds for fixed gamma with alpha approaching alpha_H. None of these estimates establishes an exact coefficient. The proof is in RESULT.md and checked symbolically by check.py.
