# A sharp phase-code rank dichotomy for Gabor tomography on a finite-field cross

## Finding
Let \(q=p^m\ge2\), where \(p\) is prime and \(m\ge1\), and put \(G=\mathbb F_q^2\) and \(N=q^2\). The two coordinate axes form a sparse cross
\[
 S=(\mathbb F_q\times\{0\})\cup(\{0\}\times\mathbb F_q),\qquad |S|=2q-1.
\]
For any \(|z|=1\), let the unit vector \(g_z\) have value \(z/\sqrt{2q-1}\) at the origin, \(1/\sqrt{2q-1}\) on the punctured cross, and zero elsewhere. Let \(\mathcal L_z\) be the complex-linear operator-intensity lifting generated by its full time-frequency orbit, as defined below, and let \(R_q(z)=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb C}\mathcal L_z\).

**Sharp cross phase-code theorem.** With \(c_p=1\) for odd \(p\) and \(c_2=\tfrac12\), the flat code has rank
\[
\boxed{R_q(1)=q^2+2q(q-1)+c_pq^2(q-1)^2.}
\]
For every odd-characteristic field, the one-point sign code \(z=-1\) has **full operator rank**
\[
\boxed{R_q(-1)=q^4.}
\]
For every field of characteristic two and **every unit-modulus center code** \(z\),
\[
\boxed{R_q(z)\le q^4-\frac{q^2(q-1)^2}{2}.}
\]
Equality in this sharp ceiling is attained by the primitive cubic phase \(z=\exp(2\pi i/3)\). In that case, every missing operator direction is explicitly determined: it is a Weyl direction labeled by \(((a,b),(u,v))\in G\times G\) with \(a,b\ne0\) and
\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb F_q/\mathbb F_2}(au+bv)=1.
\]
The complex kernel is precisely the span of these Weyl matrices. For the displayed optimal code in either characteristic the **exact gain** over the flat cross is
\[
\boxed{R_{\mathrm{opt}}-R_q(1)=2q(q-1)^2.}
\]
Among codes restricted to \(L\)-th roots of unity at the origin, with the other site phases fixed to \(1\), the least order \(L\) permitting optimal rank is \(L=2\) when \(p\) is odd and \(L=3\) when \(p=2\). This is a restriction on a **root-of-unity order**, not a claim that three distinct complex phase values are necessary.

## Assumptions and scope
Choose the standard nontrivial additive character \(\psi(t)=\exp(2\pi i\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb F_q/\mathbb F_p}(t)/p)\). Write \(x=(x_1,x_2)\), \(h=(h_1,h_2)\), and \(\xi=(\xi_1,\xi_2)\). On \(\ell^2(G)\) define \(T_h f(x)=f(x-h)\), \(M_\xi f(x)=\psi(\xi_1x_1+\xi_2x_2)f(x)\), and \(W_{h,\xi}=M_\xi T_h\). For all complex \(N\)-by-\(N\) matrices \(X\) let
\[
\mathcal L_z(X)_{h,\xi}=\operatorname{tr}\!\left(X\,|W_{h,\xi}g_z\rangle\langle W_{h,\xi}g_z|\right),\quad h,\xi\in G.
\]
The rank and kernel are over \(\mathbb C\), with \(N^2=q^4\) possible operator directions. The result is for the whole time-frequency orbit and the fixed cross-supported, constant-modulus one-center-phase family, not unrestricted choices of amplitudes or multiple windows.

## Proof
**1. Diagonalization of the lifted measurement system.** The \(N^2\) Weyl matrices form an orthogonal basis of all complex matrices, and their conjugation by any other Weyl matrix is diagonal by an additive phase character. For the rank-one projector onto \(g_z\), orthogonality of the character table therefore diagonalizes the operator-frame map \(\mathcal L_z^*\mathcal L_z\) in Weyl directions. Its eigenvalue at label \((h,\xi)\) is \(N|\langle g_z,W_{h,\xi}g_z\rangle|^2\), up to harmless Weyl index reflection. Thus the rank is exactly the number of nonzero ambiguity coefficients; its kernel consists exactly of the zero-label Weyl directions. This is the standard Gabor operator-frame mechanism, not an independent novelty claim (see References).

**2. All ambiguity coefficients.** Let \(v_z=\sqrt{2q-1}\,g_z\), and introduce a phase-convention-compatible unnormalized correlation
\[
 B_z(h,\xi)=\sum_{x\in G}v_z(x)\overline{v_z(x-h)}\,\psi(-\xi_1x_1-\xi_2x_2).
\]
Its zero labels agree with those of the ambiguity function, up to negating the modulation index. Direct intersection of the translated crosses yields the following exhaustive three cases, with \(h=(a,b)\), \(\xi=(u,v)\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
 B_z((0,0),(u,v))&=q\mathbf1_{u=0}+q\mathbf1_{v=0}-1;\\
 B_z((a,0),(u,v))&=q\mathbf1_{u=0}+(z-1)+(\overline z-1)\psi(-ua),&&a\ne0;\\
 B_z((0,b),(u,v))&=q\mathbf1_{v=0}+(z-1)+(\overline z-1)\psi(-vb),&&b\ne0;\\
 B_z((a,b),(u,v))&=\psi(-ua)+\psi(-vb),&&a,b\ne0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first line is the Fourier transform of the cross itself and is never zero, since its possible real values are \(-1,q-1,2q-1\). Each axial overlap is one full translated axis with two exceptional terms at the two translated origin sites. Every mixed translate intersects the cross in precisely two points, neither at the origin; this is why the last line is independent of \(z\).

**3. Rank of the flat code.** At \(z=1\), each nonzero axial translation has exactly \(q\) surviving modulation labels, since only the matching modulation coordinate must vanish. There are \(2(q-1)\) such translations. For mixed translations, a sum of two complex roots of unity of odd prime order cannot vanish (a ratio equal to \(-1\) is impossible); hence each contributes \(q^2\) surviving frequencies. In characteristic two, the two signs cancel for exactly half the \(q^2\) modulations. As there are \((q-1)^2\) mixed translations and \(q^2\) zero-translation coefficients, the stated flat formula follows.

**4. Optimal coding in odd characteristic.** Put \(z=-1\). For an axial translation and zero axial modulation the correlation is \(q-4\), nonzero because \(q\) is odd. For nonzero axial modulation it equals \(-2(1+\psi(-ua))\) or its vertical counterpart, and is nonzero since no odd-order root of unity is \(-1\). Mixed translations always survive by Step 3, and zero translations survive by Step 2. Hence every one of the \(q^4\) ambiguity labels survives. This is the maximal possible rank.

**5. Sharp ceiling, kernel, and phase order in characteristic two.** For every mixed translation \((a,b)\) with \(a,b\ne0\), the last line in Step 2 is zero precisely when
\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb F_q/\mathbb F_2}(au+bv)=1,
\]
which holds for exactly \(q^2/2\) frequency pairs since the displayed nonzero linear functional has a kernel of index two. These forced zeros are independent of the center phase and prove the universal upper bound. Set \(z=\omega=\exp(2\pi i/3)\). At zero axial modulation the axial correlation becomes \(q-3\ne0\), because \(q\) is a power of two. At nonzero axial modulation, the additive character equals \(1\) or \(-1\). The corresponding correlations are respectively \(\omega+\overline\omega-2=-3\) and \(\omega-\overline\omega\ne0\). Thus there are no additional zeros: the stated ceiling is attained and the displayed mixed-label Weyl matrices span the entire kernel. The rank gain in either characteristic follows by subtraction of the flat counts.

Finally, center phase order one forces \(z=1\), which is not optimal. In odd characteristic order two allows \(z=-1\), so it is minimal. In characteristic two order two allows only \(z=1\) or \(z=-1\). At \(z=-1\) any nonzero axial translation with modulation \(\psi(-ua)=-1\) still produces a zero coefficient; these exist for every \(a\ne0\) by trace surjectivity. Thus neither order one nor two attains the sharp bound, while order three does. This proves minimality and completes the classification.

## Verification
The reproducible program `verify.py` computes finite-field arithmetic, traces, and all ambiguity coefficients for field orders \(2,3,4,5,7,8,9,11\). It checks each flat rank, each odd-characteristic sign-coded full rank, each even-characteristic ternary-coded optimal rank, and every predicted even-characteristic kernel label. For field orders \(2,3,4\) it separately builds the complete dense projector Gram matrix and checks the rank directly, without relying solely on the ambiguity count. The accompanying `verification_output.txt` contains the recorded results. These finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the general proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Goldberger, Kang, and Okoudjou (2021) establish the general ambiguity-nonzero criterion and Gabor projector Gram eigenvalues; the present result computes the entire zero classification for a specific two-axis window, sharpens it to a universal characteristic-two ceiling, and supplies the minimal root-of-unity center-code order. Bojarovska and Flinth (2015) also study injectivity of discrete Gabor measurements via ambiguity functions; their general criteria alone do not state the cross-specific rank formulas or their arithmetic phase-code boundary. The known sharp fourth-moment extension result on the union of two finite-abelian coordinate axes concerns a distinct \(L^4\) optimization, rather than linear matrix tomography. Related cyclic single-coordinate perturbation ranks concern flat windows outside a sparse two-axis support. The overlap search does not prove global priority; see Limitations.

## Limitations
The theorem is an exact complex-linear operator-span statement, not a classification of pure-state nonlinear phase retrieval. It assumes a full Weyl orbit, equal modulus on the cross, and a single altered center phase; optimality in characteristic two is among that specified family, not all possible windows. The root-of-unity order threshold does not imply a minimum number of distinguishable physical phase symbols. Known general Gabor diagonalization is used as an input. An older unindexed equivalent cross-window calculation could exist despite the literature comparisons.
