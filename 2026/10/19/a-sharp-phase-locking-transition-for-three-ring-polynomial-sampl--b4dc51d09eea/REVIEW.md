# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The normalized evaluation determinant is reduced exactly to a three-point resultant product. A strictly convex step forces reflection symmetry among all maximizers; strict logarithmic concavity of the remaining one-variable objective identifies its sole optimizing cosine, the boundary threshold \(2+\sqrt3\), and the exact piecewise optimal value. The algebra treats every \(n\ge2\), every positive monomial norm, every \(r>0\), and \(h>0\). Direct finite Gram determinants and independently optimized phases corroborate the proof; computation is not used to infer infinitely many cases.

**Originality — PASS, best knowledge.** Primary full-text inspections: Gröchenig and Ortega-Cerdà (arXiv:2005.14176v1, Sections 1–3) and Gröchenig and Ortega-Cerdà (arXiv:2109.11825v1, Sections 1–7), both on sampling and reproducing kernels. Their theorems concern sampling/interpolation bounds rather than this exact fixed-three-orbit determinant optimization. Related two-ring sampling determines a single separation maximum but does not imply a transition between competing three-ring configurations. Mathematical-record searches for three-ring Fekete configurations, concentric regular sampling orbits, Vandermonde angular phases, and phase-locking yielded no exact matching statement; an older equivalent result in logarithmic-energy or resultant-maximization literature remains possible. A single-orbit Szegő sampling result is different in object and parameter range.

**Value — PASS.** The result supplies a genuine structural phase transition within a natural multiring sampling design: changing a single dimensionless radius ratio changes the complete optimizer geometry. It also establishes a universal, weight-independent design prescription and exact determinant for all degree parameters. This explains why the half-step prescription for two rings does not extend unchanged to three rings. It does not claim unrestricted Fekete optimality, uniform stability, or noise performance.

**Residual risks.** Historical priority is not completely settled; notably, a classical logarithmic-energy theorem might subsume the constrained three-circle case. Scope is only regular polygons at fixed geometric-progression radii and normalized reproducing kernels. Numerical optimization in finitely many examples is not an infinite or global proof; the global proof is analytical.
