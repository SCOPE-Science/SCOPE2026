# A sharp seven-twin threshold for solvability of characteristic-two graph evolution derivations

## Finding

Let \(k\) be any field of characteristic \(2\) and let \(G\) be a finite simple graph with no isolated vertices. The graph evolution algebra \(E_k(G)\) has basis \((e_v)_{v\in V(G)}\), with \(e_ve_w=0\) for \(v\ne w\) and \(e_v^2=\sum_{w\sim v}e_w\). A *false-twin class* is an equivalence class of vertices with identical open neighborhoods. Write \(t_C\) for its size, and let \(\mathfrak d(G)=\operatorname{Der}_k(E_k(G))\), equipped with commutator bracket.

**Theorem.** The Lie algebra \(\mathfrak d(G)\) is solvable if and only if every false-twin class has at most **six** vertices. Its exact derived length is \(\max_C(t_C-1)\) when all classes have size at most six (zero when all classes are singletons). If any class has at least seven vertices, \(\mathfrak d(G)\) contains a nonzero perfect derived ideal and is not solvable. The threshold is attained by the stars \(K_{1,6}\) and \(K_{1,7}\).

More explicitly, for every twin class of size \(t\), define
\[
L_t(k)=\{M\in M_t(k):M^T=M,\ M\mathbf 1=0\}.
\]
Then \(\mathfrak d(G)\cong\bigoplus_C L_{t_C}(k)\) as Lie algebras. The successive derived-series dimensions for \(t=1,2,3,4,5,6\) are respectively
\[
(0),\quad(1,0),\quad(3,1,0),\quad(6,3,2,0),\quad
(10,6,5,1,0),\quad(15,10,9,5,4,0).
\]
For every \(t\ge7\), the first three terms have dimensions \(\binom t2\), \(\binom{t-1}2\), and \(\binom{t-1}2-1\), and the third term is perfect.

## Assumptions and scope

The base field has characteristic two, without a finite-field restriction; in particular the theorem holds for every \(\mathbf F_{2^r}\). Graphs are finite, undirected, simple and have no isolated vertices. The Lie derived series is \(L^{(0)}=L\), \(L^{(i+1)}=[L^{(i)},L^{(i)}]\), with derived length the least \(i\) for which \(L^{(i)}=0\). The theorem concerns solvability of the **derivation Lie algebra**, not solvability of the underlying nonassociative evolution algebra.

## Proof

**1. Derivation blocks.** Write \(D(e_v)=\sum_u d_{uv}e_u\). Applying Leibniz to \(e_ve_w=0\) for \(v\ne w\) gives
\[
d_{wv}e_w^2+d_{vw}e_v^2=0.
\]
Every square is a nonzero \(0/1\) neighborhood vector. Two such vectors are linearly dependent over \(k\) precisely when their supports coincide. Thus off-block coefficients vanish, and within a false-twin class the off-diagonal coefficients are symmetric. Applying Leibniz to \(e_v^2\), the right side vanishes because \(2=0\). The left side is the sum of columns \(D(e_w)\) over all neighbors of \(v\). In each twin class the neighbor set contains either the entire class or none. Since there are no isolated vertices, this forces each row sum in every twin block to vanish. Conversely, symmetric zero-row-sum twin blocks satisfy both Leibniz identities. This proves the claimed Lie direct sum.

**2. A characteristic-two basis and brackets.** Number a class \(1,\ldots,t\). Define \(B_{ij}=(\mathbf e_i+\mathbf e_j)(\mathbf e_i+\mathbf e_j)^T\) for \(i<j\). These matrices form a basis of \(L_t\): their off-diagonal entries are independent, and zero row sums uniquely determine the diagonal. For three distinct indices put \(T_{abc}=B_{ab}+B_{ac}+B_{bc}\). Direct multiplication gives
\[
[B_{ab},B_{ac}]=T_{abc},\qquad
[B_{ab},B_{cd}]=0\quad\text{if }\{a,b\}\cap\{c,d\}=\varnothing.
\]
Consequently \(K_t=[L_t,L_t]\) is generated by all triangles \(T_{abc}\). Identifying \(B_{ij}\) with edge \(ij\) in the complete graph, \(K_t\) is its binary cycle space, of dimension \(\binom{t-1}2\). This holds over every characteristic-two field because the same edge-incidence matrix has entries in \(\mathbf F_2\).

**3. The second derived algebra.** Put \(C_{ab\mid cd}=B_{ac}+B_{ad}+B_{bc}+B_{bd}\) for four distinct indices. In particular,
\[
[T_{abc},T_{abd}]=C_{ab\mid cd}.
\]
Other triangle brackets either vanish or are four-cycles. Therefore \(H_t=[K_t,K_t]\) is precisely the span of four-cycles. For \(t\ge4\), the edge-coefficient-sum functional on the cycle space takes every triangle to \(1\). Its kernel is spanned by four-cycles: the difference (sum in characteristic two) of two triangles can be expressed as a sum of consecutive triangle differences along a path in the connected triangle-overlap graph, each consecutive difference being a four-cycle. Hence
\[
\dim H_t=\binom{t-1}2-1\quad(t\ge4).
\]

**4. Seven distinct vertices force perfection.** Whenever \(a,b,c,d,e,f,g\) are pairwise distinct, direct matrix multiplication yields the identity
\[
[C_{ab\mid ef},C_{cd\mid eg}]=C_{ab\mid cd}.
\]
The two left-hand factors lie in \(H_t\). If \(t\ge7\), any four distinct \(a,b,c,d\) admit three extra distinct vertices \(e,f,g\), so every spanning four-cycle lies in \([H_t,H_t]\). Since \(H_t\) is a Lie subalgebra, \([H_t,H_t]=H_t\ne0\). Thus \(L_t\) is not solvable.

**5. Sharp smaller cases.** For \(t=1,\ldots,6\), exact binary linear algebra on the displayed basis and multiplication formulas gives the derived-series dimension sequences in the theorem. The full structure-constant calculation and a separate matrix-product implementation are supplied as standalone checkers. Every matrix and rank calculation uses coefficients in \(\mathbf F_2\), so the ranks are unchanged under extension of scalars to any characteristic-two field. Hence the sequences certify all such fields, not merely the finite field of two elements.

Finally, the derived series of a finite direct sum is the direct sum of the derived series of the factors. Combining the non-solvable \(t\ge7\) factors and the exact shorter sequences proves the threshold and derived-length formula. In \(K_{1,t}\), the \(t\) leaves form one false-twin class while the center is a singleton, giving the sharp pair \(t=6,7\).

## Relationship to prior work

Reis and Cadavid, *Derivations of Evolution Algebras associated to graphs over a field of any characteristic* (first arXiv version 21 June 2020), characterize derivation spaces in arbitrary characteristic using the graph's twin partition. Earlier, Cadavid, Rodiño Montoya and Rodriguez established the twin-partition framework, chiefly in characteristic zero. These are the sources for the motivation and known linear-space organization; no claim is made to rediscover that classification. The present result instead determines the **Lie-solvability boundary** and complete subthreshold derived lengths, including the seven-vertex perfect derived term. The paper by Boukas, Feinsilver and Fellouris addresses Lie structures of general real zero-row-sum matrices, which do not impose the characteristic-two symmetry condition. An equivalent formulation in modular matrix-Lie-algebra literature is a remaining originality risk.

## Limitations

The statement does not classify ideals, representations, or characteristic-odd derivation Lie algebras. It does not assert that the Lie algebra is simple when \(t\ge7\): only the nonzero second-derived term is perfect. The checkers verify fixed finite dimensions exactly and the seven-vertex identity; they are not used as substitutes for the proof for arbitrary large \(t\). Originality is assessed against the specified literature and indexed searches, not established by a uniqueness certificate. No independent external mathematical audit has been performed.
