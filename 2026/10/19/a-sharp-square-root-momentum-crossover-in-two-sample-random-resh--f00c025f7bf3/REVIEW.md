# Scientific review: a square-root momentum crossover in random reshuffling

## Correctness
**PASS.** The exact epoch state is a constant affine two-dimensional recursion with independent Rademacher innovations. Jury's three strict inequalities ensure that the epoch transition matrix is Schur stable for the declared parameter domain. The covariance is the unique solution of a discrete Lyapunov equation. Explicit rational entries satisfy that equation identically, as checked symbolically; an independent full-path enumeration and stationary covariance iteration support the algebra. The nonzero-momentum and zero-momentum asymptotics follow by taking limits of the fully specified rational expression, with a uniform two-parameter expansion for the crossover. The proof does not replace an infinite-time argument with finite computation. A different gradient normalization or restarting momentum at an epoch boundary would define a different model and is outside the claim.

## Originality
**PASS, subject to the stated residual risk.** The closest direct algorithm source, Qiu and Milzarek's original 2024 random-reshuffling-with-momentum preprint (arXiv:2404.18452), gives nonconvex complexity, asymptotic diminishing-step convergence, and weighted without-replacement bounds, but not the exact invariant quadratic covariance and its nonuniform momentum/stepsize asymptotics. Tran, Tran-Dinh, and Nguyen (arXiv:2403.03180) study a differently defined epoch-anchored scheme. Mishchenko, Khaled, and Richtárik (arXiv:2006.05988) study random reshuffling without heavy-ball momentum. Wang et al. (arXiv:2307.15196) study momentum with independent gradient noise. Domingo-Enrich (arXiv:2206.00632) specifically studies shuffling momentum variance under a stationary noise approximation. Its openly available experimental code computes time-averaged error across complete iterations, not the separate completed-epoch and intermediate-phase moments. The full mathematical paper was inaccessible through the accessible lawful sources, so the possibility of phase-resolved formulas in it is a material unresolved originality risk. Relevant mathematical-record searches located methods with related stochastic covariances, but none of their stated claims implies this two-parameter crossover. General Lyapunov-equation theory is a known tool, not asserted as a discovery. Unindexed equivalent results from older switching-systems or learning-rate literature cannot be excluded, so novelty is not independently certified.

## Value
**PASS.** Constant-step random reshuffling can exhibit unusually small completed-epoch errors because component noise cancels over each epoch. The theorem identifies a sharp distinction between completed-epoch and intermediate-phase noise floors and a practical modeling boundary: fixed positive heavy-ball momentum changes the error-floor power from cubic to quadratic, whereas momentum of order at most the square root of stepsize retains the cubic power. Exact with-replacement comparison distinguishes reduced shuffling noise from the residual effect of momentum. This is not an arbitrary numerical slice: the smallest noninterpolating strongly convex finite sum exposes a consequential change of asymptotic order in a widely used optimizer. It does not promise universal behavior in multiple dimensions or for nonlinear losses.

## Closest coverage and limitations
The statement is limited to the displayed two equal-curvature components and the exact heavy-ball update with independent reshuffling of each pair. The result is phase-specific and asymptotic in constant stepsize; no finite-horizon or diminishing-step claim is made. Previously published discrete Lyapunov tools provide the route to the calculation, and more comprehensive unpublished/older formula collections could contain an equivalent special case. The result has been checked with exact algebra and finite reproducibility tests but has not undergone independent audit.
