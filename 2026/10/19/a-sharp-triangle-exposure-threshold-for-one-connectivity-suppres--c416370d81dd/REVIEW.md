# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The paper defines the precise random-sign homogeneous mean-field equation and restricts its degree comparisons to the strictly supercritical, unique-endemic-equilibrium domain. The exact identity between the derivative's sign and a degree-independent polynomial is rederived directly from the equilibrium equation, rather than assumed. After the distrust-to-odds substitution, the only potentially nonnegative coefficients of a fifth-degree polynomial occur in degrees one and two; Descartes' rule then bounds the number of positive roots with multiplicity. An attained variational threshold, its unique double root, and an implicit-function square-root expansion establish the three cases. The fully specified symmetric example and critical constant are independently checked by the executable symbolic verification artifact. Risks: no extension is proved for multiple positive equilibria, finite stochastic networks, or dependent sign assignments.

**Originality — PASS, with close-source risk.** The strongest prior source is de Kemmeter et al., arXiv:2305.03879v1, Eq. (2) and Appendix B Eq. (13). It already derives the exact degree-neutrality equation, demonstrates nonmonotonicity of prevalence in connectivity, and displays a single crossover in one parameter set. Those facts are expressly excluded from the novelty claim. Its inspected main text and appendices do not state or prove the uniform two-zero bound, the existence and uniqueness of a sharp triangle-density onset across all admissible rates, the square-root opening law, or a worked example with both crossings. Iacopini et al. (2019) model unsigned group adoption, while Cinardi and Bertotti (2026) analyze irreversible adoption with external influence; neither inspected framework provides the same stationary response classification. Mueller and Ramkumar (2023) use an agent-based signed threshold model, reviewed at abstract level only. Risks: the new conclusions follow from structural analysis of a formula supplied by the closest source, so the originality increment may be judged narrower than a model-level discovery; an unindexed equivalent theorem cannot be excluded.

**Value — PASS.** The motivating model specifically highlights a counterintuitive reduction in equilibrium adoption when pairwise connectivity is increased. The result supplies a complete shape restriction for this paradox, a sharp criterion for whether it can occur as group-contact density varies, and a quantitative onset law; those determine which topology-exposure regimes can and cannot exhibit the phenomenon without solving equilibria for every degree. Two admissible crossover points and exact degree-neutral values exhibit the nontriviality. The theorem is a structural result about the mean-field closure and is not advertised as a finite-network performance guarantee.

**Closest literature and scope.** See de Kemmeter et al., *Complex contagion in social systems with distrust*, arXiv:2305.03879v1, full-text sections III–V, Appendix B and Appendix C; Iacopini et al., *Simplicial models of social contagion*, Nat. Commun. 10:2485 (2019); Cinardi–Bertotti, *Trust, distrust and higher-order interactions* (2026); and Mueller–Ramkumar, *Signed networks — The role of negative links for the diffusion of innovation* (2023). The last comparison rests on abstract-level access, not a whole-document noncoverage claim. Related mathematical records were compared by model and implication; no directly covering result was found. Priority remains a research risk, not a certification.
