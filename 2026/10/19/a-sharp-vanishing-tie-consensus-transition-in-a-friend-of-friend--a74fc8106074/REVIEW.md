# Scientific review: vanishing-tie consensus transition

**Correctness (PASS).** The exact edge decomposition with unit diagonal gives the rigorous maximum-weight upper inequality and the individual-weight logistic lower inequality. Scalar comparison yields exponential extinction below the threshold, inverse-time extinction at the threshold, and persistent original-component links above it. Below the threshold, finite total generator norm plus Liouville's identity makes the limiting stochastic opinion map invertible: no initially nonconstant connected component can collapse to a constant profile. At the threshold, every initially present link has nonintegrable inverse-time strength, so logarithmic-time rescaling and uniform pathwise stochastic mixing prove polynomial consensus despite the disappearing graph. Above the threshold the same argument gives exponential consensus. A complete analytic proof and an independently executable exact-arithmetic checker are included. The finite computations do not certify the infinite-time theorem by themselves.

**Originality (PASS, with specified priority uncertainty).** The motivating 2023 work introduces the FOAF differential equations but does not state the present precise constant-responsiveness phase transition or the critical vanishing-link consensus result in its inspected sections. The 2026 sequel derives general qualitative clustering, not this three-regime sharp FOAF classification. General cut-balance and persistent-connectivity results imply **consensus conditional on persistent communication**, not the FOAF-specific criterion that establishes the communication regime for all initial symmetric graphs nor the invertibility obstruction below it. A previous two-agent result on *logistic* adaptive weights uses a different tie equation and does not cover the FOAF result. A related unpublished/doctoral treatment or a broadly formulated coevolving-network theorem may contain an equivalent result; publication priority should be independently checked.

**Value (PASS).** FOAF is a motivated mechanism for creation and dissolution of social ties. The theorem identifies an exact, population-dependent boundary separating preserved disagreement from agreement, including the surprising borderline where *all* ties vanish but accumulated influence still enforces consensus. The result applies to every finite initially symmetric weighted graph, not to a chosen small graph or a narrow computed parameter slice. It gives structural, reproducible predictions for when decaying social ties prevent or permit eventual agreement. General consensus results alone do not determine the sharp FOAF boundary.

The result does not extend by assertion to distance-dependent interaction or alternative tie dynamics. A related doctoral thesis was identified but not inspected in full.
