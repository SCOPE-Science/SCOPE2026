# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

Conditioning on a specified vertex being a leaf gives a uniform attachment neighbor and an independent uniform smaller labeled tree. This yields the edge and two-edge mixed moments exactly. Adjacent/disjoint crossing-edge pair enumeration and the algebraic identity \((n-2)(n-3)-4(a-1)(b-1)=(a-b)^2-n+2\) prove the full claimed formula including boundary cases. An independent exhaustive program checks all fixed cut sizes for all labeled trees of orders three through eight; this finite check is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority uncertainty

Published transfer-current methods establish edge dependence but not, in the inspected relevant material, the leaf-count/cut-square covariance and its imbalance threshold. Published random-spanning-tree cut-expansion results assess cut growth, not mixed leaf/cut quadratic dependence. Similar indexed work on recursive trees or a marked endpoint degree/distance in Cayley trees has neither the same statistics nor an implication giving the claim. The principal residual risk is an implicit computation in an older enumeration or spanning-tree moment source.

## Value — PASS

The edge boundary of a predetermined vertex partition and the total leaf count are fundamental random-tree statistics. Their exact linear orthogonality conceals a nontrivial quadratic association with a sharp sign reversal at cut imbalance of order \(\sqrt n\). The complete parameter classification explains a meaningful structural dependence beyond known single-statistic marginals and pairwise edge inclusion formulas.

## Remaining limitations

Only unweighted complete-graph uniform labeled trees and fixed cuts are covered. No general association or joint-distribution conclusion is drawn, and historical priority has not been independently verified.
