# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The first-arrival recurrence is an identity for uniform random vertex ordering. The chord can alter decisions only along increasing-priority paths originating at its endpoints; truncation can alter a decision only along a decreasing-priority path reaching outside. The theta graph has at most eight self-avoiding paths per length from a vertex, and six from a chord endpoint, yielding the stated factorial bounds. The exact 28-vertex gadget gain is positive and exceeds the uniform truncation error. Exact rational recurrence computation covers every smaller graph; direct permutation enumeration independently checks the first exceptional instances. Limitation: these checks do not prove sign classifications for other chord distances.

## Originality — PASS, residual risk
Targeted searches for cycle chords, graph random sequential adsorption, random greedy independent sets, single shortcuts, and edge-addition nonmonotonicity found no equivalent all-size sign theorem. The closest examined open-access research focuses on random graphs or leading normalized occupancy, not an order-one finite-chord difference. The 1989 Pippenger paper describes graphs with sparse nonoverlapping cycles, but the full text was inaccessible. An unindexed prior calculation could imply the theorem; this has not been ruled out conclusively.

## Value — PASS
This is a physically natural counterintuitive direction of response: a single extra repulsive bond increases final jamming coverage across an infinite family of otherwise homogeneous rings. The edge is the first triangle-free chord, and the size-seven equality is an exact boundary. The proof supplies a reusable quantitative stabilization method, not just a numerical pattern. The effect is order one rather than a change in bulk density, which limits its direct thermodynamic significance but makes it relevant to finite defects and adsorption-network design.
