## correctness

PASS

The normalized right-half-plane Schwarz representation gives exactly M_α(ω), with |ω(z)|≤|z|. Exponential is injective on the closed unit disk, so the first boundary of exp(D) is mapped back by the Möbius inverse to the stated distance minimization. Its distance is less than one, already by testing w=−i for α≥0; hence a minimizing contact is attained in the parameter disk and a rotated Schwarz extremal realizes it. Inside exp(D) the inverse exponential agrees by normalization with the analytic logarithm. The squared distance is F_a(s), independent of the sign of Re w. Its derivative has the sign of Φ_a and the only possible endpoint denominator pole cannot minimize. Differentiating gives Φ'_a=(H''+H)cos(s+a), where H''+H=(v cosh v−sinh v)/v³ is positive with limit 1/3. On [−1,0] this gives strict increase, and the sign of Φ_a(−1) changes exactly once at the quoted angle. On [0,1] the derivative can switch only from positive to negative; the endpoint values rule out another minimum. These facts give exactly the two contacts below transition (including ±1 at zero angle), and one above and at it. Negative angles follow by conjugation. At transition the stationary branch reaches the endpoint transversely with F_ss>0 and F_as≠0. The optimized squared radius has matching first derivatives and positive second-derivative jump F_as²/F_ss, which transfers to the positive radius. For the small-angle law put N=cosh(1)−1, D=cosh(1)+1. Implicit differentiation gives s'(0)=−eN, and the minimized squared radius has quadratic coefficient N(2−eN)/D². Taking the square root gives the displayed coefficient; analyticity and reflection give the O(a⁴) remainder. Independent calculation reproduces the threshold and radii and confirms the coefficient; all continuum conclusions come from the signs and analytic derivatives, not from a grid.
