# Scientific review: Hardy least-squares inverse zeros

**Correctness — PASS.** The extremal polynomial follows from a constrained orthogonal projection with an explicitly unique equality case. The binomial modulus inequality has a strict positive higher-order remainder for every integer degree at least one, including the boundary rays of the cone. The reciprocal-square removable singularity in the scaling limit is handled explicitly. Uniform convergence and Rouché's theorem justify local counting, and the simple-zero implicit-function expansion yields the stated coefficient and error order. The packaged checker verifies 38 exact polynomial identities, 16 numerically computed finite root sets, and five high-precision scaled-root cases. These tests do not replace the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS under best-knowledge comparison, with residual priority risk.** Bénéteau--Centner (2021, Example 2.2) already give the coefficients and pose the sector question (Open Question 2). Bénéteau et al. (2016) treat general optimal-approximant zero locations and Jentzsch accumulation. Orive--Sánchez-Lara--Seco (2025, Theorem 3.1) give an electrostatic interpretation and discuss a qualitative zero gap for the same Hardy family. The inspected source passages do not assert the quantified degree-uniform outward cone, the complete bounded-scale zero limit, or the first correction. Searches for the equivalent trinomial, triangular Cesàro polynomial, and Lambert-W scaling found no directly covering statement; nevertheless older Cesàro-root literature could contain an equivalent fact. This is not an assertion that absence from search proves novelty.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A 2021 survey explicitly asks whether boundary-vertex zero-free sectors exist for optimal polynomial approximants. The canonical singular case is a natural exact test, and the theorem resolves it quantitatively for all degrees, while describing precisely the local zeros that reappear at the critical inverse-degree scale. The limit-profile and correction distinguish the result from a qualitative gap or routine finite computation. It neither claims the half-angle is sharp nor generalizes the answer to all cyclic functions.
