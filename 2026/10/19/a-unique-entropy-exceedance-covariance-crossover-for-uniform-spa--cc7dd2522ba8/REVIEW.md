# Review of the entropy–exceedance crossover

## Correctness — PASS

The conditional beta/Dirichlet decomposition supplies the displayed conditional entropy formula. Its centered form is strictly concave and has negative endpoint values and zero beta mean, forcing exactly two sign transitions of its integrand. Integrating its tail then forces precisely one covariance zero, with positive-to-negative sign ordering. The asymptotic rescaling is justified by a concrete integrable majorant, and the limiting integrand's strict concavity establishes a unique positive limiting zero. The asymptotic constant is obtained from an explicitly stated convergent integral. These calculations prove all quantified statements; finite numerical checks are supplementary.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The claims were compared at statement and implication level with Slud's spacing entropy moments, Bieniek–Szynal's entropy-of-partitions formulas, and literature on spacing-function asymptotics. None of the relevant materially inspected results gives the all-sample-size unique sign reversal for entropy jointly with the threshold exceedance count. The marginal moment identities and broad central limit theorems are insufficient by themselves to imply strict finite-sample uniqueness. The closely related quadratic-concentration count crossover is a different claim, because the entropy statistic and its limiting threshold differ. Potential older implicit derivations remain a genuine residual risk; the evidence supports originality, not a guarantee of absolute novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Shannon entropy and counts of large spacings are natural, complementary goodness-of-fit diagnostics. The exact finite-sample sign classification separates two dependence regimes, while the limiting constant locates the crossover on the mean-spacing scale. The contrast with quadratic spacing concentration makes the scale and dependence change informative rather than an arbitrary statistic substitution.

## Scientific limits and relevant literature

The first cited paper studies entropy and maximum spacing, and the second develops exact entropy moments and convergence. Specialist spacing surveys, broader empirical-process limit results, and a possibly inaccessible older entropy paper could contain implicit coverage of portions of the theorem; these risks are not concealed. The result is conditional on uniform spacings and makes no claim of independence at its covariance zero. Numerical experiments do not replace the analytic proof.
