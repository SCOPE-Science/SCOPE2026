# Scientific review: A unique neutrality reversal in finite nonlinear public-goods games

## Correctness — PASS

The exact payoff difference comes from conditional hypergeometric first and second factorial moments: \(\Delta_X=c[r_1/K-1+2(X-1)(r_n-r_1)/(K(N-2))]\). Both update mechanisms share the exponential transition ratio, giving the finite reciprocal fixation sum. Its strictly convex denominator and exactly computed mean prefix prove the exhaustive all-strength classification. The endpoint identity detects a negative prefix. Uniform Riemann-sum convergence and the limiting integral's strict convexity give the unique nonzero scaling constant. The local program independently re-enumerated the relevant hypergeometric payoffs using rational arithmetic and passed 1777 assertions. No finite numerical test is used to prove the infinite-size statement.

## Originality — PASS (with residual priority risk)

Hauert and McAvoy, arXiv:2404.13728 (first public 21 April 2024), equations (26), (29)–(34), already give the model, general fixation expression, simplified finite payoff and weak-selection advantage criterion, so none of those statements is claimed as original. Their inspected section 4.2.1 evaluates weak selection and section 4.2.2 treats numerically varied selection strengths without giving an all-strength neutrality-crossover count, exact reversal windows or the inverse-population crossing law. Hindersin et al. (2019) present generic computational fixation theory and explicitly discuss variable selection intensity but not these specific thresholds. Wu et al. (2013) establish a related but mathematically different invariant: ranking \(ho_C/ho_D\), not the comparison \(ho_C\) against \(1/N\). A searched related published record concerns pairwise assortativity and continuous evolutionary branching, not affine n-person group fixation. These comparisons support an apparently distinct claim, but exhaustiveness of historical publication coverage is not guaranteed.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The novel property is not a routine substitution for a known cutoff. In the same concrete multiplayer model, selection can make cooperation both more likely to fix than defection and less likely to fix than a neutral mutant. The theorem classifies precisely when this contrast occurs for *every positive intensity*, proves there is exactly one transition and maximum, and identifies the population-size dependent selection scale. It distinguishes the commonly conflated concepts “favored” and “advantageous” and supplies a sharp finite and asymptotic interpretation for the earlier weak-selection threshold.
