In a finite nonlinear public-goods game, a sharp criterion identifies when one cooperator fixes more often than a neutral mutant at weak selection but less often at strong selection, with exactly one positive crossing and one fixation maximum. For fixed group size \(n\), cost and endpoint multipliers with \(\min(r_1,r_n)<n<\max(r_1,r_n)\) and \(r_1+2r_n>3n\), the critical strength scales as \(\tau_*/N\), where \(\tau_*\) solves an explicit integral equation. Cooperation can remain favored over defection even when it loses its advantage over neutrality.
