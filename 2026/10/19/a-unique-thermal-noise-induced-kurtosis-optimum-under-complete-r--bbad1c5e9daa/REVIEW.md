# Scientific review: complete-reset Gaussian-active positional kurtosis

## Correctness

**PASS.** The claimed process starts both position and Ornstein–Uhlenbeck force at zero after every independent rate-\(r\) reset. The exponential backward-age law and zero-mean conditional Gaussian distribution follow from renewal stationarity and linear stochastic convolution. Independent integration of the convolution gives \(V(t)=C[hu+2u-3+4e^{-u}-e^{-2u}]\), with \(C>0\) and \(u=t/\tau\). Exponential-age Laplace moments give the stated rational \(K_h(k)\); the derivative numerator is the explicit degree-seven polynomial. Its coefficient signs have precisely one change for every positive \(h\), and its opposite boundary signs force exactly one positive root. The \(h=0\) derivative is strictly positive. The proof treats all \(k>0\) and \(h\ge0\), with \(r=0\) excluded because there is no free positional stationary law. Reproducibility checks: 20 independent numerical quadratures, six derivative-root cases, three seeded simulated Gaussian mixtures and exact symbolic moment/derivative checks. Finite checks support but do not replace the algebraic proof.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to known limitations.** The closest primary source, Shankari–Sahoo (arXiv:2509.18515v1), develops the full-reset active Ornstein–Uhlenbeck propagator and stationary mean-square displacement. Its text and relevant figures have been inspected; fourth stationary moments and a whole-parameter kurtosis optimum are not stated there. Patel–Shee (arXiv:2602.21134) studies complete resetting, fourth moments and nonmonotonic excess kurtosis in a two-dimensional inertial active Brownian model with rotating fixed-speed propulsion, not a one-dimensional Gaussian active force initialized at zero. Probe resetting in a memory-preserving viscoelastic bath is a third distinct protocol. General Gaussian-mixture moment technology is prior art: originality is limited to the exact rational law and global uniqueness/boundary classification for the stated dynamics. Semantically close research-record searches and accessible prior claims were compared on force law, reset protocol, dimension and whether stronger results implied the asserted conclusion; no directly covering result was identified. Residual risk: an unlocated general Gaussian-renewal theorem or specialized fourth-moment formula could imply this case, and not every related publisher text was accessible.

## Value

**PASS.** The question arises naturally because the motivating model reports resetting-controlled stationary mean-square displacement and a position density; standardized fourth moments quantify anomalous rare-excursion weight not determined by the spread alone. The result identifies a sharp qualitative boundary: *every positive thermal noise* yields exactly one finite optimal reset rate, whereas the noise-free case has no finite maximizer and asymptotes to excess kurtosis \(57\). This is not a routine moment tabulation: the parameter-global uniqueness requires the nontrivial degree-seven sign argument, and the result distinguishes reset protocols and physical noise channels. Kurtosis optimization is a structural characterization, not a claim that search time or experimental utility is thereby optimized.

## Closest literature and limitations

Shankari–Sahoo, *Active Ornstein-Uhlenbeck particle under stochastic resetting*, arXiv:2509.18515v1, is the motivating model. Patel–Shee, *Controlling inertial active Brownian motion via stochastic resetting*, arXiv:2602.21134v1, is the closest fourth-moment and kurtosis comparator but treats a physically different driving process. Georgiou–Bressloff–Bertrand, arXiv:2609.21124, concerns first-passage times for another confined active OU resetting setup; its abstract was checked, not its full text. No asymptotic tail law, first-passage optimum, partial reset, inertial, viscoelastic or non-Gaussian-force extension is asserted. Generic Gaussian scale mixtures and Poisson-renewal moment identities are not independently novel.
