# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For the precisely defined conditional-payoff/shared-environment protocol, the Fermi imitation drift is the weighted average of the two hyperbolic tangents. Algebra reduces its sign to comparison with the explicit ratio \(q_s(t)\). Strict selection-intensity monotonicity follows from the decreasing function \(u/\sinh(su)\). The weak-intensity limit is uniform on the closed frequency interval. The strong-intensity result is not an unlicensed interchange of maximization and limit: a global \(z^2/(2\sinh z)\) bound excludes both scaled endpoints, and compact convergence identifies the unique critical scaled frequency. An exact hyperbolic-tangent double-angle identity proves the witness reversal. Standalone symbolic and high-precision checks passed; the infinite-domain proof does not depend on their finite coverage.

## Originality — PASS with unresolved literature risk
Zhu et al. (arXiv:2408.02042v1, first publicly posted 2024-08-04) establish payoff averaging before Fermi imitation for the stochastic nonlinear group game. Their full article's equations (4)–(7) do not include the alternative environment-before-Fermi dynamical equation, its monotone critical-intensity boundary, or the universal inverse-strength coefficient. The earlier article by Traulsen, Nowak and Pacheco, *Stochastic payoff evaluation increases the temperature of selection* (2007), is a genuine near neighbor about stochastic Fermi payoff comparison, primarily in finite-population interactions, but the accessible abstract does not establish the specific three-person nonlinear public-goods threshold or asymptotic. Its full text could not be completely inspected: no whole-document noncoverage claim is made. Zhang et al. (2026) analyze changed games mainly using networked weak-selection criteria. No searched published research record covered the statement. A separately documented averaged-payoff theorem on changing group size does not entail the present random-environment-before-Fermi selection-intensity optimization. Independent priority evaluation could identify an equivalent result in specialist stochastic-imitation literature.

## Value — PASS
When a nonlinear update is applied before rather than after environmental payoff averaging, increasing selection intensity can eliminate positive cooperation drift *everywhere* despite preserving positive payoff-averaged cooperative advantage at a specified state. A precise, unique finite-intensity phase boundary and a universal \(1/s\) asymptotic coefficient provide a reproducible benchmark for modeling the timing of uncertain payoffs and nonlinear learning; this is more informative than merely observing Jensen-type noncommutation. The result does not extend automatically to sampled individual co-player fluctuations, independent player environments, finite-population fixation, or general networks.
