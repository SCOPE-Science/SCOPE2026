# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The concrete bracket is taken from the cited structural classification, not inferred from a low-dimensional fit. Its three commuting equations are exactly the minors of the projected two-row matrix when the characteristic is odd. Each nonzero projected \(k\)-subspace uniquely determines a \(K\)-projective line. Choosing its center intersection, projection subspace, and linear graph gives a bijective Gaussian-binomial count; normalizers follow from the third bracket coordinate; ideals and their generated closures follow from the full-rank actions of nonzero derived coordinates. Direct exhaustive tests for \(q=3,5\) and separate extension-field checks for \(\mathbb F_9\) agree with the predicted strata. No finite test is used to establish an infinite claim.

## Originality

**PASS within investigated coverage.** [KNS] (2022) proves the classification, explicit bracket and centralizer structure of exactly these Lie algebras; all are acknowledged and excluded as novelties. [BD] (2025) already establishes the maximal abelian dimension three of the five-dimensional free class-three example, not its number of embedded abelian subalgebras or ideal-closure stratification. [BC] (2014) concerns free-nilpotent structure, derivations and automorphisms, rather than such counts. Published related work about Heisenberg subalgebra commutativity and filiform adjoint ranks does not imply this specific embedded subspace-and-ideal census. The new scope is the simultaneous, extension-degree-uniform \(k\)-linear counts, the all-abelian-ideal classification and the generated-ideal fiber distribution. Priority risk: the explicit centralizer model in [KNS] makes some of the combinatorial deductions accessible, and an older or unindexed enumeration could coincide.

## Value

**PASS.** The natural subalgebra dimension polynomial and ideal dimension polynomial are distinct finite Lie-algebra invariants; computing them uniformly for the unique class-three stem breadth-two family answers a complete intrinsic counting question. The result additionally determines the distribution of the ideal generated by each abelian subalgebra and quantifies the difference between all abelian subalgebras and those stable under the adjoint action. For example, the five-dimensional example over \(\mathbb F_3\) has \(292\) abelian subalgebras but only \(7\) abelian ideals, and the ten-dimensional restriction-of-scalars example has \(5134432\) abelian subalgebras but only \(217\) abelian ideals. This is not a dimension-only rephrasing of an existing classification.

## Limits

The theorem excludes characteristic two and makes no claim about nonabelian subalgebras. The field-spread geometry and maximum abelian dimension are standard consequences of the existing explicit bracket; priority is asserted only for the more refined census and closure laws. Full historical uniqueness and independent validation have not been established.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
