# Affine support geometry and cyclic generation in the length-73 BCH code

## Finding

The binary narrow-sense BCH code \(C=\mathcal C_{(2,73,5,1)}\) has parameters \([73,55,6]\). Let \(\mathcal B\) be the set of supports of its minimum-weight (weight-six) words. In addition to the previously determined cardinality \(|\mathcal B|=876\), the following hold.

1. The affine group of maps \(i\mapsto a+2^e i\pmod{73}\), where \(0\le a<73\) and \(0\le e<9\), has **exactly two orbits** on \(\mathcal B\): one of size \(219\) generated by \(\{0,1,11,20,59,67\}\), and one of size \(657\) generated by \(\{0,1,2,35,62,64\}\).
2. **Every** weight-six word by itself generates, through its cyclic shifts and binary linear combinations, precisely the dimension-\(54\) even-weight subcode \(C^{\mathrm{even}}\). This is a rigidity statement for all \(876\) words, not merely existence of a generating collection.
3. The blocks \(\mathcal B\) form a \(1\)-\((73,6,72)\) incidence structure but **not** a \(2\)-design. If \(H=\langle 2,-1\rangle\le(\mathbb Z/73\mathbb Z)^\times\), then \(|H|=18\) and the four cosets have representatives \(1,3,5,11\). The multiplicities of coordinate pairs of these respective difference types are **\(6,4,5,5\)**. Among the \(\binom{73}{2}=2628\) unordered pairs, \(657\) appear in four blocks, \(1314\) in five, and \(657\) in six.

## Assumptions and scope

All codes and polynomial arithmetic in the theorem are binary. Coordinate indices lie in \(\mathbb Z/73\mathbb Z\). The narrow-sense BCH defining roots are \(\beta,\beta^2,\beta^3,\beta^4\), with \(\beta\) a primitive \(73\)rd root of unity in \(\mathbb F_{512}\). The root cyclotomic classes reduce to those of \(\beta\) and \(\beta^3\), each of size nine. The generator polynomial, expressed by its one-coefficient exponents, is

\[
g(X)=\sum_{j\in\{0,1,2,3,4,6,9,10,12,16,18\}}X^j.
\]

It has degree \(18\) and \(g(1)=1\), so \(\dim C=55\) and the even-weight subcode has dimension \(54\). For any six-position support \(S\), write \(f_S(X)=\sum_{i\in S}X^i\). The cyclic code generated by all cyclic shifts of \(f_S\) is the ideal with generator \(\gcd(f_S,X^{73}+1)\).

## Proof

**A. A complete, independently checkable weight-six count.** Represent \(\mathbb F_{512}\) as \(\mathbb F_2[T]/(T^9+T^4+1)\), with \(\beta=T^7\). The integer-polynomial encodings of the two minimal polynomials are `0x299` and `0x217`; their product is `0x5165f`, the displayed \(g\). Division of \(X^{73}+1\) by \(g\) gives \(h\) of degree \(55\). The reciprocal \(h^*(X)=X^{55}h(X^{-1})\), encoded as `0xc4ccd61ebda215`, generates the dual \(C^\perp\). Its \(18\) shifts give a basis, and enumeration of all \(2^{18}\) dual words yields the complete table

| Dual Hamming weight | 0 | 24 | 28 | 32 | 36 | 40 | 44 | 48 | 52 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Multiplicity | 1 | 1533 | 12629 | 59130 | 92929 | 72927 | 20367 | 2409 | 219 |

The MacWilliams identity, applied to that exact table via binary Krawtchouk polynomials, yields \(A_1=\cdots=A_5=0\) and \(A_6=876\), which independently establishes the minimum distance and counts **all** minimum supports. The independent syndrome-collision enumeration pairs disjoint triples of positions having identical binary parity-check syndromes, giving exactly \(876\) distinct valid weight-six supports. Each weight-six support has ten unordered partitions into two triples. The accompanying source code constructs these structures from the defining polynomials; the numeric counts are not assumptions.

**B. The two affine orbits.** Cyclic shifts preserve \(C\). The coordinate substitution \(i\mapsto2i\pmod{73}\) also preserves \(C\): on a support polynomial it is the Frobenius substitution \(f(X)\mapsto f(X^2)\), which preserves zero evaluations at the two defining root classes. For the two stated supports, exact polynomial division verifies \(g\mid f_S\), and exhaustive application of the \(73\cdot9=657\) affine maps gives orbit cardinalities \(219\) and \(657\). The two generated orbits are disjoint, both consist of weight-six codewords, and their sizes total \(876\). Part A therefore proves they are precisely \(\mathcal B\).

**C. Every minimum word cyclically generates \(C^{\mathrm{even}}\).** Exact binary Euclidean polynomial division for the two orbit representatives gives

\[
\gcd\bigl(f_S(X),X^{73}+1\bigr)=(X+1)g(X),
\]

whose binary coefficient encoding is `0xf3ae1` and degree is \(19\). The generator ideal of cyclic shifts thus has dimension \(73-19=54\) and equals \(C^{\mathrm{even}}\), since the latter has generator \((X+1)g\). The gcd ideal is unchanged by a cyclic shift (multiplication by a unit \(X^a\)) and by the exponent substitution \(X\mapsto X^{2^e}\), an automorphism of \(\mathbb F_2[X]/(X^{73}+1)\) because \(\gcd(2^e,73)=1\). Part B transports the conclusion from both representatives to **every** member of \(\mathcal B\). Independently, the checker computes the same gcd for all \(876\) supports and the rank \(54\) of each seed's \(73\) shifts.

**D. Exact pair incidences.** The group \(H=\langle2,-1\rangle\) has \(18\) elements and four cosets, represented by \(1,3,5,11\). Each coset determines exactly \(73\cdot18/2=657\) unordered pairs of coordinates; translations and multiplication by \(2\) act transitively on the pairs in each coset. Counting the \(15\) unordered pair differences in each orbit representative gives

| Orbit size | Class 1 | Class 3 | Class 5 | Class 11 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \(219\) | 3 | 3 | 6 | 3 |
| \(657\) | 5 | 3 | 3 | 4 |

For an orbit consisting of \(B\) supports and representative containing \(t\) pairs of one difference type, double-counting block-pair incidences shows that **each** of the \(657\) coordinate pairs of that type appears in \(Bt/657\) blocks from that orbit. Consequently the first orbit contributes \((1,1,2,1)\), the second \((5,3,3,4)\); their sum is \((6,4,5,5)\). Each coordinate belongs to \(876\cdot6/73=72\) blocks by transitivity. The pair multiplicities vary, proving this configuration is not a \(2\)-design. This completes the theorem.

## Verification

`artifacts/verify.py` is standalone Python 3 and requires only its standard library. Run `python3 artifacts/verify.py` from the package root. Its deterministic output is embedded as `artifacts/verify_output.txt`. It independently reconstructs the two root minimal polynomials and the cyclic generator, verifies the dual basis and all \(262144\) dual codewords, evaluates the MacWilliams coefficients up to weight six, reconstructs the \(876\) supports by triple-syndrome collisions, enumerates both affine orbits, checks all \(876\) Euclidean gcds, verifies the rank of both 73-shift spans, and checks all \(2628\) pair incidences.

The finite enumeration is exhaustive for this fixed code because the dual space has dimension \(18\) and the pairs of triples cover every possible weight-six support. No conclusion for an infinite family of codes is inferred from the finite computation.

## Relationship to prior work

Wang, He, Yi, and Zheng's September 2026 paper notes the exceptional minimum distance \(6\) for the member \(\mathcal C_{(2,73,5,1)}\) of their designed-distance-five BCH family. An accessible October 2026 published computational record establishes \(876\) minimum words in **twelve cyclic orbits** and supplies their representatives. These **previously established facts are not presented as new here**. Knowing the number of cyclic orbits does not itself state the two orbits under the larger Frobenius-affine group, the exact pair-incidence profile, or that **each** minimum word singly generates the whole even-weight subcode. Both constructions and a reproducible full enumeration are provided to test those additional assertions.

Coelho and Brochero Martínez provide general counting methods for low-weight codewords in cyclic codes; their explicitly defined \(C_{t_1,\ldots,t_s}\) family has length \(2^m-1\), rather than \(73\). Neither that framework nor the nearby literature inspected gives the stated affine-support classification or the cyclic-span assertion for this fixed code.

## Limitations

The result is about one natural nonprimitive BCH code of length \(73\), not a classification of the whole BCH family or a weight enumerator beyond the coefficients used in the proof. Its minimum-weight cardinality and twelve cyclic-shift orbits were previously known. The added properties are the coarser affine decomposition, uniform cyclic generation of \(C^{\mathrm{even}}\), and exact four-class incidence profile. Although targeted published-record and full-text searches found no equivalent or dominating result, an earlier unindexed structural classification remains a priority risk. No independent scientific validation is asserted.
