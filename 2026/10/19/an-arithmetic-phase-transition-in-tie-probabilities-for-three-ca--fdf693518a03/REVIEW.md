# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The voter-score difference is reconstructed from the three possible approval counts. Rational weights reduce pair equality to a strongly aperiodic integer random walk with span one and variance \((a^2+b^2)/4\); irrational weights require two independent integer constraints and produce a two-dimensional collision constant. Covariance calculations give nondegenerate conditional Gaussian differences, and the triple-tie event is one power of \(n^{-1/2}\) smaller in each regime. The exact finite-profile checker reproduces the smallest cases. Standard lattice local central limit theorems justify the infinite asymptotics; the finite checks are supporting evidence only.

**Originality — PASS, with residual publication-priority risk.** Janeczko and Faliszewski (arXiv:2305.01769), sections 3--6, study detection/counting complexity and simulated tie prevalence, not exact three-candidate constants. Xia (arXiv:2503.03082), Theorem 14/Appendix D.1, explicitly supplies the \(\Theta(n^{-1/2})\) tie rate for integer Thiele rules, so that rate is acknowledged as established; it does not give the present denominator-dependent leading constants and excludes irrational scoring vectors. Xia (arXiv:2011.03791), Sections 1 and 4, concerns integer generalized scoring and other single-winner rules. Mathematical equivalences and relevant competing coverage were compared; a separately discovered equivalent special-case local-limit calculation remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** Tie incidence drives tie-breaking design and the interpretation of empirical committee elections. The formulas give immediately usable exact leading constants for Chamberlin--Courant, proportional approval and ordinary approval in the smallest genuine committee setting. The unexpected change of the decay exponent under irrational score weights exposes an exact-arithmetic sensitivity invisible to finite simulations; its limits to exact-tie, fixed-weight models are acknowledged.
