# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The score decomposition for all eight approval ballots was independently reconstructed. The centered four-coordinate integer increment has positive-definite covariance with eigenvalues \(1/8,1/8,3/8,3/8\), zero support and generators of its full integer lattice. For reduced rational weights the scalar score difference has unit lattice span. Full-rank lattice local limits give the two tie hyperplane return probabilities; conditional Gaussian signs supply the factor \(1/2\), and the nondegenerate joint difference laws make triple ties lower order. Thus the stated rational and irrational constants and exponents follow for every *fixed* admissible weight. The exact-arithmetic standalone verification checks all eight ballot types, finite histograms, covariance and a separate combinatorial probability identity. The finite checks are not presented as proofs of infinite asymptotics.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

Janeczko and Faliszewski, arXiv:2305.01769v1, develop tie detection and empirical tie frequencies in approval committee voting. Xia, arXiv:2503.03082v1, Theorem 14 and Appendix D.1, already establish the \(n^{-1/2}\) **order** for fixed integer Thiele scoring weights under symmetric impartial approval, and hence for every rational weight after denominator clearing. That pre-existing rate is excluded from the originality claim. Their integer score representation cannot directly encode fixed irrational weights; neither inspected source supplies the rational leading coefficient, irrational \(3/\pi\) coefficient or the arithmetic phase change. Xia–Conitzer's generalized-scoring treatment analyzes manipulability rather than this tie event. General multidimensional lattice theory plausibly provides the underlying method, so an unlocated voting-specific or general theorem could cover this application. The mathematical comparison is at the level of implications and hypotheses, not different titles alone.

## Value — PASS

Multiple optimal committees are a basic ambiguity in representative elections and motivate tie-breaking and outcome-set semantics. The finding identifies an exact change in how rapidly this ambiguity disappears with electorate size, induced solely by whether a fixed second-representative weight has a rational representation; nearby parameters can have different exponents. The fully specified constants make the effect quantitatively testable and illuminate the interaction of arithmetic score lattices with approval voting. This is a structural boundary, not a routine finite table entry.

## Limitations and comparison notes

The analysis is restricted to three candidates, two seats, independent fair approval bits, and fixed weights under globally optimal Thiele scoring. Uniform asymptotics near rational weights and practical noisy score computation are not established. Independent literature-priority verification and independent scientific audit have not been performed. Classical probability limit theorems are used rather than claimed as new.
