# Review of the rank-one Gaussian generalized Nyström ridge law

**Correctness — PASS.** A samplewise orthogonal decomposition isolates the Gaussian inverse-core term; the right-sketch angle integral has expectation \(a/(a+b)\); differentiating the exact chi-square risk gives a factor of fixed sign on either side of \(\lambda_*\). The chi-square density proves the sharp moment cutoff, and an orthogonal trace identity proves the conflicting trace objective. The standalone verifier passed 6,049 deterministic symbolic and numerical checks. Numerical comparisons do not replace the analytic all-parameter proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The core algorithm, unregularized error bounds and Gaussian inverse-core heavy tails are known. The closest previous rank-one sketch analysis provides a trace-law and Student denominator, while a separate one-sided Gaussian projection analysis provides the \(ab\) baseline. Those results do not directly establish the new *ridge-regularized Frobenius risk curve* or its unique penalty independent of the left-sketch count. A full-text comparison with the 2024 integrator paper, the 2020 generalized Nyström paper, and the 2026 sharp sketched least-squares paper found no matching normalized-core ridge optimizer. Generic ridge-regression bias–variance theory is a serious possible equivalent formulation, so mathematical priority is not certified.

**Value — PASS.** The source integrator uses repeated low-rank approximation and bounds sketch moments under oversampling assumptions. For the minimal diagonal benchmark the result distinguishes unregularized infinite mean-square error at one or two left sketch directions from a finite-risk regularized method, identifies the globally optimal normalized-core ridge for every sketch size, and documents that a Frobenius objective can conflict with trace fidelity. The result is narrow but directly tests parameter selection in a genuine two-sided randomized low-rank building block.

**Closest literature and limits.** Lam–Ceruti–Kressner (2024), Section 2.3, Equations (8)–(9), Theorem 2; Nakatsukasa (2020), generalized Nyström analysis; Tropp–Yurtsever–Udell–Cevher (2017), unregularized sketching; Epperly–Webber (2026), exact unregularized sketch-and-solve analysis. The theorem does not address higher ranks, adaptive ridge, finite precision, or a complete integration scheme.
