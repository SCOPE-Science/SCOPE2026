# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

In odd characteristic, diagonalize S. Invertible T sends each S-eigenspace isomorphically onto the other, and T²=I gives the normal form with S=diag(I,−I) and T the block swap. In an isometry group the eigenspaces are orthogonal and nondegenerate, with isometric restrictions. Hence simultaneous isometry orbits are classified by W with V isometric to W⊥W, and the pair stabilizer is exactly the diagonal copy of Isom(W) (or GL(W) with no form). The finite-field form classifications give one Hermitian type, one alternating type only in even half-dimension, and two m-dimensional symmetric types. Both symmetric doubles have square Gram determinant, exactly the necessary ambient class; the even-dimensional Witt sign is the character of (−1)^m det B. Both generators have determinant (−1)^m. For m even the special-orbit quotient is the ambient determinant image modulo the stabilizer determinant image: squares in the multiplicative and norm-one groups, trivial in the orthogonal case. The ambient determinant maps are onto (use a single diagonal norm-one entry in a unitary orthonormal basis or a reflection for O), giving the asserted equal splits. Since (ST)²=−I, the generators are reflections of an embedded D8; for each of its four reflections only the two with odd difference of reflection exponents give (ST)²=−I. This gives eight ordered generators per subgroup and does not count subgroup conjugacy classes. Independent enumeration of orthonormal frames and literal matrix conjugation over F3 verified the O4+ orbit sizes 144,288 and SO4+ sizes 72,72,144,144, including the four-way special splitting.

## originality

PASS

Credit the classical eigenspace methods, form classification and centralizer framework; the surviving supported contribution is the uniform ordered-pair and embedded-subgroup census with the determinant-one and orthogonal-type refinements. Inspected related results cover individual involutions, an unrestricted pair variety, or a different lift problem. This verdict is a bounded comparison, not a claim that the underlying representation theory or every low-dimensional case is new.

## value

PASS

This answers a natural embedding-count question with marked central element rather than a single ad hoc instance. The form on a half-space distinguishes orthogonal orbits even when the stabilizer orders agree, and the determinant quotient reveals genuinely different simultaneous orbits despite unchanged pair totals. This makes the census usable for comparing full and special groups and extracting actual embedded subgroup numbers.
