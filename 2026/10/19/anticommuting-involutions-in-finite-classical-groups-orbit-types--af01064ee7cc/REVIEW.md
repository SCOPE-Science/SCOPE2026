# Review of the anticommuting-involution pair census

## Correctness

PASS. The theorem follows from the normal form of two involutions satisfying \(ST=-TS\). The second involution identifies the two eigenspaces of the first; preservation of a bilinear or Hermitian form makes this identification an isometry. The simultaneous stabilizer is exactly the diagonal copy of the isometry group of one eigenspace. Finite-field form classification gives precise existence cases, and determinant images give the two-orbit splitting in special groups. The calculation of eight generating ordered pairs per dihedral subgroup was checked algebraically. Finite brute-force tests independently verify low-dimensional counts over several odd fields, including a Hermitian extension field.

## Originality

PASS within investigated coverage, not a claim of exhaustive novelty. Taylor–Vinroot, arXiv:1708.05246v1, Section 4.1 enumerates **single** orthogonal involutions, whereas the new claim couples **two** with the matrix relation \(ST=-TS\), including all classical types, determinant-one orbit splitting and dihedral-subgroup counts. De Franceschi, arXiv:2008.12651v1, Section 4.2 provides prior semisimple centralizer structures but does not print these coupled counts. Related indexed results about algebra anti-automorphisms and nilpotent–involution factorizations concern different algebraic objects and do not imply the stated finite-classical pair census. Residual risk: an equivalent theorem may be formulated in classical dihedral-representation or projective-subgroup literature.

## Value

PASS. These ordered pairs are canonical lifts of commuting projective involutions whose commutator is the nontrivial scalar \(-I\), equivalently embeddings of dihedral groups of order eight with specified center. Determining the existence obstruction, all simultaneous conjugacy types and every subgroup multiplicity for multiple infinite classical families is a natural complete classification, not an arbitrary numerical sample. The Witt-sign dependence on \(q\pmod4\) and the new determinant-one orbit splits are structurally informative.

## Scope and limitations

Odd finite-field characteristic only. No assertion concerns Omega groups, characteristic two, spinor-norm refinements, projective involutions without involutive lifts, or conjugacy classes of embedded dihedral subgroups. Order formulas and classification of single involutions are previously known. General proof is mathematical; finite exact tests are supporting checks only. An equivalent pre-existing theorem under different terminology remains possible.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
