In odd characteristic and in every permitted dimension, ordered anticommuting involution pairs in finite classical groups are controlled exactly by an isometric doubling of the \(+1\) eigenspace: the full group has one orbit in the general-linear, unitary and admissible symplectic cases, but two in admissible orthogonal cases. When pairs belong to the determinant-one subgroup, these orbits split into exactly two or four, and group-order quotients count both ordered pairs and embedded dihedral subgroups with central element \(-I\).
