# Antipodal Abelian-square spectrum of binary order-five de Bruijn cycles

## Finding
A binary de Bruijn cycle of order five is a circular binary word of length \(32\) in which each length-five binary word occurs precisely once as a cyclic factor. Cycles are identified up to rotation, but not under complement or reversal. There are \(2048\) such cycles.

For a cycle \(B=(b_i)_{i\in\mathbb Z/32\mathbb Z}\), define the *antipodal Abelian-square count*
\[
H(B)=\#\left\{i\in\mathbb Z/32\mathbb Z:\sum_{j=0}^{15}b_{i+j}=8\right\}.
\]
The definition is equivalent to requiring that the consecutive cyclic length-\(16\) blocks beginning at \(i\) and \(i+16\) have the same numbers of zeros and ones. Thus each qualifying starting position is an occurrence of an Abelian square of length \(32\), or equivalently a balanced cut of the whole cycle into antipodal halves. The total number of ones in any order-five binary de Bruijn cycle is exactly \(16\).

The orbits refer to the group generated by bitwise complementation and sequence reversal, acting on the rotation classes. There are \(528\) such orbits. The range \(2\le H(B)\le22\) is sharp, and every even integer in this interval is realized. With a uniform random rotation class, the exact mean and variance are
\[
\mathbb E H=\frac{1985}{256},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(H)=\frac{1278079}{65536}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The result concerns *all and only* the binary cyclic de Bruijn sequences of order exactly five, without relabeling the two symbols, reversal identification, or truncation of cyclic windows. Starting positions are distinct even if their underlying Abelian Parikh-vector pairs agree. There are exactly \(32\) potential starts, and the investigated Abelian squares have total length \(32\), not six or any other length. Enumeration is exact and finite, not a claim about arbitrary orders.

## Proof
**(1) Full-period balance and a nonzero even count.** Because every binary length-five factor occurs once, every binary four-bit factor occurs twice as a starting state, and exactly half the cyclic bits are ones. Put \(f_i=\sum_{j=0}^{15}b_{i+j}-8\). Then \(f_{i+16}=-f_i\) and \(f_{i+1}-f_i=b_{i+16}-b_i\in\{-1,0,1\}\). Hence some integer \(f_i\) vanishes along the discrete path from \(f_i\) to its opposite, and vanishing occurs in antipodal pairs. Therefore \(H(B)\) is even and at least two. The upper bound and all multiplicities below use a complete finite enumeration.

**(2) Complete pairing parameterization.** Use the order-four binary de Bruijn digraph with vertices \(v\in\{0,\ldots,15\}\) (four-bit words) and directed edges \(e\in\{0,\ldots,31\}\) (five-bit words). Edge \(e=2v+b\) goes from vertex \(v\) to vertex \((2v+b)\bmod16\). At each vertex there are exactly two entering and two leaving edges. Specifying a one-to-one matching from the entering edges to the leaving edges therefore requires one bit \(\tau_v\). If the current edge is \(e\) and its head vertex is \(v=e\bmod16\), its successor is
\[
S_\tau(e)=2v+\bigl(\tau_v\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}\lfloor e/16\rfloor\bigr).
\]
Here \(\mathrm{xor}\) denotes exclusive-or on bits. The assignment \(\tau\in\{0,1\}^{16}\) induces a permutation of the \(32\) edges. Every component cycle is an edge-disjoint directed circuit, and the assignment specifies a single Eulerian circuit exactly when the permutation is a single \(32\)-cycle. Conversely, any Eulerian circuit induces exactly these local matchings. The five-zero edge \(e=0\) is unique, so following the successor permutation starting at \(e=0\) chooses exactly one representative of every rotation class. Thus testing all \(2^{16}=65536\) assignments and retaining single-cycle permutations enumerates every desired cycle once.

**(3) Independent edge-traversal construction.** Independently, depth-first search starts by traversing the unique edge \(e=0\), then recursively chooses either unused outgoing edge from the current vertex, requiring a return to vertex zero after exactly \(32\) distinct edges. No matching assignment is used in this method. Every complete tour gives the cyclic binary sequence formed by the terminal bits of its successive edges. Fixing the unique \(e=0\) as the first edge eliminates rotational duplicates. The two finite constructions each yield exactly \(2048\) valid de Bruijn cycles and, after canonical alignment at \(e=0\), exactly the same \(2048\) sequences.

**(4) Complete evaluation and moments.** For each generated binary cycle, the pairing enumeration computes \(H\) directly by summing all \(32\) pairs of consecutive cyclic length-\(16\) blocks and comparing their sums. Separately, the edge-traversal enumeration uses prefix sums of two full periods and checks the value \(8\) at each position. Every generated word is independently checked to contain all \(32\) five-bit patterns exactly once and to have Hamming weight \(16\). The two implementations agree on every word and every \(H\). The exact spectrum is the eleven-row integer histogram above, whose multiplicities sum to \(2048\). The mean and variance follow by summing the first and second moments against that histogram.

To classify symmetry, take each cycle's rotation class and its three transforms under complement, reversal, and both. They form orbit sets of size two or four; count these sets once. Their frequencies yield the two orbit columns, whose weighted sum in each row equals the cycle count. Summing those columns gives \(528\) symmetry orbits. Exhaustiveness follows because every possible Eulerian circuit appears in both enumerations, and the verifier checks the complete finite loops rather than a statistical sample.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with Python 3, using only the standard library. The script prints the complete histogram, the orbit table, exact rational moments, explicit extremal witnesses, and the final line `HALF_PERIOD_ABELIAN_DB5_PASS words=2048 switches=65536 orbits=528`. Each graph edge occurs exactly once in every accepted circuit, and the two independent enumeration methods generate exactly identical sets of 2,048 canonical cycles. The two count implementations use different algorithms (direct cyclic sums versus prefix sums). This is an exhaustive finite verification of the order-five statement, not a proof for higher orders.

## Relationship to prior work
Fici, Mignosi, and Shallit introduced general questions about numbers of Abelian squares in finite and infinite words and the Abelian-square-richness property; their results concern distinct factors and do not give this exact antipodal, full-cycle spectrum for de Bruijn order five. Baker, Mittal, Mouli, and Tang investigated balanced generalized de Bruijn sequences and showed that ordinary de Bruijn cycles are balanced, but their existence theorem does not count which opposite-half cuts are individually balanced or classify the order-five spectrum. Existing enumerations and rule listings of the \(2048\) order-five cycles establish the size of the finite universe, not the value distribution of \(H\). The quantity here is a new statistic on that universe; an equivalent undocumented tabulation remains possible.

## Limitations
This result is a complete finite classification for binary order five only, with no claimed extension to arbitrary orders or alphabet sizes. It counts **occurrences**, not distinct Abelian-square words; rotation-equivalent cycles are identified but their cyclic start positions remain distinct. The exact spectrum uses computer enumeration, but the code implements and verifies complete bijective enumerations, not unbounded search or random experiments. Literature uniqueness is not certified, and independent mathematical review has not been performed.
