# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact \(2n\) invariant two-dimensional Fourier blocks are reconstructed from the defining dicyclic group relations. For a single-coset support their diagonal factors are \(1+\zeta^{ka}+\zeta^{kb}\); the zero-sum property of three unimodular numbers and Bezout's identity give exactly \(2d\) zero blocks and kernel dimension \(4d\). For a mixed-coset support the determinant is \(2+2\cos(2\pi ka/(2n))-(-1)^k\), so only even Fourier modes can be singular and exactly \(2\gcd(a,n)\) rank-one blocks occur under the stated divisibility condition. No exceptional one-dimensional representation is omitted because the explicit invariant fibers sum to all \(4n\) dimensions. The Chinese remainder theorem counts each primitive ordered single-coset pair; the divisor/totient sums prove the full census and sharp extremizers. Direct full-matrix modular checks passed on 1,081 chosen supports for group parameters \(2\le n\le12\); the two independent census implementations agreed for every \(2\le n\le24\). These tests are corroborative, not the unrestricted proof.

## Originality — PASS, with acknowledged risk
Arezoomand–Taeri (2013), especially its cyclic-index-two Corollary 9, supplies the general Fourier block and characteristic-polynomial mechanism and could imply an individual spectrum after specialization; no novelty is claimed for block diagonalization. The present assertion consists of the fully simplified all-three-support arithmetic kernel criterion, exact divisor-stratified support multiplicities, a closed singular-support count, and the complete equality family for maximal nullity on every dicyclic group. Those counting and classification statements are not supplied by the inspected theorem/corollary or the inspected sources studying inverse-closed generalized-dicyclic Cayley graphs. Two published three-tap classifications concern different groups: generalized dihedral groups with reflections of order two and ternary Heisenberg groups. They share Fourier methods but do not specialize to the dicyclic groups with a central fourth-order coset generator. Residual risk: a stronger or implicit equivalent in group-algebra, circulant, or Cayley graph literature might already entail the whole arithmetic census; this comparison is best-knowledge, not independent priority certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The object is the natural rank/nullity of a three-term averaging operator on every group in a canonical nonabelian infinite family. The classification covers all support placements, including asymmetric and identity-containing triples, rather than cherry-picking a finite subset. It gives a complete census with arithmetic multiplicities, an if-and-only-if global invertibility threshold determined by three-divisibility, and a sharp extremal nullity with unique support geometry. This full all-parameter classification is structurally informative for short Cayley operators and finite noncommutative Fourier analysis, not a routine small-size example or a mere restatement of the general representation formula.

## Closest literature and limitations
The most pertinent earlier result is Arezoomand–Taeri (2013), DOI 10.37236/3105, on general Cayley digraph spectra. Abdollahi–Jazaeri (2013), arXiv:1307.5413, and Behajaina–Legrand (2022), arXiv:2202.11398, investigate inverse-closed Cayley integrality or related spectral problems rather than the unrestricted asymmetric nullity census here. Relevant newer work on cubic quaternionic Cayley graphs emphasizes graph structure. The finite checker uses characteristic other than zero; its output is not an infinite certificate. This review does not claim exhaustive knowledge of all historical literature.
