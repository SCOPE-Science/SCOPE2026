# Arithmetic triple junctions in the Fourier-zero lines of a dyadic tetrahedral gasket

## Finding

Let \(\mu\) be the equal-weight self-similar probability measure on \(\mathbb R^3\) generated by the four maps \(\tau_b(x)=(x+b)/2\), with \(b\in B=\{0,e_1,e_2,e_3\}\). Use \(\widehat\mu(\xi)=\int e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot x}\,d\mu(x)\).

Its real Fourier-zero set is the locally finite union of straight lines
\[
L_{k;i;a,b}=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb R^3:
\xi_i=2^{k-1}(2a+1),\quad
\xi_j-\xi_\ell=2^{k-1}(2b+1)\right\},
\]
where \(k\geq1\), \(i\in\{1,2,3\}\), \(a,b\in\mathbb Z\), and \(j<\ell\) are the remaining coordinate indices. This line description follows from the previously known four-character mask formula; it is included to specify the setting, not claimed as new.

**Junction and order theorem.** Every real Fourier zero is incident to exactly one, two, or three distinct lines. A two-line junction is the zero of exactly one mask factor and has real-analytic Taylor order one. A three-line junction is the simultaneous zero of exactly two mask factors at distinct dyadic scales and has Taylor order two. The three line directions at such a junction are linearly independent. There are no higher-order Fourier zeros and no junctions outside \(\mathbb Z^3\).

Define \(\nu_2(n)\) as the exponent of two in a nonzero integer \(n\), with \(\nu_2(0)=\infty\). An integer point \(\xi=(\xi_1,\xi_2,\xi_3)\) is a junction precisely when, after a permutation \((p,q,r)\) of its coordinates, one has
\[
\nu_2(p)=\nu_2(q)=a<\nu_2(r),\qquad a\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}.
\]
The coordinate \(r\) is uniquely determined by its strictly larger valuation. This junction is a **three-line junction** precisely when
\[
r\ne0,\quad p\ne q,\quad \nu_2(p-q)=\nu_2(r)<\infty.
\]
Otherwise it is a two-line junction.

Let \(Q_R=[-R,R]^3\cap\mathbb Z^3\), let \(J(R)\) count junctions in \(Q_R\), and let \(T(R)\) count three-line junctions. For positive integer \(R\to\infty\),
\[
\boxed{\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{J(R)}{(2R+1)^3}=\frac37,\qquad
\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{T(R)}{(2R+1)^3}=\frac17.}
\]
Thus two-line junctions have density \(2/7\), and exactly one third of all junction lattice points are transverse triple junctions.

## Assumptions and scope

The dilation is precisely two in each of the three directions, and the four digits have equal positive weights. The stated zero set is the zero set on **real** frequency space \(\mathbb R^3\); complex zeros are not considered. At a point, *Taylor order* means the least total degree of a nonzero term in the convergent real-variable Taylor expansion of the complex-valued function \(\widehat\mu\). The junction count concerns **lattice points** incident to at least two distinct zero lines, not the uncountable set of points on individual lines. The cube boundaries are included in \(Q_R\).

## Proof

**1. Product and mask zero lines.** Self-similarity gives the locally uniformly convergent product
\[
\widehat\mu(\xi)=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}m(2^{-k}\xi),\qquad
m(t)=\frac{1+e^{-2\pi it_1}+e^{-2\pi it_2}+e^{-2\pi it_3}}4.
\]
For any fixed \(\xi\), the sum of the absolute deviations of its factors from one is finite. After finitely many factors, all are close to one; the remaining nonzero product is nonzero. Consequently a Fourier zero occurs if and only if some finite mask factor vanishes. This observation also applies locally around any fixed point, so the tail contributes a nonvanishing real-analytic factor.

Four unit complex numbers can sum to zero only by splitting into two antipodal pairs. Applying this to the four summands of \(4m\) shows that some coordinate phase is \(-1\) and the other two phases are negatives of each other. At level \(k\), this is precisely the system defining \(L_{k;i;a,b}\). Since a line at level \(k\) has a coordinate with absolute value at least \(2^{k-1}\), and its two defining integer offsets are bounded on a compact region, the line family is locally finite.

**2. Single-level junctions.** At a fixed level \(k\), a mask zero has one pivot phase \(-1\), except when two coordinate phases are \(-1\) and the third is \(+1\). In the latter case exactly two pivot representations exist: two distinct lines meet, and all three coordinates are integers. Writing the two \(-1\) coordinates as \(p,q\) and the remaining \(+1\) coordinate as \(r\), the exact condition is \(\nu_2(p)=\nu_2(q)=k-1<\nu_2(r)\). The two direction vectors are not parallel.

**3. Cross-level junctions.** Suppose levels \(k<\ell\) both have a vanishing mask factor. Choose the pivot at the larger scale, say \(r\); then \(\nu_2(r)=\ell-1\). Its phase at level \(k\) is \(+1\), forcing the other two phases at level \(k\) to equal \(-1\). Hence \(\nu_2(p)=\nu_2(q)=k-1<\nu_2(r)\). This lower factor supplies **two** crossing lines. At the larger scale the phases of \(p\) and \(q\) are antipodal if and only if \(\nu_2(p-q)=\ell-1=\nu_2(r)\), which is precisely the stated arithmetic test. No third mask factor can vanish: each coordinate can serve as a pivot only at its unique finite valuation level. The higher factor supplies exactly one further line. Its direction, together with those of the lower two, is a permutation of \((0,1,1),(1,0,1),(1,1,0)\), whose determinant is nonzero. Thus the three directions span \(\mathbb R^3\).

**4. Exact Taylor orders.** At any real mask zero, its derivative with respect to a pivot coordinate is nonzero:
\[
\partial_{t_i}m(t)=-(\pi i/2)e^{-2\pi it_i}\ne0.
\]
Therefore each vanishing factor has Taylor order one, even where that factor has a two-line real-zero crossing. The product with all other nonvanishing analytic factors has Taylor order equal to the number of distinct vanishing levels, which by Step 3 is one or two. The complete zero-locus line description from Step 1 then proves the asserted local types, including the absence of other junctions.

**5. The two-adic junction census.** In the limiting uniform distribution on \(\mathbb Z_2^3\), for any choice of the high coordinate \(r\) and fixed \(a\geq0\), the probability that \(\nu_2(p)=\nu_2(q)=a<\nu_2(r)\) is \(2^{-3a-3}\). The high coordinate is uniquely identified, so the total junction density is
\[
3\sum_{a=0}^{\infty}2^{-3a-3}=\frac37.
\]
For a triple junction put \(j=\nu_2(r)-a\geq1\). Conditional on the two low coordinates both having valuation \(a\), their odd parts are independent Haar-uniform odd integers; the probability that their difference has valuation \(j\) is \(2^{-j}\). Including the independent high-coordinate condition yields \(2^{-3a-2j-3}\). Hence the total triple density is
\[
3\sum_{a=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-3a-2j-3}=\frac17.
\]
These Haar densities are the limiting natural densities in the cubes \(Q_R\): every fixed finite collection of congruence conditions is equidistributed as \(R\to\infty\), and the tails of the valuation sums are bounded by convergent geometric series, uniformly after passing to upper densities. Subtracting gives the two-line density \(2/7\). This proves the theorem.

## Verification

The standalone `verify_tetra.py` checker uses integer modular congruences to find mask-zero pivot lines independently of the \(\nu_2\)-criterion. It compares both methods for every lattice point in cubes of radii \(7\), \(15\), and \(31\), including the cases of zero coordinates and repeated low coordinates. It also checks the defining geometric series, and the examples \((1,3,2)\) (three lines, two mask factors) and \((1,1,2)\) (two lines, one mask factor). All checks executed successfully. This finite verification does not replace the unrestricted proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Dutkay and Jorgensen's 2006 affine-IFS Fourier theory proves the underlying product identity and, in the proof of their Theorem 5.1(iii), explicitly describes the zero mask as three families of straight lines. Consequently the mask factorization and the zero-line description are **prior results**, not originality claims here. Their theorem addresses spectrality and orthogonal exponentials, rather than Taylor order, intersections across dyadic levels, or natural density of junctions.

Li's 2012 paper treats the same four-digit three-dimensional gasket within a spectrality problem. Its accessible abstract establishes spectrality and non-spectrality conclusions, not the present local singularity census; its full text could not be inspected, leaving a publication-priority risk. Hu, Zhang and Wang (2024), Section 2, give a general three-family zero-set representation for other affine matrices, with later orthogonality arguments, rather than a dyadic double-scale junction and density analysis. These prior zero-set formulas are respected; the proposed distinct assertion is the bounded-order and arithmetic junction classification, including densities \(1/7\) and \(3/7\).

## Limitations

No assertion is made about unequal digit weights, different contraction matrices, Fourier zeros off real frequency space, or uniqueness among all historical publications. The proof gives asymptotic lattice densities but not a quantitative remainder estimate for the cube counts. The 2012 same-object source remained accessible only at abstract level. A result equivalent to the junction census under a different formulation in older spectral or fractal literature is a residual originality risk; the comparison performed here is not an independent publication-priority certification.
