# Scientific review: arithmetic telegraph resonances

## Correctness — PASS
The stochastic Hamiltonian commutes at distinct times; conditional one-bit Bernoulli probabilities and the independent telegraph forward ODE give the exact Walsh transform. The count of zero signed sums is nonnegative, hence absence of zero sums is equivalent to a uniform site limit. The decay exponent is rigorously two-sided: the slowest real mode has a positive coefficient and the underdamped oscillatory case has nonzero time-averaged squared amplitude. Critical-root, rate-optimization, three-way parity, and near-resonance coefficient are covered. Direct dense conditional density matrix computations match Fourier reconstruction to below \(6\times10^{-16}\) for 96 site probabilities; finite cases alone would not prove the all-size statement.

## Originality — PASS (priority remains provisional)
The closest relevant primary papers are Moore–Russell (arXiv:quant-ph/0104137), Wold et al. (arXiv:1206.2174), Benedetti et al. (arXiv:1510.08652) and Rossi et al. (arXiv:1707.01441). The first treats homogeneous coherent hypercube mixing, the second the telegraph process in a single qubit, and the latter two stochastic tunneling on one-dimensional lattices with distinct link structures. Their stated results and the searched mathematical-record summaries do not cover this complete all-weight, common-fluctuator signed-zero-sum classification, exact TV exponent, or detuning coefficient. A more general Abelian spectral-mixing theorem outside the inspected sources remains a priority risk; absence from search is not a novelty proof.

## Value — PASS
The infinite-family classification determines precisely which shared-noise quantum hypercube networks become position-uniform, despite perfect driving correlation. The hidden three-body parity law is inaccessible through pairwise statistics. Its near-resonance quadratic time scale and uniquely optimized fluctuator switching rate offer physically motivated exact diagnostics of coupled noise, not a routine numerical example.

## Scope and limitations
This is a result about ensemble-averaged **position** distributions in a single stationary symmetric common-telegraph model with commuting weighted hypercube Hamiltonians. It is not a theorem about full-state mixing, arbitrary environmental noise, disorder, or finite-time guaranteed pointwise convergence bounds.
