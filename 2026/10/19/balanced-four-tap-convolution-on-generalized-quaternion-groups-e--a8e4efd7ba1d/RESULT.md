# Balanced four-tap convolution on generalized quaternion groups: exact nullities and logarithmic mean

## Finding
For each integer \(k\geq2\), put \(n=2^k\) and let \(G=Q_{4n}=\langle a,b\mid a^{2n}=1,\ b^2=a^n,\ bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\rangle\), a group of order \(4n\). Let \(S\subset G\) contain exactly two distinct elements of \(\langle a\rangle\) and two distinct elements of \(\langle a\rangle b\). Define the unweighted convolution operator on complex functions by
\[
(T_Sf)(x)=\sum_{s\in S}f(s^{-1}x),\qquad \nu(S)=\dim_{\mathbb C}\ker T_S.
\]
Write \(S=\{a^i,a^{i+r},a^jb,a^{j+u}b\}\), where \(1\leq r,u\leq2n-1\), and set
\[
A=\max\{\gcd(n,r+u),\gcd(n,r-u)\}.
\]
All gcd values are positive. Then the following cases are mutually exclusive and exhaustive:
\[
\nu(S)=\begin{cases}
1,&r,u\text{ have opposite parity};\\
A+1,&r,u\text{ are odd};\\
3n,&r=u=n;\\
A+2^t,&r,u\text{ are even and }v_2(r)=v_2(u)=t\in\{1,\dots,k-1\};\\
A,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The formula is invariant under independently replacing \(r\) or \(u\) by its negative modulo \(2n\), so it is well defined on unordered supports. There are exactly \(n^2(2n-1)^2\) such supports. Exactly \(2n^3(n-1)\) of them have nullity one; the maximum nullity is \(3n\), achieved by exactly \(n^2\) supports, namely left cosets of order-four subgroups generated by elements of \(\langle a\rangle b\). Moreover
\[
\sum_{S}\nu(S)=n^2\bigl((4k+2)n^2+(1-2k)n\bigr),
\qquad
\frac1{\#\{S\}}\sum_S\nu(S)=\frac{(4k+2)n^2+(1-2k)n}{(2n-1)^2}=k+\tfrac12+O(k2^{-k}).
\]
The sum is over all balanced four-element supports as specified, not over all four-element subsets of the group.

## Assumptions and scope
All vector spaces, representations and kernels are over \(\mathbb C\). The cyclic index \(n\) is restricted to powers of two with \(k\ge2\). The operator includes the identity when the support does; no inverse-closure or connectivity hypothesis is imposed. The word “balanced” refers solely to two elements in each coset of \(\langle a\rangle\). Translation of the support preserves the operator rank. The sum, average and extremum count every unordered support once.

## Proof
**1. The Fourier blocks.** The group has four one-dimensional complex characters (because \(n\) is even) and \(n-1\) irreducible representations of dimension two. For \(1\le j\le n-1\), writing \(z_j=\exp(\pi\mathrm i j/n)\), take
\[
\rho_j(a)=\begin{pmatrix}z_j&0\\0&z_j^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\rho_j(b)=\begin{pmatrix}0&(-1)^j\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
These obey the presentation and, together with the four scalar characters, exhaust the irreducibles since \(4+4(n-1)=4n\). Left multiplication of the group-algebra sum by an invertible group element leaves its rank unchanged. Thus set the first rotation tap to \(1\), and write the other reflection taps as \(a^w b\) and \(a^{w+u}b\). In the \(j\)-th block the symbol is
\[
M_j=\begin{pmatrix}
1+z_j^r&(-1)^jz_j^w(1+z_j^u)\\
z_j^{-w}(1+z_j^{-u})&1+z_j^{-r}
\end{pmatrix},
\]
with determinant
\[
\det M_j=4\left[\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi jr}{2n}\right)-(-1)^j\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi ju}{2n}\right)\right].
\]
In the regular representation each such block occurs twice. Consequently, a rank-one \(M_j\) contributes two to the convolution nullity, and a zero \(M_j\) contributes four.

**2. Even-indexed blocks.** For even \(j\), determinant zero is equivalent to \(2n\mid j(r-u)\) or \(2n\mid j(r+u)\), by the elementary identity for equal squared cosines. Write \(j=2l\), with \(1\le l\le n/2-1\). Since \(n\) is a power of two, the two divisibility sets are nested, and the union has \(\max(A/2-1,0)\) indices when \(A\ge2\), and zero when \(A=1\). An even-indexed singular block is identically zero exactly when \(z_j^r=z_j^u=-1\). This requires \(v_2(r)=v_2(u)=t\) with \(1\le t<k\) and \(v_2(j)=k-t\). There are exactly \(2^{t-1}\) admissible even indices with that valuation. Each zero block adds a further two to its usual singular-block contribution. Thus the total even-indexed contribution is \(\max(A-2,0)+2^t\) in the equal-valuation case and \(\max(A-2,0)\) otherwise. In particular it vanishes for opposite-parity \(r,u\), when \(A=1\).

**3. Odd-indexed blocks and scalar blocks.** For odd \(j\), the determinant is a sum of two nonnegative squared cosines and therefore can vanish only if both cosines are zero. As odd \(j\) is invertible modulo \(2n\), this happens precisely when \(r=u=n\). All \(n/2\) odd-indexed blocks then vanish identically, contributing \(2n\) to \(\nu(S)\).

The scalar characters are \(\chi_{\alpha,\beta}(a)=(-1)^\alpha\), \(\chi_{\alpha,\beta}(b)=(-1)^\beta\), where \(\alpha,\beta\in\{0,1\}\). Their total number of zero symbols is
\[
1+\mathbf1_{\{r,u\text{ both even}\}}+2\mathbf1_{\{r,u\text{ both odd}\}}.
\]
Combining the even, odd and scalar contributions yields the stated five-case formula. When \(r=u=n\), \(A=n\), and the contribution is \((n-2)+2n+2=3n\). This checks all boundary and normalization cases.

**4. Sharp extreme and its count.** Opposite parity gives nullity one; every same-parity case has nullity at least two. To determine the maximum, note that \(A\le n\). The odd/odd case has nullity at most \(n+1\). For even/even with common valuation \(t<k\), divisibility of \(r\pm u\) implies \(A\le n\), and the added \(2^t\le n/2\), so nullity is at most \(3n/2<3n\). In the remaining case \(\nu=A\le n\). The sole way to reach \(3n\) is \(r=u=n\). Each of the two unordered antipodal pairs in the rotation/reflection cosets has \(n\) choices, giving \(n^2\) extremal supports. Each is \(a^i\langle a^{j-i}b\rangle\), a coset of an order-four subgroup.

**5. Exact first moment.** Let \(F(k)\) be the sum of nullities over all ordered difference pairs \((r,u)\in\{1,\dots,2n-1\}^2\). The mixed-parity pairs number \(2n(n-1)\) and each contribute one. For odd/odd pairs, \(A\) has mean \(2k\): every pair has \(A\ge4\), and for \(2\le j\le k\) the proportion with \(A\ge2^j\) is \(2^{2-j}\). The tail-sum identity therefore gives \(\sum_{r,u\text{ odd}}A=2kn^2\), hence an odd/odd contribution \((2k+1)n^2\).

For even/even pairs write \(r=2x\), \(u=2y\), with \(x,y\in(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)\setminus\{0\}\). Now \(A=2B\), where \(B=\max\{\gcd(n/2,x+y),\gcd(n/2,x-y)\}\). On all \(n^2\) pairs including zeros, for \(1\le j\le k-1\),
\[
\Pr(B\ge2^j)=2^{1-j}-2^{1-2j},
\]
by counting \(x\equiv y\) or \(x\equiv-y\pmod{2^j}\) and subtracting their intersection. Therefore \(\sum B=n^2(k-1+2/n)\) on all pairs. Since \(\sum_{x\bmod n}B(0,x)=n(k+1)/2\) and \(B(0,0)=n/2\), removal of both coordinate-zero axes gives
\[
\sum_{r,u\text{ even}}A=2n^2(k-1)+(3-2k)n.
\]
The rank-zero corrections sum to
\[
\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}2^t\bigl(2^{k-t}\bigr)^2+2n=(n^2-2n)+2n=n^2.
\]
Thus the even/even contribution is \((2k-1)n^2+(3-2k)n\). Adding mixed, odd/odd and even/even contributions yields
\[
F(k)=(4k+2)n^2+(1-2k)n.
\]
For every fixed difference pair \((r,u)\), there are \((2n)^2\) initial choices of anchors. Each unordered balanced support is represented exactly four times: two choices of the first rotation tap times two choices of the first reflection tap. Because the formula respects sign changes, the total nullity sum is \(n^2F(k)\), and the number of supports is \(n^2(2n-1)^2\). Mixed-parity cases similarly contribute \(n^2\cdot2n(n-1)=2n^3(n-1)\) supports. Dividing establishes the mean and its expansion, completing the proof.

## Verification
Run `python3 CHECK.py` from a directory containing the bundled standalone script. It constructs each group directly from the presentation and the full \(4n\)-by-\(4n\) integer left-convolution matrix, and compares modular Gaussian-elimination nullities with the analytical formula. All ordered differences and three reflection offsets are checked at \(k=2,3\), totalling \(147+675=822\) modular tests. Independently seeded additional tests sample \(150\) cases at \(k=4\) and \(45\) cases at \(k=5\). The script also computes complete difference-pair nullity histograms and their first moments for \(k=2,3,4,5\). All bundled tests passed. Nonzero determinants modulo the selected prime are valid certificates of complex nonvanishing, but a modular singularity alone is not a certificate of complex singularity; the latter rests on the exact block proof above. The checker is a finite corroboration, not the proof for arbitrarily large \(k\).

## Relationship to prior work
Dobson (2003) constructs certain nonnormal Cayley digraphs of generalized quaternion groups and analyzes automorphism groups; it does not classify zero eigenvalues or regular-representation kernel dimensions over every balanced four-tap support. Minchenko and Wanless (2015) classify connected simple **undirected integral** four-regular Cayley graphs, a different restricted family that excludes most directed and identity-containing supports here. Behajaina and Legrand (2022) give representation-theoretic criteria for **integrality** and distance integrality of inverse-closed Cayley graphs over generalized dicyclic groups. Their general representation machinery overlaps the standard block decomposition, but their theorems do not give this unweighted balanced-support nullity formula, exact counts or logarithmic mean. The derived first-moment law is not implied by integrality conditions. All comparisons concern statement-level coverage, not title matching.

## Limitations
The theorem covers powers-of-two cyclic index and exactly two unweighted taps per coset. It does not classify other weights, arbitrary four-element supports, odd cyclic indices or all directed Cayley adjacency spectra. The finite checker is not exhaustive for unbounded \(k\). The available searches cannot guarantee that no equivalent computation appears in older specialized, unindexed work; independent mathematical validation and priority review have not been performed.
