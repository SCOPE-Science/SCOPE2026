# Scientific review: Brownian-powered stochastic confinement

## Correctness (C): PASS

The exact pathwise conditional Gaussian formula is derived by variation of constants. A scaling coupling reduces the rescaled variance to an endpoint Laplace integral; its local integrable bound and exponentially negligible remote tail prove convergence at every continuous Brownian path with nonzero endpoint, an almost-sure event. The scaled initial mean vanishes. A joint Gaussian representation establishes both the heavy-tailed limit and its independent self-normalization. A conditional tail estimate and dominated convergence give the exact exponent and coefficient; bounded continuous truncations give the moment-divergence statement without false uniform integrability. The reproducibility script ran and passed 109 deterministic comparisons. Computational tests supplement, not establish, the all-time theorem.

## Originality (O): PASS, with material residual risks

The 2026 primary source arXiv:2609.33751v1 already studies Brownian-squared stiffness and nonstationary particle statistics; the stochastic model, conditional Gaussian method and general variance-mixture ideas are prior art. The accessible abstract did not describe the full long-time scaled Cauchy law and generalized power-dependent tail classification. The related 2025 Gupta–Klapp primary full text treats OU-driven, rather than Brownian-powered, stiffness. General Gaussian-mixture and Cauchy-ratio mathematics are expressly excluded from novelty. Targeted research-index and published-record comparisons did not locate a statement mathematically implying the new model-specific limit. But the 2026 primary full text could not be inspected; it could include a dominating limit theorem. A more general stochastic-averaging theorem might imply it. These risks warrant independent literature verification and are not concealed by the PASS assessment.

## Value (V): PASS

A nonstationarily strengthening random trap need not yield asymptotically Gaussian thermal fluctuations after the natural position scaling. The theorem determines the full marginal law, distinguishes exact finite-time moments from the sharp heavy-tail exponent, and identifies an experimentally meaningful self-normalization that removes the random environment. It provides a natural family indexed by the physical stiffness power and is robust to a fixed baseline stiffness. These are model-specific structural results, not a mere restatement of the Gaussian-ratio identity.

## Scope and independent review

The result requires two independent Brownian drivers, strictly positive thermal diffusivity and stiffness amplitude, positive stiffness power, and fixed coefficients. It does not establish lower-order moment convergence, convergence rates, or analogous laws for correlated noises. The inaccessible motivating full text is the main unresolved originality risk.
