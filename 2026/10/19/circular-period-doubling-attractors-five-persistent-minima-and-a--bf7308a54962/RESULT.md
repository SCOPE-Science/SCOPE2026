# Circular period-doubling attractors: five persistent minima and a finite exact classification

## Finding
Let \(D_0=0\), and let \(D_n\) be obtained by iterating the binary morphism \(\varphi(0)=01\), \(\varphi(1)=00\) exactly \(n\) times. For every \(n\ge3\), put \(q=2^{n-3}\) and define the following five unordered pairs of **zero-based** positions:
\[
\mathcal P_n=\big\{\{2q-1,4q-1\},\{2q-1,5q-1\},\{3q-1,6q-1\},\{4q-1,6q-1\},\{4q-1,8q-1\}\big\}.
\]
Every pair in \(\mathcal P_n\) is a minimum circular string attractor of \(D_n\); in particular, \(\gamma_{\mathrm{circ}}(D_n)=2\) and there are at least five different minimum attractors for every \(n\ge3\). For every \(3\le n\le13\), the five pairs in \(\mathcal P_n\) are **all** the minimum circular attractors. The exact-count assertion is finite and is not claimed for \(n\ge14\).

The proof includes a more general size-preserving morphism rule. Fix \(r\ge2\), a finite input alphabet \(A\), a designated output symbol \(c\), a map \(g:A\to B\), and the constant-prefix morphism \(h(a)=c^{r-1}g(a)\). If \(\Gamma\) is a circular attractor of a nonempty cyclic input word \(w\), and some position in \(\Gamma\) contains a letter \(a\) with \(g(a)=c\), then the positions \(\{ri+(r-1):i\in\Gamma\}\), read modulo \(r|w|\), form a circular attractor of \(h(w)\) with unchanged cardinality. No injectivity of \(g\) is needed.

## Assumptions and scope
A circular factor of a length-\(N\) word is a nonempty cyclic interval of length \(1\) through \(N\), with its starting index taken modulo \(N\). A circular attractor is a set of indices meeting **some occurrence** of every distinct such factor, not necessarily every occurrence. Occurrence intersections are computed on the cyclic index set. Words have the literal alphabet \(\{0,1\}\), and the theorem counts unordered distinct two-element position sets, not rotations of these sets modulo equivalence. The five pairs are distinct for all \(n\ge3\). The finite exact classification is limited to orders \(3\) through \(13\) (largest length \(8192\)); the all-order assertion is only the minimum size and existence of the five pairs, plus the general lifting rule.

## Proof
**1. Constant-prefix circular lifting.** Let \(y\) be a circular factor of \(h(w)\). Among the positions of an occurrence of \(y\), distinguish the last positions of morphism blocks, namely those congruent to \(r-1\) modulo \(r\). If there is at least one such position, the input letters at these positions form a nonempty circular factor \(x\) of \(w\). All letters of \(y\) off the distinguished positions equal \(c\). Consequently, the factor \(y\), with its fixed starting block offset, is determined by the consecutive input letters of \(x\), together with a left and a right padding of \(c\)'s of lengths at most \(r-1\). Every cyclic occurrence of \(x\) therefore gives a cyclic occurrence of \(y\) with exactly the corresponding distinguished positions. Since \(\Gamma\) attracts \(x\), some such occurrence intersects \(\Gamma\) at an input letter of \(x\); its distinguished output position belongs both to the corresponding occurrence of \(y\) and to the lifted set. If \(y\) contains no distinguished position, then \(y=c^t\) with \(1\le t\le r-1\). By assumption a chosen input position has \(g(a)=c\), and the final \(c\) in its image is preceded by \(r-1\) further copies of \(c\); hence an occurrence of \(c^t\) crosses its distinguished output position. This proves the lifting rule, including factors that cross the cyclic boundary.

**2. Base and all-order construction.** Direct enumeration of \(D_3=01000101\) proves that its complete list of circular attractor pairs is
\[
\mathcal P_3=\{\{1,3\},\{1,4\},\{2,5\},\{3,5\},\{3,7\}\}.
\]
The enumeration checks every cyclic factor length from \(1\) to \(8\); the accompanying exact checker reproduces it. Every circular attractor of a binary word containing both symbols must intersect an occurrence of both one-letter factors, so each base pair has selected positions containing both symbols. Applying the lifting rule with \(r=2\), \(c=0\), \(g(0)=1\), and \(g(1)=0\) therefore sends any of these pairs to \(\{2i+1,2j+1\}\), a circular attractor of the next word. Repeating \(n-3\) times transforms a base index \(i\) to \(2^{n-3}(i+1)-1\), giving precisely the five asserted pairs. Each \(D_n\) contains both binary symbols, so no singleton can be a circular attractor. Thus all five constructed pairs are optimal and \(\gamma_{\mathrm{circ}}(D_n)=2\) for every \(n\ge3\).

**3. Exact finite classification.** For each \(n\in\{3,\ldots,13\}\), define the set of *test lengths*
\[
\mathcal L_n=\{1,2,3\}\cup\{2^j+1:2\le j\le n-2\}.
\]
At any test length \(\ell\), assign a distinct exact integer label to every different length-\(\ell\) circular factor. For a position \(p\), let \(M_{\ell,p}\) be the bit mask of all labels whose factors have some occurrence crossing \(p\). A pair \(\{p,q\}\) meets all factors of that length **if and only if** \(M_{\ell,p}\mathbin{\vert}M_{\ell,q}\) is the full label mask. The embedded checker directly constructs these labels from the actual substrings, applies the criterion to every unordered pair, and verifies that the intersection of the restrictions for \(\ell\in\mathcal L_n\) consists of precisely the five pairs \(\mathcal P_n\). Every circular attractor must pass these necessary restrictions; Step 2 proves all five surviving pairs are actual attractors. Therefore these are all minimum attractors for each of the eleven specified orders. In particular, at order \(13\), the last test length is \(2049\), and its filter leaves \(6145\) candidates before the remaining tests reduce the set to exactly five. The finite result does not extrapolate uniqueness beyond order \(13\).

Run `python3 verify_period_doubling_circular.py` with an ordinary Python 3 installation. The program prints exact per-length candidate counts for every \(3\le n\le13\), checks equality with the five asserted pairs, and independently checks all cyclic-factor lengths of the largest directly replayed order \(11\) by grouping lexicographically sorted rotations using exact longest-common-prefix comparisons. It also exhaustively verifies every factor length of the base \(D_3\). `period_doubling_circular_checks.txt` records the successful execution. Exact string equality and integer bit masks are used, without random hashes or statistical inference. The all-order lifting assertion and the exclusion of singleton attractors are proved above, not inferred from computations.

## Relationship to prior work
Banbara, Bannai, Guo, Köppl, Mieno and Okamoto (arXiv:2602.16152v1, February 18, 2026), characterize **linear** minimum string attractors of the finite period-doubling words: exactly two such pairs from order \(3\) onward. That theorem does not characterize circular factors or imply the five circular pairs, because cyclic occurrences can wrap the word boundary. Romana's study of **circular** string attractors (ICTCS 2023) develops the definition, a checking algorithm and the general relation for powers of a word; its cyclic-power transfer concerns repeating a whole word, not the substitution \(\varphi(0)=01,\varphi(1)=00\). The present result supplies a fixed-size circular-attractor lifting law, a sharp infinite-family minimum, and a complete finite census in a model distinct from the linear model. Previous period-doubling calculations of shortest unique **linear** factors do not imply these circular hitting-set assertions. Priority under unexamined equivalent terminology is not independently certified.

## Limitations
The **exact number five** is proved only for \(3\le n\le13\); for \(n\ge14\), this work guarantees *at least* five minimum circular attractors, not that there are only five. The all-order proof uses a special constant-prefix substitution with a selected output letter equal to its prefix symbol, not an arbitrary morphism. The numerical component is exhaustive only at the explicitly stated finite orders. No independent scientific audit or outside mathematical validation is claimed.
