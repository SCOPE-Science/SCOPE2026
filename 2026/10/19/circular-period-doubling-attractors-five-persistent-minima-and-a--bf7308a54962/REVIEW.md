# Scientific review: circular period-doubling attractors

## Correctness — PASS
The constant-prefix lift follows by separating each circular factor according to whether it crosses a block-final symbol. The factor behind its block-final positions is a circular input factor, and lifting a selected input occurrence preserves the hit. A factor without a block-final symbol is a run of the constant prefix character, which is covered by a selected output block ending in that character. Direct base verification at order three and induction prove the five pair constructions for every order; both symbols force a minimum of two positions. The finite exact classification uses only necessary mask tests for all unordered pairs at each explicitly named test length; equality of the survivors with the constructed five pairs therefore gives sufficiency without assuming that test lengths cover every factor length. The actual executable and recorded checks corroborate all eleven finite classifications. Risk: a coding error could invalidate finite classifications, but exact factor strings, integer bitmasks, independent complete-length tests at smaller sizes and deterministic replay mitigate it.

## Originality — PASS within examined evidence
Banbara et al., arXiv:2602.16152v1 (2026-02-18), prove the exact two minimum *linear* attractor pairs for period-doubling words, not the cyclic-language five-pair claim. Romana (ICTCS 2023) studies circular attractors and the power of a word, not a nontrivial constant-prefix substitution lifting construction. The available related mathematical-record findings concern linear greedy attractors, shortest unique linear factors, and circular attractors of other morphic families. Searches of mathematical research records and relevant literature returned no exact covering cyclic statement. The full Banbara preprint was inspected, but only indexed descriptive material was inspected for Romana's work. Residual risk: older literature under alternate descriptions of cyclic hitting sets or substitution transfer might contain an equivalent result; priority is not independently established.

## Value — PASS
Determining all minimum circular attractors for a recursively defined finite word family is a natural analogue of a recent sharp *linear* attractor classification, with genuinely different minimizers under boundary wrapping. The construction gives a general morphism lifting principle, an exact all-order minimum, five explicit optimal pair families, and a complete exact finite extremizer classification through length 8192. The restriction of the exact count to orders three through thirteen is explicit; no unjustified extrapolation is made. These are structural, motivated results rather than arbitrary numerical slices.
