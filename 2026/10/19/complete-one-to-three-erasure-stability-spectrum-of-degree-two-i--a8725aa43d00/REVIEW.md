# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The probability-normalized degree-two icosahedral sampler is a nine-column isometry by the spherical 5-design property; the complementary projector has the explicit dot-product kernel derived in the result. The eigenvalues of every deleted one-, two-, or three-row minor are proved analytically by direct diagonalization. The antipodal and product-sign cases partition all masks, and the associated 60/80/80 counts are established from the six opposite axes and twenty triangular faces. An exact symbolic checker verifies all 12 one-masks, 66 two-masks and 220 three-masks as well as the design moments and projection identities. The limitations are fixed nodes, degree at most two and equal original weights.

## Originality — PASS, subject to stated risk

Searches of published mathematical research records and primary spherical-harmonic sampling literature did not yield the exact seven-class icosahedral mask-level erasure spectrum or sharp worst recoverable triple. Hitchin's 2007 work is concerned with harmonic cubics vanishing at icosahedral vertices. General irregular spherical sampling papers study different settings. A September 2026 finite-defect paper gives a relevant general Naimark-complement coupling in its abstract; the specific erasure mask table was not in available text, but the full body was inaccessible, so an unstated stronger specialization remains a residual originality risk. The kernel identity itself is established mathematics and is not claimed as new.

## Value — PASS

The twelve-point icosahedral design has only three sample redundancies for nine low-degree modes. The exact classification shows that losing an opposite pair destroys unique reconstruction while all other pairs—and precisely those three-point masks without an opposite pair—remain recoverable, with exact condition numbers. The 80 worst admissible patterns and their precise amplification factor provide a natural finite, mathematically motivated robustness invariant useful for placement of redundant spherical observations.
