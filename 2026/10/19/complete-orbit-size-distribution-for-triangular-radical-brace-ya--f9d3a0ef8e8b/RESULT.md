# Complete orbit-size distribution for triangular radical-brace Yang–Baxter solutions

## Finding

For every integer \(n\geq2\) and every prime power \(q\), let \(R_n(q)\) be the algebra of strictly upper-triangular \(n\)-by-\(n\) matrices over \(\mathbb F_q\). Its nilpotent multiplication defines a two-sided brace by \(A\circ B=A+B+AB\). Let \(r\) be the canonical involutive set-theoretic Yang–Baxter solution on its underlying set, with left permutations \(\lambda_A(B)=B+AB\). The indecomposable components of this solution are precisely the orbits of these permutations.

The following **finite two-state-transition rule gives the complete component-size spectrum in every dimension and every finite characteristic**. Start with a weighted state \((d,s)=(0,0)\) of weight \(1\). For each integer \(j=n-1,n-2,\ldots,2\), simultaneously replace every state \((d,s)\) of weight \(w\) by:

\[
 (d,s+d)\text{ of weight }wq^d,\qquad
 (d+1,s+d+1)\text{ of weight }w(q^{n-j}-q^d).
\]

Discard zero-weight successors and merge duplicate states by adding weights. After the last stage, multiply each state weight by \(q^{n-1}\). Let \(a_s(n,q)\) be the sum of all resulting weights with second coordinate \(s\). Then **the exact number of indecomposable components of cardinality \(q^s\)** is

\[
\boxed{c_s(n,q)=a_s(n,q)/q^s.}
\]

No other component cardinalities occur. In particular, there are exactly \(q^{n-1}\) fixed-point components. The largest component size is

\[
\boxed{q^{(n-1)(n-2)/2},}
\]

and exactly \(q(q-1)^{n-2}\) components attain that size (also valid for \(n=2\), when all components have size \(1\)). The probability that two uniformly and independently chosen elements belong to the same component is exactly

\[
\boxed{\mathcal P_{n,q}=q^{-n(n-1)}\sum_s a_s(n,q)q^s.}
\]

For each fixed \(n\ge2\), this probability satisfies \(\mathcal P_{n,q}\sim q^{-(n-1)}\) as \(q\) ranges through increasing prime powers.

The first nontrivial explicit case, \(n=4\), has the complete component spectrum

\[
\boxed{
(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3)=
\bigl(q^3,\ q^2(q^2-1),\ q^2(q-1),\ q(q-1)^2\bigr).
}
\]

Hence the total number of indecomposable components is \(q^4+3q^3-4q^2+q\) for \(n=4\). The components have respective sizes \(1,q,q^2,q^3\). This is an enumeration of components, not of brace isomorphism types or of arbitrary solutions whose permutation brace is isomorphic to this brace.

## Assumptions and scope

The integer \(n\) is at least \(2\); \(q\) is a positive prime power. Matrix multiplication is the usual associative product over \(\mathbb F_q\). The ring \(R_n(q)\) is nilpotent, generally noncommutative, and has no multiplicative identity. Nilpotence makes \(\circ\) a group operation, with identity the zero matrix and inverses given by finite geometric series; the additive group remains abelian. Thus \(R_n(q)\) is a two-sided brace. The canonical involutive nondegenerate Yang–Baxter solution is the standard one associated with this left brace; the permutation group generated by its first-coordinate maps is generated by the \(\lambda_A\).

A component means an orbit of that permutation group on the solution set, rather than an orbit under conjugation or under the full automorphism group of the unitriangular group. Set \(N=n(n-1)/2\), so \(|R_n(q)|=q^N\).

## Proof

**1. The action and its orbit size.** For every \(A,B\in R_n(q)\), the brace identity gives

\[
\lambda_A(B)=-A+(A\circ B)=B+AB.
\]

Therefore the complete orbit of \(B\), since \(A\) ranges over all of \(R_n(q)\), is \(B+R_n(q)B\). The map \(L_B:A\mapsto AB\) is \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear, whence the orbit has cardinality \(q^{\operatorname{rank} L_B}\). This set is an orbit because the \(\lambda_A\) are exactly the left action of the multiplicative brace group, and every \(A\) occurs.

**2. Rank decomposition by rows.** Regard each row of \(B\) as a vector in \(\mathbb F_q^n\), with the forbidden entries zero. For \(j=2,\ldots,n\), let \(W_j(B)\) be the span of rows numbered \(j,j+1,\ldots,n\), and put \(d_j(B)=\dim W_j(B)\). In particular \(d_n(B)=0\). Row \(i\) of \(AB\) can be chosen independently as an arbitrary vector in \(W_{i+1}(B)\), by varying exactly the entries \(A_{i,j}\) with \(j>i\). Different input rows of \(A\) govern different output rows of \(AB\), so their images form a direct sum. Consequently

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}L_B=\sum_{j=2}^{n}d_j(B).}
\]

This equality is valid without generic-rank assumptions, in every characteristic, and including zero rows.

**3. Exhaustive row-flag enumeration.** Begin at the bottom with \(W_n=\{0\}\). In descending order, row \(j\), where \(2\le j\le n-1\), lies in the \((n-j)\)-dimensional coordinate subspace supported on columns strictly greater than \(j\). The previously chosen \(W_{j+1}\) is a subspace of that coordinate subspace and has dimension \(d=d_{j+1}\). Of the \(q^{n-j}\) possible new rows, precisely \(q^d\) already belong to \(W_{j+1}\); they leave the new dimension at \(d\). The other \(q^{n-j}-q^d\) increase it to \(d+1\). In either case the new dimension is added to the sum in step 2. This proves the two transitions and the multiplicities in the Finding. Row \(1\) has \(q^{n-1}\) unconstrained choices and does not enter the rank sum, proving the final multiplier. The transition enumerates every matrix exactly once. Dividing the number of matrices of rank \(s\) by the common orbit size \(q^s\) therefore proves the formula for \(c_s\).

**4. Extremal sizes.** Since \(d_j\le n-j\), the rank cannot exceed \(\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}(n-j)=(n-1)(n-2)/2\). This bound is achieved by choosing successively independent rows, equivalently \(d_j=n-j\) for all \(j\). The number of corresponding matrices is

\[
q^{n-1}\prod_{j=2}^{n-1}\bigl(q^{n-j}-q^{n-j-1}\bigr)
=q^{n-1+(n-2)(n-3)/2}(q-1)^{n-2}.
\]

Division by the maximal orbit size simplifies to \(q(q-1)^{n-2}\) maximal components. At the other extreme, rank zero forces every row numbered \(2\) or higher to vanish, leaving exactly \(q^{n-1}\) matrices, each fixed by every \(\lambda_A\).

**5. Collision probability and asymptotic.** There are \(c_s\) components with \(q^s\) elements each. The probability that two independent uniform choices lie in one component is

\[
\sum_s c_s(q^s/q^N)^2
=q^{-2N}\sum_s a_s q^s,
\]

as claimed. With \(n\) fixed and \(q\to\infty\), the all-increase row profile occurs for a fraction \(1+O_n(q^{-1})\) of matrices and has rank \((n-1)(n-2)/2\); all other ranks are no larger. Thus \(\sum_B q^{\operatorname{rank}L_B}=q^{N+(n-1)(n-2)/2}(1+O_n(q^{-1}))\), giving \(\mathcal P_{n,q}=q^{-(n-1)}(1+O_n(q^{-1}))\). For \(n=2\) the identity is exact.

**6. The four-row spectrum.** When \(n=4\), the two nontrivial successive spans depend only on the three entries \(B_{23},B_{24},B_{34}\). Their rank sum is \(3\) when \(B_{23}B_{34}\ne0\), \(2\) when \(B_{23}=0\) and \(B_{34}\ne0\), \(1\) when \(B_{34}=0\) and \((B_{23},B_{24})\ne(0,0)\), and \(0\) otherwise. The three free first-row entries supply a common factor \(q^3\). Counting each case and dividing by the appropriate orbit size yields the displayed four coefficients. This is the complete classification of component cardinalities for \(n=4\).

## Verification

The standalone `verify.py` constructs the original matrix-product action \(B\mapsto B+AB\). It computes orbit ranks by directly multiplying each strictly upper-triangular matrix unit by each tested \(B\) and applying finite-field Gaussian elimination, independently of the row-flag formula. It compares the resulting rank histogram with the stated recurrence. In the indicated small cases it also exhaustively constructs all distinct orbits using the original \(\lambda\)-action and compares the actual orbit sizes and multiplicities. A genuine \(\mathbb F_4\) implementation uses reduction modulo \(t^2+t+1\), not integer arithmetic modulo four.

Checked cases: \((n,q)=(2,2),(3,2),(4,2),(4,3),(4,4),(5,2),(6,2)\), with full group-action orbit enumeration in the first four cases. Every check passed. The general proof is symbolic; these finite checks cannot establish novelty or the unrestricted theorem on their own.

## Relationship to prior work

Ferrara, Trombetti and Tsang's 2026 preprint introduces probabilistic measures involving indecomposable components of finite set-theoretic Yang–Baxter solutions. Its publicly indexed abstract does not specify a component-size distribution for the strict upper-triangular radical-brace family. The present result instead computes the **complete component spectrum** and the resulting same-component probability uniformly for all matrix sizes and finite fields. Because the full preprint could not be inspected, possible coverage under different terminology remains an explicit originality risk.

Aragona, Gavioli, Iannaccone and Nozzi discuss radical-ring braces among broader near-ring constructions; the brace construction itself is classical and is not claimed as new. Fuchs and Kirillov compute coadjoint orbits of a quotient of the unitriangular group; their action is neither the left-multiplication action \(B\mapsto B+AB\) nor on the same full matrix algebra. De Melo and Kato study automorphism-group orbits, also a different equivalence relation. No checked comparison proves global priority over all literature.

## Limitations

The spectrum applies only to the canonical solution on the complete triangular radical brace, not every Yang–Baxter solution whose permutation brace is a quotient or subgroup of it. The proof relies on unrestricted independent entries in every strictly upper-triangular position; it does not automatically apply to arbitrary pattern algebras, triangular matrix algebras with imposed relations, or weighted actions. The recurrence gives exact finite calculations, but is not presented as a closed factored polynomial in \(n\) and \(q\). Full-text priority comparison with the 2026 motivating preprint remains outstanding; no independent research audit has been performed.
