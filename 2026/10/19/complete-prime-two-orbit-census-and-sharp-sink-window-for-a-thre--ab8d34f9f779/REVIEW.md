# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact lifting from the scalar cubic to the three-dimensional period-two equations proves that the census is exhaustive. Subtracting and adding the two scalar equations separates the symmetric and nonsymmetric possibilities without omitting any real states. Direct Jacobian multiplication gives distinct cubic Floquet polynomials for those classes. The strict Schur test gives the exact attracting interval, and monotonicity plus determinant fixes the nonsymmetric expanding pair. The standalone checker reproduces the symbolic identities and exact sample orbits. It does not claim to prove an infinite statement by finite experiment.

**Originality — PASS (best-knowledge assessment).** The mathematical sections and conclusions of Wang et al. describe fixed-point spectra and numerical bifurcations but not this complete primitive-period-two and three-dimensional Floquet classification. Specific published nearest matches involve a Duffing planar map and a Bogdanov planar quadratic map; their statements do not cover this parameter family, nor do they imply the cubic Floquet identities. Searches included exact author/title/map, period-two/prime-two, cubic-factor reduction, and alternate stability terms. A differently formulated earlier derivation remains possible; online search is not a priority certificate.

**Value — PASS.** The entire three-positive-parameter family has a natural two-cycle census governed by one structural combination, with two distinct exact orbit-creation boundaries. More importantly, the exact three-dimensional Floquet polynomial yields a sharp stable-cycle window and shows that the two later-created cycles are always saddle-foci. That is a genuine structural distinction from scalar periodic-point counting and gives a precise interpretation of periodic regimes in the originating study. Neutral behavior and higher-period regimes remain outside scope.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Wang et al., *Symmetry* 15 (2023), article 1879 (DOI 10.3390/sym15101879), especially §2.1, §3.1 and §5. General Jury stability theory is standard; no originality is claimed for that test. Prior resultaries involving the Duffing and Bogdanov maps use different dynamical systems. This assessment does not determine the nonlinear character of the unit-circle boundary or prove exhaustive historical priority.
