# Review of the polygonal rotation-number distribution

## Correctness — PASS
The statement uses the polygonal foliations actually established for resonant angles, not an unproved foliation for generic angles. The original source's linearity of the rotation number along a break-line segment identifies the affine interpolation between consecutive distinguished levels. A shoelace calculation produces the area as a quadratic in the break-line intersection parameter; differentiating this area gives the full probability density. Its positive zero-end density yields an inverse-rotation Pareto-one tail, the exact moment cutoff, and the logarithmic truncation law. The expansion of the original implicit resonance equation gives the asserted two-scale coefficient. Polygon arithmetic and high-precision parameter checks are replicated by a standard-library numerical verifier. Finite tests alone do not prove the infinite statements.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge assessment)
The 2013 original source establishes the map, the resonant parameter family, the nested polygonal invariant circles, and linear interpolation of their rotation numbers; these facts are credited and not claimed new. A prior exact area law for the innermost polygon fixes the normalization but not the area distribution at all intermediate rotation levels. The complete area-pushforward distribution and its sharp tail and moment consequences address a distinct invariant. Comparisons of the motivating article's full sections and appendix, the author's thesis chapter, related planar affine-map literature, mathematical search results, and accessible earlier claims found no broader statement that already includes this full law. An unindexed calculation is a genuine historical-priority risk; full text of one older, related but different twist-map reference was unavailable.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The family supplies a natural system where every point of a positive-area annulus has an invariant rotation number, making its Lebesgue distribution a meaningful statistical invariant. The full piecewise-quadratic law gives quantitatively the fraction of area turning at any prescribed speed, while the heavy tail distinguishes a typical speed from a divergent mean winding-time proxy. The \(K\)-dependent exact constant and sharp resonant scaling link geometric accumulation to the distribution's singular endpoint. This is not a rephrasing of the old single-polygon area theorem or a statement about an arbitrary scalar slice.
