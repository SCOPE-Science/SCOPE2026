# Complete two-generator dimension profile of \(\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb F_2)\)

## Finding

For the finite Lie algebra \(L=\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb F_2)\), let \(d(A,B)\) be the dimension of the Lie subalgebra generated by the ordered pair \((A,B)\). The exact distribution across **all** ordered pairs is:

| \(d(A,B)\) | Ordered pairs |
|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1 |
| 1 | 765 |
| 2 | 3,618 |
| 3 | 7,392 |
| 4 | 20,160 |
| 5 | 20,160 |
| 6 | 0 |
| 7 | 0 |
| 8 | 13,440 |

Consequently \(\Pr(\langle A,B\rangle_{\mathrm{Lie}}=L)=105/512\). There are exactly \(80\) orbits of generating ordered pairs under simultaneous conjugation by \(\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\).

The dimension gap is not simply absence of larger proper subalgebras: the stabilizer of a one-dimensional vector subspace of \(\mathbb F_2^3\) is a six-dimensional Lie subalgebra, but is not generated by two elements.

## Assumptions and scope

All matrices are over \(\mathbb F_2\), and the bracket is the ordinary matrix Lie bracket \([A,B]=AB-BA=AB+BA\). A traceless \(3\)-by-\(3\) matrix is represented by a nine-bit integer in row-major order, with the trace-zero condition requiring the XOR of bits \(0,4,8\) to vanish. There are exactly \(2^8=256\) such matrices. Every ordered pair is included, including zero and linearly dependent pairs; the generated Lie subalgebra is taken over \(\mathbb F_2\), not over an extension field. Only the fixed pair \((n,q)=(3,2)\) is asserted.

## Proof

The proof is a finite exhaustive calculation with complete source code in `verify.py`. The following facts establish that the calculation is a proof of the stated finite classification rather than a sample.

**Finite domain.** The verifier iterates over all \(2^9=512\) three-by-three binary matrices, retaining those with trace zero. This yields a set \(L\) of exactly \(256\) matrices and hence exactly \(256^2=65{,}536\) ordered pairs, with no pruning or sampling. Multiplication is calculated entrywise as \((AB)_{ij}=\sum_{k=0}^2 A_{ik}B_{kj}\pmod 2\). The bracket table is calculated for every pair, checked to remain traceless, to satisfy alternation, and to be symmetric in characteristic two.

**Exact Lie generation, first algorithm.** For each ordered pair \((A,B)\), the program begins with a binary echelon basis for their linear span. It repeatedly takes brackets of every pair of accepted basis vectors, adding each bracket when linearly independent. Because the list grows monotonically in an eight-dimensional vector space, the loop terminates. It returns the dimension of a linear space containing \(A,B\) and closed under all brackets of a basis, hence under all brackets by bilinearity. Minimality follows inductively: every added vector is a bracket of elements in the Lie subalgebra generated by the initial pair. Therefore the final subspace is exactly the generated Lie subalgebra.

**Second calculation.** An independently organized routine closes the span of \(A,B\) under the two linear operators \(\operatorname{ad}A\) and \(\operatorname{ad}B\), rather than under all basis-pair brackets. By the Jacobi identity, invariance under both operators implies invariance under all adjoint operators generated by them, so its result is again exactly \(\langle A,B\rangle_{\mathrm{Lie}}\). These two routines produce identical dimension histograms for the entire \(65{,}536\)-pair domain. The common histogram is precisely the table displayed above, including zero at dimensions six and seven; summing its entries independently recovers the complete pair count. The standard-library code is a short, directly inspectable finite certificate and returns `VERIFY_OK` only if all assertions pass.

**Existence of a six-dimensional subalgebra.** Matrices stabilizing the coordinate line \(\mathbb F_2 e_1\) have entries \(A_{21}=A_{31}=0\). These impose two independent linear equations inside traceless \(3\)-by-\(3\) matrices, giving dimension \(6\). The stabilizer is Lie-closed since it is an associative subalgebra of upper block-triangular shape intersected with the traceless subspace. The verifier independently counts its \(64\) elements, verifies closure under all brackets and checks that every pair inside it generates dimension at most five. This shows the dimension-six generation gap is substantive.

**Generating witness.** In the row-major nine-bit representation, \(A=10\) and \(B=70\) generate all of \(L\); explicitly these are the binary matrices with rows \((010,100,000)\) and \((011,000,100)\), respectively. Both closure procedures check this witness.

**Simultaneous-conjugacy orbit count.** Direct enumeration shows \(\lvert\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\rvert=168\). If a \(g\in\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\) fixes a generating ordered pair by conjugation, it commutes with all elements of their generated Lie algebra. In particular it commutes with all six off-diagonal matrix units; a direct exhaustive centralizer check shows the only such \(g\) is the identity. (The same follows by the elementary-matrix centralizer argument.) Thus simultaneous conjugation acts freely on generating pairs. Orbit-stabilizer gives \(13{,}440/168=80\) orbits.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` with a normal Python 3 interpreter and no third-party packages. It deterministically produces `VERIFY_OK`, the complete histogram, \(13{,}440\) generators, the \(168\)-element conjugating group and \(80\) free orbits. The package was checked by extracting `verify.py` from its own embedded content and replaying it. The conclusion is exact for this finite domain; no numerical approximation, probabilistic sampling or extrapolation is used.

## Relationship to prior work

Jezernik and Zozaya (arXiv:2606.31747, publicly submitted 30 June 2026) prove high-probability two-generation for growing special-linear Lie algebras and highlight the difficulty of small characteristic and fixed small fields; their theorem does not determine the exact \(3\)-by-\(3\) binary generation distribution. Cantor, Jezernik and Zozaya (arXiv:2404.11240) prove two-generation outside exceptional parameter pairs and analyze specific exceptional cases, without this exact dimension profile, probability or conjugacy-orbit count. Bowman, Towers and Varea (arXiv:0704.2723) study qualitative properties of two-generated subalgebras rather than enumerating binary traceless-matrix Lie generation fibers. The current result is a fixed, exact small-field complement to these broader theorems, not an alternative proof of their asymptotic results.

## Limitations

No formula is asserted for other field sizes, for other matrix ranks, or for the number of Lie subalgebras of each dimension. In particular, zero pair-counts in dimensions six and seven do **not** assert that all subalgebras of those dimensions are absent. The result is an exhaustive finite computation with source-code transparency but not a formally machine-certified theorem. A semantically equivalent finite census in unindexed literature remains possible; independent mathematical review has not been performed.
