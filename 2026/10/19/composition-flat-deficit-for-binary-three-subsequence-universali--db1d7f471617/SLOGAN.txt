For binary words with at least three zeros and three ones, the number missing at least one scattered subsequence of length three is exactly \(3(n-2)\), independently of how the \(n\) symbols are split between zeros and ones. Thus the number of three-universal words with \(k\) ones is \(\binom nk-3(n-2)\) whenever \(3\leq k\leq n-3\), and zero otherwise.
