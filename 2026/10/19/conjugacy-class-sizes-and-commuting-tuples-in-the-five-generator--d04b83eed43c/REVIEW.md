# Scientific review: conjugacy-class sizes of the five-generator free left-regular-band radical group

## Correctness
PASS. Saliola's proven path-algebra realization supplies the quiver with 32 vertices and 194 arrows. A new explicit enumeration of intermediate subsets partitions all 120 participating arrows into twenty isolated six-arrow blocks, with 100 independent length-two concatenations, leaving 74 two-sided annihilator arrows. The nilpotent radical therefore has dimension 294 and splits as ten rectangular blocks, ten opposites, and a 74-dimensional zero-product summand. Group inversion identifies the opposites. The conjugacy-class polynomial is derived by calculating the image ranks 0, 1 and 5 of the Heisenberg commutator and dividing each element count by its orbit size; the all-arity formula partitions tuples according to whether their scalar coordinate vector is zero. The finite checker confirms every quiver count and exhaustively checks small-field tuple and rank cases. General assertions rest on the proof, not on extrapolating those checks.

## Originality
PASS on the compared literature, subject to the stated substantive risk. The 2006 quiver/path-algebra theorem is established prior work and is explicitly credited. The 2022 quiver-Higman theorem proves radical-preserving splitting and counts commuting **pairs** for short acyclic quivers. Our two-tuple count is covered in principle by that work, and it is not independently claimed as new. The finer class-size **distribution** (as opposed to the total number of conjugacy classes), its precise missing exponents, and arbitrary-arity commuting counts were not stated in that inspected work or in the invariant-ring study of Brauner–Commins–Reiner. The new calculation applies Saliola's multiplicities to identify the exact block inventory, then computes invariants finer than pair totals. The strongest residual risk is that known general radical-splitting methods, together with standard rectangular Heisenberg orbit theory, could already yield these specific distributions elsewhere; this is not a claim of exhaustive uniqueness.

## Value
PASS. The full conjugacy-class-size distribution is a natural refinement of the class-number problem for unipotent groups, explicitly connected to the quiver analogue of Higman's conjecture. It has an exact answer for a naturally defined 294-dimensional radical with twenty interacting potential intermediate supports, and it identifies specific holes in the spectrum of allowed conjugacy-class sizes. The simultaneous all-arity commuting classification and characteristic-independent block structure make the result reusable. The five-generator case is the last rank for which the free-band quiver radical is two-step; at six generators, length-three paths appear. This is a motivated structural cutoff, not an arbitrary selection of field sizes.

## Closest sources and limits
