# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For the exactly specified six independent directed-pair noises, the centered projection has diagonal entries \(2/3\). This gives the exact conditional second-moment identity (1). The three-point distance identity transforms nonlinear attenuation into a strict state-dependent drift at \(\rho=1\). Convexity and Jensen yield the stated inverse-linear upper bound, and a nonnegative-supermartingale argument gives almost-sure vanishing disagreement. Below the threshold, the opinion mean is an \(L^2\)-bounded martingale, completing individual random-limit consensus. The proof distinguishes an analytic all-state conclusion from 58 finite exact checks.

## Originality — PASS, subject to residual priority risk

The closest primary article is Shang (2023), DOI 10.1093/comnet/cnad009, whose Theorem 2 requires strict small-gain conditions. Its theorem and proof were compared with the exact critical variance-neutral endpoint here; they do not themselves cover that endpoint. Related deterministic higher-order consensus and continuous-time multiplicative-noise theories use different assumptions. Semantic mathematical literature-record searches and recoverable historical mathematical claims revealed no covering statement. This does not rule out an equivalent theorem elsewhere, and an earlier related full manuscript remained inaccessible.

## Value — PASS

A single three-agent hyperedge is the smallest nonpairwise group interaction and a natural test of the sharp stochastic-noise boundary. The exact criterion \(\gamma_c=3/(9+4\kappa^2)\) and the recovered critical \(O(t^{-1})\) mean-square agreement quantify a substantive mechanism: decreasing group response changes the endpoint relative to constant modulation. Results are explicitly confined to second-moment disagreement at criticality and local one-step expected amplification beyond it.

## Closest literature and limitations

The proof does not establish a finite common random opinion limit at the critical gain, nor does it establish long-run divergence above it. The noise law assumes independence between all ordered pairs. Relevant uninspected broader nonlinear stochastic Lyapunov theory could supersede the novelty comparison. Independent validation has not been carried out.
