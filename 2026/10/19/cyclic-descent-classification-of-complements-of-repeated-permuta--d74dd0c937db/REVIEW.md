# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof derives the forced retained-block segmentation directly from the distinct-letter permutation condition. Its minimization of the required deleted letters gives an exact necessary-and-sufficient condition, not merely a construction. The row-difference derivation of the determinant and formal coefficient extraction justify the complete counting formula. All boundary cases, including zero deletions and empty complements, are handled. Standalone exhaustive deletion-set comparisons (117 cases) and independent transfer counts (4480 cases) corroborate the theorem. Finite tests are not presented as its proof.

**Originality — PASS within examined evidence.** The March 2026 paper by Adamson, Fleischmann, and Huch introduces these complement sets and gives general algorithms and structural results; its accessible full text does not state the cyclic-rotation/terminal-descents classification or this exact count for nested powers of a permutation. Liang, Sagan, and Zhuang investigate cyclic permutation shuffle-compatibility, which concerns different objects: shuffles of disjoint permutations rather than complements after deleting a subsequence from a repeated word. Actual published-record searches were made for complement scattered factors, cyclic rotations, periodic permutation powers, and cyclic descents; related current findings were also searched. Abstract Eulerian-type coefficients are not claimed as new. Residual risk: differently phrased or unindexed prior work.

**Value — PASS.** The new complement-scattered-factor notion motivates structural evaluation of complement sets beyond brute-force embedding enumeration. The theorem supplies a complete infinite-parameter classification and a closed enumeration with a sharp descent cutoff. It converts a potentially many-embedding problem into a finite-state descent criterion and determines when all \(q^r\) rotation-block outputs are achievable. It is not an arbitrary finite table or a routine restatement of the parent algorithms.

**Closest literature and limitations.** D. Adamson, P. Fleischmann, A. Huch, arXiv:2603.20790v1, Sections 2–4; J. Liang, B. E. Sagan, Y. Zhuang, *Annals of Combinatorics* 28 (2024), 615–654. Only whole-power deletions of distinct-letter permutation blocks are treated. Embedding multiplicities and partial-period deletions are not determined. The supplied finite tests are corroboration, not a substitute for the universal proof.
