# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The projective radial generator is normalized in physical time and dimensionless time explicitly. The positive Green operator and weighted-average lemma provide an analytic proof of the finite-threshold Laplace inequality and of the exact first coefficient. The second-moment identity and explicit mean yield the CV deficit. The separate high-precision hypergeometric checker passed 186 assertions; these corroborate, and do not replace, the proof. A hidden change of diffusion-time convention would alter raw means but not the mean-normalized theorem, and the formula fixes that convention precisely.

## Originality — PASS, with bounded-search uncertainty
Tóth (2026) already provides the exact mean, spectral density and exponential far-time tail, while D'Onofrio–Tamborrino–Lansky (2018) already provide generic Jacobi first-passage transforms and moments. Aldous's established almost-exponential rare-set theory rules out claiming generic limit-law novelty. The assessed contribution is only the explicit sharp harmonic-number first correction, one-sided quantitative bound, and variance deficit for the specified projective quantum-state process. The latter follow from a nontrivial weighted positive-resolvent comparison. Generic transform consequences and uninspected full texts remain a genuine possible implicit-coverage risk, not proof of exhaustive novelty.

## Value — PASS
A time-to-fidelity benchmark needs more than a mean: it needs the shape and reliability of waiting times at extremely high fidelity. The theorem provides both an explicit finite-threshold accuracy guarantee and an exact dimension-dependent first deviation from exponential behavior. The model remains idealized; it is not a statement about arbitrary physical preparation controls or finite-sample measurement error.
