# Review of the conditional Dirichlet phase-clock theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is over finite substochastic matrices whose reachable communicating-class blocks are primitive and whose maximal reachable Perron root is strictly between zero and one. The proof first decomposes every surviving length-\(n\) trajectory uniquely by its directed component path and intracomponent residence counts. Perron projectors on critical blocks and convergent matrix resolvents \((I-A_i/\lambda)^{-1}\) on subcritical blocks yield the exact leading constant and power \(n^{d-1}\). Uniform composition counts produce the joint Dirichlet distribution; geometric summability keeps every subcritical sojourn tight. Finite-state Perron-Doob ergodicity turns the critical-block residence fractions into the asserted random empirical-state measure. The proof addresses the \(d=1\) degenerate boundary separately and excludes imprimitive blocks. An independent exact-rational enumeration program checks the stated five-state example (12 direct survival/path identities, 24 route weights, 478 residence moments) and a two-state nonscalar Perron projector (12 powers) and a genuinely two-state subcritical detour (16 exact resolvent/path comparisons). The code checks finite cases only; the analytical argument proves the universal theorem.

## Originality — PASS, with a priority risk

Van Doorn and Pollett (2009) establish quasi-stationary distributions and terminal conditioned laws, not distributions of empirical occupation or multiple macroscopic transition times. Colonius and Rasmussen (2020, Theorems 3.14 and 3.20) establish **expectations** of empirical occupation counts, not the conditional weak limit of the random empirical measure; even a complete expectation limit cannot imply a Dirichlet distribution or nonzero limiting variance. Their Proposition 3.7 contains a closely related composition asymptotic that is explicitly credited. The present claim advances it to a joint distributional phase-clock theorem, distinguishes nonconcentration from the deterministic quasi-ergodic mean, and allows arbitrary primitive matrix-valued subcritical blocks (instead of their scalar restriction). Champagnat and Villemonais (2022, Theorem 4.1) develop reducible semigroup and terminal QSD asymptotics for broader state spaces, but do not assert the present conditional random occupation law. Dietz and Sethuraman (2007) obtain Dirichlet occupation laws in time-inhomogeneous chains, a different model. Searches for survival-conditioned simplex occupation laws, quasi-ergodic Dirichlet distributions, and finite-state maximal critical paths found no encompassing statement. The mechanism is related to established Perron/composition methods, so an earlier unindexed full-distribution theorem remains a genuine historical-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Conditioned survival is a standard observable in open symbolic dynamics and killed Markov evolution; its expected time average has an established quasi-ergodic theory. It is meaningful to ask whether a typical survivor actually approaches that average. The theorem answers **no** whenever a maximal surviving route uses two or more critical classes and identifies the entire limiting distribution, not just a counterexample. It also supplies exact route probabilities, universal order-statistic transition clocks, and a matrix-resolvent formula for subcritical bottlenecks, directly tested in a second six-state example with a non-scalar primitive subcritical block. The five-state example has two equally likely distinct routes and a nonzero limiting class-occupation variance of \(1/18\), demonstrating a genuinely different statistical phenomenon from expected occupation. General imprimitive/countable-state extensions remain open; no claim of their resolution is made.
