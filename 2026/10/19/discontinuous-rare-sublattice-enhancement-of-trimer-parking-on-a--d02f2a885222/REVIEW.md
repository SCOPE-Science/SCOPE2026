# Review of the alternating-rate trimer-parking theorem

## Correctness — PASS

Exact generator counting gives the stated empty-interval hierarchy: an intersecting length-three deposition requires a union of the observed interval with lengths \(n\), \(n+1\), or \(n+2\). The bounded infinite hierarchy admits a unique solution by factorial iteration of the off-diagonal integral inequalities. Its two-periodic ansatz produces the three linear ODEs. The proof of the singular weak-rate limit explicitly changes variables to the slow scale and uses dominated convergence with \(0\le A,B_s\le1\); it does not equate fixed-time convergence with infinite-time convergence. The autonomous zero-rate and equal-rate reductions agree with exact formulas. The packaged code checked all relevant union coefficients for interval sizes 2 through 17 and matched independent finite-ring simulations. These checks are supportive, not substitutes for the infinite-volume argument.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Actual targeted searches covered trimer deposition on periodic heterogeneous one-dimensional substrates, rare-flux jamming, nonuniform adsorption and equivalent range-two exclusion language. The 2016 Privman–Yan full preprint (arXiv:1606.01823, Sections 1–3) concerns positive landing-site width and an exact rescaling to continuum parking, not activation of an odd-start clock at exact lattice locations. Rodgers (1992) gives competitive different-length rods, not period-two flux for one length. Zuppa–Ciacera–Zgrablich (1999) address cooperative adsorption on heterogeneous topographies, and the accessible abstract does not imply this explicit limit. Percus (2000) treats nonuniform flux with next-neighbor exclusion and is the closest unresolved bibliographic risk because full text was inaccessible. Accessible prior findings on complete-graph adsorption, multipartite dimers and forced arrivals concern distinct graphs/particles, and do not imply the finite-range one-dimensional empty-interval singularity. No matching full theorem or dominating implication was identified; absence of search matches is not claimed to prove worldwide priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The precise fact is motivated by a physically natural patterned deposition substrate and noncommuting limits: a rare species can improve final coverage by a fixed \(3e^{-2}/2\) even as its rate vanishes. The sharp closed-form jump and exact three-variable reduction are structural, not an arbitrary parameter substitution or a known homogeneous density. The analytic result quantitatively distinguishes finite-time near-zero flux from terminal adsorption and is usable as a benchmark for heterogeneous deposition algorithms.

## Scientific limitations

Independent validation remains outstanding. The close Percus 2000 full text was not available, and a specialization of a broader old nonuniform-flux theorem remains a residual originality risk. No uniform relaxation estimate, finite-size crossover, or sample-to-sample limit distribution is proved.
