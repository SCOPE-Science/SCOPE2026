# Divisor-polynomial profiles for cyclic almost-abelian Lie algebras over finite fields

## Finding

Let \(q\) be any prime power, let \(m\ge4\), and let \(f\in\mathbb F_q[t]\) be monic of degree \(m\) and different from \(t^m\). Put \(V=\mathbb F_q[t]/(f)\) and define the \((m+1)\)-dimensional Lie algebra \(L_f=\mathbb F_qh\ltimes V\) by \([h,v]=tv\) and \([V,V]=0\). For each integer \(i\), let

\[
d_i(f)=\#\{g\mid f:g\text{ monic},\ \deg g=i\},\qquad
R_i(f)=\sum_{\substack{g\mid f\\\deg g=i}}\Phi_q(g),
\]

where \(\Phi_q(g)=|(\mathbb F_q[t]/(g))^\times|\) and \(\Phi_q(1)=1\). Outside \(0\le i\le m\), these numbers vanish. Let \({m\brack k}_q\) be the Gaussian binomial coefficient, zero for \(k\notin\{0,\ldots,m\}\). Then the two codimension-enumerating zeta polynomials, with both the zero and total subobject included, are

\[
\boxed{Z^{\le}_{L_f}(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}q^id_i(f)z^i+\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}{m\brack i-1}_qz^i,}
\]

\[
\boxed{Z^{\triangleleft}_{L_f}(z)=1+\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}d_{i-1}(f)z^i+\mathbf1_{t\mid f}\,qz.}
\]

Moreover, for \(0\le j\le m+1\) the number \(N_j\) of **ordered pairs** \((x,y)\in L_f^2\) whose generated Lie subalgebra has dimension \(j\) is exactly

\[
\boxed{N_j=\mathbf1_{j=0}+\mathbf1_{j=1}(q^m-1)(q+1)
+\mathbf1_{j=2}(q^m-1)(q^m-q)
+\mathbf1_{1\le j\le m+1}(q^2-1)q^mR_{j-1}(f).}
\]

In particular, the ordered-pair generation probability is

\[
\boxed{\Pr(\langle x,y\rangle_{\mathrm{Lie}}=L_f)
=(1-q^{-2})\prod_{P\mid f\text{ irreducible monic}}(1-q^{-\deg P}).}
\]

If \(f\) is irreducible, only generated dimensions \(0,1,2,m+1\) occur, with none in \(3,\ldots,m\). This **generation gap** holds in every characteristic.

## Assumptions and scope

The finite field may have arbitrary prime-power order and arbitrary characteristic. The semidirect action is **cyclic**: multiplication by the residue class of \(t\) on the polynomial quotient \(\mathbb F_q[t]/(f)\). No separability or squarefreeness of \(f\) is assumed. We exclude only \(f=t^m\) from the new enumeration claim because the zeta-polynomial portion for that standard maximal-class nilpotent family was previously obtained by Lee (2020/2022, Theorems 3.1 and 3.5); the same proof specializes correctly to that known case. We count vector-space subalgebras and ideals, not conjugacy classes, and the pairs are ordered with zero and repeated elements allowed.

For clarity, \(Z^\le(z)=\sum_{H\le L_f}z^{\operatorname{codim}H}\) and \(Z^\triangleleft(z)=\sum_{I\triangleleft L_f}z^{\operatorname{codim}I}\). The displayed polynomials are finite, rather than local zeta functions for integral Lie lattices. The invariant \(\Phi_q(g)\) counts units of a finite polynomial quotient and obeys \(\Phi_q(g)=q^{\deg g}\prod_{P\mid g}(1-q^{-\deg P})\), including repeated prime factors only once.

## Proof

**1. Stable subspaces.** As a module over \(\mathbb F_q[t]\), \(V\) is cyclic, with annihilator \((f)\). Every \(t\)-invariant vector subspace has the form \(W=(g)/(f)\) for a unique monic divisor \(g\) of \(f\). Indeed, its inverse image in \(\mathbb F_q[t]\) is an ideal containing \((f)\), hence is generated by such a \(g\). It has codimension \(\deg g\) inside \(V\). There are therefore exactly \(d_i(f)\) invariant subspaces of codimension \(i\).

**2. Subalgebras.** Every vector subspace \(H\subseteq V\) is an abelian subalgebra. If \(H\not\subseteq V\), its image in \(L_f/V\) is one-dimensional, and it has a unique description \(H=W\oplus\mathbb F_q(h+u)\), where \(W=H\cap V\) and \(u\) is specified modulo \(W\). Bracket closure holds exactly when \(tW\subseteq W\), because \([h+u,w]=tw\). For fixed invariant \(W=(g)/(f)\), there are \(|V/W|=q^{\deg g}\) distinct choices of \(u\bmod W\), and each corresponding \(H\) has codimension \(\deg g\) in \(L_f\). Meanwhile a vector subspace of dimension \(k\) in \(V\) has codimension \(m+1-k\) in \(L_f\), and there are \({m\brack k}_q\) such subspaces. This proves the subalgebra polynomial.

**3. Ideals.** An ideal \(I\subseteq V\) is precisely a \(t\)-invariant subspace, giving the term \(\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}d_{i-1}(f)z^i\). An ideal \(I\not\subseteq V\) is necessarily of the form \(W\oplus\mathbb F_q(h+u)\) from step 2 and must contain \([h+u,V]=tV\); this condition is also sufficient for idealhood because then \([h,I]\) and \([V,I]\) are in \(W\). If \(t\nmid f\), multiplication by \(t\) is invertible, hence \(W=V\) and \(I=L_f\). If \(t\mid f\), the image \(tV\) has codimension one, so \(W\supseteq tV\) is either \(tV\) or \(V\). The former gives exactly \(q\) codimension-one ideals; the latter gives \(L_f\). This proves the ideal polynomial.

**4. Pairs lying in the abelian ideal.** Write \(x=ah+u\) and \(y=bh+v\), with \(a,b\in\mathbb F_q\) and \(u,v\in V\). If \(a=b=0\), the generated Lie algebra is their ordinary span in \(V\). There is one rank-zero pair. There are \((q^m-1)(q+1)\) rank-one pairs, since each one-dimensional subspace has \(q^2-1\) ordered nonzero coefficient pairs. There are \((q^m-1)(q^m-q)\) linearly independent ordered pairs, giving rank two.

**5. Pairs with a nonzero exterior coordinate.** If \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\), an invertible linear change of the two generators gives one generator of the form \(h+u'\) and one generator proportional to \(w=av-bu\in V\). Their generated algebra is precisely \(\mathbb F_q(h+u')\oplus \mathbb F_q[t]w\): repeated brackets with \(h+u'\) produce \(tw,t^2w,\ldots\), while all brackets inside \(V\) vanish. Thus its dimension is \(1+\dim_{\mathbb F_q}(\mathbb F_q[t]w)\). For each of the \(q^2-1\) nonzero pairs \((a,b)\), the linear map \((u,v)\mapsto av-bu\) is onto \(V\) and has fibers of cardinality \(q^m\).

**6. Cyclic-vector distribution.** For any monic divisor \(g\mid f\) of degree \(i\), the elements \(w\in V\) whose \(\mathbb F_q[t]\)-annihilator is exactly \((g)\) are the classes \((f/g)c\pmod f\) with \(c\pmod g\) a unit. They number \(\Phi_q(g)\); the cyclic submodule they generate has dimension \(i\). Consequently exactly \(R_i(f)\) elements generate cyclic submodules of dimension \(i\). Multiplying these counts by \((q^2-1)q^m\) and adding step 4 proves the displayed complete pair profile. The identity \(\sum_{g\mid f}\Phi_q(g)=q^m\) also gives the total \(\sum_j N_j=q^{2(m+1)}\).

**7. Full generation and gap.** Only \(w\) with annihilator \((f)\) generate all of \(V\); their number is \(\Phi_q(f)\), giving \((q^2-1)q^m\Phi_q(f)\) generating pairs. Division by \(|L_f|^2=q^{2(m+1)}\) proves the probability formula. If \(f\) is irreducible of degree \(m\), its only monic divisors are \(1,f\), so in step 5 only dimensions \(1\) and \(m+1\) occur. Combining step 4 yields precisely the listed gap. All reasoning is field-characteristic independent.

## Verification

The accompanying standard-library program `verify.py` constructs all subspaces of each small test Lie algebra directly from row-reduced bases. It tests bracket closure against every basis vector to enumerate ideals, tests pairwise bracket closure to enumerate subalgebras, and repeatedly takes Lie brackets to determine the generated dimension of **every** ordered pair. Independently it enumerates monic polynomial divisors and unit classes and compares the three actual distributions against the displayed formulas. It reports `VERIFY_OK 11 independent finite-field configurations`, including degree-five cases over \(\mathbb F_2\) and several nonsemisimple or mixed polynomials. The nilpotent \(f=t^m\) tests serve only as known-case controls, not as novel claims. These finite checks supplement, and do not replace, the proof for all prime powers and degrees.

As a nonnilpotent example, take \(q=2\), \(m=5\), and \(f=t^5+t^2+1\) (irreducible). The subalgebra codimension counts are \((1,1,31,155,155,63,1)\), the ideal counts are \((1,1,0,0,0,0,1)\), and ordered-pair generated-dimension counts are \(N_0=1\), \(N_1=189\), \(N_2=930\), \(N_6=2976\) with all other \(N_j=0\). These sum to \(4096=|L_f|^2\).

## Relationship to prior work

Lee's 2026 study [1] enumerates the subalgebras and ideals of all solvable Lie algebras up to dimension four and motivates seeking formulas beyond low-dimensional tables. Lee's earlier work [2, Theorems 3.1 and 3.5] already gives exact ideal and subalgebra zeta polynomials for the nilpotent maximal-class algebras, which are precisely the excluded specialization \(f=t^m\). Thus those nilpotent zeta counts are **known**, not a new claim here. Brent--Gao--Lauder [3] establish the classical finite-field random-Krylov generation probability in a broad linear-map setting; in particular the multiplicative factor \(\Phi_q(f)/q^m\) is not claimed as independently new. The present result identifies the Lie subalgebras and ideals for the remaining cyclic almost-abelian family and computes the **full dimension-resolved ordered-pair Lie-generation distribution**, rather than only its endpoint probability. Jezernik--Zozaya [4] study random two-generation of special linear Lie algebras and do not give these solvable-family profiles.

## Limitations

The conclusions assume a cyclic adjoint module \(V\) and do not classify arbitrary noncyclic almost-abelian Lie algebras. The new claim excludes the previously enumerated pure nilpotent Jordan-block case. The complete pair profile follows from classical cyclic-module algebra, so an equivalent translation in the algebra-subalgebra literature could predate these formulas. Literature searches, including closely related publications, do not constitute a uniqueness proof. No independent mathematical validation has been performed. The finite verification program tests prime fields, not all extension fields; the arbitrary-prime-power conclusion is established by the proof.
