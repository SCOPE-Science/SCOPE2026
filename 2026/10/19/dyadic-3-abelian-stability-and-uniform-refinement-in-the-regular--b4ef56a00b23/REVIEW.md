# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem concerns factors at every starting position of the *infinite* regular paperfolding word, not a sampled prefix. Direct calculation gives the exact eight initial states and their eight explicit witnesses. The parity-separated recurrence \(T_{h+1}=\{(d,p,y,0),(d,x,p,1):(d,x,y,p)\in T_h\}\) represents every start of a longer dyadic factor and produces every allowed next state; by four iterations it saturates all \(3\times2^3=24\) states and thereafter is stationary. The four-block construction of all triple counts includes parity-dependent boundary corrections and a two-symbol suffix; the one- and two-gram counts are recovered without ambiguity. All possible parameter combinations have witnesses. Consequently an exact finite set image, not finite sampling, proves the values 32 and 36, the 12 coarse classes and their exact refinement sizes. The standalone program recalculates each integer image and compares the resulting signatures with independent direct factor-frequency counts for dyadic lengths from 16 to 1024; it returned `VERIFY_OK`. The limiting assertion for all \(k\ge5\) follows from the fixed-point identity and finite image stability, not from the finite prefix tests.

## Originality — PASS against inspected accessible sources

The closest classic paper, Madill and Rampersad, The abelian complexity of the paperfolding word, arXiv:1208.2856v1 (2012-08-14), https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.2856, establishes **ordinary Abelian** (one-letter/Parikh) complexity and its dyadic recurrences, not the three-letter-count equivalence or all fiber multiplicities. Its publicly available 14-page text was examined, including the definitions, main theorem, proof approach, conclusion and searched terminology. Parreau, Rigo, Rowland and Vandomme, A new approach to the 2-regularity of the ell-abelian complexity of 2-automatic sequences, arXiv:1405.3532v1 (2014-05-14), https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.3532 develops 2-Abelian complexity for **other morphic sequences** (period doubling and Thue–Morse), not 3-Abelian regular paperfolding; its full 44-page preprint was examined on the relevant introductory, proof, and concluding sections. Allouche, The number of factors in a paperfolding sequence (1992); OEIS A337120 https://oeis.org/A337120 tabulates the **number of ordinary factors**, a strictly finer classification; raw complexity \(4n\) is compatible with many 3-Abelian quotients. A 2015 presentation (Vandomme 2015 presentation, https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/176364/2/these_defense_vandomme.pdf) treats period-doubling 3-Abelian examples, but only paperfolding in the ordinary Abelian setting. A later analysis (Campbell, Currie and Rampersad, Reduced complexities for sequences over finite alphabets, arXiv:2509.16034 (2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16034) concerns a different reduction relation. The available previous regular-paperfolding 2-Abelian result of twelve dyadic classes is strictly weaker than the three-way multiplicity refinement here. Targeted scholarly literature searches and scientific comparisons with known weaker claims uncovered no equivalent or stronger implication. This is a best-knowledge literature assessment, not proof of global publication uniqueness; residual risk is unindexed work or an implicit theorem under an alternative coding.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The precise 3-Abelian complexity of the paperfolding word is a natural higher-order refinement of a classical invariant. The result classifies an *infinite* dyadic family of nontrivial word lengths, shows eventual stability at 36, identifies the exceptional length sixteen, and proves a uniform three-way refinement of each of twelve 2-Abelian classes, with exact weight counts. The small stable boundary automaton provides reusable structural control of three-gram data. The conclusion is not a routine restatement of a known lower-order complexity value, a finite table alone, or a mere parameter rename.

## Residual scientific risks and scope

The proof relies on the explicit finite 24-state signature image calculation; a reader should run the provided checker or enumerate the 192 combinations independently. It does not solve non-dyadic lengths, arbitrary paperfolding instructions or general \(r\)-Abelian complexities. An unindexed equivalent paper might exist despite the inspected primary sources and negative targeted searches. Exhaustive direct computer checks are corroborative and do not replace the recurrence proof. Independent review remains unperformed.
