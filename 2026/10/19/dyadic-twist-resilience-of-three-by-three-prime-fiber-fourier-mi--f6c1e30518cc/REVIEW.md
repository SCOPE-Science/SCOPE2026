# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The statement quantifies over every odd prime, every finite abelian two-group, and arbitrary character and point triples whose prime coordinates are separately injective. The proof is self-contained after two standard cyclotomic facts: disjoint cyclotomic fields of coprime conductors, and existence of primes above two in the integral cyclotomic ring. Irreducibility makes the coefficient vector of a putative determinant zero constant. Reduction at a dyadic prime converts that constant-vector constraint into even multiplicity of each of six affine permutation exponents. The normalized six-exponent enumeration gives a contradiction, including the boundary prime three. The finite checker enumerates exact Gaussian-integer coefficients rather than relying on floating-point ranks. The proof does not extrapolate those finite checks.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, with residual risk)

The closest established full-spark theorem is Chebotarev's prime-order result, in Tao's 2003 account. It treats a single prime cyclic factor and does not imply nonsingularity after independently twisting rows and columns by arbitrary finite dyadic characters. Alexeev–Cahill–Mixon address full-spark frames and prime-power structures; those results do not provide the stated nonprincipal mixed-factor minors. Krahmer–Pfander–Rashkov consider general finite abelian uncertainty, including composite-group singularities, without this quantified protected-minor claim. Zhou's 2025 tensor-product result concerns principal minors (possibly after column permutation), not independently selected arbitrary triples. Exact classifications on \(\mathbb Z/(2p)\), \(\mathbb Z/(p^2)\), and \(\mathbb F_p^2\) have different domains or a single dyadic factor. No inspected statement dominates the full theorem. Residual risk remains from older cyclotomic coefficient arguments or unindexed harmonic-frame literature; priority is not certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem isolates an unconditional, usable three-sample interpolation guarantee in every finite abelian dyadic extension of an odd-prime cyclic factor. It is structurally stronger than a one-dimensional prime Chebotarev minor because arbitrary dyadic character phases may vary independently across all nine matrix entries. It is not a routine prime-case restatement: ordinary composite Fourier matrices fail full spark, but this geometrically defined class of nonprincipal minors remains protected. The conclusion yields a uniform sparse-recovery obstruction and a precise theorem about mixed-order Fourier character tables, including unbounded auxiliary group order and exponent.

## Limits and relevant comparisons

The theorem does not classify all minors on the product and does not prove novelty against every historical proof of a tensor Chebotarev-type lemma. The published \(2p\)-case covers only the auxiliary group of order two. The 2025 tensor-product paper is mathematically close, but its stated principal-minor hypotheses and conclusions are not this free row/column three-by-three result.
