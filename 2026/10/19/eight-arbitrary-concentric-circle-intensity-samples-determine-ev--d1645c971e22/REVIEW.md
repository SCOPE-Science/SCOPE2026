# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The arbitrary-site kernel proof uses degree-four polynomial interpolation on the first circle, degree-two interpolation on the second, and an explicit diagonal solve. Because the only Hermitian kernel generator is invertible, no nonzero rank-two difference of pure-state lifts can be lost. The eight-sample lower bound uses a two-dimensional real Hermitian kernel and the odd determinant on its unit circle; for spanning frames the singular witness must be indefinite. The rank-two transversality inequality follows from best rank-two spectral approximation and is attained by deletion of the least singular-value component. A rational-grid exact symbolic witness independently checks rank, kernel and constant in a concrete case.

## Originality — PASS (best knowledge)
Chalendar–Partington (2024) give full-circle and infinite-set uniqueness for much broader analytic classes but not a sharp, finite five-plus-three arbitrary-angle scheme. Bodmann–Hammen (2013, Theorem 2.3) give the already-known eight-measurement optimal count for degree two using specially arranged intersecting circles, not arbitrary independent node sets on disjoint concentric circles. Jaming–Rathmair (2022) use function plus derivative intensities. The new statement is compared at the level of hypotheses, sample counts, nodes and implication, not just titles. Remaining risk: equivalent formulations in older polynomial-magnitude interpolation or Hermitian-kernel design work outside the searched/indexed corpus.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The theorem identifies a placement-robust, globally minimal sensing geometry: every choice of five distinct angles on one circle and three distinct angles on a different concentric circle works, with a fully classified nonzero yet nonsingular Hermitian obstruction. The accompanying sharp rank-two angle gives a structural separation of recoverable pure-state differences from the lifting kernel. This is a useful exact design principle rather than a small numerical improvement. No claim of uniform output measurement conditioning or degrees beyond two is made.
