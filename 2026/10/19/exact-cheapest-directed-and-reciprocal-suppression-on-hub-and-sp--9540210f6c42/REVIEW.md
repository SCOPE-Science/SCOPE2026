# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The general theorem is deduced by a block characteristic determinant valid even with zeroed controls, together with the Perron-root property of nonnegative matrices. For independent directional costs, a per-edge inequality is tight under one-direction-only attenuation, reducing the complete feasible optimization to a fractional knapsack problem. The reciprocal problem is a convex quadratic feasible-set optimization whose strictly positive multiplier gives a unique clipped formula. The reciprocity gap is bounded by a feasible square-root symmetrization of the directional optimizer; two-spoke homogeneous families attain the sharp endpoints as limits and an explicit asymmetric-cost family makes the price unbounded. Exact arithmetic reproduces a nontrivial three-spoke instance. The general result does not rest on finite enumeration.

## Originality — PASS, scoped to the policy classification

Bellingeri et al. (2026), https://doi.org/10.3389/fphy.2026.1755888, Sections 3–5, derive multi-group reproduction thresholds and discuss contact intervention; their considered two/three-group parameter families do not include the stated global minima under these two control domains. Zhang, Lu and Pei (2018), https://doi.org/10.28919/cmbn/3388, Sections 3–4, supply general geometric programming for directed epidemic resources subject to different node-rate cost structures, not the exact spoke-wide directed/reciprocal solution or sharp reciprocity price. Preciado et al. (arXiv:1303.3984) address heterogeneous node vaccination, and Saha et al. (2015), DOI 10.1137/1.9781611974010.64, concern approximate unweighted graph deletion. The Schur complement, fractional knapsack, and KKT rules are openly recognized as standard tools: the claim is the combined global optimization comparison for the specified structured transmission-control problem, not invention of those tools. A previously unindexed exact specialization is an originality risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This settles the complete minimum-cost critical-threshold resource allocation problem on a natural heterogeneous mixing family, including reducible policies, any number of spokes, exact costs, and a provably sharp price for requiring reciprocal contact reductions. Its policy reversal is structurally consequential: directional asymmetry encourages severing a few cheaper directions, whereas symmetry makes spreading small interventions across all spokes preferable. Distinguishing these design assumptions prevents applying an asymmetry-based optimum where paired attenuation is mandatory. Exact optimality is about initial reproduction, not final epidemic burden.

## Limits and closest literature

Critical equality permits neutral linear dynamics, so the result does not guarantee exponential extinction. Controls are assumed to be independent or exactly symmetric by model design, without empirical feasibility claims. The closest general precursor is Zhang–Lu–Pei (2018); their geometric-programming approach can be considered a broad optimization framework but does not supply the explicit star/reciprocity statements. An unidentified specialized resource-allocation result may cover part of this theorem. No independent verification has been performed.
