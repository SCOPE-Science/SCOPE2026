# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The defined circular half-length spectrum follows from the classical infinite Thue–Morse square-root/position classification, aligned desubstitution, explicit cyclic transition counts and alternating-triple counts. The fixed six-source-triple by sixteen-offset lemma exhausts every possible local configuration for primitive half-lengths 1, 2, 3 and 6; its 96 individual cases are executed by `verify.py`, alongside direct small-word boundary cases. Grouping dyadic scales bounds the number of squares lost at any cut by \(R_m\), and nested divisibility of equality cases leaves exactly cuts 0 and \(2^{m-1}\). Double counting the preserving cuts of each square proves the mean. The independent direct scans through level ten reproduce every assertion; larger-level spot checks do not replace the proof. Residual verification risk is an undetected oversight in the classical aligned-occurrence mapping or finite local-neighborhood reduction.

## Originality — PASS against inspected sources, with residual risk
Brown, Rampersad, Shallit and Vasiga (2006), DOI 10.1051/ita:2006030, Theorem 4.1 and Lemma 4.2 already classify all square *types* and their allowed positions in the infinite Thue–Morse word. Theorems 4.3 and 4.5 analyze automatic/regular occurrence data. These stronger positional tools were inspected in full and explicitly used, but the examined article does not assert a closed dyadic-prefix **cyclic square multiplicity by half-length**, an exact mean over rotations, or the **unique two minimizing cuts**. Their existence does not alone imply the two-cut extremal theorem without a local-cut comparison. Aberkane and Currie's circular power-avoidance paper, DOI 10.1016/j.tcs.2004.12.024, addresses existence of factors with restricted circular powers at arbitrary lengths and not the number of squares in each rotation of the specific dyadic prefixes; only the abstract and available descriptions were inspected. Targeted mathematical-record queries and accessible own historical claims disclosed no overlapping statement. Historical uniqueness remains unproven, and full-text access to the circular-power paper is a residual risk.

## Value — PASS
Circular repetition statistics of Thue–Morse blocks are naturally motivated by the word's extremal power-avoidance properties and by the distinction between cyclic and linear factors. The finding resolves an all-order length-refined occurrence law and a sharp extremal cut problem: only the two complementary canonical cuts minimize the number of nonwrapping squares. The fixed local lemma also supplies a transferable proof mechanism for optimizing repetition statistics across dyadic substitution cuts. The multiplicity formulas alone could be viewed as aggregating classical positional data, but the strict equality classification and mean are nontrivial structural consequences.

## Limits and closest literature
The result is neither a new classification of Thue–Morse square roots nor a theorem for every Thue–Morse factor. Earlier work supplies the square-root and alignment lemmas used. The 2005 circular-power-avoidance paper could not be inspected in full; a differently stated prior result is possible. Independently audited priority is not asserted.
