# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The six ballot multiplicities solve all rank constraints and have a complete interval of feasible integer values. Each head-to-head majority is an explicit affine function of the parameter. The proof of the parity-dependent lower bounds uses unavoidable cyclic triples, with the exact equality-layer classification derived from the residual ballots. The labeled-profile weights are multinomial and their neighboring ratio is strictly decreasing. An exact-integer checker enumerates the relevant finite layers and independently verifies direct ballot-count computations. The infinite-electorate statements rest on algebraic proofs, not on finite enumeration.

## Originality
PASS, conditional on the specified inspected sources and with remaining discovery risk. The closest directly relevant original study is Boehmer et al. (arXiv:2303.02538), Section 5 and Theorem 7: its Condorcet test is a necessary condition and its broader integer-programming approach does not supply an explicit characterization of the three-candidate fiber or sharp odd/even ambiguity boundaries. Searches for position-matrix fibers, three-alternative Condorcet feasibility, identical positional marginals and majority reversals in published mathematical finding records returned no equivalent statement. Related three-candidate social-choice findings instead study voting-rule divergences, participation and reinforcement, not rank-matrix feasibility. A 2025 election-map discussion could not be fully inspected; undetected equivalent small-candidate classifications are a residual risk, not a claim of exhaustive novelty certification.

## Value
PASS. Position matrices intentionally discard pairwise-ranking information, while Condorcet feasibility is a fundamental binary test of that lost information. The result fully characterizes the smallest nontrivial alternative count, provides a finite closed-form feasibility test for every electorate size, locates the sharp and different parity thresholds for ambiguity, and computes exact conditional probabilities at the first ambiguous matrices. These address a specific motivated information-loss problem rather than an arbitrary table slice. No result for larger alternative sets is asserted.

## Scientific limitations
Strict rankings and strict pairwise majority are essential. The explicit fiber and counting formulas are special to three candidates; the probability law conditions independent uniform ordered ballots on their matrix. A general position-matrix integer program is known, and the present value is the sharper structural classification, not a new general complexity bound. There may be unindexed covering literature, and independent verification is still outstanding.
