# Scientific review — exact correlation and fluctuation law

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is stated for each real \(0<b<1/2\) with a precisely specified continuous interval map and stationary initial distribution. Both slopes are expanding; the two-cell transition matrix is primitive. The proposed invariant density solves the transfer equation, integrates to one, and has strictly positive cell values. On cellwise affine functions, the four-by-four transfer matrix has spectrum \(\{1,-b\}\) and the roots of \(\lambda^2+(1-b)^2\lambda+b^2\). Centering removes the stationary eigencomponent, so the matrix resolvent gives summable correlations. The Gordin martingale decomposition converges uniformly and proves the stationary Gaussian limit, with strictly positive variance. Exact rational algebra yields the covariance generator, asymptotic variance, derivative quartic, and triple collision. An independent exact-interval integration checker matched 36 correlations. Checks of finitely many parameters are corroboration; the infinite claim rests on symbolic identities and standard ergodic arguments. The endpoint \(b=0\) and all other map-parameter slices are outside scope.

## Originality — PASS, with disclosed priority uncertainty

The 2023 extended originating article describes numerical mean tuning and explicitly leaves theoretical parameter–statistic relations for further work; its accessible full text does not give the all-lag covariance, exact Gaussian variance, unique variance maximizer, or cubic spectral collision for this Markov slice. The 2025 McDonald–Van Wyk–Cilliers paper contains general density/spectrum construction methods rather than this source-map-specific quantitative classification. General transfer theory is acknowledged and is not claimed new. Comparisons to published research on stationary covariance for other maps and coded shifts have different objects and do not imply this fixed-parameter formula. The four-page 2023 conference precursor was available only as metadata and abstract, despite bounded access attempts, so its detailed contents leave a real residual priority risk. The review supports best-knowledge originality rather than a proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The source motivation is to tune orbit statistics by changing a map's endpoint geometry. Along a natural, exactly solvable expanding slice, the stationary mean rises strictly, but the entire time-series correlation structure—and hence uncertainty in long empirical averages—changes nonmonotonically. The closed Green–Kubo variance, unique algebraic optimum, cancellation as \(b\downarrow0\), and an independent triple spectral-resonance point are precise natural invariants, not arbitrary finite tables. They distinguish instantaneous mean tuning from temporal dependence, a mathematically relevant property for time-series generation. No claims are made about generic slice parameters or finite-precision pseudorandomness.
