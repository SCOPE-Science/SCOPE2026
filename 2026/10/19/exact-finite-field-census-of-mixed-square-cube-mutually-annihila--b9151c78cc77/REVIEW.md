# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof establishes an all-dimensional point-count formula for \(A^2=B^3=AB=BA=0\) by a fixed-square-zero-rank normal form and exact Jordan-centralizer counting. The critical nonstandard step, reducing the cube-zero condition to a rectangular annihilation equation involving only size-two Jordan blocks, is proved directly. Independent direct ambient-matrix enumeration in eight small cases corroborates fixed-rank fibers; experiments are not used as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS within checked sources.** Schröer studies the identical \(V(n,2,3)\) object but its irreducible components over algebraically closed fields, not the rational-point count. Huang's arithmetic series counts annihilating pairs and all-nilpotent pairs without individual power cutoffs. Kinser–Lőrincz addresses geometric node splitting. Targeted searches for mixed nilpotency bounds and comparisons with uniform square-zero counts did not identify a formula implying the displayed asymmetric count. Canonical-form and possible Hall-polynomial equivalents remain residual risks.

**Value — PASS.** The point count is a complete natural arithmetic invariant across infinitely many representation varieties and finite fields. The length-two-block correction identifies a genuine structural obstruction that is invisible in unrefined mutual-annihilation counts; it is not a small isolated integer recomputation. No independent validation or absolute global novelty claim is made.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Schröer, *Varieties of pairs of nilpotent matrices annihilating each other* (arXiv:math/0211293); Huang, *Mutually annihilating matrices* (arXiv:2110.15566); Kinser–Lőrincz, *Representation varieties of algebras with nodes* (arXiv:1810.10997). The enumeration is for labeled pairs rather than conjugacy classes; finite checks sample only small prime fields. The canonical-forms paper of Bondarenko–Gerasimova–Sergeichuk was not available in full during this comparison, and unindexed duplicate enumerations cannot be excluded.
