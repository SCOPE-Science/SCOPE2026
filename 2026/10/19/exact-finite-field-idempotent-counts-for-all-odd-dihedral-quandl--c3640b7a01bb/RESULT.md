# Exact finite-field idempotent counts for all odd dihedral quandle algebras

## Finding
For every odd integer \(n\geq3\) and every finite field \(K=\mathbb F_q\) with \(\gcd(n,q)=1\), the complete set and exact number of idempotents of the dihedral quandle algebra \(A=K[R_n]\) are determined by finite arithmetic on the divisors of \(n\). Multiplication on its distinguished basis is \(e_xe_y=e_{2y-x}\), with subscripts modulo \(n\). Zero is included among idempotents.

For each divisor \(r>1\) of \(n\), define \(d_r=\operatorname{ord}_r(2)\) and \(H_r=\langle2,-1\rangle\leq(\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z)^\times\), with \(h_r=|H_r|\). Let \(s_r\) be the least positive integer for which \(q^{s_r}\bmod r\) belongs to \(H_r\). The following parameters are uniquely determined:

- If \(-1\in\langle2\rangle\pmod r\), put \(\epsilon_r=1\), choose the unique \(0\leq a_r<d_r\) with \(q^{s_r}\equiv2^{a_r}\pmod r\), and put \(N_r=2^{d_r/2}+1\). In this case \(d_r\) is even.
- Otherwise choose unique \(\epsilon_r\in\{1,-1\}\) and \(0\leq a_r<d_r\) with \(q^{s_r}\equiv\epsilon_r2^{a_r}\pmod r\), and put \(N_r=2^{d_r}-1\).

Set \(g_r=\gcd(N_r,q^{s_r}-\epsilon_r2^{a_r})\), taking the positive gcd, including \(\gcd(N_r,0)=N_r\). Then

\[
\boxed{\bigl|\operatorname{Idem}(K[R_n])\bigr|=
2\prod_{\substack{r\mid n\\r>1}}
(1+g_r)^{\varphi(r)/(h_rs_r)}.}
\]

There is also an exact Fourier parametrization. Fix a primitive \(n\)-th root \(\zeta\) in an algebraic closure and write \(c_k=\sum_{x\bmod n}u_x\zeta^{kx}\). One has \(u^2=u\) precisely when \(c_0\in\{0,1\}\), each nonzero-frequency \(H_r\)-orbit of indices of exact order \(r\) is either entirely zero or entirely nonzero, and a chosen representative \(k\) of every nonzero orbit has a parameter \(t\) satisfying \(t^{N_r}=1\), with
\[
c_{2^jk}=t^{2^j},\qquad c_{-2^jk}=t^{-2^j}.
\]
These choices are subject exactly to \(c_{qk}=c_k^q\). The inverse Fourier transform gives one and only one idempotent in \(K[R_n]\). Thus the formula is an exhaustive classification, not just an upper or lower bound.

Examples: the exact totals for \((n,q)=(5,2),(7,2),(9,2),(11,2),(13,2),(15,2)\) are respectively \(12,16,80,68,132,768\). For \(n=3\) and coefficient characteristic different from \(3\), the known total \(8\) is recovered.

## Assumptions and scope
The finite field may have even or odd characteristic, but its characteristic must not divide \(n\); the parity of \(n\) is odd, and \(n\geq3\). The coefficient algebra is generally nonassociative. The symbols \(r,d_r,H_r,h_r,s_r,a_r,\epsilon_r,N_r,g_r\) have exactly the meanings specified above. Because \(q\) is a unit modulo \(r\), \(s_r\) is the order of its coset in \((\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z)^\times/H_r\). Therefore \(h_rs_r\mid\varphi(r)\), so all exponents in the count are integers. The count includes zero. The underlying indexed quandle uses the right operation \(x*y=2y-x\).

## Proof
**1. Fourier linearization.** Since the characteristic does not divide \(n\), the discrete Fourier transform on \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) is invertible over the splitting field. Evaluating the bilinear product directly yields
\[
\widehat{uv}(k)=\widehat u(-k)\widehat v(2k).
\]
Consequently, idempotency is equivalent, at every residue \(k\), to
\[
c_k=c_{-k}c_{2k}.
\]
At \(k=0\), this gives exactly \(c_0\in\{0,1\}\).

**2. Nonzero support and inversion.** Since \(n\) is odd, the maps \(k\mapsto-k\) and \(k\mapsto2k\) permute the finite set of frequencies. If \(c_k\ne0\), its two right-hand factors are nonzero; thus the support is a union of orbits under \(\langle-1,2\rangle\). On the support put \(P_k=c_kc_{-k}\). Multiplying the equations for \(k\) and \(-k\) gives \(P_k=P_kP_{2k}\). Since \(P_k\ne0\), \(P_{2k}=1\); the doubling permutation on the support gives \(P_k=1\) everywhere there. Hence \(c_{-k}=c_k^{-1}\) and \(c_{2k}=c_k^2\). Conversely, these two relations on a union of orbits imply all the original equations. In particular there is no partly zero orbit.

**3. Exact parameter groups.** Indices having character order exactly \(r\) are in bijection with \((\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z)^\times\). If \(-1\) lies in the cyclic subgroup generated by \(2\), its order is \(2\), so \(2^{d_r/2}=-1\) modulo \(r\). Thus an orbit parameter \(t=c_k\) must satisfy both \(t^{2^{d_r/2}}=t^{-1}\) and the doubling cycle; equivalently \(t^{2^{d_r/2}+1}=1\). If \(-1\) lies outside that subgroup, the two doubling cycles are disjoint and paired by negation; their values are fixed by one parameter with \(t^{2^{d_r}-1}=1\). Both conditions are sufficient; they yield exactly \(t^{N_r}=1\). The zero choice is distinct from any such \(t\).

**4. Descent to the coefficient field.** A Fourier vector is the transform of an element with coefficients in \(\mathbb F_q\) if and only if \(c_{qk}=c_k^q\) for every index; this follows by applying Frobenius to the transform and using Fourier inversion. The multiplication-by-\(q\) action permutes the \(\varphi(r)/h_r\) distinct \(H_r\)-orbits, with cycles all of length \(s_r\). Once a representative orbit is either zero or given a nonzero parameter \(t\), the Frobenius condition determines all other orbits in that cycle. After \(s_r\) steps it becomes
\[
t^{q^{s_r}}=t^{\epsilon_r2^{a_r}}.
\]
Combined with \(t^{N_r}=1\), it leaves exactly \(g_r\) nonzero choices. Indeed, the common roots of the two exponent equations in an algebraic closure of \(\mathbb F_q\) number \(\gcd(N_r,q^{s_r}-\epsilon_r2^{a_r})\), because in odd coefficient characteristic the second exponent is prime to that characteristic, while in characteristic two the exponent \(N_r\) is odd. Thus the common root group has the asserted gcd order, including when the exponent difference vanishes. The zero orbit contributes one additional choice. All cycles and all exact-order strata are disjoint and independent.

**5. Counting and reconstruction.** There are precisely \(\varphi(r)/(h_rs_r)\) Frobenius cycles on the order-\(r\) frequencies. Each contributes \(1+g_r\) choices. The trivial frequency contributes two, and invertibility of the Fourier transform supplies a unique coefficient vector for every admissible collection. Multiplication proves the formula and the claimed classification for every permitted \(n,q\).

## Verification
The included standalone `verify.py` computes the divisor-orbit formula independently of the multiplication table and exhaustively enumerates all \(q^n\) coefficient vectors in the listed feasible instances. It uses genuine \(\mathbb F_4=\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^2+t+1)\) arithmetic, not integer arithmetic modulo four. All tested cases passed: \((n,q)=(3,2),(3,4),(3,5),(5,2),(5,3),(5,4),(5,7),(7,2),(7,3),(9,2),(9,5),(11,2),(13,2),(15,2)\); the final output line is `VERIFY_OK`. Run with `python3 verify.py`. The unrestricted theorem follows from the preceding proof rather than extrapolation from finite testing.

## Relationship to prior work
Zhang's *Group-ring methods for Alexander quandle rings* (arXiv:2610.08204v1, Sections 2 and 5) develops the group-ring Fourier transport and derives the same Fourier idempotency equation for complex core quandles; its idempotent classification is integral for commutative Alexander quandles, while its dihedral-core application calculates complex Peirce spectra. Elhamdadi, Ta, and Virgin's *Fourier Analysis and Idempotents in Quandle Algebras* (arXiv:2609.03799v1, Sections 3–4) similarly develops Fourier methods to prove integral idempotent statements. Neither supplies this coefficient-field-dependent divisor-and-Frobenius count for every odd \(n\) over \(\mathbb F_q\). In particular, the step from the complex Fourier equation to the exact finite-field enumeration is not a direct application of their integral Parseval argument.

A separately reported classification of the three-element case already gives eight idempotents when the coefficient characteristic is different from three, and covers the characteristic-three case as well. The \(n=3\) specialization here is **not** claimed as new. The claimed distinct contribution is the uniform formula and exhaustive parametrization for all odd orders and all finite coefficient fields of coprime characteristic. A historical equivalent formulated through finite-field character orbits cannot be ruled out absolutely.

## Limitations
No statement is made for even \(n\), nor for coefficient characteristic dividing \(n\), where Fourier inversion fails and additional modular idempotents can appear. The theorem concerns cyclic dihedral quandles, not all finite Alexander quandles or their automorphism groups. An accessible published formula under different language could subsume this result; the cited literature and indexed-record searches did not identify one. Computational enumeration covers only the named instances, and is not itself a proof of the general formula.
