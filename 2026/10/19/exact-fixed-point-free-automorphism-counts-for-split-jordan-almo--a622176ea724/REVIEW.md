# Review of exact fixed-point-free automorphism counts for split-Jordan almost-abelian Lie algebras

**Correctness — PASS.** Since the defining operator is invertible, the derived ideal identifies the abelian module intrinsically. The automorphism condition is exactly \(ST=aTS\); its finite-field Jordan orbit decomposition and the eigenvalue-one test were proved. The endomorphism ring of a direct sum of nilpotent Jordan modules has radical quotient \(\prod_j M_{m_j}(\mathbb F_q)\), giving the group size and derangement ratio. The cyclic return map and orbit independence yield the closed formula. The supplied code independently checks four entire small linear-automorphism spaces and several nonsemisimple centralizer and intertwiner families. The tests are finite consistency evidence, not the source of the theorem.

**Originality — PASS, subject to historical risk.** The strongest directly relevant full-text sources are Burde–Dekimpe, arXiv:2604.27916v1, Theorem 5.4 (complex existence, not finite-field counts) and Avetisyan, arXiv:1610.05365v2, Proposition 9 (all-field automorphism normal form, not fixed-point-free enumeration). Published finite-field Heisenberg counts concern a nilpotent defining operator, while published almost-abelian commutativity spectra concern element centralizers. Searches for the exact product-sum and equivalent Jordan-partition formulations found no direct coverage. General finite-module and matrix-derangement theory supplies component lemmas, so an earlier implicit formulation remains plausible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** This computes a natural previously unquantified finite-field invariant, the entire fixed-point-free automorphism census and density, for all invertible split-Jordan almost-abelian Lie algebras, including nonsemisimple cases in arbitrary dimension and characteristic. The existence boundary is controlled by a global scalar symmetry of full Jordan partitions, while the frequencies depend on each Jordan size via nonlinear general-linear derangement factors. The result is useful for comparing Lie algebras with identical dimension and rank but different Jordan structure; it is not a claim about nonsplit or nilpotent defining operators.

**Residual limits:** Priority cannot be proven by searching alone, and the nonsplit and zero-eigenvalue cases are not covered. Independent audit is not performed.
