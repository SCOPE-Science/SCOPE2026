# Exact Gabor tomography rank and singular spectrum for prime-plane quadratic conics

## Finding

Let \(p\ge5\) be prime, let \(d\in\mathbb F_p^\times\), and put \(\varepsilon=\chi(d)\in\{-1,1\}\), where \(\chi\) is the quadratic character. Set \(G=\mathbb F_p^2\), \(Q(x,y)=x^2-dy^2\), \(C=\{z\in G:Q(z)=1\}\), \(m=p-\varepsilon\), and \(g=\mathbf 1_C\). Use the counting inner product on \(\mathbb C^G\), define \(\psi(t)=\exp(2\pi i t/p)\), and set
\[
(T_af)(z)=f(z-a),\qquad (M_bf)(z)=\psi(b\cdot z)f(z),\qquad W_{a,b}=M_bT_a.
\]
For complex matrices \(X\in\mathrm{Mat}_{p^2}(\mathbb C)\), equipped with Hilbert--Schmidt norm, define the calibrated full-orbit Gabor intensity lifting
\[
(\mathcal L_gX)_{a,b}=p^{-1}\operatorname{tr}\!\left(X\,|W_{a,b}g\rangle\langle W_{a,b}g|\right),\qquad (a,b)\in G^2,
\]
with the Euclidean norm on the output space of \(p^4\) coordinates.

**Rank and kernel.** The complete operator rank and nullity are
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb C}\mathcal L_g=p^2\left(1+\frac{(p-\varepsilon)^2}{2}\right),\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb C}\ker\mathcal L_g=p^4-p^2\left(1+\frac{(p-\varepsilon)^2}{2}\right).}
\]
For \(h\in G\) and \(\omega\in G\), put
\[
A_g(h,\omega)=\sum_{z\in C\cap(C+h)}\psi(\omega\cdot z).
\]
All ambiguity zeros come **only from empty translated intersections**, independently of modulation: \(A_g(h,\omega)=0\) precisely when \(C\cap(C+h)=\varnothing\). Thus the kernel is the complex span of precisely those Weyl operators \(W_{h,\omega}\) with \(h\notin C-C\). Equivalently, the admissible differences are \(h=0\), \(Q(h)=4\), and those \(h\) with \(Q(h)\notin\{0,4\}\) and \(\chi(-dQ(h)(4-Q(h)))=1\). This describes every kernel direction, not only its dimension.

**Complete singular-value multiset.** The singular values of \(\mathcal L_g\), with multiplicities and with duplicates added where numerical values coincide, are exactly the following multiset. Define
\[
K=\frac{m(m-2)}{2},\qquad Q^*(u,v)=u^2-d^{-1}v^2,\qquad
\mathrm{Kl}_p(A,B)=\sum_{s\in\mathbb F_p^\times}\psi(As+B/s).
\]

| Singular value | Multiplicity |
|---|---:|
| \(m\) | \(1\) |
| \(1\) (zero-shift, nonzero isotropic frequency) | \((p-1)(1+\varepsilon)\) |
| \(\left|\mathrm{Kl}_p(-1,-t/4)\right|\), separately for each \(t\in\mathbb F_p^\times\) | \(m\) for each \(t\) |
| \(1\) (one-point translated intersections) | \(mp^2\) |
| \(2\) (two-point intersections, zero relative frequency) | \(pK\) |
| \(2\cos(\pi j/p)\), separately for \(1\le j\le(p-1)/2\) | \(2pK\) for each \(j\) |
| \(0\) | \(p^4-p^2(1+m^2/2)\) |

All listed positive entries are strictly positive. The row for zero-shift nonzero isotropic frequencies is empty in the anisotropic case. In particular, both split conics (\(\varepsilon=1\)) and nonsplit conics (\(\varepsilon=-1\)) have an explicit, arithmetic **entire linear-lifting spectrum**, not merely a rank formula.

## Assumptions and scope

The result applies to all primes \(p\ge5\), both quadratic-form isometry types, every nonzero \(d\), the unweighted indicator window and the **complete** set of translations and modulations. The complex matrix space is not restricted to Hermitian or low-rank matrices. The factor \(p^{-1}\) in \(\mathcal L_g\) is essential for the stated singular-value normalization; rescaling the window scales all nonzero singular values by its squared amplitude. The character and dot product above fix every sign and normalization. Nothing is asserted for characteristic two, composite moduli or arbitrary weighted windows.

## Proof

**1. Sizes and translated conic intersections.** Since \(Q\) is nondegenerate, a direct quadratic-character count gives \(|C|=p-\chi(d)=m\). For \(h\ne0\), write \(t=Q(h)\), and set \(z=h/2+y\). The equations \(Q(z)=Q(z-h)=1\) become
\[
2(h_1y_1-dh_2y_2)=0,\qquad Q(y)=1-t/4.
\]
If \(t=0\), the orthogonal line \(h^\perp\) is spanned by \(h\) itself; every vector on it has norm zero, contrary to the right-hand side \(1\). Hence the intersection is empty. If \(t\ne0\), its orthogonal line is generated by \((dh_2,h_1)\), whose norm equals \(-dt\). The scalar equation is
\[
(-dt)\lambda^2=1-t/4.
\]
It has one solution if \(t=4\), two solutions if \(t\notin\{0,4\}\) and \(\chi(-dt(4-t))=1\), and no solution otherwise. For every prescribed nonzero \(t\), exactly \(m\) shifts have \(Q(h)=t\). Also
\[
\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_p}\chi(t(4-t))=-\chi(-1),
\]
obtained by completing the square and counting the hyperbola \(u^2-v^2=1\). Therefore exactly \((p-2-\varepsilon)/2=(m-2)/2\) nonzero norm levels other than \(4\) have two-point intersections. It follows that precisely \(m\) nonzero shifts give one-point intersections, \(K=m(m-2)/2\) give two-point intersections, and every other nonzero shift gives none. The zero shift has \(m\) points. Thus
\[
|C-C|=1+m+K=1+\frac{m^2}{2}.
\]

**2. There are no cancellations inside any nonempty intersection.** If \(\sum_{z\in S}\psi(\omega\cdot z)=0\), write its coefficients as integer counts \(n_0,\ldots,n_{p-1}\) for the phases \(\psi(0),\ldots,\psi(p-1)\). The cyclotomic polynomial \(1+X+\cdots+X^{p-1}\) is irreducible of degree \(p-1\) over the rationals, so vanishing forces \(n_0=\cdots=n_{p-1}\), and hence \(p\mid |S|\). The only nonempty sizes from Step 1 are \(1\), \(2\), and \(m=p\pm1\), none divisible by \(p\). Thus **all** modulations survive on each active shift, and the total ambiguity support is \(p^2(1+m^2/2)\).

**3. Every ambiguity modulus.** A one-point intersection has Fourier modulus \(1\). For a two-point intersection \(\{z_1,z_2\}\), the modulus is
\[
|\psi(\omega\cdot z_1)+\psi(\omega\cdot z_2)|=2\big|\cos(\pi\omega\cdot(z_1-z_2)/p)\big|.
\]
Because \(z_1-z_2\ne0\), each residue of the linear functional \(\omega\mapsto\omega\cdot(z_1-z_2)\) occurs exactly \(p\) times. Therefore each two-point shift contributes \(p\) copies of \(2\), and \(2p\) copies of \(2\cos(\pi j/p)\) for every \(1\le j\le(p-1)/2\).

For \(h=0\) and \(\omega=(u,v)\), character orthogonality gives
\[
A_g(0,\omega)=\frac1p\sum_{s\in\mathbb F_p}\psi(-s)\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb F_p}\psi(s(x^2-dy^2)+ux+vy).
\]
At \(\omega=0\), this equals \(m\). At \(\omega\ne0\), the \(s=0\) term vanishes. Completing squares in both variables and using the elementary Gauss-sum relation \(G_p^2=\chi(-1)p\) gives the **exact** identity
\[
A_g(0,\omega)=\varepsilon\sum_{s\ne0}\psi\left(-s-\frac{Q^*(\omega)}{4s}\right)
=\varepsilon\mathrm{Kl}_p(-1,-Q^*(\omega)/4).
\]
For \(Q^*(\omega)=0\) with \(\omega\ne0\), the sum is \(-1\). The dual quadratic form has precisely \((p-1)(1+\varepsilon)\) nonzero isotropic frequencies; every nonzero prescribed dual norm has exactly \(m\) frequencies. These give every remaining singular-value row. By Step 2 none of the displayed Kloosterman magnitudes vanish, even though they need not admit simpler closed forms.

**4. Weyl-operator diagonalization and normalization.** Put \(n=|G|=p^2\). The \(n^2\) Weyl operators \(W_{h,\omega}\), divided by \(\sqrt n\), form an orthonormal Hilbert--Schmidt basis of \(\mathrm{Mat}_n(\mathbb C)\). Conjugation by \(W_{a,b}\) multiplies any Weyl basis element by a character of the phase-space group \(G\times G\). Evaluating the entire orbit of \(|g\rangle\langle g|\) therefore applies a full character matrix, followed by diagonal multiplication by the Weyl coefficients of \(|g\rangle\langle g|\). For the calibrated map \(\mathcal L_g=n^{-1/2}\operatorname{tr}(X P_{a,b})\), each resulting column has squared Euclidean norm exactly \(|A_g(h,\omega)|^2\), while distinct columns are orthogonal. Hence the singular values (including zeros) are the complete multiset \(\{|A_g(h,\omega)|:(h,\omega)\in G^2\}\). The kernel is the span of Weyl basis vectors with zero ambiguity, proving the rank and all spectral claims.

Run `python3 checks/verify_conic.py` using Python 3's standard library. It tests all \(p^4\) ambiguity coefficients for ten split/nonsplit conics, with \(p=5,7,11,13,17\), verifying all exact zero tests through integer cyclotomic residue counts (not numerical tolerances), the geometric intersection spectrum, the predicted rank, and the Kloosterman and two-point cosine moduli against complex evaluation. Reproduced output is in `checks/check_output.txt`. Run `python3 checks/dense_svd.py` with NumPy for a **separate direct linear-algebra test** at \(p=5,d=1\). It constructs the complete \(625\)-by-\(625\) intensity-lifting matrix, checks rank \(225\), and compares every singular value with the separately computed ambiguity modulus to within \(8\cdot10^{-15}\) (output in `checks/dense_svd_output.txt`). This floating-point comparison is not an exact certificate; the universal result rests on Steps 1--4. All required code and outputs are standalone files in this package.

## Relationship to prior work

Iosevich, Kolountzakis, Lyubarskii, Mayeli and Pakianathan (2017) study indicators as finite-prime-field Gabor **orthonormal basis** windows and establish a connection with spectral tilings. Their orthonormal-basis equivalences do not state the rank or singular distribution of the **full rank-one intensity lifting** of a conic indicator. Bojarovska and Flinth (2015) provide the general Gabor phase-retrieval and Weyl/ambiguity viewpoint, including generic sufficient conditions; substitution into their general operator identity does not calculate conic intersections or their exact split/nonsplit spectral multiplicities. Iosevich and Koh (2007) give finite-field sphere Fourier formulas, relevant to the Kloosterman term, but not the complete translated-intersection Gabor spectral law.

A more recent published hyperbolic quadratic-shell Gabor analysis establishes an exact rank for the split quadratic form in even dimension **at least four**. Its written hypotheses explicitly exclude the present **dimension-two** theorem and the nonsplit conic case. The low-dimensional geometry has intersections of at most two points, eliminating all nontrivial ambiguity cancellation; the current result additionally determines **every** singular value, which is not a corollary of an operator-rank statement. This is a best-knowledge prior-work comparison, not a claim of exhaustive priority verification.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns linear recovery of **all complex matrices**, not injectivity of nonlinear phase retrieval on pure-state vectors; rank deficiency alone does not resolve nonlinear injectivity. The indicator is unweighted and the spatial group is the prime-field plane; no composite-modulus, extension-field, higher-dimensional or general-conic claim is made. The Kloosterman entries give an exact indexed finite-sum spectrum, not a closed elementary description of the distribution of Kloosterman values or a universal lower bound on the smallest nonzero singular value. Algebraic proof covers every named prime; finite tests do not extend its scope. Independent mathematical auditing has not been performed. An unindexed prior equivalent dimension-two computation remains possible.
