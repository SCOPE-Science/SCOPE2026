# Scientific review: geometric clusters in one-shot antagonistic adsorption

## Correctness
The theorem fixes the infinite-chain, single-proposal, iid marking and priority model and explicit ranges \(0<p<1\), \(0<t\leq1\), and \(m\geq2\). Two independent endpoint half-lines give the exact same-species block hierarchy, using the published half-line equations with independently checked normalization. Inclusion–exclusion gives maximal-cluster intensities, and a positive geometric tail determines the inverse ratios. The proof also establishes strict adjacent covariance, and factors of iid give the ergodic spatial observation limit. An independent finite-chain dynamic-programming checker and quadratures passed 1,854 assertions. Finite enumeration is corroboration, not proof of all sizes.

## Originality
The closest full text is C. S. do Amaral and D. C. dos Santos, arXiv:2306.09786v1 (2023), which establishes the model and its one-site density. This contribution concerns the all-length joint A/B occupation events, maximal-cluster tail law, and exact inverse identification, which do not follow from density alone. The same authors' one-species exclusion paper arXiv:2210.05627v2 has a different rule that precludes adjacent occupied particles; Martins, Dickman and Ziff, arXiv:2211.04622, analyze two-dimensional clusters and one-dimensional repeated trials. Older cooperative adsorption work by Evans and collaborators was available only in abstract or bibliographic form and may contain a hidden general specialization. Independent mathematical-priority assessment is needed.

## Scientific value
A cluster-size distribution is a natural morphology statistic in sequential adsorption, rather than a parameter-selected special case. The exact conditional geometric law and two species-specific tail ratios determine both observation progress and species bias from spatial configuration, beyond previously available one-site density. This offers a rigorous benchmark for one-attempt antagonistic deposition and separates cluster-left-endpoint sampling from site-weighted sampling.

## Limits
The result requires a lattice line with iid species and uniform independent one-time priorities, one proposal per site, two species with strictly positive probabilities, and nearest-neighbor antagonistic exclusion. It does not cover repeated proposals, long-range competition, finite-size corrections, higher dimensions, or finite-sample estimation guarantees.
