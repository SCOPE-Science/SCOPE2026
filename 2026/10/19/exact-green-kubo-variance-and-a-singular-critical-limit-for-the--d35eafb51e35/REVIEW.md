# Scientific self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The annealed Perron–Frobenius operator sends affine-on-dyadic-shell densities to the same class. The two kernel roots resolve the stationary two-time generating function, and after subtraction of the squared stationary mean the would-be pole at unit time parameter cancels. The remaining denominators cannot vanish on the closed unit disk for \(p>1/2\); this proves summability rather than inferring it from finite enumerations. Algebraic elimination gives the closed Green–Kubo coefficient and its parameter derivative. The independent rational integration and shell recurrence agree for 24 exact cases, with infinite-time convergence supplied by the analytical argument.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Yan et al. give the stationary density and first few stationary coordinate autocorrelations, and explicitly motivate Green–Kubo transport coefficients without evaluating their infinite sum. Monthus's complete time transform assumes a uniform initial condition, not the stationary initial law. A pre-existing exact finite-binomial formula for every *stationary* finite lag is also recognized as important related coverage: the present finding does not claim first full-lag exactness, but supplies a nontrivial evaluated infinite Green–Kubo invariant, its strict monotonicity and sharp singular parameter limit. A previously unindexed expression or a simpler summation of that finite-binomial formula remains a priority/value risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The infinite-time variance intensity is a canonical transport statistic for precisely this random interval system. Its finite nonzero limit of \(4\) as \(p\downarrow1/2\), despite stationary weak convergence to \(\delta_0\) and one-step variance tending to zero, distinguishes intermittent long-memory fluctuations from ordinary marginal collapse. The normalized integrated correlation time diverges at an explicit reciprocal rate. This is a natural precise invariant, not a manufactured finite parameter sample.
