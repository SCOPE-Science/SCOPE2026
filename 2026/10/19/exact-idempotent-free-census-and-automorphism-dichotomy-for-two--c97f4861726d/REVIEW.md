# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** Perfectness is equivalent to invertibility of the two natural-basis squares. Their homogeneous quadratic squaring map has a rational projective fixed point if and only if the algebra has a nonzero idempotent. Its fixed-point polynomial is exactly \(cT^3-dT^2+aT-b\); irreducibility also implies nonsingular structure matrix. Degree-three extension Frobenius orbits give the marked-table count. Monomial rigidity of all natural bases, diagonal/anti-diagonal fixed-point computations and Burnside's lemma prove the class count and the complete stabilizer dichotomy. Ten finite-field cases and explicit nonzero-vector idempotent tests for seven small fields were replayed through the bundled verifier; those tests are corroboration rather than infinite proof.

## Originality
**PASS on accessible comparisons, with a named unresolved source.** The complete text of Hu–Wen (2026) treats a distinct three-dimensional complex counterexample. The open full text of Cabrera Casado et al. (2023) establishes general perfect evolution-algebra moduli, natural basis monomiality and related cube-root behavior; it does not state the full finite-field idempotent-free subclass, uniform one-third fraction or subclass orbit count. A 2026 finite-field construction already establishes minimal-dimension existence and is explicitly not claimed. Wei–Zou (2023) was read in its complete open arXiv form, including Section 4's Burnside enumeration of all perfect algebras. Its count of fixed tables is over all nonsingular structure matrices, without the irreducible-cubic filter needed for the idempotent-free subclass. The final journal text was not separately compared. Exact overlap remains possible, and an absent search hit is not a proof of novelty. No claim of absolute novelty or independence is made.

## Value
**PASS.** The exact finite-field proportion and isomorphism census distinguish the arithmetic scarcity or abundance of fixed-point-free projective squaring maps without relying on special handpicked families. A sharp cube-root-of-unity transition yields precisely one enhanced-symmetry isomorphism class and all remaining classes rigid. The result supplies natural counting data for studies of idempotents and nonsolvability in evolution algebras. It does not merely repeat existence or general isomorphism classification.

## Caveats
The theorem is restricted to dimension two and algebra automorphisms fixing the base field. The complete 2023 Wei–Zou arXiv paper was materially inspected. The distinct final journal version was not separately checked, and unindexed equivalent subclass counts remain a bibliographic risk. No external or independent audit was performed.
