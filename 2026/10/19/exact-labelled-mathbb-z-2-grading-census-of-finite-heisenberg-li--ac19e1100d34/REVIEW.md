# Mathematical review: exact Heisenberg \(\mathbb Z/2\)-grading census

**Correctness — PASS.** The commutator ideal is one-dimensional and graded. If central degree is zero, the quotient decomposition is orthogonal symplectic, and each complement admits precisely the prescribed number of central graph lifts. If central degree is one, the quotient components form a complementary Lagrangian pair; the symmetric-matrix parametrization counts all complements, including characteristic two. These two cases are exhaustive, and the direct subspace-enumeration checker matches five small prime-power examples. The symplectic group stabilizers, homogeneous-component dimensions, and asymptotic exponents were separately reconstructed.

**Originality — PASS, scoped to the explicit finite-field enumerator and concentration law.** Calderón–Draper–Martín–Sánchez (2014), Section 3, already prove the homogeneous symplectic basis theorem for group gradings and identify the Heisenberg automorphism structure. Consequently there is no new qualitative existence/classification claim here. Eick–Moede (2022), Sections 1, 2.1 and 4.1, give an inner-adjoint construction for characteristic-two simple Lie algebras; they do not count all fixed-label gradings of the nilpotent Heisenberg family. Hakavuori and collaborators (2022) consider characteristic-zero torsion-free gradings up to equivalence. The explicit finite-field embedded decomposition counts, orbit sizes, dominant central-degree proportion, and quantified inner-adjoint gap were not found in these inspected treatments or in the relevant searched records. The 2014 structural theorem is the most important residual coverage risk: these exact counts could also be independently derived from its general result using finite symplectic geometry.

**Value — PASS.** Enumerating the *embedded* gradings, rather than merely their equivalence types, measures the size of the graded-structure parameter space. Its odd-center and even-center pieces have sharply different geometric models and asymptotic dimensions; for fixed rank almost every grading has odd center over large finite fields. In characteristic two a natural inner-adjoint method recovers only the trivial grading, although the full family has explicitly many others. This supplies quantitative and structural information relevant to computational modular-Lie grading studies, not just a restatement of a known homogeneous-basis classification.

The script establishes only the listed small cases; all unbounded claims are justified in the written proof. An unindexed or stronger enumeration of labelled Heisenberg gradings remains possible. No independent mathematical audit has been conducted.
