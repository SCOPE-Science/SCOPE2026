# Scientific self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The full complex operator space splits into a five-dimensional permutation-trivial sector, \(n-1\) independent five-dimensional standard sectors, and a complementary zero-sum leaf-block sector. Directly derived block evolution equations give the factors \(z(z+\gamma)T_n\), \(((z+\gamma)^2+ng^2)S_n\), and \((z+\gamma)^{n^2-3n+1}\), respectively; their degrees sum to \((n+1)^2\). The dissipativity identity and connectedness show a unique maximally mixed stationary state. The slow \(S_n\) root is isolated algebraically; quadratic stability and the cubic Routh–Hurwitz inequalities prove strict dominance over every other spectral component. Parameterizing the root produces a unique global dephasing optimum. A direct resolvent reduction proves the physically measurable leaf-population contrast has nonzero positive slow-pole residue. Independent finite dense-superoperator calculations and a direct Laplace-resolvent comparison corroborate the formulas. They do not replace the proof for arbitrary \(n\) and real rates.

## Originality — PASS, with priority uncertainty
Xu (arXiv:0903.1149) derives unitary star walks with a Laplacian Hamiltonian. Salimi (arXiv:0904.2054) studies coherent adjacency star-product walks. Anishchenko–Blumen–Mülken (arXiv:1111.7065) studies coherent star/complete-graph spreading. Bressanini–Benedetti–Paris (DOI 10.1007/s11128-022-03647-x) applies Haken–Strobl dephasing to star graphs numerically but employs the graph Laplacian and fidelity distance. The arXiv:2507.17880 paper studies noisy star relaxation through numerical profiles and fitted time scales. None of the inspected full texts gives the exact complete adjacency-star Liouvillian factorization, demonstrates domination by the standard sector, or derives the leaf-difference resolvent under this model. The known complete-graph dephasing calculation has a rescaled copy of the slow cubic, so the gap optimum **alone is not novel by implication**. The remaining characteristic-polynomial factors, exhaustive domination comparison and star leaf-population formula distinguish the final combined theorem. Search incompleteness, unpublished stronger theory, and model-convention ambiguity are residual risks; the evidence does not amount to independent priority certification.

## Value — PASS
Uniform site dephasing on an adjacency-star graph is a natural quantum-network transport model with one distinguished hub and \(n\) equivalent leaves. The complete Liouvillian classification supplies all decay modes and multiplicities in an otherwise \((n+1)^2\)-dimensional nonnormal generator. It identifies an exact leaf-difference observable exhibiting the slowest mode, and it quantifies the trade-off of increasing connectivity and retuning noise through a sharp global optimum. This is a motivated all-size structural classification, not merely a relabeling of the complete-graph cubic. It does not establish optimal finite-time mixing or cover heterogeneous rates.

## Scope and residual risks
The Hamiltonian is adjacency-based, not the alternative Laplacian Hamiltonian used in some published graph-walk models. Generalized graph-theoretic or representation-theoretic spectral principles might yield the same formula after specialization; none was located. Some sources concern different observables or coherent-only evolution. Numerical checks establish finite instances only.
