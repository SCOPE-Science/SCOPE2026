# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The update is exactly an orthogonal rank-one projector in Hessian-energy coordinates. A direct angular change of variables gives the Poisson density, whose first two even Fourier harmonics determine all needed fourth moments. The independent-step second-moment recurrence is a strictly positive \(2\times2\) transfer matrix, and its eigenvalues give an initial-state-independent exponential rate by Perron–Frobenius. The sharp one-step Rayleigh bound and strict spectral gap follow by explicit identities. Exact symbolic and controlled periodic-quadrature checks were replayed from the included verifier. The theorem assumes iid directions, positive definite curvature, and exact one-dimensional minimization; no finite experiment is used as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk.** Dereziński–Rebrova's inspected Gaussian sketch-and-project theory analyzes \(\lambda_{\min}(\mathbb EP)\), the sharp uniform-in-time contraction certificate, not the spectral radius of the repeated **fourth-moment** transfer operator. The original Random Pursuit and Variable Metric Random Pursuit papers analyze one-step expected progress and general bounds; the R-SGN paper analyzes a trust-region method and global tolerances. None of these statements implies the exact expression proved here; equivalent specialized work elsewhere remains a real priority risk, not a certified absence.

**Value — PASS.** The smallest anisotropic quadratic already exhibits a mathematically exact discrepancy between a sharp one-step bound and the actual long-run mean-square contraction. The new fourth-moment closure computes the entire expected-error sequence, not merely one special initial state, and shows a \(3/2\) improvement in the leading spectral-gap coefficient as conditioning grows. This is useful for interpreting sketch-and-project rate certificates and separates uniform-from-asymptotic guarantees without suggesting a general runtime theorem.

**Limitations.** Planar fixed SPD Hessian; iid Gaussian sketches; exact arithmetic and subproblems; mean-square quadratic error, not almost-sure/sample-path speed; no claims on multi-dimensional or safeguarded nonlinear methods.
