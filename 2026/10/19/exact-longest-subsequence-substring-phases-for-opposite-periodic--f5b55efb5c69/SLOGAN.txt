For two words formed by repeating opposite alphabet permutations, the exact longest subsequence-substring length is \(\min\{qn,m+n-1,m+\lceil m/(q-1)\rceil\}\), and every optimal factor is classified by its starting residue. When both words have equally many periods and \(q\ge3\), requiring contiguity in one word loses a linear number of symbols compared with an ordinary common subsequence.
