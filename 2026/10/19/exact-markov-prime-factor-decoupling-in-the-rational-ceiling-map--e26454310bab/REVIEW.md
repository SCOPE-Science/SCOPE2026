# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For every modulus and horizon the numerator is initially uniform in the units modulo the required power. Conditioning on each divisor step leaves the next reduced numerator uniform in the corresponding unit residue classes, by the explicitly proved unit-derivative Hensel bijection. Repeated conditioning gives the joint path law. Multiplicativity of Euler's totient proves prime-process independence. The finite enumerator confirms the exact law for nine nontrivial moduli across three horizons, but the all-horizon claim rests on the proof rather than those computations.

## Originality — PASS, with historical priority risk
Azevedo–Carvalho–Machiavelo (2012), Proposition 2.2, Theorem 2.3 and Corollary 2.4, already prove integer hitting-time counts and prime-power marginal counts using a related polynomial bijection. Those marginal facts are not claimed as original. Their inspected full paper does not state the joint denominator trajectory law or independence of complete prime-power paths. Targeted published-result searches and mathematical record comparisons found no theorem implying the claimed full path law. The underlying Hensel bijection is close enough to the original proof that independent historical and implication review remains important.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The complete finite-dimensional law organizes a deterministic arithmetic map into a canonical divisor-valued Markov process and mutually independent prime-valuation processes. This gives nontrivial joint observables and conditional predictions unavailable from the scalar hitting-time histogram alone. The property is uniform over all starting denominators and every finite observation horizon, rather than an arbitrary finite table.

## Limits
The identity concerns compatible finite-horizon sampling laws; it does not assert independent randomness for a fixed rational starting point, nor solve the individual rational eventual-integrality question. Independent scientific review has not occurred.
