# Scientific review: Exact mean-square ridge stabilization of rank-one indefinite Nyström

## Correctness — PASS

For a Haar-uniform direction on the unit circle, the normalized quadratic form of a real symmetric matrix with eigenvalues \(\pm\lambda\) has the arcsine distribution. The matrix identity \(A^2=\lambda^2I\) yields an exact quadratic error in the scalar core coefficient. The arcsine resolvent integral \(\mathbb E[(z^2+x^2)^{-1}]=[x\sqrt{1+x^2}]^{-1}\) and its derivative reconstruct the entire Tikhonov expected-risk curve without asymptotic approximations. The factorized derivative has exactly one positive root and proves global optimality, including endpoint behavior. The hard cutoff calculation integrates the retained inverse and rejected zero-core regions separately, including all thresholds above the spectral scale. The completed-square identity independently proves the scalar-oracle lower benchmark. The plain inverse law has a \(t^{-1}\) tail and the stated exact moment boundary. The supplied verifier confirms the critical algebra symbolically, direct rational matrix identities, and independent angular quadratures. Finite checks supplement but do not establish the universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, residual literature risk

Park and Nakatsukasa, *Randomized low-rank approximation for symmetric indefinite matrices*, arXiv:2212.01127, explicitly give a two-dimensional indefinite example with arbitrarily large plain-Nyström error. Its formula entails the pointwise plain-core blow-up here; neither that observation nor the elementary arcsine mechanism is treated as an independent discovery. Their contribution is an oversampled, rank-truncated-core approximation with nuclear-norm guarantees, not the exact expected squared Frobenius risk for a normalized one-direction Tikhonov inverse and its comparison to the optimally tuned scalar hard cutoff. The primary text was materially read in its introduction, method, analysis and experiments.

Bucci, Nakatsukasa and Park (arXiv:2511.15583) investigate truncation, numerical roundoff and mainly positive-semidefinite column-subset Nyström; their full publicly reproduced text was inspected in Sections 1–3. Cai, Nagy and Xi (DOI:10.1137/21M1424627) study deterministic landmark selection for indefinite kernels. Chen and collaborators (arXiv:2608.20531) use two-sided sketched least squares and oversampling; only an abstract was accessible, so exact exclusion of a relevant special-case result in its full text is **not** claimed. Classical work by Mulholland (DOI:10.1093/biomet/57.1.155) addresses densities of ratios of quadratic forms and could subsume the distributional subclaim. Mathematical-record and in-house claim overlap comparisons did not locate the complete scalar-core ridge-risk/optimal-cutoff comparison. This is a self-review, not an independent exhaustive novelty certificate; specialized equivalent work remains possible.

## Value — PASS

Choosing a scalar-core inverse is a natural design decision when indefinite Nyström fails because its sketched core nearly cancels. A qualitative warning that plain inversion can diverge does not determine *which* stabilizer minimizes expected matrix error, whether an optimal hard cutoff matches soft regularization, or the quantitative distance from the best coefficient attainable with the same sampled column. The finding answers all three for the smallest rotationally invariant indefinite benchmark, supplies unique scale-calibrated parameter thresholds, and quantifies a strict mean-square advantage of ridge over hard truncation. This benchmark can test and diagnose scalar-core stabilization policies; it is not a generic large-dimensional algorithmic guarantee. The problem is defined by a canonical indefinite eigenvalue pair and a standard normalized Gaussian sketch, not by arbitrary hand-picked matrix entries or a gratuitous cutoff.

## Scope and remaining uncertainty

Exact arithmetic; real \(2\times2\) spectrum \(\{\lambda,-\lambda\}\); independent normalized Gaussian sketch; one rank-one Nyström column; squared Frobenius loss; fixed positive ridge parameter or hard threshold. The oracle may use the known spectral magnitude. Pointwise dominance, non-normalized sketch results, finite-precision robustness, dimension-independent optimality, and different matrix spectra are unproved. The 2026 two-sided sketched-least-squares full text was inaccessible through the attempted lawful sources and remains a priority risk. No independent scientific audit has been conducted.
