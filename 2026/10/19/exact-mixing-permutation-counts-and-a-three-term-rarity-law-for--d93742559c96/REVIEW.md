# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The refined fine-interval Markov graph of the piecewise-affine tent map and the published nonzero-spectrum reduction yield a reduced matrix of the exact form \((I+H)P_U/2\). Closure is equivalent to simultaneous invariance under \(U\) and \(H\). An independent cyclic-period parity proof makes every irreducible reduced matrix primitive. Partitioning permutations by the orbit containing the reflection's unique fixed point gives a disjoint exact recurrence. Explicit boundary coefficients and a geometric-ratio tail estimate establish the three asymptotic coefficients \(2,3,22\). The accompanying checker exhausts all 5,166 shuffles for the first three admissible grids in the full fine Markov graph, independent of the combinatorial enumeration. Its finite checks do not purport to prove infinite asymptotics.

## Originality
PASS on available primary-source and mathematical-record comparisons, with a residual priority risk from general transitive-permutation-pair enumeration. The 2014 motivating work [1] establishes the existence of nonmixing tent shuffles and analyzes spectral mixing bounds but not their exact number or fraction. The 2012 precursor [2] counts shuffles of a different, stretch-and-fold map; for odd grids that map has no nonmixing permutations. Earlier related local work counts worst-mixing shuffles for the doubling map, a different dynamical family and invariant, and does not imply this tent-map criterion or count. Public mathematical record searches on the actual claim, aliases, and involution formulation returned no coverage. A failed search is not a proof of absolute novelty.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The fraction of shuffles preserving mixing is the natural enumerative counterpart to the known existence and worst-rate spectral results for this exact family. A sharp three-term nonmixing probability, plus the precise transitive-group obstruction, distinguishes frequent mixing from isolated nonmixing examples and exposes the special role of the unique reflection fixed point. This is an exact quantitative answer for all odd grids, not a parameter-specific computation or an unmotivated combinatorial slice.

## Scientific limits
The theorem concerns topological mixing and even grid sizes are not covered. It does not predict mixing speed among mixing maps. Other group-theoretic enumerations may be equivalent, so historical-priority certainty is limited by accessible comparisons. The proof and verifier use the same mathematical definitions; the checker is an independent finite algorithm, not an independent research audit.
