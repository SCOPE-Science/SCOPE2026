## Correctness — PASS
The claim is a theorem for every integer expansion base \(b\ge2\) and every positive integer comparison length \(m\), with normalized Lebesgue initialization. The proof checks the exact domain \(E_j\), the image \(T_b(E_j)=(\alpha_j,1)\), the equivalence between admissible histories and nondecreasing labels, and the probability of each complete label cylinder \(\ell_{j_{m-1}}/b^{m-1}\). The two hockey-stick identities yield the exact probability with its normalization. Uniform positive compositions characterize full visitation, whose conditional probability tends to one. Their rescaled cuts give uniform Dirichlet spacings. The deterministic \(W_1\) bound proves the conditional occupation-measure limit; an order-statistic identity gives the limiting scalar orbit average. The precise result does not rely on finite numerical extrapolation. Exact-arithmetic verification agrees on 25 independent geometric horizons and 456 path masses.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The event of an ever-longer strictly increasing orbit is a canonical ordinal statistic of a standard Bernoulli dynamical system. Its exact probability has a nontrivial base-dependent polynomial prefactor, but the more distinctive result is statistical: conditioning a Lebesgue-typical expanding orbit on this increasingly rare ordinal event selects a *random convex combination of repelling fixed-point invariant measures*. The mixing weights converge to the uniform Dirichlet simplex law. This supplies a concrete rare-event selection mechanism with an exactly computable limiting orbit-average distribution, rather than a routine probability for an isolated short pattern.

## Limitations
The theorem covers consecutive increasing comparisons for integer \(b\ge2\), fixed \(b\) in the limit, and normalized Lebesgue initialization. It does not provide general ordinal-pattern distributions, noninteger-base formulas, or a theorem about typical unconditioned orbits. Endpoint exceptions are null sets. The literature comparison is substantial but cannot prove absolute historical novelty.
