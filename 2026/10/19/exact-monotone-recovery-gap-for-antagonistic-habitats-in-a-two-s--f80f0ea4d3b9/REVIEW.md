# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

In a positive stationary state, substituting each local occupancy as local fitness times vacancy times the relevant species mean gives two independent equations in the two vacancies. Subtracting them gives equal vacancies for δ>0, hence both vacancies equal 1/r. The two habitat occupancies then force equal species means s=(r−1)/(2r). The resulting positive state is strictly feasible and solves all four equations. Linearizing a coordinate adds the same-species mean perturbation times 1/r and subtracts s times the total habitat perturbation, in addition to the diagonal death term. The simultaneous species/habitat swap gives symmetric 2×2 restrictions with the same diagonals, −1/(r±δ)−s+1/(2r), and off-diagonal elements 1/(2r)−s and 1/(2r)+s. The latter equals 1/2 and has strictly larger absolute value, giving the frozen exact largest eigenvalue. With t=r²−δ², its derivative has sign that of (r²+δ²)/√(t²+4δ²)−r; squaring compares positive numbers and gives difference (r²−1)t² in favor of r. Thus the spectral abscissa decreases from zero, and strict negativity implies local exponential stability. Expansion in δ² gives coefficient −(r−1)/r⁴ in the exponent. At δ→r, the radical is δ/t+o(1), so the combined singular terms tend to −1/(2r) and g tends to (r−1)/(2r). At δ=0, the ratio of the two species means is conserved, their sum obeys the scalar logistic equation, and habitat differences decay transversely at the positive equilibria. This confirms neutrality along the equilibrium segment rather than attraction to a particular point.
