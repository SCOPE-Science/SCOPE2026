# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact decomposition by the number of ones has explicit boundaries: zero and singleton images contribute two, windows with three or more ones inject into image words through the distance between the first surviving ones, and two-one images reduce to finite integer intervals. The algebraic identity \(u+\ell_-=A\) closes the count in every geometric scale. The initial scales and overlapping thresholds are handled separately. The sharp limiting ratios follow by evaluating monotone ramps and plateaux. A direct enumeration of \(1400\) finite cells and an independent interval computation of \(22648\) cells agreed with the formula; these experiments are corroboration, not proof.

## Originality — PASS within checked evidence
Campbell and Rampersad, arXiv:2604.25811, introduce the one-step tortoise complexity but analyze Thue–Morse and paperfolding sequences. Their bounded-gap comparison theorem does not apply to the unbounded geometric gaps. Gheorghiciuc, arXiv:math/0512256, proves general formulas for ordinary factor complexity of gap-increasing words, even ones with exponential support positions; those formulas do not count collisions after applying tortoise. Searchable related work on powers-of-two supports describes a distinct sparse-letter invariant. No equivalent or stronger statement about this image count was located; priority cannot be guaranteed against unindexed results.

## Value — PASS
The support of powers is a canonical natural increasing-gap example already important in ordinary symbolic complexity. The new theorem completely computes the one-step image complexity over every integer base and length, exposing long alternating growth and stagnation regimes and giving two distinct sharp normalized accumulation endpoints. This furnishes a concrete test family for the recently defined tortoise invariant, rather than a small finite curiosity.

## Residual limitations
Only one tortoise operation is analyzed, only the specified geometric support is covered, and the computation does not certify an infinite theorem. Literature access and indexing are imperfect, and independent scientific review has not occurred.
