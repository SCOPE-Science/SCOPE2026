# Scientific review: exact optimal pair-batch probabilities on a three-column Gram path

**Correctness — PASS.** The claim is a conditional, one-step, squared prediction-error minimax result for exact two-coordinate least-squares updates. The residual after omitting column \(i\) is an orthogonal rank-one projection onto the normalized complement normal. The balanced sign witness \((1,-1,1)\) provides a universal Rayleigh lower bound \(\Delta/Z\), while a two-edge positive-semidefinite decomposition gives the matching upper bound. Rank-two strictness of this decomposition yields uniqueness. Domain positivity follows from positive definiteness and Cauchy–Schwarz. Reproducible exact-rational and direct-projection checks agree, without substituting experiments for proof.

**Originality — PASS, with bibliographic uncertainty.** The 2026 joint-batch least-squares preprint supplies the motivating sampling framework, while the established sketch-and-project literature supplies general projection and rate formulas. The result is not the general framework, the generic semidefinite formulation, or its normalization identities: it is the explicit unique optimizer and exact scalar value for *all* positive-definite three-column Gram paths with two unequal adjacent correlations. Scientific searches compared aliases and larger implications. An earlier public finding on optimal **singleton** sampling under inverse-positive Hessian assumptions has a different update operator and criterion; it does not state this pair-batch result. The motivating preprint's full theorem/results sections remained inaccessible, leaving a genuine but not decisive overlap risk.

**Value — PASS.** This is a natural complete model: three columns are the smallest size with competing two-coordinate batch choices, while the path permits two independently varying nonzero correlations and arbitrary column scalings. Rather than a prescribed probability heuristic, the result gives the unique provably optimal static law, an attained error factor, and a full sensitivity to adjacent correlations. In the symmetric \(u=v=3/5\) case it replaces uniform probabilities by \((4/9,1/9,4/9)\), improving the sharp worst-case one-step factor from \((41+3\sqrt{137})/96\) to \(7/9\). It can serve as an exact benchmark for learned fixed batch distributions. It makes no unsupported claim about real-world speedups.
