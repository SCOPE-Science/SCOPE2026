# Exact optimal weighting for an eight-sample quadratic Hermite Fourier phase-retrieval frame

## Finding
Let \(P(z)=a+bz+cz^2\) have arbitrary complex coefficients. Acquire five squared magnitudes \(\lvert P(\zeta_5^j)\rvert^2\), \(0\leq j<5\), and three derivative squared magnitudes \(\lvert P'(\zeta_3^k)\rvert^2\), \(0\leq k<3\), where \(\zeta_n=e^{2\pi i/n}\). Multiply the derivative-intensity channel by \(\sqrt t\), where \(t>0\), and denote by \(\mathcal L_t:\operatorname{Herm}(3)\to\mathbb R^8\) the corresponding linear lifting, so \(\mathcal L_t(xx^*)\) is precisely the weighted intensity vector of the coefficient vector \(x\). Use the Frobenius norm on Hermitian matrices and the Euclidean norm on the eight outputs.

**Theorem.** For every \(t>0\), the Hermitian kernel of \(\mathcal L_t\) is the one-dimensional subspace generated by \(Q=\operatorname{diag}(3,-4,1)\). Thus all eight measurements recover every quadratic coefficient vector up to global unit phase, although they do not recover arbitrary Hermitian matrices. Among **all positive relative derivative-channel weights**, the condition number of \(\mathcal L_t\) restricted to \((\mathbb RQ)^\perp\) has the **unique** minimizer
\[
 t_*=\frac{40}{81},\qquad
 \min_{t>0}\kappa(\mathcal L_t\vert_{Q^\perp})^2=\frac{33+\sqrt{985}}4.
\]
At this weight the squared singular values, including multiplicities, are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 0\ (1),\quad
 \frac{215-5\sqrt{985}}{27}\ (2),\quad 5\ (2),\\
 \frac{1085-25\sqrt{985}}{54}\ (1),\quad
 \frac{215+5\sqrt{985}}{27}\ (2),\quad
 \frac{1085+25\sqrt{985}}{54}\ (1).
 \end{gathered}
\]
Moreover the optimally weighted measurements satisfy the **global lifted phase-retrieval stability inequality**, for every \(x,y\in\mathbb C^3\),
\[
 \bigl\|\mathcal L_{40/81}(xx^*-yy^*)\bigr\|_2^2
 \geq \frac{215-5\sqrt{985}}{702}\,
 \bigl\|xx^*-yy^*\bigr\|_F^2.
\]
This explicit lower coefficient is not asserted sharp for the rank-two cone.

## Assumptions and scope
The two Fourier root grids have orders five and three, respectively; the polynomial degree is at most two, with no nonzero-coefficient or nonvanishing restriction. There are exactly eight scalar intensity readings. A gain \(\sqrt t\) is applied to the **recorded derivative squared intensities**, not to the derivative polynomial before squaring; the corresponding quadratic-frame vector amplitude would instead be scaled by \(t^{1/4}\). Global phase is the sole unavoidable symmetry. The optimality assertion ranges over this physically meaningful one-parameter class of equal-within-channel gains, **not** over arbitrary eight-vector complex frames, nonuniform individual sample weights, or all nonlinear phase-retrieval metrics.

## Proof
Write a Hermitian matrix as diagonal \((u_0,u_1,u_2)\) and upper off-diagonal entries \(b_{01},b_{02},b_{12}\). Fourier orthogonality separately on the five and three roots gives the exact identity
\[
 \begin{split}
 \|\mathcal L_t(H)\|_2^2={}&5\bigl[(u_0+u_1+u_2)^2+2|b_{01}+b_{12}|^2+2|b_{02}|^2\bigr]\\
 &+3t\bigl[(u_1+4u_2)^2+8|b_{12}|^2\bigr].
 \end{split}
\]
The Frobenius norm is \(\sum_i u_i^2+2\sum_{i<j}|b_{ij}|^2\). Hence the squared singular-value operator splits orthogonally into: one diagonal real block
\[
 D_t=5uu^{\mathsf T}+3tww^{\mathsf T},\qquad u=(1,1,1)^{\mathsf T},\quad w=(0,1,4)^{\mathsf T};
\]
two identical real off-diagonal blocks
\[
 C_t=\begin{pmatrix}5&5\\5&5+12t\end{pmatrix};
\]
and the scalar eigenvalue \(5\) twice from the real and imaginary parts of \(b_{02}\). The two characteristic polynomials are exactly
\[
 \det(\lambda I_2-C_t)=\lambda^2-(10+12t)\lambda+60t,
\]
\[
 \det(\lambda I_3-D_t)=\lambda\bigl[\lambda^2-(15+51t)\lambda+390t\bigr].
\]
Because \(u\) and \(w\) are linearly independent and \(t>0\), the sole diagonal null vector is \((3,-4,1)\), and the other blocks are positive definite. This proves the asserted Hermitian kernel. If two coefficient vectors give identical intensities, their projector difference has rank at most two and lies in the kernel; since \(Q\) has rank three, that difference must vanish. Equality of rank-one projectors means equality up to global phase (also for the zero vector).

It remains to optimize the condition number. Put \(r=\sqrt{985}\), \(t_*=40/81\), and \(R_*=(33+r)/4\). Construct **fixed nonzero** vectors
\[
 v=\begin{pmatrix}1\\(16-r)/27\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 x=u+\frac{11+r}{54}w.
\]
Straight substitution using \(r^2=985\) establishes, for **all** real \(t\),
\[
 \frac{x^{\mathsf T}D_t x}{x^{\mathsf T}x}
 =R_*\frac{v^{\mathsf T}C_t v}{v^{\mathsf T}v}.
\]
At \(t=t_*\), these are actual extreme eigenvectors:
\[
 C_{t_*}v=\frac{215-5r}{27}v,
 \qquad D_{t_*}x=\frac{1085+25r}{54}x.
\]
For arbitrary \(t>0\), the largest eigenvalue of the full lifting is at least the largest eigenvalue of \(D_t\), and its least positive eigenvalue is at most the least eigenvalue of \(C_t\). The fixed-vector Rayleigh certificate therefore yields
\[
 \kappa(\mathcal L_t\vert_{Q^\perp})^2
 \geq \frac{\lambda_{\max}(D_t)}{\lambda_{\min}(C_t)}
 \geq R_*.
\]
At \(t_*\) direct factorization of the two characteristic polynomials gives exactly the nine eigenvalues listed in the Finding, and the positive extremes are the eigenvalues just displayed. Thus the bound is attained. For \(t\ne t_*\), the fixed vector \(v\) cannot be an eigenvector of \(C_t\): its first row would force the same eigenvalue as at \(t_*\), and then the second row forces \(t=t_*\). The second Rayleigh inequality is therefore strict. This proves global **uniqueness** of the optimal weight, without differentiation or numerical optimization.

Finally, the normal-operator spectral decomposition and its smallest positive eigenvalue \(f_*=(215-5r)/27\) give \(\|\mathcal L_{t_*}(H)\|_2^2\geq f_*\operatorname{dist}_F(H,\mathbb RQ)^2\) for every Hermitian \(H\). Any Hermitian \(H\) of rank at most two obeys, by the two-largest-singular-value inequality applied to \(Q\),
\[
 |\operatorname{tr}(HQ)|^2\leq(4^2+3^2)\|H\|_F^2=25\|H\|_F^2.
\]
Since \(\|Q\|_F^2=26\), its squared distance to \(\mathbb RQ\) is at least \(\|H\|_F^2/26\). Substitute \(H=xx^*-yy^*\), which has rank at most two, to obtain the displayed global stability inequality.

## Verification
The supplied `checker.py` checks both characteristic polynomials symbolically, the Hermitian kernel generator, both exact eigenvector relations, the all-weight Rayleigh identity, the exact algebraic extremal ratio, and the order of the remaining eigenvalues. An independent explicit real-Hermitian frame matrix is constructed numerically for five positive weights and its nine squared singular values are compared with the two derived characteristic polynomials to \(3\times10^{-10}\). The recorded output is in `checks.txt`. The proof for every real \(t>0\) is the algebra above; numerical checks are not used as a substitute for it.

## Relationship to prior work
Jaming and Rathmair, arXiv:2205.08753 (first posted 2022-05-18), §3.1 Proposition 3.2, proved **qualitative uniqueness** for polynomial and derivative modulus sampling at sufficiently many common root sites. That argument, combined with the degree-one derivative intensity for quadratics, also implies that five polynomial samples and three derivative samples suffice. **Neither the eight-sample injectivity alone nor this simple sample-count observation is claimed as new.** Conca, Edidin, Hering, and Vinzant, arXiv:1312.0158, treat generic minimal-cardinality complex phase-retrieving frames and the rank-two Hermitian obstruction. These results do not give the singular spectrum, the unique optimal cross-channel gain \(40/81\), the sharp lifting-space condition-number value, or the stated concrete projector-stability certificate for this explicit Hermite-Fourier sampling frame. These quantitative statements are the distinct content here.

## Limitations
The sharp optimization concerns the **full Hermitian lifting modulo its fixed one-dimensional kernel**, not the sharp Lipschitz condition number on the nonlinear rank-two projector-difference cone. The displayed nonlinear stability coefficient is a certified, potentially nonsharp lower bound. No claim is made about the best weighting across all possible eight-vector frames, different Fourier grids, or perturbation robustness. A previously uncatalogued explicit weighted-sampling computation may still duplicate this formula.
