## Correctness — PASS
Every unital generating tuple must have distinct vertex evaluation vectors. Fixing them, the proof establishes necessary and sufficient surjectivity of the loop coefficient maps and the off-diagonal coefficient maps restricted to the kernel of the vertex-label difference. A telescoping identity gives necessity; lifted vertex idempotents and commutators give sufficiency. Independent full-rank counts then provide the formula for all finite fields and all quivers with square-zero radical. The standalone checker exhaustively validates 17 configurations, including an extension field, and is not substituted for the proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified by inspected sources)
Relevant inspected parts of Sercombe–Shalev (arXiv:2009.01115), Iovanov–Sistko (arXiv:1705.00762), and Kravchenko–Mazur–Petrenko (arXiv:1001.2873) give broader generation bounds, maximal-subalgebra classification, and related generator counts, respectively, but not this all-quiver pointwise formula. Searches of published mathematical records also found related commutator counts for Kronecker quiver algebras, not random-generation counts. The new theorem is an exact unconditional law, not the known qualitative generation problem. Residual risk: inclusion–exclusion from older maximal-subalgebra literature could give an unstated or equivalent law. No claim of certified literature uniqueness is made.

## Value — PASS
The probability is an algebraic invariant with a genuine family-wide answer. It supplies the sharp minimal number of generators, identifies the nontrivial loss of one free dimension for each off-diagonal arrow block, and shows surprising incidence-independence within its exact structural profile. The result quantitatively refines known general bounds and answers a natural explicit case of random associative-algebra generation, rather than simply tabulating an arbitrary small instance.

## Scientific limitations
Only split basic algebras \(kQ/J^2\) with \(J^2=0\) are covered. Finite exhaustive checks do not replace the infinite-family proof. Equivalent earlier computations remain possible, and the proof has not been independently audited.
