# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** The claimed median-rank selection follows from explicit left/right cumulative influence-weight inequalities. In the central-pair regime the middle gap obeys a two-sided linear system and decays at rate two, whereas all remaining exterior deviations decay at rate one. The proposed trajectories retain sorted rank order for every finite time, so they solve the original nonsmooth switching system rather than merely one guessed linear branch. Uniqueness and nonexpansiveness of the nearest-own median map extend the formulas to equal-valued initial conditions. Exact-rational tests from the attached source script checked 1,755 profiles and 56,160 median-versus-derivative comparisons. The proof, not the finite enumeration, establishes the infinite ranges.

**Originality (PASS, subject to literature risk).** Han et al., arXiv:2404.16318 (first submitted 25 April 2024; full 2025 revision inspected), establish the model, its equilibrium set, uniqueness and global convergence, but their statements do not determine explicit trajectories or the complete limiting-rank map on homogeneous complete networks. General cohesive-set classifications do not by themselves identify each attracting equilibrium for every ordered input. Discrete-time asynchronous median and synchronous self-appraisal references have non-equivalent trajectory rules. Targeted searches of additional mathematical findings and literature revealed no theorem containing the formulas and threshold staircase. Specialist or unindexed work could still duplicate the result.

**Value (PASS).** A finite, homogeneous social network with self-influence is a natural benchmark for median-driven compromise. The theorem gives a complete non-generic-as-well-as-generic exact solution and sharply separates universal consensus from polarization, determines which initial order statistics survive and how quickly noncore opinions approach them, and exposes a parity-sensitive discontinuity in terminal opinion selection. These questions are not answered by mere knowledge of global convergence or the existence of cohesive subsets.

**Scope and limitations.** No extrapolation is made to heterogeneous networks, stochastic updates or empirical populations; cluster counts for tied inputs can be smaller than generic counts. The most relevant directly inspected original source is the full arXiv HTML revision. Remaining overlap risk comes from unindexed exact reductions in specialist opinion-dynamics literature, not from a known stronger covering statement.
