# Scientific review: fresh-Gaussian block QB stopping

**Correctness — PASS.** For an identity input, the residual remains an exact orthogonal projector, so an independent normalized Gaussian block has a conditional chi-square statistic with degrees of freedom determined only by the remaining rank. This supplies genuinely independent first-success tests, the complete finite stopping law, and an exact false-acceptance probability. The nonstandard step is the Gumbel limit: a uniform gamma/normal moderate-deviation estimate, exponentially negligible far tails, an integrated Gaussian survival function, and a vanishing sum of squared hazards establish the product limit. Since pruning deletes at most one accepted block, it cannot affect the Gumbel scale. The standalone numerical program passed 784 finite checks; the proof, not these checks, establishes the asymptotic theorem.

**Originality — PASS, with residual uncertainty.** The closest earlier mathematical claim, opaque identifier `88fc5e21-b8ad-4125-8a76-f940bc870968`, establishes a first-order rank-deficit and near-certain tolerance failure for *unit-block relative* Frobenius QB with a dynamically estimated denominator. These qualitative and leading-order phenomena are explicitly not claimed again. A constant prescribed *absolute* denominator, the full finite chi-square product law for every fixed block width, and the centered second-order Gumbel fluctuations are not consequences of that claim. Source Algorithm 2 of Smith, Do, and Chen supplies the raw procedure and marginal unbiasedness without these laws. Gorman et al.'s HSS criteria, Pearce et al.'s LU-based ID error estimates, and Liu and Qu's shared-probe certification have different decision statistics or calibration, so their inspected results do not imply the present distribution. Record searches and source-text checks did not reveal a stronger equivalent theorem. Risk remains from unindexed or not exhaustively inspected work and the potential application of general sequential chi-square extreme-value results not explicitly framed as randomized QB.

**Value — PASS.** This is a structural sequential-testing effect in a newly studied, practical matrix-free error-indicator algorithm: an unbiased residual norm estimate does not calibrate repeated first-success stopping. Exact finite products quantify failure risk at specified dimensions and block widths; the sharp Gumbel law determines the missing second-order quantile scale and proves that last-block pruning cannot remove asymptotic false acceptance. The input is deliberately flat-spectrum to isolate stopping rather than spectral approximation quality. The relative tolerance miss tends to zero even as its probability approaches one. This is not a general theorem for arbitrary matrices, relative estimated denominators, growing block sizes, or floating point.
