# Exact spectral-rank and positivity criterion for weighted Cartesian star boxes

## Finding

For every integer \(d\ge 2\), integer vector \(r_1,\ldots,r_d\ge 2\), and positive real couplings \(\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_d\), consider the continuous-time adjacency quantum walk on a weighted Cartesian product of stars:
\[
H=\sum_{j=1}^d\omega_j I\otimes\cdots\otimes A(K_{1,r_j})\otimes\cdots\otimes I,
\qquad \nu_j=\omega_j\sqrt{r_j}.
\]
Here \(K_{1,r}\) has one hub and \(r\) leaves. The Cesàro average-mixing matrix is
\[
\widehat M=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\int_0^T
\bigl(e^{-itH}\circ\overline{e^{-itH}}\bigr)\,dt.
\]
For each factor define three mutually orthogonal projectors \(Q_{j,0},Q_{j,L},Q_{j,R}\): respectively onto the constant vector; the subspace supported on the leaves with leaf sum zero; and the radial line spanned by \((r_j,-1,\ldots,-1)\). The corresponding dimensions are \(1,r_j-1,1\).

Define the finite Laurent polynomials (the local index \(j\) is suppressed in these formulas):
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_{r,0}(z)&=1,\\
p_{r,L}(z)&=\frac{r-2+z+z^{-1}}{r},\\
p_{r,R}(z)&=\frac{2(r-1)+(r+1)(z^2+z^{-2})}{4r}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Write \(a_{r,s}(k)=[z^k]p_{r,s}(z)\), with absent coefficients interpreted as zero. For every \(\boldsymbol s=(s_1,\ldots,s_d)\in\{0,L,R\}^d\), put
\[
\Lambda_{\boldsymbol s}
=\sum_{k_1,\ldots,k_d\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}}
\mathbf1_{\{\sum_j k_j\nu_j=0\}}
\prod_{j=1}^d a_{r_j,s_j}(k_j).
\]
Then the **complete spectral and kernel classification** is
\[
\boxed{\widehat M=\sum_{\boldsymbol s\in\{0,L,R\}^d}
\Lambda_{\boldsymbol s}\bigotimes_{j=1}^d Q_{j,s_j},}
\]
with every \(\Lambda_{\boldsymbol s}\ge0\), and
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}\widehat M=
\sum_{\boldsymbol s\in\{0,L,R\}^d}
\mathbf1_{\{\Lambda_{\boldsymbol s}>0\}}
\prod_{j:s_j=L}(r_j-1).}
\]
The kernel is the direct sum of the indicated tensor sectors for which \(\Lambda_{\boldsymbol s}=0\). Since every nonzero Laurent coefficient is strictly positive, \(\Lambda_{\boldsymbol s}>0\) **if and only if** an allowed signed frequency combination \(\sum_jk_j\nu_j=0\) exists with \(a_{r_j,s_j}(k_j)>0\) at each factor.

In particular, for **every positive choice of the couplings**, the exact positive-definiteness criterion is
\[
\boxed{\widehat M\succ0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r_j\ge3\ \text{for all }j.}
\]
If there is no nonzero relation \(\sum_jk_j\nu_j=0\) with \(k_j\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}\), then the generic rank is
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}\widehat M=2^{\#\{j:r_j=2\}}
\prod_{j:r_j\ge3}(r_j+1).}
\]
At equal positive couplings on the \(d\)-fold Cartesian power of \(K_{1,2}=P_3\), the rank is exactly \((3^d+1)/2\). These are consequences of the same spectral classification, not separate assertions requiring additional hypotheses.

## Assumptions and scope

The star sizes \(r_j\) are integers at least two; the number of factors is finite and at least two; all \(\omega_j\) are strictly positive real numbers. The Hamiltonian uses real positive adjacency couplings, with no local potential, direction-dependent magnetic phase, inter-factor interaction, or stochastic resampling of different coordinates. Vertex labels distinguish the hub from its leaves. The time average is a single shared-time Cesàro limit, not a product of independent time averages. The frequency equality is an exact real equality, not a numerical tolerance.

## Proof

**Local decomposition.** Fix one star \(K_{1,r}\), let \(\nu=\omega\sqrt r\), and write \(c=\cos(\nu t)\), \(s=\sin(\nu t)\). In the hub/leaf vertex basis, the propagator generated by \(\omega A(K_{1,r})\) has entries
\[
U_{hh}=c,\qquad U_{h\ell}=-\frac{i s}{\sqrt r},\qquad
U_{\ell\ell'}=\delta_{\ell\ell'}+\frac{c-1}{r}.
\]
Indeed, the star adjacency acts on the hub and normalized uniform leaf vector as the \(2\times2\) off-diagonal matrix with coupling \(\sqrt r\), and vanishes on leaf contrasts. Taking squared moduli yields a real symmetric doubly stochastic matrix \(M_r(t)\) with the same three invariant subspaces at all times. On the all-ones vector its eigenvalue is \(1\). A leaf contrast has eigenvalue equal to the diagonal leaf entry minus an off-diagonal leaf entry:
\[
\ell_r(t)=1+\frac{2(c-1)}r=\frac{r-2+2c}{r}.
\]
On \((r,-1,\ldots,-1)\), the hub row gives
\[
\rho_r(t)=\frac{(r+1)c^2-1}{r}.
\]
Using \(2c=z+z^{-1}\) and \(2c^2=1+\cos(2\nu t)\), with \(z=e^{i\nu t}\), gives exactly \(p_{r,L}(z)\) and \(p_{r,R}(z)\) stated above. The three projectors sum to the identity and are orthogonal, including at \(r=2\) when the leaf-contrast sector has dimension one.

**Tensorization before averaging.** All summands in \(H\) commute. Therefore \(e^{-itH}=\bigotimes_j e^{-it\omega_j A(K_{1,r_j})}\) and entrywise absolute-squaring yields
\[
M(t)=\bigotimes_{j=1}^d M_{r_j}(t)
=\sum_{\boldsymbol s}\left(\prod_jp_{r_j,s_j}(e^{i\nu_jt})\right)
\bigotimes_jQ_{j,s_j}.
\]
Each scalar multiplier is a finite sum of exponentials \(e^{it\sum_j k_j\nu_j}\). Their Cesàro limits are \(1\) at zero frequency and \(0\) elsewhere. This proves the displayed spectral formula, for arbitrary positive real couplings without any rationality assumptions. The dimension of each tensor sector is \(\prod_{j:s_j=L}(r_j-1)\), so the rank and kernel formulas follow.

**No cancellation of surviving modes.** For every \(r\ge2\), all Laurent coefficients of \(p_{r,s}\) are nonnegative. In particular, \(p_{r,R}\) has strictly positive zero coefficient \((r-1)/(2r)\), and \(p_{r,L}\) has zero coefficient \((r-2)/r\), which vanishes only at \(r=2\). Thus the zero-frequency contribution is a sum of nonnegative terms. It is positive precisely when at least one allowed frequency cancellation exists; there is no destructive cancellation between such terms.

**Robust positive definiteness and the generic rank.** If all \(r_j\ge3\), all sectors have strictly positive zero coefficient, hence every \(\Lambda_{\boldsymbol s}>0\), irrespective of resonances. Conversely, if \(r_j=2\) for some \(j\), choose \(s_j=L\) and \(s_k=0\) for all \(k\ne j\). Its scalar average is the average of \(\cos(\nu_jt)\), namely zero for \(\nu_j>0\); so \(\widehat M\) has a nonzero kernel. In the absence of bounded nonzero frequency relations, only the all-zero frequency tuple survives. Exactly the sectors avoiding \(L\) at every \(r_j=2\) survive, yielding the generic rank formula.

**Isotropic three-vertex-path powers and an illustrative smallest hybrid.** For \(r_j=2\) and equal couplings, each \(L\)-sector supplies a frequency of odd integer parity \(\pm1\), each \(R\)-sector supplies \(0,\pm2\), and each \(0\)-sector supplies \(0\). A zero sum is possible exactly when the number of \(L\) coordinates is even (pair their signs and take all \(R\) frequencies zero). There are \((3^d+1)/2\) such one-dimensional tensor sectors. More finely, on the 12-vertex weighted product \(P_3\square K_{1,3}\) with effective frequencies \(\nu_1,\nu_2>0\), the rank is \(10\) if \(\nu_1=\nu_2\), \(9\) if \(\nu_1=2\nu_2\), and \(8\) otherwise: the first equality restores the two-dimensional \(L\otimes L\) sector and the second the one-dimensional \(L\otimes R\) sector. This exhaustive two-frequency case follows directly from the permitted Fourier supports and proves the sharpness of distinct resonant rank jumps.

## Verification

The included `verify.py` uses only the Python standard library and exact rational arithmetic. Run `python verify.py` after extracting this file and `verify.py` into the same directory. It reconstructs mixing matrices in two algebraically separate ways: (i) Laurent-frequency coefficients combined with fixed tensor sector projectors, and (ii) spectral projectors of the full weighted Hamiltonian, summing Schur squares over equal-energy groups. An exact Gaussian elimination then checks every predicted rank. It tests 17 profiles, spanning 9,193 exact matrix entries, including sizes up to 48 vertices, degenerate energies, both resonance types in \(P_3\square K_{1,3}\), isotropic \(P_3\) powers, and products of larger stars. In the tester the physical weights are \(\omega_j=w_j/\sqrt{r_j}\) with listed positive integer effective frequencies \(w_j\). The identity for arbitrary positive reals is proved analytically, not inferred from these finite checks.

## Relationship to prior work

Godsil's *Average Mixing of Continuous Quantum Walks* (arXiv:1103.2578v3, 2011) gives the projector formula and an explicit average-mixing matrix for a single path. Coutinho, Godsil, Guo and Zhan's *A New Perspective on the Average Mixing Matrix* (arXiv:1709.03591v1, 2017) gives commutant and rank bounds but no exact weighted star-product rank formula. Coutinho and collaborators' *Irrational quantum walks* (arXiv:2208.08971v1, 2022) gives general exact spectral algorithms. Baptista, Coutinho and Marques' *Unexpected Averages of Mixing Matrices* (arXiv:2308.16378v1, 2023), Section 5, observes synchronous Cartesian tensorization but proves a trace/covariance identity rather than this all-coupling rank and kernel classification. Kimmerling's *Continuous-Time Quantum Walks on Windmill Graphs* (Washington State University dissertation, 2023, DOI 10.7273/000006322) establishes full rank for a single star with at least three leaves; the star-box classification goes beyond a one-factor case. Related earlier rank formulas for anisotropic hypercubes and complete-graph products concern different factor spectra. A general-purpose projector algorithm does not by itself specify the present positivity boundary or signed-frequency strata.

## Limitations

The claim concerns finite Cartesian products of \(K_{1,r}\) with \(r\ge2\), strictly positive factor weights, and the adjacency-Hamiltonian shared-time Cesàro average. It does not address the two-vertex star \(K_{1,1}\), zero couplings, magnetic phases, interacting factors, finite-time averages, or independent random clock samples. The originality comparison cannot rule out every unlocated general tensor-mixing theorem; the star-dissertation full text could not be fully inspected. The exact checker tests finite commensurate-frequency instances only. No independent audit or external validation has been performed.
