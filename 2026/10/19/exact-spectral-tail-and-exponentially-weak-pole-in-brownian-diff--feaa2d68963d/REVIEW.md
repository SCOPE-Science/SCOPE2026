# Scientific assessment

## Correctness (PASS)

The exact claim concerns the unique stationary one-point density for Brownian motion with independent Poisson refreshes of a random resetting intensity. The Feynman--Kac no-reset function is the spectral survival of the self-adjoint operator \(T=\lambda+\mu(I-P)\) on the intensity space. The rank-one resolvent identity agrees algebraically with the published stationary Fourier transform. Monotonicity of the exponential-integral Stieltjes function gives exactly one eigenvalue below the essential spectral edge. Its eigenprojection is positive, nonunit and explicit; Stieltjes inversion yields a positive continuous remainder with spectral support strictly above that atom. Consequently the isolated term, not the low-wave-number moment proxy, determines the strict spatial tail. A differentiated secular equation gives the exact derivative identity. The small- and large-refresh estimates use standard, uniform-at-the-stated-limits expansions of the exponential integral. The standalone program passed 125 independent numerical assertions, including a numerical reconstruction of the spectral mass and resolvent. No numerical finite check is used as an infinite-limit proof.

## Originality (PASS, with residual risk)

The original exponential-rate-refresh model and its exact Fourier transform appear in Arutkin--Reuveni (2025), equations (23) and (24); they are not presented as new. The added results are an explicit isolated spectral rate and *its exact weight*, a positive remainder, the exact response identity, and an essential-singularity limit showing why the ultimate tail and the variance-matched exponential scale differ. The source discusses the tail qualitatively and gives a low-wave-number effective-rate interpolation but does not state these isolated spectral-mode results in its inspected main text. Rose et al. (2018) handle resetting at a constant, state-independent rate; that shift theorem does not itself evaluate the continuously modulated resetting-intensity killing operator. Search results and accessible historical claims did not reveal equivalent specific conclusions. The source's exact transform does, in principle, encode all stationary observables, and a supplemental derivation of that transform was not separately inspected. This is the principal remaining originality risk, alongside unlocated general rank-one perturbation results specialized to the same model.

## Value (PASS)

Determining a far-field decay constant from a low-wave-number expansion can be misleading in a rate-disordered stationary mixture. Here the exact spatial tail coefficient is exponentially small as intensity refresh becomes rare, yet its decay exponent is parametrically smaller than the rate inferred from the stationary variance. This is a sharp structural distinction between ultimate tails and accessible intermediate scales. The spectral response identity connects that rare-mode mass to variation of the decay rate under increased refresh, and the complete positive spectral mixture explains the mechanism. These facts answer the source-motivated question of how the strict exponential regime emerges from an algebraic slow-refresh limit; they are not simply a parameter substitution or a recomputation of a known table.
