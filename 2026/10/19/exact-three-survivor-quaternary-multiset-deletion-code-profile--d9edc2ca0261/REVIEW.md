# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. A common three-symbol deletion descendant exists exactly when the coordinatewise-minimum sum is at least three. Heavy coordinates can label at most four codewords injectively. For light vectors the identity \(I(x,y)=2n-\sum_i\max\{x_i,y_i\}\) gives uniqueness of a light codeword for \(n\ge6\), and absence of light vectors for \(n\ge9\). For \(n=5\), any compatible light pair has complementary two-coordinate sets, which excludes a third. Explicit constructions attain all upper bounds. The exhaustive independent finite checker also verifies lengths five through nine.

## Originality
PASS within the examined evidence. Kreindel–Essayag–Zabokritskiy, arXiv:2601.05636, Section 3.5, gives general three-survivor bounds rather than these exact quaternary values. Kovačević–Tan, arXiv:1612.08837, provides the general metric framework but not this profile in inspected portions. Existing separate four-symbol length-four and three-symbol three-survivor results do not imply the displayed lengths five through eight. An equivalent result in uncatalogued literature remains a risk; search absence alone is not proof of novelty.

## Value
PASS. This is the complete exact infinite-length profile for the first four-symbol three-survivor case beyond the established ternary case, within a channel model motivated by unordered molecular and permutation storage. The profile identifies two distinct pre-stabilization plateaux and the exact sharp onset of pure-symbol optimality. Its constructive extremizers and the light-vector obstruction explain the finite-to-stable transition, rather than merely adding isolated table entries.

## Scope and remaining risk
The theorem is restricted to four symbols, with lengths \(n\ge5\), and to unordered multiset deletions retaining three symbols. Neither classification of all maximizing codes nor an independent external validation is asserted. Search coverage cannot rule out an unindexed equivalent theorem.
