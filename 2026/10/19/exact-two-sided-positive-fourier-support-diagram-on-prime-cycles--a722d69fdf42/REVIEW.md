# Scientific review: two-sided positive Fourier supports

## Correctness — PASS

The support-symmetry argument forces odd cardinalities because positivity on both sides puts zero in both supports and forces both sequences to be even. Tao's theorem gives the remaining necessary inequality. For the converse the inequalities \(s\le r\le m-s\) force all selected root cosines to be strictly negative; every factor has strictly positive Laurent coefficients, whereas its sampled cosine values have exactly the stipulated opposite-pair zeros. Fourier involution treats the swapped case. There is no missing endpoint when \(r=0\) or \(s=0\). All 502 admissible pairs for ten test primes passed independent high-precision evaluation. These checks supplement rather than replace the general proof.

## Originality — PASS (subject to residual literature risk)

Tao's full-text theorem covers unconstrained complex functions, not positivity on both sides. Kolountzakis–Révész develop positive-definite Turán bounds rather than all attainable spatial/Fourier support-cardinality pairs. Wang–Ye study spectral support in bounds for function values, not exact cardinality realizability. The closest known positivity-only cardinality statement imposes no Fourier nonnegativity and therefore does not imply the extra existence assertion. Searches also compared finite-group support-incidence and sparse nonnegative-trigonometric settings. No inspected theorem entails the present simultaneous positivity support diagram. A historically unlocated equivalent deduction from positive-coefficient cosine polynomial theory remains the principal risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The intersection of the nonnegative and positive-definite cones is a standard admissible class in Turán/Delsarte harmonic optimization, but knowing only the unconstrained uncertainty inequality does not decide which exact sparsities admit a doubly nonnegative kernel. This theorem supplies a complete all-prime feasibility diagram, with constructive exact witnesses and proof that no further forbidden cardinalities appear. It does not merely compute a table or restate an unconstrained existence theorem.

## Boundaries

Only odd prime cyclic groups and support sizes are classified; supports at predetermined locations, coefficient optimization, and composite-order analogues are outside scope. Finite tests do not establish the universal quantifier. Literature checks cannot exclude every unpublished or unindexed equivalent argument.
