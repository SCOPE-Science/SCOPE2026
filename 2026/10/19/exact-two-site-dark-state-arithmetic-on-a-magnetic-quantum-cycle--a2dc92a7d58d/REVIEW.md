# Scientific review: arithmetic dark states on a magnetic quantum cycle

**Correctness — PASS.** The Fourier spectrum of the finite Peierls cycle can have nontrivial multiplicity only at total flux congruent to zero or half a flux quantum. In each two-dimensional degenerate eigenspace, simultaneous vanishing at two detector sites is equivalent to one exact integer divisibility condition, which gives the stated greatest-common-divisor counts after accounting for singleton eigenvalues. The absorptive Hamiltonian's imaginary Rayleigh quotient identifies every nondecaying state; an orthogonal reducing-space decomposition proves the survival plateau for arbitrary positive loss strength. Independent finite-matrix and time-evolution checks passed 5,473 assertions. Numerical experiments do not establish the infinite-family theorem; the argument in RESULT.md does.

**Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk.** The earlier trapped-ring analysis of Agliari, Mülken, and Blumen explicitly provides an even-cycle zero-flux perturbative mode-width formula. It implies part of the zero-flux arithmetic condition, and that component is not independently new. Thiel and collaborators' later quantum-detection works establish general dark/bright decompositions and magnetic cycle examples with one detection site. The compared full texts do not supply the two-absorber half-flux count and its parity-dependent gain/loss trichotomy for arbitrary separation and size. The result is best understood as an exact evaluated classification added to the established spectral framework, not as an invention of dark-state theory. A previously unnoticed equivalent specialization or stronger two-detector theorem remains possible; a public mathematical-record search also surfaced a two-sensor cycle Fourier-frame theorem whose full proof could not be checked and is an explicit residual comparison risk.

**Scientific value — PASS.** The all-integer phase diagram is a natural complete classification of persistent probability in two-site quantum trapping. It predicts an exact, observable distinction between flux-induced protection and flux-induced elimination of dark states: for example, a six-site cycle with absorbers separated by two edges acquires one dark mode at half flux, while absorbers separated by three edges lose two previously protected modes. The plateau is exact for every positive absorption strength, without a perturbative approximation. This classification is more informative than the existence of dark states alone, and its parity/gcd boundaries identify which trap geometries admit protected excitations.

**Limitations and literature.** Finite uniform-hopping magnetic cycles with two equal positive absorbing rates only; no robustness to generic hopping disorder, no rate-of-convergence estimate, and no assertion for arbitrary projective measurement schedules. The 2009 paper's zero-flux special cases and general non-Hermitian trap formalism are significant pre-existing coverage; 2019 papers supply general detection and magnetic-ring context. Independent priority assessment is needed to decide whether another exact specialization already exists.
