# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The all-vector event is the indefiniteness of the symmetric Jordan product of a Wishart Gram matrix and a two-eigenvalue SPD operator. Its determinant is negative exactly when the squared Gaussian sample correlation exceeds a sharp spectral-contrast threshold. The beta correlation distribution yields the uniform law; independent conditional-Gaussian/Student-tail integration and an attained Kantorovich angle yield the fixed-direction maximum. The exact parameter-shift identity and joint large-condition-number asymptotics follow algebraically. All quantifiers and boundaries are stated, and an executable reproduction program verifies finite instances without being substituted for proof.

**Originality — PASS, with bibliographic limitations.** The known fixed-pair Gaussian sign-flip formula appears in Kabán and Durrant (2020, Lemma 2.2), and standard correlation beta laws are established. The assessed addition is the exact simultaneous/adaptive sign-loss law for \((Sv)^\mathsf{T}SAv\), the identity connecting it with the optimally oriented precommitted direction after one fewer sketch row, and a sharp condition-number versus sketch-size scaling law. Relevant published numerical linear algebra studies concern subspace-embedding bounds, Krylov error and Ritz outliers rather than this simultaneous Gaussian event; queried research records did not show a dominating formulation. The 2026 motivating preprint was readable only in its abstract/secondary summary, leaving unexamined-appendix risk.

**Value — PASS.** Positivity of sketched bilinear forms is a natural sufficient diagnostic for keeping one-dimensional sketched Galerkin and harmonic projection scalars positive when the original operator is SPD. The exact formula quantifies a structural difference between a starting vector chosen before sketching and the ability to choose one after sketching. The \(k/\kappa\) threshold identifies the sketch size scale required to protect *every* possible starting direction, distinct from mere rank preservation of a two-dimensional subspace. The result is deliberately limited to the specified two-dimensional Gaussian model; neither empirical correlations nor correctness alone establish its value.
