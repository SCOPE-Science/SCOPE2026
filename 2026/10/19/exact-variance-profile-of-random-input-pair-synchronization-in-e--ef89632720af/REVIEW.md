# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is derived from first-step moment identities on the nonabsorbing unordered pair states, with the absorbed state assigned both moments zero. The fixed reset word gives a geometric-block tail, guaranteeing uniqueness of finite mean and variance solutions. Nonboundary rotations imply the exact mean and variance decrements of two. The single boundary transition then gives a two-term reflected recurrence; direct polynomial substitution proves the all-parameter quartic. The extreme pair follows by maximizing a concave quadratic mean. Exact rational state-matrix checks independently corroborate all cases with three through eight states. Risk: the degree-bound algebra in the identity check and full-state boundary matching must be read together with the proof; finite tests by themselves cannot prove an infinite result.

## Originality — PASS against inspected sources, with residual risk

Gusev, arXiv:1404.6731 (2014), Section 3 proves expectation results, including a worst-pair mean formula; this article does not state or solve the second-moment boundary system. The 2013 online study by Kisielewicz–Kowalski–Szykuła concerns shortest reset lengths of random automata rather than random input on a fixed automaton. Published mathematical-record searches for pair-coalescence variance, second moments, Bernoulli letters, and the proposed limiting ratio found no matching profile. The mean identity is already implicit in Gusev and is not claimed as original. Risk: a later unindexed variance computation, alternative convention for the same automaton, or a broader explicit hitting-time distribution could subsume the result; no search result alone proves uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The invariant is the uncertainty in the number of random letters required to repair disagreement between two copies of a canonical slowly synchronizing automaton. The all-pair quartic variance and the nonzero limiting coefficient of variation provide fluctuation information not supplied by an expected-time bound or by deterministic reset thresholds. The result is an infinite exact formula over all state counts and pairs, not an arbitrary finite numerical table. The complete variance profile can inform quantile and tail estimates, though those estimates are not themselves claimed.
