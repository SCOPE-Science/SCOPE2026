# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The complete length-\(3^k\) factor language follows from block decomposition of the nonprimitive Cantor substitution and the fact that adjacent letter pairs are precisely `00`, `01`, and `10`; all pairs occur. Its nonzero factors are zero-padded prefix cuts of the palindromic finite Cantor block and their reversals. Equality of binary scattered-subsequence statistics through length two is exactly equality of weight and first positional moment. All moment values for each fixed weight consequently form two explicitly stated progressions; the integer-interval overlap expression counts them without omissions or duplication. The binary-carry formula supplies exact consecutive Cantor-position gaps. Endpoint cases, including \(k=0\), are treated separately. An executable two-method finite check returned `VERIFY_OK` for the specified levels. It supplements rather than establishes the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (searched evidence, with residual risk)

Rigo–Salimov (2013; expanded 2015) introduces the relevant scattered-subsequence equivalence and exact results for Sturmian and Parikh-constant morphic words. The Cantor morphism is not Parikh-constant, and their inspected theorems do not entail its triadic profile. Chen–Lü–Wu (2017) investigates *contiguous* \(k\)-abelian complexity of the same Cantor word; that equivalence is not scattered \(2\)-binomial equivalence, and their class-count result does not imply the present weighted-position profile. Targeted database, published-record, synonym, and parameter searches did not locate the exact classification. The absence of a hit alone is not proof of originality; previously unindexed equivalent constructions remain possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result provides a complete nontrivial and exact class spectrum for infinitely many canonical substitution-scale lengths of a classical automatic word, with one explicit computation per binary-digit weight rather than enumeration of the full triadic-length factors. Its arithmetic-progressions-plus-overlap structure exposes the role of Cantor gaps and the positional statistic distinguishing scattered-pair equivalence from abelian or contiguous-pair equivalence. The precise numbers, including the initially sparse overlaps, are useful benchmarks for further formulas and for automated computation. The finding does not assert an unproved simpler closed expression, any arbitrary-length behavior, or independent validation.
