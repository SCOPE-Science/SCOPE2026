# Review: exact weight-two binary one-deletion code capacity

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof separates deletions of ones from deletions of zeros. Distinct weight-one outputs force \(t+2e\le n-1\); neighboring adjacent-one codewords share a weight-two output and force \(t\le\lceil(n-1)/2\rceil\). These are necessary for every code and yield the stated upper bound. The explicit family saturates it; all its weight-one and weight-two outputs are proved disjoint with boundary cases treated explicitly. The packaged checker exhaustively solves all instances through length nine and independently checks the proposed construction through length eighty. The proof, not enumeration, handles all \(n\ge2\).

**Originality — PASS against inspected primary sources and indexed scientific records.** The closest direct study, Cullina–Kulkarni–Kiyavash (arXiv:1211.4056v1, Theorem 5), determines \(\chi(L_{1,n,2})\), whereas this finding determines \(\alpha(L_{1,n,2})\); a chromatic number cannot in general determine the independence number. Kulkarni–Kiyavash (arXiv:1211.3128v1, Section V) formulate constrained-code maxima as hypergraph matchings, but do not evaluate this layer exactly or give this construction. Chee–Ge–Ling (DOI:10.1137/090751311) concern perfect-code size spectra at short lengths. Related indexed finite unrestricted and alternative-channel results have different hypotheses and do not imply the all-length exact weight-two result. Residual risk: a less easily indexed earlier solution to precisely the same sparse-layer problem could exist; full universal literature coverage is not claimed.

**Value — PASS.** Weight-two words form the first binary fixed-weight class in which the restricted one-deletion code size grows with blocklength; the weight-one class has capacity one. The exact \(3/4\) leading coefficient, periodic rounding, and explicit family resolve a naturally motivated channel-capacity problem at the smallest nontrivial sparse weight. The proof exposes the structural competition between singleton-output occupancy and forbidden neighboring adjacent-one words. This exact extremum is not merely a reproduction of existing coloring or unrestricted-code formulas.

**Scientific limitations.** The claim covers weight two and exactly one deletion, not arbitrary source sets or multiple deletions. Computational verification is finite; it is a check, not a mathematical proof of all lengths. Indexed searches and inspected papers constrain, but cannot mathematically guarantee, global priority.
