# Exact word-growth and entropy loss for the three-generator dyadic overlap semigroup

## Finding

Consider the three similarities of the unit interval

\[
 g_0(x)=x/2,\qquad g_1(x)=(x+1)/2,\qquad g_{01}(x)=(x+1)/4=g_0(g_1(x)).
\]

Their semigroup is generated by the binary tokens \(0\), \(1\), and \(01\), whose concatenation determines a similarity exactly. Let \(A_n\) count the distinct maps represented by exactly \(n\) generators, including the identity for \(n=0\). Then, as a formal power series,

\[
\boxed{\sum_{n\ge0}A_n z^n=\frac{(1-z)^2}{(1-2z)(1-3z+z^2)}.}
\]

In particular, \(A_0,A_1,A_2,A_3,A_4,A_5=(1,3,9,26,73,201)\), and the exponential generator-length growth is

\[
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log A_n}{n}=\log\!\left(\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}\right).}
\]

Give the three generators probabilities \(a,b,c>0\), respectively, with \(a+b+c=1\), and let \(G_n\) denote the product of \(n\) independent choices. Put \(C=ab+c\) and \(H_2(t)=-t\log t-(1-t)\log(1-t)\). The exact random-walk (Garsia) entropy rate is

\[
\boxed{h(a,b,c):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{H(G_n)}n
= -a\log a-b\log b-c\log c-C H_2(c/C).}
\]

The unique stationary probability measure \(\nu_{a,b,c}\) on \([0,1]\) has full support, but is singular for **every** positive probability triple. More precisely, it is exact dimensional and

\[
\boxed{\dim_H\nu_{a,b,c}=\frac{h(a,b,c)}{(1+c)\log2}<1.}
\]

For equal weights \(a=b=c=1/3\), the dimension simplifies to the explicit value

\[
\boxed{\dim_H\nu_{1/3,1/3,1/3}=\log_2 3-\frac23.}
\]

## Assumptions and scope

All logarithms are natural unless the subscript indicates base two. Entropy \(H\) is Shannon entropy of a finite or countable discrete distribution. \(\dim_H\nu\) denotes the almost-sure local/Hausdorff dimension of an exact-dimensional probability measure. All probabilities are strictly positive, and \(n\) is a nonnegative integer. Distinct similarities are distinguished by their affine functions, not by syntactic generator words. The stationary measure is the unique solution of

\[
\nu=a(g_0)_*\nu+b(g_1)_*\nu+c(g_{01})_*\nu.
\]

The binary token \(01\) is two digits but one semigroup generator. This convention is essential: all counts and entropy rates use generator time \(n\), not binary digit length.

## Proof

**1. Distinct maps and parsing multiplicity.** The composition corresponding to a concatenated binary word \(w=w_1\cdots w_L\) is

\[
 g_w(x)=2^{-L}\left(x+\sum_{i=1}^L w_i 2^{L-i}\right).
\]

The slope determines \(L\); among words of fixed length the translation determines all digits. Hence different binary words induce different affine similarities, and identical words induce identical maps.

For a binary word \(w\), write \(M(w)\) for its number of contiguous occurrences of \(01\). These occurrences cannot overlap, because the second digit of one is \(1\), whereas the first digit of another is \(0\). Any parsing of \(w\) into the tokens \(0,1,01\) consists of choosing a subset of those \(M(w)\) occurrences to be two-digit tokens. If \(|w|=L\) and \(j\) occurrences are chosen, the parsing has exactly \(n=L-j\) generators. Thus a word occurs at generator time \(n\) precisely when \(L-M(w)\le n\le L\), and then it has exactly \(\binom{M(w)}{L-n}\) parsings.

**2. Exact word-growth series.** Let

\[
 B(u,v)=\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^*}u^{|w|}v^{M(w)}.
\]

A two-state transfer calculation recording whether the last digit is \(0\) gives

\[
 B(u,v)=\frac1{1-2u+u^2(1-v)}.
\]

Consequently, using the interval of allowable generator counts established above, one obtains as a formal series

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{n\ge0} A_n z^n
 &=\sum_{w}\frac{z^{|w|-M(w)}-z^{|w|+1}}{1-z}\\
 &=\frac{B(z,z^{-1})-zB(z,1)}{1-z}\\
 &=\frac{(1-z)^2}{(1-2z)(1-3z+z^2)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The smallest-modulus positive pole is \(z=(3-\sqrt5)/2\). It is simple and has nonzero residue, giving the growth limit stated in the finding.

**3. Exact finite-time entropy and its limit.** Let \(J_n\) count the generators of type \(01\). Let \(W_n\) denote their concatenated binary word. The number of \(01\) occurrences in \(W_n\) is exactly

\[
 M_n=J_n+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathbf1_{\{X_i=0,X_{i+1}=1\}}.
\]

Indeed, the only occurrences crossing token boundaries are transitions from token \(0\) to token \(1\). For a fixed word \(w\) at generator time \(n\), all its \(\binom{M(w)}{|w|-n}\) parsings have identical probability: the counts of generators of each type depend only on the binary digit counts and on \(|w|-n\). The conditional distribution of the generator string given \(W_n=w\) is therefore uniform. Shannon's chain rule yields the **exact** identity

\[
 H(W_n)=nH(a,b,c)-\mathbb E\!\left[\log\binom{M_n}{J_n}\right],
\]

where \(H(a,b,c)=-a\log a-b\log b-c\log c\), and \(H(W_n)=H(G_n)\) because the binary word determines the affine map. The strong law for independent token choices, or its bounded one-dependent extension, gives

\[
 \frac{J_n}{n}\to c,\qquad\frac{M_n}{n}\to ab+c=C
\]

almost surely. Uniform Stirling bounds give, for all admissible integer \(J_n,M_n\),

\[
\log\binom{M_n}{J_n}=M_n H_2(J_n/M_n)+O(\log(n+1)),
\]

with the boundary convention \(0H_2(0/0)=0\). The normalized logarithm is bounded, so bounded convergence proves the asserted entropy rate.

**4. Exact dimension and strict singularity.** The three similarity contraction ratios are \(1/2,1/2,1/4\), so their average logarithmic contraction is \(\chi=-(1+c)\log2\). The entropy-rate dimension theorem of Kittle and Kogler [1, Theorem 1.1] applies to *every* finitely supported law on contracting similarities of the real line and gives

\[
\dim_H\nu=\min\{1,h(a,b,c)/((1+c)\log2)\}.
\]

To establish that the minimum never truncates for positive \(a,b,c\), consider the successive gaps between the digit \(1\)'s in the infinite binary concatenation. Every \(1\) terminates a generator, so the numbers \(K\ge0\) of intervening zeros form an independent, identically distributed renewal sequence with

\[
 \mathbb P(K=0)=b,\qquad \mathbb P(K=k)=C a^{k-1}\quad(k\ge1).
\]

Its mean block length and entropy satisfy, by elementary geometric sums,

\[
 \mathbb E(K+1)=\frac{1+c}{1-a},\qquad
 (1-a)H(K)=h(a,b,c).
\]

Compare this gap distribution \(p_k\) with the gap law \(q_k=2^{-(k+1)}\) of independent fair bits. The Kullback--Leibler divergence satisfies

\[
 D(p\Vert q)=\sum_{k\ge0}p_k\log(p_k/q_k)
 =\mathbb E(K+1)\log 2-H(K)\ge0.
\]

Equality requires \(p_k=q_k\) for all \(k\). Since \(p_{k+1}/p_k=a\) for every \(k\ge1\), equality would force \(a=1/2\) and \(b=p_0=1/2\), hence \(c=0\), excluded. Thus \(H(K)<\mathbb E(K+1)\log2\), equivalently \(h(a,b,c)<(1+c)\log2\). The dimension is therefore strictly below one. Since \(g_0([0,1])\cup g_1([0,1])=[0,1]\), the attractor and support are nevertheless the whole interval. A probability measure of dimension below one is singular with respect to Lebesgue measure.

Finally, for \(a=b=c=1/3\), direct substitution gives \(h=(4/3)\log3-(8/9)\log2\). Dividing by \((4/3)\log2\) gives \(\dim_H\nu=\log_2 3-2/3\), as claimed.

## Relationship to prior work

Kittle and Kogler [1] prove the general one-dimensional entropy-rate dimension identity. That identity, including its applicability to this overlapping three-map system, is **prior work**. Varjú [2] explains random-walk entropy and exact overlaps for similarity semigroups, and supplies the framework for comparing distinct products of generators. Böcherer, Mathar, da Rocha Jr. and Pimentel [3, Section V] explicitly study **the very same token set** \(\{0,1,01\}\) and identify the ambiguity caused by treating generator strings as uniquely decipherable. They do not compute the actual entropy of the merged product distribution; instead they exclude the ambiguous token from their admissible input process. In their special assignment \(a=b=\sqrt2-1\), \(c=(\sqrt2-1)^2\), the naive source entropy per output digit is about \(0.881374\), exceeding \(\log2\), whereas the exact collision-adjusted value from the present formula is approximately \(0.678355\), below \(\log2\). The all-weight entropy correction, generator-length growth series and self-similar dimensions are not supplied in that source. Neither [1] nor [2] states the exact rational generator-length growth series or computes the all-weight random-walk entropy loss and strict full-support singularity for this three-token system. These computations quantify a known coding ambiguity and provide an explicit natural instance of the general theory, not an improvement of its general dimension theorem. Familiar transfer-matrix and renewal arguments supply the techniques.

## Limitations

The result concerns only the specifically defined three-map semigroup and strictly positive weights, with generator time rather than binary digit time. It does not determine analogous invariants for arbitrary longer or overlapping codeword sets. The dimension step invokes the existing one-dimensional dimension theorem [1]; in fact the older algebraic weak-separation theorem summarized in [2] also covers the general dimension step for this rational system. Possible earlier equivalent examples in the variable-length code, hidden-renewal, or finite-type self-similar-measure literature remain a priority risk; a failed keyword search cannot rule those out. No independent validation is claimed.
