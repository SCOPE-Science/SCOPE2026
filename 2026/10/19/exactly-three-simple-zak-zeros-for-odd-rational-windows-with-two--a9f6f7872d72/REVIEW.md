# Review of the three-zero theorem for rational Zak transforms

**Correctness (C): PASS.** A direct Cauchy-kernel Fourier calculation gives the exact Zak multiplier as a difference of two hyperbolic quotients. The proof splits the open frequency strip into non-real spatial phases and the two real spatial phases, and handles the frequency boundary separately. Strict one-variable monotonicity excludes additional zeros in every region; differentiation gives the coincident-pole limit. At each of the three remaining points the two directional derivatives have nonzero linearly independent real and imaginary parts. The standalone numerical checker independently compares the closed transform with the defining series but is not used to prove the universal assertion.

**Originality (O): PASS, subject to residual historical-priority risk.** The accessible full text of Ghosh (arXiv:2608.10568) treats the fixed-pole window with parameters \((1,2)\), Zak polynomials and frame-set conditions; its revised abstract expands to discrete integer-pole windows. Neither states this continuous-parameter exact zero classification, especially the repeated-pole limit and nondegeneracy. Faulhuber's 2025 three-parity-zero construction applies to the first Hermite function, not a rational pole pair. Kloos (arXiv:1411.1539) gives a different one-zero theorem for totally positive, nonnegative windows. The bounded comparisons of recoverable mathematical claims and published results revealed no matching exact three-zero rational-window classification; neither a negative search nor the bounded comparison certifies universal priority. A related 2026 parity-obstruction paper was only available in limited form and is a residual comparison risk.

**Value (V): PASS.** The actual mathematical gap is not the existence of three symmetry zeros, which parity already gives, but the possible appearance of additional nontrivial zeros for an odd Gabor window. Those zeros govern cyclic Zak fibers and can produce further periodic Gabor obstructions. The exact zero-set classification throughout a natural continuous two-pole family, including its singular coalescence boundary, answers that structural issue and establishes nondegeneracy rather than merely repeating a parity observation. No broad new frame-set theorem is claimed.

**Scope and limits.** The theorem concerns the unit Zak transform only, not all lattice dilations. No human or independent audit was performed, and a complete historical-priority guarantee is unavailable.
