# Scientific review: exponential edge-tail dependence under common Poisson resetting

## Correctness — PASS
The assumptions specify independent one-dimensional Brownian motions, a single independent Poisson clock resetting all particles, a stationary backward age, finite positive diffusivity and resetting rate, and empirically standardized extrema. The proof conditions on backward age and on no intervening reset; the latter event has probability tending to one at the stated logarithmically shrinking lag. Gaussian sample-variance concentration makes empirical-width errors negligible at the Gumbel scaling, and the exact joint Gaussian exceedance calculation gives the correct Hüsler–Reiss parameter \(\lambda_a^2=s/(2a)\). Bounded convergence over the exponential age yields the joint CDF; strict Cauchy–Schwarz proves non-max-stability, and a differentiated Gaussian integral gives \(\chi(s)=e^{-\sqrt{s}}\). The numerical checker passes 82 cross-checks; its finite tests are supporting evidence, not the proof of infinite limits.

## Originality — PASS, with priority qualification
The stationary shared-age Gaussian mixture and single-time extreme statistics are in Biroli–Larralde–Majumdar–Schehr, arXiv:2211.00563v1; the fixed-age two-time Gaussian/Hüsler–Reiss extreme-value limit is substantially covered by Le Doussal, arXiv:2308.16709v1. Their conjunction alone is not represented as a new general extremal theorem. The assessed claim is the common-reset model's empirically self-normalized **stationary two-time age-mixture distribution**, its provable failure of max-stability, and the explicitly evaluated exponential-square-root ordered-limit upper-tail dependence law. The closest results, relevant aliases, and possible stronger implications were compared in the accompanying audit. Because those two substantial mathematical ingredients were published, an unlocated evaluated specialization remains a genuine priority risk; the present review is not an independent priority certification.

## Scientific value — PASS
In common-reset gases, the elapsed time since the last reset is an unobserved shared environmental parameter. Dividing the extreme position by the empirical spatial width removes that environment from every one-time extreme distribution but does not remove its effect on critical two-time dependence. The exact mixed bivariate law and parameter-free \(e^{-\sqrt{s}}\) dependence decay identify a nontrivial spatial-transport memory diagnostic at the natural extreme-particle time scale. The calculated distinction between conditional max-stability and unconditional non-max-stability can matter when inferring dynamical dependence from extreme-position measurements; it is not a mere restatement of ordinary Gaussian covariance.

## Scope and remaining limits
The tail coefficient uses the limit of large populations before the limit of extreme thresholds. There is no claim about reverse limit order, arbitrary reset protocols, independently reset particles, interacting gases, or empirical finite-sample estimation errors. The proof assumes the complete stationary shared-clock system and does not address physical-time lags fixed as the population grows. External independent audit has not been performed.
