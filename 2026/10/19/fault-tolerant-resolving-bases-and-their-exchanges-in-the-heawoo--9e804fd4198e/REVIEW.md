# Review of fault-tolerant Heawood landmark exchanges

## Correctness

PASS. The 14-vertex graph and all 91 vertex-pair conditions are explicitly defined. The point–line incidence inequalities are proved from distances. All subsets through seven landmarks are exhaustively evaluated, so the minimum value and the exact 282-state five-choice classification are finite theorems rather than statistical evidence. Two differently constructed programs independently generate the 784 exchanges, connected-component decomposition, radius, diameter, and every eccentricity; an explicit full graph certificate is checked. The scope is the unique specified graph only.

## Originality

PASS with publication-overlap risk. Héger–Takáts explicitly give the Fano ordinary metric dimension five; Bailey's distance-regular graph table gives the same ordinary value. Estrada-Moreno et al. introduce the two-fold invariant and broad inequalities. Bailey's later symmetric-design paper studies ordinary one-witness resolving conditions. None of the inspected material supplies the exact two-fold seven-basis family, five-choice description, two isolated optimal exchange states, or 280-state metric classification. Database and primary-literature searches did not identify a covering result; this is not a claim that searching proves uniqueness. An unpublished or unindexed equivalent could exist.

## Value

PASS. The Fano incidence graph is the smallest cubic girth-six graph and a natural sensor-placement model; one-landmark failure tolerance raises the optimum from five to seven. Knowing which seven-landmark installations are reachable without losing that guarantee is materially finer than a minimum-cardinality computation. The two frozen unilateral installations and the single mobile component, with exact center/periphery and a compact geometric parametrization, give interpretable constraints for optimal redeployment.
