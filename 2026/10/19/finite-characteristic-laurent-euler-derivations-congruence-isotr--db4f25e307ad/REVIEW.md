# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof first identifies the Laurent unit group, which forces all automorphisms to be scalar-monomial. The pointwise commutation condition is exactly a stabilizer condition for a full-rank coefficient matrix over the prime field. The reduction of the integral general linear group is the determinant-\(\pm1\) subgroup, not the full finite general linear group; its two frame-orbit cases give the precise indices. The additive weight map has kernel of index \(p^r\); the monomial basis gives both the constant ring and module rank. For finite fields, rank strata are Grassmannians, but full-rank strata split by the determinant quotient. Every statement, including \(n=1\) and \(p=2\), is covered. Finite enumeration over nine parameter sets confirms the formulas but is not used in place of the proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional, not an independent determination)
The principal October 2026 source assumes characteristic zero. Its general scalar-monomial automorphisms and weight equation are prior knowledge. The modular full-index invariant lattice, finite-index exponent isotropy, determinant-controlled frame orbits, and exact finite-field Euler-conjugacy count were compared against the available characteristic-zero Laurent-isotropy results, a related characteristic-zero Witt-automorphism paper, positive-characteristic Ore-isotropy results, and indexed results on other finite-field algebra automorphisms. No examined statement implies the full characteristic-\(p\) theorem. The main residual risk is an unindexed equivalent classification under algebraic-torus or modular-vector-field terminology. Literature searching cannot establish absolute novelty.

## Value — PASS
The theorem completely answers a natural isotropy/conjugacy question for a substantial uniform family of modular Laurent derivations: the weight lattice changes from torsion-free to finite torsion, the constant ring has the full original Laurent transcendence degree, and the exact arithmetic orbit count exhibits a previously non-obvious determinant-orientation splitting. It is more than a restatement of characteristic-zero rank formulas or a one-off finite enumeration.

## Limits and related literature
Porto–Veloso arXiv:2610.12777v1: genuine methodological predecessor, but characteristic-zero hypotheses; Bavula (2016): established automorphism framework, not a modular classification; Zhao (2017): only abstract inspected. The result is restricted to Euler derivations, and another treatment in modular torus geometry might subsume it.
