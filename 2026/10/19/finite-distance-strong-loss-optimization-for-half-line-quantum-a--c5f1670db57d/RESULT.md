# Finite-distance strong-loss optimization for half-line quantum absorption

## Finding

Consider a continuous-time nearest-neighbor quantum walk on the positive half-line with a Lindblad sink at the first site. Write \(s\in\{1,2,\ldots\}\) for the initially occupied site and \(\eta=\kappa/\Omega>0\) for the ratio of the sink rate to the hopping normalization. Let \(P_s(\eta)\) be the eventual absorption probability, as defined below.

**Theorem.** For every integer \(s\ge 2\), every global maximizer of \(P_s\) exists at a finite strength **strictly greater than** \(\eta=1\). For all sufficiently large integers \(s\), there is exactly one global maximizer \(\eta_s\), and

\[
\eta_s=1+\frac{3}{4s^3}+O(s^{-4}),\qquad
P_s(\eta_s)-P_s(1)=\frac{3}{16\pi s^6}+O(s^{-7}).
\]

More sharply, for every \(s\ge1\) and every \(\eta>1\), the reciprocal-coupling asymmetry is exactly

\[
P_s(\eta)-P_s(\eta^{-1})=
\frac{2\eta(\eta^2-1)}{\pi}
\int_0^1\frac{q^{2s-2}(1-q^2)^2}{(1+\eta^2q^2)(\eta^2+q^2)}\,dq>0.
\]

For each fixed \(\eta>1\), this positive discrepancy is strictly decreasing as \(s\) increases. These are exact infinite-time statements, not finite-time optimization claims.

## Assumptions and scope

The one-particle Hilbert space is \(\ell^2(\mathbb N)\), with orthonormal basis \(\{|j\rangle\}_{j\ge1}\). Fix \(\Omega>0\) and \(\kappa>0\), and define

\[
H_+=\Omega I-\frac{\Omega}{2}\sum_{j\ge1}\bigl(|j\rangle\langle j+1|+|j+1\rangle\langle j|\bigr).
\]

The absorbing environment consists of a vacuum state and the Lindblad jump \(L=\sqrt\kappa|\emptyset\rangle\langle1|\). The no-absorption propagator is generated by \(H_{\rm eff}=H_+-i\kappa|1\rangle\langle1|/2\). From initial pure state \(|s\rangle\), define

\[
P_s(\eta)=\kappa\int_0^\infty\bigl|\langle1|e^{-iH_{\rm eff}t}|s\rangle\bigr|^2\,dt,
\quad \eta=\kappa/\Omega.
\]

The established exact spectral representation from Riberi, arXiv:2605.08056v4, Eq. (15) and Appendix D.2, reads \(P_s=F+G_s\), where

\[
F(\eta)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi\frac{\eta\sin k}{1+\eta^2+2\eta\sin k}\,dk,
\qquad
G_s(\eta)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^1\frac{\eta q^{2s-2}(1-q^2)}{1+\eta^2q^2}\,dq.
\]

We use that published representation as a premise; the novel assertion is its exact global optimization and quantitative finite-distance displacement. No extension to random initial states, finite paths, multiple sinks, or other measurement protocols is asserted. All limits in \(s\) retain fixed \(\Omega\) and use infinite-time absorption first.

## Proof

**1. Reciprocity and strict finite-distance breaking.** Replacing \(\eta\) with \(\eta^{-1}\) in \(F\) leaves its value unchanged. Subtracting the two integrands in \(G_s\) gives

\[
\frac{\eta}{1+\eta^2q^2}-\frac{\eta}{\eta^2+q^2}
=\frac{\eta(\eta^2-1)(1-q^2)}{(1+\eta^2q^2)(\eta^2+q^2)}.
\]

Multiplication by \(2q^{2s-2}(1-q^2)/\pi\) and integration yields the displayed identity. For \(\eta>1\), the integrand is strictly positive on \((0,1)\); multiplication by an additional factor \(q^2<1\) proves strict decay with \(s\).

**2. Existence and strict strong-loss direction at every finite source distance.** Differentiation under the integrals gives

\[
F'(\eta)=\frac{2(1-\eta^2)}{\pi}\int_0^\pi\frac{\sin k}{(1+\eta^2+2\eta\sin k)^2}\,dk,
\]

\[
G_s'(\eta)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^1\frac{q^{2s-2}(1-q^2)(1-\eta^2q^2)}{(1+\eta^2q^2)^2}\,dq.
\]

For \(0<\eta\le1\), both derivatives are nonnegative and the second is strictly positive. Thus \(P_s\) is strictly increasing all the way through \(\eta=1\) with \(P_s'(1)>0\). For \(s\ge2\), dominated convergence shows \(P_s(\eta)\to0\) as \(\eta\downarrow0\). As \(\eta\to\infty\), \(F(\eta)\to0\), and

\[
G_s(\eta)\le\frac{2}{\pi\eta}\int_0^1q^{2s-4}(1-q^2)\,dq\to0.
\]

Consequently continuity ensures an interior global maximizer, and strict increase on \((0,1]\) excludes \(\eta\le1\). The case \(s=1\) is exceptional: \(P_1(\eta)\to1\) as \(\eta\to\infty\), so this global-existence statement is intentionally restricted to \(s\ge2\).

**3. Localization and eventual uniqueness of the global optimizer.** Since \(\eta/(1+\eta^2q^2)\le1/(2q)\) for \(q>0\),

\[
0\le G_s(\eta)\le \frac1\pi\int_0^1q^{2s-3}(1-q^2)\,dq
=\frac{1}{2\pi s(s-1)}\qquad(s\ge2),
\]

uniformly for all \(\eta>0\). Meanwhile \(F\) depends on \(\eta\) only through \(\eta+\eta^{-1}\), is strictly maximized at \(\eta=1\), and tends to zero at both ends. The uniform estimate shows that every global maximizer converges to \(1\) as \(s\to\infty\). The exact curvature is

\[
F''(1)=-\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi\frac{\sin k}{(1+\sin k)^2}\,dk=-\frac{2}{3\pi},
\]

because the substitution \(u=\tan(k/2)\) evaluates the integral as \(\int_0^\infty4u/(1+u)^4du=2/3\). Select a fixed neighborhood \(I\) of \(1\) on which \(F''<-c<0\). For \(\eta\in I\), twice differentiating the smooth rational integrand for \(G_s\) gives the uniform estimate \(\sup_{\eta\in I}|G_s''(\eta)|\le C_I\int_0^1q^{2s-2}(1-q^2)dq=O(s^{-2})\). Hence \(P_s''<-c/2<0\) on \(I\) for sufficiently large \(s\). Since all global maximizers already lie inside \(I\), strict concavity implies exactly one global maximizer for sufficiently large \(s\).

**4. Sharp cubic displacement and sixth-order probability gain.** At matched coupling,

\[
G_s'(1)=\frac2\pi\int_0^1q^{2s-2}\frac{(1-q^2)^2}{(1+q^2)^2}\,dq.
\]

Use \((1+q^2)^{-2}=1/4+O(1-q)\) uniformly near \(1\), and the exact beta-integral identity

\[
\int_0^1q^{2s-2}(1-q^2)^2\,dq
=\frac{8}{(2s-1)(2s+1)(2s+3)}=s^{-3}+O(s^{-4}).
\]

The remainder is \(O(s^{-4})\), because it is bounded by a multiple of \(\int_0^1q^{2s-2}(1-q)^3dq\). Thus

\[
P_s'(1)=G_s'(1)=\frac1{2\pi s^3}+O(s^{-4}).
\]

Strict concavity and the mean-value theorem at the maximizing \(\eta_s\to1\) first imply \(\eta_s-1=O(s^{-3})\). A second application, using \(F''(1)=-2/(3\pi)\) and \(G_s''=O(s^{-2})\), yields

\[
\eta_s-1=-\frac{P_s'(1)}{F''(1)}+O(s^{-4})
=\frac{3}{4s^3}+O(s^{-4}).
\]

Taylor expansion at \(\eta=1\) finally gives

\[
P_s(\eta_s)-P_s(1)=P_s'(1)(\eta_s-1)+\tfrac12F''(1)(\eta_s-1)^2+O(s^{-8})
=\frac{3}{16\pi s^6}+O(s^{-7}).
\]

This completes the theorem. The proof establishes eventual uniqueness, **not** uniqueness for every small \(s\).

## Relationship to prior work

Riberi, arXiv:2605.08056v4, derives the exact absorption representation \(F+G_s\), observes weak/strong approximate reciprocity, exhibits a boundary-localized mode, and identifies the asymptotic matched-coupling optimum. Appendix D.2 obtains the finite-distance correction through inverse-cubic order. Those ingredients are established and **not** claimed here. The extension determines (i) the strict side of the optimum at every finite distance, (ii) existence and eventual uniqueness of the global optimum rather than a local stationary point, (iii) the exact reciprocal asymmetry and monotone distance law, and (iv) sharp coefficients for the optimizer displacement and improvement over matching.

Ammara, Potoček, Štefaňák and Pepe, arXiv:2508.13318v3, analyze discrete-time coined walks with two terminal projective sinks on a finite line. Stefanutti and collaborators, arXiv:2601.13269v2, experimentally investigate finite coined photonic walks with a programmable leaking boundary. Their absorption observables and control parameters do not imply the continuous Lindblad half-line optimization established here. Other finite-path quantum absorption results with competing sinks concern a different geometry and cannot be substituted for the one-boundary half-line limit.

## Limitations

The theorem is conditional on the rigorously published spectral representation for the specific semi-infinite, homogeneous model and localized initial site. It does not quantify convergence of finite-time detection or finite-truncation errors in the analytical limit. For \(s\ge2\), a global maximizer exists and lies in \((1,\infty)\), but uniqueness at each small fixed \(s\) is **not proved**. The advantage over \(\eta=1\) vanishes at order \(s^{-6}\), so it can be operationally negligible at large distances. Search-based originality checks cannot rule out an unindexed or implicit prior derivation from the earlier exact integral; independent validation has not been performed.
