# Finite-field Wedderburn blocks of left multiplication envelopes of Latin Alexander quandle algebras

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(G\) be a finite abelian group, let \(\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), and suppose \(\beta=1-\varphi\) is also an automorphism. Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\) have characteristic coprime to \(|G|\). Give the vector space \(A=k[\operatorname{Alex}(G,\varphi)]\) basis \(e_g\), indexed by \(g\in G\), and bilinear (generally nonassociative) product
\[
e_xe_y=e_{\varphi x+\beta y}.
\]
Let \(\mathcal M_l(A)\subseteq\operatorname{End}_k(A)\) denote the unital associative algebra generated under composition by **left** multiplications \(L_{e_x}:e_y\mapsto e_xe_y\).

In a finite splitting extension of \(k\), let \(\widehat G\) be the character group. Let \(\mathscr O\) be the set of orbits of \(\beta^*:\chi\mapsto\chi\circ\beta\) on \(\widehat G\). The \(q\)-power Frobenius permutes \(\mathscr O\). For each Frobenius orbit \(\mathcal C\subseteq\mathscr O\), write \(f_{\mathcal C}=|\mathcal C|\) and \(t_{\mathcal C}=|O|\) for any \(O\in\mathcal C\). Then there is an isomorphism of unital \(k\)-algebras
\[
\boxed{\mathcal M_l(A)\cong\prod_{\mathcal C\in\mathscr O/\langle q\rangle}M_{t_{\mathcal C}}(\mathbb F_{q^{f_{\mathcal C}}}).}
\]
In particular, **this algebra is semisimple even when the field characteristic divides the order of the affine permutation group** \(G\rtimes\langle\beta\rangle\). Its dimension and commutant satisfy
\[
\boxed{\dim_k\mathcal M_l(A)=\sum_{O\in\mathscr O}|O|^2,\qquad
\operatorname{Cent}_{\operatorname{End}_k(A)}(\mathcal M_l(A))=Z(\mathcal M_l(A)),\qquad
\dim_k Z(\mathcal M_l(A))=|\mathscr O|.}
\]
For \(G=C_n\), if \(\beta\) acts as multiplication by a unit \(b\pmod n\), the first two dimensions are given without character enumeration by
\[
\dim_k\mathcal M_l(A)=\sum_{d\mid n}\varphi_{\mathrm E}(d)\operatorname{ord}_d(b),\qquad
\dim_k Z(\mathcal M_l(A))=\sum_{d\mid n}\frac{\varphi_{\mathrm E}(d)}{\operatorname{ord}_d(b)},
\]
where \(\varphi_{\mathrm E}\) is Euler's totient and \(\operatorname{ord}_1(b)=1\).

The decomposition is genuinely sensitive to the coefficient field. For \(G=C_7\) and \(\beta=2\), it yields \(\mathbb F_2\times M_3(\mathbb F_2)^2\) over \(\mathbb F_2\), but \(\mathbb F_3\times M_3(\mathbb F_9)\) over \(\mathbb F_3\). Both have dimension \(19\) over their respective fields. For \(G=C_2^2\) and \(\beta\) represented by \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) over \(\mathbb F_2\), the envelope over \(\mathbb F_3\) is \(\mathbb F_3\times M_3(\mathbb F_3)\) of dimension \(10\), despite \(3\mid|G\rtimes\langle\beta\rangle|=12\).

## Assumptions and scope

The restrictions that \(\varphi\) and \(1-\varphi\) are group automorphisms ensure that the Alexander quandle is Latin and connected, and that all its basis-element left multiplications are bijections. The coefficient characteristic must be coprime to \(|G|\), but may divide the order of \(\beta\) or \(G\rtimes\langle\beta\rangle\). All finite abelian groups, including even-order groups, and all prime-power field orders \(q\) satisfying these hypotheses are covered. The conclusion concerns the **associative algebra generated by the left multiplication operators**, not the nonassociative quandle algebra itself and not its right multiplication algebra.

## Proof

1. **Recover translations.** Set \(U=L_{e_0}\), so \(U(e_y)=e_{\beta y}\). Since \(\beta\) has finite order, \(U^{-1}\) is a positive power of \(U\) and belongs to \(\mathcal M_l(A)\). For every \(x\in G\), \(L_{e_x}U^{-1}(e_y)=e_{\varphi x+y}\); because \(\varphi\) is surjective, \(\mathcal M_l(A)\) is generated by all translations \(T_a:e_y\mapsto e_{a+y}\), together with \(U\). Their permutations form \(G\rtimes\langle\beta\rangle\).

2. **Fourier projectors.** Choose \(E=\mathbb F_{q^s}\) with \(\exp(G)\mid q^s-1\). In \(E\otimes_k A\) define \(v_\chi=\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g)e_g\). Since \(\operatorname{char}k\nmid|G|\), these are a basis. Translations act diagonally: \(T_av_\chi=\chi(-a)v_\chi\). Therefore
\[
P_\chi=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{a\in G}\chi(a)T_a
\]
projects onto the line \(Ev_\chi\); all such projectors belong to \(E\otimes_k\mathcal M_l(A)\).

3. **Exact split matrix units.** The operator \(U\) sends \(v_\chi\) to \(v_{\chi\circ\beta^{-1}}\), hence permutes the Fourier lines along the \(\beta^*\)-orbits. If \(\chi,\psi\) lie in the same orbit, a suitable \(P_\chi U^jP_\psi\) is the matrix unit from \(Ev_\psi\) to \(Ev_\chi\). If they lie in different orbits, every generator preserves both orbit subspaces. Consequently
\[
E\otimes_k\mathcal M_l(A)\cong\prod_{O\in\mathscr O}M_{|O|}(E).
\]
This proof uses no assumption that the characteristic is coprime to the order of \(\beta\).

4. **Descent and simple blocks.** The coefficient Frobenius sends \(P_\chi\) to \(P_{\chi^q}\) and permutes the displayed matrix-algebra factors accordingly. On each Frobenius cycle \(\mathcal C\) of length \(f_{\mathcal C}\), the descended factor is a simple finite-dimensional algebra whose center is \(\mathbb F_{q^{f_{\mathcal C}}}\), and its scalar extension is a product of \(f_{\mathcal C}\) matrix blocks of size \(t_{\mathcal C}\). By Wedderburn–Artin and the fact that every finite division ring is a field, it is \(M_{t_{\mathcal C}}(\mathbb F_{q^{f_{\mathcal C}}})\). The centers of distinct Frobenius cycles are separated by their descended central projectors. This proves the displayed finite-field decomposition.

5. **Commutant and dimensions.** Over \(E\), the natural module is a multiplicity-one direct sum of the Fourier orbit subspaces, each acted on by its entire matrix algebra. Its commutant is exactly the product of scalar operators on those subspaces, which is the center. Scalar extension is faithfully flat for finite-dimensional vector spaces, so the same equality holds over \(k\). Counting dimensions gives the formulas in the theorem.

6. **Cyclic specialization.** Characters of \(C_n\) with exact order \(d\) number \(\varphi_{\mathrm E}(d)\). Multiplication by \(b\) has orbit length \(\operatorname{ord}_d(b)\) on each such character. There are \(\varphi_{\mathrm E}(d)/\operatorname{ord}_d(b)\) orbits of that size, proving both cyclic formulas.

## Verification

The standalone script `verify_left_alexander.py` calculates, without Fourier matrix units, the rank over the coefficient characteristic of the span of the actual affine permutation matrices \(T_aU^j\). It then independently computes the proposed character-orbit dimensions and the Frobenius orbit block dimensions. Run from the package directory using `python3 verify_left_alexander.py`.

All \(19\) cyclic configurations (including two deliberate repeats with distinct assertions) and all five two-generator configurations pass. In particular, the direct matrix rank is \(19\) for the cyclic seven-element examples, \(17\) for a five-element affine example, \(101\) for an eleven-element one, \(145\) for a thirteen-element one, and \(10\) for the modular four-element example. The verifier checks finite instances; the general claims follow from the proof above, not from exhaustion of all groups or fields.

## Relationship to prior work

Zhang's 2026 group-ring treatment of Alexander quandle rings motivates the question but deals with augmentation, integral idempotents and **right** Peirce spectra, not the left multiplication associative envelope. Kaur and Singh (2024), especially Theorems 6.2 and 8.10, already prove a **complex** right-regular multiplicity-free decomposition for connected affine quandles. That result is relevant to the complex version of the present object: the left operators for \((G,\varphi)\) are the right operators for the dual affine operation with parameter \(1-\varphi\). The complex representation and multiplicity-free phenomena are therefore **not** claimed as new. The new assertions are the exact full finite-field matrix-and-center decomposition, including non-splitting Frobenius blocks and the modular case in which the field characteristic divides the order of the affine acting group. A complex multiplicity-free theorem alone does not imply semisimplicity in such modular characteristics. Elhamdadi, Senesi and Zappala (2022) decompose complex right-regular representations of dihedral quandles. Elhamdadi and collaborators (2021) study derivations and Lie transformation algebras, rather than this associative envelope.

## Limitations

The result does not cover coefficient characteristics dividing \(|G|\), non-Latin Alexander quandles, or arbitrary non-affine quandles. The ordinary complex-case multiplicity-free statement is covered by earlier work. Standard finite-group representation theory supplies much of the proof mechanism, so related finite-field decompositions may exist under affine-group or Gelfand-pair terminology. The present literature comparison does not constitute a claim of exhaustive bibliographic priority. Independent mathematical validation remains outstanding.
