# Finite-field weighted permutation evolution algebras: exact cycle, symmetry and idempotent classifications

## Finding
Let \(q\ge 2\) be a prime power, \(n\ge1\), and let \(E\) be an \(n\)-dimensional evolution algebra over \(\mathbf F_q\) admitting a natural basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\) with
\[
e_i e_j=0\quad(i\ne j),\qquad e_i^2=a_i e_{\sigma(i)},\quad a_i\in\mathbf F_q^\times,\ \sigma\in S_n.
\]
All such algebras are perfect, meaning \(E^2=E\). Algebra isomorphisms throughout are \(\mathbf F_q\)-linear; field automorphisms are **not** included.

For every positive integer \(\ell\), set
\[
g_\ell=\gcd(q-1,2^\ell-1),\qquad
h_\ell=\frac1\ell\sum_{r=0}^{\ell-1}\gcd(g_\ell,2^r-1).
\]
The complete isomorphism classification is a multiset of *weighted cycle types*. A cycle of length \(\ell\) is labeled by an orbit under squaring on the cyclic group
\[
Q_\ell=\mathbf F_q^\times/(\mathbf F_q^\times)^{2^\ell-1},
\qquad |Q_\ell|=g_\ell.
\]
There are exactly \(h_\ell\) types of length \(\ell\). Consequently, if \(a_q(n)\) counts the isomorphism classes of these algebras in dimension \(n\), with \(a_q(0)=1\) as a formal empty-object convention, then
\[
\boxed{\sum_{n\ge0}a_q(n)z^n=\prod_{\ell\ge1}(1-z^\ell)^{-h_\ell}.}
\]

For an individual length-\(\ell\) cycle type \(t\), let \(d_t\) be the size of its squaring orbit in \(Q_\ell\). If the complete type multiset has multiplicities \(m_t\), then
\[
\boxed{|\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbf F_q}(E)|
=\prod_t\left(g_{\ell(t)}\frac{\ell(t)}{d_t}\right)^{m_t}m_t!.}
\]
The number of *elements* \(x\in E\) satisfying \(x^2=x\) is
\[
\boxed{|\{x\in E:x^2=x\}|=\prod_{t\text{ with identity label in }Q_{\ell(t)}}(1+g_{\ell(t)})^{m_t}.}
\]
Thus a cycle with a nonidentity label has only the zero idempotent, whereas a cycle with the identity label has exactly \(1+g_\ell\) idempotents, including zero.

There exists a positive-dimensional algebra of this weighted-permutation form with **no nonzero idempotent element** if and only if \(q-1\) has an odd prime divisor. If so, its minimum possible dimension is
\[
\boxed{\min_{\substack{p\mid(q-1)\\p\text{ odd prime}}}\operatorname{ord}_{p}(2).}
\]
If \(q-1\) is a power of two, every such algebra has exactly \(2^{c}\) idempotents, where \(c\) is the number of cycles of \(\sigma\).

## Assumptions and scope
The coefficients \(a_i\) are required to be nonzero. Both fixed points and longer cycles of \(\sigma\) are allowed; the directed support is exactly a disjoint union of directed cycles. The result holds in all characteristics, including characteristic two, and in arbitrary positive dimension. It does not classify general evolution algebras with more than one nonzero structural coefficient per basis-square.

For an ordered cycle \(C=(i_0,i_1,\ldots,i_{\ell-1})\), with \(\sigma(i_j)=i_{j+1}\) cyclically, define
\[
P_C(a)=\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1}a_{i_j}^{2^{\ell-1-j}}.
\]
Its class in \(Q_\ell\), considered up to the cyclic action generated by squaring, is the cycle label. Reversing the direction of an individual directed cycle is not an allowed independent identification unless induced by an actual directed-graph isomorphism; the relevant graph automorphism group consists of cyclic rotations, not dihedral reflections.

## Proof
**1. All isomorphisms preserve the cycle support up to permutation.** Since \(a_i\ne0\) and \(\sigma\) is a permutation, the vectors \(e_i^2\) form a basis. Any image \(f_i\) of a natural basis under an isomorphism is again a natural basis. Writing \(f_i=\sum_j c_{ji}e_j\), the equations \(f_i f_k=0\) for \(i\ne k\) and independence of \(e_j^2\) imply \(c_{ji}c_{jk}=0\) for every \(j\) and \(i\ne k\). Invertibility therefore forces each row and column of \((c_{ji})\) to have exactly one nonzero entry. Hence every isomorphism is monomial, and induces an isomorphism of the directed permutation support graphs.

**2. The exact cycle invariant.** For one length-\(\ell\) cycle, rescale \(f_{i_j}=u_j e_{i_j}\), where all \(u_j\ne0\). The new weights are
\[
a'_{i_j}=a_{i_j}u_j^2/u_{j+1}.
\]
Multiplying with the powers in \(P_C\), all intermediate scaling factors cancel and
\[
P_C(a')=P_C(a)u_0^{2^\ell-1}.
\]
Conversely, choosing \(u_0\) to realize a prescribed change of \(P_C\) by a \((2^\ell-1)\)-st power and solving successive weight equations determines the remaining \(u_j\). Thus fixed-origin diagonal-scaling classes are precisely \(Q_\ell\). Advancing the chosen origin once gives \([P_{\mathrm{next}}]=[P_C]^2\) in \(Q_\ell\). Since \(2^\ell\equiv1\) modulo \(2^\ell-1\), squaring acts invertibly on \(Q_\ell\), and the full length-\(\ell\) cycle types are exactly its squaring orbits.

**3. Count cycle types and arbitrary direct sums.** The multiplicative group \(\mathbf F_q^\times\) is cyclic of order \(q-1\), so \(Q_\ell\) is cyclic of order \(g_\ell\). The fixed points of its \(r\)-fold squaring map are the solutions of \(x^{2^r-1}=1\), exactly \(\gcd(g_\ell,2^r-1)\) in number. Burnside's lemma gives \(h_\ell\). Distinct support components can be permuted by an isomorphism only when their weighted cycle types agree. Hence an algebra type is an unordered multiset of cycle types; the standard multiset product gives the displayed generating function.

**4. Automorphism orders.** A length-\(\ell\) cycle with squaring-orbit length \(d\) admits exactly \(\ell/d\) compatible rotations. For each such rotation, the simultaneous scaling equations have exactly \(g_\ell\) solutions, the kernel of the \((2^\ell-1)\)-power map. This yields \(g_\ell\ell/d\) automorphisms per component. Interchanging \(m_t\) isomorphic components contributes a wreath-product factor \(m_t!\), giving the stated product for the full automorphism group.

**5. Idempotent elements.** On one cycle the equations for an element \(x=\sum_i x_ie_i\) to satisfy \(x^2=x\) are \(x_{\sigma(i)}=a_i x_i^2\). If one coefficient is zero then every coefficient on that cycle is zero. Otherwise, solving recursively from the initial nonzero coefficient gives one equation of the form \(u^{2^\ell-1}=b\), whose right-hand side has class equal to the identity exactly when \([P_C(a)]\) is the identity. A solvable equation has precisely \(g_\ell\) solutions. Thus the cycle has either only zero or \(1+g_\ell\) idempotents, and independent cycles multiply their counts.

**6. Sharp existence dimension.** A cycle can have a nonidentity label if and only if \(g_\ell>1\). Since \(2^\ell-1\) is odd, this is equivalent to the existence of an odd prime \(p\mid(q-1)\) satisfying \(p\mid(2^\ell-1)\), equivalently \(\operatorname{ord}_p(2)\mid\ell\). The least possible cycle length carrying a nonidentity label is therefore the displayed minimum. A single cycle with such a label has no nonzero idempotent. At any smaller total dimension every component length has \(g_\ell=1\), so at least one nonzero idempotent exists. If \(q-1\) is a power of two, all \(g_\ell=1\), and every component contributes two idempotents.

## Verification
The standalone script `check_permutation_evolution.py` constructs the complete space of weighted permutation algebras by permuting basis positions and recording nonzero weights as exponents in the cyclic multiplicative group of order \(q-1\). It computes independent monomial-basis-change orbits via graph traversal and compares their numbers with the Euler-product coefficient formula. It also directly enumerates automorphisms and idempotent coordinates for sample complete parameter spaces without invoking the cycle-invariant formula during those enumerations.

All class-count comparisons passed for \((q,n)=(2,1),(3,1),(3,2),(4,2),(5,2),(7,2),(4,3),(5,3),(7,3),(8,3),(4,4),(5,4)\). All automorphism and element-idempotent comparisons passed for all weighted permutations in \((q,n)=(3,2),(4,2),(5,3),(7,3),(8,3)\). The final checker output was `CHECKER_OK`. These finite tests corroborate but do not replace the all-dimension proof.

For instance, over \(\mathbf F_4\), there are exactly three isomorphism classes in dimension two: one with two fixed-point components and two with a single two-cycle. The two two-cycle classes have automorphism-group orders \(6\) and \(3\), and respectively \(4\) and \(1\) idempotent elements.

## Relationship to prior work
Sriwongsa and Zou, *On Automorphism Groups of Idempotent Evolution Algebras* (2022), study automorphism groups and maximal diagonal symmetry; their normalization/classification theorem in Section 4 assumes an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. The present finite-field classification resolves the scalar-power obstruction that need not disappear over \(\mathbf F_q\), and counts symmetry and element idempotents for every weighted permutation support, not only maximally symmetric algebras.

Wei and Zou, *Isomorphism Classes of Idempotent Evolution Algebras* (2023), provide a general finite-field monomial group action and a Burnside count of all perfect evolution-algebra isomorphism classes, with explicit dimension-two-to-four totals. The present theorem supplies a cycle-by-cycle classification, arbitrary-dimension Euler product and stabilizers restricted to the natural weighted-permutation locus, together with a sharp element-idempotent criterion; the general counts alone do not give these specialized invariants explicitly.

Khudoyberdiyev, Omirov and Qaralleh (2013) normalize all nonzero weighted cycles over their characteristic-zero setting using roots of order \(2^\ell-1\). Narkuziyev's 2020 preprint treats evolution algebras defined by two permutations and some idempotents; its full text was unavailable for comparison in this review. The finite-field obstruction and exact arithmetic classification here should not be read as a claim about unrestricted evolution algebras. Work on Cayley evolution algebras addresses automorphism realization rather than the specified finite-field weighted-cycle count.

## Limitations
The class formula applies only to invertible weighted-permutation structure matrices, not arbitrary invertible structure matrices or nonperfect evolution algebras. It distinguishes \(\mathbf F_q\)-linear isomorphisms, not semilinear equivalences. The actual theorem is established algebraically; finite verification sizes provide consistency checks rather than a separate infinite certificate. Unindexed equivalent results and the uninspected full text of a closely related 2020 preprint remain originality risks.
