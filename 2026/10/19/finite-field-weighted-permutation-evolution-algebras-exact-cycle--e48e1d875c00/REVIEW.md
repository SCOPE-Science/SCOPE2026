# Scientific review: weighted-permutation evolution algebras

## Correctness — PASS
The proof derives monomial rigidity directly from the independence of the squared natural-basis elements, identifies the quotient of weight products by \((2^\ell-1)\)-st powers, and accounts for every rotation and interchange of isomorphic directed cycles. Cyclic-group fixed-point counts produce the Euler product; the exact scalar equations independently yield the automorphism orders and the zero-versus-\(g_\ell\) nonzero-idempotent dichotomy. The minimal-dimension statement follows from multiplicative orders modulo odd primes. The packaged checker independently enumerates basis-change orbits, automorphisms, and idempotent elements in the indicated finite test domains, with all tests passing. Testing is not substituted for the proof.

## Originality — PASS with stated risks
The 2013 cycle-normalization theorem uses a field in which the requisite roots can be chosen; the 2022 automorphism result assumes an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field for its maximal-diagonal theorem. Over a finite field the quotient by \((2^\ell-1)\)-st powers can be nontrivial, and the checked primary texts do not state the complete finite-field cycle-label, stabilizer, Euler-product, and idempotent-obstruction classification. Wei and Zou's 2023 general finite-field orbit formula is broader in objects but does not explicitly supply these weighted-permutation refinements. The 2020 related preprint on two-permutation evolution algebras was only accessible by abstract; equivalent results there or in unindexed work remain residual risks. Search non-detection is not a uniqueness proof.

## Value — PASS
The arithmetic failure of coefficient normalization over finite fields creates genuinely different algebras with identical directed support. The finding completely resolves their isomorphism types, automorphism-group orders, and element-idempotent counts for arbitrary dimension and field size. The sharp minimal dimension of a weighted-permutation algebra without nonzero idempotents distinguishes this restricted structural family from unrestricted perfect evolution algebras and provides a precise use for the new invariant.
