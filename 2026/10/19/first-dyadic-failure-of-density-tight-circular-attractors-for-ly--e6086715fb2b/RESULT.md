# First dyadic failure of density-tight circular attractors for Lyndon de Bruijn cycles

## Finding
Let \(F_k\) be the lexicographically least binary de Bruijn cycle of order \(k\), defined without choosing a rotation by concatenating in lexicographic order the binary Lyndon words whose lengths divide \(k\). Index its \(2^k\) cyclic positions from zero, in the specified concatenation order. Let \(\gamma_{\mathrm{circ}}(F_k)\) be the least cardinality of a set of cyclic positions intersecting some occurrence of every distinct nonempty circular factor of lengths at most \(2^k\).

For the five dyadic orders \(k\in\{1,2,4,8,16\}\), the elementary information-density lower bound is attained for exactly the first four orders. Specifically,
\[
\gamma_{\mathrm{circ}}(F_k)=2^k/k\quad(k\in\{1,2,4,8\}),
\qquad \gamma_{\mathrm{circ}}(F_{16})\ge4097>2^{16}/16=4096.
\]
For \(k=8\), the *only* set attaining \(2^8/8=32\) is the zero-based residue class \(4\pmod8\). At order sixteen, six explicitly listed circular factors of length thirteen already exclude all sixteen possible equally spaced \(4096\)-position sets.

This is a strict obstruction to lower-bound attainment for one precisely defined, canonical circular word; it is **not** an exact determination of \(\gamma_{\mathrm{circ}}(F_{16})\), nor a claim about all binary order-sixteen de Bruijn cycles.

## Assumptions and scope
A circular occurrence of a word \(u\) of length \(\ell\) starting at position \(s\) consists of positions \(s,s+1,\ldots,s+\ell-1\), taken modulo the cycle length. A set \(\Gamma\) is a circular attractor when every nonempty factor has at least one occurrence whose positions meet \(\Gamma\). The present problem concerns **full** circular attractors, not only attractors for factors of one prescribed length and not attractors for a linear unfolding.

For order sixteen set \(N=65536\). The defining Lyndon concatenation is equivalently generated by the Fredricksen–Kessler–Maiorana recursive procedure implemented in `checker.py`. The six length-thirteen factors below are ordinary binary strings; their stated occurrence positions are **all** their circular starting positions, not merely examples.

## Proof
**1. Rigidity at the density bound.** By the de Bruijn property, all \(N=2^k\) circular length-\(k\) factors are different. Thus each of the \(N\) circular length-\(k\) windows must itself contain a position of \(\Gamma\). Every position lies in exactly \(k\) such windows, so \(|\Gamma|\ge\lceil N/k\rceil\). If \(k\mid N\) and equality \(|\Gamma|=N/k\) holds, then the \(k\)-window families incident to different selected positions must be disjoint: their total incidence count is already exactly \(N\). A consecutive pair of selected positions at cyclic distance less than \(k\) would share a length-\(k\) window, a contradiction. All cyclic successive gaps are therefore at least \(k\), and their sum is \(N\), so every gap is exactly \(k\). Hence the only equality candidates are the \(k\) residue classes modulo \(k\).

**2. Six-factor obstruction at order sixteen.** If a length-\(13\) factor occurs at starts \(S\), it is intersected by the marked residue class \(r\pmod{16}\) if and only if \(r\equiv s+j\pmod{16}\) for some \(s\in S\) and \(0\le j\le12\). The certificate table identifies six factors, lists *every* occurrence start of each, and computes its forbidden residue set by this equivalence. The six forbidden sets cover all residues \(0,\ldots,15\). Consequently no equally spaced set of \(4096\) positions is a circular attractor. Step 1 shows every possible attractor of that cardinality must be equally spaced. Therefore \(\gamma_{\mathrm{circ}}(F_{16})\ge4097\).

**3. The preceding dyadic orders.** For each \(k\in\{1,2,4,8\}\), the same cyclic incidence criterion is applied to *every distinct factor of every length* \(1\le\ell\le k\). At least one residue class passes at each order, and at order eight only residue four passes. Any length exceeding \(k\) contains a length-\(k\) window, so if every length-\(k\) window meets the marked set, every occurrence of every longer circular factor meets it automatically. Thus the full attractor condition is established at these orders. The program exhausts all factor starts, factor lengths \(1\) through \(k\), and relevant residues; see Verification.

## Verification
Run `python3 checker.py` with standard Python, no nonstandard dependencies. The program independently reconstructs \(F_{16}\) by both a recursive binary de Bruijn generator and a direct enumeration/lexicographic sort of all primitive Lyndon words of divisor lengths, checks equality of both output strings, checks that every possible binary length-sixteen circular factor appears exactly once, and checks all \(48\) specified length-thirteen occurrence starts by a separate rolling-window enumeration. It computes the forbidden residues from those starts and checks that their union is all sixteen residues. It also checks the complete allowed-offset sets in orders \(1,2,4,8\), covering every factor length up to its order.

The recorded run in `checker_output.txt` terminates with `CERTIFICATE_OK: no circular string attractor of size 4096`. The mathematics in Proof steps 1 and 2 explains why these finite checks constitute an exhaustive lower-bound certificate. The checker does not enumerate all possible attractors of cardinality \(4097\) or certify a matching upper bound.

## Relationship to prior work
Mantaci, Restivo, Romana, Rosone, and Sciortino introduced circular string attractors and supplied general asymptotic bounds for attractors of **linear** de Bruijn unfoldings; their Proposition 41 does not distinguish the canonical circular order-sixteen word. Whittington explicitly separates the always-valid *sharp* order-\(k\) covering by equally spaced marks from the full attractor property, investigates the lexicographic Lyndon construction, and proves a prime-order case (Theorem 29). Since sixteen is composite, that theorem does not establish the present finding; indeed the finite certificate here shows strict lower-bound nonattainment for the circular Lyndon-generated order-sixteen cycle. Recent exact attractor classifications for finite Fibonacci, period-doubling, and Thue–Morse words concern different word families.

The crucial difference from a generic de Bruijn lower bound is the **nonexistence of every equality case** for the canonical word. An earlier independently studied binary order-eight de Bruijn cycle also exhibits nonattainment, but the existence of that different cycle does not imply nonattainment for the lexicographically least order-sixteen cycle. Conversely, the canonical order-eight cycle here **does** attain its density bound. This is the first failure along the specified dyadic sequence of canonical cycles, not the first known de Bruijn nonattainment example in general.

## Limitations
Only the five stated dyadic orders are considered. The order-sixteen result is a certified strict lower bound \(4097\), **not** an exact optimum, not an arbitrary-order formula, and not a statement that no other de Bruijn cycle attains \(4096\). The cyclic positions are fixed by the Lyndon concatenation; rotation of the entire cycle rotates the same mathematical obstruction. The six factors are an exact finite obstruction, not an extrapolation. No independent scientific audit has been performed; an unindexed equivalent prior calculation remains possible.
