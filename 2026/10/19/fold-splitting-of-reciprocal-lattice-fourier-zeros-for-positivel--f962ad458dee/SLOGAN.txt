Each reciprocal-lattice Fourier zero of the standard triangle with nonzero, distinct integer coordinates is a fold: whenever a small real bounded weight perturbation has a nonzero real Fourier coefficient at that node, precisely two simple real zeros appear or none remain locally, with square-root displacement. For the positive affine density \(1+\varepsilon x\), exactly three quarters of the lattice nodes by asymptotic angular density split into pairs when \(\varepsilon>0\), while the other quarter locally disappear.
