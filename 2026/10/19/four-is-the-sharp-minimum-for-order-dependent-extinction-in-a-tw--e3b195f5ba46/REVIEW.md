# Scientific review: four-pulse order threshold in two-stage population control

**Correctness — PASS.** The universal statement has a complete proof: a positive diagonal change of basis makes every two-stage irreducible Metzler flow matrix symmetric; conjugation by the symmetric positive-definite square root of the flow then makes every pulse factor symmetric. Cyclic rotations and reversal generate all permutations of one, two, or three factors and preserve their characteristic polynomials. For four factors an exact biologically admissible example is exhibited. Direct rational multiplication gives common determinant \(50787/16000000<1\), while the determinants at unit eigenvalue have strictly opposite signs. Positivity and Perron–Frobenius theory justify the asymptotic extinction/growth claims. `verify.py` was executed and independently replays every rational comparison. The analytic proof, not the permutation sample, covers arbitrary parameters.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risks.** The source eco-evolutionary model treats a single repeatedly used pulse matrix, and the predecessor spectral-radius work varies the interval with the matrix fixed. Mermer and colleagues' 2020/2021 pest-control study uses a more complex, time-dependent life-stage model and compares *finite-season* ordering of three applications, not the repeating-block Floquet spectrum. Their work is credited for the motivation and does not imply the sharp four-treatment boundary. Existing linear-algebra symmetrization, cyclic similarity, and transpose facts are also credited. Searches for an equivalent periodic-impulse order theorem, broader spectrally dominating results and exact published tables did not locate a covering statement. A related 2023 empirical two-application paper was accessible in abstract, but not in complete text; unindexed treatments of repeated pulse products remain a risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Ecological pulse order is a motivated intervention-design variable, and the quantitative threshold controls whether a positive stage population becomes extinct or instead grows. The theorem identifies a rigorous scheduling immunity for three or fewer types and proves the *first possible* loss of that immunity with an exact extinction-versus-growth reversal at four. This is stronger and more structural than a small improvement in a simulated population count, while carefully distinguishing asymptotic repetition from the known finite-season ordering effects.

**Scope and limitations.** The result applies only to constant two-stage linear ecological flows, equal positive gaps, strictly positive diagonal survival matrices and repeating schedules. The explicit reversal is on the invariant fully resistant genotype face; the work does not classify allele dynamics or bounded ecological permanence. Matrix identities and exact arithmetic were checked in a standalone program; independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
