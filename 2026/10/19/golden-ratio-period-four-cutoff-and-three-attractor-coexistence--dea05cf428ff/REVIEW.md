# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** Every possible primitive period-four line itinerary uses three lines with exactly one repeated supporting line, because alternating two-line perpendicular projections have no nonvertex periodic solution and vertices cannot lie on nonfixed periodic orbits in an acute triangle. The affine-return lemma gives a unique candidate for each repeated side, and its four strict squared-distance comparisons give necessary and sufficient branch admissibility. The isosceles base yields the golden-ratio threshold; either repeated leg would require a negative perfect square. The separate exhaustive three-cycle branch computation establishes its two orientations and cutoff. Return derivatives are strictly contracting. The executable independently enumerated period-three and period-four itineraries at twelve rational heights and confirmed every predicted orbit count. No finite test substitutes for the proof.

## Originality

**PASS (best-known literature comparison; priority risk disclosed).** Everett's accessible complete HTML article (arXiv:2408.16019v1, §§2–4) gives the map and numerical isosceles bifurcations, but not the exact golden-ratio period-four cutoff, all-four-cycle proof, or three-attractor coexistence classification. A published result titled *Exact two-orientation classification of primitive three-cycles for the farthest-projection triangle map* is **strongly related** and covers the period-three component; this package explicitly does not present that component as new. Its indexed statement does not imply the period-four count; the full hosted content was inaccessible, so possible hidden overlap is a residual priority risk. A previously recorded equilateral basin calculation addresses \(\(v=\sqrt3/2\)\), outside the four-cycle window, and does not imply the present threshold. No exact matching published four-cycle classification was found in the recorded searches, but that alone is not novelty proof.

## Value

**PASS.** The motivating publication emphasizes periodic-orbit bifurcations without exact height thresholds. The new sharp cutoff identifies a nontrivial golden-ratio branch collision, proves that **both** symmetric leg points of one four-cycle simultaneously become fixed ties, and locates a whole open interval of coexistence of three distinct locally attracting cycles. This is a structural explanation of a change in the geometry's short-period dynamics, rather than a routine single numerical orbit or a merely relabeled three-cycle computation.

## Limits

The theorem covers only acute isosceles triangles and prime periods three and four; other periods and global basins remain open here. It depends on the original fixed-on-tie convention. The comparison with the published three-cycle record is limited to its indexed description. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
