For farthest-perpendicular projection among the three complete lines of an acute isosceles triangle, a primitive four-cycle exists exactly below the sharp golden-ratio height \(v=\sqrt{(1+\sqrt5)/8}\), and it is uniquely determined by a symmetric rectangle. Throughout \(1/2<v<\sqrt{(1+\sqrt5)/8}\), that four-cycle coexists with two attracting three-cycles, yielding three different locally attracting periodic motions.
