# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is an isomorphism classification for finite commutative Gorenstein local rings with nonzero square of the maximal ideal and zero cube, embedding dimension at least two. Its proof has four independently checkable steps: nonsingularity of multiplication pairing from one-dimensional socle; exact annihilator/projective-orthogonality identification; finite-field isotropic point census and degree recovery; finite-field classification of nonsingular symmetric bilinear forms up to similarity. The exceptional all-equal-degree cases and characteristic two are treated explicitly. Finite checks confirm the formulas across 25 forms; twelve finite split-ring instances are checked through annihilator sets, and two distinct-characteristic sixteen-element rings through complete multiplication tables. Computation corroborates but does not replace the quantified proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified by bibliographic risk)
The closest source is Kadu 2020, Proposition 3.6 and Lemma 3.7: it *already* establishes the bilinear pairing and the projective reduction. Those ingredients are credited and are not claimed new. Kadu–Tanpure 2026 determines when the compressed and associate-class graphs of Artinian rings are isomorphic; it does not classify the compressed graphs of *different* short Gorenstein rings. The new assertion is the exact graph-isomorphism classification and recovery of residue-field size, embedding dimension and multiplication-form similarity type, including both characteristic regimes and noncoefficient mixed-characteristic rings. An earlier unpublished or unindexed equivalence with polar-graph reconstruction remains possible.

## Value — PASS
This is a natural complete inverse classification for graphs arising from a broad class of length-\(d+2\) finite local rings: it determines precisely which algebraic information the compressed graph remembers and exhibits a sharp failure to remember the ring itself through characteristic-two/characteristic-four examples. The degree census and two-dimensional Tutte specialization provide accessible invariants and tests for the classification. They are supporting consequences, not presented as the novelty by themselves.

## Closest antecedents and scientific limits
Kadu, arXiv:2010.06924v1 (2020), Sections 2–3, contains the key geometric antecedent. Kadu and Tanpure, arXiv:2609.36021v1 (September 28, 2026), Sections 2–6, motivate the comparison problem. Earlier local-ring results on perfectness of ordinary zero-divisor graphs are also related, but the present invariant is the compressed graph, with the inverse classification as its main claim. The theorem excludes non-Gorenstein, higher-nilpotence and nonlocal rings, and does not reconstruct extension/characteristic data from the graph.
