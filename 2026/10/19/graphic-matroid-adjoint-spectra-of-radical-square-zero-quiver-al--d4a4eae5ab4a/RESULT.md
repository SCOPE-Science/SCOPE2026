# Identical commuting-tuple schemes for Lie-nonisomorphic triangle orientations

## Finding

Let \(Q_{\rm cyc}\) have arrows \(1\to2\), \(2\to3\), \(3\to1\), and let \(Q_{\rm tr}\) reverse the third arrow. Over every field \(k\) of odd characteristic, the commutator Lie algebras of \(kQ_{\rm cyc}/J^2\) and \(kQ_{\rm tr}/J^2\) are not isomorphic. Nevertheless, for every integer \(s\ge2\), the ideals defining their ordered pairwise-commuting \(s\)-tuple schemes are identical in the common coordinate ring: match the three vertex coordinates by vertex and the three arrow coordinates by unordered edge. Thus even the embedded commuting schemes at all arities together do not determine the commutator Lie algebra in this family.

## Assumptions and scope

The arrow ideal is \(J\); all products of arrows vanish in the quotient. We compare Lie algebras over the same \(k\), with bracket \([a,b]=ab-ba\). Equality of ideals is literal after the prescribed linear identification, not just equality of underlying reduced varieties or finite-field counts. The sign argument for the ideals works in every characteristic; in characteristic two the matching of coordinates also matches the Lie brackets, so that characteristic is excluded from the nonisomorphism claim.

## Proof

Write \(a_i=d_i+\sum_e z_{i,e}e_e\), with \(d_i\) in the span of the vertex idempotents and \(e_e\) the basis arrow along edge \(e\). For the oriented arrow \(u\to v\), set \(\alpha_e(d)=d_u-d_v\). Vertex–arrow multiplication gives
\[
[a_i,a_j]_e=\alpha_e(d_i)z_{j,e}-\alpha_e(d_j)z_{i,e}.
\]
There are no other bracket coordinates. Reversing the third arrow multiplies exactly the polynomials for that arrow by \(-1\), a unit. Thus the ideals generated by these polynomials for all \(i<j\) are equal.

For either orientation let \(L\) denote the corresponding commutator Lie algebra. Its derived ideal \(L'\) is the three-dimensional arrow space: all brackets lie there and for each arrow \([e_u,e_e]=e_e\) at its source. This ideal is characteristic and abelian, so the action on it by brackets with lifts of elements of \(L/L'\) is well defined. The three one-dimensional arrow lines have distinct weights in \((L/L')^*\): in the cyclic orientation they are \(d_1-d_2,d_2-d_3,d_3-d_1\); in the transitive orientation they are \(d_1-d_2,d_2-d_3,d_1-d_3\). The joint weight spaces are exactly the arrow lines, since the action is diagonal and a vector supported on two different weights cannot be a joint eigenvector.

A Lie isomorphism must induce compatible linear maps on the derived ideals and on their quotients. Hence its dual quotient map must take one unordered weight multiset onto the other, with no separate rescalings of weights: rescaling a weight vector in the arrow line does not change its eigenvalue functional. In each orientation the first two weights are independent. The unique relation line on the three weights is spanned by \((1,1,1)\) for the cyclic orientation and by \((1,1,-1)\) for the transitive orientation. Simultaneous permutation of coordinates and multiplication by a common nonzero scalar cannot identify the lines if \(1\ne-1\). This proves Lie nonisomorphism.

For comparison, on a simple forest the vertex-difference weights are independent: arbitrary differences on the edges lift to vertex labels by choosing a root label in each component. Reversing a set of arrows there can be absorbed into an invertible change on the diagonal quotient, negating the specified independent functional coordinates and fixing any complementary coordinates. Together with the matching of the arrow lines this gives a Lie isomorphism. The triangle therefore exhibits the obstruction at the first possible cycle length for simple graphs; the obstruction is the sign pattern in a dependence, not an arrow-by-arrow scalar on the commuting equations.

## Verification

The comparison of ideals is a generator-by-generator polynomial identity for every arity. The nonisomorphism is certified by the two explicit weight lists and their relation lines. These proof steps require no finite-field enumeration or comparison of point sets.

## Relationship to prior work

The sign invariance of the commuting equations under reorientation is background, as is the flat formula for the adjoint-rank spectrum of an oriented simple graph. The indexed *Graphic-matroid adjoint spectra and vertex arboricity for radical-square-zero quiver algebras* states that spectrum, and *Joint principal-ideal dimension spectrum of radical-square-zero quiver algebras* explicitly gives the sign argument as context for one-sided associative multiplication invariants. Section 3 of Dutta, Basnet and Nath, [*On commuting probability of finite rings*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.08211), places comparisons of commutator tensors under independent input-quotient and output maps in the framework of additive \(Z\)-isoclinism. None of that background is the contribution here. The particular point is the Lie-nonisomorphic triangle pair: a single signed relation among the quotient action weights destroys Lie isomorphism without changing any of the commuting ideals. The corresponding path/forest comparison does not give such a Lie obstruction.

## Limitations

This is a concrete boundary to reconstruction, not a classification for graphs containing cycles, a classification of commuting schemes up to arbitrary coordinate change, or a claim of a new rank enumerator. Historical priority for this specific triangle comparison is not certified.
