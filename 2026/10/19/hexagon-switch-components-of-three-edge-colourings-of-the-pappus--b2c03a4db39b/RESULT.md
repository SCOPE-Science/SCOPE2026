# Hexagon-switch components of three-edge-colourings of the Pappus graph

## Finding
Let \(P\) be the Pappus graph, realized as the incidence graph between nine points \((x,y)\in\mathbb F_3^2\) and nine lines \((m,b)\in\mathbb F_3^2\), where a point and line are incident exactly when \(y=mx+b\). Each of its \(27\) edges receives one of three *distinguished* colours \(0,1,2\), with all three colours incident at each vertex. A **hexagon switch** interchanges two colours on one alternating six-edge cycle; intermediate colourings must remain proper. Let \(R_6(P)\) be the undirected state graph of these operations.

Define \(T(K_{3,3})\) by retaining six hubs corresponding to the vertices of \(K_{3,3}\), deleting its nine edges, and, for each former edge with endpoints \(u,v\), inserting six new vertices forming a six-cycle, with alternating vertices adjacent to \(u\) and \(v\). Distinct inserted cycles use distinct vertices. This graph has \(60\) vertices, \(108\) edges, six vertices of degree nine and fifty-four vertices of degree three.

**Theorem.** There are exactly \(120\) proper distinguished three-edge-colourings of \(P\), and
\[
R_6(P)\cong T(K_{3,3})\;\sqcup\;T(K_{3,3}).
\]
In particular, \(R_6(P)\) has \(216\) edges; its two components each have \(60\) vertices, \(108\) edges, radius \(6\) and diameter \(6\). Its degree distribution consists of \(108\) vertices of degree \(3\) and \(12\) vertices of degree \(9\).

For each point order its incident edges by slope \(m=0,1,2\), and for each line order incident edges by point abscissa \(x=0,1,2\). Given a colouring \(f\), define \(\epsilon(f)\in\{-1,+1\}\) as the product over all eighteen vertices of the permutation signs of their three colours in these local orders. Then exactly \(60\) colourings have either sign, and
\[
f\text{ and }g\text{ are connected by hexagon switches}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\epsilon(f)=\epsilon(g).
\]
Even if *every* bicoloured alternating cycle is permitted for a Kempe switch, the two components remain the same: the unrestricted transition graph has \(324\) edges and each component has radius and diameter \(4\). Thus six-cycle switches already generate full edge-Kempe reachability on \(P\), but with a larger exact worst-case distance. The quotient by the free global action permuting the three colour names has \(20\) vertices and \(36\) edges, with two copies of \(K_1\vee(3K_3)\); this *quotient* structure was already calculated, and is not claimed as new. The present theorem resolves its nontrivial six-sheeted **labelled hexagon-switch covering** and the exact distances in that cover.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple and undirected. The field \(\mathbb F_3\) uses ordinary arithmetic modulo three. The point vertices are \((x,y)\), the line vertices are \((m,b)\), and the \(27\) edges are indexed lexicographically by \((x,y,m)\), with \(b=y-mx\). A proper edge-colouring uses all three named colours, not colour-permutation equivalence classes. In a cubic graph a colour pair induces a disjoint union of even cycles; each chosen six-cycle exchange is reversible. No other changes (one-edge recolourings, arbitrary pairwise matching swaps or simultaneous switching of multiple cycles) are allowed in \(R_6(P)\).

The definition of \(T(K_{3,3})\) makes the claimed graph isomorphism combinatorially unambiguous. Its new six-cycles each alternate attachments to the two endpoints of one base edge. The parity \(\epsilon\) uses the fixed point and line orders specified above and therefore has no residual convention ambiguity.

## Proof
**1. The parity obstruction.** At either endpoint of an alternating two-colour switch, the local ordered colour triple undergoes a transposition, reversing its sign. A switch around a cycle of length \(2r\) affects exactly \(2r\) vertices, hence multiplies \(\epsilon\) by \((-1)^{2r}=1\). This applies to all permissible hexagons and also to longer edge-Kempe cycles.

**2. Complete state enumeration.** In a proper three-edge-colouring of the cubic bipartite graph, each colour class is a perfect matching. `verify_factorizations.py` enumerates *all* perfect matchings by a depth-first choice of exactly one edge at each of the nine point vertices while forbidding repeated line vertices; no partial branch is silently omitted. It obtains \(42\) perfect matchings. Removing any such matching leaves a spanning two-regular graph. Exactly \(36\) matchings leave one eighteen-cycle; the other \(6\) leave three disjoint six-cycles. For a fixed matching coloured zero, each complementary even cycle has exactly two alternating assignments of colours one and two. Thus the number of fully labelled colourings is
\[
36\cdot2+6\cdot2^3=120.
\]
This is a finite exhaustive argument, not an extrapolation from smaller orders.

**3. Exhaustive adjacency determination.** For every one of the \(120\) states, for each of the three unordered colour pairs, the first program constructs all connected components of their induced two-regular subgraph; it switches every length-six component and indexes the resulting state. Because all possible two-colour switches lie within one of these components, this produces **all and only** allowed hexagon moves. The resulting undirected edge set has \(216\) members. As a separate check, `verify_local_constraints.py` assigns the six possible colour permutations at each point and prunes exactly when an incident line would repeat a colour; after nine points it obtains the same \(120\) states. It enumerates the \(18\) geometric six-cycles by closed simple vertex walks and tests every state-cycle pair. This second construction yields precisely the same \(216\) indexed edges, verified against the complete certificate `pappus_hexagon_certificate.json` and its canonical SHA-256 digests.

**4. Components and graph isomorphism.** Breadth-first or depth-first traversals of the fully determined adjacency list give two components of \(60\) vertices, each constant under \(\epsilon\); the two signs occur \(60\) times each. Thus the parity condition is also sufficient for reachability. The degree-nine vertices in either component number exactly six; all other \(54\) vertices have degree three. Deleting the six high-degree vertices leaves exactly nine vertex-disjoint induced six-cycles. Every vertex of each remaining six-cycle has exactly one high-degree neighbour. Along each six-cycle these neighbours alternate between two distinct hubs, so three positions attach to either hub. The nine unordered pairs of hubs are distinct, each hub appears in three pairs, and the resulting six-vertex hub-incidence graph is the complete bipartite graph \(K_{3,3}\). These checked, exhaustive adjacency facts identify each component with \(T(K_{3,3})\) by the preceding explicit construction. As a comparison with the already determined colour-permutation quotient, global relabellings of colours act freely, forming six states per orbit. The same exhaustive adjacency verifies exactly \(20\) orbits and \(36\) orbit edges, with two \(10\)-vertex components, each a join of a universal vertex and three disjoint triangles. For each lifted state, its neighbours lie in distinct neighbouring orbits, so the quotient map is a **six-sheeted graph covering**, rather than simply an arbitrary surjective graph homomorphism. Its nontrivial connected cover is not determined by the small quotient's abstract graph alone.

**5. Distances and unrestricted comparison.** Each program runs exact breadth-first search from all \(60\) vertices in each component, obtaining eccentricity \(6\) for every state, hence radius and diameter \(6\). For the unrestricted comparison, the first program also switches every alternating two-colour component regardless of length. That second finite state graph has \(324\) edges, the same two parity components, and eccentricity \(4\) at every state. The checked state-edge certificate and program source are part of the package, with outputs recorded separately. These enumerations use complete finite search ranges and terminate normally.

## Relationship to prior work
McDonald–Mohar–Scheide prove Kempe equivalence for **four** edge colours on subcubic graphs, not the minimum three colours at issue here. Belcastro–Haas develop methods to count unrestricted three-edge-colouring Kempe classes and study their full transition graphs, including separate finite examples and general constructions. Their 2017 paper asks about component numbers and diameters. Goedgebeur–Östergård enumerate unrestricted Kempe-class counts for entire orders of cubic graphs, including all bipartite cubic graphs with eighteen vertices; that census may already determine the Pappus graph's **unrestricted** class count in its source data. Such class counts do not determine the length-six-restricted graph, its nine-cycle thickening, its exact degree profile or its metric. These broader notions and earlier counts are not presented as novel.

An earlier exact Pappus **three-edge-colouring quotient** computation determines the graph on colourings *modulo colour-name permutations*, allowing unrestricted alternating-cycle exchanges: it has \(20\) colour-orbits, \(36\) edges and graph structure \(2(K_1\vee(3K_3))\). It also establishes a matching-sign parity obstruction on those orbits. The present computation reproduces the same quotient even when the allowed exchanges are restricted to hexagons; neither the quotient graph nor the existence of an equivalent parity obstruction is claimed as new. Crucially, the abstract ten-state quotient component leaves its six-sheeted labelled covering, legal-move fibres, component diameter and restricted-versus-unrestricted diameter **undetermined**. The explicit connected cover \(T(K_{3,3})\), the exact \(216\) labelled hexagon moves, and local parity sufficiency are the precise additional claims. A different Pappus result classifies six-cycle switches of **individual perfect matchings**, not compatible triples of perfect matchings, and likewise does not imply the labelled colouring graph's sixfold covering or metric.

## Limitations
This theorem concerns only the specified eighteen-vertex Pappus graph, the fixed three-colour palette, and the exact six-cycle move. It gives no family-wide theorem for larger biaffine incidence structures and no claim about vertex recolouring. The proof is a reproducible complete finite enumeration, not a short closed-form derivation of all adjacency counts. Published aggregate Kempe censuses and their supplementary records may contain more Pappus-specific information than the inspected article text, and unindexed or inaccessible equivalent classifications cannot be excluded. The novelty assessment is a literature comparison rather than an independent priority certificate. No independent external audit has been performed.
