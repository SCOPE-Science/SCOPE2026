# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The graph-edge commutator equation gives projective-line constancy on each connected active component. The conjugation map has rank exactly \(|N[U]|-c(U)\): the internal incidence blocks have rank \(|U|-c(U)\), and each neighboring inactive vertex contributes one independent crossing-edge column. Orbit-stabilizer then supplies the exact central-coordinate quotient. Special cases (empty support, isolates, disconnected graphs, characteristic two) are covered by the same argument. An independent linear-algebra checker and an independent full group-conjugation checker match the formulas on the finite domains stated in RESULT.md.

## Originality

**PASS within the checked record.** Rossmann's graphical-group classification concerns ordinary single-element conjugacy and character enumeration, and does not state or imply directly the higher commuting-tuple orbit-size distribution. Kaur–Prajapati–Prasad and Sharma–Singh introduce higher simultaneous conjugacy and centralizer methods, without an all-graph support-component orbit-size polynomial. Related graphical-group character-degree and commuting-graph studies examine different invariants. The example's generic ordinary/higher inequality phenomenon is explicitly credited to earlier work. Searches for projective component colorings, joint centralizers, equivalent finite sums, and exact orbit-size tables found no covering claim in the checked sources. A possible unindexed equivalent remains the substantive originality risk.

## Value

**PASS.** This exact complete invariant is defined naturally for every finite graphical group, every field, and every tuple arity, and it records orbit sizes rather than only total orbit numbers. It unifies group-theoretic simultaneous conjugacy with induced-support graph geometry. The two four-vertex graphs give a structural example of higher orbit data distinguishing groups despite identical ordinary class numbers and equal orders. This is not a mere isolated table or change of notation.

## Scope and risks

General noncommuting tuples are excluded. The direct subset-sum formula need not be computationally efficient for large graphs. The \(k=1\) specialization is prior work. No claim of exhaustiveness over every publication is made.
