# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The necessity uses two finite permutation-cycle arguments in the Green \(\mathcal J\)-order and the fact that \(s^2\mathrel{\mathcal J}s\) forces membership in a maximal subgroup of a finite semigroup. The latter is justified via the regular principal factor and its Rees sandwich entry. For even \(k\), the CRT constructs one universal exponent \(a\) whose power map and derived power map are each bijective on all maximal groups. Clifford reduction uses identification of \(\mathcal J\)-classes with constituent groups. For odd \(k\), finite abelian 2-primary sufficiency invokes the classical Hall–Paige theorem and takes a preimage under the \(k\)-th power permutation; obstruction uses the product of group elements. The calculations were sanity-checked in 42 exhaustive semigroup/exponent combinations. The proof relies on standard but nontrivial finite principal-factor and group complete-mapping results; these dependencies are cited.

## Originality — PASS, with bibliographic residual risk
The relevant primary full text, Araújo et al. arXiv:2608.25092, states Definition 15.1 and the open Problem 15.8 and proves a separate ordinary mapping criterion for inverse semigroups (Theorem 10.1) and a restriction for involutive ordinary complete maps (Theorem 11.2). Neither assertion covers the full even-power classification or the stronger groupwise obstruction at powers at least two. The secondary Clifford semigroup reference concerns a categorical equivalence, not complete maps. Targeted public-literature and semantic mathematical-index searches found no theorem entailing the present claims. Index incompleteness and distinct uses of “k-complete” in group-sequencing literature remain risks, not proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result directly addresses an explicitly listed research problem for finite semigroups and inverse semigroups. It establishes a sharp global if-and-only-if criterion for every even higher power, supplies an explicit witness valid for any completely regular finite semigroup, explains a qualitative discontinuity from the classical \(k=1\) inverse-semigroup theory, and obtains a full criterion for finite inverse semigroups with abelian maximal groups at all powers \(k\ge2\). The general finite-semigroup theorem and Clifford reduction are structural results rather than isolated parameter calculations.
