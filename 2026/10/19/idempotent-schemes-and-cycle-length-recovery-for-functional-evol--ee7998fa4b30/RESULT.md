# Idempotent schemes and cycle-length recovery for functional evolution algebras

## Finding

Fix any nonempty finite set \(V\), an endofunction \(f:V\to V\), and a field \(k\). Give the vector space with basis \(\{e_v:v\in V\}\) the evolution-algebra multiplication \(e_ve_w=0\) for \(v\ne w\), and \(e_v^2=e_{f(v)}\). Suppose the functional digraph has \(s\) directed cycles of lengths \(L_1,\ldots,L_s\), counting each cycle once and ignoring trees feeding it.

**Theorem (schemes, counts, and reconstruction).** The affine scheme \(Z_f\) of elements satisfying \(x^2=x\) admits, on selecting one vertex per directed cycle, an isomorphism
\[
 Z_f\ \cong\ \prod_{i=1}^{s}\operatorname{Spec}
 k[T_i]/(T_i^{2^{L_i}}-T_i).
\]
The isomorphism holds scheme-theoretically, even if the coordinate ring is nonreduced; the off-cycle coordinate functions vanish identically. The scheme has dimension zero and degree \(2^{\sum_i L_i}\). It is geometrically reduced precisely when the characteristic of \(k\) does not divide any integer \(2^{L_i}-1\) (in characteristic zero, this condition is automatic). Every finite field of cardinality \(q\) satisfies
\[
 \#Z_f(\mathbb F_q)=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\left(1+\gcd(2^{L_i}-1,q-1)\right).
\]
In particular, in characteristic two,
\[
 \#Z_f(\mathbb F_{2^m})=2^{F(m)},\qquad F(m)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\gcd(L_i,m).
\]
For known \(n=|V|\), the \(n\) integers \(\#Z_f(\mathbb F_{2^m})\), \(1\le m\le n\), determine the entire multiset of directed-cycle lengths, but cannot in general determine the remaining functional digraph, even up to algebra isomorphism.

## Assumptions and scope

An **element-idempotent** is an element \(x\) with \(x^2=x\); this is distinct from the common terminology calling an evolution algebra *idempotent* when \(E^2=E\). The coordinate ring of \(Z_f\) is the quotient of \(k[X_v:v\in V]\) by the full ideal generated by the coordinate equations for \(x^2-x\), not merely the radical of that ideal. Loops are cycles of length one. All directed cycles are disjoint, and every other vertex reaches exactly one of them under iteration of \(f\). No assumption of invertibility of the structure matrix is needed. The result concerns the specified natural basis and its functional digraph.

## Proof

**1. Scheme-theoretic elimination.** Write \(x=\sum_{v\in V}X_ve_v\). Since mixed basis products vanish, the coefficient of \(e_w\) in \(x^2-x\) is
\[
 \sum_{f(v)=w}X_v^2-X_w.
\]
A vertex without incoming arrows therefore has \(X_w=0\) in the quotient coordinate ring, as an equality of elements, not merely at field-valued points. Repeatedly remove all vertices not on directed cycles in order of their distance from the incoming leaves. The equation at each removed vertex reduces to \(X_w=0\), since every incoming vertex has previously been removed. All off-cycle variables thus vanish as elements of the quotient coordinate ring. A cycle vertex has exactly one incoming arrow from the same cycle, so its residual equation is \(X_{v_{j+1}}=X_{v_j}^2\). Traversing a length-\(L\) cycle expresses all \(L\) variables as \(T,T^2,T^4,\ldots,T^{2^{L-1}}\), leaving exactly the single relation \(T^{2^L}=T\). Eliminations use monic linear substitutions and hence give mutually inverse quotient-ring isomorphisms. Different cycles have disjoint variables; this proves the displayed product of schemes and the total degree. No radical-taking occurs.

**2. Separability and multiplicities.** For a length-\(L\) factor set \(N=2^L-1\); its defining polynomial is \(T(T^N-1)\). Zero is a simple root in every characteristic. In positive characteristic \(p\), write \(N=p^aM\) with \(p\nmid M\). Then \(T^N-1=(T^M-1)^{p^a}\), so each nonzero geometric root has multiplicity exactly \(p^a\). In characteristic zero every root is simple. The product is geometrically reduced if and only if each \(p^a=1\), exactly as claimed. In characteristic two, \(N\) is odd and all factors are finite étale.

**3. Points over finite fields.** For \(q\) a prime power, the multiplicative group \(\mathbb F_q^\times\) is cyclic of order \(q-1\). The equation \(T^{2^L}=T\) has zero plus precisely \(\gcd(2^L-1,q-1)\) nonzero solutions. Independent cycles multiply their counts. For \(q=2^m\), the classical identity \(\gcd(2^L-1,2^m-1)=2^{\gcd(L,m)}-1\) yields the stated power of two. In fact the cycle factor \(\mathbb F_{2^L}\) embeds as the geometric solution space inside the algebraic closure, and the coordinate action of the arithmetic Frobenius \(z\mapsto z^2\) acts linearly on this \(\mathbb F_2\)-space. By a normal basis, it is conjugate to a single cyclic permutation of length \(L\). Thus the complete finite étale idempotent locus has an additive Frobenius permutation module with one cycle of length \(L_i\) for each original directed cycle.

**4. Explicit inversion at a finite cutoff.** Suppose \(n=|V|\), so each \(L_i\le n\). Define the integer
\[
 F(m)=\log_2\#Z_f(\mathbb F_{2^m}),\qquad 1\le m\le n.
\]
For a positive integer \(d\le n\), let \(B_d\) denote the number of directed cycles whose length is divisible by \(d\). Euler's divisor identity \(\sum_{d\mid h}\varphi(d)=h\) gives
\[
 F(m)=\sum_i\gcd(L_i,m)=\sum_{d\mid m}\varphi(d)B_d.
\]
Ordinary divisor Möbius inversion therefore gives
\[
 B_d=\frac{1}{\varphi(d)}\sum_{a\mid d}\mu(d/a)F(a),\qquad 1\le d\le n.
\]
Let \(c_\ell\) be the number of original directed cycles of length exactly \(\ell\). Because \(B_d=\sum_{j\ge1}c_{dj}\), finite multiple Möbius inversion gives
\[
 c_\ell=\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor n/\ell\rfloor}\mu(j)B_{\ell j},\qquad 1\le\ell\le n.
\]
Hence the first \(n\) extension-field counts recover all \(c_\ell\) and therefore the multiset of cycle lengths. We make no claim that the stated cutoff is minimal for every \(n\).

**5. Failure to reconstruct attached trees.** On \(V=\{0,1,2\}\), take
\[
 f_1=(0,0,0),\qquad f_2=(0,0,1),
\]
where the triples list the three image values. Each has precisely one fixed-point cycle, namely \(0\); their idempotent schemes and all finite-field point counts consequently agree, and each characteristic-two extension has exactly two idempotents. But the square subalgebra \(E_{f_i}^2\) is spanned by \(\{e_{f_i(v)}:v\in V\}\), so it has dimension \(|\operatorname{im}f_i|\), equal to \(1\) and \(2\) respectively. This dimension is preserved under algebra isomorphism. Thus even the entire idempotent scheme over all fields does not recover the evolution algebra or its attached incoming trees. \(\square\)

## Verification

The accompanying standalone file `verify.py` constructs literal finite-field arithmetic and literally multiplies general elements to test \(x^2=x\). It checks all endofunctions up to four vertices over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\), and all endofunctions up to three vertices over \(\mathbb F_4\), \(\mathbb F_8\), \(\mathbb F_5\), and \(\mathbb F_9\), totaling 704 graph-field pairs, plus 32 additional five-vertex cases over \(\mathbb F_4\). It checks the cycle-reconstruction formulas in those cases, verifies the two nonisomorphic three-vertex examples, and tests 40 polynomial squarefreeness conditions when SymPy is installed. Successful execution emits `VERIFY_OK_FUNCTIONAL_EVOLUTION_IDEMPOTENTS`. These finite tests support the uniform proof but are not substituted for it; in particular the scheme-isomorphism proof is algebraic rather than exhaustive computational checking.

## Relationship to prior work

Khudoyberdiyev–Omirov–Qaralleh [1] classify or decompose permutation-type evolution algebras according to directed cycles; cycle decomposition itself is **prior work**, not the new claim. Elduque–Labra [2] formalize the evolution-algebra digraph and relate cycles to nilpotency, but do not give the stated full element-idempotent schemes or finite-field reconstruction. Narkuziyev [3] studies absolute nilpotents and idempotents in evolution algebras corresponding to two permutations, with explicit small-dimensional real cases; the displayed all-characteristic endofunction scheme and extension-field inversion are not results identified there. General idempotent-scheme observations for perfect evolution algebras appear in the later survey [4]; the finite étale behavior for characteristic two is compatible with that general perspective, but neither a special-case separability corollary nor the elementary factorization on its own constitutes the asserted distinguishing contribution. The distinguishing conjunction is a scheme-theoretic reduction including nonperfect attached trees, exact all-field counts, the finite-cutoff recovery of the recurrent cycle multiset and its sharp information-loss example. Broad monomial dynamical-systems literature could contain an equivalent fixed-point/cycle formula under different terminology, and that is a live priority risk.

## Limitations

This result applies to unweighted functional evolution algebras with exactly one outgoing structure coefficient of value one per basis vector. It does not classify weighted or multi-arrow evolution algebras. The reconstruction recovers the multiset of lengths of recurrent directed cycles, not their labeling, ordering, or in-arborescences. It neither proves a minimal observation cutoff nor an equivalence between idempotent schemes and evolution-algebra isomorphism classes. No independent mathematical audit has been performed. A result in older finite polynomial dynamical-systems literature not located in the present comparisons might supersede the arithmetic-reconstruction portion.
