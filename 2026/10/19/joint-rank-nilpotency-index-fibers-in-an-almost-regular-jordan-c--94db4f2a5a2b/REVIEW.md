# Scientific review of the joint rank–index census

## Correctness — PASS

The centralizer identification \(\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb F_q[t]}(\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^m)\oplus\mathbb F_q)\) supplies all parameters uniquely. Nilpotence is exactly the vanishing of the two scalar semisimple components. Powers satisfy \(X^2=(F^2+bc\,t^{m-1})\) on the long block and \(X^j=F^j\) there for all \(j\ge3\), with all other blocks zero. The independent rank calculation has one exceptional socle case; the power calculation has one odd-middle-valuation cancellation. The case partition exhausts all parameters and gives the displayed polynomial. Exhaustive small-field checker results provide cross-checks but are not used to infer universality.

## Originality — PASS, with a substantive literature risk

Oblak (arXiv:math/0701561, Theorem 16) established maximal possible indices; Britnell–Wildon (arXiv:1001.0811, Proposition 4.4) established exactly which nilpotent Jordan classes commute with the almost-regular class, using the same centralizer coordinates and rank sequences. These support and maximal-index conclusions are explicitly **not** claimed as new. Neither inspected result states the finite-field count of *individual matrices* for every simultaneous ordinary-rank and nilpotency-index fiber. Sharma (arXiv:1409.2698) concerns simultaneous-conjugacy orbit counts for dimensions at most four, not the claimed all-size element census. Iarrobino et al. (arXiv:1409.2192) study generic partitions and partition fibers, not arithmetic multiplicities. The square-zero slice is a proper part of the statement and does not imply the higher-index fibers. Nonetheless, the closeness of the explicit coordinates in Britnell–Wildon is a real priority risk; an equivalent arithmetic refinement may already be implicit or published under Hall-polynomial terminology.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact joint distribution, across unbounded block sizes and all finite fields, is a natural refinement of the central subject of commuting nilpotent matrix theory. It quantifies the correlation between a matrix's rank and its vanishing-power index, identifies a specific parity-dependent cancellation fiber, exhibits genuine gaps in the index distribution, and proves the exact dimension-independent \(1-q^{-1}\) maximal-index proportion for every \(m\ge3\). The result does not assert a solution to arbitrary commuting-pair classification or a stronger support theorem than existing Jordan-type work.

## Limitations

Restricted to the fixed block partition \((m,1)\). The novelty assessment has residual bibliographic uncertainty, especially given the elementary centralizer model in the literature. General-class support and maximal-index existence are acknowledged as known. Finite computations are supporting evidence only.
