# Review of the paperfolding minimal-absent-factor theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof explicitly fixes the regular paperfolding normalization, defines minimal absence through both length-one-shorter factors, and reduces missing words to missing extension pairs at bispecial factors. The parity-track argument proves unique occurrence parity for every factor of length at least eight; a two-sided extension-preserving lift bijects bispecial factors at odd length \(2m+1\) and length \(m\) for every \(m\ge8\). The initial extension profiles for lengths \(7\) through \(16\) are derived by exhaustive membership tests in factor sets of lengths through \(18\), whose completeness follows from Allouche's independently published \(4n\) theorem. Exactly two of four bispecial factors at each base scale have two missing extensions. Induction yields four absent words at each length \(2^j+1\), \(j\ge3\), and none elsewhere above length eight. The actual standalone verifier passes all stated checks. Infinite correctness depends on the stated mathematical arguments and known complexity theorem, not on finite testing alone.

**Originality — PASS against inspected evidence, with residual risk.** Allouche (1992) proves ordinary factor complexity but not the present count of minimally forbidden extensions; its exact count \(4n\) alone cannot distinguish four missing pairs at selected lengths from other extension profiles. The full accessible manuscript of Cassaigne et al. (arXiv:1402.5843) treats cyclic complexity of paperfolding rather than its minimal-absent-factor length distribution. Search comparisons against mathematical records for aliases (minimal forbidden words, antidictionary, bispecial extensions, forbidden factors, paperfolding), known published claims, and accessible historical results found no equivalent all-length count. Allouche and Bousquet-Mélou (1994) give canonical positions for factors and are especially relevant; their abstract was reviewed but the full text was not obtained, leaving a real but nondecisive coverage risk. **Mathematical value — PASS.** The ordinary factor-complexity law \(4n\) has zero second difference, which permits cancellation of strong and weak bispecial contributions and does **not** determine the number of missing extensions. The present theorem resolves that finer invariant by showing a highly structured lacunary support: among all sufficiently long lengths, only \(2^j+1\) support any minimal forbidden factors and each contributes precisely four. The exact cumulative antidictionary growth is logarithmic, a substantive invariant of an automatic word's full factor language. This structural formula is neither an arbitrary finite slice nor a direct textbook consequence of the known factor count.
