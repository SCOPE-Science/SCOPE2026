# Scientific assessment: signed-equicorrelation cyclic Kaczmarz sweep law

**Correctness — PASS.** The real, positive Perron eigenpair of the exact Gauss--Seidel sweep is reconstructed from a unique positive scalar polynomial root; the square-root Gram representation establishes similarity to cyclic Kaczmarz. The finite root is converted to an interior exponential quadrature equation. Uniform convergence of that quadrature yields the strictly positive Lambert-branch limit. The trapezoidal-error formula and a uniform Taylor expansion yield the claimed first finite-size correction and the sharp near-simplex coefficients. The argument includes every integer \(n\ge2\), excludes the singular boundary \(\alpha=1\), and distinguishes asymptotic spectral rate from the possibly larger induced one-sweep norm. The included program completed 307 exact rational, spectral and asymptotic checks; infinity statements rest on proof, not extrapolation.

**Value — PASS.** This classification answers a natural exact-rate question on nearly regular-simplex measurement frames, where all pairwise row correlations shrink with dimension even while the smallest Gram eigenvalue stays at \(1-\alpha\). It reveals a dimension-independent limiting spectral factor, an explicit accuracy correction for finite row count, and a sharp dimension-uniform transition as the simplex loses rank. The limiting Lambert-law interpolation is a useful benchmark for cyclic row-order convergence and clarifies a geometric regime not described by the classical positive-equicorrelation lower-bound example; neither a generic method guarantee nor a claim of broader solver superiority is made.
