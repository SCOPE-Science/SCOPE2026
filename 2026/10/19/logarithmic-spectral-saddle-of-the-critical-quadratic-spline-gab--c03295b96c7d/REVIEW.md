# Scientific review: quadratic-spline Gabor spectral law

## Correctness
**PASS.** The proof specifies the unit-integral centered order-three B-spline and the unitary critical Zak transform. Direct three-term Laurent expansion gives the exact multiplier \(H=1-V+V^2T^2/4\) on the unit torus. Independent arcsine and inverse-square-root variable laws yield both the cumulative distribution and its density. All relevant critical levels are classified: the nondegenerate minimum at zero, the unique interior Morse saddle at \(1/4\), and the maximum frequency circle at one. Local area calculations establish the constants \(1/2\), \(1/\pi\), and \(1/\pi\) at their respective edges; the finite checker corroborates exact identities and sharply approaches the saddle slope. The resolvent law follows from a bounded lower-edge density remainder. No enumeration or numeric test is substituted for the proof.

## Originality
**PASS, with publication-priority uncertainty.** Kloos--Stöckler (arXiv:1311.7359v1), inspected throughout the relevant Gabor/Zak-zero and spline sections, establish unique Zak zeros and frame conditions, not a normalized spectral distribution. Kloos (arXiv:1411.1539v1) concerns Zak zeros, not spectral density. Relevant later order-two tent-spline records determine oversampled frame bounds rather than the order-three critical spectral measure. The accessible abstract of Hasannasab--Lemvig (arXiv:2609.13116v1) concerns Zak zeros and frame membership; its unavailable full text leaves a real overlap risk. Searches for equivalent density-of-states, critical multiplier and logarithmic saddle formulations did not locate a covering theorem. Search absence is not a proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The spectral distribution of a natural critical-density time-frequency operator is a meaningful conditioning invariant, strictly finer than its zero set and essential spectral range. The exact, uniquely located logarithmic van Hove saddle, including coefficient and the competing nonlogarithmic edges, reveals a topology-driven singularity invisible to qualitative frame membership; the explicit CDF and regularized inverse mean support reproducible quantitative comparison.

## Limitations
This is a one-window, one-lattice result using the precise unit-integral spline normalization. The spectral measure is the normalized Zak fiber mean, not a Hilbert-space trace. A more general unindexed result or inaccessible B-spline paper may subsume it; independent novelty and correctness review have not been performed.
