# Scientific assessment: marked-Poisson law for wrong-polarity paint islands

**Correctness — PASS.** The central claim specifies the actual sign-flip rate \(f\), not the motivating paper's doubled tumble rate. A centered finite-variance two-tumble skeleton proves the necessary almost-sure spatial hitting. Solving the coupled backward equations gives the exact orientation hitting kernel and spatial exponential-run rates. Independent exponential coupling then proves convergence of every bounded collection of island starts and rescaled widths to a marked Poisson point process; tightness of the local island count closes the infinite-point argument. The numerical boundary-value calculation corroborates the kernel but is not used as a proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual literature risk.** The source *Run, Tumble and Paint* (arXiv:2603.24277v1), inspected through its model, complete observables, conclusion and appendices, determines one-point first-passage paint probabilities and asymptotic coverage, while only qualitatively predicting islands. Classical Markov-modulated Brownian ladder-process results already imply spatial Markovianity in principle. The assessed specific assertion is the **marked small-thermal-noise island point-process limit with explicit intensity and exponential width normalization**, not the generic Markov property, nor the already known one-point color fraction. Claim-focused searches identified no explicit same-limit theorem; general ladder and rare-state limits remain an implicit-coverage risk.

**Value — PASS.** The main source motivates colored deposition as a physical spatial observable, specifically noting wrong-polarity islands without specifying their law. The result converts those qualitative islands into an exact count distribution and size spectrum in a natural singular limit. The limits distinguish the number of islands, which remains order one on fixed positive intervals, from their total colored length, which vanishes proportionally to the diffusivity. This is a structural, experimentally interpretable quantity rather than a re-expression of one-point coverage.

The important limitations are fixed positive propulsion and flipping rates, infinitely completed first-visit painting, and the absence of claims about interacting tracers or joint time-dependent deposition.
