# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** For rank-one square-zero operators, the equality of the two products can only hold when both products vanish: otherwise rank-one factor equality implies proportional operators, contradicting square-zero. This reduces a clique to a unique image-span subspace lying in every kernel. The same construction proves all canonical cliques are maximal, with distinct recoverable labels. The finite-field line counts, middle-dimension maximization, subspace Möbius inversion and falling-factorial tuple counts then establish the entire size distribution. Direct matrix enumeration and independent Bron–Kerbosch search agree in five dimension–field cases; finite checks corroborate and do not replace the proof.

**Originality — PASS within examined coverage.** The nearest relevant primary papers treat the commuting graph of full two-by-two triangular rings (arXiv:2402.12619v1), automorphisms of graphs of rank-one *upper-triangular* matrices (Ou–Zhong, doi:10.13001/1081-3810.3064), and existence of commuting representatives of nilpotent similarity classes (Britnell–Wildon, arXiv:1001.0811v1). Their inspected definitions and main results do not imply the subspace-indexed maximal-clique classification for the unrestricted rank-one square-zero conjugacy class in every dimension, nor its all-clique formula. The precise point of novelty is not the familiar Gaussian-subspace count or the low-dimensional commuting graph. Search noncoverage does not establish absolute novelty; a hidden equivalent in older incidence-algebra or matrix-semigroup terminology is a residual risk.

**Value — PASS.** Commuting graphs of matrix classes are natural algebraic graph invariants. The classification supplies a complete all-dimensional description of the maximal cliques, their incidence multiplicities, the sharp clique number, and, importantly, the full clique polynomial despite extensive overlap between maximal cliques. It makes the matrix–subspace incidence structure quantitatively explicit, rather than merely obtaining an isolated small-field census.

**Scope and limitations.** The classification applies to nonzero rank-one square-zero matrices only and makes no statement about arbitrary nilpotents. Computations were performed over two prime fields, while the field-uniform theorem is proved algebraically. Independent review remains outstanding.
