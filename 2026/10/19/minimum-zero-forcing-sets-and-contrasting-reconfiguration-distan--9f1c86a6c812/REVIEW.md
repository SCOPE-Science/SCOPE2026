# Scientific scope of the amended finding

## Correctness

The exact minimum-state and move enumerations and shortest-path traversals reproduce a connected jump graph with 97,524 edges and eccentricity seven at every vertex. As a separate check of the seed census, a nonempty set of white vertices is a fort if no outside vertex has exactly one neighbor in it. A seed fails precisely when its white complement contains a fort: a fort cannot lose its first vertex to a force, and a stalled nonempty white set is itself a fort. Enumerating forts and their supersets reproduces the state count without simulating the forcing process. The verified incidence action gives 35 orbits; all jump and slide distance distributions also follow by breadth-first searches weighted by those orbit sizes.

## Originality and comparison

The general jump rule is the one in Geneson, Haas and Hogben, arXiv:2009.00220. An exact indexed comparison on the Pappus and Desargues graphs gives the seed minimum and multiplicity and the full Pappus slide distances; these data are background in the amended finding. The read classification does not provide the 97,524 jump edges or the uniform jump eccentricity of seven. This distinction credits the available exact coverage and does not infer publication priority. The frozen assessment concerned the original broader scope, not this narrower statement.

## Value and limits

The exact eccentricity census determines the largest number of minimum-preserving unrestricted replacements anyone needs from each starting seed. On this standard cubic incidence graph it gives a quantitative comparison to the local-move geometry, not merely a count of optimal configurations. The calculation is for this graph and these move rules, not for general zero-forcing reconfiguration. No claim is made about unread or unindexed sources.
