# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The unperturbed circulant spectrum gives the monic factor 2[T_N(E/2)−C], and the (0,0) cofactor is the path polynomial U_(N−1)(E/2), since the reciprocal hoppings gauge to unit hoppings on the path. This gives the claimed impurity sign and normalization. On each full negative-sine lobe write the normalized secular ratio as (1−T_N(x)/C)/S(x), with x=cos θ and S=−U_(N−1)>0. Its critical-point numerator converges with derivatives to −S′. That limit has exactly one simple zero in the lobe and none at the endpoints by interlacing of simple polynomial roots, so for fixed N and large C there is one nondegenerate minimum and no extra critical points. A lobe contributes two real roots at and above its minimum, counting the double root, and none below. For E=2 cosh ξ the positive-V equation has exactly one root: for ξ>g, (cosh(Nξ)−C)/sinh(Nξ) is positive and increasing, as is the remaining sinh ξ factor. For odd N there is no root at or below −2. For even N, −2 is crossed at V=2(C−1)/N: the exterior negative-energy polynomial increases strictly in ξ for E=−2 cosh ξ, and on the terminal interior lobe θ=π−t the ratio 2(C−cos(Nt))sin(t)/sin(Nt) increases strictly for 0<t<π/N (the Chebyshev denominator sin(Nt)/sin(t) decreases from N to 0). This accounts for exactly one additional real root without double counting −2. Thus the maximum full-lobe minimum is the eventual threshold for large C. The bounds 2(C−1)/M_j≤V_j≤2(C+1)/M_j give the fixed-N prefactor. Every noncentral negative antinode has q>1, while the central negative antinode exists precisely for N≡3 mod 4 and has maximum q=1. In that lobe the analytic minimizer of (1−t sin(Nδ))cos δ/cos(Nδ), t=1/C, is odd in t; its slope is N/(N²−1). Its minimum is even in t with quadratic coefficient −N²/[2(N²−1)], giving the C^(-1) correction and C^(-3) remainder after multiplying by 2C. The cutoff deficit follows by expanding 2 sqrt(C²−1). For the separate large-N limit, write each lobe as cos u / cos((D+u)/N), with D the nonnegative scaled distance of its antinode from π/2. Maximization can be restricted to u≥0 by reflection within the lobe, and the maximum increases with D. The nearest distances are 0, π/2, π in the listed classes. The stationarity equation tan u=tan((d_N+u)/N)/N gives u=d_N/N²+O(N^(-4)) and the claimed reciprocal expansion. Values at N=4,5 agree with the extrema of U_3 and U_4.

## originality

PASS

Liu et al. already give the same secular equation, an appropriate sufficient cutoff and the realification phenomenon. The supported distinct results are the fixed-size strong-asymmetry prefactor, its arithmetic classification, and the two different asymptotic refinements. The closest inspected primary passage does not state these, and the sufficient bound and its large-system approximation cannot replace them.

## value

PASS

The previous sufficient cutoff leaves open whether it is a quantitatively useful stand-in for the true last realification threshold on a finite ring. The result distinguishes an exponentially small absolute error for one residue class from a leading-order gap for the others at fixed N, gives an exact expression to compute the prefactor, and quantifies how these classes converge as N grows. This is a substantive sharpness criterion for the stated single-impurity model.
