# Motivic commuting-tuple profiles of \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\)-module extensions and a ten-dimensional nonreconstruction pair

## Finding
Let \(k\) be a field of characteristic zero or a field of characteristic \(p>\max(2,m_1,\ldots,m_t)\). Take nonnegative integers \(m_1,\ldots,m_t\), let \(V=\bigoplus_{j=1}^t\operatorname{Sym}^{m_j}(k^2)\), and equip \(L=\mathfrak{sl}_2(k)\ltimes V\) with the usual semidirect Lie bracket and abelian radical \(V\). Write \(d=\sum_j(m_j+1)\), \(e=\#\{j:m_j\text{ even}\}\), and \(t=\#\{j\}\). For every integer \(s\ge1\), the reduced algebraic variety of ordered, pairwise-commuting \(s\)-tuples has class
\[
[\mathcal C_s(L)]=\mathbb L^{ds}+(\mathbb L^s-1)\left(\mathbb L^{d+2+(s-1)e}+(\mathbb L+1)\mathbb L^{d+(s-1)t}\right)
\]
in the Grothendieck ring \(K_0(\mathrm{Var}_k)\), where \(\mathbb L=[\mathbb A^1_k]\). In particular, over \(k=\mathbb F_q\) in the permitted characteristics,
\[
\#\mathcal C_s(L)(\mathbb F_q)=q^{ds}+(q^s-1)\left(q^{d+2+(s-1)e}+(q+1)q^{d+(s-1)t}\right).
\]

An especially informative collision occurs for \(V_1=\operatorname{Sym}^1(k^2)\oplus\operatorname{Sym}^4(k^2)\) and \(V_2=\operatorname{Sym}^2(k^2)\oplus\operatorname{Sym}^3(k^2)\). Both have \((d,e,t)=(7,1,2)\). The Lie algebras \(L_1=\mathfrak{sl}_2\ltimes V_1\) and \(L_2=\mathfrak{sl}_2\ltimes V_2\) are ten-dimensional, perfect, centerless, and nonisomorphic, but their commuting-tuple varieties have the same Grothendieck class for **every** tuple length; over each finite field of characteristic at least five, they have the same Hasse--Weil zeta function in every arity.

## Assumptions and scope
The above identities are equalities of classes of **reduced varieties**, not assertions that their full bracket-equation schemes are reduced or pairwise isomorphic. The characteristic bound ensures that the standard \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) weight string in each \(\operatorname{Sym}^m\) is irreducible and its raising operators have one-dimensional kernel. The case \(t=0\) is included, with the empty sum convention \(d=e=t=0\). The formula works over any base field subject to this characteristic restriction, whether algebraically closed or not. The finite-field formula applies equally over every finite extension, since the same representation and field constraints remain valid.

## Proof
**1. The first-coordinate stratification.** Write \((x_i,v_i)\in \mathfrak{sl}_2(k)\oplus V\). Pairwise commutation is equivalent to
\[
[x_i,x_j]=0,\qquad \rho(x_i)v_j-\rho(x_j)v_i=0\quad (i<j).
\]
If every \(x_i=0\), the fiber is all of \(V^s\), an affine space of dimension \(ds\). On the complementary locus, all the \(x_i\) span a unique one-dimensional subspace of \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\): for every nonzero traceless \(2\times2\) matrix, its centralizer inside \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) is precisely its own span (the characteristic is not two). Thus the nonzero base tuple lies in the total space of \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(-1)^{\oplus s}\) with the zero section removed. On any locally closed subset of \(\mathbb P^2\), this base is a Zariski-locally trivial bundle with fiber \(\mathbb A^s\setminus\{0\}\), so its class is \(\mathbb L^s-1\) times the class of that subset.

**2. Kernel dimensions.** Identify \(x\in\mathfrak{sl}_2\) with \(\begin{psmallmatrix}a&c\\b&-a\end{psmallmatrix}\), so its nilpotency condition is \(a^2+bc=0\). The projectivized nonzero nilpotent locus is the split nonsingular conic \(\{a^2+bc=0\}\subset \mathbb P^2\), isomorphic over \(k\) to \(\mathbb P^1\); its class is \(\mathbb L+1\). Its complement has class \(\mathbb L^2\). For nonzero nilpotent \(x\), the action on each \(\operatorname{Sym}^m(k^2)\) has a single Jordan string and one-dimensional kernel (also for \(m=0\)). The assumption \(p>m\) guarantees that the string coefficients do not vanish. Consequently \(\dim\ker\rho(x)=t\). For nonnilpotent \(x\), after passing to a separable quadratic extension or an algebraic closure, \(x\) is semisimple with distinct eigenvalues \(\lambda,-\lambda\); its weights on \(\operatorname{Sym}^m\) are \(m\lambda,(m-2)\lambda,\ldots,-m\lambda\). The zero weight occurs exactly once for even \(m\) and never for odd \(m\), because \(p>m\). Descent of linear rank gives \(\dim\ker\rho(x)=e\) already over \(k\).

**3. Each radical fiber is an affine bundle.** Fix a nonzero coordinate tuple \((x_1,\ldots,x_s)=(\lambda_1x,\ldots,\lambda_sx)\) with some \(\lambda_i\ne0\), and put \(K=\ker\rho(x)\), \(r=\dim K\). The equations are equivalent to \(\lambda_iv_j-\lambda_jv_i\in K\) for every pair. Choose an index \(h\) with \(\lambda_h\ne0\). Modulo \(K\), all \(v_i\) are determined by the free choice of \(v_h\bmod K\), with \(sr\) further independent choices from \(K\). The resulting affine solution space has dimension \((d-r)+sr=d+(s-1)r\). On either projective stratum, the defining linear maps have constant rank, so their kernels form genuine Zariski-locally trivial vector bundles of the stated rank over the previously described base. Combining this with the base classes yields the displayed Grothendieck-ring formula.

**4. The nonreconstruction pair.** The two specified representations are seven-dimensional with \((e,t)=(1,2)\), hence give exactly identical formulas. The solvable radical of each associated Lie algebra is precisely its characteristic abelian ideal \(V_i\), as its quotient is \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\), and it is also the unique maximal solvable ideal. Each summand has positive degree, so \([\mathfrak{sl}_2,V_i]=V_i\); consequently \([L_i,L_i]=L_i\). The center is zero because \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) has zero center and neither representation has nonzero fixed vectors. An isomorphism \(L_1\cong L_2\) would induce an automorphism of the \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) quotient and identify the radical modules after twisting. Over an algebraic closure in the stated characteristics, all automorphisms of \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) are inner and the irreducible highest-weight multiplicities are invariant under twisting. The multisets \(\{1,4\}\) and \(\{2,3\}\) differ; thus the algebras cannot be isomorphic even after scalar extension.

For each finite field and every extension degree \(a\ge1\), replacing \(q\) by \(q^a\) produces the same counts for both algebras. Equality of Hasse--Weil zeta functions follows directly from their logarithmic definitions.

## Verification
Run the accompanying, standalone Python 3 script `verify.py`; it builds the three \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) action matrices directly, enumerates all projected \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\)-coordinate tuples for selected small fields, solves the full radical commutation equations by modular row reduction, and compares with the symbolic formula. Unlike a test of the stratification alone, this procedure reconstructs the bracket equations independently. The script also checks the module nullities and formula equality for the collision pair over field sizes \(5,7,11\) for tuple lengths one through five. Exact total counts include \(1,215,625\) for \(\operatorname{Sym}^3\) over \(\mathbb F_5\) with two tuples, and \(6,619,140,625\) for each collision algebra over \(\mathbb F_5\) with two tuples. These finite checks support, but do not substitute for, the general vector-bundle proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Panyushev's 2005 analysis of semidirect products of Lie algebras develops invariant-theoretic structure and discusses generic stabilizers. Its generic results do not supply the complete nilpotent-versus-nonnilpotent kernel stratification and the all-arity Grothendieck classes here. Work of Goodwin and Röhrle studies commuting varieties of nilradicals of Borel subalgebras; those are different Lie algebras and it does not yield the present perfect-centerless collision. Hobson and Shank study the invariants of the third symmetric power representation of a finite \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) group; they do not investigate commuting tuples in its Lie semidirect product. Fulman and Guralnick enumerate commuting pairs in classical matrix Lie algebras, not all arities for the nonreductive semidirect products here. The spectral decomposition of \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) and its symmetric powers is standard and is not claimed as new. The claimed contribution is the motivic all-arity formula and the exact reconstruction counterexample.

## Limitations
The proof computes reduced-variety classes; it does not establish defining-ideal radicality, isomorphism of distinct commuting varieties, or categorical equivalence of the underlying Lie algebras. The fields excluded by the small-characteristic assumptions require separate analysis. A Grothendieck-class equality is strictly weaker than an isomorphism of varieties, though it forces equal extension-field counts in the finite-field setting through the point-counting realization. An equivalent formula may exist in unlocated specialized literature; the identified published antecedents and searches do not prove global uniqueness. No independent audit has been performed.
