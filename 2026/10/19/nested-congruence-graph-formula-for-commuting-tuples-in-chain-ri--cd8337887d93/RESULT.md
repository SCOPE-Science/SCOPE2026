# Nested congruence graph formula for commuting tuples in chain-ring quiver algebras

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(R\) be a finite commutative chain ring with maximal ideal \((\pi)\), residue-field order \(q\), and nilpotency length \(L\ge 2\): \(\pi^L=0\) and \(\pi^{L-1}\ne0\). Let \(Q=(V,E)\) be any finite directed multigraph, allowing loops, parallel arrows, and isolated vertices, with \(n=|V|\ge1\) and \(m=|E|\). Form the unital arrow-square-zero quiver algebra
\[
A=RQ/\langle\text{all paths of length two}\rangle.
\]
For an integer \(t\ge1\), let \(C_t(A)\) count ordered \(t\)-tuples of pairwise commuting elements of \(A\). Set
\[
w_0=1,\qquad w_j=q^{(t-1)j}-q^{(t-1)(j-1)}\quad(1\le j\le L).
\]
For a level assignment \(\alpha:E\to\{0,\ldots,L\}\) and each \(1\le h\le L\), let \(E_h(\alpha)=\{e\in E:\alpha(e)\ge h\}\). Write \(c(E_h)\) for the number of connected components of the undirected spanning multigraph \((V,E_h)\), counting isolated vertices. Then
\[
\boxed{
C_t(A)=q^{Lm}
\sum_{\alpha\in\{0,\ldots,L\}^{E}}
\Bigl(\prod_{e\in E}w_{\alpha(e)}\Bigr)
q^{\,t\sum_{h=1}^{L}c(E_h(\alpha))}.
}
\]
This count depends only on \(q\), \(L\), \(t\), \(n\), and the cycle matroid of the underlying multigraph: in particular, it is unchanged by reversal of any arrow or replacement of \(R\) by a different finite chain ring with the same \(q,L\). Since \(|A|=q^{L(n+m)}\), the probability that \(t\) independent uniformly chosen elements commute pairwise equals \(C_t(A)/q^{Lt(n+m)}\).

For \(L=1\) the same expression is the ordinary finite-field Potts/Tutte expansion
\[
C_t(A)=q^m\sum_{S\subseteq E}(q^{t-1}-1)^{|S|}q^{t c(S)}.
\]
The new chain-ring assertion is the nested congruence formula for \(L\ge2\), not the elementary finite-field specialization.

For example, taking two vertices and one arrow with \(q=L=t=2\) gives \(C_2(A)=1408\), hence commuting probability \(11/32\). This is identical for the nonisomorphic coefficient rings \(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\) and \(\mathbb F_2[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\). For \(t=3\) the corresponding count is \(25600\); for a three-vertex path and \(t=2\) it is \(123904\) over either ring.

## Assumptions and scope

A finite chain ring is a finite commutative unital local ring whose ideals form a chain; its unique maximal ideal is generated by \(\pi\). The successive quotients \(\pi^jR/\pi^{j+1}R\) have order \(q\). Define \(\nu(a)=j\) for \(a\in\pi^jR\setminus\pi^{j+1}R\), and \(\nu(0)=L\). For vectors, \(\nu(d)=\min_i\nu(d_i)\). All quotients and equalities used below are ring-theoretic, with no assumption that \(R\) has equal characteristic or a canonical choice of residue digits.

The algebra has basis \(e_v\) for vertices and \(a_e\) for arrows. Its multiplication is \(e_ve_w=\delta_{vw}e_v\), \(e_u a_e=a_e\) if \(u\) is the source of \(e\) (zero otherwise), \(a_e e_v=a_e\) if \(v\) is its target (zero otherwise), and \(a_ea_f=0\). This convention covers loops, with both endpoint actions equal. If the quiver has no arrows the formula reduces to \(C_t(A)=q^{Lnt}\).

## Proof

Fix \(t\). Write the \(i\)-th algebra element of a tuple as \(x_i=\sum_{v\in V}d_v^{(i)}e_v+\sum_{e\in E}z_e^{(i)}a_e\). Organize the vertex coefficients as \(d_v\in R^t\) and each arrow coefficient as \(z_e\in R^t\). The coefficient of the arrow \(e:u\to v\) in \([x_i,x_j]\) equals
\[
(d_u^{(i)}-d_v^{(i)})z_e^{(j)}-(d_u^{(j)}-d_v^{(j)})z_e^{(i)}.
\]
Thus all tuple entries commute if and only if, independently for each arrow, every two-by-two minor of the two-row array with rows \(\delta_e=d_u-d_v\) and \(z_e\) vanishes. Vertex coefficients commute automatically, and products of two arrow terms vanish.

**One-arrow lemma.** For any \(\delta\in R^t\) with \(a=\nu(\delta)\), there are exactly \(q^{L+(t-1)a}\) vectors \(z\in R^t\) satisfying all those minors. If \(\delta=0\), there are \(q^{Lt}\) unrestricted vectors, equal to the expression with \(a=L\). Otherwise write \(\delta=\pi^a u\) with at least one unit coordinate of \(u\). Elementary invertible column operations over the local ring carry \(u\) to the first standard vector. They preserve the minors' common vanishing condition and biject the choices of \(z\). In these coordinates the constraints are \(\pi^a z_j=0\) for the \(t-1\) coordinates after the first. Each such coordinate has \(q^a\) choices, since \(\operatorname{Ann}(\pi^a)=\pi^{L-a}R\), and the first is arbitrary with \(q^L\) choices. For \(t=1\) there are no minors and the same formula applies.

Summing independently over arrow coefficients, with vertex vectors fixed, now gives the exact identity
\[
C_t(A)=q^{Lm}\sum_{d:V\to R^t}
\prod_{e:u\to v}q^{(t-1)\nu(d_u-d_v)}.
\tag{1}
\]
For every \(a\in\{0,\ldots,L\}\), the elementary telescoping identity
\[
q^{(t-1)a}=\sum_{j=0}^{a}w_j
\]
holds, also when \(t=1\) (then every positive-level weight is zero). Expand the edge products in (1). Choosing the term \(w_{\alpha(e)}\) requires \(\nu(d_u-d_v)\ge\alpha(e)\), meaning \(d_u\equiv d_v\pmod{\pi^{\alpha(e)}}\) coordinatewise.

For a fixed assignment \(\alpha\), count these constrained vertex vectors. For each level \(h=1,\ldots,L\), the new residue coordinate in \(\pi^{h-1}R/\pi^hR\), for each of the \(t\) tuple positions, must be constant across every connected component of \((V,E_h(\alpha))\); no other conditions arise. In the successive lifting filtration, exactly \(q^{tc(E_h(\alpha))}\) choices remain at level \(h\). Multiplying over levels gives
\[
\#\{d: \nu(d_u-d_v)\ge\alpha(e)\text{ for all }e\}
=q^{t\sum_{h=1}^{L}c(E_h(\alpha))}.
\]
Substituting into (1) proves the boxed identity.

For orientation invariance, reversing an arrow changes \(d_u-d_v\) only by a sign, which preserves valuation. For matroid invariance, the component number is \(c(S)=n-r_G(S)\), where \(r_G\) is the graphic-matroid rank on the labeled edge ground set, including loops and multiple edges. Every exponent and weight in the formula is therefore determined by that rank function, \(n\), \(q\), \(L\), and \(t\). Finite commutative chain rings sharing \(q,L\) have identical successive quotient sizes and annihilator cardinalities; no other ring structure was used. This proves the remaining assertions. For \(L=1\), each assignment is a subset \(S=\{e:\alpha(e)=1\}\), yielding the stated finite-field specialization.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` independently builds finite quotient rings \(\mathbb Z/p^L\mathbb Z\) and \(\mathbb F_p[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^L)\), constructs the quiver algebra's products from its basis-multiplication rule, and enumerates every ordered pair or triple of commuting elements via explicit multiplication. It compares those totals against the nested-subgraph formula. Its twelve successful configurations include length-two and length-three rings, reverse arrows, parallel arrows, a loop, a three-vertex path, \(t=1,2,3\), and \(q=3\). Run `python verify.py`; the final marker is `VERIFY_OK_NESTED_CHAIN_QUIVER`.

The exhaustive checks are finite examples, not a proof of the all-parameter result. The proof uses the one-arrow lemma and the filtration count above.

## Relationship to prior work

The rank-nullity expansion of the Tutte polynomial and its Potts-model specialization provide the standard combinatorial framework; they do not count commuting tuples in algebras over finite chain rings. Classical structure theory supplies the chain-ring ideal filtration. A prior treatment of generalized Kronecker path algebras over finite **fields** gives their centralizers and two-element commuting probabilities. Its two-vertex, one-level formulas are recovered as special cases here, not claimed new. Finite-field adjoint-rank and commuting-scheme formulas for arrow-square-zero quiver algebras likewise concern one residue level and do not yield the nested finite-chain-ring lifting formula.

The asserted extension is to **every** finite commutative chain ring, including mixed-characteristic rings, all directed multigraphs, and **every** tuple arity, with a graph-matroid formula independent of the coefficient ring's isomorphism class. No directly equivalent all-length statement was identified in the examined sources. This negative observation does not establish priority against all historical terminology (e.g. bilinear-map commuting probabilities or multilevel Potts partition functions).

## Limitations

The arrow ideal has square zero by construction. The theorem does not cover ordinary path algebras with nonzero paths of length two, non-chain coefficient rings, or noncommutative coefficient rings. It counts points over the **finite ring**, not scheme-theoretic components of an algebraic commuting variety. The matroid alone does not specify the number of vertices, so \(n\) is separately retained. Independent validation and exhaustive literature-priority confirmation are not claimed.
