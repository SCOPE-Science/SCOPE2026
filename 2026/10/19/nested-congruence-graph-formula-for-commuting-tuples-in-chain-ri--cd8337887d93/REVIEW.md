# Scientific review: nested congruence formula for commuting tuples

## Correctness

**PASS.** The algebra basis and multiplication force one independent minor-vanishing condition per arrow; no arrow-by-arrow independence is assumed before vertex coefficients are fixed. For each arrow, unimodular coordinate reduction of its vertex difference proves that the number of permitted arrow coefficient vectors is exactly \(q^{L+(t-1)a}\), including zero differences and \(t=1\). The telescoping expansion of this local factor converts valuation thresholds into nested edge subsets. Counting compatible lifts through the length-\(L\) ideal filtration yields the component exponent at every level. The proof treats loops, multiple edges, disconnections, and mixed-characteristic finite chain rings. Explicit literal-product checks cover twelve small configurations; these are corroboration only.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to historical-priority risk.** A finite-field Kronecker quiver treatment proves centralizer and two-element commuting probability results (see referenced complete proof); the new theorem encompasses its elementary one-level special case but addresses finite coefficient rings of every longer chain length and all tuple arities. Standard Tutte/Potts identities and chain-ring structure do not already state or imply the combined algebraic lifting theorem without the nontrivial one-arrow minor lemma. A prior finite-field quiver adjoint-rank description similarly does not include chain-ring lifting or universality across mixed- and equal-characteristic coefficient rings. Direct searches for finite-chain-ring quiver commuting tuples, nested Potts expressions, and graphic-matroid commuting probabilities found no covering theorem. Residual risk: a multilevel bilinear-map or p-adic Potts formulation may subsume the result under different terminology. An absence of search hits is not treated as a novelty proof.

## Value

**PASS.** The all-arity commuting-tuple distribution is a natural noncommutativity invariant of finite algebras. The formula converts a priori ring-dependent quadratic congruence constraints into a finite graphic-matroid invariant, identifies exactly which ring structure is invisible, and establishes the result simultaneously for all lengths and arities, not an isolated small-parameter enumeration. The equality of counts for \(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\) and \(\mathbb F_2[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\), despite differing characteristic, is an informative manifestation of the structural result. This is not asserted for general local rings.

## Limitations and closest source

The primary direct comparison is *Commutator fibers and commuting probabilities of finite Kronecker path algebras*, which is narrower both in coefficient ring and quiver family. The relevant standard background is Ellis-Monaghan and Merino, arXiv:0803.3079, and Wu–Yu–Lu, arXiv:1105.5179. The reduction needs the chain property and arrow-square-zero hypothesis. Earlier equivalent accounts under different nomenclature remain possible; independent review is outstanding.
