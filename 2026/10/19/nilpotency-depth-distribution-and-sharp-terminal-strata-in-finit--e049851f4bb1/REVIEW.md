# Scientific review: nilpotency-depth distribution in Munn algebras

## Correctness — PASS

The precise algebra is the rectangular matrix vector space with multiplication \(X\star Y=XPY\). After reduction of the rank-\(r\) sandwich matrix, a direct induction gives the four blocks of \(X^{\star j}\). The only nontrivial conditional count depends on the rank of \(A^{j-2}:\ker A^{j-1}\to k^r/\operatorname{im}A^{j-1}\), which is exactly the number of length-\(j-1\) Jordan blocks when \(A^j=0\). The number of rank-\(t\) images of \(B\) and the annihilation conditions on \(C\) then give the Gaussian formula, while the standard nilpotent centralizer orders give each similarity-orbit factor. The sharp terminal multiplicities follow by checking the unique regular-nilpotent block and two nonzero terminal vectors. All characteristic, zero-excess, and rank-one boundaries were checked. Eighteen brute-force finite-field configurations, including nonprime \(\mathbb F_4\), agree with the all-index formula. The proof is not inferred from those finite checks.

## Originality — PASS with stated residual risk

Rees and Munn constructions and Jordan centralizer orders are prior mathematical inputs. The inspected 2022 Drozd–Plakosh paper proves a Munn normal form and representation-type classification; its normal form alone does not enumerate powers of individual algebra elements with specified depth. The 2024 Yu–Luo–Li full text addresses generation by idempotents/commutators and zero-product conditions, not a nilpotency-depth enumeration. The nearby nilpotency-index literature on matrices over finite dual numbers concerns another multiplication and another deformation parameter. Searches for equivalent sandwich-algebra, Munn-algebra and Rees-algebra depth tables did not locate a covering formula; this negative search is not a uniqueness proof. An older monograph, other terminology, or a known general operator-counting formula could still subsume the result and is recorded as residual risk.

## Value — PASS

The distribution \(\#\{X:X^{\star j}=0\}\) is a natural and previously unaccounted-for elementwise invariant of finite Rees/Munn algebras. The result gives every depth for arbitrary dimensions, rank and field order, not a few isolated cases. The sharp cutoff changes with the two rectangular defects; the top stratum has an unexpectedly simple factorization, and the reduced nilpotent locus is geometrically irreducible. The formula is useful for comparing finite semigroup-algebra models and for verifying finite-algebra censuses. It is not claimed that a routine normal form, ordinary nilpotent-matrix total count, or orbit centralizer formula is itself new.
