# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The missing-colour lemma gives an exact, necessary-and-sufficient local transition rule: an edge is recolourable precisely when its two endpoints have the same unique missing colour. The enumeration is exhaustive because it explores all proper assignments of fifteen edges from a fixed four-colour palette. Two independent graph encodings, GP(5,2) and KG(5,2), produce matching total counts (49,920 states, 109,440 transitions, 960 singleton components, and two components of 24,480 states). The second program exhausts all 120 natural symbol permutations and 24 colour permutations, checking the symmetry action on component representatives and the entire frozen orbit. Recorded program outputs were reproduced by execution; the proof rests on the verified finite algorithms, not on logs alone.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The known colour count 49,920 (OEIS A159233) is excluded from the novelty claim. Osawa–Suzuki–Ito–Zhou (arXiv:1609.00109v1, 2016) establish reconfiguration complexity results, not this named graph's exact component classification. Ito–Kawamura–Zhou (2012) address trees. A later Bousquet habilitation manuscript exhibits a frozen Petersen **five**-edge-colouring, rather than the complete four-colour state-space decomposition. Full text of the 2016 paper's relevant model and theorem statements, the OEIS A159233 table, and the later thesis's relevant graph-recolouring pages were inspected. Targeted literature and mathematical-record searches found no stronger statement covering the two giant components and 960-state frozen orbit. An unindexed earlier census or unretrieved source remains a possible risk; failed searches do not prove uniqueness.

**Value — PASS.** The Petersen graph is the prototypical cubic class-two graph, and four is its minimum feasible edge palette. Its local-recolouring landscape is not merely disconnected by frozen configurations: two large, symmetry-conjugate irreducible classes remain separated even after all isolated colourings are removed. This matters for reachability and irreducibility of local recolouring dynamics and refines the published chromatic polynomial's state count into the complete component structure. The missing-colour lemma and symmetry action give an interpretable structural explanation rather than only a census.

**Limitations.** The result is finite and specific to the four-colour Petersen edge-colouring state graph. No independent external validation has occurred. Broader palettes, other snarks, and Kempe-chain moves are outside the claim.
