# Scientific review

**Correctness:** PASS. The all-base argument is split at base three: a length-four window sees at most one nonstandard boundary difference when the base is at least three; in base two, parity types and the absence of a constant three-letter factor exclude every possible reversed partner. For length three, any reversible factor is necessarily a palindrome, giving the claimed complexity equality. Independent finite checks completed successfully. No induction from finite enumeration is used.

**Originality:** PASS subject to the documented residual risk. Allouche–Shallit (2000) treat palindrome avoidance (a special case of ordinary reversal intersection), Starosta (2011) treats dihedral twisted reversals and ordinary factor complexity, and Rampersad–Shallit (2003) provide unrelated reversal-avoiding constructions. The closest direct comparison is Question 62 of Allouche et al. (2025), which states the identity for base two, modulus three and asks about larger moduli; the stated all-base theorem includes a universal sharp cutoff. Relevant indexed mathematical records inspected showed no implication subsuming this full assertion. Negative searches do not prove uniqueness; possible unindexed equivalent formulations remain a risk.

**Value:** PASS. Ordinary reversal-equivalence counting is a natural complexity invariant, and a recent explicit research question proposes precisely this family for generalization. The theorem replaces a suggested family of separate calculations with a uniform structural theorem across every base and nonbinary modulus, including periodic and aperiodic cases. It is not just a new finite sample or an arbitrary parameter slice.

The paper's contribution is ordinary reversal rather than twisted alphabet-complement reversal. No assertion is made for modulus one or two, or for independently audited certainty.
