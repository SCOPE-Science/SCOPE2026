# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The domain, normalization, and reciprocal lattice are explicit. Direct integration produces the cosine-quotient formula. At every off-axis integer reciprocal site the first nonzero Taylor term is a harmonic indefinite quadratic form with exact nonzero coefficient. The analytic Morse lemma gives exactly two orthogonal real-analytic zero arcs. Differentiation and removable-limit calculations prove all other reciprocal zeros simple. Lattice-covolume counting gives the one-third law. The independent defining-integral checker tests symbolic Hessians and representative sites, not the infinite theorem.

Primary source comparison distinguishes (i) known reciprocal-lattice zero locations and hexagonal Fourier interpolation (Li–Sun–Xu and Li–Xu), (ii) known general aperture integrals (Huang–Christian–McDonald), and (iii) first-zero convex-body optimization (Gómez-Serrano et al.), from the exact jets, right-angle nodal crossings and density formula. published-finding corpus published-record searches with aliases and stronger-coverage formulations did not identify an implication-equivalent result. ## Mathematical value — PASS
At Fourier nodes forced by spectral tiling, the striking distinction between order-one nodes and harmonic quadratic crossings governs the actual geometry of diffraction nodal curves. The exact half-angle law determines their orientation, and the one-third density classifies all such singularities on the natural sampling lattice. The result is neither a trivial restatement of lattice orthogonality nor a finite enumeration.
