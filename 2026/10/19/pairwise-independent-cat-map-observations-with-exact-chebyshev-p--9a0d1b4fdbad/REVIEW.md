# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof does not infer independence from decorrelation: full-rank integer torus homomorphisms establish pairwise Haar independence. Cayley–Hamilton and four conditional sign choices give the full predictive distribution. The unrestricted cubic Fourier resonance classification is proved using both real eigenprojections and invertibility modulo the trace; it includes repeated-time exclusions. This implies the exact variance and third-cumulant formulas at every length. Exact finite-frequency computations in `verify.py` corroborate but do not replace the unrestricted arguments.

## Originality — PASS (subject to bibliographic uncertainty)
Barash–Shchur (arXiv:physics/0409069, first public 2004-09-14) analyze known cat-map recurrence and higher-block correlations for finite-lattice binary observations, which are not claimed new here. Ayyer–Liverani–Stenlund (arXiv:0711.3818) give CLTs for randomly composed maps. Conze–Le Borgne–Roger (arXiv:1006.4051) develop Fourier frequency-separation and Gaussian limits, not this full exact triple law. Public-record searches of Chebyshev prediction, pairwise-independent cat-map observations, and trace-harmonic cubic sums did not yield an implication-equivalent theorem. Residual risk is earlier unlocated Fourier cumulant or deterministic toral sensor literature; searches do not prove uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The choice of the trace harmonic is forced by the map's minimal polynomial rather than selected as an arbitrary Fourier slice. Exact pairwise independence alongside deterministic third-order prediction is germane to statistical diagnostics of chaos and pseudorandom generators. The universal all-length cubic fluctuation formula supplies a finite-time statistic invisible to variance and the central-limit theorem. It is a structural classification across all positive-trace hyperbolic two-dimensional toral automorphisms, not a finite numerical curiosity.

## Limitations and audit state
The proof does not assert independence of nonconsecutive triples, any fourth-order moment, or an Edgeworth theorem. Comparison with every possible past treatment of these identities cannot be guaranteed from searchable literature. Independent mathematical audit remains outstanding.
