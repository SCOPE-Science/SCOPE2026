# Minimal normal-derivative repair for one equatorial spin-two sampling ring

## Finding

Fix a harmonic degree \(\ell\ge2\), spin \(s=\pm2\), and the \(N=2\ell+1\) equatorial point evaluations at longitudes \(2\pi j/N\), \(0\le j<N\). Coefficients are complex and unconstrained. Write \(B\) for the blind azimuthal orders of these evaluations, and \(d=|B|\). Then exactly \(d\) additional equatorial point readings of \(\partial_\theta f\), the normal derivative of the field component in the standard spherical spin frame, are necessary and sufficient for unique recovery. The lower bound holds even if the extra readings can be arbitrary complex-linear scalar measurements. The following explicit derivative longitudes attain it:

| Blind orders \(B\) | Derivative longitudes |
| --- | --- |
| \(\varnothing\) | none |
| \(\{0\}\) | \(0\) |
| \(\{-M,M\}\) | \(0,\pi/(2M)\) |
| \(\{-M,0,M\}\) | \(0,\pi/(2M),\pi/M\) |

These longitudes are not required to belong to the original sampling grid. The theorem is about exact recovery, not its conditioning.

## Equatorial evaluation data used by the repair

Use the orthonormal basis in ordinary area measure with convention \({}_sY_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)=(-1)^s\sqrt{(2\ell+1)/(4\pi)}\,d^\ell_{m,-s}(\theta)e^{im\phi}\). Set \(a_m={}_sY_{\ell m}(\pi/2,0)\), \(q=|m|\), and \(T=\ell(\ell+1)\). The point matrix has columns \(a_m(e^{2\pi imj/N})_{j=0}^{N-1}\), so the Gram eigenvalues indexed by order are \(N|a_m|^2\). For reference,
\[
|a_m|^2=\frac{N}{4\pi}\frac{(\ell-2)!(\ell-q)!}{(\ell+2)!(\ell+q)!}
\begin{cases}
(2m^2-T)^2\big[(\ell+q-1)!!/(\ell-q)!!\big]^2,&\ell+q\text{ even},\\
4m^2\big[(\ell+q)!!/(\ell-q-1)!!\big]^2,&\ell+q\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
The conventions are \((-1)!!=0!!=1\). The point data are achievable exactly when their discrete Fourier coefficients \(\widehat y_m=N^{-1}\sum_j y_je^{-2\pi imj/N}\) vanish on \(B\). All visible coefficients are \(c_m=\widehat y_m/a_m\); setting the blind coefficients to zero gives the minimum-norm point interpolant.

Here \(B\) contains \(0\) if and only if \(\ell\) is odd, and contains \(\pm M\) at the degrees
\[
\ell=(x_j-1)/2,\qquad M=y_j/2,\qquad x_j+y_j\sqrt2=(3+2\sqrt2)^j,\quad j\ge2.
\]
In particular \(d\) ranges from zero to three. The first exceptional degrees are \(8,49,288,1681\).

To derive this input, write the scalar harmonic as \(R(\theta)e^{im\phi}\) with its usual normalization. The two spin raises, evaluated at the equator, give \(R''-2mR'+m^2R\) times the normalization \(\sqrt{(\ell-2)!/(\ell+2)!}\) and the longitude phase. The scalar equation gives \(R''=(m^2-T)R\) there. The lowering expression has the opposite sign in the term linear in \(mR'\). The Legendre parity law and the double-factorial values at zero give the displayed squares, including the nonzero scalar \(R\) in the even branch and the nonzero scalar \(R'\) in the odd branch. Hence the only possible nonzero blind orders satisfy \(2M^2=T\) with \(\ell+M\) even. The equation is \((2\ell+1)^2-2(2M)^2=1\). The positive Pell solutions are generated by \(3+2\sqrt2\); the first gives the excluded \(\ell=1\). All have \(y_j\) even and \(x_j+y_j\equiv1\pmod4\), preserved by \((x,y)\mapsto(3x+4y,2x+3y)\), so \(\ell+M\) is even. Also \(M^2=\ell(\ell+1)/2\le\ell^2\). This explains the blind sets used in the theorem.

## Proof of minimal repair

The polar Wigner function \(u(\theta)=d^\ell_{m,-s}(\theta)\) is nonzero as a function and satisfies
\[
u''+\cot\theta\,u'+\left[T-\frac{m^2+s^2+2ms\cos\theta}{\sin^2\theta}\right]u=0\qquad(0<\theta<\pi).
\]
At the equator this equation is regular. Uniqueness for its initial value problem shows that when \(u\) vanishes there, its first derivative does not. Thus \(b_m=\partial_\theta{}_sY_{\ell m}(\pi/2,0)\ne0\) for all \(m\in B\).

After recovery of the visible coefficients from the point readings, subtract their known contribution from each derivative reading. The residual at longitude \(\phi\) is \(\sum_{m\in B}b_m c_m e^{im\phi}\). For \(B=\{0\}\) one residual solves directly for \(c_0\). For \(B=\{-M,M\}\), with \(z=e^{iM\phi}\) the two columns are \(z^{-1},z\); multiplying each row by \(z\) gives columns \(1,z^2\), which separate \(z=1,i\). For \(B=\{-M,0,M\}\) the same row rescaling gives \(1,z,z^2\) at the three distinct phases \(1,i,-1\), so the Vandermonde determinant is nonzero. Nonzero \(b_m\) then give every blind coefficient. Finally the point kernel has complex dimension \(d\); fewer than \(d\) extra complex-linear scalar measurements cannot be injective on it.

## Evidence and scope

The accompanying `verify_spin2.py` is a finite exact comparison of the Wigner factorial sum and the squared equatorial amplitudes through degree 60, with Wigner column-sum and Pell checks. It checks the point-evaluation input, not the derivative repair; the latter and its optimality are proved above. The derivatives are of components in a specified spin frame, not gauge-independent scalar observables. There is no noise or stability claim.

The spin-to-scalar identities are classical and appear explicitly in Wiaux, Jacques and Vandergheynst, *Fast spin ±2 spherical harmonics transforms and application in cosmology*, [arXiv:astro-ph/0508514](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0508514), equations (14)–(15). Equatorial Wigner matrices also occur in Huffenberger and Wandelt, [arXiv:1007.3514](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.3514). Elahi et al., [arXiv:1809.01321](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.01321), consider band-limited multisampling rings. The point-only rank and Pell classification above are context, not a separate priority claim. The specific contribution here is the exact minimal sparse pointwise derivative completion for every blind-set type of the one-ring fixed-degree problem.
