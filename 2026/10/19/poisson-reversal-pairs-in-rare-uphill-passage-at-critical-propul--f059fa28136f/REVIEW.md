# Review of the conditional rare-uphill passage limit

**Correctness — PASS.** The backward generator with a flip-count marker yields a quartic characteristic equation. Two decaying spatial modes, their uniform scale separation at fixed transform parameters, and the explicit small-noise eigenvalue expansion establish the conditional transform. The no-reversal endpoint is solved independently, and the transform limit determines the joint law. Exact finite-diffusivity calculations substantiate the expansion but are not substituted for the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Singh and Aghion study the same class of thermally diffusive active particles in a full bistable potential but report density, mean-passage and relaxation results. Grange and Yuan treat a drifted RTP without independent Brownian translation; Neri supplies broader first-passage martingale technology and a lattice RTP model. These are genuine antecedents and possible routes to implicit general coverage, but none of the inspected statements supplies the singular matched-load conditional marked count law. The novelty assessed is the explicit joint weak-noise law, not the general backward equations.

**Value — PASS.** The count of orientation changes en route to rare success separates a pathwise switching cost from elapsed time. The sharp even-Poisson statistics are not evident from marginal first-passage moments and characterize the crossover between active propulsion and thermal leakage at critical loading. This is a physically motivated structural limit, not an arbitrary finite enumeration.

**Closest literature:** Singh–Aghion (2026, arXiv:2610.08029), Grange–Yuan (2025/2026, arXiv:2509.11308), Neri (2025, arXiv:2504.03441), and Mukherjee–Le Doussal–Smith (2024, arXiv:2405.07032). The result applies only to the constant-force full-line model, the specified initial orientation, and an exponentially rare hitting event. Broader Markov-modulated Brownian theory may implicitly imply the singular limit.
