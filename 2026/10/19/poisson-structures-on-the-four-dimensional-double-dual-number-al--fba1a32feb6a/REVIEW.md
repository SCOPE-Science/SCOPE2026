# Mathematical review

**Correctness (PASS).** The classification begins with the universal biderivation parameter \(f=\{x,y\}\) and the two relation constraints \(2xf=2yf=0\). The Jacobi identity follows from the alternating-triderivation property in two generators. The automorphism group is established from the local maximal ideal, and the characteristic-two conjugation formula proves exact invariance of the coefficient of \(xy\), not merely an experimental pattern. All three orbit strata and odd-characteristic signs are proved. The simple-derived-Lie criterion follows from a perfect three-dimensional Lie algebra with no proper one- or two-dimensional ideal. An exhaustive script checks all bracket parameters over five small finite fields and full automorphism orbits over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_4\); these checks do not replace the proof.

**Originality (PASS with a recorded residual risk).** The 2026 arbitrary-field classification by Petrov and Pypka stops at dimension three; the 2025 four-dimensional result of Abdelwahab and Sánchez is restricted to complex coefficients. Neither implies characteristic-two classification on this four-dimensional ring. The complex classification can account for the easier odd-characteristic pattern but does not cover the exceptional characteristic-two orbit invariant and simple-derived-Lie boundary. Launois and Richard (2009) explicitly study the same truncated-algebra family with a fixed log-canonical complex bracket and its cohomology, which supplies a direct special-case precedent but does not imply all-bracket orbit classification. Multiple targeted literature and record searches found no equivalent result. The closest recorded finite-field Dedekind Poisson enumeration applies only when every Poisson subalgebra is an ideal; the present unital local algebra has the proper subalgebra \(K1\) that is not an ideal, so it cannot be covered by that theorem. Smooth four-dimensional Poisson-germ analyses have different coefficient algebras. Unindexed equivalent classification remains possible; the full appendix-by-appendix inspection of the complex classification was not achieved, but its stated characteristic-zero scope resolves the decisive coverage distinction.

**Value (PASS).** The theorem completes the Poisson orbit problem on a natural minimal four-dimensional complete intersection beyond the recent arbitrary-field dimension-three classification. It gives all-field normal forms, a sharp change in the number and sizes of finite-field orbits, and the particularly informative characteristic-two transition from a Heisenberg derived Lie algebra to a simple one, not merely a table of small computations. Its scope is one algebra, not all four-dimensional Poisson algebras.
