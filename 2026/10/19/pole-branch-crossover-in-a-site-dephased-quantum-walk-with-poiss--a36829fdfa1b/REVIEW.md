# Scientific assessment — reset quantum-walk pole–branch crossover

## Correctness — PASS
The stationary resolvent is valid because the reset term contracts trace-distance differences at rate \(r>0\). The position-projector Lindblad dynamics admit a site-monitoring renewal, whose free-flight kernel is exactly the Bessel-square distribution; this proves the normalized Fourier transform. An algebraic simple-pole/square-root split yields a positive Fourier-cut integral. For fixed \(\gamma>0\) the nearest singularity is a simple pole, whereas for \(\gamma=0\) the controlling singularity is square-root, giving distinct prefactors. The scaling \(\gamma\sqrt{m/C_\gamma}\) keeps the pole's quadratic separation from the branch edge finite; dominated convergence and elementary error-function integration establish the entire transition profile, including its zero endpoint. Independent ring-Lindbladian, Bessel quadrature, branch-integral, and window computations agree. Finite tests corroborate but do not certify infinite-size asymptotics.

## Value — PASS
The spatial crossover at distance \(m\asymp\gamma^{-2}\) quantifies a noncommuting zero-dephasing and large-distance limit, not an arbitrary parameter slice. It distinguishes an ultimate pure-exponential tail at any positive site dephasing from the inverse-square-root-modified tail under perfectly coherent propagation. The universal error-function crossover makes the transition quantitatively testable and provides a structural reference for noisy reset transport.
