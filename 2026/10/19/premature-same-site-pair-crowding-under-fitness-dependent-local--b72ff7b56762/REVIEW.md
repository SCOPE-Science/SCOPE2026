# Scientific review: premature same-site pair crowding

**Correctness — PASS.** Under the explicitly stated mean-field and sequential-limit hypotheses, applying the one-site jump generator to \(N\) and \(N(N-1)\) gives the exact factorial moments. The pair observable is normalized per fitness level, not per lattice site; this distinction is essential for a nonzero continuum answer. The stationary global density is bounded for \(0<\nu<1\) under the stated strict hopping-rate condition. Dominated convergence treats \(\nu<1/2\), a matched logarithmic layer treats \(\nu=1/2\), and rescaling plus an absolutely convergent beta integral treats \(1/2<\nu<1\). The rates and coefficients were independently checked numerically, with 32 passing consistency assertions. Finite calculations are not the analytic proof.

**Originality — PASS, subject to priority risk.** Grange, arXiv:2311.12414, already gives the local-reset master equation, first-moment closure, a full generating-function evolution, and the \(\nu=1\) first-moment condensation boundary. It does not state the integrated within-site second factorial moment or its earlier \(\nu=1/2\) divergence threshold in the inspected full text. Grange, arXiv:1910.05991, treats global rather than independently local resetting. Generic factorial-moment equations and endpoint regular-variation techniques are known and are not claimed as new. The assessed additional statement is the concrete pair-crowding phase classification and its universal critical amplitudes. An equivalent specialization of a more general moment theory cannot be excluded without independent scholarly review.

**Value — PASS.** Same-site ordered pairs measure local crowding/collisions in network-link or population-occupancy interpretations, whereas the established first-moment observable measures aggregate occupation. A finite mean density coexisting with a singular pair density separates intermittent local crowding from a genuine one-particle condensate; the precise half-power threshold and critical amplitudes offer observable benchmarks and clarify what the first-moment condensation criterion cannot detect. This is a structural model-specific critical phenomenon, not merely evaluation of an arbitrary small parameter case.

The theorem applies to the continuum mean-field stationary model with \(\beta<\beta_c\) and ordered limits: network size first, continuum second, critical removal third. It does not establish finite-network fluctuations, total-particle variance, or independence of particle counts at distinct sites.
