# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** A commuting idempotent pair has idempotent product whose image is the common fixed-point set. Both maps preserve each product fiber. The only overlap of their images in a fiber is its common fixed point, so the disjoint decomposition into points fixed only by the first map, only by the second, or by neither is canonical. The two arbitrary images of each remaining point give the exact factor \(\bigl((a+1)(b+1)\bigr)^c\). Conversely every such choice yields an idempotent commuting pair. The labeled component exponential formula follows bijectively and is valid for all finite sizes. The identity centrality proof and diagonal/identity correction give the graph edge count. An independent brute-force composition checker confirms complete coefficient distributions through size six; integer recurrences confirm higher sizes. Neither a finite test nor a reference search is substituted for the all-size proof.

**Originality: PASS, with explicit residual risk.** Primary full text of Araújo--Konieczny's maximal subsemilattice paper was inspected, especially the centralizer criterion and the proofs of its extremal classification. The subsequent independence-algebra extremal paper and the general digraph centralizer paper were compared at their definitions/results, and the commuting-graph paper's main results and applications were read. They concern maximum clique/semilattice cardinalities, centralizer descriptions, or path lengths rather than a simultaneous all-size coefficient formula for the product-rank distribution of all ordered commuting idempotent pairs. An exact-sequence and equivalent-formulation search identified no directly matching table or stronger implication in accessible material. The 2002 paper on commuting with a fixed idempotent was not obtained in full and remains a material residual risk; failed searches were not treated as proof of absolute novelty.

**Value: PASS.** All-arity parameter \(n\) is intrinsic to the standard full transformation monoids, and the rank of a commuting idempotent product is the size of the meet of the two elements in their generated semilattice. An explicit rank-refined enumeration is substantially more informative than the known extremal semilattice-size theorem. It determines the exact edge count of the natural induced commuting graph in every order, supplies a structural component decomposition, and supports subsequent probabilistic and graph-invariant studies. This is not a fixed numerical slice or a change of notation.
