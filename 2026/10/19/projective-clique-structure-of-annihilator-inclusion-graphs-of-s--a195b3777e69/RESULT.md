# Projective-clique structure of annihilator-inclusion graphs of short local rings

## Finding

**Theorem (exact graph structure, reconstruction, and failure of ring rigidity).** Let \(R\) be a finite commutative local ring with identity and maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\). Assume \(\mathfrak m^3=0\) and that \(\mathfrak m^2\) is one-dimensional over the residue field \(k=R/\mathfrak m=\mathbb F_q\) (in particular, \(\mathfrak m^2\ne0\)). Let
\[
V=\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2,\qquad d=\dim_k V.
\]
Multiplication induces a symmetric bilinear pairing \(B:V\times V\to\mathfrak m^2\). Set \(U=\operatorname{rad}(B)\), \(r=\dim_k U\), and \(t=d-r\). Then \(t\ge1\), and the graph whose vertices are the nonzero zero-divisors of \(R\), with two distinct vertices adjacent when their annihilator ideals are comparable under inclusion, has the explicit form
\[
\boxed{\Gamma_{\mathrm{inc}}(R)\cong K_c\vee\bigl(NK_b\bigr),\quad
c=q^{r+1}-1,\quad b=q^{r+1}(q-1),\quad N=\frac{q^t-1}{q-1}.}
\]
Here \(NK_b\) denotes the disjoint union of \(N\) complete graphs of order \(b\), and \(\vee\) denotes graph join. This formula applies without requiring a coefficient field inside \(R\) and hence includes mixed-characteristic local rings.

The graph is complete exactly when \(t=1\); in that case it is \(K_{q^{d+1}-1}\). If \(t\ge2\), the graph recovers the triple \((q,r,t)\) uniquely from its isomorphism type. More precisely, among the rings covered by the theorem with noncomplete graphs,
\[
\Gamma_{\mathrm{inc}}(R)\cong\Gamma_{\mathrm{inc}}(S)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(q_R,r_R,t_R)=(q_S,r_S,t_S).
\]
For \(t\ge2\), its automorphism group and chromatic/clique numbers are
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{inc}}(R))\cong
\mathfrak S_c\times(\mathfrak S_b\wr\mathfrak S_N),\qquad
\chi(\Gamma_{\mathrm{inc}}(R))=\omega(\Gamma_{\mathrm{inc}}(R))=q^{r+2}-1.
\]
All triples consisting of a prime power \(q\), an integer \(r\ge0\), and an integer \(t\ge1\) occur. Nevertheless the graph does not generally determine the ring: the two nonisomorphic algebras
\[
R_h=\mathbb F_3[x,y]/(x^2,y^2),\qquad
R_a=\mathbb F_3[x,y]/(xy,x^2-y^2)
\]
have the same inclusion graph \(K_2\vee(4K_6)\).

## Assumptions and scope

A finite commutative local ring has a unique maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\); its nonunits are precisely \(\mathfrak m\). Because \(\mathfrak m^3=0\), all nonzero elements of \(\mathfrak m\) are nonzero zero-divisors. For an element \(a\), write \(\operatorname{Ann}(a)=\{z\in R:za=0\}\). Adjacency means \(\operatorname{Ann}(a)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(b)\) or the reverse. The graph is simple (no loops) and its vertices are elements, not equivalence classes of annihilators or ideals.

The radical dimension \(r\) is the dimension of \(\{v\in V:B(v,w)=0\ \forall w\in V\}\). It can be positive; the theorem does not assume that \(R\) is Gorenstein or that \(B\) is nonsingular. Only the one-dimensionality of \(\mathfrak m^2\) and cube-zero radical are required. For each \(R\), the multiplication pairing is \(k\)-bilinear even if \(R\) contains no subfield isomorphic to \(k\), because the action of \(\mathfrak m\) on \(\mathfrak m^2\) is zero.

## Proof

**1. The multiplication pairing and its radical.** If \(a,b\in\mathfrak m\), their product lies in \(\mathfrak m^2\). Altering either by an element of \(\mathfrak m^2\) does not change the product, since \(\mathfrak m^3=0\). Thus multiplication descends to a symmetric \(k\)-bilinear map \(B\) on \(V\). It is nonzero because \(\mathfrak m^2\ne0\) is generated by products of elements of \(\mathfrak m\). Hence \(t\ge1\).

Let \(\pi:\mathfrak m\to V\) be the quotient map. By definition,
\[
\operatorname{Soc}(R)=\operatorname{Ann}(\mathfrak m)=\pi^{-1}(U),
\qquad |\operatorname{Soc}(R)|=q^{r+1}.
\]
(The equality uses that no unit annihilates \(\mathfrak m\ne0\).) Every nonzero socle element has annihilator exactly \(\mathfrak m\). If \(a\in\mathfrak m\setminus\operatorname{Soc}(R)\), no unit annihilates it and
\[
\operatorname{Ann}(a)
=\pi^{-1}\bigl(\{v\in V:B(\pi(a),v)=0\}\bigr).
\]
Because \(\pi(a)\notin U\), the displayed kernel is a hyperplane in \(V\), so each such annihilator is a proper subideal of \(\mathfrak m\), of cardinality \(q^d\).

**2. Projective classes of annihilators.** The pairing descends to a nondegenerate bilinear pairing on \(\overline V=V/U\), a \(t\)-dimensional \(k\)-space. The resulting map
\[
\overline V\longrightarrow\overline V^*,\qquad
\bar v\longmapsto B(\bar v,-)
\]
is an isomorphism. Two nonzero vectors induce the same annihilator hyperplane if and only if their images in \(\overline V\) span the same one-dimensional \(k\)-subspace. The number of such projective lines is \(N=(q^t-1)/(q-1)\).

For a fixed line in \(\overline V\), there are \(q-1\) nonzero vectors on it. Each has \(q^r\) lifts to \(V\), and every vector of \(V\) has \(q\) lifts to \(\mathfrak m\). Thus each proper-annihilator class contains exactly \(b=q^{r+1}(q-1)\) elements. The remaining \(c=q^{r+1}-1\) vertices are the nonzero socle elements.

**3. Exact adjacencies.** Socle-vertex annihilators are \(\mathfrak m\), which contain every other vertex annihilator. Any two proper-annihilator hyperplanes have the same finite cardinality \(q^d\); one contains the other if and only if they coincide. Hence each projective class induces a clique, different projective classes are mutually nonadjacent, and all socle vertices are universal. This is exactly \(K_c\vee(NK_b)\).

When \(t=1\), \(N=1\) and the whole graph is complete on \(c+b=q^{d+1}-1\) vertices. When \(t\ge2\), \(N\ge3\), so precisely the \(c\) socle vertices are universal and their removal leaves \(N\) disjoint connected cliques, each of order \(b\). Both \(c\) and \(b\) and the number \(N\) are therefore intrinsic graph data, from which
\[
q=1+\frac b{c+1},\qquad
q^{r+1}=c+1,\qquad
q^t=(q-1)N+1
\]
recover \((q,r,t)\) uniquely. The automorphism group independently permutes the universal vertices, the \(N\) cliques, and vertices within each clique, giving the stated direct/wreath product. Selecting all universal vertices plus one entire clique gives a maximum clique of size \(c+b=q^{r+2}-1\); coloring each clique with the same \(b\) colors and the universal vertices with \(c\) extra colors gives the matching chromatic number.

**4. Realizability and non-rigidity.** For any finite field \(k\) and \(r\ge0,t\ge1\), let \(V\) have basis of size \(r+t\) and choose the symmetric bilinear form with matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(1,\ldots,1,0,\ldots,0)\), with \(t\) ones and \(r\) zeros. On \(k\oplus V\oplus ks\), let \(sV=s^2=0\) and \(uv=B(u,v)s\). This is an associative commutative local ring with the required dimensions and radical rank.

For the claimed pair over \(\mathbb F_3\), the products on \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\) have respective nonsingular Gram matrices
\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both have \((q,r,t)=(3,0,2)\), hence graph \(K_2\vee(4K_6)\). But \(R_h\) has an element \(x\notin\operatorname{Soc}(R_h)\) with \(x^2=0\). In \(R_a\), if \(a x+b y+z x^2\in\mathfrak m\), its square is \((a^2+b^2)x^2\). Over \(\mathbb F_3\), this vanishes only for \(a=b=0\), so no element outside its socle squares to zero. This ring-theoretic distinction proves \(R_h\not\cong R_a\).

## Verification

The supplied standalone `verify.py` enumerates elements, multiplies them exactly, reconstructs every annihilator by testing every ring element, and compares all unordered vertex pairs against the predicted projective-class adjacency rule. It checks eleven finite rings: nine split test algebras with prime residue field \(2\), \(3\), or \(5\), spanning nonsingular and degenerate pairings and the complete-graph boundary; and two nonsplit/mixed-characteristic examples. It includes both inequivalent quadratic forms over \(\mathbb F_3\).

Run `python3 verify.py`. Its last line is `ALL_TESTS_PASS: 11 exact ring configurations`. These tests check finite instances only; the arbitrary-field and arbitrary mixed-characteristic assertions follow from the proof, not from enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work

Safari, Shaveisi, and Nikandish introduced this annihilator-*inclusion* graph in 2026 and established general connectivity, diameter, girth, and completeness criteria. Their Theorem 4.4 identifies completeness with the annihilator ideals forming a chain; their concluding section proposes studying clique and chromatic numbers. The present result evaluates the full adjacency structure, a graph-isomorphism criterion, and those two graph invariants for all finite local rings with cube-zero maximal ideal and one-dimensional radical square. Theorem 4.4 alone does not provide the necessary rank classification or projective-clique decomposition.

This is different from the *annihilator-compressed zero-divisor graph* of Spiroff and Wickham, whose vertices are annihilator-equivalence classes and whose edges represent zero products, not ideal comparability. Likewise, previous work on monomializability of two-generated short local algebras examines the multiplication tensor up to change of basis, not this element-level graph and its reconstruction behavior.

## Limitations

Only commutative finite local rings with \(\mathfrak m^3=0\) and one-dimensional nonzero \(\mathfrak m^2\) are treated. The theorem does not classify inclusion graphs for higher-dimensional radical squares, arbitrary products of local rings, or general noncommutative rings. When \(t=1\), completeness destroys the asserted noncomplete-graph reconstruction; in all ranks, even identical graph parameters need not imply ring isomorphism. The test program covers specific small rings but is not a replacement for the general proof. Originality is supported by comparison with accessible literature and published results, not by an exhaustive universal index of all papers.
