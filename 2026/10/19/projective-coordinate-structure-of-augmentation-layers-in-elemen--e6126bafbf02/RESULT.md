# Projective coordinate structure of augmentation layers in elementary-abelian core quandle rings

## Finding
Let \(p\) be an odd prime and \(V\cong\mathbb F_p^d\) with \(d\ge1\). Equip \(V\) with the core-quandle operation \(a*b=2b-a\), and write \(\Delta\) for the augmentation ideal of its integral quandle ring \(\mathbb Z[\operatorname{Core}(V)]\). The nonassociative augmentation powers may be defined by \(\Delta^{r+1}=\Delta^r\Delta\); the spans using every parenthesization agree.

Set \(P=\mathbb F_p[X_1,\ldots,X_d]\) and let \(J\) be its homogeneous ideal generated by \(X_i^pX_j-X_iX_j^p\) for \(1\le i<j\le d\). Then there is an isomorphism of positive-degree graded nonassociative \(\mathbb F_p\)-algebras
\[
\operatorname{gr}^{>0}_{\Delta}\mathbb Z[\operatorname{Core}(V)]\cong(P/J)_{>0},
\]
where the product on homogeneous elements of degrees \(r,s\ge1\) on the right is
\[
f\star g=(-1)^r2^sfg.
\]
Here \(fg\) means the ordinary multiplication in the quotient commutative polynomial ring. Moreover, \(J\) is exactly the homogeneous vanishing ideal of \(\mathbb P^{d-1}(\mathbb F_p)\), so the underlying graded vector space is the positive-degree homogeneous coordinate ring of all its rational points. The displayed isomorphism identifies each \(\Delta^r/\Delta^{r+1}\) with the degree-\(r\) piece of this ring.

In particular, every quotient \(\Delta^r/\Delta^{r+1}\) is elementary abelian of order \(p^{h_{p,d}(r)}\), where
\[
h_{p,d}(r)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\#\left\{(a_1,\ldots,a_{i-1})\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}^{i-1}:\sum_j a_j\le r-1\right\}.
\]
The entire Hilbert series is
\[
\sum_{r\ge1}h_{p,d}(r)z^r=\frac{z}{1-z}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}(1+z+\cdots+z^{p-1})^j.
\]
Consequently \(h_{p,d}(r)=(p^d-1)/(p-1)\) for all \(r\ge1+(d-1)(p-1)\); for \(d\ge2\) this is the earliest stabilization degree.

## Assumptions and scope
All rings are over \(\mathbb Z\) before taking the positive-degree associated graded. The prime \(p\) is odd so multiplication by two is an automorphism of \(V\), ensuring the core operation is an Alexander-quandle operation with \((1-(-1))V=V\). The rank \(d\) is any positive integer, and every positive degree \(r\) is covered. There are no asymptotic or generic-position assumptions. The polynomial presentation refers to the commutative underlying coordinate ring; the transported quandle product is the explicitly twisted \(\star\)-product, not ordinary multiplication.

## Proof
**1. Reduction to a commutative integral group ring.** Put \(A=\mathbb Z[V]\), with basis \(e_v\), and \(I=\ker(A\to\mathbb Z)\). Under the identification of quandle-ring basis elements with \(e_v\), the quandle multiplication is \(u\star v=\alpha(u)\beta(v)\), where \(\alpha(e_a)=e_{-a}\), \(\beta(e_b)=e_{2b}\), and the product on the right is the ordinary product in \(A\). The augmentation-transport theorem of Zhang, Theorem 2.2, gives \(\Delta^r=I^r\) as additive subgroups for all \(r\ge1\), including the span of arbitrary parenthesized products: \((1-(-1))V=V\). The maps \(\alpha,\beta\) preserve \(I^r\), so \(\star\) induces a product on \(\bigoplus_{r\ge1}I^r/I^{r+1}\).

**2. Polynomial generators and their relations.** Choose an \(\mathbb F_p\)-basis \(v_1,\ldots,v_d\) of \(V\), and put \(t_i=e_{v_i}-1\). The \(I\)-adic graded ring is generated in degree one by the classes of these \(t_i\). The standard isomorphism \(I/I^2\cong V\) and multiplication show that \(pI^r\subseteq I^{r+1}\) for every \(r\ge1\). There is therefore a surjective graded map from \(P\) in all positive degrees, with \(X_i\mapsto[t_i]\).

Since \((1+t_i)^p=1\), the binomial identity can be written \(pt_i u_i=-t_i^p\), with
\[
u_i=1+\sum_{k=2}^{p-1}\frac1p\binom pk t_i^{k-1}\in1+I.
\]
Initially \(pt_i\in I^2\). The identity \(pt_i=-t_i^p-pt_i(u_i-1)\) inductively yields \(pt_i\in I^p\); it then yields \(pt_i\equiv-t_i^p\pmod{I^{p+1}}\). Multiplying this congruence by \(t_j\) and interchanging \(i,j\) gives \(t_i^pt_j-t_it_j^p\in I^{p+2}\). Thus every generator of \(J\) vanishes in the associated graded, giving a graded surjection \(P/J\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{gr}^{>0}_I A\).

**3. Projective evaluation proves injectivity.** Fix a primitive \(p\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\) and put \(\pi=\zeta-1\). For each nonzero linear functional \(\ell:V\to\mathbb F_p\), the character homomorphism \(\chi_\ell:A\to\mathbb Z[\zeta]\) given by \(e_v\mapsto\zeta^{\ell(v)}\) maps \(I^r\) into \(\pi^r\mathbb Z[\zeta]\). Dividing by \(\pi^r\) and reducing modulo \(\pi\) gives an \(\mathbb F_p\)-linear functional on \(I^r/I^{r+1}\). Its value on the class of \(t_1^{a_1}\cdots t_d^{a_d}\), \(\sum a_i=r\), is \(\ell(v_1)^{a_1}\cdots\ell(v_d)^{a_d}\). Hence the composite \(P_r/J_r\to I^r/I^{r+1}\to\prod_{[\ell]\in\mathbb P(V^\vee)(\mathbb F_p)}\mathbb F_p\) is exactly homogeneous-polynomial evaluation on one normalized representative of each projective point. This evaluation is injective, as shown next, so the graded surjection is an isomorphism and \(J\) is the entire homogeneous point ideal.

**4. A normal-monomial proof of projective evaluation injectivity.** For each degree \(r\ge1\), use \(X_j^pX_i=X_jX_i^p\) for \(j<i\) to reduce every monomial to a linear combination of monomials whose largest occurring variable is \(X_i\) and whose earlier exponents satisfy \(0\le a_j<p\) and \(\sum_{j<i}a_j\le r-1\); the exponent of \(X_i\) is then \(r-\sum_{j<i}a_j\ge1\). These monomials span \((P/J)_r\). Evaluate them successively on projective points whose *last* nonzero coordinate is \(1,2,\ldots,d\), normalized so that coordinate equals one. At stratum \(i\), all terms with largest variable index greater than \(i\) vanish, and the terms with smaller index have already been shown to have zero coefficient. The remaining evaluations are the functions \(a_1^{e_1}\cdots a_{i-1}^{e_{i-1}}\) on \(\mathbb F_p^{i-1}\), with \(0\le e_j<p\) and \(\sum e_j\le r-1\). These are linearly independent because reduced monomials form a basis of all functions on \(\mathbb F_p^{i-1}\). Ascending induction proves every normal-monomial coefficient zero if all projective evaluations vanish. The displayed normal monomials consequently form a basis of degree \(r\).

**5. Nonassociative product and Hilbert function.** On \(I/I^2\), the induced endomorphisms \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) act by \(-1\) and \(2\). On homogeneous degree \(r\) and \(s\), respectively, they act by \((-1)^r\) and \(2^s\). Thus \(u\star v=\alpha(u)\beta(v)\) induces exactly \(f\star g=(-1)^r2^sfg\). Counting the normal monomials from step 4 proves the formula for \(h_{p,d}(r)\) and the Hilbert series. For \(r-1\ge(d-1)(p-1)\), all \(p^{i-1}\) exponent tuples occur at every stratum, giving \(\sum_{i=1}^dp^{i-1}=(p^d-1)/(p-1)\). Before this bound, the last stratum lacks at least the tuple with all \(d-1\) exponents equal to \(p-1\), so the stabilization bound is sharp when \(d\ge2\). This completes the proof.

## Verification
Run `python verify.py` with Python 3 and SymPy installed. The script constructs the integral multiplication operators by \(e_{v_i}-1\), computes exact Hermite and Smith normal forms for successive powers of \(I\), compares the nontrivial Smith factors to the Hilbert formula, and independently checks the finite-projective evaluation ranks. Tested configurations are \((p,d,r_{\max})=(3,2,6),(5,2,5),(3,3,5),(2,3,4)\); the last configuration is only a separate group-ring boundary check and is **not** included in the core-quandle theorem, which assumes odd \(p\). Core multiplication is checked directly in these finite examples. The general, all-degree claim relies on the proof, not on these finite computations.

## Relationship to prior work
Zhang's 2026 paper proves the underlying Alexander-quandle augmentation transport and computes all cyclic-group quotients. It does not provide the present higher-rank projective-coordinate algebra, its twisted graded product, or its sharp multivariate Hilbert function. Tang (2001) and Bak–Tang (2004) give additive presentations of augmentation powers of elementary or wider abelian groups; Chang–Tang (2011) studies additive quotient bases, and Chang (2021) proves eventual stability in commutative augmented rings. These are important possible sources of equivalent **additive** counts. The present identification of the complete positive-degree graded algebra with the homogeneous point-coordinate algebra, together with the core-quandle twist, goes beyond the explicitly accessible statements of those references. The full texts of Tang (2001), Bak–Tang (2004), and Chang–Tang (2011) were not accessible for comparison, and equivalent geometric ring formulations therein cannot be excluded.

## Limitations
The theorem applies to odd-prime elementary abelian core quandles and to integral augmentation powers; it makes no statement about higher-exponent groups or arbitrary Alexander automorphisms. The quandle graded product is nonassociative in general. Finite computations are illustrative rather than proof of the universal formulas. The bibliographic comparison with older additive-presentation literature is incomplete, and independent validation has not been performed.
