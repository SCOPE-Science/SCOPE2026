# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The new claim is a local-in-parameter theorem for the stated smooth lift. The two-step displacement has a uniform quadratic Taylor expansion at its only first-order contact points. Exact completed-square discriminants decide positivity, and an explicit integral-clock argument yields the coefficient rather than inferring it from a simulation. The boundary identity at \(b=4a\), the half-translation conjugacy for \(b=-4a\), and the direct \(b=0\) orbit close the exceptional cases. The standalone checks include exact algebra and secondary finite tests; they are not substitutes for the universal proof.

**Originality — PASS subject to residual bibliographic uncertainty.** Glendinning's arXiv:2506.19508v2 §6 equation (6.36) proves a first-order coefficient under \(a>|c|\); it does not imply the quadratic response when \(c=a\), because its integral diverges. Boyland arXiv:2506.02988v2 derives generic statements about pinched tongues, not the particular sharp \(|b|=4a\) coefficient and exact locked complement. Parkhe arXiv:1109.3214 concerns strict monotonicity of other one-parameter circle families. Relevant published-result searches were compared at statement level. The principal risk is that classical normal-form/Arnol'd-tongue analysis could contain an equivalent implicit specialization.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The equality \(c=a\) is the structurally distinguished boundary where the original first-order transversality integral becomes singular. The theorem classifies both complementary regimes across the natural two-harmonic parameter and calculates a nontrivial, nonmonotone exact second-order coefficient. It demonstrates that a smooth family may have a differentiable but not twice-differentiable rotation number at a rational rigid rotation.

**Limits.** Local in the perturbation, fixed parameter ratio, and specific to the half-turn two-harmonic forcing; it does not claim a general criterion for arbitrary circle maps. Literature completeness and independent validation remain open.
