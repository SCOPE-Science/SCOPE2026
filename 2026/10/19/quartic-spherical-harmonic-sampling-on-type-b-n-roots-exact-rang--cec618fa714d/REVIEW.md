## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Dunkl's *Symmetric Functions and \(B_N\)-invariant Spherical Harmonics* (arXiv:math/0207122v1) develops a basis and inner-product Gram determinants for invariant (signed-permutation symmetric) harmonic polynomials, including evaluations at selected points. Its actual Gram matrix is not the sampling Gram on all antipodal representatives of short and long roots, and the invariant subspace cannot provide the full noninvariant standard and signed-pair spectral sectors without substantial further work. Dai and Xu's *Spherical Harmonics* (arXiv:1304.2585v1) proves the general addition formula and dimension identities but does not specialize them to this finite root sampling problem in the examined sections. Delsarte–Goethals–Seidel's 1977 foundational spherical-design paper was located but not available for adequate full-text comparison; an older equivalent in spherical design or association-scheme language remains possible. The publication search did not identify a theorem providing the proposed full fourth-degree image, four-branch spectrum and unique dimension-six conditioning optimum. Generic reproduction kernels alone do not assert these specializations, although they provide necessary ingredients. The nonaccess risk is acknowledged rather than treated as proof of absence.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The problem is motivated independently by the two natural length classes of the type-\(B_n\) reflection roots and the identifiability of a fixed spherical harmonic degree from their samples. The \(n\) exact incidence constraints characterize which data can actually arise, while the constructive interpolant solves the corresponding inverse problem. The entire exact Gram spectrum quantifies stability, shows five discrete level-collision dimensions, and singles out dimension six as the unique global optimum for unweighted condition number. These are structural, nontrivial facts for an infinite natural root-system family, rather than just finite calculations or a generic application of a dimension count.

## Scope and limitations
The result concerns homogeneous harmonic quartics, independently unit-normalized short and long roots, unweighted sampling on antipodal representatives, and integer dimensions at least three. The claims do not extend to other degrees or arbitrary sampling weights. Explicit numerical tests cover only finite sets of dimensions; the proof supplies all-dimensional identities. The 1977 foundational design source's full text was unavailable. Independent external assessment remains outstanding.
