# Review of the cubic central-extension generator law

## Correctness — PASS

The stated result concerns every integer \(d\ge3\), every alternating bilinear \(B\) and linear \(L\) over \(\mathbb F_3\) for which \(B\wedge L\ne0\). The defining operation is shown to be commutative, idempotent, Latin, and right-self-distributive. The cocycle calculation gives exactly \(3(Ca+Db)=0\), and the medial-defect computation equals \(-T\) on the three projected differences. If projected generators span affinely, nonzero trilinearity gives a four-term defect on seed points; this creates a full central fibre and spreads it through every projected point. Necessity follows from closure of affine subspaces under the base operation. The rank-matrix enumeration then gives the exact generating-tuple count for every \(k\); no infinite conclusion is extrapolated from experiments. The included checker passed all canonical 81-point triples and the complete finite projected quadruple count, plus tests of the general polynomial identity.

## Originality — PASS, subject to bibliographic risks

The publicly dated January 20, 2026 article by Lực Ta and its February 9 preprint explicitly contain a nonmedial four-point witness; the existence of four-generated nonmedial commutative quandles is therefore already implied by prior work and is **not** the claimed contribution. Kepka–Němec (1981), especially their §14 classification, and the later enumeration of distributive/trimedial quasigroups address the classical objects and their isomorphism types. The new theorem here is the all-dimensional equivalence between quandle generation and affine generation of the projection, with exact ordered generating-tuple probabilities for all arities and every nonzero form \(B\wedge L\). The actual primary sections and mathematical index were compared at the statement/implication level; no inspected source supplies this criterion or its count. The remaining risk is an equivalent, possibly routine-looking formulation via commutative Moufang-loop Frattini quotients or specialized generator enumeration absent from inspected sources.

## Value — PASS

The family is a natural collection of finite nonmedial commutative (indeed involutory) quandles, including the first nonmedial order-81 case studied in the classical literature. Exact generator laws are structural invariants that identify how strongly a nonmedial central extension differs from its affine quotient. The result is uniform in dimension, independent of deformation parameters, and detects precisely when random samples recover the whole operation. The sharp minimal generating rank \(d+1\) and its entire random-generation probability profile are useful well beyond a single finite census. No classification of arbitrary commutative quandles is claimed.
