# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** The unrestricted claim reduces to the explicitly stated two-parameter EPPF. Marking a size-\(r\) block shifts the remaining EPPF parameter from \(\theta\) to \(\theta+\alpha\); averaging membership of a fixed pair over all marked \(r\)-subsets proves the full mixed moment. Quadratic signs follow from strict endpoint inequalities. Independent exact-rational brute-force checks (1,015 comparisons across 35 laws and four graph families) corroborate the calculation, but are not substituted for the proof.

**Originality (PASS; historical priority not independently established).** The closest foundational source, Gnedin–Pitman (2004), gives the two-parameter EPPF and generalized Gibbs weights, not the evaluated cross-covariance rank and sharp size threshold. Chern–Diaconis–Kane–Rhoades (2013, 2015) give broad moment methods and levels statistics for uniform Bell set partitions, a different probability law, without establishing this parameter-dependent sign phase. A published size-frequency/total-block-count covariance result for uniform set partitions concerns a different statistic and distribution. Residual risk: general EPPF and deletion results could encode an earlier equivalent derived formula; no independent priority certification is asserted.

**Value (PASS).** Within-cluster graph-edge counts are natural measures of co-clustering along a fixed observational adjacency graph, while block-size frequencies describe the clustering spectrum. The formula establishes an exact rank-one dependency interface for *all* deterministic graph weights and a unique finite-\(n\) sign transition in block size across the full Ewens–Pitman parameter family. This identifies which block sizes move in opposition to, versus together with, local co-clustering, including the explicit Ewens transition; it is not merely a reformulation of a marginal mean.

The result does not establish independence, stochastic order, or higher-order covariance geometry, and broader implicit historical coverage remains a risk.
