Removing at most \(Dn\) cross edges from a balanced complete bipartite adsorption graph, with each vertex missing at most a fixed \(D\) neighbors, creates rare tiny jammed states: the expected loss is asymptotic to deleted edges divided by \(n\), while the terminal-count variance is asymptotic to the number of deleted edges. When the removed graph is any union of bipartite cycles of length at least six, the exact occupied count is either \(n\) or \(3\), with \(\Pr(X_n=3)=2n/(n^2-1)\) for every \(n\ge4\).
