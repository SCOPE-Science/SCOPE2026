# Rare-error law for positive first-harmonic kernel quadrature on centrally symmetric curves

## Finding

Let \(g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R^2\) be a \(2\pi\)-periodic \(C^3\) parametrization of a strictly convex, simple closed curve, with \(g(\theta+\pi)=-g(\theta)\) and \(\det(g(\theta),g'(\theta))>0\) for every \(\theta\). Let \(\Theta_1,\ldots,\Theta_N\), \(N\ge2\), be independent uniform angles on \([0,2\pi)\). For the finite-rank positive-semidefinite kernel

\[
k(\theta,\varphi)=g(\theta)^{\mathsf T}g(\varphi)
\]

and uniform target measure, define the *optimal simplex-weighted worst-case quadrature error*

\[
E_N=\min_{w_i\ge0,\ \sum_{i=1}^{N}w_i=1}
\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_i g(\Theta_i)\right\|_2.
\]

Its target kernel mean is zero. Set the *positive angular-speed functional*

\[
J(g)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
\frac{\det(g(\theta),g'(\theta))^2}{\|g(\theta)\|_2^2}\,\mathrm d\theta.
\]

**Theorem.** For every \(N\ge2\), the exact probability of nonzero optimal error is

\[
\Pr(E_N>0)=\frac{N}{2^{N-1}}.
\]

If \(U\) is uniform on \([0,2\pi)\), \(Z\) has the unit-rate exponential distribution, and \(U,Z\) are independent, then

\[
N E_N\ \big|\ \{E_N>0\}\ \Longrightarrow\
\frac{\pi\det(g(U),g'(U))}{2\|g(U)\|_2}\,Z.
\]

In particular the **unconditional sharp mean-square law** is

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle\mathbb E E_N^2
=\frac{\pi^2 J(g)}{N 2^N}(1+o(1)),\qquad N\to\infty.}
\]

For the anisotropic first-harmonic feature curve \(g(\theta)=(a\cos\theta,b\sin\theta)\), where \(a,b>0\), \(J(g)=ab\), and hence

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle \mathbb E E_N^2\sim \frac{\pi^2 ab}{N2^N}.}
\]

This distinguishes the geometric mean \(ab\) from a naive worst-semiaxis squared constant. In the isotropic case \(a=b=r\), the full finite-sample tail is also explicit: for \(0\le t<r\),

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\Pr(E_N>t)=\frac{N}{2^{N-1}}
\left(1-\frac2\pi\arcsin\frac tr\right)^{N-1}.}
\]

It vanishes for \(t\ge r\). The exact-zero probability and the circular-spacing distribution are classical inputs, not originality claims; the assessed contribution is the conditional size law and the sharp shape-dependent mean-square constant in the numerical-integration formulation.

## Assumptions and scope

1. The kernel is the **finite-rank** real positive-semidefinite kernel given above, and the target is normalized Lebesgue measure in the angle parameter, not arclength measure on the image curve unless those happen to agree.
2. All \(N\) candidate nodes are sampled independently and uniformly, and the weights are optimized *after* sampling with simplex constraints. Optimization is exact.
3. The parametrization is fixed as \(N\) grows. Central symmetry, strict convexity, and \(\det(g,g')>0\) ensure that each half-plane through the origin corresponds to a half-period of the angular parameter. The same statements hold after reversing orientation with absolute determinants.
4. The result concerns worst-case integration error in the RKHS generated by \(k\), not pointwise error for an arbitrary integrand, numerical floating-point stability, or the behavior of a specific approximate optimization algorithm.
5. A kernel with \(a\ne b\) is not translation-invariant in the angle; this is intentional, representing unequal first-harmonic feature weights.

## Proof

**1. The RKHS reduction.** By the feature-map representation of a positive kernel, the RKHS embedding of the uniform target is \(\int_0^{2\pi}g(\theta)\,\mathrm d\theta/(2\pi)=0\), since \(g(\theta+\pi)=-g(\theta)\). The norm of the difference between the target mean and any simplex-weighted sample mean is its Euclidean feature norm. Hence \(E_N=\operatorname{dist}(0,\operatorname{conv}\{g(\Theta_i)\})\).

**2. Exact rare-event parametrization.** Sort the sampled parameter angles cyclically, and let \(\Delta_N\) be their largest circular spacing. For a strictly convex centrally symmetric curve winding once about zero, the origin is outside the sampled convex hull if and only if some open half-plane through zero contains all sampled points; equivalently \(\Delta_N>\pi\), outside probability-zero degeneracies. The cyclic spacings divided by \(2\pi\) have the uniform Dirichlet distribution on the simplex. Since two spacings cannot both exceed \(\pi\), for \(\pi\le x<2\pi\),

\[
\Pr(\Delta_N>x)=N\left(1-\frac{x}{2\pi}\right)^{N-1}.
\]

Consequently \(\Pr(E_N>0)=N2^{1-N}\). Conditional on this event, put \(\delta_N=\Delta_N-\pi\). Its exact conditional survival function is

\[
\Pr(\delta_N>t\mid E_N>0)=\left(1-\frac t\pi\right)^{N-1},\qquad0\le t<\pi.
\]

Thus \(N\delta_N\) converges to \(\pi Z\), where \(Z\) is unit exponential. By rotation invariance of the underlying uniform parameter angles, the start angle \(U_N\) of the unique long spacing is uniform on \([0,2\pi)\) and independent of \(\delta_N\).

**3. Uniform local convex-hull geometry.** Write the two endpoints of the long gap as \(g(u)\) and \(g(u+\pi+\delta)=-g(u+\delta)\). The remaining points are on the complementary strictly convex arc and thus on the same side of the endpoint chord. For \(\delta=0\) the endpoints are \(g(u)\) and \(-g(u)\), and the perpendicular projection of the origin lies at their midpoint. Compactness and \(\inf_{u}\|g(u)\|_2>0\) show that, uniformly in \(u\), for all sufficiently small positive \(\delta\) this projection remains in the open chord segment. The chord is a supporting edge of the sampled polygon. Hence the distance to the entire sampled convex hull equals the perpendicular distance to the chord. Taylor expansion, uniformly in \(u\), gives

\[
E_N=\frac{\delta_N}{2}
\frac{\det(g(U_N),g'(U_N))}{\|g(U_N)\|_2}
+O(\delta_N^2)
\]

on \(0<\delta_N<\delta_0\) for some fixed \(\delta_0>0\). No assertion is made that the chord-foot formula remains valid for all large gaps.

**4. Conditional weak convergence and squared-moment passage.** The exact beta tail implies \(\Pr(N\delta_N>z\mid E_N>0)=(1-z/(N\pi))^{N-1}\) for \(0\le z<N\pi\), and this is bounded by \(e^{-z/(2\pi)}\) for all \(N\ge2\). For every gap, the midpoint of its endpoint chord is feasible, and central symmetry gives

\[
0\le E_N\le\frac12\|g(u)-g(u+\delta_N)\|_2
\le\frac{\|g'\|_\infty}{2}\delta_N.
\]

Therefore conditional moments of \(N E_N\) of every fixed finite order are uniformly bounded. The local geometric expansion and independence of the long-gap starting angle yield the stated conditional weak limit and uniform integrability of the squares. Since \(\mathbb EZ^2=2\),

\[
\mathbb E[(N E_N)^2\mid E_N>0]
\longrightarrow\frac{\pi^2}{2}J(g).
\]

Multiplication by the **exact** failure probability \(N2^{1-N}\) gives \(\mathbb E E_N^2\sim\pi^2 J(g)/(N2^N)\).

**5. Elliptic evaluation and exact circular tail.** For \(g(\theta)=(a\cos\theta,b\sin\theta)\), \(\det(g,g')=ab\). The elementary identity

\[
\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
\frac{\mathrm d\theta}{a^2\cos^2\theta+b^2\sin^2\theta}
=\frac1{ab}
\]

proves \(J(g)=ab\). When \(a=b=r\), the gap endpoints make a chord at distance exactly \(r\sin(\delta_N/2)\) from the origin, with perpendicular foot on the chord for **all** positive gaps. Substituting the conditional spacing survival law at \(\delta_N=2\arcsin(t/r)\) proves the exact tail.

## Relationship to prior work

Hayakawa (2026), *Convex-Geometric Error Bounds for Positive-Weight Kernel Quadrature*, develops broad random-convex-hull bounds and simplex-weight optimization for fixed-pool kernel quadrature. It establishes general convergence guarantees but does not give this conditional failure-size distribution or the shape functional \(J(g)\). The finite-rank feature-map definition follows that framework.

Hayakawa, Lyons, and Oberhauser (2023) study the probability that a target point belongs to a random convex hull and general distance/depth bounds. Wendel's theorem gives the classical *exact inclusion probability* for centrally symmetric distributions. Classical circular-spacing/coverage work provides the law of the unique spacing longer than half a circle. These inputs already imply the exact zero-error probability, and in the isotropic circle they imply the finite tail after the elementary chord relation. This fact is credited, not presented as an independent novelty. The further uniform chord-foot lemma, the **conditional** geometric-size distribution for a curved anisotropic feature map, and its exact mean-square prefactor \(J(g)\) are the scoped contribution; no broader claim of priority over all random-polytope literature is made.

## Limitations

The source literature and indexed search did not reveal an equivalent shape-dependent conditional error theorem, but unindexed geometric-probability results might exist. The largest-spacing law is classical, so the result is **not** a claim to new random-spacing theory. The theorem does not apply as stated to arbitrary nonuniform target angles, noncentrally symmetric feature curves, infinite-rank kernels, approximately optimized weights, or independent numerical errors. In particular, the \(2^{-N}/N\) rate is a special finite-rank phenomenon and is not a general convergence rate for kernel quadrature.
