# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is a complete quantifier-level classification for all real \(r>1\) and \(L>0\). Four explicit half-open intervals are bijectively cycled; their return has unit slope and zero displacement, so every point therein has least period four. The only remaining core endpoints are mapped into the cyclic family in at most three steps. Two disjoint open holes have one admissible alternating inverse branch per step, contracting lengths by \(r^{-1}\); infinite survivors are the uniquely solved repelling two-cycle. This simultaneously proves eventual periodicity and the exact geometric exit law. The attached exact-rational tests validate the branch conventions at representative values, without being used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (with residual priority risk)

The 2021 originating paper [1] establishes the map and studies fixed-point-free and chaotic parameter classes; its indexed abstract and first-page text do not give the full reciprocal-slope periodicity and geometric law. Its complete full text could not be inspected, which leaves a meaningful antecedent risk. The 2023 full open-access treatment [2, Sections 2–5] studies numerical bifurcation and FPGA implementation for a different parameter curve, not the global invariant-core census or exact distribution. The 2022 comparative chapter [3] has an accessible abstract describing numerical entropy and Lyapunov comparisons but not an accessible full proof. Related results on periodic orbit classifications in different maps do not cover this map by a known reduction. A prior expansion estimate for this same map assumes \(m_1^3m_2>1\), whereas here \(m_1^3m_2=m_1^2<1\); its claimed invariant expansion therefore does not imply the present neutral regime. Unindexed earlier period-four analyses remain possible; absence from search is not proof of first publication.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact return identity on an entire interval family and the full exceptional-orbit census reveal an identifiable resonant, nonchaotic regime in a model motivated partly by chaotic signal generation. The exact transient-length law gives measurable content beyond the existence of periodic cycles: despite eventual periodicity, mean observation time before entering the core diverges like \((r-1)^{-1}\) as \(r\downarrow1\). The reciprocal slope constraint is not an arbitrary slice: it is the natural cancellation of alternating branch multipliers and causes an exact finite-time collapse to neutral dynamics. No general chaos or cryptographic security claim is made.
