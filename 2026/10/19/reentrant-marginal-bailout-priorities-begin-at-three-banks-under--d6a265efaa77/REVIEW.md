# Scientific review: reentrant marginal bailout priorities

**Correctness — PASS.** The unique clearing vector follows from contraction because every bank owes a positive fraction externally. On each fixed default set, differentiating the linear clearing system gives the standard threat-index vector; solvent recipients have zero local gain. Increasing all exogenous bank assets coordinatewise causes defaults only to disappear, yielding a complete two-bank impossibility argument. For the three-bank witness, the displayed payment branches satisfy the fixed-point conditions by exact affine inequalities over each full interval. Direct matrix inversion gives the three rational gradient vectors, which strictly select bank 1, bank 2, then bank 1. The packaged stdlib verifier independently enumerates default sets, checks rational identities and finite injections at three interior points. At breakpoints and in the fully solvent regime unique-winner claims are deliberately excluded.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Demange (2012, revised 2015) gives the threat index and its optimality for sufficiently small cash transfers (Propositions 1–2). Section 3.2.4 even presents a non-monotone finite-budget policy, so merely restating rank changes would not be original. What is assessed here is the smallest-network result for *strict reentry of the unique optimal marginal recipient while all pre-bailout exogenous assets rise proportionally*, with an all-positive three-bank matrix and exact transitions. The finite-budget changes in Demange do not state these quantifiers; the general mitigation optimization of Li et al. (2014) likewise does not supply a three-bank minimality theorem. Search of published indexed mathematical records and the specific related primary full texts did not reveal an identical claim. Nonetheless it might be an unstated corollary or known small-network example in other specialized literature, so priority remains provisional until independent literature assessment.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A sharp three-bank cutoff for policy-target reentry is a natural baseline for stress testing the dependence of a marginal financial-network intervention on the state of the economy. The example shows that even a connected three-bank system with strict external liabilities can exhibit two changes in its unique intervention priority, although individual marginal gains only decrease at solvency transitions. This exact lower-dimensional distinction is more informative than another arbitrary rank-change example; it does not purport to give a calibrated regulatory rule. The gross-payment objective and infinitesimal-budget restriction are scientifically consequential limitations.

**Closest prior work:** Demange, *Contagion in Financial Networks: A Threat Index* (CESifo 5307, especially Propositions 1–2 and Section 3.2.4); Li et al., arXiv:1410.2570; Eisenberg and Noe (2001). Demange's fixed-set sensitivity is an essential prior input, and its finite-budget non-monotony is the main threat to broad novelty; only the precisely delimited minimum/reentry assertion is assessed.
