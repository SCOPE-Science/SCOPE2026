# Correctness
PASS. The exact definitions specify whether neighborhoods are open or closed, whether a defensive replacement requires total domination, and when token exchanges are allowed. Two independently implemented exhaustive enumerations check all 139264 subsets and agree on zero double/secure-total discrepancies and each polynomial coefficient. Both build exchange graphs from the complete minimum-set lists and recover the same exact component and distance results. The affine orbit computation and a direct neighbor check give an independent structural argument for the order-17 matching decomposition. The finite certificate and runnable source code are included.

# Originality
PASS, with bounded uncertainty. The 2007 Benecke–Cockayne–Mynhardt introduction treats paths and forests according to its accessible abstract; a later foundational paper is Klostermeyer and Mynhardt, DOI 10.7151/dmgt.1405; its abstract and bibliographic record were read, but its complete PDF was inaccessible. The full accessible 2019 paper by Cabrera Martínez, Montejano and Rodríguez-Velázquez treats general bounds and does not provide the two Paley-graph polynomials or the exact optimal reconfiguration spaces. Published results for complete multipartite graphs do not cover Paley graphs. Searches for Paley/double domination, Paley/secure-total domination, reconfiguration aliases, and exact order-17 count and component pattern revealed no cover; absence from search is not proof of priority. Residual overlap in inaccessible text, supplementary tables or unindexed records is possible.

# Value
PASS. This is a motivated pair of naturally symmetric quadratic-residue graphs and two non-equivalent defensive notions. The exact coefficientwise equality for all subsets is substantially stronger than a comparison of minimum numbers and is not a general theorem (the six-cycle is an explicit counterexample to the converse). The opposite mobility outcomes for the minimum sets distinguish optimal coverage from reconfigurability even after unrestricted moves. The results yield complete finite classifications with explicit boundary of applicability.

# Closest literature and limitations
Benecke, Cockayne and Mynhardt (2007) introduced secure-total domination (abstract/metadata accessible). Klostermeyer and Mynhardt (2008), DOI 10.7151/dmgt.1405, provide foundational definitions and general bounds; their complete text could not be inspected. Cabrera Martínez et al. (2019), DOI 10.3390/sym11091165, provide broader secure-total bounds but not the exact Paley enumerations. The result applies to orders 13 and 17, not to every Paley graph. The finite certificates prove only the enumerated finite statements; no publication priority or external audit is asserted.
