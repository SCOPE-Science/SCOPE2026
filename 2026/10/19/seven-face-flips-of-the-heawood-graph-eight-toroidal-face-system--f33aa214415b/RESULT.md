# Seven-face flips of the Heawood graph: eight toroidal face systems and frozen matchings

## Finding
Let \(H\) be the Heawood graph, the point–line incidence graph of the Fano plane. A *hexagonal toroidal face system* is a seven-element set \(\mathscr F\) of distinct simple six-cycles which bounds an orientable cellular embedding of \(H\) with precisely those seven faces; orientation-reversed embeddings are not distinguished. Let \(\mathcal R(H;\mathscr F)\) have all perfect matchings of \(H\) as vertices, with \(M\) and \(M'\) adjacent exactly when \(M\triangle M'\) is the edge set of one face in \(\mathscr F\).

There are exactly **eight** such face systems. For each, the facial resonance graph is
\[
\mathcal R(H;\mathscr F)\cong 3C_7\,\sqcup\,3K_1.
\]
Its 24 vertices comprise three disjoint seven-cycles and three isolated (frozen) perfect matchings; it has 21 edges. Each of its seven faces labels exactly one edge in each of the three \(C_7\) components.

The three frozen matchings of every face system partition all 21 graph edges: they form a one-factorization. The assignment **face system \(\mapsto\) frozen one-factorization** is a bijection between the eight hexagonal toroidal face systems and the eight unordered one-factorizations of \(H\). Each of the 24 perfect matchings is frozen for exactly one face system. Each of the 28 six-cycles belongs to precisely two face systems, and each of the 84 distinct six-cycle-flip adjacencies on perfect matchings appears in precisely two facial resonance graphs.

The odd girth of each nontrivial facial resonance component is exactly seven. More precisely, for **every** proper subset \(\mathscr S\subsetneq\mathscr F\) of the seven faces, the graph \(\mathcal R(H;\mathscr S)\) is a forest with precisely \(24-3|\mathscr S|\) connected components; at the full set it instead has six components and three odd cycles. Thus the all-faces resonance graph can be non-bipartite even for a cubic bipartite graph on the torus, unlike the proper-face-subset situation.

## Assumptions and scope
The points of the Fano plane are \(P=\{1,\ldots,7\}\), interpreted as the nonzero three-bit vectors. Its seven lines are the distinct triples \(\{a,b,a\oplus b\}\), where \(\oplus\) is bitwise XOR. A graph vertex is either a point or a line, with an edge \((p,\ell)\) when \(p\in\ell\). Hence \(H\) has 14 vertices, 21 edges, and degree three. Face boundaries here must be seven **distinct simple six-cycles**. No assertion is made about embeddings whose face boundaries are nonsimple closed walks or about other graph families. The 24 perfect matchings are taken without color labels.

A six-cycle is \(M\)-alternating if exactly three of its six edges belong to \(M\); because \(M\) is a matching, these edges necessarily alternate. Toggling all six edges produces the unique opposite perfect matching. A matching is *frozen for* \(\mathscr F\) precisely when it is incident with no such facial toggle. A one-factorization is an unordered triple of edge-disjoint perfect matchings covering \(E(H)\).

## Proof
**Hexagon and embedding completeness.** Every six-cycle contains exactly three point vertices and three line vertices, each incident within the cycle with exactly two of its selected six edges. Conversely, any six incidences on three points and three lines with all six induced degrees two form a single six-cycle: a disconnected union of even cycles would require a four-cycle, and the Fano incidence graph has no four-cycle. Enumerating the \(\binom{21}{6}\) possible six-edge subsets subject to this degree test yields exactly 28 cycles. The second verifier instead enumerates pairs of three-point and three-line selections using the cyclic Fano difference set.

At each cubic vertex there are exactly three unordered consecutive incident-edge pairs (*angles*), giving 42 vertex-angles in total. Every simple six-cycle traverses six distinct angles. In a seven-hexagon cellular face system, these seven six-element angle sets form an exact partition of all 42 angles. Conversely, such a partition glues seven hexagonal disks along every graph edge exactly twice, with every vertex link a circle. It therefore yields a closed connected cellular surface with Euler characteristic \(14-21+7=0\). One checks orientability by assigning coherent orientations to all seven face boundaries so that each shared edge is traversed in opposite directions; orientability then forces the surface to be the torus.

For a reproducible exhaustive count, construct the 28 six-angle sets. The exact-cover recurrence starts from the 42 unused angles; choose one unused angle having the fewest fully admissible six-angle sets, branch over every such set, and delete its six angles. At zero remaining angles retain exactly seven selected cycles. This recurrence misses no admissible partition because every cover must contain one of the branches covering the chosen angle, and includes none with repeated angles. It returns eight face systems. For each, the orientation constraints have a consistent solution, and every pair of distinct faces shares exactly one original graph edge; the face-dual is \(K_7\). All enumerated finite objects, incidence maps, and eight systems are specified in `HEAWOOD_CERTIFICATE.json` and regenerated by `verify_torus_main.py`. An independently represented and independently enumerated cyclic-difference-set implementation is `verify_torus_independent.py`.

**Matching and flip completeness.** Recursively assign to each point exactly one incident line, excluding previously used lines. This gives exactly 24 perfect matchings. Each matching/face pair is checked for the precise six-edge symmetric-difference condition above, thereby constructing every legal edge of \(\mathcal R(H;\mathscr F)\); there are no other moves by definition. For each of the eight face systems, the resulting 24-vertex adjacency graph has three seven-vertex degree-two connected components and three isolated vertices, hence \(3C_7\sqcup3K_1\). Every face provides three distinct toggles, one in each of the seven-cycles; each seven-cycle uses the seven distinct face labels once.

A direct enumeration of triples of pairwise edge-disjoint matchings gives exactly eight one-factorizations. In each face system the triple of isolated vertices equals one of these, and the eight triples are pairwise different and exhaust all eight one-factorizations. This proves the stated bijection, and since the eight triples partition all 24 matchings, proves unique freezing. Across the eight systems, each of the 28 six-cycles occurs twice. Independently enumerating flips against **all** 28 six-cycles gives 84 distinct matching adjacencies (each directed arc is counted twice before deduplication). These are precisely the union of the facial adjacencies, each appearing in two systems. The certificate records each of the 24 matchings, 28 hexagons, and eight face systems with their frozen triples and all 21 face-switch edges.

**Why seven is the natural odd length.** For any closed walk of face toggles, the XOR of its toggled face boundaries is empty, because the final matching equals the initial one. Every edge of the embedded graph belongs to exactly two distinct faces, so this XOR vanishes only when the parity of the number of toggles is equal for all seven faces (the face-dual is connected). Any odd closed walk must therefore toggle every face an odd number of times, requiring at least seven steps. The three exhibited seven-cycles attain this bound and toggle every face once. In each seven-cycle every edge has a distinct face label; choosing a proper subset of faces therefore deletes at least one edge from each cycle and leaves a forest. Exactly three new flip edges occur per retained face, so on 24 vertices the forest has \(24-3|\mathscr S|\) components. This shows why using *all* faces, rather than a proper subset, is essential to the odd cycle.

## Verification
Run the two self-contained standard-library programs from the same directory:

```
python3 verify_torus_main.py HEAWOOD_CERTIFICATE.json
python3 verify_torus_independent.py
```

The main program exhaustively enumerates point–line perfect matchings, all six-cycles, exact angle covers, orientability constraints, face intersections, one-factorizations, connected components, edge multiplicities, and all explicit certificate entries. The second program constructs the Fano plane from the cyclic \((7,3,1)\) difference set, enumerates perfect matchings by permutations and six-cycles by point/line triples, and independently checks the face-cover and flip classifications using integer edge masks. Their two graph constructions are independent; both are finite exhaustive algorithms, not sampling or extrapolation. The certificate is data, not a substitute for the exhaustive recurrence and its checks.

## Relationship to prior work
The existence of Heawood's seven-hexagon torus map is classical. Shiu–Lam–Zhang (2005) study \(k\)-resonance in toroidal polyhexes with two lattice dimensions at least two; Zhang–Ye (first online September 2007) extend \(k\)-resonance to the one-layer family. Their resonance criteria address the existence of perfect matchings alternating on chosen faces, not the complete graph of individual face-flip transitions on the 24 matchings. Ye–Zhang (2014) discuss the Heawood graph's Pfaffian torus embedding and face width, not its facial reconfiguration components.

Tratnik–Ye (arXiv:1710.00761, Proposition 3.2 and Theorem 3.5) prove bipartiteness and induced-hypercube representability for resonance graphs using a **proper subset of all faces**; their separate full-face nonbipartite example is a small plane graph, not the Heawood torus. The seven-cycle classification here provides a different, symmetric full-face boundary example.

Klin–Muzychuk–Reichard (arXiv:1911.06160, Propositions 13.2–13.6) **already establish** the 24 perfect matchings, their eight disjoint one-factorization triples, and three seven-regular Klein association graphs. Those counts and the broader Klein graphs are not claimed as new. They do not identify the seven facial flips of each of the eight toroidal face systems with the much sparser \(3C_7\sqcup3K_1\) graph, nor establish the correspondence between its frozen triple and its embedding or the twofold cover by face systems. A seven-regular graph on 24 matchings is not the same statement as an embedding-dependent graph having three seven-cycles and three isolated matchings.

## Limitations
This is an exact finite classification of a single 14-vertex graph, not a theorem about all toroidal polyhexes. The eight face systems are distinguished by their labeled hexagonal boundary sets (not by an arbitrary choice of coordinates or orientation); no claim about all isotopy classes of arbitrary nonsimple-face embeddings is made. Exact exhaustive checks require the supplied code and definitions and have not been independently audited. Historical priority beyond the inspected literature is not certified: earlier unindexed or supplemental dimer-flip tables may contain equivalent embedding-specific data. The structural relation with the previously published Klein graphs is intentionally distinguished from any claim of first discovering their 24-state structure.
