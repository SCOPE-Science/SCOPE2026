# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem is supported by a complete all-size proof: the unmatched-set intersection \(T_k\) has a three-branch rational transition law. Its first and second falling-factorial moments yield the explicit harmonic error by telescoping. No finite enumeration is used to justify the infinite limit. The separately implemented exact checker compares independent graph-state and reduced-state recurrences for \(n=2,\ldots,7\), all \(720\) edge orders for \(n=3\), and exact moment/bound inequalities for \(n=2,\ldots,17\).

## Originality — PASS, with limits
Searches examined equivalent derangement sampling, random greedy matching, graph random adsorption and published crown-graph records. Mendonça's fixed-row derangement sampler has a different transition rule and does not imply the present random-edge prefactor. Dyer–Frieze addresses general random matching, and Pippenger chiefly sparse-local-structure adsorption; accessible claims do not cover the specific dense crown probability and harmonic bound. Publisher abstracts, rather than complete texts, were available for the older papers; an earlier equivalent result is possible.

## Value — PASS
Uniform-priority dimer adsorption on a complete bipartite graph is always perfectly covering; deleting a single perfect matching preserves perfect matchability while generating genuine rare greedy jamming. The exact \(2/n\) failure law and a quantitative finite-size bound provide a natural benchmark for dense-substrate defects, whose failure remains invisible to leading coverage density.

## Scientific limitations
The theorem is for uniform random priorities and one missing perfect matching, not general defect sets or nonuniform deposition. A complete historical uniqueness claim and independent external validation are not made.
