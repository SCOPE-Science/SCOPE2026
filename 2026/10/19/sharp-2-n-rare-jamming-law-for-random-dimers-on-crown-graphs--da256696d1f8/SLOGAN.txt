For uniformly prioritized irreversible dimers on the crown graph \(K_{n,n}\) with one perfect matching deleted, the probability of leaving two vacancies is \(P_n=2/n+O(\log n/n^2)\), with explicit two-sided finite-size bounds. Every jammed state otherwise has perfect coverage, so this natural dense-graph defect creates a rare but quantitatively sharp adsorption failure despite the existence of perfect matchings.
