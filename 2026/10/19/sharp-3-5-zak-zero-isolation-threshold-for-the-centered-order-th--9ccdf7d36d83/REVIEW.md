# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness
PASS. The interval \(3/5\le\lambda\le1\) is controlled by a monotonicity inequality for the imaginary part, strict positivity of the position boundary, and an exact Poisson-summation rescaling of the half-frequency fiber. The rescaled fiber is evaluated piecewise, including both endpoints, and has exactly one zero. The lower sharpness claim uses a separate exact rescaling. The proof is analytic for all real parameters; finite verification is supplementary.

## Originality
PASS under a bounded source comparison. The closest primary publication, Hasannasab--Lemvig (arXiv:2609.13116), characterizes higher-order cardinal spline Zak zeros for scale greater than one; the present interval is below one and supplies the missing full zero exclusion for order three. Lemvig--Nielsen (arXiv:1507.03982) and Lemvig (arXiv:2601.07642) establish Gabor obstructions rather than an all-position, all-frequency one-zero classification in this range. Kloos--Stöckler (arXiv:1311.7359) prove a one-zero theorem for a distinct totally-positive/exponential-spline regime; equivalence at the noninteger rescalings considered here is not implied by the inspected scope. Historical-priority risk from old Zak-zero literature remains, and the horizontal-segment obstruction itself is expressly credited rather than claimed as new.

## Value
PASS. The precise loss of zero isolation is a natural boundary for Fourier phase and Gabor-window analysis. The theorem closes an infinite continuous parameter interval on the unclassified side of the motivating source, rules out all previously possible hidden zeros, and pinpoints the sharp transition from one zero to a positive-dimensional zero fiber, without claiming a full lower-scale classification.

## Scientific limitations
No statement is established for every scale below \(3/5\) or for orders greater than three. No external review or independent audit has been conducted; the originality comparison is bounded.
