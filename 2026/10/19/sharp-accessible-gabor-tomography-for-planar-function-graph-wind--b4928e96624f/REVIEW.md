# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The claim specifies an odd field of order at least five, a planar graph, full Weyl orbit, normalized Hilbert-space counting measure, and the forced accessible Weyl-coordinate operator subspace. The Gabor projector Gram spectrum is diagonalized by Weyl commutator characters. Planarity makes every nonvertical, nonzero translate overlap a singleton; this reduces the smallest relevant ambiguity to the product of the two smallest graph masses. The exact probability lemma, including its case split, cubic factorization, derivative bound, and lower-root sign, gives the universal bound. Parseval forces a Fourier coefficient of magnitude at most \(1/(q+1)\). Equality yields the one-doubled mass pattern, while the planar bent-character identity proves sufficiency for every finite odd field of order at least five. Checker experiments separately verify all claimed small-field ambiguity patterns and spectral normalizations. Finite computations do not replace the unrestricted proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority uncertainty
Feichtinger–Kozek–Luef supply finite-abelian spreading calculus; Bojarovska–Flinth already supply general Gabor frame bounds; Goldberger–Kang–Okoudjou establish the projector-Gram eigenvalue formula and arbitrary-window rank. Pott–Schmidt–Zhou give the planar-function/relative-difference-set equivalence. Accordingly, this result does **not** claim novelty for those component lemmas or for the uniform-window spectral corollary. The inspected texts and two close published works (cyclic two-sparse multi-window span capacity; a nontiling Gabor orthonormal basis) do not give the constrained sharp \((q+1)^2\) optimum on planar graph supports or classify all optimal weights. Their statements do not imply the probability lemma or its equality cases without additional optimization. Residual risks include an overlooked formulation in older finite-frame design or relative-difference-set optimization literature, and unindexed material. Independent priority certification has not been performed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A graph of a planar function is a canonical relative difference set with mathematically forced missing time-frequency directions. Instead of an arbitrary support slice, the accessible subspace is maximal by exact support geometry. Determining its best possible Gabor tomography conditioning and all optimal windows gives a natural exact design invariant uniform over every planar function, including nonlinear examples, and quantifies the price of sparse relative-difference-set support. The nontrivial sharp probability inequality shows the optimum is not a mere restatement of the known Gabor rank criterion. The theorem deliberately makes no assertion of full-space quantum informational completeness.
