**Originality — PASS with residual risks.** The closely matching passive anisotropic colloid source (Chaki, Olsen and Löwen, arXiv:2406.17550, Section V) resets to a fixed orientation and addresses second spatial moments; the fixed-direction reset is excluded by the higher-order isotropy conditions. Baouche et al. (arXiv:2405.06769) formulate a more general orientation-reset distribution in an active swimmer with isotropic translational noise, but specialize their calculations to a fixed-angle law and identify variable laws as a further direction. The active follow-up (Ghosh and Mandal, arXiv:2501.05149) uses a self-propelled model and a fixed reset direction. Peherstorfer's circle quadrature theory covers the *static* finite-support Fourier moment comparison; that lemma is not claimed as new. The contribution assessed here is the exact dynamical if-and-only-if correspondence and its sharp physical reset-alphabet/cumulant implications. A broader additive-functional or harmonic quadrature statement might implicitly contain part of the dynamics, and independent priority confirmation is still required.

**Value — PASS.** The colloid's second-moment anisotropy under orientation resetting is experimentally motivated, but an isotropic covariance can conceal higher-order anisotropy. The theorem tells how many different resetting axes are necessary and sufficient to eliminate that signature through a chosen order *for every observation time*, proves sharpness including the equality protocol, and exhibits the surviving fourth cumulant and diagnostic sixth-harmonic leakage. This is a nontrivial protocol-design and moment-identifiability result, not a static quadrature restatement alone. It does not promise full distributional isotropy.
