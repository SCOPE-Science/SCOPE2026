# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The four-level centered eigenvector, grounded-matrix interlacing, and positive-semidefinite dual witness prove the exact optimal value and unrestricted uniqueness in the closed threshold region. The strict exterior is proved by a budget-preserving perturbation with positive derivatives for both eigenvalue branches meeting at the tree allocation. Every quantifier includes integers \(q\ge2\), \(p>2q\), arbitrary \(B>0\), and independent nonnegative weights. Exact arithmetic checks from `verify.py` corroborate the critical identities; no finite sweep is used as a proof of the infinite result.

**Originality — PASS with residual risk.** The 2006 Sun--Boyd--Diaconis--Xiao paper establishes the general convex framework and not the explicit imbalanced barbell phase boundary. The familiar sparse bridge-and-spoke candidate in symmetric and moderately imbalanced designs does not imply this theorem: it analyzes strictly larger-clique ratios and establishes both a new exact exterior interval and a strict, necessary chord-activation threshold. The 1990 Fiedler tree theorem can cover a tree subproblem but does not by itself establish the barbell-wide chord-activation statement. Ghosh--Boyd--Saberi treats effective resistance, and Jafarizadeh--Jamalipour treats discrete-time SLEM. A plausible specialized paper on barbell connectivity and the original Fiedler full text were not fully accessible; coverage elsewhere remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** Allocating scarce coupling strengths on two densely connected communities joined by a bottleneck is a natural continuous-time diffusion and consensus problem. The result resolves a meaningful previously open imbalanced parameter interval, gives a unique global allocation there, and proves an exact boundary at which formerly useless within-community edges become indispensable. Neither correctness, novelty of notation, nor a finite table alone establishes this value; the mathematical significance is the sharp sparsity-to-chord activation transition.

**Scientific limits.** The exact optimizer above the transition remains unknown in this result. The analysis assumes complete cliques, linear uniform edge costs, no per-edge caps, and nonnegative rather than strictly positive allowed weights. Independent mathematical audit remains outstanding.
