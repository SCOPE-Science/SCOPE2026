## correctness

PASS

Integrating the order-one Cauchy kernel gives the signed simple-pole endpoint sum. With the common denominator, the numerator has degree exactly 2n−2 and is nonzero at all endpoints, so n−1 upper-half-plane zeros counted with multiplicity is equivalent to no real zeros. Outside the pulses every summand is positive. In pulse k the sign is the sign of Φ_k−w_k, with strictly negative limiting sign at each interior endpoint. This gives necessity including equality and sufficiency of the strict maximum inequalities. They are homogeneous linear tests indexed by compact real intervals; the maxima are continuous functions of w. For equal positive heights, the contribution from the other pulses is strictly smaller than the integral of the positive kernel over the entire complement of pulse k. That latter integral equals the absolute value of the negative own-pulse summand, independently confirming strict feasibility. Mixing any positive weakly feasible vector with the equal-height vector shows that the positive-relative boundary consists exactly of weakly feasible vectors with at least one tight inequality; at the interior maximizer both F and its real derivative vanish. This is not a converse for arbitrary multiple real roots. For two pulses, computing the quadratic's discriminant gives the stated strict gap test and its double and distinct real regimes. As an exact threshold check, intervals [−1,0], [1/4,5/4] with weights (4,1) give P(z)=5(z−1/2)^2. The one-pulse case has no zeros and no boundary inside the positive orthant.
