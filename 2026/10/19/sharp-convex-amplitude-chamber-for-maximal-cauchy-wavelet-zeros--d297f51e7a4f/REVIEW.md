# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof includes explicit hypotheses and interval ordering, the exact rational transform and numerator degree, noncancelling poles, the sign criterion, the convex-cone argument, the equal-height interlacing certificate, and a completed-square two-pulse discriminant. Equality at a chamber-boundary maximizer yields a real zero of multiplicity at least two; this is the precise assertion, not an unwarranted generic-double statement for arbitrary pulse count. Ninety-six exact rational cases were tested with a standalone symbolic checker. This finite test is supplementary to the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
The closest inspected full sources are Mallat–Waldspurger (arxiv:1404.1183, 2014), which defines the analytic Cauchy-wavelet/phase-retrieval framework, and Abakumov–Baranov–Belov (arxiv:1312.6706, 2013), which investigates zeros in spaces of Cauchy transforms of discrete measures. Neither inspected statement implies the amplitude cone or the two-pulse sharp threshold. The equal-height interlacing formula is a classical rational-function mechanism and is not presented as a novelty by itself. A more general theorem in older critical-point literature could give an equivalent characterization and remains the main publication-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The all-pulse chamber gives an exact answer to when a positive rectangular waveform has the maximal possible analytic wavelet zeros, simultaneously supplying a convex parameter region and a completely explicit sharp two-pulse contrast/separation law. Both measure a mathematically natural oscillatory signal feature; the assertion covers continuously many geometries and amplitudes and is not a computational table of arbitrary parameters.

## Limitations and nearest work
Only strictly separated positive rectangular pulses and the first-order analytic Cauchy wavelet are included. The general boundary is implicit through one-dimensional interval maxima. Possible higher multiple zeros for larger pulse counts are not classified. There are no data-noise stability claims, and publication priority awaits independent bibliographic verification.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
