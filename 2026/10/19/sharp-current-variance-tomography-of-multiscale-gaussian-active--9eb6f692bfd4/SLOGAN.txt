In a homogeneous Poisson gas driven by a sum of \(N\) stationary Ornstein–Uhlenbeck propulsions, exactly \(2N+1\) ideal net-current variance measurements determine every positive relaxation rate and amplitude, together with the unknown thermal diffusivity; this predetermined-measurement count is sharp. Arbitrary Gaussian propulsion is identifiable from the entire variance curve but not from finitely many time points, and unrestricted high-frequency noise makes the inverse unstable.
