# Scientific review: sharp dispersion envelope for biased periodic capture

**Correctness (PASS).** The scope fixes a one-dimensional free Brownian particle, rate-\(r\) instantaneous capture and the half-open asymmetric cell partition. Independent exponential-renewal cycles give a two-sided Laplace displacement, geometric capture-index tails, and the exact drift and second moment. The reward-duration covariance cannot be omitted: differentiating the rate-Laplace mixture produces the \(z m\partial_zm\) term in effective diffusivity. The strict gate-order comparison follows from an explicit derivative and the elementary bounds \(0<(s\coth s)'<\tanh s\), with endpoint equalities at symmetric and maximally directed gates. All rate and asymmetry boundaries have been checked. Finite numerical checks corroborate, not prove, the assertions. Risks are limited to the explicitly idealized model and the published regenerative central-limit theorem's standard finite-moment conditions, met here.

**Originality (PASS, subject to independent priority confirmation).** Ghosh et al. (arXiv:2307.06202v1, 2023) propose local well-reset-induced ratcheting. Roberts–Sezik–Lardet (arXiv:2405.10698v1, 2024) derive exact *currents* in a related switching-ratchet model, so the drift is not claimed as new. Alston–Bertrand (arXiv:2401.12772v1, 2024) derive the *symmetric nearest-site* diffusivity and an optimal placement of target sites: it is exactly the lower equality case, hence also not claimed as new. The new statement is the asymmetric instantaneous-capture diffusivity with rate/reward covariance, the strict monotone envelope as gates move off center, and both sharp endpoints including the singular strong-reset behavior. Examined broader ratchet and asymmetric-resetting sources operate with different generators or resetting rules. Semantic database searches and public literature search have not revealed a stronger covering inequality, but that is not a proof of worldwide uniqueness. Residual risk: unpublished, older, or differently phrased renewal-reward transport results may imply the envelope.

**Scientific value (PASS).** Biased capture basins are a natural control parameter for instantaneous molecular-ratchet idealizations. The result determines the exact drift–dispersion tradeoff under a fixed lattice and reset cadence, not a special numeric case. It shows a sharp, strictly increasing dispersion penalty for bias, proves both attainable extremal protocols, and identifies a singular difference between zero-width and positive-width opposing basins at rapid capture. Symmetric-site diffusion enhancement was already known; the new physical conclusion is the uniform geometry envelope and its rigorous asymmetry ordering. No claim is made for a general finite-relaxation ratchet, and no unique intermediate capture-rate crossover is asserted.

Rev. E 65, 031104 (2002), different tilted-potential process. Standard renewal-reward formulas are prior ingredients.
