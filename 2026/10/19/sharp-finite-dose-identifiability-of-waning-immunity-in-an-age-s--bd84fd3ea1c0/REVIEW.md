# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The proof independently identifies the forward equilibrium normalization in Yang–Saldaña–Scoglio Eq. (4) and reconstructs its inversion. For the hyperexponential class, the difference of two normalized rational transforms has numerator degree at most \(2k-2\), proving sufficiency of \(2k-1\) distinct measurements. The necessity proof checks full row rank by a rational function with forbidden \(k\) distinct double zeros and applies the implicit function theorem inside the positive-parameter region. Nonparametric uniqueness follows from analyticity and determinacy of moments on \([0,1]\); finite-panel nonuniqueness uses disjoint smooth bump functions and preserves positivity, normalization, mean, and admissible hazard. A separate infectivity example validates selective identification limits. The finite program is supportive rather than an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS with priority qualification. The 2023 primary source provides the forward epidemic formula; that formula is NOT a new contribution. The claim here is a *sharp inverse* sampling threshold, complete finite-mixture uniqueness, continuum ambiguity with one fewer sample, and unrestricted smooth-law nonidentification from any finite panel. Standard partial fractions, rational interpolation, implicit-function, and Laplace uniqueness are acknowledged mathematical prerequisites; the clinical-model-specific inversion and sharp observation boundary were not located in checked literature or mathematical research records. The closest stochastic waning-rate inference source addresses epidemic time series rather than exact vaccination-indexed equilibria. A directly covering published inverse-problem theorem or a specialization of general rational interpolation could affect priority, and no independent literature audit has been performed.

## Value
PASS. The forward SIRVS equilibrium formula suggests varying vaccination intensity as an observational probe, but whether finitely many equilibria identify a nonexponential recovered-immunity law is a nontrivial experimental-design question. The exact \(2k-1\) threshold establishes an achievable finite protocol under a natural smooth mixture-of-exponentials duration family, while the smooth nonparametric construction proves the impossibility of unrestricted identification from finitely many regimes even with the mean known. Both assertions are model-relevant and form a sharp boundary, rather than an arbitrary slice or numerical example.
