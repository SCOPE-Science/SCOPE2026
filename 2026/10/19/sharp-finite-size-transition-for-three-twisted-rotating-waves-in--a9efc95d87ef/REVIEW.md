# Mathematical review

## Correctness
PASS (self-review). The exact directed circulant linearization reduces orbital stability to the sign of the real first-mode factor after uniform positivity is established for every other mode. For fixed low frequencies, all-angle bounds produce strict continuum margins; Abel summation handles the unbounded frequencies. The first-mode exact trigonometric sum, its \(C^1\)-uniform midpoint expansion and an implicit root argument establish uniqueness and the two correction coefficients. The standalone numerical test agrees for explicitly checked finite degrees, but the infinite result depends on the written proof.

## Originality
PASS under the accessible-source comparison, with priority risk. Sun–Wang–Dong's Fourier stability/instability criterion and the two lowest-winding classifications are prior. Bick–Böhle–Kuehn establish continuum stability for symmetric counterparts and discuss finite-size displacement; Sinha et al. give general finite-ring spectra and show that a numerical third-twist state can be misclassified by continuum theory. Their displayed statements do not give the present uniform all-large-degree classification with an exact second correction. The continuum limiting root alone is **not** presented as novel, nor is the generic Fourier criterion. The same real-part spectra under symmetrization create the principal residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The first unresolved higher-twist branch of a recent directed-network analysis receives a sharp threshold for every sufficiently large degree, including a closed form for the limiting connectivity constant, a half-neighbor finite-size correction, and a nonzero next coefficient. This gives a concrete connectivity budget and quantifies the finite-versus-continuum discrepancy; it is not a single numerical instance. The theorem does not address finite degrees below an unspecified threshold, exact boundary nonlinear dynamics, or other coupling ratios.
