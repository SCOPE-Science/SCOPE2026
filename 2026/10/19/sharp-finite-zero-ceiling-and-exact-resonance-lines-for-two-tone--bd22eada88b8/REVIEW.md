# Scientific review of the two-tone Hermite-window zero theorem

**Correctness (PASS).** Direct Gaussian Fourier computation gives the exact two-tone STFT with two real coefficient functions. Their only possible nonresonant vanishing phases occur on two time slices in one zero-pattern period, whose root multiplicities can be bounded by a generalized Rolle argument for the difference of two degree-\(n\) polynomial–exponentials. Parity sharpens the sum of individual bounds to \(4n+1\). Every root-gap resonance produces exactly the stated full time line and there are no other nonisolated components. Separation of shifted Hermite root neighborhoods and the implicit function theorem prove eventual attainment with distinct nonsingular points. The standard-library script's completed 95 checks corroborate the formulas but are not used as an infinite proof.

**Originality (PASS, qualified).** Bellourak et al. (arXiv:2610.13531v1) study white-noise Hermite spectrogram-zero statistics; their almost-sure isolation result does not imply a deterministic resonance-line classification. Chand et al. (arXiv:2601.10910) study two-tone Gaussian-window interference, not arbitrary Hermite orders. Alaifari and Yang (arXiv:2410.05486) use Hermite windows for phase-retrieval coverage, not deterministic two-tone zero envelopes. A prior first-Hermite two-tone classification covers order one but neither higher-order line resonance nor all-order optimal bounds. Search comparisons also examined published related finite STFT and Hermite spectral records without finding an implication of the final claim. The Rolle-type zero bound and Hermite-root facts are classical inputs, not separately claimed original. The remaining risk is unindexed prior literature on polynomial–Gaussian two-tone spectrograms, and relevant abstracts without accessible complete texts.

**Value (PASS).** The result is a sharp, all-orders spectral-complexity statement for a natural two-frequency benchmark, with an exact finite exceptional set that changes isolated zeros into complete zero lines for higher Hermite windows. It distinguishes stochastic almost-sure isolation from deterministic resonance and identifies the exact arithmetic-free root-gap geometry needed to avoid flat spectrogram zeros in Hermite-based interference experiments. The combination of an attained \(4n+1\) ceiling and a complete classification of the nonisolated set is more than an isolated parameter computation or a textbook restatement.

**Scope and risks.** The theorem does not enumerate isolated zero counts at every intermediate separation or their phase indices. Multiplicity means the explicitly defined scalar real root order. The non-square-integrable two-tone input is legitimate because the STFT integrals are absolutely convergent. Historical priority and independent checking remain open.
