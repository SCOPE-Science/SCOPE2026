# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For distinct sample roots, the generalized four-by-four Fourier determinant is the nonzero Vandermonde times the complete homogeneous polynomial \(h_2\). Each root of order dividing \(2p^k\) splits into an independent sign and a \(p^k\)-root. A vanishing Schur quotient yields an integer polynomial divisible by the cyclotomic polynomial \(\Phi_{p^k}\), and evaluation at one forces the odd prime \(p\) to divide a signed value in \(\{2,4,10\}\). Therefore \(p=5\) is the only possible obstruction. At five, four distinct fifth-root columns make rows zero and five equal. This proves both directions for every odd prime and positive exponent; the finite checker is corroboration only.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The principal pre-existing source, Alexeev–Cahill–Mixon, *Full Spark Frames* (arXiv:1110.3548, first public October 17, 2011), develops the Schur/cyclotomic device and settles the prime-power case. Its main equivalence does not extend automatically to the composite modulus \(2p^k\); it exhibits limitations of residue-uniformity tests for composite orders. The present exact classification for mask \(\{0,1,2,5\}\) uses the additional signed-Schur obstruction and treats every exponent simultaneously. Published records for masks \(\{0,1,2,4\}\) and \(\{0,1,3,4\}\) are non-equivalent. Modulus-ten and modulus-fourteen censuses imply individual instances only. Generalization of the cyclotomic reduction itself is closely related to known arguments and is not claimed to be wholly unprecedented.

Relevant primary text, including Theorem 9 and Lemmas 10–11 and a composite-order example, was inspected. Published-record comparisons were limited to search summaries where their full texts were inaccessible; a more general earlier theorem could subsume this statement. Literature-search failure does not prove novelty. Exact priority remains a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The first degree-two Schur perturbation of the consecutive four-row band, following the known degree-one even-order failure, gives an explicit infinite full-spark family on even composite cyclic orders, outside the prime-power sufficiency theorem, and isolates exactly one exceptional odd prime. Full spark is a substantive exact interpolation and sparse-identifiability property: any four columns form a basis for the four-dimensional data space, and no nonzero vector of column support at most four lies in the kernel. The signed-Schur method explains the arithmetic exception and contrasts with nearby masks that are never full spark for even orders.

## Limits
The result does not estimate stability under noise, does not classify arbitrary four-row masks, and does not claim a general converse to the signed-Schur criterion. Mathematical review is not independent verification.
