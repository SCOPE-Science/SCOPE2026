For distinct column roots the Vandermonde factor is nonzero. The alternant quotient is an integer Schur polynomial, equal to h₂ for exponents 0,1,2,5. Every 2p^k-th root has a unique product representation as a sign times a p^k-th root. A vanishing Schur value therefore makes the integer polynomial formed by replacing the p^k-th root with X divisible by Φ_{p^k}; evaluation at 1 gives a signed Schur value divisible by p. This proves the general sufficient criterion, with distinct rows and N greater than the largest row. Here h₂ on four signs is (4+(sum of signs)^2)/2, taking values 2,4,10. This proves full spark for every odd p other than 5, including N=6. When p=5, four fifth-root columns make rows 0 and 5 identical for every k.
