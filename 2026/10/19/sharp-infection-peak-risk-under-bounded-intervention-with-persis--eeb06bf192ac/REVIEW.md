# Scientific review: sharp infection-peak risk with persistent infected noise

## Correctness — PASS
The global positive solution follows from a pathwise susceptible bound and the exponential representation of infection. The key identity is a nonnegative time integral for \(\log(I_t/Z_t)\), valid under all bounded progressively measurable controls. Full transmission suppression and maximal treatment make this integral identically zero, so a single control realizes the entire lower-envelope process. The Gaussian terminal law, Brownian first-passage density, method-of-images cumulative formula, and infinite-horizon limit establish all stated exact risks. Terminal total-variation separation follows by testing the event \(\{I_T>L\}\). The proof states boundary cases, the random-initial-data extension, and the limitations of the zero-flux interpretation. The accompanying checker independently integrates four finite-time hitting densities and reproduces the stated numerical example. Residual risk: the numerical checker does not prove the stochastic comparison or the reflection lemma, which are justified analytically.

## Originality — PASS
The motivating 2026 controlled SIR/Fokker–Planck paper discusses bounded-domain control and hospital-capacity functionals but does not establish an intervention-uniform, simultaneously sharp Gaussian and first-passage risk for persistent infected-only noise. Its simulations use transmission-proportional noise instead, and therefore do not subsume the claim. Gray et al. (2011) use a common transmission-noise driver and a fixed total in a stochastic SIS model. Chen et al. (2026) derive first-passage distributions for finite-state Markov jump SIS dynamics, not a controlled multiplicative diffusion; only an abstract-level comparison and accessible excerpts were possible for that paper. A related public finding studies covariance under independent versus shared transmission noise, not the first-passage risk and support-barrier laws proved here. The Brownian reflection principle is standard and is expressly not asserted to be novel. No located stronger statement implies the particular all-control optimum or corresponding quantitative support-error floor. Residual risk: unindexed diffusion-control literature could contain an equivalent specialization; full text of the Chen et al. source remained inaccessible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The capacity-threshold objective is explicit in the motivating control paper. This theorem gives its previously unavailable exact mathematical floor in an independently motivated, persistent-noise specialization. The first-passage risk is substantially larger than the terminal tail in a concrete example, revealing why endpoint-only objectives can miss peak overload. All admissible adapted policies are covered and a single attainable policy optimizes every horizon and threshold simultaneously. The nonzero tail also yields a quantitative total-variation floor for bounded-domain probability-law approximations, not just a qualitative observation that Gaussian fluctuations are unbounded. This is a structural boundary result for a natural epidemic-noise modeling choice; it does not purport to establish a universal biological limitation.

## Closest literature and limits
C. Parkinson and S. Roy, *A Fokker-Planck framework for control of epidemics*, arXiv:2601.20181 / DOI:10.1007/s00285-026-02462-7 (bounded Fokker–Planck domain and hospital-capacity objective); A. Gray et al., *A stochastic differential equation SIS epidemic model*, DOI:10.1137/10081856X (different conservation-preserving noise); T. Chen et al., *Exact results for the stochastic SIS epidemic model in densely populated environments*, DOI:10.1137/25M1805321 (finite-state jump-process first passage). The derivation is confined to positive-infected initial states with noise persisting under full transmission suppression and bounded treatment. It does not apply to bounded finite-population chains or to noise that vanishes upon full incidence suppression. The evidence is an analytic proof with supplementary finite numerical checks, not independent validation.
