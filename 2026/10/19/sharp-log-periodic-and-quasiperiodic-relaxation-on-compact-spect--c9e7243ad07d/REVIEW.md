# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** For the compact diagonal generator \(Q_b\), the exact source-ball orbit norm reduces to the maximum of the explicitly computed scalar weights. Their adjacent ratio changes sign exactly once; this proves the maximizing coordinate, not only an estimate. The continuous phase profile has equal endpoints, an interior maximum, and a strictly lower minimum. Approximating prescribed exponential times by integers proves single-scale phase density. Irrational-line density on the two-torus plus integer approximation proves the full two-scale cluster interval. The proof treats index boundaries, ties, parameter ranges, and the distinction between pointwise and uniform orbit decay. The self-contained checker provides finite consistency tests; no infinite-dimensional conclusion relies on those tests.

**Originality — PASS, subject to bibliographic limits.** Bag–Mandal–Das (2026) prove qualitative one-sided asymptotic-expansivity distinctions but no source-ball optimal orbit envelope. The classical normal spectral theorem supplies the supremum reduction, which is not claimed new. Rozendaal–Seifert–Stahn (arXiv:1709.08895v3) address other semigroup norms and resolvent hypotheses; their Theorem 4.4 requires the semigroup generator to have no imaginary-axis spectrum, whereas this compact generator has spectral zero. Directly inspected mathematical records on the boundary spectral-projection criterion, scalar failure-set realization and endpoint maximal regularity have differing statements and do not imply the displayed sharp one-/two-ladder amplitude cluster intervals. The full novelty conclusion is conditional on broader, possibly unindexed spectral-source literature, especially exact lacunary semigroup formulas. General spectral calculus is acknowledged as prior rather than attributed as discovery.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** This is a quantitative resolution of a natural failure of uniform decay for strongly stable infinite-dimensional discrete linear dynamics. The object is the standard spectral fractional-power source ball, not an arbitrary finite slice. The optimal exponent and all sharp amplitude subsequences are determined for an entire continuous family of gap ratios and source exponents, and two incommensurable ladders give a nontrivial exact change of the lower envelope. The formulas quantify when source regularity restores uniform relaxation and how spectral sparsity obstructs an asymptotic constant; the sharp coefficients can calibrate spectral truncation and decay benchmarks. No independent external validation is asserted.

**Closest literature and limits.** Primary motivating source: *Asymptotic Expansive and Hyperbolic Linear Operators*, arXiv:2610.01196v1 (1 October 2026). Other primary text: *Optimal rates of decay for operator semigroups on Hilbert spaces*, arXiv:1709.08895v3. The closely related published normal-operator criteria and finite-dimensional endpoint regularity record were read in full at the claim/proof level. The present result is restricted to diagonal positive compact generators, Hilbert spectral-source balls, integer-time orbits, and rationally independent logarithms for the dual-scale cluster result. A general normal operator need not have this profile.
