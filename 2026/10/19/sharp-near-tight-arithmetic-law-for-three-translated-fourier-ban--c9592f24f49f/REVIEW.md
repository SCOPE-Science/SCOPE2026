# Scientific review

## Correctness (C): PASS
A complete proof reduces the infinite Riesz-basis problem to a three-by-three Gram matrix, derives the equilateral asymptotic and establishes matching upper/lower first-order bounds. The nonstandard lower-bound argument checks all possible near-optimizing phases, not just the proposed shift. The exact matrix values for finite cases were additionally tested. The finite comparisons are corroboration, not infinite proof.

## Originality (O): PASS with documented priority risk
Berman--Feuer (2007) already proves the exact-tightness residue criterion; this portion is acknowledged as prior knowledge. The new claim is solely the sharp \(1/n\) coefficient for the non-tight residue classes after optimization over every continuous shift. The full 2007 primary article, full 2025/2026 Asipchuk--De Carli primary article, related general Vandermonde results, and indexed mathematical findings were compared by implication. None of the inspected theorems supplies the new asymptotic coefficient. A possible unindexed specialization and partial full-text access for ancillary literature remain risks.

## Value (V): PASS
The rate and its exact coefficient quantify the residual instability of a natural sparse three-tile Fourier sampling design when exact orthogonality is forbidden. The result provides an explicit asymptotically optimal design and a global lower bound, not just a construction.

## Limits
Only three integer-frequency cosets with equal step are optimized. No finite-dimensional experiments substitute for the proof; independent review was not performed.
