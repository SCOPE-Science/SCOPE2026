A finite Bernoulli Griffiths-region survival surrogate has a sharp typical-sample cutoff: beyond the natural time scale \( (\log L)^{\beta+1} \), the sample-wise logarithmic decay becomes a linear longest-run law while the disorder mean retains the original stretched-exponential saddle. The two rates separate at the explicit scaled threshold \(u_c=\xi^\beta/\beta\), distinguishing typical experiments from rare samples that dominate ensemble averages.
