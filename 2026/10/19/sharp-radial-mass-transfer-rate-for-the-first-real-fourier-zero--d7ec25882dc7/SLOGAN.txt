Among all equal-volume radial perturbations of the unit ball obtained by exchanging an infinitesimal mass fraction \(t\), the largest first-real-Fourier-zero displacement has an exact sharp coefficient \(C_d\) determined by the first two positive zeros of \(J_{d/2}\). Removing an inner boundary shell and depositing the mass at radius \(j_{d/2,2}/j_{d/2,1}\) attains this first-order optimum in every dimension \(d\ge2\).
