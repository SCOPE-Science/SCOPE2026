# Mathematical assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** Exact finite sampling probabilities follow by choosing the smaller and larger selected ranks around the order statistic. The ratio of successive weights proves unimodality and the interval extremum. A collision coupling and binomial central limit theorem yield the normal-window envelope; the endpoint cases follow from uniform CDF convergence and tightness. The explicit two-column unit-row construction has strictly ordered residuals and a full-rank clean system. Its projection-error formulas and asymptotic expectation are derived algebraically, and the supplied exact-rational checker passed 152 finite configurations. Risks: the result is about one step and the specified sampling rule only.

**Originality — PASS, subject to residual literature risk.** The closest source is Haddock–Ma–Rebrova (arXiv:2308.07987), which defines small-sample rank selection in Algorithm 2 but provides heuristics for that regime rather than the exact sharp adversarial rank envelope or its Gaussian crossover. The foundational full-sample threshold algorithms of Haddock–Needell–Rebrova–Swartworth and Steinerberger use a different selection mechanism. Cai–Chen–Ma–Wu (arXiv:2507.15185) studies a distinct independently drawn update row tested against a sample quantile. Classical order-statistic facts are inputs, not claimed discoveries. An unindexed treatment of the same rank-selected vulnerability remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** The source explicitly motivates analyzing small sampled quantiles. The new result identifies an exact worst-case rank allocation, a sharp \(k^{-1/2}\) corruption-fraction crossover, and a full-column-rank example in which increasing the sample size concentrates updates onto a vanishing fraction of malicious rows. This establishes a concrete design limitation of rank-selected, small-sample quantile filtering without overstating a one-step observation as global divergence.
