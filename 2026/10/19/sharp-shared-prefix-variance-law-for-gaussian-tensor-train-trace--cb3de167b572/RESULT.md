# Sharp shared-prefix variance law for Gaussian tensor-train trace probes

## Finding

Let \(d\geq2\), \(n\geq2\), \(r\geq1\), \(L\geq1\) be integers and \(0\leq k\leq d-1\). Generate \(L\) normalized Gaussian tensor-train (TT) vectors of order \(d\) and constant internal bond rank \(r\), sharing exactly their first \(k\) Gaussian cores and independently resampling all remaining cores. For any real symmetric positive-semidefinite matrix \(A\in\mathbb R^{n^d\times n^d}\), define the trace estimator
\[
H_L(A)=\frac1L\sum_{j=1}^{L}\omega_j^{\mathsf T}A\omega_j.
\]
It is unbiased. Moreover, writing \(v_h=(1+2/r)^h\), its **exact sharp worst-case relative variance** is
\[
\boxed{\ \sup_{A\succeq0,\ \operatorname{tr}A=1}\operatorname{Var}H_L(A)
=\frac{3v_{d-1}-1}{L}+\frac{L-1}{L}(v_k-1).\ }
\]
Equality is attained by every projector \(A=uu^{\mathsf T}\) onto a unit *separable tensor* \(u=u_1\otimes\cdots\otimes u_d\). The bound for general nonzero \(A\succeq0\) is obtained by multiplying the right-hand side by \((\operatorname{tr}A)^2\).

For every such separable rank-one projector, as the number of probes increases while the shared prefix stays fixed,
\[
H_L(A)\longrightarrow S_k\quad\text{almost surely},\qquad
S_k\ \overset{\mathrm d}=\ \prod_{h=1}^{k}\frac{\chi_{r,h}^{2}}{r},
\]
where the chi-square variables are independent and the empty product is one. More precisely, with all chi-square factors on the right mutually independent, the *entire finite-probe law* is
\[
H_L(uu^{\mathsf T})\ \overset{\mathrm d}=\ S_k\,\frac1L\sum_{j=1}^{L}
\left(\chi_{1,j}^2\prod_{h=k+1}^{d-1}\frac{\chi_{r,h,j}^2}{r}\right),
\]
where the factor \(S_k\) is independent of every summand. The independence uses rotational invariance of each fresh Gaussian core, not independence of the probes' shared prefix. Thus for every \(k\geq1\) and finite \(r\), the estimator retains nonzero asymptotic relative mean-square error \(v_k-1\) and is not consistent on these rank-one targets despite remaining unbiased for each \(L\). Completely independent vectors, corresponding to \(k=0\), recover the ordinary inverse-sample variance decay.

There is a sharp proportional-order regime. Suppose \(d\to\infty\), \(r/d\to\alpha\in(0,\infty)\), \(k/d\to\theta\in[0,1]\), and \(L\to\infty\). Then the sharp worst-case relative variance tends to
\[
\boxed{\ \exp(2\theta/\alpha)-1.\ }
\]
For \(\theta>0\), the rank-one limiting prefix random variable also satisfies
\[
\log S_k\ \Longrightarrow\ N(-\theta/\alpha,\ 2\theta/\alpha).
\]
In particular, linear growth of the TT rank with tensor order does not remove the error floor when a positive proportion of cores is reused across all probes.

## Assumptions and scope

For each probe \(j\), the first core \(G_1^{(j)}(i_1)\) is a \(1\times r\) row; intermediate cores \(G_h^{(j)}(i_h)\), \(2\leq h\leq d-1\), are \(r\times r\) matrices; and the final core \(G_d^{(j)}(i_d)\) is an \(r\times1\) column. All scalar core entries have the standard real Gaussian distribution. Distinct scalar entries are independent within any realization, and across probes except that \(G_h^{(1)}=\cdots=G_h^{(L)}\) for \(h\leq k\). For each multi-index \(I=(i_1,\ldots,i_d)\),
\[
\omega_{j,I}=r^{-(d-1)/2}G_1^{(j)}(i_1)G_2^{(j)}(i_2)\cdots G_d^{(j)}(i_d).
\]
The usual repeated-bond-index summation is understood. The normalization ensures \(\mathbb E\omega_j\omega_j^{\mathsf T}=I\). This is a model of **fully shared prefix cores and independent suffix cores**; it is not a claim about arbitrary partially correlated tensor trains, row-orthogonalized TT sketches, or all reuse strategies.

The target \(A\) is deterministic, symmetric and positive semidefinite. No restriction \(r\geq d-1\) is needed for this exact finite-dimensional law. All statements use exact real arithmetic. The variance is over all sampled Gaussian cores, including the common prefix. The limit in \(L\) uses one common random prefix followed by an infinite conditionally independent sequence of suffixes.

## Proof

**1. A partial contraction and its conditional second moment.** Fix a deterministic unit vector \(u\in\mathbb R^{n^d}\). Let \(U_{(h)}\) denote its unfolding into the first \(h\) physical indices and the remaining indices. Let \(\Omega_{\leq h}\) be the (unnormalized) contraction of the first \(h\) TT cores, with its physical indices as rows and its surviving rank index as column. Define
\[
\Pi_h=(U_{(h)})^{\mathsf T}\Omega_{\leq h},\qquad
S_h=r^{-h}\|\Pi_h\|_F^2,\qquad S_0=1.
\]
The matrix \(\Pi_h\) has \(n^{d-h}\) rows and \(r\) columns for \(1\leq h\leq d-1\). For \(Z_j=u^{\mathsf T}\omega_j\), Gaussian independence of the unshared suffix and iterative isotropy of the remaining cores imply, for the sigma field \(\mathcal F_k\) generated by the first \(k\) cores,
\[
\mathbb E[Z_j^2\mid\mathcal F_k]=S_k,\qquad
\mathbb E[Z_j^2]=\mathbb E S_k=1.
\]
At \(k=d-1\), the only remaining core is a Gaussian column. Consequently
\[
\mathbb E[Z_j^4\mid\mathcal F_{d-1}]=3S_{d-1}^2.
\]

**2. Sharp partial contraction moments.** Conditional on \(\mathcal F_{h-1}\), a reshaping \(C\) of \(\Pi_{h-1}\) gives the \(r\) independent columns of \(\Pi_h\) as \(C g_1,\ldots,C g_r\), with independent standard Gaussian \(g_a\). Hence
\[
\mathbb E[\|\Pi_h\|_F^4\mid\mathcal F_{h-1}]
=r^2\|C\|_F^4+2r\operatorname{tr}((CC^{\mathsf T})^2)
\leq r(r+2)\|\Pi_{h-1}\|_F^4.
\]
For \(h=1\), take \(C=U_{(1)}^{\mathsf T}\); its squared Frobenius norm is one. Dividing by \(r^{2h}\), iterating, and using \(S_0=1\) yield
\[
\mathbb E S_h^2\leq(1+2/r)^h=v_h.
\]
If \(u\) is separable, each relevant \(C\) has rank one (almost surely), so \(\operatorname{tr}((CC^{\mathsf T})^2)=\|C\|_F^4\), with equality at every stage. In particular,
\[
\operatorname{Var}(Z_j^2)\leq3v_{d-1}-1,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(S_k)\leq v_k-1,
\]
and both inequalities become equalities for separable \(u\).

**3. Conditional independence converts core reuse into a covariance.** For \(Y_j=\omega_j^{\mathsf T}A\omega_j\), the \(Y_j\) are identically distributed and conditionally independent given \(\mathcal F_k\). Therefore the total-covariance identity gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}H_L(A)=\frac{\operatorname{Var}Y_1}{L}
+\frac{L-1}{L}\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[Y_1\mid\mathcal F_k]).
\]
Write the spectral decomposition \(A=\sum_a\lambda_a u_au_a^{\mathsf T}\), with \(\lambda_a\geq0\) and unit \(u_a\). The centered \(L^2\) triangle inequality and Step 2 give
\[
\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}Y_1}
\leq\sum_a\lambda_a\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}((u_a^{\mathsf T}\omega_1)^2)}
\leq\operatorname{tr}(A)\sqrt{3v_{d-1}-1},
\]
and likewise, since the conditional mean of each quadratic component is \(S_k(u_a)\),
\[
\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[Y_1\mid\mathcal F_k])}
\leq\operatorname{tr}(A)\sqrt{v_k-1}.
\]
Substitution proves the bound. Setting \(A=uu^{\mathsf T}\) with separable \(u\) makes the two estimates equalities simultaneously, proving the claimed supremum for **every** admissible \((d,n,r,k,L)\). No finite sampling experiment is used to infer a universal maximum.

**4. Exact residual randomness and its scaling.** For separable unit \(u\), each contraction of a fresh Gaussian core produces a new independent chi-square factor in the normalized squared Frobenius norm. Conditional rotational invariance makes those factors independent even though the input norm is random; it also separates the shared-prefix scale from all independently completed suffix factors, giving the stated product and finite-probe distribution identities. All \(Y_j\), conditioned on \(\mathcal F_k\), are identically distributed with integrable conditional mean \(S_k\); the conditional strong law yields \(H_L(uu^{\mathsf T})\to S_k\) almost surely. For fixed \(k\geq1\), \(\operatorname{Var}S_k=v_k-1>0\), so the limit does not equal the trace almost surely. For the joint proportional scaling, the finite sharp formula gives \(v_k\to e^{2\theta/\alpha}\), while \(v_{d-1}\) remains bounded, establishing the variance limit as \(L\to\infty\).

For the distributional assertion, the product representation yields, for each fixed real \(t\) near zero,
\[
\mathbb E e^{t\log S_k}
=\left[\left(\frac2r\right)^t\frac{\Gamma(r/2+t)}{\Gamma(r/2)}\right]^k.
\]
The standard gamma-ratio expansion gives the logarithm of the bracket as \((t^2-t)/r+O_t(r^{-2})\). Thus the moment-generating functions of \(\log S_k\) converge to \(\exp[(\theta/\alpha)(t^2-t)]\), the mgf of \(N(-\theta/\alpha,2\theta/\alpha)\). This argument establishes weak convergence for \(\theta>0\); the \(\theta=0\) limit follows from \(\mathbb E S_k=1\) and \(\operatorname{Var}S_k\to0\).

## Relationship to prior work

Bujanović, Kressner and Olić (2026), arXiv:2606.15679, define normalized Gaussian TT vectors and prove the sharp separable-vector fourth-moment equality. In particular, their Lemma 4 and Theorem 5 supply the marginal moment tools used in Step 2; their Theorem 7 concerns trace estimation with **independent complete TT vectors**. The present contribution addresses a different sampling law: several marginally valid Gaussian TT vectors are coupled through identical prefix cores. Its simultaneous, sharp two-term variance envelope and positive sample-reuse error floor are not consequences of the independent-probe variance formula alone.

Cazeaux, Dupuy and Figueroa Justiniano (2026), arXiv:2603.11009, study TTStack and Gaussian TT sketches for oblivious embeddings and numerical rounding. Their row-block sketches are built by stacking independent realizations (Section 5.2) and their partial contractions across mode boundaries are dependent, but this is not an analysis of *multiple Hutchinson trace samples sharing one initial TT prefix*. Meyer and Avron (arXiv:2309.04952) obtain exact variances for rank-one Kronecker probes with independently sampled tensor factors; this supplies a useful rank-one comparison, not the general-bond-rank coupled-core result. Bujanović and Kressner (arXiv:2004.06433) likewise study rank-one structured probes. The current formulas specialize to those earlier marginal fourth-moment bounds at \(L=1\) and to independent TT sampling at \(k=0\); no novelty is claimed for either specialization.

## Limitations

All results are for independent standard real Gaussian scalar core entries with *identically shared first* \(k\) *cores* and mutually independent suffixes. The sharp supremum ranges over symmetric positive-semidefinite matrices; it is not asserted for indefinite or complex matrices, arbitrary cross-probe dependencies, nonuniform bond dimensions, or orthogonalized sketch cores. The nonconsistency statement is witnessed by separable rank-one PSD matrices; it is not claimed for every nonzero target. The high-order lognormal result assumes the specified proportional limits. No wall-clock performance improvement from core reuse, nor finite-precision stability or downstream convergence, is asserted. A targeted literature comparison cannot exclude every unindexed prior treatment of correlated TT sampling. There has been no independent audit.
