# Scientific review: sharp two-jump first-order cyclicity

## Correctness

PASS. The claim concerns simple roots of the precise Abel displacement coefficient, not all nonlinear cycles. The affine-step representation follows from pairing values at opposite positions, including negative jump locations. Direct integration supplies the exact \(\arccos\)-square-root expression; differentiation reduces the critical equation to an affine polynomial for the inner zone and to a nonzero cubic eliminant for the outer zone. Rolle's theorem yields an unconditional two-plus-four root bound. The explicit six-root witness reproduces the two-jump parameters in Chen–Jin–Li–Lu and carries six strictly alternating interval-certified sign brackets. Six roots saturate the derivative bound, hence are simple. The saved verifier executed successfully. The Poincaré-map correspondence is limited to transversal regular finite radii and small perturbation size.

## Originality

PASS within the accessible comparisons. Chen–Jin–Li–Lu prove six as a *lower* bound using their multiscale first-order construction and a very large upper bound for the full nonlinear class; those statements do not imply that six is the *maximum first-order* simple-root count. Chen–Li–Zhang–Zhang prove a weaker construction and analyze infinity. One-jump exact global results, two-fold continuous results and switching-curve first-order Melnikov results have differing geometry and do not imply the sharp two-jump bound. Available mathematical-record searches and the accessible cumulative claim ledger did not reveal an equivalent or stronger statement. The older Tonnelier paper was not inspected in complete primary text; an unindexed derivation remains a residual priority risk. Failed discovery by itself is not proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value

PASS. Two jumps are the first discontinuous Liénard case in which the recent construction disproves the earlier conjectured four-cycle count. Determining the *exact* number accessible by the authors' first-order perturbation mechanism isolates the combinatorial contribution of two interfaces without falsely resolving the global problem. The cubic eliminant provides an effective analytic root-count certificate and predicts a forced two/four distribution in every extremal example. The result is neither a mere restatement of the published lower bound nor a special numerical recomputation.

## Limitations and priority risk

The upper bound does not control higher-order multiple-zero bifurcations, shrinking/growing-radius singular limits, grazing or sliding, or all finite-perturbation cycles. No public claim of independent verification is made. Earlier specialized first-order literature not available in full could contain an equivalent upper bound.
