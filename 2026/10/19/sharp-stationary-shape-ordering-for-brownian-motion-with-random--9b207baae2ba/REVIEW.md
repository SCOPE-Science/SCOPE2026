# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The stationary past-series representation is valid for every multiplier law on \([0,1]\) with \(0<m=\mathbb E A^2<1\). The finite recursive kernels preserve convex order both in their input clocks and in the multiplier law, and the positive series converges in \(L^1\). The stop-loss characterization justifies passage to the limit. The moment formula follows independently from the stationary generator; its fourth-moment identity yields the exact sharp kurtosis endpoints and rigid equality cases. The moment-generating-function bound has its correct finite domain. The reproducibility script verifies distinct exact recursions and seeded simulations without treating numerical tests as proof.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to residual priority risk.** Dahlenburg et al. (arXiv:2104.14866) develop random-amplitude resets but specialize the dependent random-amplitude calculations to ballistic motion. Tal-Friedman et al. (arXiv:2205.03363), Pierce (arXiv:2204.07215), and Di Bello et al. (arXiv:2305.13722) analyze deterministic proportional factors for diffusive motion. Olsen (arXiv:2310.04840) considers resets to an independently sampled spatial distribution, not random contraction of the pre-reset position. None of the inspected relevant sections presents the fixed-variance global convex-order sandwich, complete sharp kurtosis interval, or central-peak versus absolute-displacement comparison for random factors. A broader published convex-order theorem for affine perpetuities might imply the comparison and remains a material residual risk; this review does not establish priority by search absence alone.

## Scientific value

**PASS.** A central motivation of partial resetting is the continuous variation from Laplace-like to Gaussian-like stationary shapes. When reset strengths fluctuate, stationary variance alone loses information about shape. The theorem gives a universal design and comparison principle for all coefficient distributions of a specified mean-squared strength, not a single new parameter example. It supplies attainable extrema, equality rigidity, the entire kurtosis interval, and a peakedness/tail-moment tradeoff. The result is confined to a physically standard Brownian Poisson resetting model and makes no claim about entire path laws or first-passage times.

## Scope and limitations

The comparison is for the stationary latent Gaussian-mixing clock, and its consequences for selected position observables, not for position convex order or pointwise tail probabilities. Broad random-recursion literature could provide equivalent implication results. Priority needs independent examination; no independent audit or validation was performed.
