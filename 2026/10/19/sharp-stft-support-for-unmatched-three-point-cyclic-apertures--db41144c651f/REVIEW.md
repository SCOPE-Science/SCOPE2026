# Scientific review: unmatched three-tap cyclic STFT support

## Correctness — PASS
The proof identifies all nine spatial sample products with their difference shifts. Nontranslated three-point supports give at most two products per shift. Each doubly occupied slice has at most one Fourier zero, by injectivity of nontrivial characters of a prime-order cyclic group. The overlap number \(E\) is exactly the scalar product of unordered pair-difference multiplicities. A parity system determines how many of the \(E\) zeros can be simultaneous. For \(p\ge7\), the only dependent three-constraint pattern is the reflection of a nonprogression triangle; the complex cross-ratios around that triangle multiply to a negative \(p\)-th root and therefore cannot all equal one. The \(p=5\) boundary has a separate explicit four-cancellation construction. Exact combinatorial checks and direct exact cyclotomic STFT sign-witness tests for representative cases complement every unmatched support-pair enumeration for primes \(5,7,11,13\) pass, and the all-prime statement is supplied by the independent algebraic proof steps, not by extrapolation. A computational or independent mathematical audit beyond these checks has not been performed.

## Originality — PASS, with bibliographic risk
Krahmer, Pfander, and Rashkov, arXiv:math/0611493, Proposition 4.4, give a support-size-only lower bound; the fixed-pair statistic \(E\) and reflected triangle correction are not consequences of that bound. Nicola, arXiv:2204.14176, Theorems 1.1–1.2, treats equality at the global lower level \(p\), not near the unmatched three-tap level \(6p-3\). The published two-sparse prime STFT trichotomy has only four product entries and lacks any three-edge consistency cycle. Relevant mathematical-search results did not identify the present all-prime fixed-support classification. Residual risk: an equivalent formula may exist under a different ambiguity-function or difference-multiset terminology. Absence from searches is not conclusive proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS
The claim completely determines the best achievable STFT sparsity for every pair of unmatched three-point supports on all odd prime cyclic groups, not merely the globally smallest number. The reflected nonprogression case is a motivated, nontrivial obstruction: counting product collisions alone overpredicts attainable zeros by exactly one, due to an odd cycle of phase constraints. The theorem also identifies all exceptional ordered support pairs and their exact multiplicity and improves the support-size-only uncertainty bound for the unmatched stratum at primes at least seven. This structural information has a natural role in sparse time-frequency interpolation and ambiguity-support design.

## Scope and unresolved risks
The proof assumes prime cyclic order, exactly three nonzero entries in each input, and inequivalent supports under translation. The arithmetic of the two-slice Fourier zeros uses primality, and the sign consistency analysis does not extend automatically to four or more taps. Unindexed equivalences and quantitative stability questions remain open.
