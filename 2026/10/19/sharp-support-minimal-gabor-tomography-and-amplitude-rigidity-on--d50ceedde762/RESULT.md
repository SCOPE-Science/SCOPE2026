# Sharp support-minimal Gabor tomography and amplitude rigidity on perfect difference sets

## Finding
Let \(G\) be a finite abelian group with \(|G|=v=k(k-1)+1\) and \(k\ge3\), and suppose that \(G\) possesses a perfect difference set of size \(k\). Consider complete intensity-lifted Gabor measurements generated by a unit vector \(g\in\mathbb C^G\), and require the associated \(v^2\) rank-one projectors to span all complex \(v\)-by-\(v\) matrices.

Among **all** such informationally complete windows supported on at most \(k\) coordinates, the least possible operator-frame spectral condition number is exactly \(k^2\). Every minimizer, and no other window, has support equal to a \((v,k,1)\) difference set and constant squared magnitude \(1/k\) on that support; all pointwise complex phases are free. Such supports are absolutely support-minimal among informationally complete single-window Gabor systems. The full optimal operator-frame spectrum is
\[
\lambda=v\ \text{(multiplicity 1)},\qquad
\lambda=\frac{v(k-1)}{k^2}\ \text{(multiplicity }v-1\text{)},\qquad
\lambda=\frac{v}{k^2}\ \text{(multiplicity }v(v-1)\text{)}.
\]

More generally, write \(D=\operatorname{supp}g\) with \(|D|=k\) a perfect difference set, \(p_x=|g(x)|^2>0\), and \(\sum_{x\in D}p_x=1\). The operator frame is invertible exactly when
\[
\sum_{x\in D}p_x\chi(x)\ne0\qquad\text{for every nontrivial }\chi\in\widehat G.
\]
Its exact rank is \(v(v-1)+\#\{\chi\in\widehat G:\sum_{x\in D}p_x\chi(x)\ne0\}\). All its eigenvalues, including multiplicities, are independent of the phases of the coefficients of \(g\).

The least eigenvalue obeys the quantitative amplitude-rigidity inequality
\[
\boxed{\quad \lambda_{\min}\le\frac{v}{k^2}-\frac{v}{k(k-1)}\sum_{x\in D}\left(p_x-\frac1k\right)^2.\quad}
\]
Equality holds for strictly positive weights only for the constant distribution, and the coefficient \(1/[k(k-1)]\) cannot be increased uniformly over strictly positive weights on a perfect difference set. Thus every non-flat informative window is strictly less well-conditioned than the flat window. The family includes Singer cyclic difference sets with \(v=q^2+q+1\), \(k=q+1\) for every prime power \(q\ge2\).

## Assumptions and scope
A perfect \((v,k,1)\) difference set \(D\subset G\) is a \(k\)-element subset such that each nonzero \(t\in G\) has exactly one representation \(t=x-y\) with \(x,y\in D\) and \(x\ne y\). The existence hypothesis is explicit: it is satisfied for all cited Singer cyclic parameters and may fail for some other group orders. All sums are over finite groups; no primality of \(v\) is assumed.

For a character \(\chi\in\widehat G\), define the Weyl shift \((\pi(t,\chi)f)(x)=\chi(x)f(x-t)\). For a unit-norm window \(g\), set \(g_{t,\chi}=\pi(t,\chi)g\), \(P_{t,\chi}=g_{t,\chi}g_{t,\chi}^{*}\), and
\[
\mathcal S_g(X)=\sum_{t\in G,\,\chi\in\widehat G}\operatorname{Tr}(P_{t,\chi}X)P_{t,\chi},\qquad X\in\mathbb C^{G\times G}.
\]
The operator-frame condition number is the largest eigenvalue divided by the smallest, with condition number \(+\infty\) if the frame fails to span the full matrix space. The quantity \(\lambda_{\min}\) includes zero eigenvalues when singular. This is complex-linear operator tomography, not a claim of optimal nonlinear phase-retrieval noise constants or of optimality among unrestricted-support windows.

## Proof
**1. Complete spectral reduction.** The \(v^2\) matrices \(\pi(t,\chi)\) form an orthogonal Hilbert--Schmidt basis of \(\mathbb C^{G\times G}\), each of squared Hilbert--Schmidt norm \(v\). Conjugation by \(\pi(s,\eta)\) multiplies a Weyl matrix by a scalar character in \((s,\eta)\). The projector-frame operator \(\mathcal S_g\) commutes with every such conjugation and is consequently diagonal in the Weyl basis. In fact, let
\[
A_g(t,\chi)=\langle g,\pi(t,\chi)g\rangle
=\sum_{x\in G}\overline{g(x)}\chi(x)g(x-t).
\]
For each orbit projector \(P_{s,\eta}\), the modulus of \(\operatorname{Tr}(P_{s,\eta}\pi(t,\chi))\) equals \(|A_g(t,\chi)|\). Taking the Hilbert--Schmidt Rayleigh quotient of the Weyl matrix gives, with all \(v^2\) projectors included,
\[
\mathcal S_g\pi(t,\chi)=v|A_g(t,\chi)|^2\pi(t,\chi).
\]
In particular, \(\lambda_{\max}=v\), because \(|A_g(t,\chi)|\le1\) by Cauchy--Schwarz and \(A_g(0,1)=1\). This general Gabor spectral mechanism is explicitly prior work [1].

**2. The absolute support threshold.** Let \(E=\operatorname{supp}g\) have \(m\le k\) points. If a nonzero shift \(t\notin E-E\), then \(A_g(t,\chi)=0\) for all \(\chi\), so \(\mathcal S_g\) is singular. Thus operator-space spanning requires \(E-E\) to contain all \(v-1\) nonzero shifts. There are at most \(m(m-1)\) ordered pairs of distinct points in \(E\). Since \(v-1=k(k-1)\), \(m<k\) is impossible; for \(m=k\), equality forces every nonzero shift to occur once, precisely the perfect difference-set property.

**3. Exact weighted spectrum and rank.** Assume \(E=D\) is perfect and \(p_x>0\). For each nonzero \(t\), let \((x_t,y_t)\) be the unique ordered pair in \(D^2\) with \(t=x_t-y_t\). Then
\[
A_g(t,\chi)=\overline{g(x_t)}\chi(x_t)g(y_t),
\qquad |A_g(t,\chi)|^2=p_{x_t}p_{y_t}>0.
\]
For \(t=0\), one has \(A_g(0,\chi)=\sum_{x\in D}p_x\chi(x)\). Consequently the complete eigenvalue multiset consists of \(v\) times these \(v\) squared Fourier magnitudes (one per character) and, for each ordered distinct pair \((x,y)\in D^2\), the value \(vp_xp_y\) repeated \(v\) times. The rank and phase-independence claims follow immediately. No complex phase appears in these eigenvalue magnitudes.

**4. The global minimax and complete optimizers.** Let \(p_{(1)}\le p_{(2)}\) be the two smallest weights. The preceding spectrum gives
\[
\lambda_{\min}\le v p_{(1)}p_{(2)}\le \frac{v}{k^2}.
\]
Indeed all \({k\choose2}\) products \(p_xp_y\), \(x<y\), are at least \(p_{(1)}p_{(2)}\). Since their sum equals \((1-\sum_xp_x^2)/2\),
\[
 k(k-1)p_{(1)}p_{(2)}\le1-\sum_xp_x^2
=\frac{k-1}{k}-\sum_x\left(p_x-\frac1k\right)^2.
\]
This proves the displayed quantitative bound, with strict inequality in the second step unless all \(p_x\) are equal. Because \(\lambda_{\max}=v\), an invertible window therefore has condition number at least \(k^2\), with equality possible only for flat weights.

For the flat choice \(p_x=1/k\), each nonzero-shift ambiguity has squared modulus \(1/k^2\). For nontrivial \(\chi\), the difference-set identity gives
\[
\left|\sum_{x\in D}\chi(x)\right|^2
=\sum_{x,y\in D}\chi(x-y)
=k+\sum_{t\ne0}\chi(t)=k-1.
\]
Hence each zero-shift, nontrivial-character squared ambiguity equals \((k-1)/k^2\), which is strictly greater than \(1/k^2\) for \(k\ge3\). The trivial ambiguity equals one. All eigenvalues are positive and the three values and their multiplicities are exactly those stated. Pointwise phases are arbitrary, since Step 3 showed that the entire spectrum depends only on weights.

**5. Sharpness of the rigidity coefficient.** The coefficient is best possible among inequalities of the same global form. Fix \(D\), assign one point weight \(1-(k-1)\varepsilon\) and each other point weight \(\varepsilon\), with \(\varepsilon>0\) small. Some two distinct small-weight points exist because \(k\ge3\). Every nonzero-shift squared ambiguity is either \(\varepsilon^2\) or \(\varepsilon[1-(k-1)\varepsilon]\). Every zero-shift ambiguity is bounded below in modulus by \(1-2(k-1)\varepsilon\). Thus for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), \(\lambda_{\min}=v\varepsilon^2>0\), so these are still informationally complete. Meanwhile the variance tends to \((k-1)/k\) and \(\lambda_{\min}\) tends to zero. Therefore the ratio \([v/k^2-\lambda_{\min}]/[v\sum_x(p_x-1/k)^2]\) tends to \(1/[k(k-1)]\). No larger uniform coefficient can hold.

## Verification
The standalone Python 3 standard-library program `verify.py` independently enumerates perfect cyclic difference sets of sizes \(3,4,6\) in groups of orders \(7,13,31\), respectively. It checks the complete weighted ambiguity spectra, the quantitative rigidity bound and its sharp limiting family, and the exact Weyl eigen-equation of the actual lifted projector operator on all \(49\) Weyl matrices in dimension \(7\). These finite checks corroborate the direct, all-orders proof above; they are not its basis.

## Relationship to prior work
Bojarovska and Flinth [1], especially Theorem 2.3, already prove the general Gabor operator-frame extremal eigenvalue identity. Their Proposition 2.2 gives the nonzero-ambiguity construction from suitable unweighted difference sets. Bojarovska and Paternostro [2] already prove coherent-frame and fusion-frame properties of difference-set windows, and explicitly discuss Singer parameters. None of those general mechanisms or flat-design constructions is asserted to be new here. The contribution assessed here is the **global optimization and complete classification at the absolute support floor**, including a sharp quantitative penalty for any amplitude imbalance and a phase-insensitive weighted rank criterion. Their use of "optimally sparse" refers to a fusion-frame representation property, not this operator-tomography support-minimax problem.

Published related work on two-sparse multiwindow operator-span capacity concerns a distinct multiwindow problem, while the two-line and planar-graph Gabor tomography problems concern different support geometries. Searches for equivalent minimax, dual, weighted, and dominating formulations revealed no stronger source establishing the new whole claim. This comparison cannot exclude an obscure historical equivalent.

## Limitations
The condition is imposed on windows supported on at most \(k\) coordinates; no optimality is claimed among windows with larger supports. The theorem presupposes a perfect difference set; it neither constructs one in every admissible group nor classifies which groups possess one. For singular weighted windows the rank formula still applies, but their full-operator condition number is infinite. The spectral calculation employs known general Gabor-frame facts, explicitly credited above. Independent priority and mathematical audits have not been performed.
