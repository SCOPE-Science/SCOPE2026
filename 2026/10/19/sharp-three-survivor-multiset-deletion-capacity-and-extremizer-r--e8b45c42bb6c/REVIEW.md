# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** The size-three shadow condition is precisely intersection at most two. Assigning each heavy multiset a triple-occupied symbol gives at most \(q\) heavy members. Two light multisets overlap in at least \(2n-2q\) symbols, so there is at most one light member from \(n=q+2\), and none once \(n>2q\). Explicit constructions attain both upper bounds. The unique light member at the final two plateau lengths forces, respectively, the unique codebook and the \(q^2\) classification. Every boundary case and the labeled-codebook counting convention were checked. The standalone finite verification is a cross-check, not a replacement for the all-parameter proof.

**Originality: PASS within inspected evidence.** Kreindel, Essayag and Zabokritskiy, arXiv:2601.05636v2 §4.4, establish \(S_q(n,n-3)\le q+\binom q2\), which does not determine the exact \(q+1\) and \(q\) phases or count their endpoint extremizers. Existing small-alphabet capacity claims for \(q=3,4\) are acknowledged and excluded from the claimed new scope. Kovačević–Tan (arXiv:1612.08837) and the later deletion-ball geometry study (arXiv:2603.18322) do not state the exact proposed phase in inspected passages. A differently phrased classical intersection theorem or uninspected specialization could still cover it; priority remains subject to external examination.

**Value: PASS.** This is a sharp general-alphabet capacity determination with two structurally complete optimal-codebook classifications and an exact onset boundary, addressing a previously weaker high-deletion certificate bound for a motivated coding channel. It is not an isolated numerical computation.

Independent validation has not been performed. These judgments apply only to the stated three-survivor theorem with \(q\ge5\).
