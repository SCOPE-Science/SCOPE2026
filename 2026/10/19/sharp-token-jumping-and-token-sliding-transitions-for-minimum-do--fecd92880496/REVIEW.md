**Correctness (PASS).** Theorem 1 of Ivančo and Zelinka (1993) supplies the known minimum dominated-triple classification, also reconstructed by the elementary edge transversal argument. A triangle has no possible one-vertex substitution; matching degree is counted by the four ground endpoints held fixed. Connectivity of the jumping matching layer follows by installing target matching edges one at a time with one spare ground vertex, preserving installed edges. For sliding, at order eight the four pairs of a perfect matching are invariant, producing exactly \(105K_4\), while at order nine or above two extra spare endpoints simulate every non-sliding jump by two legal slides. All boundary values are checked by exhaustive domination enumeration through order ten and a structurally independent matching-family construction through order eleven. Finite testing is not relied upon as proof for unbounded \(n\).

Relevant Kneser tuple-domination work reproduces the old triple classification. None of the materially inspected primary texts states the sharp sliding sequence (isolation until seven, exactly \(105K_4\) at eight, a connected matching component from nine) or the paired jumping threshold. Targeted searchable mathematical literature and published claim-index comparisons returned no implication covering the full claim; abstract-only and unindexed sources remain residual risks, so independent priority review remains outstanding.

**Value (PASS).** The problem asks for the topology of the complete optimal-solution space under the two canonical constrained exchange rules. The results identify two different sharp thresholds, a rare sharply classified intermediate fragmentation into \(105\) cliques, and a constructive simulation connecting the constrained and unconstrained reconfiguration models for every sufficiently large order. The exact degrees and edge counts are structural consequences of this mobility theorem, not an arbitrary finite measurement. This is a natural infinite family rather than a narrow sample.

**Limits.** Only minimum dominating sets, single selected-vertex exchanges and integer \(n\ge5\) are treated. General diameters, Hamiltonicity, and other move models are not settled.
