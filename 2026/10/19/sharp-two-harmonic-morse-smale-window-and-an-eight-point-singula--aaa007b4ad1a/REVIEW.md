# Scientific assessment: nonlinear two-harmonic Huygens criterion

## Correctness — PASS

The central claim is the exact \(0<h<1/[\lambda q(c)]\) Morse–Smale window and the one-to-eight singular-Jacobian splitting for the three-edge star. The scalar maximum is obtained from \(\max_{0\le u\le1}u(1+3c-3cu^2)\). Positive signed diagonal dominance and nonsingularity make \(K\) positive definite. The gradient potential gives strict descent and identifies the only recurrent points; similarity to a symmetric Jacobian and Sylvester inertia verify global invertibility, fixed-point hyperbolicity, and Morse indices.

The critical nonstandard step is stable/unstable transversality: the signed row maximum in a two-sided adjoint covector sequence forces one maximizing coordinate into a forward-invariant negative-derivative slab, then propagates its positive absolute maximum forever, contradicting forward decay. The argument has been checked with \(g'_c(s)=\cos s(1+3c\sin^2s)\), strict scalar monotonicity, and exact endpoint amplitude. At \(h=1/[\lambda q(c)]\), the star singularity set follows from a sum of two nonnegative quadratic forms, whose simultaneous zero requires all three scalar derivatives to attain their common minimum. The first singular Jacobian has rank two at each solution. The explicit rational witness \(c=1\) and independent scalar finite checks support these calculations, but they do not substitute for the proof.

## Originality — PASS under checked evidence

The closest source is Buescu–D'Aniello–Oliveira, *Morse–Smale dynamics in discrete Huygens networks* (arXiv:2610.10993v1). Its Theorem 3.2 already establishes **general-odd-law** Lyapunov descent and the spectral local-diffeomorphism bound, and those ingredients are acknowledged as prior methodology; its Theorem 4.1 and the weighted counterpart establish global Morse–Smale dynamics for the **pure sine** interaction. Its structural-stability corollary guarantees only unspecified small perturbations near an individual sine system. None of those conclusions quantifies the entire, arbitrarily large two-harmonic parameter range, asserts transversality at the exact shape-dependent ceiling, or classifies the eight nonfixed first-singularity points beyond the transition \(c=1/6\).

Earlier three-clock synchronisation studies (arXiv:2503.13230v1 and arXiv:2502.15371v1) concern sine models and do not imply the new scalar threshold or its singular-locus bifurcation. Relevant semantic mathematical-record queries and accessible related claims, including earlier pure-sine star singular and preimage results, were compared on their implications. No covering prior claim was identified. This is evidence-based originality, not a proof of global priority. A wider treatment of monotone odd couplings or a directly applicable general gradient-map theorem could still subsume the claim; independent specialist priority checking has not been performed.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The earliest Jacobian singularity determines the exact limit of the robust Morse–Smale *diffeomorphism* regime for an entire physically interpretable two-harmonic interaction family. Its first failure migrates from a hyperbolicity loss at the anti-phase sink to eight off-equilibrium singular points as \(c\) crosses the explicitly determined \(1/6\) threshold. The quartic contact at the transition and the fact that the sink stays hyperbolic in the eight-point regime explain a genuine qualitative change that the pure sine criterion and generic small-perturbation structural stability do not predict quantitatively. This is a natural mechanism, not an arbitrary parameter slice or a routine numerical calculation.

## Scope and limitations

The results require finite-dimensional torus maps, signed weak diagonal dominance with positive diagonal, nonsingular symmetric \(K\), identical two-harmonic coordinate law and \(c\geq0\). No classification of the dynamics beyond the first singular step is asserted. The eight-point locus is only for the three-edge star. The 37,958 finite numerical/algebraic assertions support but cannot certify universal statements. Priority remains subject to independently assessing wider gradient-map literature.
