# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The admissible parameter region gives strict positive definiteness by an explicit Schur complement. The key globalization is an exact averaging argument in the two linear matrix inequalities \(E\preceq A\preceq K E\), not an unsupported assumption of optimizer symmetry. The complete reduced spectrum is a two-dimensional block plus a repeated eigenvalue; strict monotonicity and the crossing equation yield both optimal branches and their boundary. Independent finite tests reconstruct full matrices and verify the formulas for numerous admissible parameters, as recorded in `VERIFICATION.md` and `verify.py`. The proof, not the tests, supplies the all-parameter quantifiers.

**Originality — PASS with qualified residual risk.** The 2011 Lu–Pong work solves a general diagonal-preconditioner convex formulation, not the displayed two-coupling closed form. The 2006 Kolotilina complete three-dimensional theory covers the two-leaf case, which is expressly excluded. Shapiro's general block scaling and variational conditions may imply an equivalent specialization, but an exact all-\(m\ge3\) piecewise hub–exchangeable formula was not identified in the inspected accessible material and targeted searches. Shapiro's entire paper was not accessible for full-text comparison; this is a substantive residual risk, not a claim of exhaustive novelty proof.

**Value — PASS.** The matrix family is a natural minimal nonbipartite extension of star-coupled positive definite systems, arising in regularized coupled subsystems with a fully exchangeable block. A global closed-form preconditioner, sharp transition from repeated to simple top eigenmode, exact Jacobi-optimality frontier, and nonvanishing large-system scaling law provide a structured benchmark beyond a generic numerical SDP or a single small example. The result does not claim to improve the solution of arbitrary diagonal scaling problems.
