# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness
**PASS.** The explicit weighted monomial expansions give the norm and correlation identities and reduce the optimal-approximant coefficient ratio to a three-variable generalized Rayleigh quotient. The symmetric matrix has eigenvalues \(0,\pm\eta_\alpha\), with a simple positive extremal eigenvector. Equality in both the quotient and the preceding triangle inequality yields the stated one-angle family. The zero exclusion follows by the triangle inequality for the affine approximant, with diagonal equality. Closed-bidisk nonvanishing of the extremizers follows from the displayed strict Möbius boundary inequality and the maximum-modulus principle. The final monotonicity and asymptotic formula are algebraic consequences of \(\eta_\alpha^2=h_\alpha(1+t_\alpha)/(1+2t_\alpha)\). The checker confirms exact symbolic instances and independently recomputes sample coefficients, but is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
**PASS, with historical-priority qualification.** The closest recent bidisk counterexamples require higher-degree symmetric generators and hence do not imply the sharp multiaffine exclusion radius; the one-variable Hardy-space metric-projection work does not cover the bidisk parameter-dependent exact value. Direct mathematical comparisons against related published bidisk approximant results identified no implication that supplies the complete equality and sharp-radius classification. Unindexed earlier filter-design/least-squares inverse calculations remain a material priority risk. No claim of independent priority verification is made.

## Value
**PASS.** Recent polynomial counterexamples to weak Shanks zero exclusion make the minimal generator complexity and the precise stable low-degree cutoff mathematically motivated. The theorem proves all-exponent exclusion with an optimal numerical radius and exactly identifies the extremizers, which themselves satisfy the strong closed-bidisk-zero-free constraint for positive parameter. This gives a sharp, non-routine boundary for the counterexample phenomenon, not merely a numerical finite table.

## Scope and limitations
Applies only to symmetric bidegree-at-most-\((1,1)\) generators, isotropic weights \((j+1)^\alpha(k+1)^\alpha\) with \(\alpha\ge0\), and affine optimal polynomial approximants. Does not give an obstruction for general higher-degree generators. The absence of an indexed equivalent claim does not prove absolute novelty. Independent verification is outstanding.
