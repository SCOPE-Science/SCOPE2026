# Scientific review of the spectral-band ball-proximal path-length theorem

**Correctness: PASS.** For positive-definite quadratics the exact nonterminal ball-minimizer is a variable-resolvent step. Inside a dyadic spectral band, each coordinate's reduction factor lies within a factor strictly less than two of the other factors; therefore the Euclidean displacement is no more than twice the decrease in projected Euclidean radius. Bandwise telescoping and Cauchy--Schwarz give the universal bound. Independent monotonicity in each eigencoordinate gives the alternative \(\sqrt d\) bound. Every nonterminal fixed-radius step has exact length \(h\), yielding the call count. For the lower-bound construction, disjoint gradient-flow time windows supply length proportional to \(\sqrt p\); an implicit-Euler convergence argument on a compact nonzero subarc transfers that bound to small-radius ball-proximal steps. The included checker supports, but does not replace, these arguments.

**Originality: PASS on best available comparative evidence.** Richtárik and Li (arXiv:2609.35147, §§1,4,8) prove general fixed-dimensional linear-in-\(D/h\) termination, without this spectral parameter. Gruntkowska et al. (arXiv:2502.02002) define the ball-proximal method and normalized implicit step but do not give the present spectral-band path-length theorem. Gupta, Balakrishnan, and Ramdas (arXiv:1908.01089, Theorems 10 and 18) already prove the analogous sharp-order gradient-flow/gradient-descent path-length exponent; that exponent and the continuous geometric-spectrum mechanism are specifically credited rather than claimed as new. The difference is the nonasymptotic, arbitrary-radius variable-resolvent ball-proximal algorithm. Several searches of published mathematical research records and relevant prior literature found no statement implying that extension. The remaining risk is an unindexed theorem for implicit proximal trajectories or trust-region iterates.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** The general 2026 oracle complexity is linear in \(D/h\) with a dimension-dependent constant; its worst-case dimension dependence cannot be removed for unrestricted convex objectives. For a ubiquitous structured subclass—positive-definite quadratics—this theorem replaces that unknown dimensional dependence by \(O(\sqrt{\log\kappa})\) even when the radii change adaptively. Its matching geometric-spectrum family shows that the spectral factor represents genuine trajectory geometry rather than a loose algebraic bound. The result is an ideal-oracle benchmark, not a claim of practical efficiency for arbitrary approximate subsolves.

**Limitations and bibliographic risk.** Finite-dimensional SPD quadratic functions, exact Euclidean ball subproblems, and exact arithmetic are essential. The constants in the upper and lower inequalities are not claimed optimal. The small-radius lower bound holds along a specified family, not for every spectrum. Earlier gradient-flow results have the same square-root logarithmic order, and hidden equivalences in specialized implicit-gradient work remain possible.
