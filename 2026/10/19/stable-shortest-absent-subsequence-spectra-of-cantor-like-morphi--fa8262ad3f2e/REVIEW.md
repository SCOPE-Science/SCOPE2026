# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim specifies all integers \(r\ge1\), \(k\ge2\), and binary scattered subsequences. The base cases exhaust all thirty-two length-five candidates; for larger \(r\), the base is transported by a valid subsequence embedding. The doubling identity yields universality inductively. The two overlapping forbidden halves limit every possible new absent word to five middle triples in the ordinary Cantor case, or two middle symbols otherwise. Explicit middle-zero embeddings eliminate every nonlisted possibility, and first/last-one counts show the remaining four or nine words cannot occur. No inference from a bounded computation is used for arbitrary \(r\) or \(k\).

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge comparison)
The inspected 2015 Cantor-like paper computes factor/permutation complexity and not scattered-subsequence absence. The inspected full text of the foundational absent-subsequence paper describes general arch characterizations and algorithms, but does not give the Cantor-like generation-by-generation formula. Comparable published generic word-length universality and extremal shortest-absence results concern distributions or maxima over unrestricted words, not this exact family. Previously studied Cantor-word invariants concerning rotation, binomial factor classes, and first repeated contiguous factors do not imply this shortest-absent scattered-subsequence classification. Remaining risk: an unindexed sequence-specific specialization or an indirect generic theorem may cover the statement and was not ruled out by a mathematical uniqueness proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The invariant is canonical in subsequence universality and the Cantor-like substitutions are a long-studied automatic-word family. A complete classification across every substitution parameter and every generation is more informative than an isolated finite census: the shortest-absent count is *uniformly bounded* despite exponentially growing universality, with a sharp and unexplained-by-generic-bounds transition from nine to four at the shortest zero-block parameter. The full missing **sets**, rather than just their counts, are determined. The proof's overlap-of-forbidden-halves mechanism is reusable for recursive concatenations of universal words.
