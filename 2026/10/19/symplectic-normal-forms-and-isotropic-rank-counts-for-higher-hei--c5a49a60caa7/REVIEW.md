# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof derives preservation of the one-dimensional center directly from the Rota–Baxter identity, identifies the additive quotient endomorphism, and derives its central-coordinate cocycle. Symmetry of this cocycle is equivalent to the symplectic identity; its polarization yields a unique linear correction. The sufficiency calculation reverses every step. The two isotropic strata are counted as isotropic image subspaces times surjections times independent linear corrections. The exact checker covers every parameter triple on the order-\(27\) group, eighteen higher-rank matrices on the order-\(243\) group, and a characteristic-five higher-rank sample. Exhaustive finite tests are corroboration, not the proof for every prime and rank.

## Originality — PASS

The most relevant full-text primary work, Savelyev, arXiv:2608.28291v1, treats extraspecial groups of order \(p^3\), proves center preservation in general, and gives a rank-one matrix relation; the higher-rank \(m\ge2\) normal form, its unique linear correction, and rank-stratified counts are not among its classified cases. Rathee, arXiv:2402.15428, studies three-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebras and their groups. Published-work searches for higher-rank group Rota–Baxter operators, symplectic parametrizations, rank distributions, and similitude reparametrizations did not identify a covering theorem; general Lie-group theory and results for associative Rota–Baxter operators do not imply these explicit finite-group counts. The remaining risk is an incompletely indexed equivalent treatment.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result classifies a natural nonabelian group-operator problem at all higher odd-prime Heisenberg ranks, isolates a nontrivial symplectic quadratic constraint, counts two infinite families by exact rank, and supplies a useful similitude chart outside the degenerate central characters. Unlike a one-case computation, it gives structural and enumerative information for every odd prime and all higher ranks.

## Boundaries

No claim is made about the characteristic-two, exponent-\(p^2\), conjugacy, or isomorphism-class cases; for nonexceptional \(c\), only the matrix criterion and an invertible chart are stated. The main source predates this classification and supplies important parts of its conceptual setup. These points delimit the theorem rather than independently certify it.
