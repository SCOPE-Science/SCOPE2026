## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The coordinate-free spherical-harmonic addition theorem is published by Pérez-Saborid (arXiv:0806.3367). General tensor/spin addition identities occur in Bouzas (arXiv:1103.2983), and a later spin-weighted addition-theorem source is Monteverdi–Winstanley (2024). Their inspected material provides bilocal foundations, not the tetrahedral eight-coordinate Hessian Gram decomposition, unique cubic blind mode, or all-degree full-rank threshold. Targeted semantic-result and literature searches for tetrahedral spin-two sampling, trace-free Hessian interpolation, cubic invisibility, exact rank sequences and Gram spectra did not identify a covering theorem. A previously developed tetrahedral **gradient** sampling rank theorem concerns first derivatives, not the trace-free second-derivative operator here; neither direction directly implies the other. Residual risk is older unindexed angular-momentum or finite-symmetry tables and an incompletely accessible 2024 article. A failed search is not proof of absolute novelty; independent priority review is not claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Trace-free intrinsic Hessians are the natural spin-two/quadrupolar second-jet measurements of scalar spherical harmonics. Their complete interpolation threshold at a canonical symmetric four-point set is a motivated sampling invariant. The answer includes a three-branch exact Gram spectrum for every degree and the structural distinction between a single invisible harmonic cubic and permanent surjectivity from degree four; this is more than a normalization identity or routine finite enumeration. It enables exact rank and conditioning calculations of these four-point curvature measurements. It does not assert a new universal sampling method, uniform stability, or a paper-sized generalization.
