# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The domain is the finite incidence algebra of the complete height-two poset with exactly two minimal and \(m\ge1\) maximal elements, over any \(\mathbb F_q\). For an idempotent and each relation, its off-diagonal coefficient is free if and only if the two diagonal bits differ. Two idempotents produce four-color endpoint data. The commutator coordinate is identically zero at color Hamming distance zero or one; at distance two it is a surjective linear functional on two free coordinates. This gives independent weighted column distributions for each of the three bottom-color types. Rank-zero and rank-one counts follow by summing over zero vectors and the \(q+1\) projective lines in \(\mathbb F_q^2\); subtraction from the squared single-idempotent count gives rank two. Every case is covered, and the argument is characteristic independent.

The independent program enumerates the entire two-idempotent space over small prime fields and computes each commutator rank; the counts agree with the formulas. Computation checks examples, not the infinitely many finite fields.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Earlier single-idempotent conjugacy and potent-element enumerations can cover the total idempotent count and potentially the commuting-pair stratum; those are expressly not separate novelty claims. The positive-rank commutator distribution counts noncommuting pairs, which neither an individual-idempotent classification nor the examined potent-element statements determines. The published generalized-commutator-rank and Kronecker-algebra commutator results have different objects and constraints. The inaccessible full text of Radjavi–Rosenthal and possible unindexed finite-ring enumeration remain coverage risks. Negative searches are not a proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS
The family is the first complete bipartite height-two incidence algebra whose commutators can have rank two: a single bottom row only permits rank one. The three exact rank strata and sharp \(m=2\) threshold determine how frequently two natural projectors fail to commute, including an asymptotic generic-rank theorem. These are algebraically motivated invariants rather than an arbitrary small-field table.
