# Scientific review: generalized Cantor forbidden factors

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem's domain is the complete one-sided fixed point for every integral \(q\ge3\). The proof derives the gap law \(L_{\nu_2(i)}\) directly from the substitution, translates exact and truncated zero runs into a pairwise-checkable family of congruences, and proves that a minimal incompatibility must sit at both endpoints. Its boundary–boundary and exact–exact analyses force the two stated recursive families; mixed endpoints are excluded by explicit intermediate valuation contradictions. Each proposed forbidden word is separately shown to have both proper length-one-deleted factors present. Distinctness, length formulas and generating series follow by exact counting. The self-contained verification program passed all bounded tests. Residual risk is an undetected proof oversight; finite tests alone would not establish the theorem.

## Originality — PASS against inspected evidence

Barton et al., arXiv:1406.6341 (2014), give finite-word minimal-absence algorithms; Crochemore et al., arXiv:1506.04917 (2015), give finite/circular sequence comparison and the formal factorial-language definition. Neither inspected full-text treatment gives an infinite generalized-Cantor antidictionary. Lü et al., doi:10.1016/j.tcs.2015.12.022, describe right-special factors and ordinary/permutation complexity for precisely the Cantor-like substitution, but the accessible sections do not state this minimal-absent-factor language, its two block-centered forbidden families or its enumerator. The right-special classification could plausibly yield an alternative proof, and the complete publisher document was inaccessible; the originality assessment is therefore qualified rather than a claim of literature-wide uniqueness. Additional mathematical-record searches, including synonymous forbidden-substring formulations, found no directly covering result. Absence of search results alone does not establish novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

An antidictionary characterizes a factorial word language by its minimal exclusions. The theorem determines this entire canonical object for every member of a classical automatic family, not merely sample lengths. The two recursive obstruction families reveal how \(2\)-adic gap placement interacts with substitution blocks; the exact lacunary spike spectrum and generating function are natural combinatorial invariants of these words. This has potential uses as a compact exclusion rule for factor membership and comparisons with other automatic-word forbidden languages. The result is restricted to contiguous factors and does not claim a short algorithm for arbitrary alternate substitutions.

## Limits and closest work

The largest overlap risk is the complete Cantor-like right-special-factor classification in Lü et al., rather than the general finite-word minimal-absence algorithms. The publisher article could not be retrieved in full with authorized access, so publication-level deduplication remains incomplete. All scientific assertions in the finding have an internal mathematical proof and computational corroboration, but no external independent validation is asserted.
