# The complete characteristic-two anticommutator semigroup of two-by-two matrices

## Finding
Let \(K\) be any field of characteristic \(2\), let \(V=M_2(K)\), and define
\[
L_A:V\to V,\qquad L_A(X)=AX+XA\quad(A,X\in V).
\]
Let \(\mathcal S(K)\) be the **semigroup of nonempty compositions** of the operators \(L_A\). The following classification determines every element of \(\mathcal S(K)\), not merely its linear span.

Put \(W=K^2\) and \(\omega((b,c),(b',c'))=bc'+cb'\). Write an arbitrary matrix uniquely as
\[
X=sI+tE_{22}+bE_{12}+cE_{21},\qquad(s,t,(b,c))\in K\times K\times W.
\]
For \(h,c_0\in K\) and \(p,a\in W\), define the linear operator
\[
F_{h,p,a,c_0}(s,t,w)=\bigl(\omega(p,w)+c_0t,\ 0,\ hw+at\bigr).
\]
Then \(\mathcal S(K)\) is the **disjoint union** of precisely these three sets:

1. \(F_{h,p,a,c_0}\) with \(h\ne0\) and \(hc_0=\omega(p,a)\), for arbitrary \(p,a\in W\).
2. \(F_{0,p,a,c_0}\) with \(p=0\) **or** \(a=0\), for arbitrary \(c_0\in K\).
3. \(F_{0,u,u,0}\) with \(u\in W\setminus\{0\}\).

Every member has a representation by **at most two** multiplication operators; every product of three or more multiplication operators can be shortened to two. Exactly the operators in the third set occur only at word length one (they are not products of two or more operators).

For \(K=\mathbb F_q\), with \(q=2^m\) and \(m\ge1\), this gives the exact cardinality
\[
\boxed{|\mathcal S(\mathbb F_q)|=(q-1)q^4+2q^3+q^2-q-1.}
\]
Its full rank distribution as a semigroup of endomorphisms of the four-dimensional space \(V\) is
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
N_0&=1,\\
N_1&=2q^3-q-1,\\
N_2&=(q-1)q^4+q^2-1,\\
N_3&=N_4=0.
\end{aligned}}
\]
The number of distinct one-letter generators is \(q^3\), and the number of distinct two-letter products is \((q-1)q^4+2q^3-q\). In particular, \(|\mathcal S(\mathbb F_2)|=33\), \(|\mathcal S(\mathbb F_4)|=907\), and \(|\mathcal S(\mathbb F_8)|=29751\).

## Assumptions and scope
All products and linear endomorphisms are over the same field \(K\), which has characteristic exactly \(2\). Composition is read right to left; the empty word and identity endomorphism are **not** adjoined. The finite counts assume that \(K\) is the field \(\mathbb F_q\), not merely a ring of cardinality \(q\). The normal forms remain valid for infinite fields of characteristic \(2\); only the finite counting conclusions require \(q<\infty\).

These are unnormalized symmetrized operators. In characteristic \(2\) one has \(AX+XA=[A,X]\), so this is also the semigroup generated by inner-derivation operators of the matrix Lie algebra. It is **not** the normalized Jordan product \((AX+XA)/2\), which is undefined in this characteristic.

## Proof
**Step 1: action of a generator.** A scalar matrix produces the zero multiplication operator. Thus for some unique \(\lambda\in K\) and \(u=(\beta,\gamma)\in W\), every \(L_A\) equals \(T_{\lambda,u}:=L_{\lambda E_{11}+\beta E_{12}+\gamma E_{21}}\). Direct multiplication of the two-by-two matrices gives
\[
T_{\lambda,u}(s,t,w)
=\bigl(\omega(u,w),0,\lambda w+ut\bigr)
=F_{\lambda,u,u,0}(s,t,w).
\]
This also proves injectivity of the parameterization of distinct generators.

**Step 2: the two-factor law.** The first operator annihilates the central coordinate returned by the second, and the second output has trace zero. Hence, for all \(\lambda,\mu\in K\) and \(u,v\in W\),
\[
T_{\lambda,u}T_{\mu,v}=F_{\lambda\mu,\,\mu u,\,\lambda v,\,\omega(u,v)}.
\]
For three factors the same observation gives the exact operator identity
\[
T_{\lambda,u}T_{\mu,v}T_{\nu,w}
=T_{\lambda,u}T_{\mu\nu,\mu w}.
\]
Replacing the right-hand pair of any longer word inductively proves that every word of length at least two collapses to a two-letter word.

**Step 3: complete characterization of two-letter products.** Write the four parameters of a product from Step 2 as \(h=\lambda\mu\), \(p=\mu u\), \(a=\lambda v\), \(c_0=\omega(u,v)\). If \(h\ne0\), then \(hc_0=\omega(p,a)\). Conversely, given \(h\ne0\) and \(p,a\in W\), choose \(\lambda=h\), \(\mu=1\), \(u=p\), and \(v=h^{-1}a\). The resulting product realizes precisely the first set.

If \(h=0\), either \(\lambda=0\) (so \(a=0\)) or \(\mu=0\) (so \(p=0\)). Conversely, every operator with \(h=0\) and \(a=0\) is realized as follows. If \(p\ne0\), choose \(\lambda=0\), \(\mu=1\), \(u=p\), and \(v\) satisfying \(\omega(p,v)=c_0\). If \(p=0\), choose \(\lambda=\mu=0\) and \(u,v\) satisfying \(\omega(u,v)=c_0\). Such vectors exist because \(\omega\) is nondegenerate on \(W\). Finally, if \(h=p=0\) and \(a\ne0\), choose \(\lambda=1\), \(\mu=0\), \(v=a\) and \(u\) satisfying \(\omega(u,a)=c_0\). This proves that the second set consists **exactly** of the remaining two-letter products.

**Step 4: exceptional single factors.** If \(\lambda\ne0\), then \(T_{\lambda,u}\) belongs to the first set because \(\omega(u,u)=0\). The zero generator \(T_{0,0}\) belongs to the second set. For \(u\ne0\), however, \(T_{0,u}=F_{0,u,u,0}\) has both \(p\ne0\) and \(a\ne0\), excluding it from the second set; it also has \(h=0\), excluding it from the first. These are exactly the exceptional third set. Distinct tuples \((h,p,a,c_0)\) define distinct operators, as their matrix coefficients relative to \((s,t,b,c)\) show. The three sets are therefore disjoint and exhaustive.

**Step 5: cardinalities.** For \(q=|K|\), the first set has \((q-1)q^4\) elements. The second has
\[
q^2q+(q^2-1)q=2q^3-q
\]
(the subcases \(a=0\) and \(a\ne0,p=0\) are disjoint). The third has \(q^2-1\). Addition yields the claimed total. The distinct generators are parametrized by \((\lambda,u)\in K\times W\), so there are \(q^3\), and every two-letter product lies in the first or second set.

**Step 6: ranks.** If \(h\ne0\) and \(hc_0=\omega(p,a)\), then the first output coordinate equals \(h^{-1}\omega(p,hw+at)\). Thus the image is the graph of a linear functional on the freely varying two-dimensional output \(W\), and the rank is \(2\). When \(h=0,a=0\), the image is the central line or zero. When \(h=0,p=0,a\ne0\), the image is the line spanned by \((c_0,0,a)\). Finally, \(F_{0,u,u,0}\) with \(u\ne0\) has independent central and \(W\)-valued outputs, hence rank \(2\). The unique zero map lies in the second set. Counting these strata gives the stated rank distribution.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify.py` implements finite fields of orders \(2\), \(4\), and \(8\) using polynomial arithmetic, constructs the operators in the displayed coordinate basis, enumerates **every** one- and two-factor product, compares the generated set to the claimed three normal-form strata, and checks their cardinalities. For \(q=2,4\), it also tests closure of the asserted semigroup under right multiplication by every generator. It separately checks the generator action against direct \(2\times2\) products \(AX+XA\), using all matrix arguments for \(q=2,4\) and a deterministic sample for \(q=8\).

The verified counts are \(8\) generators, \(30\) two-factor products, and \(33\) total elements over \(\mathbb F_2\); \(64,892,907\) over \(\mathbb F_4\); and \(512,29688,29751\) over \(\mathbb F_8\). The verifier ends with `VERIFY_OK`. The finite computations are corroboration; the unrestricted theorem follows from Steps 1–6.

## Relationship to prior work
Gogić, Kazalicki and Tomašević (arXiv:2604.17664v1, first posted April 19, 2026) prove that normalized Jordan multiplication operators on full matrix algebras over fields of characteristic different from \(2\) generate the **entire** endomorphism semigroup. Their introduction explicitly notes that, in characteristic \(2\), the unnormalized symmetrized operators are commutators, kill the scalar matrices and have traceless image. Their finite-field corollary, later sections and explicit two-by-two appendix all maintain the exclusion of characteristic \(2\). Those observations do not determine which of the many possible singular endomorphisms actually occur as compositions, their ranks, their minimal lengths, or their number. The present characteristic-two classification addresses precisely that boundary.

A related published count for prescribed-commutator **graphs** of \(M_2(\mathbb F_q)\) concerns pairs of matrices satisfying a prescribed additive commutator equation, rather than compositions of the associated linear operators. The two invariants have different domains and neither count implies the other. General literature on the *adjoint semigroup of a ring* usually refers to the circle operation on ring elements, which is not this semigroup of compositions.

## Limitations
No classification is claimed for \(M_n(K)\) with \(n\ge3\), for characteristic other than \(2\), or for semigroups that also adjoin sums of operators or the identity transformation. Historical priority has not been independently established; an older equivalent result under inner-derivation semigroup terminology might exist. The exhaustive checks address only the stated small finite fields and do not replace the general algebraic argument.
