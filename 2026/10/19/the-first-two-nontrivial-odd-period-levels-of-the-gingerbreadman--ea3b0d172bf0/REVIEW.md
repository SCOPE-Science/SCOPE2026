# Scientific review: the first two nontrivial odd-period levels of the Gingerbreadman map

## Correctness — PASS

The statement refers to every point of \(\mathbb R^2\), not a bounded sampling domain. Each of the \(2^n\) weak-sign words yields a complete affine periodicity system; exact Fourier–Motzkin elimination is necessary and sufficient for existence, and every feasible odd-word system has a nonzero determinant and a directly replayed rational root. The explicit derived totals are \(1,1,11,1,1,23\) for \(n=1,3,5,7,9,11\). Prime-period orbit division produces two least-period-five and two least-period-eleven cycles. Direct integer matrix products give traces \(3,1,5,-5\), all away from the switching boundary. The embedded checker was rerun from the packaged source; no numerical simulation is promoted to an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS with source-access risk

Devaney's original 1984 article introduces the map and its zones of instability, but its full text could not be read, so it cannot be declared noncovering. The accessible abstract does not enumerate the requested period-eleven orbits. The later full-text inspection of Zolkin–Kharkov–Nagaitsev (arXiv:2201.13133, in particular its polygon-mapping discussion) and the MathWorld overview yielded polygonal invariant/rotation and familiar period-five facts, but did not imply a whole-plane unique orbit count at periods \(7,9,11\) or the two distinct period-eleven traces. Meaning-matched mathematical-result searches surfaced no relevant dominating record, but unsuccessful searches alone do not certify novelty. Historical-priority risk remains for unavailable earlier full text.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The cutoff at eleven is motivated by the **first return of realized odd primitive periods after the classical period-five island chain**: excluding both intervening odd periods, showing exactly two rational saddles at eleven, and assigning opposite signed Floquet traces give a complete, nontrivial small-resonance census for this nonsmooth conservative map. The fact is global over the whole plane and is not a textbook special case of the period-five polygons. It does not claim a large-period growth theorem.

## Sources and residual risks

The 1984 source is available in abstract/bibliographic form, but not in full; its possibility of earlier equivalent results is the primary priority limitation. The 2022/2023 polygon-mapping treatment is readable in full online and lacks the particular finite odd-period census in inspected sections. A compact exact symbolic computation is proof only for the enumerated lengths; no statement about longer odd cycles is made. The earliest foundational source is indexed at month resolution, March 1984, not at a verified exact day.
