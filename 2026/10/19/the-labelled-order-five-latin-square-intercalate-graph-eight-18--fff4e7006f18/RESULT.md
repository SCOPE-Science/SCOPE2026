# The labelled order-five Latin-square intercalate graph: eight \(18{,}000\)-state components of diameter \(13\)

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal L_5\) be the set of all Latin squares \(L=(L_{rc})_{0\le r,c<5}\) with rows, columns and symbols each labelled \(0,1,2,3,4\). Define the *intercalate-switch graph* \(\mathcal I_5\) on \(\mathcal L_5\) by joining \(L\) and \(L'\) precisely when they agree outside a two-row, two-column subarray of the form
\[\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&a\end{pmatrix},\qquad a\ne b,\]
and differ there by its replacement with \(\begin{pmatrix}b&a\\a&b\end{pmatrix}\).

**Theorem.** The graph \(\mathcal I_5\) has exactly \(161{,}280\) vertices and \(288{,}000\) edges. Its connected components consist of exactly \(17{,}280\) isolated vertices and eight nontrivial components, each on \(18{,}000\) vertices. Each nontrivial component is \(4\)-regular, vertex-transitive, and has radius and diameter \(13\). Every nontrivial component has the same full distance distribution from each of its vertices, at distances \(0\) through \(13\):
\[(1,4,12,36,108,252,612,1284,2256,3624,4812,3828,1112,59).\]

Define the parity triple \(\pi(L)\in\{0,1\}^3\) by taking modulo two, respectively, the numbers of odd row permutations, odd column permutations, and odd symbol-position permutations (for each symbol, its column as a function of row). Every intercalate switch preserves \(\pi\). Exactly four parity triples occur, namely
\[(0,0,0),\quad(0,1,1),\quad(1,0,1),\quad(1,1,0).\]
Each parity class consists of exactly \(4{,}320\) isolated vertices and two of the \(18{,}000\)-vertex connected components. Thus parity alone does *not* determine which labelled Latin squares are intercalate-reachable.

The \(56\) reduced order-five Latin squares partition into \(6\) intercalate-free and \(50\) intercalate-bearing squares. These two families form the two isotopy classes at order five: in particular, independent relabellings of rows, columns and symbols act transitively on all \(144{,}000\) nonisolated labelled squares.

## Assumptions and scope
A Latin square has every symbol exactly once in every row and column. Rows, columns, and symbols are **fixed distinct labels**; no isotopy, paratopy, or simultaneous relabelling identifies different vertices. The four entries of a switch are changed simultaneously. Distance is measured in the resulting simple undirected graph with one edge for each pair of distinct squares related by one switch. Isolated vertices do not have finite distance to other components. The diameter and radius claims concern each nontrivial component, *not* the disconnected full graph.

A reduced square has first row and first column in the order \(0,1,2,3,4\). For the parity triple, a row is a permutation of the symbol labels, a column is a permutation of the symbol labels, and each symbol gives a permutation mapping each row index to its column index. A coordinate is the sum of the corresponding permutation signs modulo \(2\).

## Proof
**1. Complete generation.** Fix the first row as the identity and recursively insert permutations as further rows, forbidding any repetition within columns. This gives exactly \(1{,}344\) squares with fixed first row; applying all \(5!=120\) symbol permutations gives \(161{,}280\) distinct labelled squares. An independently implemented generation fixes both the first row and column, enumerates \(56\) reduced squares, then chooses all \(4!=24\) orderings of the remaining rows and all \(5!=120\) symbol permutations. These constructions exhaust the same finite set because every labelled square has a unique normal form after symbol-normalizing its first row and ordering rows by their entries in column zero.

**2. Switch graph.** For each generated square, test all \(\binom52^2=100\) choices of two rows and two columns for the equal-diagonal/opposite-diagonal pattern. Swapping the two entries within each of the two selected rows produces the unique neighbor for that intercalate. The change is its own inverse and preserves the Latin condition. Two independently implemented exhaustive enumerations, one using reduced-square expansion and one using first-row-fixed expansion, independently construct and unite all such pairs. Both give degree histogram \(0:17{,}280\), \(4:144{,}000\), hence \(288{,}000\) undirected edges. Both produce precisely \(17{,}280\) singleton components and eight components of size \(18{,}000\). The complete lexicographically ordered component-assignment certificate, reconstructed by both programs byte-for-byte, accompanies this theorem.

**3. Parity and component splitting.** A switch exchanges exactly two values in each of two rows and each of two columns, so two signs flip in each of those two collections and their total parities remain unchanged. The position permutations for the two switched symbols each undergo one transposition, and the others remain fixed, so the symbol-position parity likewise remains unchanged. Exhaustive exact integer counts of the three signatures yield precisely the four profiles in the theorem; each contains \(4{,}320\) isolated squares and exactly two whole nontrivial connected components. The two independently implemented counters agree.

**4. Isotopy symmetry and distances.** Normalize any square by uniquely relabelling its first row as \(0,1,2,3,4\), then sorting the rows according to the first column. This gives a reduced representative. Iterating all independent row and column permutations of one mobile representative and reducing gives exactly the same \(50\) mobile reduced squares; the corresponding operation on one frozen representative gives exactly the \(6\) frozen reduced squares. Since normalization accounts for all symbol permutations, the full row/column/symbol isotopy group acts transitively on the \(144{,}000\) mobile labelled squares. Every isotopy is an automorphism of \(\mathcal I_5\), hence it acts transitively on the eight nontrivial components and the stabilizer of each component acts transitively on its vertices.

Finally, breadth-first search over *all* \(18{,}000\) vertices from the lexicographically first vertex of each of the eight nontrivial components, using the same independently checked switch rule and verifying every destination's certificate tag, gives the displayed identical distance histogram. The highest occupied distance is exactly \(13\). Vertex transitivity then makes this histogram valid from *every* vertex of each nontrivial component, proving radius and diameter \(13\) and the asserted isomorphism of the eight components. No finite sampling or partial enumeration is used in these steps.

## Verification
The accompanying 26-byte-per-state binary certificate consists of all \(161{,}280\) squares in lexicographic order as \(25\) row-major symbol bytes (values \(0\) through \(4\)), followed by a one-byte component tag. Tag \(0\) denotes an isolated vertex and tags \(1\) through \(8\) denote nontrivial components, ordered by their smallest square. It has exactly \(4{,}193{,}280\) bytes and SHA-256
`ce71d8163426d2c0d139614f33225224948d267553e76aa2ff6cda7bc5c556f6`.

The Python 3 standard-library program `verification/verify_latin5.py` and C++17 program `verification/verify_latin5.cpp` independently enumerate all states and switches, verify the count, degree and parity assertions, and reconstruct the same binary certificate. The independent structural checker `verification/check_structure.py` reads the certificate, verifies the two reduced-square isotopy orbits and performs exhaustive breadth-first search on all eight nontrivial components to establish their distance distributions. Exact build and replay commands and limits appear in `VERIFICATION.md`.

## Relationship to prior work
Ian Wanless, *Cycle Switches in Latin Squares* (2004), studies cycle-switch graphs on **isotopy or main classes** for orders through eight, including switches limited to intercalates, and observes parity phenomena. The present theorem concerns the much finer space of **all individually labelled** squares, whose \(161{,}280\) states, eight connected mobile components, frozen configurations, and sharp distance \(13\) are not consequences of the class-quotient statements reported in that publication's accessible abstract. The unavailable complete text leaves an originality risk that is stated below.

McKay and Wanless, *On the Number of Latin Squares* (first arXiv release 2009), gives the known enumeration of reduced and labelled squares. Its accessible full text discusses enumeration and symmetry, not their complete intercalate reconfiguration graph. McKay, Meynert, and Myrvold, *Small Latin Squares, Quasigroups, and Loops* (first online publication 2006), classifies isotopy and main classes through order ten rather than distances between labelled states. The total Latin-square and isotopy-class counts themselves are prior art here; the contribution is the labelled switching-component structure, parity splitting, and metric profile.

## Limitations
The theorem is about order five and switches on exactly \(2\times2\) intercalates. It does not cover row-cycle trades longer than two symbols, other Latin-square orders, or quotient graphs modulo isotopy. The conclusion is proved by independently cross-checked exhaustive finite computation and a finite certificate, not by a closed-form infinite-family classification. Full text of Wanless's directly relevant 2004 study was not accessible; its abstract was inspected, and possible detailed overlap in its full text, appendices or further unindexed work remains a genuine priority risk. Independent external scientific validation has not been performed.
