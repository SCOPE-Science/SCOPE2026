# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

For every \(n\ge3\), the conditional completion lemma decomposes each partially prescribed bijection into closed cycles and open directed chains; contracting chains leaves an exactly uniform permutation of \(n-3\) components. Counting one-, two-, and three-cycles among the prescribed arrows yields the six covariance values without asymptotic assumptions. The counted weights and the harmonic correction in equation (1) were independently reconstructed. The standalone exact-rational verifier passed 66,282 checks (all \(n!\) permutations through \(n=8\), plus combinatorial counts through \(n=150\)). Finite tests are corroboration, not the unrestricted proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The closest primary treatments establish cycle type with descent sets (Gessel--Reutenauer, 1993), cycle type with peak/valley statistics (Diaconis--Fulman--Holmes, 2011), and general cycle-type/descent generating functions (Gessel--Zhuang, 2020). These can cover peak and valley **sums**; they do not, on the inspected statements, determine 132 versus 231 or 213 versus 312 individually, which have the same local descent signatures. Parviainen (2006) uses patterns in cycle notation; that is not the same statistic. Fu--Li (2018) does explicitly refine 132/231 peak counts, but its inspected generating functions do not track total cycle count or the position of a particular window. Related published results on inversion indicators and cycle count contain only pairwise comparisons, not three-way rank events. A related six-pattern calculation for permutations conditioned to be one cycle involves different conditioning and does not imply the all-cycle covariance matrix. Residual priority risk: an uninspected classical full text or a more general joint pattern/cycle theorem might imply this table; no independent priority audit has occurred. The reviewed novelty is restricted to the exact six-by-position law and its rank-two structure, not aggregate peaks.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The cycle count and short consecutive patterns are natural complementary permutation observables, relevant to the joint study of cycle type and order pattern tests. The complete six-pattern classification separates the two peak subtypes (and the two valley subtypes) that descent-set statistics cannot distinguish, and shows their position effects cancel under aggregation. This sharp, all-size rank-two profile provides an exact finite baseline for joint cycle/order features rather than a small numerical improvement or an arbitrary parameter slice.
