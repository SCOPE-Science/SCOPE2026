# Three-cell trades of cyclic Latin transversals: parity-complete connectivity at order \(9\)

## Finding
Let \(D_9\) be the Latin square with rows, columns and symbols in \(\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z\) and cell \((r,c)\) carrying symbol \(c-r\). Define \(\mathcal T_9\) to have as vertices all transversals of \(D_9\); distinct transversals are adjacent exactly when they share six of their nine selected cells (equivalently, change exactly three rows at once). For a transversal represented by a permutation \(f:r\mapsto c\), put \(s(r)=f(r)-r\pmod 9\), also a permutation. Write \(\epsilon(f),\epsilon(s)\in\{-1,+1\}\) for their permutation signs.

**Theorem.** Two transversals of \(D_9\) lie in the same component of \(\mathcal T_9\) **if and only if** their ordered signs \((\epsilon(f),\epsilon(s))\) agree. The graph has \(2025\) vertices, \(4131\) edges, and precisely four connected components. Its full component profile is:

| \((\epsilon(f),\epsilon(s))\) | Transversals | Edges inside class | Diameter |
|:--:|--:|--:|--:|
| \((-1,-1)\) | 486 | 972 | 8 |
| \((-1,+1)\) | 486 | 972 | 8 |
| \((+1,-1)\) | 486 | 972 | 8 |
| \((+1,+1)\) | 567 | 1215 | 7 |

The degree distribution of the whole graph is \(486\) vertices of degree \(3\), \(1458\) of degree \(4\), \(27\) of degree \(6\), and \(54\) of degree \(15\); in particular no transversal is frozen. As a finite-order contrast under the **same** operation, at order \(7\) there are \(133\) transversals and \(196\) edges, with one connected component of size \(112\) and \(21\) isolated transversals; at order \(5\), all \(15\) transversals are isolated. These smaller-order counts are comparison data, not an assertion about every larger order.

## Assumptions and scope
All arithmetic is modulo the indicated order, and all permutations act on the labeled set \(\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}\). A transversal is a set containing exactly one cell of each row, column and symbol. Thus it is uniquely encoded by a column permutation \(f\) for which \(s(r)=f(r)-r\pmod n\) is a permutation. Our graph is on **labeled** transversals: it is not an isotopy-class graph, a graph of entire Latin squares, or a graph permitting moves through nontransversals. An edge has exactly three changed cells, not at most three, and intermediate configurations must be transversals. The difference table \(D_n\) is isomorphic to the cyclic-group addition table \(B_n\) by negating the row coordinate, so the source literature on cyclic-group-table transversals applies after relabeling. The claim is restricted to orders \(5,7,9\), with the parity-completeness theorem only at order \(9\).

## Proof
**1. Invariance.** Suppose transversals \(f\) and \(g\) agree outside a set \(R\) of three rows and disagree on every row in \(R\). Since both are column permutations, their images on \(R\) are the same three-element set. The permutation sending the three old images to the three new images has no fixed point and is therefore a \(3\)-cycle, which is even. Hence \(\epsilon(g)=\epsilon(f)\). The corresponding symbol permutations \(s_f(r)=f(r)-r\) and \(s_g(r)=g(r)-r\) likewise agree outside \(R\), have the same three symbol images on \(R\), and differ there by a fixed-point-free permutation; thus \(\epsilon(s_g)=\epsilon(s_f)\). This proves that every component is contained in one sign class. Distinct valid transversals cannot differ in just one row since their column maps are permutations. If they differed only at two distinct rows \(a,b\), the column maps would have to swap those two column values. The symbol maps would also have to swap their corresponding symbol values because all others are fixed. Then the new symbol at row \(a\) would simultaneously equal \(f(b)-a\) by definition and \(f(b)-b\) by the required swap, forcing \(a=b\pmod n\), a contradiction. Thus distinct valid transversals cannot differ in exactly one or two rows.

**2. Exhaustive upper bound and connectivity.** The following finite enumeration is a complete decision procedure, rather than random testing. Recursively fill rows \(r=0,1,\ldots,8\); at each row choose a column \(c\) not used earlier and require that \(c-r\pmod 9\) is also unused. At depth nine every leaf is a transversal, and every transversal has exactly one root-to-leaf route. This produces \(2025\) permutations. For each leaf and each of the \(\binom{9}{3}=84\) three-row sets, cyclically permute the three selected column values in either direction, and retain the result precisely when it is a transversal. Every allowed edge is obtained this way by Step 1, with each undirected edge represented twice. Explicit graph construction and breadth-first searches produce \(4131\) edges and four components, each equal to one of the four sign classes; full all-source breadth-first searches yield diameters \(8,8,8,7\) in the listed order. The complete executable implementation and its output are `verify_A.py` and `verify_A.out`.

**3. Independent exhaustive check.** Independently enumerate **symbol** permutations \(s\) and set \(f(r)=r+s(r)\pmod 9\), retaining precisely those with distinct column images. This again yields \(2025\) transversals without the row-by-row generator. For each set of six rows, place transversals in a bucket indexed by their six column entries and connect distinct members of a bucket. By Step 1, no two distinct transversals can agree on seven or eight rows, so each pair in such a bucket is exactly a legal three-cell trade and is counted once. This independent method gives \(4131\) edges, parity sizes \(486,486,486,567\), and exactly the same four connected components. It separately searches from one transversal in each sign class and checks that every member of that class is reached. See `verify_B.py` and `verify_B.out`.

Together Steps 1–3 prove both directions of the connectivity criterion and every finite enumerative assertion. The same algorithms applied with modulus \(7\) and \(5\) establish the comparison data.

## Verification
Run with Python 3 and no third-party packages: `python verify_A.py` and `python verify_B.py`. Both programs enumerate transversals exhaustively by different representations and construct the swap graph using different adjacency rules; they print their exact class counts, edge counts and degree histograms. The final success sentinels are `VERIFY_A_OK` and `VERIFY_B_OK`. `verify_A.py` exhaustively computes every vertex eccentricity at order \(9\); `verify_B.py` checks connectivity within each parity class by a second method. Code and captured actual output are included, with exact byte hashes in the private transport manifest.

## Relationship to prior work
Cavenagh and Wanless (available online 29 September 2009) studied the cyclic-group addition table's transversal counts and proved the spectrum of **pairwise intersection sizes**; their Theorem 5 allows intersections of size \(n-3\) for odd \(n\ne5\). That provides possible individual three-cell trades, but does not classify their iterated reachability. The total number \(2025\) at order \(9\) is established enumeration data and is not asserted as new. Wanless (2004) classified switching graphs whose **vertices are entire Latin squares**, not transversals of a fixed square, so those connectivity statements do not imply the present one. Best and Wanless (2019) explicitly defined the parity types of transversals; preserving these parity types under three-cell trades is an elementary obstruction. The substantive new claim is that these are **all** the obstructions at cyclic order \(9\), together with the exact sizes, edges, degree strata and diameters of the trade graph. Searches across publications and mathematical records did not identify a stronger theorem or a table encoding this graph, but that is not a global priority certificate.

## Limitations
The connectivity theorem is finite, at cyclic order \(9\). The smaller orders \(5\) and \(7\) show that a naive all-odd-orders extension is false. We do not claim irreducibility at higher odd orders, asymptotic mixing bounds, or unique publication priority. Exhaustive algorithms prove the stated finite graph facts but are not a symbolic proof for arbitrary order. Independent external mathematical audit has not been performed.
