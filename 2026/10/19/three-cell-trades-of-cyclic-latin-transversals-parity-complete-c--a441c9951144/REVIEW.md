# Scientific assessment

## Correctness (PASS)
The parity-invariance lemma is proved by permutation signs on the three altered rows. Both exhaustive generators are bijective with the labeled transversal space: one assigns unused columns and symbols row by row; the other assigns a full symbol permutation and checks the induced column permutation. Two different adjacency constructions then recover \(2025\) vertices, \(4131\) edges, four exact parity components and degree statistics, and an all-sources breadth-first search determines the four component diameters. The procedures have no truncation and have been executed on the embedded source files. The claim is finite at order \(9\), not a numerical extrapolation.

## Originality (PASS, subject to residual publication risk)
The closest relevant full-text source, Cavenagh–Wanless (2009/2010, Theorem 5), classifies attainable *intersection cardinalities*, which does not entail connectivity of the graph generated by successive three-cell changes. Wanless (2004) connects *complete Latin squares* by different moves, not transversals of one fixed square. Best–Wanless (2019, Lemma 5 and Definition 6) establishes general transversal parity types, but does not supply the order-nine sign-complete trade graph. The known count of \(2025\) transversals and the elementary parity obstruction are not asserted as new. Searches of relevant mathematical records yielded no dominating claim. Residual risk remains from unindexed computations or terminology such as triple trades.

## Value (PASS)
Three-cell changes are the first possible local transitions between distinct transversals of a Latin square; connectivity is necessary for their use as a constrained sampling or navigation move. The cyclic order-nine square is the smallest composite odd cyclic group table admitting transversals, so its complete parity-obstruction classification is a naturally selected case, not an arbitrary numerical slice. The result identifies exact components, their diameters, and a concrete contrast with frozen configurations at order \(7\). This establishes a sharp finite structural fact without claiming a general sampler or extension theorem.
