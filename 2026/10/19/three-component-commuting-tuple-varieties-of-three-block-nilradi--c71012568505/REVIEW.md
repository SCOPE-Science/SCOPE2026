# Scientific review: three-block nilradical commuting tuples

## Correctness — PASS

The matrix-product identity reduces all commutation conditions to \(u_iv_j=u_jv_i\). The proof treats separately tuples containing a mixed row and tuples with no mixed row. The first obey a single rank-one determinantal condition by a scalar-rigidity argument, and the second lie wholly in one of the two pure coordinate subspaces. Each set is algebraically closed and geometrically irreducible; explicit noncontainment witnesses require and use \(r,s,t\ge2\). The determinantal dimension follows from an outer-product parametrization. Direct equality of three component dimensions has the sole integral solution \((2,2,3)\). Finite-field point counting partitions pure tuples from nonzero mixed rank-one lines without double counting. The central \(Z_i\) factor is completely free. The arbitrary-field statements follow from algebra, not experimental extrapolation. The packaged verifier independently checks the equations and counts over nine small finite-field configurations.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The 2012 Goodwin–Röhrle work provides a particularly close predecessor: a two-component result for the parabolic boundary flag with blocks \((1,1,m)\), alongside general Borel nilradical results. That boundary example is not claimed as new. Its result on commuting pairs cannot directly imply the three noncontained components for \((r,1,s)\) with both \(r,s\ge2\), and it does not state an all-arity finite-field count. Goddard–Goodwin treat the entire parabolic Lie algebra, while Bulois–Evain consider all nilpotent elements of a parabolic subalgebra. Boos–Bulois focus on parabolic conjugation and commuting varieties; the inspected thesis and original sections do not state this all-arity classification. Accessible published research-record comparisons included Kronecker path-algebra commutator fibers, almost-abelian Lie-algebra tuple varieties, incidence-algebra commuting tuples, and centralizer counts of unitriangular groups. None has a direct implication giving the current rank-one plus two pure component theorem. Residual risk remains that an earlier determinantal reformulation already treats exactly these equations; absence from a bounded search is not a uniqueness proof.

## Value — PASS

The parabolic block-size boundary known in the literature motivates a clear structural question: what changes when neither exterior block is one-dimensional? The answer is not just a point count: it identifies three and only three geometrically irreducible components in every characteristic and isolates the unique equidimensional member \((2,2,3)\) within a three-parameter infinite family. The finite-field formula supplies the entire higher-commutativity hierarchy at once. This refines qualitative commuting-variety results by exact component geometry and arithmetic enumeration. Rewriting the problem as a tensor equation is a proof method, not the sole value claim.

## Closest literature and limits

Goodwin–Röhrle, arXiv:1209.1289v1, Remark 3.1, treats the adjacent \((1,1,m)\) two-component boundary; the new claim excludes that parameter range. Goddard–Goodwin, arXiv:1606.02262, Theorem 1.1, concerns entire parabolic Lie algebras. Bulois–Evain, arXiv:1306.4838, concerns nilpotent matrices inside parabolic Lie algebras. Boos–Bulois, arXiv:1606.08840, addresses parabolic conjugation and larger commuting spaces. Additional equivalent-formulation work could alter novelty priority. This result is limited to reduced varieties, block pattern \((r,1,s)\), and labeled tuples; it does not settle embedded scheme structure or simultaneous conjugacy classes.
