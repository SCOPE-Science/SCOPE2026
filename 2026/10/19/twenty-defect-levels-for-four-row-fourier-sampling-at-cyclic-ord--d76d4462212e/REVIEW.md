# Scientific review of the order-twelve Fourier-minor incidence result

## Correctness — PASS
The final claim is a finite exact theorem about \(495^2=245\,025\) possible four-by-four minors of the unnormalized order-twelve DFT. Affine row transformations leave \(d(R)\) invariant and divide all 495 four-row sets into 21 directly enumerated orbits. For each representative all 495 four-column determinants are decided both in the degree-four cyclotomic integer ring and by four separate finite-field Gaussian eliminations. The four primitive roots modulo 61 and the coefficient bound \(24<61\) convert the modular test into a rigorous if-and-only-if zero test, not merely numerical supporting evidence. The full certificate reproduces the 21 orbit counts and their aggregate 20-level distribution. The proof of the divisor-occupancy counterexample directly checks the sorted counts modulo 2, 3, 4, and 6. The independent implementations agree on every one of the 10,395 representative/column pairs; correctness here does not depend on floating-point thresholds.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge)
Caragea and Lee, *On the principal minors of Fourier matrices* (arXiv:2409.09793), prove existence/nonexistence properties for **principal** minors and squarefree-order phenomena; the examined text does not report the full four-row/off-principal distribution at order twelve. Cabrelli, Molter and Negreira, *Weaving Riesz Bases* (arXiv:2404.02229), develop Fourier principal-minor criteria for weaving, not numerical incidence of all 4-subsets of columns. Alexeev, Cahill and Mixon, *Full Spark Frames* (arXiv:1110.3548), provide Boolean full-spark and divisor-occupancy theory, not the 20-level defect-count law. The closest published order-twelve full-spark classification identifies the 24 zero-defect rows without counting the other 471 rows' bad column choices. The published all-modulus row-pattern theorem for \(\{0,1,2,4\}\) determines the 15-defect entry, but is restricted to one affine orbit. The nine-point affine-plane incidence theorem concerns \(\mathbb F_3^2\), not the cyclic order-twelve group. These statements do not imply the full table or the two equal-occupancy, unequal-incidence row patterns. An unindexed equivalent incidence table is a residual priority risk; absolute originality is not asserted.

## Value — PASS
Fourier-submatrix nonsingularity controls whether specified sampling/interpolation patterns are invertible. Existing full-spark results give only a yes/no verdict for a chosen row set, which discards the number of four-column configurations on which reconstruction fails. The order-twelve DFT is a natural first small mixed-primary **nonsquarefree** test case. Its 21 affine row types produce 20 distinct defect levels, varying from zero to 414 of 495 column sets, revealing a nontrivial distribution rather than a routine scaling of the order-ten case. Especially, identical divisor-occupancy multisets can yield 15 versus 27 defects: quantitative failure is not determined by the residue data that traditionally motivate full-spark criteria. The result is a complete natural finite invariant relevant to Fourier sampling, not an arbitrary truncated enumeration or an unsupported extrapolation.

## Scope and risks
The theorem is only for four-row/four-column submatrices of the cyclic DFT at twelve; it neither supplies universal composite-order formulas nor estimates small but nonzero singular values. Exhaustive finite arithmetic is a proof of this finite result, not an infinite theorem. An earlier quantitative table under a differently named Fourier-matroid or coding-theoretic invariant might escape the searched literature. No outside mathematical validation is represented.
