# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The key reduction is an exact integral group-ring argument: the two-by-two determinant has at most eight signed cyclotomic terms and zero augmentation. For a prime power with base prime at least five, a nonzero multiple of the cyclotomic polynomial with zero augmentation needs at least two full prime-sized coefficient fibers, contradicting the eight-term bound. Odd-order signed-class basis comparison then yields exactly the three modular alternatives, and the prime-square count follows by disjoint pair strata. The exceptional order-nine case is supported by exact finite-field nonvanishing of all 36 determinants. Exhaustive modular tests cover four prime-square orders plus other prime powers; finite evidence is not used as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with bibliographic limits.** Garcia--Karaali--Katz, Theorem 1.3, proves that all minors are nonzero at prime order and identifies a composite-order zero entry, but does not give the composite prime-power two-by-two criterion and census. Christie--Dykema--Klep treat general vanishing roots-of-unity sums, rather than this incidence problem. Published records about three-by-three *ordinary* Fourier minors at prime squares, two-dimensional affine Fourier matrices, and finite-field multiplicative compressions have different matrix objects; their stated theorems do not imply the compressed odd sine result. Searches do not establish priority and access to the full text of some indexed nearby records was unavailable; equivalent older formulations remain a residual risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The exact classification identifies all first nontrivial linear dependencies among pairs of odd Fourier measurements, with a prime-power rigidity principle, a closed prime-square incidence census, and a notable order-nine exception. This is an object-specific structural classification rather than an arbitrary numerical subcase, and it supplies exact information beyond nonvanishing-at-prime statements.

**Limitations.** The full classification requires base prime at least five; the order-nine exception is only a finite special case, larger minors are untouched, and independent audit remains outstanding. No claim is made of an optimal uncertainty inequality beyond the stated minor incidence.
