# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For every allowed real parameter and positive integer lag, the inverse-cylinder decomposition integrates the event correctly: the inverse branch maps the full unit interval to each disjoint cylinder with constant Jacobian magnitude, and its unique fixed point separates rising and falling comparisons. The absolutely convergent expansion by powers of the signed branch slope gives exact mode coefficients. All tail estimates use explicit sums of geometric weights, not unproved spectral assumptions. The algebraic comparison of the dominant bases establishes the two critical parameter values; both parameter-window limits follow from Taylor expansions with a uniformly subdominant remainder near each critical value. Independent exact-rational cylinder enumeration provides additional finite corroboration.

## Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority
The same skew tent map and some consecutive ordinal pattern distributions are published by Arroyo, Alvarez and Amigó (2008); this is the closest primary source and is not claimed anew. Abrams and coauthors (2010) study broader realizable ordinal distributions, and Unakafov and Keller (2014) treat ordinal entropy. Their inspected work does not state the specific arbitrary-lag comparison probability, its two spectral thresholds, or the \(1/n\)-scale resonance windows. Published research records on random skew tent dynamics and higher polynomial lag moments concern different observables. The 2022 periodic-points paper's full text was not accessible, and an implicit earlier derivation remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Pairwise ordinal order is a natural scale-invariant statistic for time series of deterministic interval maps. A full exact sign/rate classification across infinitely many lags reveals a genuine alternation threshold and a distinct repeated-mode polynomial prefactor, information not captured by ordinary first-order correlations or isolated short-order frequencies. The finite-time bound and narrow critical windows make the distinctions quantitatively testable. The result is restricted to full-branch skew tents and the Lebesgue stationary law.
