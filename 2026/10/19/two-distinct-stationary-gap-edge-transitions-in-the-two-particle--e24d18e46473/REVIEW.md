# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The domain is \(\lambda,g,v,\gamma>0\), two ordered run-and-tumble particles, and a stationary separation marginal with no independent thermal diffusion. The gap dynamics are derived directly from the two particle equations. Three-state switching rates and zero-current transport equations are independently reconstructed. The critical nonstandard step is the bounded competing-flux ratio near each edge, which turns the first-order mode equation into a logarithmic derivative with an integrable bounded remainder. It yields finite strictly positive leading constants, not just a guessed indicial exponent. The drifts' derivatives give exactly the stated exponents and critical rates. The 96 finite-parameter assertions passed, but the continuum conclusion is established analytically.

## Originality — PASS, with documented priority risk

The 2021 Le Doussal–Majumdar–Schehr source already develops general pair-separation stationary equations and a three-state switching representation, so those are **not new**. The 2023 Touzo–Le Doussal–Schehr active-Dyson study defines the particle system and considers finite-particle one-point position singularities; those are **not new**. The 2019 Das–Dhar–Kundu gap-statistics paper uses ring-constrained hard-core interactions and thermal diffusion rather than inverse-gap repulsion and harmonic confinement. The 2019 Basu et al. paper gives edge-shape transitions for three-state RTPs in a linear trap, not the nonlinear reciprocal separation force, which has different boundary slopes. The present explicit opposite-edge density powers, two distinct sharp phase thresholds, positive finite constants, and coupling-independent exponent-sum law were not found stated in the material read. A prior general equation that implies the boundary asymptotics is a real priority limitation; no absence-of-prior-work guarantee is asserted.

## Scientific value — PASS

Interparticle gap distributions are natural measures of active-particle clustering and are not determined by single-particle stationary marginals. A positive repulsive force changes the effective local relaxation differently at the two extremal separations. The full two-transition phase diagram establishes when small-gap clustering singularities coexist with large-gap depletion or divergence; the exact sum rule separates the tumbling-to-confinement ratio from the repulsion-dependent contrast. These are structural all-parameter facts on the motivating two-particle model rather than arbitrary isolated numerical constants.
