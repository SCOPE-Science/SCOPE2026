# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** Uniform Dirichlet cyclic spacings give the exact mixed moment for the isolation count and the uncovered length. Differentiation and a strictly convex logarithmic comparison show one zero below arc length \(1/3\), and a single-turn monotonicity argument shows one above it; the limiting scales follow from explicit expansions. Rational identities, lattice-quadrature comparisons, and decimal root checks are reproducible in `verify.py`. The finite checks support, rather than replace, the proof. Boundary cases outside \(n\ge5\) and \(0<a<1/2\) are not claimed.

**Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority.** Actual searches compared random arc isolation, gap counts, fixed-length circular coverage, clipped Dirichlet spacings, and exact covariance sign changes with published mathematical findings and primary literature. The closest detailed primary article (Moriarty et al., 2007) addresses Poisson approximation/error bounds for the uncovered-gap count, not the isolation–coverage mixed covariance. Huffer (1988) studies joint moments of uncovered-gap count and uncovered length; isolation depends on *adjacent* exceedances and is not determined by gap count alone. Accessible metadata for Huffer do not settle whether its full proofs indirectly imply the present covariance. That full paper and one related older work could not be materially inspected. Absence of a search hit is not asserted as proof of novelty.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The two distinct sign transitions give a complete finite-size dependence classification between two natural but competing coverage descriptors, and their \(1/n\) versus exponentially close-to-half scales are structurally different. In applications motivated by random fragment coverage, isolated arcs represent disconnected single fragments while union measure quantifies effective coverage; neither marginal alone determines this dependence. The second transition is especially easy to miss in fixed-precision experiments.
