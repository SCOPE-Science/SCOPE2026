# Two-site absorption on Boolean hypercubes: antipodal spectral redundancy and exact site probabilities

## Finding
For the continuous-time quantum walk generated by the adjacency Hamiltonian on the \(d\)-cube, two independent absorbing vertices produce an unexpected placement dichotomy. If they are distinct but not antipodal, the exactly absorbing spectral subspace has dimension \(2d\), regardless of their Hamming separation. If they are antipodal, its dimension drops to \(d+1\), exactly the same as with a single absorbing vertex. The complete eventual absorption probability from every initial vertex has an explicit Krawtchouk formula, with no dependence on either positive loss rate.

Specifically, let \(d\ge2\), let \(s\in\{0,1\}^d\) have weight \(h\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), and write \(C_k=\binom dk\) and
\[
K_k(u)=\sum_{j=\max(0,k-d+u)}^{\min(k,u)}(-1)^j\binom uj\binom{d-u}{k-j}.
\]
For any initial vertex \(x\) put \(w=|x|\) and \(z=|x\oplus s|\). If \(1\le h<d\), the eventual absorption probability is exactly
\[
P_{d,s}(x)=\frac1{2^d}\left[2+\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\frac{C_k\bigl(K_k(w)^2+K_k(z)^2\bigr)-2K_k(h)K_k(w)K_k(z)}{C_k^2-K_k(h)^2}\right].
\]
Every displayed denominator is strictly positive. If \(h=d\), then
\[
P_{d,s}(x)=\frac1{2^d}\sum_{k=0}^d\frac{K_k(w)^2}{C_k},
\]
which is identically the one-sink absorption law. In particular, the absorption probability averaged over all \(2^d\) initial vertices equals \(2d/2^d\) for \(h<d\), and \((d+1)/2^d\) for \(h=d\). Thus, in dimension five the uniform averages are \(5/16\) and \(3/16\), respectively.

## Assumptions and scope
The vertex set is \(V=\{0,1\}^d\), with bitwise addition \(\oplus\) and Hamming weight \(|x|\). Put \(H=JA(Q_d)\), with \(J>0\). There are two independent irreversible sinks at \(0\) and \(s\), with strictly positive rates \(\gamma_0\), \(\gamma_s\). Conditional on remaining in the walker's Hilbert space, the unnormalized state is propagated by
\[
T(t)=\exp(-itH_{\mathrm{eff}}),\quad H_{\mathrm{eff}}=H-\frac i2\bigl(\gamma_0|0\rangle\langle0|+\gamma_s|s\rangle\langle s|\bigr).
\]
For each localized initial state, define \(P_{d,s}(x)=1-\lim_{t\to\infty}\|T(t)|x\rangle\|^2\). All \(d\ge2\), all permitted sink separations, all vertices \(x\), all \(J>0\), and all positive rates are covered. The uniform-average formula includes starting vertices that are themselves sink locations.

## Proof
**1. Exact bright subspace and full-time convergence.** Walsh characters \(\chi_a(x)=2^{-d/2}(-1)^{a\cdot x}\), indexed by \(a\in V\), diagonalize \(H\): the energy of character \(a\) is \(J(d-2|a|)\). Let \(P_k\) be the orthogonal projector onto the Walsh-weight-\(k\) eigenspace. Define
\[
B=\bigoplus_{k=0}^d\operatorname{span}\{P_k|0\rangle,P_k|s\rangle\},\qquad D=B^\perp.
\]
Both \(B\) and \(D\) are invariant for \(H\), and every vector in \(D\) has zero amplitude at both sinks; hence \(D\) is invariant under \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}\), with purely unitary evolution. The same orthogonal decomposition also reduces the sink projectors. If a vector in \(B\) were an eigenvector of \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}\) with a real eigenvalue, taking imaginary parts of its inner-product eigenvalue equation would force zero amplitude at both sinks. It would then be an eigenvector of \(H\), orthogonal to \(P_k|0\rangle\) and \(P_k|s\rangle\) for its energy level, contrary to membership in \(B\). All eigenvalues of the finite-dimensional restriction of \(-iH_{\mathrm{eff}}\) to \(B\) consequently have strictly negative real part. Jordan blocks cannot obstruct convergence, so \(T(t)|_B\to0\), whereas \(T(t)|_D\) is unitary. Therefore \(P_{d,s}(x)=\|P_B|x\rangle\|^2\).

**2. Two-site Gram matrix.** For each \(k\), set \(u_k=P_k|0\rangle\) and \(v_k=P_k|s\rangle\). Walsh orthogonality gives exactly
\[
G_k=\begin{pmatrix}\langle u_k,u_k\rangle&\langle u_k,v_k\rangle\\\langle v_k,u_k\rangle&\langle v_k,v_k\rangle\end{pmatrix}
=2^{-d}\begin{pmatrix}C_k&K_k(h)\\K_k(h)&C_k\end{pmatrix}.
\]
At the extreme weights \(k=0,d\), both vectors are collinear, so each contributes rank one. If \(1\le h<d\) and \(1\le k<d\), there exist weight-\(k\) index vectors \(a\) with both even and odd overlap \(|a\cap s|\): that overlap ranges across all integers from \(\max(0,k-d+h)\) to \(\min(k,h)\), and this interval has at least two values because both \(h\) and \(d-h\) are positive. Hence \(|K_k(h)|<C_k\). The corresponding Gram determinant is positive, and its rank is two. Summing gives \(\dim B=2+2(d-1)=2d\). If \(h=d\), then \(\chi_a(s)=(-1)^{|a|}\chi_a(0)\), so \(v_k=(-1)^ku_k\) for all \(k\), and \(\dim B=d+1\). This also proves that adding an antipodal sink does not change \(B\).

**3. Localized-state projection.** Direct Walsh expansion yields
\[
\langle u_k|x\rangle=2^{-d}K_k(w),\qquad\langle v_k|x\rangle=2^{-d}K_k(z).
\]
For interior weights in the nonantipodal case, the squared length of the projection onto \(\operatorname{span}\{u_k,v_k\}\) is the Gram inverse quadratic form \(b_k^*G_k^{-1}b_k\), with \(b_k=2^{-d}(K_k(w),K_k(z))^T\). Multiplying out this matrix inverse gives exactly the interior summand shown in the finding, with the overall factor \(2^{-d}\). Weights zero and \(d\) each contribute \(2^{-d}\), producing the leading two. In the antipodal case, each weight contributes \(2^{-d}K_k(w)^2/C_k\) by projection onto the single ray \(u_k\). This proves the two formulas for every initial vertex.

**4. Uniform-average law.** The trace of an orthogonal projection equals its rank, so \(2^{-d}\sum_x P_{d,s}(x)=2^{-d}\operatorname{tr}P_B=2^{-d}\dim B\). Using the already established ranks proves both averages. Since the range \(B\) depends only on \(H\) and the set of nonzero absorbing vertices, the final absorption probabilities are independent of the numerical values of \(J>0\), \(\gamma_0>0\), and \(\gamma_s>0\).

## Verification
The bundled standalone script `artifacts/verify_two_sink.py` constructs independent Walsh spectral-projector matrices, computes the exact binomial Krawtchouk expression, and checks every starting vertex in all dimensions \(2\le d\le6\) and all distinct sink Hamming distances. It checks projector ranks and traces, and simulates loss evolution with unequal positive rates for dimensions \(2\le d\le5\), comparing long-time survival with the dark projection. It reports 739 successful finite checks. These checks corroborate the proof; no finite enumeration is used to infer its all-dimensional statements.

## Relationship to prior work
Le, Donati, Severini and Caruso (arXiv:1707.07482) characterized the dark subspace of a lossy quantum network via Hamiltonian eigenstates orthogonal to a target node. The proof explicitly uses that principle, but the inspected paper does not present the two-point hypercube Gram ranks or all-site Krawtchouk absorption law. Thiel and collaborators (arXiv:1906.08112 and arXiv:1909.02114) studied quantum first detection via repeated projective measurements, including hypercube-related symmetry examples, rather than independently rate-controlled simultaneous continuous-time absorbers. Their spectral-detection viewpoint is relevant, but does not directly imply the present exact two-sink classification. Classical hypercube spectral/Krawtchouk identities are standard tools, not claimed as new. The added mathematical content is the two-trap placement dichotomy and exact absorption probabilities for each initial vertex.

## Limitations
The model assumes ideal coherent adjacency hopping, two site-local irreversible sinks, and strictly positive constant sink rates. With a zero sink rate the bright subspace changes. The theorem concerns eventual absorption; time to absorption and finite-time decay rates can depend on the sink rates. Uniform averages include sink starting sites. The general dark-space spectral principle is established prior work, and an equivalent special-case consequence hidden in the broader controllability or strongly cospectral graph literature remains possible.
