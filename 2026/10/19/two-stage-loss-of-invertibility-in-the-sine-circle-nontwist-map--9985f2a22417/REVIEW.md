# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness

**PASS.** Differentiating the degree-one lift gives \(F'(x)=1+4\pi a b^2 g_r(2\pi x)\) with \(g_r(t)=\cos t(r-\sin t)\). The exact quadratic stationarity equation \(2\sin^2t-r\sin t-1=0\) has at most two admissible sine values. The proof orders their negative well depths, counts all level intersections including double zeros, and establishes strict monotonicity through the homeomorphism endpoint. Taylor series at the secondary-well disappearance verifies the \(3/2\)-power law. The standalone finite checker passed 144 consistency checks; the proof, not the finite checks, supports statements over the full parameter domain.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to a genuine historical priority risk.** The originating 2022 full text (sections II–V and Appendix) gives the model, says the criticality boundary is not straightforward, and analyzes fixed-point saddle-node curves from \(F'=1\). The present claim concerns global derivative zeros \(F'=0\), the distinct double-well thresholds, precise multiplicities, and the disappearance exponent; its implication is not contained in those fixed-point results. The classical one-harmonic and general bicritical-circle theories provide background but not this three-parameter formula. Searches of published mathematical findings, aliases, and related literature identified no covering statement. The full 2025 related publication was not obtainable from the accessible sources, and priority outside indexed literature remains uncertain.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The original study identifies criticality as the boundary of invertibility but leaves its location analytically unspecified. The result exactly closes that motivated three-parameter classification, including two-stage topology of the critical set, a nongeneric simultaneous two-pair transition, and a \(3/2\) blow-up as the shallow well disappears. It distinguishes the homeomorphism regime from the noninjective regime without claiming that every noninjective map is chaotic. The four-critical-point boundary is a natural global invariant of the model rather than an arbitrary numerical slice.
