# Scientific review: first-step Lyapunov projection

**Correctness — PASS.** The projected harmonic PMR correction is reconstructed from the published formula, giving the scalar coefficient \(a/[2(a^2+b^2)]\); independently minimizing the full residual gives \(a/(2a^2+b^2)\). Strict convexity proves the residual order. The Gramian representation and strict spectral Jensen inequality prove that the actual Frobenius-optimal coefficient exceeds Galerkin's \(1/(2a)\), reversing the ordering. A rational two-eigenvalue family proves the relative distortion diverges at rate \(K/(2\sqrt3)\), rather than merely exhibiting isolated bad numerical examples. All formulas were independently checked by the package's exact-rational verifier (3695 passed assertions). The verifier is finite corroboration, not the universal proof.

**Originality — PASS, subject to publication-priority uncertainty.** The closest source, Lund–Palitta (2023/2024, arXiv:2312.00463), introduces PMR, with its residual and positivity analysis; the novel claim is not the PMR formula. Lin–Simoncini (2013) introduces Lyapunov MR; Palitta–Simoncini (2019/2020) proves Galerkin energy-norm optimality. The assessed all-dimensional strict ordering in the **unweighted Frobenius solution norm** and the unbounded PMR/Galerkin solution-error ratio are not stated or supplied by those specific results as inspected. Relevant mathematical record searches found no direct covering statement; an unindexed equivalent argument remains possible. The energy-norm theorem is respected, not contradicted.

**Value — PASS.** Solvers for Lyapunov equations commonly use residuals as accessible progress measures. Here residual-minimizing and residual-improving extraction can be arbitrarily worse than Galerkin in the actual matrix solution error, even with positive-semidefinite approximations, on a natural one-step Krylov benchmark. The spectral-outlier family isolates the mechanism: vanishing starting-vector mass at a stiff mode leaves a nonvanishing Arnoldi correction, while the true solution and Galerkin approximation approach the same limit. This gives a concrete structural stress test of residual-based selection without requiring a claim about complete multi-step solvers.

**Limitations and remaining risk.** The result is restricted to exact arithmetic, selfadjoint negative-definite drift, rank-one forcing, and the first Krylov dimension; no higher-dimensional comparison is asserted. The 2013 and 2019 related full texts were materially inspected in the relevant formulations/theorems, but not every later discussion in every edition was exhaustively checked. Independent priority review remains outstanding.
