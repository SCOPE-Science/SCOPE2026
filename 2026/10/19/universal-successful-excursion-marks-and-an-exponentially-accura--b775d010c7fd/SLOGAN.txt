When a Brownian searcher on a circle repeatedly relocates to sites whose density scales like a power near its target, the last successful excursion has a universal joint Gamma-mark law: its rescaled starting distance and duration have correlation exactly \(1/\sqrt2\). For symmetric power-law relocation, the full joint law is approximated by its half-line limit with an explicit exponentially small total-variation error, and the mean search time changes high-rate behavior at target-density exponent \(1\).
