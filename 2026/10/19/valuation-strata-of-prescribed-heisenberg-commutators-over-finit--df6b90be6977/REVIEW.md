# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

For each fixed nonzero horizontal vector, the commutator pairing is an \(R\)-linear functional whose image is precisely the ideal generated by that vector's coordinates. The proof establishes its kernel size by a finite-group image–kernel count and proves the exact cardinalities of the valuation strata. A zero-vector contribution is included only for the zero target. Central-coordinate independence and the strict positivity of adjacent stratum contributions complete the group-fiber law. The proof treats arbitrary finite commutative chain rings (including characteristic two) without requiring a field. Standalone enumeration checks match every target over several nonisomorphic rings and independently evaluate group commutator words in small examples.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The arithmetic formulas and their strict valuation stratification concern \(H_m(R)\) over finite chain rings of length at least two. Diaconis–Malliaris (arXiv:2107.02923) treat primarily finite-field Heisenberg groups and the zero-commutator count. Nath–Yadav (arXiv:1212.4306) and Naik (arXiv:1805.00091) establish foundational fiber machinery and separate examples with only two conjugacy class sizes; in contrast the groups studied here have \(n+1\) conjugacy class sizes. The existence of arbitrarily many distinct fibers and the length-one field case are not claimed as new. Published results on commuting graphs determine zero fibers in overlapping rank-one cases, not the full positive-target valuation law. A general character-theoretic or local-ring reference could still imply the formula through specialization; that remains a bibliographic risk rather than a proven absence.

## Value — PASS

This determines an exact natural word-map invariant for an infinite family of nilpotent finite groups. Every nonidentity target has an explicit fiber size; the \(n\) valuation levels have sharp, unequal counts; and the formulas are uniform across possibly nonisomorphic chain rings with the same residue-field size and length. These statements extend the finite-field fiber calculation and reveal a structural connection between ideal filtration and commutator statistics.
