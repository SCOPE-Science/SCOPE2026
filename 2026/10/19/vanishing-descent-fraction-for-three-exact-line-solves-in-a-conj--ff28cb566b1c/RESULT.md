# Vanishing descent fraction for three exact line solves in a conjugate proximal-Newton plane

## Finding
For every integer \(N\ge3\), an explicit strongly convex two-variable composite quadratic has a full two-dimensional Hessian-conjugate subspace for which two *independent* exact one-dimensional proximal Newton minimizations, followed by a third exact minimization along their combined direction, recover only the fraction
\[
R_N=\frac{N^{-2}[1+(2-N^{-1})^2]^2}{2[1+(2-N^{-1})^2N^{-2}]}
\]
of the decrease available to exact joint two-dimensional minimization. In particular, \(\lim_{N\to\infty}N^2R_N=25/2\), so there is **no positive uniform fraction-of-optimal-decrease guarantee** for this three-line construction under strong convexity and exact local curvature alone. The first line is a genuine preconditioned proximal-gradient direction generated by \(P=A^{\mathsf T}A\) with the full-rank linear operator \(A\) in the composite regularizer, not an independently assigned arbitrary direction.

## Assumptions and scope
Fix an integer \(N\ge3\) and abbreviate \(\delta=N^{-1}\), \(h=2-\delta\), and \(q=h\delta^2\). On \(\mathbb R^2\), set
\[
A=\operatorname{diag}(1,N),\qquad g(z)=|z_1+\delta z_2|,
\]
\[
f(x)=\tfrac12(x_1^2+x_2^2)-(1+\delta)x_1+(1-\delta)x_2,
\quad
\Phi(x)=f(x)+g(Ax)=f(x)+|x_1+x_2|.
\]
The base point is \(x=0\), the exact smooth Hessian is \(H=I_2\), and the structure-induced preconditioner is \(P=A^{\mathsf T}A=\operatorname{diag}(1,N^2)\). Define the first basis vector \(v\) as the **unique exact** preconditioned proximal-gradient direction
\[
v=\arg\min_d\left\{\nabla f(0)^{\mathsf T}d+g(Ad)+\tfrac12d^{\mathsf T}Pd\right\}.
\]
Choose a second, already Hessian-conjugate basis vector \(s=(q,\delta)^{\mathsf T}\). Let \(t_1\) and \(t_2\) be independent minimizers of \(\Phi(t v)\) and \(\Phi(t s)\) over \(t\in\mathbb R\), each starting at the common base \(0\). Set \(\widetilde d=t_1v+t_2s\), and then choose \(t_3\) minimizing \(\Phi(t\widetilde d)\) over \(t\in\mathbb R\). Compare the resulting decrease \(\Phi(0)-\Phi(t_3\widetilde d)\) against the ideal decrease \(\Phi(0)-\min_{d\in\operatorname{span}\{v,s\}}\Phi(d)\). These are *model* minimizations with exact curvature; no assertion is made about realizability of \(s\) along a particular historical trajectory of a multi-iteration algorithm.

## Proof
**The first direction and conjugacy.** Since \(0<q<\delta\), the vector \(v=(\delta,-q)^{\mathsf T}\) has positive coordinate sum. The gradient at the base point and preconditioned vector are
\[
\nabla f(0)=(-1-\delta,1-\delta)^{\mathsf T},\qquad Pv=(\delta,-h)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Thus \(\nabla f(0)+Pv+(1,1)^{\mathsf T}=0\), the full optimality condition for the strongly convex proximal subproblem, so its unique minimizer is \(v\). Also \(v^{\mathsf T}Hs=\delta q-q\delta=0\), and \(\det[v,s]=\delta^2+q^2>0\); the conjugate pair spans all of \(\mathbb R^2\).

**First exact line solve.** Set \(K=\delta^2+q^2>0\) and \(D=\delta^2+h q=\delta^2(1+h^2)>0\). For \(t\ge0\), direct substitution gives
\[
\Phi(tv)=\tfrac12Kt^2-Dt.
\]
At \(t=0\), the right derivative is \(-D<0\), and the left derivative is \(-D-2(\delta-q)<0\). Strict convexity of \(t\mapsto\Phi(tv)\) then implies that its unique global minimizer is
\[
t_1=D/K>0,\qquad \Phi(t_1v)=-D^2/(2K).
\]

**Second exact line solve.** Write \(C=\nabla f(0)^{\mathsf T}s=(-1-\delta)q+(1-\delta)\delta\), and \(S=\delta+q>0\). Then
\[
\Phi(ts)=\tfrac12Kt^2+Ct+S|t|.
\]
The two strict inequalities
\[
S-C=\delta^2+(2+\delta)q>0,\qquad S+C=\delta(h-q)>0
\]
show that \(|C|<S\). Hence \(t_2=0\) is the unique exact minimum, even though the full subspace has dimension two. The combined direction is therefore \(\widetilde d=t_1v\), and its third exact ray minimizer is \(t_3=1\).

**Exact joint optimum and ratio.** The matrix \(\nabla^2f=I_2\) makes \(\Phi\) strictly convex. At \(x^*=(1,-1)^{\mathsf T}\), the smooth gradient is \((-\delta,-\delta)^{\mathsf T}\), while \(x_1^*+x_2^*=0\) and \(\delta(1,1)^{\mathsf T}\) belongs to the absolute-value subdifferential (because \(0<\delta<1\)). The convex optimality condition gives the unique global minimizer \(x^*\). Direct evaluation yields \(\Phi(0)=0\) and \(\Phi(x^*)=-1\), so the exact joint decrease is one. The three-line decrease is \(D^2/(2K)\). Substituting \(q=h\delta^2\) proves
\[
R_N=\frac{D^2}{2K}=\frac{\delta^2(1+h^2)^2}{2(1+h^2\delta^2)}.
\]
As \(\delta\downarrow0\), \(h\to2\), giving \(R_N\sim25/(2N^2)\). Every \(R_N\) is strictly positive, but their infimum is zero. This establishes the claimed lack of a positive uniform relative-decrease lower bound.

## Verification
The included standalone `verify.py` uses only Python standard-library exact fractions. It checks the preconditioned optimality equations, conjugacy, linear-independence determinant, strict kink inequalities, three exact line-solve formulas, the joint nonsmooth optimality certificate, and the independent direct objective evaluation for \(3\le N\le256\). It also checks exact benchmarks and rational bounds on the asymptotic limit. Run `python verify.py`. This finite replay corroborates identities; the all-\(N\) assertion follows from the symbolic inequalities in the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Chen and Yang, *Preconditioned Proximal Gradient Methods with Conjugate Momentum: A Subspace Perspective* (public 2026-03-16), give the structure-aware preconditioner in equation (6) and the exact independent one-dimensional model solves plus combined-ray refinement in equations (19)–(22), with implementable curvature approximations in equations (24)–(27). Their theorem proves global convergence under stated conditions, not a fixed fraction of the **joint model decrease** at each step. The present example concerns that separate approximation-quality question and does not dispute their convergence theorem.

Chen and Yang, *Second-order Methods for Multiobjective Composite Optimization: Preconditioning Strategies, Subspace Variants and Inexact Solutions* (2026-06-25), discuss separable surrogate models for a related multiobjective construction in sections 4.3–4.5. The inspected discussion does not supply this scalar three-line fraction under the linear-operator-aware preconditioner. Tseng's 2001 work addresses general nonsmooth block-coordinate convergence phenomena, which motivate caution about coordinate splitting; no novelty is claimed for the broad observation that coupled nonsmooth terms may spoil separability.

## Limitations
The quantified obstruction applies to the **local exact three-line subproblem** with a chosen Hessian-conjugate second direction, not to a proven trajectory of the full iterative algorithm, its Armijo globalization, or its total iteration complexity. In the family, the smooth Hessian condition number equals one, but the linear operator and the preconditioner become ill-conditioned as \(N\to\infty\); the theorem does not rule out guarantees that depend on those conditioning parameters. More recent revisions of the motivating manuscript and older specialized coordinate-splitting results could contain an equivalent formulation; independent originality review remains outstanding. No computational experiment by itself proves the infinite-family statement.
