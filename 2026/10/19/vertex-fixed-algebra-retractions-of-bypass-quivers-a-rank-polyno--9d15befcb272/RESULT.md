# Vertex-fixed algebra retractions of bypass quivers: a rank polynomial with multiplicative gaps

## Finding
Let \(q\) be any prime power, and let \(r,s,t\ge1\). Form the acyclic quiver \(Q_{r,s,t}\) with vertices \(1,2,3\), with \(r\) arrows from \(1\) to \(2\), \(s\) arrows from \(2\) to \(3\), and \(t\) direct arrows from \(1\) to \(3\). Let \(A=\mathbb F_q Q_{r,s,t}\) be its unital path algebra, and let \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) denote the distinguished primitive vertex idempotents. An **anchored retraction** is an \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear unital algebra endomorphism \(F:A\to A\) with \(F(e_i)=e_i\) for all three vertices and \(F\circ F=F\).

For \(0\le k\le n\), write \({n\brack k}_q=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(q^{n-j}-1)/(q^{k-j}-1)\), with the empty product equal to one, and put \(I_q(n,k)={n\brack k}_q q^{k(n-k)}\). The complete vector-space image-rank generating polynomial of anchored retractions is
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathcal R_{r,s,t;q}(z)=\sum_{a=0}^{r}\sum_{b=0}^{s}\sum_{d=0}^{t}
&I_q(r,a)I_q(s,b)I_q(t,d)\\
&\quad\cdot q^{d(rs-ab)+(t-d)ab}\,z^{3+a+b+d+ab}.
\end{aligned}}
\]
All sums are finite and their coefficients count *distinct endomorphisms*, rather than abstract image isomorphism classes. Their attainable ranks are **exactly**
\[
\boxed{\left\{2+uv+d:1\le u\le r+1,\ 1\le v\le s+1,\ 0\le d\le t\right\}.}
\]
The constant-at-the-arrows projection is the unique rank-three anchored retraction, the identity is the unique full-rank anchored retraction, and, writing \(N=3+r+s+t+rs\), the number of anchored retractions of rank \(N-1\) is
\[
\boxed{[z^{N-1}]\mathcal R_{r,s,t;q}(z)={t\brack1}_q q^{t-1+rs}.}
\]
The rank spectrum can have gaps. In particular, for \((r,s,t)=(2,2,1)\), it is \(\{3,4,5,6,7,8,9,11,12\}\): rank \(10\) does not occur over **any** finite field. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), the polynomial is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal R_{2,2,1;2}(z)=z^3+28z^4+194z^5+104z^6+288z^7+48z^8+48z^9+16z^{11}+z^{12}.}
\]

## Assumptions and scope
All ranks are ranks of the underlying \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear maps \(F:A\to A\), not dimensions of quotients or ranks of semigroup generating sets. The anchored (vertex-fixed) condition is part of the mathematical object: endomorphisms that permute, merge, or move the distinguished vertex idempotents are not counted. The quiver has no loops or arrows other than the specified three families; all length-two paths are retained without relations. The characteristic of \(\mathbb F_q\) is arbitrary. Composition, not multiplication in the path algebra, defines idempotence of \(F\).

## Proof
Write \(X\cong\mathbb F_q^r\), \(Y\cong\mathbb F_q^s\), and \(W\cong\mathbb F_q^t\) for the spans of arrows from \(1\) to \(2\), from \(2\) to \(3\), and directly from \(1\) to \(3\), respectively. The length-two path space is \(Z=X\otimes Y\), of dimension \(rs\). The path basis gives the direct sum
\[
A=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{3}\mathbb F_qe_i\ \oplus X\oplus Y\oplus W\oplus Z.
\]
Apart from multiplication by vertex idempotents, the sole nonzero product of positive-length paths is the natural concatenation \(X\times Y\to Z\), \((x,y)\mapsto x\otimes y\). In particular, the vertex-corner spaces are \(e_1Ae_2=X\), \(e_2Ae_3=Y\), and \(e_1Ae_3=W\oplus Z\).

An algebra endomorphism fixing the three vertex idempotents must preserve those corners. Its restrictions to \(X\) and \(Y\) are arbitrary linear maps \(S\in\operatorname{End}(X)\) and \(T\in\operatorname{End}(Y)\). Its restriction to \(W\) is an arbitrary pair of linear maps \(D:W\to W\) and \(B:W\to Z\), so that \(F(w)=D(w)+B(w)\). Multiplicativity forces \(F|_Z=S\otimes T\), and imposes no additional constraints: the path algebra is freely generated by its vertices and arrows with exactly the path-concatenation rules. These four maps \((S,T,D,B)\) uniquely determine \(F\).

Let \(P=S\otimes T\). The condition \(F\circ F=F\) holds if and only if
\[
S^2=S,\qquad T^2=T,\qquad D^2=D,\qquad BD+PB=B.
\]
The first two equations imply \(P^2=P\). Set \(a=\operatorname{rank}S\), \(b=\operatorname{rank}T\), and \(d=\operatorname{rank}D\); then \(\operatorname{rank}P=ab\). Split \(W=\operatorname{im}D\oplus\ker D\) and \(Z=\operatorname{im}P\oplus\ker P\). Evaluating the fourth equation on the two summands of \(W\) proves its precise equivalence to
\[
B(\operatorname{im}D)\subseteq\ker P,\qquad B(\ker D)\subseteq\operatorname{im}P.
\]
Thus, for specified projections \(S,T,D\), the number of permitted \(B\) is \(q^{d(rs-ab)+(t-d)ab}\): the first summand of \(W\) has dimension \(d\) with \((rs-ab)\)-dimensional target, and the second has dimension \(t-d\) with \(ab\)-dimensional target.

The number of idempotent linear maps of rank \(k\) on an \(n\)-dimensional vector space over \(\mathbb F_q\) is \(I_q(n,k)\): choose their image among \({n\brack k}_q\) subspaces and then their kernel among \(q^{k(n-k)}\) complementary subspaces. Since all choices of \(S,T,D,B\) are unique and independent subject only to the displayed conditions, multiplying and summing gives exactly the asserted polynomial.

On \(W\oplus Z\), the linear map of \(F\) has block form \(\begin{psmallmatrix}D&0\\B&P\end{psmallmatrix}\), which is a projection. Its image projects onto \(\operatorname{im}D\) along \(W\) and has intersection \(\operatorname{im}P\) with \(Z\), so its rank is \(d+ab\). Including vertices, \(X\), and \(Y\) gives \(\operatorname{rank}F=3+a+b+d+ab=2+(a+1)(b+1)+d\). Every triple \((a,b,d)\) in the indicated ranges occurs over every finite field, and all summands in the polynomial are positive, proving the exact rank spectrum.

The minimal rank is three, occurring only at \(a=b=d=0\), in which case the fourth equation forces \(B=0\). The full rank \(N\) requires \(a=r,b=s,d=t\); all three projections are identities and \(B=0\), so this is the identity endomorphism. For rank \(N-1\), lowering \(a\) from \(r\) costs at least \(s+1\ge2\) dimensions, and lowering \(b\) costs at least \(r+1\ge2\). Hence necessarily \(a=r,b=s,d=t-1\). There are \({t\brack1}_q q^{t-1}\) rank-\((t-1)\) projections \(D\), and \(q^{rs}\) permitted shears \(B\), proving the boundary coefficient. Finally, for \((r,s,t)=(2,2,1)\), the possible products \((a+1)(b+1)\) are \(1,2,3,4,6,9\); allowing \(d=0,1\) gives precisely the displayed spectrum, including its missing rank.

## Verification
Run `python check.py` with Python 3. Its exhaustive test independently enumerates all linear maps on the arrow-corner spaces for six small cases; it builds the induced linear map on the full path algebra, checks multiplication on every pair of path-basis elements, tests composition-idempotence on the full basis, computes actual matrix ranks by finite-field elimination, and matches the coefficient table with the finite sum. This includes characteristic two, an odd prime field, and \(\mathbb F_4\). The \((q,r,s,t)=(2,2,2,1)\) case examines all 8,192 vertex-fixed endomorphisms and obtains exactly 728 retractions distributed by the polynomial printed above. All six cases returned `ALL_EXACT_CHECKS_PASSED`. These exhaustive *finite* tests check the formulas only for their listed parameter values; the general result relies on the preceding proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Iusenko and MacQuarrie, *The path algebra as a left adjoint functor* (first public 20 April 2017), formalize quiver-to-algebra morphisms. In their Section 4.4 they exhibit the same seven-dimensional triangle-with-bypass quiver and an automorphism replacing a direct arrow by that arrow plus a length-two path. This already establishes the relevance of the shear \(B\) and is not claimed as new. Their Section 7 studies morphisms up to an equivalence relation; the inspected work does not state a rank-enumeration theorem for composition-idempotent vertex-fixed endomorphisms. Iovanov and Sistko, *Maximal Subalgebras of Finite-Dimensional Algebras* (first public 2 May 2017), Theorem 4.1 and Proposition 4.2, classify maximal subalgebras via vertex mergers and removal of arrow directions; this does not classify split-image retractions or count them by rank. Gargate and Gargate, *\((k+1)\)-potent Matrices in triangular matrix Groups and Incidence Algebras of Finite Posets* (first public 30 August 2020), count potent **elements** of triangular/incidence algebras rather than idempotent **algebra endomorphisms**. Ordinary Gaussian-binomial projection counts are a prior ingredient, not a new discovery. The new claim is the explicitly weighted complete rank polynomial and the resulting multiplicative rank gaps for this vertex-fixed path-algebra endomorphism monoid.

## Limitations
This does not enumerate all unital algebra endomorphisms of \(A\) or those that move the vertex idempotents. The theorem is for path algebras with precisely three vertices and specified parallel-arrow families; it makes no assertion for quivers with relations or longer paths. The finite checker is neither an infinite proof nor independent scholarly validation. Although targeted literature comparison found no covering formula, an equivalent result under quiver-transvection or parabolic-endomorphism terminology may exist; no claim of exhaustive novelty across all literature is made.
