# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The simple socle is m² because the latter is nonzero and is killed by m. For any nonzero ideal I, the last nonzero m^j I lies in its intersection with the socle, so I contains m², also when I=m². A proper ideal lies in m. The action on m/m² factors through k=R/m; thus the quotient subspace lattice gives exactly the nonzero proper ideals, plus boundary ideals 0 and R. Multiplication of lifts is well-defined because m·m²=0, and k-bilinear by taking a lift of the residue scalar and using the same quotient action on the socle. A radical vector lifts into Ann(m)=m², proving perfection with no splitting and including characteristic two. The product vanishes iff W is contained in U-perp, whose dimension is d−a; this yields the stated Gaussian factor even for a or b zero or a+b>d. Summation gives H_q(d); in an ordered tuple, a zero factor or three proper factors makes the product zero. With no zero factors, the counts of nonzero products with zero, one and two proper entries are 1, sG, and choose(s,2)(G²−H); the ordered pair occupies the two positions in their natural order. This proves the formula for all s>=2 with no exclusion on repeats. In d=1 the lattice is the four-ideal chain, giving G=2 and H=3. Direct replay recovers small ideal lattices by sums of principal ideals: characteristic four gives seven ideals and 25/49 for s=2 without a coefficient field, and the two ternary forms each give 15/32 but respectively 3 and 1 nonzero square-zero ideals. It also verifies the refined pair counts and exact s=3,4 probabilities in all three examples. The replay corroborates the proof and is not used to generalize its quantifiers.
