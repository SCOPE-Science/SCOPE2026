# Review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The complement-group claim follows from the published wedge theorem when the second factor has simply connected complement. The local-genus bound follows from the wedge formula and the genus-one property of the attached factor. The intrinsic recovery proof handles both cases \(g_x(C)=0\) and \(g_x(C)\ge1\), and uses finiteness of the base genus-one locus to remove its isolated points from the derived set.

## Originality

**PASS, with bounded-search limitations.** The 2013 paper explicitly asks whether same-fundamental-group Cantor-set examples can have bounded local genus, while its construction uses unbounded genus markers. The 2014 paper supplies a pairwise inequivalent family with local genus one everywhere. The new comparison is the use of the derived set of the genus-one locus to recover that factor and keep the wedge family bounded. published-finding corpus semantic searches returned unrelated finite-space and graph-homology records; this is supporting deduplication evidence, not a proof of novelty.

Closest literature:
- Garity–Repovš (2013), Theorems 4.7 and 5.4, Theorem 1.4, and Question 8.1.
- Garity–Repovš–Wright (2014), Theorem 4.2, Lemma 4.3, and Theorem 4.4.

## Value

**PASS.** The result gives a precise sufficient condition for a bounded-local-genus family of inequivalent Cantor embeddings with identical complement fundamental group. It addresses a named gap in the earlier paper without claiming a full solution for arbitrary base Cantor sets.

## Limitations

The result is conditional. No explicit base \(C\) with nontrivial complement group and finite genus-one locus was established in this run, so the theorem should not be read as a full resolution of the earlier open question.
