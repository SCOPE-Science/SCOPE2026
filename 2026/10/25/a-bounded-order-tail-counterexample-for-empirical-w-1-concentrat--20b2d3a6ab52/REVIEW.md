# Review

**Correctness — PASS.** The density perturbations are nonnegative and normalized because their perturbation has zero integral. The Gaussian exponential-moment identity shows that \(\mu_+\) and \(\mu_-\) have the same moments \(e^{k^2/2}\) for every integer \(k\ge0\), while their densities differ. Conditional independence and equality of the conditional moments give the stated \(O_k(n^{-1})\) variance formula. Conditional expected-\(W_1\) convergence and the one-dimensional CDF formula give the exact positive limit.

**Originality — PASS, narrowly scoped.** Searches of the mathematical finding index found no result stating this exact combination of bounded-order \(\Psi_R\) control, vanishing variance of every fixed empirical power moment, and the exact nonzero expected-\(W_1\) limit. Classical moment indeterminacy covers the underlying mechanism; the present statement is limited to the explicit empirical-measure counterexample to weakening the recent theorem's tail assumption.

**Value — PASS.** The result identifies a precise failure mode for a plausible weakening of a recent concentration theorem: finite-order tail control and concentration of all fixed empirical moments do not by themselves force expected \(W_1\) convergence under dependence. The exact gap makes the obstruction quantitative rather than merely qualitative.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Amini–Vinas, arXiv:2601.07228v1, Theorem 3 and its proof, requires a controlled moment order tending to infinity. Lin (2017) records the classical Fréchet–Shohat caveat and the lognormal Stieltjes class. The construction is an elementary use of these classical ingredients, so the originality claim is restricted to this exact empirical-\(W_1\) boundary statement; no global historical-priority claim is made.
