# A complete orbit census of smooth binary filling curves of bidegree \( (4,4) \)

## Finding
On \(Q=\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf F_2}\times\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf F_2}\), there are exactly \(2{,}328\) geometrically smooth curves of bidegree \((4,4)\) containing all nine points of \(Q(\mathbf F_2)\), counted as bihomogeneous defining equations (necessarily unique up to the sole nonzero scalar in \(\mathbf F_2\)). Their complete orbit decomposition under \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbf F_2}(Q)=(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_2)^2)\rtimes C_2\) has **34 orbits**: 31 of size 72, two of size 36, and one of size 24. In particular, their stabilizer orders are 1, 2, and 3 respectively. All are geometrically integral curves of genus nine.

## Assumptions and scope
A bidegree-\((4,4)\) form means
\[
F=\sum_{i=0}^4\sum_{j=0}^4 c_{ij}X_0^{4-i}X_1^iY_0^{4-j}Y_1^j,\qquad c_{ij}\in\mathbf F_2.
\]
Its coefficient mask has bit \(5i+j\) equal to \(c_{ij}\). A *filling* curve satisfies \(F(P)=0\) at all nine rational points. Geometric smoothness means absence of singular points over \(\overline{\mathbf F}_2\), not merely at rational points. The 34 orbits classify embedded curves under the specified automorphism group, not abstract isomorphism classes.

## Proof
1. The vector space of bidegree-\((4,4)\) equations has dimension 25. Evaluation at the nine rational points has rank nine, as also witnessed by tensor products of sections interpolating the three points on each copy of \(\mathbf P^1\). Hence its filling kernel has dimension 16, with exactly \(2^{16}-1=65{,}535\) nonzero elements.

2. Set \(D_X=X_0^2X_1+X_0X_1^2\) and \(D_Y=Y_0^2Y_1+Y_0Y_1^2\). Each vanishes on every rational point in its corresponding factor. The 20 monomial multiples of bidegree \((4,4)\) (ten for each of \(D_X,D_Y\)) span a 16-dimensional space, thus exactly the entire filling kernel. This furnishes a second, independently built enumeration of its \(65{,}535\) nonzero equations.

3. On each of the four standard affine charts, a curve is geometrically smooth precisely when the ideal \((f,\partial f/\partial x,\partial f/\partial y)\) equals \((1)\) in \(\mathbf F_2[x,y]\). Indeed the Hilbert Nullstellensatz over the algebraic closure makes this equivalent to having no geometric common zero. At each of the nine rational points, filling already forces \(f=0\), so two first derivatives suffice to test local smoothness. Exactly \(3{,}648\) of the nonzero filling equations pass those rational first-jet tests.

4. The complete group of order \(6\cdot6\cdot2=72\) is generated by the two independent \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbf F_2)=\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_2)\) substitutions and interchange of factors. Exact coefficient substitutions partition the \(3{,}648\) rationally nonsingular forms into 54 group orbits. Their orbit-size distribution is \(49\times72+2\times36+1\times24+2\times12\). A Gröbner-basis unit-ideal test on all four charts of one representative per orbit, using geometric-smoothness invariance under the group, retains precisely \(31\times72+2\times36+1\times24=2{,}328\) equations. The excluded 20 orbits have geometric singularities outside the rational locus. Thus the stated 34 smooth orbits are exhaustive.

5. An independent algorithm constructs the kernel from \(D_X,D_Y\) using finite-field row reduction and uses a separate characteristic-two Buchberger implementation for the chart ideals. Its set of exactly 2,328 smooth coefficient masks agrees **element by element** with the SymPy-based computation. The complete orbit representatives and sizes occur in `data/ORBIT_REPRESENTATIVES.json`.

6. A geometrically smooth effective divisor of bidegree \((4,4)\) is geometrically integral: if it had disconnected irreducible components, their positive intersection products on \(\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\) would contradict smoothness, since the total bidegree is positive in both directions. Adjunction gives genus \((4-1)(4-1)=9\).

The first script writes `biquartic_census_output.json`; the second writes `biquartic_independent_output.json`; the third checks the exact sets of smooth coefficient masks, the two orbit counts, and the 34 provided orbit representatives. The verifier reports `PASS: 2,328 identical smooth coefficient masks in 34 orbits (31x72,2x36,1x24)`.

## Relationship to prior work
Homma and Kim (2022), Lemma 2.1 and Theorem 4.1, identify the rational-point vanishing generators, exclude geometrically irreducible filling curves of bidegree \((3,3)\) over \(\mathbf F_2\), and construct examples of bidegree \((4,3)\) and \((3,4)\); they do not give this exhaustive balanced-bidegree \((4,4)\) enumeration. Ahammed, Campbell and Moon (2025) give existence criteria and finite-field enumerations centered on the minimal bidegree for odd fields, including their \(\mathbf F_3\) calculation; their Theorems 1.1–1.2 assume odd prime powers and do not imply the count or orbit structure over \(\mathbf F_2\) at bidegree \((4,4)\). The result is a complete exact enumerative extension in the first symmetric bidegree above the exceptional binary minimum.

## Limitations
The count concerns embedded equations and the specific rational automorphism group, not isomorphism classes over algebraic closures or other ground fields. The proof relies on exhaustive small-field arithmetic and independent implementable Gröbner-basis tests; it does not give a symbolic closed counting formula uniform in the field size. Earlier unindexed classifications may affect priority; no independent external audit has been performed.
