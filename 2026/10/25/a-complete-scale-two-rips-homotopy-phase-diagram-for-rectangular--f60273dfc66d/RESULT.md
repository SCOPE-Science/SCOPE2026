# A complete scale-two Rips homotopy phase diagram for rectangular tori of circumference at least five

## Finding
Let \(m,n\ge5\) be integers. Equip the vertices of the Cartesian product graph \(C_m\square C_n\) with their shortest-path distance, and let \(X_{m,n}=\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(C_m\square C_n)\) be the inclusive vertex Vietoris–Rips complex. Set
\[
s(t)=\begin{cases}1&t=5,\\2&t=6,\\0&t\ge7,\end{cases}\qquad A=ns(m),\quad B=ms(n).
\]
Then the **integral homotopy type**, with no localization of coefficients, is
\[
|X_{m,n}|\simeq\begin{cases}
S^1\times S^1,&A=B=0,\\
S^1\vee\bigvee^{A+B}S^2,&A+B>0,\ AB=0,\\
\bigvee^{A+B-1}S^2,&A>0,\ B>0.
\end{cases}
\]
The new rectangular transition cases include \(|X_{5,n}|\simeq S^1\vee\bigvee^nS^2\) and \(|X_{6,n}|\simeq S^1\vee\bigvee^{2n}S^2\) for every \(n\ge7\), and \(|X_{5,6}|\simeq\bigvee^{15}S^2\). The square values \(m=n=5,6\) agree with previously established calculations; they are included to give the complete uniform phase diagram, not claimed anew.

At scale one, \(X^{(1)}_{m,n}=\operatorname{VR}_{\le1}(C_m\square C_n)\) is a graph with \(mn\) vertices and \(2mn\) edges. Its integral first homology has rank \(mn+1\). Inclusion into \(X_{m,n}\) is surjective on first homology and kills a free subgroup of rank \(mn+1-\beta_1(X_{m,n})\), where \(\beta_1\) is respectively \(2,1,0\) in the three cases above. This describes the degree-one persistence change at threshold two, not the full higher-scale filtration.

## Assumptions and scope
The cycle \(C_t\) has vertices \(\mathbb Z/t\mathbb Z\), consecutive vertices adjacent, and cyclic distance \(d_t(a,b)=\min(|a-b|,t-|a-b|)\). The product graph metric is \(d((a,b),(c,d))=d_m(a,c)+d_n(b,d)\). Every face is a nonempty vertex set of pairwise distance at most two. All homotopy and homology assertions concern the geometric realization of the **finite vertex complex**, not the continuum torus or metric graph. The bounds \(m,n\ge5\) ensure that cyclic distances zero, one and two have unique short lifts.

For each grid vertex \(v=(i,j)\), denote its neighbors by \(E,W,N,S\) in the two cyclic coordinates. Let \(D_v=\{v,E,W,N,S\}\) be the five-point diamond. For each unit square with cyclic corners \(a=(i,j), b=(i+1,j), c=(i+1,j+1),d=(i,j+1)\), let \(Q_{i,j}=\{a,b,c,d\}\). Define \(M_{m,n}\) as the simplicial complex generated by the \(mn\) diamonds and the \(mn\) squares. The notation \(\bigvee^qS^2\) means the wedge of \(q\) ordinary two-spheres; no claim of a canonical wedge decomposition is made.

## Proof
**1. Exact facet classification.** Suppose a scale-two clique has both cyclic coordinates nonconstant. It must contain a pair of vertices differing in both coordinates: otherwise a pair that changes the first coordinate forces every further vertex to have the same second coordinate. Any pair changing both coordinates has difference one in each, up to signs, so after translation and reflection it is \(u=(0,0)\), \(v=(1,1)\). Their common scale-two neighbors, other than \(u,v\), are exactly
\[
(1,0),\ (2,0),\ (1,-1),\ (0,1),\ (-1,1),\ (0,2).
\]
Among these six vertices the compatibility graph has two triangles, on \(\{(1,0),(2,0),(1,-1)\}\) and \(\{(0,1),(-1,1),(0,2)\}\), with exactly one additional edge \(\{(1,0),(0,1)\}\). These statements follow by inserting cyclic distances zero, one and two into the product-distance formula; the calculation is unchanged for any \(m,n\ge5\). Thus every clique containing \(u,v\) is a face of one of the two adjacent diamonds or their unit square. Consequently all cliques varying in both coordinates belong to \(M_{m,n}\).

A clique contained in a single cycle fiber is instead a simplex of \(\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(C_t)\). For \(t\ge7\), every maximal such clique is a triple of three consecutive vertices, already a face of a diamond. For \(t=5\), the full fiber is a four-simplex, since all five vertices have mutual distance at most two. For \(t=6\), the fiber Rips complex is the boundary of an octahedron: only the three antipodal pairs are forbidden. Its eight triangular facets consist of six consecutive triples already in \(M_{m,n}\), plus the two alternating triangles \(\{0,2,4\}\) and \(\{1,3,5\}\). This exhausts every face of \(X_{m,n}\). Distinct fibers intersect only at a vertex when they are perpendicular and are otherwise disjoint.

**2. The baseline complex is a torus, with its coordinate circles fixed.** Here is a uniform \(9mn\)-pair elementary collapse \(M_{m,n}\searrow T_{m,n}\), where \(T_{m,n}\) is the torus triangulation consisting of the two triangles \(\{a,b,c\}\) and \(\{a,c,d\}\) in every square. Perform these five phases in the indicated order, with each phase processed at every relevant grid vertex or square:

1. Pair the neighbor tetrahedron \(\{E,W,N,S\}\) with its containing diamond \(D_v\).
2. For each three-element subset \(U\subseteq\{E,W,N,S\}\), pair \(U\) with \(U\cup\{v\}\).
3. Pair the corner triangle \(\{a,b,d\}\) with its containing square tetrahedron \(Q_{i,j}\).
4. Pair the undesired diagonal edge \(\{b,d\}\) with the triangle \(\{b,c,d\}\).
5. Pair each straight distance-two edge \(\{W,E\}\) with \(\{W,v,E\}\), and similarly \(\{S,N\}\) with \(\{S,v,N\}\).

Every lower face is free when removed: in the first two phases any opposite-neighbor pair determines its unique midpoint, and no such triple occurs in a square; next, every square has its own corner triple, and the discarded diagonal is contained in only its second remaining corner triangle; finally, each straight distance-two edge has a unique midpoint and only its collinear triangle remains. Cyclic circumferences at least five prevent an alternative midpoint or an accidental identification. The five phases delete respectively \(mn,4mn,mn,mn,2mn\) codimension-one pairs. The remaining faces are exactly the stated triangulation, preserving every original graph edge. Hence \(M_{m,n}\simeq S^1\times S^1\), and each original row or column cycle represents a primitive coordinate generator under this equivalence.

**3. Fiber intersections represent primitive circles.** For any fiber \(F\) of length \(t=5\) or \(6\), its intersection with \(M_{m,n}\) is the subcomplex \(U_F\) generated by all \(t\) consecutive triples \(\{i,i+1,i+2\}\) (cyclic indices). Each length-two diagonal edge \(\{i,i+2\}\) belongs to exactly one of these triangles. Collapsing each triangle along its free diagonal leaves the original \(C_t\), so \(U_F\simeq S^1\), with a primitive coordinate loop mapped into \(M_{m,n}\). For \(t=5\), the entire fiber Rips complex is a simplex, contractible; attaching it along \(U_F\) therefore has the homotopy-pushout effect of adding one disk along that primitive loop. For \(t=6\), the fiber Rips complex adds exactly two alternating triangles to \(U_F\). The boundary of either alternating triangle is homotopic in \(U_F\) to the original \(C_6\) loop with degree \(\pm1\): the consecutive triangles identify every directed length-two edge with the sum of its two length-one edges, and the three alternating edges together traverse the cycle once. Thus it adds two disks along that primitive loop. In either case the attaching loops are parallel coordinate circles in the baseline torus.

**4. Homotopy pushout and enumeration.** There are \(n\) fibers of circumference \(m\) and \(m\) fibers of circumference \(n\); hence the effective numbers of attached meridional and longitudinal disks are respectively \(A=ns(m)\) and \(B=ms(n)\). All overlaps between additional fiber pieces lie inside \(M_{m,n}\); attaching their disks is therefore a homotopy-pushout model for the entire complex. Replace the torus by its CW model with one vertex, meridian \(a\), longitude \(b\) and one two-cell attached by \([a,b]\). The added disks attach by \(a^{\pm1}\), repeated \(A\) times, and \(b^{\pm1}\), repeated \(B\) times. If neither is attached, the space is the torus. If only one family is attached, the first corresponding two-cell cancels its primitive one-cell; the torus two-cell and each additional disk then attach trivially, leaving \(S^1\vee\bigvee^{A+B}S^2\). If both families occur, the first disk in each family cancels both one-cells, and the original torus two-cell and the remaining \(A+B-2\) disks become \(A+B-1\) two-sphere summands. These are integral homotopy equivalences, not deductions from field ranks.

**5. Degree-one persistence.** At scale one the graph has \(mn\) vertices, \(2mn\) edges and one connected component, giving first Betti number \(mn+1\). Every length-two edge at scale two is homologous, via a triangle through an intermediate graph vertex, to two original graph edges. Therefore inclusion at scale one surjects onto \(H_1(X_{m,n};\mathbb Z)\). Both source and target are free abelian by the established homotopy types, so the kernel is free of the stated rank. In the one-short-direction regime the surviving generator runs around the long cycle.

## Verification
The standalone Python 3 checker `verify_rectangular_tori.py` uses only the standard library. It independently enumerates maximal graph-distance cliques with Bron–Kerbosch search, compares them with the proposed diamond/square/fiber facet classification, checks every short-fiber diagonal collapse and the complete mod-two simplicial boundary ranks, and replays each step of the \(9mn\)-pair baseline simplicial collapse with an exact remaining-coface test. It covers fifteen rectangular parameter pairs through circumference ten, including all three small-square and mixed-short boundary cases, and eight separate explicit baseline collapses. Successful replay prints `VERIFY_OK` and `COLLAPSE_VERIFY_OK`. Finite checks are corroboration; the parameter-uniform combinatorial proof is Steps 1–5.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams, Adetowubo, Barriga-Acosta, Feng and Sterling [1] study square torus grids. Their scale-two results include the \((5,5)\) and \((6,6)\) sphere-wedge values, and give a torus result for large square circumference. These published cases are explicitly not new components of the claim. The main new contribution here is a **single complete rectangular classification** for every \(m,n\ge5\), particularly the infinite families with one circumference five or six, the mixed \((5,6)\) case, and the precise first-persistence transition. Related work on width-four toroidal grids [2] yields three-spheres rather than the two-spheres appearing here; work on cycle-by-clique cylinders [3] uses \(K_t\) fibers and does not imply results for \(C_5\) or \(C_6\). Work on twisted toroidal grids [4] assumes both circumferences at least seven and a different quotient topology. Published statements about those families do not imply this short-cycle phase diagram.

## Limitations
No claim is made for a circumference below five, for scale greater than two, or for the full persistence filtration. The finite checks do not prove the infinite parameter assertion by themselves. The homotopy model is not an equivariant classification. Priority searches can miss an equivalent rectangular theorem under another construction or name, and independent scientific validation has not been carried out.
