The radial eigenvalues follow directly from orthogonality; integration by parts proves their exact first-difference sign, and at each logarithmic-mean integer tie exactly two adjacent modes attain the strictly isolated maximum. A trace-class coherent-state integral establishes twice differentiable dependence on the two boundary perturbations. The first variation on the two-mode eigenspace is a Hermitian matrix with exact radial coefficient \(\alpha\) and center-shift coefficient \(\gamma\). The positive spectral gap makes the Schur-complement remainder quadratic, yielding the full conical norm formula. The area constraint is exact, and Weyl covariance proves common-center invariance. ## Originality — PASS (provisional)
Radial Fock spectral formulas are classical and already imply the concentric integer crossings, so those ingredients are expressly excluded from the novel claim. Relevant prior work gives Gaussian concentration inequalities, quasi-radial eigenvalue formulas, and disk stability; the inspected texts do not show the exact first-order eccentricity cusp at a double leading Hermite eigenvalue or its three-real-parameter cone. Semantic database searches and accessible earlier mathematical claims were compared for equivalent formulations, stronger implications and exact values. Residual priority risk comes from general spectral-perturbation work or an unindexed specialized eccentric-annulus result.

## Value — PASS
The identified family is canonical in Gaussian time–frequency localization and preserves phase-space area exactly. Rather than an arbitrary finite benchmark, the formula fully describes each symmetry-induced collision and the failure of differentiability of optimal captured energy, sharply distinguishes independent-center motion from rigid translation, and provides a computable instability coefficient for every Hermite level and annulus width. The original object and invariant are natural; the exact cone is not provided by knowing radial eigenvalues alone.
