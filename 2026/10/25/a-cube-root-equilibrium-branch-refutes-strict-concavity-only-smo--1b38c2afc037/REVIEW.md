## Correctness

**PASS.** The two best-response equations reduce to \(x=y+\varepsilon\) and \(y=x-x^3\). For \(0\le\varepsilon\le1/8\), the best responses remain interior at equilibrium, and the unique solution is \((\varepsilon^{1/3},\varepsilon^{1/3}-\varepsilon)\). The response quotient diverges as \(\varepsilon^{-2/3}\); the joint first-order-condition Jacobian at zero has determinant zero. The included exact-rational script is supplementary, not the proof of the universal statement.

The cube-root branch is an exact, reusable diagnostic for the missing joint nondegeneracy assumption. Its value is specifically that uniqueness persists, so the failure cannot be attributed to choosing among multiple equilibria.
