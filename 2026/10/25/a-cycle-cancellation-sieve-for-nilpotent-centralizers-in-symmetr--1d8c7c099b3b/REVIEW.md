# Scientific review: rank-refined nilpotent centralizers in symmetric inverse monoids

## Correctness — PASS

The claim concerns all nilpotent partial injections, classified by a finite list of positive vertex-lengths of their chain components. Commutation forces each participating source chain's domain to be a prefix and its image to be an equal-length suffix of a unique target chain. A decorated partial permutation of chain indices therefore parametrizes every centralizing partial injection exactly once, with decorated edge-length equal to its contribution to rank. Cycles of the decorated index permutation create actual periodic points precisely when all constituent chains have equal lengths and all decorations have that full length. Excluding these bad cycles and applying inclusion-exclusion gives a signed sum over permutations of their selected vertices. The identity \(\sum_{\pi\in S_s}(-1)^{\#\mathrm{cycles}(\pi)}=0\) for \(s\ge2\) leaves only one selected chain of each distinct length, proving the sieve. The equal-chain exponential generating function follows from the labeled path/cycle decomposition. This is a proof for arbitrary sizes, not an extrapolation from finite tests. Both included programs passed exact coefficientwise checks in their documented finite ranges.

## Originality — PASS, with acknowledged priority uncertainty

Konieczny's 2012 general centralizer characterization contains the underlying chain-prefix/terminal-suffix rule, so the structural characterization and elementary weighted-rook formula are not claimed as new. Gomes–Howie (1987) give the chain decomposition of nilpotent partial injections. Laradji–Umar (2004) enumerate unrestricted nilpotent partial injections, covering the zero-map boundary. The assessed new claim is the cancellation that turns a potentially large inclusion-exclusion over all decorated periodic components into a signed deletion sum with at most one chain per distinct length, and its rank-shift specialization. The inspected primary text of Konieczny (2012) does not state this rank-refined nilpotent centralizer enumeration; the inspected portion of Gomes–Howie and Laradji–Umar's accessible abstract concern unconditioned nilpotence rather than centralizer fibers. The 2020 paper on finite centralizer structure could hide an equivalent formulation; its full text was not obtainable through available authorized access and this remains an explicit priority risk. Published-record and semantic-search queries did not identify an exact predecessor, but negative searches are not proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS

For a fixed nilpotent element, the nilpotent members of its centralizer are a natural intersection of two fundamental semigroup-theoretic classes, graded by transformation rank. The general cancellation identity provides exact computable coefficients for all chain types, not an arbitrary finite slice. The unexpectedly short rank-shift law in the uniform family identifies the entire nonnilpotent locus with a weighted centralizer polynomial on one fewer chain. This sharp structural enumeration refines previously available characterizations and unconditioned counts, and supplies benchmarks for studying commuting nilpotent subsemigroups.

## Closest literature and limitations

Konieczny, *Centralisers in the Infinite Symmetric Inverse Semigroup* (DOI 10.1017/S0004972712000779), Theorem 3.4, is the strongest structural predecessor; its full paper was read, particularly Sections 2–4 and the chain criterion. Gomes–Howie, *Nilpotents in Finite Symmetric Inverse Semigroups* (DOI 10.1017/S0013091500026778), Section 2, supplies the chain description. Laradji–Umar, *On the Number of Nilpotents in the Partial Symmetric Semigroup* (DOI 10.1081/AGB-120038637), gives known unconditioned counts. Konieczny (2020), DOI 10.1080/00927872.2020.1782926, has an accessible abstract about centralizer Green-relation structure, but the full text was not read. This claim does not cover fixed maps containing cycles, centralizer multiplication tables, or conjugacy classes. Earlier equivalent counts may exist; independent verification of historical priority and independent mathematical audit are still needed.
