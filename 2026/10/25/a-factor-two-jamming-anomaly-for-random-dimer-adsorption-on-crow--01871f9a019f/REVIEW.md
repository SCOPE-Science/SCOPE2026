# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For the crown graph with one forbidden perfect matching, the common-unmatched-index count is an exact Markov state. The three transition multiplicities sum to the number of available edges. The first normalized factorial moment gives an exact negative drift, and the second falling-factorial moment gives a supermartingale bound. Summing the drift proves the two-sided harmonic inequalities and the sharp coefficient two. The finite-state recurrence and independent vertex-set enumeration agree for all tested small sizes. No finite experiment is substituted for an infinite-size proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources
The 1989 graph-adsorption framework, the 1994 one-dimensional adsorption study, and the 2015 square-lattice defect study do not exhibit the crown-graph defect probability or its factor-two coefficient. The 2022 deranged-matching work counts static perfect matchings, rather than random greedy matching outcomes. Available research-record queries and recoverable same-topic historical claims were checked; none found was an equivalent asymptotic formula. The original 1989 article was available only through an abstract and metadata, so a broader implicit specialization remains a historical-priority risk; independent review may find stronger prior coverage.

## Value — PASS
A fixed deleted matching is a vanishing fraction of potential adsorption locations in a dense bipartite substrate, yet it yields a terminal jamming defect with a sharp probability twice the independently uniform-pair estimate. The proof provides a structural Markov reduction, an exact rational finite-size algorithm, and explicit all-size error bounds. This is a natural quantitative jamming invariant, not an arbitrary graph-subset census.

## Closest literature and limits
Pippenger, *Random Sequential Adsorption on Graphs* (1989), covers the graph adsorption framework but the stated high-girth/local-cycle formula does not cover the many-four-cycle crown graph. Bonnier–Leroyer–Pommiers (arXiv:cond-mat/9412019), inspected in full-text sections, concern a one-dimensional dashed substrate. Lebovka et al. (arXiv:1507.04590), inspected through the model, simulation, result, and discussion sections, concern square lattice rods with defects. Johnston–Kayll–Palmer (arXiv:2209.11319) concern static deranged perfect-matching counts. The result does not address adsorption kinetics, inhomogeneous rates, or arbitrary forbidden edge sets. Its mathematical proof has not undergone an independent audit.
