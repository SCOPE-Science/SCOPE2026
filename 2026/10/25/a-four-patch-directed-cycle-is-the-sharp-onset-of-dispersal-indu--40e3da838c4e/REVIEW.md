# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** For the exact lossy directed ring, the unique positive equilibrium follows from the maximum/minimum argument. Linearization cancels the non-delay local equilibrium derivative against migration mortality and reduces exactly to scalar Fourier equations. Imaginary-root modulus and phase identities enumerate the possible earliest crossings; concavity and convexity establish the strict three-versus-four distinction, including the critical equality and all postcritical delays. The crossing-derivative formula is reconstructed symbolically and the supplementary program passes its independent numerical checks. Mathematical limitations: this is a local **linear** stability theorem; numerical checks do not establish the continuum claim.

**Originality — PASS (qualified bibliographic judgment).** The source full text arXiv:2302.01814 contains small- and near-extinction dispersal results, topology-dependent perturbative thresholds and an identified difficulty at intermediate dispersal; it does not establish a universal four-cycle transition. Related earlier patch papers treat different asymptotic regimes. The later open-chain advective model and ring-coupled neural work do not imply this exact logistic loss-mode comparison. Semantic mathematical-record searches including aliases for rotating-wave, transverse, and synchronous Hopf thresholds identified no exact claim. Residual risk: a general modal criterion for circulant delay systems could subsume the specific first-mode ordering under different vocabulary. The novelty claim is not proof of uniqueness in the literature.

**Value — PASS.** The onset of spatially heterogeneous versus synchronous population instability is a natural unresolved quantitative distinction in lossy patch models. This supplies an explicit all-rate instability diagram and an exact minimal directed-cycle size within a specified natural family. Its threshold can guide selection of spatial monitoring patterns without claiming nonlinear cycle emergence.
