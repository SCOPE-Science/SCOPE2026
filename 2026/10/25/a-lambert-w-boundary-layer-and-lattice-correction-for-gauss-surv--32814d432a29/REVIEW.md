# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The Gauss survivor \(E(1/N)\) equals the minimum-digit continued-fraction shift except for dimension-zero endpoints. Its conformal inverse branches \(1/(a+x)\), \(a\ge N\), are uniformly contracting, have disjoint interiors, and possess global derivative bounds \((a+1/N)^{-2}\le|\phi_a'|\le a^{-2}\). The pressure principle gives the two Hurwitz-zeta root enclosures. The mean-value argument bounds their separation by \(2/(N^2\log N)\). Euler--Maclaurin with a uniform differentiated remainder and Taylor expansion at the Lambert-W saddle determine the finite-\(N\) correction. Monotonicity supplies the continuous-parameter consequence. The six-point numerical sample and executable source check only finite consequences; the universal theorem is analytical.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Liu--Liao identify a discontinuous critical-dimension jump at zero hole size. The new quantitative result determines how that dimension approaches the critical value, which cannot be inferred from the limit and devil's staircase statements. The finite-parameter Hurwitz enclosure is computable, and its nonlinear pressure uncertainty is one full inverse power of \(N\) below the resolved Euler--Maclaurin correction. Thus the first nontrivial finite-digit effect is mathematically identifiable without numerically approximating a countable transfer operator. Both the all-hole leading asymptotic and the finer reciprocal-hole correction are attached to a natural dynamical parameter, not an arbitrary slice.
