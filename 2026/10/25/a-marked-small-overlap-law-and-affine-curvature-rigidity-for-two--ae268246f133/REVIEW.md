## Value — PASS

The cross covariogram measures how two different convex bodies overlap under translation. The theorem converts rare, nearly tangent overlaps into a precise curvature-weighted normal distribution, identifies a universal conditional beta law, and determines which aspect of the two individual curvatures can be recovered from boundary measurements. The affine-arclength equality test rigidly characterizes reflected homothety; this is a structural geometric distinction that cannot be read off merely from the known leading tangency exponent. The result is specific and limited, not a general resolution of cross-covariogram uniqueness.
