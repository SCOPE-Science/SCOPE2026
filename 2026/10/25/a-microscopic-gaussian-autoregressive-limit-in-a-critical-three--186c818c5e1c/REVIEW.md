# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The mutant-family pgf gives a critical finite-moment offspring law with variance \(1/r\). Under the population-size Doob transform, the exact conditional contrast drift is \(-D(1+1/(rS))/2\); the leading one-step contrast variance is \(S(1+3r)/(6r)\). Contracting both non-Perron type contrasts bounds their second moments by \(O(n)\). A finite-block conditional central limit argument, geometric decay of remote allele-imbalance memory, and studentization establish the joint Gaussian autoregressive limit. The Gamma scale follows from the critical Q population pgf asymptotics. The finite checker corroborates algebraic identities but does not replace the proof.

## Originality
PASS, subject to independent priority checking. The closest earlier critical multitype work already covers one-time Gaussian deviations and the macroscopic functional population limit. It does not, in the inspected material, state the fixed-lag joint Gaussian autoregression for the Q-process with the explicit oscillatory correlation. The motivating 2026 paper supplies the underlying Q-process framework; the finite-window fluctuation theorem is a distinct assertion. Remaining risk: an older multitime limit theorem under alternative terminology could imply this particular family.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The universal negative-one-half lag correlation distinguishes rapidly relaxing allele-composition noise from the nondegenerate critical population-size fluctuations. The limiting independence after self-normalization provides a natural local-timescale description complementary to known macroscopic Bessel limits. The result is restricted to one explicit, mathematically natural neutral-mutation family; no broader universality is claimed.

## Sources and limitations
Primary comparisons: Blancas–Fittipaldi–Hernández-Torres (arXiv:2610.20896v1), Baake–Cordero–Mellis–Wachtel (arXiv:2504.21714v1), Fleischmann–Vatutin (WIAS 455), and Athreya–Ney (1974). No result is asserted about growing lag lengths or finite-horizon survival conditioning.
