# A mixed-dimensional sphere wedge for binary de Bruijn directed forests

## Finding
Let \(D\) be the binary order-three de Bruijn digraph, and let \(\operatorname{DT}(D)\) be its simplicial complex of directed forests, with every selected vertex receiving at most one selected incoming arc. Then
\[
\operatorname{DT}(D)\simeq S^{5}\vee S^{6}\vee S^{6}.
\]
The simultaneous bit-complement symmetry acts by the sign representation on \(\widetilde H_5(\operatorname{DT}(D);\mathbb Q)\), and by the direct sum of the trivial and sign representations on \(\widetilde H_6(\operatorname{DT}(D);\mathbb Q)\). All other reduced integral homology groups vanish.

## Assumptions and scope
The eight vertices are binary words \((a,b,c)\) of length three. For each word and each \(d\in\{0,1\}\), the directed graph contains the arc \((a,b,c)\to(b,c,d)\). The two loops at \(000\) and \(111\) cannot belong to an acyclic edge set and are omitted from the simplicial vertex set. Index the other fourteen arcs by the lexicographic order of their source words, then by their appended bits, starting at zero; `certificate.json` records the resulting endpoints.

A nonempty subset of these fourteen arcs is a face if each vertex has at most one selected incoming arc and the resulting digraph has no directed cycle. Include the empty face as usual. All statements concern this specific eight-vertex directed graph and its natural two-element automorphism group generated by simultaneous bit complement; no infinite family classification is asserted. The relevant earliest verified supporting public preprint date is 2002-10-03 (Kozlov, arXiv:math/0210045v1).

## Proof
**Exact finite face model.** Exhaustively consider all \(2^{14}\) arc subsets, rejecting every subset with repeated incoming target or a directed cycle detected by parent-pointer iteration. Independently, choose either no incoming arc or one available incoming arc at each of eight vertices, and reject cycles. Both complete enumerations produce the same 2,186 nonempty faces. Their face vector, in dimensions zero through six, is
\[
(14,84,280,560,672,448,128).
\]
There are 128 maximal faces of dimension six and four maximal faces of dimension five, hence the complex is nonpure.

**Discrete-Morse reduction.** Process arc indices from zero to thirteen. At each index, pair each still-unpaired nonempty face not containing that arc with the face obtained by adjoining it, provided both are present and still unpaired. Process the candidate lower faces in increasing integer-mask order. The resulting explicit list consists of 1,091 matched codimension-one pairs; precisely four faces are critical: mask `0x1` in dimension zero, mask `0x348a` in dimension five, and masks `0x2cd2` and `0x32cc` in dimension six.

For all 10,192 incidences of a nonempty face with a codimension-one nonempty face, orient the matched incidences downward and the others upward. A complete Kahn topological sort visits all 2,186 faces; thus the reversed-matching Hasse digraph has no directed cycle. The matching is acyclic and the finite discrete-Morse theorem yields a CW complex with precisely the four stated critical cells.

**Exact attaching degrees.** Use ascending arc indices for simplex orientations and compute signed boundaries over \(\mathbb Z\). Eliminate the paired five–six cells using their unit incidence coefficients, processing the smallest currently occurring paired lower face first. Each critical six-cell requires seven exact signed cancellations, and both have zero residual boundary. Hence the cellular boundary from the two critical six-cells to the unique critical five-cell is the zero \(1\times2\) matrix. The Morse CW model has one zero-cell, one five-cell and two six-cells, and no other cells. Its five-skeleton is \(S^5\); every six-cell attaches along a map \(S^5\to S^5\) whose degree is zero. Since degree classifies \(\pi_5(S^5)\cong\mathbb Z\), these attaching maps are nullhomotopic. This proves the displayed integral homotopy equivalence, not merely its homology.

**Complement representation.** Send each word \((a,b,c)\) to \((1-a,1-b,1-c)\) and apply the resulting permutation of oriented arcs to simplicial chains with the induced orientation signs. Independent exact rational elimination on the ordinary six-dimensional boundary matrix gives two eight-term integral cycle generators in top degree. In that basis the complement transformation exchanges the generators, with matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Therefore its rational degree-six representation has trace zero and splits as trivial plus sign. The signed fixed-simplex chain traces in dimensions zero through six are \((0,-6,0,12,0,-8,0)\). Subtracting the empty-face contribution yields reduced Lefschetz number \(1\). Since reduced homology occurs only in degrees five and six, \(-\operatorname{tr}(\tau\mid\widetilde H_5)+\operatorname{tr}(\tau\mid\widetilde H_6)=1\). Thus the one-dimensional fifth homology has trace \(-1\), giving the sign action.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` in the directory containing `certificate.json`. This standard-library-only verifier regenerates all valid faces by two enumeration methods; checks the explicitly recorded faces, matched pairs, facets, and critical masks against independently regenerated sets; checks that the entire reversed-matching Hasse digraph is acyclic; calculates exact signed Morse boundaries; and computes the ordinary mod-two simplicial boundary ranks \((0,13,71,209,351,321,126)\) for dimensions zero through six. It also constructs exact rational top cycles and the signed symmetry action, then checks the reduced Lefschetz number. A successful run prints `VERIFY_OK`, with representative output preserved in `verifier-output.txt`. Integer calculations are exhaustive finite certificates rather than statistical experiments.

## Relationship to prior work
Kozlov introduced and studied complexes of directed trees and analyzed their topology for selected directed graph classes; his 2002 preprint on double-directed strings and trees does not specialize to the present binary de Bruijn graph. Engström established shellability results for directed *acyclic* graphs, whereas this de Bruijn digraph contains directed cycles. Jojić studied complete-source and other special directed-tree complexes, including complete double-directed structures and underlying-tree cases; none of those checked hypotheses provides the stated homotopy and complement-action classification of this shift-register digraph. The general definitions and techniques motivate the question but do not provide these exact invariants. Indexed comparisons against directed-forest, line-digraph, and graph-Morse formulations did not identify a covering implication; absence of a search match is not a uniqueness proof.

## Limitations
This is an exact classification for only the order-three binary de Bruijn digraph and its bit-complement action. It does not classify directed forests on larger de Bruijn graphs, and no invariant statements about an arbitrary graph are inferred. Some historically relevant sources were available only as abstracts or limited previews, including the full 1999 Kozlov article and a 2020 thesis on Cayley directed-forest complexes; an unindexed equivalent calculation cannot be excluded. The proof and reproducibility materials are computationally finite and use standard discrete-Morse and homotopy theory; independent scientific validation has not been performed.
