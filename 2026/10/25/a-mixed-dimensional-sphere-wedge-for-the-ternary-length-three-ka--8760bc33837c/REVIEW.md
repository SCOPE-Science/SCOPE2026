# Scientific review: ternary length-three Kautz directed forests

## Correctness
PASS. The full graph/face definitions are explicit. A count of all \(531,441\) permitted incoming-edge assignments leaves \(314,928\) faces including the empty face, checked independently by the undirected-cycle criterion for indegree-at-most-one digraphs. The directed matrix-tree theorem independently gives \(12\cdot384=4,608\) top facets. The complete Morse matching and the orientation of all \(2,361,936\) nonempty face incidences are regenerated by `verify.py`, which checks acyclicity rather than inferring it from face counts. Exact signed gradient paths yield the displayed three-by-twenty-two and twenty-two-by-twenty-three boundaries, and three plus eleven integral unit cancellations. The nullhomotopy of all remaining ten-cell attachments follows from the ninth Hurewicz isomorphism for a wedge of nine-spheres. The assertion has been proved for this exact finite digraph; there is no extrapolation to a family.

## Originality
PASS on accessible evidence, with nonzero residual risk. The principal results inspected in Kozlov's double-directed-tree paper, Engström's acyclic-digraph paper and Jojić's complete-source/essentially-tree paper do not entail this cyclic Kautz case. The six-vertex predecessor digraph has a different directed-forest complex, and its calculated homotopy does not determine the line digraph's complex. Exact-object, line-digraph, rooted-forest, equivalent-formulation and broader-theorem searches in the accessible research literature identified no covering statement. The foundational 1999 paper and a thematically relevant 2020 thesis could not be inspected in full; neither is represented as having been ruled out.

## Value
PASS. The length-three degree-two Kautz graph is a canonical shift-network example and the natural directed-line-digraph successor of its six-vertex predecessor. The result is not a parameter renaming: it determines the complete homotopy type of a 314,927-face, nonpure complex with two independent positive-dimensional homology degrees. Its 132 maximal, nonspanning two-root forests show exactly where pure arborescence counts cease to describe the directed-forest topology. It provides a natural finite benchmark for rooted-forest complexes of regular directed networks, without claiming an infinite-family theorem.
