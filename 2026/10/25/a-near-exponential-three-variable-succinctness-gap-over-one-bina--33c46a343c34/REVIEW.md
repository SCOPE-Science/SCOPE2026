# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** The encoding has a one-relation finite domain \(A\times T\), with roots and bit markers recognized by explicit bounded-size first-order tests. Exactly one loop-free path profile for each original binary symbol survives the length and marker conditions. Three-variable rebinding expresses the entire fixed-length path with only \(O(\log k)\) syntax. More importantly, complement and converse are accounted for in a block-by-block translation of *arbitrary* CoR circuits, and composition costs at most \(M_k^3\) gates per old composition. The root-root block computes the published hard \(\Phi_k\), giving the claimed bound via Theorem 1.3 of [1]. Exact finite checks of encoded paths and block operations completed successfully; the proof, not a finite check, supplies the universal quantification.

**Originality (PASS, qualified).** The triggering paper [1] explicitly isolates the fixed-vocabulary exponential problem in Remark 4.1. Its theorem permits growing vocabularies and does not state this near-exponential one-symbol reduction. A set of semantic published-finding corpus searches involving fixed signatures, CoR/FO3, and encoding of multiple relations with one relation did not identify an equivalent result. Existing graphical signature interpretations and block-matrix relations are neighboring methods, but the precise implication must preserve three-variable syntax length and the composition gate lower bound simultaneously. Absence of a match is not proof of priority; the residual possibility of an earlier equivalent compression is recorded.

**Value (PASS).** The proven quantitative gap is within one logarithmic factor in the exponent of the open fixed-signature exponential-gap target and persists even for circuit sharing and free Boolean/converse operations. It is a structural partial advance on the exact signature restriction highlighted in [1], not a routine numerical instance. Crucially, it cannot be promoted to a fully exponential fixed-signature lower bound, positivity, or a single quantifier.

Closest literature: Yuya Uezato (2026), Theorem 1.3 and Remark 4.1, arXiv:2609.26778v1. Related surveys and translation results discussed in that paper provide the foundational FO3/CoR expressiveness equivalence but not the presented one-symbol near-exponential tradeoff. External historical priority and any undiscovered equivalent one-relational coding remain open limitations.
