# A nonorientable manifold core and index-two orientation cycle in an exceptional circulant independence complex

## Finding
Let \(G=C_{16}(1,4,8)\), with vertices \(\mathbb Z/16\mathbb Z\) and edges of cyclic differences \(\pm1,\pm4,8\), and let \(K=\operatorname{Ind}(G)\). Remove the orbit \(\mathcal O=\{\{i,i+2,i+7,i+9\}:i\in\mathbb Z/16\mathbb Z\}\) of sixteen tetrahedra. Let \(M\subset K\) be the subcomplex generated by the other sixty-four tetrahedra. Then \(M\) is a connected closed **nonorientable combinatorial three-manifold**, with
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3)(M)=(16,80,128,64),\qquad
H_j(M;\mathbb Z)=\begin{cases}\mathbb Z&j=0,1,\\\mathbb Z/2&j=2,\\0&j=3,\end{cases}
\]
and all other integral homology zero. The full complex has \((f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3)(K)=(16,80,144,80)\) and \(K\simeq S^3\) (its shellability is previously published). The relative cellular boundary \(\partial_3:C_3(K,M)\to C_2(K,M)\) has Smith factors \(1\) fifteen times and one zero, so \(K/M\simeq S^2\vee S^3\). The homology exact sequence contains
\[
0\longrightarrow H_3(K;\mathbb Z)\overset{\times2}{\longrightarrow}H_3(K,M;\mathbb Z)\overset{\bmod 2}{\longrightarrow}H_2(M;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow0
\]
and \(H_2(K,M;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(M;\mathbb Z)\) is an isomorphism. Up to global sign, the unique primitive integral three-cycle of \(K\) has coefficients of absolute value **one** on the sixty-four tetrahedra of \(M\), and **two** on the sixteen tetrahedra of \(\mathcal O\). The coefficient-doubling is the chain-level expression of the index-two exact sequence.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph, independent-set complex, and subcomplex are defined above, with all indices reduced modulo sixteen and each tetrahedron given its increasing vertex ordering when forming simplicial chains. This is a complete **finite** result for one naturally distinguished circulant graph. It does not claim that the core is homeomorphic to a specified sphere bundle, or that similar decompositions hold for other circulant graphs.

## Proof
**1. Exhaustive face classification.** A subset is a simplex precisely when it contains no pair whose difference is in \(\{1,4,8,12,15\}\) modulo sixteen. Enumerating all subsets through size five gives exactly \(16,80,144,80\) faces of sizes one through four and no independent five-set. Each member of \(\mathcal O\) is a tetrahedron and the sixteen members are distinct. The other sixty-four tetrahedra generate \(M\); its face counts are as stated. The supplied verifier independently enumerates every relevant subset from the given adjacency rule, rather than importing a facet list.

**2. Closed-manifold check.** Each of the 128 triangles in \(M\) lies in exactly two of its 64 tetrahedra. Every vertex link has 10 vertices, 24 edges, and 16 triangles, is connected, and has each edge in two triangles. Thus every link is a connected closed triangulated surface of Euler characteristic \(10-24+16=2\), hence a two-sphere. All sixteen vertices occur, and the tetrahedral dual graph is connected (as verified by the signed-orientation propagation visiting every tetrahedron). Therefore \(M\) is a connected closed combinatorial three-manifold.

**3. Nonorientability certificate.** For increasing-ordered tetrahedra \(t\) and \(t'\) meeting in a triangle, let \(\epsilon(t,t')\in\{+1,-1\}\) be the required ratio of coefficients for cancellation of their induced oriented boundary triangles. Along the following nine-tetrahedron cyclic walk in the dual graph, the successive required ratios are \((+,-,+,+,-,-,-,-,+)\), with product \(-1\):
\[
\begin{gathered}
(4,7,9,14),\ (2,4,7,9),\ (2,7,9,12),\ (2,5,7,12),\\
(0,2,5,7),\ (0,2,5,11),\ (0,5,11,14),\ (0,9,11,14),\ (4,9,11,14).
\end{gathered}
\]
The last tetrahedron meets the first along a triangular face. The negative product makes a consistent integral orientation impossible. Since \(M\) is connected and closed, it is nonorientable.

**4. Integral boundary calculations.** Use signed simplicial boundaries \(\partial[t_0,\ldots,t_k]=\sum_{i=0}^k(-1)^i[t_0,\ldots,\widehat{t_i},\ldots,t_k]\). Exact integer Smith calculations, with zeros omitted, give for \(M\): \(\partial_2\) rank 64 and invariant factors \(1^{64}\); \(\partial_3\) rank 64 and factors \(1^{63},2\). The connected one-skeleton has \(\partial_1\) rank 15. Thus \(H_1(M)=\mathbb Z^{80-15-64}=\mathbb Z\), \(H_2(M)=\mathbb Z/2\), and \(H_3(M)=0\). The factor two is genuine integral torsion, not a finite-field rank inference. For \(K\), the corresponding \(\partial_2\) factors are \(1^{65}\), and \(\partial_3\) factors \(1^{79}\). With the same connected one-skeleton, \(H_3(K)=\mathbb Z\) and all other reduced integral homology vanishes. The established shellability of this particular independence complex upgrades this homology description to \(K\simeq S^3\) (a pure shellable three-complex is a wedge of three-spheres).

**5. The cyclic belt and quotient.** Exactly sixteen triangles of \(K\) are missing from \(M\), and the sixteen extra tetrahedra are precisely \(\mathcal O\). Every such relative triangle meets two extra tetrahedra, every extra tetrahedron meets two such relative triangles, and their incidence graph is connected. Consequently it is a single length-sixteen cycle, with signed incidence matrix having fifteen unit Smith factors and one zero. The quotient \(K/M\) has one zero-cell and just sixteen two-cells and sixteen three-cells; because its two-skeleton is a wedge of two-spheres and \(\pi_2\) is its degree lattice, elementary integral changes of basis and cellular cancellations yield \(K/M\simeq S^2\vee S^3\). In particular \(H_2(K,M)=H_3(K,M)=\mathbb Z\).

**6. Primitive chain and exact sequence.** The kernel of the explicit \(144\times80\) integral three-boundary matrix is rank one. Its primitive generator has absolute coefficients 1 on every core tetrahedron and 2 on every belt tetrahedron, as independently checked by rational nullspace clearing and gcd normalization. Modulo two this becomes the (unique nonzero) mod-two fundamental chain of the nonorientable closed core. Projecting the primitive generator to relative chains gives exactly twice the primitive generator of \(\ker\partial_3(K,M)\). The long exact sequence of \((K,M)\), together with the integral groups already determined, proves the displayed \(\times2\) and reduction-mod-two maps and the degree-two relative-to-absolute connecting isomorphism.

## Relationship to prior work
Earl, Vander Meulen and Van Tuyl (2015), *Independence complexes of well-covered circulant graphs*, identify \(C_{16}(1,4,8)\) as an exceptional shellable but non-vertex-decomposable example and analyze its independence-complex combinatorial structure. The previously known shellability is **not** claimed as new. What is isolated here is a canonical deleted tetrahedral orbit, a **closed nonorientable three-manifold core with integral two-torsion**, the cyclic relative attaching structure, and the exact doubled primitive top cycle. Other work on torsion in circulant independence complexes concerns different graphs and does not imply this subcomplex statement by its stated conclusions.

## Limitations
The homeomorphism type and fundamental group of \(M\) are not determined here; homology alone does not identify a three-manifold. This is one finite example and not a family theorem. The absence of a matching indexed result is not an exhaustive originality guarantee, and an equivalent description in an unindexed triangulation catalogue is possible. All finite computations must be replayed from the supplied source; no outside audit has been performed.
