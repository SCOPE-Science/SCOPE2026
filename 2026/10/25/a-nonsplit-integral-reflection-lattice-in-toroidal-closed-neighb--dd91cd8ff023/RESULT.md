# A nonsplit integral reflection lattice in toroidal closed-neighborhood homology

## Finding
For all integers \(m,n\ge5\), let \(G=C_m\square C_n\) have vertices \((i,j)\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) and horizontal and vertical unit edges. Define its closed-neighborhood simplicial complex \(K=\mathcal N[G]\): a finite vertex set is a simplex if it lies in \(\{v\}\cup N_G(v)\) for some \(v\). Let \(\mathcal F\) be the \(mn\) elementary four-cycles and \(P=\mathbb Z[\mathcal F]\) their ordinary integral permutation lattice under \(D_m\times D_n\). Let \(\varepsilon\) be the orientation character, trivial on rotations and negative on either coordinate reflection.

**Theorem (complete natural integral homology and its extension).** The only nonzero reduced integral homology groups of \(K\) are \(\widetilde H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2\) and \(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{mn+1}\). The former is the sum of the two natural circle-orientation characters. The latter fits into a canonical exact sequence of integral \(D_m\times D_n\)-lattices
\[
0\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow \widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow0.
\]
**This sequence does not split integrally under the full group; its extension class has exact order two.** It does split over \(\mathbb Q\). More explicitly, choose an oriented torus face \(f=(a,b,c,d)\) in counterclockwise order and let \(D_f=[a,b,c]+[a,c,d]\), \(E_f=[a,b,d]+[b,c,d]\), and \(S_f=D_f-E_f\). Then \(S_f\) is the boundary of the missing tetrahedron (up to sign), and the \(mn+1\) integral cycles
\[
\{[S_f]:f\in\mathcal F\}\ \cup\ \{[F]\},\qquad F=\sum_{f\in\mathcal F}D_f,
\]
form a basis of \(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\). Translations permute the \(S_f\) and fix \(F\). Either coordinate reflection \(h\) acts by
\[
h[S_f]=[S_{hf}],\qquad h[F]=-[F]+\sum_{f\in\mathcal F}[S_f].
\]
The class \(2[F]-\sum_f[S_f]\) is an integral orientation-eigenvector but projects to twice, never once, the torus fundamental class.

## Assumptions and scope
The cycles are simple undirected graphs with at least five vertices. Coordinates are read modulo \(m,n\). All simplicial chains use **oriented** simplices over \(\mathbb Z\), where brackets denote the standard alternating orientation relation. The natural group considered is the specified subgroup \(D_m\times D_n\) acting independently on the two cycle coordinates; when \(m=n\), the additional coordinate-interchange automorphism is not included in this claim. The exact sequence uses the collapse-induced map to the square-filled torus, not an arbitrary choice of nonequivariant wedge equivalence. The character \(\varepsilon\) takes value \(-1\) on a reflection of exactly one coordinate and \(+1\) on two reflections.

## Proof
**1. Canonical two-dimensional collapse.** The graph has no triangles and any two vertices have at most two common neighbors. For each vertex \(v\), its closed neighborhood consists of \(v\) and four ordered neighbors \(N,E,S,W\). The only neighbor pairs having another common neighbor are the four perpendicular pairs \(\{N,E\},\{E,S\},\{S,W\},\{W,N\}\), one in each adjacent unit square. Every higher neighbor subset and each of the two opposite pairs is contained in no other closed neighborhood. In descending cardinality (four, three, two), pair each such subset \(A\) with \(A\cup\{v\}\). The lower face is free: all larger proper cofaces have already been removed, and the unique remaining codimension-one coface is the displayed upper face. The pairs centered at different vertices do not interfere. There are exactly \(1+4+2=7\) elementary collapse pairs per vertex. What remains is the original graph together with all four triangular faces of the tetrahedron boundary on the vertices of every elementary square, and no other faces of dimension two or above. Denote this natural residual by \(Y\). In particular, \(K\searrow Y\), so all homology assertions may be checked in \(Y\).

**2. Integral chain presentation.** On each oriented square \(f=(a,b,c,d)\), the two triangles along diagonal \(ac\) form an oriented disk \(D_f=[a,b,c]+[a,c,d]\). The other two triangles along diagonal \(bd\) form the disk \(E_f=[a,b,d]+[b,c,d]\). Both have boundary \([a,b]+[b,c]+[c,d]+[d,a]\), the positive oriented graph square. Distinct squares meet only along original graph edges and vertices, since an opposite pair determines its unique square. Thus \(Y\) admits a CW structure obtained from \(G\) by attaching **two** oriented two-cells \(D_f,E_f\) along the same oriented perimeter of every square. There are no cells of dimension at least three in this CW structure. Let \(B:\mathbb Z^{\mathcal F}\to C_1(G;\mathbb Z)\) be the usual cellular boundary of the square-filled torus. Its kernel is precisely \(\mathbb Z\mathbf1\), generated by the coherent sum of all oriented unit squares: the torus has one primitive integral top class and no three-cells. Hence a two-chain \(\sum_f(a_fD_f+b_fE_f)\) is a cycle exactly when \(a_f+b_f=t\) is the same integer for every \(f\). It can then be written uniquely as \(tF+\sum_f(a_f-t)S_f\) (or equivalently with the corresponding change of coefficients). This proves the asserted integral basis, freeness, and zero higher homology. The graph-square complex is a torus, so quotienting the graph cycle space by the square boundaries leaves \(H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2\), with the obvious coordinate-sign action. The map \(\pi:H_2(Y)\to H_2(T^2)\) sends each \(D_f\) and \(E_f\) to the corresponding oriented torus square. It kills the \(S_f\) and sends \(F\) to the primitive torus fundamental class, yielding the exact sequence.

**3. Symmetry action.** A coordinate translation preserves all counterclockwise face orientations and each chosen diagonal, so it permutes \(D_f,E_f,S_f\) without signs and fixes \(F\). The horizontal reflection \((i,j)\mapsto(-i,j)\) reverses each face orientation and exchanges its two diagonals. Thus \(D_f\mapsto-E_{hf}\), \(E_f\mapsto-D_{hf}\), and \(S_f\mapsto S_{hf}\). The vertical reflection has the same formulas. Consequently either reflection sends \(F=\sum_fD_f\) to \(-\sum_fE_f=-F+\sum_fS_f\). Since these transformations generate \(D_m\times D_n\), they determine the entire module.

**4. Exact order-two obstruction.** A translation-invariant lift of the torus fundamental class must be \(F+c\sum_f S_f\) for a single integer \(c\): the translations act transitively on \(\mathcal F\), and \(\{S_f\}\) is a free integral basis of the kernel. An equivariant section of the orientation-character quotient would require a horizontal reflection to negate this lift. The displayed action gives \(1+2c=0\), impossible for \(c\in\mathbb Z\). Thus the sequence does not split. On the other hand, \(2F-\sum_fS_f\) is fixed by all translations and negated by either reflection. It is an equivariant lift of **twice** the quotient generator. Equivalently, after pulling back the extension along multiplication by two on \(\mathbb Z_\varepsilon\), it splits. Hence its class has exact additive order two in the integral extension group. Over \(\mathbb Q\), the lift \(F-\tfrac12\sum_fS_f\) provides a splitting.

## Verification
`VERIFY.py` is a standalone Python 3 script using only the standard library. Run `python3 VERIFY.py`. It independently reconstructs every nonempty simplex of \(\mathcal N[C_m\square C_n]\), checks every proposed codimension-one elementary collapse with a unique-coface assertion, and verifies that the remaining complex consists exactly of the graph and four triangular faces per square. It then verifies all signed two-triangle transformations for the two coordinate reflections and two unit translations, separately on each square. Cases \((m,n)=(5,5),(5,6),(6,5),(6,6),(5,7),(7,5)\) all passed, comprising 1,337 elementary collapse checks and 1,528 signed disk-action comparisons. The universal assertion is proved symbolically above; finite tests are only checks, not extrapolation.

## Relationship to prior work
Matsushita's *Closed neighborhood complexes of graphs* (arXiv:2511.12608, first public November 16, 2025) establishes the closed-neighborhood definition, its connection with graph Čech nerves and other complexes, and fundamental-group results. It studies certain Cartesian products of **complete graphs**, not this integral toroidal-grid reflection lattice. Adams–Shukla–Singh's *Čech complexes of hypercube graphs* (arXiv:2212.05871) treats the graph-Čech construction and cube homotopy, not torus reflection extensions. The published findings *Square-cell splitting for closed neighborhood complexes of triangle-free graphs* (research record 344271f4218a) and *Square-completion decomposition of scale-two graph Čech nerves* (record 98a5c720a71e) already establish the general nonequivariant collapse and, in the latter, the homotopy type \(T^2\vee\bigvee^{mn}S^2\) for this same toroidal family. **Neither that homotopy statement nor its existence of a wedge splitting determines the new integral action or the nonsplit order-two equivariant extension.** In a different complex, an integral face–winding obstruction has been studied for toroidal total two-cut complexes; it is not a theorem about the closed-neighborhood complex and has a different kernel and quotient.

## Limitations
This is a theorem about the subgroup \(D_m\times D_n\), not about all possible automorphisms of a square torus when \(m=n\). No claim is made for circumference four or smaller, for higher Čech scales, for arbitrary graph products. The wedge decomposition and Betti numbers are **prior work**, not newly claimed. The new assertion is the explicit integral equivariant lattice and its order-two nonsplitting; unindexed equivalent results remain a possible originality risk.
