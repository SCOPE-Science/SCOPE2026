# Review of odd hyper-base expansion counts

**Correctness (PASS).** The last-digit decomposition is valid including the zero boundary. A four-state parity automaton (with the zero state absorbing) gives a three-by-three integer transfer matrix. Its invariant difference between counts of states \(10\) and \(01\) is exactly one, reducing the transfer law to the stated inhomogeneous second-order recurrence. Solving that recurrence proves the rational generating function and the exact growth exponent; the binary case is separately classified modulo three. Finite independent generating-product computations corroborate the transfer identity. No asymptotic theorem is inferred from finite computations.

**Originality (PASS under inspected evidence).** Defant (2015) and Coons–Spiegelhofer (2015) supply the underlying generalized Stern recurrences and maximal-order information, not odd-value frequency. Dilcher–Ericksen (2015) examine binary polynomial refinements, and Wakhare et al. (2018) examine generalized polynomial refinements. Their inspected materials do not state or dominate the universal parity-frequency law. The exact base-three numerical count appears as shifted OEIS A052948 and the exact base-four count appears as OEIS A180144, both in unrelated enumerations; these tables and corresponding one-base rational generating functions are explicitly *not* claimed as new. Existing binary Stern residue laws cover the \(b=2\) comparison, not the \(b\ge3\) density transition. Searches cannot exclude unindexed equivalent work.

**Value (PASS).** Hyper-base representations count a natural restricted partition function of powers of the base. The parity frequency is a natural statistic that was not determined by existing extremal growth results. The structural state invariant yields an exact all-bases block census, an explicit sublinear exponent for each larger base, and a sharp qualitative boundary between positive and zero natural density. This is an infinite arithmetic distribution statement, not an arbitrary sample, isolated small case, or recomputation of OEIS entries.

**Scientific limitations.** Classification concerns parity only; no claim is made for higher moduli or uniformity as the base varies. The source comparison does not establish exhaustive priority. The program's finite checks do not replace the proof. Independent mathematical validation has not occurred.
