# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The claim is exact for all finite \(n\ge2\) and all birth labels. A change-of-parent coupling, the known uniform-ancestor probability \(1/t\), and harmonic-depth expectations prove the single-arrow covariance lemma. Subtraction yields the exact \(-1/j^2\) decrement. Strictly decreasing reciprocal-square tails prove a single sign crossing, and Euler–Maclaurin expansions justify the quadratic horizon. The standalone exact-rational verifier independently checks all entries through nine vertices. Its finite checks are not treated as proof of asymptotic statements.

**Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, with stated priority risk.** The closest primary literature is Eslava, arXiv:1611.07466, and Lodewijks, arXiv:2204.09032. The former studies high-degree-vertex depth limits, and the latter joint limiting laws (including specified labels) rather than the exact covariance between a child count at one fixed label and the depth at a different fixed label. A related publicly indexed root-attachment/total-path-length covariance concerns a different aggregate observable. Neither the inspected primary text nor the searched research records produced an equivalent all-label finite formula. This is not a proof of historical novelty; implicit prior derivations remain possible.

**Value — PASS.** The universal decrement quantifies how a local degree statistic influences distant future depths, and reveals an unexpected sign reversal at a *quadratic* later birth label. The effect survives as an exact finite identity, even though normalized large-tree limits may hide it. It is directly motivated by joint degree–depth dependence in birth-ordered random networks and is not merely a reparameterization of marginal moments.

The zero-covariance boundary is not an independence theorem; no claim of general growth-model universality is made.
