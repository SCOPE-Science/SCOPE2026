# A rank-eight defect and a 2204-fold rowmotion split for two cubic incidence posets

## Finding

Let \(Q\) be the three-dimensional hypercube graph and let \(W\) be the eight-vertex Wagner graph (the Möbius ladder). Let \(P(G)\) denote the height-two incidence poset of a graph \(G\): one minimal element for each vertex, one maximal element for each edge, with incidence as the order relation. Write
\[
H_G(t)=\sum_{I\in J(P(G))}t^{|I|},\qquad
F_G(u,v)=\sum_{U\subseteq V(G)}u^{|U|}v^{|E(G[U])|}.
\]
Then the exact polynomial identities are
\[
\boxed{F_Q(u,v)-F_W(u,v)=2u^4(1-v)^4},
\qquad
\boxed{H_Q(t)-H_W(t)=2t^8}.
\]
Thus all order-ideal ranks agree except rank eight, which has two more ideals for the cube. In particular, the cube and Wagner incidence posets have respectively \(12643\) and \(12641\) ideals.

Let \(\rho_G\) be *classical rowmotion* on \(J(P(G))\), sending an ideal to the ideal generated by the minimal elements of its complement. Its exact orbit statistics are:

| Invariant | Cube \(Q\) | Wagner \(W\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Number of order ideals | 12643 | 12641 |
| Number of rowmotion orbits | 1093 | 901 |
| Largest orbit length | 90 | 232 |
| Number of largest-length orbits | 16 | 2 |
| Permutation order of rowmotion | 110880 | 244379520 |
| Vertex automorphism group order | 48 | 16 |

In particular,
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{ord}(\rho_W)=2204\,\operatorname{ord}(\rho_Q)}.
\]
The full orbit-length multiplicities, written as \((\text{length},\text{number of orbits})\), are

- Cube: \((2,226),(3,1),(4,18),(5,4),(6,210),(8,222),(10,82),(12,96),(14,64),(18,24),(20,30),(22,12),(28,12),(32,12),(36,32),(42,8),(48,12),(56,12),(90,16)\).
- Wagner: \((2,209),(3,1),(6,112),(7,8),(8,131),(10,42),(11,16),(12,76),(14,68),(16,32),(18,16),(20,46),(21,8),(22,8),(24,20),(28,16),(32,6),(36,32),(38,16),(48,4),(58,16),(72,2),(76,8),(112,4),(128,2),(232,2)\).

## Assumptions and scope

Both graphs have vertex set \(\{0,1,\ldots,7\}\), with the following unambiguous edge definitions. For the cube, \(\{a,b\}\) is an edge exactly when the binary expansions of \(a\) and \(b\) differ in one bit. For the Wagner graph, the edges are the eight consecutive pairs \(\{i,i+1\}\) modulo eight, together with the four opposite pairs \(\{i,i+4\}\) for \(0\le i<4\). Both graphs are connected, triangle-free, cubic and vertex-transitive, and have twelve edges.

Order ideals are lower ideals: inclusion of an edge element forces inclusion of both its endpoint elements. No other comparabilities exist. The result concerns ordinary finite combinatorial rowmotion, not birational or piecewise-linear rowmotion. All counts concern labelled graph vertices and labelled incidence-poset elements; no quotient by symmetry is used in the rowmotion counts.

## Proof

**Rank-polynomial identity.** Every ideal of \(P(G)\) is uniquely a pair \((U,T)\) where \(U\subseteq V(G)\) and \(T\subseteq E(G[U])\). This gives \(H_G(t)=F_G(t,1+t)\).

For any eight-vertex, cubic, triangle-free graph with twelve edges, the numbers of induced edges among all vertex subsets of each size \(k\le3\) are determined by the common parameters. For triples, for example, there are \(24\) triples spanning two edges (one centered path per incident edge pair), \(24\) spanning one edge, and \(8\) spanning no edges. For any complementary vertex subsets \(U,B=V\setminus U\), regularity yields
\[
|E(G[U])|=12-3|B|+|E(G[B])|.
\]
Consequently the two graphs also have identical induced-edge distributions for subsets of sizes \(k\ge5\); only four-vertex subsets can distinguish them.

Let \(a_j(G)\) count the four-vertex subsets inducing \(j\) edges, for \(0\le j\le4\). Triangle-freeness excludes more than four induced edges. The cube has exactly two independent four-sets: its two bipartition classes. Indeed, connected regular bipartite graphs have \(|N(X)|>|X|\) for any nonempty proper subset \(X\) of one class, so a maximum independent four-set cannot meet both classes. The Wagner graph has no independent four-set: the only independent four-sets of its underlying eight-cycle are its two alternating classes, and each contains opposite-pair chords. Therefore \(a_0(Q)=2\) and \(a_0(W)=0\).

An induced four-set spanning four edges must be a four-cycle. The cube has exactly six square faces, hence \(a_4(Q)=6\). The Wagner graph has exactly four four-cycles: each uses two opposite-pair chords and two cycle edges; hence \(a_4(W)=4\). The following common equations hold:
\[
\sum_{j=0}^4 a_j=\binom84=70,\qquad
\sum_{j=0}^4j a_j=12\binom62=180,
\]
\[
\sum_{j=0}^4\binom j2a_j
=8\binom32(8-3)+\left(\binom{12}{2}-8\binom32\right)=162.
\]
The last equation counts the contribution of adjacent and disjoint pairs of graph edges. Solving these three equations using the stated values of \(a_0\) and \(a_4\) gives:

| Induced edges \(j\) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(a_j(Q)\) | 2 | 0 | 30 | 32 | 6 |
| \(a_j(W)\) | 0 | 8 | 18 | 40 | 4 |
| Difference | 2 | -8 | 12 | -8 | 2 |

Therefore \(F_Q(u,v)-F_W(u,v)=2u^4(1-v)^4\), and evaluating at \((u,v)=(t,1+t)\) gives \(H_Q(t)-H_W(t)=2t^8\).

**Rowmotion transition.** For an ideal \((U,T)\), the minimal elements outside it are precisely the missing vertex elements \(V\setminus U\) and the missing edge elements corresponding to \(E(G[U])\setminus T\). Therefore rowmotion is explicitly
\[
\rho_G(U,T)=\left((V\setminus U)\cup
\bigcup_{e\in E(G[U])\setminus T}V(e),\quad E(G[U])\setminus T\right),
\]
where \(V(e)\) denotes the two endpoints of \(e\). This formula is valid for every ideal, with no exceptions.

**Exhaustive finite evaluation.** The accompanying standalone Python 3 verifier enumerates all pairs \((U,T)\), computes this explicit map at each state, and separately computes rowmotion by toggling all twenty poset elements in reverse linear-extension order. It checks equality of the two maps at *every* state; bijectivity; complete disjoint cycle traversal; each orbit-length histogram; and the identity \(\sum_{\ell}\ell c_\ell=|J(P(G))|\). The full finite evaluation gives the orbit tables above. The least common multiple of the lengths is the permutation order; its two exact values have ratio \(2204\). These tests reconstruct all 25284 states and every transition rather than sampling states.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_incidence_rowmotion.py` with Python 3.10 or later, using only the standard library. The script constructs both graphs from their stated definitions and enumerates all \(256\) vertex subsets and all permitted edge subsets, hence all ideals. It independently checks all rowmotion images by generic top-to-bottom toggles, exact cycle closure and uniqueness, the complete orbit lists, vertex transitivity, and the rank-polynomial defect. Its final successful output line is `VERIFY_OK`. The verification covers the *entire* claimed finite state spaces.

## Relationship to prior work

Classical rowmotion and its toggle description are established mathematics; in particular Grinberg and Roby's preprint of 25 February 2014 develops the framework and order results for rectangles, triangles and skeletal posets. Neither incidence poset here is a rectangle, triangle-family poset, or connected skeletal height-two poset of the form considered there: each rank-two edge element lies above exactly two of eight rank-one elements, whereas a connected skeletal height-two poset is a complete bipartite two-rank poset. The paper does not give these finite orbit censuses.

Elizalde, Plante, Roby and Sagan study rowmotion on fence posets (whose Hasse cover graphs are paths) and derive orbit descriptions in that setting, rather than for the two 20-element cubic-graph incidence posets considered here. The novelty claimed is only the specific rank-polynomial identity and complete paired orbit computation; foundational rowmotion definitions, general results and the graph constructions are not claimed as new. Queries and comparisons against indexed mathematics records did not reveal a source implying both identities or the orbit tables.

## Limitations

This is an exact comparison of two natural finite posets, not a statement about every cubic graph or an unproved asymptotic behavior. The rowmotion orbit tables rely on reproducible exhaustive integer computation. Their finite enumeration does not prove properties of unenumerated larger posets. A previous unpublished or unindexed computation of these particular ranks or rowmotion orbits remains possible; lack of a search hit alone cannot establish priority. Independent audit has not been performed.
