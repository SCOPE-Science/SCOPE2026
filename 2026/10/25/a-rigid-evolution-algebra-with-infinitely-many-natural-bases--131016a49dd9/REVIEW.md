# Review: Exact mixed-prime formula for a finite abelian factor

## Correctness — PASS
The multiplication is \(xy=(x_1y_1-x_2y_2)v+x_3y_3w\). It yields \(A^2=\operatorname{span}\{v,w\}\) of dimension \(2\), zero annihilator, the stated natural-basis family, and the rank-based proof that every automorphism is the identity. The quantifiers are explicit: one algebra over \(\mathbb{Q}\), and one basis for each integer \(t\ge2\).

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The recent primary survey asks whether finite automorphism groups can coexist with infinitely many natural-basis classes. The broad finite-group phenomenon has a related two-dimensional rank-one example with a nontrivial group of order \(2\); it does not imply the stronger trivial-group case here, with \(\dim A=3\) and \(\dim A^2=2\). Targeted published-finding corpus searches for the trivial-Aut formulation returned no exact match, while the closest retrieved records treat different families or invariants. Search absence is not treated as a novelty proof.

## Value — PASS
The result isolates a natural rigidity boundary: even when the algebra has no nonidentity automorphisms, its natural bases can still have infinitely many equivalence classes. This strengthens the finite-group phenomenon rather than repeating the broad existence statement.
