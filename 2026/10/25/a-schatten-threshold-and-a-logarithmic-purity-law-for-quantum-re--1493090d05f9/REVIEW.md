# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem applies to the time-nonlocal density-matrix evolution defined in RESULT.md, with fixed rate and an arbitrary separable-space self-adjoint Hamiltonian. The key steps have been reconstructed independently: the scalar hypergeometric multiplier and its zero-frequency normalization; its interpretation as a random-unitary characteristic function; the Hilbert–Schmidt spectral projection onto the commutant of the Hamiltonian; the compact-operator argument excluding invariant continuous-spectrum components; the exact trace-mass obstruction; the uniformly localized Kummer asymptotic for the flat-band purity; and a two-level compression witnessing arbitrarily slow pure-point decoherence. The long-time Gaussian frequency integral yields precisely the coefficient \(r\sqrt{\pi/2}\). The finite program checks 49 assertions but is not substituted for any infinite-dimensional argument. Finite energy moments in the compact-resolvent construction follow from geometric initial weights versus linearly increasing energy. Standard special-function and spectral-theorem facts are invoked; no independent formal proof certificate is claimed.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks

The exact hypergeometric solution, pointwise decay of nonzero Bohr frequencies, and energy-basis diagonal stationary state in separated discrete spectra **are already established** by de Mauro, Kulkarni and Majumdar (arXiv:2608.02297v1). Accordingly none is claimed as a new finding. The precise contribution instead comprises: (i) a complete trace-class versus \(\mathcal S_p\) limiting criterion including mixed and singular-continuous spectrum, (ii) the sharp \(1/\sqrt{\log(rt)}\) flat-band purity constant, and (iii) arbitrarily slow trace-norm convergence for a compact-resolvent Hamiltonian and a pure state having all polynomial energy moments. These are motivated spectral and quantitative extensions, not a reformulation of the paper's gapped/gapless dichotomy. Rose et al. (arXiv:1806.01298) analyze fixed-state Markovian resets instead of history sampling. Related published results on pure-dephasing approximation error, Schatten-operator problems, and trace-class geometry concern different operators or evolution equations. A general quantum mean-ergodic or dephasing theorem could imply the topology criterion, and the exact purity coefficient might be derivable from published special-function estimates; these remain residual priority risks. Absence of search hits alone is not treated as a novelty proof.

## Value — PASS

The memory-reset model explicitly distinguishes gapped versus continuous spectra, but its laboratory observables can be sensitive to the convergence topology. The trace-norm criterion says exactly when a normalized stationary density operator exists, while the Schatten result explains why experimentally useful quadratic observables can nevertheless equilibrate. The explicit logarithmic purity coefficient supplies a quantitative continuum benchmark. The slow pure-point example shows that even discrete confinement, compact resolvent, and arbitrarily many finite energy moments do not justify a universal algebraic rate without a Bohr-gap bound. None of these statements relies on arbitrarily chosen numerical windows or merely reproduces an existing spectral table.
