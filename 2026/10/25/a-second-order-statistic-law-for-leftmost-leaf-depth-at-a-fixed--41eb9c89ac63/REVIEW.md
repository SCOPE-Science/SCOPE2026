# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The Dyck-contour bijection translates leaf count to peak count and leftmost-leaf depth to initial-ascent height. Lagrange inversion of a specified bivariate Catalan generating function proves the conditional probability mass formula. Direct uniform-subset counting yields an exact second-order-statistic representation; nested subsets give the simultaneous coupling. The gap formulation gives moments and a hypergeometric tail, and finite-population limits give the three asymptotic regimes. The independent standard-library checker reports 7,672 exact assertions. Finite checks are not used to infer infinite validity.

## Originality — PASS, provisional
The closest fully inspected original work is Deutsch's 1998 paper, which concerns *marginal* equidistribution of first-peak height and returns, without determining conditioning on total peaks. Janson's random-tree treatise supplies the probability model but not the specified conditional leaf-depth identity in the inspected relevant sections. Deutsch's longer 1999 Dyck-path enumeration could imply a stronger joint identity, but its full text could not be inspected. The originality assessment is therefore qualified by a genuine historical-priority risk and should not be interpreted as proof of absolute novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
For a natural family of uniform rooted ordered random trees, the result determines the leaf-conditioned local depth law exactly, strengthens it to a nested all-level coupling, and gives a macroscopic–mesoscopic–bounded transition as the number of leaves grows. The statistic and conditioning are intrinsic, not ad hoc; a marginal Catalan/Narayana count does not supply these dependences.

## Closest literature and limitations
The underlying Catalan/Narayana enumeration is standard. The claim does not couple complete trees or extend automatically to labeled trees. Some historical full text remained inaccessible and no independent historical priority or mathematical audit has been performed.
