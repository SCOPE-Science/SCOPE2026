# A seven-torsion Moore quotient of the Fano cap complex

## Finding

Let \(F\) be the Fano plane on \(V=\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z\), with lines the seven translates of \(\{0,1,3\}\). Its **cap complex** \(K\) has a face for every subset of \(V\) containing no entire Fano line. The Singer translation \(r:i\mapsto i+1\) acts freely on its realization and satisfies

\[
|K|/\langle r\rangle\simeq M(\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z,1),
\]

where the Moore space is the CW complex with one vertex, one loop, and one two-cell attached by a degree-seven map. The associated covering has

\[
K\simeq\bigvee^{6}S^2,
\qquad
\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong
\ker\bigl(\varepsilon:\mathbb Z[C_7]\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\bigr)
\]

as an **integral** \(C_7\)-representation. All other reduced integral homology groups of \(K\) vanish. In particular, \(H_1(|K|/C_7;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z\), with no other positive-degree integral homology.

## Assumptions and scope

All simplicial complexes are finite, with ordinary geometric realizations. The vertex set is \(V=\{0,1,\ldots,6\}\), with arithmetic modulo seven, and the line set is precisely \(L_i=\{i,i+1,i+3\}\) for \(i\in V\). The group \(C_7\) is generated by simultaneous translation of these point labels. The quotient is the topological orbit space, with its induced \(\Delta\)-complex/CW structure; it is not a quotient obtained by identifying vertices of an abstract simplicial complex and deleting loops.

The statement is limited to the order-two projective plane and this specific free cyclic symmetry, not all projective planes or all of \(\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\).

## Proof

**The cap complex and its free simplicial action.** Two Fano lines meet in one point. Thus the complement of a line is a four-point cap, since otherwise a line would be disjoint from it. Conversely, any four-point set containing a line is the union of that line with one of four other points. Since two distinct lines have union of size five, exactly \(7\cdot4=28\) four-point sets contain a line, leaving exactly seven four-point caps; they are all the complements of lines. Every five-point set contains a line (two Fano lines avoid each prescribed pair of excluded vertices). Consequently, the seven tetrahedra \(V\setminus L_i\) are exactly the facets. Distinct tetrahedra intersect in two vertices. The complex therefore has \(f\)-vector \((7,21,28,7)\), and every triangle belongs to exactly one tetrahedron. A nonidentity translation cyclically permutes all seven vertices, so no nonempty face of size at most four can be stable. This proves that the induced action on \(|K|\) is free.

**Equivariant collapse.** The tetrahedron \(\tau=\{0,1,2,5\}\) is complementary to \(L_3=\{3,4,6\}\). Its face \(\eta=\{1,2,5\}\) occurs in no other tetrahedron. Removing \(\tau\) together with \(\eta\), simultaneously for all seven translates, is an equivariant elementary collapse. It leaves a two-dimensional \(C_7\)-complex \(Y\) and an equivariant strong deformation retraction \(|K|\to|Y|\). Hence \(|K|/C_7\simeq |Y|/C_7\).

**The quotient attaching maps.** Every vertex, edge, triangle, and tetrahedron orbit has seven elements. Before the collapse the orbit-cell counts are \((1,3,4,1)\). Choose oriented quotient loops \(a,b,c\) represented respectively by edges \((0,1),(0,2),(0,3)\). For a directed edge with step \(d\) modulo seven, write \(a,b,c\) if \(d=1,2,3\), and the corresponding inverse if \(d=6,5,4\). The four oriented triangle-orbit representatives and their attaching words are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
(0,1,2)&a^2b^{-1}\\
(0,1,4)&ac^2\\
(0,1,5)&ac^{-1}b\\
(0,2,4)&b^2c
\end{array}.
\]

The translate \(\eta=(1,2,5)\) is exactly the translate by one of \((0,1,4)\). Its orbit is the face orbit removed by the equivariant collapse. Thus \(|Y|/C_7\) is a two-dimensional CW complex with one vertex, three loops, and exactly three two-cell attaching words

\[
a^2b^{-1},\qquad ac^{-1}b,\qquad b^2c.
\]

**Homotopy cancellations, not only an abelianized presentation.** A two-cell whose attaching word contains a given loop once with exponent \(+1\) or \(-1\) permits a cellular one–two cancellation after replacing that loop by the corresponding word in the other generators. The first relator cancels \(b\), substituting \(b=a^2\). The second then cancels \(c\), substituting \(c=ba=a^3\). The last attaching word becomes \((a^2)^2a^3=a^7\). These are elementary homotopy-preserving CW cancellations (equivalently, the corresponding generator-elimination Tietze moves on the two-dimensional presentation complex). The remaining CW complex has one vertex, one loop, and one two-cell of attaching degree seven, proving the asserted Moore homotopy type.

**The cover and its integral module.** The quotient has fundamental group \(C_7\). Since \(|K|\to |K|/C_7\) is a connected regular cover of degree seven, it is the universal cover. Pulling back the above quotient homotopy equivalence to universal covers identifies the integral deck-group homology module with that of the universal cover of the Moore space, up to automorphism of \(C_7\). Put \(R=\mathbb Z[C_7]\), \(t=r\), and \(N=1+t+\cdots+t^6\). Its equivariant cellular chain complex in positive degrees is

\[
0\longrightarrow R\xrightarrow{N}R\xrightarrow{t-1}R\longrightarrow0.
\]

Because \(Nx=\varepsilon(x)N\), its second homology is \(\ker N=\ker\varepsilon\), the integral augmentation ideal of rank six; its first homology vanishes. The cover is homotopy equivalent to the two-dimensional \(Y\), so a simply connected two-complex with free second integral homology of rank six. Hurewicz and the homological Whitehead theorem give \(K\simeq\bigvee^6 S^2\). This completes the proof.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library checker `checker.py` reconstructs the seven facets, enumerates all faces and translation orbits, checks the uniqueness of the free-face orbit, independently derives all four attaching words, and calculates the signed quotient cellular boundaries

\[
\partial_2=\begin{pmatrix}2&1&1&0\\-1&0&1&2\\0&2&-1&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\partial_3=\begin{pmatrix}-1\\1\\1\\-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

It verifies \(\partial_2\partial_3=0\), that the successive determinantal divisors of \(\partial_2\) are \(1,1,7\), and that the surviving three relators have abelianized determinant \(7\). These are finite combinatorial cross-checks; the infinite homotopy/module conclusions follow from the explicit cell-cancellation and universal-cover arguments above, not from a finite-rank calculation alone. Reproduce with `python3 checker.py`.

## Relationship to prior work

Superdock's 2021 preprint constructs complexes from Fano and other projective-plane incidence structures, including the seven-vertex torus with two seven-triangle Fano systems and free cyclic symmetry. That two-dimensional torus is **not** the present seven-tetrahedron cap complex, and its results do not identify this cap quotient as a Moore space. The Fano plane's cyclic Singer difference set and seven complementary hyperovals are classical. Separately studied Heawood-graph independence homology carries a related rational cyclic character, but a character alone determines neither this quotient's homotopy type nor its integral cell attaching map. The contribution here is the explicit quotient homotopy classification and consequent integral covering module.

## Limitations

No homotopy classification is asserted for higher-order finite projective-plane cap complexes or other cyclic subgroups. Equivariant homotopy equivalence under the full Fano collineation group is not claimed. The result has undergone mathematical and executable checks described above but no independent external validation. The literature comparison cannot rule out inaccessible or unindexed equivalent work.
