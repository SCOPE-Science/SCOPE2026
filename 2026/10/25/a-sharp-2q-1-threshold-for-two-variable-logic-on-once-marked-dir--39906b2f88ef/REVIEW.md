# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof distinguishes every length through \(2q\) by rank-bounded two-variable sentences. It separately proves indistinguishability for all larger lengths by a uniform two-pebble strategy, whose only counting step requires at least four interior choices before excluding at most three. The loop on the one-vertex cycle and rank-zero and rank-one boundary cases are explicit. Exact finite type refinement and threshold-sentence evaluations agreed with the theorem in the specified test ranges; finite checking is not treated as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to stated priority risk
The examined 2026 successor papers study exact successors of linear orders, hence directed acyclic paths, and their main results are model existence/complexity bounds rather than rank-equivalence classes of one-marked cycles. Related published rank claims for unmarked paths and one-red linear orders do not imply the exact statement because of changes in structure, vocabulary, variable count, and sharp parameter. Older work on order-invariant two-variable logic and rankers is a residual priority risk; not all plausible older full texts were available for complete comparison. No claim of universal priority follows from failed searches.

## Value — PASS
This is a complete structural classification of a natural bounded-variable elementary-equivalence invariant, giving an exact representative bound and optimal distinguishing sentences for every finite exception. It isolates the cost of closing a successor path into a marked directed cycle, rather than reporting an arbitrary sample of finite enumerations.

## Limitations
Only the one-red directed successor cycle family, with equality, no order, no counting, and two variable names, is classified. Broader coloured structures and counting logics are not covered. No independent validation has been performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
