# Scientific review: branching crossover for broom zonotope widths

**Correctness (PASS).** The support identity is exact: summing centered graph-root segments gives width equal to the sum of absolute edge-coordinate differences. Under a projected Gaussian direction, the single hub produces the conditional mean \(d\psi(G_0)\); its conditional residual has variance at most \(2d\). The remaining long path and Gaussian radial fluctuation have a joint central limit theorem by one-dependence and blocking. Direct Gaussian covariances yield \(\sigma^2=8/3+(4\sqrt3-14)/\pi\). The two regimes follow from Slutsky's theorem; \(\psi(G_0)\) is non-Gaussian because it is nonconstant and bounded below, and Gaussian convolution cannot disguise that. Finite code checks support identities and constants but do not replace the proof. All integers \(d=o(n)\), including zero, are covered; linear-degree hubs are not.

**Originality (PASS, with bibliographic risk).** The closest indexed result, *Sharp width-variance extremizers among tree graphical zonotopes*, proves exact first-two-width-moment formulas and tree extremizers; its full theorem and limitations section explicitly do not establish complete width distributions. The current claim identifies a distributional phase transition and gives its non-Gaussian law, which is not implied by moments. Padrol--Pilaud--Poullot develop deformations and normal fans, while Deza--Pournin address optimization; Schneider treats randomly generated zonotopes rather than Haar directions in fixed graphical zonotopes. Exact graph-indexed Gaussian variation results could exist outside indexed searches, so independent priority checking remains useful.

**Mathematical value (PASS).** All tree graphical zonotopes in this class are combinatorial cubes with equal intrinsic volume and mean spherical width. Nevertheless, a hub with only order-\(\sqrt n\) leaves changes the *limiting type* of fluctuations from normal to a genuinely non-normal Gaussian convolution; larger sublinear hubs exhibit a different normalization and limit. This is a structural law rather than a routine numeric refinement of known variance extrema.

**Limitations.** The paper covers unweighted broom trees only, not arbitrary high-degree graph sequences, weighted generators, local limits or effective rates. Numerical integration and finite Monte Carlo cannot certify the asymptotic claims. Historical searches establish best-knowledge priority, not absolute uniqueness.
