# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For all \(r,K,b>0\), the increasing transformation \(z=bx-1/x\) makes predator pressure additive in the scalar equation. Differentiating the unforced drift gives the exact sign condition \(-1+b x^2(1-2x/K)\), whose maximum over positive prey equals \(-1+bK^2/27\). The cumulative-pressure comparison proves arbitrary-duration, arbitrary-block sorting below the threshold. A second-order flow calculation proves sharpness and both local reversal regions above it. Equality at \(bK^2=27\) is included because the transformed drift remains strictly decreasing. The example and the expansion were reconstructed with exact rational algebra; the standalone executable reproduced the stated tests. Proof boundaries: no optimal-policy claim for arbitrary durations above threshold; all finite simulations are corroboration only.

## Originality — PASS, with documented limits

A semantic research-record comparison and accessible earlier findings were checked for fixed-pressure treatment sorting, Holling-III equivalences, critical carrying-capacity ratios, and broader scalar rearrangement implications. Jiang and Wang (2013) and Prakash and Vamsi (2025) were read as full open-access primary texts. The latter contains a coefficient-27 nullcline threshold in a distinct two-population model: the number or nullcline shape itself is not new. Denny (2014) establishes Holling background. The closest controllability paper by Kar and Matsuda (2007) was not fully accessible; its abstract cannot decide absence of coverage. General scalar comparison methods may subsume proof technique but no inspected item states this fixed-block, all-duration application and its exact two-window converse. This is evidence for originality, not a claim of exhaustive uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

The threshold \(K/H=\sqrt{27}\) is a natural dimensionless comparison between carrying capacity and Type-III half-saturation density, not an arbitrary parameter slice. The result supplies a duration-independent management-ordering theorem for any number of prescribed treatments and pinpoints precisely why the rule fails, with a robust, strictly suppressive witness. This improves decision structure in an idealized ecological-control model; it does not validate field efficacy or unknown predator dynamics.
