# A sharp dyadic-sparsity criterion for quenched Brownian independence in random bootstrap walks

## Finding
Let \(0<\gamma<1/2\), \(0<\varepsilon\le1/2\), and let integers \(r_n\) obey \(0\le r_n\le\gamma\log_2 n\). For each \(0\le j<r_n\), independently sample \(B_{n,j}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_{n,j})\) with \(\varepsilon\le p_{n,j}\le1-\varepsilon\). Let \((\xi_i)_{i\ge1}\) be independent fair signs, independent of all these bits, and \(S_n=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1_{\{\xi_i=-1\}}\). With \(d_j(s)\) the \(j\)-th binary digit of \(s\), set \(\zeta_0=1\), \(\zeta_n=(-1)^{\sum_{j<r_n}B_{n,j}d_j(S_n)}\), \(X_N=\sum_{i=1}^N\xi_i\) and \(Y_N=\sum_{i=1}^N\zeta_{i-1}\xi_i\). Put \(b_n=\prod_{j<r_n}(1-p_{n,j})\) (empty product one) and \(c_N=N^{-1}\sum_{n=1}^N b_n\).

**Theorem.** For almost every environment and almost every sign trajectory, \(N^{-1}\sum_{n=1}^N(\zeta_n-b_n)\to0\). For each \(\rho\in[0,1]\), conditional on almost every environment, the two-dimensional rescaled path \(N^{-1/2}(X_{\lfloor Nt\rfloor},Y_{\lfloor Nt\rfloor})\) converges in \(D([0,\infty),\mathbb R^2)\) to Brownian motion with covariance matrix \(t\begin{psmallmatrix}1&\rho\\\rho&1\end{psmallmatrix}\) **if and only if** \(c_N\to\rho\). In particular, the components converge to independent Brownian motions if and only if, for each fixed integer \(M\ge0\), \(N^{-1}\#\{n\le N:r_n\le M\}\to0\). Thus only \(O(\log n)\) randomized dyadic coefficients per time suffice, while their divergence in density is the exact independence criterion.

## Assumptions and scope
Everything is discrete-time and one-dimensional before pairing. The environmental bits are resampled independently at each time, but unlike a fully elliptic family of all inclusion coefficients, only indices \(2^j\) for \(j<r_n\) are active; all other inclusion coefficients are identically zero. The construction is an inclusion-induced generalized bootstrap transform by Lucas's binary congruence. Statements are quenched with respect to the environment; the stronger joint almost-sure Cesàro assertion is proved first. Constants in the mixing estimates depend only on \(\gamma\), not on the individual probabilities. The case \(r_n=0\) is included.

## Proof
For each \(n\), define \(q_n=2^{r_n}\) and the periodic function \(g_n(s)=\prod_{j<r_n:d_j(s)=1}(1-2p_{n,j})\). Independence of the environmental bits gives \(\mathbb E_B[\zeta_n\mid S_n=s]=g_n(s)\). Averaging over all binary words of length \(r_n\) gives the exact residue mean \(q_n^{-1}\sum_{s=0}^{q_n-1}g_n(s)=\prod_{j<r_n}(1-p_{n,j})=b_n\).

The binomial transition \(S_n-S_m\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n-m,1/2)\) is independent of \(S_m\). Fourier inversion on the cyclic group of order \(q\) and \(|(1+e^{2\pi i\ell/q})/2|=|\cos(\pi\ell/q)|\) give, for \(q\ge2\), the uniform bound \(\sum_{a<q}|\mathbb P(\operatorname{Binomial}(h,1/2)\equiv a\pmod q)-q^{-1}|\le q\exp(-\pi^2h/(2q^2))\). The same deviation is zero when \(q=1\). Therefore, uniformly in the starting residue, \(|\mathbb E[g_n(s+\operatorname{Binomial}(h,1/2))]-b_n|\le q_n\exp(-\pi^2h/(2q_n^2))\). In particular \(\sum_n|\mathbb E\zeta_n-b_n|<\infty\), since \(q_n\le n^\gamma\) and \(\gamma<1/2\).

For \(m<n\), independence between environmental rows reduces the centered covariance exactly to \(\mathbb E[(g_m(S_m)-b_m)(g_n(S_n)-b_n)]\); conditioning on \(S_m\) bounds its magnitude by \(2q_n\exp(-\pi^2(n-m)/(2q_n^2))\), as well as trivially by \(4\). Choose \(\theta\) with \(2\gamma<\theta<1\). The \(O(N^{1+\theta})\) pairs with \(0<n-m<n^\theta\) contribute \(O(N^{\theta-1})\) to the variance of \(N^{-1}\sum_{n\le N}(\zeta_n-b_n)\); all farther pairs have an absolutely summable total bound \(\sum_n 2nq_n\exp(-\pi^2 n^\theta/(2q_n^2))<\infty\). Diagonal terms contribute \(O(N^{-1})\). Thus the second moment tends to zero at polynomial rate \(O(N^{\theta-1})\). Take a polynomial subsequence \(N_k=\lceil k^a\rceil\) for an integer \(a>1/(1-\theta)\). Chebyshev and Borel–Cantelli give almost-sure convergence along that subsequence; since the summands are bounded by two and \(N_{k+1}/N_k\to1\), deterministic interpolation proves almost-sure convergence for all \(N\). Fubini yields the claimed quenched almost-sure assertion.

For fixed environmental bits, both coordinates are square-integrable martingales in the sign filtration, since their increments are \((\xi_i,\zeta_{i-1}\xi_i)\) with predictable \(\zeta_{i-1}\). Their bracket matrix has diagonal entries \(N\) and off-diagonal entry \(\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\zeta_i\). The preceding almost-sure result makes the off-diagonal bracket divided by \(N\) converge to \(\rho\) if \(c_N\to\rho\); bounded increments give Lindeberg and the martingale functional central limit theorem. The convergence of the monotone-time brackets is uniform on compact time intervals by bounded increments and a finite grid.

Conversely, if quenched convergence to Brownian correlation \(\rho\) holds, then \(\mathbb E^B[X_NY_N]/N\to\rho\) by uniform integrability: both conditional fourth moments are at most \(3N^2\) by the elementary fair-sign martingale recurrence. Integrating this bounded conditional product expectation over environments gives \(N^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathbb E\zeta_i\to\rho\). The summable mean-error bound forces \(c_N\to\rho\). Finally, \(\varepsilon^{r_n}\le b_n\le(1-\varepsilon)^{r_n}\). Their Cesàro means vanish exactly when \(r_n\to\infty\) in natural density, completing the equivalence.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone Python program uses exact rational arithmetic, tests the residue product identity, environmental averaging, and binomial residue reduction, and compares the cross-time factorization against exhaustive short sign paths. Its recorded output reports 723 passing rational identities/comparisons. These finite checks do not certify the asymptotic theorem; the Fourier mixing, moment bound, almost-sure interpolation, and martingale limit are proved above.

## Relationship to prior work
Collevecchio–Hamza–Shi–Williams (2022, original arXiv 2020) develop general bootstrap random walks and their martingale functional limit criteria. Hamza–Tournier (2026) study independently randomized inclusion-induced walks with an elliptic parameter for every coefficient, establishing annealed functional convergence while discussing quenched difficulty. Their fully elliptic assumptions do not cover the present class, in which all but \(O(\log n)\) coefficients are deterministically zero. The contribution here is an explicit quenched limit and an exact density criterion for this sparse binary-digit class, not a new general martingale invariance principle. Standard cyclic Fourier estimates and Lucas's theorem are used without originality claims.

## Limitations
Only dyadic inclusion indices are active, environmental rows are independent, and \(r_n\le\gamma\log_2 n\) strictly below the \(\sqrt n\) mixing scale. The theorem does not claim a sharp result at \(\gamma=1/2\), nor cover arbitrary inclusion sets, correlated environmental rows, or non-fair input signs. The necessity statement is for convergence to a specified Brownian law under almost every environment; it does not classify non-Brownian subsequential phenomena. The priority comparison cannot guarantee exhaustive coverage under other terminology. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
