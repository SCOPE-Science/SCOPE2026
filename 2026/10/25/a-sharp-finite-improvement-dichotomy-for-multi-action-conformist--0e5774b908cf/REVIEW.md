# Scientific review: finite-improvement dichotomy for conformist–rebel network games

## Correctness

PASS. Every CR-free unilateral utility difference equals the difference of a bounded integer edge potential. When a CR edge exists and two unused stubborn-independent actions are available, an explicit four-state cycle provides strict gain one at every move, independently of all outside neighbors. Three-vertex coexistence and the two-action boundary are verified analytically from the complete strategy table. Exact definition-level program checks of graph cases are corroborative, not an infinite proof.

## Originality

PASS, qualified by publication-priority uncertainty. The primary complete 2026 multi-action manuscript establishes the game and sufficient potential results but does not identify this necessity direction for the all-strict-improvement-path FIP. The binary predecessor and earlier matching-pennies work concern different action/update regimes. Semantic mathematical-result searches found no equivalent returned claims; a more general unindexed improvement-cycle theorem and the incompletely inspected 2019 full text remain risks. No independent originality certification is asserted.

## Value

PASS. The result separates static Nash equilibrium existence from convergence of arbitrary decentralized better-response updates and gives a sharp, readily testable graph criterion throughout a natural multi-action regime. The smallest coexistence witness exhibits precisely why the distinction matters. No claim is made about probabilities of cycling under stochastic schedulers.
