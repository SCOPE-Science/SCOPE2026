# Scientific assessment: geometric-mean noise entanglement threshold

## Correctness — PASS

The statement fixes a trace-preserving, completely positive two-qubit Lindblad generator, an explicit global Poisson reset rule, and strictly positive \(J,a,b,r\). The stationary resolvent produces the displayed positive trace-one \(X\)-state. Pointwise positivity of the inactive partial-transpose branch, followed by Cauchy–Schwarz under the reset-age mixture, rules out a spurious second concurrence branch. Exact symbolic cancellation identifies the remaining partial-transpose determinant as minus a specified quartic over a positive denominator. Nonnegative coefficients for \(J^2\le ab\), and exactly one Descartes sign change plus opposing endpoint signs for \(J^2>ab\), prove the global phase classification. Expanding the explicit stationary matrix establishes the strong-reset concurrence coefficient. The standalone numerical checker additionally passed 3646 tests including independent full-matrix solutions, though no finite test collection substitutes for the analytic sign proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to scholarly priority review

Hartmann, Dür, and Briegel (arXiv:quant-ph/0512219 and arXiv:quant-ph/0703138, 2005–2007) already demonstrate dissipative steady-state entanglement, qualitative resetting-rate thresholds, and persistent finite-rate entanglement under **independently acting local single-qubit resets**; those antecedents are not claimed as new. In contrast, the present reset event erases/replaces the *whole pair* at one clock and the model has two independently tunable bit-flip rates, leading to an exact product cutoff. Kulkarni and Majumdar (arXiv:2307.07485, 2023) derive reset-generated two-spin entanglement for **unitary** dynamics and make the key comparison study; their full accessible article does not include the specific independent local bit-flip dissipators or derive the geometric-mean noise cutoff with a single reset-rate threshold. Magoni and coauthors (arXiv:2202.12655) analyze reset-generated quantum correlations in a **noninteracting** Rabi spin system. Sevilla and Valdés-Hernández (arXiv:2209.05643) develop general closed-system reset renewal, from which the elementary resolvent principle follows, but not the dissipative two-qubit PPT boundary without additional work. Shea and Romito (arXiv:2403.08422) study local noise and measurement-induced entanglement with a **different continuous-monitoring scheme**, not a full-product global reset. Wald and coauthors (arXiv:2410.11497) analyze **discrete-time unitary-gate** resetting. Riera-Campeny et al. (arXiv:2011.04403) and Perfetto et al. (arXiv:2112.05078) give renewal structures for open-system resets, not this exact stationary two-qubit concurrence criterion. The contribution assessed here is an exact *dissipative* global-reset concurrence phase diagram, not the stationary resolvent itself or two-qubit PPT criterion. General separability results, potentially covering the phase boundary after a nonobvious reduction, remain a residual priority risk; no independent audit has occurred.

## Scientific value — PASS

The theorem answers a motivated robustness question left open by the ideal unitary reset-entanglement setting: whether entanglement survives two independent local flip channels, and what reset rate is required. An exact and sharp coupling-versus-noise product threshold, with a provably unique reset-rate onset, replaces an unconstrained parameter scan. In particular, highly asymmetric local noise does not by itself forbid entanglement if its geometric mean remains below coupling; the reset intervention threshold becomes large rather than disappearing. This is a physically interpretable boundary for stationary entanglement engineering. The finding is not claimed to cover other noise operators, partial resets, or time-dependent finite-time entanglement.
