# A sharp sphere-to-Möbius transition in scale-two Rips complexes of Möbius ladders

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\), let \(M_{2n}\) be the Möbius ladder with vertices \(\mathbb Z/(2n)\), joining \(i\) to \(i\pm1\) and \(i+n\) modulo \(2n\). Give this graph's **vertex set** the shortest-path metric, and let \(X_n=\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(V(M_{2n}))\) be the inclusive diameter-two Vietoris–Rips complex. Then
\[
X_n\simeq\begin{cases}
\ast,&n=3,4,\\
S^2,&n=5,\\
S^1,&n\ge6.
\end{cases}
\]
For every \(n\ge6\), \(X_n\) admits **exactly \(6n\) explicitly prescribed elementary free-face collapses** onto an embedded triangulation \(T_n\) of the Möbius band. Its unique boundary component is the original Hamiltonian \(2n\)-cycle \(R_n\). In integral first homology, the image of \([R_n]\) is **twice a primitive generator** of \(H_1(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\). More precisely, the inclusion from scale one is onto, with kernel freely generated by the boundaries of the \(n\) squares of the Möbius ladder:
\[
H_1(M_{2n};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{n+1}\longrightarrow H_1(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,
\qquad\ker\cong\mathbb Z^n.
\]
The change at \(n=5\) is sharp. There, \(X_5\) has a 36-pair simplicial collapse to an explicitly certified ten-vertex triangulated two-sphere.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is simple and unweighted; only its \(2n\) vertices, **not edge interiors**, are points of the metric space. Distance two is inclusive. All indices in formulas for \(n\ge6\) are reduced modulo \(N=2n\), and sets of vertices are interpreted as unordered simplices. The ordinary graph is the scale-one complex, since Möbius ladders are triangle-free for \(n\ge3\). Homotopy equivalences refer to geometric realizations; the stronger collapse assertions concern the actual finite abstract complexes.

## Proof
**1. Full classification of facets for \(n\ge6\).** Distance at most two in the graph means a difference in the residue classes
\[
\{\pm1,\pm2,n-1,n,n+1\}\subset\mathbb Z/(2n).
\]
The graph-square neighborhood of a vertex therefore has exactly seven elements. Direct comparison of the differences among those seven residues shows that all maximal cliques have exactly four vertices, and consist precisely of the \(3n\) tetrahedra
\[
Q_i=\{i,i+1,i+n,i+n+1\}\quad(0\le i<n),
\]
\[
P_i=\{i,i+1,i+2,i+n+1\}\quad(0\le i<2n).
\]
This comparison also excludes five-cliques: among the seven neighbors of any vertex there is no four-clique. The modular distinction is valid at the boundary \(n=6\), as well as above it. Counting all distinct subfaces yields the full face vector \((2n,7n,8n,3n)\), in dimensions zero through three.

**2. Four uniform free-face phases.** The following four phases contain \(2n,n,2n,n\) elementary pairs, respectively. Within a phase, use increasing \(i\); complete a phase before starting the next one. An arrow \(B\rightsquigarrow A\) means removal of the free proper face \(A\) and its unique coface \(B\), where \(A\) has one fewer vertex.

- For every \(0\le i<2n\), delete \(P_i\rightsquigarrow\{i,i+1,i+2\}\). A triple of consecutive rim vertices lies in just this tetrahedron.
- For every \(0\le i<n\), delete \(Q_i\rightsquigarrow\{i,i+1,i+n\}\). Any competing tetrahedron was removed in the first phase.
- For every \(0\le i<2n\), delete \(\{i,i+2,i+n+1\}\rightsquigarrow\{i,i+2\}\). This length-two rim diagonal occurs in just that remaining triangle.
- For every \(0\le i<n\), delete \(\{i+n,i+n+1,i+1\}\rightsquigarrow\{i+n,i+1\}\). This cross-rim diagonal occurs in just that remaining triangle.

Each of these is a codimension-one **elementary simplicial collapse**, not only an acyclic matching. The uniqueness claims follow by exhausting the \(Q\)- and \(P\)-type tetrahedron subfaces from step 1. Explicitly, after the first two phases, the surviving triangles split into three types: the \(n\) triangles \(\{i+n,i+n+1,i\}\), the \(2n\) triangles \(\{i,i+2,i+n+1\}\), and the \(2n\) triangles \(\{i,i+1,i+n+1\}\); the last phase removes exactly the \(n\) triangles of the third type with \(n\le i<2n\). No other faces are left outside the downward closure of
\[
\mathcal T=\left\{\{i,i+1,i+n+1\},\{i,i+n,i+n+1\}:0\le i<n\right\}.
\]
Thus \(X_n\searrow T_n=\langle\mathcal T\rangle\). Its face vector is \((2n,4n,2n)\). Every rim edge occurs in one triangle, every other edge in two. Every vertex link is a path, and these \(2n\) rim edges form a single boundary cycle. The surface is connected and has Euler characteristic zero. A compact connected surface with one boundary component and Euler characteristic zero is a Möbius band. Equivalently, the \(n\) quadrilaterals \(Q_i\), each triangulated by its chosen diagonal, form a strip whose end identification reverses the two rows. This proves the stable homotopy type and the stronger simple-homotopy collapse.

**3. The integral doubling and the scale-one map.** The original ladder is contained in \(T_n\); its \(3n\) edges consist of the \(2n\) rim edges and \(n\) rungs. Adding the \(n\) two-cells corresponding to the squares gives the Möbius band, so the first-homology map is surjective and has kernel generated by their \(n\) boundary cycles. Since the quotient is \(\mathbb Z\) and the domain has rank \(n+1\), these \(n\) boundaries form a free basis of the kernel.

For an explicit primitive generator, select the spanning tree comprising all \(n\) rungs \(\{i,i+n\}\), together with the \(n-1\) rim edges \(\{i,i+1\}\) for \(0\le i<n-1\). In the quotient by this tree, there remain \(n+1\) loops: the edge from \(n-1\) to \(n\), plus the \(n\) rim edges on the opposite side. The first \(n-1\) square relations kill the opposite-side loops corresponding to the tree-side edges; the final square relation identifies the two remaining loops with equal sign. Hence \(H_1(T_n;\mathbb Z)\) is generated freely by the loop \(\gamma\) obtained by the rim path \(0\to1\to\cdots\to n\) followed by the rung \(n\to0\). The full Hamiltonian rim \(R_n\) traverses the two identified surviving loops with the same sign, so \([R_n]=2[\gamma]\). This is the claimed index-two winding of the visible boundary, despite the abstract group being torsion-free.

**4. Sharp exceptional cases.** At \(n=3,4\), the graph has diameter two, so \(X_n\) is the full simplex. At \(n=5\) the full face vector is \((10,35,50,25,2)\). An exact 36-pair free-face reduction leaves the simplicial complex generated by the sixteen triangles, writing \(abc\) for \(\{a,b,c\}\):

`026, 028, 046, 048, 126, 127, 137, 139, 156, 159, 237, 238, 348, 349, 456, 459`.

The deterministic deletion rule is: search dimensions downward, tetrahedra/other cofaces in reverse lexicographic order, and codimension-one faces in forward lexicographic order; remove the first free pair, then repeat. The included standalone checker reconstructs all 122 initial nonempty faces and verifies each deletion, including freedom against **all** cofaces. The residual has 10 vertices, 24 edges and 16 triangles; every edge belongs to exactly two triangles, all vertex links are connected cycles, and the space is connected. It is therefore a connected closed surface with Euler characteristic two, hence \(S^2\). This finite certificate completes the exceptional proof without extrapolation from numerical Betti numbers.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_mobius_rips.py` using Python 3 and the standard library only. It independently generates graph distances by breadth-first search, enumerates every clique without using the claimed facet description, and checks the predicted facets and each free-face pair. The exceptional ten-vertex sphere is tested by its edge incidences, vertex links, connectedness and Euler characteristic. It checks the stable construction for every integer \(6\le n\le30\), including the minimum permitted value. The parameter-independent proof is the exhaustive seven-neighbor facet classification and the four local free-face arguments above; the finite verifier is corroboration and a reproducibility tool, not a substitute for that proof. The verifier does not assess radii above two.

## Relationship to prior work
Adamaszek's *Clique complexes and graph powers* (2011 arXiv original) supplies the graph-power framework and classifies powers of a **single cycle**. It explicitly exhibits a Möbius strip for the squared seven-cycle; that smaller example is acknowledged, not claimed as new. Its cycle-power calculation does not cover the cubic Möbius ladder family (which has extra antipodal rungs), and its general high-girth collapse is inapplicable to their four-cycles. The 2026 Agarwal–Basak results concern **independence and perfect matching complexes** of Möbius and circular ladders, not the clique complexes of their squared graphs. The related published finite-vertex Rips result for **untwisted circular ladders** collapses to one rim and has a primitive rim class; no rim in the present twisted strip is primitive, and its quotient is not a functorial consequence of the untwisted result. A published twisted-square-grid Rips collapse treats closed grids of both widths at least seven and produces a Klein bottle, not this width-one strip or its boundary-doubling law. These are comparisons of objects and implications, not of names alone.

## Limitations
The classification concerns unweighted graph **vertices** and the inclusive radius-two scale. It does not classify other thresholds, the continuous geometric graph, weighted rungs, or arbitrary twisted strips. Although searched literature and a related graph-power paper were inspected in full, equivalent results may occur in unindexed or inaccessible work. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
