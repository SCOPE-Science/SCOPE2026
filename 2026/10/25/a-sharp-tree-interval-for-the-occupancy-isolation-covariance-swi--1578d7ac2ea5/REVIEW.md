No finite enumeration is used to infer the all-order theorem.

The fully accessible Diskin–Krivelevich (2023) paper addresses large-degree pseudo-random graphs and giant components. Related published graph-covariance findings concern different random variables, especially bond rather than site percolation; an accessible earlier isolation-variance study gives the degree-based mean but not the unique-switch theorem. Searches by equivalent formulations, broader percolation language and degree-polynomial terminology found no dominating exact statement. Earlier unindexed combinatorial probability work remains a genuine residual risk; a failed search is not novelty proof.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The one-switch theorem gives an all-size, tree-topology-independent range for an interpretable ecological richness–fragmentation relationship, and shows that the interval cannot be uniformly narrowed. The explicit connected cyclic example demonstrates a genuine graph-class boundary rather than a numerical or isolated special case. Unlike a routine evaluation of a degree polynomial at a given parameter, the result proves global uniqueness via tree degree constraints for every size. It is a theoretical structural guarantee, not a claim of field efficacy.
