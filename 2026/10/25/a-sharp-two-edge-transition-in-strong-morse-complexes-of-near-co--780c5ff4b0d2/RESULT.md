# A sharp two-edge transition in strong-Morse complexes of near-complete graphs

## Finding
Let \(n\ge4\), and form the **one-dimensional** simplicial graph \(G_n\) by deleting precisely the two incident edges \(\{0,1\}\) and \(\{0,2\}\) from the complete graph on \(\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\). Write \(U=\{3,\ldots,n-1\}\), \(k=n-3\), and \(X_n=\mathcal{SM}(G_n)\) for the full strong-discrete-Morse matching complex generated by vertex filtrations (not the ordinary Morse-matching complex and not the pure subcomplex). Then \(X_n\) is one-dimensional, with one nontrivial connected component and \(k(k-1)\) isolated vertices. The nontrivial component is homotopy equivalent to \(\bigvee^{2k-1}S^1\); equivalently,
\[
 X_n\simeq \left(\bigvee^{2n-7}S^1\right)\;\sqcup\;\bigsqcup^{(n-3)(n-4)}\{\mathrm{pt}\}.
\]
In particular, \(\widetilde H_0(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{k(k-1)}\) and \(H_1(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{2k-1}\), with no other nonzero reduced integral homology.

There is a more precise integral equivariant statement. The natural group \(\Gamma=S_U\times\langle\tau\rangle\), where \(\tau\) exchanges \(1\) and \(2\), acts on \(X_n\). Let \(\mathbb Z[U]\) be the permutation lattice and \(I_U=\ker(\mathbb Z[U]\xrightarrow{\mathrm{sum}}\mathbb Z)\). Let \(\varepsilon\) be the sign lattice for \(\langle\tau\rangle\), trivial on \(S_U\). Then
\[
 H_1(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\varepsilon\otimes\bigl(\mathbb Z[U]\oplus I_U\bigr),\qquad
 \widetilde H_0(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z[U^{(2)}],
\]
where \(U^{(2)}=\{(u,v)\in U^2:u\ne v\}\); \(\tau\) acts trivially on the second lattice. Both isomorphisms are integral, not just rational character equalities.

## Assumptions and scope
Treat \(G_n\) as a simplicial complex of dimension one, with graph edges but without filled triangles. For an injective vertex filtration, an arrow \(v\to w\) denotes the matching pair consisting of vertex \(v\) and graph edge \(\{v,w\}\), present precisely when \(w\) is the **unique earlier neighbor** of \(v\). The simplices of \(\mathcal{SM}(G_n)\) are the subsets of all such filtration-generated matching sets. A lower link in a one-dimensional complex is a discrete set; it is a cone precisely if it is a singleton. All claims hold for every integer \(n\ge4\), without an upper bound.

## Proof
Each vertex has at most two nonneighbors. At the fifth and subsequent positions of a vertex order, at least two earlier vertices are adjacent to the new vertex, so no new strong pair can be formed. Three pairs cannot occur: to match vertices at positions two and three, the first two vertices must be adjacent and the third must be nonadjacent to exactly one of them. The only missing pairs are \(\{0,1\}\) and \(\{0,2\}\), and the only viable first-three set is \(\{0,i,u\}\), with \(i\in\{1,2\}\) and \(u\in U\). The fourth vertex, if it is the other leaf \(3-i\), is adjacent to both \(i\) and \(u\); if it is in \(U\), it is adjacent to all three. Thus it cannot be matched. Every face consequently has at most two vertices.

Every oriented graph edge occurs as a vertex of \(X_n\), by choosing its target before its source. Write \(p=(1\to2)\), \(q=(2\to1)\), and, for each \(u\in U\),
\[
 a_u=(0\to u),\ b_u=(1\to u),\ c_u=(2\to u),\ d_u=(u\to0),\ e_u=(u\to1),\ f_u=(u\to2).
\]
A complete case distinction among orders of the first four original graph vertices gives **exactly** the following eight one-simplices for each \(u\):
\[
\{a_u,e_u\},\ \{a_u,f_u\},\ \{a_u,p\},\ \{a_u,q\},\ \{a_u,b_u\},\ \{a_u,c_u\},\ \{b_u,d_u\},\ \{c_u,d_u\}.
\]
To make exhaustiveness explicit, if the matched positions are two and three, the first three graph vertices must be \(0,i,u\) in an order whose first two are adjacent: this produces exactly the six edge types above not involving \(p,q\). If the matched positions are two and four, position four must be \(0\) (the only vertex with two nonneighbors), and the first three must be \(1,2,u\); the two remaining edge types are \(\{a_u,p\}\) and \(\{a_u,q\}\), while the other possible first-two orders reproduce previous types. There is no matching at positions three and four without a matching at position two: the first pair must then be the missing edge \(\{0,i\}\), and the fourth vertex cannot have exactly one earlier neighbor. Each displayed edge is realized by an appropriate four-vertex prefix; every prefix can be extended to a complete filtration without altering the earlier pairs.

The remaining vertices are exactly the ordered arrows \(u\to v\) for distinct \(u,v\in U\). They are isolated, because after a filtration produces a pair between two universal vertices, all subsequent vertices have at least two previously seen neighbors. There are \(k(k-1)\) of them.

The nonisolated graph has vertices \(p,q\) and \(a_u,b_u,c_u,d_u,e_u,f_u\) for each \(u\). Its only edges are those displayed. The vertices \(e_u,f_u\) are leaves and can be removed by deformation retraction. The remainder consists of \(k\) four-cycles \(a_u-b_u-d_u-c_u-a_u\), each attached at \(a_u\) to one of \(k\) length-two paths \(p-a_u-q\) between common endpoints \(p,q\). This graph is connected, has \(6k+2\) vertices and \(8k\) edges before removal of leaves, hence cycle rank \(8k-(6k+2)+1=2k-1\). Every connected graph is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of its cycle-rank-many circles.

The square loops form a \(\mathbb Z[U]\)-lattice. Oriented loops \(p-a_u-q-a_v-p\) correspond to differences of paths and form the augmentation lattice \(I_U\). These two groups have disjoint edge-coordinate support, so their direct-sum decomposition in the integral first-cycle lattice is canonical. Relabeling \(U\) permutes the square loops and the paths. Swapping \(1\) and \(2\) reverses the orientation of every square and of each \(p\)-to-\(q\) path-difference loop. This proves the asserted \(\Gamma\)-module decomposition of \(H_1\). The isolated components are indexed by ordered distinct pairs in \(U\), while the core gives a distinguished fixed component; the reduced component lattice is therefore \(\mathbb Z[U^{(2)}]\), proving the \(\widetilde H_0\) statement.

## Verification
The supplied standalone script `verify.py` enumerates all injective order prefixes of length four for \(4\le n\le11\), constructs the corresponding full strong matching pairs, and compares **every** vertex and one-simplex with the eight-edge description. It checks the absence of higher-dimensional faces, the exact numbers of components, cycle ranks, and equivariant Lefschetz characters for explicit vertex permutations and both states of the leaf swap. For \(4\le n\le8\), it independently enumerates every full vertex order as an additional check. The recorded run ends with `VERIFY_OK`. These finite computations corroborate the universal proof but are not used as a substitute for it.

## Relationship to prior work
Knudson and Owens-White introduced \(\mathcal{SM}\) in September 2026, proved that the complete-graph complex \(\mathcal{SM}(K_n)\) is zero-dimensional (Proposition 10.2), and identified the four-vertex triangle-with-a-leaf case as a single circle (Example 10.5). The latter is exactly the \(n=4\) base case here, **not** a newly discovered case. The new theorem determines all larger universal-vertex extensions, their isolated-component transition, and their full integral branch-permutation and involution modules. The one-edge-deletion graph \(K_n\setminus\{0,1\}\) has a forest strong-Morse complex, by the same unique-earlier-neighbor analysis: it consists of a four-vertex path for each unaffected vertex and isolated arrows between unaffected vertices. Thus deleting two incident edges is the smallest number of deletions from a clique that can create nonzero first homology in this setting. The ordinary Morse-matching complex, perfect matching complexes, and complete-multipartite families studied elsewhere are different objects or graphs; the complement of \(G_n\) is a three-vertex path plus isolates, not a disjoint union of cliques.

## Limitations
The statement concerns only strong matchings induced by vertex filtrations of the one-dimensional graph; it does not apply to arbitrary acyclic matchings, clique-filled complexes, or arbitrary edge deletions. The complete homotopy classification is nonequivariant; the equivariant conclusion explicitly concerns integral homology, not an equivariant homotopy equivalence. The general claim has a short case-based proof rather than independent external validation. An unindexed equivalent calculation for these near-cliques remains a bibliographic risk.
