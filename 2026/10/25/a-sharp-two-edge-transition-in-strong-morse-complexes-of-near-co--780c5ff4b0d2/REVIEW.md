# Review of the near-clique strong-Morse theorem

## Correctness
PASS. For a graph regarded as a one-dimensional simplicial complex, the filtration-generated strong match at a vertex exists exactly with one earlier neighbor. Two deleted incident edges force all nonempty matchings to occur in the first four positions and prevent three matches. A complete enumeration of the possible two-match positions gives eight graph edges per unaffected vertex. The component decomposition, Euler cycle rank, and integral lattice isomorphisms follow by explicit graph and cycle bases. Standalone exhaustive order checks through eleven vertices support the combinatorial classification. The result does not rely on computations as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS under available literature and records. The most relevant September 2026 full text proves the complete-graph complex is discrete and its four-vertex triangle-with-leaf instance has type \(S^1\), but does not give the infinite universal-vertex-extension family or its integral symmetry modules. Existing results for complete multipartite graphs do not imply this claim because its complement is a path of length two rather than a union of cliques. published-finding corpus searches for the exact graph, equivalent complement description, homotopy and symmetry formulas returned no dominating theorem. Indexed omissions remain possible.

## Value
PASS. The theorem develops the isolated four-vertex example into an infinite, sharply quantified edge-deletion transition: one deleted clique edge produces no first-homology cycles, whereas two incident deletions produce an increasing family of circles and quadratic isolated-component growth. Its integral equivariant decomposition distinguishes symmetry behavior hidden by homology ranks, with an explicit graph model useful for further sensitivity and filtration comparisons. The case is a natural minimal perturbation, not an arbitrary finite graph computation.

## Closest literature and limitations
Knudson–Owens-White, arXiv:2609.06844v1, Proposition 10.2 and Example 10.5 (known complete-graph and four-vertex results); Fernández, arXiv:2504.15729v1 (foundational definitions). Only full filtration-generated strong matchings on unfilled graphs are covered. General arbitrary Morse matchings and equivariant homotopy classification are not claimed.
