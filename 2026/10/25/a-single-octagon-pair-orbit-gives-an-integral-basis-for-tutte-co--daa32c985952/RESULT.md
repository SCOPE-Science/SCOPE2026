# A single octagon-pair orbit gives an integral basis for Tutte–Coxeter two-particle homology

## Finding
Let \(T\) be the Tutte–Coxeter eight-cage in its duad–syntheme realization and set \(X=\operatorname{UConf}_2(|T|)\). Its degree-two integral homology is
\[
H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{90}.
\]
There are 90 simple eight-cycles and 1,125 unordered vertex-disjoint pairs of eight-cycles. Each pair defines an embedded product torus in \(X\). The natural six-letter permutation group \(S_6\) partitions the pairs into four orbits of sizes \(45,360,360,360\). In the unique 360-pair orbit for which exactly two edges run between the two octagons, with their attachment vertices separated by three cycle edges on **each** octagon, the torus classes generate \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\) **integrally**. Indeed, 90 tori from that orbit form an integral basis. Each of the three other individual orbits fails to generate this integral homology group.

## Assumptions and scope
Use six symbols \(\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\). The vertices of \(T\) are its fifteen **duads** (unordered two-symbol subsets) and its fifteen **synthemes** (partitions of the six symbols into three duads); a duad is adjacent to a syntheme exactly when it belongs to that partition. This fixes an unambiguous connected, cubic, bipartite, thirty-vertex graph with forty-five edges and girth eight. It is nonplanar: a planar graph with girth at least eight and thirty vertices has at most \(4(30-2)/3<38\) edges, while this graph has 45. Permuting the six symbols induces a faithful \(S_6\)-action.

The configuration space is unordered and collision-free; graph edges are treated as geometric intervals. The cellular calculation uses the finite deleted-product model, whose quotient by exchange of the two particles strongly deformation retracts \(X\). Two eight-cycles are **disjoint** when their vertex sets are disjoint. The bridge profile of such a pair is the number of graph edges between its two vertex sets and, when that number is two, the unordered pair of their attachment-point separations measured along the two octagons. Distance three is cyclic graph distance along an octagon, not an ambient shortest-path distance in \(T\). Only this graph, two particles, and integral second homology are classified here.

## Proof
**Cellular model and signs.** Order the thirty vertices, then graph edges lexicographically, orient every edge from its smaller to its larger endpoint, and orient a two-cell by the ordered pair of its two disjoint edge indices. The unordered deleted-product complex has 435 zero-cells \(\{u,v\}\), 1,260 one-cells \((e,v)\) with \(v\notin e\), and 900 two-cells \((e_i,e_j)\) for \(i<j\) and disjoint \(e_i,e_j\). If \(e_i=[a,b]\) and \(e_j=[c,d]\) have increasing endpoints, then its cellular differential is
\[
\partial_2[e_i,e_j]=[e_j,b]-[e_j,a]-[e_i,d]+[e_i,c].
\]
These incidences satisfy \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\); the verifier checks every square. The two-dimensional complex has no three-cells, so \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)=\ker\partial_2\subseteq\mathbb Z^{900}\), a primitive sublattice.

**Natural geometric cycles.** Enumerate all simple eight-cycles by requiring their least numbered vertex first and choosing the lexicographically smaller of the two orientations. Exhaustive bounded-depth adjacency search finds 90; there are no shorter cycles. Enumerating pairs gives exactly 1,125 disjoint pairs. Each oriented pair \((C,D)\) supplies a signed cellular two-cycle \(z(C,D)\): take the cross-product of the signed edge sums of \(C\) and \(D\), and insert a minus sign when the two edge indices must be swapped to put the resulting square in canonical increasing-index order. Each such cycle has 64 nonzero square coefficients. Direct substitution in the displayed boundary verifies \(\partial_2 z(C,D)=0\).

**The distinguished orbit.** The adjacent swap \((0\,1)\) and the six-cycle \((0\,1\,2\,3\,4\,5)\) generate the faithful six-letter action. Breadth-first orbit enumeration on the 1,125 pairs gives sizes \(45,360,360,360\). The 45-pair orbit has no bridge edges; the remaining three have two bridge edges. Their two cyclic attachment-distance profiles are \((2,4),(2,4),(3,3)\). The final profile defines precisely one orbit of 360 tori. Direct binary linear elimination on their 900 square-coordinate vectors has rank 90. Select, in ascending pair-index order, the first 90 vectors that increase binary rank; select, in ascending square-index order, the first 90 square-coordinate rows that increase the rank of the corresponding transposed incidence vectors. With the signed integral coefficients, the resulting \(90\times90\) matrix has determinant **\(-1\)**. All cycle and pair orderings, signs, and these exact selections are implemented without external graph data in `verify_tutte_coxeter.py`.

**Integral saturation and completeness.** The unit determinant shows that the 90 selected product-torus cycles span a rank-90 lattice \(L\) primitive in \(\mathbb Z^{900}\). Independently, exact sparse elimination over \(\mathbb F_{101}\) gives \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_{101}}\partial_2=810\), hence \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\partial_2\ge810\). Since \(L\subseteq\ker\partial_2\) has rank 90, the opposite inequality holds. Thus \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z}\ker\partial_2=90\). The inclusion \(L\subseteq\ker\partial_2\subseteq\mathbb Z^{900}\) is then an equality: \(\ker\partial_2/L\) would be a finite subgroup of the torsion-free group \(\mathbb Z^{900}/L\). This proves the integral basis assertion, not only rational or modular spanning.

Finally, reducing the four individual torus-orbit families modulo two gives ranks \(35,89,89,90\), in the order above. A family whose reduction has rank below 90 cannot generate the rank-90 integral lattice; hence each of the three other individual orbits fails. The two different \((2,4)\)-profile orbits are kept separate by the exact permutation action, not conflated by their identical bridge profile.

## Verification
Run `python verify_tutte_coxeter.py` with Python 3 and SymPy 1.14.0. The standalone program constructs all vertices and edges directly from six-symbol set partitions, proves the graph's girth bound by finite exhaustive search, enumerates all octagons and their disjoint pairs, constructs every signed two-cell boundary and torus cycle, checks \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\), calculates the rank over \(\mathbb F_{101}\), and checks both signed unimodular determinant certificates. It also constructs all 720 six-letter permutations and verifies the orbit profiles and modular orbit ranks. Its final line is `VERIFY_OK_DISTINGUISHED_S6_ORBIT_INTEGRAL_H2`. The deterministic 90 torus indices and 90 square-coordinate indices are printed with the proof certificate. All indexing starts at zero.

## Relationship to prior work
Barnett and Farber's 2009 paper develops the equivariant deleted-product model and intersection theory for two particles on a graph; it supplies the topological model used here, but not this octagon-orbit basis. Farber and Hanbury's 2010 paper studies mature graphs and second Betti numbers, not the present fine integral, shortest-cycle, and orbit-selective generating statement. An and Knudsen establish geometric second-homology generation results for planar graph braid groups and exhibit nonplanar limitations; their theorem does not imply this nonplanar eight-cage's exceptional generating orbit. Related calculations of independence and graph-metric complexes for this same graph involve different spaces. The present theorem identifies integral geometric generators for a particular canonical nonplanar graph; an exhaustive literature-priority claim is not made.

## Limitations
The computation classifies \(H_2\), not the full fundamental group, higher-particle configuration spaces, or all cubic girth-eight graphs. The action used is the natural label-permutation subgroup \(S_6\); no claim about the full graph-automorphism representation is made. The two nondistinguished 360-element orbits have insufficient modular rank for **integral** generation; this alone does not determine their rational ranks or complete Smith quotients. Finite exact calculations prove this finite graph's result, not a uniform theorem for all members of a graph family. Equivalent independent calculations may exist outside the searched literature.
