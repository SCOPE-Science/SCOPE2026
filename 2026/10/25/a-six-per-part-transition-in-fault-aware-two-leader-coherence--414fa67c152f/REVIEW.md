# Scientific review of the defective-biclique two-leader theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The claim fixes a connected graph \(K_{n,n}\) with one missing edge, exactly two noise-free leaders, and a grounded-Laplacian inverse trace. Every pair belongs to one of five symmetry classes. The proof decomposes the follower subspace into within-class contrasts and a quotient of dimension at most four, derives five exact rational objectives, and proves three of them strictly exceed the missing-endpoint objective. The remaining polynomial comparison \(f(n)=n^3-6n^2+5n-1\) has \(f(5)=-1\), \(f(6)=29\), and strictly positive first difference for integers \(n\ge4\). This proves all minimizing classes and multiplicities without extrapolating from experiments. The supplied SymPy program separately reconstructs the quotient identities and performs 245 direct rational inverse comparisons. The \(n=3\) zero-sized group boundary was checked independently.

## Originality — PASS, with limits
Fitch and Leonard, arXiv:1407.1569, provide the general joint-centrality approach to choosing noise-free leaders, and explicitly solve cycles and paths, not a single-edge-defective balanced biclique. Their earlier 2013 conference preprint also gives a general joint-centrality description but no such classification. Patterson, McGlohon and Dyagilev, arXiv:1412.6595, treat paths and rings via a shortest-path reduction. Exact published-result searches for defective bicliques, grounded-Laplacian inverse traces, and alternative leader-selection formulations found no explicit integer-six switch. Closest indexed work on complete-bipartite graphs concerns different objectives, such as discounted hitting domination and geodesic subpath counts following matching deletions; neither implies the reported coherence optimum. The general leader-selection criterion supplies a method to evaluate any specific graph, but the five-orbit exact reduction, sign proof and sharp optimizer transition are not among the inspected stated results. A covering result outside the examined sources remains possible; this is not an assertion of exhaustive historical priority.

## Value — PASS
Removing a single link from a dense two-community network creates a fault-aware leader-placement dilemma: pin the endpoints of the failed communication link, or pin two uncompromised agents in one community. The complete answer changes exactly between five and six agents per community and differs from the intact biclique optimizer in the smaller networks. This is a motivated exact robustness benchmark over an unbounded family, not a freely chosen numerical instance; the \(1/720\) near-tie at five makes exact comparison rather than floating-point ranking particularly relevant. The theorem is deliberately limited to unit edges, balanced parts and two fixed leaders; it does not imply optimality under other noise models or weight patterns.
