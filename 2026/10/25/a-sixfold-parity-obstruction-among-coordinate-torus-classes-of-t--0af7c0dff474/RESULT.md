# A sixfold parity obstruction among coordinate-torus classes of the four-cube two-particle space

## Finding

Let \(Q_4\) be the graph with vertex set \(\{0,1\}^4\), whose edges join strings differing in one coordinate, and let \(X=\operatorname{UConf}_2(|Q_4|)\) be the ordinary **unordered, collision-free** configuration space of two points in its geometric realization. There are \(24\) coordinate square faces of the four-cube; \(132\) unordered pairs of those faces have disjoint vertex sets. Each pair defines an embedded two-torus in \(X\) through the product of its two cycles.

**Theorem.** The integral second homology group and the coordinate-face product tori satisfy
\[
H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{88},
\qquad\langle\text{all 132 coordinate-face tori}\rangle=H_2(X;\mathbb Z).
\]
Indeed, \(88\) of those tori form an integral basis. The full \(384\)-element graph-automorphism group divides the \(132\) tori into three orbits: two parallel-face orbits of sizes \(24\) and \(12\) (complementary fixed bits at Hamming distance one and two, respectively), and one nonparallel-face orbit of size \(96\) (the face directions share one free coordinate). Denote by \(L_{\mathrm{mix}}\) the subgroup generated by the \(96\) nonparallel-face tori. Then
\[
\boxed{H_2(X;\mathbb Z)/L_{\mathrm{mix}}\cong(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^6.}
\]
Thus those \(96\) tori rationally span second homology but have **integral index \(64\)**. Adjoining either complete parallel-face orbit alone suffices to generate all of \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\) integrally. The individual orbit ranks are, respectively, \(24\), \(12\), and \(88\); the two parallel orbits together have rank \(36\).

## Assumptions and scope

The graph is the one-dimensional one-skeleton of a four-dimensional cube, **not** its filled four-dimensional cubical body. The configuration space excludes collisions everywhere, including edge interiors, and is quotiented by swapping two particles. Homology is ordinary singular homology over \(\mathbb Z\), identified with cellular homology through the deleted-product cubical model; no orientability or planarity hypothesis is imposed. A product torus uses two full four-edge square loops with disjoint vertex sets; orientation signs do not affect the generated sublattice. All statements concern this one graph, not arbitrary hypercube dimensions or all graph-cycle product tori.

## Proof

**1. Finite cellular model.** By the standard two-particle graph-configuration deformation-retraction theorem of Barnett and Farber [1], the unordered configuration space deformation retracts to the quotient deleted-product cube complex. Its zero-cells are unordered distinct vertex pairs; one-cells are pairs \((v,e)\) where vertex \(v\) is outside the closed edge \(e\); and two-cells are unordered pairs \(\{e,f\}\) of edges with disjoint endpoint sets. There are respectively \(120\), \(448\), and \(400\) cells. Order the graph vertices by their integer binary labels; orient edges by increasing label and two-cells by the lexicographic order of their two edges. With \(e=(e_0,e_1)\) and \(f=(f_0,f_1)\) in increasing endpoint order, the signed second boundary is
\[
\partial_2[e,f]=[e_1,f]-[e_0,f]-[f_1,e]+[f_0,e].
\]
The first boundary of \([v,e]\) is \([v,e_1]-[v,e_0]\). The construction in `verify.py` checks \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\) over integers.

**2. Square torus chains.** For each of the \(132\) vertex-disjoint pairs of coordinate square faces \(A,B\), take the cross product of their four-edge fundamental cycles and map it into the unordered complex. Its signed \(16\)-square cellular chain is \(t_{A,B}\); the verifier constructs its edge-direction and ordering signs explicitly. Let \(D\) be the \(448\)-by-\(400\) second boundary and \(T\) the \(400\)-by-\(132\) integer matrix of these columns. Exact multiplication gives \(DT=0\). Exact Gaussian elimination modulo \(101\) gives \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_{101}}D=312\). Exact elimination modulo \(2\) gives \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}T=88\). Because \(\operatorname{im}T\subseteq\ker D\subseteq\mathbb Z^{400}\), these two modular bounds force \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}D=312\), \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}T=88\), and \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z}\ker D=88\). There are no three-dimensional cells.

**3. Integral saturation and completeness.** Deterministic modular pivot selection yields \(88\) columns of \(T\) and \(88\) two-cell coordinate rows whose square integer submatrix \(B\) has **determinant \(+1\)**. Hence those \(88\) torus chains generate a saturated rank-\(88\) subgroup of \(\mathbb Z^{400}\). Since \(\ker D\) has the same rank and is saturated, it equals this subgroup. Thus all of \(H_2\) is freely generated by the selected geometric tori. This is an exact integer determinant certificate, not an inference from floating-point or finite-field rank alone.

**4. Symmetry and index.** Any cube-graph automorphism is determined by the image of vertex zero and the permutation of its four neighbors; binary translations and coordinate permutations realize all \(16\cdot24=384\) possibilities. Enumerating their action on the \(132\) face pairs gives orbits of sizes \(24\), \(12\), and \(96\), characterized in the statement. Write the remaining torus columns in the established **unimodular** integral basis, using \(B^{-1}\) applied to the same \(88\) pivot rows. The exact Smith normal form of the resulting \(88\)-by-\(96\) nonparallel-orbit coordinate matrix has nonzero diagonal invariants
\[
\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{82\text{ times}},
\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{6\text{ times}}.
\]
Therefore its quotient lattice is precisely \((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^6\). The corresponding matrices obtained by adjoining either the \(24\)-torus parallel orbit or the \(12\)-torus parallel orbit each have \(88\) unit Smith invariants. Each augmented orbit collection therefore generates the full lattice integrally.

## Relationship to prior work

Barnett--Farber [1] provide the graph configuration deformation retract and a cycle-intersection approach, with explicit planar results. Farber--Hanbury [2] study edge additions and mature graphs. Barnett's dissertation [3] discusses cycle-product generators and distinguishes nonplanar phenomena. An--Knudsen [4] prove a planar second-homology generation theorem and give a nonplanar obstruction example. The present computation identifies both *complete* integral generation by elementary coordinate-face tori and a strict **index-64** sublattice generated by the natural nonparallel orbit for a specific nonplanar graph. These conclusions do not follow from the named general planar theorems; the relevant investigated texts do not state this exact four-cube lattice classification.

## Limitations

The result is a finite, exact graph-specific classification. It does not assert a uniform theorem for higher-dimensional hypercubes, a presentation for the complete graph braid group, or a description of all possible product-torus orbit filtrations. Its integer Smith and minor certificates are supplied as reproducible code rather than independently checked machine-formal proofs. Although relevant texts and record searches were compared by scientific implications, an unindexed equivalent treatment is a residual bibliographic possibility. No independent external audit has been performed.
