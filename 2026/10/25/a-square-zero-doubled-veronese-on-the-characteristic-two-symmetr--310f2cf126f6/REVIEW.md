# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The intrinsic Jacobian ideal has exactly three principal-minor quadrics because the off-diagonal derivatives vanish in characteristic \(2\), and the determinant lies in their ideal. On the three standard affine charts, the scheme is exactly the dual-number thickening of an affine plane. Transition functions give the square-zero nilradical \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(-3)\). The height-three quadratic regular sequence gives degree eight, Hilbert series and Hilbert polynomial. The integral cubic is normal by the codimension of its reduced singular support. The packaged exact-arithmetic checker reproduces derivatives, Buchberger reductions, nilpotent products and low-degree Hilbert coefficients.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The closest antecedents are Grenet–Monteil–Thomassé (arXiv:1210.5879, 2012) for symmetric determinants in characteristic \(2\) and Piontkowski (Michigan Math. J. 54, 2006) for complex symmetric determinantal singularities. The former develops representations and its determinant-cancellation identity, while the latter works over \(\mathbb C\). Neither inspected material gives this exact characteristic-two Jacobian doubling, its nilpotent line bundle, or the stated Hilbert series. Searches included singular schemes, double Veronese surfaces, principal minors and characteristic-two matrix determinant variants. The known reduced rank-one Veronese and the determinant expansion are expressly prior. An unlocated equivalent historical modular or determinantal treatment remains possible, so this is an evidence-based self-review, not a priority certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Scheme-theoretic singularities of determinantal hypersurfaces are natural invariants; here the intrinsic scheme is a nonreduced doubled Veronese rather than the familiar reduced rank-one surface. Its square-zero line bundle and Hilbert series identify the precise infinitesimal discrepancy, which matters in calculations involving Jacobian and singularity schemes. The comparison is a natural modular boundary, not a relabeling or arbitrarily chosen parameter instance.
