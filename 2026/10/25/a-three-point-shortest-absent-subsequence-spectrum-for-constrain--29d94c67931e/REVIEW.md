## Correctness
PASS. The two explicit comparison words \((01)^r\) and \((01)^r0\) establish absence restrictions and universality. A complete partition of marked cuts into second-zero, first-zero, and one cuts yields respectively \(D\), \(D+S\), and \(D+S\) positions. For the first-zero case the only potentially extra absence is determined by the last internal zero gap; the nonadjacent-short-gap condition forces every earlier candidate to occur. Reversal transfers the count to cuts at ones. The finite checker verified 119 legal cyclic gap tuples and 2146 cuts for \(2\le r\le8\); the symbolic argument, not that enumeration, proves the arbitrary-length claim.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The exclusion of two consecutive short gaps is a natural local finite-type constraint on a circular run-length-limited word, yet forces an exact *global* rotation spectrum invariant under reordering allowed gaps. The law gives not just universality but precise shortest-absence counts, a refinement useful for assessing deletion-pattern ambiguity of constrained cyclic inputs. The forbidden boundary \((1,1,2)\) already produces a fourth value, showing that the phenomenon is genuinely tied to the combinatorial hypothesis rather than merely a normalization or parameter substitution.
