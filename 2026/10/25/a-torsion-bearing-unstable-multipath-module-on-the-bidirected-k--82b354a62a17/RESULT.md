# A torsion-bearing unstable multipath module on the bidirected \(K_{2,4}\)

## Finding

Let \(D_n=\overleftrightarrow{K}_{2,n}\) have two hubs \(a,b\), \(n\) leaves \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\), and arcs in both directions between every hub and leaf. Let \(X(D_n)\) be the simplicial complex whose faces are directed edge subsets forming disjoint unions of directed paths (isolated vertices allowed). For \(1\le n\le4\), the **nonzero reduced integral homology groups** are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde H_0(X(D_1);\mathbb Z)&\cong\mathbb Z,\\
\widetilde H_1(X(D_2);\mathbb Z)&\cong\mathbb Z^3, &\widetilde H_2(X(D_2);\mathbb Z)&\cong\mathbb Z^2,\\
\widetilde H_1(X(D_3);\mathbb Z)&\cong\mathbb Z, &\widetilde H_2(X(D_3);\mathbb Z)&\cong\mathbb Z^6,\\
\widetilde H_2(X(D_4);\mathbb Z)&\cong\mathbb Z^7\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\end{aligned}
\]

Every unspecified reduced homology group vanishes. In particular, the least torsion-bearing instance **within this four-term family** has four leaves. Moreover \(D_4\) is itself one *unstable dynamical module* in the sense of [1]; its multipath complex has nonzero torsion. This contradicts the conjecture stated in the introduction of [1] that a dynamical module with torsion-bearing multipath complex must be stable. The result asserts integral homology, not a complete homotopy type.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph has no loops and precisely \(4n\) arcs, ordered for reproducibility by increasing leaf, then hub, then outgoing and incoming orientation:

\[
(a,z_1),(z_1,a),(b,z_1),(z_1,b),\ldots,(a,z_n),(z_n,a),(b,z_n),(z_n,b).
\]

A face is a subset \(F\) of arcs such that each vertex has at most one selected outgoing and at most one selected incoming arc, and there is **no directed cycle** in \(F\). This is equivalent to the disjoint-directed-path definition, including single-edge paths. Every face uses at most four arcs because the two hubs support at most two incoming and two outgoing incidences in total. Homology uses integer coefficients and the augmented simplicial chain complex for reduced groups, with the boundary signs determined by the displayed arc order. Statements about the minimal torsion parameter quantify only over \(n=1,2,3,4\), not all digraphs and not all larger integers.

## Proof

**Enumeration.** Enumerate all \(k\)-subsets of the ordered \(4n\) arcs for \(0\le k\le4\). Admit a subset exactly when it meets the three tests above. These are necessary and sufficient because a finite directed graph with maximum indegree and outdegree one decomposes into directed cycles and directed paths. For \(n=4\), the number of faces of cardinality \(k=0,1,2,3,4\) is

\[
(1,16,80,144,72).
\]

For \(n=1,2,3\), the counts are respectively \((1,4,2,0,0)\), \((1,8,16,8,0)\), and \((1,12,42,48,12)\). The empty face is included in the augmented chain complex. Let \(C_{k-1}\) be freely generated by the \(k\)-arc faces and let \(\partial_{k-1}\) delete one arc with the alternating sign in increasing arc order.

**Integral chain reduction.** The attached `checker.py` enumerates every face and constructs the entire augmented integral boundary matrices. It repeatedly chooses an entry \(u=\pm1\) in the boundary from a \(k\)-arc face \(A\) to a \((k-1)\)-arc face \(B\), splits off the resulting integral contractible two-generator summand, and replaces every surviving entry by the exact Schur complement

\[
d'_{Y,X}=d_{Y,X}-d_{Y,A}d_{B,X}/u.
\]

This is a unimodular change of chain bases, so it preserves integral homology, **including torsion**. The routine updates neighboring boundary matrices and verifies \(\partial^2=0\) after each elimination. It chooses the first available unit in descending face-cardinality order, with deterministic sorted indices. The number of removed unit pairs by face cardinality is

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
n & k=4&k=3&k=2&k=1\\\hline
1&0&0&2&1\\
2&0&6&7&1\\
3&12&30&11&1\\
4&71&65&15&1
\end{array}
\]

The corresponding surviving (augmented) chain ranks indexed by face size \(k=0,1,2,3,4\) are \((0,1,0,0,0)\), \((0,0,3,2,0)\), \((0,0,1,6,0)\), and \((0,0,0,8,1)\). For the first three values of \(n\), all surviving differentials are zero. At \(n=4\), every surviving differential is zero *except* the map from the single surviving tetrahedral generator to eight triangular generators; relative to the certificate's surviving generator indices, it is

\[
\partial_3(e_{71})=2e_{138}-2e_{137}+2e_{141}.
\]

As the coefficient vector has greatest common divisor exactly \(2\), its Smith normal form is one nonzero diagonal entry \(2\). The kernel in degree three is zero, and the degree-two cokernel is \(\mathbb Z^7\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\). The previously listed homology descriptions follow by reading the surviving chain groups. Thus the torsion is **integral and genuine**, rather than a characteristic-two artefact.

**Unstable module.** Every arc of \(D_4\) belongs to a directed four-cycle of the form \(a\to z_i\to b\to z_j\to a\), or its role-reversed counterpart, with \(i\ne j\). These four-cycles connect all arcs into one equivalence class under the relation generated by coexistence on directed cycles: one can pass among the four orientation types by a four-cycle, and vary leaf indices by sharing arcs. Definition 4.2(b) of [1] says that any dynamical region containing an arc must contain every directed cycle of the ambient digraph using that arc. Consequently, every nonempty dynamical region contains every arc, and must equal \(D_4\). The whole digraph is itself a dynamical region: it is connected, has edges, and its boundary is empty, so the boundary condition is vacuous and no cycle escapes. Hence it is a *minimal* dynamical region, i.e. one dynamical module by Definition 4.4 of [1]. Each vertex of \(D_4\) has positive incoming and outgoing degree and is unstable, making this a strictly unstable module by Definition 4.2 of [1]. The preceding torsion calculation therefore disproves the quoted stability conjecture.

## Verification

Run `python3 checker.py` using only Python 3 standard-library modules. The program independently reconstructs every arc, face, and oriented simplicial boundary, asserts downward closure, verifies all consecutive boundary compositions vanish before and after every integer unit-pivot cancellation, and reports the residual integral boundary. It also performs independent Gaussian-elimination rank computations on the original matrices over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\). The resulting reduced Betti vectors in degrees zero through three for \(n=4\) are \((0,0,8,1)\) in characteristic two and \((0,0,7,0)\) in characteristic three. The integral Smith diagonals of the **original, unreduced** matrices were also independently calculated using exact integer Smith normal form: for cardinalities one through four they are respectively \((1)\), \((1^{15})\), \((1^{65})\), and \((1^{71},2)\). The integral conclusion does not rest on these field tests.

## Relationship to prior work

Caputi, Collari, Di Trani and Smith [1], first posted on 9 August 2022, introduce multipath complexes, dynamical regions and modules, prove families of wedge decompositions, and explicitly conjecture that torsion in a dynamical module forces stability. Their complete-bipartite enumeration uses an *alternating unidirectional* orientation whose multipaths are matchings; it does not calculate the complex of the doubly oriented biclique here. Caputi, Collari and Ramos [2] study quiver minor phenomena, including multipath homology and torsion in a different stable, alternating setting; their general statements do not determine the integral multipath complex of \(D_4\). Published work on bidirected polygons and on bidirected double-star **trees** concerns different digraphs; none of those restricted classifications implies this result. The explicit counterexample is a stricter and mathematically incompatible outcome than the stability conjecture.

## Limitations

No claim is made about other bidirected biclique parameters \(n\ge5\), about global minimality across all digraphs, or about the full homotopy type of \(X(D_4)\). Integral homology calculations were checked with two independent algebraic reductions, but neither they nor the literature comparison constitute independent external peer review. A coincident calculation could exist in unindexed literature; the published works specifically inspected do not cover this assertion.
