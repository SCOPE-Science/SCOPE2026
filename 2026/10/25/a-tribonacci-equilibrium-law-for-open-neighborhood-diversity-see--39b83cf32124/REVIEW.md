# Scientific review: open-neighborhood diversity-seeking cycle swaps

## Correctness — PASS
Each agent's payoff is determined by whether its two neighbor colors disagree, independent of its own color. A nonadjacent opposite-color swap exchanges the two agents' former utilities and cannot benefit both. For an adjacent opposite-color pair, direct evaluation of four surrounding positions proves that both improve exactly on a cyclic `0101` or `1010` pattern. The exchange destroys two bichromatic edges; every sequence terminates with at most \((b(x)-2)/2\) steps from a mixed initial coloring. An eight-state binary de Bruijn transfer counts exactly the cyclic forbidden-word avoiders. Its formal determinant is \((1+zt^2)(1-(1+z)t+z^2t^4)\), giving the asserted weighted count and tribonacci-Lucas total. The bundled symbolic determinant and independently specified payoff-enumeration program passed for all profiles of cycle lengths \(3\) through \(14\). These finite experiments are not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The 2022 Bilò–Bilò–Lenzner–Molitor single-peaked work defines satisfaction through closed neighborhoods, counting an agent as part of its own comparison group. Its cycle observations, equilibrium-existence, convergence and price bounds do not establish the present open-neighborhood exact weighted enumeration. Its equilibrium notion is non-equivalent: alternating six-vertex profiles exhibit different status in the two models. The same authors' *Topological influence and locality in swap Schelling games* uses open neighborhoods but monotone same-color preferences. Its cycle efficiency theorems do not imply these diversity-seeking equilibria. Published exact-cycle counts in an earlier closed-neighborhood study and complete-bipartite exact counts differ both in utility and topology. Targeted searches of published mathematical records, primary sources and cyclic forbidden-word aliases found no covering result. The finite-state technique and abstract tribonacci sequence are not new; an unindexed equivalent game formulation remains possible.

## Value — PASS
Single-peaked neighborhood diversity is a motivated alternative to monotone segregation incentives. For each cycle size the theorem gives the complete labeled equilibrium-by-population polynomial, not merely existence; it also classifies precisely which deviations can occur and proves an exact improvement potential with worst-case step bound. The fact that only adjacent swaps can ever strictly benefit both parties, even if long-distance swaps are allowed, is a structural dynamic property. The difference from the closed-neighborhood model isolates the modeling effect of counting one's own type. The particular degree-two neighborhood is natural, not an arbitrary restriction on the population parameter.

## Sources, distinctions and limitations
Primary sources: Bilò et al., *Tolerance is Necessary for Stability: Single-Peaked Swap Schelling Games* (arXiv:2204.12599, April 26, 2022); Bilò et al., *Topological influence and locality in swap schelling games* (arXiv:2005.02752; DOI 10.1007/s10458-022-09573-7); Agarwal et al., *Schelling games on graphs* (Artificial Intelligence 301, 2021). Their exact conditions and relevant cycle sections were inspected rather than treating titles or snippets as novelty evidence. The theorem does not cover more than two colors, empty positions, heterogeneous peaks, arbitrary networks or non-strict swaps. Outside published or indexed literature may contain equivalent constructions; independent verification remains outstanding.
