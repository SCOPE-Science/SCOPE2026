# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact two-prime equation reduces to \(aq+bp=(p-1)(q-1)\), yielding one exponent pair per permitted prime residue class. Its logarithmic size grows as \((t/p)q\log q+O_p(\log q)\), so the cutoff differs by \(O_p(1)\) from a Lambert \(W\) threshold. Fixed-modulus prime number theory and a uniform finite expansion of \(\operatorname{Li}\) give the stated leading and next-order constants. The zero case for \(p=2\) follows from the absence of permissible exponents. The exact verification script passes, but does not prove the asymptotic.

## Originality — PASS with residual uncertainty

OEIS A166374 is decisive prior art for equality examples, infinitude, and the two-prime linear Diophantine construction, which are excluded from the novelty claim. The 2003 foundational paper and 2012 antiderivative paper were checked for the same distributional question. Targeted literature and research-record comparisons did not reveal an equivalent fixed-prime **two-term** counting result. The prime-number-theorem argument is classical; its combined explicit asymptotic for this natural coincidence family is the claim. Undiscovered mathematical treatments may subsume it.

## Value — PASS

The 2014 existence assertion does not quantify the count of its solutions with a given smaller prime. The theorem supplies an infinite exact-support asymptotic with explicit leading harmonic constant and curvature/secondary term, allowing quantitative comparison of these natural fixed-prime layers. This is an arithmetic-distribution question about a documented integer sequence, not a chosen finite range or a renamed identity.

## Boundaries

Only fixed odd \(p\), exactly two distinct prime factors, and \(X\to\infty\) are covered. The remainder is not uniform in \(p\). No independent validation is claimed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
