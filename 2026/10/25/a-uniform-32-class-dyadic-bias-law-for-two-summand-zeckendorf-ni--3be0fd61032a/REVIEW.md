# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem concerns exact legal index-pair counts, not integer-size density. The proof supplies a valuation lemma obtained by doubling Fibonacci indices, a case-by-case bit-flip lifting argument for all three index classes modulo 6, the full base-32 residue distribution, and a 32-term exact convolution. Using the known period \(P_a=3\cdot2^{a-1}\), it derives the exact asymptotic normalization with an \(O_a(N)\) boundary term. An integer-only verifier reproduces the base case and 4,064 residue cells over seven dyadic moduli, as well as 16 direct pair-count samples. Infinite conclusions use the induction, not finite tests.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

The 2026 Zeckendorf–Niven source concerns infinitude in progressions, not weight-two local leading coefficients. Bragman–Rowland study Fibonacci residue *support*, not complete index multiplicities or convolution. Prodinger's index-class generating-function convolution is different. The already known weight-two aggregate fraction \(5/9\) is not claimed original; the new feature is the exact 16-representative-class nonuniform law and its stable lift across every higher power of two. Targeted published-result searches did not identify an implication reproducing the 32-class table. A systematic specialization of older period counts could subsume the result, and the full Ray–Cooper paper was inaccessible: both are explicit residual risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Exact local asymptotic distributions under natural arithmetic progression restrictions refine mere existence results and reveal an unexpected order-one \(7/3\) disparity between dyadic classes. The uniform residue lifting law gives a reusable arithmetic mechanism for two-term Zeckendorf statistics; it is not just a single modulus enumeration. The claim remains limited to dyadic moduli and an index cutoff.
