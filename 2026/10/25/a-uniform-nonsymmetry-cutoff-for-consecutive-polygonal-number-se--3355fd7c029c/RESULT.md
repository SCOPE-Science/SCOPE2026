# A uniform nonsymmetry cutoff for consecutive nontriangular polygonal numbers

## Finding

For integers \(m\ge4\) and \(n\ge2\), define the ordinary polygonal numbers and the semigroup generated by three successive terms by
\[
P_m(j)=\frac{j((m-2)j-m+4)}{2},\qquad
S_{m,n}=\langle P_m(n),P_m(n+1),P_m(n+2)\rangle.
\]
The three numbers have gcd one. If \(n>12m(3m-8)\), they form a minimal generating set and \(S_{m,n}\) is nonsymmetric, non-complete-intersection and noncyclotomic. Thus for each \(m\ge4\), finding all symmetric triples reduces to a search through the finite interval \(2\le n\le12m(3m-8)\). The bound is sufficient, not asserted sharp, and the theorem does not classify indices within that interval.

## Assumptions and scope

Angle brackets denote nonnegative integer linear combinations. A numerical semigroup has finite complement in \(\mathbb N_0\); its Frobenius number \(F\) is the greatest gap and its genus \(g\) is the number of gaps. Symmetry is equivalent to \(2g=F+1\). The polygonal numbers here are ordinary, not centered. We use two known facts for embedding dimension three: symmetry forces a gluing with a pair \(p,q\) having \(d=\gcd(p,q)>1\) and the remaining generator \(r\in\langle p/d,q/d\rangle\) [1, Section 5.1, Example 6; 2, Section 1]; and symmetry, complete intersection and cyclotomicity are equivalent in embedding dimension at most three [1, Lemma 7].

## Proof

Set \(t=m-2\), let \(a,b,c\) denote the three successive generators in increasing order, and set
\[
u=b-a=tn+1,\qquad e=c-a=2tn+t+2=2u+t.
\]
Since \(\gcd(u,t)=1\), also \(\gcd(u,e)=1\). Any common divisor of \(a,b,c\) divides both differences, proving the numerical semigroup assertion.

Put \(M=m(3m-8)=(t+2)(3t-2)\). Direct substitution gives
\[
2ta-(t-1)=u(tn-t+1),\qquad 8ta-M=e(2tn-3t+2).
\]
Thus \(\gcd(a,b)\mid t-1\), and shifting the same identity forward once gives \(\gcd(b,c)\mid t-1\). The second identity gives \(\gcd(a,c)\mid M\). Each pair gcd is therefore at most \(M\), uniformly in \(n\).

For \(t\ge2\) and \(n\ge2\) we have \(a\ge tn^2/4\) and \(e\le3tn\). If \(n>12M\), then \(e/a\le12/n<1/M\), and \(e>M\) since \(e>n\). In particular \(a<b<c<2a\), so no generator can be written as a sum of the other generators and the set is minimal.

If the semigroup were symmetric, the gluing criterion would give \(d\in[2,M]\) and nonnegative integers \(x,y\) with \(dr=xp+yq\). For \(s=x+y\), the bounds \(p,q,r\in[a,c]\) give \(da/c\le s\le dc/a\). Write \(z=e/a\). Then \(dz<1\) and \(d-d/(1+z)=dz/(1+z)<1\), so \(d-1<s<d+1\). Consequently \(s=d\), making \(r\) a weighted average of the other generators. Only \(r=b\) is possible. Taking \(p=a,q=c\) and subtracting \(da\) gives \(du=ye\). Coprimality yields \(e\mid d\), contradicting \(e>M\ge d\). The equivalences in [1] give the other two exclusions.

## Known boundary and fixed slices

The triangular boundary is prior work, not part of the claimed new cutoff. Robles-Pérez and Rosales [3, Propositions 5 and 6, and Section 4] show that consecutive triangular triples are telescopic and compute their Frobenius number; telescopic numerical semigroups are symmetric. In the notation \(T_j=j(j+1)/2\), their Frobenius formula is
\[
F=\lfloor n/2\rfloor(T_n+T_{n+1}+T_{n+2}-1)-1.
\]
In particular the genus is \((F+1)/2\). Komatsu [4, Theorem 1 at \(p=0\)] reproduces the ordinary Frobenius formulas. For \(m=4\), Fel [2] gives an exact symmetry classification of consecutive squares with a shifted center index. The ordinary pentagonal and hexagonal classifications [5, 6] are also stronger on those individual slices. Hashuga, Herbine and Jensen [7, Sections 3–4 and the conclusion] study semigroups from an entire infinite quadratic sequence, not a three-term window starting at arbitrary \(n\). The contribution here is the explicit two-parameter bound for ordinary nontriangular triples, not the fixed-slice classifications or the general gluing criterion.

## Reproducibility

The accompanying `verify.py` is a bounded check of the arithmetic identities, the pair gcd bounds and the strict inequalities, together with a two-instance Apéry-set calculation just above the square-case cutoff. It does not prove the infinite quantifiers. The all-parameter conclusion rests on the proof above.
