Two co-located Gaussian pollution plumes can have two distinct ground-level hotspots; inside their two-peak regime, increasing the taller stack's emission rate switches the unique global hotspot from near to far downwind exactly once. The switching emission ratio has the sharp large-height-contrast expansion \(q_b=r-e+e(1-e/2)/r+O(r^{-2})\), and the two-hotspot regime is born in a quantified cubic-cusp transition.
