For every pair of positive epidemic reproduction means \(a<b\), using the smaller mean first in a two-generation Poisson branching outbreak reduces expected infections and all probability-generating-function risks, yet eventually increases every sufficiently extreme outbreak-tail probability. The sharp logarithmic tail comparison is \(\lim_{n\to\infty}(\Pr(C_{a,b}\ge n)/\Pr(C_{b,a}\ge n))^{1/n}=b/a\): postponing strong suppression changes the rare-event exponent, not just the mean.
