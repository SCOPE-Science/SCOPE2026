# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof reduces an arbitrary base affinely to John position, proves containment of the proposed ellipsoid for every \(d\ge2\) and \(q\in[0,1]\), explicitly constructs contact points from arbitrary nonsymmetric base John contacts, and checks both vector and matrix John identities including the zero-weight threshold. John’s exact sufficiency theorem then proves global maximum-volume optimality and uniqueness. The endpoint cone and prism formulas and the cap-contact boundary are covered. The standalone program independently checks 70 numerical contact systems and direct containment; numerical diagnostics supplement, rather than replace, the analytical argument.

## Originality — PASS (relative to accessible prior work)

Güler–Gürtuna (arXiv:0709.0707v1) provide a directly relevant close comparison: they study truncated second-order cones but explicitly leave the inscribed ellipsoid problem for that class untreated, while solving an outer-ellipsoid problem. Gruber–Schuster (arXiv:1207.7246v1) prove the general contact characterization but do not specialize it to homothetic frusta or derive the universal threshold. Published results on frustum Santaló points/volume products and a specific half-cone concern different invariants or narrower bodies. No statement inspected implies the all-base functorial formula or exact upper-cap onset; older literature not fully indexed remains a genuine priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem computes an extremal geometric object in every dimension for an infinite family with arbitrary asymmetric or nonsmooth convex bases, reducing it exactly to one lower-dimensional John-ellipsoid computation. A sharp cap-contact transition is independent of base shape, and an explicit positive John-weight certificate explains its mechanism. This is a natural geometric classification and an effective structural reduction, not a routine scalar substitution.

## Limits and closest literature

The result addresses only homothetic frusta and **inner** volume-maximizing ellipsoids. It does not describe nonhomothetic truncated cones or outer ellipsoids. The closest direct paper (Güler–Gürtuna 2007) omits the relevant inner problem, but an unidentified earlier paper might still contain an equivalent. Analytic proof and finite numerical checks have not been independently reviewed.
