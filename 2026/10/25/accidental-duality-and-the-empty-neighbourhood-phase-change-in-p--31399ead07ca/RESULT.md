# Accidental duality and the empty-neighbourhood phase change in positive instantial semantics

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge 1\), let \(W\) be a labelled set of \(n\) worlds and let \(\mathcal P_+(W)=\mathcal P(W)\setminus\{\varnothing\}\). Independently and uniformly choose two neighbourhood families \(\mathcal B,\mathcal D\subseteq\mathcal P_+(W)\); equivalently, include each nonempty set as a neighbourhood in either family by an independent fair coin. Define their zero-instance positive instantial predicates on arbitrary scopes \(A\subseteq W\) by

\[
\operatorname{Box}_{\mathcal B}(A)\iff (\exists U\in\mathcal B)\ U\subseteq A,\qquad
\operatorname{Dia}_{\mathcal D}(A)\iff (\forall U\in\mathcal D)\ U\cap A\ne\varnothing.
\]

Say the two primitives are *accidentally complementary* at this zero-instance level if, simultaneously for every \(A\subseteq W\),

\[
\operatorname{Box}_{\mathcal B}(A)\iff
\neg\operatorname{Dia}_{\mathcal D}(W\setminus A).
\tag{1}
\]

The complement and external negation in (1) describe a semantic comparison, not extra connectives in the positive language. Let \(p_n\) be the probability of (1). For an upward-closed family \(\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal P_+(W)\), write \(\operatorname{Min}(\mathcal U)\) for its inclusion-minimal members. Then the following exact counting and asymptotic statements hold:

\[
\boxed{
 p_n=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\mathcal U\text{ an upset of }\mathcal P_+(W)}4^{|\mathcal U|-|\operatorname{Min}(\mathcal U)|}}
 {2^{2(2^n-1)}}.
}
\tag{2}
\]

Furthermore, for every \(n\ge1\),

\[
\boxed{
4^{-n}\le p_n\le 4^{-n}\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk2^{-\binom k2}
\le 4^{-n}(n+1)2^{\frac12(\log_2n+\frac12)^2}.
}
\tag{3}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log_2p_n=-2.}
\tag{4}
\]

If the empty set is *permitted* as a neighbourhood and independently selected into each family by a fair coin, let \(\widehat p_n\) denote the analogous probability of (1). In sharp contrast,

\[
\boxed{\widehat p_n=\frac14+\frac14p_n\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\widehat p_n=\frac14.}
\tag{5}
\]

Thus accidental agreement of independent positive zero-instance modalities has exponential rate \(4^{-n}\) up to subexponential factors if neighbourhoods must be nonempty, but has a nonzero limiting probability if empty neighbourhoods are allowed.

## Assumptions and scope

The carrier \(W\) is finite, nonempty, and equipped with the discrete persistence order. This makes every set of worlds available as a scope and makes arbitrary neighbourhood families admissible. The two neighbourhood families are sampled independently at one fixed evaluation world, with uniform fair inclusion of candidate neighbourhood subsets. No seriality, monotonicity, nonemptiness of the families themselves, or higher-instance witnessing is imposed.

For the first ensemble the set \(\varnothing\) may not be a neighbourhood, although either selected family may be empty. In the second ensemble \(\varnothing\) is an independently selectable neighbourhood. The zero-instance operators have the witness and co-witness meaning displayed above. The result is about simultaneous semantic comparison over all scopes at this one world, not a proof of internal definability of either primitive in a negation-free language or of compatibility for nonzero instance arities.

## Proof

**1. Reduction to generated upsets.** For any family \(\mathcal F\) of subsets of \(W\), put

\[
\uparrow\mathcal F=\{A\subseteq W:(\exists U\in\mathcal F)\ U\subseteq A\}.
\]

By the semantics, \(\operatorname{Box}_{\mathcal B}(A)\) means \(A\in\uparrow\mathcal B\), while failure of \(\operatorname{Dia}_{\mathcal D}(W\setminus A)\) means that some \(U\in\mathcal D\) is disjoint from the complement of \(A\), equivalently \(A\in\uparrow\mathcal D\). Thus (1) holds for all \(A\) exactly when \(\uparrow\mathcal B=\uparrow\mathcal D\). This includes empty selected families and vacuous universal quantification for the diamond predicate.

**2. Exact fiber count.** When selected neighbourhoods are nonempty, any generated upset \(\mathcal U\) is a subset of \(\mathcal P_+(W)\). Conversely every upset \(\mathcal U\) is generated by its uniquely determined antichain \(\operatorname{Min}(\mathcal U)\). A family \(\mathcal F\) generates exactly \(\mathcal U\) if and only if

\[
\operatorname{Min}(\mathcal U)\subseteq\mathcal F\subseteq\mathcal U.
\]

Indeed, omitting a minimal member cannot be repaired by any smaller generator, while adding any element of \(\mathcal U\) leaves the closure unchanged. Hence the number of families with this closure is \(2^{|\mathcal U|-|\operatorname{Min}(\mathcal U)|}\), including one family for the empty upset. For an ordered pair the fiber sizes square, giving the sum in (2). Each of the two independent families has \(2^{2^n-1}\) possibilities, proving its denominator.

**3. Exponential bounds.** At every singleton \(\{w\}\), membership in \(\uparrow\mathcal F\) is exactly the selection bit for the neighbourhood \(\{w\}\); there is no smaller permitted neighbourhood. Thus closure agreement requires the two selection bits at each singleton to be equal. Fix the set \(T\subseteq W\) of singleton positions where both bits are zero, and write \(|T|=k\). The probability of this exact matched-singleton pattern is \(4^{-n}\).

For each pair \(\{u,v\}\subseteq T\), no singleton can generate that two-element set. Closure equality therefore requires the independent two-family selection bits of that pair to match; this has probability \(1/2\) and these events are independent over all \(\binom k2\) pairs. All higher-set requirements can only reduce the probability. Summing over all \(\binom nk\) possibilities for \(T\) proves the middle bound of (3).

For the last bound, put \(L=\log_2 n\). The elementary estimate \(\binom nk\le n^k\) yields

\[
\binom nk 2^{-\binom k2}\le
2^{kL-k(k-1)/2}
=2^{\frac12(L+\frac12)^2-\frac12(k-L-\frac12)^2}
\le2^{\frac12(L+\frac12)^2}.
\]

There are \(n+1\) summands. For the lower bound, force both families to contain every singleton: a probability \(4^{-n}\) event under which their upward closures are both all of \(\mathcal P_+(W)\). Taking base-two logarithms of (3) and dividing by \(n\) gives (4), because \((\log n)^2/n\to0\).

**4. Allowing empty neighbourhoods.** The fair inclusion bits of \(\varnothing\) are independent of all other bits. If both families include \(\varnothing\), their generated upsets both equal \(\mathcal P(W)\), regardless of other choices: probability \(1/4\). If exactly one includes it, the upsets cannot match, since only that family generates \(\varnothing\). If neither includes it, probability \(1/4\) times the original conditional probability \(p_n\) remains. This proves the exact identity (5), and (4) gives its limit.

## Verification

A standalone reproducible Python checker, `check_duality.py`, builds all selected families and their generated-upset signatures for \(n=1,2,3,4\). Independently it enumerates every upset and evaluates the fiber-weight sum in (2), obtaining exact ordered-pair counts \(2,14,1438,35572286\) and upset counts \(2,5,19,167\). For \(n\le3\) it further enumerates every ordered pair of neighbourhood families and every scope, directly evaluating both predicates in (1) and checking agreement against the generated-upset comparison. It also checks (5) symbolically using exact rational arithmetic.

The resulting probabilities for \(n=1,2,3,4\) are, respectively, \(1/2\), \(7/32\), \(719/8192\), and \(17786143/536870912\). The general inequalities and asymptotic assertions are mathematical deductions above, not extrapolations from those finite computations.

## Relationship to prior work

Das, Khanra and Sardar introduce two independent positive instantial modalities and their neighbourhood witness/co-witness semantics. Their contribution is a soundness/completeness and algebraic representation theory, not this fair-independent-family coupling law. Přenosil investigates qualitative compatibility of box and diamond in distributive modal logic, using relational representation and compatibility axioms; it does not supply the probability of accidental agreement of two random neighbourhood families. Bezhanishvili, Enqvist and de Groot study descriptive-frame and coalgebraic duality for ordinary instantial neighbourhood logic. Classical correspondence between antichains and finite Boolean-lattice upsets supplies the elementary counting language used here. The specific weighted collision sum, exponential decay rate, and empty-neighbourhood discontinuity are the quantitative claims, not the background semantic or antichain correspondence.

## Limitations

The quantitative law assumes fair and independent selection of every candidate neighbourhood; biased or correlated sampling need not obey (3) or (5). The sharp result is only the exponential rate, not a full subexponential equivalent for \(p_n\). The permitted-empty-sets limit is driven by the degenerate case of two simultaneously selected empty neighbourhoods. The proof compares the zero-instance truth sets for all valuation scopes and does not address the full instantial arity hierarchy, internal positive-language definability, or any axiomatically forced interaction. Historical priority relative to less accessible random-hypergraph or Boolean-function collision literature remains uncertain; independent verification has not been performed.
