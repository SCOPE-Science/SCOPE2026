# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The odd-dihedral maximal-subgroup classification gives the Frattini subgroup \(\langle a^{\operatorname{rad}(n)}\rangle\). The maximal-subgroup cover has simplicial intersections with stabilizer-fixed barycenters, so its nerve comparison is equivariant. The complete face criterion isolates a cone invariant under all automorphisms and exactly one transversal facet for each residue modulo the radical. Collapsing that invariant cone produces a canonical sphere-permutation model: block indices are fixed, so no orientation sign arises. Fixed-locus and character deductions follow from explicit affine congruences. The supplied script separately checks complete nerve intersections for seven odd parameters and all automorphism traces in the smallest cases; finite checks are only corroboration.

## Originality — PASS (with explicit residual risk)

The non-generating Frattini reduction is already Proposition 3.2 of Dupont García (2026); the general solvable-lattice wedge-of-spheres theorem is already credited to Thévenaz in Woodroofe's Theorem 1.4. Neither ordinary equivalence nor the sphere count alone is asserted as novel. The new work supplies the full \(\operatorname{Aut}(D_{2n})\)-equivariant homotopy model, together with every fixed locus and integral affine permutation representation, which do not follow from the cited *nonequivariant* equivalences as stated. Subgroup-lattice equivariant topology has existing literature, particularly Welker (1995), whose full text was not accessible for comparison. No uniqueness claim is made; prior stronger or equivalent equivariant results remain a genuine risk.

## Value — PASS

The exact action matters because scalar Betti counts cannot recover fixed-subgroup topology, degrees of symmetry maps, or the automorphism quotient. The theorem classifies these invariants for an infinite natural family, including nonsquarefree odd parameters, with a canonical invariant-cone proof. It isolates the structural difference between a merely solvable-lattice sphere decomposition and one compatible with *every* group automorphism; this is substantive topology rather than a routine numerical refinement.
