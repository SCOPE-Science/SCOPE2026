For convex octahedra whose eight triangular faces are congruent to a prescribed scalene triangle \(T\), there are exactly four possible length-incidence patterns: one occurs precisely when \(T\) is acute, and three occur according to whether the corresponding side of \(T\) faces an acute angle. Each feasible pattern is rigid up to Euclidean congruence, giving four octahedral shapes for acute \(T\) and two for right or obtuse \(T\).
