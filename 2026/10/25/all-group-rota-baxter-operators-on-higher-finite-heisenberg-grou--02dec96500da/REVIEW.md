# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS

The Rota–Baxter identity forces preservation of the central subgroup, additivity of the quotient coordinate, and the displayed polarization equation. Its alternating part is exactly a shifted symplectic-similitude condition. The quadratic correction accounts for its symmetric part and leaves an arbitrary linear functional. Rank and enumeration follow from isotropic-subspace counting and symplectic-group orders. The proof is valid for every odd prime and all \(n\ge2\); a direct verifier checks finite low-dimensional instances without extrapolating from them.

## Originality: PASS, qualified

Savelyev (arXiv:2608.28291, §§3–5) studies general extraspecial center preservation but gives an explicit full classification only for order-\(p^3\), and an automorphism-based general construction rather than an all-map high-rank census. Rathee (arXiv:2402.15428) treats the three-dimensional Heisenberg Lie group. The exact higher-rank finite enumeration and the two singular scalar strata were not identified as consequences of any compared primary result. A previously unindexed or differently phrased higher-dimensional operator theorem remains possible; absolute priority is not claimed.

## Mathematical value: PASS

The theorem extends a contemporary order-\(p^3\) classification problem to the entire natural exponent-\(p\) Heisenberg family, and gives a complete, uniform arithmetic answer. The dichotomy between symplectic-similitude fibers and arbitrary isotropic-image maps, including exact rank-stratified counts, resolves a nontrivial structural question and not merely an isolated numerical example.

## Scientific limits

No statement is made for characteristic two, extraspecial groups of exponent \(p^2\), or orbits under conjugation. Tests are supporting evidence, not an exhaustive proof. Historical priority requires independent expert assessment.
