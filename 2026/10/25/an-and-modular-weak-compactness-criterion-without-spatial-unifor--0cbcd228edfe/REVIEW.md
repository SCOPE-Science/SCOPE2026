# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The key replacement for the spatially uniform truncation lemma is valid on finite measure by a pointwise-divergence and finite-measure argument: \(\mu\{|u_n|>b_n\}\le\mu\{\varphi(x,b_n)<M\}+1/M\). Properness with \(\Delta_2\) makes \(\varphi(x,1)\) integrable; dominated convergence supplies the necessary small constants. Fenchel equality controls the supremum dual witness by a summable modular. Sufficiency is a direct application of Young's inequality and the full weak-compactness criterion in [1]. The conjugate in the strict example is locally bounded by an integrable \(x^{-1/2}\) weight.

## Originality
**PASS with residual priority risk.** The most directly pertinent published primary result [1, Theorem 3.8] includes a genuinely stronger *constrained* hypothesis, and [1, Remark 3.7] specifically identifies the obstacle on infinite measure. Here a new finite-measure argument removes that restriction for all proper generalized \(N\)-functions satisfying uniform \(\Delta_2\), not solely standard Orlicz or variable-power spaces. The strict non-constrained, nonatomic, mixed-power example is not directly implied by published variable-exponent or sequence-space statements. Abstract-only comparisons remain an explicit bibliographic limitation.

## Value
**PASS.** The theorem removes a substantial spatial growth assumption from an exact necessary-and-sufficient weak compactness criterion on the natural finite-measure domain, including fibers that degenerate in their spatial argument. Weak compactness otherwise requires checking every dual multiplier, whereas the modular test uses only the generating integrand; the explicit mixed-power family illustrates where the relaxation adds usable coverage.
