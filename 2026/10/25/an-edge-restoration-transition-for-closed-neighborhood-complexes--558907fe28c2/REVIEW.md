# Scientific review: An edge-restoration transition for closed-neighborhood complexes of crown graphs

## Correctness — PASS
Step 1 proves the hemispherical intersection is exactly the graph because a mixed edge must be an original cross edge and no same-side edge lies in both covers. Step 2 explicitly collapses each cone relative to its nonempty base, leaving whole part simplices under opposite-side universal vertices. Step 3 invokes the homotopy-pushout theorem and the integral Mayer–Vietoris isomorphism. The n>=4 crown boundary-sphere endpoint is separated from j>=1, and the first map is compared by naturality of the MV sequence; at n=4 the two lost degree-two generators and one new generator are accounted for. For later steps graph edge insertion injects cycle homology with primitive rank-one cokernel. verify.py independently enumerates 26 crown stages and 64 random universal-pair graphs, checks 8352 free-face pairs and nine inclusion ranks, supplementing rather than proving the infinite claim.

The finite checks and map-rank tests do not substitute for the integral homotopy-pushout proof. In particular, the first-stage degree-two kernel is two only when \(n=4\); from \(n\ge5\) the kernel vanishes, while higher-dimensional classes die as specified.

## Originality — PASS, with priority uncertainty
The exact crown endpoint is already classified by two published open-neighborhood prism theorems, and is explicitly credited as old. Their facet descriptions show equivalence to the closed-neighborhood crown endpoint; this is a strong implication comparison, not a mere title comparison. Their coverage stops before any deleted matching pair is restored and contains no degree-two inclusion-map statement. The first restoration supplies a genuine change of topology and a natural persistence law for every further restoration. Matsushita’s full available 2025 treatment defines closed neighborhoods and relates their fundamental groups to graph path groups, but does not prove this universal-pair suspension or the specific integral persistence maps. Dense bipartite restored crowns contain K2,3, so the separate published triangle-free/K2,3-free closed-neighborhood normal form does not apply. Related prior crown-graph findings about two-independence complexes concern a different invariant and do not imply the claimed homotopy types. Searches and inspections reduce but cannot eliminate priority uncertainty.

Closest fully read records: Matsushita's *Closed neighborhood complexes of graphs* (2025, arXiv:2511.12608); *Neighborhood complexes of two-row rook graphs split into two spheres and a crown suspension* (https://github.com/published-finding corpus/2026/blob/main/2026/10/11/SCOPE-neighborhood-complexes-of-two-row-rook-graphs-split-into-two-sph--9b16e10ce620/RESULT.md); *Open-neighborhood complexes of clique prisms* (https://github.com/published-finding corpus/2026/blob/main/2026/10/11/SCOPE-open-neighborhood-complexes-of-clique-prisms-a-mixed-dimensional--d1fca5b06808/RESULT.md); *Square-cell splitting for closed neighborhood complexes of triangle-free graphs* (https://github.com/published-finding corpus/2026/blob/main/2026/10/11/SCOPE-square-cell-splitting-for-closed-neighborhood-complexes-of-trian--dff1a4056142/RESULT.md). The first two open-neighborhood records completely cover the previously known un-restored crown endpoint; the new content starts at the restored stages and includes the general universal-pair criterion and natural persistence maps.

## Value — PASS
The result identifies a sharp, natural topology-changing edge insertion in a one-parameter filtration starting at a previously studied, nontrivial mixed-dimensional wedge. The universal-pair criterion upgrades a graph cycle-space invariant to integral second-homology by a constructive two-hemisphere model across a broad graph class, and the restoration filtration makes exact predictions for homotopy types and all integral degree-two persistence maps. At n=4 the first inclusion has two independent dying classes and one new birth; for n>=5 two higher-dimensional classes die while the later sequence has primitive rank-one births. These features are motivated by the known crown endpoint rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Limitations
Noncanonical homotopy equivalences; no global simplicial collapses asserted for entire complexes. The general theorem needs two opposite universal vertices. No non-homological persistence or higher-homotopy maps are classified. Priority searches cannot rule out an unpublished or unindexed equivalent statement.
