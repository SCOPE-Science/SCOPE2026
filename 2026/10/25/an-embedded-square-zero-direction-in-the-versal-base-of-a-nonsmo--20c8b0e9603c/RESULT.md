# An embedded square-zero direction in the versal base of a nonsmoothable pentagon cone

## Finding
Let \(Q=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(-2,-1),(-3,-2),(-2,-3),(-1,-2)\}\) and let \(U_Q\) denote the complex affine Gorenstein toric threefold given by the height-one cone over \(Q\). The completed local ring at the distinguished point of a miniversal deformation base of \(U_Q\) is
\[
\widehat{\mathcal O}_{\operatorname{Def}(U_Q),0}\simeq\mathbb C[[u,v]]/(u^2,uv).
\]
The reduced base is the smooth formal line \(u=0\). Its unique extra infinitesimal direction is the square-zero class of \(u\), supported at the origin, not a nilpotent thickening of the general point of the line. Thus the tangent dimension is \(2\), the reduced-base dimension is \(1\), and the local ring has associated primes \((u)\) and \((u,v)\). The claim concerns the local singularity, not the global deformation base of a containing Calabi--Yau variety.

## Assumptions and scope
All rings and deformations are over \(\mathbb C\). The five counterclockwise primitive edge vectors of \(Q\) are
\[
d_0=(-2,-1),\quad d_1=(-1,-1),\quad d_2=(1,-1),\quad d_3=(1,1),\quad d_4=(1,2).
\]
The edges are primitive, so the affine Gorenstein threefold has an isolated singularity at its torus-fixed point. Its polygonal (Minkowski) deformation base is miniversal by Altmann's theorem for isolated toric Gorenstein singularities. Coordinate choices below determine the isomorphism class of the completed base, not a canonical choice of its parameters.

## Proof
Altmann's scheme is cut out by the two coordinates of the vector equations
\[
G_k(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{4}d_jt_j^k=0\qquad (k\ge1),
\]
with the common translation of the five \(t_j\) discarded. For \(k=1\), write \(t_0=a,t_1=b,t_2=c\). The two linear equations solve uniquely as
\[
t_3=3a+b-3c,\qquad t_4=-a+2c.
\]
After removing common translation \(c\), set \(u=a-c\), \(v=b-c\). The resulting five edge parameters are
\[
(t_0,t_1,t_2,t_3,t_4)=(u,v,0,3u+v,-u).
\]
At order \(k=2\), the two components of \(G_2\) are exactly
\[
8u^2+6uv,\qquad10u^2+6uv.
\]
Over \(\mathbb C\), their generated ideal is precisely \((u^2,uv)\): their difference equals \(2u^2\), and eliminating this term from either equation yields \(6uv\).

No equation of higher order imposes another relation. Indeed, in \(R=\mathbb C[u,v]/(u^2,uv)\), for every integer \(k\ge2\) the classes of \(u^k\) and \((-u)^k\) vanish and \((v+3u)^k=v^k\). The only surviving edge contributions to \(G_k\) are then \(d_1v^k\) and \(d_3v^k\); these cancel because \(d_1+d_3=0\). Consequently the ideal generated by all \(G_k\), \(k\ge2\), equals \((u^2,uv)\) already generated in degree two. Completion at \((u,v)\) gives the claimed miniversal complete local ring.

The nilradical is \((u)\), with \(u\ne0\), \(u^2=uv=0\); its annihilator is the maximal ideal \((u,v)\). This is the embedded associated point. Inverting \(v\) forces \(u=0\), so the base is generically reduced along the line. The cotangent space has basis the classes of \(u,v\), proving tangent dimension two. The radical ideal \((u)\) proves the reduced-base statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Wuebben (arXiv:2609.28499, first posted 2026-09-09) exhibits exactly this pentagon as a local model in a nonsmoothable Calabi--Yau threefold, determines that the reduced miniversal deformation base is a smooth curve, and notes that the tangent space is two-dimensional and the base is nonreduced. The present finding determines the *scheme structure and the support of its nilpotents*, which those statements do not specify.

Altmann (arXiv:alg-geom/9403004, especially Sections 2 and 9.1(v)) proves the general miniversal Minkowski-scheme construction and computes the *same abstract base ring* for a different pentagon of normalized area \(5\), compared with normalized area \(7\) here. Thus the algebra type itself is not a new phenomenon; the exact assignment of this base and its embedded infinitesimal obstruction to the recently studied singularity is the new claim. Filip (arXiv:2610.10230) extends universal equivariant constructions; that general framework is not an explicit calculation of this local ring.

## Limitations
The result is a specific computation under established deformation-theoretic theorems, not a new general construction. It does not establish properties of the miniversal deformation base of any projective Calabi--Yau threefold globally, nor a smoothing. The abstract nonreduced base algebra occurs in Altmann's older, geometrically different example. As with any literature comparison, an unindexed prior calculation for precisely \(Q\) is possible.
