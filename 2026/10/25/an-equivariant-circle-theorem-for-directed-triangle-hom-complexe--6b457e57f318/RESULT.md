# An equivariant circle theorem for directed triangle Hom complexes of all regular cyclic tournaments

## Finding
For every odd integer \(n=2m+1\ge5\), put \(R_n\) for the tournament on \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) with \(i\to j\) precisely when \(1\le(j-i)\bmod n\le m\). Let \(X_n=\operatorname{Hom}(\overrightarrow C_3,R_n)\), where the source is an oriented triangle. Then there is a \(C_n\)-equivariant homotopy equivalence
\[
X_n\simeq_{C_n} S^1,
\]
where the cyclic target-rotation group acts on the right as the free rotation group of order \(n\). Consequently \(H_0(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\) and all higher integral homology vanishes. Every rotation acts trivially on \(H_1\). The quotient is homotopy equivalent to a circle, and the quotient covering induces multiplication by \(n\) on first integral homology under compatible orientations.

## Assumptions and scope
A multihomomorphism is a triple \((A,B,C)\) of nonempty subsets of \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) such that every edge from \(A\) to \(B\), from \(B\) to \(C\), and from \(C\) to \(A\) is an arc. The regular CW complex has one cell for each such triple, of dimension \(|A|+|B|+|C|-3\), with cellular face order given by coordinatewise inclusion. Its realization is \(X_n\). Let \(C_n\) rotate all three target-coordinate sets simultaneously. All statements hold for every odd \(n\ge5\); they do not hold at \(n=3\), when there are three isolated zero-cells.

## Proof
**1. A cyclic subcomplex cover.** For each target vertex \(j\), let \(X_j\) be the subcomplex of those cells \((A,B,C)\) for which \((A\cup\{j\},B,C)\) is a valid cell. The condition persists under passage to nonempty coordinatewise subsets, so \(X_j\) is a subcomplex. Since \(A\ne\varnothing\), the \(n\) subcomplexes cover \(X_n\). Rotating target vertices permutes these subcomplexes.

**2. Every nonempty intersection is contractible.** Let \(\varnothing\ne J\subseteq\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) and suppose \(X_J=\bigcap_{j\in J}X_j\ne\varnothing\). On the face poset of \(X_J\), the order maps
\[
(A,B,C)\longmapsto(A\cup J,B,C)\longmapsto(J,B,C)
\]
are respectively extensive and reductive. Their order-homotopies identify the homotopy type of \(X_J\) with the poset \(Y_J\) of nonempty \((B,C)\) such that all arcs \(J\to B\to C\to J\) hold. Pick one \(j_0\in J\) and, just for the following ordered comparisons, write \(j_0=0\) by rotating labels. The allowed elements of \(B\) lie in \(\{1,\dots,m\}\); those of \(C\) lie in \(\{m+1,\dots,2m\}\). In this numerical range, \(b\to c\) holds if and only if \(c\le b+m\).

Let \(B_0\) be the set of all vertices \(b\) satisfying \(J\to b\), and put \(b_*:=\max B_0\). It is nonempty since \(Y_J\ne\varnothing\). Whenever \((B,C)\in Y_J\), the two poset maps
\[
(B,C)\longmapsto(B\cup\{b_*\},C)\longmapsto(\{b_*\},C)
\]
are valid and give homotopy equivalences because all inequalities \(c\le b+m\) remain true on replacing \(b\) by the larger \(b_*\). With \(B=\{b_*\}\), permissible nonempty \(C\)'s form the nonempty face poset of the simplex on
\[
C_*:=\{c:c\to J\text{ and }b_*\to c\}.
\]
This set is nonempty since the original intersection was. Hence \(X_J\) is contractible.

**3. Identify the nerve.** The cover nerve has a face \(J\) if and only if there are vertices \(b,c\) with \(J\to b\to c\to J\). Rotating labels so \(b=0\), we have \(c=d\in\{1,\dots,m\}\). For each \(j\in J\), the conditions \(j\to0\) and \(d\to j\) force \(m+1\le j\le m+d\); thus \(J\) is contained in a consecutive cyclic interval of \(m\) vertices. Conversely, if \(J\subseteq\{i,i+1,\dots,i+m-1\}\) modulo \(n\), choose \(b=i+m\) and \(c=i+2m\) modulo \(n\). Every \(j\) in the interval satisfies \(j\to b\), \(b\to c\), and \(c\to j\). The nerve is therefore precisely the abstract simplicial complex \(N_n\) generated by the \(n\) cyclic intervals of \(m\) successive vertices.

**4. A circle cover with the same nerve.** Regard \(S^1\) as \(\mathbb R/n\mathbb Z\). Choose \(0<\varepsilon<1/4\) and cover it by the open arcs
\[
U_j=(j-m+1-\varepsilon,\,j+\varepsilon)\pmod n,\qquad j\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z.
\]
For \(m\ge2\), these arcs cover the circle. Each has length \(m-1+2\varepsilon<n/2\). Any nonempty finite intersection is a single open arc: after lifting one member to the real line, intersections with each other arc cannot have two components, because a second component would require two arcs whose lengths sum to at least \(n\). The indices of arcs containing a given point lie among \(m\) consecutive cyclic indices. Conversely the arcs indexed by any complete interval of \(m\) consecutive vertices meet in an open neighborhood of an integer point. Thus their nerve is exactly \(N_n\), and all nonempty intersections are contractible.

By the finite subcomplex nerve theorem and the good-open-cover nerve theorem, both \(X_n\) and \(S^1\) have homotopy type \(|N_n|\). These constructions are natural under cyclic rotation of all index labels, hence the nerve equivalences are \(C_n\)-equivariant. More explicitly, no nonempty simplex of \(N_n\) is stabilized by a nontrivial rotation: its vertices lie in an arc of at most \(m\) successive labels, strictly less than half the circle, whereas a nontrivial cyclic orbit is not so contained. Consequently all relevant isotropy groups are trivial, and the equivariant nerve maps are equivalences on fixed points for every subgroup (only the trivial subgroup has nonempty fixed points). This proves the stated equivariant circle equivalence.

**5. Homology and quotient.** The free rotation of \(S^1\) is orientation-preserving. An equivariant homotopy equivalence preserves the rotation character on homology and descends to a homotopy equivalence of quotients. Since the standard circle rotation quotient is an \(n\)-fold circle covering, the induced first-homology map is multiplication by \(n\) up to orientation.

## Verification
A standalone Python program `verify.py` independently enumerates the valid cells and boundary maps for odd \(n\in\{5,7,9,11\}\). It verifies the predicted cell counts, Euler characteristics and mod-two Betti ranks, and independently checks the asserted nerve criterion for every possible subset of target labels. The finite computations are cross-checks, not a replacement for the all-parameter proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Dochtermann and Singh (arXiv:2108.10948, first made public 24 August 2021) introduced and analyzed directed homomorphism complexes, including results for transitive tournaments and nontransitive examples; their published results do not assert this all-parameter regular-cyclic-tournament theorem. The published calculation of \(\operatorname{Hom}(\overrightarrow C_3,R_9)\simeq S^1\) is a single case and is subsumed by the present family theorem, but it does not imply the uniform result or the equivariant conclusion. Adamaszek, Adams, Frick, Peterson and Previte-Johnson classified homotopy of circular-arc nerves and symmetry representations; these results can classify the nerve \(N_n\) but do not identify the directed Hom complex with that nerve. The contractible-subcomplex-cover reduction in Steps 1–3 supplies the missing implication. Tournament flag complexes and standard directed path homology concern different spaces.

## Limitations
The theorem assumes regular cyclic tournament targets and an oriented three-cycle source; it does not classify other tournaments, longer source cycles, or the source-rotation symmetry. Homology statements are integral but do not describe the integral cellular \(C_n\)-chain lattice. Possible equivalent descriptions in unindexed literature remain a novelty risk. No independent external audit has been performed.
