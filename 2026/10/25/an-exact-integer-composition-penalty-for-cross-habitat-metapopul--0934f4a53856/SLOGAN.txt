In a finite two-class habitat network, the minimum cross-habitat colonization strength that makes any integer patch allocation supercritical can be found by checking only the floor and ceiling of one explicit real-valued optimizer; an exact positive square-law penalty quantifies the cost of rounding. With five patches, homogeneous configurations fail and a continuous relaxation predicts rescue for \(c>7/5\), but genuine integer rescue requires \(c>\sqrt2\).
