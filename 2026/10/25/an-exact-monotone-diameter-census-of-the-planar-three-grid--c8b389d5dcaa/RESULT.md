# An exact monotone-diameter census of the planar three-grid

## Finding

Let \(G=\{0,1,2,3\}^2\). Among all full-dimensional planar lattice polygons \(P=\operatorname{conv}(S)\subseteq[0,3]^2\), \(S\subseteq G\), there are precisely \(2719\) distinct convex hulls, distinguished by their actual vertex-coordinate sets. For a nonzero generic objective \(c\in\mathbb R^2\), direct each edge toward its endpoint with larger \(c\)-height. For a vertex \(v\), let \(d_c(v)\) be the minimum number of strictly increasing edges on a path from \(v\) to the unique maximizer. Define \(\mu(P)=\max_c\max_v d_c(v)\) with \(c\) generic. The complete distribution is:

| Vertices | Monotone diameter | Number of polygons |
|:--:|:--:|--:|
| 3 | 1 | 516 |
| 4 | 2 | 1038 |
| 5 | 2 | 648 |
| 5 | 3 | 200 |
| 6 | 3 | 292 |
| 7 | 3 | 24 |
| 8 | 4 | 1 |

In particular \(\max_P\mu(P)=4\). The unique equality case in the fixed grid (without quotienting by lattice equivalence) is
\[
H=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,1),(1,0),(2,0),(3,1),(3,2),(2,3),(1,3),(0,2)\}.
\]
For \(c=(3,1)\), the vertex \((0,1)\) has strictly increasing distance four to the unique \(c\)-maximal vertex \((3,2)\). Including nongeneric \(c\) does not alter \(\mu(P)\) for any of the \(2719\) polygons.

## Assumptions and scope

The ambient coordinate box is fixed, with all vertex coordinates integral. Full-dimensional means positive Euclidean area. Each convex polygon is counted once by its coordinate vertex cycle in counterclockwise order, not once per subset that generates it. A generic objective assigns distinct values to adjacent vertices; this is sufficient for the strict linear-objective monotone diameter. A nongeneric objective is also checked with the convention that any vertex of its maximizing face is a terminal sink and zero-length paths are allowed. The claim concerns dimension two and grid parameter exactly three; it does not claim a general dimension or grid-width bound.

## Proof

**Finite completeness.** A convex lattice polygon contained in the square must have every extreme point in \(G\), which contains sixteen points. Therefore it occurs among the \(2^{16}\) subsets of \(G\). In `verify_monotone_census.py`, each subset with at least three points is converted, using the monotone-chain convex-hull algorithm and exact signed integer cross products, to the unique counterclockwise sequence of strict corner vertices. Collinear intermediate points are removed. Taking a set of these sequences therefore lists precisely all distinct admissible polygons, without double-counting subsets of their lattice points. The computed set has cardinality \(2719\).

**Finite exhaustive objective reduction.** Fix one of these polygons. The only objective directions at which the sign of a directed edge can change are lines orthogonal to its edges. On the objective-direction circle, mark each primitive integer outward edge normal and its negative. Between any consecutive marked rays, the sign of \(c\cdot(w-v)\) for each polygon edge \(vw\) is constant. These rays partition all possible nonzero objectives into the ray directions and open sectors. The exact procedure orders primitive normals by the upper-half-plane predicate and integer determinant sign. For consecutive normals \(u,v\), the positive integer direction
\[
c_{u,v}=\|v\|_1u+\|u\|_1v
\]
lies strictly inside their open sector: the angle gap is less than \(\pi\) because a full-dimensional polygon supplies more than one unoriented edge direction. The procedure checks both every critical ray \(u\) and its interior representative \(c_{u,v}\). Thus no real objective direction is omitted by restricting to these exact integer directions. For generic objectives the interior representatives alone suffice.

**Exact shortest-path computation.** Given a direction, evaluate every integer vertex height exactly. In descending order of height, set the distance of a maximal-height vertex to zero. At every other vertex \(v\), all strict outgoing arcs lead to already processed vertices, and the shortest increasing-path recursion is
\[
d_c(v)=1+\min_{w\sim v,\ c\cdot w>c\cdot v}d_c(w).
\]
A convex polygon has no nonoptimal local maximum for a linear functional: its tangent cone at each vertex is generated by its two incident edge directions, so at a nonmaximal vertex at least one incident edge strictly increases the objective. The implementation asserts this for every case. Accordingly, dynamic programming computes the exact minimum length, not an arbitrary improving route. The maximum over all vertex distances, rays, and sector samples is precisely the defined monotone diameter (and also verifies the nongeneric extension).

**Certificate and enumeration.** The accompanying executable, using only Python standard-library integer arithmetic, checks all input subsets, all distinct polygon hulls, and every direction chamber, then asserts the complete seven-stratum table displayed above. In particular, it finds just one eight-vertex polygon, whose vertices appear in the displayed order, and its shortest-path distance vector for \(c=(3,1)\) is \((4,3,2,1,0,1,2,3)\). It separately scans only interior-sector objectives and asserts that this produces the same monotone diameter for every polygon. These finite assertions, coupled with the preceding exhaustive-coverage lemmas, constitute a complete finite proof of all claimed integer counts and uniqueness. The supplied `verify_jarvis_independently.py` corroborates completeness with independently implemented gift-wrapping hull extraction and checks the computed value against each nonzero integer objective in the square \([-8,8]^2\); that secondary sample check is corroboration, not the exhaustive proof.

## Verification

With Python 3, run `python verify_monotone_census.py` and then `python verify_jarvis_independently.py` from a directory containing both embedded files. The main certificate prints `CERT_OK polygons 2719` and an exact stratification matching the above table, followed by `UNIQUE_EXTREMAL` with the stated eight vertices and distance vector. The independent checker prints `independent hulls 2719` and `independent objective sample consistency 2719`. Both checks were executed successfully using the files included in this package. Integer cross products, integer objectives, comparison of exact integers and exhaustive enumeration avoid floating-point or tolerance assumptions.

## Relationship to prior work

Black, *Monotone Diameters of Lattice Polytopes* (arXiv:2609.08647v1, first public 2026-09-08), constructs exponentially large monotone diameter examples in \([0,3]^{6d}\), highlighting grid parameter three as a threshold. It neither enumerates polygons in the planar three-grid nor states the present sharp planar diameter or equality classification. Black, *Small Shadows of Lattice Polytopes* (arXiv:2204.09129), establishes polynomial bounds at grid parameter two in arbitrary dimension; its hypotheses differ. Chadder and Deza, *Computational determination of the largest lattice polytope diameter* (arXiv:1704.01687), study the undirected edge-graph diameter: shortest paths without monotonicity do not imply the present directed optimum distances. Research-record searches for equivalent planar lattice-grid diameter data and directed monotone paths did not identify a result covering the seven-stratum table. Absence from searched sources cannot establish global historical priority.

## Limitations

The classification is finite, for exactly one fixed coordinate grid, and is not a bound for arbitrary real-coordinate polygons, for larger grids, or for higher dimensions. It is a computationally certified theorem with an explicit finite exhaustiveness argument, not a short closed-form derivation of the seven counts. The uniqueness statement is for coordinate polygons in the given box, not a count of affine-unimodular equivalence classes. Historical priority needs further expert review; no independent scientific audit is asserted.
