# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For 12 antipodal icosahedron vertices at radius R, every projected shadow has diameter 2R sqrt(1-m(U)^2), m(U)=min_i |U·a_i|. Hence the upper-tail event is the union of six spherical equator bands. All 15 absolute pairwise axis dots equal 1/sqrt(5); independent enumeration of 20 three-axis Gram matrices gives ten minimax thresholds t*=sqrt((1-2/sqrt(5))/3) and ten 1/sqrt(5), establishing no positive-area triple overlap on 0<=t<=t*. Pair-band derivative is 2/pi times [asin(phi*t/sqrt(1-t²))+asin(t/(phi*sqrt(1-t²)))], with primitive J_phi+J_1/phi. Inclusion-exclusion gives exactly 6t-30/pi [J_phi+J_1/phi]. Direct differentiation and Taylor series give the claimed t²,t^4 and near-maximal-diameter coefficients; strict endpoint and real inverse-trigonometric domains checked. The result is ONLY this upper branch and does not claim a full CDF.

## originality

PASS

A regular icosahedron planar-shadow diameter is not the same random variable as the directional width of its one-dimensional projection, or the diameter of a CENTRAL SECTION. Finch (2016) and Kabluchko–Litvak–Zaporozhets (2016) study regular-polytope width moments/distributions for random LINES, not this maximum length within a random plane. Published icosahedral central-section type record classifies polygon types, not planar projected diameter. The finite six-belt union and its sharp first triple-touch threshold were not covered by inspected primary results. Originality is best-of-knowledge; older six-great-circle arrangement monographs could conceal an equivalent zone-union law.
