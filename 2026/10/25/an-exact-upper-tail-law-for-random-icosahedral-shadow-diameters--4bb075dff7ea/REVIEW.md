# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The projected-diameter identity follows from central symmetry and maximization over all twelve projected vertices. The six equiangular axes have pairwise absolute inner product \(1/\sqrt5\); an exact Gram-matrix minimax argument separates ten positive-sign from ten negative-sign triples and identifies the first triple-belt threshold. Pairwise inclusion-exclusion is therefore exact before and at that threshold. Differentiating the spherical-pair-band probability and integrating the explicit inverse-sine primitive reproduces the closed formula. Independent direct-vertex, Gram, quadrature and Haar-sampling checks pass. Boundary equalities carry zero Haar measure, and the theorem is deliberately limited to this exact interval.

## Originality

PASS relative to inspected evidence. The most relevant pre-existing full texts treat line-projection widths and their moments, not the diameter of a two-dimensional icosahedral shadow. Published adjacent results on octahedral projection inradius/diameter and icosahedral central-section polygon types likewise do not imply this statistic. The older six-great-circle geometry is acknowledged rather than claimed. Searches for shadow diameter, the six equiangular vertex axes, and spherical belt-union equivalences found no dominating law. Priority is provisional because the referenced 2012 geometric monograph was not inspected in full and publication indexing is imperfect.

## Value

PASS. The formula is exact on a nontrivial, maximal interval terminating at the first genuine triple intersection, not merely a small-threshold Taylor coefficient. At its endpoint its tail probability is approximately \(0.7441\), covering nearly three quarters of random orientations. It yields finite-threshold probabilities, the geometrically sharp phase boundary and higher endpoint coefficients for a canonical Platonic solid, and makes a concrete distinction between single-direction width and planar-shadow diameter statistics.

## Limits

No claim is made about the remainder of the diameter law, anisotropic solid deformations, or independent external validation. The numerical computations support rather than prove the result.
