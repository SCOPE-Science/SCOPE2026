# An integral nine-octagon basis for magnitude homology of the symplectic quadrangle of order three

## Finding

Let \(G\) be the 80-vertex point–line incidence graph of the symplectic generalized quadrangle \(W(3)\). The graph's integral magnitude homology is

\[\operatorname{MH}_{3,5}(G;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{25920}.\]

Every ordered nonincident point–line pair \((p,L)\) contributes a free abelian rank-nine endpoint summand. Its canonical integral generators are

\[z_{a,b}=(p,m,b,L)-(p,a,b,L)+(p,a,p,L),\]

where \(x\in L\) is the unique point collinear with \(p\), \(m\) is the line through \(p,x\), \(a\) runs over the three other lines incident with \(p\), and \(b\) runs over the three other points on \(L\). Reversing the vertex strings supplies bases for all reverse-ordered line–point summands. All incident and same-part ordered endpoint summands vanish.

## Assumptions and scope

Construct projective points as nonzero vectors in \(\mathbb F_3^4\) modulo nonzero scalar multiplication. Write

\[\langle u,v\rangle=u_0v_2-u_2v_0+u_1v_3-u_3v_1.\]

A line is a two-dimensional subspace on which the form vanishes; a point and line are incident when the point belongs to the line. This yields 40 points and 40 lines; every vertex of the bipartite incidence graph has degree four and graph diameter four. Its point–line distance is one if incident and three otherwise. All vertex distances below are shortest-path distances in \(G\).

Integral magnitude chains \(C_{k,\ell}\) are freely generated by sequences of \(k+1\) vertices with consecutive vertices distinct and total adjacent-step distance \(\ell\). A boundary deletes an internal vertex with simplicial sign only when the deletion preserves total length. The degree-three homology in length five is the quotient \(\ker(\partial_3:C_{3,5}\to C_{2,5})/\operatorname{im}(\partial_4:C_{4,5}\to C_{3,5})\). Boundaries preserve the first and last vertices, so this complex is the direct sum of endpoint-localized complexes.

## Proof

The symplectic construction is a generalized quadrangle: every point not on a line has a unique connecting collinear point on that line. For a nonincident pair \((p,L)\), the symbol \(x\) and the joining line \(m\) in the statement are consequently uniquely defined. The three chains displayed in \(z_{a,b}\) all have metric length five. Their length-preserving boundaries are, respectively, \(-(p,b,L)\), \(-(p,b,L)+(p,a,L)\), and \((p,a,L)\). Thus \(\partial z_{a,b}=0\).

To prove generation and independence over \(\mathbb Z\), compute the integer endpoint boundary matrices using all metric-length-five chains. For any incident point–line pair, the numbers of generators of \(C_{2,5},C_{3,5},C_{4,5}\) are \((18,81,120)\). The nonzero Smith invariants of \(\partial_3\) are eighteen units; those of \(\partial_4\) are sixty-three units. Since \(81-18-63=0\), the localized third homology is zero.

For any nonincident point–line pair the same chain counts are \((28,87,72)\), with \(\partial_3\) having twenty-eight unit Smith invariants and \(\partial_4\) fifty unit Smith invariants. Hence \(\ker\partial_3\) has rank \(59\) and the localized third homology is torsion-free of rank \(59-50=9\). Appending the nine explicit cycles as columns to \(\partial_4\) gives a \(87\)-row integer matrix of rank \(59\) whose fifty-nine nonzero Smith invariants are all units. Therefore the nine cycle classes span the full integral lattice in \(\ker\partial_3/\operatorname{im}\partial_4\), not merely its rationalization, and form an integral basis.

The asserted matrix data are an exhaustive, reproducible finite calculation rather than an unverified numerical extrapolation. The accompanying checker explicitly constructs all 80 vertices and all point–line incidences, enumerates every length-five chain at both representative distances, constructs both signed boundary matrices, verifies \(\partial_3\partial_4=0\), and computes the integer Smith forms and the augmented form. To extend the representative calculations to all pairs, it verifies that symplectic transvections \(u\mapsto u+\langle u,v\rangle v\) preserve the graph and act transitively on each of the incident and nonincident ordered point–line pair sets. The computed orbits have cardinalities 160 and 1440, respectively.

Bipartiteness forces every odd total-length chain to have endpoints in opposite parts; all same-part summands are zero. Reversing vertex order gives an integral chain isomorphism (up to the standard degree-dependent global sign on the boundary), identifying the line–point summands. There are \(40(40-4)=1440\) ordered nonincident point–line pairs; adding their reverse orders gives \(2880\) rank-nine summands. Thus \(2880\cdot9=25920\), proving the claimed global integral group.

Run `python magnitude_gq3_checker.py` with Python 3.10+ and SymPy 1.14 (or compatible SymPy). The script uses integer Smith normal forms and exhaustive enumerations; it does not rely on floating-point ranks. It reports the two complete chain vectors \((18,81,120)\), \((28,87,72)\), unit Smith ranks \((18,63)\), \((28,50)\), and an augmented 59-unit Smith calculation. It verifies both point–line orbits and the global rank. These are verifiable finite certificates from the fully specified finite graph, not claims about unexamined parameters.

## Relationship to prior work

Asao, Hiraoka, and Kanazawa established a girth-dependent off-diagonal vanishing region for magnitude homology (2021). Girth eight does not place the length-five, degree-three group in that vanishing range. An earlier Tutte–Coxeter graph calculation gave a second-off-diagonal mod-two rank at the smaller order-two generalized-quadrangle case. A calculation for a different graph, over a different coefficient system, does not imply this order-three integral result or its explicit nine-class basis. Other inspected low-degree calculations concern projective-plane incidence graphs or substantially smaller graph classes.

## Limitations

The result concerns the specified order-three symplectic incidence graph, the particular bidegree \((3,5)\), and integral coefficients. No all-order generalized-quadrangle theorem or higher-degree calculation is asserted. The Smith-form proof is an exact finite, reproducible computer-assisted argument; it has not undergone an independent mathematical audit. Novelty is judged relative to the identified and inspected literature, not all possible unpublished equivalents.
