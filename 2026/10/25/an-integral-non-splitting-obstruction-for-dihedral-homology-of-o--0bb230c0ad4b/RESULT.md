# An integral non-splitting obstruction for dihedral homology of odd-cycle cut complexes

## Finding
For every odd integer \(n\ge7\) and even integer \(k\) satisfying \(4\le k\le n-3\), set \(q=n-k\), which is odd and at least three. The natural dihedral action on the ordinary cycle cut complex gives a canonical exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\widetilde H_{q-1}(\Delta_k(C_n);\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow\bigwedge^q U_n
\xrightarrow{\pi}\operatorname{Ind}_{\langle s\rangle}^{D_n}\varepsilon_q
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Here \(U_n=\ker(\mathbb Z^n\xrightarrow{\sum}\mathbb Z)\), and \(s\) is the reflection \(i\mapsto k-1-i\pmod n\) stabilizing the distinguished missing facet. The stabilizer sign is \(\varepsilon_q(s)=(-1)^{(q-1)/2}\).

**This integral short exact sequence is non-split. It is also non-split after tensoring with \(\mathbb F_2\)**. This obstruction is invisible to the known rational dihedral-character formula, since rational sequences split by Maschke's theorem.

## Assumptions and scope
Vertices of \(C_n\) are \(0,\ldots,n-1\) modulo \(n\); \(D_n\) is generated by the rotation \(i\mapsto i+1\) and reflection \(i\mapsto-i\). The ordinary \(k\)-cut complex has facets precisely the complements of disconnected induced \(k\)-vertex subsets. Let \(B=\{0,\ldots,k-1\}\), \(F=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\setminus B\), and \(s(i)=k-1-i\pmod n\). Let \(\Sigma\) be the full \((q-1)\)-skeleton of the simplex on the same vertices. Its top chain group uses oriented \(q\)-element faces.

## Proof
**The canonical sequence.** The only \(q\)-element subsets excluded from \(\Delta_k(C_n)\) are the \(n\) complements of consecutive \(k\)-vertex intervals. For the stated range, every face of size at most \(q-1\) is present, so \(\Delta_k(C_n)\subset\Sigma\) share the complete \((q-2)\)-skeleton. The pair \((\Sigma,\Delta_k(C_n))\) has one free relative \((q-1)\)-chain for each excluded face, with zero relative differential. The shellability theorem for cycle cut complexes implies \(\widetilde H_{q-2}(\Delta_k(C_n);\mathbb Z)=0\). The long exact sequence of the pair therefore gives
\[
0\to\widetilde H_{q-1}(\Delta_k(C_n);\mathbb Z)
\to\widetilde H_{q-1}(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)
\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathbb Z[\text{oriented missing faces}]\to0.
\]
The well-known augmented-simplex chain contraction identifies \(\widetilde H_{q-1}(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)\cong\bigwedge^q U_n\), naturally under vertex permutations. The dihedral group acts transitively on missing faces. The reflection stabilizer of \(F\) is generated by \(s\); its action on the orientation of the odd-cardinality set \(F\) is the sign of \((q-1)/2\) transpositions. This identifies the quotient with the induced signed rank-one module in the statement. All maps are \(D_n\)-equivariant and integral.

**The mod-two obstruction.** Since \(n\) is odd, \(s\) has exactly one fixed vertex \(v\). Because \(k\) is even, every vertex of \(B\) is paired with another vertex of \(B\), so \(v\in F\). Put \(R=F\setminus\{v\}\), an \(s\)-invariant set of size \(q-1\). Suppose a \(D_n\)-equivariant section existed, even just over \(\mathbb F_2\). Let \(w\) be the image of the missing-face basis vector \([F]\). Since \(s[F]=\varepsilon_q(s)[F]\), the reduction \(\bar w\) of the lifted simplicial cycle is \(s\)-fixed modulo two. Its coefficient at \(F\) is one, because \(\pi(w)=[F]\).

Evaluate the boundary of \(\bar w\) at the oriented face \(R\), disregarding signs modulo two. The coefficient is the sum of the coefficients of the \(q\)-faces \(R\cup\{x\}\) as \(x\) ranges over vertices outside \(R\). The extension with \(x=v\) is exactly \(F\), with coefficient one. All remaining extensions have \(x\in B\); these vertices form two-element \(s\)-orbits, and the corresponding coefficients agree since \(s\bar w=\bar w\). Their contributions therefore sum to zero modulo two. Thus the boundary coefficient at \(R\) is one, contradicting \(\partial\bar w=0\). This proves non-splitting modulo two. An integral section would reduce to one modulo two, so integral non-splitting follows. \(\square\)

## Verification
The standalone checker `check_nonsplit.py` constructs all \(q\)-faces, their reflection orbits, all mod-two cycle equations, and the missing-facet lifting equations. It solves the complete linear systems for \(5\le n\le11\), \(3\le k\le n-2\), confirming non-solvability for all six odd-\(n\), even-\(k\) cases in this range. These finite calculations are independent checks, not a substitute for the general parity proof. For the smallest instance \((n,k,q)=(7,4,3)\), the middle integral lattice has rank \(\binom63=20\), the quotient rank seven, and the kernel rank thirteen.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer–Denker–Jelić Milutinović–Rowlands–Sundaram–Xue established shellability, sphere counts and the cyclic rational homology representation for cut complexes of cycles (Theorems 7.11, 7.13, 7.15). Bayer–Denker–Jelić Milutinović–Sundaram–Xue subsequently determined the *full dihedral rational character* in Theorem 6.6 of their sequel; that complete rational representation is **prior work and is not claimed here**. The present statement concerns an integral extension class: the rational character identity does not specify whether the natural sequence splits over \(\mathbb Z[D_n]\), and the explicit fixed-face parity obstruction establishes that it cannot in the stated family.

## Limitations
No classification of the extension class's exact order, splitting for other parities, or torsion in unrelated cut complexes is asserted. The argument proves non-splitting of this canonical exact sequence, not that the middle and end modules never admit other abstract decompositions. The result remains subject to comparison with potentially unindexed integral-equivariant literature.
