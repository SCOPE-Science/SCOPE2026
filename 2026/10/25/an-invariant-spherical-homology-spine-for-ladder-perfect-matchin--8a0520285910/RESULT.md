# An invariant spherical homology spine for ladder perfect-matching complexes

## Finding
Let \(k\geq 1\) be an integer, let \(L_{2k}=P_2\square P_{2k}\) be the two-row ladder with \(2k\) columns, and let \(X_{2k}=\mathcal M_p(L_{2k})\) be its perfect-matching complex. Thus a simplex of \(X_{2k}\) is a set of graph edges contained in at least one perfect matching. Let \(s\) interchange the rows and let \(r\) reverse the columns. Their actions generate a Klein-four group \(G=\langle s,r\rangle\).

For each consecutive two-column block \(j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\), let \(A_j\) be its two rung edges and let \(B_j\) be its two horizontal edges. The subcomplex generated by perfect matchings using only within-block edges is
\[
Y_k=\bigast_{j=1}^{k}\bigl(\Delta(A_j)\sqcup\Delta(B_j)\bigr)\subseteq X_{2k}.
\]
The inclusion \(Y_k\hookrightarrow X_{2k}\) is a homotopy equivalence and is \(G\)-equivariant. The invariant subcomplex \(Y_k\) has a \(G\)-equivariant deformation retraction to an embedded \((k-1)\)-sphere obtained by taking the join of the two barycenters of the edge simplices in each block. In particular the sphere inclusion induces an isomorphism on all integral homology groups of \(X_{2k}\). **No claim is made that the inclusion is a \(G\)-homotopy equivalence.**

On the generator of the unique nonzero reduced integral homology group
\[
\widetilde H_{k-1}(X_{2k};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,
\]
the two geometric involutions act by
\[
s_* = +1,\qquad r_*=(-1)^{\binom{k}{2}},\qquad (sr)_*=(-1)^{\binom{k}{2}}.
\]
All other reduced integral homology groups vanish. As a consequence, the symmetry quotient has reduced rational homology
\[
\widetilde H_i(X_{2k}/G;\mathbb Q)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Q,&i=k-1\text{ and } k\equiv0,1\pmod4,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Equivalently, the nonzero rational class survives the quotient exactly when the column number is \(0\) or \(2\) modulo \(8\).

## Assumptions and scope
The graph \(P_m\) is the path on \(m\) vertices and \(\square\) denotes Cartesian graph product. Number the ladder columns \(1,\ldots,n\). Let \(a_i\) denote the rung in column \(i\), and let \(b_i,c_i\) denote the upper and lower horizontal edges from column \(i\) to \(i+1\). A perfect matching is necessarily a domino tiling: it begins either with the rung \(a_1\) or with both edges \(b_1,c_1\). Define \(X_n=\mathcal M_p(P_2\square P_n)\) for positive integers \(n\).

For \(n=2k\), set
\[
A_j=\{a_{2j-1},a_{2j}\},\qquad
B_j=\{b_{2j-1},c_{2j-1}\}.
\]
The symbol \(\Delta(S)\) means the full simplex on the finite vertex set \(S\). The union \(\Delta(A_j)\sqcup\Delta(B_j)\) is disjoint because its vertices denote four different graph edges. The group action on graph edges induces an action by simplicial automorphisms on the perfect-matching complex. The quotient refers to the ordinary topological quotient of its geometric realization.

## Proof
**Step 1 (two contractible pieces).** For every \(n\geq3\), let \(U_n\) be the subcomplex generated by perfect matchings using \(a_1\), and let \(V_n\) be the subcomplex generated by perfect matchings using \(b_1,c_1\). Since all perfect matchings begin in exactly one of these two ways,
\[
X_n=U_n\cup V_n.
\]
By removing the initial tiles, these subcomplexes are, with their natural coordinate embeddings,
\[
U_n=\Delta(\{a_1\})*X_{n-1},\quad
V_n=\Delta(\{b_1,c_1\})*X_{n-2},\quad
U_n\cap V_n=X_{n-2}.
\]
The first two are cones. To check the intersection, a face common to both pieces cannot contain \(a_1,b_1,c_1\); it also cannot contain \(a_2\), because the second piece has the first two columns already matched. It is therefore supported in columns \(3,\ldots,n\), and any such face extending to a perfect matching there also extends to the first piece by the rung \(a_2\). This proves the stated equality, rather than just a homological equivalence.

**Step 2 (blockwise inclusion).** For \(n=2k\geq4\), split \(Y_k\) into the subcomplexes \(U'_k=\Delta(A_1)*Y_{k-1}\) and \(V'_k=\Delta(B_1)*Y_{k-1}\). Both are cones; their intersection is exactly \(Y_{k-1}\). Under the natural inclusions into \(U_{2k},V_{2k}\), the map on intersections is the smaller blockwise inclusion \(Y_{k-1}\hookrightarrow X_{2k-2}\). For \(k=1\), \(Y_1=X_2\) consists of two disjoint edge simplices. Inductively, the three maps from \(U'_k,V'_k,U'_k\cap V'_k\) to \(U_{2k},V_{2k},U_{2k}\cap V_{2k}\) are homotopy equivalences: the first two because their nonempty domains and targets are contractible, the third by induction. The gluing lemma for CW complexes (the homotopy-pushout invariance of unions of subcomplexes) proves that the induced inclusion \(Y_k\hookrightarrow X_{2k}\) is a homotopy equivalence.

**Step 3 (invariant sphere).** Each local component \(\Delta(A_j)\) and \(\Delta(B_j)\) is a closed interval. Contracting each to its barycenter, uniformly over blocks, yields a \(G\)-equivariant strong deformation retraction of \(Y_k\) onto
\[
Z_k=\bigast_{j=1}^{k}\{\operatorname{bar}(\Delta(A_j)),\operatorname{bar}(\Delta(B_j))\}
\cong S^{k-1}.
\]
The row swap fixes both local component labels and their barycenters. Column reversal sends block \(j\) to block \(k+1-j\) and also preserves both local component labels. Thus \(s\) acts trivially on \(Z_k\), whereas \(r\) reverses the order of its \(k\) zero-sphere join factors.

**Step 4 (integral action).** An adjacent interchange of two zero-sphere factors changes the orientation of their join by \(-1\): it exchanges two suspended degree-one generators in the reduced homological join formula. Reversing all \(k\) factors involves exactly \(\binom{k}{2}\) transpositions. Thus the top integral homology line of \(Z_k\) has \(s\)-character \(+1\) and \(r\)-character \((-1)^{\binom{k}{2}}\). Both the retraction of \(Y_k\) and its inclusion in \(X_{2k}\) induce integral homology isomorphisms and commute with \(s,r\). This proves the exact character on \(X_{2k}\) without assuming an equivariant homotopy inverse exists.

**Step 5 (quotient).** A finite simplicial group action can be barycentrically subdivided so that simplicial stabilizers act without inversions. Over \(\mathbb Q\), quotient simplicial chains agree with the coinvariants of the original chains. Because finite-group coinvariants over \(\mathbb Q\) are exact and naturally isomorphic to invariants, the reduced rational homology of the quotient equals the invariant subspace of reduced rational homology. The one-dimensional character in Step 4 has an invariant generator if and only if \((-1)^{\binom{k}{2}}=+1\), which occurs exactly for \(k\equiv0,1\pmod4\). This proves the quotient formula.

## Verification
The standalone script `checker.py` enumerates every perfect matching, constructs every face and verifies that the blockwise facets belong to the complete complex. For \(n=2,4,6,8,10\), the respective nonempty face counts are \(6,60,546,4920,44286\). Direct augmented oriented-chain Lefschetz sums verify that row swap acts by \(+1\), and that column reversal acts by \(+1,-1,-1,+1,+1\). Independent binary boundary reductions for \(n=2,4,6,8\) give exactly one nonzero reduced Betti number, in degree \(n/2-1\). The script prints `VERIFY_OK` after all assertions pass. These finite checks are ancillary; the induction and join-orientation argument establish the theorem for every \(k\geq1\).

## Relationship to prior work
Chandrakar and Singh, *Perfect Matching Complexes of Polygonal Line Tilings*, arXiv:2407.05809 (first posted July 8, 2024), Theorem 3.3 and its appendix, already prove the **nonequivariant** homotopy classification: the even-width ladder complex is a sphere and the odd-width ladder complex is contractible. Those results are not claimed as new here. The new statement is an explicit symmetry-invariant blockwise sphere carrying the integral homology, the complete row-exchange/column-reversal homology character, and its rational quotient consequence. A nonequivariant sphere classification does not determine which one-dimensional integral character the natural automorphism group realizes. The inspected theorem and appendix contain no statement of these actions or of this invariant geometric model.

Bayer, Jelić Milutinović and Vega, *Perfect Matching Complexes of Honeycomb Graphs*, arXiv:2209.02803 (first posted September 6, 2022), introduce the complexes and study honeycomb rather than two-row ladder symmetry. Other matching and ladder complexes must not be conflated with the perfect-matching complex of the Cartesian two-row path grid.

## Limitations
The map \(Y_k\hookrightarrow X_{2k}\) is proved to be an ordinary homotopy equivalence that is equivariant as a map; a \(G\)-equivariant homotopy equivalence is **not** proved. The quotient conclusion is rational, not an integral calculation of torsion in the quotient. The nonequivariant homotopy type is previously known, and equivalent unindexed equivariant results may exist. The checker covers finitely many widths and is not substituted for the all-width proof.
