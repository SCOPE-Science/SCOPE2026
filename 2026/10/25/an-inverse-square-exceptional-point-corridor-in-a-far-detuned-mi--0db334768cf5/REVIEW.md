# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim fixes positive tunnelling and pairing at equality, strictly unequal nonnegative lead loss rates, and a scaled far-detuned onsite-energy interval. The four-by-four matrix and quartic determinant are explicit. Exact rescaling gives the polynomial \(F(z,\ell,s)\); its limiting discriminant creates exactly two folds, and the analytic implicit function theorem resolves them without a numerical-only argument. Differentiation determines both coefficients of the corridor half-width. Uniform separation of the other two eigenvalues and the nonvanishing low-block Schur coupling establish the complete local eigenvalue count and genuine geometric defectiveness. Direct symbolic and numerical tests reproduced the identities and sample fold boundaries. The result neither asserts nor proves an arbitrary-detuning global phase diagram.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The 2024 Cayao–Aguado study (arXiv:2406.18974) established the model, non-Hermitian spectral pinning and exceptional points, and illustrated the unequal-dot detuning phase diagram. Its published text does not state the inverse-square large-remote-detuning corridor width, the next coefficient, or the quantified local two-to-zero imaginary-axis eigenvalue classification. The 2026 Cayao–Sato paper (arXiv:2606.23956) instead considers a phase-biased Josephson junction made of two minimal chains, not the present single-chain detuning asymptotics. The earlier Agarwal–Joglekar analysis (arXiv:2103.07058) treats parity–time-symmetric gain/loss in a different Kitaev chain. A relevant related gain/loss Kitaev claim concerns a balanced-gain/loss open chain, not an asymmetrically lossy two-dot system. None of these statements entails the new coefficient through a quoted existing theorem. General matrix perturbation, Schur complements and fold geometry are acknowledged as known tools. Residual risk remains from unindexed literature or an equivalent derivation not explicitly formulated in available sources.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The source's qualitative exceptional-point robustness becomes an exact calibration law: when the distant dot is detuned by \(R\), the tolerated local onsite mismatch collapses as \(R^{-2}\), not merely some unspecified small correction. The leading coefficient explicitly couples loss contrast and cotunnelling/pairing, and the next coefficient resolves finite-detuning calibration error. The associated sharp two-to-zero count and defective endpoints distinguish a genuine non-Hermitian spectral phase from merely small nonzero splitting. The statement is a motivated asymptotic boundary result, not an arbitrary parameter slice or a recomputation of a listed table.
