# An odd–even rotation-fixed filtration dichotomy for finite cyclic Vietoris–Rips complexes

## Finding

Let \(n\ge3\) and \(d\mid n\), with \(d\ge2\), and write \(m=n/d\). Give \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) the cycle graph distance \(\delta_n(a,b)=\min\{(a-b)\bmod n,(b-a)\bmod n\}\). Denote by \(K_n(r)=\operatorname{VR}_{\le r}(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,\delta_n)\) the inclusive Vietoris–Rips simplicial complex at integer scale \(0\le r\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). Let \(H_d\) be the order-\(d\) rotation group generated by the shift \(a\mapsto a+m\). Fixed points are **geometric fixed points** in the realization \(|K_n(r)|\), not fixed vertices.

**Odd-order subgroup.** If \(d=2s+1\), the fixed space is empty for \(r<sm\), and for \(sm\le r\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) there is a natural piecewise-linear homeomorphism
\[
|K_n(r)|^{H_d}\ \cong_{\mathrm{PL}}\ |K_m(r-sm)|.
\]
Here \(K_m(t)\) uses the same cycle-distance definition even for \(m=1,2\), with the evident point/two-point conventions. These homeomorphisms commute with all scale-inclusion maps. Thus the **entire fixed-point filtration** is an exactly shifted smaller cyclic Rips filtration, preceded by an empty stage.

**Even-order subgroup.** If \(d\) is even, then \(|K_n(r)|^{H_d}=\varnothing\) for \(r<n/2\), while at \(r=n/2\) the fixed space is piecewise-linearly the full simplex \(\Delta^{m-1}\). The filtration is constant thereafter.

For example, for \(n=45\) and \(d=3\), the fixed space appears at \(r=15\) as fifteen points, becomes the circle \(|C_{15}|\) at \(r=16\), and becomes contractible at \(r=22\). More generally, if \(d\) is odd and \(m\ge4\), a geometric fixed circle occurs at scale \(r=(d-1)m/2+1\). The fixed-point persistence of every odd-order rotational subgroup is therefore determined (in every homological degree and with every coefficient ring) by the established ordinary cyclic-Rips persistence after the exact time shift.

## Assumptions and scope

All complexes are **ordinary finite abstract simplicial complexes** realized using affine barycentric coordinates; no infinite-circle or metric-thickening model is substituted. The metric is the **shortest-path metric on the cycle of \(n\) vertices**, equivalently an integer-rescaling of geodesic distance between equally spaced points on a circle. The scale inequality is inclusive, so equalities at \(sm\) and \(n/2\) are part of the statement. At higher real scales the filtration is constant between integers, and the same assertion holds after rounding down to the last integer threshold. The cyclic subgroup is specified by its **order \(d\)**, not by an arbitrarily labeled rotation generator. In the odd-order case \(0\le r-sm\le\lfloor m/2\rfloor\), because \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor=sm+\lfloor m/2\rfloor\).

## Proof

**1. From geometric fixed points to orbit simplices.** The \(H_d\)-orbits of vertices of \(K_n(r)\) are \(O_a=\{a+jm\pmod n:0\le j<d\}\), one for each \(a\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\). A point of the geometric realization has unique barycentric coordinates on the original vertices. It is fixed by every group element precisely when these weights are constant on each orbit. Consequently its nonzero support is a union of entire orbits. A set of orbit labels \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\) supports fixed barycentric points precisely when \(\bigcup_{a\in A}O_a\) is a face of \(K_n(r)\). The map assigning equal mass within each orbit gives a canonical affine simplicial identification of this orbit-support complex with \(|K_n(r)|^{H_d}\). The identification is compatible with inclusions in \(r\).

**2. Even order.** If \(d\) is even, each orbit contains both \(a\) and its antipode \(a+n/2\), because \((d/2)m=n/2\). Thus no entire orbit is a face for \(r<n/2\), so no geometric fixed point exists there. At \(r=n/2\), every pair of original vertices is allowed and \(K_n(r)\) is the full simplex on \(n\) vertices. Its fixed barycentric subspace is the full simplex on the \(m\) orbit barycenters. This establishes both the boundary value and persistence.

**3. Odd-order orbit distances.** Assume \(d=2s+1\). Write \(q=b-a\pmod m\) with \(0\le q<m\). All differences between a vertex in \(O_a\) and one in \(O_b\) are the residues \(q+jm\pmod n\), for \(j=0,\dots,2s\). Viewed as points of the cyclic interval of length \(n=(2s+1)m\), the largest value of \(\min\{x,n-x\}\) among these differences equals
\[
sm+\min\{q,m-q\}=sm+\delta_m(a,b).
\]
Indeed, the antipodal half-turn lies at \((s+\tfrac12)m\); the nearest residues on the displayed arithmetic progression have distance \(m/2-\min\{q,m-q\}\) from that midpoint. Subtracting this from \(n/2\) gives the formula. This argument remains valid for \(q=0\), proving that the diameter of an individual orbit is exactly \(sm\), and for \(m=1\).

**4. Exact orbit-support criterion and filtration.** For \(r<sm\), not even one full orbit is a face, so the fixed space is empty. For \(r\ge sm\), each orbit is a face, and by Step 3 the union of orbit labels \(A\) is a face precisely when
\[
\delta_m(a,b)\le r-sm\quad\text{for every }a,b\in A.
\]
This is *exactly* the defining condition for \(A\) to be a simplex in \(K_m(r-sm)\). Thus the orbit-support complex itself is simplicially isomorphic to \(K_m(r-sm)\), rather than just homotopy equivalent. Every identification sends the barycenter of \(O_a\) to the vertex \(a\) of \(K_m\) and is therefore unchanged as \(r\) increases. This proves the claimed natural PL equivalence of entire filtrations.

**5. Circle consequence.** If \(m\ge4\), then \(K_m(1)\) is the cycle graph \(C_m\), whose realization is a circle. Set \(r=sm+1\) to obtain the stated fixed circle. The fifteen-point example follows from \(K_{15}(0)\), \(K_{15}(1)\), and the full-simplex threshold \(\lfloor45/2\rfloor=22\). No homotopy-type assertion about other stages is required for the proof: their full types and persistence already follow from the explicit smaller-complex identification and the cited cyclic-graph-power results.

For each possible scale it independently compares the actual compatibility of unions of orbits with the theorem's predicted orbit-support edges. Execution produced the marker `CYCLIC_RIPS_ROTATION_FIXED_VERIFIED 90 2637 50953 853035`. The last three numbers count orbit-diameter tests, ordered coset-pair distance tests, and scale-dependent pair-compatibility tests. This corroborates the combinatorial lemma on finite instances. The universal assertion is proved in Steps 1–5, not inferred from this enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work

Adams–Lagoda–Moy–Sadovek–De Saha [1, §8, Question 3] specifically pose persistent dihedral-equivariant cohomology of evenly spaced finite circle Rips complexes as a research direction. They classify fixed sets of **continuous-circle metric thickenings** in [1, §7], which are not the finite vertex-set geometric fixed spaces computed here. The present theorem answers a separate, narrower ingredient: every **rotational-subgroup geometric fixed filtration** in the discrete finite problem, but **not** the reflection-fixed filtrations, Borel equivariant cohomology ring, or complete dihedral-equivariant persistence.

Adamaszek et al. [2, §8] identify these complexes with cycle-power clique complexes and recall their ordinary homotopy classification; [2, §7] determines a dihedral action on homology for a **circular-arc nerve model**. Neither statement describes the geometric fixed-point spaces of every rotational subgroup of the finite Rips flag complexes. In particular, a character of a representation is not a PL classification of simultaneous fixed loci or their persistence inclusions. Adamaszek [3] and Adamaszek–Adams [4] supply ordinary cyclic homotopy types; the regular-polygon work [5] studies related Rips complexes for polygonal spaces; and [6] studies metric thickenings under group actions and quotients. Those are acknowledged structural antecedents. The elementary orbit-barycenter observation by itself is standard; the specific odd–even cyclic **threshold and exact smaller-cycle filtration formula** are the additional assertions established here.

## Limitations

The theorem is an exact statement about subgroup-fixed geometric realizations for cyclic rotations. It does not classify fixed points of reflections or of noncyclic dihedral subgroups, does not compute Borel equivariant cohomology, and does not assert an equivariant simple-homotopy equivalence of the ambient complexes. The metric is graph distance on an evenly spaced cyclic sample, not Euclidean chord distance at arbitrary noninteger scales; the latter has the same filtration ordering under a monotone rescaling, but its numerical thresholds differ. Known ordinary cyclic-Rips classifications are invoked only to describe the significance of the reduction, not as hidden premises of the proof. An independently published equivalent fixed-space formula not identified in the checked sources remains a novelty risk; the result has not undergone independent mathematical review.
