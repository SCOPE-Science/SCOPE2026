# Anti-forcing polynomial and symmetry-orbit profile of the four-letter permutohedron

## Finding
Let \(G\) be the graph whose vertices are the \(24\) permutations of \(\{0,1,2,3\}\), with two vertices joined exactly when they differ by swapping adjacent positions. This is the one-skeleton of the classical three-dimensional permutohedron, also known as the truncated-octahedral graph. For a perfect matching \(M\) of \(G\), define its anti-forcing number \(\operatorname{af}(G,M)\) to be the minimum cardinality of a set of **edges outside \(M\)** whose deletion leaves \(M\) as the unique perfect matching.

The complete anti-forcing polynomial is
\[
\boxed{A_G(z)=\sum_{M\text{ perfect}}z^{\operatorname{af}(G,M)}=4z^4+16z^5+44z^6+48z^7+39z^8+16z^9+2z^{10}.}
\]
There are exactly \(169\) perfect matchings. Under the natural order-\(48\) action generated by relabeling the four symbols and reversing the four positions, these matchings split into exactly \(17\) orbits. The following is their **complete classification**; each row is one distinct orbit, and the last column lists the vertex lengths of the cycles in the complementary spanning two-factor \(G-E(M)\).

| Orbit size | \(\operatorname{af}(G,M)\) | Complementary two-factor cycle lengths |
| ---: | ---: | :--- |
| 1 | 8 | \((4,4,4,4,4,4)\) |
| 2 | 10 | \((6,6,6,6)\) |
| 4 | 4 | \((12,12)\) |
| 4 | 5 | \((24)\) |
| 4 | 7 | \((6,6,12)\) |
| 4 | 9 | \((24)\) |
| 6 | 8 | \((12,12)\) |
| 8 | 6 | \((4,4,4,12)\) |
| 8 | 7 | \((8,8,8)\) |
| 8 | 8 | \((6,18)\) |
| 12 | 5 | \((4,10,10)\) |
| 12 | 6 | \((4,20)\) |
| 12 | 7 | \((24)\) |
| 12 | 9 | \((6,6,12)\) |
| 24 | 6 | \((8,16)\) |
| 24 | 7 | \((24)\) |
| 24 | 8 | \((6,18)\) |

In particular, precisely four matchings attain the minimum anti-forcing number \(4\), and precisely two attain the maximum \(10\). The complementary two-factor cycle type alone does **not** determine the anti-forcing number: a single Hamiltonian complementary cycle occurs with anti-forcing numbers \(5\), \(7\), and \(9\); the type \((12,12)\) occurs with values \(4\) and \(8\). These are exact finite-graph statements, not claims about other permutohedra or all \((4,6)\)-fullerenes.

## Assumptions and scope
A matching covers every vertex exactly once. The graph \(G\) is simple, undirected, connected, cubic and bipartite: parity of the inversion count of a permutation gives its two vertex classes, both of size \(12\). Thus \(G\) has \(24\) vertices and \(36\) edges. A matching edge is **never** an allowable anti-forcing deletion, because the surviving graph must retain \(M\). The group referred to in the theorem is specifically the \(48\) explicit bijections obtained by simultaneous symbol relabeling and optional position reversal, rather than an unspecified quotient under geometric rotations only.

## Proof
**1. Exhaustive enumeration of the perfect matchings.** Index vertices lexicographically by their permutation words and edges by scanning the three adjacent-position swaps and retaining each undirected edge once. A recursive procedure on the least uncovered vertex \(u\) branches over its at most three uncovered neighbors \(v\), chooses \(uv\), and removes both endpoints. Every perfect matching contains exactly one edge incident with \(u\), so induction on the number of uncovered vertices proves the enumeration is complete and disjoint. There are \(169\) terminal branches. Independently, form the \(12\)-by-\(12\) parity-bipartite adjacency matrix \(B\). Its permanent computed using the inclusion-exclusion identity
\[
\operatorname{per}(B)=\sum_{S\subseteq\{1,\dots,12\}}(-1)^{12-|S|}\prod_{i=1}^{12}\sum_{j\in S}B_{ij}
\]
is \(169\), cross-checking the cardinality without relying on the matching recursion.

**2. Converting anti-forcing into a directed feedback-arc minimum.** Fix a matching \(M\). The symmetric difference of \(M\) and any distinct perfect matching consists of disjoint even \(M\)-alternating cycles. Conversely, flipping edges along any such alternating cycle yields another perfect matching. It follows that an edge set outside \(M\) makes \(M\) unique exactly when it intersects every \(M\)-alternating cycle. Contract each of the \(12\) matching edges to a vertex and direct every nonmatching edge from its even-parity endpoint to its odd-parity endpoint. An alternating traversal goes from an even endpoint across a nonmatching edge to an odd endpoint, then across a matching edge to an even endpoint; hence alternating cycles correspond bijectively to directed cycles in the resulting \(12\)-vertex directed multigraph \(D_M\) (in fact there are no parallel ordered arcs here). Thus \(\operatorname{af}(G,M)\) equals the fewest arcs to delete to make \(D_M\) acyclic.

For any total ordering of \(V(D_M)\), deleting all arcs that run backward produces an acyclic digraph. Conversely, any deletion yielding an acyclic digraph gives a topological ordering of the surviving arcs, so the deleted set contains all backward arcs for that order. Therefore anti-forcing is exactly the minimum number of backward arcs over all \(12!\) possible orders. Let \(d(S)\) be this minimum for the subdigraph on a subset \(S\) of the contracted vertices. Choosing the *last* vertex \(v\in S\) gives the exact recurrence
\[
 d(\varnothing)=0,\qquad
 d(S)=\min_{v\in S}\left[d(S\setminus\{v\})+\#\{(v,u)\in E(D_M):u\in S\setminus\{v\}\}\right].
\]
Induction on \(|S|\) proves this recurrence enumerates every ordering and calculates the global optimum, not an approximation. The supplied program evaluates its \(2^{12}=4096\) states for each of the \(169\) matchings. Tallying the resulting exact integer anti-forcing numbers yields the displayed polynomial. Its coefficient sum is \(169\), agreeing with both matching counts.

**3. Symmetry orbit classification.** Every permutation of the four symbols preserves the adjacent-swap graph. Reversing all four positions also preserves it, and together they give \(24\cdot2=48\) distinct graph automorphisms. The verifier explicitly constructs and checks all \(48\) vertex and edge maps; for each unclassified perfect matching it computes its orbit by applying all \(48\) maps. The \(17\) orbits are disjoint and cover all \(169\) matchings. The anti-forcing number is invariant under automorphisms, and the complementary two-factor cycle lengths are also invariant. Evaluating these quantities on one representative of each orbit yields exactly the \(17\) rows of the table. The orbit-size weighted sums for anti-forcing values \(4,5,6,7,8,9,10\) are respectively \(4,16,44,48,39,16,2\), as asserted.

**4. Independent optimality computation for orbit representatives.** For each of the \(17\) representatives the verifier also constructs the \(168\) constraints \(E(N)\setminus E(M)\), one for each other perfect matching \(N\). An anti-forcing edge set must hit every constraint, and hitting them all suffices for uniqueness. A second exact algorithm removes redundant superset constraints and recursively branches on every candidate edge in a currently unhit smallest constraint, with memoization. This independent minimum-transversal computation agrees with the feedback-arc dynamic program on all \(17\) orbit representatives. Automorphism invariance then verifies the class-wise values for all \(169\) matchings. No random sampling is used.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_permutohedron.py` with Python 3.8 or later; it uses only the standard library. The program reconstructs the graph, checks all \(24\) vertices and \(36\) edges and bipartition sizes, enumerates all \(169\) distinct perfect matchings, computes the permanent independently, evaluates all subset-DP anti-forcing minima, computes all \(17\) natural symmetry orbits and their two-factor types, and runs the independent exact hitting-set solver on all \(17\) representatives. It asserts the displayed polynomial and orbit table and prints `VERIFY_OK` only if every check succeeds. Exhaustion is possible because the underlying finite graph has only \(24\) vertices and the contracted digraph has only \(12\).

## Relationship to prior work
Lei, Yeh and Zhang (2014) formalized the anti-forcing number of a fixed perfect matching and proved the alternating-cycle characterization and planar-bipartite minimax theorem. These foundational facts are used here, not claimed as new. Williams (2015) supplies standard adjacent-transposition permutohedron context. Existing studies of \((4,6)\)-fullerenes, notably Shi, Wang and Zhang (2017), imply the maximum anti-forcing value \(\lfloor24/3\rfloor+2=10\), so the maximum alone is **not** presented as novel. The 2022 study by Shi, Zhang and Zhao investigates anti-forcing **spectra** for classes of \((4,6)\)-fullerenes, not necessarily multiplicities. The contribution here is the **complete coefficient polynomial** and refined **natural symmetry orbit classification** for the specific graph, together with the demonstration that complementary two-factor cycle types do not determine its anti-forcing number. Searches of accessible scholarly material did not locate these exact counts.

## Limitations
This is an exact, finite computationally assisted proof for the specific \(24\)-vertex graph. It does not give an asymptotic formula, an infinite-family classification, or the full spectrum for any other graph. The orbit classification is relative to the explicitly defined natural \(48\)-element group. The 2017 and 2022 papers on \((4,6)\)-fullerenes were available only via their abstracts during this comparison; their inaccessible full texts could in principle contain relevant specialized tables, so previously published exact multiplicities cannot be ruled out with certainty. The result is subject to ordinary independent mathematical audit; no independent audit is asserted here.
