# Scientific review: antipodal distance tails on cyclic spherical footballs

## Correctness — PASS
The quotient-distance cosine law follows directly from minimizing over the finite rotation group. For every cyclic order at least two, nonnegative folded azimuthal cosine localizes near-diameter pairs *only* to opposite polar caps. Area normalization gives the factor of one half; rescaling and dominated convergence reduce the endpoint probability to a positive quadrant integral. The explicit evaluation and strict monotonicity have been independently reconstructed, with the round-sphere case treated separately. Computations from the embedded standalone scripts corroborate four cyclic orders, 1,600 direct distance comparisons and 16 quadrature values. Remaining limit: finite computations do not prove asymptotics, which are instead analytically established.

## Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic risk
The 2006 orbifold-geodesic paper fixes relevant orbifold geometry, Bacher–Sturm supplies the spherical cosine-law framework, and 2020 lens-space work covers free cyclic quotients of a three-sphere. None of those inspected claims gives the near-diameter fourth-order probability law on the two-dimensional fixed-point quotient or the sharp strictly order-identifying coefficient. Published search results do not rule out an obscure equivalent calculation; that is a genuine residual bibliographic risk, not a basis for claiming uniqueness merely from a search failure.

## Value — PASS
The finding identifies a natural, exact statistic that recovers a cone-singularity order from unlabelled pairwise distances, including a change of decay exponent between the smooth sphere and every nontrivial cyclic quotient. An all-order formula, strict ordering, and an inverse conclusion make it more than a routine finite calculation.
