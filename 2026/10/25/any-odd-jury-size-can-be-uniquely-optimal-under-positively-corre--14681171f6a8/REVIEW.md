# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The coupling gain identity follows by enumerating the two pivotal changes after appending two voters, and the exact geometric sign threshold is an algebraic consequence of the two-atom mixture. A strict inequality interval chooses the unique optimum for every prescribed odd size; exact two-voter moment matching is verified independently. The proof applies to all allowed real parameters and all odd jury sizes, not only finite numerical checks.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks
The Bahadur correlation analysis of Kaniovski and Zaigraev explicitly establishes that higher-order vote dependence matters. Its central second-order-truncated family imposes a group-size-dependent upper bound on positive correlation, unlike the present fixed-latent-environment setting. Dietrich and Spiekermann prove growth under a pointwise tendency to competence, a condition absent in the finite-optimum example. Berend and Sapir give enlargement conditions in a different dependent-voter framework. The particular exact geometric phase law, arbitrary unique finite optimum construction, and matched-two-voter-law opposite monotonicity were not located in the inspected results; the earlier generic pairwise-insufficiency observation is not claimed as new. An equivalent special-case result in uninspected full literature remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The design question is how many correlated voters should participate when some shared tasks systematically mislead everyone. The theorem completely classifies the size optimum for a natural two-environment family and proves that first- and second-order vote summaries can fail to determine whether added voters improve correctness. Arbitrarily remote exact optimal sizes are achieved with marginal competence above one-half and positive correlation. These are structural sampling-design limits, not an arbitrary isolated numerical instance.

## Limitations and nearest literature
The conclusion requires a common once-per-task latent correctness parameter and conditional i.i.d. correctness indicators. It does not model voter incentives or estimated parameters. Foundational prior work already states the need for higher-order dependence; it does not establish the specific phase construction here. Independent literature audit remains necessary.
