The intrinsic distance from the apex of any symmetric regular Euclidean bipyramid to a uniform random surface point has exactly the planar vertex-to-uniform-rhombus law, independent of the number of equatorial faces once lateral length and apex angle are fixed. An explicit elementary distribution exhibits a quadratic first-contact deficit for acute face angles and a \(3/2\)-power onset for right or obtuse ones; the obtuse transition can occur only for triangular bipyramids.
