# Scientific review — two-absorber flux-dependent dark survival

## Correctness: PASS

The precise domain is every \(N\ge3\), every \(1\le a<N\), every real flux modulo \(2\pi\), \(J>0\), and two strictly positive absorber rates; all single-site pure inputs and all density operators are covered. The no-jump Lindblad reduction has generator \(B_\Phi=-iH_\Phi-\Gamma/2\). The norm derivative proves contractivity, and every zero-dissipation eigenvector must be a Hamiltonian eigenvector in the joint absorber kernel. This dark space reduces the generator; all complementary eigenvalues have strictly negative real part in finite dimensions. Thus the long-time limit is exactly the dark spectral projection, without assuming an unwarranted spectral gap uniform in the parameters.

The Hamiltonian eigenvalues are explicit Fourier cosines. Only flux \(0\) or \(\pi\) modulo \(2\pi\) permits distinct wave-number pairs at equal energy; each pair contributes one candidate dark wave. The exact second-sink condition \(Q\mid(2m+s)\), with \(Q=N/\gcd(N,a)\), is both necessary and sufficient. Its fixed-point counts give the integer dark dimensions. Discrete Fourier character sums of the squared antisymmetric pair amplitudes produce each displayed site weight. The \(N=3\) boundary, even/odd \(g,Q\), sink positions, and arbitrary strictly positive unequal rates all fit this argument. The zero-flux formulas sum over all sites to their stated dimensions, as do the half-flux formulas. No finite computation has been promoted to an all-size proof.

Reproducibility: `verify.py` was executed successfully and reported 1,151,916 modular/Fourier checks, 9,471 direct eigenprojector checks, and 24 dissipative time-evolution checks. The exact enumerator is an independent algebraic route to the finite formulas; the spectral and time-domain checks use direct linear algebra. These are corroboration, not certification.

## Originality: PASS (best-knowledge, with residual risk)

The motivating paper [1] treats a rank-one sink on a semi-infinite line and does not supply a finite magnetic cycle with two absorbing sites or a gcd-dependent survival classification. Thiel et al. [2] already demonstrate the known mechanism of single-site dark eigenmodes and magnetic lifting of degeneracy on a ring, but use intermittent projective detection; their magnetic-ring analysis does not give a two-site Lindblad-sink formula. Razzoli et al. [3] establish the general one-trap dark-subspace transport principle used in the proof; their explicit graph results do not provide these two-trap magnetic arithmetic laws. Xu [4] considers long-range interacting cyclic graphs and trap-induced survival, not a universal two-local-sink nearest-neighbor flux classification.

Actual semantic database searches included “Continuous-time quantum walk on a finite cycle with two local Lindblad sinks at vertices separated by a, magnetic Peierls flux, dark-state dimension gcd and eventual absorption”, “Two-site trapping on ring quantum walk exact survival probability gcd(N,a), parity and flux 0/pi”, “Aharonov Bohm flux breaks degenerate dark states, coherent excitation trapping on cycle with multiple absorbing vertices”, and “Lindblad quantum walk cycle two trap vertices sink magnetic flux gcd(N,a) dark state projection asymptotic survival exact”. The closest published records concerned topological particle-sink configurations of cycles, finite-cycle spectral sensor frames, cyclic quantum-graph band density and gauge twists in other Hamiltonians, rather than this claim. A search failure cannot prove global novelty: a general graph-observability result or less searchable two-trap calculation could subsume the formulas; this remains a residual priority risk.

## Value: PASS

Transport with absorption may retain a nonabsorbed coherent subspace even on a finite connected cycle. The classification turns a physically meaningful sink-placement question into a sharp all-flux criterion: \(\gcd(N,a)=1\) if and only if every input is absorbed at every flux. It also quantifies otherwise hidden arithmetic phase changes, including complete half-flux removal of dark modes when \(N/\gcd(N,a)\) is even, and a switch from zero to nonzero survival for a specified spatial residue class when the reduced length is odd and the gcd is even. The exact site-by-site weights are essential for excitation injection and sensor placement; they do not follow from a dimension count alone. This is more informative than a generic statement that symmetry causes dark states, and it resolves a natural finite/two-absorber setting alongside the recent half-line analysis.
