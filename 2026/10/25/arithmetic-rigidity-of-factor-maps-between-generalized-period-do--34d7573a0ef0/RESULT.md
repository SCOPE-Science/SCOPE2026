# Arithmetic rigidity of factor maps between generalized period-doubling shifts

## Finding

For each integer \(q\ge 2\), let \(X_q\) be the two-sided symbolic system generated by the fixed point of the constant-length substitution
\[
\theta_q(j)=0\,((j+1)\bmod q),\qquad j\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\},
\]
with left shift \(\sigma\). For every \(q,r\ge2\), there exists a continuous, surjective map \(\Phi:X_q\to X_r\) commuting with \(\sigma\) **if and only if** \(r\mid q\). When \(r\mid q\), **every** such map is uniquely of the form
\[
\boxed{\Phi=\sigma^k\circ R_{q,r},\quad k\in\mathbb Z,\qquad (R_{q,r}x)_n=x_n\bmod r.}
\]
In particular, the factor relation in this infinite family is exactly reverse divisibility: \(X_r\) is a factor of \(X_q\) precisely for \(r\mid q\). The systems for different alphabet sizes are not conjugate, and every continuous shift-commuting self-map of \(X_q\) is a power of the shift.

## Assumptions and scope

All symbolic systems are two-sided, have alphabet \(\{0,\ldots,q-1\}\), and are equipped with the product topology. A factor map is continuous, shift-commuting, and surjective. The assertion covers **all** integers \(q,r\ge2\) and **all** such factor maps, without bounds on the sliding-block-code radius. The target \(X_r\) is the specific generalized period-doubling system of modulus \(r\), not an arbitrary Toeplitz factor.

Write \(\mathbb Z_2\) for the \(2\)-adic integers, \(\mathbb Z\subset\mathbb Z_2\) for the embedded ordinary integers, and \(\nu_2(z)\) for the valuation of nonzero \(z\in\mathbb Z_2\). The one-sided fixed sequence of \(\theta_q\), indexed from zero, is
\[
 u_q(n)=\nu_2(n+1)\bmod q\quad(n\ge0).
\]
Indeed the substitution recursion gives \(u_q(2n)=0\) and \(u_q(2n+1)=(u_q(n)+1)\bmod q\), which agrees with the valuation formula. We use the usual two-sided shift orbit closure associated with this uniformly recurrent substitution sequence.

## Proof

**1. Exact odometer fibres.** For \(c\in\mathbb Z_2\setminus\mathbb Z\), set
\[
 w^{(q,c)}_n=\nu_2(n+c)\bmod q,\qquad n\in\mathbb Z.
\]
Every valuation is finite, and the sequence depends continuously on \(c\) away from the ordinary integers. Its orbit under the left shift satisfies \(\sigma w^{(q,c)}=w^{(q,c+1)}\). Density of the ordinary positive integers inside \(\mathbb Z_2\), the valuation formula for \(u_q\), and continuity on nonsingular fibres show that the closure of these sequences is precisely \(X_q\).

If \(c\in\mathbb Z\), every coordinate \(n\ne-c\) has a forced value \(\nu_2(n+c)\bmod q\), whereas the exceptional coordinate \(n=-c\) can carry any symbol \(t\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}\). Denote the resulting sequence by \(w^{(q,c,t)}\). Each such sequence is attained as a limit of nonsingular fibres: choose \(c_j=c+2^{L_j}v\), where \(v\in\mathbb Z_2\setminus\mathbb Z\) is odd, \(L_j\to\infty\), and \(L_j\equiv t\pmod q\). At the exceptional coordinate the valuation is \(L_j\), and at any other fixed coordinate the valuation eventually stabilizes. Conversely, coordinatewise stabilization forces every limit over \(c\) to be of this form. Thus the fibres have exactly
\[
 \#\pi_q^{-1}(c)=\begin{cases}q,&c\in\mathbb Z,\\1,&c\in\mathbb Z_2\setminus\mathbb Z.\end{cases}
\]

Here \(\pi_q:X_q\to\mathbb Z_2\) sends a sequence to its parameter \(c\). To see that it is well-defined, let \(c\ne d\) and put \(s=\nu_2(c-d)\). There exist infinitely many ordinary integer coordinates \(n\) with \(\nu_2(n+d)=s+1\); at each such coordinate \(\nu_2(n+c)=s\). Avoiding the at most two exceptional coordinates for singular fibres, the respective symbols differ because \(q\ge2\). Thus distinct parameters never give the same sequence. The closed-graph construction and compactness imply continuity of \(\pi_q\). It is onto and satisfies \(\pi_q\circ\sigma=\pi_q+1\).

The fibres over \(\mathbb Z_2\setminus\mathbb Z\) are singleton and dense. In fact every fibre above an integer is approached along odometer translates with any prescribed exceptional residue \(t\bmod q\): for a nonsingular starting parameter \(u\), choose integers \(k_j\) so that \(\nu_2(u+k_j-c)=L_j\), with \(L_j\to\infty\) and \(L_j\equiv t\pmod q\). Such integers exist because every prescribed finite dyadic congruence class meets \(\mathbb Z\). Hence the orbit of every nonsingular sequence is dense in \(X_q\), so the system is minimal. Two points in any singular fibre differ at exactly one coordinate and are therefore forward asymptotic under \(\sigma\). An equivariant continuous map to an equicontinuous system identifies any forward-asymptotic pair, since the latter system admits a compatible invariant metric. Thus every equicontinuous factor is constant on the fibres of \(\pi_q\) and factors through it. This proves directly that \(\pi_q\) is the maximal equicontinuous factor; the almost-one-to-one property is also explicit.

**2. Every prospective factor induces an integer odometer translation.** Let \(\Phi:X_q\to X_r\) be continuous, onto, and shift-commuting. By maximality of \(\pi_q\), the map \(\pi_r\circ\Phi\) factors through \(\pi_q\), say \(\pi_r\circ\Phi=h\circ\pi_q\). Shift commutation gives \(h(c+1)=h(c)+1\). As \(\mathbb Z\) is dense in \(\mathbb Z_2\), continuity forces
\[
 h(c)=c+a\qquad(c\in\mathbb Z_2)
\]
for one \(a\in\mathbb Z_2\). The target fibre over \(0\in\mathbb Z\) has \(r\ge2\) members. Surjectivity of \(\Phi\) requires their preimages all to lie in \(\pi_q^{-1}(-a)\), which cannot be a singleton. By the fibre count, \(-a\in\mathbb Z\), hence \(a=k\in\mathbb Z\).

**3. Continuity forces divisibility.** Suppose \(r\nmid q\). Choose an odd noninteger \(v\in\mathbb Z_2\) and exponents \(L_j\to\infty\) with \(L_j\equiv0\pmod q\). Set \(c_j=2^{L_j}v\), which is never an ordinary integer. Then \(w^{(q,c_j)}\) tends to the **same** singular source configuration \(w^{(q,0,0)}\), regardless of which such exponents are used. The induced translation from step 2 gives
\[
 \Phi(w^{(q,c_j)})=w^{(r,c_j+k)},
\]
because both parameters are nonsingular and the target fibre is a singleton. At target coordinate \(n=-k\), the right-hand value is \(\nu_2(c_j)\bmod r=L_j\bmod r\). Because \(r\nmid q\), there are two choices of increasing sequences \(L_j\equiv0\pmod q\) for which these residues are different (choose two distinct residues from the cyclic subgroup generated by \(q\bmod r\)). The source sequences converge to the same point while their images have distinct limiting values at coordinate \(-k\), contradicting continuity. Therefore \(r\mid q\).

**4. Sufficiency and rigidity.** If \(r\mid q\), coordinatewise reduction \(R_{q,r}\) maps \(w^{(q,c)}\) exactly to \(w^{(r,c)}\) for nonsingular \(c\). It therefore extends continuously to all of \(X_q\), maps onto \(X_r\) by density of these fibres, and commutes with the shift. Every \(\sigma^kR_{q,r}\) is accordingly a factor map.

Conversely, for an arbitrary \(\Phi\), step 2 fixes its integer translation \(k\). At every nonsingular parameter \(c\), the target singleton fibre forces \(\Phi(w^{(q,c)})=w^{(r,c+k)}=\sigma^kR_{q,r}(w^{(q,c)})\). These points are dense in \(X_q\), so continuity proves equality everywhere. Different values of \(k\) induce different translations of \(\mathbb Z_2\), proving uniqueness. Taking \(q=r\) gives the self-map assertion: every continuous shift-commuting self-map is automatically onto because its image is a nonempty closed invariant subset of the minimal system \(X_q\). A conjugacy between different moduli would require divisibility in both directions, and hence equal moduli. \(\square\)

The expected output begins with `VERIFY_OK`.

These tests independently check the explicit arithmetic and local models. They are **not** an exhaustive search over arbitrary sliding block codes, nor a proof of the universal theorem. The infinite-family classification follows from the four analytical steps above.

## Relationship to prior work

Bognini and Ammar (arXiv:2511.20881, first posted November 25, 2025) define exactly these \(q\)-letter period-doubling substitutions and study factorization of their *words* into kernel words and gap sequences. That is a combinatorial word decomposition, not a classification of shift-equivariant factor maps between different alphabet moduli.

Gómez and Petite (arXiv:2610.06488, first posted October 5, 2026) study automorphism and normalizer groups of Toeplitz subshifts, exploiting maximal equicontinuous factors and semicocycle discontinuities. Their general framework and automorphism constructions are acknowledged; the result here concerns **all cross-modulus factor maps of one explicitly defined family**, including non-invertible surjections, with the arithmetic necessity \(r\mid q\).

Sell and Sieron (2026) study the boundary of the separating cover in almost automorphic systems and prove structural inheritance under factor maps; this is compatible with the singleton/singular-fibre argument, but their stated structural inheritance alone does not give the modulus-divisibility criterion. Earlier analyses of simple Toeplitz subshifts, notably Sell (2018/2020), give combinatorial complexity and related invariants, not the stated complete factor-map classification. The divisibility law is not asserted to be a general law for all Toeplitz systems.

## Limitations

This is a theorem about the specified substitution family and continuous two-sided shift-equivariant **surjections**, not about arbitrary measurable factors, finite-to-one nonsurjective maps, orbit equivalences, or every possible intermediate factor of one \(X_q\). An intermediate factor might lie outside the collection \(\{X_r:r\ge2\}\). The argument establishes a mathematical implication from the explicit fibre description and does not establish historical priority against all unpublished or differently named results. The accessible literature was inspected by statement and implication, but an earlier formulation under semicocycles or factor code terminology remains possible. No independent audit has been performed.
