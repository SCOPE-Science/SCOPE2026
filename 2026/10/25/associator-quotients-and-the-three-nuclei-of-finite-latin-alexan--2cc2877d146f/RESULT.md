# Associator quotients and the three nuclei of finite Latin Alexander quandle rings

## Finding
Let \(G\) be a finite abelian group, \(T\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), and \(R\) a nonzero commutative unital ring. Form the Alexander quandle \(Q=\operatorname{Alex}(G,T)\), whose operation is \(x*y=Tx+(1-T)y\), and its \(R\)-algebra \(A=R[Q]\) with basis \(e_g\) and product \(e_g e_h=e_{g*h}\). Let \(H=(1-T)G\). Write \([a,b,c]=(ab)c-a(bc)\) for an associator, and let \(\mathfrak A\) be the two-sided ideal generated by all associators.

**Theorem.** The \(R\)-linear span of the associators is itself a two-sided ideal and equals
\[
\mathfrak A=\ker\bigl(R[G]\longrightarrow R[G/H]\bigr),
\]
where the kernel is understood under the standard basis identification of \(A\) with the ordinary group ring \(R[G]\). More explicitly, it is spanned by every \(e_g-e_{g+h}\) with \(g\in G\) and \(h\in H\). The associative quotient \(A/\mathfrak A\) is free of rank \(|G/H|\), with multiplication \(e_{[g]}e_{[h]}=e_{[g]}\). It is the universal associative quotient of \(A\).

If \(1-T\) is also an automorphism, then the quandle is Latin, \(\mathfrak A=\ker\varepsilon\), and the **left, middle and right associative nuclei coincide**:
\[
N_{\ell}(A)=N_m(A)=N_r(A)=R\Omega,
\qquad \Omega=\sum_{g\in G}e_g.
\]
Here \(\varepsilon(\sum_g r_ge_g)=\sum_g r_g\), and the nuclei are respectively the sets of \(u\) for which \([u,a,b]\), \([a,u,b]\), or \([a,b,u]\) vanishes for all \(a,b\in A\). The rank-one nuclear subalgebra satisfies
\[
\Omega a=a\Omega=\varepsilon(a)\Omega,\qquad \Omega^2=|G|\Omega.
\]
These conclusions hold over arbitrary nonzero commutative unital coefficient rings, including fields of characteristic dividing \(|G|\). In such characteristic the nonzero nuclear generator \(\Omega\) squares to zero. If \(|G|\) is invertible in \(R\), \(|G|^{-1}\Omega\) is instead a nuclear idempotent.

## Assumptions and scope
All groups are finite abelian groups written additively. The endomorphisms \(T\) and \(1-T\) commute. For the associator-ideal statement only \(T\) is assumed invertible; for the nucleus statement both are invertible. There are no restrictions on \(R\) beyond being a nonzero commutative ring with identity. The singleton group is included, in which case the associative quotient and each nucleus equal \(A\).

We distinguish the **ordinary associative group-ring multiplication** (juxtaposition) on \(B=R[G]\) from the quandle multiplication \(\star\). The basis element \(X^0\) is the ordinary group-ring identity; generally it is not an identity for \(\star\). For \(s\in G\), denote the ordinary basis element by \(X^s\).

## Proof
**1. Transport and associator formula.** Define commuting unital endomorphisms of \(B\) by
\[
\alpha(X^g)=X^{Tg},\qquad \beta(X^g)=X^{(1-T)g}.
\]
The former is an automorphism. Under \(e_g\leftrightarrow X^g\), the quandle product is
\[
u\star v=\alpha(u)\beta(v).
\]
Its associator, with parentheses as in the theorem, is
\[
[u,v,w]_{\star}
=\alpha\beta(v)\bigl(\alpha^2(u)\beta(w)-\alpha(u)\beta^2(w)\bigr).
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, \((u\star v)\star w=\alpha^2(u)\alpha\beta(v)\beta(w)\), whereas \(u\star(v\star w)=\alpha(u)\beta\alpha(v)\beta^2(w)\); the two endomorphisms commute.

**2. Every associator, and nothing more, generates the subgroup kernel.** Put \(H=(1-T)G\) and \(L_H=\ker(B\to R[G/H])\), the free \(R\)-submodule generated by \(X^g-X^{g+h}\) for all \(g\in G\), \(h\in H\). For basis elements \(X^a,X^b,X^c\), the two exponents in (1) differ by \(T(1-T)(c-a)\in H\). Thus all associators lie in \(L_H\).

Conversely, choose the basis triple \((e_x,e_x,e_{x+d})\). Because the quandle is idempotent, its associator is
\[
(e_xe_x)e_{x+d}-e_x(e_xe_{x+d})
=e_{x+(1-T)d}-e_{x+(1-T)^2d}.
\tag{2}
\]
The difference between the two subscripts is \(T(1-T)d\). As \(d\) varies, these differences range over all of \(H\), because \(T\) is invertible. For each \(d\), letting \(x\) vary gives every translate of the corresponding difference. Therefore the \(R\)-linear span of (2) is the whole of \(L_H\), proving equality.

The quotient map \(B\to R[G/H]\) is compatible with \(\star\): modulo \(H\) the first exponent becomes \(Ta\equiv a\), while the second becomes \((1-T)b\equiv0\). Hence its image has basis multiplication \(e_{[a]}e_{[b]}=e_{[a]}\), which is associative. The kernel is consequently a two-sided \(\star\)-ideal. Any homomorphism from \(A\) into any associative algebra kills every associator, hence kills this kernel and factors uniquely through the quotient. This proves the claimed universal property.

**3. A coefficient-ring-independent annihilator lemma.** Let \(I=\ker(\varepsilon:B\to R)\) be the ordinary group-ring augmentation ideal. Put \(\Omega=\sum_{g\in G}X^g\). Then
\[
\operatorname{Ann}_{B}(I)=R\Omega.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, \(I\) is generated as an ordinary ideal by \(X^g-1\). If \(b=\sum_g b_gX^g\) annihilates it, every equality \(bX^h=b\) forces \(b_{g-h}=b_g\) for all \(g,h\), so every coefficient equals a common element of \(R\). Conversely \(\Omega(X^h-1)=0\). This uses neither division by \(|G|\) nor the absence of zero divisors in \(R\).

**4. The three nuclei in the Latin case.** Assume \(\beta\) is an automorphism, equivalently \(1-T\) is invertible on \(G\). The elements
\[
\beta(X^w)-\beta^2(X^w),\qquad w\in G,
\tag{4}
\]
generate \(I\) as an ordinary \(B\)-ideal: their exponent differences \((1-T)T w\) range over all of \(G\). Likewise the elements
\[
\alpha^2(X^u)-\alpha(X^u),\qquad u\in G,
\tag{5}
\]
generate \(I\), because \(T(T-1)\) is invertible. We write \(1_B=X^0\) for the ordinary group-ring identity.

Suppose \(z\in N_{\ell}(A)\). In (1) put \(v=w=1_B\). This gives \(\alpha^2(z)=\alpha(z)\). Keeping \(v=1_B\) and varying \(w\) then yields \(\alpha(z)(\beta(w)-\beta^2(w))=0\). By (3)–(4), \(\alpha(z)\in R\Omega\), and hence \(z\in R\Omega\).

Suppose \(z\in N_m(A)\). Put \(u=1_B\) in (1). Then \(\alpha\beta(z)(\beta(w)-\beta^2(w))=0\) for every \(w\). By (3)–(4), \(\alpha\beta(z)\in R\Omega\). Both \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are automorphisms that fix \(\Omega\), so \(z\in R\Omega\).

Suppose \(z\in N_r(A)\). Setting \(u=v=1_B\) in (1) yields \(\beta(z)=\beta^2(z)\). Now set \(v=1_B\) and vary \(u\): formula (1) reduces to \(\beta(z)(\alpha^2(u)-\alpha(u))=0\). By (3) and (5), \(\beta(z)\in R\Omega\), whence \(z\in R\Omega\).

Finally, since \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are augmentation-preserving permutation automorphisms, \(\alpha(\Omega)=\beta(\Omega)=\Omega\) and ordinary group-ring multiplication gives \(\Omega b=\varepsilon(b)\Omega\). Therefore \(\Omega\star a=a\star\Omega=\varepsilon(a)\Omega\), making every element of \(R\Omega\) belong to all three nuclei. This proves their equality. Multiplying \(\Omega\) by itself yields \(\Omega\star\Omega=|G|\Omega\).

As \(H=G\) under the Latin hypothesis, step 2 identifies the associator ideal with \(I\) and its quotient with \(R\). This completes the proof. \(\square\)

## Relationship to prior work
Sangare and Ta (August 2026), Proposition 4.7, ALREADY prove the left-nucleus identity \(N_{\ell}(R[R_n])=R\Omega\) for every odd dihedral quandle and every commutative unital coefficient ring. That subfamily is a previously established case, and this work does not claim it as new. Their article does not determine all three nuclei for general finite abelian Latin Alexander quandles, nor the general associator quotient. Zhang (2026), Proposition 2.1, establishes exactly the group-ring transport \(u\star v=\alpha(u)\beta(v)\) that is used as the starting point here. His Theorems 2.2 and 2.3 calculate augmentation-product ideals and **commutator** subalgebras, respectively, but do not state the associator quotient or the complete three-nucleus classification. These distinct operations should not be conflated. Ta (2026) proves zero-divisor and idempotent/augmentation statements for medial quandle rings and develops finite Alexander Fourier methods. His Proposition 2.13 recalls the sum-of-basis-element identity for finite Latin quandles, which is a known ingredient of the final paragraph above; it does not determine all three nuclei or the associator ideal. Bardakov–Passi–Singh (2018, 2022) develop quandle rings, their augmentation and commutator theory, and nonassociativity properties; their specifically inspected relevant sections do not provide this complete classification. The older literature on general quasigroup algebras could conceivably contain a stronger implication; no absolute priority or independence is asserted.

## Limitations
The three-nucleus classification requires the Latin condition; the non-Latin portion of the theorem determines only the universal associative quotient. The groups are finite and abelian; nothing here classifies nuclei for general quandles or infinite groups, or noncommutative coefficient rings. The modular-square-zero statement concerns the rank-one nuclear generator only, not the entire quandle algebra. Finite computations cannot establish the general result. Historical completeness is not claimed; equivalence under older terminology such as quasigroup algebras remains a residual literature risk. Independent external mathematical audit has not been performed.
