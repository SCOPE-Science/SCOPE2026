# Scientific review: asymptotic enumeration by corank

## Correctness — PASS
The statement quantifies over a fixed finite field, every dimension, and every corank; the error in the corank formula is normalized **absolute**, uniform in corank. The proof reduces the genuine algebra-isomorphism enumeration to a two-sided squeeze between density-one 2LI monomial orbits and all monomial orbits. The natural-basis rigidity criterion is re-proved using multiplication-map rank, so it does not require full rank of the structure matrix. The nonidentity Burnside sum is bounded separately for nontrivial permutations (via orbits on ordered pairs) and diagonal scalings (via modal multiplicity); the crucial quantity is the sum of fixed-point fractions rather than a probability of possessing an automorphism. The exact finite-field rank count then supplies the corank constants. The sums, normalizations, and characteristic-two boundary have been checked. Exact small-case enumeration found 9 genuine versus 10 monomial classes at dimension two over the binary field, 13 versus 15 over the ternary field, and 85 versus 104 at dimension three over the binary field, consistent with the squeeze but not constituting an asymptotic proof. No external correctness certification is claimed.

## Originality — PASS, with bounded-source risk
Question 21 of the October 2026 survey explicitly asks for the asymptotic number of all finite-field evolution algebras, including nonperfect examples. Wei–Zou's 2023 Burnside formula counts only **perfect** algebras; it does not imply the corank-resolved isomorphism census for all singular algebras, because nonmonomial changes of natural basis can occur there. The 2LI natural-basis theorem of Boudi–Cabrera Casado–Siles Molina and its 2025 extension establish rigidity when 2LI holds but do not estimate singular isomorphism-class counts. The present proof controls exceptional non-2LI matrices **at the orbit-counting level**, not merely in probability, and identifies a positive, explicit asymptotic proportion for each fixed positive corank. A literature overlap search did not locate that combined theorem. An unindexed equivalent theorem or an unnoticed implication in another counting paper remains a residual risk; searches are not a uniqueness certificate.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result gives the asymptotic count requested by an explicitly posed problem, together with the entire fixed-corank distribution and a uniform absolute error even for growing corank. Singular, genuinely nonperfect evolution algebras account for a positive limiting proportion of isomorphism classes, so they cannot be treated as negligible exceptions to perfect-algebra classification. The proof offers a transferable enumeration principle: a density-one identifiable basis type plus a **summable Burnside stabilizer bound** controls unlabelled isomorphism types despite exceptional changes of basis. This is a natural classification-scale conclusion, not an arbitrary finite computation or a relabeling of the perfect case.

(2025), Theorem 5.3. Earlier perfect-algebra asymptotics and labelled-model rigidity phenomena do not by themselves supply the asserted fixed-positive-corank isomorphism-class distribution. The error remains absolute, not relative, in rare high-corank strata. The field size is fixed; arbitrary-field and jointly growing dimension/field regimes are outside scope.
