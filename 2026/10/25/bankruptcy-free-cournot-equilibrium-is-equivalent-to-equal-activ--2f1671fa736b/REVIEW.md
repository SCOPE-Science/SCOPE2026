# Review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The two directions are proved for every interior equilibrium under the stated demand and cost assumptions. The predation deviation chooses a target aggregate output with price strictly between two active firms' marginal costs; both profit signs follow directly from the definition of operating profit. No numerical evidence substitutes for the proof.

## Originality

**PASS, with residual risk.** The closest literature defines bankruptcy-free output profiles and studies their role in repeated oligopoly. The claim here is a demand-curve-independent if-and-only-if criterion for the static interior Cournot benchmark with constant marginal costs and any number of active firms. Accessible abstracts and an institutional summary do not state this exact criterion. The full text of the closest 2024 source was not available for inspection, so a direct implication from its general characterization remains a risk rather than a ruled-out possibility.

## Value

**PASS.** Bankruptcy-free status is a key restriction on credible punishment in repeated oligopoly. The result gives a sharp, model-wide boundary in a canonical homogeneous-good model: any active marginal-cost heterogeneity rules out BF status, while equal active costs guarantee it. This replaces parameter-by-parameter checks within this model class; it is not a claim about the repeated-game folk theorem itself.
