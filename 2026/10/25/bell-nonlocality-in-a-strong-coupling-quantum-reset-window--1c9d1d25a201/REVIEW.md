# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim concerns the stationary full-reset two-qubit state with exactly specified Hamiltonian, dimensionless parameters, and projective CHSH maximum. Stationarity of Pauli expectations gives an explicit rational real symmetric correlation matrix, independently cross-checked against a spectral integral of the density operator at 400 parameter pairs. The Horodecki variational criterion and an explicit lower bound provide an **all-couplings** witness for \(\alpha\ge2\) at \(R=1/(2\alpha)\). Endpoint locality is proved by strict limiting inequalities and a large-rate expansion. The inverse-coupling scaling follows by direct matrix convergence and singular-value continuity; a one-variable derivative gives the unique scaled optimum. No finite experiments are used to infer universal quantifiers. The numerical checks support but do not replace the mathematical proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Kulkarni and Majumdar derive the original exact stationary density and report concurrence; they do not state a projective Bell-CHSH activation region or the singular \(\sqrt5\) CHSH scaling theorem. The 1995 Horodecki criterion supplies the pre-existing measurement-optimization method. Wald and collaborators study discrete-time qubit-reset circuits with a different entangling propagator. Relevant primary mathematical content was inspected and no exact equivalent claim found in the compared sources. Because the originating paper gives the full density operator, the new result is a nontrivial derived consequence of published raw information, **not** an independent solution of that state; an uncatalogued implication or broader Bell-resetting treatment remains a priority risk.

## Value — PASS
Concurrence alone cannot determine whether a state certifies Bell nonlocality. The finite-ratio guarantee specifies a realizable reset rate and gives a strict CHSH violation even though sufficiently fast or slow resetting is Bell-local at the same finite coupling. The unique inverse-coupling profile and limiting \(\sqrt5\) Bell strength explain which joint scaling of the physical rates survives in the strong-interaction limit. This supplies a measurement-level classification not resolved by the published concurrence plots. Claims about the exact finite-coupling phase boundary or a global optimum are excluded.

## Closest work and residual limitations
Kulkarni–Majumdar (arXiv:2307.07485) directly covers the stationary state and concurrence. Horodecki–Horodecki–Horodecki (1995) covers the universal two-qubit CHSH criterion. Wald et al. (arXiv:2410.11497) covers different discrete-time reset dynamics. The main residual risk is another explicit or implicit Bell analysis of the same continuous-reset state. Numerical spectral eigenvectors become ill-conditioned at extremely large coupling; symbolic identities, rather than extrapolation, establish the limit.
