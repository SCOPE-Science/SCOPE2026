# Scientific review: finite-exponential Fock Toeplitz commutators

## Correctness — PASS

The dense kernel domain is stable under all indicated exponential Toeplitz symbols; two Gaussian projection identities give the exact coefficient expansion. The necessity direction uses a separately proved finite-exponential boundedness lemma rather than assuming the Berezin transform detects every unbounded operator. Its proof addresses repeated values of the real growth exponent by isolating a nonvanishing trigonometric polynomial. The converse is the unitary Weyl representation. Essential-norm equality uses conjugation by Weyl displacements and weakly escaping unit vectors; no inference from finite matrix compression is used. The two-frequency norm follows from full unit-circle spectrum for every nontrivial Weyl displacement. The 1775 finite checker assertions corroborate coefficients and normalization, not universal conclusions.

## Originality — PASS, subject to publication-priority limitations

Qin–Liu–Huang, *Commuting Toeplitz operators with harmonic symbols on the Fock space* (2026), arXiv:2610.22136v1, was materially inspected through the principal statements and proofs in Sections 1–4. Their corrected Theorems 4.1–4.2 concern **vanishing** commutators; they do not specify bounded **nonzero** commutators, their essential norms, or sharp two-frequency norms. The coefficient-cancellation setting is explicitly acknowledged as shared background. Cho–Park–Zhu, *Products of Toeplitz operators on the Fock space* (2012), arXiv:1212.0045v1, was compared through its Main Theorem, Lemma 1 and compactness corollary. It treats one Toeplitz product, not cancellation of two mixed commutator differences. The 2015 Bauer–Choe–Koo commutation article was checked through its publication record and the 2026 paper's precise account of its propositions, but the earlier article's full text was inaccessible; this creates residual historical-priority risk. General Weyl-algebra theory may also subsume the finite-Weyl essential-norm identity independently, but does not by itself express the four-symbol boundedness criterion.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The motivating literature identifies a substantive cancellation issue for finite harmonic-exponential symbols. The theorem gives a complete coefficientwise decision procedure for whether the associated *nonzero* commutator is bounded; it then determines its distance to compact operators without an approximation factor, and has an exact nontrivial two-frequency norm family. These address naturally occurring operator-theoretic questions rather than arbitrary parameter slices.

## Limits and follow-up

Only one-variable finite pure exponential-polynomial symbols are covered. Polynomially weighted exponential symbols, general entire exponential-type symbols, and evaluated norms of three-or-more Weyl terms are not claimed. Historical priority is not independently verified.
