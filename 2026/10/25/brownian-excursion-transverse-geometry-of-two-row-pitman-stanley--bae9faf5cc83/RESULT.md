# Brownian-excursion transverse geometry of two-row Pitman–Stanley polytopes

## Finding

For every integer \(m\ge1\), consider the two-row generalized Pitman–Stanley polytope \(P_m=\mathrm{PS}_2^m((1,0),(0,0))\) in its canonical coordinate Euclidean space. Let \(X_m\) be uniform in \(P_m\) by \(2m\)-dimensional volume, and let \(F_m\) be the face on which its second row vanishes. Then the squared Euclidean distance \(Q_m=\operatorname{dist}(X_m,F_m)^2\) satisfies

\[
Q_m\ \Longrightarrow\ 2\int_0^1 e(t)^2\,dt,
\]

where \(e\) is a standard unit-duration Brownian excursion. Every positive moment converges. The limiting squared-radius law has Laplace transform

\[
\mathbb E\exp(-sQ)=\left(\frac{2\sqrt{s}}{\sinh(2\sqrt{s})}\right)^{3/2}\quad(s\ge0),
\]

continuously interpreted as \(1\) at \(s=0\). In particular, \(\lim_m\mathbb E Q_m=1\) and \(\lim_m\operatorname{Var}(Q_m)=4/15\).

More strongly, write \(C_{m,j}=(X_m)_{2j}\), and linearly interpolate the values \(V_m(0)=0\) and \(V_m(j/m)=\sqrt m\,C_{m,m+1-j}\), for \(1\le j\le m\). Then

\[
V_m\ \Longrightarrow\ \sqrt2\,e\qquad\text{in }C([0,1])
\]

in the uniform topology. In particular, \(\sqrt m\max_j C_{m,j}\Longrightarrow\sqrt2\max_{0\le t\le1}e(t)\), with convergence of all positive moments.

## Assumptions and scope

Define \(P_m\) as the set of real matrices \(x=(x_{ij})\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{2\times m}\) satisfying, for \(i=1,2\),

\[
0\le\sum_{r=1}^{i}x_{rm}\le\sum_{r=1}^{i}x_{r,m-1}\le\cdots\le\sum_{r=1}^{i}x_{r1}\le1.
\]

This is the specified instance of the generalized Pitman–Stanley definition in Dugan–Hegarty–Morales–Raymond. It is full-dimensional and has ordinary Euclidean coordinates, not the intrinsic flow-metric of a different representation. The face \(F_m=P_m\cap\{x_{21}=\cdots=x_{2m}=0\}\) is precisely the nonincreasing simplex of first-row coordinates with second row zero. Since setting the second row to zero preserves all inequalities, the orthogonal projection of every point of \(P_m\) onto \(F_m\) is obtained by that operation; hence \(Q_m=\sum_{j=1}^m C_{m,j}^2\) exactly. All probability distributions are ordinary normalized Euclidean volume measure on \(P_m\).

A standard Brownian excursion here means the weak limit of a uniform Dyck path of length \(2m\), with height scaled by \(\sqrt{2m}\). Equivalently it has the law of the Euclidean norm of a three-dimensional standard Brownian bridge on \([0,1]\).

## Proof

**1. The order-polytope representation.** Put \(a_j=x_{1j}\), \(b_j=x_{1j}+x_{2j}\). This triangular coordinate change has determinant one. The defining conditions are equivalent to \(0\le a_j\le b_j\le1\) and both rows \(a_1,\ldots,a_m\) and \(b_1,\ldots,b_m\) weakly decreasing. Reverse columns to form \(A_j=a_{m+1-j}\), \(B_j=b_{m+1-j}\). These are weakly increasing, with \(A_j\le B_j\) for each \(j\), so their allowable domain is the ordinary order polytope of the rectangular two-chain poset. Stanley's linear-extension triangulation divides it into congruent open simplices of volume \(1/(2m)!\). Its extensions are the \(\operatorname{Cat}_m=\binom{2m}{m}/(m+1)\) Dyck words: record the occurrence of \(A\) as an up step and of \(B\) as a down step. Thus a uniform volume point is generated by choosing a uniformly random Dyck word, and independently \(2m\) uniform order statistics \(Y_{(1)}<\cdots<Y_{(2m)}\), then assigning \(A_j=Y_{(r_{A,j})}\), \(B_j=Y_{(r_{B,j})}\), where \(r_{A,j}<r_{B,j}\) are the step positions of the \(j\)th up and down steps respectively. In particular the exact coordinate volume is \(\operatorname{Cat}_m/(2m)!\); this volume fact is an ingredient, not the new limit claim.

**2. Gap-length and excursion process.** Let \(H_k\) be the Dyck height after step \(k\), linearly interpolated between integer times, and let \(d_j=r_{B,j}-r_{A,j}\). The up-step rank satisfies \(r_{A,j}=2j-H_{r_{A,j}}\), and the down-step rank satisfies \(r_{B,j}=2j+H_{r_{B,j}}\). Hence

\[
d_j=H_{r_{A,j}}+H_{r_{B,j}}.
\]

For a uniformly random Dyck word, the functional invariance principle gives \(H_{\lfloor2mt\rfloor}/\sqrt{2m}\Longrightarrow e(t)\) in the uniform topology. Moreover \(\max_k H_k=O_{\mathbb P}(\sqrt m)\). Uniform continuity in the tight rescaled paths, together with \(\max_j|r_{A,j}-2j|,\max_j|r_{B,j}-2j|\le\max H_k\), yields

\[
\max_{1\le j\le m}\left|\frac{d_j}{2\sqrt m}-\frac{H_{2j}}{\sqrt m}\right|\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.
\]

Here \(H_{2j}/\sqrt m\Longrightarrow\sqrt2 e(j/m)\) as an interpolated profile. The endpoint convention sets the value at time zero to zero.

**3. Euclidean coordinate-gap control.** Uniform order-statistic spacings, including the gaps from zero to the first and last to one, have the same law as \(E_0/S,\ldots,E_{2m}/S\), where all \(E_r\) are independent rate-one exponentials and \(S=\sum E_r\). Thus each difference \(B_j-A_j\) is a sum of \(d_j\) consecutive exponentials divided by \(S\), with spacing variables independent of the Dyck path. The uniform Dyck-height bound gives \(\max_jd_j=O_{\mathbb P}(\sqrt m)\). Conditional on the event \(\max_jd_j\le K\sqrt m\), standard exponential Bernstein bounds and a union bound over at most \((2m+1)^2\) consecutive blocks show

\[
\max_j\left|\sum_{r\in I_j}E_r-d_j\right|
=O_{\mathbb P}(m^{1/4}\sqrt{\log m}+\log m)=o_{\mathbb P}(\sqrt m),
\]

where \(I_j\) is the interval of spacings between those two order statistics. Because \(S/(2m)\to1\) in probability,

\[
\max_j\left|\sqrt m(B_j-A_j)-\frac{d_j}{2\sqrt m}\right|\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.
\]

Combining this with step 2 proves the stated process limit for \(V_m\), using the continuous interpolation of the nodes. The continuous mapping theorem and Riemann-sum convergence then give

\[
Q_m=\sum_{j=1}^m(B_j-A_j)^2=\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m V_m(j/m)^2
\Longrightarrow2\int_0^1 e(t)^2\,dt,
\]

as well as the maximum-coordinate limit.

**4. All moment orders.** For each integer \(p\ge1\), conditional on a Dyck word, a gap across \(d_j\) uniform spacings has beta distribution with parameters \(d_j\) and \(2m+1-d_j\). Denoting rising factorials by \(z^{\overline k}\),

\[
\mathbb E[(B_j-A_j)^{2p}\mid H]
=\frac{d_j^{\overline{2p}}}{(2m+1)^{\overline{2p}}}.
\]

The inequalities \(d_j\le2\max_kH_k\) and \(Q_m^p\le m^{p-1}\sum_j(B_j-A_j)^{2p}\) imply

\[
\mathbb E Q_m^p\le C_p\left(1+\mathbb E\left[(\max_kH_k/\sqrt m)^{2p}\right]\right).
\]

The standard uniform exponential-moment bounds for the maximum of rescaled Dyck paths imply a finite bound independent of \(m\) for every fixed \(p\). Increasing the integer exponent when necessary gives uniform integrability for every positive real moment order, including the supremum observable. Consequently these moments converge to their excursion-functional counterparts.

**5. Exact Laplace law.** A standard excursion is the radial process of three independent standard Brownian bridges \(\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3\). Their Karhunen–Loève eigenvalues for the quadratic integral are \(1/(\pi^2k^2)\), \(k\ge1\). Hence for \(s\ge0\),

\[
\mathbb E\exp\left(-2s\int_0^1 e(t)^2dt\right)
=\prod_{k=1}^\infty\left(1+\frac{4s}{\pi^2k^2}\right)^{-3/2}
=\left(\frac{2\sqrt s}{\sinh(2\sqrt s)}\right)^{3/2}.
\]

Expanding its logarithm at zero as \(-s+(2/15)s^2+O(s^3)\) gives the mean \(1\) and variance \(4/15\), which are strictly positive and finite. This completes the proof.

## Verification

The standalone script `check.py` reconstructs the original nonnegative-coordinate constraints, enumerates all vertices through the order-polytope representation in small dimensions, and cross-checks independent convex-hull volumes against \(\operatorname{Cat}_m/(2m)!\) for \(1\le m\le4\). It enumerates Dyck paths to compute exact finite squared-distance means for \(1\le m\le8\), checks the exact beta-spacing identity, and samples valid uniform-volume points in dimensions \(m=40,120,350\) for distributional diagnostics. Run from the artifact directory with `python check.py` after installing `requirements.txt`. Exact finite-volume and moment results provide reproducibility diagnostics; the infinite limiting law follows from the analytic proof, not from simulation.

## Relationship to prior work

Dugan, Hegarty, Morales and Raymond introduce and study the generalized Pitman–Stanley polytopes, giving their flow interpretation and face/vertex descriptions. Their companion volume study concerns lattice and Euclidean volumes. Stanley's order-polytope triangulation, conditioned Dyck-path invariance, Dyck maximum bounds, and the Bessel-bridge representation are classical ingredients. The distinct claim here is the limiting distribution and process-level law for **Euclidean distance from a canonical face under normalized volume**, including the exact radial Laplace transform and moment limits. Volume identities, Brownian excursion convergence of the Dyck word alone, and intrinsic inradius formulas do not state this Euclidean observable.

## Limitations

The theorem is specific to the two-row family with upper vector \((1,0)\) and zero lower vector, with its canonical coordinate Euclidean metric. No universality over arbitrary boundary vectors or numbers of rows is asserted. The sampling code cannot verify convergence or literature uniqueness. Comparable limits for other rectangular order polytopes and related random-tableau observables might yield an equivalent formulation not identified here; the claim should not be treated as independently validated.
