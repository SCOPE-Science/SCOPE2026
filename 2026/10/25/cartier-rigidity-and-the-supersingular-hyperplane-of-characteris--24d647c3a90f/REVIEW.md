# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
A complete genus-three differential basis is specified and all three Cartier images follow from the characteristic-two Cartier rules. The operator has rank two for every smooth model, stable rank zero exactly when the cubic coefficient vanishes, and stable rank two otherwise. The supersingular Newton polygon is proved independently of this stable-rank computation by three explicit elliptic involution quotients and an odd-adic character decomposition. Each elliptic quotient is smooth with \(j=0\), so supersingular. In characteristic two, separability and counting use the exact single-root derivative equation. The executable check matches the four finite fields reported in the finding. Risks: the Jacobian isogeny is over an algebraic closure; finite checks are not an exhaustive proof for arbitrary fields.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Bouw–Koutsianas–Sijsling–Wewers (arXiv:1902.09624) gives the exact quartic normal form, not its characteristic-two Cartier stratification. Nart–Ritzenthaler (arXiv:math/0312366) classify all characteristic-two genus-three quartics by Newton polygon and therefore already cover the existence and possible shapes of these polygons at the general-moduli level; those facts alone are not claimed original. Their full-text treatment and searchable text do not supply the explicit coefficient criterion, constant \(a\)-number for the monic Picard family, and the coordinate-marked counts given here. Nart–Ritzenthaler (arXiv:0905.0546) supplies the general involution-isogeny approach and is explicitly credited. The precise new statement combines those specialized coefficient-level facts; there remains risk of an unindexed source or an unrecognized short corollary of general results.

The family is the standard cyclic-trigonal genus-three Picard model. The result describes all smooth coefficient points simultaneously, including a geometric supersingular divisor inside an everywhere nonordinary family, a uniform non-superspecial condition, and exact extension-field presentation counts. The statement gives a useful explicit interface between the classical Picard quartic normal form and characteristic-two moduli/Newton strata; it is not merely a pointwise sample or arbitrary slice.
