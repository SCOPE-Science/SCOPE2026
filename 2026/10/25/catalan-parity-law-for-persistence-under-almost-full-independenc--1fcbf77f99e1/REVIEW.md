# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The proper-subset independence assumptions force every nonempty proper Walsh moment to vanish. The event probability is therefore determined by its count and its full-product signed count. The ballot/reflection identity gives the ordinary count, and the alternating binomial sum gives the stated Catalan parity coefficient.

## Originality

PASS for the all-horizon discrete-sign claim. Berger–Béthencourt prove persistence bounds for exchangeable and sign-invariant variables, with equality in the atomless case; the present law has atoms and may fail sign-invariance at odd horizons. The closest related finite-horizon result treats a fourth-order interaction at horizon four, not this all-\(n\) formula.

## Value

PASS. The result exactly quantifies how the only unconstrained \(n\)-way correlation changes strict persistence under \((n-1)\)-wise independence. The parity cancellation and Catalan coefficient give a natural complete finite-horizon law.

## Closest literature and limitations

Berger and Béthencourt, arXiv:2304.09031. The theorem is limited to fair signs, independence of every proper subset, and a zero barrier.
