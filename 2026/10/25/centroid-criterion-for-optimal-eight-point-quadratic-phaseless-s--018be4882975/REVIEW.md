# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The restriction of a Hermitian quadratic-evaluation form to a horizontal line is a real quartic with height-independent two leading coefficients. Four roots per line and comparison of cubic coefficients force the centroid condition; the remaining one-dimensional nullspace is generated by a full-rank matrix of determinant \(2\). If row centroids coincide, a second kernel direction exists and a real determinant cubic supplies a singular indefinite witness. The Vandermonde argument rules out rank-one and semidefinite kernel matrices. The proof covers all distinct real row nodes, arbitrary nonzero line separation, all complex coefficient triples, and the zero case. An exact-arithmetic standalone checker supplements, but is not substituted for, the proof.

**Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, not independent priority).** Conca–Edidin–Hering–Vinzant give the general Hermitian rank-two test and the eight-measurement lower bound; neither is the two-row geometric criterion. Przybyłek–Siedlecki give a general degree-two phaseless interpolation procedure based on twenty-five samples, not this sharp eight-node equivalence. Wellershoff and Liehr analyze entire parallel lines and broader holomorphic function classes, not these finite degree-two sampling arrays. Direct semantic comparisons found no matching parallel-line centroid theorem. Residual risk: an unindexed finite Vandermonde-frame construction could contain an equivalent formula.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The result attains the global complex dimension-three minimum of eight magnitude measurements with an elementary structured point-evaluation design. Its complete if-and-only-if criterion exposes a codimension-one failure geometry and provides both success and failure for explicit sampling arrays. No noise-conditioning or higher-degree extension is claimed.
