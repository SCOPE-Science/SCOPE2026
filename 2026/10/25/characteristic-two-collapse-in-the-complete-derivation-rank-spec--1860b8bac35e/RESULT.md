# Characteristic-two collapse in the complete derivation-rank spectrum of a truncated free algebra

## Finding
Let \(q\) be any prime power, \(k=\mathbb F_q\), and
\[
A=k\langle x,y\rangle/(x,y)^3.
\]
This is a seven-dimensional associative unital algebra. For \(0\le r\le6\), write
\[
N_r(q)=\#\{D\in\operatorname{Der}_k(A):\operatorname{rank}_k D=r\}.
\]
Put \(u=q^2-1\), \(g=q(q-1)(q^2-1)=|\operatorname{GL}_2(k)|\), and \(c=q^2(q-1)\).

If \(q\) is odd, the **complete** rank distribution is
\[
\begin{aligned}
N_0&=1,\\
N_1&=(q^4-1)(q+1),\\
N_2&=(q^4-1)(q^4-q),\\
N_3&=u q^6,\\
N_4&=u(q^8-q^6)+quq^7+cq^8,\\
N_5&=qu(q^8-q^7),\\
N_6&=(g-c)q^8.
\end{aligned}
\]
If \(q\) is even, the complete rank distribution is
\[
\begin{aligned}
N_0&=1,\\
N_1&=(q^4-1)(q+1),\\
N_2&=(q^4-1)(q^4-q)+(q-1)q^8,\\
N_3&=(q+1)u q^6,\\
N_4&=(q+1)u(q^8-q^6)+(g-q+1)q^8,\\
N_5&=N_6=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
In both cases \(\sum_{r=0}^6 N_r(q)=q^{12}\), and every displayed positive stratum actually occurs. Thus the maximal rank of a derivation drops **from six in every odd characteristic to four in characteristic two**, even though the derivation space has dimension twelve in all characteristics.

## Assumptions and scope
All derivations are \(k\)-linear associative-algebra derivations and all ranks refer to their maps on the full seven-dimensional vector space \(A\). In particular, \(D(1)=0\). The ideal \((x,y)\) is generated by the two noncommuting variables, and the quotient kills all words of degree three and greater, not words of degree two. The theorem holds over every finite field, not merely prime fields, and for all characteristics including two.

The natural path-length filtration is \(A=k1\oplus V\oplus W\), with
\[
V=kx\oplus ky,\qquad W=V\otimes_k V
=\operatorname{span}_k\{x^2,xy,yx,y^2\}.
\]
The radical has square \(W\) and cube zero. This two-loop truncated path algebra is the smallest free, noncommutative, radical-cube-zero algebra in which a derivation has a nontrivial induced tensor-square action.

## Proof
**1. Parameterization of all derivations.** A derivation is determined by its values on \(x,y\). Suppose their constant terms are \(a,b\). Since \(xxy=0\), the coefficient of \(x^2\) in \(D(xxy)\) is \(b\); since \(xyy=0\), the coefficient of \(y^2\) in \(D(xyy)\) is \(a\). Therefore \(a=b=0\) in *every* characteristic. Conversely, assigning arbitrary elements of \(V\oplus W\) to \(x,y\) extends uniquely to a derivation, because Leibniz differentiation preserves the ideal generated by cubic words. Hence \(\dim_k\operatorname{Der}_k(A)=2(2+4)=12\).

Relative to \(V\oplus W\), a derivation has the block matrix
\[
D=\begin{pmatrix}M&0\\ B&T_M\end{pmatrix},
\quad M\in M_2(k),\quad B\in M_{4\times2}(k),\quad
T_M=M\otimes I_2+I_2\otimes M.
\]
Every pair \((M,B)\) occurs, without restrictions, and all \(q^8\) matrices \(B\) are equally frequent for a fixed \(M\). Relative to the ordered tensor basis \((xx,xy,yx,yy)\), writing \(M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
\[
T_M=\begin{pmatrix}
2a&b&b&0\\
c&a+d&0&b\\
c&0&a+d&b\\
0&c&c&2d
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

**2. The tensor-square rank.** For odd characteristic, \(\operatorname{rank}T_M\) equals \(0\) if \(M=0\), equals \(2\) if \(M\ne0\) and \(\operatorname{tr}M=0\), equals \(3\) if \(\operatorname{rank}M=1\) and \(\operatorname{tr}M\ne0\), and equals \(4\) if \(\det M\ne0\) and \(\operatorname{tr}M\ne0\). To see this, a nonscalar \(M\) is cyclic and is similar to
\[
C=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\delta\\1&\tau\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \delta=\det M,\quad\tau=\operatorname{tr}M.
\]
The displayed matrix of \(T_C\) has determinant \(4\delta\tau^2\). If \(\tau=0\) and \(C\ne0\), its first and fourth columns are dependent, its middle two columns coincide, and its first and second columns are linearly independent; hence its rank is two. If \(\delta=0\) and \(\tau\ne0\), \(C\) is diagonalizable with eigenvalues \(0,\tau\), giving tensor-square eigenvalues \(0,\tau,\tau,2\tau\), hence rank three. The scalar case follows from \(T_{aI}=2aI_4\).

For characteristic two, \(T_{aI}=0\), while *every nonscalar* \(M\) has \(\operatorname{rank}T_M=2\). Indeed a nonscalar \(M\) is cyclic; in the basis for \(C\) above the first and fourth columns of \(T_C\) are proportional, the middle columns differ by \(\tau\) times the first, and the first and second columns are independent. These statements also cover the trace-zero and inseparable cases without assuming diagonalizability.

**3. A rank-additivity lemma with an exact fiber law.** For any fixed \(M,T_M\), elementary elimination gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}D=\operatorname{rank}M+\operatorname{rank}T_M+
\operatorname{rank}\bigl(\ker M\xrightarrow{\,B\,}W/\operatorname{im}T_M\bigr).
\]
Let \(h=\dim\ker M\) and \(j=4-\operatorname{rank}T_M\). As \(B\) varies over all \(4\times2\) matrices, the induced map from \(\ker M\) to the \(j\)-dimensional quotient is uniformly distributed over the \(q^{hj}\) possible linear maps; every map has \(q^{8-hj}\) lifts. If \(M=0\), this becomes the rank distribution of a uniform \(4\times2\) matrix:
\[
H_0=1,\quad H_1=\frac{(q^4-1)(q^2-1)}{q-1}=(q^4-1)(q+1),\quad H_2=(q^4-1)(q^4-q).
\]
For \(\operatorname{rank}M=1\), the quotient map has a one-dimensional domain. It is zero for exactly \(q^{8-j}\) choices of \(B\), and nonzero for \(q^8-q^{8-j}\) choices.

**4. Counting the \(2\times2\) leading matrices.** In odd characteristic, the five disjoint types \((\operatorname{rank}M,\operatorname{rank}T_M;\#M)\) are
\[
(0,0;1),\quad(1,2;q^2-1),\quad(1,3;q(q^2-1)),\quad
(2,2;q^2(q-1)),\quad(2,4;g-q^2(q-1)).
\]
For example, the nonzero traceless singular matrices are precisely the \(q^2-1\) nonzero nilpotent matrices. There are \(q^3\) traceless matrices in total; precisely \(q^2\) are singular (count \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&-a\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) with \(a^2+bc=0\)). Hence \(q^2(q-1)\) are invertible and traceless. For each nonzero trace \(t\), the rank-one matrices are conjugate to \(\operatorname{diag}(t,0)\); the conjugacy class has \(q(q+1)\) members, yielding \(q(q^2-1)\) rank-one matrices with nonzero trace.

In characteristic two the four types are
\[
(0,0;1),\quad (2,0;q-1)\ \text{(nonzero scalar)},\quad
(1,2;(q+1)(q^2-1)),\quad
(2,2;g-q+1)\ \text{(nonscalar invertible)}.
\]
The total number of rank-one \(2\times2\) matrices is \((q^2-1)^2/(q-1)=(q+1)(q^2-1)\), and the number of invertible matrices is \(g\).

**5. Assembly.** Insert these multiplicities into the rank-additivity lemma. For instance, in odd characteristic, nilpotent rank-one \(M\) contribute \((q^2-1)q^6\) derivations of rank three and \((q^2-1)(q^8-q^6)\) of rank four; rank-one \(M\) of nonzero trace contribute \(q(q^2-1)q^7\) of rank four and \(q(q^2-1)(q^8-q^7)\) of rank five. The invertible traceless class contributes only rank four, while invertible nontraceless matrices contribute only rank six. In even characteristic, nonzero scalar \(M\) contribute only rank two; rank-one \(M\) contribute rank three or four, and nonscalar invertibles contribute only rank four. Adding these disjoint contributions yields the two displayed formulas, completes the count, and proves the sharp maximum-rank dichotomy.

## Verification
The accompanying self-contained file `check_derivations.py` implements independent Gaussian elimination for the explicit block matrices over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_4,\mathbb F_5\). It exhaustively enumerates **all** \(2^{12}=4096\) derivations over \(\mathbb F_2\), and **all** \(3^{12}=531441\) derivations over \(\mathbb F_3\); these observed rank histograms are, respectively,
\[
(1,45,466,576,3008,0,0),
\qquad(1,320,6240,5832,217242,104976,196830).
\]
It separately enumerates every leading \(2\times2\) matrix over the four test fields, samples 250 quadratic blocks per such leading matrix for the characteristic-dependent strata, and checks the Leibniz identity on the multiplication-table basis for 160 independent random derivations in each tested field. The terminal markers are `LEIBNIZ_BASIS_OK` and `DERIVATION_SPECTRUM_OK`. Reproduce with `python3 check_derivations.py` in a working directory containing the extracted files. These finite tests do not replace the proof for arbitrary prime powers.

## Relationship to prior work
Strametz, *The Lie algebra structure on the first Hochschild cohomology group of a monomial algebra* (first posted 6 November 2001), develops the derivation/outer-derivation complex of monomial algebras using parallel paths and studies Lie-theoretic structure. It encompasses the class of algebras containing the present two-loop quotient but does not give a distribution of ranks of the underlying derivation operators over finite fields. Guo and Li's work characterizes derivations on **untruncated path algebras** and studies canonical bases and Hochschild cohomology rather than the rank spectrum on this finite quotient. Dugas, Huisgen-Zimmermann and Learned study truncated path algebras homologically, especially projective-dimension spectra of modules, a different invariant.

The published *Segre-quadric monomiality criterion for two-generated short local algebras* addresses whether an algebra with \(J^3=0\) is monomial, classifying the full-kernel-free Hilbert type occupied by this algebra; it does **not** treat ranks of derivations. The published *Exact rank spectrum of derivations of a truncated polynomial algebra* concerns the **commutative, one-generator** quotient, which has a different multiplication tensor and different derivation operators. The published *Adjoint-rank gap and Veronese centralizer stratum in a truncated free algebra* counts **inner commutator operators** on the length-four quotient, not all derivations of the length-three quotient.

The tensor-sum calculation and the finite-field matrix census above are additional work, not consequences stated by these sources. Targeted full-text and published-record comparisons found no exact distribution or statement subsuming the characteristic-two rank collapse. This is a bounded best-knowledge priority assessment, not proof of uniqueness in the literature.

## Limitations
The result is specific to two generators and the length-three truncation; it does not classify derivation ranks of arbitrary path-algebra quotients or higher truncation lengths. It is a statement about linear rank of derivations on the algebra, not their Jordan types, conjugacy orbits, or ranks in the derivation Lie algebra under the adjoint representation. The proof is complete for every finite field; no general claim about publication priority or independent validation is made. The comparison with all possibly relevant finite-dimensional monomial-algebra literature is necessarily bounded.
