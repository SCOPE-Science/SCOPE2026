# Characteristic-two loop and reversal defects of Jordan derivations on radical-square-zero quiver algebras

## Finding

**Theorem (exact square-Jordan defect of every finite quiver).** Let \(Q\) be any finite directed multigraph (parallel arrows, loops, isolated vertices and oriented cycles allowed), let \(k\) be any field, and let \(A=kQ/J^2\), with \(J\) the ideal generated by arrows. Write \(\operatorname{JDer}_{\square}(A)\) for the \(k\)-linear maps \(D:A\to A\) obeying the **square-only** Jordan rule

\[
D(x^2)=D(x)x+xD(x)\quad(x\in A),
\]

and write \(\operatorname{Der}(A)\) for ordinary derivations. For vertices \(u,v\), let \(m_{uv}\) be the number of arrows \(u\to v\) when \(u\ne v\), and let \(\ell_u\) be the number of loops at vertex \(u\). Put \(L=\sum_u\ell_u\), let \(c\) be the number of connected components of the **underlying undirected non-loop graph**, and let \(s\) count vertices having no incident non-loop arrow and exactly one loop. Then

\[
\boxed{\dim_k\bigl(\operatorname{JDer}_{\square}(A)/\operatorname{Der}(A)\bigr)=
\sum_{u\ne v}m_{uv}m_{vu}
+\begin{cases}
0,&\operatorname{char}k\ne2,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{u\ne v}\ell_u\ell_v+Lc-s,&\operatorname{char}k=2.
\end{cases}}
\]

All sums \(\sum_{u\ne v}\) are over **ordered** pairs of distinct vertices. In particular the first term counts both orientations of every opposing arrow pair.

Equivalently, if \(k=\mathbb F_q\), the exact relative number of square-Jordan maps is

\[
\frac{|\operatorname{JDer}_{\square}(A)|}{|\operatorname{Der}(A)|}=q^{\delta(Q,k)},
\]

with \(\delta(Q,k)\) the displayed nonnegative integer. This is an exact whole-space invariant, not just a bound. In characteristic different from \(2\), every square-Jordan derivation is ordinary exactly when there are no directed opposing arrow pairs. In characteristic \(2\), it is ordinary exactly when there are no directed opposing arrow pairs **and** either there are no loops, or the whole quiver consists of a single vertex with exactly one loop. The characteristic-two exceptional one-loop algebra is the dual-number algebra, where sending the loop to the identity is itself a derivation.

## Assumptions and scope

There are finitely many vertices and arrows. The field \(k\) is arbitrary, including imperfect fields and extensions of \(\mathbb F_2\). The algebra \(A\) is the finite-dimensional quotient of the path algebra by all paths of length at least \(2\). Its standard basis consists of the orthogonal vertex idempotents \(e_u\) and arrow symbols. Arrows with source \(u\) and target \(v\) span a space \(V_{uv}\) of dimension \(m_{uv}\) for \(u\ne v\), while loops span \(V_{uu}\) of dimension \(\ell_u\). The product is specified by \(e_u e_v=\delta_{uv}e_u\), \(e_u a=a\) if \(a\) starts at \(u\), \(a e_v=a\) if \(a\) ends at \(v\), and \(J^2=0\). Here \(\operatorname{JDer}_{\square}\) means the single quadratic identity, NOT a convention that additionally assumes the triple-product Jordan identity.

## Proof

**1. Normalize the primitive idempotents without dividing by two.** Since \(D\) is linear, the square rule polarizes to

\[
D(xy+yx)=D(x)y+xD(y)+D(y)x+yD(x).\tag{1}
\]

Idempotence gives \(D(e_u)=D(e_u)e_u+e_uD(e_u)\). Thus \(D(e_u)\) consists only of non-loop arrows incident to \(u\), since the coefficient of \(e_u\), or of any loop at \(u\), would equal twice itself and so must be zero. Applying (1) to distinct orthogonal vertices shows that their coefficients on each arrow are negatives. Define \(b\) as the uniquely determined linear combination of **non-loop arrows** with \(b_a=-[D(e_{\mathrm{source}(a)})]_a\). Then \(D(e_u)=[b,e_u]\) for all \(u\). Subtracting the ordinary inner derivation \(\operatorname{ad}_b\), we reduce to maps \(E\) satisfying \(E(e_u)=0\). Because \(b\in J\) and \(J^2=0\), subtracting it does not change the values on arrows. This normalization is canonical and works in every characteristic.

**2. The non-loop-arrow fibers.** Let \(a:u\to v\) be a non-loop arrow. Applying (1) to \(e_u,a\) and to \(e_v,a\) forces

\[
E(a)\in V_{uv}\oplus V_{vu}.
\]

There are no other possible coefficients. Since all products of arrows are zero, every linear map \(V_{uv}\to V_{uv}\oplus V_{vu}\), specified independently for each ordered pair \(u\ne v\), satisfies the square-Jordan equations involving these arrows. However the **Leibniz** rule for \(e_u a=a\) and \(a e_v=a\) forces the reverse-direction component to vanish. Thus the precise non-loop contribution to the quotient dimension is

\[
\sum_{u\ne v}\dim\operatorname{Hom}_k(V_{uv},V_{vu})
=\sum_{u\ne v}m_{uv}m_{vu}.
\tag{2}
\]

**3. Loop fibers outside characteristic two.** Let \(z\in V_{uu}\). The conditions obtained by polarizing it with every vertex idempotent rule out non-loop-arrow components of \(E(z)\). In characteristic different from \(2\), they also rule out loops and idempotents at any other vertex, leaving only \(E(z)\in k e_u\oplus V_{uu}\). The square rule at \(z^2=0\) then makes the coefficient of \(e_u\) zero, as it would give \(2\lambda z=0\). Hence any normalized square-Jordan map on loops preserves each \(V_{uu}\) separately and is already a derivation there. This proves that (2) is the entire defect when \(\operatorname{char}k\ne2\).

**4. Loop fibers in characteristic two.** Suppose \(\operatorname{char}k=2\). Polarization with all vertex idempotents still eliminates every non-loop-arrow component of \(E(z)\), but imposes no restriction on the loop components or the semisimple diagonal \(\sum_v\lambda_v e_v\). For any non-loop arrow \(a:u\to v\), polarization with \(z\) gives

\[
0=E(z)a+aE(z)=(\lambda_u+\lambda_v)a,
\]

since all radical products vanish. Therefore the diagonal coefficients are equal along every arrow and hence constant on each connected component of the underlying non-loop graph. Conversely, that condition suffices: the diagonal image is a linear combination of **component identities**, central in \(A\), so its symmetrized product with any element is zero in characteristic \(2\); every loop-to-loop map also satisfies all polarized equations because \(J^2=0\) and loops have the same source and target. Consequently, after idempotent normalization, the independent characteristic-two loop data are

\[
E|_{L_Q}\in\operatorname{Hom}_k(L_Q,L_Q)\oplus\operatorname{Hom}_k(L_Q,k^c),
\qquad L_Q=\bigoplus_u V_{uu}.
\tag{3}
\]

Their dimension is \(L^2+Lc\). To determine which of these maps satisfy Leibniz, use \(e_u z=z=z e_u\) and \(e_vz=ze_v=0\) for \(v\ne u\): a Leibniz map must send \(z\in V_{uu}\) into \(V_{uu}\oplus k e_u\). For the \(k e_u\)-coefficient to be allowed, \(u\) must have **no incident non-loop arrows**, because \(za=az=0\) but \(e_u a\) or \(a e_u\) is nonzero whenever such an arrow is present. Even when the vertex is non-loop-isolated, if it has at least two loops \(z,w\), applying Leibniz to \(zw=0\) forces both diagonal coefficients to vanish (the two distinct loop basis vectors are linearly independent). Thus a nonzero idempotent image in a genuine derivation is possible precisely for the unique loop at a non-loop-isolated, one-loop vertex. There are exactly \(s\) independent choices of this kind. All other derivation loop data are precisely \(\bigoplus_u\operatorname{End}_k(V_{uu})\), of dimension \(\sum_u\ell_u^2\).

Subtracting these ordinary derivation parameters from (3) yields

\[
L^2+Lc-\sum_u\ell_u^2-s
=\sum_{u\ne v}\ell_u\ell_v+Lc-s.\tag{4}
\]

**5. Complete decomposition, dimensions and rigidity criterion.** The normalized arrow and loop parameters from steps 2–4 are independent because all products of arrows vanish and (1) is bilinear. Conversely, each displayed parameter family satisfies the **original** square identity: the basis-square equations and all polarization equations imply it for arbitrary linear combinations. The inner-normalization subspace is common to square-Jordan maps and derivations, and the normalized inclusion is explicit, so their vector-space quotient has dimension (2) plus (4) in characteristic two, or (2) in other characteristics. For a finite field, the quotient of their cardinalities is the field size to this dimension.

For the rigidity criterion, each displayed term is nonnegative. The first vanishes exactly when no reverse-direction pair of non-loop arrows exists. When \(\operatorname{char}k=2\), if \(L=0\) the rest vanish. If \(L>0\) and the quiver has at least two vertices, then \(Lc-s>0\) or a cross-loop term is positive (in fact \(Lc-s\ge0\), with equality possible only in a one-vertex, one-loop component with no other component); hence there is a proper square-Jordan derivation. For a one-vertex quiver, the loop defect is \(L-1\) if \(L=1\), and \(L\) if \(L\ge2\), so it vanishes exactly when the vertex has one loop (or no loops). This establishes the asserted sharp criterion. \(\square\)

## Verification

A standalone Python script `verify_quiver_jordan_defect.py` separately constructs the finite algebra multiplication table from every listed quiver, constructs the integer coefficient equations for (i) square-Jordan maps and (ii) Leibniz maps, and computes the respective solution dimensions over \(\mathbb F_2\), \(\mathbb F_3\), and \(\mathbb F_5\). In **16** test families, including one-vertex loop cases, parallel opposing arrows, disconnected loops, a directed three-cycle, and graphs with both loops and opposing arrows, all observed dimension differences equal the formula. Its success line is `QUIVER_JORDAN_DEFECT_OK 16 examples`. To replay, run `python verify_quiver_jordan_defect.py` using Python 3. The finite checks are reproducibility checks, NOT a universal proof; steps 1–5 provide that proof over arbitrary fields.

## Relationship to prior work

- Yanbo Li, Leon van Wyk and Feng Wei, *Jordan Derivations and Antiderivations of Generalized Matrix Algebras*, arXiv:1202.2527v1 (public 2012-02-12), give a **general necessary-and-sufficient Jordan-map parameterization in arbitrary characteristic** (Proposition 3.2) for two-block Morita-context matrix algebras, and specialize it to a derivation-plus-antiderivation decomposition under **\(2\)-torsion-freeness** and zero pairings (Theorem 3.11). Recursive vertex block splitting places many radical-square-zero quiver algebras within this general framework, so operator parameterization and two-block arrow reversal are not claimed to be new. The contribution asserted here is the **evaluated, graph-invariant closed dimension formula**, especially the characteristic-two loop-central correction and sharp global rigidity criterion. Extracting that formula requires following all component and loop constraints rather than simply substituting one parameter into the published theorem.
- Yanbo Li and Feng Wei, *Jordan Derivations and Lie derivations on Path Algebras*, arXiv:1203.4925v1 (public 2012-03-22), proves rigidity for a class of acyclic path algebras. Those rigid cases are consistent with the zero-obstruction special cases here, but the source does not enumerate the defect for arbitrary radical-square-zero quivers with loops, opposing arrows and characteristic two.
- Mykola Khrypchenko, *Jordan derivations of finitary incidence rings*, arXiv:1510.00944v1 (public 2015-10-04), uses a stronger definition requiring square and triple-product identities. It is relevant to distinguishing definitions but does not assert the characteristic-two square-only defect count here.
- Peter Danchev et al., *Jordan Derivation On Generalized Triangular Matrix Rings*, arXiv:2507.06871v1 (public 2025-07-09), handles square-only maps on certain generalized triangular rings with faithful final-column bimodules; that gives zero-defect results for overlapping triangular subclasses, not for arbitrary cyclic radical-square-zero quivers with loops.

The novelty assessment is comparison of exact hypotheses and implications, not a proof that no equivalent formula occurs in all literature.

## Limitations

The statement is about \(k\)-linear maps on **radical-square-zero quiver quotients**, not the untruncated path algebra, arbitrary admissible bound-quiver ideals, nonlinear maps or Jordan homomorphisms. The quotient counts maps, not automorphism-conjugacy orbits. The earlier general two-block Jordan-map parameterization already structurally encompasses recursive specializations to quiver algebras. The odd-characteristic reverse-arrow phenomenon is already motivated by earlier antiderivations; any priority claim is strictly for the non-tabulated closed all-quiver defect invariant and characteristic-two loop correction, not for the general parameterization. The possibility that the formula is regarded as an already-covered corollary of that broad framework is a material residual originality risk. Publication-priority verification is not exhaustive, and no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
