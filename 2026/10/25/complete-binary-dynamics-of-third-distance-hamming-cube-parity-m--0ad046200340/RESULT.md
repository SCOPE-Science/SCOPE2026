# Complete binary dynamics of third-distance Hamming-cube parity maps

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\), write \(V_n=\mathbb F_2^n\), let \(X_n=\mathbb F_2^{V_n}\), and put \(M_n=2^n\). For a choice \(b\in\{0,1\}\), define the synchronous binary update
\[
 (F_{n,b}x)(v)=b\,x(v)+\sum_{\substack{u\in V_n\\d_H(u,v)=3}}x(u)\pmod2.
\]
Thus \(b=0\) excludes the site itself and \(b=1\) includes it. Define
\[
 c_n=\begin{cases}1,&n\equiv3\pmod4,\\0,&n\equiv0,1,2\pmod4,\end{cases}
 \qquad
 r_n=\begin{cases}
 2^{n-1},&n\equiv0,3\pmod4,\\
 2^{n-1}-2^{(n-1)/2},&n\equiv1\pmod4,\\
 2^{n-2}-2^{(n-2)/2},&n\equiv2\pmod4.
 \end{cases}
\]
**Exact normal form:** \((F_{n,b}+(b+c_n)I)^2=0\), and the square-zero operator \(N_n=F_{n,b}+(b+c_n)I\) has binary rank exactly \(r_n\), independent of \(b\). Its Jordan normal form over \(\mathbb F_2\) consists of \(r_n\) blocks of size two and \(M_n-2r_n\) zero blocks of size one.

**All trajectories and exact counts:** If \(b=c_n\), then \(F_{n,b}^2=0\). Zero is the only periodic configuration. The numbers of initial configurations whose **first** hit of zero occurs at times \(0,1,2\) are, respectively,
\[
 1,\qquad 2^{M_n-r_n}-1,\qquad 2^{M_n}-2^{M_n-r_n}.
\]
If \(b\ne c_n\), then \(F_{n,b}^2=I\). Every initial configuration is periodic, with exactly \(2^{M_n-r_n}\) fixed configurations and exactly
\[
 \frac{2^{M_n}-2^{M_n-r_n}}2
\]
primitive two-cycles. There are no other periods or transient lengths. Equivalently, \(F_{n,b}\) is completely linearly conjugate to the sum of the corresponding size-one and size-two Jordan blocks, with eigenvalue \(b+c_n\). In particular, the **self-inclusive versus self-exclusive dynamical roles exchange only when** \(n\equiv3\pmod4\).

## Assumptions and scope
The vertices of the graph are all binary vectors of dimension \(n\), and \(d_H\) is Hamming distance. The interaction graph is precisely the third relation of the binary Hamming association scheme. There are \(\binom n3\) neighbors of every vertex, without loops; a loop input is added only when \(b=1\). Every sum, rank, and Jordan form refers to the field \(\mathbb F_2\). The full configuration space has dimension \(M_n\) over this field and contains \(2^{M_n}\) configurations. All statements apply to every dimension \(n\ge3\), not merely a bounded family.

## Proof
**1. Group algebra and the four residue classes.** Let \(t_i\) translate a labeling by toggling vertex coordinate \(i\). These commuting operators satisfy \(t_i^2=I\), and the no-self update is
\[
 A_n=\sum_{1\le i<j<k\le n}t_it_jt_k.
\]
Identify the regular module with the square-zero polynomial algebra
\[
 R_n=\mathbb F_2[y_1,\ldots,y_n]/(y_1^2,\ldots,y_n^2),\qquad t_i=1+y_i.
\]
Writing \(e_j\) for the degree-\(j\) elementary symmetric polynomial in the \(y_i\), expansion of all triples gives
\[
 A_n=\binom n3 I+\binom{n-1}2 e_1+(n-2)e_2+e_3\quad\text{in }R_n.
\]
By direct expansion, \(e_1e_2=e_3\) in characteristic two: each squarefree triple is counted three times and repeated indices vanish. Parity of the binomial coefficients yields the exact four-row table
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 n\bmod4&A_n&c_n\\\hline
0&e_1(1+e_2)&0\\
1&e_2(1+e_1)&0\\
2&e_1e_2&0\\
3&1+e_1+e_2+e_3&1
\end{array}.
\]
Since \(t_it_jt_k\) is an involution, the Frobenius identity in characteristic two gives \(A_n^2=\binom n3 I=c_nI\). Equivalently \(N_n=A_n+c_nI\) satisfies \(N_n^2=0\). Adding \(bI\) does not change \(N_n\).

**2. A rank lemma for the quadratic exterior operator.** In \(R_q\), multiplication by \(e_2\) has rank
\[
\operatorname{rank}(m_{e_2})=
\begin{cases}
2^{q-1}-2^{q/2-1},&q\text{ even},\\
2^{q-1}-2^{(q-1)/2},&q\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
Only the odd case is required below. For \(q=2m+1\), the alternating coefficient matrix of \(e_2\) is \(J+I\); its kernel consists of the all-ones vector and has dimension one. An invertible linear generator change therefore puts the element into the standard form \(\sum_{j=1}^m u_jv_j\), with one unused generator \(w\). Every linear generator change is an algebra automorphism, since squares of linear combinations vanish in characteristic two.

Multiplication by this element is a square-zero differential on the tensor product of the \(m\) four-dimensional algebras \(\mathbb F_2[u_j,v_j]/(u_j^2,v_j^2)\) and the two-dimensional algebra generated by \(w\). In each four-dimensional factor, the differential sends \(1\) to \(u_jv_j\) and annihilates the other three basis elements, leaving two-dimensional homology. The field Künneth theorem gives total homology dimension \(2^{m+1}\). A square-zero linear endomorphism on a vector space of dimension \(M\) has homology dimension \(M-2\operatorname{rank}\). Thus the required odd-dimensional rank is \(2^{2m}-2^m\). (The even-dimensional case follows from the standard nondegenerate alternating form and the same tensor calculation.)

**3. Rank in each residue class.** In the \(n\equiv0\pmod4\) case, \(N_n=e_1(1+e_2)\), and \(1+e_2\) is a unit because \(e_2^2=0\). Multiplication by the nonzero linear form \(e_1\) has rank \(2^{n-1}\), since it can be made a coordinate generator.

For \(n\equiv1\pmod4\), \(N_n=e_2(1+e_1)\), with a unit factor \(1+e_1\). The odd-dimensional quadratic rank lemma gives \(2^{n-1}-2^{(n-1)/2}\).

For \(n\equiv2\pmod4\), put \(z=e_1\) and retain \(y_1,\ldots,y_{n-1}\) as the other generators. This is an invertible linear generator change, since \(y_n=z+\sum_{i<n}y_i\). Let \(s=\sum_{i<n}y_i\). Then
\[
 e_2(y_1,\ldots,y_n)=e_2(y_1,\ldots,y_{n-1})+(z+s)s=e_2(y_1,\ldots,y_{n-1})+zs
\]
because \(s^2=0\). Consequently \(N_n=e_1e_2= z e_2(y_1,\ldots,y_{n-1})\). Multiplication by this element maps \(R_{n-1}\oplus zR_{n-1}\) into \(zR_{n-1}\), with rank exactly that of multiplication by \(e_2\) on the odd-dimensional algebra \(R_{n-1}\). The rank is \(2^{n-2}-2^{(n-2)/2}\).

For \(n\equiv3\pmod4\), \(N_n=e_1+e_2+e_3\). Its degree-one component is the nonzero linear form \(e_1\). We use the following elementary coordinate lemma: if a square-zero element \(f\) of \(R_n\) has a nonzero linear component, choose coordinates so that its coefficient of \(y_1\) in degree one is one; write \(f=y_1a(z)+b(z)\), where \(z=(y_2,\ldots,y_n)\) and \(a(0)=1\). Since \(a\) is a unit, the substitution \(y_1\mapsto f\), \(y_j\mapsto y_j\) for \(j\ge2\) has explicit inverse \(y_1=(f+b(z))a(z)^{-1}\). It is an algebra automorphism because all elements without constant term square to zero. Therefore \(m_f\) is conjugate to multiplication by a single coordinate and has rank \(2^{n-1}\). Applying it to \(N_n\) proves the final row.

**4. Jordan forms and dynamics.** A square-zero map of rank \(r_n\) on an \(M_n\)-dimensional vector space has exactly \(r_n\) length-two Jordan blocks and \(M_n-2r_n\) length-one blocks. Because \(F_{n,b}=(b+c_n)I+N_n\), it is either square-zero (if \(b=c_n\)) or an involution (if \(b\ne c_n\)). In the square-zero case the kernel has \(2^{M_n-r_n}\) elements and is exactly the set that reaches zero within one update; every other point first reaches zero after two. In the involutive case the same kernel is the fixed-point set, and all remaining points partition into disjoint two-cycles. This proves each count, boundary, and quantifier above.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` directly constructs both the unlooped and looped adjacency updates using Hamming-distance-three masks. It performs exact binary row reduction to verify the predicted shifted rank for every \(n=3,\ldots,12\), tests square identities by exact convolution in all these dimensions, checks every standard basis vector for both loop choices when \(3\le n\le8\), and checks independent random configurations for all tested dimensions. It exhaustively enumerates all \(256\) initial configurations at \(n=3\) and all \(65{,}536\) at \(n=4\) to confirm the complete orbit and hitting-time counts. Its expected output begins `VERIFY_OK`. These finite tests support but do not replace the unrestricted proof.

## Relationship to prior work
The published *A mod-four orbit census for parity automata on distance-two Boolean cubes* (2026, published-finding corpus identifier `2026/10/11/SCOPE-a-mod-four-orbit-census-for-parity-automata-on-distance-two-bool--f288ef2fe8b4`) proves an exact shifted-adjacency rank and orbit census for the **second** Hamming shell. Its proof already supplies a useful quadratic Künneth calculation, which is reused here and is not claimed as new. Its stated scope explicitly excludes higher Hamming shells. The present third-shell claim requires a different four-class group-algebra expansion, a multiplication-by-\(e_1e_2\) reduction in the residue-two case, and the reversal of which loop choice is involutive in precisely one residue class. The displayed rank and dynamics for the third shell are neither stated nor implied by the second-shell rank alone.

Key's 2010 survey of Hamming-graph design codes defines the distance-\(k\) Hamming graphs, including \(k=3\), but the inspected adjacency-code results on pages 38–41 concern the distance-one Hamming graph and do not give the third-shell binary rank or orbit census. Blázquez-Sanz and Muñoz (2013) study group-convolution cellular automata primarily over complex states and do not calculate the modular third-shell adjacency rank. Bors (2014) provides general structural results for finite dynamical group endomorphisms but no specific dimension-by-dimension Hamming-shell rank. The general structural results alone do not supply the numerical counts without the rank computation above.

## Limitations
Only the exact third-Hamming-distance parity neighborhood and the two self-loop choices are classified. This does not cover arbitrary Hamming shells, nonbinary alphabets, noisy updates, or asynchronous dynamics. The proof reuses standard alternating-form normal forms and Künneth theory; their use is not asserted original. A potentially earlier coding-theoretic rank formula for the third Hamming relation, especially work not accessible in full, remains a historical-priority risk. No independent audit has been performed.
