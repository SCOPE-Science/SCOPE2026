# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Direct stars-and-bars moment counting gives the precise covariance as a coefficient of a rational formal series. An exact cancellation leaves a nonnegative coefficient sequence, with separate parity analysis at three and four parts. The proof treats all integers \(n\ge1\), \(1\le k\le n\), including degenerate cases. Independent exact-algorithm checks reported 19,745 assertions; bounded enumeration supports but does not replace the proof.

**Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority risk.** Heubach–Mansour's first-moment formula for the number of levels at fixed composition length does not determine the mixed moment with unit-part count, which is essential for the new sign classification. Louchard–Prodinger treats compositions with no adjacent equalities, while Gafni treats the maximum equal run. The original Knopfmacher–Prodinger paper (1998) explicitly analyzes equal adjacencies; only its author abstract and a contemporary description could be accessed, so possible implicit precedence remains unresolved. Prior published result records concerning composition maximum or fixed-binary-word run transitions have different statistics and do not imply the result.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A complete finite-parameter sign classification for two natural statistics — singleton abundance and local equality clustering — reveals a sharp three-to-four-part reversal for even totals and an exact parity boundary. The fact is structural rather than an isolated numeric calculation or a routine fixed-instance computation. It is restricted to uniform ordered fixed-length compositions, with no assertion for weighted, cyclic, or higher-order analogues.

**Closest literature and limits.** Heubach–Mansour (arXiv:math/0310197v1, 2003), Louchard–Prodinger (DMTCS, 2002), Knopfmacher–Prodinger (EJC, 1998), and Gafni (arXiv:0907.5553v1, 2009). Historical priority requires independent assessment because the 1998 full text was unavailable. The verifier is standard-library-only and the unrestricted proof is analytic, not experimental.
