# Complete dihedral homology characters of higher-independence complexes of cycles

## Finding
For every pair of integers \(r\ge2\) and \(n\ge r+2\), let
\[
X_{n,r}=\operatorname{Ind}_{r}(C_n)
\]
be the higher-independence complex of the simple cycle with vertices \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). A vertex subset is a face exactly when each connected component of the induced graph has at most \(r\) vertices. The natural dihedral action is generated by \(\rho(i)=i+1\) and \(\sigma(i)=-i\).

Set \(d=r+2\), and write \(n=dk+t\) with \(k\ge1\) and \(0\le t<d\). Put
\[
q=\begin{cases}rk-1,&t=0,\\rk+t-2,&1\le t<d.\end{cases}
\]
The only nonzero reduced rational homology is in degree \(q\); its dimension is \(r+1\) if \(t=0\), and \(1\) otherwise. This homology concentration and its rank are the published nonequivariant theorem of Deshpande and Singh, not a new claim.

**New equivariant theorem.** The complete character \(\chi_{n,r}\) of the \(D_n\)-action on \(\widetilde H_q(X_{n,r};\mathbb Q)\) is given by the following formulas. For every rotation \(\rho^a\), set \(g=\gcd(n,a)\) (so \(g=n\) for \(a=0\)). Then
\[
\boxed{\chi_{n,r}(\rho^a)=(-1)^{q+1+g}\bigl(d\,\mathbf1_{d\mid g}-1\bigr).}
\]
For reflections, set \(u=\lceil r/2\rceil\), \(v=\lfloor r/2\rfloor\), define the polynomials \(A_j(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{j}(-z)^i\), and form the rational power series
\[
F_{a,b}(z)=\frac{z(1+z)A_a(z)A_b(z)}{1-(-z)^d}.
\]
Write \([z^L]F\) for the coefficient of \(z^L\) in the formal series at zero. If \(n=2m+1\), every reflection has trace
\[
\boxed{\chi_{n,r}(\rho^a\sigma)=(-1)^{q+1}[z^{m+1}]F_{u,v}(z).}
\]
If \(n=2m\), there are two reflection types:
\[
\boxed{\chi_{n,r}(\rho^a\sigma)=(-1)^{q+1}
\begin{cases}
[z^{m+1}]F_{u,u}(z),&a\text{ even},\\
[z^{m}]F_{v,v}(z),&a\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}}
\]
Thus the rational representation is determined on **every element** of the natural dihedral group; no integral module or equivariant homotopy equivalence is asserted. The reflection formula is a short periodic coefficient extraction, not an enumeration of exponentially many simplices. In particular, every reflection trace belongs to \(\{-1,0,1\}\).

For example, at \((r,n)=(2,6)\), the unique reduced homology is one-dimensional in degree two. The generator rotation acts by \(-1\), and the two types of reflection act by \(-1\) and \(+1\). At \((r,n)=(2,8)\), the rank-three degree-three homology has rotation traces \((3,1,-1,1,3,1,-1,1)\) in exponent order and all reflection traces \(+1\). These representations are not determined by the nonequivariant sphere ranks.

## Assumptions and scope
The group \(D_n\) has \(2n\) elements \(\rho^a\) and \(\rho^a\sigma\), where \(0\le a<n\). All complexes are finite abstract simplicial complexes with an empty face, and all homology characters are over \(\mathbb Q\). For \(n\ge r+2\), a subset is a face exactly when its cyclic binary characteristic word has **no consecutive block of \(r+1\) ones**, including wrap-around blocks. This characterization includes the exclusion of the all-one word. The stated homology degree is positive for \(r\ge2\) in the given range.

## Proof
**1. Concentration and equivariant Euler trace.** Theorem 4.6 of Deshpande and Singh gives \(X_{n,r}\simeq\bigvee^{r+1}S^q\) when \(d\mid n\), and \(X_{n,r}\simeq S^q\) otherwise. Its proof is nonequivariant; no equivariant Morse matching is assumed here. Let \(h\in D_n\). In the augmented simplicial chain complex, a setwise fixed face \(S\) is a union of \(b\) vertex orbits of \(h\). If nonempty, the sign of the induced permutation of the oriented simplex is \((-1)^{|S|-b}\). Its alternating chain trace is therefore \((-1)^{b-1}\); the empty face contributes \(-1\). Consequently
\[
\widetilde L(h)=-\sum_{S\in X_{n,r}:hS=S}(-1)^{\#\mathrm{orbits}_{h}(S)}.
\]
Since reduced homology is concentrated in degree \(q\), the Hopf trace formula gives
\[
\chi_{n,r}(h)=(-1)^{q+1}
\sum_{S\in X_{n,r}:hS=S}(-1)^{\#\mathrm{orbits}_{h}(S)}.\tag{1}
\]
Here the empty face is included and has zero selected orbits.

**2. Rotation trace as a cyclic run partition function.** For \(h=\rho^a\), put \(g=\gcd(n,a)\). The vertex orbits are residue classes modulo \(g\). An invariant face is therefore a \(g\)-periodic binary word, and it is admissible if and only if that word has a zero and no cyclic run of ones longer than \(r\). Each occupied residue class contributes one selected orbit. Denote the resulting alternating word sum by \(Z_r(g)\).

Use states \(0,1,\ldots,r\) recording the number of successive ones since the last zero. A zero resets the state to zero with weight \(+1\); a one increments a state less than \(r\) with weight \(-1\). The weighted transition matrix \(T_r\) has characteristic polynomial
\[
\det(\lambda I-T_r)=\lambda^{r+1}-\lambda^r+\lambda^{r-1}-\cdots+(-1)^{r+1}
=\frac{\lambda^{r+2}-(-1)^{r+2}}{\lambda+1}.
\]
A cyclic admissible word has a zero, so its compatible state sequence is unique; conversely any closed state walk yields such a word. Hence \(Z_r(g)=\operatorname{tr}(T_r^g)\). The eigenvalues of \(T_r\) are all \(d\) numbers \(-\omega^j\), with \(\omega^d=1\), except \(-1\). Thus the roots-of-unity sum gives
\[
Z_r(g)=(-1)^g\bigl(d\,\mathbf1_{d\mid g}-1\bigr).
\]
Substitution into (1) proves the rotation formula, including the identity element.

**3. Reflection orbit words and boundary run limits.** A reflection folds the cyclic binary word into an interval of orbit variables. An occupied run of \(\ell\) orbit variables next to an axis through a fixed vertex expands to \(2\ell-1\) occupied original vertices; hence it must satisfy \(\ell\le u=\lceil r/2\rceil\). Next to an axis through an edge, it expands to \(2\ell\) occupied vertices and must satisfy \(\ell\le v=\lfloor r/2\rfloor\). Internal runs of interval variables have length at most \(r\).

For odd \(n=2m+1\), the two axes of a reflection cut the cycle at one vertex and the opposite edge. The folded interval has \(m+1\) orbit variables, with left and right end run bounds \((u,v)\). For even \(n=2m\), \(\rho^a\sigma\) has two fixed vertices when \(a\) is even, yielding \(m+1\) interval variables and end bounds \((u,u)\). When \(a\) is odd it fixes no vertex, yielding \(m\) interval variables and end bounds \((v,v)\). In all cases the sign of a selected word is \((-1)\) per occupied orbit variable.

**4. Exact interval generating function.** Let \(W_{a,b}(L)\) be the sum \((-1)^{\#\text{ones}}\) over binary interval words of length \(L\) containing at least one zero, with every internal run of ones at most \(r\), the initial run at most \(a\), and the terminal run at most \(b\). Every such word has a unique form
\[
1^{j_0}\,0\,(1^{j_1}0)\cdots(1^{j_s}0)\,1^{j_{s+1}},
\]
where \(s\ge0\), \(0\le j_0\le a\), \(0\le j_{s+1}\le b\), and \(0\le j_i\le r\) for \(1\le i\le s\). Giving each zero weight \(z\) and each one weight \(-z\) yields
\[
\sum_{L\ge0}W_{a,b}(L)z^L
=\frac{zA_a(z)A_b(z)}{1-zA_r(z)}
=\frac{z(1+z)A_a(z)A_b(z)}{1-(-z)^d}
=F_{a,b}(z).
\]
All-one words are excluded because \(n\ge r+2\). This proves each of the three reflection formulas by Step 3 and (1). The coefficients are periodic up to sign with period \(d\), because the numerator has degree at most \(d+1\), and only the finitely many residue classes of the denominator contribute. Using \((1+z)A_b(z)=1-(-z)^{b+1}\), the numerator is a difference of two shifted alternating blocks, each coefficient being \(0\) or \(\pm1\). For odd \(r\) with two vertex axes, the sole possible overlap between consecutive denominator periods at exponents \(1\) and \(d+1\) cancels, since \((-1)^d=-1\). Thus the reflection coefficients in the three cases lie in \(\{-1,0,1\}\). The character determines the rational representation of a finite group uniquely. QED.

## Verification
The bundled standalone script `check_characters.py` constructs every vertex orbit of each dihedral symmetry independently, enumerates the corresponding invariant cyclic subsets, checks each circular run condition directly, and compares the resulting signed Lefschetz trace with the formulas. It passes **1,200 exact signed fixed-face comparisons** over \(2\le r\le6\), \(r+2\le n\le\min(16,r+14)\) and every rotation and reflection. It also builds simplicial boundary matrices independently and checks the published degree and Betti number pattern in **18 small instances** over \(\mathbb F_{101}\). These computations test finite instances; the all-parameter theorem follows from the proof above and the cited homotopy theorem, not from extrapolation.

Reproduce with `python3 check_characters.py` from the directory containing the extracted supplied file. No external software packages are required.

## Relationship to prior work
Deshpande and Singh, *Higher Independence Complexes of graphs and their homotopy types* (2020), Theorem 4.6, determines the nonequivariant wedge-of-spheres decomposition for exactly these complexes. It supplies the homology concentration used here but does not provide a group action, character, or dihedral quotient. The 2021 work of Abdelmalek and collaborators on chordal graphs concerns shellability and vertex decomposability of higher-independence complexes, not this cyclic character calculation.

The already recorded complete dihedral character of the **ordinary** cycle matching complex corresponds to \(r=1\), which is deliberately outside the theorem. The bounded-independence complex \(B_d(C_n)=\{S:\alpha(C_n[S])<d\}\), appearing in a separate dihedral-orientation calculation, uses an induced independence-number bound, not the induced-component-size bound defining \(\operatorname{Ind}_r(C_n)\). Its rank-one sphere dimension is independent of \(n\) for fixed \(d\), whereas the dimension above typically grows with \(n\). The finite-state circulant independence graph calculations of Wang and Li concern \(\operatorname{Ind}(\operatorname{Cay}(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,J))\) for a fixed jump set, a different simplicial construction from the independence complex of a cyclic-interval uniform hypergraph. Their critical-face rational series does not specify this character.

## Limitations
No integral \(\mathbb Z[D_n]\)-module classification, equivariant homotopy type, or torsion calculation for quotient spaces is asserted. The key nonequivariant homotopy theorem is an established external input. The new contribution is the complete rational character, obtained from a signed fixed-face argument and finite-state/interval evaluations. Unindexed papers or theses with an equivalent full character formula remain a possible originality risk. The standalone finite tests are not independent certification of the infinite statement.
