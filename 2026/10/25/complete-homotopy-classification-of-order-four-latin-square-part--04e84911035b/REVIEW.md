# Scientific review of the order-four partial-transversal classification

## Correctness
The complete claim is checked through a finite certificate, rather than inferred from approximate Betti numbers. Every order-four Latin square reduces by isotopy to one of four enumerated reduced squares. Explicit independent row, column and symbol permutations map those to the two representative tables. For the cyclic table, every edge has two triangle cofaces, all vertex links are connected circles, and a consistent integral orientation exists; Euler characteristic zero then implies an actual torus. For the Klein table, all thirty-two recorded free-face collapses are individually checked and leave a connected \((16,24)\)-graph with cycle rank nine. Both face enumerators agree. The finite checker and regeneration script were executed successfully from their packaged directory.

## Originality
The closest primary texts were Wanless (2002), Evans--Mammoliti--Wanless (arXiv:1911.05912), and Hämäläinen (arXiv:0907.1481). The first two classify or count transversals and maximal transversal lengths, not the homotopy type of the entire down-closed partial-transversal complex. The third studies a fundamentally different simplicial complex whose *vertices are Latin trades* and gives a different homology table for the same two group names. Additional searches considered rainbow matchings of edge-coloured complete bipartite graphs, complements of Latin-square conflict graphs, torus formulations and potentially stronger general independence-complex statements. Available related statements did not imply the two topological classifications. Novelty remains subject to the usual risk of an unindexed equivalent theorem.

## Value
Order four has exactly two isotopy classes, so the theorem classifies a natural complete family rather than an arbitrary isolated subgraph or a parameter chosen for convenience. The cyclic case yields a genuine closed surface with nonzero degree-two fundamental class and nontrivial cohomological product, while the Klein case has a one-dimensional homotopy model with nine free fundamental-group generators. This substantial topological distinction refines the classical zero-versus-eight full-transversal contrast and cannot be reconstructed from counts of maximal transversal lengths.

## Limits
No higher-order classification, symmetry representation, or independent mathematical audit is asserted. The exhaustive code establishes the stated finite claims under the supplied definitions; external equivalence searching remains fallible.
