# Complete integral four-cut homology of binary hypercubes

## Finding
For every integer \(d\geq4\), let \(Q_d\) be the graph on the \(N=2^d\) binary words of length \(d\), with edges joining words differing in one coordinate. Its *ordinary four-cut complex* \(\Delta_4(Q_d)\) is generated by the complements of disconnected, four-vertex induced subgraphs. Put
\[
 f_j=2^{d-j}\binom{d}{j}\qquad (0\leq j\leq3),
\]
\[
 a_d=4f_3-f_2+f_1-f_0+1,\qquad
 b_d=\binom N3-\binom N2-28f_3-2f_2+f_1.
\]
Then its **complete reduced integral homology** is
\[
 \widetilde H_i(\Delta_4(Q_d);\mathbb Z)\cong
 \begin{cases}
 \mathbb Z^{a_d},&i=N-6,\\
 \mathbb Z^{b_d},&i=N-5,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
In particular all groups are torsion-free. Both ranks are positive for \(d\geq4\). Since the complex is pure of dimension \(N-5\), each such complex is nonshellable. The first values are \((a_4,b_4)=(25,200)\), \((a_5,b_5)=(129,3264)\), and \((a_6,b_6)=(529,34880)\).

## Assumptions and scope
All complexes are abstract simplicial complexes on the specified graph vertex set. An induced set is connected when the graph induced on those vertices is connected. The Alexander dual of \(\Delta\) on a fixed vertex set \(V\) is \(\Delta^\vee=\{\sigma\subseteq V:V\setminus\sigma\notin\Delta\}\). Let \(K=\Delta_4(Q_d)^\vee\). By the cut-complex definition, \(\sigma\in K\) precisely when \(\sigma\) contains no disconnected four-element subset. Thus every subset with at most three vertices belongs to \(K\), and its tetrahedra are exactly the connected induced four-vertex subsets of \(Q_d\).

## Proof
**1. Dimension and four-set count.** No five-element vertex set of \(Q_d\) can have every four-element subset connected. Otherwise its induced bipartite graph is connected after deletion of any vertex, hence has minimum degree at least two. Its bipartition has sizes two and three (the graph has no triangles and five vertices); each of the three vertices on the larger side must be adjacent to both on the smaller side. This would contain \(K_{2,3}\), impossible in a hypercube, where distinct vertices have at most two common neighbors. Thus \(K\) has no simplices of dimension at least four.

A connected induced four-set is either the four vertices of a coordinate square, or it spans precisely three coordinate directions and lies in a unique coordinate three-cube. The three-cube has 38 connected four-sets, of which six are its coordinate squares. Consequently the numbers of tetrahedra of \(K\) and of its three-direction kind are respectively \(f_2+32f_3\) and \(32f_3\). All edges and triangles occur in \(K\), so
\[
 (f_0(K),f_1(K),f_2(K),f_3(K))
 =\left(N,\binom N2,\binom N3,f_2+32f_3\right).
\]

**2. The tetrahedral support.** Let \(L\subseteq K\) be the subcomplex generated by all tetrahedra, and let \(S\subseteq L\) be the subcomplex generated by coordinate-square tetrahedra. In each square tetrahedron choose a triangular facet and collapse the tetrahedron across it; next collapse the remaining triangle containing the other diagonal across that diagonal. Neither free face occurs in a different coordinate-square tetrahedron. This gives a simplicial collapse of \(S\) onto a triangulation of the ordinary cubical two-skeleton of the solid \(d\)-cube. Therefore
\[
 H_1(S;\mathbb Z)=0,\qquad
 H_2(S;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{f_2-f_1+f_0-1}.
\]
The cellular boundaries of all coordinate three-cubes generate \(H_2(S;\mathbb Z)\): the full cubical chain complex of a solid cube is exact in degree two.

Every simplex in \(L\setminus S\) spans exactly three coordinate directions, and it is contained in a unique coordinate three-cube. Accordingly the *relative chain complex*, including its boundary operators, decomposes as the direct sum of the relative complexes of \((L(Q_3),S(Q_3))\), one for each of the \(f_3\) coordinate three-cubes. Here \(L(Q_3)\) is generated by connected quadruples of the three-cube and \(S(Q_3)\) by its six square quadruples.

**3. Integral local calculation.** The three-cube has \(38\) connected quadruples and \(L(Q_3)\) has exactly \((8,28,56,38)\) nonempty faces by dimension. Its signed three-boundary matrix has integral Smith form with 35 diagonal entries equal to one and three zeros; its signed two-boundary matrix has rank 21. These are exact, reproducible integer calculations (see Verification). Hence
\[
 H_3(L(Q_3);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^3,\quad
 H_2(L(Q_3);\mathbb Z)=H_1(L(Q_3);\mathbb Z)=0.
\]
The square subcomplex \(S(Q_3)\) is a two-sphere, so the long exact sequence of the local pair gives
\[
 H_3(L(Q_3),S(Q_3);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^4,
 \quad H_2(L(Q_3),S(Q_3);\mathbb Z)=H_1(L(Q_3),S(Q_3);\mathbb Z)=0.
\]
Moreover its connecting homomorphism to \(H_2(S(Q_3);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\) is surjective.

By the relative splitting in Step 2, \(H_3(L,S;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{4f_3}\), with other positive-degree relative homology zero. The connecting map \(H_3(L,S)\to H_2(S)\) is surjective, since it contains the fundamental boundary of every coordinate three-cube, which together generate \(H_2(S)\). The long exact sequence now yields
\[
 H_3(L;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{4f_3-f_2+f_1-f_0+1},
 \qquad H_2(L;\mathbb Z)=H_1(L;\mathbb Z)=0.
\]
The kernel in degree three is free because it is a subgroup of a free abelian group.

**4. Passing from \(L\) to \(K\).** These complexes have precisely the same three-simplices and no higher simplices, hence \(H_3(K;\mathbb Z)=H_3(L;\mathbb Z)\). The full two-skeleton of the simplex in \(K\) implies \(H_1(K;\mathbb Z)=0\). For the pair \((K,L)\), there are no relative chains in degree three; with \(H_2(L)=H_1(L)=0\), its long exact sequence identifies \(H_2(K)\) with a subgroup of the free group \(C_2(K,L;\mathbb Z)\), hence makes it torsion-free. Taking the reduced Euler characteristic of \(K\) from its face vector in Step 1 gives
\[
 \operatorname{rank}H_2(K;\mathbb Z)
 =\binom N3-\binom N2-28f_3-2f_2+f_1=b_d.
\]
Thus \(\widetilde H_3(K)=\mathbb Z^{a_d}\), \(\widetilde H_2(K)=\mathbb Z^{b_d}\), with all other reduced integral groups zero.

**5. Alexander duality.** Integral combinatorial Alexander duality gives
\[
 \widetilde H_i(\Delta_4(Q_d);\mathbb Z)
 \cong\widetilde H^{N-i-3}(K;\mathbb Z).
\]
As the homology of \(K\) is free, universal coefficients produce precisely the two claimed homology groups of the cut complex, without torsion. The dimension of this ordinary cut complex is \(N-5\), because its facets are complements of disconnected four-sets; such sets exist for \(d\geq4\). The positive lower-degree homology \(\widetilde H_{N-6}\) precludes shellability of a pure complex. This proves the theorem.

## Verification
Run `python3 check_four_cut_hypercube.py` with Python 3.10+ and SymPy. It independently enumerates *every* connected induced four-set for dimensions \(d=3,4,5,6\), builds the exact signed local boundary matrices, computes integer Smith form at \(d=3\), verifies all 56 local triangles, and checks the predicted ranks via independent mod-two elimination in each dimension. The output ends in `FINITE_CHECKS_OK`. The four-cube has 280 connected four-sets and 25 independent three-cycles in the Alexander dual; the five-cube has 1360 and 129, and the six-cube has 5360 and 529. The proof of all dimensions is structural; these finite checks are not an extrapolation or a substitute for the relative-chain splitting.

## Relationship to prior work
The original cut-complex framework and the relation of shellability to induced subgraphs were established in Bayer et al., *Topology of Cut Complexes of Graphs* (arXiv:2304.13675, particularly its cut-complex definitions and Proposition 4.6). That hereditary shellability result, together with the known three-cube example, can already imply nonshellability of larger cube examples; **nonshellability alone is not claimed as the novelty here**. A previously recorded calculation establishes the four-cut homotopy type of the three-cube as a wedge of three two-spheres, which agrees with the \(d=3\) boundary of the formulas. Grid calculations by Chandrakar et al. (arXiv:2408.07646) concern different Cartesian grid families or parameters. The present contribution is the explicit complete *two-degree integral homology* and the cubical relative decomposition simultaneously for all dimensions \(d\geq4\).

## Limitations
This does not determine the homotopy type or equivariant homology representations of the original cut complex; an integral homology computation does not by itself imply a sphere-wedge decomposition. The proof uses a finite exact local Smith calculation whose matrix construction is fully supplied. It does not assert bibliographic uniqueness of the result beyond the inspected sources; equivalent work outside the searched literature may exist.
