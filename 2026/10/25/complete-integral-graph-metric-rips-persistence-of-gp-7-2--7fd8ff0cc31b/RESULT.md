# Complete integral graph-metric Rips persistence of \(GP(7,2)\)

## Finding
Let \(G=GP(7,2)\) have the unit-edge graph metric, and define the inclusive Vietoris–Rips complex \(\mathcal R_r(G)=\{\varnothing\ne\sigma\subseteq V(G):d_G(x,y)\le r\text{ for all }x,y\in\sigma\}\). Then for every real \(r\ge0\), its homotopy type is
\[
\mathcal R_r(G)\simeq
\begin{cases}
\bigsqcup^{14}\mathrm{pt},&0\le r<1,\\
\bigvee^{8}S^1,&1\le r<2,\\
\bigvee^{6}S^2,&2\le r<3,\\
\mathrm{pt},&r\ge3.
\end{cases}
\]
Consequently the complete reduced integral persistent homology barcode has thirteen degree-zero intervals \([0,1)\), eight degree-one intervals \([1,2)\), and six degree-two intervals \([2,3)\). There are no other reduced bars, and all integral homology groups at all scales are torsion-free.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is specified without reliance on a drawing: \(V(G)=\{u_i,v_i:i\in\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z\}\), with undirected edges \(\{u_i,u_{i+1}\}\), \(\{u_i,v_i\}\), and \(\{v_i,v_{i+2}\}\). In the finite certificate, \(u_i\) has label \(i\) and \(v_i\) has label \(7+i\). Graph distance is shortest-path distance; the vertices, not points in the geometric realization of edges, form the metric space. Distances are integers, and each upper scale inequality is inclusive. Homotopy equivalences stated are ordinary, not equivariant, and are not claimed to be compatible choices across scales.

## Proof
**1. Scale zero, one, and three.** The graph is connected, has fourteen vertices and twenty-one edges, and has no triangles. At scales strictly below one, the Rips complex is fourteen isolated points. At scale one it is precisely the graph \(G\), a connected one-dimensional CW complex with first Betti number \(21-14+1=8\). Exhaustive breadth-first search from every vertex gives graph diameter exactly three (also certified below), so at scale three the complex is the full simplex on fourteen vertices and therefore contractible.

**2. An exact elementary-collapse certificate at scale two.** Write \(K=\mathcal R_2(G)\). Enumerating all nonempty vertex subsets and imposing pairwise graph distance at most two yields the face counts by dimension \((14,63,105,56,7)\): the count is exhaustive over \(2^{14}-1\) masks. The accompanying independent `verifier.py` reconstructs the graph and these distances, then replays the sixty-eight explicit codimension-one face pairs in `certificate.json`. For every pair \((\sigma,\tau)\) it verifies that \(\sigma\subset\tau\), \(\tau\setminus\sigma\) has one vertex, \(\sigma\) has exactly one codimension-one coface, and \(\tau\) is a facet of the complex remaining at that step. Therefore each removal is an actual elementary simplicial collapse, rather than a matrix reduction or a proposed Morse pairing. After all removals the remaining simplicial complex \(L\) has exactly fourteen vertices, fifty-one edges, and forty-four triangles, and no higher-dimensional faces. Thus \(K\searrow L\), \(L\) is two-dimensional, and \(\chi(L)=14-51+44=7\).

**3. The fundamental group vanishes.** The inclusion of the graph \(G\) into \(K\) induces a surjection on fundamental groups: any edge \(\{x,z\}\) added at scale two can be replaced by an edge path \(x-y-z\) of \(G\), and the triangle \(\{x,y,z\}\) in \(K\) realizes the homotopy. Choose the spanning tree whose thirteen edges are recorded as `tree_edges` in `certificate.json`, rooted at vertex seven. Every one of its eight non-tree edges determines a fundamental cycle of graph length five or six; their exact vertex paths, the completeness of the eight non-tree edges, and all path adjacencies are independently checked by the verifier. The Rips complex at scale two of an abstract five-cycle is a full four-simplex, while that of a six-cycle is the join of three zero-spheres, the boundary of an octahedron and hence a two-sphere. Both are simply connected. Inclusion of each five- or six-cycle into \(G\) induces a simplicial map from its scale-two Rips complex into \(K\), because ambient distances are no larger than distances measured inside the cycle. Each of the eight fundamental graph loops therefore bounds a null-homotopy in \(K\). Since these loops freely generate \(\pi_1(G)\) and \(\pi_1(G)\to\pi_1(K)\) is surjective, \(\pi_1(K)=1\). Consequently \(\pi_1(L)=1\).

**4. Integral homotopy type and barcodes.** The two-dimensional finite complex \(L\) is connected and simply connected. Integral \(H_1(L)=0\), and \(H_2(L)\) is a subgroup of the free abelian cellular chain group in degree two, so is free abelian. Euler characteristic \(7\) forces \(H_2(L;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^6\); higher reduced groups vanish by dimension. Integral Hurewicz gives \(\pi_2(L)\cong H_2(L;\mathbb Z)\). Choose six sphere maps realizing a basis and wedge them. This yields a homology equivalence \(\bigvee^{6}S^2\to L\); the homological Whitehead theorem for simply connected CW complexes makes it a homotopy equivalence. Thus \(K\simeq\bigvee^{6}S^2\) integrally, not merely over a finite field. Because the successive stages have reduced homology in mutually disjoint degrees and the final stage is contractible, the asserted intervals exhaust the integral persistence module, without any unknown inter-stage maps.

## Verification
The package contains `verifier.py` and `certificate.json`. From a directory containing both, execute `python3 verifier.py` (Python standard library only). It independently constructs the graph from the three edge families; verifies size, degrees, triangle-freeness, diameter; enumerates every scale-two simplex; validates all sixty-eight actual free-face deletions and the final face vector; checks all eight fundamental cycles against the certified spanning tree; and independently computes the two-dimensional binary boundary ranks \(13\) and \(38\). The expected exact terminal line starts with `G72_RIPS_CERTIFICATE_OK`. The binary calculation is a cross-check: the integral and homotopy conclusions rely on the explicit collapses and fundamental-group proof, not on mod-two ranks. The checker code and certificate are self-contained, and no prior computation logs or external code are required.

## Relationship to prior work
Adamaszek's *Clique complexes and graph powers* identifies graph-metric Rips complexes with flag complexes of graph powers and supplies the graph-power fundamental-group framework. Its girth-at-least-seven collapse criterion does **not** apply: \(GP(7,2)\) has a five-cycle, and the diameter-two clique complex has four-simplices whereas a closed neighborhood in the cubic original graph contains only four vertices. Adamaszek et al.'s *On Homotopy Types of Vietoris–Rips Complexes of Metric Gluings* studies wedge and short-path gluings, not seven spokes connecting the two cycles. Saleh, Titz Mite and Witzel investigate complete Rips filtrations of Platonic solids, but not generalized Petersen graphs.

The published *A sharp twelve-to-thirteen Rips collapse transition in generalized Petersen graphs* proves a scale-two circle collapse for \(GP(n,2)\) when \(n\ge13\), with an exceptional calculation at \(n=12\). Its theorem and finite boundary case do not determine any stage of \(GP(7,2)\). Another published complete Rips classification concerns \(GP(6,2)\), with a single scale-two two-sphere, but does not imply the six-sphere result for \(n=7\). The present claim is a complete integral homotopy/persistence computation specifically for the next sevenfold member, not a claim for all generalized Petersen graphs.

## Limitations
The result treats one canonical finite graph, its vertex-set shortest-path metric, and inclusive Rips filtration. It does not classify the metric realization of its edges, all generalized Petersen parameters, other graph metrics, or any equivariant homotopy type. The novelty comparison is based on inspected primary sources and targeted mathematical searches and cannot exclude all uncatalogued equivalent calculations. Every finite face and collapse assertion is replayable from the included certificate; the infinite-dimensional topological deductions use standard homotopy theorems.
