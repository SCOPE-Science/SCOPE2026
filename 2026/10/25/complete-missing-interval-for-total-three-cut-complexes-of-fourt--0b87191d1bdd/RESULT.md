# Complete missing interval for total three-cut complexes of fourth-power cycles

## Finding
For each \(n\in\{16,17,18,19,20\}\), let \(C_n^4\) be the fourth power of the cycle on \(n\) vertices. Its total three-cut complex has integral homotopy type
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_3^t(C_{16}^4)&\simeq\bigvee^3 S^6,\\
\Delta_3^t(C_{17}^4)&\simeq S^9,\\
\Delta_3^t(C_{18}^4)&\simeq S^{10},\\
\Delta_3^t(C_{19}^4)&\simeq S^{11},\\
\Delta_3^t(C_{20}^4)&\simeq\bigvee^3 S^{13}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The five orders are precisely the finite parameter interval posed by Question 49 of Carnero Bravo for \(r=4\) and \(d=3\). Together with the published boundary \(n=15\) and the published range \(n\ge21\), this determines the integral homotopy type for every nonvoid member of the fixed-fourth-power, total-three-cut cycle sequence. The contribution here is solely the five intervening cases.

## Assumptions and scope
Use the vertex set \(V=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), represented by integers \(0,\ldots,n-1\). Distinct vertices \(i,j\) are adjacent in \(C_n^4\) exactly when \(\min(|i-j|,n-|i-j|)\le4\). An independent triple consists of three vertices at cyclic pairwise distances greater than four. The total three-cut complex \(K_n=\Delta_3^t(C_n^4)\) is the simplicial complex generated by complements of those independent triples, equivalently
\[
\sigma\in K_n\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V\setminus\sigma\text{ contains an independent triple}.
\]
The entire assertion concerns ordinary, nonequivariant, integral homotopy equivalence, with exactly the five indicated values of \(n\). No claim is made about other cycle powers or cut parameters.

## Proof
**1. Finite face predicate.** Encode a vertex subset by an \(n\)-bit unsigned integer. Initialize a Boolean array \(g\) on all \(2^n\) masks by marking precisely the masks of independent triples. For each bit position \(b=0,\ldots,n-1\), propagate truth from each mask omitting \(b\) to its union with \(\{b\}\). After the \(n\) sweeps, \(g(T)=1\) if and only if \(T\) contains an independent triple: each independent-triple mask propagates to all its supersets, and no other mask is ever marked. Set \(f(S)=g(V\setminus S)\). Thus \(f\) is exactly the characteristic function of the full face set of \(K_n\), including its empty face.

**2. Explicit acyclic matching.** On the face poset, take bit coordinates in the fixed order \(0,1,\ldots,n-1\). At coordinate \(b\), scan masks \(S\) omitting \(b\) in increasing numerical order. Whenever both \(S\) and \(S\cup\{b\}\) are faces and neither has previously been matched, match this covering pair. Once all coordinates are processed, unmatch the pair \((\varnothing,\{0\})\); this creates a single critical vertex in the usual nonaugmented Morse convention.

The accompanying complete C++17 verifier implements precisely this predicate and pairing. It then constructs the orientations of **every** Hasse cover \(S\subset S\cup\{b\}\): upward for matched covers and downward for all other covers. Its integer indegree computation and Kahn topological traversal visit every face including the empty face, proving absence of any directed cycle. This exhaustively checks the acyclicity condition of discrete Morse theory, not merely nonexistence of cycles below a length cutoff. The audited finite outputs are:

| \(n\) | Independent triples | Nonempty faces | Matched pairs | Hasse covers | Critical cells by dimension |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 16 | 16 | 48,380 | 24,188 | 354,544 | one \(0\)-cell, three \(6\)-cells |
| 17 | 34 | 112,438 | 56,218 | 908,803 | one \(0\)-cell, one \(9\)-cell |
| 18 | 60 | 240,902 | 120,450 | 2,103,534 | one \(0\)-cell, one \(10\)-cell |
| 19 | 95 | 499,624 | 249,811 | 4,659,180 | one \(0\)-cell, one \(11\)-cell |
| 20 | 140 | 1,020,090 | 510,043 | 10,086,000 | one \(0\)-cell, three \(13\)-cells |

For each row, twice the listed matching-pair count plus the total number of listed critical cells equals the number of nonempty faces. The acyclicity check's topological traversal processes exactly one more vertex than the nonempty-face count, because it also includes the empty face. All integer and Boolean operations range over \(2^n\) possible masks, so this is an exhaustive finite proof for each of the five stated \(n\)-values.

**3. Homotopy conclusion.** Forman's discrete-Morse theorem produces a CW complex with one critical \(0\)-cell and the listed positive-dimensional critical cells, with no cells in other dimensions. Since every positive critical dimension is at least two, every attaching map goes to the single \(0\)-cell. The Morse CW model is therefore exactly the indicated wedge of spheres. The conclusion is integral and homotopical, not an inference from finite-field Betti numbers.

## Verification
The standalone source `tools/verify_morse.cpp` and its saved output `evidence/verify_morse_output.txt` reproduce the entire finite face enumeration, matching and full Hasse-DAG acyclicity test. Compile with `c++ -O2 -std=c++17 -Wall -Wextra tools/verify_morse.cpp -o verify_morse` and run `./verify_morse 16 20`. The program exits unsuccessfully if it detects an invalid matching, cycle or accounting inconsistency. No external data files or libraries are required beyond a C++17 compiler.

An independently written checker `tools/verify_dual.py` constructs the Alexander dual, whose faces are exactly subsets containing **no** independent triple. It enumerates them by admissible extensions, builds mod-two simplicial boundary matrices and calculates ranks by exact binary elimination. The resulting reduced Alexander-dual Betti groups occur in degrees \(7,5,5,5,4\) with respective ranks \(3,1,1,1,3\), as predicted by combinatorial Alexander duality from the five Morse wedge types. Run with `python3 tools/verify_dual.py`. This second check is independent corroboration, not the justification for integral homotopy equivalence.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer, Denker, Jelić Milutinović, Rowlands, Sundaram and Xue introduced total cut complexes as complements of independent sets (arXiv:2209.13503, Definition 2.3). Carnero Bravo, *Total cut complexes and their duals* (arXiv:2602.21427v3), Proposition 44, settles \(n=(r+1)d=15\) for the present parameters; Theorem 43(c) covers \(21\le n\le23\), and Theorem 43(a) covers all \(n\ge24\). Its Question 49 explicitly leaves \((r+1)d+1\le n\le2rd-r\), which specializes to precisely \(16\le n\le20\). No portion of those previously proved parameter ranges is claimed as new. Work on total two-cut complexes (e.g., arXiv:2512.04486) does not imply these three-cut cases.

## Limitations
These five computations, although exhaustive for each stated order, do not prove a general theorem for unbounded \(r\) or \(d\). The result makes no claim of shellability, equivariant homotopy, canonical sphere generators, or a closed-form Morse matching for other parameters. A complete independent audit and a fully exhaustive search of unindexed literature have not been performed. All new homotopy assertions rely on the deterministic finite computation specified above and not on floating-point or probabilistic tests.
