# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem is reconstructible from the eight multiplicity-five Kunz inequalities and the Apéry maximal-element criterion. The proof enumerates all possible consecutive maximal residue pairs, eliminates the middle pair, derives both necessary parameter cones, verifies sufficiency, and obtains the exact counting intervals. The strictly interior families are tested independently by a membership-based program, while the proof covers all parameter sizes. Main risk: standard Apéry and Kunz identities are used without re-proving their general theory; their special multiplicity-five consequences are derived explicitly.

## Originality — PASS
The 2026 source [1] proves the consecutive pseudo-Frobenius characterization and enumerates each Frobenius stratum algorithmically, but does not present the two uniform Kunz cones or their genus/Frobenius formulas. The 2020 complete embedding-dimension-three theorem [2] subsumes the boundary cases, which are fully credited. It cannot cover the infinite embedding-dimension-four interiors; the latter dominate the new quadratic count. The 2023/2024 fixed-Frobenius covariety paper [3] and 2019 fixed-multiplicity algorithm abstract [4] provide constructive finite enumeration methods but no inspected exact two-cone count. Relevant mathematical search records identified Wilf bounds at multiplicity five rather than a consecutive-gap classification. Residual risk: an equivalent unindexed theorem or a closed classification in the inaccessible full 2019 text.

## Value — PASS
The work resolves a natural low-multiplicity classification question left at the algorithmic level by the 2026 study, beyond the already classified embedding-dimension-three case. It gives a complete exact fixed-genus and fixed-Frobenius census, identifies precisely when embedding dimension jumps from three to four, and proves a quadratic counting law with generic embedding dimension four. The invariants are native to numerical-semigroup theory, not an arbitrary sliced computation.
