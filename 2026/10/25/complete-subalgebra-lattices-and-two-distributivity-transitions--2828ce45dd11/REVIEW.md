# Scientific review: complete subalgebra lattices

## Correctness — PASS
The direct multiplication table in the \(u,v,w\) basis is verified by expansion. In dimension one, square-zero lines split as \(Ku\) and \(K(w-u/2)\) in characteristic different from two, while nonzero-square lines in the characteristic square \(E^2\) satisfy a forced coefficient relation, yielding the unique additional line only off \(\lambda=2\). In dimension two, closure of a plane containing \(Ku\) forces one of the two named planes; for a plane missing that line, its intersection with \(E^2\) forces the extra line and closure of the second generator forces \(\lambda=4\). The complete list implies the lattice criteria via explicit lattice isomorphisms or a witnessed distributivity failure. All zero/nonzero, characteristic, and coefficient-division boundaries are stated. Exact finite-field enumeration across all 22 nonzero parameters in four odd prime fields independently checks the classification but does not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks
Hu and Wen's full 2026 preprint provides the family and idempotent/non-solvability results, not a subalgebra-lattice census. The published 2026 mathematical result *A single symmetry jump in the E-lambda evolution-algebra family* gives an automorphism-group classification, not the lattice or its distributivity criterion. The full available article of Ladra, Páez-Guillán, and Pérez-Rodríguez develops lattice distributivity for solvable evolution algebras; its hypotheses exclude every algebra under consideration. The full nonregularity \(\dim E^2=2<3\) similarly excludes the regular-algebra inheritance theory. Specific full-text passages and indexed scientific-record comparisons have been examined. Residual risk: an earlier general three-dimensional isomorphism classification or implicit subalgebra calculation could encode this case; accessible material did not settle every such possibility.

## Value — PASS
This is a complete classification of the natural subalgebra lattice, rather than an isolated easy subspace or a slight numeric improvement. It crosses a meaningful structural boundary: previously studied solvable evolutions can have distributive lattices, but these non-solvable examples do as well, at exactly two sharply identified parameters, and one is Boolean. Both the exhaustive lattice and distributivity boundary are potentially useful for understanding non-solvable small evolution algebras.

## Limitations and independent status
