# Complete three-grain avalanche homotopy types on directed cycles

## Finding
For the consistently oriented cycle on \(n\ge3\) vertices, every binary configuration with exactly three occupied vertices has an explicitly determined **integral homotopy type**. Write the occupied vertices in circular order as \(u_0<u_1<u_2\), and define their positive cyclic gaps and reduced period by
\[
a=u_1-u_0,\qquad b=u_2-u_1,\qquad c=n-u_2+u_0,\qquad
g=\gcd(a,b,c),\qquad N=n/g.
\]
Let \(\mathcal A_n(a,b,c)\) be its avalanche complex, generated by the vertex sets firing together in a parallel sandpile round. Then \(\mathcal A_n(a,b,c)\) is a disjoint union of \(g\) identical components. The integral homotopy type of **each component** is completely specified as follows:

1. If \(a=b=c\), a filled triangle (a contractible component).
2. If the multiset of gaps equals \(\{q,q,2q\}\) for an integer \(q\ge1\), the boundary of a tetrahedron, \(S^2\).
3. If exactly two gaps agree and the preceding case does not apply, \(S^1\).
4. If the gaps are pairwise distinct and one of them equals \(n/2\), a wedge \(\bigvee^{N/2+1}S^1\).
5. If the gaps are pairwise distinct and none equals \(n/2\), a wedge \(\bigvee^{N+1}S^1\).

The five alternatives are disjoint and exhaustive. Every positive-dimensional reduced integral homology group is free, and the classification is about **homotopy**, not only Betti numbers. In particular, for \((n;u_0,u_1,u_2)=(9;0,2,5)\) the complex is \(\bigvee^{10}S^1\); for \((10;0,2,5)\) it is \(\bigvee^6S^1\); for \((12;0,3,6)\) it is three disjoint copies of \(S^2\).

## Assumptions and scope
The directed graph has vertex set \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) and edges \(j\to j+1\). Its firing threshold is its outdegree, one: in every synchronous round, each vertex containing at least one grain sends one grain to its successor. Start from a **binary** configuration with one grain on each of three distinct vertices and zero elsewhere. At every round the configuration remains binary. A face of the avalanche complex is a subset of some simultaneously firing vertex set; all such sets over the eventual periodic orbit are included. No assertion is made for nonbinary initial states, weighted cycles, undirected firing or more than three occupied vertices.

The complex is a genuine (unoriented) abstract simplicial complex. The displayed number \(g\) counts connected components, not wedge summands. For \(n=3\), the avalanche complex is a single filled triangle; for the tetrahedral case the full two-skeleton on four vertices is a boundary sphere, not a filled tetrahedron. When \(g>1\), components must not be identified at a common basepoint.

## Proof
**1. Dynamical reduction to a triangle-translation complex.** If \(c_j^{(t)}\in\{0,1\}\) is the grain count, all occupied vertices fire and
\[
c_j^{(t+1)}=c_{j-1}^{(t)}.
\]
Thus the simultaneous firing sets are exactly the translates of \(T=\{0,a,a+b\}\) modulo \(n\); translating the initial triple to start at zero is harmless. Their downward closure is the avalanche complex. The support \(T\) has a nontrivial translation stabilizer precisely when \(a=b=c\): an order-three translation must cyclically permute the three vertices. Therefore there are \(n/3\) distinct facets in that case and \(n\) distinct facets otherwise.

**2. The common divisor gives the components.** Every edge of a translated facet joins two vertices at one of the three unsigned cyclic steps \(a,b,c\); changing orientation replaces a step by its negative. Thus the one-skeleton is the undirected Cayley graph on \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) generated by these steps, and its components are the cosets of the subgroup they generate. Since \(a+b+c=n\), this subgroup has index
\[
g=\gcd(n,a,b)=\gcd(a,b,c).
\]
Translation by \(1\) permutes these cosets transitively, so the \(g\) connected components are simplicially isomorphic.

**3. Counting edge translation-orbits.** For a positive gap \(d\), the undirected edges of cyclic step \(d\) form an orbit of size \(n\), except when \(2d=n\), in which case they form an antipodal orbit of size \(n/2\). Distinct positive gap *values* give disjoint edge orbits: otherwise one value would be the complement of the other modulo \(n\), forcing their sum to equal \(n\), contrary to the third positive gap. If a gap value occurs just once and is not antipodal, every edge in its \(n\)-element orbit belongs to exactly one translated triangle. Indeed the \(n\) translated triangles each contain precisely one such edge; the corresponding edge orbit also contains \(n\) edges. All these are free edges, so we can collapse every two-simplex along its uniquely associated free edge. The collapsed edges are pairwise distinct and every elementary collapse remains legitimate.

**4. The two exceptional families.** If \(a=b=c=q\), then \(n=3q\). Each component is exactly one filled triangle on the three vertices in a congruence class modulo \(q\), hence contractible. For all other triples, a singleton non-antipodal gap exists except when the gap multiset equals \(\{q,q,2q\}\). In that latter case \(n=4q\) and \(g=q\). Each congruence class modulo \(q\) has four vertices, and the four translated facets contained in that class are exactly its four three-vertex subsets. It is the boundary of a tetrahedron, hence \(S^2\). These two alternatives cover all cases in which the above full collapse is not performed.

**5. The graph after the collapse.** In the remaining cases, collapse along the complete edge orbit of a gap occurring only once and different from \(n/2\). The vertices remain, and the residual graph is a union of the other edge orbits. It still has \(g\) connected components: any two of the three gap steps generate the same subgroup as all three, since their sum determines the third modulo \(n\). Hence its first Betti number is \(E-n+g\), where \(E\) denotes the total number of remaining edges. A connected graph is a wedge of as many circles as its cycle rank, and translation makes the cycle rank equal across components.

If two gaps agree, their common non-antipodal edge orbit contains \(n\) edges. Thus \(E=n\), giving total cycle rank \(g\): one circle per component. If all three gaps are distinct and none is antipodal, two distinct non-antipodal edge orbits survive; \(E=2n\), giving total cycle rank \(n+g=g(N+1)\): \(N+1\) circles per component. If all three are distinct and one equals \(n/2\), the surviving antipodal orbit has \(n/2\) edges and the other surviving orbit has \(n\); hence \(E=3n/2\), giving total cycle rank \(n/2+g=g(N/2+1)\): \(N/2+1\) circles per component. Because \(n/2\) is a multiple of \(g\), \(N\) is even in this last case.

Every step is a simplicial isomorphism or a sequence of elementary simplicial collapses. Therefore the conclusions are integral homotopy equivalences, and no field-dependent approximation is used in the proof. ∎

## Verification
The accompanying standard-library program `verify.py` independently simulates each parallel firing orbit and reconstructs all translated triangle facets. It enumerates every three-element support for every \(3\le n\le30\), exhaustively performs the free-edge/two-simplex collapses, checks component counts and graph cycle ranks, and verifies the two exceptional triangle families. This gives **31,465** tested initial triples. It separately calculates exact simplicial boundary ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\) for all supports with \(3\le n\le15\). Run `python3 verify.py` and compare the output with `verification_output.txt`. The finite computations corroborate the classification; the proof above establishes the infinite parameter range and the integral homotopy statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Riihimäki and Smith (arXiv:2606.26786v1, June 25, 2026) introduce avalanche homology and prove, in particular, their Proposition 3.23 for binary three-grain configurations having at least one adjacent occupied pair; their Corollary 3.22 handles complements of single vertices, including the small four-cycle case. Those special cases are **prior work, not claimed as new**. Their Question 1 asks for a classification by starting configuration. The present theorem completes the entire **binary three-grain stratum**, including every pairwise separated support and all component-count and exceptional-sphere transitions.

Adamaszek, Adams, Frick, Peterson and Previte-Johnson (arXiv:1410.4336v1) classify nerves generated by **consecutive circular intervals**. These do not cover arbitrary triples of occupied vertices: for example the separated triple \(\{0,2,5\}\subset\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z\) has a wedge of ten circles, which is not among the possible nontrivial connected homotopy types in their interval-nerve classification. The published related study *Sharp arithmetic transitions in complementary residue avalanche complexes* treats specific modular residue-class supports and their complements. It does not classify arbitrary three-gap configurations or imply the five-case statement here.

## Limitations
This completely classifies only the **binary three-occupied-vertex** subset of the full configuration space; it does not settle the motivating paper's wider Question 1. The homotopy equivalences do not supply canonical equivariant retractions for cyclic rotation. Numerical checks stop at \(n=30\) and mod-two chain-rank computations stop at \(n=15\); neither finite bound is used in the infinite proof. Although searches compared the closest full texts and mathematical records, an unindexed subsequent classification of the same three-grain stratum cannot be excluded without further independent literature review.
