# Scientific review: three-grain directed-cycle avalanche homotopy

## Correctness
**PASS.** Parallel firing of a binary support translates it by one vertex. The facet orbit has \(n\) triangles except for the equally spaced case, where it has \(n/3\). The three positive gap values identify all edge-orbits; a singleton gap different from \(n/2\) supplies a disjoint orbit of genuine free edges. Elementary collapse removes every triangle for all but the explicitly treated equilateral and \(q,q,2q\) cases. The retained graph's connected components are the cosets of the subgroup of \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) generated by two gaps, and its edge count determines the exact integral wedge-of-circles homotopy type. The exceptions are disjoint filled triangles and disjoint tetrahedral boundary spheres. Direct firing simulation and elementary collapse agreed for all 31,465 three-subsets with \(n\le30\); exact mod-two boundary ranks agreed for all \(n\le15\). Finite evidence independently supports, but does not replace, the parameter-uniform collapse proof.

## Originality
**PASS, subject to residual bibliographic uncertainty.** Riihimäki–Smith (2026, arXiv:2606.26786v1) classify the adjacent-one triple subfamily in Proposition 3.23 and formulate broader Question 1; those already known subcases are explicitly included only for completeness. Their full text does not give the full three-gap classification, disconnected cases or general separated-grain formulas. The complete circular-arc nerve classification by Adamaszek et al. (2014, arXiv:1410.4336v1) addresses consecutive-interval facet families, not arbitrary cyclic three-subset orbits; the ten-circle example proves it cannot imply the generic-case claim through an unmodified nerve identification. The closest indexed published modular-residue avalanche study treats a narrower arithmetic family; its full mathematical statement was compared with the present general three-gap theorem. Record searches did not identify a stronger equivalent claim. Independent bibliographic review remains unperformed.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The classification supplies the full previously unresolved binary three-grain stratum of a stated configuration-space homotopy question. It introduces a sharp elementary structural division between graph collapses and retained two-dimensional spheres, with exact component counts, a distinct antipodal transition, and arbitrarily large integral first-Betti rank for nonadjacent supports. The result is infinite-family and not a tabulation of small numerical instances.

## Scientific limitations
Only binary supports of size three are covered, not arbitrary grain distributions. Numerical checks are finite and mod-two checks do not alone determine integral homotopy. Potential undiscovered literature overlap remains possible. The review concerns the stated theorem, not a general solution to the original full configuration-space problem.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
