For every unordered triple of distinct binary length-\(n\) centers, the common exact-two-substitution sphere is either empty or has size \(n-2\), \(4\), \(3\), or \(1\), with an explicit binomial formula counting all occurrences for every \(n\ge3\). Consequently, \(1+\max\{4,n-2\}\) distinct exact-two-error reads are sharply sufficient for at most two candidate messages when \(n\ge4\), while the threshold is two when \(n=3\).
