# Composability graphs determine radical commutator-rank spectra in truncated path algebras

## Finding

Let \(Q\) be an arbitrary finite directed quiver, with loops and parallel arrows allowed, let \(K=\mathbb F_q\) be a finite field, and let \(A=KQ/R^3\) where \(R\) is the ideal generated by all arrows. Let \(J=R/R^3\) be its Jacobson radical. Write \(m=|Q_1|\) for its number of individual arrows and \(p=|Q_2|\) for its number of length-two paths. Construct the simple undirected **composability graph** \(H\) on the individual arrows: distinct arrows are adjacent when they compose in at least one order. A loop arrow is a vertex; self-composition does not create a graph loop.

For \(S\subseteq V(H)\), let \(c(S)\) be the number of connected components of the induced graph \(H[S]\), with \(c(\varnothing)=0\), and let \(N[S]\) be its closed neighborhood, with \(N[\varnothing]=\varnothing\). If \(a\in J\) has support \(S\) in the degree-one arrow coordinates, then
\[
\operatorname{rank}_K(\operatorname{ad}_a:J\to J)=|N[S]|-c(S).
\]
Consequently the complete **element-centralizer codimension polynomial** is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal R_{Q,q}(t)=\sum_{a\in J}t^{\dim J-\dim C_J(a)}=q^p\sum_{S\subseteq V(H)}(q-1)^{|S|}t^{|N[S]|-c(S)}.}
\]
The exact number of ordered commuting pairs in \(J\) and their probability are, respectively,
\[
\boxed{q^{2p}\sum_{S\subseteq V(H)}(q-1)^{|S|}(q+1)^{c(S)},\qquad
\operatorname{cp}(J)=q^{-2m}\sum_{S\subseteq V(H)}(q-1)^{|S|}(q+1)^{c(S)}.}
\]
These formulas hold for all finite fields, all finite quivers (including cycles), and all \(m,p\ge0\).

If \(Q\) is a coherently oriented linear chain with \(m\) arrows, its composability graph is the path \(P_m\). The commuting numerator \(F_m=\sum_S(q-1)^{|S|}(q+1)^{c(S)}\) satisfies
\[
F_0=1,\quad F_1=q^2,\quad F_{m+2}=qF_{m+1}+q(q-1)F_m\quad(m\ge0).
\]
For the coherently oriented cyclic quiver with \(m\ge3\) arrows, the graph is \(C_m\), and
\[
\boxed{F_m^{\mathrm{cyc}}=\lambda_+^m+\lambda_-^m+q(q-1)^m,\qquad
\lambda_\pm=\frac{q\pm\sqrt{5q^2-4q}}2.}
\]
For fixed finite field, in both families the commuting probability decays exponentially. In the linear case it is asymptotic to \(A_+(\lambda_+/q^2)^m\), where \(A_+=(q^2-\lambda_-)/(\lambda_+-\lambda_-)\); in the cyclic case it is asymptotic to \((\lambda_+/q^2)^m\). In both cases \(0<\lambda_+/q^2<1\).

## Assumptions and scope

The path algebra uses one basis element for each oriented path. The quotient truncates at path length three; hence \(J\) has the arrow basis and the length-two path basis, and \(J^3=0\). The **centralizer** \(C_J(a)=\{b\in J:ab=ba\}\) and the rank of \(b\mapsto ab-ba\) are taken in the (generally nonunital) radical algebra \(J\), not in the full unital algebra \(A\). This distinction is essential. The graph \(H\) is an undirected simple graph even when distinct arrows compose in both orders, and it may have isolated vertices. Zero-dimensional boundaries \(m=0\) are included. The full polynomial counts the degree-two choices with multiplicity \(q^p\); the pair probability divides by \(|J|^2=q^{2(m+p)}\).

## Proof

Write each element of \(J\) uniquely as \(a=\sum_e\alpha_e e+\sum_\pi u_\pi\pi\) with arrows \(e\) and length-two paths \(\pi\). Similarly write \(b=\sum_e\beta_e e+\sum_\pi v_\pi\pi\). Products involving a length-two path and a positive-length path vanish modulo \(R^3\). For each *ordered* composable pair of arrows \(e,f\), the coefficient of the basis path \(ef\) in \(ab-ba\) is precisely
\[
\alpha_e\beta_f-\beta_e\alpha_f.
\]
The path coordinates are independent, including when the opposite path \(fe\) also exists. For \(e=f\) the coefficient vanishes tautologically. Thus \(ab=ba\) if and only if \(\alpha_e\beta_f-\beta_e\alpha_f=0\) on every edge \(\{e,f\}\) of \(H\).

Fix \(a\) and put \(S=\{e:\alpha_e\ne0\}\). On any edge with \(e\in S\) and \(f\notin S\), the equation forces \(\beta_f=0\). For adjacent \(e,f\in S\), it forces \(\beta_e/\alpha_e=\beta_f/\alpha_f\). Hence the arrow coordinates of \(b\) are parametrized by one free scalar for each connected component of \(H[S]\), plus one free scalar on each vertex outside \(N[S]\); there are exactly \(c(S)+m-|N[S]|\) such scalars. All \(p\) length-two coefficients in \(b\) are unrestricted, so
\[
\dim_K C_J(a)=p+c(S)+m-|N[S]|,
\]
proving the rank identity. A fixed arrow support \(S\) has \((q-1)^{|S|}\) possible nonzero degree-one coefficients, and \(q^p\) unrestricted higher coefficients. Summing gives \(\mathcal R_{Q,q}\).

To count *ordered commuting pairs*, for each arrow \(e\) form the vector \(w_e=(\alpha_e,\beta_e)\in K^2\). The commuting equations say that vectors at adjacent vertices are linearly dependent. Let \(S\) be the set of vertices with \(w_e\ne0\). Each component of \(H[S]\) has one common one-dimensional subspace of \(K^2\), chosen among \(q+1\) projective lines, and each vertex on it independently chooses one of \(q-1\) nonzero vectors. This gives exactly \((q-1)^{|S|}(q+1)^{c(S)}\) choices. The degree-two coefficients of both \(a,b\) contribute independently \(q^{2p}\), proving the commuting count and the probability.

For paths, the component-count sum is a two-state transfer process: a zero vertex has weight one, a nonzero vertex has one of \(q+1\) colors each of weight \(q-1\), with successive nonzero vertices required to have the same color. A path of \(m\) vertices has partition function \(F_m\). Track whether its last vertex is zero or nonzero and eliminate the states; direct recurrence or the characteristic polynomial of the transfer matrix gives \(F_{m+2}=qF_{m+1}+q(q-1)F_m\), with \(F_0=1\) and \(F_1=1+(q+1)(q-1)=q^2\).

For cycles, use a transfer matrix indexed by zero and the \(q+1\) projective colors, giving a transition weight of \(1\) on the destination zero, \(q-1\) on a destination nonzero color if the source is zero or the same color, and zero otherwise. A cyclic coloring contributes exactly to the trace of its \(m\)-th power. The color-difference subspace has dimension \(q\) and eigenvalue \(q-1\). The remaining two-dimensional quotient has characteristic polynomial \(x^2-qx-q(q-1)\), so its eigenvalues are \(\lambda_\pm\). Taking the trace gives the cyclic formula. The displayed exponential asymptotics follow because \(\lambda_+>q-1\), \(|\lambda_-|<\lambda_+\), and \(\lambda_+<q^2\) for \(q\ge2\). For paths solve the second-order recurrence for its positive leading coefficient \(A_+\).

## Verification

The accompanying `checker.py` constructs every length-two path of each test quiver and independently builds the full coefficient matrix of \(\operatorname{ad}_a\) on arrow coordinates. It computes ranks by finite-field Gaussian elimination rather than using the graph proof. Exhaustive checks over prime fields of orders 2, 3, and 5 covered 55 quiver-field cases and 3,889 arrow coefficient assignments, counting degree-two choices separately. A second exhaustive method checked 30,946 ordered two-vector assignments; it confirmed both the graph component count and the rank-derived commuting count. Linear recurrence cases through seven arrows and cyclic cases through eight arrows were checked at each field order. Its recorded output is in `checker_output.txt`. Finite checks are corroboration, not a proof for all quivers or fields.

## Relationship to prior work

Msapato studies \(\tau\)-exceptional sequences in Nakayama algebras and supplies a standard truncated-path-algebra framework, not the distribution of elementwise radical commutator ranks. Xie–Gao–Huang study tilting modules over Auslander algebras of radical-cube-zero Nakayama algebras, not the finite-field commuting spectrum. Tittmann–Averbouch–Makowsky introduced and studied the **vertex-induced subgraph component polynomial** \(Q(H;x,y)=\sum_{S\subseteq V(H)}x^{|S|}y^{c(S)}\). Thus the abstract graph polynomial and its path/cycle recurrences already exist and **are not claimed as new**. The mathematical contribution here is the exact translation from radical commutation to an arrow-composability graph for all finite quivers, the complete *closed-neighborhood* centralizer-rank spectrum, and the resulting concrete finite-algebra specializations. Existing Coxeter-matrix-periodicity work on \(R^3\)-truncated linear Nakayama algebras concerns a different invariant and implies none of these elementwise formulas.

## Limitations

This theorem treats the radical \(J\), not commuting pairs of arbitrary elements of the ambient algebra \(A\), and it truncates paths at length three. Its claimed priority is specifically the algebraic spectrum/graph correspondence; the graph polynomial, transfer-matrix method, and elementary eigenvalue recurrence are existing mathematics. Exhaustive checks use prime fields, although the proof covers every prime-power field. Equivalent algebraic formulations in broader work on bilinear commutator maps or graph polynomials are possible and warrant independent literature review; no claim of uniqueness or independent validation is made.
