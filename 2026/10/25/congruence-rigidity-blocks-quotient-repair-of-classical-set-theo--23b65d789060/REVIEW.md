# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. If two functions differ, the coordinatewise singleton of the first belongs to that singleton-valued function in the direct-power relation, while the second does not. Any relation-compatible equivalence must therefore identify no distinct functions. With two or more coordinates, two distinct functions with an empty value at one coordinate have no members, so extensionality fails and the identity quotient cannot repair it.

## Originality

PASS, within the stated scope. The closest primary preprint supplies a witness to failure of extensionality for the same direct-power relation, but does not state congruence rigidity or the resulting no-quotient-repair theorem. The coordinatewise singleton argument is uniform over every nonempty index set. Older priority is not fully ruled out because a cited direct-product thesis was not available in full text.

## Value

PASS. The result answers a concrete semantic repair question raised by the primary preprint’s discussion of intensional identity: no nontrivial quotient of the classical direct-power membership structure can preserve membership, so that particular repair route cannot resolve the extensionality failure. Alternative Boolean-valued semantics remain outside the claim.

## Closest literature and limitations

The principal source is *Models of Arbitrary Sets*, arXiv:2610.23219v1, Section 2. It defines the coordinatewise membership relation and gives one extensionality counterexample. The theorem here proves congruence rigidity and its quotient consequence. Weinstein’s 1965 thesis on direct-product preservation is cited as broader background; its full text was not accessed, so the residual priority risk remains.
