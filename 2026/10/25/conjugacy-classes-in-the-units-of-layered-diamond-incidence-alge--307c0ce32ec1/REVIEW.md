# Review

## Correctness

Incidence-algebra convolution gives all three necessary and sufficient commutation equations. The outer-diagonal-pair stratification is exhaustive. For unequal pairs the last equation has rank one and gives exactly \(q\) solutions; for equal pairs the determinant sum becomes one nondegenerate bilinear orthogonality equation and has the exact count obtained by splitting the first vector into zero and nonzero. Binomial summation and division by the unit-group order give the stated identity without field-characteristic assumptions. An independent executable checks small direct group-pair enumerations and larger diagonal-stratum sums; the general proof does not rely on those finite tests.

## Originality

The closest primary papers by Can (2021) and Can–Casimiro–Malheiro (2022) analyze the incidence monoid and conjugacy in bipartite antichain monoids. The latter's abelian-unipotent hypothesis excludes this height-three diamond's nonabelian radical. The known graphic-Tutte class-number formula covers the radical-square-zero height-two comparator and implies exactly the first two displayed terms, but cannot directly imply the extra composition-dependent term. The \(b=1\) case is classical and is a boundary check rather than a novelty claim. An older equivalent formula from broader pattern-group class-number methods cannot be completely ruled out.

## Value

The count is a natural infinite-family invariant, simultaneously the number of complex irreducible characters, for unit groups at the first non-square-zero incidence-algebra layer. A uniform correction of exactly \((q-1)^{b+1}\) classes makes the failure of the abelian-radical approximation quantitatively explicit, for all field characteristics and arbitrary width.
