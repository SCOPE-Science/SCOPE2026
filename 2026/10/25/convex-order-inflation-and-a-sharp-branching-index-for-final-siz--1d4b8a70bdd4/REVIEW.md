# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The root-to-vertex uniqueness of paths gives exact first moments, while joint root paths show that only incomparable vertex pairs retain a shared infectiousness second moment. The displayed lowest-common-ancestor exponent is the number of distinct other source vertices in the pair's path union. Conditioning away from one parent reduces every convex final-size functional to a polynomial in that parent's common Bernoulli success parameter; the nonnegative mixed discrete differences prove the convex-order comparison. The exact extremal index bounds follow from the number of incomparable pairs. The independently supplied rational-arithmetic script checks 1,441 rooted labeled trees and the four-star witness; its finite enumeration is not substituted for the universal proof. The model assumes independent periods and independent contacts conditional on each period.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

The nearest literature is Kenah–Robins' general epidemic percolation framework, which explicitly diagnoses dependent sibling transmissions, and Wilkinson–Sharkey's probability-of-reaching-subsets comparisons. Hall–Siebert supplies rooted-tree SIR/SEIR state-marginal equations. The separate finite-tree final-size convex-order theorem, the exact lowest-common-ancestor variance excess, and the sharp rooted-tree topology extremizers were not identified in the inspected material or mathematical-record searches. General epidemic-percolation and branching-process formulas may subsume this combination after nontrivial specialization; independent priority confirmation remains necessary.

## Value — PASS

A mean-matched independent-bond epidemic forecast gives the exact average number infected on every rooted tree, yet understates total outbreak-size variance whenever the root-directed tree branches and infectiousness varies. The branching index quantifies the discrepancy exactly and has complete path/star extremizers. A four-person star already shows a 50% variance inflation with identical mean final size and finite positive infection durations. This directly motivates uncertainty calibration for tree-based infection tracing and epidemic simulations.

## Scope and limitations

The result covers finite undirected trees with one imported infection, independently sampled periods, constant contact rate, and comparisons at fixed **mean transmissibility** rather than fixed mean infectious duration. The monotonicity is in convex order, not pointwise tail order. A general directed or cyclic graph, correlated infectiousness across vertices, and multiple initial infections are outside scope. Published general stochastic-order or moment identities could reduce novelty; independent review has not been performed.
