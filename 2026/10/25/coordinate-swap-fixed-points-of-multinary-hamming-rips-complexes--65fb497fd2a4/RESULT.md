# Coordinate-swap fixed points of multinary Hamming Rips complexes are wedges of two-spheres

## Finding

For every integer \(q\ge3\), write \([q]=\{0,1,\ldots,q-1\}\), give \([q]^3\) the Hamming metric, and let \(X_q=\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}([q]^3,d_H)\). The involution \(\tau(a,b,c)=(b,a,c)\) acts simplicially. Its **geometric fixed-point space** has the integral homotopy type

\[
|X_q|^{\langle\tau\rangle}\simeq\bigvee^{\frac{(q-1)^2(q^2-2)}2}S^2.
\]

In particular the first three multinary sphere counts, for \(q=3,4,5\), are \(14,63,184\). The conclusion concerns actual points fixed in the realization, not the simplicial subcomplex on pointwise fixed vertices and not an orbit quotient. The finite-dimensional topological classification holds for arbitrary finite alphabet sets of the indicated size; no field structure is needed.

## Assumptions and scope

The Rips convention is inclusive: a nonempty finite vertex set spans a simplex if and only if every pair of words has Hamming distance at most two. A fixed point of the realization is a point fixed by the geometric action of the transposition, and may lie in a simplex whose vertices are exchanged. All homotopy and homology assertions are integral. Unless otherwise noted \(q\ge3\). We use \(\binom q2=q(q-1)/2\) and ordinary (unreduced) fundamental groups of connected complexes.

## Proof

**1. Orbit-simplex realization.** If a finite group acts by simplicial automorphisms on a finite complex, any fixed point has equal barycentric coefficients on vertices belonging to the same orbit; conversely every orbit of positive coefficients must have its entire union in one simplex. Taking normalized orbit barycenters gives a canonical piecewise-linear homeomorphism between the geometric fixed-point set and a simplicial complex whose vertices are eligible vertex orbits, with a face for each collection whose combined original vertices form a simplex. This can also be checked simplex by simplex by intersecting the fixed affine subspace with the simplex, so there is no appeal to an identity between fixed points and the original fixed-vertex subcomplex.

The eligible orbits for \(\tau\) are \(A_{a,c}=\{(a,a,c)\}\), with \(a,c\in[q]\), and \(B_{\{a,b\},c}=\{(a,b,c),(b,a,c)\}\), with \(a\ne b\). Each \(B\)-orbit is eligible because its two vertices have Hamming distance two. Write \(L_q\) for the corresponding orbit complex. Its one-skeleton is determined exactly by the following compatibility conditions (all representatives must be pairwise within distance two):

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_{a,c}\sim A_{b,d}&\iff a=b\ \text{or}\ c=d;\\
B_{\{a,b\},c}\sim B_{\{e,f\},d}&\iff c=d\quad\text{for distinct }B\text{-vertices};\\
A_{x,d}\sim B_{\{a,b\},c}&\iff d=c\ \text{or}\ x\in\{a,b\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The second equivalence follows because when the third coordinates differ, suitable representatives of any two two-element orbits differ in both first coordinates as well; at the same third-coordinate level the Hamming distance is always at most two. As \(X_q\) is flag, these pairwise rules determine every orbit simplex.

**2. Exact maximal simplices.** There are exactly three types of maximal simplices, with no others:

\[
\begin{aligned}
S_c&=\{A_{a,c}:a\in[q]\}\cup\{B_{\{a,b\},c}:a<b\};\\
T_{a,c}&=\{A_{a,d}:d\in[q]\}\cup\{B_{\{a,b\},c}:b\ne a\};\\
U_{a,b,c,d}&=\{B_{\{a,b\},c},A_{a,d},A_{b,d}\},\quad a<b,\ c\ne d.
\end{aligned}
\]

Indeed, any clique with two \(B\)-vertices forces a common third-coordinate level. If its \(A\)-vertices have differing alphabet labels, the clique is contained in the row simplex \(S_c\); otherwise its \(A\)-vertices are confined to one alphabet column, and compatibility with every \(B\) forces an incident family, giving \(T_{a,c}\). If exactly one \(B\)-vertex is present, \(A\)-vertices outside level \(c\) must have labels in its two-element alphabet set; when both labels occur they must be on the same other level \(d\), giving the triangle \(U_{a,b,c,d}\). Cliques with no \(B\)-vertices lie in a row or column simplex. These cases exhaust all cliques; each displayed simplex is inclusion-maximal by the same compatibility rules. There are \(q\) simplices \(S\), \(q^2\) simplices \(T\), and exactly

\[
N=q(q-1)\binom q2=\frac{q^2(q-1)^2}{2}
\]

triangles \(U\).

**3. Removing the two-point orbit vertices before attaching triangles.** Let \(Y\subset L_q\) be the subcomplex generated by all \(S_c\) and \(T_{a,c}\) simplices, but no extra triangles \(U\). Its full subcomplex on the \(A\)-vertices, denoted \(A\), is the clique complex of the \(q\)-by-\(q\) rook graph: the union of \(q\) row simplices and \(q\) column simplices. We show \(Y\simeq A\) by sequentially deleting all \(B\)-vertices. For any still present vertex \(B_{\{a,b\},c}\), its link is covered by the traces of three simplices \(S_c,T_{a,c},T_{b,c}\). Their intersections, after removing the distinguished vertex, obey

\[
(S_c\cap T_{a,c})\setminus\{B\}\supset\{A_{a,c}\},\quad
(S_c\cap T_{b,c})\setminus\{B\}\supset\{A_{b,c}\},\quad
(T_{a,c}\cap T_{b,c})\setminus\{B\}=\varnothing.
\]

These facts remain valid regardless of which other \(B\)-vertices have already been deleted, since the indicated \(A\)-vertices remain. Every nonempty intersection is a simplex, and the link nerve is an interval with two edges. Hence each such link is contractible. The closed star is a cone on that link, so deleting this vertex induces a homotopy equivalence. After deleting all \(B\)-vertices we obtain \(Y\simeq A\).

The \(q\) row simplices and \(q\) column simplices of \(A\) form a good cover: each row meets each column in one vertex and no two distinct rows or columns meet. Its nerve is precisely \(K_{q,q}\). Thus

\[
Y\simeq A\simeq K_{q,q},\qquad
\pi_1(Y)\cong F_{(q-1)^2},\quad H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{(q-1)^2},\quad H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)=0.
\]

**4. Triangles kill the graph's loops.** Every \(U_{a,b,c,d}\) adds one two-cell to \(Y\), with its entire boundary already in \(Y\): the two \(B\)-to-\(A\) edges are in \(T_{a,c}\) and \(T_{b,c}\), while the remaining \(A\)-edge lies in \(S_d\). No \(U\)-triangle is a face of an \(S\) or \(T\) simplex and no two such triangles have the same interior. Hence the relative cellular pair \((L_q,Y)\) comprises exactly \(N\) two-cells and no other relative cells.

The boundary loop of \(U_{a,b,c,d}\) is homotopic inside \(Y\), using triangles in \(S_c,T_{a,c},T_{b,c}\), to the rook rectangle

\[
A_{a,c}\to A_{b,c}\to A_{b,d}\to A_{a,d}\to A_{a,c}.
\]

These are exactly the four-edge cycles of the nerve \(K_{q,q}\) indexed by two distinct rows and two distinct columns. Fixing one row \(c_0\) and one column \(a_0\), the \((q-1)^2\) rectangles through \((a_0,c_0)\), indexed by \(a\ne a_0\) and \(d\ne c_0\), generate the free fundamental group of the complete bipartite graph (choose its standard two-star spanning tree). Every one is the boundary of one of the listed triangles \(U\). By van Kampen, all generators are killed and \(\pi_1(L_q)=0\).

**5. Integral homology and homotopy type.** The relative cellular long exact sequence becomes

\[
0\longrightarrow H_2(L_q;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^N
\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{(q-1)^2}
\longrightarrow H_1(L_q;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow0.
\]

Simple connectivity gives \(H_1(L_q;\mathbb Z)=0\), so \(H_2(L_q;\mathbb Z)\) is a free abelian subgroup of \(\mathbb Z^N\) of rank

\[
N-(q-1)^2=\frac{(q-1)^2(q^2-2)}2.
\]

There is no higher homology because \(L_q\) is homotopy equivalent to a two-dimensional CW complex obtained by attaching \(N\) two-cells to the graph-type space \(Y\). Hurewicz represents an integral basis of \(H_2(L_q;\mathbb Z)\) by maps of two-spheres; their wedge induces an integral homology equivalence and, because both spaces are simply connected CW complexes, is a homotopy equivalence by the homological Whitehead theorem. Combining with the orbit-simplex homeomorphism proves the claim.

## Verification

The accompanying standard-library-only `verify_orbit_complex.py` reconstructs allowed orbits directly from Hamming distances, uses an independent Bron--Kerbosch clique enumeration, and compares the entire family of maximal simplices with the displayed \(S/T/U\) classification. It passed for \(q=2,3,4,5\), obtaining respectively \(8,30,92,230\) maximal facets. For \(q=2,3,4\), it additionally enumerates every face and computes simplicial boundary ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\), finding reduced homology only in dimension two with ranks \(1,14,63\); for \(q=5\) it checks facets only, and does not calculate homology. Execute `python3 verify_orbit_complex.py`. These are finite cross-checks, not a proof of the all-parameter classification.

## Relationship to prior work

Bendersky, Elia and Grbić study the ordinary binary-hypercube Vietoris--Rips complexes through connectivity, cohomology and toric-topological algebra; the inspected open-access article uses \(\{0,1\}^n\), not multinary geometric fixed-point spaces. Galetto, Montaño and Wellner compute ambient hyperoctahedral representations of ordinary binary-hypercube Rips homology; such ambient character data do not determine geometric fixed loci for the distinct multinary complexes. Existing exact ternary and quaternary three-cube Rips calculations identify the ambient complexes at scale two as wedges of three-spheres, but do not identify their coordinate-swap fixed spaces. A previously established binary-hypercube coordinate-transposition theorem already includes the boundary \(q=2\); that case is verified here only as a consistency check, and is **not** claimed as new. A separate multinary cyclic three-coordinate rotation fixed-point calculation yields a wedge of circles; the order-two coordinate-swap fixed loci studied here are distinct and have a different homotopy dimension.

## Limitations

No claim is made about arbitrary permutations of more than three coordinates, larger Hamming thresholds, fixed-point spaces under alphabet permutations or canonical induced representations on the newly computed second homology. The sphere wedge equivalence is nonequivariant. Equivalent results might exist under unindexed terminology. The finite checker is not an infinite proof. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
