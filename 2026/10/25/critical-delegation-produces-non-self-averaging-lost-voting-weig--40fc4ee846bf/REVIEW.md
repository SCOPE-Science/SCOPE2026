# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is the critical mean and positive limiting variance of represented voting weight, together with the sharp off-critical thresholds. A one-lineage reveal computes the exact finite expected fraction. A second-lineage reveal uses a backward recurrence distinguishing hitting an established successful path, discovering a fresh voter, and self-collision; this yields exact finite joint probabilities and hence the variance. At the square-root scale, both lineages have Gaussian collision-hazard tails, supporting dominated Riemann-sum limits. The resulting covariance is strictly positive because the probability of absorption before a self-collision strictly increases when another successful path is present. A monotone coupling gives off-critical concentration. The standalone checker exhaustively verifies both moment formulas with rational arithmetic on 100136 finite action profiles; it is not evidence of the asymptotic limit on its own.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Christoff and Grossi (arXiv:1612.08048, Section 3.2.1) analyze the probability of a completely guru-free proxy profile; that event does not determine the critical represented fraction. Berinsky et al. (arXiv:2107.11868, Sections 2–4 and Appendix B) study bounded concentration and truth tracking under positive fixed direct-voter probabilities, not a shrinking square-root regime. Colley, Delemazure and Gilbert (arXiv:2301.02462, Section 5) provide a more general finite partition recursion for the same complete-network delegation choices: all finite enumerations it implies are credited as prior coverage. A related critical mean crossover also occurs on a different bipartite delegation network, so the first moment is not treated as original. The distinctive theorem here is the nonzero critical *variance* limit and consequent failure of self-averaging. That conclusion requires comparing two growing ancestral chains; it is not given in the inspected primary statements. Residual risk: older random mapping theory might establish an equivalent variance asymptotic under different terminology.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A represented vote is exactly the natural unit needed to judge election participation under transitive delegations that have unresolved cycles. The positive limit of the variance, not merely its mean, establishes persistent macroscopic uncertainty in a regime where the number of direct voters diverges. At the central scaling example, the limiting standard deviation of participation is about \(0.2647\). This materially changes what mean-weight forecasts say about an individual election. The theorem identifies a sharp structural threshold and is useful independently of truth-tracking or strategic behavior.

## Limits and relationship to prior work

The theorem is specific to the independent uniform complete directed delegation network and the convention that cyclic chains lose their vote. It proves two limiting moments, not a complete limit distribution, nor conclusions about electoral winner accuracy. Existing full finite partition recursions remain acknowledged as strong prior coverage; older random-mapping formulations remain the primary originality risk.
