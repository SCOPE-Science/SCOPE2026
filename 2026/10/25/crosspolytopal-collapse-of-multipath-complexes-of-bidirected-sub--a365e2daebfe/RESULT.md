# Crosspolytopal collapse of multipath complexes of bidirected subdivided forests

## Finding
Let \(F\) be any finite simple forest with \(m\geq1\) edges, including arbitrary branching, disconnected components, and isolated vertices. Subdivide every edge once, using a distinct midpoint, and replace each resulting undirected edge with its two oppositely directed arcs; call the resulting digraph \(D_F\). The multipath complex \(X(D_F)\) has the directed arcs as vertices and admits precisely those arc subsets that form a disjoint union of vertex-simple directed paths and isolated vertices. There is a sequence of elementary simplicial collapses
\[
X(D_F)\searrow\underbrace{S^0*S^0*\cdots*S^0}_{m\ \mathrm{factors}}=\partial\Diamond^m.
\]
Consequently \(X(D_F)\simeq S^{m-1}\) integrally, and its only nonzero reduced homology is \(\widetilde H_{m-1}(X(D_F);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\). The global arrow-reversal involution \(J\) acts on this generator by multiplication by \((-1)^m\).

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple, and loopless. The original forest \(F\) may be disconnected but contains at least one edge. Each edge \(\{u,v\}\) is replaced by \(u-w_{uv}-v\), where the midpoint \(w_{uv}\) is new and distinct for every edge. For each resulting unoriented edge both directed arcs occur. A multipath contains no directed cycle, including no pair of mutually opposite arcs, and every vertex has indegree and outdegree at most one among selected arcs. The empty arc subset is an augmented face; isolated original graph vertices create no arc vertices. The join is a simplicial join on disjoint vertex sets. The boundary \(\partial\Diamond^m\) is the join of \(m\) zero-spheres, with \(m=1\) giving two isolated points. The involution \(J\) exchanges both directions of each arc and acts simplicially on \(X(D_F)\).

## Proof
Choose an original leaf \(u\) of a nontrivial component of \(F\), with neighbor \(v\), and denote the unique inserted midpoint on \(uv\) by \(w\). The four corresponding directed arcs are \(a:u\to w\), \(b:w\to u\), \(c:w\to v\), and \(d:v\to w\).

**First domination.** For every multipath face \(\sigma\) containing \(c\), the union \(\sigma\cup\{a\}\) is also a multipath. Indeed, \(c\) already uses the sole outgoing slot at \(w\), precluding \(b\); it also excludes \(d\) because that would create a directed two-cycle on \(wv\). Since \(u\) is a leaf and \(w\) has degree two, no other chosen arc meets \(u\) or \(w\) in a way incompatible with adding \(a\). The new path segment \(u\to w\to v\) cannot create any directed cycle because \(u\) has no other incident edge. Thus every maximal face containing \(c\) also contains \(a\), so the simplicial vertex \(c\) is dominated by \(a\). Its deletion is a sequence of elementary collapses.

**Second domination.** After deleting \(c\), every remaining face containing \(d\) can freely adjoin \(b\). The arc \(a\) cannot coexist with \(d\) (two incoming arcs at \(w\)); the deleted arc \(c\) is unavailable; and \(u\) is otherwise isolated. Therefore adding \(b\) produces the directed segment \(v\to w\to u\) and no cycle or degree violation. Hence \(d\) is dominated by \(b\), and may also be removed by elementary collapses.

**Join decomposition.** With \(c,d\) deleted, the only arcs meeting \(u\) or \(w\) are \(a,b\); they cannot be chosen together because they constitute a directed two-cycle. Every selection of either one, or neither one, is compatible with an arbitrary multipath on the bidirected once-subdivision of the smaller forest \(F-uv\), where \(u\) is left isolated (or omitted). Thus the residual simplicial complex is *identically*
\[
S^0_{\{a,b\}}*X(D_{F-uv}).
\]
This local argument remains valid if \(F\) has other components, if \(v\) branches, or if \(F-uv\) contains isolated vertices. Repeatedly remove original leaf edges. Because a forest has a leaf in every nontrivial component, induction gives exactly \(m\) two-vertex factors. Their join is the boundary of the \(m\)-dimensional cross-polytope. Each domination deletion is realized by elementary collapses: pair faces containing the deleted vertex but not its dominator with the faces obtained by adjoining that dominator, using descending face dimension. This proves the asserted simple-homotopy collapse and integral reduced homology.

**Arrow reversal.** The simplicial involution \(J\) has no nonempty invariant face: invariance would require the opposite arc to accompany every chosen arc, violating the directed two-cycle prohibition. A fixed geometric simplex under the induced simplicial map would require an invariant set of simplex vertices, so the geometric realization has no fixed point. The (ordinary) Lefschetz number is therefore zero. The realization is \(S^{m-1}\) up to homotopy; for \(m\geq2\), its only ordinary homology is \(H_0\cong\mathbb Z\) and \(H_{m-1}\cong\mathbb Z\). Hence \(0=1+(-1)^{m-1}\operatorname{tr}(J_*|H_{m-1})\), giving \((-1)^m\). For \(m=1\), the two points are exchanged, so the reduced \(H_0\) action is \(-1\), again \((-1)^m\). This proves both assertions.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` (standard library only). It constructs all labeled forests on two through five original vertices and five additional branching forests with six original vertices, forms the digraph with one subdivision vertex per original edge, enumerates its complete multipath face set, and independently checks every local face-extension domination and the exact residual cross-polytope join. The recorded run prints `PASS forests 339 total_original_faces 226133 domination_face_checks 210764 largest_complex_faces 8119 total_graph_edges_across_tests 1049`. The verifier additionally checks Euler parity and the unpaired opposite-arc condition in all cases. The unbounded theorem is proven by the leaf-dominance induction, not by finite enumeration. The reversal sign is proven with the fixed-point Lefschetz argument, not empirically inferred from tests.

## Relationship to prior work
Caputi, Collari, and Smith (2026, Proposition 2.2) compute the multipath homotopy types of bidirected paths; the present family contains such paths but also all once-subdivided trees and forests with arbitrarily many branching vertices. Their polygon theorem addresses nonforest inputs, not this collapse. Caputi, Collari, Di Trani, and Smith (2024) study multipath homotopy by dynamical regions and particular directed-tree orientations. Those constructions do not directly yield a strong collapse for globally bidirected subdivided branching forests. An accessible published classification of *unsubdivided* bidirected double stars gives, in some cases, wedges in two different dimensions; edge subdivision is essential and the prior statement does not imply this uniform sphere theorem. The claims are compared by object, local configurations, and implication, not by title alone.

## Limitations
The result applies only when the input underlying graph is a forest and every edge is subdivided exactly once before bidirection. It does not assert an analogous single-sphere collapse for arbitrary unsubdivided trees, graphs with cycles, partial edge subdivisions, or arbitrary orientations. The collapsing sequence need not preserve the global reversal action at each stage, although its homological trace is determined separately. The finite verifier tests 339 forest instances; the induction supplies the unbounded proof. The literature comparison is not an exhaustive guarantee against equivalent unindexed or inaccessible work. No independent scientific audit has been performed.
