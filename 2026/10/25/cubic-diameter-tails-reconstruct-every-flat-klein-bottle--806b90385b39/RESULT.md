# Cubic diameter tails reconstruct every flat Klein bottle

## Finding

Let \(K_{L,H}\) be the compact flat Klein bottle obtained from \(\mathbb R^2\) by the deck isometries
\[
T(x,y)=(x+L,y),\qquad G(x,y)=(-x,y+H),\qquad L,H>0.
\]
Choose \(P,Q\) independently from normalized Riemannian area on \(K_{L,H}\), and let \(D=d(P,Q)\) be their intrinsic shortest-geodesic distance. Write \(F(r)=\Pr(D\le r)\) and \(\rho=\sup\operatorname{supp}(D)\).

The law of \(D\) has the exact two-end signatures
\[
F(r)=\frac{\pi r^2}{LH}\qquad(0\le r<\tfrac12\min\{L,H\}),
\]
\[
\rho=\frac{\sqrt{L^2+H^2}}2,
\qquad
\Pr(D^2>\rho^2-s)
=\frac{16}{3L^4H^2}s^3+o(s^3)\quad(s\downarrow0).
\]
In particular, the full distance law uniquely determines the isometry class of every flat Klein bottle, without any restriction on aspect ratio. Indeed, put
\[
P_0=\frac{\pi}{\lim_{r\downarrow0}F(r)/r^2},\qquad
K_0=\lim_{s\downarrow0}\frac{\Pr(D^2>\rho^2-s)}{s^3}.
\]
Then \(P_0=LH\), \(K_0=16/(3L^4H^2)\), and the labeled geometric periods (horizontal translation and vertical glide) are reconstructed by
\[
\boxed{L=\frac{4}{P_0\sqrt{3K_0}},\qquad
H=\frac{P_0^2\sqrt{3K_0}}{4}.}
\]
The differing powers also distinguish these surfaces from rectangular flat tori: for the torus with translation periods \(L,H\), the corresponding tail is \(2s^2/(L^2H^2)+o(s^2)\), not cubic.

## Assumptions and scope

Both periods are arbitrary positive real numbers; the underlying metric is the smooth, intrinsically flat quotient metric, without extrinsic embedding. Area is normalized to total mass one. The group generated by \(T,G\) has a fundamental rectangle \([0,L)\times[0,H)\) of area \(LH\); \(G^2\) translates vertically by \(2H\). These are the standard orthogonal translation/glide coordinates for a compact flat Klein bottle. The conclusions are pointwise in \(L,H\), including \(L=H\) and extremely thin or long bottles.

## Proof

**1. Exact reduction to three independent scalar variables.** Lift two sampled points to \((x,y),(x',y')\) in the fundamental rectangle. Even powers of the glide give orbit points \((x'+mL,y'+2nH)\); odd powers give \((-x'+mL,y'+(2n+1)H)\). As \(|y-y'|\le H\), the shortest vertical offsets in these parity classes are \(|y-y'|\) and \(H-|y-y'|\), respectively. Defining the circular gap \(d_L(t)=\min_{m\in\mathbb Z}|t-mL|\), we obtain exactly
\[
D^2=\min\{A^2+V^2,\,B^2+(H-V)^2\},
\quad
A=d_L(x-x'),\;B=d_L(x+x'),\;V=|y-y'|.
\]
The map of two horizontal circle coordinates \((x,x')\mapsto(x-x',x+x')\pmod L\) is a surjective group homomorphism with finite kernel. It sends Haar measure to Haar measure on the product circle. Thus \(A,B\) are independent, each uniform on \([0,L/2]\), and are jointly independent of \(V\). The last variable has density \(w(v)=2(H-v)/H^2\) for \(0<v<H\).

Consequently the entire law even admits a reproducible one-dimensional integral. With
\[
g_r(t)=\min\left\{1,\frac2L\sqrt{(r^2-t^2)_+}\right\},
\quad (z)_+=\max\{z,0\},
\]
its survival function is exactly
\[
\Pr(D>r)=\int_0^H\frac{2(H-v)}{H^2}
[1-g_r(v)][1-g_r(H-v)]\,dv \qquad(r\ge0).
\]

**2. Small distances and diameter.** Every nonidentity deck transformation displaces each point by at least \(\min\{L,H\}\): nonzero horizontal translations have displacement at least \(L\), odd glides have vertical displacement at least \(H\), and nonzero even vertical translations have displacement at least \(2H\). Thus all metric balls of radius less than \(\min\{L,H\}/2\) embed isometrically as Euclidean disks. Their normalized area is \(\pi r^2/(LH)\), proving the first displayed formula. Since \(A,B\le L/2\), the reduction yields
\[
D^2\le \frac{L^2}{4}+\min\{V^2,(H-V)^2\}
\le\frac{L^2+H^2}{4}.
\]
Equality is attained at \(A=B=L/2\) and \(V=H/2\), and neighborhoods of that triple have positive probability. Hence the support endpoint is the asserted \(\rho\).

**3. Cubic terminal law.** Put \(a=L/2\), \(\alpha=a-A\ge0\), \(\beta=a-B\ge0\), \(\delta=V-H/2\), and \(s=\rho^2-r^2\). The event \(D^2>\rho^2-s\) imposes both
\[
-L\alpha+\alpha^2+H\delta+\delta^2>-s,
\qquad
-L\beta+\beta^2-H\delta+\delta^2>-s.
\]
As \(s\downarrow0\), this event concentrates at \((\alpha,\beta,\delta)=(0,0,0)\): from the inequality for the smaller of \(V,H-V\), \(H|\delta|-\delta^2<s\), so \(|\delta|=O(s)\); both inequalities then force \(\alpha,\beta=O(s)\). Set \((u,w,z)=(\alpha/s,\beta/s,\delta/s)\). After division by \(s\), the allowed region converges, with bounded rescaled support, to
\[
-\frac1H<z<\frac1H,\qquad
0<u<\frac{1+Hz}{L},\qquad
0<w<\frac{1-Hz}{L}.
\]
The density of \((A,B,V)\) is \(4w(V)/L^2\), converging at \(V=H/2\) to \(4/(L^2H)\). Dominated convergence in rescaled coordinates gives
\[
\lim_{s\downarrow0}\frac{\Pr(D^2>\rho^2-s)}{s^3}
=\frac4{L^2H}\int_{-1/H}^{1/H}
\frac{(1+Hz)(1-Hz)}{L^2}\,dz
=\frac{16}{3L^4H^2}.
\]
This constant is strictly positive for every permitted period pair.

**4. Reconstruction and a sharp contrast.** Let \(P_0=LH\). The preceding limit equals \(16/(3L^2P_0^2)\), which algebraically yields \(L=4/(P_0\sqrt{3K_0})\) and \(H=P_0/L\), as claimed. In particular, \(K_{2,1}\) and \(K_{1,2}\) have the same area \(2\) and diameter \(\sqrt5/2\), but their terminal coefficients are \(1/3\) and \(4/3\), respectively. This is why area and diameter alone are insufficient but the tail resolves the ambiguity. For the rectangular flat torus the two independent folded coordinate differences are uniform on \([0,L/2]\) and \([0,H/2]\). Near their joint maximum, the deficit condition is a two-dimensional triangle \(L\alpha+H\beta<s\), giving \(2s^2/(L^2H^2)+o(s^2)\).

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. Run `python3 verify.py`. It tests 3,505 exact rational quotient and diameter identities against explicit deck-image minimization, checks small-radius area laws and near-diameter coefficients for four aspect ratios, checks the swapped-period ambiguity exactly, and compares the integral law against 180,000 independently sampled pairs. These are finite diagnostics; the all-period inverse statement follows from the analytic derivation above.

## Relationship to prior work

El Mir and Yassine's preprint specifies the flat Klein-bottle quotient geometry and studies conformal systolic inequalities, not two-point distance reconstruction. Borman and collaborators use the mirror-identification geometry for random-disk continuum percolation, but their outputs are wrapping probabilities rather than the intrinsic two-point law. Mémoli and Needham develop general distance-distribution inverse problems and analyze flat-torus and Riemannian-surface examples; their manifold result invokes orientable hypotheses, and their flat-torus example is not this Klein-bottle family. The present contribution is the exact cubic endpoint coefficient and its global, orientation-sensitive inverse reconstruction. Earlier specialized formulations beyond the examined sources cannot be excluded absolutely.

## Limitations

The theorem assumes a smooth flat Klein metric and exactly area-uniform independent points. It is not a rigidity theorem for curved Klein-bottle metrics or arbitrary compact nonorientable manifolds. It establishes mathematical identifiability, not statistically stable reconstruction from finite noisy samples. It does not assert that every orientable surface has a quadratic tail. The initial-radius and terminal-asymptotic limits are exact theoretical data, and experimental checks alone are not infinite proofs.
