# Self-review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The deck group splits shortest distances into two parity classes, and a finite-kernel torus homomorphism makes the two horizontal folded differences independent uniform variables. This yields an exact one-dimensional integral for the law. The small-ball area identity holds on every flat quotient below half the shortest displacement. The terminal asymptotic follows from a bounded three-variable rescaling and dominated convergence; the limiting region has explicit positive volume \(16/(3L^4H^2)\). Algebra then reconstructs the labeled translation and glide lengths for every \(L,H>0\). The standalone exact-rational and numerical checks support, but do not replace, the proof.

## Originality

**PASS (best-knowledge assessment).** The most relevant full primary texts examined address Klein-bottle conformal systoles (El Mir--Yassine), Klein-bottle continuum percolation (Borman et al.), and the broader metric-measure distance-distribution problem including flat tori (Mémoli--Needham). The precise global flat-Klein reconstruction from local and cubic terminal distance coefficients is not supplied by those results. A flat-torus argument cannot imply this statement: its orientability and quotient parity are different, and in the rectangular case its upper-tail exponent is two. Residual bibliographic risk is the possibility of an unlocated older explicit Klein-bottle pair-distance formula or equivalent inverse theorem.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The theorem settles a natural global inverse problem for the full two-parameter family of compact flat Klein metrics. The cubic endpoint exponent records the constraint of the glide reflection, while its coefficient distinguishes two metric shapes with identical area and diameter, e.g. the \((L,H)=(2,1)\) and \((1,2)\) pair. This is an intrinsic geometric reconstruction mechanism, not a routine renaming of the torus or cylinder formula.

## Scope and literature limits

All periods are positive, no genericity or perturbative hypothesis is used, and the theorem concerns the intrinsic geodesic metric with uniform area. The conclusion does not extend here to arbitrary nonflat Riemannian Klein bottles or offer finite-sample stability. Bibliographic searches do not mathematically prove absolute priority; more historical specialist literature could exist.
