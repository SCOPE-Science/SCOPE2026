# Scientific review
## Correctness — PASS
The claimed domain is finite oriented complete bipartite digraphs with all \(2\times2\) orientation sums zero modulo two. The proof reconstructs the affine factorization, counts its twofold gauge precisely, separately derives each permutation's fixed vectors, and proves the side-swap even-cycle obstruction is necessary and sufficient. Burnside on the integer weight grid gives both isomorphism formulas, including odd sizes and singleton sides. Exhaustive checks over seven shape pairs independently enumerate matrices and test 288 permutation-character instances. These calculations corroborate, but do not replace, the general proof.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge; residual risk)
The two-class rank-one factorization is previously in Coskey--Ellis (2015) and is also implicit in Jasiński et al. (2013). The labelled orbit profile in public mathematical finding `5135d85e91a6` is stronger in the number of classes but does not provide its finite permutation character. Public mathematical finding `f8b9342567cf` studies translation orbits of arbitrary binary matrices under switch groups, rather than relabelling symmetries of parity-allowed matrices. The new claim is the exact side-preserving character, side-swap character with sharp all-even cycle criterion, and the ensuing unlabeled two-part census. A specialized enumeration not discovered in the checked literature remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The two-class case is the smallest natural semigeneric age layer carrying the nontrivial rectangle axiom. The theorem classifies every finite label-permutation symmetry and shows a sharp obstruction to part exchange not visible from the labelled count. The resulting exact isomorphism counts quantify how exponentially many labelled configurations collapse to polynomially many classes. This is a complete structural character computation, not a naked recomputation of an old type count.

## Limitations
The theorem is deliberately limited to two nonempty classes; no result is asserted for the full multipartite symmetry character. The comparison is bounded by accessible primary papers and a published research index. Finite verification is not a substitute for proof.
