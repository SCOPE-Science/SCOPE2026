# Cycle-type geometric fixed points of complete-graph matching complexes

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge2\) and every permutation \(\sigma\in S_n\), let \(M_n\) be the ordinary matching complex of the loopless complete graph \(K_n\), and let \(m_d\) be the number of length-\(d\) cycles of \(\sigma\), including fixed points as length-one cycles.

Define an abstract simplicial complex \(B_{d,m}\) on labelled blocks: for every \(1\le i<j\le m\) it has \(d\) distinct vertices \(e_{ij}^{(t)}\), \(0\le t<d\), each occupying both indices \(i,j\); if \(d\) is even it also has one vertex \(u_i\) occupying index \(i\). A set of these vertices is a face exactly when their occupied index-sets are pairwise disjoint. A factor \(B_{d,0}=\{\emptyset\}\), as well as \(B_{d,1}\) for odd \(d\), has no nonempty faces and acts as the **simplicial join unit**.

The geometric fixed-point space admits an exact PL model
\[
  |M_n|^{\langle\sigma\rangle}\ \cong_{\rm PL}\
  \left|\bigast_{d\ge1}B_{d,m_d}\right|.
\]
Only nonzero cycle multiplicities need be included. Thus every cycle type has a specified finite simplicial model, not just an Euler characteristic.

The face polynomial of each factor, with the empty face counted, is
\[
 \Phi_{d,m}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}
 \frac{m!}{2^j j!(m-2j)!}\,(dz)^j
 (1+z)^{(m-2j)\mathbf1_{\{2\mid d\}}}.
\]
The fixed-point face polynomial is \(\prod_{d\ge1}\Phi_{d,m_d}(z)\).

For every **even** \(d\) and every \(m\ge1\), the factor has the complete integral homotopy classification
\[
 |B_{d,m}|\simeq
 \begin{cases}
  \mathrm{pt},&m\text{ odd},\\
  \displaystyle\bigvee^{\,d^{m/2}m!/(2^{m/2}(m/2)!)} S^{m/2-1},&m\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Consequently, if **any even cycle length occurs an odd positive number of times**, the entire geometric fixed-point space is contractible. If \(\sigma\) consists only of even cycles, and every \(m_d\) is even, set
\[
 k=\sum_{d\text{ even}}\frac{m_d}{2},\qquad
 Q=\prod_{d\text{ even}}\frac{d^{m_d/2}m_d!}{2^{m_d/2}(m_d/2)!}.
\]
Then \(k\ge1\), and the **complete** fixed-point homotopy type is
\[
 \boxed{|M_n|^{\langle\sigma\rangle}\simeq\bigvee^Q S^{k-1}.}
\]
For example, two disjoint four-cycles have a fixed-point space homotopy equivalent to \(\bigvee^4 S^0\) (five components). Two four-cycles together with two six-cycles give \(\bigvee^{24}S^1\). The formula also specializes to the previously determined involution family of cycle type \(2^m1^r\); that specialization is **not** claimed as new.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph has all two-element edges and no loops. A matching is a set of pairwise vertex-disjoint edges. Its simplicial complex has one vertex for each graph edge, and all edge-disjoint collections as faces. The action permutes ground vertices, hence simplicial vertices. The notation \(|M_n|^{\langle\sigma\rangle}\) means the topological fixed set of the cyclic subgroup generated by \(\sigma\), **not** the subcomplex on individually fixed graph edges. All homotopy statements use the ordinary topology of geometric realizations and integral homotopy types; the join of empty-face-only complexes is understood at the abstract-complex level.

## Proof

**1. Exact fixed-simplex reduction.** In a simplex of \(M_n\), barycentric coordinates are indexed by its matching edges. A point fixed by \(\sigma\) has equal coordinates on each cyclic orbit of those edges. Hence its support is a disjoint union of full edge orbits. Conversely, any union of edge orbits that is itself a matching provides a fixed simplex, with vertices the normalized barycenters of the selected edge orbits. These affine simplices glue to give a PL triangulation of the entire geometric fixed set.

**2. Classification of usable edge orbits.** Consider two distinct vertex cycles of lengths \(a,b\). An orbit of an edge crossing them has length \(\operatorname{lcm}(a,b)\) and hits every vertex in the respective cycles \(b/\gcd(a,b)\) and \(a/\gcd(a,b)\) times. It is a matching if and only if \(a=b=d\). In that case, its edges are the \(d\) pairs in a phase-shifted bijection between cycles, and there are exactly \(d\) distinct phase shifts. Within a single vertex cycle, an edge orbit can be a matching if and only if the cycle length \(d\) is even and the endpoints are antipodal; this yields one orbit of \(d/2\) edges. Any usable edge orbit occupies **all** vertices of each ground cycle it meets, so two usable edge orbits are compatible precisely when their occupied ground cycles are disjoint. Different cycle lengths never interact. This proves the announced join decomposition with factors \(B_{d,m_d}\).

**3. Face count.** Select \(j\) pair-blocks among the \(m\) ground cycles: there are \(m!/(2^j j!(m-2j)!)\) matchings of the cycle-index set with \(j\) pairs. Each chosen pair permits \(d\) phase labels. When \(d\) is even, any subset of the remaining \(m-2j\) indices can additionally carry its single antipodal block; for odd \(d\) none can. Weighting every block by \(z\) gives \(\Phi_{d,m}(z)\), and the simplicial join gives multiplication.

**4. Complete even-length Morse reduction.** Fix even \(d\). On the full face poset of \(B_{d,m}\), including the empty face, project each face to its selected pair-blocks and forget all antipodal singleton-blocks. For any fixed pair-block matching, its unused ground-cycle indices form a set \(R\). If \(R\ne\emptyset\), toggle the unique antipodal vertex \(u_i\) for the smallest \(i\in R\). This is a complete acyclic toggle matching on the Boolean fiber over the selected pair-blocks. Since forgetting singleton-blocks is order-preserving, the standard poset cluster lemma pastes these fiberwise matchings into one acyclic matching on the full face poset. The only unmatched faces occur for \(R=\emptyset\), i.e. perfect pair-block matchings. If \(m\) is odd there are none and the resulting complex is contractible (the empty face is paired). If \(m=2h\), the unmatched faces all have \(h\) selected pair-block vertices and dimension \(h-1\). Their number is
\[
 \frac{m!}{2^h h!}\,d^h.
\]
The reduced discrete-Morse CW model has one implicit basepoint and precisely these \((h-1)\)-cells, with no other positive-dimensional cells. Therefore it is a wedge of the stated spheres, including the \(h=1\) zero-sphere case. This is an integral homotopy conclusion, not an inference from rational Betti numbers.

**5. Assembly.** A join with a nonempty contractible factor is contractible. Otherwise, the simplicial join of the classified even-length factors is homotopy equivalent to the join of their sphere wedges, giving sphere dimension \(k-1\) and multiplicity \(Q\). The cases containing odd-length factors remain exactly described by the join model and face polynomial; their general homotopy types are not asserted to be wedges of spheres.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify_cycle_fixed.py` reconstructs all edge orbits directly from 19 explicit permutation cycle types, tests the support-index classification and face polynomials against enumerated compatible orbit sets, computes exact mod-two reduced homology for 15 even-length factors (lengths two, four, six; multiplicities one to five), and checks the complete acyclic-toggle Hasse graph on 3,366 enumerated faces across those factors. It uses only Python's standard library. Expected successful completion is `CYCLE_ORBIT_FIXED_SPACE_CHECKS_OK 19 cycle_types 15 even_component_mod2_cases 3366 acyclic_matched_faces`. Finite checks supplement the general orbit and poset arguments; they do not independently establish the infinite family.

## Relationship to prior work

Singh's work on higher matching complexes of complete graphs and bipartite graphs develops nonequivariant discrete-Morse methods and gives homotopy types for different bounded-degree parameters. Mondal, Mukherjee, and Saha analyze the nonequivariant matching complexes of complete graphs, notably integral torsion for \(M_7\). Neither work's relevant theorem asserts the fixed-space cycle-type factorization or the all-even-cycle classification above. A previously recorded involution fixed-space classification gives the special length-one/length-two case; that case is expressly excluded from the novelty claim. The claimed contribution is the exact structure for arbitrary cyclic permutation types and the resulting closed classification for all-even cycle types. Searches for equivalent general geometric fixed-space results did not identify a covering antecedent; this is not a proof of priority.

## Limitations

No general homotopy classification of the residual odd-cycle factors \(B_{d,m}\) for odd \(d\) and arbitrary \(m\ge3\) is claimed. The face polynomial and join decomposition hold for those factors without asserting vanishing torsion or sphere-wedge type. The result concerns the fixed **space**, not equivariant homology representations of the full symmetric group. Bibliographic noncoverage of unindexed equivalent work remains a risk. The verification script checks selected finite parameters only. Independent mathematical auditing has not been performed.
