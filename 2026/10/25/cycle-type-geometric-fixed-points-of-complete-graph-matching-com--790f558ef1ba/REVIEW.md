# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof identifies geometric fixed points through equal barycentric weights on edge orbits, then classifies **every** usable edge orbit: length-mismatched cycles are impossible, equal-length cycles admit exactly one phase orbit per shift, and an even cycle contributes one antipodal orbit. The support rule proves the exact simplicial join. For even lengths, the forgetful map to pair-block faces has Boolean fibers; toggling the smallest unused singleton is an acyclic matching in each fiber, and the poset cluster lemma establishes global acyclicity. Unmatched cells are precisely the phase-labelled perfect matchings, yielding the stated integral wedge count and dimensions. Explicit small-case computation corroborates the orbit, face-polynomial, homology, and acyclicity statements. Scope includes empty-face-only factors and zero-dimensional wedges.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The closest inspected primary works concern ordinary topology and integral homology of complete-graph matching complexes, not geometric fixed sets for arbitrary permutations. A previously established involution formula *does* imply the length-\(2\)/length-\(1\) special case; that case is not claimed as new. It does not imply the phase-multigraph factorization for unequal nontrivial lengths, the even-cycle length-four and length-six formulas, or the complete general cycle-index face polynomial. Mathematical-source searches for those formulations found no covering theorem. Equivalent unindexed work remains an explicit priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Permutation fixed sets are natural topological invariants of the symmetric-group action on matching complexes. This classification handles an unbounded family of permutation cycle types, gives exact integral sphere-wedge dimensions and multiplicities for all-even types, produces a sharp contractibility criterion, and reduces the remaining cases to explicit independent factors. Ordinary Betti numbers or group characters do not determine these fixed-point homotopy types. The extension beyond involutions is substantive at the level of the geometric orbit decomposition, while the involution case itself receives no novelty credit.
