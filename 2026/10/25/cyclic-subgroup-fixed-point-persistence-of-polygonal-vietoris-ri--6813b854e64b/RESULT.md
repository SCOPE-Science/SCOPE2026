# Cyclic-subgroup fixed-point persistence of polygonal Vietoris–Rips complexes

## Finding

Let \(n\ge 3\), let \(d\mid n\) be any positive divisor, and put \(m=n/d\). Equip \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) with the shortest-cycle metric \(\delta_n(a,b)=\min\{(a-b)\bmod n,(b-a)\bmod n\}\). For a real scale \(r\ge0\), let \(K_n(r)=\operatorname{VR}_{\le r}(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,\delta_n)\), including a nonempty simplex precisely when every pair of its vertices has distance at most \(r\). Let \(H_d\le C_n\) be the order-\(d\) rotation subgroup generated by addition of \(m\) modulo \(n\). Its action on the geometric realization induces fixed-point spaces \(|K_n(r)|^{H_d}\).

**Theorem (entire fixed-point filtration).** If \(d\) is odd, write \(d=2s+1\). There are canonical piecewise-linear homeomorphisms, natural with respect to all inclusions of scales, such that
\[
|K_n(r)|^{H_d}\ \cong\ \begin{cases}
\varnothing,&0\le r<sm,\\
|K_m(r-sm)|,&r\ge sm.
\end{cases}
\]
The metric and Rips complex on \(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\) have their literal definitions also for \(m=1\) and \(m=2\). If \(d\) is even, the fixed-point space is empty for \(0\le r<n/2\), and is a nonempty simplex for \(r\ge n/2\). Consequently, with coefficients in any commutative ring, every reduced positive-degree fixed-point persistence module is zero for even \(d\) and is the degree-preserving translation by \(sm\) of the ordinary Rips persistence module of the \(m\)-cycle for odd \(d\). The isomorphisms also intertwine the rotations and reflections induced on the \(m\) orbit classes.

The theorem concerns **geometric fixed points** of the realized complex, not the orbit-space quotient of the realized complex. No original vertex must be fixed for the geometric fixed space to be nonempty.

## Assumptions and scope

The Rips convention is inclusive diameter \(\le r\), with the graph shortest-path distance on an \(n\)-cycle. Scales may be real, but changes occur only at integers. The ambient vertex set has \(n\ge3\) points. The subgroup has order \(d\ge1\) and divides \(n\), with exactly \(m=n/d\) vertex orbits of size \(d\). For \(m=1\), the quotient Rips complex consists of a single vertex at every nonnegative scale; for \(m=2\), it consists of two vertices before scale one and their edge afterwards. The identity subgroup \(d=1\) is included. Homology of the empty fixed space is not assigned an artificial generator.

## Proof

**Step 1: geometric fixed simplices are unions of complete vertex orbits.** Every point of \(|K_n(r)|\) has unique barycentric coordinates relative to the original vertices, supported on one simplex. The point is fixed by all of \(H_d\) exactly when its barycentric coefficients are constant on each orbit. Therefore the fixed set is naturally the geometric realization of a simplicial complex \(F_{n,d}(r)\) with one vertex for each of the \(m\) original vertex orbits; a subset of these orbit vertices spans precisely if the union of its original orbits is a simplex of \(K_n(r)\). The homeomorphism sends an orbit vertex to its uniform barycenter. Because it is independent of \(r\), it commutes with all filtration maps. Equivariance with respect to induced quotient rotations and reflections follows from their permutation of the orbits.

**Step 2: the exact orbit-diameter identity for odd order.** Suppose \(d=2s+1\). Label the orbit of a residue \(a\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\) by
\[
O_a=\{a+tm\pmod n:0\le t<d\}.
\]
For arbitrary orbit classes \(a,b\), let \(c=\delta_m(a,b)\in\{0,1,\ldots,\lfloor m/2\rfloor\}\). The differences between a vertex of \(O_a\) and one of \(O_b\), modulo \(n\), are exactly the \(d\) residues \(c+tm\pmod n\). Among these residues, the largest shortest-cycle distance equals \(sm+c\). To see this, on orienting the difference so that its representative is \(c\), the term \(t=s\) has shortest-cycle distance \(sm+c\), since \(c\le m/2\). Every term with \(t<s\) has distance at most \(sm+c\); every term with \(t>s\) is at distance at most \(sm-c\) or smaller after reflecting around \(n/2\). Hence, for all \(a,b\), including \(a=b\),
\[
\max_{x\in O_a,y\in O_b}\delta_n(x,y)=sm+\delta_m(a,b).
\]
In particular each nonempty vertex orbit spans an original simplex if and only if \(r\ge sm\). For any nonempty set \(S\) of orbit classes, its union has diameter \(sm+\max_{a,b\in S}\delta_m(a,b)\); this also holds for singletons because the \(a=b\) term measures intrafiber diameter. Therefore the faces of \(F_{n,d}(r)\) are exactly those of \(K_m(r-sm)\) when \(r\ge sm\), and there are no faces when \(r<sm\). The identification is simplicial, canonical, and simultaneous in \(r\).

**Step 3: the even-order obstruction.** If \(d\) is even, every vertex orbit contains an antipodal pair separated by \(n/2\), because its half-turn is \((d/2)m=n/2\). For \(r<n/2\), no nonempty complete orbit is a Rips simplex, and hence no geometric fixed point exists. For \(r\ge n/2\), every two original vertices have distance at most \(r\), so \(K_n(r)\) is the full simplex on \(n\) vertices; its fixed set is exactly the simplex of \(H_d\)-invariant probability vectors on the \(m\) orbit classes. This proves the even case and completes the theorem.

**Step 4: persistence consequence.** The maps in Step 1 identify fixed complexes at all scales by the same orbit-barycenter assignments. Applying reduced simplicial/singular homology with arbitrary coefficients therefore identifies the complete persistence modules, not only the ranks at individual scales. In the even case the nonempty fixed space is always contractible, so all positive-degree reduced homology vanishes. In the odd case it is precisely the translated ordinary cyclic Rips persistence module as stated.

## Verification

A standalone standard-library Python checker, `check_cycle_fixed.py`, independently enumerates cyclic orbit diameters for all divisors through \(n=100\), exhaustively compares the predicted and actual face sets for small quotient sizes and all diameter thresholds, and compares signed simplicial Lefschetz numbers with fixed-space Euler characteristics on smaller cycles. It reports \(407813\) diameter cases, \(49472\) nonempty orbit-subset face cases, and \(202\) signed Lefschetz cases checked. These are finite corroborations only; the preceding argument is the proof for every \(n,d,r\).

For illustration, take \(n=15\) and \(d=3\), so \(m=5\) and \(sm=5\). Then the fixed filtration has empty space before scale \(5\), five isolated points at scale \(5\), a circle at scale \(6\), and a simplex from scale \(7\). In reduced fixed-point homology it exhibits four degree-zero intervals \([5,6)\) and one degree-one interval \([6,7)\). In contrast, every even-order rotation subgroup has an empty-to-contractible fixed filtration with no positive-degree bars.

## Relationship to prior work

Adamaszek and Adams, *The Vietoris–Rips complexes of a circle* (arXiv:1503.03669, especially Theorem 4.3), establish ordinary homotopy types of clique complexes of cycle powers. Adams, Coldren and Willmot, *The persistent homology of cyclic graphs* (arXiv:1812.03374, especially Theorem 1 and Section 4), establish nonequivariant persistence methods for cyclic graph filtrations. Moy, *Vietoris–Rips Metric Thickenings of the Circle* (arXiv:2206.03539; first public 2022-06-07), studies metric thickenings, CW quotients, and ordinary persistence of the continuum circle, not the complete finite polygon fixed-space diagrams derived here. Adams, Heim and Peterson, *Metric thickenings and group actions* (arXiv:1911.00732, Section 3), compare Rips complexes on the metric orbit quotient with orbit quotients of Rips complexes; neither operation is geometric fixed points. The present theorem gives an exact, inclusion-compatible *fixed-point* reduction with a parity obstruction, rather than reasserting ordinary homotopy types or quotient-space identities.

## Limitations

The metric is the graph/geodesic cyclic metric, not arbitrary irregular polygons or higher-dimensional tori; the subgroup is rotational (not a reflection subgroup). The fixed spaces are taken after geometric realization; the abstract simplicial vertex-fixed subcomplex is a different object. No independent assessment of priority has been made. Equivalent unindexed proofs, for example via the general orbit-simplex construction, remain possible. Neither the quotient \(|K_n(r)|/H_d\) nor the homotopy type of its homotopy fixed points is determined. For even \(d\) the nonempty fixed space is a simplex of dimension \(m-1\), not necessarily a point; for odd \(d\), its nonequivariant homotopy groups follow from the established cyclic Rips theory but are not independently reclassified here.
