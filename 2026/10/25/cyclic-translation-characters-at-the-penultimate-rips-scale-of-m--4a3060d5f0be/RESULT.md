# Cyclic translation characters at the penultimate Rips scale of mixed-parity torus grids

## Finding
Let \(m\ge3\) be odd and \(b\ge2\), and put \(G=C_m\square C_{2b}\) with the shortest-path graph metric. The diameter is \(D=(m-1)/2+b\). Write \(X_{m,b}=\operatorname{VR}_{\le D-1}(G)\), where all finite vertex subsets having diameter at most \(D-1\) are simplices. Let \(t(i,j)=(i,j+1)\) be translation in the even cycle coordinate, of order \(2b\).

Define \(q_m=2\) when \(3\mid m\), and \(q_m=1\) otherwise, and
\[
d_m=\begin{cases}
2m/3-1,&3\mid m,\\
(2m-2)/3,&m\equiv1\pmod3,\\
(2m-4)/3,&m\equiv2\pmod3.
\end{cases}
\qquad B=b(d_m+1)-1.
\]
Then \(X_{m,b}\simeq\bigvee^{q_m^b}S^B\), and its only nonzero reduced integral homology is \(\widetilde H_B(X_{m,b};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{q_m^b}\). The additional **equivariant conclusion** is the following exact translation module and all its character values:

* If \(3\mid m\), identify this integral homology with \((\mathbb Z^2)^{\otimes b}\). Under a suitable choice of the factor identifications, \(t_*\) acts as the negative of cyclic tensor-factor rotation; hence for every integer \(k\),
\[
\operatorname{tr}(t_*^k)=(-1)^k 2^{\gcd(b,k)}.
\]
* If \(3\nmid m\), this homology is a rank-one integral lattice and \(t_*\) acts by \((-1)^b\), so \(\operatorname{tr}(t_*^k)=(-1)^{bk}\).

In particular \(t_*^b=(-1)^bI\) in both regimes. The integer character, rather than only rational Betti ranks, determines the cyclic representation specified above.

## Assumptions and scope
All cycles \(C_r\) are simple graph cycles; \(C_3\) is a triangle. The Cartesian product has edges that change exactly one factor along an edge. The Rips filtration uses closed, inclusive integral distance thresholds. The group acting is the cyclic group generated by the translation in the \(C_{2b}\) coordinate; no claim about all automorphisms or the entire filtration is made. Reduced homology is over \(\mathbb Z\), with the usual join orientation sign conventions.

## Proof
**1. Farthest-pair graph.** Distance in the Cartesian product is the sum of the two cyclic distances. For \(a=(m-1)/2\), the two vertices at maximal first-coordinate distance from \(i\) are \(i\pm a\), while the unique vertex at maximal second-coordinate distance from \(j\) is \(j+b\). Hence the only pairs forbidden at the threshold \(D-1\) are the edges
\[
(i,j)\sim(i+a,j+b),\qquad(i,j)\sim(i-a,j+b).
\]
For each pair of opposite columns \(\{j,j+b\}\), these edges form a single cycle of length \(2m\). Indeed each vertex has degree two, and \(\gcd(a,m)=1\), so the even two-step motion covers all first coordinates. There are exactly \(b\) such components. A vertex subset is a Rips simplex exactly when it avoids every farthest-pair edge. Thus, *as simplicial complexes*,
\[
X_{m,b}=\operatorname{Ind}\bigl(\bigsqcup_{j=0}^{b-1}C_{2m}\bigr)
=\operatorname{Ind}(C_{2m})^{*b}.
\]

**2. Factor topology.** The standard independence-complex cycle formula, obtained from the vertex-deletion cofibration and the path recurrence, states
\[
\operatorname{Ind}(C_{3r})\simeq S^{r-1}\vee S^{r-1},\quad
\operatorname{Ind}(C_{3r+1})\simeq S^{r-1},\quad
\operatorname{Ind}(C_{3r+2})\simeq S^r.
\]
For clarity, the path recurrence follows by deleting its second vertex: the deletion complex is a cone (the endpoint becomes isolated), while its link is the independence complex of a path with three fewer vertices. In the cycle, deleting a vertex gives the independence complex of a path on one fewer vertex and its link is the independence complex of a path on three fewer vertices. The resulting inclusion is null in the relevant dimensions and yields the displayed cycle formula. Applying the formula to \(2m\) gives \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_{2m})\simeq\bigvee^{q_m}S^{d_m}\). The join of \(b\) such wedges has integral reduced homology \((\mathbb Z^{q_m})^{\otimes b}\) in degree \(B=b(d_m+1)-1\), and is a wedge of \(q_m^b\) spheres in that degree. This proves the nonequivariant statements.

**3. Deck half-turn on one factor.** Let \(h\) exchange the two opposite columns within a farthest-pair cycle; on the canonical \(C_{2m}\) this is its antipodal half-turn. Consider \(K=\operatorname{Ind}(C_{2m})\). A point in \(|K|\) fixed by \(h\) has barycentric coefficients constant on each antipodal orbit; its support must be a union of antipodal pairs and must be independent. Quotienting those pairs yields exactly the independence complex of \(C_m\). Consequently \(|K|^h\cong|\operatorname{Ind}(C_m)|\), and the Lefschetz number of the finite simplicial involution is
\[
L(h;K)=\chi(\operatorname{Ind}(C_m)).
\]
If \(3\mid m\), then \(m/3\) is odd, so \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_m)\simeq S^{m/3-1}\vee S^{m/3-1}\) has Euler characteristic three. Here \(d_m\) is odd; therefore \(3=L(h)=1-\operatorname{tr}(h_*|\widetilde H_{d_m}(K;\mathbb Q))\) and its rank-two trace is \(-2\). Because \(h_*^2=I\), this forces \(h_*=-I\), integrally as well. If \(3\nmid m\), the cycle formula gives \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_m)\) as an odd-dimensional sphere, so its Euler characteristic is zero. Now \(d_m\) is even and the rank-one equation \(0=1+\operatorname{tr}(h_*)\) gives \(h_*=-1\). Thus in every case the deck half-turn acts as minus the identity on the complete nonzero reduced integral homology group of the factor.

**4. Translation sign and tensor trace.** Translation \(t\) shifts the \(b\) opposite-column components cyclically. After one trip through the cyclic list, the coordinate labeling of the wrapped component changes by the deck half-turn \(h\). On reduced homology of the iterated join, permuting two factor classes of degree \(d_m\) contributes the Koszul sign \((-1)^{(d_m+1)^2}=(-1)^{d_m+1}\). Cyclically rotating \(b\) factors therefore contributes \((-1)^{(d_m+1)(b-1)}\), and the wrapped deck map contributes another \(-1\).

When \(3\mid m\), \(d_m\) is odd, so \(t_*\) is the negative cyclic permutation of the factors \((\mathbb Z^2)^{\otimes b}\). Its \(k\)-th power has \(\gcd(b,k)\) tensor-coordinate cycles; in the standard tensor basis the underlying permutation fixes \(2^{\gcd(b,k)}\) basis tensors. Its sign is \((-1)^k\), establishing the character formula. When \(3\nmid m\), \(d_m\) is even and the rank-one action is \((-1)^{b-1}(-1)=(-1)^b\). This proves the integral cyclic module claims and the indicated half-period action.

## Verification
The standalone `check.py` reconstructs the exact farthest-pair graph using all pairwise graph distances. It tested all \(15\) pairs from \(m\in\{3,5,7,9,11\}\) and \(2b\in\{4,6,8\}\): in each case the forbidden graph has exactly \(b\) connected \(2m\)-cycles. Independent mod-two boundary-matrix calculations verified the expected reduced Betti numbers for \(C_6,C_{10},C_{14}\) and the full \(C_3\square C_4\) metric complex. For \((m,2b)=(3,4),(3,6),(5,4)\), direct alternating chain traces on fixed simplices matched the theoretical character for every power of the translation. Run with `python3 check.py`; no external packages are required. Finite tests corroborate but do not substitute for the preceding proof.

## Relationship to prior work
The full text of Adams, Adetowubo, Barriga-Acosta, Feng, and Sterling studies the Rips topology of torus grids, including final Rips scales in equal-side cases, but does not give the mixed-parity **integral translation representation** above. Antolín Camarena and Carnero Bravo explicitly identify cycle double covers and recall the standard independence-complex cycle theorem; hence the ordinary factor-wedge formula in the present work is an application of established mathematics, **not claimed as a novel theorem by itself**. The separate translation-character and integral signed-tensor description are the asserted new contribution. Equivalence under an uninspected formulation elsewhere remains a residual originality risk.

## Limitations
This concerns odd-by-even rectangular tori at precisely the next-to-last integer graph-distance scale, and the cyclic subgroup acting by the even-factor shift. It does not classify more general product metrics, higher factors, other Rips scales, or the action of the complete automorphism group. The argument proves the representation, not a compatible equivariant sphere-wedge decomposition. An independent audit has not been performed.
