# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The claim specifies the Fano-triple Coxeter graph and one alternating simple cycle per switch, avoiding confusion between a 24-edge symmetric difference and a 24-cycle. All 84 perfect matchings are generated by a complete recursive branching argument. A pairwise construction and an independent explicit alternating-cycle construction agree on 42 eight-switch edges, 168 ten-switch edges and every breadth-first distance layer. The exact 42 antipodal pairs have the disjoint cycle partition (8,16), not a single long alternating cycle. An explicit isomorphism witness reconciles the two host-graph presentations. The standard-library programs and complete adjacency certificate are included and replayed.

**Originality — PASS, with uncertainty.** The 84 perfect matchings and their symmetry transitivity are known from the 2011 Coxeter exposition. The 2015 Figueroa–Fresán-Figueroa–Rivera-Campo manuscript defines perfect-matching graphs for cycle families and provides general connectivity criteria; these results neither enumerate Coxeter ten-cycle exchanges nor give a distance profile or classify antipodes. Ito et al. (2019) allow unrestricted alternating-cycle lengths, a strictly different mobility model. No directly covering claim was identified in targeted literature, record and accessible prior-result searches. Unindexed equivalences remain possible; the 2025 matching-polynomial article was not fully accessible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Coxeter's highly symmetric cubic girth-seven non-Hamiltonian graph is a natural boundary case for length-restricted perfect-matching dynamics. The result identifies the sharp move-length threshold for connectivity and gives a complete uniform distance profile; notably, every state has a unique farthest counterpart with an exact two-cycle decomposition. These facts determine global reconfiguration geometry, not merely repeat the known matching count or an arbitrary subset enumeration.
