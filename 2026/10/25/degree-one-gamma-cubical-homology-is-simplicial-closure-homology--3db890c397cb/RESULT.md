# Degree-one \(\Gamma\)-cubical homology is simplicial-closure homology

## Finding

Let \(H=(V,E)\) be a finite hypergraph in which every vertex belongs to a nonempty hyperedge. Its **simplicial closure** is
\[
K(H)=\{S\subseteq V:S\subseteq e\text{ for some }e\in E\}.
\]
Use the \(\Gamma\)-cubical chain complex of Zaidi and Jamil, with integer coefficients and degenerate cubes quotiented out. For every such \(H\), there are canonical, natural integral isomorphisms
\[
\boxed{H_i^{\Gamma}(H;\mathbb Z)\cong H_i(K(H);\mathbb Z)\qquad (i=0,1).}
\]
The degree-one map sends the singular oriented edge with endpoint images \((a,b)\) to the oriented simplicial edge \([a,b]\). It is compatible with hypergraph morphisms and therefore identifies the degree-one persistent modules of any inclusion filtration of finite hypergraphs with the ordinary degree-one simplicial persistence modules of their closures, over \(\mathbb Z\) or any fixed commutative coefficient ring.

The equivalence is genuinely integral: the hypergraph with six vertices and ten triple hyperedges
\[
123,\ 124,\ 135,\ 146,\ 156,\ 236,\ 245,\ 256,\ 345,\ 346
\]
(where the strings name sets of vertices) satisfies
\[
\boxed{H_1^{\Gamma}(H;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.}
\]
Thus first \(\Gamma\)-homology admits torsion, even though the examples of this theory in the motivating work primarily display free groups.

## Assumptions and scope

Hyperedges are nonempty subsets of the finite vertex set; every vertex is contained in a hyperedge. No uniformity, linearity, connectedness, purity, or restriction on intersections is imposed. The simplicial closure \(K(H)\) is the downward-closed complex generated by the hyperedges, not the clique complex of the two-section graph. Ordinary simplicial chains use integer coefficients and a fixed total order on \(V\).

For \(n\ge1\), the source \(\Gamma\)-hypercube has vertex set \(\{0,1\}^n\) and hyperedges equal to Hamming-radius-one closed balls. A singular cube is a hypergraph morphism from that hypercube to \(H\); a cube independent of any coordinate is degenerate. The differential is
\[
\partial_n\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^i(A_i\sigma-B_i\sigma).
\]
The theorem identifies only degrees zero and one; it does **not** assert an isomorphism in higher homological degrees, or an equivalence of chain complexes in all degrees.

## Proof

**Step 1: degrees zero and one.** Because every vertex lies in a hyperedge, singular zero-cubes correspond to vertices of \(K(H)\). A nondegenerate singular one-cube is precisely an ordered pair \((a,b)\) of *distinct* vertices belonging to a common hyperedge. Let \(u_{ab}\) denote its generator. Thus the cubical first chain group is freely generated by both orientations of every simplicial edge. The boundary is \(\partial u_{ab}=b-a\).

Define a surjective homomorphism
\[
q:C_1^{\Gamma}(H)\longrightarrow C_1(K(H)),\qquad
q(u_{ab})=[a,b].
\]
Its kernel is freely generated by the backtracking relations \(r_{ab}=u_{ab}+u_{ba}\), one for each unordered edge. The degree-zero map is the identity on vertices and commutes with boundaries.

**Step 2: all cubical two-boundaries become simplicial triangle boundaries.** Write the four images of a singular \(\Gamma\)-two-cube at \((0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\) as \(a,b,c,d\). Every Hamming-radius-one ball in the source contains three of the four corners. Hence each of \(\{a,b,c\}\) and \(\{b,c,d\}\) is a face of \(K(H)\), with repetitions allowed. Direct expansion gives
\[
\partial_2\sigma=-u_{ac}+u_{bd}+u_{ab}-u_{cd},
\]
where \(u_{xx}=0\). Under \(q\), this becomes
\[
q(\partial_2\sigma)
=\partial_2^{\mathrm{simp}}[a,b,c]
 +\partial_2^{\mathrm{simp}}[b,d,c].
\]
Triangles with repeated vertices mean zero. Consequently
\(q(\operatorname{im}\partial_2^{\Gamma})\subseteq
\operatorname{im}\partial_2^{\mathrm{simp}}\).

**Step 3: all necessary relations occur as cubical two-boundaries.** Given any simplicial edge \(\{a,b\}\), the singular two-cube with corner values \((a,b,a,a)\) is admissible, because its image lies in a common hyperedge. Its boundary equals \(r_{ab}\). Hence \(\ker q\subseteq\operatorname{im}\partial_2^{\Gamma}\).

Given any simplicial triangle \(\{a,b,c\}\), the admissible two-cube with corner values \((a,b,c,b)\) has boundary
\[
\partial_2\sigma=u_{ab}-u_{ac}-u_{cb}.
\]
Its image under \(q\) is \(\partial_2^{\mathrm{simp}}[a,b,c]\). Therefore
\(q(\operatorname{im}\partial_2^{\Gamma})=
\operatorname{im}\partial_2^{\mathrm{simp}}\).

**Step 4: integral, natural identification.** The surjection \(q\) identifies the cubical and simplicial first-cycle groups modulo its backtracking kernel. Since that kernel already lies in the cubical two-boundaries, and their images under \(q\) are exactly the simplicial two-boundaries, the first isomorphism theorem gives
\[
\ker\partial_1^{\Gamma}/\operatorname{im}\partial_2^{\Gamma}
\cong
\ker\partial_1^{\mathrm{simp}}/\operatorname{im}\partial_2^{\mathrm{simp}}.
\]
The same degree-zero compatibility gives the stated zero-degree isomorphism. All constructions are integral and natural: any hypergraph morphism preserves containment in hyperedges and induces the corresponding map of simplicial closures. Tensoring with a coefficient ring yields the coefficient version; naturality gives the persistence statement.

**Step 5: integral two-torsion example.** The ten displayed triples generate a six-vertex triangulated closed connected surface. Every edge is in exactly two triangles, every vertex link is a five-cycle, and the face numbers are \((6,15,10)\), so its Euler characteristic is \(1\); it is the real projective plane. For direct integer verification, the signed \(15\times10\) simplicial triangle-boundary matrix has Smith invariants
\[
1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1,2,
\]
while the \(6\times15\) edge-boundary matrix has five unit invariant factors. Therefore its first integral homology is exactly \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\), with no free summand. Step 4 transfers this to \(H_1^{\Gamma}\).

## Verification

Run `python3 gamma_compare_verify.py` with Python 3 and SymPy 1.14 (or a compatible recent SymPy version). The standalone script constructs admissible singular \(\Gamma\)-two-cubes directly from the four radius-one stars. It checks the triangle-boundary identity over the integer edge group in 65 finite hypergraphs (including the projective-plane example, the triangle, the square, and a non-uniform cycle), encompassing 25,427 admissible two-cubes. It independently checks the ten-facet projective-plane surface conditions, the chain condition, and the integral Smith invariants. Expected final marker: `VERIFY_OK`.

The exhaustive finite tests support the implementation, but the general theorem for all finite hypergraphs follows from Steps 1–4, not extrapolation from those cases.

## Relationship to prior work

Zaidi and Jamil, *A cubical approach to homology theories for hypergraphs* (2026), define \(\Gamma\)-cubical chains and compute several isolated first-homology examples, including a triangle, a non-uniform cycle and linked families of triangles. Their Proposition 3.10 treats an explicit family with free first homology; their Section 3.1 supplies the singular-cube definitions used here. The present statement supplies a functorial, **all-hypergraph** comparison to ordinary simplicial-closure homology and derives an integral torsion realization; none of those specific comparison or torsion assertions is stated in the inspected portions of their work.

Associated simplicial complexes and embedded homology of hypergraphs predate \(\Gamma\)-cubical homology; they furnish the classical comparison object, not a pre-existing identification with this recently defined chain theory. In particular, simplicial-closure homology must not be conflated with embedded hypergraph homology.

## Limitations

No conclusion about \(H_i^{\Gamma}\) for \(i\ge2\) is claimed. Vertices that belong to no hyperedge are excluded so that the two degree-zero conventions align without extra bookkeeping. The public-priority assessment is limited by the searchable and inspected literature; absence of a matching statement in searches does not prove absolute novelty. All computation is same-work verification and is not independent mathematical validation.
