# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The integer-chain comparison is explicit: degree-one singular cubes are oriented pairs from common hyperedges; the reversal kernel is killed by the admissible cube with images \((a,b,a,a)\); every two-cube boundary maps to the sum of two triangle boundaries on the opposite radius-one stars; and every triangle boundary comes from \((a,b,c,b)\). These identities establish equality of integral boundary subgroups and hence the degree-one isomorphism without assuming finite-test extrapolation. The projective-plane triangulation is checked by vertex links, edge incidences and integral Smith normal form. The statement and proof handle all covered-vertex finite hypergraphs and natural morphisms, but claim nothing in higher degrees.

## Originality — PASS

The closest primary source is Zaidi–Jamil, arXiv:2610.08196v1, Section 3.1, Examples 3.6–3.11 and Proposition 3.10. It defines the exact chain theory and proves specific examples with free degree-one homology; it does not state the universal natural isomorphism to the simplicial-closure first homology, nor the order-two torsion consequence, in the inspected material. Bressan–Li–Ren–Wu and Ren–Wang–Wu–Wu define classical associated complexes and different embedded homology, predating the new theory. Searches for the comparison, simplicial-closure aliases, degree-one generators, and torsion found no stronger or equivalent covering statement. Residual bibliographic uncertainty remains; a search alone is not exhaustive priority evidence.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a complete degree-one classification of a recently introduced theory on **every** finite hypergraph, rather than a selected graph or numerical case. It reveals a structural limit on what \(\Gamma\)-homology can distinguish in degree one, transfers integral torsion and persistence calculations to established simplicial methods, and produces a smallest-standard-surface type of genuinely torsion-bearing example absent from the source's free-homology examples. The chain argument is concise but its universal comparison changes interpretation and computation of the new invariant.

## Scope, sources, and residual risk

The theorem concerns only degrees zero and one, excludes vertices absent from every hyperedge, and uses the original definition of \(\Gamma\)-hypercubes in arXiv:2610.08196v1. Closest related mathematical constructions are the downward simplicial closure and embedded hypergraph homology, which are distinct theories. The source was inspected at its defining Section 3.1 and all relevant degree-one examples in Section 3.5; bibliographic coverage may be incomplete. Finite checker runs do not replace the general argument or an independent review.
