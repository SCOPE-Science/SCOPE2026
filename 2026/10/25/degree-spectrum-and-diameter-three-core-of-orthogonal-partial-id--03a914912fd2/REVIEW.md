# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
Every idempotent is a partial retraction onto its fixed set. The zero-composition conditions reduce to two disjointness tests and give an exact conditional count of orthogonal idempotents at each rank. Counting the possible fixed sets and domains provides the degree polynomial. The diameter proof gives a universal three-edge route through singleton identities and an explicit pair with no common neighbor. The extremal-degree and clique arguments are constructive. Complete enumeration of ranks, domains and graph distances in the specified finite cases agrees with the formulas.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The comparison is for the stated *vertexwise degree spectrum and graph metric*, not the global number of orthogonal idempotent pairs: the latter can be implied by an older full-idempotent commuting-pair generating function after adding a zero point. Garba's abstract concerns projection characteristics and idempotent generation, not an orthogonality graph. The accessible 2011 enumeration treats order-restricted transformation monoids. The full 2024 and 2026 graph treatments use common-annihilator adjacency or its complement on a different vertex set. Published-record and semantic database searches found no statement that implies the local degree formula and sharp diameter. Full access to Garba's 1990 paper was unavailable; undiscovered equivalence remains a priority risk. Search absence is not a uniqueness certificate.

## Value — PASS
The degree multiset and connectivity give natural local and global invariants of a canonical product-defined graph associated to an important finite transformation semigroup. Domain size changes degrees even at fixed rank. The classification isolates a sharp structural distinction (full-domain retractions versus a diameter-three core) and determines both maximum- and minimum-degree families for every size. This is substantially richer than a numerical check or a restatement of global rank-pair counts.
