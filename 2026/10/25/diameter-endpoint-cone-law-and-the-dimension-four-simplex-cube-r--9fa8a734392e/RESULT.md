# Diameter-endpoint cone law and the dimension-four simplex–cube reversal

## Finding

Let \(d\ge3\) and let \(P\subset\mathbb R^d\) be any full-dimensional compact convex polytope of volume \(V\) and Euclidean diameter \(D>0\). If \(X,Y\) are independent and uniformly distributed on \(P\), then

\[
\Pr(\|X-Y\|>D-\varepsilon)=C(P)\varepsilon^{2d}+o(\varepsilon^{2d})\quad(\varepsilon\downarrow0),
\]

with a strictly positive, computable tangent-cone coefficient \(C(P)\). For every **ordered** diametral vertex pair \((v,w)\), set \(e=(v-w)/D\), and let \(T_v=\operatorname{cone}(P-v)\) and \(T_w=\operatorname{cone}(P-w)\) be the vertex tangent cones. Define their directionally truncated volumes by

\[
H_v(v,w)=\operatorname{vol}_d\{z\in T_v:-e\cdot z\le1\},\qquad
H_w(v,w)=\operatorname{vol}_d\{u\in T_w:e\cdot u\le1\}.
\]

Every listed cap has finite, strictly positive volume. The exact leading coefficient is

\[
\boxed{C(P)=\frac{(d!)^2}{(2d)!\,V^2}
\sum_{\substack{(v,w)\text{ ordered vertices}\\\|v-w\|=D}}
H_v(v,w)H_w(v,w).}
\]

For diameter-one regular \(d\)-simplices and diameter-one \(d\)-cubes the two constants simplify to

\[
C_{\rm simplex}(d)=\frac{d(d+1)4^{d-1}}{\binom{2d}{d}},\qquad
C_{\rm cube}(d)=\frac{2^d d^{2d}}{(2d)!}.
\]

Their ratio is strictly decreasing for integer \(d\ge2\), and its sign relative to one changes exactly between dimensions three and four:

\[
\frac{C_{\rm simplex}(3)}{C_{\rm cube}(3)}=\frac{32}{27}>1,
\qquad
\frac{C_{\rm simplex}(4)}{C_{\rm cube}(4)}=\frac{45}{64}<1.
\]

Thus a regular tetrahedron has a greater *sufficiently near-diameter* interpoint-distance tail than a same-diameter cube, whereas a regular \(d\)-simplex has a smaller tail than a same-diameter cube for every \(d\ge4\). In dimension three the coefficients are \(48/5\) and \(81/10\), respectively; in dimension four they are \(128/7\) and \(8192/315\).

## Assumptions and scope

Distances are ambient Euclidean, not boundary intrinsic or difference-body gauge distances. Points are independent and distributed according to normalized \(d\)-dimensional Lebesgue measure *inside* the polytope. The polytope is fixed while \(\varepsilon\) tends to zero. Diameter-one normalization is used **only** in the simplex–cube comparison. The general cone formula is scale covariant and does not require a simple or simplicial polytope. The sum ranges over ordered, not unordered, diametral vertex pairs; the orientation is needed for the cap definitions.

## Proof

**1. Only isolated vertex pairs attain the diameter.** Fix \(y\in P\). A strict convexity argument for the Euclidean norm shows that a nonvertex \(x\in P\) cannot maximize \(\|x-y\|\): express \(x\) as a nontrivial convex combination of distinct vertices; equality in the triangle inequality at the maximal distance \(D\) would require all the vectors from \(y\) to these vertices to coincide, an impossibility. The same argument applies to \(y\). Thus \(\|x-y\|=D\) only for finitely many pairs of vertices. Distinct product neighborhoods of these ordered pairs contain all pairs with \(\|x-y\|>D-\varepsilon\) when \(\varepsilon\) is sufficiently small, by compactness.

**2. Directional caps are bounded.** Fix a diametral pair \((v,w)\) and \(e=(v-w)/D\). For every vertex \(q\ne v\), the diameter inequality gives

\[
0\ge\|q-w\|^2-D^2=2D\,e\cdot(q-v)+\|q-v\|^2,
\]

hence \(-e\cdot(q-v)>0\). Since \(T_v\) is generated by the finitely many vectors from \(v\) to other vertices, \(-e\cdot z\) is strictly positive for every nonzero \(z\in T_v\). Its restriction to the unit spherical slice of the cone is bounded away from zero; therefore \(\{z\in T_v:-e\cdot z\le1\}\) is compact with positive \(d\)-volume. The same argument at \(w\) yields positivity of \(e\cdot u\) on \(T_w\setminus\{0\}\). It also supplies \(c>0\) such that, for \(z\in T_v,u\in T_w\),

\[
-e\cdot z+e\cdot u\ge c(\|z\|+\|u\|).
\]

**3. Blow up each vertex pair.** On sufficiently small fixed neighborhoods, the polytope agrees with its translated vertex tangent cones. For \(x=v+\varepsilon z\) and \(y=w+\varepsilon u\),

\[
\|x-y\|=D+\varepsilon e\cdot(z-u)+O(\varepsilon^2(\|z\|+\|u\|)^2).
\]

The positive linear bound from step 2, together with a sufficiently small choice of neighborhoods, implies that the distance deficit \(D-\|x-y\|\) is at least \(c'(\|x-v\|+\|y-w\|)\) there, for some \(c'>0\). Hence any contributing rescaled pair \((z,u)\) lies inside one fixed bounded set, independently of sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\). Apart from a measure-zero boundary, the rescaled event converges pointwise to

\[
\big\{(z,u)\in T_v\times T_w:-e\cdot z+e\cdot u<1\big\}.
\]

Dominated convergence applies. The physical \(2d\)-dimensional volume contributes a factor \(\varepsilon^{2d}\).

**4. Evaluate the cone-pair integral.** Let \(a=-e\cdot z\) and \(b=e\cdot u\). Cone homogeneity gives the cumulative cap volumes \(H_v a^d\) and \(H_w b^d\) for positive thresholds. The volume of the limiting condition \(a+b<1\) is therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
&d^2H_vH_w\int_{a,b>0\,,\,a+b<1}a^{d-1}b^{d-1}\,da\,db\\
&\hspace{20mm}=\frac{(d!)^2}{(2d)!}\,H_vH_w.
\end{aligned}
\]

Sum over the disjoint neighborhoods and divide by \(V^2\), the joint uniform density, to obtain the claimed \(C(P)\). Every term is positive.

**5. Exact examples.** If a vertex cone is simplicial with edge generators \(r_1,\ldots,r_d\), and \(a_j=-e\cdot r_j>0\), then its cap volume is

\[
H_v=\frac{|\det(r_1,\ldots,r_d)|}{d!\prod_{j=1}^d a_j}.
\]

A triangulation gives a finite sum of such expressions for any nonsimplicial vertex cone. In a diameter-one regular simplex, the \(d\) outgoing edge vectors have axial projection factors \(1,1/2,\ldots,1/2\) relative to any opposite vertex. Their determinant has absolute value \(d!V\); thus \(H_v=2^{d-1}V\). There are \(d(d+1)\) ordered diametral vertex pairs. Substitution gives \(C_{\rm simplex}(d)\).

For an orthotope of side lengths \(a_1,\ldots,a_d>0\), \(D=(\sum_i a_i^2)^{1/2}\), \(V=\prod_i a_i\). Its only diametral pairs are opposite vertices (\(2^d\) ordered pairs), and at each pair \(H_v=D^d/(d!V)\). Thus

\[
C_{\rm orthotope}=\frac{2^dD^{2d}}{(2d)!V^4}.
\]

For a diameter-one cube \(a_i=1/\sqrt d\), this is \(C_{\rm cube}(d)\).

**6. The unique dimension-four reversal.** Their ratio is

\[
R_d=\frac{C_{\rm simplex}(d)}{C_{\rm cube}(d)}
=\frac{(d+1)2^{d-2}(d!)^2}{d^{2d-1}},
\quad
\frac{R_{d+1}}{R_d}=\frac{2(d+2)d^{2d-1}}{(d+1)^{2d}}.
\]

The last quotient is strictly smaller than one for \(d\ge2\), since the first three binomial terms give

\[
\left(1+\frac1d\right)^{2d}\ge
1+2+\frac{2d-1}{d}=5-\frac1d>2+\frac4d.
\]

Direct exact evaluation yields \(R_3=32/27\) and \(R_4=45/64\). Strict monotonicity then proves the crossover and all higher-dimensional inequalities.

## Verification

The standalone `verify.py` checks all exact constants and monotonicity through dimension 61, reconstructs a unit regular tetrahedron from explicit Euclidean vertices, independently derives cube difference-coordinate densities, and performs reproducible importance sampling for three-dimensional simplices and cubes in dimensions three and four. The normalized finite-threshold coefficients approach their analytical limits as the threshold decreases. All numerical tests are finite corroboration, not substitutes for steps 1–6 of the proof. Run `python verify.py` with Python 3 and NumPy installed.

## Relationship to prior work

Complete planar random-point distance distributions, including all triangles and polygons, are already known; the result therefore concerns \(d\ge3\) and does not claim a new planar distribution. Full distance laws for three-dimensional cubes are also known, so the three-dimensional cube coefficient is a check and comparison ingredient, not independently claimed as new. A general convolution/covariogram identity already gives interpoint distance distributions of compact sets, including hypercubes; the distinct statement here is the **local tangent-cone coefficient valid for all full-dimensional polytopes**, together with the all-dimensional simplex–cube comparison and its sharp dimension-four reversal. Work on Hausdorff errors of random polytopes concerns a different statistic: a random sample hull rather than the distance between two prescribed random points. No claim of definitive historical priority is made.

## Limitations

No statement is made about smooth strictly convex bodies, whose near-diameter tails can have different exponents; or about a polytope that changes with \(\varepsilon\). The comparison is strictly asymptotic, not a stochastic order at every distance threshold. Formulae for general tangent-cone volumes can require nontrivial geometric triangulation. Existing full cube-distribution results cover the cube component in isolation. Historical-priority verification remains provisional, particularly for older higher-dimensional random-distance literature. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
