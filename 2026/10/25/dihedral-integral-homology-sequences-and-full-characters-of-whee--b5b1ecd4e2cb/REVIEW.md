# Review of the dihedral wheel matching-complex theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The key literal decomposition separates matchings with no spoke from the cones with exactly one spoke. It gives an equivariant relative-chain decomposition, not merely an Euler-characteristic identity. Standard integral path and cycle independence-complex homotopy types restrict the long exact sequence to the three stated cases. In the residue \(n\equiv1\pmod3\), both auxiliary inclusions have contractible links and identify every spoke summand with the same rim module, yielding the augmentation kernel. In the residue \(n\equiv2\pmod3\), the spoke stabilizer reverses a path; an explicit signed Lefschetz calculation proves the sign used in the induced module. Independent finite chain and character checks pass (see VERIFICATION.md). No integral splitting is asserted in the last case. The proof, rather than any finite test, handles all \(n\ge3\).

## Originality — PASS with stated access risk

The strongest directly covering paper, Vega's *2-Matching Complexes* (arXiv:1909.10406v2, Theorem 5.2), establishes exactly the nonequivariant wheel homotopy ranks; that existing result is not repackaged as new. The inspected outerplanar paper (arXiv:2411.04601v2, Theorem 2 and Conclusion) discusses wheels but establishes a general wedge property for outerplanar graphs. Public research records about complete dihedral characters of *cycle* matching complexes cover the rim term only; wheel discrete-Morse complexes are different objects from ordinary wheel matching complexes. The new integral augmentation identification, induced-module exact sequence, and explicit wheel character require additional inclusion-map calculations. Full text of some public research records could not be opened, so unnoticed equivalent calculations remain a stated risk. Searches are not treated as a proof of uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

Automorphisms act nontrivially even when ordinary Betti numbers and homotopy sphere counts are fixed. The formulas determine all rotation and reflection traces in an infinite natural planar family, distinguish the two reflection types for even rim size, and identify the origin of extra homology from spoke attachments. They give usable symmetry-sensitive invariants without assuming an unproved equivariant wedge splitting. This is a structural representation calculation, not a one-parameter numerical increment.

## Limits and closest evidence

The 2019 wheel homotopy theorem and 2024 outerplanar work are closely relevant, but do not state this representation theorem. Some independent cycle-character records were available by summarized mathematical statements only, not complete text. No claim of publication priority, independent review, integral splitting in the third residue, or equivariant homotopy classification is made.
