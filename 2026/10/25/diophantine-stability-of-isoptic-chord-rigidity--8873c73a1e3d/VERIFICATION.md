The Fourier multiplier was derived from the stated shift formula. For even and odd indices separately, the bad-approximability hypothesis bounds the small factor below by a constant divided by the frequency, while separation of adjacent zero sets bounds the other factor away from zero. Parseval then gives the estimate for the full Fourier series. The continued-fraction sequence gives a matching order of small multipliers, and the chosen support functions have positive radius of curvature. No finite-frequency truncation is used to infer the infinite-dimensional estimate.

Limits: the constant depends on the Diophantine constant; the result gives no stronger-norm or Hausdorff stability and does not cover every irrational angle.
