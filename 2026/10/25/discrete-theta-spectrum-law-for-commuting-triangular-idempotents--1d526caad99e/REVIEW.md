# Scientific review: discrete-theta spectrum law for commuting triangular idempotents

**Correctness — PASS.** Joint spectral projections give a unitriangular simultaneous conjugator even in characteristic two. The orbit-stabilizer computation is exact for every prime-power field. The lattice exponent identity, Stirling normalization, and a uniform multinomial maximum bound justify dominated convergence and total-variation convergence. The sharp dimension-defect rate follows from matching balanced-color lower and Cauchy--Schwarz upper bounds. The packaged finite-field checker exhaustively validates twelve small cases, including a nonprime field.

**Originality — PASS for the stated probability laws, not for the underlying finite color-count polynomial.** Hou and Wright study triangular idempotents; Gargate--Gargate and Słowik supply the same color multiplicity weight for scalar-root-potent or finite-order triangular matrices, so the weighted count must not be claimed as a new formula. The reviewed new assertion is a fixed-field, labelled-joint-spectrum discrete-theta law and an exact speed-\(n^2\) rate for failure of full split-subalgebra generation in a commuting idempotent tuple. Relevant full text of Gargate--Gargate gives counts but not these limits. Searches of research records and primary literature found no equivalent asymptotic theorem. The connection to complete-graph antiferromagnetic Potts statistics could permit an earlier equivalent theorem; this is a genuine residual priority risk, as are two inaccessible relevant primary full texts. Lack of a match is not proof of absolute novelty.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** This is a natural, label-sensitive spectral invariant of tuples generating commutative split subalgebras of a noncommutative finite matrix algebra. Bounded discrete-Gaussian fluctuations sharply contrast with ordinary multinomial square-root fluctuations. The exact superexponential rate of missing full joint spectrum quantifies a meaningful generation property across all finite fields, including characteristic two. It is not merely a finite table or a routine normalization check.
