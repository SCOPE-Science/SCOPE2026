# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** The specified hopping phases give the tridiagonal backbone matrix \(BB^*\) with off-diagonal coupling \(g=2\cos(\phi/2)\). For \(0<\phi<2\pi\), row independence and irreducibility show that the initial backbone state has no dark component off the caging flux. The two distal end modes are simple at zero intercell coupling and have exact modal dissipation rate asymptotic to \(g^{2L}/(\gamma4^{L-1})\); their weights sum to one. Interior and sink-proximal modes have lower-order integrated effects. Numerical Lyapunov comparisons support the calculation, but are not used to prove the infinite family. Risks: a simultaneous growing-chain/vanishing-sink limit requires separate analysis.

**Originality: PASS on the inspected evidence, without exhaustive priority certification.** Lopes–Dias give the magnetic diamond-chain bands and localized cage states; Gligorić–Leykam–Maluckov analyze disorder; Zhang–Jin treat non-Hermitian exceptional-point caging; Riberi analyzes semi-infinite boundary-sink transport. None of their inspected claims states or directly specializes to the finite-chain sharp flux-detuning terminal first-passage coefficient. The closest available research-record hits address different quantum graph band fractions and diamond structures. Risk: a more general transport/Green-function theorem or unindexed specialist work might imply the coefficient without matching its wording.

**Value: PASS.** Terminal escape time is a natural transport observable for Aharonov–Bohm-caged photonic/excitonic chains. The theorem quantifies a nonuniform flux tolerance: each additional cell costs two powers of detuning, with a sharp prefactor. This goes beyond recognizing the already-known perfectly flat spectrum and exact caging at half flux.

**Scope:** The finding is a fixed finite-length, fixed positive-loss detuning asymptotic and does not classify all absorbing geometries or the full first-passage law.
