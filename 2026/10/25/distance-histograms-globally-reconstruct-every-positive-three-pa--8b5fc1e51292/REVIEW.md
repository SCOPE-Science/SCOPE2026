# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The claim concerns every real triple \(0<a\le b\le c\), with normalized arclength on the three-edge theta graph. The proof recovers \(L\) from the universal local ball law, identifies the support endpoint \((b+c)/2\) by explicit paths, and reduces near-diameter events to four exact half-plane inequalities in a rectangle. The first agreement threshold \((b-a)/2\) is shown to be sharp by a strictly positive first-order jump from new edge-pair families, including the \(b=c\) case. The \(a=b<c\) and \(a=b=c\) alternatives are calculated separately. No infinite inference is drawn from numerical experiments. The standalone exact rational checker completed 961 tests and four independently sampled comparisons; its results agree with the analytic computations.

## Originality — PASS, with uncertainty

The closest relevant literature is Mémoli–Needham's distance-distribution inverse-problem paper (arXiv:1810.09646), whose accessible abstract specifies *local* metric-graph injectivity rather than a global all-length theta inversion. The other closest graph-reconstruction literature (Aanjaneya et al. 2011, Lecci et al. 2013) uses labeled/sample-point geometric data, not one scalar random-pair law. Wei et al. give forward network-distance formulas; this does not imply injectivity. published-finding corpus semantic searches for theta-graph distance histograms, three parallel path lengths, and banana-network inversions returned theta-related homology and algebraic-invariant results but no covering geodesic-distance reconstruction claim. The source manuscript's full text could not be obtained; this is a real priority risk, not evidence that it lacks an equivalent statement.

## Value — PASS

The theorem is a global inverse result on the simplest two-branch-vertex graph with overlapping cycles, going beyond a single-branch-vertex star or rose. It demonstrates that a one-dimensional unmarked law, ordinarily noninjective on broad metric-graph classes, can retain every positive edge length even across degeneracies where length thresholds coalesce. The exact quadratic tail and its first breaking point provide a transparent geometric reconstruction mechanism. Finite-sample statistical stability is not proved.
