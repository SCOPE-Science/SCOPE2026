# Scientific review: uniform-matroid flat lattices

## Correctness

PASS. The objects are specified as exactly the proper subsets of size below the matroid rank plus the whole ground set. An exact four-Venn-cell extension system computes the full rank-one pair type; projecting through all second choices gives rank-two pointed types, and projecting through first choices gives rank-three sentence types. The crucial uniform algebraic row table is included in `RESULT.md` and fully expanded as actual masks in `PROFILES.json`. Exact subset enumeration at the eleven distinct minimal representatives (with a twelfth equal-rank check) agrees with an independent orbit-based calculation. The script checks each parameter pair through ground-set size 25; the infinite claim depends on the displayed inequality elimination and seven-row lemma, rather than numerical extrapolation. All positive rank, size, and endpoint cases are included.

## Originality

PASS on available evidence, with a residual historical-overlap risk. The 2015 truncated-Boolean paper studies cutset profiles and extremal width, not three-round logical theories. Standard matroid-flat theory supplies the object, not the eleven-class first-order classification. The already known Boolean-inclusion classification is logically the restriction to \(n=r\); it cannot imply the new classes for \(n>r\) or their sharp 121-element representative. Search results involving Boolean algebras in functional signatures are not stronger statements in this relational signature. The available publisher synopsis of Faigle's lattice chapter does not permit a whole-chapter novelty exclusion. Searches cannot certify global uniqueness.

## Value

PASS. The lattice of flats is a canonical invariant of a matroid. The finding determines a full natural rank-three logical invariant over a two-parameter classical family, establishes a non-obvious Boolean/truncation boundary that remains visible when rank itself has stabilized, and gives an optimal finite testing bound of 121 flats. This is a complete family classification, not merely a code-generated finite row count or a restatement of the uniform-matroid flat description.

## Evidence and limitations

The explicit four-cell formulas and symbolic endpoint cases are the central proof. Independent direct subset enumeration and the enumerated parameter sweep are useful cross-checks but not substitutes for the all-parameter lemma. The first-order equivalence does not apply to functional lattice languages, higher quantifier rank, or arbitrary matroids. The 1986 chapter was not inspected in full, and unindexed equivalent work remains possible.
