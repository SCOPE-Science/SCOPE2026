# Research review

## Correctness

**PASS.** Equilibrium petal coordinates are forced equal by the exact difference identity, and any live petal satisfies one shared infection parameter. Together with the hub balance this yields a necessary-and-sufficient scalar cubic, and its unique maximum gives all physical roots, including equality at the fold. The full Jacobian splits into within-petal antisymmetric, between-petal symmetric, and hub-coupled modes. Every inactive petal supplies a strictly positive eigenvalue, while the full-support two-dimensional determinant changes sign at the cubic maximum. The disease-free Jacobian is negative identity. The analytic argument handles all integers \(m\ge1\), every \(\lambda>0\), all boundary states, and the degenerate threshold. The reproduced standalone checks exercise the critical algebra and direct ODE residuals.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to bibliographic risk.** The closest primary mathematical sources already establish that higher-order contagion can create bistability and that homogeneous populations can have two nonzero fixed points. Those generic observations are excluded from the novelty claim. Iacopini et al. (2019), inspected through their full open-access article, derive only an aggregate homogeneous-mixing equation and mention heterogeneous simplicial degrees as open. Cisneros-Velarde and Bullo (2020) give multi-group sufficient conditions; their irreducible pairwise hypotheses cannot directly cover a zero-pairwise model, and no exact all-subset count appears in the inspected preprint. Kiss et al. (2023), inspected in the open manuscript, address fully connected complexes rather than hub-shared independent petals. A 2023 triadic-approximation paper was compared at abstract level but not excluded by full-text inspection. The new result's subset-resolved census, strict threshold hierarchy, and transverse-instability theorem were not found in these sources or in the relevant bounded literature searches. An unindexed equivalent under another model name remains a genuine uncertainty.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The friendship graph is an elementary clustered contact motif in which different triangles share precisely one potential spreader. The distinction between a huge number of stationary patterns and only one attracting endemic pattern is structurally informative: after \(\lambda>4\), the model has exponentially many equilibria but only two hyperbolically stable ones. The exact threshold cascade and its \(m^{-1/3}\) scaling reveal how increasing the number of group interactions changes the onset of endemic states. This is a full classification of a natural infinite family, not a generic restatement of a scalar fold or a finite numerical census.

## Closest literature and limitations

The original analytical homogeneous-mixing model is Iacopini et al., *Simplicial models of social contagion*, arXiv:1810.07031. The multi-group framework is Cisneros-Velarde and Bullo, arXiv:2005.11404. The exact higher-order-complete-network setting is Kiss et al., arXiv:2309.12752; a related triadic approximation is Burgio et al., arXiv:2306.11441. The claim is confined to the displayed deterministic node-wise mean-field ODE with pure triangle transmission and fixed unit recovery. It does not characterize basin sizes, global trajectories, or exact stochastic infection processes. Equivalent results under different terminology remain a residual originality risk.
