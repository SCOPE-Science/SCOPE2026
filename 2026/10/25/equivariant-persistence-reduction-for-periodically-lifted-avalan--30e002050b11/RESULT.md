# Equivariant persistence reduction for periodically lifted avalanche complexes

## Finding
Let \(k\ge2\) and \(m\ge2\), and let \(b=(b_0,\ldots,b_{k-1})\) be a nonzero vector of nonnegative integers. On the consistently oriented directed cycle \(\overrightarrow C_{mk}\), place \(\widetilde b_j=b_{j\bmod k}\) grains at vertex \(j\). Write \(\mathcal A_t\) for the avalanche complex generated by all simultaneous firing sets at times \(0,\ldots,t\), and let \(\rho(j)=j+k\pmod{mk}\), a rotation of order \(m\).

**Theorem (all filtration stages, integral and equivariant).** For each integer \(t\ge0\), the geometric fixed locus of the rotation has a canonical piecewise-linear homeomorphism
\[
|\mathcal A_t(\overrightarrow C_{mk},\widetilde b)|^{\langle\rho\rangle}
\cong |\mathcal A_t(\overrightarrow C_k,b)|.
\]
The fixed-locus inclusion is a **strong, rotation-equivariant deformation retract** of the entire larger realization. These retractions are compatible with every inclusion in the avalanche filtration. Consequently the two persistence modules are naturally isomorphic over \(\mathbb Z\) and over every coefficient ring, in all homological degrees; their persistence barcodes coincide over every field, and the rotation acts trivially on integral homology at every stage.

In particular, take \(b=(0,1,\ldots,1)\). For every \(m\ge2\), the corresponding avalanche complex on \(\overrightarrow C_{mk}\), whose zero-grain vertices are one complete residue class modulo \(k\), has integral homotopy type
\[
\mathcal A(\overrightarrow C_{mk},\widetilde b)\simeq S^{k-2}.
\]
For \(b=(1,0,\ldots,0)\), the full avalanche complex has exactly \(k\) contractible components. The former proves the period-dividing case of the residue-zero sphere prediction in Riihimäki–Smith (2026); no such formula is asserted for arbitrary cycle lengths not divisible by the residue modulus.

## Assumptions and scope
A directed cycle \(\overrightarrow C_s\), \(s\ge2\), has vertices \(\mathbb Z/s\mathbb Z\) and arcs \(j\to j+1\). In the two-vertex case the arcs have both directions, without loops. Parallel sandpile firing declares exactly the vertices with at least one grain unstable, fires them simultaneously, removes one grain from each firing vertex, and transfers one grain to its successor. The avalanche complex \(\mathcal A_t\) is the downward simplicial closure of the firing sets at times at most \(t\). The final avalanche complex is generated by all times. A nonzero initial vector ensures each firing set is nonempty. All homotopy statements concern ordinary geometric realizations of finite simplicial complexes, with no basepoint requirement for disconnected spaces.

The action \(\rho\) rotates the \(m\) vertices in each residue fiber of the quotient map \(p:\mathbb Z/(mk)\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/k\mathbb Z\). The term *compatible with the filtration* means that the retraction and its homotopy have the same barycentric formulas for every \(t\), hence commute strictly with inclusion maps. The statement concerns arbitrary nonnegative multigrain patterns, not only binary patterns.

## Proof
**1. The dynamics descends exactly.** Let \(c^{(t)}_j\) be the grain count, and let \(e^{(t)}_j\) equal \(1\) if \(c^{(t)}_j\ge1\), and \(0\) otherwise. Simultaneous firing gives the exact recurrence
\[
c^{(t+1)}_j=c^{(t)}_j-e^{(t)}_j+e^{(t)}_{j-1}.
\]
Initially \(c^{(0)}_{j+k}=c^{(0)}_j\). If this holds at time \(t\), it also holds for the indicators and, by the recurrence, at time \(t+1\). Hence the larger system stays constant along each fiber at every time. Writing the common value in fiber \(r\) as \(d^{(t)}_r\) yields precisely the same recurrence as for the smaller cycle with initial configuration \(b\). Thus, with \(\sigma_t\) denoting its firing set,
\[
\widetilde\sigma_t=p^{-1}(\sigma_t)
\quad\text{for every }t\ge0.
\]
This identity holds also through transients and recurring phases; no assumption of eventual binary configurations is used.

**2. Full-simplex fiber inflation.** By the generating-set identity, a subset \(F\) of the \(mk\) vertices is a face of the larger filtered avalanche complex if and only if \(p(F)\) is a face of the smaller filtered avalanche complex. In one direction, \(F\subseteq p^{-1}(\sigma_i)\) implies \(p(F)\subseteq\sigma_i\). Conversely, if \(p(F)\subseteq\sigma_i\), then \(F\subseteq p^{-1}(\sigma_i)\). In particular the full fiber preimage of every smaller face is a larger face. This is *simplex inflation* (all copies within an included vertex block appear in the same maximal simplex), not the independent-fiber blow-up of a digraph.

**3. Explicit equivariant deformation.** Write a point of the larger geometric realization in barycentric coordinates \(x_{r,a}\ge0\), where \(0\le r<k\), \(0\le a<m\) and vertex \((r,a)\) means \(r+ak\). Define maps
\[
\Pi(x)_r=\sum_{a=0}^{m-1}x_{r,a},\qquad
J(y)_{r,a}=\frac{y_r}{m},\qquad R=J\Pi.
\]
By step 2, \(\Pi\) maps the larger realization to the smaller, and \(J\) maps the smaller to the larger. We have \(\Pi J=\mathrm{id}\). The rotation \(\rho\) permutes coordinates cyclically within each fiber, so its geometric fixed locus is exactly the set of points with equal barycentric coordinates within each fiber, namely \(J(|\mathcal A_t(\overrightarrow C_k,b)|)\).

For any \(x\), choose a full lifted firing simplex containing its support. Both \(x\) and \(R(x)\) lie in that same simplex. Therefore
\[
H_s(x)=(1-s)x+sR(x),\qquad 0\le s\le1,
\]
is a well-defined continuous homotopy inside the larger realization. It fixes the fixed locus pointwise, ends at \(R\), and commutes with \(\rho\), since \(R\rho=R\) and \(\rho R=R\). The formulas are independent of \(t\); hence each \(H_s\) restricts compatibly to every earlier filtration stage. This proves the filtered equivariant strong deformation retraction and the fixed-locus homeomorphism.

**4. Homological and sphere consequences.** Filtration-compatible homotopy equivalences induce mutually inverse natural transformations on simplicial (or singular) homology with arbitrary coefficients. Hence all persistent modules agree; field barcodes are equal at the same integer filtration parameters, with no rescaling. Since \(\rho\) fixes the retraction target pointwise, its induced action on homology is the identity. The same deformation descends to the orbit-space quotient.

For \(b=(0,1,\ldots,1)\), each firing set of the smaller cycle omits one distinct vertex, and all \(k\) omissions occur in successive rounds. Their maximal simplices are exactly the \(k\) facets of the boundary of a \((k-1)\)-simplex. Thus the small final complex is \(\partial\Delta^{k-1}\cong S^{k-2}\). The theorem transfers this *integral homotopy* classification to every repeated lift. For \(b=(1,0,\ldots,0)\), the smaller firing sets are the \(k\) distinct singletons, giving exactly \(k\) components; transfer again follows. These arguments cover \(k=2\) with the usual \(S^0\) convention.

## Relationship to prior work
Riihimäki and Smith, arXiv:2606.26786v1, define avalanche complexes in Definition 2.4, introduce their time filtration in Section 4, and discuss residue-class firing patterns in Lemma 3.17 and Conjecture 3.18. Their results do not give the all-multigrain, all-stage equivariant fixed-locus reduction here. The newly established period-dividing sphere consequence is compatible with their proposed formula only under the stated divisibility restriction; the unrestricted residue-class assertions have independently documented wraparound counterexamples.

The prior published-finding corpus record *Sharp arithmetic transitions in complementary residue avalanche complexes* concerns \(n=kq+s\) with \(1\le s<k\) and \(\gcd(s,k)=1\), so excludes the present periodic-lift setting \(n=mk\). The record explicitly leaves the divisible case outside its theorem. The separate record *Forward-shift blow-ups preserve digraph homotopy type* concerns path homology under independent-fiber **digraph** blow-ups, not sandpile avalanche complexes, geometric fixed loci, or persistence. Standard simplicial fiber-inflation contraction is not claimed as an abstractly new topology construction: the contribution is the exact sandpile-dynamical realization of that contraction, its simultaneous filtration compatibility and rotation-fixed identification for every multigrain base pattern.

## Limitations
The reduction assumes a consistently oriented cycle and an initial vector constant on residue fibers. It does not classify arbitrary nonperiodic configurations or graph coverings, and it gives no new closed formula for the smaller cycle's homology for arbitrary \(b\). The result is a natural equivariant homotopy equivalence, not an isomorphism of uncompressed simplicial complexes. Finite checking does not certify the full parameter range; the symbolic proof does. The literature searches and specific full-text comparisons cannot exclude an unindexed earlier formulation of the same filtered-equivariant reduction.
