# Equivariant Rips persistence of subdivided doubles of complete graphs

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge 4\), form the subdivided double \(F_n\) of the complete graph \(K_n\): its vertices are \(v_{i,0},v_{i,1}\) for \(1\le i\le n\) and \(e_{ij}=e_{ji}\) for \(1\le i<j\le n\). Each \(e_{ij}\) is adjacent to the four vertices \(v_{i,0},v_{i,1},v_{j,0},v_{j,1}\), and there are no other edges. Endow this finite vertex set with graph distance and let \(X_r=\operatorname{VR}_{\le r}(F_n)\) be its inclusive Vietoris--Rips simplicial complex. The graph has its natural symmetry group \(G_n=(C_2)^n\rtimes S_n\), which independently swaps the two copies of each original vertex and permutes their labels.

**The symmetry-refined theorem** determines the entire positive-dimensional rational persistence as \(G_n\)-modules. Write \(P_n=\mathbb Q^n\), the coordinate-permutation representation of \(S_n\), and \(V_n=\ker(\mathbb Q^n\xrightarrow{\mathrm{sum}}\mathbb Q)\). Let \(\varepsilon_i\) denote the sign character of the normal subgroup \((C_2)^n\) associated with swapping the \(i\)-th pair. At scale one, with its natural semidirect-product action,
\[
\boxed{H_1(X_1;\mathbb Q)\cong
\bigwedge^2 V_n\ \oplus\
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\left(\varepsilon_i\otimes
\ker\bigl(\mathbb Q^{[n]\setminus\{i\}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{sum}}\mathbb Q\bigr)\right).}
\]
The first summand is fixed by all pair swaps; the latter summands are permuted by vertex relabelings, and the \(i\)-th summand is negated precisely by the \(i\)-th swap. These spaces have dimensions \(\binom{n-1}{2}\) and \(n(n-2)\), respectively, totaling \(3\binom n2-2n+1\).

At scale two,
\[
\boxed{H_2(X_2;\mathbb Q)\cong\bigwedge^3 P_n,}
\]
where the entire subgroup \((C_2)^n\) acts trivially; all positive-degree homology apart from the indicated group vanishes at each respective scale. At scale three and above the complex is contractible. Thus the complete positive-dimensional persistence consists of an interval \([1,2)\) carrying the first displayed \(G_n\)-representation and an interval \([2,3)\) carrying \(\bigwedge^3P_n\). No assertion that the underlying nonequivariant Betti numbers or sphere-wedge types are new is made.

For an explicit complete character, let \(g=(\pi,\delta)\in G_n\), where \(\pi\in S_n\) and \(\delta_i\in\{0,1\}\) swaps the \(i\)-th pair before relabeling. Let \(f\) be the number of fixed labels of \(\pi\), let \(f_0\) count fixed labels \(i\) with \(\delta_i=0\), and let \(t_2\) count the two-cycles of \(\pi\). Then
\[
\boxed{\chi_{H_1}(g)=2f_0(f-2)-\binom f2-t_2+1.}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\boxed{\chi_{H_2}(g)=\sum_{\substack{T\subseteq[n],\,|T|=3\\\pi(T)=T}}\operatorname{sgn}(\pi|_T),}
\]
independent of \(\delta\). For \(n=5\), the graph \(F_5\) is the twenty-vertex Folkman graph, whose full automorphism group is \(G_5\) of order \(3840\). Here \(\dim H_1(X_1;\mathbb Q)=21\) and \(\dim H_2(X_2;\mathbb Q)=10\). As an \(S_5\)-module, the second group decomposes as \(S^{(3,1,1)}\oplus S^{(2,1,1,1)}\), of dimensions six and four. Every element of the normal \((C_2)^5\) acts trivially on \(H_2\), whereas \(H_1\) has a six-dimensional flip-invariant part and five distinct three-dimensional single-flip character parts. Both positive-degree modules have zero full-group invariant subspace.

## Assumptions and scope
All graph vertices, edges, and distances are defined explicitly in the preceding paragraph. The filtration is on the **finite vertex set** using integer shortest-path distance, not on a continuum metric realization. Homology with representation structure is over \(\mathbb Q\); nonequivariant homotopy assertions below are integral. We orient every graph edge from a doubled vertex toward an edge-subdivision vertex and every simplex by a fixed total ordering of its vertices. A natural \(S_n\) action permutes \(i\) and maps \(e_{ij}\) to \(e_{\pi(i)\pi(j)}\), while a generator of \((C_2)^n\) exchanges \(v_{i,0}\) and \(v_{i,1}\).

## Proof
**One-dimensional module.** Because \(F_n\) is a connected bipartite graph, \(X_1\) is precisely its one-dimensional graph; no triangles appear. With the indicated common bipartite edge orientation, the augmented cellular chain sequence is \(0\to H_1(F_n;\mathbb Q)\to C_1\xrightarrow{\partial}C_0\to\mathbb Q\to0\), and is \(G_n\)-equivariant. Decompose this sequence under the commuting involutions of \((C_2)^n\). In the trivial joint eigenspace, identify every doubled pair by its average. The resulting chain complex is the ordinary once-subdivided \(K_n\), so its graph cycle lattice is naturally \(\bigwedge^2 V_n\) as an \(S_n\)-representation. This follows also from \(C_1(K_n)=\bigwedge^2P_n\) and the exact contraction map \(\bigwedge^2P_n\to V_n\), whose kernel is \(\bigwedge^2V_n\).

For the joint character \(\varepsilon_i\), the only nonzero edge-chain coordinates are the signed differences of the two edges connecting \(v_{i,0}\) and \(v_{i,1}\) to each \(e_{ij}\), \(j\ne i\). The edge-subdivision boundary contributions cancel. At the two doubled vertices the boundary is the negative of the sum of these coordinates. The kernel therefore consists exactly of the \((n-2)\)-dimensional augmentation subspace with coordinate sum zero. No nontrivial character supported on two or more swaps occurs in the cellular chain complex, hence none occurs in its homology. Label permutations transport the \(i\)-summand to the corresponding \(\pi(i)\)-summand, proving the asserted semidirect representation decomposition. In particular, \(|V(F_n)|=2n+\binom n2\) and \(|E(F_n)|=4\binom n2\); the cycle rank is \(4\binom n2-(2n+\binom n2)+1=3\binom n2-2n+1\).

**Two-dimensional module.** At threshold two, the vertices \(v_{i,0}\) and \(v_{i,1}\) have identical closed neighborhoods in the threshold graph: each is within distance two of every doubled vertex, and it is within distance at most two of precisely those subdivision vertices with endpoint \(i\). Deleting one clone from every pair is a simplicial dominated-vertex collapse, leaving the threshold-two clique complex of the once-subdivided complete graph \(S(K_n)\). Moreover, each transposition of a clone pair is contiguous to the identity simplicial map (the union of any face with its image is again a face), so \((C_2)^n\) acts trivially on the homology of \(X_2\), not merely after choosing a nonequivariant collapse.

Write the original vertices of \(S(K_n)\) as \(p_i\). The threshold-two clique complex \(Y\) of \(S(K_n)\) has exactly four types of maximal generating simplices: the full simplex \(P=\{p_i:1\le i\le n\}\), the vertex stars \(C_i=\{p_i\}\cup\{e_{ij}:j\ne i\}\), the triangles \(D_{ij}=\{p_i,p_j,e_{ij}\}\), and the subdivision triangles \(T_{ijk}=\{e_{ij},e_{ik},e_{jk}\}\). Let \(Z\) be the subcomplex generated by the first three kinds. Each subdivision vertex can be removed from \(Z\) in any order while its link remains a union of two simplices on its opposite original endpoints, connected by the edge joining those endpoints; this is contractible. After those removals, \(Z\) is the simplex \(P\). Thus \(Z\) is contractible and invariant under \(S_n\).

For each triple \(i<j<k\), the oriented simplex \(T_{ijk}\) is the only type of face of \(Y\) outside \(Z\); its entire boundary lies in \(Z\). Thus \(H_2(Y,Z;\mathbb Z)\) is freely generated by the \(\binom n3\) triangle classes, and the long exact sequence for \((Y,Z)\) identifies this free module with \(H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\) equivariantly. A permutation mapping the triple \((i,j,k)\) onto another triple permutes its three two-element subsets with the **same orientation sign** as the permutation of \((i,j,k)\). Hence the signed triangle-permutation module is \(\bigwedge^3\mathbb Z^n\), and the rational claim follows. The quotient \(Y/Z\) is a wedge of these \(\binom n3\) two-spheres, giving the integral nonequivariant type.

**Character and death.** An automorphism fixes a doubled vertex precisely when its label is fixed by \(\pi\) and its own flip bit is zero: there are \(2f_0\) such vertices. It fixes a subdivision vertex when its unordered endpoint pair is invariant: there are \(\binom f2+t_2\). It fixes a graph edge precisely when a fixed doubled vertex is incident to a fixed subdivision vertex, giving \(2f_0(f-1)\). The Lefschetz trace in the connected graph is \(\chi_{H_1}=\operatorname{tr}C_1-\operatorname{tr}C_0+\operatorname{tr}H_0\), which equals the displayed formula. The exterior-cube character is the indicated sum of signed invariant three-subsets. Finally, at scale three each doubled vertex lies within distance three of **every** vertex of \(F_n\), so \(X_3\) is a simplicial cone. The inclusion maps cannot sustain the degree-one classes past scale two or the degree-two classes past scale three because the corresponding groups vanish at those stages. This identifies the equivariant persistence intervals.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` independently constructs \(F_5\), computes all shortest-path distances, generates its full threshold-two clique complex, and checks an explicit acyclic matching on its actual face poset. The complete complex has \(1413\) nonempty simplices, \(6390\) codimension-one incidences, and an acyclic matching of \(701\) pairs, leaving one critical vertex and ten critical two-cells. Therefore the threshold-two integral homotopy type is \(\bigvee^{10}S^2\), independently of the symbolic \(n\)-parameter proof.

The verifier separately checks the graph and signed fixed-simplex Lefschetz traces for all \(3840\) elements of \(G_5\), reproducing both full character formulas. It also checks all maximal cliques, the graph cycle rank, and conicality at threshold three. Execute `python3 verify.py` (Python standard library); its stored complete output is `verification_output.txt`, ending in `VERIFY_OK`. The finite computation checks the canonical first semisymmetric example but is **not** an exhaustive check for unbounded \(n\); the general theorem is the symbolic chain and relative-homology argument above.

## Relationship to prior work
M. Adamaszek, *Clique complexes and graph powers* (first public arXiv version 2011-04-03), Section 5, already develops the scale-two clique topology of subdivisions of arbitrary graphs and supplies the core-and-triangle-attachment method underlying the nonequivariant sphere wedge. A later published calculation, *The Rips filtration of the complete-graph subdivision has two consecutive sphere wedges* (2026), specifically gives the entire **nonequivariant** Rips barcode of \(S(K_n)\), including the \(\binom n3\) two-spheres at scale two. These facts are treated as prior work, not as original findings.

The genuinely additional claim here is the complete \((C_2)^n\rtimes S_n\) character **across two successive persistence degrees** for the doubled-subdivision family: the explicit anti-twin cycle modules at degree one and their disappearance, together with the signed triple-permutation representation at degree two on which every twin swap acts trivially. The undoubled subdivision calculation has no anti-twin symmetry and does not imply its full graph-cycle representation. Likewise, knowing that the scale-two Betti number is \(\binom n3\) does not determine the alternating permutation action. Targeted searches against the subdivision/graph-power, Folkman graph, symmetry-module, and Rips-persistence formulations found no earlier combined full-group result.

## Limitations
The symmetry claim is for the explicitly defined finite-vertex graph-metric filtration. It does not classify equivariant homotopy types, equivariant integral splitting at every scale, or analogous filtrations of arbitrary base graphs. The degree-two integral homology identification follows from relative cells; the combined barcode decomposition is stated over \(\mathbb Q\). The literature comparison cannot rule out an older unpublished or differently titled full-character theorem. The construction itself dates to the classical study of semisymmetric graphs; the archived 2011 version supplies the dated primary Rips-topology anchor, not the earliest date the underlying graph was ever known.
