# Exact affine least-squares amplification across simplex skeleta

## Finding

Let \(n\ge3\) and \(1\le s\le n\). A simplex has \(n\) affinely independent vertices. Form the *face-balanced skeletal probability measure* \(\mu_{n,s}\) by choosing one of its \(\binom ns\) faces with \(s\) vertices uniformly and then taking normalized \((s-1)\)-dimensional Hausdorff measure on that face. At \(s=1\) this is uniform measure on the vertices, and at \(s=n\) it is normalized volume measure. On a regular simplex it is exactly normalized Hausdorff measure on the entire \((s-1)\)-skeleton.

Let \(P_{n,s}\) be the \(L^2(\mu_{n,s})\)-orthogonal projector onto restrictions of all affine functions. Set
\[
B_{n,s}=\frac{(n-1)(s+1)}{2n-s-1},\qquad
u_{n,s}=\frac{s-1}{(n-1)(s+1)}.
\]
Then its exact sharp endpoint norm is
\[
\boxed{\|P_{n,s}\|_{L^1\to L^1}=\|P_{n,s}\|_{L^\infty\to L^\infty}=2B_{n,s}(1-\nu_{n,s})^s-1.}
\]
The supremum of the absolute integral kernel is attained at every simplex vertex. For \(s\ge2\), the sign of its kernel at any vertex gives an \(L^\infty\)-extremizing input; for \(s=1\) the operator is the identity on vertex data.

The exact norms include \(\|P_{3,2}\|=16/9\), \(\|P_{4,2}\|=83/45\), and \(\|P_{4,3}\|=89/36\). For each fixed positive integer \(s\),
\[\lim_{n\to\infty}\|P_{n,s}\|=s.\]
If \(s=s(n)\) and \(s/n\to\alpha\in(0,1]\), then
\[\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\|P_{n,s}\|}{n}=\frac{2\alpha e^{-\alpha}}{2-\alpha}.}\]
In particular, the intermediate skeleta interpolate in a nontrivial, exactly quantified way between vertex interpolation and volume least squares.

## Assumptions and scope

All measures are probability measures. The projector is the orthogonal projection onto an \(n\)-dimensional affine-function space, understood on the support of the measure; no finite sampling, empirical fit, interpolation beyond \(s=1\), or higher-degree polynomial projection is involved. For a nonregular simplex the face-balanced mixture remains well-defined but generally differs from unrestricted normalized Hausdorff measure on the union of faces because the face volumes need not agree. The result is affine-invariant for this specified mixture.

Represent any simplex point by barycentric coordinates \(t=(t_1,\ldots,t_n)\), where \(t_i\ge0\) and \(\sum_i t_i=1\). A sample \(T\sim\mu_{n,s}\) can equivalently be generated by selecting a uniformly random \(s\)-subset \(S\) and taking a \(\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1,\ldots,1)\) distribution on the coordinates indexed by \(S\), all other coordinates being zero. These definitions include the zero-dimensional vertex skeleton.

## Proof

**1. The covariance and reproducing kernel.** By exchangeability and the elementary moments of a uniform Dirichlet vector,
\[
\mathbb ET_i=\frac1n,\qquad
\mathbb ET_i^2=\frac{2}{n(s+1)},\qquad
\mathbb ET_iT_j=\frac{s-1}{n(n-1)(s+1)}\quad(i\ne j).
\]
Consequently, on the contrast space \(\{z\in\mathbb R^n:\sum z_i=0\}\), the covariance matrix acts as scalar multiplication by
\[
\lambda_{n,s}=\frac{2n-s-1}{n(n-1)(s+1)}>0.
\]
Writing \(\Gamma_{n,s}=1/\lambda_{n,s}=nB_{n,s}\), an orthogonal decomposition into constants and centered contrasts gives the exact symmetric projection kernel
\[
K(t,u)=1+\Gamma_{n,s}\left(\sum_{i=1}^n t_i u_i-\frac1n\right).
\]
Its integral in \(u\) is exactly one. This is a genuine reproducing kernel: integrating it against any affine function in \(u\) reproduces that function at \(t\).

**2. The maximum lies at a vertex.** For any fixed \(u\), the function \(t\mapsto |K(t,u)|\) is convex. Hence
\[
F(t)=\int |K(t,u)|\,d\mu_{n,s}(u)
\]
is convex on the closed simplex. Since each \(t\) is a convex combination of its vertices \(v_i\), \(F(t)\le\max_i F(v_i)\). By vertex-transitivity of the face-balanced law, all these vertex values coincide. The support of \(\mu_{n,s}\) contains every vertex, and the continuous function \(F\) attains values arbitrarily close to its vertex value on sets of positive measure. Thus the essential supremum required for \(L^\infty\) is the vertex value, even when the vertices themselves have zero measure.

**3. An exact one-coordinate beta calculation.** At \(t=v_1\), the kernel is
\[
K(v_1,T)=1-B_{n,s}+nB_{n,s}T_1.
\]
For \(s=1\), every input is vertex data and the projection is the identity, agreeing with the claimed value one. Assume \(2\le s\le n\). Now \(B_{n,s}>1\), and the zero of the displayed kernel occurs at
\[
\nu_{n,s}=\frac{B_{n,s}-1}{nB_{n,s}}=\frac{s-1}{(n-1)(s+1)}\in(0,1).
\]
The variable \(T_1\) is zero with probability \(1-s/n\). Conditional on its being selected, it has \(\operatorname{Beta}(1,s-1)\) distribution. The beta survival function is \(\Pr(T_1>z\mid1\in S)=(1-z)^{s-1}\) for \(0\le z\le1\); therefore
\[
\mathbb E\min(T_1,\nu_{n,s})
=\frac{s}{n}\int_0^{\nu_{n,s}}(1-z)^{s-1}\,dz
=\frac{1-(1-\nu_{n,s})^s}{n}.
\]
Using \((a-bx)_+=a-b\min(x,a/b)\) for \(a,b>0\), this proves
\[
\mathbb E(-K(v_1,T))_+
=(B_{n,s}-1)-nB_{n,s}\mathbb E\min(T_1,\nu_{n,s})
=B_{n,s}(1-\nu_{n,s})^s-1.
\]
Since \(\int K(v_1,u)d\mu(u)=1\), the vertex absolute row integral is exactly \(1+2\mathbb E(-K)_+\), giving the stated \(L^\infty\) norm. The sign input \(f(u)=\operatorname{sgn}K(v_1,u)\) has essential supremum one and achieves equality after applying the projection. Because the kernel is symmetric, the \(L^1\)-operator norm is the same absolute column integral supremum by Fubini and duality. Attainment in \(L^1\) is not asserted.

**4. The two limits.** At fixed \(s\), \(B_{n,s}\to(s+1)/2\) and \(\nu_{n,s}\to0\); substituting into the exact norm yields \(s\). If \(s/n\to\alpha\in(0,1]\), then \(B_{n,s}/n\to\alpha/(2-\alpha)\), \(n\nu_{n,s}\to1\), and \(s\nu_{n,s}\to\alpha\), while \(s\nu_{n,s}^2\to0\). Thus \((1-\nu_{n,s})^s\to e^{-\alpha}\) and division of the norm formula by \(n\) proves the stated extensive-skeleton limit. No limiting interchange is needed for the exact finite result.

## Verification

The standalone file `check_skeleton.py` independently inverts the affine Gram matrix over rational numbers, checks the vertex reproducing kernel and projector identities, integrates the beta-distribution negative part by a separate polynomial expansion, and verifies exact benchmarks and asymptotic consistency. Execute `python3 check_skeleton.py` using only the Python standard library. The checker passes 4,563 assertions. These finite tests supplement but do not prove the quantified exact formula; that follows from the preceding argument.

## Relationship to prior work

The classical theory of orthogonal polynomials on simplex faces, including Aktaş and Xu's singular-weight Sobolev constructions, motivates comparison with lower-dimensional face measures, but their Sobolev derivative inner products and polynomial bases do not give the sharp \(L^\infty\) norm of the plain face-balanced skeletal \(L^2\) projector. Cohen, Davenport and Leviatan analyze stability of randomly sampled least squares by Gram-matrix concentration rather than the present deterministic exact endpoint norm. Chernov studies trace estimates for volume-defined polynomial \(L^2\) projections; only the accessible abstract was available for this comparison.

The volume case \(s=n\) is a known sharp affine-simplex projection constant and is included only as a boundary consistency case, not claimed as a new special-case discovery. Likewise, \(s=1\) is ordinary vertex interpolation. The substantive statement is the entire intermediate face-skeleton profile \(2\le s\le n-1\), including its sharp norms and both dimension-dependent limits. Prior statements about a simplex skeleton's Minkowski-convexification threshold concern a different operation and neither imply nor are implied by the present projector bounds.

## Limitations

No claim of novelty is made for the full-volume endpoint or the elementary vertex endpoint. The exact formulas are for affine functions, face-balanced measures and true \(L^2\) orthogonal projection; they do not address arbitrary unequal face weights, polynomial degree higher than one, non-simplicial cell complexes, or stability of finite random samples. Broader or unindexed literature could contain an equivalent skeleton projection calculation. The Chernov source was compared at the level of its accessible abstract, not treated as a fully inspected proof. No independent external proof or priority certification is claimed.
