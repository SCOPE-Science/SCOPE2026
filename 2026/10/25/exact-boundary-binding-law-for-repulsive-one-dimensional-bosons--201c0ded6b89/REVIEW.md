# Review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The chamber wavefunction satisfies the free equation with energy
\(E_N=-\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(\kappa-jg)^2\), the attractive Robin boundary
condition, and the contact jump for coefficient \(2g\). The gap-coordinate
Jacobian is one and the integral factorizes; the stated normalization and
normalizability condition follow exactly. Its eigenvalue lies below the one-free-particle cluster threshold by the positive
gap \(\Delta_N\); positivity improvement then identifies it as the ground state.

## Originality

**PASS, best-knowledge assessment.** published-finding corpus semantic searches for the exact
Robin-boundary cluster formula and its aliases returned related but non-equivalent
results. The closest primary source, Caudrelier–Crampe (2005 preprint / 2007 journal
article), supplies general impurity Bethe equations and one-/two-particle examples;
the material inspected does not explicitly give this closed \(N\)-particle state,
its threshold energy sum, or the outer-gap asymptotic. Gaudin's hard-wall box and
the later attractive-bulk boundary-string paper have different boundary/bulk
assumptions. The general Bethe equations may encode the same state implicitly, so
originality is a best-knowledge conclusion rather than a uniqueness certificate.

## Value

**PASS.** The explicit state answers a natural boundary-binding question for a
repulsive gas: how much attraction is needed for a fully localized \(N\)-particle
cluster, and how does the final particle unbind? The exact separation energy
\(\Delta_N=(\kappa-(N-1)g)^2\) and inverse-linear mean-gap divergence provide a
sharp threshold law useful for distinguishing a normalizable cluster from a state
at the unbinding boundary.

## Closest literature and limitations

- Caudrelier and Crampe, arXiv:cond-mat/0501110; DOI 10.1142/S0129055X07002973.
- Gaudin, *Boundary Energy of a Bose Gas in One Dimension* (1971), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.4.386.
- De Nardis et al., arXiv:1911.06133; its boundary strings concern attractive bulk coupling.

The result is restricted to the continuum half-line and one attractive Robin wall.
It does not classify every excited state or prove that no other bound-state family
exists below the explicit state's threshold.
