# Exact catenary degrees and shortest trades in generalized repunit numerical semigroups

## Finding
For integers \(n,b\ge2\) and \(a\ge1\) satisfying \(\gcd(a,R_n)=1\), define
\[
R_j=1+b+\cdots+b^{j-1}=\frac{b^j-1}{b-1}\quad(j\ge1),\qquad R_0=0,
\]
and let \(S=\langle g_1,\ldots,g_n\rangle\) with \(g_i=R_n+aR_{i-1}\). The exact ordinary catenary degree is
\[
\boxed{\mathsf c(S)=a+b+1.}
\]
More precisely, among all positive multiples of the least generator \(g_1\), the smallest multiple admitting a factorization with no \(g_1\) is \((a+b+1)g_1\). It has exactly the following two factorizations:
\[
(a+b+1)g_1=g_2+b g_n.
\]
For \(n=2\), the second factorization is \((b+1)g_2\). The assertion is uniform in \(n\); in particular the catenary degree is independent of the embedding dimension once \(a,b\) are fixed.

## Assumptions and scope
The integers \(a,b,n\) have the ranges and coprimality hypothesis above. The generators are strictly increasing and form a minimal generating set, as also follows from the determinantal presentation cited below. A factorization of \(s\in S\) is a vector \(z\in\mathbb N_0^n\) satisfying \(s=\sum_i z_i g_i\); its length is \(|z|=\sum_i z_i\). For factorizations \(z,z'\), let \(u_i=\min(z_i,z'_i)\) and define
\[
d(z,z')=\max(|z-u|,|z'-u|).
\]
The ordinary catenary degree of \(s\) is the least \(N\) such that every pair of its factorizations can be joined by a chain in its factorization fiber with successive distances at most \(N\). The ordinary catenary degree of \(S\) is the supremum of these degrees over \(s\in S\). No claim is made about monotone or equal-length catenary degrees.

## Proof
**Step 1: least replacement multiple.** Suppose \(k\ge1\) and
\[
kg_1=\sum_{i=2}^n u_i g_i,\qquad u_i\in\mathbb N_0,
\]
where at least one \(u_i\) is positive. Put \(L=\sum_{i=2}^n u_i\) and \(E=\sum_{i=2}^n u_iR_{i-1}\). Since \(g_1=R_n\), comparison of the two sides yields
\[
(k-L)R_n=aE.
\]
The assumption \(\gcd(a,R_n)=1\) forces \(E=qR_n\) for an integer \(q\ge1\), and then \(k=aq+L\). Since \(E\le LR_{n-1}\) and
\[
R_n=bR_{n-1}+1>bR_{n-1},
\]
we have \(L>qb\), hence \(L\ge qb+1\). It follows that
\[
k\ge q(a+b)+1\ge a+b+1.
\]
Equality forces \(q=1\) and \(L=b+1\). Conversely, the defining repunit identity gives
\[
g_2+b g_n=(b+1)R_n+a(1+bR_{n-1})=(a+b+1)R_n.
\]

**Step 2: the critical fiber has exactly two factorizations.** A factorization of \((a+b+1)g_1\) containing \(g_1\) and some other generator would, after removing its \(g_1\) terms, contradict the strict lower bound for a smaller positive multiple from Step 1. Hence the only factorization containing \(g_1\) is the pure one. Any remaining factorization must have \(q=1\), \(L=b+1\), and
\[
\sum_{i=2}^n u_iR_{i-1}=R_n=bR_{n-1}+1.
\]
For \(n=2\) this forces \(u_2=b+1\). For \(n\ge3\), set \(U=R_{n-1}\). Relative to a sum of \(b+1\) copies of \(U\), the total deficit is \(U-1\). Any summand less than \(U\) has deficit at least
\[
U-R_{n-2}=b^{n-2}.
\]
But \(0<U-1<2b^{n-2}\) for every \(b\ge2\), \(n\ge3\). There must therefore be exactly one summand less than \(U\); its deficit is \(U-1\), so that summand is \(R_1=1\). Thus the second factorization is uniquely \(g_2+b g_n\). These two factorizations have disjoint supports and lengths \(a+b+1\) and \(b+1\), respectively. Their mutual distance is \(a+b+1\), and no intermediate factorization exists, proving
\[
\mathsf c((a+b+1)g_1)=a+b+1.
\]

**Step 3: global upper bound.** Branco, Colaço and Ojeda, Corollary 9 in arXiv:2111.03714, prove that the full toric ideal of this monoid is generated by the \(2\times2\) minors of
\[
X=\begin{pmatrix}
x_1^b&x_2^b&\cdots&x_n^b\\
x_2&x_3&\cdots&x_1^{a+1}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Each binomial minor replaces one factorization fragment by another, with length at most \(a+b+1\) on either side. Since those minors generate the toric ideal, any binomial connecting two factorizations is a finite sum of monomial multiples of these minors; equivalently, those factorizations admit a sequence of replacements within the same fiber. Removing the common monomial in each replacement, every step has distance at most \(a+b+1\). Consequently \(\mathsf c(S)\le a+b+1\). Step 2 gives the matching lower bound and completes the proof.

## Verification
Run the included standalone Python 3 script `verify.py`. It uses exact integer arithmetic to check the generator identity, the degree and weights of every stated determinantal minor, and the nonexistence of shorter pure-generator replacements for 118 admissible triples with \(2\le b\le5\), \(2\le n\le5\), and \(1\le a\le10\). For 18 small triples it enumerates all factorizations in the relevant minor-degree fibers (59 distinct fibers in all), computes bottleneck spanning-tree thresholds, and reproduces the exact catenary degree. These checks are finite; the proof above applies to all parameters without a finite bound.

## Relationship to prior work
The determinantal description of the toric ideal is **not** new; it is the crucial published input, due to Branco, Colaço and Ojeda (2021). Their paper gives generating binomials and minimal presentations but does not explicitly state the ordinary catenary-degree classification or the critical two-factorization fiber. Their later Frobenius paper treats Apéry sets, genus, Frobenius number and type. Kiers, O'Neill and Ponomarenko compute catenary degrees for numerical semigroups generated by multiplicative compound sequences, a different generative form; it does not directly imply the present parameter-wide additive repunit theorem. The novelty claimed is therefore the sharp uniform numerical invariant and the unique shortest repunit trade, **not** the determinantal presentation.

## Limitations
The classification requires \(\gcd(a,R_n)=1\), \(b,n\ge2\), and \(a\ge1\). It concerns the ordinary catenary degree, not related invariants. Novelty searches do not prove priority: a derived but unstated application of the 2021 binomial presentation, or a broader earlier theorem, could subsume the formula. The computational checks do not themselves prove the infinite statement. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
