# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The geometric support identity for each cube reduces the joint width moment to a sum of bivariate absolute-value moments on the sphere. Gaussian radial-angular factorization and the explicit folded-normal identity give the precise coefficient \(2/(d\pi)\). The mean \(\mu_d\) and same-cube variance follow from standard sphere beta integrals. The integral identity for \(g''\) establishes a lower bound \(1/12\) uniformly on the interior, and the orthogonality sum makes the first-order term vanish. Equality occurs only for flat squared entries, without asserting existence of real Hadamard matrices in every dimension. Strict negativity follows from averaging the covariance over the orthogonal group; the dimension asymptotic follows from the Taylor and gamma expansions. Numerical code separately tests direct support geometry, multi-frame inequalities, finite-dimensional mixed moments, and the asymptotic coefficient. Risks: finite floating-point diagnostics are only corroborative, not an infinite certificate; the proof itself handles all dimensions and boundary entries.

## Originality — PASS relative to evidence inspected
Banica--Collins--Schlenker (arXiv:0901.2923) and Banica--Nechita (arXiv:1211.2669) already contain the Hadamard equality criterion for entrywise orthogonal-matrix norm inequalities, so that ingredient is excluded from the novelty claim. Finch (arXiv:1110.0671) and Kabluchko--Litvak--Zaporozhets (arXiv:1511.08479) handle single-polytope width moments and Gaussian width representations; their inspected statements do not supply the two-cube same-direction cross-covariance functional. Searches for rotated-cube width cross-covariances and aliases did not identify an exact all-rotation formula, an anticorrelation law, or the paired-view variance penalty. Novel content is the geometric covariance identity, its negative Hadamard baseline, explicit quantitative defect, and the combined estimation implication. Residual risk: folded Gaussian absolute moments and matrix convexity are classical; a literature result on mixed widths or variance-reduced rotations could imply the calculation in another notation. Exact historical priority is not certified.

## Value — PASS
Single-body mean width and its variance do not determine the joint behavior of two simultaneously viewed orientations. The mixed law gives the full dependence on the relative orthogonal transformation, a constructive negative-correlation mechanism for geometric Monte Carlo integration, an equality/rigidity condition and a quantitative near-optimality bound. It also identifies precisely when a familiar flat-frame optimizer can and cannot be asserted to exist. These are natural geometric and statistical questions, not an arbitrary numerical slice or just a restatement of the classical matrix norm theorem. The variance advantage is explicit and observable already in dimension four. Scope is confined to cubes and the stated shared-Haar model.
