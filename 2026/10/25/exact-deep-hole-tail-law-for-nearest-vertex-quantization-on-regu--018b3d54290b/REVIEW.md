# Mathematical review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The Euclidean Gram identity converts nearest-vertex error into \(T-\frac12\sum z_i^2\) and proves that only the centroid maximizes it. The inner-cap homothety, independent Dirichlet facet shape, monotone quadratic inversion, and finite-radius Lagrange series prove the exact probability for every integer \(d\ge2\). Independent rational moment calculations, geometric Monte Carlo and low-dimensional quadrature confirm the formulas within finite sampling error; they are not the basis of the infinite proof.

## Originality
**PASS relative to the examined records.** Conway--Sloane (1982) treats second moments and classical lattice quantization; Geiß--Klein--Penninger--Rote (2012) proves weighted Voronoi transport optimality; Yurochkin--Guha--Sun--Nguyen (2019) treats Dirichlet simplices and inference using Voronoi centroids. None of their inspected arguments states or implies the exact endpoint law with rational higher-order coefficients. The closest indexed discrete-simplex deep-hole spectrum concerns a different discretized set, and the beta difference-body law concerns independent point pairs rather than the distance to fixed vertices. Priority is not proved merely by negative searches; additional unindexed historical work could exist.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** Fixed-vertex quantization has a natural worst-case error at the simplex centroid, yet mean square error does not quantify how much uniform mass lies close to that worst error. The theorem gives an all-dimensional exact rare-error law, computable all-order corrections, and the sharp extreme-sample scaling relevant to worst-observed quantization performance. This is more information than a routine moment or only the easily seen centroid maximum.

## Residual limitations
The law is for uniform interior points of a unit-edge regular simplex in the small-deficit regime and does not identify globally optimal sites. Independent audit: not performed. Literature priority remains provisional.
