# Exact dependence phases of elementary cellular-automaton Bernoulli images

## Finding

Let \(X=(X_i)_{i\in\mathbb Z}\) consist of independent Bernoulli\((p)\) bits with \(0<p<1\). For each of the \(256\) elementary binary cellular-automaton rules \(r\), form the one-step output
\[
Y_i=f_r(X_{i-1},X_i,X_{i+1}),\qquad
f_r(a,b,c)=\left\lfloor\frac{r}{2^{4a+2b+c}}\right\rfloor\bmod2.
\]
Here a stationary process is **one-dependent** when the output sigma-fields on any two index sets separated by at least two positions are independent; it is **two-dependent** when the same holds for separation at least three.

**Classification theorem.** Exactly \(32\) rule codes produce a one-dependent output process for *every* \(0<p<1\). Exactly \(24\) additional codes do so at \(p=1/2\), and at no other interior bias. Thus the one-dependence count is exactly \(56\) for \(p=1/2\) and \(32\) for every other \(0<p<1\). Every one of the remaining outputs is two-dependent but not one-dependent.

The \(32\) all-bias codes are

`0, 3, 12, 15, 17, 24, 34, 48, 51, 60, 63, 66, 68, 85, 102, 119, 136, 153, 170, 187, 189, 192, 195, 204, 207, 221, 231, 238, 240, 243, 252, 255`.

The \(24\) fair-only codes are

`30, 36, 45, 75, 86, 89, 90, 101, 105, 106, 120, 126, 129, 135, 149, 150, 154, 165, 166, 169, 180, 210, 219, 225`.

**Pairwise-dependence contrast.** If one tests only independence of \(Y_0\) and \(Y_2\), exactly \(42\) rule codes satisfy it at every interior bias, but seven special biases give larger counts:

| Input bias \(p\) | Rules with \(Y_0\) independent of \(Y_2\) |
|---|---:|
| \(1/2\) | 104 |
| \(1/3\), \(2/3\) | 48 each |
| \((3-\sqrt5)/2\), \((\sqrt5-1)/2\) | 56 each |
| \(1-\sqrt2/2\), \(\sqrt2/2\) | 46 each |
| Every other interior bias | 42 |

Despite these seven pairwise resonances, **only** \(p=1/2\) produces any extra genuinely one-dependent output processes. As a concrete obstruction, for Rule 22 at \(p=1/3\), \(Y_0\) and \(Y_2\) are independent, yet if \(A=\{Y_{-1}=Y_0=0\}\) and \(B=\{Y_2=Y_3=0\}\), then
\[
\Pr(A)=\Pr(B)=\frac{29}{81},\quad
\Pr(A\cap B)=\frac{97}{729},\quad
\Pr(A\cap B)-\Pr(A)\Pr(B)=\frac{32}{6561}>0.
\]
Thus vanishing second-lag pair correlations can conceal genuine longer-block dependence even within finite-radius images of independent bits.

## Assumptions and scope

The input is a bilateral, stationary product measure with each bit equal to one with probability \(p\), where \(0<p<1\). The rule convention is the usual Wolfram numbering, with neighborhood `111` corresponding to the highest rule bit. All classifications include constant and degenerate rules, and count individual rule codes rather than symmetry classes. Only **one application** of a centered radius-one local rule is studied. The fixed coordinate shift implicit in recoding the input blocks does not change the independence statements.

## Proof

**Step 1: a single shared input variable.** Write \(\mathcal A=\sigma(Y_i:i\le0)\), \(\mathcal B=\sigma(Y_i:i\ge2)\). The input bit \(X_1\) is the only source variable shared by these output half-lines, so the half-lines are conditionally independent given \(X_1\). For arbitrary cylinder events \(A\in\mathcal A\), \(B\in\mathcal B\), the law of total covariance gives the exact identity
\[
\Pr(A\cap B)-\Pr(A)\Pr(B)
=p(1-p)\bigl[\Pr(A\mid X_1=1)-\Pr(A\mid X_1=0)\bigr]
\bigl[\Pr(B\mid X_1=1)-\Pr(B\mid X_1=0)\bigr].
\]
Since \(0<p<1\), independence of the two output half-lines holds if and only if at least one half-line has a conditional law independent of \(X_1\). Stationarity extends this to every gap of at least two. Any output is automatically two-dependent because its defining input windows are disjoint once output positions are separated by three.

**Step 2: a finite-state, exact all-length test.** Use four pair states \((a,b)\in\{0,1\}^2\). Define the probability row \(\pi_{ab}=p^{a+b}(1-p)^{2-a-b}\) and, for output bit \(z\), the matrix
\[
M_z[(a,b),(b',c)]={\bf1}_{\{b'=b,\ f_r(a,b,c)=z\}}p^c(1-p)^{1-c}.
\]
For a word \(w=z_1\cdots z_m\), write \(M_w=M_{z_1}\cdots M_{z_m}\), let \(\mathbf1\) be the all-ones vector, let \(h_{ab}={\bf1}_{\{b=1\}}-p\), and let \(H\) be diagonal with \(H_{(a,b),(a,b)}={\bf1}_{\{a=1\}}-p\). The past half-line is independent of its rightmost shared input bit exactly when \(\pi M_wh=0\) for every word \(w\). The future half-line is independent of its leftmost shared bit exactly when \(\pi H M_w\mathbf1=0\) for every word \(w\).

In the four-dimensional row space generated by \(\{\pi M_w\}\), the increasing spans of words of length at most \(m\) stabilize after at most three strict increases; an equality of consecutive spans makes the span invariant under both matrices. Therefore words of lengths \(0,1,2,3\) suffice for **every** word. The dual column-space argument gives the same length-three bound for \(\{M_w\mathbf1\}\). The empty-word equations vanish automatically. Thus the infinite half-line independence problem is decided by finitely many exact polynomial identities, not by extrapolating a finite experiment.

**Step 3: exhaustive polynomial certificate.** For each rule and for each output word of lengths one, two, and three, enumerate the corresponding input words of lengths three, four, and five. If \(P_1(w)\) and \(P_0(w)\) are joint probabilities of the output word and respectively the right or left endpoint input bit, form
\[
Q_w(p)=(1-p)P_1(w)-pP_0(w).
\]
Its vanishing is exactly the conditional-law equality. Compute the monic greatest common divisor of all nonzero \(Q_w\) for each endpoint; an empty list yields the zero polynomial, expressing identically equal conditional laws. Exact rational-polynomial arithmetic for all 256 truth tables yields 18 identically zero past-endpoint gcds, 18 identically zero future-endpoint gcds, and 32 rules in their union. Every nonzero endpoint gcd has no root in \((0,1)\) except possibly \(p=1/2\); their unions at \(p=1/2\) contain 56 rule codes. The full per-rule gcds are in `classification.csv`, reproducible from `verify.py`.

**Step 4: pairwise criterion and algebraic bias spectrum.** Conditional on \(X_1\), the individual outputs \(Y_0\) and \(Y_2\) are independent. Define
\[
D_R(p)=\sum_{a,b\in\{0,1\}}p^{a+b}(1-p)^{2-a-b}[f_r(a,b,1)-f_r(a,b,0)],
\]
\[
D_L(p)=\sum_{b,c\in\{0,1\}}p^{b+c}(1-p)^{2-b-c}[f_r(1,b,c)-f_r(0,b,c)].
\]
Then \(\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_2)=p(1-p)D_R(p)D_L(p)\). Two Bernoulli variables are independent exactly when their covariance is zero. Each difference is a polynomial of degree at most two with integer coefficients. Enumerating the eight truth-table values for each of 256 rules and factoring the two polynomials gives the seven special input biases and all the listed counts. The Rule-22 block probabilities follow by direct enumeration of its seven relevant independent input bits.

## Verification

Run `python verify.py` with Python 3.10 or newer and SymPy installed. This regenerates `classification.csv` with all 256 rows. The verifier uses exact symbolic probabilities, exact polynomial greatest common divisors, and an exact finite-state stopping argument in the mathematical proof. It independently enumerates nine-bit input blocks at input biases \(1/2\), \(1/3\), \(1/4\), and \(2/3\), obtaining respectively 56, 32, 32, and 32 one-dependent rules without using the symbolic gcd classification. The Rule-22 rational witness can be independently verified by direct seven-bit enumeration. No numerical tolerance, unbounded search, or probabilistic estimate is used.

## Relationship to prior work

Wolfram's 1983 foundational article studies statistical behavior of elementary cellular automata from random initial states. Host, Maass, and Martínez (2003) analyze invariant uniform Bernoulli measures for permutative rules of algebraic origin. Kari and Taati (2013 preprint, 2015 journal) establish general relationships between surjective cellular automata, invariant Gibbs measures, and Bernoulli measures; their discussion of XOR randomization concerns temporal evolution and asymptotic behavior. Borriello and Walker (2016) classify elementary rules by empirical transfer-entropy variation. These results motivate finite-block dependence questions but do not, in the inspected portions, supply the complete one-update one-dependence classification or its sharp pairwise-versus-block distinction. In particular, preservation of the uniform Bernoulli measure by surjective rules covers some special cases and is not asserted as new here.

## Limitations

The theorem is restricted to one-step images of independent binary inputs under centered radius-one rules. It does not classify temporal iterations, entropy production, or higher-radius maps. “Not one-dependent” means a dependence witness across output blocks separated by one unobserved site, not dependence at arbitrarily large distances. Only the exact finite-state calculation plus the given proof certify the 256-rule census; the independent direct tests provide cross-checks at selected biases. Historical priority against all unpublished or unindexed tables is not established; Wolfram's broad early work and some other sources were not searched line-by-line for every conceivable equivalent tabulation. No independent scientific audit is claimed.
