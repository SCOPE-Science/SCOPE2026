# Scientific review: opposite-corner clipped cubes

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed set identity is reconstructed through exact coordinatewise difference feasibility. For any candidate difference vector the sum of feasible lower endpoints and upper endpoints of the second point is the interval \([q,d-p]\), and the two clipping constraints supply two further intervals. Their pairwise intersection conditions reduce to the candidate slab inequality plus bounds on \(p\) and \(q\) which follow automatically from the signs of the coordinates. Thus the proposed difference body is exact, including boundary points. The two independently calculated corner-cap volumes are valid for \(a,b\le1\), \(s\le2\) and \(d\ge3\). Strict convexity of \(a^d+(s-a)^d\) on the admissible interval gives all claimed sharp equality cases. The included exact rational checker passes 34,875 set-membership comparisons and 297 fixed-sum ratio comparisons. Finite checks do not establish the infinite-dimensional assertion; the detailed proof does.

## Originality — PASS under checked evidence

The closest general result is the classical Rogers–Shephard difference-body volume theory; the general Brunn–Minkowski lower bound yields the ratio \(2^d\) for symmetric bodies, but does not identify the same full difference body for a two-parameter family of differently clipped parallelotopes. Related recent Godbersen inequalities concern mixed-volume inequalities and do not, by their inspected statements, imply the exact slab identity. Available related research records include a one-vertex-truncated simplex mixed-volume profile, a cube–octahedron Mahler product, and a one-corner-capped cube John ellipsoid: their objects or invariants differ, and no observed claim dominates the present set identity and strict sharp fixed-fiber volume law. A similar earlier claim using different terminology might exist. Inaccessible primary full texts mean historical priority is provisional rather than independently established.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The difference-body map is a fundamental noninjective symmetrization; an exact all-dimensional fiber in a canonical class of convex polytopes provides more structural information than a volume inequality. Distinct asymmetric parallelotope corner cuts have identical difference bodies but variable ambient volumes. The sharp and rigid monotonicity quantifies precisely how much information about volume is discarded, with the affine-invariant Rogers–Shephard ratio ranging from its central-symmetry value to a new explicit upper endpoint within the prescribed fiber. The statement is not a general classification of all inverse images of the difference-body map.
