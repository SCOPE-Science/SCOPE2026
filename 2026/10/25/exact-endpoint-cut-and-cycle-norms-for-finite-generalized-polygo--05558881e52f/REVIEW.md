# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof works for every edge of every finite connected biregular girth-\(2m\), diameter-\(m\) graph. Girth gives unique geodesics shorter than \(m\); the two-sided distance partition is equitable and has an exactly specified cycle quotient. A dipole potential lies in this invariant subspace, so its edge currents are those of a direct unit edge in parallel with an explicit series chain of edge bundles. Summation of all absolute current magnitudes proves both projection formulas. Boundary cases at \(m\ge3\), positive degrees and equal-order \(q=2,3\) are checked algebraically. Eight actual graph families, 312 exact columns, and 70 geometric-sum checks agree with the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with literature uncertainty
The closest directly relevant primary source is Dilworth–Kutzarova–Ostrovskii (2023), which computes endpoint norms for Hamming cubes and investigates tori and Hamming graphs; its inspected text contains no generalized-polygon incidence-graph norm formula. The 2018 finite Lipschitz-free-space paper supplies general cycle-space machinery but studies different graph families. De Wispelaere–Van Maldeghem (2006) study regular partitions of generalized polygons, and Colangelo–Monzillo–Siciliano (2024) compute flag association schemes. Their geometry is related, but the exact absolute-current sums and norm crossover are not their stated results. Public mathematical research-index searches did not identify a directly covering result. This is not a proof of unique precedence: equivalent electrical-network calculations in less-indexed literature remain a risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result is a complete parameter-only classification of two natural Banach operator invariants simultaneously for finite thick projective planes, generalized quadrangles, hexagons and octagons. The proof does not require edge-transitivity or a group action: a local geodesic property alone enforces identical absolute column norms. The universal order-two/order-three reversal in the relative size of the projections is a substantive structural consequence, and the exact formulas furnish sharp equal-order asymptotic limits wherever the parameter families exist.

## Scope and residual risks
The result does not minimize over arbitrary projections and does not cover weighted edges or other \(\ell_p\) norms. Existing association schemes and equitable partitions could offer an alternate derivation; a previously published explicit current-sum formula may have escaped the searched sources. The unrestricted part is proved analytically; finite checks are corroboration only. No independent audit is represented.
