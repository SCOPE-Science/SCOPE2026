# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** From the exact multiplication law, idempotency yields the common quadratic \(x_i^2+x_i=S\), proving a two-coordinate-value classification. Substitution produces a single polynomial indexed by the support size. The odd-characteristic root count, singular linear reduction, coincident-root correction, and characteristic-two trace criterion produce all three formulas. The fixed set of eighteen exhaustive tests uses an independent direct multiplication implementation over finite fields; every result agreed. Finite tests are supportive, not proofs of general quantification. The proof covers every prime power and every integer dimension \(n\ge3\), including the boundary cases where \(p\mid n-1\) or \(p\mid n+1\).

**Originality — PASS (qualified).** The recent evolution-algebra survey (arXiv:2610.22248v1, Theorem 17 and Remark 23) establishes geometric scheme-length bounds and an average over all invertible structure matrices, respectively, not exact finite-field idempotent counts for the fixed complete-graph adjacency matrix; the singular adjacency case cannot follow from a theorem limited to perfect algebras. The fully inspected 2025 paper arXiv:2512.12418 treats complex existence and subalgebra completeness, rather than a finite-field complete-graph count. Targeted full-text, bibliographic, and indexed mathematical searches found no equivalent closed character-sum formula or the extension-degree parity law. The absence of an indexed match does not rule out unindexed or differently framed work. The graph convention and classical quadratic-root count are not claimed as original.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Idempotents are algebraic fixed points relevant to evolution dynamics and a natural structural invariant. This gives the entire idempotent set for a canonical maximally symmetric graph family, including explicit dependence on finite-field extension and nonperfect singular reductions. The sharp characteristic-two transition shows that the count changes with extension-degree parity at fixed characteristic, as seen for field orders \(2,4,8\). This is a full natural-family classification, not a finite census or a generic textbook quadratic alone.
