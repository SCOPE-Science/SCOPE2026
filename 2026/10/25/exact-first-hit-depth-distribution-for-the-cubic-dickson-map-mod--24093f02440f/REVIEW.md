# Mathematical review

## Correctness: PASS
The two factorizations \(f(x)=x(x^2-3)\) and \(f(x)-\rho=(x-\rho)(x+\rho/2)^2\) give exact valuation increments of one versus two in their respective basins. The proof includes the initial exceptional step, the fixed and critical roots, precision truncation, the \(k=1\) endpoint, and an exhaustive congruence partition of each nonzero basin. Independent explicit orbit iteration for all 9,840 residues at precisions one through eight agrees with the pointwise expressions and the histogram. These tests support but do not replace the proof.

## Originality: PASS (qualified)
The 2012 finite-field graph counts in Gassert explicitly exclude characteristic equal to degree, and so do not cover this characteristic-three ring problem. The Diarra–Sylla 2013 p-adic basin theorem identifies the limiting fixed point, but not first-hit times in finite quotients. Panraksa–Tangboonduangjit prove the periodic-point classification for the Chebyshev–Fermat case (three fixed points here), which is used as context, not claimed as new; their result does not imply the complete nonperiodic depth histogram. The 2026 ternary-ring permutation case excludes a nonpermutation degree divisible by three. Precise equivalent formulations under the monic Dickson/Chebyshev conjugacy and broader potential coverage were searched; an unlocated specialization in arithmetic-dynamics literature remains a residual risk.

## Value: PASS
The known basins and cycles alone do not prescribe when each initial residue first reaches the fixed point. The result provides a uniform exact first-hit classification and a complete distribution at every modulus, while revealing a structural, sharp branch-depth gap arising from a double critical factor at the two nonzero fixed points. This is a natural graph invariant, not a selected numerical example or a routine finite table.

## Limitations
Only the cubic Dickson map and powers of three are covered. Absolute bibliographic uniqueness and independent validation are not established.
