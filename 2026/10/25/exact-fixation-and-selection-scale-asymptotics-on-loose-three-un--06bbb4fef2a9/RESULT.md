# Exact fixation and selection-scale asymptotics on loose three-uniform hyperstars

## Finding

Let \(m\ge2\) be an integer. A *loose three-uniform hyperstar* has a hub \(o\) and \(m\) disjoint pairs of leaves, with exactly the \(m\) hyperedges consisting of \(o\) and each individual leaf pair. Under fitness-proportional parent selection followed by uniform choice among the parent's incident hyperedges and simultaneous copying of that parent's type to the whole chosen hyperedge, residents have fitness \(1\) and mutants have fitness \(r>0\). Write
\[
a=\frac{r}{r+2m},\qquad b=\frac{2mr}{2mr+1},\qquad c=\frac{mr}{m(r+1)+1},\qquad q=\frac{2m+r}{r(2mr+1)},
\]
\[
H=\left(q^{m-1}+b\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}q^j\right)^{-1}.
\]
For a unique initial mutant at the hub, fixation probability is exactly \(aH\); for a unique initial mutant at any leaf, it is exactly \(cH\). Thus a uniformly located single mutant fixates with probability
\[
\boxed{\rho_m(r)=\frac{a+2mc}{2m+1}\left(q^{m-1}+b\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}q^j\right)^{-1}.}
\]
These expressions hold at neutrality \(r=1\) without removable-singularity conventions. In particular, \(\rho_m(1)=1/(2m+1)\), while the individual hub and leaf probabilities are \(1/(2m^2+1)\) and \(m/(2m^2+1)\), respectively.

For each *fixed* \(r>1\), as \(m\to\infty\),
\[
\rho_m(r)=1-\frac1r-\frac{(r-1)(r+3)}{2r(r+1)m}+O_r(m^{-2}).
\]
For each *fixed* \(0<r<1\),
\[
\rho_m(r)\sim(1-r)r^{2m-1}\exp\!\left(\frac{r^{-1}-r}{2}\right),
\qquad
\frac{\rho_m(r)}{\rho_M(2m+1,r)}\longrightarrow r^{-1}\exp\!\left(\frac{r^{-1}-r}{2}\right)>1,
\]
where \(\rho_M(N,r)=(1-r^{-1})/(1-r^{-N})\) is Moran fixation for \(r\ne1\). Hence the loose hyperstar suppresses selection relative to the Moran benchmark for all sufficiently large \(m\) at each fixed nonneutral fitness. The statement is pointwise in \(r\), **not uniform near \(r=1\)**.

At the first nontrivial size \(m=2\), it is a strict suppressor for *every* \(r\ne1\):
\[
\rho_M(5,r)-\rho_2(r)=
\frac{r^2(r-1)(8r^4+30r^3+41r^2+44r+7)}{(r+4)(2r+3)(4r^2+r+4)(r^4+r^3+r^2+r+1)}.
\]
The numerator's remaining polynomial has strictly positive coefficients, making the direction of the comparison explicit on both sides of neutrality.

## Assumptions and scope

The hypergraph is finite, undirected, connected, unweighted, simple and exactly the above \(m\)-petal loose hyperstar; \(m\ge2\) and \(r\in(0,\infty)\). Its vertex count is \(2m+1\). At each discrete step exactly one parent is chosen proportionally to fitness; the parent picks an incident hyperedge uniformly, and **all vertices of that hyperedge** adopt its type simultaneously. There is no mutation, immigration, majority qualification, or preferential hyperedge sampling. The initial mutant is unique and either placed at the hub or a specified leaf; the uniform result averages these \(2m+1\) initial locations. Fixation means eventual all-mutant absorption. A finite positive fitness ensures that from every nonabsorbing configuration an absorbing consensus is reachable in finitely many steps with positive probability; finiteness yields almost-sure eventual absorption.

The word *loose* matters: different hyperedges meet only at the hub. The dense three-uniform star analyzed in the motivating paper instead includes **every** pair of leaves with the hub as a hyperedge; it is a different hypergraph for \(m\ge2\).

## Proof

**1. Permanent petal synchrony.** Whenever an edge is updated, its two leaves become the parent's type simultaneously. Thereafter those two leaves always have the same type, since their unique incident hyperedge is updated as a whole and no other edge touches them. In the hub-mutant singleton initial state all petals are already synchronized. In a leaf-mutant singleton initial state just one petal is unsynchronized. Before its first type-changing update, all other vertices are residents. The competing event rates on that petal are mutant-leaf reproduction \(r\), resident-leaf reproduction \(1\), and resident-hub reproduction with that edge selected \(1/m\), after canceling the common fitness normalization. The first outcome leads to the synchronized configuration with mutant hub and exactly one mutant petal; the other two lead to resident consensus. Thus its success probability before any further evolution is exactly \(c=r/(r+1+1/m)\). Self-loops generated by residents acting on other petals do not alter these conditional probabilities.

**2. Embedded synchronized dynamics.** Write \(u_k\) for mutant fixation from a resident hub and \(k\) all-mutant petals, and \(v_k\) for fixation from a mutant hub and \(k\) all-mutant petals. At \(1\le k\le m\), the only possible nontrivial changes from \((0,k)\) are an active mutant petal converting the hub (total unnormalized rate \(2rk\)) or the resident hub converting one mutant petal (rate \(k/m\)). Conditioning on a change gives
\[
u_k=bv_k+(1-b)u_{k-1},\qquad b=\frac{2mr}{2mr+1}.
\]
At \(0\le k<m\), the mutant hub converts one resident petal at rate \(r(m-k)/m\), or a resident petal converts the hub at rate \(2(m-k)\). Consequently
\[
v_k=av_{k+1}+(1-a)u_k,\qquad a=\frac r{r+2m}.
\]
The boundary conditions are \(u_0=0\), \(v_m=1\). These recurrences use conditional transition *rates* so state-dependent fitness normalizers cancel, but do **not** presume the total mutant count is a Markov process.

**3. Solve the harmonic equations.** Eliminating \(v_k\) gives
\[
a(u_{k+1}-u_k)=(1-b)(u_k-u_{k-1}),\qquad 1\le k\le m-1.
\]
The increments form a geometric progression with ratio \(q=(1-b)/a\). Thus
\[
u_k=u_1\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}q^j,\qquad
v_1=\frac{u_1}{b}.
\]
The boundary \(v_m=1\) then yields
\[
v_1=\left[q^{m-1}+b\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}q^j\right]^{-1}=H.
\]
Also \(v_0=av_1=aH\), and the one-leaf initial value from Step 1 is \(cv_1=cH\). Averaging exactly one hub location and \(2m\) leaves proves the formula. The positive finite denominator, unique boundary conditions and almost-sure absorption validate the harmonic solution.

**4. Neutrality and nonneutral simplification.** At \(r=1\), \(q=1\), \(b=2m/(2m+1)\); direct substitution gives \(H=(2m+1)/(2m^2+1)\), proving the stated neutral probabilities. For \(r\ne1\), direct algebra gives the useful alternative
\[
H=\frac{1-r^{-2}}{1-r^{-2}q^{m-1}}.
\]
No equality of numerator and denominator zeros is invoked for the neutral case.

**5. Large populations.** For fixed \(r\ne1\),
\[
q=r^{-2}\left(1+\frac{r-r^{-1}}{2m}+O_r(m^{-2})\right),\qquad
\frac{a+2mc}{2m+1}=\frac r{1+r}+O_r(m^{-1}).
\]
For \(r>1\), \(q^{m-1}\) is exponentially small, and expanding the rational prefactor to first order gives the stated \(1/m\) deficit. For \(r<1\), \(q^{m-1}\) diverges exponentially and
\[
q^{-(m-1)}=r^{2m-2}\exp\!\left(\frac{r^{-1}-r}{2}+O_r(m^{-1})\right).
\]
Substitution in the exact formula proves the disadvantaged-mutant equivalent. The Moran asymptotics \(\rho_M(2m+1,r)\sim(1-r)r^{2m}\) then yield the explicit ratio. The sign of the leading deficit (\(r>1\)) and the limiting ratio above one (\(r<1\)) establish eventual suppression in both fixed-fitness regimes.

**6. Exact finite benchmark.** For \(m=2\), simplifying the finite sum gives
\[
\rho_2(r)=\frac{r^2(2r+7)(4r+1)}{(r+4)(2r+3)(4r^2+r+4)}.
\]
Subtracting from \(\rho_M(5,r)=r^4/(1+r+r^2+r^3+r^4)\) and clearing positive denominators gives the displayed factorization. Every factor other than \(r-1\) is strictly positive when \(r>0\), so the sign is exactly that of \(r-1\).

## Verification

The included standalone `verify.py` independently constructs the full labeled group-overwrite Markov transition matrix and solves its absorption equations using exact rational arithmetic for \(m=1,2\) and multiple positive rational fitnesses, verifying the hub, leaf, and uniformly averaged formulas. It also checks 48 exact-rational harmonic cases for \(1\le m\le8\), including neutrality, and verifies the all-variable finite suppression factorization by exact integer-polynomial convolution. Numerical large-\(m\) comparisons support the independent asymptotic derivation but are not used as an infinite proof. Run `python verify.py` in a Python 3 environment; no third-party packages are needed.

## Relationship to prior work

Liu and Masuda (2023, arXiv:2301.05343, *Fixation dynamics on hypergraphs*) define precisely the fitness-proportional parent/whole-hyperedge overwrite rule used here, and analyze complete and cyclic three-uniform hypergraphs and a **dense** three-uniform star containing every hub–leaf-pair hyperedge (their Sections 2, 3.1 and Supplementary Text G/H). Their explicit small-order formulas and numerical large-order results are credited. The loose hyperstar has only \(m\) nonoverlapping petals instead of their \(\binom{2m}{2}\) edges, so their two-coordinate dense-star reduction does not imply the formula here. The exact formula and fixed-fitness two-sided asymptotics are the contribution assessed here.

Keller and Uğurlu (2024, arXiv:2403.12598, *Fixation probability in Moran-like Processes on graphs*) treat vertex-to-single-neighbor replacement on weighted graphs and martingale fixation conditions, not three-node simultaneous replacement of an entire hyperedge. Classical Moran fixation is used solely as a benchmark; none of these established general results is claimed as new.

## Limitations

The all-parameter finite formula applies only to loose three-uniform hyperstars and model 1 whole-hyperedge overwriting. Eventual suppression is proved *pointwise at each fixed nonneutral fitness*; no uniform-in-selection large-population assertion or all-\(m\) suppressor theorem is claimed. The finite five-vertex all-fitness comparison is exact, not an exhaustive classification of all hypergraphs. No result is asserted for majority-qualified updates, variable-size petals, other graph structures, alternative seeding distributions, or stochastic fitness. Published-priority searches and accessible texts cannot certify absolute originality. Independent scientific audit has not been performed.
