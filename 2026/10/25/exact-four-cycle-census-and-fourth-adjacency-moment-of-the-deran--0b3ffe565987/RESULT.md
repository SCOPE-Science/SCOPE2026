# Exact four-cycle census and fourth adjacency moment of the derangement-switch graph

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge4\), let \(G_n\) have the derangements of \([n]\) as vertices. Two vertices are adjacent exactly when one results from swapping values at two positions of the other, with no fixed points created. Write \(Q_3(n)\) for simple four-cycles whose four edge swaps collectively move exactly three positions and \(Q_4(n)\) for those involving four positions. Every simple four-cycle has exactly one of these two types.

For integer \(k\ge0\), set \(D_k=\sum_{j=0}^k(-1)^j\binom kj(k-j)!\), with \(D_0=1\). Define the truncated inclusion-exclusion sums
\[
B_0(n)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-6}(-1)^j\binom{n-6}j(n-3-j)!,&n\ge6,\\0,&n=4,5,\end{cases}
\]
\[
B_1(n)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-5}(-1)^j\binom{n-5}j(n-3-j)!,&n\ge5,\\0,&n=4.\end{cases}
\]
Then the **exact complete four-cycle census** is
\[
\boxed{Q_3(n)=\binom n3\left[9\binom{n-3}3B_0(n)+3\binom{n-3}2B_1(n)\right],}
\]
\[
\boxed{Q_4(n)=\frac{3}{4}\binom n4\sum_{i=0}^{4}\sum_{j=0}^{n-4}(-1)^{i+j}a_i\binom{n-4}{j}(n-i-j)!},\qquad(a_0,\ldots,a_4)=(1,8,20,16,4).
\]
All binomial coefficients with impossible lower arguments are zero. Thus \(Q_3(n)+Q_4(n)\) is the number of **unoriented, unrooted simple four-cycles**, counted once each, in \(G_n\).

Let \(A_n\) be its adjacency matrix (not the rook sum above), put \(b=\binom n2-n\) and \(m=\frac12[bD_n+\binom n2D_{n-2}]\). The theorem also supplies the exact **fourth closed-walk spectral moment**
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{tr}(A_n^4)=2m+4\left[D_n\binom b2+b\binom n2D_{n-2}+3\binom n4D_{n-4}\right]+8(Q_3(n)+Q_4(n)).}
\]
For \(n=4,5,6,7\), the respective pairs \((Q_3,Q_4)\) are \((0,3),(60,60),(1800,1305),(31500,24150)\). For \(n=8\), the pair is \((500640,426930)\).

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is simple and undirected; values and positions are labeled, and a move is an arbitrary transposition of two positions, not only adjacent positions. A simple four-cycle consists of four distinct vertices and four distinct edges; its edges need not be induced in a larger ambient graph. Here every four-cycle in fact lies in a bipartite graph, so its two diagonals are absent. The theorem holds for **all** integers \(n\ge4\), without asymptotic assumptions. The same state graph is the usual four-edge switch graph on perfect matchings of the crown graph \(K_{n,n}\) with one diagonal perfect matching deleted.

## Proof
**Step 1: supports of four-cycles.** Work in the full permutation graph generated by transpositions of positions. Opposite corners of any four-cycle differ by a product of two *different* transpositions, either a three-cycle if the transpositions overlap or a product of disjoint transpositions otherwise. A three-cycle has three two-transposition factorizations and the resulting local full graph on the corresponding six permutations is \(K_{3,3}\); disjoint transpositions commute and produce a unique four-vertex square from each initial permutation. Consequently the support of a four-cycle is either a unique three-position set or a unique partition of four positions into two unordered pairs. Restricting to derangements deletes vertices and never creates additional four-cycles.

**Step 2: three-position squares.** Fix a three-element position set \(T\) and an image set \(S\) of size three. Also fix the bijection from the remaining \(n-3\) positions onto \([n]\setminus S\), subject to avoidance of fixed points outside \(T\). The six possible bijections \(T\to S\) span the full \(K_{3,3}\). Put \(q=|T\cap S|\). Exactly \(2q\) of the six have a fixed point in \(T\) when \(q\le1\), so for \(q=0\) all six survive and contribute nine distinct four-cycles; for \(q=1\), exactly four survive (two of each parity), forming one \(K_{2,2}\), hence one four-cycle. For \(q\ge2\), at most three survive: if \(q=2\), the two forbidden equalities each exclude two permutations and have one common member, leaving three; if \(q=3\), only the two three-cycles survive. Thus there are no further four-cycles.

When \(q=0\), there are \(\binom{n-3}3\) choices of \(S\), and the outside bijection has \(n-6\) forbidden diagonal cells, producing \(B_0(n)\) by inclusion-exclusion. When \(q=1\), there are \(3\binom{n-3}2\) choices of \(S\), with \(n-5\) outside forbidden diagonal cells, producing \(B_1(n)\). Summing nine or one squares respectively and multiplying by \(\binom n3\) choices of \(T\) proves the formula for \(Q_3(n)\), without double counting: support and outside restriction are uniquely determined by any four-cycle.

**Step 3: four-position squares.** Fix an unordered pair of disjoint position transpositions, using four positions. The four corners generated from a permutation are all derangements if and only if the permutation avoids the ordinary diagonal cells \((i,i)\) for all \(i\), *plus* the directed cross cells \((a,b),(b,a)\) for each of the two pairs. Each pair contributes a forbidden \(2\times2\) board with rook polynomial \(1+4x+2x^2\); the \(n-4\) untouched positions contribute \((1+x)^{n-4}\). The forbidden-board rook polynomial is therefore
\[
(1+4x+2x^2)^2(1+x)^{n-4}=(1+8x+20x^2+16x^3+4x^4)(1+x)^{n-4}.
\]
Rook inclusion-exclusion yields the double sum in the boxed formula as the number of valid starting permutations for these two disjoint transpositions. There are \(3\binom n4\) choices of the two pairs. Each square is counted from its four possible starting corners, so division by four proves the \(Q_4(n)\) expression. Different pair partitions cannot encode the same square because its two commuting directions are unique.

**Step 4: fourth moment.** A transposition at positions \(i,j\) is forbidden at a derangement precisely if \(\pi(i)=j\) or \(\pi(j)=i\). These two failures coincide exactly when \(i,j\) form a two-cycle of \(\pi\). Consequently
\[\deg(\pi)=\binom n2-n+c_2(\pi)=b+c_2(\pi),\]
where \(c_2\) counts permutation two-cycles; the degree formula is already implicit in the earlier study of this graph and is used here as input. Counting designated disjoint two-cycles gives
\[\sum_{\pi\in D_n}c_2(\pi)=\binom n2D_{n-2},\qquad\sum_{\pi\in D_n}\binom{c_2(\pi)}2=3\binom n4D_{n-4}.\]
Thus the handshaking identity gives \(m\), and the total number of unordered pairs of incident edges is
\[\sum_{\pi\in D_n}\binom{\deg\pi}2=D_n\binom b2+b\binom n2D_{n-2}+3\binom n4D_{n-4}.\]
Every length-four closed walk in a simple graph is either an edge repeated four times (two per edge), a two-edge backtrack (four per unordered pair of adjacent edges), or a traversal of a simple four-cycle (eight per such cycle). This gives the boxed trace identity and completes the proof.

## Verification
Two runnable **Python 3, standard-library-only** programs accompany this result. `verify_formula.py` evaluates all closed formulas for \(4\le n\le10\); `verify_exhaustive.py` independently constructs the derangement graph from permutations, enumerates every unordered four-cycle, and classifies its support and independently computes closed-four-walk traces for \(4\le n\le7\). The emitted standard output is embedded as `formula_output.txt` and `exhaustive_output.txt`, respectively; both programs have been executed. For instance, the independent exact enumeration gives \(9\) derangements and \(3\) four-cycles at \(n=4\), and \(1854\) derangements with \(31500\) three-support and \(24150\) four-support squares at \(n=7\). The general result is proved algebraically; these finite computations test the combinatorial reductions and boundary values, not an extrapolation to infinity.

## Relationship to prior work
Jain, *Transpositions in Derangements* (2021), studies precisely this graph (denoted \(\alpha(n)\)): connectivity, degree bounds, spanning walks and connectivity proofs. The latter explicitly construct some three- and four-position alternative routes; **their existence and the underlying graph are prior work**. The present statement adds the complete numerical census of every two-step ambiguity square, resolved into the two uniquely defined support classes, and its fourth adjacency moment. Baril--Vajnovszki (2004) concern Gray-code generation of derangements, not this quadrilateral enumeration. Dyer--Jerrum--Müller (2015, initially available 2015-01-30) study the general switch chain for bipartite perfect matchings; the crown graph is a special state space, but their mixing analysis does not imply these exact cycle or spectral formulas. The exact parameter of a general graph alone is not sufficient to imply the present result: the formulas require restricted rook-board counts. These observations support best-knowledge priority, not a proof that no equivalent result exists elsewhere.

## Limitations
No independence, external audit or publication-priority certification is claimed. A prior published calculation of the same quadrilateral census, possibly under terminology such as interchange graph, switch chain, derangement Cayley subgraph, or alternating four-cycles, would subsume this result. No claim is made about higher adjacency moments, spectral gaps, mixing times or complete spectra. The derivation assumes distinct labeled positions and standard simple four-cycle counting, not quotient classes or multi-switch edges.
