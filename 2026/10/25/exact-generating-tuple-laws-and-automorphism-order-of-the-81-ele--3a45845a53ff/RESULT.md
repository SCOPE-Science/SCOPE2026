# Exact generating-tuple laws and automorphism order of the 81-element free commutative kei

## Finding

Let \(Q\) be the nonmedial commutative kei \(D(1)\) of order \(81\), equivalently the free commutative kei \(\overline Q(4,3)\) on four generators. In the explicit model and quotient projection \(\pi:Q\to\mathbb F_3^3\) defined below, an ordered \(k\)-tuple generates \(Q\) **if and only if** its \(k\) projected points affinely span \(\mathbb F_3^3\), for every integer \(k\ge1\).

Consequently, the number of ordered generating \(k\)-tuples is zero for \(k<4\); for every \(k\ge4\) it equals

\[
G_k=3^{k+3}(3^{k-1}-1)(3^{k-1}-3)(3^{k-1}-9).
\]

For \(k\) independent uniformly chosen elements, the exact probability of generation is

\[
\Pr(\langle x_1,\ldots,x_k\rangle=Q)
=\prod_{j=0}^{2}\left(1-3^{j-(k-1)}\right) \quad(k\ge4).
\]

In particular, the number of generating ordered quadruples is \(24\,564\,384\) and their probability is \(416/729\). Since \(Q\) is free on four generators, each generating quadruple is the image of the standard free generating quadruple under a unique automorphism. Thus

\[
|\operatorname{Aut}(Q)|=24\,564\,384=3^7\,|\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb F_3)|.
\]

The previously known four-generator freeness, 81-element order and nonmediality are not claimed as new; the contribution is the **complete generating-tuple criterion and distribution**, including the resulting automorphism-group order.

## Assumptions and scope

All operations below are over \(\mathbb F_3\). Put \(W=\mathbb F_3^3\) and write elements of \(Q=\mathbb F_3\times W\) as \((s,u)\), where \(u=(u_1,u_2,u_3)\). Let

\[
\omega(u,v)=u_1v_2-u_2v_1,\qquad
h(u,v)=-\omega(u,v)(u_3-v_3),
\]

and define the binary operation

\[
(s,u)\star(t,v)=\bigl(-s-t+h(u,v),\;-u-v\bigr).
\]

This is the classical \(D(1)\) construction: with \(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4\) the standard basis of \(\mathbb F_3^4\), the trilinear map with \(f(e_2,e_3,e_4)=e_1\), \(f(e_3,e_2,e_4)=-e_1\), and other basis values zero gives \(x\cdot y=x+y+f(x,y,x-y)\); the displayed operation is \(x\star y=(-x)\cdot(-y)\). The projection \(\pi(s,u)=u\) maps onto the affine Steiner kei \((W,u\diamond v=-u-v)\). A generated subquandle means closure under \(\star\); no generators outside the given tuple are adjoined.

## Proof

**1. Quandle structure and a diagnostic for fibers.** The definition has \(h(u,v)=h(v,u)\), \(h(u,u)=0\), and \(h(-u,-v)=-h(u,v)\). The 81-element operation is commutative and idempotent, each right translation is involutive, and the operation is right self-distributive. These finite statements can be checked directly from the displayed polynomial (or by the complete \(81^3\)-instance verification specified below). They also follow from the established \(D(1)\) construction [2,4].

For four elements \(a,b,c,d\in Q\), write their projections as \(A,B,C,D\in W\). The two expressions

\[
P=(a\star b)\star(c\star d),\qquad
R=(a\star c)\star(b\star d)
\]

have the same projection \(A+B+C+D\). Direct expansion of the first coordinates using the definition of \(h\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_0-R_0
={}&-h(A,B)-h(C,D)-h(A+B,C+D)\\
&+h(A,C)+h(B,D)+h(A+C,B+D)\\
={}&\det(B-A,C-A,D-A)\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_3.
\end{aligned}
\]

The last equality is a polynomial identity in twelve variables. The accompanying checker independently exhausts all \(27^4=531\,441\) projected quadruples, in addition to its straightforward coordinate expansion. Thus every affinely independent projected quadruple produces **two different elements of the generated subquandle in the same \(\pi\)-fiber**.

**2. Projection of a generated subquandle.** If \(T\subseteq W\) is a nonempty subquandle of \((W,\diamond)\), fix \(a\in T\). The closure identities

\[
a\diamond(a+v)=a-v,
\qquad
a\diamond((a+v)\diamond(a+w))=a+v+w
\]

show that \(T-a\) is closed under additive inverses and sums. Thus \(T\) is an affine \(\mathbb F_3\)-subspace. Conversely, each affine subspace is \(\diamond\)-closed. Hence the projection of the subquandle generated by \(x_1,\ldots,x_k\) is exactly the affine span of \(\pi(x_1),\ldots,\pi(x_k)\).

**3. Fiber propagation.** Suppose the projections span \(W\). Four of them form an affine basis. Step 1 gives two different elements \(p,q\) of their generated subquandle with the same projection \(u\). Since the fiber \(\pi^{-1}(u)\) has three elements, the three points \(p,q,p\star q\) exhaust it: if \(p=(s,u)\) and \(q=(t,u)\), then \(p\star q=(-s-t,u)\), and for \(s\ne t\) this is the third element of \(\mathbb F_3\).

For any \(w\in W\), surjectivity of the projected subquandle supplies \(y\) with \(\pi(y)=-u-w\). Multiplying all three elements of \(\pi^{-1}(u)\) by \(y\) gives three distinct elements over \(w\), since their first coordinates each have coefficient \(-1\) in the product. The generated subquandle therefore contains every fiber, hence all \(81\) elements of \(Q\). Conversely, a generating tuple must project onto \(W\), so its projections must affinely span \(W\). This proves the criterion for all \(k\).

**4. Enumeration.** Choose \(\pi(x_1)\) arbitrarily in \(W\) (\(3^3\) options). The remaining \(k-1\) differences form a \(3\)-by-\((k-1)\) matrix with rank three, counted by

\[
(3^{k-1}-1)(3^{k-1}-3)(3^{k-1}-9)
\]

when \(k\ge4\), and by zero otherwise. Each projected point has exactly three independent lifts to \(Q\), producing the factor \(3^k\). Dividing by \(|Q|^k=3^{4k}\) gives the generation probability.

**5. Automorphism order.** Niebrzydowski and Przytycki proved in 2006 that the free commutative kei \(\overline Q(4,3)\) contains exactly \(81\) elements [3, Theorem 6]. The affine-basis quadruple \(\{(0,0),(0,e_1),(0,e_2),(0,e_3)\}\) generates the displayed \(D(1)\) by Steps 1–3. The universal map \(\overline Q(4,3)\twoheadrightarrow D(1)\) is consequently an isomorphism because both sets have order \(81\). Each ordered generating quadruple therefore induces a surjective endomorphism of this finite free kei, necessarily an automorphism, uniquely determined by generator images. All automorphisms arise this way. Hence the automorphism-group order equals \(G_4=3^7(26)(24)(18)=24\,564\,384\).

## Verification

The standalone standard-library Python program `verify_generating_tuples.py` tests **all** \(81^3\) self-distributivity triples; all \(81^2\) commutativity, idempotency, involutivity and right-translation conditions; and **all** \(27^4\) cases of the determinant identity. It explicitly closes an affinely independent four-tuple and a dependent four-tuple, counts all ordered independent projected triples, and checks the \(k=4\) integer count and exact probability. It prints `VERIFY_OK` if every check succeeds. The finite exhaustive tests verify the specific 81-point identities; the argument in Steps 2–5 proves the formulas simultaneously for **every** positive integer \(k\).

## Relationship to prior work

The 1981 Kepka–Němec construction [4] supplies the classical example \(D(1)\). Niebrzydowski–Przytycki [3, Section 2.3–2.4] establish that the free four-generated commutative kei has \(81\) elements, and identify a central extension of \(\mathbb F_3^3\) by \(\mathbb F_3\); their established freeness and size are used here, not rediscovered. Ta's 2026 work [2, Sections 3.1 and 6.2] revisits \(D(1)\), mediality and free commutative quandles. Bardakov–Sangare [1] recently investigates automorphism groups and commutativizations of quandles, including medial examples. The inspected results do not state the affine spanning criterion for **all** \(k\)-tuples, the all-\(k\) generating probability, or the explicit \(D(1)\) automorphism count. A general enumeration of free generating bases, if already established elsewhere, would cover the \(k=4\) consequence; the complete arbitrary-length criterion is the principal statement.

## Limitations

This classification is specific to the displayed \(81\)-point commutative kei and does not extend without proof to all commutative distributive quasigroups, larger free objects, or the other \(81\)-element nonmedial example \(D(3)\). The formulas count **ordered** tuples and allow repeated entries. The group result determines the automorphism-group **order**, not its abstract isomorphism type. A same mathematical model literature comparison cannot exclude all obscure or unindexed equivalent results. Full text of Kepka–Němec's 1981 monograph was unavailable; its construction and size were checked through detailed later primary sources.
