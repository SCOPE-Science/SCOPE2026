# Exact generic silhouette law of the Miller–Aškinuze polyhedron

## Finding

Let \(J\) be the elongated square gyrobicupola (Johnson solid 37), with the Cartesian realization specified below. Let \(U\) be uniformly distributed on \(S^2\), and let \(N_J(U)\) be the number of vertices of the orthogonal projection \(J|U^\perp\). Outside a finite union of great circles,
\[
N_J(U)\in\{10,11,12\},
\]
with the following **complete, exact** probability law:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(N_J=10)&=0.44710927047909373\ldots,\\
\Pr(N_J=11)&=0.40386283467452530\ldots,\\
\Pr(N_J=12)&=0.14902789484638097\ldots.
\end{aligned}
\]
These numerals are evaluations of the finite spherical-angle expressions below, not estimates. In particular, a generic eleven-vertex shadow has positive probability.

For a noteworthy comparison, the ordinary (uniform) rhombicuboctahedron \(R\), whose faces have the same sizes and vertex configuration as \(J\), has generic shadow counts only \(10\) and \(12\), with
\[
\Pr(N_R=10)=\frac{12}{\pi}\arctan(3-2\sqrt2),\qquad
\Pr(N_R=12)=1-\Pr(N_R=10).
\]
Nevertheless, both solids have **exactly the same mean silhouette count**:
\[
\mathbb E[N_J]=\mathbb E[N_R]
=6+\frac{24}{\pi}\arctan\frac{1}{\sqrt2}
=10.701918624367287\ldots.
\]
The claimed new determination is the complete \(J\) law, not the pre-existing cube–octahedron silhouette result underlying the comparison.

## Assumptions and scope

Set \(\eta=1+\sqrt2\). Define \(J\) to be the convex hull of the following exactly \(24\) points, with independent sign choices wherever \(\pm\) appears:
\[
\begin{gathered}
(\eta,0,\pm\sqrt2),\quad(\eta,\pm\sqrt2,0),\\
(\pm1,\pm1,\pm\eta),\quad(\pm1,\pm\eta,\pm1),\\
(-\eta,\pm1,\pm1).
\end{gathered}
\]
All edges have length \(2\). A generic viewing direction is one avoiding the great circles orthogonal to the \(26\) outward facet normals. This exclusion has spherical measure zero. Views are orthographic, and silhouette vertex counts are intrinsic to the projected convex polygon; antipodal normals describe the same view plane.

For a representative normal \(u\) in an exact symmetry wedge, write
\[
u\ \parallel\ (p,1,q),\qquad p>0,\quad 0<q<1.
\]
The finite symmetry group generated by \((x,y,z)\mapsto(x,-z,y)\), \((x,y,z)\mapsto(x,z,y)\), and \((x,y,z)\mapsto(-x,(y-z)/\sqrt2,(y+z)/\sqrt2)\) sends every generic viewing direction into such a wedge. Its area on \(S^2\) is \(\pi/4\).

## Proof

**1. Exact face and silhouette reduction.** Enumerating all triples of the \(24\) displayed vertices, taking their cross-product plane normals in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\), and retaining precisely the planes that support the other vertices gives \(26\) faces: \(8\) triangles and \(18\) squares. Every edge is the intersection of exactly two faces, producing \(48\) edges. Let \(n_i,n_j\) be outward normals to an edge's two faces. In any generic direction \(u\), that edge belongs to the silhouette exactly when \((u\cdot n_i)(u\cdot n_j)<0\). The number of these edges equals the number of projected polygon vertices. These finite statements follow by exact comparisons in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\), not by floating-point hull recognition; `verify_j37.py` performs and records the complete enumeration.

**2. Complete directional partition.** In the wedge let
\[
\begin{aligned}
A(q)&=\min\{\sqrt2q,1-q\},&B(q)&=\max\{\sqrt2q,1-q\},\\
C(q)&=\min\{\sqrt2,1+q\},&D(q)&=\max\{\sqrt2,1+q\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Exact facet-normal reduction shows the only positive-\(p\) change lines in this wedge are
\[
p\in\{q,\sqrt2q,(1-q)/\sqrt2,(1+q)/\sqrt2,1-q,1,1+q,\sqrt2\}.
\]
Their vertical ordering changes only at
\[
q\in\{3-2\sqrt2,\;1/3,\;\sqrt2-1,\;1/2,\;1/\sqrt2\}.
\]
Consequently there are exactly \(6\times9=54\) open sign cells. Evaluating every face-sign product exactly at one interior point of each cell proves, simultaneously for the complete continuum of generic directions,
\[
N_J(p,1,q)=
\begin{cases}
10,&0<p<A(q)\ \text{or}\ p>D(q),\\
11,&A(q)<p<B(q)\ \text{or}\ C(q)<p<D(q),\\
12,&B(q)<p<C(q).
\end{cases}
\]
The signs cannot change within a cell, so the finite symbolic test is an exhaustive cell proof, not a sampling inference. All equality-direction cases lie in the excluded measure-zero exceptional set.

**3. Exact spherical integration.** The projective coordinates \((p,q)\mapsto (p,1,q)/\sqrt{1+p^2+q^2}\) give spherical area element
\[
\frac{dp\,dq}{(1+p^2+q^2)^{3/2}}.
\]
For three nonzero rays \(v,w,z\) in one hemisphere define the elementary spherical-triangle area
\[
T(v,w,z)=2\arctan\frac{|\det(v,w,z)|}{
\|v\|\|w\|\|z\|+(v\cdot w)\|z\|+(w\cdot z)\|v\|+(z\cdot v)\|w\|}.
\]
For \(a+bq\ge0\) throughout \(s\le q\le t\), set
\[
\begin{aligned}
X&=(0,1,s),&Y&=(a+bs,1,s),\\
Z&=(a+bt,1,t),&W&=(0,1,t),\\
S(a,b;s,t)&=T(X,Y,Z)+T(X,Z,W).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(S(a,b;s,t)\) is the exact area of the angular strip \(s<q<t\), \(0<p<a+bq\). Set \(r=\sqrt2-1\). Integrating the piecewise count regions gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{10}&=1+\frac4\pi\bigl[S(0,\sqrt2;0,r)+S(1,-1;r,1)
-S(\sqrt2,0;0,r)-S(1,1;r,1)\bigr],\\
P_{12}&=\frac4\pi\bigl[S(1,1;0,r)-S(1,-1;0,r)
+S(\sqrt2,0;r,1)-S(0,\sqrt2;r,1)\bigr],\\
P_{11}&=1-P_{10}-P_{12}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These are finite combinations of arctangents of explicit algebraic numbers and therefore constitute exact closed forms. Numerical evaluation gives the displayed probability values; all three are strictly positive.

**4. First-moment check and uniform-solid comparison.** The exact support enumeration shows that \(J\) has \(24\) edges with adjacent outward-face-normal angle \(\pi/4\), and \(24\) edges with angle \(\arctan(1/\sqrt2)\). A uniformly random normal places two specified outward normals on opposite sides of their great-circle boundaries with probability equal to their mutual angle divided by \(\pi\). Summing the silhouette-edge indicators proves the stated expectation.

The standard rhombicuboctahedron is the Minkowski sum of a cube and a scaled regular octahedron:
\[
R=[-1,1]^3+\sqrt2\operatorname{conv}\{\pm e_1,\pm e_2,\pm e_3\}.
\]
For a generic plane its projected cube is a hexagon, while the projected octahedron is a quadrilateral precisely when its largest absolute normal coordinate exceeds the sum of the other two, and a hexagon otherwise. Generic polygonal Minkowski sums add edge counts, producing exactly the comparison's \(10\)- and \(12\)-vertex cases. Integration of this octahedral inequality gives \(\Pr(N_R=10)=12\arctan(3-2\sqrt2)/\pi\). Its edge-normal angle multiplicities are the same as for \(J\), so the two means agree although their full distributions do not.

## Relationship to prior work

Johnson's 1966 classification identifies the non-uniform Miller–Askinuze solid despite its common vertex figure with the uniform rhombicuboctahedron. The explicit coordinate reference identifies the same \(24\)-point realization and displays several individual special orthographic projections. Glisse and Lazard (first publicly dated arXiv version, 14 February 2007) establish asymptotic bounds on expected silhouette complexity of families of meshes, not the full distribution of one fixed Johnson solid. Previously available cube–octahedron silhouette laws cover the comparison polyhedron through Minkowski addition, not the gyrated Johnson solid. The present pointwise partition and three-atom law are not implied by those statements. These comparisons establish originality relative to the sources inspected, not definitive global priority.

## Limitations

Only regular J37 and generic orthographic projections are covered. Special viewing directions, perspective views, arbitrary affine deformations, and distribution of projected perimeter or area are not classified. The exact closed forms may be evaluated to any precision; displayed decimals are approximations. Novelty claims are relative to a bounded literature/database comparison; historical priority is not independently certified. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
