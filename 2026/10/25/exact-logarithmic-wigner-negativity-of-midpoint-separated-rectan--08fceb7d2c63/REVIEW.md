# Scientific review: logarithmic Wigner negativity of separated rectangular pulses

## Correctness — PASS

For every unordered pulse pair the time-lag autocorrelation is an interval of half-length \(\ell-2|x-m_{jk}|\). Pair-midpoint separation ensures the absolute Wigner function decomposes into disjoint spatial channels, making the spatial-absolute integral an *exact* product of a sine-envelope integral and a trigonometric amplitude factor. The means of \(|\sin|\) and \(|\cos|\) are both \(2/\pi\), with bounded-primitive estimates supplying an actual \(O(1)\) integrated remainder. The signed Wigner mass over finite frequency cutoffs stays bounded by Plancherel. The coefficient, phase invariance, normalization, \(N=1\) boundary case, and sharp amplitude maximum follow. Independent overlap-interval numerical checks agree with the explicit Wigner expression, and integrated cutoff-decade slopes agree with the analytic coefficient to roughly a few parts in a thousand.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The published 2004 Wigner-negativity definition, the 2008 square-well negative-volume divergence, and the 2018 nonoverlapping-wavefunction cross-Wigner decomposition are credited as distinct antecedents. The inspected primary texts do not state the general midpoint-separated rectangular train logarithmic coefficient, its independence from the separation/phase parameters, or the sharp equal-magnitude extremizer. Relevant mathematical records and existing research claims were checked for stronger or equivalent statements. This is best-knowledge originality, not a guarantee of first publication. An older time-frequency treatment that already evaluates the same truncated \(L^1\) norm would dominate the present claim, if found.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The precise ultraviolet growth of negative Wigner volume is a natural quantitative obstruction to using that volume unregularized for compactly supported discontinuous signals. The theorem gives a sharp, reusable formula for a nontrivial finite family with pairwise interference, showing that each coherent pulse pair contributes an explicit amount and that the instability's coefficient can increase without bound as pulse count grows. Its exact phase invariance contrasts with the phase dependence of the Wigner cross terms themselves; the equal-magnitude maximum is sharp. The restricted midpoint separation is essential and is disclosed.

## Closest literature and limitations

Kenfack–Życzkowski (2004) studies Gaussian cats and Fock states; Keating–Steege–Pattanayak (2008) treats square-well divergence and coarse graining; Sala Mayato–Loughlin–Cohen (2018) derives cross-Wigner formulas but addresses moment-indeterminacy. None of the inspected sections proves the claimed coefficient. The proof requires common pulse length, strict midpoint disjointness, and a fixed parameter set; it supplies neither a center-uniform remainder nor a treatment of overlapping midpoint channels. Independent assessment remains outstanding.
