# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The definition is the invariant-probability Radon transform of two-plane projection areas. The box projection formula, circle absolute-coordinate average, and disk support functions fix the normalization. The six repeated-index mixed-volume coefficients equal \(2\pi\), obtained from first normal-width variation. The triple coefficient is integrated exactly using the disk's segment-valued zonoid representation and the determinant of three orthogonal-plane directions, giving the displayed convergent \({}_3F_2\) constant. Dividing by the squared box volume yields a sum of three nonnegative, explicitly squared anisotropy terms; equality forces all half-sides equal. The complete analytic derivation covers every positive triple. Direct quadrature and independently polygonized-disk convex hulls confirm normalization and numerical coefficients, but are not treated as a universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to named priority risk
The closely relevant 2026 primary full text [1] defines the transform and proves general functional inequalities but does not state the rectangular-box mixed-disk decomposition, its exact third-order mixed-volume constant, or the anisotropy-defect identity. Historical source [2] emphasizes injectivity and remained abstract-only; the public author-posted full text of [3] was read and concerns geometric averages, Crofton formulas and operator-theoretic reconstruction without a box evaluation. Older calculations, especially in inaccessible [2], remain a named priority risk. Exact geometric convex **hulls** of orthogonal disks are not equivalent to the Minkowski **sums** here. Published mathematical-record and source-title/claim searches identified no covering formula; this is evidence against observed overlap, not proof of uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
The volume of the canonical mean section body is a natural invariant whose exact value is not supplied by the general theory. For boxes, this calculation yields a universal special-function coefficient **and** a dimensionless exact stability law detecting every deviation from cubical shape. That stability identity both identifies the unique minimizer at fixed volume and quantifies the entire anisotropy penalty without unspecified constants. Generalizations to other orthogonal zonoids and fixed-volume families remain natural research directions.

## Boundaries
The theorem is restricted to three-dimensional Euclidean boxes and the explicitly normalized even mean-section operator. Unindexed historical computations might contain an equivalent coefficient or formula. Numerical validation was finite. The source comparisons are not an independent audit.
