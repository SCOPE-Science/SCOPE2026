For the number \(r(n)\) of overlapping `11` blocks in the binary expansion, the natural density of \(n\) for which \(r(n+2^k-1)\ge r(n)\) is exactly \(13/16,47/64,87/128\) for \(k=1,2,3\), respectively, and exactly \(83/128\) for **every** \(k\ge4\). Thus a one-run family of arbitrarily long Mersenne shifts satisfies the strict adjacent-one analogue of Cusick's inequality with an unexpectedly constant bias.
