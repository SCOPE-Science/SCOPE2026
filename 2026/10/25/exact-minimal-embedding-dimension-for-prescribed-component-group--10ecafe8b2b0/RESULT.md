# Exact minimal embedding dimension for prescribed component groups of monomial-algebra automorphisms

## Finding

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(0\), and let \(\Gamma\) be a finite group. Define \(\mu(\Gamma)=\min\{n\ge0:\Gamma\hookrightarrow S_n\}\), taking \(S_0=\{1\}\). The minimum embedding dimension of a finite-dimensional monomial \(k\)-algebra \(A\) with \(\pi_0\operatorname{Aut}_k(A)\cong\Gamma\) is exactly \(\mu(\Gamma)\). Moreover, a realization exists with \(\operatorname{Aut}_k(A)\cong\operatorname{Aut}_k(A)^0\rtimes\Gamma\) and solvable identity component.

## Assumptions and scope

The field \(k\) is algebraically closed of characteristic \(0\). A full monomial presentation is \(A=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/I\), where \(I\) is a monomial ideal, no \(x_i\) lies in \(I\), and \(A\) is finite-dimensional; then \(n=\dim_k(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2)\), the embedding dimension. The zero-variable algebra \(k\) is allowed for the trivial group; if presentations are required to have at least one variable, only the trivial-group minimum changes from \(0\) to \(1\). The claim concerns embedding dimension, not \(\dim_k A\) or the minimum number of ideal generators.

## Proof

**Lower bound.** Let \(G=\operatorname{Aut}_k(A)\), \(G^0\) its identity component, and \(H\le S_n\) the group of variable permutations preserving \(I\). The structural description of automorphism groups of zero-dimensional monomial algebras gives a surjection \(H\to G/G^0\). The degree-sum-zero root groups determine a standard Levi subgroup \(L_1\cong\prod_{a=1}^r\operatorname{GL}(B_a)\), where the nonempty coordinate blocks \(B_a\) partition the variables and \(r\le n\). The blocks are determined by pairs of opposite roots \(\pm(e_i-e_j)\), so conjugation by \(H\) permutes them.

The kernel of this block action is exactly \(H\cap G^0\). A permutation preserving every block is block diagonal and lies in \(L_1\subseteq G^0\). Conversely, the linear part of \(G^0\) is block upper triangular with diagonal blocks \(\operatorname{GL}(B_a)\); a permutation matrix in that group must be block diagonal. Also, a variable permutation acts trivially on \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\) only when it is the identity. Thus \(G/G^0\cong H/(H\cap G^0)\) embeds in \(S_r\), giving \(\mu(\Gamma)\le r\le n\).

**Construction attaining the bound.** If \(\Gamma\) is trivial, take \(A=k\). Otherwise set \(n=\mu(\Gamma)\ge2\), fix an embedding \(\Gamma\le S_n\), let \(v=(1,2,\ldots,n)\), \(s=n(n+1)/2\), and \(N=s+1\). For an exponent vector \(a\), write \(x^a=\prod_{i=1}^n x_i^{a_i}\), and define

\[
I=(x_1^N,\ldots,x_n^N,\;x^{\sigma v}:\sigma\in\Gamma).
\]

The pure powers make \(A=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/I\) finite-dimensional. Each orbit monomial has degree \(s<N\) and positive exponent in every variable, so none of these monomials divides another or a pure power; the displayed generators are minimal and no variable lies in \(I\). The setwise stabilizer of \(\Gamma v\) in \(S_n\) is exactly \(\Gamma\): if \(\pi(\Gamma v)=\Gamma v\), then \(\pi v=\gamma v\) for some \(\gamma\in\Gamma\), and the distinct coordinates of \(v\) force \(\pi=\gamma\). Thus the variable-permutation subgroup is \(H=\Gamma\).

For every \(i\ne j\), the degree-zero-sum derivation \(D_{ij}=x_i\partial/\partial x_j\) fails to preserve \(I\). If \(j\ne i+1\), apply it to \(x^v\): the resulting exponent vector \(v-e_j+e_i\) is not a permutation of \(v\). If \(j=i+1\) and the transposition \(\tau=(ij)\) is not in \(\Gamma\), the image of \(x^v\) is a nonzero scalar multiple of \(x^{\tau v}\), which is not in the orbit generating set. If \(j=i+1\) and \(\tau\in\Gamma\), apply \(D_{ij}\) to \(x^{\tau v}\); its exponent vector \(\tau v-e_j+e_i\) is not a permutation of \(v\). All coefficients are nonzero in characteristic \(0\). In each case the output has degree \(s\), so it cannot be divisible by a pure power of degree \(N>s\), and it is not an orbit generator; hence it is not in \(I\).

The structural theorem says that the identity component of the linear part is generated by the torus and degree-zero-sum root groups. Since none of those root groups exists here, \(G_1^0=T\). Consequently \(G^0=U_1\rtimes T\), where \(U_1\) is connected unipotent, so \(G^0\) is solvable. A permutation matrix lies in this torus only if it is the identity, hence \(H\cap G^0=1\). The surjection \(H\to G/G^0\) is therefore an isomorphism, giving \(\pi_0G\cong\Gamma\) and the split product. This matches the lower bound.

## Verification

The lower bound is an all-dimensions argument through the action on Levi blocks, not a finite enumeration. In the construction, the orbit monomials all have degree \(s\), the pure powers have degree \(N=s+1\), and the stabilizer of the distinct-exponent vector is trivial; these facts establish finite-dimensionality, the variable-permutation group, and the root-exclusion test. The trivial-group case is direct. No computer experiment or external certificate is used.

## Relationship to prior work

The paper *The structure of automorphism groups of zero-dimensional monomial algebras* proves that every finite group occurs as a component group and gives a split extension with solvable identity component, using a Frucht-graph construction (Theorem 3.4). Its structural results on the linear part and standard Levi subgroup support the lower-bound argument here, but the paper does not state the exact minimum \(\mu(\Gamma)\) or the orbit-monomial construction. For example, the construction realizes \(C_3\) in three variables; no simple graph on three vertices has automorphism group \(C_3\), so the graph-based construction cannot attain that variable count in this case. The paper *Finite-dimensional monomial algebras are determined by their automorphism group* addresses reconstruction from the identity component, a different question.

## Limitations

The structural group results used here require an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(0\). The result minimizes embedding dimension only; it does not minimize quotient vector-space dimension, the largest exponent, or the number of monomial generators. For the trivial group, the minimum depends on whether the zero-variable algebra \(k\) is admitted.
