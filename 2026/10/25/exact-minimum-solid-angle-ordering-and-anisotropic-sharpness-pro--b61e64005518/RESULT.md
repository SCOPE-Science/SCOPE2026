# Exact minimum-solid-angle ordering and anisotropic sharpness profile for right simplices

## Finding
For an integer \(n\ge 2\) and arbitrary lengths \(a_1,\ldots,a_n>0\), let
\[
S(a)=\operatorname{conv}\{0,a_1e_1,\ldots,a_ne_n\}\subset\mathbb R^n.
\]
Let \(\Omega_i\) denote the **unnormalized** \((n-1)\)-dimensional spherical measure of the tangent cone at \(a_ie_i\), let \(\Omega_0\) denote that at the origin, and put \(\Omega_{\min}=\min_{0\le i\le n}\Omega_i\). For \(\Delta_{n-1}=\{t\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{n-1}:\sum t_k\le1\}\), each nonzero vertex has the exact profile
\[
\Omega_i=\Big(\prod_{k=1}^n a_k\Big)
\int_{\Delta_{n-1}}
\left(a_i^2+\sum_{j\ne i}a_j^2t_j^2\right)^{-n/2}\,dt,
\]
where the simplex coordinates are indexed by \(j\ne i\). These angles obey the **complete ordering**
\[
\Omega_i\le\Omega_j\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a_i\ge a_j,
\qquad \Omega_i<\Omega_0=\frac{|\mathbb S^{n-1}|}{2^n}.
\]
In particular the minimum occurs precisely at the nonzero vertices corresponding to longest orthogonal legs.

This gives a sharp quantitative realization of the all-dimensional fullness-versus-solid-angle estimate. Fix \(b_2,\ldots,b_n>0\), put \(S_L=S(L,b_2,\ldots,b_n)\), \(q=\max_{j\ge2}b_j\) and \(B_2=\sum_{j=2}^n b_j^2\). Define fullness \(\mu_L=\operatorname{vol}_n(S_L)/\operatorname{diam}(S_L)^n\). Then, as \(L\to\infty\),
\[
\boxed{\frac{\Omega_{\min}(S_L)}{n\mu_L}
=1+\left(\frac n2q^2-\frac{B_2}{n+1}\right)L^{-2}+O(L^{-4}).}
\]
The displayed positive coefficient is exact for every choice of the \(n-1\) fixed legs. Consequently the coefficient \(1/n\) in the general bound \(\mu(S)\le\Omega_{\min}(S)/n\) is **optimal in every dimension**, although equality is impossible for nondegenerate right simplices.

## Assumptions and scope
The geometry is Euclidean, \(n\ge2\), and all legs are strictly positive. Surface measure on \(\mathbb S^{n-1}\) is not normalized to probability one. The dimension \(n\) is fixed in the asymptotic statement, as are the positive lengths \(b_2,\ldots,b_n\); the error constant depends on these data. The ordering statement applies to all positive lengths, including repeated legs. The optimal coefficient claim concerns the known **global inequality** for all nondegenerate simplices; the newly proved exact profile and expansion concern right simplices only.

## Proof
**1. Exact cone integral.** Fix \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\). The tangent cone at \(a_ie_i\) is generated by \(-a_ie_i\) and \(-a_ie_i+a_je_j\) for \(j\ne i\). Intersecting this cone with the affine plane whose \(i\)-th coordinate equals \(-1\) gives the simplex
\[
\left\{(-1,z):z_j\ge0,\quad\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{a_i z_j}{a_j}\le1\right\}.
\]
Radial projection from this plane to the unit sphere has area Jacobian \((1+\|z\|^2)^{-n/2}\) in the remaining \(n-1\) coordinates. On substituting \(z_j=a_jt_j/a_i\), the integral is exactly the one stated above. At the origin, the tangent cone is the positive orthant, of solid angle \(|\mathbb S^{n-1}|/2^n\). For each \(i\ge1\), the cone is a strict subset, up to measure-zero boundaries, of the orthant \(\{x_i\le0,\ x_j\ge0\ (j\ne i)\}\), proving \(\Omega_i<\Omega_0\).

**2. Exact ranking.** Compare two distinct indices \(i,j\). Relabel the integration coordinates so the distinguished coordinate in the first integral \(t_j\) is the distinguished coordinate \(t_i\) in the second. Their denominators differ by
\[
(a_i^2-a_j^2)(1-t^2),
\]
where \(0\le t\le1\) throughout \(\Delta_{n-1}\). The other squared terms coincide, and the prefactor \(\prod a_k\) is common. Thus \(a_i\ge a_j\) gives \(\Omega_i\le\Omega_j\). If the leg lengths differ, the inequality is strict because \(t<1\) almost everywhere in the simplex. Equality holds exactly for equal legs. The preceding origin comparison completes the minimum classification.

**3. Strict global-coefficient inequality within the right family.** Every squared edge length of \(S(a)\) is either \(a_i^2\) or \(a_i^2+a_j^2\); let their maximum be \(\delta^2=\operatorname{diam}(S(a))^2\). On \(\Delta_{n-1}\),
\[
a_i^2+\sum_{j\ne i}a_j^2t_j^2
\le a_i^2+\max_{j\ne i}a_j^2\sum_{j\ne i}t_j^2
\le a_i^2+\max_{j\ne i}a_j^2\le\delta^2.
\]
All inequalities are strict on a positive-measure subset of the interior. As \(\operatorname{vol}(\Delta_{n-1})=1/(n-1)!\), the exact integral yields
\[
\Omega_i>\frac{\prod a_k}{(n-1)!\delta^n}
=n\frac{\prod a_k}{n!\delta^n}=n\mu(S(a)).
\]
Thus \(\Omega_{\min}>n\mu\) for each nondegenerate right simplex.

**4. Anisotropic expansion.** For \(L>q\), the longest leg is \(L\), and the diameter equals \(\sqrt{L^2+q^2}\). Let \(B=\prod_{j\ge2}b_j\). Taylor's theorem, uniformly on the compact simplex, gives
\[
\Omega_{\min}=
\frac B{L^{n-1}}\int_{\Delta_{n-1}}
\left(1+\frac{\sum_{j\ge2}b_j^2t_j^2}{L^2}\right)^{-n/2}\,dt
=\frac{B}{(n-1)!L^{n-1}}
\left[1-\frac{B_2}{(n+1)L^2}+O(L^{-4})\right].
\]
Indeed, for the uniform distribution on \(\Delta_{n-1}\), each coordinate has second moment \(2/[n(n+1)]\), obtained by a beta integral (or the Dirichlet \((1,\ldots,1)\) moment formula). On the other hand,
\[
\mu_L=\frac{LB}{n!(L^2+q^2)^{n/2}}
=\frac{B}{n!L^{n-1}}\left[1-\frac{nq^2}{2L^2}+O(L^{-4})\right].
\]
Dividing gives the displayed sharp second-order coefficient. It is strictly positive since \(B_2\le(n-1)q^2\) implies
\[
\frac n2q^2-\frac{B_2}{n+1}\ge
\frac{n^2-n+2}{2(n+1)}q^2>0.
\]
Finally \(\lim_{L\to\infty}\mu_L/\Omega_{\min}(S_L)=1/n\), so no smaller universal constant could replace \(1/n\) in the known general inequality.

## Relationship to prior work
Martin W. Licht, *On solid angles and simplex shape measures*, arXiv:2610.05883v1 (public October 5, 2026), proves the global inequality \(\mu\le\Omega_{\min}/n\) and constructs a related degenerating simplex family to establish optimality of a *power exponent*. The equal-short-leg specialization here is congruent, after scaling and translation, to that source's degeneration; neither the degeneration itself nor the general inequality is claimed as new. What is determined here is the full anisotropic vertex-angle ordering, the exact multi-leg integral profile, and the **strict, explicit second-order distance from equality** for all fixed positive short-leg arrays. Akopyan and Karasev's earlier work studies upper bounds for the minimum solid angle and does not supply this angle/fullness deficit profile.

## Limitations
No sharp second-order inequality for all simplices is asserted. The deficit coefficient is an asymptotic invariant along the specified family only, not a universal stability constant. The known inequality's exponent and the existence of a degenerate family are prior work. Although searches did not identify these exact right-simplex refinements, earlier specialized simplex-angle literature may contain equivalent formulas or conclusions; this self-review is not independent validation.
