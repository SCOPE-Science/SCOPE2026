# Exact Minkowski chirality of a right trapezoid

## Finding

Let
\[
K=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(2,0),(1,1),(0,1)\}\subset\mathbb R^2.
\]
For a convex body \(C\), write
\[
R(K,C)=\inf\{\lambda>0:\exists x\in\mathbb R^2,\ K\subset x+\lambda C\}.
\]
If \(\Phi_U\) is reflection in an affine line \(U\), the first Minkowski chirality is
\[
\alpha_1(K)=\inf_{\dim U=1}R(K,\Phi_U(K)).
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{\alpha_1(K)=\frac{3}{\sqrt5}}.
\]
The minimizing reflection-axis direction is unique modulo \(\pi\) and equals
\[
\theta=-\frac12\arctan\!\left(\frac12\right)\pmod{\pi}.
\]
The position of the affine line does not affect the value, because translations are absorbed into the containment vector \(x\).

## Assumptions and scope

The body \(K\) is the nondegenerate Euclidean right trapezoid with horizontal bases of lengths \(2\) and \(1\), height \(1\), and vertices listed above in cyclic order. The result concerns only this specified body and the first Minkowski chirality. It is not a classification of all trapezoids or all convex quadrilaterals.

## Proof

Let \(H(\phi)\) be the support function of \(K\) at the unit vector of angle \(\phi\). From the four vertices,
\[
H(\phi)=\max\{0,\,2\cos\phi,\,\cos\phi+\sin\phi,\,\sin\phi\}.
\]
The outward unit normals of the bottom, sloping, top, and left edges have angles
\[
\phi_b=-\frac{\pi}{2},\quad
\phi_r=\frac{\pi}{4},\quad
\phi_t=\frac{\pi}{2},\quad
\phi_\ell=\pi.
\]
Their support numbers are \(H(\phi_b)=0\), \(H(\phi_r)=\sqrt2\), \(H(\phi_t)=1\), and \(H(\phi_\ell)=0\).

Put \(t=2\theta\). After reflection in an axis of direction \(\theta\), the corresponding edge-normal angles are \(t-\phi_i\). A translate \(x+\lambda\Phi_U(K)\) contains \(K\) precisely when
\[
H(t-\phi_i)\leq x\cdot n_i(t)+\lambda H(\phi_i)
\]
for all four edge normals \(n_i(t)\). By the finite-dimensional alternative for systems of linear inequalities, such an \(x\) exists exactly when every nonnegative dependence among the \(n_i(t)\) gives a nonpositive weighted sum of the right-hand-side deficits.

The cone of nonnegative dependencies is generated by exactly two minimal dependencies. In the chosen order these are
\[
n_b(t)+n_t(t)=0,\qquad
n_b(t)+\sqrt2\,n_r(t)+n_\ell(t)=0.
\]
Indeed, if the coefficients of \(n_r(t)\) and \(n_t(t)\) in any nonnegative dependence are \(a\) and \(b\), respectively, the coefficients of \(n_b(t)\) and \(n_\ell(t)\) must be \(a/\sqrt2+b\) and \(a/\sqrt2\). Thus the two displayed dependencies generate the whole cone.

Consequently, for a fixed \(t\), the least feasible dilation is
\[
\max\{W(t),Q(t)\},
\]
where
\[
W(t)=H\!\left(t+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)+H\!\left(t-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)
\]
and
\[
Q(t)=\frac{
H(t+\pi/2)+\sqrt2\,H(t-\pi/4)+H(t-\pi)
}{2}.
\]
The denominator \(1\) in \(W\) is the vertical width of \(K\); the denominator \(2\) in \(Q\) is \(H(\phi_b)+\sqrt2H(\phi_r)+H(\phi_\ell)=2\). Hence
\[
\alpha_1(K)=\min_{0\leq t<2\pi}\max\{W(t),Q(t)\}.
\]

To bound \(W\), let \(\psi=(t+\pi/2)\bmod\pi\), with \(0\leq\psi<\pi\). Directly taking the largest and smallest vertex projections gives the width profile
\[
W(t)=
\begin{cases}
2\cos\psi,&0\leq\psi\leq\pi/4,\\
\cos\psi+\sin\psi,&\pi/4\leq\psi\leq\pi/2,\\
\sin\psi-2\cos\psi,&\pi/2\leq\psi<\pi.
\end{cases}
\]
Set
\[
\delta=\arctan(1/2),\qquad
\varepsilon=\arctan 2-\arccos(3/5),\qquad
c=\frac{3}{\sqrt5}.
\]
These constants satisfy \(0<\varepsilon<\delta\): since \(\tan(\arccos(3/5))=4/3\), the inequality \(\varepsilon<\delta\) is equivalent to \(\arctan(3/4)<\arctan(4/3)\). The width profile shows that \(W(t)<c\) only when
\[
t\in(2\pi-\delta,2\pi)\ \cup\ [0,\varepsilon)\ \cup\
(\pi-\delta,\pi+\varepsilon).
\]
Outside these intervals, \(W(t)\geq c\).

On the three intervals where \(W(t)<c\), substituting the support-function branches into \(Q\) gives, respectively,
\[
Q(t)=\frac32\cos t,\qquad
Q(t)=\frac{3\cos t+2\sin t}{2},\qquad
Q(t)=\frac{\sin|t-\pi|+3\cos|t-\pi|}{2}.
\]
On the first interval, \(\cos t\geq\cos\delta=2/\sqrt5\), so \(Q(t)\geq c\). On the second, \(t<\varepsilon<\delta\) and \(\sin t\geq0\), so \(Q(t)\geq(3/2)\cos t>c\). On the third, \(|t-\pi|<\delta\), so \(Q(t)\geq(3/2)\cos|t-\pi|>c\). Thus \(\max\{W(t),Q(t)\}\geq c\) for every \(t\).

At \(t_*=2\pi-\delta\), the support-function branches give
\[
W(t_*)=\frac{3}{\sqrt5},\qquad
Q(t_*)=\frac32\cos\delta=\frac{3}{\sqrt5}.
\]
Therefore the lower bound is attained. The other boundary points of the intervals where \(W<c\) have \(Q>c\), and the inequalities above are strict in their interiors except at \(t_*\). Hence this is the unique minimizing \(t\) modulo \(2\pi\). Since \(t=2\theta\), the unique axis direction modulo \(\pi\) is
\[
\theta=\pi-\frac{\delta}{2}
=-\frac12\arctan(1/2)\pmod{\pi}.
\]
This proves the claim.

## Verification

The support function and all four support numbers follow by taking dot products with the four stated vertices. The two-generator description of the positive-dependence cone is explicit, so the containment reduction is exact rather than a sampled orientation search. The interval analysis covers every \(t\in[0,2\pi)\); no finite numerical experiment is used as a substitute for the global proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Caragea, von Dichter, Gottwald, Grundbacher, Jahn, and Runge define Minkowski chirality through translated containment in reflected copies and prove general bounds, as well as optimizer classifications for triangles and parallelograms. Their paper identifies sharper planar bounds and optimal axes for general convex bodies as open directions. The trapezoid here is neither a triangle nor a parallelogram, so those shape-specific theorems do not give its exact value or its unique optimal axis. The result supplies an exact benchmark for the first non-parallelogram quadrilateral case treated here.

## Limitations

The conclusion applies only to \(K=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(2,0),(1,1),(0,1)\}\). It does not determine \(\alpha_1\) for a general right trapezoid, prove a sharp universal planar upper bound, or characterize optimal reflection axes for arbitrary convex bodies. Independent audit has not been performed.
