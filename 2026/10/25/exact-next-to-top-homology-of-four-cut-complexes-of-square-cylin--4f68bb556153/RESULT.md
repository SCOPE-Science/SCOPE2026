# Exact next-to-top homology of four-cut complexes of square cylinders

## Finding
For every integer \(m\ge2\), set \(G_m=C_4\square P_m\), with \(4m\) vertices, and let \(\Delta_4(G_m)\) denote the *ordinary* four-cut complex. Then
\[
\dim_{\mathbb Q}\widetilde H_{4m-6}(\Delta_4(G_m);\mathbb Q)
=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\widetilde H_{4m-6}(\Delta_4(G_m);\mathbb F_2)
=3(m-1).
\]
Moreover \(H_3(\Delta_4(G_m)^\vee;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{3(m-1)}\). In particular, \(\Delta_4(G_m)\) is pure of dimension \(4m-5\) but is not shellable. The new quantitative assertion is the exact next-to-top homology rank, not the already-implied non-shellability.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is the Cartesian product of a four-cycle with a path on \(m\) vertices, with vertices \((i,j)\) for \(i\in\{0,1,2,3\}\) modulo four and \(0\le j<m\); edges join neighbors in either coordinate. The ordinary \(4\)-cut complex has facets \(V(G_m)\setminus T\), where \(T\) is a disconnected induced four-vertex set. This is distinct from the total cut complex. All homology is reduced except for the explicitly written integral third homology of the Alexander dual. The theorem computes exactly the specified groups over the rational and binary fields; it makes no claim of a complete integral homology classification of the cut complex.

## Proof
**1. Dual description and dimension.** Put \(K_m=\Delta_4(G_m)^\vee\). An arbitrary vertex subset \(F\) is a simplex of \(K_m\) precisely when *every* four-element subset of \(F\) induces a connected graph. Thus all subsets of size at most three belong to \(K_m\), and its tetrahedra are exactly connected induced four-sets. Each \(G_m\) is bipartite and has no \(K_{2,3}\) subgraph: distinct vertices in one bipartition have at most two common neighbors, as follows immediately from the four-cycle by path adjacency rule. If a bipartite graph on five vertices remains connected after deleting any vertex, its minimum degree is at least two. Its bipartition must therefore have sizes two and three, forcing all six edges of \(K_{2,3}\). Hence \(K_m\) contains no five-element simplex, and has dimension three.

**2. Relative boundary of a new layer.** Embed \(K_{m-1}\) into \(K_m\) by deleting the last graph layer. The relative third chain group is freely generated by connected induced four-sets meeting that layer, and relative second chains are generated by triangles meeting the same layer. A tetrahedron boundary is the usual alternating sum of its four three-element faces, retaining only faces touching the final layer. The standalone checker enumerates these exact relative matrices directly from this definition. For \(m=2\) there are 37 columns and binary rank 34; for \(m=3\), 69 columns and binary rank 66; for \(m=4\), 73 columns and binary rank 70. Their relative third-kernel dimensions are therefore exactly three. For every \(m>4\), each connected induced four-set meeting the final layer is entirely contained in the final four consecutive layers (a connected four-vertex subgraph has diameter at most three); all boundary triangles with nonzero incidence lie there as well. Relabeling these four layers identifies the nonzero relative matrix with the \(m=4\) matrix, while any additional relative triangle rows are zero. Consequently the binary relative third-kernel always has dimension three. This step is a local finite certificate plus an exact stabilization argument, not extrapolation from a bounded census.

**3. Three integral cycles supplied by each consecutive cube.** Two consecutive graph layers induce the three-dimensional cube \(Q_3\), regarded as \(K_{4,4}\) minus a perfect matching. Label each bipartition side \(0,1,2,3\) so that the missing edges join equal labels. All connected four-sets having two vertices in each side correspond to pairs of distinct two-subsets \((A,B)\) of \(\{0,1,2,3\}\), giving exactly thirty tetrahedra. The checker stores three explicit integral signed length-thirty coefficient vectors in lexicographic order of those pairs. Direct signed boundary evaluation shows that each is an integral third cycle; each involves sixteen tetrahedra, and the three supports are independent modulo two. Their images in \(C_3(K_m,K_{m-1})\) remain independent: if a linear combination were supported entirely on the preceding four-vertex layer, it would be a third cycle supported on its single tetrahedron, which is impossible because that tetrahedron has nonzero boundary. Thus three independent relative third cycles lift to genuine third cycles in \(K_m\), for each added layer.

**4. Exact field ranks and the integral dual statement.** The relative third homology has dimension three over \(\mathbb F_2\), by Step 2. Step 3 gives three independent liftable classes, so the connecting homomorphism to degree-two homology of \(K_{m-1}\) vanishes, and the long exact sequence for \((K_m,K_{m-1})\) yields an increase of exactly three in \(\dim H_3(-;\mathbb F_2)\). Starting with \(K_1\), a single tetrahedron, whose third homology is zero, gives \(\dim H_3(K_m;\mathbb F_2)=3(m-1)\). For every integer boundary matrix, its rank over \(\mathbb Q\) is at least its rank over \(\mathbb F_2\). The three independent integral cube cycles supply a three-dimensional rational kernel for each relative matrix, while the binary rank bounds that kernel above by three. Hence the same argument gives \(\dim H_3(K_m;\mathbb Q)=3(m-1)\). Since \(K_m\) is three-dimensional, its integral third homology is a subgroup of a free integral chain group, and is therefore free of this rank.

**5. Transfer to the cut complex.** Combinatorial Alexander duality on the \(4m\)-vertex ground set identifies \(\widetilde H_{4m-6}(\Delta_4(G_m);F)\) with \(\widetilde H^{3}(K_m;F)\) for either field \(F\in\{\mathbb Q,\mathbb F_2\}\). Over a field this has the same dimension as third homology, giving the stated formula. Disconnected four-sets exist and all cut-complex facets have \(4m-4\) vertices; thus \(\Delta_4(G_m)\) is pure of dimension \(4m-5\). A pure shellable complex has no reduced homology below its facet dimension, contradicting the proven nonzero next-to-top group.

## Verification
Run `python3 check_cylinder_fourcut.py` with the standard Python library. It reconstructs edges, connected four-sets, relative triangle incidence matrices, binary column ranks, the three signed integral cube cycles, and the absence of dual five-faces in the initial test cases. The exact output for \(m=2,3,4\) is \((37,34),(69,66),(73,70)\) for (relative tetrahedra, binary boundary rank), together with `CYLINDER_FOURCUT_EXACT_LOCAL_CERTIFICATES_OK`. Additional direct relative computations for \(m=5,6,7\) independently returned \((73,70)\) each; they are checks, not a substitute for the stabilization proof. The proof does not require machine computation beyond the finite, fully specified local matrices and their exact ranks.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer, Denker, Jelić Milutinović, Rowlands, Sundaram, and Xue establish the ordinary cut-complex definition and graph-theoretic tools. A separate published classification of the ordinary four-cut complex of the three-cube already gives three degree-two spheres at the base \(m=2\). Together with the induced-subgraph inheritance obstruction to shellability, that literature already yields non-shellability of cylinders containing the cube, so non-shellability alone is not claimed as new. The asserted extension is the exact, uniform next-to-top Betti rank \(3(m-1)\) across every cylinder length, obtained from layer-relative boundary ranks and local signed cube cycles. Results about three-cut complexes, total cut complexes, or third-power paths do not compute this four-cut cylinder invariant.

## Limitations
This theorem does not determine the other homology groups, integral torsion of the cut complex, its entire homotopy type, or an equivariant representation. The finite checker supports the signed local algebra; the all-length conclusion additionally uses the proved locality and relative exact sequence. Bibliographic novelty is assessed against inspected and indexed literature and remains subject to unindexed equivalents. Independent external audit has not been performed.
