# Exact one-face rotation census of the Heawood graph

## Finding

Let \(H\) be the Heawood graph, the point-line incidence graph of the Fano plane. Of its \(2^{14}=16{,}384\) rotation systems, exactly \(4{,}480\) have one face. Under the full automorphism group of order \(336\), these form \(16\) orientation-preserving orbits (\(12\) of size \(336\), \(4\) of size \(112\)). Mirror reversal pairs these into exactly \(8\) unoriented types: six of combined labeled size \(672\) and two of size \(224\). Every one-face embedding has genus \(4\) and thickens to an orientable strip with one boundary component.

## Assumptions and scope

The Heawood graph has \(14\) vertices and \(21\) edges. Its vertices are the seven nonzero vectors of \(\mathbb F_2^3\) and seven nonzero covectors; point \(p\) is adjacent to line \(\ell\) exactly when \(p\cdot\ell=0\). Thus every vertex has degree \(3\). A rotation system chooses one of the two cyclic orders of the three incident darts at every vertex. The claim concerns all labeled rotation systems of this fixed graph, not arbitrary graphs or all Riemannian metrics.

## Proof

There are exactly \(2^{14}\) rotations because each of the \(14\) cubic vertices has two cyclic orders. Let \(\alpha\) reverse each directed edge and let \(\sigma\) advance a dart to the next dart in the local cyclic order. The cycles of the face permutation \(\sigma\circ\alpha\) are the facial walks. The included program exhausts all bit vectors in \(\{0,1\}^{14}\) and counts these cycles. A second face tracer using the predecessor convention agrees for all rotations. The exact distribution is
\[
\#\{F=1\}=4{,}480,\quad \#\{F=3\}=10{,}880,\quad
\#\{F=5\}=1{,}008,\quad \#\{F=7\}=16,
\]
which sums to \(16{,}384\).

The automorphism group is generated by the \(168\) collineations from \(\mathrm{GL}(3,\mathbb F_2)\) and their \(168\) compositions with point-line duality. All \(336\) distinct maps are checked to preserve adjacency. Direct orbit enumeration gives \(16\) orbits of one-face systems, with \(12\) of size \(336\) and \(4\) of size \(112\). Burnside's lemma independently confirms this: one automorphism fixes \(4{,}480\) systems, \(56\) fix \(16\) each, and \(279\) fix none, so
\[
(4{,}480+56\cdot16)/336=16.
\]
Complementing all \(14\) bits reverses every local cyclic order; no orbit is fixed by this operation, so there are \(8\) mirror pairs, with combined sizes \(672\) (six pairs) and \(224\) (two pairs).

For one face, Euler's formula gives
\[
\chi(H)=V-E+F=14-21+1=-6=2-2g,
\]
so \(g=4\). The regular neighborhood of a one-face embedding has one boundary component and retracts onto \(H\), hence is a one-boundary orientable graph strip. Itoh–Vîlcu's graph-strip realization theorem then gives a cut-locus realization for each such strip type.

## Verification

The standalone file `verification/heawood_rotation_census.py` uses only the Python standard library. It verifies the graph size and degree sequence, the \(336\)-element automorphism group, all \(16{,}384\) rotations, the face-count distribution with two independent tracers, the orbit partition, Burnside's lemma, mirror pairing, and Euler genus. It completed with exit code \(0\); the exact output reports \(4{,}480\) one-face systems, \(16\) orientation-preserving orbits, and \(8\) mirror-identified types.

## Relationship to prior work

Itoh and Vîlcu proved that every connected graph can be realized as a cut locus and developed orientability criteria for graph strips. Their explicit orientable classification treats graphs with four generating cycles and discusses the Petersen graph, but does not enumerate the Heawood graph's one-face rotations. Biggs's rotation-system discussion treats the Petersen, Coxeter, and a larger distance-transitive graph, not the Heawood graph. Brinkmann, Tucker, and Van Cleemput enumerate *polyhedral* embeddings of cubic graphs; their definition requires every facial walk to be a simple cycle, excluding the one-face embedding here, whose facial walk traverses all \(42\) darts. The inspected literature and database searches did not locate an exact Heawood genus-four one-face census.

## Limitations

This is an exhaustive finite census for one fixed graph, not a theorem about all cubic graphs. It does not classify metric realizations, curvature choices, geodesic lengths, or stability of the resulting cut loci. A specialized map-enumeration source not found by the searches could contain the same counts, so the originality assessment is limited to the inspected literature and database results.
