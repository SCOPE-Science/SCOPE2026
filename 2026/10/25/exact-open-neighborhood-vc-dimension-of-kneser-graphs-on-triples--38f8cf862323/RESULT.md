# Exact open-neighborhood VC dimension of Kneser graphs on triples

## Finding

Let \(\operatorname{KG}(n,3)\), for every integer \(n\ge3\), denote the graph on all three-element subsets of \([n]\), with adjacency when the subsets are disjoint. Let \(v(n)\) denote the VC dimension of its **open** neighborhood family \(\{N(C):C\in\binom{[n]}3\}\). Then
\[
v(n)=\begin{cases}
0,&3\le n\le5,\\
1,&n=6,\\
2,&n=7,\\
3,&n=8,\\
4,&n=9,10,\\
5,&n=11,12,\\
6,&n\ge13.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus six is the exact dimension for all sufficiently large graphs in this natural fixed-rank Kneser family, with first occurrence at thirteen ground elements.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite and simple, but the auxiliary obstruction permits multigraphs, including parallel edges. Empty neighborhoods are permitted. Shattering means realizing **every** trace on a chosen sample of graph vertices, by some open neighborhood. The theorem concerns rank-three Kneser graphs; it does not claim this bound for variable rank, closed neighborhoods, or induced subgraphs with arbitrary vertex deletion.

For a fixed sample \(F_1,\ldots,F_m\) of distinct triples, the trace of the open neighborhood of a center triple \(C\) is \(\{i:C\cap F_i=\varnothing\}\). Taking complements inside \([m]\) shows that the same sample is shattered exactly when each **hit** trace \(\{i:C\cap F_i\ne\varnothing\}\) can occur. The latter convention is used in the proof and checker.

## Proof

**Private coordinates.** For each sample index \(i\), shattering provides a center hitting just \(F_i\). Some coordinate \(p_i\in F_i\) must consequently belong to no other sampled triple. The \(p_i\) are distinct. The residual pairs \(F_i\setminus\{p_i\}\) define a loopless multigraph \(H\) with labeled edges \(e_i\). Every possible center contributes a union of at most three members of the collection consisting of the edge stars \(S_x=\{i:x\in e_i\}\), the singletons \(\{i\}\) (from the private coordinates), and the empty trace (from coordinates outside the sample union).

**Three-vertex cover.** One center realizes the full hit trace \([m]\). Replacing each selected private coordinate by either residual endpoint of its edge cannot lose any hit in the full trace and uses no more coordinates. Removing irrelevant outside coordinates similarly cannot lose a hit. Hence \(H\) admits a vertex cover of size at most three. This reduction applies without assuming that the original sample supports a nonrepeated auxiliary graph.

**Complete seven-edge obstruction.** Every multigraph with seven labeled edges and a vertex cover of size at most three has, after selecting and ordering the cover vertices, three nonnegative multiplicities for edges joining pairs of core vertices, together with a multiset of nonzero peripheral incidence vectors \((a,b,c)\) recording the numbers of parallel edges from each remaining vertex to the three core vertices. The total number of edges is seven; core vertices may be unused. The vectors are ordered lexicographically within each fixed total size, and the multiset is listed in nondecreasing order. This enumerates every eligible graph, including parallel edges and all configurations on arbitrarily many auxiliary vertices. Its entire edge-index trace collection is generated by taking unions of at most three core/peripheral stars or singleton edge sets.

`verify_kneser_triple_vc.py` enumerates exactly \(9177\) such profiles. The independent coefficient calculation \([z^7](1-z)^{-3}\prod_{s=1}^{7}(1-z^s)^{-\binom{s+2}{2}}=9177\) verifies completeness of the profile count. Across all profiles the largest number of realizable hit traces is \(124<128\), so no seven-element sample is shattered. Any larger shattered sample would contain a shattered seven-element subfamily. Therefore \(v(n)\le6\) for every \(n\).

**Small-ground lower restrictions.** The trace hitting none of the \(m\) sample triples requires at least three ground elements disjoint from their union. Together with the \(m\) private coordinates and at least two residual coordinates, this yields \(n\ge m+5\). If \(m\ge5\) and only two residual coordinates are available, every residual pair uses these same two coordinates. A hit trace consisting of exactly four of the \(m\) edges is then impossible with three center coordinates: a residual coordinate hits all \(m\), while private coordinates each hit only one. Hence a shattered five-sample needs \(n\ge11\).

For a shattered six-sample, three residual coordinates also do not suffice. Each residual pair is then one of the three pair types on these coordinates. If two different pair types are represented, exclude one sample edge of each type; if only one type occurs, exclude any two of that type. The resulting four-edge hit trace cannot use any residual coordinate because every residual coordinate that hits any sample also hits an excluded edge. Four private coordinates would be needed, but a center has size three. Hence a shattered six-sample has at least four residual coordinates and requires \(n\ge6+4+3=13\).

**Witnesses.** For every \(1\le m\le4\), use sample triples \(F_i=\{a,b,p_i\}\), where the private \(p_i\) and shared \(a,b\) are distinct, and append three unused coordinates. Every proper hit subset has size at most three and is realized by its privates (padded with unused coordinates); the full set is hit by \(a\). The ground set has size \(m+5\).

For \(m=5\), use a shared point \(c\), two block-specific points \(x,y\), and five private points. Put two sample triples in the \(x\)-block and three in the \(y\)-block, each of the form \(\{c,x,p_i\}\) or \(\{c,y,p_i\}\). For any proper subset of the sample indices, use one block point for any completely selected block and otherwise one private point per selected index: at most three points suffice. For the full subset, use \(c\). Three unused ground coordinates permit padding; the ground size is eleven.

For \(m=6\), divide six indices into three pairs. Each sampled triple has a common coordinate \(c\), its pair-specific coordinate, and its private coordinate. Every proper subset leaves at least one pair incomplete, so choosing a pair-specific coordinate for each complete pair and private coordinates for selected members of incomplete pairs uses at most three points. The full subset is hit by \(c\). With three unused coordinates the ground size is thirteen. The standalone verifier tests all center triples for these witnesses and gets precisely \(2^m\) hit traces.

The upper restrictions together with these monotone-in-ground-size witnesses give every case in the displayed formula. For \(3\le n\le5\) the Kneser graph has no edges and all open neighborhoods are empty.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_kneser_triple_vc.py` using the Python standard library. The script exhaustively generates the seven-edge obstruction classes (not just graph orders up to a chosen bound), checks every profile's complete collection of at-most-three-star/singleton unions, and confirms \(9177\) profiles, \(0\) fully shattering profiles, and a maximum of \(124\) of \(128\) traces. It independently checks the profile count by a generating-function coefficient. It also checks all center triples of three explicit finite witness constructions and the minimum auxiliary shared-vertex counts for smaller profile sizes. Successful output ends with `COMPLETE FINITE OBSTRUCTION CERTIFICATE: PASS`.

The finite enumeration establishes a universal upper bound because the preceding structural reduction makes the list exhaustive. By itself, inspecting small Kneser graphs would not suffice for that conclusion.

## Relationship to prior work

Adler, Benediktsson, and Macpherson (2020 preprint; published 2022) determined the neighborhood VC dimension for the adjacency relations on Johnson and Hamming graphs. Their Johnson adjacency means intersection in two elements for triples; the present Kneser relation means intersection in zero elements. Their sharp Johnson bound four therefore neither gives nor dominates the current six, and their full preprint's statements and checked proof sections concern Johnson and Hamming relations rather than Kneser disjointness.

Coudert, Csikós, Ducoffe, and Viennot (2024) give general algorithms to compute graph VC dimension and related estimates. Such an algorithm can compute each fixed finite Kneser graph but does not state this uniform exact formula or replace the seven-sample obstruction argument. Byrne, Byrne, and Cioabă (2026) study strongly regular graphs and give selected family results; general rank-three Kneser graphs do not constitute that class, and their paper does not state this formula. No claim of exhaustive worldwide publication coverage is made.

## Limitations

The sharp finite computation depends on correctly executing and inspecting the included standalone code; no independent audit or formal proof assistant validation was performed. The result concerns open rather than closed neighborhoods and fixed Kneser rank three only. Priority uncertainty remains for unindexed or inaccessible specialized literature. The primary mathematical reduction and witness proofs are supplied independently of the computation.
