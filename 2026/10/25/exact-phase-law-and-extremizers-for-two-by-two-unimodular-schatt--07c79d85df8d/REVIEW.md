# Scientific review of the exact two-by-two Schatten-four phase law

## Correctness — PASS

For arbitrary complex two-by-two inputs, the singular-value identity reduces fourth Schatten powers to Frobenius mass and squared determinant. For a nontrivial gain, both reductions of the entry magnitudes are strict unless all four moduli coincide. A final one-angle cosine inequality is equivalent to a scalar quadratic whose larger root gives the exact claimed constant. Explicit unit-modulus witnesses achieve equality, and the trigonometric equality condition classifies every norming matrix. The factorable and antipodal endpoints are separately handled. The cb part follows from Arhancet's fully read Theorem 3.2, not a numerical assumption. The local checker passed 8,418 deterministic tests; these only check examples. No nonstandard assertion remains dependent solely on finite tests.

## Originality — PASS, with priority caveat

Huang–Sukochev–Tomskova's full available preprint (arXiv:2608.20933) supplies larger-matrix separation examples but no two-by-two unimodular phase formula. Arhancet's full available preprint (arXiv:2609.29643, especially Theorem 3.2 and Sections 4–5) proves equality of ordinary/cb norms for two-by-two symbols, and exact norms for a different three-by-two symbol; it does not state the phase law or its full norming-input classification. Caspers–Wildschut (arXiv:1902.07949) investigates general Schur bounds, not this exact finite family. Related exact zero-one Schur idempotent results concern a strictly different symbol class. The known weak trace-class two-by-two phase identity also has a different target norm, so its numerical value neither establishes nor subsumes this formula. Targeted public-literature and mathematical-record searches did not locate equivalent coverage. This is a best-knowledge judgment, not proof of historical uniqueness. Some older specialized sources remain a residual priority risk.

## Value — PASS

The natural complete two-by-two unimodular family is the maximal matrix size for which ordinary and completely bounded Schatten-four norms universally coincide, immediately preceding known three-by-three separation. An exact, phase-invariant norm, all scalar extremizers, and the sharp linear perturbation law provide quantitative information not supplied by the existing qualitative cb-equality theorem. The result is a meaningful endpoint benchmark for finite Schur-multiplier geometry rather than an arbitrary isolated example; it does not claim a larger-dimensional or all-exponent extension.
