# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For fixed positive pitch, conjugate times near an exceptional phase are roots of \(F=A\sigma^2+B\). At \((\tau,\alpha)=(\pi,0)\), direct evaluation gives \(F=0\), \(F_\tau=-\pi^3(1+4\sigma^2)/2\), \(F_\alpha=0\), and \(F_{\alpha\alpha}=2\pi^4\sigma^2\). The implicit-function theorem therefore gives \(\tau''(0)=4\pi\sigma^2/(1+4\sigma^2)\), hence the stated half-second-derivative coefficient. The same calculation applies at \(\alpha=\pi\). The source's no-conjugate-points-before-cut result identifies the nearby root as the first conjugate time.

## Originality — PASS

The primary paper gives the global cut locus, the equality phases, and an implicit Jacobian equation, but the inspected sections do not state the exact phase-stiffness profile \(\kappa(\sigma)=2\pi\sigma^2/(1+4\sigma^2)\) or its monotone saturation across pitch. Targeted literature-record searches for the exact coefficient, aliases, and the source equation returned no matching source; the closest surfaced records concern unrelated small-parameter laws. These searches are bounded negative evidence, not proof of universal novelty. The claim is limited to the local phase coefficient and does not assert a new global optimal-synthesis theorem.

## Value — PASS

The cut time marks loss of global minimizing, whereas the conjugate time marks the first singularity of the exponential map. The coefficient \(\kappa(\sigma)\) is a natural local sensitivity profile: it quantifies how the conjugate branch leaves the two exceptional phases, is strictly increasing with pitch, and saturates at \(\pi/2\) in rescaled time. This provides a precise phase-dependent conditioning scale for local analysis of the optimal-synthesis map, beyond the source's qualitative equality criterion and coarse global upper bound.
