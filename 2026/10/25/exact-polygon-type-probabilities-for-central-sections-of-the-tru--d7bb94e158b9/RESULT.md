# Exact polygon-type probabilities for central sections of the truncated octahedron

## Finding
Let
\[
K=\left\{(x_1,x_2,x_3)\in[-1,1]^3:\ |x_1|+|x_2|+|x_3|\le\frac32\right\},
\]
the regular Archimedean truncated octahedron, and let \(U\) be uniform on the unit sphere \(S^2\). Define \(N(U)\) as the number of vertices (equivalently, sides) of the central polygon \(K\cap U^\perp\). Almost surely, \(N(U)\) takes **exactly three possible values: \(6,8,10\)**. Their probabilities have the following elementary closed forms.

For nonzero real vectors \(a,b,c\) with the denominator below positive, define their (unsigned) simplicial solid angle by
\[
\Omega(a,b,c)=2\arctan\frac{|\det(a,b,c)|}
{\|a\|\|b\|\|c\|+(a\cdot b)\|c\|+(b\cdot c)\|a\|+(c\cdot a)\|b\|}.
\]
Introduce seven integer rays
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_0&=(1,0,0),&v_1&=(4,2,1),&v_2&=(2,1,1),\\
v_3&=(2,2,1),&v_4&=(1,1,1),&v_5&=(2,1,0),&v_6&=(1,1,0).
\end{aligned}
\]
Then the complete polygon-type law is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\boxed{\Pr(N=10)}&=\frac{12}{\pi}\Omega(v_0,v_1,v_2)
\ =0.1854404545021058\ldots,\\
\boxed{\Pr(N=6)}&=\frac{12}{\pi}\big[\Omega(v_2,v_3,v_4)+\Omega(v_5,v_6,v_3)+\Omega(v_5,v_3,v_1)\big]
\ =0.4984506899177037\ldots,\\
\boxed{\Pr(N=8)}&=1-\Pr(N=6)-\Pr(N=10)
\ =0.3161088555801904\ldots.
\end{aligned}
\]
Each displayed solid angle is twice an arctangent of an explicit radical expression, so no unevaluated integral or probabilistic limit is involved. Independently, the expected number of section vertices has the particularly short expression
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E[N]=\frac{36}{\pi}\arccos\frac45
\ =7.373979529168803\ldots.}
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The Haar probability measure on unoriented central planes is the pushforward of normalized surface area on \(S^2\) under \(u\mapsto u^\perp\); the two antipodal normals define the same plane. The polytope is the fixed full-dimensional body \(K\) above. Its 24 vertices are the independently signed coordinate permutations of \((1,1/2,0)\); it has six square and eight regular-hexagonal facets and 36 edges. Section planes passing through vertices or containing a polytope edge form a set of orientation measure zero. The probabilities and polygon counts refer to generic sections; no claim is made for those exceptional orientations.

## Proof
**1. Count intersected facet pairs.** For every generic central plane, the number of sides of \(K\cap U^\perp\) equals the number of facets of \(K\) which that plane cuts in a nondegenerate segment. Centrally opposite facets occur in pairs and are simultaneously intersected, so each such pair contributes exactly two sides. By signed coordinate permutations we may assume the coordinates of \(U\), before normalization, satisfy \(x>y>z>0\); the limiting equal-coordinate cases have probability zero.

The square facet \(x_i=1\) has relative vertices \((\pm1/2,0)\) and \((0,\pm1/2)\) in the remaining coordinate plane. Its pair is intersected exactly when \(2|u_i|<\max_{j\ne i}|u_j|\). In the chosen fundamental cone this gives no square pair along the largest coordinate, the second-coordinate pair iff \(2y<x\), and the third-coordinate pair iff \(2z<x\).

For a hexagonal facet, choose a sign vector \(\sigma\in\{\pm1\}^3\). Its six vertices are the permutations, with corresponding signs, of \((1,1/2,0)\). Among the four opposing facet pairs represented by \((+,+,+)\), \((+,+,-)\), \((+,-,+)\), and \((+,-,-)\), the first is never cut and the last two are always cut. The \((+,+,-)\) pair is cut precisely when \(2z>y\): the minimum dot product over its six vertices is \(-z+y/2\), while its maximum is positive. Consequently the exact generic count is
\[
\boxed{N=4+2\mathbf 1_{\{2z>y\}}+2\mathbf 1_{\{2y<x\}}+2\mathbf 1_{\{2z<x\}}.}\tag{1}
\]
These three indicators cannot all vanish, and they cannot all be one unless \(2y<x\); in particular the only possible values of \(N\) are \(6,8,10\). All three are realized on open cones.

**2. Partition the fundamental spherical chamber.** Set \(t=y/x\), \(s=z/x\). The chamber \(x>y>z>0\) is represented by the planar triangle \(0<s<t<1\), with sphere point \((1,t,s)/\sqrt{1+t^2+s^2}\). In this triangle, condition (1) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\{N=10\}&:\quad 0<t<\tfrac12,\quad \tfrac t2<s<t;\\
\{N=6\}_A&:\quad \tfrac12<t<1,\quad \tfrac12<s<t;\\
\{N=6\}_B&:\quad \tfrac12<t<1,\quad 0<s<\tfrac t2.
\end{aligned}
\]
The remaining region has \(N=8\). The \(N=10\) triangular cone has ray generators \(v_0,v_1,v_2\). The first \(N=6\) triangular cone has rays \(v_2,v_3,v_4\). The second \(N=6\) region is a spherical quadrilateral with successive rays \(v_5,v_6,v_3,v_1\); its diagonal from \(v_5\) to \(v_3\) partitions it into the two triangular cones in the theorem.

**3. Integrate exact spherical areas.** Each of the 48 signed-coordinate-permutation chambers has equal spherical area \(4\pi/48=\pi/12\). The spherical triangle generated by rays \(a,b,c\) has area \(\Omega(a,b,c)\), as follows from the standard oriented solid-angle identity (valid here with positive denominator). Summing the disjoint triangles in step 2 and dividing by \(\pi/12\) proves all three stated exact probabilities. The rays have integer entries and the formula uses only dot products, determinants, radicals, and arctangents.

**4. Independent expectation identity.** A generic section meets an edge precisely when its endpoints have opposite signs under \(u\mapsto u\cdot v\). For two nonzero vertex vectors making an angle \(\theta\), this crossing probability under a uniform normal is \(\theta/\pi\). Every one of the 36 edges joins vertex vectors of squared length \(5/4\) and dot product \(1\), hence \(\cos\theta=4/5\). Summing 36 crossing indicators yields \(\mathbb E[N]=36\arccos(4/5)/\pi\), in agreement with the three-point law.

## Verification
The included standalone Python program `verify.py` requires only the standard library. Run `python3 verify.py`. It reconstructs all 24 vertices and 36 true edges as exact integer triples scaled by two, checks the independent edge-crossing vertex count against (1) for 13,297 generic rational directions, evaluates all four solid angles, and compares their probabilities both with an independent spherical-projective quadrature and with the exact 36-edge expectation identity. Finally it tests 250,000 seeded Haar-uniform normals using *edge crossings*, not the claimed formula, with an explicit six-standard-error threshold. The successful output ends in `ALL_TRUNCATED_OCTAHEDRON_CENTRAL_SECTION_LAW_CHECKS_OK`. These finite diagnostics corroborate rather than prove the theorem; the full-domain proof is steps 1–4 above.

## Relationship to prior work
Lonke's *On random sections of the cube* (1998) establishes expected face counts for Haar-random central sections of cubes, including the three-dimensional cube case. It does not evaluate the full distribution for the noncubical body \(K\). De Leo's *Topology of plane sections of periodic polyhedra with an application to the Truncated Octahedron* (2005) studies foliations and stereographic maps of periodically continued polyhedral surfaces; the geometric object appears, but a Haar central-section side-count law of the single convex cell is not among its results. The separately established tetrahedral-volume profile of the one-parameter family \([-1,1]^3\cap\{\sum|x_i|\le c\}\) contains this body at \(c=3/2\), but optimizes a different invariant and does not imply any orientation-distribution statement. General angle and polarity identities explain the method, but neither an expectation nor an unrelated extremum gives the three event probabilities above. Historical priority beyond the inspected literature remains provisional.

## Limitations
The theorem fixes a single regular Archimedean truncated octahedron in three dimensions and the Haar law on central plane orientations. It does not classify sections by metric geometry beyond their number of sides, give offset-plane distributions, or extend automatically to arbitrary stretches or higher-dimensional permutohedra. Independent mathematical audit and a complete historical priority determination have not been performed.
