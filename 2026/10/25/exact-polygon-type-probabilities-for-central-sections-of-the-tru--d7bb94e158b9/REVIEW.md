# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The polytope's six square and eight hexagonal facets yield an explicit facet-pair intersection formula for every generic central plane, without sampling. In a single 48-fold symmetric spherical chamber, the three side-count events partition a projective triangle into disjoint spherical triangles and one triangulated quadrilateral. The exact solid-angle formula integrates each region, and a second edge-crossing argument derives the expected side count. The standalone verifier reconstructs the full 24-vertex, 36-edge incidence, checks 13,297 exact rational normals, independently integrates the spherical regions, checks the mean identity, and samples 250,000 Haar orientations. Its empirical tests are corroboration, not infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS relative to the documented comparisons, not a claim of exhaustive priority. Lonke's 1998 cube work gives an expectation for a different body and not this three-class distribution. De Leo's 2005 truncated-octahedron study treats an infinite periodic surface and plane-foliation topology, not the polygon-side law for one convex cell. A prior exact tetrahedral-volume result evaluates an extremum over a family containing this body but supplies no directional section probabilities. Searches of published mathematical records using the object's names, side-count classes, spherical-angle equivalences, and body-centered-cubic Voronoi-cell terminology did not produce a stronger covering statement. The possibility of an older unindexed stereology table remains a residual risk; no claim of definitive historical priority is made.

## Value
PASS. Central random plane sections of a canonical Archimedean solid form a natural invariant in geometric probability. The complete three-type distribution distinguishes the probability of a decagon from that of a hexagon or octagon; the expectation alone cannot recover it. The exact spherical chamber partition provides a reusable geometric explanation, while the independent edge-angle expectation identity checks normalization. This is more than a routine numeric estimate or tabulation of known data.

## Scope and limits
The probabilities apply only to Haar-uniform central sections of the specified three-dimensional solid, almost surely away from degeneracies. They do not furnish metric section distributions or shifted-plane analogues. No independent audit has been performed.
