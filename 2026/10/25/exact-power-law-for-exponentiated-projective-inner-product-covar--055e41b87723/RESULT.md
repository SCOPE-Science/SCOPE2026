# Exact power law for exponentiated projective inner-product covariances

## Finding
Let \(\mathbb F\) be any of \(\mathbb R,\mathbb C,\mathbb H\), let \(d\ge 2\) be an integer, and let \(\mathbb FP^{d-1}\) denote lines in \(\mathbb F^d\). For unit representatives \(u,v\), put
\[
K_{\alpha,\kappa}([u],[v])=\exp\bigl(\kappa|\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathbb F}|^{\alpha}\bigr),\qquad \alpha>0,\quad\kappa>0.
\]
Then \(K_{\alpha,\kappa}\) is positive semidefinite on every finite point configuration if and only if \(\alpha\) is a positive even integer. For these even exponents, it is strictly positive definite on every finite set of distinct projective lines. At every other exponent and every positive concentration, its restriction to an embedded real projective line already fails positive definiteness. Quantitatively, the Fourier coefficients of this restriction have a nonzero alternating leading term: for \(n\to+\infty\),
\[
\widehat k(n)=(-1)^{n+1}\frac{\kappa\Gamma(\alpha+1)\sin(\pi\alpha/2)}{\pi 2^{\alpha}}n^{-\alpha-1}+o(n^{-\alpha-1}),\quad \alpha\notin2\mathbb N.
\]
Here \(k(t)=\exp(\kappa|\cos t|^{\alpha})\) is periodic modulo \(\pi\), and \(\widehat k(n)=\pi^{-1}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}k(t)e^{-2int}\,dt\). The alternating leading sign supplies infinitely many negative coefficients.

## Assumptions and scope
The projective line \([u]\) identifies unit vectors differing by a unit scalar in \(\mathbb F\); the quantity \(|\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathbb F}|\) is independent of representatives. Positive semidefiniteness means \(\sum_{i,j}\overline{c_i}c_jK([u_i],[u_j])\ge0\) for all finite choices of points and complex coefficients. No restriction is imposed on the number of sample sites, the ambient finite dimension \(d\ge2\), or the fixed finite concentration \(\kappa>0\). The zero-concentration constant kernel and the nonexistent power \(\alpha=0\) are outside this statement.

## Proof
**Even powers.** Write \(\alpha=2m\) with integer \(m\ge1\). In the real Hilbert space of Hermitian \(d\times d\) matrices over \(\mathbb F\), equipped with real Hilbert–Schmidt pairing, the rank-one projection \(P_u=uu^*\) satisfies
\[
\langle P_u,P_v\rangle_{\mathrm{HS},\mathbb R}=\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}(P_uP_v)=|\langle u,v\rangle_{\mathbb F}|^2.
\]
This identity follows directly by cyclically evaluating the real trace, which is legitimate for quaternionic matrices. Set \(X_u=P_u^{\otimes m}\) in the real tensor-power Hilbert space. Then \(\|X_u\|=1\), \(\langle X_u,X_v\rangle=|\langle u,v\rangle|^{2m}\), and distinct projective lines have distinct \(X_u\) because their inner product is strictly less than one. Therefore
\[
K_{2m,\kappa}([u],[v])=e^{\kappa}\exp\left(-\frac{\kappa}{2}\|X_u-X_v\|^2\right).
\]
The Euclidean Gaussian kernel is strictly positive definite on every finite set of distinct vectors: in the finite-dimensional span of the differences its Fourier density is strictly positive everywhere, while an exponential polynomial associated with distinct points cannot vanish identically. This proves both positivity and strictness in all three scalar fields.

**Failure for non-even powers.** Restrict to the real two-dimensional subspace generated by two orthogonal coordinate vectors, using \(u(t)=(\cos t,\sin t,0,\ldots,0)\) for \(t\in\mathbb R/\pi\mathbb Z\). On this circle the kernel is translation invariant and equals \(k(t-s)=\exp(\kappa|\cos(t-s)|^\alpha)\). By the Fourier positivity criterion on a compact abelian group, a continuous translation-invariant positive-definite kernel must have nonnegative Fourier coefficients.

We record the local Fourier fact used below. If an even periodic function is smooth away from a single interior cusp, and locally has \(f(x)=f(0)+b|x|^p+O_{\mathrm{symbol}}(|x|^{p+\delta})\) for \(p>0\), \(\delta>0\), \(p\notin 2\mathbb N\), then its Fourier transform at integer frequency \(N\) has leading term
\[
-2b\,\Gamma(p+1)\sin(\pi p/2)|N|^{-p-1}
\]
under the unnormalized integral convention, with remainder \(o(|N|^{-p-1})\). Here the symbol remainder condition means that each fixed derivative of the remainder is bounded by the corresponding power \(O(|x|^{p+\delta-j})\) near zero. To verify the fact, multiply \(|x|^p\) by a smooth cutoff, insert the Abel factor \(e^{-\varepsilon|x|}\), and evaluate the half-line oscillatory integral using \(\int_0^\infty x^pe^{-(\varepsilon+iN)x}\,dx=\Gamma(p+1)(\varepsilon+iN)^{-p-1}\). Taking the real part as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) gives the stated coefficient. The difference between the cutoff distribution and its homogeneous model is smooth off zero and decreases faster than any fixed inverse power by repeated integrations by parts. The symbol remainder has faster Fourier decay: split at \(|x|=|N|^{-1}\) and integrate by parts sufficiently often on the complement. This yields the claimed little-oh bound. No finite numerical truncation is used in this implication.

At the cusp \(t=\pi/2\pmod\pi\), put \(x=t-\pi/2\). As \(|\cos(\pi/2+x)|=|\sin x|\), for every \(\alpha>0\)
\[
\exp(\kappa|\sin x|^\alpha)=1+\kappa|x|^\alpha+O_{\mathrm{symbol}}\bigl(|x|^{\alpha+\min(\alpha,2)}\bigr).
\]
Apply the preceding Fourier fact with \(N=2n\). The cusp location contributes the phase \(e^{-2in\pi/2}=(-1)^n\), and normalization by \(1/\pi\) gives exactly the asymptotic in the Finding section. For \(\alpha\notin2\mathbb N\), the sine factor is nonzero. Because \((-1)^{n+1}\) alternates, infinitely many coefficients are negative, contradicting positive definiteness. This proves necessity for every \(\kappa>0\), \(d\ge2\), and \(\mathbb F\).

## Verification
The companion program `verify_projective_kernel.py` independently compares Fourier-integral quadrature with the convergent gamma-series formula
\[
\widehat k(n)=\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{\kappa^j}{j!}\frac{\Gamma(j\alpha+1)}{2^{j\alpha}\Gamma(j\alpha/2+n+1)\Gamma(j\alpha/2-n+1)},\qquad n\ge1,
\]
where singular denominator-gamma factors are interpreted by continuity as zero. It exercises non-even and even exponents, multiple concentrations, asymptotic signs, and explicit finite negative Fourier witnesses. It reports 63 successful quadrature and finite tests. This is a numerical consistency check only; the universal statements follow from the preceding proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Gneiting's 2011 spherical positive-definiteness treatment supplies the standard harmonic framework for isotropic kernels but does not classify exponentiated powers of projective absolute inner products. Feragen–Lauze–Hauberg (2014) concern exponentials of geodesic distances and their negative-type obstructions; the argument above is instead a cut-locus Fourier asymptotic for powered inner-product affinities. Matzke's 2021 thesis, §6.2 and Lemma 6.2.2, analyzes Fourier coefficients of the **unexponentiated** projective power \(|\langle u,v\rangle|^p\) and identifies alternating signs away from even integers. That result is closely related but by itself does not settle whether the infinitely many higher-order powers in an exponential can restore Fourier positivity for some concentration. The local singularity argument shows they cannot, and proves the classification uniformly for every concentration and for three projective scalar fields. The novelty assessment is bounded by inspected literature; an equivalent formulation may exist elsewhere.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns strictly positive concentrations and the specific family \(e^{\kappa|\langle u,v\rangle|^\alpha}\). It does not classify arbitrary functions of projective overlap, arbitrary phase-sensitive inner products, negative concentrations, or infinite-dimensional quaternionic geometry. Literature comparison is nonexhaustive. Numerical tests cannot certify the asymptotic theorem.
