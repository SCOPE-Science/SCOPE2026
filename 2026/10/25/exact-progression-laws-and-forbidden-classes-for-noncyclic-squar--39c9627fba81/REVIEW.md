# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. The equivalence between noncyclic squarefree semiprime orders and \(p\mid(q-1)\) follows directly from the totient formula. Both fixed-prime CRT systems were reconstructed. The passage from fixed primes to the convergent series uses Brun–Titchmarsh for the intermediate tail and the elementary \(q=1+kp\) bound for larger primes. The prime-series coefficient and the finite forbidden-class cutoff follow from these cases, including \(m=1\). The included finite checker reports `VERIFY_OK`; it supplements rather than establishes the infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS with bibliographic reservations. Cohen (2025) asks about the distribution of all cyclic numbers in progressions; its theorem is on power sums, not the present noncyclic semiprime law. Ibarra (2026) gives a different finite subadditivity counterexample; unrestricted semiprime asymptotics do not imply the constrained count. The closest recorded Duffinian-semiprime result changes the relevant divisor-sum invariant and proves a relative-density statement, not the full local CRT constant and sharp forbidden-class cutoff. Specific statement and implication comparisons found no dominating theorem in the accessible references. Residual risks include unindexed applications of prime-pair sieve methods and incomplete access to the public record.

## Mathematical value
PASS. This is an exact positive-density-versus-finite-support dichotomy across *all* moduli and residue classes for a natural multiplicatively constrained group-order family, not a single special progression. The result isolates the prime-square congruence obstruction, quantifies permitted classes, and gives a precisely delimited complementary perspective on the question of progression distributions for cyclic numbers. It does not settle Cohen's full problem.
