# Scientific review: exact diameter law for octahedral planar shadows

## Correctness — PASS
The exact invariant is the **metric diameter of an orthogonal two-dimensional projection**, not a central-section diameter or a one-dimensional width. For the unit coordinate octahedron, its projected antipodal generating vertices force \(D=2\sqrt{1-m^2}\), where \(m\) is the smallest absolute coordinate of a Haar-spherical normal. The event \(m\ge t\) is the union of eight congruent spherical triangular patches. Computing their three interior angles and three small-circle geodesic-curvature integrals and applying Gauss--Bonnet gives the exact elementary CDF. The derivative identity \(\alpha'=t\beta'\) gives the normalized density. The lower endpoint is controlled by a smooth tangent-plane equilateral triangle; the upper endpoint by an ordinary positive minimum-coordinate density. Local graph coordinates and dominated convergence establish the two conditional laws. The packaged verification program independently reconstructs projected pairwise distances, performs spherical-area quadrature, compares a numerical derivative, checks endpoint constants, and checks 260,000 random spherical normals. No finite simulation is used to infer an infinite statement.

## Originality — PASS relative to checked evidence
Finch's random-width studies concern one-dimensional projection lengths, controlled by the largest absolute spherical coordinate for the regular octahedron. Böröczky and Henk study face numbers of random regular-polytope shadows, not their metric diameter laws in the inspected octahedral sections. The published exact inradius law for an octahedron's planar shadow and exact diameter law for its *central section* solve different polar or intersection problems. Their formulas neither assert nor imply the proposed CDF without a new order-statistic calculation: the inradius is inverse to a cube-section circumradius, whereas the present shadow diameter is the largest Euclidean radius of the octahedron's own planar projection. The published octahedral random-width CDF concerns the coordinate maximum; the present law concerns its coordinate minimum. Direct semantic searches under projected diameter, circumradius, minimum spherical coordinate, crosspolytope, caliper and Feret diameter did not identify a covering prior result. This is a best-evidence scientific comparison, not a guarantee of priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A full closed-form probability distribution, exact density, different sharp endpoint exponents with leading constants, and the conditional equilateral-simplex shape law constitute a natural, coherent description of a classical regular polyhedron's random projected diameter. A finite set of first moments, a face-count probability, or the one-dimensional width law does not recover the same statistic. The spherical triangular conditioning law explains geometrically why near-minimal shadow diameters are quadratically rare rather than merely supplying an isolated numerical coefficient.

## Limitations and residual risks
The proof covers only Haar-uniform planar orthogonal projections of a fixed three-dimensional regular octahedron. Priority is provisional because classical stereological work might state the identical spherical minimum-coordinate law under different terminology, and some related literature was examined only in its relevant mathematical passages or abstract rather than exhaustively. No external mathematician has independently reproduced the analysis. The standalone numerical checker corroborates but does not certify the analytical steps.
