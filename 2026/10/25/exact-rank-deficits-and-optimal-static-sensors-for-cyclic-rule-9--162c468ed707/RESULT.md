# Exact rank deficits and optimal static sensors for cyclic Rule 90

## Finding
Fix any integer \(n\ge3\). On the binary ring \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), consider synchronous Rule 90,
\[
(Fx)_j=x_{j-1}+x_{j+1}\pmod2,
\]
and a nonempty set \(S\) of sites monitored at every integer time \(t=0,1,\ldots,n-1\). The observation operator \(\mathcal O_S\) maps an initial state to all those sensor readings. Choose \(s_0\in S\) and set
\[
g=\gcd\bigl(n,\{s-s_0:s\in S\}\bigr).
\]
For a singleton, this convention means \(g=n\). Then the **exact dimension of the invisible initial-state space** over \(\mathbb F_2\) is
\[
\boxed{\dim\ker\mathcal O_S=
\begin{cases}
\lfloor g/2\rfloor,&n/g\text{ odd},\\
g-1,&n/g\text{ even}.
\end{cases}}
\]
In particular, the initial state is uniquely recoverable if and only if \(g=1\). Thus the sharp minimum is **two stationary sensors**, and two sites separated by \(d\) are sufficient precisely when \(\gcd(n,d)=1\). Every successful pair reconstructs the initial state within the sharp horizon \(\lceil n/2\rceil\) consecutive time samples per sensor (including time zero); fewer samples cannot suffice.

For every \(1\le k\le n\), the exact number of successful **unordered** \(k\)-site sensor sets is
\[
\boxed{N_{n,k}=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\,d\binom{n/d}{k},}
\]
with the convention that out-of-range binomial coefficients vanish. Here \(\mu\) is the number-theoretic Möbius function. In particular, \(N_{n,1}=0\) and \(N_{n,2}=n\varphi(n)/2\), where \(\varphi\) is Euler's totient function.

## Assumptions and scope
The cellular automaton uses binary arithmetic and synchronous updates with cyclic boundary conditions. All initial states in \(\mathbb F_2^n\) are allowed, sensors are fixed sites and noiseless, and no unknown disturbances are added. Observability means **finite-time initial-state uniqueness** given sensor readings, not reconstruction from one spatial snapshot or prediction under noise. The exact nullity concerns times \(0\) through \(n-1\), although a smaller horizon suffices for every observable two-sensor placement.

The expression for \(g\) is independent of the choice of \(s_0\), because the subgroup generated by all differences between sensor sites is unchanged. All indices are interpreted modulo \(n\).

## Proof
Let \(e_s\) denote the standard basis vector of the binary state space, with the usual dot product. As \(F\) is symmetric, the space of sensor observation rows from site \(s\) equals the Krylov space \(K_s=\operatorname{span}\{F^t e_s:t\ge0\}\). For each integer \(1\le j<n/2\), put \(v_{s,j}=e_{s-j}+e_{s+j}\). The vectors
\[
 e_s,\ v_{s,1},\ldots,v_{s,\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}
\]
are linearly independent. For \(t\le\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\), expanding \(F^t e_s\) as signed-step walks shows that the coefficient at both sites at distance \(t\) is one, whereas more distant sites have coefficient zero. Hence the first \(\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor+1\) Krylov vectors triangularly span the displayed set. All later vectors remain in that span: reflection about \(s\) preserves the state, and, if \(n\) is even, the coefficient at the antipodal site is always zero. The latter assertion follows by pairing each walk from \(s\) to its antipode with the walk obtained by reversing every step; no path is paired with itself at positive length, and time zero does not visit the antipode.

It follows that an initial configuration \(x\) is invisible at site \(s\) exactly when
\[
 x_s=0,\qquad x_{s+j}=x_{s-j}\quad\text{for every }j.
\]
In other words, \(x\) is invariant under the reflection \(R_s(j)=2s-j\) and zero at the reflection center. This is the critical single-sensor lemma; it is valid for odd and even \(n\), including the antipodal fixed point for even \(n\).

Translate coordinates so that \(s_0=0\), and write \(D=\{s-s_0:s\in S\}\). The product \(R_sR_0\) is translation by \(2s\); therefore the joint reflection group is generated by \(R_0\) and translations through the subgroup generated by \(\{2s:s\in D\}\). Put
\[
h=\gcd\bigl(n,\{2s:s\in D\}\bigr)=\gcd(n,2g).
\]
Jointly invariant vectors correspond exactly to functions on \(\mathbb Z/h\mathbb Z\) invariant under negation. The sensors additionally force their represented negation orbits to have value zero.

If \(n/g\) is odd then \(h=g\). Every sensor belongs to residue zero modulo \(h\); negation on \(\mathbb Z/g\mathbb Z\) has \(\lfloor g/2\rfloor+1\) orbits, and exactly one is forced zero. Thus the invisible dimension is \(\lfloor g/2\rfloor\).

If \(n/g\) is even then \(h=2g\), the negation involution on \(\mathbb Z/(2g)\mathbb Z\) has \(g+1\) orbits, and both fixed residues \(0\) and \(g\) occur among the sensors. Indeed, because the integers \(\{s/g:s\in D\}\) together with \(n/g\) have gcd one and \(n/g\) is even, at least one \(s/g\) is odd. Hence exactly two orbits are forced to zero, leaving dimension \(g-1\). This proves the rank formula and the equivalence between observability and \(g=1\).

A singleton has \(g=n\), giving a positive nullity for every \(n\ge3\). A pair separated by any unit modulo \(n\) has \(g=1\). Each individual sensor's entire Krylov space is generated by its first \(\lceil n/2\rceil\) temporal rows, as established in the triangular argument; thus an observable pair has full joint rank by that time. With fewer than \(\lceil n/2\rceil\) time samples, two sensors supply fewer than \(n\) scalar equations, so this horizon is optimal.

Finally, for nonempty \(S\), the identity \(\mathbf1_{\{g=1\}}=\sum_{d\mid g}\mu(d)\) gives the census. The condition \(d\mid g\) says exactly that all sensors lie in one residue class modulo \(d\). There are \(d\) such classes, each containing \(n/d\) sites, so the number of \(k\)-sets satisfying it equals \(d\binom{n/d}{k}\). Interchange the finite sums to obtain \(N_{n,k}\). For pairs, this agrees with the elementary count \(n\varphi(n)/2\) of unordered pairs at unit separation.

## Verification
A standalone Python 3 standard-library checker, `verify.py`, independently forms the direct binary transition for Rule 90, computes observation-matrix ranks via Gaussian elimination over \(\mathbb F_2\), and compares them with the gcd formulas. It exhaustively inspects all \(4{,}079\) nonempty sensor subsets for sizes \(3\) through \(11\), compares all \(63\) nontrivial finite-size census entries, checks sharp horizons for all two-site placements in those sizes, and tests \(1{,}520\) reproducible random sensor configurations at larger ring sizes through \(127\).

The expected successful output begins `VERIFY_OK`. Finite checks are corroboration; all-size correctness follows from the reflection-orbit and Möbius arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work
The trace literature (Cervelle–Formenti–Guillon, 2010) studies which temporal words can occur at a cellular-automaton site, primarily in infinite configurations. El Yacoubi and collaborators (2021) provide general observability criteria for finite cellular automata, extending the familiar rank test and considering sensor choices. Such general rank tests do not themselves give the present closed gcd-dependent rank deficit, universal two-sensor classification, minimal paired observation horizon, or Möbius enumeration for finite cyclic Rule 90.

Plénet and collaborators (2022) further study bounded-CA observability with fixed and mobile sensors; the accessible abstract covers general linear and nonlinear criteria, but the complete article could not be read in this examination. Its possibly relevant examples are therefore a specific unresolved priority risk, rather than evidence of either coverage or noncoverage. The present proof is fully self-contained and does not rely on claims of novelty from search nonresults.

## Limitations
The theorem is restricted to synchronous, binary Rule 90, cyclic boundaries, stationary noiseless site sensors, and recovery of the initial state. It does not address sensor noise, nonlinear perturbations, asynchronous updates, higher-dimensional automata, mobile sensors, or observability after discarding all early transient readings. The optimal horizon is established for successful **pairs**; the minimum possible horizon for a given larger sensor set is not classified. An independent historical-priority assessment remains outstanding, especially for potentially overlapping worked examples in the inaccessible 2022 article.
