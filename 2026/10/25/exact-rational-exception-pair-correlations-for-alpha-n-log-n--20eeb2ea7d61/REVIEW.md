# Scientific assessment

## Correctness
**PASS.** The argument separates all short circle differences into disjoint exponential-ratio branches indexed by integers. The zero branch is counted by an explicit threshold on integer gaps. In each positive branch, irrationality of the exponential ratio and Weyl equidistribution give the exact leading count; a summable exponential tail justifies adding infinitely many branches. Positivity, signs, ordered-pair normalization, every fixed window, and the endpoint at zero are treated. The accompanying numerical checks corroborate but do not substitute for the proof.

## Originality
**PASS, subject to the specified residual comparison risk.** The October 2026 almost-everywhere theorem does not cover rational dilations. Earlier logarithmic-sequence work develops strong gap and lattice-process techniques, with potential to recover the integer-dilation subcase, but does not explicitly present the rationally twisted ordered pair law or its invariance under the reduced denominator. An exact non-Poissonian calculation for a different logarithmic sequence also does not imply this identity. Targeted searches of research literature and published mathematical findings located no equivalent stated formula. A broader lattice-superposition theorem might imply the law after a nontrivial reduction and remains a priority risk.

## Value
**PASS.** The new arithmetic exceptional-parameter profile completes a quantitative contrast with the motivating almost-everywhere Poissonian theorem. It identifies an exact function for every nonzero rational parameter, proves denominator universality, and yields explicit non-Poissonian thresholds and cusp locations. The count and its parameter dependence are natural invariants in metric pair-correlation theory, not an arbitrary small numerical slice.
