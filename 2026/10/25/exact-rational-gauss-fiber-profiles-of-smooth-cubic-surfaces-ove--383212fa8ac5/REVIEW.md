# Scientific assessment: rational Gauss fibers of binary cubic surfaces

**Correctness — PASS.** The claimed finite domain consists of all \(2^{20}-1\) nonzero quaternary binary cubic forms. Four explicit coordinate transformations generate the complete projective group, and exhaustive orbit closure gives 141 orbits totaling that domain. Exact four-chart Gröbner-basis unit-ideal tests leave 36 geometrically smooth orbits of size 322,560. Formal first derivatives over \(\mathbf F_2\) define tangent planes at all rational surface points, and orbit-weighted rational Gauss-fiber partitions give the fourteen-row table. A second, separately written derivative evaluator agrees on each smooth representative. Orbit decomposition and smoothness use one primary exact implementation; the second evaluator only corroborates the Gauss calculations.

**Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk.** Karaoğlu's complete classification of binary cubic surfaces supplies the underlying 141 classes, 36 smooth classes, and several point-line invariants, none claimed as new. The submitted contribution instead enumerates the fibers of the *arithmetic Gauss map*. Two explicit surfaces with the same rational points, lines, Eckardt points and double-line counts have different Gauss partitions, proving that this table is not equivalent to Karaoğlu's displayed incidence columns. Das's Frobenius line-marking statistics and the characteristic-two line-triangle classification of Kadyrsizova et al. do not print or directly identify these Gauss partitions. The inaccessible ancillary historical atlas and potential unindexed classifications remain priority risks. The absence of a matching database search result by itself was not used as novelty proof.

**Value — PASS.** Tangency multiplicities over a rational point set are intrinsic incidence data of the Gauss map into the dual projective space and are particularly delicate in characteristic two. The census covers *every* smooth binary cubic surface in fixed coordinates, resolves all rational tangent-plane multiplicity types, and exhibits distinct multiplicity profiles invisible to previously published point/line incidence statistics. The answers are exact natural arithmetic invariants, not arbitrary coefficient slices or routine reparameterizations.

**Limits.** Counts are for scalar-normalized equations in fixed coordinates and cannot be directly read as unweighted isomorphism-class counts. No extension-field or geometric-degree assertion is made. The Gröbner-based and finite-group enumeration certificate must be replayed for external reproduction. A separate independent audit has not been performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
