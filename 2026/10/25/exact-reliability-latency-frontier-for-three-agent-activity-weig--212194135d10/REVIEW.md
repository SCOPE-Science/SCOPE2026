# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The edge probability is calculated directly from first-node and weighted-neighbor selection. First averaging either creates an irreversibly nonreceptive other edge or places all three opinions in a diameter below the confidence threshold. On a successful branch, switch count exactly determines diameter, with a 1-dependent switch process satisfying the strong law. Alternating geometric waiting times prove the conditional expected hitting time. The Cauchy–Schwarz equality case proves the unique interior optimizer when the number of required switches is at least two; the single-switch endpoint exception is expressly excluded. The exact witness satisfies both initial receptivity inequalities and the strict 3-switch tolerance calculation. A separate rational checker passed 988640 assertions over independently enumerated configurations; it does not purport to prove continuous parameter generality.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

Li–Porter (arXiv:2206.09490) specifies the activity-weighted selection mechanism and reports numerical fragmentation and convergence times, not the joint exact three-agent success and conditional-tolerance-time law. Su–Chen–Mei–Bullo (arXiv:2409.01593) studies general convergence, not this selection-conditioned quantitative frontier. Zhang–Chen (2015) gives general convergence-rate estimates for a different asymmetric process; its accessible abstract does not settle this exact discrete three-agent calculation, and complete published priority remains a risk. Elementary cancellation of middle weight, qualitative fragmentation, and a standalone two-edge switch recurrence are *not* asserted as novel. The evaluated joint result is an exact end-to-end reliability–latency characterization and its meaningful optimizer for the smallest path exhibiting competing initial outcomes. Searches of available research records did not reveal a direct covering result; this is not a claim of exhaustive publication priority.

## Value — PASS

The original model highlights increased fragmentation and convergence times caused by heterogeneity, but without an exact finite-network joint design law. The three-person path is the smallest network with distinct possible initial edge choices and lasting confidence fragmentation. Its quantified objective is motivated: probability of a successful consensus and time until a prescribed tolerance, conditioned on success, conflict nontrivially when activities are reallocated. The sharp witness makes conditional latency arbitrarily large even as the consensus probability tends to one, and yields an exact nontrivial positive-weight optimizer; these facts are not merely a reformulation of the edge probability. The finding is intentionally narrow and not presented as a large-network result.
