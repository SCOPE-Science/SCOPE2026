# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Every abelian-square subsequence in a word with exactly two ones either is a positive even zero-run or has both ones in opposite halves. This gives the exact \(i,j,k\) admissibility and the two formulas, checked with two distinct finite enumerations. For the asymptotic theorem, an explicit uniform lattice-to-integral bound reduces the problem to an elementary two-dimensional variational integral. Strict balancing, differentiation of the reduced integral, and compactness prove its unique optimizer and rigidity. The verifier checks 343 complete length spectra by generating all subsequences and 4913 independent total counts by difference bands. Boundary cases including adjacent ones are covered. Finite calculations support, but do not replace, the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Richmond–Shallit counts abelian squares in the full free monoid (2008, arXiv:0807.5028), not the distinct strings embedded in one source word. Fici–Mignosi–Shallit, Section 3 (2017, arXiv:1701.00948), uses the same sparse-one family for **contiguous** factors and quadratic growth; this cannot imply our complete *scattered* spectrum and cubic optimizer. The 2022 Fici–Puzynina survey discusses abelian square factors and binomial/subsequence equivalence but no identical formula was found in the materially read sections. Searches of additional available published-record summaries returned no logically dominating claim. An equivalent derivation in another unindexed source remains a genuine residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The restriction to two minority-symbol occurrences is the first natural nontrivial sparse family in which the same abelian-square target can be realized at multiple scattered positions. The full length-resolved distribution gives a reusable benchmark for subsequence-repetition statistics. The sharp cubic coefficient \(2/147\), unique ratio \(2:3:2\), and contrast with the known quadratic *contiguous-factor* construction are structural information, not an isolated numerical or merely notational variant. No generalization to words with more minority symbols is claimed.
