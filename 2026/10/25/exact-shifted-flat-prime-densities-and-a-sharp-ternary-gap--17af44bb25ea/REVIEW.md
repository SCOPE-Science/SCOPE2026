# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The fixed-odd-shift density follows from exact local residue enumeration at each odd prime square, the Chinese remainder theorem, the prime number theorem in fixed progressions, and a two-range tail bound using Brun--Titchmarsh and elementary integer counting. The sharp split follows by removing the factor at three and proving the base Euler product exceeds one half. The packaged finite checker tests the key arithmetic independently, without being used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS with residual priority risk
Broughan and Zhou (2010) prove the unit-shift case and explicitly describe the odd-shift dichotomy as numerical and requiring further investigation. The parameterized prime-divisor correction and sharp gap are not stated in that full-text source. Literature and published research-record searches found no dominating explicit result. Mirsky's related classical single-shift work and a newer specialized shifted-squarefree preprint were examined only to the extent accessible. A more general unindexed sieve theorem could subsume the claim, and the method is closely related to the 2010 proof.

## Value — PASS
The exact law settles the source's explicit mathematical observation, supplies sharp separated ranges for two infinite classes of odd shifts, and shows optimal extremal behavior along primorial shifts. This is a structural theorem about prime densities, not a fixed-cutoff enumeration.

## Scientific limitations
Shifts are fixed and odd; no growing-shift uniformity or effective least-prime bound is claimed. Some potentially relevant later work was available only by abstract. Independent review remains necessary before publication.
