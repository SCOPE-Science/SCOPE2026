# Exact six-voter coherence census with sharp integer margins and a four-bloc obstruction

## Finding
For a labelled electorate \(W=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}\), take all subsets of size at least \(4\) as winning, all subsets of size at most \(2\) as losing, and choose exactly one winner from each complementary pair of triples. Every such choice is a maximal standard social-decision frame; there are \(2^{10}=1024\) choices. Of these, exactly \(332\) admit a strict positive probability representation and \(692\) do not. The coherent choices fall into six \(S_6\)-orbits of sizes \(6,6,20,60,60,180\); the incoherent choices fall into seven orbits of sizes \(12,20,90,90,120,180,180\).

Every incoherent choice contains a balanced family of four admitted triples; there are precisely \(30\) possible such four-triple families which contain no complementary pair, and every incoherent frame has incoherence index exactly \(4\). Every coherent frame admits an explicit integral probability representation with weights \(7+w_i\), where \(\sum_i w_i=0\) and \(\max_i |w_i|\le3\). Exactly \(20\) coherent frames have a representation of this centered form with \(\max_i|w_i|\le1\); the same \(20\) and no others admit bound \(2\), while all \(332\) admit bound \(3\).

The specific ten-triple example printed in Moss and Pedersen (2026), Example 5.4, includes \(\{1,2,3\},\{1,4,5\},\{2,5,6\},\{3,4,6\}\). Each voter occurs in exactly two of those four triples. Thus the printed instance has index \(4\), not the \(6\) asserted for it in Proposition 5.5 and Proposition B.23. This does not affect the independent ten-point construction in their Proposition B.24 or the later unbounded-index results.

## Assumptions and scope
A **selector** \(F\) contains precisely ten three-element subsets of \(W\), one from each pair \(\{A,W\setminus A\}\) with \(|A|=3\). The frame is \(M_F=F\cup\{A\subseteq W:|A|\ge4\}\). **Coherent** means there is a positive probability measure \(\mu\) on \(W\) for which \(M_F=\{A:\mu(A)>1/2\}\). **Balanced** means that each voter occurs equally often among a chosen finite family of triples. Here a four-triple balanced family has each voter in exactly two triples. The incoherence index is the minimum length of a majority-block sequence violating the Moss–Pedersen coherence condition. The quantitative integer-radius assertion concerns only centered, **integral** perturbations \(w\) about the uniform baseline, not arbitrary real-valued representations or minimal total integer quotas.

Fix the reproducible encoding: represent \(A\subseteq W\) by \(\sum_{i\in A}2^{i-1}\). Sort all complementary pairs \((a,63\oplus a)\) having \(a<63\oplus a\) by their first entry. A selector mask is the integer \(s\in\{0,\ldots,1023\}\); bit \(j\) chooses the larger member of complementary pair \(j\), and a zero bit chooses the smaller member. Under this convention the six coherent orbit representatives are \(0,1,3,7,15,19\), with respective orbit sizes \(6,60,180,60,6,20\). The seven incoherent representatives are \(20,21,28,29,58,126,220\), with sizes \(90,180,180,90,120,20,12\).

## Proof
**Reduction to exact finite predicates.** There are \(\binom63/2=10\) complementary pairs of triples, so a selector is determined by ten independent bits. Any probability representation must assign more than half the mass to each of the selected triples. If four selected triples contain every voter exactly twice, then the sum of their masses equals \(2\), contradicting the strict inequality that their four masses sum to more than \(2\). Hence such a balanced quadruple is a rigorous obstruction. Since there are no complementary selected pairs, a length-two obstruction is impossible. The odd-length coherence obstructions are impossible for standard frames on an even voter set with empty tie family, as follows also from the direct sum-of-cardinalities argument. Therefore a four-block witness has minimum index \(4\).

**Finite certificate of exhaustiveness.** The standalone `checker.py` enumerates all \(\binom{20}{4}\) distinct quadruples of triples, retains those with two occurrences of each of the six voters, discards those containing a complementary pair, and obtains \(30\) admissible forbidden quadruples. Each such quadruple fixes four selector bits. It then loops through **every** integer selector \(0\le s<1024\), testing inclusion of each forbidden quadruple by integer masks; exactly \(692\) masks are obstructed, leaving exactly \(332\). Repeated triples need not be searched separately: in any balanced four-term list a repeated triple forces two appearances of its complement in the remaining positions, impossible for a selector. This is an exhaustive finite computation, not sampling.

**Sufficiency and sharp weight bound.** Independently loop over all \(w_1,\ldots,w_5\in[-3,3]\cap\mathbb Z\), put \(w_6=-\sum_{i=1}^{5}w_i\), and retain vectors with \(|w_6|\le3\) and with nonzero triple sums for all ten complementary pairs. Each retained vector induces the winning choice on each pair by the sign of its triple sum. The exhaustive integer loop produces exactly the same \(332\) selectors, so every unblocked selector is represented. Define integer weights \(q_i=7+w_i\). Their sum is \(42\) and their minimum is at least \(4\). Each two-point set weighs at most \(20<21\); every four-point set therefore weighs at least \(22>21\); and a triple weighs greater than \(21\) exactly when its centered sum is positive. Thus \(\mu_i=q_i/42\) is positive and represents exactly the intended frame. Repeating the complete loop with bounds \(1\) and \(2\) gives exactly \(20\) selectors in each case. Because bounded solution sets are nested, \(3\) is the sharp universal bound and \(312\) coherent selectors genuinely need radius \(3\) in this normalization.

**Relabelling classes.** For each of the \(720\) permutations of the voter labels, transport every selected triple and decode the selector mask. Partitioning the exactly enumerated coherent and incoherent masks under these actions yields the six and seven orbit sizes stated above. This step is also independently reproducible from `checker.py`.

**Published six-point configuration.** The ten triples in Moss–Pedersen, Example 5.4, are \(123,124,126,135,145,146,156,245,256,346\), where each digit denotes a voter. Four of them, \(123,145,256,346\), have incidence sum \((2,2,2,2,2,2)\). The frame admits no complementary selected pair, so its incoherence index is precisely \(4\). This directly contradicts the printed assertion that this specific family contains no balanced four-subfamily.

## Verification
Execute `python3 checker.py` with Python \(3\) and its standard library; the final line starts with `ALL_CHECKS_OK`. The checker uses integer arithmetic only, directly checks every one of the \(2^{10}\) selectors, verifies all \(332\) proposed integral realizations against **all** \(64\) subsets, and computes all relabeling orbits. Its classification is complete because the proof fixes finite exhaustive search spaces and the script explicitly traverses every member. For example, the range for the integer certificate is a complete \(7\)-ary search for each of the first five coordinates, with the sixth uniquely forced by the zero-sum condition.

## Relationship to prior work
Moss and Pedersen, *The Measurable Majority* (first posted June \(22\), \(2026\), arXiv:2606.23853) introduce maximal standard majority frames, prove their probability-representation criterion, and print the six-point example; those definitions and general criteria are prior results. Blanco, *On a Taylor–Zwicker Construction for Balanced Families and a Conjecture of Moss and Pedersen* (first posted June \(15\), \(2026\), arXiv:2606.16172), proves large-family trade constructions. Moss and Pedersen's follow-up (June \(24\), \(2026\), arXiv:2606.25954) proves general unboundedness of the coherence index. The older work of Freixas, Freixas and Kurz on weighted complete simple games, and Gvozdeva and Slinko on trade robustness, provides broader contextual criteria. The present quantitative complete \(1024\)-case census, orbit partition, sharp centered-integer perturbation bound and explicit four-block check of the particular printed example are the claimed new content; the general strict-majority representation criterion and trade obstruction itself are not claimed new.

## Limitations
This theorem concerns the labelled six-voter **maximal standard** subfamily, not all simple games, larger electorates, or arbitrary nonmaximal frames. The six orbit counts may also be recoverable by filtering older full tabulations of six-variable self-dual threshold functions; a historical tabulation comparison remains a priority risk. A related 2021 article about weighted voting games presents global isomorphism tables but its full text was not accessible in the current comparison. The correction concerns the exact set family printed in a particular 2026 example, not any theorem about existence of six-index frames on ten voters or about unbounded index in general.
