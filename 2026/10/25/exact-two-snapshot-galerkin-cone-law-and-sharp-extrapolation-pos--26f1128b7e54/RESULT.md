# Exact two-snapshot Galerkin cone law and sharp extrapolation positivity failure

## Finding
For a one-parameter affine symmetric positive-definite linear system with a parameter-independent right-hand side, the two-snapshot Galerkin reduced solution belongs to the positive cone generated by its two independent snapshots **if and only if** the parameter lies between the snapshot parameters. This statement holds in every ambient dimension and requires neither monotonicity of the parameter derivative nor a coordinatewise positivity assumption.

In particular, if both snapshots are componentwise nonnegative, the Galerkin approximation is componentwise nonnegative throughout the sampled parameter interval. The interpolation guarantee is sharp for physical (componentwise) positivity: already in dimension three, a strictly positive full-order solution and two strictly positive snapshots can produce a Galerkin vector with a negative coordinate at an extrapolated parameter. Dimensions at most two cannot exhibit this phenomenon when all exact solutions under consideration are positive.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(I\subseteq\mathbb R\) be an interval, \(A(y)=A_0+yA_1\in\mathbb R^{m\times m}\) be real symmetric positive definite for each \(y\in I\), and let \(b\in\mathbb R^m\setminus\{0\}\) not depend on \(y\). Choose \(s,t\in I\) with \(s<t\). Define exact snapshots \(u_s=A(s)^{-1}b\), \(u_t=A(t)^{-1}b\), and assume that they are linearly independent. Set \(U=[u_s,u_t]\), \(G(y)=U^\mathsf{T}A(y)U\), and the two-snapshot Galerkin solution \(\widetilde u(y)=Uc(y)\), where \(G(y)c(y)=U^\mathsf{T}b\).

Define positive numbers
\[
a=b^\mathsf{T}u_s,\qquad d=b^\mathsf{T}u_t,
\qquad K_s=a\,u_t^\mathsf{T}A(s)u_t-d^2,
\qquad K_t=d\,u_s^\mathsf{T}A(t)u_s-a^2.
\]
Then \(K_s,K_t>0\) and \(\Delta(y)=\det G(y)>0\), and for every \(y\in I\),
\[
\boxed{c_s(y)=\frac{t-y}{t-s}\frac{K_s}{\Delta(y)},\qquad
c_t(y)=\frac{y-s}{t-s}\frac{K_t}{\Delta(y)}.}
\]
Consequently \(\widetilde u(y)\in\{\alpha u_s+\beta u_t:\alpha,\beta\ge0\}\) exactly for \(y\in[s,t]\). The word *cone* is essential: the coefficients need not sum to one, and this is not a claim of convex-hull membership. The original matrix and parameter interval may be arbitrary under the stated hypotheses.

## Proof
**1. Strict Gram positivity.** The vectors \(u_s,u_t\) are independent and \(A(s)\) is positive definite. By the strict Cauchy–Schwarz inequality in its associated inner product,
\[
K_s=(u_s^\mathsf{T}A(s)u_s)(u_t^\mathsf{T}A(s)u_t)
 -(u_s^\mathsf{T}A(s)u_t)^2>0,
\]
because \(u_s^\mathsf{T}A(s)u_s=b^\mathsf{T}u_s=a\) and \(u_s^\mathsf{T}A(s)u_t=b^\mathsf{T}u_t=d\). The analogous argument using \(A(t)u_t=b\) gives \(K_t>0\). For each \(y\in I\), \(G(y)\) is positive definite because \(U\) has full column rank; therefore \(\Delta(y)>0\).

**2. Exact coefficients.** Put \(\alpha=(t-y)/(t-s)\) and \(\beta=(y-s)/(t-s)\). The affine identity \(A(y)=\alpha A(s)+\beta A(t)\) holds even for extrapolated \(y\). By the snapshot equations and symmetry,
\[
G_{11}(y)=\alpha a+\beta h_t,\quad
G_{12}(y)=\alpha d+\beta a,\quad
G_{22}(y)=\alpha h_s+\beta d,
\]
where \(h_s=u_t^\mathsf{T}A(s)u_t\) and \(h_t=u_s^\mathsf{T}A(t)u_s\). The reduced right-hand side equals \((a,d)^\mathsf{T}\). Inverting the two-by-two matrix gives
\[
\Delta c_s=aG_{22}-dG_{12}=\alpha(ah_s-d^2)=\alpha K_s,
\qquad
\Delta c_t=dG_{11}-aG_{12}=\beta(dh_t-a^2)=\beta K_t.
\]
This proves the displayed formulas with all denominators strictly positive. Since the snapshot representation is unique, both coefficients are nonnegative exactly when \(\alpha,\beta\ge0\), which is equivalent to \(s\le y\le t\). Strict positivity of both coefficients holds on the open interval, with the expected endpoint interpolation \(\widetilde u(s)=u_s\) and \(\widetilde u(t)=u_t\).

**3. Sharp componentwise extrapolation failure in dimension three.** Take \(I=(-1,1)\) and
\[
A(y)=I_3+y\,\operatorname{diag}(1/10,1/2,9/10),\quad
b=(1,3,10)^\mathsf{T},\quad s=0,\quad t=1/2.
\]
All diagonal entries of \(A(y)\) are positive throughout \(I\), so the full-order solution \(A(y)^{-1}b\) is strictly positive at every parameter. The snapshots are
\[
u_s=(1,3,10)^\mathsf{T},\qquad
u_t=(20/21,12/5,200/29)^\mathsf{T},
\]
which are independent and strictly positive. At \(y=-9/10\), exact rational elimination yields
\[
G(y)=\begin{pmatrix}1243/50&38998/2175\\38998/2175&86297768/6622875\end{pmatrix},\quad
\Delta(y)=404522672/165571875>0,
\]
\[
c(y)=\frac1{25282667}(523575200,-570826575)^\mathsf{T},
\]
\[
\widetilde u(y)=\left(
-\frac{140484100}{176978669},
\frac{200741820}{25282667},
\frac{1299017000}{25282667}
\right)^\mathsf{T}.
\]
Thus its first coordinate is negative, while the full-order vector is
\[
u(y)=(100/91,60/11,1000/19)^\mathsf{T}>0
\]
coordinatewise. Here the parameter derivative is even positive definite, so the failure does not rely on an indefinite affine perturbation.

**4. Minimality of dimension three.** With a one-dimensional ambient space and strictly positive exact snapshots, any Galerkin approximation in their one-dimensional span equals a positive scalar multiple of the snapshot: the numerator \(u_s^\mathsf{T}b=u_s^\mathsf{T}A(s)u_s\) and reduced stiffness \(u_s^\mathsf{T}A(y)u_s\) are both positive. The same reasoning holds in dimension two when the snapshots are dependent. If two snapshots are independent in dimension two, their span is the whole space, and the Galerkin solution equals the exact full-order solution, which is positive by hypothesis. Therefore a positivity failure despite all exact solutions and both snapshots being positive requires dimension at least three, achieved by the construction.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library Python file `verify.py` uses exact rational arithmetic. It checks the exact three-dimensional Gram determinant, coefficient vector, reduced vector and full-order solution, together with 9,250 Galerkin-orthogonality and coefficient-sign identities across 250 deterministic rational diagonal SPD families. Run `python verify.py`; it prints `PASS: exact 3D counterexample; 9250 exact rational Galerkin/sign identities`. These computations are independent cross-checks of the algebra; the dimension-uniform theorem is proved by the preceding identities and strict Gram inequalities, not by finite tests.

## Relationship to prior work
Hannukainen and Toresen (2026), equations (8), (15), and Theorem 5.1, study the affine symmetric coercive finite-element system and Galerkin Lagrange-snapshot reduction. They establish quantitative approximation-error bounds, not signs of its two-snapshot Galerkin coefficients or sharp interpolation/extrapolation cone membership. Quarteroni, Rozza and Manzoni (2011) discuss reduced-basis Galerkin approximation, stability and posterior error estimation; these do not imply the displayed exact coefficient signs. Maday, Patera and Turinici (2002) study uniform error convergence for this class; available abstract-level material does not resolve whether an equivalent two-snapshot sign observation occurs in that paper. The present finite-dimensional sign theorem follows directly from the snapshot equations and affine dependence rather than a generic error bound.

## Limitations
The guarantee concerns **exactly two independent snapshots**, affine dependence, a **parameter-independent right-hand side**, and symmetric positive-definite systems. It does not establish a sign theorem for three or more snapshots, nonsymmetric systems, Petrov–Galerkin methods, varying right-hand sides, or POD bases with different coefficient interpretations. A negative snapshot coefficient outside the training interval does not force a negative physical component for *every* problem; the three-dimensional construction establishes that such loss is possible, not universal. The theorem does not quantify approximation error or imply a discrete maximum principle for arbitrary reduced subspaces. Originality is assessed against inspected sources; equivalent formulae might occur in uncatalogued or older reduced-basis literature. No independent audit has been conducted.
