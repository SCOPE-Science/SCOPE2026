# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The signed cycle-matching inclusion–exclusion identity is valid because adjacent forbidden directed edges are incompatible for \(2\ell\le a\). The removed-edge exponential integral equals \(e^{at}(\cosh(\ell t)-1)/t^2\), while an untouched coordinate contributes \((e^{at}-1)/t\). The cycle matching generating polynomial has the claimed two transfer-matrix roots. The ratio bound \(d/b^2\le1/8\) prevents eigenvalue collision on the real axis. The centered roots are even analytic functions, giving exact centering; differentiation gives the finite variance and its strict inflation factor. Dominance of the larger root proves the Gaussian limit by convergence of moment-generating functions. The differentiable globally finite limiting log-mgf, exposed interior slopes and bounded support prove the large-deviation principle. The checker reproduces 32 exact rational moment cases, 32 matching-count identities, three independent tilted-volume quadratures and a 750000-point Monte Carlo diagnostic. No computation is treated as a proof for untested dimensions.

## Originality — PASS, with bibliographic qualifications
The motivating full paper by Kantarcı Oğuz, Özel and Ravichandran, arXiv:2211.08382v1, §6 Proposition 6.8, already proves the unweighted cycle-matching volume trace, which is expressly excluded from the novelty claim. The full source was reviewed around its chainlink definition, sections on rank and Ehrhart symmetries, vertex/facet geometry and matching volume proof. It does not state a tilted-volume formula, an exact coordinate-sum variance, a strict dimension-monotone inflation law or a continuum Gaussian/LDP statement. Kantarcı Oğuz and Ravichandran, arXiv:2112.00518v1, treat discrete fence-poset rank-polynomial symmetry and unimodality, which does not imply a variance for the uniform continuous polytope. General monomer–dimer transfer-matrix methodology can underlie analogous limit theorems, but it does not by itself specify this model's explicit sharp variance sign and finite-size correction. Related public mathematical-record searches found a chainlink Euclidean-inradius theorem, mathematically distinct from a volume-law statistic. Absence of a direct hit is not a proof of world-wide uniqueness; a differently named finite-range Gibbs-gas result remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The problem is motivated by the source's complementary-section volume symmetry: the distribution across all diagonal sections is an intrinsic canonical-volume observable not specified by the total volume. The new weighted transform controls that distribution, its exact finite-size variance and all-order fluctuations. The strictly positive and dimension-monotone excess over the ambient cube's variance is a structural dependence effect for every permissible nonzero link, not a recomputation of a known table or a narrow isolated numerical constant. The explicit Gaussian scaling law and large-deviation rate characterize both typical and atypical sum-coordinate slices throughout an infinite family.

## Closest sources and limitations
The closest source is Proposition 6.8 of arXiv:2211.08382v1: its unweighted trace is the \(t=0\) specialization of the displayed transform; the signed-matching proof idea is credited. The related circular-fence rank polynomial paper arXiv:2112.00518v1 is discrete and concerns a different probability distribution. General one-dimensional finite-state transfer methods make asymptotic normality plausible but do not cover the specialized formula without further calculation. The theorem is restricted to the homogeneous cyclic family and fixed parameters for asymptotics. A mathematically equivalent prior variance expression under different stochastic terminology cannot be ruled out absolutely.
