# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The unit-edge normalization gives tetrahedral surface area \(\sqrt3\). The last-entry argument reduces distances in the opposite face exactly to a nearest-site problem with three reflected copies of the fixed vertex. The three Voronoi regions are triangles and the associated radial bounds are elementary. The integral expressions for each incident and opposite subtriangle were reconstructed independently and their endpoint ranges coincide; direct quadrature and boundary identities agree. The proof is global for all radii and does not infer a continuum theorem from finite samples.

## Originality
**PASS (best-knowledge primary-text comparison).** Geodesic trajectories from a tetrahedral vertex are described in Davis–Dods–Traub–Yang (2015); full shortest-distance formulas appear in Fontenot et al. (2022); tetrahedral cut loci are analyzed by Davis (2023); and Freitas–Krejčiřík (2014) discusses geometric area/cut-locus inequalities. Neither the inspected tetrahedral sections nor their identified conclusions give the stated complete piecewise radial area law, its exact cancellation before the edge-length threshold, or the corresponding surface-distance probability distribution. Broad pointwise distance results make the present radial calculation feasible, but do not already state the exact area evaluation. Targeted database and literature searches found no explicit covering formula. The material residual risk is an unindexed equivalent radial integral or geodesic-ball tabulation.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** Uniform-area intrinsic-distance laws and geodesic-ball growth are natural global invariants of a polyhedral metric, used to relate metric radius, cut loci, and area. The result gives a complete tractable benchmark at a cone-point singularity, including a sharp transition from the exact cone-area law at the unexpectedly late radius \(1\), the endpoint eccentricity \(2/\sqrt3\), and a nonanalytic but continuously differentiable level-length profile. This is more than a check of pointwise distances: the essential structural contribution is the three-site Voronoi area decomposition and its cancellation.

## Scope and limitations
Restricted to a fixed *vertex* on the regular tetrahedron. Shortest-path formulas for general source points, geodesic complexity, and arbitrary convex polyhedra remain outside the claim. Bibliographic assessment cannot rule out all unindexed equivalents.
