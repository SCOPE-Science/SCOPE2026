# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

On every balanced sign vector the star edge matrix acts as the identity; the point-exchange identity follows from the strictly monotone equation \(h\sin\delta=2\delta\). Derivatives at both orbit points agree exactly, so the squared spectral factors give the complete linear stability test. The unique threshold of \((m+1)\delta\cot\delta=1\), large-star asymptotics, and weight-persistence implicit-function argument establish all asserted quantifiers. The numerical checker is nonexhaustive and is used solely as supplementary evidence. The theorem is restricted to this explicitly constructed branch.

## Originality — PASS under inspected evidence

The primary Huygens-network paper analyzes small-step gradient dynamics, Morse–Smale properties, and a three-leaf star, not exponentially many high-step attracting flips on even-leaf stars. Known synchronized star flips lie in the all-equal eigendirection and have a different parameter scaling, so neither those nor the broader complete-graph phase-network statements imply this balanced-sign family. The present proof depends on both balanced-sign cancellation and the collective eigenvalue \(m+1\), and introduces the sharp simultaneous stability window and robust exponential coexistence. Searches for star-specific high-step phase-flip branches found no directly covering record. The absence of a match is not proof of historical uniqueness, and earlier high-step oscillator literature remains a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact family exhibits exponentially growing *dynamically distinct open attraction basins*, rather than only a large count of algebraic solutions. Despite their number, the shared attracting parameter window vanishes at a computable inverse-leaf rate. Persistence under independent edge-weight perturbations shows this is genuine multistability, not a fragile symmetry artifact. These facts supply a motivated high-step complement to the source's small-step dynamics.
