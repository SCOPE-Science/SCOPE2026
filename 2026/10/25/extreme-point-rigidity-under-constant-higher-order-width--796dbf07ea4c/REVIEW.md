# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The support-function identity implies positivity and domination of the curvature measure by \(c\,d\theta\). Two-valued densities force identical summand curvature measures. Conversely, if a simplex field has two positive entries on positive measure, a three-part moment-null perturbation preserves both its orbit-sum and its first harmonic, produces distinct convex summands, and disproves extremality. The absolutely convergent Fourier area formula gives strict concavity modulo translations. Fixed perimeter, Steiner normalization, Blaschke selection and closedness give existence of an area minimizer, hence the two-valued necessary condition. All steps are analytic and cover nonsmooth bodies. The explicit three-rotation lens was independently checked by finite-resolution Fourier integration; numeric checks are not relied upon as proofs.

The two-rotation Kallay criterion is known and is not claimed. Hynd's 2025 discussion identifies its \(k=2\) scope. Gao–Martini–Zhang (arXiv:2404.05267) treats smooth curvature flows, not all extreme bodies or area minimizers. Pan–Zhang–Chao (2016, DOI 10.3934/dcdsb.2016012) concerns a different biwidth notion. Similar local Fourier or fixed-harmonic results do not characterize the full infinite-dimensional feasible class. The closest incompletely inspected primary work is Deyan Zhang, DOI 10.1007/s13366-015-0252-8, whose abstract mentions other characterizations of higher-order width; its full text remained inaccessible, leaving a genuine priority risk. The available evidence and claim-level comparisons did not identify covering work.

The result gives the complete Minkowski extreme-point structure for all higher-order width constraints, not just generic or smooth members. The two global first-moment closure constraints mean extremality is not a purely pointwise simplex exercise; constructing compatible decompositions requires a nontrivial moment-null perturbation. The global least-area problem posed by Ou and Pan is reduced to an extremal curvature selection condition without pretending to solve its exact optimal value. A concrete nonsmooth three-rotation lens supplies a new benchmark outside finite-degree Fourier families.
