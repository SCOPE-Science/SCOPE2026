# Fifty-five minimal induced spheres generate integral homology of the Paley-eleven directed flag complex

## Finding
Let \(T\) be the Paley tournament with vertex set \(\mathbb F_{11}\) and arcs \(i\to j\) when \(j-i\) is a nonzero quadratic residue, namely a member of \(Q=\{1,3,4,5,9\}\). Let \(K=\operatorname{dFl}(T)\), the directed flag complex consisting of all transitive subtournaments, with each face ordered by its tournament orientation. For a vertex subset \(W\), write \(K[W]\) for the induced subcomplex.

**Theorem.** There are precisely 55 five-element subsets \(W\) with \(H_2(K[W];\mathbb Z)\ne0\); every corresponding induced complex is the boundary of a triangular bipyramid, with 5 vertices, 9 edges, and 6 triangles. All other induced subcomplexes on at most five vertices have zero second integral homology. These 55 supports form a free transitive orbit of the affine tournament automorphism group \(\mathbb F_{11}\rtimes Q\). Under inclusion, their oriented fundamental two-cycles generate
\[
H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{10}\oplus\mathbb Z/2.
\]
The displayed abstract group is known from Govc's earlier homotopy computation; its generation by the 55 *minimum-vertex-support* induced sphere cycles is the result here. In particular the integral torsion class is expressible as a linear combination of these sphere classes, even though every source sphere has free second homology.

## Assumptions and scope
Only the explicitly defined eleven-vertex Paley tournament and its oriented directed-flag chain complex over \(\mathbb Z\) are considered. A nonzero induced second-homology class on a support \(W\) means nonzero \(H_2(K[W];\mathbb Z)\); the support need not be nontrivial after inclusion. The assertion of generation refers to the group homomorphism from the direct sum of the 55 integral sphere homologies to \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\), not to an independent basis or a splitting. The group \(Q\) acts multiplicatively on \(\mathbb F_{11}\); translations and multiplication by elements of \(Q\) preserve all arcs.

## Proof
**1. Complete chain model and small supports.** For each \(d=0,1,2,3\), enumerate all \((d+1)\)-subsets that form transitive tournaments and put their unique directed linear orders on the corresponding faces. There are no higher faces, and the resulting cell vector is \((11,55,110,55)\). Deleting the \(i\)-th entry contributes the signed boundary coefficient \((-1)^i\). This defines integer matrices \(\partial_2\) of size \(55\times110\) and \(\partial_3\) of size \(110\times55\), with \(\partial_2\partial_3=0\).

Enumerate every induced subset of size at most five. For each, compute its second mod-two Betti number by the exact equality \(\beta_2=f_2-\operatorname{rank}\partial_2-\operatorname{rank}\partial_3\). There is no such homology for any support on at most four vertices. Among all \(\binom{11}{5}=462\) five-subsets, exactly 55 have \(\beta_2=1\), and 407 have \(\beta_2=0\). All 55 exceptional induced subcomplexes have face vector \((5,9,6)\), no tetrahedra, and every edge in exactly two triangles; their connected oriented triangle chain is the fundamental cycle of the five-vertex bipyramid sphere. Direct face incidence determines this as the triangular-bipyramid boundary. The remaining 407 have zero integral second homology as well: their induced complexes are directed-flag complexes of tournaments on five vertices, hence torsion-free by the earlier complete sphere-wedge classification for tournaments on at most ten vertices (Govc, Proposition 3.5), so vanishing over \(\mathbb F_2\) implies vanishing integrally.

**2. Affine orbit.** A representative exceptional support is \(W_0=\{0,1,2,3,7\}\). The 55 images \(aW_0+b\), with \(a\in Q\) and \(b\in\mathbb F_{11}\), are distinct, and the verifier compares this set to the complete exception census. Thus they form one free transitive orbit. For every support, the signed sum of its six oriented triangles is obtained by enforcing cancellation on each of its nine edges. It is a primitive integral two-cycle in the global \(110\)-triangle chain lattice.

**3. Integral kernel coordinates and generation.** The complete directed one-skeleton has 11 vertices and 55 edges. Use the star at vertex zero as a spanning tree and express graph cycles in the 45 non-tree-edge coordinates. The resulting \(45\times110\) presentation of \(\partial_2\) admits a sequence of 45 unit pivots with integer elementary row and column operations. The verifier explicitly tracks the unimodular column change \(V\) and its inverse \(V^{-1}\), confirms their product is the identity, and checks that the transformed boundary has exactly \([I_{45}\ 0]\) as its 45-row matrix. The final 65 coordinates consequently identify \(\ker\partial_2\) as an integral lattice. Denote the induced matrices for the tetrahedral boundaries and for the 55 primitive sphere cycles by \(T_3\) (size \(65\times55\)) and \(C_5\) (size \(65\times55\)), respectively.

Exact integer Smith calculations in the standalone verifier yield
\[
\operatorname{SNF}(T_3)\text{ has nonzero invariant factors }\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{54},2.
\]
Since the target lattice has rank 65 and the image rank is 55, the quotient has ten free generators. The \(65\times110\) block matrix \([T_3\mid C_5]\) has 65 nonzero Smith factors, **all equal to one**. It follows first that \(\ker\partial_2/\operatorname{im}\partial_3\cong\mathbb Z^{10}\oplus\mathbb Z/2\), and secondly that \(\operatorname{im}\partial_3+\langle C_5\rangle=\ker\partial_2\) as **integral sublattices**, not merely after tensoring with \(\mathbb Q\). Taking the quotient by \(\operatorname{im}\partial_3\) gives precisely the claimed surjection from the 55 sphere homologies. The matrix claim is independently reproducible by the supplied standalone script.

## Verification
Run `python verify.py` after installing the two dependencies in `requirements.txt`. The script does not load precomputed matrices: it reconstructs each transitive face, enumerates every small induced subcomplex, verifies all 55 spheres and their affine orbit, checks integer chain identities, produces the unimodular kernel presentation, and computes both Smith forms over \(\mathbb Z\). A successful run prints `VERIFY_OK_PALEY11_MINIMAL_SPHERE_GENERATORS`. The accompanying output records the observed exact invariant factors.

## Relationship to prior work
Pálvölgyi and Gyárfás define Paley tournaments using square differences in finite fields in *Domination in transitive colorings of tournaments* (public preprint first posted 19 February 2013), Section 3. Govc, *Computing Homotopy Types of Directed Flag Complexes* (first posted 9 June 2020), Proposition 3.4, already identifies the homotopy type of the unique eleven-vertex doubly regular tournament's directed flag complex as \(M(\mathbb Z/2,2)\vee\bigvee^{10}S^2\). Thus neither the homotopy type nor the abstract integral homology is presented as new. The present induced-support census, free affine orbit and the **integral generation by all minimum-size sphere cycles** are finer inclusion-level facts and do not follow from the previously determined homotopy type without additional combinatorial comparison. No directly equivalent statement was identified in targeted research-record or paper comparisons.

## Limitations
The calculation concerns this one canonical eleven-vertex tournament, not arbitrary tournaments, and does not establish an integral affine-equivariant splitting of homology, a classification of every individual generator relation, or uniqueness beyond the checked five-vertex induced supports. All numerical calculations are finite and exhaustive; the result is a fully finite theorem, not an extrapolation. The search for equivalent work is not a proof of publication uniqueness, and unindexed equivalent calculations remain possible. An independent external mathematical audit has not been performed.
