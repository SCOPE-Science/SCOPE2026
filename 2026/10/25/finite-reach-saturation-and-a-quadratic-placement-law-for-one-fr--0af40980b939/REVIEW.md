# Mathematical review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The solution of the forced linear oxygen balance is reconstructed on both sides of the impulse. The unimodal pre- and post-aeration branches are explicit. On the only competitive placement interval, the maximum upstream deficit is strictly increasing with position while the maximum downstream deficit is strictly decreasing, making their unique crossing the global optimum. The full-peak envelope yields a unique critical reach length; the finite-horizon scalar equation applies strictly below it. Implicit differentiation proves strict movement of the site, and the identity for curvature at the downstream peak yields the quadratic saturation coefficient. Boundaries \(T\downarrow0\), \(T=T_c\), and \(T>T_c\) are accounted for. The numerical checks are corroboration only.

## Originality

**PASS, scoped to this mathematical theorem, with residual risk.** The 1925 Streeter–Phelps model is not new. In the accessible full 1977 Rinaldi–Soncini-Sessa report, Property A concerns minimizing aerator cost under lower oxygen-standard constraints, not minimizing the largest deficit at a fixed multiplicative efficiency. Their constraint geometry motivates, but does not state or imply without further analysis, a unique finite-reach minimax site, exact critical length and a quadratic convergence coefficient. The closely titled 1975 Koivo–Phillips source could not be fully inspected, so potential prior equivalent coverage remains an explicit risk. Broader elementary minimax and control literature may contain a reduction; absence from search results is not proof of novelty.

## Value

**PASS.** Reach length is a natural design constraint, and a fixed proportional deficit removal is a well-defined device-response idealization. The theorem supplies a complete, sharp answer to when endpoint oxygen level ceases to control site selection, makes the unique optimal placement computable from a scalar equation, and quantifies the unusually second-order sensitivity as a river reach approaches the saturation threshold. This is more informative than a textbook plot of the unmodified oxygen sag or a routine numerical calibration; it does not purport to optimize real equipment cost.

## Closest literature and limitations

Streeter and Phelps (1925), Bulletin 146, establishes the oxygen-demand and reaeration balance. Rinaldi and Soncini-Sessa (1977), RR-77-007, particularly pp. 7–19, analyze aerator impulses, prove their cost-minimizing placement Property A, and use dynamic programming; their objective and device policy differ from the fixed-fraction finite-reach minimax problem here. Koivo and Phillips (1975), IFAC World Congress, is a plausible equivalent-prior risk because its title describes optimizing river oxygen concentration with in-stream aeration; only the bibliographic description was inspectable. The full paper was not available through accessible lawful sources. Assumed homogeneous transport, no photosynthesis or sediments, initial zero deficit, strictly ordered positive rates, fixed proportional reset, and single intervention limit physical applicability. No independent audit has been performed.
