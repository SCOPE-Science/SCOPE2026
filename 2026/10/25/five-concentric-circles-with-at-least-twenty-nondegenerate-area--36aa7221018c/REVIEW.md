# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The critical equations reduce to \(A\cos\delta_i=B\cos\delta_5\). Four disjoint multiplier intervals yield the sign patterns listed in the result; six further configurations occur at \(\lambda=1\). The exact-rational verifier checks all eight strict endpoint signs, and the Hessian is nonsingular at each of the twenty exhibited points. The result is a lower bound, not a claim of exhaustive enumeration.

## Originality

PASS, subject to the stated literature-access limits. The closest primary source is Siersma's *Extremal Area of Polygons sliding along Curves* (arXiv:2211.13978v1). Its Section 3.4 states the concentric-circle critical equations, proves the three- and four-circle cases, and explicitly leaves the general case open; it does not state the five-circle radius tuple or the twenty-point obstruction. Searches of published-finding corpus and primary-literature titles/abstracts found related but different problems, including perimeter critical points for concentric circles and a three-dimensional linkage area function.

## Value

PASS. This is a concrete counterexample to a natural perfect-Morse question for a geometrically defined area function, rather than a routine formula or a numerical-only observation. It gives an explicit radius tuple and a finite analytic proof of at least twenty nondegenerate critical points, exceeding the sixteen required for a perfect Morse function on \(\mathbb T^4\).
