# Scientific review: five-primary torsion in the Paley-25 neighborhood complex

## Correctness
PASS. The complete complex is reconstructed from its 25 open neighborhoods, including all 93,350 nonempty simplices. A deterministic exhaustive procedure confirms 43,546 legal elementary collapses. On the remaining 6,258-face complex, all 2,967 Morse pairs are checked against 24,126 oriented Hasse incidences and a complete acyclicity sort. The signed integral Morse matrix is regenerated from 262 critical three-cells and 61 critical four-cells, then checked against the packaged certificate. Its Smith invariants are 56 units and one factor five; the four-dimensional kernel has rank four. Independent direct mod-two and mod-five simplicial boundary calculations agree with the predicted torsion jump. The recurrence and verifier operate on exactly the domain asserted, without an extrapolation from sampled parameters.

## Originality
PASS, with bibliographic limitations. The close published records for Paley orders 13 and 17 were inspected in full and concern strictly different complexes: a two-sphere wedge and order-three degree-two torsion, respectively. They do not imply the order-five degree-three torsion for a non-prime square field. Kahle's general random-graph results and Matsushita's fundamental-group identification do not compute these integral homology groups. Searches for Paley(25), quadratic-residue graph of order 25, conference-graph parameters (25,12,5,6), Paulus graph, aliases of the neighborhood complex and exact torsion or Betti data revealed no directly dominating prior theorem or tabulated entry. Unindexed equivalent computations remain a residual risk; search silence is not a proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The first square-field Paley example after the previously investigated small prime orders exhibits a qualitatively new primary torsion phenomenon, including its exact integral multiplicity and a separate fourth-homology group. Integral five-torsion is invisible to rational Betti data and is not determined by the strongly regular graph parameters or any generic random-neighborhood theorem. The complete computation is a useful finite benchmark and boundary case for studying prime-primary torsion in highly symmetric graph neighborhood complexes. It is not presented as an infinite-family conjecture or homotopy decomposition.

## Limits and audit status
The result is a finite exact integral homology classification, not an assertion about general Paley graphs or an independent audit. Relevant alternate aliases and the possibility of inaccessible equivalent work remain specifically acknowledged.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
