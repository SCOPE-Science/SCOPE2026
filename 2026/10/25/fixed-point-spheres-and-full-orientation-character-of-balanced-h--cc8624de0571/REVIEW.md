# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The geometry-to-orbit-simplex correspondence is explicit. For the fixed complex, its \(n\) minimal nonfaces are incident stars and the nerve of the Alexander-dual simplex cover is a simplex boundary; integral Alexander duality gives a single homology generator in degree \(\binom n2-1\). The special \(n=2,3\) simple-connectivity issues are treated separately; higher values have a complete two-skeleton. Hurewicz plus homology Whitehead promotes integral homology to homotopy. The full orientation character comes from natural Alexander duality, its ambient sign twist, and the row-column nerve join. Signed exhaustive finite tests agree at \(n=2,3,4\).

**Originality — PASS, with residual uncertainty.** Singh (2020) supplies the ordinary sphere theorem but no fixed-point space dimensions under bipartition interchange. Reiner–Roberts (2000), Theorem 3.3, already supplies rational side-preserving representations of generalized chessboard complexes; no restricted representation is claimed as new. The closest searched modern complete-bipartite Morse-complex results concern a different simplicial construction. No checked source provided the all-\(n\) geometric fixed-point spheres or full bipartition-exchange extension. A differently named or unindexed pre-existing equivalence remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** This is an infinite-parameter structural fixed-point classification for canonical higher-matching homotopy spheres: the dimensions \(\binom n2-1\) vary quadratically and are not implied by the nonequivariant sphere theorem or the side-preserving homology representation. It also determines the complete orientation action under graph automorphisms and gives a genuine mod-four topological dichotomy for full symmetry quotients. Its main contribution is the fixed-point homotopy theorem, not merely a sign calculation.

**Scientific limits.** No arbitrary-subgroup fixed points, genuine equivariant sphere splitting, or integral quotient torsion is claimed. The checks through \(n=4\) do not replace the uniform proof, and originality remains literature-relative.
