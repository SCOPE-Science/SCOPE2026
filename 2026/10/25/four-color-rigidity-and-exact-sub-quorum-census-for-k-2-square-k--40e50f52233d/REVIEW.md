# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The row multiplicity inequality \(2a_{r,c}\ge s_r-1\) is proved from the exact closed neighborhood. Three row colors imply three singleton vertices with matching partners of the same colors, so no globally new color can occur in the opposite row. Two colors per row bound the remaining cases by four. In an optimum four-coloring, the two row palettes must be disjoint, and summing the weak-majority inequalities forces balanced color multiplicities and forbids any matched pair of simultaneously selected vertices. Both necessity and sufficiency are proved with quantifiers for all \(n\ge4\), and the small orders have explicit separate arguments. The factorial enumeration counts unordered two-way color splits and disjoint column supports; its EGF follows formally. Direct exhaustive finite verification checks the graph's actual neighborhoods rather than merely restating the proof.

## Originality — PASS (bounded historical risk)
The full accessible primary paper *Sub-quorum colorings of graphs* gives the definitions, general bounds, complete multipartite families, path-based grids, and hypercubes. It does not give the two matched-clique family, the four-color rigidity structure, or the exact optimum census. The earlier quorum-coloring literature is distinguished from partial sub-quorum coloring. Actual published-finding corpus claim searches for line graphs, complete graph prisms, matched cliques, rook graphs, alliance partitions and four-color balanced-support conditions identified no implication covering the claim. The closest rook-graph records concern homotopy or other coloring invariants. A 2013 foundational paper was not fully inspected, and historical priority under alternative terminology remains uncertain; absence of a search hit is not a proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result solves a natural dense graph family, improving the generic degree bound by establishing a constant value as order grows. The all-optimum rigidity theorem and exact EGF record nontrivial structure even where the optimum itself is constant. The motivating mathematics is local-majority partial-coloring behavior under matched clique coupling, not an arbitrary small numerical example.

## Limitations and closest literature
The complete graph prism \(K_2\square K_n\) differs from the Cartesian product of two paths and from complete multipartite graphs; the family restrictions are essential. Finite enumeration is corroboration rather than infinite proof. Full historical verification and independent audit are still outstanding.
