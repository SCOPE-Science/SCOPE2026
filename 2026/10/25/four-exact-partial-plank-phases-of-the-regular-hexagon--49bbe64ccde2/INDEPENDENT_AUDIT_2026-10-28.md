# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For a unit-circumradius hexagon, the six projected vertices at ±A, ±B, ±C, with A=B+C, yield a central cap, quadrilateral cap and triangular cap. The cap formulas agree at switching surfaces. The change u=sqrt(3)tan(theta) reduces angular minimization to derivative signs in the three valid regimes; the quadrilateral switching function T(u) decreases strictly, and the triangle derivative changes sign at the second oblique branch. Evaluating these exact minimizers gives all four stated normalized area fractions and the two distinct strictly oblique width intervals. Centering follows from even concave section lengths. The existing general planar partial-plank theorem justifies reducing arbitrary finite plank families to one width-W plank; it is not newly proved here. At the three transition widths the formulas and minimizing normal agree. No claim is made that every optimal multi-plank family is a singleton.

## originality

PASS

The strong all-planar single-plank reduction is acknowledged as prior work; the independently optimized, piecewise rational/quadratic captured-area function and two separated oblique normal transitions for the regular hexagon are not entailed by existence alone. Related hexagonal norm, enclosing-parallelogram and translative-hull records optimize different functionals. This is a qualified best-of-knowledge priority judgment, not proof that no older hexagon strip formula exists.

## value

PASS

This is a natural one-parameter shape-restricted extremal problem once all-planar single-plank optimality is known. The complete sharp area law and genuinely disjoint oblique orientation phases are geometric structure, not a trivial normalization or a few numerically sampled widths.
