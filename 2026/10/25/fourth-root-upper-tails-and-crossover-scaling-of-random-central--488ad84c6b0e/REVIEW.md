# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The central-section formula is obtained by an explicit Jacobian and disk-chord integral; its baseline and maximal-section transition agree with Dirksen's known formula and Theorem 3 (2016). A direct derivative factorization reduces uniqueness to strict monotonicity of a positive integral. The normalized fourth-order coefficient is \(-12\sqrt3/5\), and the nonzero maximizing cosine satisfies \(c_*^2=(5/36)(3-k^2)+O((3-k^2)^2)\). The crossover follows from compact-uniform Taylor expansion, a strict-maximizer localization bound, and the exact length of a quartic sublevel set. The embedded numerical checker was run and independently evaluates plane sections; no numerical experiment is substituted for an asymptotic proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence

Dirksen (arXiv:1602.07854, 2016) already proves the sharp cylinder-section maximizer and supplies the exact area formula, so those are explicitly treated as prior knowledge. Its relevant full-text sections (Lemma 2.1, Theorem 3, Section 3.1) do not assert near-maximal Haar probabilities, the fourth-root tail exponent, or the two-sided window function. Finch (arXiv:1212.5946, 2012) treats surface-area and mean-width calculations for cones/cylinders, not these central-section tail events. Semantically related records on cylinder planar **shadows**, double-cone sections, box section combinatorics, and cross-polytope sections have different random variables or domains, and do not imply the stated quartic central-cylinder probability. Residual risk: uninspected secondary literature might already compute these tail asymptotics.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The critical exponent and its coefficient measure how many randomly oriented cutting planes nearly attain an exact geometric extremum. The two-parameter crossover proves a nontrivial change from a centered quartic peak to a pair of oblique maxima, with a continuous explicit scaling function. This gives a natural, quantitatively useful refinement of the already known maximizer bifurcation, beyond a routine restatement of that threshold.

## Boundaries

Only central sections of right circular cylinders in dimension three are covered. The crossover assumes a fixed real scaling parameter. Numerical checks corroborate rather than prove the universal assertions; independent review and definitive priority verification remain outstanding.
