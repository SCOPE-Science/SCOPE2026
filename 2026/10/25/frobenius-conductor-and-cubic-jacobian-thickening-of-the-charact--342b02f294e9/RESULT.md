# Frobenius conductor and cubic Jacobian thickening of the characteristic-three universal cubic discriminant

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(3\). Let \(D\subset\mathbb A^3_{a,b,c}\) be the reduced discriminant of the universal monic cubic \(f(T)=T^3-aT^2+bT-c\). Then
\[
D=\operatorname{Spec}A,\qquad A=k[a,b,c]/(a^2b^2-b^3-a^3c).
\]
Its normalization is \(\operatorname{Spec}B\cong\mathbb A^2_{a,\lambda}\), with
\[
A\hookrightarrow B=k[a,\lambda],\quad b\longmapsto-a\lambda,\quad c\longmapsto\lambda^3+a\lambda^2.
\]
The intrinsic singular scheme is defined in \(A\) by \(J=(a,b)^3\), while the normalization conductor is exactly \(\mathfrak c=(a,b)^2\). More explicitly, as a \(k[a,c]\)-module,
\[
B/A\cong (k[a,c]/(a))\oplus(k[a,c]/(a^2)),
\]
with summands represented by \(\lambda\) and \(\lambda^2\), respectively. The common reduced support is the singular line \(L=V(a,b)\cong\mathbb A^1_c\). Over this line, the normalization restricts (on reduced supports) to the purely inseparable degree-three morphism \(\mathbb A^1_{\lambda}\to\mathbb A^1_c\), \(c=\lambda^3\).

## Assumptions and scope
All assertions concern the **affine monic chart** of the binary-cubic discriminant over an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(3\). The conductor is \(\{x\in A:xB\subseteq A\}\), and the intrinsic Jacobian ideal is generated by the partial derivatives of the reduced hypersurface equation in the ambient smooth \(\mathbb A^3\), modulo its equation. The quotient is asserted as a module over \(R=k[a,c]\), not as an \(A\)-algebra or a direct sum of \(A\)-modules. No claim is made about the complete projective discriminant or its compactification.

## Proof
**1. Discriminant and parameterization.** Reducing the standard discriminant of \(T^3-aT^2+bT-c\) modulo three yields \(\Delta=a^2b^2-b^3-a^3c\). A repeated root \(\lambda\) and a remaining root \(\mu\) give
\[
f(T)=(T-\lambda)^2(T-\mu),\quad a=\mu-\lambda,\quad b=-a\lambda,\quad c=\lambda^3+a\lambda^2.
\]
The polynomial \(\Delta\) is primitive and linear in \(c\) over \(k[a,b]\), because \(\gcd(a^3,b^2(a^2-b))=1\). Hence \(\Delta\) is irreducible. Substitution gives \(\Delta=0\). After inverting \(a\), the equation recovers \(\lambda=-b/a\), so the induced homomorphism \(A\to B\) is injective and birational. Moreover \(\lambda\) satisfies the monic equation \(\lambda^3+a\lambda^2-c=0\), making \(B\) finite over \(A\). Since \(B\) is a polynomial ring and normal, it is the integral closure of \(A\).

**2. Explicit normalization module.** Put \(R=k[a,c]\). Because \(\lambda^3=c-a\lambda^2\), the ring \(B\) is free as an \(R\)-module with basis \(1,\lambda,\lambda^2\). The image of \(A=R[b]\) is exactly
\[
A=R\cdot1\ \oplus\ aR\cdot\lambda\ \oplus\ a^2R\cdot\lambda^2\ \subset B.
\]
Indeed, \(b=-a\lambda\), \(b^2=a^2\lambda^2\), and multiplication by \(b\) preserves the displayed module: in particular \(b(a^2\lambda^2)=-a^3c+a^4\lambda^2\). This proves the indicated quotient-module decomposition, including the exponents \(1\) and \(2\) of its \(a\)-torsion.

**3. Conductor.** Write any element of \(A\) uniquely as \(h=h_0+a h_1\lambda+a^2 h_2\lambda^2\) with \(h_i\in R\). An element \(h\) lies in the conductor precisely when both \(h\lambda\) and \(h\lambda^2\) lie in \(A\), since \(1,\lambda,\lambda^2\) generate \(B\) over \(R\). Reducing powers by \(\lambda^3=c-a\lambda^2\), the coefficient of \(\lambda^2\) in \(h\lambda\) is \(a h_1-a^3h_2\), and its \(\lambda\)-coefficient is \(h_0\). The coefficient of \(\lambda^2\) in \(h\lambda^2\) is \(h_0-a^2h_1+a^4h_2\), and the \(\lambda\)-coefficient is \(a^2ch_2\). The module membership conditions are therefore equivalent to \(a\mid h_1\) and \(a^2\mid h_0\); no restriction is imposed on \(h_2\). Thus
\[
\mathfrak c=a^2 B=(a^2,ab,b^2)A=(a,b)^2A,
\]
where \(a^2B\subset A\), and on \(B\) the extended ideal is simply \((a^2)\).

**4. Intrinsic singular scheme and Frobenius.** Direct differentiation in characteristic three gives
\[
\Delta_a=-ab^2,\qquad \Delta_b=-a^2b,\qquad \Delta_c=-a^3.
\]
As \(b^3=a^2b^2-a^3c\) in \(A\), these derivatives generate exactly the ideal \(J=(a,b)^3\). Hence its radical is \((a,b)\) and the reduced singular locus is \(L=\operatorname{Spec}k[c]\). In the normalization \(a=b=0\) becomes \(a=0\), and there \(c=\lambda^3\). Over \(k(c)\), the polynomial \(U^3-c\) is irreducible and inseparable, so the induced reduced conductor map has degree \(3\) and inseparable degree \(3\).

## Relationship to prior work
The discriminant and the repeated-root resolution are classical. Fehér--Némethi--Rimányi (2003) study coincident-root loci and their equivariant classes over \(\mathbb C\), including the discriminant, but their characteristic-zero formulas do not imply the explicit modular conductor and Jacobian ideals here. Shimada (2006) analyzes characteristic-three degenerations of dual/discriminant varieties and establishes a general purely inseparable degree-three phenomenon on a critical locus; the occurrence of Frobenius degree three is not itself claimed as a new general principle. The contribution here is the exact simultaneous determination, on the monic universal cubic discriminant, of its normalization quotient, square conductor and cubic intrinsic Jacobian thickening, and their precise relation to the Frobenius conductor cover.

## Limitations
The result is restricted to characteristic \(3\) and the monic affine chart. No independent audit has been performed. Alternative historical presentations of the modular discriminant or a general conductor formula could imply these identities; the searches and inspected papers did not resolve all such possibilities. The proof determines the intrinsic hypersurface Jacobian scheme, not the possibly different scheme defined by the resultant of \(f\) and \(f'\) before reduction.
