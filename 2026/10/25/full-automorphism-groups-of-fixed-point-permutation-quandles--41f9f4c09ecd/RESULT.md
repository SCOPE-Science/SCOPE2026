# Full automorphism groups of fixed-point permutation quandles

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge3\), let \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), let \(S_n\) be the symmetric group acting on \([n]\) by ordinary functions, and put
\[
P_n=\{(i,\sigma)\in[n]\times S_n:\sigma(i)=i\}.
\]
Give this set the binary operation
\[
(i,\sigma)\triangleright(j,\tau)
 = (\tau(i),\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}).
\]
Define \(p(k)\) to be the number of partitions of the nonnegative integer \(k\), with \(p(0)=1\). Then
\[
|P_n|=n!,\qquad \operatorname{Inn}(P_n)\cong S_n,
\qquad \boxed{\operatorname{Aut}(P_n)\cong
S_n\times (C_2)^{p(n-2)-p(n-3)}}.
\]
The elementary abelian factor is not merely an abstract order formula: its canonical generators independently exchange the two fixed coordinates within each conjugacy class of permutations with exactly two fixed points. In particular the groups for \(n=3,4,5,6\) have orders \(6,48,240,2880\), respectively.

The definition of this fixed-point subquandle and the ambient Cayley-type quandle are established by Kai [1]. The full automorphism group above, rather than the already-known subquandle construction, is the claim addressed here.

## Assumptions and scope

Permutations are composed in the standard functional order: \((\alpha\beta)(i)=\alpha(\beta(i))\). The notation \(\operatorname{Inn}\) means the subgroup generated by right translations \(R_y(x)=x\triangleright y\); \(\operatorname{Aut}\) means all bijections preserving the quandle operation. A conjugacy class in \(S_n\) corresponds to a partition of \(n\), and an element has exactly two fixed points precisely when its cycle type is \((1,1)\) together with a partition of \(n-2\) with all parts at least two. For all \(n\ge3\), such types number \(p(n-2)-p(n-3)\).

Every \(n\)-element quandle embeds into \(P_n\): for a labeled quandle, each right translation fixes its own label, so Kai's embedding into the ambient action quandle has image in this fixed-point subquandle. This embedding property is recalled for motivation, not claimed as a new theorem.

## Proof

**1. Right translations and a canonical labeled subset.** For any \((j,\tau)\in P_n\), its right translation is the diagonal permutation action
\[
T_\tau(i,\sigma)=(\tau(i),\tau\sigma\tau^{-1})
\quad ((i,\sigma)\in P_n).
\]
Since \(n\ge3\), every transposition of \([n]\) fixes some point; hence each transposition occurs as the second coordinate of an element of \(P_n\). Thus the right translations generate all diagonal actions \(T_\tau\), giving a group isomorphic to \(S_n\): faithfulness follows from the action on
\[
D=\{(i,\mathrm{id}): i\in[n]\}.
\]
The set \(D\) is intrinsically definable as the elements of \(P_n\) whose right translation is the identity, because the action of \(T_\sigma\) on \(D\) is exactly \(\sigma\). Hence every quandle automorphism preserves \(D\).

**2. Reduction to the kernel of the action on \(D\).** For each \(f\in S_n\), the map
\[
F_f(i,\sigma)=(f(i),f\sigma f^{-1})
\]
is a quandle automorphism, with restriction \(f\) on \(D\). Let \(\psi\) be any automorphism. Composing with one such \(F_f^{-1}\), we may assume \(\psi\) fixes \(D\) pointwise. The quandle identity \(\psi R_y\psi^{-1}=R_{\psi(y)}\), restricted to \(D\), forces the second coordinate of \(\psi(i,\sigma)\) to be precisely \(\sigma\), because \(R_{(i,\sigma)}|_D=\sigma\). Therefore for each \(\sigma\), \(\psi\) permutes the finite fiber
\[
P_{n,\sigma}=\{(i,\sigma):i\in\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)\}
\]
and can be written \(\psi(i,\sigma)=(b_\sigma(i),\sigma)\). Since \(\psi\) conjugates each \(T_\tau\) to itself (both have the same restriction to \(D\)), it commutes with the whole diagonal \(S_n\)-action. Thus
\[
b_{\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}}(\tau(i))=\tau(b_\sigma(i))
\quad(\tau\in S_n,\ i\in\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)).
\]

**3. Centralizer rigidity.** Write \(m=|\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)|\). The centralizer \(C_{S_n}(\sigma)\) contains every permutation of the \(m\) fixed points, extended by the identity on the remaining points. Its induced action on \(\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)\) is the full \(S_m\). Equivariance therefore requires \(b_\sigma\) to lie in the center of \(S_m\). The center is trivial unless \(m=2\), when it is \(C_2\); for \(m=0\), the fiber is empty. Consequently \(b_\sigma\) is the identity except possibly when \(\sigma\) has exactly two fixed points, in which case it is either the identity or their transposition.

**4. Independent choices, existence and product structure.** The displayed equivariance condition transports \(b_\sigma\) across each conjugacy class. Choosing to swap the two fixed points for *every* permutation in any one conjugacy class with exactly two fixed points defines a bijection of \(P_n\). It commutes with each \(T_\tau\), preserves every second coordinate, and is consequently a quandle automorphism: if \(x\triangleright y=T_{\sigma_y}(x)\), then \(\psi(x\triangleright y)=T_{\sigma_y}\psi(x)=\psi(x)\triangleright\psi(y)\). Choices for distinct conjugacy classes have disjoint support and commute. Every automorphism in the kernel arises uniquely this way by Steps 2–3. There is one such class for each partition of \(n-2\) without parts of size one. The number of these partitions is \(p(n-2)-p(n-3)\). Finally every flip is conjugacy-invariant, so it commutes with every \(F_f\). This proves the claimed direct product, not merely a semidirect product.

The cardinality follows independently: for each of the \(n\) labels there are \((n-1)!\) permutations fixing it.

## Verification

The standalone dependency-free script `check_pointed_quandle.py` constructs \(P_n\) and its operation table using function composition. It checks the quandle axioms at \(n=3,4\), exhaustively enumerates all \(6!\) bijections of \(P_3\) to confirm that exactly six preserve the operation, and verifies all \(6,48,240\) distinct predicted automorphisms for \(n=3,4,5\), respectively, against every pair of elements. The script prints `VERIFY_OK` on success. The group classification for arbitrary \(n\) follows from the proof, not from finite testing.

## Relationship to prior work

Kai [1], Sections 3.5 and 4, defines the fixed-point subquandle of a group-action quandle and establishes its general Cayley-type embedding theorem; Proposition 4.4 classifies ambient diagonal orbits, and Proposition 4.3 computes the inner group of the larger quandle. These statements explain the setting but do not give the full automorphism group of this fixed-point subquandle. Bardakov, Nasybullov and Singh [2] determine automorphisms in distinct constructions, notably conjugation quandles and cocycle extensions; their Theorem 7.3 gives a subgroup of the automorphism group of an extension and does not imply the complete kernel calculation in Steps 2–4. Elhamdadi, MacQuarrie and Restrepo [3] tabulate automorphism groups of all six-element quandles, which already covers the \(n=3\) instance but not the infinite family. The new structural feature is the exceptional two-fixed-point centralizer and its independent partition-indexed outer involutions.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns the fixed-point subquandle \(P_n\), **not** the full action quandle \([n]\times S_n\). The finite checker corroborates the first three values only; there is no machine formalization of the infinite argument. Earlier order-six automorphism tables cover the small \(n=3\) instance. Unindexed equivalences phrased as automorphisms of group-action fixed-point bundles or augmented quandles are a residual literature-priority risk.
