# Full bipartition and particle-swap homology of two-point complete-bipartite configurations

## Finding

For every pair of integers \(p,q\geq 3\), the full rational homology representation of \(S_p\times S_q\times C_2\) on the ordered collision-free two-point configuration space \(F_2(K_{p,q})\) has an explicit multiplicity-free decomposition in degrees one and two. The first two factors independently permute the vertices in each bipartition class, while \(\tau\in C_2\) exchanges the particle labels.

Let \(A_m=\ker(\mathbb Q^m\xrightarrow{\mathrm{sum}}\mathbb Q)\), let \(W_m=\bigwedge^2 A_m\cong S^{(m-2,1,1)}\), and set \(T_m=S^{(m-2,2)}\) for \(m\geq4\), with \(T_3=0\). Denote external tensor products for \(S_p\times S_q\) by \(\boxtimes\), and indicate particle-exchange eigenvalue with a superscript \(+\) or \(-\). Then

\[
H_0(F_2(K_{p,q});\mathbb Q)\cong \mathbb Q,
\quad
H_1(F_2(K_{p,q});\mathbb Q)\cong (A_p\boxtimes A_q)^+\oplus(A_p\boxtimes A_q)^-,
\]
\[
H_2(F_2(K_{p,q});\mathbb Q)\cong
(W_p\boxtimes T_q)^+\oplus (T_p\boxtimes W_q)^+
\oplus (W_p\boxtimes W_q)^-\oplus(T_p\boxtimes T_q)^-.
\]

Every rational homology group above degree two vanishes. For an automorphism \((\sigma,\pi)\in S_p\times S_q\), let \(f_\sigma,f_\pi\) be the numbers of fixed vertices, and let \(t_\sigma,t_\pi\) be the numbers of transpositions appearing as disjoint two-cycles in their cycle decompositions. The complete characters are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\chi_1(\sigma,\pi)&=2(f_\sigma-1)(f_\pi-1),
&\chi_1(\sigma,\pi,\tau)&=0,\\
\chi_2(\sigma,\pi)&=(f_\sigma^2-3f_\sigma+1)(f_\pi^2-3f_\pi+1),
&\chi_2(\sigma,\pi,\tau)&=-(2t_\sigma-1)(2t_\pi-1).
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular, \(b_1=2(p-1)(q-1)\), \(b_2=(p^2-3p+1)(q^2-3q+1)\), and particle interchange has second-homology trace \(-1\) for every \(p,q\ge3\). The restriction to the diagonal \(S_n\subset S_n\times S_n\) when \(p=q=n\) recovers the previously studied balanced representation, but the stated full product-group action and all unequal bipartition sizes are additional information.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph \(K_{p,q}\) has two explicitly distinguished vertex classes of sizes \(p\) and \(q\), with a length-one edge between every pair of opposite-class vertices. Its realization is a one-dimensional CW complex. The ordered configuration space is \(F_2(K_{p,q})=\{(x,y)\in |K_{p,q}|\times|K_{p,q}|: x\ne y\}\), with no collisions at vertices or elsewhere. Throughout, \(p,q\) are integers at least three, and homology and representations use rational coefficients unless an integral statement is specifically attributed to the literature. In the balanced case, the theorem concerns the distinguished-subclass-preserving group \(S_p\times S_q\) and particle swap, not the additional automorphism interchanging the graph's two color classes. The notation \(S^{\lambda}\) means the ordinary irreducible rational Specht representation for partition \(\lambda\); a summand containing \(T_3=0\) is omitted.

## Proof

**Step 1: equivariant first homology.** Farber and Hanbury prove that \(K_{p,q}\) is *mature* whenever \(p,q\ge3\), meaning that the inclusion \(F_2(K_{p,q})\hookrightarrow |K_{p,q}|^2\) induces an isomorphism on integral first homology [1]. The inclusion is natural under both independent vertex permutations and particle exchange. Orient every graph edge from the first vertex class to the second. Its cellular edge space is \(\mathbb Q^p\otimes\mathbb Q^q\), and the kernel of the incidence map is canonically \(H_1(K_{p,q};\mathbb Q)\cong A_p\boxtimes A_q\). The degree-one homology of the product of two graphs is the direct sum of two copies of this cycle space. Particle exchange interchanges the two copies. Over \(\mathbb Q\), their diagonal and anti-diagonal subspaces therefore give exactly the displayed \(H_1\) decomposition, including its particle-swap eigenvalues.

**Step 2: the equivariant deleted-product chain model.** The finite cubical subcomplex \(D_2(K_{p,q})\) is generated by products of graph cells whose closed supports are disjoint. It deformation retracts onto \(F_2(K_{p,q})\) [1]. This construction is natural under cellular graph automorphisms and particle interchange; the deformation-retract model can therefore be used for the rational equivariant Euler characteristic. There are no chains above degree two. Orient graph edges from the first vertex class to the second, and orient an ordered pair of edges by its product orientation. In degree two, interchanging the coordinate factors contributes a minus sign.

Fix \((\sigma,\pi)\) and abbreviate \(x=f_\sigma\), \(y=f_\pi\). The signed traces on cubical chains in dimensions zero, one and two are, respectively,

\[
\operatorname{tr}(C_0)= (x+y)(x+y-1),\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(C_1)=2xy(x+y-2),\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(C_2)=xy(x-1)(y-1).
\]

Indeed, a fixed zero-cell is an ordered pair of distinct fixed vertices; a fixed one-cell is one of two coordinate positions holding a fixed oriented edge and a fixed vertex not on that edge; a fixed two-cell is an ordered pair of fixed edges with disjoint endpoints.

For the composite of \((\sigma,\pi)\) with particle exchange, abbreviate \(a=t_\sigma\), \(b=t_\pi\). A fixed zero-cell consists of two vertices interchanged by the graph automorphism, giving \(2(a+b)\) such cells. No one-cell can be fixed because coordinate exchange swaps the dimensions of its factors. A fixed two-cell consists of two graph edges exchanged by the automorphism and disjoint in their endpoints; this requires a two-cycle in both vertex classes. There are \(4ab\) ordered pairs, each with the negative orientation imposed by exchanging two one-dimensional factors. Thus

\[
\operatorname{tr}(C_0)=2(a+b),\qquad \operatorname{tr}(C_1)=0,\qquad\operatorname{tr}(C_2)=-4ab.
\]

**Step 3: homology character.** The space is connected, hence \(H_0=\mathbb Q\) with trivial action. Apply the equivariant Euler–Poincaré identity \(\chi_0-\chi_1+\chi_2=\operatorname{tr}(C_0)-\operatorname{tr}(C_1)+\operatorname{tr}(C_2)\), using the already established \(H_1\) character. Simplification gives the displayed two formulas for \(\chi_2\) and, by setting the group element to the identity, both Betti numbers. The dimension of the cubical model proves vanishing above degree two.

**Step 4: irreducible decomposition.** The standard symmetric-group decompositions are

\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2 A_m\cong\mathbf 1\oplus A_m\oplus T_m,
\qquad \bigwedge^2 A_m=W_m,
\]

where \(T_3=0\). The two associated characters, for a permutation with \(f\) fixed points and \(t\) two-cycles, satisfy

\[
\chi_{T_m}+\chi_{W_m}=f^2-3f+1,
\qquad \chi_{T_m}-\chi_{W_m}=2t-1.
\]

(The second identity follows by subtracting the standard symmetric- and exterior-square character identities, with \(\chi_{A_m}=f-1\).) Taking the \(\tau=+1\) and \(\tau=-1\) components of \(H_2\) as half the sum and half the difference of the computed characters yields \(W_p\boxtimes T_q\oplus T_p\boxtimes W_q\) on the positive eigenspace and \(W_p\boxtimes W_q\oplus T_p\boxtimes T_q\) on the negative eigenspace. Over \(\mathbb Q\) all these characters identify genuine representations of the finite group, because ordinary irreducible characters form a basis and the representations are semisimple. For \(m\ge4\), \(W_m\) and \(T_m\) are distinct irreducibles; at \(m=3\), only \(W_3\) survives. Thus every displayed external tensor summand is a distinct irreducible for the full stated group. This proves the decomposition and the multiplicity-free assertion.

## Verification

The accompanying standard-library program `verify.py` constructs the deleted-product cellular chains independently from the graph's vertices and oriented edges; computes mod-two boundary ranks; calculates signed fixed-cell traces for every conjugacy-class pair of permutations with and without particle exchange; and, for selected small graphs, computes the dimensions of the two particle-swap eigenspaces in second homology. A complete execution checks ten parameter pairs from \((3,3)\) to \((6,6)\) and 880 group-action character instances and prints `COMPLETE_BIPARTITE_TWO_POINT_CHARACTERS_VERIFIED 880`. These exhaustive *finite* tests do not establish the theorem for all parameter pairs; the general proof above does.

## Relationship to prior work

Maciążek and Sawicki [2], initially publicly posted on 2018-06-07, study homology and particle statistics for graph configuration spaces, explicitly discussing the complete bipartite graph \(K_{3,3}\) and its low-particle configuration topology. Their work motivates the present bipartite configuration and exchange-symmetry question but does not state the full \(S_p\times S_q\times C_2\) representation classification. Farber and Hanbury [1] establish the mature-graph property and integral Betti numbers of ordinary two-point configurations on complete bipartite graphs. Their theorem supplies the key first-homology input but does not identify the independent vertex-permutation action on second homology.

Ramos and He Yun [3] analyze the diagonal \(S_n\)-action on configurations over the balanced graph \(K_{n,n}\), with particle-swap refinements and a stable character classification (Theorem 3.16 and Table 3). Those prior balanced diagonal representations are genuine overlap: they are restrictions of the current classification and must not be presented as new. Their published theorems do **not** determine the independent \(S_p\times S_q\)-action or the family \(p\ne q\); a diagonal character generally does not determine an external tensor representation. The sink-configuration literature [4] permits collisions at designated vertices, unlike the ordinary collision-free space considered here.

## Limitations

Only two labeled, collision-free particles are considered, only over complete bipartite graphs with both sides at least three, and only the bipartition-preserving graph automorphism group together with label exchange. The theorem does not classify additional side-interchanging automorphisms when \(p=q\), nor fundamental groups, cohomology rings, or higher-particle spaces. The new representation decomposition is rational; although relevant integral homology groups are known to be torsion-free [1], no claim about integral equivariant lattice is made. The finite verification is not an infinite proof. A mathematically equivalent representation statement in unindexed or differently worded literature remains a possibility notwithstanding the source comparisons.
