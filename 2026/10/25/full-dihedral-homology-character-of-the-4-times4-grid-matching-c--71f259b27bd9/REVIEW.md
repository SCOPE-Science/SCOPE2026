# Scientific assessment: dihedral homology of the square-grid matching complex

## Correctness — PASS
The claim concerns the complete rational \(D_4\)-action on the ordinary matching complex of the Cartesian \(4\times4\) grid, with unambiguous edge enumeration and signed simplicial action. The packaged exact checker constructs all 10,012 augmented faces, all 47,968 Hasse incidences, and an acyclic matching with ten degree-four and six degree-five critical cells. It computes the signed integral \(10\times6\) differential and its rank-four unimodular minor, so no hidden torsion or unproved concentration assumption enters the rational character computation. It independently constructs two explicit integer fifth-homology cycles and applies all eight group elements. Correcting the transformed cycles by *actual integral boundaries* produces the displayed \(2\times2\) matrices. Full signed augmented Lefschetz traces then recover the fourth-homology representation. Exact representation-character comparisons give the stated irreducible decomposition and quotient invariant. Finite verification is exhaustive for this single complex, not an induction to other grid sizes.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The square grid has a natural full order-eight automorphism group and already-known mixed-dimensional homology, making its *missing action* a well-motivated topological invariant. Both degrees are genuinely equivariant: the two-dimensional top homology is the irreducible square representation; the six-dimensional lower homology contains two nontrivial sign-character types and exactly one invariant line. The resulting rational quotient has a fourth-homology generator but no fifth-homology generator. None of these conclusions follow from the previously recorded wedge-of-spheres homotopy type. This is a complete natural symmetry classification for a canonical finite planar grid rather than an arbitrary parameter slice; no general higher-grid theorem is claimed.

## Closest literature and limitations

The result is limited to rational modules and quotient rational homology, not integral \(\mathbb Z[D_4]\)-modules or a stability theorem. The independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
