# Review of the noncommuting-pair distribution

## Correctness

PASS. The statement counts an unordered noncommuting pair exactly when its two elements lie in the same weak-order block. The previously established unique ordered-partition/projection description turns the problem into an exact positive-composition enumeration. A real-Fourier-parameter pole calculation yields the Gaussian limit with strictly positive variance; a large-block factorial-cost argument yields matching \(n\log n\) upper and lower tail rates. The proof distinguishes formal generating functions from analytic characteristic functions. Direct multiplication-table enumeration agrees with the exact recurrence for all sizes through four.

## Originality

PASS within examined coverage. The structural classification and unmarked count are known [1]; their eventual ratio asymptotic is explicitly available in [2], so neither is claimed as a discovery. The examined relevant primary sections of [1] and [2] give whole-class counts and extreme commutative/anticommutative classifications, but not a distribution of the number of partially noncommuting pairs or the sharp positive-density tail exponent. Related asymptotic work on *noncrossing* and *unordered* set partitions addresses different probability spaces. Possible equivalence to earlier preferential-arrangement or general conditioned-renewal results remains a risk. No result is certified historically unique.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The number of noncommuting pairs is a canonical invariant of a finite semigroup law. Its exact finite census has a Gaussian linear-size bulk, while a fixed positive proportion of all pairs can fail to commute only on a distinct giant-block, superexponential scale. These two compatible scales reveal structural information not given by total-count enumerations and supply explicit model-dependent constants and a sharp upper-tail rate.

## Boundaries

The Gaussian statement is for uniform **labeled** associative quasitrivial laws. The upper-tail exponent holds only for fixed \(0<\delta<1/2\) and does not provide a sharp prefactor. The quadratic marked series is formal for general marks. The finite checker corroborates, not substitutes for, the full proof. The class of all weak orders alone is not the probability model: nonsingleton blocks carry two projection choices.
