# Geometric fixed loci of two-bit hypercube translations and their low-scale homology

## Finding
Let \(n\ge2\), let \(Q_n=\{0,1\}^n\) have Hamming distance, and let \(X_r=\operatorname{VR}_{\le r}(Q_n)\) be the inclusive Vietoris–Rips complex. Let \(\tau\) simultaneously toggle the first two coordinates, let \(F_r=|X_r|^{\langle\tau\rangle}\) be the geometric fixed-point space, and put \(m=n-2\). Then \(F_r\) is empty for \(0\le r<2\), whereas at scale two there is an explicit piecewise-linear homeomorphism
\[
F_2\cong|Q_{n-1}|,
\]
where the right side is the geometric realization of the **one-dimensional cube graph**, not its filled cubical polytope. Therefore
\[
F_2\simeq\bigvee^{(n-3)2^{n-2}+1}S^1,
\]
with zero circles interpreted as a point. All first-homology classes at scale two map to zero under the inclusion-induced homomorphism \(H_1(F_2;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(F_3;\mathbb Z)\). Thus these classes contribute exactly \((n-3)2^{n-2}+1\) persistence intervals \([2,3)\), over every coefficient field; this does not classify any classes first born at scale three.

There is also a symmetry-refined conclusion. Let \(\eta\) toggle the first coordinate alone, and let \(\kappa_z\) translate the final \(m\) coordinates by \(z\in\mathbb F_2^m\). Their action on \(F_2\) factors through \(H=\langle\eta,\kappa_z\rangle\cong\mathbb F_2^{m+1}\). For \(a\in\mathbb F_2\), \(b\in\mathbb F_2^m\), write \(\chi_{a,b}(\eta^u\kappa_z)=(-1)^{au+b\cdot z}\). The multiplicity of \(\chi_{a,b}\) in \(H_1(F_2;\mathbb C)\) is
\[
\operatorname{mult}(a,b)=
\begin{cases}
m-|b|,&a=1,\\
|b|-1,&a=0,\ b\ne0,\\
0,&a=0,\ b=0,
\end{cases}
\]
where \(|b|\) denotes Hamming weight. All coefficients in the homotopy and persistence assertions are integral before changing to a field; the character decomposition is over \(\mathbb C\).

More generally, at scale two any nonzero binary translation of Hamming weight at least three has empty geometric fixed locus. The weight-one case is the previously established single-coordinate fixed-locus reduction and is **not** claimed as new here.

## Assumptions and scope
The binary cube is the finite vertex metric space, not the continuous Euclidean cube. The operator \(\operatorname{VR}_{\le r}\) uses pairwise Hamming distance at most \(r\), including equality; \(r\) is any nonnegative real number. The fixed locus is taken in the geometric realization (barycentric coordinates), not the fixed-vertex induced subcomplex or the orbit-space quotient. Integers \(n\ge2\) include the boundary case \(n=2\), when \(m=0\), the fixed graph is one edge, and the homology representation is zero. The statements make no claim about the complete homotopy type of \(F_r\) for \(r\ge3\), nor about integral decomposition of the representation lattice.

## Proof
**1. Geometric fixed points and orbit simplices.** Every orbit of \(\tau\) on cube vertices has size two. A point in an invariant simplex of \(X_r\) is fixed precisely when it assigns equal barycentric weights to the two vertices in each contained orbit. Thus the fixed subset of that simplex is a simplex on the barycenters of the included two-vertex orbits. These simplexwise homeomorphisms glue to a piecewise-linear homeomorphism between \(F_r\) and the *orbit-admissibility complex*: its vertices are orbits whose unions are simplices of \(X_r\), and its faces are collections of orbits whose total union is a simplex. The distance within each orbit is two, so no nonempty fixed simplex exists for \(r<2\).

**2. Exact orbit graph at scale two.** Index the \(2^{m+1}\) orbits by \(p\in\mathbb F_2\) and \(z\in\mathbb F_2^m\):
\[
O_{p,z}=\{(0,p,z),(1,1-p,z)\}.
\]
For distinct orbits of the same \(p\)-type the maximum cross-distance is \(2+d(z,z')\), so none can share a scale-two simplex. For opposite \(p\)-types the cross-distances are all \(1+d(z,z')\). Hence two distinct orbit vertices span an edge exactly when their types differ and \(z'=z\) or \(z'=z+e_i\) for a standard basis vector \(e_i\). No three orbit vertices form a simplex because two would have the same type. The fixed locus is therefore a graph with vertices \((p,z)\) and edges of differences
\[
s_0=(1,0),\qquad s_i=(1,e_i)\quad(1\le i\le m).
\]
The explicit coordinate transform \(\Phi(p,z)=(p+\sum_i z_i,z)\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) sends the \(m+1\) differences \(s_0,s_i\) to the distinct standard coordinate vectors. Thus this graph is *isomorphic* to the one-skeleton of \(Q_{m+1}\). It has \(2^{m+1}\) vertices, \((m+1)2^m\) edges, and one component; its first integral Betti number is \((m-1)2^m+1\), with all higher reduced homology zero. Every connected graph is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of this number of circles.

**3. Death of all scale-two one-cycles at scale three.** The integral cycle group of a hypercube graph is generated by its elementary square boundaries: induct on cube dimension using the two parallel subcubes and their vertical edges, with the extra cycles decomposing into the vertical squares along paths in a spanning tree. In the orbit model, at scale three distinct same-type orbits become adjacent when \(d(z,z')\le1\), and distinct opposite-type orbits are adjacent when \(d(z,z')\le2\). These pairwise admissibility rules also characterize orbit triangles, because the underlying Vietoris–Rips complex is flag.

For a cube square using \(s_0\) and \(s_i\), its diagonal joining the two same-type vertices with \(z\)-labels differing by \(e_i\) is an edge at scale three; the two resulting triangles fill the square. For a square using two distinct \(s_i,s_j\) with \(i,j\ge1\), the additional orbit vertex \((p+1,z)\), when the starting vertex is \((p,z)\), is adjacent at scale three to all four vertices, yielding a four-triangle cone filling. All square boundaries, and thus all integral first-homology classes of \(F_2\), become boundaries in \(F_3\). Because the filtration changes only at integer distances, each nonzero scale-two one-cycle is born at two and dies at three. The same statement follows after tensoring with any field.

**4. Translation-character multiplicities.** On the orbit graph, the group \(H\) acts regularly on the vertices by \((u,z):(p,x)\mapsto(p+u,x+z)\). For each edge type \(s_j\), its unoriented-edge stabilizer is the order-two subgroup generated by \(s_j\); the induced action on an oriented edge reverses orientation under this generator. Consequently, as complex \(H\)-modules,
\[
C_0(F_2;\mathbb C)\cong\mathbb C[H],\qquad
C_1(F_2;\mathbb C)\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{m}\operatorname{Ind}_{\langle s_j\rangle}^{H}(\mathrm{sgn}).
\]
A character \(\chi\) occurs in the latter exactly once for each generator \(s_j\) satisfying \(\chi(s_j)=-1\). Since the graph is connected, the exact augmented cellular-chain sequence implies that every nontrivial character occurs in first homology with multiplicity \(\#\{j:\chi(s_j)=-1\}-1\); the trivial character does not occur. For \(a=1\), this count is \(1+m-|b|\); for \(a=0\), it is \(|b|\). Subtracting one in the nontrivial cases gives the stated multiplicities. Their total is the previously calculated first Betti number.

**5. Weight cutoff.** For a general nonzero translation by a vector of weight \(w\), every vertex orbit consists of two points distance \(w\). If \(w\ge3\), no such orbit can belong to a simplex of \(X_2\); hence the fixed locus is empty. The case \(w=1\) is known from the single-coordinate reduction and is not used to establish the two-bit theorem.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone Python script `verify_translation_fixed.py` independently enumerates translation orbits and all compatible orbit pairs for every \(2\le n\le7\) and every nonzero translation support weight. It checks the empty/fixed cases at scales zero, one, and two, all graph edges against the explicit cube coordinates, triangle-freeness, connectivity, and integral first Betti ranks. For weight two it separately computes all finite Fourier multiplicities from the signed action on edges and verifies 1,404 explicit triangular fillings of cube squares at scale three. Successful invocation terminates with `VERIFY_OK`. These bounded calculations are checks of the definitions; the preceding argument proves the claims for all \(n\ge2\).

## Relationship to prior work
Galetto, Montaño and Wellner (2026) determine the hyperoctahedral representations on the **ambient** Vietoris–Rips homology of binary hypercubes at small scales. Their scale-two spectral-sequence and character formulas do not describe the geometric fixed-point space of a weight-two translation; its homology is not the invariant subspace of ambient homology. Adamaszek and Adams (2021) determine ordinary ambient scale-two homotopy types, not these fixed loci. General equivariant Vietoris–Rips methods of Varisco and Zaremsky (2021) do not supply this explicit finite-cube orbit graph or its death-at-three calculation. A previously available reduction treats one-coordinate flips, while coordinate transpositions and longer permutations are different automorphisms and do not cover a simultaneous two-coordinate flip. The contribution is the **weight-two** fixed-locus identification, its centralizer-character formula, and the vanishing scale-two-to-three persistence map; the familiar cube-graph homology computation itself is not claimed as a new standalone invariant.

## Limitations
The result does not identify all homology groups newly appearing at scale three or larger scales, and does not classify the full group of automorphisms of the fixed graph. The character statement concerns complex representations and does not assert an integral semisimple splitting. No claim of exhaustive literature priority is made; unindexed equivalent work remains possible. The finite verifier is not a substitute for the all-parameter proof.
