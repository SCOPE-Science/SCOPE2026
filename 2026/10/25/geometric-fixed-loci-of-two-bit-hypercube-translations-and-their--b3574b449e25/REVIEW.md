# Review of two-bit translation fixed loci of hypercube Rips complexes

## Correctness — PASS
A geometric fixed point must be supported on entire two-vertex translation orbits, with equal barycentric coefficients. For a weight-two translation all such orbits enter at exactly scale two. Their orbit-admissibility graph has two vertex types and cross-type adjacency determined exactly by untouched-coordinate Hamming distance at most one. The displayed linear change of coordinates takes its \(m+1\) generator differences to the standard basis, giving a graph homeomorphic to \(Q_{m+1}\). The integral cycle rank follows from the vertex and edge counts. At scale three every elementary cube square is filled by either two triangles or a four-triangle cone, so the integral first-homology inclusion is zero. The complex character calculation follows from the regular vertex module, the signed induced edge modules and connectedness. The finite checker reproduced 27 support-weight cases, all character multiplicities for dimensions 2–7 and 1,404 explicit square fillings. All quantifiers and the boundary case \(n=2\) were checked.

## Originality — PASS, subject to bibliographic uncertainty
The closest primary source is Galetto–Montaño–Wellner, arXiv:2606.20784 (2026), whose relevant introduction, scale-two representation calculation and open-problems discussion concern **ambient** hyperoctahedral homology, not geometric fixed points for the two-bit translation. Adamaszek–Adams, arXiv:2103.01040, treat ambient homotopy types. The separate single-bit fixed-locus reduction is acknowledged and not presented as new. Coordinate transpositions and coordinate cycles act differently from the nonconjugate two-bit translation. The similarly titled indexed Čech-complex character calculations use a different nerve construction, so they do not imply the present Rips fixed-space theorem. Searches using 'simultaneous two-coordinate flip', 'weight-two translation', 'hypercube Rips fixed locus', 'cube-graph orbit complex', and the exact character-multiplicity and death-at-three phrases located no covering theorem. The published general equivariant-Morse paper was available only at abstract level during this review, but its stated subject is the construction of universal spaces for proper actions, not this explicit family. Possible unindexed equivalent literature remains a genuine risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This computes the fixed-point homotopy type for a distinct, natural conjugacy class of cube symmetries. The ambient scale-two Rips complex is a wedge of three-spheres in the nontrivial range, yet the two-bit-translation fixed locus has an exponentially growing free first homology; these are different invariants. Beyond a graph identification, the proof determines the exact complex character of the residual translation action and shows every fixed one-cycle dies at the next scale. The weight cutoff supplies a sharply motivated boundary for an equivariant persistence problem. These features make the result a structurally useful test case rather than a numerical increment or a homology recomputation of the ordinary cube graph.

## Limits
The conclusion is not a classification of homology born at scale three or of fixed loci at arbitrary later scales; the complex character formula does not classify the integral representation lattice. The rank and multiplicity certificates are checked computationally only in dimensions 2–7, with the all-dimensional statement supplied by a separate proof. Scientific priority remains provisional until independent literature examination.
