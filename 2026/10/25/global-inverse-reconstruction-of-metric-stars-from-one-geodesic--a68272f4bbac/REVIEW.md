# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The full theorem has explicitly quantified domain: all finite stars with at least three strictly positive arm lengths, sampled by normalized arclength. The same-edge and different-edge pair counts give an exact piecewise-linear density. Its small-radius intercept and slope recover total length and valence; distributional second derivatives yield a signed finite atomic measure. The least-unrecovered-atom induction is valid because each retained pair sum involving an unrecovered arm is strictly larger than the smallest unrecovered arm. Collision cancellation is therefore handled and no distinctness assumption is used. Exact rational checks reproduced the recovery on 368 configurations, with Monte Carlo separately testing four forward distributions; the general claim is analytically proved.

## Originality — PASS, subject to a specific access risk

The forward network-distance formula is already known from Wei–Walteros–Batta and is not claimed as original. The principal directly relevant inverse paper, Mémoli–Needham, reports local injectivity on metric graphs; its available abstract does not assert a global inversion valid at every tied or coincident arm vector, but its full content could not be inspected. Dey–Shi–Wang uses persistent topology rather than the one-variable random-distance law. published-finding corpus semantic comparisons located studies of metric-star configuration-space persistence and spider-tree curvature, neither implying a uniform distance-histogram inverse. The finite star theorem is therefore not identified as previously covered under the available evidence; this is not a certification of absolute novelty.

## Value — PASS

A two-point distance histogram is much weaker than full pairwise distance data, and general distance histograms need not determine a metric-measure space. Determining *all* arm lengths from one scalar marginal is a natural and useful positive rigidity statement for a widely used junction/network geometry. The exact global result, especially its recovery despite additive collisions and sign reversal at true lengths, provides more than a generic or infinitesimal inverse and resolves a well-motivated all-parameters identifiability question for the class. No unstable finite-sample inference or broader network identifiability is implied.

## Sources and limitations

Primary comparisons: Dey–Shi–Wang (2015), arXiv:1503.07414 (paper material inspected); Wei–Walteros–Batta (2019), DOI:10.1002/net.21919 (author-uploaded full text, forward-model and conclusion sections inspected); Mémoli–Needham (2018), arXiv:1810.09646 / DOI:10.1111/sapm.12526 (abstract and metadata only). The last is a material residual overlap risk: open-access full-text requests failed, and institutional access was unavailable. Numerical tests do not establish infinite validity or absolute novelty. The theorem does not address noisy histograms, other measures, or general trees.
