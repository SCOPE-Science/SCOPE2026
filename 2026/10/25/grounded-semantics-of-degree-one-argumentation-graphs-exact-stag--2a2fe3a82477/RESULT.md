# Grounded semantics of degree-one argumentation graphs: exact stages and degree spectrum

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal F=(A,R)\) be a countable abstract argumentation framework in which every argument has at most one attacker and attacks at most one argument. Each connected component is a finite directed path, a one-way infinite directed path, a two-way infinite directed path, or a directed cycle (including a self-loop); every path is consistently oriented. The grounded extension contains exactly the vertices at even finite distance from the unique source of each source-rooted path component. Components with no source contribute no grounded vertices.

A source-rooted finite path with \(m\) vertices has grounding stage \(\lceil m/2\rceil\). The global grounding ordinal is \(0\) if there are no source-rooted path components; it is the maximum of \(\lceil m/2\rceil\) over source-rooted finite paths when those lengths are bounded and no source-rooted ray exists; and it is \(\omega\) exactly when a source-rooted ray exists or the finite source-rooted path lengths are unbounded.

For effectively presented degree-one frameworks, where the partial predecessor and successor functions are computable, the degree spectrum is exact: only the computable Turing degree occurs at a finite grounding ordinal, whereas at grounding ordinal \(\omega\) the possible grounded-extension degrees are precisely the c.e. Turing degrees. Every finite ordinal is realized by a computable example, and every c.e. degree is realized at \(\omega\).

## Assumptions and scope
An argumentation framework is a directed graph \(\mathcal F=(A,R)\); \(yRx\) means that \(y\) attacks \(x\). The local degree hypothesis is both \(|\{y:yRx\}|\leq1\) and \(|\{y:xRy\}|\leq1\) for every \(x\). A source is a vertex with no attacker. Define the defense operator by
\[
F(S)=\{x\in A: \text{for every }y\text{ with }yRx,\text{ some }z\in S\text{ satisfies }zRy\}.
\]
The grounded sequence is \(G_0=\varnothing\), \(G_{\alpha+1}=F(G_\alpha)\), and \(G_\lambda=\bigcup_{\beta<\lambda}G_\beta\) at limit ordinals. The grounding ordinal is the least \(\alpha\) with \(G_\alpha=G_{\alpha+1}\). The effective degree-spectrum statement assumes computable partial predecessor and successor functions; it does not assume that a mere promise of finite degree comes with an effective neighbor list.

## Proof
Because every vertex has at most one incoming and one outgoing edge, an underlying connected component cannot branch. If it has a vertex of undirected degree two, that vertex has one incoming and one outgoing edge, so orientations propagate consistently along the component. Thus each component is a finite path, a one-way ray, a two-way infinite path, or a directed cycle; a loop is a one-vertex directed cycle.

Consider a source-rooted path with vertices \(v_0,v_1,\ldots\) in attack direction, where \(v_0\) is the source. The source belongs to \(G_1\). For \(d\geq1\), the only attacker of \(v_d\) is \(v_{d-1}\). To defend \(v_d\), a grounded vertex must attack \(v_{d-1}\). For \(d=1\) this is impossible because \(v_0\) has no attacker. For \(d\geq2\), the only possible defender is \(v_{d-2}\). Induction therefore gives \(v_{2k}\in G_{k+1}\) for every available even distance and excludes every odd-distance vertex. A finite path of \(m\) vertices consequently stabilizes at stage \(\lceil m/2\rceil\).

A component with no source has an attacker at every vertex, and there are no edges between components. If no vertex of such a component is in \(G_\alpha\), no vertex in it can be defended by \(G_\alpha\); transfinite induction shows that it contributes nothing at any stage. A source-rooted ray adds a new even-distance vertex at every finite stage, so its closure ordinal is \(\omega\). A family of finite source-rooted paths with unbounded lengths also requires arbitrarily late finite stages and stabilizes at \(\omega\). If their lengths are bounded and there is no source-rooted ray, the maximum finite stage is the global grounding ordinal. If there is no source-rooted path, the grounded extension is empty and the ordinal is \(0\).

In an effective degree-one framework, following the unique predecessor of a vertex enumerates the grounded extension: enumerate a vertex exactly when its predecessor chain reaches a source at even distance. Thus the grounded extension is c.e. If the grounding ordinal is a finite number \(n\), every source-rooted path has at most \(2n\) vertices and no source-rooted ray exists. Searching at most \(2n\) predecessor steps decides membership; the bound is fixed for that framework but is not claimed to be uniformly extractable from its index. Hence the grounded extension has computable degree at every finite grounding ordinal.

For the converse at \(\omega\), let \(W\subseteq\mathbb N\) be any c.e. set and put \(W^*=\{2e:e\in W\}\cup\{2k+1:k\in\mathbb N\}\). Enumerate the odd numbers at unbounded stages and enumerate the even-coded copy of \(W\) as \(W\) is enumerated. Then \(W^*\equiv_T W\). Use vertices \(a_{e,n},b_{e,n}\), with edges \(a_{e,n}\to b_{e,n}\) for all \(e,n\), and \(b_{e,n+1}\to a_{e,n}\) exactly when \(e\notin W^*[n]\), where \(W^*[n]\) is the computable stage-\(n\) approximation. This relation and both partial neighbor functions are computable, and all in- and out-degrees are at most one.

If \(e\) first enters \(W^*\) at stage \(s\), the component containing \(a_{e,0}\) is a finite source-rooted path whose source is \(a_{e,s}\); for \(n>s\), the pairs \(a_{e,n}\to b_{e,n}\) are separate source-rooted edges. All \(a_{e,n}\) are grounded and no \(b_{e,n}\) is grounded. If \(e\notin W^*\), the component is a source-free infinite ray and contributes nothing. Therefore the grounded extension is exactly \(\{a_{e,n}:e\in W^*,\ n\in\mathbb N\}\), which has Turing degree \(\deg_T(W)\). Since odd numbers enter at unbounded stages, finite source-rooted path lengths are unbounded and the grounding ordinal is \(\omega\). This realizes every c.e. degree at \(\omega\). Finally, a finite path with \(2n-1\) vertices realizes any prescribed finite ordinal \(n\geq1\); countably many self-loop components can be added without changing the ordinal. A countable union of self-loops realizes ordinal \(0\).

## Verification
The proof uses a complete component-type case split and induction on path distance; it does not infer an infinite statement from finite experiments. The lower-bound construction is uniform in a c.e. index, because membership in \(W^*[n]\) is decidable by finite simulation. The effective upper bound depends on computable predecessor iteration. The finite-stage decision procedure is nonuniform in the finite grounding bound. No computational certificate is needed for the deductive classification.

## Relationship to prior work
The tree-to-argumentation encoding in Andrews and San Mauro, Theorem 19, relates ranks of computable trees to stages of the grounded construction, and their later theorem establishes high descriptive complexity for general computable frameworks. This is closely related to the path-based lower-bound construction above, but it does not state the componentwise normal form for all degree-one frameworks, including source-free rays, two-way paths, and cycles, or the joint degree/grounding-ordinal spectrum proved here.

The study of computably finitary argumentation frameworks gives a general complexity classification for acceptance problems under complete semantics. That broader classification is relevant to the c.e. upper bound but does not provide the exact local-degree component formula or the distinction between finite grounding ordinals and \(\omega\) for degree-one frameworks. The c.e.-degree witness is a simple path construction and is not presented as an independent priority claim; the substantive statement is the full componentwise and ordinal/degree classification.

## Limitations
The structural theorem assumes both in-degree and out-degree at most one. The effective degree-spectrum result additionally assumes computable partial predecessor and successor functions; it does not cover a decidable edge relation supplied only with a nonuniform promise of finite degree. No conclusion is claimed for higher-degree frameworks or grounding ordinals above \(\omega\). Targeted literature searches did not establish global priority, and an independent audit has not been performed.
