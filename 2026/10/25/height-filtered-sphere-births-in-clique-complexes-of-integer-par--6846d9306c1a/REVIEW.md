# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The strict upper neighbors of each partition correspond bijectively to donor/recipient edges of a connected threshold bipartite graph. Their induced adjacency is exactly the line graph; a simplex-star cover and the nerve lemma identify the upper link with the bipartite graph. Induction from the highest partition, simple connectivity of previous stages, and nullity of maps from graphs provide the cone-cofiber sphere splitting. The relative integral homology exact sequence proves injection of every previously born class; the free cycle-lattice quotient has rank equal to the explicit proper part-size support-pair count. The coefficient series follows by marking the required distinct part sizes. Independent exhaustive finite arithmetic checks are supplementary and do not establish the infinite theorem.

## Originality — PASS, subject to independent checking
The 2026 source arXiv:2603.14370 proves the global wedge of two-spheres and gives a height ordering; arXiv:2603.18696 describes full local neighborhoods via line graphs; arXiv:2603.26656 computes combinatorial Euler data; arXiv:2606.20773 Section 8.2 explicitly raises the lack of a direct partition-theoretic interpretation of bouquet counts. None of their inspected theorem/proof/appendix material establishes the height-by-height upper-link integral birth quotients or persistence of primitive classes. A prior published exact face-vector formula also implies the same *numerical* sphere counts by Euler's relation. Neither the bare count nor the numerical generating series is claimed as independently new. Unindexed equivalent filtration work remains a risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The natural height ranks all partitions, and the theorem determines which exact height layers produce the topological spheres; each survives integrally without later cancellation. The birth multiplicity has a transparent criterion requiring distinct positive part sizes with a genuine donor/recipient gap and a larger maximum part. This provides explicit local structural answers to the published bouquet-interpretation question rather than arbitrary finite computations or a renamed Euler sum.

## Scope and limitations
The splitting is only up to homotopy and is not a canonical choice of generators; only the induced integral filtration and graded cycle lattices are natural for the specified height filtration. No analogous result is asserted for unrelated partition graphs. Numerical experiments through a finite cutoff and the absence of retrieved equivalents are not proofs of universality or uniqueness. Independent validation is pending.
