# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The local binary-word lemma proves the explicit coefficient formula; the omitted-spoke condition restricts the hub to zero, one, or all selected spokes. All three cases are enumerated with exact boundary conditions, and the matching restriction yields a complete modular classification. The standalone checker independently enumerated all edge subsets for 132 bouquets (up to 15 total edges), finding agreement for every coefficient and all 30 dominating induced matchings. Finite evidence corroborates rather than replaces the unrestricted proof.

## Originality — PASS with material residual uncertainty
The closest primary source is Grippo–Lin–Moyano–Vera, arXiv:2607.03894v1, whose Section 4 counts the total number of perfect edge dominating sets and dominating induced matchings on all generalized series-parallel graphs, including these bouquets. This already covers unrefined instance counts and cannot be dismissed by the different graph-family title. The new claim is instead the explicit **two-variable** cardinality/hub-spoke generating polynomial with its three structural cases and the universal 0/1/2 induced-matching residue classification. These formulas were not found in the inspected primary text or the retrieved records. Search cannot establish publication priority; a weighted elaboration of the general algorithm or an inaccessible older paper may imply related formulas.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Bouquets are a natural obstruction-bearing cactus subfamily of the generalized series-parallel graphs studied by the 2026 counting paper. The exact all-coefficient factorization makes hub-state coupling explicit and yields an arithmetic classification of which bouquets admit an efficient edge dominating set and how many such sets exist. It supplies a directly usable parametric expression, not merely another way of running a known general total-count algorithm. This is a narrow structural result, not a new algorithmic complexity bound or a classification of all cactus graphs.

## Closest literature and limitations
The 2026 linear-time generalized-series-parallel counting algorithm is the most substantial prior overlap; the 2025 P6-free paper also develops counting algorithms but has different coverage. Lu–Ko–Tang (2002) underlies the graph-decomposition framework, but its full publisher text could not be read. Equivalent formulas in unindexed literature and weight-enumerator specialization are continuing risks. Independent external validation has not been performed.
