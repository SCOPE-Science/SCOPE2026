# Integral cohomology torsion and modular resonance of positive-weight almost-abelian Lie rings

## Finding
Let \(d_1,\ldots,d_s\) be strictly increasing positive integers with \(\gcd(d_1,\ldots,d_s)=1\), and let \(m_i\) be positive multiplicities. Form the integral Lie ring \(\mathfrak r_{\mathbb Z}\) freely generated by \(h\) and \(v_{ij}\), with \([h,v_{ij}]=d_i v_{ij}\), all \([v_{ij},v_{k\ell}]=0\), and alternating brackets. This is the canonical diagonal integral form of the positive-weight family appearing in Heib's complex Lie-algebra classification. Put \(n=\sum_i m_i\), list the \(n\) weights with repetitions as \(a_1,\ldots,a_n\), and write \(w(S)=\sum_{j\in S}a_j\) for any nonempty subset \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\).

The **complete integral Chevalley–Eilenberg cohomology**, with trivial coefficients and as graded abelian groups, is
\[
H^0(\mathfrak r_{\mathbb Z};\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z,\qquad H^1(\mathfrak r_{\mathbb Z};\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z,\qquad
H^k(\mathfrak r_{\mathbb Z};\mathbb Z)\cong\bigoplus_{\substack{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\\|S|=k-1}}\mathbb Z/w(S)\mathbb Z\quad(2\le k\le n+1).
\]
A cyclic factor of order one is the zero group; beyond degree \(n+1\), cohomology is zero. The product of **any two positive-degree cohomology classes is zero**. Thus all positive-degree cohomology beyond degree one is finite torsion, with its complete multiset of elementary cyclic summands determined by positive subset sums of the weights.

For any prime \(p\), base change to \(\mathbb F_p\) has cohomology dimensions
\[
\dim_{\mathbb F_p}H^k(\mathfrak r_{\mathbb F_p};\mathbb F_p)=c_k(p)+c_{k-1}(p),\qquad
c_j(p)=\#\{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}:|S|=j,\ w(S)\equiv0\pmod p\},
\]
with \(c_0(p)=1\) and \(c_j(p)=0\) outside \(0\le j\le n\). The cohomology of the characteristic-\(p\) reduction differs in Betti numbers from characteristic zero **if and only if** \(p\) divides a nonempty subset sum \(w(S)\). Hence its complete exceptional-prime set is finite and explicit; there are no exceptional primes exceeding \(\sum_i m_i d_i\). The finite-field cohomology formula also follows from general toral semidirect-product cohomology theory; the principal integral conclusion is the complete torsion decomposition and its coefficient-change interpretation.

For the normalized three-dimensional family with weights \((1,m)\), \(m\ge2\), one obtains
\[
H^2\cong\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z,\qquad H^3\cong\mathbb Z/(m+1)\mathbb Z.
\]
Thus successive degrees carry arbitrarily large, coprime torsion orders already in dimension three, although the complex cohomology is always \(\mathbb C\) in degrees zero and one and vanishes above degree one.

## Assumptions and scope
The integral object is a **specified free \(\mathbb Z\)-Lie ring with its normalized diagonal basis**, not an arbitrary integral lattice in the associated complex Lie algebra. Different integral forms of the same complex algebra need not have the same integral cohomology. The base changes are the Lie algebras defined by this specific bracket over fields of characteristic \(p\), which are **not claimed** to embed into the characteristic-zero enveloping algebra. The coefficient module is trivial in every case; the result does **not** concern adjoint or group cohomology.

## Proof
Let \(\alpha\) be dual to \(h\), and let \(\beta_j\) be dual to \(v_j\). In the integral Chevalley–Eilenberg complex, \(d\alpha=0\) and \(d\beta_j=-a_j\alpha\wedge\beta_j\). By the graded Leibniz rule, writing \(\beta_S\) for the exterior product of the \(\beta_j\) indexed by \(S\), we have
\[
d(\beta_S)=-w(S)\alpha\wedge\beta_S,\qquad d(\alpha\wedge\beta_S)=0.
\]
(The irrelevant overall sign depends on exterior-differential convention.) The cochain complex therefore decomposes, **over the integers and before taking cohomology**, as the direct sum of the one-dimensional constant complex \(\mathbb Z\{1\}\to\mathbb Z\{\alpha\}\) with zero differential and, for every nonempty subset \(S\), a two-term complex \(\mathbb Z\{\beta_S\}\xrightarrow{\pm w(S)}\mathbb Z\{\alpha\wedge\beta_S\}\). Because all \(a_j>0\), multiplication by \(w(S)\) is injective over \(\mathbb Z\). Its cokernel is \(\mathbb Z/w(S)\mathbb Z\) in degree \(|S|+1\), proving all integral group identities. Every positive-degree integral cocycle representing cohomology is a linear combination of \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha\wedge\beta_S\). Two such exterior products vanish since each contains \(\alpha\). This proves the complete integral ring assertion.

Modulo \(p\), the same two-term summand has zero differential precisely when \(p\mid w(S)\), in which case it contributes one class in degree \(|S|\) and one in degree \(|S|+1\). The empty subset contributes the classes in degrees zero and one. Counting summands yields \(c_k(p)+c_{k-1}(p)\). Since all subset sums are positive, over characteristic zero only the empty subset survives. Consequently exactly primes dividing at least one positive subset sum cause a Betti-number jump; any prime larger than their total maximum \(\sum_i m_i d_i\) cannot do so. For weights \((1,m)\) the nonempty subset sums are \(1,m,m+1\), giving the three-dimensional example.

## Relationship to prior work
Heib (2026, arXiv:2610.04001) classifies the complex solvable Lie algebras that motivate the normalized positive-weight family; that complex classification is not claimed here. Avetisyan (2016, arXiv:1610.05365) classifies almost-abelian Lie algebras and their subalgebras, ideals and derivations over arbitrary fields, not the integral torsion groups determined here. Coll and Gerstenhaber (2015/2016, arXiv:1407.0428), in particular their semidirect-product cohomology formula for **fields**, already imply the characteristic-\(p\) weight-zero cohomology calculation as an application of toral actions. The new mathematical assertion assessed here is the **entire integral torsion and multiplicative profile for the normalized positive-integer family**, together with its exact arithmetic relation to the field reductions. This relationship does not imply independence or rule out an earlier unlocated integral calculation.

## Limitations
The integrality claim concerns cohomology of a Lie **ring**, not singular cohomology of any associated Lie group. Over an arbitrary integral form, the result need not persist. The elementary decomposition of the diagonal Chevalley–Eilenberg differential is the specific proof mechanism and does not address non-diagonal or noncommuting derivations. The finite-field formula, by itself, is not asserted to be new. A complete inspection of Heib's full preprint was unavailable during the literature check; the published abstract was inspected and its full text was attempted, leaving some residual overlap risk.
