# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The universal rank and degree concentration come from Proposition 12 of Antolín Camarena–Carnero Bravo, arXiv:2307.12401, read in its original full text. The independent two-color calculation is certified by a deterministic sequence of 48 free elementary-collapse pairs onto the octahedral boundary. All positions of the two-color generator and its orientation actions are specified, with the reflection and color-exchange signs computed by signed augmented Lefschetz traces. Color-merging simplicial maps give a coordinate left inverse to all proposed generators; the prior integral homology rank then proves a full integral basis. The argument holds for every integer \(n\ge2\). The standalone checker also passes all 1,520 group-element character tests in sizes two through five; these tests corroborate, rather than substitute for, the proof.

## Originality
PASS with residual literature risk. The closest article, arXiv:2307.12401, determines a nonequivariant wedge of two-spheres (Proposition 12), but does not state the natural full \(S_n\times D_5\) integral homology module. Its two-color cycle discussion (Proposition 11) gives topology, not an integral color-augmentation basis. Additional mathematical-result searches found nearby dihedral cycle homology, generalized Mycielskian symmetric-group modules and a rotation character for cycle lengths divisible by three; their objects or cycle parameters differ, and none imply this five-cycle integral lattice. The new claim is strictly stronger than the source's sphere count, yet an unindexed equivalent formulation cannot be completely ruled out.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The integral module supplies an explicit geometric basis and full automorphism character for an infinite, natural family of independence complexes. Homotopy type and Betti numbers alone do not determine the integral permutation lattice or whether cycle reflections preserve or reverse homology. The split retraction argument distinguishes the actual integral augmentation lattice from other full-rank rationally equivalent lattices. The theorem is not presented as an equivariant homotopy-equivalence classification.
