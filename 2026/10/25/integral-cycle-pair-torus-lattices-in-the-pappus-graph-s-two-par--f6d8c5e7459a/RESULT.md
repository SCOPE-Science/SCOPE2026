# Integral cycle-pair torus lattices in the Pappus graph's two-particle configuration space

## Finding

Let \(P\) be the Pappus graph, in the eighteen-vertex LCF labeling \([5,7,-7,7,-7,-5]^3\), and let \(X=\operatorname{UConf}_2(|P|)\) be the space of unordered pairs of distinct points on the geometric graph. For disjoint simple cycles \(C,D\subset P\), let \([T_{C,D}]\in H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\) be the oriented product-torus class. For integer lengths \(p\le q\), let \(L_{p,q}\) be the subgroup generated by all these classes with \((|C|,|D|)=(p,q)\).

The full second integral homology is \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{27}\). The graph contains exactly 234 unordered vertex-disjoint pairs of simple cycles. Their length types and exact integral subgroup quotients are:

| Length pair | Number of product tori | Rank of subgroup | Quotient \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)/L_{p,q}\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| \((6,6)\) | 45 | 27 | \(0\) |
| \((6,8)\) | 108 | 27 | \(0\) |
| \((6,10)\) | 54 | 27 | \((\mathbb Z/2)^4\oplus\mathbb Z/4\) |
| \((8,8)\) | 27 | 27 | \((\mathbb Z/2)^5\) |

The complete graph-automorphism group has order \(216\) and divides the 45 shortest-cycle tori into orbits of sizes \(18\) and \(27\). The former spans a primitive rank-\(17\) sublattice (quotient \(\mathbb Z^{10}\)); the latter spans a full-rank sublattice of index \(32\), with quotient \((\mathbb Z/2)^5\). Neither orbit alone spans the integral homology, although the 27-element orbit spans over \(\mathbb Q\); all 45 together generate integrally. In particular the order-four obstruction in the \((6,10)\) family is cyclic, not two independent order-two factors.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite simple combinatorial graphs with each edge geometrically realized as a unit interval. The Pappus graph \(P\) has vertex set \(\{0,\ldots,17\}\), cycle edges \(\{i,i+1\}\) modulo \(18\), and chord edges \(\{i,i+a_i\}\) modulo \(18\) where \((a_i)_{i=0}^{17}=(5,7,-7,7,-7,-5)^3\). This gives exactly 27 edges. Cycle length is the number of graph edges, and a pair of cycles is disjoint if its vertex sets are disjoint. The sign of a torus class depends on cycle orientations but its generated subgroup does not.

The finite deleted-product cubical model is valid for two distinct particles on a graph and is equivariantly homotopy equivalent to the continuous configuration space under particle interchange [1, Section 1, Lemma 1.1]. Passing to the unordered quotient therefore preserves the homotopy type. All homology here uses integer coefficients unless a finite field is explicitly indicated.

## Proof

**Cellular model.** Enumerate graph edges lexicographically as \(e_0,\ldots,e_{26}\), orienting \((a,b)\) from \(a\) to \(b\) for \(a<b\). The unordered two-particle model has 153 cells \(\{u,v\}\) in dimension zero, 432 cells \((e,v)\) for vertices not incident to \(e\) in dimension one, and 297 cells \((e,f)\) for disjoint edges \(e<f\) in dimension two. For \(e=(a,b)\) and \(f=(c,d)\), with increasing endpoints, use the signed cellular boundary

\[\partial_2(e,f)=(f,b)-(f,a)-(e,d)+(e,c).\]

The boundary of \((e,v)\) is \(\{b,v\}-\{a,v\}\). These conventions yield \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\), checked coefficient by coefficient. Since the model has no three-cells, \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)=\ker(\partial_2:\mathbb Z^{297}\to\mathbb Z^{432})\), a saturated free subgroup of \(\mathbb Z^{297}\).

**Complete cycle census.** The standalone verifier enumerates each simple undirected graph cycle once. It starts each cycle at its smallest vertex, follows depth-first paths through larger vertices in increasing-neighbor order, and retains only the orientation whose second vertex is smaller than its last vertex. This is an exhaustive, non-duplicating parametrization: each simple undirected cycle has a unique least vertex and exactly two traversal directions. It gives exactly 783 simple cycles with length multiplicities \(18,54,54,243,162,216,36\) at lengths \(6,8,10,12,14,16,18\). Testing every unordered pair for disjoint vertex masks gives precisely the four listed length types and counts \(45,108,54,27\).

**Top-dimensional cycles and integral basis.** For disjoint oriented cycles \(C=\sum_e c_e e\) and \(D=\sum_f d_f f\), define the cubical product chain

\[z_{C,D}=\sum_{e\in C,\,f\in D}c_ed_f\operatorname{sgn}(f-e)\,[\min(e,f),\max(e,f)],\]

where \(\operatorname{sgn}(f-e)=+1\) when the edge index of \(e\) is less than that of \(f\), and \(-1\) otherwise. This is the cellular fundamental cycle of their embedded product torus; the verifier confirms \(\partial_2z_{C,D}=0\) for all 234 pairs.

The exact reduction of the 297 columns of \(\partial_2\) over \(\mathbb F_{101}\) gives rank 270, hence its rank over \(\mathbb Q\) is at least 270. Among the 45 hexagon-pair cycle vectors, exact reduction over \(\mathbb F_2\) selects 27 independent columns and 27 coordinate rows whose *integer* minor has determinant \(+1\). Thus \(\ker\partial_2\) has rank at least 27 and at most 27, so exactly 27.

For reproducibility, index the 45 hexagon pairs by their increasing index pairs within the above depth-first cycle enumeration. The selected **zero-based hexagon-pair column positions** are:

`[0,1,2,3,5,6,7,8,10,11,12,13,15,16,17,18,20,21,22,23,25,26,27,30,31,33,36]`.

The selected **zero-based two-cell coordinate positions** in lexicographically enumerated disjoint edge pairs are:

`[7,9,12,16,2,4,29,31,0,26,76,80,24,34,66,68,22,71,106,109,111,114,143,145,152,179,221]`.

The resulting 27-by-27 signed coefficient minor is computed exactly and has determinant \(+1\). Consequently those 27 torus vectors span a direct summand of \(\mathbb Z^{297}\). This direct summand is contained in the saturated kernel of \(\partial_2\) and has the same rank 27. Their finite-index quotient inside the kernel is simultaneously torsion (by equal rank) and torsion-free (as a subgroup of the quotient by a direct summand), hence zero. **The 27 chosen product tori form an integral basis of all \(H_2\).**

**Orbit and length lattices.** The verifier exhaustively constructs all graph automorphisms using a graph-isomorphism backtracking enumerator, checking all 216 mappings. Their action on unordered pairs of six-cycles has two orbits of lengths 18 and 27. Invert the specified unimodular 27-by-27 minor over the integers, and use the 27 selected rows to express each of the 234 product-torus vectors in the integral basis. Each full 297-entry reconstruction is then independently checked. Integer Smith normal form applied to the resulting coefficient matrices gives:

| Generator family | Matrix rank | Nonunit positive Smith factors |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| 18-member hexagon orbit | 17 | none |
| 27-member hexagon orbit | 27 | \(2,2,2,2,2\) |
| \((6,6)\) family | 27 | none |
| \((6,8)\) family | 27 | none |
| \((6,10)\) family | 27 | \(2,2,2,2,4\) |
| \((8,8)\) family | 27 | \(2,2,2,2,2\) |

The invariant-factor theorem gives every quotient in the Finding section. For the rank-17 orbit, the 17 unit Smith factors imply a primitive sublattice with free quotient of rank ten. No rational spanning statement alone is used to infer any integral index.

## Verification

`verify_pappus.py` is a standalone exact-arithmetic verifier; its requirements are in `requirements.txt`. It rebuilds the LCF graph and independently checks graph isomorphism with the standard Pappus graph, constructs and checks both cellular boundary operators, verifies one-skeleton connectivity, enumerates all cycles and all disjoint cycle pairs, checks all torus boundaries, and computes ranks over explicit finite fields. It reconstructs the 27-by-27 integer determinant certificate and all 234 integral torus coordinate vectors, enumerates graph automorphisms, and calculates all cited Smith normal forms. The definitive output begins `VERIFY_OK_PAPPUS_TWO_POINT_HEXAGON_TORUS_LATTICES`.

The boundary-rank calculation modulo \(101\) supplies a lower bound for rational rank; the unit minor and cycle boundaries supply the matching upper bound. Finite-field results alone do not establish the integral quotient claims; those depend on the exact integer unit minor and Smith computations, both present in the package.

## Relationship to prior work

Barnett–Farber [1] constructs the equivariant deleted-product model and an intersection-form description of the **ordered** two-point graph configuration space, with specific planar cases and examples of complete graphs. Farber–Hanbury [2] develops homology results for mature graphs and edge-addition operations, again not specifying the Pappus graph's separate cycle-length-generated integral sublattices. An–Knudsen [3] gives general geometric generators for planar graph-braid homology and a nonplanar obstruction, but neither a Pappus calculation nor the stated orbit/invariant-factor classification. The Pappus graph itself is the classical eighteen-vertex cubic graph described in [4].

The new assertion is **not** merely the Betti number or existence of product tori: it is the precise integral generation by shortest disjoint cycles and the exact orbit-by-orbit and length-by-length sublattice quotients. The cited general theories do not imply those Smith normal forms without additional specific calculations.

## Limitations

This is a complete finite result for the Pappus graph only; it does not establish a uniform theorem for cubic symmetric graphs or classify the first integral homology torsion. It establishes no new result for configurations of three or more particles. Literature searches and comparison of relevant full-text mathematical sections found no directly covering orbit-lattice computation, but coverage of all specialized or unindexed papers cannot be guaranteed. No independent scientific audit or publication-priority certification is claimed.
