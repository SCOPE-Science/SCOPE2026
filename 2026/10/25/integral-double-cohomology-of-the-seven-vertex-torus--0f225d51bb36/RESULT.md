# Integral double cohomology of the seven-vertex torus

## Finding
Let \(K\) have vertex set \(\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z\) and fourteen two-faces given by the seven translates each of \(\{0,1,3\}\) and \(\{0,1,5\}\). The associated moment-angle complex \(\mathcal Z_K\) has integral double cohomology
\[
HH^{-k,2\ell}(\mathcal Z_K;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&(-k,2\ell)\in\{(0,0),(-1,6),(-3,10),(-4,14)\},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus all double-cohomology groups are torsion-free, and their total rank is four.

## Assumptions and scope
The complex is precisely the finite abstract simplicial complex generated by those fourteen listed faces; indices and additions on vertices are modulo seven. It has seven vertices, all twenty-one edges, fourteen triangles, every edge in exactly two triangles, and every vertex link a six-cycle. Its second integral homology is \(\mathbb Z\) (the boundary of the oriented connected surface); Euler characteristic is zero. Thus its realization is the orientable two-torus. The theorem concerns **integral bigraded double cohomology**, not the ordinary cohomology of the moment-angle complex and not just rational or mod-two ranks.

For each \(I\subseteq\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z\), write \(K_I\) for the induced subcomplex. Use the double (co)homology defined in [1], with bidegree \((-k,2\ell)\) for a summand \(\widetilde H_p(K_I)\) whenever \(|I|=\ell\) and \(p=\ell-k-1\). The horizontal integral homology differential inserts a missing vertex \(j\) with sign \((-1)^{p+1+\#\{i\in I:i<j\}}\) and uses the induced map \(\widetilde H_p(K_I)\to\widetilde H_p(K_{I\cup\{j\}})\).

## Proof
**(1) Vertical homology.** Because the one-skeleton is complete, every nonempty \(K_I\) is connected. For \(|I|\geq3\), pick the smallest vertex \(r\in I\), and represent the one-cycles of that complete graph by the fundamental triangle cycles \((r,a)+(a,b)-(r,b)\) for \(a<b\) in \(I\setminus\{r\}\). Relative to these cycles, each oriented triangular two-face contributes its signed simplicial boundary. The resulting integral relation matrix \(B_I\) presents \(H_1(K_I;\mathbb Z)\). Exact Smith reductions of **all** such matrices give unit nonzero diagonal entries, and the following complete rank distribution:

| \(|I|\) | Counts of \(\operatorname{rank}H_1(K_I;\mathbb Z)\) | Total rank |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| 3 | 21 subsets of rank 1, 14 of rank 0 | 21 |
| 4 | 21 subsets of rank 1, 14 of rank 2 | 49 |
| 5 | 21 subsets of rank 2 | 42 |
| 6 | 7 subsets of rank 2 | 14 |
| 7 | 1 subset of rank 2 | 2 |

All first homology groups are free. No proper induced subcomplex carries a two-dimensional homology class: for each deleted vertex, every remaining triangle is part of a proper subset of the connected closed orientable surface, and any nonzero integral two-cycle of the whole surface necessarily uses every triangle. Consequently the \(p=2\) vertical line has a single \(\mathbb Z\) at \(|I|=7\). The reduced degree \(-1\) line has one \(\mathbb Z\) from \(I=\varnothing\), and the degree zero line vanishes.

**(2) The horizontal first-homology complex.** For each \(I\), choose an integral basis of \(H_1(K_I;\mathbb Z)\) by taking an integer basis of the annihilator of \(B_I\). The checker verifies that these annihilator coordinates have unit Smith factors, hence give genuine integral, not merely rational, coordinates. An inclusion \(I\subset I\cup\{j\}\) acts by carrying fundamental cycles into the target and expressing them in the target coordinates. Summing with the insertion signs yields the exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{21}\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{D_3}\mathbb Z^{49}
\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{D_4}\mathbb Z^{42}
\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{D_5}\mathbb Z^{14}
\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{D_6}\mathbb Z^2\longrightarrow0.
\]
Integer calculations establish \(D_{\ell+1}D_\ell=0\), with the following complete Smith data (zeros omitted):

| Map | Matrix size | Nonzero Smith entries | Rank |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| \(D_3\) | \(49\times21\) | twenty entries equal to 1 | 20 |
| \(D_4\) | \(42\times49\) | twenty-nine entries equal to 1 | 29 |
| \(D_5\) | \(14\times42\) | twelve entries equal to 1 | 12 |
| \(D_6\) | \(2\times14\) | two entries equal to 1 | 2 |

Its integral homology ranks at subset sizes \(\ell=3,4,5,6,7\) are respectively \(1,0,1,0,0\). The unit Smith entries prove the quotient groups are torsion-free. Since the first-homology vertical line has \(p=1\), its surviving bidegrees are \((-1,6)\) and \((-3,10)\).

**(3) The remaining lines and duality.** The single orientation class in \(\widetilde H_2(K)\) has no neighboring degree-two vertical group and survives at \((-4,14)\). The empty-set \(\widetilde H_{-1}(K_\varnothing)\cong\mathbb Z\) survives at \((0,0)\). These are the only vertical degrees. All vertical groups are free, so the integral cohomology Hochster complex is the degreewise integer dual of the just-computed homology complex. Since the horizontal homology groups are free (Smith invariants above), the universal coefficient theorem identifies the dual complex's cohomology with their integer duals in the same bigrades. This proves the displayed result.

## Verification
The bundled self-contained scientific checker `torus_checker.py` uses Python and SymPy. It generates **all 99** induced complexes with three through seven vertices, builds every integral relation matrix and every inclusion map, verifies that the induced maps are well-defined and integer-valued, checks all three consecutive differential compositions vanish, and computes every nonzero Smith invariant. It also checks the surface links and the facet incidences. Execution reproduces the table and the four horizontal Smith forms. The Smith normal form is computed over \(\mathbb Z\); field rank alone is not used to rule out torsion.

## Relationship to prior work
The foundational definition and the four-term integral result for ordinary polygons are in [1, §§3,7]. The seven-vertex triangulated torus is explicitly mentioned in [1, Remark 6.11] as a Golod, one-neighborly non-sphere, but its double cohomology is not computed there. Article [2] examines double homology primarily for **sphere triangulations**, including its theorems on neighborliness and non-primitive spheres; their spherical hypotheses do not cover this torus. The published icosahedral-sphere examples have different complexes and do not imply these torus values. This calculation gives a complete integral answer for the canonical minimum-vertex orientable genus-one surface.

## Limitations
The result concerns this specific seven-vertex combinatorial torus; it does not classify all surface triangulations or moment-angle complexes. The matrix ranks and Smith factors are exhaustive finite computations with a supplied reproducible checker, not a symbolic general-family classification. The comparison with indexed work does not exclude an equivalent calculation in unindexed literature. The underlying moment-angle complex's homotopy type is not asserted.
