# Integral Frobenius trace lattices and arithmetic cones of degree-five del Pezzo surfaces

## Finding

For every smooth degree-five del Pezzo surface \(S\) over a finite field \(\mathbb F_q\), the action of geometric Frobenius on the ten exceptional curves defines one of seven conjugacy classes of \(W(A_4)\cong S_5\). Define \(T(S)\) to be the integral subgroup of \(\operatorname{Pic}(S)\) generated by the sums of exceptional-curve classes over complete Frobenius orbits. The following table simultaneously gives the Picard rank \(\rho\), the number of extremal rays of the arithmetic effective/Mori cone and of the arithmetic nef cone, the **signed** determinant of the arithmetic Picard intersection lattice, its trace-lattice index, and the isomorphism type of its discriminant group:

| Frobenius cycle type in \(S_5\) | \(\rho\) | Mori rays | Nef rays | Signed determinant | Trace index | Discriminant group |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|:--|
| \((1,1,1,1,1)\) | 5 | 10 | 10 | \(+1\) | 1 | trivial |
| \((2,1,1,1)\) | 4 | 7 | 7 | \(-2\) | 1 | \(\mathbb Z/2\) |
| \((2,2,1)\) | 3 | 4 | 4 | \(+4\) | 1 | \((\mathbb Z/2)^2\) |
| \((3,1,1)\) | 3 | 4 | 4 | \(+3\) | 3 | \(\mathbb Z/3\) |
| \((3,2)\) | 2 | 2 | 2 | \(-6\) | 1 | \(\mathbb Z/6\) |
| \((4,1)\) | 2 | 2 | 2 | \(-4\) | 2 | \(\mathbb Z/4\) |
| \((5)\) | 1 | 1 | 1 | \(+5\) | 1 | \(\mathbb Z/5\) |

In particular, although orbit traces span the entire **rational** effective cone in every case, they fail to span the full **integral Picard lattice** precisely for cycle types \((3,1,1)\) and \((4,1)\):
\[
\operatorname{Pic}(S)/T(S)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z/3,&(3,1,1),\\
\mathbb Z/2,&(4,1),\\
0,&\text{the other five classes}.
\end{cases}
\]
Every row occurs for some surface over **every** finite field; thus this is a uniform classification, not a census for a particular field. The already known classification of Frobenius types and ranks is used as input and is not claimed as new.

## Assumptions and scope

Let \(S\) be a geometrically smooth del Pezzo surface with \(K_S^2=5\) over an arbitrary finite field \(\mathbb F_q\), of any characteristic. Intersection products refer to divisor classes, not numerical counts of rational points. The effective and nef cones are the closed cones in \(\operatorname{Pic}(S)\otimes_\mathbb Z\mathbb R\), with the effective cone generated by effective classes and nef dual under intersection. The discriminant group is \(\operatorname{Pic}(S)^\vee/\operatorname{Pic}(S)\) for the **integral intersection form**; signs in the table indicate the determinant, not its absolute discriminant. Frobenius types are unordered cycle decompositions of permutations of five letters. This claim does not concern nonsmooth surfaces, the characteristic cone of curves in unrelated dimensions, or the effective divisor monoid, which need not be saturated.

## Proof

**Geometric lattice.** Over the algebraic closure, \(S\) is the blowup of four general points of \(\mathbb P^2\). In coordinates \(H,E_1,E_2,E_3,E_4\), the Picard pairing has matrix \(Q=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1,-1)\). Its ten exceptional curves have classes \(E_i\) and \(H-E_i-E_j\) for \(1\le i<j\le4\). The classical degree-five del Pezzo cone theorem states that their positive span is the geometric effective cone. The anticanonical class is \(-K=3H-E_1-E_2-E_3-E_4\). The simple roots \(E_1-E_2,E_2-E_3,E_3-E_4,H-E_1-E_2-E_3\) have square \(-2\); the reflections \(s_a(v)=v+(v\cdot a)a\) generate \(W(A_4)\cong S_5\), preserving all ten exceptional classes.

**Arithmetic descent and primitive fixed lattice.** For \(\mathbb F_q\), the cohomological Brauer obstruction vanishes because \(\operatorname{Br}(\mathbb F_q)=0\). Consequently \(\operatorname{Pic}(S)=\operatorname{Pic}(S_{\overline{\mathbb F}_q})^F\), as an **integral** sublattice, for geometric Frobenius \(F\). For each of the \(120\) elements of \(W(A_4)\), the attached verifier constructs a unimodular integral kernel basis \(B\) of \(F-I\). It does not replace this by a rational nullspace. The arithmetic Gram matrix is \(B^\mathsf TQB\). Determinants and Smith normal forms give the signed determinants and discriminant groups in the table. The computation verifies constancy throughout each of the seven conjugacy classes, whose sizes are \(1,10,15,20,20,30,24\).

**Effective and nef cones.** The \(F\)-invariant slice of a rational polyhedral cone generated by permuted integral rays is generated by complete orbit sums: average a nonnegative expression over the finite cyclic group induced by \(F\). Thus the arithmetic effective cone is generated by \(u_\mathcal O=\sum_{L\in\mathcal O}[L]\), over the Frobenius orbits \(\mathcal O\) of ten exceptional curves; its dual nef cone consists of classes \(D\) satisfying \(D\cdot u_\mathcal O\ge0\). The verifier expresses each orbit sum in the primitive fixed basis, removes rays contained in the nonnegative span of the others using exact rational linear systems, and enumerates every extreme dual ray as the intersection of \(\rho-1\) independent supporting hyperplanes. It then checks all inequalities, and obtains the two complete ray-count columns in the table. For \(\rho=1\), the cone is a single ray. This enumeration is exhaustive because the cones are pointed and full-dimensional.

**Integral trace-lattice indices.** Let \(M\) be the integral matrix whose columns are orbit sums in basis \(B\). Since these orbit sums span the invariant rational space, their lattice index is the greatest common divisor of the absolute values of the maximal \(\rho\times\rho\) minors. Exact integer minors yield precisely the indices in the table. The two exceptional values also have short intrinsic explanations:

For cycle type \((3,1,1)\), choose a representative fixing \(E_4\) and cycling \(E_1,E_2,E_3\). Then \(\operatorname{Pic}(S)=\mathbb ZH+\mathbb ZU+\mathbb ZE_4\) where \(U=E_1+E_2+E_3\). The four distinct orbit-trace classes are \(U,E_4,3H-2U,3H-U-3E_4\). All have \(H\)-coordinate divisible by three, and together they generate exactly \(3\mathbb ZH+\mathbb ZU+\mathbb ZE_4\). Hence the index and quotient are \(3\) and \(\mathbb Z/3\).

For cycle type \((4,1)\), choose a representative cycling \(E_1,E_2,E_3,E_4\), and write \(U=E_1+E_2+E_3+E_4\). The three orbit sums are \(U,4H-2U,2H-U\), so their lattice equals \(\mathbb ZU+\mathbb Z(2H-U)\), of index \(2\) in \(\mathbb ZH+\mathbb ZU\). The other five types have gcd-one maximal minors, as independently recomputed by the verifier.

**Realizability.** The classical classification of degree-five del Pezzo forms identifies the seven classes with the seven conjugacy classes of \(S_5\); the existence of all seven over every finite field is established in prior literature, including Trepalin's full classification. Therefore the classification has no missing geometric cases or field-size exceptions.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_degree5.py` with a current SymPy installation (`requirements.txt`). No external data file is needed. The verifier builds all simple-reflection matrices from the intersection form, reconstructs and checks the faithful \(S_5\)-action, checks the permutation action on ten line classes, computes 120 primitive invariant lattices by integral unimodular elimination, computes all trace-lattice indices by maximal minors, enumerates primal and dual extreme rays over exact rationals, and checks every row of the table across each conjugacy class. Its final expected status string is:

`VERIFY_OK: all 120 Weyl group elements; seven Frobenius types, primitive Picard lattices, Gram SNF, full trace indices, exact effective and nef cone rays`

The verifier is a finite exhaustive certificate **only for the 120 Weyl actions**. The passage from those actions to all smooth degree-five del Pezzo surfaces over finite fields comes from the geometric classification and the proved descent and cone arguments, not from empirically sampling surfaces.

## Relationship to prior work

Banwait–Fité–Loughran (2016 preprint) study Frobenius classes and traces on del Pezzo surfaces; their discussion treats degree five as classified background. Trepalin's later full-text work explicitly lists all seven degree-five Frobenius types, their Picard ranks, and realizability over finite fields. Boitrel (2023), Theorem 1.1 and Section 3, classifies degree-five del Pezzo surfaces over perfect fields using the Galois action on the Petersen configuration of exceptional curves. **None of the classification of types, known ranks, or realizability is claimed here.** The present claim is the additional *integral* trace-lattice saturation table, primitive discriminant groups, and full arithmetic effective/nef cone-ray table. Although Boitrel's geometric classification supplies ingredients from which these data can be computed, the inspected theorem and sections do not provide the two exceptional trace indices or the complete cone-ray counts. This is a narrower arithmetic computation, not an assertion that earlier work was unrelated.

## Limitations

The novelty comparison is literature-led but not an exhaustive proof of priority. In particular, an equivalent tabulation might be encoded in a specialized Weyl-group/del Pezzo cone paper or derived as a corollary of Boitrel's classification. The result is a self-reviewed mathematical computation, not independently audited; no claim about independent validation or novel proofs of the background classification is made. The proof classifies rational polyhedral cones and integral Picard classes, not integral effectivity of every lattice point in these cones.
