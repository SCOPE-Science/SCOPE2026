# Integral Frobenius-trace Picard lattice of the Fermat quartic over five elements

## Finding
Let \(X/\mathbb F_5\) be the smooth Fermat quartic K3 surface
\[
X:\quad x_0^4+x_1^4+x_2^4+x_3^4=0\subset\mathbb P^3_{\mathbb F_5}.
\]
Let \(\sigma\) denote geometric Frobenius on the geometric Néron–Severi group and let \(\mathcal L\) be the collection of the forty-eight standard geometric lines on \(X\). Every member of \(\mathcal L\) is defined over \(\mathbb F_{25}\), none over \(\mathbb F_5\), and Frobenius acts as twenty-four disjoint transpositions. For each pair \(\{L,\sigma L\}\), form the rational divisor \(D_L=L+\sigma L\).

**Theorem.** The twenty-four divisor classes \([D_L]\) generate **integrally**, with index one, the entire arithmetic Néron–Severi lattice \(\operatorname{NS}(X)\). Its rank is \(8\), its discriminant is \(-4096\), and its discriminant group is
\[
\operatorname{NS}(X)^\vee/\operatorname{NS}(X)
\cong (\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^6\oplus(\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z)^2.
\]
The invariant lattice has signature \((1,7)\). In contrast, the geometric Néron–Severi lattice has rank \(20\), discriminant \(-64\), and is generated integrally by the forty-eight lines.

The trace-divisor intersection matrix \(H=(D_i\cdot D_j)\) is \(24\times24\), with diagonal \(-4\); its \(276\) strictly upper-triangular entries consist of \(168\) zeros, \(48\) twos and \(60\) fours. It has rank \(8\) and nonzero eigenvalues \(24\), \(-24\) and \(-16\) (the last of multiplicity six). The theorem concerns the **integral** invariant lattice, not merely the rational Frobenius eigenspace.

As an arithmetic consistency consequence, the surface has no \(\mathbb F_5\)-point, has \(1112\) points over \(\mathbb F_{25}\), and has zeta function
\[
Z(X/\mathbb F_5,T)=\frac{1}{(1-T)(1-25T)(1-5T)^8(1+5T)^{12}(1+6T+25T^2)}.
\]
The point-count/zeta determination is not asserted independently as a novel formula; the new target is the exact integral Frobenius-invariant divisor lattice.

## Assumptions and scope
All line and intersection computations are in characteristic \(5\), using the explicit field \(\mathbb F_{25}=\mathbb F_5[a]/(a^2-2)\) with \(a^4=-1\). We work with the intersection pairing on divisors of the indicated smooth quartic, without restriction to effective divisor classes. Arithmetic Néron–Severi means divisors defined over \(\mathbb F_5\) modulo algebraic equivalence. For a K3 surface over a finite field, the Brauer group of the base field vanishes and \(\operatorname{Pic}^0=0\), so this agrees with the Frobenius fixed subgroup of the geometric Néron–Severi group. The geometric Picard rank and cohomology arguments use smooth proper base change and the cycle class map at primes different from \(5\).

## Proof
**1. Explicit lines and intersection pairing.** There are three partitions of \(\{0,1,2,3\}\) into ordered pairs of pairs, namely
\[
(01\mid23),\qquad(02\mid13),\qquad(03\mid12).
\]
For a partition \((ij\mid k\ell)\) and nonzero \(u,v\in\mathbb F_5\), define
\[
L_{ij\mid k\ell;u,v}:\quad x_i=au x_j,\qquad x_k=av x_\ell.
\]
Because \((au)^4=(av)^4=-1\), all these forty-eight distinct projective lines lie on \(X\). Frobenius sends \(a\) to \(-a\), and therefore sends \((u,v)\) to \((-u,-v)\) without a fixed line. Two different lines meet precisely when the combined four-by-four matrix of their defining linear forms has rank at most three over \(\mathbb F_{25}\). By adjunction, any line on this K3 surface has self-intersection \(-2\); distinct intersecting lines contribute \(1\), otherwise \(0\).

This gives the \(48\times48\) Gram matrix \(G\) in the deterministic ordering: the three partitions as listed, followed in each partition by \(u=1,2,3,4\) then \(v=1,2,3,4\). Exact finite-field Gaussian elimination yields \(\operatorname{rank}(G)=20\), with eigenvalue profile
\[
12^1,\quad(-12)^1,\quad(-8)^6,\quad(-4)^{12},\quad0^{28}.
\]
Every line intersects fourteen other lines from the list. Frobenius-conjugate lines are disjoint. Applying the trace-incidence matrix \(S\) (one column for each two-line orbit, having two unit entries) gives \(H=S^\mathsf{T}GS\) and the specified intersection statistics and rank.

**2. Integral geometric Picard lattice.** The twenty columns of \(G\) with zero-based indices
\[
0,1,2,3,4,5,6,8,9,10,16,17,18,20,21,22,32,33,34,36
\]
form a rational basis of the line lattice and have Gram determinant \(-64\). Exact Hermite-normal-form reduction shows every remaining line has integral coordinates relative to this basis, so the full geometric *line* lattice \(L\) has discriminant \(-64\) and rank \(20\).

The characteristic-zero Fermat quartic has geometric Néron–Severi group integrally generated by lines, rank \(20\) and discriminant \(-64\), by Mizukami–Inose as reviewed and reproved in Schütt–Shioda–van Luijk, Proposition 6.2; Shimada–Shioda, Section 3 gives a full explicit line basis. The good-reduction specialization identifies its line intersection lattice with \(L\). For every prime \(\ell\ne5\), the Kummer sequence and smooth proper base change show that this specialization is saturated in \(\ell\)-adic divisor cohomology: the image of the complete generic geometric Picard group remains \(\ell\)-primitive. Thus the quotient of the geometric special-fiber Néron–Severi group by \(L\), *when its rank is also twenty*, has no prime-to-five torsion.

For completeness, that rank equality follows independently from Frobenius. On \(L\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell\), Frobenius has eigenvalues \(+1\) of multiplicity eight and \(-1\) of multiplicity twelve. The étale \(H^2\) of a K3 surface has dimension twenty-two. Over \(\mathbb F_5\), every nonzero fourth power is one, so no sum of one to four nonzero fourth powers vanishes: \(\#X(\mathbb F_5)=0\). The Grothendieck–Lefschetz trace formula gives full \(H^2\) Frobenius trace \(-26\); the algebraic subspace contributes \(5(8-12)=-20\), leaving \(-6\) on the orthogonal two-dimensional complement. Deligne purity and integrality determine its characteristic polynomial as \(T^2+6T+25\), with roots \(-3\pm4i\). Dividing these roots by \(5\) does not yield roots of unity (their sum is \(-6/5\), not an integer); hence neither complementary eigendirection can be algebraic over any finite extension. The geometric Picard rank is therefore exactly \(20\).

Consequently \(L\) is a finite-index sublattice of the full geometric Néron–Severi group, and the index is a power of five. Its square divides \(64=|\operatorname{disc} L|\), because the ambient lattice is integral. The index is therefore one, and \(L=\operatorname{NS}(X_{\overline{\mathbb F}_5})\) integrally.

**3. Integral Frobenius fixed lattice.** Express the twenty-four trace classes in the twenty-line basis of Step 2. Apply integer Hermite normal form to their \(20\times24\) column-coordinate matrix \(W\). This yields an integral basis \(t_1,\ldots,t_8\) for the trace sublattice \(T_0\). With the ordering and algorithm fixed above, its Gram matrix is
\[
Q=\begin{pmatrix}
4&4&4&6&-2&-2&4&4\\
4&0&4&4&0&0&6&4\\
4&4&0&4&0&0&4&6\\
6&4&4&4&0&0&8&6\\
-2&0&0&0&-4&0&-4&-4\\
-2&0&0&0&0&-4&-2&-2\\
4&6&4&8&-4&-2&0&4\\
4&4&6&6&-4&-2&4&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Exact integer elimination yields \(\det Q=-4096\) and Smith diagonal \((2,2,2,2,2,2,8,8)\).

The basis-coordinate matrix \((t_1\ \cdots\ t_8)\) has rank eight modulo two; a nonsingular eight-row minor is obtained at the zero-based row indices \(0,1,2,4,5,6,10,11\). It follows that \(T_0\cap 2L=2T_0\). Every Frobenius-fixed class \(v\in L\) satisfies \(2v=v+\sigma v\in T_0\), since each integral basis line belongs to one of the twenty-four Frobenius orbits. Thus \(L^\sigma/T_0\) has exponent at most two. The proven two-saturation forces \(L^\sigma=T_0\), establishing integral generation and its exact invariants. The Hodge index theorem gives signature \((1,7)\).

**4. Zeta consistency and point counts.** The explicit Frobenius eigenvalues from Step 2 yield the displayed zeta function. Direct exhaustive enumeration of all projective points of \(\mathbb P^3(\mathbb F_{25})\), normalizing the first nonzero coordinate to one, gives \(\#X(\mathbb F_{25})=1112\), also predicted by the zeta function. These finite counts check the formula but do not replace Steps 2–3 proving the infinite-extension assertions.

## Verification
Run the standalone program `verify.py` using Python 3.10+ and SymPy 1.12+ (executed with SymPy 1.14). It regenerates the forty-eight lines over the explicitly defined \(\mathbb F_{25}\), checks every pairwise intersection by exact Gaussian elimination, computes the Gram matrices and eigenvalue/rank profiles, extracts the twenty-line geometric integral basis, and computes the trace sublattice via Hermite and Smith normal forms. It then exhaustively tests all 255 nonzero half-trace combinations for nondivisibility by two, tests the \(\mathbb F_5\)-point count, and counts the \(\mathbb F_{25}\) projective points. Its final assertion is `VERIFY_OK: 48 lines, 24 conjugate pairs, Picard rank 8, discriminant -4096, SNF 2^6 8^2; F25 points 1112`.

## Relationship to prior work
Mizukami and Inose (1970s), and Schütt–Shioda–van Luijk (arXiv:0812.2377, Section 6.1), establish the integral geometric lattice of the *complex* Fermat quartic. Shimada–Shioda (arXiv:1604.06265, Sections 3 and 5) give an explicit forty-eight-line model and study reductions and a distinct fifty-six-line embedding. Their geometric line and Picard results are used as established inputs, not asserted as new. Classical Weil–Jacobi-sum methods can determine Fermat hypersurface zeta functions; the zeta formula here is ancillary.

The specific proposed contribution is the **integral arithmetic invariant lattice** of the characteristic-five Fermat quartic, including index-one generation by twenty-four degree-two trace divisors and the complete Smith invariant profile. Exact finite-field coefficient specialization and integral two-saturation are necessary additions beyond the geometric rank and discriminant in the cited sources. Equivalent computations may exist under cyclotomic Galois representations or K3 automorphism-lattice terminology; this is a priority uncertainty, not a mathematical caveat about the stated proof.

## Limitations
The theorem applies to this single explicitly defined \(\mathbb F_5\)-surface and its standard line configuration; it is not a classification of every quartic or every Fermat reduction. The proof uses established K3 étale cohomology, specialization, trace and intersection theory, and published geometric line generation; it is not a formal proof-assistant certificate. No independence or external peer-review certification is asserted. Historical and specialized arithmetic invariant-lattice literature cannot be claimed to have been exhaustively searched; an equivalent formulation remains possible.
