# Integral homology and the full cube-symmetry lattice of the five-cube independence complex

## Finding

For the five-dimensional hypercube graph \(Q_5\), let \(K=\operatorname{Ind}(Q_5)\). The graph has vertex set \(\mathbb F_2^5\), with an edge joining words that differ in exactly one coordinate. Its independence complex consists of the vertex subsets containing no graph edge.

**Integral homology:**
\[
\widetilde H_i(K;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&i=5,\\
\mathbb Z^{10},&i=7,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, all integral homology is torsion-free. This gives an integral strengthening of the previously computed rational ranks.

**Explicit integral sphere basis:** For each coordinate \(k\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}\) and parity \(p\in\mathbb F_2\), consider the eight disjoint edges
\[
E_{k,p}=\bigl\{\{x,x+e_k\}: x_k=0,\ \sum_{j\ne k}x_j=p\pmod 2\bigr\}.
\]
These edges form an induced matching: there are no graph edges between two different members. Their sixteen endpoints induce eight disjoint copies of \(K_2\), so the restriction of \(K\) to these endpoints is the simplicial seven-sphere \((S^0)^{*8}\). Orient its fundamental seven-cycle by ordering the eight edges according to their endpoints with \(k\)-th bit zero and taking the product of differences of endpoints. The ten resulting classes \([z_{k,p}]\) form a **unimodular integral basis** of \(\widetilde H_7(K;\mathbb Z)\).

**Full integral symmetry action:** The full cube automorphism group is \(B_5=(C_2)^5\rtimes S_5\), where \((t,\pi)\) sends a vertex \(x\) to \(t+\pi(x)\). On the ten oriented sphere classes, its action is exactly the permutation
\[
(t,\pi)[z_{k,p}]=[z_{\pi(k),\,p+\sum_{j\ne\pi(k)}t_j}],
\]
with parity taken modulo two and **no extra orientation sign**. Thus \(\widetilde H_7(K;\mathbb Z)\) is the permutation lattice on the ten pairs \((k,p)\). On the one-dimensional degree-five group,
\[
(t,\pi)|_{\widetilde H_5}=(-1)^{\sum_j t_j}\operatorname{sgn}(\pi).
\]
The degree-seven invariant subgroup is a primitive free rank-one subgroup generated by the sum of all ten sphere classes; degree five has no nonzero full-group invariant class.

## Assumptions and scope

All homology is reduced simplicial homology with integral coefficients unless \(\mathbb Q\) is displayed. The empty face participates in enumeration, but the acyclic matching is performed on **nonempty** simplices. The orientation of a simplex is induced by increasing integer labels of its bit strings. Cube coordinates are numbered from zero through four. The group action is by coordinate permutations and independent coordinate bit flips; the formula above specifies all \(2^5\cdot5!=3840\) elements. The claim is a complete **homology and equivariant lattice classification**, not a classification of homotopy type.

## Proof

1. **Exhaustive simplicial complex and acyclic matching.** Enumerate independent sets by the exhaustive recurrence that, at each least remaining vertex \(v\), branches into excluding \(v\) and including \(v\) while excluding all five neighbors. This partitions all independent sets without duplication and yields 254,475 faces including the empty face, or 254,474 nonempty faces. Enumerated counts by dimension \(0\) through \(15\) are
\[
(32,416,2880,11760,29856,48960,54304,44240,29920,17952,9088,3672,1120,240,32,2).
\]
Starting with the nonempty faces, process the 32 vertices ordered first by the parity of their Hamming weight, then by integer label. At vertex \(v\), for every still-unpaired face \(\sigma\) not containing \(v\), pair it with \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\) if that coface remains unpaired. Delete both from subsequent stages. This yields exactly 127,231 matched face–coface pairs. The unmatched faces consist of one vertex, one five-simplex and ten seven-simplices. Orient all nonmatched incidences downward and matched incidences upward; a complete directed topological sort visits all 254,474 faces, proving acyclicity. The 1,844,864 codimension-one incidences are all accounted for. By discrete Morse theory, \(K\) has the homotopy type of a CW complex with one cell in each of dimensions \(0\) and \(5\), and ten in dimension \(7\). Because there are no critical dimensions \(4\) or \(6\), the integral cellular boundary maps vanish. Therefore \(\widetilde H_5\cong\mathbb Z\), \(\widetilde H_7\cong\mathbb Z^{10}\), and all other reduced groups vanish. In particular no finite torsion is concealed by rational Betti numbers.

2. **The ten geometric sphere classes form an integral basis.** For fixed \((k,p)\), the eight edges in \(E_{k,p}\) have no cross-edge: changing a non-\(k\) bit changes the parity and hence exits the chosen set. The induced independence complex is therefore exactly a join of eight zero-spheres. Expanding the oriented crosspolytope fundamental cycle gives 256 oriented seven-simplices; cancellation on every six-simplex verifies that it is a cycle. Eliminate the matched seven/six face pairs in the signed simplicial chain complex, following the acyclic matching. The resulting chain projection of each sphere cycle onto the ten critical seven-cells yields a \(10\times10\) integer matrix. With the deterministic ordering and orientations implemented in `verify.py`, the exact Bareiss determinant is \(-1\). Thus the ten cycles span the rank-ten group as a full **integral** basis, not merely a rational basis or a finite-index sublattice.

3. **Action on seven-dimensional homology.** Each cube automorphism sends \(E_{k,p}\) to \(E_{\pi(k),p+\sum_{j\ne\pi(k)}t_j}\). In the oriented join, the sign is the parity of the permutation of the eight two-vertex factors multiplied by the sign of the internal endpoint flips. Both together equal \(+1\). The implementation checks this identity for every \((k,p)\) and all 3840 group elements. Since these spheres form an integral basis, the resulting integral module is the claimed permutation lattice.

4. **Action on five-dimensional homology.** The degree-five integral homology has rank one, hence each automorphism acts by \(+1\) or \(-1\). For a graph automorphism, a fixed nonempty simplex is a union of vertex orbits that are individually independent. Grouping admissible vertex orbits into a conflict graph, its independence polynomial evaluated at \(-1\) gives the sum of degree-five and degree-seven reduced homological character traces. The ten-basis formula gives the degree-seven trace simply by counting fixed pairs \((k,p)\). For one coordinate bit flip this count is \(2\) and the independence-polynomial evaluation is \(1\), giving degree-five trace \(-1\); for a coordinate transposition these numbers are \(6\) and \(5\), giving \(-1\) again. Such flips and transpositions generate \(B_5\), so the degree-five character is the product of bit-flip parity and permutation sign. The verifier independently checks the Lefschetz identity for **all 3840 elements**, not merely the generators.

## Relationship to prior work

Conant and Thistlethwaite, *Boolean formulae, hypergraphs and combinatorial topology* (arXiv:0808.0739), Theorem 5, already give the **rational** Betti numbers for the Alexander dual theta complex \(\Theta(\operatorname{Cube}(5,1))\): one class in dimension \(24\) and ten in dimension \(22\). Combinatorial Alexander duality on 32 vertices translates these to the rational dimensions one and ten in dimensions \(5\) and \(7\) of \(\operatorname{Ind}(Q_5)\). The rational ranks are thus **not a new claim**. The paper explicitly leaves integral homology undetermined and does not provide the ten integral crosspolytope generators or their full symmetry representation. Its Section 6 studies Euler-characteristic congruences under group actions, not these integral homology modules.

The known four-cube independence-complex result and the folded-five-cube independence-complex result address different graphs; the latter uses a sixteen-vertex quotient rather than the 32-vertex hypercube. Hypercube Čech and Rips complexes have different simplex conditions. General independence-complex results for forests do not cover this cyclic cube. The specific new contribution here is the **torsion-free integral lift, unimodular sphere basis and full \(B_5\)-lattice action** for the same natural example studied by Conant and Thistlethwaite.

## Limitations

The ten 7-spheres and one 5-cell supply a minimal Morse CW model, but possible unstable attaching maps of the seven-cells to the five-sphere have **not** been classified. The conclusion does **not** assert that \(K\) is a wedge of spheres; knowing integral homology is insufficient for that assertion. The certificate establishes the statement for the five-dimensional cube, not an infinite-family formula. Literature comparisons do not rule out an unindexed equivalent treatment or later unpublished computation. No independent scientific audit is claimed.
