# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof classifies every Alexander-dual simplex, including why four-element simplices are exactly cubical square tetrahedra and why there are no larger simplices. It reduces the square core to a simply connected two-complex using private triangular tetrahedron faces, computes the exact Euler characteristic, counts the unattached long-distance graph edges, and applies integral Alexander duality with all degree shifts explicitly fixed. The claims are for every integer \(d\ge3\), and no finite experiment is used as proof for infinitely many dimensions. As a separate check, exact signed Smith forms of the extracted checker give ranks \((7,17,6)\) and \((15,65,24)\) for small cases; no torsion factors appear.

## Originality — PASS against examined evidence
The first defining cut-complex paper [1] and its sequel [2] do not state the cube-family dual wedge or integral Betti formula. The complete-codimension-one-skeleton hypothesis of [1, Theorem 6.1] fails for every cube here. The previously published dimension-three cube case [4] is explicitly subsumed and credited, not claimed new. The dimension-four total cut complex [6] uses independent triples, whereas the current complex uses all disconnected triples. The more general edge-disjoint-square theorem [5] fails on cubes because every cube edge lies in multiple squares. The new assertion is the uniform classification for all dimensions, not a re-publication of those individual claims. Risk: uncatalogued equivalent descriptions of the Alexander-dual complex or work under commutative-algebra terminology.

## Value — PASS
For a canonical infinite sequence of cubical graphs, the formula determines every integral homology group, excludes torsion for all dimensions, and establishes two independent nonzero adjacent degrees. In particular it proves nonshellability for the entire sequence and gives an explicit example with ranks seven and forty in degrees eleven and twelve. The geometric square-core mechanism explains the two degrees and provides a reusable way to analyze complexes generated by connected induced triples. This is a structural infinite-family result, not a small tabulated increment.

## Limitations
The original cut complexes' homotopy types and automorphism-module decompositions remain undetermined. Completeness of literature coverage is not independently certified. The exact computer checks are limited to dimensions three through five.
