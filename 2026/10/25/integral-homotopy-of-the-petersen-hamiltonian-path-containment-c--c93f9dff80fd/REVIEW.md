# Scientific review of the Petersen Hamiltonian-path containment complex

## Correctness — PASS
The graph is defined explicitly as \(\mathrm{KG}(5,2)\), and the face condition is containment in a spanning path. The finite verifier exhausts 120 nine-edge paths, 10,212 faces including the empty face, and all 58,830 face-cover incidences. It verifies a complete acyclic matching by topological sort, with one critical zero-cell, four five-cells and 24 six-cells. The signed Morse matrix is identically zero over \(\mathbb Z\), not merely modulo a prime; the relevant Hurewicz homomorphism identifies its columns with attaching-map classes. That proves the wedge decomposition. Independent simplicial mod-two ranks corroborate the homology. The 160 missing linear forests and their two \(S_5\)-orbits are exhaustively enumerated. Risks: the argument is a finite computer-assisted proof rather than an independently audited proof, and requires the reader to check the short verifier and classical discrete-Morse theorem.

## Originality — PASS
The full-text Li–Wu (2015) paper studies Hamiltonian path extendibility and explicitly discusses Petersen path endpoints, but not the simplicial complex or its integral homotopy. The full-text Forman theorem provides the method without this application. Full-text Grinberg–Katthän–Lewis concerns fixed-endpoint directed path-free/path-missing complexes, not undirected spanning-path containment. Indexed Petersen matching, higher-matching, cut and Morse complexes have genuinely different face predicates and cannot directly imply the stated homotopy calculation. Publisher information from Holton–Sheehan's 1993 monograph establishes classical context, though the entire book was unavailable. A non-indexed equivalent classification remains possible; no absolute novelty theorem is asserted.

## Value — PASS
A Hamiltonian-path containment complex encodes which partial edge constraints extend to a spanning path; the Petersen graph is a canonical hypohamiltonian test object. Its pure eight-dimensional complex collapsing to a mixed five-/six-dimensional wedge is a substantial topological classification, not a relabeling of previously known graph-invariant counts. The sharp extension threshold at five edges, the ten and thirty minimal obstruction forests, and the two full symmetry orbits make the result relevant to path-extendibility and explain which forbidden patterns the topology organizes. No broader theorem is required to interpret the natural exact invariant.

## Closest literature and residual risks
The closest exact-object primary paper is Li–Wu (2015), Section 2.2 and Example 20(c), on extension of linear forests to Hamiltonian paths; crucially, its forest-size definition counts isolated vertices. Grinberg–Katthän–Lewis (2021) and Forman (1998) were inspected in material full text for differing constructions and proof methods. Holton–Sheehan (1993) was checked via publisher catalog and contents, not full text. Record searches of related Petersen complexes identified different mathematical objects. These limitations leave some duplication risk and require eventual independent checking.
