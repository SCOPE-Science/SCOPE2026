# Integral homotopy type of cyclic Latin-square clique complexes of odd order

## Finding
For every odd integer \(n\ge3\), let \(G_n\) have vertex set \((\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^2\), with two distinct vertices \((x,y)\) and \((x',y')\) adjacent precisely if \(x=x'\), \(y=y'\), or \(x+y=x'+y'\) modulo \(n\). Let \(X_n=\operatorname{Cl}(G_n)\) be its clique (flag) complex. Then
\[
X_n\simeq\bigvee^{(n-1)^3}S^2.
\]
In particular \(\pi_1(X_n)=0\) and \(\widetilde H_2(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{(n-1)^3}\), with every other reduced integral homology group zero.

## Assumptions and scope
Here \(n\) is any odd integer, not necessarily prime. The three parallel classes of maximal line cliques consist of all rows \(x=a\), all columns \(y=b\), and all diagonals \(x+y=c\), with \(a,b,c\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). A clique simplex is the full simplex on the vertices of a graph clique. The claim concerns the entire clique complex, not a two-skeleton or a field-homology calculation.

## Proof
**1. Classify simplices.** Let \(Y_n\subseteq X_n\) be the union of the \(3n\) simplices on all row, column and diagonal lines. Any two distinct line simplices meet in either no vertex or exactly one vertex. A triangle not contained in a line has exactly one edge of each of the three line directions. Its unique vertex at the meeting of its row and column edges determines it as
\[
\{(x,y),(x+a,y),(x,y+a)\},\qquad (x,y)\in(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^2,\quad a\in(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)\setminus\{0\}.
\]
There are exactly \(n^2(n-1)\) such triangles, pairwise distinct, and their entire boundaries lie in \(Y_n\). Any putative fourth vertex adjacent to the standard mixed triangle \(\{(0,0),(a,0),(0,a)\}\), after permuting the three line directions, must lie on one of the three lines through \((0,0)\). On a row or column line it is already one of the triangle's vertices. On the diagonal \(\{(t,-t)\}\), adjacency to \((a,0)\) forces \(t=a\), and adjacency to \((0,a)\) forces \(t=-a\), which would give \(2a=0\). Since \(n\) is odd and \(a\ne0\), this is impossible. Thus every simplex of \(X_n\) either lies in \(Y_n\) or is one of those mixed triangles.

**2. Collapse the line core to a graph.** The \(3n\) line simplices and all their nonempty intersections are contractible, so the simplicial nerve theorem identifies \(Y_n\) up to homotopy with their cover nerve. Nerve vertices are the \(3n\) lines; each pair of differently directed lines meets and each triple (one of each direction) meets exactly when their constants satisfy \(c=a+b\). Hence the nerve has exactly \(n^2\) two-simplices. Each of its row-column edges belongs to exactly one two-simplex, so collapsing these \(n^2\) distinct free pairs reduces the nerve to a connected graph. Therefore \(Y_n\simeq\) a connected graph. Attaching the \(n^2(n-1)\) mixed triangles along their boundary loops in \(Y_n\) shows \(X_n\) has the homotopy type of a connected CW complex of dimension at most two. This conclusion uses homotopy invariance of cellular attachments (replace the core by a homotopy-equivalent graph and transport each attaching map).

**3. Prove simple connectivity.** Let \(T_n\subseteq X_n\) be the standard \(n\)-periodic triangulation of the torus: its vertices are \((x,y)\in(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^2\), its edges have steps \((1,0)\), \((0,1)\), or \((1,-1)\), and its triangles are the two triangles in each periodic unit square cut along the \((1,-1)\) diagonal. For \(n\ge3\), \(|T_n|\) is a triangulated two-torus. Every edge of \(X_n\) lies entirely in one of the three line simplices and is homotopic relative its endpoints, within that line simplex, to a path of unit-step edges in \(T_n\). Thus every edge loop of \(X_n\) is homotopic to one in \(T_n\), and \(\pi_1(T_n)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_1(X_n)\). The torus fundamental group is generated by the horizontal and vertical length-\(n\) coordinate loops. The first bounds a disk in its row simplex of \(Y_n\), and the second bounds a disk in its column simplex. Hence both generators die, and \(\pi_1(X_n)=0\).

**4. Count two-spheres and conclude.** The three line-simplex families have \(3n^2\) pairwise intersections and \(n^2\) triple intersections; no four distinct lines have a common vertex. Therefore inclusion-exclusion yields \(\chi(Y_n)=3n-3n^2+n^2=3n-2n^2\). Attaching the mixed triangles adds \(n^2(n-1)\) to Euler characteristic, giving
\[
\chi(X_n)=3n-2n^2+n^2(n-1)=1+(n-1)^3.
\]
Since \(X_n\) is simply connected and has a two-dimensional CW model, its reduced integral homology vanishes outside degree two, and \(H_2\) is a free abelian group of rank \(\chi-1=(n-1)^3\). The Hurewicz theorem represents a basis by sphere maps; their wedge map \(\bigvee^{(n-1)^3}S^2\to X_n\) is an isomorphism on integral homology. As both spaces are simply connected CW complexes, the homology form of Whitehead's theorem makes it a homotopy equivalence. This proves the claim for every allowed \(n\).

## Verification
The self-contained `verify_latin.py` enumerates all maximal cliques directly from the graph by Bron–Kerbosch, expands their faces and compares them with the independent line-plus-mixed-triangle description. It calculates reduced mod-two chain homology and checks the formula for \(n=3,5,7,9\), with output in `verify_latin_output.txt`. Finite calculations independently stress-test the classification but do not replace Steps 1–4, which prove the assertion for all odd \(n\).

## Relationship to prior work
Cioabă and Mim, *On the homology groups of clique complexes of strongly regular graphs*, arXiv:2606.27328 (first posted June 25, 2026), consider Latin-square clique complexes and obtain field-homology concentration and Betti formulas; for cyclic odd-order squares their result specializes to the rank \((n-1)^3\) in degree two. That field-homology theorem is not claimed as new here. Previously documented results also determine the rational translation characters for the more general abelian-group construction, which is likewise **not** claimed here. The present contribution upgrades these invariants to an integral **homotopy** decomposition by the additional two-dimensional-model and fundamental-group arguments. The earlier homology and representation statements alone do not establish the vanishing of \(\pi_1\) or a wedge-of-spheres homotopy type.

## Limitations
The argument relies on odd order: for even \(n\), the obstruction \(2a=0\) may fail and extra four-cliques arise, so no even-order conclusion is made. It concerns cyclic Latin squares, not arbitrary Latin squares or all strongly regular graphs. It gives an unpointed integral homotopy equivalence; no equivariant homotopy equivalence, canonical sphere-basis action, or publication priority beyond the inspected sources is claimed. The self-review has not received independent audit.
