# Scientific review: icosahedral independence homology

## Correctness — PASS
The claim follows from six explicitly listed icosahedral edge families and the exact distance partition \((1,5,5,1)\). The complementary edges split into thirty distance-two pairs and six antipodal pairs. An explicit vertex relabeling proves the distance-two graph is icosahedral. No independent simplex containing an antipodal edge has dimension two, so the entire complex is a triangulated two-sphere plus six separate one-cells attached to its vertices. The ordinary wedge type, torsion-free integral homology, and the canonical signed antipodal-edge module follow by elementary CW topology and the relative long exact sequence. The rotation and inversion characters follow from the fixed-vertex difference formula and the fixed-simplex Lefschetz argument. The standalone finite verifier checks all graph, simplex and symmetry computations, including all 120 automorphisms, rather than sampling a subset. Boundary limits: this proves statements only for the twelve-vertex icosahedron, not an infinite family.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Exact searches of mathematical research records, indexed graph-topology literature, and relevant publicly indexed icosahedral complexes yielded no result implying the canonical integral anti-invariant vertex-lattice identification for the ordinary independence complex. Engström's 2005 full-text studies give the general complex definition, graph reductions and claw-free connectivity bounds, not this homology representation. A full-text distance-neighborhood-complex classification uses *unions of distance shells* and does not compute independence complexes; a second full-text open-neighborhood-complex symmetry theorem concerns \(\mathcal N(I)\), not \(\operatorname{Ind}(I)\). The latter even has a different face count and homological degree. MathWorld's graph table gives group and graph parameters but not the claimed homological \(\mathbb Z[G]\)-module. Searches and comparison of implications do not certify uniqueness: an unindexed equivalent geometric observation or equivariant topological computation may exist.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The icosahedron is a canonical antipodal polyhedron. The result explains its independence complex structurally as a distance-two Platonic sphere with the six vertex diameters reattached, rather than merely giving Betti numbers or an arbitrary finite tabulation. The integral first-homology lattice has a natural group-equivariant identification, which refines the rational decomposition and determines orientation reversal, all symmetry traces, and invariant homology. The contrast between the rank-six antipodal module and the rank-one sphere orientation character is meaningful in equivariant topological combinatorics; no large-family claim is required to make this exact canonical calculation useful.
