# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The integer deck-orbit path groups are free on base paths, so the integral differential is well defined. The edge boundary is the signed incidence operator. On a triangle- and quadrilateral-free graph, a nonbacktracking allowed two-path has a unique forbidden shortcut boundary coefficient, while a backtrack gives a primitive relation identifying the reversed edge; this exhausts the degree-two boundary image. The resulting two-term complex is the standard signed cellular incidence complex, whose spanning-tree Smith reduction gives integral degree-zero torsion and free degree-one ranks. Connectedness of an unbalanced signed cover yields the stated discrepancy with homology-level deck anti-invariants. Finite checks are corroborative only.

The September 2026 Bai–Li–Yau paper introduces **real** signed GLMY homology, proves its real deck-anti-invariant description and the signed incidence boundary, and discusses backtracks for a signed bowtie. Those premises are credited, not claimed anew. The new statement here concerns an explicitly integral refinement, its unavoidable \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\) degree-zero torsion on every connected unbalanced signing, the full no-triangle/no-four-cycle integral first-homology formula, and the sharp failure of commuting integral homology with deck anti-invariants. Exact-claim and alternative-terminology searches, relevant prior literature inspection, and accessible earlier-result comparisons revealed no dominating identical statement. Local-coefficient algebra itself is classical, and residual priority risk remains.

## Value — PASS (qualified)

This is a natural coefficient-boundary test for a newly introduced signed graph homology theory: a real homological identification cannot be transferred verbatim to integers. It distinguishes every unbalanced signing integrally in degree zero despite zero real degree-zero rank, and supplies a complete family-wide integer low-degree calculation under a clear combinatorial condition. The contribution is not a new formula for elementary twisted graph incidence alone, nor a higher-degree classification. The integral obstruction directly informs whether proposed coefficient extensions preserve the central double-cover comparison.
