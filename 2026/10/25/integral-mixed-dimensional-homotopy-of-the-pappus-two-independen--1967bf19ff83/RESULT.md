# Integral mixed-dimensional homotopy of the Pappus two-independence complex

## Finding

Let \(P\) be the eighteen-vertex Pappus graph, with labeling fixed below. Its two-independence simplicial complex, equivalently its three-path-free complex, has **integral homotopy type**
\[
|\operatorname{Ind}_2(P)|\simeq
\left(\bigvee^{9}S^{5}\right)\vee S^{6}.
\]
In particular, its reduced integral homology is \(\mathbb Z^{9}\) in degree \(5\), \(\mathbb Z\) in degree \(6\), and zero otherwise. The two nonzero dimensions occur simultaneously, despite the original complex having dimension \(9\).

## Assumptions and scope

The Pappus graph \(P\) is the finite undirected simple graph on \(V=\{0,1,\ldots,17\}\), with cyclic edges \(\{i,i+1\bmod18\}\) for all \(0\le i<18\) and nine further edges
\[
\{0,5\},\{1,8\},\{2,13\},\{3,10\},\{4,15\},
\{6,11\},\{7,14\},\{9,16\},\{12,17\}.
\]
This is the usual Hamiltonian (LCF) presentation \([5,7,-7,7,-7,-5]^3\) of the Pappus graph. A subset \(F\subseteq V\) is a face of \(K=\operatorname{Ind}_2(P)\) exactly when every connected component of the induced graph \(P[F]\) has at most two vertices. Equivalently, \(F\) does not contain the three vertices of any length-two path. All homotopy and homology statements have integral coefficients, and no claim is made for other graphs or higher independence parameters.

## Proof

**1. Complete finite face test.** Every vertex of \(P\) has degree three. The graph has girth six, so the forbidden triples are the \(18\binom{3}{2}=54\) distinct triples containing a vertex and two distinct neighbors. A subset is a face precisely when it avoids these triples. The standalone verifier `verify_pappus_ind2.py` enumerates every one of the \(2^{18}\) subsets in increasing binary-mask order, with two mathematically independent characterizations of the face predicate: the forbidden-length-two-path extension test, and full induced-component traversal. The predicates agree on all subsets. The numbers of nonempty faces in dimensions \(0\) through \(9\) are
\[
(18,153,762,2394,4806,6027,4428,1728,308,27),
\]
respectively, totaling \(20{,}651\) nonempty faces.

**2. A complete discrete-Morse matching.** Starting with all nonempty faces unmatched, visit vertices \(v=0,1,\ldots,17\) in that order. At vertex \(v\), visit all still-unmatched faces \(F\) in increasing numerical order of their binary masks. When \(v\notin F\) and \(F\cup\{v\}\) is also a still-unmatched face, match the covering pair \((F,F\cup\{v\})\) and remove both faces from further matching. This completely determines an explicit face-poset matching with \(10{,}320\) pairs. Its complete list is regenerated by the enclosed verifier, not inferred from a random search.

Orient each Hasse incidence of the **nonempty** face poset from upper face to lower face unless it is one of the selected matching incidences, in which case reverse the arrow. The verifier constructs all \(120{,}222\) directed incidences and performs a Kahn topological ordering. Every one of the \(20{,}651\) face vertices is removed, proving the directed graph acyclic and hence the matching a valid Forman matching. Its critical faces (binary masks in hexadecimal) are

`0x1` (dimension \(0\)); `0x3330`, `0x6622`, `0x9912`, `0xcc06`, `0x12264`, `0x18886`, `0x21198`, `0x2448a`, and `0x300cc` (dimension \(5\)); and `0x2cd02` (dimension \(6\)).

Thus discrete Morse theory gives a CW complex with one \(0\)-cell, nine \(5\)-cells, one \(6\)-cell, and no other cells. The complex's \(5\)-skeleton is therefore a wedge of nine \(5\)-spheres; the remaining issue is the integral attaching map of the \(6\)-cell.

**3. Exact signed Morse boundary.** Use the orientation given by increasing vertex labels. If \(F\) is a five-dimensional face and \(F\subset U\) is its matched six-dimensional partner, write \(\varepsilon(F,U)\in\{+1,-1\}\) for the incidence coefficient in the ordinary oriented simplicial boundary of \(U\). Define the vector \(R(F)\) on the nine critical five-dimensional faces as follows: it is the corresponding unit basis vector for a critical face, zero for a noncritical face that is **not** the lower member of a \((5,6)\) matching pair, and otherwise
\[
R(F)=-\varepsilon(F,U)\sum_{F'\subset U,\ |F'|=6,\ F'\ne F}
\varepsilon(F',U)R(F').
\]
Acyclicity guarantees that this integer recursion terminates. The recursion exactly performs the unit-pivot cancellations along the \((5,6)\)-matched pairs in the simplicial cellular boundary. The signed Morse boundary of the sole critical six-dimensional face \(T\), mask `0x2cd02`, is
\[
\partial^{\mathrm{Morse}}_6(T)
=\sum_{F\subset T,\ |F|=6}\varepsilon(F,T)R(F)
=(0,0,0,0,0,0,0,0,0)\in\mathbb Z^9.
\]
The verifier executes every step of this signed recursion in exact Python integers, independently of coefficient reduction modulo any prime.

**4. Homotopy, not merely homology.** The one-point-plus-nine-five-cell skeleton is \(W=\bigvee^9S^5\), a \(4\)-connected CW complex. Its Hurewicz homomorphism \(\pi_5(W)\to H_5(W;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^9\) is an isomorphism. The six-cell attaching map represents precisely the nine integral degrees constituting the Morse boundary above. Because these degrees are all zero, the attaching map is null-homotopic. Attaching the six-cell therefore wedges on a six-sphere. This proves the displayed integral homotopy equivalence.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_pappus_ind2.py` with Python \(3.10\) or later; only the standard library is needed. Expected final line: `VERIFY_OK`. The actual run examined all \(262{,}144\) vertex subsets, proved the \(20{,}651\)-face matching acyclic by ordering all \(120{,}222\) nonempty Hasse incidences, reconstructed critical cell masks and dimensions, and calculated the exact zero signed Morse differential. The script and deterministic checks are part of the finding; there is no external binary, database, unpublished certificate, or network dependency.

## Relationship to prior work

Sawant's 2026 work introduces the relevant higher-independence questions and proves vertex decomposability for all trees; it explicitly identifies three-path-free complexes with two-independence complexes. The Pappus graph, a cubic symmetric graph of girth six and the Levi graph of the Pappus configuration, is not a tree or a chordal graph. Deshpande–Goyal–Sawant's 2026 classification concerns complete multipartite graphs, which do not include this graph. Separately published calculations of the **ordinary** Pappus independence complex and the **ordinary matching** complex concern different complexes and imply no specific integral homotopy type for \(\operatorname{Ind}_2(P)\). Indexed mathematical searches located no earlier equivalent calculation; this is evidence of noncoverage, not a proof of global priority.

## Limitations

The result concerns exactly the labeled Pappus graph (hence any isomorphic presentation) and the two-independence parameter. It does not extend to an arbitrary cubic symmetric graph and does not infer unseen higher homotopy invariants by homology alone. The nonstandard finite steps are exhaustively proved by the bundled deterministic checker; independently implemented replication and complete bibliographic priority certification remain outstanding. The available summaries of two related records did not provide their complete manuscripts, leaving a residual discovery risk.
