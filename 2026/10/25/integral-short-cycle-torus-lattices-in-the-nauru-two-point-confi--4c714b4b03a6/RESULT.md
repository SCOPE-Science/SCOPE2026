# Integral short-cycle torus lattices in the Nauru two-point configuration space

## Finding

Let \(N=\operatorname{GP}(12,5)\) be the 24-vertex Nauru graph, and \(X=\operatorname{UConf}_2(|N|)\) the unordered configuration space of two distinct points on its geometric realization. For two vertex-disjoint simple graph cycles \(C,D\), let \(T(C,D)\in H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\) denote the embedded product-torus fundamental class (with an arbitrary orientation). For \(p,q\in\{6,8\}\), \(p\le q\), write \(L_{p,q}\) for the integral span of those classes for which the component cycle lengths are \(p,q\).

**Theorem (integral short-cycle torus classification).** The group \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\) is free of rank \(54\). There are exactly \(30\), \(216\), and \(171\) disjoint simple-cycle pairs of lengths \((6,6)\), \((6,8)\), and \((8,8)\), respectively; their torus lattices have

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
(p,q)&\operatorname{rank} L_{p,q}&H_2(X;\mathbb Z)/L_{p,q}\\\hline
(6,6)&30&\mathbb Z^{24}\\
(6,8)&54&(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2\\
(8,8)&54&0.
\end{array}
\]

In particular, \(54\) explicitly selected octagon-pair tori form an **integral basis**, whereas the hexagon–octagon tori rationally span but leave precisely two independent parity obstructions. The full \(144\)-element automorphism group has orbit-size partitions \((12,18)\), \((72,72,72)\), and \((9,18,36,36,72)\) on these three families, respectively.

## Assumptions and scope

Use the standard, unweighted simple graph with outer vertices \(u_i=i\), inner vertices \(v_i=12+i\), for \(0\le i<12\), and edges \(\{u_i,u_{i+1}\},\{u_i,v_i\},\{v_i,v_{i+5}\}\), where indices are reduced modulo \(12\). Edges are oriented from smaller to larger endpoint. The topological configuration space excludes collisions; the disjoint-support cubical model is a deformation retract for two particles by the equivariant deleted-product result of Barnett–Farber. For disjoint graph cycles, their geometric product embeds a torus in the unordered space; the two factors are distinguished by their disjoint supports. The theorem concerns cycle lengths at most eight, not all longer-cycle sublattices or the fundamental group.

## Proof

**Finite cubical model.** The unordered disjoint-support model has \(276\) zero-cells \(\{v,w\}\), \(792\) one-cells \((e,w)\) for edges \(e\) remote from \(w\), and \(558\) two-cells \(\{e,f\}\) with disjoint endpoints. For an oriented edge \(e=[a,b]\), the one-cell boundary is \(\{b,w\}-\{a,w\}\). Order graph edges lexicographically. For \(e_i=[a,b]\), \(e_j=[c,d]\), \(i<j\), the two-cell boundary is \((e_j,b)-(e_j,a)-(e_i,d)+(e_i,c)\). The verifier builds these integral boundary operators, checks their composite is zero for every two-cell, and performs a complete elimination modulo \(101\), exhibiting \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_{101}}\partial_2=504\). Therefore the rational boundary rank is at least \(504\).

**Geometric cycle construction.** Orient each simple cycle by its canonical DFS listing and write its signed edge chain as \(\sum_e a_e e\). For disjoint cycles \(C,D\), their torus chain has coefficient \(a_e b_f\) on \(\{e,f\}\) when \(e<f\), and \(-a_e b_f\) when \(f<e\). Its boundary vanishes by the product boundary formula, also checked directly. An exhaustive least-vertex/simple-path search lists exactly \(12\) six-cycles and \(54\) eight-cycles; unordered vertex-disjoint pairs give the three family sizes in the theorem. The verifier constructs each of the \(417\) signed torus cycles and tests that its boundary is zero.

**Unit-minor certificate and integral basis.** In increasing order of cycle-search output, take octagon-pair torus columns numbered (zero-based)

`[0,1,2,3,5,6,7,8,9,11,12,13,14,15,17,18,19,20,21,23,25,26,27,28,30,31,32,33,36,38,39,40,41,43,44,49,56,57,58,59,60,62,63,65,68,76,79,83,84,89,106,117,122,148]`.

Select cubical two-cell rows (zero-based, the lexicographically indexed disjoint graph-edge pairs)

`[0,2,4,6,8,10,12,14,16,19,31,33,35,37,39,41,43,45,47,93,95,97,99,101,103,105,107,151,153,155,157,159,161,163,205,207,209,211,213,215,255,257,259,261,263,301,303,305,307,343,345,347,381,383]`.

Their \(54\)-by-\(54\) integer minor has exact determinant \(-1\). Thus the selected cycle lattice is a direct summand of the free group of all two-chains, with rank \(54\). Because these are genuine two-cycles, \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\partial_2\le558-54=504\). Combining this with the preceding modular lower bound gives exact rational rank \(504\), hence \(\ker\partial_2\) has rank \(54\). The kernel is itself a free subgroup of an integral chain group; the selected unit-minor cycles form a direct summand of the ambient free chain group. Since that summand lies in the kernel with the same rank, the remaining kernel intersection has rank zero and is therefore zero. Consequently \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)=\ker\partial_2\cong\mathbb Z^{54}\), freely generated by these octagon tori; there are no three-cells.

**Integral family quotients.** Invert the displayed unimodular minor over \(\mathbb Z\), and use the specified 54 rows to express all torus cycles as unique integer coordinate columns in that basis. The full-vector reconstruction is checked for every torus. Exact Smith normal forms of the resulting integer matrices have \(30\) unit factors for the \((6,6)\) family; \(52\) unit factors and two factors \(2\) for \((6,8)\); and \(54\) unit factors for \((8,8)\). This gives all three integral quotients, without identifying a mod-two rank with an integral index.

**Symmetry.** Complete graph isomorphism backtracking enumerates \(144\) automorphisms. Acting on unordered pairs of cycle vertex supports and closing each orbit gives precisely the claimed orbit-size partitions. This action does not depend on torus orientation.

## Verification

The standalone file `verify.py` constructs the graph and all cells and cycles from the edge formula, checks \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\), obtains boundary ranks modulo \(2,3,101\), verifies the integral determinant, performs exact integer homology-coordinate recovery and Smith normal forms, and enumerates all automorphisms and orbit sizes. Run `python verify.py` after installing the versions in `requirements.txt`. Its final success line is `VERIFY_OK_NAURU_SHORT_CYCLE_TORUS_LATTICES`. No supplied numerical log is needed to replay a certificate.

## Relationship to prior work

Eppstein's December 2007 exposition identifies the Nauru graph with \(\operatorname{GP}(12,5)\), exhibits its hexagonal torus map, and gives its symmetry order. It does not classify two-point configuration-space homology. Barnett–Farber established the equivariant two-particle deleted-product model and developed intersection theory, principally giving explicit planar graph calculations. Farber–Hanbury studied mature graphs and general nonplanar intersection methods; their results do not specify the Nauru octagon-torus integral lattice. An–Knudsen prove a planar graph-braid geometric-generator theorem and exhibit a nonplanar obstruction, but their planar hypothesis does not imply the present classification for this nonplanar graph. The new information is the explicit integral classification of **each natural short-cycle torus family**, including a unit-minor octagon basis and the index-four mixed family, rather than a generic claim that product tori exist.

## Limitations

The theorem does not identify \(H_1\) integrally, the graph-braid fundamental group, or cup products. The result is specific to the Nauru graph and gives no automatic theorem for all generalized Petersen graphs or all toroidal graphs. All claims about bases and indices depend on finite exact computations, which are fully executable from the included definitions and not a substitute for a symbolic family-wide proof. Originality has not been independently audited; unindexed specialized tables or inaccessible literature might already contain equivalent lattice calculations.
