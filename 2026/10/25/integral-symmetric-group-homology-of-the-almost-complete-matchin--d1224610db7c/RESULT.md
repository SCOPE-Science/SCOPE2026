# Integral symmetric-group homology of the almost-complete matching complex

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge3\), consider the higher-matching complex \(X_n=M_{n-2}(K_n)\), consisting of edge subsets of the **simple, loopless** complete graph on \([n]\) in which no vertex meets all other vertices. Let \(d_n=\binom{n-1}{2}-1\). The complete *integral* symmetric-group representation on reduced homology is
\[
\widetilde H_{d_n}(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\operatorname{sgn}_n^{\otimes(n-1)}\otimes U_{n,\mathbb Z},\qquad
U_{n,\mathbb Z}=\{(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\in\mathbb Z^n:\sum_i a_i=0\},
\]
where \(S_n\) acts by permuting the coordinates of \(U_{n,\mathbb Z}\); all other reduced integral homology groups vanish. In particular, the degree-\(d_n\) rational character is
\[
\chi_n(\sigma)=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)^{n-1}\bigl(\#\operatorname{Fix}_{[n]}(\sigma)-1\bigr).
\]
When \(n\) is odd this is the standard augmentation module, while for even \(n\) it is its sign twist. These are assertions about the natural action, not just abstract free abelian groups.

## Assumptions and scope
Fix an integer \(n\ge3\), let \(E=\binom{[n]}2\) be the \(m=\binom n2\) unordered edges, and let \(S_v\subset E\) be the full star at \(v\). Each simplex of \(X_n\) is an \(F\subseteq E\) with \(S_v\not\subseteq F\) for all \(v\). The action is induced by ground-vertex permutations, with the usual oriented simplicial-chain action. All homology and representations in the main theorem use integer coefficients. No loops, multiple edges, or extra graph parameters are allowed. The known nonequivariant sphere-wedge theorem is due to Singh [1]; the integral equivariant identification is the finding here.

## Proof
**1. Dual cover.** Let \(Y=X_n^\vee\) be the combinatorial Alexander dual on the full edge universe \(E\): a subset \(T\subseteq E\) belongs to \(Y\) exactly when \(E\setminus T\notin X_n\). Hence \(Y=\bigcup_{v=1}^n \Delta(E\setminus S_v)\). For a nonempty subset \(A\subseteq[n]\), the intersection of those simplex facets indexed by \(A\) is precisely \(\Delta(E(K_{n-|A|}))\), the simplex whose vertices are edges both of whose endpoints lie outside \(A\). Its geometric realization is nonempty and contractible exactly when \(|A|\le n-2\); when \(|A|\ge n-1\) its edge set is empty and its geometric realization is empty. In particular, the nerve is the \((n-3)\)-skeleton \(N\) of the \((n-1)\)-simplex with vertex set \([n]\).

**2. Integral equivariance of the nerve step.** The cover is preserved by \(S_n\). The augmented Čech double complex of this finite cover, constructed from simplicial chains of each intersection, has vertical ordinary homology equal to \(\mathbb Z\) in degree zero and zero in higher degrees for every nonempty intersection. The horizontal augmented complex is thus the augmented simplicial chain complex of \(N\), with the actual signed permutation of indexed intersections. The spectral-sequence comparison gives an \(S_n\)-equivariant integral homology isomorphism \(\widetilde H_*(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H_*(N;\mathbb Z)\); it does not require choosing a nonequivariant nerve homotopy equivalence.

**3. Top cycles of the skeleton.** The complex \(N\) has zero reduced integral homology except in degree \(n-3\). Indeed, its simplicial chain complex agrees with the full simplex in every degree through \(n-3\), and full-simplex augmented chains are exact. The top cycles are \(\operatorname{im}(\partial_{n-2})\) from the full simplex. Orient the facets of the boundary of that simplex by the global ground order. The free group generated by its \((n-2)\)-faces is the \(S_n\)-lattice \(\operatorname{sgn}_n\otimes\mathbb Z^n\): an oriented facet missing \(v\), multiplied by \((-1)^{v-1}\), transforms by total permutation sign and permutation of the missing label. The kernel of the boundary from those facets is the rank-one \(\operatorname{sgn}_n\)-lattice spanned by the oriented boundary of the full simplex, which corresponds to the diagonal \(\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1)\). Therefore
\[
\widetilde H_{n-3}(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong
\operatorname{sgn}_n\otimes\bigl(\mathbb Z^n/\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1)\bigr).
\]
For \(n=3\), this is the ordinary reduced degree-zero homology of three points, and the same augmented argument applies. This is torsion-free. By the integral universal-coefficient theorem,
\[
\widetilde H^{n-3}(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong
\operatorname{sgn}_n\otimes U_{n,\mathbb Z},
\]
and all other reduced integral cohomology vanishes. Here the dual of the quotient permutation lattice is exactly the kernel of the coordinate-sum map, not the quotient lattice itself.

**4. Equivariant Alexander duality and its orientation sign.** Combinatorial Alexander duality on the \(m\)-element edge universe, with its signed complementary-face chain identification, gives
\[
\widetilde H_j(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong
\operatorname{sgn}_{E}\otimes\widetilde H^{m-j-3}(Y;\mathbb Z)
\]
as \(\mathbb Z[S_n]\)-modules, where \(\operatorname{sgn}_{E}\) is the one-dimensional orientation character of the permutation of the \(m\) graph edges. Complementation reverses boundary versus coboundary with the ambient edge-order sign; reordering that full ambient edge order under a group element contributes exactly \(\operatorname{sgn}_{E}\). Since a transposition of ground vertices swaps exactly \(n-2\) pairs of edges and all transpositions generate \(S_n\), \(\operatorname{sgn}_{E}=\operatorname{sgn}_n^{n-2}\). Solving \(m-j-3=n-3\) gives \(j=m-n=\binom{n-1}2-1=d_n\). Tensoring the two orientation characters yields \(\operatorname{sgn}_n^{n-1}\otimes U_{n,\mathbb Z}\) in precisely that degree. This proves both the integral module and homology concentration.

**5. Character and invariant-space consequences.** The rational augmentation character is the number of fixed ground vertices minus one; tensoring with the one-dimensional sign power gives the formula. For any subgroup \(H\le S_n\), if \(n\) is odd (or \(H\subseteq A_n\)) the rational \(H\)-fixed top homology has dimension one fewer than the number of \(H\)-orbits on \([n]\). If \(n\) is even and \(H\not\subseteq A_n\), its dimension equals the number of vertex orbits for which the point stabilizer is contained in \(A_n\). Indeed, a sign-equivariant function on a transitive orbit exists exactly when the stabilizer acts trivially through sign; when sign is nontrivial on \(H\), every such function automatically has coordinate sum zero. These formulas are consequences of the integral lattice classification, not substitute evidence for it.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` using Python's standard library. It constructs the edge-orbit decomposition under a representative of every ground-permutation conjugacy class for \(3\le n\le6\), enumerates invariant simplices, and computes the exact signed augmented Lefschetz trace. There are \(26\) checked conjugacy classes, and every trace agrees with the predicted character after the degree parity correction. It independently enumerates all simplices and computes reduced homology ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\) for \(n=3,4,5\); the only nonzero reduced Betti number equals \(n-1\) in degree \(d_n\). This is corroborative finite evidence, whereas Steps 1–4 are the proof for all \(n\ge3\).

## Relationship to prior work
Singh [1, Theorem 1.1] already proves the **nonequivariant** homotopy equivalence \(M_{n-2}(K_n)\simeq\bigvee^{n-1} S^{d_n}\). That statement does not specify the symmetric-group action, its parity dependence, or an integral isomorphism class of group lattices. Wachs's survey [2, §7.1, Theorem 7.1] discusses much broader rational symmetric-group representations of bounded-degree **loop-allowing** graph complexes, following Reiner–Roberts; here the edge universe is explicitly loopless, and the integral lattice distinction is essential. The explicit dual nerve computation independently handles this special loopless family. Other group-equivariant calculations on matching, Morse, or graph-domination complexes use different simplicial objects and do not imply the present integral lattice classification simply by matching ranks or character shapes.

## Limitations
The theorem does not classify the equivariant homotopy type, extend to degree bounds other than \(n-2\), or assert that a homology-module isomorphism is an equivariant homotopy equivalence. It does not determine integral torsion in arbitrary higher matching complexes; this special family is torsion-free. The finite verifier is not an infinite proof. A differently formulated or unindexed antecedent for this specific loopless integral lattice remains possible and would require further literature comparison.
