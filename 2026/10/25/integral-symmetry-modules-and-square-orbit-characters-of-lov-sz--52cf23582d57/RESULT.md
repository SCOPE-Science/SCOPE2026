# Integral symmetry modules and square-orbit characters of Lovász neighborhood complexes

## Finding

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite connected simple graph with at least one edge, and suppose **no edge belongs to two different simple four-cycles**. Write \(\mathcal N(G)\) for the ordinary *open*-neighborhood simplicial complex: its nonempty faces are the vertex subsets contained in \(N_G(u)\) for some \(u\in V\). Let \(\Gamma\leq\operatorname{Aut}(G)\) be any group of graph automorphisms.

Form the canonical bipartite double cover \(B(G)\), with vertices \(v_L,v_R\) for \(v\in V\) and an edge \([u_L,v_R]\) for each ordered adjacent pair \((u,v)\). Each simple four-cycle of \(G\) lifts to exactly **two** four-cycles in \(B(G)\); let \(\mathscr L\) denote this set of lifted squares. Attach one two-cell along every square in \(\mathscr L\), and call the resulting cellular complex \(W(G)\).

**Equivariant model.** The natural \(\Gamma\)-action on \(\mathcal N(G)\) and on \(W(G)\) is related by a zigzag of explicit \(\Gamma\)-equivariant strong deformation retractions. Consequently the integral homology representation has the following canonical exact sequence of \(\mathbb Z[\Gamma]\)-modules:

\[
0\longrightarrow \mathbb Z[\mathscr L]_{\mathrm{or}}
\overset{\partial_2}{\longrightarrow}
\ker\!\left(\mathbb Z^{\vec E(G)}\overset{\partial_1}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb Z^{V_L\sqcup V_R}\right)
\longrightarrow H_1(\mathcal N(G);\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow0.
\]

The middle free module has basis the edges \([u_L,v_R]\), each oriented from left to right, and \(\partial_1[u_L,v_R]=[v_R]-[u_L]\). The first module is freely generated by choices of orientations of the lifted squares, with orientation reversal acting as a minus sign. The map \(\partial_2\) sends each oriented square to its cellular boundary; it is injective. In particular, \(H_1\) is torsion-free, and all homology in dimensions at least two vanishes.

**Complete rational automorphism character.** For \(\gamma\in\Gamma\), let \(v_\gamma\) be the number of vertices fixed by \(\gamma\); let \(e_\gamma\) count edges \(\{u,v\}\) with **both endpoints individually fixed**. Let \(c_\gamma\) be the number of connected components of \(B(G)\) preserved setwise by \(\gamma\). For every lifted square \(Q\in\mathscr L\) fixed setwise by \(\gamma\), put \(\epsilon_\gamma(Q)=1\) when its cyclic orientation is preserved and \(-1\) when reversed. Then

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\gamma\mid H_1(\mathcal N(G);\mathbb Q)\right)
=c_\gamma+2e_\gamma-2v_\gamma-
\sum_{\substack{Q\in\mathscr L\\\gamma Q=Q}}\epsilon_\gamma(Q).
}
\]

Here \(c_\gamma=1\) if \(G\) is non-bipartite. If \(G\) is bipartite, it equals \(2\) when \(\gamma\) preserves the two original color classes and \(0\) when \(\gamma\) exchanges them. Thus the formula gives the *full rational character* of the action of every automorphism group, rather than just the homology ranks.

For each original four-cycle fixed by \(\gamma\), the two lifts either are exchanged and contribute zero to the signed sum, or are individually fixed. In the latter case they contribute \(+2\) for the identity or half-turn on that cycle and \(-2\) for a reflection that preserves its opposite-vertex pairs. The trace formula therefore requires only fixed vertices, pointwise-fixed edges, and the induced permutations of the original four-cycles.

## Assumptions and scope

A simple four-cycle uses four distinct vertices and four cyclic edges, and two cycles are identified under reversal and cyclic rotation; they may have diagonals. The hypothesis allows triangles, bridges, leaves, arbitrarily large degrees, and distinct squares that meet at vertices. Only **shared edges between different squares** are forbidden. There are no isolated vertices because \(G\) is connected and has an edge.

The automorphism action is the ordinary vertex relabeling on the open-neighborhood complex, not the extra factor-swapping involution of a box complex. The square module uses the naturally induced signed cellular action. No arbitrary orientation choice affects its abstract module or character. The theorem does not claim an explicit irreducible *integral* splitting, which need not follow from a rational character.

## Proof

**1. Unique-center lemma.** If two vertices \(p,q\) have at least two common neighbors \(u,w\), then \(u-p-w-q-u\) is a four-cycle. They cannot have three common neighbors: two resulting distinct squares would share an edge. Hence every pair of vertices has at most two common neighbors. Moreover, a pair has exactly two common neighbors precisely when it is one opposite-vertex pair of a square. Every face of \(\mathcal N(G)\) with at least three vertices has a *unique* common-neighbor center; otherwise two distinct squares using different pairs of the face would share an edge.

**2. A canonical two-sided bridge.** Introduce left and right copies of the vertex set. Let \(Z(G)\) be the simplicial complex
\[
Z(G)=\bigcup_{u\in V}\bigl(u_L*\Delta(N_G(u)_R)\bigr).
\]
Its right-only subcomplex is the copy \(\mathcal N(G)_R\). Send every left vertex \(u_L\) affinely to the barycenter of \(\Delta(N_G(u)_R)\), fixing the right vertices. On each simplex the straight-line homotopy stays inside the corresponding simplex \(u_L*\Delta(N_G(u)_R)\), and fixes \(\mathcal N(G)_R\). Since barycenters commute with graph automorphisms, this is a \(\Gamma\)-equivariant strong deformation retraction \(|Z(G)|\to|\mathcal N(G)|\).

**3. A second canonical retraction.** Inside \(Z(G)\), retain the double-cover graph \(B(G)\). Whenever \(p_R,q_R\) have precisely two common left neighbors \(u_L,w_L\), also retain \([p_R,q_R]\) and the two triangles \([u_L,p_R,q_R]\), \([w_L,p_R,q_R]\). Denote the resulting subcomplex \(W_\Delta(G)\). Its paired triangles fill the two lifted squares arising from each original four-cycle, so its realization is canonically \(|W(G)|\). All these constructions are invariant under \(\Gamma\).

For a nonempty face \(F\) of \(Z(G)\), write \(b_F\) for its barycenter. Define a map on barycentric-subdivision vertices as follows. If \(F\) lies in \(W_\Delta(G)\), send \(b_F\) to itself. Otherwise, send \(b_F\) to the left vertex \(u_L\), where \(u\) is the **unique** center with \(F\subseteq\Delta(N_G(u)_R)\) if \(F\) is right-only, or the left vertex already in \(F\) if \(F\) contains one. Uniqueness follows from Step 1; every non-retained right-only face has at least two vertices and cannot be a pair with two common neighbors.

This extends affinely over each simplex of the barycentric subdivision. Indeed, along a nested chain of faces, once a non-retained face appears all non-retained faces have the same center; the retained lower faces belong to one retained right vertex or edge, possibly joined with that center. Hence their images lie together in a simplex of \(W_\Delta(G)\). The resulting map fixes \(|W_\Delta(G)|\). Moreover, on each subdivided simplex its source and image lie together in the original cone over its maximal right face, so their straight-line interpolation lies in \(|Z(G)|\). It is therefore a strong deformation retraction \(|Z(G)|\to|W_\Delta(G)|\). Every assignment is uniquely defined from the graph and its four-cycles, making this retraction \(\Gamma\)-equivariant. Combined with Step 2 it establishes the equivariant model.

**4. Integral chains and vanishing of higher homology.** The two triangles in each paired disk can be viewed as one square two-cell with four boundary edges in \(B(G)\). Distinct lifted squares share **no edges** of \(B(G)\): their underlying original squares are edge-disjoint, and the two different lifts of one original square use complementary lifted edges. Thus each cellular two-boundary has an edge with coefficient \(\pm1\) absent from all other two-boundaries. The cellular boundary map \(\partial_2\) is injective over \(\mathbb Z\), and its image is a primitive subgroup of the free one-chain module. It follows that \(H_2(W(G);\mathbb Z)=0\), all higher chain groups vanish, and \(H_1\) is torsion-free. The cellular exact sequence in the finding is now simply \(\ker(\partial_1)/\operatorname{im}(\partial_2)\), transported across the equivariant deformation retractions.

**5. The character.** Give each edge \([u_L,v_R]\) its left-to-right orientation. No graph automorphism reverses such a directed edge because left and right copies remain distinct. Therefore the traces on cellular zero- and one-chains are \(2v_\gamma\) and \(2e_\gamma\), respectively. The trace on two-chains is the signed count \(\sum_{\gamma Q=Q}\epsilon_\gamma(Q)\). The zeroth homology is the permutation module on components of the double cover, with trace \(c_\gamma\). As there is no higher homology, the equivariant Euler identity gives exactly the displayed formula for the trace on first rational homology. The stated component cases follow from the parity of walks in a connected graph.

## Verification

Run `python verify.py` after installing the single package in `requirements.txt`. The verifier independently builds the actual simplicial neighborhood complex, computes the signed simplicial Lefschetz sum face-by-face for every tested automorphism, builds both oriented cellular square-boundary vectors, checks disjoint boundary supports and their induced signed permutation action, and compares the two trace computations. It also verifies the Euler characteristic and predicted integral Betti rank.

The exhaustive *unlabeled Graph Atlas* check covers **all 126 connected isomorphism types with at most seven vertices that contain at least one four-cycle and satisfy the edge-disjoint-square condition**, comprising **386 distinct automorphism actions**. Twelve more actions are checked on larger graphs assembled from a square joined by a bridge to another square or a pentagon. Every check passes. These finite computations test the sign and normalization conventions; they do not replace the proof for arbitrary finite graphs or constitute external validation.

## Relationship to prior work

The 2026 study *Graph-like neighborhood complexes with edge-disjoint four-cycles* constructs a graph-and-filled-square model and determines nonequivariant homotopy types. Its published statement and proof do not establish the full graph-automorphism-equivariant equivalence, the natural **integral representation presentation**, or the signed fixed-square character of every automorphism. The present result gives a canonical equivariant intermediary and an explicit character on the same broad graph family; it does **not** claim the previously known Betti or wedge formulas as new.

Kamibeppu's 2008 Theorem 3 gives a strong \(\mathbb Z_2\)-retraction for box complexes **if and only if there is no four-cycle**; it cannot give the square-cell correction for the graphs here that contain squares. Arana and Stehlík's 2025 quadrangulation theorem classifies a different surface regime, rather than arbitrary connected edge-disjoint-square graphs or their complete automorphism characters. Known isolated character computations on specific high-girth graphs do not supply the present general square-orbit formula.

## Limitations

The edge-disjointness assumption is essential for the unique-center construction and independent cellular square boundaries. When squares share edges, the two-cell model and its homological injection can fail. The theorem determines the rational character and an explicit integral module presentation, but does not assert an integral irreducible decomposition or a fixed-point-space classification. It does not upgrade finite checker results into an infinite enumeration. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed. Unindexed mathematically equivalent work cannot be categorically excluded.
