# Integral symmetry modules of multipartite induced-forest complexes

## Finding
Let \(r\ge 2\), let \(A_1,\ldots,A_r\) be nonempty pairwise disjoint finite sets, and let \(G=K_{|A_1|,\ldots,|A_r|}\) have all edges between distinct parts and none inside a part. Define \(\mathcal F(G)=\{S\subseteq V(G):G[S]\text{ is acyclic}\}\). Write \(U_i=\ker(\mathbb Z[A_i]\xrightarrow{\epsilon}\mathbb Z)\) and \(W=\ker(\mathbb Z^{[r]}\xrightarrow{\epsilon}\mathbb Z)\), where \(\epsilon\) sums coefficients.

For the entire automorphism group \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), including permutations of parts of equal cardinality, there are **natural isomorphisms of integral modules**
\[
\widetilde H_1(\mathcal F(G);\mathbb Z)\cong\bigwedge^2 W,
\qquad
\widetilde H_2(\mathcal F(G);\mathbb Z)\cong\bigoplus_{1\le i<j\le r}U_i\otimes_{\mathbb Z}U_j.
\]
All other reduced integral homology groups vanish. Part permutations act on \(\bigwedge^2W\) by permuting the basis of \(\mathbb Z^{[r]}\), and on the second direct sum by transporting factors; **a swap of two parts exchanges tensor factors without an extra minus sign**. Thus \(\widetilde\beta_1=\frac{(r-1)(r-2)}2\) and \(\widetilde\beta_2=\sum_{i<j}(|A_i|-1)(|A_j|-1)\). The underlying nonequivariant homotopy ranks are known from Carnero Bravo; the new assertion is the full integral automorphism-module structure and its character formula below.

For any \(g\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), let \(\sigma\) be its permutation of the parts. Set \(c_1=|\{i:\sigma(i)=i\}|\) and let \(c_2\) be the number of two-cycles of \(\sigma\). For fixed parts set \(f_i=|\operatorname{Fix}(g|_{A_i})|\). For a two-cycle \((i\ j)\) of parts, set \(h_{ij}=|\operatorname{Fix}(g^2|_{A_i})|\) (the same value occurs for \(A_j\)). The integral characters, equivalently the rational traces, are
\[
\chi_1(g)=\frac{(c_1-1)(c_1-2)}2-c_2,
\]
\[
\chi_2(g)=\sum_{\substack{i<j\\\sigma(i)=i,\ \sigma(j)=j}}(f_i-1)(f_j-1)
+\sum_{(i\ j)\text{ a two-cycle of }\sigma}(h_{ij}-1).
\]
These formulas include all part-size patterns, including singleton parts. When all parts have the same size, there are no positive-dimensional rational homology classes fixed by every automorphism.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple and undirected. No lower bound beyond \(|A_i|\ge1\) is imposed; \(r\ge2\). The automorphism group consists of all within-part bijections together with permitted permutations of equally sized parts. This is the full automorphism group because graph nonadjacency (including equality) recovers the multipartition. Reduced simplicial homology is over \(\mathbb Z\); group actions include the canonical orientation signs on simplicial chains. The claim is about actual integral homology lattices, not only their ranks, rational characters, or a chosen nonequivariant sphere decomposition.

## Proof
**1. Description of faces and cover.** Every induced subgraph meeting three different parts contains a triangle. Every induced subgraph containing at least two vertices from each of two different parts contains a four-cycle. Conversely, a vertex set contained in one part is edgeless, and a vertex set meeting precisely two parts with a singleton on one side induces a star. Therefore \(S\in\mathcal F(G)\) exactly when it meets at most one part, or meets exactly two parts with one of the two intersections a singleton. For each unordered pair \(e=\{i,j\}\), put \(Y_e=\mathcal F(G[A_i\cup A_j])\). These complexes cover \(\mathcal F(G)\). If at least two different \(Y_e\)'s meet geometrically, their intersection is \(\Delta(A_i)\) if all their index pairs share one common index \(i\), and is otherwise empty. Thus every nonempty multiple intersection is a simplex, with \(H_q=0\) for \(q>0\) and \(H_0=\mathbb Z\).

**2. The two-part integral lattice.** Fix \(e=\{i,j\}\), abbreviate \(A=A_i\) and \(B=A_j\). Decompose \(Y_e=X_A\cup X_B\), where \(X_A=\Delta(A)*B^{(0)}\) and \(X_B=A^{(0)}*\Delta(B)\). Each is contractible, while \(X_A\cap X_B=A^{(0)}*B^{(0)}\) is the complete bipartite graph \(K_{|A|,|B|}\) as a one-dimensional complex. The reduced Mayer--Vietoris connecting homomorphism is an **integral isomorphism** \(\widetilde H_2(Y_e;\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(K_{|A|,|B|};\mathbb Z)\) and all other positive homology of \(Y_e\) vanishes. Orient cross edges from \(A\) toward \(B\). Then the cycle lattice of the complete bipartite graph is canonically \(U_A\otimes U_B\): a simple tensor \(([a]-[a'])\otimes([b]-[b'])\) maps to the oriented four-edge cycle on \(a,a',b,b'\). Equivalently, its inverse image in \(H_2(Y_e)\) is represented, up to one global sign convention, by the triangular boundary \(\partial[a,a',b,b']\) of the formal tetrahedron missing from \(Y_e\). The four actual triangular faces all lie in \(Y_e\), so this is a genuine integral cycle. Switching \(a,a'\) or \(b,b'\) changes its sign; swapping entire parts maps \([a,a',b,b']\) to \([b,b',a,a']\), an even permutation. Hence part interchange acts by the **unsigned tensor flip** \(U_A\otimes U_B\to U_B\otimes U_A\). The construction is natural under all bijections of the parts.

**3. Equivariant cover spectral sequence.** Use the augmented Čech--simplicial double complex of the finite cover \(\{Y_e\}_{e\in E(K_r)}\), with the usual alternating signs from an ordering of cover members. Its first page is the direct sum of homology groups of all intersections. By step 1, only \(q=0\) for multiple intersections and \(q=2\) for single sets are nonzero. The \(q=0\) row is the ordinary simplicial chain complex of the cover nerve \(N\); the \((p,q)=(0,2)\) term is \(\bigoplus_e H_2(Y_e)\) and has no horizontal differential. This construction is natural, including the signs arising when an automorphism permutes cover indices. The nerve \(N\) has one vertex for every pair \(e=\{i,j\}\), with a simplex whenever a collection of pairs has a common index. It is covered by the simplices \(\Delta(E_i)\), where \(E_i=\{\{i,j\}:j\ne i\}\). Their pairwise intersections are single vertices and triple intersections are empty. A second application of the nerve theorem, or its natural Čech chain complex, identifies \(H_1(N;\mathbb Z)\) equivariantly with the cycle lattice \(H_1(K_r;\mathbb Z)\), and gives \(H_p(N)=0\) for \(p\ge2\). Consequently every potentially nonzero higher differential into or out of \((0,2)\) vanishes, and the first cover spectral sequence gives the canonical integral equivariant isomorphisms
\[
\widetilde H_1(\mathcal F(G))\cong H_1(K_r),\qquad
\widetilde H_2(\mathcal F(G))\cong\bigoplus_e H_2(Y_e),
\]
with all other reduced homology zero. There are no extension problems, since each positive homological degree has a single nonzero graded piece. A basis-independent identification of \(H_1(K_r)\) with \(\bigwedge^2W\) follows by taking the boundary map \(\partial:\bigwedge^2\mathbb Z^{[r]}\to W\), \([i]\wedge[j]\mapsto[j]-[i]\). Its kernel is exactly \(\bigwedge^2W\): split \(\mathbb Z^{[r]}=W\oplus\mathbb Z[i_0]\) as abelian groups. This completes the integral representation claim.

**4. Character calculation.** The trace of a permutation on \(W\) is \(c_1-1\); the trace of its square is \(c_1+2c_2-1\). Applying \(\operatorname{tr}(\wedge^2 T)=((\operatorname{tr}T)^2-\operatorname{tr}(T^2))/2\) gives \(\chi_1\). In degree two, an unordered part pair contributes to a trace only if that pair is setwise fixed. For two individually fixed parts, the tensor product trace is \((f_i-1)(f_j-1)\). For two parts interchanged by \(g\), the trace of the unsigned tensor flip composed with the bijections is the trace of \(g^2\) on \(U_i\), namely \(h_{ij}-1\). These exhaust the fixed unordered pairs, proving \(\chi_2\). All matrices arise from genuine integral permutation/augmentation lattices.

## Verification
The included standalone Python program checks eleven multipartite graphs, including singleton parts, unequal part sizes, and equal-part wreath actions. It enumerates all graph vertex subsets; verifies the forest-face characterization against an independent union--find cycle test; computes all simplicial boundary ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\); and checks the signed simplicial Lefschetz number against \(\chi_2(g)-\chi_1(g)\) for **every automorphism** in each of the eleven cases, **2,238 elements in total**. It reproduces the packaged output ending in `VERIFY_OK`. This finite computation corroborates the formula; the proof above establishes it for all integers and integrally. Mod-two rank checks alone would not establish integral torsion-freeness, which instead follows from the integral cover and connecting-map argument.

## Relationship to prior work
Taylan's 2015 paper introduced general forbidden-induced-subgraph (devoid) complexes and homotopy techniques. Carnero Bravo's 2023 preprint studies induced-forest filtrations and **already establishes the nonequivariant sphere-wedge classification** for complete multipartite graphs in its complete-bipartite theorem and multipartite corollary (latest inspected version: Theorem 33, Corollary 35; numbering may differ between versions). The ranks and wedge dimensions given above are therefore expressly **not claimed as new**. The present theorem refines this known calculation to natural integral automorphism modules and gives the complete character for block permutations, including the sign-sensitive two-block interchange. Nonequivariant homotopy classifications alone do not determine these actions. A separate 2026 path-free-complex paper on multipartite graphs treats a different forbidden-graph condition; it is not used as evidence of novelty.

## Limitations
The result determines homology with its automorphism action, not an equivariant homotopy-equivalence class or fixed-point-set homotopy types. It makes no assertion for non-complete multipartite graphs, or for other levels of the maximum-degree forest filtration. Although two highly relevant full texts were inspected and published mathematical records searched, an unindexed equivalent representation calculation remains possible. The finite verification examples are corroborative, not a substitute for the general proof. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
