# Scientific review of the integral triple-Kneser result

## Correctness

PASS. Every face of the six-point auxiliary complex is represented by one of the \(3^{10}\) complementary-pair choices and tested against all fifteen possible two-point transversals. All \(54,870\) nonempty faces and \(358,760\) Hasse covering relations are checked. An explicit \(27,398\)-pair acyclic Morse matching leaves critical-cell counts \((1,42,19,6,6)\) in dimensions \((0,5,6,7,8)\). Exact signed gradient reduction yields integer Morse boundary Smith forms with only 13 and six unit pivots, proving complete torsion-freeness. The published topological star-cluster/concentration construction is separately instantiated for the seven-point case.

## Originality

PASS under the available literature comparison, with residual bibliographic uncertainty. Feng and Wang (2024), Table 1, computed only mod-two ranks. Feng (2026), Theorem 18, establishes the rational ranks and expressly leaves integral torsion open in Question 19. Their combined results do not exclude odd-order torsion, whereas the present all-integer certificate does. Barmak's star-cluster theorem is acknowledged as a tool; it does not compute the needed integer Smith forms. Searches of mathematical records and available historical claims did not identify this exact integral result. The absence of indexed overlap is not a uniqueness proof.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The exact integral homology of \(\operatorname{Ind}(\mathrm{KG}(3,1))\) gives the first nontrivial answer to an explicit, recently posed family-wide torsion question. In particular it excludes odd torsion, which neither prior characteristic-two nor rational Betti calculations can detect. The proof also supplies a natural six-point finite certificate and a \(5\)-connectivity statement. No higher-parameter or sphere-wedge classification is claimed.

## Remaining risks

The discovery search cannot exclude unpublished overlap. The numerical portion is exact and exhaustively reproducible but computer-assisted. The stronger homotopy wedge question remains open in this argument. Independent scientific audit has not been performed.
