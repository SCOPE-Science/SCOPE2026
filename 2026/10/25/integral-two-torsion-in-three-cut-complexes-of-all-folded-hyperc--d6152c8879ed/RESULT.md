# Integral two-torsion in ordinary three-cut complexes of folded hypercubes

## Finding

**Theorem.** For every integer \(n\ge5\), let \(FQ_n\) be the antipodal vertex quotient of the binary \(n\)-cube, and put \(N=2^{n-1}\). Let \(\Delta_3(FQ_n)\) denote the **ordinary**, not total, three-cut complex. Write
\[
h_n=2^{n-4}(n^2-5n+8)-1,
\qquad b_n=\binom N2-\frac{Nn(n+1)}4.
\]
Then, **with integer coefficients**, its complete reduced homology is
\[
\boxed{\widetilde H_j(\Delta_3(FQ_n);\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z^{h_n}\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,&j=N-5,\\
\mathbb Z^{b_n},&j=N-4,\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}}
\]
Both ranks are nonnegative integers. For every \(n\ge5\), the nonzero two-torsion in the next-to-top degree prevents shellability. In fact, if \(X_n\) is the cubical two-skeleton of the antipodal quotient of the boundary of the standard \(n\)-cube, then the **combinatorial Alexander dual** has the full homotopy description
\[
\Delta_3(FQ_n)^\vee\simeq X_n\vee\bigvee^{b_n}S^1.
\]
The cubical quotient is homeomorphic to \(\mathbb{RP}^{n-1}\), so \(X_n\) is one of its CW two-skeleta. This is a statement about the Alexander dual; no homotopy decomposition of the original three-cut complex is claimed.

Examples: for the sixteen-vertex Clebsch graph \(FQ_5\), the only nonzero reduced group is \(\widetilde H_{11}\cong\mathbb Z^{15}\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\). For \(FQ_6\), the nonzero groups are \(\widetilde H_{27}\cong\mathbb Z^{55}\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\) and \(\widetilde H_{28}\cong\mathbb Z^{160}\).

## Assumptions and scope

The vertex set of \(FQ_n\) comprises the \(N\) antipodal classes \([x]=\{x,x+\mathbf1\}\) for words \(x\in\mathbb F_2^n\). Two distinct classes are adjacent exactly when some representatives differ in one coordinate. The graph is simple, connected, triangle-free, and \(n\)-regular for \(n\ge5\). Every unordered nonadjacent pair has either zero or exactly two common neighbors; in the latter case, the pair comprises opposite corners of a unique induced square. In particular, every four-cycle is an antipodal image of an ordinary cubical two-face, and no antipodal pair of cube points is joined by a walk of length four for \(n\ge5\). All cycles referred to below are embedded, with four distinct vertices.

For a finite graph \(G=(V,E)\), the ordinary three-cut complex \(\Delta_3(G)\) is generated by all facets \(V\setminus U\), where \(|U|=3\) and \(G[U]\) is disconnected. Its combinatorial Alexander dual is \(K=\{\sigma\subseteq V:V\setminus\sigma\notin\Delta_3(G)\}\). We use reduced homology and cohomology over \(\mathbb Z\) unless stated. The hypotheses \(n\ge5\) exclude the exceptional antipodal four-step identifications occurring in the four-cube.

## Proof

**1. Exact faces of the Alexander dual.** Let \(K=\Delta_3(FQ_n)^\vee\). Every vertex and every pair of graph vertices is a face of \(K\). A set \(\sigma\) with at least three vertices is in \(K\) exactly when *every* three-element subset induces a connected subgraph of \(FQ_n\). Because the graph has no triangles, its connected three-sets are precisely induced paths of length two. Hence the 2-faces of \(K\) are the two-edge paths, and the 3-faces are exactly induced four-cycles, viewed as filled tetrahedra: every triple in such a four-set is a two-edge path. Conversely a four-set whose every triple is connected and triangle-free must be a four-cycle.

There is no face containing five vertices. Otherwise, each of the ten three-element subsets of those five vertices would contain at least two graph edges, giving at least twenty edge occurrences. Each edge belongs to three of the ten triples, so there would be at least seven graph edges on five vertices. A triangle-free five-vertex graph has at most six edges by Mantel's bound, a contradiction. Thus \(K\) has no faces of dimension four or higher.

Let \(E=Nn/2\) be the number of graph edges and
\[
q=\frac{N}{4}\binom n2
\]
the number of embedded squares. The number of two-path triangles is \(N\binom n2=4q\), and every such triangle belongs to **exactly one** square tetrahedron: the nonadjacent endpoints have precisely two common neighbors. The two diagonals of each square are the only pairs of graph vertices at distance two in that square; the total number of unordered distance-two pairs is \(2q\).

**2. Explicit elementary collapses.** Consider an embedded square with cyclic vertices \(a,b,c,d\). Its tetrahedron \(abcd\) contains the triangle \(abc\) as a free codimension-one face, because every two-path triangle belongs to exactly one tetrahedron. Delete \((abc,abcd)\) by an elementary simplicial collapse. The diagonal edge \(ac\) now occurs in precisely one surviving triangle, \(acd\), since the diagonal has exactly the two common neighbors \(b,d\). Delete \((ac,acd)\) by a second elementary simplicial collapse. This leaves exactly the two triangles \(abd\) and \(bcd\), a square disk split along the other diagonal \(bd\). The two collapses can be carried out for **every** square independently: no diagonal belongs to two squares, and no two-path triangle belongs to two tetrahedra. Edges originally in \(FQ_n\) are never removed.

After all \(2q\) collapses, the surviving complex is precisely the original graph with one two-triangle disk filling *each* square, plus all edges of the complete graph joining vertices at graph distance at least three. These latter edges lie in no remaining triangle. The square-filled subcomplex is connected. Adding \(b\) edges between points of a connected CW complex wedges on exactly \(b\) circles (choose connecting paths, or contract a spanning tree). Since \(K\) initially contains all \(\binom N2\) edges, and exactly \(E+2q\) are either graph edges or distance-two diagonals, their number is
\[
b=\binom N2-E-2q
=\binom N2-\frac{Nn(n+1)}4=b_n.
\]
This proves \(K\simeq X_n\vee\bigvee^{b_n}S^1\), where \(X_n\) is the graph with its embedded squares attached as two-cells.

**3. Identify the projective square skeleton.** The boundary of the Euclidean \(n\)-cube is an \((n-1)\)-sphere, and central antipodal symmetry acts freely on it. Its quotient is homeomorphic to \(\mathbb{RP}^{n-1}\), using radial projection to the unit sphere and identification of antipodes. The cubical cell structure on the boundary descends to a CW structure, with vertices the antipodal classes of cube vertices, edges those of \(FQ_n\), and two-cells precisely the antipodal pairs of ordinary square faces. A projected square is embedded for \(n\ge5\); conversely, every four-cycle in \(FQ_n\) lifts to an ordinary cube square, since any four-step path cannot connect antipodes at Hamming distance \(n\ge5\). Hence the two-skeleton of this CW quotient is exactly \(X_n\).

Attaching cells of dimension at least three does not change the fundamental group. Therefore
\[
\pi_1(X_n)\cong\pi_1(\mathbb{RP}^{n-1})\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,
\quad H_1(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]
The two-dimensional cellular chain group is free, so \(H_2(X_n;\mathbb Z)\) is free as its subgroup. Counting quotient cells gives \(N\) vertices, \(E=Nn/2\) edges, and \(q=N\binom n2/4\) squares. Euler characteristic and the finiteness of \(H_1\) give
\[
\operatorname{rank}H_2(X_n)=\chi(X_n)-1=N-\frac{Nn}2+\frac N4\binom n2-1
=2^{n-4}(n^2-5n+8)-1=h_n.
\]
Because \(X_n\) is two-dimensional, all homology above degree two vanishes. Thus the reduced homology of \(K\) consists of \(H_1(K)\cong\mathbb Z^{b_n}\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\) and \(H_2(K)\cong\mathbb Z^{h_n}\), with no other nonzero groups.

**4. Integral cohomology and Alexander duality.** The integral universal-coefficient theorem yields
\[
\widetilde H^1(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{b_n},\qquad
\widetilde H^2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{h_n}\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]
The torsion contribution comes from \(\operatorname{Ext}(H_1(K),\mathbb Z)\), not from torsion in \(H_2(K)\). Combinatorial Alexander duality on \(N\) vertices gives
\[
\widetilde H_j(\Delta_3(FQ_n);\mathbb Z)
\cong\widetilde H^{N-j-3}(K;\mathbb Z).
\]
Only cohomological degrees one and two remain, proving the stated complete integral classification.

Finally, the facets of \(\Delta_3(FQ_n)\) all have \(N-3\) vertices, so it is pure of dimension \(N-4\). Nonzero torsion in degree \(N-5\) is incompatible with shellability of a pure complex. This proves nonshellability uniformly.

## Verification

The independent construction in the standalone, standard-library `verify.py` enumerates every vertex and edge of \(FQ_n\) for \(n=5,6,7\), verifies each common-neighbor intersection and every candidate four-set, and certifies that all two-path triangles are covered by exactly one four-cycle. It replays **both free-face collapses per square on the changing face sets**, checking the freeness conditions. The resulting full Alexander-dual face vectors (by dimension zero through three) are respectively \((16,120,160,40)\), \((32,496,480,120)\), and \((64,2016,1344,336)\). The program computes exact signed simplicial boundary ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\), and compares all resulting dimensions with the integer theorem's predictions via universal coefficients. Run `python3 verify.py` to reproduce all checks; it ends with `VERIFY_OK`.

These bounded checks do not themselves establish integral torsion or the theorem for untested dimensions; the topology of the quotient sphere and the integral universal-coefficient calculation do that work.

## Relationship to prior work

Bayer et al. [1,2] define and develop **ordinary** cut complexes and classify forests, cycles, prisms, various graph constructions, squared paths, and rectangular grids. Neither paper's inspected arguments classify antipodally folded cubes or prove their integral three-cut torsion. The graph theorem for edge-disjoint four-cycles [3] cannot apply: each edge of \(FQ_n\) lies in \(n-1\ge4\) distinct squares, so its required disjointness fails.

The square-completion result for closed-neighborhood Čech nerves [4] identifies a related square-filled graph topology; the Clebsch closed-neighborhood example [5] already exhibits projective two-torsion, and the folded-cube **open** neighborhood result [6] also uses the quotient square skeleton. These are acknowledged close precedents for the geometric mechanism. **They concern different simplicial complexes**: the closed Čech nerve retains different two-sphere cells, and the open neighborhood complex admits a different simplex criterion; neither theorem states or implies the displayed full integral homology of the **ordinary three-cut complex** without the present exact three-subset analysis, two square-by-square collapses, and Alexander duality. No novelty is claimed for the standard topology of projective space itself.

## Limitations

The result assumes \(n\ge5\), the ordinary (not total) cut convention, and exactly three vertices in each deleted disconnected set. The geometric conclusion is an **Alexander-dual homotopy equivalence** and an **integral homology classification of the original**; it does not give the homotopy type of the original cut complex, an equivariant classification, or persistence maps. Modulo-two and modulo-three calculations for three finite dimensions are corroboration only. Equivalent or stronger results may exist outside the inspected literature, especially under square-complex, Stanley–Reisner, or antipodal-cover terminology; the novelty comparison is not a proof of uniqueness.
