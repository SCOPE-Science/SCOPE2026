# Integral wedge homotopy for the multipath complex of the bidirected \(K_{3,3}\)

## Finding
Let \(D\) be the loopless bidirected complete bipartite digraph with vertex classes \(A=\{0,1,2\}\) and \(B=\{3,4,5\}\), so each of the nine undirected edges is represented by two opposite directed arcs. Define \(X(D)\) as the simplicial complex whose vertices are the 18 arcs of \(D\), with a finite arc set a simplex precisely when it contains at most one arc leaving and at most one arc entering each digraph vertex and it contains no directed cycle (including a directed two-cycle).

The complete integral homotopy type is
\[
X(D)\simeq\bigvee^{19}S^3\vee\bigvee^{12}S^4.
\]
Thus \(\widetilde H_3(X(D);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{19}\), \(\widetilde H_4(X(D);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{12}\), and all other reduced integral homology groups vanish. In particular no torsion occurs.

## Assumptions and scope
The digraph has exactly six distinct labeled vertices and precisely the 18 arcs between its bipartition classes, both orientations included; it has no loops or within-part arcs. The simplicial face condition rules out all directed cycles rather than allowing disjoint cyclic components. Isolated digraph vertices impose no additional restriction. Homotopy and homology refer to the geometric realization of this finite abstract simplicial complex, with integral coefficients for the latter. No statement for larger bipartite parts is asserted.

## Proof
**Exhaustive face set.** Index arcs in this order: first all \((a,b)\) for \(a\in A\), \(b\in B\) in increasing lexicographic order, then all \((b,a)\) with \(a\in A\), \(b\in B\) in the same order. A face is a strictly increasing tuple of these indices. The face predicate checks the indegree and outdegree restrictions and follows successor pointers to rule out cycles. Every allowed arc set has at most five arcs: the directed components are vertex-disjoint paths on six vertices. Enumeration of every subset of 18 arcs of size at most five gives the exact cardinality-specific face counts \((18,108,264,252,72)\). The alternating sum gives reduced Euler characteristic \(-7\), independently agreeing with the claimed wedge.

**A complete acyclic discrete Morse matching.** Start with the finite nonempty face poset and traverse arc indices in the deterministic order \((11,9,0,16,2,15,10,3,5,17,14,6,7,8,4,1,13,12)\). For each index, inspect unmatched faces in increasing (cardinality, lexicographic) order and pair an unmatched face \(F\) with \(F\cup\{i\}\) whenever both remain unmatched. Orient each pair upward and each other covering relation downward. A topological sort of all 714 nodes establishes acyclicity. Initially this matching leaves one vertex, four two-dimensional simplices, 23 three-dimensional simplices, and 12 four-dimensional simplices.

Four elementary cancellations then remove the remaining four critical two-cells along unique gradient paths to critical three-cells. The successive (lower-critical tuple; upper-critical tuple; number of alternating incidences) are \(((2,10,12);(2,4,12,17);3)\), \(((0,4,17);(1,6,12,17);7)\), \(((5,7,9);(1,8,9,13);7)\), and \(((0,7,14);(1,8,14,15);9)\). For each step the verifier searches all gradient paths (stopping after finding two), checks uniqueness for the selected pair, reverses its gradient path, and verifies the new directed Hasse diagram is acyclic. This is a finite explicit, reproducible certificate, not a statistical experiment. The resulting 341 pairs cover 682 faces, leaving one critical zero-cell, nineteen critical three-cells, and twelve critical four-cells, and no other critical cells.

**Integral attaching maps.** Orient each simplex by ascending arc indices. For a critical simplex, compute its cellular Morse boundary over \(\mathbb Z\) by recursively eliminating matched lower faces using the oriented simplicial incidence numbers. The standalone checker evaluates every critical cell in every dimension, and **all resulting integral Morse boundary columns are exactly zero**. In particular the Morse CW complex has one zero-cell, no one- or two-cells, 19 three-cells and 12 four-cells. Its three-skeleton is therefore \(\bigvee^{19}S^3\). Each four-cell attaching map lies in \(\pi_3(\bigvee^{19}S^3)\cong H_3(\bigvee^{19}S^3;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{19}\), by the Hurewicz theorem. Its coordinates are the corresponding integral Morse differential column, which is zero. Hence each attaching map is nullhomotopic, and the four-cells split off as twelve four-spheres. This proves the displayed homotopy equivalence.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_bidirected_k33.py` with Python 3 and the standard library; no external data are required. The checker independently regenerates all faces, tests all Hasse incidence directions by topological sorting, reproduces four uniquely permitted gradient-path cancellations, and reduces every critical oriented boundary over the integers. The expected output states 714 nonempty faces, 341 matched pairs, critical-cell counts \((1,19,12)\) in degrees \((0,3,4)\), and zero nonzero integral boundary columns. The argument above explains why a zero boundary implies the homotopy type in this special two-consecutive-high-dimension setting; equality of homology groups alone would not suffice.

## Relationship to prior work
Caputi–Collari–Di Trani–Smith, *On the homotopy type of multipath complexes* (first public preprint 9 August 2022, arXiv:2208.04656), supplies the multipath-complex framework. Its complete bipartite digraph calculation treats all arcs consistently oriented from one part to the other, rather than both orientations; that complex is a different (chessboard/matching) complex and does not imply the present ranks or wedge type. Earlier, Vrećica–Živaljević (arXiv:0710.5252) introduced cycle-free chessboard complexes and general digraph path complexes; the present complex can be viewed as a six-by-six off-diagonal-block restriction, while their displayed connectivity and structural results for broader chessboards do not give this exact homotopy calculation. The multipath-complex work of Caputi–Collari–Di Trani on path posets treats related homotopy-realizability questions but not this case. The bidirected double-star calculations concern a different underlying graph and cannot imply this statement. A separately studied discrete-Morse-matching complex of the undirected graph \(K_{3,3}\) is not the directed multipath complex.

## Limitations
This is a finite six-vertex classification, not an all-parameter theorem for bidirected complete bipartite graphs. The integer computation is mathematically exhaustive only because the face predicate and finite upper bound are proved and because every incidence and gradient path is checked; the program's output alone is not a proof without those arguments. Literature equivalence risk remains for unindexed or differently named restricted cycle-free chessboard complexes. There has been no independent external validation.
