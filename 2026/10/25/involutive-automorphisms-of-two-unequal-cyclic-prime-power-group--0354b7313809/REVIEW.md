# Scientific review: involutive automorphisms of two unequal cyclic prime-power groups

## Correctness — PASS
The matrix parameterization follows from the homomorphism order constraints and the diagonal-unit invertibility criterion. Direct squaring gives exactly four congruences, including both mixed-entry conditions. For odd primes, the complementary \(\pm1\) eigenspaces give four cyclic summand-allocation types; orbit–stabilizer yields precisely two singleton types and two orbits of size \(p^{2a}\). For the prime two, the four-square-root criterion modulo \(2^b\) and a complete partition by the solutions of \(r^2=1\pmod{2^a}\) yield the three exponent-gap branches. The separate small-exponent cases handle the otherwise exceptional root counts. All 27 independent direct-squaring sample computations pass. Finite tests do not prove the universal formula; the proof does.

## Originality — PASS (under best-knowledge scope)
Hillar–Rhea's matrix model and Mader's structural theory provide the standard automorphism setup but do not count elements with square equal to the identity. Hage–Harju (2007) directly studies involutions of finite abelian groups: its cyclic counting theorem and direct-product decomposition theorem were inspected in full, and neither gives the mixed-exponent rank-two count; the report explicitly warns that direct-product involution counts cannot simply be multiplied. Published fixed-point enumeration research counts all automorphisms with a prescribed size of fixed subgroup, which neither specializes to nor determines the order-dividing-two count. Searches for both exact terminology and equivalent binary gap-stabilization statements identified no formula of the claimed form. An unindexed modular representation-theoretic classification could still imply the result, so the novelty assessment is not an absolute priority certificate.

## Value — PASS
This is a complete natural element-order invariant of automorphism groups for infinitely many mixed-exponent abelian groups. It exposes a sharp three-step binary exponent-gap stabilization and a contrasting odd-prime independence law, rather than isolated small-case numbers. The count distinguishes involutory symmetries from total automorphism-group order and is relevant to classifying actions of cyclic groups of order two on such modules.
