# Scientific review: matched-block language Hankel operator

## Correctness (C): PASS

The object is the real-scalar Boolean language Hankel operator defined by Hristov–Ilchev–Nedelcheva–Zlatanov. For every infinite positive-index set, its derivatives split exactly into two disjoint, nonempty coordinate-support families; the cases for pure-zero prefixes, partial one-block prefixes, and all other prefixes have been distinguished. This yields a surjective linear isometry onto a standard \(c_0\) space and an explicit bounded factorization through \(\ell_1\hookrightarrow c_0\). Weak compactness follows via coordinatewise diagonal extraction plus the Eberlein–Šmulian theorem; strict singularity follows via tail-supported weakly null sequences and the Schur property of \(\ell_1\). Separated columns prove noncompactness. The half-distance and approximation-number statements follow by an exact rank-one comparison and are also covered by the primary paper. The finite checker passed 68,825 exact assertions; no infinite-dimensional conclusion is inferred from the finite sample.

## Originality (O): PASS, subject to historical priority

The complete primary text of [1], especially Sections 6–8, was compared at statement and implication level. That paper establishes the general finite-rank/compactness equivalence for languages, the exact compact distance, and approximation numbers. It does not provide an isometric \(c_0\) normal form or test strict singularity/weak compactness for the balanced-block language family. The classical factor-inclusion argument is not itself new, but the explicit factorization of these Boolean Hankel maps, valid for every infinite index set, is a specific new structural assertion. Searches for equivalent formulations involving left quotients, balanced one-counter languages, stable languages, and weakly compact/strictly singular Hankel maps did not identify a direct covering source. An older equivalence under automata, weakly almost periodic language, or operator-ideal terminology is a remaining priority risk. Search failure is not proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS

The 2026 paper does not address the ideal behavior of nonregular-language Hankel maps beyond compactness and finite-rank approximation. The most canonical nonregular binary language \(\{0^n1^n\}\), and all of its arbitrary infinite-length restrictions, have a rigid explicit \(c_0\) geometry. The resulting weakly compact and strictly singular yet noncompact maps distinguish three important operator ideals despite the same sharp compact-distance obstruction. Arbitrary index-set dependence does not disturb the classification, making this a natural whole-family structural result rather than a chosen numerical slice.
