# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact simultaneous-survivor identity follows by removing the largest required number of copies independently for every part size; each residual partition is counted once. Hardy–Ramanujan ratios apply uniformly because all compulsory sums are \(O(\sqrt n)\) for fixed ranks and scaled locations. The marked coefficient identity enforces exactly \(j\) copies of the stopping part by multiplying by \(1-q^m\). A uniform local estimate at \(m=\Theta(n^{1/4})\), tightness from the exact single-rank survivor, and Riemann summation give the independent uniform defect mark. The identity \(M_r=M_s\iff J_s<r\) proves the point-mass collision law without attempting to infer equality probabilities from weak convergence. The packaged verifier independently enumerates all partitions through \(n=26\) for the exact formulas and checks larger values by exact partition arithmetic. No computation is claimed to establish the infinite theorem.

Bhowmick–Meher (arXiv:2609.31104), complete article, proves single-rank moment formulas and an odd/even asymptotic, not the marked simultaneous law. Published semantic searches did not locate an equivalent claim. A publisher preview, rather than full text, was available for Baruah–Talukdar (DOI:10.1007/s00026-025-00772-z). Literature that subsumes these formulas implicitly or is unindexed remains a priority risk. Originality is assessed at the level of the complete joint-plus-mark theorem, not the already-known one-dimensional marginals.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The previously known Rayleigh marginals admit many different couplings. The joint theorem specifies the actual singular dependence across multiplicity thresholds, including a record-minima representation. The uniform defect multiplicity explains exact positive tie probabilities \(r/s\), an intrinsic statistic of the least-gap hierarchy, and gives a complete asymptotic law for how many ranks first fail at one part. This supplies motivated structural information absent from single-variable moment identities, with finite-n exact counts that support future multirank questions.
