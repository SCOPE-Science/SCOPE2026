# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The face-level equivalence is proved in both directions: any layer-invariant extendable matching consists of disjoint horizontal edge-pairs plus nonconflicting rungs; every such choice extends by unused rungs. Midpoint coordinates establish a PL homeomorphism on **geometric fixed points**, not just on individually fixed vertices. The pivot matching on the fully whiskered matching complex has an explicit, lexicographic rule; its acyclicity follows from monotonicity of the base-edge set and of the chosen pendant edge within each fiber. The critical cells consist of a single vertex and one \((|V(H)|/2-1)\)-cell per perfect matching of \(H\); the low-dimensional exceptional case is handled separately. The reduced Lefschetz formula follows from equivariant triangulation and Euler characteristic. Independent finite enumeration validates 1,103 prism face models and 79 complete directed acyclicity tests. The proof covers all finite simple nonempty base graphs, with no connectedness or bipartiteness restriction.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The defining 2026 paper (Agarwal–Basak, arXiv:2608.30601v2, Sections 3 and 5.2) studies nonequivariant homotopy of ladders and does not identify involution-fixed geometric points for all prism base graphs. The open-ladder and polygonal tiling complex calculations in Chandrakar–Singh, arXiv:2407.05809v2, Sections 1–3, do not supply the present equivariant construction. The matching-complex wedge theorem in Bayer–Jelić Milutinović–Vega, arXiv:2411.04601v2, Sections 1–2, lacks the exact multiplicity and does not cover arbitrary fully whiskered graphs. Published research record queries on prism perfect-matching involutions, orbit fixed loci, and fully whiskered matching complexes returned nearby results about **different** complexes (ordinary prism independence, Möbius ladders, and ordinary graph matching), not a statement implying the present PL identification. The search is not a uniqueness proof; unindexed equivalent work is the main residual risk.

## Value — PASS

The result identifies a natural fixed-point invariant absent from ordinary homotopy-type classifications, proves a new structural bridge between two different graph-complex constructions, and recovers the exact one-factor count of an arbitrary finite base graph from integral fixed-locus homology. This applies simultaneously to sparse, disconnected, nonplanar and complete graphs; e.g. an even cycle yields two spheres and a complete bipartite graph \(K_{k,k}\) yields \(k!\) spheres. The explicit acyclic matching makes the integer result constructive rather than a field-specific numerical observation. The theorem claims neither a classification of the entire prism complex nor a full graded character.
