# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The new theorem is for every finite simple connected base graph of order at least one. Direct four-cycle computations show that the difference between a globally vertical matching hop and a square-cycle hop is one diagonal transposition, in each orientation. Those transpositions are exactly the edges of the base graph’s bipartite double cover; standard edge-transposition generation and a complete connectivity dichotomy of this cover give the lower bounds. In the bipartite case the prism’s two classes restrict every hop, and a vertical matching hop achieves the exchanging coset, giving the matching upper bound. The singleton, cycle and hypercube boundaries are explicit. Exhaustive independent finite checks cover 49 connected base graphs and 77,477 admissible hops. No finite check is substituted for the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Schachner and Udell (arXiv:2610.18463v1, 2026-10-13) explicitly list the cycle-prism and hypercube leap-group computation as open Question 5.4; they do not provide the all-connected-prisms result. Clark (2013) studies existence and cycle types of graph derangements; Bucić et al. (2019/2021) study matching counts and derangement probabilities; Bailey et al. (2020/2021) study derangements within specified permutation groups, a different generating set. Comparisons against related published mathematical statements and mathematical-alias searches found no equivalent or dominating theorem. Historical priority is not independently certified and unpublished computations may exist.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The classification answers two explicitly raised infinite-family questions at once and identifies the exact group permitted by the connectivity/bipartiteness dichotomy, for every connected input. The elementary square-switch lemma and double-cover proof supply a reusable structural mechanism; this is substantially more than a small numeric group computation.

**Limitations and nearest literature.** Only connected finite simple undirected base graphs and ordinary graph-admissible hops are classified. A general disconnected-base classification and directed/lazy group variants are not established. The originating article includes a finite graph database whose unavailable detail may contain isolated prior small cases, but not an inspected infinite theorem. Closest primary source: Schachner--Udell, *The group generated by a graph’s derangements*, arXiv:2610.18463v1, especially Section 5.
