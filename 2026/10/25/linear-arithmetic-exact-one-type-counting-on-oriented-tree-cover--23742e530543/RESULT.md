# Linear-arithmetic exact one-type counting on oriented tree-cover posets

## Finding
Let \(P\) be a nonempty finite poset whose undirected Hasse cover graph is a tree; the cover edges may have **arbitrary orientations** consistent with \(P\). Regard the elements of \(P\) as individually named parameters in the countable homogeneous universal poset. Let \(t(P)\) be the number of complete nonalgebraic one-types over these parameters.

For every \(v\in P\), define integers by the two directed recurrences
\[
D_v=1+\prod_{w\lessdot v}D_w,
\qquad
U_v=1+\prod_{v\lessdot w}U_w,
\]
where \(\lessdot\) is the cover relation and an empty product is one. Write \(J(P)\) for the number of order ideals of \(P\), including the empty ideal. Then
\[
\boxed{t(P)=2J(P)-1+\sum_{v\in P}(D_v-2)
 +\sum_{v\in P}(D_v-1)
 \left(U_v-2-\sum_{v\lessdot w}(U_w-1)\right).}
\]
All the quantities, including \(J(P)\), can be evaluated using \(O(|P|)\) exact integer-arithmetic operations from the directed cover tree. Their integers have \(O(|P|)\) bits, so the algorithm also has polynomial bit complexity. The claim is an exact count, not an asymptotic estimate.

The four-element N-poset with covers \(a<b\), \(c<b\), and \(c<d\), which is not series-parallel, gives \(t(P)=20\). Thus the formula handles orientations not obtainable from ancestor-order rooted forests or from the series-parallel construction.

## Assumptions and scope
The language has one strict partial-order relation. A cover \(u\lessdot v\) means \(u<v\) and no element lies strictly between them. The input is the transitive reduction of a nonempty finite poset with connected acyclic underlying graph; equivalently it is any orientation of a nonempty finite tree, with order defined by existence of a directed path. Parameters are individually fixed, not permuted. An extension point is distinct from every parameter. No claim is made for cover graphs containing cycles. The empty-poset convention \(t(\varnothing)=1\) may be handled separately.

The count concerns complete realized one-types outside the parameter set in the universal countable homogeneous poset. That structure admits every finite induced one-point poset extension; two such extension points have the same complete type precisely when their relations to all individually named parameters agree. Each such type has infinitely many realizations, so is nonalgebraic.

## Proof
**Step 1: Extension profiles.** For a new element \(x\), let \(L=\{p\in P:p<x\}\) and \(U=\{p\in P:x<p\}\). A profile is admissible exactly if \(L\) is an order ideal, \(U\) is an order filter, they are disjoint, and every element of \(L\) is strictly less than every element of \(U\). The last condition already implies disjointness. Necessity follows from transitivity and preservation of the induced order on \(P\); sufficiency follows by adjoining \(x\), taking the transitive closure, and noting that no new comparison between old elements is generated. Homogeneity and the one-point extension property identify these profiles with the claimed complete nonalgebraic types.

If \(L=\varnothing\), each of the \(J(P)\) filters is allowed. If \(U=\varnothing\), each of the \(J(P)\) ideals is allowed. The both-empty profile is counted twice, giving \(2J(P)-1\). It remains to count profiles with both parts nonempty.

**Step 2: Two local subtree counts.** The principal lower set \(P_{\le v}\) is the directed in-tree consisting of all vertices whose unique path to \(v\) points upward. Different lower-cover subtrees are disjoint and pairwise incomparable. An ideal in \(P_{\le v}\) either contains \(v\), in which case it contains everything, or excludes \(v\), in which case it is an independent choice of ideals in the lower-cover subtrees. Hence \(D_v=J(P_{\le v})=1+\prod_{w\lessdot v}D_w\). Dually \(U_v\) is the number of filters in \(P_{\ge v}\). In particular the strict lower set \(P_{<v}\) has \(D_v-1\) ideals.

**Step 3: A singleton minimal upper element.** Every nonempty filter \(U\) has a unique antichain \(A=\min U\), and it is the filter generated by \(A\). If \(A=\{v\}\), then admissible \(L\)'s are precisely the ideals contained in \(P_{<v}\). There are \(D_v-2\) nonempty ones. Summing over \(v\) yields the first correction \(\sum_v(D_v-2)\).

**Step 4: The unique common-ancestor branching point.** Suppose \(|A|\ge2\) and there exists at least one \(l\) strictly below every member of \(A\). Since the undirected cover graph is a tree, the union of the unique directed paths from \(l\) to vertices of \(A\) is an outward rooted subtree. Let \(v\) be the last common vertex of all these paths. It is strictly below every member of \(A\); the paths from \(v\) to \(A\) enter at least two distinct upper-cover branches of \(v\). Moreover, \(v\) does not depend on the chosen \(l\). Indeed, any other common lower bound \(l'\) must have a directed path to members of \(A\) in at least two of these branches; the unique-path property forces its path to those branches through \(v\), and thus \(l'\le v\). Conversely every vertex below \(v\), including \(v\) itself, is a lower bound of \(A\). Hence the common lower bounds of \(A\) form exactly the principal ideal \(P_{\le v}\).

Fixing this uniquely determined \(v\), admissible upper filters with minima distributed among at least two upper-cover branches are counted by
\[
B_v=\prod_{v\lessdot w}U_w-1-\sum_{v\lessdot w}(U_w-1)
=U_v-2-\sum_{v\lessdot w}(U_w-1).
\]
Here an independent filter is selected in each outward branch. From the product one subtracts the all-empty selection and selections supported in exactly one branch. The resulting global filter has the required minimal-element distribution. Conversely each filter with at least two minimal elements and a nonempty common strict lower set is counted for exactly one \(v\). Every nonempty admissible \(L\) for such a filter is an ideal contained in \(P_{\le v}\), of which there are \(D_v-1\). The second correction is therefore \(\sum_v(D_v-1)B_v\).

These cases are mutually exclusive and exhaust every admissible profile, proving the formula.

**Step 5: Evaluation.** Directed lower-neighbor recursion computes all \(D_v\) once, and directed upper-neighbor recursion all \(U_v\) once. To compute \(J(P)\), root the undirected cover tree arbitrarily and maintain for each rooted subtree the numbers of order ideals omitting and containing its root. A cover edge permits three of the four choices for its two membership bits: inclusion of an upper endpoint forces inclusion of its lower endpoint. Each child contributes a sum of its two states or just one state as dictated by the parent state. Multiplying child contributions yields a two-state tree dynamic program with \(O(|P|)\) arithmetic operations. The remaining sums require one traversal of the cover edges. This completes the complexity statement.

## Verification
The included stand-alone file `verify_tree_posets.py` implements the formula and, independently, enumerates the three possible relations of each parameter to a new element, rejecting violations of transitivity or induced-order preservation. It exhaustively compares all orientations of all labeled trees on 2 through 6 vertices (43,614 comparisons in total) and additionally compares 480 reproducible pseudorandom oriented trees on 7 through 10 vertices. The six hand-specified examples include a chain, N-poset, alternating fence, and mixed-branching tree. Its recorded output is `verification_output.txt`; run with `python verify_tree_posets.py` using Python's standard library. The independent enumeration is finite corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Relationship to prior work
The basic finite one-point extension criterion for homogeneous posets is classical and is not claimed here. Pretzel (1980) studies a poset of insertion positions and dimensions for restricted input posets; the accessible abstract does not settle this arbitrary directed-cover-tree count. Jochemko (arXiv:1310.0838) studies order-preserving maps to chains and their orbit polynomials, whose ideal counts supply useful context but do not by themselves enumerate compatible mixed lower/upper insertion profiles. Felsner and Manneville study linear extensions of N-free orders, a different statistic and a restricted class. General exact one-type counting has already been shown hard, and special recurrences are available for rooted forests and series-parallel posets. The present formula handles tree-cover orientations containing the N-poset and arbitrary widths without relying on those restrictions.

## Limitations
The tree condition is essential to the proof of a unique maximal common lower bound and the independent branch factorization. The result neither characterizes the complexity for cover graphs of higher treewidth nor proves a lower bound for any restricted family. The 1980 insertion-position source was accessible at abstract level but not at full-text level; specialized earlier equivalent formulas may exist. Exhaustive computations verify only finite inputs. Publication priority has not been independently established.
