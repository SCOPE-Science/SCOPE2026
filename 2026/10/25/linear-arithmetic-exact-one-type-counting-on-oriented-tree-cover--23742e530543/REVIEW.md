# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The single claim is an exact formula and linear-number-of-arithmetic-operations algorithm for complete nonalgebraic one-types over finite tree-cover parameter posets in the universal homogeneous poset. The argument classifies one-point profiles by an ideal and filter with every lower element strictly below every upper element. The critical tree lemma gives a unique maximal common lower bound for each upper-minimum antichain admitting a lower witness; this partitions all mixed profiles into a singleton-minimum case and a multiply branching case. The two local recurrences and independent order-ideal dynamic program give the stated formula and complexity. Exhaustive comparison on 43,614 directed labeled trees through six vertices and 480 larger samples passed. No finite enumeration is treated as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The full N-poset orientation is in the domain; prior exact restricted recurrences for ancestor-rooted forests and series-parallel posets do not cover it, and bounded-width results do not cover arbitrary-width oriented trees. Related published work addresses order-preserving maps (Jochemko), linear extensions of N-free orders (Felsner–Manneville), and the insertion-position poset's dimension (Pretzel). The inspected materials and research-index comparisons did not supply an equivalent arbitrary-tree-cover formula. Residual risk: Pretzel's full text was unavailable, and obscure preexisting enumeration identities cannot be excluded.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The theorem gives a concrete exact and efficient counting method for a natural structural class beyond two previously tractable subclasses and includes both arbitrary width and N-shaped subposets. The unique-branching decomposition is the mathematical mechanism; the result is not an arbitrary finite experiment or a renaming of an old formula. This is a specialized counting theorem, not a solution to the general one-type counting problem.

## Limits
No statement for cover graphs containing cycles, no claim of optimal bit complexity, no independent review, and no assertion of publication priority.
