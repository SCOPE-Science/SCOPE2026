# Linear minimal-generator growth for multiplicative powers of the numerical semigroup \(\langle3,4,5\rangle\)

## Finding
Let \(H=\langle3,4,5\rangle=\{0,3,4,5,6,\ldots\}\). The ideal product of additive submonoids of \(\mathbb N_0\) is the additive monoid generated by all ordinary integer products of their elements; its iterates are denoted \(H^{\odot k}\). For every integer \(k\ge1\), the **unique minimal additive generating set** of \(H^{\odot k}\) is
\[
\mathcal G_k=\{3^{k-i}4^i:0\le i\le k\}\;\cup\;\{5\,3^{k-i}4^{i-1}:1\le i\le k\}.
\]
Consequently the embedding dimension is exactly \(e(H^{\odot k})=2k+1\), for every \(k\ge1\). The displayed generators are all distinct; in increasing order they interleave as
\[
X_0<X_1<Y_1<X_2<Y_2<\cdots<X_k<Y_k,\quad
X_i=3^{k-i}4^i,\quad Y_i=5\,3^{k-i}4^{i-1}.
\]
In particular, \(H^{\odot2}=\langle9,12,15,16,20\rangle\) and \(H^{\odot3}=\langle27,36,45,48,60,64,80\rangle\).

## Assumptions and scope
The ambient semiring is the nonnegative integers with usual addition and multiplication. Here \(H\) is the ordinary numerical semigroup of multiplicity three. Its multiplication is **not** pointwise set multiplication: the product \(A\odot B\) is the additive submonoid generated by \(\{ab:a\in A,b\in B\}\), as in the ideal multiplication of Chen, Hyde, Laurens, Piermarini, and Simons. The exponent \(k\) is any positive integer; no bound on \(k\) or numerical cutoff is assumed. The embedding dimension counts minimal **additive** generators of the product ideal.

## Proof
**Generating all products.** Every element of \(H\) is a finite nonnegative-integer sum of \(3,4,5\). Distributivity of multiplication over addition implies that \(H^{\odot k}\) is generated additively by the degree-\(k\) products
\[
P_{a,b,c}=3^a4^b5^c,\qquad a,b,c\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\quad a+b+c=k.
\]
The integer identity \(5^2=3^2+4^2\) gives, whenever \(c\ge2\),
\[
P_{a,b,c}=P_{a+2,b,c-2}+P_{a,b+2,c-2}.
\]
Each right-hand monomial still has total exponent \(k\), and has two fewer factors of five. Inducting on \(c\) therefore reduces every product to a sum of products with \(c=0\) or \(c=1\). These latter products are exactly \(X_i\) for \(0\le i\le k\) and \(Y_i\) for \(1\le i\le k\). Hence \(\mathcal G_k\) generates \(H^{\odot k}\).

**Their ordering.** For \(1\le i\le k\), we have \(X_{i-1}<X_i\), since the ratio of successive \(X\)'s is \(4/3>1\). Moreover
\[
X_i<Y_i<X_{i+1}\quad(1\le i<k),
\]
because \(Y_i/X_i=5/4\) and \(X_{i+1}/X_i=4/3\), with \(1<5/4<4/3\). The end-of-chain inequalities are \(X_0<X_1<Y_1\) and \(X_k<Y_k\). All \(2k+1\) generators are therefore distinct and have the claimed order.

**Minimality of each \(X_i\).** The smallest generator \(X_0=3^k\) is indispensable. For \(i\ge1\), every generator strictly smaller than \(X_i\) is divisible by \(3^{k-i+1}\): these are \(X_j\) with \(j<i\) and \(Y_j\) with \(j<i\). However \(X_i\) has exact \(3\)-adic valuation \(k-i\), since \(3\nmid4\). Therefore \(X_i\) is not a nonnegative-integer linear combination of smaller generators.

**Minimality of each \(Y_i\).** Fix \(1\le i\le k\). Of all generators preceding \(Y_i\), the sole exception to divisibility by \(3^{k-i+1}\) is \(X_i\); it has exact valuation \(k-i\). Because \(Y_i=(5/4)X_i<2X_i\), a nonnegative-integer combination expressing \(Y_i\) can contain at most one copy of \(X_i\). If it contains none, divisibility by \(3^{k-i+1}\) is impossible. If it contains exactly one, the remainder would be
\[
Y_i-X_i=3^{k-i}4^{i-1}(5-4)=3^{k-i}4^{i-1},
\]
which again has exact valuation \(k-i\), whereas every remaining available smaller generator is divisible by \(3^{k-i+1}\). This is impossible. Thus \(Y_i\) is also indispensable.

In an additive submonoid of \(\mathbb N_0\), an element cannot be expressed as a sum of generators strictly larger than itself. Consequently the preceding arguments establish that no element of \(\mathcal G_k\) is redundant, and \(\mathcal G_k\) is the unique minimal generating set. It contains \((k+1)+k=2k+1\) elements. \(\square\)

Run `python3 verify.py` in this directory. The dependency-free verifier independently generates every degree-\(k\) monomial in \(3,4,5\) and finds its minimal additive generating subset by dynamic programming, without assuming the proposed normal form. It then compares that subset with \(\mathcal G_k\), tests generation of all raw monomials, and independently checks indispensability of every proposed generator for every \(1\le k\le8\). The observed minimal-generator counts are \(3,5,7,9,11,13,15,17\); the run ends `VERIFY_OK`. These are finite consistency tests and not a substitute for the proof for all \(k\).

## Relationship to prior work
Chen, Hyde, Laurens, Piermarini, and Simons (2026, arXiv:2607.10778, first posted July 12) study ideal multiplication of numerical semigroups. Their Lemma 3.13 proves that the \(k\)-th ideal power of a **two-generated** semigroup has exactly \(k+1\) minimal generators; they explicitly describe the higher-embedding-dimension situation as less accessible. Our result instead concerns the first ordinary **three-generated** numerical semigroup and gives a different \(2k+1\) growth law, using the additive reduction \(25=9+16\) and a valuation argument. The 2015/2016 literature on compound-sequence numerical semigroups considers different sequences of generators, and does not directly state this ideal-power normal form in its available abstract. This comparison is a scoped novelty assessment, not an assertion of absolute priority.

## Limitations
The formula is proved for this specific, naturally distinguished semigroup, and not for all three-generated numerical semigroups. It does not determine Frobenius number, genus, type, or minimal presentations of the powers. Earlier two-generator power formulas are not claimed as new. Literature searches cannot prove global uniqueness, and an unindexed equivalent formula remains possible. The finite verifier tests exponents at most eight, while the proof applies to every positive exponent.
