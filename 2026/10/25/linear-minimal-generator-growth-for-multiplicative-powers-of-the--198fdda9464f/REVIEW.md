# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The definition of ideal multiplication gives all degree-\(k\) products of the three additive generators. The integer identity \(5^2=3^2+4^2\) reduces all products with at least two factors of five without changing degree. The normal-form values have a strict interleaving order; valuations at the prime three exclude additive decompositions of every proposed generator. The proof is valid for all integers \(k\ge1\). Standalone finite enumeration independently matches the result for \(1\le k\le8\), without being used as an infinite certificate.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Chen et al. (2026, arXiv:2607.10778), Lemma 3.13, determine exactly \(k+1\) generators for powers of **two-generated** numerical semigroups; Sections 3–4 inspected in the accessible primary text do not give this three-generated formula. Kiers–O'Neill–Ponomarenko (2016, arXiv:1503.05993) develop single compound-sequence semigroups; their available abstract does not directly state this union-of-two-progressions ideal-power result. Searches for equivalent parameters and older geometric-sequence coverage found no matching theorem, but inaccessible/unindexed equivalences remain a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Ideal multiplication on numerical semigroups has recent direct research motivation, and the first ordinary three-generated semigroup is the smallest case beyond the source's exact two-generated power theorem. The result gives the complete minimal generator set for infinitely many powers, with a sharp linear \(2k+1\) growth law rather than the quadratic count of raw degree-\(k\) products. The special Pythagorean reduction and independent valuation obstruction explain the phenomenon. No Frobenius, genus, or type formula is asserted.
