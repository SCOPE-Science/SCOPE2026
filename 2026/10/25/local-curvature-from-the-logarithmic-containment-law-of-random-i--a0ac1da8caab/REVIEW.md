# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The geometric half-circle anchor identity is exact for every interior test point and any continuous positive directional sampling law. A signed-arclength chord expansion at the boundary supplies the singular \(\delta/s\) displacement responsible for the logarithm. The remainder proof explicitly controls both sides of the boundary point, relying on \(n-1\ge2\) to make the second boundary layer integrable. Numerical crosschecks of the known circle case, nonuniform densities, elliptic arclength and direct sampled-polygon containment agree. The proof is analytical; finite checks are supporting evidence.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources

Brunel's boundary-sampled random-polytope Hausdorff estimates concern increasing sample sizes, not the fixed-sample local point-containment statistic. Prochno et al. study a global approximation measure. Ismailov's circle/three-point dilogarithm result already covers the unit-circle \(n=3\) specialization and is explicitly excluded from the novelty claim. No inspected source or searched research record established the general smooth-curve, local-density-squared, fixed-\(n\) inverse-curvature coefficient. Searches are not proofs of priority, and older stochastic-geometry literature may contain an equivalent statement.

## Value — PASS

The coefficient is a natural local stochastic-geometric invariant that recovers pointwise curvature from fixed-sample inscribed-polygon containment probabilities. Its logarithmic boundary layer and quadratic dependence on local sampling density explain a nonuniform rare-event mechanism; the result is not a repackaging of a global expected Hausdorff error. A useful limitation is that lower-order terms can still depend on nonlocal geometry.

## Closest literature and limitations

Relevant comparisons are Brunel (2015, arXiv:1503.01504), Prochno et al. (2024, arXiv:2404.02870), and Ismailov (2026, arXiv:2601.02929). The exact circular \(n=3\) expression is known. The new claim is limited to strictly positively curved \(C^3\) curves, positive \(C^1\) densities and fixed \(n\ge3\). No independent validation or definitive priority determination was performed.
