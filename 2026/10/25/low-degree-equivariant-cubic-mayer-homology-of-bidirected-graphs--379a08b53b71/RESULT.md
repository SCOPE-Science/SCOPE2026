# Low-degree equivariant cubic Mayer homology of bidirected graphs of girth at least five

## Finding
Let \(G=(V,E)\) be any finite connected simple graph with \(n=|V|\ge 2\), \(m=|E|\), and no triangles or four-cycles, and let \(D(G)\) contain both arcs of every edge. Fix a primitive cube root of unity \(\xi\in\mathbb C\), and use the **nonregular** cubic Mayer path differential of Karaguler--Wei. Put \(c(G)=2\) if \(G\) is bipartite and \(c(G)=1\) otherwise. For the Mayer groups defined using kernels of the first or second power of the differential, the *complete degree-zero-through-three Betti vectors* are
\[
\bigl(\beta_0^{3,1},\beta_1^{3,1},\beta_2^{3,1},\beta_3^{3,1}\bigr)
=(c(G),\,2m-n,\,0,\,0),
\]
\[
\bigl(\beta_0^{3,2},\beta_1^{3,2},\beta_2^{3,2},\beta_3^{3,2}\bigr)
=(0,\,n,\,2m-2n+c(G),\,0).
\]
These statements include all trees, even and odd chordless cycles of length at least five, and connected graphs of higher girth.

More precisely, the identifications below are canonical and equivariant for every graph automorphism. Let \(\mathcal E(G)\) be the set of equivalence classes of vertices under the equivalence relation generated by pairs having a common neighbor; these are the even-walk classes. Set
\[
B(G)=\bigoplus_{v\in V}\left\{(a_w)_{w\sim v}\in\mathbb C^{N(v)}:\sum_{w\sim v}a_w=0\right\}.
\]
There are exact sequences and identifications
\[
0\longrightarrow H_2^{3,2}(D(G))\longrightarrow B(G)
\xrightarrow{\ T\ }\mathbb C^V\longrightarrow\mathbb C^{\mathcal E(G)}\longrightarrow0,
\quad T((a_{vw}))=-\sum_{v,w\sim v}a_{vw}e_w,
\]
\[
H_1^{3,2}(D(G))\cong\mathbb C^V,\qquad
H_0^{3,1}(D(G))\cong\mathbb C^{\mathcal E(G)},\qquad
H_1^{3,1}(D(G))\cong\ker\bigl(\partial_1:\mathbb C^{\vec E}\to\mathbb C^V\bigr).
\]
Here \(\vec E\) denotes the set of oriented edges. If \(g\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), let \(f_V(g)\), \(f_A(g)\), and \(f_{\mathcal E}(g)\) count its fixed vertices, fixed oriented arcs, and fixed even-walk classes. The characters of the four potentially nonzero groups are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\chi_{H_0^{3,1}}(g)&=f_{\mathcal E}(g), &
\chi_{H_1^{3,1}}(g)&=f_A(g)-f_V(g),\\
\chi_{H_1^{3,2}}(g)&=f_V(g), &
\chi_{H_2^{3,2}}(g)&=f_A(g)-2f_V(g)+f_{\mathcal E}(g).
\end{aligned}
\]
The remaining groups in degrees zero through three are zero.

## Assumptions and scope
All vector spaces and representations use coefficients in \(\mathbb C\). The graph is connected, simple, finite, has at least one edge, and has girth at least five (infinite girth for trees). The directed graph is **bidirected**, not an arbitrary orientation. A basis \((v_0,\ldots,v_k)\) of the allowed path space \(A_k\) is a directed walk, so revisiting a vertex after two steps is permitted. The ambient path space also includes nonallowed words such as consecutive repetitions; one must not quotient these out when taking the differential.

The cubic differential on all elementary words is
\[
\partial(v_0,\ldots,v_k)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\xi^j(v_0,\ldots,\widehat v_j,\ldots,v_k),
\qquad \xi^3=1,\quad\xi\ne1,
\]
with \(\partial:A_0\to0\). For \(k\ge0\), let
\[
\Omega_k=\{x\in A_k:\partial x\in A_{k-1},\ \partial^2x\in A_{k-2}\},
\qquad A_j=0\ \text{if }j<0.
\]
Define \(H_k^{3,q}=\ker(\partial^q:\Omega_k\to\Omega_{k-q})/\operatorname{im}(\partial^{3-q}:\Omega_{k+3-q}\to\Omega_k)\), for \(q\in\{1,2\}\). In particular, \(\ker(\partial^2:\Omega_2\to\Omega_0)\) is generally **smaller** than \(\Omega_2\). Only degrees \(0\) through \(3\) are classified; no assertion is made for degrees at least four.

## Proof
**1. The first two chain spaces.** All vertices and oriented edges are allowed and have allowed boundaries, so \(\Omega_0=\mathbb C^V\) and \(\Omega_1=\mathbb C^{\vec E}\). The first differential satisfies \(\partial(v,w)=e_w+\xi e_v\). For any undirected edge, the two opposite arcs give two linearly independent columns on its endpoints, since \(1-\xi^2\ne0\). Every vertex lies on an edge, hence \(\partial_1:\Omega_1\to\Omega_0\) is surjective. Consequently, \(\dim\ker\partial_1=2m-n\).

**2. The degree-two invariant paths.** A length-two walk \((a,b,c)\) has a nonallowed middle-deletion face \((a,c)\) whenever \(a\ne c\): otherwise it would form a triangle. Two distinct intermediates between the same endpoints would form a four-cycle. Such coefficients therefore cannot cancel. Every element of \(\Omega_2\) is consequently a sum of backtracks \((v,w,v)\). The only nonallowed first-boundary face of a backtrack is \(\xi(v,v)\), so its coefficients around each center \(v\) must sum to zero. Thus
\[
\Omega_2\cong B(G),\qquad \dim\Omega_2=\sum_{v\in V}(\deg(v)-1)=2m-n.
\]
The second boundary of any allowed two-path is a linear combination of vertices and imposes no additional condition on \(\Omega_2\).

**3. Vanishing of the degree-three invariant path space.** Consider an arbitrary linear combination of allowed three-walks \((a,b,c,d)\) with first boundary in \(A_2\). If \(a\ne c\), its middle deletion \((a,c,d)\) has the nonedge \(a\not\sim c\). That invalid face can occur from no other deletion position, and its missing intermediary \(b\) is unique by four-cycle exclusion; hence the coefficient of \((a,b,c,d)\) is zero. Among the survivors \((a,b,a,d)\), if \(b\ne d\), deletion of \(a\) in position two creates the invalid face \((a,b,d)\). Its missing intermediary is again unique, and its coefficient must vanish. Only alternating walks \((a,b,a,b)\) remain. Their invalid face \((a,a,b)\), obtained by deleting position one, occurs uniquely among these survivors. Thus every coefficient vanishes. It follows that \(\Omega_3=0\); imposing the second-boundary admissibility condition cannot enlarge this space.

**4. The two distinct maps out of degree two.** Write \(b_{vw}=(v,w,v)\). On the unrestricted word space,
\[
\partial b_{vw}=(w,v)+\xi(v,v)+\xi^2(v,w),\qquad
\partial^2b_{vw}=e_v-e_w.
\]
For a coefficient family in \(B(G)\), the inadmissible loops cancel, and its first boundary is \(\sum_{v,w}a_{vw}L_{vw}\), where \(L_{vw}=(w,v)+\xi^2(v,w)\). On each undirected edge, \(L_{vw}\) and \(L_{wv}\) form a basis of the two oriented-arc vectors because their determinant is \(1-\xi^4=1-\xi\ne0\). Thus \(\partial:\Omega_2\to\Omega_1\) is injective.

The second boundary is
\[
\partial^2\!\left(\sum_{v,w}a_{vw}b_{vw}\right)
=\sum_{v,w}a_{vw}(e_v-e_w)=-\sum_{v,w}a_{vw}e_w=T((a_{vw})).
\]
Its image is precisely the span of all differences \(e_w-e_u\) for vertices \(u,w\) that have a common neighbor. For connected \(G\), those differences identify the two bipartition sides separately if \(G\) is bipartite, and all vertices otherwise. Therefore \(\operatorname{coker}T\cong\mathbb C^{\mathcal E(G)}\), \(\operatorname{rank}T=n-c(G)\), and \(\dim\ker T=2m-2n+c(G)\).

**5. Homology and symmetry.** The definition of \(q\)-cycles and \(q\)-boundaries, together with \(\Omega_3=0\), \(\partial_1\) surjective, and \(\partial_2\) injective, gives exactly the displayed Betti vectors and exact sequence. For \(q=2\), the first-degree group is \(\Omega_1/\partial\Omega_2\); the port vectors \(L_{vw}\) identify \(\Omega_1\) with the permutation module on neighbor incidences \((v,w)\), and quotienting by the local zero-sum subspaces leaves one basis vector per center \(v\). All constructions commute with graph automorphisms. Finally \(\Omega_2\) is the kernel of the incidence-sum map \(\mathbb C^{\vec E}\to\mathbb C^V\), so its character is \(f_A-f_V\). Additivity of characters in the displayed equivariant short exact sequences yields the four formulas.

## Verification
The bundled executable `verify_mayer.py` independently builds all allowed walks through degree three, computes the nonregular differential on unrestricted words, imposes both admissibility conditions, and performs exact sparse elimination modulo seven and thirteen. Both fields contain the stated primitive cube roots. It checked all 26 graph/field combinations: paths on two through six vertices, stars on four through six, cycles of length five through eight, and the Petersen graph. In every case it reconstructed \(\dim\Omega_2\), \(\dim\Omega_3=0\), and ranks of \(\partial_1\), \(\partial|_{\Omega_2}\), and \(\partial^2|_{\Omega_2}\), confirming the asserted Betti vectors. These finite-field checks independently corroborate, but do not replace, the proof over \(\mathbb C\) for all finite graphs in the stated class.

## Relationship to prior work
Karaguler and Wei introduced Mayer path homology and classified the local generator types of \(\Omega_2\) and \(\Omega_3\), together with a qualitative spanning-tree description of first cycles (arXiv:2605.16525, sections 3.1--3.2). Their double-edge, triangle, and square generator theorem is consistent with Step 2. The new result specializes the underlying chain structure to *all* connected bidirected graphs without triangles or squares, then determines **both** Mayer homologies through degree three, their equivariant exact sequences, and every graph-automorphism character. The original general generator list and first-cycle description do not themselves state these homology and symmetry formulas. General ordinary path homology and simplicial multipath-complex calculations concern different differentials or complexes and cannot replace this comparison.

## Limitations
The theorem assumes no isolated vertices, connectivity, absence of triangles and four-cycles, bidirectionality, and complex coefficients with a primitive cube root. It does not give homology in degrees four or above; the degree-three vanishing does not imply that higher chain spaces vanish. The representation formulas concern linear homology actions, not equivariant homotopy types. The primary 2026 article supplies the general framework and generator classification, so exact novelty relative to unindexed deductions of that framework remains a residual risk. Independent validation has not been performed.
