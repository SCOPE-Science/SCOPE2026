# Low-energy single-spin geometry of antiferromagnetic Möbius ladders

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge3\), let \(W_n\) be the graph with vertex set \(\mathbb Z/(2n)\mathbb Z\), cycle edges \(\{i,i+1\}\), and antipodal edges \(\{i,i+n\}\) for \(0\le i<n\). A *spin configuration* is a map \(s:V(W_n)\to\{+1,-1\}\). Its defect count \(u(s)\) is the number of edges whose endpoints have equal spins. Let \(\mathcal L_n(q)\) be the graph of all labeled spin configurations with \(u(s)\le q\), adjoining configurations differing at exactly one vertex. Spin-reversed configurations are distinct.

**Odd width.** For every odd \(n\ge3\), the minimum defect count is zero and there are exactly two ground configurations. There are no states with one or two defects, exactly \(4n\) states with three defects, and exactly \(n(n+3)\) with four. Define \(B_n\) from \(W_n\) by adding a root, one node for each original vertex, and one node for each original edge; join the root to every vertex-node, and join an edge-node to its two endpoint vertex-nodes. Then
\[
\mathcal L_n(4)\cong B_n\sqcup B_n\sqcup n(n-3)K_1.
\]
Each copy of \(B_n\) has \(5n+1\) vertices. Consequently, the two ground configurations lie in different components at all defect thresholds at most four; their *exact communication threshold* under single-spin flips is five: they can be connected through configurations with at most five defects and cannot be connected with a smaller maximum defect count.

**Even width.** For every even \(n\ge4\), the minimum defect count is two and there are exactly \(2n\) ground configurations. Exactly \(4n\) configurations have three defects. The sublevel graph \(\mathcal L_n(3)\) is obtained from \(C_{2n}\) by replacing **each** original edge with two internally vertex-disjoint paths of length two (and deleting the original edge). In particular, it is connected, has \(6n\) vertices and \(8n\) edges, every ground vertex has degree four, every three-defect vertex has degree two, and each of the \(2n\) original edges becomes a four-cycle. The ground configurations themselves have no single-spin edges, but communicate already at threshold three.

The numerical even-width ground-state degeneracy and defect minimum are established in earlier Ising literature. The claims here concern the exact low-energy sublevel graphs and the associated one-spin communication geometry; the odd-width cutwidth-five construction is also known as a graph-layout bound.

## Assumptions and scope
All edges have equal antiferromagnetic coupling, all vertices have binary labeled spins, and transitions flip exactly one spin. A defect is an *uncut* edge. If one instead writes the unweighted antiferromagnetic Ising energy as \(H(s)=\sum_{\{v,w\}\in E}s_vs_w\), then \(H(s)=2u(s)-3n\). Therefore an increase of one defect means an energy increase of two in this normalization. No quotient by overall spin reversal or graph automorphisms is taken. The smallest graph is \(W_3\cong K_{3,3}\). The even statement excludes \(n=2\), where the graph is exceptional.

## Proof
**1. Folded-column accounting.** Pair vertex \(i\) with \(i+n\) into column \(i\) for \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). Consecutive columns are linked by two cycle edges, straight except at the closing seam, where their endpoints are exchanged. A column is *split* if its two spins differ and *uniform* otherwise. Under the antiferromagnetic defect count a split column satisfies its chord, while a uniform column creates one chord defect. Every link between a split and a uniform column creates precisely one cycle-edge defect. Each link between two split columns creates either zero or two defects, with zero exactly when their signs alternate in the straight part and satisfy the exchanged seam. Each link between two uniform columns also creates zero or two defects. If every column is split, the seam induces an odd parity obstruction when \(n\) is even: the number of defective links is odd. For even \(n\), one defective link, contributing two bad cycle edges, is the smallest possibility.

**2. Even-width ground and one-excitation states.** Assume \(n\ge4\) is even. If no chord is defective, the preceding parity condition gives at least two defects. Equality means exactly one defective link. Its position has \(n\) choices, and a first spin has two choices; hence precisely \(2n\) ground states. A single uniform column causes at least two cycle-edge defects (one at each interface to the split-column chain), so cannot be a ground state. Two or more uniform columns likewise cannot achieve two defects: unless every column is uniform, there are at least two split--uniform interfaces, and with two chord defects this already costs four; if every column is uniform, the cycle-edge contribution cannot reduce the \(n\ge4\) chord defects. Thus the minimum is two.

For exactly three defects, zero uniform columns are impossible because cycle-edge defects come in pairs. Two or more uniform columns are impossible by the interface bound (and the case of all-uniform columns has at least four chord defects). Hence there is exactly one uniform column. The other \(n-1\) columns form a path of split columns and all links inside that path must be satisfied. Their signs must alternate, leaving precisely two assignments. The uniform column may have either sign. This constructs exactly \(4n\) three-defect configurations, indexed by their unique uniform column and two independent binary choices.

Flipping either endpoint of the unique uniform chord yields a ground configuration: after rotating the uniform column to position zero, the alternating split-column path has equal endpoint orientations because \(n-2\) is even; the closing seam makes the two uniform-column interfaces opposite on the two layers, so either endpoint flip leaves exactly one defective link. These are the only two possible ground neighbors, because removing the sole defective chord requires flipping one of its endpoints. Conversely, a ground configuration has precisely two antipodal defective cycle edges, with four distinct incident vertices. Flipping any such incident vertex increases the defect count from two to three; flipping any other vertex increases it to five. Hence every ground state has four neighbors in \(\mathcal L_n(3)\), each excited state has two, and no edges join vertices at equal defect count (flipping one vertex in a cubic graph changes \(u\) by an odd number).

For the underlying graph on ground states, regard two ground states as linked when they share a three-defect neighbor. They differ by flipping the two endpoints of one antipodal chord, necessarily incident with the unique antipodal pair of defective cycle edges. Flipping the pair at either end moves the defective link one position clockwise or counterclockwise. These are exactly two neighbors per ground state. Moving the defect through all \(n\) column positions flips each antipodal pair once, producing overall spin reversal rather than the original state. A second circuit restores the original state, so this ground-state graph is one \(2n\)-cycle. Each ground-to-ground edge has two distinct three-defect intermediates, given by the two possible orders of its two endpoint flips. The claimed doubled two-subdivision follows.

**3. Odd-width defects as cuts.** Assume \(n\ge3\) is odd. The alternating spin assignment along the outer cycle satisfies every chord as well, so \(W_n\) is connected and bipartite and has exactly two defect-zero configurations. Fix one and write any spin configuration as the result of flipping the vertex subset \(S\). An originally satisfied edge becomes defective exactly when one endpoint lies in \(S\). Therefore \(u(s)=|\delta(S)|\), the edge boundary of \(S\).

For a boundary calculation, classify each paired column of the binary indicator of \(S\) as split or uniform. With \(r\) split columns the chord contribution is \(r\). Every split--uniform interface adds one boundary edge; every uniform--uniform or split--split link adds zero or two, with a reversed correspondence at the seam. If \(r=0\), the uniform-column binary string is cyclic: it contributes zero for the two constant strings, four for exactly two transitions, and at least eight otherwise. The exactly-two-transition strings are the \(n(n-1)\) oriented proper contiguous blocks of full columns.

If \(r=1\), the two necessary split--uniform interfaces contribute two, hence the minimum boundary is three. Boundary at most four requires every remaining uniform column to have the same bit, yielding exactly \(4n\) sets: the singletons and their complements. If \(r=2\), boundary at most four forces the two split columns to be adjacent, their connecting link to contribute zero, and the uniform columns to have one common bit; these are exactly the \(4n\) sets given by endpoints of one cycle edge, and their complements. If \(r\ge3\) and there is a uniform column, the two split--uniform interfaces force boundary at least \(r+2\ge5\). If all columns are split, their chord contribution is at least five for odd \(n\ge5\); for \(n=3\), the twisted seam prevents all three intercolumn links from contributing zero and forces at least two more boundary edges. Thus no other nontrivial sets have boundary at most four.

Among the \(n(n-1)\) full-column contiguous blocks, exactly \(2n\) have length one or \(n-1\); together with the \(4n\) cycle-edge pairs and their complements, these give all \(6n\) vertex-pairs spanning an edge of \(W_n\) and their complements. The other \(n(n-3)\) full-column blocks have lengths \(2,\ldots,n-2\). No such block can flip one vertex while retaining boundary at most four: the only odd boundary size at most four for nontrivial sets is three, and all three-boundary sets are a singleton or co-singleton. They are isolated.

The remaining states form two disjoint rooted incidence graphs \(B_n\): for each of \(S=\varnothing\) and \(S=V\), there is a ground state, its \(2n\) one-flip states, and its \(3n\) adjacent-pair states. Every adjacent-pair state is connected to precisely its two singleton predecessors, and each singleton is connected to its ground state. Thus each component has \(1+2n+3n=5n+1\) states. In particular, the ground states cannot communicate under a defect ceiling of four.

**4. Sharp odd-width communication threshold.** Flip vertices in the order \(0,n,1,n+1,\ldots,n-1,2n-1\). After every even number of flips, the flipped set is a contiguous sequence of complete antipodal columns, with edge boundary at most four. At odd intermediate steps it consists of such a sequence and one endpoint of the next chord, with boundary at most five (the first and last odd steps have boundary three). This gives a single-spin path between the complementary ground states with at most five defects. The disconnection established in step 3 proves optimality.

## Verification
The accompanying dependency-free `verify.py` exhaustively enumerates all \(2^{2n}\) spin states for every \(3\le n\le10\). It checks the minimum levels, exact counts, odd-width component sizes at defect ceiling four, connectedness at ceiling five, and the explicit five-defect path. For even width it constructs every edge of the sublevel graph and confirms two neighbors per excited state, four per ground state, two parallel two-step routes per ground-state cycle edge, and one connected cycle of ground-state adjacencies. Run `python3 verify.py` from the extracted artifact directory; the expected final line is `ALL_VERIFY_OK`. Finite testing is corroboration only; the universal statements follow from the proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Kaul and West (2008) study single-vertex cut flips for general graphs and give the classical local-search context. Greeni and Rajasingh's circulant-graph cutwidth paper gives the width-five interleaved arrangement for Möbius ladders (apart from the smallest boundary case); therefore **the five-defect construction is not claimed to be novel in isolation**. Kalinin and Berloff (2022) expressly identify \(2n\) even-width antiferromagnetic ground states with two frustrated edges. Neither cited full-text analysis determines the complete three- and four-defect sublevel graphs, the isolated-band count in odd width, or the doubled-square necklace in even width. The new object is the low-energy one-spin transition graph, not a new optimum count or a new cutwidth formula. An independently identified equivalent low-temperature transition classification would subsume this result.

## Limitations
The claim is for unweighted, zero-field, binary-spin Möbius ladders only; varying coupling strengths or soft-spin continuous dynamics changes the geometry. The odd threshold concerns communication **between the two ground configurations**; no general connectivity claim is made about every state with at most five defects. The even necklace includes both spin reversals as separate states. This is a complete mathematical classification within the stated energy windows, but no independent mathematical audit or publication-priority certification has been performed.
