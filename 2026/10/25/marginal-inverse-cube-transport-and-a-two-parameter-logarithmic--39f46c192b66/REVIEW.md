# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The mathematical object is the fully defined positive continuum spectral integral, not an asserted exact stochastic-current correlation. The proof derives critical series expansions, group-speed coefficients, the three normalized asymptotic amplitudes, a fixed-exponent collision crossover, and a locally uniform two-dimensional near-marginal crossover. The critical coefficients follow from explicit Gaussian Mellin integration at the wave-number scale \(k_t=[2\zeta(3)/(\gamma t\log t)]^{1/2}\). Uniformity uses a split at reciprocal wave number and positive summation bounds, with integrability guaranteed by \(\delta>2\). Independent finite-precision quadratures corroborate the asymptotic behavior and preserve the distinction between finite experiments and infinite-time proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result closes an explicitly excluded marginal boundary in a natural spectral model of long-range thermal transport, gives full constants and corrected logarithmic factors, and exhibits a meaningful joint crossover when *both* range exponents are within a moving critical window. It explains why naive extrapolation of the four pure-power regimes fails close to the inverse-cube boundary. The quantitative two-parameter scaling is usable in finite-time interpretation of long-range kinetic simulations, while its status as a kinetic rather than complete stochastic-current result is transparent.
