# Review: Maximal lattice sections of integer cubes

## Correctness — PASS
For each affine \(\ell\)-flat an injective projection onto \(\ell\) coordinate positions bounds its grid intersection by \((k+1)^\ell\). Equality forces the entire projected grid. Every dependent coordinate is then an integer-affine map with variation at most \(k\), forcing it to be a constant, a projected variable, or its complement. This proves the complete equality structure and prevents hidden exceptional flats. The intrinsic active blocks, signs modulo block complement, and fixed coordinates are counted without duplication. The Stirling sum, exponential generating function, and finite-difference expression follow by standard exact combinatorial identities. There is no dimension-dependent or sufficiently-large-dilation gap. Rational enumeration checks ten small grid parameter cases; the infinite assertion relies on the complete proof.

## Originality — PASS under inspected evidence
Nguyên's 2019 paper proves the maximum \((k+1)^\ell\), not the cardinality of the maximizing-flat family. Melo and Winter analyze linear/affine binary-cube intersection patterns and a gap below maximum. Their extremal reasoning may yield parts of the signed-block structure at \(k=1\), but the all-positive-integer-grid, all-rank count, generating function, and exact-in-dilation degree statement were not found in the inspected material. Averkov et al. (2025/2026), Conjecture 4.8, predict eventual quasipolynomial maximal-section counts for general polytopes; this theorem settles the cubical special case in an explicit stronger exact form, not the general conjecture. Targeted mathematical-database searches and comparison with accessible prior results yielded no equivalent exact count. These searches cannot establish absolute historical priority; an unindexed combinatorial-subspace enumeration remains a risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This result calculates a naturally motivated complete counting invariant in every rank, dimension, and positive dilation for a central test family of a current general maximal-section counting conjecture. It strengthens eventual quasipolynomiality to an all-dilation polynomial with a precise leading coefficient, and its equality classification exposes the signed-partition geometry underlying the count. The conclusion is not a trivial restatement of the known maximum bound. The theorem is restricted to cubes and does not settle general polytopes.
