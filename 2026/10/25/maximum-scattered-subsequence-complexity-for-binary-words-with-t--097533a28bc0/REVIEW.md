# Scientific review of a fixed-weight subsequence extremum

**Correctness — PASS.** The previous-occurrence recurrence determines the exact quartic gap formula; two strict gap-smoothing inequalities restrict every optimum to pair-balanced gaps. Four explicit two-unit-transfer polynomials prove the parity-class optimizers, and positive parity-comparison differences determine the final complete equality cases for all lengths. The accompanying checker compared direct subsequence-set generation, an independent dynamic recurrence, the gap formula and the equality classification. The exhaustive computations corroborate, rather than replace, the symbolic argument.

**Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, not historical certification).** Flaxman–Harrow–Sorkin (2004, DOI 10.37236/1761) proves the length-only optimum and its alternating unique maximizing form, not the fixed-Hamming-weight-three optimum or its equality cases. Liron–Langberg (2012, arXiv:1202.1644, especially Theorem III.1 and Lemma II.7) gives extremal deletion-descendant bounds for prescribed run count, with an equal-run-length optimizer not generally satisfying three ones. Nearby searchable results on three-deletion ambiguity, composition spectra and sparse subsequence universality do not state or imply the quartic fixed-content optimum. Actual semantic searches and comparisons are recorded in the structured audit. Absence of a directly covering item in those sources is evidence, not a proof of historical priority.

**Value — PASS.** Prescribed Hamming weight is a natural input restriction in deletion-channel combinatorics. It changes the maximal all-length scattered-subsequence count from the unrestricted Fibonacci/exponential regime to a quartic, and the exact optimizer classification fixes the zero-gap arrangement at every length, not just asymptotically. This is a complete natural composition-constrained extremal classification, with explicit comparison to the standard unrestricted theorem.

**Limitations.** The number of distinct subsequences of all lengths is optimized, not each fixed-length deletion sphere simultaneously; the theorem only treats weight three.
