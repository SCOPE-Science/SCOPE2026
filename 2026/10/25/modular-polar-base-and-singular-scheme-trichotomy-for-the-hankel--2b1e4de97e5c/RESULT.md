# Modular polar-base and singular-scheme trichotomy for the Hankel permanental cubic

## Finding
Let \(k\) be algebraically closed of arbitrary characteristic, and let \(X\subset\mathbb P^4_k\) be the cubic hypersurface obtained by taking the permanent of the generic \(3\times3\) Hankel matrix,
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b&c\\b&c&d\\c&d&e\end{pmatrix},\qquad F=\operatorname{per}(M)=ace+ad^2+b^2e+2bcd+c^3.
\]
Write \(\Sigma=V(F,F_a,F_b,F_c,F_d,F_e)\) for the intrinsic projective Jacobian singular scheme and \(B=V(F_a,F_b,F_c,F_d,F_e)\) for the polar base scheme. They need not agree when the characteristic divides the degree. The following gives their complete scheme-theoretic behavior, with all schemes over \(k\):

1. If \(\operatorname{char}k\notin\{2,3\}\), then \(B=\Sigma\) comprises two disjoint curvilinear length-five schemes at \(P_a=[1:0:0:0:0]\) and \(P_e=[0:0:0:0:1]\). Each completed hypersurface local ring is \(k[[t,U,V,W]]/(W^2+UV-2t^6)\). In characteristic zero these are ordinary \(A_5\)-type threefold hypersurface singularities; the explicit local equation, rather than the terminology, is asserted uniformly.
2. If \(\operatorname{char}k=3\), then the polar base \(B\) is **reduced** and is the disjoint union \(C_-\sqcup\{Q\}\), where \(Q=[0:0:1:0:0]\) and \(C_-\) is the smooth rational normal quartic
\[
[s:t]\longmapsto[s^4:s^3t:-s^2t^2:st^3:t^4].
\]
The restriction \(F|_{C_-}=-2s^6t^6\) is nonzero. Thus \(Q\notin X\), most of \(C_-\) is outside \(X\), and the intrinsic singular scheme is the divisor \(6P_a+6P_e\) on \(C_-\), of total degree \(12\); its two components are curvilinear length-six schemes.
3. If \(\operatorname{char}k=2\), then \(B=\Sigma\) is exactly the complete intersection of three quadrics
\[
ce+d^2=ae+c^2=ac+b^2=0.
\]
It is a square-zero ribbon supported on \(C_+:[s:t]\mapsto[s^4:s^3t:s^2t^2:st^3:t^4]\). The ribbon has degree \(8\), arithmetic genus \(5\) and Hilbert polynomial \(8m-4\).

The cubic \(X\) is geometrically integral and normal in all characteristics. The characteristic-zero \(A_5\) cubic family and the characteristic-two chordal cubic/ribbon occur in prior work. The modular polar behavior and full comparison of the three Jacobian schemes are the specific contribution addressed here.

## Assumptions and scope
The field is algebraically closed; \(p=0\) denotes characteristic zero. The singular scheme is defined by the hypersurface equation **and** all five first partials, whereas the polar base scheme is defined by the partials alone. Lengths are lengths of local Artin rings, and the projective singular-scheme degree is the sum of lengths only when this scheme is zero-dimensional. In characteristic two the reduced support has positive dimension. Assertions about equality of ideals are scheme-theoretic.

## Proof
**A universal affine normal form.** Work on \(a\ne0\), rescale so \(a=1\), and put \(t=b\). Define polynomial coordinates
\[
U=c+b^2,\quad W=d+bc,\quad V=e+U^2-4b^2U+5b^4.
\]
This is an invertible triangular polynomial coordinate change over \(\mathbb Z\), with inverse \(c=U-t^2\), \(d=W-t(U-t^2)\), and \(e=V-U^2+4t^2U-5t^4\). Direct expansion gives the **integral** identity
\[
F\big|_{a=1}=W^2+UV-2t^6.
\]
The reversal involution \((a,b,c,d,e)\mapsto(e,d,c,b,a)\) preserves \(F\), so the same normal form holds at \(P_e\).

**No singular point lies outside these two affine charts.** At \(a=e=0\), the partials \(F_a=ce+d^2=d^2\) and \(F_e=ac+b^2=b^2\) force \(b=d=0\) at a geometric singular point, while \(F=c^3\) forces \(c=0\). Therefore all singular points lie in \(\{a\ne0\}\cup\{e\ne0\}\). In all characteristics, \(F\) is irreducible: as a polynomial of degree one in \(a\), it has coefficient \(ce+d^2\) and constant term \(b^2e+2bcd+c^3\), which are coprime over \(k[b,c,d,e]\); \(ce+d^2\) is irreducible and does not divide the constant term. Thus the hypersurface is integral. Its singular set has dimension at most one by the calculations below, hence codimension at least two in the threefold. Serre's \(R_1+S_2\) criterion gives normality.

**Characteristic different from two and three.** For \(G=W^2+UV-2t^6\), the affine hypersurface Jacobian ideal is generated by
\[
G,\quad V,\quad U,\quad 2W,\quad -12t^5.
\]
Since \(2\) and \(3\) are invertible, this equals \((U,V,W,t^5)\). Its local quotient is \(k[t]/(t^5)\), supported only at \(t=0\). By reversal there are exactly two distinct support points \(P_a,P_e\), each of length five. Euler's formula \(3F=aF_a+bF_b+cF_c+dF_d+eF_e\) shows that \(B=\Sigma\) in this characteristic range.

**Characteristic three and the polar base.** Here \(-12=0\), while \(2\) is invertible. On \(a=1\), the ideal generated by the first partials (equivalently their transforms under the triangular coordinate automorphism) is \((U,V,W)\), a reduced affine line. In original coordinates its points are \([1:t:-t^2:t^3:t^4]\), the affine chart of \(C_-\). Reversal gives the complementary chart of the same rational normal quartic. If \(a=0\) and all first partials vanish, \(F_e=b^2\) forces \(b=0\); \(F_b=2cd\) and \(F_a=ce+d^2\) then give either the endpoint \(P_e\) (when \(c=0\)) or the additional point \(Q=[0:0:1:0:0]\) (when \(c\ne0\)). At \(Q\), in the chart \(c=1\), the equations \(F_e=a+b^2=0\), \(F_a=e+d^2=0\), \(F_b=2(be+d)=0\), \(F_d=2(ad+b)=0\) imply \(d(1-bd)=b(1-bd)=0\), hence \(b=d=0\) because \(1-bd\) is a unit there. Thus \(Q\) is an isolated **reduced** polar-base point. The two components are disjoint and \(B=C_-\sqcup Q\) scheme-theoretically.

Substituting the quartic parameterization gives
\[
F(s^4,s^3t,-s^2t^2,st^3,t^4)=-2s^6t^6,
\]
and \(F(Q)=1\). The scheme \(\Sigma=B\cap V(F)\) is therefore the effective divisor \(6P_a+6P_e\) on the smooth quartic, which has length twelve. This also follows locally from \((G,U,V,W)=(U,V,W,t^6)\); it is essential to include \(F\) because Euler's identity becomes \(0=0\) in characteristic three.

**Characteristic two.** Now \(G=W^2+UV\); the polar ideal in the chart \(a=1\) is \((U,V)\), and the intrinsic singular scheme has ring \(k[t,W]/(W^2)\). Since \(3\) is a unit in characteristic two, Euler's identity implies \(B=\Sigma\). Globally the five derivatives reduce to the three quadrics shown above (the other two vanish identically); they vanish precisely on the rational normal quartic \(C_+\) after passing to the reduced support. Indeed on \(a=1\) the reduced parametrization is \((b,c,d,e)=(t,t^2,t^3,t^4)\); if \(a=0\), then the quadrics force \(b=c=d=0\), giving only \(P_e\). The ideal has codimension three and three generators, hence is a complete intersection. Its local double structure is square-zero along its smooth reduced curve, making it a ribbon. The degree is \(2^3=8\), and adjunction yields \(\omega_\Sigma\simeq\mathcal O_\Sigma(1)\), so the arithmetic genus is \(5\) and the Hilbert polynomial is \(8m-4\). This completes the classification.

## Verification
The standalone `check_hankel_permanent.py` uses exact symbolic arithmetic to re-expand the integral normal form, verify the inverse coordinate substitution and reversal symmetry, compare transformed Jacobian ideals by Gröbner reduction modulo \(2,3,5,7,11\), and verify the quartic polar parameterization and its restriction equation. It also enumerates all rational projective points over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_5\) to check polar and singular support in those small fields. Execute `python check_hankel_permanent.py` with Python 3 and SymPy. The successful marker is `VERIFY_OK`. Finite-field points do not prove equality after algebraic closure; the displayed coordinate calculations do.

## Relationship to prior work
Grieco–Guerrieri–Swanson (2005) investigate ideals of **two-by-two** permanents of Hankel matrices, not the intrinsic singular schemes of the hypersurface defined by the **three-by-three** permanent. Ballico–Elia (2014) analyze a different family of **determinantal**, rather than permanental, Hankel varieties. Cheltsov–Marquand–Tschinkel–Zhang (2025, §5) explicitly give the \(2A_5\) cubic-threefold normal-form family, including the characteristic-zero polynomial here after permuting variables and taking its coefficient to be \(2\); thus the characteristic-zero classification is not asserted as a novelty. The characteristic-two reduction equals the classical Hankel determinant and its already studied nonreduced canonical-ribbon geometry. The claim left after these deductions is the **simultaneous modular Jacobian/polar-scheme trichotomy**, particularly the unexpected reduced characteristic-three polar quartic outside the cubic and the length-six intersection scheme. No claim of exhaustive novelty verification is made.

## Limitations
No conclusions are claimed for arithmetic integral models, deformations beyond this fixed cubic, or resolution invariants other than those explicitly given. The mathematical calculation is exact, but equivalent modular results could exist under another cubic-threefold or catalecticant normalization. Independent audit has not been performed.
