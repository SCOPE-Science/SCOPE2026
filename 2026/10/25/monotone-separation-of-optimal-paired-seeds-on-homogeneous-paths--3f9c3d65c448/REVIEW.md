# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For every \(n\ge2\) and \(0<p<1\), activation probabilities on the unique paths give the stated exact two-seed spread polynomial. A strict concavity/exchange argument balances both exterior tails. The derived adjacent-gap difference has a factor \(p^{h_b}\) times \(R_b-1\), where \(R_b\) strictly decreases in seed distance. This proves strict unimodality in the integer gap. A one-variable strictly concave logarithmic function has one interior threshold for every eligible gap, and comparison of successive \(R_b\) proves all thresholds strictly ordered. The sharp \(n=4\) switch follows from an explicit factorization. Independent finite checks cover exact rational live-edge enumeration and adjacent-gap polynomial identities; none are misrepresented as proving an infinite assertion.

## Originality — PASS, with explicit literature risk

The general independent-cascade/live-edge model and influence maximization were established by Kempe–Kleinberg–Tardos (2003). Undirected-network approximation results of Khanna–Lucier (2014) have broader graph scope but do not state this exact optimal-location phase diagram. Mouravski (2011) is directly relevant: its accessible abstract announces an algorithm for choosing optimal seeds on paths, but no complete thesis text was retrievable. The precise strictly monotone, algebraically parameterized all-\(n\) threshold theorem is not implied by the abstract or the prior algorithm's existence alone; equivalent coverage inside that thesis remains unresolved. The relevant full technical texts of the first two publications were inspected; the thesis assessment is explicitly abstract-only. Independent bibliographic review should revisit its full technical results.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result solves a nontrivial continuous-parameter intervention-design question: as per-contact adoption probability improves, should a marketer bring two initial seeds together, keep them apart, or move them toward endpoints? A sharp all-network-size answer and fully ordered algebraic switching thresholds make optimization possible without enumerating all source pairs for each probability. The structural transition rule matters even where a generic polynomial-time path-optimization algorithm is available; it describes the behavior of the optimum over the entire probability interval. The result does not claim general-network or heterogeneous-contact optimality.

## Scope and residual risks

The model is restricted to paths, exactly two seeds, and one common independent undirected-edge transmission probability. The Mouravski (2011) full thesis was not accessible for a decisive same-theorem comparison; a separately verifiable originality risk remains. No independent mathematical audit has been conducted.
