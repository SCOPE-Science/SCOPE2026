# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** Every conditional law follows from the uniform Prüfer code and Stirling surjection counts; the zero-multiplicity case uses exchangeability. The positive size-bias has fixed normalizer \(\mathbb E X=(n-2)/n\). The strict Stirling-ratio inequality is derived from the simple real roots of \(\sum_r S(t,r)z^r\) and the Stirling recurrence, without assuming numerical checks beyond the theorem's scope. Ratios between adjacent conditional size-biased laws increase in the positive multiplicity, including the necessary endpoint for strictness and the \(j=2\) deterministic case. Dividing convex increments by the positive multiplicity recovers all convex payoffs at fixed mean, proving the theorem for all \(n\ge4\). Exact rational tests and complete short-word enumeration agree. Risk: mistakes in translating multiplicity to degree were separately checked by direct word enumeration.

**Originality — PASS under checked coverage, subject to historical-priority risk.** A prior exact Cayley-tree result characterizes the leaf-count law conditioned on tagged degree, including stochastic comparisons across degrees; it determines the joint masses by Bayes but does **not** establish the distinct all-count strict convex order of the degree given leaves. The new Stirling ratio and size-bias comparison supplies that missing implication. The closely relevant Remmel–Williamson 2010 fixed-leaf tree paper counts and ranks trees with specified leaves, not an all-count strict tagged-degree convex order. The Bakhtin–Heitsch 2007 full text concerns asymptotic degree-frequency large deviations in a Gibbs tree model, not the exact conditional law. The Sagitov 2026 full text concerns multinomial allocation moments without this conditioning. Searches for equivalent phrases (surjection fibers, occupancy, size-biased box load, Stirling total positivity, tagged Cayley-tree degree) and implication comparisons did not reveal a dominating theorem. The full body of classical occupancy ordering literature is not exhaustively audited; absence from searchable summaries is not proof of global historical novelty.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Fixed leaf count is a canonical degree-sequence constraint on Cayley trees, and convex degree costs measure concentration and hub penalties in network structure. The exact mean remains unchanged across all leaf counts, yet **every strictly convex cost increases**; this is a structural family of inequalities stronger than a variance identity, with an explicit likelihood-ratio mechanism and sharp end regimes. The result is not just a renaming of the leaf-count formula.

**Limits and comparisons.** The conclusion is finite and complete for uniform labeled trees and exact feasible leaf counts. It does not establish a joint degree-vector order, independence, or transfer to other tree ensembles. Potential coverage by a more general classical occupancy stochastic-order theorem is a residual risk, not silently certified away.
