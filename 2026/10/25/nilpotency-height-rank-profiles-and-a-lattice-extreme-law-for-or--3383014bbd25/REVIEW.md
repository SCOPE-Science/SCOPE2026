# Scientific review: nilpotency-height and rank profiles of partial chain isometries

## Correctness
**PASS.** Every nonempty order-preserving chain isometry is a unique nonzero-displacement translation or a partial identity. Its least zero power is one above the longest occupied run along a displacement residue chain. The forbidden-run bivariate word polynomial follows from a unique run-and-zero decomposition; the empty map is counted just once. Direct independent enumeration and composition check all rank-by-index coefficients for sizes through 11. For the limit, minimally preceded length-\(k\) runs have mean tending to \(\theta/2\), conflicting run starts are mutually exclusive, and all sufficiently separated starts are independent. Factorial moments give the Poisson law. The displacement distribution is geometrically tight, permitting passage from fixed shift to uniformly sampled nilpotents. Only the displayed subsequential lattice limit is asserted.

## Originality
**PASS within the examined sources, subject to residual priority risks.** Ali, Jada and Zubairu's full 2018/2019 text, especially Proposition 2.5, Theorem 2.6, and Sections 3–4, counts nilpotents and studies nilpotent-generated subsemigroup ranks; it does not give the rank-by-least-zero-power filtration. Al-Kharousi–Kehinde–Umar's earlier total/rank counts (as recorded in OEIS A183153 and A183155) are acknowledged as prior results. Classical longest-Bernoulli-run estimates are also acknowledged, so the probability limit is not presented as a new general probability theorem. The new assessed combination is the exact semigroup-specific joint filtration and the displacement-mixed extreme law. Two relevant earlier full texts, including the 2014 paper on combinatorial equivalences and the 2016 paper on cycle structure, were not comprehensively inspected; they remain priority risks rather than being described as ruled out. A 2023 natural-partial-order paper was accessible only at the public abstract level, which appears to address a different invariant.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The least zero power measures nilpotent depth and is different from transformation rank and nilpotent-generated semigroup rank. The theorem resolves the whole two-variable rank-depth distribution for every finite chain and explains the typical nilpotent depth at large size. This identifies a natural algebraic refinement of known element counts, with an exact computable finite formula and a nontrivial lattice-extreme transition. The result is not claimed to create a new theory of extreme values or to count an arbitrary artificially selected slice.

## Closest literature and limitations
- B. Ali, M. A. Jada and M. M. Zubairu, *On the ranks of certain semigroups of order-preserving partial isometries of a finite chain* (2018/2019), DOI 10.22124/JART.2019.10875.1109: primary nilpotent count and nilpotent-generated ranks.
- F. Al-Kharousi, R. Kehinde and A. Umar, *Combinatorial results for certain semigroups of partial isometries of a finite chain* (2014), and *On the semigroup of partial isometries of a finite chain* (2016): basic cardinality and structure, with incomplete full-text access.
- OEIS A183153 and A183155: existing unrefined enumeration, neither claimed as new.
- Gordon, Schilling and Waterman (1986); Konstantopoulos, Liu and Yang (2016): classical longest-run theory, used as context; the required Poisson calculation is explicitly supplied in the proof.
- Further residual risk: results indexed under translation inverse monoids, power indices, and run-avoiding binary words may be equivalent to part of the statement.
