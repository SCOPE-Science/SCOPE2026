# Scientific review: sharp four-dimensional cube-vertex volume threshold

## Correctness — PASS
The complete implication is explicit: enclosing a nine-vertex subset forces the inverse edge matrix's four row functionals into its compact difference-width polar; multi-affinity reduces the determinant maximum to extreme vertices. Every vertex set in the rational table is certified exact by feasibility, full affine span, and an exhaustive test that all supporting facet hyperplanes are original width constraints. The exact standard-library verifier covers all 56 cube-symmetry orbits (all 11440 nine-subsets), 1066 listed rational vertices, 11812 supporting-quadruple incidences, and 290663 four-row determinant quadruples, with maximum absolute determinant one. Its integers are unbounded-precision. The unit cube gives equality and a thin slab containing eight facet vertices establishes sharpness of cardinality.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)
Grozev and Nikolov (arXiv:2610.21572v1, sections 1–2, inspected as an accessible full text) establish covering in dimension three for any five vertices, and in all dimensions under an entire-facet-plus-one-opposite-vertex hypothesis. They ask whether all more-than-half vertex configurations force covering. Their result does not imply the present all-nine-configuration volume theorem in dimension four: even-parity vertices plus one odd vertex contain no full facet. Literature and published mathematical-finding searches on nine of sixteen cube vertices, minimum-volume enclosing parallelotopes, and determinant width polars found no equivalent full statement or stronger theorem that covers it. The search is not exhaustive of older print literature; an implicit earlier volume-only theorem remains a priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is the sharp necessary volume obstruction for *every* majority subset in the smallest dimension not settled by the source's full covering result. It answers a natural measurable weakening of an expressly posed covering question, handles every one of the 56 nontrivial symmetry types rather than selected configurations, and establishes that the threshold of nine cannot be relaxed to eight. Its finite cutoff is motivated by the open four-dimensional incidence problem; the result does not claim that the full covering question is solved.

## Closest literature and limitations

The present theorem is not about packing, lattice-covering sufficiency, or equality classification; nothing is asserted for dimension five or above.
