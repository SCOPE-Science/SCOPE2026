# Research review

## Correctness — PASS
The reciprocal identity \(t^2\Phi_6(t^{-1})=\Phi_6(t)\) and the quotient \(z=\Phi_6(x)/y\) force a strict descent from any assumed nontrivial pair. The proof handles 1, equality, ordering and all positive integer boundaries. Multiplicativity of \(A(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}d\varphi(d)\) yields the squarefree applications without extra hypotheses. The reproduced exact checker confirms a finite range and a concrete four-cycle; it is not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, residual risk
The closest full-text primary source is De Koninck–Letendre, arXiv:2610.10572 (Section 5, Proposition 1): it bounds, but does not declare empty, the set of mutually dividing sixth-cyclotomic prime pairs. Its count upper bound, and its general theorem on the scarcity of integral reciprocal-gcd sums, do not logically imply the exact zero case. OEIS A317480 lists integral examples and A057660 gives a multiplicative formula; neither is an infinite absence theorem. Searches of mathematical-research records for two-cycles, mutual sixth-cyclotomic divisibility, semiprime reciprocal-gcd integrality and aliases produced no direct covering statement. The elementary descent might nevertheless appear in older unindexed literature. The available checked material has not ruled out that risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The recent study organizes its counting argument around functional-graph components and expends a dedicated proposition on the prime-first-power two-cycle possibility. The theorem eliminates **that entire structural case** exactly, including all positive integer inputs; it also proves a natural nonexistence statement for squarefree semiprimes. This is a motivated obstruction in the paper's central object, not a random finite parameter slice. It does not imply a stronger global count or eliminate prime-square-local cycles.
