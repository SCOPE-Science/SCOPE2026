# Odd cyclic rotation spectra of degree-three homology in two-cover complexes

## Finding

For every **odd integer** \(m\ge5\), let \(K_m=\operatorname{Cov}_{m,2}\): its vertices are the unordered edges of the complete graph on the labeled points \(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\), and its simplices are families of edges admitting a vertex cover of size at most two. Let \(\rho\) advance each label by one modulo \(m\), let \(V_m=\widetilde H_3(K_m;\mathbb Q)\), and write \(B_m=\binom{m-1}{4}\).

**Complete rotation character.** For every integer \(k\),

\[
\operatorname{tr}(\rho^k\mid V_m)=
\begin{cases}
 B_m,&m\mid k,\\
 1-m/3,&\rho^k\text{ has order }3,\\
 1,&\rho^k\ne 1\text{ and has order other than }3.
\end{cases}
\]

**All eigenvalue multiplicities.** For \(j\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\), let \(\mu_j\) be the multiplicity of \(\exp(2\pi i j/m)\) on \(V_m\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb C\). Then

\[
\mu_j=\mathbf 1_{j=0}+\frac{B_m-1}{m}
-\mathbf 1_{3\mid m}\frac{c_3(j)}3,
\qquad
c_3(j)=\begin{cases}2,&3\mid j,\\-1,&3\nmid j.\end{cases}
\]

Thus the formula specifies the entire representation of the cyclic group generated by \(\rho\), rather than just the dimension of \(V_m\). In particular the rational quotient cohomology has
\[
\dim_{\mathbb Q} H^3(|K_m|/\langle\rho\rangle;\mathbb Q)=\mu_0.
\]
For instance, at \(m=9\) the multiplicities in order \(j=0,1,\ldots,8\) are \((8,8,8,7,8,8,7,8,8)\); at \(m=15\), the invariant subspace has dimension \(67\).

## Assumptions and scope

All graph edges are unordered pairs of distinct points. A face of \(K_m\) is any collection of graph edges incident to at most two common covering points, not necessarily a matching. A group permutation acts on the vertices of this simplicial complex and induces the **signed** simplicial chain action and its rational homology representation. The complex is finite and coefficients are rational for the representation statements. The theorem concerns **odd** cycle lengths \(m\ge5\); it makes no claim for even \(m\), integral equivariant modules or an equivariant homotopy splitting.

We use two previously established results, neither claimed as new: Jonsson's integral homology calculation [1] gives
\[
\widetilde H_i(K_m;\mathbb Q)=0\quad(i\notin\{3,4\}),\qquad
\dim V_m=B_m,
\]
and the four-subset basis theorem [3] identifies \(\widetilde H_4(K_m;\mathbb Q)\) with the permutation representation on the four-element subsets of the graph-vertex labels. The same two-cover complex is a finite-set Čech complex in the \(4\le r<8\) regime of [2].

## Proof

**1. Invariant edge-orbit cover costs.** Fix a nonidentity power \(\sigma=\rho^k\). Since \(m\) is odd, every orbit of graph vertices under \(\sigma\) has the same odd length \(\ell=m/\gcd(m,k)\ge3\). Write \(g=m/\ell\) for their number. Every simplex fixed setwise by \(\sigma\) is the union of some orbits of the induced action on the *graph edges*.

For a graph edge joining two different \(\sigma\)-vertex orbits, its full \(\sigma\)-orbit consists of \(\ell\) disjoint graph edges and therefore has vertex-cover number \(\ell\ge3\): none can occur in a simplex of \(K_m\).

For a graph edge with both endpoints in one \(\sigma\)-vertex orbit, the graph consisting of its full edge orbit is a disjoint union of cycles, each having odd length at least three. Indeed, after numbering the \(\ell\) points cyclically, the graph joins point \(u\) to \(u+d\) for one nonzero increment \(d\) and decomposes into \(\gcd(\ell,d)\) cycles of length \(\ell/\gcd(\ell,d)\), all odd. Its minimum vertex-cover number is at least \(\lceil\ell/2\rceil\). In particular, if \(\ell\ge5\), every graph-edge orbit already requires at least three covering points, and **no nonempty invariant face exists**.

If \(\ell=3\), a graph-edge orbit internal to any one of the \(g\) graph-vertex cycles is exactly its three-edge triangle and has vertex-cover number two. Those \(g\) triangle orbits are all the graph-edge orbits that are individually faces. A union of two distinct triangle orbits requires at least four covering vertices because their graph-vertex sets are disjoint. Hence the **only** nonempty setwise-fixed faces are the \(g\) three-vertex simplices given by these triangle orbits.

**2. Reduced simplicial Lefschetz traces.** The reduced Lefschetz number \(\widetilde L(\sigma)\) equals the alternating trace on the augmented simplicial chains; the empty face contributes \(-1\). A simplex setwise fixed by a permutation contributes \((-1)^{\dim F}\) multiplied by the sign of the induced permutation on its vertices. When \(\ell\ge5\), there is no nonempty invariant face, so \(\widetilde L(\sigma)=-1\). When \(\ell=3\), the only nonempty invariant faces are the \(g\) two-simplices above; each three-cycle of simplicial vertices is even, so each contributes \(+1\). Consequently
\[
\widetilde L(\sigma)=\begin{cases}-1,&\ell\ge5,\\g-1,&\ell=3.\end{cases}
\]

**3. Separation of homological degrees.** The prior four-subset basis theorem [3] says that the trace on degree-four homology is the number of \(\sigma\)-invariant four-element subsets of the graph-vertex set. Every nonidentity orbit has odd size \(\ell\ge3\), so no union of these orbits has cardinality four; therefore
\[
\operatorname{tr}(\sigma\mid\widetilde H_4(K_m;\mathbb Q))=0.
\]
By [1], only homological degrees three and four are nonzero, and hence
\[
\widetilde L(\sigma)=-\operatorname{tr}(\sigma\mid V_m)
+\operatorname{tr}(\sigma\mid\widetilde H_4(K_m;\mathbb Q))
=-\operatorname{tr}(\sigma\mid V_m).
\]
Using Step 2 yields trace \(1\) if \(\ell\ge5\), and \(1-m/3\) if \(\ell=3\). For the identity, [1] gives trace \(\dim V_m=B_m\). This proves the complete character.

**4. Exact Fourier multiplicities.** Let \(\zeta=\exp(2\pi i/m)\). Projection to the \(\zeta^j\)-eigenspace gives
\[
\mu_j=\frac1m\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}
\operatorname{tr}(\rho^k\mid V_m)\,\zeta^{-jk}.
\]
If \(3\nmid m\), all nonidentity traces equal one, immediately giving \(\mu_j=\mathbf1_{j=0}+(B_m-1)/m\). If \(3\mid m\), exactly the two powers \(\rho^{m/3}\) and \(\rho^{2m/3}\) have order three. Their traces decrease from the baseline value one by \(m/3\). The resulting correction is
\[
-\tfrac13(\zeta^{-jm/3}+\zeta^{-2jm/3})=-\tfrac13 c_3(j),
\]
which proves the stated formula. Fourier projection also proves all the displayed multiplicities are nonnegative integers; the accompanying verifier tests their explicit integer arithmetic separately.

**5. Quotient invariant.** A finite group acting on a finite simplicial complex admits a barycentric subdivision with simplicial quotient compatible with rational averaging. Rational cohomology of the orbit space is the invariant part of the original rational cohomology. Thus its degree-three dimension is the trivial-character multiplicity \(\mu_0\), as asserted.

## Verification

The accompanying standard-library Python program `verify_rotation.py` exhaustively reconstructs graph-edge orbits under 304 nonidentity rotation powers for odd sizes \(5\le m\le35\), verifies the cover-cost dichotomy, and computes the exact alternating signed trace over **all invariant simplices** for 28 small cases. It separately checks integrality and nonnegativity of every Fourier multiplicity for 49 odd sizes \(5\le m\le101\) and numerically reconstructs all predicted character traces from those multiplicities. Output is recorded in `verify_rotation.out`, with a terminating `VERIFY_OK` marker.

These finite calculations corroborate the orbit analysis, parity signs and Fourier formula. They do **not** replace the infinite proof or the earlier published homology and degree-four character results, which are explicit dependencies. Run `python3 verify_rotation.py` using Python 3 and the standard library.

## Relationship to prior work

Jonsson [1] computed the integral Betti numbers of the same two-cover complex, including \(\dim V_m=B_m\), but that rank does not determine a nontrivial cyclic character. Biswas [2] identifies the complex with its Čech formulation and discusses nonequivariant homotopy. The published four-subset theorem [3] supplies the degree-four permutation character and states that degree-three symmetry remains undescribed; the new calculation uses that degree-four theorem to isolate the previously undetermined third-homology cyclic character by a direct simplicial fixed-orbit count.

The **new conclusion** is the full degree-three cyclic character and its eigenvalue multiplicities at **all odd orders**, including the exceptional order-three correction and the quotient invariant; existing degree-three Betti numbers, degree-four characters, and finite nonequivariant homotopy types are not presented as new. Rotational symmetry examples for unrelated graph and Rips complexes do not imply this specific statement. The most relevant older full text could not be inspected page by page; a previously indexed exact theorem statement was available, so undetected literature overlap remains a risk.

## Limitations

The theorem uses rational representations and applies only to **odd** \(m\ge5\). It does not determine the full \(S_m\)-representation in degree three, any even-order rotation spectra, torsion-sensitive equivariant data, an integral lattice decomposition, or equivariant homotopy types. Its correctness uses Jonsson's published homology theorem [1] and the published four-subset basis theorem [3] without re-proving them. The older paper [1] was not available in full text for a page-by-page novelty exclusion, and independently unindexed equivalent results remain possible. The finite verification is not a substitute for the exact proof.
