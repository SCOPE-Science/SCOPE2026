# Review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact Dirichlet–multinomial mass and indicator decomposition give the covariance directly, including edge cases \(r=1\), \(r=n\), \(m=3\), and \(\alpha=1\). A positive monotone ratio proves the one-crossing theorem; strict endpoint inequalities are established for \(n\ge2\). The weak-composition zero criterion follows from exact cancellation with strictly positive denominators. Gamma-ratio asymptotics prove the fixed-concentration critical fraction, and Stirling asymptotics plus the finite-\(n\) independent-color limit prove noncommuting fractions. Exact rational enumeration covers 420 parameter-frequency cases, but is supplementary, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (with priority risk)

Huillet–Martínez (arXiv:1307.3000), Proposition 5, already give *general joint factorial moments* and Section 3 gives the very Dirichlet–multinomial model. These classical foundations encompass raw moment calculation; novelty is not claimed for the distribution or the unclassified moment. Its inspected treatment does not provide the strictly ordered zero-versus-frequency sign geometry, the exact arithmetic zero locus, or the two noncommuting critical fractions. Dello Schiavo–Quattrocchi (arXiv:2309.11292) likewise treat the Dirichlet mixture in a broader polychromatic framework without these sign conclusions in the inspected Example 5.54. The closest independent-occupancy result gives a different finite law: never-zero covariance and a distinct critical fraction. Algebraic derivability from general moments is acknowledged; an earlier explicit equivalent phase classification remains a historical-priority risk. The original contribution is the combined classification, not the classical ingredients.

## Value — PASS

The exact zero locus marks an observable sharp difference between reinforced categorical sampling and independent allocations, where zero covariance cannot occur. The unique threshold classifies all positive frequencies simultaneously without numerical root searches. The limiting critical fractions distinguish a sample-size asymptotic under fixed random environment from an iid limit: even arbitrarily large fixed concentration is insufficient to replace the order of limits. These facts are natural for unseen-species and frequency-of-frequencies statistics and not a chosen arbitrary parameter slice.
