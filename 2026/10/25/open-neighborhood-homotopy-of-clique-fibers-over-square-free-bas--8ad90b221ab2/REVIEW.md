**Correctness — PASS.** The absence of base four-cycles gives unique centers for every pair of vertical neighborhood vertices. The explicitly enumerated free-face pairs are disjoint, and all their alternative cofaces disappear in descending order. The exact residual is a union of edge blocks glued along simply connected clique-fiber boundaries. The crown-graph intersection has cycle rank \(m^2-3m+1\), and null attaching maps provide a homotopy-colimit proof of the claimed integral wedge. Exact finite binary-homology and collapse checks support but do not replace the proof.

**Originality — PASS (with residual literature risk).** The closest full-text open-neighborhood source proves the single-clique-prism case, and another covers a single base cycle of length at least five; neither yields an arbitrary square-free connected base. The most relevant arbitrary-base clique-fiber source is about the different *closed-neighborhood Čech* complex. An additional primary survey covers bounded-degree and special four-regular families. Focused semantic searches found no covering general-base open-neighborhood statement. Unindexed equivalent prior work remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem gives a motivated structural classification, including independent fundamental-group and higher-homology contributions, for branching trees, triangles and graphs with several independent cycles. Its global gluing proof addresses a real obstruction not resolved by the restricted prior edge/cycle formulas.

**Limitations.** The theorem assumes a finite connected base with no four-cycle and clique width at least four; it does not assert equivariant naturality, persistence, or an extension past these bounds. Computational examples do not certify the general statement. Novelty is a careful comparison, not an exhaustive world-literature proof.
