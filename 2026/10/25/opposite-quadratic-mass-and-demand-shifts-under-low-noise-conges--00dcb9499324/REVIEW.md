# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The scalar fixed-point map is strictly monotone by coordinatewise monotonicity of congestion. Logistic probability moments are represented as clipped random-threshold moments and expanded uniformly away from endpoints. An implicit equation with positive derivative \(D\) produces the two quadratic coefficients; exact softplus integration gives the linear \(L^1\) coefficient. The non-potential witness has \(A=5/4\), \(B=1\), and \(D=7/4\), and 174 independent numerical assertions reproduce the stated coefficients. Finite verification is not used to replace the global proof.

**Originality — PASS (same-model, qualified).** The full primary text of Dupont–Petit–Séguret, arXiv:2610.18035v1, Theorems 2.1–2.2, Sections 3.1 and 4.3.1 and conclusion, treats uniqueness and convergence but asks for quantitative zero-noise rates; it does not state this coupled two-aggregate asymptotic. The full Bagchi–Boyles manuscript, arXiv:2605.21843v2, Sections 4–7 and conclusion, derives iteration rates for fixed stochastic equilibrium, a different limiting parameter. An elementary Sommerfeld moment formula is acknowledged as prior mathematical technique. Koster et al. (2014, TI Discussion Paper 14-078/VIII) analyze probabilistic two-link traffic pricing with discrete groups, but the inspected sections and appendices do not establish this continuous-threshold and moment-feedback expansion. Broader unindexed work still poses priority risk; search non-detection is not a uniqueness proof.

**Value — PASS.** The motivating paper distinguishes charging-station headcount from delivered energy and highlights quantitative small-noise convergence as open. This theorem shows the two aggregates can move in rigorously opposite directions in a genuinely non-potential setting, gives exact leading constants, and separates quadratic aggregate bias from linear choice-profile error. The restriction to an interior threshold, uniform characteristic and smooth monotone costs is substantive.

**Limitations and sources.** No independent validation has been performed. Arbitrary distributions, discontinuous costs, multiple thresholds, general multi-resource models and full publication priority remain open.
