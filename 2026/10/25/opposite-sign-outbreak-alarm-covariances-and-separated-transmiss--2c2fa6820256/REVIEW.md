# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed theorem concerns a uniformly selected single source, independently retained star edges, and *ever-reached* alarm indicators. The proof enumerates four exact first/second moments by conditioning on the seed; their subtractions factor into the displayed hub–leaf quadratic and distinct-leaf cubic. Monotonicity of the quadratic and strict convexity of the cubic yield one-sided unique root claims; a positive concave-quadratic comparison orders their thresholds. Rescaling the polynomials and squeezing their unique crossings prove both asymptotic limits. The standalone exact-rational verifier reproduces 42 exhaustive small-size cases and the four-vertex opposite signs, without treating these checks as infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The inspected Kenah–Robins epidemic-percolation framework and Leskovec et al. network-detection treatment do not themselves imply a classification of two pairwise final-monitor covariances on uniform-source stars or the distinct scaling exponents. The inspected Rodriguez–Roldán-Correa–Valencia counterexample concerns fixed-time current-infection states, not final ever-reached monitors. Donnelly (1993) has relevant positive-association claims, but its complete text was not accessible; exactly-one-uniform-source initial infection is nonproduct and is not evidently within those positive-association premises. Searches for aliases, stronger implications, equivalences, and previously tabulated thresholds did not reveal the precise theorem. This is a literature-supported self-review, **not** independent novelty certification; an equivalent formulation under stochastic epidemic association or star reliability remains a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The direction is motivated by whether distinct epidemic monitors supply corroborating information, rather than just whether some monitor detects an outbreak. The exact phase diagram exhibits a nonempty regime of simultaneous positive hub–leaf and negative leaf–leaf covariance and gives the *entire* transition structure for every star size. The different threshold exponents give a structural separation of the two sensor-pair types in large populations. This is a complete natural classification under a specified model, rather than a tiny numerical adjustment or an arbitrary finite slice.

## Scope and literature limits

The result is confined to homogeneous independent bond percolation with one uniformly selected source and final-ever-reached alarms. General SIR, heterogeneous transmission, dependent source status, and instantaneous infection correlations are not covered. The relevant Donnelly 1993 full text and Cator–Van Mieghem 2014 full text were unavailable; their abstracts and the freely accessible 2018 comment were compared at the level of model assumptions and observables. No exhaustive external novelty guarantee follows.
