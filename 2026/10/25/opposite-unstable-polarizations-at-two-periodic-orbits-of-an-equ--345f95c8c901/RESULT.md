# Opposite unstable polarizations at two periodic orbits of an equilateral open billiard
## Finding
Consider three congruent oblate ellipsoids, all with planar-equatorial radius \(r\), vertical radius \(r/\beta\), and centers at the vertices of an equilateral triangle of side \(D\). Assume \(0<r<D/10\) and \(\beta\ge1\). The open billiard outside the three obstacles possesses a normal-incidence period-two orbit and a symmetric period-three orbit. Their two positive **per-collision** Lyapunov exponents in the invariant planar and vertical Jacobi directions are, respectively,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{2,\parallel}&=\operatorname{arcosh}(1+q_2),&\lambda_{2,\perp}&=\operatorname{arcosh}(1+\beta^2q_2),\\
\lambda_{3,\parallel}&=\operatorname{arcosh}(1+q_3/c),&\lambda_{3,\perp}&=\operatorname{arcosh}(1+\beta^2 c q_3),
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(q_2=(D-2r)/r\), \(q_3=(D-\sqrt3r)/r\), and \(c=\sqrt3/2\). Thus, for every \(1<\beta<2/\sqrt3\), the period-two orbit expands faster vertically but the period-three orbit expands faster in the obstacle-center plane. At precisely \(\beta_*=2/\sqrt3\), the two positive exponents of the period-three orbit coincide; their ordering reverses across this value. These strict opposite orderings persist for sufficiently small perturbations of the obstacle centers and radii at any fixed parameter strictly inside that interval. Consequently, neither canonical unstable line subbundle (vertical or planar) uniformly dominates the other throughout the non-wandering set in that parameter range. The critical value does not depend on \(D/r\).
## Assumptions and scope
Let \(C_0,C_1,C_2\in\mathbb R^2\times\{0\}\) form an equilateral triangle of side \(D>0\). For \(r\in(0,D/10)\), \(\beta\ge1\), the pairwise disjoint obstacles are \(K_j=\{(x,y,z):|(x,y)-C_j|^2+\beta^2z^2\le r^2\}\). The exterior billiard reflects specularly on \(\partial K_j\). All assertions concern **per-collision** rather than per-unit-flight-time exponents of the billiard ball map, and the periodic-orbit invariant probabilities, not Gibbs probabilities with full symbolic support. The two transverse Jacobi modes are perpendicular to and within the plane of obstacle centers, respectively. The no-eclipse condition follows from \(\sqrt3D/2>2r\).
## Proof
**1. Periodic rays and their incidences.** Write \(e_j=(C_j-G)/|C_j-G|\), where \(G\) is the centroid. The three impact points \(Q_j=C_j-r e_j\) form a concentric equilateral triangle of side \(\ell_3=D-\sqrt3r\). At each \(Q_j\) the outward surface normal is \(-e_j\). Direct dot products of the preceding and following unit flight directions with this normal have values \(-c\) and \(+c\), with \(c=\sqrt3/2\). Specular reflection is therefore exactly satisfied; these are the impacts of a three-bounce periodic ray. Along any pair of centers, the two closest boundary points supply a normal-incidence two-bounce ray with flight length \(\ell_2=D-2r\). With \(r<D/10\), both rays are unobstructed by the third obstacle.

**2. The physically normalized collision matrix.** At an equatorial impact, write the surface locally as the graph \(x=r-\frac{y^2+\beta^2z^2}{2r}+O((|y|+|z|)^4)\), using axes with \(x\) normal to the obstacle. Thus the principal normal curvatures in the planar and vertical tangent directions are \(1/r\) and \(\beta^2/r\); in particular the vertical curvature is computed from the specified ellipsoid equation, not from a change of flattening parameter. For incidence cosine \(c_0>0\), differentiation of \(v^+=v^--2(v^-\cdot n)n\), with the Poincaré displacement measured in the plane perpendicular to the outgoing ray, gives reflection kicks \(\psi_{\parallel}=2/(r c_0)\) and \(\psi_{\perp}=2\beta^2c_0/r\). Indeed, a vertical impact displacement \(\delta z\) changes \(n_z\) by \(\beta^2\delta z/r\), hence the reflected vertical angular displacement by \(2c_0\beta^2\delta z/r\). In the incidence plane the boundary arclength displacement projects to \(c_0\) times that displacement in the ray-normal section, giving \(2/(r c_0)\). Free flight of length \(\ell\) has displacement-angle shear \(\begin{psmallmatrix}1&\ell\\0&1\end{psmallmatrix}\). Each scalar block of the one-collision return is conjugate (up to orientation signs) to the positive symplectic matrix
\[
P(\ell,\psi)=\begin{pmatrix}1&\ell\\\psi&1+\ell\psi\end{pmatrix},\quad \det P=1,\quad \tfrac12\operatorname{tr}P=1+\ell\psi/2.
\]
Its expanding multiplier is \(\exp(\operatorname{arcosh}(1+\ell\psi/2))\). Both periodic rays have constant incidence, curvature, and flight length, so after one period the corresponding scalar blocks are powers (up to a bounded periodic frame change) of this matrix. Taking the per-collision logarithmic growth gives \(\operatorname{arcosh}(1+\ell\psi/2)\). For the two-bounce ray \(c_0=1\), while for the three-bounce ray \(c_0=c=\sqrt3/2\). The four displayed formulas follow.

**3. Crossing and obstruction.** The real function \(s\mapsto\operatorname{arcosh}(1+s)\) is strictly increasing for \(s>0\). Therefore \(\lambda_{2,\perp}>\lambda_{2,\parallel}\) precisely when \(\beta>1\), and \(\lambda_{3,\perp}>\lambda_{3,\parallel}\) precisely when \(\beta^2c^2>1\). Thus the three-bounce gap vanishes exactly at \(\beta_*=1/c=2/\sqrt3\), and has strictly positive derivative there:
\[
\left.\frac{d}{d\beta}(\lambda_{3,\perp}-\lambda_{3,\parallel})\right|_{\beta_*}
=\frac{2q_3}{\sqrt{(1+q_3/c)^2-1}}>0.
\]
If either canonical unstable line bundle uniformly dominated the other on the non-wandering set, the corresponding asymptotic expansion inequality would hold at **every** periodic orbit; the two computed opposite signs rule out both possibilities. Finally, the two periodic rays are hyperbolic (each scalar block has positive trace exceeding two); continuation under small smooth perturbations of the scatterer geometry, together with continuous dependence of their positive multipliers, preserves the two strict inequalities. The nondegenerate critical crossing also continues as a local smooth parameter hypersurface by the implicit-function theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Al Dowais and Stoyanov (2026) formulate the same ellipsoidal open-billiard family, prove directional Lyapunov separation for Gibbs measures under sufficiently strong anisotropy, and provide general ray-section linearization. Their stated theorem does not give a sharp crossing for either of these two periodic itineraries or identify coexistence of opposite directional orderings in the weak-flattening regime. Their 2023/2025 spherical-obstacle paper treats Gibbs-measure splitting at spherical geometry, not this anisotropy crossing. General billiard reflection matrices are used as established tools; the new assertion is the explicit periodic-orbit phase transition and resulting global uniform-dominance obstruction. Previously indexed large-flattening expansions concern \(\beta\to\infty\), not the finite \(2/\sqrt3\) boundary.
## Limitations
This concerns three symmetric no-eclipse scatterers and their two named periodic orbits. No claim is made about the ordering for all Gibbs measures when \(\beta\le2/\sqrt3\), or about the nonexistence of some different dominated splitting unrelated to the canonical planar/vertical lines. The open-perturbation assertion guarantees only persistence of these opposite periodic inequalities. The result is a self-review mathematical argument and has not received an independent audit. General optical formulas from classical dispersing-billiard theory remain substantial prior ingredients.
