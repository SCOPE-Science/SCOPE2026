# Orientation-twisted carrier homology and the simplex law for finite covering scenarios

## Finding
Let \(X\) be a connected finite triangulation of a closed \(n\)-manifold, \(n\geq1\), and let \(f:E\to X\) be a simplicial map with finite fibers. Given a simplicial probability distribution \(p\) on \(f\), write \(S=E(p)\) for the simplicial subset generated by its support and \(g=f|_S\). Let \(\mathfrak o_X\) be the integral orientation local system of \(|X|\). Then
\[
\boxed{\dim\operatorname{carr}(p)=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z} H_n(|S|;g^*\mathfrak o_X)-1.}
\]
The equality holds whether \(X\) is orientable or not. More precisely, the twisted top-chain boundary matrix is integrally equivalent, by signed changes of bases, to the facet-compatibility matrix of the support, so their integer kernels are isomorphic. If \(\widehat S\to S\) is the orientation double cover obtained by pullback, the same rank is \(\dim_{\mathbb Q} H_n(|\widehat S|;\mathbb Q)^-\), the negative eigenspace of the deck transformation.

As a concrete classification following from the same chain identification, if \(f:E\to X\) is a finite simplicial covering with connected components \(E_1,\ldots,E_c\) of degrees \(d_1,\ldots,d_c\), its **entire** simplicial-distribution polytope is the simplex
\[
\{(a_1,\ldots,a_c):a_j\geq0,\ \sum_jd_ja_j=1\}.
\]
At its \(j\)-th vertex, every lift lying in \(E_j\) has weight \(1/d_j\), and all other lifts have weight zero. In particular a connected nontrivial finite cover has a unique, generally nondeterministic, extremal distribution. The top twisted homology is \(\mathbb Z^c\) for the full support, irrespective of orientability.

## Assumptions and scope
A finite triangulation is viewed as a simplicial set with its nondegenerate faces; \(X\) is pure, every codimension-one face lies in exactly two top simplices, and facet adjacency is connected. We specifically require a closed manifold so that \(\mathfrak o_X\) is an honest rank-one local system on all of \(|X|\), not merely on the nonsingular stratum of a pseudomanifold. The event map \(f\) need not be a covering for the principal statement. A simplicial distribution assigns a probability measure to every fiber over every simplex, compatible with the face and degeneracy maps; its carrier is the minimal face of the resulting finite-dimensional polytope containing \(p\). The finite support \(S\) contains precisely the nondegenerate simplices with positive weights and their faces. The special covering assertion assumes a finite-sheeted simplicial covering, with covering degrees constant because \(|X|\) is connected.

## Proof
**1. Compatible weights as a lattice kernel.** For each nondegenerate top simplex \(e\) of \(S\), introduce a real variable \(z_e\). For a nondegenerate codimension-one face \(w\), its image under \(g\) lies in exactly two top simplices \(\sigma_+\) and \(\sigma_-\) of \(X\). The marginal equation at \(w\) reads
\[
\sum_{\substack{e:\,w\subset e\\g(e)=\sigma_+}} z_e-\sum_{\substack{e:\,w\subset e\\g(e)=\sigma_-}}z_e=0.
\]
Denote the integer matrix of these equations by \(A_g\). The support of a genuine distribution is positive in every top-simplex coordinate. The kernel of \(A_g\) contains that positive vector, so intersecting with the affine normalization hyperplane and coordinate halfspaces does not reduce the local affine dimension except by one. Equivalently, the facet-matrix theorem of Kharoof–Lai, Proposition 3.16, gives
\[
\dim\operatorname{carr}(p)+1=\dim_{\mathbb R}\ker A_g.
\]
The hypothesis on supports guaranteeing the indicated two-sided incidence is their Proposition 3.8 and Proposition 3.10, not an assumption that \(E\) itself is a covering.

**2. The missing orientation signs are supplied by a local system.** The orientation local system has stalk \(\mathfrak o_{X,x}=H_n(|X|,|X|\setminus\{x\};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\). Each oriented top simplex determines a generator at its interior; choose bases in these stalks and independently at codimension-one face interiors. The cellular normalized differential for \(S\) with coefficients in \(g^*\mathfrak o_X\) contains one signed unit for each incident pair \((w,e)\). Two lifts \(e,e'\) of opposite base cofaces adjacent to the same \(w\) contribute opposite signs after transporting the orientation generators to \(g(w)\). Indeed, in a manifold chart through \(g(w)\), the induced boundary orientations from the two sides of its codimension-one face are opposite. Choose the row basis for \(w\) using one side, and the column basis for each \(e\) from the local fundamental orientation of its image. Then the row contains \(+1\) for lifts from \(\sigma_+\), \(-1\) for lifts from \(\sigma_-\), and zero otherwise: **exactly the row of \(A_g\)**, up to a row sign. These choices are legitimate locally even when global choices are obstructed by orientation monodromy. Thus the integral top boundary is a signed-base version of \(A_g\), not merely a matrix of the same rank. Because the support has dimension \(n\), there are no nondegenerate \((n+1)\)-simplices, so
\[
H_n(|S|;g^*\mathfrak o_X)=\ker\partial_n^{g^*\mathfrak o_X}\cong\ker(A_g:\mathbb Z^{S_n^\circ}\to\mathbb Z^{S_{n-1}^\circ}).
\]
Rank commutes with extension to \(\mathbb R\), establishing the claimed carrier formula. If \(X\) is orientable, its orientation local system is trivial and this specializes to Kharoof–Lai's ordinary-homology theorem, Theorem 4.8.

**3. The double-cover interpretation.** Let \(\widehat X\to X\) be the orientation double cover (two copies if \(X\) is orientable), and let \(\widehat S\) be its pullback along \(g\). Over \(\mathbb Q\), the anti-invariant summand of the ordinary chain complex of \(\widehat S\) is canonically the local-coefficient chain complex of \(S\) for \(g^*\mathfrak o_X\otimes\mathbb Q\). The idempotent \((1-\iota)/2\) commutes with the boundary, hence with homology. Taking degree \(n\) proves the eigenspace formula; no integer splitting of eigenspaces is asserted.

**4. Finite coverings.** For a simplicial covering \(E\to X\), each incident \((n-1)\)-face of a lifted top simplex has exactly one opposite lifted top simplex. The marginal equations therefore force equal probabilities along each pair of lifted adjacent facets. Facet adjacency in each connected covering component is connected, so the positive weight is one constant \(a_j\) on all lifts belonging to \(E_j\). There are exactly \(d_j\) lifts of every base top simplex inside \(E_j\), giving the sole normalization \(\sum_jd_ja_j=1\). Conversely these constants define compatible probability distributions on all lower faces; hence the described simplex is the **whole polytope**, not just one carrier face. Each connected \(E_j\) is a closed manifold with orientation system equal to the pullback of \(\mathfrak o_X\), so its twisted top homology is \(\mathbb Z\) by the integral fundamental-class theorem. Taking their disjoint union gives \(\mathbb Z^c\).

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` uses exact rational matrix ranks to check a six-vertex, ten-triangle triangulation of \(\mathbb{RP}^2\). Its \(15\) edges each occur in exactly two facets and every vertex link is a five-cycle. The ordinary top-boundary matrix has rank \(10\), whereas the orientation-twisted top matrix has rank \(9\); the facet dual adjacency carries nontrivial orientation monodromy. For the disjoint union of \(r\) copies mapped identically to the base, \(1\leq r\leq5\), the program checks ordinary top rank \(0\), twisted top rank \(r\), and carrier dimension \(r-1\). Running `python verify.py` prints `VERIFY_OK`. These computations are finite examples; the proof above supplies the all-dimensional statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Kharoof and Lai, *Homology and carrier faces of simplicial distributions* (arXiv:2610.23672v1, first public October 16, 2026), prove the general facet-matrix nullity formula without orientability (Proposition 3.16 and Theorem 1.2) and the **ordinary** top-homology formula only when the base is orientable (Theorem 4.8). The present finding gives a local-coefficient interpretation valid on nonorientable closed manifolds, an integral kernel identification, the orientation-cover eigenspace formulation, and an explicit full-polytope description for finite covering scenarios. The numerical carrier dimensions for these scenarios are also computable from their general matrix; no priority is claimed for the matrix formula or the orientable case. Kharoof and Okay (arXiv:2607.02754) study sufficient connectivity criteria for extremality but do not in their abstract present the all-component simplex-with-degrees classification; that plausible source presents a residual priority risk. Classical orientation local systems and the orientation-cover description are longstanding; the mathematical contribution here is their specific compatibility with the carrier matrix and consequences, not the local-system machinery itself.

## Limitations
Closed manifold triangulation is essential to define the stated orientation local system globally; no extension to arbitrary facet-paired singular simplicial sets is asserted. The event map must have finite fibers and the distribution's support must meet the hypotheses of the source's facet-matrix theorem. An integral orientation-cover ± eigenspace splitting is **not** claimed (the rational splitting is all that is used). This same-model derivation has not undergone independent audit. Bibliographic novelty remains subject to comparison with potentially unindexed results and complete treatment of other closely related contextuality literature.
