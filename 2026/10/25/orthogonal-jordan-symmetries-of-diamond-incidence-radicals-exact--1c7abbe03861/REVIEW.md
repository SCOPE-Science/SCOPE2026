# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Multiplication in the diamond incidence radical is a one-dimensional bilinear-product algebra: \(x_i y_i=z\) and all other ordered basis products vanish. Square-preservation reduces exactly to similitudes of the split quadratic form \(q(x,y)=x^{\mathsf T}y\), while ordered-product preservation reduces to block-diagonal similitudes of the nonsymmetric bilinear form. The quadratic Jordan-derivation identity gives alternating off-diagonal blocks, with zero diagonals imposed even in characteristic two. The written hyperbolic-basis recurrence proves every finite-field group order without invoking a characteristic-restricted orthogonal-group formula. Separate modular linear-algebra and small orthogonal-group enumerations agree with all stated predictions; no finite test is used as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS with bounded literature risk.** Compared exact object, definition of Jordan maps, dimensions and implications against Cheng–Jing (arXiv:0706.1942v1), Kuzucuoğlu–Levchuk (DOI:10.1142/S0219498810004282), Kuzucuoğlu (DOI:10.1142/S1005386711000393), and Wang–Li (DOI:10.1017/S0017089510000406), as well as published research-record searches. The first concerns triangular algebras under torsion restrictions; the others concern full niltriangular *chains* or unital triangular matrix algebras. This radical has branching incomparable middle elements and is not one of those named algebras when \(r\ge2\). An indirect implication from the complete 2010/2011 articles remains a risk because only their opening material and abstracts, respectively, were accessible. Classical orthogonal-group counts are expressly background, not claimed as original.

**Value — PASS.** The family is the radical of a canonical small-width incidence algebra, so its square-preserving symmetries and ordinary symmetries are intrinsic. The full classification relates a natural algebra-preserving subgroup to the classical split-orthogonal group and provides an exact index over all finite fields. At the infinitesimal level the deviation is quadratic in the number of middle branches, despite both theories having the same shear directions. This simultaneously explains discrete and infinitesimal ways Jordan symmetry can exceed genuine algebra symmetry, rather than merely tabulating isolated cases.

Limitations: the complete 2010/2011 niltriangular literature was not inspected; more general algebra families are not covered; absolute publication priority and independent mathematical validation remain open.
