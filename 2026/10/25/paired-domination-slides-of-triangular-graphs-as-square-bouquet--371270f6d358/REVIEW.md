# Scientific review: paired-domination slides of triangular graphs

## Correctness — PASS
The domination translation is exact: an omitted edge of the complete graph can evade every selected edge exactly when its two endpoints are both unused. The perfect matching inside the selected triangular-graph vertices pairs the selected edges into incident pairs; each pair covers exactly three original labels. At \(\(n=3k+1\)\), the resulting coverage lower bound is saturated only by \(k\) disjoint two-edge stars with a single unused vertex. This proves the optimizer characterization and enumeration, not just an experimental bound. The complete classification of feasible slides then yields the square-bouquet token-swap quotient, connectivity, vertex-transitivity, degree, edge count, and triangle count for all \(k\ge1\). Direct exhaustive enumeration of all candidate optimal-size subsets at \(\(n\in\{4,7\}\)\) agrees with the structural characterization. Separately implemented Python and C++ programs enumerate all states and edges for \(\(n\in\{4,7,10\}\)\) and reproduce distance profiles and diameters \(3,6,10\). Computation is only used for these three finite diameters.

## Originality — PASS with specific residual risk
The claim concerns minimum paired domination in the triangular graphs and the exact *token-sliding* graph on their optima, not paired domination of complete multipartite or chain graphs, general existence of paired-dominating sets, or token swapping on arbitrary connected graphs. Haynes–Slater (1998) introduced paired domination; Studer–Haynes–Lawson (2000) studied induced-paired domination, including trees and cycles; Suzuki–Mouawad–Nishimura (2014) treated the different add/delete reconfiguration model. Mathematical-record searches for triangular-graph, line-graph, three-star-packing, token-swap-quotient, and paired-domination aliases recovered no statement with the same saturated star-forest classification or slide-distance profile. The closest published mathematical records for chain and complete multipartite graphs do not apply to \(T_n\) when \(n\ge7\). The \(\(n=4\)\) case is in the complete-multipartite class and is included as the smallest boundary case, not as standalone novelty. The complete Haynes–Slater paper was not accessible and only selected pages of the scan of Studer et al. were visually examined; an unpublished or unindexed result could subsume the new claim.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The tight equality regime \(n\equiv1\pmod3\) identifies all minimum solutions to a classical guard-pairing problem as a natural combinatorial packing, not an arbitrary induced subproblem. Fixed-optimum token sliding is not decided by the optimization number. The nontrivial square-bouquet quotient gives an all-order reachability theorem, a regular vertex-transitive graph model, its exact state/edge/triangle counts, and reproducible geodesic benchmarks through \(75{,}600\) configurations. These are useful structural facts for local search and constrained reconfiguration; no downstream algorithmic gain or beyond-range diameter conjecture is presented as a theorem.
