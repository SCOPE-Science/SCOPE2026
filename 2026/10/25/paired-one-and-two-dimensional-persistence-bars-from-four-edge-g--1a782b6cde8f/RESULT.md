# Paired one- and two-dimensional persistence bars from four-edge graph ears

## Finding
For every finite connected simple graph \(H\), choose \(m\geq1\) ordered pairs of distinct old vertices \((x_i,y_i)\) with \(d_H(x_i,y_i)=2\); repeated pairs and overlapping endpoints are allowed. Attach internally vertex-disjoint four-edge paths \(x_i-b_i-c_i-d_i-y_i\), with the three vertices on each path new, to obtain \(G\). Let \(K_r(F)\) denote the flag complex on the vertices of \(F\), with an edge between distinct vertices at shortest-path distance at most \(r\). At every integer scale:

\[
K_1(G)\simeq K_1(H)\vee\bigvee^m S^1,\qquad
K_2(G)\simeq K_2(H)\vee\bigvee^m S^2,\qquad
K_r(G)\searrow K_r(H)\quad (r\geq3).
\]
The wedge expressions are pointed at vertices of the connected base. For every coefficient field, the full persistent homology barcode of \(G\) is the barcode of \(H\), plus \(3m\) degree-zero intervals \([0,1)\), \(m\) degree-one intervals \([1,2)\), and \(m\) degree-two intervals \([2,3)\). In particular these bars are not merely isolated Betti-number changes.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, connected, undirected, simple and unweighted. For \(r=0\) the complex is discrete; for noninteger real \(r\geq0\), the complex is constant between successive integers. The \(i\)-th ear has private new vertices \(b_i,c_i,d_i\). For each ear fix an *old* common neighbor \(a_i\in V(H)\) of its endpoints, possible because their distance is exactly two. The choices need not be distinct. No restrictions on triangles, four-cycles, degree, endpoints shared by different ears, or other features of \(H\) are imposed. The claim is about vertex-sampled graph metrics, not the Vietoris--Rips complex of the continuum metric graph.

## Proof
**Isometry and a persistent retraction.** Each ear is a detour of length four between endpoints already joined in \(H\) by a two-edge path; replacing every ear segment of a walk by that shorter old path proves \(d_G|_{V(H)}=d_H\). Define a graph map \(p:G\to H\) to be the identity on old vertices and to send each of \(b_i,c_i,d_i\) to \(a_i\). Every edge maps to an edge or collapses to a vertex. Therefore \(p\) is nonexpanding for shortest-path distances, induces simplicial maps \(p_r:K_r(G)\to K_r(H)\) at all scales, and restricts to the identity on \(K_r(H)\). Consequently the old persistent homology is a natural direct summand of the new one.

**Scale one.** The flag complex of the original simple graph is a graph apart from any filled triangles already contained in \(H\); the four-edge ear adds only four edges and three private vertices and never creates a graph triangle. Each ear adds one free relative one-cycle to the connected base. Contract a spanning tree of each new ear relative to its endpoints to obtain the asserted wedge of \(m\) circles with \(K_1(H)\).

**Scale two and the octahedral generator.** The vertices within distance at most two of the private center \(c_i\) are exactly \(b_i,d_i,x_i,y_i\), independent of all other ears. Their induced graph in the square of \(G\) is the chordless quadrilateral with cyclic order \(b_i,x_i,y_i,d_i\): its two diagonal distances are three. Hence the simplicial link of \(c_i\) in \(K_2(G)\) is the four-cycle \(C_4\). The *same* \(C_4\) bounds a simplicial cone with apex \(a_i\), because \(a_i\) lies within distance two of each of its vertices. Since no two different centers are adjacent at scale two, delete their cones one at a time: each elementary cone attachment along a nullhomotopic embedded circle adds one wedge summand \(S^2\).

In the complex with all centers deleted, each \(b_i\) and \(d_i\) is simplicially dominated by \(a_i\). Indeed, a scale-two neighbor of \(b_i\) outside its ear must be reached via \(x_i\) in at most one additional edge, whereas \(a_i\) reaches \(x_i\) in one edge; the only opposite-ear neighbor is \(d_i\), to which \(a_i\) has distance two. The case \(d_i\) is symmetric. Simplicial domination removes \(b_i,d_i\) by elementary collapses, yielding precisely \(K_2(H)\). The resulting explicit two-sphere has six vertices \(\{x_i,b_i,c_i,d_i,y_i,a_i\}\) and its flag subcomplex is an octahedral boundary: the only missing edges are \(x_i d_i\), \(b_i y_i\), and \(a_i c_i\). Since the retraction \(p_2\) maps this sphere to the triangle spanned by \(x_i,a_i,y_i\), the new summands lie in the kernel of \((p_2)_*\).

**Every scale at least three.** Fix any \(r\geq3\). First remove all centers \(c_i\). To see that each \(c_i\) is dominated by \(a_i\) in the graph power, note that any shortest path from \(c_i\) to a vertex outside its private ear leaves through \(x_i\) or \(y_i\) after two steps, while \(a_i\) can reach the same endpoint in one. Every other vertex in the private ear is at distance at most three from \(a_i\). Thus its closed \(r\)-neighborhood is contained in that of \(a_i\); deleting any other centers does not change this. After all centers are gone, each \(b_i\) and \(d_i\) is similarly dominated by \(a_i\): outside the ear every path exits through an endpoint no farther from \(a_i\), and the other private endpoint is at distance two. Deleting all private vertices gives the full old subcomplex \(K_r(H)\), because old-vertex distances are unchanged. The standard dominated-vertex lemma for flag complexes gives an actual finite sequence of elementary simplicial collapses, for every \(r\geq3\).

**Persistence maps.** At scale zero the new vertices contribute exactly \(3m\) extra components, merged at scale one. At scale one the \(m\) new circle classes are killed in scale two: for the \(i\)-th ear, the cycle following the ear and returning along \(x_i-a_i-y_i\) is filled by the scale-two triangles \((x_i,b_i,c_i)\), \((x_i,c_i,y_i)\), \((c_i,d_i,y_i)\), and \((x_i,a_i,y_i)\), with appropriate orientations. At scale two the \(m\) octahedral classes die by scale three, since all six of their vertices have pairwise distance at most three, giving a containing five-simplex. The natural retraction \(p_r\) splits off the full persistence module of \(H\); the previous wedge and collapse calculations show its complementary module is zero outside scales zero, one and two, in dimensions zero, one and two respectively. Consequently its interval decomposition consists of exactly the stated ephemeral intervals over any field.

## Verification
The bundled `verify.py` constructs example host graphs (including a tree, a cycle, a chorded cycle, repeated ears, and overlapping-ear endpoints), calculates all-pairs graph distances, checks isometric inclusion, octahedral links, vertex domination, and the full mod-two Betti vectors at integer scales one, two and three. All five examples pass. These calculations corroborate the structural proof but are not used to extrapolate any infinite-graph conclusion.

## Relationship to prior work
Adams and Coskunuzer (2021, arXiv:2103.06408, Section 2.2 and Theorem 3.3) develop persistent homology of graph-power clique filtrations and characterize degree-one cycle lifetimes, but do not state an arbitrary-host local ear operation producing a prescribed new two-dimensional persistence bar, a graph-metric retraction, and a scale-three simplicial collapse. Adamaszek et al. (2017 preprint; SoCG 2018) give Vietoris--Rips gluing theorems for wedge sums and admissible isometric metric intersections, especially sufficiently short common paths, rather than the present four-edge detours between old vertices already at distance two. Their general gluing approach is closely relevant but the directly inspected formulations do not give the present simultaneous two-dimensional relative-persistence calculation. The result remains subject to the possibility of equivalent descriptions in unindexed literature.

## Limitations
The theorem is limited to internally disjoint paths of precisely four edges attached between old vertices at distance exactly two. It does not classify arbitrary path attachments or Vietoris--Rips complexes of continuum metric graphs. A same-method mathematical review is not independent validation; equivalence with unindexed or more general existing gluing theorems remains a residual originality risk.
