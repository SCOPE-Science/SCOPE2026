# Paley-17 flag complex: sixteen 2-spheres and its affine homology representation

## Finding

Let \(P_{17}\) be the Paley graph on \(\mathbb Z/17\mathbb Z\): distinct vertices \(u,v\) are joined precisely when \(v-u\) is a nonzero quadratic residue modulo \(17\). Let \(X=\operatorname{Cl}(P_{17})\) be the flag/clique complex. The following homotopy type and rational symmetry representation hold:

\[
 X\simeq\bigvee^{16}S^2,
 \qquad \widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{16},
\]

and every other reduced integral homology group is zero. Let
\(A=\{u\mapsto au+b:a\in(\mathbb F_{17}^{\times})^2,\ b\in\mathbb F_{17}\}\), an order-\(136\) affine graph-automorphism group. As rational \(A\)-modules,

\[
 \widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Q)\cong
 \ker\left(\mathbb Q^{17}\xrightarrow{\ \sum\ }\mathbb Q\right),
\]

where \(A\) permutes the standard basis of \(\mathbb Q^{17}\) according to its action on the graph vertices. Thus, writing an affine element as \(g(u)=au+b\), its homological character is \(16\) for the identity, \(-1\) for any nonidentity translation \((a=1,b\ne0)\), and \(0\) when \(a\ne1\).

Since this graph has diameter two, its inclusive vertex-metric Vietoris–Rips filtration consists of seventeen isolated points at \(0\le r<1\), the displayed sixteen-sphere wedge at \(1\le r<2\), and a simplex at \(r\ge2\). Over any coefficient field, its reduced degree-two barcode consists of sixteen intervals \([1,2)\), with sixteen reduced degree-zero intervals \([0,1)\), and no others.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph is the explicitly defined undirected quadratic-residue graph with vertex set \(\{0,1,\ldots,16\}\). Its set of nonzero square differences is \(R=\{1,2,4,8,9,13,15,16\}\). The simplicial complex includes exactly the cliques of this graph and uses the usual geometric realization. The homology representation is asserted over \(\mathbb Q\) for the specified natural affine subgroup \(A\), not as an integral equivariant module identification. The Rips filtration uses the **shortest-path metric** on graph vertices and inclusive threshold \(r\ge0\).

## Proof

**Combinatorial dimensions.** There are \(17\) vertices and \(17\cdot8/2=68\) edges. Direct pairwise adjacency verification gives exactly \(68\) triangles and no four-vertex clique, so \(X\) is two-dimensional, with face counts \((17,68,68)\). The adjacency data and complete clique test are generated from the residue set \(R\) in the attached verifier. The graph is connected, and every nonadjacent pair has a common neighbor, giving diameter two.

**Fundamental group.** Choose the following \(16\)-edge spanning tree, writing \(ab\) for the unordered edge between vertices \(a\) and \(b\):

`(0,1), (0,2), (0,4), (0,8), (0,9), (0,13), (0,15), (0,16), (1,3), (1,5), (1,10), (1,14), (2,6), (2,11), (3,7), (3,12)`.

Collapsing the tree identifies the fundamental group with the group generated by the remaining \(52\) oriented graph edges, with one triangular boundary relator for each of the \(68\) triangles. Label non-tree edges lexicographically by \(1,\ldots,52\). For \(a<b<c\), the relator is the freely reduced word obtained by traversing \(a\to b\to c\to a\), deleting tree-edge letters and using inverse letters on oppositely oriented edges.

The attached deterministic exact verifier exhibits a sequence of **52** legitimate Tietze eliminations. At each step an existing relator contains exactly one occurrence of a generator or its inverse; the generator is solved for as a word in the other generators and eliminated from every other relator. The certified eliminated-generator order is:

`1, 2, 3, 7, 6, 11, 5, 4, 10, 9, 8, 14, 13, 12, 16, 15, 32, 36, 39, 42, 45, 35, 31, 52, 51, 47, 34, 27, 23, 33, 24, 40, 41, 46, 38, 19, 20, 21, 48, 49, 43, 44, 37, 29, 28, 18, 17, 22, 30, 26, 25, 50`.

The verifier recomputes every substitution, checks that each eliminated generator occurs exactly once in its defining relator, and verifies the remaining \(16\) relators are empty after all \(52\) generators are removed. Hence \(\pi_1(X)\) is trivial. This is a finite presentation proof, not an inference from a homology rank or an experimental failure to find loops.

**Integral homotopy type.** Connectedness and simple connectivity give \(H_1(X;\mathbb Z)=0\). The Euler characteristic is \(17-68+68=17\). As \(X\) is two-dimensional, \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\) is a subgroup of a free integral chain group, hence free; the Euler characteristic fixes its rank to be \(16\). By Hurewicz, this \(H_2\) basis can be represented by spheres. The resulting map \(\bigvee^{16}S^2\to X\) induces an integral homology isomorphism, and the simply-connected version of Whitehead's theorem makes it a homotopy equivalence.

**Complete affine character.** Every map \(u\mapsto au+b\) for square \(a\) preserves square differences and acts simplicially on \(X\). The reduced Lefschetz trace identity, using the proven homology concentration, is

\[
 \operatorname{tr}(g\mid\widetilde H_2)=
 \operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_2)-\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_1)
 +\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_0)-1.
\]

The identity has trace \(68-68+17-1=16\). A nontrivial translation has order \(17\), so fixes no nonempty simplex of size at most three, giving trace \(-1\). If the multiplier \(a\) has order four or eight, it has exactly one fixed vertex and no invariant edge or triangle, giving trace zero. The sole nonidentity multiplier of order two is \(a=-1\): this affine involution has one fixed vertex and four invariant edges paired by transposition, together with four invariant triangles paired by transposition of the two nonfixed vertices. Their signed chain traces are both \(-4\), so the reduced homology trace again is zero. The verifier reproduces the signed traces for all \(136\) affine maps using orientations of individual simplices. The vertex permutation module minus its invariant constant line has precisely these traces; character equality for the finite group \(A\) implies the displayed rational isomorphism.

**Filtration.** Since the graph has diameter two, at threshold one the inclusive graph-metric Rips complex equals \(X\), and at threshold two it equals the full \(16\)-simplex. Thus there are no nontrivial persistence maps between two distinct noncontractible positive-dimensional stages. The stated integral and field-valued barcode follows.

## Relationship to prior work

The ordinary graph and clique-number framework is classical. Goyal–Shukla–Singh (2019) study homotopy types of independence complexes for different graph families, establishing relevant methodology but no result for this quadratic-residue graph. Gallo–Videla (2023) study clique counts of Paley-type graphs, which can imply face-count data but do not determine the fundamental group or symmetry module.

Cioabă–Guo–Ji–Mim (2025), Section 5 (including the discussion after Theorem 5.21 on page 31 of the PDF), **explicitly include Paley(17)** among graphs whose first real clique-cohomology vanishes. That prior result already determines the first real Betti number and, with the known face counts, the second rational Betti number. **Those dimensions are not claimed as independently new.** Vanishing abelianized first homology does not establish triviality of \(\pi_1\), and it does not specify the action of the affine group on second homology. Cioabă–Mim (2026) treat broader first-homology vanishing criteria, not this homotopy type or representation.

## Limitations

This theorem is for a single canonical graph and the stated natural affine group. It proves the homotopy type nonequivariantly and identifies only the **rational** group representation, not an integral module structure or an equivariant homotopy equivalence. It does not claim a comparable formula for general Paley graphs, strong regularity classes, or neighborhood complexes. While specific primary sources and indexed related results were compared by object and implication, an unindexed prior homotopy computation or implicit equivalent representation formula remains a priority risk.
