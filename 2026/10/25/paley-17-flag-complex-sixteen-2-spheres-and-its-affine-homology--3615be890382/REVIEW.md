# Review: Paley-17 flag-complex homotopy and affine homology

## Correctness

PASS. The flag complex is explicitly defined by eight quadratic residues and has face vector \((17,68,68)\) and no higher faces. Its spanning-tree presentation has fifty-two edge generators and sixty-eight triangular relators. The included verifier replays fifty-two valid one-occurrence Tietze eliminations and ends with sixteen empty relators, proving simple connectivity, not merely vanishing field homology. The integral wedge type follows from Euler characteristic, Hurewicz and Whitehead. The signed chain traces were checked for all 136 affine symmetries and agree with the permutation augmentation character. The graph diameter is checked directly.

## Originality

PASS with residual priority risk. Gallo–Videla supply generalized Paley clique-count formulas, which can account for the f-vector. Cioabă–Guo–Ji–Mim explicitly obtain vanishing first real cohomology for Paley(17); together with Euler characteristic this covers ordinary rational homology ranks. Neither inspected source determines the fundamental group or the complete affine action on the second homology. Goyal–Shukla–Singh treat other independence-complex graph families, and Cioabă–Mim concentrate on first homology of strongly regular graphs. A closely named indexed finding on Paley(17) concerns its *open-neighborhood* complex, not its flag complex; another concerns Paley(29), not Paley(17). Equivalent unindexed work is not excluded.

## Value

PASS. Paley(17) is the canonical seventeen-vertex self-complementary Ramsey graph. A complete integral homotopy classification and natural 136-symmetry homology character are mathematically meaningful beyond the already-covered Betti numbers. The explicit perfect collapse of the nonabelian presentation and full representation together distinguish a true wedge-of-spheres homotopy statement from a field-rank computation. No extrapolation to all Paley orders is asserted.

## Limitations

Only one graph is classified; the group representation is rational; the provided homotopy equivalence is nonequivariant. The computation does not establish a uniform family classification, nor rule out future identification of equivalent prior art. The 2025 paper's first real cohomology result is explicitly credited.
