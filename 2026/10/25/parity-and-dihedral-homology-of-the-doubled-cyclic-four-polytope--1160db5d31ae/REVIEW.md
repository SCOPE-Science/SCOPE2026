# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The four-dimensional Gale-evenness characterization supplies exactly the facets that are unions of two disjoint polygon edges. Their three complementary-pair partitions account for every edge of the double without duplication. Explicit two-step arc reductions prove the precise number of connected components; spanning-tree contraction proves the integral homotopy formulas. The equivariant graph chain exact sequence and invariant-facet counts give each dihedral character. The included standalone finite checker independently builds Gale-even facets and reproduces all component and signed-character formulas on twenty consecutive sizes (580 dihedral elements). Its tests are corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS on checked evidence, with residual uncertainty. The 2025 primary doubling paper introduces the construction and proves a connectivity bound for broad classes, but does not analyze cyclic polytopes; the full HTML Sections 1–4 were examined for such coverage. Gale-evenness establishes the already-known facet structure, not the double's parity-dependent connected components or its dihedral homology character. Actual searches for doubling cyclic polytopes, parity components, and the rank formulas found no exact overlap in surveyed mathematical sources. Cycle-matching-complex character results concern a different simplicial complex: the matching complex of a cycle, not the double of the boundary of a cyclic four-polytope. Equivalent unindexed work could still exist.

## Value
PASS. Cyclic four-polytopes form a canonical infinite family of maximally neighborly simplicial three-spheres. The result reveals a sharp parity-dependent loss of connectedness under a recently introduced topological operation; the full dihedral character then detects symmetry distinctions invisible to the Betti ranks. This gives a natural complete structural classification, not a selected numerical example.

## Limitations and closest literature
Lesh–Schreiner–Wahl, arXiv:2506.10436v1, provides the definition and a connectivity bound. Gale-evenness determines the facets. The theorem is restricted to the first double in dimension four and the natural dihedral subgroup; equivalent prior work is not conclusively ruled out.
