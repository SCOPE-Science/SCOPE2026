# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The degree-two subdivision condition forces different original endpoint colors. With three colors the intervening vertex is forced, and the two original parts must split into disjoint odd-sized color classes; this proves the complete parity classification and the three-color census. For the even-even case, the explicitly given two-color checkerboard on subdivision vertices has odd counts in every original neighborhood. For the exact four-color census of the two-by-even family, the six page triples and the nine absent-color choices are enumerated by a four-term character filter; the seven-column signed-multiplicity table and the final closed form have been independently recomputed. The independent dynamic program tracks support and parity separately, preventing the potentially fatal confusion of absent colors with colors occurring positively an even number of times. The two verifiers passed. The exhaustive tests are corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This provides the sharp chromatic phase transition for a natural two-parameter family of graph-incidence subdivisions and counts all minimal-color solutions whenever three colors suffice. The four-color closed census solves the natural two-terminal length-four multipath family in its nontrivial even-degree regime; the positive-even-versus-zero parity distinction is structurally necessary and its exponential enumeration is not a direct coloring-polynomial substitution. A general even-even census is deliberately left open.
