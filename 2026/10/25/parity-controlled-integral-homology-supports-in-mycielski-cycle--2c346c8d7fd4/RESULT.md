# Parity-controlled integral homology supports in Mycielski cycle neighborhood complexes

## Finding

Let \(G_n=\mu(C_n)\) be the ordinary Mycielskian of the cycle on \(n\ge5\) vertices and let \(K_n=\mathcal N(G_n)\) be the open Lovász neighborhood complex. The vertex set is \(\{w\}\cup\{x_i,y_i:i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\}\), where \(w\) is the apex, \(x_i\) the original cycle vertices, and \(y_i\) their shadows. Homology has integral coefficients throughout this theorem.

**Odd cycle:** If \(n\) is odd, then \(K_n\simeq S^2\), and every proper induced subcomplex \(K_n[W]\subsetneq K_n\) maps trivially to the ambient second homology. Thus its nonzero degree-two class can only be represented on the full \(2n+1\) vertices; deletion of any single vertex leaves a contractible complex.

**Even cycle:** If \(n\) is even, then \(K_n\simeq S^1\vee S^2\vee S^2\). For each \(\epsilon\in\{0,1\}\), put
\[
P_\epsilon=\{w\}\cup\{x_i,y_i:i\equiv\epsilon\pmod2\},\qquad |P_\epsilon|=n+1.
\]
There is an integral basis \(e_0,e_1\) of \(H_2(K_n;\mathbb Z)\) with each \(e_\epsilon\) generated by a sphere in \(K_n[P_\epsilon]\). For **every** vertex subset \(W\), the inclusion-induced image is exactly
\[
\operatorname{im}\big(H_2(K_n[W];\mathbb Z)\to H_2(K_n;\mathbb Z)\big)
=\bigoplus_{\epsilon:P_\epsilon\subseteq W}\mathbb Z e_\epsilon.
\]
Consequently there are exactly two inclusion-minimal supports of nonzero ambient second homology, of size \(n+1\). Every class with both basis coordinates nonzero requires all \(2n+1\) vertices. Removing the apex makes the complex contractible; removing any one other vertex gives a complex homotopy equivalent to \(S^1\vee S^2\).

## Assumptions and scope

Graphs are finite, undirected, simple. Subscripts are modulo \(n\). The graph \(G_n\) has edges \(x_ix_{i+1}\), \(x_iy_{i-1}\), \(x_iy_{i+1}\), and \(wy_i\), for all indices. A face of the **open** neighborhood complex is a nonempty vertex subset whose members all have a common neighbor in \(G_n\). For \(W\subseteq V(G_n)\), \(K_n[W]\) is the simplicial induced subcomplex of \(K_n\), *not* generally \(\mathcal N(G_n[W])\). All conclusions hold for every integer \(n\ge5\); no asymptotic qualifications or characteristic restrictions are used.

## Proof

**1. An explicit decomposition and the ambient integral groups.** Write \(Y=\{y_i:i\in\mathbb Z/n\}\), and let \(A\) be the full simplex on \(Y\). The maximal common-neighbor simplices of \(K_n\) are precisely
\[
A,\quad B_i=\{w,x_{i-1},x_{i+1}\},\quad
C_i=\{x_{i-1},x_{i+1},y_{i-1},y_{i+1}\}.
\]
Indeed they are the neighborhoods, respectively, of the apex, a shadow \(y_i\), and an original vertex \(x_i\). Let \(L\) be the one-dimensional simplicial graph on the \(x_i\) with edges \(x_{i-1}x_{i+1}\). It is the link of \(w\) in \(K_n\). For \(n=6\), each of its triangles remains a one-dimensional cycle: the definition of the link has no filled triangle. If \(n\) is odd, \(L\) is one \(n\)-cycle; if \(n\) is even, it is two disjoint \(n/2\)-cycles.

Let \(D=K_n[V(G_n)\setminus\{w\}]\). Every original vertex \(x_i\) is dominated by the shadow \(y_i\) in \(D\): every maximal simplex containing \(x_i\) also contains \(y_i\). Therefore successively deleting all original vertices strongly collapses \(D\) onto the simplex \(A\); in particular \(D\) is contractible. Since \(K_n=D\cup(w*L)\) and \(D\cap(w*L)=L\), the natural Mayer–Vietoris boundary is an **integral isomorphism**
\[
\partial_{\mathrm{MV}}:H_2(K_n;\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{\cong}H_1(L;\mathbb Z).
\]
The union is homotopy equivalent to the (unreduced) suspension of \(L\). This gives \(S^2\) for odd \(n\), and \(S^1\vee S^2\vee S^2\) for even \(n\). In the latter case the two circle components of \(L\) give the preferred integral basis \(e_0,e_1\) via \(\partial_{\mathrm{MV}}\).

**2. Original-vertex deletions.** Fix \(j\). In \(D-x_j\), the remaining original vertices remain dominated by their own shadows, so this subcomplex collapses onto \(A\). The apex link of \(K_n-x_j\) is \(L-x_j\). Thus \(K_n-x_j\simeq\Sigma(L-x_j)\). For odd \(n\), \(L-x_j\) is a path and the deletion is contractible. For even \(n\), \(L-x_j\) is a path disjoint from a circle, giving \(S^1\vee S^2\). Its inclusion in \(K_n\) has second-homology image the direct summand corresponding to the parity component **not** containing \(x_j\). This follows from naturality of the preceding Mayer–Vietoris isomorphism and the inclusion \(H_1(L-x_j;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(L;\mathbb Z)\).

**3. Shadow-vertex deletions.** Fix \(j\). Put \(D_j=D-y_j\). Strongly delete all original vertices except \(x_j\), each dominated by its surviving shadow. The resulting one-dimensional homotopy spine is the union of the simplex on \(Y\setminus\{y_j\}\) and the two edges from \(x_j\) to \(y_{j-2}\) and \(y_{j+2}\). Their endpoints are distinct for \(n\ge5\). This complex deformation-retracts onto a circle, denoted \(Q_j\), formed by the two spokes and a path between their endpoints within the shadow simplex. In particular \(D_j\simeq S^1\).

The inclusion \(L\to D_j\), followed by that retraction, has degree \(\pm1\) on the circle component of \(L\) containing \(x_j\): the component traverses the two spokes exactly once, and all its remaining edges map into the simply connected shadow simplex. If \(n\) is even, the other component of \(L\) maps entirely into that simplex and has degree zero. Consequently the homomorphism
\[
H_1(L;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow H_1(D_j;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z
\]
is a unit projection onto the \(j\)-parity coordinate (for odd \(n\), it is multiplication by \(\pm1\)). Since \(K_n-y_j=D_j\cup(w*L)\), the integral Mayer–Vietoris sequence identifies \(H_2(K_n-y_j;\mathbb Z)\) with the kernel of that projection, and the inclusion-induced image in \(H_2(K_n;\mathbb Z)\) is this **same** kernel. For odd \(n\) the kernel is zero and the mapping cone of a degree-one circle map is contractible. For even \(n\) the kernel is the opposite parity \(\mathbb Z\) summand, and an elementary CW reduction of the mapping cone gives \(S^1\vee S^2\): the degree-one circle attaches a two-cell that cancels its generator, while the null-attached circle contributes a two-sphere and the disconnected domain contributes a connecting one-cell. This also proves the stated deletion homotopy types without reliance on numerical homology alone.

**4. Smallest carriers and every induced-support image.** For even \(n\), the induced complex \(K_n[P_\epsilon]\) decomposes as its contractible apex-deleted part (all its original vertices are dominated by their corresponding shadows) together with a cone on the full cycle \(L_\epsilon\). Therefore \(K_n[P_\epsilon]\simeq S^2\) and its fundamental class maps under \(\partial_{\mathrm{MV}}\) to \(\pm[L_\epsilon]\), a primitive basis element. Conversely, if \(W\) omits the apex, its induced complex lies in contractible \(D\), so its ambient degree-two image vanishes. If it omits an original or shadow vertex of parity \(\epsilon\), factor its inclusion through the corresponding vertex deletion. Parts 2–3 show that the resulting image has zero \(e_\epsilon\) coordinate. Consequently any element in the image from \(K_n[W]\) has a nonzero \(e_\epsilon\) coordinate only when \(P_\epsilon\subseteq W\). If \(P_\epsilon\subseteq W\), the primitive class \(e_\epsilon\) is in the image by the preceding paragraph. This proves the exact equality of subgroups for every \(W\). When \(n\) is odd, all single-vertex deletions are contractible by parts 2–3; every proper induced complex is contained in one of them, so its ambient second-homology image is zero. The full complex realizes its nonzero class, proving full support necessity.

## Verification

The accompanying standard-library program `verify_myc_support.py` independently constructs the graph from its edges, enumerates common-neighbor simplices, computes simplicial boundary ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\), checks full and single-deletion Betti numbers for \(5\le n\le11\), and exhaustively tests inclusion images for every vertex subset at \(n=5\) and \(n=6\). These finite tests confirm small cases; the uniform **integral** result rests on the deformation retractions, explicit degree computation, and natural Mayer–Vietoris maps in the proof, not on extrapolation from finite tests. Reproduce with `python3 verify_myc_support.py`.

## Relationship to prior work

Csorba established the general suspension behavior of Mycielski constructions for homomorphism/box complexes. The ambient homotopy types above are consistent with, and should not be presented as novel beyond, that general suspension phenomenon. The extra statements proved here concern **induced vertex-deletion homotopy types, exact images of integral second homology from every induced subcomplex, and uniquely characterized parity-minimal carriers**. An ambient suspension theorem alone does not determine these fixed-vertex support maps or the preferred primitive generators. The proof makes those comparisons explicit rather than relying on absence of a matching keyword.

## Limitations

The result treats the ordinary Mycielski construction on cycles \(C_n\) for \(n\ge5\), not arbitrary starting graphs or iterated constructions. It concerns inclusion-induced images, not minimal simplex counts of chains. No claim of independent validation or uniqueness in all mathematical literature is made. The exact induced-support statement is elementary relative to the decomposition but is not inferred from the ambient homotopy equivalence; further literature may contain equivalent statements under different terminology.
