# Parity-normal forms for mixed products of ordinary numerical semigroups

## Finding

For every integer \(m\ge3\), let
\[
O_m=\{0\}\cup\{n\in\mathbb N:n\ge m\},\qquad
P_m=O_2\odot O_m
\]
where \(\mathbb N=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\), and \(\odot\) denotes multiplication of ideals of the semiring \(\mathbb N\): \(A\odot B\) is the *additive submonoid generated by* the ordinary integer products \(ab\) with \(a\in A\), \(b\in B\). Thus \(P_m\) is an additive numerical semigroup; \(\odot\) is not the elementwise set product.

Write
\[
\varepsilon=\begin{cases}1,&m\text{ even},\\0,&m\text{ odd},\end{cases}
\quad r=m+\varepsilon,\quad u=3r,\quad T=u+2m,
\quad s=\left\lceil\frac m3\right\rceil,
\quad\delta=\begin{cases}1,&m\equiv1\pmod3,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]
Then the complete membership classification is
\[
\boxed{
P_m=\{0\}\cup\{2n:n\ge m\}
\cup\{u+6j:0\le j<s\}
\cup\{n:n\ge T,\ n\text{ odd}\}.}
\]
The three positive parts in this union are pairwise disjoint. The formula describes all elements, not just generators.

The unique minimal additive generating set is
\[
\boxed{
\{2m+2j:0\le j<m\}\ \cup\
\{u+6j:0\le j<s\},
}
\qquad
\boxed{\operatorname{edim}(P_m)=m+s.}
\]
The Frobenius number and genus are
\[
\boxed{F(P_m)=T-2-2\delta,}\qquad
\boxed{g(P_m)=m-1+\frac{T-1}{2}-s.}
\]
The complete pseudo-Frobenius set is
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{PF}(P_m)=
\{6j:1\le j,\ 6j<2m\}
\cup\{u+2j:0\le j<m,\ j\not\equiv0\pmod3\}
\cup\begin{cases}\{u-2\},&\delta=1,\\\varnothing,&\delta=0,\end{cases}
}
\]
so the Cohen–Macaulay type of the numerical semigroup is
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{t}(P_m)
=m-s+\left\lfloor\frac{2m-1}{6}\right\rfloor+\delta.}
\]
Here \(\operatorname{PF}(S)\) means the gaps \(x\) for which \(x+y\in S\) for every nonzero \(y\in S\). The formulas hold uniformly for all \(m\ge3\). They also happen to hold for \(m=2\), where \(P_2=\langle4,6,9\rangle\) is a known geometric-sequence example; that boundary case is not claimed as a new result.

## Assumptions and scope

All semigroups are additive submonoids of the nonnegative integers with finite complement. In the *multiplicative ideal product* \(A\odot B\), individual products \(ab\) are first formed using ordinary integer multiplication, then arbitrary finite *additive* combinations are allowed. Both factors are the ordinary maximal-embedding-dimension numerical semigroups \(O_2=\langle2,3\rangle\) and \(O_m=\langle m,m+1,\ldots,2m-1\rangle\). The first factor is fixed; the integer \(m\) is arbitrary and at least \(3\). The multiplicity, Frobenius number, genus, embedding dimension and pseudo-Frobenius set always refer to the resulting *additive* numerical semigroup \(P_m\).

## Proof

**1. A sharp parity description of the product.** Because additive ideal multiplication distributes over addition, the finite generating set obtained directly from the generators of the factors is
\[
P_m=\big\langle 2k,3k:m\le k<2m\big\rangle_+.
\]
Every even integer at least \(2m\) belongs to \(P_m\), since it equals \(2b\) for some \(b\ge m\) in \(O_m\). No positive element of \(P_m\) is smaller than \(2m\).

Let \(r\) be the smallest odd integer at least \(m\). The least *odd* ordinary product of positive elements from \(O_2\) and \(O_m\) is \(u=3r\). If an odd element of \(P_m\) is a sum of at least two positive ordinary products, its odd summand has size at least \(u\) and another summand has size at least \(2m\); therefore the total is at least \(T=u+2m\). If an odd integer strictly smaller than \(T\) is just one ordinary product \(ab\), both factors are odd. If \(a\ge5\), then \(ab\ge5r\ge3r+2m=T\), a contradiction. Thus \(a=3\), \(b=r+2j\), and \(ab=u+6j\). The condition \(ab<T\) is \(6j<2m\), equivalent to \(0\le j<s=\lceil m/3\rceil\).

Conversely each such \(u+6j\) is a product of \(3\in O_2\) and \(r+2j\in O_m\). Every odd integer \(z\ge T\) equals \(u+(z-u)\); the difference is even and at least \(2m\), so both summands lie in \(P_m\). This proves the full membership formula. It also proves that the positive elements have gcd \(1\) and that \(P_m\) is cofinite.

**2. Minimal generators.** Each even integer in \([2m,4m)\) is indecomposable, since a sum of two nonzero elements of \(P_m\) is at least \(4m\). These even integers are precisely \(2m+2j\) for \(0\le j<m\). Every even integer at least \(4m\) splits as \(2m+(z-2m)\), with both summands in \(P_m\). Every odd element below \(T\) must be indecomposable by the preceding argument. Every odd element \(z\ge T\) splits as \(u+(z-u)\), again with two positive summands in \(P_m\). This proves precisely the asserted minimal generating set and gives \(\operatorname{edim}(P_m)=m+s\).

**3. Frobenius number and genus.** The gaps of even parity are exactly \(2,4,\ldots,2m-2\), numbering \(m-1\). The odd integers in \([1,T)\) number \((T-1)/2\); among them, precisely the \(s\) numbers \(u,u+6,\ldots,u+6(s-1)\) lie in \(P_m\). There are no gaps \(\ge T\). Thus
\[
g(P_m)=m-1+\frac{T-1}{2}-s.
\]
The largest possible odd gap is \(T-2\). This number is present exactly when \(T-2-u=2m-2\) is divisible by \(6\), equivalently \(m\equiv1\pmod3\). In that case \(T-4\) is a gap, since it lies in another residue class modulo \(6\). The largest even gap \(2m-2\) is smaller than \(T-4\) for \(m\ge3\). It follows that \(F(P_m)=T-2-2\delta\).

**4. The pseudo-Frobenius set.** Since \(P_m\) is generated by the explicit finite set from Part 2, a gap \(x\) is pseudo-Frobenius if and only if adding every member of that set sends \(x\) into \(P_m\).

For an even gap \(x\), necessarily \(0<x<2m\). Adding any even generator yields an even member of \(P_m\). Adding the smallest odd generator \(u\) yields an odd integer in \([u,T)\), which belongs to \(P_m\) exactly if \(x\equiv0\pmod6\). If this condition holds, adding every other odd generator \(u+6j\) gives either an element in the same \(u\bmod6\) progression below \(T\) or a member of the odd tail \([T,\infty)\). Thus the even pseudo-Frobenius numbers are exactly the positive multiples of \(6\) below \(2m\).

For an odd gap \(x\ge u\), adding any even generator yields an odd number at least \(T\), and adding any odd generator yields an even number at least \(2u\ge2m\). Thus every odd gap in \([u,T)\) is pseudo-Frobenius, namely \(u+2j\) for \(0\le j<m\) with \(j\not\equiv0\pmod3\).

For an odd gap \(x\le u-4\), both \(x+2m\) and \(x+2m+2\) are odd integers below \(T\), but they differ by \(2\) and therefore cannot both belong to the single admissible residue class \(u\pmod6\); the gap is not pseudo-Frobenius. The only remaining odd gap below \(u\) is \(u-2\). Adding the smallest even generator produces \(T-2\), which belongs to \(P_m\) precisely if \(\delta=1\); adding each larger even generator then produces an odd integer at least \(T\), and adding an odd generator produces an even integer above \(2m\). Hence \(u-2\) is pseudo-Frobenius exactly when \(m\equiv1\pmod3\). This completes the classification. Counting its three disjoint parts gives the displayed type formula.

## Verification

Run \(`python3 verify.py`\) with Python 3 (no external dependencies). The verifier forms the finite generating set \(\{2k,3k:m\le k<2m\}\), independently computes its additive closure by dynamic programming, and checks membership at every integer through \(T+6m\). It then independently computes gaps, indecomposable elements, and pseudo-Frobenius gaps by the defining operations, and checks the complete theorems for each integer \(2\le m\le160\). The program emits `VERIFY_OK` on success. For example, the \((F,g,\operatorname{edim},\operatorname t)\) tuples for \(m=3,4,5,6,7\) are respectively \((13,8,4,2)\), \((19,12,6,4)\), \((23,14,7,4)\), \((31,19,8,5)\), and \((31,20,10,7)\).

The finite checks do *not* constitute proof for unbounded \(m\): Parts 1–4 are the symbolic proof. All computations and their bounds are explicit; no code dependency, solver certificate, or external dataset is required.

## Relationship to prior work

Chen, Hyde, Laurens, Piermarini and Simons (2026, arXiv:2607.10778) introduce and investigate multiplicative ideal arithmetic in \(\mathbb N\), including irreducibility, multiplicative invariants, and the asymptotic growth of classical invariants of *powers of a fixed semigroup*. Their Section 4 recalls geometric-sequence powers of two-generator semigroups and treats their Frobenius number and genus; their Proposition 4.9 concerns products of two two-generator semigroups with common multiplicity. These statements do not specify the gap set or pseudo-Frobenius numbers for the *mixed* product \(O_2\odot O_m\) with ordinary \(O_m\) and arbitrary \(m\ge3\).

Tripathi (2008, Integers 8, A43) and Ong–Ponomarenko (2008, Integers 8, A33) determine invariants of numerical semigroups generated by the geometric sequences \(a^k,a^{k-1}b,\ldots,b^k\) with \(\gcd(a,b)=1\). The product \(O_2\odot O_2=\langle4,6,9\rangle\) is covered by that theory and is only a boundary consistency check. For \(m\ge3\), the minimal generating set here consists of an even arithmetic interval and a truncated odd arithmetic progression, not a single geometric sequence. The new contribution is the exact parity normal form and full additive-invariant classification of this natural mixed-ideal product. It is not a claim to have introduced ideal multiplication or the general theory of numerical semigroups.

## Limitations

The classification concerns exactly the mixed products of \(O_2\) with ordinary numerical semigroups \(O_m\), \(m\ge3\); it does not determine products of two arbitrary numerical semigroups, powers \(O_m^{\odot k}\), or ordinary elementwise set products. The known \(m=2\) geometric-sequence case is explicitly outside the originality claim. Targeted searches of indexed and accessible published literature did not reveal a covering classification, but this cannot rule out an equivalent calculation in older, unindexed work. Independent mathematical auditing and external certification have not been performed.
