# Partial inner automorphisms of two-dimensional linear endomorphism monoids

## Finding

Let \(k\) be any field, \(V=k^2\), and \(S=\operatorname{End}_k(V)=M_2(k)\) with multiplication as the semigroup operation. For \(g,h\in S\), define
\[
D_{g,h}=\{X\in S:(gh)X=X=X(gh)\},\qquad
\phi_{g,h}:D_{g,h}\longrightarrow D_{h,g},\quad X\longmapsto hXg.
\]
Let \(\operatorname{Inn}(S)\) denote the inverse monoid of partial bijections generated by the maps \(\phi_{g,h}\), with composition of partial maps.

**Classification theorem.** Its elements are exactly the following mutually disjoint families:

1. The full-domain conjugations \(X\mapsto u^{-1}Xu\), with \(u\in\operatorname{GL}_2(k)\), forming the group \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(k)\).
2. The unique zero-only map \(\mathbf 0:\{0\}	o\{0\}\), which is the absorbing element of the inverse monoid.
3. All trace-preserving \(k\)-linear isomorphisms between one-dimensional matrix subspaces \(kA\) and \(kB\) generated by nonzero rank-one matrices. Equivalently, such maps are exactly the unique trace-preserving maps between ordered pairs of rank-one idempotent lines, together with all linear isomorphisms between ordered pairs of nonzero nilpotent lines. There are no maps from an idempotent line to a nilpotent line.

The multiplication is composition with restricted domains. In particular, two line maps compose to a line map when the target line of the first equals the source line of the second, and otherwise compose to \(\mathbf 0\). Each full-domain conjugation acts by composition on these line maps. Consequently the two disjoint line-map sectors, with their shared zero, are Brandt matrix-unit semigroups, with local groups respectively trivial and \(k^	imes\).

## Assumptions and scope

There are no characteristic restrictions on \(k\). The result is for the multiplicative monoid of all \(2\)-by-\(2\) matrices, not the additive matrix group, not the unit group alone, and not the monoid of arbitrary transformations of the underlying set. The partial maps always contain the zero matrix in their domains. A *rank-one idempotent line* is a subspace \(kE\), where \(E^2=E\) and \(\operatorname{rank}(E)=1\); it contains exactly one nonzero idempotent. A *nilpotent line* is \(kN\), where \(N
e0\) and \(N^2=0\), with rank one. Every rank-one matrix has characteristic polynomial \(t(t-\operatorname{tr}(A))\), so its trace is zero precisely when it is nilpotent.

## Proof

**1. Domain trichotomy.** Fix \(g,h\), put \(T=gh\), and let \(R=T-I\). Then \(D_{g,h}=\{X:RX=XR=0\}\). If \(R\) is invertible, the domain is \(\{0\}\). If \(R=0\), the domain is all of \(S\). If \(R\) has rank one, both \(\ker R\) and \(V/\operatorname{im}R\) are one-dimensional, and
\[
D_{g,h}\cong\operatorname{Hom}_k(V/\operatorname{im}R,\ker R)
\]
as vector spaces: hence \(D_{g,h}\) is a line consisting of zero and rank-one matrices. These are the only possibilities for \(2\)-by-\(2\) matrices.

**2. Necessary form of each generator.** Each \(\phi_{g,h}\) is \(k\)-linear, bijective on its domain with inverse \(\phi_{h,g}\), and trace preserving, since
\[
\operatorname{tr}(hXg)=\operatorname{tr}(ghX)=\operatorname{tr}(TX)=\operatorname{tr}(X)
\quad	ext{for }X\in D_{g,h}.
\]
A nonzero element of any rank-one domain maps to a nonzero rank-one element: the map is injective and sandwich multiplication cannot raise matrix rank. It follows that a line-domain generator preserves the dichotomy between idempotent lines and nilpotent lines. When \(D_{g,h}=S\), we have \(gh=I\), and hence \(g,h\) are invertible and \(h=g^{-1}\); the resulting full-domain map is conjugation. Its kernel as a representation of \(\operatorname{GL}_2(k)\) consists of scalar matrices, giving \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(k)\). The zero-only map is available by taking \(g=h=0\).

**3. Every permitted line map is itself a generator.** Given rank-one idempotents \(E,F\), choose \(u\in\operatorname{GL}_2(k)\) such that \(u^{-1}Eu=F\). Take \(g=Eu\) and \(h=u^{-1}E\). Then \(gh=E\), so \(D_{g,h}=kE\) and \(\phi_{g,h}(E)=F\). Every trace-preserving linear map \(kE	o kF\) must send \(E\) to \(F\), and thus arises.

Given nonzero nilpotent \(N,M\), both rank one, choose \(u\in\operatorname{GL}_2(k)\) with \(u^{-1}Nu=M\). Take \(g=(I+N)u\), \(h=u^{-1}\). Then \(gh=I+N\), and \(D_{g,h}=\{X:NX=XN=0\}=kN\) because \(\operatorname{im}N=\ker N\) is a line. Moreover \(\phi_{g,h}(N)=u^{-1}N(I+N)u=M\). As \(M\) may be **any** nonzero matrix in its nilpotent line (including any nonzero scalar multiple), this realizes every nonzero linear isomorphism between nilpotent lines. All units yield full conjugations by \(g=u,h=u^{-1}\).

**4. Closure under composition.** The union of these three families is closed under composition and inversion. Composing full conjugations gives a full conjugation. Composing with a line map gives a trace-preserving line map, because conjugation preserves rank and trace. Composing two line maps yields either a trace-preserving line map (when their intermediate lines agree) or the zero-only map (when the intermediate lines intersect only at \(0\)). Composing with the zero-only map again yields that map. Thus this union contains and is generated by precisely the original \(\phi_{g,h}\) maps. This also establishes the stated multiplication law and the two Brandt sectors.

**5. Finite-field count.** Rank-one idempotents correspond bijectively to decompositions \(V=U\oplus K\) into lines. There are \(q+1\) choices for \(U\) and \(q\) complementary choices for \(K\), giving \(e=q(q+1)\) idempotent lines and \(e^2\) ordered line maps. Nonzero rank-one nilpotent lines correspond bijectively to their common image/kernel, giving \(n=q+1\) nilpotent lines; each ordered pair admits \(q-1\) nonzero linear isomorphisms, giving \(n^2(q-1)\) maps. Finally the unit group has \(|\operatorname{PGL}_2(q)|=q(q^2-1)\) elements. These three sectors and the zero-only map are disjoint, so addition gives the asserted formula.

## Verification

The included script `artifacts/verify.py` enumerates all \(g,h\in M_2(\mathbb F_q)\) for \(q=2,3\), builds each partial map directly from the domain equations, and compares the number and domain-size distribution with the formula. The actual output is:

```text

VERIFY_OK 3 order 201 generator-domain distribution {3: 176, 81: 24, 1: 1}
```

These finite checks corroborate the two cases only. The all-field theorem follows from the five proof steps above, not from finite enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work

Araújo, Bentz, Kinyon, Konieczny, Malheiro and Mercier define the partial-inner monoid and classify it for completely simple semigroups, full transformation monoids, and endomorphism monoids of finite abelian group actions. Their **Problem 7.9** asks for the corresponding monoid of linear endomorphisms of a finite-dimensional vector space. The theorem here solves the first genuinely nontrivial dimension of that requested linear problem, over every field, and computes an exact finite-field order. The older preprint on natural semigroup conjugacy contains the precursor construction and transformation/completely-simple cases; none of the material inspected supplies the line-map classification for \(M_2(k)\). The whole transformation monoid of an underlying set and a completely simple semigroup are not identical to \(M_2(k)\), so their classifications do not directly imply this description. Broader unrelated classifications of *all* partial automorphisms do not automatically characterize which maps are generated by sandwich conjugations.

## Limitations

No claim is made for dimensions at least three, arbitrary division rings, or non-linear semigroup automorphisms. The result does not assert that every abstract isomorphism between arbitrary subsemigroups is partial inner. The 2026 article explicitly acknowledges substantive overlap with its 2023 precursor; the specific linear endomorphism problem appears explicitly in the separately published 2026 formulation. The available literature and public research searches may miss a prior classification of this precise dimension-two case. Checks over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\) are not independent verification of the all-field proof.
