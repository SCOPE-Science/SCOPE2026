# Perfect matchings govern the integral topology of fully whiskered matching complexes

## Finding
For every finite simple graph \(G=(V,E)\) with \(n=|V|\geq1\), attach a distinct leaf \(v'\) to each \(v\in V\), obtaining the fully whiskered graph \(W(G)\). Let \(K=\mathcal M(W(G))\) be the **ordinary matching complex**: its simplicial vertices are the edges of \(W(G)\), and its faces are arbitrary collections of pairwise-disjoint graph edges. Denote by \(\operatorname{PM}(G)\) the set of perfect matchings of the **original** graph \(G\).

If \(n\) is odd, or if \(\operatorname{PM}(G)=\varnothing\), then \(K\) is contractible. If \(n=2m\), then
\[
K\simeq\bigvee^{|\operatorname{PM}(G)|}S^{m-1}.
\]
The empty wedge means a point. This describes all integral homology, without a torsion qualification. More precisely, there is a natural isomorphism of integral \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\)-modules
\[
\widetilde H_{m-1}(K;\mathbb Z)\cong
\bigoplus_{P\in\operatorname{PM}(G)}\bigwedge^m\mathbb Z^{P}.
\]
Here \(\mathbb Z^P\) is the permutation lattice freely generated by the \(m\) edges in \(P\). An automorphism sends the orientation line at \(P\) to the line at its image and contributes exactly the sign of the induced ordering of these edges. Every other reduced integral homology group is zero.

In particular, a fully whiskered even cycle has a wedge of **two** spheres of dimension \(m-1\), whereas a fully whiskered odd cycle is contractible. For the fully whiskered complete graph on \(2m\) original vertices, the rank is \((2m-1)!!\), and its symmetric-group representation is the oriented permutation module on perfect matchings.

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, simple and undirected, with at least one original vertex; no connectedness or planarity hypothesis is needed. Distinguish three objects: an edge of \(G\), its corresponding vertex \(b_e\) of \(K\), and the vertex \(w_v\) of \(K\) representing the new leaf-edge \(\{v,v'\}\). The number \(m\) is defined only in the even case \(n=2m\), so \(m\geq1\). All joins of reduced chains use the standard orientation convention; exchanging factors of reduced degrees \(p\) and \(q\) changes the sign by \((-1)^{(p+1)(q+1)}\).

## Proof
**1. Exact acyclic matching.** Fix any total order on \(V\). Every simplex of \(K\) has a unique presentation \(B\sqcup A\), where \(B\subseteq E\) is a matching in the original graph and \(A\) consists of leaf-edges at an arbitrary subset of the original vertices not covered by \(B\). If \(B\) is not perfect, let \(u(B)\) be its least uncovered original vertex. Pair every simplex with the same \(B\) by toggling the leaf-edge \(w_{u(B)}\):
\[
(B,A)\longleftrightarrow (B,A\triangle\{w_{u(B)}\}).
\]
This is a disjoint partial matching on the **augmented** face poset, including the empty simplex; the only unpaired simplices are precisely the base perfect matchings \(P\). To prove acyclicity, direct matched cover arrows upward and all other cover arrows downward. Along any directed path, the collection \(B\) of original edges can never increase, since no matched arrow changes \(B\). Hence a directed cycle has constant \(B\). Within a fixed \(B\), each upward step introduces the fixed pivot \(w_{u(B)}\), which cannot be deleted by an unmatched downward step, so no directed cycle is possible. Deleting the pair between the empty face and the pivot singleton leaves an ordinary acyclic matching with one critical zero-simplex and one critical \((m-1)\)-simplex for each \(P\). Each \(P\) is maximal. Standard elementary discrete Morse theory, including the zero-dimensional case, therefore gives a point when there are no perfect matchings and a wedge of precisely \(|\operatorname{PM}(G)|\) spheres of dimension \(m-1\) otherwise. In particular, no integral torsion can occur.

**2. Explicit primitive cycles.** Suppose \(n=2m\). For each perfect base matching \(P=\{e_1,\ldots,e_m\}\), order its base edges and choose one endpoint \(v_i\in e_i\) for each. For a base edge \(e\), define the reduced zero-chain \(c_{e,v}=[b_e]-[w_v]\). Since the edges of \(P\) are pairwise disjoint, every vertex from one of these two-term chains is compatible with every vertex from a different chain. Thus their simplicial chain join
\[
z_{P} = c_{e_1,v_1}*\cdots*c_{e_m,v_m}\in\widetilde C_{m-1}(K;\mathbb Z)
\]
is well-defined and is a cycle by the augmented join-boundary identity (each \(c_{e,v}\) is a reduced zero-cycle). Changing the selected endpoint of \(e=\{v,u\}\) changes its factor by \([w_u]-[w_v]\), the boundary of an oriented edge of \(K\): the two leaf-edges are disjoint graph edges. Joining that one-chain with the other cycle factors exhibits the change of \(z_P\) as a boundary. Hence \([z_P]\) depends only on an orientation (ordering) of \(P\), not the endpoint choices.

The simplex consisting of all \(m\) base edges in \(P\) is maximal in \(K\), so the coefficient functional \(\delta_P\) extracting its oriented coefficient is an integral cocycle. The unique all-base top-dimensional term of \(z_P\) is \([b_{e_1},\ldots,b_{e_m}]\) with coefficient \(+1\), whereas \(z_Q\), for \(Q\neq P\), has zero coefficient on this simplex. Thus the cocycle-cycle pairing is the identity matrix: \(\langle\delta_P,[z_Q]\rangle=\delta_{PQ}\). By step 1, the free homology rank is \(|\operatorname{PM}(G)|\); therefore these explicitly constructed classes form a **primitive integral basis**, not merely a rationally independent family.

**3. Naturality and signs.** Every \(g\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)\) extends uniquely to the leaf-labelled action on \(W(G)\); it takes \(b_e\) to \(b_{g e}\) and \(w_v\) to \(w_{gv}\). Consequently, \(g z_P\) is the join of the corresponding factors for \(gP\). Endpoint changes give boundaries by step 2. Exchanging two reduced zero-chain factors contributes a minus sign, because \((-1)^{(0+1)(0+1)}=-1\). Therefore the resulting action on the primitive integral basis is precisely the oriented permutation action on the perfect matching edge-sets, proving the claimed determinant-line decomposition.

## Verification
The included standalone Python checker `whiskered_matching_check.py` enumerates all matching faces and verifies the complete directed acyclic matching on 190 graph instances, including every graph on four original vertices. It independently performs 162 complete reduced homology computations over \(\mathbb F_2\) and compares 54 exact signed simplicial Lefschetz traces to the orientation-line character for automorphisms of complete graphs and cycles. The accompanying JSON contains the actual reported counts and `status: PASS`. The proof, rather than these finite checks, establishes the universal assertion, its integral saturation, and naturality.

## Relationship to prior work
Barik–Mondal–Mukherjee (arXiv:2604.12606v1; first public 14 April 2026; primary MSC2020 57Q70) construct acyclic matchings for independence complexes having simplicial vertices and supply recursive approaches for certain graph families. The ordinary matching complex is the independence complex of the line graph; leaf-edges furnish simplicial vertices in this line graph. Their broader method does not state the closed perfect-matching count, the explicit primitive integral generators, or the signed full automorphism representation of all fully whiskered matching complexes.

Bayer–Jelić Milutinović–Vega (arXiv:2411.04601v1, updated in v2) establish that outerplanar-graph matching complexes are wedges of spheres or contractible, without such a general exact classification or integral symmetry representation; fully whiskered graphs need not be outerplanar. Singh (arXiv:2101.05534) establishes vertex decomposability for the ordinary matching complex of a fully whiskered graph, which implies a general wedge property but does not provide the present uniform sphere multiplicity and primitive equivariant basis. Deshpande–Singh's higher-independence complexes of fully whiskered graphs and Vega's *2-matching complexes* concern different simplicial constructions. Unindexed equivalent results cannot be excluded by literature search alone.

## Limitations
The result concerns the ordinary **one-matching complex of the fully whiskered graph**; it does not determine homology for the perfect-matching complex of the whiskered graph or for generalized matching capacities. The homotopy equivalence produced by the vertex-order Morse matching is not claimed to be equivariant; only the integral homology identification is canonical and equivariant. The discrete Morse cancellation is performed in the augmented face poset and made ordinary by choosing a base vertex. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
