# Periodic zeta rationality versus Sturmian nonsoficity in decorated gap shifts

## Finding
Fix any bi-infinite Sturmian word \(w\) on \(\{1,2\}\), and any infinite \(S\subseteq\mathbb N_0\). Let \(X_w(S)\) be the closed shift generated by concatenations of words \(0u\), with \(u\) a length-\(s\) factor of \(w\) and \(s\in S\). Define
\[
F_S(z)=\sum_{s\in S}(s+1)z^{s+1}.
\]
Then the Artin–Mazur zeta function and all fixed-point counts are exactly
\[
\zeta_{X_w(S)}(z)=\frac{1}{1-F_S(z)},\qquad
|\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^m)|=m[z^m]\bigl(-\log(1-F_S(z))\bigr)\quad(m\ge1).
\]
Moreover, \(X_w(S)\) is **nonsofic for every such \(S\)**, although its zeta function is rational **if and only if \(S\) is eventually periodic**. If \(S\) is not eventually periodic, the unit circle is a **natural boundary for meromorphic continuation** of this zeta function from its canonical meromorphic domain \(|z|<1\). The zeta function and fixed-point counts depend on \(S\) but not on the Sturmian slope.

## Assumptions and scope
A Sturmian word means a bi-infinite binary word whose factor language has exactly \(s+1\) distinct factors of every length \(s\ge0\), equivalently the language of a coding of an irrational rotation. In particular, its orbit closure is minimal, infinite, and has no periodic point. The letter \(0\) is a marker not occurring in \(w\). An eventually periodic subset of \(\mathbb N_0\) is one whose membership indicator is periodic outside a finite initial segment. The shift \(X_w(S)\) is the **closure** of all bi-infinite concatenations \(\cdots 0u_{-1}0u_00u_1\cdots\), with each \(u_j\) a factor of \(w\) and \(|u_j|\in S\). Here \(S\) is infinite, hence unbounded, but need not contain zero, be syndetic, or have any asymptotic density. The Artin–Mazur zeta is the formal power series \(\exp(\sum_{m\ge1}P_m z^m/m)\), where \(P_m\) counts configurations fixed by \(\sigma^m\).

## Proof
**Periodic configurations must contain a marker.** A configuration of \(X_w(S)\) with no \(0\) has each finite block in the language of \(w\), by the defining closure. Conversely, each finite Sturmian factor occurs inside an admissible gap word of some length in unbounded \(S\): extend the occurrence inside the bi-infinite recurrent word \(w\). Hence
\[
X_w(S)\cap\{1,2\}^{\mathbb Z}=\overline{\{\sigma^j w:j\in\mathbb Z\}}.
\]
The right-hand side has no periodic points by irrationality. Every periodic configuration therefore has at least one marker, and between consecutive markers its intervening word is a factor of \(w\) of an allowed length. Such a periodic configuration is recovered uniquely from its marker positions and those intervening words; the limit operation adds no new finite adjacent-marker words.

**Periodic-point enumeration.** Set \(c_s=|\mathcal L_s(w)|=s+1\), including \(c_0=1\). A specified marker and its following gap form a code word of length \(s+1\), and there are \(c_s\) choices. The ordinary code-length enumerator is therefore \(F_S(z)\). Given \(k\ge1\) distinguished successive marker-to-marker code words with total length \(m\), there are \([z^m]F_S(z)^k\) choices. Among period-\(m\) configurations having \(k\) markers in one length-\(m\) coordinate window, there are \(k\) possible distinguished markers and \(m\) possible coordinates for such a distinguished marker. Counting pairs of a configuration and a distinguished marker, and then forgetting the distinction, gives
\[
P_m=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{m}{k}[z^m]F_S(z)^k.
\]
(The factor \(m/k\) is valid also for points of least period properly dividing \(m\), because all counts are made with labeled coordinates modulo \(m\).) Dividing by \(m\), summing formally over \(m\ge1\), and applying \(\sum_{k\ge1}F^k/k=-\log(1-F)\), yields the two claimed identities. Every coefficient of \(F_S\) at degree \(s+1\) equals \(s+1\) or zero, so its power-series radius is exactly one, as \(S\) is infinite. Thus \(1/(1-F_S)\) gives a meromorphic continuation of the zeta germ throughout \(|z|<1\), though its Taylor-series radius may be smaller due to a pole inside that disk.

**Rationality classification and natural boundary.** If membership in \(S\) is eventually periodic, \(F_S\) is a finite polynomial plus a finite sum of series of the form \(\sum_{j\ge j_0}(a+bj)z^{r+pj}\), so it and the zeta function are rational. Conversely, if the zeta function is rational, so is \(F_S=1-1/\zeta\). The coefficients \(a_m=[z^m]F_S\) then satisfy a constant-coefficient linear recurrence eventually. The Skolem–Mahler–Lech theorem states that the zero set of such a recurrence is a finite set together with finitely many full arithmetic progressions, up to the initial indices. Since \(a_m=0\) exactly when \(m-1\notin S\), the complement and therefore the indicator of \(S\) is eventually periodic. This proves the equivalence.

If \(S\) is not eventually periodic, \(F_S\) is not rational. Its integer coefficients and exact radius one allow application of the Pólya–Carlson dichotomy: its unit circle is a natural boundary for analytic continuation. An assumed meromorphic continuation of \(\zeta=1/(1-F_S)\) across any open arc of \(|z|=1\) would make \(F_S=1-1/\zeta\) meromorphic there; deleting isolated poles or zeros leaves a subarc across which \(F_S\) continues holomorphically, a contradiction. Thus the unit circle is also a natural boundary for meromorphic continuation of \(\zeta\). **This is not a claim that the Maclaurin series for \(\zeta\) has radius one.**

**Nonsoficity.** As proved above, the intersection of \(X_w(S)\) with the full two-symbol shift is the Sturmian orbit closure. If \(X_w(S)\) were sofic, this intersection would also be sofic: intersection with the two-symbol full shift is a finite-type symbol restriction and preserves soficity. Every nonempty sofic shift has a periodic point, since a finite labeled presenting graph has a directed cycle supporting a periodic label sequence. But the Sturmian orbit closure has no periodic point. Contradiction. Nonsoficity holds equally when \(\zeta\) is rational.

## Verification
The self-contained `verify.py` generates Sturmian factors and enumerates all cyclic ternary strings for periods \(1\) through \(10\), comparing direct marker/gap admissibility with the exact coefficient formula. Four choices of \(S\) (all gaps, even gaps, powers-of-two gaps, and an irregular sparse set) give **40 exact fixed-point counts**, all matching. This is finite evidence only; the preceding formal periodic-point bijection and the named mathematical theorems establish the all-period and analytic conclusions. An illustrative rational-zeta nonsofic example is \(S=\mathbb N_0\), for which \(F_S(z)=z/(1-z)^2\) and
\[
\zeta(z)=\frac{(1-z)^2}{1-3z+z^2}.
\]
For an infinite non-eventually-periodic example, \(S=\{2^j:j\ge0\}\) has a nonrational zeta function with natural boundary \(|z|=1\) for meromorphic continuation.

## Relationship to prior work
Ramirez and Somers (2025), especially their definition of \((S,w)\)-gap shifts and Theorem 1.1, establish entropy in terms of factor complexity as well as irreducibility, synchronization, and mixing. The present work instead counts all periodic points, determines the zeta function, and classifies rationality and its analytic boundary in the Sturmian case. Ahmadi Dastjerdi and Jangjoo (2011/2012) derive zeta functions of classical binary \(S\)-gap shifts, with different contributions from their marker-free periodic backgrounds; that result does not imply the stated nonsofic Sturmian-decoration dichotomy. Dillon (2022), Theorem 1.3 and the zeta section, treats \(\mathcal S\)-graph shifts encoded by finite directed graphs and vertex run-length sets, not the unbounded aperiodic Sturmian factor language. Hong (2011) studies zeta functions of renewal systems generated by finite word lists; the abstract was located but the full primary text could not be inspected, so a possible broader formulation is an unresolved bibliographic risk. The Skolem–Mahler–Lech and Pólya–Carlson theorems are explicitly used as classical inputs and are not new assertions.

## Limitations
The claim is restricted to bi-infinite binary Sturmian decorations, infinite allowed-gap sets, and one distinguished marker. It does not classify the zeta function for arbitrary non-Sturmian decorations. Period-count tests are finite and do not prove Pólya–Carlson or Skolem–Mahler–Lech. The rationality and boundary conclusions depend on those standard theorems. The natural-boundary statement concerns meromorphic continuation through the **unit** circle, not the convergence radius of the initial zeta series. Full inspection of the 2011 finite-renewal paper was unavailable, leaving historical-priority risk for an equivalent abstract coding statement.
