## Correctness — PASS
The argument fixes unit graph-distance and inclusive radius two for every integer \(n\ge19\). The short-cycle count is derived from the spoke/outer/inner edge structure, not extrapolated from tests. Unique distance-two midpoints and explicit radius-two ball intersections identify exactly the centered simplices and two exceptional triangles per hexagon. The centered subcomplex has an explicit sequence of \(8n\) free-face pairs; attached triangles map to paired copies of hexagon loops. Each first disk has a private inner edge, collapsing the base to the outer circle; each second disk gives one sphere. The octahedral join orientations give the integral basis and dihedral signs. The checker substantiates all local steps in a finite sample while the proof covers every parameter in the stated range.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks
Adamaszek's graph-power theorem covers girth at least seven or graph squares whose cliques are centered, not the girth-six exceptional triangles here. The published hexagon-packing theorems require pairwise edge-disjoint six-cycles, whereas every outer edge in the present family lies in three of them. The closest indexed generalized-Petersen result treats inner step two, not three. Published source text, variant formulations and indexed research records were compared by assumptions and implications. No checked source covers the uniform wedge together with primitive persistence and the integral symmetry module. Unindexed equivalences remain possible; the search is not a uniqueness proof.

The result explains how strongly overlapping six-cycles nevertheless give a saturated family of homological sphere generators. It determines the entire stable homotopy type and the disappearing first-homology classes, plus an integral dihedral module invisible to Betti ranks alone. The preceding size eighteen has different mod-two homology, giving a motivated sharp boundary. The classification is a natural infinite-family structural theorem, rather than a small parameter increment or isolated numerical calculation.
