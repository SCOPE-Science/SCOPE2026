# Petersen three-colouring Kempe graph: an oriented-edge quotient and sharp diameter

## Finding
Let \(P=\operatorname{KG}(5,2)\) be the Petersen graph. Define \(\mathcal K(P)\) to have all proper vertex colourings \(c:V(P)\to\{0,1,2\}\) as its vertices. Join two *distinct* colourings exactly when a single Kempe interchange transforms one into the other. Let \(\overline{\mathcal K}(P)\) identify colourings differing by a global permutation of the palette, retaining an edge between two distinct orbits if a Kempe interchange joins them.

**Theorem.** The quotient \(\overline{\mathcal K}(P)\) is isomorphic to the line graph of the graph obtained by subdividing every edge of \(K_5\) once. Explicitly, quotient states are ordered pairs \((i,j)\) of distinct elements of \([5]\), and different states are adjacent exactly when they share their first coordinate or are reversals \((i,j),(j,i)\). Thus it has \(20\) vertices, \(40\) edges, is \(4\)-regular, and has both radius and diameter \(3\), with common distance shells \((1,4,6,9)\). The labelled graph \(\mathcal K(P)\) has \(120\) vertices, \(360\) edges, is \(6\)-regular and vertex-transitive, and has both radius and diameter \(4\), with common distance shells \((1,6,25,61,27)\).

## Assumptions and scope
Take \([5]=\{0,1,2,3,4\}\). A vertex of \(P\) is a two-element subset of \([5]\); two vertices are adjacent if and only if the subsets are disjoint. A colour class is an independent set of \(P\). A Kempe interchange chooses two distinct palette labels and **one entire connected component** of the subgraph induced by vertices bearing either label, and exchanges these labels on that component. The labelled graph retains global palette changes as genuine moves when they arise from an interchange. In the quotient, all palette permutations are identified; loops are discarded and multiple edges suppressed. No automorphism of \(P\) is used in forming the quotient.

## Proof
**1. All colourings and the ordered-pair parametrisation.** Every pairwise-intersecting family of two-element subsets of a five-set has at most four members. If it has four, they must all share one element: otherwise three of them form a triangle of edges on three underlying elements, and no fourth distinct edge can meet all three. Hence every independent four-set of \(P\) is one of the five stars \(A_i=\{\{i,k\}:k\ne i\}\). Since ten vertices are split into three independent classes, some class has at least four elements; it is an \(A_i\) of size exactly four. There cannot be two such classes, because any two different stars meet in \(\{i,j\}\). Thus the three class sizes are precisely \(4,3,3\).

After deleting \(A_i\), the six remaining vertices are the edges of the complete graph on \([5]\setminus\{i\}\), and the induced Petersen subgraph is three disjoint edges, joining complementary pairs. Every proper two-colouring chooses one endpoint from each of these three edges. Its two three-member colour classes consist of a three-edge star and the complementary triangle in that four-vertex complete graph. The centre \(j\ne i\) of the small star is unique. Conversely, for each ordered pair \((i,j)\), set \(B_{i,j}=\{\{j,k\}: k\notin\{i,j\}\}\) and \(C_{i,j}=\{\{k,l\}: k,l\notin\{i,j\},\ k\ne l\}\). The three sets \(A_i,B_{i,j},C_{i,j}\) are independent and partition all ten vertices, and all proper colourings are obtained by assigning the three distinct palette labels to these sets. There are therefore \(5\cdot4=20\) palette-unlabelled states and \(20\cdot 3!=120\) labelled states.

**2. Exact Kempe adjacency.** Fix \((i,j)\) and write \(U=[5]\setminus\{i,j\}\). The subgraph on the small star \(B_{i,j}\) and its complementary triangle \(C_{i,j}\) is three disjoint edges, each pairing complementary two-subsets of \([5]\setminus\{i\}\). Interchanging on any one component changes the centre of the small star to one of the three respective elements of \(U\). Therefore these moves yield precisely \((i,k)\) for \(k\in U\).

The subgraph on \(A_i\cup B_{i,j}\) consists of the isolated vertex \(\{i,j\}\) and a six-cycle. Interchanging on *either* of its two components makes \(B_{i,j}\cup\{\{i,j\}\}=A_j\) the new large class and makes the small star centred at \(i\), giving exactly \((j,i)\). The subgraph on \(A_i\cup C_{i,j}\) is connected: \(\{i,j\}\) meets all three vertices of \(C_{i,j}\) by Petersen adjacency, and each other vertex of \(A_i\) meets exactly one; its only interchange globally swaps two palette labels, leaving \((i,j)\) unchanged. Thus the quotient adjacency is exactly
\[
(i,j)\sim(i,k)\quad(k\notin\{i,j\}),\qquad (i,j)\sim(j,i).
\]
For each labelled colouring there are respectively three, two, and one distinct moves in these cases, proving degree six and hence \(120\cdot6/2=360\) labelled edges.

**3. Structural quotient and its metric.** Subdivide every edge \(\{i,j\}\) of \(K_5\) once. The edges of the subdivided graph correspond to incidences \((i,\{i,j\})\), which we identify with the ordered pairs \((i,j)\). Two such incidences have an endpoint in common exactly when their ordered pairs share the first coordinate or are reversals. Thus their line graph is the quotient just described. It has \(20\) vertices and degree four, hence \(40\) edges. From any \((i,j)\), reach any \((k,l)\) with \(k\ne i\) along \((i,j)\to(i,k)\to(k,i)\to(k,l)\), omitting repeated vertices where necessary; if \(k=i\), one step suffices. Hence every distance is at most three. Four mutually distinct indices \(i,j,k,l\) give \((i,j)\) and \((k,l)\) with neither an edge nor a common neighbour, so their distance is three. The natural action of the symmetric group on \([5]\) is transitive on ordered pairs, and the distance-shell counts \(1,4,6,9\) follow by enumerating these twenty explicit neighbours at distances \(0\) through \(3\).

**4. Labelled metric.** Permuting the five underlying elements acts as graph automorphisms of \(P\), transitively on the ordered pairs \((i,j)\); independently, global palette permutations act transitively on the six labelled states over each ordered pair. Thus the labelled Kempe graph is vertex-transitive. The attached stand-alone verifier constructs all \(120\) colourings in two independent ways, constructs all Kempe neighbours by both connected-component breadth-first search and disjoint-set union, and checks all-source shortest-path distances. It establishes the exact common shells \(1,6,25,61,27\), exhausting all \(120\) states. In particular the labelled radius and diameter are both four. This last metric step is a complete finite computation, not an inference from the quotient diameter alone.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` from the directory containing the accompanying script (standard library only). The checker verifies two independently generated copies of the entire colouring state space, equality of two component algorithms for every state, symmetry of every move, exact quotient adjacency with a separately constructed line graph, and breadth-first distances from **every** state of both graphs. It concludes with `VERIFY_OK`; the asserted distance shells are \((1,6,25,61,27)\) and \((1,4,6,9)\). The finite metric proof assumes precisely the explicitly enumerated ten-vertex graph and its stated move rule.

## Relationship to prior work
Feghali, Johnson, and Paulusma's 2015 preprint proves that all proper three-colourings of every cubic graph other than the complete four-vertex graph and the triangular prism form one Kempe class. Its theorem therefore implies connectivity for the Petersen graph, but does **not** supply this quotient isomorphism, the exact vertex and edge counts of its Kempe graph, or its shortest-move diameter and distance shells. Literature on perfect/equitable colourings and on single-vertex recolouring concerns different constraints or moves. The present theorem is a constructive, quantitative refinement for this canonical cubic graph, not a strengthening of the general connectivity theorem to all cubic graphs.

## Limitations
The result is specific to proper **vertex** three-colourings of the Petersen graph, not edge-colourings, four-colourings, or other cubic graphs. The labelled diameter and shells use an exhaustive finite verifier; they are not asserted as a closed-form infinite-family formula. Exhaustive literature coverage cannot be guaranteed, and the full text of a potentially related 2018 paper on *perfect* (equitable) colourings was not available for a decisive full-document comparison. No independent audit has been performed.
