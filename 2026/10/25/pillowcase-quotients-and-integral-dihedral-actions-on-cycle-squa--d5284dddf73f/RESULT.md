# Pillowcase quotients and integral dihedral actions on cycle-square neighborhood tori

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge9\), let \(G_n=C_n(1,2)\) be the graph on \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) with edges between vertices whose difference is \(\pm1\) or \(\pm2\), and let \(X_n=\mathcal N(G_n)\) denote its open-neighborhood simplicial complex (subsets with a common graph neighbor). The natural dihedral group \(D_n=\langle r,s\mid r^n=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle\), where \(r(i)=i+1\) and \(s(i)=-i\), acts simplicially.

There is an explicit \(D_n\)-equivariant strong deformation retraction onto a triangulated torus \(K_n\). Every nonidentity rotation acts without fixed points on \(|K_n|\). Each reflection \(s_t(i)=t-i\) has precisely four isolated fixed points, and distinct reflections have disjoint fixed-point sets. The quotient \(|K_n|/D_n\) is an orientable sphere with exactly four branch points, each of ramification order two, hence has orbifold signature \((0;2,2,2,2)\). The quotient \(|K_n|/C_n\) is a torus. Consequently \(|X_n|/D_n\simeq S^2\) and \(|X_n|/C_n\simeq T^2\).

The complete integral homology action is \(H_1(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2\), with every rotation acting as the identity and every reflection as minus the identity; \(H_0\cong H_2\cong\mathbb Z\) with trivial action, and all other homology vanishes.

## Assumptions and scope
All graph and simplicial indices are modulo \(n\), and the claim holds for every integer \(n\ge9\), with no primality or parity restriction. The complex uses *open*, not closed, neighborhoods. A reflection is any \(s_t:i\mapsto t-i\); the rotation subgroup \(C_n\) is generated by \(r:i\mapsto i+1\). The orbifold assertion concerns the explicit invariant surface core, while the assertions about the full original complex's quotients are homotopy equivalences induced by the equivariant retraction. No claim that the original three-dimensional complex is itself a surface is made.

## Proof
**Step 1: Equivariant surface core.** The maximal simplices of \(X_n\) are the tetrahedra
\[
F_i=\{i-2,i-1,i+1,i+2\},\qquad i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z.
\]
For \(n\ge9\), two distinct tetrahedra meet in at most two vertices, and the edge \(e_i=\{i-1,i+1\}\) belongs to \(F_i\) alone. Define a subcomplex \(K_n\) by retaining exactly the two triangles in each tetrahedron that do not contain \(e_i\), with oriented triangles
\[
A_i=[i-2,i+1,i+2],\qquad B_i=[i-2,i+2,i-1].
\]
The cyclic square-neighborhood complex has face vector \((n,4n,4n,n)\) and the core has face vector \((n,3n,2n)\).

On \(|F_i|\), write a point with barycentric coefficients \((u,c,d,v)\) corresponding in order to vertices \((i-2,i-1,i+1,i+2)\). Put \(m=\min(c,d)\). Define \(h_i(u,c,d,v)=(u+m,c-m,d-m,v+m)\), and interpolate linearly \(H_{i,t}=(1-t)\operatorname{id}+t h_i\). The sum of the four coefficients remains one, all remain nonnegative, and at the endpoint at least one of \(c,d\) vanishes: the image lies in \(|A_i\cup B_i|\). The map fixes that subcomplex pointwise. Any intersection of different tetrahedra avoids the uniquely contained edge \(e_i\); therefore it already has \(c=0\) or \(d=0\) and is fixed throughout, so the local homotopies glue. Dihedral symmetries permute the tetrahedra, preserve the partition of their vertices into the two distance-two versus two distance-one vertices, and preserve \(m\). Thus the glued strong deformation retraction is \(D_n\)-equivariant.

**Step 2: The core is an oriented torus.** Edges have cyclic step sizes one, three, or four, giving exactly \(3n\) edges. Each edge lies in exactly two displayed triangles. At each vertex the link is a single cycle on six vertices (for example the link of zero runs through \(-1,3,4,1,-3,-4\)); these are distinct for \(n\ge9\). Hence \(K_n\) is a connected closed triangulated surface. Its oriented two-chain \(Z=\sum_i(A_i+B_i)\) has zero boundary, by cancellation along every shared edge, and every face coefficient is one, so \(K_n\) is orientable. Its Euler characteristic is \(n-3n+2n=0\), hence \(|K_n|\cong T^2\).

**Step 3: Orientation and rotation fixed points.** A translation carries \(A_i\) to \(A_{i+a}\) and \(B_i\) to \(B_{i+a}\). Every reflection \(s_t\) sends the oriented \(A_i\) to the oriented \(B_{t-i}\) with positive sign, and \(B_i\) to \(A_{t-i}\) with positive sign. Thus the entire group preserves the orientation class \([Z]\). Nontrivial rotations stabilize no vertex or triangle, and they stabilize no edge either: a nontrivial rotation stabilizing a two-element set would have to be the antipodal translation, but all edge step sizes are one, three, or four, strictly smaller than \(n/2\) for \(n\ge9\) in the cyclic-distance sense where appropriate. Every fixed point of a simplicial map lies inside an invariant simplex; hence nonidentity rotations act freely.

**Step 4: Fixed sets of reflections.** A reflection preserves no two-simplex, because it exchanges the two kinds of oriented faces. For odd \(n\), each reflection has exactly one fixed vertex and three invariant edges whose endpoints are swapped. For even \(n\), a reflection with even \(t\) has two fixed vertices and two swapped edges, while one with odd \(t\) has no fixed vertices and four swapped edges. No invariant edge has both endpoints fixed. Each swapped edge contributes just its midpoint as a fixed point. In every parity case the total is \(4\) isolated fixed points. Fixed sets of distinct reflections are disjoint, since their product is a nonidentity rotation and those are free. There are therefore \(4n\) points with stabilizer two, partitioned into exactly four \(D_n\)-orbits of size \(n\).

**Step 5: Quotients and homology action.** All group elements are orientation-preserving homeomorphisms of the torus. Its finite-group quotient is therefore an orientable closed surface with precisely four cone points of order two. Riemann–Hurwitz gives \(0=2n(\chi-4(1-1/2))\), so its Euler characteristic is two and its underlying topological space is \(S^2\). The free rotation subgroup quotient is an oriented closed surface of Euler characteristic zero, hence \(T^2\).

Since the core is a torus, its integral first homology is torsion-free of rank two. For a nonidentity rotation, the simplicial Lefschetz number is zero because no simplex is invariant. For a reflection, the signed simplicial Lefschetz number is \(f_0-(-f_1)=4\), since every invariant edge is swapped and there are no invariant triangles. For all group elements, the traces in degrees zero and two are both one; hence \(\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H_1)=2-L(g)\). Thus every nonidentity rotation has first-homology trace two and every reflection trace minus two. A finite-order \(2\times2\) rational matrix of trace two (respectively an involution with trace minus two) is \(+I\) (respectively \(-I\)). These equalities hold over integers as well, giving the asserted complete integral module. Equivariance of the deformation retraction transfers the quotient homotopy types and integral module from \(K_n\) to \(X_n\).

## Verification
The standalone file `cycle_neighborhood_orbifold_checker.py` generates all simplices and the two-dimensional core directly from their defining facets. Execute `python3 cycle_neighborhood_orbifold_checker.py` with Python 3. It tests all integer sizes from nine through twenty-four, checks both face vectors, omitted-edge uniqueness, exact vanishing of the oriented integral boundary, six-cycle vertex links, all reflection actions and fixed simplex counts, absence of nontrivial rotation-invariant simplices, and quotient Euler characteristics using independently enumerated barycentric chain orbits. These finite tests supplement and do not replace the preceding proof for all integers.

## Relationship to prior work
Samir Shukla's 2018 preprint (later revised in 2019), especially Theorem 2.5(C) and the proof in Section 4, already classifies the **ordinary homotopy type** of the neighborhood complex of four-regular circulant graphs and constructs nonequivariant surface reductions. This earlier result includes the torus homotopy type relevant here and is not claimed as new. The present contribution is an **equivariant** strong deformation retraction preserving every dihedral symmetry, the complete reflection fixed-point census, the four-orbit branching and sphere/pillowcase quotient, and the explicit **integral** dihedral homology representation. The nonequivariant homotopy type alone cannot determine these actions or quotient branch types. Other published records on neighborhood complexes of third powers of cycles and toroidal grid graphs involve different graph families, and their statements do not imply these conclusions.

## Limitations
The quotient orbifold signature is asserted for the invariant torus core, not as a literal orbifold structure on the higher-dimensional original complex. An equivariant retraction gives the original quotient spaces their stated homotopy types, but not homeomorphism to the quotient surfaces. An independent external mathematical audit has not been performed. Unindexed equivalent equivariant work may exist.
