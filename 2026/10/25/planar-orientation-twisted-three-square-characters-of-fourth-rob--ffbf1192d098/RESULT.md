# Planar-orientation-twisted three-square characters of fourth robust grid complexes through sixteen vertices

## Finding
For integers \(3\le m\le n\) with \(mn\le16\), set \(G_{m,n}=P_m\square P_n\), with vertices \((i,j)\) for \(0\le i<m\), \(0\le j<n\), adjacent exactly when their Manhattan distance is one. Let
\[
K_{m,n}=\operatorname{Cliq}_{4}(G_{m,n})
=\{W\subseteq V(G_{m,n}):\alpha(G_{m,n}[W])<4\}.
\]
Let \(Q_{m,n}\) be its \((m-1)(n-1)\) elementary squares and \(\Gamma_{m,n}\) its full grid isometry group. Each \(g\in\Gamma_{m,n}\) has planar orientation sign \(\delta(g)\in\{1,-1\}\). The **new equivariant finding** is the rational representation isomorphism
\[
\boxed{\ \widetilde H_5(K_{m,n};\mathbb Q)
\cong \mathbb Q\bigl[\binom{Q_{m,n}}3\bigr]\otimes\delta\ }
\qquad\text{as }\mathbb Q[\Gamma_{m,n}]\text{-modules}.
\]
Equivalently, for every grid symmetry \(g\),
\[
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(g\mid\widetilde H_5(K_{m,n};\mathbb Q)\bigr)
=\delta(g)\,\#\{T\subseteq Q_{m,n}:|T|=3,\ gT=T\}.
\]
The four pairs \((m,n)\), up to transpose, are \((3,3),(3,4),(3,5),(4,4)\). For the two square cases, write \(r\) for a quarter-turn and \(s\) for any axial reflection, so that \(D_4=\langle r,s:r^4=s^2=1,srs=r^{-1}\rangle\). The character values, in the order \(1,r^2,r,r^3,s,rs\) (with the two quarter-turn values equal), are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
(m,n)&1&r^2&r&r^3&s&rs\\\hline
(3,3)&4&0&0&0&0&-2\\
(4,4)&84&4&0&0&-10&-10.
\end{array}
\]
For the nonsquare cases, write \(h\) for the reflection reversing the rows and \(v\) for the reflection reversing the columns. In the order \((1,h,v,hv)\), the characters are \((20,0,-4,0)\) for \((3,4)\) and \((56,0,0,0)\) for \((3,5)\). In particular, the full \(D_4\)-invariant subspace for \((4,4)\) has rank \(6\) over \(\mathbb Q\), and the degree-five rational homology of the symmetry quotient has dimension \(6\). The negative reflection traces show that the action is **not** the unmodified permutation representation on triples of elementary squares.

## Assumptions and scope
Each simplicial vertex of \(K_{m,n}\) is one grid vertex. A face is a vertex set containing no independent subset of cardinality four; its dimension is its cardinality minus one. For rectangular grids, the symmetry group is generated by independent row and column reflections. For square grids it is the eight-element dihedral group generated by a quarter-turn and a reflection. The character and module statements use rational coefficients. Nonequivariant integral homotopy types
\[
K_{3,3}\simeq\bigvee^4S^5,\quad
K_{3,4}\simeq\bigvee^{20}S^5,\quad
K_{3,5}\simeq\bigvee^{56}S^5,\quad
K_{4,4}\simeq\bigvee^{84}S^5
\]
are recorded for contextual proof completeness, **not claimed as new independent findings**. This is a complete finite check for \(3\le m\le n\), \(mn\le16\), not a proof for arbitrarily large grids.

## Proof
**Step 1: Exact face posets.** Order the \(mn\) vertices lexicographically by row and column, encoding subsets as binary integers. Independently enumerate the independent four-subsets of the grid. A nonempty subset belongs to \(K_{m,n}\) exactly when it contains none of those forbidden four-subsets. The respective counts of independent four-sets are \(6,61,276,405\), and the total numbers of nonempty faces are \(389,1415,3641,4811\), in the displayed case order. The complete face vectors by dimensions zero through five are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
(3,3)&9&36&84&120&100&40\\
(3,4)&12&66&220&434&466&217\\
(3,5)&15&105&455&1089&1323&654\\
(4,4)&16&120&560&1415&1788&912.
\end{array}
\]

**Step 2: Reconstructible acyclic matchings.** Begin with all nonempty faces unmatched. For vertices \(v=0,1,\ldots,mn-1\) in row-major order, traverse the still-unmatched faces in increasing binary-integer order and match each face \(F\) not containing \(v\) with \(F\cup\{v\}\), provided both remain unmatched. This explicitly specifies every pair without a separate certificate file. It produces \(192,697,1792,2363\) pairs respectively, with unmatched cells exactly one zero-cell and \(4,20,56,84\) five-cells. To verify this is an acyclic face matching, orient each codimension-one face incidence downward except incidences belonging to the selected matching, which are oriented upward. The independent executable `verify_robust4_symmetry.py` topologically sorts these complete directed incidence graphs and confirms all vertices are visited. The respective incidence counts are \(1544,6160,16470,21992\). Forman's discrete-Morse theorem then produces a CW complex with one zero-cell and only cells of dimension five; consequently its integral homotopy type is precisely the indicated wedge of five-spheres. This step also establishes torsion-free, concentrated integral homology without borrowing an earlier certificate.

**Step 3: Signed fixed-face traces.** Every grid symmetry \(g\) permutes the vertices and hence the oriented simplicial chains. An oriented face \([v_0,\ldots,v_d]\), with the vertices in increasing index order, contributes to the trace of \(g\) on chains exactly when its vertex set is \(g\)-invariant. In that case its contribution is the sign of the induced vertex permutation. Since the only nonzero reduced homology degree is five, the simplicial Lefschetz identity gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}(g\mid\widetilde H_5(K_{m,n};\mathbb Q))
=1-\sum_{d\ge0}(-1)^d
\sum_{\substack{F\in K_{m,n},\ |F|=d+1\\gF=F}}
\operatorname{sgn}(g\mid_F).
\]
The standalone verifier reconstructs every grid automorphism, checks it preserves every graph edge, and calculates these signed sums exactly over the complete finite face posets.

**Step 4: Compare with triple-square characters.** Each symmetry also permutes the elementary squares. Directly enumerate the three-element subsets of those squares that it fixes setwise, multiply the count by \(\delta(g)\), and compare with Step 3. Exact equality holds for every element of each of the four symmetry groups, including both inequivalent reflection classes in both square cases. Since finite-dimensional rational representations of finite groups are semisimple and characterized by their ordinary characters, this establishes the displayed rational representation isomorphism. A character equality alone is **not** an explicit canonical basis or an integral lattice isomorphism.

**Step 5: The invariant subspace.** For the \(4\times4\) grid, the class values are \((84,4,0,-10,-10)\) at \((1,r^2,\{r,r^3\},\{s,r^2s\},\{rs,r^3s\})\). Their average over all eight group elements is \((84+4-20-20)/8=6\). Finite-group transfer over \(\mathbb Q\) identifies the quotient's positive-degree rational homology with invariants, establishing the quotient dimension claim.

## Relationship to prior work
Filakovský, *The Topology of \(k\)-Robust Clique Complexes in Grid-like Graphs* (arXiv:2602.11365, first posted 2026-02-11), introduced the 2026 grid-focused problem and proves the wedge structure for \(k=2,3\), together with arbitrary \(k\) under edge-only square gluing. His conjecture for corner gluing does not determine these fourth-robust symmetry representations. Chandrakar--Hazra--Rout--Singh (arXiv:2408.07646) cover total cut complexes of two-row grids for general cut size and three-row grids for cut size three, not the fourth robust grid characters considered here. Carnero Bravo (arXiv:2602.21427) studies Alexander-dual complexes for other graph families and Cartesian products chiefly for total two-cut complexes. Accessible published computations already determine the above nonequivariant homotopy types for several of these grids, and published symmetry results cover third-robust grid complexes; no novelty is claimed for those inputs. The new content is the **fourth-robust top-homology representation** and its negative reflection character, beyond what nonequivariant wedge multiplicities imply.

## Limitations
There is no theorem here for all rectangular grids, no integral equivariant module isomorphism, no canonical geometrically constructed basis indexed by square triples, and no equivariant homotopy equivalence. Exact finite acyclic Morse matchings certify just the listed four grids. The quotient statement is rational; it does not determine integral quotient homology or torsion. Equivalent unpublished or unindexed equivariant calculations remain a residual originality risk. The scientific review was limited to the available literature and exact finite certificates; independent validation has not been performed.
