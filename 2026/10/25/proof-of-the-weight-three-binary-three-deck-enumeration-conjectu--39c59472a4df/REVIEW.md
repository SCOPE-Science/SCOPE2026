# Scientific review of the weight-three binary three-deck theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The complete eight-entry deck is determined by \(N\), \(A=T_{011}\), \(B=T_{110}\) and \(F=T_{001}\). Equality of \(N,A,B\) leaves one integer parameter; equality of \(F\) imposes a quadratic equation with precisely one possible nonzero root. Nonnegative gap parametrization counts every collision once. The algebraic generating-function identity is thus proven for all lengths. The embedded code independently verifies raw subsequence counts and the exact collision classification for bounded lengths.

## Originality — PASS within examined evidence
Chrisnata et al. explicitly state the same rational generating function as *Conjecture 1* and prove an asymptotic estimate; they do not establish the conjecture. Their zero-gap nullspace result is acknowledged as prior. The proof and complete size-two fiber involution here go beyond that source. Nilforoushan–Parvaresh (2026) treat independent deck parameters and unrestricted polynomial growth rather than exact weight-three fibers. Checked mathematical research records and recoverable earlier claims exposed no covering theorem, but this does not establish absolute priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This settles a natural exact enumerative conjecture with a short algebraic classification and gives the sharp ambiguity structure and reconstruction probability in the first nontrivial fixed-weight regime. The result is an all-length theorem, not a few new coefficients. The conjectured equation itself, older computational tables and existing asymptotic estimates are not claimed as original.

## Limits and closest literature
The result applies only to binary length-three scattered-subsequence multisets for words having exactly three ones. Other weights and general \(k\)-decks remain outside scope. The main comparison sources are Chrisnata et al., *On the number of distinct k-decks: Enumeration and bounds*, Conjecture 1, DOI:10.3934/amc.2021032; and Nilforoushan–Parvaresh, *Improved upper bound on the number of distinct k-decks*, arXiv:2609.23106. An unindexed prior proof remains a historical-priority risk.
