# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim specifies the cube, Haar normal law, area normalization, type classification, and an absolute deficit. The exact area formula follows from the convolution of three independent uniform random variables; its two chamber formulas were independently verified against convex-hull areas computed from the twelve cube edges. Ball's known extremizer characterization localizes the upper tail at permutations of the two-coordinate diagonal. In a local spherical chart the area deficit is linear and piecewise homogeneous: quadrilateral \(2\sqrt2 s\) and hexagon \(\sqrt2(t+s^2/t)\). Uniform remainder estimates and comparability of the deficit to \(s+t\) justify the bounded blow-up and dominated convergence. Both planar domain integrals and the Haar multiplicity factor were recomputed. This proves the separate \(\delta^2\) constants and their limiting ratio. The numerical checker corroborates the analytic proof but cannot establish it alone.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority risk

Lonke's `math/9812123` treats mean face counts of random central cube sections, not their joint near-maximal area/type distribution. Ball's 1986 cube slicing theorem supplies the maximum, not the probability of a near-maximizer. Ambrus's `2107.14778` classifies critical directions and gives volume integral representations without the sharp two-type tail. The publicly indexed 2026 exact Haar hexagon law for rectangular boxes gives unconditional section-type probabilities, not the area-tail split; its theorem, proof and limitations were inspected. Searches for upper tails, extreme-area type splits, corner asymptotics, equivalent random-central-plane formulations, and tabulated coefficients did not reveal a covering statement. No such search proves global novelty. Unindexed or inaccessible work may contain an equivalent asymptotic; this risk remains expressly open.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Maximal sections occur exactly at a degeneracy separating four- and six-vertex polygons. Consequently a standard smooth-Hessian Laplace approximation does not recover the upper-tail measure. The computed piecewise blow-up gives both a sharp probability rate and a nonobvious conditional combinatorial law: although the optimizer is quadrilateral, hexagons contribute the majority \((2+\pi)/(4+\pi)\) of almost-maximal directions. This is a motivated quantitative refinement of classical cube slicing and random-section counting, not merely a restatement of either. The fact is specific to the cube and does not assert a general higher-dimensional theory.

The proof assumes central Haar planes and uses known cube extremizers.
