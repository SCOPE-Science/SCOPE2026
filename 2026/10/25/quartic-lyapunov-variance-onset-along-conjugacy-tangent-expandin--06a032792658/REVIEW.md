# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The proof derives the \(C^2\) normal form of the conjugated expanding maps directly, computes the second-order observable \(w'/d\) with all normalizations explicit, and uses the invariance of Green–Kubo variance under additive coboundaries. The uniform cylinder-tail estimate proves continuity of the maximal-entropy correlation series without using pointwise finite computations as an infinite proof. In the concrete degree-two family, exact Fourier correlations \(7/4\), \(0\), \(1/4\), and all later zeros give the coefficient \(9/4\). The independent checker verifies the rational arithmetic and tests the Taylor expansion at finite parameters.

## Originality

**PASS under accessible literature, not independent priority certification.** Drach–Fu–Kaloshin–Li–Liverani formulate the fixed-map variance and prove a periodic-exponent CLT, but do not present the quartic response formula or the explicit two-harmonic coefficient. Classical response results concern expectations of observables under different equilibrium measures; mean-exponent flexibility does not imply fluctuation-variance onset. Semantic research-record searches for second-order conjugacy defect, Fourier aliases, and quartic Green–Kubo variance found no covering claim. A general pressure or perturbation theorem might imply the abstract continuity argument and remains the main priority risk.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Near a rigid integer covering, Lyapunov fluctuations degenerate, while generic perturbations create variance at quadratic order. Tangency to a smooth-conjugacy direction creates a natural, mathematically motivated cancellation; the theorem computes the next nonzero order through an explicit geometric obstruction. The nonzero exact \(9/4\) coefficient proves a genuine quartic onset and distinguishes parameter-wise approximate rigidity from actual smooth conjugacy. This quantifies how the normalization in the recent periodic Lyapunov CLT degenerates and supplies an exact benchmark for perturbative pressure/variance calculations.
