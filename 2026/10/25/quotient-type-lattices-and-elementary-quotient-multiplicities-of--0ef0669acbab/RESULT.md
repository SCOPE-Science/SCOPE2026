# Quotient-type lattices and elementary quotient multiplicities of finite generalized dihedral groups

## Finding
Let \(A\) be any finite abelian group (including the trivial group), and let \(G=\operatorname{Dih}(A)=A\rtimes C_2\), with inversion action. Let \(\mathcal Q(G)\) be the set of isomorphism classes of quotients of \(G\), ordered by subgroup embeddability. Then
\[
\mathcal Q(G)\cong\{\mathbf 1\}\oplus \mathcal T(A),
\]
where \(\mathcal T(A)\) is the poset of isomorphism classes of subgroups of \(A\), ordered by embeddability, and \(\oplus\) means adjoining a new least point. The type \(B\in\mathcal T(A)\) corresponds to \(\operatorname{Dih}(B)\), and \(\mathbf 1\) corresponds to the trivial quotient.

Write \(A_p\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s_p}C_{p^{\lambda_{p,i}}}\), with \(\lambda_{p,1}\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_{p,s_p}>0\). Let \(\mathcal P(\lambda_p)\) denote the integer partitions \(\mu\) whose parts, padded with zeros, satisfy \(0\le\mu_i\le\lambda_{p,i}\). Then
\[
\mathcal Q(G)\cong\{\mathbf 1\}\oplus\prod_{p\mid |A|}\mathcal P(\lambda_p),\qquad
|\mathcal Q(G)|=1+\prod_{p\mid |A|}|\mathcal P(\lambda_p)|.
\]
In particular \(\mathcal Q(G)\) is a finite distributive lattice. Under the reversed embedding convention defining the quotient-isomorphism poset \(\operatorname{QIso}(G)\), the order is dual and hence is also distributive. Every quotient of \(G\) embeds in \(G\): the group is quotient-self-dual.

Moreover, put \(d=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}(A/2A)\). For every integer \(1\le r\le d+1\), the **exact number of distinct normal subgroups** \(N\trianglelefteq G\) for which \(G/N\cong(C_2)^r\) equals
\[
M_r(G)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{d}{r-1}_2+2^r\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{d}{r}_2,
\]
with out-of-range Gaussian binomial coefficients defined to be zero. The trivial quotient arises from exactly one normal subgroup. For \(A=C_4\oplus C_2\), there are six quotient isomorphism types, and \(M_1=M_2=7\), \(M_3=1\).

## Assumptions and scope
All groups are finite. The inversion semidirect product is defined by multiplication \((a,t)(b,u)=(a+(-1)^tb,t+u\pmod2)\), in additive notation on \(A\). We define \(\operatorname{Dih}(\{0\})=C_2\). Embeddability of finite groups means existence of an injective group homomorphism, not containment of fixed representatives. All Sylow partitions and Gaussian coefficients are exact, and the empty product (when \(A\) is trivial) is one.

## Proof
**1. Quotients.** Write \(G=A\sqcup As\), where \(s^2=1\) and \(sas=a^{-1}\). For any normal subgroup \(N\), set \(H=N\cap A\). If \(N\subseteq A\), then \(G/N\cong\operatorname{Dih}(A/H)\); conversely every \(H\le A\) is normal in \(G\), since inversion preserves subgroups.

If \(N\) meets \(As\), choose \(as\in N\). Conjugating by every \(x\in A\) shows \((a+2x)s\in N\), hence \(2A\subseteq H\). Because the projection \(N\to G/A=C_2\) is surjective, \(G/N\cong A/H\), an elementary abelian \(2\)-group of rank \(r\le d\). For every such \(r\ge1\), there exists a quotient \((C_2)^{r-1}\) of \(A\), and \((C_2)^r\cong\operatorname{Dih}((C_2)^{r-1})\). Thus all nontrivial quotient types, including those arising from reflection-containing kernels, are exactly \(\operatorname{Dih}(B)\) with \(B\) an abelian quotient type of \(A\). The trivial quotient is separate. A finite abelian group has the same subgroup and quotient types, by the elementary divisor theorem (or duality).

**2. Rigidity of embedding.** We prove for finite abelian \(B,C\) that
\[
\operatorname{Dih}(B)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Dih}(C)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B\hookrightarrow C.
\]
The reverse implication extends an embedding of \(B\) into \(C\) by sending the involutory inversion generator to that of \(\operatorname{Dih}(C)\). For the forward implication, suppose first that \(B\) has an element of order greater than two. In \(\operatorname{Dih}(B)\), every element outside \(B\) is an involution, and the subgroup generated by all elements of order greater than two equals \(B\): an involution \(b\in B\) is the product of two non-involutions \(a\) and \(b-a\), for any fixed non-involution \(a\in B\). Consequently any injection into \(\operatorname{Dih}(C)\) takes \(B\) into \(C\). If instead \(B=(C_2)^e\), then \(\operatorname{Dih}(B)=(C_2)^{e+1}\). Every elementary abelian subgroup of \(\operatorname{Dih}(C)\) intersects \(C\) in a subgroup of \(C[2]\), and its image under the quotient map onto \(C_2\) has rank at most one. Thus \(e+1\le1+\dim_{\mathbb F_2}C[2]\), which is equivalent to \(B\hookrightarrow C\). This proves rigidity and the uniqueness of all labels \(B\).

**3. Lattice.** Finite abelian subgroup types are exactly the partitions \(\mu_{p,i}\le\lambda_{p,i}\), with all \(\mu_p\) nonincreasing. The componentwise minimum and maximum of any two such partitions remain nonincreasing and bounded by \(\lambda_p\). Therefore each \(\mathcal P(\lambda_p)\), and their direct product, is a distributive lattice. Adjoining a new least element preserves distributivity. Embedding rigidity identifies this lattice with the quotient-type poset in the stated direction. The reversed convention is its order dual. As every \(B\le A\) gives \(\operatorname{Dih}(B)\le G\), every quotient of \(G\) embeds in \(G\).

**4. Multiplicity.** For \(r\ge1\), normal subgroups \(N\subseteq A\) with \(G/N\cong(C_2)^r\) have \(A/N\cong(C_2)^{r-1}\): they correspond to subspaces of codimension \(r-1\) in \(A/2A\), numbering \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{d}{r-1}_2\). Normal subgroups meeting \(As\) have the form \(N=H\sqcup H(as)\), where \(H\ge2A\) and \(a\in A/H\). Conversely, every such pair defines a normal subgroup, since conjugation by \(A\) changes \(as\) by an element of \(2A\), and conjugation by \(s\) sends \(a\) to \(-a\), which is congruent to \(a\) modulo \(H\). For \(G/N\cong(C_2)^r\), there are \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{d}{r}_2\) choices of \(H\) and \(2^r\) choices of \(a+H\), giving the second term. The two cases are disjoint and exhaustive. The only kernel of the trivial quotient is \(N=G\), which does not enter the positive-rank count.

## Relationship to prior work
Halder and Ballal, *Classes of Subgroups of a finite group with isomorphic quotient groups* (arXiv:2610.13076, first public 2026-10-09), define the quotient-isomorphism poset, show finite abelian cases are distributive (Theorem 2.1), and prove the ordinary cyclic-kernel dihedral formula \(|\operatorname{QIso}(D_n)|=\tau(n)+1\) (Theorem 2.3). The cyclic case here is therefore **not new**. The present result treats every finite abelian rotation kernel, including noncyclic and even-primary kernels, proves an embedding-rigidity description of the whole poset, and adds exact multiplicities of all elementary abelian quotient fibers. No claim is made that the older abelian classification or the cyclic special case is new.

## Limitations
The statement concerns only generalized dihedral groups with inversion action, not arbitrary semidirect products. The subgroup lattice itself need not be distributive; only its quotient-isomorphism type poset is classified. Finite computations are checks, not a substitute for proof. Earlier results phrased through general normal-subgroup or subgroup-type theory might imply parts of the theorem; an independent originality audit has not been performed.
