# Rank-one weight-zero Rota–Baxter operators on full matrix algebras: structure and exact counts

## Finding

For every field \(k\) and integer \(n\ge 2\), let \(A=M_n(k)\). A weight-zero Rota–Baxter operator means a \(k\)-linear map \(R:A\to A\) satisfying
\[
R(X)R(Y)=R(R(X)Y+XR(Y))\qquad(X,Y\in A).
\]
Every nonzero such map of **operator rank one** belongs to exactly one of the following two mutually exclusive families, where \(f\in A^*\) is nonzero.

**Square-zero image family.** There is a nonzero \(B\in A\) with \(B^2=0\) and
\[
R(X)=f(X)B,\qquad f(BA)=f(AB)=0.
\]
Here \(BA=\{BX:X\in A\}\) and \(AB=\{XB:X\in A\}\); the two displayed conditions mean that \(f\) annihilates these subspaces. Necessarily \(1\le\operatorname{rank}B\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). Two pairs \((B,f)\) represent the same operator precisely when \((B,f)\) is replaced by \((sB,s^{-1}f)\) for some \(s\in k^\times\).

**Idempotent image family.** There is a uniquely determined nontrivial idempotent \(P\in A\) and an \(f\in A^*\) with
\[
R(X)=f(X)P,
\]
where exactly one of the following conditions holds:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&f(PX)=f(X),&&f(XP)=0\quad\text{for every }X\in A;\\
\text{or }&f(PX)=0,&&f(XP)=f(X)\quad\text{for every }X\in A.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(1\le\operatorname{rank}P\le n-1\). The two nonzero functional spaces are disjoint. Every operator in **both** families obeys \(R^2=0\), over fields of arbitrary characteristic.

For \(k=\mathbb F_q\), let \({a\brack b}_q\) denote a Gaussian binomial coefficient and \(G_r(q)=\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(q^r-q^j)=|\operatorname{GL}_r(\mathbb F_q)|\). The exact number of nonzero rank-one weight-zero Rota–Baxter operators is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
T_n(q)=\sum_{r=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
\frac{{n\brack r}_q{n-r\brack r}_qG_r(q)}{q-1}
\bigl(q^{(n-r)^2}-1\bigr)
+2\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}{n\brack r}_q q^{r(n-r)}
\bigl(q^{r(n-r)}-1\bigr).}
\]
The first sum is the square-zero family, stratified by matrix rank \(r\), and the second is the idempotent family, stratified by projector rank \(r\). In particular
\[
T_2(q)=(q^2-1)(2q+1),\qquad T_3(2)=651,\qquad T_3(3)=7904.
\]
For fixed \(n\ge3\), as \(q\to\infty\) through prime powers, \(T_n(q)=q^{n^2-2}(1+O_n(q^{-1}))\), and asymptotically all counted operators have square-zero image generated by a rank-one square-zero matrix. For \(n=2\), the idempotent-image family instead supplies the highest-degree term \(2q^3\).

## Assumptions and scope

Operator rank refers to the \(k\)-vector-space dimension of \(\operatorname{im}R\), not the rank of an individual image matrix. The algebra is the full unital associative matrix algebra, and the Rota–Baxter weight is exactly zero. The structure theorem is valid over any field; the counting and asymptotic statements require the finite field \(\mathbb F_q\). The zero operator is excluded, so the total number of rank-at-most-one operators satisfying the identity is \(T_n(q)+1\). No assertion is made concerning operators of image dimension at least two.

## Proof

Take a nonzero operator of rank one and write \(R(X)=f(X)B\), with \(f\ne0\), \(B\ne0\). Inserting this into the Rota–Baxter identity gives
\[
f(X)f(Y)B^2=\bigl(f(X)f(BY)+f(Y)f(XB)\bigr)B.
\tag{1}
\]
Choose \(X_0\) with \(f(X_0)=1\). Equation (1) at \(X=Y=X_0\) implies \(B^2=tB\) for a scalar \(t\in k\). If \(f(Y)=0\), equation (1) at \(X=X_0\) gives \(f(BY)=0\); likewise, if \(f(X)=0\), then \(f(XB)=0\). Thus the linear functionals \(f\circ L_B\) and \(f\circ R_B\) are scalar multiples \(\alpha f\) and \(\beta f\), respectively, and (1) is equivalent to
\[
\alpha+\beta=t,\qquad f(BX)=\alpha f(X),\quad f(XB)=\beta f(X).
\tag{2}
\]
From \(B^2=tB\), applying each side of these last two identities twice gives \(\alpha^2=t\alpha\) and \(\beta^2=t\beta\). If \(t=0\), field integrality implies \(\alpha=\beta=0\), giving the square-zero family. Conversely these annihilation conditions make both sides of (1) zero.

If \(t\ne0\), then \(\alpha,\beta\in\{0,t\}\), and their sum is \(t\), so exactly one equals \(t\). This conclusion remains valid in characteristic two. Normalize \(P=t^{-1}B\) and replace \(f\) by \(tf\); then \(P^2=P\), and (2) gives exactly the two stated functional types. Conversely either type satisfies (1). Uniqueness of the idempotent follows because the only nonzero idempotent in a one-dimensional line spanned by an idempotent is the given projector. In both families \(f(B)=0\), or respectively \(f(P)=0\), by substituting the identity matrix in the vanishing functional condition; therefore \(R^2=0\).

For the first finite-field sum, fix \(r\in\{1,\ldots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\}\). A square-zero \(B\) of matrix rank \(r\) is specified by \(U=\operatorname{im}B\), \(K=\ker B\) with \(U\subseteq K\), and an isomorphism \(k^n/K\to U\). There are \({n\brack r}_q\) choices for \(U\), \({n-r\brack r}_q\) for \(K\) after choosing \(U\), and \(G_r(q)\) for the isomorphism. The spaces \(BA\) and \(AB\) each have dimension \(rn\), while their intersection has dimension \(r^2\). Their sum therefore has codimension \((n-r)^2\) in \(A\), and exactly \(q^{(n-r)^2}-1\) nonzero functionals annihilate it. Each operator is represented by precisely \(q-1\) pairs related by simultaneous rescaling. This proves the first sum.

For the second sum, fix projector rank \(r\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\). Its image is an \(r\)-space, and it projects along any chosen complement; hence the number of such idempotents is \({n\brack r}_q q^{r(n-r)}\). With respect to the decomposition \(k^n=\operatorname{im}P\oplus\ker P\), the two allowed functional spaces are the duals of the upper-right and lower-left off-diagonal matrix blocks, each of dimension \(r(n-r)\). They meet only in zero. Each projector thus admits \(2(q^{r(n-r)}-1)\) nonzero choices for \(f\). Different normalized idempotents yield different operators. This proves the second sum.

For asymptotics, the rank-\(r\) square-zero summand has degree \(n^2-r^2-1\) in \(q\); the rank-\(r\) idempotent summand has degree \(3r(n-r)\). For \(n\ge3\), the square-zero rank-one degree \(n^2-2\) strictly exceeds the degree of every other summand, including \(3\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor\). Its leading coefficient is one. For \(n=2\), the second sum has leading term \(2q^3\), proving the stated transition.

## Verification

The included standalone `check.py` uses only the Python standard library. Execute `python check.py`. It enumerates all nonzero decomposable linear-map tensors \((B,f)\) over \(M_2(\mathbb F_2)\) and \(M_2(\mathbb F_3)\), directly tests the bilinear Rota–Baxter identity on each pair of matrix units, deduplicates scalar-equivalent presentations, and obtains respectively \(15\) and \(56\) valid operators. It also screens all \(261121\) nonzero pairs in \(M_3(\mathbb F_2)\), eliminates impossible image matrices by their squared-value condition, and tests the full bilinear identity for each remaining pair. The exact count is \(651\), split as \(315\) square-zero-image and \(336\) idempotent-image operators. The last program line is `RANK_ONE_RB_OK`. These finite verifications are checks, not substitutes for the all-field proof.

## Relationship to prior work

The weight-zero Rota–Baxter identity and explicit low-dimensional classifications were studied in Guo–Rosenkranz–Zheng (first arXiv posting 2014-02-15) and Tang–Zhang–Sun (2014). In particular, Tang–Zhang–Sun classify weight-zero operators on \(M_2(\mathbb C)\), and Benito–Gubarev–Pozhidaev (2018, Theorem 5.2) present the four complex-field conjugacy types, including the two rank-one image types. Those works therefore cover the algebraically closed \(n=2\) classification, not the all-size finite-field rank-one count. Gubarev (arXiv:1805.00723, Theorem 3.4 and Section 5.4) gives the general correspondence with associative Yang–Baxter solutions and nilpotency bounds over characteristic zero; it does not state this rank-one finite-field enumeration. The present result supplies a uniform rank-one parametrization over arbitrary characteristic, a closed Gaussian-binomial census for every \(n\), and the dimension-two/three asymptotic change. These claims do not assert novelty for the already classified complex \(n=2\) case.

## Limitations

The full rank-two-and-above Rota–Baxter variety remains unclassified. The counts concern actual linear maps, not orbits under algebra automorphisms. The comparison with Tang–Zhang–Sun used its bibliographic abstract and the explicit independent reproduction of its theorem in Benito–Gubarev–Pozhidaev; the original full text was not accessible. No claim of absolute publication priority or independent validation is made. In particular, literature not found by the bounded searches may cover the counting formula.
