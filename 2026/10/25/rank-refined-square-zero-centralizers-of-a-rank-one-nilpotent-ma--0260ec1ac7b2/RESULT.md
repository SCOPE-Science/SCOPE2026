# Rank-refined square-zero centralizers of a rank-one nilpotent matrix

## Finding

Fix a finite field \(k=\mathbb F_q\), with \(q\) an arbitrary prime power, and an integer \(b\geq2\). Let \(N\) be any rank-one square-zero endomorphism of a \((b+2)\)-dimensional \(k\)-space. For a commuting square-zero endomorphism \(X\), the induced endomorphism \(\bar X\) of \(\ker N/\operatorname{im}N\) is also square-zero. The joint distribution of \(r=\operatorname{rank}\bar X\) and \(R=\operatorname{rank}X\) is exactly determined below.

For each \(0\le r\le\lfloor b/2\rfloor\), put \(s=b-2r\) and
\[
M_{b,r}(q)={b\brack r}_q{b-r\brack r}_q\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(q^r-q^i),
\]
where the brackets are Gaussian binomial coefficients, and an empty product is one. Then the number of \(X\) with the specified quotient rank \(r\) and ordinary rank \(R\) is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
R&\#X\\ \hline
r&M_{b,r}(q)q^{2r}\\
r+1&M_{b,r}(q)q^{2r}(q-1+2q(q^s-1))\\
r+2&M_{b,r}(q)q^{2r+1}(q^s-1)(q^{s-1}-1)\quad(s\ge1).
\end{array}
\]
For \(s=0\), the last entry is zero. No other ordinary ranks occur. In particular, the rank-generating polynomial is
\[
P_{b,q}(z)=\sum_{r=0}^{\lfloor b/2\rfloor}M_{b,r}(q)q^{2r}z^r\left(1+[q-1+2q(q^s-1)]z+\mathbf 1_{s\ge1}q(q^s-1)(q^{s-1}-1)z^2\right),\quad s=b-2r.
\]
Its value at \(z=1\), the total square-zero centralizer count, is
\[
P_{b,q}(1)=\sum_{r=0}^{\lfloor b/2\rfloor}M_{b,r}(q)\left[q^{2b-2r}+(q-1)q^b\right].
\]
Thus the count for every possible square-zero Jordan type is explicit: rank \(R\) means Jordan partition \((2^R,1^{b+2-2R})\). The quotient rank additionally distinguishes intersections hidden by an unrefined Jordan-type count.

## Assumptions and scope

All matrices and dimensions are over \(\mathbb F_q\), with no restriction on characteristic; \(b\ge2\). Conjugating \(N\) does not alter the number of commuting \(X\) nor the quotient-rank statistic. Choose a direct decomposition \(V=U\oplus W\oplus T\) with \(\dim U=\dim T=1\), \(\dim W=b\), and \(N(T)=U\), while \(N(U\oplus W)=0\). Then the quotient \(\ker N/\operatorname{im}N\) canonically identifies with \(W\). We count **labeled matrices** \(X\), not similarity classes or orbit representatives. Here \(X^2=0\) includes \(X=0\).

## Proof

**Step 1: centralizer normal form.** Matrix multiplication with the displayed \(N\) forces every \(XN=NX\) to have a unique block form
\[
X=\begin{pmatrix}a&f&c\\0&B&g\\0&0&a\end{pmatrix},
\]
where \(a,c\in k\), \(B\in\operatorname{End}_k(W)\), \(f\in W^*\) and \(g\in W\). The top-right block of \(X^2\) is \(2ac+f(g)\), whereas diagonal blocks are \(a^2,B^2,a^2\). Since a field has no nonzero square-zero scalars, \(X^2=0\) forces \(a=0\), and then is equivalent to
\[
B^2=0,\qquad fB=0,\qquad Bg=0,\qquad f(g)=0.
\]
The scalar \(c\) is unrestricted. This conclusion holds in characteristic two as well as odd characteristic.

**Step 2: count the quotient maps.** Fix \(r=\operatorname{rank}B\). Put \(I=\operatorname{im}B\subseteq K=\ker B\). Their dimensions are \(r\) and \(b-r\), and the quotient \(H=K/I\) has dimension \(s=b-2r\). Conversely, a square-zero \(B\) of rank \(r\) is uniquely specified by a flag \(I\subseteq K\subseteq W\) of those dimensions and an isomorphism \(W/K\to I\). The number is
\[
{b\brack r}_q{b-r\brack r}_q\lvert\operatorname{GL}_r(q)\rvert=M_{b,r}(q),
\]
including \(r=0\).

**Step 3: count the covector and vector constraints.** Because \(fB=0\), the covector \(f\) vanishes on \(I\) and restricts on \(K\) to a unique \(\phi\in H^*\). Every \(\phi\) has \(q^r\) lifts \(f\): outside \(K\) the covector has \(r\) free coordinates. Because \(Bg=0\), the vector \(g\) belongs to \(K\), and its image \(h\in H\) has \(q^r\) lifts \(g\). The remaining condition is precisely \(\phi(h)=0\). The scalar \(c\) contributes \(q\) possibilities.

**Step 4: determine rank rather than merely count solutions.** The image of \(X\), inside \(U\oplus W\), is generated by \(\{(f(w),Bw):w\in W\}\) and \((c,g)\). If \(\phi\ne0\), the first set spans a space of dimension \(r+1\); if \(\phi=0\), its span has dimension \(r\). In the first case \(h=0\) yields total rank \(r+1\) for every \(c\), while \(h\ne0\) yields total rank \(r+2\). In the second case \(h\ne0\) yields rank \(r+1\) for every \(c\). Finally, when \(\phi=h=0\), one has \(f=\ell\circ B\), \(g=Bv\), for some \(\ell\) and \(v\); the last column belongs to the previous image for exactly one value of \(c\), and therefore the other \(q-1\) values increase rank from \(r\) to \(r+1\).

For \(\phi=h=0\), the fiber sizes are \(q^{2r}\) of rank \(r\), and \((q-1)q^{2r}\) of rank \(r+1\). For exactly one of \(\phi,h\) nonzero, there are \(2(q^s-1)\) choices of their classes; all \(q^{2r+1}\) lifts/scalars have rank \(r+1\). For both nonzero with \(\phi(h)=0\), choose \(h\ne0\) in \(q^s-1\) ways, and then a nonzero \(\phi\) in the annihilator of \(h\) in \(q^{s-1}-1\) ways. Every one of their \(q^{2r+1}\) lifts/scalars has rank \(r+2\). The last situation is impossible for \(s=0\) or \(s=1\). Multiplying by the count \(M_{b,r}(q)\) proves all rank formulas.

**Step 5: total.** Summing three rank possibilities at fixed \(r\), with the \(s=0\) boundary interpreted directly, yields \(M_{b,r}(q)[q^{2b-2r}+(q-1)q^b]\). This proves the displayed total. There are no omitted square-zero maps because Step 1 is an if-and-only-if normal form and all fields/quotient dimensions and rank possibilities were exhausted.

## Verification

The included `verification/verify.py` enumerates all admissible blocks \(B,f,g,c\) directly and computes the two ranks by finite-field Gaussian elimination, independently of the flag-count expression. The exact computed totals at \((q,b)=(2,2),(2,3),(3,2),(4,2)\) are respectively \(44,576,315,1264\). The run also checks four smaller boundary cases, including \(b=1\) as a comparison with the previously understood one-extra-block instance. Enumeration is a finite cross-check; the proof above establishes all prime powers and all \(b\ge2\).

## Relationship to prior work

Oblak's initial study of commuting nilpotent Jordan forms (2007) addresses permissible partitions and maximal nilpotency indices; it does not supply finite-field rank-fiber multiplicities. Britnell and Wildon (2010 preprint) determine when similarity classes contain commuting representatives, including nilpotent cases, but their existence criterion does not enumerate every representative for a fixed \(N\). Oblak (2011) proves that a square-zero nilpotent commutant meets every nilpotent orbit over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field, again an existence statement. Košir and Oblak (2007 preprint) study generic simultaneous Jordan orbits and the geometry of commuting nilpotents. The result here is the exact **arbitrary finite-field**, quotient-rank-refined labeled count in the rank-one \(N\) family. Separate research on square-zero centralizers with \(N=J_n\oplus J_1\) addresses a one-extra-block subfamily, not the present \(b\ge2\) formula. These comparisons establish a concrete gap, not definitive publication priority.

## Limitations

This theorem is a finite-field labeled-matrix count; it does not classify simultaneous-similarity orbits, all ranks of the fixed \(N\), or the scheme structure of the rank strata. Standard flag/Hall-polynomial methods might in principle imply some or all of these formulas without identical wording. No claim of independently verified publication priority or independent mathematical audit is made.
