# Scientific review: effective Kripke-representability recognition

**Correctness — PASS.** The upper bound is a constructive semidecision algorithm for *failure* of KRep: enumerate the nonmember formula and one excluded theory formula per falsifying world. Finiteness of the actual carrier is exactly what makes each certificate finite. The lower bound maps any Turing program to a one-world closure model using a uniformly primitive-recursive theory-membership algorithm; structural induction on modal formulas and bounded halting simulation establish effectiveness. The all-valuation argument proves the frame-level (G) and (E) conditions, while the intersection-of-theories argument proves (M), (C), (N), (K). The model admits a reflexive one-world Kripke representation exactly when the program never halts; after halting, the empty and looping one-world accessibility relations are both separated by explicit formulas. No computational run claims to prove arbitrary nonhalting.

**Originality — PASS within inspected evidence.** Nishimura's October 2026 manuscript defines KRep and proves its representation theorem, including a countably infinite normality-versus-representation counterexample; the inspected introduction, defining section and representation proofs do not identify the decision complexity of recognizing KRep for *finite effectively presented* models. The present one-world, fully normal, primitive-recursive hardness statement neither follows merely from the previously exhibited countable counterexample nor asserts its novelty. Rudolph's 2017 paper concerns closure representations over *finite ground sets* (only abstract inspected), not infinite formula-membership procedures; Chen and Takahashi's 2026 theorem studies properties of finitely axiomatized tense logics (abstract inspected), not closure-model index sets. Closest available published semantic-comparison records address other kinds of Kripke or finite algebraic representation; none retrieved supplied this exact promised problem or its \(\Pi^0_1\) classification. A semantic theorem under different coding or a general effective-representation theorem remains a priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** The canonical representation criterion for a newly developed semantic framework has an exact algorithmic boundary. The upper bound says finite models have effectively enumerable finite certificates of failure; the sharp one-world lower bound shows that even standard normality, standard closure-operator regularity, and primitive-recursive membership do not decide success. The distinction between finite worlds and finitely enumerated modal theories directly affects any implementation of the representation criterion and rules out an otherwise natural finite-state decision assumption.

The infinite language, fixed-carrier notion and total membership-predicate input model are essential scope restrictions. No claim is made about explicitly finite formula tables, finite propositional alphabets, or representations permitted to add worlds. The full text of possible older work on index-set complexity was not exhaustively accessible; independent expert checking would improve priority confidence. The checker ran 44,624 finite diagnostic assertions, which cannot decide the infinite cases.
