# Reflection-fixed loci of pure strong Morse cycle complexes are two simplices

## Finding
Let \(n\ge6\), let \(C_n\) be the simplicial cycle with vertices \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), and let \(X_n=\mathcal{SM}_{\mathrm{pure}}(C_n)\) be its **pure strong discrete Morse matching complex**. Each reflection \(s_a(i)=a-i\pmod n\) induces an involution on \(X_n\). Put \(m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). The geometric fixed-point spaces, with their natural simplicial structures obtained by orbit barycenters, are
\[
\boxed{|X_{2m+1}|^{s_a}\cong\Delta^{m-2}\sqcup\Delta^{m-2}\quad(m\ge3),}
\]
\[
\boxed{|X_{2m}|^{s_a}\cong
\begin{cases}
\Delta^{m-2}\sqcup\Delta^{m-2},&a\equiv0\pmod2,\\
\Delta^{m-3}\sqcup\Delta^{m-3},&a\equiv1\pmod2,
\end{cases}\quad(m\ge3).}
\]
Here \(\Delta^d\) denotes a **closed standard simplex**, not its boundary. Thus every such reflection fixes **exactly two contractible connected components**, with ordinary fixed-point Euler characteristic \(2\). The induced reduced homological Lefschetz number on \(X_n\) is \(1\).

## Assumptions and scope
The vertices of \(X_n\) are the two incidences \((i,b)\), \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), \(b\in\{0,1\}\), joining edge \(e_i=\{i,i+1\}\) to endpoint \(i+b\). The complex is generated by maximal strong discrete Morse matchings obtained by taking a path of \(n-1\) vertices, deleting one cycle vertex, choosing a root on the path, and matching each other path vertex to the incident edge directed away from the root. This is the **pure** subcomplex, not the full strong or ordinary Morse-matching complex. The reflection acts on incidence vertices by
\[
s_a(i,b)=(a-i-1,1-b).
\]
All indices are taken modulo \(n\). The fixed point set is pointwise fixed in the **geometric realization**, rather than a subcomplex on individually fixed incidence vertices (there are no such vertices).

## Proof
**1. Explicit facets.** For every gap vertex \(j\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), list the remaining path vertices in positive cyclic order as \(p_k=j+1+k\pmod n\) for \(0\le k\le n-2\). For a root index \(0\le r\le n-2\), the facet is
\[
F_{j,r}=\{(p_{k-1},0):1\le k\le r\}\;\cup\;\{(p_{k-1},1):r<k\le n-2\}.
\]
Every facet has \(n-2\) incidences and exactly two consecutive absent edges \(e_{j-1},e_j\); on the other edges, the incidence bits have one block of zeros followed by one block of ones in their cyclic order. There are \(n(n-1)\) facets, by the rooted-path characterization (Knudson--Owens--White, Proposition 8.3).

**2. Orbit-simplex realization lemma.** Since \(s_a\) has no fixed incidence vertex, all its incidence orbits have size two. A point of \(|X_n|\) is fixed by \(s_a\) precisely when it is supported on an invariant face and has equal barycentric weights at the two vertices in every selected orbit. Thus its fixed-point space is naturally the realization of a simplicial complex with one vertex for each incidence orbit whose union is a face, and whose faces are collections of such orbits with jointly admissible union. The equivalence is a piecewise-linear homeomorphism, not just a homology equivalence.

**3. Vertex-axis reflections and odd cycles.** First take \(s_0\). Write \(m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), and define two kinds of size-two incidence orbits:
\[
A_i=\{(i,0),(n-1-i,1)\},\qquad
B_i=\{(i,1),(n-1-i,0)\}.
\]
The invariant-orbit complex has precisely two maximal faces:
\[
\boxed{\mathcal A=\{A_1,A_2,\ldots,A_{m-1}\},\qquad
\mathcal B=\{B_0,B_1,\ldots,B_{m-2}\}.}
\]
These collections are admissible: \(\mathcal A\) lies in facet \(F_{0,m-1}\), while \(\mathcal B\) lies in \(F_{m,m-1}\) when \(n=2m\), and in \(F_{m,m}\) when \(n=2m+1\). Direct substitution into the displayed facet formula verifies all incidences.

For necessity, every reflection orbit has one entry in the first half of the edge list, except for the central edge in the odd case. The omitted orbit \(A_0\) uses two incidences matching the same vertex \(0\). In the even case \(B_{m-1}\) matches vertex \(m\) twice. In the odd case \(B_{m-1}\) selects incidence bit \(1\) on \(e_{m-1}\) and bit \(0\) on \(e_{m+1}\), with only \(e_m\) between them: this is the forbidden bit transition from \(1\) to \(0\) without **two** missing consecutive gap edges. Both incidences of the odd central edge \(e_m\) cannot appear together because one edge cannot be matched twice. No further reflection orbit can therefore be present in any facet.

A mixture of \(A_i\) and \(B_j\), where \(i=j\), selects both ends of the same edge. If \(i\ne j\), the four forced incidence bits alternate \(0,1,0,1\) or \(1,0,1,0\) around the cycle. No facet has four cyclic bit alternations: its bits form a single zero block and a single one block, interrupted by just one gap of two consecutive missing edges. Hence a face cannot contain both kinds. Every subcollection of \(\mathcal A\) or of \(\mathcal B\) is admissible by the displayed containing facets. The orbit complex is consequently precisely the disjoint union of two \((m-2)\)-simplices.

**4. Edge-axis reflections of even cycles.** Let \(n=2m\) and \(s_1(i)=1-i\). Its fixed underlying edges are \(e_0,e_m\); the two incidences of each fixed edge constitute one orbit, which is forbidden in a matching. Define
\[
A_i=\{(i,0),(n-i,1)\},\qquad B_i=\{(i,1),(n-i,0)\}.
\]
The **only** maximal collections of admissible incidence orbits are
\[
\boxed{\mathcal A'=\{A_2,\ldots,A_{m-1}\},\qquad
\mathcal B'=\{B_1,\ldots,B_{m-2}\}.}
\]
Again \(\mathcal A'\subset F_{0,m-1}\) and \(\mathcal B'\subset F_{m,m-1}\). The remaining boundary orbit \(A_1\) has a bit-\(0\) incidence on \(e_1\) and a bit-\(1\) incidence on \(e_{n-1}\), separated across \(e_0\) by only one potentially missing edge. Similarly the remaining \(B_{m-1}\) forces the forbidden reverse transition across only \(e_m\). The same four-bit alternation or double-use-of-an-edge argument excludes mixing the two orbit families. The orbit complex is therefore two disjoint \((m-3)\)-simplices.

**5. Other axes and Lefschetz.** Conjugation by cycle rotation sends \(s_a\) to \(s_{a+2t}\), so all odd-length reflections are conjugate and, for even lengths, the two parity classes above exhaust the possibilities. The fixed-set descriptions apply to every reflection. The ordinary fixed-point Euler characteristic is \(2\). By the simplicial Lefschetz fixed-point formula (or the oriented fixed-face trace after barycentric subdivision), the unreduced homological Lefschetz number equals \(2\); subtracting the degree-zero augmentation contribution gives the reduced Lefschetz number \(1\).

## Verification
The standalone standard-library program `verify.py` builds **all** \(n(n-1)\) rooted-path facets independently from the stated definition, computes all incidence orbits under each reflection type, and enumerates the maximal invariant orbit subsets. It checks that the exact maximal orbit subsets are precisely the displayed families, not merely that they have the right cardinalities. Exhaustive checks cover every \(6\le n\le40\), both axis types for even \(n\), and the odd conjugacy class, a total of \(53\) parameter/axis cases. These finite checks corroborate the explicit all-length proof and are not used as a substitute for it.

## Relationship to prior work
Knudson and Owens--White (arXiv:2609.06844v1, first public 2026-09-06; primary MSC 57Q70) define the pure strong complex and classify its ordinary homotopy types and maximal facets; their inspected Section 8 does not determine reflection-fixed geometric loci. The prior result *The threefold rotation character of pure strong Morse matching complexes of cycles* computes **rotation** Lefschetz traces on this same pure complex and explicitly leaves reflections untreated. A separate result, *Dihedral Euler characters of full strong Morse complexes of cycles*, computes virtual characters for the **full** complex, a different simplicial space; its variable reflection traces do not imply these fixed-point loci. Chari--Joswig's older ordinary discrete Morse construction differs from the strong complex. Knowing an abstract nonequivariant homotopy type or a rotation character cannot determine the pointwise fixed-set homeomorphism types.

## Limitations
This proves fixed-point **topology**, not a complete equivariant homotopy classification of \(X_n\). It does not compute reflection actions on individual integral homology groups or fixed spaces of non-reflection dihedral subgroups. The dimensions of the two contractible components depend on the reflection-axis conjugacy class; the common Euler value alone would not detect this distinction. Literature comparison cannot rule out unindexed or unpublished equivalent descriptions.
