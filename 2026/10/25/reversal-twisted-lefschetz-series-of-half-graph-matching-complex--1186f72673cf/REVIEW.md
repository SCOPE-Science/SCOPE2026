# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The identification of half-graph matchings with directed paths of the transitive tournament, hence with set partitions, is bijective in every size. Reversal-invariant partitions split uniquely into self-reversed blocks, exchanged block pairs, and at most one central block; their signed arc-permutation weights give the two labelled exponential series. Augmented Hopf trace and the finite-group Euler averaging formula then prove the stated Lefschetz and quotient identities. Both orientation signs and the empty-simplex normalization were independently checked by exact exhaustive enumeration for \(1\leq n\leq9\). The finite check is corroboration; the symbolic decomposition proves the infinite family.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The closest primary works, arXiv:2208.04656 (Theorems 3.4 and 5.1) and arXiv:2401.01248 (Theorems 4.20 and 4.23), respectively provide nonequivariant Stirling-number Euler/homotopy information and the half-graph matching-complex identification. Neither inspected source computes the *signed graded reversal trace* or its quotient Euler characteristic. The most relevant public-index comparisons concern different graph families (rectangular grid and ladder matching complexes), and symmetric partition tables give unsigned counts rather than cellular orientation weights. A previously unindexed equivalent signed enumeration is a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The theorem supplies the full chain-level symmetry statistic, not just one finite example or a restatement of ordinary face counts. Its labelled-block decomposition explains why paired versus self-reversed partition blocks acquire different signs; the resulting all-size series determines reduced Lefschetz numbers and quotient Euler characteristics even when nonzero homology occurs in several degrees. These are natural equivariant invariants that cannot be recovered from the ordinary Stirling-number Euler characteristic alone.

## Boundaries
No individual-degree homology character, torsion classification, homotopy classification of the quotient, or claim of independent validation follows from the series. Finite checks covered \(1\leq n\leq9\); the all-size theorem rests on the combinatorial proof.
