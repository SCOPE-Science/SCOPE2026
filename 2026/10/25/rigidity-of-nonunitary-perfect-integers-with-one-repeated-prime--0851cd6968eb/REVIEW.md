# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** The theorem counts repeated prime supports, not total prime multiplicity. Equation (1) is obtained by multiplicativity of the ordinary and unitary sums and the fact that they agree on squarefree factors. With a sole repeated odd prime, parity excludes odd squarefree cofactors, a modulo-four obstruction excludes two or more additional odd primes when the singleton factor \(2\) is present, and the strict rational bound excludes the remaining cases. With the sole repeated prime equal to \(2\), the equation forces a Mersenne candidate \(M\mid s\); the abundancy monotonicity inequality makes \(s=M\) prime. The small boundary \(a=2\) and the converse are explicit. The standard-library checker corroborated all integers through \(300000\).

**Originality: PASS (with priority risk).** Ligh–Wall (1987) proves the Mersenne construction and formulates the unrestricted converse as a conjecture. The 2024 two-prime-support MathOverflow converse does not cover arbitrarily many squarefree cofactor primes. Hagis (1990) treats the odd case without this all-one-powerful-component classification; Graham (1989) concerns unitary-perfect, rather than nonunitary-perfect, integers. Targeted published mathematical-record searches for equivalent terminology, powerful cores, and the precise Mersenne iff statement returned no covering theorem; the nearest compared records concern distinct divisor-sum equations. An unindexed older elementary observation could still dominate the claim.

**Value: PASS.** The class of integers with \(\rho(n)\le1\) is a natural structural layer for a conjecture about the existence of any non-Mersenne nonunitary-perfect number. The theorem closes that layer for unbounded prime support and shows any counterexample must have at least two repeated prime components; this is not merely verifying more known numerical examples or adding a numerical cutoff. The proof is elementary and short; that is a scope and priority risk, not evidence that an arbitrary slice was chosen.

Primary literature: Ligh–Wall, *The Fibonacci Quarterly* 25 (1987), 333–338; Hagis, *The Fibonacci Quarterly* 28 (1990), 11–15; Graham, *The Fibonacci Quarterly* 27 (1989), 316–322. Closest posted implication: Zelinsky's MathOverflow response (4 February 2024) for exactly two prime supports. The general conjecture is unresolved by this work. No claim of independent validation is made.
