# Root-of-unity angles force a sharp harmonic sampling transition on the three-sphere

## Finding
Let \(L\ge4\) be even, and let \(X\subset S^3\) be a finite, nonempty, antipodal set of \(N\) distinct points. Assume that every pair of distinct nonantipodal points \(x,y\in X\) has angular separation \(\theta=\arccos\langle x,y\rangle\in(0,\pi)\) satisfying \(L\theta\in2\pi\mathbb Z\). Write \(P\) for the antipodal permutation matrix, \(J\) for the all-ones matrix, and
\[
K_\ell=(U_\ell(\langle x,y\rangle))_{x,y\in X},
\qquad U_\ell(\cos\theta)=\frac{\sin((\ell+1)\theta)}{\sin\theta},
\]
with the continuous endpoint values \(U_\ell(1)=\ell+1\) and \(U_\ell(-1)=(-1)^\ell(\ell+1)\).

Then the cardinality bound \(N\le2L\) holds, and for every \(\ell\ge0\),
\[
K_{\ell+L}=K_\ell+L(I+(-1)^\ell P).
\]
Moreover,
\[
K_{L-2}=L(I+P)-J,\qquad K_{L-1}=L(I-P).
\]
The nonzero spectrum of \(K_{L-2}\) is \(2L\) with multiplicity \(N/2-1\) and, when positive, \(2L-N\) with multiplicity one. At degree \(L-1\), the positive spectrum is exactly \(2L\) with multiplicity \(N/2\). For every \(\ell\ge L\), the degree-\(\ell\) harmonic evaluation map is onto the \(N/2\)-dimensional parity-compatible sample space.

In the extremal case \(N=2L\), degree \(L-2\) has rank \(L-1\), whereas degree \(L-1\) and every later degree has rank \(L\): thus \(L-1\) is the **sharp eventual-surjectivity threshold**, and degree \(L-1\) sampling is exactly tight on its output space. The bound and threshold are attained by infinitely many binary dihedral quaternion groups of even index. In particular, the unit-quaternion 600-cell has \(N=120\), \(L=60\), rank \(59\) at degree \(58\), rank \(60\) and an exactly flat positive spectrum at degree \(59\), and rank \(60\) in every degree at least \(59\).

## Assumptions and scope
The sphere \(S^3\) is the unit sphere in \(\mathbb R^4\); its uniform surface probability measure defines the \(L^2\) orthonormality of spherical harmonics. The harmonic space \(\mathcal H_\ell\) consists of restrictions of homogeneous harmonic polynomials of exact degree \(\ell\), and has dimension \((\ell+1)^2\). The evaluation operator \(E_\ell:\mathcal H_\ell\to\mathbb C^X\) maps \(f\) to \((f(x))_{x\in X}\). Every output satisfies \(f(-x)=(-1)^\ell f(x)\), so surjectivity is only claimed onto this parity-compatible subspace; no uniqueness of the harmonic interpolant is implied. The matrix \(K_\ell\) is the evaluation Gram divided by \(\ell+1\), namely \(E_\ell E_\ell^*=(\ell+1)K_\ell\) with this normalization. The angle hypothesis is not assumed to arise from a group.

## Proof
The addition theorem for harmonics on \(S^3\) gives the reproducing kernel \((\ell+1)U_\ell(\langle x,y\rangle)\). Thus every \(K_\ell\) is positive semidefinite. For distinct nonantipodal points, the angle hypothesis and the trigonometric formula for \(U_\ell\) yield
\[
U_{\ell+L}(\cos\theta)=U_\ell(\cos\theta),\quad
U_{L-2}(\cos\theta)=-1,\quad
U_{L-1}(\cos\theta)=0.
\]
For coincident points, the corresponding values are \(\ell+1\), \(L-1\), and \(L\). For antipodes, the same endpoint values carry the sign \((-1)^\ell\). Because \(L\) is even, all entries together give the three displayed exact matrix identities.

The commuting involution \(P\) has equally sized eigenspaces \(V_+\) and \(V_-\), each of dimension \(N/2\). The all-ones vector belongs to \(V_+\), and \(J\) acts as \(N\) on this vector and as zero on its orthogonal complement. Hence \(K_{L-2}\) has eigenvalues \(2L-N\) on the constants, \(2L\) on the \(N/2-1\) other even vectors, and zero on the odd vectors. Positive semidefiniteness forces \(N\le2L\). Likewise, \(K_{L-1}=L(I-P)\) has eigenvalue \(2L\) exactly on \(V_-\).

For any \(\ell\ge L\), write \(\ell=r+kL\) with \(0\le r<L\) and integer \(k\ge1\). Iterating the recurrence gives
\[
K_\ell=K_r+kL(I+(-1)^rP).
\]
On the parity-compatible eigenspace of \(P\), the second term is \(2kL I\), while the restriction of \(K_r\) is positive semidefinite. Therefore the restriction of \(K_\ell\) is strictly positive, giving rank \(N/2\) and surjectivity. For \(N=2L\), the degree \(L-2\) constant vector is the sole compatible null mode, and the next degree and all higher ones are fully surjective. At degree \(L-1\), all nonzero squared singular values of \(E_{L-1}\) are equal to \(2L^2\) (the normalization factor is \(L\)).

For sharpness across infinitely many configurations, let \(\mathrm{Dic}_n\) for even integers \(n\ge2\) be the unit-quaternion binary dihedral group generated by \(a=e^{i\pi/n}\) and \(b=j\), where \(b^2=a^n=-1\) and \(bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\). Its \(4n\) elements are distinct, include antipodes, and every element has order dividing \(2n\) (the elements outside the cyclic subgroup have order four, which divides \(2n\) because \(n\) is even). Set \(L=2n\). For any two group elements \(x,y\), the unit quaternion \(x^{-1}y\) has real part \(\langle x,y\rangle\) and satisfies \((x^{-1}y)^L=1\), hence \(L\arccos\langle x,y\rangle\in2\pi\mathbb Z\). Therefore \(N=4n=2L\), proving infinite sharpness.

The regular 600-cell also meets the hypotheses: its 120 unit-quaternion vertices form the binary icosahedral group, whose exponent is 60. Hence every relative quaternion has order dividing 60. Alternatively, its distinct nonantipodal inner products belong to \(\{0,\pm1/2,\pm(1+\sqrt5)/4,\pm(\sqrt5-1)/4\}\), whose angles are integral multiples of \(\pi/2\), \(\pi/3\), or \(\pi/5\); multiplying each angle by 60 gives an integral multiple of \(2\pi\). The preceding theorem applies with \(L=60\).

## Verification
The self-contained exact-arithmetic checker `checker.py` constructs all 120 standard 600-cell vertices in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)^4\) and the eight vertices of the quaternion group, checks normalization, antipodal pairing, and every distinct inner-product class, and verifies the three critical Chebyshev values and the periodic update on each class. It uses only Python's standard library and rational arithmetic. Run `python3 checker.py` and compare the printed summaries with `checker_output.txt`. These computations test the stated natural examples; the general theorem is proved by the addition formula and matrix identities above, not inferred from finite tests.

## Relationship to prior work
The classical addition theorem and Chebyshev–Gegenbauer kernel positivity are foundational. Mamone–Pileio–Levitt (2010) analyze quaternionic polytope configurations and spherical moments for rotational quadrature. Ćaćić–Marcolli–Teh (2011) give the order, exponent, and character table of the binary icosahedral group. Bannai–Okuda–Tagami (2013) note that the 600-cell is a harmonic-index-58 design, consistent with the constant compatible null mode at degree 58. These sources do not state the angle-only cardinality bound for *arbitrary* antipodal sets together with the universal Gram shift identity and consequent sharp all-higher-degree interpolation law. The group examples are applications of the general statement, not its hypotheses.

## Limitations
The result concerns exact-degree harmonic evaluation on \(S^3\), not other dimensions, mixed degree bands, noisy samples, or arbitrary point sets without the angle condition. It does not classify all equality configurations or describe ranks below the transition. The claim of original literature coverage is provisional: orientational-sampling work predating the cited sources and general positive-definite spherical-code bounds might contain related formulations. In particular, the publisher's complete 2010 orientational-sampling text was not reliably retrievable for exhaustive comparison; its accessible sections and abstract were compared, and that limitation remains a publication-priority risk. No independent validation is asserted.
