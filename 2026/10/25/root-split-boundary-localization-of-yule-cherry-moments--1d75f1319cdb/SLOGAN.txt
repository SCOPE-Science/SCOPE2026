In a Yule–Harding phylogenetic tree, conditioning on the root-daughter sizes changes the expected number of cherries only at one- or two-leaf daughter splits, and changes its second moment only at the four extreme split sizes. Remarkably, the event that some root daughter has at most two leaves leaves the cherry mean at \(n/3\) but raises its conditional variance by exactly \(2/45\), for every \(n\ge10\).
