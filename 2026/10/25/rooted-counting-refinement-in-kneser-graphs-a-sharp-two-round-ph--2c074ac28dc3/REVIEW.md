# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof explicitly constructs the orbital quotient \(B_{tu}\), triangularizes its action on binomial-polynomial functions, and uses the full graph's symmetry algebra and spectral projectors to obtain \(k+1\) independent adjacency powers of the root indicator. Since these vectors must be constant on the corresponding counting-refinement classes, the partition equals all intersection strata by round \(k\). A direct binomial-neighbour count establishes the if-and-only-if two-round threshold, and the odd graph's quotient-walk order establishes exact depth \(k-1\). The matching case and small \(k\) are handled separately. Independent finite computations cover both quotient and full adjacency definitions. The computations are not relied on as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The inspected literature develops the counting-logic/colour-refinement paradigm and Kneser association schemes but does not give the present pointed Kneser classification or sharp refinement-round phase. The known two-marked-vertex Kneser result at a more restrictive density does not imply the one-marked-vertex theorem. Standard eigenvalues and odd-graph distance transitivity are not claimed as original. Bounded searching cannot exclude every specialized earlier result; association-scheme folklore is the primary residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The exact logical orbit recovery threshold is structurally motivated: one labelled vertex is the least nonuniform initial condition, and the Kneser family interpolates between sparse odd graphs and dense disjointness graphs. A sharp transition at \(n=3k-2\), unbounded exact depth \(k-1\) at the odd boundary, and complete stratum sizes quantify a genuinely nontrivial distinction between stable information and the time needed to recover it. The result is an infinite-family classification rather than an arbitrary small numerical computation.

## Closest literature and limitations
Kiefer–Schweitzer–Selman (arXiv:1503.08792, 2015) addresses finite-variable counting logic and graph identification; it does not imply orbit recovery in this particular graph. Van Dam–Sotirov (arXiv:1312.0332, 2013) uses Kneser association schemes for graph-partition bounds, not marked counting refinement. The proof does not assert graph identification among unrestricted competitors, nor exact stabilization times at all intermediate densities.
