# Second Gamma-cubical homology misses the tetrahedral two-sphere

## Finding
Let \(H\) have vertices \(\{0,1,2,3\}\) and exactly the four triple hyperedges. In the normalized Γ-cubical theory of Zaidi and Jamil, there is a strict degree-two disagreement:
\[
H_2^\Gamma(H;\mathbb Z)=0,\qquad H_2(K(H);\mathbb Z)\cong H_2^{\mathrm{emb}}(H;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,
\]
where \(K(H)\) is the simplicial closure, namely the tetrahedral boundary \(\partial\Delta^3\).

## Assumptions and scope
The coefficient ring is \(\mathbb Z\). A Γ-singular \(n\)-cube is a hypergraph morphism from the Γ-hypercube with vertex set \(\{0,1\}^n\) and hyperedges consisting of Hamming-radius-one closed stars. A morphism sends each source hyperedge to a set contained in a target hyperedge. Degenerate cubes are constant in at least one coordinate, and the normalized chain groups are the free abelian groups on nondegenerate morphisms, with the signed coordinate-face differential. The theorem is about degree two of this Γ-theory, not the distinct complete-graph cubical theory.

## Proof
Every subset of at most three target vertices is contained in a target hyperedge. Consequently, all nondegenerate ordered two-cubes are admissible because every closed star in a square has three vertices. The first two normalized chain groups have ranks
\[
\operatorname{rank}C_1^\Gamma=4\cdot3=12,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}C_2^\Gamma=4^4-2\cdot4^2+4=228.
\]
For a square with bit-indexed vertices \((u_{00},u_{10},u_{01},u_{11})\), its boundary is the alternating sum of its four ordered edges (constant faces omitted after normalization). The first differential is ordinary directed-edge incidence of the complete graph on four vertices, hence has rank three. The degree-two boundaries contain every directed-edge backtrack \([a,b]+[b,a]\), arising from square \((a,b,a,a)\), and every oriented triangle relation \([a,b]+[b,c]-[a,c]\), arising, up to an overall sign and orientation convention, from square \((a,b,c,c)\). These relations generate all integral one-cycles of the bidirected complete graph: first identify reversed directed edges by the backtracks, then use the triangle relations to reduce every circuit to zero. Thus \(\operatorname{im}\partial_2=\ker\partial_1\) integrally, so \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_2=12-3=9\), and \(\ker\partial_2\) is a primitive rank-219 sublattice of \(C_2^\Gamma\).

The accompanying file `gamma_tetra_certificate.json` specifies 219 distinct admissible, nondegenerate three-cubes and a respective pivot coordinate in the lexicographically ordered two-cube basis. The standalone `verify_gamma_tetra.py` reconstructs the Γ-cube admissibility and signed boundary from the definitions and checks that (i) all 219 boundaries are two-cycles, and (ii) successive integer elimination in the **listed order** has coefficient \(\pm1\) at each new pivot and zero in all previously used pivot coordinates. Therefore these 219 boundaries generate a primitive rank-219 sublattice of the free group \(C_2^\Gamma\). They lie in \(\ker\partial_2\), another primitive rank-219 sublattice: inclusion at the same rank between two primitive sublattices forces equality. Consequently \(\operatorname{im}\partial_3=\ker\partial_2\), giving \(H_2^\Gamma(H;\mathbb Z)=0\), without passing merely to a field.

For the simplicial comparison, the four oriented triangular facets give the usual cellular two-cycle \(\partial[0,1,2,3]\) generating the top homology of the tetrahedral boundary. For embedded hypergraph homology, there are no hyperedges of size two or four: the embedded two-chain group is the kernel of the simplicial boundary on the free group spanned by the four triples, again generated by the alternating tetrahedral boundary, and no embedded three-chains exist. Hence both comparison groups are \(\mathbb Z\).

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_gamma_tetra.py gamma_tetra_certificate.json` with standard Python 3. The program enumerates the full bases of nondegenerate one- and two-cubes, verifies the exact ranks of the lower differentials and their chain identities, checks every one of the 219 signed three-boundary columns against the certificate, and enumerates all \(4^8\) vertex maps in degree three, obtaining 32,868 admissible nondegenerate cubes. It exits with `VERIFY_OK` only when all checks succeed. Integer unit pivots, not a finite-field Betti number, establish the crucial integral saturation. Only the certificate's 219 selected three-cubes are needed for surjectivity; the exhaustive degree-three count is corroboration.

## Relationship to prior work
Zaidi and Jamil introduced Γ-cubical hypergraph homology, with definitions and selected computations in arXiv:2610.08196v1 (October 6, 2026); their inspected definitions and computations do not establish this tetrahedral degree-two comparison. Earlier cubical homology constructions on directed graphs, including work of Grigor'yan and Muranov, use different source cubes and do not automatically imply a statement about Γ-hypergraph morphisms. The comparison is specifically with simplicial-closure and embedded hypergraph homology, not the other cubical theories of the motivating article.

## Limitations
This example does not determine Γ-homology in higher degrees or classify which hypergraphs preserve their simplicial second homology. The exact finite certificate is part of the proof; the checker verifies it using standard integer arithmetic. The novelty comparison is not a priority guarantee: unindexed or inaccessible earlier work may exist, and there has been no independent audit.
