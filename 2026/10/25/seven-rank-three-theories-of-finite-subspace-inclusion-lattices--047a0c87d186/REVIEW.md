# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The full proof analyzes the one-move extension patterns for two arbitrary subspaces, establishes all nine nontrivial existence cases constructively over every finite field, and proves a dimension-truncation identity. Rank-two formulas with one free variable distinguish every truncated dimension pair, giving the exact unpointed rank-three partition. The finite-field verification program independently reconstructs actual subspaces and checks the algebraic pattern table. The size bound follows from Gaussian binomial coefficients. Risks: none affecting the stated pure-order domain identified; finite checks are corroboration, not the universal proof.

## Originality
PASS, subject to conventional historical-search uncertainty. Pikhurko–Verbitsky (2010, §3) treat Ehrenfeucht-game methodology primarily for graphs, Cusick (1998) counts subspaces, and Yue (2014, §§1–3) examines geometricity and characteristic polynomials of subspace-derived lattices. These do not state the rank-three field-independent classification. An earlier Boolean *subset*-inclusion classification reaches the same dimension threshold, but is not a consequence establishing the result for non-distributive subspace lattices, nor vice versa. Searches for subspace lattice, geometric lattice, quantifier rank, and Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé exact classifications revealed no equivalent theorem. Risk: older or unindexed specialist logic or geometry literature might contain an equivalent statement.

## Value
PASS. The result gives an exact finite testing set for an entire two-parameter family of geometric lattices, demonstrates that even varying field characteristic/order is invisible at rank three, and proves a sharp (rather than merely finite) bound of \(2825\) elements. Its non-distributive, field-uniform extension-pattern argument goes beyond a size-count or a Boolean-lattice calculation.
