# Shared-apex Poisson coupling for near-tied triangle diameters

## Finding

A triangle whose two longest sides differ by order \(n^{-1/2}\) has a coupled, explicitly computable extreme-distance law. The coupling is generated by the *same* near-apex Poisson cloud, and the distribution does not factor into independent side contributions even when the limiting side lengths are equal. The theorem below includes the complete near-tie crossover and a piecewise-quadratic joint void exponent.

For any fixed \(0<\gamma<\pi/3\) and \(\delta\ge 0\), take triangles \(T_n=A B C_n\) with \(|AB|=1\), \(|AC_n|=1-\delta/\sqrt n\) and \(\angle BAC_n=\gamma\). Choose \(n\) points independently and uniformly by area in \(T_n\); let \(D_n\) be their maximum pairwise Euclidean distance. Set \(c=\cos\gamma\), \(\lambda=(1-c)^{-1}\). Then

\[
\sqrt n\,(1-D_n)\ \Longrightarrow\ Z_\delta
 =\min\{A_B+U_B,\ \delta+A_C+U_C\}.
\]

Here \(U_B,U_C\) are independent, independent of \((A_B,A_C)\), and satisfy \(\Pr(U_B>u)=\Pr(U_C>u)=e^{-\lambda u^2}\) for \(u\ge0\). The coupled apex variables are determined by

\[
\Pr(A_B>s,A_C>t)=\exp[-Q_c(s,t)],\quad s,t\ge0,
\]

\[
Q_c(s,t)=
\begin{cases}
 s^2/c,& 0\le t\le cs,\\
 t^2/c,& 0\le s\le ct,\\
 \displaystyle\frac{(s^2+t^2)/c-2st}{1-c^2},& cs<t<s/c.
\end{cases}
\]

The branches agree at boundaries. For \(z\ge0\) put \(w=(z-\delta)_+\), \(g_y(s)=2\lambda(y-s)e^{-\lambda(y-s)^2}\) on \(0\le s\le y\). The limit has the explicit survival function

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(Z_\delta>z)={}&e^{-\lambda(z^2+w^2)}\\
&+e^{-\lambda w^2}\int_0^z g_z(s)e^{-s^2/c}\,ds\\
&+e^{-\lambda z^2}\int_0^w g_w(t)e^{-t^2/c}\,dt\\
&+\int_0^z\!\int_0^w g_z(s)g_w(t)e^{-Q_c(s,t)}\,dt\,ds.
\end{aligned}
\]

The equality-of-sides case \(\delta=0\) is genuinely dependent: defining the one-side survival
\[
H_c(z)=e^{-\lambda z^2}+\int_0^z g_z(s)e^{-s^2/c}\,ds,
\]
we have \(\Pr(Z_0>z)>H_c(z)^2\) for **every** \(z>0\). When \(\delta\ge z\), \(\Pr(Z_\delta>z)=H_c(z)\) exactly. This gives the explicit critical \(n^{-1/2}\) side-splitting regime.

## Assumptions and scope

All triangles are filled planar triangles, not boundaries, and sampling is by normalized Lebesgue area. The angle \(\gamma\) and deficit parameter \(\delta\) remain fixed as \(n\to\infty\). The restriction \(\gamma<\pi/3\) keeps the opposing base strictly shorter than both limiting apex edges; \(\gamma>0\) ensures nondegenerate limiting area. Statements concern weak convergence, not a uniform rate in \(\gamma\) or \(\delta\). For finite \(n\), take only indices with \(1-\delta/\sqrt n>0\).

## Proof

**1. Geometric localization and linearization.** In the limit triangle \(T=ABC\), the longest sides are exactly \(AB\) and \(AC\), both length one, while \(|BC|=\sqrt{2(1-c)}<1\). Since a convex polygon attains its Euclidean diameter at vertices, every pair at distance within \(o(1)\) of one must approach one of the two ordered vertex pairs \((A,B),(A,C)\). At each endpoint the distance-to-opposite-vertex has a strictly negative first derivative along both incident inward rays: the corresponding cosine coefficients are strictly positive for the present angle range. Compactness plus these strict derivatives implies that if a pair has deficit \(O(n^{-1/2})\), its two points lie within \(O(n^{-1/2})\) of the respective endpoints. Uniformly for such bounded rescaled displacements, the Euclidean norm expansion has error \(O(n^{-1})\). Thus side \(AB\) contributes its length minus the sum of its two inward endpoint projections; side \(AC_n\) similarly contributes \(1-\delta/\sqrt n\) minus two projections. Every side-\(BC_n\) pair is separated from these by a fixed positive gap.

**2. Endpoint Poisson processes.** Near \(A\), write a point as \(A+(r e_{AB}+q e_{AC})/\sqrt n\), where \(r,q\ge0\), and the two unit edge directions meet at angle \(\gamma\). The Jacobian is \(\sin\gamma/n\) and \(\operatorname{area}(T_n)\to\sin\gamma/2\). Therefore the rescaled sample converges locally to a planar Poisson point process of constant intensity **2** in the first quadrant of \((r,q)\)-coordinates. The first-order projections onto \(AB\) and \(AC\) are \(r+cq\) and \(cr+q\). Consequently, if \(A_B,A_C\) are their respective Poisson minima, their joint survivor is the exponential of minus twice the area of the union of the two triangles
\[
\{r,q\ge0:r+cq<s\},\qquad \{r,q\ge0:cr+q<t\}.
\]
Elementary intersection of those triangles gives precisely the three displayed branches of \(Q_c\). This also verifies the marginals \(\Pr(A_B>s)=\Pr(A_C>s)=e^{-s^2/c}\).

Near \(B\) and \(C_n\) the limiting interior angle equals \(\beta=(\pi-\gamma)/2\). The subset of a planar wedge of angle \(\beta\) having inward projection less than \(u\) has area \(u^2\tan\beta/2\). Dividing by limiting total area \(\sin\gamma/2\) gives the Poisson void exponent
\[
\frac{\tan\beta}{\sin\gamma}u^2=\frac{u^2}{1-c}=\lambda u^2.
\]
The three endpoint neighborhoods are separated by positive distances, so their Poisson limits are independent. The two apex projections intentionally remain dependent because they arise from one cloud.

**3. Convergence of the sample diameter.** The local point-process convergence and continuous mapping theorem apply to the finite minima of the positive linear projections. Each such minimum is almost surely finite and unique; its law has no atoms. Truncate rescaled corner neighborhoods at an arbitrary \(K\): by the preceding localization lemma and tightness of the endpoint minima, the probability that a pair determining the normalized diameter lies outside all three \(K\)-neighborhoods can be made arbitrarily small by increasing \(K\). Letting \(n\to\infty\), then \(K\to\infty\), gives the claimed minimum of the two projected pair deficits. The triangular-array change \(|AC_n|=1-\delta/\sqrt n\) supplies precisely the additive \(\delta\).

**4. Distribution and strict dependence.** For any nonnegative random variable \(A\) and \(y\ge0\), integration by parts gives
\[
\mathbb E[e^{-\lambda(y-A)_+^2}]
=e^{-\lambda y^2}+\int_0^y g_y(s)\Pr(A>s)\,ds.
\]
The two-dimensional version applied to the joint apex survivor yields the four-term display. The two threshold triangles have a positive-area intersection whenever \(s,t>0\), so \(Q_c(s,t)<(s^2+t^2)/c\) strictly. Since \(g_z(s)g_z(t)>0\) for interior \(s,t\in(0,z)\), the two-dimensional integral is strictly larger than in the fictitious independent-apex model. Thus \(\Pr(Z_0>z)>H_c(z)^2\) for \(z>0\). If \(\delta\ge z\), the second candidate cannot have deficit at most \(z\), proving the exact one-side equality.

## Verification

The included `verify.py` separately computes the overlap of two rational polygons using exact fraction arithmetic, rather than relying on the piecewise formula. The 900 exact area comparisons passed for four distinct rational angular cosines and 225 threshold pairs each. Independent numerical quadrature evaluates the four-term survival law for three distinct cosines and several near-tie parameters. A finite-sample comparison at \(c=3/4\), \(n=700\), with 1,000 independent uniform samples computed via convex hulls, yielded respective empirical/theoretical survivals at \(z=0.4,0.7,1.0\): \(0.971/0.96546\), \(0.791/0.79840\), and \(0.521/0.52970\). The analytic argument, not the simulations, proves the asymptotic theorem. To reproduce: run `python verify.py` with Python 3, NumPy and SciPy installed.

## Relationship to prior work

Lao (2010), Chapter 6.4, Theorem 6.8, proves an extreme-diameter limit for uniform interior points of regular *even*-gons, whose opposite diameter endpoint neighborhoods are disjoint and hence lead to independent Weibull variables. Chapter 5 separately treats random points **on edges** of polygons, identifying the endpoint-overlap issue for odd-gons; neither theorem contains the present filled-triangle shared-endpoint joint exponent or the near-tie triangular array. Appel, Najim and Russo (2002) treat planar sets with a unique major axis according to the contemporary mathematical account in Lao (2010), Chapter 1, and Schrempp (2015), Introduction. Schrempp's full accessible preprint assumes a unique major axis and square-root curved boundary behavior. Mayer and Molchanov (2006) analyze ball-based rotationally symmetric samples. The present result instead treats a two-axis, shared-endpoint polygonal singularity and quantifies the critical splitting of those axes. No independently audited historical novelty determination is claimed.

## Limitations

This is a one-parameter angular family and a critical \(n^{-1/2}\) near-tie scaling, not a classification of arbitrary polygons or dimensions. The 2002 Appel–Najim–Russo original full manuscript was inaccessible during the literature comparison; its abstract and later detailed descriptions of its unique-axis hypothesis were available. Other historical priority risks remain. No error rate or finite-\(n\) uniformity across degenerating angles is established. Only analytic parts of the claim are proved; the numerical verifier is supplementary.
