# Review of the sharp angular envelope for sketched Ritz values

## Correctness — PASS
The reduction from an arbitrary two-dimensional trial plane against a codimension-one observed hyperplane to the orthonormal triple is exact. In those coordinates the projected matrix is an explicitly nonsymmetric \(2\times2\) matrix. Its discriminant isolates the only potentially positive imaginary-part square as \(tbv-b^2\). Contractivity of the centered original matrix gives \(b^2+v^2\le1\), and an explicit two-by-two Rayleigh-quotient maximization yields the displayed constant. The rank-two symmetric contraction constructed in the proof attains every positive-angle bound with extreme eigenvalues exactly at the prescribed endpoints. The zero-angle case reduces to the ordinary symmetric Ritz compression. The exact rational witness and the standalone tests were replayed successfully; finite checks are corroboration rather than proof of the quantified theorem.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
Liu and Liu (arXiv:2610.22261v1, Sections 1 and 3.1) and Nakatsukasa–Tropp (DOI:10.1137/23M1565413, Section 6.8) already explain that symmetric original matrices can give nonreal sketched Ritz values. The present finding does not claim that phenomenon. An earlier restricted three-state E-optimal row-selection Krylov construction *does* attain the same angle profile after \(\gamma=(1+p^2)^{-1/2}\); its maximization is only inside that constructed family, so it does not imply a universal upper bound. The new claim is the exact supremum over **all** SPD matrices with prescribed spectral enclosure and all two-planes at the prescribed sampled singular value, and the complete attainability assertion. Snapshot-row-subsampling work and general nearly-Hermitian estimates were considered by implication, not title similarity. Targeted mathematical-record and literature searches revealed no directly covering claim. An uninspected oblique-projection or numerical-range specialization remains a priority risk; absent search results cannot prove novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A real-Schur sketched eigensolver must account for possible complex conjugate Ritz blocks. The theorem gives the exact worst-case size of such a block's imaginary part from observable input parameters (spectral width and sketch singular value), with an attainment construction showing no improvement without extra assumptions. It sharply distinguishes angle-small from angle-zero sampling. This is a motivated stability/diagnostic boundary, not an arbitrary numerical instance.

## Closest sources and restrictions
The direct prior constructions are Liu–Liu (2026), Nakatsukasa–Tropp (2024), and Hrobat–Nakatsukasa (2026); Kahan's 1974/1975 nearly-Hermitian bounds and older oblique-compression literature may imply related controls. Only the first two were examined in depth for sketched Ritz formulations; comparison with historical numerical-range literature was not exhaustive. The statement does not address general sketches, more than two trial columns, random embedding probabilities, finite-precision behavior or convergence of an entire eigensolver.
