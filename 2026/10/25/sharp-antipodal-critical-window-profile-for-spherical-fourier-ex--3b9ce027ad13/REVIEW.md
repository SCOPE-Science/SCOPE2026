# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The exact Funk–Hecke Bessel decomposition reduces finite harmonic sums to the two antipodal energy invariants; fixed-order Bessel asymptotics identify the leading logarithmic term. An explicit periodic-mean argument proves the critical-window profile, and Xu's published supercritical mixed-norm bound controls all \(L^2\) approximation errors uniformly in bounded window parameters. Nonstandard critical issues were checked: the powers remain Lipschitz at zero, exponents just below threshold are handled by finite-annulus Hölder, and arbitrary high angular degrees enter only through the uniform published bound. Positive-series expansions establish the sharp global parity inequality, including the edge dimension \(d=2\). The standalone numerical checker passed its finite sanity tests; it is not relied upon for the universal argument.

## Originality
PASS under accessible evidence, with explicit historical-priority risk. Xu's full-text supercritical theorem excludes the critical exponent and contains no corresponding log-window leading profile. Carneiro–Oliveira e Silva–Sousa treat the earlier sharp mixed-norm framework, not the logarithmic antipodal classification. Bakas–Ciccone–Wright describe a logarithmic upper bound at the planar endpoint, not the exact all-data coefficient. Published analogous logarithmic crossover results for mollified Fourier-ball kernels concern the different exponent \(2d/(d+1)\), as opposed to the spherical extension exponent \(2d/(d-1)\). Literature searches did not locate an equivalent or dominating claim; this is not a proof of global novelty. The full 2023 article was not compared line by line and unindexed or classical endpoint results could still overlap.

## Value
PASS. Critical mixed-norm behavior is a natural excluded endpoint of the sharp supercritical theorem; determining its precise logarithmic coefficient for every input, all dimensions, and an entire two-sided critical scaling window distinguishes structure that a generic logarithmic upper bound cannot detect. The exact antipodal equality and quantitatively optimal deficit give structural information useful when handling near-extremizers, rather than only another exponent estimate.

## Limitations
Only fixed data and leading-order logarithmic asymptotics are claimed. No ordinary spatial extension theorem or lower-order asymptotic is implied. Historical priority and independent validation are outstanding.
