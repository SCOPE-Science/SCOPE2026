# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** Each area-halfplane is unique for its direction. The fundamental sector is exhausted by chords with endpoints on the same reflected edge pair, whose wedge-triangle area is constant exactly when their ray-distance product is constant. Parametrization by reciprocal endpoint distances, the normal-angle identity, and cosine rule yield the complete closed length law. The edge-interval containment and both sector endpoints, including the triangular case, are addressed analytically. Nine odd side counts and 37 normal angles each were independently checked by polygon clipping, with discrepancies below \(4\times10^{-13}\); these are supporting finite checks only.

**Originality — PASS, bibliographically provisional.** General convex-set halving distances and bounds occur in Grüne et al. (2007); general shortest fences occur in Esposito et al. (2010). A later regular-pentagon envelope paper establishes hyperbolic structure, but its inspected claims do not give this closed all-odd-\(n\) length law. Closest indexed claims describe central lines, perimeter cuts, or even-sided diameter optimization, which do not imply lengths of generally shifted equal-area chords. A plausible Berele--Catoiu 2022 bisecting-envelope study was inaccessible in full, so an implicit published equivalent cannot be excluded.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The direction-dependent invariant of a canonical infinite polygon family determines both exact extremal area-halving distances and all equality cases, providing a sharp benchmark for general convex-body inequalities and quantifying the regular-odd-polygon approach to the circle.

**Limitations.** This proof treats Euclidean regular odd polygons, not arbitrary convex sets or perimeter halving. No independent audit, formal proof assistant, or full literature completeness is claimed.
