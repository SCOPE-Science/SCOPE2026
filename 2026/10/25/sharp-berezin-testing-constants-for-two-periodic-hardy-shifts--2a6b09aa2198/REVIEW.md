# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The normalized Hardy-kernel calculation gives exactly \(z(a+b|z|^2)/(1+|z|^2)\). The norm is the larger weight magnitude, and the numerical radius is the arithmetic mean of the magnitudes by the half-line Jacobi row-sum upper bound and long-block lower bound. A two-case proof uses only two radial witnesses and exact identities in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\). The strict-in-the-first-case argument, the monotone-in-parameter lower bounds in the second case, and the exact extremal radial derivative establish all quantifiers and uniqueness. The finite verification file corroborates the identities but is not needed for the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

The accessible full text of Bhattacharya–Patra, arXiv:2601.02334v2, particularly its introduction, weighted-shift theorem 3.18, and conclusion, concerns geometric disk properties of \(q\)-Berezin ranges; it does not calculate a sharp two-periodic Berezin-to-norm or Berezin-to-numerical-radius testing coefficient. The accessible full text of Augustine–Garayev–Shankar, arXiv:2411.10771v2, is focused on finite-rank Berezin ranges and generic inequalities. The ordinary numerical-range paper of Wang–Wu (2011) treats a different functional and is credited for qualitative periodic-shift context. Earlier indexed work on geometric-decay shifts gives a different coefficient law rather than the periodic two-parameter minimax bound. The calculations do not claim independence or unqualified publication priority; unindexed formulas remain a risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The Berezin number can substantially underestimate both the norm and numerical radius of an infinite-dimensional operator. The first nonconstant periodic weighted-shift family admits two different natural quantitative testing comparisons, and the exact constants and a common unique complex-phase extremizer resolve their sharpness simultaneously. The result isolates an anti-phase cancellation mechanism that is invisible to ordinary numerical radius, providing a reproducible benchmark for reproducing-kernel testing. It is not a statement about arbitrary shifts, larger periods, or \(q<1\).
