# Sharp chip-budget homology in directed-cycle broadcast avalanches

## Finding

Let \(k\ge2\), \(g\ge1\), and let \(m_1,\ldots,m_k\ge2\) be pairwise coprime integers with \(n_i=gm_i\ge3\). Construct a directed graph \(B\) with a broadcaster \(b\) and \(k\) vertex-disjoint consistently oriented cycles \(C_i\) of length \(n_i\). Add the directed arc \(b\to v_{i,0}\) for every \(i\), and no other arcs. Put \(k\) grains at \(b\) and zero elsewhere, and use synchronous parallel sandpile firing. Define the avalanche complex \(\mathcal A\) to be generated by all the simultaneous firing supports, including the initial firing support.

Writing \(D_m\) for the zero-dimensional simplicial complex on \(m\) isolated vertices, there is an **isomorphism of simplicial complexes**
\[
 \mathcal A\ \cong\ \{b\}\ \sqcup\ \bigsqcup_{r=0}^{g-1}
 \bigl(D_{m_1}*\cdots*D_{m_k}\bigr).
\]
Each join is realized on the cycle vertices whose indices are congruent to \(r\pmod g\). Consequently, with \(P=\prod_{i=1}^k(m_i-1)\),
\[
 \mathcal A\simeq\{\mathrm{pt}\}\ \sqcup\
 \bigsqcup_{r=0}^{g-1}\bigvee^{P}S^{k-1},
\]
and its reduced **integral** homology is precisely
\[
 \widetilde H_j(\mathcal A;\mathbb Z)=
 \begin{cases}
 \mathbb Z^{g},&j=0,\\
 \mathbb Z^{gP},&j=k-1,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
This attains the universal maximum nonzero homology degree permitted by a \(k\)-grain configuration on any finite directed graph with positive outdegree at every vertex: such an avalanche complex has dimension at most \(k-1\).

## Assumptions and scope

All digraphs are finite, loopless, and without multiple arcs. Every vertex of \(B\) has positive outdegree, so there are no sinks. The broadcaster has outdegree \(k\), each cycle vertex has outdegree one, and all grains are nonnegative integers. At a parallel step every vertex with at least its outdegree many grains fires once, losing its outdegree and sending one grain along each outgoing arc. The outgoing arcs from the broadcaster point only to the fixed index-zero vertices; none point back to the broadcaster. The underlying undirected graph is connected. The avalanche complex includes all finite times starting at time zero, including their nonempty simultaneous firing supports and all their subsets. The empty simplex is implicit. The pairwise coprimality assumption concerns the quotients \(m_i\), *not* the cycle lengths \(n_i\), which have pairwise greatest common divisor \(g\).

## Proof

**1. Exact firing dynamics.** At time zero the only unstable vertex is \(b\) because its chip count is exactly its outdegree \(k\). It fires and sends one grain to \(v_{i,0}\) on each cycle. The broadcaster now has zero grains and cannot receive grains later. Every cycle contains exactly one grain, and its unique unstable vertex moves by one directed edge per parallel step. Hence the support at time \(t\ge1\) is exactly
\[
 F_t=\bigl\{v_{i,(t-1)\bmod n_i}:1\le i\le k\bigr\},
\]
while \(F_0=\{b\}\). This inductive formula is valid for all times: on each cycle precisely the currently occupied vertex fires and transfers its only grain to its successor. Conservation of all \(k\) grains also follows.

**2. Generalized Chinese remainder theorem.** For a tuple of indices \(0\le j_i<n_i\) to occur as \(F_t\), its congruences \(t-1\equiv j_i\pmod{n_i}\) must have a common solution. Since \(\gcd(n_i,n_j)=g\) for every pair, the generalized Chinese remainder theorem says that this holds if and only if every \(j_i\) has the same residue \(r\) modulo \(g\). Conversely, every admissible tuple appears during the period \(L=\operatorname{lcm}(n_1,\dots,n_k)=g\prod_i m_i\). For each \(r\) there are \(m_i\) choices of a cycle-\(i\) vertex. All choices occur, giving exactly the maximal faces of the join \(D_{m_1}*\cdots*D_{m_k}\). Supports for distinct residues are vertex-disjoint; the source vertex appears in no support after time zero. Thus the displayed decomposition is an exact simplicial isomorphism, rather than merely a homological equality.

**3. Integral homotopy and homology.** A discrete \(m\)-point space is based-homotopy equivalent to a wedge of \(m-1\) zero-spheres. The join identity \(X*Y\simeq\Sigma(X\wedge Y)\) and distributivity of smash products over finite wedge sums give
\[
 D_{m_1}*\cdots*D_{m_k}\simeq
 \bigvee^{\prod_i(m_i-1)}S^{k-1}.
\]
For \(k\ge2\), every such join is path-connected. With \(g\) joins plus the isolated source point, there are exactly \(g+1\) components, proving the asserted reduced degree-zero rank. The positive reduced homology is the direct sum of the join summands, all free and in degree \(k-1\). This proves the exact integral statement; no appeal to finite experiments or field coefficients is required.

**4. Sharp chip-budget boundary.** More generally, on a digraph where each vertex has outdegree at least one, every simultaneously firing vertex must hold at least one grain. With a total of \(k\) grains and parallel firing, at most \(k\) vertices can fire at the same step. Every generating support consequently has cardinality at most \(k\), so the avalanche complex has dimension at most \(k-1\). The above family has \(\widetilde H_{k-1}\cong\mathbb Z^{gP}\ne0\), proving that this dimensional bound is optimal for every \(k\ge2\).

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` implements the parallel chip dynamics and reconstructs all firing supports through the full period. It independently generates the predicted CRT facets and computes simplicial boundary ranks over the fields with \(2,3,5,7\) elements for six small cases. Cases examined in full include cycle-length tuples \((3,4)\), \((6,10)\), \((9,15)\), \((3,4,5)\), \((4,6,10)\), and \((6,10,14)\). Three further instances were checked for exact facet equality through their complete period, including \((3,5,7,11)\). All finite checks passed. These computations corroborate but do not replace the general proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Riihimäki and Smith introduced avalanche homology for parallel sandpile firing on directed graphs in 2026, analyzing cycles, paths, and a configuration of two equal cycles sharing a vertex. The latter is a different digraph and chip configuration from the present broadcaster feeding vertex-disjoint cycles. A related subsequent analysis of complementary residue supports on single cycles addresses a different synchronized-support problem: it neither supplies the broadcaster construction nor the complete multipartite join decomposition. The present statement is derived from the same foundational definition but gives an independent arithmetic topological family and proves a sharp general upper bound on achievable homological degree at a fixed grain budget. Searches for stronger/equivalent formulas did not establish novelty conclusively; unindexed or otherwise inaccessible work could still cover these statements.

## Limitations

The avalanche complex includes an isolated source vertex, and is therefore disconnected. No classification is claimed for arbitrary chip distributions, cycles with nonuniform pairwise greatest common divisors, feedback arcs to the broadcaster, or arbitrary digraphs. The result does not settle universality of avalanche homology types. It concerns nonnegative integer parallel firing with all outdegrees positive; configurations with sinks, grain creation, or sequential firing fall outside the claim. Computations test finitely many parameters only; the proof covers the specified infinite family. No independent scientific audit has been performed.
