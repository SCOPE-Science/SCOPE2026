# Sharp colour-rigidity window for uniform finite unary functions

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge 1\), choose independently a uniformly random function \(f_n:[n]\to[n]\) and independent vertex colours \(\chi_n:[n]\to[q_n]\), with each of the \(q_n\) colours equally likely. An automorphism is a permutation \(\pi\) satisfying \(f_n(\pi x)=\pi f_n(x)\) and \(\chi_n(\pi x)=\chi_n(x)\) for all vertices \(x\). Write \(R_n\) for the event that the only automorphism is the identity.

**Theorem.** If \(q_n/n\to\alpha\in(0,\infty)\), then
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\Pr(R_n)=\exp\!\left(-\frac{e^{-2}}{2\alpha}\right).
\]
In fact, if \(X_n\) counts unordered *leaf twins*—pairs of distinct vertices with the same image under \(f_n\), with no preimages of either vertex, and with equal colours—then
\[
X_n\ \xrightarrow{d}\ \operatorname{Poisson}\!\left(\frac{e^{-2}}{2\alpha}\right),
\qquad
\Pr\bigl(R_n\mathbin{\triangle}\{X_n=0\}\bigr)\to0.
\]
Here \(\triangle\) is symmetric difference of events. Thus the critical finite-structure automorphisms are almost surely generated by transpositions of same-coloured leaf siblings. This statement holds without conditioning the underlying function to be connected, acyclic, or surjective.

## Assumptions and scope

The labels \([n]\) are distinct domain elements, but colour names are fixed and must be preserved by automorphisms (they may be unused). The function and colours are mutually independent. The theorem concerns the critical regime \(q_n/n\to\alpha\) with fixed finite positive \(\alpha\); it does not assert an asymptotic formula when \(q_n/n\to0\). No first-order definability claim is made with a fixed vocabulary while the number of colours varies. The subject is rigidity of finite unary-function expansions and its precise probability.

A vertex \(x\) is a leaf when \(f_n^{-1}(\{x\})=\varnothing\). Two distinct leaves \(x,y\) are twins if \(f_n(x)=f_n(y)\) and \(\chi_n(x)=\chi_n(y)\). Transposing such twins preserves the whole expansion, hence \(X_n>0\) implies non-rigidity *exactly*, at every finite \(n\).

## Proof

**1. Exact first moment and disjoint factorial moments.** For any fixed distinct \(x,y\), to be twin leaves their common image must avoid \(\{x,y\}\); after this, all other \(n-2\) function values must avoid those two vertices. Therefore, for \(n\ge3\),
\[
\mathbb E X_n=
\binom n2\frac1{q_n}\frac{n-2}{n^2}
\left(1-\frac2n\right)^{n-2}
\longrightarrow\frac{e^{-2}}{2\alpha}.
\]
For a fixed \(r\), the contribution to \(\mathbb E(X_n)_r\) from ordered \(r\)-tuples of distinct, vertex-disjoint twin pairs (take \(2r\le n\)) is *exactly*
\[
\frac{(n)_{2r}}{2^r q_n^r}\,
\frac{(n-2r)^r}{n^{2r}}
\left(1-\frac{2r}n\right)^{n-2r},
\]
with \((n)_j=n(n-1)\cdots(n-j+1)\). The factor \((n-2r)^r\) allows common images to coincide with one another, but not to equal any of the \(2r\) leaves. This expression tends to \((e^{-2}/(2\alpha))^r\).

For the remaining configurations, regard the chosen distinct unordered pairs as edges of a graph on their participating vertices. If the graph has \(v\) vertices in \(c\) nontrivial connected components, all function values and colours must agree inside each component. There are at most \(O_r(n^v)\) choices of vertices, and the probability of these necessary equalities is at most \(n^{-(v-c)}q_n^{-(v-c)}\). Under \(q_n\asymp n\), the contribution is \(O_r(n^{2c-v})\). Unless all components have exactly two vertices, \(v\ge2c+1\), so it is \(O_r(n^{-1})\). The all-two-vertex case is precisely the disjoint case, because the selected pairs are distinct. Hence every fixed factorial moment converges to the stated Poisson factorial moment; the method of moments proves the Poisson limit.

**2. The cyclic part almost surely has no nontrivial colour-preserving symmetry.** Every functional digraph comprises directed cycles with rooted in-trees. Let \(C_\ell\) count cycles of length \(\ell\). For a uniform map,
\[
\mathbb E C_\ell=\frac{(n)_\ell}{\ell n^\ell},\qquad
\mathbb E(C_\ell)_2=\frac{(n)_{2\ell}}{\ell^2 n^{2\ell}},
\]
with the second expression interpreted as zero when \(2\ell>n\). A nonidentity automorphism of the permutation on cyclic vertices either rotates some one cycle or exchanges two distinct equal-length cycles, perhaps with a shift. Each specific nonidentity rotation or aligned exchange requires at least one equality of independent vertex colours, hence has probability at most \(q_n^{-1}\). The expected number of candidate rotations preserving colours is at most
\[
\frac1{q_n}\sum_{\ell\ge2}(\ell-1)\mathbb E C_\ell
\le\frac1{q_n}\sum_{\ell\ge1}e^{-\ell(\ell-1)/(2n)}
=O(\sqrt n/q_n)=o(1).
\]
The expected number of colour-preserving aligned exchanges between distinct cycles is at most
\[
\frac1{2q_n}\sum_{1\le\ell\le n/2}\ell\,\mathbb E(C_\ell)_2
\le\frac1{2q_n}\sum_{\ell\le n/2}\frac1\ell
=O(\log n/q_n)=o(1).
\]
Therefore with probability tending to one, every automorphism fixes each cyclic vertex individually.

**3. All remaining nontrivial symmetries require a rare four-vertex witness.** Suppose an automorphism fixes every cyclic vertex but moves some other vertex. Choose a moved vertex \(x\) of minimal directed distance to the cycle. Its parent \(z=f_n(x)\) is fixed, so \(y=\pi(x)\ne x\) is a sibling of \(x\) with \(f_n(y)=z\) and \(\chi_n(y)=\chi_n(x)\). If both \(x,y\) are leaves, then \(X_n>0\). Otherwise, since \(\pi\) bijectively maps the preimage set of \(x\) to that of \(y\), both have a child: there exist distinct vertices \(u,v\) with \(f_n(u)=x\), \(f_n(v)=y\), and \(\chi_n(u)=\chi_n(v)\). The four vertices \(x,y,u,v\) are pairwise distinct because \(x,y\) are noncyclic siblings. Count all ordered quadruples with these conditions, whether or not they actually generate an automorphism. For fixed distinct \(x,y,u,v\), the probability of \(f_n(x)=f_n(y)\), \(f_n(u)=x\), \(f_n(v)=y\) is \(n^{-3}\); two independent colour equalities contribute \(q_n^{-2}\). Thus the expected number of witnesses is at most
\[
\frac{(n)_4}{n^3q_n^2}=O(n/q_n^2)=o(1).
\]
By Markov's inequality, with high probability there is no such witness. On the intersection of this event with the event from step 2, every automorphism fixes every non-leaf vertex: a moved non-leaf of minimum depth would force the excluded four-vertex witness. It follows that each automorphism merely permutes leaf vertices within their same-parent, same-colour classes, and these permutations are generated by twin transpositions. In particular, any nonidentity automorphism forces \(X_n>0\). The reverse implication was exact in the assumptions section. Therefore the symmetric difference probability tends to zero and \(\Pr(R_n)\to e^{-e^{-2}/(2\alpha)}\), completing the proof.

## Verification

The included `verify_colored_mapping.py` is self-contained Python 3 code. Execute it with `python verify_colored_mapping.py`. It exhaustively enumerates all functions and all colourings for \((n,q)=(2,2),(3,3),(4,3)\), independently searches all permutations for nonidentity automorphisms, checks that every observed leaf-twin collision implies a nontrivial automorphism, and verifies the exact first-moment formula with rational arithmetic. It also performs seeded, explicitly non-exhaustive simulations for \(n=100,300,1000\) with \(q=n\). The output ends with `COLOURED_RANDOM_MAP_RIGIDITY_OK` if these checks pass. Finite enumeration and simulation are corroboration, not a proof of the asymptotic assertion.

## Relationship to prior work

Kuželka (arXiv:2608.30580, first submitted 2026-08-31), *Unary Functions, Automorphisms, and Unlabeled First-Order Model Counting*, studies exact model-counting complexity and automorphism marking for finite structures with unary functions. His Theorems 1–3 and Section 7 do not give asymptotic probabilities of colour-preserving rigidity for independent uniformly random functions and growing colour palettes. Koponen (arXiv:2610.16163, first submitted 2026-10-12) studies finite-model properties and conditions for random-expansion logical convergence, rather than the present colour-rigidity window. Lehner (arXiv:1304.6642) treats random distinguishing 2-colourings in *locally finite infinite graphs*, not the \(q_n\asymp n\) expansion of a random finite unary function. General distinguishing-colouring language is classical, and the Poisson and elementary orbit-counting ingredients are not independently claimed as inventions.

## Limitations

The leading probability concerns a nonconstant, growing palette; it is not a fixed-language first-order zero-one or convergence theorem. The bound for non-leaf symmetries is only asymptotic and need not be small at fixed \(n\). No quantitative total-variation rate for \(X_n\) is claimed. The numerical evidence does not establish the infinite limit. A stronger unlocated theorem about random colourings of random functional digraphs could subsume this specialization, so independent historical-priority checking remains appropriate. Independent scientific validation has not been performed.
