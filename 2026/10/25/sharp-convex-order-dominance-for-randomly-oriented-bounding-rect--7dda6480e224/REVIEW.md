# Scientific review of the random bounding-rectangle perimeter law

**Correctness — PASS.** For a full-dimensional convex body, the normalized tight rectangle perimeter is the arclength mixture of the quarter-periodic kernel \(q(t)=|\cos t|+|\sin t|\). Uniform-angle Jensen proves the complete convex-order statement with the correct common mean. Fourier coefficients and Parseval reproduce the sharp variance, while the first-mode defect gives the displayed angular-dispersion bound with its correct numerical coefficient. Equality in the Fourier triangle inequality forces exactly two perpendicular unoriented tangent directions, hence a rectangle. The rectangular survival formula is derived by exact trigonometric inversion. Polygon vertex checks and independent Fourier computations corroborate normalization and coefficients; no finite simulation is used as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources.** Audet–Hansen–Messine optimize deterministic minimum width; Akiyama–Kamae study the coefficient of variation of one projection width; the published positive-direction Cauchy quadrature theorem minimizes worst-case deterministic sampling error. None of their inspected claims implies the all-convex-loss bound on the **paired perpendicular widths from a single random orientation**, the aspect-ratio-independent extremizers or the explicit spectral stability defect. The absence of an equivalent claim from searches is not proof of definitive historical priority, and unindexed earlier work remains a risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Tight bounding rectangles are canonical geometric enclosures, and a universally sharp law for all convex orientation penalties answers a natural extremal question rather than an arbitrary parameter slice. It also reveals that every rectangle, however elongated, has precisely the same normalized rotation risk, and supplies an explicit first-harmonic rigidity diagnostic. The novelty is the sharp distributional comparison and geometric equality structure, not the classical Cauchy or Jensen identities on their own.

The scope is planar Euclidean convex bodies with uniform orientation and bounding-rectangle perimeter. The result does not apply to the minimum-area rectangle, enclosing-box area, or arbitrary dimension. The same-model analysis cannot establish historical priority or independent certification.
