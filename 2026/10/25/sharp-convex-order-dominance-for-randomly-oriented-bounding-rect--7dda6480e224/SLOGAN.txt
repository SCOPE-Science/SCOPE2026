For every planar convex body, the perimeter of its uniformly rotated tight axis-parallel bounding rectangle, normalized by the body's own perimeter, is dominated in convex order by the universal rectangle law \(\cos U+\sin U\), where \(U\) is uniform on \([0,\pi/2]\). Rectangles of every aspect ratio are the only maximizers of any strictly convex orientation loss, and a sharp Fourier defect quantitatively detects departures from their two perpendicular edge-direction families.
