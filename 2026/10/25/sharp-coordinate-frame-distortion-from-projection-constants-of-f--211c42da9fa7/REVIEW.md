# Scientific review

The proof is independent of finite enumeration: coordinate elimination computes every admissible inverse map; the row-norm minimax argument computes the absolute projection constant for real and complex scalars; monotonicity of a scalar fractional function proves the new bound and equality cases. Exactly 1,001 deterministic exact-arithmetic/linear-program configurations passed. The checks corroborate, but do not certify, the general proof.

## Originality

Its Proposition 3.1 does not state the improved \(\lambda/(2-\lambda)\) comparison, the coordinate-deletion equality classification, or complex-field coverage. Relevant mathematical-research searches found no statement subsuming this comparison. Historical projection-norm formulas themselves may occur in Blatter--Cheney (1974), whose full text was unavailable; the isolated projection formula is not claimed novel. Earlier equivalent distance comparisons remain a residual historical-priority risk.

## Mathematical value
The improved estimate answers a concrete sharpness question adjacent to a newly published Banach--Mazur projection-constant problem. It halves the near-unit first-order loss, removes the restrictive \(4/3\) threshold for hyperplanes, handles both scalar fields, and characterizes all equality cases of the natural coordinate-frame comparison. These are dimension-free quantitative conclusions; they do not solve the unrestricted sharp Banach--Mazur problem.
