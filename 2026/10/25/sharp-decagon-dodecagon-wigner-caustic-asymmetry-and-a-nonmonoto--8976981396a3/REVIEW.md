# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem covers precisely the strict equiangular discrete-constant-width polygons with ten or twelve sides. Its parameters express opposite edge lengths as a regular-symmetral scale plus or minus a closure vector. The Wigner area is a quadratic form; Brunn–Minkowski gives strict positivity off symmetry. Maximization over a compact cube section reduces rigorously to a complete 80-case or 240-case vertex calculation. The exact symbolic checker verifies all vertices, signed-cyclic orbits, sharp constants, independent shoelace examples, and the inequality between the constants. The nonattained suprema are approached by uniformly shrinking boundary parameter vectors. No finite sampling is offered as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (with residual literature risk)

The closest motivating 2026 article, Konicer et al., arXiv:2606.22612, proves the universal fixed-even-count CPPOS upper ratio of one quarter and characterizes isoperimetric equality; it does not determine the restricted ten- and twelve-side sharp values. The published eight-side classification (identifier e2fc8c8ec4e3) was examined in full and explicitly leaves ten or more sides unresolved. Craizer–Teixeira–da Silva provide the earlier evolute definitions; Kwong's Fourier isoperimetry was reviewed at abstract/introduction level only. Genuine searches for equivalent constants, polygonal variants and strength of related inequalities found no covering result. Unindexed work and the uninspected full Kwong theorem remain uncertainty, not claims of proven uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

The result supplies the complete next two sharp asymmetry profiles within a geometrically motivated equality family, not merely a numerical example. The ten-sided optimizer saturates all twelve vertices of its parameter polytope, while twelve sides have a three-level vertex spectrum and just four maximizing vertices. The sharp area ratio unexpectedly rebounds as sides increase, revealing a discrete resonance relevant to approximations of constant-width ovals. The exact values are motivated by a natural Wigner asymmetry invariant and can be reused in studying side-count behavior.

## Limits

Only ten- and twelve-sided equality-family polygons are covered. Strictly convex extremizers do not exist; the sharp constants are approached by degenerating edge lengths. The full unindexed literature cannot be excluded, and no independent audit or external certification has been performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
