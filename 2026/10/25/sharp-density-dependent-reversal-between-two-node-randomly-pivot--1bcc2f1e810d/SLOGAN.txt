For two optimally weighted quadrature nodes on the circle kernel \(k_b(\theta,\varphi)=1+b\cos(\theta-\varphi)\), randomly pivoted Cholesky has lower expected squared worst-case error than iid uniform nodes for every nonnegative first-harmonic probability density exactly when \(0<b<8\), with equality for all targets at \(b=8\). Above that sharp boundary, iid wins for weakly modulated densities while randomly pivoted Cholesky wins for sufficiently strong modulation, with exact crossover amplitude \(\sqrt{8/b}\).
