# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** On a singleton fuzzy Gödel model, every term result is selected from \(0,1,r,x\). The complete list of eleven weak-order cases therefore gives an exact finite separating semantics. The bundled derivation-and-closure certificate exhibits precisely \(60\) box-only signatures and \(342\) diamond-only signatures, including an independent calculation showing that depth-one propositional closures exhaust them. The equality \(\Diamond\top=r\) proves the diamond-to-box translation, and a valuation with \(p=1\) proves strict reverse nondefinability. The proof needs neither a numerical approximation nor any extrapolation beyond the finite certificate.

## Originality

**PASS, with historical-priority risk.** Ferrari et al. (arXiv:2606.31906, Proposition 2.5) prove modal non-interdefinability on general fuzzy frames, not the one-world quotient or its exact fragments. Valota (2019), following Horn (1969), covers the previously known \(342\)-element free Gödel algebra and this part is explicitly not claimed new. The published finite-frame regrading theorem preserves validity under ordinal changes but does not count the \(60\)-element box-only term subalgebra or establish its depth-one normalization. The closest abstract free-algebra literature might contain an unstated equivalent special case, which remains a residual risk.

## Value

**PASS.** The exact \(60:342\) contrast measures the expressive resource available from necessity versus possibility in the smallest nontrivial fuzzy-accessibility setting. Unlike a count for a freely chosen table, these quotients are generated by the native modal operations, and the sharp depth-one cutoff classifies every possible formula of either fragment. This provides a controlled finite test case for modal definability in the witnessed Gödel semantics, with a reproducible complete subalgebra certificate. The case is deliberately restricted, and no claim of a broad complexity consequence is made.
