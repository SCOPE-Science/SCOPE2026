# Sharp embedding dimension five for genus-two nonlinear surface contractions

## Finding

For every monic squarefree quintic \(f_{5}\in\mathbb C[x]\), take the smooth genus-two curve \(C: z^{2}=f_{5}(x)\) and its unique point \(p\) at infinity. Contract the zero section in the nonlinear neighborhood specified below. The resulting normal complex surface germ \((V,0)\) has

\[
\operatorname{edim}(V,0)=\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{2}=5.
\]

This is an **exact upper-and-lower result** for the genus-two, zero-Wahl-defect case of the Park--Shin family. It does not assert the exact embedding dimension for higher-genus or negative-defect examples.

## Assumptions and scope

Work over \(\mathbb C\). Let \(A=\mathcal O_C(p)\), with \(\deg A=1\). The Weierstrass pole orders of \(x\) and \(z\) at \(p\) are \(2\) and \(5\), respectively. On a sufficiently small disc \(W\) around \(p\), put \(t=x^{2}/z\); then \(t\) is a local parameter. On \(U=C\setminus\{p\}\) trivialize \(A\) by \(a_U=1\); on \(W\) use \(a_W=t^{-1}\), so \(a_U=t a_W\).

Represent \(\zeta\in H^{1}(C,A)\) by the cocycle \(c=t^{-3}\) in the \(U\)-frame. It is nonzero: a generator of \(H^{0}(C,K_C(-p))\) is \(dx/z\), and \(dx/z=(-2t^{2}+O(t^{4}))\,dt\), so \(\operatorname{Res}_{p}(t^{-3}dx/z)=-2\). Thus the construction's required cohomology class is nonzero. For the fiber coordinates use the nonlinear transition

\[
y_W=\frac{t^{-1}y_U}{1+t^{-3}y_U}.
\]

The analytic neighborhood and normal contraction are those of Park--Shin, Section 2, with \(k=1\). Its exceptional curve has self-intersection \(-1\). By their Theorem III the germ has Wahl defect zero and is neither \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein nor quasihomogeneous. Those features are published inputs; only the exact embedding dimension is asserted here.

## Proof

**(1) The formal function filtration.** Let \(\widehat R\) be the completed local algebra of the contraction and let \(F^{j}\widehat R\) be order at least \(j\) along the exceptional curve. The theorem on formal functions identifies it with compatible functions on infinitesimal neighborhoods. Park--Shin Lemma 8.4 and Proposition 8.6 give

\[
J:=\operatorname{gr}_{F}\widehat R
=\mathbb C\oplus\bigoplus_{j\ge2}H^{0}(C,\mathcal O_C(jp)),\qquad J_1=0.
\]

The pole semigroup of \(p\) is \(\langle2,5\rangle\). Introduce bookkeeping variables \(T,X,Z\) of weights \(1,2,5\), identifying \(T^{j}\), \(T^{j-2}X\), and \(T^{j-5}Z\) with degree-\(j\) copies of functions \(1,x,z\). The homogeneous relation is \(Z^{2}=T^{10}f_{5}(X/T^{2})\). The positive-degree part of \(J\), which starts in degree two, is generated as a \(\mathbb C\)-algebra by the six homogeneous classes

\[
T^{2},\ X,\ T^{3},\ TX,\ Z,\ TZ.
\]

Indeed each section is represented by a monomial \(T^{r}X^{a}Z^{b}\) with \(b\in\{0,1\}\) and \(r+2a+5b\ge2\), reduced using the displayed relation. If \(b=0\), all cases are generated by \(T^{2},T^{3},X,TX\): the only exceptional exponent \(r=1\) is paired with \(a\ge1\). If \(b=1\), take \(Z\) when \(r=0\), \(TZ\) when \(r=1\), and multiply \(Z\) by powers of \(T^{2},T^{3}\) when \(r\ge2\), with additional powers of \(X\) as needed.

**(2) Explicit compatible formal functions.** If \(F_U=\sum_{j\ge2}f_j(x,z)y_U^j\), its series on the second chart has coefficients

\[
b_q(t)=\sum_{j=2}^{q}\binom{q-1}{j-1} f_j(x,z)\,t^{3j-2q}.
\]

This follows from the inverted gluing formula \(y_U=t y_W/(1-t^{-2}y_W)\). A formal function is compatible precisely when every \(b_q\) is regular at \(p\). We now choose five such functions, denoted \(P,Q,R,S,H\), with the indicated leading coefficients in the \(U\)-chart:

| Function | First coefficient | Next coefficient |
|---|---|---|
| \(P\) | \(f_2=1\) | \(f_3=0\) |
| \(Q\) | \(f_2=x\) | \(f_3=-2z\) |
| \(R\) | \(f_3=1\) | \(f_4=0\) |
| \(S\) | \(f_3=x\) | \(f_4=-3z\) |
| \(H\) | \(f_5=z\) | no next coefficient prescribed |

All omitted lower coefficients vanish. The cases \(P\) and \(R\) satisfy \(b_2=t^{2}\) and \(b_3=2\), or \(b_3=t^{3}\) and \(b_4=3t\), respectively. For \(Q\),

\[
b_2=t^{2}x,\qquad b_3=2(x-t^{3}z).
\]

For \(S\),

\[
b_3=t^{3}x,\qquad b_4=3t(x-t^{3}z).
\]

For \(H\), \(b_5=t^{5}z\). All displayed coefficients are regular, since

\[
t^{3}z=\frac{x^{6}}{f_{5}(x)}=x+O(1)
\]

at \(p\); and \(t^{2}x\), \(t^{3}x\), and \(t^{5}z\) are holomorphic there. The displayed initial choices extend recursively to all orders. At each remaining order \(q\ge3\), one must choose a function \(f_q\) regular outside \(p\) with prescribed principal part of \(t^{q}f_q\). The Mittag--Leffler obstruction belongs to \(H^{1}(C,\mathcal O_C(qp))\), which vanishes for \(q\ge3\), because \(K_C\simeq\mathcal O_C(2p)\). Thus the required corrections exist. The theorem on formal functions places all five completed series in \(\widehat R\).

**(3) A hidden sixth associated-graded class.** Their initial coefficients produce the first five generators \(T^{2},X,T^{3},TX,Z\). The product

\[
G=PS-QR
\]

has zero coefficient in filtration order five: \(1\cdot x-x\cdot1=0\). Its order-six coefficient is

\[
1\cdot(-3z)+0\cdot x-x\cdot0-(-2z)\cdot1=-z.
\]

Hence \(G\in F^{6}\widehat R\), with initial section \(-z\) in the degree-six copy of \(H^{0}(C,\mathcal O_C(6p))\). This is precisely \(-TZ\), the remaining sixth generator of \(J\). Consequently all six homogeneous generators of \(J\) are initial coefficients of elements in the subalgebra generated by \(P,Q,R,S,H\). The complete-filtered-algebra lifting lemma (Park--Shin Lemma 8.3) therefore gives a surjection

\[
\mathbb C[[U_1,U_2,U_3,U_4,U_5]]\twoheadrightarrow\widehat R.
\]

In particular, \(\operatorname{edim}(V,0)\le5\).

**(4) Sharpness.** In filtration degrees two and three the independent classes are represented by \(1,x\) in each degree; in degree five, \(z\) survives modulo products of positive-order classes. The multiplication \(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}J_2\to J_4\) is injective, with image spanned by \(1,x,x^2\). Park--Shin Lemma 8.2 therefore identifies these five classes with linearly independent pieces of the cotangent space, and their Proposition 8.6 proves \(\operatorname{edim}(V,0)\ge5\). Combining both bounds gives equality.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library script `verify.py` reproduces the exact binomial-coefficient exponents, the cancellation of order five, the coefficient \(-z\) in order six, and the generation of all Weierstrass monomial basis elements through degree 100. The universal result does **not** follow from this finite cutoff; it follows from the explicit six-generator semigroup proof, the Mittag--Leffler lifting argument, and the complete-filtration lifting lemma.

To reproduce: run `python3 verify.py`. The expected output begins `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work

Park--Shin (arXiv:2610.21093v1, 15 October 2026), §2, constructs the contractions and establishes their normality. Their Theorem III includes the genus-two zero-defect example. Their Lemmas 8.2--8.4 describe the formal filtration and their Proposition 8.6 proves **only the lower bound five** for genus two. The added ingredient here is the explicit cancellation \(PS-QR\), which generates the degree-six associated-graded class without a sixth analytic generator.

The affine-torsor construction was already described by Grauert (1962); Fujiki (1996), §5, revisits it for holomorphic additive group actions. Fujiki's discussion concerns orbit geometry and extension obstructions rather than the stated exact embedding dimension. No claim is made that the construction, the zero Wahl defect, the theorem on formal functions, or the lifting lemma is new.

## Limitations

The proof concerns the genus-two Weierstrass model with the specified nonzero cocycle. It does not assert exact embedding dimensions for negative-Wahl-defect examples, higher genera, arbitrary neighborhoods, equations of the singularity, or analytic isomorphism classification. The literature comparison does not rule out an implicit earlier calculation of the same embedding dimension in older sources, particularly Grauert's original 1962 treatment. Formal lifting is based on the established theorem on formal functions and does not independently establish convergence of the selected series as functions on a fixed analytic neighborhood.
