# Sharp equilibrium capacity and a unique rationality threshold in binary aspiration dynamics

## Finding

For the positive-temperature, well-mixed, two-strategy aspiration-driven evolutionary game of Aguilar-Janita, Khalil, Leyva, and Sendiña-Nadal, the deterministic mean-field drift has **at most five interior equilibria, counted with multiplicity**, and **at most three locally asymptotically stable equilibria** for every admissible payoff matrix and aspiration. Both bounds are sharp.

More precisely, take the symmetric coordination payoffs \(R=P=1\), \(S=T=-2\), common aspiration \(m=0\), and positive effective temperature \(\theta\). With \(L=1/(2\theta)\), there is a **unique** threshold \(L_*\in(0,3)\) at which two reflection-related, nondegenerate saddle-node folds occur simultaneously. For \(0<L<L_*\) the only equilibrium is the attracting coexistence point \(x=1/2\); at \(L=L_*\) there are exactly three interior equilibria (two double, semistable folds and the simple attracting center); and for \(L>L_*\) there are exactly five simple equilibria, precisely three attracting and two repelling. The center is hyperbolically attracting for every \(L>0\). In temperature terms, \(\theta_*=1/(2L_*)\), with numerically \(\theta_*\approx0.17834937263726\); the implicit characterization, not the decimal, defines the exact threshold.

An exact characterization is provided by the **unique** pair \((L_*,x_*)\in(0,\infty)\times(0,1/3)\) satisfying
\[
G_{L_*}(x_*)=\partial_xG_{L_*}(x_*)=0,
\quad
G_L(x)=1-2x+(1-x)e^{-L(2-3x)}-xe^{L(1-3x)}.
\]
The two folds are located at \(x_*\) and \(1-x_*\). The analytical proof gives existence, uniqueness, multiplicities, and the phase diagram; the numerical estimate is not used to certify any assertion.

## Assumptions and scope

The binary mean-field variable \(x\in[0,1]\) is the cooperation fraction. Let \(R,S,T,P,m\in\mathbb R\), choose \(\theta>0\), and impose nonzero aspiration normalizers
\[
M_C=\max\{|R-m|,|S-m|\}>0,\qquad
M_D=\max\{|T-m|,|P-m|\}>0.
\]
Define the affine normalized payoff–aspiration functions
\[
a(x)=\frac{(R-m)x+(S-m)(1-x)}{\theta M_C},\qquad
b(x)=\frac{(T-m)x+(P-m)(1-x)}{\theta M_D}.
\]
After choosing a unit of time, the mean-field equation is
\[
\dot x=F(x):=\frac{1-x}{1+e^{b(x)}}-\frac{x}{1+e^{a(x)}}.
\]
It is the \(N\to\infty\) version of the published asynchronous aspiration rule, not a statement about the finite-population birth–death chain's stationary distribution. An arbitrary positive time-unit multiplier leaves equilibria and their stability unchanged. The singular zero-temperature rule and undefined zero normalizers are excluded.

## Proof

**1. Uniform zero bound.** Multiplication by positive denominators yields
\[
F(x)\bigl(1+e^{a(x)}\bigr)\bigl(1+e^{b(x)}\bigr)
=G(x):=(1-2x)+(1-x)e^{a(x)}-xe^{b(x)}.
\]
Since \(a,b\) are affine, \(G\) belongs to the exponential-polynomial space generated by \(\{1,x,e^{ux},xe^{ux},e^{vx},xe^{vx}\}\), with real slopes \(u,v\). Coincident exponents only lower the dimension. Every nonzero real exponential polynomial \(\sum_jp_j(x)e^{\lambda_jx}\), with distinct real \(\lambda_j\) and \(\deg p_j<d_j\), has at most \(\sum_jd_j-1\) real zeros counted with multiplicity. Here is a self-contained proof of this standard extended-Chebyshev fact: order the exponents, divide by the positive factor \(e^{\lambda_1x}\), and differentiate. The first polynomial loses a degree while every other term keeps degree at most its previous bound because \(\lambda_j-\lambda_1\ne0\). The total degree budget decreases by one. Rolle's theorem including multiplicities gives at least one fewer zero for the derivative; induction from a single exponential constant proves the bound. If a differentiated expression vanishes identically, the original expression is a single nonzero exponential constant and has no zeros. The endpoint value \(G(0)>0\) establishes \(G\not\equiv0\). Thus \(G\), and therefore \(F\), has at most five zeros on \([0,1]\) counting multiplicities. At the endpoints, \(G(0)=1+e^{a(0)}>0\) and \(G(1)=-1-e^{b(1)}<0\), so equilibria occur only in \((0,1)\) and at least one exists.

Between any two attracting equilibria in an interior one-dimensional autonomous flow there must be another equilibrium: a locally attracting zero has the flow pointing toward it on both sides. Thus \(s\) distinct locally asymptotically stable interior equilibria demand at least \(2s-1\) distinct zeros; the five-zero bound gives \(s\le3\), also when a zero is nonhyperbolic.

**2. Symmetric reduction.** For \(R=P=1\), \(S=T=-2\), \(m=0\), the two normalizers equal \(2\), and the exponents become \(a(x)=L(3x-2)\), \(b(x)=L(1-3x)\), where \(L=1/(2\theta)>0\). The cleared drift is the explicitly displayed \(G_L\), and obeys the exact antisymmetry \(G_L(1-x)=-G_L(x)\). In particular \(x=1/2\) is always an equilibrium. Its derivative is
\[
G_L'(1/2)=-2+e^{-L/2}(3L-2)<0.
\]
Indeed, setting \(z=L/2\), strict negativity is equivalent to \(3z<1+e^z\). The latter holds for all \(z\ge0\): the function \(1+e^z-3z\) has its minimum \(4-3\log3>0\) at \(z=\log3\). Positivity of the denominator relating \(G_L\) to \(F\) makes the center hyperbolically attracting.

For \(1/3\le x<1/2\), the inequality \(e^{L(1-3x)}\le1\) implies
\[
G_L(x)\ge 1-3x+(1-x)e^{-L(2-3x)}>0.
\]
Thus every noncentral equilibrium in the left half lies in \((0,1/3)\). On this interval
\[
\partial_LG_L(x)=-(1-x)(2-3x)e^{-L(2-3x)}-x(1-3x)e^{L(1-3x)}<0.
\]
Moreover \(G_0(x)=2(1-2x)>0\) and \(G_L(x)\to-\infty\) as \(L\to\infty\) for each fixed \(x\in(0,1/3)\). Consequently each such \(x\) possesses one and only one crossing parameter \(L_c(x)>0\), defined by \(G_{L_c(x)}(x)=0\). Implicit differentiation makes \(L_c\) continuous and smooth. As \(x\to0^+\) or \(x\to(1/3)^-\), \(L_c(x)\to\infty\), because \(G_L(0)=1+e^{-2L}>0\) and \(G_L(1/3)=(2/3)e^{-L}>0\) for every finite \(L\). Therefore \(L_*:=\min_{0<x<1/3}L_c(x)\) exists, is positive and finite.

If \(0<L<L_*\), the cleared drift is positive throughout \((0,1/2)\), negative throughout \((1/2,1)\), and has exactly the attracting center. If \(L>L_*\), there is an \(x\in(0,1/3)\) with \(G_L(x)<0\), whereas \(G_L(0)>0\) and \(G_L(1/3)>0\). Hence two distinct left zeros exist; reflection gives two right zeros in addition to the center. The five-zero theorem forces **exactly** five simple zeros. Their alternating signs yield exactly three attractors and two repellers, with no additional equilibria.

At \(L=L_*\), the function is nonnegative on the left half. An interior minimizing crossing \(x_*\) is therefore a zero of even multiplicity, at least two, and reflection supplies another such zero at \(1-x_*\). The central zero is simple. The five-zero budget forces **exactly** these three zeros, with multiplicities \(2,1,2\). In particular, the minimizing point \(x_*\) is unique and \(G_{L_*}''(x_*)>0\); moreover \(\partial_LG_{L_*}(x_*)<0\). Hence the two simultaneous folds are nondegenerate, and the unique pair \((L_*,x_*)\) solves the displayed pair of equations. At the double zeros the field points in the same direction on both sides; they are semistable, not two-sided attractors. This proves the exhaustive parameter phase diagram.

**3. Sharpness by a rational sign certificate.** Choose \(L=3\), equivalently \(\theta=1/6\). At \(x=1/6\),
\[
6G_3(1/6)=4+5e^{-9/2}-e^{3/2}<0.
\]
To check the strict sign without decimals, the first four Taylor terms give \(e^{3/2}>67/16\) and \(e^{9/2}>493/16\). Consequently \(4+5e^{-9/2}<4+80/493<67/16<e^{3/2}\). The other relevant exact signs are \(G_3(0)>0\), \(G_3(1/3)>0\), and the reflected negatives on the right. Therefore five simple equilibria exist for this exact positive-temperature payoff configuration, three attracting. In particular \(L_*<3\), verifying sharp attainability without any root-finding routine.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone program `verify.py` checks the rational exponential-Taylor inequalities used for \(L=3\), the special-case symmetry and denominator-clearing identities at selected rational states using high-precision decimal arithmetic, the location and multiplicities of the fold by numerical diagnostic calculations, and the five alternating drift signs. The proof of the arbitrary-parameter upper bound, existence/uniqueness of the threshold, and all-parameter classifications is the analytical argument above; the finite checks alone are not evidence for those infinite statements. A numerical solution gives \(L_*\approx2.80348617214894\), \(x_*\approx0.18235270718715\), and \(\theta_*\approx0.17834937263726\); these are illustrative approximations.

## Relationship to prior work

Aguilar-Janita et al. (arXiv:2308.00860, first public August 1, 2023; Phys. Rev. E 109, 024107) define the same aspiration-driven mean-field equation. Their Section III (equations 21–22) and Section IV (especially Figure 5) display coexistence and two saddle-node transitions for the symmetric parameter example, estimating a critical effective temperature near \(0.18\). **The existence of bistability, its graphical example, and the approximate transition were already known and are not claimed as discoveries here.** The additional conclusions proved here are the model-wide sharp five-equilibrium/three-attractor ceiling and the exact all-temperature bifurcation classification, including a unique common fold parameter, exact multiplicities, and an implicit characterization. These are not logical consequences of numerical plots alone.

The exponential-polynomial zero bound is an established extended-Chebyshev principle, not a new theorem in approximation theory (see Aldaz–Kounchev–Render, arXiv:0805.1618); its application alone is insufficient for the full phase diagram. The later networked aspiration paper by the same authors (arXiv:2502.03109) investigates topology and updating and explicitly treats the earlier mean-field study as background; it does not furnish the proved sharp symmetric temperature diagram in the portions inspected. The 2022 paper arXiv:2210.01710 uses imitation based on comparisons between agents rather than the aspiration-only rates used here. No assertion of absolute publication priority is made.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns a deterministic infinite-population mean-field ODE and positive temperature. It does not describe finite-population stationary probabilities, random escape times, arbitrary network topology, heterogeneous aspirations, or the discontinuous zero-temperature limit. The exact threshold is implicit; its decimal approximation is diagnostic and is not a certified interval. Published numerical observations already anticipate the appearance of two saddle-node pairs. Although nearby published work and mathematical research records were compared, an undiscovered theorem proving the same all-temperature classification remains a residual publication-priority risk. Independent scientific validation has not been performed.
