# Sharp inballs and center-locus rigidity for complete-bipartite graphical zonotopes

## Finding
Let \(2\le p\le q\) be integers and \(N=p+q\). In the Euclidean hyperplane \(H=\{x\in\mathbb R^N:\sum_i x_i=0\}\), form the centered graphical zonotope
\[
Z_{p,q}=\sum_{\substack{i\in A\\j\in B}}\left[-\frac{e_i-e_j}{2},\frac{e_i-e_j}{2}\right],
\qquad |A|=p,\quad |B|=q.
\]
Its **intrinsic Euclidean inradius** is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
r(Z_{p,q})=\begin{cases}
1,&(p,q)=(2,2),\\
\dfrac p2\sqrt{\dfrac{p+q}{p+q-1}},&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
\]
Except for \(K_{2,2}\), exactly the singleton-cut facet pairs at vertices of minimum graph degree touch the largest ball centered at zero: there are \(q\) antipodal facet pairs when \(p<q\) and \(p+q\) antipodal pairs when \(p=q\ge3\). For \(K_{2,2}\), the two mixed cut pairs (four facets) touch that ball instead.

The set \(\mathcal C_{p,q}\) of **all** centers of balls with the maximum radius has affine dimension
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\dim\mathcal C_{p,q}=\begin{cases}
1,&(p,q)=(2,2),\\
p-1,&p<q,\\
0,&p=q\ge3.
\end{cases}}
\]
Thus even though every one of these zonotopes is centrally symmetric, a maximal insphere is not usually uniquely centered. For the two-row family \(p=2<q\), write \(c=(t,-t,0,\ldots,0)\) in the order \(A,B\). The **entire center set** is precisely
\[
\boxed{\quad |t|\le \frac q2-\sqrt{\frac{\lfloor(q+2)^2/4\rfloor}{q+1}}\quad (q\ge3).}
\]
For the exceptional four-cycle, the entire center set is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_{2,2}=\{(t,t,-t,-t):|t|\le1-\sqrt3/2\}.}
\]
The four-cycle is the unique case in the stated two-parameter family where a nonsingleton cut, rather than a minimum-degree vertex, determines the insphere.

## Assumptions and scope
All edges have the same unit coefficient in the Minkowski sum, and distances are intrinsic to \(H\) with the Euclidean norm inherited from \(\mathbb R^N\). The ordering of coordinates is arbitrary except in the explicit center-locus formulas, where the first \(p\) coordinates index \(A\). A tangent facet means a genuine codimension-one facet whose supporting hyperplane contacts the maximum-radius ball centered at zero. Additional facets may touch balls centered away from zero. The theorem does not cover weighted or general bipartite graphs or altered norms.

For any nonempty proper vertex subset \(S\), denote by \(\partial S\) its graph edge cut, set \(k=|S|\), and write
\[
u_S=\mathbf1_S-\frac{k}{N}\mathbf1,\quad
\|u_S\|^2=\frac{k(N-k)}N,\quad
h_{Z_{p,q}}(u_S)=\frac{|\partial S|}{2}.
\]
If \(a=|S\cap A|\) and \(b=|S\cap B|\), then \(|\partial S|=a(q-b)+b(p-a)\).

## Proof
**1. Cut normals and centered inspheres.** The support function is
\[
h_Z(u)=\frac12\sum_{i\in A,j\in B}|u_i-u_j|\qquad (u\in H).
\]
At a support vector \(u\), the exposed face is a translate of the zonotope generated by those edges satisfying \(u_i=u_j\). The rank of these edge vectors is \(N-c\), where \(c\) is the number of connected components in the graph of zero-difference edges. A facet has dimension \(N-2\), so \(c=2\); its normal is therefore proportional to a cut vector \(u_S\). Conversely, whenever both induced sides are connected, the cut supports a facet. Hence the distance from zero to the supporting hyperplane of a cut facet is
\[
d(S)=\frac{|\partial S|}{2}\sqrt{\frac N{k(N-k)}}.
\tag{1}
\]
This also gives a lower bound when evaluated over all nontrivial subsets, including nonfacial cuts. Central symmetry ensures that the inradius is attained at zero: if both \(c+rB_H\) and its reflection \(-c+rB_H\) are inside \(Z\), their midpoint Minkowski average is \(rB_H\subset Z\).

**2. Spectral exclusion of nonsingleton cuts.** Assume \(p\le q\). The Laplacian matrix of \(K_{p,q}\) is
\[
L=\begin{pmatrix}qI_p&-J_{p,q}\\-J_{q,p}&pI_q\end{pmatrix},
\]
with eigenvalues \(0\), \(p\) (multiplicity \(q-1\)), \(q\) (multiplicity \(p-1\)), and \(N\). Thus every nonempty proper \(S\) satisfies the Rayleigh bound
\[
|\partial S|=u_S^{\mathsf T}Lu_S
\ge p\frac{k(N-k)}N.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(N\ge6\) and \(2\le k\le N-2\),
\[
k(N-k)\ge2(N-2)>\frac{N^2}{N-1}.
\]
Consequently (2) gives the **strict** estimate
\[
|\partial S|>p\sqrt{\frac{k(N-k)}{N-1}},
\quad\text{so}\quad d(S)>\frac p2\sqrt{\frac N{N-1}}.
\tag{3}
\]
Every singleton cut has \(d(S)=\deg(v)\sqrt{N/(N-1)}/2\); its minimum is exactly the right-hand side of (3), attained at the \(q\) vertices of degree \(p\), and also at the other \(p\) vertices when \(p=q\).

The only possibility \(N=5\) is \(K_{2,3}\). For every two- or three-vertex cut, \(k(N-k)=6\) and \(|\partial S|\ge3>2\sqrt{6/4}\), proving the same strict separation. For \(N=4\), the graph is \(K_{2,2}\). Its singleton cuts have support distance \(2/\sqrt3\), its mixed two-vertex cuts have distance \(1\), and its same-color two-vertex cuts have distance \(2\). Therefore the unique exceptional inradius is \(1\), determined by the two antipodal pairs of mixed-cut facets. This completes the radius and centered-contact claims.

**3. Dimensions of the full center sets.** For any cut \(S\), a ball of the already established maximal radius \(r\) and center \(c\in H\) satisfies both antipodal supporting inequalities exactly when
\[
\left|\sum_{i\in S}c_i\right|+r\sqrt{\frac{k(N-k)}N}
\le\frac{|\partial S|}{2}.
\tag{4}
\]
Cut normals contain all facet normals, so (4) for all nontrivial subsets is both necessary and sufficient.

Suppose \(p<q\). Each singleton \(\{j\}\) in \(B\) saturates (4) at \(c=0\); hence every optimal center has \(c_j=0\) for all \(j\in B\). The zero-sum condition leaves the \((p-1)\)-dimensional subspace \(\{c:c_B=0,\sum_{i\in A}c_i=0\}\). All cuts other than those singleton cuts and their complements have strictly positive slack at zero, by step 2, and finitely many constraints cannot eliminate a sufficiently small neighborhood in that subspace. Thus the center set has affine dimension \(p-1\). If \(p=q\ge3\), all singleton cuts are tight, forcing every coordinate of \(c\) to vanish: zero is the unique optimal center.

For \(K_{2,2}\), the two antipodal pairs of mixed-cut facets impose \(c_1=c_2\) and \(c_3=c_4\). The zero-sum equation gives \(c=(t,t,-t,-t)\). Every singleton facet imposes \(|t|+\sqrt3/2\le1\), and these are the only remaining facet inequalities. Hence the exceptional center segment in the statement is exact.

**4. Exact two-row center interval.** Fix \(p=2\), \(q\ge3\). Step 3 gives \(c=(t,-t,0,\ldots,0)\). A cut containing either zero or both vertices of \(A\) has \(\sum_{i\in S}c_i=0\), so it imposes no additional condition beyond containment of the zero-centered ball. For a cut containing exactly one vertex of \(A\) and \(b\) vertices of \(B\), the cut size is \(q\), independent of \(b\), while \(k=b+1\). Formula (4) becomes
\[
|t|\le\frac q2-\sqrt{\frac{(b+1)(q+1-b)}{q+1}},
\quad0\le b\le q.
\]
The product \((b+1)(q+1-b)=k(N-k)\) is maximized at an integer \(k\) nearest \(N/2\), with maximum \(\lfloor N^2/4\rfloor\). The resulting sharp bound is exactly the displayed interval. Strict separation in step 2 proves that its half-length is positive.

## Verification
The included standalone `verify.py` checks all cut sizes with exact rational squared facet distances for \(345\) complete-bipartite graph parameters through \(p=24\), \(q=27\), comprising \(79{,}994\) nontrivial cut-size types. It identifies every minimizing cut type exactly. Independent \(75\)-digit arithmetic checks the full two-row interval constraints for \(3\le q\le70\), plus \(K_{2,2}\), over \(7{,}521\) cut-size types. Replay command: `python3 verify.py`. The program is corroboration only; steps 1–4 prove all integer parameters and all optimal centers claimed.

## Relationship to prior work
Padrol and Poullot (first posted 2025-12-04) study complete-bipartite graphical zonotopes and their deep vertex truncations, with indecomposability and deformation-cone results. Their full-text sections on these objects do not evaluate their Euclidean inballs. Padrol, Pilaud, and Poullot (2021) give normal-fan/deformation-cone descriptions of graphical zonotopes, not intrinsic Euclidean incenter sets.

The public result *Euclidean inradius of weighted graphical zonotopes is a cut expansion constant* gives a general cut-minimum formula, from which equation (1) can be recovered. **That reduction is background, not the novel claim.** It does not evaluate the cut minimum for all complete bipartite parameters, isolate the four-cycle exception, classify all optimal center dimensions, or derive the exact two-row center intervals. The explicit Laplacian sharpness argument and Chebyshev-center classification above supply those missing implications.

The public results *Exact Euclidean radii and tangent facets of vertex-transitive zonotopes* and *Exact Löwner ellipsoid and contact vertices of complete-bipartite graphical zonotopes* address, respectively, a general vertex-transitive subclass and the minimum **enclosing ellipsoid** of the same family. Neither identifies the full maximum-inscribed-ball center loci proved here. The former can be relevant to equal bipartition sizes but does not state the incenter uniqueness exception. Searches for equivalent terminology such as inball, Chebyshev center, and normalized cut expansion did not reveal an exact preexisting complete-bipartite center-locus classification; absence from searched sources is not a proof of global priority.

## Limitations
This is an intrinsic Euclidean metric result for the unweighted standard realization, not a claim about arbitrary affine images, John ellipsoids, or weighted graph zonotopes. General center sets for \(2<p<q\) are classified by exact affine dimension and halfspace inequalities (4), but their vertex/facet enumerations are not determined. Historical priority remains provisional, especially for older formulations in graph Cheeger-constant or normed-space terminology.
