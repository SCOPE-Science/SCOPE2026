The theorem concerns the explicitly defined two-channel Zak energy for the unnormalized first Hermite window at \(ab=1/2\), not an undefined spectral normalization. Its symmetry zeros and Taylor remainder follow from oddness and absolute convergence. The exact derivative sums are real and imaginary in complementary directions. Elliptic polar integration gives the two local area laws. Finite high-precision checks, detailed in `VERIFICATION.md`, support the analytic derivation but are not substituted for it.

Lyubarskii–Nes (arXiv:1108.2684, Theorem 1) already prove the odd-window nonframe obstruction at \(ab=1/2\). Horst–Lemvig–Videbæk (arXiv:2311.01547) study Zak zero symmetries; Faulhuber–Petersen (arXiv:2609.02610, Theorem 1.2) completely classify the first-Hermite frame set. These known results are credited and do not, in their inspected statements and methods, furnish the exact two-channel Hessian, duality coefficients, thin/wide anisotropy scales or local near-zero area. Published-record and literature searches revealed no directly implication-covering statement. Boon–Zak–Zucker (1983) remains a serious residual priority risk because its full text was not accessible. The review therefore does not certify absolute historical novelty.
