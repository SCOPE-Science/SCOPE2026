# Sharp market-share peak and two-exponent ambiguity in a two-district Huff model

## Finding
Consider two fixed competing retail facilities with effective attractiveness ratio \(\lambda>1\), two customer districts with equal and opposite distance ratios \(R>1\), and the fraction \(w\in[0,1]\) of total demand in the district nearer the more attractive facility. In the power-decay Huff choice model, its total expected market share as a function of distance-decay exponent \(\beta\geq0\) is
\[
S(\beta)=w\frac{\lambda R^\beta}{1+\lambda R^\beta}+(1-w)\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+R^\beta}.
\]
The complete response classification is:

1. If \(0\leq w\leq1/2\), \(S\) is strictly decreasing on \([0,\infty)\).
2. If \(1/2<w<\lambda^2/(1+\lambda^2)\), \(S\) increases strictly to one finite, strict global maximum and decreases strictly thereafter. The unique maximizing exponent is
\[
\beta_* =\frac{1}{\log R}\log\!\left(\frac{\lambda\sqrt w-\sqrt{1-w}}{\lambda\sqrt{1-w}-\sqrt w}\right)>0,
\]
and the maximum is exactly
\[
S(\beta_*)=\frac{\lambda(\lambda-2\sqrt{w(1-w)})}{\lambda^2-1}.
\]
3. If \(\lambda^2/(1+\lambda^2)\leq w\leq1\), \(S\) is strictly increasing on \([0,\infty)\).

In every case \(S(0)=\lambda/(1+\lambda)\) and \(\lim_{\beta\to\infty}S(\beta)=w\). Thus, in the intermediate regime each target market share strictly between \(\max\{\lambda/(1+\lambda),w\}\) and \(S(\beta_*)\) is generated by exactly two distinct positive distance-decay exponents. In particular, \(\lambda=2\), \(w=3/5\), \(R=2\), and observed share \(27/40\) have the two exact solutions
\[
\beta_\pm=\log_2\left(\frac{25\pm\sqrt{97}}{12}\right)>0.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The two facilities are fixed, each district is a positive distance \(d\) from its nearer facility and \(D> d\) from its farther facility, and \(R=D/d\). Distances swap between the two districts. The first facility has effective attraction \(\lambda\) times the second's (any fixed original attractiveness exponent is absorbed in this effective ratio). Total potential demand is fixed; its shares in the near-first and near-second districts are \(w\) and \(1-w\). Each shopper's facility-choice probability is proportional to attraction times distance raised to \(-\beta\). No additional alternatives, no-purchase option, endogenous price, capacity, or demand elasticity are present. The statements quantify over every \(\lambda>1\), \(R>1\), \(w\in[0,1]\), and \(\beta\geq0\). Infinite \(\beta\) is a limit, not an admissible finite estimate.

## Proof
Put \(x=R^\beta\geq1\) and \(F(x)=w\lambda x/(1+\lambda x)+(1-w)\lambda/(\lambda+x)\). Direct differentiation gives
\[
F'(x)=\lambda\left(\frac{w}{(1+\lambda x)^2}-\frac{1-w}{(x+\lambda)^2}\right).
\]
For \(0<w<1\), its sign is that of
\[
\sqrt{\frac{w}{1-w}}-\frac{1+\lambda x}{x+\lambda}.
\]
The second term is strictly increasing from \(1\) at \(x=1\) toward \(\lambda\) at \(x\to\infty\), because its derivative is \((\lambda^2-1)/(x+\lambda)^2>0\). Hence \(F'<0\) everywhere beyond \(x=1\) if \(w\leq1/2\); \(F'>0\) everywhere at finite \(x\geq1\) if \(w\geq\lambda^2/(1+\lambda^2)\); and otherwise precisely one sign change from positive to negative occurs. The endpoint values \(w=0,1\) follow directly from the derivative formula. Since \(x=R^\beta\) is strictly increasing, the same classification holds for \(S\).

In the interior case, solving \(F'(x)=0\) after taking positive square roots yields the stated \(x_*\) and \(\beta_*\). Writing \(a=\sqrt{w}\), \(b=\sqrt{1-w}\) gives \(x_*=(\lambda a-b)/(\lambda b-a)\). Substitution, using
\[
1+\lambda x_*=\frac{a(\lambda^2-1)}{\lambda b-a},\qquad x_*+\lambda=\frac{b(\lambda^2-1)}{\lambda b-a},
\]
reduces the peak to \(\lambda(\lambda-2ab)/(\lambda^2-1)\). The strict increase/decrease and the endpoint limits imply the stated double-inverse interval by the intermediate value theorem and uniqueness on each branch.

For the exact example,
\[
F(x)=\frac{2(3x^2+10x+2)}{5(x+2)(2x+1)}.
\]
The equation \(F(x)=27/40\) is equivalent to \(6x^2-25x+22=0\). Its two roots are \((25\pm\sqrt{97})/12\); both exceed one because \(\sqrt{97}<13\). Therefore both are distinct admissible \(x=2^\beta\), proving the claim. The example is an actual one-dimensional geometry: facilities at positions \(0\) and \(3\), districts at \(1\) and \(2\), and first-district demand fraction \(3/5\).

## Verification
The standalone \(\texttt{verify_huff.py}\) reconstructs the exact rational share, the two roots and the maximizing value symbolically using SymPy. It also checks derivative-sign regimes and peak formulas for 5,000 reproducibly seeded numerical parameter draws. Run with \(\texttt{python verify_huff.py}\) in an environment with SymPy installed. These finite draws are supplementary checks; the derivative and intermediate-value arguments above cover all real admissible parameters.

## Relationship to prior work
Huff's probabilistic trading-area model supplies the source choice law, not the classification established here. Griffith (1982) develops a price-elasticity generalization and discusses iterative parameter fitting rather than the exact noninjectivity threshold for an inelastic two-district share. Nakanishi and Cooper (1974) study least-squares estimation for multiplicative competitive interactions. Wieland (2017) studies applied Huff/MCI fitting and optimizing attraction using aggregate sales, generally at a fixed distance exponent. None of the fully inspected material established the displayed \(\lambda^2/(1+\lambda^2)\) phase boundary or the exact two-exponent witness. The original Huff 1964 and Nakanishi–Cooper 1974 complete texts were not available for comparison, so the originality assessment is provisional, not a proof of historical priority.

## Limitations
This is a deliberately symmetric distance matrix, but an asymmetric demand split; the conclusion does not extend automatically to arbitrary distance matrices, three or more retailers, endogenous demand, or simultaneous estimation of attractiveness and distance decay. The exact double inverse concerns only one aggregate share with known distance ratios and effective attractiveness. Measurement noise may obscure the two branches. Earlier Huff work exists in 1962–1963; its exact first public day was not verified from the available bibliographic records, whereas the selected dated 1964 article has an explicit July 1 indexing date. The originality comparison is limited by inaccessible historical full texts and possible general rational-mixture results.
