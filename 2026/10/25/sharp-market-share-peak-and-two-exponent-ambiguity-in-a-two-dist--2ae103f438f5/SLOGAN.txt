For two competing retail facilities and two mirrored-distance customer districts, the stronger facility's aggregate Huff market share has exactly one peak in distance friction if and only if the population fraction nearer it lies strictly between \(1/2\) and \(\lambda^2/(1+\lambda^2)\). This gives a sharp, explicitly solvable failure of identifying distance decay from one aggregate market share: two different exponents can reproduce exactly the same sales share.
