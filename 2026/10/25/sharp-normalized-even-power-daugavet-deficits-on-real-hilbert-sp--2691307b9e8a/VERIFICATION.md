# Reproducibility and verification

The operator is reduced exactly to the two-dimensional real matrix displayed in the proof, and the derivation of the norm uses the determinant and Frobenius trace of that concrete block. The scalar derivative establishes the square optimum without numerical root finding. The high-degree boundary layer uses uniform compact convergence and an explicit exponentially decaying tail bound, so no finite enumeration is relied upon for the asymptotic.

`CHECK.py` recomputes the scalar formula using ordinary floating-point arithmetic, independently using the two-by-two matrix invariants, and compares numerical maxima to the exact square and Lambert limits for selected even degrees. A floating-point grid does not independently certify an infinite-dimensional theorem or absolute originality. It does not test odd degrees, higher-rank operators, or non-Hilbert Banach spaces.

The mathematical research statement and limitations are documented in `RESULT.md`; external independent audit has not been performed.
