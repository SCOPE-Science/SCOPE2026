With \(2k\) metric agents and \(k\) additive-distance facilities, **any** choice of \(k\) agent locations costs at most \(3-2/k\) times the unrestricted optimum, and the constant is sharp. Consequently, nominating one representative from each of \(k\) two-agent groups attains this exact worst-case ratio for every deterministic local rule; the matching lower-bound example was known, while the universal metric upper bound is the contribution.
