# Scientific review: quadratic spherical-lune concentration

## Correctness — PASS
The statement fixes dimension \(n\ge3\), nonparallel/nonantipodal hemisphere normals, probability surface measure, and the complex exact-degree-two harmonic space. Its proof computes beta and transverse-sphere moments, decomposes the trace-free quadratic representation into invariant blocks, and explicitly diagonalizes the only non-scalar block. The claimed transition is the sign of the unique positive root of a quadratic polynomial evaluated against \(1\), giving the exact threshold \(\cos L=2/n\). The strict upper-branch comparisons and top-eigenspace characterization are complete on \(0<L<\pi\). The independent checker reconstructs the full concentration Gram matrix in a different basis and checks spectra, traces, Rayleigh optimizers, and crossover at 32 dimension-aperture pairs; it supports, but does not replace, the analytical proof.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge assessment)
The general spherical Slepian matrix characterization of Simons–Dahlen–Wieczorek (2004/2006) is an essential precursor. Michel–Plattner–Seibert (2021) develops spin-weighted Slepian operators, with commuting simplification for caps. Seto–Wei–Zhu (2020) gives Dirichlet-Laplacian eigenvalues on spherical lunes, which are different from energy concentration of globally smooth harmonic quadratics. The close published hemispherical Slepian half-energy multiplicity formula addresses a single hemisphere, not intersections of two nonidentical hemispheres. A previously reported two-cap quadratic-operator commutator concerns a commutator of compressed cap operators, not the compression of the intersection indicator. Directly evaluated spectra and the precise dimension-dependent optimizer transition were not found in the inspected portions or targeted search results. General numerical matrix formulations alone do not explicitly supply this sharp, all-dimensional parameter classification. An equivalent specialized paper or unpublished calculation remains possible; independent audit is needed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The theorem gives an exact spectrum and equality classification for a natural non-axisymmetric Slepian region, uniformly in dimension and aperture. The switch from a one-dimensional anisotropic quadratic extremizing space to a transverse mixed-mode space of dimension \(n-2\), with a unique critical aperture and a jump to multiplicity \(n-1\), is a qualitative phenomenon that does not follow from the trace or generic Slepian eigenproblem alone. The full spectrum makes the example usable as an exact benchmark for spherical localization algorithms. The result is limited to degree two; higher-degree and general-region classification remain open in this analysis.
