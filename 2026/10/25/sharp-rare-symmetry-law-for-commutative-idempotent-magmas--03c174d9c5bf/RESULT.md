# Sharp rare-symmetry law for commutative idempotent magmas

## Finding

For each integer \(n\ge 3\), choose uniformly from the \(n^{\binom n2}\) binary operations \(\star\) on \([n]\) satisfying \(x\star y=y\star x\) and \(x\star x=x\). No associativity or quasigroup axiom is imposed. Write \(A_n=([n],\star)\), and let \(E_n\) be the event that \(\operatorname{Aut}(A_n)\) is nontrivial.

Set
\[
 m_n=\binom{n-2}{2}+1,\qquad
 p_n=n^{-(n-2)}\left(1-\frac2n\right)^{m_n}.
\]
For each particular transposition \(\tau\in S_n\), the probability that \(\tau\) is an automorphism of \(A_n\) is **exactly** \(p_n\). Moreover,
\[
 \Pr(E_n)=\binom n2 p_n\,(1+o(1))
 =\frac{e^4}{2}\,e^{-n}n^{4-n}(1+o(1)).
\]
Conditional on \(E_n\), with probability tending to one the **entire** automorphism group is generated by exactly one transposition. Equivalently, the asymptotic probability of any nontrivial symmetry other than a unique transposition is negligible compared with \(\Pr(E_n)\).

There is a quantitative bound. For \(n\ge20\), define
\[
 B_n=\frac{n^{-3(3n-12)/8}}{1-n^{-(3n-12)/8}}+
 n!\,n^{-n(n-2)/8}.
\]
Then
\[
 \left|\Pr(E_n)-\binom n2 p_n\right|
 \le \left(1+\binom n2\right)B_n,
\]
and the probability of a symmetry moving three or more elements is at most \(B_n\). In particular, \(B_n=o(p_n/n^2)\).

As a consequence of the same orbit estimates, the number \(u_n\) of isomorphism classes of such magmas satisfies the first-correction asymptotic
\[
 u_n=\frac{n^{\binom n2}}{n!}\left[1+\binom n2 p_n(1+o(1))\right].
\]
This last statement refines the known exact Burnside enumeration; the rarity law and conditional automorphism-group classification are the central results.

## Assumptions and scope

The uniform distribution is **labeled**: for each unordered pair of distinct elements of \([n]\), its product is chosen independently and uniformly from \([n]\). Diagonal products are forced to equal their arguments. Automorphisms are bijections of the underlying set preserving this single operation. All probabilities refer to this distribution. The asymptotic variable is the integer \(n\to\infty\).

For \(n=1,2\), nontrivial automorphisms are impossible, but the exact transposition formula is only asserted for \(n\ge3\). The quantitative tail estimate above is asserted only for \(n\ge20\); the asymptotic results concern arbitrarily large \(n\).

## Proof

**1. Exact orbit formula.** Let \(\mathcal E_n\) be the set of unordered two-element subsets of \([n]\), with \(N=|\mathcal E_n|=\binom n2\). Given a permutation \(\sigma\in S_n\), let \(\mathcal O(\sigma)\) denote its orbits on \(\mathcal E_n\). For any integer \(\ell\ge1\), write \(f_\sigma(\ell)=|\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^\ell)|\), where the fixed set is taken on \([n]\). Then
\[
 \Pr(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(A_n))
 =n^{-N}\prod_{O\in\mathcal O(\sigma)}f_\sigma(|O|).
 \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, products at positions in one input-pair orbit must be images under successive powers of \(\sigma\) of one initial output. Consistency on closing the orbit requires the initial output to be fixed by \(\sigma^{|O|}\), giving exactly \(f_\sigma(|O|)\) choices. Distinct input-pair orbits have independent choices. The diagonal identities are automatically equivariant. This proves (1), including any zero factors.

**2. Transpositions.** If \(\tau\) swaps two elements, the input-pair action has exactly \(m_n=\binom{n-2}{2}+1\) fixed pairs (including the swapped pair itself), and \(n-2\) orbits of length two. Outputs at fixed pairs must be fixed by \(\tau\), giving \(n-2\) choices each. Outputs on a two-cycle have \(n\) choices at one position and are then forced at the other. Formula (1) gives
\[
 \Pr(\tau\in\operatorname{Aut}(A_n))
 =\frac{(n-2)^{m_n}n^{n-2}}{n^N}
 =p_n.
 \tag{2}
\]
Consequently, if \(T_n\) counts the transpositions contained in \(\operatorname{Aut}(A_n)\), then \(\mathbb E T_n=\binom n2p_n\) exactly.

**3. Uniform suppression of larger-support symmetries.** Suppose \(\sigma\) moves exactly \(k\ge3\) elements. Put \(o(\sigma)=|\mathcal O(\sigma)|\) and \(D(\sigma)=N-o(\sigma)\). Each input-pair orbit contributes at most \(n\) initial-output choices to (1), hence
\[
 \Pr(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(A_n))\le n^{-D(\sigma)}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Among the \(k(n-k)\) input pairs with exactly one moved endpoint, each of the \(n-k\) fixed endpoints sees at most \(k/2\) orbits, since all cycles on the moved set have length at least two. These pairs therefore contribute at least \(k(n-k)/2\) to \(D(\sigma)\).

Among the \(\binom k2\) pairs having both endpoints moved, the only singleton input-pair orbits are the pairs of elements that form a two-cycle of \(\sigma\). There are at most \(k/2\) such singleton orbits; every other orbit has length at least two. The contribution to \(D(\sigma)\) is therefore at least \(k(k-2)/4\). Adding the two contributions yields
\[
 D(\sigma)\ge\frac{k(2n-k-2)}4.\tag{4}
\]
The number of permutations with support size \(k\) is at most \(n^k\). For \(3\le k\le n/2\), (4) gives \(D(\sigma)\ge k(3n-4)/8\); the sum of (3) over such permutations is at most
\[
 \sum_{k=3}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}n^{-k(3n-12)/8}
 \le\frac{n^{-3(3n-12)/8}}{1-n^{-(3n-12)/8}}.
\]
For \(k>n/2\), (4) implies \(D(\sigma)\ge n(n-2)/8\); summing crudely over at most \(n!\) permutations gives the other term in \(B_n\). Therefore, writing \(F_n\) for the event that some automorphism moves at least three points, \(\Pr(F_n)\le B_n\).

**4. From expected transpositions to existence and group structure.** Outside \(F_n\), two different transpositions cannot both be automorphisms: their product would be an automorphism moving at least three elements. Thus, outside \(F_n\), either the automorphism group is trivial or it is generated by exactly one transposition. Since \(0\le T_n\le\binom n2\), comparison of \(T_n\) with the indicator of \(E_n\) gives
\[
 \bigl|\Pr(E_n)-\mathbb E T_n\bigr|
 \le\left(1+\binom n2\right)\Pr(F_n)
 \le\left(1+\binom n2\right)B_n.
 \tag{5}
\]
The ratio of the right side of (5) to \(\binom n2p_n\) tends to zero: the first term in \(B_n\) decays on the scale \(n^{-9n/8+O(1)}\) and the second superexponentially relative to \(n^{-n}e^{-n}\). This proves the first asymptotic and, because all exceptional nontrivial groups are contained in \(F_n\), the conditional group-structure statement.

**5. Leading constant and unlabeled correction.** Taylor expansion gives
\[
 m_n\log(1-2/n)=-n+4+O(1/n),
\]
so (2) yields
\[
 \binom n2p_n=\frac{e^4}{2}e^{-n}n^{4-n}(1+O(1/n)).
\]
Finally, Burnside's lemma says
\[
 u_n=\frac{n^N}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}
 \Pr(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(A_n)).
\]
The identity contributes one, all transpositions together contribute exactly \(\binom n2p_n\), and the sum of all remaining terms is at most \(B_n=o(\binom n2p_n)\). This proves the last display of the finding.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone Python checker requires only the standard library. Running `python verify_ci_magma_symmetry.py` reproduces `verify_ci_magma_symmetry.out` and the final marker `ALL_CI_MAGMA_SYMMETRY_CHECKS_PASSED`.

It independently enumerates all \(27\) labeled commutative idempotent magmas of order \(3\) and all \(4096\) of order \(4\), tests each permutation directly against each operation table, and independently checks the pair-orbit formula. The nontrivial-automorphism counts are respectively \(9\) and \(400\); the counts having full automorphism group generated by a single transposition are \(6\) and \(336\). It also reconstructs the \(7\) and \(192\) isomorphism classes and the \(4\) and \(38\) symmetric isomorphism classes, respectively.

For every permutation on up to seven points, the checker evaluates (1), checks the deficit inequality (4), and verifies (2). These are finite checks only; the asymptotic and the explicit tail bound follow from steps 3–5 of the proof, not from computational extrapolation.

## Relationship to prior work

Bergman and Szendrei [1, Lemma 5.4] proved qualitative almost-sure asymmetry for random models of idempotent linear conditions; their argument uses a general upper bound, not the exact rare-symmetry probability or the conditional stabilizer structure given here. Tureček [2, Theorems 4 and 10] established Burnside enumerations of unrestricted finite magmas and arbitrary-arity operations. The finite commutative-idempotent orbit counts are also catalogued in OEIS A030257 [3], and the small-order counts with nontrivial symmetry in A030259 [4]. These exact orbit counts are not presented as new.

The present claim concerns a different statistic from the ordinary Burnside isomorphism-class count: the labeled probability of at least one nonidentity automorphism and the typical **complete** automorphism group conditional on that event, together with its sharp constant. The passage from an expected number of automorphisms to an existence probability requires the uniform support estimate and the exclusion of multiple simultaneous transpositions. The leading correction to the class count is stated only as a consequence. Cardó [5] also discusses qualitative asymmetry of unrestricted random groupoids. His accessible abstract, not the complete text, was examined, so an equivalent stronger formulation there remains a priority risk.

## Limitations

The theorem does not provide a closed formula for the exact number of labeled nonrigid operations at arbitrary finite \(n\), and it does not classify the exceptional automorphism groups whose probability is negligible. The checker exhausts operation tables only through order four; its larger-order permutation checks do not enumerate all operation tables. The originality comparison is based on accessible primary text and indexed records, not exhaustive worldwide literature or an independent mathematical audit.
