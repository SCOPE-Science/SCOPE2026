# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** A reachable complete deterministic undirected automaton has a transitive group of involutive letter permutations. The reverse-subset construction is itself undirected, every reachable subset is distinguishable by moving a point from the symmetric difference to the original start state, and the exact orbit/stabilizer formula follows. The ternary construction contains both a full cycle (a product of two reflections) and a neighboring swap, so generates the full symmetric group. For binary letters, the group is generated by two involutions and is cyclic-by-two; transitivity bounds its order by twice the state count. Divisibility and a reflection stabilizer rule out the exceptional orders four and five, yielding the exact alphabet threshold. An independent finite implementation checked all stated sample ranges; finite checks were not substituted for the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS (with residual indexing risk).** Kutrib–Malcher–Schneider (DCFS 2018; *Acta Informatica*, published online 2021), full journal Sections 3–4 and 6, explicitly posed DUFA reversal closure and its state complexity as unresolved when multiple states accept. Hospodár–Mlynárčik (2020), full author-posted text Lemma 7 and Theorem 11, proves a central binomial bound for broader *permutation automata*; the sharp binary witness uses a genuinely noninvolutive full cycle, hence gives neither the DUFA sharp construction nor the three-letter necessity. Their upper-bound and minimality techniques are appropriately acknowledged, not passed off as novel. Targeted searches of published literature found no logically dominating result. The literature is not guaranteed exhaustive, and independently verifying absence of a later resolution remains worthwhile.

**Value — PASS.** The work answers two specifically motivated open problems—whether DUFA languages are reversal closed and what the exact sharp reversal-state growth is—while the group orbit formula resolves each individual automaton exactly. The smallest alphabet achieving the global exponential extremal complexity is a substantive qualitative separation from the known permutation-automaton binary witnesses; it is not an arbitrary parameter slice.

**Limits and comparison.** The statement applies to complete deterministic undirected automata and to full deterministic reversal minimization; it does not transfer to nondeterministic undirected automata, for which nonclosure under reversal is known. The binomial upper bound itself is inherited from the permutation superclass. The novelty claim is the involutive specialization with sharp realizations and alphabet necessity, conditional on the available literature record.
