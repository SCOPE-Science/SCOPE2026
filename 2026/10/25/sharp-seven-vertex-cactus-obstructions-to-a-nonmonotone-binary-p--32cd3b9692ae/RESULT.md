# Sharp seven-vertex cactus obstructions to a nonmonotone binary public-goods equilibrium

## Finding
Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite simple undirected interaction graph. Each vertex chooses \(x_v\in\{0,1\}\) according to the same strict binary best-response rule
\[
x_v=T\!\left(\sum_{u:\,uv\in E}x_u\right).
\]
For the **picky rule** \(T(k)=1\) exactly when \(k\in\{0,2\}\), the first connected cactus graph without a pure Nash equilibrium has seven vertices. Among the connected seven-vertex cacti, exactly **two nonisomorphic graphs** have no equilibrium: (i) two four-cycles sharing exactly one vertex and (ii) two triangles sharing exactly one vertex with a pendant leaf attached to one noncentral vertex of each triangle. There are exactly \(3150\) labelled counterexamples among the \(111982\) connected labelled seven-vertex cacti.

A structural existence theorem explains the boundary: if all simple cycles of \(G\) are pairwise **vertex-disjoint**, then an equilibrium exists for *every* binary rule satisfying \(T(0)=T(2)=1\) and \(T(k)=0\) for every positive odd integer \(k\), irrespective of its values at even integers at least four. An equilibrium is constructible by Gaussian elimination over \(\mathbb F_2\).

## Assumptions and scope
Vertices are finitely many strategic individuals with binary actions; the only input to each best response is the **number** of active neighbors, not the individual's own action. The response at each nonnegative integer is unique. A cactus is a simple graph in which any two simple cycles share at most one vertex; connected cacti are enumerated up to ordinary graph isomorphism. The general existence statement assumes the stronger condition that no two distinct cycles share a vertex; this permits trees, unicyclic graphs, and several vertex-disjoint cycles joined by bridges. The sharp seven-vertex classification applies specifically to the picky response \(T(k)=\mathbf 1_{\{0,2\}}(k)\). All numbers count the labelled graphs on the standard vertex set, unless described as nonisomorphic types.

## Proof
**Parity existence lemma.** Let \(A\) be the binary adjacency matrix and work over \(\mathbb F_2\). Put \(M=I+A\). For any \(y\in\ker M\), the symmetry of \(A\) and cancellation of off-diagonal terms give \(y^{\mathsf T}Ay=0\). Consequently
\[
y^{\mathsf T}\mathbf1=y^{\mathsf T}My=0.
\]
Thus \(\mathbf1\) is perpendicular to \(\ker M\). Because \(M\) is symmetric, \(\operatorname{im}M=(\ker M)^\perp\); so \(Mx=\mathbf1\) is solvable. At a solution, a selected vertex \(x_v=1\) has an **even** number of selected neighbors, whereas an unselected vertex has an **odd** number. The selected induced subgraph therefore has all degrees even. If simple cycles are vertex-disjoint, any Eulerian subgraph has degree at most two at every vertex: its edge set decomposes into cycles, and two cycles cannot meet at a vertex. Hence selected vertices have exactly zero or two selected neighbors, and the specified values of \(T\) make them best responders. Every unselected vertex has an odd positive number of selected neighbors, so it too is a best responder. This constructs a pure equilibrium. Gaussian elimination costs \(O(|V|^3)\) field operations.

**The two counterexamples.** For two four-cycles meeting at center \(c\), fix the central action \(s\in\{0,1\}\). On either arm \(c-u-v-w-c\), the equations at \(u\) and \(w\) give \(x_u=x_w=1\) exactly when \(s=x_v\). Then \(v\) sees either zero or two selected neighbors and so \(x_v=1\). It follows that both immediate neighbors of \(c\) on each cycle equal \(s\). Hence \(c\) sees four selected neighbors if \(s=1\), and zero if \(s=0\). In either case \(T\) requires action \(1-s\), contradiction.

For two triangles sharing center \(c\), attach one pendant leaf to one noncentral vertex on each triangle. On one arm write the triangle \(c-a-b-c\), with pendant leaf \(d\) on \(b\). Fix \(x_c=s\). The leaf equation gives \(x_d=1-x_b\). The equation at \(a\) gives \(x_a=1\) iff \(s=x_b\). Substitution in the equation at \(b\) has the unique solution \((x_a,x_b,x_d)=(0,1,0)\) when \(s=0\), and \((1,1,0)\) when \(s=1\). With two identical arms, the center sees two selected neighbors when \(s=0\), forcing \(x_c=1\); or four when \(s=1\), forcing \(x_c=0\). Contradiction in both cases.

**Exhaustive minimality and classification.** The standalone `verify.py` uses standard-library integer arithmetic, with no random sampling. For each \(n\le6\), it visits every simple graph on the fixed vertex set, retaining precisely the connected cacti. It tests all \(2^n\) strategy profiles and also independently checks the parity certificate above where applicable. All \(6075\) connected labelled cacti of order at most six admit an equilibrium. At order seven, any connected cactus has \(6\) through \(9\) edges, because each new cycle consumes at least two vertices and the cyclomatic number is at most three. The program enumerates every subset of the \(21\) possible edges of cardinality \(6\), \(7\), \(8\), or \(9\), retains the connected cacti via exact biconnected-block checks, and tests all \(128\) action profiles. This yields \(111982\) cacti, exactly \(3150\) without equilibrium. It partitions those failing graphs by an explicit bijection-preserving adjacency isomorphism test and obtains exactly two classes, distinguished by degree multisets \(\{2,2,2,2,2,2,4\}\) and \(\{1,1,2,2,3,3,4\}\). Their representatives are precisely the two analytically checked constructions above. As the enumeration runs over **all** labelled edge subsets of the permissible cardinalities, it is exhaustive, not a sampled computational search.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` using Python 3.9 or later; the program requires only the Python standard library. Expected final output reports `seven_vertex_labelled_cacti= 111982 no_equilibrium= 3150 isomorphism_classes= 2`, together with successful exhaustive checks of all connected labelled cacti on at most six vertices. Computation verifies only the finite classification; the unrestricted-order existence theorem is proved algebraically above. The exact graph-isomorphism routine compares adjacencies under explicit bijections and does not use hash-based canonical labels or approximate floating-point tests.

## Relationship to prior work
Klimm and Stahlberg's 2023 binary network public-goods paper defines the picky response and proves general-graph equilibrium-existence complexity results, including NP-completeness for this response; the present result instead identifies the sharp *cactus* order-seven boundary and proves a graph-structural existence condition. Gilboa independently studies nonmonotone binary public-goods games and complexity, not the stated cactus census. Yu, Zhou, Brantingham and Vorobeychik develop tree algorithms for equilibrium existence; an algorithm deciding whether an equilibrium exists is not itself a universal existence theorem. A classical odd-domination theorem, described by Caro, Klostermeyer and Yuster, supplies the parity-system construction at the heart of the existence proof. The general odd-domination theorem alone does not assert that the resulting selected-neighbor counts are restricted to zero or two, nor classify the first picky-rule cactus obstructions; this restriction is obtained here by the vertex-disjoint-cycle hypothesis. The seven-vertex boundary is therefore the essential new finite structural classification, with the parity construction used transparently as supporting prior theory.

## Limitations
The sharp census is restricted to connected cacti of at most seven vertices; it does not characterize all larger cacti, all connected graphs, or arbitrary heterogeneous players. The general existence lemma covers only graphs with **vertex-disjoint** cycles, and the claimed best-response conditions are sufficient rather than necessary. Although the all-size existence proof is independent of enumeration, its polynomial-time construction applies only under those stated graph and response assumptions. A closely equivalent graph-theoretic or game-theoretic treatment beyond the inspected literature could affect publication priority; an independent audit has not been performed.
