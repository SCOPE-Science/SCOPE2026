**Correctness: PASS.** Each edge's mass and stiffness matrices are explicitly defined, and the global mass matrix is SPD. The generalized eigenvalues are exactly \(0\), \(3/\ell^2\), and \(12/\ell^2\), with explicit mass-orthogonal projectors. The resulting entry classes reduce all nonnegative entries to (i) the sharp lower constraint \(x\ge1/(6\theta)\) and (ii) one part-size-dependent quadratic diagonal test. Its sign and derivative at the lower boundary establish all phase transitions. The same formulas were independently compared with directly inverted rational matrices in 2,952 cases; these are supporting finite checks, not substitutes for proof.

**Originality: PASS, with stated residual risk.** Li–Huang's full-text Theorem 3.1 gives sufficient mesh-dependent two-sided time-step restrictions for the standard finite-element \(\theta\)-scheme on simplicial domains, not an exact Kirchhoff-biclique if-and-only-if result. Jin–Lazarov–Thomée–Zhou study subdiffusion and backward-Euler-based convolution quadrature on polygonal domains; their nonnegativity obstruction does not imply the all-\(\theta\) boundary or upper-root formula here. Relevant indexed mathematical findings and accessible earlier records were checked for same objects, projector reductions, special cases, and stronger coverage. Previously studied consistent-mass interval, periodic-cycle, and tree cases are acknowledged, particularly the overlapping \(K_{2,2}\) Crank–Nicolson special case. A 2015 Chatzipantelidis–Horváth–Thomée article was available through abstract/metadata only and remains a residual risk; novelty is not equated with failed search.

**Value: PASS.** This is a complete positivity-preserving parameter-phase diagram for a natural meshed wire network with junctions and cycles, including the isolated nonzero admissible step at \(\theta=1/3\), a topology-dependent transition from a bounded window to a half-line, and an exact criterion based only on the larger partition size. These conditions govern whether all nonnegative nodal data remain nonnegative after a step. The finite upper bound is genuinely sharper than general sufficient entrywise-coefficient restrictions; for instance Crank–Nicolson on \(K_{2,2}\) allows steps up to \((1+\sqrt3)\ell^2/3\) whereas nonnegativity of the right-hand coefficient matrix alone only ensures \(t\le2\ell^2/3\). The mechanism is motivated by positivity and maximum-principle enforcement, not arbitrary graph enumeration.
