# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The domain is every real \(r>0\) and every translation \((t,u)\). Vertical sections first reduce the optimization to \(u=0\). Both full-containment plateaus follow from necessary and sufficient vertex inequalities, including their sharp endpoints. In the strict middle regime, the verified strict ordering of section breakpoints yields exact antiderivatives with local form \(M(r)-2(t-t_*)^2\). The known Brunn--Minkowski concavity of the square-root overlap converts local optimality to the unique global horizontal optimum; equality of half-widths at two interior crossings excludes nonzero vertical shifts. The precise equal-size centroid gap is an algebraic corollary. Independent polygon clipping on eleven radii and 1,980 translations, with sixteen local tests, passed. A finite test grid alone would not prove global optimization; the analytic concavity argument does.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The problem is a canonical nontrivial instance of shape matching and centroid-alignment efficiency: a regular triangle and a regular square have individually strong symmetries but lack a common nontrivial rotational symmetry. The complete piecewise-sharp optimum, two geometrically meaningful contact transitions, a unique intermediate optimizer, and an exact quantitative counterexample to center alignment classify an entire natural size family. This is not just a generic optimizer invocation or a trivial rescaling of a known symmetric solution.
