# Sharp two-frequency inverse reconstruction of a pinned-lattice mass and substrate impurity

## Finding

Consider the infinite linear monatomic spring lattice described below. Its host onsite pinning is known and positive, but the mass and positive onsite spring at one impurity are unknown. Let the observed squared frequencies of **two spatially localized normal modes** satisfy

\[
0<z_-<\kappa,\qquad z_+>\kappa+4.
\]

Define the unique exterior-band decay factors

\[
a=\kappa+2-z_->2,\quad b=z_+-\kappa-2>2,\qquad
p=\frac{2}{a+\sqrt{a^2-4}},\quad q=\frac{2}{b+\sqrt{b^2-4}}.
\]

These measurements are generated by an impurity of **strictly positive mass and strictly positive onsite pinning**, with all host bonds unaltered, **if and only if**

\[
\boxed{\kappa pq>(1-p)(1+q).}
\]

Whenever this holds, the two unknowns are **uniquely** reconstructed by

\[
\boxed{\mu=\frac{2pq}{1+pq},\qquad
\kappa_0=\frac{2[\kappa pq-(1-p)(1+q)]}{1+pq}.}
\]

The reconstructed infinite lattice possesses exactly these two (simple) square-summable frequencies outside its host band and no other point-spectrum frequencies. This is an exact inverse **image and uniqueness** theorem. The broader forward phenomenon of localized modes below and above a host band is already documented in impurity-lattice literature; it is not claimed as a new discovery.

## Assumptions and scope

Every site \(n\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\) has mass one; every nearest-neighbour connecting spring has stiffness one; every nondefect site is attached to a fixed substrate by a spring with stiffness \(\kappa>0\). At \(n=0\), mass is \(\mu>0\) and substrate spring stiffness is \(\kappa_0>0\), with the two adjacent connecting springs **unchanged**. All motion is small, linear, undamped and scalar. Frequencies are nonnegative and \(z=\omega^2\). The normal-mode equations are

\[
z u_n=(\kappa+2)u_n-u_{n-1}-u_{n+1}\quad(n\ne0),\qquad
\mu z u_0=(\kappa_0+2)u_0-u_{-1}-u_1.
\]

A localized mode has nonzero displacement vector \(u\in\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\). The host has one continuous squared-frequency band \([\kappa,\kappa+4]\). Measurements refer to the **same defect** and both sides of this one band; no claim concerns disorder, finite lattices or independently altered connecting springs.

## Proof

**1. Location and form of possible modes.** Write \(L\) for the stiffness matrix and \(M\) for the diagonal mass matrix. The bounded selfadjoint generalized-frequency operator \(M^{-1/2}LM^{-1/2}\) differs from its homogeneous counterpart only by a finite-rank operator, so its essential spectrum is \([\kappa,\kappa+4]\). The quadratic form

\[
\langle u,Lu\rangle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}|u_{n+1}-u_n|^2+\kappa\sum_{n\ne0}|u_n|^2+\kappa_0|u_0|^2
\]

is strictly positive on every nonzero square-summable vector, ruling out nonpositive normal-mode squared frequencies. Inside the band the recurrence has unit-modulus characteristic roots, while at its edges it has repeated roots with at most polynomial factors. No nonzero such half-line solution is square-summable. The recurrence at site \(n=1\) (or \(n=-1\)) also shows that a nonzero square-summable mode must have \(u_0\ne0\) and symmetric tails. Consequently every localized mode is proportional to \(u_n=r^{|n|}\), with \(r\in(0,1)\) below the band and \(r\in(-1,0)\) above it. Its frequency and defect matching condition are

\[
z=\kappa+2-r-r^{-1},\qquad F(r):=\kappa_0+2-2r-\mu(\kappa+2-r-r^{-1})=0.
\]

**2. Exact spectral count.** On each of \((-1,0)\) and \((0,1)\),

\[
F'(r)=-2+\mu(1-r^{-2})<0.
\]

Moreover, \(F(0+)\to+\infty\), \(F(0-)\to-\infty\), \(F(1)=\kappa_0-\mu\kappa\) and \(F(-1)=\kappa_0+4-\mu(\kappa+4)\). Thus one lower-band localized mode exists exactly when \(\kappa_0<\mu\kappa\), and one upper-band mode exactly when \(\kappa_0+4>\mu(\kappa+4)\). Each is simple; none else can exist. Equality gives a non-square-summable band-edge resonance, not a localized mode. This supplies a forward classification used only as a lemma in the inverse proof.

**3. Necessary reconstruction.** By the exterior recurrence the measured modes must have \(r_-=p\in(0,1)\) and \(r_+=-q\in(-1,0)\), as given above. Their defect equations are

\[
\kappa_0+2-2p=\mu z_-,\qquad
\kappa_0+2+2q=\mu z_+.
\]

The difference, together with

\[
z_+-z_-=(p+q)\left(1+\frac1{pq}\right),
\]

forces \(\mu=2pq/(1+pq)\). Substitution uniquely gives \(\kappa_0=2[\kappa pq-(1-p)(1+q)]/(1+pq)\). Since \(p,q\in(0,1)\), the recovered mass is positive and strictly less than one. Positivity of the recovered onsite pinning is **equivalent** to the stated strict inequality. Any physically admissible pair must therefore satisfy it.

**4. Sufficiency and uniqueness.** Conversely, given an exterior-frequency pair satisfying the inequality, these formulas define \(\mu>0\) and \(\kappa_0>0\). Direct substitution satisfies both defect matching equations. The sequences \(p^{|n|}\) and \((-q)^{|n|}\) are legitimate nonzero square-summable eigenmodes at the prescribed two frequencies. Step 2 rules out any additional localized mode, and Step 3 rules out a second pair of positive physical parameters. The strict inequality is necessary and sufficient, including the open physical boundary: at equality it yields \(\kappa_0=0\), which is excluded here. \(\square\)

## Verification

An executable Python checker accompanies this result. It tests 1,600 random positive parameter triples against the exact count and recurrence; reconstructs 337 randomly encountered two-mode triples from only their two squared frequencies; compares eight strongly localized infinite-lattice predictions with extremal eigenvalues of independent 601-site generalized symmetric tridiagonal matrices; and checks 98 additional physically relevant inverse-feasibility probes. All tests passed in an extracted copy of the supplied checker. Numerical checks corroborate formulas but are **not** a proof of the global infinite-lattice theorem; that proof is above.

To reproduce with Python 3, NumPy and SciPy installed, run `python check_modes.py`. The checker uses a fixed random seed; the finite truncation intentionally selects modes whose decay factors have magnitude below \(0.82\), avoiding false near-edge discrepancies from finite-size boundary effects.

## Relationship to prior work

Localized lattice modes and physically meaningful impurity parameters are established objects. Boechler and coauthors (2011) analyze defect modes in compressed granular chains without positive distributed substrate pinning. Tian and Li (2013) consider inverse reconstruction of finite partially grounded spring-mass systems from **two eigenpairs** plus other physical inputs; this does not provide a two-**eigenvalue** image classification for one infinite-host defect. Lecomte (2014) studies root loci and local disturbances in finite spring-mass systems. Ramakrishnan and Matlack (2024) characterize grounded monoatomic chain defect resonances and observe two resonances in a finite edge-defect setting. Most closely, Manzhelii and Feodosyev (2025) derive a broader **three-parameter forward** description of localized vibrations both below and above the band in a substrate-bound chain. That paper clearly covers the general forward existence of both types of mode; no novelty is attributed here to that phenomenon or its one-defect specialization. The present statement is the converse **sharp two-frequency feasibility inequality and unique recovery of mass and onsite stiffness from spectral values alone** at known host coupling. The 2025 forward formulas may be algebraically relevant to this inverse specialization, creating a genuine priority risk requiring further independent literature review.

## Limitations

The theorem requires an infinite homogeneous chain, known host mass/spring normalization and strictly positive host and defect substrate pinning. It uses two exact frequencies straddling the *same* host band, not noisy spectral estimates or mode-shape measurements. No claim is made about inverse conditioning, finite-size robustness, multiple defects or practical sensing accuracy. The 2025 broader forward literature and finite-chain inverse theory delimit the priority claim; absence of a retrieved exact inverse statement is not proof of novelty. No independent audit has been performed.
