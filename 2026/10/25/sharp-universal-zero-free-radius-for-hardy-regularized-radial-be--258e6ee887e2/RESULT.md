# Sharp universal zero-free radius for Hardy-regularized radial Bergman kernels

## Finding
Let \(\mu\) be any rotation-invariant Borel probability measure supported in the open unit disk \(\mathbb D\), let \(m\) be normalized arclength measure on its boundary, and let \(\kappa>0\). Equip analytic polynomials, and then their Hilbert-space completion, with
\[
\|f\|_{\mu,\kappa}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb D}|f|^2\,d\mu+\kappa\int_{\partial\mathbb D}|f|^2\,dm.
\]
Its reproducing kernel is \(K_{\mu,\kappa}(z,w)=\mathcal K_{\mu,\kappa}(z\overline w)\), where
\[
\mathcal K_{\mu,\kappa}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^n}{\kappa+M_n},\qquad M_n=\int_{\mathbb D}|z|^{2n}\,d\mu(z).
\]
For every \(|t|<\min\{\kappa,1\}\), the following **uniform and sharp** modulus estimate holds:
\[
\boxed{\left|(1-t)\mathcal K_{\mu,\kappa}(t)\right|\ge
\frac{\kappa-|t|}{\kappa(1+\kappa)}.}
\]
It is attained for the radial point mass \(\mu=\delta_0\) at negative real \(t\), and it is approachable with smooth, strictly positive interior radial area densities. The exact largest open radius on which nonvanishing holds **uniformly over all** such \(\mu\) is \(R_\kappa=\min\{\kappa,1\}\); for the smooth beta-weight counterexamples, the zero approaches that radius from the exterior with the explicit first-order displacement proved below. In particular, **every** radial probability measure has an everywhere zero-free regularized kernel if \(\kappa\ge1\); for **every** \(0<\kappa<1\), some smooth radial area probability measure gives a genuine zero in \(\mathbb D\times\mathbb D\), arbitrarily close in product coordinate to \(-\kappa\).

For a fully explicit instance, set \(\kappa=1/6\) and \(d\mu(z)=4(1-|z|^2)^3\,dA(z)/\pi\). Then \(\mathcal K_{\mu,\kappa}\) has a negative-real zero in \((-24/25,-9/10)\), certified by exact-rational series bounds in the accompanying checker. Both the unregularized weighted Bergman kernel and the pure Hardy kernel are individually zero-free: the zero is generated by *adding their squared norms*, not by adding their kernels.

## Assumptions and scope
The probability normalization \(\mu(\mathbb D)=1\) and boundary normalization \(m(\partial\mathbb D)=1\) are essential to the numerical threshold \(\kappa=1\). The measure is rotation-invariant and supported strictly inside \(\mathbb D\); it may have atoms. The theorem has \(\kappa>0\), with the completion canonically identified with \(H^2(\mathbb D)\). A scalar product value \(t\in\mathbb D\) is realized by some \(z,w\in\mathbb D\) satisfying \(z\overline w=t\). For the sharpness statements requiring a smooth area weight, one may restrict to integer parameters \(\alpha\ge0\) in the family below.

## Proof
**Monomial diagonalization.** Rotational invariance gives orthogonality of the monomials, with squared norms \(\kappa+M_n\). Since \(0\le M_n\le1\), this norm is equivalent to the usual Hardy coefficient norm; monomials are complete, evaluation is bounded, and the displayed kernel expansion is exact. The moments decrease from \(M_0=1\) to \(0\), the latter by dominated convergence because every point in the support has modulus strictly less than one.

**Positive-increment factorization.** Write \(a_n=(\kappa+M_n)^{-1}\), and \(c_n=a_n-a_{n-1}\) for integers \(n\ge1\). The moments decrease, so every \(c_n\ge0\), while
\[
a_0=\frac1{1+\kappa},\qquad
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_n=\frac1\kappa-\frac1{1+\kappa}=\frac1{\kappa(1+\kappa)}.
\]
Multiplying the absolutely convergent kernel power series by \(1-t\) yields
\[
(1-t)\mathcal K_{\mu,\kappa}(t)=a_0+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_n t^n.
\]
For \(r=|t|<1\), its modulus is at least \(a_0-\sum_{n\ge1}c_nr^n\ge a_0-r\sum_{n\ge1}c_n=(\kappa-r)/(\kappa(1+\kappa))\). The right side is positive for \(r<\min\{\kappa,1\}\), proving uniform nonvanishing there. For \(\kappa\ge1\), every \(|t|<1\) satisfies this condition.

**Exact sharpness and smooth approximation.** For \(\mu=\delta_0\), one has \(M_0=1\) and \(M_n=0\) for all \(n\ge1\), hence
\[
\mathcal K_{\delta_0,\kappa}(t)=\frac{\kappa+t}{\kappa(1+\kappa)(1-t)}.
\]
At \(t=-r\) this realizes equality in the modulus estimate for every \(0\le r<\min\{\kappa,1\}\). When \(\kappa<1\), the same rational function has its unique simple zero at \(t=-\kappa\).

To obtain strictly positive smooth interior densities, define for nonnegative integers \(\alpha\)
\[
d\mu_\alpha(z)=\frac{\alpha+1}{\pi}(1-|z|^2)^\alpha\,dA(z),\qquad
M_n(\alpha)=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+2)\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+2)}.
\]
Here \(M_0(\alpha)=1\) and \(M_n(\alpha)\to0\) as \(\alpha\to\infty\) for every fixed \(n\ge1\). Every reciprocal coefficient is bounded by \(1/\kappa\), so dominated convergence of power series implies locally uniform convergence \(\mathcal K_{\mu_\alpha,\kappa}\to\mathcal K_{\delta_0,\kappa}\) in \(|t|<1\). If \(\kappa<1\), choose a small conjugation-invariant disk about \(-\kappa\) contained in the unit disk. Rouché's theorem gives exactly one zero therein for all sufficiently large integer \(\alpha\), counted with multiplicity; therefore it is simple and, by real Taylor coefficients and uniqueness, real. These zeros converge to \(-\kappa\). More precisely, for each fixed \(0<\kappa<1\),
\[
\boxed{t_\alpha=-\kappa-\frac{(1+\kappa)^2}{\alpha+2}+O_\kappa(\alpha^{-2}).}
\]
To prove this refinement, observe that \(M_1(\alpha)=1/(\alpha+2)\) and \(0\le M_n(\alpha)\le M_2(\alpha)=2/[(\alpha+2)(\alpha+3)]\) for every \(n\ge2\). Therefore on every compact subdisk,
\[
\mathcal K_{\mu_\alpha,\kappa}(t)=\mathcal K_{\delta_0,\kappa}(t)-\frac{t}{\kappa^2(\alpha+2)}+O_\kappa(\alpha^{-2}),
\]
with the same control after differentiation. Since \(\mathcal K'_{\delta_0,\kappa}(-\kappa)=1/[\kappa(1+\kappa)^2]\), the simple-root perturbation formula gives the displayed displacement. In particular the smooth zeros approach the sharp excluded circle from *outside*. Every proposed uniform zero-free radius larger than \(\kappa\) consequently fails for a smooth positive weight. When \(\kappa\ge1\), radius one is maximal simply because it is the kernel's specified product domain.

**Certified finite smooth witness.** At \(\alpha=3\), \(M_n=24/[(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)(n+4)]\). For any rational \(0<r<1\), truncate \(\mathcal K(-r)\) at index \(N\). Since each coefficient lies between zero and \(1/\kappa\), the absolute remainder is bounded by \(r^{N+1}/[\kappa(1-r)]\). The exact computations in the checker show strictly positive \(\mathcal K(-9/10)\) and strictly negative \(\mathcal K(-24/25)\) at \(\kappa=1/6\). The intermediate value theorem supplies the stated root. The beta-weight kernel without boundary mass equals \((1-t)^{-5}\), and the pure Hardy kernel is a positive multiple of \((1-t)^{-1}\), so each component is indeed zero-free.

## Relationship to prior work
Zeytuncu (2010, arXiv:1009.2806) studies zeros of ordinary weighted Bergman kernels for radial area weights and constructs a weight comparable to one giving a zero; the source does not add a boundary Hardy norm or state a universal boundary-mass threshold. Seip (2011, arXiv:1106.3847) studies interpolation and sampling between weighted Bergman and Hardy regimes, not zeros of the explicitly summed two-norm kernel. Mitkovski and Wick (2012, arXiv:1212.0507) study boundedness and compactness tests in Bergman-type reproducing-kernel spaces rather than zero-fibre classifications. Published work on other zero-free radial Bergman families involves interior area weights without this boundary regularization. The classical kernel and moment facts are credited; the new assertion is the sharp universal mass/radius law for the *mixed norm*, with smooth counterexamples below threshold.

## Limitations
The uniform radius is a guarantee over all radial probability measures; it does not classify the zeros of an individual mixed kernel when \(\kappa<1\). The result does not apply without rotational invariance, with a differently normalized total interior mass, or at \(\kappa=0\). It makes no stability claim under nonradial perturbations. The literature comparison does not prove absolute publication uniqueness, and no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
