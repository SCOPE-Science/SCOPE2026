# Shellability and homotopy type of the icosahedral two-independence complex

## Finding

Let \(I\) be the edge graph of the regular icosahedron. A subset \(F\subseteq V(I)\) is *two-independent* when every connected component of the induced graph \(I[F]\) has at most two vertices; write \(K=\operatorname{Ind}_2(I)\) for the simplicial complex of all these sets.

**Theorem.** The complex \(K\) is a pure, three-dimensional shellable simplicial complex. Its \(f\)-vector, in dimensions zero through three, is \((12,66,140,105)\), and its \(h\)-vector is \((1,8,36,40,20)\). Consequently there is an **integral homotopy equivalence**
\[
|K|\simeq\bigvee^{20} S^3,
\]
so \(\widetilde H_3(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{20}\), all other reduced integral homology vanishes, and the same holds after change to any coefficient field.

The shellability conclusion is stronger than a Betti-number calculation: the proof supplies a complete, checkable shelling of all 105 maximal tetrahedra. This concerns a natural nonchordal, non-block graph, rather than the tree-like graph classes treated in available general shellability theorems.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph is fixed, finite and simple. Give its twelve vertices the labels \(0,1,\ldots,11\). Vertices \(0\) and \(11\) are opposite poles; write \(a_i=1+i\) and \(b_i=6+i\), with \(i\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z\). The edges are exactly
\[
\{0,a_i\},\ \{11,b_i\},\ \{a_i,a_{i+1}\},\ \{b_i,b_{i+1}\},\ \{a_i,b_i\},\ \{a_i,b_{i-1}\}
\quad (i\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z).
\]
Thus each of the twelve vertices has degree five, and this is the standard staggered-two-pentagons model of the icosahedral graph. A face of \(K\) is *not* a geometric face of the polyhedron: it is a vertex subset satisfying the induced-component restriction above. Only the parameter two is asserted; no generalization to all regular polyhedra, graph families or higher independence parameters is claimed.

A finite simplicial complex with all facets of cardinality four is *pure three-dimensional*. An order \(F_1,\ldots,F_t\) of its facets is a *shelling* when, for each \(j\ge2\), the intersection of the simplex on \(F_j\) with the subcomplex generated by preceding facets is nonempty and pure two-dimensional.

## Proof

**Step 1: finite presentation by forbidden triples.** Any connected graph component on at least three vertices contains a connected induced triple: choose two adjacent edges of a spanning tree. Conversely any connected induced triple violates two-independence. Hence \(F\) is a face of \(K\) if and only if it contains no three vertices spanning two or three edges of \(I\). The graph above has exactly 80 distinct forbidden triples. Exhaustively applying this criterion to the \(2^{12}=4096\) subsets gives precisely 324 faces, including the empty face, distributed by cardinality as
\[
(1,12,66,140,105).
\]
There are exactly 105 inclusion-maximal faces, and each has cardinality four. These claims can be checked independently by induced-component breadth-first search; both criteria are implemented and compared on every subset in `verify.py`.

**Step 2: complete shelling certificate.** Encode a subset \(F\) by the integer \(m(F)=\sum_{i\in F}2^i\). The file `shelling.json` gives an explicit permutation \((m(F_1),\ldots,m(F_{105}))\) of the maximal face masks. For each \(j\ge2\), write \(C_j=2^{F_j}\cap\langle F_1,\ldots,F_{j-1}\rangle\). The checker independently enumerates the subsets of \(F_j\), computes \(C_j\), and verifies that **every inclusion-maximal face of \(C_j\) has cardinality three**. It also verifies that the newly introduced faces of \(F_j\) have a unique least element \(R_j\), and are exactly \(\{T\subseteq F_j:R_j\subseteq T\}\). Thus each intersection is a nonempty pure two-dimensional subcomplex, which is precisely the shelling condition. No probabilistic or heuristic step appears in the certificate verification.

**Step 3: integral homotopy consequence.** A pure, three-dimensional shellable simplicial complex has the homotopy type of a wedge of three-spheres. In the explicit shelling there are precisely twenty indices \(j\ge2\) with restriction face \(R_j=F_j\), corresponding to the attachments along the complete boundary of a tetrahedron. Therefore the sphere count is twenty. Independently, the reduced Euler characteristic computed from the face vector equals \(-20\), and the top coefficient of the \(h\)-polynomial is twenty. Both agree with the shelling count. This completes the integral homotopy statement.

## Verification

The eight-file standalone package includes `shelling.json` and a Python standard-library checker `verify.py`. Extract both into the same directory and run:

```sh
python3 verify.py shelling.json
```

The checker enumerates all 4096 subsets by two independent face predicates (forbidden connected triples and component-wise breadth-first search), checks the entire \(f\)-vector and facet set, checks all 104 shelling intersections by the *maximal-intersection-face* criterion, independently verifies the unique restriction-face property, counts the twenty homology facets, and recomputes the \(h\)-vector and Euler characteristic. Its successful final line is `ICO_2_SHELLING_VERIFY_OK facets=105 spheres=20`.

## Relationship to prior work

Deshpande–Roy–Sawant introduced the co-connected complex \(\Sigma_r(G)\), equal to the **Alexander dual** of \(\operatorname{Ind}_r(G)\) for empty auxiliary set, and established structural results for several graph classes. Their results address the dual complex or families such as chordal, cograph, cycle and small-grid graphs; they do not give the present primal icosahedral shelling. Ghosh–Selvaraja established shellability conditions for higher-independence complexes of block graphs and certain tree-like or clique-attached graph families, none of whose quoted hypotheses applies to the unmodified icosahedral graph. Deshpande–Singh analyzed complete multipartite, whiskered, cycle and perfect-ary-tree families, not this graph. Different constructions on the icosahedral graph, such as its neighborhood and three-cut complexes, do not imply this result by matching their graph parameter alone.

The comparison is statement-level, not an assertion that unsuccessful searching proves priority. The proof and explicit shelling are self-contained and independent of those results. Bibliographic priority remains subject to unindexed works or terminology variants.

## Limitations

This is a classification for one canonical finite graph and one independence parameter. It does not establish general shellability of two-independence complexes of all planar, regular, diameter-three or polyhedral graphs. The shelling is a finite combinatorial certificate; the standard shellability-to-wedge theorem provides the topological implication. Independent mathematical review is not claimed. Inaccessible or unindexed equivalent literature remains a possibility.
