# Shellability at the first two omitted orders for the third-power-cycle three-cut complex

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge 13\), the ordinary three-cut complex \(\Delta_3(C_n^3)\) of the third power of the cycle is shellable. The new contributions are explicit, exhaustive shelling certificates for the two previously untreated orders \(n=13\) and \(n=14\); the existing theorem of Chauhan and Shukla establishes the cases \(n\ge15\). Consequently,
\[
\Delta_3(C_n^3)\simeq\bigvee^{(n^2-13n+2)/2}S^{n-4}\qquad(n\ge13).
\]
In particular, the new shelling proofs yield \(\Delta_3(C_{13}^3)\simeq S^9\) and \(\Delta_3(C_{14}^3)\simeq\bigvee^8 S^{10}\). The corresponding homology ranks for these two orders were previously tabulated, so the new assertion is **shellability**, not the homology ranks alone. This establishes the positive, \(p=3\), branch of a published shellability conjecture at its exact conjectural starting order.

## Assumptions and scope
The simple graph \(C_n^3\) has vertex set \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) and edges between distinct residues of cyclic distance at most three. For any graph \(G\) on \(n\) vertices, the *ordinary* three-cut complex \(\Delta_3(G)\) is generated by the complements \(V(G)\setminus T\), where \(T\) ranges over three-vertex sets for which the induced subgraph \(G[T]\) is disconnected. This is not the *total* three-cut complex (which requires \(T\) to be independent). Every listed facet has \(n-3\) vertices, so the complex is pure of dimension \(n-4\). The finite certificates below use the labels \(0,\ldots,n-1\) and represent a facet \(F\) by the bitmask \(\sum_{v\in F}2^v\). These masks are combinatorial encodings, not numerical approximations.

## Proof
**Shelling criterion.** Let \(F_1,\ldots,F_m\) be an ordering of distinct facets of a pure simplicial complex, all with \(q\) vertices. For \(i>1\), define
\[
R_i=\{v\in F_i:\text{for some }j<i,\ F_i\setminus\{v\}\subseteq F_j\}.
\]
The order is a pure shelling if and only if \(R_i\ne\varnothing\) and, for every \(j<i\), \(R_i\nsubseteq F_j\). Indeed, if these conditions hold, choose \(v\in R_i\setminus F_j\). Then \(F_i\cap F_j\subseteq F_i\setminus\{v\}\), and the latter codimension-one face occurs in an earlier facet. Thus the intersection of the new simplex with the preceding union is nonempty and pure of codimension one. Conversely, if the intersection is pure of codimension one, each \(F_i\cap F_j\) lies in an earlier ridge \(F_i\setminus\{v\}\) with \(v\notin F_j\), giving the stated conditions.

**Finite shelling certificates.** The accompanying `shellings.json` contains, in order, every facet mask for \(n=13\) and \(n=14\). The first checker reconstructs the complete set of disconnected triples and hence the complete set of facets, establishes that each order is a permutation of all facets, and checks both shelling conditions against every earlier facet. It verifies exactly \(169\) facets when \(n=13\) and \(238\) when \(n=14\). The independent checker recomputes disconnected triples by graph traversal rather than union-find and directly tests, for each earlier facet \(F_j\), that \(F_i\cap F_j\) is covered by an earlier codimension-one face of \(F_i\). It also enumerates the complete down-closures, independently obtaining \(7,904\) and \(16,067\) nonempty simplices. These finite exhaustive checks prove the two shelling claims without inferring any infinite assertion from experimentation.

**Sphere counts.** For each facet after the first, the newly introduced restriction face has vertex set \(R_i\). A spanning facet, i.e. one contributing a sphere of top dimension in a pure shelling, occurs precisely when \(R_i=F_i\). The two certificate audits find exactly one such facet for \(n=13\) and eight for \(n=14\). Thus these complexes are respectively a nine-sphere and a wedge of eight ten-spheres. This recovers, but does not claim priority for, their previously computed homology ranks.

**All larger orders.** Theorem 1.4 of Chauhan and Shukla, arXiv:2603.23155, states that \(\Delta_3(C_n^p)\) is shellable for \(p\ge2\), \(n\ge6p-3\), and has \(\binom{n-2p}{2}-(2p^2+p-1)\) top-dimensional spheres. Putting \(p=3\) covers all \(n\ge15\), with sphere count \((n^2-13n+2)/2\). The two certified orders finish the range \(n\ge13\). No extension of that published theorem is silently assumed below its stated range.

## Verification
Place `shellings.json`, `check_shelling.py` and `check_shelling_independent.py` in the same directory, then run `python3 check_shelling.py` and `python3 check_shelling_independent.py`. Both use only the Python standard library. Their exact recorded standard outputs appear in `verification_output.txt`. The optional `generate_certificate.py` deterministically regenerates the finite shelling orders using a maximal-ridge greedy selection; the conclusion relies on *checking the stored orders*, not on assuming greedy success. The two checkers use different connectivity and shelling-verification algorithms and do not depend on the generation heuristic.

## Relationship to prior work
Chauhan, Shukla and Vinayak, arXiv:2406.01979, Conjecture 4.2(ii), proposed shellability of \(\Delta_3(C_n^p)\) for \(p\ge3\), \(n\ge4p+1\). Their Table 2 already reports the homology groups at the small values used here; neither a homology table nor an expected sphere wedge establishes shellability. Chauhan and Shukla, arXiv:2603.23155, proved shellability for \(n\ge6p-3\), which for \(p=3\) starts at \(n=15\). The result here supplies exactly the two missing orders and thereby proves the positive conjectural range for \(p=3\). Published work on ordinary three-cut complexes of squared cycles applies to \(p=2\); results on *total* cut complexes concern a different functor. The finite shelling property is stronger than the previously reported integral homology data.

## Limitations
The new certificates concern only \(n=13\) and \(n=14\); the statement for \(n\ge15\) relies on the specifically cited published theorem. No new uniform shelling order for arbitrary \(n\) is constructed. No assertion about the non-shellability or homotopy types at \(9\le n\le12\) is made; hence the negative half of Conjecture 4.2(i) remains outside scope. The work does not settle the general \(p\ge4\) conjecture. Bibliographic searches cannot eliminate unpublished or unindexed overlap. Independent scientific audit has not been performed.
