# Scientific assessment

## Correctness: PASS
The entire argument reduces to independent integer coordinate counts for the two relative Ehrhart functions. Equal degree and leading coefficient permit exact comparison of the multisets of roots. Equation (3) restricts an integral shift to one or two. In the shift-one case, nesting supplies the two extremal-root obstructions whenever a side has length one; absent such a side, the reciprocal-average equality forces every side to have length two. The stated boundary cases and converse factor identities are exact. The standalone standard-library checker corroborates the formulas and witness on finite families; the general assertion rests on the proof.

## Originality: PASS, with residual historical risk
Hirotsu (arXiv:2608.10370v7, Example 2) already establishes the factorized eventual counting polynomials; these are **not new**. Definition 6 and Problem 4 introduce the integer-shifted-duality classification problem. Theorem 7 determines shifted duality under extended inscription, where an inner polytope is represented as a scaling of one meeting every outer facet; it does not directly characterize all coordinatewise nested inner boxes, including lower-dimensional and non-proportional ones. An all-dimensional necessity-and-sufficiency result independent of the inner box was not found in the inspected source or returned specialized research searches. The new implication comes from combining leading-coefficient and extremal-root arguments; an unstated or differently named precedent remains possible.

## Mathematical value: PASS
The criterion is a complete natural-family classification directly motivated by an explicit current open problem. It isolates an all-dimensional rigidity phenomenon, makes the inner-box independence precise, and proves that nesting is essential through a dimension-minimal counterexample. Its value is the sharp structural boundary, not a new closed formula for counting lattice translates. It neither resolves the unrestricted problem nor yields a classification of rotated or rational-lattice boxes.
