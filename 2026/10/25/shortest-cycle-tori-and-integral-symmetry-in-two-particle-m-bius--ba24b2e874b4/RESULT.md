# Shortest-cycle tori and integral symmetry in two-particle Möbius–Kantor configurations

## Finding

Let \(M=\operatorname{GP}(8,3)\) be the Möbius–Kantor graph and let \(X=\operatorname{UConf}_2(|M|)\) be the space of unordered pairs of distinct points of its geometric realization. Then
\[
H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^9\oplus\mathbb Z/2,\qquad
H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{20},
\]
and there is no homology above degree two. All \(60\) unordered pairs of vertex-disjoint six-cycles define embedded product tori. The full \(96\)-element automorphism group splits those tori into orbits of sizes \(12\) and \(48\); **the larger orbit alone contains an integral basis of all twenty second-homology directions**, whereas the smaller orbit spans a primitive rank-\(12\) sublattice.

For \(p\le q\), let \(L_{p,q}\subseteq H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\) be the sublattice generated by product tori from disjoint simple \(p\)- and \(q\)-cycles. The complete nonempty length types, their numbers of tori, and their integral quotients are
\[
\begin{array}{c|r|l}
(p,q)&\#\text{ tori}&H_2(X;\mathbb Z)/L_{p,q}\\\hline
(6,6)&60&0\\
(6,8)&24&\mathbb Z\oplus(\mathbb Z/2)^3\\
(6,10)&24&\mathbb Z\oplus(\mathbb Z/2)^5\\
(8,8)&3&\mathbb Z^{17}.
\end{array}
\]
Thus the longer-cycle families can lose integral information even when they span a rank-\(19\) subgroup. The natural action of \(\operatorname{Aut}(M)\) on \(H_2(X;\mathbb Q)\) has no invariant vectors. Its character assumes the values \(20,2,0,-4\) on respectively \(1,16,66,13\) automorphisms.

## Assumptions and scope

Indices in the graph definition run through \(\mathbb Z/8\). There are vertices \(u_i,v_i\), outer edges \(u_iu_{i+1}\), spokes \(u_iv_i\), and inner edges \(v_iv_{i+3}\), with undirected edges counted once. Thus \(M\) has \(16\) vertices and \(24\) edges. An embedded product torus is formed by placing one particle on each of two vertex-disjoint simple cycles; the cycles are unlabelled, but their oriented product has a well-defined homology class up to sign. A cycle's length is its number of graph edges. All homology groups use integral coefficients unless the coefficient field is written explicitly.

The graph is connected, cubic, and has girth \(6\). Prue–Scrimshaw's stable-equivalence theorem applies to two particles: distinct essential vertices are separated by at least one edge and every essential closed edge path has at least three edges. Consequently the usual discretized unordered two-particle cubical model computes the homotopy and homology of \(X\).

## Proof

Orient every graph edge \(e=(a,b)\) from its smaller numeric endpoint to its larger endpoint, and lexicographically order graph edges. The discretized cubical complex has a zero-cell for each unordered pair of distinct graph vertices, a one-cell \([v,e]\) whenever \(v\notin e\), and a two-cell \([e,f]\) for every lexicographically ordered pair of edges with disjoint endpoint sets. Its numbers of cells in dimensions \(0,1,2\) are \((120,336,228)\). With \(e=(a,b)\) and \(f=(c,d)\), the oriented cellular boundary is
\[
\partial_2[e,f]=[b,f]-[a,f]-[d,e]+[c,e].
\]
The standalone verifier constructs both integer boundary matrices and checks \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\). Exact Smith reduction gives nonzero diagonal factors \(1\) repeated \(207\) times and \(2\) once for \(\partial_2\); \(\partial_1\) has rank \(119\). The resulting homology groups are precisely those in the finding. In particular \(\ker\partial_2\) is a saturated sublattice of \(C_2\) of rank \(20\).

Every unoriented simple cycle has a unique representative beginning at its least vertex and traversed so that its second vertex is less than its last. Exhaustive depth-first search with this canonical rule gives exactly \(400\) cycles, with length distribution
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
\text{length}&6&8&10&12&14&16\\\hline
\text{count}&24&30&96&100&144&6.
\end{array}
\]
There are exactly \(111\) unordered vertex-disjoint cycle pairs, with the length counts in the finding; longer pairs are impossible on sixteen graph vertices.

For oriented disjoint cycles \(C,D\), write \(\epsilon_C(e)\in\{-1,0,1\}\) for the incidence coefficient of edge \(e\) in the first cycle. The product torus has cubical cycle
\[
z_{C,D}=\sum_{e<f}\bigl(\epsilon_C(e)\epsilon_D(f)-\epsilon_D(e)\epsilon_C(f)\bigr)[e,f].
\]
Its boundary vanishes by the cellular product rule and the fact that both graph cycles are closed. Every such cycle is reconstructed and its vanishing boundary checked directly.

A complete graph-automorphism backtracking enumeration gives exactly \(96\) automorphisms. Their orbits on disjoint hexagon pairs have sizes \(12\) and \(48\). For the size-\(48\) orbit, the verifier first orders all graph cycles by the canonical depth-first enumeration, then uses its first linearly independent columns over \(\mathbb F_{101}\) and first independent coordinate rows. The selected orbit-column positions are
\[
(0,1,2,4,5,6,8,9,10,12,13,14,16,18,20,21,22,24,25,26),
\]
and the selected two-cell row positions are
\[
(0,2,4,6,8,11,19,21,23,25,27,57,59,61,63,91,93,95,121,123).
\]
Both lists use zero-based indexing into the verifier's canonical orbit and two-cell sequences. The associated \(20\)-by-\(20\) integer determinant is \(+1\). The twenty torus cycles therefore span a primitive rank-\(20\) subgroup of \(C_2\). Since \(\ker\partial_2\) is saturated and has the same rank, this subgroup is all of \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\). This establishes actual integral generation, not just generation over a field.

For each length type the exact Smith factors of the matrix of its torus cycles, viewed in \(\mathbb Z^{228}\), are: \((6,6)\) twenty units; \((6,8)\) sixteen units and three twos; \((6,10)\) fourteen units and five twos; and \((8,8)\) three units. Because \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\) is primitive in \(C_2\), these factors give exactly the stated integral quotients. The small six-cycle orbit has twelve unit Smith factors.

For the symmetry character, the map sending a particle's trajectory to its underlying graph one-cycle identifies \(H_1(X;\mathbb Q)\) equivariantly with \(H_1(M;\mathbb Q)\): the \(24\) graph hexagons generate the latter integral cycle lattice of rank \(9\), each can be traced with the second particle parked outside its vertex set, and the source and target rational first-homology ranks both equal \(9\). The cellular Lefschetz formula hence yields
\[
\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H_2)=
\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_2)-\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_1)
+\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_0)
+\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_1(M))-\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_0(M)).
\]
All traces use oriented-cell signs. The \(96\) evaluations have the distribution above; their sum is zero, so averaging gives zero invariant vectors. A separate direct action on the explicit integral torus basis verifies the same trace distribution without invoking the first-homology argument.

## Relationship to prior work

Graph configuration spaces and discretized braid spaces originate in Abrams's work; the precise subdivision bound used here is Theorem 3.2 of Prue–Scrimshaw. Farley–Sabalka develop a general discrete-Morse method. Barnett–Farber investigate two-particle homology and give planar-graph torus bases in the ordered setting; the planar hypotheses do not yield this nonplanar unordered integral orbit lattice. An–Knudsen characterize degree-two generators for planar graph braid groups and explicitly identify nonplanar limitations; their results do not supply the graph-specific hexagon orbit, length-restricted Smith quotients, or full character computed here. Existing published calculations of independence and matching complexes of this graph concern different topological constructions. The neighboring complete-tripartite and Heawood two-point torus calculations concern different graphs and do not imply the present orbit classification.

## Limitations

The theorem is restricted to two unordered particles on the specified sixteen-vertex graph; it does not assert general length-lattice behavior for all generalized Petersen graphs or all nonplanar graphs. The full representation is specified by its rational character, not by an integral indecomposable decomposition. Equivalent unpublished, unindexed, or inaccessible calculations remain a bibliographic possibility, and no claim of comprehensive priority over such sources is made.
