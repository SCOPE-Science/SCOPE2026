# Shortest octahedral spheres and integral symmetry orbit lattices in the Petersen matching complex

## Finding
Write \(P=\operatorname{KG}(5,2)\) for the Petersen graph and \(K=\mathcal M(P)\) for its ordinary matching complex. The complex \(K\) has exactly **110 induced octahedral two-spheres on six vertices**. They are all nonzero primitive carriers in \(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^5\), and six is the minimum possible support for any nonzero second-homology class. Under \(\operatorname{Aut}(P)\cong S_5\), the 110 spheres split into three orbits of 10, 60, and 40.

For the subgroup \(L_j\) generated by the signed fundamental classes in orbit \(j\), ordered by those three sizes, the exact integral quotients are
\[
(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)/L_{10},\ \widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)/L_{60},\ \widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)/L_{40})\cong(\mathbb Z/2,\ 0,\ 0).
\]
In particular the 10-element orbit is rationally complete but has integral index two, whereas **either larger orbit generates all integral second homology**.

The complete rational symmetry representations are
\[
\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Q)\cong\operatorname{sgn}\otimes\mathbb Q^{[5]}\cong\operatorname{sgn}\oplus S^{(2,1,1,1)},\qquad
\widetilde H_3(K;\mathbb Q)\cong S^{(3,2)}.
\]
The notation \(\mathbb Q^{[5]}\) denotes the permutation module on five symbols; \(S^{\lambda}\) is the rational Specht module. The previously known integral homotopy equivalence \(K\simeq\bigvee^5S^2\vee\bigvee^5S^3\) is background, not claimed as new.

## Assumptions and scope
Represent the ten vertices of \(P\) by unordered two-subsets of \(\{0,1,2,3,4\}\), with an edge joining two disjoint subsets. Lexicographically sort the fifteen edges of \(P\) and index them by \(0,\ldots,14\). A simplex of \(K\) is a subset of these indices corresponding to pairwise vertex-disjoint Petersen edges, oriented by increasing index. The natural symmetric group permutes the underlying five symbols and acts with the induced simplicial orientation signs. Everything concerns this finite complex and its ordinary matching simplices, not its two-matching complex, forest complex, or two-particle configuration space.

An *octahedral carrier* is an induced subcomplex on six matching-complex vertices whose incompatibility graph is three disjoint edges. Such a subcomplex is \((S^0)^{*3}\cong S^2\). The cyclic orientation for incompatibility pairs \(\{a_1,b_1\},\{a_2,b_2\},\{a_3,b_3\}\), with each pair and the pairs themselves sorted, is the join product \(([a_1]-[b_1])*([a_2]-[b_2])*([a_3]-[b_3])\); it is a signed eight-triangle cycle.

## Proof
**1. Integral ambient groups.** Enumerate every matching by choosing distinct Petersen edges with mutually disjoint endpoints, ordered increasingly. The counts in simplicial dimensions zero through four are \((15,75,145,90,6)\). Form the signed integral boundary maps using the standard alternating deletion signs. They have ranks \((14,61,79,6)\) in boundary degrees one through four; in each case every nonzero Smith invariant factor is one. The adjacent compositions are zero. Thus \(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^5\) and \(\widetilde H_3(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^5\), agreeing with the previously published homotopy type [3].

**2. Exhaustion and the sharp support bound.** The ordinary matching complex is flag. A nonzero second-dimensional cycle of a flag complex needs at least six support vertices; if six suffice, the supported complex must be the boundary of a three-dimensional crosspolytope (octahedron). The elementary flag-carrier lemma is discussed in [2]; equivalently it follows by the vertex-link induction for a minimal cycle. For each six-subset of the 15 Petersen-edge indices, enumerate its incompatible pairs. The necessary and sufficient induced-octahedron test is precisely that its incompatibility graph is a three-edge perfect matching. There are exactly 110 solutions. Every displayed eight-triangle join chain is a cycle by the alternating boundary identity, verified in the ordinary chain complex.

**3. Full symmetry orbits and primitive classes.** Enumerate all 120 permutations of the five underlying symbols and their actions on the fifteen ordered Petersen edges. The 110 induced octahedra have three orbits of sizes \(10,60,40\), with respective representative incompatibility-pair triples (edge-index notation)

`((0,1),(3,4),(6,9))`, `((0,1),(3,4),(6,12))`, `((0,1),(3,5),(6,7))`.

Let \(D_3\) be the signed integral boundary from three-chains to two-chains, and let \(Z_j\) be the 145-row matrix of octahedral fundamental cycles from the \(j\)-th orbit. Since \(\ker D_2\) is saturated inside the free group of two-chains, and \([D_3\mid Z_j]\) has rank \(84=79+5\), the torsion subgroup of the cokernel of \([D_3\mid Z_j]\) is canonically the finite group \(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)/L_j\). The respective **nonunit Smith invariant factors** of those three augmented matrices are \((2)\), the empty list, and the empty list. These yield exactly the three quotients claimed. Likewise, for each representative individual cycle \(z\), the augmented matrix \([D_3\mid z]\) has rank 80 and **all 80 Smith invariant factors equal to one**. Thus every representative gives a primitive nonzero homology class; the automorphism action gives the conclusion for all 110.

**4. Equivariant characters.** The signed simplex actions commute with boundary. For each of the seven cycle types of \(S_5\), compute the trace on the rational eleven-dimensional kernel of \(D_3\) and subtract the trace on its six-dimensional subspace \(\operatorname{im}D_4\) to obtain the rational third-homology trace. The equivariant reduced Euler identity then gives the second-homology trace, since only reduced degrees two and three occur. For cycle types \((1^5),(2,1^3),(2^2,1),(3,1^2),(3,2),(4,1),(5)\), the two character rows are respectively
\[
\chi_2=(5,-3,1,2,0,-1,0),\qquad \chi_3=(5,1,1,-1,1,-1,0).
\]
If \(f(g)\) is the number of fixed symbols and \(c_2(g)\) the number of two-cycles of \(g\), direct character comparison gives \(\chi_2(g)=\operatorname{sgn}(g) f(g)\) and \(\chi_3(g)=\binom{f(g)}2+c_2(g)-f(g)\). These are exactly the characters of \(\operatorname{sgn}\otimes\mathbb Q^{[5]}\) and \(S^{(3,2)}\). Rational semisimplicity and character uniqueness imply the stated isomorphisms. No integral equivariant decomposition is inferred from rational character equality.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_petersen_crosscycle.py` with Python 3.10+ and SymPy as specified in `requirements.txt`. The verifier constructs the graph, every simplex, every signed boundary, every six-vertex octahedron, all 120 group permutations, the exact Smith forms of the three orbit-augmented integral matrices, each representative's primitivity, and all seven rational character values. It asserts all numerical results above, checks boundary-squared-zero, and terminates with `VERIFY_OK_PETERSEN_MATCHING_MINIMUM_OCTAHEDRAL_SPHERES`. There are no opaque input tables or precomputed rank certificates: exact computations start from \(P=\operatorname{KG}(5,2)\). The script is an exhaustive certificate for the specified finite simplicial complex, not a general-purpose theorem prover.

## Relationship to prior work
A publicly indexed calculation [3] already proves the **entire nonequivariant integral homotopy type** of this exact matching complex. Its face vector and Betti numbers are therefore background. That prior calculation does not classify shortest nonzero two-cycle carriers or compute their integral \(S_5\)-orbit lattices or either full rational character, and these conclusions do not follow from its sphere-wedge homotopy equivalence. A separately indexed construction also establishes this same nonequivariant homotopy result [4].

General work on crosspolytopal carriers in flag/independence complexes [2] supplies definitions and general methods, but not the Petersen-specific 110-carrier census, three orbit indices, or full homology characters. Barmak's general independence-complex results [1] give homotopical tools without this exact equivariant analysis. Other published calculations on the Petersen two-matching complex, Petersen forest complex and unordered two-particle configuration space concern different functorial constructions, not alternative names for the present matching complex. Targeted equivalence and implication comparisons did not identify a stronger covering theorem.

## Limitations
The theorem is an exact finite, reproducible calculation for one graph, with a general flag-minimum-support lemma. The rational representation theorem does **not** determine the complete integral \(\mathbb Z[S_5]\)-module extension structure; the integral assertion concerns the explicitly specified orbit-generated sublattices in second homology. Computer-executed Smith forms and rational linear algebra should be independently replayed. The publication-priority search cannot exclude unindexed or inaccessible specialized work. No independent scientific audit has been performed.
