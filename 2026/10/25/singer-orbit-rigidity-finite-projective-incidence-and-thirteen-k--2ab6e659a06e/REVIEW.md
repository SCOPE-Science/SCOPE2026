# Scientific review: Singer orbits and rational incidence in a smooth normal-basis system

## Correctness: PASS

The normal-basis construction is an invertible Moore-coordinate change. Coefficientwise trace makes every nonzero rational parameter a diagonal form with all nonzero coefficients, hence geometrically smooth when the characteristic does not divide the degree [1, Section 3]. Multiplication in the extension field acts by rational projectivities and takes the parameter \([\lambda]\) to \([\lambda\nu^d]\); the cyclic quotient of order \(N\) therefore has exactly \(\gcd(d,N)\) orbits of equal sizes. Under coprimality, the power map is a bijection on projective points, and the nondegenerate field-trace pairing carries the rational hypersurface-point incidence to ordinary projective point--hyperplane incidence. None of these infinite statements depends on finite enumeration.

For the degree-four characteristic-three case, an exact trace expansion and a displayed invertible \(3\)-by-\(3\) matrix establish a polynomial identity with the Klein quartic over \(\mathbb F_3\). Independent exact evaluations of both equations on all projective points over \(\mathbb F_3\), \(\mathbb F_9\), and \(\mathbb F_{27}\) give \(4,10,28\). Genus three, Newton identities and the Weil functional equation give \(1+27T^6\), hence the extension-field formula. The supplied executable reports `VERIFY_OK`. The proof and the actual executable were checked together.

## Originality: PASS, with specific residual risk

The closest source, Asgarli--Ghioca--Reichstein [1], **already constructs** these smooth linear systems; no novelty is asserted for that construction or the smoothness lemma. Its proof in Section 3 gives no Singer orbit classification, rational-point design, Klein-curve identification or zeta numerator. Meagher--Top [2] and Dolgachev [5] discuss the classical characteristic-three Klein/Fermat equivalence, but not the normal-basis system and its uniform projective incidence. In fact, the explicit twist tables for the Dyck--Fermat case in [2, Section 7] assume characteristic at least five. Lercier and collaborators [3] study quartic moduli and twist parametrization principally away from small characteristic, and their enumerations reported there concern primes at least eleven. These sources neither state nor imply the combined member-orbit and point-incidence theorem. Related published computations could cover the Klein zeta polynomial alone; no novelty is attributed to that isolated polynomial or to basic Singer groups and trace pairings.

Searches also compared the family with normal-basis smooth conics over even fields, smooth-cubic systems, published Galois-orbit linear systems [4], known projective designs, and algebraic-geometry records about quartic curves. No identical net-specific theorem or broader covering implication was found. Residual risk: an unindexed finite-geometry treatment might identify the same normal-basis net with a Singer design, and a specialized finite-field classification might already give the Klein-model equivalence. This same mathematical model literature review is not independent certification of novelty.

## Mathematical value: PASS

The question is motivated by the maximal possible projective dimension of a linear system whose rational members remain geometrically smooth. The result exposes an unexpected arithmetic rigidity: although the normal-basis parameter space contains \(N\) distinct smooth rational hypersurfaces, the number of Singer isomorphism orbits is controlled exactly by \(\gcd(d,N)\). The coprime case gives an entire family of smooth positive-genus curves with uniformly \(q+1\) rational points, realizing a complete projective-plane design rather than an arbitrary finite slice. The first characteristic-three quartic instance additionally identifies every member with the Klein curve and determines all extension-field point counts. The design and isomorphism connections are relevant to finite-field geometry, rational-point constructions, and the arithmetic interpretation of all-smooth linear systems; correctness of the verifier alone is not the reason for the value assessment.

## Limits and literature

The all-field theorem concerns the specific normal-basis construction, not all smooth systems. For noncoprime degree and Singer order, only Singer subgroup orbit counts are established. The zeta polynomial is proved for the stated thirteen characteristic-three quartics, not general \(q\). Distinct curves can share further geometric intersection points beyond their unique rational intersection point.
