For bipartite interaction networks made of two even cycles sharing one species, making cycle lengths more unequal always increases the probability that some two-neighbor-supported community survives independent species loss, yet the expected size of that community reverses its ranking at exactly one retention probability. The first same-guild-degree example has eleven species: cycle lengths \((4,8)\) versus \((6,6)\), with mean-size crossover at \(p=1/\sqrt 2\).
