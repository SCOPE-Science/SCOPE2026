# Singular strata, canonical curves, and orbifold Euler data of a sextic Log Enriques fourfold

## Finding
Let \(\zeta\) be a primitive sixth root of unity. On the Fermat sextic fourfold
\[
X=\{[x_0:\cdots:x_5]\in\mathbb P^5_{\mathbb C}:x_0^6+\cdots+x_5^6=0\},
\]
let \(g([x_0:\cdots:x_5])=[x_0:\zeta x_1:\cdots:\zeta^5x_5]\), let \(G=\langle g\rangle\cong C_6\), and put \(Y=X/G\). Then the reduced singular locus of the normal projective fourfold \(Y\) is the disjoint union of **two smooth genus-four curves and nine isolated points**. Both singular curves are canonically embedded by the same smooth complete intersection
\[
\mathcal C=V(U^2+V^2+W^2,\ T^3-UVW)\subset\mathbb P^3_{\mathbb C}.
\]
At every point of either curve, the analytic local quotient is \(\mathbb A^1\times(\mathbb A^3/\frac12(1,1,1))\), and each isolated singularity has type \(\frac13(1,1,2,2)\). The ordinary topological Euler characteristic of the coarse space is \(e(Y)=435\). Its explicitly defined commuting-pair orbifold Euler invariant is
\[
e_{\mathrm{cp}}(X,G):=\frac16\sum_{a,b=0}^{5}e(X^{g^a}\cap X^{g^b})=441.
\]
Thus this quotient has a six-unit difference between the commuting-pair invariant and the coarse Euler characteristic. No equality with another convention of stack Euler characteristic is asserted.

## Assumptions and scope
Everything is over \(\mathbb C\); all Euler characteristics are the usual topological Euler characteristics of complex algebraic varieties. The action is projective, and \(Y\) denotes the coarse quotient, not a chosen resolution. The types \(\frac1r(a_1,\ldots,a_k)\) mean the quotient by a cyclic group of order \(r\), with the indicated weights in local analytic coordinates. The assertion describes the **reduced** singular locus; it makes no claim about its full Jacobian or Fitting scheme structure. The commuting-pair invariant is precisely the displayed expression, and must not be conflated with the Satake orbifold Euler characteristic.

## Proof
**1. Complete stabilizer analysis.** Since the six eigenvalues of \(g\) on \(\mathbb C^6\) are distinct, its fixed points in projective space are coordinate vertices, none lying in \(X\). The same holds for \(g^5\). The order-three subgroup \(\langle g^2\rangle\) fixes on \(X\) exactly three coordinate-line intersections, with nonzero coordinate pairs indexed by \(\{j,j+3\}\) for \(j=0,1,2\). Each line contributes the six roots of \(x_j^6+x_{j+3}^6=0\), hence eighteen points. Their stabilizers are exactly \(\langle g^2\rangle\), since no point is fixed by \(g\) or \(g^3\). Each orbit has two elements, producing nine quotient points.

The involution \(g^3\) fixes exactly two plane sextics \(C_0,C_1\), supported on the even and odd coordinate triples respectively. They are smooth, disjoint and of genus \((6-1)(6-2)/2=10\). No point lies in both types of fixed locus, since the index sets of a line pair \(\{j,j+3\}\) have opposite parity. The restriction of \(g\) to each \(C_i\) acts by the projective order-three transformation \([x:y:z]\mapsto[x:\omega y:\omega^2z]\), for some primitive cube root \(\omega\). It is free: fixed projective points would be coordinate vertices, excluded by the Fermat equation. Riemann–Hurwitz yields \(2(10-1)=3(2g(C_i/G)-2)\), hence each quotient curve has genus four. The group preserves each parity plane, so the two curves remain distinct. Their points and the nine isolated points exhaust the locus of nontrivial stabilizers.

**2. Analytic types.** At a point of either fixed curve, \(g^3\) acts as the identity on its one-dimensional tangent space and as minus the identity on the three normal directions: in the ambient projective space, the complementary parity coordinates are negated, and the sextic defining equation eliminates one tangent direction *within* the fixed plane. Therefore the stabilizer representation on the four-dimensional tangent space is \(\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)\). At a point supported on \(\{j,j+3\}\), the two occupied coordinates have the same \(g^2\)-eigenvalue. The four unoccupied coordinates have relative residue weights \(1,1,2,2\) modulo three. The hypersurface equation eliminates the single tangent direction along the occupied projective line; consequently the tangent-space stabilizer weights are precisely \((1,1,2,2)\). Finite-group holomorphic linearization supplies the stated analytic quotient models. Neither representation is generated by pseudoreflections, so every point of those strata is singular and there are no other singularities.

**3. Canonical model of each singular curve.** On a fixed Fermat sextic \(C=V(x^6+y^6+z^6)\subset\mathbb P^2\), write \(h([x:y:z])=[x:\omega y:\omega^2z]\). The invariant cubic forms are spanned by \(x^3,y^3,z^3,xyz\). Thus the map
\[
[x:y:z]\longmapsto[U:V:W:T]=[x^3:y^3:z^3:xyz]
\]
factors through \(C/\langle h\rangle\) and lands on \(\mathcal C\). Every point of \(\mathcal C\) has a preimage: take cube roots of \(U,V,W\), choosing their product to equal \(T\), which is possible since \(T^3=UVW\). Thus the map is onto. The complete intersection \(\mathcal C\) is smooth: if \(T\ne0\), the derivative of \(T^3-UVW\) in the \(T\)-direction is nonzero, and the gradient of the quadric is independent; if \(T=0\), precisely one of \(U,V,W\) vanishes, and the two gradients again are independent. It has degree six. The pullback of its hyperplane bundle has degree eighteen on \(C\), so the map is finite of degree \(18/6=3\), exactly the order of \(\langle h\rangle\). The induced morphism \(C/\langle h\rangle\to\mathcal C\) is therefore finite birational, hence an isomorphism between smooth projective curves. By adjunction \(K_{\mathcal C}\cong\mathcal O_{\mathcal C}(2+3-4)=\mathcal O_{\mathcal C}(1)\); this is the canonical genus-four model, and its unique containing quadric is a cone.

**4. Coarse Euler characteristic.** The tangent Chern class of \(X\) is \(c(TX)=(1+H)^6/(1+6H)\), and \(\int_XH^4=6\). The degree-four coefficient is \(435\), giving \(e(X)=2610\). The fixed-set Euler characteristics for exponents \(0,1,2,3,4,5\) are \((2610,0,18,-36,18,0)\); here each plane sextic has \(e=2-2(10)=-18\). The finite-group quotient formula gives
\[
e(Y)=\frac{2610+18-36+18}{6}=435.
\]

**5. Commuting-pair invariant.** For \(a,b\in\{0,\ldots,5\}\), the common fixed locus equals \(X^{g^{\gcd(a,b,6)}}\), with \(\gcd(0,0,6)=6\) denoting the identity subgroup. The multiplicities of gcd \(1,2,3,6\) are \(24,8,3,1\), respectively. Therefore
\[
e_{\mathrm{cp}}(X,G)=\frac{24(0)+8(18)+3(-36)+1(2610)}6=441.
\]
In particular the difference is \(441-435=6\), equally obtained by adding the stabilizer-order corrections \((2-1)(-12)+(3-1)(9)=6\).

## Relationship to prior work
Billi, Palomino and Sarti (arXiv:2610.21752v1, Example 2.13(2), Example 4.5 and Proposition 5.9) establish the same smooth sextic, diagonal automorphism, the eighteen order-three fixed points, the two plane sextics fixed by the involution, and terminality of the quotient. Those results supply the object and fixed-locus starting point; the present statement concerns its **coarse singular-stratum orbit counts, exact analytic weight types, canonical models of its singular curves, and paired Euler invariants**, which are not stated in the inspected portions of that source. Standard theory of cyclic quotients of Fermat curves and their Jacobians is relevant as background, but a curve-Jacobian classification cannot by itself imply these specific fourfold quotient singularity and Euler calculations. Related computations of the coarse fundamental group and torsion divisor classes of generalized Fermat quotients address different invariants.

## Limitations
The theorem is for this explicitly defined order-six action on one complex Fermat sextic; it asserts nothing about arbitrary generalized Fermat complete intersections, deformations, resolutions, stringy Hodge numbers, or the scheme structure of the singular locus. The commuting-pair Euler number is defined here by its formula and has no claimed identification with the coarse Euler number or a resolution Euler number. Older cyclic-Fermat quotient literature could independently contain the genus-four curve model, and unindexed literature may contain related quotient calculations; independent literature auditing has not been performed.
