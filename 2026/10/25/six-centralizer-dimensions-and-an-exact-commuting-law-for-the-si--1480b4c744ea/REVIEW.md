# Scientific review: six-generator exterior-algebra centralizers

## Correctness — PASS

Graded commutativity in odd characteristic reduces element-centralizers to kernels of wedge multiplication on the odd part. The degree-one case has independent free dimensions \(1+10+5=16\). When the degree-one term vanishes but the trivector is nonzero, the kernel has dimension \(25+k\), where the vector wedge-annihilator dimension is exactly \(k\in\{0,1,3\}\). A unique-line incidence count gives \(D_1\); decomposable trivectors give \(D_3\); subtraction gives \(D_0\). Pure five-forms and zero supply the last two rows. Exact algebraic summation yields the commuting probability. Direct rank calculations, finite trivector enumeration, and finite-field count identities agree. The all-field statement relies on the written proof.

## Originality — PASS within investigated literature

The closest publications are Bovdi–Leung (arXiv:1803.03457) and Domokos–Zubor (arXiv:1403.2916), which study commutative subalgebras, not an element-by-element centralizer dimension distribution. De Bruyn–Kwiatkowski classify trivector orbits: this is stronger as an orbit classification and can imply the coarse wedge-annihilator trichotomy. The current claim does **not** treat this trichotomy, decomposable-trivector counts, graded commutation, or the maximal commutative dimension as original in themselves; it gives the whole inhomogeneous algebra centralizer census and a field-uniform commuting law. Searches of related mathematical records and direct matching formulas did not locate this precise theorem. Residual risk: an equivalent result might have been stated as a derived corollary in unexamined work on finite Grassmann algebras, and orbit tables might be used to rederive individual rows. Uniqueness is not certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The probability that two algebra elements commute is an intrinsic ring invariant, and the full centralizer spectrum captures significantly more information than the maximum possible commutative-subalgebra dimension. Six generators is the first even rank with a nontrivial middle-degree pairing between two three-forms and both full-rank and one-line-annihilator trivectors; these create centralizer dimensions not seen in the smaller five-generator principal-unit analysis. The result gives a complete, exact formula uniform over all odd finite fields, and explains each stratum geometrically rather than merely fitting a sequence.

## Scientific limits

Characteristic two is excluded. The result concerns algebra elements, not the principal-unit group; and it does not classify centralizer algebra isomorphism types. The standalone finite checker is corroboration, not proof of the symbolic generality.
