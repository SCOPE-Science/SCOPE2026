# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** Every allowed two-arrow face extends to a unique directed Hamiltonian four-cycle, every three-arrow face is a Hamiltonian path, and the six resulting tetrahedron boundaries have no positive-dimensional intersections. A connected bipartite incidence graph with 18 vertices and 24 edges gives exactly seven independent circle classes while the six sphere fundamental classes give all degree-two homology. The proof is integral. The stated group characters follow from signed fixed-cycle contributions and an exact sequence of permutation modules on the incidence graph; the embedded checker exhaustively tests all simplices and all 48 actions. No infinite extrapolation occurs.

**Originality: PASS on inspected evidence.** The nearest same-object predecessor is Vrećica–Živaljević, arXiv:0710.5252: their Theorem 10 identifies \(\Omega_4\) as connected but does not determine its mixed-dimensional homotopy type or full joint action of vertex relabeling and arrow reversal. Caputi–Collari–Di Trani (arXiv:2110.11206, Section 6) supplies the simplicial multipath framework, and Caputi–Collari–Di Trani–Smith (arXiv:2208.04656) handles other directed-graph families, not the claimed representation. Later bidirectional polygon results (arXiv:2601.05670) do not cover the complete digraph. The ordinary \(4\times3\) rook complex is distinct despite the coincident face vector. Directed-sphere-gluing and complete character comparisons returned no covering published theorem in the checked sources and record database. The risk of a separately indexed exact calculation remains.

**Value: PASS.** The four-vertex case is the first connected cycle-free chessboard complex (the three-vertex case is disconnected), and the basic connectivity theorem leaves its homotopy ranks unspecified. The explicit six-tetrahedron decomposition upgrades that to an integral mixed-dimensional homotopy classification and determines both nontrivial rational symmetry modules. It also provides a small exact benchmark for multipath/cohomology computations and illustrates why Euler characteristic alone loses information. This is a natural complete classification, not an arbitrary parameter slice.

**Limitations.** No larger-vertex statement or integral group-ring decomposition is asserted; no independent scientific audit has occurred.
