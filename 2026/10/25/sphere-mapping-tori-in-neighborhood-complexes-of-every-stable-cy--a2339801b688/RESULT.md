# Sphere mapping-tori in neighborhood complexes of every stable cycle power

## Finding
Let \(k\ge2\) and \(n\ge4k+1\) be integers. Let \(C_n^{(k)}\) be the simple graph with vertex set \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) and edges joining vertices of cyclic distance \(1,\ldots,k\). Write \(K_{n,k}=\mathcal N(C_n^{(k)})\) for its ordinary **open** Lovász neighborhood complex: a nonempty set of graph vertices is a face precisely when its members admit one common, distinct graph neighbor.

**Theorem.** The complex \(K_{n,k}\) is homotopy equivalent to the mapping torus of a sphere self-map \(S^{k-1}\to S^{k-1}\) of degree \((-1)^{kn}\). Hence it is homotopy equivalent to \(S^{k-1}\times S^1\) if \(kn\) is even, and to the mapping torus of a reflection of \(S^{k-1}\) if \(k\) and \(n\) are both odd. Its integral homology is torsion-free for even \(kn\); for odd \(kn\), its only torsion is \(H_{k-1}(K_{n,k};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\). More precisely, for \(k\ge3\), \(H_0=H_1=\mathbb Z\) and \((H_{k-1},H_k)=(\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z)\) in the even case and \((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,0)\) in the odd case, with other groups zero. For \(k=2\), \(K_{n,2}\simeq S^1\times S^1\), so \((H_0,H_1,H_2)=(\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z^2,\mathbb Z)\).

The essential new range is \(k\ge4\): this gives arbitrarily high-dimensional sphere mapping-tori and parity-dependent integral torsion in every even sphere-fiber dimension. The bound \(n\ge4k+1\) is a sufficient stable-range condition, not asserted sharp.

## Assumptions and scope
All simplicial complexes use their standard geometric realizations. For a simple graph \(G\), the open neighborhood of a vertex \(v\) is \(N_G(v)=\{w\ne v:\{v,w\}\in E(G)\}\). Thus \(\mathcal N(G)=\bigcup_v \Delta(N_G(v))\); closed neighborhoods would produce a different complex. The assertions are homotopy classifications, not homeomorphism or PL-manifold assertions for the finite complexes. No smaller-period cases are claimed. A mapping torus of a sphere reflection is the standard orientation-reversing sphere bundle only up to the claimed homotopy equivalence.

## Proof
**1. The infinite neighborhood nerve has spherical homotopy.** Let \(G_\infty\) be the graph on \(\mathbb Z\) whose edges have positive difference at most \(k\). Set \(L_k=\mathcal N(G_\infty)\), with facets \(F_i=\Delta(\{i-k,\ldots,i-1,i+1,\ldots,i+k\})\), indexed by \(i\in\mathbb Z\). All nonempty finite intersections of the simplices \(F_i\) are simplices, so the simplicial nerve theorem applies to any finite subunion.

In \(A=\bigcup_{i=0}^{k}F_i\), every proper collection of facets intersects: if the center \(j\in\{0,\ldots,k\}\) is omitted, the graph vertex \(j\) belongs to every remaining neighborhood. But the intersection of all \(k+1\) facets is empty: any potential common neighbor must lie between \(0\) and \(k\) to be within distance \(k\) of both extreme centers, and it equals one of those forbidden centers. Consequently the nerve of this cover is exactly \(\partial\Delta^k\), yielding \(A\simeq S^{k-1}\).

For every integer segment \([a,b]\) with \(b-a\ge k\), adjoining \(F_{b+1}\) leaves the homotopy type unchanged. To see this, translate so the new facet is \(F_0\) and the old facets are \(F_{-1},\ldots,F_{-d}\), with \(d\ge k+1\). Its intersection with the old union is \(C=F_0\cap\bigcup_{j=1}^{d}F_{-j}\). Put \(Q=\{-k,\ldots,-1\}\). The entire simplex \(\Delta(Q)\) lies in \(C\), since it is \(F_0\cap F_{-(k+1)}\). For \(1\le j<k\), the simplex \(F_0\cap F_{-j}\) contains the vertex \(-k\), while for \(j\ge k\), every such intersection is a face of \(\Delta(Q)\) (or empty). Thus \(C\) is a simplicial cone with apex \(-k\), and both \(C\) and \(F_0\) are contractible. The standard homotopy-pushout property of CW subcomplex inclusions implies \(\bigcup_{i=a}^{b}F_i\to\bigcup_{i=a}^{b+1}F_i\) is a homotopy equivalence. The leftward extension follows by reflection. Passing to the increasing union (equivalently its CW mapping telescope), \(L_k\simeq S^{k-1}\).

**2. Translation acts by degree \((-1)^k\).** Let \(t:L_k\to L_k\) send each vertex \(i\) to \(i+1\). To calculate its action on the cyclic top homology, use the subunion \(B=\bigcup_{i=0}^{k+1}F_i\). The nerve contains all subsets of \(\{0,\ldots,k+1\}\) of size at most \(k\), along with precisely the \((k+1)\)-subsets omitting an interior center \(j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\). Indeed the latter have common neighbor \(j\); the two omitted-endpoint subsets \(\{0,\ldots,k\}\) and \(\{1,\ldots,k+1\}\) have empty intersection by step 1. The full \((k+2)\)-fold intersection is also empty.

Orient the formal vertex simplices increasingly. The two boundary cycles \(z_0=\partial[0,\ldots,k]\) and \(z_1=\partial[1,\ldots,k+1]\) represent the corresponding nerve generators. From \(\partial^2[0,\ldots,k+1]=0\), discarding the two absent top faces but retaining the present interior-index-omission top faces, one obtains the relation \([z_1]=(-1)^k[z_0]\) in the homology of this nerve. Nerve equivalences are natural for inclusion of facet-indexed covers, so this identity gives \(t_*=(-1)^k\) on \(H_{k-1}(L_k;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\).

**3. Finite cyclic quotient and the mapping torus.** Reduction modulo \(n\) gives a graph map \(G_\infty\to C_n^{(k)}\). Because \(n\ge4k+1\), it identifies no two integers within a graph two-neighborhood \([i-2k,i+2k]\); every graph walk of at most two steps has a unique lift after its initial vertex is chosen. It is therefore a graph 2-covering. The graph-covering-to-neighborhood-covering lemma (Matsushita, Proposition 3.5) gives a regular simplicial realization covering \(L_k\to K_{n,k}\) with deck group \(n\mathbb Z\), generated by \(t^n\). Equivalently, \(K_{n,k}=L_k/\langle t^n\rangle\).

For a free cellular action of \(\mathbb Z\), the quotient has the homotopy type of the Borel construction, which is the mapping torus of its generator. Conjugating the self-homotopy equivalence \(t^n\) through \(L_k\simeq S^{k-1}\) yields a sphere self-homotopy equivalence of degree \(((-1)^k)^n=(-1)^{kn}\). Sphere self-maps are classified up to homotopy by degree, and hence this mapping torus is homotopy equivalent to one of the two stated sphere-bundle mapping tori.

**4. Integral homology.** The Wang sequence of the mapping torus is determined by \(1-(-1)^{kn}\) on the top reduced integral homology of the fiber. For \(k\ge3\), the fiber is simply connected, and the sequence gives \(H_1=\mathbb Z\), \(H_{k-1}=\operatorname{coker}(1-(-1)^{kn})\), and \(H_k=\ker(1-(-1)^{kn})\). For \(k=2\), \(kn\) is always even, and the mapping torus of degree-one circle map is homotopy equivalent to the two-torus. These calculations prove every claimed integral group.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library `verify.py` checks the decisive finite combinatorial nerve identities for \(2\le k\le7\), and independently computes simplicial homology modulo \(101\) for \(2\le k\le5\) at \(n=4k+1,4k+2\). At odd \(kn\), it also checks mod-two homology to detect the predicted torsion signature. The checks passed and their output is embedded as `verification_output.txt`. These finite tests do not imply the theorem for unbounded parameters; the general conclusions rest on the proved cone, nerve, covering, and Wang-sequence arguments.

## Relationship to prior work
Matsushita [1] supplies the graph-covering theory used in step 3 and is not claimed as new. Shukla [2] classified neighborhood complexes for 4-regular circulants (covering the \(k=2\) member), not arbitrary higher-degree cycle powers. A published third-power calculation [3] gives a stronger concrete simplicial collapse and a PL sphere-bundle identification for \(k=3\); this is an acknowledged special case of the present *homotopy* classification, not an independently novel subcase. The new result is the uniform unrestricted-power theorem for \(k\ge4\), including integral monodromy and dimension-dependent torsion, derived by an infinite-nerve and covering argument rather than finite pattern extrapolation. No claim is made that these sphere bundles themselves are newly discovered spaces.

## Limitations
The result assumes \(n\ge4k+1\), and this sufficient cutoff has not been optimized. It computes homotopy types, not literal triangulated homeomorphism types or refined integral automorphism lattices. Equivalent general results might exist in unindexed literature; particularly, a full-text survey across all higher-valency circulant complexes was not completed. The published third-power result is genuinely overlapping and has explicitly different, stronger PL and collapse information. This is a self-review; independent scientific audit has not been performed.
