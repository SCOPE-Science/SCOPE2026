# Review of the stable cycle-power neighborhood-complex theorem

## Correctness
PASS. The proof identifies the nerve of exactly \(k+1\) consecutive infinite-cover neighborhood simplices with the boundary of a \(k\)-simplex. Every further facet attaches along an explicitly conical intersection. The signed relation in the nerve of the next \(k+2\) facets gives translation degree \((-1)^k\), while \(n\ge4k+1\) ensures the required graph two-covering. The mapping-torus Wang sequence yields the integral homology, including the \(k=2\) overlapping-degree exception. The included checker corroborates critical combinatorics and finite mod-prime predictions.

## Originality
PASS within inspected sources, subject to bibliographic risk. The published third-power computation already covers \(k=3\) and has more detailed PL results; Shukla covers the 4-regular case \(k=2\). Neither inspected source proves the all-power homotopy theorem \(k\ge4\), and Matsushita's graph covering lemma is an input rather than an equivalent classification. Literal homology and homotopy implications, not merely titles, were compared. A general result outside indexed materials remains possible.

## Mathematical value
PASS. One argument organizes every stable valency \(2k\) into two monodromy types, proves all-dimensional integral homology, and exposes order-two torsion for arbitrarily high even sphere-fiber dimensions. This is structural topology, not a routine finite census or a one-parameter numerical improvement.

## Limits
No optimality of the stable-range cutoff or PL homeomorphism statement is asserted; the overlap with the published third-power case is explicit. Independent external validation is absent.
