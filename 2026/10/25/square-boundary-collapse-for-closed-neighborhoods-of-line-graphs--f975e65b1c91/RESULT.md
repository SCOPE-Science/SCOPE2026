# Square-boundary collapse for closed neighborhoods of line graphs

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(G\) be any finite, connected, simple, triangle-free graph with at least one edge. Let \(L(G)\) be its line graph, \(K=\mathcal N[L(G)]\) the closed-neighborhood complex, and \(Y=\operatorname{Cl}(L(G))\) its clique complex. For each unoriented simple four-cycle \(C\) in \(G\), its four edges form the vertex set of a tetrahedral boundary \(B_C=\partial\Delta^3_{E(C)}\) inside \(K\). There is an explicit sequence of elementary simplicial collapses
\[
K\searrow Y\cup\bigcup_{C\in\mathscr Q(G)}B_C.
\]
Let \(Q(G)\) denote the CW square-completion of \(G\), obtained by attaching **one separate two-cell along each unoriented four-cycle**, and let \(q=|\mathscr Q(G)|\). Then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal N[L(G)]\simeq Q(G)\vee\bigvee^{q}S^2.}
\]
No degree bound, bound on common neighbors, or restriction on how distinct four-cycles intersect is imposed. In particular, the inclusion of \(Y\) induces a surjection on integral first homology whose kernel, under the canonical identification \(H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(G;\mathbb Z)\), is generated by the integral cycles of the four-cycles of \(G\). The integral homology of \(K\) therefore satisfies
\[
H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(G;\mathbb Z)/\langle[C]:C\in\mathscr Q(G)\rangle,\qquad
H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong H_2(Q(G);\mathbb Z)\oplus\mathbb Z^q,
\]
with \(\widetilde H_i(K;\mathbb Z)=0\) for \(i\ge3\). No assertion that the displayed degree-one quotient is torsion-free is needed.

## Assumptions and scope

All graph edges are unordered and simple; a graph-theoretic four-cycle is counted once modulo cyclic rotation and reversal, including when different cycles share edges or vertices. The vertices of \(L(G)\) are the edges of \(G\). For a vertex \(v\in V(G)\), put \(S_v=\{f\in E(G):v\in f\}\), and write \(\Delta(S)\) for the simplex whose vertex set is \(S\). The complexes \(K\), \(Y\), and \(B_C\) include all their faces. A closed-neighborhood simplex is a subset of the closed neighborhood of some vertex of \(L(G)\), including that vertex itself. The result also describes the intrinsic vertex-centered graph Čech nerve at **scale two** under the convention of Adams–Shukla–Singh; it is not about Vietoris–Rips complexes or arbitrary scales.

## Proof

**1. Identify the generating simplices.** For an edge \(e=uv\in E(G)\), the closed neighborhood of \(e\) in \(L(G)\) is precisely \(S_u\cup S_v\). Consequently
\[
K=\bigcup_{e=uv\in E(G)}\Delta(S_u\cup S_v).
\]
Because \(G\) contains no triangles, every pairwise-intersecting collection of its edges has a common endpoint. Therefore
\[
Y=\bigcup_{v\in V(G)}\Delta(S_v).
\]
A face outside \(Y\) necessarily contains two disjoint edges \(a,b\) of \(G\).

**2. Classify ambiguous generating centers.** Call an edge \(e=uv\) a *center* of a face \(\sigma\in K\) if \(\sigma\subseteq S_u\cup S_v\). A center of any face containing two disjoint edges \(a,b\) must connect an endpoint of \(a\) to an endpoint of \(b\). For triangle-free \(G\), there are at most two such connecting edges: if two connectors shared an endpoint, the edge \(a\) or \(b\) would complete a triangle. If there are two, they are disjoint, and together with \(a,b\) are exactly the four edges of an embedded four-cycle \(C\). Moreover, the only edges incident to at least one endpoint of **each** of those two disjoint connectors are \(a\) and \(b\); any third would form a triangle. Thus a face outside \(Y\) has more than one center **if and only if** it is the opposite-edge pair \(\{a,b\}\) of a four-cycle. Such a pair has exactly two centers.

For each square \(C=(e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3)\), with the listed graph edges in cyclic order, all four triples of the four edge-vertices belong to \(K\): the middle edge of any consecutive triple is a center. All four triples and their faces constitute \(B_C=\partial\Delta^3_{E(C)}\). The four-element set cannot lie in \(K\), since the two opposite-edge pairs would require a common center but have disjoint sets of centers. Thus \(B_C\) is a subcomplex of \(K\), and \(T=Y\cup\bigcup_CB_C\subseteq K\).

**3. Explicit elementary collapses onto \(T\).** For every \(\sigma\in K\setminus T\) not containing its unique center \(e=e(\sigma)\), pair
\[
\sigma\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad\sigma\cup\{e\}.
\]
This is a partition of \(K\setminus T\) into pairs. To check the nontrivial direction, suppose \(\tau\in K\setminus T\) contains its center \(e=uv\). Then \(\sigma=\tau\setminus\{e\}\) still contains edges incident separately at \(u\) and at \(v\) (otherwise \(\tau\in Y\)); those edges are disjoint by triangle-freeness. Hence \(\sigma\notin Y\). If \(\sigma\) were an opposite-edge pair of a square, then \(\tau\) would be one of the retained triangular faces in \(B_C\). If \(\sigma\) were a larger face of \(B_C\), its three-edge case has its unique generating center **among those three edges**, so a different \(e\notin\sigma\) could not witness \(\tau\). Thus \(\sigma\notin T\) and has the same unique center \(e\), proving it is the lower partner of \(\tau\).

Remove all pairs in decreasing order of the cardinality of the lower face \(\sigma\), with arbitrary tie-breaking. Every coface of \(\sigma\) has the same unique center \(e\). (A coface cannot belong to \(T\), since \(T\) is a subcomplex; and a center of a coface is also a center of \(\sigma\).) At its turn, any strictly larger coface other than \(\sigma\cup\{e\}\) has already been removed: if it omits \(e\), it was a larger lower face; if it contains \(e\), its partner after deleting \(e\) was a larger lower face. Therefore \(\sigma\) is a free codimension-one face of precisely \(\sigma\cup\{e\}\), and each removal is an elementary simplicial collapse. At completion the remaining complex is exactly \(T\). These arguments include degree-one vertices and overlapping four-cycles; they never use a girth or common-neighbor restriction beyond triangle-freeness.

**4. Identify the square-completion and split spheres.** Cover \(Y\) by the simplices \(\Delta(S_v)\). For distinct \(u,v\), the intersection is a point precisely when \(uv\in E(G)\), and otherwise empty; intersections of three distinct covering simplices are empty. The good-cover nerve is therefore \(G\). More specifically, replacing every \(\Delta(S_v)\) by the tree connecting its incident edge-vertices to a new vertex \(v\) gives a homotopy equivalence \(Y\simeq G\), carrying the four-cycle on the edge-vertices of any \(C\) to the barycentric subdivision of the corresponding four-cycle of \(G\).

On \(B_C\), choose one of its two diagonals and the two triangular faces containing it. They form a disk with boundary the four-cycle on edge-vertices of \(C\). Distinct squares have distinct new diagonals: an opposite pair of edges of \(G\) determines at most one four-cycle, as shown in Step 2. Hence the union of \(Y\) and these chosen disks, one per \(C\), is a CW square filling of \(Y\), homotopy equivalent to \(Q(G)\) compatibly with the attaching maps. The other two triangular faces of \(B_C\) form a second disk attached along the same four-cycle, already null-homotopic through the first disk. Attaching each second disk therefore wedges on one \(S^2\). The distinct second disks have distinct interior diagonals, so the attachments can be performed one by one. This proves the homotopy equivalence.

**5. Integral homology and inclusion.** The relative collapse leaves \(Y\) and adds, for each square, two disks with the same attaching loop. Each first disk kills the corresponding four-cycle class in integral \(H_1(Y)\); each second disk adds a free integral degree-two summand. The homotopy equivalence \(Y\simeq G\) identifies the first attaching loops with precisely the graph four-cycles. The displayed integral formulas follow from the homotopy splitting and the cellular chain complex of the two-dimensional \(Q(G)\), including all possible relations among square boundaries.

## Verification

The standalone `line_graph_square_verifier.py` generates every face of \(K\), the predicted \(T\), and the center pairing, then verifies that every proposed lower face is free at the moment it is removed. It checks exact final face-set equality, without relying on finite-field Betti numbers. Execution on 480 triangle-free graph instances (including all labeled triangle-free graphs on at most five vertices having an edge; sampled six-vertex graphs; theta graphs; \(K_{2,3}\); \(K_{3,3}\); the three-cube; a \(3\times3\) grid; and the Petersen graph) checked 7,881 nonempty faces, 1,555 elementary collapse pairs and 143 four-cycle occurrences; it returned `VERIFY_OK`. All computations use only Python's standard library and integer bitmasks. Finite checks corroborate Steps 1–3 but are not an infinite-family proof.

For example \(G=K_{2,3}\) has three four-cycles, and its square completion is \(S^2\): three two-cells kill its rank-two graph cycle group with one integral relation. The theorem gives \(\mathcal N[L(K_{2,3})]\simeq\bigvee^4S^2\), a case not covered by an assumption excluding \(K_{2,3}\). For three internally disjoint paths of lengths \(2,2,\ell\), with \(\ell\ge3\), the square completion fills only their unique four-cycle, leaving a circle; thus the line-graph neighborhood complex has type \(S^1\vee S^2\). These examples include genuine overlapping long cycles and are not used as proof of the general theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Matsushita (2025) sets up closed neighborhood complexes and general suspension/fundamental-group correspondences. Adams–Shukla–Singh supply the scale-two graph Čech neighborhood description. Goyal–Shukla–Singh establish the previously known equivalence \(\operatorname{Cl}(L(G))\simeq G\) for triangle-free \(G\). A prior line-graph Čech collapse theorem covers **girth at least five**, hence no squares. Previously published square-completion theorems concern the Čech complex of \(G\) itself under an additional no-\(K_{2,3}\) hypothesis; applying them with input \(L(G)\) is invalid when \(G\) has degree at least three because \(L(G)\) contains triangles. The present explicit **line-graph** relative collapse admits all four-cycle overlaps and all triangle-free degrees, and its square-completion relation is not supplied by those earlier implications.

## Limitations

Triangle-free is essential to the center argument: if \(G\) has triangles, a mixed face may have extra centers and the pairing above need not work. The theorem does not calculate the homotopy type of the square-completion \(Q(G)\) for arbitrary graphs, does not classify higher Čech scales, and does not claim that homology of \(Q(G)\) is always free. The wedge decomposition is homotopy-theoretic, not a simplicial collapse to a literal wedge. A result expressed using an uninspected equivalent construction remains a residual originality risk. Independent scientific validation has not been performed.
