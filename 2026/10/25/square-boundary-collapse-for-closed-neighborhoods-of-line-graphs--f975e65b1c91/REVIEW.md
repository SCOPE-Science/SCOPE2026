# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Each mixed simplex has disjoint graph edges and at most two connector centers; two distinct centers occur precisely for the opposite-edge pairs of four-cycles. The described unique-center pairing deletes exactly those faces outside the clique complex and square tetrahedral boundaries by actual descending free-face elementary collapses. The clique-star nerve identifies the four-cycle loops in the original graph, and attaching first and second square disks yields the stated homotopy splitting integrally. An exact executable confirms 480 graph cases, 7,881 simplices, 1,555 valid elementary collapses, 143 four-cycle occurrences; exhaustive within its explicit finite test domain, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS

The closest published line-graph Čech collapse applies only to girth at least five and expressly excludes every square. Published square-completion decompositions concern closed-neighborhood complexes of the triangle-free input graph itself subject to forbidding \(K_{2,3}\); their assumptions fail on the line graph at branching vertices. The Goyal–Shukla–Singh clique-complex theorem and Matsushita closed-neighborhood framework do not imply the new unrestricted relative-collapse claim. Exact manuscript sections and published results were compared by mathematical implication, not title. Unindexed equivalent material remains possible.

## Value — PASS

The theorem gives the exact homotopy type for an infinite unrestricted triangle-free graph class, even for arbitrary overlapping squares and \(K_{2,3}\), reducing the entire question to a motivated CW square completion and a precisely counted wedge of two-spheres. The \(K_{2,3}\) example exhibits essential square relations outside the reach of earlier hypotheses, so this is more than a routine special case or Betti-number calculation.

## Closest literature and limits

Matsushita (arXiv:2511.12608), Adams–Shukla–Singh (arXiv:2212.05871), Goyal–Shukla–Singh (arXiv:2009.12130), and the previously published line-graph girth-five and square-completion theorems are the principal comparisons. The triangular-free hypothesis is essential to the center argument; the theorem does not simplify the square-filled CW complex in general, rule out torsion in its integral first homology, or cover other Čech scales. No independent validation has been performed.
