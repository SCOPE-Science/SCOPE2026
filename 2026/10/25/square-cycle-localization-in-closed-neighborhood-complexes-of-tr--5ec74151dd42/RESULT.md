# Square-cycle localization in closed-neighborhood complexes of triangle-free cactus line graphs

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(G\) be a finite connected simple **triangle-free cactus** with at least one edge. Here cactus means that any two simple cycles have at most one common vertex, equivalently each edge belongs to at most one simple cycle. Write \(q\) for the number of its four-cycle blocks and \(r\) for the number of its cycle blocks of length at least five. Let \(L(G)\) be its line graph and define the closed-neighborhood simplicial complex
\[
K(G)=\mathcal N[L(G)]=\bigcup_{e\in E(G)}\Delta(N_{L(G)}[e]).
\]
Then, with the zero-fold wedge interpreted as a point,
\[
\boxed{K(G)\simeq \bigvee^{q}S^2\vee\bigvee^{r}S^1.}
\]
All reduced integral homology groups are free, with \(\widetilde H_1(K(G);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^r\), \(\widetilde H_2(K(G);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^q\), and zero otherwise.

**Integral inclusion refinement.** Let \(T(G)=\operatorname{Cl}(L(G))\subseteq K(G)\), the clique (flag) complex of the line graph. The natural inclusion induces a surjection
\[
H_1(T(G);\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow H_1(K(G);\mathbb Z)
\]
whose kernel is the primitive rank-\(q\) direct summand generated by the four-cycle fundamental classes, one for each square block. The surviving rank-\(r\) classes are precisely the longer cycle-block classes. This is a **flag-to-closed-neighborhood** inclusion, not an assertion about the raw graph-to-Čech map at scale one.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite, undirected, loopless and simple. The vertices of \(L(G)\) are the original edges. For a graph vertex \(v\) set \(S_v=\{e\in E(G):v\in e\}\). An edge \(e=uv\) in \(G\) gives the line-graph closed neighborhood \(N_{L(G)}[e]=S_u\cup S_v\), so
\[
K(G)=\bigcup_{uv\in E(G)}\Delta(S_u\cup S_v).
\]
Here \(\Delta(S)\) denotes the simplex with vertex set \(S\). The condition of being triangle-free excludes triangular cycle blocks; leaves, bridges, arbitrary branching, multiple square blocks, and arbitrarily long blocks are allowed. A block can meet other blocks at articulation vertices, but blocks do not share edges. The theorem covers all finite such cacti, not only bouquet graphs or paths of square blocks.

## Proof

**1. Flag complex and the original graph.** In a triangle-free graph, any pairwise incident family of edges has a common endpoint: three pairwise incident edges without a common endpoint would form a triangle. Thus
\[
T(G)=\bigcup_{v\in V(G)}\Delta(S_v)\subseteq K(G).
\]
These star-simplices intersect pairwise at one vertex exactly when their centers are adjacent, and every three distinct stars have empty common intersection. The nerve lemma, applied to their contractible nonempty intersections, gives \(T(G)\simeq G\). In particular its integral first homology has the freely generated basis of the cactus cycle blocks.

**2. The gluing lemma.** Suppose \(G=G_1\cup G_2\) is a union of two nonempty triangle-free subgraphs with disjoint edge sets and exactly one common vertex \(v\). Write \(A=S_v\cap E(G_1)\), \(B=S_v\cap E(G_2)\), and
\[
J=K(G_1)\cup K(G_2)\cup\Delta(A\cup B).
\]
All three subcomplexes lie in \(K(G)\). Their intersections satisfy \(K(G_1)\cap K(G_2)=\varnothing\), \(K(G_1)\cap\Delta(A\cup B)=\Delta(A)\), and \(K(G_2)\cap\Delta(A\cup B)=\Delta(B)\). Therefore \(J\simeq K(G_1)\vee K(G_2)\); moreover \(T(G)\subseteq J\).

We now give an explicit elementary collapse \(K(G)\searrow J\). Every face \(F\notin J\) is mixed. After interchanging the two sides if necessary, it contains some \(b\in B\) and some \(x\in E(G_1)\setminus A\). It cannot contain edges outside the common star on both sides, since no generating closed neighborhood could contain such a pair. Every generator simplex containing \(F\) is centered at an edge \(e=vu\in A\) incident to \(x\). There is at most one such edge: if both \(vu\) and \(vw\) met the original edge \(x\), then \(x=uw\) and \(v,u,w\) would form a forbidden triangle. Existence follows because \(F\in K(G)\). Thus each face outside \(J\) has a **unique generator** \(e(F)\in A\) (or symmetrically \(B\)). Its crossing witnesses \(b,x\) are distinct from \(e(F)\); consequently adding or deleting \(e(F)\) leaves the face outside \(J\) and preserves its unique generator.

Pair every face \(F\notin J\) that does not contain \(e(F)\) with \(F\cup\{e(F)\}\). These pairs partition all faces outside \(J\). Remove them in descending order of the number of vertices in the lower face. Indeed, an additional remaining coface of \(F\) would still be outside \(J\) and have the same unique generator. If it omits the generator, it is a larger lower face already removed; if it contains it, its paired lower face is larger and already removed, unless the coface is precisely the designated upper face. Hence at every removal \(F\) is a free face with unique remaining cofacet \(F\cup\{e(F)\}\). This proves the claimed sequence of elementary collapses and fixes \(J\) pointwise.

**3. Blocks.** Any connected cactus is formed by successive one-vertex gluings of bridge blocks and cycle blocks, following its block-cut tree. The preceding lemma therefore splits \(K(G)\) as a wedge of the complexes of its blocks. The one-edge bridge block has a point as its closed-neighborhood complex. For an \(m\)-cycle with \(m\ge5\), its line graph is again a cycle, and each consecutive-three-vertex triangle in \(K(C_m)\) has a free diagonal edge. Collapse these triangles along their diagonals to obtain the cycle \(T(C_m)=C_m\), so \(K(C_m)\simeq S^1\). For the four-cycle, the four closed neighborhoods are the four triangular facets of a tetrahedron boundary, whence \(K(C_4)=\partial\Delta^3\cong S^2\). Wedge decomposition yields exactly \(q\) two-spheres and \(r\) circles over the integers.

**4. Integral inclusion and primitive kernel.** The gluing collapse in step 2 fixes \(T(G)\). The intersections of the block complexes with the connecting simplex are contractible, so Mayer–Vietoris identifies the inclusion map on \(H_1\) with the direct sum of the blockwise inclusion maps. The bridge contribution is zero. A long cycle contributes an isomorphism \(\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z\) by its explicit collapse, whereas a four-cycle contributes \(\mathbb Z\to0\) because it embeds into \(S^2\). Consequently the integral kernel is exactly the direct sum of the square-block fundamental classes, each a basis element of \(H_1(T(G);\mathbb Z)\); this proves its primitivity, the surjectivity, and the absence of torsion.

## Verification

The standalone `verify.py` reconstructs the closed-neighborhood faces directly from the graph edge sets and enumerates every mixed-face pairing for 16 independent triangle-free one-vertex sums of cycles. It checks that generators are unique, all outside faces are paired, and each of the 384 proposed removals is a genuine elementary free-face collapse. It also computes mod-101 Betti numbers for ten multi-cycle cacti with additional pendant edges, and verifies eight cycle examples with lengths three through ten. Executing `python verify.py` reports three PASS lines. These finite computations corroborate the construction only; the argument in the Proof section is the proof for every finite cactus and establishes the integral statements.

## Relationship to prior work

Matsushita [1] defined and investigated closed-neighborhood complexes and their relationship to graph fundamental groups. A published line-graph Čech collapse theorem [2] covers graphs with no triangles or four-cycles, so it implies the long-cycle and bridge subfamily but **does not** cover any cactus with a square block. The known direct computation \(K(C_4)=S^2\) also appears as a sharp boundary example in [2], but neither it nor the no-square theorem determines how multiple square and longer-cycle blocks interact at shared cut vertices or how the flag-complex cycle lattice maps integrally. Broader square-completion results [3] apply to triangle-free **input** graphs; line graphs of branched cacti contain triangles, so their hypotheses are not satisfied. The present contribution is the elementary gluing collapse and its resulting mixed-dimensional and integral block-localization conclusions. In particular it does not claim discovery of the individual cycle base cases.

## Limitations

The assumption that the original cactus has no triangles is essential to the proof: triangular blocks and triangles spanning gluing parts are not treated. The wedge equivalence is a homotopy equivalence rather than a claim that the full closed-neighborhood complex is homeomorphic to a wedge. No claim is made for arbitrary graphs with overlapping cycle blocks, arbitrary line graphs, higher Čech scales, or complete persistence modules. Prior calculations settle the square-free special case, and independent novelty assessment of the full block-gluing result remains appropriate.
