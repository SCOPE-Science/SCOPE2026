# Square-zero local rings exhibit a unique nonfield collision with the field ideal-product probability

## Finding
Let \(R\) be a finite commutative local ring with identity, maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) satisfying \(\mathfrak m^2=0\), residue field \(\mathbb F_q=R/\mathfrak m\), and \(r=\dim_{\mathbb F_q}\mathfrak m\ge0\). Write
\[
L(q,r)=1+\sum_{j=0}^{r}{r\brack j}_q,
\qquad
{r\brack j}_q=\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{q^{r-i}-1}{q^{j-i}-1}.
\]
(The empty product is \(1\).) If \(k\ge2\) ideals are selected independently and uniformly from all two-sided ideals, then their ideal product is zero with probability
\[
\boxed{\zeta_k(R)=1-\frac{1+k(L(q,r)-2)}{L(q,r)^k}.}
\]
For a field \(F\), \(\zeta_k(F)=1-2^{-k}\). Among nonfields in this square-zero local class, equality \(\zeta_k(R)=\zeta_k(F)\) holds **if and only if** \(k=2\), \(q=2\), and \(r=2\). In particular, each of the nonisomorphic eight-element rings
\[
R_1=\mathbb F_2[x,y]/(x,y)^2,
\qquad
R_2=(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)[t]/(2t,t^2)
\]
has exactly six ideals and \(\zeta_2(R_i)=27/36=3/4\), the value for every finite field. This contradicts the field-characterization assertion posed as Conjecture 4.2 of Chakraborty and Patra (2026).

The sign of the deviation from the field value is also exact for every square-zero local nonfield. For \(k=2\), it is negative when \(r=1\), zero exactly when \((q,r)=(2,2)\), and positive for all other \(r\ge2\). For \(k=3\), it is negative when \(r=1\) and positive when \(r\ge2\). For \(k\ge4\), it is always positive.

## Assumptions and scope
All rings here are finite, associative, commutative, and unital. A local ring means one with a unique maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\). The condition \(\mathfrak m^2=0\) includes fields by \(\mathfrak m=0\). Ideals include \((0)\) and \(R\); tuples are ordered and sampled with replacement. The product \(I_1\cdots I_k\) is the ideal generated by the products of elements of the factors. The probability uses the number of ideals, not the number of ring elements. Only this square-zero local class is classified: no global characterization of arbitrary finite commutative rings is claimed.

## Proof
**Ideals.** Every proper ideal \(I\) is contained in \(\mathfrak m\). Since \(\mathfrak m^2=0\), multiplication on \(\mathfrak m\) by \(R\) factors through \(R/\mathfrak m=\mathbb F_q\), and every \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear subspace of \(\mathfrak m\) is an ideal. Conversely every proper ideal is such a subspace. There are \({r\brack j}_q\) subspaces of dimension \(j\), so there are exactly \(L(q,r)\) ideals, including the additional ideal \(R\).

**Nonzero products.** Any product containing two proper ideals is zero, because both lie in \(\mathfrak m\). Any product containing the zero ideal is zero as well. A product is therefore nonzero if and only if either all \(k\) factors are \(R\), or exactly one factor is a nonzero proper ideal and the remaining \(k-1\) factors are \(R\). There are \(1+k(L(q,r)-2)\) such ordered tuples among \(L(q,r)^k\) tuples. Subtracting their proportion proves the probability formula. For \(r=0\), \(L(q,0)=2\), giving \(1-2^{-k}\).

**Exact equality and signs.** Let \(L=L(q,r)\) and
\[
D_k(L)=L^k-2^k\bigl(1+k(L-2)\bigr).
\]
The sign of \(\zeta_k(R)-(1-2^{-k})\) is the sign of \(D_k(L)\). For nonfields \(L\ge3\). Direct factorization gives
\[
D_2(L)=(L-2)(L-6),\qquad
D_3(L)=(L-2)(L^2+2L-20).
\]
For \(r=1\), \(L=3\); for \(r=2\), \(L=q+4\ge6\); for \(r\ge3\), monotonicity of subspace counts yields \(L\ge17\) (the value at \(q=2,r=3\)). Hence at \(k=2\), the only nonfield equality occurs at \(L=6\), equivalently \(q=2,r=2\), and the signs follow. At \(k=3\), \(D_3(3)<0\) while \(D_3(L)>0\) for every integer \(L\ge4\).

For all real \(L\ge3\), \(D_4(L)=L^4-64L+112\) is positive: it equals \(1\) at \(L=3\) and has derivative \(4L^3-64>0\). Moreover, for every \(k\ge4\),
\[
D_{k+1}(L)=L D_k(L)+2^k(L-2)\bigl(k(L-2)-1\bigr)>0.
\]
This proves the positive sign for every \(k\ge4\), completing the classification.

**Concrete rings.** In \(R_1\), the maximal ideal is the span of the classes of \(x,y\), and its square vanishes. In \(R_2\), the maximal ideal is generated by the classes of \(2,t\) and has the same square-zero property and residue field \(\mathbb F_2\). Thus both have \(r=2,q=2\), six ideals, and exactly \(1+2(6-2)=9\) ordered pairs with nonzero ideal product. The rings are not isomorphic because their characteristics are \(2\) and \(4\), respectively. Both have eight elements.

## Verification
A standalone Python program `verify.py` enumerates every additive subgroup of each of the two concrete eight-element rings, filters exactly those closed under multiplication by arbitrary ring elements, and confirms that there are six ideals. For each \(2\le k\le5\), it enumerates all ordered ideal tuples, computes their products, and compares the result to the closed formula. It independently tests Gaussian-binomial counts and field-value equality for \(q\in\{2,3,4,5\}\), \(0\le r\le3\), and \(2\le k\le12\). The run reports `VERIFY_OK`. These finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the proof for every finite field and dimension.

## Relationship to prior work
Chakraborty and Patra, *Ideal Zero-Product Probability in Finite Commutative Rings* (arXiv:2608.14618v1, first publicly verified August 19, 2026), define \(\zeta_k\), prove formulas for fields, chain rings, and finite principal ideal rings, and formulate Conjecture 4.2 that equality with the field value forces the ring to be a field. Their Section 10 explicitly asks for extensions beyond chain rings. The present result works in nonprincipal local rings and gives a direct, finite counterexample plus a complete classification within the square-zero local class; it does not claim the definition or the field baseline as new.

Carvalho, Lomp, and Smith, *A note on simple modules over quasi-local rings* (arXiv:1711.10580v1, 2017), §4.1, use the vector-space structure of square-zero radicals and study injective hulls of simple modules, not ideal-product probabilities. Kim and Koç, *Annihilator Multiplication Modules: Ring Characterizations and Constructions* (arXiv:2609.16701v1, 2026), §5.3, analyze modules over square-zero local rings, but do not state a \(\zeta_k\) enumeration or field-value classification. The fact that proper ideals are radical subspaces is standard; the new point is the exact probability and its sharp collision with the recently proposed field criterion.

## Limitations
This is not a classification of all rings with \(\zeta_k(R)=\zeta_k(F)\): rings with nonzero radical square or multiple local factors are not addressed. There is no claim that standard subspace enumeration is novel. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed. The closest motivating preprint was inspected in an accessible full-text version, and related square-zero-ring texts were inspected in relevant sections; earlier equivalent presentations in unindexed literature remain possible.
