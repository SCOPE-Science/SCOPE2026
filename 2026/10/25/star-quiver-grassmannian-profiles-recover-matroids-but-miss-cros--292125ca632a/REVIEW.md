# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

For a star whose one-dimensional leaf arrows land in the central space, a subrepresentation with full leaves at exactly \(S\) is a central subspace containing the fixed span \(L_S\). Quotienting by that span proves the ordinary Grassmannian description on all test schemes, including empty boundary cases. The least permitted central dimension recovers each labelled matroid rank. Over a finite field, the Gaussian point counts imply a lower-triangular transform with unit diagonal and an invertible subset-rank expression for the Tutte polynomial. For the four-leaf family, the first three distinct projective lines force every endomorphism to be scalar; the fourth line preserves the cross-ratio parameter. The checker independently enumerated actual central subspaces for 96 configurations; finite checks support but do not prove the all-field theorem.

## Originality — PASS within inspected primary coverage

Quiver-Grassmannian Euler characteristics and cellularity are established areas. Jun--Sistko (arXiv:2112.06291) and Cerulli Irelli--Esposito--Franzen--Reineke (arXiv:1804.07736) provide relevant background, and in applicable finite/affine-type cases earlier cellularity theorems already imply some weaker geometric consequences. Jarra--Lorscheid--Vital (arXiv:2404.09255) provide a broader theory of quiver matroids and their moduli, but the material inspected does not give this specific two-way indexed star-scheme/matroid classification, coarse Tutte recovery, or cross-ratio blindness among bricks. Lorscheid--Weist (arXiv:1706.00860) concern representation-type detection, not individual brick identification. The rank-function and projective-line ingredients are classical. A specialist prior result may imply the short reduction or family; historical priority is a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The complete family of indexed abstract quiver Grassmannians is a natural geometric invariant. Showing precisely what it detects (the labelled matroid and, after coarse finite-field aggregation, its Tutte polynomial) and what it cannot detect (an infinite family of pairwise nonisomorphic bricks with varying projective cross ratio) is a motivated, sharp reconstruction boundary. The classification works across all fields for the specified stars, not just one finite example.
