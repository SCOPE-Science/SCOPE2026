# Review of Stirling–Bell fibers of one-step Catalan transformations

## Correctness
**PASS (self-review).** The normal form of stylic elements as nested-set \(N\)-tableaux and the row-word evaluation of the quotient are explicit in Abram–Reutenauer and Stein. The proof independently identifies the bottom row with the moved-point set, establishes the precise condition that all upper entries be starts of consecutive support runs, and gives inverse constructions between the resulting tableaux and restricted-growth encodings of set partitions. The support and idempotence counts then follow. Exhaustive, direct generator-action enumeration for all ranks through eight agrees with every Stirling stratum.

## Originality
**PASS (self-review; residual risk).** The October 2026 primary paper gives a general full-core criterion for deciding when two tableaux have the same Catalan image. It does not give the present numerical or rank-stratified fiber formula. The Bell and Stirling counts here are not a mere parameter substitution into an existing enumeration: their proof uses a new restriction of upper-row letters to run starts and a canonical partition encoding. Targeted searches of mathematical records, relevant prior findings and publication text located no equivalent enumerative theorem. This remains a same mathematical model literature comparison, not a uniqueness certificate; an unindexed equivalent enumeration or an implicit derivation in earlier tableau literature remains possible.

## Value
**PASS (self-review).** The quotient's fiber cardinalities are natural invariants of a recently elucidated semigroup homomorphism. The theorem completely computes them, *including row-count strata*, for all \(2^n\) one-step transformations and identifies the exact control parameter (number of support runs). It also gives a transparent family of idempotent images with Bell-number fibers and a closed binomial multiplicity law. This is motivated fiber structure, not an arbitrary finite slice. The theorem does not purport to settle arbitrary-fiber counting.

## Closest literature and limits
Primary full text inspected: I. Stein, *From stylic monoid to Catalan monoid*, arXiv:2610.07983v1, sections 2, 6, 7 and 8. The defining tableau normal forms and quotient construction are credited to A. Abram and C. Reutenauer, *The Stylic Monoid*, arXiv:2106.06556, and M. V. Volkov, *Identities of the stylic monoid*, arXiv:2206.09129. The latter two were compared using accessible bibliographic records and primary abstracts, not claimed to have been exhaustively read. The new formula addresses only one-step transformations. The finite checker cannot replace the proof. The main unresolved bibliographic risk is a previously unindexed fiber count, possibly implicit in older tableau enumeration theory.
