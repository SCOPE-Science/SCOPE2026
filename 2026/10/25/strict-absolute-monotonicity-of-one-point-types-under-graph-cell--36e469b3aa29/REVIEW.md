# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim concerns mixed finite differences for graph **edge-cell attachment**, where adding an edge creates a distinct maximal parameter. The ideal/filter classification gives the exact count: ideals are weighted vertex subsets, and admissible both-nonempty insertion cuts consist of one vertex with incident edges or one graph edge with its two endpoints. Boolean alternating differences factor into indicators that every edge of the edited set is supported by a chosen vertex subset. This yields the claimed exact formula; the full vertex set supplies a strictly positive term in every order. The all-order Möbius coefficients are evaluated at the empty graph, and the small crown specialization uses the already-known Lucas ideal count. Tests enumerate all graphs through four vertices directly and 1,284 derivative instances. The proof, not the finite tests, establishes the universal quantifiers.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
An existing finite-height-two exact one-type theorem covers **individual** incidence-poset counts, and therefore the pointwise census is explicitly excluded from the asserted novel contribution. The all-order strict inequality and mixed-derivative formula compare counts for different finite parameter sets simultaneously; those relations are not presented by the earlier theorem. An earlier sensitivity theorem concerns insertion of a comparability between existing parameters and is not an implication in this setting, since that operation can decrease type counts. The published crown-ideal Lucas formula is acknowledged, not claimed. Queries compared graph-incidence, mixed-difference, partition-function and type-enumeration aliases and did not identify the all-order law. This is not an independence proof; the possibility of an implicit corollary or overlooked related identity remains the main residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The new invariant is not an arbitrary selected graph statistic: it records complete one-point orbits over the canonical vertex-and-edge face poset of any graph. A natural question is whether graph-cell attachments exhibit diminishing or reinforcing marginal type growth. The theorem resolves not only the pairwise sign but the entire hierarchy of finite differences, in a way that contrasts sharply with edge-as-order-comparability modifications. Its positive support decomposition is an exact tool for extremal and incremental comparisons for every finite graph, not a single small numerical table or isolated normalization. The graph-polynomial identity is a necessary bridge, not offered alone as mathematical value.
