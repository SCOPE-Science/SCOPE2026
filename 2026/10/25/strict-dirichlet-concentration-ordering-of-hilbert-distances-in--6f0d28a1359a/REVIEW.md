# Review of the strict Dirichlet Hilbert-distance concentration theorem

## Correctness
PASS. The intrinsic-to-projective simplex formula is explicitly normalized as the full log cross-ratio. Independent gamma ratios give the generalized-logistic coordinate law; the Euler gamma product supplies a convergent independent Bernoulli–Laplace decomposition with the correct characteristic function. The range sublevel set is convex and centrally symmetric. Prékopa's theorem gives non-strict comparison and the analyticity-plus-escape argument supplies strictness for every finite positive threshold. Boundary concentration limits are separately established. `check.py` and `check.log` check the CDF, stochastic order, and characteristic identities on finite test cases, not the universal theorem.

## Originality
PASS under currently accessible bibliographic evidence. The 2004 Foertsch–Karlsson simplex Hilbert metric framework and Nielsen–Sun's coordinate-distance formula are previously known; neither inspected material contains the concentration-parameter comparison. Gupta–Shah's 1965 ordinary-logistic range results cover the \(\alpha=1\) baseline and are not asserted as new. Anderson's 1955 symmetric-convex-set comparison is also classical. The claimed new combination is an explicit infinitely factored convolution ordering for the symmetric generalized-logistic family together with its strict full-threshold Hilbert-distance consequence. Searches of published published-finding corpus records and primary-literature aliases found no statement that dominates this concentration-family theorem; broader generalized-logistic range-order literature is a genuine residual risk. The Gupta–Shah and Anderson original texts were inaccessible in full during this review, and this is not evidence of their noncoverage; the accessible titles/abstracts support only the limited comparisons described.

## Mathematical value
PASS. Concentration is the canonical parameter governing Dirichlet mass near the simplex center, but Hilbert distance is nonlinear and unbounded near facets. The theorem supplies a simultaneous all-radius, all-dimensional stochastic order, rather than an isolated moment inequality, plus an explicit coupling that can be applied to any symmetric convex log-ratio event. It is meaningful even for fixed dimension, while its universal small-/large-concentration limits and recovery of the classical uniform-volume case give boundary normalization.

## Limits and risks
The proof does not address nonsymmetric Dirichlet parameters or invariant Hilbert volume. The classical generalized-logistic range literature may contain a stronger ordering not visible in the accessible searches. Independent external audit has not been performed; numerical checks are not a substitute.
