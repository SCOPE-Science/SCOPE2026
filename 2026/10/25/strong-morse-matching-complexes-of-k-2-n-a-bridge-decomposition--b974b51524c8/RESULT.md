# Strong Morse matching complexes of \(K_{2,n}\): a bridge decomposition and equivariant homology

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge3\), let \(G=K_{2,n}\) be the complete bipartite graph viewed as a one-dimensional simplicial complex. Form the **full** strong discrete Morse matching complex \(X_n=\mathcal{SM}(G)\) defined from all total orders of its vertices. Then

\[
|X_n|\simeq\bigvee^{3n-1}S^1.
\]

There is also a natural integral equivariant refinement. When \(S_n\) permutes the \(n\) vertices in the large part, its action on the only nonzero positive-degree homology is

\[
H_1(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong
\ker\!\left(\epsilon:(\mathbb Z^n)^{\oplus3}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\right),
\qquad \epsilon((c_{tj}))=\sum_{t=1}^{3}\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{tj},
\]

with \(S_n\) permuting the coordinate \(j\) simultaneously in the three summands. The rational character satisfies

\[
\chi_{H_1}(\sigma)=3\,\#\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma)-1
\qquad(\sigma\in S_n).
\]

The structural facet classification below also gives the full face vector. For each \(1\le r\le n\), the number of faces with \(r\) vertices is

\[
f_{r-1}=2\left[\binom nr+n\binom{n-1}{r-1}\right]
   +3n\,\mathbf1_{r=2}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The simplicial complex \(G\) is the graph \(K_{2,n}\), **not** its clique complex in any higher-dimensional interpretation (there are no graph triangles). Fix its bipartition \(\{a,b\}\sqcup\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}\). A strong matching is the acyclic matching produced by a total order on the graph vertices using descending domination. In a graph, a vertex has a descending link that is a simplicial cone precisely when it has **exactly one** earlier neighbor. It is then paired with its incident edge to that neighbor. The simplicial complex \(\mathcal{SM}(G)\) is generated by all such matching sets as the order varies; its faces are subsets of those sets, as in Knudson and Owens-White.

Write \(v_j^a\) for the matching pair \((\{x_j\},\{a,x_j\})\), and \(w_j^a\) for \((\{a\},\{a,x_j\})\), and analogously with \(b\). These are \(4n\) distinct vertices of \(X_n\). This statement is for \(n\ge3\); \(n=2\) recovers the already studied four-cycle case and is not asserted to be new.

## Proof

**1. Complete description of the facets.** Define two subcomplexes \(D_a,D_b\), with vertex sets respectively \(\{v_j^a,w_j^a:1\le j\le n\}\) and \(\{v_j^b,w_j^b:1\le j\le n\}\). For \(i\in\{a,b\}\), give \(D_i\) the following \(n+1\) facets:

\[
F_i^0=\{v_1^i,\ldots,v_n^i\},\qquad
F_i^j=\{w_j^i\}\cup\{v_k^i:k\ne j\}\quad(1\le j\le n).
\]

The remaining maximal faces of \(X_n\) are precisely the \(3n\) edges

\[
E_j^{(1)}=\{w_j^a,w_j^b\},\quad
E_j^{(2)}=\{v_j^a,w_j^b\},\quad
E_j^{(3)}=\{w_j^a,v_j^b\}
\qquad (1\le j\le n).
\]

To establish exhaustiveness, take an arbitrary vertex order and suppose \(a\) precedes \(b\), the other case being symmetric. A vertex \(x_j\) is paired if and only if it occurs strictly between \(a\) and \(b\), and then its matching vertex is \(v_j^a\). The vertex \(a\) is paired if and only if exactly one \(x_j\) precedes it, yielding \(w_j^a\). The vertex \(b\) is paired if and only if exactly one \(x_j\) precedes it.

If no \(x_j\) precedes \(a\), then either the matching belongs to \(F_a^0\), or exactly one \(x_j\) lies between \(a\) and \(b\), giving \(E_j^{(2)}\). If exactly one \(x_j\) precedes \(a\), the matching belongs to \(F_a^j\), except when no \(x_k\) lies between \(a\) and \(b\), in which case it is \(E_j^{(1)}\). If at least two \(x_j\)'s precede \(a\), neither \(a\) nor \(b\) can be paired, so the matching belongs to \(F_a^0\). The symmetric case gives \(F_b^j\) and \(E_j^{(3)}\). Thus no additional faces occur.

Conversely, all stated facets are realized by the total orders \(a,x_1,\ldots,x_n,b\) for \(F_a^0\); \(x_j,a,(x_k)_{k\ne j},b\) for \(F_a^j\); \(x_j,a,b,(x_k)_{k\ne j}\) for \(E_j^{(1)}\); and \(a,x_j,b,(x_k)_{k\ne j}\) for \(E_j^{(2)}\). Swapping \(a,b\) realizes the \(b\)-facets and \(E_j^{(3)}\). Here an arbitrary fixed order is used within parenthesized groups. The assumption \(n\ge3\) ensures that a vertex with all \(n\) earlier neighbors is not paired. All facets are maximal because the displayed mixed edges involve both vertex-disjoint groups and no mixed higher-dimensional face occurs.

**2. The two large pieces are contractible.** The subcomplex \(D_i\) contains a central \((n-1)\)-simplex on the \(v_j^i\), with \(n\) additional \((n-1)\)-simplices, each attached along the ridge omitting \(v_j^i\). Each additional simplex has a unique extra vertex \(w_j^i\). Define an affine map on every simplex by fixing all \(v_k^i\) and mapping \(w_j^i\) to the barycenter of the ridge \(\{v_k^i:k\ne j\}\). It is continuous and agrees on overlaps. On each extra simplex, the straight-line homotopy between the identity and this map stays in that simplex, and it fixes the central simplex. Hence \(D_i\) strongly deformation retracts onto its central simplex. This homotopy is also \(S_n\)-equivariant.

**3. Homotopy type.** The two \(D_i\)'s are disjoint, and the only remaining faces are the \(3n\) distinct edges joining them. Collapsing each contractible subcomplex to a point is a homotopy equivalence of CW complexes (the subcomplex inclusions are cofibrations). The quotient is a connected multigraph on two vertices with \(3n\) parallel edges. Its geometric realization is a wedge of \(3n-1\) circles. This proves the homotopy classification **integrally**, not just over a field.

**4. Symmetry representation.** Let \(D=D_a\sqcup D_b\). In the exact sequence for the pair \((X_n,D)\), the relative degree-one cellular chain group is freely generated by the \(3n\) mixed edges, while \(H_1(D;\mathbb Z)=0\). The connecting map

\[
H_1(X_n,D;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{3n}
\longrightarrow H_0(D;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2
\]

sends every bridge, oriented from \(D_a\) to \(D_b\), to the difference of the two component generators. Exactness therefore identifies \(H_1(X_n;\mathbb Z)\) with the coefficient-sum-zero sublattice. Permuting the \(x_j\) preserves each of the three bridge types and merely permutes their labels, so this identification is integral and \(S_n\)-equivariant. Taking rational traces in the resulting short exact sequence gives the stated character.

**5. Face enumeration.** In each \(D_i\), an \(r\)-vertex face either uses only central vertices, in \(\binom nr\) ways, or uses one of the \(n\) unique outer vertices and \(r-1\) of the other \(n-1\) central vertices, in \(n\binom{n-1}{r-1}\) ways. Both copies contribute twice this sum, and the only additional faces are the \(3n\) mixed edges. This yields the face-vector formula for every \(1\le r\le n\).

## Verification

The accompanying standalone script `verify.py` independently generates strong matching sets from **every** vertex order for \(2\le n\le7\), rather than assuming the facet classification. It compares all generated simplices against the symbolic model, verifies the exact face vector, and computes the reduced mod-two Betti numbers from boundary matrices. All tests passed, with nonzero reduced Betti number \(3n-1\) only in degree one. These finite checks corroborate the explicit all-parameter proof; they do not establish it independently.

## Relationship to prior work

Knudson and Owens-White introduced this filtration-generated matching complex and computed homotopy types for paths, stars, simplices, simplex boundaries, and the pure cycle complex, together with finite-range homology for full cycle complexes. The four-cycle \(K_{2,2}\) is therefore a previously investigated endpoint, not new here. The present result instead classifies every \(K_{2,n}\) for \(n\ge3\), including its facets, all integral homotopy groups determined by the wedge type, and a natural equivariant homology module.

Results for the **ordinary** Chari–Joswig discrete-Morse matching complex of the same graph do not imply the answer for this strictly different filtration-generated subcomplex. In particular, nonequivariant information on the larger ordinary matching complex does not determine the subcomplex's equivariant integral first homology.

## Limitations

The theorem treats the one-dimensional complete bipartite graph with one part of size two and \(n\ge3\); it does not classify larger left bipartition sizes or arbitrary graph complexes. The equivariant statement uses the natural \(S_n\) subgroup fixing the two left vertices; no full automorphism-group representation is asserted. Literature coverage is not exhaustive, and later sections of the originating 2026 preprint could not be fully re-opened during the comparison, so an unlocated equivalent presentation remains a residual originality risk. The proof itself is a finite combinatorial all-parameter argument and is independent of those accessibility limits.
