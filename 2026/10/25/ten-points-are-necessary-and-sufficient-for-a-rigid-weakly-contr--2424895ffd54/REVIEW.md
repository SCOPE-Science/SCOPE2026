# Review: Ten-point rigidity threshold

## Correctness

**PASS.** The source theorem exhausts all ten-point beat-point-free weakly contractible noncontractible models, and their cover incidences uniquely determine order automorphisms. The layer-preserving computation yields \(C_2,S_3,C_2\times C_2,C_2\times C_2,C_2,C_2,1\) for Types I--VII. In Types IV--VII, the colored omission-graph principle gives a structural proof: the graphs are a four-cycle with two isolated vertices, a five-vertex path with an isolated maximum, a five-vertex path with an isolated minimum, and a disjoint four-vertex plus two-vertex path, respectively. The latter has no nontrivial partition-preserving symmetry and exactly one partition-reversing involution. The original 2016 nine-point classification plus the 2024 paper's recorded nontrivial symmetry of the classical Rival example excludes all smaller cardinalities. Permutation enumeration using the actual published incidence lists and an independently coded cover-graph isomorphism check agree on each of the seven cases.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to the stated residual literature risk.** The main 2026 classification provides the incidence models and self-duality, not the automorphism-group census or the smallest size for a rigid example. The 2016 paper handles the nine-point size threshold, while the 2024 group-realization paper notes rigidity constructions in larger spaces and gives the classical nine-point group's nontrivial symmetry. Neither its general existence theorem nor the distinct known nine-point topology claims implies the exact group census for the ten classified models without the new incidence-graph evaluation. Searches for automorphism groups, rigidity, order reversal, aliases and ten-point classification found no directly covering statement in the queried scholarly sources or published finding database. This is not a claim that search proves novelty.

## Value

**PASS.** This closes a natural least-cardinality problem, distinguishes the smallest possible rigid finite model from the nine-point nonrigid ones, and establishes the unique rigid **self-dual** core at that cardinality. The omission-graph argument supplies a reusable mechanism for studying finite three-level posets; the invariant is topological homeomorphism symmetry, not an arbitrary numerical sample. Weak contractibility alone does not determine this symmetry group. The cardinality-ten completeness follows from existing classification; only the additional symmetry refinement is asserted here.
