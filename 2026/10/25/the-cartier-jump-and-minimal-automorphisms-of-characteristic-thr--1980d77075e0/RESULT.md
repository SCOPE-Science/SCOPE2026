# The Cartier jump and minimal automorphisms of characteristic-three genus-five Artin--Schreier curves

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(3\), and let \(X/k\) be any smooth projective Artin--Schreier curve of genus \(5\) and \(3\)-rank \(2\). Then the full geometric automorphism group is \(\operatorname{Aut}_k(X)\cong C_3\). The \(a\)-number of its Jacobian is either \(2\) or \(3\), and both values occur. More precisely, the unique cyclic degree-three rational subfield permits a reduced model
\[
 y^3-y=A x^4+B x^2+C x+D x^{-1},\qquad A,D\in k^\times,\quad B,C\in k.
\]
Its \(a\)-number is
\[
 a(X)=\begin{cases}3,&B=0,\\2,&B\ne 0.\end{cases}
\]
Thus \(B=0\) is exactly the jump hypersurface in this normalized family. The stable Cartier rank is \(2\), while the first Cartier rank is \(2\) or \(3\) respectively. The nilpotent Cartier module has respectively three blocks of length one or blocks of lengths two and one. The coefficient-vanishing condition is independent of the reduced coordinates, since it is equivalent to the intrinsic \(a\)-number value \(3\).

## Assumptions and scope
The term Artin--Schreier curve means a smooth, projective, geometrically integral curve with a faithful \(C_3\)-action whose quotient is \(\mathbb P^1_k\). Its \(3\)-rank is that of its Jacobian and its \(a\)-number is \(\dim_k\ker(\mathcal C)\), where \(\mathcal C\) is the Cartier operator on global regular one-forms. The automorphism conclusion concerns the full automorphism group of the smooth projective curve, not only the covering deck group. The field is algebraically closed; concrete examples below descend to \(\mathbb F_3\).

The Deuring--Shafarevich formula forces exactly two branch poles when the \(3\)-rank is \(2\). The Riemann--Hurwitz formula then forces their positive, prime-to-three lower jumps to sum to \(5\); the only possibility is \(\{1,4\}\). Move the distinguished jump-\(1\) and jump-\(4\) branch points to \(0\) and \(\infty\). Artin--Schreier reduction removes powers divisible by three, and an additive constant is absorbed by a translation of \(y\). This gives the displayed model with nonzero extreme coefficients. Conversely every such reduced model defines a smooth projective curve with genus \(5\) and \(3\)-rank \(2\).

## Proof
**Regular differentials.** Let \(P_0,P_\infty\) be the totally ramified points above \(0,\infty\). Their different exponents are \(4\) and \(10\). The orders of \(x,y,dx\) at \(P_0\) are respectively \(3,-1,4\); at \(P_\infty\) they are \(-3,-4,4\). Consequently a monomial \(x^i y^j dx\), \(0\le j\le2\), is regular at both poles precisely when
\[
3i-j+4\ge0,\qquad -3i-4j+4\ge0.
\]
The five solutions are \((i,j)=(-1,0),(0,0),(1,0),(-1,1),(0,1)\). There are no other poles, and the five monomials are linearly independent over \(k\), by the degree-three field extension. Since the genus is five, they form a basis
\[
e_1=dx/x,\qquad e_2=dx,\qquad e_3=x\,dx,\qquad e_4=y\,dx/x,\qquad e_5=y\,dx.
\]

**Cartier calculation.** In characteristic three, \(\mathcal C(x^i dx)=0\) unless \(i\equiv2\pmod3\); in the latter case it is \(x^{(i-2)/3}dx\). The identities \(y=y^3-f(x)\) and \(\mathcal C(g^3\omega)=g\mathcal C(\omega)\) yield, writing unique cube roots in \(k\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathcal C(e_1)=e_1,\quad \mathcal C(e_2)=\mathcal C(e_3)=0,\quad \mathcal C(e_4)=e_4,\\
&\mathcal C(e_5)=-D^{1/3}e_1-B^{1/3}e_2.
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular the image is the span of \(e_1,e_4\), enlarged by \(e_2\) exactly when \(B\ne0\). Hence the first Cartier rank is \(2\) when \(B=0\), and \(3\) otherwise, proving the \(a\)-number classification. The basis vector \(v=e_5+D e_1\) satisfies \(\mathcal C(v)=-B^{1/3}e_2\) and \(\mathcal C^2(v)=0\). Thus the nilpotent Cartier part is generated by \(e_2,e_3,v\) with the asserted block lengths, while \(\langle e_1,e_4\rangle\) is the bijective part. This also gives rank \(\mathcal C^n=2\) for every \(n\ge2\).

**Full automorphisms.** A theorem of Valentini--Madan on uniqueness of the rational subfield of degree \(3\), in the form of Proposition 2.2 and Lemma 2.6 of Tafazolian--Kazemifard, applies: genus \(5\) and \(3\)-rank \(2\) are outside all their exceptional pairs. Hence every automorphism preserves \(k(x)\). Its induced base transformation preserves the two unequal lower jumps, so must be \(x\mapsto\lambda x\). Artin--Schreier generator uniqueness gives \(y\mapsto u y+h(x)\), with \(u\in\mathbb F_3^\times\), and
\[
f(\lambda x)-u f(x)=h(x)^3-h(x).
\]
At a pole of \(h\), the right side has pole order divisible by three. At \(0\), the left side has at most a simple pole; at \(\infty\), its possible pole orders are \(4,2,1\). As none is divisible by three, all poles of the left side must vanish. Comparing coefficients of \(x^{-1}\) and \(x^4\) gives \(\lambda^{-1}=u=\lambda^4\). Since \(u\in\{1,-1\}\), necessarily \(\lambda=u\), while \(\lambda^4=1\); consequently \(u=\lambda=1\). Any remaining \(h\) is constant and satisfies \(h^3-h=0\), so \(h\in\mathbb F_3\). Thus precisely the three deck translations survive, proving the full-automorphism claim.

**Both loci occur over the prime field.** For \(b=0,1\), take the smooth projective model of
\[
y^3-y=x^4+b x^2+x+x^{-1}\qquad\text{over }\mathbb F_3.
\]
Both curves have genus \(5\), \(3\)-rank \(2\), and full geometric automorphism group \(C_3\); their \(a\)-numbers are respectively \(3\) and \(2\).

## Verification
The accompanying standard-library checker `check_cartier.py` enumerates the five regular monomials from both valuation inequalities and recomputes the Cartier matrix independently by the Laurent-monomial rule over \(\mathbb F_3\). It checks all \(36\) coefficient choices with \(A,D\in\mathbb F_3^\times\) and \(B,C\in\mathbb F_3\), reproducing the rank dichotomy, and checks the extreme-coefficient scaling obstruction. These are finite arithmetic cross-checks, **not** proofs for arbitrary algebraically closed \(k\); the general conclusions follow from the preceding identities and uniqueness theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Tafazolian--Kazemifard (2026), Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 4.2, establish existence of minimally symmetric Artin--Schreier curves in all admissible genus/\(p\)-rank pairs, and prove the descent and branch-jump machinery used here. Their Question 7.5 asks for extra control of \(a\)-numbers or Newton polygons at fixed \(p\)-rank. The present theorem classifies **every** genus-five, rank-two curve in characteristic three, including the complete coefficient locus for both \(a\)-numbers and the absence of any extra automorphisms, rather than only an example of existence.

Farnell--Pries (2012/2013), Theorem 1.1, gives a constant-\(a\) formula when every lower jump divides \(p-1\); the jump \(4\) here does not divide \(2\), so that theorem does not imply the result. Booher--Cais (2020), Theorem 1.1, gives bounds \(2\le a\le3\) for jumps \(1,4\) in characteristic three, but does not decide the coefficient divisor. Pries--Zhu classify \(p\)-rank strata and their dimensions, not this Cartier-rank jump. The calculation uses standard Cartier theory; no novelty is claimed for those general ingredients.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns the Artin--Schreier locus in characteristic three and a single natural genus/\(p\)-rank stratum. It does not classify arbitrary non-Artin--Schreier genus-five curves, the full Ekedahl--Oort type, or the Newton polygon; the explicit Cartier-module description is only for global one-forms. A complete historical-priority audit, including implicit consequences in older literature on ramification-restricted covers, has not been independently performed.
