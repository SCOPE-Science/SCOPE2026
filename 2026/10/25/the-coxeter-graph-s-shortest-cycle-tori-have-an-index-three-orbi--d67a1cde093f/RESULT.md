# The Coxeter graph's shortest-cycle tori have an index-three orbit obstruction

## Finding
Let \(G\) be the 28-vertex Coxeter graph and let \(X=\operatorname{UConf}_2(|G|)\), the space of unordered, collision-free pairs of points on its geometric realization. Then
\[
H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{77}.
\]
There are precisely 24 simple shortest cycles, each of length seven, and 108 unordered vertex-disjoint pairs of these cycles. Every pair determines an embedded product torus in \(X\), whose oriented fundamental class we consider in \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\). The 108 classes integrally generate the whole group, and an explicit choice of 77 is an integral basis.

The full automorphism group of \(G\), of order 336, acts on the disjoint heptagon pairs with exactly two orbits. One consists of 84 pairs with exactly two graph edges connecting the two cycles. The other has 24 pairs with no such connecting edges. Denote the integral sublattices generated by the respective torus classes by \(L_2\) and \(L_0\). Then
\[
\operatorname{rank}L_2=77,\qquad H_2(X;\mathbb Z)/L_2\cong\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z,
\]
\[
\operatorname{rank}L_0=23,\qquad L_2+L_0=H_2(X;\mathbb Z).
\]
More sharply, adding **any one** torus class from the 24-element orbit to \(L_2\) generates all of \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\).

## Assumptions and scope
Represent the seven points of the Fano plane by the nonzero elements \(1,\ldots,7\) of \(\mathbb F_2^3\), using bitwise exclusive-or for vector addition. A Fano line is a triple \(\{a,b,a\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}b\}\). The vertices of \(G\) are the 28 three-element subsets that are not Fano lines; two vertices are adjacent exactly when their subsets are disjoint. This convention completely fixes the graph. Its 28 vertices and 42 edges are sorted lexicographically, with the vertex indices starting at zero.

A simple cycle is canonically represented by starting at its least vertex and choosing the traversal whose second vertex is smaller than its last. The 24 heptagons are sorted lexicographically. An unordered heptagon pair consists of two vertex-disjoint heptagons and is given the lexicographic pair order; the 108 pairs are likewise ordered lexicographically. The chosen torus orientations come from these directed cycles. Reversing an orientation changes only the sign of a generator, not any lattice asserted here.

The configuration space means the ordinary unordered topological configuration space with no permitted collisions, not a sink-configuration space or an ordered space. No coefficient field other than the explicitly indicated one is implicit in the integral claims.

## Proof
**1. Combinatorial census.** Enumerating the 35 triples of seven Fano points and removing the seven lines gives 28 vertices. Adjacency by disjointness gives 42 edges, every vertex of degree three. Depth-first simple-cycle enumeration with least-vertex and orientation normalization finds no cycle shorter than seven and exactly 24 length-seven cycles. The vertex-disjoint test on the \(\binom{24}{2}\) unordered pairs yields 108 products. Exhaustive graph isomorphism self-maps give 336 automorphisms. Acting on the canonical 108 pairs gives orbits of sizes 84 and 24, with 2 and 0 cross-edges respectively. The code `verify.py` makes each enumeration deterministic and exhaustive.

**2. Cubical complex and boundaries.** For two particles the unordered cubical deleted-product subcomplex has cells given by unordered pairs of disjoint closed cells of the graph. This cubical complex deformation retracts onto \(X\) under the usual sufficient-subdivision conditions for two particles; they hold here because the graph is simple, every relevant vertex-to-vertex path has at least one edge, and every cycle has more than two edges. Its cell ranks are
\[
(c_0,c_1,c_2)=(378,1092,777).
\]
Order the edges by increasing endpoints. For distinct disjoint edges \(e_i=[a,b]\) and \(e_j=[c,d]\), with \(i<j\), orient their square by the ordered edge factors. Its cellular boundary is
\[
\partial_2[e_i,e_j]=[e_j,b]-[e_j,a]-[e_i,d]+[e_i,c],
\]
where \([e,v]\) is the cell consisting of one moving particle in \(e\) and one at vertex \(v\). The program constructs \(\partial_1\) as well and checks \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\) integrally for all 777 squares. Every signed product torus from two disjoint heptagons is checked to have zero integral boundary.

**3. Exact integral homology basis.** Sparse Gaussian elimination modulo 101 establishes \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_{101}}\partial_2=700\). Thus \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\partial_2\geq700\) and \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\ker\partial_2\leq77\). Independently, the verifier selects 77 of the 108 integral heptagon-pair cycles and 77 square coordinates for which the corresponding integer minor has determinant \(-1\). Consequently, the 77 classes are independent and span a primitive rank-77 sublattice of the entire free two-chain group. As this sublattice is contained in \(\ker\partial_2\), and both have rank 77, equality follows: a finite-index subgroup that is primitive in the ambient free group cannot be proper in its kernel. There are no three-cells, so \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)=\ker\partial_2\cong\mathbb Z^{77}\). This also proves integral generation by all 108 torus cycles. The verifier prints the exact row and column indices for the unimodular minor.

**4. The three-primary orbit obstruction.** Let \(L_2\) be generated by the 84 pairs with exactly two cross-edges. In the integral basis constructed in Step 3, these 84 cycles give a \(77\times84\) integer matrix. Its rank modulo 3 is exactly 76, so the full-rank lattice \(L_2\) has index divisible by 3. Two explicitly and deterministically selected maximal minors of this matrix have signed determinants \(-273\) and \(5427\), whose absolute greatest common divisor equals 3. The index must divide every maximal minor, hence divides 3. It is therefore exactly 3. An independent Smith-normal-form computation obtains 76 unit factors and one factor 3. The other orbit has rational rank 23 (its integral coordinate matrix has 23 unit Smith factors).

**5. One bridge-free torus suffices.** Since all 108 classes generate, at least one class from the 24-element orbit maps nontrivially to \(H_2/L_2\cong\mathbb Z/3\). The automorphism group acts transitively on those 24 pairs and preserves \(L_2\). Its induced action on the cyclic group of order three is by units. Thus every orbit member has nonzero quotient image, hence every single one generates that quotient. This proves the final assertion.

## Verification
Run `python verify.py` with Python 3.11 or later and the declared dependencies. The script independently constructs the graph from Fano triples, enumerates shortest cycles and all 336 automorphisms, constructs both signed cellular boundaries and all product tori, checks exact integral cycle identities, verifies finite-field ranks, calculates the unimodular 77-class certificate, computes the two index-three minors and their gcd, and cross-checks both orbit lattices by integer Smith normal form. The deterministically generated indices printed at the end are part of the reproducibility specification. The result concerns these exact finite complexes; no finite sampling is used to infer a universal theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Barnett and Farber (arXiv:0903.2180) introduce the graph intersection formalism and supply an explicit planar-graph torus description for **ordered** two-point configurations. Farber and Hanbury (arXiv:1005.2300) study the ordered intersection form, mature graphs, and broad families such as complete and complete-bipartite graphs; their general Betti-number statements do not determine shortest-cycle integral sublattices for this Coxeter graph. An and Knudsen (arXiv:2008.10371), Theorem 1.1, give planar graph-braid second-homology generation and exhibit nonplanar limitations in Example 6.8. The Coxeter graph is nonplanar, and neither the planar theorem nor that example implies the present integral orbit-index computation. Calculations of the Coxeter graph's independence complex study a different topological construction.

## Limitations
This is a self-reviewed, computationally certified finite-graph assertion, not an independently audited publication. The result makes no assertion about \(H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\), multi-particle spaces, or other cubic girth-seven graphs. Nonappearance in inspected sources is not a proof of global novelty. Barnett's 2010 doctoral thesis was located but its full text could not be retrieved during this comparison; its abstract addresses ordered two-particle spaces and planar torus generation. That access gap and unindexed specialized literature remain bibliographic risks.
