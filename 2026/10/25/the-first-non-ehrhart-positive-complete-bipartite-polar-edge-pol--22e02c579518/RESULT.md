# The first non-Ehrhart-positive complete-bipartite polar edge polytope

## Finding
For integers \(p,q\geq1\), let \(Q_{p,q}\) be the polar of the symmetric edge polytope of the complete bipartite graph \(K_{p,q}\), taken in the dual of the graph's root lattice. Its dimension is \(d=p+q-1\). Write its Ehrhart polynomial as
\[
L_{p,q}(n)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}c_j(p,q)n^j.
\]
Every coefficient is strictly positive whenever \(d\leq12\). In dimension \(d=13\), there are seven unordered part-size classes \(1\leq p\leq q\), \(p+q=14\); precisely one class, \((p,q)=(7,7)\), has a nonpositive coefficient. Its unique negative coefficient is
\[
\boxed{c_1(7,7)=-\frac{7184}{715}},
\]
and all its other coefficients are strictly positive. Thus dimension thirteen is the sharp first dimension for ordinary Ehrhart nonpositivity **within this family**, with a unique first-dimensional obstruction up to exchange of the two parts.

For every \(p,q\geq1\) and integer \(n\geq0\), the complete lattice-point count is
\[
\boxed{L_{p,q}(n)=(2n+1)^q+\sum_{r=1}^{2n}\bigl((r+1)^p-2r^p+(r-1)^p\bigr)(2n+1-r)^q.}
\]
In particular, the normalized lattice volume is \(d!\operatorname{vol}(Q_{p,q})=2^d p!q!\). The formula is symmetric in \(p,q\), although its displayed derivation distinguishes the first part.

## Assumptions and scope
The standard root-lattice model of the symmetric edge polytope is \(P_G=\operatorname{conv}\{\pm(e_i-e_j):ij\in E(G)\}\) in the hyperplane of coordinate sum zero. Its polar is represented in the dual quotient by fixing one vertex potential equal to zero. For \(G=K_{p,q}\), write first-part potentials \(x_1=0,x_2,\ldots,x_p\), and second-part potentials \(y_1,\ldots,y_q\). Then
\[
Q_{p,q}=\{(x_2,\ldots,x_p,y_1,\ldots,y_q)\in\mathbb R^d:|x_i-y_j|\leq1\ \text{for all }i,j\}.
\]
All lattice coordinates are integers in this potential model. The graph is connected, so the polytope is bounded and full-dimensional. The term *Ehrhart positive* means that **all** \(d+1\) coefficients of the polynomial in the ordinary monomial basis \(1,n,\ldots,n^d\) are strictly positive. It does not mean positivity in the different magic basis.

## Proof
**Integrality and degree.** At a vertex of \(Q_{p,q}\), there are \(d\) linearly independent tight inequalities \(x_i-y_j=\pm1\). The graph of tight edges therefore contains a spanning tree: the rank of an oriented incidence system on a graph is the number of vertices minus the number of connected components. Propagating these integral differences along the tree from \(x_1=0\) makes every coordinate integral. Thus \(Q_{p,q}\) is a lattice polytope of dimension \(d\), and Ehrhart's theorem gives a polynomial of degree \(d\) that agrees with its lattice-point count for every integer \(n\geq0\).

**Counting by range.** Fix an integral first-part potential vector \(x\) with \(x_1=0\), and set \(r=\max_i x_i-\min_i x_i\). For the dilated inequalities \(|x_i-y_j|\leq n\), each \(y_j\) must lie in the integer interval
\[
[\max_i x_i-n,\ \min_i x_i+n],
\]
which has \(2n+1-r\) points if \(r\leq2n\), and none otherwise. The \(q\) second-part coordinates are independent given \(x\). There is one first-part vector of range zero. For range \(r\geq1\), count the possible locations of its minimum relative to \(x_1=0\). The two endpoint locations contribute \(2((r+1)^{p-1}-r^{p-1})\). The \(r-1\) interior locations contribute \((r-1)((r+1)^{p-1}-2r^{p-1}+(r-1)^{p-1})\). Their sum is
\[
N_p(r)=(r+1)^p-2r^p+(r-1)^p.
\]
Multiplying by the number of second-part completions and summing \(0\leq r\leq2n\) proves the boxed formula. For \(p=1\) all positive-range terms vanish, yielding the lattice cube count \((2n+1)^q\).

**Finite sharp classification.** For every dimension \(1\leq d\leq13\), part interchange reduces enumeration to exactly \(1\leq p\leq\lfloor(d+1)/2\rfloor\), \(q=d+1-p\). There are \(42\) such classes in dimensions \(1\) through \(12\), and seven more in dimension thirteen. For each class, evaluate the proved count at all \(d+1\) integers \(n=0,1,\ldots,d\). Set \(D_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^k(-1)^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}L_{p,q}(j)\). Exact Newton interpolation gives
\[
L_{p,q}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}D_k\binom{n}{k}.
\]
Expand \(\binom{n}{k}=\prod_{h=0}^{k-1}(n-h)/k!\) using rational arithmetic. The complete 49-row exact coefficient certificate is `coefficients.csv`, reproducibly regenerated by the included standard-library `verify.py`. Its exhaustive sign checks show that all 42 coefficients vectors in dimensions at most twelve are strictly positive. At dimension thirteen, the sole exceptional row is \((p,q)=(7,7)\); its coefficient vector, from constant term to top degree, is
\[
\left(1,-\frac{7184}{715},\frac{126}{11},\frac{26852}{99},896,\frac{28784}{15},\frac{9520}{3},4064,4032,\frac{27776}{9},1792,\frac{25088}{33},\frac{7168}{33},\frac{14336}{429}\right).
\]
The coefficient checks are finite *exhaustive verification of a finite statement*, not extrapolation from samples: all allowable dimension/part-size classes up to the claimed cutoff are traversed, and interpolation is justified independently by the lattice-polytope degree theorem.

**Leading coefficient.** For \(p\geq2\), \(N_p(r)=p(p-1)r^{p-2}+O(r^{p-3})\). The leading term of the range sum is therefore the beta integral \(p(p-1)\int_0^2t^{p-2}(2-t)^q\,dt=2^dp!q!/d!\), with \(d=p+q-1\). For \(p=1\) the cube formula gives the same value. The exact certificate checks this independently in all 49 cases.

## Relationship to prior work
Konoike, *A new class of magic positive Ehrhart polynomials of reflexive polytopes* (2024 preprint; 2025 proceedings), Example 4.3 and its table, studies the **different, stronger magic-basis positivity** of these same complete-bipartite polars. In particular the paper already shows magic-positivity failure for \(K_{3,7}\), whose ordinary Ehrhart coefficients are all positive. No claim is made to discovering the earlier magic-basis failures or their table. The present theorem determines the first failure and exact boundary classification **in the ordinary monomial basis**. Ohsugi and Tsuchiya (first posted October 5, 2026) prove positivity for other specified polar-polytope classes and resolve the cycle case, motivating the distinction. Earlier work of Higashitani, Jochemko and Michałek determines the Ehrhart \(h^*\)-polynomial of the *original symmetric edge polytope* for complete-bipartite graphs; the polarity distinction is essential. Generic low-dimensional counterexamples to Ehrhart positivity outside this family do not imply the class-specific threshold.

## Limitations
The sharp minimality assertion is only for polars of complete-bipartite symmetric edge polytopes, not all graph polars or all lattice polytopes. It settles dimensions through thirteen; it does not classify larger part sizes, all negative-coefficient patterns, or magic positivity. An independently established historical priority review has not been performed. The October 2026 paper's accessible abstract was reviewed, but its full text could not be examined; a differently formulated overlapping statement in uninspected literature remains a residual risk. The computational certificate supports a finite, completely enumerated claim and does not imply an infinite-dimensional bound.
