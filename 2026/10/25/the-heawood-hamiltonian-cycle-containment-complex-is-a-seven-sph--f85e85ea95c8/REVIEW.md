# Scientific review — Hamiltonian-cycle containment on the Heawood graph

**Correctness — PASS.** The standalone finite certificate constructs all twenty-four 14-edge Hamiltonian-cycle facets and independently checks that these are complements of the twenty-four perfect matchings. It generates all 252,902 nonempty faces, computes an explicit acyclic matching with 126,450 face–coface pairs, and proves that the remaining nonempty faces are a vertex and a seven-simplex. A topological sort traverses all 1,936,998 oriented face incidences with no directed cycle. Discrete Morse theory therefore proves the complete homotopy equivalence to a seven-sphere; the argument uses an exhaustive finite proof, not an inference from experimental homology ranks.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risks.** The exact 24 Hamiltonian cycles are known from Lawrence–Wilson (2019), and the 2-factor-Hamiltonian property of related graphs appears in earlier graph-theoretic literature. Neither fact implies the new face-poset homotopy type. The published Heawood perfect-matching complex is generated by seven-edge matching facets and has a wedge of sixteen two-spheres; the ordinary matching complex similarly has sixteen four-spheres. Their facet families differ from the complementary fourteen-edge Hamiltonian facets here. published-finding corpus alias/implication searches did not identify an exact or stronger covering theorem. The indexed matching-complex summaries and some older papers could not be read completely; an unindexed equivalent theorem is a residual risk.

**Value — PASS.** This is a complete topological classification of the natural complex of Hamiltonian-extendible edge sets on the canonical Fano-plane incidence graph. The pure thirteen-dimensional simplicial complex reduces to one sphere in dimension seven, a structural feature not provided by graph-theoretic Hamiltonian counts or existing matching-complex classifications. The deterministic acyclic matching is readily reusable as a finite benchmark.

No statement is made for other cubic or 2-factor-Hamiltonian graphs. The finite proof is internally replayable; independent certification remains outstanding.
