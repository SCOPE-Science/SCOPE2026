# The Herschel matching complex is a wedge of twelve 3-spheres

## Finding

Let \(H\) be the Herschel graph, the unique eleven-vertex, eighteen-edge non-Hamiltonian polyhedral graph, and let \(\mathcal M(H)\) be its **ordinary matching complex**. Its vertices are the eighteen edges of \(H\); a set of these vertices spans a simplex precisely when the corresponding graph edges have pairwise disjoint endpoints. Then

\[
\mathcal M(H)\simeq\bigvee^{12} S^3.
\]

In particular, \(\widetilde H_3(\mathcal M(H);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{12}\), all other reduced integral homology groups vanish, and the complex is simply connected. This is a homotopy classification, not just a homology computation.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are simple undirected graphs. Label the vertices of \(H\) by \(0,\ldots,10\), with bipartition \(\{0,1,2,3,4\}\sqcup\{5,6,7,8,9,10\}\). Enumerate its edges in the following fixed order (indexing begins at zero):

```
0:(0,5)   1:(0,6)   2:(0,9)   3:(0,10)
4:(1,5)   5:(1,6)   6:(1,7)   7:(1,8)
8:(2,7)   9:(2,8)  10:(2,9)  11:(2,10)
12:(3,5) 13:(3,7)  14:(3,9)
15:(4,6) 16:(4,8)  17:(4,10)
```

The graph is planar and three-vertex-connected, with eleven vertices and eighteen edges. The embedded rotation system in `verify.py` has nine facial four-cycles (Euler characteristic two), and exhaustive deletion of at most two vertices leaves it connected. Its odd order and bipartiteness imply non-Hamiltonicity. The known uniqueness of the eleven-vertex, eighteen-edge non-Hamiltonian polyhedral graph identifies this explicit graph with the Herschel graph. These properties are independently checked by the standalone verifier.

## Proof

**Enumerate the complex.** Encode each nonempty matching by the integer bit mask of its selected edges in the fixed indexing. Exhaustively recurse over all eighteen graph edges, including an edge only when neither endpoint has appeared earlier. The resulting \(751\) nonempty simplices have the complete \(f\)-vector

\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4)=(18,111,280,270,72).
\]

The verifier constructs the simplicial boundary over the integers using the increasing edge-index orientation and checks \(\partial^2=0\) for every column.

**Construct a discrete Morse matching.** Start with the nonempty face set. At each successive edge index

```
17, 14, 16, 13, 7, 12, 5, 3, 1, 10
```

pair every as-yet-unpaired face \(\sigma\) omitting that index with \(\sigma\cup\{e\}\), whenever the latter is also unpaired. Remove both faces from consideration before advancing to the next index. The stagewise pair counts are

\[
159,94,40,28,16,10,8,6,4,2,
\]

summing to \(367\). There are precisely seventeen critical simplices: one in dimension zero (bit mask `131072`), two in dimension two (masks `8328` and `73730`), and fourteen in dimension three (masks `4488`, `4680`, `6276`, `8728`, `10369`, `33044`, `33857`, `37380`, `38976`, `41473`, `43024`, `65812`, `66625`, `69890`). The full directed Hasse graph of the \(751\) nonempty faces has \(2502\) cover incidences. Directing matched incidences upwards and all others downwards yields a directed acyclic graph: the verifier exhaustively topologically sorts all \(751\) vertices. Thus Forman's discrete Morse theorem supplies a homotopy-equivalent CW complex with exactly these critical cells.

**Determine the attaching maps.** Perform integral cellular chain cancellations along all \(367\) matched pairs using their incidence coefficients \(\pm1\). At each pivot \((\sigma,\tau)\), eliminate the \(\sigma\)-coefficient of any same-dimensional column \(z\) by subtracting \(\langle\partial z,\sigma\rangle/\langle\partial\tau,\sigma\rangle\) times the \(\tau\)-column, then delete the paired rows and columns. No floating-point arithmetic or modular inference is used. The resulting Morse chain groups have ranks \(1\) in degree zero, \(2\) in degree two, and \(14\) in degree three, with no other generators. The reduced boundary in degree three has a two-by-two integral minor equal to \(+1\): with the critical dimension-two cells ordered by masks `8328,73730`, the critical dimension-three columns `4488,4680` are exactly

\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

There are no one-cells, so the Morse two-skeleton is \(S^2\vee S^2\). By the Hurewicz theorem, its second homotopy group is freely generated by the two sphere summands; hence each three-cell attaching map is completely determined by its two integral cellular degrees. The two selected three-cell attaching maps form a basis and attach along the two sphere generators, producing a contractible subcomplex. The remaining twelve three-cells then attach to a contractible two-connected subcomplex, so each attaches nullhomotopically. This proves the asserted wedge of twelve three-spheres.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` using Python 3 with no external dependencies. The accompanying `verify_output.txt` is the output of the standalone program. Its checks include graph connectivity and planarity certificates, exhaustive matching enumeration, all \(2502\) oriented face incidences, acyclicity of the complete Morse matching, a full integral boundary-squared test, all \(367\) integral pivot cancellations, the unimodular minor, and an independent direct mod-two boundary-rank computation giving

\[
(\operatorname{rank}\partial_1,\ldots,\operatorname{rank}\partial_4)=(17,94,186,72).
\]

The Euler characteristic is \(-11\), consistent with \(\bigvee^{12}S^3\). The exact chain cancellation, rather than the mod-two ranks alone, is what rules out integral torsion and, together with the CW attaching-map argument, establishes the homotopy type.

## Relationship to prior work

The graph is a classical extremal non-Hamiltonian polyhedron; its uniqueness and extremal status are summarized by MathWorld (Herschel Graph, citing Coxeter and later graph theory sources). Matching-complex topology and simplicial Morse methods are developed in the older graph-complex literature, including Linusson–Shareshian–Welker (2004) on the distinct *bounded-matching-number graph complex* and Engström (2005) on independence complexes of claw-free graphs. The ordinary matching complex here is the independence complex of the line graph of \(H\), not the independence complex of \(H\) itself.

Bayer–Jelić Milutinović–Vega (2024, full text, Theorem 2) prove wedge-of-spheres homotopy types for **outerplanar** graph matching complexes. The Herschel graph is three-connected, eleven-vertex, and nonouterplanar: indeed, it has no degree-two vertex, whereas every outerplanar graph on at least three vertices with no leaf contains a degree-two vertex. The cited theorem does not determine its matching-complex homotopy type. That article's discussion of nonouterplanar planar graph classes (and its question about torsion) underscores why a specific three-connected polyhedral example is a natural test case, while it does not provide the present exact rank or attaching-map calculation.

Searches for the Herschel matching complex, the independence complex of its line graph, and the twelve-sphere homotopy type did not reveal a covering theorem or existing table. Related exact results about the Herschel **independence complex**, the Goldner–Harary **matching complex**, and the Franklin-graph matching complex concern different objects and do not imply this claim. The coincidence of twelve three-spheres for the Franklin graph has no known implication for \(H\).

## Limitations

This is a theorem for one distinguished finite graph, not a characterization of all planar graphs. The discrete-Morse matching and integral cancellations are exhaustively verified finite certificates; there is no claim of a general infinite-family theorem. Published-index and web searches are not completeness proofs, and a result buried in an unindexed monograph or an uninspected graph table remains a residual novelty risk. No independent scientific audit has been performed.
