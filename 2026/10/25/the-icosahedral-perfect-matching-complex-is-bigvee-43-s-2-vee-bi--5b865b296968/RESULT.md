# The icosahedral perfect-matching complex is \(\bigvee^{43}S^2\vee\bigvee^{19}S^3\)

## Finding
Let \(I\) be the one-skeleton of the regular icosahedron. The *perfect-matching complex* \(\mathcal P(I)\) has the thirty edges of \(I\) as potential vertices; a nonempty edge set is a simplex if it extends to a perfect matching of \(I\). There is an integral homotopy equivalence
\[
\mathcal P(I)\simeq\left(\bigvee^{43}S^2\right)\vee\left(\bigvee^{19}S^3\right).
\]
In particular, its only nonzero reduced integral homology groups are \(\widetilde H_2\cong\mathbb Z^{43}\) and \(\widetilde H_3\cong\mathbb Z^{19}\).

The complex has all \(315\) possible two-edge matchings of \(I\) as edges, yet excludes \(140\) of its \(1400\) three-edge matchings. Thus it agrees with the ordinary matching complex through its one-skeleton, but differs in higher simplices. It is pure five-dimensional, despite the displayed mixed-dimensional homotopy type.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite and simple. Specify the icosahedral graph on vertices \(0,1,\ldots,11\): the poles are \(0,11\), upper ring vertices are \(t_i=1+i\), lower ring vertices are \(b_i=6+i\), for \(i\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z\). Its edges are
\[
\{0,t_i\},\quad\{11,b_i\},\quad\{t_i,t_{i+1}\},\quad\{b_i,b_{i+1}\},\quad\{t_i,b_i\},\quad\{t_i,b_{i-1}\},
\]
for each \(i\). This specifies a connected, five-regular, twelve-vertex, thirty-edge graph, isomorphic to the regular icosahedron's one-skeleton. Each perfect matching uses six disjoint edges. The conclusion concerns the geometric realization of the abstract simplicial complex generated by all perfect matchings, **not** the ordinary matching complex of all nonempty matchings, and not the perfect-matching polytope.

The archival literature starting point is Plummer's 1994 study of extending matchings in claw-free graphs; its first verified public date in the source metadata is 20 October 1994. Later literature supplies the perfect-matching-complex language and the discrete-Morse methodology; the newer dates are not substituted for the archival source date.

## Proof
**Enumeration.** Order the thirty graph edges lexicographically as pairs of integer endpoints. Recursively take the least unmatched graph vertex \(u\), pair it with every still unmatched neighbor \(v\), and continue on the other vertices. This lists each perfect matching exactly once. There are \(125\), confirmed separately by a subset-mask recurrence for the number of perfect matchings of a remaining vertex set. Generate each nonempty subset of each perfect matching and deduplicate. The resulting face counts \((f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4,f_5)\) are
\[
(30,315,1260,1575,750,125),
\]
so there are \(4055\) nonempty faces. An independent enumeration of all two-edge and three-edge matchings yields \(315\) and \(1400\), respectively; hence exactly \(140\) three-edge matchings are not extendable.

**Acyclic matching.** For graph-edge indices \(j=0,1,\ldots,29\), sequentially match every still-unmatched simplex \(\sigma\) not containing \(j\) to \(\sigma\cup\{j\}\), whenever both remain. Remove both faces after each stage. This is the element-pairing construction of acyclic discrete Morse theory. More strongly, the executable certificate directly orients every Hasse incidence downwards except matched incidences, oriented upwards, and topologically sorts the entire directed graph. All \(15210\) incidences on the \(4055\) faces participate, and every face is processed. Therefore there are no closed gradient paths.

This matching has exactly \(1977\) pairs and leaves one critical vertex, \(62\) critical two-simplices, and \(38\) critical three-simplices, with no other critical simplices. Forman's theorem gives a CW model with one zero-cell, \(62\) two-cells and \(38\) three-cells. It is simply connected, since there are no one-cells.

**Integral Morse boundary.** Use increasing edge-index orientation for every simplex. If a noncritical oriented \(p\)-simplex \(s\) is the lower partner of a paired \((p+1)\)-simplex \(h\), write \(\epsilon=[h:s]\in\{-1,1\}\), and recursively replace the formal generator \(s\) in its degree-\(p\) projection by
\[
-\epsilon\sum_{t\subset h,\,\dim t=p,\,t\ne s}[h:t]\,\operatorname{proj}(t).
\]
Critical \(p\)-cells project to themselves, and upper partners of lower-dimensional pairs project to zero. Acyclicity makes this finite recursion well defined. Apply it to each oriented boundary of the \(38\) critical three-cells. The resulting integral cellular boundary matrix \(B\) has shape \(38\times62\), \(240\) nonzero coefficients and maximum absolute coefficient \(2\).

An explicit exact-integer elimination in `verify.py` performs only row swaps, column swaps, sign changes, and additions of integer row or column multiples. It produces precisely \(19\) diagonal entries equal to \(1\) and a wholly zero remaining submatrix. Every operation is unimodular; thus \(B\) has integral Smith factors \(1\) repeated \(19\) times and otherwise zero.

**Homotopy identification.** The attaching maps of the three-cells have domain \(S^2\) and codomain a wedge of \(62\) copies of \(S^2\); they are classified by \(\pi_2(\bigvee^{62}S^2)\cong\mathbb Z^{62}\). Elementary unimodular changes of two-cell generators are realized by self-homotopy equivalences of that wedge, while unimodular changes of three-cell generators are realized by cellular homotopy moves. Every unit diagonal attaching degree cancels one two-cell with one three-cell. The remaining attaching maps are null-homotopic because the reduced integral boundary matrix is zero and the remaining two-sphere wedge has \(\pi_2\) freely generated by its summands. The resulting CW complex is a wedge of \(62-19=43\) two-spheres and \(38-19=19\) three-spheres, as claimed.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library Python file `verify.py` regenerates the graph, perfect matchings, faces, sequential Morse matching, full directed incidence graph, integral Morse boundary and exact integer pivot eliminations; its output is in `verification_output.txt`. It also checks the mod-two ranks of the original simplicial boundary maps in cardinalities two through six, obtaining \((29,286,931,625,125)\). These imply mod-two Betti numbers \(43\) in degree two and \(19\) in degree three, agreeing with the integral CW reduction. All assertions ran successfully on the packaged script. The enumeration is exhaustive for the precisely specified finite graph, not a heuristic sampling exercise.

## Relationship to prior work
Plummer's work on matching extension singles out strong two-edge extendability for claw-free planar graphs, motivating the first higher-dimensional obstruction to extendability on the icosahedron. Bayer–Jelić Milutinović–Vega determine perfect-matching complexes of certain honeycomb graphs; Chandrakar–Singh treat grids and polygonal line tilings. Neither graph family includes this triangular, five-regular icosahedral graph, nor do their statements imply its two- and three-dimensional homotopy groups.

An indexed exact-graph calculation describes the **ordinary** icosahedral matching complex, with three-primary torsion. It is a strictly larger complex: \(140\) three-edge matchings in that complex are absent from \(\mathcal P(I)\). A homotopy description of a complex does not determine the homotopy type of an arbitrary proper subcomplex. The present classification gives a free, torsionless mixed-dimensional wedge for the perfect-matching subcomplex. No covering perfect-matching-complex result was identified in the inspected literature and indexed searches; this negative search is not itself a uniqueness proof.

## Limitations
The claim is specific to the canonical twelve-vertex icosahedron, not to other planar matching-covered graphs or higher extendability complexes. It does not determine the induced map on homology from \(\mathcal P(I)\) into the ordinary matching complex or any equivariant representation. An older closely related primary article on claw-free matching extension was accessible only through metadata and abstract-level material, not verified in full text; an unindexed homotopy classification remains possible. The executable finite certificate and mathematical review are not independent external certification.
