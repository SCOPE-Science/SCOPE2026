# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The claimed homotopy type follows from an explicit acyclic discrete-Morse matching with one critical zero-cell and three six-cells, with no other critical cells. The graph and faces are fully defined; the standalone program independently enumerates all sixty-five thousand five hundred thirty-five nonempty vertex subsets using forbidden triples and induced connected-component traversal, confirms the complete matching and checks the acyclicity of every one of 35,104 oriented cover relations. A CW model with just one vertex and three six-cells is a wedge of three six-spheres integrally. Auxiliary boundary-matrix reductions agree over characteristic two. No assertion about an infinite family is made.

## Originality
PASS within inspected evidence, not an absolute priority certificate. The most pertinent new source, Sawant (arXiv:2605.25150v1, 2026-05-24), explicitly identifies three-path-free complexes with two-independence complexes but proves vertex decomposability for trees only; the Möbius–Kantor graph is a cubic graph with cycles. Deshpande–Singh (arXiv:2001.05448v2) and Deshpande–Shukla–Singh (arXiv:2001.06775v1) treat classes or bounds that do not fix this case. Public same-graph computations of ordinary independence homotopy and total three-cut homotopy concern different simplicial complexes, and neither statement determines the present invariant. Searches under generalized Petersen, Möbius–Kantor, two-independence and three-path-free aliases disclosed no covering calculation. The related Knill paper's abstract was accessible but its full text was not; a residual bibliographic risk remains.

## Mathematical value
PASS. This identifies a precise missing higher-independence invariant for the unique cubic symmetric sixteen-vertex graph, a natural incidence graph of an eight-point configuration. The first higher-independence step is structurally meaningful: compared with the separately determined mixed-dimensional ordinary independence homotopy, it yields three equidimensional six-spheres, and supplies a fully reproducible nonchordal benchmark beyond the tree and chordal classes of the recent literature. The claim is a complete integral homotopy classification of a natural finite test object, not an arbitrary slice or a mod-two-only table.

## Scope and residual risks
Finite exact result only: no general generalized Petersen classification, equivariant type, shellability or vertex decomposability. An unindexed previously equivalent calculation remains logically possible. The same-graph Knill preprint has only been inspected at abstract level; it appears to address a different topological graph construction, but it was not used to certify noncoverage from a snippet. The supplied code is essential reproducible finite evidence, not independent external validation.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
