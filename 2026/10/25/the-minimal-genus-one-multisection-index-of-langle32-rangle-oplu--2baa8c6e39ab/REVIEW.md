# Review

## Correctness

PASS. For an isotropic vector the norm equation forces all four \(D_4\)-coordinates to be divisible by four, by two successive reductions modulo eight. This makes every lattice pairing divisible by four. The explicit primitive isotropic vector \((1;4,4,0,0)\) has a pairing equal to \(-4\), so its divisibility is exactly four. The standard K3 fiber-class correspondence transfers this lattice computation to multisection index.

## Originality

PASS, with residual risk. The closest inspected source uses the same rank-five lattice to discuss automorphism groups and Mordell–Weil rank, but does not state the minimum multisection index. Targeted published-finding corpus searches returned no matching claim. General literature supplies the definition of multisection index, not this calculation. A missed older classification remains possible.

## Value

PASS. The lattice is singled out in recent K3 literature as an example with infinite automorphism group and zero maximal Mordell–Weil rank. Determining its minimum multisection index gives a concrete geometric invariant of that example and rules out fibrations with section, bisection, or trisection.

## Closest literature and limitations

The main comparison is Fujiwara–Oguiso–Yu, arXiv:2507.13726, Remark 1.5(4). Meinsma–Shinder (IMRN 2024, Section 3.1) provides the multisection-index convention. The claim does not classify all fibrations or their singular fibers.
