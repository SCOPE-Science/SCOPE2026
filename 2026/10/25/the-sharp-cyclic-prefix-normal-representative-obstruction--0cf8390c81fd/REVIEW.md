# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The direct binary-word enumeration evaluates the exact prefix-versus-factor inequalities on every rotation of every word through length eleven. The independent cyclic-gap composition enumeration has a proved bijection with nonzero binary necklaces and uses the independent shortest-consecutive-ones criterion. Both return exactly the same three failing classes at length eleven and none at shorter lengths. Explicit factor witnesses certify all 33 cuts of the three classes. The infinite obstruction family is a separate elementary argument based on the five possible one-starts and four fixed prefix/factor comparisons; the finite tests of that family are not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality

PASS against sources inspected, with a residual historical-priority risk. The 2014 generation and 2014 prefix-normal-form papers give linear enumeration and gap inequalities; the 2016 consolidated paper does not state a cyclic representative existence threshold or the first-exception census. Published mathematical-record searches for cyclic prefix-normal cuts, conjugate representative failure, and the explicit counterexample found no logically equivalent conclusion. A previously unindexed necklace census or independent derivation remains possible.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The phenomenon tests a natural limitation of prefix-normal canonicalization for circular strings: an origin cannot always be chosen to make the original circular word prefix normal. The result gives the sharp length threshold, classifies *all* first obstructions (three necklaces), and proves an obstruction exists at every greater length. These are exact structural boundaries for a standard combinatorial-word normality property, not a routine fixed-density coefficient or a mere isolated negative example.

## Scientific limits

Exact classification beyond the first failure length is not claimed. Only ones-prefix normality of non-wrapping linear factors after cuts is considered; the more stringent circular-factor normality, complementing, and reversing are distinct questions. Independent literature-priority validation remains open.
