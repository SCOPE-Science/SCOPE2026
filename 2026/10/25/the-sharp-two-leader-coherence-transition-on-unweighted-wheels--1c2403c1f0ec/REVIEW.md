# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The domain is every integer \(n\ge3\), unweighted \(K_1\vee C_n\), two perfectly pinned leaders, and unit isotropic follower noise. The exact objective is twice the steady-state total variance, \(\operatorname{tr}(L_{FF}^{-1})\). The electrical two-grounding identity is derived by minimizing a quadratic flow energy; the periodic tridiagonal Green kernel follows from its explicit second-order recurrence. Its diagonal and differentiated kernel give a closed formula for any pair of rim leaders. Strict convexity of \(t/(e^{ct}-1)\) and monotonicity of the Green difference prove the entire separation ranking. Exact rational comparisons cover \(n=3,\ldots,7\); an increasing uniform lower bound, already positive at \(n=8\), rules out every pair of rim leaders for all larger sizes. The hub--rim expression is an exact log-determinant derivative. A standalone exact-rational inverse program checks all orbit classes for \(3\le n\le11\). It does not serve as proof for larger \(n\).

## Originality — PASS (subject to documented risk)
The closest primary theory is Fitch–Leonard (arXiv:1407.1569), which supplies general joint-centrality principles and explicitly solves cycle/path leader placement. Patterson–McGlohon–Dyagilev (arXiv:1412.6595) supplies path/ring algorithms, rather than wheel hub-placement inequalities. Patterson–Yi–Zhang (arXiv:1708.06873) supplies resistance formulas and explicit cases for paths, cycles and certain trees. The wheel is not a cycle, path or tree, and has a degree-distinguished hub. None of the inspected specialized statements implies the sharp switch at seven rim vertices and strict classification of all maximizers without the additional nontrivial wheel Green-kernel comparisons. Published mathematical-record searches found no covering wheel theorem. A source could still encode the same result in less directly indexed terms; this is not a historical uniqueness certificate.

## Value — PASS
Two noise-free leader placement in noisy network consensus is a natural \(H_2\)-coherence optimization question, not an arbitrary computed invariant. The wheel is the smallest symmetric cycle-plus-hub test family where centrality versus coverage genuinely competes. The theorem exhibits a sharp qualitative design transition at a fixed graph size, gives all exact optimizer sets and their counts, and provides a closed formula for the full two-rim alternative family. This information cannot be read off a generic resistance identity without optimizing and certifying the strict inequalities. The finding is useful as an analytical benchmark for leader-selection algorithms.

## Closest literature and scientific limits
The model and joint-centrality framework are established in arXiv:1407.1569; ring and path algorithms in arXiv:1412.6595; general grounding resistance and certain topology-specific formulas in arXiv:1708.06873. Later results are compared for implications but not claimed as archival source dates. Unknown equivalent formulations or stronger specialized literature are possible. The theorem does not address different noise, finite-gain/noisy leaders, weighted wheels, or three or more leaders.
