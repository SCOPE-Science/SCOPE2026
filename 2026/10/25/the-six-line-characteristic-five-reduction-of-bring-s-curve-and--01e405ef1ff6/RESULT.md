# The six-line characteristic-five reduction of Bring’s curve and its cubic conductor

## Finding

Let \(\mathscr B\subset\mathbb P^4_{\mathbb Z_5}\) be the closed projective scheme defined by
\[
\sum_{i=1}^5 X_i=\sum_{i=1}^5 X_i^2=\sum_{i=1}^5 X_i^3=0.
\]
Its geometric special fiber \(B_5\) is exactly the **reduced union of six distinct projective lines**, all defined over \(\mathbb F_5\) and meeting only at \(E=[1:1:1:1:1]\). Explicitly, its components are the distinct lines among
\[
L_\sigma=\mathbb P\langle(1,1,1,1,1),\;\sigma(0,1,2,3,4)\rangle,
\qquad\sigma\in S_5.
\]
There are six such lines; their stabilizer in \(S_5\) is \(\mathrm{AGL}_1(\mathbb F_5)\) of order \(20\), equivalently the normalizer of a Sylow-\(5\) subgroup. The special fiber is a degree-\(6\), arithmetic-genus-\(4\) complete intersection with one singular point, \(E\).

Let \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_5\). With \(\mathfrak m\) the maximal ideal of \(\mathcal O_{B_5,E}\), the normalization quotient and conductor satisfy
\[
\ell_k(\widetilde{\mathcal O}_{B_5,E}/\mathcal O_{B_5,E})=9,\qquad
\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathcal O_{B_5,E}}(\widetilde{\mathcal O}_{B_5,E}/\mathcal O_{B_5,E})=\mathfrak m^3.
\]
Moreover, \(\ell_k(\mathcal O_{B_5,E}/\mathfrak m^3)=9\) and \(\ell_k(\widetilde{\mathcal O}_{B_5,E}/\mathfrak m^3)=18\). The family \(\mathscr B\) is flat over \(\mathbb Z_5\).

## Assumptions and scope

All geometric assertions concern the specific integral homogeneous power-sum model, not an arbitrary regular or stable model. The prime is exactly \(5\), with residue field \(\mathbb F_5\) and its algebraic closure \(k\). The relevant special fiber is understood scheme-theoretically, before normalization. By \(\mathbb P\langle v,w\rangle\) we mean the projective line spanned by independent vectors. The conductor is an ideal of the local ring and of its normalization through the canonical inclusion. Lengths are taken after extending to \(k\).

## Proof

**1. The exact special-fiber equations.** In characteristic five, put
\[
t=X_5,\quad a=X_1-X_5,\quad b=X_2-X_5,\quad c=X_3-X_5.
\]
The linear power-sum equation gives \(X_4=t-a-b-c\). Set
\[
Q=a^2+b^2+c^2+ab+ac+bc,\qquad P=(a+b)(a+c)(b+c).
\]
Direct expansion in \(\mathbb F_5[a,b,c,t]\) gives
\[
\sum X_i^2=2Q,\qquad
\sum X_i^3=3t\sum X_i^2-3P.
\]
Since \(2\) and \(3\) are invertible, the complete defining ideal in the hyperplane \(\sum X_i=0\cong\mathbb P^3\) is **exactly** \((Q,P)\), not merely an ideal with the same support. Both polynomials are homogeneous in the variables \(a,b,c\) and independent of \(t\). Thus \(B_5\) is the projective cone over \(D=V(Q,P)\subset\mathbb P^2_{[a:b:c]}\), with vertex \(E\).

**2. Six transverse points and reduced lines.** The cubic \(P\) is the union of three distinct lines \(a+b=0\), \(a+c=0\), and \(b+c=0\). Restricting \(Q\) to any of these lines gives a nonzero binary quadratic with two distinct roots over \(\mathbb F_5\) (the relevant discriminant is a nonzero square). The intersections of pairs of those three lines are not on \(Q\). More explicitly,
\[
D=\{[1:4:2],[1:4:3],[1:2:4],[1:3:4],[1:2:3],[1:3:2]\}.
\]
Each point is a transverse intersection of \(Q\) and exactly one factor of \(P\). Bezout's degree \(2\cdot3=6\) exhausts the geometric intersection over \(k\); hence \(D\) is reduced and consists of exactly these six rational points. Its projective cone is therefore the reduced union of six distinct lines through \(E\), with no further components or embedded scheme structure. Away from \(E\) the cone is a disjoint union of smooth punctured lines, and \(E\) is singular because at least two branches meet there.

**3. Symmetry and arithmetic invariants.** For \(v=(0,1,2,3,4)\in\mathbb F_5^5\), all three sums \(\sum v_i^j\), \(j=1,2,3\), vanish in \(\mathbb F_5\), and the same holds on the line generated by \(v\) and \(E\) because \(\sum 1=0\) in characteristic five. Every coordinate permutation therefore produces a component, and these are all components by Step 2. A permutation preserves \(\mathbb P\langle E,v\rangle\) precisely when its action on the five labels is an affine transformation \(z\mapsto\alpha z+\beta\) of \(\mathbb F_5\), with \(\alpha\in\mathbb F_5^\times\), \(\beta\in\mathbb F_5\). This stabilizer is \(\mathrm{AGL}_1(\mathbb F_5)\) of order \(20\); its orbit has \(120/20=6\) elements. Its normal cyclic subgroup of order five consists of translations, so it is the normalizer of a Sylow-five subgroup. The special fiber is a complete intersection of degrees \(2,3\) in \(\mathbb P^3\), with Hilbert polynomial \(6n-3\) for large \(n\), hence degree \(6\) and arithmetic genus \(4\).

**4. Normalization, defect, and exact conductor.** At \(E\) take the affine chart \(t=1\). The completed local ring is
\[
A=k[[a,b,c]]/(Q,P).
\]
Each of the six distinct branches has tangent direction \([1:b_j:c_j]\) from Step 2, so its normalization is \(k[[s_j]]\), parametrized by \(a=s_j\), \(b=b_js_j\), \(c=c_js_j\). Thus \(\widetilde A=\prod_{j=1}^6k[[s_j]]\). The graded coordinate ring \(R=k[a,b,c]/(Q,P)\) embeds into \(\prod_{j=1}^6k[a]\). Its degree-\(n\) image is the space of evaluations of ternary degree-\(n\) forms at the six projective directions, with \(a=1\). The complete-intersection Hilbert series
\[
\frac{(1-z^2)(1-z^3)}{(1-z)^3}
\]
gives the exact evaluation ranks \(1,3,5,6\) in degrees \(n=0,1,2,3\), respectively. Multiplication by \(a\), which is nonzero at every direction, shows that the ranks remain six for every \(n\ge3\).

No evaluation space of degree \(0,1,2\) contains an individual branch coordinate vector: otherwise coordinate-permutation symmetry, transitive on the six branches, would force it to contain all six branch vectors, contrary to its rank being below six. Consequently \(R\) contains the whole degree-at-least-three subspace of its normalization and no element of smaller order supported on a single branch. The conductor of \(R\) in its normalization is therefore its irrelevant ideal cubed, and completion/localization gives exactly
\[
(A:\widetilde A)=\mathfrak m^3.
\]
The normalization defect is the sum of six minus each degree's evaluation rank:
\[
\ell_k(\widetilde A/A)=(6-1)+(6-3)+(6-5)=9.
\]
Similarly \(\ell_k(A/\mathfrak m^3)=1+3+5=9\), and each normalized branch contributes three coefficients below order three, giving \(\ell_k(\widetilde A/\mathfrak m^3)=6\cdot3=18\).

**5. Flatness.** Over \(\mathbb Z_5\), the three power sums define a codimension-three complete intersection in the ambient projective four-space: their generic fiber is the classical smooth Bring curve, while Step 2 shows that their special fiber has codimension three and has no embedded components. In the regular local rings of the ambient integral projective space, the residue parameter \(5\) and the three equations form a regular sequence. The quotient is \(5\)-torsion-free, hence flat over the discrete valuation ring \(\mathbb Z_5\).

## Verification

The accompanying `bring5_verify.py` is executable with Python 3 and the dependency listed in `requirements.txt`. It uses exact arithmetic over \(\mathbb F_5\) to check the polynomial identity, the complete six-point intersection, transversality, all \(31\) rational points of the special fiber, the permutation-orbit count and stabilizer order, the complete-intersection Hilbert function, and the evaluation ranks and forbidden branch-unit vectors through degree two. A successful execution prints `VERIFY_OK` and `CONDUCTOR_OK`. These finite checks are supplementary to the algebraic arguments proving geometric completeness over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_5\), the conductor equality, and flatness.

## Relationship to prior work

Braden–Northover give the classical Bring-curve model and its period geometry. Korchmáros–Lia–Timpanella analyze a higher-dimensional power-sum generalization under the positive-characteristic hypothesis \(p\ge7\) and \(m\le p-1\), explicitly excluding \(m=p=5\). Their separate discussion of a larger two-power-sum variety when \(m=p\) supplies related rational lines through the diagonal point, but does not identify the scheme cut out by the **third** power sum, its entire reduced component decomposition, or its conductor. Braden–Disney-Hogg provide an extensive study of the characteristic-zero curve, explicitly leaving positive-characteristic extensions outside scope. The characteristic-five complete-intersection reduction and conductor asserted here were not found in these inspected sources; the possibility of prior equivalent treatments remains a limitation.

## Limitations

This result concerns the indicated integral projective model, not a stable reduction theorem for the generic smooth curve and not the minimal desingularization over a ramified extension. The calculation of \(31\) rational points describes the \(\mathbb F_5\)-special fiber only. The novelty comparison cannot exclude all older modular or finite-geometric treatments, including formulations in other coordinates. Independent scientific assessment has not been carried out.
