# The Witt pentad-avoidance complex is Alexander self-dual and a wedge of 120 four-spheres

## Finding

Let \(V=\mathbb Z/11\mathbb Z\). Take the six pentads
\[
\{0,1,3,4,7\},\quad\{0,1,2,7,8\},\quad\{0,1,5,7,9\},\quad
\{0,1,2,6,9\},\quad\{0,1,3,6,8\},\quad\{0,1,2,3,5\},
\]
and let \(\mathcal B\) be the union of their translates under the cyclic action of \(V\). Let \(K\) be the simplicial complex of all subsets of \(V\) containing no block of \(\mathcal B\). The \(66\) blocks form the Witt Steiner system \(S(4,5,11)\), so this complex is defined, up to isomorphism, by the classical Witt design.

Then \(K\) is **Alexander self-dual** on its eleven-vertex ground set, \(K^*=K\), and
\[
K\simeq\bigvee^{120}S^4.
\]
In particular \(\widetilde H_4(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{120}\), all other reduced integral homology groups vanish, and there is no torsion.

## Assumptions and scope

The point set is precisely \(V\), addition in the six displayed orbits is modulo \(11\), and a simplex means any subset avoiding every five-element block in its entirety. The empty simplex is included by convention, but discrete-Morse matching acts on all nonempty faces. The Alexander dual of a complex \(L\) on \(V\) is \(L^*=\{\sigma\subseteq V: V\setminus\sigma\notin L\}\). The assertion is for the unique classical \(S(4,5,11)\) up to relabeling, not for arbitrary hypergraphs with eleven vertices.

The result concerns the **topological realization** of this block-avoiding complex. It is distinct from the matroid complex generated by complements of *nonblocks* and from the simplicial complex generated by the blocks themselves.

## Proof

**1. The six orbits define the required design.** The included `verify.py` constructs all \(66\) different translates. It visits all \(\binom{11}{4}=330\) tetrads and checks that each occurs in exactly one pentad. Thus \(\mathcal B\) is an \(S(4,5,11)\). The known uniqueness of the small derived Witt design identifies this model with every other realization up to isomorphism.

**2. Structural description and Alexander self-duality.** The design has \(66\) blocks. Fix one block \(B\), and let \(a_j\) be the number of other blocks meeting \(B\) in exactly \(j\) points. Any two distinct blocks meet in at most three points. Using the design replication counts \(\lambda_3=4\), \(\lambda_2=12\), and \(\lambda_1=30\), respectively, double-counting incidences with three-, two-, and one-subsets of \(B\) gives
\[
a_3=30,\quad a_2=20,\quad a_1=15,\quad a_0=0.
\]
Hence any two pentads intersect, so the complement of each block is a block-free hexad.

Every four-set is a face. Exactly \(\binom{11}{5}-66=396\) pentads are faces. For a nonblock pentad \(P\), each of its five tetrads has a unique containing block. These five blocks must have distinct fifth points outside \(P\), since two distinct blocks cannot share four points. Of the six vertices outside \(P\), exactly five produce a forbidden extension, leaving **exactly one** block-free hexad containing \(P\). Counting incidences between nonblock pentads and free hexads yields \(396/6=66\) free hexads. Since the 66 block complements are already free, they exhaust them. Any free seven-set would provide two free hexad extensions of each of its pentads, an impossibility. Thus the facets of \(K\) are precisely
\[
\{V\setminus B:B\in\mathcal B\}.
\]
By the definition of Alexander duality, \(K^*\) is the complex generated by these same complements. Therefore \(K^*=K\).

**3. Exact discrete-Morse reduction.** Direct enumeration yields the face vector
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4,f_5)=(11,55,165,330,396,66),
\]
with exactly \(1023\) nonempty faces. The following fully specified matching uses increasing vertex order \(i=0,1,\ldots,10\). Initially mark every nonempty face unmatched. At stage \(i\), pair every *currently unmatched* face \(S\) that omits \(i\) with \(S\cup\{i\}\) when both faces are currently unmatched. Remove each paired face from subsequent stages. Every pair differs by exactly one vertex; there are no conflicts within a stage. The exact numbers of pairs at the eleven stages are
\[
(391,36,16,6,2,0,0,0,0,0,0).
\]
This is \(451\) pairs. Exactly one vertex and \(120\) four-dimensional faces remain critical.

A matching is acyclic precisely when the directed face-incidence Hasse diagram, with matched edges reversed relative to the ordinary downward orientation, has no directed cycle. The supplied verifier constructs every one of the \(4301\) covering incidences among the \(1023\) nonempty faces, reverses precisely the \(451\) matching edges, and performs a complete topological sort. All \(1023\) nodes are removed, proving acyclicity of this explicit finite matching. By discrete Morse theory, \(K\) is homotopy equivalent to a CW complex with one zero-cell and \(120\) four-cells, and no other cells. Every attaching map of a four-cell lands in the zero-skeleton, hence is constant. This CW complex is \(\bigvee^{120} S^4\).

## Verification

`verify.py` is a dependency-free Python 3 verifier. Running `python verify.py` constructs the six cyclic pentad orbits, checks the complete Steiner property, enumerates every face, verifies the 66 complementary facets and each pentad's unique hexad extension, reconstructs all 451 matching pairs, and checks the acyclicity of the entire oriented Hasse graph by topological sort. The exact observed output is provided in `verification_output.txt`. No probabilistic sampling, floating-point arithmetic, or extrapolation is used.

## Relationship to prior work

The Witt design is a classical Mathieu-group object; Havlicek and Lenz provide a full-text construction of the small Witt design and its defining incidence properties. Standard references record uniqueness of the derived \(S(4,5,11)\). Ballico, Favacchio, Guardo, and Milazzo study Steiner-system-associated Stanley–Reisner and **different matroid** complexes, including the complex whose facets are complements of nonblocks; their graded algebraic Betti numbers are not a homotopy classification of the present pentad-avoidance complex, whose facets are complements of blocks. The homotopy classification here additionally requires the acyclic Morse matching, and does not follow from the shared Steiner parameter counts or from Alexander duality alone.

## Limitations

This theorem is restricted to the Witt system \(S(4,5,11)\); it asserts no analogous wedge decomposition for arbitrary Steiner systems. The cyclic model and exhaustive finite certificate prove only this fixed finite case, not a universal parameter theorem. As usual, literature searches cannot exclude an unindexed equivalent homotopy classification, and the result has not undergone independent scientific audit.
