# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The asserted result is a limit of ordered box averages of an explicitly defined integral-point count. The proof begins with the pointwise lattice-Pick formula and proves the precise identity \(2I=g^2uvw-g(u+v+w)+2\). Pairwise coprimality of normalized pair-GCDs is justified valuation by valuation, allowing an exhaustive monotonicity classification for \(I\le2\). All local factors are computed from disjoint valuation conditions; the coefficients were independently recomputed as exact rational fractions. CRT plus the uniform bad-prime tail \(3/Y\) promotes local expressions to true limits, rather than finite empirical extrapolations. The included verifier compares thousands of triples with direct positive-solution enumeration. The proof does not claim a convergence rate.

## Originality — PASS, residual risk acknowledged
The closest source, Aldrich–Gulbrandsen–Keddy (arXiv:2610.20899v1, 14 October 2026), gives the triangle's individual lattice-point count and Ehrhart polynomial but no box-average distribution, and was inspected in full HTML (Introduction, Section 3, Future Work). Heyman (arXiv:1309.5578), also inspected in full HTML (Theorem 1, Section 4), derives a related coprimality inclusion-exclusion constant, substantially covering the baseline behind the \(I=0\) formula. It does not classify the exact GCD valuation patterns corresponding to one and two interior points, or state their densities \(3583P/17280\) and \(151P/2100\). published-finding corpus searches for both the source and the density formulas found no matching or dominating claim; neighboring records concern other triangle invariants or different gcd distributions. Residual risk: unindexed equivalent tables/classifications and further implicit deductions from general local-distribution theorems. This is not a claim that the zero-interior component alone is novel.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Unlike computing one triangle's Ehrhart polynomial, the theorem answers the motivated probabilistic question of how often random integer-axis triangles have their first three interior-point counts. The finite exact classification is structurally informative (it isolates all relevant gcd valuation patterns), and the positive Euler-product densities quantify genuinely different geometric strata. The new probabilities for one and two interior points require nontrivial local arithmetic beyond the known coprimality constants. The result does not rely on an arbitrary finite cutoff or a recoding of individual point counts.
