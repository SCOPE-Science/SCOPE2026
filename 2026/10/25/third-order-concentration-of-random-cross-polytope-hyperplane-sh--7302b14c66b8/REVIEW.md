# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The facet-normal Cauchy formula exactly reduces hyperplane brightness to a Rademacher absolute mean. The spherical-to-Gaussian reduction and the bivariate absolute Gaussian formula provide the second moment with binomial sign-overlap weights. Explicit fourth- and sixth-sign moments and a controlled eighth-moment/Taylor remainder give the third-order expansion; the gamma-ratio expansion agrees termwise and proves the variance coefficient. The standard-library diagnostics independently compare geometric shadows of the actual vertices, not just the same analytic formula. No finite computation is used as proof of an infinite-dimensional statement.

## Originality — PASS with bibliographic residual risk
Horváth–Lángi's translated-convex-hull identity is a known geometric precursor. The higher moments in Kabluchko–Litvak–Zaporozhets concern **one-dimensional** projected lengths, not random hyperplane-brightness volumes. Ivanov's full-text subject is extrema of projection volumes rather than uniform-direction variance. The binomial formula is a specialization of a general spherical absolute-product method; priority of this precise specialization and the sharp \(d^{-3}\) coefficient has not been independently established, and unindexed earlier brightness literature may contain them. Searches and comparison details appear in the accompanying structured review.

## Value — PASS
Projection brightness is a central convex-geometric functional and is exactly the directional growth of a convex hull under translation. The proved variance is much smaller than naive first-order concentration estimates would suggest, because cancellation persists through the \(d^{-2}\) level. Its exact sharp constant and all-dimensional binomial formula quantify the resulting high-dimensional isotropy; this is more than a numerical special case or a routine normalization check. The theorem does not claim a distributional limit.
