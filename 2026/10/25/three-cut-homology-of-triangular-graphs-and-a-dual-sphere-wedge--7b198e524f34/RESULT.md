# Three-cut homology of triangular graphs and a dual sphere-wedge theorem

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge5\), let \(T_n=L(K_n)=J(n,2)\) be the triangular graph with vertices the \(N=\binom n2\) edges of the complete graph, where two edges are adjacent precisely when they intersect. Let \(X_n=\Delta_3(T_n)\) denote the **ordinary** three-cut complex: its facets are complements of disconnected three-vertex induced subgraphs of \(T_n\). Then the combinatorial Alexander dual satisfies
\[
 X_n^\vee\simeq\bigvee^{\binom{n-1}{4}} S^3.
\]
Consequently its reduced **integral** homology is
\[
 \widetilde H_i(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong
 \begin{cases}
 \mathbb Z^{\binom{n-1}{4}},&i=\binom n2-6,\\
 0,&i\ne\binom n2-6.
 \end{cases}
\]
The complex \(X_n\) is pure of dimension \(\binom n2-4\), but has nonzero homology two dimensions below the top, so it is nonshellable for every \(n\ge5\). The first four homology ranks, for \(n=5,6,7,8\), are \(1,5,15,35\), respectively.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple, and undirected. Write \([n]=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}\), and identify a vertex of \(T_n\) with a two-subset (an edge) of \([n]\). The ordinary cut complex is defined by **disconnected induced triples**, not by independent triples (which define the total cut complex). The dual \(R_n=X_n^\vee\) is formed relative to the complete set of \(N\) vertices. Thus a subset \(F\subseteq E(K_n)\) is a face of \(R_n\) if and only if no three edges of \(F\) induce a disconnected triple in \(T_n\), equivalently every three distinct edges of \(F\) have at least two intersecting pairs. The empty face is included formally, but the Morse matching below uses the ordinary nonempty face poset. The assertion concerns integral homology of \(X_n\) and an ordinary, nonequivariant homotopy type of its dual, not a homotopy type of \(X_n\) itself.

## Proof
**1. Classify faces of the dual.** Associate to \(F\subseteq E(K_n)\) the graph \(D(F)\) on the members of \(F\), where two edges of \(K_n\) are adjacent in \(D(F)\) exactly when they are disjoint. A three-element subset of \(F\) induces a connected triple in \(T_n\) precisely when its disjointness graph contains at most one edge. Consequently,
\[
 F\in R_n\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \max_{e\in F}\deg_{D(F)}(e)\le1.
\]
If \(F\) contains disjoint edges \(ab\) and \(cd\), every other member must meet both, or one of \(ab,cd\) would have two disjoint partners. Thus \(F\subseteq E(K_4[\{a,b,c,d\}])\). Conversely, any subfamily of the six edges of a four-vertex complete graph satisfies the degree bound, since each of its edges has only one possible disjoint edge. Therefore \(R_n\) is generated by the full simplices on the stars of \(K_n\) together with full simplices \(P_S=\Delta(E(K_4[S]))\) for every four-subset \(S\subset[n]\). Pairwise-intersecting families of edges are stars or the three edges of a triangle; this elementary classification accounts for all faces without a disjoint pair.

**2. Exhibit a contractible subcomplex.** Designate label \(0\). Let \(B(F)=\{e\in F:0\notin e\}\) and let \(R_0\subseteq R_n\) be the subcomplex on the faces for which \(B(F)\) is pairwise intersecting. For a fixed nonempty \(B\), all face extensions by edges incident with \(0\) form a cone: if the members of \(B\) have common endpoint \(a\), then the spoke \(0a\) may be added to any face with that \(B\); if \(B\) is a triangle on \(a,b,c\), the admissible spokes are among \(0a,0b,0c\), and any of them provides a cone apex. For \(B=\varnothing\) the spokes form a full simplex. Choose, deterministically, the smallest common endpoint in the star case, the smallest triangle vertex in the triangle case, and spoke \(01\) in the empty case. In each fiber except the empty face, match every face omitting its chosen spoke with the face obtained by adding that spoke; in the empty-\(B\) fiber this leaves the vertex \(\{01\}\) unmatched.

This matching is acyclic. Within each fixed \(B\), an oriented gradient path cannot cross a matched edge in both directions because all matched pairs toggle one fixed spoke; the cone matching has no directed cycles. Along an unmatched downward face-poset relation the set \(B\) can only decrease, whereas every matched relation preserves \(B\). Hence any hypothetical directed cycle would be contained in one fiber, where none exists. The matching has exactly one critical zero-cell, and no other critical cells. By discrete Morse theory \(R_0\) is contractible (in fact collapsible).

**3. Isolate the four-vertex summands.** Suppose \(F\in R_n\setminus R_0\). Then \(B(F)\) contains two disjoint edges \(ab,cd\). No edge incident with \(0\) is allowed: it must be disjoint from at least one of \(ab,cd\), making that edge have two disjoint partners. By step 1, every member of \(F\) is one of the six edges on the unique four-element support \(S=\{a,b,c,d\}\subseteq[n]\setminus\{0\}\). The support is unique because a disjoint pair already exhausts four labels. Thus
\[
 R_n=R_0\cup\bigcup_{\substack{S\subseteq[n]\setminus\{0\}\\|S|=4}}P_S,
\]
and for distinct \(S,T\) the intersection \(P_S\cap P_T\) is contained in \(R_0\), since \(|S\cap T|\le3\).

Within a fixed four-set \(S\), the six edges of \(K_4[S]\) split into three opposite (disjoint) pairs. Therefore
\[
 P_S\cap R_0=\{F\subseteq E(K_4[S]): F\text{ contains at most one edge from each opposite pair}\}
 \cong S^0*S^0*S^0\cong S^2.
\]
Here \(P_S\) is a full five-simplex, hence contractible, and its intersection with \(R_0\) is the boundary of a three-dimensional cross-polytope, not the boundary of the five-simplex.

**4. Pass to homotopy and integral homology.** Since \(R_0\hookrightarrow R_n\) is an inclusion of CW subcomplexes and \(R_0\) is nonempty and contractible, the quotient \(R_n/R_0\) is homotopy equivalent to \(R_n\). Distinct \(P_S\) intersect only inside the collapsed subcomplex, so
\[
 R_n\simeq R_n/R_0\cong\bigvee_S P_S/(P_S\cap R_0)
 \simeq\bigvee_S \Sigma(S^2)
 \simeq\bigvee^{\binom{n-1}{4}}S^3.
\]
The middle equivalence follows because the cofibration of \(S^2\) into the contractible five-simplex has cofiber homotopy equivalent to its suspension. Integral combinatorial Alexander duality on the \(N\)-vertex ground set gives
\[
 \widetilde H_i(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H^{N-i-3}(R_n;\mathbb Z).
\]
The only nonzero reduced cohomology of the wedge is free, in degree three. Thus the sole nonzero homology degree is \(i=N-6\), of rank \(\binom{n-1}{4}\), with no torsion. Each facet of \(X_n\) has \(N-3\) vertices, hence dimension \(N-4\). For \(n\ge5\), \(\binom{n-1}{4}>0\); a pure shellable complex has no reduced homology below its top dimension. Therefore \(X_n\) is not shellable.

## Verification
The provided standalone standard-library script `CHECK.py` enumerates all nonempty dual faces, directly checks the connected-triple criterion, constructs an explicit acyclic matching on the full dual, and verifies the oriented Hasse graph has no cycles for each \(n=5,6,7,8\). It reports respectively \(260,746,1750,3634\) nonempty dual faces, one critical vertex in each case, and \(1,5,15,35\) critical three-cells. Execution: `python3 CHECK.py`. The finite tests corroborate the all-parameter proof but do not themselves establish it; the algebraic topological argument above establishes every integer \(n\ge5\).

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer, Denker, Jelić Milutinović, Rowlands, Sundaram and Xue introduced ordinary cut complexes in *Topology of Cut Complexes of Graphs* (arXiv:2304.13675, first public April 26, 2023) and classified several graph families. Their chordal and high-girth criteria do not cover \(T_n\), which contains induced four-cycles as well as triangles for \(n\ge5\). Their follow-up *Topology of Cut Complexes II* (arXiv:2407.08158) treats different graph constructions and does not state this triangular-graph family. Previously published calculations for a four-cut complex of \(J(5,2)\), and ordinary three-cut complexes of rook graphs \(L(K_{m,n})\), concern different cut parameters or different graphs and do not imply this theorem. The cut complex defined by independent triples (the **total** cut complex) is also distinct from the ordinary disconnected-triple object here.

## Limitations
No homotopy type or equivariant representation of \(X_n\) itself is asserted; the proved homotopy decomposition concerns only its combinatorial Alexander dual. This result does not classify \(k\)-cut complexes for \(k\ne3\), nor arbitrary Johnson graphs \(J(n,k)\). Non-indexed equivalent prior work remains a literature risk. Finite exhaustive checks for four sample parameters are not a substitute for the full proof given above.
