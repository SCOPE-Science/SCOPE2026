# Review of the rank-one Nijenhuis locus of \(\mathfrak n_4\)

## Correctness — PASS
The Nijenhuis identity for a rank-one map reduces to the alternating scalar equation (1) of `RESULT.md`. Evaluating all matrix-unit pairs gives the explicit kernel spaces of dimensions \(1,3,6\), and substituting the parametrizations verifies sufficiency in every characteristic. The projective incidence description proves three distinct irreducible component closures, of dimensions \(5,6,7\); the affine dimensions are one larger. Pair-counting under reciprocal scalar action proves the exact finite-field polynomial and square/non-square split. The standalone checker independently reconstructs the brackets and verifies the counts for four field sizes. All-field proof is algebraic; finite tests are supportive only.

## Originality — PASS, with historical limitations
The closest relevant literature found defines Lie Nijenhuis operators and develops their deformation and compatibility theory (Azimi–Laurent-Gengoux–Nunes da Costa, arXiv:1308.6119; Hu–Liu–Sheng, arXiv:2004.10065). The evaluated new content is the complete fiber classification and the resulting three non-equidimensional components and finite-field rank-one count for the particular class-three Lie algebra \(\mathfrak n_4\); the general Nijenhuis identity itself is not claimed. Broader Lie Nijenhuis classifications, especially ones using different terminology, remain a priority risk. Two-step nilpotent formulas do not imply this result because the derived ideal of \(\mathfrak n_4\) is not central.

## Value — PASS
The smallest standard niltriangular Lie algebra of nilpotency class three is a natural test beyond the two-step case. The theorem explains a sharp three-stage change in admissible image dimension controlled by the annihilator filtration of the derived ideal, together with an unusual non-equidimensional component decomposition. Exact finite-field counts give an arithmetic measurement of this geometric stratification. These invariants are motivated by the deformation/operator problem, not by a numerical slice chosen solely for computational convenience.

## Limits
Only rank-one endomorphisms and reduced geometric loci are classified. The classification is not a conjugacy classification, a higher-rank classification, or a certification of publication priority.
