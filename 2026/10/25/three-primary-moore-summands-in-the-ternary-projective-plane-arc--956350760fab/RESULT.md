# Three-primary Moore summands in the ternary projective-plane arc complex

## Finding
Let \(P=\operatorname{PG}(2,\mathbb F_3)\) be the thirteen-point Desarguesian projective plane. Define its **arc complex** \(K(P)\) on the point set of \(P\): a subset is a face exactly when it contains no three collinear points. Then
\[
K(P)\simeq \bigvee^{66} S^3\ \vee\ \bigvee^{3} M(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z,2),
\]
where \(M(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z,2)\) is the Moore space obtained from \(S^2\) by attaching one three-cell by a map of degree three. In particular,
\[
\widetilde H_i(K(P);\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z)^3,&i=2,\\
\mathbb Z^{66},&i=3,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
This is a complete homotopy decomposition, not merely an assertion about the groups above.

## Assumptions and scope
Coordinates are the nonzero triples over \(\mathbb F_3\), modulo nonzero scalar multiples; each representative is normalized so that its first nonzero entry is one, then the thirteen representatives are sorted lexicographically and indexed from zero to twelve. The thirteen projective lines are the zero sets of the thirteen nonzero linear forms normalized in the same way. No other projective-plane order, non-Desarguesian configuration, or family-wide classification is asserted. The result concerns the **noncollinear-set complex**, not the rank-three matroid independence complex (which only admits independent sets of size at most three) or the incidence complex whose facets are projective lines.

The input geometric structure is classical. A directly dated public source discussing its incidence geometry and a distinct incidence simplicial complex is Cook, Migliore, Nagel and Zanello, arXiv:1503.04335, first submitted 2015-03-14. The topological decomposition above is not asserted in that source.

## Proof
**1. Finite geometry and faces.** The construction in `verify.py` generates thirteen distinct points and thirteen four-point lines, and checks that each pair of points lies on exactly one line. Each line contributes four different forbidden collinear triples; different lines cannot share a triple. Thus there are \(52\) forbidden triples. The complex has \(13\) vertices, \(78\) edges, \(234=\binom{13}{3}-52\) triangles and \(234\) tetrahedra. Each noncollinear triple extends to precisely four four-point arcs: the excluded fourth points lie on one of its three side lines, whose union has nine points; the four points outside those lines are permissible. There is no five-point arc, as verified by exhausting the \(2^{13}-1\) nonempty subsets. Its face vector, by dimension, is therefore \((13,78,234,234)\), a total of \(559\) nonempty faces.

**2. Explicit acyclic discrete-Morse matching.** For each vertex index \(v=0,1,\ldots,12\), successively pair every currently unmatched nonempty face \(\sigma\) not containing \(v\) with \(\sigma\cup\{v\}\), provided both faces remain unmatched. Traverse candidate masks in increasing numerical order. This pairs exactly \(242\) face–coface pairs. Only one zero-cell, four two-cells, and seventy three-cells remain critical. Reverse the matched Hasse arrows and direct all other face-incidence arrows downward. `verify.py` constructs all \(1,794\) directed incidences and topologically sorts all \(559\) faces: no directed cycle remains. Thus this is an acyclic discrete-Morse matching, and \(K(P)\) has a homotopy-equivalent CW complex with exactly those critical cells and no one-cells.

**3. Exact integral attaching matrix.** Give every simplex its increasing-vertex orientation. Compute the four-by-seventy integer Morse boundary matrix \(B\) from critical three-cells to critical two-cells, summing the signed descending gradient paths. Equivalently, whenever a two-face \(\sigma\) is paired with a three-face \(\tau\), eliminate it from the signed boundary via
\[
\sigma=-[\tau:\sigma]^{-1}\!\sum_{\substack{\eta\subset\tau\\|\eta|=3,\ \eta\ne\sigma}}[\tau:\eta]\,\eta,
\]
recursing until only critical two-faces remain; the other noncritical two-faces are removed by the lower-dimensional pairings. Here all signed incidences are \(\pm1\). The four surviving two-face masks are `38, 42, 70, 74` in increasing order, and the seventy surviving three-face masks are independently generated by the verifier.

The exact matrix has \(B_{0,5}=-1\) (zero-based indices). Integer row and column operations isolate this unit pivot; the remaining three-by-sixty-nine Schur complement has **every entry divisible by three**. After dividing that complement by three, its columns corresponding to original column indices `1, 31, 50` contain the minor
\[
\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\1&0&-1\\-1&-1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\det=-1.
\]
Consequently the residual divided matrix has a unimodular maximal minor. Elementary integral operations bring the whole boundary matrix to diagonal form with nonzero Smith factors \(1,3,3,3\), and all remaining columns zero. This proves the claimed integral homology, including all three order-three factors.

**4. Homotopy type, not just homology.** The Morse CW complex has one zero-cell and no one-cells, so its two-skeleton is \(\bigvee^4 S^2\). By the degree-two Hurewicz isomorphism, homotopy classes of three-cell attaching maps into this wedge are completely recorded by their four-component integer degree vectors. Unimodular changes of the two-cell basis are realized by homotopy self-equivalences of the wedge; elementary three-cell slides realize column operations. The preceding diagonal form therefore identifies one contractible two/three-cell pair, three degree-three Moore summands, and sixty-six three-cells attached trivially. This is the displayed wedge decomposition.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` using Python 3 with no nonstandard packages. `verification_output.txt` records the expected result, ending `VERIFY_OK`. The verifier regenerates finite-field projective points and lines, enumerates all subsets, checks incidence and forbidden triples, constructs every discrete-Morse pair, proves matching acyclicity by an exhaustive topological sort, regenerates the signed Morse boundary, and checks the integral Smith-factor certificate using only integer arithmetic. It also independently builds the ordinary simplicial boundary matrices and confirms their ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\): respectively \((12,66,168)\) and \((12,66,165)\) in face sizes two, three, four. These modular cross-checks agree with the integral torsion claim but do not replace the integer certificate.

## Relationship to prior work
Cook, Migliore, Nagel and Zanello (2015), *An algebraic approach to finite projective planes*, construct the **incidence complex** with facets equal to the lines and prove properties of its Alexander dual and Stanley–Reisner resolution. That incidence complex has thirteen line facets, while the present arc complex has 234 four-point arc facets. Neither their result nor the line-complex Alexander dual gives the stated homotopy classification. Superdock (2021, revised 2023), *Simplicial complexes from finite projective planes and colored configurations*, studies a different construction with two copies of the plane's line configuration and its covering/topological properties. Its facets are not the no-three-collinear arcs. Historical finite-geometry literature records the projective plane and its arcs but was not used as evidence for this homotopy decomposition. Published results on cap complexes of other geometries do not imply the present odd-order case.

## Limitations
The result is restricted to \(\operatorname{PG}(2,3)\). It is supported by a transparent finite certificate and mathematical reasoning about the resulting CW complex, but has not received independent scientific review. The available literature searches did not identify the same homotopy decomposition; that does not prove global uniqueness. A covering unindexed or inaccessible source could exist. There is no claim about any infinite family, combinatorial shellability, or a universal torsion law.
