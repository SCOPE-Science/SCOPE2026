# Scientific review: ternary projective-plane arc complex

## Correctness — PASS

The domain consists of the thirteen points and thirteen four-point lines of \(\operatorname{PG}(2,\mathbb F_3)\). Every nonempty subset is enumerated and checked against the 52 collinear triples. The face vector is \((13,78,234,234)\). The staged vertex-toggle matching has 242 pairs, and an exhaustive topological sort proves acyclicity of all 1,794 reversed/ordinary face incidences. The reduced CW model has one zero-cell, four two-cells and seventy three-cells. Exact signed Morse reduction gives a four-by-seventy integral boundary with Smith factors \((1,3,3,3)\), certified by a unit pivot, a three-divisible Schur complement and a unimodular maximal minor after division. The simply connected two-skeleton allows the full homotopy conclusion, not merely homology. Direct simplicial mod-two/mod-three chain ranks agree. The complete algorithm and expected output are included.

## Originality — PASS

Compared claims, not just titles: Cook–Migliore–Nagel–Zanello (arXiv:1503.04335, Sections 1–2) use a line-incidence complex; their Alexander-dual vertex-decomposability result does not imply an arc-complex homotopy type. Superdock (arXiv:2110.12314, Abstract and Sections 1–3, 7) constructs complexes from *two copies* of a line configuration, not the 234 noncollinear four-arcs. Published results on the ternary plane's graph-neighborhood complexes and on different cap planes do not cover the present statement. Queries for projective arcs, no-collinear-triple hypergraph complexes, Alexander-dual descriptions, 234 four-arcs and integral three-torsion did not reveal an exact covering statement. Originality remains contingent on source coverage: an unindexed equivalent or an uninspected book treatment may exist. The Hirschfeld monograph was not available in full and is not characterized as noncovering.

## Value — PASS

The central natural object is the arc complex of the first odd prime-order projective plane beyond the Fano plane. A precise, previously non-implied integral invariant occurs: three order-three Moore spaces, not only rank-six-sphere homology. The homotopy statement is stronger than face counts or rational homology and highlights the possibility of intrinsic odd-primary torsion in cap complexes. A broad family theorem is neither established nor claimed. The exact small-plane result is mathematically substantive on its own.

## Residual limitations

The decomposition concerns only \(\operatorname{PG}(2,3)\); exhaustive finite verification is not proof of a uniform formula for other orders. The primary full texts inspected have distinct definitions. The older finite-geometry monograph was accessible only at catalog level, and originality cannot be established solely by unsuccessful search. Independent scientific validation remains outstanding.
