# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed finite-jet injectivity is derived from the published exact convex-polygon quartic and independently proved through the inradius tangency formula. For the new step, the explicitly differentiated ratio \(g''/f''\) is strictly increasing because \(\tan v>v\). This makes the Jacobian of the two symmetric angle sums strictly signed inside the scalene chamber. Each fixed-perimeter-to-inradius level is a connected arc, yielding global injectivity; the isosceles endpoints and isolated equilateral minimizer are covered. The resulting three side lengths come directly from incircle tangencies. The standalone checker independently tests intersection integrals and monotone level arcs, but finite tests are not substituted for the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic risk

The exact quartic law and angular coefficient are explicitly *known* from the published short-pair-distance coefficient result. The new claim is that those three *local* coefficients reconstruct **every** triangle. This does not follow from the earlier all-distribution recognition theorem of Gates (1982), the generic full-profile theorem of O'Hara (2026), the direction-dependent covariogram results of Gasparyan–Ohanyan (2013), or the forward distance calculations by Bäsel (2012) and Pure et al. (2019): these provide different or strictly more data but no local finite-jet injectivity implication. The relevant published coefficient proof was read in full; selected primary-paper sections were inspected. Gates and Gasparyan–Ohanyan were available only in abstracts or summaries, leaving a specific residual risk of an earlier equivalent coefficient-level criterion.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a global, exception-free inverse geometric theorem for a natural observable, reducing an entire two-point distance distribution to just three local coefficients. Two recover size and perimeter while one angular statistic resolves the remaining triangle-shape ambiguity, a nontrivial fact that requires an analytic curvature-ratio theorem and connected level-set geometry. It materially strengthens full-profile reconstruction as an information requirement and supplies an explicit one-dimensional recovery procedure.

No noisy statistical estimation guarantees, quantitative condition number, or result for polygons with four or more sides is claimed. The novelty assessment relies on accessible full texts together with abstract-only historical sources; independent specialist review remains useful.
