# Translation characters of cyclic Latin-square clique complexes in all odd orders

## Finding
For every odd integer \(n\ge3\), define the cyclic Latin-square graph \(G_n\) on \(A=(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^2\) by joining distinct \((x,y),(u,v)\) exactly when \(x=u\), \(y=v\), or \(x+y=u+v\). Write \(K_n=\operatorname{Cl}(G_n)\) for its clique complex. The translation group \(A\) acts simplicially by \(t_{a,b}(x,y)=(x+a,y+b)\).

**Main equivariant theorem.** For the complex character \(\chi_{r,s}(a,b)=\exp(2\pi i(ra+sb)/n)\), the multiplicity in \(\widetilde H_2(K_n;\mathbb C)\) is
\[
 m_{r,s}=n-3+\mathbf1_{r=0}+\mathbf1_{s=0}+\mathbf1_{r=s}-\mathbf1_{(r,s)=(0,0)}.
\]
Here every index is taken modulo \(n\). Thus the invariant subspace has dimension \(n-1\); each nontrivial character on any of the three dual lines \(r=0\), \(s=0\), or \(r=s\) has multiplicity \(n-2\); and every other nontrivial character has multiplicity \(n-3\). For \(n\ge5\), every translation character occurs.

Equivalently, the top-homology character has the following complete integer-valued trace:
\[
\operatorname{tr}\bigl(t_{a,b}\mid\widetilde H_2(K_n;\mathbb Q)\bigr)=
\begin{cases}
(n-1)^3,&(a,b)=(0,0),\\
n-1,&(a,b)\ne(0,0)\text{ and }(a=0\text{ or }b=0\text{ or }a+b=0),\\
-1,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The underlying space is a wedge \(K_n\simeq\bigvee^{(n-1)^3}S^2\). The nonequivariant homology ranks for Latin-square graphs of order \(n\ge5\) are already established in earlier work [2]; **the translation character, not the ordinary Betti number, is the new assertion.**

## Assumptions and scope
This theorem concerns odd integers \(n\ge3\), not only odd prime or prime-power orders. All homology groups are ordinary simplicial homology with integer coefficients unless a field is specified. The complex is the **clique** complex of the *simple, undirected* graph, not its independence complex, neighborhood complex, or directed flag complex. The group action is simultaneous addition on the two cell coordinates, not arbitrary Latin-square autotopisms. Rational traces are exact integer traces of maps on rational homology. The quotient consequence \(\dim_\mathbb Q H_2(K_n/A;\mathbb Q)=n-1\) follows using a barycentric subdivision and finite-group rational transfer.

## Proof
**1. All maximal faces.** The \(3n\) row, column, and symbol lines are the sets \(\{(x,y):x=c\}\), \(\{(x,y):y=c\}\), and \(\{(x,y):x+y=c\}\). Every such line is a clique with \(n\) vertices. Every graph edge belongs to exactly one line: any two distinct points cannot agree in two of their three coordinates \(x,y,x+y\). A triangle not lying in one line has exactly one edge of each type; by moving its right-angled vertex to \((x,y)\), it has the unique form
\[
\{(x,y),(x+d,y),(x,y+d)\},\qquad d\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}.
\]
In this triangle the third edge joins the two points with the same sum. A fourth point joined to all three, while not on one common line, would have to equal both \((x+d,y-d)\) and \((x-d,y+d)\), requiring \(2d=0\) in \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). Since \(n\) is odd, this forces \(d=0\), a contradiction. Hence every noncollinear maximal face is one of the exactly \(n^2(n-1)\) distinct mixed triangles, and there are no noncollinear faces in dimension greater than two.

**2. Euler characteristic and two-dimensional collapse.** Let \(U\) be the union of the \(3n\) full line simplices. Parallel lines are disjoint; each pair from two different directions meets at exactly one vertex, and each of the \(n^2\) graph vertices lies in precisely three line simplices. Inclusion-exclusion therefore gives \(\chi(U)=3n-3n^2+n^2=3n-2n^2\). Every mixed triangle contributes a new two-simplex whose edges already lie in \(U\), so
\[
\chi(K_n)=3n-2n^2+n^2(n-1)=1+(n-1)^3.
\]
Choose a base vertex in each line. Inside each line, collapse every face \(\sigma\) of cardinality at least three not containing the base, together with the coface obtained by adjoining that base, in descending cardinality. Distinct lines intersect in at most one vertex; a face of cardinality at least three on one line belongs to no other full line or mixed triangle. Consequently these are elementary *free-face* collapses. What remains from a line is its star of edges and triangles through its base vertex, so the resulting entire complex is two-dimensional.

**3. Simple connectivity.** Let \(U_{xy}\) be the union of the row and column simplices alone. The cover by its \(2n\) convex simplices has nerve the complete bipartite graph \(K_{n,n}\) (distinct rows and columns meet in exactly one point). Hence its fundamental group is generated by boundaries of four-edge row-column rectangles. Each such rectangle is nullhomotopic in the full \(K_n\): decompose it into unit coordinate squares, whose diagonals from \((x+1,y)\) to \((x,y+1)\) are sum-coordinate edges; both halves are mixed triangles. Each long row or column segment may be replaced by a sequence of unit steps because the corresponding full line is a simplex. Moreover, any sum-coordinate edge lies in a mixed triangle with two row/column edges, so every loop in \(K_n\) is homotopic to one in \(U_{xy}\). Thus \(\pi_1(K_n)=0\). The two-dimensional collapse, vanishing first homology, and Euler characteristic show that \(H_2(K_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{(n-1)^3}\) and no other reduced homology survives. A simply connected two-dimensional CW complex with free second homology of rank \(b\) is homotopy equivalent to \(\bigvee^bS^2\): choose cellular representatives of a basis of \(H_2\), apply the Hurewicz theorem and Whitehead's theorem.

**4. Translation fixed-point Euler characteristics.** For a finite simplicial complex, the simplicial Lefschetz number equals the Euler characteristic of its geometric fixed set after subdividing to remove setwise stabilized faces. For \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\), the only nonempty faces stabilized by translation are those contained in an invariant complete line. Indeed, a stabilized mixed triangle that is not contained in a line would be an orbit of exactly three vertices under a translation of order three. Its three vertices then have the same pairwise difference direction, and thus must either all lie on one allowed line or fail to be pairwise adjacent: a mixed triangle cannot be stabilized. A translation preserves any line iff its vector belongs to the line's direction subgroup
\[
D_x=\{(0,b)\},\qquad D_y=\{(a,0)\},\qquad D_s=\{(a,-a)\}.
\]
These three subgroups intersect pairwise only at identity. A nonidentity vector in any one of them preserves all \(n\) lines of that type and no other line. The geometric fixed set on each invariant full simplex is a nonempty convex simplex (fixed barycenters of finite orbits), and the \(n\) invariant line simplices are disjoint. Hence the Lefschetz number of that translation is \(n\); for a nonidentity translation outside the three direction subgroups, it is zero. At identity it is \(1+(n-1)^3\). Since the only nonzero homology groups are \(H_0\cong\mathbb Z\) and \(H_2\cong\mathbb Z^{(n-1)^3}\), subtracting the degree-zero trace \(1\) gives exactly the displayed trace formula.

**5. Orthogonality yields the multiplicity formula.** The trace character as a function on \(A\) has the group-ring expression
\[
T=-\mathbf 1+n\bigl(\mathbf1_{D_x}+\mathbf1_{D_y}+\mathbf1_{D_s}\bigr)+n^2(n-3)\mathbf1_{\{0\}}.
\]
For any \(\chi_{r,s}\), apply \(m_{r,s}=n^{-2}\sum_{g\in A}T(g)\overline{\chi_{r,s}(g)}\). The three line-subgroup sums are \(n\mathbf1_{r=0}\), \(n\mathbf1_{s=0}\), and \(n\mathbf1_{r=s}\), respectively, and the whole-group sum is \(n^2\mathbf1_{(r,s)=0}\). Substitution proves the stated multiplicity formula. Summing all multiplicities gives \((n-1)^3\), as required by the homology computation.

## Verification
The independent, standalone `verify_latin_translation.py` uses only Python's standard library. For each \(n\in\{3,5,7\}\), it constructs the graph from the coordinate equality rules, exhaustively enumerates all maximal cliques, confirms they agree exactly with the three line families and mixed triangles, reconstructs all simplices, evaluates signed simplicial Lefschetz numbers for every translation, and checks complex Fourier multiplicities against the closed formula. Finite tests corroborate the uniform proof and are not claimed as an all-order proof. Run `python3 verify_latin_translation.py`; expected final line is `VERIFY_OK_CYCLIC_LATIN_CLIQUE_TRANSLATION_CHARACTER`.

## Relationship to prior work
Besharati–Goddyn–Mahmoodian–Mortezaeefar [1] investigate circulant Latin-square graphs and their chromatic properties, providing a pre-2016 primary account of this canonical graph family. Cioabă–Mim [2], Theorem 3.12, **already determine the ordinary clique-homology ranks over arbitrary fields**, with \(\dim H_2=(n-1)^3-I(M)\) for a Latin square \(M\) of order at least five. The odd cyclic Latin square has no intercalates because \(2d=0\) implies \(d=0\). Thus the Betti number in the present paper is covered by [2]; it is included only to fix the graded homology group on which translations act. Neither inspected source provides the three-direction trace formula or the multiplicity decomposition of the natural regular translation action. Those equivariant statements distinguish characters that the ordinary Betti number cannot recover.

## Limitations
The theorem does not address even \(n\), where intercalate triangles and four-cliques alter the face structure; it does not classify the whole automorphism group or determine integral module splitting over \(\mathbb Z[A]\). The character decomposition is over \(\mathbb C\), while all displayed translation traces are integral. No assertion of independence or externally certified verification is made. A later or unindexed equivalent character calculation is possible despite the inspected comparison.
