# Translation characters of the Shrikhande independence complex

## Finding
Write \(A=(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^2\) additively and let \(S\) be the Shrikhande Cayley graph with connection set
\[
D=\{(1,0),(3,0),(0,1),(0,3),(1,1),(3,3)\}.
\]
Let \(K=\operatorname{Ind}(S)\), and let \(t_{a,b}\) translate vertices by \((a,b)\). The complete rational translation character on \(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Q)\) is, with rows indexed by \(a\) and columns by \(b\), each from \(0\) to \(3\),
\[
\bigl(\operatorname{tr}(t_{a,b}\mid\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Q))\bigr)_{a,b}
=\begin{pmatrix}
23&-1&-1&-1\\
-1&-1&3&3\\
-1&3&-1&3\\
-1&3&3&-1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Equivalently, define \(\chi_{p,q}(a,b)=\mathrm i^{pa+qb}\), where \(\mathrm i^2=-1\). Its multiplicity matrix, with row \(p\) and column \(q\), is
\[
\bigl(m_{p,q}\bigr)_{p,q}=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&1&1&1\\
1&2&2&1\\
1&2&1&2\\
1&1&2&2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
In particular the space of translation-invariant degree-two homology classes is **two-dimensional**. Over \(\mathbb Q\), the whole module splits as
\[
\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q[A]\oplus\mathbf1\oplus V_{1,1}\oplus V_{1,2}\oplus V_{2,1},
\]
where \(\mathbf1\) is the trivial module and \(V_{p,q}\) is the two-dimensional rational representation whose complexification is \(\chi_{p,q}\oplus\chi_{-p,-q}\). The previously determined homotopy type is \(K\simeq\bigvee^{23}S^2\); the new assertion is the **representation**, not a new claim to that nonequivariant homotopy classification.

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, simple, undirected; all homology actions are induced by simplicial translations. The nonempty independent sets are the nonempty faces. Vertices \((i,j)\) correspond to bit positions \(4i+j\), with coordinate arithmetic modulo four. The group is the full translation subgroup \(A\) only, not the full graph automorphism group. Rational or complex coefficients are used for character decompositions; the integral homotopy type quoted above is an existing result. No extrapolation to other Cayley graphs or larger group tables is made.

## Proof
**Independent homotopy and cell inventory.** Exhausting all \(2^{16}\) bit masks gives the independent-set face vector \((16,72,96,16)\), with no larger faces. The 16 four-vertex faces are exactly the cosets of four order-four subgroups of \(A\): three cyclic groups generated by \((1,2)\), \((1,3)\), \((2,1)\), and the Klein four-group generated by \((2,0)\), \((0,2)\). Each subgroup has four cosets. The included finite verifier checks the equality as exact sets of bit masks, not only their counts. An exhaustive acyclic face-poset matching has 88 pairs among 200 nonempty faces, leaving one vertex and 23 two-cells; the directed Hasse test checks all 496 cover relations and removes all 200 nodes in a topological sort. By the acyclic-matching theorem this independently establishes the earlier homotopy calculation \(K\simeq\bigvee^{23}S^2\), including \(\widetilde H_i(K;\mathbb Q)=0\) for \(i\ne2\).

**Signed fixed-face Lefschetz formula.** For any translation \(g\), the simplicial action on the oriented chain basis contributes \(\operatorname{sgn}(g\mid_F)\) exactly when the simplex \(F\) is setwise fixed. Including the augmented empty-face term, the reduced Lefschetz number is
\[
\widetilde L(g)=-1+\sum_{\substack{\varnothing\ne F\in K\\gF=F}}
(-1)^{|F|-1}\operatorname{sgn}(g\mid_F).
\]
The alternating trace on rational reduced chains equals that on rational reduced homology. Since reduced homology is supported only in degree two, \(\widetilde L(g)=\operatorname{tr}(g\mid\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Q))\). This identity is an equality of exact finite traces, not an approximation using numerical eigenvalues.

**Classify translation-fixed faces.** The identity has reduced Lefschetz value \(-1+16-72+96-16=23\). A nonzero involution has eight fixed two-vertex faces, each exchanged by a transposition and contributing \((-1)^1(-1)=+1\). It also fixes eight four-vertex faces, each permuted by two disjoint transpositions and contributing \((-1)^3(+1)=-1\); no odd-cardinality face is fixed. Thus all three nonidentity involutions have trace \(-1+8-8=-1\). A translation of order four fixes no nonempty face of size below four. Its fixed four-vertex faces are cosets of the cyclic subgroup it generates, precisely four if that subgroup is one of the three listed above, and none otherwise. Each fixed four-vertex face is cyclically permuted, contributing \((-1)^3(-1)=+1\). The six nonzero elements in those three cyclic groups therefore have trace \(3\); every other order-four translation has trace \(-1\). These cases exhaust the 16 group elements and give the displayed character matrix.

**Fourier inversion and rational decomposition.** Since \(A\) is finite abelian, the complex isotypic multiplicities are exactly
\[
m_{p,q}=\frac1{16}\sum_{a=0}^3\sum_{b=0}^3
\operatorname{tr}(t_{a,b}\mid\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb C))\,\mathrm i^{-pa-qb}.
\]
Evaluating these sixteen finite sums gives the displayed nonnegative integral multiplicity matrix, whose entries sum to 23. Specifically the trivial character occurs twice, each other character occurs once except the six characters indexed by \((1,1),(3,3),(1,2),(3,2),(2,1),(2,3)\), which occur twice. The six extra complex characters occur in three conjugate pairs that each form a two-dimensional rational module \(V_{p,q}\). The baseline sixteen characters form the regular module, and the second trivial character contributes \(\mathbf1\). This proves the asserted rational decomposition with total dimension \(16+1+3\cdot2=23\).

## Relationship to prior work
The nonequivariant Shrikhande calculation \(\operatorname{Ind}(S)\simeq\bigvee^{23}S^2\) was already published, including a direct comparison with the rook graph and an explicit Morse proof. Our character calculation does not reclaim that conclusion: its genuine extra information is **the action of all sixteen translations on the 23-dimensional homology group** and the complete isotypic decomposition. Earlier work on the four-by-four chessboard complex determines the entire rook-graph side as a symmetric-group module; it does not imply the Shrikhande module because the latter is a different graph complex. The published scale-two Čech results for these graphs concern a different nerve even when a numerical Betti rank agrees. The independence-complex theory developed by Barmak supplies general topological context but no covering translation-character statement. Peeters' analysis of Shrikhande cocliques supports the natural four-set geometry but does not give a character of homology. These differences were compared by mathematical implications, not only by titles or parameter matches.

## Limitations
The computed group is only the natural 16-element translation subgroup and not the full 192-element automorphism group. The conclusion determines the rational representation; it does not determine the integral lattice structure as a module over \(\mathbb Z[A]\). The proof is an exact finite calculation using a reproducible face enumeration and established Morse and Lefschetz theorems, not a general formula for arbitrary even-order Latin-square graphs. An unindexed equivalent character calculation cannot be categorically excluded, and no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
