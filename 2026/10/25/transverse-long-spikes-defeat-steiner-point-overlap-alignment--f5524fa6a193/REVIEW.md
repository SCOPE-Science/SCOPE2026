# Scientific review: transverse long-spike Steiner alignment

## Correctness — PASS
The finding concerns compact full-dimensional cones-over-unit-balls, with independently optimized translations. The Steiner-point normalization is explicit. The support function of each long-spike body differs by at most one from that of its axis segment, making the center's axial displacement \(t/2+O(d)\). A tangent construction gives the exact transverse section radius \((t-z)/\sqrt{t^2-1}\) on the relevant axial interval. The bounded intersection \(D_d\) of orthogonal unit cylinders provides a common volume normalizer. Brunn--Minkowski and central symmetry show that arbitrary translates of these cylinders never intersect in larger volume than \(D_d\). The exact inclusions between scaled copies of \(D_d\) and the Steiner/competitively translated spike intersections prove both volume limits and their ratio. The bundled standalone script checks the analytic formulas and explicit quantitative bounds. No finite sampling is presented as proof.

## Originality — PASS, with bounded literature risk
The immediate motivating source, Feldman (arXiv:2608.04516v1, first public 2026-08-05), establishes the corresponding sharp planar constant \(4/9\) for the **centroid** selector and explicitly poses the comparison of selectors, mentioning the Steiner point in Question 20. Its statements on centroid cores, cones and higher-dimensional constructions do not cover a Steiner-point overlap asymptotic. The classical support-function definition of the Steiner point and the Brunn--Minkowski argument are acknowledged as known; neither alone implies the combined asymptotic overlap theorem. Nearby indexed findings on centroid-core geometry and Cartesian-product centering address centroid rather than the Steiner selector; results about optimal \(L_p\) matching use a different objective. Searches for alternate terminology including curvature centroid, Minkowski-additive selector, spike/cone centering, translative overlap and planar quarter bound did not identify a result implying the claim. Negative search evidence is not a proof of uniqueness; an unindexed classical selector/overlap argument remains a stated risk.

## Value — PASS
The recent source poses selection of an optimal rigid-motion-covariant center as an explicit open geometric problem and singles out the Steiner point as a natural candidate. The theorem gives a decisive negative comparison in the smallest nontrivial dimension and even with only **two** bodies: the Steiner selector's global guarantee is at most \(1/4\), whereas centroids guarantee more than \(4/9\) for each instance. Its dimension-uniform geometric mechanism, exact limiting volume ratio, and certified finite-parameter bounds distinguish it from a routine particular numerical example. It does not claim the exact unrestricted Steiner constant, and this restriction is essential.
