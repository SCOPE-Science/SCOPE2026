# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The two-cycle equations are derived exactly by restricting the published phase-difference map to each symmetry-fixed line. The analytic implicit-function theorem applies because the first equation has midpoint derivative \(-3/2\) at the origin; the remaining equation gives a uniquely determined analytic parameter function. Its positive quadratic coefficient proves an actual supercritical branch. On the entire quotient tangent plane, the parallel and transverse eigenvectors are explicit, and the return multipliers have coefficients \(-16\) at quadratic order and \(224/5\) at transverse quartic order. The attached symbolic checker reproduces the expansions and the full-map test confirms seven representative exact-equation numerical cases. Numerical checks are supporting rather than a substitute for the local analytic proof.

## Originality

PASS under the literature and record comparisons made. Buescu–Oliveira (arXiv:2610.19953v1) already supply the \(4/3\) loss-of-sink threshold; that threshold is explicitly prior. Their inspected statements do not construct the six axial period-two saddles or give the delayed transverse instability coefficient. The earlier three-clock paper (arXiv:2503.13230) and network analysis (arXiv:2610.10993) establish small-step Morse–Smale and Lyapunov results. The nearest related prior record for a bipartite sine network has a single critical mode and attracting cycles; the triangle is not bipartite and its critical mode is double. A separate record treats attracting prime-three travelling orbits at step \(57/50\), not the near-\(4/3\) reflection-fixed prime-two saddles. General symmetry theory may provide broader frameworks; it was not found to specialize to this coefficient in inspected accessible material, and a full historical priority search remains outstanding.

## Mathematical value

PASS. For frustrated odd rings the loss of the twisted sink is not covered by the standard descending-Lyapunov regime. The triangle is the first odd ring and has a double, symmetry-forced flip. Showing that the naturally emerging axial two-cycles are *not attracting* in the full quotient resolves a substantive stability question that a scalar period-doubling calculation would answer incorrectly. The exact positive transverse coefficient \(63/10\) reveals a second-order instability invisible to the leading linear return multiplier. No all-attractor classification or uniqueness off the reflection axes is asserted.

## Closest literature and remaining limits

Most relevant: Buescu–Oliveira, arXiv:2610.19953v1, §§2, 4 and 6; its explicit twist multiplier implies the already-known threshold, but its strict Lyapunov theorem concerns \(h<2/3\) on the triangle. Additional context: arXiv:2503.13230 and arXiv:2610.10993, for small coupling, and Chossat–Golubitsky, *Iterates of Maps with Symmetry*, for abstract equivariant bifurcation theory (abstract inspected; full paper not read). The theorem covers only six local reflection-fixed branches, not all nearby prime-two orbits. Historical priority under generic dihedral-map terminology is a residual uncertainty.
