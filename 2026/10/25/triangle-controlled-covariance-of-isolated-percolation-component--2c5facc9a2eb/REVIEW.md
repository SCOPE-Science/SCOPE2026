# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The variables and probability space are explicitly defined for every finite simple regular host of degree at least two. Exact indicator means and the pairwise joint event probabilities are recomputed by counting overlapping closed-edge constraints. All relevant classes (endpoint, exactly one adjacent endpoint, two adjacent endpoints, no adjacency) are exhaustive. The identity \(\sum_e c_e=3t(G)\), the discriminant factorization, the sign boundaries, and the hypercube scaling are checked from the displayed proof. Independently exhaustive rational enumeration verifies 24 finite graph/parameter strata; these finite checks do not establish the all-graph result by themselves.

## Originality — PASS on the inspected sources

Hulshof–Nachmias [1] analyze critical hypercube cluster geometry, not the mixed singleton/dimer covariance. McDiarmid–Scott–Withers [2, Eq. (2), Theorem 17, Section 6] establish means and fixed-parameter joint Poisson approximation, not exact finite mixed moments or their triangle-only host correction and one-root sign classification. The nearest public mathematical-index record on component/triangle covariance studies a different pair of statistics and does not imply the present law. Actual targeted searches revealed no equivalent statement, but failure to locate one is not a uniqueness proof. The Das thesis [4] discusses isolated-edge polynomials and could not be read in full through its restricted repository access: this remains a concrete historical-priority risk.

## Value — PASS

Singleton and dimer components are canonical low-order observables in percolation. The result exhibits an all-parameter dependence phase transition that is exactly universal for triangle-free regular hosts, while *host triangles and no other host motifs* shift its finite root. On the hypercube the crossover occurs precisely at the branching-scale parameter \(dp=1\) and yields the nontrivial normalized sparse-limit function \(\lambda(\lambda-1)e^{-3\lambda}\). Fixed-parameter joint-Poisson approximations by themselves do not resolve this crossover. The theorem is a self-contained structural second-moment statement, not a claim about larger-scale connectivity or the exact critical percolation probability.
