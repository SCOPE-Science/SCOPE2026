# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

An admissible face in CF3 of a graph join may meet at most two factors; a face meeting two factors must be an independent set in each. Thus CF3 is the union of P=union_{i<j}(A_i*A_j) and disjoint full factor simplices Delta_i, with P intersect Delta_i=A_i. The inclusion A_i into every adjacent A_i*A_j is nullhomotopic via a vertex in the opposite factor. In the cofibrant incidence graph-of-spaces diagram there are no higher composable morphisms, so the homotopy colimit splits using constant incidence maps. Its incidence graph first Betti number is binom(r-1,2); each singleton contributes r-2 unreduced suspensions, and all pairwise join vertex spaces remain. Attaching each contractible Delta_i along its nullhomotopic A_i inclusion contributes one further suspension. This derives the integral wedge of binom(r-1,2) circles, (r-1) suspensions per factor and all A_i*A_j. Works for disconnected independence complexes, r=2 and trivial factors. New full GF(2) simplicial enumerations for cycle combinations (4,4), (5,5), (4,4,4), (5,5,5) matched the predicted degrees; the all-graph statement follows from the homotopy-colimit argument.

## originality

PASS

Ghosh–Selvaraja arxiv:2602.09623 Proposition 3.2 concerns *disjoint unions* and gives a simplicial-join identity for clique-free complexes; Theorem 3.3 shellability applies to chordal families. Neither addresses *graph joins* with complete cross-factor adjacency. Kozlov/Adamaszek establish the independent complex of cycles, which is used and credited. An earlier multipartite matching construction is a specialization, not a theorem covering arbitrary triangle-free factors with nontrivial homotopy. Standard graph-of-spaces splitting technique is credited rather than claimed novel.

## value

PASS

An exact and general homotopy classification of a natural forbidden-triangle complex under an important graph operation converts arbitrary factor independence-complex topology into explicit integral suspensions and joins; it detects the universal degree-one wedge and infinite mixed-dimensional cycle families. It is an all-input structural theorem, not a table of small graphs or a reformulation of a known disjoint-union identity.
