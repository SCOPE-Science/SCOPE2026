# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The projective plane over F2 has seven points and lines of size three. Two distinct rational lines cover at most five points, so a tangent-filling smooth curve needs at least three distinct rational tangents/curve points. The binary quartic F=X^3 Z+Y^4+Y Z^3 has gradient (X^2Z,Z^3,X^3+YZ^2); a common projective zero of F and all derivatives cannot exist: derivative conditions force X=Z=0, then F forces Y=0. Its three rational tangents fill the Fano plane, proving the sharp lower bound. For the exhaustive classification, the 15-dimensional F2 coefficient space contains exactly 32767 nonzero scalar-normalized quartics. A fresh independently constructed incidence sieve enumerated all 2782 rationally nonsingular tangent-filling equations. Closing them under coordinate swap, cycle and shear (generators of GL3(F2)) produced 22 candidate orbits. On every representative, a unit-ideal Groebner calculation of (f,f_u,f_v) on each of three affine projective charts distinguishes geometric smoothness from mere F2-point regularity. Exactly 20 orbits, size 2656 equations, are geometrically smooth; 126 equations in two orbits fail geometric smoothness. The joint (number of rational points, number distinct rational tangent lines) counts are (3,3):364, (4,3):84, (4,4):1176, (5,4):252, (5,5):588, (6,6):168, (7,7):24. Exactly 616 of the 2656 are nonblocking point sets. Four minimum-three-point orbits have sizes 84,84,28,168. Their multiplicities are checked against full orbit profiles, not inferred merely from a sample. The original package also classifies all 279 projective orbits and 78 smooth orbits, whereas the independent checker targets the complete 2782-member candidate locus. No finite-extension cutoff is used for geometric smoothness.

## originality

PASS

The known complete classification of the underlying 78 smooth binary quartic projective classes is not claimed as original. The substantive result is the new complete tangent-filling sublocus, including its joint rational incidence, nonblocking split, and sharp three-point equality locus. This is not a restatement of a known marginal genus-three class census.

## value

PASS

Tangent-filling was introduced as a natural rational dual-plane incidence problem; smooth quartic canonical models over the smallest field form a meaningful finite complete classification. The 20-orbit locus, sharp point minimum, and separation of tangent covering from rational-point blocking are precise reusable genus-three finite-geometry facts. The finite size is not itself a defect because the census exhausts all objects in a natural space.
