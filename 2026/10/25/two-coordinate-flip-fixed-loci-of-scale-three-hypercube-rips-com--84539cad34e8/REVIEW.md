# Review of the geometric two-coordinate fixed-locus classification

## Correctness — PASS

The involution partitions cube vertices into pairs, and invariant simplex supports identify the geometric fixed space with an explicitly defined orbit-clique complex. The orbit compatibility criterion is derived from all four Hamming distances. The square-block argument covers all cliques for every dimension at least two, with each square block a simplicial \(S^3\), and all intersections occurring in the common edge thickening. The latter retracts to the one-skeleton of the smaller cube. Attaching sphere blocks through their circle intersections adds one three-sphere and one cubical square two-cell per square. The resulting cubical two-skeleton has free second homology and no fundamental group. The integral wedge and boundary cases follow. The independent finite checker confirms all orbit simplices, local face counts and mod-two Betti numbers for dimensions four through nine, but finite checking is not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with risk

Feng (arXiv:2305.07084) provides ordinary scale-three hypercube Rips homotopy, and Bendersky–Elia–Grbić (arXiv:2605.00705) address its cohomology. Galetto–Montaño–Wellner (arXiv:2606.20784v1) establish a stronger *rational group representation* classification than these earlier works, but their full manuscript's Main Theorem and Section 6 concern ambient homology modules and not the **geometric fixed subspace homotopy type**. The distinction is material: a rational character records Lefschetz numbers, which cannot infer a fixed locus's integral second and third homology separately. Exact two-coordinate fixed-locus comparisons were checked separately against accessible prior records; the earlier low-scale model determines the graph stage and the death of earlier first homology, but not the new degree-two and degree-three classes at scale three. Overlapping work not indexed or not inspected may still exist.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Geometric fixed sets carry equivariant topology beyond character data, especially when a symmetry creates new homology in two degrees. This all-dimension formula exhibits a cubical two-skeleton and precisely one three-sphere per square, making the geometric basis of these classes transparent and exposing the sharp transition from scale two. The fully specified dimensions, homotopy types and integral homology provide a natural structural refinement rather than an isolated numerical census.

## Boundaries

The assertion does not cover other Hamming scales, other involution weights, nor secondary equivariant group actions. Only self-review and reproducible finite computations have been completed.
