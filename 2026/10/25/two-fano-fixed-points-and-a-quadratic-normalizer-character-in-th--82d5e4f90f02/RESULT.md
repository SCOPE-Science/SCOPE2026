# Two Fano fixed points and a quadratic normalizer character in the seven-point intersecting-triples complex

## Finding
Let \(X=|\operatorname{Ind}(\operatorname{KG}(3,1))|\). The seven-cycle translating the underlying labels modulo \(7\) has a geometric fixed-point set consisting of exactly two points. They are the barycenters of the translation-orbit simplices generated by \(\{0,1,3\}\) and \(\{0,1,5\}\). The affine normalizer \(\operatorname{AGL}(1,7)\) preserves the two points separately for square multipliers and interchanges them for nonsquare multipliers. Thus its action on \(\widetilde H_0(X^{C_7};\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\) is the quadratic sign character. No other reduced integral homology occurs in the fixed space.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph \(\operatorname{KG}(3,1)\) has all \(35\) three-element subsets of \(\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z\) as vertices; two vertices are adjacent when they are disjoint. Its independence complex has as faces the pairwise-intersecting collections of triples. The seven-element translation group \(C_7=\langle t\rangle\) acts by \(t(A)=A+1\). Fixed points always mean points of the geometric realization, not vertices fixed individually. The normalizer acts through affine permutations \(x\mapsto ax+b\), with \(a\in\mathbb F_7^\times\) and \(b\in\mathbb F_7\).

## Proof
The translation action is free on the \(35\) triples, so there are exactly five vertex orbits of size seven. Choose representatives \(012,013,014,015,024\), where a three-digit string denotes the corresponding subset of \(\mathbb F_7\). The orbits of \(012,014,024\) each contain disjoint triples: the first is disjoint from its translate by \(3\), the second from its translate by \(2\), and the third from its translate by \(1\). None of those three orbits spans a simplex.

By contrast, the differences between distinct elements of \(\{0,1,3\}\) exhaust each of the six nonzero residues once; the same is true of \(\{0,1,5\}\). Therefore any two different translates of either triple meet in exactly one point. Each orbit spans a six-dimensional simplex. The two orbits cannot jointly span a simplex: \(\{0,1,3\}\cap\{2,4,5\}=\varnothing\), and \(\{2,4,5\}=\{0,1,5\}+4\).

If a point of a simplicial realization is fixed by a permutation group, its barycentric coordinates are constant along every orbit of vertices. Its support must therefore be a union of full vertex orbits, and that union must be a face. The preceding classification leaves only the two nonempty invariant faces, with no invariant face containing both. On each orbit-simplex, invariant barycentric coordinates necessarily equal \(1/7\). Hence the fixed-point space is precisely their two barycenters, with the discrete topology.

Any permutation normalizing the regular translation group is affine: conjugation sends translation by one to translation by some \(a\ne0\), and the image of zero then determines \(b\). Translation by \(b\) preserves each vertex orbit. Multiplication by \(2\) preserves each of the two admissible orbits; multiplication by \(3\) interchanges them. Because \(\mathbb F_7^\times=\langle3\rangle\) and its subgroup of squares is \(\langle2\rangle=\{1,2,4\}\), this is exactly the quadratic-residue action. The reduced degree-zero integral homology of a two-point space is the difference of its point-generators; swapping the points acts by \(-1\), proving the claimed character and vanishing in all other degrees.

## Verification
The standalone file `verify_fano.py` enumerates the \(35\) triples, their five translation orbits, orbitwise disjointness and compatibility, and the actions of all six nonzero multipliers and seven translations. Run `python verify_fano.py`; the script raises an assertion on any discrepancy. The analytic proof above independently certifies every orbit and normalizer step, including the geometric-realization fixed-point identification.

## Relationship to prior work
Feng's October 2026 work computes nonequivariant rational Betti numbers of the full family \(\operatorname{Ind}(\operatorname{KG}(3,k))\), including this first nontrivial seven-point case. It does not identify translation-fixed geometric realizations or the affine normalizer action; those are distinct equivariant invariants. The classical seven-point Fano plane explains why the two admissible orbits are natural: each is a seven-line Steiner triple system. Classification of labeled Fano planes alone does not state the fixed-point topology of the full independence complex. The present claim is only the specified fixed-locus classification, not a new calculation of the ambient Betti numbers.

## Limitations
The calculation concerns the order-seven translation subgroup and its normalizer on the seven-point triple complex. It does not determine the \(S_7\)-equivariant homotopy type of the ambient complex, its full equivariant homology, or fixed loci for other groups or larger ground sets. The two fixed points are barycenters, not original vertices. Literature coverage beyond the checked works is a residual novelty risk.
