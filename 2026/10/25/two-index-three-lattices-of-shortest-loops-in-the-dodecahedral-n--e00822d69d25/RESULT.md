# Two index-three lattices of shortest loops in the dodecahedral neighborhood complex

## Finding
Let \(D=GP(10,2)\) be the dodecahedral graph, and let \(K=\mathcal N(D)\) be its **ordinary open-neighborhood complex**: a nonempty vertex set is a simplex exactly when its members have a common neighbor in \(D\). Its integral first homology is
\[
H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{21}.
\]
Every simple loop of length at most three in the one-skeleton of \(K\) bounds, whereas precisely sixty unoriented simple four-edge loops occur, all induced, homologically nonzero and primitive. Consequently the homological one-systole (for unit edge lengths) is four.

The complete \(120\)-element graph-automorphism group of \(D\) separates the sixty shortest loops into **two orbits of thirty**, distinguished by the following intrinsic geometric property: of the four vertices supporting the loop, the original graph \(D\) induces either exactly one edge or no edges. Write \(L_1\) and \(L_0\) for the respective subgroups of \(H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\) generated by oriented cycles from these two orbits. Then
\[
H_1(K;\mathbb Z)/L_1\cong H_1(K;\mathbb Z)/L_0\cong\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z,
\qquad L_1+L_0=H_1(K;\mathbb Z).
\]
Both orbits therefore span all rational first homology yet **each misses an integral index-three sublattice**. Adjoining *any single* loop from the opposite orbit generates the whole integral homology group. Explicitly, twenty loops from one orbit together with one from the other can be chosen as an integral basis; the same is true with the two orbit roles exchanged.

## Assumptions and scope
Use vertices \(u_i=i\) and \(v_i=10+i\) for \(0\leq i<10\), with subscripts modulo ten. The graph \(D\) has edges \(u_i u_{i+1}\), \(u_i v_i\), and \(v_i v_{i+2}\), giving twenty vertices and thirty edges. A simplicial one-chain is oriented by increasing vertex order on each edge, and an oriented four-cycle is the sum of its four directed edges. Loops and orbit sizes are **unoriented**, while the generated subgroups allow either orientation. The statement concerns the integral homology of this one finite complex, not arbitrary cubic graphs or higher configuration spaces.

## Proof
**1. Chain complex and the systole.** The twenty open-neighbor triples are pairwise distinct and form all two-faces of \(K\). Since \(D\) contains no four-cycle, distinct neighbor triples share at most one vertex. Consequently all sixty edges are distinct and each belongs to exactly one filled triangle. Direct examination of the displayed graph gives a connected one-skeleton. Removing each two-face with one of its free edges produces a connected graph with twenty vertices and forty edges; thus \(K\simeq\bigvee^{21}S^1\) and \(H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{21}\). A triangle in the one-skeleton must equal one of the twenty neighborhood triples (verified by all \(\binom{20}{3}\) vertex triples); hence all three-edge loops bound. The one-skeleton has exactly sixty simple four-cycles, with sixty different vertex supports and no diagonal edge, as checked by the complete \(\binom{20}{4}\)-subset enumeration in the verifier. Their coefficients cannot be canceled by any nonempty boundary of two-faces: boundaries of distinct filled triangles have pairwise disjoint edge supports, while an induced square shares at most one edge with any single triangle. Each square therefore represents a nonzero class. Moreover, for an edge \(e\) of any square, its unique filled triangle has another edge \(f\) outside that square. The integral one-cochain with coefficient \(+1\) on \(e\), coefficient \(\pm1\) on \(f\) chosen to evaluate to zero on the oriented triangle boundary, and zero elsewhere is a cocycle pairing \(\pm1\) with that square. Thus every square class is primitive.

**2. Exhaustive symmetry.** The verifier enumerates all graph automorphisms as isomorphisms of the explicitly given twenty-vertex, thirty-edge graph. There are exactly \(120\). Applying each permutation to the sixty unoriented four-cycles gives exactly two orbits, each of cardinality thirty. The orbit supported on a four-set containing one original graph edge has representative \((0,2,4,12)\); the zero-edge orbit has representative \((0,2,13,19)\). Each orbit is preserved under the entire automorphism group, so each integral span \(L_j\) is an invariant sublattice.

**3. Integral quotient coordinates.** Choose the deterministic breadth-first spanning tree of the one-skeleton, rooting at zero and visiting neighbors in increasing order, as constructed by the verifier. It has nineteen edges; the remaining forty-one ordered cotree edges give integral coordinates for the graph's forty-one-dimensional cycle lattice. Let \(T\) be the \(41\times20\) integer matrix consisting of the oriented boundaries of all filled triangles, and \(S\) the \(41\times60\) matrix of the oriented square cycles. The verifier chooses twenty pivot rows \(P\), using exact rational row reduction of \(T^\mathsf{T}\), for which \(\det(T_P)=+1\). Let \(F\) be the complementary twenty-one rows. Because \(T_P\) is unimodular, the integral quotient by triangle boundaries is identified with \(\mathbb Z^{21}\) by the explicit formula
\[
q(c)=c_F-T_F T_P^{-1}c_P.
\]
The matrix \(Q=q(S)\) has twenty-one rows and sixty columns with integral entries. This description is an **isomorphism of integral lattices**, not just a rational homology calculation.

**4. Exact finite certificates.** Order the sixty square cycles lexicographically as in the accompanying checker; let \(Q_1\) and \(Q_0\) be the submatrices of its two thirty-column geometric orbits. The displayed reproducible calculation selects a twenty-one-column minor of \(Q_1\) with determinant \(+3\), and one of \(Q_0\) with determinant \(-3\). Independent modular Gaussian elimination gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_3}(Q_1)=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_3}(Q_0)=20.
\]
Thus in each case the orbit lattice has full rank and index dividing three (by the determinant), while the modular rank deficiency makes the index divisible by three. Both indices are exactly three. A mixed twenty-one-column minor, selecting twenty columns from one orbit and one from the other, has determinant \(+1\); reversing the orbit roles produces determinant \(-1\). Hence the two orbit lattices together generate integrally, with an explicit basis of the asserted form. Finally the graph automorphism group acts transitively within each orbit and preserves the other orbit's lattice. Since one opposite-orbit loop has nonzero image in its quotient \(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\), **every** opposite-orbit loop does; its image generates that quotient and therefore completes the lattice. The verifier prints all selected column indices, pivot rows, integer determinants and modular ranks.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_dodeca_square_lattices.py` with the dependencies declared in `requirements.txt`. The code builds the graph solely from the displayed edge rules, enumerates every simplex and four-cycle, enumerates the complete automorphism group, constructs signed cycles and an integral homology quotient, and checks the determinant and rank certificates using exact integer/rational arithmetic. In particular the calculation does not infer an integral index from a floating-point approximation or a rational Betti number. The checksum and dependency-independent definition of the graph are contained in this package; no unprovided input files are needed.

## Relationship to prior work
The open-neighborhood construction and its topological role are classical; Kahle's 2005 article uses it for random graphs, not for these orbit lattices. Shukla's theorem on graphs of maximum degree three proves the broad wedge-of-circles homotopy classification. For the dodecahedron, the rank twenty-one also follows immediately from its free-edge collapse and thus is **not** asserted to be new. The publicly available *Complete rational dodecahedral neighborhood-homology character* determines the entire rational \(A_5\times C_2\)-representation of the same twenty-one-dimensional group, but that rational decomposition does not determine the integral sublattice generated by a specified orbit of shortest loops: both index-three orbit lattices become the same full vector space after tensoring with \(\mathbb Q\). The new contribution is the geometric enumeration of systolic loops, their two orbit lattices, the sharp integral obstruction, and the twenty-plus-one basis property.

## Limitations
This is a finite, exact computation-supported theorem for one canonical graph. It does not classify longer loops, the full integral automorphism module, or a family of neighborhood complexes. Publication overlap has been checked against the accessible primary and mathematical-record material identified below; unindexed equivalent calculations remain a residual priority risk. Independent scientific validation has not been performed.
