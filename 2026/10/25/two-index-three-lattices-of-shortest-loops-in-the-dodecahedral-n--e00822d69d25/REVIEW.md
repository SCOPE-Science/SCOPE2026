# Review of the shortest-loop orbit-lattice theorem

**Correctness.** The object is the open-neighborhood simplicial complex of the explicitly labeled dodecahedral graph. The verifier reconstructs all twenty filled triangles and sixty edges, checks their edge-disjoint boundaries and connected one-skeleton, and enumerates precisely sixty induced four-edge loops. A simplicial collapse proves the integral first-homology rank is twenty-one; absence of unfilled triangles establishes the shortest-loop lower bound. The primitive claim has a direct integral cocycle proof. For the lattice calculation, the cotree coordinates identify the full integral graph-cycle group, a twenty-row triangle-boundary minor has determinant \(+1\), and therefore the twenty-one quotient coordinates are integral isomorphisms. Two twenty-one-column orbit minors have determinants \(+3\) and \(-3\); independent modular elimination gives rank twenty for each orbit modulo three. Mixed minors have determinants \(+1\) and \(-1\). These exact certificates establish both index-three quotients and the integral generation and basis assertions. The group-orbit enumeration runs through all 120 graph automorphisms.

**Originality.** The exact same-complex rational representation previously published for the dodecahedral neighborhood complex determines the twenty-one-dimensional rational homology representation, but not the index of sublattices generated by specified short geometric cycles. Shukla's theorem gives only the nonequivariant wedge-of-circles homotopy class, and Kahle's foundational treatment concerns the random-graph regime. The more general square-free graph-like neighborhood results give homotopy ranks rather than shortest-cycle orbit lattices. Comparisons included aliases for the dodecahedral graph, the generalized Petersen notation, the distance-two graph, and the open Lovász complex. No inspected stronger result directly implies the two index-three claims. Unindexed specialized calculations are a remaining priority risk.

**Mathematical value.** The dodecahedron is a canonical highly symmetric cubic graph; the four-edge systoles arise from a natural combinatorial metric on the usual Lovász complex. The two full-symmetry orbits have a sharp intrinsic geometric distinction—whether the support induces an original dodecahedral edge—and each orbit spans rational homology but fails integrally by exactly three. The need for one opposite-orbit loop isolates a genuinely integral obstruction that the known Betti number and rational character cannot detect. This is a structurally motivated, exact finite theorem, not an arbitrary parameter slice.

**Scientific limits.** The statement is restricted to this finite complex; the determinant and enumeration certificates require the provided executable dependencies. No classification of longer-cycle lattices or of the complete integral symmetry module is claimed. Absolute novelty cannot be established from indexing coverage alone.
