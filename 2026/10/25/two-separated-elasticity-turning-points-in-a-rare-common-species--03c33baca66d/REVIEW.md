# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The two-species independent-Poisson model gives \(S_\varepsilon(x)=2-e^{-x}-e^{-\varepsilon x}\) and a well-defined local elasticity for every \(x>0\). Direct differentiation gives the exact signed numerator in the proof. The proof partitions the **entire** positive real axis (not a finite numerical grid), using a uniform negative early segment, a locally strictly increasing early crossover, a uniformly positive middle segment, a locally strictly decreasing late crossover, and a uniformly negative tail. This establishes **exactly** two simple critical points and the strict derivative signs for all sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\). Expansions at the early root and convergence at the late root give the displayed location and height laws. The 2,000 algebraic consistency checks and five computed pairs supplement these arguments; they are not infinite proofs. No smallest admissible \(\varepsilon\) is computed.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Tjørve, Kunin, Polce and Calf Tjørve (2008, DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2745.2008.01433.x) **already showed two-species nonmonotone shapes from sharply different abundances**. Their full accessible article—including two-species artificial cases and discussion—was read. Turner and Tjørve (2005, DOI 10.1111/j.2005.0906-7590.04273.x) was likewise read and discusses varying species–area slopes across scale. Neither inspected text establishes the present globally exact two-turning-point classification for a continuous independent-Poisson model in the extreme rare-species limit or its double-log early scale and universal late peak. Searches for equivalent formulas, mathematical curve mixtures, and the numerical peak constant identified no explicit stronger source. These statements are narrower than a claim to discover S-shaped species–area curves. Risk remains because the complete Coleman (1981) and Coleman et al. (1982) texts were unavailable, and an unrelated elasticity-of-exponential-mixtures theorem might cover this specialization. Unlocated earlier priority cannot be ruled out.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The relative-abundance mechanism is already ecologically motivated: the common species saturates at small survey area while a rare species becomes detectable much later. The exact two-critical-point theorem rules out unobserved extra reversals, identifies **two qualitatively different** survey-area scales—\(\log(1/\varepsilon)+\log\log(1/\varepsilon)\) and \(1/\varepsilon\)—and gives a universal limiting secondary local exponent of \(0.2319609529\ldots\). These are structural quantitative limits on local slope interpretation, not merely a restatement of the expected-richness formula or an arbitrary numerical instance. The result makes no claim about actual field biodiversity beyond its clearly stated model.

## Closest literature and remaining limits

The 2008 article is the closest conceptual predecessor and explicitly anticipates shape reversals. The 2005 scale-dependence account confirms their ecological relevance. Original Coleman's placement analysis remains incompletely available. The newly proved asymptotic threshold is existential, and all conclusions are confined to expected richness for two independent homogeneous Poisson species.
