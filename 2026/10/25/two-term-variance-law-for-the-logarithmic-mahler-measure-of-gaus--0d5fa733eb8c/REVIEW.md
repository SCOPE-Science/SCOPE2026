# Scientific review

Correctness — PASS. The Gaussian logarithm-Laguerre covariance identity yields the exact dilogarithmic variance; an exact Parseval identity isolates the leading linear term, and a uniform integrable bound controls the nonlinear term through the \(n^{-3}\) correction. Boundary behavior at zero, tail truncation, quantifiers and positivity are explicit in the proof. Twenty-one numeric/integer identities were checked, but the proof does not rely on their exhaustive force.

Originality — PASS with residual historical-priority risk. The 2023 Fekete Mahler measure theorem (arXiv:2306.07156v1) treats character-coefficient polynomials and a non-Gaussian limit, whereas the present statement is an exact Gaussian finite-\(n\) variance and two-term expansion. Kiro–Nishry (DOI:10.1112/jlms.12457) give the underlying Gaussian logarithmic covariance and results on zero-count variance, not this circle-integrated observable's sharp second term. Choi–Mossinghoff (DOI:10.2140/pjm.2011.252.31) gives a distinct unimodular coefficient model and mean/concentration conclusions. Direct searches for Gaussian Mahler variance, covariance equivalences and Dirichlet-kernel dilogarithms found no covering source; search nonmatches alone are not a proof of priority.

Value — PASS. The logarithmic Mahler measure is a natural geometric mean of the boundary modulus and is relevant to random complex polynomial zeros. The result establishes the exact leading noise scale, detects a structurally forced missing second order, and quantifies the first nonzero correction by a positive convergent integral. It goes beyond the elementary mean and beyond a mere convergence-in-probability statement.

Scientific limitations include the independent circular Gaussian assumption, absence of a central limit theorem, lack of a quantitative remainder beyond the stated little-o term, and unresolved possibility of a differently stated older equivalent formula. Numerical constants are approximations, not rigorous interval certificates.
