# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

For the explicitly stated autonomous two-mass equations, eliminating the translation coordinate gives exactly the stable cubic \(s^3+gs^2+s+\theta g\). The derivation tracks the normalization \(g=\gamma/(m_1\omega)\), \(\omega^2=k(1/m_1+1/m_2)\) and the open domain \(0<\theta<1\), \(g>0\). Parameterizing a real eigenvalue \(-r\) yields the exact damping map and conjugate-pair decay function. Its folds, its real-part crossing and its unique decay maximum give all three branches, including their shared endpoints. At the two defective boundaries the result is explicitly about spectral exponents, not pure-exponential bounds at the precise optimum. Independently generated physical four-state eigenvalues, symbolic identities and 3,210 sampled numerical comparisons agreed with the formulas. These samples test rather than prove global optimality; the calculus and root-classification argument supplies that proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

General optimal-damping theory and exceptional-point effects in coupled systems are well established. Freitas and Lancaster address minimization over an unrestricted damping matrix, not a unique optimizer under the rank-one grounded-dashpot constraint with a free translation mode. Main and Krenk give approximations and structural examples rather than a displayed two-mass exact mass-fraction phase diagram in their accessible abstract. The accessible full text of Dolfo and Vigué adopts anelastic rather than velocity damping; it has a different oscillator topology. A recent treatment of unrestricted two-degree-of-freedom critical damping likewise optimizes a different admissible class. Specific literature and mathematical-record searches did not return the two thresholds, the exact three-regime optimizer, or an equivalent specialized theorem. This is best-knowledge priority only: historical articles on damper sizing and lumped-system energy absorption could contain equivalent calculations, and some full texts could not be inspected. The absent source access does not establish novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A single viscous damper is a common restricted mechanical design; fastest asymptotic relaxation is a natural, quantitatively distinct objective from forced resonance suppression. The sharp mass fractions \(1/9\) and \(1/5\) separate three different optimizer types: a defective root collision, an equal-real-part bottleneck without collision, and the slow-complex-pair maximum. The full necessity, uniqueness, boundary and exact tuning formulas are not a direct scalar critical-damping substitution. The result is useful for deciding whether searching for an exceptional point is correct under a physical one-dashpot constraint.
