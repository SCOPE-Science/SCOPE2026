# Review of finite periodic-spectrum entropy non-identifiability

## Correctness (C): PASS
The theorem quantifies over all finite horizons, arbitrary positive uniform distance and arbitrary entropy target. An explicit dyadic interval is disjoint from its first \(P-1\) images, so no period below \(P\) can enter the surgically modified region. The new map coincides with the tent map on every old short orbit, establishing exact coincidence of embedded periodic-orbit collections, not merely the counts. The alternating \(M\)-lap branch generates a full \(M\)-horseshoe for the \(P\)-th iterate, while a cloned finite Markov graph with positive \(2P\)-th power proves exactness. The stated uniform-distance estimate is immediate from the image diameter. Twelve exact-rational parameter tests support the construction, independently of its universal proof.

## Originality (O): PASS subject to residual priority risk
Fuhrmann–Gröger–Passeggi's October 2026 full text, especially Theorems A–C, Corollary 4.13 and Section 5, concerns equality of *entire* embedded orbit spectra; it does not establish the present fixed finite-prefix, arbitrarily large entropy and arbitrarily small uniform-distance construction. Ruette's 2019 full-text construction preserves Sharkovskii period *type*, not the embedded sets of every short-period orbit. Searches of public research records covering period spectra, interval surgery, state cloning and entropy did not yield an implication covering this construction. A broader classical surgery theorem under different terminology remains a reasonable residual priority risk; searches are not a proof of uniqueness.

## Value (V): PASS
The recent complete-orbit rigidity result raises a concrete inverse-data question: how much can be inferred from any feasible finite observation horizon? The theorem gives a strong structural negative boundary, retaining the entire spatial short-orbit information within a topologically exact, piecewise uniformly expanding class and allowing arbitrary uniform proximity to the reference map. It separates qualitative infinite-data identifiability from every fixed finite-data entropy estimate, rather than adding a parameter example or merely reproducing a Sharkovskii type.

## Limitations
The number and size of slopes are unbounded as entropy increases, and there is no claimed stability with a common Lipschitz or smoothness bound. Exact numerical checks cover finite samples only; the proof gives the all-parameter conclusion. Priority is not independently certified.
