# Uniform ordinarity of the smooth Wiman–Edge pencil in characteristic five

## Finding
Let \(k\) be any algebraically closed field of characteristic \(5\). Consider the classical Wiman–Edge pencil of plane sextics
\[
W+tQ=0,\qquad W=X^6+Y^6+Z^6+(X^2+Y^2+Z^2)(X^4+Y^4+Z^4)-12X^2Y^2Z^2,
\]
\[
Q=(X^2-Y^2)(Y^2-Z^2)(Z^2-X^2).
\]
**Theorem.** For every \(t\in k\) with \(t\ne0\) and \(t^2\ne2\), the plane sextic is irreducible and has exactly four ordinary nodes, located at \([1:\pm1:\pm1]\). Its smooth normalization \(C_t\) has genus \(6\) and is **ordinary**: the Jacobian has \(5\)-rank \(6\). In fact the three genus-two involution quotients are isomorphic to
\[
D_t:\quad Y^2=B_t(r)(B_t(r)-A(r)),
\quad A(r)=3r^2+2r+3,\quad
B_t(r)=2r^3+(1-t)r^2+(1+t)r+2,
\]
and the norm and pullback maps give an isogeny
\[
\operatorname{Jac}(C_t)\sim\operatorname{Jac}(D_t)^3.
\]
For the genus-two hyperelliptic model, the coefficient-form Cartier–Manin matrix has determinant \(t^8\). Thus the ordinary locus is the *entire* smooth genus-six locus of this one-parameter characteristic-five model. The excluded parameter \(t=0\) is a degenerate, rational normalization; \(t^2=2\) yields a nontransverse branch degeneration. This is a statement about the displayed explicit model in characteristic five, not a claim that the characteristic-zero symmetry representation specializes without changes.

## Assumptions and scope
All calculations are in \(k\) with \(5=0\); no finite-field cardinality or hypothesis about roots of unity is needed. The normalization of the plane curve is taken before computing genus, Jacobians or ordinarity. Throughout \(t\in k^\times\) and \(t^2\ne2\). The pencil point at infinity, given by \(Q=0\), is excluded. The statement is geometric and valid over an algebraic closure of any finite extension of \(\mathbb F_5\).

## Proof
**1. A rational intermediate quotient.** Write \(a=X^2\), \(b=Y^2\), \(c=Z^2\). Direct expansion modulo \(5\) gives
\[
W=2(a+b+c)^3+abc.
\]
The Klein four-group generated by coordinate sign changes modulo simultaneous global sign fixes \((a:b:c)\); its quotient is the plane cubic
\[
E_t:\quad 2(a+b+c)^3+abc+t(a-b)(b-c)(c-a)=0.
\]
It has an ordinary double point at \((1:1:1)\). In the chart \(c=1\), put \(a=1+rv\), \(b=1+v\). The cubic becomes exactly
\[
v^2\bigl(A(r)+vB_t(r)\bigr)=0,
\quad A(r)=3r^2+2r+3,
\quad B_t(r)=2r^3+(1-t)r^2+(1+t)r+2.
\]
The quadratic \(A\) has two distinct roots, since its discriminant is \(3\in\mathbb F_5^\times\). The resultant \(\operatorname{Res}_r(A,B_t)=2t^2+1=2(t^2-2)\) is nonzero in the stated range, so the normalization of \(E_t\) is \(\mathbb P^1_r\), parametrized by \(v=-A/B_t\), and there are no further singularities of \(E_t\).

**2. The nine branch points and genus.** On the normalization, the three coordinate functions are
\[
\frac{a}{c}=\frac{D_t(r)}{B_t(r)},\qquad
\frac{b}{c}=\frac{C_t(r)}{B_t(r)},\qquad
C_t=B_t-A,\quad D_t=B_t-rA.
\]
The three cubics \(B_t,C_t,D_t\) have simple, pairwise disjoint zero sets when \(t\ne0\) and \(t^2\ne2\). Here is an exact resultant certificate in \(\mathbb F_5[t]\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Res}(B_t,C_t)&=t^2-2, &
\operatorname{Res}(B_t,D_t)&=2-t^2, &
\operatorname{Res}(C_t,D_t)&=t^2-2,\\
\operatorname{Res}(B_t,B_t')&=-t^2(2t^2+1), &
\operatorname{Res}(C_t,C_t')&=-t^2(2t^2+1), &
\operatorname{Res}(D_t,D_t')&=t^2(t^2-2).
\end{aligned}
\]
The two independent square roots of \(a/c\) and \(b/c\) define a connected degree-four Klein-four cover \(C_t\to\mathbb P^1\), branched simply at exactly these nine points. At each branch point, two of the four sheets have ramification index \(2\). Riemann–Hurwitz gives
\[
2g(C_t)-2=4(-2)+9\cdot2=10,
\quad g(C_t)=6.
\]
The quotient cubic has only its one ordinary node, and the sign-cover is étale there. Its inverse image gives the four ordinary plane nodes \([1:\pm1:\pm1]\). Away from these nodes, the separated simple branch points show the plane sextic is smooth. This also establishes geometric irreducibility.

**3. Three genus-two quotients.** Each of the three nontrivial involutions of the Klein four-group has quotient genus two, because it corresponds to a double cover of \(\mathbb P^1_r\) branched at the six zeroes of one pair from \(B_t,C_t,D_t\). The involution \(X\mapsto-X\) fixes \(b/c=C_t/B_t\). Multiplying the square root by \(B_t\) gives the displayed model \(D_t:Y^2=B_tC_t\). Cyclically permuting \((X,Y,Z)\) preserves \(W\) and \(Q\), so the other two genus-two quotients are isomorphic to \(D_t\). Since the full Klein-four quotient has genus zero, character idempotents (or pullback and norm maps) produce an isogeny
\[
\operatorname{Jac}(C_t)\sim\operatorname{Jac}(D_t)^3.
\]
The group order \(4\) is prime to characteristic \(5\), so this isogeny transfers Newton slopes and ordinary rank.

**4. Uniform Hasse–Witt determinant.** Put \(h_t=B_tC_t\) and \(h_t(r)^2=\sum_j c_j(t)r^j\). For a genus-two hyperelliptic curve in characteristic \(5\), a coefficient-form Cartier–Manin matrix is \(M_t=(c_{5i-j})_{1\le i,j\le2}\), with the usual fifth-root Frobenius semilinearity understood. Direct multiplication in \(\mathbb F_5[t,r]\) yields
\[
M_t=\begin{pmatrix}
t^4-t^3+t&2t^3-2t\\
2t^3-2t&t^4+t^3-t
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\det M_t=t^8.
\]
Thus \(D_t\) is ordinary for every permitted nonzero \(t\); the isogeny makes \(C_t\) ordinary with rank \(3\cdot2=6\). This proves the theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Edge introduced and studied the pencil of four-nodal plane sextics (1981), including its genus-six geometric structure. Candelas–de la Ossa–van Geemen–van Straten (2012) identify the same pencil in a Dwork-quintic context. Dolgachev–Farb–Looijenga (2018) and Zamora (2018) study its characteristic-zero geometry and degenerations. Giulietti–Kawakita–Lia–Montanucci (2018) explicitly state that the *single Wiman sextic* \(t=0\) has rational normalization in characteristic five. Those geometric facts are inputs or complementary statements, not new claims here. The additional point here is the global characteristic-five ordinary-locus computation and the explicit parameter-uniform genus-two Cartier determinant. We located no statement among the inspected works determining the ordinary status of every nondegenerate member of this pencil in characteristic five; an unnoticed equivalent reduction or prior Hasse–Witt computation remains a priority risk.

## Limitations
No claim is made for \(t=0\), \(t^2=2\), or the pencil point at infinity, where genus/singularity behavior changes. No assertion is made about the complete arithmetic zeta functions, canonical model over a non-algebraically-closed base, or isomorphism classes of the genus-two quotients as \(t\) varies. The known characteristic-zero automorphism theory is not assumed in characteristic five. The full historical novelty of this specific characteristic-five calculation has not been independently certified.
