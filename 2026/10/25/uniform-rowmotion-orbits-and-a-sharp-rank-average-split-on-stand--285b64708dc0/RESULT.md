# Uniform rowmotion orbits and a sharp rank-average split on standard-example posets

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\), the standard-example poset \(S_n\) has rowmotion cycle polynomial
\[
C_n(t)=\bigl(2^n-2n-2\bigr)t^2+t^3+nt^5.
\]
In particular, all order-ideal rowmotion orbits have lengths two, three, or five. The rowmotion permutation has order 15 for \(n=3\) and 30 for every \(n\ge4\), even though the order dimension of \(S_n\) is \(n\).

More precisely, for the cardinality statistic \(I\mapsto |I|\), the unique three-cycle and all \(n\) five-cycles have orbit average \(n\). When \(n\ge4\), the \(2^n-2n-2\) two-cycles split equally between orbit averages \(n/2\) and \(3n/2\). At \(n=3\) all orbits average \(n\); at every larger order, even orbits of the same size have different averages.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\). The finite height-two poset \(S_n\) has minimal elements \(a_i\) and maximal elements \(b_i\) for \(i\in[n]\), with relations \(a_i<b_j\) if and only if \(i\ne j\). No other strict relations exist. An order ideal \(I\) is encoded by \((A,B)\), where \(A=\{i:a_i\in I\}\) and \(B=\{i:b_i\in I\}\). Classical rowmotion \(\rho\) sends \(I\) to the down-set generated by the minimal elements of \(S_n\setminus I\). Throughout \(n\ge3\). Distinct orbits mean orbits of labeled order ideals, without quotienting by automorphisms. The orbit average is \(|\mathcal O|^{-1}\sum_{I\in\mathcal O}|I|\).

## Proof
An ideal \((A,B)\) is valid precisely when \([n]\setminus\{j\}\subseteq A\) for every \(j\in B\). Thus all ideals are the disjoint collection:

1. \((A,\varnothing)\) for arbitrary \(A\subseteq[n]\);
2. \(([n]\setminus\{i\},\{i\})\) for \(i\in[n]\);
3. \(([n],B)\) for every nonempty \(B\subseteq[n]\).

This gives \(2^n+n+(2^n-1)=2^{n+1}+n-1\) ideals.

Write \(N=[n]\). From the definition of rowmotion, the minimal elements of the complement consist of the missing minima \(a_i\) for \(i\in N\setminus A\) and precisely those missing maxima \(b_j\) whose lower neighbors \(a_i\) for \(i\ne j\) all lie in \(I\). Generating their down-set determines \(\rho\) without any external theorem.

First, the following forms one three-cycle:
\[
(\varnothing,\varnothing)\longmapsto(N,\varnothing)
\longmapsto(N,N)\longmapsto(\varnothing,\varnothing).
\]
For each \(i\in N\), there is one distinct five-cycle:
\[
(\{i\},\varnothing)\longmapsto(N\setminus\{i\},\varnothing)
\longmapsto(N,\{i\})\longmapsto(N,N\setminus\{i\})
\longmapsto(N\setminus\{i\},\{i\})
\longmapsto(\{i\},\varnothing).
\]
The case \(n\ge3\) ensures that these states from different indices do not coincide.

For every subset \(S\subseteq N\) with \(2\le|S|\le n-2\), rowmotion also satisfies
\[
(S,\varnothing)\longleftrightarrow(N\setminus S,\varnothing)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(N,S)\longleftrightarrow(N,N\setminus S).
\]
Both are two-cycles, with each pair indexed twice by the complementary choices of \(S\). These cases cover all ideals: the listed small and co-small subsets are in the three- and five-cycles, and every remaining eligible subset has cardinality between two and \(n-2\). This is an exhaustive constructive partition, not a finite-order extrapolation.

There are \(2^n-2-2n\) subsets of \(N\) in the eligible cardinality range. Hence each of the two disjoint two-cycle families has \((2^n-2-2n)/2=2^{n-1}-n-1\) cycles, yielding the stated total. The sum of orbit sizes is \(3+5n+2(2^n-2n-2)=2^{n+1}+n-1\), independently checking completeness. The least common multiple of the present lengths is 15 at \(n=3\); for \(n\ge4\), two-cycles exist and the order is 30.

For cardinality averages, the three-cycle has ranks \(0,n,2n\), with mean \(n\). The five-cycle for index \(i\) has ranks \(1,n-1,n+1,2n-1,n\), summing to \(5n\). An all-minima two-cycle has ranks \(|S|,n-|S|\), hence mean \(n/2\). A full-minima two-cycle has ranks \(n+|S|,2n-|S|\), hence mean \(3n/2\). This proves the rank-average split and the exact boundary at \(n=3\).

## Verification
The standalone programs `rowmotion_direct.py` and `rowmotion_toggles.py` reproduce the cycle structure and orbit rank averages by different finite methods. Run `python3 rowmotion_direct.py` and `python3 rowmotion_toggles.py`; compare stdout to `direct_output.txt` and `toggle_output.txt`. The first constructs rowmotion directly from minimal missing elements for \(3\le n\le11\). The second enumerates every lower-set bitmask and applies all-vertex toggles in reverse linear-extension order for \(3\le n\le8\). Their agreement tests the implementation, but the above explicit partition and case-by-case transitions are the proof for every \(n\ge3\).

## Relationship to prior work
The standard example \(S_n\) and its dimension are classical; Biró–Hamburger–Pór–Trotter provide a definition and context in arXiv:1402.5113. Striker–Williams, arXiv:1108.1172, define and study classical rowmotion, with global results for other structured poset families. Grinberg–Roby, arXiv:1402.6178, study birational rowmotion and skeletal posets; \(S_n\) for \(n\ge3\) is not a complete height-two skeletal poset. Elizalde–Plante–Roby–Sagan, arXiv:2108.12443, study fence rowmotion and orbit-statistic homomesy, a different poset family. The present statement is specifically the complete labeled orbit classification and exact cardinality averages for \(S_n\); neither knowing its dimension nor having generic rowmotion definitions implies these formulas. Earlier general antichain-orbit papers by Cameron–Fon-der-Flaass remain a potential source of overlapping methods or results.

## Limitations
This result concerns only classical order-ideal rowmotion on \(S_n\) for \(n\ge3\). It does not classify birational or piecewise-linear rowmotion, or rowmotion of arbitrary height-two posets. The claimed orbit classification is proved internally but independent expert audit and exhaustive publication-priority verification have not been performed. Full text of the 1995 general antichain-orbit paper was not accessible during this literature comparison; an equivalent unindexed classification remains possible.
