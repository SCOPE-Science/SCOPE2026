# Uniform sphere-wedge homotopy type of multipath complexes of bidirected cycles

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge6\), the multipath complex \(X_n=X(B_n)\) of the bidirected cycle \(B_n\) on \(n+1\) vertices is a wedge of spheres, with exact integral homotopy type
\[
X_n\simeq\bigvee^{r_n}S^{d_n}\vee S^{n-1}\vee S^{n-1},
\]
where \(r_n=3\) if \(n\equiv3\pmod4\), and \(r_n=1\) otherwise, and
\[
d_n=\begin{cases}\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor+1,&n\equiv0\pmod4,\\ \lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor,&n\not\equiv0\pmod4.\end{cases}\]
The two top-dimensional generators come from the boundaries of the clockwise and counterclockwise oriented edge simplices. For example, the first four new parameters give \(X_6\simeq S^2\vee2S^5\), \(X_7\simeq3S^3\vee2S^6\), \(X_8\simeq S^4\vee2S^7\), and \(X_9\simeq S^4\vee2S^8\). This determines an infinite family left open at the level of homotopy in the motivating paper.

## Assumptions and scope
Label the vertices of the undirected cycle by \(\mathbb Z/(n+1)\mathbb Z\). The directed graph \(B_n\) has both arcs \(e_i^+=(i,i+1)\) and \(e_i^-=(i+1,i)\) for each cyclic index \(i\). A face of \(X_n\) is a set of arcs inducing disjoint directed simple paths, possibly with isolated vertices. A directed 2-cycle is not a path. Define \(L=n+1\) and \(m=L-2=n-1\). The statement concerns ordinary integral homotopy type, not an equivariant equivalence.

## Proof
**1. Two geometric sphere generators and exhaustive top-chain support.** Encode a face by a cyclic word of length \(L\) over \(\{+, -,0\}\), choosing at most one of the two arcs on each undirected edge. Nonzero neighboring signs cannot differ: otherwise the intervening graph vertex has indegree two or outdegree two. Conversely, this local condition, together with the exclusion of the all-positive and all-negative words, characterizes the faces: every proper consistently directed run is a directed path, and the only directed cycles in the underlying simple cycle of length \(L\ge7\) are the full uniformly oriented cycles (the forbidden two-cycles have already been excluded).

If a face uses both signs, it must contain at least two zeros, because the cyclic sign runs must have two separating gaps. Hence it has at most \(L-2\) arcs and simplex dimension at most \(L-3=m-1\). Therefore **every** \(m\)-simplex consists of precisely \(L-1\) arcs of just one orientation. For either choice of sign, all proper subsets of the full set of \(L\) arcs occur, whereas the full set is a directed cycle and is excluded. These pure-sign subcomplexes \(S_+\) and \(S_-\) are the boundaries \(\partial\Delta^{L-1}\cong S^{L-2}=S^m\). The \(m\)-chain group of \(X_n\) is exactly the direct sum of the two top-chain groups generated by their facets, with no other top-dimensional chains and no chains in dimension \(m+1\). Moreover the images under \(\partial_m\) of the two summands have disjoint supports, since the corresponding codimension-one pure faces use disjoint signed arc sets. The usual simplex-boundary calculation gives a rank-one integral kernel in each summand, generated primitively by the alternating boundary of its missing top simplex. Consequently,
\[
H_m(X_n;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[S_+]\oplus\mathbb Z[S_-],
\]
and the two *actual sphere inclusion maps* give an integral basis in top homology. This geometric realization, rather than the mere rank of top homology, is the essential additional input.

**2. Lower homology and connectivity.** Caputi--Collari--Smith [1, Theorems 2.8 and 2.10] establish that \(X_n\) is \(\nu_n\)-connected for \(\nu_n=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor-1\), and compute its complete reduced integral homology. In addition to the two top summands above, the only nonzero reduced group is \(H_{d_n}(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{r_n}\), with \(d_n,r_n\) as stated. For \(n\ge6\), \(\nu_n\ge1\), so \(X_n\) is simply connected. If \(n\not\equiv0\pmod4\), the stated bound already says it is \((d_n-1)\)-connected. If \(n\equiv0\pmod4\), the bound gives \((d_n-2)\)-connectivity, but \(d_n\ge4\) and the calculated group \(H_{d_n-1}\) is zero. The Hurewicz theorem at degree \(d_n-1\) thus gives \(\pi_{d_n-1}=0\). In either case, \(X_n\) is \((d_n-1)\)-connected, and the Hurewicz map \(\pi_{d_n}(X_n)\to H_{d_n}(X_n;\mathbb Z)\) is an isomorphism.

**3. Realization of a homology equivalence by spheres.** Choose \(r_n\) based maps \(S^{d_n}\to X_n\) whose Hurewicz images form an integral basis in lower-degree homology. Choose two based versions of the inclusions \(S_+,S_-\hookrightarrow X_n\), using whiskers to a common basepoint if needed. Their wedge gives a map
\[
 f:\bigvee^{r_n}S^{d_n}\vee S^m\vee S^m\longrightarrow X_n.
\]
By Steps 1 and 2 this map induces an isomorphism in **every** integral homology degree. Both spaces are connected simply connected CW complexes, so the homology form of Whitehead's theorem makes \(f\) a homotopy equivalence. This proves the asserted integral homotopy type for all \(n\ge6\).

## Verification
The geometric top-face lemma is proved for arbitrary \(n\) above, and depends only on the explicit arc definition. A standalone Python verifier enumerates **all** faces for \(n=6,7,8,9,10,11,12,13\), confirms that the only top facets are the \(2(n+1)\) pure directed paths missing one edge, constructs a lexicographic face-poset matching, and exhaustively verifies its directed-Hasse-diagram acyclicity and critical-cell dimensions. These finite checks corroborate the general statement but do not constitute its infinite proof. Run `python3 verify.py`; expected output is in `verification_output.txt`.

## Relationship to prior work
Caputi, Collari and Smith [1] derive the complete **integral homology groups** and connectivity of these complexes and explicitly leave their wedge-of-spheres homotopy question open (their introduction and Section 3). Neither the homology ranks nor simple connectivity alone forces a sphere wedge: higher attaching maps can remain undetected by homology. The new step is to show that the entire highest-dimensional homology has explicit sphere representatives, allowing Hurewicz and the homology Whitehead theorem to realize an actual homotopy equivalence. Earlier same-family exact computations treated only \(n\in\{3,4,5\}\); these parameters are excluded from the new theorem. Existing dihedral character and orbit-space calculations concern distinct equivariant or quotient invariants and do not imply the stated integral homotopy equivalence. See the review for remaining bibliographic limitations.

## Limitations
The theorem assumes \(n\ge6\), uses the already-published connectivity and integral-homology theorems [1], and does not supply a new independent derivation of their chain-level homology computation. It supplies no canonical choice of lower-dimensional Hurewicz generators, no equivariant splitting, and no statement about arbitrary digraphs. The finite executable check cannot certify the infinitely quantified result; that follows from the written argument and the cited published theorems.
