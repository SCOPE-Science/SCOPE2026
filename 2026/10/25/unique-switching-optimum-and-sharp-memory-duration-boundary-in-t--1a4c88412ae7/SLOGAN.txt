The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck Ising–Kuramoto linear-response threshold has exactly one best nonnegative spin-switching rate, and this rate is strictly positive precisely below a unique memory-time boundary when \(0<\mu/\beta_s<D\). At small memory the optimal switching rate diverges as \(\gamma_*\sim \tau^{-1}\sqrt{1-\mu/(\beta_s D)}/[1-\sqrt{1-\mu/(\beta_s D)}]\), whereas sufficiently long memory makes no switching optimal.
