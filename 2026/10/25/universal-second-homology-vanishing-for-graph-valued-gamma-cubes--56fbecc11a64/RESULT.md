# Universal second-homology vanishing for graph-valued \(\Gamma\)-cubes

## Finding
For every finite simple undirected graph \(G\) with no isolated vertices, regarded as a \(2\)-uniform hypergraph, the normalized integral \(\Gamma\)-cubical homology recently defined using Hamming-radius-one hypercubes satisfies
\[
H_2^{\Gamma}(G;\mathbb Z)=0.
\]
The same assertion holds over every abelian coefficient group \(A\): \(H_2^{\Gamma}(G;A)=0\). This is a structural property specific to the \(\Gamma\)-hypercube domain; it does not assert that the distinct ordinary discrete (\(\Box\)) cubical homology of graphs always vanishes in degree two.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(G=(V,E)\) be any finite loopless simple undirected graph with every vertex incident to an edge. Its hyperedges are the two-element edge subsets. In dimension \(k\geq1\), the \(\Gamma\)-hypercube has vertices \(\{0,1\}^k\) and, for each vertex \(w\), a hyperedge consisting of \(w\) and its \(k\) Hamming-distance-one neighbors. A singular \(\Gamma\)-cube is a map to \(V\) taking each such source hyperedge into some edge of \(G\). Normalize the free abelian cubical chains by quotienting cubes constant in any coordinate. Use the standard alternating cubical boundary: for the face at coordinate \(i\) fixed to \(s\), write \(F_{i,s}\); then \(\partial=\sum_{i=1}^k(-1)^{i-1}(F_{i,1}-F_{i,0})\). All chains here are finite integer linear combinations.

## Proof
**Lemma 1 (unique-edge support in degree two).** The source \(\Gamma\)-hypercube in dimension two has four vertices; each of its four hyperedges contains exactly three of them. Let \(f\) be a singular two-cube. If the image of \(f\) contained three distinct vertices, some three source vertices would have three distinct images. These source vertices constitute one of the four hyperedges, whose image could not lie in a two-element edge of \(G\). Therefore \(f\) has at most two image values. A normalized nondegenerate two-cube cannot be constant, so its image is precisely the endpoints of one (necessarily unique) graph edge.

**Lemma 2 (one-edge contraction in degree two).** Let \(e=\{a,b\}\) be a hyperedge viewed as a two-vertex hypergraph. Every map from any \(\Gamma\)-hypercube into \(e\) is singular, because each image of a source hyperedge is a subset of \(e\). Fix a vertex \(a\). For a normalized \(k\)-cube \(f\) of \(e\), define a prism \(P_kf\) on \(\{0,1\}^{k+1}\) by
\[
(P_kf)(t_1,\ldots,t_k,0)=f(t_1,\ldots,t_k),\qquad (P_kf)(t_1,\ldots,t_k,1)=a.
\]
The prism is always singular and respects degeneracies. In dimensions \(k\geq1\), the fixed-final-coordinate \(1\) face is degenerate, so the alternating face formula gives
\[
\partial P_kf-P_{k-1}\partial f=(-1)^{k+1}f.
\]
In particular, for any normalized two-cycle \(z\) supported on \(e\), one has \(z=-\partial P_2z\). Thus the degree-two homology of the one-edge target vanishes integrally.

**Conclusion.** By Lemma 1, the normalized degree-two chain group of \(G\) decomposes as a direct sum \(C_2^{\Gamma}(G)=\bigoplus_{e\in E}C_2^{\Gamma}(e)\), where each summand consists of the two-cubes whose images are the endpoints of \(e\). Similarly, the normalized degree-one chain group decomposes as a direct sum over graph edges (one summand generated by the two orientations of each edge). The boundary \(\partial_2\) preserves these edge summands. Hence every two-cycle \(z\) splits uniquely as \(z=\sum_{e\in E}z_e\) with \(\partial z_e=0\) separately. Apply Lemma 2 to each \(z_e\):
\[
z=-\partial\Big(\sum_{e\in E}P_2 z_e\Big).
\]
The expression is a valid normalized \(\Gamma\)-three-chain in \(G\), proving the integral vanishing. The same explicit integral chain contraction works after extending scalars to any abelian group \(A\), proving the coefficient assertion. No claim about \(H_k^{\Gamma}\) for \(k\ge3\) follows from this proof, because higher-dimensional singular cubes need not be supported on a single edge.

## Verification
The accompanying standard-library script `verify_gamma_graph_h2.py` reconstructs normalized singular cubes and their signed boundaries. It checks the integer prism identity on all ten nondegenerate two-color square maps; checks edge localization for every two-cube in the tested graphs; and independently computes the mod-two boundary ranks in degrees two and three for all simple graphs with at most four vertices and six additional five-vertex graphs. In the executed check, all 81 graphs had zero second mod-two homology, and all 2,330 admissible two-cube generators were uniquely edge-supported. The universal integral conclusion rests on the preceding proof rather than extrapolation from finite checks.

## Relationship to prior work
Zaidi and Jamil introduced the \(\Gamma\)-hypercube and the resulting normalized homology in October 2026 (arXiv:2610.08196). The present statement uses their construction and gives a universal degree-two vanishing theorem on the full subcategory of ordinary graphs. It is distinct from the already established degree-zero and degree-one comparison between \(\Gamma\)-homology and simplicial-closure homology. It is also distinct from conventional discrete cubical graph homology, for which nonzero degree-two classes are known: see Barcelo–Greene–Jarrah–Welker's graph cubical-homology investigations. The particular obstruction here is the three-vertex hyperedge geometry of the two-dimensional \(\Gamma\)-source cube, which localizes every two-chain to individual graph edges.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns a finite \(2\)-uniform target graph without isolated vertices and exactly the \(\Gamma\)-source cubes; it does not assert vanishing for general hypergraphs or for other cubical theories, nor in dimensions three and above. The foundational preprint's introduction and basic definitions were inspected, but its complete primary text was not available during the literature comparison; an equivalent theorem in its uninspected sections or other literature remains an explicit originality risk.
