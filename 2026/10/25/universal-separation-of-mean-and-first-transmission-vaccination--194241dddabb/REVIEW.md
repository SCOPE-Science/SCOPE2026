# Scientific review: path vaccination objectives

## Correctness — PASS
The mean identity follows from independent transmission along a unique path and discrete convexity; the first-secondary-transmission probability is a direct local-degree expression. The strict objective gaps distinguish every non-optimal deletion. The complete lower-bound classification uses the two trees of order four and smaller path cases. The exact rational verification program agrees with all 162 finite test cases, but only the proof covers unbounded size and continuous parameter ranges.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, not independently certified)
Yuan et al. (2015; DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0115826) already compare expected epidemic size and tail events in a two-population vaccination model. Holme (2017; arXiv:1708.06456) analyzes mean-outbreak node vaccination on small graphs with a different SIR process. Neither inspected original text gives the present joint all-path optimizer classification for independent bond transmission or its sharp five-vertex tree boundary. Searches under outbreak, non-singleton cluster and vaccine-placement aliases yielded no direct equivalent. An older equivalent statement remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The two vaccination objectives are both natural, and the theorem provides a permanent, transmissibility-independent disagreement on every path of order at least five, with a rigorous minimum-order boundary and explicit cost-versus-risk formula. The result is not an empirical public-health prediction; its assumptions on source sampling, transmission and perfect vaccination are essential.
