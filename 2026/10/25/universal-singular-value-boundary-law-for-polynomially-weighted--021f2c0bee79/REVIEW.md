# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The normalized radial Fock monomials give exact gamma-ratio weights for every monomial weighted dilation. Compact-scaled asymptotics and globally dominated Riemann sums establish the singular-value superlevel law and the all-\(p\) Schatten constants. Operator-norm and Schatten perturbation estimates eliminate every lower-degree polynomial term, including for \(0<p<1\). The strict inverse distribution gives the approximation-number curve. The two Lambert branches come from the two solutions of a unimodal scalar equation. The finite computational checks supplement rather than replace this argument.

## Originality — PASS (provisional priority)
Barik–Sen–Paul (2026) supplies the exact space and fixed-symbol boundedness, compactness and Hilbert–Schmidt results, including the monomial dilation example; it does not contain the scaled spectral curve. Mengestie–Takele (2023), Theorem 3.2, and Mengestie's earlier Schatten work already establish broad membership criteria, which are not claimed as new. The claimed new content is quantitative: the epsilon-dependent singular-value distribution, exact Schatten blow-up constants, two-branch level counting, and optimal rank profile, with universal leading-polynomial dependence. No covering statement was found in the inspected full texts. The 2013 and 2015 papers were accessible only in abstract/bibliographic form, leaving substantive publication-priority uncertainty; searches cannot certify uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The natural radial-power weighted composition family becomes singular as dilation tends to the identity. The theorem quantifies how many singular directions exceed a prescribed renormalized scale, an invariant directly governing finite-rank approximation cost. It identifies a two-ended mode population around a unique peak and sharp all-ideal blow-up rates. These data are not implied by a boundedness/compactness classification or by Schatten membership alone.

## Limits
Only fixed nonconstant polynomials and radial-power Hilbert Fock weights are covered; no claim is made about finite-epsilon correction terms, arbitrary entire weights, or independently certified priority. Numerical tests are finite.
