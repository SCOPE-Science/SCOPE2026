# Vertex-minimal spheres miss two integral homology directions in the icosahedral neighborhood complex

## Finding
Let \(I\) be the simple icosahedral graph on twelve vertices and let \(K=\mathcal N(I)\) be its ordinary **open** Lovász neighborhood complex. For a vertex subset \(W\subseteq V(I)\), let \(K[W]\) be the induced simplicial subcomplex. Define the vertex-support filtration on second integral homology by
\[
L_s=\sum_{|W|\le s}\operatorname{im}\bigl(H_2(K[W];\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\bigr).
\]
Then
\[
H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{23},\qquad
L_4=0,\qquad L_5\cong\mathbb Z^{21},\qquad
H_2(K;\mathbb Z)/L_5\cong\mathbb Z^2,\qquad L_6=H_2(K;\mathbb Z).
\]
Exactly sixty five-vertex subsets \(W\) carry a nonzero image in second integral homology. These subsets are precisely those for which the induced graph \(I[W]\) is a triangle with a pendant edge at each of two different triangle vertices. They form one orbit under the complete, order-\(120\) icosahedral graph-automorphism group. Each has a six-triangle bipyramid-sphere cycle representing a primitive nonzero class. Twenty-one of these classes can be chosen as a basis of \(L_5\); two additional octahedral-sphere cycles, each using six vertices and eight triangles, complete an integral basis of \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\).

The support threshold \(5\) is sharp for **existence of a nonzero class**, while \(6\) is sharp for **generation of the whole integral group by vertex-supported classes**. This is not the assertion that every individual class has a six-vertex representative.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph \(I\) has vertex labels \(0,\ldots,11\), adjacency data in `certificate.json`, and the standard combinatorial isomorphism type of the regular icosahedron. The complex is defined without taking closures of neighborhoods: a nonempty \(S\subseteq V(I)\) is a simplex exactly when \(S\subseteq N_I(v)\) for at least one graph vertex \(v\). The entire result concerns ordinary simplicial homology over \(\mathbb Z\), not field-only Betti numbers or an arbitrary geometric realization of a different graph complex. Chain groups use increasing vertex order and the signed simplicial boundary.

The symbols \(L_s\) describe images of *all* integral two-cycles supported on at most \(s\) vertices. Counts of vertex supports do not count orientations twice. The two six-vertex carriers below are particular explicit witnesses to generation; the theorem does not classify all six-vertex supports.

## Proof
The complex has face numbers \((f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4)=(12,60,120,60,12)\). The integer boundary matrices \(\partial_2\colon\mathbb Z^{120}\to\mathbb Z^{60}\), \(\partial_3\colon\mathbb Z^{60}\to\mathbb Z^{120}\), and \(\partial_4\colon\mathbb Z^{12}\to\mathbb Z^{60}\) are constructed by alternating deletion of one vertex. Their compositions vanish. Their ranks modulo \(101\) are respectively \(49,48,12\). Since \(\partial_3\partial_4=0\) and \(\partial_4\) is injective, the integral rank of \(\partial_3\) cannot exceed \(48\); its modular rank proves equality.

For each of the \(\binom{12}{4}\) four-vertex sets and \(\binom{12}{5}\) five-vertex sets, the exact rational nullspace of the restricted \(\partial_2\) is generated by integer cycles after clearing denominators. The integral kernel-basis certificate described below gives exact integer coordinates of each such cycle modulo the image of \(\partial_3\). This calculation shows that no four-vertex set carries nonzero ambient homology and that exactly sixty five-vertex sets do, each with a one-dimensional image. Their induced graph types are all the triangle-with-two-distinct-pendants shape stated above; exhaustive graph isomorphism and automorphism enumeration prove the classification and transitivity. Every selected five-vertex cycle has six triangles, with each edge occurring in two triangles, connected support, and Euler characteristic two, hence a bipyramid triangulation of the two-sphere.

Form a matrix \(A_5\) by adjoining the sixty oriented five-vertex sphere cycles to the sixty columns of \(\partial_3\). A deterministic modulo-\(101\) pivot rule selects \(48\) boundary columns and \(21\) sphere columns. These sixty-nine integral columns possess an explicitly listed sixty-nine-row square minor with determinant \(+1\). Consequently their lattice is saturated in \(\mathbb Z^{120}\), and the sublattice generated by all five-vertex cycles and all three-boundaries has rank \(69\). Thus, upon passage to second homology, the shortest-sphere lattice has rank \(21\) and has no torsion in its quotient.

Two additional six-vertex cycles are supported on
\[
\{0,1,2,3,4,7\},\qquad \{0,1,2,3,5,6\}.
\]
Their exact signed lists of eight triangles are in `certificate.json`. Each is an octahedral sphere cycle. Appending them to \(A_5\) gives \(A_6\), from which the deterministic pivot rule chooses \(48\) boundary columns, \(21\) five-vertex sphere columns, and both six-vertex sphere columns. Their seventy-one-row square minor has determinant \(+1\). These \(71\) cycles are primitive and independent in \(\mathbb Z^{120}\), and they lie in \(\ker\partial_2\). Therefore \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_2\le120-71=49\); combined with the modular lower bound, this proves \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_2=49\). The kernel is a saturated rank-\(71\) sublattice of \(\mathbb Z^{120}\); the seventy-one independent cycles with a unit maximal minor form its full integral basis.

The first forty-eight chosen columns are three-boundaries and span \(\operatorname{im}\partial_3\) integrally: they are a subset of a primitive basis and have the same rank as the full image. The remaining twenty-three columns project to a basis of \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\), proving its free rank \(23\) and the displayed quotient. The analogous sixty-nine-column unit minor proves the shortest-cycle subgroup is a primitive rank-\(21\) lattice. Since its chosen twenty-one sphere columns are primitive and the automorphism action is transitive, every one of the sixty shortest classes is primitive.

## Verification
Run `python verify_icosahedral.py certificate.json` with the versions specified in `requirements.txt`. The program constructs the graph and every simplex from common neighbors, verifies both boundary compositions, computes modular boundary ranks, constructs all candidate local cycles using exact rational nullspaces, and obtains an integer kernel-basis certificate from a determinant-\(+1\) minor. It then checks exact coordinates for **every** four- and five-vertex candidate, verifies the topological sphere carriers, enumerates the entire graph automorphism group, and checks the separate determinant-\(+1\) certificates for the size-five and size-six spanning lattices. It compares every computed certificate field against the embedded `certificate.json`.

The certificate records original triangle row indices, original column indices in \(A_5\) and \(A_6\), exact signed sphere chains, and the complete list of sixty five-vertex supports; all indexing uses lexicographically sorted faces. The computations use finite exact integer arithmetic for the decisive minors and integer coordinate checks. Modular ranks serve only as lower bounds where explicitly supported by integral arguments, not as evidence of torsion-freeness by themselves.

## Relationship to prior work
Kozlov's 2005 constructive equivalence of neighborhood complexes and graph-homomorphism complexes supplies the canonical context for \(\mathcal N(I)\) [1]. Kahle's 2005 work studies random-graph neighborhood connectivity and some sphere retractions, not the integral support filtration of this fixed icosahedral complex [2]. Csorba and Lutz investigate graph-coloring manifold families of Hom-complexes, again without the displayed support-lattice theorem in the inspected material [3].

Previously published calculations of this *same* icosahedral neighborhood complex establish its homotopy type as a wedge of twenty-three two-spheres and determine its rational icosahedral homology representation [4–6]. Those conclusions are **not** claimed here as new. They do not specify which induced vertex supports carry integral homology, the sixty transitive bipyramid carriers, or the rank-two free quotient of their geometric span. The new contribution is this sharp geometric and integral support-filtration statement.

## Limitations
This is an exact finite calculation for one canonical graph, not an all-graph theorem. The classification of all six-vertex homology carriers is not asserted. No independent external audit or formal proof-assistant certification is claimed. The relation to indexed prior mathematical results was checked, but unpublished or unindexed equivalent results cannot be categorically excluded. Earlier homotopy/representation results were compared at their recoverable claim level where complete linked documents were unavailable.
