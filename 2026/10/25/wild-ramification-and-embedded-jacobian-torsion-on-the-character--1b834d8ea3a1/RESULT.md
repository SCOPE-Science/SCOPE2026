# Wild ramification and embedded Jacobian torsion on the characteristic-two Steiner Roman quartic

## Finding

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(2\), and let
\[
X=V(F)\subset\mathbb P^3_k,\qquad
F=x^2y^2+y^2z^2+z^2x^2+xyzw.
\]
Then \(X\) is geometrically integral. Its finite birational normalization is
\[
\nu:\mathbb P^2_k\longrightarrow X,\qquad
[s:t:u]\longmapsto[tu:su:st:s^2+t^2+u^2].
\]
The reduced singular support consists of the three concurrent projective lines
\[
L_x=V(y,z),\qquad L_y=V(x,z),\qquad L_z=V(x,y),
\]
meeting at \(E=[0:0:0:1]\). Scheme-theoretically, its intrinsic Jacobian singular subscheme is reduced along all three punctured lines except for **one embedded length-three defect** at each of the points
\[
P_x=[1:0:0:0],\quad P_y=[0:1:0:0],\quad P_z=[0:0:1:0].
\]
More precisely, at \(P_x\), with affine coordinates \((y,z,w)\), its local coordinate ring has the presentation
\[
k[y,z,w]_{(y,z,w)}/(yz,yw,zw,y^2+z^2),
\]
whose torsion submodule over the reduced singular-line parameter ring \(k[w]_{(w)}\) is killed by \(w\) and has basis \(y,z,y^2=z^2\). The corresponding assertions hold at \(P_y,P_z\) by symmetry. At the common intersection \(E\), the Jacobian singular scheme is reduced and is the union of three coordinate axes.

On the source coordinate line mapping dominantly to each singular line, \(\nu\) induces a separable double cover \(\mathbb P^1\to\mathbb P^1\), which has exactly **one wild branch point**, the corresponding \(P_x,P_y\), or \(P_z\), and different exponent \(2\). The branch occurs where the two characteristic-zero pinching values coalesce modulo \(2\).

For every \(r\ge1\), the model over \(\mathbb F_2\) has
\[
\#X(\mathbb F_{2^r})=2^{2r}+2^r+2,
\qquad
Z(X/\mathbb F_2,T)=\frac{1}{(1-T)^2(1-2T)(1-4T)}.
\]
The point counts are a consequence of a torus-and-boundary decomposition and are not asserted to be characteristic-two-specific; the wild ramification and length-three Jacobian defects are the characteristic-two feature.

## Assumptions and scope

The characteristic is exactly \(2\). The assertions about singular schemes use the intrinsic second Fitting singular locus \(\operatorname{Fitt}_2(\Omega_{X/k})\), represented by \((F,F_x,F_y,F_z,F_w)\) in homogeneous coordinates; inclusion of \(F\) is essential when the degree is divisible by the characteristic. The three embedded lengths are local torsion lengths over the corresponding reduced line, not lengths of the entire one-dimensional singular subscheme. No assertion is made about an arbitrary projection of the Veronese surface or a different integral model of the Roman surface.

## Proof

**1. Integral equation and normalization.** Treat \(F\) as a polynomial of degree one in \(w\), with coefficient \(xyz\). The remaining coefficient \(x^2y^2+y^2z^2+z^2x^2\) is divisible by none of \(x,y,z\). Its greatest common divisor with \(xyz\) is therefore one. Gauss's lemma makes \(F\) absolutely irreducible. The displayed four quadrics have no common projective zero: if \(tu=su=st=0\), at most one of \(s,t,u\) is nonzero, and then \(s^2+t^2+u^2\neq0\). Direct substitution proves \(F\circ\nu=0\). On the dense open \(xyz\neq0\), the inverse to \(\nu\) is \([x:y:z:w]\mapsto[yz:xz:xy]\), proving birationality. Every fiber is finite: it is one point off the singular lines; over a singular line away from \(E\), it lies in the corresponding source coordinate line and has at most two points; over \(E\) it is the three coordinate vertices of \(\mathbb P^2\). A projective quasi-finite map is finite. Since \(\mathbb P^2\) is normal, this finite birational morphism is the normalization.

**2. Complete singular support.** In characteristic two the partial derivatives are
\[
(F_x,F_y,F_z,F_w)=(yzw,xzw,xyw,xyz).
\]
If \(xyz\neq0\), the last derivative is nonzero. If exactly one among \(x,y,z\) vanishes, the equation \(F=0\) becomes the nonzero square of the product of the other two. Thus the singular points have at least two of \(x,y,z\) zero, and every such point satisfies both \(F=0\) and all its derivatives. This proves the three-line support exactly over the algebraic closure.

**3. Scheme structure.** In the chart \(w=1\) containing \(E\), the singular ideal is \((xy,xz,yz)\), since \(F\) lies in this ideal. It defines three reduced concurrent affine coordinate axes. Along \(L_x\) in the chart \(x=1\), the Jacobian ideal is
\[
(yz,yw,zw,y^2+z^2),
\]
because \(F=y^2+z^2+y^2z^2+yzw\) reduces to \(y^2+z^2\) modulo \((yz,yw,zw)\). Localizing further where \(w\neq0\) gives \((y,z)\), hence a reduced line. At \(P_x\), the relations give \(yz=yw=zw=0\), \(y^2=z^2\), and \(y^3=z^3=0\). The quotient decomposes as a \(k[w]_{(w)}\)-module into \(k[w]_{(w)}\) and a torsion module generated by the independent classes \(y,z,y^2\); a lexicographic Gröbner basis is \(\{y^2+z^2,yz,yw,z^3,zw\}\). Thus the embedded torsion has length \(3\). The other two charts follow by permuting \(x,y,z\). These charts cover the singular support, ruling out any additional embedded defects.

**4. Wild branch structure.** The unique component of \(\nu^{-1}(L_x)\) dominating \(L_x\) is \(s=0\); the remaining source vertex \([1:0:0]\) maps to the common intersection \(E\) but is not on that component. The map on the source line \(s=0\) is
\[
[t:u]\longmapsto[tu:t^2+u^2]
\]
in target coordinates \([x:w]\). On the torus coordinate \(v=t/u\), the target affine coordinate is \(\lambda=w/x=v+v^{-1}\). The function field extension has separable degree \(2\), since \(v\) satisfies \(v^2+\lambda v+1=0\) and \(\lambda\) is transcendental. In characteristic \(2\), \(d\lambda/dv=1+v^{-2}\), so the unique finite critical point is \(v=1\). The points \(v=0,\infty\) are unramified above \(\lambda=\infty\). Near \(v=1\), with \(h=v+1\),
\[
\lambda=\frac{h^2}{1+h},
\]
so the ramification index is \(2\) at \(\lambda=0\), i.e. \(P_x\). Riemann--Hurwitz for this separable map \(\mathbb P^1\to\mathbb P^1\) gives total different degree \(2\), concentrated at this single point. For characteristic different from two, the analogous cover has branch values \(\lambda=\pm2\); they merge after reduction modulo two. The other lines are symmetric.

**5. Arithmetic.** Over each \(\mathbb F_q\) with \(q=2^r\), a point with \(x,y,z\neq0\) has uniquely determined \(w\), giving \((q-1)^2\) projective points. A point with exactly one of \(x,y,z\) zero cannot satisfy the equation. With at least two zero, the three lines give \(3q+1\) points. Hence \((q-1)^2+3q+1=q^2+q+2\). The claimed rational zeta function follows by exponentiating these counts over all finite extensions.

## Verification

The standalone \(check.py\) verifies the substitution identity and four partial derivatives in polynomial arithmetic modulo two, computes the local Gröbner-basis relations proving the three-dimensional embedded torsion, and independently enumerates all projective points over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_4,\mathbb F_8\). The exact enumerated pairs \((\#X,\#\operatorname{Sing})\) are \((8,7),(22,13),(74,25)\), respectively. The finite-field counts do not replace the algebraic arguments for geometric completeness or the infinite family of point-count identities.

## Relationship to prior work

Steiner's Roman quartic, its quadratic parametrization, three double lines, classical six pinch points, and triple intersection are classical. The author-hosted detailed exposition of Coffman, Schwartz, and Stanton provides precisely the homogeneous equation and parametrization used here. The distinct content isolated here is the characteristic-two **intrinsic embedded Jacobian torsion of length three at exactly three points**, coupled to the exactly one-point wild ramification on each normalized singular line. The normalization \(\mathbb P^2\) and the elementary point count are included as geometric context, not asserted as independently novel. Work on moving planes and rational parametrizations concerns general algorithms over real or complex coefficients and does not itself supply the characteristic-two Fitting-scheme computation. The possibility of an unlocated historical discussion of the same specialization remains a genuine originality risk.

## Limitations

The full 1996 paper of Coffman--Schwartz--Stanton and the full 2012 Wang--Chen paper were not available for direct inspection; the author-hosted Roman-surface treatment and a separate 2009 Chen--Wang arXiv paper were inspected. Consequently absence from all historical work is not certified. The singular-scheme statement concerns this exact projective integral equation. No independent specialist audit or claim about stable reduction is made.
