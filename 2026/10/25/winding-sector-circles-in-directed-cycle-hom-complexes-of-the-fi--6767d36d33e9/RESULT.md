# Winding-sector circles in directed cycle Hom complexes of the five-vertex tournament

## Finding
For every integer \(k\ge4\) such that \(5\nmid k\), let \(R_5\) denote the regular cyclic tournament with vertex set \(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z\) and arrows \(a\to a+1\) and \(a\to a+2\). Write \(\overrightarrow C_k\) for the consistently directed cycle on vertices \(0,\ldots,k-1\). Define the directed Hom complex \(X_k=\operatorname{Hom}(\overrightarrow C_k,R_5)\), whose cells are nonempty coordinate subsets \((A_0,\ldots,A_{k-1})\), with every element of \(A_i\) pointing to every element of \(A_{i+1}\), cyclically.

Define the finite integer set \(W_k=\{w\in\mathbb Z\mid k<5w<2k\}\). Then
\[
 X_k\simeq\coprod_{w\in W_k}S^1.
\]
More precisely, the displayed connected components are canonical winding-number sectors. Translation of target vertex colors gives a free \(C_5\)-action preserving each sector. Each sector is a connected five-sheeted regular cover of a discretized unordered configuration space of particles on a circle. Its quotient by target translation is homotopy equivalent to \(S^1\), and its covering projection has absolute degree five on \(\pi_1\) and on first integral homology. Consequently \(H_1(X_k;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{|W_k|}\), \(H_0(X_k;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{|W_k|}\), all higher homology vanishes, and every target translation acts trivially on first homology.

## Assumptions and scope
The full directed Hom polyhedral complex is used, not merely its recoloring graph, directed flag complex, or path homology. The integer restriction is \(k\ge4\) and \(5\nmid k\); no statement is claimed here about cycles whose length is divisible by five, or about other target tournaments. The target translation group is \(C_5\). Let \(UD_r(C_k)\) be the cubical complex of \(r\) unordered particles on the ordinary \(k\)-edge circle, with each occupied cell a vertex or open edge, and the closures of different occupied cells disjoint.

A vertex of \(X_k\) is uniquely represented by a color \(a=f(0)\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z\) and a cyclic word of steps \(d_0,\ldots,d_{k-1}\in\{1,2\}\), with \(d_i=f(i+1)-f(i)\) modulo five. Let \(r\) count the number of steps equal to two. The closure condition is
\[
 k+r\equiv0\pmod5.
\]
The integer \(w=(k+r)/5\) is its winding sector; because \(5\nmid k\), any such \(r\) satisfies \(0<r<k\).

## Proof
**1. Local cell structure.** Every nonempty coordinate set \(A_i\) has at most two elements, since an out-neighborhood in \(R_5\) has size two. If it has two, it must consist of consecutive residues \(\{b+1,b+2\}\), for some predecessor color \(b\). Both neighboring coordinates are then singletons (there is no directed complete bipartite subgraph with both sides of size two). Replacing the value at \(i\) by its other allowed choice interchanges the two adjacent increments \((1,2)\leftrightarrow(2,1)\). Hence no cell changes \(r\), and the varying coordinates of a cell are pairwise nonadjacent. Conversely, any selection of pairwise nonadjacent adjacent-step swaps produces exactly one cell when its starting color is fixed. Therefore the connected components split by \(r\), equivalently \(w\).

**2. Identification of the quotient cells.** Regard the sites \(i\) with \(d_i=2\) as occupied vertices of \(C_k\); a swap of neighboring steps is one occupied particle moving across an adjacent vacant site. A set of pairwise disjoint such moves is a cube, with exactly the faces prescribed by the Hom-cell coordinate products. Thus forgetting \(f(0)\) gives a cubical map
\[
 p_r:X_{k,r}\longrightarrow UD_r(C_k).
\]
Every base vertex and every base cube has exactly five lifts, uniquely reconstructed from \(f(0)\). The face identifications are compatible, so \(p_r\) is a regular five-sheeted cubical covering; its deck group is target translation by \(C_5\). The condition \(k+r\equiv0\pmod5\) is exactly what permits the reconstruction to close cyclically.

**3. The base has circle homotopy type.** For \(0<r<k\), the standard discretized graph-configuration equivalence, for a circle subdivided into at least \(r+1\) edges, makes \(UD_r(C_k)\) a deformation retract of the unordered configuration space \(U\operatorname{Conf}_r(S^1)\). This subdivision condition holds because \(r\le k-1\). The latter unordered configuration space is homotopy equivalent to a circle. One way to see this is the multiplication map \(\{z_1,\ldots,z_r\}\mapsto z_1\cdots z_r\) to \(S^1\): it has contractible open-simplex fibers and is a locally trivial bundle. In particular \(\pi_1(UD_r(C_k))\cong\mathbb Z\), generated by moving an unlabeled particle once past a chosen cut in the circle, with all particles remaining distinct.

**4. The covering is connected, with primitive monodromy.** Place the occupied sites initially at \(0,1,\ldots,r-1\). Move the particle at \(r-1\) through the vacant sites clockwise to \(k-1\); shift the particles at \(r-2,r-3,\ldots,0\) one position clockwise in that order; finally move the particle at \(k-1\) across the cycle cut to site \(0\). This is a closed loop of unordered configurations, with exactly one crossing of the cut, hence a generator of the fundamental group of the base. Before its last move, the steps at sites \(k-1\) and \(0\) are \((2,1)\); the last swap changes them to \((1,2)\). Keeping color \(f(k-1)\) fixed changes \(f(0)\) by \(-1\pmod5\). Thus the holonomy of that generator is a generator of the deck group \(C_5\). It follows that the five-sheeted covering is connected, that its total space is homotopy equivalent to \(S^1\), and that it induces multiplication by five (up to sign) on \(\pi_1\).

**5. Enumerating the sectors.** For each admissible \(r\), the integer \(w=(k+r)/5\) satisfies \(k<5w<2k\). Conversely every integer in that open interval gives the unique integer \(r=5w-k\) with \(0<r<k\), hence one connected component. Each circle component has the claimed homology; translation by a deck transformation preserves its orientation and therefore acts trivially on first homology.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `check_hom_r5.py` enumerates *all* cells for \(3\le k\le10\), reconstructs the winding sectors at every cell, checks each sector's exact cubical face counts, and calculates all mod-two and mod-three Betti numbers directly from the signed cellular boundaries. In the claimed range it returns: one circle at \(k=4\), one at \(k=6\), one at \(k=7\), two at \(k=8\), and two at \(k=9\), with no higher homology. Values \(k=3,5,10\) are tested only as boundary controls, not offered as new cases.

In particular, for a sector with \(r\) occupied step sites, the predicted number of \(q\)-cells is
\[
 F_q(k,r)=5M(k,q)\binom{k-2q}{r-q},\qquad M(k,0)=1,
\]
where \(M(k,q)=\frac{k}{k-q}\binom{k-q}{q}\) for \(1\le q\le\lfloor k/2\rfloor\), and out-of-range binomial coefficients are zero. Here \(M(k,q)\) counts disjoint edge sets of size \(q\) in the cycle. Finite calculations test, but do not replace, the preceding proof for all admissible lengths.

## Relationship to prior work
Dochtermann and Singh (arXiv:2108.10948, first public 2021-08-24) introduce directed Hom complexes and examine tournament examples, including directed triangle sources. The directed-triangle regular-tournament circle result is also known for source length three; this finding expressly starts at length four. Fujii, Iwamasa, Kimura, Nozaki and Suzuki (arXiv:2408.04802, first public 2024-08-09) show that ordinary *undirected* Hom complexes with a cycle as target are unions of circles and points. That result applies to a different morphism category and cannot directly determine the sectors or cyclic deck monodromy here.

There is a useful, nontrivial overlap that must be distinguished: the coordinate change \(g_i=2f(i)-3i\pmod5\) converts an arrow increment \(1\) or \(2\) into an undirected cycle increment \(-1\) or \(+1\). It has ordinary periodic boundary conditions only when \(5\mid k\). Thus in the divisible-length cases an isomorphism to \(\operatorname{Hom}(C_k,C_5)\) explains why much of their ordinary homotopy theory is already known; those cases are excluded from the present claim. For the claimed \(5\nmid k\), the same change carries a *nontrivial twisted boundary condition*, not covered by the periodic undirected theorem. Prue and Scrimshaw (arXiv:0909.5511) provide the graph-configuration subdivision equivalence used as an ingredient, not the new directed-Hom classification.

## Limitations
The result is a homotopy and cyclic-equivariant classification; it does not classify integral chain modules under larger source symmetries. The novelty assessment is relative to the directly inspected primary works, accessible previous claims and targeted literature/database searches, not a proof of global uniqueness. All higher-homology assertions use the infinite-family topological argument; finite enumerations alone do not establish them.
