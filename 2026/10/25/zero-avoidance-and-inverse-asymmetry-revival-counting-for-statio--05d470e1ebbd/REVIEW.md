# Scientific review: singular recovery-window transition in stationary telegraph dephasing

## Correctness

PASS. The tilted two-state generator has eigenvalues \(-a\pm z\); its exact amplitude, branch conventions, and stationary initial distribution are explicitly derived. The strict inequality \(\operatorname{Re}(b\overline z)>0\) excludes every finite-time complex zero for nonzero rate asymmetry. The derivative of squared modulus is an oscillatory cosine minus a strictly convex positive exponential envelope. Concavity on each positive cosine lobe yields at most one revival component; a uniform scaled-envelope limit with one crossing supplies upper and lower component counts and the sharp inverse-asymmetry coefficient. The simple-zero implicit-function calculation establishes the local minimum depth. Independent numerical matrix propagation and deterministic lobe counts support the algebra but are not substituted for infinite-time proof.

## Originality

PASS within inspected literature, with explicit residual uncertainty. The exact dephasing factor for asymmetric rates is already in Feng et al. (2026), Eq. (26), and is not claimed. Their full-text discussion of Figures 1–4 reports finite transient recoherence and numerical dependence on rate imbalance, without counting recovery intervals or obtaining a singular asymptotic law. Wold et al. (2012) treat the physical asymmetric telegraph model and give a symmetric closed formula, not the all-time asymmetry counting theorem. Hadipour (2025) perturbs the initial telegraph distribution at symmetric jump rates, not the stationary rate imbalance. A related theorem for symmetric telegraph *plus depolarization* counts entanglement-breaking windows, not derivative-positive intervals for asymmetric stationary noise. Searches for zero-freeness, time-integrated telegraph characteristic functions, and inverse-asymmetry recovery counts did not locate an equivalent theorem. The closest-source risk is that deeper analysis of the already published exact formula, or unlocated Markov-additive-process literature, may establish the same counting limit.

## Mathematical value

PASS. An infinitesimal thermal switching imbalance changes a process with infinitely many coherence-recovery windows and exact coherence zeros into one with finitely many windows and no zeros. The leading number of surviving windows is determined quantitatively and globally, with a sharp nonzero parameter-dependent constant; each formerly exact zero acquires a calculated linear floor. This is a motivated singular observable of non-Gaussian dephasing, not an arbitrary numerical parameter slice or a restatement of the already known closed form.

## Boundaries

Only a stationary Markov two-state signal, strictly positive jump rates, and fixed strong coupling are covered by the inverse-asymmetry limit. There is no claim about other noise models, simultaneous critical-coupling limits, or full qubit-channel entanglement properties. Independent validation is not asserted.
