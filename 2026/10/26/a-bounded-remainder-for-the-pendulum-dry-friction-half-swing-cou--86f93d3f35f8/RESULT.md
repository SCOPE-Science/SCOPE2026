# A bounded remainder for the pendulum dry-friction half-swing count

## Finding

Consider the controlled pendulum
\[
\dot x=y,\qquad \dot y=-\sin x+\epsilon u,\qquad |u|\leq1,
\]
and the low-energy Poincaré map generated by \(u=-\operatorname{sign}(y)\). Let \(X_0=\pi\). Whenever \(X_n\geq6\epsilon\), define \(X_{n+1}\in(0,X_n)\) by
\[
\cos X_{n+1}-\cos X_n=\epsilon(X_n+X_{n+1}).
\]
Let \(K_\epsilon\) be the first index with \(X_{K_\epsilon}<6\epsilon\), and set
\[
D=\int_0^\pi\frac{\sin x}{2x}\,dx=0.925968525991233\ldots,
\]
with the integrand continuously extended to \(1/2\) at \(x=0\). Then
\[
\boxed{K_\epsilon=\frac{D}{\epsilon}+O(1)\quad(\epsilon\downarrow0).}
\]
The implicit constant is independent of sufficiently small \(\epsilon\). The count is the number of Poincaré half-swing steps until the \(O(\epsilon)\) lower standstill zone; a switching count differs by at most a bounded endpoint convention.

## Assumptions and scope

Assume \(0<\epsilon<\epsilon_0\), for a sufficiently small fixed \(\epsilon_0\). Define the recurrence only while \(X_n\geq6\epsilon\). It is the exact energy-loss map for the stated constant-sign control between adjacent zero-velocity points in the low-energy region. This result does not assert that the policy is globally time-optimal and does not bound the maximum switch count among all optimal controls.

## Proof

Define
\[
F(x)=\int_0^x\frac{\sin s}{2s}\,ds,\qquad F(0)=0,
\]
so \(F(\pi)=D\).

**Existence and uniqueness.** For \(x=X_n\geq6\epsilon\), the next state \(z\) solves
\[
g(z)=\cos z-\cos x-\epsilon(x+z)=0.
\]
Since \(g'(z)=-\sin z-\epsilon<0\), there is at most one root. Moreover \(g(x)=-2\epsilon x<0\), while
\[
g(0)=1-\cos x-\epsilon x
\geq x\epsilon\left(\frac{12}{\pi^2}-1\right)>0,
\]
using \(1-\cos x\geq2x^2/\pi^2\) and \(x\geq6\epsilon\). Thus exactly one root lies in \((0,x)\).

Write \(\Delta_n=X_n-X_{n+1}\) and \(m_n=(X_n+X_{n+1})/2\). The recurrence gives
\[
\int_{X_{n+1}}^{X_n}\sin s\,ds=2\epsilon m_n.
\]
The local defect is
\[
e_n:=F(X_n)-F(X_{n+1})-\epsilon
=\int_{X_{n+1}}^{X_n}\sin s\left(\frac1{2s}-\frac1{2m_n}\right)ds.
\]
With \(s=m_n+t\), the interval is symmetric:
\[
e_n=\int_{-\Delta_n/2}^{\Delta_n/2}h_{m_n}(t)\,dt,\quad
h_m(t)=\sin(m+t)\left(\frac1{2(m+t)}-\frac1{2m}\right).
\]
Here \(h_m(0)=0\) and \(h_m'(0)=-\sin(m)/(2m^2)\), so the linear Taylor term integrates to zero.

Also,
\[
\int_{X_{n+1}}^{X_n}\sin s\,ds
=2\sin(\Delta_n/2)\sin m_n
\geq1-\cos\Delta_n\geq\frac{2\Delta_n^2}{\pi^2}.
\]
Since the same integral equals \(2\epsilon m_n\leq2\pi\epsilon\), we have \(\Delta_n\leq C\sqrt{\epsilon}\). If \(X_n\leq\pi/2\), then \(\sin s\geq2s/\pi\) gives \(\Delta_n\leq\pi\epsilon\). Combined with \(X_n\geq6\epsilon\), these estimates imply \(\Delta_n\leq m_n\) for sufficiently small \(\epsilon\). Thus \(m_n+t\in[m_n/2,3m_n/2]\). The function \(s\mapsto\sin s/(2s)\), extended at zero, has bounded second derivative on \([0,\pi]\); also \(|\sin(m_n+t)|/(2m_n)\leq3/4\). Hence \(|h_{m_n}''(t)|\leq C\) uniformly. Taylor's theorem and cancellation of the linear term yield
\[
|e_n|\leq C\Delta_n^3.
\]

The intervals are disjoint, \(\sum_{n<K_\epsilon}\Delta_n\leq\pi\), and \(\max_n\Delta_n^2\leq C\epsilon\). Therefore
\[
\sum_{n<K_\epsilon}|e_n|
\leq C(\max_n\Delta_n^2)\sum_{n<K_\epsilon}\Delta_n
=O(\epsilon).
\]
Finally \(0\leq F(X_{K_\epsilon})\leq X_{K_\epsilon}/2<3\epsilon\). Telescoping gives
\[
D-F(X_{K_\epsilon})
=\sum_{n<K_\epsilon}\bigl(F(X_n)-F(X_{n+1})\bigr)
=K_\epsilon\epsilon+O(\epsilon).
\]
Hence \(D=K_\epsilon\epsilon+O(\epsilon)\), proving \(K_\epsilon=D/\epsilon+O(1)\).

## Verification

The proof uses an exact recurrence identity and a uniform Taylor-remainder bound, not a finite experiment. The included standalone script reproduces the recurrence and compares its counts with \(D/\epsilon\) at several small positive parameter values; those computations are diagnostic only. The analytic estimates establish the asymptotic claim.

## Relationship to prior work

Ovseevich's public preprint derives the same low-energy Poincaré recurrence and proves the leading limit \(\epsilon N_\epsilon(u)\to D\). Its stated theorem gives the leading asymptotic but does not state a uniform \(O(1)\) remainder for the recurrence count. This result sharpens that particular quasioptimal half-swing count by bounding the accumulated quadrature error and the terminal \(O(\epsilon)\) tail. It does not strengthen the theorem about the maximum switch count of all optimal trajectories.

Reshmin's 2009 paper concerns a sufficient large-control-amplitude regime with at most one switching. Its accessible abstract does not address the small-\(\epsilon\) remainder.

## Limitations

The estimate applies only to the recurrence above, stopped at \(X<6\epsilon\). The bounded remainder is not claimed to have a limit, to be optimal in constant, or to extend to the globally optimal switch count. A priority risk remains from unindexed literature on quantitative errors for this exact Poincaré map; the full text of Reshmin (2009) was not accessible.
