# Review: characteristic-two Jordan-plane representations

## Correctness — PASS

The centralizer/trace-pairing lemma works in every characteristic: a nonscalar two-dimensional matrix has a two-dimensional centralizer, so the solvability conditions are exactly two traces and the solution fiber has dimension two. For odd characteristic, these force \(\operatorname{tr}Y=\det Y=0\), reducing to commuting pairs with nonzero square-zero \(Y\); the other component is \(Y=0\). For characteristic two, a direct matrix calculation yields the union of \(Y=0\) and a six-coordinate irreducible hypersurface \(a^2+bc+vc+bw=0\). Its irreducibility follows from primitivity as a linear polynomial in \(v\); the intersections, singular locus, and point counts follow exactly. The proof treats scalar \(Y\), finite fields of nonprime order, and geometric base change. Independently programmed literal-matrix enumeration over fields of orders \(2,3,4,5,7\) matches all component and fiber counts. Neither sample enumeration nor irreducibility alone is presented as a proof of the rest.

## Originality — PASS with limitations

Primary materials inspected: Iyudu’s complex-field MPIM preprint 2005 (20), Iyudu, *Representation theory of Jordanian algebra* (arXiv:0802.1545v1, 2008), Iyudu, *Irreducible components of the Jordan varieties* (arXiv:0903.3820, 2009), and Iyudu, *Representation spaces of the Jordan plane* (arXiv:1209.0746v1, 2012; 2014 journal publication). The latter explicitly assumes algebraically closed characteristic zero for the representation-theoretic component analysis and proves equidimensionality there. The former recognizes that positive-characteristic \(Y\) need not be nilpotent, a fact not claimed here as new. Their inspected component/stratum results do not provide the explicit modular two-dimensional characteristic-two hypersurface decomposition, non-equidimensionality, singular line, and even/odd finite-field census. published-finding corpus queries covering the equation, Jordanian aliases, characteristic-two representation geometry, and finite-field counts found no direct or stronger statement; neighboring hits concern other matrix identities or other algebras. Accessible related mathematical claims were compared for implication, including radical-square-zero quiver representations and Lie-homomorphism varieties, which do not imply this Jordan-plane calculation. Not all potentially relevant historical literature was accessible. Semantic search failure and partitioned records do not prove global novelty; broader implicit literature and uninspected sources remain priority risks.

## Value — PASS

The classical Jordan-plane representation variety is a natural noncommutative-algebra geometric object, and published characteristic-zero results establish equidimensionality. Determining the precise failure in characteristic two, a dimension jump from \(4\) to \(5\) in the smallest nontrivial matrix size, has direct conceptual value; the rational point census and explicit reduced singular locus make the boundary arithmetic and geometric rather than merely anecdotal. The results hold uniformly over all finite fields and arbitrary characteristic-two fields. No paper-sized downstream consequence is assumed.

## Limitations

Only size-two labelled representation varieties and their reductions are classified; not representation schemes, higher-dimensional varieties, isomorphism-orbit counts, or independent validation. Publication priority remains uncertain beyond the inspected primary sources and accessible mathematical-record and ledger comparisons.
