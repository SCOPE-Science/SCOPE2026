# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The equation is q(y)=h_c(x)=x(c-p(x)) over C*, with exactly m simple nonzero zeros. A singular point of the total hypersurface would have y=0 and h_c'=0 at such a zero, impossible. The regular quadric A retracts to S^(n-2) by writing its points as a+ib and shrinking b while maintaining a perpendicular and adjusting its norm. To justify the global reduction in Step 4 without properness, take disjoint closed simple-zero disks D_j defined by the coordinate h_c, with distinct attachment points on their boundaries. The rest of C* retracts, fixing the boundary circles of these disks, to a graph of those circles and an origin circle joined by a tree of stems: first trim the two annular ends, then cut the resulting planar surface along stems and retract onto its inner boundary circles and the joining tree. This base homotopy H_t lifts over the regular region by multiplying y by the continuous square root of h_c(H_t(x))/h_c(x) starting at 1. The lift fixes the disk boundaries, so extends by the identity on the disk pieces. Each inverse image of D_j is the contractible set |q(y)|<=epsilon, by radial contraction of y. Since the boundary loop over each D_j is already in that disk piece, its stem contributes only a cylinder over A connecting the disk piece to the regular graph. Contracting the tree and using the mapping-cylinder pushouts leaves the origin mapping torus with m cones on its fibers, all inclusions homotopic by transport. Collapsing the first fiber gives the suspension of A with its two poles identified, homotopy equivalent to S^1 wedge Sigma A. The later cone maps are nullhomotopic and each wedges on Sigma A. This proves the full bouquet, including n=3, where A retracts to S^1; connectedness and the claimed integral groups follow. The Euler characteristic and the frozen verification checklist are not used to infer homotopy type.
