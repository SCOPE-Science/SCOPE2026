# A common wreath-product automorphism group for infinitely many nonconjugate geometric spacing shifts

## Finding
For each integer \(b\ge2\), let \(X_b\subset\{0,1\}^{\mathbb Z}\) consist of all sequences in which the distance between **every pair** of occupied sites belongs to \(P_b=\{b^j:j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\}\). Write \(\sigma\) for the left shift and \(p_b(n)\) for the number of length-\(n\) words in \(X_b\). Let \(k=\lfloor\log_b n\rfloor\) for any integer \(n\ge1\). Then
\[
p_b(n)=\begin{cases}
(2k+2)n-2^{k+2}+4,&b=2,\\
(k+2)n+1-\dfrac{b^{k+1}-1}{b-1},&b\ge3.
\end{cases}
\]
All configurations have at most two occupied sites when \(b\ge3\), and at most three when \(b=2\); the three-site configurations in the latter case are precisely the translates of \(\{0,2^j,2^{j+1}\}\).

For every \(b\ge2\), the complete group of invertible sliding-block codes is abstractly
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(X_b,\sigma)\cong
\mathbb Z\times\bigl(\mathbb Z^{(\mathbb N)}\rtimes\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbb N)\bigr),
\]
where \(\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathrm{fin}}\) is the group of finitely supported permutations, acting by permuting the coordinates of the finitely supported direct sum. The first factor is generated by \(\sigma\). In particular the center of the automorphism group is precisely the powers of \(\sigma\).

The shifts \(X_b\) are pairwise not topologically conjugate as \(b\) varies, even though they have isomorphic full automorphism groups. Each has zero topological entropy and its only invariant Borel probability is the Dirac mass at the all-zero sequence. Their nonconjugacy is detected by the exact complexity coefficients
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{p_b(n)}{n\log n}=
\begin{cases}2/\log2,&b=2,\\1/\log b,&b\ge3.\end{cases}
\]
More precisely the centered count has a nonconstant digital-periodic profile: if \(t=\{\log_b n\}\), then for \(b\ge3\),
\[
\frac{p_b(n)}n-\log_b n=2-t-\frac{b^{1-t}}{b-1}+\frac{b}{(b-1)n},
\]
while for \(b=2\),
\[
\frac{p_2(n)}n-2\log_2 n=2-2t-2^{2-t}+\frac4n.
\]
The continuous, nonconstant functions of \(t\in[0,1]\) displayed here have equal endpoint values, so they define well-defined one-periodic functions.

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet is exactly \(\{0,1\}\), the index set is the bilateral integer line, and the spacing restriction applies to all pairs of distinct occupied positions, not only consecutive occupied positions. The integer base satisfies \(b\ge2\) throughout. Automorphisms mean homeomorphisms commuting with the bilateral shift, equivalently invertible finite-radius sliding-block codes. The asymptotic logarithm \(\log\) is natural; \(\log_b\) has base \(b\).

## Proof
**1. Complete support classification.** Suppose three occupied sites are \(u<v<w\). Their successive distances and total distance are powers of \(b\), so for some nonnegative integers \(r,s,t\), \(b^r+b^s=b^t\). If \(r<s\), division by \(b^r\) gives \(1+b^{s-r}=b^{t-r}\), impossible modulo \(b\) since the right side either equals \(1\) or is divisible by \(b\). Thus \(r=s\), and \(2=b^{t-r}\), which occurs exactly for \(b=2\) with \(t=r+1\). Hence only in base two are triples possible, with equal adjacent gaps \(2^r\). Four sites would force three consecutive gaps to be the same power by applying the triple result to each consecutive triple; their first-to-fourth distance would equal three times that power, not itself a power of two. This proves the complete classification and the uniform support bounds.

**2. Exact words and oscillations.** Every allowed finite word extends to a bilateral allowed configuration by setting all outside entries to zero. The empty support contributes one word, singletons contribute \(n\), and pairs at distance \(b^j<n\) contribute \(n-b^j\). For \(b=2\), the triples \(\{u,u+2^j,u+2^{j+1}\}\) contribute another \(n-2^{j+1}\) words for \(2^{j+1}<n\). Summing the finite geometric series gives the stated formulas with \(k=\lfloor\log_b n\rfloor\); terms whose distance equals \(n\) contribute zero, so no endpoint adjustment is needed. Writing \(n=b^{k+t}\), \(0\le t<1\), gives the exact displayed digital profiles. Their derivative cannot vanish identically, so the profiles are nonconstant.

**3. Topological strata.** For \(b\ge3\), each configuration with two markers is isolated: after specifying a window containing both markers there is no admissible third marker. For \(b=2\), a configuration with two markers separated by \(d\) has at most three possible positions for an added third marker (\(u-d\), \(u+d/2\) if integral and admissible, and \(u+2d\)), each within a finite window; by prescribing zeros there, it is isolated. Every triple is isolated because no fourth marker exists. Conversely each singleton-marker configuration is approached by pairs of separation \(b^j\to\infty\), and the all-zero point is approached by translated singletons. Thus the first Cantor–Bendixson derivative of \(X_b\) is exactly the singleton orbit together with zero, and its second derivative is zero alone. An automorphism must preserve these strata and, being shift equivariant, sends every singleton to the same integral translate. Composing with a unique inverse power of \(\sigma\), we may assume it fixes every singleton and zero.

**4. Eventual rigidity and exact group.** By the Curtis–Hedlund–Lyndon theorem, a normalized automorphism \(F\) has some finite radius \(R\). If a configuration has all distinct markers separated by more than \(2R\), then any radius-\(R\) neighborhood meets at most one marker. Because \(F\) fixes zero and each singleton, its local rule reproduces each such isolated marker exactly and introduces no other markers. Hence \(F(x)=x\) for every such configuration. For \(b\ge3\), this fixes every pair orbit with spacing \(b^j>2R\); for \(b=2\), it fixes both the pair and triple orbits with smallest gap \(2^j>2R\). There are exactly countably many other free shift orbits containing at least two markers, indexed by \(I_b\): one orbit per exponent if \(b\ge3\), and a pair orbit plus a triple orbit per exponent if \(b=2\). All are pointwise discrete and have zero as their only orbit-closure limit. Thus a normalized \(F\) acts on finitely many orbit types by a finite permutation \(\pi\in\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathrm{fin}}(I_b)\), together with arbitrary integer shifts on those types; it fixes all remaining types.

Conversely every such finite permutation with prescribed integer phase shifts defines a shift-commuting bijection by acting on those finitely many orbits and fixing zero, all singleton points, and the other orbits. It is continuous at isolated points trivially; near a singleton the moved finite collection of orbits cannot accumulate, and near zero each moved orbit and its image tend to zero under translations escaping every finite window. Its inverse has the same form, so the map is a homeomorphism and, by Curtis–Hedlund–Lyndon, a sliding-block automorphism. This proves that the normalized subgroup is the restricted permutation wreath product \(\mathbb Z^{(I_b)}\rtimes\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathrm{fin}}(I_b)\). The global shift is central, and intersects that subgroup trivially because it moves the singleton orbit; hence the product is direct. Relabeling the countable set \(I_b\) by \(\mathbb N\) proves the asserted common abstract group. Its center has no nontrivial element in the wreath factor: commuting with every finitely supported permutation forces any finitely supported phase vector to be zero, and the finitary symmetric group on an infinite set has trivial center. The full center is therefore \(\langle\sigma\rangle\).

**5. Nonconjugacy and invariant measure.** The formulas show \(p_b(n)=c_b n\log n+O_b(n)\), with the pairwise distinct constants displayed in the finding. If \(X_b\) and \(X_c\) were conjugate, the two conjugating finite-radius codes would bound the complexity in each direction by the other complexity at \(n+O(1)\); the normalized leading \(n\log n\) coefficient would therefore agree, a contradiction. All configurations contain at most three markers, so for any invariant Borel probability \(\mu\), \(n\mu([1])\le3\) for every \(n\), hence \(\mu([1])=0\) and shift invariance forces \(\mu\) to concentrate on the all-zero point. Finally \(p_b(n)=O_b(n\log n)\) implies zero topological entropy.

It prints `VERIFY_OK word_counts=98 normal_forms=98 digital_profiles=192 and orbit_freeness`. The unbounded claims and full group description rely on the analytical proof, not on finite enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Spacing shifts and their connection to all-pair distance sets and zero entropy are established in Kwietniak's work (arXiv:1111.0190), whose definitions and hereditary-entropy classification are used as context, not asserted as new. Donoso–Durand–Maass–Petite (arXiv:1501.00510) prove strong automorphism restrictions for minimal or suitably transitive low-complexity shifts; Cyr–Kra (arXiv:1509.08493) study minimal low-complexity shifts. The present family is countable, uniquely ergodic but highly nontransitive; neither cited paper gives its finitary wreath-product automorphism group or its pairwise-nonconjugate geometric-base classification. A different geometric \(S\)-gap model restricts distances between consecutive markers, not every pair; its entropy findings do not imply the finite-support classification here.

## Limitations
This computes the topological automorphism group, not the endomorphism monoid or the full conjugacy classification among arbitrary spacing shifts. Its invariant measure is atomic; no mixing or spectral behavior of a nontrivial stationary process is claimed. The result was checked against selected relevant primary publications and search records; absence of a matching result is not proof of global historical priority. A full-text inspection of Banks–Nguyen–Oprocha–Stanley–Trotta's `Dynamics of spacing shifts` was not obtained; its abstract emphasizes transitivity, weak mixing, and chaos, but an unlocated specialized example remains a residual priority risk.
