# A complete splitting trichotomy for weighted-cycle evolution algebras over \(\mathbb F_4\)

## Finding
Let \(K=\mathbb F_4\), and fix a generator \(\zeta\) of its multiplicative group of order three. For every integer \(n\geq1\) and each \(\boldsymbol a=(a_0,\ldots,a_{n-1})\in(K^\times)^n\), form the perfect evolution algebra \(E(\boldsymbol a)\) with natural basis \(e_0,\ldots,e_{n-1}\), multiplication \(e_i e_j=0\) for distinct indices and \(e_i^2=a_i e_{i+1}\), with indices modulo \(n\). Write \(a_i=\zeta^{A_i}\) with \(A_i\in\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\).

**Classification theorem.** When \(n\) is odd, all \(E(\boldsymbol a)\) are isomorphic, and \(\operatorname{Aut}_K(E(\boldsymbol a))\cong C_n\). When \(n=2h\) is even, put
\[
\Delta(\boldsymbol a)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(-1)^i A_i\in\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z.
\]
There are exactly two isomorphism classes: \(\Delta=0\) (balanced) and \(\Delta\ne0\) (unbalanced), because a rotation of odd parity replaces \(\Delta\) by \(-\Delta\). The balanced class has representatives with all \(a_i=1\), and
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_K(E(\boldsymbol a))\cong C_3\rtimes C_n,
\]
where a generator of \(C_n\) inverts \(C_3\); the diagonal-automorphism exact sequence splits. The unbalanced class has representative \(a_0=\zeta\) and \(a_i=1\) otherwise, and
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_K(E(\boldsymbol a))\cong C_{3h},\qquad
1\longrightarrow C_3\longrightarrow C_{3h}\longrightarrow C_h\longrightarrow1.
\]
This latter exact sequence splits **if and only if** \(3\nmid h\). Thus every dimension \(n\equiv0\pmod6\) supports an unbalanced single-cycle perfect evolution algebra with a nonsplit extension; in those dimensions its full automorphism group is cyclic of order \(3n/2\).

## Assumptions and scope
The coefficient field is exactly \(\mathbb F_4\); the algebra and all automorphisms are \(K\)-linear. The nonzero structure constants are arbitrary, but the directed graph of the natural basis is exactly one directed cycle, including a loop when \(n=1\). The group statements concern groups of \(K\)-rational automorphisms, **not** the entire automorphism group scheme after base extension. The algebra is perfect because its structure matrix is a nonzero weighted permutation matrix. The group \(C_j\) denotes a cyclic group of order \(j\) (and \(C_1\) is trivial).

## Proof
**1. Reduction to monomial maps and an exact recurrence.** Every automorphism or isomorphism between these perfect evolution algebras is monomial. One direct proof avoids assumptions on the characteristic: if \(f_i=\sum_jm_{ji}e_j\) form another natural basis, then \(f_i f_k=\sum_j m_{ji}m_{jk}e_j^2=0\) for \(i\ne k\). The squares \(e_j^2\) form a basis, so distinct columns of an invertible change-of-basis matrix have disjoint supports; thus each column contains precisely one nonzero entry. Preservation of the directed-cycle graph forces the resulting permutation to be a rotation \(i\mapsto i+k\pmod n\).

Consider an isomorphism from \(E(\boldsymbol a)\) to \(E(\boldsymbol b)\) given by \(f(e_i)=\zeta^{u_i}f_{i+k}\), where \(b_i=\zeta^{B_i}\). Comparing \(f(e_i^2)\) with \(f(e_i)^2\) yields the complete necessary-and-sufficient recurrence
\[
u_{i+1}=2u_i+B_{i+k}-A_i\pmod3,\qquad u_n=u_0.
\]
For a fixed rotation \(k\), no other equations are needed: mixed products vanish automatically. Iteration yields \(u_n=2^nu_0+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}2^{n-1-j}(B_{j+k}-A_j)\). Thus the multiplicity of solutions is either zero or the number of solutions to a single linear equation over \(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\).

**2. Classify the weights.** Since \(2=-1\pmod3\), if \(n\) is odd then \(1-2^n=2\) is nonzero in \(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\): for every \(\boldsymbol a,\boldsymbol b,k\) the recurrence has a unique solution. In particular, every coefficient tuple normalizes to all ones.

If \(n\) is even, the recurrence has solutions exactly when
\[
\sum_i(-1)^i A_i=\sum_i(-1)^iB_{i+k}=(-1)^k\sum_i(-1)^iB_i.
\]
Whenever it is soluble, there are exactly three solutions, since \(u_0\) is arbitrary. All tuples of vanishing alternating exponent normalize to all ones, and all tuples with a fixed nonzero alternating exponent normalize to \((\zeta,1,\ldots,1)\). An odd rotation identifies the two nonzero alternating exponents. The two classes cannot be isomorphic because zero cannot equal a nonzero exponent up to sign. This also proves the claimed counts of isomorphism classes for all even dimensions, including \(n=2\).

**3. Automorphisms and the balanced split case.** Apply the recurrence with \(\boldsymbol b=\boldsymbol a\). For odd \(n\), each of the \(n\) rotations has exactly one monomial lift; after normalizing the coefficients to one, these lifts are the pure rotations, forming \(C_n\). For even \(n\), the diagonal subgroup has exactly three elements generated by
\[
d(e_i)=\zeta^{(-1)^i}e_i,
\]
since its exponent vectors satisfy \(u_{i+1}=2u_i\). When \(\Delta=0\), every rotation lifts, so there are \(3n\) automorphisms. In the all-ones representative, rotation \(r(e_i)=e_{i+1}\) has order \(n\), its powers meet the diagonal subgroup only in the identity, and \(rdr^{-1}=d^{-1}\). This gives the stated split semidirect product.

**4. The unbalanced group and the exact splitting test.** In the unbalanced normal form \(\boldsymbol a=(\zeta,1,\ldots,1)\), the alternating invariant excludes every odd rotation. Exactly the \(h=n/2\) even rotations lift, with three lifts each, so the group has order \(3h\), and its permutation image is the cyclic group generated by the two-step rotation. Define a particular lift \(g\) of that rotation by \(g(e_i)=\zeta^{v_i}e_{i+2}\), where
\[
v_0=v_{n-1}=0,\quad v_i=1\ (2\le i\le n-2,\ i\text{ even}),\quad
v_i=-1\ (1\le i\le n-3,\ i\text{ odd}).
\]
For \(n=2\), the two-step rotation and \(g\) are the identity. Substitution into the recurrence directly confirms \(g\) is an automorphism in every even dimension. Because two-step rotation preserves parity, \(g\) commutes with \(d\). During \(h\) successive applications, each parity class visits all its \(h\) indices, whose \(v_i\) sum to \(h-1\) and \(-(h-1)\), respectively. Therefore
\[
g^h=d^{h-1},\qquad d^3=1,\qquad dg=gd,
\]
and \(g,d\) generate the full automorphism group. If \(h\not\equiv1\pmod3\), then \(g^h\) generates the three-element diagonal subgroup, so \(g\) has order \(3h\). If \(h\equiv1\pmod3\), then \(g\) has order \(h\) and its commuting product with \(d\) has order \(3h\), because \(\gcd(3,h)=1\). Consequently, the group is always \(C_{3h}\). It has a subgroup of order \(h\) complementary to its unique subgroup of order three precisely when \(\gcd(3,h)=1\), proving the splitting criterion.

## Verification
Run the two standard-library-only Python programs `verify.py` and `direct_f4_check.py` with Python 3. `verify.py` exhaustively checks every one of the \(3^n\) coefficient exponent tuples for \(1\le n\le6\), their predicted normal-form isomorphisms and automorphism counts; it checks the complete group law for the balanced/unbalanced normal forms in dimensions \(1,2,3,4,5,6,8,10,12,18\), and checks the exact extension-splitting test in even dimensions. `direct_f4_check.py` implements multiplication in \(\mathbb F_4=\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^2+t+1)\) using polynomial bit operations, independently enumerates all monomial candidates at dimensions \(2,4,6\), and confirms that each lift of the two-step rotation in the twisted six-cycle has order nine. Both programs return success. This finite verification is supporting evidence; the full quantification over dimensions follows from the recurrence and group-theoretic proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
The Elduque–Labra automorphism exact sequence and the monomial rigidity of perfect evolution algebras are known; here the contribution is a **complete finite-field isomorphism and splitting classification** for an infinite single-cycle family, including cyclic nonsplit extensions in every dimension divisible by six. Elduque and Labra (2019/2021, Example 3.5) treat a two-dimensional weighted cycle over a general field, providing a distinct non-splitting discussion at the group-scheme level; it does not give the all-dimensional \(\mathbb F_4\) orbit or extension classification. Kaygorodov, Martín González and Viruel (2026, Proposition 21) construct a six-dimensional nonsplit \(C_9\) example whose directed graph has three two-cycles and extra arrows, unlike the single oriented cycle here. Their Question 33 explicitly asks for splitting criteria and realizable extensions; the present result addresses a restricted natural family, not that general problem. The unweighted oriented-cycle example in the same survey gives an automorphism-group order under roots-of-unity assumptions; its unit-weight restriction is covered here and credited as known, not presented as new.

Martín Barquero and collaborators (2025) provide a weighted-graph inverse-limit description of monomial automorphisms. Their Section 6 also contains a broad splitting formulation; its interpretation requires care in light of explicit nonsplit examples from the 2026 survey and the direct finite-field computation here. This finding does not presume that the broad formulation can be applied as a valid splitting theorem: every relevant map and group relation is proved from the displayed coefficient recurrence.

## Limitations
The theorem does not classify arbitrary graphs, general finite fields, automorphism group schemes over extension rings, or other types of non-perfect evolution algebras. The literature comparison is based on accessible full primary texts and targeted research-record searches; an uncatalogued statement of the same special finite-field cycle classification remains possible. The 2025 weighted-graph splitting formulation is a material interpretation/coverage risk, disclosed above. All calculations are finite corroboration, not a replacement for the proof. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
