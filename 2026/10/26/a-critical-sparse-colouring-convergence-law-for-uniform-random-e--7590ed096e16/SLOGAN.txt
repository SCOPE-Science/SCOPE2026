Sparse independent marking at probability \(p_n\sim c/W(n)\) turns the wholly marked equivalence classes of a uniform random set partition into independent Poisson counts with mean \(c^j/j!\) at every size \(j\). Every first-order sentence in the equivalence-plus-unary language then has an effectively computable, analytic limit from finitely many of these Poisson counts; the existence of a wholly marked class has sharp limiting probability \(1-\exp(1-e^c)\).
