# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The object is the open random Pelikan map at the critical probability \(p=4/(4+(2-\varepsilon)^2)\), started from uniform mass in the highest dyadic cell. The exact cell-preserving map gives a substochastic transition law with only \(p\varepsilon\) killing from cell zero. The renewal law \(G=1/(1-f)\), with a first-return generating function computed from a biased nearest-neighbor hitting kernel, has the explicitly verified singular expansion \(G(z)\sim\sqrt2(1-\varepsilon)/(2-\varepsilon)(1-\lambda z)^{-1/2}\). The survival renewal and the bivariate occupancy resolvent yield the stated prefactor and the total-variation conditioned limit. The potential negative-axis branchpoint is of lower coefficient order, while aperiodicity of the first return excludes other dominant poles. Exact-rational and numerical checks agree with independently derived formulae; the proof, not the finite checks, supports the infinite limit.

## Value — PASS
At the boundary between a pole-dominated geometric survival law and a square-root branchpoint, the leading exponential escape factor cannot describe finite-time survival accurately without the inverse-square-root amplitude. Its explicit singular exponent and positive coefficient, and the simultaneous conditional limiting distribution despite non-positive recurrence, give a substantive boundary classification for a natural open random dynamical system. They are not mere substitutions into a known QSD formula and distinguish asymptotic conditioned behavior from existence of an eigenvector.

## Limitations
The fixed-parameter, uniform-cell initialization and piecewise-affine cell-preserving structure are essential. No uniformity near the endpoint openings, unrestricted starting measures, or quasi-ergodic limit theorem is established. Literature priority remains subject to unavailable full thesis text and other unindexed equivalent formulations.
