# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** Lucas's prime-digit criterion forces possible minima below six to the unique interior indices \(k=3p\) or \(k=2p\). The respective ternary and binary carry tests give necessary and sufficient conditions, and \(k=p\) always realizes gcd six. The infinite claims follow without extrapolation from finite checks; the counting bound uses standard digit-string enumeration and the prime number theorem. The implementation independently checked every eligible binomial coefficient for 34 primes and the Lucas conditions for 165 primes.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Erdős–Szekeres (1978) posed the minimum-gcd problem and included \(f(30)=6\), but not the complete formula for prime-indexed \(6p\). Bergman's arXiv:0806.0607v1 concerns common divisors of multiple binomial/multinomial coefficients and does not imply the present single-row minimization. The number-theoretic digit conditions were compared with relevant result-index hits and previous claim records; no directly equivalent or dominating published statement was identified. Lucas and Kummer supply classical proof mechanisms, and an unlocated short deduction is a material originality risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A natural first three-prime-support family of the original Erdős–Szekeres minimum problem has a complete, precise classification and an unexpected interaction between two different digit constraints, with a power-saving exceptional-prime count. This is a natural exact invariant rather than an arbitrary numerical slice; the density-one conclusion supports the classification's broader significance without asserting infinitely many primes in the exceptional sets.

**Limits.** The result does not settle the unrestricted minimization problem, does not prove infinitude in either exceptional digit family, and has not received independent validation.
