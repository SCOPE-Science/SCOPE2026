# Review

## Correctness — PASS
The source gives a Lagrangian immersion with one transverse double point and \(c_2(X)=12\). The normal bundle is \(\Omega_X^1\), so the double-point formula gives \([Z]^2=12+2=14\). Pullback by the degree-nine isogeny gives square \(126\). The integral divisor lattice on \(A	imes A\) has top intersections \(144,-144,864\) (all other basis monomials vanish), so every integral divisor-product square is divisible by \(144\). Since \(126\) is not, the claimed product cannot exist.

## Originality — PASS, narrowly stated
The inspected primary paper provides the premises but does not state this divisibility obstruction for its Albanese cycle. The closest cited non-complete-intersection result concerns Schoen surfaces, a different family. published-finding corpus searches found no exact match; this is not proof of global novelty. The claim is limited to one integral divisor product.

## Value — PASS
The obstruction rules out a concrete geometric representation of the Albanese cycle as an actual complete intersection of two divisors. This is relevant to the paper's discussion of the algebraic cycle class and possible Hodge-theoretic role, without claiming anything about the full rational span of divisor products.

Closest literature and limitations: Grossi–Moretti, arXiv:2502.13087v2, gives the surface construction and transverse double point. Its Schoen-surface comparison concerns a different family. The original Schoen-surface paper was not inspected in full during this run. The conclusion does not exclude rational combinations of divisor products.
