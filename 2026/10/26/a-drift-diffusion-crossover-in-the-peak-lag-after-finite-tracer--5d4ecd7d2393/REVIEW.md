# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The density is the classical first-passage flux at an absorbing boundary; comparison of two shifted density values gives a quadratic sign test and a single global peak. Implicit differentiation proves strict parameter monotonicity. The dimensionless root equation and its fixed-drift, driftless, and transitional asymptotic regimes follow with quantified limits. The bundled script independently reproduces the scalar comparisons, but the analytic sign and limit arguments—not randomized tests—establish the universal conclusions.

**Originality — PASS, with material residual risk.** The underlying inverse-Gaussian density and the convolution formula are classical and are not claimed as novel. The candidate contribution is the complete lag characterization including the singular drift–diffusion crossover. Kim, Eckford and Chae (2014, arXiv:1407.5776v1, introductory sections examined) study instantaneous molecular releases; Haselmayr, Efrosinin and Guo (2018, arXiv:1809.09995v1, entire article examined) study differences of first-arrival times. Neither inspected material gives the finite constant-release peak and drift crossover. A particularly relevant Yu–Warrick–Conklin (1999, DOI:10.1029/1999WR900225) step-input article could only be assessed from its abstract; Kreft–Zuber (1978, DOI:10.1016/0009-2509(78)85196-3) was not fully accessible. It is possible an unexamined theorem there or a general translated-density result implies the present specialization. No historical uniqueness is asserted.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Arrival-peak delay is a directly interpretable property of a finite contamination event, unlike raw distribution moments. A finite release must eventually be detected after shutoff, and the quantitative lag affects temporal resolution of monitoring. The inverse-logarithmic versus inverse-duration regimes and the crossover parameter reveal a structural singular limit not predicted by the impulse mode or the zeroth-order step response alone. The theorem characterizes the full positive parameter domain and excludes changes of measured observable.
