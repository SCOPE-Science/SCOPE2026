# Scientific review

**Correctness (C): PASS.** Strictly positive valuations and the total rare-item deficit of \(100\) justify the cardinality lemma for all integer padding quantities. Equal-bundle and mixed-size PMMS conditions are reduced to exact integer optimization over all rare-item count splits, with no floating-point approximations or heuristic pruning. The accompanying independently executable code counts every one of the \(62{,}720\) possible labeled rare-good allocations, checks balanced allocations for \(k=4\) through \(13\), and directly verifies constructive allocations with original integer utilities. The stabilization at \(k\ge13\) follows analytically because only thirteen goods have a nonzero deficit; all additional goods share value \(101\). The nonmultiple-of-four constructions hold for every permitted parameter, not just sampled values. No infinite statement follows solely from an experiment.

**Originality (O): PASS (historical-priority uncertainty).** Aziz's full 2026 counterexample fixes \(p=39\) and proves nonexistence at that value. The present classification is different: it determines the entire padding dependence, establishes a sharp family-specific first failure at \(p=15\), and exhibits periodic reappearance of PMMS existence. The separate unrestricted small-market counterexamples of Gölz and Bei et al. neither imply nor contradict the family-specific periodic equivalence without additional analysis; neither inspected text supplies such an equivalence. Public mathematical-record searches for padding, 28/32-good thresholds, and PMMS non-monotonicity found no match. Semantic search cannot rule out an unindexed, differently worded, or unpublished stronger theorem; this remains a material residual risk.

**Value (V): PASS.** Adding goods of common strictly positive value is a natural resource-supply perturbation. The theorem proves a complete infinite periodic classification of whether a stringent fair allocation exists under that perturbation, revealing that an obstruction can disappear after one added good and return three additions later. The first obstruction is smaller than the original 52-good presentation *within exactly this valuation family*. This is a structural distinction between universal existence/nonexistence and fixed-supply feasibility rather than a merely routine parameter substitution or an unsupported global smallest-counterexample claim.
