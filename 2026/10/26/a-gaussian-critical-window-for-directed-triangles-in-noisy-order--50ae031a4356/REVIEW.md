# Scientific review: Gaussian critical window for ordered-tournament directed triangles

**Correctness — PASS.** The finite identity is established by cancellation of cubic monomials in a directed-cycle indicator, followed by a centered linear-plus-quadratic expansion. Pairwise orthogonality of distinct quadratic monomials and the exact squared-gap sum yield the exact variance with no asymptotic approximation. In the critical window, a connected-diagram cumulant estimate gives order \(O_k(n^{k+1})\) for each fixed cumulant of order \(k\ge3\), versus the \(n^{3k/2}\) normalization; the second cumulant tends to \(1/32+c^2/12\). Outside the window, a Lindeberg theorem for the linear term and a vanishing quadratic remainder give the stated Gaussian law. The exact-rational checker independently enumerated all orientations for 16 finite \((n,p)\) pairs, checking 135,456 statewise polynomial identities and the exact moments. Finite enumeration corroborates identities but does not prove asymptotic convergence; the proof is analytical. Risk: finite-sample quantitative convergence rates are not established.

**Originality — PASS within accessible prior literature, with residual historical-priority risk.** Saile–Suksompong (2018; arXiv:1804.02743) describe the ordered-edge-bias Condorcet model but analyze tournament choice sets; Malinovsky–Rinott (2022; arXiv:2206.08461) establish negative association of scores; Malinovsky–Moon (2021; arXiv:2104.01450) treat scores and maxima. None of the inspected relevant primary material presents the displayed moving-bias directed-triangle count CLT and variance coefficient. Andersson (1998) studies fair random tournaments, so the fair endpoint is credited and not claimed individually as new. The full Andersson article was not accessible for material comparison; its abstract specifies fair orientations. General subgraph-count and generalized U-statistic limit theory is an additional derivability risk, but no verified published statement directly subsuming this explicit near-symmetry crossover was found. Mathematical-result searches and accessible prior claims were compared, including an earlier uniform-tournament result about cyclic-count/score covariance; their hypotheses and conclusions do not imply a varying-bias Gaussian crossover.

**Value — PASS.** The rank-ordered Condorcet model is a natural noisy preference/competition model. The directed-cycle count is its basic global measure of intransitivity. The result identifies a genuine change of fluctuation order, from \(n^3\) variance at fair orientation to \(n^4\) at fixed nonzero bias, and proves a sharp \(n^{-1/2}\) transition with the explicit continuous variance profile. The variance competition comes from two distinct orthogonal random mechanisms rather than a routine parameter substitution. The result supplies an interpretable sharp limit law for a model and statistic already studied separately in the probability and tournament literature.

**Limitations.** The proof requires independent identically biased pairwise orientations relative to a fixed order. It does not give a local CLT, Berry–Esseen bound, joint motif limit, or off-window result when probabilities approach zero or one. The inaccessible fair-model primary paper and general random-subgraph U-statistic literature leave a historical-priority risk. These risks do not affect the finite algebra or the self-contained cumulant proof.
