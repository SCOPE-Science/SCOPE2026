# Scientific assessment: global angular stability of critical Bergman pole energy

## Correctness — PASS
The exact monomial norm is \(2/[m(m+1)]\), and its absolutely convergent coefficient expansion gives a pairwise circular potential. The critical second derivative has the Poisson representation \(\Phi''(t)=\int_0^1(1-s^2)/(1-2s\cos t+s^2)\,ds\), which has the exact global minimum \(2\log2-1\) at the antipode. Subtracting this curvature and applying integral Taylor remainders to every cyclic \(r\)-step arc yields an exact nonnegative remainder. The identity \(\sum_{r,j}(d_{j+r}-d_j)^2=2N\sum_j(d_j-\bar d)^2\) closes the proof for arbitrary \(N\), including collisions by continuity. Rigidity follows from strictness off the unique minimizer of the pair curvature; two-pole perturbation proves the constant cannot increase. The normalization, sign, endpoint integrability, and variance factor were checked independently. Finite numerical tests are explicitly supplementary.

## Originality — PASS, with specific residual risk
The 2020/2021 theorem of Abakumov–Borichev–Fedorovskiy and Corbett's 2026 complete manuscript prove the qualitative equispaced minimum and underlying strict convexity. The publicly available complete note *Full Fourier Hessian spectrum and sharp local stability for equispaced Bergman poles* computes the sharp **local** modal coefficient, and states explicitly that it does not assert a global quadratic bound. The new claim supplies a single best \(N\)-independent coefficient for *every* angular configuration, including coincident poles, with a global positive-remainder identity and a saturating two-pole limit. The logical distinction is quantitative, not a new minimizer or Hessian. The original 2020 source's entire manuscript was not accessible in the current review; its abstract and Corbett's specific quotations of Lemma 12 were examined. A prior unindexed application of strong convexity with exactly this discrepancy and constant cannot be excluded, and is the main priority risk. The full generalization by Bezrukov was not materially inspected; available statements concern qualitative global minimization and do not decisively settle the new coefficient.

## Value — PASS
The exact sharp universal modulus yields a globally effective inverse-stability estimate: a squared-energy deficit of \(\eta\) forces squared optimal ordered-angle error at most \(\eta/[(2\log2-1)N]\), without assuming a small perturbation, a minimum pole separation, or a fixed number of poles. The universal optimal coefficient is nontrivial and explicitly realized by a singular low-dimensional endpoint; the new exact remainder resolves equality and possible additional stability gains. This is a natural quantitative strengthening of the recent qualitative minimizing problem rather than an arbitrary parameter slice. It does not assert the best coefficient at each fixed \(N>2\).
