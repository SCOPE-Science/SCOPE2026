# Scientific review: weak-monitoring resonance crossover

## Correctness

PASS. The claim specifies the measurement instruments, initial state, exact two-by-two no-click matrix, the moving-angle domain and the two distinct limiting regimes. The weak limit follows from a uniform finite-dimensional matrix Euler product. The three real density coordinates give exact integrated first and second moments, including the defective-generator case. The exact finite-probe mean is established by the convergent discrete Gramian identity, not from finite simulation. The unusually delayed tail follows from the two real roots of the exact characteristic polynomial and a rank-one slow spectral projection. The variance divergence is obtained from the exact second-moment tail sum. An executable symbolic/numerical check verifies the critical identities but does not replace the proof. No higher-dimensional or unbounded-coupling conclusion is asserted.

## Originality

PASS on the documented best-knowledge comparison, with explicit residual risk. Heine, Barkai, Ziegler and Tornow (arXiv:2506.21168) establish the model and exact mean return discontinuity, which are not claimed as new. Ziegler, Heine and Tornow (arXiv:2603.26933) derive finite-probe spectral formulas for the return-time moments and describe resonant peaks, which are also prior knowledge. Their formulas create a plausible route to the current asymptotics, but the inspected sections do not state the joint weak-probe/near-resonance distributional crossover, the normalized rare-tail profile or the nonuniform-integrability mechanism. Strong projective return detection (arXiv:1903.03394) and continuously sampled threshold measurements (arXiv:2308.07810) study different regimes. Related published research records concern different graphs and probing optimizations. The claimed novelty is specifically the joint singular law and its normalized late tail, not the exact finite-strength eigenvalues or finite-strength mean. A broader theorem or uninspected appendix could weaken priority; global novelty is not certified.

## Value

PASS. The probability distribution, not only its first moment, is a physically relevant observable in quantum detection. The theorem resolves why a nearly resonant detector can exhibit exponentially distributed typical waiting times while the mean remains twice that scale: an asymptotically tiny set of slow trajectories carries half of the mean and forces a divergent variance. It also locates a non-Hermitian exceptional point of the limiting no-click dynamics. These are structural crossover statements with exact amplitude, timescale and profile, not isolated numerical parameter evaluations.

## Scope and risk

Only one rank-one detector on a two-site coherent walk is covered. Finite-parameter moments were previously expressible through general spectral formulas; novelty is limited to the singular asymptotic analysis and should be checked further before a priority claim. No independent audit or external validation was performed.
