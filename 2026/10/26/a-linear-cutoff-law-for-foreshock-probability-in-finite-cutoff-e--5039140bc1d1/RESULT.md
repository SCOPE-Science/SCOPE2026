# A linear cutoff law for foreshock probability in finite-cutoff ETAS cascades

## Finding

Consider a marked ETAS branching cascade with magnitudes in the finite interval \([m_0,m_{\max}]\). Every triggered event has an independent magnitude mark with density

\[
f(m)=\frac{b\ln 10\,10^{-b(m-m_0)}}{1-10^{-b(m_{\max}-m_0)}},\qquad m_0\le m\le m_{\max},
\]

and an event of magnitude \(m\) produces a Poisson number of direct offspring with mean \(\kappa\mu(m)\), where \(\mu(m)=10^{\alpha(m-m_0)}\). Assume the mean branching ratio \(n=\kappa\mathbb E[\mu(M)]\) is strictly below one. Let \(F(\rho)\) be the probability that the full descendant cascade of a root with fixed magnitude \(\rho\) contains at least one event of magnitude greater than \(\rho\), including descendants of every generation but not the root itself. Then

\[
F(\rho)\sim \frac{\kappa\mu(m_{\max})}{1-n}\Pr\{M>\rho\}
\sim \frac{\kappa\mu(m_{\max})f(m_{\max})}{1-n}(m_{\max}-\rho)
\quad\text{as }\rho\uparrow m_{\max}.
\]

Thus the all-generation probability of a larger aftershock vanishes linearly at the physical upper magnitude cutoff. Its leading slope is the endpoint mark density multiplied by the expected number of descendants generated by a root at the cutoff.

## Assumptions and scope

Here \(M\) denotes a generic triggered-event magnitude with density \(f\). Parameters satisfy \(m_{\max}>m_0\), \(b>0\), \(\alpha\ge0\), \(\kappa>0\), and \(n<1\). Since the magnitude interval is bounded, \(\mu\) and all conditional Poisson means are bounded. The tree is the ordinary independent-branching ETAS model: a node's mark determines its offspring mean, while each child's mark is drawn independently from \(f\). The root magnitude is fixed at \(\rho\); it is not drawn from \(f\). The probability is unconditional on the existence of any offspring and counts any descendant exceeding \(\rho\), whether direct or indirect.

## Proof

Write \(p_\rho=\Pr\{M>\rho\}\). Let \(x_\rho\) be the probability that a child drawn from the mark law fails the requirement that its own magnitude and all of its descendants are at most \(\rho\). Conditioning on the child's mark and using the Poisson offspring law gives

\[
x_\rho=1-\int_{m_0}^{\rho}f(u)e^{-\kappa\mu(u)x_\rho}\,du
=p_\rho+\int_{m_0}^{\rho}f(u)\bigl(1-e^{-\kappa\mu(u)x_\rho}\bigr)\,du. \tag{1}
\]

The right side of the second expression, as a function of \(x\in[0,1]\), has derivative at most \(\kappa\mathbb E[\mu(M)]=n<1\). It therefore has a unique fixed point. As \(\rho\uparrow m_{\max}\), \(p_\rho\to0\) and \(x_\rho\to0\): any positive subsequential limit \(x\) would, by (1), satisfy

\[
x=\int_{m_0}^{m_{\max}}f(u)(1-e^{-\kappa\mu(u)x})\,du
\le \kappa\mathbb E[\mu(M)]x=nx<x,
\]

which is impossible.

Because \(\mu\) is bounded on the finite magnitude interval, Taylor expansion of the integrand in (1) is uniform as \(x_\rho\to0\). Consequently,

\[
\int_{m_0}^{\rho}f(u)(1-e^{-\kappa\mu(u)x_\rho})\,du
=\kappa x_\rho\int_{m_0}^{\rho}\mu(u)f(u)\,du+O(x_\rho^2)
=nx_\rho+o(x_\rho).
\]

Equation (1) becomes \((1-n)x_\rho=p_\rho+o(x_\rho)\), so \(x_\rho\sim p_\rho/(1-n)\). A root of fixed magnitude \(\rho\) has a Poisson number of children with mean \(\kappa\mu(\rho)\); each child and its descendants meet the cutoff requirement with probability \(1-x_\rho\). The Poisson generating function therefore yields the exact identity

\[
F(\rho)=1-e^{-\kappa\mu(\rho)x_\rho}.
\]

Since \(\mu(\rho)\to\mu(m_{\max})\), the first asymptotic follows. Finally, continuity and positivity of the truncated density at its upper endpoint give \(p_\rho\sim f(m_{\max})(m_{\max}-\rho)\), proving the second asymptotic. No numerical approximation is used.

## Verification

The fixed-point equation is obtained by conditioning on a child's mark and applying the Poisson probability-generating function; it includes all generations. The contraction bound uses the full mean branching ratio, not a first-generation-only approximation. Bounded productivity justifies the uniform quadratic remainder. The limit is for fixed subcritical \(n<1\); it is not uniform as \(n\uparrow1\), and the theorem makes no assertion at criticality.

## Relationship to prior work

Saichev and Sornette derived the all-generation maximum-magnitude equation and the second Båth law for an unbounded Gutenberg–Richter model, with its scale-free behavior associated with the near-critical regime. Sornette and Werner used a finite upper magnitude cutoff in the normalized Gutenberg–Richter law and derived the expected total aftershock count, but did not state the near-cutoff asymptotic for the probability that the largest descendant exceeds a fixed root. The present result isolates that boundary regime and gives its exact first-order coefficient. The expected-descendant factor agrees with the branching-process mean \(\kappa\mu(m_{\max})/(1-n)\); the new comparison is the probability-level linear asymptotic at the finite endpoint.

## Limitations

The result assumes independent Gutenberg–Richter marks, Poisson productivity, a hard finite upper cutoff, and fixed subcritical branching ratio. It does not model time-window censoring, magnitude-dependent detection, spatial dependence, or a smooth taper instead of a hard cutoff. The formula is an asymptotic as \(\rho\uparrow m_{\max}\), not an approximation with a stated finite-distance error bound. A broader general theorem on rare marks in marked branching trees could imply the same leading behavior; the literature comparison below found no exact statement for this finite-cutoff ETAS observable, but it cannot certify global novelty.
