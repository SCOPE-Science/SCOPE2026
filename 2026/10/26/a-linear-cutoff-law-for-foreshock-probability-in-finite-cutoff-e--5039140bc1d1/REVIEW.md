# Review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact fixed-point equation is reconstructed from the independent-mark branching model and the Poisson generating function. The contraction uses \(n<1\); bounded productivity gives a uniform Taylor remainder; the endpoint-density expansion gives the stated linear coefficient. The claim excludes the critical case and does not infer an infinite-tree fact from finite computation.

## Originality — PASS, with a stated residual risk

The closest primary source, Saichev and Sornette (2005; arXiv:physics/0501102), derives an all-generation maximum-magnitude equation and the second Båth law for an unbounded Gutenberg–Richter model. Sornette and Werner (2005; DOI 10.1029/2004JB003535) use a finite upper cutoff and give the mean total aftershock count, but their inspected text does not give the probability-level linear endpoint asymptotic established here. Searches for the finite-cutoff ETAS maximum/foreshock boundary statement returned no exact match. A general rare-mark theorem for subcritical marked branching trees could imply the same asymptotic, so global novelty is not certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result quantifies a physically motivated boundary effect: the scale-free second Båth-law behavior cannot remain magnitude-independent arbitrarily close to a hard maximum magnitude. It provides an exact slope in terms of the endpoint density and expected cascade size, useful for distinguishing a hard-cutoff model from an unbounded approximation.

## Closest literature and limits

The claim is restricted to the hard-cutoff, subcritical ETAS model and the limit \(\rho\uparrow m_{\max}\). It does not cover time censoring, imperfect detection, spatial dependence, smooth tapering, or the critical regime. The two primary texts above were inspected in the passages on the magnitude law, upper cutoff, total aftershock count, and all-generation maximum distribution.
