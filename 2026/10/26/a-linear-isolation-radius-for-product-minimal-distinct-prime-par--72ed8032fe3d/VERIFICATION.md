The theorem is proved by the universal inequality \(ku/R\le5/2\), the elementary product and sum comparisons, and unique prime factorization. All cases \(k=1\), \(k=2\), \(k=3\), and \(k\ge4\) are treated explicitly. The finite rational check over subsets of the first eleven primes with \(2\le k\le7\) returned exactly \(5/2\) as the maximum, attained for the two smaller primes \(2,3\). This is a sanity check, not an infinite certificate. Run `python verify.py` to reproduce that check. No quantitative prime-gap existence computation and no independent audit were performed.
