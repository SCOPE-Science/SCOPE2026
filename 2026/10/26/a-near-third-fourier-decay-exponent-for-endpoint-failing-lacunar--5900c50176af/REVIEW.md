# Review

**Correctness — PASS.** The claim follows from the uniform random-point approximation, the exact Fourier transform of the short-interval mixture, three explicit frequency regimes, and the atom-and-separated-scales lower bound. The strict inequality \(\beta<1/3\) absorbs the logarithmic factor; no endpoint claim is made.

**Originality — PASS.** The closest primary result, Zhou's arXiv:2610.21197, proves exponent \(1/8\) and expressly leaves the optimal exponent open. The present proof improves the point-selection tolerance to \(O(\sqrt{(\log N)/N})\) and uses spike half-width \(N^{-3/2}\), while retaining a linear-in-\(\log N\) collection of bad scales. Targeted literature searches returned no same-object result with decay exponents approaching \(1/3\). The 2011 and 2017 sources frame the endpoint problem but do not imply this quantitative refinement.

**Value — PASS.** Raising the guaranteed Fourier-decay range from one eighth to every exponent below one third materially narrows the gap in a stated open quantitative direction, while preserving the same endpoint failure for a single compactly supported probability measure. The optimal exponent and the endpoint \(1/3\) remain unresolved.

Closest literature: Zhou (arXiv:2610.21197, Theorem 1.1 and Remark 1.2); Seeger–Wright (arXiv:1007.4731); Grafakos et al. (arXiv:1701.06637, Problems 14–15). Residual risk: targeted searches are not an exhaustive proof of global novelty, and an unindexed independent optimization of the same construction may exist.
