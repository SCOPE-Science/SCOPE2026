For uniform finite-population sampling from one dominant species plus \(r\) true singletons, the covariance between observed singleton and doubleton species counts changes sign exactly once as dominant abundance increases, for every sample depth \(n\ge3\) and \(r\ge n-1\). Its positive-sign threshold has an exact hypergeometric criterion and a sharp large-\(r\) limit set by \(nq[1-(1-q)^{n-1}]=2\); two-draw surveys can never exhibit positive covariance.
