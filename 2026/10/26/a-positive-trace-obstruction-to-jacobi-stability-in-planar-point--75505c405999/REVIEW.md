# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The inverse-distance Hessian has trace \(m_j/r_j^3\) in two dimensions. The quadratic centrifugal Hessian cancels the constant \(-I_2\) term in the KCC tensor, leaving a symmetric matrix with strictly positive trace at every nonsingular point. The proof establishes failure of the strict KCC criterion at every point on every regular trajectory, and makes no claim about ordinary orbital stability.

## Originality
PASS, with a stated residual risk. The primary source on the classical restricted three-body problem gives the two-center trace identity, which already implies that special case globally. The present claim is the arbitrary finite-center extension. Exact-claim searches found no direct arbitrary-center statement; general KCC references cited by the primary source were not individually inspected.

## Value
PASS. The result identifies a global limitation of the strict Jacobi-stability criterion for normalized planar point-mass fields, rather than merely classifying equilibrium points. This prevents the criterion's label from being mistaken for a conclusion about physical orbital stability.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest primary source is *Jacobi stability analysis of the classical restricted three-body problem*, arXiv:2104.02432v1 (first posted 2021-04-06). Its two-center formula is acknowledged as prior coverage. The arbitrary-center extension is elementary; uninspected general KCC literature remains a residual risk. The theorem assumes the stated normalized potential and excludes singular centers.
