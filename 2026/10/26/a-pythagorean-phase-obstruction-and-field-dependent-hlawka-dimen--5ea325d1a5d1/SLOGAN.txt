For every \(p>q\approx3.57234633\), real coordinate \(\ell_p\) spaces first fail the Hornich–Hlawka inequality in dimension three, whereas complex coordinate \(\ell_p\) spaces already fail in dimension two. The two-complex-coordinate obstruction uses a Pythagorean imaginary phase, optimal among fixed-amplitude phases, and \(q\) is the exact failure threshold for that particular witness, not a global critical exponent.
