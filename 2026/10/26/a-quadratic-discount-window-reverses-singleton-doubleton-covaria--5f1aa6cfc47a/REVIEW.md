# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Exact marked-block probabilities from the exchangeable Chinese-restaurant law yield the one- and two-frequency moments and an explicit positive-normalized covariance ratio. Differentiating its logarithm, obtaining an \(n\)-uniform second-derivative bound and applying Taylor's theorem proves the local one-root classification and sharp \(n^{-2}\) limit. The separate rational-arithmetic state-enumeration program passed 2315 assertions. Limitations: the finite checks alone are not an infinite proof, and the sign at a zero limiting coefficient is not asserted.

**Originality — PASS (historical uncertainty remains).** The source sampling formula and its possible factorial-moment consequences are expressly treated as known. The comparison is at the level of the particular positive-strength, near-zero-discount covariance-sign phase boundary and local root uniqueness, rather than nomenclature. Balocchi–Favaro–Naulet (arXiv:2203.06076) study Bayesian nonparametric species sampling, prevalence and posterior counts. Franssen–van der Vaart (arXiv:2208.14255) study parameter estimation, not the above microscopic signed joint-frequency transition. An existing calculation at strength zero concerns a different corner (discount approaching one). No equivalent threshold was found in the relevant mathematical search results. A specialized earlier factorial-moment study might imply this result and remains the principal priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** The distinction between negative versus positive dependence of counts of rare species has a natural interpretation in a two-parameter species-sampling law. The complete local sign classification and a sharp \(n^{-2}\) transition distinguish the Dirichlet limit from even extremely small positive discount; neither the known EPPF nor numerical novelty alone is offered as value. The absolute covariance is small; the result makes no claim of practical detection power at fixed finite sample size.
