# Scientific review: complete split-network inversion boundary

## Correctness
PASS. The core's closed neighborhood is the full odd electorate, forcing its local vote to match the initial majority; each peripheral vote is classified by the core support count. The tie-retention band cannot reverse the global outcome, and a strict larger periphery dictates the outcome outside the band. Complementation symmetry gives the exact double-binomial total. A joint binomial central limit argument with correlation \((1+\rho)^{-1/2}\) and a Gaussian angular calculation yields the \(1/4\) boundary. The finite checker independently evaluates every individual local vote for 114,408 profiles. No asymptotic statement relies on finite checks.

## Originality
PASS, with bibliographic uncertainty. Chebotarev–Peleg (arXiv:2210.03410), including its section 7.1 discussion of complete-bipartite minority control, is the closest majority-of-majorities source. That already establishes *possibility* of very small controlling coalitions; the new claim is instead the exact inversion probability and a zero-to-quarter limit on complete **split** graphs with overlapping neighborhoods. The 2022 Kaniovski–Zaigraev full manuscript supplies a general binomial inversion theory for **disjoint** electoral constituencies, so it neither directly covers nor implies this specific local-neighborhood map. Cruciani and colleagues study repeated random two-neighbor updates, not the one-round local-majority decision. Targeted literature and published-result searches found no equivalent complete-split inversion formula; unidentified older accounts remain possible. The independent audit has not yet been performed.

## Mathematical value
PASS. This is a sharp, naturally parameterized structural boundary in representation error for core–periphery social voting. It distinguishes a majority-preserving architecture from a family in which an asymptotically positive \(25\%\) of fair initial profiles have their popular majority reversed, even when the architecture changes by reclassifying one vertex. The exact finite count and the general Gaussian phase law allow quantitative comparison of social-network compositions; the result is more than a minimal coalition example.

## Limitations and relationship to earlier work
Only independent unbiased initial votes, binary alternatives, complete split graphs with an odd total, one simultaneous local vote, self-included neighborhoods, and tie retention are covered. Existing results on local minority control and on inversion in disjoint-district voting are acknowledged, not claimed as new. Equivalent unpublished or unindexed derivations cannot be excluded.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
