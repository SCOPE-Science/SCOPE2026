# A rational cyclic-cubic criterion for tame elliptic covers over \(\mathbf F_2\)

## Finding
For every elliptic curve \(E/\mathbf F_2\), a finite separable everywhere tamely ramified morphism \(E\to\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf F_2}\) of degree \(3\) exists **if and only if** \(\#E(\mathbf F_2)=3\). When it exists, such a morphism is geometrically Galois with cyclic group of order \(3\), and its geometric ramification consists of exactly three totally ramified points. Among the five \(\mathbf F_2\)-isomorphism classes of elliptic curves, the class with three rational points is the only one admitting a tame cubic map. In addition, the number of Frobenius-stable order-three subgroups of \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\overline{\mathbf F}_2}(E,O)\) is exactly \(2\) for this class and \(0\) for the other four classes.

This is a statement about **tame degree three**, not existence of tame covers of higher degree. The presence of a tame cubic is equivalent to tame gonality exactly \(3\): separable degree-two covers in characteristic two are never everywhere tame.

## Assumptions and scope
All curves are smooth, proper, geometrically connected elliptic curves over \(k=\mathbf F_2\) with their given \(k\)-rational origins. All coverings are finite, separable, nonconstant, and everywhere tamely ramified, with ramification indices measured geometrically. Frobenius means coefficient squaring. An origin-fixing automorphism subgroup is counted as a subgroup, not as a choice of generator or as an arbitrary translated deck group.

An explicit complete list of elliptic \(k\)-isomorphism-class representatives is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&y^2+xy=x^3+1 && (\#E(k)=4),\\
&y^2+xy=x^3+x^2+1 && (\#E(k)=2),\\
&E_{0,0}:\ y^2+y=x^3 && (\#E(k)=3),\\
&E_{1,0}:\ y^2+y=x^3+x && (\#E(k)=5),\\
&E_{1,1}:\ y^2+y=x^3+x+1 && (\#E(k)=1).
\end{aligned}
\]
These are the usual characteristic-two normal forms obtained by Weierstrass coordinate changes; their counts follow by evaluating the two affine \(x\)-coordinates and adding the point at infinity. In particular the ordinary models have \(j\ne0\); the final three are supersingular, with \(j=0\). This list is also independently tabulated in standard elliptic-curve classifications; the cubic-cover obstruction below does not rely on a census of auxiliary equations.

## Proof
**1. Tame degree-three covers are geometrically cyclic.** Let \(f:E\to\mathbf P^1_k\) be tame and separable of degree \(3\). After base change to \(\overline{k}\), Riemann–Hurwitz gives
\[
0=-6+\sum_{P\in E(\overline{k})}(e_P-1).
\]
In characteristic two a tame ramification index for a cubic map is either \(1\) or \(3\); therefore there are precisely three geometric ramification points, each with index \(3\), above distinct branch values. In the geometric Galois closure, every inertia generator acts as a three-cycle in \(S_3\). The global monodromy group is generated by the inertia groups: the quotient by their normal closure would define an unramified cover of \(\mathbf P^1_{\overline{k}}\), which is trivial. Hence monodromy is the cyclic subgroup \(A_3\) and \(f\) is geometrically Galois of group \(C_3\). Its deck subgroup \(H\subset\operatorname{Aut}_{\overline{k}}(E)\) is Frobenius-stable because \(f\) is defined over \(k\).

**2. The origin-fixing obstruction.** Relative to the rational origin \(O\), the geometric automorphism group decomposes as \(E(\overline{k})\rtimes\operatorname{Aut}_{\overline{k}}(E,O)\). Projection onto the second factor is Frobenius-equivariant. Its restriction to the order-three deck subgroup \(H\) is injective: otherwise \(H\) consists of nontrivial translations, which have no fixed points, contrary to geometric ramification. Thus any tame cubic forces a Frobenius-stable subgroup of order three in the origin-fixing group. For ordinary elliptic curves in characteristic two, \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\overline{k}}(E,O)\) has order two; hence neither ordinary \(\mathbf F_2\)-class admits such a cover.

**3. Frobenius descent on supersingular twists.** Work with
\[
E_{a,b}:\quad y^2+y=x^3+a x+b,\qquad a,b\in\mathbf F_2.
\]
Every geometric origin-fixing automorphism can be expressed in the usual Weierstrass variables by
\[
\tau(x)=u^2x+r,\qquad \tau(y)=u^3y+u^2sx+t,
\]
where \(u^3=1\) and comparison of the Weierstrass coefficients yields
\[
s=r^2+a(1+u),\qquad s^2=r,\qquad t^2+t=r^3+ar.
\]
For a nonidentity order-three automorphism, \(u\) must be a primitive cubic root of unity (the kernel of the multiplier map is a two-group). Choose its orientation such that \(u^2+u+1=0\). If \(\langle\tau\rangle\) is Frobenius-stable, Frobenius sends \(\tau\) to \(\tau^2\), since it sends \(u\) to \(u^2\). Comparing their translations in the \(x\)-coordinate gives
\[
r^2=(1+u^2)r=u r,\quad\text{hence}\quad r\in\{0,u\}.
\]
If \(a=1\) and \(r=0\), the displayed equations give \(s=1+u=u^2\) but \(s^2=u\ne r\). If \(a=1\) and \(r=u\), they give \(s=u^2+(1+u)=0\) but \(s^2=0\ne r\). So neither of the supersingular classes with \(a=1\) has a Frobenius-stable cubic origin-fixing subgroup, and a tame cubic morphism cannot exist on either twist.

If \(a=0\), each of \(r=0\) and \(r=u\) produces precisely one Frobenius-stable order-three subgroup: for \(r=0\) the generator is \((u,r,s,t)=(u,0,0,0)\), and for \(r=u\) it is \((u,u,u^2,u)\), with the transformation coordinates above. Direct substitution verifies order three and Frobenius conjugation \(\tau^{(2)}=\tau^2\). These are distinct subgroups because their multipliers identify a unique generator in each. Conversely, the restriction \(r\in\{0,u\}\) shows completeness. Hence the stated subgroup counts are \(2\) for \(a=0\) and \(0\) for \(a=1\).

**4. Existence and sharp degree.** On \(E_{0,0}\), the coordinate function \(y\) gives a degree-three map to the projective line, because \(x^3=y^2+y\). The extension of function fields is separable since \(3x^2\) is a nonzero derivative. Its only geometric ramification occurs at the two simple roots of \(y^2+y\) and at infinity, with index three at each; thus the cover is everywhere tame. A separable quadratic cover in characteristic two would have only wild ramification, so an everywhere tame one would have to be unramified, contradicting Riemann–Hurwitz for a genus-one source and genus-zero target. Consequently the minimal tame degree for \(E_{0,0}\) is three. Since the five forms above exhaust the \(k\)-isomorphism classes, the result follows. \(\square\)

## Verification
An executable pure-Python checker `verify_tame_cubic.py` implements exact arithmetic in \(\mathbf F_{16}\), enumerates the geometric origin-fixing automorphisms of the three supersingular representatives, tests order three and Frobenius descent, and checks the three rational-point counts. It returns exactly eight nonidentity order-three generators in each of the three supersingular automorphism groups, but respectively \(2,0,0\) Frobenius-stable cyclic subgroups. Its finite calculation corroborates the complete explicit automorphism argument rather than replacing it. The two ordinary models and absence of tame quadratic maps follow directly from the standard group description and Riemann–Hurwitz, not from a computer search.

## Relationship to prior work
Kedlaya–Litt–Witaszek (arXiv:2010.01130, 2020) prove existence of some tame map for every elliptic curve over a finite field and explicitly observe that \(y\) yields a tame map for the characteristic-two supersingular model with \(a=0\) (Remark 8.4); neither existence nor this construction is claimed as new. The present result gives the **degree-three if-and-only-if arithmetic descent obstruction** for all \(\mathbf F_2\) twists and refines it by enumerating rationally stable order-three origin-fixing subgroups. Fukasawa–Speziali (arXiv:1801.03198) classify plane curves with two outer Galois points under additional group hypotheses; their classification does not provide this finite-field one-cover descent criterion. Helminck (arXiv:1711.07034) considers tame degree-three covers in a different, characteristic-zero/prime-to-six residue setting.

## Limitations
The theorem does not determine the minimal higher tame degrees for the other four classes; known general tame-existence results apply to them. The numerical origin-fixing subgroup refinement does not count all degree-three rational functions or translated cyclic deck subgroups. The novelty assessment is limited to inspected published sources and research-record searches; an unindexed or unpublished equivalent classification could predate this statement. No independent audit has been performed.
