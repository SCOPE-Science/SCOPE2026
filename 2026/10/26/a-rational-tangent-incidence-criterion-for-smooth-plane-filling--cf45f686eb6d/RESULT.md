# A rational tangent-incidence criterion for smooth plane-filling quintics over \(\mathbf F_2\)

## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbf F_2\), let \(Z=\mathbf P^2(k)\) be the seven-point Fano plane, and let \(Z^\vee\) be its seven rational lines. An *incident tangent assignment* is a map \(\tau:Z\to Z^\vee\) with \(P\in\tau(P)\) for every \(P\). Every such assignment comes from a **unique** homogeneous quintic \(F_\tau\in k[X,Y,Z]_5\) which vanishes at all seven rational points and is nonsingular at those points, with tangent line \(\tau(P)\) at \(P\).

Write \(m_L=|\tau^{-1}(L)|\). The plane quintic \(V(F_\tau)\) is geometrically smooth **if and only if** precisely one of the following conditions holds:

1. Its nonzero \(m_L\)'s form the partition \((2,1,1,1,1,1)\).
2. Its nonzero \(m_L\)'s form \((2,2,1,1,1)\).
3. Its nonzero \(m_L\)'s form \((2,2,2,1)\), and the three lines with \(m_L=2\) are **not concurrent** over \(k\) (equivalently, their three row covectors are linearly independent over \(k\)).

The first, second and third classes contain, respectively, **420, 924 and 168** assignments. Thus exactly **1512 of the 2187** rationally nonsingular plane-filling quintics are geometrically smooth. They constitute **10** orbits under \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(k)\): three in the first class, six in the second and one in the third. All 675 others have geometric singularities *away from the seven rational points*.

In particular, three rational bitangent lines (each tangent at two distinct rational points of the curve) can occur together on such a smooth quintic only if they form a triangle rather than a concurrent triple. This last case occurs in exactly one projective orbit, containing 168 equations.

## Assumptions and scope
All curves are degree-five plane divisors defined by **nonzero scalar-normalized** forms over \(k\); because \(k^\times=\{1\}\), an equation has no nontrivial scalar ambiguity. "Plane-filling" means containing every point of \(Z\), not all points over extension fields. Geometric smoothness means regularity after extension to an algebraic closure. An *ordinary rational tangent* here means the unique tangent line at a smooth rational point; two such points sharing a line produce a rational bitangent in this incidence sense, without imposing a prescribed higher contact order. All counts are of embedded equations/assignments, not abstract isomorphism classes.

A concurrency test refers to a common projective point of the three \(k\)-lines. Since the lines are defined over \(k\), a triple is concurrent geometrically if and only if its defining covectors have rank at most two over \(k\).

## Proof
**1. Unique first-jet interpolation.** The space \(k[X,Y,Z]_5\) has dimension 21. At each of the seven rational points, retain the value and two independent partial derivatives, making 21 linear jet conditions. The kernel consists of quintics vanishing to order at least two at every point of \(Z\). Every rational line contains three of these points. A degree-five restriction to that line with three double zeros must vanish identically, so all seven distinct rational lines divide any kernel element. Their product has degree seven, forcing that kernel element to be zero. Therefore the first-jet map is an isomorphism. For a vanishing form the Euler relation \(F=X F_X+Y F_Y+Z F_Z\) holds in characteristic two; the gradient at each rational point lies in its incidence plane. There are exactly three nonzero such gradient vectors, each representing one incident tangent line. No scaling ambiguity exists over \(k\). Thus all \(3^7=2187\) assignments have unique locally nonsingular lifts.

**2. Obvious geometric obstruction.** If any line has three assigned tangent points, its intersection with \(V(F_\tau)\) has total multiplicity at least six, greater than the curve's degree five. That line is a component. By Bézout it meets the residual quartic, and the plane divisor is singular over the algebraic closure. Consequently all 567 assignments with a fiber of size three are excluded. (An incident assignment cannot have a larger fiber, because a line contains only three rational points.)

**3. Finite exact classification for the remaining configurations.** Identify a point or line covector with a nonzero vector of three bits. The 168 invertible binary \(3\times3\) matrices act on points and contragrediently on line covectors. Exhausting their action partitions all 2187 assignments into exactly 18 orbits; all orbit sizes and representative quintic bitmasks appear in `orbit_table.tsv`. For every representative the 21-by-21 first-jet system is solved by exact \(\mathbf F_2\)-linear algebra. For each of the three affine charts, form the ideal generated by its three homogeneous partial derivatives with the chart coordinate set to one. Buchberger's algorithm in \(\mathbf F_2[u,v]\) decides whether this ideal contains one. Such unit ideals on all three charts are **equivalent** to geometric smoothness, since the Nullstellensatz detects *all* algebraic-closure singular points; Euler's identity makes an additional equation \(F=0\) unnecessary for degree five in characteristic two. The exact table yields ten smooth orbits and eight singular orbits. An independently implemented symbolic Gröbner calculation agrees on all 18 representatives.

**4. Classification and counts.** The 18-orbit results show that every assignment of partition \((2,1,1,1,1,1)\) or \((2,2,1,1,1)\) is geometrically smooth; all 24 bijective assignments are singular; and among the 252 assignments with partition \((2,2,2,1)\), precisely the 84 with concurrent doubled lines are singular. The 168 others are smooth. The remaining 567 assignments have a triple fiber and are singular by step 2 (also independently checked algebraically). Summing the 10 good orbit sizes yields \(420+924+168=1512\). The singular count is \(567+24+84=675\), and all cases total 2187.

## Verification
`verify.py` is a complete Python-standard-library verifier, reconstructing the 21-by-21 first-jet inverse, enumerating all 2187 assignments and 168 group elements, obtaining all 18 representative orbits, testing all affine partial-derivative ideals with a bivariate Buchberger implementation over \(\mathbf F_2\), and comparing each result against the stated incidence criterion. Run `python3 verify.py`; its full expected stdout is in `expected_output.txt`. The last line is `VERIFY_OK`. The 18 representative coefficient masks, sizes, tangent partitions, concurrency flags and geometric-smoothness decisions are also serialized in `orbit_table.tsv`.

`crosscheck_sympy.py` independently builds all 18 representative equations from these coefficient masks and runs SymPy's independent \(\mathbf F_2\) Gröbner implementation on all three affine charts. Run `python3 crosscheck_sympy.py` in the same directory (requires SymPy); its expected last line is `INDEPENDENT_GROEBNER_CROSSCHECK_18_ORBITS_OK`. The two implementations were executed and agreed on every representative. A separate enumeration of all 16,383 nonzero plane-filling quintic forms, using a different coefficient-nullspace basis and symbolic smoothness tests on 139 form-orbit representatives, independently returned 1512 smooth equations in ten projective orbits. This last search is corroboration, not a dependency for the packaged proof.

For `orbit_table.tsv`, bits are indexed by the 21 monomials \(X^aY^bZ^{5-a-b}\), ordered by increasing \(a\), then increasing \(b\); the decimal mask's bit \(i\) is the corresponding coefficient in \(\mathbf F_2\). Thus the catalogue and all computations use an unambiguous normalization.

## Relationship to prior work
Asgarli–Ghioca (first public arXiv version 2023-07-06) study plane-filling curves of degree \(q+3\) and exhibit families with rational-point smoothness criteria; their general theorems and conjectures are not a classification of all seven-point tangent assignments for quintics over \(\mathbf F_2\). Huang–Kedlaya–Lau (first public arXiv version 2024-02-01) provide a **complete genus-six curve census**, including all smooth binary plane quintic equations. In particular, the mere smooth-equation count and ten plane-embedding orbits should **not** be presented independently as unprecedented: the underlying equations occur within their broader data. The result distinguished here is the explicit *if-and-only-if combinatorial criterion for geometric smoothness from rational first jets*, including the exceptional concurrency obstruction, together with its exact Gauss-fiber distributions. This characterization is not stated in their paper's smooth-plane-quintic section or the accompanying `plane_quintic` README examined here. Faber–Grantham's work on point counts and gonality provides broader small-field context rather than such a local-to-global tangent test.

## Limitations
This is a finite characteristic-two classification at field size two and degree five, not an all-field/all-degree result. The 2024 database contains complete raw equations and could permit the new characterization to be recovered by subsequent processing; that is a substantive priority and subsumption risk, not resolved by unsuccessful searches. No independent mathematical audit has been conducted. The source files provide reproducible finite proof calculations rather than a formal theorem-prover certificate. The computation tests geometric singularities using exact ideals, not bounded extension-field point searches.
