# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The modular discriminant identity is reproduced by a direct resultant computation. The five partials vanish identically on the triple-root and square loci. Étale local factorization and the quadratic discriminant prove smoothness at every remaining \((2,1,1)\) partition, giving complete reduced singular support. The square-map and Segre geometric arguments establish the component degrees and inseparability. On the embedded square surface, the pullbacks \(C=t^2-su\) and \(AE-BD=-su(t^2-su)\) prove the smooth conic scheme intersection and its rational-quartic image. A runnable exact-arithmetic checker independently reproduces the polynomial identities. The finite-point check is corroborating only.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Chipalkatti (arXiv:math/0110224), Kurmann (arXiv:1108.4532), and Katz (arXiv:math/0211281) analyze coincident roots and discriminants in characteristic zero or over real/complex ground fields. Their results do not imply the characteristic-three inseparability and Segre–Veronese decomposition. Searches of mathematical-record summaries for exact and aliased versions found no covering statement; closest indexed records study a characteristic-two binary-quartic discriminant and characteristic-three binary-cubic normalization, both different objects. Historical modular invariant-theory coverage is not exhaustive; an equivalent formulation could exist.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a complete natural singular-support classification for the universal binary-quartic discriminant in an exceptional characteristic, including degrees, intersection scheme, and the Frobenius mechanism behind its collapsed triple-root stratum. It distinguishes characteristic-three geometry from standard characteristic-zero root-locus expectations. It is not an arbitrary finite parameter slice.

## Limitations
