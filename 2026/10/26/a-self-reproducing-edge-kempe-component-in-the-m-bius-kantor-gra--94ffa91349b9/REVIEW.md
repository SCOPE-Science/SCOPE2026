# Review of the Möbius–Kantor edge-Kempe graph classification

## Correctness — PASS
The graph and state equivalence are fully specified. The finite-state proof generates every perfect matching and therefore every unordered three-edge factorization. An independent proper-edge-colouring recursion produces exactly the same twenty states. The two complete adjacency constructions agree on all twenty-seven edges; component calculations return one sixteen-vertex cubic component and one three-leaf star. An explicit sixteen-state bijection verifies adjacency and nonadjacency against the defining Möbius–Kantor graph, and direct cycle decomposition verifies every claimed profile. These are exhaustive checks, not numerical extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS (subject to residual literature risk)
Belcastro–Haas (arXiv:1209.1730) treat Kempe equivalence-class counts, not this named graph's complete exchange isomorphism. Their 2017 treatment establishes general properties of edge-Kempe equivalence graphs and contains a worked crossed-prism example but no Möbius–Kantor classification. Goedgebeur–Östergård (arXiv:2105.01363) tabulate class counts throughout bounded-order cubic graphs, but their displayed tables aggregate numbers of classes instead of giving this graph's twenty-state graph, explicit self-isomorphism, or its individual factorization profiles. A targeted search for the same graph under generalized Petersen, incidence-configuration, one-factorization, and edge-Kempe terminology found no covering statement. Potential unindexed exact computer classifications remain a risk; absence from a search is not a proof of global novelty.

## Value — PASS
The original graph reappearing as an entire component of its **edge-colouring reconfiguration graph** is a specific structural phenomenon for a canonical nonplanar cubic symmetric graph, not a routine scalar recount. The classification also resolves the exact disconnected-state geometry and explains it through three sharply different bichromatic cycle profiles. It refines the known general component-count framework in a natural low-order example.

## Scientific limitations
The assertion is finite and for one graph only, uses unlabelled colour classes but keeps graph vertices fixed, and is supported by independently cross-checked exhaustive recursions and a precise isomorphism certificate. No claim about all cubic symmetric graphs or about world-wide publication priority is made.
