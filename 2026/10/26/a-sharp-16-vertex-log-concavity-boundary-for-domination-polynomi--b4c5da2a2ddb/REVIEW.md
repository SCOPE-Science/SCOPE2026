# Research assessment

## Correctness
PASS. Every trivially perfect graph is a rooted-forest transitive closure. The root/disjoint-union domination-polynomial recurrences are exact. The attached nondecreasing-multiset generator enumerates all rooted forest shapes of sizes at most sixteen, and exact integer inequalities yield no failure below sixteen and precisely one at sixteen. Independent subset enumeration corroborates all coefficients of the explicit witness. The infinite family follows from its first three coefficients and a positive quadratic factor. Finite minimality depends on the transparent exhaustive certificate rather than a noncomputational exclusion theorem.

## Originality
PASS. Rather (2025), Theorem 2.16, already covers the join-of-cliques polynomial, so the witness formula alone is not claimed as original. That work leaves questions about coefficient shape of general clique-join constructions; Omar (2026) treats threshold graphs. The contribution here is the sharp first counterexample order and its uniqueness across the larger class of all trivially perfect graphs, requiring an exhaustive comparison over all rooted-forest forms rather than substitution into the join formula. Targeted literature and research-record searches found no theorem or table covering this exact cutoff; a differently indexed enumeration remains a risk.

## Value
PASS. The smallest failing order and unique extremal graph identify a natural, precise boundary to the search for log-concavity beyond threshold graphs. Exact minimal counterexamples and finite cutoffs are useful even without a theorem describing all larger graphs; the infinite family shows this is not an isolated computational anomaly. The result concerns log-concavity, not failure of unimodality.

## Closest work and limits
Rather (2025), DOI 10.61091/jcmcc126-19, Theorem 2.16 explicitly gives a relevant general polynomial; Omar (2026), arXiv:2601.14494, proves the threshold-graph result. Neither provides an exact all-trivially-perfect census to order sixteen in the material examined. The enumerator is part of the correctness argument and has not been independently validated. Publication priority under an alternative class name is not ruled out.
