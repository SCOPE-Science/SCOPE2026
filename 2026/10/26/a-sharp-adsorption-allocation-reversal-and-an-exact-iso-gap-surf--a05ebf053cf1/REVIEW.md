# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

All rates are strictly positive and the reversible weights and conductances satisfy detailed balance. The Rayleigh quotient places the first positive eigenvalue strictly between zero and the common surface desorption rate. Eliminating the surface states gives the exact three-by-three secular determinant and its derivative in the adsorption product at fixed total intensity. The irreducible path has simple eigenvalues and a one-sign-change first excited mode. This identifies the balanced gap, the exact all-allocation plateau, and strict monotonic directions on either side without numerical extrapolation. An independent generalized-eigenvalue implementation passed 4,106 numerical comparisons and verified the determinant factorization symbolically. Risk: the proof requires strict positivity, exactly five states, and a common desorption rate.

## Originality — PASS, subject to priority limits

The surface adsorption/desorption model and the general birth–death spectral theory are established. The separate claim assessed here is the sharp three-regime fixed-sum coating allocation with a *critical central storage mass yielding exactly allocation-independent gap*. Actual prior-source comparisons included wall-adsorption transport, numerical sticky-boundary diffusion, and general Jacobi eigenvalue-variation theory. No inspected source states this joint three-regime result. In particular, the general directional eigenvalue formulas are not an explicit implication of a fixed-sum perturbation with opposite endpoint changes; model-specific elimination establishes the sign and the exact zero derivative. A relevant 2012 original article could not be fully inspected, and specialized priority uncertainty remains; the claim of originality is best-knowledge rather than a uniqueness certificate.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a motivated surface-coating control problem for reversible equilibration, not a renaming of an isolated spectral computation. The central compartment's storage capacity reverses the optimal split, and the critical value yields a continuum of distinct stationary adsorbate distributions with the *same* relaxation gap. Both the optimizer reversal and the gap-flat surface are sharp, necessary-and-sufficient global statements; they can guide coarse-grained design and reveal when spectral-gap measurements cannot distinguish surface allocation. Future continuum modelling may or may not preserve the effect.
