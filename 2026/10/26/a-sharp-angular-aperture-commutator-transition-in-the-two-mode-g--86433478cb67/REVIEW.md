# Review: A sharp angular-aperture commutator transition in the two-mode Gaussian Fock space

## Correctness — PASS
The polar-coordinate integrals and Gaussian normalization give the displayed two-by-two matrices with exact incomplete-gamma coefficient. The sharp angular first-harmonic rearrangement is strict outside circular arcs. The Pauli commutator identity, concave quadratic maximization, and strictly decreasing integral formula for the critical ratio together prove the bound, all equality cases, and exactly one transition radius. A packaged checker passes 1,353 symbolic and finite numerical checks; those experiments support, but do not replace, the proof for all measurable sets and real radii.

## Originality — PASS (best knowledge)
Primary comparisons include Keller–Luef (arXiv:1905.07741, smooth-symbol asymptotic commutator calculus), Dewage–Ólafsson (arXiv:2107.01465, commuting quasi-radial Fock operators), and Galbis (arXiv:2102.02031, a radial single-operator norm estimate). None of their inspected relevant statements implies the exact disk-clipped angular-mask design optimum or its phase boundary. Semantically related published indexed results about abstract rank-one projections and other commutators do not cover these symbol classes. A mathematically equivalent unindexed aperture result remains possible; absence from searches is not proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The theorem answers a natural exact design question for finite-band Gaussian localization: how to choose two prescribed-area angular apertures to maximize incompatibility. Its continuum shape rigidity and strictly unique radius-dependent change in optimizing geometry supply information not present in the trivial diagonal radial case or the generic two-by-two commutator inequality. The conclusion is deliberately limited to the constant-plus-linear Fock band and common radial cutoff.

## Scientific limitations and risks
The result is not a full-Fock-space operator bound, does not handle unequal cutoff radii or higher polynomial bands, and does not establish a new semiclassical quantization law. An independent check of the mathematical proof and bibliographic priority has not been performed.
