# Review of the edge-density resetting theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** The Brownian first-hit transform, the independent-launch renewal identity, and the incomplete-gamma integral are explicitly derived in `RESULT.md`. The large-rate asymptotic is uniform in the nonnegative gap because its relative correction is bounded by the incomplete-gamma tail plus \(G_p(k)\). Comparison of **all global** minimizers with the trial \(k=(2-p)/d\) forces convergence to the unique minimizer of \(e^v/v^{2-p}\); no unproved finite-gap uniqueness is used. At \(p=2\), the exact elementary kernel proves a strict finite-optimum advantage over the infinite-rate limit. The last-launch Gamma law follows from its explicitly normalized, exponentially tilted density. `verify.py` supplies independent finite-parameter checks of the numerical constants and the elementary critical kernel; the infinite-parameter statements rest on the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with identified uncertainty.** Méndez–Flaquer-Galmés–Campos (2024), arXiv:2401.01125, supplies the distributed-reset formula and analyzes uniform spatial resets; its expressions already imply the displayed first-passage renewal law. Julián-Salgado–Dagdug–Boyer (2025), arXiv:2507.14483, analyzes discontinuous optimum-rate transitions and obtains the tilted successful-last-launch density, but its explicit edge Laplace estimate assumes finite nonzero density at the closest reset site. Neither inspected statement gives the sharp trichotomy in the edge exponent, the universal constants for all global optima as the closest reset site approaches the target, or the optimized Gamma law. These conclusions require controlling optimization concurrently with the singular near-edge limit. The implicit general formulas remain a significant risk of an overlooked equivalent asymptotic treatment; the literature search is not a uniqueness certificate.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Resetting distributions with small probability near the target are central to interpreting the competition between common, distant relaunches and rare, nearby ones. The theorem locates the exact suppression threshold for diverging optimal reset frequency at the linear edge-density law and quantifies both the optimal frequency and the optimized first-passage time on either side. The coupled Gamma description identifies *where successful final searches begin* under an optimally tuned protocol. Its control of every global minimizer remains valid even when finite-parameter landscapes admit multiple stationary points, which is important in light of the 2025 coexistence results.

**Closest literature and limits.** The 2024 paper's uniform reset case corresponds to \(p=1\) and provides qualitative optimal-rate behavior, not the sharp gap coefficient. The 2025 paper's general renewal and last-launch identities are prior facts and are explicitly attributed. The theorem does not cover general slowly varying edge densities, nonlinear drift, non-Poisson resets, or global optimizer uniqueness. No independent expert audit was performed.
