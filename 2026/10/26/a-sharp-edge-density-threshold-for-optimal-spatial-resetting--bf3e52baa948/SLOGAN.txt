For Brownian searches reset to a bounded spatial distribution with edge density proportional to the \((p-1)\)-st power of distance, the gap-to-target limit has a sharp \(p=2\) threshold: below it every globally optimal reset rate scales as \(D(2-p)^2/d^2\), while at or above it optimal rates remain finite. Below the threshold the minimized mean time scales as \(d^{2-p}\), and successful final launches have a universal Gamma-distributed distance from the edge on the scale \(d\).
