Analytical checks recorded for this result:

- The definition of \(Q\) uses the exact operator norm and is homogeneous of degree \(p\).
- For even \(p\), the norm powers are polynomial expressions and \(Q\ge0\) for all real inputs.
- The excluded scalar-isometry case is exactly the case in which \(Q\) can vanish identically.
- A nonzero nonnegative real form has even multiplicity at each real projective zero, including the direction represented in the reciprocal coordinate chart.
- The number of distinct real projective zero directions is at most \(p/2\), yielding at most \(p\) antipodal unit vectors.
- For \(p=4\), the exact expansion and equality condition give the four stated norm-attainment points; two unit inputs have unequal image norms, so the example is not a scalar multiple of an isometry.

Limits: no numerical experiment is used to establish the theorem. The argument does not give sharpness for even \(p\ge6\), and it does not cover odd exponents, complex spaces, or dimensions above two. Independent audit and external validation are not reported here.
