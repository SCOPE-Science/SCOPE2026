# Self-review

## Correctness
The finite cycle decomposition gives an exact first-moment identity for all dicyclic orders. Two noncentral coset cycles have length two and the remaining \(n-1\) have length four; the rotation cases are obtained from an explicit exponent-shift permutation. The inequality \(I_L\le(5/3)^L\) for \(L\ge3\) follows inductively; its consequence proves positivity for all \(n\ge80\). Exact integer sweeps establish the negative and positive signs on the remaining finite range and recover the boundary values. An independent full group-subset census at orders 8, 12, 16, 20, 24 reproduces the first moments. The finite check is not substituted for the infinite proof.

## Originality
Amit et al., arXiv:2610.12912v1, §8, explicitly identify the dicyclic family as requiring further lower-order moment analysis. Their general character-theoretic formula provides a possible route to specializing the first moment but does not itself state or resolve the sharp sign transition. Mandal and Neetu's arXiv:2602.09073v1 tables contain finite cardinality counts through dicyclic order 20, not this universal statement. Targeted literature and research-record searches did not identify a prior \(n=30\) sign threshold. Residual risk: equivalent manipulations of the published general formula or an unindexed earlier calculation.

## Value
The first-moment sign is a statistic specifically used in recent work to investigate product/quotient dominance in groups that escape simpler asymptotic criteria. An exact phase transition at order 120, with an analytic proof beyond all finite checks, gives a structural fact about an explicitly open family. The first moment does not settle majority dominance; this distinction is essential.
