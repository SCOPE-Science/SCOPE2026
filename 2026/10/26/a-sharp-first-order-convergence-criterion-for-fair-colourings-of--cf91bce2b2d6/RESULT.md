# A sharp first-order convergence criterion for fair colourings of equal-block equivalence structures

## Finding

Let \(b\) range through the positive integers, with \(b\to\infty\), and let \(m_b\to\infty\) be arbitrary positive integers. Take an equivalence relation with exactly \(m_b\) classes, each of size \(b\), and independently colour every vertex red with probability \(1/2\). Let \(P\) denote the unary red predicate and \(E\) the equivalence relation. One may use either a fixed labelled partition or a uniformly sampled labelled partition having these class sizes: first-order sentence probabilities agree.

For every fixed integer \(j\geq0\), define
\[
 a_{j,b}=m_b\binom bj2^{-b}.
\]

**Complete convergence criterion.** The resulting random \(\{E,P\}\)-structures satisfy a first-order convergence law if and only if, for every fixed \(j\geq0\), the sequence \(a_{j,b}\) has a limit in the extended interval \([0,+\infty]\). If all these limits belong to \(\{0,+\infty\}\), the law is a zero–one law.

If one limit is finite and strictly positive, it is necessarily unique: there is an index \(k\geq0\) and a number \(\lambda\in(0,\infty)\) for which \(a_{k,b}\to\lambda\), while \(a_{j,b}\to0\) for every \(j<k\) and \(a_{j,b}\to+\infty\) for every \(j>k\). The numbers of classes with exactly \(k\) red vertices and with exactly \(k\) nonred vertices converge jointly to **independent** Poisson variables \(Z_+,Z_-\), both with mean \(\lambda\).

In this critical case the *complete set* of attainable first-order limiting probabilities is exactly
\[
 \bigl\{\Pr((Z_+,Z_-)\in B): B\text{ belongs to the finite Boolean algebra generated by coordinate threshold events}\bigr\}.
\]
Here "finite Boolean algebra generated" means each event is a finite Boolean combination of \(\{Z_+\geq t\}\) and \(\{Z_-\geq t\}\), for nonnegative integer thresholds \(t\). In particular, the existence of at least one class of each rare kind has limiting probability \((1-e^{-\lambda})^2\), so there is no zero–one law.

The critical scales form an explicit logarithmic hierarchy: for each fixed \(k\geq0\),
\[
 m_b\sim\lambda k!\,2^b/b^k
\]
is exactly the regime with the \(k\)-th critical pair. In the canonical choice \(m_b=\lfloor k!\,2^b/b^k\rfloor\) (for sufficiently large \(b\)), \(\lambda=1\), and all the limits are effectively obtainable as finite polynomials in \(e^{-1}\) with rational coefficients.

## Assumptions and scope

The vocabulary is exactly one binary equivalence symbol \(E\), one unary predicate \(P\), and equality. Blocks are equipotent, there are \(m_b\) blocks of size \(b\), both \(m_b\) and \(b\) tend to infinity, and all vertex colours are independent unbiased Bernoulli choices. No ordering, counting quantifier, or additional relation is available. "First-order convergence" means convergence of the satisfaction probability of **every fixed** first-order sentence as \(b\to\infty\). The necessary-and-sufficient criterion does not assume a computable rule for \(m_b\); consequently no uniform algorithm for inferring the critical index from an arbitrary sequence is claimed.

## Proof

**Step 1 (the necessary parameters are observable).** Put \(p_{j,b}=\binom bj2^{-b}\), so \(a_{j,b}=m_bp_{j,b}\). Let \(N_{j,b}\) count blocks with exactly \(j\) red vertices. The statement \(N_{j,b}\geq1\) is first-order: a representative \(x\) of the block may be chosen, followed by a finite list of precisely \(j\) distinct red vertices in its \(E\)-class, with a universal condition excluding any further red vertices. For fixed \(j\), \(p_{j,b}\to0\) and
\[
 \Pr(N_{j,b}\geq1)=1-(1-p_{j,b})^{m_b}.
\]
Writing \(r_{j,b}=-m_b\log(1-p_{j,b})\), we have \(r_{j,b}/a_{j,b}\to1\) when \(a_{j,b}>0\), because \(-\log(1-p)/p\to1\). As the map \(r\mapsto1-e^{-r}\) is a homeomorphism from \([0,+\infty]\) to \([0,1]\), convergence of this first-order probability forces \(a_{j,b}\) to converge in \([0,+\infty]\). This holds for every fixed \(j\), establishing necessity.

**Step 2 (only one positive finite intensity is possible).** For fixed \(j\),
\[
 \frac{a_{j+1,b}}{a_{j,b}}=\frac{b-j}{j+1}\longrightarrow+\infty.
\]
Thus if \(a_{k,b}\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)\), every lower fixed index has limit zero and every higher fixed index has limit infinity. Two distinct finite positive intensities are impossible. For \(b>2k\), the types "exactly \(k\) red" and "exactly \(k\) nonred" are disjoint. Their count vector is multinomial with \(m_b\) trials and cell probabilities \(p_{k,b},p_{k,b},1-2p_{k,b}\). Its joint probability-generating function is
\[
 \bigl(1+p_{k,b}(s-1)+p_{k,b}(t-1)\bigr)^{m_b}
 \longrightarrow \exp\bigl(\lambda(s-1)+\lambda(t-1)\bigr).
\]
Hence the two counts converge to independent \(\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)\) variables. If \(a_{j,b}\to0\), the corresponding block count vanishes in probability by Markov's inequality. If \(a_{j,b}\to\infty\), the count is binomial with diverging mean and tends to infinity in probability, by its variance bound \(\operatorname{Var}N_{j,b}\leq\mathbb EN_{j,b}\).

**Step 3 (a finite-rank equivalence lemma).** For a coloured equivalence structure and an integer \(q\geq1\), associate to each block its *truncated colour type*
\[
 \bigl(\min\{\#P,q\},\min\{\#\neg P,q\}\bigr),
\]
and record the number of blocks of each type, again truncated at \(q\). Two structures with the same such profile satisfy the same sentences of quantifier rank at most \(q\). Indeed the duplicator wins the \(q\)-move Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game: when a new block is entered, match it to an unused block of the same type; when returning to a previously matched block, choose an unused vertex of the specified colour in the corresponding block. Truncation at \(q\) guarantees that no move exhausts a needed block or a needed colour supply. The correspondence preserves equality, \(E\), and \(P\) throughout.

**Step 4 (sufficiency and the complete spectrum).** Fix \(q\geq1\) and take \(b>2q\). The only truncated colour types are \((j,q)\) and \((q,j)\) for \(0\leq j<q\), and the central type \((q,q)\). Each extreme type has block-count mean \(a_{j,b}\); the central type occurs in a proportion tending to one among the \(m_b\) blocks and therefore has at least \(q\) representatives with probability tending to one. When every \(a_{j,b}\) has an extended limit, the counts of extreme types truncated at \(q\) tend either to zero, to the constant \(q\), or (at a single critical index) to the corresponding truncation of a Poisson variable. The two critical extremes are asymptotically independent by Step 2. By Step 3, the truth of every rank-\(q\) sentence is a Boolean function of this finite profile. Its probability therefore converges.

If there is no finite positive intensity, every profile becomes deterministic, giving a zero–one law. If \(a_{k,b}\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)\), every limiting rank-\(q\) sentence event is a finite Boolean combination of threshold comparisons in \(Z_+,Z_-\). Conversely, \(\{Z_+\geq t\}\) and \(\{Z_-\geq t\}\) are first-order expressible by asking for \(t\) pairwise inequivalent blocks, each containing exactly \(k\) elements of the corresponding colour. Thus every finite Boolean combination is attained, proving equality of the spectra. The critical scale follows from \(\binom bk\sim b^k/k!\).

## Verification

The accompanying `colour_block_verifier.py` uses only the Python standard library. Executing `python3 colour_block_verifier.py` prints `COLOURED_EQ_EXACT_OK 123856 1346 24`. It exhaustively counts binary colourings for small block parameters and checks the exact joint multinomial probabilities of the two complementary rare class types. It also tests 1,346 matched finite truncated-profile pairs against a direct finite Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game solver at depths one and two, and checks 24 finite critical-intensity calculations. These are checks of finite instances, not proofs of the universal convergence criterion or its asymptotic consequences.

## Relationship to prior work

Vera Koponen, *A characterization of the finite model property for \(\omega\)-categorical theories* (arXiv:2610.16163v1, publicly posted 12 October 2026), develops structural zero–one law criteria and explicitly discusses random expansions of finite base structures in Example 2.12 and Remark 2.15. The present theorem treats an explicit independent unary expansion of equipotent equivalence classes and supplies a necessary-and-sufficient **stochastic** convergence criterion, a complete zero–one/nonzero–one phase classification, and the exact limiting-probability spectrum. Those quantitative claims are not contained in Koponen's stated Theorem 3.1 or Corollary 2.14.

Ove Ahlman and Vera Koponen, *Limit laws and automorphism groups of random nonrigid structures* (arXiv:1305.3097v3), study uniform random finite relational structures conditioned on automorphism-group properties; their Sections 1 and 6 do not cover independent fair colourings of a fixed equal-block equivalence template. The standard Poisson law of small numbers and Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game characterization are used as classical tools, not claimed as new. A general theorem for independent-colour expansions that subsumes this particular complete criterion may exist outside the inspected literature; historical priority is not independently established.

## Limitations

The criterion is specific to **equal-size blocks**, **fair independent binary colours**, and **first-order** sentences. Unequal or dependent blocks, biased colours, monadic second-order quantification, and additional order relations are not treated. Effectivity is asserted for the explicitly computable canonical critical sequences, not arbitrary noncomputable input sequences. The finite checker is not an infinite certificate; independent assessment has not been performed.
