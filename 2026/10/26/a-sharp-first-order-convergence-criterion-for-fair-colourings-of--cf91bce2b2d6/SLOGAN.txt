Fairly colouring equal-size equivalence classes has a first-order convergence law exactly when every expected fixed-minority class count \(m_b\binom bj2^{-b}\) has an extended limit. At any nontrivial critical scale \(m_b\sim\lambda k!2^b/b^k\), the entire first-order probability spectrum is generated by **two independent** \(\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)\) counts of complementary rare block types; otherwise convergence, when it holds, is a zero–one law.
