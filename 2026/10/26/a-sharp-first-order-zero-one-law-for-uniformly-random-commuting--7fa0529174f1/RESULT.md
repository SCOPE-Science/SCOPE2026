# A sharp first-order zero-one law for uniformly random commuting involutions

## Finding

Choose an ordered pair of commuting involutions uniformly from
\[\mathcal C_n=\{(f,g)\in S_n^2:f^2=g^2=1,\ fg=gf\}.\]
Regard it as a first-order structure with equality and the two **total unary function symbols** \(f,g\). For every integer \(q\ge1\), let \(A_q\) be the finite structure consisting of exactly \(q\) orbits of each of the five transitive action types of the Klein four group. The orbit sizes are \(1,2,2,2,4\), so \(|A_q|=11q\).

**Theorem.** For every first-order sentence \(\varphi\) of quantifier rank at most \(q\),
\[
\Pr(( [n],f,g)\models\varphi)\longrightarrow {\bf1}_{A_q\models\varphi}.
\]
Thus there is an effective first-order zero-one law; moreover, **\(11q\) is the smallest cardinality of any finite structure realizing the almost-sure complete rank-\(q\) theory**. Put \(r=r_n>0\) for the unique root of
\[r+3r^2+r^4=n.\]
For every fixed \(q\), the probability that the random structure disagrees with \(A_q\) on even one sentence of rank at most \(q\) is bounded by
\[
O_q\!\left(\sqrt n\,r^{q-1}e^{-r}\right).
\]
In particular every rank-\(q\) sentence converges with stretched-exponential speed \(\exp(-(1-o(1))n^{1/4})\) when its probability differs from its limit. This exponential **rate exponent and its leading constant are sharp**, in the following parity-sensitive sense:
\[
\Pr(\nexists x\,(f(x)=x\wedge g(x)=x))=
\begin{cases}
0,&n\text{ odd},\\
2e^{-r_n}(1+o(1)),&n\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
Therefore the logarithmic error rate of the first-order sentence asserting a common fixed point is exactly \(-1\) on the \(n^{1/4}\) scale along even \(n\).

## Assumptions and scope

The pair \((f,g)\) is **uniform on commuting pairs conditioned to be involutions**, not a pair of independently sampled involutions and not an independent uniform commuting pair without the involution restriction. Here an involution may have fixed points. The group generated by \(f,g\) is a quotient of \(C_2\times C_2\). Each orbit is a transitive \(C_2\times C_2\)-set: one singleton (both maps trivial), three two-point types distinguished by which nonidentity group element stabilizes a point, and one four-point regular type. We index their counts by \(N_1,N_{2,a},N_{2,b},N_{2,c},N_4\). No predicate, linear order, counting quantifier, or varying signature is added. Quantifier rank \(q\ge1\) is fixed before \(n\to\infty\). The structure \(A_q\) has precisely \(q\) copies of **each** type.

## Proof

**1. Exact assembly law.** A labelled orbit of size \(d\) has \(d!/d\) distinct ways to carry each transitive action type: this follows because its automorphism group as an equivariant set has size \(d\). The exponential generating function for ordered commuting involution pairs is therefore
\[
F(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{|\mathcal C_n|}{n!}z^n
=\exp\!\left(z+\frac{3z^2}{2}+\frac{z^4}{4}\right).
\]
For any real \(r>0\), take five independent Poisson variables with respective means
\[
r,\quad r^2/2,\quad r^2/2,\quad r^2/2,\quad r^4/4.
\]
Condition on their weighted sum \(S=N_1+2(N_{2,a}+N_{2,b}+N_{2,c})+4N_4=n\). The resulting joint law is **exactly** the orbit-count law of a uniformly chosen element of \(\mathcal C_n\), since its mass is proportional to \(1/(N_1!\prod_j(2^{N_{2,j}}N_{2,j}!)\,4^{N_4}N_4!)\). Pick \(r=r_n\) by \(\mathbb ES=r+3r^2+r^4=n\). Then \(r_n\sim n^{1/4}\) and
\[V=\operatorname{Var}S=r+6r^2+4r^4\sim 4n.\]

**2. Local central coefficient.** The characteristic function of the centred lattice sum \(S\) has uncentred logarithm
\[
r(e^{it}-1)+\frac{3r^2}{2}(e^{2it}-1)+\frac{r^4}{4}(e^{4it}-1).
\]
Fourier inversion and Taylor expansion near \(t=0\) yield
\[\Pr(S=n)\sim (2\pi V)^{-1/2}.\]
Here is a justification of the possible competing lattice peaks. The modulus at any \(t\) is
\[
\exp\!\left(-r(1-\cos t)-\frac{3r^2}{2}(1-\cos2t)-\frac{r^4}{4}(1-\cos4t)\right).
\]
Away from the four points where \(\cos4t=1\), it is uniformly exponentially small in a diverging power of \(r\), after removing shrinking neighborhoods. Near the additional peaks \(t=\pm\pi/2\), the \(2t\) term contributes a factor \(e^{-3r^2}\); near \(t=\pi\), the singleton term contributes \(e^{-2r}\). All are negligible compared with the Gaussian peak width \(V^{-1/2}\asymp r^{-2}\). On a window \(|t|=O(r^{-2}\log r)\) around zero the cubic Taylor error is \(O(r^4|t|^3)=o(1)\) and the quadratic term is \(-Vt^2/2\). Outside such windows standard \(1-\cos u\ge c\operatorname{dist}(u,2\pi\mathbb Z)^2\) bounds control the integral. This proves the stated span-one local limit.

**3. All types appear many times.** For any fixed \(q\), the conditional estimate and independence before conditioning give
\[
\Pr\bigl(\min_jN_j<q\mid S=n\bigr)
\le\frac{\sum_j\Pr(\operatorname{Poi}(\lambda_j)<q)}{\Pr(S=n)}
=O_q\!\left(\sqrt n\,r^{q-1}e^{-r}\right),
\]
because the five intensities are \(r,\,r^2/2\) (three times), and \(r^4/4\); the first gives the dominant lower-tail estimate as \(r\to\infty\). This bound is uniform over **all** rank-\(q\) sentences simultaneously.

**4. Rank-\(q\) equivalence and optimality.** If two action structures have at least \(q\) orbits of every one of the five types, Duplicator wins their \(q\)-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game. Whenever Spoiler chooses a point in an already touched orbit, Duplicator maps it through the unique equivariant bijection determined by the earlier choice; when Spoiler enters a fresh orbit, Duplicator selects a previously untouched orbit of the same type. With fewer than \(q\) rounds previously touched orbits number at most \(q-1\). The maintained map is a partial isomorphism of the full unary-function signature because each touched orbit is mapped in full, preserving all term identities \(f,g,fg\). Hence all those structures share the rank-\(q\) theory of \(A_q\). Together with Step 3 this proves the effective zero-one law.

To show **minimality**, let \(\theta_T(x)\) be a quantifier-free formula defining membership in orbit type \(T\) through equalities among \(x,f(x),g(x),f(g(x))\). For instance, the singleton is \(f(x)=x\wedge g(x)=x\), and the size-four regular type is \(f(x)\ne x\wedge g(x)\ne x\wedge f(x)\ne g(x)\). The equivalence relation \(R(x,y)\) of belonging to the same orbit is quantifier-free:
\[
R(x,y)\equiv y=x\vee y=f(x)\vee y=g(x)\vee y=f(g(x)).
\]
For each of the five types \(T\), the rank-\(q\) existential formula
\[
\exists x_1\cdots\exists x_q\left(\bigwedge_i\theta_T(x_i)\wedge\bigwedge_{i<j}\neg R(x_i,x_j)\right)
\]
holds in \(A_q\), forcing **at least** \(q\) separate orbits of that type in any structure with the same rank-\(q\) theory. Summing the five corresponding orbit sizes gives \(|M|\ge q(1+2+2+2+4)=11q\), with equality in \(A_q\).

**5. Parity-sensitive sharpness.** Let \(T=S-N_1\), independent of \(N_1\), and \(V_T=6r^2+4r^4\). Conditioning gives the exact identity
\[
\Pr(N_1=0\mid S=n)=e^{-r}\frac{\Pr(T=n)}{\Pr(S=n)}.
\]
Since \(T\) has even support, its probability at odd \(n\) is zero. For even \(n\), \(\mathbb ET=n-r\) and \(r/\sqrt{V_T}\to0\). Its Fourier characteristic function has principal Gaussian peaks at both \(0\) and \(\pi\); the peaks at \(\pm\pi/2\) are suppressed by \(e^{-3r^2}\). Thus the span-two local central limit gives
\[
\Pr(T=n)\sim\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi V_T}}
\quad(n\text{ even}),
\]
and consequently \(\Pr(N_1=0\mid S=n)\sim2e^{-r}\) along even \(n\), since \(V_T/V\to1\). There is no common fixed point precisely when \(N_1=0\). The asymptotic error exponent equals \(-1\) because \(r/n^{1/4}\to1\). This also shows why a span-one Poisson approximation incorrectly loses a factor of two here.

## Verification

The accompanying `CHECK.py` requires only Python 3 standard libraries. It independently constructs every involution on up to seven labels, filters commuting ordered pairs, classifies every orbit by its stabilizer, and compares exact counts against the coefficient recurrences derived by differentiating the exponential generating functions. The recurrences for \(a_n=[z^n]F(z)\) and for the no-common-fixed coefficients \(b_n=[z^n]\exp(3z^2/2+z^4/4)\) are
\[
na_n=a_{n-1}+3a_{n-2}+a_{n-4},\qquad
nb_n=3b_{n-2}+b_{n-4},
\]
with \(a_0=b_0=1\) and negative indices interpreted as zero. Exact enumeration gives respectively \(52\) commuting pairs and \(33\) without common fixed points on four labels; at six labels the counts are \(1216\) and \(675\). Odd sizes have no examples without a common fixed point. The checker also evaluates the exact coefficient-ratio approximation with high-precision tilted Poisson recurrence through \(n=1600\) along even indices and checks that its ratio to \(2e^{-r_n}\) approaches one. This numerics **does not certify the infinite local limits**: the proof above supplies those.

## Relationship to prior work

Component-locality and zero-one-law techniques are classical. Mirabi and Shelah's 2026 work studies logical limit laws of component-pruned sparse random graphs and percolated tori, not conditioned uniform Klein-four actions; its use of component profiles provides motivating context, not an assertion of novelty for the general logical principle. Abdesselam's work on **unrestricted** commuting permutation tuples concerns the log-concavity of orbit counts and the large-tuple-number regime, rather than pairs constrained to be involutions, and does not furnish the parity-corrected common-fixed-point estimate or the minimal rank-\(q\) finite representative stated here. Diaconis, Fulman, and Guralnick study fixed points in permutation settings but not this conditioned commuting-involution pair model. The exponential formula and Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé composition argument are standard tools; the contribution assessed here is their **combined exact, sharp specialization** to the five-orbit action model, including the factor-two parity obstruction and optimal \(11q\)-point rank representative. Historical priority cannot be certified by an incomplete literature search.

## Limitations

Only **fixed quantifier rank** and first-order logic in the two-function signature are covered. The result makes no claim about monadic second-order convergence, added orders, arbitrary commuting permutations, independent (unconditioned) involutions, or uniformity when \(q\) grows with \(n\). The implied \(O_q\) constant depends on \(q\). The odd-even distinction is essential to the exact common-fixed-point asymptotic. The full historical-priority status, especially possible deductions from general saddle-point theorems or general finite-group-action zero-one laws, remains open to independent literature review; no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
