# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof is an explicit obstruction to *primitive*, not to ordinary, representations. For even \(L\), the chosen \(N\) is \(1\pmod a\), while the chosen \(b\) is \(-1\pmod a\). Any ordinary representation forces \(y=aj-1\) for \(1\le j\le L\), so \(y\mid N\) and \(y\mid x\) because \(\gcd(a,y)=1\). The lower bound \(y\ge a-1\ge2\) rules out gcd \(1\). The least-residue-class choice controls \(b\) within \(a\) of \(N/(aL)\), and exponential growth of \(N\) proves the divergent ratio. Finite enumerations corroborate, but do not replace, these deductions.

## Originality — PASS (residual bibliographic risk acknowledged)
The full relevant portions of Jaffe (arXiv:2610.17267v1) were examined: its Theorem 4.3 proves finite gaps, Propositions 5.1–5.2 handle fixed \(\(a\in\{1,2\}\)\), and §§6–7 discuss upper bounds and varying parameters. None states the fixed-\(a\ge3\) unbounded normalized gap. Searches for aliases including primitive Frobenius, coprime coefficients, and Stern/Farey denominators did not identify a dominating theorem. A similarly titled published record on *primitive Pythagorean quadruples* concerns a different numerical semigroup. Residual risk: broader or older unindexed restricted-representation results could imply the claim; no database search certifies uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The invariant is the natural sharp missing comparison after the published \(\(a\in\{1,2\}\)\) formulas: whether linear-in-\(b\) bounds persist when one generator is fixed. The answer is a full dichotomy, not an arbitrary finite cutoff or a parameter substitution. The construction gives arbitrarily many algebraically forced nonprimitive candidates and explains why attempts to extrapolate a uniform linear law from small cases cannot work.

## Limits
The theorem proves a subsequential lower bound and does not calculate each full primitive Frobenius number. Scientific priority remains subject to outside examination.
