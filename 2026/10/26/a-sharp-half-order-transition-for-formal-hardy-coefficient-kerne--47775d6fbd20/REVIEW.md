# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The coefficient sequence is the binomial sequence \(c_n=(\alpha)_n/n!\) times a summable pure-point Fourier series. For \(\alpha<1/2\), square-summability and outer-function cyclicity force injectivity. For \(\alpha\ge1/2\), the exact divergence of \(\sum c_n^2\) makes the finite Vandermonde correction norms arbitrarily small. The critical lemma controls the whole block, using monotonicity, Abel sums for every off-diagonal frequency, and dominated convergence of the \(\ell^1\) weights; therefore ordinary partial sums, not just block endpoints, tend to zero. The polynomial triangular map yields full graph closure. The Poisson atomic-recovery bound establishes atomlessness of the associated Herglotz measure. Proof applies to arbitrarily clustered unit-circle phases and the critical endpoint.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Ransford's 2026 counterexample uses explicitly separated dyadic *simple poles* and proves existence of an out-of-space zero-output function. The present claim concerns **fractional powers \(0<\alpha<1\)**, arbitrary infinite phase sets, non-atomic Herglotz boundary data, and a sharp transition at \(\alpha=1/2\). The 2019 Łanucha–Nowak paper studies spaces generated by an individual fractional singularity and the canonical closed Toeplitz operator, not the infinite-mode maximal ordinary-series graph. A previously reported dense-kernel result for Herglotz measures with infinitely many actual atoms does not imply this atomless case. Targeted research-record searches for weighted fractional Hankel kernels, dense formal coefficient kernels and equivalent nonclosability formulations did not find a dominating statement. Historical under-indexing remains a risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A previously exhibited isolated formal-coefficient pathology becomes a mathematically natural half-order phase transition in a smooth fractional family, with the sharp endpoint included and a full-graph consequence. The new class is genuinely atomless in the Herglotz representation. The result explicitly separates formal summation from the canonical closed Smirnov operator and explains exactly when the Hardy-symbol \(H^2\) threshold protects injectivity in this family.
