# Scientific review: sharp orbit-diffusion spectral law

## Correctness — PASS

The domain, weight, natural endpoint condition and normalization are independently reconstructed from Tindall's differential operator in Eq. (13). Reflection symmetry and simple Sturm oscillation establish that the smallest nonzero eigenmode is odd; thus its gap is the Dirichlet-at-zero variational minimum on a half-interval. The comparison of weighted denominators establishes strict gap growth. The hyperbolic-coordinate change preserves the energy exactly and yields a second quotient establishing strict *normalized* gap decrease. Differentiation of the simple Legendre eigenpair gives the coefficient \(3/10\) at weak dissipation. The critical constant \(1/16\) follows from a global Hardy lower bound and a separately constructed sequence of near-Hardy extremizers for the matching upper bound. The finite spectral computations corroborate the derivation but are not treated as proof for infinite parameter ranges.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, with stated access risk)

The motivating paper [1] gives the orbit-label eigenproblem and its \(\mu\to1/2\) weak-dissipation limit, but the full inspected main paper and supplement do not state both global spectral monotonicities, the explicit first weak-dissipation correction or the sharp singular limit. The 2025 spectral-collapse result [4] concerns a different nonlinear-spin Lindbladian and does not imply the weighted Legendre problem. The classical orbit and stationary diffusion model itself are credited to Drummond–Carmichael [2,3]. Their complete old articles were not accessible, and they remain a material priority risk because a hidden spectrum calculation could imply part of the result; no claim of complete literature-wide noncoverage is made. The 2022 quantum-fluctuation article [5] was available only in abstract, also limiting priority confidence. Searches of public research records found related spectral limits but no identified statement dominating this particular coefficient and strict pair of monotonicities.

## Value — PASS

The lowest positive eigenvalue is not an arbitrarily defined quantity: in [1] it directly parametrizes how quantum noise selects the stationary orbit distribution. Its exact evolution between the small-dissipation Legendre regime and the singular drive threshold therefore determines a natural dynamical time scale of the reduced model. The critical limit produces a nontrivial *sharp* Hardy spectral edge, and the opposing monotonicities show that normalizing by the vanishing deterministic frequency qualitatively changes the rate comparison. This is a structural spectral result, not a reparameterization or a finite parameter-table recomputation.

## Limitations and closest literature

The statement concerns only the orbit-label diffusion eigenproblem under the natural no-flux realization; it does not prove a uniform finite-spin Liouvillian gap law when \(\omega\downarrow\kappa\). The numerical checks are finite-dimensional approximations and cannot prove the analytic statements on all parameters. Earlier foundational cooperative-fluorescence sources [2,3] and the full 2022 boundary-time-crystal paper [5] were not available for full-text comparison, so independent priority verification remains important. The complete source model [1] and a related 2025 spectral paper [4] were materially inspected.
