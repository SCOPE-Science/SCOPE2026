# Scientific review of the two-pulse infiltration theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The proof states the complete domain \(f_c,A,k,h,T>0\), a fully recovered initial infiltration capacity, excess rainfall on the two wet segments, dry capacity recovery with no actual infiltration, and the restriction to a single possible dry gap. Composing these two affine-exponential flows gives an exact cumulative-uptake formula. Strict AM–GM fixes the unique equal wet-time split for every positive internal gap; the second derivative of the resulting scalar objective is strictly negative on the whole feasible interval. The sign of its initial derivative yields the explicit if-and-only-if horizon threshold. A positive horizon partial derivative of the first-order condition establishes monotonic movement of the unique gap, while the derivative-zero limit yields finite saturation. The derivative-zero equation is nondegenerate at threshold, giving a rigorous linear onset coefficient. The standalone Python checks confirm 3,000 parameterized fixed-gap inequalities, 3,000 concavity cases, 3,000 gap optima against 52 trial gaps, 120 numerical integral comparisons and 800 finite-difference derivatives; the exact example is checked symbolically in the main proof. These finite computations do not substitute for the proof.

## Originality — PASS with stated residual risks

The Horton wet-decay law, the conventional dry-recovery model and intermittent irrigation are all established. The OpenSWMM Chapter 4 material was inspected for the governing wet and dry equations. The author-available complete Gençoğlan et al. (2005) manuscript was inspected in its model, cycle-count and results sections: its ponding-time and fixed-dose cycle calculations do not establish a unique gap optimized jointly with two wet durations over a fixed time horizon. The full accessible Assouline (2019) article optimizes rate/duration to avoid runoff for prescribed doses, not the present fixed-clock maximum uptake. Karmeli–Peri (1974) describes multiple-cycle pulse irrigation in accessible abstract material. Dadhich et al. (2012) and James–Larson (1976) are relevant historical comparisons but their full texts were not available; this is an acknowledged priority risk, not a claim of full-text exclusion. Targeted mathematical-record and literature searches did not identify the complete combined threshold, monotone bounded rest, and onset classification. A broader specialized theorem might imply this exact result, and uninspected sources might contain it; publication priority remains provisional.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a natural finite-control design problem in a standard hydrological model, not a mere arbitrary parameter slice. It answers the operational question of whether a finite respite can compensate for the infiltration time it sacrifices. The explicit necessary-and-sufficient ratio test \(hA>kf_c\), the exact horizon cutoff, and the non-obvious fact that an improving long-horizon optimal rest is uniformly bounded provide a complete allocation classification with an interpretable time scale. The proof also supplies a sharp onset sensitivity and exact rational-logarithmic benchmark. These structural results are useful as solvable model diagnostics and as comparisons for numerical pulsed-irrigation schemes, without claiming field validation or arbitrary switching optimality.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest physically related sources are Horton (1941), Karmeli and Peri (1974), James and Larson (1976), Gençoğlan et al. (2005), Dadhich et al. (2012), the OpenSWMM infiltration documentation, and Assouline (2019). Primary full-text access was incomplete for some historical sources. The theorem assumes capacity-saturating rain and unrestricted runoff, an exponential dry recovery law, and a single optional hiatus. No claim is made about fixed-dose/low-runoff irrigation, more than two wet pulses, or physically detailed Richards-equation soil transport.
