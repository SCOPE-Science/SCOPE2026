# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The independent Gaussian left and right sketches yield a two-factor distribution by orthogonal decomposition and rotational invariance. The Student-density integral fixes precisely which moments exist; beta-integral limits yield all asymptotic constants. The \(q=2\) angular integral is elementary and makes the exact squared-risk identity valid at every \(0<h<1\). A separately run standard-library verifier checks direct 2×2 matrix products against the projection identity and finite numerical quadratures. The general moment conclusions follow from integration and domination, not from finite experiments.

**Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, not independent verification).** Lam–Ceruti–Kressner introduced the projector and the low-rank integration framework. Nakatsukasa established classical oblique-projection and stabilized-sketch bounds; Kressner–Lam proved related oversampled mean-square estimates. The specific unoversampled right sample, the exact two-singular-value error distribution, its fractional-moment transition, and the all-\(h\) squared-risk identity were not found within the inspected theorems or searched prior-result summaries. The classical Gaussian-ratio calculation itself is prior mathematical knowledge. An equivalent result in older Gaussian range-finder analysis remains a residual risk.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem gives a sharp quantitative boundary for a natural edge case of randomized low-rank projection: pathwise first-order convergence of individual fixed sketches does not translate into first-order root-mean-square accuracy, even with arbitrary left oversampling. The q=1 logarithmic boundary and exact q=2 identity explain the nonuniform integrability mechanism. The constant-forcing matrix ODE supplies an immediate one-step numerical-analysis interpretation without making unsupported claims about fully iterated methods.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Lam–Ceruti–Kressner (2024), arXiv:2409.06384; Nakatsukasa (2020), arXiv:2009.11392; Kressner–Lam (2023), arXiv:2302.12761; Halko–Martinsson–Tropp (2011), arXiv:0909.4061. All conclusions assume independent real Gaussian sampling, a two-column rank-two matrix, a one-column right sketch, and exact arithmetic. The result does not analyze stabilization, positive right oversampling, structured sketches, global numerical convergence, or floating-point hazards.
