# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact rank-three witness is checked directly, and the group algebra of \(\mathbb F_3^r\) gives a self-contained proof of the Davenport bound \(2r+1\). Every occupied line's complement has exactly the quotient Davenport length, forcing a nonzero return to that line. Two new final-coordinate-one vectors preserve zero-sum freeness and distinct lines inductively. The rank-two case is proved by an explicit length-at-most-four dichotomy; exact enumeration of all zero-sum-free multisequences through length four supports the case split. The finite verifier is not presented as a proof for all ranks.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** He (2013), Section 5, proposes the separation statement without providing the rank threshold; here the nonempty-sum version is precisely specified to avoid the empty-subset issue. Gao–Geroldinger (2003), Theorem 6.2 and Remark 6.4, give a weaker cardinality condition and high-rank squarefree examples, which together with elementary quotient arguments cover some high-rank implications, but do not cover the rank-three seed and the rank-one/two sharp converse. Kim (2014) and Qu–Xia–Xue–Zhong (2015) address different cross-number and subsum-cardinality questions. Equivalent small-rank witnesses in unlocated literature remain possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The low-rank boundary is motivated by an explicit conjectural structural property. The first obstruction occurs in dimension three, survives removal of the empty-sum ambiguity, persists at maximal zero-sum-free length, and has a uniform induction and an elementary algebraic explanation. This is not a finite list of arbitrary computations or merely pointing out an inconsistent convention.

**Scientific limitations.** The result is confined to characteristic three, does not resolve the weaker Conjecture 22, and makes no claim of independent certification. Literature access is incomplete beyond the identified full texts and abstracts.
