# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

A coercive logistic potential has a nonzero maximizer and positivity follows by graph connectedness; scaling at the maximal coordinate ratio gives uniqueness of the positive equilibrium. At equilibrium v_i=x_i z_i, v^T Jv=-sum x_i v_i²-d sum w_ij x_i x_j(z_i-z_j)², so J is negative definite. Summing equilibrium equations yields sum (x_i-r_i/2)^2=nq²/4; Cauchy-Schwarz and the constant-vector Rayleigh quotient give alpha<=q. Equality forces x=(r+q)/2, substitution yields r_i²-q²=2d(Lr)_i, and then J=-qI-dL establishes sufficiency. Connectedness and fast transverse mode separation yield alpha(infty)=m<q for heterogeneous rates, closing strict maximum for unattainable systems. Two-class quadratic identity and Laplacian eigenvector show graph equitable criterion; under mean-field, a monic quadratic cannot vanish at >=3 distinct rates. Independent exact two-class examples and 70 positive weighted graph equilibrium/eigenvalue calculations corroborate.

## originality

PASS

The 2023-2024 motivating paper derives the 2-patch logistic equilibrium and optimizes total biomass. Under equal quadratic limitation its optimal dispersal can coincide with d=(a+b)/2, a known overlap properly credited. Optimizing biomass does not mathematically imply maximizing the leading positive-equilibrium Jacobian decay, nor the all-graph RMS ceiling and graph-equitable equality criterion. General logistic mixing papers compare carrying capacity, not these exact spectral quantities. The 2015 Arditi-Lobry-Sari full text was inaccessible, and a generic matrix bound might imply components; best-of-knowledge PASS for the combined graph and three-level spectral theorem.

## value

PASS

The question is to maximize the worst local post-disturbance decay rate, not equilibrium biomass. A network-independent RMS upper ceiling and equality defined by a graph eigenvector/equitable partition are structural, exact, and useful in optimizing conservative dispersal. The two-rate vs three-rate complete mean-field classification, plus the finite (1,1,4) example attaining sqrt(6)>max{1,2}, reveal a natural mixing threshold rather than a small unsupported gain.
