# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The argument is geometric over the algebraic closure. Nonsplit Frobenius exchanges the rulings and enforces even degree; five rational points force trace -2 for a putative genus-one degree-four curve and hence no new degree-two Picard classes over the quadratic extension. The two ruling bundles would coincide, contradicting the distinct projections of the embedded bidegree-(2,2) curve. The explicit genus-four degree-six curve was checked for smoothness by Gröbner bases in every projective chart, each giving the unit ideal. The exact finite-field point set has five points, all on the cubic.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Homma–Kim, arXiv:2203.11595, Section 5, explicitly pose the elliptic-quadric filling-curve question. Their theorem concerns a split quadric and does not determine a smooth minimal degree on an elliptic quadric. Ahammed–Campbell–Moon, arXiv:2512.02796, likewise treat the product of projective lines. Targeted searches against mathematical records and literature found no exact smooth degree-six/minimality statement. Residual risk is unindexed older classifications of curves on finite quadrics; absence of a search hit alone is not a proof of priority.

## Value — PASS
This gives a sharp and naturally motivated smallest-field answer for smooth curves to a distinct case raised by the initiating paper: the anisotropic quadric's rational-point filling degree. The obstruction links maximal rational point count on an elliptic curve with Frobenius exchange of rulings, rather than being a brute-force negative computation. The explicit sextic realizes the sharp threshold. The distinction between smooth and singular irreducible curves is essential and retained throughout.
