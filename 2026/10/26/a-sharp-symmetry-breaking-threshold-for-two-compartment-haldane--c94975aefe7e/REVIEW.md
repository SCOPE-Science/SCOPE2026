# Scientific assessment: sharp Haldane–Andrews allocation transition

**Correctness (PASS).** For every \(b\ge0\) and \(N>0\), the exact symmetric product-coordinate identity has positive denominator. The derivative numerator \(G\) is strictly decreasing in the feasible product variable, and its right-endpoint sign is exactly that of \(12N+8b-N^3\). This proves the unique cubic threshold and exhausts all local and global optima, including both allocation endpoints and the quartic critical case. Implicit differentiation proves the stated onset coefficient; asymptotic expansion of the exact positive root and direct response evaluation give the large-budget formulas. The exact \(b=2,N=6\) calculation and reproducible finite checks agree with the proof. Finite numerical tests are not used as an infinite proof.

**Originality (PASS with residual risk).** The inhibitory single-compartment response is established by Andrews (1968), and broader nonconcave resource-optimization machinery predates this result. Fazel and Chiang (2005, full nine-page text) provide an algorithmic framework and increasing rational sigmoidal examples, not this complete parameter-uniform Haldane threshold. Calcagno and collaborators (2013, accessible full HTML) optimize patch residence/movement responses, not the present fixed-total substrate split. Their closely related 2014 article discusses type-IV responses and patch heterogeneity, but only its abstract and indexed excerpts were accessible; it remains a meaningful priority risk. Available related literature and mathematical records have not established the specific cubic threshold, exact optima and asymptotic bifurcation as a combined theorem; this does not prove exhaustive historical novelty. A related two-patch monotone Hill-response result in earlier records has different objective geometry and does not imply the nonmonotone result.

**Mathematical value (PASS).** Haldane substrate inhibition makes an equal-versus-unequal resource decision genuinely nonlinear. The theorem gives a natural sharp boundary, the exact number and types of stationary allocations, rigorous square-root onset, and a high-load asymptotic prescription that avoids the vanishing growth rate of equal loading. These results are a complete and interpretable structural classification for the simplest two-compartment fixed-biomass biochemical-kinetics model. They do not claim general steady-state process optimality or direct empirical calibration.

**Residual scientific limitations.** The 2014 Ecology Letters full text and the full classical Andrews article were not obtained, so unknown prior special-case theorems could qualify historical priority. The physical reading assumes equal volumes and equal fixed biomass; no dynamic concentration depletion or microbial population feedback is modeled. The case \(b=0\) is a mathematical limit.
