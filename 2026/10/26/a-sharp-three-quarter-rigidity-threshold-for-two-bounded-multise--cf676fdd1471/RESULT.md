# A sharp three-quarter rigidity threshold for two-bounded multiset Eventown families

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge 2\), consider families \(\mathcal F\subseteq\{0,1,2\}^n\) satisfying
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n\min(x_i,y_i)\equiv0\pmod 2\qquad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathcal F,
\]
including the case \(x=y\). Call a family **noncanonical** if at least one of its vectors has an entry equal to \(1\). Then the largest cardinality of a noncanonical family is exactly
\[
\boxed{\max_{\mathcal F\text{ noncanonical}}|\mathcal F|=3\cdot2^{n-2}.}
\]
Equivalently, every two-bounded multiset Eventown family of size strictly greater than \(3\cdot2^{n-2}\) consists entirely of vectors with entries in \(\{0,2\}\). The constant \(3/4\) is sharp for every \(n\ge2\).

## Assumptions and scope
All vectors are ordered multiplicity vectors on a fixed labelled ground set of size \(n\). The cardinality of an intersection is the sum of the coordinatewise minima, counted with multiplicity. The Eventown condition includes even cardinality of each individual multiset. There is no requirement that a family be maximal by inclusion. The distinguished entry \(1\) is the sole odd multiplicity permitted by the bound \(2\).

## Proof
**Parity embedding.** Over \(\mathbb F_2\), define
\[
\phi(0)=(0,0),\qquad \phi(1)=(1,0),\qquad\phi(2)=(1,1).
\]
Write \(\Phi(x)\in\mathbb F_2^{2n}\) for the concatenation of these coordinate pairs and equip the ambient space with the standard nondegenerate symmetric bilinear dot product \(B\). A direct check of the nine pairs \((a,b)\in\{0,1,2\}^2\) gives
\[
\min(a,b)\bmod2=\phi(a)\cdot\phi(b).
\]
Thus \(B(\Phi(x),\Phi(y))=0\) for all \(x,y\in\mathcal F\). The span \(U=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_2}\Phi(\mathcal F)\) is totally isotropic, so \(U\subseteq U^{\perp}\). Nondegeneracy yields \(\dim U\le n\). Because \(\Phi\) is injective, \(|\mathcal F|\le |U|\le2^n\).

**A rank-two coordinate.** Let \(\pi_i:U\to\mathbb F_2^2\) be the two-bit projection at coordinate \(i\). If some \(\pi_i\) has rank two, its four outputs each occur exactly \(2^{\dim U-2}\) times. The pair \((0,1)\) is not in the image of \(\phi\), so none of those \(2^{\dim U-2}\) vectors can lie in \(\Phi(\mathcal F)\). Therefore
\[
|\mathcal F|\le 3\cdot2^{\dim U-2}\le3\cdot2^{n-2}.
\]

**All coordinate projections have rank at most one.** Since \(U\) is spanned by \(\Phi(\mathcal F)\), every nonzero one-dimensional coordinate image must be generated by \((1,0)\) or by \((1,1)\); the forbidden line generated by \((0,1)\) cannot occur. Partition the coordinates into \(I\), where the image is the \((1,0)\)-line; \(J\), where it is the \((1,1)\)-line; and zero coordinates \(K\). On \(U\), the bilinear form is precisely the binary dot product of the \(I\)-coordinate bits: contributions from \(J\) cancel in pairs over \(\mathbb F_2\). Hence the projection \(C\) of \(U\) to \(\mathbb F_2^I\) is a self-orthogonal binary code. The restricted standard dot product is nondegenerate, giving \(\dim C\le\lfloor |I|/2\rfloor\). The projection kernel injects into the \(J\)-coordinates, so
\[
\dim U\le |J|+\lfloor |I|/2\rfloor.
\]
Noncanonicity implies that some member has an entry \(1\), so \(C\ne0\); self-orthogonality then forces \(|I|\ge2\). Thus \(\dim U\le n-1\), and
\[
|\mathcal F|\le2^{n-1}<3\cdot2^{n-2}.
\]
The two cases together prove the upper bound.

**Sharpness.** Take
\[
\mathcal A_n=\{(0,0),(1,1),(2,2)\}\times\{0,2\}^{n-2}.
\]
The minimum-intersection contributions from its first two equal coordinates are always even, and contributions from its remaining even-valued coordinates are always even. Thus \(\mathcal A_n\) is Eventown, contains the vector with two entries equal to \(1\), and has exactly \(3\cdot2^{n-2}\) members. This proves sharpness for all \(n\ge2\).

## Verification
A standalone exhaustive finite checker in `verify.py` independently implements coordinatewise-minimum parity and the binary embedding. It verifies the parity identity for every pair of even-weight multiplicity vectors through \(n=5\), enumerates all maximal compatibility cliques and finds the largest clique containing an odd-multiplicity vector, and checks the construction directly. The observed maxima for \(n=2,3,4,5\) are \(3,6,12,24\), respectively. Recorded output is in `verify_output.txt`. These finite tests corroborate, but do not replace, the unrestricted linear-algebra proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Banerjee and Dewan (2026, arXiv:2610.21636v1) prove that the unrestricted maximum size of a two-bounded multiset Eventown family is \(2^n\), attained uniquely by \(\{0,2\}^n\). They do not state the sharp size of a family that contains an odd multiplicity. The result here gives the dimension-independent quantitative rigidity gap from \(2^n\) down to \((3/4)2^n\) under that noncanonicality constraint. Its argument uses a two-bit parity embedding and a forced forbidden projection state, rather than the even-multiplicity replacement in their extremal proof. Han, Zhang and Zhao (2026, arXiv:2608.16321v1) study supersaturation and stability for ordinary Eventown set families; those are one-bounded families and do not imply the present two-bounded rigidity threshold.

## Limitations
This result is specific to multiplicities in \(\{0,1,2\}\); it neither determines the corresponding gap for larger even multiplicity bounds nor classifies all noncanonical families attaining equality. The literature comparison cannot rule out equivalent statements in unindexed works or terminology not captured by the searches. Exhaustive computation covers \(2\le n\le5\) only; the unrestricted bound is supplied by the proof.
