# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The fixed-target linear programming reduction to three-point cap-saturated laws is valid for all integer supports, including infinite ones. The elementary representation-count ceilings \(3,4,5,7\) and the rigidity of the equality-seven case reduce the problem to the six explicit cubic polynomials. Their dominance comparisons are nonnegative products on the entire \(t\in[0,1]\) range. The two exact witnesses realize the two final branches. Exact rational verification reproduced all finite coefficients; the general proof does not rely on exhaustive lattice experiments.

## Originality

PASS with residual priority uncertainty. The 2026 Kurauskas source explicitly leaves the sharp finite point-mass problem as a conjecture except for known regimes, and its theorem is asymptotic. The 2019 Juškevičius–Kurauskas sharp point-concentration theorem is restricted to even numbers of summands. The 2023 sharp Kolmogorov–Rogozin inequality controls a different interval-concentration hypothesis. The relevant full sources were compared by objects and hypotheses, rather than titles. No directly covering indexed result was found in the actual semantic searches, but searches alone are not priority proof.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The formula gives a sharp small-n instance of a natural open extremal-convolution problem in the noninteger reciprocal cap range. The switch at \(\alpha=2/5\) between all-aligned and mixed-left-right extremizers is a structural phase transition with explicit sharp witnesses, not an arbitrary finite table.

## Limitations

The theorem only covers three independent integer-valued variables and the common cap \(1/3\le\alpha\le1/2\). It does not establish uniqueness of optimizers, other caps, or general odd-n sharpness. A differently named exact result in older literature remains a residual priority risk.
