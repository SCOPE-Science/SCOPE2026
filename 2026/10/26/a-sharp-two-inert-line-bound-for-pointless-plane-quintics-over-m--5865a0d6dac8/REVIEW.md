# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The seven independent point-evaluation constraints produce an affine family of exactly \(2^{14}=16{,}384\) forms. Exact \(168\)-element projective orbit enumeration covers it with \(104\) disjoint orbits. Algebraic geometric-smoothness criteria computed over all three charts give \(98\) smooth orbits weighing \(15{,}792\) forms. Two independently structured checkers agree on each orbit's smoothness, line-section count, weight, and the displayed six-bin histogram. Both algorithms and the complete certificate are supplied; no finite sampling is substituted for exhaustiveness.

## Originality
**PASS, with residual risk.** Huang–Kedlaya–Lau (arXiv:2402.00716) give the underlying genus-six census, but their class table has point counts, group labels and models, not the seven-line irreducibility statistic or the sharp minimum and extremal orbit. Entin (arXiv:1805.05454) proves asymptotic hyperplane-section cycle frequencies and does not give exact \(q=2\) genus-six enumerations. Comparisons with semantic mathematical research records and earlier embedded degree-four line-section counts uncovered no stronger applicable statement. An unindexed table or nontrivial derivation from existing complete class data remains possible, so publication priority is not guaranteed.

## Value
**PASS.** Inert rational hyperplane sections realize Frobenius five-cycles, a natural arithmetic invariant; on point-free genus-six plane quintics, the bound of two is structurally sharp and a unique embedded extremal orbit realizes it. The theorem supplies the exact finite-field boundary distribution at the smallest field size, which asymptotic monodromy theory cannot determine.
