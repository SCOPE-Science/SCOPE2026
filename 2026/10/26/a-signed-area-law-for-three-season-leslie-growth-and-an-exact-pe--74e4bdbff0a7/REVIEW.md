# Review of the three-season Leslie orientation theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The object is the explicitly specified positive two-age matrix family. Direct rational matrix arithmetic independently verifies the two possible three-step traces \(F+bL_\pm\) and common determinant \(-b^3Q\). Strictly positive three-step products justify the Perron asymptotics for every nonzero nonnegative initial state. The positive root of the quadratic is strictly increasing with trace at fixed determinant. The sign and zeros of the signed-area determinant give all six ordering cases, including equalities. For \(F<1\), each persistence polynomial decreases strictly in \(b>0\) and crosses exactly once. The rational witness gives opposite signs of the characteristic polynomial at one, so opposite growth conclusions are exact. Critical multipliers are stated as neutral. The executable rational checks corroborate but do not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with specific residual risk

The 1945 Leslie article's accessible original sections develop constant-matrix age projections and spectral growth, not this explicit signed-area three-season permutation classification. The 2012 periodic-Leslie treatment establishes general periodic analysis and may be stronger in scope, but the available sections do not show an implied area or persistence-window theorem; the complete paper was not inspectable. The 2006 stochastic variable-environment analysis asks a different question. Fertility-only switching with season-invariant maturation is an exact zero-area subcase and does not exhibit the claimed reversal. Comparison of equivalent formulations (trace/commutator, phase reversal, cyclic permutations) found no direct earlier matching claim. However a matrix-product theorem under different nomenclature could imply it; originality is qualified by this real coverage risk, not asserted as proven by failed searches.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Long-run viability is the central question in periodic population demography. Here identical seasonal component populations can have opposite fate solely because the season order reverses. The complete six-permutation classification uses a geometrically interpretable and directly measurable covariance statistic, includes the exact no-effect boundary and minimal period, and locates a nonempty, necessary-and-sufficient fecundity window for the striking phenomenon. The exact rational witness demonstrates substantive biological-model consequences, rather than a renaming of a generic trace formula. Restriction to three seasons is the smallest dimension where chronological ordering can change the growth exponent; the common fecundity parameter is motivated by asking when adult productivity can rescue a population. The result is limited to deterministic linear models.
