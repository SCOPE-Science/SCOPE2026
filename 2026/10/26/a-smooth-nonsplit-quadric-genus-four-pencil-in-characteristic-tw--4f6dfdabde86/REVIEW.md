# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The explicit Segre identity is exact in characteristic two, and it reduces geometric smoothness to the complete calculation for \(f(s)f(t)=\kappa\) with \(f(T)=T^3+T^2+1\). Both affine and infinity charts are handled, including the singular boundary parameters \(\kappa=0,1\). Adjunction, connectedness and canonical-quadric theory yield genus four and a ground-field gonality lower bound of four; the displayed rational point and Riemann–Roch give the matching upper bound. The field-size argument is uniform for all \(q\ge4\), not a finite experiment. The exact verifier checks the defining identity and rational smoothness over several base and quadratic-extension fields.

## Originality

**PASS, with an explicit residual risk.** Faber–Grantham–Howe, arXiv:2207.14307 (2022), poses the genus-equals-gonality existence question and supplies an odd-characteristic heuristic, while Faber–Grantham, arXiv:2005.07054 (2020), supplies canonical-quadric geometry and an explicit binary-field example. The present characteristic-two pencil, sharp parameter discriminant, and construction valid for all \(\mathbb F_{2^m}\) with \(m\ge2\) are not implied by these sources. Targeted searches did not identify a covering earlier formula. An unindexed equivalent construction remains possible; the search is not a mathematical proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The result is not a field-by-field census or an automatic specialization: it gives one algebraic smoothness criterion for a full one-parameter genus-four family, proves that the nonsplit quadratic twist eliminates degree-three maps over the ground field, and supplies rational points uniformly over all even fields above the sharp exceptional base field. This provides an explicit characteristic-two counterpart to the finite-field maximal-gonality construction problem.

## Boundaries and closest literature

The family is geometrically trigonal, although it has ground-field gonality four. There is no smooth member of this pencil over \(\mathbb F_2\); the known existence result over that field uses a different cubic. No classification of isomorphism types, zeta functions or point counts is included. The relevant prior literature is arXiv:2207.14307, Question 1.3, and arXiv:2005.07054, Lemma 5.1 and Theorem 5.3. These are background and prior coverage, not newly claimed theorems.
