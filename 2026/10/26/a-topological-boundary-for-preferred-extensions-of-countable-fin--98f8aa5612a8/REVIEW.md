# Review of the preferred-extension topological boundary

## Correctness: PASS
The exact local defense clauses are clopen under finite indegree, hence admissible sets are closed; projection of a compact inclusion-witness relation makes nonmaximal admissibles an \(F_\sigma\) union and preferred extensions \(G_\delta\). Symmetry makes admissibility identical to independentness, and local finiteness makes maximality a closed dominating condition. In the explicit directed construction, each self-attacker is excluded, each core member forces its entire row, different rows conflict, each whole row defends itself, and the resulting rows converge to the nonpreferred empty set. Degrees and the computable attack predicate are directly checked. The finite checker confirms cyclic analogues, not the infinite claim.

## Originality: PASS within inspected sources
Full material from Xu–Luo arXiv:2610.12008 (§3 and §5), Andrews–San Mauro arXiv:2508.16986 (§2 and the results on finitely branching encodings), and Elaroussi et al. arXiv:2202.05506 (§2 and maximality) was compared by domain and implication rather than by title. Those sources analyze complexity or finite preferred semantics and do not give the present closed-versus-nonclosed contrast at bounded directed degree. The general compact maximal-point lemma is elementary and not claimed independently as new. Residual risk: an older topological presentation of argumentation semantics or a stronger general compact-family theorem could subsume some statements, and an alternative bounded-degree construction might already be published.

## Value: PASS
This identifies a robust Borel regularity of preferred semantics for fixed finitary infinite frameworks while giving an exact structural reason that compactness can fail, even with uniformly bounded local interaction. It also separates symmetric from directed attacks under the same local finiteness hypothesis. These boundaries directly address the mismatch between maximality in an infinite family and local decidability.
