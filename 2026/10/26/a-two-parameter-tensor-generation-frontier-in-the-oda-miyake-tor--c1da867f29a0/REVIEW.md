# Scientific review: two-parameter toric tensor-generation frontier

**Correctness — PASS.** The source fan and the established hyperplane/degree criterion are correctly specialized. The proof gives explicit convex certificates for every integer parameter outside \(|d-a|\le1\), and supporting facets inside this range; all eighteen invariant-wall degrees are derived from the exact fan relations. The exhaustive sign disjunction is algebraic, not numerical, and independently checked symbolic identities support the calculations. The scope excludes all other choices of the second and third fan parameters.

**Originality — PASS (qualified).** The closest primary source is Ito–Zeng–Zeng, arXiv:2610.22279v1, Corollary 3.6, Theorems 6.3–6.4 and Question 6.6. Its examples give only the slices \(d=a\) and \(d=a+1\), not the two-dimensional phase diagram or the explicit two-parameter frontier. A previously recorded calculation already establishes this obstruction phenomenon for \(a=-2,d\ge2\); this special case is included in the theorem, not counted as a novel counterexample. The general fan criterion, wall formulas, and prior existence of divisorial obstructions are credited, not claimed as new. Mathematical-record searches for the exact fan and both geometric phenomena did not identify another statement implying the result; the 2024 Fujino–Sato paper is partially accessible, leaving a priority risk. Absence of a search result is not a uniqueness proof.

**Value — PASS.** The authors explicitly request parameter classification and ask whether invariant \((-1,-1)\)-curves account for every obstruction. This theorem is a complete natural two-parameter slice containing their two previously solved families, with a sharp parameter frontier and an exhaustive identification of divisorial obstructions outside the frontier, including integer parameters not covered by the known one-dimensional examples. These are substantive answers to two parts of that motivated question, not isolated computations or a relabeling of one existing case.

The general theorem over arbitrary base field follows from the toric criterion and rational convex geometry; computational tests provide reproducible checks, not an infinite proof. The existence of *non-invariant* zero-degree curves in the failure regime is consistent with the published curve criterion.
