# A Two-Regime Occupation Law for Superattracting Return Flows

## Finding

Consider a trajectory with successive returns to a section at times \(t_n\), with positive section coordinates \(x_n\to0\) satisfying
\[
x_{n+1}=c x_n^p\bigl(1+O(x_n^\delta)\bigr),
\qquad c>0,\quad p>1,\quad \delta>0.
\]
Let \(\tau_n=t_{n+1}-t_n\) be the duration of cycle \(n\). Let \(b_n\) be the time spent outside a fixed target neighbourhood during that cycle, and assume \(b_n\to b_0>0\). Write \(N(T)\) for the number of completed cycles by time \(T\), and \(L(T)\) for the total time outside the neighbourhood during \([0,T]\).

There are two distinct occupation laws, determined by the cycle-duration scale:

1. If \(\tau_n\sim a x_n^{-q}\) for \(a,q>0\), then
\[
N(T)\sim\frac{\log\log T}{\log p},\qquad
L(T)\sim\frac{b_0}{\log p}\log\log T,
\]
and hence
\[
\frac{L(T)}{T}\sim
\frac{b_0}{\log p}\frac{\log\log T}{T}.
\]

2. If \(\tau_n\sim a\log(1/x_n)\) for \(a>0\), then
\[
N(T)\sim\frac{\log T}{\log p},\qquad
L(T)\sim\frac{b_0}{\log p}\log T,
\]
and hence
\[
\frac{L(T)}{T}\sim
\frac{b_0}{\log p}\frac{\log T}{T}.
\]

All logarithms are natural. The result concerns trajectories with infinitely many section returns and a bounded outside-time contribution per incomplete cycle. It is a conditional return-map theorem, not a claim about arbitrary statistical attractors.

## Assumptions and scope

The section coordinate is positive and tends to zero. The return map has a superattracting power law with a controlled relative error, as displayed above. The two cases are alternatives, not simultaneous assumptions. In either case, the outside time \(b_n\) during a complete cycle converges to a positive constant; the outside time accumulated in a partial cycle is therefore uniformly bounded.

The algebraic-duration case models cycles whose long part is generated by a slowly decaying coordinate. The logarithmic-duration case models a long passage near a hyperbolic saddle. The theorem does not cover parabolic return maps with \(p=1\), unbounded outside-time contributions, or duration laws outside the two stated classes.

## Proof

Set \(u_n=-\log x_n\). Taking logarithms of the return law gives
\[
u_{n+1}=p u_n-\log c+O(e^{-\delta u_n}).
\]
Since \(u_n\to\infty\), the error is summable after division by \(p^{n+1}\). Solving this perturbed affine recurrence yields
\[
u_n=Cp^n+\frac{\log c}{p-1}+o(1)
\]
for a constant \(C>0\). Positivity follows because \(u_n\to\infty\); a zero leading coefficient would leave \(u_n\) bounded.

In the algebraic-duration case, \(\tau_n\sim a e^{q u_n}\). Consecutive durations have ratio tending to infinity, because \(u_{n+1}-u_n\to\infty\). Thus the elapsed time \(t_n=\sum_{j<n}\tau_j\) is asymptotic to its last term, \(\tau_{n-1}\). Consequently
\[
\log t_n=qCp^{n-1}+O(1),\qquad
\log\log t_n=(n-1)\log p+O(1).
\]
The difference between \(\log\log t_n\) and \(\log\log t_{n+1}\) stays bounded, so for all large \(T\),
\[
N(T)=\frac{\log\log T}{\log p}+O(1).
\]

In the logarithmic-duration case, \(\tau_n\sim a u_n\sim aCp^n\). Summing this geometric-scale sequence gives \(t_n\asymp p^n\), and therefore
\[
N(T)=\frac{\log T}{\log p}+O(1).
\]

In both cases, the cumulative outside time is
\[
L(T)=\sum_{j<N(T)}b_j+O(1).
\]
Because \(b_j\to b_0\), the Cesàro mean of the summands tends to \(b_0\), so \(L(T)\sim b_0N(T)\). Substituting the two cycle-count laws proves both occupation laws and their time-fraction versions.

## Verification

For the piecewise-smooth flow studied in Oljača, Ashwin, and Rasmussen,
\[
\dot\theta=
\begin{cases}
y^3,&\theta\le y,\\
1,&\theta>y,
\end{cases}
\qquad \dot y=-y^2,
\]
with \(\theta\) taken modulo \(1\), consider section returns at \(\theta=0\). For \(0<x\le1\), the return map satisfies \(0<F(x)<x\), so the section heights decrease to zero and every \(y>0\) trajectory has infinitely many finite-time returns. The slow branch gives the crossing height
\[
r(x)=\sqrt{1+x^2}-1.
\]
The fast branch then gives the exact return map
\[
F(x)=\frac{r(x)}{1+r(x)-r(x)^2}
=\frac{x^2}{2}-\frac{3x^4}{8}+O(x^6).
\]
Thus \(p=2\), \(c=1/2\), and \(\delta=2\). The vertical equation has the exact solution \(y(t)=x/(1+xt)\), so the cycle duration is
\[
\tau(x)=\frac1{F(x)}-\frac1x\sim\frac2{x^2}.
\]
This is the algebraic-duration case with \(q=2\).

For \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\), take the neighbourhood
\[
U_\varepsilon=\{(\theta,y):\operatorname{dist}_{S^1}(\theta,0)<\varepsilon,\ 0\le y<\varepsilon\}.
\]
After finitely many cycles, the slow branch lies inside \(U_\varepsilon\). During the fast branch, \(\dot\theta=1\), and the time outside \(U_\varepsilon\) is exactly \(1-2\varepsilon\). Hence \(b_0=1-2\varepsilon\), and for every initial condition with \(y>0\),
\[
\frac{L_\varepsilon(T)}{T}\sim
\frac{1-2\varepsilon}{\log 2}\frac{\log\log T}{T}.
\]
The boundary trajectories with \(y=0\) are excluded from this return-law statement.

A symbolic check of the exact return-map expansion and duration coefficient is included in `verify.py`. The proof above, rather than finite numerical testing, establishes the asymptotic laws.

## Relationship to prior work

Oljača, Ashwin, and Rasmussen introduce the displayed flow as an example of a statistical attractor and derive its return-map expansion and qualitative time-average property. Their paper does not state the two-regime return-map theorem or the resulting \(\log\log T/T\) rate. The present statement separates the return-map order \(p\) from the cycle-duration scale, which is why the same power-law return map leads to different occupation rates in the two cases.

Newman, Ashwin, and Rasmussen develop bounded-return-time statistical attractors and study a hyperbolic homoclinic-loop example. That work is close in motivation: logarithmic saddle-passage times naturally fit the second duration regime when the return map has a power-law contraction and the outside-time contribution converges. The theorem here is conditional on the displayed asymptotics and does not assert that every homoclinic loop satisfies them.

## Limitations

The result assumes a one-dimensional positive return coordinate, a superattracting power-law return map, one of the two explicit cycle-duration laws, and a positive limiting outside-time contribution. It does not cover all statistical or bounded-return-time attractors. It supplies asymptotic equivalences, not uniform finite-\(T\) error bounds. The application to the piecewise-smooth flow uses only trajectories with \(y>0\) and infinitely many returns.
