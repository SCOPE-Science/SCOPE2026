# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

In char7, solving gradients forces all homogeneous coordinates nonzero, Y^7=2 after X=1, hence unique P=[1:2:4]; direct substitution confirms it is on C. The exact local shear Z=4+3u+3u^2+5u^3+h with Y=2+u yields coefficient valuations in h: constant order7, linear order4, quadratic coefficient a unit, cubic degree1. Weierstrass reduction/completing the square gives analytic v^2=u^7 up to a unit, a single branch of semigroup <2,7> and delta3. Geometric integrality follows because distinct positive-degree projective components must meet in a multiple-branch singularity, contradicted by the unique unibranch point. Arithmetic genus3 minus delta3 gives genus-zero normalization with the unique F7-rational preimage of P, hence P^1; normalization bijective on every F_{7^m} rational point yields 7^m+1 and the displayed zeta. Independent exact finite-field checks q=7 and49 and symbolic local Newton valuation computations corroborate but do not prove the all-extension theorem.

## originality

PASS

Dolgachev–Martin 2024 explicitly observe that this plane Klein model becomes singular in characteristic seven; the inspected discussion does not determine the completed local cusp or complete rational-point zeta formula. van Bommel et al concern stable plane-quartic reduction in a different characteristic scope. Elkies 1999 treats the Klein quartic arithmetic in depth and is an especially plausible historical predecessor, but full retrieval encountered HTTP429; only the accessible summary was read, so priority uncertainty is material but not alone a scientific FAIL.

## value

PASS

The naive plane Klein quartic is a canonical genus-three curve with exceptional reduction at the wild prime seven. Pinning down its exact geometric singularity, genus loss and all-extension zeta for this literal model—not confusing it with a potentially good stable model—is a natural and reusable bad-reduction fact, not an arbitrary finite-field statistic.
