# Review of the chain-ring square-zero image-length theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** A primitive two-by-two matrix has Smith diagonal form \(\operatorname{diag}(1,d)\), determining image length from determinant valuation. The proof partitions by minimum entry valuation, separates automatic square-zero shells from constrained shells, and uses cyclicity of nonzero residue square-zero matrices plus a two-coordinate smooth trace–determinant fiber calculation. Each shell and every determinant valuation is counted without overlap. The deep-shell total agrees with the cardinality of an ideal-valued full matrix space; the shallow shells give equal masses. An independent brute-force test verified every coefficient in nineteen complete finite cases, including extension and mixed characteristics. Residual risk: the trace–determinant chart and Smith normal form use the commutative chain-ring assumption, and must not be applied to non-chain or noncommutative rings.

**Originality — PASS under the inspected comparisons, with priority risk.** Shape-counting results for unrestricted matrices do not impose the nonlinear square-zero condition; traceless-matrix enumerations do not impose zero determinant. Drazin-index and historic nilpotent counts may already imply an *unrefined* total and are not asserted original. The claimed claim is the uniform all-length distribution by image module length, its valuation-fiber formula and acyclic count. The closest separately recorded results for length-two rings (all matrix sizes) and length-four local matrix factors (size two) address bounded ring lengths, not the all-length uniform statement. Earlier results may provide an unrecognized implication, especially the inaccessible Drazin-index full text; this residual risk precludes a claim of certified first publication.

**Value — PASS.** The image length of a square-zero matrix measures boundaries in a two-term periodic differential over a finite local ring. Its complete distribution is a natural matrix-algebra invariant, not an arbitrarily selected coefficient. The uniformity between unequal-characteristic chain rings and the rational variation with length give structural insight inaccessible from a single small-ring census. The acyclic locus and its exact size have an independent homological interpretation.
