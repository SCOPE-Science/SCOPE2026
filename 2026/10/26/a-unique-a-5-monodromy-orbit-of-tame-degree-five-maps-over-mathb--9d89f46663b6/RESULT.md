# A unique \(A_5\)-monodromy orbit of tame degree-five maps over \(\mathbf F_2\)

## Finding
Over \(\mathbf F_2\), there are exactly **36** separable degree-five rational maps \(\mathbf P^1\to\mathbf P^1\) that are tamely ramified and have geometric monodromy group \(A_5\). They form one orbit under independent source and target changes of coordinates by \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_2)\), and every member has trivial stabilizer for this action. One representative is
\[
f(x)=\frac{x^2+x+1}{x^5+x^3+1}.
\]
Every such map has exactly four geometric ramification points, each with ramification index \(3\); Frobenius partitions these points into orbits of degrees \(1\) and \(3\).

For context, the **complete tame degree-five census** comprises \(60\) morphisms and three left-right orbits of sizes \(6,18,36\). The first two are cyclic-monodromy families, already described structurally by the power-map/Rédei theory; their enumeration is a completeness check, not a separate originality claim.

## Assumptions and scope
All curves, maps and automorphisms are defined over \(\mathbf F_2\), and ramification and monodromy are geometric, over \(\overline{\mathbf F}_2\). Maps are counted as actual morphisms in **fixed** source and target coordinates, not up to equality only on rational points. A degree-five rational morphism is a pair of coprime binary homogeneous degree-five forms, modulo common scalar. Because \(\mathbf F_2^\times=\{1\}\), that pair has a unique coefficient representation. A map is **tame** when no geometric ramification index is divisible by the characteristic \(2\).

A nonzero polynomial represented by the integer mask \(a\) has coefficient of \(x^j\) equal to bit \(j\) of \(a\). Its homogeneous degree-five lift is \(\sum_{j=0}^5 a_j X^j Z^{5-j}\). Thus the witness has the pair of masks \((7,41)\). Two maps are equivalent when one is obtained from the other by a Möbius substitution in the source and an independent Möbius transformation of the target.

## Proof
**Complete parameter space.** Every pair of binary masks \(0\le a,b<64\), with at least one degree-five coefficient nonzero and coprime as polynomials, defines a degree-five map. Conversely, every such map has this form. Direct exact polynomial gcd enumeration gives \(1536\) maps; this also equals \(2^{2\cdot5-1}(2^2-1)\).

**Exhaustive geometric ramification.** At a finite point \(u\), define
\[
N_u(t)=A(u+t)B(u)-A(u)B(u+t).
\]
Since \(A,B\) are coprime, the ramification index is the least \(k\ge1\) for which the coefficient of \(t^k\) in \(N_u(t)\) is nonzero; this works whether the image is finite or infinite. The analogous formula in coordinate \(z=1/x\) checks infinity. The coefficient of \(t\) is the Wronskian \(A'B-AB'\), which in characteristic \(2\) is a square with degree at most \(8\). Consequently, all finite ramification points have minimal polynomials of degree at most \(4\). The eight monic irreducibles of degrees \(1\) through \(4\) are exhaustively checked. A second checker instead evaluates all points of \(\mathbf F_{16}\) and all non-\(\mathbf F_2\) points of \(\mathbf F_8\), plus infinity. Together these cover all geometric ramification points. Each computation finds exactly \(60\) tame maps, with distributions
\[
36\times(3,3,3,3),\qquad24\times(5,5),
\]
where the lists give all indices strictly larger than \(1\).

**Orbit and monodromy.** The group \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_2)\) has order \(6\). Exhausting all \(6^2=36\) left-right changes for each candidate partitions the \(60\) tame maps into three orbits: \(6\) represented by masks \((19,33)\), \(18\) represented by \((1,32)\), and \(36\) represented by \((7,41)\). The first two have profile \((5,5)\); the third has profile \((3,3,3,3)\), so every \(A_5\) candidate belongs to the one size-36 orbit, whose stabilizer has order \(1\).

For a tame degree-five cover with profile \((3,3,3,3)\), geometric inertia acts in the degree-five permutation representation as a \(3\)-cycle. The geometric monodromy group is generated by its inertia groups: their normal closure yields a quotient unramified over the entire projective line, and that quotient must be trivial. Thus the group is generated by even permutations and embeds in \(A_5\). Transitivity and the presence of a \(3\)-cycle imply that its order is divisible by \(15\). As \(A_5\) has no subgroups of orders \(15\) or \(30\), the group is \(A_5\). Conversely, a tame cover with a 5-cycle at its only two geometric ramification points has cyclic geometric monodromy (or is checked as part of the explicit orbit representatives). Therefore exactly the \(36\) maps in the third orbit have geometric monodromy \(A_5\).

**Frobenius signature.** For the displayed representative,
\[
A'B-AB'=(x^3+x+1)^2.
\]
The polynomial \(x^3+x+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbf F_2\), so the three finite ramification points form a single degree-three Frobenius orbit, each of index \(3\). At infinity the rational map has index \(3\). No additional ramification points occur, by the explicit exhaustive local calculations (also consistent with tame Riemann–Hurwitz: \(4(3-1)=2\cdot5-2\)). The property persists throughout the \(\mathbf F_2\)-rational coordinate orbit.

## Verification
Run the two standard-library-only scripts:

```
python3 verify_symbolic.py
python3 verify_extensions.py
python3 verify_pairwise.py
```

Each must print `VERIFY_OK`. The symbolic checker enumerates irreducible critical-point factors and all group orbits; the second uses multiplication tables for \(\mathbf F_{16}\) and \(\mathbf F_8\); the third asserts equality of the two exact coefficient-pair sets. The complete list of \(60\) pairs is provided as `tame_masks.csv`, and exact outputs in `verification_output.txt`. The checkers determine an exhaustive **finite** classification; no finite experimentation is invoked as a proof of general statements about arbitrary field orders.

## Relationship to prior work
Kedlaya, Litt and Witaszek establish existence of tame morphisms from smooth projective curves over finite fields, but do not classify or count degree-five maps over \(\mathbf F_2\). Bouw and Wewers prove existence of tame \(A_n\)-monodromy covers in positive characteristic, including relevant algebraically closed characteristic-two cases, without the present smallest-field arithmetic orbit enumeration. Tang and Fan classify exceptional degree-five maps and identify the cyclic geometric-monodromy cases with power and Rédei maps; this already covers the **cyclic** background in our full census but does not imply the count or rigidity of the nonexceptional \(A_5\) orbit. Bell, Booher, Chen and Liu study tame alternating/symmetric monodromy for three-point punctured projective lines, not this complete four-branch-point arithmetic census. The available mathematical records and primary texts inspected did not exhibit an implication covering the \(36\)-map theorem.

## Limitations
The result is over \(\mathbf F_2\) only, concerns degree five only, and classifies **two-sided rational coordinate equivalence** rather than equivalence over algebraic closures. The covering maps are not claimed to be exceptional permutations. The cyclic subfamilies and geometric possibility of \(A_5\) monodromy were previously known; the present contribution is the exact \(\mathbf F_2\)-rational count and single-orbit rigidity of the \(A_5\) locus. Unindexed computations may predate this explicit arithmetic classification. Independent mathematical auditing has not been performed.
