# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The conditional-maximum inclusion–exclusion formula integrates exactly to the stated covariance. Convexity of the conditional maximum and the decreasing beta density establish precisely one interior sign reversal, including its two-sided bounds. An exponential-coordinate coupling plus a uniform gamma concentration estimate supplies the double-logarithmic asymptotic. The attached checker passed 13,112 finite checks, which do not replace the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS with documented historical risk

Holst (1980) supplies the known uniform-spacing maximum laws. Decreusefond–Ferraz (arXiv:1008.5140) computes component-count laws for a Poisson geometric model. Holst (1981), Hüsler (1982) and Holst–Hüsler (1984) are the closest circle-spacing antecedents, including asymptotic joint count/maximum questions. The accessible claims did not cover the exact all-size maximum–exceedance covariance, single sign reversal, or sharp double-logarithmic location. Comparisons also separated existing results on counts versus uncovered total length, counts versus power sums, and pairs of count statistics. Full texts of the highly relevant 1982 and 1984 papers were not available; historical priority is therefore uncertain and not guaranteed.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The largest gap captures geometric extremes, whereas the number of gaps above a threshold captures connectivity and coverage. The strict covariance-sign phase transition exists for every sample size and, unexpectedly, occurs at a cutoff far smaller than the classical longest-gap threshold while the expected exceedance count still diverges. This is a structural gap-dependence law, not merely a computation of a marginal distribution.

## Scope and limitations

Only symmetric unit-parameter Dirichlet spacings and deterministic thresholds are covered. The evidence is analytic and computational, without an independent audit. Inaccessible historical primary works remain a priority risk.
