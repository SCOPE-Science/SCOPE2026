# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof specifies independent single-type negative-binomial Galton–Watson offspring, two positive means and dispersions, a common binomial-thinning factor, and the unrestricted analytic continuation \(p>1\). The fixed-point theorem establishes the critical boundaries. The strictly increasing inverse map \(\Phi_k\) and its strictly increasing ratio for two distinct dispersion parameters prove uniqueness and all strict comparisons. The exact example solves both pgf equations by factorization; a separately executable verifier reproduces its values and exercises parameter samples. No finite computational sample is presented as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, residual risk

The 2005 original article explicitly derives the negative-binomial pgf and compares extinction versus mean and dispersion, including population-wide control. Those objects and fixed-point computations are not claimed as discoveries. Its comparison at equal reproduction mean and across differently designed intervention mechanisms does not state the present **different means, different dispersions, shared intervention** exact global one-crossing theorem. The 2014 full-text moment-comparison work concerns bounded offspring laws and moment envelopes; the 2026 full-text negative-binomial bounds concern finite-generation extinction convergence, not paired rank reversals. A mathematically related public household-epidemic ranking result compares distinct reproduction-number definitions, not ultimate survival probabilities of two scalar branching laws. A specialized prior theorem using a different parameterization could still subsume this comparison. The 2005 supplementary derivations were not completely accessible; priority is not independently certified.

## Value — PASS

The nontrivial structural question is whether the epidemic line with higher mean but more variable transmission can exchange the *ordering of major-outbreak probability* as contacts are uniformly suppressed. A single-crossing theorem forbids reentrant risk rankings for this widely used negative-binomial family; the exact crossing equation and the explicit physical-control example provide a diagnostic unavailable from the mean threshold alone. It is not an empirical containment recommendation, a vaccine comparison, or an assertion about finite outbreaks.

## Boundaries

At low retention, both processes can have zero survival; the uniqueness claim excludes those trivial ties. The positive crossing always exists mathematically for \(p>0\), but lies within physical thinning \(p\in[0,1]\) only when \(p_*\leq1\). The model's generation-independence and fixed dispersions are essential; the main 2005 paper also discusses control designs in which dispersion changes.
