# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The result is derived from the two-qubit global secular rate generator, with explicit assumptions on frequency positivity, local transition strengths and bath spectra. Direct subtraction gives the exact current-difference factorization. Strict increase of \(z\coth z\) and its derivative proves strict increase of the logarithmic frequency ratio, and opposite low- and high-temperature limits force exactly one crossing. A separate four-state generator computation reproduced the currents and factorization in 175 deterministic parameter cases; finite tests supplement, not establish, the universal proof.

**Originality — PASS, with disclosed risks.** Upadhyay et al. (arXiv:2106.02891) have substantially the same global heat-current expressions (their Eqs. 18 and 26) for a locally phase-equivalent two-qubit hopping model. They acknowledge competing frequency contributions but describe the rectification sign as detuning-determined; neither their discussion nor Fogedby's (arXiv:2301.13544) stationary-current derivation supplies a theorem that a fixed bath-temperature ratio has precisely one polarity inversion as both temperatures are scaled. Khandelwal et al. (arXiv:2208.10809) study a different transport/rectification optimization and different limits of two-qubit models. Earlier spin-chain rectification reversals do not contain this specified two-frequency uniqueness statement. The scalar factorization is derived from previously known equations: novelty is restricted to the global monotonicity argument, unique-crossing classification and the model-specific consequence. A mathematically equivalent analysis in unindexed or unsurveyed literature is an ongoing risk, and the review is not independent.

**Value — PASS.** The all-temperature uniqueness theorem determines whether and how a heat diode's preferred orientation must change with absolute temperature despite fixed detuning, interaction and temperature ratio. It gives a single scalar inversion calibration point and a rigorous bound of one inversion, resolving the competition between the two known heat-transfer channels; this is not only a finite numerical observation or an arbitrary parameter slice.

**Scientific limitations.** Strict bath spectral flatness and symmetric rates, the global secular approximation, nonzero detuning/coupling and positive separated Bohr frequencies are essential. No statement is made for unequal or structured baths, local/nonsecular master equations, or resonance. Previous current formulas and general rectification physics are not claimed as new.
