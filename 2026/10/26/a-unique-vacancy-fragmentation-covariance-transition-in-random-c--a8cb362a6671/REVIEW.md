# Review of the vacancy–fragmentation covariance transition

## Correctness — PASS

The exact joint moments are derived from uniformly distributed simplex gaps, including the diagonal/off-diagonal terms. A derivative sign argument supplies a single zero at every finite \(n\), and local-uniform asymptotics plus a nonvanishing limiting derivative give the second-order critical length. Exact rational tests, independent finite lattice-composition evaluations, and numerical root checks support, but do not replace, the general symbolic proof. The exceptional \(n=2\) boundary is treated explicitly.

## Originality — PASS (self-review, with material priority risk)

Huillet (2003, DOI 10.1239/aap/1059486818) gives gap-count and vacancy marginal laws in the accessible full text; Moriarty et al. (2007, DOI 10.1186/1471-2105-8-70) give a gap-count Poisson approximation with genomic applications. Huffer (1988, DOI 10.2307/3214306), and a related 1987 report, explicitly study the joint moments of the **same** two variables in a more general random-arc-size model, so the covariance formula alone is potentially covered and is not treated as novel. The supported new contribution is the nontrivial proof that its sign changes **exactly once** for every \(n\ge2\), with precise finite-size displacement of the unique transition from the limiting depth. The full Huffer article could not be inspected; if it already gives this sign classification, originality would fail. This residual risk is substantive and no independent priority certification is claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The sign transition concerns the natural joint behavior of gap count and uncovered fraction in a classical random coverage model. It distinguishes few large gaps from many small gaps, and supplies a sharp depth boundary rather than only moment formulas. The strict all-\(n\) classification and explicit correction have mathematical relevance independent of unmotivated parameter slices.

## Limitations and closest sources

The statement is for iid uniform starts on a unit circle with fixed equal arc length, not variable arcs or boundary-affected interval coverage. Closest sources are Huffer (1988), Holst–Hüsler (1984), Huillet (2003), and Moriarty–Marchesi–Metcalfe (2007). Huffer's unavailable full text leaves historical-priority uncertainty even though it does not invalidate the self-contained proof.
