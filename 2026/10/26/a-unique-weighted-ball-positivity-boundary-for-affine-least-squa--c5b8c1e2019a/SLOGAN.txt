For radial beta-weighted least-squares projection onto affine functions on the Euclidean ball, an input between zero and one can fit below \(-1\) exactly when the effective dimension \(d+2\alpha+2\) exceeds one unique threshold strictly between \(12\) and \(13\). Thus dimension \(11\) is the first uniform-weight case, and a strict likelihood-ratio ordering establishes the continuous-weight phase boundary rather than merely tabulating integer norms.
