# Adjoint-rank gap and Veronese centralizer stratum in a truncated free algebra

## Finding

For every finite field \(\mathbb F_q\), let \(A=\mathbb F_q\langle x,y\rangle/(x,y)^4\). Define \(\operatorname{ad}_a(b)=ab-ba\). The complete rank distribution of this linear map, as \(a\) ranges over \(A\), is
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrr}
 r&0&1&2&3&4\\\hline
 \#\{a:\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{ad}_a=r\}&q^9&(q^2-1)q^9&(q^4-q^2)q^9&0&(q^2-1)q^{13}
\end{array}
\]
The rank-three stratum is empty in all characteristics. The rank-one locus modulo the center is the cone of nonzero squares of linear forms in the quadratic component. In particular,
\[
\Pr(ab=ba)=2q^{-4}+q^{-5}-q^{-6}-q^{-7}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope

Here \((x,y)\) is the two-sided ideal generated by \(x,y\) in the free **unital associative** algebra. Its fourth power consists of words of length at least four. Thus the images of all words of lengths \(0,1,2,3\) form a basis and \(\dim_{\mathbb F_q}A=1+2+4+8=15\); in particular, \(|A|=q^{15}\). Every element has a unique decomposition
\[
 a=c+v+Q+R,\quad c\in\mathbb F_q,\quad v\in V,\quad Q\in V^{\otimes2},\quad R\in V^{\otimes3},\quad V=\mathbb F_qx\oplus\mathbb F_qy.
\]
The probability is for two independent uniform elements of the entire algebra, not just its units. The field size \(q\) is any prime power, without a characteristic restriction.

## Proof

Any cubic word commutes with every positive-degree element in this quotient; scalars also commute. Therefore \(\operatorname{ad}_a\) depends only on \(v,Q\). Its only possibly nonzero homogeneous blocks are
\[
 V\longrightarrow V^{\otimes2}\oplus V^{\otimes3},\quad w\longmapsto [v,w]+[Q,w],
\qquad V^{\otimes2}\longrightarrow V^{\otimes3},\quad B\longmapsto[v,B].
\]

**Nonzero linear part.** If \(v\ne0\), change the basis of \(V\) so that \(v=x\). The images of quadratic words under commutation with \(x\) are \([x,x^2]=0\), \([x,xy]=x^2y-xyx\), \([x,yx]=xyx-yx^2\), and \([x,y^2]=xy^2-y^2x\). These are three independent cubic tensors over *any* field. Meanwhile the degree-two commutator map \(V\to V^{\otimes2}\), \(w\mapsto[x,w]\), has rank one and kernel \(\mathbb F_qx\). For that kernel vector the cubic image \([Q,x]=-[x,Q]\) already belongs to the three-dimensional image of the quadratic block. Thus the full map has rank \(3+1=4\), regardless of \(Q\).

**Zero linear part.** If \(v=0\), only \(w\mapsto[Q,w]\) from \(V\) to \(V^{\otimes3}\) survives. If \(Q=0\), its rank is zero. If \(Q\ne0\), its rank is one if and only if a nonzero \(w\in V\) satisfies \([Q,w]=0\). Choose an independent linear word \(z\), and express \(Q=\alpha w^2+\beta wz+\gamma zw+\delta z^2\). Then
\[
 [Q,w]=\beta(wzw-w^2z)+\gamma(zw^2-wzw)+\delta(z^2w-wz^2).
\]
The coefficients of the five distinct words force \(\beta=\gamma=\delta=0\). Hence \(Q=\alpha w^2\), with \(\alpha\ne0\). Conversely, every such square commutes with \(w\), and the map is nonzero because \(w\) does not commute with an independent \(z\). In particular, \(Q\) has rank one exactly on the nonzero quadratic Veronese cone and otherwise has rank two. No characteristic-specific division is used.

The cone has exactly \(q^2-1\) points: each projective line \(\mathbb F_qw\subset V\) determines a distinct one-dimensional subspace \(\mathbb F_qw^2\subset V^{\otimes2}\), and each has \(q-1\) nonzero elements. There are \(q+1\) projective lines, so \((q+1)(q-1)=q^2-1\). Injectivity of the projective square map follows because if two squares are proportional, the tensors have the same one-dimensional image under contraction on either factor.

For \(v=0\), there are \(q^9\) choices of the scalar and cubic parts. Therefore the rank-zero, rank-one, and rank-two counts are respectively \(q^9\), \((q^2-1)q^9\), and \((q^4-q^2)q^9\). For \(v\ne0\), the \((q^2-1)\) possibilities for \(v\), the \(q^4\) for \(Q\), and the \(q^9\) for the scalar and cubic parts give \((q^2-1)q^{13}\) rank-four elements. Their total is \(q^{15}\).

For each rank \(r\), the centralizer has size \(q^{15-r}\). Thus averaging centralizer sizes over the four strata gives
\[
 \frac{q^9+q^{-1}(q^2-1)q^9+q^{-2}(q^4-q^2)q^9+q^{-4}(q^2-1)q^{13}}{q^{15}}
 =2q^{-4}+q^{-5}-q^{-6}-q^{-7}.
\]
This completes the proof.

## Verification

The standalone file `verify_free_trunc.py` independently constructs the full multiplication table on the fifteen word basis vectors and performs exact modular Gaussian elimination on the resulting commutator matrices. It exhaustively checks all \(q^6\) combinations of the linear and quadratic coefficients for \(q=2,3\), with stratum counts respectively \((1,3,12,48)\) and \((1,8,72,648)\) before multiplying by the unrestricted \(q^9\) scalar/cubic choices. Additionally, it compares the derived classification with direct ranks on 1,000 pseudorandom full algebra elements in each of \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_5\), with explicit rank-one and rank-two witnesses. The script prints `AD_RANK_OK` after all comparisons. These are finite checks only; the proof above supplies the infinite family.

## Relationship to prior work

Dutta, Basnet, and Nath (2015), *On commuting probability of finite rings*, define the same finite-ring probability and develop general bounds and isoclinism invariance; their full text gives the centralizer-sum identity but not an explicit rank census for this truncated free algebra. Bremner (2013), *Free associative algebras, noncommutative Gröbner bases, and universal associative envelopes for nonassociative structures*, establishes the free-word and quotient-algebra background; its constructions do not state this commutator spectrum. Bergman's centralizer theorem concerns nontruncated free algebras, whose centralizers have a radically different structure; the length-four quotient has a large cubic central socle, so that theorem cannot determine the multiplicities here. Related rank spectra for filiform Lie algebras or commuting probabilities for Kronecker path algebras do not imply the present statement: their multiplication laws and tensor blocks are different.

The new contribution is the complete all-characteristic distribution and its geometrically defined Veronese rank-one locus and forbidden rank-three stratum, not the general definition of commuting probability.

## Limitations

The result is for exactly two generators and truncation by words of length four. It does not classify centralizers up to algebra isomorphism, and no formula for longer truncations or more generators is claimed. The executable checks cover prime fields only; the proof covers every prime-power field. The literature checks are bounded and do not establish absolute publication priority; an unindexed equivalent census remains possible.
