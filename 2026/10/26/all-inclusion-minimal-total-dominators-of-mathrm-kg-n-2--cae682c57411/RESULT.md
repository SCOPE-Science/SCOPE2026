# All inclusion-minimal total dominators of \(\mathrm{KG}(n,2)\)

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge4\), an inclusion-minimal total dominating set of the Kneser graph \(\mathrm{KG}(n,2)\) is exactly an edge set of one of the following four simple graphs on the underlying \(n\) labels, with all unused labels isolated: a three-edge matching \(3K_2\), the disjoint union \(K_3\sqcup K_2\), a five-cycle \(C_5\), or a four-clique \(K_4\). Conversely, every edge set of one of these four types is an inclusion-minimal total dominating set. With the convention \(\binom{n}{r}=0\) for \(r>n\), the entire cardinality enumerator is
\[
\mathcal M_n(z)=15\binom n6 z^3+10\binom n5 z^4+12\binom n5 z^5+\binom n4 z^6.
\]
In particular the upper total domination number (the largest cardinality of an inclusion-minimal total dominating set) is \(\Gamma_t(\mathrm{KG}(n,2))=6\) for all \(n\ge4\).

## Assumptions and scope
The vertices of \(\mathrm{KG}(n,2)\) are the two-element subsets of \([n]\); two vertices are adjacent if and only if the subsets are disjoint. Total domination uses **open** neighborhoods: every vertex, including a selected vertex, must have a neighbor in the selected set. "Minimal" means inclusion-minimal, not minimum cardinality. Given a selected family \(S\) of two-element subsets, identify it with the edge set of the simple graph \(H=([n],S)\). Write \(\tau(H)\) for the minimum cardinality of a vertex cover of \(H\). Isolated labels in \(H\) are allowed and do not change its vertex-cover number. \(n=3\) is excluded because its Kneser graph has isolated vertices and admits no total dominating set.

## Proof
**Step 1: exact translation.** A vertex \(\{a,b\}\) of \(\mathrm{KG}(n,2)\) has no neighbor in \(S\) if and only if every edge of \(H\) meets \(\{a,b\}\). The latter says that \(\{a,b\}\) is a vertex cover of \(H\). Because any cover of size below two can be extended to a two-element cover when \(n\ge4\), the family \(S\) is total dominating exactly when \(\tau(H)\ge3\). Deleting a chosen vertex of \(S\) means deleting its corresponding edge in \(H\). Thus inclusion-minimality is equivalent to \(\tau(H)\ge3\) and \(\tau(H-e)\le2\) for every \(e\in E(H)\). Deleting one edge can lower the minimum vertex-cover number by at most one, so in fact \(\tau(H)=3\) and \(\tau(H-e)=2\) for each edge.

**Step 2: six-edge bound.** List the edges as \(e_1,\ldots,e_q\). For each \(i\), choose a two-element vertex cover \(B_i\) of \(H-e_i\). The sets \(e_i\) and \(B_i\) are disjoint, since otherwise \(B_i\) would cover \(H\), contradicting \(\tau(H)=3\). For all \(j\ne i\), the edge \(e_j\) intersects \(B_i\). Consider a uniformly random permutation of the vertices in the support of \(H\). The event \(A_i\) that both elements of \(e_i\) precede both elements of \(B_i\) has probability \(1/\binom42=1/6\). The events are pairwise disjoint: if \(A_i\) and \(A_j\) both occurred, pick \(x\in e_i\cap B_j\) and \(y\in e_j\cap B_i\); \(A_i\) forces \(x\) before \(y\), while \(A_j\) forces \(y\) before \(x\). Therefore \(q/6\le1\), so \(|E(H)|\le6\). This self-contained permutation argument is an application of the classical set-pair bound, not a new general extremal inequality.

**Step 3: complete finite classification.** It now suffices to classify edge graphs with at most six edges, ignoring isolated vertices. The bundled `verify_edge_critical.py` is a fully specified exhaustive finite classifier. Starting from the empty edge graph, it augments each representative by one missing edge whose endpoints are either both existing vertices, one existing and one new vertex, or two new vertices. These three cases are exhaustive. It identifies isomorphic candidates by a canonical signature that enumerates all permutations within each degree class of each connected component, then sorts the signatures of components; this is an exhaustive isomorphism test, not a heuristic graph invariant. Every no-isolate graph with a positive number of edges is generated by this augmentation: remove an edge, discard any newly isolated vertices, use induction on edge count, and add the removed edge in one of the three exhaustive endpoint cases. The program checks vertex-cover numbers directly over all vertex subsets of cardinality at most three. It enumerates \(1,1,2,5,11,26,68\) isomorphism classes with respectively zero through six edges. Exactly four classes satisfy \(\tau(H)=3\) and \(\tau(H-e)=2\) for all edges: \(3K_2\), \(K_3\sqcup K_2\), \(C_5\), and \(K_4\). All four also pass the defining condition directly, establishing both directions of the classification.

**Step 4: labeled counting and upper parameter.** On a fixed six-label support, \(3K_2\) has \(6!/(2^3\,3!)=15\) distinct edge sets. On a fixed five-label support, \(K_3\sqcup K_2\) has \(\binom53=10\), while \(C_5\) has \(5!/10=12\). On a fixed four-label support there is one \(K_4\). Choosing the support among \(n\) labels yields the displayed polynomial. No edge-minimal cover-three graph can have more than six edges, and a copy of \(K_4\) exists whenever \(n\ge4\), giving the sharp upper total domination value \(6\).

## Verification
The self-contained `verify_edge_critical.py` performs complete generation up to the theoretically proven six-edge bound and prints the four class representatives by structural label; its output is in `verify_edge_critical_output.txt`. Independently, `verify_kneser_direct.py` constructs the Kneser open neighborhoods and checks every possible candidate of sizes three through six for orders \(n=4,5,6,7,8\), including single-vertex deletion tests. It matches the coefficient predictions exactly; recorded output is `verify_kneser_direct_output.txt`. Both programs use only the Python standard library. The finite classification is exhaustive because of the six-edge proof and complete augmentation; direct Kneser tests alone would not establish the theorem for unbounded \(n\).

## Relationship to prior work
Ivančo and Zelinka (1993), Theorem 3, established the **minimum** total domination numbers of \(\mathrm{KG}(n,2)\): six for \(n=4\), four for \(n=5\), and three for \(n\ge6\), and characterized three-element total dominators. Those results, including the three-edge matching case, are not presented as new here. Kanté, Limouzy, Mary, and Nourine (arXiv:1407.2053v1, 8 July 2014) studied the general enumeration of inclusion-minimal total dominating sets and reductions to transversal enumeration; their general results do not give this four-type classification or polynomial. Brešar, Cornet, Dravec, and Henning (arXiv:2312.15464v1) studied minimum-cardinality \(k\)-tuple total domination of Kneser graphs, not the complete spectrum of inclusion-minimal sets. Earlier ordinary-domination classifications of \(\mathrm{KG}(n,2)\) have distinct quantifiers: total domination requires neighbors also for selected vertices. The present claim is the *complete inclusion-minimal total-domination classification*, the resulting all-size polynomial, and the corresponding upper invariant. No novelty is asserted for the general permutation/set-pair inequality or for the known minimum cardinalities.

## Limitations
The theorem is restricted to \(2\)-subset Kneser graphs with \(n\ge4\), not all \(\mathrm{KG}(n,r)\). The finite classifier is exact through six edges; its exhaustive coverage relies on the independently proved universal six-edge bound and the stated augmentation induction. The result has undergone only self-review, not an external independent audit. Classical vertex-cover edge-critical graph classifications or material not identified in the inspected literature might independently imply these four finite types; thus absolute publication priority is not established. The 1993 publication supplies historical background but only a year-level public date; the recorded day-resolved relevant archive source is the 8 July 2014 first arXiv version, not a claimed first-ever public appearance of the topic.
