# All minimum simple-path edge decompositions of cycle bouquets

## Finding
For \(t\ge2\) simple cycles of lengths \(\ell_i\ge3\) sharing exactly one hub vertex \(h\), the least number \(p(G)\) of edge-disjoint simple paths partitioning the edge set is \(t\). The complete set of minimum decompositions admits a bijection with an admissible perfect matching of the \(2t\) hub spokes and one internal cut choice per cycle. Consequently
\[
N(G)=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{t}(\ell_i-1)\right)D_t,\qquad D_t=\sum_{j=0}^{t}(-1)^j\binom tj(2t-2j-1)!!.
\]
Here \(D_t\) counts perfect matchings of the distinguished spokes in which the two spokes belonging to a single cycle are never matched together. By convention \((-1)!!=1\). Equivalently, \(D_1=0\), \(D_2=2\), and \(D_t=(2t-2)(D_{t-1}+D_{t-2})\) for \(t\ge3\). The single-cycle boundary is \(p(C_\ell)=2\), with exactly \(\binom\ell2\) minimum path partitions.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, undirected, simple and labeled by their actual vertices. A path is a nonempty *simple* path (no repeated vertices), regarded as an undirected edge set, so reversal gives the same path. A path decomposition is an unordered set of paths whose edge sets are disjoint and have union exactly \(E(G)\). Different cut vertices or hub pairings yield different edge partitions, and permuting the constituent paths does not change a decomposition. Construct \(G\) from \(t\) cycles by identifying exactly one vertex of all cycles, with no other vertex identifications. The two edges incident with the hub in cycle \(i\) are its two distinguished spokes. The claimed formula is for \(t\ge2\) and all integer lengths \(\ell_i\ge3\). This is ordinary edge-disjoint path decomposition, NOT the larger family of path odd-covers allowing temporary nonedges.

## Proof
**Minimum.** The hub has degree \(2t\). Every simple path uses at most two of the edges incident with the hub, so any edge partition into paths has at least \(t\) members. For \(t\ge2\), pair the spokes by a perfect matching in which each pair comes from different cycles; such a matching exists, for example by cyclically pairing the first spoke of cycle \(i\) with the second spoke of cycle \(i+1\) (indices modulo \(t\)). On each cycle delete the hub. The remaining \(\ell_i-1\)-vertex path has \(\ell_i-2\) edges. Choose any one of its \(\ell_i-1\) vertices as a cut vertex. Assign the two complementary paths from the two hub-neighbor endpoints to that cut vertex to the respective spokes. A zero-edge subpath is allowed as a spoke branch. Joining branches from distinct cycles through the hub gives \(t\) simple paths. They partition all edges, proving \(p(G)=t\).

**Rigidity of equality.** In any decomposition consisting of exactly \(t\) simple paths, all \(2t\) spokes must be used, and each path must use exactly two of them. Consequently every path has the hub as an interior vertex, and its portions on each side of the hub lie wholly inside individual cycles. A path cannot pair the two spokes belonging to the same cycle: otherwise it is the sole decomposition path using edges incident with that cycle's hub, while all edges of the cycle's hub-deleted internal path must be covered by this same path. Its two terminal branches would then meet at an internal vertex, repeating that vertex along the purported simple path (or overlap on an edge), both impossible. Thus the hub spokes define a perfect matching that avoids all \(t\) forbidden within-cycle pairs.

**Cut vertices.** Fix a cycle and its two spokes. They belong to different paths. All remaining edges of that cycle form the internal path from one hub-neighbor to the other. Since the two associated decomposition paths are simple and cannot leave this cycle without passing the hub a second time, their portions on this internal path are two disjoint prefixes from opposite ends. Their union must be the entire internal path. Exactly one cut vertex separates the two prefixes, including the two end vertices for which one prefix is empty. There are precisely \(\ell_i-1\) possibilities. Conversely, any admissible spoke matching and any choice of these cut vertices constructs disjoint simple paths as above. The matching and cut positions can be uniquely recovered from a decomposition. This establishes the bijection.

**Counting.** There are \((2t-1)!!\) perfect matchings of the \(2t\) distinguished spokes. For each of the \(t\) disallowed within-cycle spoke pairs, requiring that pair leaves \((2t-3)!!\) matchings on the other spokes. Inclusion-exclusion over \(j\) forbidden pairs yields \(D_t=\sum_{j=0}^t(-1)^j\binom tj(2t-2j-1)!!\). The internal cuts are independent across cycles, giving the product factor. For a single cycle, one path cannot partition all edges, while choosing two distinct cut vertices partitions its edges into two complementary simple paths, uniquely and without ordering, so there are \(\binom\ell2\) partitions.

## Verification
The accompanying standard-library program `verify_path_partitions.py` generates all simple paths as edge masks, exhaustively enumerates unordered minimum-cardinality edge partitions for nine small two- and three-petal bouquets, and compares each count against the formula. Examples: \(N(C_3\vee C_3)=8\), \(N(C_3\vee C_3\vee C_3)=64\), \(N(C_3\vee C_3\vee C_4)=96\). The finite checks supplement rather than prove the all-parameter result. Run using `python verify_path_partitions.py`.

## Relationship to prior work
The 2023 preprint by Borgwardt, Buchanan, Culver, Frederickson, Rombach, and Yoo, *Path Odd-Covers of Graphs* (arXiv:2306.06487), distinguishes ordinary edge-disjoint path partitions \(p(G)\) from path odd-covers \(p_2(G)\), and supplies general degree lower bounds and covering results rather than a census of minimum path partitions of cycle bouquets. Anto and Basavaraju, *Gallai's Path Decomposition for 2-degenerate Graphs* (arXiv:2211.07159), prove a general existence upper bound for 2-degenerate graphs, not this exact count. The hub-degree lower bound \(p(G)\ge t\) is elementary and alone is not presented as the contribution; the assessed finding is the complete bijection and formula for **every** optimal edge partition. Existing separate work on odd biclique covers of cycle bouquets concerns a different graph invariant, with no implication for enumeration of simple-path partitions. A symmetric-difference odd-cover may employ nonedges twice; such optimal covers are not enumerated by this formula.

## Limitations
No claim is made about enumeration of all path odd-covers, nonminimal path partitions, or general cactus graphs with several articulation vertices. The result is for graph-simple paths and ordinary edge partitions, not trail decompositions. A literature search cannot establish absolute priority, and some equivalent enumeration may be unindexed. The proof does not rely on finite experiment or external independent certification.
