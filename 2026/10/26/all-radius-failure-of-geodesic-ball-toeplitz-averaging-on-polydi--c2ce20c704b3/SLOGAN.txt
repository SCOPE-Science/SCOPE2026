For every fixed positive Bergman geodesic radius on \(\mathbb D^n\), \(n\ge2\), vanishing Euclidean ball averages fail to characterize Toeplitz compactness: a bounded smooth real counterexample exists. The geometric Fourier multiplier has an explicit nonzero oscillatory stationary-phase leading term at every radius, guaranteeing infinitely many positive zeros and removing the previously required small-radius hypothesis.
