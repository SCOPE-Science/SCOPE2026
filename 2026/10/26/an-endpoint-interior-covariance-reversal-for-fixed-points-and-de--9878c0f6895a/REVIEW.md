# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is an exact finite statement for every integer \(n\ge4\) and \(\theta>0\). The proof starts with the Ewens two-arrow deletion identity, obtains the adjacent descent marginal, and conditions on a designated fixed point. Deletion leaves an Ewens law on \(n-1\) labels. Counting comparisons unaffected by deletion and those incident to the deleted point yields the two exact regressions. Algebra yields the stated rational covariances, and each sign is decided by an explicitly positive factor. Exact rational enumeration of all permutations up to size eight checks 25 distinct size/concentration strata but is not used as a substitute for the all-size proof.

## Originality — PASS on the inspected evidence

The closest published full texts concern the limiting Ewens descent process (Kammoun 2018), vincular pattern count asymptotics within conjugacy classes (Féray–Kammoun 2023), and Ewens inversion probabilities conditional on the *number* of fixed points (Pinsky–Schickentanz 2025). The 2025 marginal inversion formula is credited rather than claimed as new. A separate indexed finding establishes the uniform-permutation \(\theta=1\) endpoint/bulk cancellation; that special case is credited as covered. The finite specified-label Ewens covariance across all \(\theta>0\), including its sharp interior sign reversal, is not stated in the inspected primary theorems. Residual historical-priority risk is genuine, especially for unpublished or uninspected equivalent local inversion identities.

## Value — PASS

This resolves a motivated dependence question left ambiguous by marginal descent laws and conditioning only on the total number of fixed points. A rare local fixed-point event has opposite signs in the bulk and at the endpoints when \(\theta<1\), even though the aggregated covariance stays negative. Its exact zero locus and different \(n^{-1}\)/\(n^{-2}\) scales explain why the uniform cancellation is a critical boundary rather than a generic independence phenomenon.

## Limitations

The fixed-parameter result is not a claim about arbitrary conjugacy-invariant distributions, arbitrary varying \(\theta\), or cyclic descents. The indexed uniform special-case description was readable only as a search summary; it cannot exclude uninspected stronger theorems in its full text. Historic priority is not independently certified.
