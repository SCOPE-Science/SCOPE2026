# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Every geometric smoothness test uses all three affine-chart Jacobian ideals containing the defining polynomial; the criterion is exact over an algebraic closure. The 279 coefficient-form orbits are complete under explicit generating transformations and a second Gröbner term order independently checks all representatives. Counting ordered independent bases of a net gives exactly 168, and two separate exact autocorrelation implementations (Walsh and shifted bitsets) both yield 4,760,477,568 ordered bases and 28,336,176 nets. The rational-base-point-free assertion follows from the first-jet dimension inequality at a rational point.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The 2022 smooth-pencil and smooth-linear-family papers establish existence and dimension constraints but not this exact number of three-dimensional subspaces. The previously enumerated individual smooth quartics and 5,796,728 smooth pencils are explicit **prior coverage**, not part of the newly claimed contribution. Neither the inspected primary sources nor targeted mathematical-record searches identify an equivalent count of ternary-quartic nets. An unpublished prior finite computation cannot be excluded.

**Value — PASS.** The counted object is the maximal permitted projective-dimensional smooth linear system for plane quartics over the smallest field. Unlike an arbitrary coefficient slice, it is the entire Grassmannian incidence condition, with all seven rational members required to be smooth and the natural rational-base obstruction forced geometrically. The precise count is useful in small-field finite Bertini geometry and provides a checkable benchmark for rational linear systems lying outside the discriminant.

**Scope and risks.** No net orbit decomposition or field-uniform theorem is proved, and the result awaits independent replication. Exact finite exhaustion and orbit reduction, not probabilistic sampling, support the claim.
