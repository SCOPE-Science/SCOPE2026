# An exact cubic Frobenius number for a four-generator quadratic family

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge2\), set
\[
S_n=\langle n^2,n^2+1,n^2+n+1,n^2+n+2\rangle.
\]
The Frobenius number, defined as the largest nonnegative integer outside this additive semigroup, is exactly
\[
F(S_n)=n^3-2.
\]
In particular, the conductor is \(n^3-1\). For every \(n\ge3\), all four displayed generators are minimal; the claim for \(n=2\) remains valid although the fourth generator is redundant.

## Assumptions and scope
All coefficients in representations are nonnegative integers. The parameter \(n\) is an arbitrary integer at least \(2\); there are no asymptotic qualifiers. Put \(a=n^2\), and call \(1,n+1,n+2\) the offsets of the three generators other than \(a\).

## Proof
Let \(r\) range over \(0\le r<a\). Divide uniquely \(r=q(n+1)+s\), with \(0\le s\le n\). Necessarily \(0\le q\le n-1\), and \(q=n-1\) forces \(s=0\). The offset \(r\) is a sum of \(L=\max(q,s)\) offsets from \(\{1,n+1,n+2\}\): if \(s\le q\), take \(s\) copies of \(n+2\) and \(q-s\) copies of \(n+1\); if \(s>q\), take \(q\) copies of \(n+2\) and \(s-q\) copies of \(1\). Thus \(aL+r\) belongs to \(S_n\) and represents residue \(r\) modulo \(a\).

If \(s=n\), then \(q\le n-2\), \(L=n\), and \(r\le a-2\); hence \(aL+r\le an+a-2\). If \(s<n\), then \(L\le n-1\), and \(aL+r\le a(n-1)+a-1<an+a-2\). Therefore every residue modulo \(a\) has some representative in \(S_n\) at most \(an+a-2\). Indeed, put \(B=an+a-2\). If \(m\ge B-a+1\) is congruent to such a representative \(w\le B\), then \(m<w\) would imply \(w\ge m+a>B\), a contradiction. Hence all integers at least \(an-1=n^3-1\) lie in \(S_n\). Consequently \(F(S_n)\le n^3-2\).

For the reverse inequality, suppose \(an-2\) is represented. Write its representation as \(ax+Lb+t\) only conceptually as follows: let \(x\) be the coefficient of \(a\), let \(L\) be the total number of the other generators, and let \(t\) be the sum of their offsets. Then \(an-2=a(x+L)+t\), so \(x+L\le n-1\), and \(t\equiv a-2\pmod a\). Furthermore \(0\le t\le L(n+2)\le(n-1)(n+2)<2a-2\). Hence \(t=a-2\). If \(L\le n-2\), then \(t\le(n-2)(n+2)=a-4\), a contradiction. Therefore \(L=n-1\) and \(x=0\). Starting with \(n-1\) copies of the largest offset \(n+2\) gives offset sum \(a+n-2\). Attaining \(a-2\) requires a reduction of exactly \(n\). Replacing any \(n+2\) by \(1\) reduces the sum by \(n+1\), already too much. Using only replacements by \(n+1\) reduces it by at most \(n-1\). This is impossible. Thus \(an-2\notin S_n\), proving equality.

## Verification
The proof is valid for every \(n\ge2\). A separate direct shortest-path computation of the smallest representable number in each residue class modulo \(n^2\) corroborates the formula for \(2\le n\le30\); this finite computation is not used to infer the general theorem. Run the included `verify.py` with Python 3 to repeat a wider finite check.

## Relationship to prior work
Roune and Woods, *The Parametric Frobenius Problem* (2015, arXiv:1502.06009; primary MSC 11D07), prove eventual quasipolynomiality for polynomial-generator families containing at most three generators, and formulate the general conjecture. The family here has embedding dimension four for \(n\ge3\) and the displayed exact formula holds without exceptions or a parameter threshold. Nari, Numata, and Watanabe, *Genus of numerical semigroups generated by three elements* (2011, arXiv:1104.3600), address three-generated pseudo-symmetric semigroups, not this four-generator family.

## Limitations
No general assertion is made for arbitrary four-generated polynomial families, and the result does not establish the full parametric Frobenius conjecture. Closely related special families may be covered by unindexed or inaccessible literature; independent priority assessment is still needed.
