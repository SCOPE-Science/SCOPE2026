**Correctness — PASS.** The residue-class proof is universal: every remainder modulo the multiplicity \(n^2\) has a constructed semigroup representative below the required cutoff, and the candidate largest gap is excluded by a sharp deficit-of-\(n\) argument among at most \(n-1\) offsets. The proof handles the boundary \(n=2\). A separate shortest-path implementation agrees for all parameters from \(2\) through \(100\), but finite cases are not relied on for the general theorem.

**Originality — PASS under inspected evidence, with residual risk.** Roune–Woods prove eventual quasipolynomiality for up to three polynomial generators; their full general four-generator conjecture is not the present exact formula. Nari–Numata–Watanabe classify pseudo-symmetric three-generated semigroups and do not entail the four-generator claim. Exact-generator web searches and meaning-based research-record comparisons found no statement implying this cubic identity. Unindexed papers or a specialized formula for semigroups generated by two disjoint consecutive pairs remain a publication-priority risk; this self-review cannot establish absolute novelty.

**Value — PASS.** This gives a uniform, all-parameter exact Frobenius and conductor law in a genuinely four-generated polynomial family outside the established three-generator structural theorem. The two adjacent generator pairs are tied by their square multiplicity and an offset of \(n\), producing a cubic sharp cutoff rather than merely a finite example or computational table. Its explicit bound is relevant to the unresolved broader four-generator parametric Frobenius problem.
