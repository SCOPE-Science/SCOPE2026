# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The generated Boolean partition is exactly equality of full temporal itineraries because the two tense operators are mutually inverse permutation actions. Primitive cyclic words separate vertices of a cycle, and distinct primitive necklaces separate different cycles. The conditional product count correctly accounts for rotations and distinct necklace choices. Cycle-index averaging yields the product of binomial generating factors; Witt's formal identity simplifies it exactly to \((1-qz^2)/(1-qz)\). Coefficient extraction and the uniform sample-space denominator establish \(1-2^{-k}\) for every \(n\ge2\). The standalone checker tests finite domains by a method independent of the product formula.

**Originality — PASS (qualified).** The primitive-necklace polynomial, Witt identity, squarefree-polynomial count and Krieger's periodic-orbit embedding criterion are established; neither the arithmetic identity nor the existence criterion is claimed as new. The assessed new statement is the direct logical identification of a generating set in a finite reversible tense algebra with **distinct primitive itineraries**, and its resulting exact all-size joint random-generation probability. The inspected recent tense-algebra papers treat completeness and operator structure rather than generator counts. Relevant necklace-bijection literature is acknowledged; an older specialized equivalence may exist and remains a priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** Generation is a natural invariant of a finite tense algebra: it measures whether a collection of atomic observations can reconstruct every subset of worlds by Boolean and temporal operations. Although a fixed permutation may require more observational predicates, jointly sampling the temporal dynamics and predicates yields a simple, exact constant independent of frame size. The identity is a structural bridge from temporal distinguishability to squarefree factorization, not a single arbitrary small-size census.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Figallo–Sarmiento–Pezzini (arXiv:2610.01547v1) and Bezhanishvili–Kornell (arXiv:2308.08664v2) supply tense-algebra semantics; Krieger's embedding theorem supplies a broader periodic-point criterion for *existence* of a coding, but not the count of all admissible codings of a randomly chosen permutation. Gessel–Restivo–Reutenauer (2012) gives related word/necklace bijections; its full text was not accessible in the checked route. The theorem does not extend without modification to noninvertible relations or nonuniform sampling.
