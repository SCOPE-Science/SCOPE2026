# An exact \(\mathbf F_2\) census of all-smooth pencils of plane cubics

## Finding
In the \(10\)-dimensional vector space \(V=\mathbf F_2[X,Y,Z]_3\), let an **all-smooth rational pencil** be a two-dimensional linear subspace \(L\subset V\) for which each of its three nonzero elements defines a geometrically smooth cubic in \(\mathbf P^2\). There are exactly \(5544\) such \(L\), grouped into exactly \(37\) orbits under \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_2)\). Of these, \(4508\) have geometrically reduced base schemes \(B_L\), whereas \(1036\) have nonreduced base schemes. Each \(B_L\) has degree \(9\). The programs below report one representative per orbit, its orbit size, rational base-point count, rational point counts on its three cubic members, and Frobenius degree partition of its geometric support.

## Assumptions and scope
The field is exactly \(\mathbf F_2\), and two cubic equations represent the same member only if they differ by a nonzero scalar (necessarily \(1\)). A pencil is a *subspace*, not an ordered basis, and distinct pencils are counted in fixed homogeneous coordinates. A nonsingular cubic means geometrically nonsingular over an algebraic closure. The rational smoothness condition tests only the three \(\mathbf F_2\)-members; no condition is imposed on other geometric members. The base scheme is the scheme-theoretic complete intersection of two independent cubic forms. All degree-nine multiplicities are counted scheme-theoretically.

The monomial order for an equation bitmask is \(X^iY^jZ^{3-i-j}\), with \(i=0,1,2,3\) in ascending order and \(j=0,\dots,3-i\) in ascending order. Thus masks encode all \(1024\) ternary cubic forms, of which \(1023\) are nonzero.

## Proof
**Geometric smoothness test.** For a homogeneous cubic \(F\) in characteristic \(2\), Euler's identity is \(XF_X+YF_Y+ZF_Z=3F=F\). Hence a projective singular point is precisely a common projective zero of the three partial derivatives. Such a point exists over the algebraic closure if and only if on at least one chart \(X=1\), \(Y=1\), or \(Z=1\), the ideal of the dehomogenized partial derivatives is proper. The test in `verify_pencils.py` computes an exact reduced Gröbner basis over \(\mathbf F_2\) in each chart, and checks whether it is the unit ideal. This tests the algebraic closure, not merely rational or finite-extension points. Running the test over all \(1023\) nonzero masks gives precisely \(336\) geometrically smooth cubics.

**Exhaustive pencil construction.** Every two-dimensional subspace over \(\mathbf F_2\) has three distinct nonzero vectors \(\{F,G,F+G\}\), and conversely every such unordered triple is a unique pencil. The program examines every pair \(F,G\) in the \(336\)-element smooth set and retains the triple precisely when \(F+G\) is also smooth. Canonical sorting and set deduplication identify each pencil once, yielding exactly \(5544\).

**Projective-equivalence census.** Every projective change of homogeneous coordinates over \(\mathbf F_2\) is represented by an invertible \(3\times3\) binary matrix: there is no nontrivial scalar ambiguity. Exhaustive enumeration of all \(512\) binary matrices yields \(168\) invertible matrices. Exact monomial substitution turns each into a permutation of the \(5544\) pencils. Each representative's orbit is generated by applying all \(168\) transformations; removing complete disjoint orbits exhausts the set and produces exactly \(37\) orbits. Their weighted orbit sizes sum to \(5544\). The representative masks and sizes are printed in `verify_pencils.out`.

**Reduced base schemes.** Two distinct smooth plane cubics have no common curve component. Therefore their scheme-theoretic intersection has length \(3\cdot3=9\) over the algebraic closure, by Bézout. A zero-dimensional complete intersection on a smooth surface is reduced if and only if the two differentials are linearly independent at every common zero. On each coordinate chart the program verifies that the ideal generated by the two dehomogenized cubics and their \(2\times2\) Jacobian determinant is the unit ideal over \(\mathbf F_2\). This is an exact geometric criterion. Reducedness is constant on each projective orbit. Summing corresponding orbit sizes gives \(4508\) reduced and \(1036\) nonreduced pencils.

**Frobenius support.** For each of the \(37\) representatives, an independent C++ program enumerates common projective zeros over \(\mathbf F_{2^d}\), for each \(1\le d\le9\), using explicit irreducible binary polynomials. If \(N_d\) is the number of closed points of residue degree \(d\) in the reduced support, the counts \(B_m\) obey \(B_m=\sum_{d\mid m}dN_d\). Recursive Möbius inversion produces the degree partitions printed in `verify_base_splitting.out`. For all \(37\) representatives the support degree sum is \(9\) if and only if the independent Jacobian ideal test declares the base scheme reduced; smaller sums detect nonreduced multiplicities. The orbit-mass refinement by Frobenius support is determined by these two finite certificates.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_pencils.py` (Python 3 with SymPy), and compare standard output byte-for-byte with `verify_pencils.out`. Compile with `g++ -O2 -std=c++17 verify_base_splitting.cpp -o verify_base_splitting`, run the executable, and compare its output with `verify_base_splitting.out`. The Python output exhibits all 37 orbit representatives with orbit sizes and reports the mass by rational base points and smooth-member rational-point profiles. The C++ output gives each representative's \(\mathbf F_{2^d}\) support counts and residue-degree partition for \(1\le d\le9\). Both were executed during verification, and the recorded output files match the exact output produced. The argument is exhaustive because it treats all \(1023\) nonzero cubic forms and all \(168\) rational projective transformations; no extrapolation is made from a bounded subset.

## Relationship to prior work
Asgarli and Ghioca (first public version March 14, 2022), *Smoothness in pencils of hypersurfaces over finite fields*, formulate precisely the all-smooth rational pencil problem. They prove existence at sufficiently large field size and give explicit finite-field examples for conics and quadric surfaces. Their theorem and examples do not determine the \(\mathbf F_2\) plane-cubic census, projective equivalence orbit distribution, or reduced-base count. Moschetti, Pirola and Stoppino (2024), *Pencils of plane cubics with one base point*, classify pencils over the complex numbers under a different condition: a single geometric base point and a general smooth member. Their result does not enumerate the finite-field pencils considered here. General nine-base-point facts for pencils of plane cubics are used, not claimed as new. The new numerical classification is not asserted to have undergone an independent audit or exhaustive search of unpublished datasets.

## Limitations
The computation establishes exact facts for \(\mathbf F_2\) only. The \(37\) classes are rational projective-coordinate orbits, not isomorphism classes of genus-one fibrations; the base scheme may be nonreduced, and the reduced-support Frobenius partition records closed points, not their infinitesimal multiplicities. The condition that the rational members are smooth does not imply geometric smoothness of *every* pencil member. The originality assessment is qualified by possible unpublished or unindexed finite-field catalogues. Verification uses exact SymPy Gröbner operations and independent finite-field arithmetic, but no independent human audit was performed.
