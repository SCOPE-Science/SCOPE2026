Strict comparison of the defect-distance factor proves every placement extremum. Risks: numerical coefficient is illustrative, no unique limiting optimizer is asserted, and finite checks are not used as infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS within available evidence
Full text of Turban's 2016 preprint was inspected at its zero-field partition-function and correlation sections: it concerns consecutive multispin interactions, not two localized negative two-spin bonds and their distance law. The freely accessible full 2026 antiferromagnetic-chain preprint studies homogeneous exchange and boundary conditions; its finite-ring susceptibility maxima do not imply the exact two-defect optimization or the macroscopic optimized coefficient. Focused prior-record and literature searches for alternative defect/gauge/temperature and scaling formulations found no identified stronger result. Risks: unlocated older inhomogeneous Ising susceptibility formulas may subsume the result; the review is not exhaustive or independently validated.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The exact result answers a physically motivated defect-engineering question at every finite ring size, determines strict extremizing placements, and shows a nontrivial size-linear thermal noise peak in the perfectly compensated two-domain geometry. The global optimized scaling statement is stronger than a finite-temperature evaluation or a single chosen scaling limit, and it identifies a universal crossover without alleging a phase transition.

## Scientific limitations
The finding concerns equal-magnitude signed bonds, two negative bonds only, zero applied field, and temperature-rescaled rather than physical isothermal susceptibility. Numerical optimizer localization is not a uniqueness proof, and an exhaustive historical novelty search remains outstanding.
