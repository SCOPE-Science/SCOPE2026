# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact anticommuting-matrix identity yields the full Fourier-pair spectrum. A Lyapunov almost-supermartingale gives the deterministic limit; the martingale stochastic-approximation theorem gives the two Gaussian sizes. In all other even sizes an explicit gamma-product normalization yields a convergent complex martingale with strictly positive first-step variance. The accompanying verifier tests 74 finite identities and inequalities; no finite computation is treated as an infinite-time proof.

**Originality — PASS, with priority limits.** The 2026 general interacting-system theorem already supplies a general drift framework and upper fluctuation rates, Zhang (2016) supplies general complex-mode theory, and Kaur–Sahasrabudhe (2023) covers signed single-neighbour cycles. This result specifically treats a noncommuting two-source kernel, exactly classifies the exceptional six-cycle, and proves nondegeneracy of the slow random amplitude. General technology and ordinary single-neighbour parity reductions are not claimed as new. Alternative historical spectral parametrizations remain a possibility.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The unexpected nonmonotonic dimension dependence distinguishes Gaussian from genuinely anomalous fluctuations under a fixed simple update rule; the strict random-amplitude lower bound makes the spectral separation substantive rather than a mere rate estimate.

The exact amplitude distribution is unknown. The theorem assumes balanced initial urns, even cycle size, alternating preferences and equal two-source sampling weights. Sharpness applies to an eigenprojection. Literature priority and the proof should receive independent review.
