# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The elementary-symmetric recurrence samples the prescribed weighted subsets exactly, while Newton log-concavity orders the inclusion probabilities for adjacent budgets. A common uniform couples the budgets until their sole discrepancy, yielding the written product law. The finite block-order identity reconstructs the factorial Gibbs mass independently. All boundary cases including the one-block and singleton-block layers are covered. The attached exact verifier tests cut marginals, pair-transition marginals and strict one-split refinement; finite tests are not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
The primary 2006 Ewens cut encoding provides unconditional Bernoulli indicators, and the 2009 general covering theorem, quoted in a fully inspected 2013 preprint, provides the *existence* of monotone adjacent conditional cardinality couplings. Consequently existence is not claimed as novel. Neither inspected source specifies the concrete shared-uniform suffix-symmetric algorithm nor its exact displayed rational probability for individual adjacent cut-set pairs. Published-record and related-literature searches did not reveal that formula, but a known generic algorithm may lead to it; that remains a real priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The source literature explicitly seeks practical finite-size discrete fragmentation sampling with the fixed-count Gibbs law. An executable exact-adjacent-transition specification fills an algorithmic need not supplied by a qualitative existence theorem: it provides transition probabilities, straightforward exact finite computations and coupled simulation of every block-count layer. The standard component lemmas are openly acknowledged and are not standalone contributions.

## Limits
The scientific novelty assessment is provisional; later or more obscure implementations of conditional-Bernoulli covering algorithms may produce equivalent pair formulas. The result does not assert a closed partition-level kernel depending only on block sizes.
