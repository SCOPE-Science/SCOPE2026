# An explicit linear family of pseudo-Frobenius elements in Proth semigroups
## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\), put \(M=7\cdot2^n\) and \(a=M+1\). Let
\[
S_n=\langle M2^i+1:i\ge0\rangle.
\]
There are at least \(n+2\) distinct pseudo-Frobenius elements. Explicitly, for the Apéry representative \(W(t)\) in the residue class \(-t\pmod a\), the following are pseudo-Frobenius:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&W(4\cdot2^n-n-2)-a,\quad W(6\cdot2^n-n-2)-a,\\
&W(M-j)-a\quad(5\le j\le n+2),\\
&W(M-1)-a,\quad W(M)-a.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(\operatorname{type}(S_n)\ge n+2\). The known embedding-dimension formula for these Proth semigroups gives \(e(S_n)=n+3\), so the lower bound is \(e(S_n)-1\). This statement is a lower bound, not an assertion of equality.

## Assumptions and scope
All integers \(n\ge3\) are covered. The semigroup is numerical because \(a\) and \(2M+1=2a-1\) are coprime. By the Proth minimal-generator theorem, \(S_n\) is generated by \(a\) and \(M2^i+1\) for \(1\le i\le n+2\). Put \(L=n+2\), \(c_i=2^i-1\) for \(1\le i\le L\), and let \(\ell(t)\) be the least number of such coins summing to \(t\), for \(t\ge0\). Published greedy-coin and Apéry results give
\[
W(t)=Mt+a\ell(t)\qquad(0\le t\le M).
\]
The result treats type and explicitly exhibited pseudo-Frobenius elements, not the full pseudo-Frobenius set.

## Proof
The referenced greedy-coin lemma for denominations \(2^i-1\) makes greedy decompositions optimal. In each greedy vector the coin multiplicities lie in \(\{0,1,2\}\), and a digit equal to two forces all smaller digits to be zero. Since \(M<2(2^L-1)\), the largest coin occurs at most once. A greedy representation with \(L+1\) coins could only have two unit coins and one of every higher coin, with total value \(2c_1+\sum_{i=2}^{L}c_i=2^{n+3}-n-3>M\). Therefore \(\ell(t)\le L=n+2\) for every \(0\le t\le M\).

For any \(0\le t\le M\) and generator \(s_i=a+Mc_i\), the identity \(W(t)+s_i=W(t+c_i)\) can hold, if \(t+c_i\le M\), only when \(\ell(t+c_i)=\ell(t)+1\). When \(t+c_i>M\), reducing the index by \(a\) makes the difference from the corresponding Apéry representative equal to \(a[M+\ell(t)+1-\ell(t+c_i-a)]>0\), since \(0\le\ell(u)\le u\le M\). Hence \(W(t)-a\) is pseudo-Frobenius precisely when \(\ell(t+c_i)\le\ell(t)\) for every coin with \(t+c_i\le M\). The generator \(a\) itself always increases an Apéry element.

Both \(t_A=4\cdot2^n-n-2=2c_1+\sum_{i=2}^{n+1}c_i\) and \(t_B=6\cdot2^n-n-2=c_{n+2}+2c_1+\sum_{i=2}^{n}c_i\) are greedy decompositions of length \(n+2\). No eligible coin addition can increase their coin counts beyond the global upper bound; therefore both yield pseudo-Frobenius elements.

Next, \(M-3=c_{n+2}+c_{n+1}+c_n\), hence \(\ell(M-3)=3\). Replacing a coin \(c_i\) by two copies of \(c_{i-1}\), or deleting \(c_1\), shows \(\ell(t-1)\le\ell(t)+1\) for every positive \(t\). Thus \(\ell(M-j)\le j\) for integers \(3\le j\le n+2\). For the reverse inequality, a hypothetical representation of \(M-j\) with \(q<j\) coins would express \(M-j+q\) as a sum of \(q\) powers of two (each at least two), giving \(\operatorname{popcount}(M-j+q)\le q\). With \(h=j-q\), one has \(1\le h\le n+2\le2^n\) and
\[
\operatorname{popcount}(M-h)=n+2-\operatorname{popcount}(h-1)\ge n+3-h>j-h=q,
\]
a contradiction. Therefore \(\ell(M-j)=j\) for the stated range.

Moreover \(\ell(M)=4\), \(\ell(M-1)=5\), and \(\ell(M-2)=4\): representations follow from \(M-3=c_{n+2}+c_{n+1}+c_n\) by adding respectively \(c_2\), \(2c_1\), and \(c_1\). Minimality follows by considering \(q<4\), \(q<5\), or \(q<4\) coins and the parity and binary popcounts of \(M+q\), \(M-1+q\), \(M-2+q\), respectively; a sum of \(q\) powers of two must be even and have popcount at most \(q\). The only potentially feasible cases are excluded by \(\operatorname{popcount}(M)=3\) and \(\operatorname{popcount}(M+2)=4\).

For \(5\le j\le n+2\), any eligible addition \(c_i\le j\) maps \(M-j\) to \(M-h\) with \(0\le h<j\). If \(h\ge3\), its optimal coin count is \(h\le j\); if \(h\le2\), the count is at most five, again at most \(j\). So every \(W(M-j)-a\) is pseudo-Frobenius. For \(M-1\), only \(c_1\) is eligible and \(\ell(M)=4\le5\); for \(M\), no positive coin addition is eligible. These two yield pseudo-Frobenius elements as well. Their indices are pairwise distinct modulo \(a\), and the two first indices are below the third family. The claimed count is \(2+(n-2)+2=n+2\).

Finally, substitute the established coin counts into \(W(t)-a=Mt+a\ell(t)-a\) to obtain the explicit numeric values stated in the finding.

## Verification
The accompanying stdlib-only `verify.py` independently computes each complete Apéry set by shortest paths for \(3\le n\le10\), compares it with the greedy formula at every residue, and verifies all exhibited elements using the complete generator list. All checks pass. Finite checks corroborate but do not establish the all-\(n\) proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Srivastava and Thakkar introduced these Proth semigroups, determined embedding dimension, and gave pseudo-Frobenius formulas for a different subfamily of odd multipliers. Liu, Xin, Ye, and Yin provide the broader greedy Apéry framework and Frobenius/genus results for related semigroups, including arithmetic specializations relevant to \(7\cdot2^n+1\). The present structural extraction is the explicit growing family of pseudo-Frobenius elements for multiplier seven; it is not a claim that the general Apéry algorithm or the Frobenius number is new.

## Limitations
No upper bound on the type is proved here. Thus equality \(\operatorname{type}(S_n)=n+2\) is not claimed, despite finite confirmation. Earlier unindexed or implicit extractions from the generic Apéry theorem remain a priority risk. Independent review has not been performed.
