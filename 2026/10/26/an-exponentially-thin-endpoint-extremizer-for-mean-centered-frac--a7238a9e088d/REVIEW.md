# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact kernel follows from Fubini. Sharp operator norm reduces to the absolute \(L^1\) mass of its rows, with a sign-function lower certificate. One explicit row branch has one stationary maximum; the other is proved convex using the moving zero and an exact derivative comparison. A weighted arithmetic–geometric mean bound excludes the final endpoint for all \(\frac12\le\alpha<1\). Algebraic substitution gives the Abel radical, and logarithms of the stationary equation give the exponentially small transition. The independent numerical checker replayed 125 assertions. Numerical tests are supplementary, not a substitute for the analytic proof.

The closest rank-one-corrected Volterra record concerns Hilbert/Schatten singular values, not this order-dependent \(L^\infty\) problem. Historical priority is provisional: Curbera (1999) was reviewed only through an abstract, and unindexed sources remain possible.

## Value — PASS
Removing an output's spatial mean is a natural normalization for fractional antiderivatives and oscillation estimates. The result does not merely compute an arbitrary constant: it locates *all* extremizers through a continuum of canonical fractional orders, identifies the exact Abel case, and exposes a nonperturbative transition as ordinary integration is approached. The exponential layer quantifies why regular order expansions miss a real strict improvement. It is a sharp restricted-range theorem, not a classification of every fractional order.
