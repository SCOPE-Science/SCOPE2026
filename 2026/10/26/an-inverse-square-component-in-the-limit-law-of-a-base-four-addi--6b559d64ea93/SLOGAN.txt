For the standard base-four additive complement \(A\) with digits \(\{0,1\}\) and \(B=2A\), the ratio \(A(n)B(n)/n\), sampled uniformly up to \(4^k\), converges in \(W_1\) at rate \(O(k2^{-k})\) to an absolutely continuous law supported on \([1,3/2]\). An explicit inverse-square component of this law is \(2t^{-2}\,dt\) on \((1,3/2)\), with mass exactly \(2/3\); rational finite-gap calculations certify its entire CDF to an error at most \(2^{-L}\).
