# Scientific review: ordinary characteristic-three Fermat-descendant normalization

## Correctness — PASS
The six geometric singularities follow from the exact characteristic-three gradient and local equation \(u^2+v^3=0\). A connected tame Klein-four cover of a smooth Hesse cubic independently establishes geometric integrality and genus four. Its genus-two intermediate quotient has an explicit hyperelliptic model with two commuting elliptic involutions, yielding the \(k\)-isogeny \(\operatorname{Jac}(C_\lambda)\sim E_\lambda	imes F_\lambda^3\). The elliptic Hasse invariants are \(\lambda^2\) and \(2\lambda\), both nonzero for \(\lambda
e0\); the normalization and point-count statements follow. The finite-field verifier independently checks several small extensions but is not used as proof of the universal theorem.

## Originality — PASS
The source paper by Badr and Bars (arXiv:2208.12749; Proposition 10.4) already presents the Fermat-descendant equation, but its smooth-curve automorphism claims are restricted to characteristic zero or greater than \(21\). The related generalized-Fermat Jacobian decomposition (arXiv:1507.02903) has a genus-zero quotient, while this family has a genus-one Klein-four quotient. Actual searches of named statements, parameters, reductions, and elliptic-factor consequences found no equivalent or dominating result. Similar local claims on other characteristic-three sextic degenerations do not cover this equation. Older unindexed computations could still overlap.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a complete characteristic-three normalization and arithmetic classification of a natural one-parameter symmetric sextic: six cusps, genus four, an explicit four-elliptic isogeny, all-parameter ordinarity, and uniform finite-field zeta formulas. It explains precisely how the small-characteristic family differs from the smooth-sextic moduli studied in the motivating literature. The two pointless \(\mathbb F_3\) examples additionally have distinct genus-four Weil numerators.

## Limitations
The model is the literal mod-three plane equation, not a stable reduction. Isogeny does not identify principal polarizations, and distinct parameter isomorphisms are not classified. Independent validation and a comprehensive historical-literature audit have not been performed.
