# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The result fixes the exact approval sets and Hare quota and applies the core+ assignment constraints as introduced in arXiv:2609.11912v1. Integral coalitions are ruled out because any improved ordinary voter needs both their private incumbent and the common challenger, while the guardian cannot improve. Pareto efficiency follows from the guardian's maximum incumbent utility. The guardian's fractional voter weight is forced to zero, and summing the remaining assignment equations yields the global upper bound. A rational feasible assignment attains it. The polynomial threshold and asymptotic are exact. Exhaustive finite checks and rational witness calculations corroborate, but do not replace, the all-size proof.

## Originality — PASS with priority risk
The primary arXiv:2609.11912v1 defines core+ and proves existence of a qualifying committee but does not give the specific exact, unbounded fractional load for a specified Pareto-efficient ordinary-core committee. Teh, arXiv:2608.11500v1, Section 5.2, explicitly repeats a **known** two-voter separation of core from EJR+; this review therefore excludes general strict separation and any global minimum from the novelty claim. Its FJR+ and priceability comparisons use related but different criteria. Peters, arXiv:2501.18304, analyzes ordinary core and approximate stability, not this exact core+ assignment load. Targeted semantic mathematical-record checks found no statement implying the quantified family. Possible broader prior bounds on fractional proportionality relaxations remain a historical-priority risk; no independent priority audit is claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The newer core+ axiom is intended to certify strong proportional representation. Determining whether its fractional test quantitatively approximates ordinary coalitional stability is therefore a natural structural question. The theorem shows no universal constant bound even if an ordinary-core committee is Pareto-efficient, supplies a sharp exact load rather than an isolated counterexample, and identifies the first family parameter where the stronger test becomes active. The family uses only one challenger and a guardian who makes the incumbent committee Pareto-efficient, eliminating a simple efficiency-based explanation for the disparity. It gives a benchmark for evaluating alternative relaxations, not an assertion about unrestricted tight bounds.

## Boundaries
The theorem is restricted to the explicitly defined committee family. The load measures fractional assignment affordability and does not measure real-coalition gains or social welfare. No mathematical statement about arbitrary approval profiles follows from this construction.
