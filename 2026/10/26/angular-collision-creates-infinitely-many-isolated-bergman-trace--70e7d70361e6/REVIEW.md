# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The normalized Bergman kernel and \(x=\tanh u\) unitary conversion give the exact one-chord convolution. The Fourier beta transform yields its full spectral band. For distinct diameters, the analytic cross kernel is Hilbert–Schmidt, so the Calkin product vanishes. A strict beta-function Rayleigh gap produces a discrete eigenvalue above the band; strong angular convergence and the min–max principle force arbitrarily many such eigenvalues under collision. The proof treats \(\theta=0\) separately and fixes \(\alpha>-1\) throughout. The included checker confirms sample beta and gamma identities, not infinite-dimensional conclusions.

## Originality — PASS
Dawson–Ólafsson–Quiroga-Barranco (arXiv:1805.00141) already diagonalize the relevant one-orbit model: that ingredient is not original. Halder–Kumar (arXiv:2607.13543, first public 2026-07-15) establish current Bergman diameter inequalities without a two-diameter spectral crowding theorem. published-finding corpus searches compared these claims and related harmonic circle/Frazer findings by implication, and found no direct two-orbit collision theorem. Andreev (2012) was not inspectable in full because institution access was unavailable; priority remains a genuine risk.

## Value — PASS
The coexistence of a fixed essential spectral edge, a uniform isolated state, and arbitrarily many discrete states at a collapsing angle is a structural, motivated spectral phase transition in a natural two-trace geometry. It is stronger than the qualitative two-diameter inequality and is not a single numerical constant.

## Limits and closest literature
Individual eigenvalues and optimal fixed-angle norms are not determined, and no quantitative rate of proliferation is claimed. The one-orbit spectrum is classical restriction-principle input; the new result is the paired-orbit collision theorem.
