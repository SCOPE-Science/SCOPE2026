# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The result is an exact asymptotic theorem with all quantifiers fixed before limits. The Legendre generating function, verified integral representation, and an absolutely convergent geometric tail give a finite-\(N\) identity. Uniform convergence of its angular integrand proves the \(J_0\) coefficient. The three-dimensional full Poisson kernel supplies the \(1/2\) term. The identity \(J_0(4)<-1/3\) is certified by an alternating-series argument, not by decimal arithmetic. The source's maximum-principle kernel equivalence then transfers a negative value into the one-sided radius obstruction. The proof does not infer global optimality from a single angle.

## Originality — PASS, subject to incomplete indexing

The closest primary paper is Reusken, arXiv:2610.13540v1, Sections 4--8. The source's Lemma 11 addresses only odd degrees in three dimensions; Remark 7.3 highlights the even-degree gap and a parity effect. The present fixed-angle Bessel scaling resolves that even-degree obstruction and identifies a refined critical logarithm-of-logarithm profile. Reusken's 2025 paper arXiv:2511.01616v1 concerns planar Fourier truncation. Other located harmonic-analysis results concern different operators or endpoint norms, and do not imply this kernel's signed antipodal boundary layer. Search cannot exclude older unindexed formulations; that is the principal bibliographic risk.

## Value — PASS

Positivity of this truncated Poisson kernel is exactly the numerical maximum-principle question. The theorem addresses a named missing parity in a freshly published numerical-analysis result, gives an explicit direction where positivity fails, and locates its competing continuous-positive and truncation-tail terms at an \(N^{-1}\log\log N\) refinement. It is a meaningful asymptotic boundary for the actual discretization, not an arbitrary toy invariant.

## Limitations

This is one-sided: it proves neither an exact global positivity radius nor equality of the displayed constants, and it does not establish that even and odd actual second-order constants differ. The error term is for fixed angular scaling, not a global minimization over angles. Independent audit has not been performed.
