# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The complete proof supplies the explicit ambient norm, Hilbert equivalence, quadratic uniform-convexity estimate, unbounded affine slice, and an explicit support functional for each escaping sequence. The crucial quotient has the strictly positive limit \(\beta(1-b)/\sqrt{1+\beta}\); that contradicts membership in any unbounded localization set. The invoked Kirk–Ray theorem is stated in Japón–Laguna-Ricart (2026), Section 2; uniformly convex bounded-set fixed points are classical. These are cited inputs, not experimental extrapolations.

## Originality — PASS, subject to independent priority verification
The directly motivating paper constructs a fixed-point set in an equivalent Hilbert renorming but supplies neither a uniformly convex norm nor an arbitrarily small Hilbert distortion for which the geometry survives. The quadratic-sum perturbation and its norm-one support functionals supply both. The 2010 smooth-space firmly-nonexpansive-like Ray theorem does not cover this explicitly nonsmooth construction or the same class of all ordinary nonexpansive mappings. Searches of full primary text, related literature, and published mathematical-result records did not find the simultaneous sharp strengthening, but cannot exclude an older uninspected general theorem.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result identifies a genuine geometric boundary: arbitrarily small power-type-two uniformly convex perturbations of Hilbert geometry admit unbounded nonexpansive fixed-point sets, while the exact Hilbert metric does not. It answers the natural stability and uniform-convexity refinements of a recent construction and maintains the same unbounded set uniformly across every positive perturbation parameter. Neither quantitative uniform convexity nor arbitrary closeness is an automatic consequence of the published max-gauge construction.

## Scope and risks
The norm need not be smooth; the result says nothing about all nearby norms, or common fixed points for semigroups. Full historical uniqueness cannot be certified from literature retrieval. The accepted statement rests on the named Kirk–Ray theorem and the standard bounded-set theorem, rather than on a new independent formal proof of those classical ingredients.
