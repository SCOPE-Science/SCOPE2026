# Arithmetic census of squarefree quintic pencils over \(\mathbf F_3\)

## Finding
Let \(V=H^0(\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf F_3},\mathcal O(5))\). A pencil \(W\subset V\) is *all-squarefree* if every member of \(\mathbf P(W)(\mathbf F_3)\) is a reduced geometric divisor of degree five. There are exactly \(1\,098\) all-squarefree pencils among \(11\,011\) rational projective pencils. Under source-coordinate \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_3)\), they form \(54\) orbits: two of size \(3\), two of size \(6\), ten of size \(12\), and forty of size \(24\).

The degree of the common geometric divisor of a pencil has distribution \(0:1029,\ 2:24,\ 3:24,\ 4:21\). No pencil has a common rational point. Thus exactly \(1\,029\) pencils are basepoint-free and define separable degree-five morphisms \(\mathbf P^1\to\mathbf P^1\) with all four rational target fibres unramified. These comprise precisely \(49\) source-coordinate orbits (target-coordinate changes are already quotiented out by the pencil definition).

The multiset of Frobenius factor-degree partitions of the four rational fibres assumes exactly \(15\) types, tabulated in `fiber_profiles.csv`. Precisely \(330\), \(576\), and \(192\) pencils contain respectively zero, one, and two geometrically irreducible degree-five members; none contains three. The compatibility graph on the \(48\) irreducible rational quintic divisors, with an edge for an all-squarefree span, has \(192\) edges, is connected, and has degrees \(7\) on \(24\) vertices and \(9\) on the other \(24\). These two degree classes are exactly the two orbits of irreducible quintics under source-coordinate transformations. There are \(48\) edges internal to the degree-seven class, \(72\) internal to the degree-nine class, and \(72\) across the classes.

## Assumptions and scope
Everything is over \(\mathbf F_3\). Each form is a nonzero homogeneous binary quintic up to multiplication by \(\mathbf F_3^\times\). An all-squarefree pencil means a two-dimensional *linear subspace*, not an ordered basis. Two pencils are equivalent exactly when a rational linear coordinate change of the source projective line carries one to the other; coordinate changes of the target are implicit in the passage from ordered bases to pencils. Base-divisor degree is computed over an algebraic closure with multiplicity and need not measure rational base points. A fibre partition is the multiset of the degrees of its geometrically distinct closed points over \(\mathbf F_3\); all fibres here are reduced.

## Proof
Write an arbitrary quintic as \(F(X,Z)=\sum_{i=0}^{5}c_iX^iZ^{5-i}\). Represent its coefficients by the integer \(q(F)=\sum_{i=0}^{5}c_i3^i\), where \(c_i\in\{0,1,2\}\), normalized so that the first nonzero coefficient is \(1\). There are \((3^6-1)/2=364\) projective forms. Set \(f(x)=F(x,1)\). The divisor is geometrically reduced precisely when \(\deg f\ge4\) and \(\gcd(f,f')=1\) in \(\mathbf F_3[x]\). The degree condition treats the possible point at infinity: its multiplicity is \(5-\deg f\). Exactly \(216\) projective forms satisfy the criterion; this agrees with the standard total of monic squarefree polynomials of degree five and degree four over \(\mathbf F_3\), namely \((3^5-3^4)+(3^4-3^3)=216\).

Every two-dimensional subspace has four projective members, represented for an independent pair \(F,G\) by \(F,G,F+G,F+2G\). Enumerating every pair of distinct squarefree projective forms and testing the other two members produces \(6\,588\) qualifying unordered pairs. Each admissible pencil has exactly six such pairs, so the total is \(6\,588/6=1\,098\). The complete list appears in `pencils.txt`. An independent enumeration through all \(11\,011\) unique reduced-row-echelon \(2\times6\) matrices over \(\mathbf F_3\) obtains exactly the same catalogue, using a separate symbolic gcd implementation.

For a spanning pair \(F,G\), compute its greatest common homogeneous factor by taking \(\gcd(F(x,1),G(x,1))\) and including the point at infinity only when both affine polynomials have degree four. The resulting exact degree frequencies are \(1029,24,24,21\) in degrees \(0,2,3,4\), with none in degrees \(1\) or \(5\). More generally, if a point rational over \(\mathbf F_3\) were common to a pencil, the first derivative at that point would give a scalar linear functional on the two-dimensional pencil and would vanish on a nonzero rational member, contradicting squarefreeness. Hence all nonempty base divisors here have no rational support.

To determine coordinate orbits, substitute all invertible \(2\times2\) matrices over \(\mathbf F_3\) into every form and divide by scalar matrices. This gives all \(24\) elements of \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_3)\). Transform each unordered four-member pencil, normalize its coefficients, and form its orbit. The orbit sizes sum to \(2(3)+2(6)+10(12)+40(24)=1\,098\); the \(54\) canonical representatives, stabilizer-implied sizes and base degrees appear in `orbit_representatives.csv`. Exactly \(49\) representatives have base degree zero.

Factor each of the four squarefree quintics into irreducibles over \(\mathbf F_3\), including an infinity root when its affine degree is four. Sorting the four factor-degree partitions yields the \(15\) profiles and their frequencies in `fiber_profiles.csv`. The irreducible-factor degree partition \((5)\) occurs at most twice per pencil. There are \((3^5-3)/5=48\) monic irreducible quintics. On these \(48\) vertices, adjacency is checked directly against the full verified pencil catalogue. The resulting graph data in `inert_compatibility.json` give exactly \(192\) distinct edges, degree frequencies \(7:24,9:24\), a single connected component, and the within/across degree-class edge totals stated above. Group-orbit enumeration on the \(48\) irreducible forms gives two orbits of size \(24\), matching the degree classes.

## Verification
Run `python verify_pencils.py`, `python verify_rref.py`, `python verify_profiles.py`, and `python verify_inert_graph.py` in the same directory as the included data files. Python's standard library is used for the direct finite-field enumeration; SymPy is required for the independent row-reduced gcd checks and the independent irreducible-divisor calculation. The programs reconstruct the full list of pencils from mathematical predicates, check the canonical projective orbits, and check all \(192\) edges of the inert-fibre compatibility graph. These scripts were executed against the included data files, and all assertions passed. Exact code, not a sample of random equations, determines the finite counts. The full list and all tables permit independent replay or replacement implementations.

## Relationship to prior work
Asgarli and Ghioca, *Smoothness in pencils of hypersurfaces over finite fields* (2022, arXiv:2203.07169v1), study the existence of pencils with every rational member smooth, mainly for hypersurfaces in projective spaces of dimension at least two. Their Section 3 explicitly constructs pencils of quadric surfaces but does not classify degree-five divisors on the projective line. Günay and Lavrauw, *On pencils of cubics on the projective line over finite fields of characteristic greater than three* (2021, arXiv:2104.04756v1), study source-coordinate orbit invariants, but their statements concern degree three and exclude characteristic three. Neither result implies the count or the orbit, fibre, and compatibility-graph tables here. Tang and Fan's 2026 preprint on *exceptional* degree-five rational functions (arXiv:2607.05075v1) concerns a restricted class of covers and its abstract does not establish this all-fibres-unramified census. Earlier unindexed computations or other classifications remain possible.

## Limitations
These are arithmetic classifications over \(\mathbf F_3\), not uniform formulas for other finite fields and not a classification over the algebraic closure. The \(49\) classes are of degree-five morphisms under rational projective changes of source and target, not under arbitrary conjugacy by rational maps. A linearly dependent pair is never counted as a pencil. The proof is a complete finite calculation with independently checked mathematical predicates; no global novelty or independent external validation is asserted. The inaccessible or unexamined portion of the literature remains a publication-priority uncertainty.
