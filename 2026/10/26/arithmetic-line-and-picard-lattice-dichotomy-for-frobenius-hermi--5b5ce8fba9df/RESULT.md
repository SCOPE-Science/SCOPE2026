# Arithmetic line and Picard-lattice dichotomy for Frobenius–Hermitian quartics over \(\mathbb F_3\)

## Finding

Let \(A\in\operatorname{Mat}_{4}(\mathbb F_3)\) be an invertible symmetric matrix. Define the smooth quartic surface
\[
X_A=\{[x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3]\in\mathbb P^3_{\mathbb F_3}:\;x^{(3)T}Ax=0\},
\]
where \(x^{(3)}\) denotes the column of cubed coordinates. Let \(\delta(A)\in\mathbb F_3^\times/(\mathbb F_3^\times)^2\) denote its determinant square class. Every such surface contains exactly \(112\) geometric lines, all defined over \(\mathbb F_9\); their line scheme is finite étale over \(\mathbb F_3\).

There are exactly two projective-isomorphism classes of these surfaces over \(\mathbb F_3\), characterized by the following **complete arithmetic and lattice dichotomy**.

| Invariant | \(\delta(A)=1\) (square) | \(\delta(A)=-1\) (nonsquare) |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Projective model | \(x_0^4+x_1^4+x_2^4+x_3^4=0\) | \(-x_0^4+x_1^4+x_2^4+x_3^4=0\) |
| \(\#X_A(\mathbb F_3)\) | \(16\) | \(10\) |
| \(\mathbb F_3\)-rational lines | \(8\) | \(0\) |
| Frobenius-conjugate pairs of lines | \(52\) | \(56\) |
| Conjugate pairs that meet | \(16\) | \(20\) |
| Conjugate pairs that are skew | \(36\) | \(36\) |
| Arithmetic Picard rank \(\rho(X_A/\mathbb F_3)\) | \(12\) | \(11\) |
| Discriminant of the arithmetic Picard lattice | \(-3072\) | \(6144\) |
| Discriminant group of the arithmetic Picard lattice | \((\mathbb Z/2)^{9}\oplus\mathbb Z/6\) | \((\mathbb Z/2)^{10}\oplus\mathbb Z/6\) |

In both cases, **the full integral arithmetic Picard lattice is generated by the classes of rational lines and by the Galois traces of conjugate line pairs** (the sums of their two line classes). In the nonsquare case, this generation uses only degree-two traces, because there are no rational lines.

In particular, the finite étale scheme of lines is \(\coprod_8\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_3\;\amalg\;\coprod_{52}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_9\) in the square case and \(\coprod_{56}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_9\) in the nonsquare case. The discriminants and discriminant groups above are those of the **integral fixed Picard lattices**, not merely of rational Frobenius eigenspaces.

## Assumptions and scope

All fields are of characteristic \(3\). The coefficient matrix \(A\) is symmetric and invertible over \(\mathbb F_3\). The term *arithmetic Picard lattice* means the integral group \(\operatorname{Pic}(X_A)\), endowed with its intersection pairing. This is the Frobenius-fixed sublattice of the geometric Néron–Severi group: the geometric surface is K3, hence has \(\operatorname{Pic}^0=0\), and \(\operatorname{Br}(\mathbb F_3)=0\). The classification concerns this entire nonsingular symmetric-Frobenius family; it does not assert a classification of arbitrary smooth quartic surfaces.

## Proof

### Reduction to two canonical forms

Direct differentiation gives \(\nabla(x^{(3)T}Ax)=Ax^{(3)}\), which vanishes only at the irrelevant origin because \(A\) is invertible. A smooth quartic in \(\mathbb P^3\) is a K3 surface. Under any \(M\in\operatorname{GL}_4(\mathbb F_3)\), the polynomial changes by congruence \(A\mapsto M^TAM\). The classification of nondegenerate quadratic forms of dimension four over \(\mathbb F_3\) gives precisely two congruence classes: the square-determinant form \(I_4\) and the nonsquare-determinant form \(\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)\). Multiplication of \(A\) by a nonzero scalar does not change its determinant square class in dimension four. The displayed rational-point counts already show that the two classes are not isomorphic as \(\mathbb F_3\)-varieties.

### Geometric lines and their rational incidence

Choose \(i\in\mathbb F_9\) with \(i^2=-1\), and put \(t=1+i\). Since \(t^4=-1\), multiplying the first coordinate of the nonsquare model by \(t\) identifies both canonical models over \(\mathbb F_9\) with \(\sum_j y_j^4=0\), the Hermitian quartic. The latter has \(112\) lines over \(\mathbb F_9\), and all its geometric lines are defined there [1,2]. On a smooth quartic, a line has normal bundle \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-2)\), so its Hilbert-scheme point is reduced. Thus the geometric line scheme consists of exactly \(112\) reduced points.

For \(u,v\in\mathbb F_3^4\), expanding along their projective line gives
\[
(u s+v t)^{(3)T}A(u s+v t)
= s^4 Q_A(u)+s^3t\,u^TAv+s t^3\,v^TAu+t^4 Q_A(v),
\]
where \(Q_A(w)=w^TAw\). Hence an \(\mathbb F_3\)-rational line lies on the quartic exactly when its spanning plane is totally singular for the associated quadratic form. The split four-dimensional form has two reguli, each with four rational lines; the nonsplit form has Witt index one and no rational lines. On \(\mathbb F_3\)-points, the quartic equation reduces to \(Q_A(x)=0\), giving \(16\) and \(10\) points respectively.

At any \(\mathbb F_9\)-point of the Hermitian surface, exactly four of its geometric lines meet. In the split case every \(\mathbb F_3\)-point lies on precisely two rational lines (one of each regulus), so its other two lines form one conjugate pair. There are \(16\) meeting conjugate pairs. In the nonsplit case no line is rational, so the four lines through each of the \(10\) rational points form two meeting pairs, giving \(20\). Conversely, the unique intersection of a line and its Frobenius image is Frobenius-fixed, hence rational. Therefore these counts exhaust all intersecting conjugate pairs, and the remaining \(36\) pairs are skew in either case.

### The integral fixed Picard lattices

By [1], the \(112\) Hermitian lines generate the geometric Néron–Severi lattice \(N\), which has rank \(22\) and discriminant \(-9\). For explicit reproducibility, use \(\mathbb F_9=\mathbb F_3[i]\) and enumerate all \(7462\) two-dimensional row-reduced subspaces of \(\mathbb F_9^4\). Precisely \(112\) satisfy the Hermitian conditions \(h(u,u)=h(v,v)=h(u,v)=0\), where \(h(u,v)=\sum_j u_j^3v_j\). Order these \(112\) planes lexicographically; set
\[
G_{ab}=\begin{cases}-2&a=b,\\1&a\ne b\text{ and the two lines intersect},\\0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]
The first nonzero pivot columns of \(G\), numbered from zero, are
\[
J=(0,1,2,3,4,5,6,8,9,10,16,17,18,20,21,22,24,25,26,32,34,48).
\]
The corresponding \(22\times22\) submatrix has determinant \(-9\), so these line classes are an integral basis of \(N\); the coordinates of all remaining lines in this basis, given by \(G[J,J]^{-1}G[J,:]\), are integral.

In the split case Frobenius acts by \(y\mapsto y^{(3)}\). Under the first-coordinate scaling above, Frobenius on the nonsplit canonical model acts on Hermitian coordinates by \(y\mapsto(i y_0^3,y_1^3,y_2^3,y_3^3)\). Both actions permute the \(112\) lines and induce integer isometries \(P_+,P_-\) of the displayed \(22\)-line basis. Their squares are identity. Compute the integral fixed lattices by obtaining a **primitive integer kernel basis** for \(P_\pm-I\), using unimodular integer row operations on \((P_\pm-I)^T\). Their restricted Gram matrices have ranks \(12,11\), signed determinants \(-3072,+6144\), and Smith diagonal entries respectively
\[
(1,1,\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{9},6)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{10},6).
\]
For the stronger integral-generation statement, form a matrix whose columns are the coordinates of Frobenius-fixed line classes and of pairwise line-class sums. In a primitive basis of the fixed kernel its coordinate matrix is integral, and its column Hermite normal form has determinant \(1\) in both cases. Therefore these line orbit sums generate the **entire** invariant lattice, not merely a finite-index sublattice. The complete finite exact-arithmetic procedure appears in the standalone verifier. Integer, rather than rational, kernels are essential here: rational eigenspaces do not determine the discriminant. Since \(\operatorname{Br}(\mathbb F_3)=0\), Galois descent identifies these saturated fixed lattices with \(\operatorname{Pic}(X_A)\), proving the stated arithmetic rank and discriminant formulas for every invertible symmetric \(A\).

## Relationship to prior work

Kondō and Shimada [1, §3] give the \(112\)-line Hermitian model and a \(22\)-line integral basis of the geometric Néron–Severi lattice. Their §8 computes Frobenius on that lattice for the standard (square-determinant) Fermat model and describes geometric automorphisms. Those facts are used here, not claimed as new. Rams and Schütt [2] establish the sharp \(112\)-line bound and geometric uniqueness over an algebraic closure. Neither source states the **two-class arithmetic classification of all symmetric Frobenius forms over \(\mathbb F_3\)** or the integral nonsquare-form Picard-lattice discriminant and discriminant group. The derived square-case arithmetic invariants are included only to make the classification complete. Because the full geometric automorphism representation is available in [1], some entries could also be deduced through additional calculations from its tables; the specific arithmetic comparison still requires integral descent and an invariant-sublattice computation.

## Limitations

The family is restricted to invertible symmetric coefficient matrices over \(\mathbb F_3\). Its two-class classification does not enumerate every possible form of the underlying supersingular K3 surface. The geometric \(112\)-line count and geometric Picard lattice are prior results. The non-square-form integral lattice invariants were checked by exact symbolic computation, not by a separately published independent audit. A description of the same two arithmetic invariant lattices under alternative Hermitian coordinates could exist in specialized literature; such a description has not been verified as absent.
