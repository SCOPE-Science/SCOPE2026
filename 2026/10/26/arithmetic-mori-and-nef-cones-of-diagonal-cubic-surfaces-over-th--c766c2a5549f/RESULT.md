# Arithmetic Mori and nef cones of diagonal cubic surfaces over the seven-element field

## Finding
Let \(X_{\mathbf a}\subset\mathbb P^3_{\mathbb F_7}\) be defined by \(\sum_{i=0}^3a_i x_i^3=0\), with all coefficients nonzero. Let \(m_i\in\mathbb Z/3\) denote the cube class determined by \(a_i/3^{m_i}\in(\mathbb F_7^\times)^3\). The five coefficient patterns below classify the *diagonal-coordinate forms*: we allow multiplying all coefficients by the same element, multiplying individual variables by nonzero scalars, and permuting variables. We do not claim these are five classes under arbitrary projective isomorphisms.

| Ordered cube-class normal form \((m_0,m_1,m_2,m_3)\) | Number of cube-class vectors (among 81) | Rank \(\operatorname{Pic}(X_{\mathbf a})\) | Rational lines | Geometric-line Galois orbits | Effective extremal rays | Nef extremal rays | \(\#X_{\mathbf a}(\mathbb F_7)\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \((0,0,0,0)\) | 3 | 7 | 27 | 27 | 27 | 99 | 99 |
| \((0,0,0,1)\) | 12 | 1 | 0 | 9 | 1 | 1 | 36 |
| \((0,0,0,2)\) | 12 | 1 | 0 | 9 | 1 | 1 | 36 |
| \((0,0,1,1)\) | 18 | 5 | 9 | 15 | 15 | 21 | 78 |
| \((0,0,1,2)\) | 36 | 3 | 0 | 9 | 6 | 6 | 57 |

The arithmetic effective cone here means the cone of effective numerical divisor classes after \(\mathbb R\)-extension of \(\operatorname{Pic}(X_{\mathbf a})\). Its dual, under the intersection pairing, is the nef cone. In every case the arithmetic effective cone is generated by sums of geometric line classes over Frobenius orbits. For pattern \((0,0,1,2)\), six three-line traces of self-intersection \(-3\) are extremal. The other three traces all equal the hyperplane class \(H\), and the sum of the six negative traces is \(6H\). For pattern \((0,0,1,1)\), all fifteen orbit traces have negative square (nine have square \(-1\), six have square \(-3\)) and each generates an extremal ray.

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is exactly \(\mathbb F_7\); all four coefficients are required to be nonzero. Since \(7\) is not \(3\), these surfaces are smooth. Distinct listed normal forms are distinct for the stated diagonal-coordinate operations, not necessarily for the full projective automorphism relation. No claim is made about integral generators of the invariant lattice or about other finite fields. All rank and cone statements concern divisor classes, not pointwise curve incidence over the base field.

## Proof
Choose \(\zeta=2\in\mathbb F_7\), a primitive cubic root of unity, and write \(a_i=3^{m_i}u_i^3\). After scaling variables, choose cube roots \(b_i\) of \(3^{m_i}\) over \(\mathbb F_{7^3}\), and put \(y_i=b_i x_i\). This identifies the geometric surface with \(\sum_i y_i^3=0\). Frobenius acts on these roots as \(b_i^7=\zeta^{m_i}b_i\). The 27 lines consist of three sets of nine, one for each pairing \(\{(0,1),(2,3)\}\), \(\{(0,2),(1,3)\}\), \(\{(0,3),(1,2)\}\); a line in each set is given by \(y_i+\zeta^u y_j=y_k+\zeta^v y_l=0\), \((u,v)\in(\mathbb Z/3)^2\). For the ordered pairs \((i,j),(k,l)\), Frobenius carries \((u,v)\) to \((u+m_j-m_i,v+m_l-m_k)\). Therefore every Frobenius orbit has length one or three. The normalization of 81 coefficient-class vectors under coordinate permutations and uniform shifts gives exactly the five rows, of respective sizes \(3,12,12,18,36\).

Two distinct lines meet if and only if the four defining linear equations have rank three. Their intersection pairing is \(1\) if they meet, \(0\) otherwise; each geometric line has square \(-1\). The resulting \(27\)-line Gram matrix has rank seven, and the line indices \((12,17,25,10,23,1,5)\), with indices ordered by the pairing and then \(u,v\), have a \(7\times7\) Gram determinant of absolute value one. Since the geometric Picard lattice of a smooth cubic surface is the unimodular rank-seven blow-up lattice, these seven lines are an integral Picard basis. Hence the \(\mathbb Q\)-span of Frobenius-orbit sums is precisely the invariant Picard space, and its rank is exactly the arithmetic Picard rank (the Brauer group of a finite field vanishes).

The geometric effective cone of a smooth cubic surface is generated by its 27 line classes. Averaging any geometric effective class over its Frobenius orbit shows that the invariant cone is generated by the line-orbit sums. The computed squared lengths are \(-1\) for each of 27 split rays; \(3\) for the nine coincident traces in the rank-one cases; \(-1\) for nine and \(-3\) for six traces in the rank-five case; and \(3\) for three and \(-3\) for six in the rank-three case. Distinct orbit sums have nonnegative mutual intersection. A negative-square orbit sum is therefore an extremal ray. In the rank-three case the three square-three traces are equal to \(H\), and their positive dependence \(6H=\sum_{j=1}^{6}D_j\) shows they are not extremal. This proves all effective-ray counts.

For the nef cone, take rational coordinates on the invariant space from an independent set of orbit-sum columns. The dual inequalities are \(\ell(D_j)\ge0\) for all orbit traces \(D_j\). In rank \(r\), every extremal dual ray lies in the intersection of \(r-1\) independent active hyperplanes. Exhaustively enumerate all such subsets, use exact integer signed minors for their null vectors, test every inequality, primitive-normalize, and deduplicate. The resulting ray counts are exactly \(99,1,1,21,6\), respectively; in rank seven the exhaustive search examines \(\binom{27}{6}=296010\) subsets. Both source and independent replay instructions are included in the verification artifacts.

Finally, Frobenius has order dividing three on geometric divisor classes. For invariant dimension \(r\) in a seven-dimensional space, its trace is \((3r-7)/2\). The rational-surface Lefschetz formula gives \(\#X(\mathbb F_7)=1+49+7(3r-7)/2\), yielding \(99,36,36,78,57\). Direct enumeration of all \(\mathbb F_7\)-projective coordinate points independently checks these five counts.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_diagonal_cubic.py` in a directory containing `dual_cone_exact.cpp`. Python 3, SymPy, and a C++17 compiler are required. The program rebuilds the 27 line equations and intersection matrix over \(\mathbb F_7\), checks the integral unimodular geometric Picard basis, exhausts all \(81\) cube-class vectors, computes Frobenius orbits, ranks and orbit-trace squares, verifies the positive decomposition in the rank-three case, compiles an exact-integer dual-cone enumerator, and checks all stated dual-ray counts. It also independently enumerates projective points over \(\mathbb F_7\). Its successful terminal line begins `VERIFY_OK:`.

## Relationship to prior work
The classical 27-line theorem and the standard effective-cone description of a smooth cubic surface are inputs, not contributions. Gouvêa and Yui (1994) give general twisting and Picard-number formulas for diagonal hypersurfaces, so the twist dependence of the arithmetic Picard rank is not claimed to be new in isolation. Banwait, Fité, and Loughran (2016) tabulate cubic-surface Frobenius conjugacy classes, traces and line-orbit types; they do not, in the inspected portions of their work, provide this coefficientwise diagonal family and its effective/nef ray counts. The main contribution asserted here is the complete coefficient-resolved arithmetic Mori/nef cone table and the explicit degeneration of orbit sums, not a new Weyl-group classification. These prior results supply substantial ingredients: the result should not be represented as independent of them.

## Limitations
The five normal forms are for diagonal-coordinate equivalence, and may overcount under unrestricted isomorphisms. The study determines real rational polyhedral cones and Picard ranks; it does not compute the full integral arithmetic Picard lattice or its Brauer group. The exact extreme-ray census is specific to the 27-line cubic-surface setting, and does not assert originality of the general technique. The existing full Frobenius conjugacy-class classification creates a residual priority risk: an equivalent coefficientwise cone table may exist in another presentation. No independent scientific audit has been performed.
