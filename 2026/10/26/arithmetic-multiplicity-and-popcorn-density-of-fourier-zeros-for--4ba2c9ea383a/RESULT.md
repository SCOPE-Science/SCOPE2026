# Arithmetic multiplicity and popcorn density of Fourier zeros for symmetric triadic digit measures

## Finding
For \(0<w<1\), let \(D_1,D_2,\ldots\) be independent random variables taking values \(0,1,2\) with probabilities \(((1-w)/2,w,(1-w)/2)\), and let \(\mu_w\) be the distribution of \(\sum_{n\ge1}D_n3^{-n}\). Define the entire Fourier transform and its real zero set by
\[
 F_w(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-2\pi itx}\,d\mu_w(x),\qquad Z_w=\{t\in\mathbb R:F_w(t)=0\}.
\]
For an integer \(m\ge1\), let \(N_{w,m}(R)\) count the **distinct** zeros in \([-R,R]\) having analytic multiplicity exactly \(m\), and let \(N_w(R)=\sum_{m\ge1}N_{w,m}(R)\). The following classification holds.

1. If \(w>1/2\), then \(Z_w=\varnothing\).
2. If \(w=1/2\), every zero has even analytic multiplicity, and for each fixed \(m\ge1\),
\[
 N_{w,2m}(R)=2R\frac{2}{3^{m+1}}+O_m(1),\qquad N_{w,2m-1}(R)=0,
\]
with \(N_w(R)=2R/3+O(1)\).
3. If \(0<w<1/2\), set
\[
 \alpha(w)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\arccos\!\left(-\frac{w}{1-w}\right)\in(1/4,1/2).
\]
If \(\alpha(w)\) is irrational, or if its lowest-term denominator is divisible by \(3\), all real zeros are simple and \(N_w(R)=2R+O_w(\log(2+R))\).
4. In the remaining case, write \(\alpha(w)=p/q\) in lowest terms, \(3\nmid q\), and let \(L=L(q)\) be the **least** positive integer such that \(3^L\equiv1\) or \(-1\pmod q\). Then, for every fixed \(m\ge1\),
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle N_{w,m}(R)=2R(1-3^{-L})^2\,3^{-(m-1)L}+O_{w,m}(1).}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle \lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{N_w(R)}{2R}=1-3^{-L}.}
\]
For every \(0<w\le1/2\), irrespective of resonances, the multiplicity-weighted count obeys
\[
 \sum_{t\in Z_w\cap[-R,R]}\operatorname{ord}_{t}F_w=2R+O_w(\log(2+R)).
\]

Consequently the distinct-zero density \(\delta(w)=\lim_{R\to\infty}N_w(R)/(2R)\) exists for all \(0<w<1\). On \((0,1/2)\) it is \(1\) except at the countable dense collection where \(\alpha(w)=p/q\), \(3\nmid q\); there it drops by exactly \(3^{-L(q)}\). These are exactly its discontinuities within \((0,1/2)\). At the boundary, \(\delta(1/2)=1/3\), \(\lim_{w\uparrow1/2}\delta(w)=1\), and \(\delta(w)=0\) for \(w>1/2\).

## Assumptions and scope
The distribution is a positive, compactly supported probability measure on \([0,1]\), with all three digit weights strictly positive. Frequencies are real, with Fourier normalization \(e^{-2\pi itx}\). Analytic multiplicity means the order of a zero of the entire function \(F_w\), not the multiplicity of a frequency in a union representation. The asymptotics are for \(R\to\infty\), holding pointwise for each fixed parameter and each fixed multiplicity. No uniform error estimate in \(w\) is claimed.

## Proof
**(1) Product and its zeros.** Independence gives
\[
 F_w(t)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}M_w(t/3^n),\qquad
 M_w(u)=\frac{1-w}{2}(1+e^{-4\pi iu})+we^{-2\pi iu}
 =e^{-2\pi iu}\bigl[w+(1-w)\cos(2\pi u)\bigr].
\]
For each fixed complex \(t\), the tail differences \(M_w(t/3^n)-1\) form an absolutely summable sequence locally uniformly on compact sets. Hence the product is entire and has a zero precisely when one of its finitely many relevant factors vanishes; the vanishing orders add. If \(w>1/2\), \(w+(1-w)\cos(2\pi u)>0\) for real \(u\), proving nonvanishing. For \(0<w<1/2\), the factor has two simple real-root classes \(u\equiv\pm\alpha(w)\pmod{\mathbb Z}\), where \(\sin(2\pi\alpha(w))\ne0\). Therefore
\[
 Z_w=\bigcup_{n\ge1}S_n,\qquad
 S_n=3^n(\mathbb Z+\alpha)\ \cup\ 3^n(\mathbb Z-\alpha).
\]
Each \(S_n\) has two disjoint arithmetic progressions, each of spacing \(3^n\). At \(w=1/2\), instead \(M_w(u)=e^{-2\pi iu}\cos^2(\pi u)\): the single class \(u\equiv1/2\pmod{\mathbb Z}\) has a zero of exact order two. Here put \(S_n=3^n(\mathbb Z+1/2)\).

**(2) Exact collisions.** For \(0<w<1/2\), suppose \(S_n\cap S_{n+h}\ne\varnothing\) with \(h\ge1\). Substituting the two signed progressions shows that for signs \(\sigma,\tau\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
 (3^h\sigma-\tau)\alpha\in\mathbb Z.
\]
For irrational \(\alpha\) this is impossible. If \(\alpha=p/q\) is reduced and \(3\mid q\), it is also impossible because \(3^h\sigma-\tau\) is nonzero modulo \(3\). Thus all levels are pairwise disjoint in either of these two cases. If \(3\nmid q\), the condition is exactly \(3^h\equiv\pm1\pmod q\). The set of such positive exponents is \(L\mathbb N\), where \(L\) is the least one: indeed the exponents for which the cyclic group generated by \(3\) enters its subgroup \(\{1,-1\}\) are precisely the multiples of the least such exponent. Further, \(S_{n+L}\subseteq S_n\), since multiplication by \(3^L\) sends either signed residue class modulo \(q\) to one of the same two classes. Hence the \(L\) chains
\[
 S_r\supseteq S_{r+L}\supseteq S_{r+2L}\supseteq\cdots,
 \qquad 1\le r\le L,
\]
are disjoint from one another, and there are no other coincidences. A zero has analytic multiplicity \(m\) exactly when it belongs to
\[
 \bigcup_{r=1}^L\bigl(S_{r+(m-1)L}\setminus S_{r+mL}\bigr).
\]
At \(w=1/2\), the chains reduce to \(S_1\supseteq S_2\supseteq\cdots\), because \(3(\mathbb Z+1/2)\subseteq\mathbb Z+1/2\); its multiplicity is twice the number of containing levels.

**(3) Counting.** Each signed progression in \(S_n\) has \(2R/3^n+O(1)\) points in \([-R,R]\). Consequently \(\#(S_n\cap[-R,R])=4R/3^n+O(1)\) for \(0<w<1/2\), while at the boundary it equals \(2R/3^n+O(1)\). In the pairwise-disjoint case, there are no points of \(S_n\) in \([-R,R]\) once \(3^n\min\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}>R\). Summing the finite geometric series and its \(O(\log R)\) counting errors gives \(N_w(R)=2R+O_w(\log(2+R))\); all these roots are simple.

In the rational coprime case, the chain difference at depth \(m\) has density \(2(3^{-r-(m-1)L}-3^{-r-mL})\). Summing this over \(r=1,\dots,L\) gives
\[
 2\sum_{r=1}^L\bigl(3^{-r-(m-1)L}-3^{-r-mL}\bigr)
 =(1-3^{-L})^2 3^{-(m-1)L}.
\]
Each difference is a difference of two nested finite unions of arithmetic progressions, hence incurs \(O_{w,m}(1)\) counting error. The union of its \(L\) top-level chains gives total distinct-zero density \(2\sum_{r=1}^L3^{-r}=1-3^{-L}\). At \(w=1/2\), zeros of analytic order \(2m\) occur in \(S_m\setminus S_{m+1}\), which has density \(3^{-m}-3^{-(m+1)}=2/3^{m+1}\), and the distinct-zero density is \(1/3\).

The weighted counting formula follows directly by summing the number of zeros of each mask factor with their mask multiplicities. In all cases \(0<w\le1/2\), each scale contributes asymptotic weighted density \(2/3^n\), and truncation at logarithmic depth incurs \(O_w(\log(2+R))\); \(\sum_{n\ge1}2/3^n=1\).

**(4) Continuity.** The map \(w\mapsto\alpha(w)\) is a homeomorphism from \((0,1/2)\) onto \((1/4,1/2)\), reversing orientation. Resonances occur exactly at reduced rational \(p/q\) with \(3\nmid q\), a countable dense set. At such a point the neighboring irrational parameters have density \(1\), while the value is \(1-3^{-L(q)}\). At any nonresonant point, rational resonances approaching it must have denominators \(q\to\infty\). Since \(q\mid3^{L(q)}\pm1\), necessarily \(3^{-L(q)}\le1/(q-1)\to0\). Thus the density is continuous at every nonresonant point. The claimed endpoint limits follow from the same denominator argument as \(\alpha\uparrow1/2\), and from the separate critical calculation.

The standalone checker `checker.py` performs exact rational enumeration of the mask-root progressions and their analytic multiplicities for \(\alpha=2/5,2/7,3/8,1/3,1/2\), tests the modular minimality of \(L\), and compares observed count densities at three radii with the formulas. Reproduce with `python3 checker.py`. The recorded execution output is `checker_output.txt`. These finite checks do not certify the infinite limiting statements; the proof above supplies those.

## Relationship to prior work
Classical harmonic analysis of self-similar and Bernoulli measures uses a Fourier mask infinite product and its induced orthogonality-zero set. The 2009 preprint by Jorgensen, Kornelson and Shuman explicitly presents the two-digit Bernoulli product and its zeros; the 2014 work of Dutkay and Haussermann studies arithmetic orders for Fourier bases of a different Cantor measure. Su, Wu and Chen (2022), Section 2, writes the general unweighted-mask zero-union identity and then treats spectrality of product-form digit sets. Those identities and the orthogonality motivation are prior art. The claim here instead gives the complete *analytic multiplicity distribution*, the weight-dependent rational resonance law, and the exact continuity/discontinuity locus for three *unequally weighted* digits. The generic mask-product mechanism alone does not state these quantitative count laws.

## Limitations
The family has a single free probability weight and the fixed triadic contraction and digits; this is not a general classification of weighted IFS measures or Fourier orthonormal bases. All density limits count real zeros of an entire Fourier transform, not zeros throughout the complex plane. The error constants are not uniform as the weight approaches a resonance or the threshold. Publication priority must still be checked against older general treatments of self-similar Fourier zeros, including R. S. Strichartz, *Self-Similar Measures and Their Fourier Transforms. III* (1993), for which full text was not obtained in this comparison. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
