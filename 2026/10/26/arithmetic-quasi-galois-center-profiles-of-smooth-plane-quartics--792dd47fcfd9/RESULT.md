# Arithmetic quasi-Galois center profiles of smooth plane quartics over the binary field

## Finding
For a smooth plane quartic \(C/\mathbf F_2\) and a rational projective-plane point \(P\), define \(G_C(P)\) as the subgroup of \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbf F_2}(C)\) inducing the identity on the function-field subextension associated with projection from \(P\). Equivalently, every member preserves **each fiber** of that projection, rather than merely permuting the lines through \(P\). Call \(P\) an arithmetic quasi-Galois center if \(|G_C(P)|>1\). Write \(n_2(C)\) and \(n_4(C)\) for the number of rational centers with groups of sizes \(2\) and \(4\), respectively.

**Theorem.** Exactly 17 of the 78 smooth genus-three nonhyperelliptic curve classes over \(\mathbf F_2\) have at least one rational arithmetic quasi-Galois center. Their complete distribution is:

| \((n_2,n_4)\) | Isomorphism classes | Coordinate-fixed smooth quartic equations |
|---|---:|---:|
| \((0,0)\) | 61 | 9,736 |
| \((1,0)\) | 14 | 1,134 |
| \((3,0)\) | 1 | 28 |
| \((2,1)\) | 1 | 21 |
| \((0,7)\) | 1 | 1 |
| **Total** | **78** | **10,920** |

Every nontrivial fiber-preserving group is isomorphic to \(C_2\) or \(C_2\times C_2\), and every such rational center is outer (does not belong to the curve). The two exceptional profiles with \(n_4>0\) are the only ones containing rational degree-four Galois projection centers, with one and seven such centers respectively. The seven-center case is an existing characteristic-two Klein-quartic model and is not claimed as a previously unknown Galois-point example.

Among the 17 classes, the finer distribution by \((n_2,n_4,\#C(\mathbf F_2))\) is:

| Profile | Number of classes |
|---|---:|
| (0,7,0) | 1 |
| (1,0,0) | 1 |
| (1,0,1) | 2 |
| (1,0,2) | 4 |
| (1,0,3) | 3 |
| (1,0,4) | 2 |
| (1,0,5) | 2 |
| (2,1,4) | 1 |
| (3,0,3) | 1 |

## Assumptions and scope
Base field is \(\mathbf F_2\); all source curves are geometrically smooth plane quartics, hence canonically embedded nonhyperelliptic genus-three curves. Arithmetic projective equivalence means under \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_2)\) (order \(168\)); since a smooth plane quartic is its canonical model, these are precisely arithmetic isomorphism classes. There are \(15\) degree-four monomials and \(2^{15}-1=32,767\) nonzero quartic coefficient masks (scaling is trivial over \(\mathbf F_2\)). The center is specifically \(\mathbf F_2\)-rational and the fiber-preserving group is specifically defined over \(\mathbf F_2\), not after extension to the algebraic closure.

## Proof
**Projective elation lemma.** Every \(\mathbf F_2\)-automorphism of a smooth plane quartic is induced by a rational projective-linear transformation of its canonical embedding. Put the center at \((1:0:0)\). If that transformation fixes the projection \((Y:Z)\) as a function, its matrix, after projective scaling, induces the identity on the quotient \(\langle Y,Z\rangle\), and consequently has the form \((X,Y,Z)\mapsto(X+bY+cZ,Y,Z)\) for \(b,c\in\mathbf F_2\). The four matrices form \(C_2^2\), and any nonidentity subgroup has order \(2\) or \(4\). For a nontrivial involution, after changing the two base coordinates one can take \(X\mapsto X+Y\). Its invariant homogeneous quartic is a sum \(a(X^2+XY)^2+(X^2+XY)Q_2(Y,Z)+Q_4(Y,Z)\). If the center lies on the quartic, then \(a=0\) and all three first derivatives vanish at that center, contradicting smoothness. So every rational arithmetic quasi-Galois center is outer. For an outer center, projection has degree \(4\), so it is Galois exactly when its full deck group of order \(4\) occurs.

**Complete coefficient-space classification.** Enumerate the \(2^{15}-1\) masks and \(168\) invertible \(3\)-by-\(3\) binary matrices. This gives exactly \(279\) quartic-equation orbits. At each orbit representative, dehomogenize in the three standard affine charts and compute a Gröbner basis for the ideal generated by the chart equation and its two first partial derivatives. Over the algebraic closure, the chart has no singular point if and only if this ideal is the unit ideal, by the weak Nullstellensatz. Thus three unit-ideal tests characterize geometric smoothness. Exactly \(78\) orbits, containing \(10,920\) masks, are smooth.

**Fiberwise groups.** For each smooth representative, compute the binary projective stabilizer and intersect it with the \(21\) nonidentity elations \(I+P\otimes\ell\), where \(P\) ranges over the seven rational projective points and \(\ell(P)=0\) ranges over the three nonzero linear functionals per center. The intersection over each center has either zero, one or three nonidentity elements, hence group size \(1,2,4\). The resulting orbit counts are exactly the theorem's distribution.

**Different enumeration.** Independently enumerate the union of fixed coefficient subspaces of the \(21\) elations: \(8,415\) nonzero forms. The projective elation lemma excludes candidates passing through the invariant center(s); this reduces to \(4,054\) possible masks. Independently using lexicographic Gröbner bases, exactly \(1,184\) of these forms are smooth; their orbits number \(17\), with the same refined size/group/rational-point profile as above. This second approach does not assume the initial full smooth-quartic orbit census.

## Verification
Two separately implemented Python 3 programs reproduce the claims, using exact \(\mathbf F_2\) polynomial arithmetic provided by SymPy (tested with SymPy 1.14.0): `compute.py` begins from all projective quartic equations and partitions them; `verify_independent.py` begins with the union of invariant subspaces of all projective elations. Running each from the same directory writes `quasi_census.json` and `independent.json`. The embedded output files contain, respectively, the 78 smooth orbit records and the 17 nontrivial-center orbit records (with monomial orders specified by code). A direct cross-program profile comparison and both programme-level assertions passed. The counts use exhaustive finite searches, not random sampling.

## Relationship to prior work
Nart–Ritzenthaler [1] already establish the general arithmetic classification and automorphism descriptions of binary smooth plane quartics; therefore the 78-class genus-three census is **not** new. Fukasawa–Miura–Takahashi [2] introduce quasi-Galois points in arbitrary characteristic, while their later arrangement results [3] explicitly assume characteristic zero and hence do not give the present arithmetic wild-characteristic profile. Fukasawa [4] determines geometric Galois-point classifications, including exceptional Klein phenomena; neither the existence of the Klein model nor its seven Galois centers is claimed here as novel. The distinguished result is instead the complete **joint arithmetic distribution of partial fiber-preserving groups at all seven rational centers**, including the precise class/equation frequencies. It requires identifying how rational projective elations sit inside each stabilizer, which is information not stated in the cited prior enumeration tables. This is a computable refinement of the known underlying curve classification, not a new genus-three moduli classification.

## Limitations
The result is only for \(\mathbf F_2\) and rational centers defined over that field, not geometric quasi-Galois centers or larger finite fields. The canonical-model and Gröbner-basis arguments justify the finite exhaustive computation, but no separate proof-free closed formula uniform in field size is claimed. Because [1] supplies extensive normal forms and automorphism groups, the new profile may be implicitly derivable from older tables; explicit priority for this joint derived invariant is not independently established. Complete unrelated classifications or unindexed numerical tables could have reported it already. Computational verification and the self-review do not constitute independent external validation.
