# Review of the Bernoulli Fourier-zero multiplicity census

## Correctness

**PASS.** The precise hypotheses are \(0<\lambda<1\), unbiased independent signs, the index-zero product normalization, and positive real Fourier zeros counted by analytic order. Entire compact-uniform convergence and nonvanishing of the cosine tail justify counting vanishing factors, rather than inferring zeros from an unproved infinite-product heuristic. Coincidence requires an odd-over-odd rational power; its minimal exponent partitions the factor levels into disjoint arithmetic chains. Within a chain, coprimality gives the unique primitive representation and order \(1+v_b(u)\). Exact periodic counting then gives every asserted coefficient and the logarithmic remainder. The sharp first-occurrence bound follows from the primitive lower bound \(u\ge b^{m-1}\). Exact-arithmetic finite checks independently reproduce the formulas for six rational and two irrational-algebraic parameter values. No finite test was used as an infinite proof.

## Originality

**PASS, with a close-source risk.** Jorgensen, Kornelson and Shuman (2007), Lemmas 2.1–2.2 and Section 4, give the cosine product, show zeros arise only from factors, and classify parity phenomena governing orthogonal exponential families for rational parameters. Their 2009 preprint, equations (6)–(8), explicitly gives the set of factor-zero locations; Section 4 treats orthogonal systems. These are strong antecedents. The new theorem does not claim their product or zero-set formula as novel: it establishes a complete *analytic multiplicity* dichotomy for every real parameter, the resonant algebraic-power criterion, the exact least location of each multiplicity, and geometric multiplicity-by-multiplicity zero densities. The inspected sources do not present these quantitative invariants. Their product identities make the present proof possible, so an earlier unindexed arithmetic elaboration remains a concrete originality risk.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The Fourier zero set governs orthogonality of exponentials in \(L^2(\mu_\lambda)\), while the order of each zero controls the rate at which that orthogonality disappears under a frequency perturbation. The theorem supplies the complete distribution of these orders, including a sharp first-occurrence scale, for a canonical one-parameter family of singular and overlapping self-similar measures. It identifies a countable dense resonance set where arbitrary orders occur and a complementary regime of simple zeros, while the total count with multiplicity is unchanged. This classification is not merely a restatement of the zero set because that set does not record analytic orders or their densities.

## Closest literature and limitations

Primary antecedents: Jorgensen–Kornelson–Shuman, *Orthogonal Exponentials for Bernoulli Iterated Function Systems*, arXiv:math/0703385 (2007), especially Section 4; and *Families of Spectral Sets for Bernoulli Convolutions*, arXiv:0911.2435 (2009), especially equations (6)–(8). The published product and location formulas are enough to suggest the question, and remain a meaningful derivability/priority risk. No claim is made about parameter-uniform asymptotics, weighted unequal-sign measures, or quantitative recovery from noisy Fourier data. Searches cannot certify global publication priority. There has been no independent verification by another reviewer.
