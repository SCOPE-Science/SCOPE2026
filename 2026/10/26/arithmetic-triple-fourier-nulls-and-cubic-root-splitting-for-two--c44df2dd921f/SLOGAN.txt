For the equally weighted probability measures on two concentric spheres in \(\mathbb R^3\), every positive real Fourier null is simple except for exact arithmetic families of triple nulls: a reduced radius ratio \(p/q\) produces them precisely when \(p+q\) is odd, at frequencies \((2j+1)q\pi\). Perturbing the radius ratio splits each triple null into one real and two nonreal zeros at the sharp cube-root scale \(|\delta|^{1/3}\).
