# Automorphism schemes of split square-zero local algebras

## Finding

For every field \(k\) and every \(r\)-dimensional \(k\)-vector space \(V\) with \(r\ge1\), the automorphism group scheme of \(A=k\oplus V\) with multiplication \((c,v)(d,w)=(cd,cw+dv)\) is \(\mathrm{GL}(V)\) when \(r\ge2\), is \(\mathbb G_m\) when \(r=1\) and \(\operatorname{char}k\ne2\), and when \(r=1\) and \(\operatorname{char}k=2\) is \(\operatorname{Spec}k[a,b,b^{-1}]/(a^2)\), parametrizing \(\varepsilon\mapsto a+b\varepsilon\); in the last case its reduced subgroup is \(\mathbb G_m\) but its tangent space at the identity has dimension \(2\), so it is non-smooth.

## Assumptions and scope

Let \(k\) be any field, let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional \(k\)-vector space of dimension \(r\ge1\), and define the unital commutative algebra \(A=k\oplus V\) by
\[
(c,v)(d,w)=(cd,cw+dv).
\]
Thus \(V\) is the unique maximal ideal and \(V^2=0\). The automorphism group scheme is considered functorially: for each commutative \(k\)-algebra \(R\), its \(R\)-points are the \(R\)-algebra automorphisms of \(A\otimes_k R\). This functorial scope is essential because field-valued points alone do not detect nilpotent parameters.

## Proof

For an arbitrary commutative \(k\)-algebra \(R\), write \(V_R=V\otimes_k R\). Any unital \(R\)-algebra automorphism \(\phi\) fixes \(1\) and has a unique form
\[
\phi(v)=\alpha(v)1+T(v),\qquad \alpha\in\operatorname{Hom}_R(V_R,R),\quad T\in\operatorname{End}_R(V_R),
\]
where invertibility of \(\phi\) is equivalent to invertibility of \(T\). Since \(vw=0\) for all \(v,w\in V_R\), multiplicativity is equivalent to
\[
\alpha(v)\alpha(w)=0,\qquad \alpha(v)T(w)+\alpha(w)T(v)=0
\]
for every \(v,w\in V_R\).

Set \(\beta=\alpha\circ T^{-1}\). Replacing \(v,w\) by their inverse images under \(T\) gives
\[
\beta(u)\beta(z)=0,\qquad \beta(u)z+\beta(z)u=0
\]
for every \(u,z\in V_R\).

If \(r\ge2\), choose any basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_r\) of \(V_R\). For each pair \(i\ne j\), the second equation gives \(\beta(e_i)e_j+\beta(e_j)e_i=0\). Linear independence of the basis over \(R\) forces both coefficients to vanish. Every index has a distinct partner, so \(\beta=0\), hence \(\alpha=0\). Conversely every invertible \(T\) with \(\alpha=0\) defines an automorphism. The automorphism functor is therefore \(\mathrm{GL}(V)\), scheme-theoretically, for every field when \(r\ge2\).

If \(r=1\), choose a basis \(\varepsilon\) of \(V\). An automorphism is determined by \(\phi(\varepsilon)=a+b\varepsilon\), with \(b\in R^\times\). The relations above reduce to \(a^2=0\) and \(2a=0\). If \(\operatorname{char}k\ne2\), these force \(a=0\), giving \(\mathbb G_m\). If \(\operatorname{char}k=2\), the second equation is automatic and the sole equation is \(a^2=0\), giving the affine scheme \(\operatorname{Spec}k[a,b,b^{-1}]/(a^2)\).

In characteristic two the nilradical is generated by \(a\), so the reduced subgroup is \(a=0\), isomorphic to \(\mathbb G_m\) and of dimension one. At the identity \((a,b)=(0,1)\), the cotangent maximal ideal modulo its square has independent classes represented by \(a\) and \(b-1\); the tangent dimension is therefore two. The scheme is not smooth. This establishes all cases.

## Verification

The proof works over arbitrary commutative base algebras \(R\), not merely over fields. In the \(r\ge2\) case, the coefficient comparison uses a free-module basis and does not assume that \(R\) is reduced. In the one-dimensional case, the equations \(a^2=0\) and \(2a=0\) are retained over arbitrary \(R\), which is precisely what detects the characteristic-two infinitesimal direction. No finite enumeration is used as a substitute for the proof.

As a consistency check, for every field-valued point the nilpotent scalar \(a\) must vanish, so the ordinary automorphism group is \(\mathrm{GL}(V)(k)\) in all cases. The non-smooth distinction is scheme-theoretic and appears only on nonreduced base rings.

## Relationship to prior work

Díaz, Liendo, Manzano-Flores, and Regeta study automorphism groups of zero-dimensional monomial algebras over fields of characteristic zero, including ordinary automorphism groups in a setting containing \(k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\). Their scope is characteristic zero and the ordinary algebraic group; it does not determine the nonreduced automorphism scheme in characteristic two. Cabrera Casado and coauthors compute automorphism group schemes for two-dimensional evolution algebras and connect them to Hopf algebras. Those algebras are nonassociative evolution algebras, rather than the associative square-zero local algebras treated here. The present statement is a scheme-level classification of the latter family, with an exact characteristic-and-dimension boundary.

## Limitations

The result concerns only split algebras with square-zero maximal ideal. It does not classify automorphism schemes of nonsplit local algebras, algebras with nonzero higher radical powers, or noncommutative algebras. The literature comparison is bounded and does not prove universal novelty; the elementary one-dimensional case may have appeared implicitly in broader treatments of group schemes.
