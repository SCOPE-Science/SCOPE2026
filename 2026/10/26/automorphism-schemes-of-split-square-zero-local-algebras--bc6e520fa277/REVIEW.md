# Review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The proof derives the functor of automorphisms over an arbitrary commutative base algebra, reduces multiplicativity to two equations, and handles the cases \(r\ge2\), \(r=1\) with characteristic different from two, and \(r=1\) in characteristic two separately. The characteristic-two coordinate ring has a nonzero nilpotent parameter, and its tangent dimension is two while its reduced dimension is one.

## Originality

**PASS, best-knowledge and bounded-search scope.** The closest exact ordinary-group result found concerns automorphisms of truncated coordinate-cross/zero-dimensional monomial algebras, with characteristic-zero assumptions; it does not classify the automorphism scheme on nonreduced bases in characteristic two. A 2024 paper computes automorphism group schemes for two-dimensional evolution algebras, but those are a different, nonassociative family. Semantic searches for dual numbers, square-zero local algebras, automorphism schemes, and characteristic-two infinitesimals found related results but no exact statement of this full arbitrary-characteristic classification. The elementary one-dimensional case may be implicit in broader literature, so universal novelty is not asserted.

## Value

**PASS.** The result gives a complete and sharp smoothness boundary for a natural family of local algebras. It explains why the ordinary field-valued automorphism group can be \(\mathrm{GL}(V)(k)\) in every case while the representing scheme still has an infinitesimal direction in the smallest characteristic-two case. This is a useful benchmark for scheme-theoretic automorphism and deformation calculations, rather than a numerical recomputation.

## Closest literature and limitations

- Díaz, Liendo, Manzano-Flores, and Regeta, arXiv:2408.02197: automorphism groups of zero-dimensional monomial algebras in characteristic zero; ordinary algebraic-group scope.
- Cabrera Casado et al., arXiv:2407.15769: automorphism group schemes of two-dimensional evolution algebras; different algebra class.
- The proof covers only split square-zero local algebras and does not extend to higher radical powers without new arguments.
