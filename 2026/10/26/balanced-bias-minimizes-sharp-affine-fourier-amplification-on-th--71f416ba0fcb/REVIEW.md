# Scientific assessment: sharp affine Fourier endpoint projection on a biased Boolean cube

## Correctness — PASS
The independent-coordinate orthonormal basis gives an explicit real symmetric kernel. The induced endpoint norm is the maximum absolute row mass and is sharp using kernel signs; symmetry proves equality of the dual endpoint norms. Convex-order comparison between centered two-point coordinate contributions locates a maximizing corner. A strict conditional Jensen argument at a single coordinate (with continuity at exceptional parameter values) proves that balanced bias uniquely minimizes the norm. Binomial summation gives the closed expression and even–odd equality. The CLT with second-moment uniform integrability, and binomial-to-Poisson convergence, give two distinct limiting regimes. A checked exact-rational script enumerated all rows in dimensions 2–7 at seven biases and passed 1,866 assertions. It does not replace the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence
Nayar (arXiv:1311.3179v1, pp. 1–3 and later proofs) and O'Donnell (*Analysis of Boolean Functions*, 2014, §8.4) give the biased Fourier framework; inspected material does not contain the sharp endpoint projection norm or its global bias optimizer. Published results on second-homogeneous Walsh projection, higher-alphabet Hamming projections and Lipschitz-free Hamming spaces concern different projection operators and do not imply the bias-minimum theorem. A separate affine projector on a fixed-cardinality slice is on a different measure space. Searches under Boolean Fourier, least-squares and binomial-deviation aliases identified no exact claim but cannot prove historical uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
Bias is an intrinsic model parameter of least-squares first-order approximation on product Bernoulli spaces. The theorem identifies the exact worst-case bounded-input distortion, the unique optimally stable bias, a nonobvious even–odd parity coincidence, and an informative Gaussian-to-Poisson change of scale. It is a complete sharp classification across dimensions and biases, not an arbitrary numerical instance.

## Literature and limits
Nayar's biased-cube FKN theorem and O'Donnell's standard Fourier treatment are background, not claimed as new. The theorem does not cover heterogeneous biases or higher degrees. A possible unindexed equivalent in older approximation theory remains a historical-priority risk. Finite checks are not an all-dimensional certificate. Independent auditing and bibliographic validation have not occurred.
