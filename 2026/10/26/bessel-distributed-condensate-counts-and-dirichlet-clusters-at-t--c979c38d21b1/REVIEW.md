# Review: critical fast-rate condensate statistics

## Correctness — PASS
For any fixed integer \(A\ge2\), positive bulk weights with \(u_0=1\), excess density \(e=\rho-\mu>0\), and \(\theta_L/L^2\to\Theta\in(0,\infty)\), the proof partitions the exact canonical normalization according to the number \(K_L\) of occupation-at-least-\(A\) sites. Its finite coefficient identity is proved by choosing sites and counting weak compositions. Fixed-count asymptotics give the coefficient \(b^{k-1}/[k!(k-1)!]\), and factorial domination justifies summing over all counts; a bounded-support large-deviation bound eliminates zero clusters. Reweighting the background by at most a fixed-degree polynomial preserves concentration, and uniform weak compositions converge to the uniform Dirichlet simplex. This proves the count and conditional-mass joint limit and its exact normalization. The independently implemented finite exact-sum and state-enumeration calculations agree, and three finite-size examples approach the law. These numerical checks are ancillary, not infinite-size proofs.

## Originality — PASS, subject to identifiable residual risk
Jatuviriyapornchai–Grosskinsky (2024), arXiv:2404.02590, gives the same stationary weights and proves the subquadratic independent-cluster and superquadratic single-cluster regimes; Section 3.2.3 and Section 4 specifically exclude and identify the quadratic border as difficult. The 2026 generalization by Blank and collaborators, arXiv:2603.00806, Theorems 2–3, likewise explicitly excludes its transition line \(\gamma=\kappa+2\), whose constant-tail case is \(\kappa=0\). Earlier inclusion-process Poisson–Dirichlet limits concern a different long-occupation tail (approximately reciprocal in occupancy), not the exact constant-tail Bessel–Dirichlet count-and-mass law proved here. Comparison of these actual implications and searches for the Bessel count law, quadratic fast-rate threshold, and uniform-simplex mass partition did not identify covering prior work. The original 2024 paper already gives the general cluster-composition decomposition; the new contribution is the rigorous *critical-scale evaluation* and complete joint limiting law, not that decomposition. Related or unindexed equivalent calculations remain a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The exact borderline \(\theta_L\asymp L^2\) is singled out by the source as the meeting point of infinitely many mesoscopic clusters and a unique macroscopic condensate. Its unknown number of macroscopic clusters, their occupation fractions, and the finite-size transition mechanism are natural physical invariants, not arbitrary finite slices. The theorem gives the entire limiting distribution on random finite simplices with explicit Bessel weights and a probability of unique-condensate formation, closing a published structural gap. No hydrodynamic, dynamical-metastable, or near-critical-density statements follow automatically.

## Reproducibility and limitations
Run `python checker.py` in the extracted package using standard Python 3. It checks the finite partition formula against direct enumeration in three small systems, then checks convergence of the finite count law for three bulk-weight examples at sizes \(L=40,80,160\). The infinite proof uses elementary probability, uniform summable bounds, and lattice Riemann sums. Independence of the analysis has not been claimed. The remaining scientific risk is overlap with unindexed or informal work on the same critical model; no stronger published comparison located here establishes the joint law.
