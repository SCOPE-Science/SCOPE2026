# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The sixteen-state transfer condition matches both agents in every row-pair column and tracks each producer exactly once. The row swap gives the crossed closure, and direct exact symbolic reduction yields the stated ten- and six-dimensional characteristic factors. Their power traces and the formal-logarithm coefficient argument prove the formula for all strip lengths. Standalone exhaustive checks cover 699008 profiles for both families at lengths 3 through 9. Finite tests corroborate, but do not substitute for, the all-length proof.

## Originality — PASS
The motivating public-goods manuscripts establish strict best-response patterns and computational complexity, not the Möbius producer-count enumerator. The earlier untwisted prism enumerator and the distinct even-parity response on Möbius ladders do not imply the crossed-seam **exact-two** enumerator. The essential distinction is a signed row-swap trace, which preserves the quartic bulk power sum while swapping the exceptional congruence class. Targeted records and literature searches found no directly covering statement. This is best-knowledge originality, not a claim of exhaustive absence; an unindexed fixed-point enumeration may exist.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a natural complete classification of the producer-number spectrum for an infinite family of spatial public-goods games, and it proves a structural robustness phenomenon: a nontrivial boundary twist changes only bounded exceptional multiplicities, not the exponentially many bulk counts. The exact topological-defect identity distinguishes boundary-sensitive phases from local bulk equilibria. The result is not simply an increment in graph size and is not obtained by substituting a parameter into the known untwisted formula.

## Closest literature and limitations
Gilboa–Nisan (2022), arXiv:2207.04238, establishes the formal game framework. Klimm–Stahlberg (2023), arXiv:2301.11849, focuses on complexity. Related random-graph cellular automata work is background only. Neither accessible primary manuscript directly tabulates the crossed-strip polynomial. The scope is exact-two-neighbor response on cubic prism and Möbius graphs, not dynamics, welfare or arbitrary graphs. Further expert comparison with fixed-point enumerations remains appropriate.
