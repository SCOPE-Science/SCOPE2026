# Assessment: explicit cubic-threefold double cover

## Correctness — PASS

Direct substitution and inverse function-field formulas prove that the rational parametrization induces exactly the stated quadratic cover. Smoothness of the cubic, irreducibility of the elliptic branch cones, triple-line intersection and the deck involution are verified by exact polynomial identities. Local implicit-function arguments yield generic transverse \(A_5\) singularities and the two stated \(\widetilde E_7\) transverse sections. Serre's criterion and the double-cover canonical formula establish normality and the anticanonical volume. Risk: the exceptional transverse sections do not establish full analytic threefold singularity normal forms, which are not claimed.

Older unindexed geometric descriptions and incompletely accessible historical ledgers remain residual priority risks; unsuccessful searches are not a proof of absolute novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The originating paper's degree-two parametrization raises a concrete natural geometric question: what is the branch divisor and how does its normalization compactify? The answer is an exact classification rather than an arbitrary numerical slice: two elliptic cones have contact three along a whole line, creating an \(A_5\) generic transverse stratum and two exceptional elliptic sections. The anticanonical volume and rationality expose the geometry of the natural finite covering threefold, potentially useful for studying singular Fano degenerations and explicit unirationality.

## Scientific limitations

Only the displayed cubic and its distinguished quadratic cover are treated. The singularity assertions at the cone vertices are limited to explicitly chosen transverse surfaces, and no assertion about a full resolution is made. General global priority remains subject to additional archival checking.
