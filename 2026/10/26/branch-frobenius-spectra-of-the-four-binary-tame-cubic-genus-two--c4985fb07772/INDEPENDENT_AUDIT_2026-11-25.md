# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Tame inertia in degree three acts by 3-cycles, and killing the normal subgroup generated by inertia would give an étale cover of the geometric projective line, so the geometric deck group is C₃. It acts faithfully on the hyperelliptic line. In the reduced Artin–Schreier equation the genus permits only pole orders (5), (3,1), or (1,1,1). At a fixed pole the primitive cube-root tangent multiplier forces the highest odd pole order to be divisible by three: a coboundary cannot supply a competing highest pole of odd order. The first two types are thus impossible. The unique cyclic permutation of three simple poles up to inversion has a one-dimensional invariant principal-part space, which descends under Frobenius; its one nonzero F₂-vector gives two constant twists per divisor. Checking the divisor stabilizers identifies the twists in the split and mixed cases, but not in the cubic case. The displayed functions are invariant under the stated order-three maps; adjoining y base-changes their degree-three covers by the separable quadratic Artin–Schreier map. The two field extensions have coprime degrees, so the degree is still three. The preimage of the pole C₃ in the curve automorphism group is a central extension of C₃ by the hyperelliptic C₂ and has a unique C₃ subgroup. This gives target-coordinate uniqueness over F₂. The nonpole critical x-values of the split and cubic functions are ω,ω² and their function values are ω²,ω; those of the mixed function are 0,1 with values 1,0. All poles are simple and infinity for the cubic functions has local degree one. Pulling back the critical values through T²+T gives the displayed quartics; the polynomial with constant one has no roots even in F₄ and hence is irreducible. Counting the unique points at rational poles and two or zero elsewhere by trace gives the four point-count pairs. The degree-one and degree-two exclusions follow from genus and wild hyperelliptic ramification.
