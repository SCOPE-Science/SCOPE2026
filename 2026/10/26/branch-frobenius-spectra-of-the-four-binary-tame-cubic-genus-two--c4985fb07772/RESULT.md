# Branch Frobenius spectra of the four binary tame-cubic genus-two classes

## Finding
Let \(C\) be a smooth, projective, geometrically connected curve of genus two over \(\mathbf F_2\). Call its *tame gonality* the least degree of a finite separable morphism \(C\to\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf F_2}\) all of whose geometric ramification indices are odd. Then tame gonality is three precisely for the following **four** \(\mathbf F_2\)-isomorphism classes. Each displayed affine Artin–Schreier equation means its unique smooth projective model; all rational expressions are taken in \(\mathbf F_2(x)\).

| Model \(y^2+y=f(x)\) | \(\#C(\mathbf F_2)\) | \(\#C(\mathbf F_4)\) | Frobenius degrees of the four branch values of a tame cubic morphism |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| \(f=x+1/x+1/(x+1)\) | 3 | 3 | \((4)\) |
| \(f=x+1/(x^2+x+1)\) | 3 | 7 | \((1,1,2)\) |
| \(f=(x^2+x)/(x^3+x+1)\) | 6 | 6 | \((4)\) |
| \(f=(x^2+x)/(x^3+x+1)+1\) | 0 | 6 | \((4)\) |

For all four classes the coordinate \(y\) defines an everywhere-tame morphism of degree three. For each class, **every** tame degree-three morphism to \(\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf F_2}\) differs from \(y\) by an automorphism of its target; in particular, the listed branch Frobenius partition is intrinsic. With \(T\) a target affine coordinate, the branch divisor of \(y\) is cut out by \(T^4+T\) in the second class and by \(T^4+T+1\) in the other three. Every genus-two curve not isomorphic to one of these has tame gonality at least four. The classification concerns *minimal tame degree and branch arithmetic*, not a new enumeration of all genus-two models.

## Assumptions and scope
All morphisms and ramification indices are taken over an algebraic closure unless a field of definition is specified. A morphism is tame if every index is prime to the characteristic (two); degree-three tame covers here have only indices one or three. The branch partition is the multiset of degrees of closed points of the reduced geometric branch divisor on the target, over \(\mathbf F_2\). An isomorphism class is over \(\mathbf F_2\), not only over its algebraic closure. The base curve is smooth, projective, and geometrically connected with genus two. All normal forms use \(\mathbf F_2\)-rational coordinates on the hyperelliptic quotient, available because genus-zero curves over finite fields have rational points.

## Proof
**1. Tame cubics are geometric cyclic quotients.** For a separable tame morphism \(C\to\mathbf P^1\) of degree three in characteristic two, each nontrivial inertia permutation is a 3-cycle. In the geometric Galois closure, the normal subgroup generated by inertia has quotient giving an everywhere-étale cover of \(\mathbf P^1\). There is no nontrivial finite connected étale cover of \(\mathbf P^1\) over an algebraically closed field, so the geometric monodromy is generated by 3-cycles and equals \(C_3\), rather than \(S_3\). Thus the cubic cover is geometrically Galois with deck group \(H\cong C_3\).

**2. Only the ordinary Artin–Schreier pole type survives.** The canonical hyperelliptic involution commutes with \(H\), so \(H\) projects injectively to an order-three subgroup of \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\overline{\mathbf F}_2)\) acting on the hyperelliptic coordinate \(x\). Choose an Artin–Schreier generator in reduced form \(y^2+y=f(x)\), with every pole of \(f\) of odd order. The genus formula is \(2g+2=\sum_P(m_P+1)\), summing over all geometric poles with odd orders \(m_P\). For genus two, the possibilities are \((5)\), \((3,1)\), and \((1,1,1)\). At a pole fixed by a nonidentity order-three Möbius transformation, its tangent multiplier is a primitive cube root of unity, and comparison of the highest odd-order principal part shows that its pole order must be divisible by three. (A change \(y\mapsto y+r(x)\) cannot cancel a nonzero highest odd-order pole, because the highest pole of \(r^2+r\) has even order.) The types \((5)\) and \((3,1)\) cannot be preserved: an order-three permutation of one or two poles fixes both, including a pole whose order is not divisible by three. Thus only three *simple* poles are possible; equivalently the 2-rank is two. They are permuted cyclically by \(H\).

**3. Unique invariant polar class for each pole divisor.** Fix a reduced \(\mathbf F_2\)-rational divisor \(D\) of degree three on \(\mathbf P^1\), with three distinct geometric support points. A Möbius 3-cycle of these points is unique up to inversion. Its action on the three-dimensional space of simple principal parts \(L(D)/\overline{\mathbf F}_2\) has a one-dimensional invariant subspace. Frobenius normalizes the 3-cycle subgroup and preserves this line, so the line descends to \(\mathbf F_2\). Since \(\mathbf F_2^*=\{1\}\), there is exactly one nonzero rational invariant principal-part vector, and two Artin–Schreier twists, differing by the constant one. Indeed, if a reduced simple-pole function is preserved by an order-three lift of the curve, its difference after Möbius pullback cannot be a nonconstant Artin–Schreier coboundary; it is constant. Iterating three times makes that constant zero in characteristic two.

**4. Arithmetic pole types and their quotient stabilizers.** A degree-three reduced divisor has precisely three possible Frobenius splitting types: \((1,1,1)\), \((1,2)\), and \((3)\). Under \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_2)\), each forms one orbit. For representative divisors the unique invariant rational functions, modulo constants, are respectively
\[
 f_s=x+\frac1x+\frac1{x+1},\qquad
 f_m=x+\frac1{x^2+x+1},\qquad
 f_c=\frac{x^2+x}{x^3+x+1}.
\]
The divisor stabilizers have orders six, two, and three. In the split case \(x\mapsto1/x\) sends \(f_s\) to \(f_s+1\); in the mixed case \(x\mapsto x+1\) sends \(f_m\) to \(f_m+1\). The cubic-divisor stabilizer is the order-three subgroup generated by \(x\mapsto1/(x+1)\), fixing \(f_c\) exactly, hence its two constant twists remain distinct. Any isomorphism between reduced simple-pole Artin–Schreier models must preserve their pole divisors via a projective transformation; a nonconstant Artin–Schreier correction would create an even-order pole. These observations yield exactly \(1+1+2=4\) arithmetic classes. For the cubic pole type, the two irreducible monic cubic divisors are exchanged by \(x\mapsto1/x\), and there is no additional class.

**5. Existence, uniqueness of quotient, and tame ramification.** Each displayed \(f\) has degree three as a rational map \(\mathbf P^1_x\to\mathbf P^1\). The order-three Möbius transformation \(x\mapsto 1/(x+1)\) fixes \(f_s,f_c\); the mixed \(f_m\) is fixed by \(x\mapsto x/(\omega x+\omega^2)\), where \(\omega^2+\omega+1=0\) in \(\mathbf F_4\). Therefore \(y\) gives a *geometrically cyclic* degree-three morphism over \(\mathbf F_2\) in all cases. Its deck group is stable under Frobenius (even when its individual generators require \(\mathbf F_4\)), so its quotient is the rational target with coordinate \(y\). Being a separable Galois cover of degree three in characteristic two, it is everywhere tame. Every possible tame cubic cover has a deck subgroup whose action on the three hyperelliptic poles is the unique order-three subgroup of their permutation group. Its lift is unique because the hyperelliptic kernel has order two, so its invariant subfield, and hence the quotient map up to a target automorphism, is unique.

**6. Branch values.** The relevant rational-function derivatives, written up to nonzero square denominators, are \((x^2+x+1)^2\) for \(f_s,f_c\), and \(x^2(x+1)^2\) for \(f_m\). In the split and cubic cases the two geometric critical \(x\)-points lie in \(\mathbf F_4\setminus\mathbf F_2\), and their \(f\)-values are \(\omega,\omega^2\). In the mixed case the critical \(x\)-points are \(0,1\), with values \(1,0\). Each such \(x\)-point lifts to two points of \(C\), with their \(y\)-coordinates solving \(y^2+y=f(x)\). The four branch values therefore have squarefree equations \((T^2+T+\omega)(T^2+T+\omega^2)=T^4+T+1\), or \((T^2+T)(T^2+T+1)=T^4+T\). The constant-one twist interchanges \(\omega\) and \(\omega^2\), hence retains the quartic branch polynomial. The first polynomial is irreducible over \(\mathbf F_2\); the second has factors of degrees \(1,1,2\).

**7. Point counts and distinction.** For each closed pole of the hyperelliptic Artin–Schreier function there is one point over it; elsewhere each rational \(x\)-value contributes two points if the field trace of \(f(x)\) is zero, and none otherwise. Direct evaluations over \(\mathbf F_2\) and \(\mathbf F_4\) give the point-count columns above. The split and mixed models are not isomorphic because of their different numbers of degree-two points, while the two cubic twists have different rational point counts. Other distinctions already follow from the hyperelliptic pole splitting types. A genus-two curve cannot have a degree-one map to \(\mathbf P^1\); its unique degree-two map is hyperelliptic and wildly ramified in characteristic two. Thus the four examples have tame gonality exactly three and all remaining classes have tame gonality at least four.

## Relationship to prior work
Kedlaya–Litt–Witaszek (first publicly available 2 October 2020) prove existence of some tame morphism for every curve over a finite field; their general existence theorem neither minimizes the degree for genus two over \(\mathbf F_2\) nor gives branch Frobenius spectra. Cardona–Nart–Pujolàs (2002) completely classify genus-two models and automorphism groups in characteristic two. Their normal forms, orbit formulas and twist classifications are stronger than the underlying *curve enumeration* here; that enumeration is not claimed independently. The differentiated statement is the classification of the **unique minimal tame cubic quotient and its arithmetic branch divisor** across these arithmetic classes. A combination of the older full automorphism classification with additional group-theoretic and branch calculations may yield the theorem, which limits any publication-priority claim. No claim is made of independent verification or of priority over possible unindexed computations.

## Limitations
The result is restricted to genus two over \(\mathbf F_2\) and degree-three tame morphisms; it does not compute tame gonality for the remaining sixteen genus-two classes, nor classify higher-degree tame morphisms. The branch polynomials describe branch divisors up to target automorphism, not canonically labelled branch values. Any future claim of worldwide novelty needs independent bibliographic review, particularly against prior automorphism and low-degree Hurwitz classifications.
