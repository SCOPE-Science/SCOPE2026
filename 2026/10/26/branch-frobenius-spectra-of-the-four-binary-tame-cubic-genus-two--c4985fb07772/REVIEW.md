# Scientific review: tame-cubic genus-two branch spectra

**Correctness — PASS.** The claim is an arithmetic classification of *all* genus-two curves over the binary field possessing a degree-three everywhere-tame morphism, with uniqueness of the quotient and two branch Frobenius types. The proof uses geometric monodromy and triviality of étale covers of the projective line to force a geometric cyclic cubic, then the hyperelliptic involution and odd Artin–Schreier conductors to reduce to three simple poles. The invariant principal-part line, complete three-type pole-divisor orbit classification, and exact stabilizer computations give one, one, and two isomorphism classes. Tameness, ramification, point counts, and uniqueness are independently justified. Exact finite-field checks reproduce every explicit small-case identity and count. Residual correctness risk is reliance on the standard hyperelliptic uniqueness, the Artin–Schreier genus/p-rank formula, and the no-unramified-cover theorem; all are established background, and no finite sampling stands in for these implications.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The first-public 2020 Kedlaya–Litt–Witaszek paper establishes existence of tame morphisms over finite fields but does not classify minimal cubic covers or their Frobenius branch values. Cardona–Nart–Pujolàs (2002) provides a *complete stronger classification of genus-two models, isomorphisms and automorphism structures* in characteristic two. Accordingly, the normal forms and unrefined curve counts are explicitly prior-covered and not independently claimed. The differentiated result is the **unique minimal tame cubic quotient with its exact reduced branch divisor and Frobenius splitting for every arithmetic class**, which requires a geometric monodromy/quotient argument and critical-value arithmetic beyond the cited curve enumeration. Searches of related published records, exact model aliases and tame trigonal/cyclic terminology did not identify the same four-class branch theorem. Its possible derivation from prior complete automorphism tables is a significant residual priority risk; independent literature assessment remains necessary.

**Value — PASS.** Tame gonality is a natural invariant in characteristic two, where the unique hyperelliptic double map is wildly ramified. Identifying the exact minimum-degree tame locus among all twenty genus-two classes, proving uniqueness of its degree-three quotient and determining branch splitting as either a single degree-four point or a pair of rational points plus one quadratic point supplies a full arithmetic boundary. The result separates the point-free, six-point, and two three-point cases and provides concrete minimal-degree targets relevant to tame Hurwitz and positive-characteristic Belyi questions. The scientific contribution is the quotient/branch refinement, not the existing general curve census.

The statement is only for curves over the binary field and degree-three tame maps; no bound on the exact tame gonality of remaining classes is claimed. A complete automorphism-group classification is prior art and materially limits novelty; publication priority is qualified.
