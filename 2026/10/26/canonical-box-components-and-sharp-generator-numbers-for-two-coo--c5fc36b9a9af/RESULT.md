# Canonical box components and sharp generator numbers for two-coordinate Horn closures

## Finding
Consider the finite implication system on words in \(X=\prod_{i=1}^{d}A_i\): from two words at Hamming distance exactly two, infer the other two vertices of their two-coordinate rectangle. Its closed sets admit a unique normal form as unions of coordinate boxes mutually separated by Hamming distance at least three. The closure of any seed is calculated by greedily merging boxes at distance at most two. The sharp size of a smallest seed generating the entire word product is
\[
 g(q_1,\ldots,q_d)=1+\max\left\{\max_{1\le i\le d}(q_i-1),\left\lceil\frac{\sum_{i=1}^d(q_i-1)}2\right\rceil\right\}.
\]
The formula applies to arbitrary finite, nonuniform alphabets. In equal alphabets it becomes \(q\) for \(d=1\), and \(1+\lceil d(q-1)/2\rceil\) for \(d\ge2\). A vertex in the Cartesian product of cliques is one word, and the generating implications correspond to the two endpoints of an induced three-vertex path; thus this is a structural theorem about a particular finite Horn closure and its lattice of closed sets.

## Assumptions and scope
Fix integers \(d\ge1\) and \(q_i\ge2\), and fixed finite sets \(A_i\) of cardinality \(q_i\). Define the Hamming distance \(h(x,y)\) to be the number of differing coordinates. For \(h(x,y)=2\), with distinct coordinates \(i,j\), the associated implication has premise \(\{x,y\}\) and conclusion the two words obtained by swapping only coordinate \(i\) or only coordinate \(j\) between \(x\) and \(y\). Closure \(c(S)\) is the least set containing \(S\) and satisfying every implication. A coordinate box is any nonempty \(B=\prod_iB_i\) with \(\varnothing\ne B_i\subseteq A_i\); its box-hull with another box \(D\) is \(B\vee D=\prod_i(B_i\cup D_i)\). The distance of two sets is the minimum Hamming distance between their elements. A generator is a subset \(S\subseteq X\) with \(c(S)=X\). No claim is made about geodesic, arbitrary-length induced-path, or ordinary non-induced three-vertex-path closure.

## Proof
**1. Box closure.** A coordinate box is closed: if two words in the box differ in two coordinates, both missing corners use coordinate symbols already permitted in that box. More generally a union of boxes at pairwise distance at least three is closed: a triggering pair must lie within one box.

**2. Absorption lemma.** Let \(B\) be a coordinate box and let \(z\in X\) satisfy \(h(z,B)\le2\). Then \(c(B\cup\{z\})=\operatorname{box}(B\cup\{z\})\). Inclusion from left to right follows from step 1. To prove the opposite inclusion, first suppose \(z\) differs from some \(b\in B\) in exactly one coordinate \(i\). For every coordinate \(j\ne i\) and every allowed symbol in \(B_j\), the rectangle rule applied to \(z\) and a suitable vertex of \(B\), first keeping all other coordinates equal to \(b\), copies the new \(i\)-symbol across coordinate \(j\). Repeating this operation across coordinates, and across their values, produces every point of the required box. If \(z\) differs from a nearest point \(b\in B\) in exactly two coordinates, the rule with endpoints \(z,b\) first creates the two intermediate points, each differing from \(B\) in only one coordinate; applying the one-coordinate case successively proves the assertion. The case \(z\in B\) is immediate.

**3. Box merging.** For boxes \(B,D\) at distance at most two, choose \(z\in D\) witnessing this distance. Step 2 absorbs \(z\) into \(B\). The induced Hamming-distance-one graph on \(D\) is connected; traverse a spanning tree of that graph. Every next vertex of \(D\) is at distance at most one from the previously expanded box and hence can be absorbed by step 2. The resulting closed hull is exactly \(B\vee D\). Starting from singleton boxes of a seed \(S\), repeat any such merge. Each merge strictly reduces the number of boxes, hence the process terminates. The final union is closed by step 1, and every replacement is contained in \(c(S)\) by the merging lemma; conversely the final union contains \(S\). Consequently the final union equals \(c(S)\), independently of merge order.

**4. Uniqueness.** In a closed set, a distance-one connected component containing \(z\) is itself a box: traverse its distance-one edges from \(z\), and apply step 2 after each traversal, all inside the ambient closed set; a product of coordinate projections of the visited vertices is thereby included. The product is distance-one connected, so it cannot leave the component. Hence each component equals its box. Two components cannot be at set-distance two, since the merge lemma would make their union connected within the closed set. They also cannot be at distance one by the component definition. Thus the box components are mutually at distance at least three and uniquely determined. Conversely every such union is closed by step 1.

**5. Sharp generator lower bound.** Put \(r_i=q_i-1\), \(T=\sum_ir_i\) and \(M=\max_ir_i\). The implication rules never introduce a coordinate symbol absent from all seeds. Every generating seed has at least \(q_i\) points for each coordinate \(i\), so \(|S|\ge 1+M\). For a box define its rank \(\rho(B)=\sum_i(|B_i|-1)\). The merger of two boxes of distance at most two satisfies
\[
 \rho(B\vee D)-\rho(B)-\rho(D)=\sum_i\bigl(1-|B_i\cap D_i|\bigr)\le2.
\]
Indeed at most two coordinate intersections are empty, contributing one each; every other coordinate contributes at most zero. Begin with \(k\) rank-zero singleton boxes. After exactly \(k-1\) merges to the single full box of rank \(T\), the rank can have increased by at most \(2(k-1)\). Thus \(k\ge1+\lceil T/2\rceil\). Combining yields the displayed lower bound.

**6. A matching construction attaining the bound.** Fix one distinguished symbol \(0_i\in A_i\) in every coordinate and let \(o\) be the all-distinguished word. For each other symbol \(a\in A_i\setminus\{0_i\}\), form a token \((i,a)\); there are \(r_i\) tokens in part \(i\). Partition these \(T\) tokens into the fewest possible groups of one or two, with the tokens of each pair coming from different parts. Such a partition contains exactly \(m=\max\{M,\lceil T/2\rceil\}\) groups. To see this, if the largest part has \(M>T/2\) tokens, pair every other token with a distinct token of that part and leave its remaining tokens singleton. Otherwise a pairing of different parts leaving at most one singleton exists: repeatedly pair tokens from two largest nonempty parts; after removal, no part can exceed half the remaining total by more than one, so the process ends with no more than one singleton. (Equivalently, apply the matching criterion for a complete multipartite graph.) For each token group create one word obtained from \(o\) by changing precisely its one or two specified coordinates to the token's symbols. Take these \(m\) words together with \(o\). The rectangle rule applied to \(o\) and any two-token word creates both one-coordinate words. Hence all one-coordinate variants of \(o\) are in the closure. By induction on the number of non-distinguished coordinates, every word with at least two changes also enters the closure: delete two distinct changed coordinates in turn, use the resulting two words of one lower support size as premises, and recover the target as a missing corner. Therefore \(m+1\) seeds generate all \(X\), proving the claimed equality.

## Verification
A standalone pure-Python checker `verify.py` independently computes the Horn closure, box components and greedy box mergers. It exhaustively checks all seed subsets for six small heterogeneous/binary products with at most sixteen words (70,480 seed sets) and checks 800 deterministic sampled subsets for each of three larger products. It independently checks smaller generating-set sizes where feasible and finds optimal witnesses in the enumerated cases. The executable prints `VERIFY_OK` when its assertions succeed. The finite tests are corroboration, not the proof for arbitrary parameters.

## Relationship to prior work
The general concept of finite closure operators and implication bases, including minimum-cardinality generators, is treated by Bichoupan (2022). Brešar and Valencia-Pabon determine the hull number of Hamming graphs under the *ordinary* (non-induced) three-vertex-path closure, which is distinct from the two-coordinate rule here when an alphabet has more than two symbols: ordinary inference can trigger on pairs of neighbors that are adjacent to each other. In the binary case the graph is triangle-free, and the generator formula in this note specializes to the already established ordinary hull number, so that special case is not claimed as new. Dourado, Penso, and Rautenbach study the induced-path-of-order-three hull number, chiefly complexity for chordal graphs and algorithms for unit interval graphs; their examined material does not determine these box decompositions or heterogeneous-alphabet sharp formula. No general novelty guarantee is asserted: equivalent descriptions in constraint satisfaction, Horn closure, or graph convexity may exist.

## Limitations
This theorem only covers the exact two-coordinate implication relation on full finite word products; it does not determine a minimum implication basis, a polynomial-time algorithm for arbitrary input closure systems, or the distribution of all closed-set ranks. The mathematical proof is unconditional within the stated domain. Finite computational tests do not verify all product sizes. The literature comparison leaves residual risk of an equivalent result under different terminology, particularly in less accessible induced-path convexity literature. Independent validation has not been carried out.
