# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The full Leibniz identity reduces to the matrix equation \(A^T+A=cI_m\) after establishing that the square ideal \(kz\) is characteristic under derivations. Characteristic-two matrix ranks are enumerated through unique radicals of symmetric forms, with a nonsingular-block recurrence that explicitly includes nonalternating symmetric forms; appending the freely chosen central row gives all possible ranks. The odd-characteristic four-dimensional case follows by a direct determinant calculation, the standard nonzero-value ternary quadratic-form count, and separate treatment of the zero scalar. The standalone code independently checks the linear-system dimensions, Leibniz identities and finite-field rank counts. Limits: finite checks do not prove arbitrary-field statements.

## Originality

PASS, with bounded residual risk. The exact claim concerns full derivation-rank counts, rather than the previously classified low-dimensional algebra [1], the bilinear-form construction [2], the structure theory of associative one-dimensional-square algebras [5], or general descriptions of derivations [3,4]. Inspected full texts [1]–[3] and [5] did not supply the all-dimensional characteristic-two rank census or the odd-characteristic four-dimensional rank vector. Relevant research-record searches for derivation ranks, aliases, symmetric/conformal orthogonal matrices and characteristic-two finite-field spectra returned no equivalent theorem. A particularly pertinent broader derivation-characterization paper [4] was not inspected in full and could imply the structural normal form; the exact arithmetic rank distribution is not claimed novel merely because the parameterization is absent from a title. Absolute priority is unverified.

## Value

PASS. Derivation rank is a natural intrinsic invariant of an algebra, controlling singular derivations and, in particular, the existence of invertible derivations. The all-dimensional characteristic-two formula gives the entire rank spectrum, not one arbitrary sample statistic, while the complete four-dimensional comparison demonstrates a sharp exceptional-characteristic change. The family includes the established low-dimensional nilpotent evolution-algebra example and every higher analogue, so the uniform finite-field formula is reusable for arithmetic questions about derivation strata rather than a one-off table computation.

## Scope and risks

The proofs cover the stated family over all fields and exact rank distributions over even finite fields, plus the specified four-dimensional odd-characteristic case. They do not cover every evolution algebra, the odd-dimensional general odd-field count, or orbit counts. Full text of [4] could not be inspected; publication priority is not certified. Self-review has not been replaced by independent mathematical validation.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
