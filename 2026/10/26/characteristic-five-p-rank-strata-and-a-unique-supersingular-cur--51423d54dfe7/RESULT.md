# Characteristic-five \(p\)-rank strata and a unique supersingular curve in a genus-four cyclic-six family

## Finding

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(5\). For \(a,b\in k\), let \(H_{a,b}\) be the smooth projective curve associated to
\[
y^2=F_{a,b}(x)=x^{10}+x^7+ax^4+bx.
\]
Put
\[
\Delta=-4a^3+a^2+18ab-27b^2-4b,
\quad U=a^2+2b,\quad
D=b^2(2a+1)^5+2[2(a+b)]^5.
\]
Precisely the parameters satisfying \(b\Delta\ne0\) define smooth curves; they have genus \(4\). Write \(f\) for the \(5\)-rank of the Jacobian and \(a_J\) for its \(a\)-number (to distinguish it from the equation coefficient \(a\)). Then **for every smooth member**
\[
\boxed{\ a_J=1+\mathbf 1_{U=0},\qquad f=\mathbf 1_{U\ne0}+2\mathbf 1_{D\ne0}.\ }
\]
In particular, all four values \(f=0,1,2,3\) occur, every member is nonordinary, and no member is superspecial. Explicit smooth witnesses over \(\mathbb F_5\) are \((a,b)=(0,2),(1,2),(0,1),(4,2)\) for \(f=3,2,1,0\), respectively. The \(5\)-rank-zero stratum is the **single parameter pair** \((a,b)=(4,2)\) in the algebraic closure. For this curve the \(a\)-number is \(2\), and the Jacobian is supersingular with full zeta function
\[
\boxed{\ Z(H_{4,2}/\mathbb F_5,T)=\frac{(1+5T^2)^2(1-5T^2+25T^4)}{(1-T)(1-5T)}.\ }
\]
Consequently it is the unique supersingular member of the smooth two-parameter family. This is a statement about these marked equations, not a count of geometric isomorphism classes.

## Assumptions and scope

All assertions about geometric \(5\)-rank, \(a\)-number and uniqueness use algebraically closed \(k\) of characteristic \(5\). The exact zeta function concerns the unique member over \(\mathbb F_5\). A supersingular genus-four Jacobian here means all eight Newton slopes are \(1/2\); a superspecial Jacobian would have \(a\)-number \(4\). The supersingular conclusion from the explicit zeta factorization is stronger than merely having \(5\)-rank zero.

## Proof

**Smoothness and genus.** Factor \(F_{a,b}(x)=x h(x^3)\), where \(h(t)=t^3+t^2+at+b\). In characteristic \(5\), \(x\mapsto x^3\) is separable on \(\mathbb G_m\). The degree-ten polynomial \(F_{a,b}\) has ten distinct roots exactly when \(b=h(0)\ne0\) and the cubic \(h\) has three distinct roots. Its usual cubic discriminant is \(\Delta=-4a^3+a^2+18ab-27b^2-4b\). A smooth degree-ten hyperelliptic cover has genus \(4\), and two rational points at infinity because the leading coefficient is \(1\).

**Cartier calculation.** On the standard basis \(\omega_j=x^{j-1}dx/y\), \(j=1,2,3,4\), the Cartier operator is \(5^{-1}\)-semilinear. The coefficients of \(F_{a,b}(x)^2\) in degrees \(5i-j\) give, before entrywise fifth roots, the matrix
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&b^2&0\\
0&a^2+2b&0&0\\
2a+1&0&0&2(a+b)\\
0&0&2&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
This direct expansion is also implemented in the standalone verifier. The subspace generated by \(\omega_2\) contributes Cartier rank one and stable rank one if \(U\ne0\), otherwise both ranks are zero. The complementary three-dimensional subspace is spanned by \(\omega_1,\omega_3,\omega_4\). It has Cartier rank exactly two for every smooth parameter: its two potentially vanishing entries \(2a+1\) and \(2(a+b)\) cannot both vanish on the smooth locus, since their common zero \((a,b)=(2,3)\) makes \(h(t)=(t+2)^3\). Thus \(\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal C)=2+\mathbf1_{U\ne0}\), proving the formula for \(a_J=4-\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal C)\).

On the complementary subspace, the direct semilinear calculation yields
\[
\mathcal C^2(\omega_3)=D^{1/25}\omega_3.
\]
When \(D\ne0\), the eventual Cartier image there is exactly two-dimensional, generated by \(\omega_3\) and \(\mathcal C(\omega_3)\). When \(D=0\), a third Cartier iterate kills the entire complementary subspace. As the \(p\)-rank of a Jacobian equals the dimension of the stable Cartier image on regular differentials, this proves the formula for \(f\) over all algebraically closed \(k\).

**The zero-rank locus.** The equations \(U=D=0\) require \(b=2a^2\) in characteristic \(5\). On this curve in parameter space, elementary polynomial factorization in \(\mathbb F_5[a]\) gives
\[
\Delta\big|_{b=2a^2}=2a^2(a-2)^2,\qquad
D\big|_{b=2a^2}=3a^4(a-2)^5(a+1).
\]
Smoothness removes \(a=0\) and \(a=2\), leaving exactly \(a=-1=4\), \(b=2\). The associated Jacobian has \(5\)-rank zero and \(a_J=2\).

**Exact zeta numerator at the unique zero-rank member.** The standalone verifier exhaustively enumerates points over \(\mathbb F_{5^n}\) for \(n=1,2,3,4\) using explicit irreducible defining polynomials. The respective counts are \(6,36,126,576\). With \(q=5\), the power sums of the eight geometric Frobenius eigenvalues are \(s_n=q^n+1-\#H(\mathbb F_{q^n})\). Newton identities determine the initial numerator coefficients \((c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4)=(1,0,5,0,0)\); the genus-four Weil functional equation gives the remaining four coefficients. Thus
\[
L(T)=1+5T^2+125T^6+625T^8
=(1+5T^2)^2(1-5T^2+25T^4).
\]
Writing \(u=\sqrt5\,T\), these factors are \(\Phi_4(u)^2\Phi_{12}(u)\), where \(\Phi_n\) is the ordinary cyclotomic polynomial. Every Frobenius eigenvalue therefore has \(5\)-adic valuation \(1/2\), giving the asserted supersingularity. Every other smooth family member has positive \(5\)-rank, so cannot be supersingular.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` from the package directory (Python standard library only). The computation symbolically reconstructs the displayed Cartier matrix from the square of the defining polynomial, exhaustively checks all \(25\) coefficient pairs over \(\mathbb F_5\) subject to smoothness and compares ranks of iterates, verifies irreducibility of the finite-field moduli, enumerates all points over \(\mathbb F_{5^n}\) for \(1\le n\le4\), and reconstructs the zeta numerator. The observed parameter census has \(12\) cases \((a_J,f)=(1,3)\), \(2\) cases \((1,1)\), \(2\) cases \((2,2)\), and \(1\) case \((2,0)\). The run prints `VERIFY_OK`. The polynomial identities and Cartier stable-rank argument above establish the universal geometric assertions; enumerations merely provide exact finite-field inputs and checks.

## Relationship to prior work

Kudo, Nakagawa and Takagi introduce precisely the genus-four hyperelliptic cyclic-six equation, explain the efficient computation of Cartier–Manin matrices, and search for superspecial curves, concentrating their explicit classification tables on larger prime characteristics [1]. Their definition of the family and general coefficient method are prior results, not new here. The present statement instead gives the full \(5\)-rank and \(a\)-number strata in characteristic \(5\), the unique supersingular non-superspecial parameter, and its zeta polynomial. Broader treatments of hyperelliptic \(p\)-rank strata [2] and cyclic-cover Newton polygons [3] provide geometric context but not this explicit two-parameter calculation in the inspected sections. Related characteristic-five genus-four calculations for *different defining families* do not alone imply the coefficient criteria for this cyclic-six family. No claim of globally unique supersingular genus-four curves or isomorphism classes is made.

## Limitations

The geometric classification does not determine complete Newton polygons at every positive \(5\)-rank parameter. It concerns equations with the fixed coefficients of \(x^{10}\) and \(x^7\) normalized to one, on the discriminant-nonzero locus. The \(\mathbb F_5\) census counts marked coefficient pairs, not isomorphism classes. The zeta determination is specific to \((a,b)=(4,2)\); no characteristic-zero deformation, universal statement outside the family or independent validation is asserted. Unindexed prior arithmetic computations might cover some special cases.
