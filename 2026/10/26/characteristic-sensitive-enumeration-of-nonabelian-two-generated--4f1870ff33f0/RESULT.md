# Characteristic-sensitive enumeration of nonabelian two-generated subalgebras of the four-by-four niltriangular Lie algebra

## Finding
For every finite field \(k=\mathbb F_q\), write \(\mathfrak n_4(k)\) for the Lie algebra of strictly upper-triangular \(4\times4\) matrices with the matrix commutator. Let a **two-generated subalgebra** mean one generated *as a Lie algebra* by a pair of elements (zero entries permitted). Distinct subalgebras, not generating pairs, are counted.

Every nonabelian two-generated subalgebra has one of precisely two abstract types: the three-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra \(\mathfrak h_3\), or the four-dimensional filiform algebra \(\mathfrak f_4\) with basis \(u,v,w,z\) and nonzero brackets \([u,v]=w\), \([u,w]=z\). Their embedding counts are
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
&\#\{\mathfrak h_3\text{ subalgebras}\}&\#\{\mathfrak f_4\text{ subalgebras}\}\\\hline
\operatorname{char}k=2 &(q+3)q^4&(q^2-1)q^2\\
\operatorname{char}k\ne2 &4q^4 &(q^2+q-2)q^2
\end{array}
\]
In particular, these two counts are *not* uniform polynomials across characteristics. The abelian two-plane enumeration, available separately, is not asserted as a new result here.

## Assumptions and scope
Take any prime power \(q\ge2\). Use the ordered matrix-unit basis
\[
(a,b,c,d,e,f)\longleftrightarrow aE_{12}+bE_{23}+cE_{34}+dE_{13}+eE_{24}+fE_{14}.
\]
The unique central direction is \(kE_{14}\), the derived algebra is \(\langle E_{13},E_{24},E_{14}\rangle\), and the lower central series terminates after the third bracket. A subalgebra is counted once regardless of the number of its ordered generating pairs. There is no restriction to restricted or algebraic group subalgebras in small characteristic.

## Proof
Let \(x=(a,b,c,d,e,f)\) and \(y=(a',b',c',d',e',f')\), with coordinates in \(k\). Put
\[
\alpha=ab'-ba',\quad \beta=bc'-cb',\quad \tau=ac'-ca',\quad
\gamma=ae'-ea'+dc'-cd'.
\]
Direct multiplication gives
\[
[x,y]=(0,0,0,\alpha,\beta,\gamma),\quad
[x,[x,y]]=(0,0,0,0,0,a\beta-c\alpha),\quad
[y,[x,y]]=(0,0,0,0,0,a'\beta-c'\alpha).
\]
All brackets of weight four vanish. Therefore the Lie algebra generated by \(x,y\) is exactly the span of these five displayed vectors and \(x,y\). If it is nonabelian but has vanishing triple brackets, it is three-dimensional Heisenberg: a two-dimensional nonabelian Lie algebra cannot be nilpotent, and the nonzero commutator must be independent of the generators. If a triple bracket is nonzero, the generated subalgebra has dimension four, derived dimension two and one-dimensional center, hence is the four-dimensional filiform algebra. This holds even in characteristic two because a nonzero linear functional on a two-space can be normalized by an invertible change of generators.

Consider the projection \(\pi:\mathfrak n_4\to k^3\) onto the coordinates \((a,b,c)\). For pairs with \(\dim\langle\pi x,\pi y\rangle=2\), let \(P\) denote their projected plane. Its normal vector is \((\beta,-\tau,\alpha)\), in the standard coordinate pairing. The two triple brackets vanish precisely when the functional \((\beta,0,-\alpha)\) vanishes identically on \(P\). The exceptional plane \(P=\langle(1,0,0),(0,0,1)\rangle\) has \(\alpha=\beta=0\), so its commutator is only central. For every other plane, the triple brackets vanish precisely in these cases:

- In characteristic two, \(\tau=0\), giving exactly \(q+1\) projected planes.
- In odd characteristic, \(\tau=0\) and \(\alpha\beta=0\), giving exactly two projected planes.

Indeed, when \((\alpha,\beta)\ne(0,0)\), the vectors \((\beta,0,-\alpha)\) and \((\beta,-\tau,\alpha)\) are proportional exactly as stated. The projective plane has \(q^2+q+1\) points. Set
\[
L=q^2+q+1,\qquad h=\begin{cases}q+1&\operatorname{char}k=2,\\2&\operatorname{char}k\ne2,\end{cases}\qquad s=L-1-h,
\]
and \(B=(q^2-1)(q^2-q)\), the number of ordered bases of any projected two-plane.

Every projected basis admits \(q^6\) independent lifts in the coordinates \((d,e,f,d',e',f')\). Thus pairs generating four-dimensional filiform subalgebras number exactly \(sBq^6\). A particular filiform subalgebra is generated by a pair precisely when their images in its two-dimensional abelianization are independent. This gives exactly \(Bq^4\) generating pairs per embedded copy (four free derived coordinates), and consequently \(s q^2\) distinct filiform subalgebras, as asserted.

For three-dimensional Heisenberg subalgebras, there are three disjoint contributing kinds of ordered generating pairs:

1. Projected rank two and a nonexceptional plane where the triple bracket vanishes: \(hBq^6\) pairs.
2. Projected rank two and the exceptional plane \(\alpha=\beta=0\), with \(\gamma\ne0\): \(B(q-1)q^5\) pairs. Here \(\gamma\) is a nonzero linear functional on the degree-two lift coefficients, so exactly a fraction \((q-1)/q\) of lifts qualify.
3. Projected rank one and a nonzero commutator: \((q^2-1)(L-1)(q-1)q^5\) pairs. The projected line may be any of \(L-1\) lines except the middle coordinate axis, and for each nonzero ordered coefficient pair its commutator is a nonzero linear functional in four lift coefficients, leaving \(q^5(q-1)\) possibilities.

Each Heisenberg subalgebra has exactly \(Bq^2\) ordered generating pairs, because the images of the two generators must form a basis modulo its one-dimensional center and their central coordinates are arbitrary. Dividing the sum by \(Bq^2\) gives
\[
(L-1)q^2+(q-1)q^3+hq^4=(h+2)q^4.
\]
Substituting the two values of \(h\) proves the Heisenberg formula. The nilpotent class bound rules out further nonabelian types.

## Verification
The supplied standalone Python programs `CHECK_PAIR_COUNTS.py` and `CHECK_SUBALGEBRAS.py` reproduce the pair generation laws, rank distributions and distinct-subalgebra counts. Run each independently with Python 3. The first exhaustively checks all \(2^{12}=4096\) and \(3^{12}=531441\) ordered pairs and samples additional odd-characteristic cases. The second independently enumerates the distinct row-reduced spans of all generated subalgebras for \(q=2,3\). Both completed without assertion failures. In particular, the distinct nonabelian counts at \(q=2\) are \(80\) Heisenberg and \(12\) filiform; at \(q=3\), \(324\) and \(90\). Computations over these fields check instances, not the proof over arbitrary finite fields.

## Relationship to prior work
Bowman, Towers and Varea (arXiv:0704.2723, 2007) investigate the structural significance of two-generated Lie subalgebras and qualitative properties of supersolvability and triangulability, not the finite-field embedding census of this specific six-dimensional niltriangular algebra. Ceballos, Núñez and Tenorio (2008) study maximal abelian subalgebras of upper-triangular Lie algebras; their 2014 work uses triangular configurations and matrix representations. Woodward (2007) studies local *ideal* zeta functions of upper-triangular Lie rings, a different counting object. An already available exact count of abelian two-planes in the six-dimensional niltriangular Lie algebra is background; the present claim concerns only nonabelian two-generated subalgebras. No claim of absolute publication priority is made.

## Limitations
Only Lie generation by two elements is considered, and only the \(4\times4\) strictly upper-triangular algebra. Counts are by embedded linear subalgebras over finite fields, not conjugacy orbits, associative generated subalgebras, subgroup generation in unitriangular groups, or arbitrary scalar-extension schemes. The formulas do not enumerate three-generator subalgebras. A bounded literature search cannot exclude unidentified or indirectly stronger results. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
