# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The tame \((\mathbb Z/2)^5\)-character decomposition produces fifteen genus-one and one genus-two factors via an isogeny of degree a power of two, so it preserves \(3\)-primary invariants. The harmonic-quadruple uniqueness argument bounds their number by three. Normalizing any harmonic quadruple to \(\{\infty,0,1,-1\}\) gives the residual genus-two Cartier matrix with determinant \(-(u-v)^2\ne0\). Rank and \(a\)-number formulas follow. Exact field arithmetic in `verify.py` certifies examples of all six allowed \((h,r)\) pairs; the universal bound does not depend on enumeration.

## Originality — PASS, residual risk
Carvacho–Hidalgo–Quispe, arXiv:1507.02903, §5.3 and Theorem 5.3, already derive the entire complex fifteen-elliptic-plus-genus-two decomposition. This is explicitly outside the novel contribution. The characteristic-three compatibility lemma and complete six-profile rank/\(a\)-number classification are not stated in the inspected relevant primary sections or in the inspected literature comparisons. Searches for generalized Fermat type \((2,5)\), genus \(17\), characteristic \(3\) rank spectra, harmonic four-subsets, and genus-two ordinarity showed no covering result. Searches alone do not establish priority. An equivalent statement under different moduli notation could exist; a full-text comparison with Yamauchi (2008) was unavailable (its available abstract concerns number fields).

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a complete positive-characteristic arithmetic classification for a natural three-dimensional family of genus-seventeen curves. The genus-two compatibility is a nontrivial structural obstruction, not a replacement of a parameter in the known five-branch result. Six explicit profiles occur, and the same \(3\)-rank \(15\) has two possible \(a\)-numbers despite the same Newton polygon, distinguishing information invisible to the rank alone.

## Scientific limitations
The result does not enumerate isomorphism classes, deal with branch collisions, or furnish a classification for other characteristics. Finite examples support attainment only, while the universal theorem rests on the character and Cartier arguments. The relevant complex source was inspected in its exact decomposition section; the comparison does not certify absence of equivalent claims in other literature.
