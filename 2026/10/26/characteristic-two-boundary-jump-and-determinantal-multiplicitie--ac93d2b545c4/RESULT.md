# Characteristic-two boundary jump and determinantal multiplicities in the projected Togliatti surface

## Finding

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic, and let \(Y\subset\mathbb P^5_k\) be the image of the blowup of \(\mathbb P^2_k\) at its three coordinate vertices by the six cubic sections
\[
[x:y:z]\longmapsto[x^2y:x^2z:xy^2:y^2z:xz^2:yz^2].
\]
This map is an embedding; \(Y\) is the smooth degree-six toric del Pezzo surface in its classical projected Togliatti embedding. Write \(L=\mathcal O_Y(1)\), and let \(j_2:W\otimes\mathcal O_Y\to\mathcal P_Y^2(L)\) be the order-two principal-parts evaluation of the displayed six-dimensional space \(W\). Principal parts mean Hasse--Taylor coefficients, not ordinary unnormalized second derivatives. Let \(D_r(j_2)\) be the determinantal subscheme defined by the minors of size \(r+1\).

**Theorem.** The jet map has rank at most five everywhere, and has rank five off the six toric boundary curves. Its two lower determinantal subschemes have the following complete scheme structures:

1. In characteristic \(2\), \(D_4(j_2)=E\) as schemes, where \(E\) is the **reduced** toric anticanonical boundary: a hexagon of six \((-1)\)-curves. The rank is four at every smooth point of \(E\), and three at the six vertices.
2. In every characteristic different from \(2\), \(D_4(j_2)\) is supported at precisely the six torus-fixed vertices, with local ideal \((a,b)^2\) at each. It is a zero-dimensional scheme of length \(18\), consisting of six first infinitesimal neighborhoods of length three. The rank is five away from those vertices and three at them.
3. In **every** characteristic, \(D_3(j_2)\) is exactly the reduced scheme of the six torus-fixed vertices, with local ideal \((a,b)\). No point has jet rank below three.

Thus the characteristic-two jump is a genuine change in the one-dimensional **degeneracy scheme**, not merely a change in a generic osculating rank. In characteristic zero, the rank stratification as a set was already established by Lanteri and Mallavibarrena; the claims here concern the determinantal thickenings and the characteristic-two extension.

## Assumptions and scope

Everything is scheme-theoretic over an algebraically closed field, with no restriction on \(\operatorname{char}(k)\). The second-jet map evaluates the six specified sections of \(L\), rather than the full seven-dimensional anticanonical linear series. At a point, the projective second osculating space has dimension one less than the jet rank. The rank conditions are defined using Fitting/minor ideals in local free bases; these are invariant under base changes.

The hexagonal toric polygon, expressed in the affine lattice of exponent pairs after \(z=1\), has consecutive vertices
\[
(2,0),(2,1),(1,2),(0,2),(0,1),(1,0).
\]
These are the six section exponents. Its adjacent primitive edges form lattice bases and define the six smooth toric corner charts; their boundary divisors form the anticanonical hexagon. The identification \(E=-K_Y\) is the standard toric boundary identity.

## Proof

**Cover by identical charts.** At the corner corresponding to \(x^2y\), put \(a=y/x\), \(b=z/y\). Dividing all six projective coordinates by \(x^2y\) yields, after reordering, the six affine coordinate functions
\[
1,\ b,\ a,\ a^2b,\ ab^2,\ a^2b^2.
\]
Because \(a\) and \(b\) occur as coordinates, this is a closed immersion of an affine plane chart. A lattice computation for every vertex of the displayed hexagon shows that differences to the two adjacent vertices form a lattice basis and that, in that basis, the remaining exponents are exactly
\[
(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(2,1),(1,2),(2,2).
\]
Consequently the six charts are identical after interchanging and permuting coordinates; they cover \(Y\). In each, the reduced boundary is \(ab=0\), and the torus-fixed vertex is \(a=b=0\).

**Hasse--Taylor matrix.** With rows indexed by \((0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(2,0),(1,1),(0,2)\), the order-two principal-parts matrix of the six coordinate functions is
\[
J_2=\begin{pmatrix}
1&b&a&a^2b&ab^2&a^2b^2\\
0&0&1&2ab&b^2&2ab^2\\
0&1&0&a^2&2ab&2a^2b\\
0&0&0&b&0&b^2\\
0&0&0&2a&2b&4ab\\
0&0&0&0&a&a^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The first three columns have an invertible top-left block, and the last three entries of those columns vanish. Thus elementary row and column operations identify \(J_2\) with a block diagonal matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(I_3,B)\), where
\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}
b&0&b^2\\
2a&2b&4ab\\
0&a&a^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The determinant of \(B\) vanishes identically as an integer polynomial. Therefore the jet rank is everywhere at most five. The ideal of \(5\times5\) minors of \(J_2\) equals the ideal of \(2\times2\) minors of \(B\), and the ideal of \(4\times4\) minors equals the ideal generated by the entries of \(B\).

**Minor ideals.** Expanding the nine minors of \(B\) gives, up to signs,
\[
2b^2,\ 2ab^2,\ 2b^3,\ ab,\ a^2b,\ ab^2,\ 2a^2,\ 2a^3,\ 2a^2b.
\]
If \(\operatorname{char}(k)\ne2\), they generate \((a^2,ab,b^2)=(a,b)^2\). If \(\operatorname{char}(k)=2\), the even-coefficient minors vanish and the remainder generate precisely \((ab)\). In all characteristics, the entries of \(B\) generate \((a,b)\), so \(D_3(j_2)\) is the reduced vertex. Since the six charts cover \(Y\), these local equations give the claimed global subschemes, with no unexamined points. In particular the characteristic-two locus is reduced, since \((ab)\) defines the reduced pair of coordinate axes.

Finally, \(\dim_k k[a,b]/(a,b)^2=3\) at each of the six distinct vertices; hence the length is \(6\cdot3=18\) in characteristic different from two. The minor \(ab\) makes \(B\) rank at least two on the torus. On either boundary component away from its vertex, \(B\) has rank one in characteristic two and rank two otherwise. At a vertex \(B=0\), so the jet rank is three. All rank and length assertions follow.

## Verification

The included standalone `verify.py` checks by exact integer arithmetic the unimodular corner charts and their common local exponent list, computes all nine minors of the displayed \(B\), checks the two determinantal ideal descriptions (modulo \(2\) and with \(2\) invertible), and samples fiber ranks in characteristics \(2,3,5,7,11\). Running `python3 verify.py` prints `VERIFY_OK`. The universal proof uses the displayed local polynomial identities and the full six-chart cover; the sampled ranks are checks, not substitutes for its all-characteristics argument.

## Relationship to prior work

Lanteri--Mallavibarrena (2001, Section 4, Proposition 4.3) prove the complex set-theoretic jet-rank stratification \(5\) away from six vertices and \(3\) at those vertices for the projected degree-six del Pezzo surface; this rank statement is not new. Vallès (2006) revisits the Togliatti projection and its Laplace equation, and Mezzetti--Miró-Roig (2015) place it within the characteristic-zero theory of monomial Togliatti systems. Perkinson (1996) supplies a general toric principal-parts framework. Laface--Ugaglia (2019, Example 2.4) discuss the same toric polygon and its second fundamental form at a general complex torus point. These results do not explicitly determine \(D_4\) and \(D_3\) as local determinantal schemes or the characteristic-two boundary jump proved here; the distinction is one of statements and implications, not merely titles.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns the specific projected Togliatti embedding by the six displayed cubics, not all projections or all degree-six anticanonical embeddings. It is not a classification of all osculatory defects on toric surfaces. A potentially equivalent result in older specialized toric-inflection literature, particularly Perkinson's 2000 *Inflections of toric varieties*, cannot be excluded without further full-text comparison. No independent scientific audit has been performed.
