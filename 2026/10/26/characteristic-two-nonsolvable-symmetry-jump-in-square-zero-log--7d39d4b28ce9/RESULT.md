# Characteristic-two nonsolvable symmetry jump in square-zero log-canonical Poisson algebras

## Finding
Let \(k\) be **any field** and \(m\ge 2\). Equip the square-zero truncated polynomial algebra
\[
P_m(k)=k[x_1,\ldots,x_m]/(x_1^2,\ldots,x_m^2)
\]
with the log-canonical Poisson bracket determined by \(\{x_i,x_j\}=x_ix_j\) for \(i<j\). Let \(\mathfrak m=(x_1,\ldots,x_m)\) and let \(G_m(k)=\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{Pois}}(P_m(k))\). Its canonical cotangent representation is
\[
\rho:G_m(k)\longrightarrow \operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2)\cong\operatorname{GL}_m(k).
\]
**Theorem.** This map has a group-theoretic section and its kernel is nilpotent. Its image is exactly the diagonal torus \((k^\times)^m\) when \(\operatorname{char}k\ne2\), but the entire \(\operatorname{GL}_m(k)\) when \(\operatorname{char}k=2\). Consequently, \(G_m(k)\) is solvable for every field of characteristic other than two, whereas \(G_m(k)\) is **not solvable** over any field of characteristic two whenever \(m\ge3\).

Over \(\mathbb F_q\), the number of realizable cotangent matrices is exactly
\[
|\rho(G_m(\mathbb F_q))|=
\begin{cases}
(q-1)^m,& q\text{ odd},\\
\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(q^m-q^j),&q\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
For \(m=2\) the full number of Poisson automorphisms is \(q^2\) times the displayed cotangent-image count, for every prime power \(q\).

## Assumptions and scope
All automorphisms are unital \(k\)-linear associative algebra automorphisms preserving the stated Poisson bracket. There are no smoothness, algebraic-closure, or field-size hypotheses. The group is a group of \(k\)-points; no assertion is made about smoothness of its representing group scheme. The ring has \(k\)-basis of the squarefree monomials \(x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_r}\) with strictly increasing indices, with \(\mathfrak m^{m+1}=0\). The bracket is the quotient of the log-canonical Poisson bracket with constant skew coefficient matrix on the polynomial ring: \(\{x_i,x_j\}=\varepsilon_{ij}x_ix_j\), where \(\varepsilon_{ij}=1\) if \(i<j\), \(\varepsilon_{ij}=-1\) if \(i>j\), and \(\varepsilon_{ii}=0\). Jacobi holds on the polynomial ring and the ideal generated by the squares is Poisson-stable, as \(\{x_i^2,f\}=2x_i\{x_i,f\}\). This validates the quotient bracket even in characteristic two.

## Proof
**Cotangent restrictions outside characteristic two.** Every automorphism preserves the unique maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\). Write \(\phi(x_i)\equiv\sum_j a_{ij}x_j\pmod{\mathfrak m^2}\), with \(A=(a_{ij})\) invertible. Reducing the relation \(\phi(x_i)^2=0\) modulo \(\mathfrak m^3\) gives
\[
2\sum_{j<\ell}a_{ij}a_{i\ell}x_jx_\ell=0.
\]
For \(\operatorname{char}k\ne2\), squarefree quadratic monomials are independent, so each row has at most one nonzero entry. Invertibility implies exactly one per row and column. Thus \(\rho(\phi)\) is a monomial matrix, associated with a permutation \(\sigma\). For every \(i<j\), reduce \(\{\phi(x_i),\phi(x_j)\}=\phi(x_ix_j)\) modulo \(\mathfrak m^3\). Since nonzero leading scalars cancel, \(\varepsilon_{\sigma(i),\sigma(j)}=1\). In characteristic other than two this forces \(\sigma(i)<\sigma(j)\) for all \(i<j\), hence \(\sigma\) is the identity. Every diagonal scaling \(x_i\mapsto a_ix_i\) is itself a Poisson automorphism, giving both exactness of the image and a section.

**Characteristic two.** For arbitrary \(A\in\operatorname{GL}_m(k)\), put \(L_i=\sum_j a_{ij}x_j\). In characteristic two, \(L_i^2=0\), because cross terms cancel and the generator squares vanish. Furthermore, for each pair \(i<j\),
\[
\{L_i,L_j\}=\sum_{a<b}(a_{ia}a_{jb}+a_{ib}a_{ja})x_ax_b=L_iL_j.
\]
Thus \(x_i\mapsto L_i\) respects all defining multiplicative and Poisson relations. Its inverse is the linear substitution from \(A^{-1}\), so every invertible matrix lifts; the linear substitutions form a section.

**Nilpotence of the kernel.** For each \(r\ge2\), set \(K_r=\{\phi\in G_m(k):\phi(x_i)-x_i\in\mathfrak m^r\text{ for every }i\}\). The kernel of \(\rho\) is \(K_2\); moreover \(K_{m+1}=1\). Substituting \(x_i\mapsto x_i+\) terms of degree at least \(r\) changes any homogeneous degree-\(d\) expression only in degrees at least \(d+r-1\). Comparing the two orders of substitution therefore gives
\[
[K_r,K_s]\subseteq K_{r+s-1}.
\]
In particular \([K_2,K_r]\subseteq K_{r+1}\). The finite descending filtration is central, so \(K_2\) is nilpotent. The split image is abelian outside characteristic two, proving solvability there.

**Nonsolvability in characteristic two.** If \(m\ge3\), the linear-substitution subgroup contains the block-diagonal copy of \(\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\), because every characteristic-two field contains \(\mathbb F_2\). This group is nontrivial and perfect: its elementary transvections \(I+E_{ij}\) generate it, and for mutually distinct \(i,j,h\),
\[
[I+E_{ih},I+E_{hj}]=I+E_{ij}.
\]
A nontrivial perfect group is not solvable. Since a subgroup of a solvable group would be solvable, \(G_m(k)\) cannot be solvable.

**Counts and the two-generator endpoint.** The finite-field cotangent counts are the orders of the displayed torus and general linear group. If \(m=2\), the sole quadratic basis element is \(z=x_1x_2\), whose Poisson bracket with each generator is zero and whose product with \(\mathfrak m\) vanishes. Any Poisson automorphism with a fixed allowable cotangent matrix has precisely \(q^2\) lifts, obtained by independently adding \(c_1z\) and \(c_2z\) to the generator images. Such additions change neither the square relations nor the Poisson relation. No other nonlinear terms exist. This proves the stated full automorphism counts.

## Verification
The standalone program `verify.py` exhaustively enumerates generator images for \(m=2\) over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_4,\mathbb F_5\) using the relation checks \(X_i^2=0\), \(\{X_i,X_j\}=X_iX_j\), and invertibility of the first jet. Its respective full-group counts are \(24,36,2880,400\). It also exhaustively tests all invertible linear substitutions for \(m=3\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\); respectively \(168\) of \(168\) and \(8\) of \(11232\) matrices preserve the Poisson algebra. These finite checks do not establish the assertions for all fields or dimensions; those follow from the proof above. Run with `python3 verify.py`; the expected final line is `LOGCANONICAL_POISSON_JET_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work
Launois and Richard (arXiv:0805.4694, first posted 2008-05-30) study the two-variable truncated log-canonical Poisson algebra over \(\mathbb C\), calculating its Poisson (co)homology and twisted homology. Their full text contains the defining two-variable Poisson structure and characteristic-zero derivation formulas, but does not state the group-theoretic first-jet image or the characteristic-two nonsolvability transition. The present algebra agrees with their square-zero two-variable example at \(m=2\); the new statement concerns all numbers of square-zero generators and all characteristics, particularly the change in abstract automorphism-group solvability.

Related work on automorphisms of ordinary polynomial Poisson algebras over characteristic-zero fields (Turusbekova and Azieva, 2017) addresses a different, untruncated algebra. An indexed result about automorphisms of truncated coordinate-cross algebras concerns a different algebraic construction; only its descriptive summary was available, so no full-document noncoverage claim is made. The described result does not provide the specified log-canonical Poisson first-jet theorem, but an unforeseen reduction remains a literature risk.

## Limitations
The theorem determines the first-jet quotient and the solvability dichotomy, **not** the full higher-order automorphism group for \(m\ge3\); nilpotence of its kernel is established without computing its exact order. The \(m=2\) exact group counts do not extend by the same factor to larger \(m\). Published-priority searches are bounded and cannot eliminate every possible indirect appearance in the literature.
