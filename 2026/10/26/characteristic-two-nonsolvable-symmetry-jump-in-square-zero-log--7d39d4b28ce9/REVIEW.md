# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem fixes the ground field, the full squarefree quotient and its Poisson bracket. The degree-two comparison uses the independent basis \(x_ix_j\), and the sign of the log-canonical bracket forbids all nonidentity permutations when \(2\ne0\). In characteristic two an arbitrary invertible linear substitution satisfies the square and Poisson relations, providing an explicit section. A degree filtration proves nilpotence of the first-jet kernel, and a nontrivial perfect subgroup inside \(\operatorname{GL}_m(k)\) proves nonsolvability for \(m\ge3\). The two-generator exact counts use a complete quadratic expansion. The reproducible finite checks in `verify.py` corroborate cases of both characteristics but are not substituted for the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Launois–Richard, arXiv:0805.4694, was read in full: its two-variable square-zero log-canonical algebra supplies the historical object, but its conclusions are characteristic-zero Poisson cohomology and twisted homology, not first-jet automorphism groups or the characteristic-two solvability transition. The 2017 polynomial Poisson automorphism study describes a different, untruncated algebra; only its publicly available abstract was used. An indexed result on truncated coordinate-cross algebras describes another algebraic construction; its full text was unavailable, so that comparison is limited to the indexed description and is retained as a residual priority risk. Statement-level searches for square-zero Poisson cotangent actions, positive-characteristic linear Poisson symmetries and equivalent automorphism-group quotients returned no matching result. Searches cannot prove publication priority, and an overlooked generalized modular Poisson automorphism theorem is the remaining risk.

## Value — PASS
This is a structural solvability classification of the actual Poisson automorphism groups, rather than an isolated coefficient or formal sign check. The same natural algebra family has an abelian canonical quotient over every field outside characteristic two and a full general linear quotient in characteristic two; the latter contains a nontrivial perfect group in every generator dimension at least three. The resulting solvable-versus-nonsolvable boundary is uniform in the field and all dimensions, and the cotangent-image formulas give exact finite-field symmetry counts. The nilpotent-kernel theorem makes the quotient a genuine global group invariant, not merely an approximation to one.

## Limitations
The higher-order nilpotent kernel is not enumerated for \(m\ge3\); priority is not independently verified. There has been no independent mathematical audit.
