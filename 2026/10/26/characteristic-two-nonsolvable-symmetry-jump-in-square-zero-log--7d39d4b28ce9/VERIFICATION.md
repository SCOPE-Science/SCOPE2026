# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The square-zero ideal is Poisson-stable for the log-canonical bracket in every characteristic. An automorphism mod m^2 has invertible A; for char!=2 the squares of its linear images force each row monomial, and the quadratic bracket coefficient signs enforce an increasing permutation, hence diagonal. Every diagonal scaling lifts. In char2 all linear L_i square to zero and their brackets equal L_i L_j, hence every GL_m lift with matrix inverse. The filtration K_r of automorphisms trivial mod m^r satisfies [K_r,K_s] subset K_(r+s-1) by degree substitution, so K2 is nilpotent (ends at K_(m+1)). For m>=3 in char2, GL3(F2) embeds and is generated by transvections that are themselves commutators; it is nontrivial perfect and not solvable. For m=2 quadratic top monomial is central and annihilates the maximal ideal, giving q^2 higher-jet choices for each allowed first jet. All cases including F2 and char0 follow without algebraic closure; finite checks are independent corroboration.

## originality

PASS

The two-generator characteristic-zero truncated Poisson example of Launois–Richard is a genuine ancestor of the algebra, but focuses on (co)homology, not automorphism jet images. The arbitrary-field, arbitrary-m characteristic 2 lift and explicit nonsolvability transition were not found among materially read primary results; an equivalent structural theorem under squarefree-algebra terminology remains possible.

## value

PASS

The characteristic two change of automorphism quotient from a torus to GL_m and the resulting solvability jump are a natural sharp modular deformation in a Poisson algebra family. Explicit finite-field orbit-size data make the phenomenon structurally precise.
