# Chordal parity-check topologies of MDS codes are exactly the highly connected chordal graphs

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal C\subseteq\mathbb F_q^n\) be **any fixed linear MDS code** of length \(n\), dimension \(k\), and minimum distance \(n-k+1\), with \(1\le k<n\). For a parity-check matrix \(H\) of \(\mathcal C\), allowing redundant rows, form the **column-primal graph** \(P(H)\) on the labeled coordinates \([n]\): distinct vertices are adjacent precisely when some row of \(H\) is nonzero at both coordinates.

**Topology theorem.** A labeled chordal graph \(G\) on \([n]\) is \(P(H)\) for some parity-check matrix \(H\) of this **same code** \(\mathcal C\) if and only if \(G\) is \(k\)-vertex-connected. In fact, necessity of \(k\)-vertex-connectivity holds for all \(P(H)\), whether chordal or not. For the converse, take one dual parity check on every \((k+1)\)-vertex clique of \(G\); these checks span \(\mathcal C^\perp\), and their primal graph equals \(G\).

**Sharp interaction count.** Among chordal parity-check presentations of \(\mathcal C\),
\[
\min_H |E(P(H))|=kn-\binom{k+1}{2}.
\]
Equality holds **if and only if** \(P(H)\) is a \(k\)-tree. Every labeled \(k\)-tree occurs as an equality graph for the fixed code using exactly \(n-k\) independent dual checks, each of weight \(k+1\).

A \(k\)-tree starts from \(K_{k+1}\) and repeatedly adds one vertex adjacent to all vertices of an existing \(k\)-clique. A graph is \(k\)-vertex-connected when deleting fewer than \(k\) vertices leaves it connected (on more than one surviving vertex). The result includes the boundary cases \(k=1\) and \(k=n-1\).

## Assumptions and scope
The alphabet is an arbitrary finite field \(\mathbb F_q\) for which the specified \([n,k,n-k+1]_q\) MDS code exists. Coordinates and graph vertices are labeled. A parity-check matrix has rowspace exactly \(\mathcal C^\perp\), but may have dependent rows. The graph counts distinct unordered edges (pairwise co-occurrences in check supports), not the number of nonzero entries, arithmetic operations, or decoder runtime. A graph is chordal if it has no induced cycle longer than three. This theorem is about parity-check **representations**, not the normal-graph treewidth of a code.

## Proof
**1. Fixed-support dual dimension.** Write \(D=\mathcal C^\perp\), of dimension \(r=n-k\). Its MDS property says that projection of \(D\) onto any \(t\) coordinates has rank \(\min\{r,t\}\). Thus, for every \(T\subseteq[n]\), the subspace \(D[T]=\{d\in D:\operatorname{supp}(d)\subseteq T\}\) has dimension
\[
\dim D[T]=r-\min\{r,n-|T|\}=\max\{|T|-k,0\}.
\]
In particular, each \((k+1)\)-set \(T\) supports a unique one-dimensional family of nonzero checks. Every member of this family has support **exactly** \(T\), since \(D\) has minimum distance \(k+1\).

**2. Any primal graph is \(k\)-connected.** Suppose removal of a set \(S\) of size \(s<k\) disconnects \(P(H)\). Partition the nonempty remaining components into nonempty unions \(A,B\) of cardinalities \(a,b\). No row of \(H\) can meet both \(A\) and \(B\), because that would create a cross-edge. Its rowspace is therefore generated by vectors in \(D[A\cup S]\) and \(D[B\cup S]\). The preceding dimension formula yields
\[
 n-k\le\max\{a+s-k,0\}+\max\{b+s-k,0\}.
\]
The right-hand side is strictly below \(n-k\): if both summands are positive it is \((n-k)+(s-k)\); if only one is positive, it equals \((n-k)-b\) or \((n-k)-a\); if neither is positive it is zero. Contradiction. Hence \(P(H)\) is \(k\)-vertex-connected, with no assumption of chordality.

**3. Simplicial deletion preserves high connectivity.** A simplicial vertex in a graph has a clique as its neighborhood. Every nonempty chordal graph has a simplicial vertex. If a \(k\)-connected chordal graph \(G\) has at least \(k+2\) vertices and \(v\) is simplicial, then \(G-v\) is \(k\)-connected: a separating set \(S\) with \(|S|<k\) in \(G-v\) cannot be bridged through \(v\), because all surviving neighbors of \(v\) form a clique and hence belong to a single component of \((G-v)-S\). This contradicts connectivity of \(G-S\). Moreover, \(\deg_G(v)\ge k\).

**4. Every \(k\)-connected chordal graph is attainable over the *original field and code*.** Induct on \(n\). At \(n=k+1\), the only \(k\)-connected graph is \(K_{k+1}\), and its unique minimum-weight dual check spans \(D\). Otherwise choose a simplicial vertex \(v\). The puncturing \(\mathcal C'\) of \(\mathcal C\) at \(v\) is again an \([n-1,k]\) MDS code. Its dual consists exactly of the checks in \(D\) that vanish at \(v\), with that zero coordinate suppressed. Since \(G-v\) is \(k\)-connected and chordal, induction shows that checks on all \((k+1)\)-cliques of \(G-v\) span a dimension-\((n-1-k)\) subspace of \(D\). As \(v\) has at least \(k\) mutually adjacent neighbors, choose \(k\) of them. The check supported on these \(k\) neighbors together with \(v\) has nonzero \(v\)-coordinate, increasing the span to dimension \(n-k\), namely all of \(D\).

Each selected check is supported in a clique of \(G\), so the resulting primal graph is a subgraph of \(G\). Conversely every edge of \(G\) belongs to some \((k+1)\)-clique: by the same induction, edges not incident to \(v\) are covered in \(G-v\), while edges incident to \(v\) lie in its clique neighborhood of at least \(k\) vertices. Thus taking **all** clique-supported checks gives \(P(H)=G\), including if some checks are redundant.

**5. Exact edge minimum and equality.** If \(n=k+1\), the only allowed graph is \(K_{k+1}\), with \(\binom{k+1}{2}\) edges. For \(n>k+1\), choose a simplicial vertex \(v\). By Step 3, \(G-v\) remains \(k\)-connected and chordal and \(\deg(v)\ge k\). Induction yields
\[
|E(G)|=|E(G-v)|+\deg(v)\ge k(n-1)-\binom{k+1}{2}+k.
\]
Equality forces \(\deg(v)=k\) and equality for \(G-v\). Recursively, \(G\) is a \(k\)-tree; conversely every \(k\)-tree meets equality. Step 4 realizes each as a primal graph. A \(k\)-tree has exactly \(n-k\) maximal \((k+1)\)-cliques. Their corresponding minimum-weight checks form a basis of \(D\): in reverse order of the construction, the last-added vertex occurs in only its creation clique, certifying independence, and the argument repeats on the preceding tree. This gives an \((n-k)\)-row, weight-\((k+1)\) realization for every equality graph. \(\square\)

## Verification
A standalone Python script `verify.py` constructs generalized Reed--Solomon examples over prime fields and independently checks dual orthogonality, check-matrix rank, exact clique supports, graph edges, and vertex connectivity. It verifies 180 randomly generated \(k\)-tree representations, samples row-basis transformations, and exhaustively considers 480 full-rank bases of the dual of a \([4,2]\) code over \(\mathbb F_5\). It also enumerates all labeled simple graphs on at most six vertices for each admissible \(k\), selecting chordal, \(k\)-connected graphs and verifying that all their \((k+1)\)-clique dual checks span the exact dual and realize precisely the given edge set. In particular, it checks 13,302 eligible six-vertex graphs for \(k=1\), 3,036 for \(k=2\), and 276 for \(k=3\). The stored output terminates with `CHORDAL_REALIZATION_VERIFY_OK`. Exact, all-parameter correctness follows from the proof, **not** from this finite test.

## Relationship to prior work
Chen, Guo, Li and Zhu (first public October 13, 2026) introduce chordal parity-check codes and a systematic split-graph construction; they prove chordality survives column deletion and bound elimination complexity. They do not formulate the necessity-and-sufficiency classification of **every** attainable chordal topology for a fixed MDS code, nor the exact minimum edge count and all equality topologies. Their split-graph construction gives one instance of the upper bound, but not the converse or the characterization.

Han, Yildiz and Hassibi (first public May 9, 2026) establish general support-mask distance bounds and an MDS existence theorem for sufficiently large fields. These do not specify the column-primal graph or assert realization of all \(k\)-connected chordal graphs for an arbitrary **pre-existing** MDS code over its given field. Kashyap and Thangaraj determine the distinct normal-graph **treewidth of the code** for MDS codes; this is not the edge-sparsity of a parity-check column-primal graph. The elementary facts on MDS dual supports and chordal simplicial elimination are used as ingredients, not presented as novel by themselves.

## Limitations
The classification is for chordal **primal graphs**, with dependent parity checks permitted. It says nothing about minimum number of rows needed to realize a non-minimal-edge graph, finite-length decoding success probability, actual fill-in count, field-operation cost, or non-MDS codes. The graph edge count is an interaction-topology statistic and is not a measured decoder-speed guarantee. Originality is based on inspected accessible literature and mathematical-record comparisons; an equivalent formulation in unindexed or older work remains possible. Independent audit has not been performed.
