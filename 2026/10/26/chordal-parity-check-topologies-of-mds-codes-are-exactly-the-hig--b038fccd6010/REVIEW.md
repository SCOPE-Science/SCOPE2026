# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof's critical step is the exact shortened-dual dimension formula \(\dim D[T]=\max\{|T|-k,0\}\), a direct consequence of MDS projections. A separator of fewer than \(k\) vertices forces the dual rowspace to split between two supports whose combined dimensions are strictly smaller than \(n-k\), establishing necessary connectivity. Simplicial deletion then gives the converse inductively for the *fixed original code*, not merely for a newly constructed code over a larger field. The same induction gives the sharp edge bound and characterizes equality as \(k\)-trees; construction cliques yield an independent minimum-weight basis. Exhaustive graph and finite-field checks agree. Risks: computations are only finite tests; the full theorem relies on the proof.

**Originality — PASS, subject to residual historical coverage risk.** The October 2026 chordal-code paper proves the preservation/construction principles but not the graph-class characterization or extremal edge topology. The 2026 support-constrained parity-check paper gives generic mask bounds and large-field existence, not realizability of every allowed topology for each fixed MDS code. The 2011 code-treewidth theorem optimizes a different graphical complexity measure. Searches for equivalent and stronger formulations did not identify a covering theorem. These comparisons do not prove absolute novelty; possible older matroid or chordal-graph reformulations are a residual risk.

**Value — PASS.** An exact characterization of *all* possible chordal interaction topologies and *all* minimum-edge topologies for any fixed MDS code addresses the recently introduced chordal erasure-decoding representation model. It translates coding constraints into sharp graph connectivity and structural optimality, with no restrictions to a small alphabet or small length. A minimum-edge representation is a natural structural design benchmark, but does not itself certify an actual runtime speedup.
