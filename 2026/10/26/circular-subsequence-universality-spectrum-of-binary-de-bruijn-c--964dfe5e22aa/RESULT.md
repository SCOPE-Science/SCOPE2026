# Circular subsequence-universality spectrum of binary de Bruijn cycles of order five

## Finding
A **binary de Bruijn cycle of order five** is a circular binary word of length \(32\) in which every binary length-five factor occurs exactly once, counting factors across the circular boundary. Two presentations differing by rotation represent the same cycle. For a linear word \(u\), let \(\iota(u)\) be the largest \(k\) for which every binary length-\(k\) word occurs as a scattered subsequence of \(u\). For a cycle \(C\), define its circular universality index by
\[
\zeta(C)=\max\{\iota(u):u\text{ is a rotation of }C\}.
\]

**Exact finite classification.** Of the \(2048\) binary de Bruijn cycles of order five, exactly \(512\) have \(\zeta(C)=10\) and exactly \(1536\) have \(\zeta(C)=11\). No other circular universality index occurs. Thus exactly three quarters of these cycles admit a rotation containing every length-\(11\) binary word as a scattered subsequence, while no order-five cycle admits a rotation universal through length \(12\).

Refining the same classification, rotate each cycle to begin at its unique factor `00000`. The joint counts by the resulting linear index \(\iota\) and circular index \(\zeta\) are

| \(\iota\) | \(\zeta\) | Cycles |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 9 | 10 | 256 |
| 10 | 10 | 256 |
| 10 | 11 | 1152 |
| 11 | 11 | 384 |

For the group generated by bit complementation and reversal, the \(\zeta=10\) cycles comprise \(16\) orbits of size two and \(120\) of size four; the \(\zeta=11\) cycles comprise \(16\) orbits of size two and \(376\) of size four. These are refinements of the same order-five classification, not separate asymptotic statements.

## Assumptions and scope
Words use alphabet \(\{0,1\}\); a scattered subsequence preserves order but need not use adjacent positions. A *rotation* preserves all letters in one traversal of the length-\(32\) cycle; the definition does **not** permit arbitrary lengths of repeated traversal. All counts are of cycles modulo rotation, but **not** modulo complementation or reversal. The theorem concerns precisely de Bruijn order five. It makes no claim about the indices of all orders or the behavior of random arbitrary binary words.

## Proof
The proof is an exhaustive finite classification with a complete, independently re-enumerable search certificate, rather than an extrapolation from a sample.

**1. Exhaustive graph model.** Form a directed graph with the \(32\) five-bit strings as vertices. From vertex \(v\), the two outgoing edges lead to the five-bit suffix of \(v\) followed by bit `0` or bit `1`. Consecutive length-five windows of any binary de Bruijn cycle traverse each of the \(32\) vertices once, and consecutive windows differ by this graph's shift-and-append operation. Conversely, any directed Hamiltonian cycle on this graph determines a circular word: read the first bit of each vertex in cyclic order. Its windows are exactly the distinct visited vertices, so it is a de Bruijn cycle.

The all-zero window `00000` occurs uniquely. Consequently every rotation class has exactly one Hamiltonian representation rooted at vertex `00000`. The routine `cycles_via_windows` starts there, recursively tries both outgoing bits at each step, rejects only previously visited vertices, and after all \(32\) vertices have been visited retains the path exactly when an edge closes to the start. No valid Hamiltonian cycle can be pruned: a valid cycle never revisits a vertex before its closing edge. Nor is any cycle counted twice, because its start is fixed at its unique all-zero window. Direct execution yields \(2048\) distinct cycles, each independently checked for all \(32\) cyclic length-five factors.

As an independent enumeration check, `cycles_via_edges` traverses each of the \(32\) five-bit edges once in the order-four overlap graph, requiring return to the all-zero four-bit state. It obtains \(4096\) rooted edge tours (each circle twice), whose rotation-normalized outputs are exactly the same \(2048\) cycles. This also agrees with the classical binary de Bruijn count \(2^{2^{4}-5}=2048\).

**2. Exact subsequence index recurrence.** For a binary linear word \(u\) of length \(n\), write \(L_i\) for the shortest length of a binary word absent as a scattered subsequence from its suffix beginning at position \(i\) (zero-indexed). Take \(L_n=1\). For each bit \(b\), let \(j_i(b)\) be the earliest position at or after \(i\) containing \(b\), if it exists. Then
\[
L_i=\min_{b\in\{0,1\}}
\begin{cases}
1,& j_i(b)\text{ does not exist},\\
1+L_{j_i(b)+1},& j_i(b)\text{ exists}.
\end{cases}
\]
Indeed, a word starting with \(b\) is a subsequence of the suffix exactly when its remaining part is a subsequence after the *first* \(b\); taking the first occurrence cannot remove any possible embedding. The recurrence therefore considers every shortest absent initial bit and is exact by backwards induction. Its base case is the empty suffix. Thus \(\iota(u)=L_0-1\), because all binary words shorter than the first absent word are present.

**3. Full finite tally.** For each of the \(2048\) canonical cycles, the verifier computes \(\iota\) for **all \(32\) rotations** by the recurrence, takes their maximum, and increments the corresponding exact integer tally. It yields \(512\) at \(10\) and \(1536\) at \(11\), with zero elsewhere. It checks each value independently by the classical greedy arch count (scan left to right, end an arch as soon as both bits have appeared). The arch theorem establishes the latter count equals \(\iota\); the two calculations use different code and local invariants. The file `spectrum.tsv` lists all \(2048\) canonical cycles and their indices, and `verify.py` re-enumerates both graphs, recomputes every table entry, compares the entire supplied data file byte-for-byte, and checks the joint and symmetry-orbit tallies. Hence the finite enumeration includes every permitted object, not merely tested examples.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` in the directory containing `spectrum.tsv`. The program uses only the Python standard library, reconstructs the two independently rooted graph enumerations, checks canonical rotation and factor uniqueness, compares dynamic-programming and greedy-arch indices for every rotation, and checks every claimed count and the whole accompanying certificate. Its final line is `VERIFIED`. The supplied `spectrum.tsv` has \(73{,}768\) bytes and SHA-256 `23ee91707bce812a56925a20a464ade6ca7e32437b04182f33b031251a97dfaa`.

Examples demonstrating both cases are `00000100011001010011101011011111`, of circular index \(10\), and `00000100011001010110100111011111`, of circular index \(11\). For the smaller orders \(1\) through \(4\), the corresponding distributions are respectively \(\{1:1\}\), \(\{2:1\}\), \(\{3:2\}\), and \(\{5:8,6:8\}\). Order five is the first order exhibiting a nonuniform split among attainable circular universality indices.

## Relationship to prior work
De Bruijn's classical construction and enumeration concern coverage of **contiguous cyclic factors**. The scattered-subsequence universality index instead measures simultaneous coverage of all binary words after deletions. Hébrard's arch factorization and later analyses of scattered-factor and circular universality provide the general definitions and algorithms, including bounds relating an individual rotation to the circular index. These bounds do not themselves give the conditional \(512/1536\) distribution over the restricted set of de Bruijn cycles. The inspected primary works on circular universality mention de Bruijn sequences as motivation or describe algorithms for arbitrary input words; no order-five cycle table was identified in the inspected material. A previously published unrestricted binary-word universality distribution is not a distribution conditioned on the de Bruijn property.

## Limitations
This is a finite, computer-assisted exhaustive classification at one exact order, with two independently structured enumerators and reproducible source code. It is not an analytic all-order formula, nor a probabilistic asymptotic theorem. Correctness rests on the fully specified finite graph traversal and exact integer checks; the data alone would not establish exhaustiveness. No independent external audit has been performed. Full text of Hébrard's 1991 paper was unavailable during source comparison, although its abstract and subsequent descriptions of its arch method were accessible; specialized earlier enumerations or unindexed equivalent work cannot be excluded.
