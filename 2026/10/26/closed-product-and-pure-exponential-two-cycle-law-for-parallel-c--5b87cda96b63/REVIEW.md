# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The two-step Laplacian balance forces every vertex to fire exactly once per period-two orbit. The threshold interval at a vertex has exactly as many possibilities as incident firing-phase disagreements, producing the weighted edge-subset bijection. A two-child recursion yields a matrix recurrence and a scalar squaring factor, giving the exact product. Comparing the tail of its logarithm to the weighted infinite sum proves the precise asymptotic prefactor. The accompanying finite checker independently enumerates cut-edge configurations and direct graph transitions through height three; it does not replace the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The generic two-step firing-phase parametrization was previously used to count cycles on paths, and the classification of attainable periods on trees is classical. This contribution is narrowly confined to the full-binary-tree closed product, asymptotic exponential rate per vertex, and exact leading constant. Full text of Jiang (2010) and Jiang–Scully–Zhang (2012) was inspected for comparable formulas and no equivalent complete binary-tree census was found. The recent infinite-tree sequential-firing work of Inagaki–Khovanova–Luo has distinct update rules and boundary conditions. Bitar–Goles (1992) was inspected through accessible bibliographic and abstract materials only; possible stronger coverage in that text, or an unindexed equivalent, is a residual risk. Negative search results do not certify uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The full finite rooted binary tree is a natural expanding interaction network. The formula supplies an exact infinite-family count of nonlinear recurrent dynamics, and determines a non-obvious per-vertex entropy and positive asymptotic prefactor. This refines the generic statement that two-periodic orbits exist on trees, and is structurally different from the known Pell enumeration on paths. The claim does not rely on arbitrary parameter slicing or on computer-only enumeration.
