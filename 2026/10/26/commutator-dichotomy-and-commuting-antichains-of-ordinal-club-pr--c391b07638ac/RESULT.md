# Commutator dichotomy and commuting antichains of ordinal-club projections

## Finding
Let \(\omega_1\) be the first uncountable ordinal and let \(X=C_0([0,\omega_1))\), the Banach space of scalar-valued continuous functions that vanish eventually. For each club (closed unbounded set) \(D\subseteq[0,\omega_1)\), define the retraction
\[
\pi_D(\alpha)=\min\bigl(D\cap[\alpha,\omega_1)\bigr),\qquad P_Df=f\circ\pi_D.
\]
For any two clubs \(D,E\), the commutator has the exact dichotomy
\[
\boxed{\|P_DP_E-P_EP_D\|=0\ \text{or}\ 2.}
\]
More precisely, \(P_D\) and \(P_E\) commute if and only if both conditions hold:
\[
\pi_E(d)\in D\quad(d\in D\setminus E),\qquad
\pi_D(e)\in E\quad(e\in E\setminus D).
\]
If either condition fails, the commutator norm is exactly \(2\), the largest possible value for the difference of two contractions. There is also a continuum-sized family of pairwise incomparable clubs whose associated projections commute pairwise.

## Assumptions and scope
The scalar field may be \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\). A club is closed and unbounded in the ordinal space \([0,\omega_1)\) with its order topology. The maps \(\pi_D\) are the standard continuous club retractions; the induced maps \(P_D\) are norm-one projections on \(X\). The result concerns this canonical family only, not arbitrary projections on \(X\) or arbitrary operator commutators.

## Proof
Put \(\phi=\pi_E\circ\pi_D\) and \(\psi=\pi_D\circ\pi_E\). Direct composition gives
\[
P_DP_Ef=f\circ\phi,\qquad P_EP_Df=f\circ\psi.
\]
If \(\phi=\psi\), the commutator is zero. If they differ, choose \(\alpha\) with \(x=\phi(\alpha)\ne\psi(\alpha)=y\), and interchange the names if necessary so that \(x<y\). The sets \([0,x]\) and \([x+1,y]\) are compact and clopen in \([0,\omega_1)\). Hence
\[
f=\mathbf 1_{[0,x]}-\mathbf 1_{[x+1,y]}\in X,\qquad \|f\|_\infty=1,
\]
with \(f(x)=1\) and \(f(y)=-1\). Therefore the commutator applied to \(f\), evaluated at \(\alpha\), has absolute value \(2\). On the other hand, \(\|P_DP_E-P_EP_D\|\le\|P_DP_E\|+\|P_EP_D\|\le2\). This proves the norm dichotomy.

For the commutativity criterion, fix \(\alpha\) and write \(d=\pi_D(\alpha)\), \(e=\pi_E(\alpha)\). If \(d=e\), the two compositions agree at \(\alpha\). If \(d<e\), then \(e=\pi_E(d)\), and the compositions agree at \(\alpha\) exactly when \(\pi_D(e)=e\), equivalently \(e\in D\cap E\). Applying this at every \(d\in D\setminus E\) gives the first condition. The case \(e<d\) gives the second condition symmetrically. Conversely, the two conditions force agreement in each of the three cases \(d=e\), \(d<e\), and \(e<d\), so the retractions commute.

To exhibit a large commuting family, write every countable ordinal uniquely as \(\lambda+n\), where \(\lambda=0\) or a limit ordinal and \(n\in\mathbb N_0\). For each subset \(A\subseteq\{4k+1,4k+3:k\in\mathbb N_0\}\), let \(D_A\) contain all ordinals with even finite tail \(n\), together with those with odd tail \(n\in A\). Each \(D_A\) contains every limit ordinal and is unbounded, hence is a club. If \(d\in D_A\setminus D_B\), its finite tail is odd, so the next point \(\pi_{D_B}(d)\) has even tail and lies in \(D_A\cap D_B\); the symmetric condition also holds. Thus all \(P_{D_A}\) commute pairwise. Choosing, for each binary sequence \(s\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N_0}\),
\[
A_s=\{4k+1:s_k=0\}\cup\{4k+3:s_k=1\}
\]
produces \(2^{\aleph_0}\) pairwise incomparable sets \(A_s\), and therefore \(2^{\aleph_0}\) pairwise incomparable clubs with commuting projections.

## Verification
The norm estimate is exact: the compactly supported step function above gives the lower bound, and the triangle inequality gives the upper bound. The commutativity criterion follows pointwise from the least-point definition of each retraction. The club construction is checked by closure at limit ordinals and by the even-tail successor immediately following every odd-tail point not shared by a pair. No numerical experiment is used.

## Relationship to prior work
Kania, Koszmider, and Laustsen introduced these ordinal retraction projections in their study of \(C_0([0,\omega_1))\) and the Loy–Willis ideal, and used their complements in a bounded left approximate identity. Their stated operator-theoretic results supply the canonical family and its role, but the pairwise commutativity criterion and the continuum-sized commuting antichain are not among the results identified in the inspected statements. Matache’s work on distances between composition operators treats selected Hardy-space cases; it does not provide the club-specific criterion. The generic norm argument explains the \(0\)-or-\(2\) gap once the two point maps are known to differ, while the ordinal membership conditions classify exactly when they differ.

## Limitations
The result does not classify the full algebra generated by all \(P_D\), nor commutators involving arbitrary operators. The literature comparison was targeted rather than exhaustive; an equivalent club-specific criterion in an uninspected source remains a residual risk. Independent audit has not been performed.
