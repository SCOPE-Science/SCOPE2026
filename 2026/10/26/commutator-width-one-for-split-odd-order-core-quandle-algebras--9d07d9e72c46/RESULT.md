# Commutator width one for split odd-order core quandle algebras

## Finding

Let \(A\) be a finite abelian group of odd order \(N\), and let \(K\) be a field such that \(\operatorname{char}K\nmid N\) and every root of unity of order dividing the exponent of \(A\) lies in \(K\). Equip \(A\) with its core (Takasaki) quandle operation \(x\triangleright y=2y-x\), and let \(E=K[\operatorname{Core}(A)]\) be the nonassociative quandle algebra. Define \([u,v]=uv-vu\), and let \(E'\) denote the *nonunital subalgebra* generated by these commutators. Every element of \(E'\) is **one commutator**. Consequently

\[
\operatorname{cw}(E)=\begin{cases}0,&3A=\{0\},\\1,&3A\ne\{0\}.\end{cases}
\]

Here zero commutator width means \(E'=0\). The commutator-space dimension is \(N-|A[3]|\), where \(A[3]=\{a\in A:3a=0\}\). This dimension also follows from the previously established group-ring formula for the commutator subalgebra [3]; the new assertion is that no sum of two or more commutators is required in the specified split, nonmodular regime.

For instance, \(\operatorname{cw}(\mathbb F_{11}[R_5])=1\), whereas the known modular example \(\operatorname{cw}(\mathbb F_5[R_5])=2\) [2] shows that the restriction on characteristic cannot simply be removed.

## Assumptions and scope

All groups are written additively. The elements \(e_x\), for \(x\in A\), form the natural \(K\)-basis of \(E\), with \(e_xe_y=e_{2y-x}\). The coefficient field is allowed to have characteristic two, provided its characteristic does not divide \(N\) and the stipulated roots of unity are present. The result concerns arbitrary finite abelian groups of **odd** order, not arbitrary racks, non-split fields, or modular characteristic dividing \(N\). The commutator width is defined relative to the nonunital commutator-generated algebra, following [1,2].

## Proof

**Fourier multiplication.** Let \(\widehat A=\operatorname{Hom}(A,K^\times)\). By the splitting and nonmodularity hypotheses, \(|\widehat A|=N\), and

\[
F_\chi=\sum_{x\in A}\chi(x)e_x\qquad(\chi\in\widehat A)
\]

form a basis of \(E\). For \(\chi,\psi\in\widehat A\), substituting \(z=2y-x\) into the product yields

\[
F_\chi F_\psi
=\sum_{z,y\in A}\chi(2y-z)\psi(y)e_z
=N\,\mathbf1_{\{\psi=\chi^{-2}\}}F_{\chi^{-1}}.
\tag{1}
\]

Here the coefficient \(N\) denotes its nonzero image in \(K\). Put \(T\chi=\chi^{-2}\). Since \(N\) is odd, \(T\) permutes \(\widehat A\). If \(T^2\chi=\chi\), then \(\chi^3=1\) and already \(T\chi=\chi\). Thus each \(T\)-orbit has size one or at least three; the fixed characters are precisely those satisfying \(\chi^3=1\).

Write \(u=\sum a_\chi F_\chi\) and \(v=\sum b_\chi F_\chi\). Formula (1) says that the coefficient of \(F_{\chi^{-1}}\) in \([u,v]\) equals

\[
N\left(a_\chi b_{T\chi}-b_\chi a_{T\chi}\right).
\tag{2}
\]

In particular, every commutator has zero coefficient at each fixed character. Conversely, if \(\chi\) is not fixed, the commutator \([F_\chi,F_{T\chi}]=NF_{\chi^{-1}}\), since \(T^2\chi\ne\chi\). Hence the *linear span* of commutators is exactly the span \(W\) of the \(F_\eta\) with \(\eta^3\ne1\). Equation (1) also shows \(W\cdot W\subseteq W\), so \(E'=W\). This agrees with the already-known formula \(E'=L_{3A}\) of [3]. Counting characters of order dividing three gives \(\dim_K E'=N-|A[3]|\).

**Determinant-cycle lemma.** Let \(K\) be any field, and let \(\ell\ge3\). For any scalars \(c_0,\ldots,c_{\ell-1}\in K\), there exist vectors \(v_0,\ldots,v_{\ell-1}\in K^2\) such that

\[
\det(v_i,v_{i+1})=c_i\quad(0\le i<\ell),\qquad v_\ell=v_0.
\tag{3}
\]

To verify this constructively, first suppose \(\ell=2r\ge4\). Choose the even-index vectors \(v_0,v_2,\ldots,v_{2r-2}\) among \((1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\) so that consecutive even-index vectors, cyclically, are linearly independent. This is possible because a cycle of length \(r\ge2\) admits a proper three-coloring (for \(r=2\), use two distinct vectors). Each odd-index vector is then uniquely determined by its two prescribed adjacent determinants. Explicitly, if \(d=\det(a,b)\ne0\), the unique \(v\) satisfying \(\det(a,v)=s\) and \(\det(v,b)=t\) is \(v=(ta+sb)/d\).

Next suppose \(\ell=2r+1\ge5\). Choose \(v_0=(1,0)\) and \(v_{2r}=(0,-c_{2r})\) if \(c_{2r}\ne0\), or \(v_{2r}=(1,0)\) otherwise. Then \(\det(v_{2r},v_0)=c_{2r}\). Choose the intermediate even-index vectors on a path of \(r\ge2\) steps, each with one of the three displayed projective directions, so that successive even-index vectors are independent. A proper three-coloring of a path with arbitrary prescribed endpoint colors always exists when its length is at least two. All remaining odd-index vectors follow uniquely from the two adjacent determinant equations as above.

Finally take \(\ell=3\). If \(c_2\ne0\), choose \(v_0=(1,0)\) and \(v_2=(0,-c_2)\), then solve uniquely for \(v_1\). If \(c_2=0\) but \(c_0\ne0\), use

\[
v_0=(1,0),\quad v_1=(0,c_0),\quad v_2=(-c_1/c_0,0).
\]

If \(c_2=c_0=0\) but \(c_1\ne0\), choose \(v_0=0\), \(v_1=(1,0)\), \(v_2=(0,c_1)\); for three zero targets take all vectors zero. This proves (3) over any field, including characteristic two.

**One commutator suffices.** Let \(w\in E'\), and write \(w=\sum_{\eta\in\widehat A}t_\eta F_\eta\), where \(t_\eta=0\) whenever \(\eta^3=1\). On each nonfixed orbit \((\chi_0,\ldots,\chi_{\ell-1})\) of \(T\), impose the determinant targets \(c_i=N^{-1}t_{\chi_i^{-1}}\). As \(\ell\ge3\), the lemma produces \(v_i=(a_{\chi_i},b_{\chi_i})\). On fixed orbits set both coordinates to zero. These choices are independent across orbits. Form \(u=\sum a_\chi F_\chi\) and \(v=\sum b_\chi F_\chi\). Equation (2) and (3) yield \([u,v]=w\). Thus \(\operatorname{cw}(E)\le1\); it equals one if \(E'\ne0\), equivalently if \(3A\ne0\). This completes the proof.

## Verification

The standalone program `verify.py` implements the determinant-cycle construction and separately evaluates the original multiplication table \(e_xe_y=e_{2y-x}\). It tests 300 sampled determinant patterns for every cycle length from 3 through 14 over each of the prime fields of orders 2, 3, 5, 7, and 11. It then constructs Fourier witnesses and checks the full original-basis commutator identity for cyclic groups of orders 5, 7, 9, 15 and for products of cyclic groups, using 120, 50, or 40 seeded samples as detailed in the script. This is finite diagnostic evidence only; the proof of arbitrary length and group order is the algebraic argument above. The script prints `VERIFY_OK` on success.

## Relationship to prior work

Bardakov, Passi and Singh [1] introduced quandle-algebra commutator width and asked for dihedral and free quandles; their results give general bounds and isolated exact values. Sangare and Ta [2] computed many small finite-field cases and found width two for \(\mathbb F_5[R_5]\), but did not state an all-odd-order split-field width-one theorem. Zhang [3, Theorem 2.3] subsequently determined the *linear subspace/subalgebra* generated by commutators of any Alexander quandle algebra, specifically identifying the core case as the subgroup ideal associated with \(3A\). This already implies the dimension formula above; it does **not** determine whether an arbitrary element of that subspace is representable by a *single* commutator. The cycle-determinant surjectivity and the resulting width-one conclusion are the additional assertion here. More general unstated consequences of existing Fourier literature remain an originality risk.

## Limitations

The proof uses a character basis and division by \(|A|\), and therefore makes no claim about fields lacking enough roots of unity or with characteristic dividing \(|A|\). The theorem does not compute lengths in arbitrary non-split coefficient fields. The existing modular example \(\mathbb F_5[R_5]\) shows genuine width-two behavior outside the stated scope. Independent mathematical auditing has not been performed.
