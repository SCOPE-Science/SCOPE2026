# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem treats the exact graph \(K_{a+2}\cup_{K_2} K_{b+2}\) for all integers \(a,b\ge2\). Three explicit electrical dipole solutions and their part exchanges span all five edge orbits; substituting the potentials in the combinatorial Laplacian yields the specified dipole equations. Strict potential ordering proves all absolute-current signs in the common–private case. The diagonal resistance identity converts the cut column sums into the cycle column sums. Rational factorization identifies both phase curves, and the asymptotic sequence \(a=k,b=k^2\) establishes the sharp unattained suprema. Independent small-instance grounded-Laplacian inversions confirm the complete column classification. Computations are only supplementary evidence, not proofs for all parameters.

## Originality — PASS with residual bibliographic uncertainty

The closest mathematical reference, Dilworth–Kutzarova–Ostrovskii (2023, arXiv:2305.12582), gives invariant projections for Hamming graphs and an endpoint cut norm for binary cubes, not for cliques identified along an edge. Polettini (2014, arXiv:1405.0899) develops general complementary **oblique** graph projections and spectral matrices, without the family-specific Euclidean-orthogonal absolute-column norms. Published-research index searches using edge-clique sum, common-edge cliques, canonical cycle projections and their equivalent join formulation did not identify a covering statement. Existing complete-multipartite, single-deleted-edge and clique-join graph claims concern different graph families or different projection optimization questions. Some less-indexed network literature may contain an equivalent computation; a negative search is not a proof of global novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Edge identification is a natural, nontrivial graph composition operation, and induced endpoint projection norms encode worst-case total absolute electrical current, which need not behave additively under composition. The exact two-parameter classification proves a qualitative phenomenon: the maximizers of complementary canonical projections can occupy disjoint interface-to-private edge classes for infinitely many sizes. It also identifies two different algebraic phase curves and sharp global bounds \(3\) and \(4\), each one unit above the asymptotic counterpart for standalone cliques. The phenomenon is neither an isolated numerical example nor a mere graph-relabeling of an earlier covered family.

## Closest literature and limitations

The model theorem in arXiv:2305.12582 is a Hamming-cube calculation, and its Hamming-graph discussion concerns invariant projections, not this edge-gluing norm classification. The separate 2014 oblique-projection formalism does not imply the explicit norms. No claim is made about minimum norms among arbitrary projections, weighted graphs, intermediate \(\ell_p\) spaces or independent certification; obscure equivalent electrical-current formulas remain a bibliographic risk.
