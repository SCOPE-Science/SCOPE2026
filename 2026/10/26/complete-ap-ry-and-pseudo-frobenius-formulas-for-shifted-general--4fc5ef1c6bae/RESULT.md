# Complete Apéry and pseudo-Frobenius formulas for shifted generalized Jacobsthal semigroups

## Finding
For an integer \(b\ge2\), let \(U_0=0\), \(U_1=1\), and \(U_{n+2}=(b-1)U_{n+1}+bU_n\). Study the infinite numerical semigroup
\[S_b=\langle U_4+U_n:n\ge0\rangle.\]
Put \(c=b-1\), \(d=c^2+c+1\), and \(m=c(c^2+2c+2)=(c+1)d-1\). Define the greedy coin count \(\alpha(x)=k+q+r\) for \(x=kd+qc+r\), where \(k=\lfloor x/d\rfloor\), \(q=\lfloor(x-kd)/c\rfloor\), and \(0\le r<c\). For every \(b\ge2\),
\[ S_b=\langle m,m+1,m+c,m+d\rangle,\qquad \operatorname{Ap}(S_b,m)=\{\alpha(x)m+x:0\le x<m\}.\]
Its Frobenius number, genus, and pseudo-Frobenius elements are exactly
\[
F(S_b)=(3c-1)m-1,\qquad
 g(S_b)=\frac{dc(c+1)+(c+1)^2(2c^2-c+2)}2-(2c+1),
\]
\[
 \operatorname{PF}(S_b)=\{(2c-1)m+cd-1,\ (3c-1)m-1\}.
\]
In particular, the Cohen–Macaulay type is two. When \(b=2\) the four listed generators reduce to three distinct generators; when \(b\ge3\), all four are minimal.

## Assumptions and scope
The generating-index set includes zero and all nonnegative indices. A numerical semigroup is a cofinite additive submonoid of the nonnegative integers. Its Apéry set with respect to \(m\) consists of its minimal representatives modulo \(m\); the Frobenius number is its largest missing integer, the genus its number of missing nonnegative integers, and a pseudo-Frobenius number is a gap \(f\) satisfying \(f+s\in S\) for every nonzero \(s\in S\). The formulas quantify over every integer \(b\ge2\), without any upper bound on the generating index or on \(b\).

## Proof
**1. Four generators suffice.** The recurrence has closed form \(U_n=(b^n-(-1)^n)/(b+1)\), and \(U_a+U_{a-1}=b^{a-1}\). Direct substitution gives, for \(a\ge2\), \(t\ge0\),
\[U_a+U_{a+t}=bU_t(U_a+U_{a-1})+(1+(-1)^t)U_a.\]
For \(a=4\), each generator with index at least four therefore belongs to the semigroup generated by \(U_4\) and \(U_4+U_3\). Indices zero through three give \(m,m+1,m+c,m+d\), as claimed. Their gcd is one because of \(m,m+1\). Since \(0<1\le c<d<m\) for \(c\ge2\), all four are minimal: they lie strictly between \(m\) and \(2m\). The case \(c=1\) repeats \(m+1\).

**2. Optimal coin count and monotonicity.** Regard the offsets as coins of denominations \(1,c,d\). Their minimum coin count for value \(x\ge0\) is \(\alpha(x)\). For \(c\ge2\), in a minimum representation the number of unit coins is less than \(c\). At least \(c+2\) coins of value \(c\) could be replaced, using \((c+2)c=d+(c-1)\), by only \(c\) coins. Also \((c+1)c+1=d\), so \(c+1\) coins of value \(c\) can occur only with zero unit coins. These restrictions force the remainder below \(d\), and hence the greedy quotient–remainder representation. For \(c=1\) this is ordinary greedy change for denominations \(1,3\). Thus the count is optimal for every nonnegative integer. Write \(\beta(s)=\lfloor s/c\rfloor+(s\bmod c)\) for \(0\le s<d\). As \(m=(c+1)d-1\), if \(x=kd+s\) with \(s\ge1\), then \(x+m=(k+c+1)d+(s-1)\) and
\[\alpha(x+m)-\alpha(x)=c+1+\beta(s-1)-\beta(s)\ge c,\]
because adding one unit can increase a minimum coin count by at most one. If \(s=0\), then \(x+m=(k+c)d+(d-1)\), where \(\beta(d-1)=c+1\), giving difference \(2c+1\). Therefore, for all \(x\ge0\),
\[\alpha(x+m)\ge\alpha(x)+c.\]

**3. The Apéry set.** Every expression using \(m+1,m+c,m+d\) equals \(Lm+N\), where \(L\) is its number of coins and \(N\ge0\) its offset sum. An expression with residue \(x\), \(0\le x<m\), has \(N=x+tm\) for some \(t\ge0\), so its value is \((L+t)m+x\). By optimal change and Step 2,
\[L+t\ge\alpha(x+tm)+t\ge\alpha(x)+(c+1)t.\]
The minimum occurs at \(t=0\) and equals \(\alpha(x)m+x\). Extra copies of the generator \(m\) cannot improve this minimum. This proves the Apéry formula.

**4. Frobenius number and genus.** For \(0\le x<m=(c+1)d-1\), the quotient \(k=\lfloor x/d\rfloor\) is at most \(c\); its final offset \(s\le d-2\) when \(k=c\). The maximum possible \(\alpha(x)\) is \(3c-1\), attained at \(x=m-1=cd+(d-2)\). Since \(\alpha(x)m+x\) grows first with the count and then with the residue, this same value is the largest Apéry representative. The standard Apéry maximum formula gives \(F=(3c-1)m-1\). The genus equals \(\sum_{x=0}^{m-1}\alpha(x)\), because the Apéry element in residue \(x\) is \(x+\alpha(x)m\). For each complete block of \(d\) consecutive offsets, summing the coin counts \(\beta(s)\) over \(0\le s<d\) yields
\[\sum_{s=0}^{d-1}\beta(s)=\frac{(c+1)(2c^2-c+2)}2.\]
There are \(c+1\) blocks with quotient \(0,\ldots,c\), except that their very last term \(x=m\), with count \(\alpha(m)=2c+1\), is excluded. Summing the quotient contributions and subtracting the excluded term gives the stated genus.

**5. Pseudo-Frobenius elements.** A residue \(x\), \(0\le x<m\), yields a pseudo-Frobenius element \(\alpha(x)m+x-m\) exactly when its Apéry representative is maximal under addition of each minimal generator. For an offset \(e\in\{1,c,d\}\), if \(x+e<m\) this is equivalent to \(\alpha(x+e)\le\alpha(x)\). If \(x+e\ge m\), the condition is automatic: adding an \(e\)-coin gives \(\alpha(x+e)\le\alpha(x)+1\), while Step 2 gives \(\alpha(x+e)\ge\alpha(x+e-m)+c\). Since adding \(d\) without wrap increases the count by one, maximality forces \(x\ge m-d=cd-1\). The boundary residue \(x=cd-1\) satisfies the other inequalities, and gives \((2c-1)m+cd-1\). For \(c\ge2\), a residue \(x=cd+s\) with \(0\le s\le d-2\) must have \(s\equiv c-1\pmod c\) to avoid an increase on adding one; among these, adding \(c\) without wrap increases the count unless \(s=d-2\), namely \(x=m-1\). This latter residue gives \((3c-1)m-1\). For \(c=1\), direct examination of \(x=2,3,4\) gives maximal residues \(2,4\), the same formula. There are precisely two pseudo-Frobenius elements, completing the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Robles-Pérez and Rosales study the infinite Fibonacci-shift semigroup and its Apéry set (arXiv:2210.00816), but their recurrence has characteristic roots of different form. Komatsu, Laishram, and Punyani study generalized Fibonacci/Jacobsthal **triples** and generalized Frobenius numbers (arXiv:2304.00443), not this four-generator reduction of an infinite shifted semigroup. The later \(r\)-Fibonacci shifted-sequence treatment by López-Medel and collaborators restricts recurrence parameters to an ordered regime that excludes \((r_1,r_2)=(b-1,b)\), and explicitly identifies the opposite inequality as a future direction. The present result combines a new exact infinite-generator reduction with closed Apéry, genus, Frobenius, and pseudo-Frobenius formulas for that excluded regime.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns the fixed shift index four and nonnegative generating indices; it does not claim an analogous formula for every shift index. Full literature uniqueness is not certified, and possible unindexed earlier formulas or stronger general semigroup theorems require independent examination. No independent mathematical audit has been conducted.
