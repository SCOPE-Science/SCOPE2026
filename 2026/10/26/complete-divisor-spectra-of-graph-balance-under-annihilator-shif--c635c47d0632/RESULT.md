# Complete divisor spectra of graph balance under annihilator shifts in cyclic evolution algebras

## Finding

Let \(K\) be any field, \(m\ge 2\), and \(E_m\) the evolution \(K\)-algebra with natural basis \(e_0,\ldots,e_{m-1},z\), defined by \(e_i^2=e_{i+1\pmod m}\), \(z^2=0\), and \(e_ie_j=e_iz=0\) for \(i\ne j\). For any natural basis \(B\), let \(b(B)\) denote the balance of its directed support graph: the greatest common divisor of the absolute signed forward-minus-backward lengths of all closed walks.

**Theorem.** Up to reordering its elements, every natural basis of \(E_m\) is exactly
\[
 f_i=\lambda_i e_i+\alpha_i z\quad (0\le i<m),\qquad f_z=t z,
 \quad \lambda_i,t\in K^\times,\quad \alpha_i\in K.
\]
Conversely, every displayed family is a natural basis. For such a basis put \(S=\{i\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z:\alpha_{i+1}\ne 0\}\), with \(i+1\) reduced modulo \(m\). Then
\[
 \boxed{\ b(B)=\gcd\bigl(m,\{i-j:i,j\in S\}\bigr).\ }
\]
Here a set of integer representatives of the residues is chosen; the formula is independent of those representatives because \(m\) is included in the gcd. When \(S\) is empty or has one element, its difference set contributes no nonzero constraint and \(b(B)=m\).

Thus the **complete set of graph balances** attainable by varying a natural basis of this one fixed algebra is
\[
 \boxed{\ \{b(B): B\text{ a natural basis of }E_m\}=\{d\in\mathbb Z_{>0}:d\mid m\}.\ }
\]
The same complete divisor spectrum is attained using **only connected underlying support graphs**. The algebra dimension \(3\), obtained with \(m=2\), is the smallest possible for different positive balance values across natural bases, even with both graphs connected.

## Assumptions and scope

An evolution algebra is a not-necessarily-associative commutative \(K\)-algebra with a *natural basis* whose distinct basis elements multiply to zero. The directed support graph of a natural basis has an arrow \(u\to v\) whenever the coefficient of \(v\) in \(u^2\) is nonzero. A graph's balance is defined as in Alshatnawi–Costoya–Viruel, Definitions 2.2 and 2.4: an oriented edge contributes \(+1\) to a walk traversed forwards, \(-1\) when traversed backwards; take the gcd of the absolute totals over closed walks. Opposite directed arcs count as distinct edges, including for \(m=2\). A graph is connected when its underlying undirected graph is connected.

There are no restrictions on the characteristic or cardinality of \(K\). The index \(m\) is an arbitrary integer at least \(2\). We make no assertion about balance invariance for general degenerate evolution algebras beyond this explicit counterfamily.

## Proof

**1. All natural bases.** An element \(a=\sum_i a_i e_i+c z\) annihilates every element of \(E_m\) exactly when \(a_i=0\) for all \(i\): multiplying by \(e_i\) gives \(a_i e_{i+1}\). Hence \(\operatorname{Ann}(E_m)=Kz\). The quotient by this annihilator has basis \(\bar e_i\) and nonzero linearly independent squares \(\bar e_i^2=\bar e_{i+1}\). Write a natural basis as \(B=\{u_0,\ldots,u_m\}\). For distinct members \(u,v\), the relation \(uv=0\) in the quotient gives \(\sum_i u_i v_i\bar e_{i+1}=0\); independence forces \(u_iv_i=0\) for every \(i\). Consequently the coordinate supports of the images of any two different basis members are disjoint. At most \(m\) basis elements have nonzero quotient images. Since the images span the \(m\)-dimensional quotient, exactly \(m\) do, and each has support on exactly one different coordinate. The remaining member spans \(Kz\). This proves the displayed normal form. Direct multiplication establishes its converse.

**2. Support graph.** Squaring the normal-form generators yields
\[
 f_i^2=\frac{\lambda_i^2}{\lambda_{i+1}}f_{i+1}
 -\frac{\lambda_i^2\alpha_{i+1}}{\lambda_{i+1}t}f_z.
\]
All \(\lambda_i,t\) are nonzero, so the graph has every cyclic edge \(i\to i+1\) (indices modulo \(m\)), plus exactly the spokes \(i\to z\) indexed by \(S\); \(z\) is a sink. No cancellation across these distinct basis coordinates is possible, in any characteristic.

**3. Signed-walk lattice and gcd.** On any directed graph, assign an integer height \(h\) to each vertex and define an edge residual \(r(u\to v)=1+h(u)-h(v)\). The signed sum of the residuals along a closed walk equals its signed forward-minus-backward length, by telescoping; a backward traversal contributes \(-r\). If \(h\) is chosen so all edges of a spanning forest have residual zero, the integer subgroup generated by closed-walk signed lengths is exactly the subgroup generated by residuals on all remaining edges. Indeed each nonforest edge, concatenated with the unique tree return path, generates a closed walk of signed length equal to that residual. These arguments also apply to directed antiparallel edges.

If \(S=\varnothing\), the graph is the disjoint union of the directed \(m\)-cycle and an isolated sink, so its balance is \(m\). If \(S\ne\varnothing\), choose \(s\in S\), set heights \(h(i)=i\) for the representatives \(0,\ldots,m-1\), and \(h(z)=s+1\). The forest consisting of the edges \(i\to i+1\) for \(0\le i<m-1\) and the edge \(s\to z\) is a spanning tree and all its residuals vanish. The residual on the wraparound edge \((m-1)\to 0\) equals \(m\). The residual on a spoke \(i\to z\) equals \(i-s\). Thus the group of signed walk lengths is generated by \(m\) and the differences \(i-s\) for \(i\in S\). Pairwise differences \(i-j\) generate the same group modulo \(m\), proving the formula for \(b(B)\).

**4. Every divisor, preserving connectivity.** Given a divisor \(d\mid m\), choose \(\lambda_i=t=1\) and set \(\alpha_{i+1}=1\) exactly for the indices \(i\) in \(S\). For \(d=m\), take \(S=\{0\}\), giving \(b(B)=m\). For \(d<m\), take \(S=\{0,d\}\) using the distinct representatives between \(0\) and \(m-1\); then \(b(B)=\gcd(m,d)=d\). Since every selected \(S\) is nonempty, each support graph contains the whole directed cycle and has its sink attached by at least one spoke, hence is connected. Conversely the gcd formula ensures each balance divides \(m\); the divisor list is complete.

**5. Dimension-three witness and sharpness.** For \(m=2\), use \(B_2=(e_0,e_1+z,z)\) and \(B_1=(e_0+z,e_1+z,z)\). Both are natural bases of the same three-dimensional algebra with \(e_0^2=e_1\), \(e_1^2=e_0\), and \(z^2=0\). The first support graph has both cycle arrows and one spoke, hence balance \(2\); the second has both spokes, hence balance \(1\). Both are connected. In dimension at most two, a nondegenerate evolution algebra has basis-independent balance by Alshatnawi–Costoya–Viruel, Theorem 6.1. A degenerate two-dimensional evolution algebra with nonzero product has one-dimensional annihilator; relative to any natural basis there can be at most one vertex with nonzero square, so every positively balanced closed walk is supported on its only possible loop and has balance \(1\). If the loop is absent, there is no positive balance to compare. Zero multiplication also supplies no positive balance. A one-dimensional algebra trivially has no two different positive balance values. Therefore three is the minimal dimension for the stated phenomenon. \(\square\)

## Verification

`verify.py` reconstructs the structure matrices over small finite fields, tests all ordered natural bases for \((m,q)=(2,2),(2,3),(3,2)\), and checks predicted positive balances and connected-graph spectra. It separately tests each divisor-realising basis for every \(2\le m\le12\) and \(q\in\{2,3,5,7\}\), computing the signed-walk lattice via integer heights. It outputs `VERIFY_OK` when these finite tests pass. The general theorem is established by the proof above, not by extrapolation from finite tests. Execute `python3 verify.py` in a directory containing the extracted file.

## Relationship to prior work

Kaygorodov, Martín González, and Viruel ask explicitly in their 2026 survey, Question 27 (§2.2.4), whether balance is independent of natural basis for *degenerate* evolution algebras, and mark this unknown. The family here provides a negative answer, with a minimum-dimensional connected counterexample and complete attainable divisor spectra. Their Example 2 discusses the underlying perfect cyclic evolution algebra, but not annihilator-shift graph balance.

Alshatnawi, Costoya, and Viruel introduce the signed-walk gcd balance in §2 of arXiv:2607.15154v1 and prove natural-basis invariance in Theorem 6.1 specifically for **nondegenerate** evolution algebras. Our examples have nonzero annihilator, so their theorem does not cover them. Boudi, Cabrera Casado, and Siles Molina describe natural-family decompositions and changes of natural basis for degenerate evolution algebras, but do not compute this balance spectrum. The natural-basis rigidity argument above is proved here directly for the concrete family.

## Limitations

This is a statement about one explicit degenerate family, not a classification of all degenerate evolution algebras. The attainable balances for this family are exactly the positive divisors of its fixed cycle length, and do not describe balances in arbitrary digraphs. The finite-field checks are supplementary. Earlier broad results on canonical graph constructions or natural basis changes could implicitly yield parts of the gcd formula; novelty remains subject to independent expert verification.
