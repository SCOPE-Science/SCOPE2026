For every alphabet size \(q\ge3\), all one-deletion-separated pairs of words of length \(3\), \(4\), \(5\), or \(6\) maximizing their common two-deletion outputs are classified by exactly \(1,6,1,12\) universal equality patterns. The complete classification gives the exact labeled pair counts as falling-factorial polynomials and shows the abrupt change from a unique three-symbol pattern at length five to binary, ternary, and four-symbol extremizers at length six.
