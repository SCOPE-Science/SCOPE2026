# Complete-graph-or-cycle criterion for equilibrium in a nonlinear spin-flip sampler

## Finding
Let \(G=(V,E)\) be any finite simple undirected graph (possibly disconnected), fix \(J>0\) and \(0<s<1\), and put one spin \(z_i\in\{-1,+1\}\) at each vertex. Define the continuous-time single-spin-flip dynamics by
\[
 w_i(z)=\frac12[1-z_i\tanh(J\sum_{j\sim i}z_j)]
               [1-sz_i\tanh(J\sum_{j\sim i}z_j)].
\]
These are the strong-dephasing, uncalibrated population rates derived in Zhang–Chen–Kwek–Wang (2026); all rates are strictly positive. **The dynamics is reversible if and only if every connected component of \(G\) is a complete graph or a cycle.** Here cycles have at least three vertices, and isolated vertices and single edges count as complete graphs. Equivalently, every graph not of this form has strictly positive steady-state entropy production.

The classification is independent of the positive values of \(J\) and intermediate \(s\). For example, the three-vertex path already violates detailed balance, while the four-vertex complete graph is reversible despite having a genuine four-spin term in its stationary log weight.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is finite, loopless and without parallel edges; the same positive edge coupling \(J\) is used on every edge; external fields vanish; updates are continuous-time asynchronous single-spin flips with the displayed **strong-dephasing** rates. The result does not concern finite dephasing, feedback-calibrated channels, directed/weighted graphs, or synchronous measurement updates. The physical sampler parameter is \(0<s<1\); at the formal endpoints \(s=0\) and \(s=1\), and at \(J=0\), the effective log-response becomes linear and every graph is reversible.

Write \(z^i\) for the configuration with spin \(i\) flipped and \(N(i)\) for the neighbors of \(i\). A process is reversible if a probability \(\pi(z)>0\) satisfies \(\pi(z)w_i(z)=\pi(z^i)w_i(z^i)\) for all \(z,i\). Strict positivity makes the stationary distribution unique; in the absence of reversibility the standard stationary entropy-production rate is strictly positive.

## Proof
**1. Log-rate response and square affinities.** Put
\[
 F(a)=a+\operatorname{artanh}(s\tanh a),\qquad
 H(x)=F(J(x+1))-F(J(x-1)).
\]
Directly dividing the two single-spin rates yields
\[
 \log\frac{w_i(z^i)}{w_i(z)}=2z_i F\bigl(J\sum_{k\in N(i)}z_k\bigr).
\]
For an edge \(ij\), let \(A=N(i)\setminus\{j\}\), \(B=N(j)\setminus\{i\}\), \(R_i=\sum_{k\in A}z_k\) and \(R_j=\sum_{k\in B}z_k\). The log ratio of the products of rates around the configuration-space square \(z\to z^i\to z^{ij}\to z^j\to z\), versus its reverse, is exactly
\[
 \mathcal A_{ij}(z)=2z_i z_j[H(R_j)-H(R_i)].
\]
For nonadjacent sites this affinity vanishes automatically. Detailed balance forces every such square affinity to be zero. Conversely, vanishing on all squares lets one integrate the log edge-rate ratios to a potential on the spin hypercube: swapping two adjacent coordinate flips changes a path by one square, and cancelling two consecutive flips changes no integral. This gives path independence and hence a reversible measure. Thus the square test is necessary and sufficient.

**2. Strict response curvature.** The derivative \(F'\) is even, and for every \(a>0\)
\[
 F''(a)=\frac{-2s(1-s^2)\tanh(a)\operatorname{sech}^2(a)}
 {(1-s^2\tanh^2(a))^2}<0.
\]
Consequently \(F'\) decreases strictly with \(|a|\). The function \(H\) is even and, for \(x>0\),
\[
 H'(x)=J\bigl[F'(J(x+1))-F'(J(x-1))\bigr]<0
\]
because \(|x+1|>|x-1|\). Therefore the square test is equivalent to
\[
 |\sum_{k\in A}z_k|=|\sum_{k\in B}z_k|
 \quad\text{for every assignment of the other spins and every edge }ij.
\]

**3. Graph structure forced by the square test.** Set all spins in \(A\cup B\) to \(+1\). The displayed equality gives \(|A|=|B|\), so adjacent vertices have equal degrees. Each connected component must therefore be regular, of some degree \(d\). If \(d\ge3\), then \(|A|=|B|=d-1\ge2\). Squaring the equality for all independent spin assignments and comparing coefficients of each monomial \(z_kz_\ell\) shows that the unordered pairs contained in \(A\) and \(B\) coincide. Because each set contains at least two vertices, \(A=B\). Thus adjacent vertices have identical closed neighborhoods, \(N[i]=N[j]\). This property propagates along paths within a connected component, forcing every two vertices to be adjacent: the component is a clique. If \(d=2\), a connected finite simple graph is a cycle; if \(d=1\), it is a single edge; if \(d=0\), an isolated vertex. This proves necessity.

**4. Reversible weights on all permitted graphs.** On a cycle \(C_n\), \(n\ge3\), the rate ratio is generated by the Ising weight
\[
 \pi_{C_n}(z)\propto
 \exp\!\left[\frac{F(2J)}2\sum_{ij\in E(C_n)}z_i z_j\right].
\]
On a complete component \(K_n\), let the total magnetization be \(M=\sum_i z_i\). Define, for \(0\le k\le n\),
\[
 \Phi_n(-n+2k)=2\sum_{m=0}^{k-1}F(J(-n+2m+1)),
 \qquad\pi_{K_n}(z)\propto e^{\Phi_n(M)},
\]
where an empty sum is zero. If a positive spin is flipped, its neighbor field is \(J(M-1)\), and \(\Phi_n(M)-\Phi_n(M-2)=2F(J(M-1))\), exactly the required log-rate ratio. Products of these independent component weights give a reversible measure on every union of cliques and cycles. This proves sufficiency.

**5. Sharp small examples and non-pairwise equilibrium.** On the path \(1-2-3\), taking all spins positive in the square associated with edge \(12\) gives
\[
 |\mathcal A_{12}|=2[2F(J)-F(2J)]>0.
\]
Hence even an open three-spin chain is genuinely nonequilibrium. For \(K_4\) the weight has the explicit form
\[
 \pi(z)\propto\exp\left[A\sum_{i<j}z_i z_j+Bz_1z_2z_3z_4\right],
 \quad A=\frac{F(3J)+F(J)}4,\quad B=\frac{F(3J)-3F(J)}4<0.
\]
The nonzero four-spin term coexists with exact detailed balance, showing that higher-order effective interactions alone do not diagnose nonequilibrium currents.

## Verification
Run `python verify_graph_balance.py` with Python 3 and no third-party packages. The program independently evaluates both orientations of every elementary four-flip rate cycle on every labelled simple graph with one through five vertices (1,099 graphs total), checking equivalence with the claimed component classification. It additionally checks 608 exact form-of-detailed-balance residuals (numerically evaluated to tolerance \(5\times10^{-11}\)) for \(K_4\), \(C_5\), and \(K_6\), and checks the stated nonzero affinity on the three-vertex path and four-body coefficient on \(K_4\). The analytic arguments, not the finite tests, prove the theorem for arbitrary finite graph size.

## Relationship to prior work
Zhang, Chen, Kwek and Wang (2026), arXiv:2609.19591v1, derive the strong-dephasing spin-flip rates, the response \(F\), and detailed-balance behavior on a periodic one-dimensional chain, alongside explicit nonequilibrium currents on a square lattice. They also analyze complete-graph mean-field stationary distributions. Those formulas and special geometries are prior results; the contribution here is the **if-and-only-if classification on every finite simple graph**, including the strict open-chain obstruction and the distinction between a clique's reversible multispin interactions and genuinely irreversible non-clique geometries. Godrèche and Bray (2009) discuss Gibbs stationary states and reversible versus directed Ising spin dynamics on specific regular lattices, for a different family of rates. The general Kolmogorov cycle test is classical and is not claimed as new.

## Limitations
Only the strong-dephasing population Markov dynamics with zero fields, uniform ferromagnetic couplings, and intermediate partial-reset parameter is classified. The statement does not say that a nonreversible stationary distribution is non-Gibbsian in every possible generalized potential representation: every strictly positive finite-state distribution can be written as the exponential of some energy function. It proves **failure of detailed balance and positive steady-state entropy production**, not absence of a mathematical energy representation. No quantitative lower bound on entropy-production magnitude is claimed, and no classification of finite-dephasing quantum Lindbladian reversibility follows. Literature-wide priority is not independently certified.
