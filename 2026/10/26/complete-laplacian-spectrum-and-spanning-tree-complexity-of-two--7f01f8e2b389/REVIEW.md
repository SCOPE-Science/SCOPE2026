# Review of the complete two-token biclique spectrum

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem is an exact infinite-family claim for all integers \(m,n\geq3\). The token-graph layers, degrees, unsigned-incidence couplings and their orthogonal decompositions are explicitly defined. The identity \(B_r^{\mathsf T}B_r=(r-2)I+J\) gives full incidence rank for \(r\geq3\), hence accounts for every residual eigenspace, including its multiplicity. Both families of two-dimensional blocks and the constant three-dimensional block have independently checked characteristic polynomials. Multiplicities sum to \(\binom{m+n}{2}\), the only zero eigenvalue is simple, and the positive-eigenvalue product divided by \(\binom{m+n}{2}\) yields the stated spanning-tree integer. The standalone checker directly constructs the graph and compares all eigenvalues in 14 parameter cases; a separate modular determinant route verifies nine spanning-tree cases. Finite checks are supporting evidence, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The closest 2026 source (Song–Dalfó–Fiol–Zhang, arXiv:2610.00500, Corollary 4.14) obtains the largest-eigenvalue lower bound for two-token bicliques, rather than a full Laplacian factorization. The 2023 Reyes–Dalfó–Fiol–Messegué equitable-quotient argument already implies that endpoint is exact for bipartite token graphs, so no priority is asserted for that endpoint. The 2021 Dalfó et al. complement-commutation theorem provides a general tool, not the explicit aligned multiplicity list or a Kirchhoff product for \(F_2(K_{m,n})\). The 2026 Verma cograph theorem covers integrality without specifying this spectrum. The accessible full 2024 Reyes–Dalfó–Fiol article derives quotient spectral information for distance-regular host graphs, not complete multiplicities for this family. Comparisons against analogous formulas for biclique symmetric-edge-polytope graphs and other published spectral graph families find structurally different underlying graphs. The full 2024 Barik–Verma article could not be accessed; its abstract discusses bounds but cannot exclude unexamined formulas, so this is a real historical-priority risk. An undiscovered equivalent under the names symmetric square or two-particle exclusion graph is another residual risk. The claim is limited to the explicit full spectral list and spanning-tree formula, not known general methods or extremal eigenvalues.

## Value — PASS
A two-token biclique is a natural finite exclusion-process state graph with \(\binom{m+n}{2}\) states. Determining just its spectral gap, radius or integrality leaves most of its spectral information unspecified. The ten-family complete factorization provides all eigenvalue multiplicities uniformly over two unbounded part sizes and gives the exact tree-complexity product, a natural enumeration of connected spanning structures. The work resolves a meaningful instance of a current spectral-token-graph question and is not a numerical slice, relabelling, or merely a tiny improvement to an inequality. No new overarching theorem for all token graphs is claimed.

## Closest literature and limitations
Relevant full sections of arXiv:2610.00500, arXiv:2309.07089, arXiv:2012.00808, the full 2024 Reyes–Dalfó–Fiol journal article, and Verma's February 2026 open-access journal article were compared by mathematical implication. The 2024 Barik–Verma abstract was inspected, but the publisher full text and an attempted institutional retrieval were unavailable. The existing work implies several endpoints and integrality; the proposed remaining content is the entire explicit spectrum and tree count for two-token complete bipartite hosts only. A theorem for more than two tokens is not established. Historical priority and proof have not been independently audited.
