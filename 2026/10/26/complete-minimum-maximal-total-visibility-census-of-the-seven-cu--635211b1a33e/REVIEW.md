# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The distance-two characterization is both reproved and explicitly attributed to Bujtás–Klavžar–Tian, Theorem 6. Parity factorization converts the minimum-maximal problem into two independent dominating-set problems on 64-vertex halved cubes. Each closed neighborhood has 22 vertices; intersections of centers at distance four have six, and centers at distance six have none. The independence restriction forces distance-six pairs to form a matching. Inclusion-exclusion on four centers determines domination exactly when there are two distance-six pairs. Their two-pair geometry forces an affine plane, and the combinatorial double count yields 1,120 planes per parity class and 1,254,400 total sets. Independent exhaustive code verifies all small candidate sizes, all 1,120 halfcube optimum sets, and a direct shortest-path witness with all one-vertex extensions.

## Originality — PASS, conditional on accessible bibliography
The 2023 lower-visibility paper asks for hypercubes; the 2023 Hamming-visibility paper supplies the distance-two criterion. The pre-existing halved-cube domination table gives the scalar value four in the halfcube, so the scalar value eight for the seven-cube is not treated as novel. The reviewed contribution is the exhaustive *classification and number* of the smallest maximal sets: two arbitrary parity-affine planes of prescribed weights, with exactly 1,254,400 labeled unions. Searches for halved-cube minimum dominating-set enumerations, complete code coverings, equivalent affine-plane classifications and the precise count did not identify a dominating source. An unindexed classification remains a priority risk.

## Value — PASS
The source explicitly poses lower total visibility on hypercubes as a natural open direction. The seven-cube is the first small dimension where the per-parity minimum reaches four rather than one or two. The full equality-case classification counts and parametrizes all optimal greedy outcomes, revealing a rigid affine structure not derivable from the previously published scalar domination number. The result is a self-contained, exact natural cutoff rather than an arbitrary induced subgraph or a routine restatement of the prior table.
