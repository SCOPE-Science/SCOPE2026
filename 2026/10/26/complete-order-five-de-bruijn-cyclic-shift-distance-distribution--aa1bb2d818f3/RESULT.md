# Complete order-five de Bruijn cyclic shift-distance distribution

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal B_5\) be the set of binary de Bruijn cycles of order \(5\), counted modulo rotation but not modulo bit complementation or reversal. Each \(C=(c_0,\ldots,c_{31})\in\mathcal B_5\) is a circular word of length \(32\) whose cyclic length-five factors run through all \(32\) binary words exactly once. Define its minimum nontrivial cyclic shift distance by
\[
\delta(C)=\min_{1\le s\le31}\sum_{i=0}^{31}\mathbf 1[c_i\ne c_{(i+s)\bmod32}].
\]
The exact distribution is

| \(\delta(C)\) | \(8\) | \(10\) | \(12\) | \(14\) | \(16\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Number of cycles | 36 | 114 | 1212 | 682 | 4 |

In particular, the largest attainable minimum shift distance is \(16\), attained by precisely four cycles. With the unique cyclic occurrence of \(00000\) positioned first, their complete list is

```
00000100101000111110101100110111
00000101001100100011111011010111
00000111010110111110001001100101
00000111011001101011111000101001
```

These four cycles form one orbit under reversal and bit complementation (rotations remain identified). All four have the identical nontrivial Hamming-distance profile: distance \(18\) at shifts \(5,27\), distance \(20\) at shifts \(14,16,18\), and distance \(16\) at every other nonzero shift. Equivalently, their unnormalized bipolar autocorrelation has value \(-4\) at shifts \(5,27\), value \(-8\) at shifts \(14,16,18\), and zero at all remaining nonzero shifts.

## Assumptions and scope
A binary de Bruijn cycle of order five is a cyclic length-\(32\) word with each five-letter binary factor appearing exactly once, including wraparound factors. Distinct cycles are identified only by rotation. Distances refer to the entire cyclic word, not to linear prefixes and not to the distance between different de Bruijn cycles. The code in `verify.py` uses Python 3 and only its standard library. `certificate.csv` is a complete list, in lexicographic order, of canonical cycles, each accompanied by the 31 nontrivial shift distances.

## Proof
**Natural upper bound.** Each of the four ordered binary length-two factors appears exactly \(8\) times in every order-five de Bruijn cycle. In particular, the cyclic adjacent pairs \(01\) and \(10\) each occur eight times, so every such cycle has \(d(C,1)=16\). Hence \(\delta(C)\le16\).

**Exhaustive coverage lemma.** Every cycle has exactly one occurrence of \(00000\), giving a unique linear representation beginning with that factor. From this initial factor, iteratively append either binary digit, retaining an extension precisely if its newly created five-letter suffix has not occurred previously. Every de Bruijn cycle starting at \(00000\) follows one of these paths; no legal next digit is omitted. When the accumulated length reaches \(32\), check its four remaining wraparound five-letter factors. Accept precisely when all \(32\) cyclic five-letter factors are distinct. Any accepted word is a de Bruijn cycle, because there are exactly \(32\) binary five-letter words. Conversely, every such cycle is accepted by this complete binary search, and uniqueness of the starting \(00000\) removes all rotational duplication. This establishes a finite, fully exhaustive enumeration procedure, not a statistical sample.

**Count and spectrum certificate.** In `verify.py`, `cycles_dfs()` implements the preceding complete search using a 32-bit mask of already encountered five-letter factors. It yields \(2048\) pairwise distinct cycles. A second, structurally different enumeration, `cycles_euler()`, independently enumerates all \(2^{16}\) binary local edge-order choices on the sixteen-vertex de Bruijn graph of order four, constructs the associated Euler tours and canonicalizes their bit cycles. Each Euler tour can be generated by ordering the two outgoing arcs at each vertex in their tour order. Both independent enumerations give exactly the same sorted set of \(2048\) cycles. This also agrees with the classical count \(2^{2^{5-1}-5}=2048\).

For every enumerated cycle, `distances_direct()` computes \(d(C,s)\) by the 32 explicit comparisons for each shift \(1\le s\le31\). The independent bit-rotation and XOR-popcount implementation `distances_bits()` agrees on every cycle and shift. Taking the minimum gives exactly the five frequencies in the Finding table. The complete per-cycle distances are recorded in `certificate.csv`; the verifier regenerates all rows and checks the file byte-independent CSV content against them. The frequencies add to \(2048\), proving the exact distribution. As \(d(C,1)=16\), its top bin proves optimality, including that there are exactly four optimizers.

**Optimal-profile and symmetry assertions.** The checker identifies and writes the four canonical optimal words shown above, validates their full profiles, and verifies that their canonical complements and canonical reversals stay within the four-element set. Starting from the lexicographically first optimizer, the four combinations of reversal and complementation generate exactly those four words. Bipolar correlation \(R_C(s)=\sum_i(-1)^{c_i+c_{i+s}}\) satisfies \(R_C(s)=32-2d(C,s)\), proving the displayed autocorrelation profile.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` in the directory containing `verify.py` and `certificate.csv`. On a standard Python 3 implementation supporting integer `bit_count()`, the script asserts exhaustive agreement between the two cycle generators; validates every cyclic five-factor condition; recomputes distances independently by comparison and bit operations; checks the exact frequency histogram; verifies all optimal profiles, complementation and reversal orbits; and regenerates every CSV entry. Successful output ends in `VERIFY_OK`. The source and the full enumeration certificate are included with this result. The computational census is finite and exhaustive; no assertion is made for de Bruijn orders other than five.

## Relationship to prior work
The de Bruijn/Flye Sainte-Marie enumeration formula gives \(2048\) order-five binary cycles but does not distinguish them by cyclic shift distance. Spinsante, Andrenacci and Gambi (2011) analyze exactly these \(2048\) cycles for spreading-code applications and give average autocorrelation profiles and counts for thresholds on the *absolute* autocorrelation sidelobe, including \(12\) and \(784\) cycles at bounds \(4\) and \(8\). Those thresholds are different from the present one-sided maximum *positive* autocorrelation statistic, which is equivalent to the minimum Hamming shift distance. Fujisaki and Nabeshima (2011) establish general autocorrelation bounds and tabulate order-five worst and second-worst cases. Their worst positive sidelobe \(16\), attained by \(36\) sequences, agrees with the \(\delta=8\) bin here and is not claimed as new. Neither inspected primary paper provides the five-bin minimum-distance distribution, the full four-word positive-sidelobe optimizer classification, or the common detailed profile as a theorem. The exact census here is therefore a refinement, not a claim that autocorrelation of de Bruijn sequences was previously unstudied.

## Limitations
This is an exact finite order-five result, not a bound for all orders or a proof that similar optimizer symmetries persist elsewhere. The result concerns one-sided positive correlation: minimizing maximum *absolute* correlation is a distinct optimization problem, and negative sidelobes must not be ignored in applications where they matter. The historical literature cannot be considered exhaustively indexed; the possibility of an earlier equivalent computation remains. The computational proof relies on the stated exhaustive algorithms and certificate and has not received independent external auditing.
