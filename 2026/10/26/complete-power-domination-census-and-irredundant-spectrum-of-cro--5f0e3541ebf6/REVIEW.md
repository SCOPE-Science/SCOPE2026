# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For any selected pair from opposite sides of a crown graph, the closed-neighborhood step leaves at most two vertices, each with a witnessed unique-neighbor force. For a selection entirely on one side, the unobserved count after domination is \(w=n-|S|\); an observed vertex opposite the selection has either \(w\) or \(w-1\) remaining neighbors. This proves success exactly for \(w\le2\) (after separately checking singleton selections), and failure for \(w\ge3\). Monotonicity proves the complete mixed-set characterization. Direct enumeration of the disjoint families gives the bivariate polynomial and inclusion-minimal census. The standalone checker verified the original dynamics and all coefficient tables for \(4\le n\le8\), totaling \(87,296\) distinct vertex subsets. The formal proof, rather than finite extrapolation, supplies the infinite quantifier.

The classification of inclusion-minimal solutions gives an arbitrarily large \(n-4\) disparity between the least and greatest irredundant PMU selections, and \(n-5\) missing interior cardinalities for \(n\ge6\). These are natural structural features of the canonical complete-bipartite-minus-perfect-matching family, not parameter-renaming or a single numerical table entry.
