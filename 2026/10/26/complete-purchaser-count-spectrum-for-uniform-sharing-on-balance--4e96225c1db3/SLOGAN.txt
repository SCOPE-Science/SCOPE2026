In a uniform-capacity public-goods sharing game on \(K_{n,n}\), the possible equilibrium purchaser counts form one explicit interval together with the isolated count \(n\). At capacity \(n-1\) the entire spectrum is \(\{2,n\}\), but at capacity \(n\) it collapses to \(\{n\}\): increasing everyone's sharing capacity by one can force an arbitrarily larger number of purchasers even on a regular network.
