# Complete quadrangle-seeded colour refinement on quadratic Desarguesian planes

## Finding
For every prime \(p\ge2\), put \(q=p^2\), take the point--line incidence graph of \(\mathrm{PG}(2,q)\), distinguish the point and line sides, and give four points forming a projective quadrangle four separate initial colours. Let \(B\) be the unique embedded Baer subplane \(\mathrm{PG}(2,p)\) containing the quadrangle, and write \(b=p^2+p+1\). The final stable one-dimensional Weisfeiler--Leman colour partition consists of exactly \(4b\) cells: each of the \(b\) points and \(b\) lines of \(B\) is a singleton; for every line \(\ell\) in \(B\), the \(p(p-1)\) exterior points on \(\ell\) constitute one whole non-singleton cell; and, dually, for every point \(P\) of \(B\), the \(p(p-1)\) exterior lines through \(P\) constitute one whole non-singleton cell. No two displayed cells merge and none splits. Thus precisely \(2b\) singleton cells and \(2b\) equal-size non-singleton cells occur. The conclusion is independent of the quadrangle, for every prime \(p\), without a field-generator fifth point. No assertion is made for nonquadratic extension degrees or non-Desarguesian planes.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(p\) be a prime (so \(p\ge2\)), \(q=p^2\), \(G\) the bipartite incidence graph of the finite Desarguesian projective plane \(\mathrm{PG}(2,\mathbb{F}_q)\), and \(Q\) any ordered four-point quadrangle. Initially give all point vertices one colour and all line vertices another, then replace the four point colours for \(Q\) by four unique colours. In each synchronous refinement round the new vertex colour encodes its previous colour and the multiset of its neighbours' previous colours. Colour identifiers are arbitrary; partitions, not numeric identifiers, matter. A singleton means a class of exactly one vertex. Define \(B\) to be the unique Baer subplane through \(Q\), and \(b=p^2+p+1\).

## Proof
**1. The prime Baer subplane becomes entirely singleton.** Projectively normalize the quadrangle to the standard affine unit frame. Whenever two already singleton point vertices are distinct, their common neighbour is their joining line, hence that line becomes singleton after a refinement. Dually, the intersection of two singleton lines becomes singleton. Projective join--meet constructions from an affine unit frame implement the operations of addition and multiplication of coordinates (intersections with lines at infinity give parallels). Thus the points and lines obtainable from the four vertices contain the entire prime-field subplane \(\mathrm{PG}(2,p)\). A standard way to verify the coordinate assertion is to construct the x-axis scalar \(a+b\) by transferring two directed segments through a parallelogram, and \(ab\) by the intercept theorem using the unit point; each operation uses finitely many joins and meets. Starting from \(0,1\), repeated addition reaches every element of the prime field. Therefore after finitely many rounds every one of the \(b\) points and \(b\) lines of \(B\) has its own singleton colour. No uniform bound on the number of rounds is asserted.

**2. Baer uniqueness gives the candidate cells.** Each exterior point \(x\notin B\) belongs to exactly one line \(\ell(x)\) of \(B\), and each exterior line \(m\notin B\) contains exactly one point \(P(m)\) of \(B\). There are \(b\) choices for each. A line of \(B\) has \(p^2+1\) ambient points, of which \(p+1\) belong to \(B\), so exactly \(p^2-p=p(p-1)\) are exterior. The dual statement holds for exterior lines through each point of \(B\). Define the cells \(E_\ell=\{x\notin B:\ell(x)=\ell\}\) and \(F_P=\{m\notin B:P(m)=P\}\), along with all singleton vertices of \(B\).

**3. This partition is equitable.** An exterior point \(x\in E_\ell\) meets its one rational line \(\ell\) and no other rational line. For each rational point \(P\notin\ell\), the unique line \(Px\) is exterior with its unique rational point \(P\), so \(x\) is adjacent to exactly one vertex of \(F_P\). For each \(P\in\ell\), the line \(Px=\ell\) is rational, hence \(x\) has zero neighbours in \(F_P\). This accounts for all \(p^2+1\) neighbours of \(x\), since \(B\) has \(b-(p+1)=p^2\) points off \(\ell\). Duality supplies the same uniform statement for each \(m\in F_P\). Singleton cells trivially have constant neighbour-count vectors within their own cells. Hence the displayed partition is equitable and refines the initial colours.

**4. Exactness of stable refinement.** One-dimensional refinement is the coarsest equitable refinement of the initial partition. Thus it cannot split any displayed cell because they form an equitable refinement. By Step 1, rational points and rational lines are ultimately singleton. Two distinct \(E_\ell\) and \(E_{\ell'}\) must be separated because their vertices are adjacent to different singleton rational lines. Similarly, distinct \(F_P\) must be separated by their different singleton rational point neighbours. The point-versus-line initial colours separate opposite-side classes, and Step 2 separates non-singletons from all singleton rational vertices. Consequently the displayed partition is exactly stable. There are \(2b\) singleton and \(2b\) exterior cells, each exterior cell having \(p(p-1)\) vertices, for total \(4b\) colours.

## Relationship to prior work
Cofman's 1972 paper proves, in a far broader geometric context, that every quadrangle of a square-order Desarguesian projective plane lies in a unique Baer subplane, and recalls the relevant field-coordinate structure. Grohe--Kersting--Mladenov--Selman (2013) give the coarsest-equitable-refinement viewpoint and its general applications, without this particular explicit partition. Arvind--Köbler--Rattan--Verbitsky (2015) treat general graph isomorphism and colour refinement. A related earlier singleton-subplane criterion determines which classes have cardinality one, but does **not** determine the number or contents of non-singleton stable cells; the present result identifies every such cell and the entire equitable quotient. The new claim is precisely this complete colour partition, not the previously known Baer incidence or singleton facts.

## Limitations
The result concerns ordinary 1-WL with four separately coloured point vertices in a Desarguesian plane of prime-square order. It does not give a uniform iteration count, apply to arbitrary extensions, classify all non-Desarguesian planes, or prove that the theorem is absent from unindexed specialist sources. A related singleton criterion plus elementary Baer geometry supplies much of its input; the new quotient classification is a deliberately narrow but complete algorithmic invariant. Independent validation has not been performed.
