# Scientific review

## Correctness (C): PASS

The proof uses the full distance table for the subdivision-incidence graph, characterizes domination by compulsory inserted vertices inside the omitted-original subset, and derives the exact conditions for every selected vertex to have a witness at distance at most two. The only extra constraint is a nonempty cross-edge choice for one omitted original, two omitted originals, or one selected original. The four resulting configuration regimes are disjoint; binomial generating factors count every set exactly once. The minimum and exceptional \(n=4\) count follow by minimizing the exponent in each omitted-size case. A direct adjacency/distance checker exhaustively agrees for \(n=3,4,5,6\), totaling \(2{,}131{,}004\) nonempty subsets. This exhaustive finite experiment does not certify the all-\(n\) theorem, for which a proof is provided.

## Originality (O): PASS, subject to remaining bibliographic risk

Alikhani and Zaherifar (arXiv:2107.01424v1, full 10-page PDF inspected) count semitotal dominating sets in stars, complete bipartite graphs, friendship graphs, and other discussed families; the precise subdivision enumeration is not present in the inspected article. Cabrera-Martínez et al. (arXiv:2609.33932v1, HTML full text inspected) prove a general formula for the minimum semitotal number on subdivision graphs. That stronger scalar theorem covers \(\gamma_{t2}(S(K_n))=n\), which is explicitly excluded from the novelty claim, but does not determine the all-cardinality polynomial or multiplicity of minimum solutions. The operation-based work of Kartal Yıldız and Aytaç (DOI:10.1002/num.22746) was accessible as abstract/metadata only; the listed operations concern corona, join, modular and lexicographic products, and its full text remains a possible residual source. Semantic research-index and web searches for subdivision, incidence-graph and polynomial aliases found no decisive matching enumerator. Failed search cannot establish global uniqueness; unindexed equivalent enumeration remains possible.

## Value (V): PASS

The specific graph is a canonical graph subdivision and incidence graph; the cardinality enumerator is a natural refinement of the published semitotal minimum parameter, and distinguishes witness-feasible sets from ordinary dominating sets. The theorem counts every configuration, identifies the precise obstruction caused by two omitted original vertices, and yields a closed polynomial-size formula and exact minimum-set multiplicities, including a genuine four-vertex exception. The new result concerns combinatorial multiplicity, not a routine restatement of the already known minimum.

## Limitations and priority risks

Scope is \(S(K_n)\), \(n\ge3\); no claim for arbitrary subdivisions or polynomial-root properties. The minimum parameter alone is covered by arXiv:2609.33932v1. Equivalent unindexed literature and the inaccessible full text of DOI:10.1002/num.22746 remain bibliographic risks. There is no independent validation.
