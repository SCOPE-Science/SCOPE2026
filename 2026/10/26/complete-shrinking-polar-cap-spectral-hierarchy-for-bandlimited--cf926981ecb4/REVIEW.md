# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The coefficient formula is derived from a unitary radial polynomial reduction, a rank-one leading term for each exterior power of the restriction map, and exact shifted-Jacobi norms and endpoint kernels. Dividing successive exterior-power norm asymptotics yields every eigenvalue, not merely a determinant or trace. The eventual extremal modes and the condition-number constant follow by comparing the finitely many strictly positive leading powers. The packaged exact-rational checker completed 291 assertions on bandwidths through eight. The general proof is analytical and does not rely on these finite checks.

## Originality — PASS (residual priority risk)
Simons–Dahlen–Wieczorek (arXiv:math/0408424) give the spherical-cap block problem and a different small-cap/large-bandwidth fixed-Shannon asymptotic. Michel–Plattner–Seibert (arXiv:2103.14650) develop spin-weighted concentration and commuting matrices, without presenting the fixed-bandwidth shrinking-cap hierarchy or these constants in the inspected text. Closely matched published-result and mathematical-literature searches found no theorem logically implying the complete power distribution and extremal constant. The 2018 Seibert thesis was only inspected at abstract level; an equivalent result in it or in less easily indexed literature cannot be definitively excluded.

## Value — PASS
The complete triangular spectral hierarchy quantifies which angular/radial modes become invisible at each small-cap power, and the exact least-eigenvalue constant gives a sharp inverse-problem conditioning law. This is materially stronger than a Shannon-number estimate or an operator matrix representation. It is a structural asymptotic classification for every bandwidth, not an arbitrary finite numerical example.

## Boundaries
The claim is for fixed scalar bandwidth on \(S^2\); neither growing bandwidth nor nonzero spin is covered. The cap-radius asymptotic is not a finite-radius eigenvalue formula. Independently reproduce the algebraic and analysis arguments before using as a certified published theorem.
