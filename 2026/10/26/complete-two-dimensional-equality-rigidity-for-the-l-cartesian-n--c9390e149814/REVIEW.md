# Self-review of two-dimensional Cartesian-radius equality rigidity

**Correctness (PASS).** The Pauli decomposition reduces the real parts of every complex \(2\times2\) matrix to a scalar plus a traceless Hermitian vector. Its exact eigenvalues give the angular integrand. The operator-sum lower deficit is precisely a vector-integral triangle defect. Equality requires either the scalar trace vanish or the two Cartesian parts be real-linearly dependent, as shown by the affine-line argument. For the upper bound, constancy of the angular norm is necessary and sufficient, and the scalar trace would cause a noncancellable cusp; the remaining constant-amplitude vector condition is exactly square-zero. Direct numerical quadrature tested 1200 matrix families, but the universal claims rely on the analytical argument. Boundary cases, including zero, scalar, trace-zero invertible, and nonnormal rank-one cases, are included.

**Originality (PASS, with risk).** The motivating source (Audeh–Moradi–Sahoo, arXiv:2610.04844v1, Theorems 4, 5, 17) gives sufficient equality examples, not the complete two-dimensional characterizations. The published *Exact rank-one overlap profile* obtains the integrated invariant for rank-one operators only; its full RESULT.md explicitly excludes higher rank and all higher-rank equality classification. The published *Cubic operator-norm stability* characterizes the different lower comparison with half the squared numerical radius, not the operator-sum lower bound. Kittaneh–Moslehian–Yamazaki's older formula is supremal, not this integrated invariant. Searches covered the precise \(L^2\)-Cartesian name, trace-zero aliases, nilpotent equivalent formulations, and operator-sum equality criteria. Residual risk: a pre-existing characterization in convex support-function language might specialize to these matrix identities.

**Value (PASS).** Equality classes are natural invariants for a newly introduced sharp operator bound. The complete two-dimensional description reveals an unexpected rank-two lower-bound mechanism (arbitrary traceless matrices, including invertible nonnormal examples) beyond the source's phase-Hermitian family and separates it cleanly from the square-zero upper class. These mechanisms cannot be read off from rank-one sharpness examples and supply exact tests for the first nontrivial matrix dimension. The conclusion does not resolve higher-dimensional equality.
