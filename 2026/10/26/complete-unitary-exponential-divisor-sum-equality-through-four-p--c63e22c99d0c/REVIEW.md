PASS. Multiplicativity reduces the total-prime-factor bound to three exponent patterns. The two-prime patterns reduce to the prime restriction \(p-1\mid2\). In the remaining three-prime pattern, rational inequalities force the squared prime to be smallest; the two modular divisibilities then force a common divisor \(t\mid2\), finally leaving only the composite candidate \(q=25\). Every cancellation uses positive integers and every edge case, including \(r=2\) and \(n=1\), is separately resolved. An independent exact-arithmetic program checks all applicable integers through one million and a separate necessary-divisor sieve.

## Originality
PASS within the documented literature search, with residual risk. Trudgian's original 2013 work poses the global equality question and lists examples but no bounded-\(\Omega\) classification. OEIS A236474 is a partial table, not a universal absence theorem. The published two-prime exponent-layer theorem covers \(p^a q\) but not the three-prime obstruction. Minculete and Lelechenko examine related divisor-function inequalities and distributions. Searches for equivalent phrasing and the first three-prime multiplicity pattern did not locate a stronger result; an unlocated result cannot be ruled out.

## Mathematical value
PASS. This is the first complete classification by the natural total-prime-factor invariant beyond the semiprime level, including the first possible three-distinct-prime nonsquarefree shape. The obstruction is not a finite computation or a simple instance of the known two-prime result; it provides a structural limitation on solutions of Trudgian's explicitly stated infinitude question. It leaves the next total-prime-factor levels open.
