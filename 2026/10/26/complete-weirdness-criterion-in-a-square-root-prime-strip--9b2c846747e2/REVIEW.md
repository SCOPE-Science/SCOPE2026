# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The divisor-sum identity and the full binary-coefficient reduction are established in the proof, including the upper limit on quotient coefficients. If both \(t=0\) and \(t=1\) were forbidden, the two terminal residues would force a difference \(\delta\) too short to satisfy the prime congruence when \(p>x(x-1)\). This yields \(a<q\), and the converse is proved independently. An exact standalone verifier reproduces all 48,596 eligible prime-pair comparisons in its stated finite domain. The all-index result does not depend on the finite test.

**Originality — PASS under inspected evidence.** Iannucci's general computable subset criterion and bounded-layer census do not themselves state the short-gap automatic exclusion of \(a\ge q\), nor the resulting rational interval and single-residue equivalence. The previously published three-quarter abundance bound is weaker and has no such criterion. Published example tables enumerate instances only. Full primary texts and topic searches were compared at the level of implications. There remains a risk that a different presentation of the same short-gap argument exists outside the searched corpus.

**Value — PASS.** The result proves a proposed abundance upper bound, stronger than the general published bound, across a structurally natural square-root prime strip of potentially unbounded size. Moreover it converts a potentially long subset-sum test into a uniform congruence-and-interval classification with actual non-Fermat-prime examples. Neither a tiny numerical improvement nor a restatement of a known construction, it identifies a precise arithmetic obstruction.

**Limits.** The condition on the gap is essential to this argument and is not asserted optimal. Existence for infinitely many indices is not proved.
