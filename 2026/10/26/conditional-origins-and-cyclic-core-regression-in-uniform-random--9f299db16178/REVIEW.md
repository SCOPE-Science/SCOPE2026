# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The distinguished-origin deletion map preserves an independent uniform mapping on the remaining labels. Labeled rooted-forest enumeration gives the exact conditional factorial moments, hence the full conditional generating polynomial. Direct factorization proves strict antitone regression. The finite distribution of cyclic vertices and a Gaussian-tail bound establish Rayleigh convergence with uniform moments; a uniform quadratic bound legitimizes averaging the conditional expansion and yields the coefficient of \(\sqrt n\). A separate exact moment identity and exhaustive enumeration through seven vertices corroborate the calculations. The origin-degree convention includes self-images; edge cases \(k=n\), \(n=2\), and \(t=n-1\) are treated explicitly. No finite experiment is being used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS with residual risk

Arney–Bender (1982) treats origins and cyclic vertices, and Section 7 develops a stronger general generating-function framework. It explicitly marks origins and *cycle count*, not the present conditional cyclic-*vertex* regression, monotonicity, or \(\sqrt n\)-scale covariance coefficient. Hansen–Jaworski (arXiv:math/0603529v1, 2006) gives a stronger general result for the distribution of cyclic vertices with exchangeable indegrees; its proof can in principle encode the finite joint law by conditioning on zero indegrees. Accordingly the finite joint-law derivation alone is not asserted as independent novelty. The specific strict regression and quadratic Rayleigh-scale dependence required further analysis and were not stated or immediately supplied as specialized consequences in the inspected primary portions. The 2010 core-conditioned component-size work concerns different observables. Search coverage is not exhaustive; historical implicit derivations remain a genuine risk, as does incomplete full-text access for older Flajolet–Odlyzko work.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Cyclic-core size and number of indegree-zero vertices are canonical global and local structural observables in functional digraphs. The conditional law supplies exact finite-size prediction, the strict sign theorem rules out an intuitive competing clustering direction, and the second-order quadratic correction quantifies dependence on the natural Rayleigh scale despite vanishing correlation. These conclusions are not an arbitrary single-parameter calculation, table recomputation, or merely a restatement of classical marginal means.

## Scope and limits

Uniform independent images are essential. Only conditional-mean monotonicity is proved, not stochastic dominance. Higher-order refinements and nonuniform mapping models are not treated. Historical priority is provisional, and external independent scientific audit is outstanding.
