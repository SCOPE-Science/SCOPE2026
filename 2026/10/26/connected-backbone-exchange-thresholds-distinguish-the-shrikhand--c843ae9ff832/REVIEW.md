The graph definitions, domination predicate, selected-set connectivity, exact-cardinality vertex replacements and eccentricity conventions are unambiguous. A complete enumeration of all 65,535 nonempty subsets of each 16-vertex graph proves the minimum cardinalities, configuration counts and edges; all-source shortest-path searches at five vertices prove the stated diameters and radii. The Shrikhande optimum claw classification and rook complete-row/column classification give a conceptual check on the four-set cases.

## Originality — PASS, subject to stated risks
The main literature comparison distinguishes connected domination and fixed-cardinality token jumping from ordinary domination, total domination, and token addition/removal. Caro–West–Yuster and Chandran et al. investigate connected-domination and routing bounds; Suzuki et al. investigate the add/delete reconfiguration of ordinary domination; Lokshtanov et al. analyze connected-domination reconfiguration under add/delete, mainly complexity rather than exact graph metrics. Shirkol–Shekharappa–Gudgeri analyze minimum *total* domination and associated designs in the Shrikhande graph; the accessible abstract does not address fixed-size connected-domination exchanges. Their full text was unavailable, and the abstract is not used to certify a complete classification of connected sets. Available mathematical research records concerning the Shrikhande/rook pair address independent-set complexes and other invariants, not these exact connected-domination exchanges. Closely related earlier records on different rook-graph optimization parameters are likewise noncovering by mathematical implication. Unindexed equivalent work, or unexpected results in the inaccessible complete 2013 article, remain residual risks; absence of a search hit is not treated as uniqueness proof.

## Value — PASS
The two canonical nonisomorphic cospectral strongly regular graphs offer a mathematically motivated test of whether their connected domination landscapes behave alike. Both admit a twelve-leaf spanning tree and connected backbone size four, but the exact one-node substitution dynamics distinguish them sharply: the Shrikhande graph mixes at size five and is self-centered with diameter five, while the rook graph has a row/column orientation obstruction at five and mixes first at six. This supplies an exact, reproducible, finite benchmark in network-backbone reconfiguration and maximum-leaf spanning-tree optimization that scalar domination numbers and common spectra cannot resolve.

## Boundaries
The numerical and metric conclusions are finite and are proved by exhaustive integer-mask algorithms, not asymptotic inference. Earlier sources need not enumerate all exact solution configurations; their different models are compared explicitly. No claim of publication priority or external independent certification is made.
