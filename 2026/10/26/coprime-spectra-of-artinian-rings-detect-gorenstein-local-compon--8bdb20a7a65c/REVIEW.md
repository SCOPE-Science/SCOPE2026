# Scientific review: Artinian coprime spectra

## Correctness — PASS

The stated classification was reconstructed from the definition of the coproduct. In an Artinian local ring, the socle intersects every nonzero ideal; a socle line has annihilator equal to the maximal ideal. For any proposed coprime ideal other than the sole socle line, the explicit choices \(I=L\) and \(J=\mathfrak m C\) produce \(C\subseteq[I:J]\), while \(C\not\subseteq I,J\), using Nakayama. A one-dimensional socle is indeed coprime. Coordinatewise annihilators and complementary central idempotents settle the product case. The topology follows because distinct coordinate socles give singleton closed sets. Independent finite exhaustive computations tested twelve rings, including mixed-type products and integer quotients. No finite enumeration is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The September 2026 introduction of the coprime spectrum by Murphy-Hernandez and Leon-Rodriguez is the decisive prior source. It provides the definition, a negative nilpotent-socle example and a homeomorphism with the usual prime spectrum **for dual rings**. The present statement is stronger in a different direction: a complete criterion for every local Artinian ring, including the non-dual case, and for arbitrary products whose Gorenstein factors form a proper subset. The Stacks Project provides the standard Artinian decomposition and Gorenstein-socle equivalence, not the new coproduct classification. Searches for equivalent formulations, broader coverage and exact tables returned no matching classification. This supports originality relative to inspected material, but does not eliminate unindexed or indirectly implied prior results.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The new coprime spectrum is a natural ideal-theoretic invariant whose behavior on general Artinian rings was not determined by the dual-ring theorem. The result classifies an entire major ring class and specifies precisely what the new spectrum detects: Gorenstein local components, with emptiness and mixed-product cases included. The empty-spectrum phenomenon is not a consequence merely of one failed minimal ideal; the proof excludes **all** ideals in every non-Gorenstein local factor. This is useful when comparing the invariant to the ordinary prime spectrum and when examining how nilpotent structure is retained or lost.

## Closest literature and limits

- Murphy-Hernandez and Leon-Rodriguez, *The Coproduct of Ideals and the Coprime Spectrum*, arXiv:2609.23351 (2026). Dual-ring comparison, definitions and square-zero example are used as prior work. Full bibliographic abstract and directly relevant accessible HTML portions were inspected; a complete line-by-line inspection of all 19 pages was not available.
- Stacks Project, Tags 00J4 and 0DW6. The product decomposition and Gorenstein socle criterion are known inputs.
- The theorem does not cover positive-dimensional or general non-Artinian rings. An equivalent result under another lattice-theoretic name remains a plausible residual risk and warrants independent literature assessment.
