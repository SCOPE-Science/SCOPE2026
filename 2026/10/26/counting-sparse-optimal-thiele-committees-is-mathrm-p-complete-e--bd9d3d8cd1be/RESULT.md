# Counting optimal Thiele committees on cubic bipartite incidence elections

## Finding

Fix rational utilities \(u(0)=0\), \(u(1)=c>0\), \(u(2)<2c\), and \(u(3)<3c\). Counting all optimal committees is \(\#\mathrm P\)-complete under polynomial-time Turing reductions on the following restricted family: voters are the vertices of a simple cubic bipartite graph with \(n\) vertices on each side, candidates are its edges, a voter approves the incident edges, and the committee size is \(n\). Thus each voter approves exactly three candidates and each candidate has exactly two approvers. Every instance in this family has known optimal score \(2nc\), and its winning committees are exactly the perfect matchings. The map from the graph to the election preserves the count exactly. For proportional approval voting (PAV), \(c=1\) and the known optimum is \(2n\).

## Assumptions and scope

The graph \(G=(L\sqcup R,E)\) is finite and undirected, with \(|L|=|R|=n\ge3\), has no parallel edges, and is 3-regular. The election has \(2n\) voters and \(3n\) distinct candidates. A committee \(W\subseteq E\) with \(|W|=n\) has score \(\Phi_u(W)=\sum_x u(d_W(x))\), where \(d_W(x)\) is the number of selected edges incident to voter \(x\). No assumptions on higher utility values, nor monotonicity or concavity of the four specified values, are needed. There is no tie-breaking. The input may be a graph or its incidence election; the family can be recognized in polynomial time by reconstructing the graph from the candidate supporter pairs and checking the conditions. On unrecognized inputs the restricted counting function may be defined to be zero.

## Proof

**Score and equality.** The inequality \(u(d)\le cd\) holds for \(0\le d\le3\), with equality exactly at \(d=0,1\). Every edge contributes exactly two incidences, so \(\sum_x d_W(x)=2n\) and \(\Phi_u(W)\le2nc\). A committee attains this bound exactly when all \(2n\) voters have selected degree one: equality first forces each degree to be at most one, and their sum forces every degree to equal one. This is the perfect-matching condition. To see that it is attained, each \(S\subseteq L\) sends \(3|S|\) edges to its neighbors and each neighbor receives at most three, so Hall's condition \(|N(S)|\ge |S|\) holds. A matching saturating \(L\) is perfect as the sides have the same size.

**Complexity.** The cubic bipartite perfect-matching counting theorem of Dagum and Luby gives \(\#\mathrm P\)-hardness under polynomial-time counting reductions. To make the simple-graph and reduction conventions explicit, even a version of that source theorem allowing parallel edges suffices: replace every edge copy \(xy\), \(x\in L,y\in R\), by its own copy of \(K_{3,3}\) with an edge removed. Join \(x\) to the right endpoint of that removed edge and \(y\) to the left endpoint. The resulting graph is simple, cubic and bipartite. A perfect matching uses either neither attachment to a gadget or both, by balance of its two shores. There are \(6-2=4\) internal completions if neither is used, and \(2\) if both are used. The gadgets using both correspond to a perfect matching of the original graph, with parallel copies distinguished. Writing \(M\) for its number of edges and \(n\) for its original shore size, divide the new count by \(4^{M-n}2^n\) to obtain the original count. This is a polynomial-time counting reduction even if the theorem is taken in the multigraph convention. If the source is already simple, no gadgets are needed.

From the simple cubic bipartite graph use its incidence election directly: the exact equality condition is a bijection to the perfect matchings. This reduction step is parsimonious, not a claim that the overall hardness reduction from an unrestricted counting problem preserves counts. For membership in \(\#\mathrm P\), guess one bit for each distinct candidate, reject unless exactly \(n\) bits are set, and accept exactly when each voter has selected degree one. Every committee is counted on exactly one computation path. This proves completeness on the specified family. It also shows the separation between finding one optimum (polynomial by bipartite matching) and counting the tied optima.

**PAV example.** The first four PAV utilities are \(0,1,3/2,11/6\); both strict inequalities hold. For \(K_{3,3}\) there are \(3!=6\) optimal triples out of \(\binom93=84\).

## Verification

The accompanying `CHECK.py` is a standalone Python 3 standard-library program with exact rational arithmetic. It scores 87,837 committees on 12 simple cubic bipartite circulants, under PAV and three more utility tables (351,348 score evaluations). It checks the score bound and equality test for each committee, and independently counts the perfect matchings by permutations of a bipartition. The tests include 6 winners for \(K_{3,3}\) and 13 for the tested order-five shores. These finite tests corroborate the bound and winner correspondence; the inequality and Hall's theorem establish them for all graph sizes, and the source counting theorem establishes hardness.

## Relationship to prior work and limitations

Ai proves unrestricted PAV optimum counting complete for \(\#\cdot\mathrm{OptP}\). That result does not restrict both approval degrees as above. Janeczko and Faliszewski use the edge-candidate/vertex-voter encoding for counting winners of greedy rules; that existing encoding is not the result here. Lassota and Sornat give polynomial-time winner determination for at most two approvers per candidate for their Thiele rules with nonnegative, nonincreasing marginal weights. This includes PAV, and then makes the optimum test polynomial even on the wider domain; it does not give a count of all winners. The present theorem handles the stated rational utility tables, including nonconcave ones, on the **cubic bipartite incidence family**. No \(\#\mathrm P\) membership on all elections satisfying just the two approval degrees is claimed for arbitrary nonconcave tables. The hard inputs have unbounded committee size; planar perfect-matching counting is polynomial, and no hardness for the planar version is asserted. Historical priority beyond the compared literature is not certified.
