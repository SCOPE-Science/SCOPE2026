# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem quantifies over all integers \(m\ge2\) and \(\ell\ge4\) and uses ordinary graph cubes. The eigenvector construction is exact: three distinct path eigenvalues contribute independent \((m-1)\)-dimensional arm-antisymmetric eigenspaces. The polynomial matrix \(Q=A^3-s_1A^2+s_2A-s_3I\) is a valid real symmetric matrix for the graph cube. Distances two and three have entries \(-s_1\) and \(1\), while adjacent entries are \(\deg(u)+\deg(v)-1+s_2\) and are strictly positive. The separate \(\ell=4\) calculation rules out cancellation at the only borderline arm length. Hence \(M\ge3(m-1)\), and the explicit upper-seed forcing order gives \(Z\le3(m-1)\). The standard inequality \(M\le Z\) closes the proof. The checker tests actual small graphs but is not being used as an all-order certificate.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The closest 2026 paper proves the upper bound and explicitly leaves the all-power equality as Conjecture 10. Its Theorem 20 contains a general conditional polynomial technique, but does not verify the cubic support criterion uniformly for these spiders or state the sharp cubic conclusion. The new work supplies those missing hypotheses and derives the third-power all-parameter equality. The 2012 paper treats strict powers of trees; the Chang–Lin theorem treats ordinary squares of trees, which is why no square-power result is claimed here. Targeted searches of published mathematical findings, primary literature, and the accessible own claim ledger identified no equivalent cubic result. Unindexed or inaccessible equivalent literature remains possible; a failed search does not establish uniqueness.

This is the full two-parameter cubic-power case of a specifically posed 2026 open conjecture, not a finite table, arbitrary specialization, or a mere renamed inequality.
