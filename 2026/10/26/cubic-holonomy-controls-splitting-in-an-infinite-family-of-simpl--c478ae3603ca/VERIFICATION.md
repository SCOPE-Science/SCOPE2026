# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The b-squares span the a-space; the a-squares span the b-space exactly when det(I+diag(c_i)S)=1−(−1)^nP is nonzero. Only the identity and the full shift contribute to that determinant. Since −1 is not a cube root of unity in the allowed characteristics, this gives precisely the stated perfection test, including the excluded even n, P=1 case. The support graph is strongly connected. Multiplying a nonzero ideal element by a basis vector extracts a nonzero multiple of a basis square, and multiplication by a nonzero component's basis vector yields the next square along each arrow. All squares are thus in the ideal and they span the algebra when it is perfect. Independence of these squares also implies pairwise disjoint supports for the images of basis vectors under any automorphism, hence monomiality. Outdegrees distinguish the two layers, the b→a arrow pairs indices, and the cross-layer successor arrow forces rotations. More explicitly, any lift of rotation by k maps b_i to β_i b_(i+k) and a_i to β_i² a_(i+k). Square preservation gives β_i³=1 and β_(i+1)/β_i=c_(i+k)/c_i. For k=0 this is the central uniform μ₃; for k=1 choose β_i=c_i. Its lift φ winds to δ_P in n steps, so the group is generated by φ and the kernel with φ^n=δ_P. For P≠1 this is cyclic of order 3n; for P=1 it is the product C₃×C_n. A generator lift modified by t∈μ₃ has nth power δ_(P t^n), yielding exactly the asserted splitting criterion. In particular the two choices at odd multiples of 3 give perfect algebras on the same graph with genuinely different extension behavior.
