# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The complex gradient of the Poisson integral is the computed rational derivative. Its reciprocal numerator, strict nonvanishing on each smooth boundary arc or gap, and noncancellation at all eight endpoints prove an exact disk root count of three. The conformal Joukowski reduction transports multiplicities to one explicit real cubic. The discriminant and antipodal double-cover cases are proved separately, including the triple root. `check.py` verifies the algebraic identities, exact rational phase witnesses, and high-precision Fourier evaluations, without being used as a substitute for the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with specific historical risk
The closest theoretical antecedents are Walsh's 1947–1950 studies of harmonic-measure critical points, particularly his 1948 article on linear combinations. That highly relevant full text was not available for a complete implication comparison, and historical priority remains uncertain. Complete accessible Ortel (1975) and Dubinin–Vuorinen (2010) articles study, respectively, interpolation and extremal domain decomposition, not the asserted full four-arc equilibrium phase test. The nearest recoverable prior three-arc theorem has only two critical multiplicities on a different domain boundary partition. No directly covering four-arc table or theorem appeared in the documented formulation/alias searches. These are bounded, qualified originality grounds, not a certificate of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Four independently prescribed boundary electrodes produce exactly three interior equilibrium multiplicities. Determining whether the equilibria lie on the symmetry diameter or split into a conjugate pair is a natural phase classification. Both generic phases occur with exact nonantipodal rational witnesses, and a width-independent triple-collision point is explicit. This supplies a complete motivated parameter-dependent invariant, not an arbitrary finite slice.
