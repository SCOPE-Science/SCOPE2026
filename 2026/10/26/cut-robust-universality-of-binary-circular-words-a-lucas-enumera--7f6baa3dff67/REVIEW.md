# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The binary greedy arch count equals a maximum matching size in the path of unequal adjacencies. Cutting a cyclic run of \(\ell\) unequal edges decreases the maximum exactly at odd edges of odd-length runs, proving the structural theorem and the exact bad-cut count. Even transition runs tile uniquely into pairs; only the full even alternating circle has two possible pairings. A two-state transfer trace with this correction gives the claimed bivariate formula. Independent exhaustive enumeration checked all \(524286\) binary words of lengths \(1\) through \(18\), with actual subsequence-set calculations through length \(9\). Finite testing is supplementary to the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Barker et al. (arXiv:2003.04629, 2020, Lemma 20) established the one-arch circular bound; Fleischmann et al. (arXiv:2104.09063, 2021) and Schnoebelen–Veron (arXiv:2304.11932, 2023, Lemma 3.4) studied the general circular index and its algorithms. The reviewed materials do not provide the binary all-cuts transition-run criterion, exact bad-cut enumeration, or its fully index-refined Lucas series. An earlier exact cyclic-maximum enumeration and classical Lucas/matching formulas were compared by implication, not treated as novelties. The latter facts alone do not specify which words keep their index under every cut. Unindexed equivalent formulations, including possible discussions in broader survey or dissertation literature, remain the main originality risk.

**Value — PASS.** Circular sequencing has an origin-choice ambiguity: maximizing universality does not ensure that every linear read origin is equally universal. The theorem resolves this robustness question for every binary length, specifies the number of harmful cut origins for each individual word, gives all index-conditioned counts, and shows that robust words are exponentially rare. This is a natural completeness statement, not a small ad hoc parameter table.

**Scope and limitations.** Fixed binary alphabet; indexed words rather than necklaces; no claim for larger alphabets or contiguous-factor universality. The Lucas polynomial is classical; the claimed contribution is its proved interpretation as an all-cuts universality enumeration. No independent verification is represented.
