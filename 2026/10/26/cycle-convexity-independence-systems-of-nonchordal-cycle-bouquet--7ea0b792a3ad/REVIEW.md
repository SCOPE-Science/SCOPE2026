# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

A vertex enters the cycle-convex hull only by completing one of the specified cycle blocks; bridge vertices are never generated. The case distinction according to the center and the number of complete peripheral blocks is necessary and sufficient for convex independence. The proof then gives a growth-free side of every partition (Radon independence) and one deletion blocking every potential outsider (Helly independence). Expanding the disjoint cases yields the stated polynomial, optimum, and exact extremizer count. Exhaustive computations independently evaluate the original hull definition on finite instances.

## Originality — PASS, subject to stated residual risk

Nair et al. (arXiv:2609.34236v1) study the same three cycle-convexity invariants but their accessible text and described extremal results do not assert this entire nonchordal bouquet independence system or polynomial. Aráujo et al. (DOI:10.1007/978-3-032-27732-9_6) give rank algorithms for classes including chordal/starlike graphs and prove the maximum induced forest identity for the general-position number. Their general-position identity is reused, and numerical rank in the all-triangle chordal subfamily is not presented as new. Full author-report text was access restricted, leaving residual exact-overlap uncertainty. The assessed statement covers the full three-system polynomial for nonchordal cycle bouquets, not just an isolated rank value.

## Value — PASS

Helly, Radon, and convex independence need not coincide in general abstract convexity; showing their complete agreement and enumerating every cardinality on a natural nonchordal cactus family strengthens isolated extremal-parameter evaluations. The formula exhibits an unbounded separation of \(k-1\) between cycle-convexity general position and all three other invariants while counting every optimum exactly. The attachments show that the phenomenon is genuinely controlled by cycle blocks, rather than by pendant-tree size.
