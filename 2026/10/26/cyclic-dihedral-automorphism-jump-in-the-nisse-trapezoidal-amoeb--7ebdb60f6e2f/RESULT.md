# Cyclic–dihedral automorphism jump in the Nisse trapezoidal amoeba family

## Finding

Let \(m\ge6\) be an integer, and let \(a,b,c>0\) be real numbers with \(b\ne c\). For the smooth affine curve
\[
C_{m,a,b,c}=\{(z,w)\in(\mathbb C^\times)^2:(z^m-a)w=(z^m+b)(z^m+c)\},
\]
its full group of regular automorphisms as a complex algebraic variety is
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb C}(C_{m,a,b,c})\cong
\begin{cases}
C_m,&a^2\ne bc,\\
D_m,&a^2=bc,
\end{cases}
\]
where \(C_m\) is cyclic of order \(m\) and \(D_m\) is dihedral of order \(2m\).

Rotations are \((z,w)\mapsto(\zeta z,w)\) for \(\zeta^m=1\). Exactly when \(a^2=bc\), choose any \(v\in\mathbb C^\times\) with \(v^m=a^2\). An additional involution is
\[
\iota_v(z,w)=\left(\frac v z,-\frac{a}{z^m}w\right).
\]
It is the restriction of a monomial automorphism of the ambient algebraic torus, and it inverts the rotation generator by conjugation. The theorem classifies all automorphisms of the **abstract affine curve**, not just ambient restrictions.

## Assumptions and scope

The base field is \(\mathbb C\). All automorphisms are regular morphisms with regular inverses, not merely birational transformations. The hypotheses are \(m\in\mathbb Z\) with \(m\ge6\) and \(a,b,c\in\mathbb R_{>0}\) with \(b\ne c\); thus \(a,-b,-c\) are distinct and nonzero. The family and its non-Harnack contour geometry originate in Theorem 3.1 of Nisse's paper [1]. This theorem concerns additional intrinsic symmetry information.

## Proof

**1. Identify the punctures.** Put
\[
F(t)=\frac{(t+b)(t+c)}{t-a},\qquad T=\{a,-b,-c\}.
\]
The projection onto the first torus coordinate is an isomorphism
\[
C_{m,a,b,c}\simeq\mathbb P^1\setminus S,
\qquad S=\{0,\infty\}\cup\{z\in\mathbb C^\times:z^m\in T\},
\]
with inverse \(z\mapsto(z,F(z^m))\). Indeed, the denominator and numerator are nonzero exactly outside those punctures. The set \(S\) has \(3m+2\) distinct points. An automorphism of this affine curve extends uniquely to an automorphism of its smooth projective completion \(\mathbb P^1\); therefore
\[
H:=\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb C}(C_{m,a,b,c})
=\{g\in\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbb C):g(S)=S\}.
\]
It is finite because it injects into the permutation group of the finite set \(S\), and a projectivity fixing at least three distinct points is the identity.

**2. Reduce to the normalizer of a rotation.** The subgroup generated by \(r:z\mapsto\zeta z\), with \(\zeta\) a primitive \(m\)-th root of unity, is contained in \(H\). By the classical classification of finite subgroups of \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbb C)\), the possibilities are cyclic, dihedral, tetrahedral, octahedral, and icosahedral. In the last three groups the maximum element order is, respectively, \(3\), \(4\), and \(5\), so they are impossible for \(m\ge6\). In a cyclic or dihedral group, every element of order at least three lies in the distinguished cyclic rotation subgroup. Consequently \(\langle r\rangle\) is normal in \(H\), and every member of \(H\) preserves its fixed-point pair \(\{0,\infty\}\). Thus every element has one of the two forms \(z\mapsto uz\) or \(z\mapsto v/z\), with \(u,v\ne0\).

**3. All scalings.** The map \(z\mapsto uz\) stabilizes \(S\) precisely when \(u^mT=T\). Multiplication by \(u^m\) acts freely on the three-element subset \(T\) unless \(u^m=1\). If nontrivial, its permutation of \(T\) must be a three-cycle, making \(u^m\) a primitive third root of unity. But \(T\subset\mathbb R^\times\), so \(u^m a\in\mathbb R^\times\) forces \(u^m\) real, an impossibility. Hence \(u^m=1\), and the scaling subgroup is precisely \(\mu_m\).

**4. All inversions.** The map \(z\mapsto v/z\) stabilizes \(S\) precisely when \(t\mapsto d/t\) permutes \(T\), where \(d=v^m\ne0\). Since \(T\) has one positive and two negative real members, preservation first forces \(d>0\). The sole positive point \(a\) must be fixed, giving \(d=a^2\). The two distinct negative points must then be interchanged (they cannot both be fixed, as each would equal \(-\sqrt d\)), which is equivalent to \(bc=d=a^2\). Conversely these conditions make \(d/t\) interchange \(-b\) and \(-c\) and fix \(a\), so the inversion preserves \(S\).

**5. Lift to the curve and determine the group.** If \(a^2=bc\), an exact rational identity is
\[
F\left(\frac{a^2}{t}\right)+\frac a t F(t)
=\frac{(a+t)(a^2-bc)}{at}=0.
\]
With \(v^m=a^2\), the stated \(\iota_v\) preserves the graph equation. Its exponent matrix on the torus is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\-m&1\end{pmatrix}
\]
and has determinant \(-1\); the formula is therefore an invertible monomial torus map. A direct substitution gives \(\iota_v^2=\mathrm{id}\), and \(\iota_v r\iota_v=r^{-1}\). The preceding classification shows that there are no other automorphisms. If \(a^2\ne bc\), there is no inversion, giving \(C_m\). If \(a^2=bc\), the rotations and one inversion form exactly \(D_m\).

## Relationship to prior work

Nisse [1, Theorem 3.1] introduces this family and proves its smoothness, irreducibility, Newton nondegeneracy, embedded amoeba contour, equality of contour with boundary, and non-Harnack behavior. Those findings are **not** new here. The new invariant is the complete abstract regular-automorphism group and its sharp parameter-jump locus. A related paper by the same author [2] studies opposite contour behavior for different toric curves; its available full text does not classify the present punctured curves. Classical classification of finite subgroups of \(\operatorname{PGL}_2\) and the uniqueness of smooth completion are standard inputs; the particular invariant computation is not asserted to constitute a new group-theoretic principle.

## Limitations

No assertion is made for \(m\le5\), where exceptional finite projective symmetry groups require separate analysis. The positivity and distinctness hypotheses are essential for the three-point sign argument; arbitrary complex parameters can admit additional symmetries. The result is about complex regular automorphisms of the affine curve, not its birational self-maps or all automorphisms of a compactification. Historical priority has not undergone an independent literature audit; an unindexed older classification of this special puncture configuration could exist.
