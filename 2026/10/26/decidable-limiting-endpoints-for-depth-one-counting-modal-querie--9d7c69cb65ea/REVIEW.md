# Scientific assessment: decidable endpoints for shallow counting modal graph queries

## Correctness — PASS

The stated domain is finite-vocabulary, depth-one \(K^{\#}\), with independent labelled directed edges of fixed rational probability strictly between zero and one and independent full-support rational propositional types. The root-row decomposition is exact, including the self-loop. A finite elimination of current-state truth indicators reduces all tests to integer-affine inequalities in multinomial cell counts. The centered covariance \(\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\pi)-\pi\pi^{\mathsf T}\) is strictly positive definite because nonedges have positive probability and every type has positive probability. The law of large numbers resolves nonzero drifts; a joint central limit theorem and zero Gaussian hyperplane mass settle all balanced comparisons. Strict rational sign cells are feasible exactly when their unit-margin rational systems are feasible, so positive Gaussian density makes limiting probability zero/one equivalent to finite feasibility assertions. The irrationality proof uses the exact covariance and the algebraic-integrality obstruction for rational multiples of \(\pi\). The packaged standard-library checker independently reproduces exact finite formulas for three depth-one queries through five worlds, enumerates all graphs through three worlds for the main example, and verifies exact rational moments. Finite checking is corroboration, not proof of asymptotics.

## Originality — PASS within inspected literature

Ghosh and Schwarzentruber, *On Counting Graphs for Modal Logic with Counting*, arXiv:2610.26187v1 (2026), give general graph-counting semantics and prove convergence of an alternative limiting quantity in tree models (Theorem 28), with central-limit Gaussian calculations and a two-inequality orthant argument. **Convergence alone for depth-one graph queries can be obtained by an edge-mark translation of that tree theorem**, so that part is not claimed independent. The inspected paper's Section 7 leaves graph convergence and its decision aspects open and does not supply an exact rational-feasibility algorithm deciding whether the limit for every depth-one graph query equals zero or one. It supplies only the \(\#p\geq\#q\) finite graph census in Appendix A. The distinctive new contribution assessed here is the complete endpoint decision for arbitrary Boolean combinations and nested current-state indicators in this graph model, supported by a positive-definite full type-count covariance and a verifiable irrational graph example. The earlier primary papers on counting modalities and aggregate-combine graph logic concern expressivity and satisfiability; inspected GNN zero-one work restricts aggregation or excludes balanced boundaries. No searched published record was found to imply the complete endpoint classification. A general older result about polyhedral threshold events for multinomial counts remains a plausible priority risk; absence of a match does not establish uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

The graph convergence question is explicitly raised in the motivating 2026 paper. Distinguishing genuine almost-sure truth or falsity from nontrivial limiting probabilities, by terminating rational feasibility tests, is useful for evaluating large-graph one-hop counting queries and finite-step sum-aggregation classifiers. The theorem handles the otherwise delicate balanced-threshold cases and all indicator nesting at one modal step. Its explicit irrational example demonstrates that the qualitative algorithm is necessary: limiting truth need not be binary or rational. It settles an entire natural finite-depth boundary without claiming to settle general nested-counting convergence, and it provides reproducible exact finite formulas.
