# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For positive integers a,b the source is the product of reflexive finite chains on a and b labelled points, ordered coordinatewise; the target is the labelled four-point Boolean diamond B2. Order-preserving surjective p-morphisms satisfy the exact back condition f(up x)=up f(x). Express the two Boolean coordinates as upset threshold sequences, whose unique zero-region boundary has two differently singleton-coloured immediate covers at each maximal zero. The resulting unequal-threshold state normal form yields G_{0,M}=(1−z)^{−M−1} and G_{m,M}=z(sum_{v=m+1}^M G_{m,v}+sum_{u=0}^{m−1}G_{u,m}); solving H_m=(1+z)^{m−1}/(1−z)^{m+1} and extracting coefficients proves F(a,b)=sum_{k=2}^{min(a,b)}(2^k−2)binom(a,k)binom(b,k). Vandermonde supplies D(a,b)−2binom(a+b,a)+1, the no-map boundary min(a,b)<2, and sample diagonal 2,24,182,1180,7142. An independently written exhaustive checker enumerated every pair of upset sets on all grids 1<=a,b<=4, applied actual upward-cone lifting and surjectivity, and recovered the claimed values, including F(2,2)=2, F(2,3)=6 and F(4,4)=182. Exhaustive finite tests supplement rather than prove unbounded a,b.

## originality

PASS

The exact count of surjective bounded morphisms from labelled rectangular Kripke grids onto B2 includes a strong back-lifting condition; it is not simply the known count of Delannoy paths, monotone maps, or chains in a product lattice. Searches in finite-frame logic and combinatorial path literature did not identify this exact quotient-count theorem, subject to primary full-text access limits and possible lattice-duality reformulations.

## value

PASS

Bounded morphisms preserve modal/intuitionistic truth structure and surjective ones classify meaningful finite frame quotients. An all-size closed formula, an exact existence threshold, and a direct Delannoy identity for a natural grid-to-diamond target constitute an independently useful enumerative invariant, not a bare reindexing of classical Delannoy numbers: the back condition is essential to selecting a special subset.
