## Correctness

**PASS.** Multiplicativity reduces the valuation to local factors. When a prime residue class makes \(1+q^r\) divisible by \(p\), the divergent reciprocal sum of that prime class and independence of the events \(q\parallel n\) force arbitrarily many local contributions in density. When no such class exists, only prime exponents at least two contribute; the displayed convergent series bounds the mean uniformly in \(x\).

## Originality

**PASS, with residual priority risk.** The closest inspected literature treats \(\sigma_1\) or local valuations of that ordinary divisor sum. The result gives an exact criterion in the pair \((p,r)\) for generalized divisor power sums and proves a complementary tightness statement. Searches did not establish an equivalent formulation, but search absence is not a global novelty proof.

## Value

**PASS.** The criterion identifies a structural boundary governed by the existence of an \(r\)-th root of \(-1\) modulo \(p\), and distinguishes density divergence from a uniformly bounded mean. This is a complete classification for a natural valuation invariant, not a finite numerical census.

Closest literature: Pollack–Pomerance (2016), Amdeberhan–Moll–Sharma–Villamizar (2020), and MacMillan–Sondow (2010). The proof gives no uniform rate in the parameters and no exact tail law in the non-root case.
