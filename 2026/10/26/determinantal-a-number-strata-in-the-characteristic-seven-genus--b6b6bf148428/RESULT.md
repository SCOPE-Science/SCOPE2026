# Determinantal a-number strata in the characteristic-seven genus-nine Artin–Schreier family

## Finding

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(7\). On the parameter plane \(S=\operatorname{Spec}k[a,b]\), let \(C_{a,b}\) be the smooth projective model of the Artin–Schreier equation
\[
y^7-y=x^4+a x^2+b x.
\]
Every fiber is a genus-\(9\) curve with \(7\)-rank zero. For the relative Hasse–Witt map on the rank-\(9\) first cohomology bundle, let \(I_r\) be the ideal generated by its \(r\times r\) minors in the coordinates \(a,b\). Then, **as ideals, not only radicals,**
\[
I_1=(a,b),\qquad I_2=(a,b)^2,\qquad
I_3=a(a,b)^2=(a)\cap(a,b)^3,\qquad I_4=0.
\]
Thus the rank-at-most-\(2\) determinantal scheme is the line \(a=0\) with an embedded point at \((a,b)=(0,0)\). The quotient of its ideal by that of the underlying reduced line, \((a)/a(a,b)^2\), has length \(3\). The rank-at-most-\(1\) scheme is the length-\(3\) first infinitesimal neighborhood of the origin; the rank-zero scheme is the reduced origin. On geometric fibers the Jacobian a-number is exactly
\[
\mathfrak a(C_{a,b})=
\begin{cases}
6,&a\ne0,\\
7,&a=0,\ b\ne0,\\
9,&a=b=0.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular \(8\) does not occur in this one-pole family. The statement is a complete relative determinantal classification on the specified normal-form parameter plane, not a claim about the moduli stack's global scheme structure or the other ramification patterns of genus \(9\).

## Assumptions and scope

The ground field is algebraically closed of characteristic \(7\); all parameter values \(a,b\in k\) are allowed. The polynomial has a unique pole of order \(4\) at infinity, prime to \(7\). Riemann–Hurwitz gives \(2g-2=-14+6(4+1)=16\), so \(g=9\). The Deuring–Shafarevich formula gives \(7\)-rank \(0\) because there is exactly one branch point. The unique point over infinity has \(\operatorname{ord}_\infty(x)=-7\), \(\operatorname{ord}_\infty(y)=-4\), and \(\operatorname{ord}_\infty(dx)=16\). The affine equation is smooth since its derivative with respect to \(y\) is \(-1\); the smooth normalization at infinity has the listed fixed ramification. This yields a smooth proper family on the whole parameter plane.

The normal form is exhaustive for one-pole degree-\(4\) Artin–Schreier covers with the distinguished covering coordinate: translate \(x\) to remove its cubic term (since \(4\) is invertible), scale \(x\) to make the leading coefficient \(1\), and translate \(y\) to remove the constant term (possible over \(k\)). Distinct pairs \((a,b)\) are not asserted to classify unmarked curves injectively.

## Proof

The divisor of \(dx\) is \(16P_\infty\). Therefore the nine differentials
\[
\mathcal B=(dx,\; y\,dx,\; y^2dx,\; y^3dx,\; y^4dx,\; x\,dx,\; xy\,dx,\; xy^2dx,\; x^2dx)
\]
form a basis of regular differentials: for \(x^iy^jdx\) regularity means \(7i+4j\le16\), and these are exactly nine distinct monomials. Linear independence follows from the \(k(x)\)-basis \(1,y,\ldots,y^6\) and pole considerations; their number equals the genus.

Let \(f=x^4+ax^2+bx\). Since \(y=y^7-f\), semilinearity and \(\mathcal C(x^m dx)=0\) unless \(m\equiv6\pmod7\) give, for each basis vector,
\[
\mathcal C(x^iy^jdx)=\sum_{\ell=0}^j(-1)^{j-\ell}\binom j\ell y^\ell\,\mathcal C\bigl(x^if^{j-\ell}dx\bigr).
\]
Write \(M\) for the matrix of seventh powers of the Cartier coefficients (equivalently the transpose of the Hasse–Witt matrix in the dual basis). This is a polynomial matrix over \(k[a,b]\), not a matrix containing formal seventh roots. Number rows and columns by the ordered basis \(\mathcal B\), beginning at \(1\). Its only nonzero entries are
\[
\begin{aligned}
&M_{1,3}=2a,&&M_{1,4}=-a^3-3b^2,&&M_{1,5}=-a^2b^2,&&M_{1,8}=2b,\\
&M_{2,4}=-a,&&M_{2,5}=3a^3+2b^2,&&M_{3,5}=-2a,&&M_{6,5}=-3b.
\end{aligned}
\]
All coefficients are reduced modulo \(7\). The transpose does not change determinantal ideals.

The entries visibly generate \(I_1=(a,b)\). Every \(2\times2\) minor lies in \((a,b)^2\); conversely the minors in row/column blocks \(((1,2),(3,4))\), \(((1,2),(4,8))\), and \(((1,6),(5,8))\) respectively equal \(-2a^2\), \(2ab\), and \(-b^2\). Hence \(I_2=(a,b)^2\). By direct multilinear expansion of the sparse matrix, every \(3\times3\) minor lies in \(a(a,b)^2\). Three minors witnessing all generators are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\det M_{(1,2,3),(3,4,5)}&=-3a^3,\\
\det M_{(1,2,3),(4,5,8)}&=-3a^2b,\\
\det M_{(1,2,6),(4,5,8)}&=-ab^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore \(I_3=(a^3,a^2b,ab^2)=a(a,b)^2\). Since rows \(3\) and \(6\) are both supported in column \(5\), and all other nonzero rows are \(1\) and \(2\), the matrix has rank at most \(3\), so \(I_4=0\).

For the scheme-theoretic decomposition, \(a(a,b)^2=(a)\cap(a,b)^3\) follows by comparing monomial ideals. The embedded punctual contribution has \(k\)-length \(\dim_k(a)/a(a,b)^2=\dim_k k[a,b]/(a,b)^2=3\). Since the a-number is genus minus the geometric rank of Cartier/Hasse–Witt, these ideals give the asserted three a-number classes without asserting that rank equals the \(7\)-rank.

## Relationship to prior work

Farnell–Pries, *Families of Artin–Schreier curves with Cartier–Manin matrix of constant rank* (first arXiv submission 19 February 2012, arXiv:1202.4183), gives a basis and Cartier expansion formula and establishes constant a-number under \(p\equiv1\pmod L\). That hypothesis does not hold here: \(p=7\) and the pole order is \(L=4\). Our proof uses the standard Cartier method but identifies a rank-jumping family and its nonreduced determinantal loci instead.

Zhou, *On the existence of curves with prescribed a-number* (arXiv:1901.08375, submitted 24 January 2019), Proposition 1.2 and its proof, already establish the rank-\(2\) case for \(y^7-y=x^4+bx\) with \(b\ne0\); its superspecial criterion also covers the monomial case. Those special cases are **not** claimed as new. The contribution is the universal polynomial Hasse–Witt matrix, the complete generic rank-\(3\) class, and particularly the exact scheme ideals \(I_2,I_3\) and the embedded length-\(3\) point. Neither the bounds nor the special-stratum descriptions in the inspected portions of the cited papers supply these scheme-theoretic equations.

## Limitations

The embedded punctual length is measured on the affine normal-form parameter plane, not on the coarse moduli space or stack quotient by coordinate changes. The theorem does not classify the other genus-nine Artin–Schreier ramification type, geometric Ekedahl–Oort types beyond a-number, or Newton polygons. An equivalent unpublished or otherwise uninspected historical calculation could exist.
