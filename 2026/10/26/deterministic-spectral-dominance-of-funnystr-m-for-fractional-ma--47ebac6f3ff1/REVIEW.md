# Scientific review: Deterministic spectral dominance of funNyström for fractional matrix powers

## Correctness — PASS
The precise domain is positive-definite matrices with an identical full-column-rank sketch, identical integer subspace iteration counts and real positive powers. The pencil factorization and generalized Rayleigh characterization are exact. Convexity of logarithmic spectral moments orders every Rayleigh quotient and hence all ordered generalized eigenvalues. For strictly concave powers, the Nyström-projector formula and Löwner–Heinz make both nuclear residuals positive semidefinite and reduce their norms to trace deficits. In the explicit example, setting \(u=L^{1/4}\) gives the polynomial \(u^4-u^3+u^2-u+1\), from which the \(2L^{1/4}\) ratio follows. The included symbolic checker and 1728 finite tests replayed successfully. They corroborate but do not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge)
The closest primary work is Persson–Kressner, arXiv:2209.11023, especially its Formula (3), Algorithm 1, Lemma 3.2 and Section 4.3. It already defines funNyström, supplies numerical evidence for competitiveness, and develops randomized error bounds; these are credited. Persson–Meyer–Musco, arXiv:2311.14023, derives algorithm-agnostic near-optimality transfer for operator-monotone matrix functions. Its bound relative to the best approximation does not imply an ordering of two nonoptimal approximations generated by the same sketch. Literature-index comparison identified no implication-covering result, though search is not proof of uniqueness. Another known statement concerns monotonicity in the iteration parameter within a single funNyström construction and does not establish a direct-versus-fun comparison. The standard moment log-convexity and min-max lemmas are not represented as novel. Residual originality risk remains for equivalent formulations outside inspected literature.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A deterministic *paired-algorithm* inequality decides which method gives the smaller nuclear/trace approximation error for every sketch, every rank and power-iteration count in the fractional-power regime. An exact two-dimensional family gives an unbounded separation, establishing that the quantitative advantage need not be small. The comparison addresses an explicitly observed but not mathematically classified empirical pattern in the original algorithm paper, with direct relevance to low-rank trace approximation.

## Scope and residual limitations
No Loewner ordering between the two competing low-rank approximations is asserted; Frobenius/operator residuals are not compared. Singular matrices and rank-deficient sketches, general operator-monotone functions, typical random-sketch performance, and finite precision are outside the claim. The \(p>1\) eigenvalue reversal does not imply a residual nuclear-norm direction. The assessed originality is best-knowledge, not independent validation.
