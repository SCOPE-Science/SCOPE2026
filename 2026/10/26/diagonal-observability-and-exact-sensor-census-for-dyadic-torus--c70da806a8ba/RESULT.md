# Diagonal observability and exact sensor census for dyadic torus XOR dynamics

## Finding
Consider a binary cellular automaton on a periodic rectangular lattice, with each site synchronously replaced by the XOR of its east and north neighbors. Suppose the two side lengths are powers of two, and write \(L\) for the larger and \(s\) for the smaller length, with \(L\ge s\ge2\). A fixed sensor set \(S\) reads the values at its sites at each time \(t=0,1,\ldots,L-1\). Define the *diagonal class* of the site \((i,j)\) by \(i+j\pmod s\) (the coordinates are taken modulo the respective side lengths).

**Complete sensor criterion.** The entire unknown initial configuration can be recovered uniquely from these readings if and only if the sensor set meets every one of the \(s\) diagonal classes. This criterion holds for *every* sensor set, without an upper bound on its size. No sensor set that misses a class can recover the initial state, even if observations continue indefinitely.

Consequently, the least possible number of fixed sensors is exactly \(s\). Precisely \(L^s\) unordered sensor sets of that minimum size succeed. More generally, for any integer \(0\le k\le Ls\), the exact number of successful \(k\)-site sensor sets is
\[
N_k=\sum_{j=0}^{s}(-1)^j\binom{s}{j}\binom{L(s-j)}{k},
\]
where a binomial coefficient with upper argument smaller than \(k\) is zero. For a minimum-size successful set, the \(Ls\) readings at times \(0,\ldots,L-1\) constitute an invertible linear transformation of the \(Ls\) initial bits; fewer than \(L\) time frames cannot suffice using only \(s\) sensors.

**Random-placement threshold.** Let \(s\to\infty\) through powers of two, choose any power-of-two \(L\ge s\), and select \(k=\lfloor s(\log s+c)\rfloor\) distinct sensors uniformly at random, for a fixed real \(c\). Then
\[
\Pr(\text{initial configuration is recoverable})\longrightarrow
\exp(-e^{-c}).
\]
The logarithm here is natural. Thus the sharp typical sensor requirement is \(s\log s\), despite the optimum deterministic requirement being only \(s\).

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(a,b\) be integers with \(a\ge b\ge1\); put \(L=2^a\) and \(s=2^b\). Index the sites by \((i,j)\in\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/s\mathbb Z\). For a configuration \(x\), write
\[
(Fx)_{i,j}=x_{i+1,j}+x_{i,j+1}\pmod2.
\]
The initial configuration is arbitrary in the full vector space \(\mathbb F_2^{Ls}\). Sensor readings are noiseless, their locations remain stationary, and the update rule and sensor positions are known. An observation record consists of \((F^t x)_{i,j}\) for all sensors and \(0\le t<L\). Identifiability means that distinct initial configurations yield distinct records. The choice \(L\ge s\) is without loss of generality because the two coordinate directions are symmetric. A sensor set is a subset of distinct lattice positions, not a multiset.

## Proof
**1. The dual dynamics and extinction.** Identify the vector space of linear functionals on configurations with the finite group algebra
\[
R=\mathbb F_2[X,Y]/(X^L-1,Y^s-1),
\]
where a site evaluation functional corresponds to its monomial \(X^iY^j\). Pulling a functional backward through one update multiplies it by \(W=X+Y\), because the value at \((i,j)\) after one update is the sum of the two neighboring initial values. Since \(L\) is a power of two and \(s\mid L\), the Frobenius identity gives
\[
W^L=X^L+Y^L=1+1=0.
\]
Therefore \(F^L=0\). All observations after time \(L-1\) are zero and cannot add information.

**2. A free module of exactly \(s\) temporal chains.** Substitute \(X=W+Y\). The relations \(X^L=Y^s=1\) become \(W^L=0\) and \(Y^s=1\), again because \(s\mid L\) and the exponents are powers of two. This yields an explicit isomorphism
\[
R\cong B[Y]/(Y^s-1),\qquad B=\mathbb F_2[W]/(W^L).
\]
Thus \(R\) is a free module of rank \(s\) over the local Artinian ring \(B\), with basis \(1,Y,\ldots,Y^{s-1}\). The \(L\) time readings from one sensor span exactly the \(B\)-submodule generated by its site monomial: their dual functionals are \(W^tX^iY^j\), for \(0\le t<L\).

**3. The exact geometric criterion.** Reducing the monomial \(X^iY^j=(W+Y)^iY^j\) modulo the ideal \((W)\) gives \(Y^{i+j}\), reduced modulo \(Y^s-1\). These reductions are precisely the \(s\) basis vectors indexed by the diagonal residue \(i+j\pmod s\). If any residue is absent, the generated submodule cannot be all of \(R\), because its reduction modulo \(W\) lacks a basis vector. Conversely, if all residues occur, select one corresponding sensor for each. The \(s\times s\) matrix of their monomials in the displayed \(B\)-basis reduces modulo \(W\) to a permutation matrix. Its determinant has constant coefficient \(1\), hence is a unit in \(B\). The matrix is invertible, so these \(s\) sensor monomials generate the full module \(R\). Their \(Ls\) time shifts form an \(\mathbb F_2\)-basis, establishing injectivity and the precise criterion. The dimension bound \(sT<Ls\) excludes any shorter \(T<L\) horizon when there are only \(s\) sensors.

**4. Exact counting.** Each diagonal residue has exactly \(L\) positions: each of the \(L\) choices of \(i\) determines exactly one \(j\pmod s\). Successful sets are exactly subsets intersecting all \(s\) disjoint classes. Inclusion-exclusion over the omitted classes gives the formula for \(N_k\). For \(k=s\), precisely one position per class must be selected, yielding \(N_s=L^s\).

**5. Critical random threshold.** Let \(E\) be the number of diagonal classes missed by a uniformly random \(k\)-element sensor set. For each fixed positive integer \(r\), its falling factorial moment equals
\[
\mathbb E[(E)_r]=(s)_r\frac{\binom{L(s-r)}{k}}{\binom{Ls}{k}}.
\]
For \(k=\lfloor s(\log s+c)\rfloor\), expansion of the finite product for the binomial quotient gives
\[
\log\frac{\binom{L(s-r)}{k}}{\binom{Ls}{k}}
=-\frac{rk}{s}+O_r\!\left(\frac{k}{s^2}+\frac{k^2}{Ls^2}\right)
=-r\log s-rc+o(1),
\]
using \(L\ge s\to\infty\). Hence \(\mathbb E[(E)_r]\to e^{-rc}\) for every fixed \(r\). These are the factorial moments of a Poisson random variable of mean \(e^{-c}\); the usual factorial-moment criterion yields convergence to that law. Therefore \(\Pr(E=0)\to\exp(-e^{-c})\), as required.

## Verification
The standalone program `verify.py` constructs the time-observation matrix directly from the local rule, uses independent binary Gaussian elimination to test injectivity, checks the residue criterion, and checks the inclusion-exclusion census. It exhausts all sensor subsets for rectangles \(2\times2\), \(4\times2\), and \(4\times4\), totaling \(65{,}808\) subsets, and tests \(640\) seeded random placements on larger dyadic rectangles. It checks that every time row has vanished after \(L\) steps. Run with `python verify.py`. The computations verify finite cases only; the unrestricted conclusions and asymptotic law follow from the proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Tadaki's 1993 analysis of linear cellular-automaton orbits studies one-dimensional Rule 90 and Rule 150, with relaxation and periods rather than multi-sensor recovery on dyadic two-dimensional tori. General linear-system observability through the Kalman rank condition is classical; Sundaram and Hadjicostis analyze finite-field observability, and El Yacoubi and collaborators explicitly formulate the observability matrix for affine cellular automata. The result here does **not** claim a new rank criterion in general: it evaluates that criterion exactly for the specified two-dimensional rule, identifies all successful placements by a geometric residue condition, counts them at every cardinality, and derives their sharp random-placement threshold. The accessible abstract of Plénet and collaborators' 2022 bounded-CA observability paper describes general criteria and examples, but its full text was not available for a complete priority comparison. These results must not be represented as independently audited historical novelty.

## Limitations
The statement is restricted to noiseless stationary coordinate sensors, simultaneous updates, binary XOR, and periodic rectangles whose side lengths are powers of two. It does not give a general classification for other lengths, fields, update rules, mobile or noisy sensors, or arbitrary partial observation horizons. The exact classification concerns recovery of the initial state rather than recovery of the (eventually zero) terminal state. Independent literature priority assessment remains outstanding, especially where full texts of broad sensor-observability treatments could not be obtained.
