# Scientific review: two distinct universal optimal couplings

## Correctness — PASS
The drift matrix has eigenvalues \(k\) and \(k+2u\), both strictly positive. The stationary covariance is the solution of \(AC+CA=2\Theta\), and its diagonal difference gives the antisymmetric lag covariance exactly. Analytic delay differentiation supplies the unique peak lag. The area optimum follows from \(h'(x)=(2-x^2)/[(x+1)^2(x+2)^2]\); the peak optimum follows from the strict concavity of \(F(x)=(x+2)\log(1+x)-x^2\). The strict inequality \(x_*>2>\sqrt2\) proves the two optima differ. The packaged checker reproduces 113 finite symbolic and deterministic numeric assertions. It is supporting evidence, not a proof of asymptotic/global assertions.

## Scientific value — PASS
Measurable asymmetry of delayed cross-covariances is a physically motivated signature of broken detailed balance. Maximizing its single-lag excursion and maximizing its all-lag integrated response are two natural but distinct measurement strategies. Their optimal couplings are unique, universal under temperature changes, and provably different, even though the infinitesimal-lag current grows monotonically. This clarifies why tuning for circulation, peak detection, or integrated response is not interchangeable. It is not claimed to resolve higher-dimensional nonlinear transport, experimental finite-sample efficiency, or a new general theorem on OU propagators.
