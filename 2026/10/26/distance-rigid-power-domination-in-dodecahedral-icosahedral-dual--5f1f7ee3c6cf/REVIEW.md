# Review

## Correctness: PASS

The claim is confined to two explicitly defined finite graphs. Singletons cannot propagate: in the dodecahedron girth five prevents a unique white neighbor after a single closed-neighborhood observation; in the icosahedron each observed neighbor has two white neighbors because vertex links are pentagons. The included verifier enumerates all \(190\) and \(66\) distinct-vertex pairs and checks that two independent propagation implementations yield identical stage-size sequences. The minimum-set exchange graphs are constructed in full, and every shortest exchange distance is found by BFS. The explicit equality criterion plus line-graph construction proves the numerical counts and connectivity claims. No finite trial is used to assert an infinite theorem.

## Originality: PASS, with residual access risk

Ferrero–Hogben–Kenter–Young (arXiv:1512.06413, full accessible HTML) define the time recurrence. Barrera–Ferrero (DOI 10.1002/net.20413, accessible author PDF) give generalized-Petersen power-domination bounds, without a pair-placement or exchange classification. Xu–Kang (2011, DOI 10.1007/s10878-010-9293-y) and Kang–Shan–Zhao (2020, DOI 10.7151/dmgt.2137) have relevant but abstract-only accessible material focused on cardinalities/bounds, leaving nontrivial full-text coverage risk. General power-domination polynomial literature (arXiv:1805.10984) could contain overlapping coefficient data, but no accessible statement implies the sharp pair-distance classification, propagation stages, and exchange geometry. Published-record alias and implication searches found no stronger covering item, which is evidence rather than proof of uniqueness.

## Value: PASS

This is the complete equality-case classification for a naturally motivated graph-monitoring problem on a planar dual pair. The dichotomy between \(60\) special distance-three optimal dodecahedral placements and all \(66\) icosahedral pairs is structurally meaningful; the exact forcing stages quantify their different observation behavior. The connected exchange graphs with diameters \(3\) and \(2\) permit optimal placements to be changed without losing optimality. The conclusions are finite and make no unsupported general-family assertion.
