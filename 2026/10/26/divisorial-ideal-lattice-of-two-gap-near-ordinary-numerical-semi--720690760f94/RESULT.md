# Divisorial-ideal lattice of two-gap near-ordinary numerical semigroups

## Finding

For each integer \(m\ge4\), define the numerical semigroup
\[
S_m=\{0\}\cup\{m,m+1,\ldots,2m-3\}\cup\{2m,2m+1,\ldots\}.
\]
Its gaps are \(1,\ldots,m-1,2m-2,2m-1\). A *relative ideal* is a nonempty, bounded-below subset \(I\subseteq\mathbb Z\) with \(I+S_m=I\); it is *normalized* if \(\min I=0\). Set \(S-I=\{t\in\mathbb Z:t+I\subseteq S\}\). Such an ideal is *divisorial* (or reflexive) when \(S_m-(S_m-I)=I\).

Write \(L=m-2\) and, for each \(g\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}\), define
\[
F_m(g)=\{t-m+1:m\le t\le 2m-3,\quad g+t\in\{2m-2,2m-1\}\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,L\}.
\]
Equivalently, \(F_m(m-1)=\{1\}\), \(F_m(1)=\{L\}\), and \(F_m(g)=\{m-1-g,m-g\}\) for \(2\le g\le m-2\). The middle formula indexes consecutive *positions* from 1 through \(L\).

**Theorem.** The normalized divisorial ideals are exactly \(S_m\) and the pairwise distinct ideals
\[
I_U=S_m\cup\{2m-2,2m-1\}\cup
\{g\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}:F_m(g)\subseteq U\},
\]
where \(U\subseteq\{1,\ldots,L\}\) and its length-\(L\) binary indicator word avoids the contiguous pattern \(010\). Moreover \(I_U\subseteq I_V\) precisely when \(U\subseteq V\).

If \(a_L\) is the number of binary words of length \(L\) avoiding \(010\), then the number of normalized divisorial ideals (equivalently, their translation classes) is
\[
D_m=1+a_{m-2},\quad
 a_0=1,\ a_1=2,\ a_2=4,\quad
 a_j=2a_{j-1}-a_{j-2}+a_{j-3}\quad(j\ge3),
\]
with generating function
\[
\sum_{j\ge0}a_jz^j=\frac{1+z^2}{1-2z+z^2-z^3}.
\]
The total number of *all* normalized relative ideals is \(2^{m-1}+5\), so the divisorial proportion tends to zero exponentially: \(D_m=\Theta(\alpha^m)\), where \(\alpha\approx1.754877666\) is the real root of \(\alpha^3-2\alpha^2+\alpha-1=0\). The lattice of normalized divisorial ideals under inclusion is distributive for \(m=4\), but **nonmodular for every \(m\ge5\)**.

The initial pairs \((m,D_m)\) are \((4,5),(5,8),(6,13),(7,22),(8,38),(9,66),(10,115),(11,201),(12,352)\).

## Assumptions and scope

All claims hold for every integer \(m\ge4\), without probabilistic or genericity assumptions. Integer intervals in the definition include both endpoints; the final interval is infinite. An ideal's translation class has a unique normalized representative, obtained by subtracting its minimum. *Lattice* here means the set of normalized divisorial ideals ordered by inclusion, not the semigroup of ideals under ideal addition. For \(m=4\), the displayed semigroup is \(\{0,4,5,8,9,10,11,\ldots\}\); the definition does not mean merely the monoid generated by the integers \(m\) through \(2m-3\) in this smallest case.

## Proof

**1. Normalized ideals and their duals.** The displayed set \(S_m\) is a numerical semigroup: the sum of two positive members is at least \(2m\) and hence belongs to it. Since \(0\in I\), any normalized relative ideal contains \(S_m\), hence is \(I=S_m\cup A\) with \(A\) a subset of the finite gap set. Conversely such a set is an ideal exactly when it is closed upon adding \(m,m+1,\ldots,2m-3\). For a selected small gap \(g\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}\), the only sums of this kind outside \(S_m\) are the two high gaps. Therefore including a small gap \(g\) forces inclusion of exactly those high gaps that are hit by adding an element of the middle block. In particular, selecting \(g=1\) forces \(2m-2\), selecting \(g=m-1\) forces \(2m-1\), and selecting any \(2\le g\le m-2\) forces both high gaps.

For \(I\ne S_m\) put \(B=A\cap\{1,\ldots,m-1\}\) and \(U=\bigcup_{g\in B}F_m(g)\). As \(0\in I\) but \(I\not\subseteq S_m\), every element of \(S_m-I\) lies in \(S_m\setminus\{0\}\). Every integer at least \(2m\) belongs to \(S_m-I\), since all elements of \(I\) are nonnegative. In the middle block \(m,\ldots,2m-3\), the elements missing from \(S_m-I\) are exactly those with position in \(U\); selected high gaps impose no further restriction. Consequently
\[
S_m-I=\{m+j-1:1\le j\le L,\ j\notin U\}\cup\{2m,2m+1,\ldots\}.
\]

**2. Bidual closure.** No negative integer belongs to \(I^v=S_m-(S_m-I)\): for \(x<0\), the element \(t=2m-1-x\ge2m\) is in \(S_m-I\), but \(x+t=2m-1\notin S_m\). The closure contains \(S_m\), both high gaps (their sums with all elements of \(S_m-I\) are at least \(2m\)), and exactly the small gaps \(g\) for which \(F_m(g)\subseteq U\). Hence \(I\ne S_m\) is divisorial precisely when it contains both high gaps and
\[
B=\{g\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}:F_m(g)\subseteq U\},
\quad U=\bigcup_{g\in B}F_m(g).
\]
The ideal \(S_m\) is itself divisorial because \(S_m-S_m=S_m\).

**3. A path covering condition.** Regard \(1,\ldots,L\) as the vertices of a path. The sets \(F_m(g)\) are exactly its consecutive two-vertex edges, together with the singleton sets at the two endpoints. For \(U\) a vertex subset, let \(B(U)=\{g:F_m(g)\subseteq U\}\). The union \(\bigcup_{g\in B(U)}F_m(g)\) equals \(U\) if and only if \(U\) has no isolated selected *interior* vertex. Such a vertex is exactly an occurrence of the substring \(010\) in the indicator word. Thus the displayed ideals \(I_U\) are exactly the divisorial ideals other than \(S_m\), and \(U\) is recovered from \(I_U\) by the union formula. This establishes both uniqueness and the inclusion equivalence.

**4. Enumeration.** Let \(a_j\) count binary words of length \(j\) containing no \(010\), and let \(b_j\) count those among them ending in \(01\). Appending either bit to a valid word of length \(j-1\) fails exactly when a word ending in \(01\) is followed by \(0\); thus \(a_j=2a_{j-1}-b_{j-1}\). For \(j\ge3\), forming a valid word of length \(j-1\) ending in \(01\) from a valid length-\(j-3\) prefix fails precisely when that prefix ends in \(01\), so \(b_{j-1}=a_{j-3}-b_{j-3}\). Eliminating the \(b\)'s gives the stated recurrence, checked directly at \(j=3\); the initial values and generating function follow. The characteristic polynomial \(x^3-2x^2+x-1\) has one real root \(\alpha\in(1,2)\), and its other two roots have modulus less than one. Its positive leading coefficient in the enumeration gives \(a_j=\Theta(\alpha^j)\).

For comparison, the unclosed normalized ideals can be counted by their small-gap subsets. When the subset is empty there are four choices of high gaps; for exactly \(\{1\}\) or exactly \(\{m-1\}\) there are two choices each; every other nonempty small-gap subset forces both highs, giving \(2^{m-1}-3\) choices. The total is \(4+2+2+(2^{m-1}-3)=2^{m-1}+5\). As \(\alpha<2\), the ratio of divisorial to all normalized ideals decays exponentially.

**5. Lattice boundary.** The admissible vertex sets are closed under unions. Their greatest admissible subset inside \(U\cap V\) is obtained by deleting isolated selected interior vertices, because deleting such vertices cannot isolate any others. These give the join and meet for ideals other than \(S_m\), with \(S_m\) an additional minimum. When \(L=2\), all four subsets are admissible, so this is a Boolean four-element lattice with a new bottom, hence distributive. When \(L\ge3\), the five admissible sets \(\varnothing,\{1\},\{1,2\},\{2,3\},\{1,2,3\}\) form a sublattice isomorphic to the pentagon \(N_5\): the meets of \(\{2,3\}\) with \(\{1\}\) and \(\{1,2\}\) are empty, and both joins are \(\{1,2,3\}\). This proves nonmodularity.

## Verification

The standalone Python 3 program `verify.py` constructs \(S_m\) directly from its gaps, enumerates all normalized relative ideals and their colon ideals, and compares the resulting bidual fixed points with the independently generated \(010\)-avoiding-word parametrization. It checks every \(m\) from 4 through 12, including all 4,133 normalized ideals in those cases, as well as the total-ideal count and recurrence. A successful run prints `VERIFY_OK normalized_ideals_examined=4133 multiplicities=9`. This finite enumeration corroborates the proof but is not offered as an infinite certificate.

## Relationship to prior work

Campochiaro, D'Anna and García-Sánchez, *Reflexive ideals of a numerical semigroup* (arXiv:2610.06426v1, publicly posted 2026-10-05), establish the general double-dual framework, express reflexive ideals as intersections of principal ideals, and give Apéry formulas for ideal differences and divisorial closure. Those general results provide an alternative algorithm for each fixed semigroup but do not state the explicit path-word classification, the generating function, or the nonmodularity boundary for this two-gap family. The present argument specializes the colon operation and then performs the family-wide combinatorial classification.

Bonzio and García-Sánchez, *The poset of normalized ideals of numerical semigroups with multiplicity three* (arXiv:2407.21697, *Communications in Algebra* 53 (2025), 3610–3624), work in multiplicity three; this family has multiplicity at least four. The published multiplicity-five Wilf-gap theorem for embedding-dimension-three numerical semigroups concerns a different invariant even when the underlying semigroup is \(S_5=\langle5,6,7\rangle\). No claim is made that avoiding \(010\) is a new combinatorial sequence in isolation; the contribution is its exact appearance as this divisorial ideal lattice.

## Limitations

The classification concerns a specific infinite family, not arbitrary numerical semigroups or all near-ordinary deformations. General Apéry formulas imply that each finite case is computable; the new contribution is the uniform explicit parametrization and structural consequences, not a new definition of divisorial closure. A plausible residual originality risk is an equivalent family-specific result in unindexed earlier research. The finite verifier checks only \(4\le m\le12\); validity for larger \(m\) rests on the proof. The normalized ideal counts apply to translation classes and should not be confused with the infinite number of translated relative ideals themselves.
