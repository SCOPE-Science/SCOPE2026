# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The greedy arch lemma supplies the exact subsequence-universality index, with a direct earliest-occurrence obstruction to another arch. Signed height changes across the arches have total positive variation \(n-\iota(w)\). This proves the sharp height inequality. For height at most two, positive arch excess can happen only once, as \(001\), and negative excess cannot occur; the stated normal form follows. Direct subsequence checks for \(n\le8\) and complete Dyck enumeration through \(n=12\) pass. The computations are finite corroboration, not the all-length proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Adamson (arXiv:2304.04583) counts unrestricted universal words; Schnoebelen and Veron (arXiv:2304.11932) develop generic arch-index methods; Mol, Rampersad, and Shallit (arXiv:2301.06145) investigate Dyck nesting but not scattered-subsequence universality. Related record searches do not identify a covering theorem. The height-two Dyck count itself is standard; the new content is the height/universality equivalence, sharp inequality, and forced arch normal form. An equivalent result may remain unlocated.

## Value — PASS

The theorem identifies an explicit sharp relation between nesting depth and subword universality for a canonical nonregular language, classifies the full first near-maximal stratum, and quantifies its exponentially small proportion in the Catalan family. These are natural invariants with all-length proofs rather than arbitrary slices.

## Scope and risks

The result is binary and applies to complete Dyck words, not arbitrary balanced words. There is no claim of a full joint-index distribution, uniqueness against all unpublished literature, or external mathematical certification.
