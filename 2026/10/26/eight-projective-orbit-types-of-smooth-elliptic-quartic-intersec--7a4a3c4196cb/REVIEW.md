# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The problem is a finite Grassmannian with \(174,251\) elements. The Pfaffian argument bounds all possible polar-degenerate parameters to \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_4)\), unless the Pfaffian vanishes identically; the latter contributes no accepted pencils in the exhaustive run. The rank-two kernel restriction and exact second-quadric test establish the absence of geometric singularities. Separately written Jacobian computations over two extensions produce the identical list of \(40,320\) canonical pencils. The generating transformations of the complete ambient linear group partition those pencils into eight orbits.

## Originality — PASS (with residual risk)
The closest geometric classification (Fevola–Mandelshtam–Sturmfels 2020, arXiv:2009.04334) concerns general pencils and principally algebraically closed characteristic different from two, not this specific arithmetic orbit census. Number-field genus-one quartic models in Bhargava–Cremona–Fisher 2020 (arXiv:2004.12085) likewise do not imply the counts or the characteristic-two orbit distinction. Research database searches and accessible historical claim comparisons found no logically covering assertion. This is a best-knowledge comparison, not proof that every unindexed finite-field catalogue lacks the classification.

## Value — PASS
A complete, intrinsic finite-field moduli incidence problem is answered: exactly which embedded genus-one quartics arise and how they split under the natural ambient projective group. Most notably, two inequivalent projective orbits are indistinguishable by their number of rational intersection points and the three constituent quadric point counts, so the orbit census detects structure invisible to those simpler arithmetic invariants.

Independent external validation is absent. Historical priority and the possibility of an earlier unindexed computation remain open.
