# Eight Singer nets of smooth tangent-filling binary plane cubics

## Finding

Put \(k=\mathbf F_2\), and let \(V=H^0(\mathbf P^2_k,\mathcal O(3))\), the ten-dimensional \(k\)-vector space of ternary homogeneous cubics. Call a nonzero form \(F\in V\) **admissible** when its zero curve \(C_F\) is geometrically smooth and the union of the tangent lines \(T_PC_F\), over \(P\in C_F(k)\), contains all seven points of \(\mathbf P^2(k)\). A vector subspace is **admissible** if every nonzero vector is admissible.

**Theorem.** There are exactly **eight** admissible vector subspaces of dimension three in \(V\). They form a single orbit under \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(k)\), each with stabilizer of order \(21\). No admissible subspace has dimension four or more. Consequently, three is the sharp maximal vector-space dimension, or projective linear-system dimension two. For every one of the eight nets, each of its seven nonzero cubic forms has exactly three rational points, all three are rational flexes, and the seven rational point sets are precisely the seven rational lines of the Fano plane, each occurring once.

Each net stabilizer has a unique normal subgroup of order seven. The eight nets thereby correspond equivariantly to the eight cyclic Singer subgroups of \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(k)\).

An explicit spanning triple for one net is
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_1&=X^2Z+XZ^2+Y^3,\\
F_2&=X^3+X^2Z+XZ^2+Y^2Z+YZ^2,\\
F_3&=X^3+X^2Y+XY^2+Z^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
Every nonzero combination of these three forms is geometrically smooth and tangent-filling.

## Assumptions and scope

Coordinates \([X:Y:Z]\) are fixed over \(k\). A rational tangent at a rational point is the line determined by the three homogeneous first partials of the form; smoothness always means smooth over the algebraic closure. The eight are embedded \(k\)-linear three-spaces, not abstract isomorphism classes of elliptic curves. Projective equivalence refers to the natural \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(k)\) action by substitution, not arbitrary changes in the seven-member parameter space. The statement is restricted to the seven \(k\)-rational members of a net; it does not claim that every member after extension of scalars is smooth.

## Proof

**1. The exhaustive domain and exact smoothness test.** There are ten degree-three monomials in three variables, hence exactly \(2^{10}-1=1023\) nonzero coefficient-normalized cubics. The exhaustive check uses the fixed ordering
\[
X^3,\ X^2Y,\ X^2Z,\ XY^2,\ XYZ,\ XZ^2,\ Y^3,\ Y^2Z,\ YZ^2,\ Z^3.
\]
For each form \(F\), the three projective affine charts are formed by setting one coordinate to one. On each chart, compute the reduced Gröbner basis over \(k\) of the ideal generated by \(F\) and its three partial derivatives. By the weak Nullstellensatz over the algebraic closure, the chart has no singular geometric point if and only if the basis is the unit ideal. This is an exact algebraic criterion, not a bounded-degree point search. For each of the 1023 forms the charts are tested until one singular chart is found or all three charts have passed. Exactly \(336\) are geometrically smooth.

**2. Exact tangent incidence.** For every one of the seven rational projective points, evaluation of \(F\) and its gradient uses arithmetic modulo two. For each rational zero \(P\), construct its tangent line from the nonzero gradient and enumerate its three rational points by the dot-product incidence condition. Exactly \(154\) of the \(336\) smooth cubics have tangent lines covering all seven rational points. The seven-point incidence test is exhaustive by construction.

**3. Exact enumeration of subspaces.** A two-dimensional binary subspace with all three nonzero elements admissible is uniquely its triple \(\{a,b,a+b\}\). The checker enumerates all unordered pairs of the 154 admissible coefficient vectors and retains their three-element sums whenever all are admissible; this gives exactly \(392\) distinct admissible pencils. Adjoin each admissible vector outside a pencil and construct the seven-element set
\[
\{a,b,a+b,c,c+a,c+b,c+a+b\}.
\]
It is an admissible three-space precisely if all seven elements lie in the enumerated 154-element set. Every three-space contains a pencil and an element outside it, so this loop is exhaustive; set equality removes duplicates. It gives exactly **eight** admissible nets, whose seven-element coefficient-mask lists are in the attached reference output. The independent lift search described below reproduces those same eight mask sets.

**4. Sharpness.** A four-dimensional binary vector space contains \((2^4-1)/(2-1)=15\) distinct three-dimensional subspaces. If an admissible four-space existed, all fifteen of these would be admissible, contradicting the complete count of eight in the entire ten-dimensional space. No larger admissible subspace can exist because it would contain an admissible four-space.

**5. Orbit and geometry.** Exhaustively enumerate the \(168\) invertible binary three-by-three matrices, compute their substitutions on the ten degree-three monomials and act on the eight nets. The orbit of the displayed representative contains all eight nets. Thus each stabilizer has size \(168/8=21\). The stabilizer acts transitively on the seven forms of the representative net; indeed all seven images of the first basis form occur under the twenty-one substitutions preserving the net. Its geometric smoothness also follows directly from the gradient \([Z^2,Y^2,X^2]\), which has no projective zero, and its rational tangents are \(Z=0\), \(X=0\), \(X+Z=0\), covering the Fano plane. A subgroup of order \(21\) has a unique normal Sylow subgroup of order seven; the eight distinct conjugate stabilizers furnish the eight Singer subgroups, giving the equivariant correspondence. For each member of every net, direct rational-point evaluation gives a three-element zero set that is exactly one of the seven projective lines. Within each net the seven zero sets are distinct and therefore exhaust the seven lines. Restricting the form to its tangent line in a parameter \(t\), the coefficient of \(t^2\) vanishes at each of its three rational zeros, giving intersection multiplicity at least three (a flex). This restriction is computed directly in characteristic two for all 56 member occurrences.

**6. Independent linear-evaluation search.** Evaluation of cubics on the seven rational points has a three-dimensional kernel spanned by \(XY(X+Y)\), \(YZ(Y+Z)\), \(ZX(Z+X)\). The cubics whose evaluations agree with linear functions form a six-dimensional preimage. Every linear splitting of its three-dimensional evaluation quotient is specified by choosing three kernel offsets, each in an eight-element space, giving \(8^3=512\) possibilities. A separate checker enumerates these 512 splittings, tests all seven members with exact Jacobian ideals and the tangent-incidence condition, and obtains exactly the same eight nets. The complete search in Step 3 establishes that none of the other admissible nets are missed by imposing linear evaluation in this independent cross-check.

## Relationship to prior work

Asgarli and Ghioca (2023, first posted 26 February 2023) introduced tangent-filling plane curves and studied degree bounds and explicit examples; their arguments do not classify systems of binary cubics with every rational member tangent-filling. Asgarli, Ghioca and Reichstein (2022) study linear systems with every rational member smooth, without imposing this tangent-incidence condition. Ishitsuka (2016) explicitly gives the two projective classes of smooth binary plane cubics with three rational points, one with three rational flexes; this supplies a useful comparison for individual curves but does not classify compatible seven-member nets or establish the maximal dimension and Singer correspondence. These distinctions concern mathematical implications, not merely differences in terminology.

## Limitations

The result is about geometrically smooth plane cubics defined over \(\mathbf F_2\) and their **rational** tangent lines, not arbitrary cubics or every geometric member of a linear system. It does not classify nets under arbitrary equivalences outside the indicated projective coordinate group. The subgroup correspondence is derived from the computed stabilizer order and the group structure, not from an independent conceptual uniqueness theorem. Computations use exact polynomial ideal calculations rather than a separately formalized proof assistant. Absence of an indexed prior enumeration is not proof of publication priority; unindexed catalogues and older incidence constructions remain a residual risk.
