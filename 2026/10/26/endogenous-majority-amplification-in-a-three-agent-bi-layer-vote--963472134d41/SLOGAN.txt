For three asynchronous bi-layer voter agents initially split as two intolerant \(A\) supporters and one intolerant \(B\) supporter, the exact probability of eventual \(A\)-consensus exceeds \(2/3\) for every relative intolerant-switching rate \(0<\gamma<1\). The amplification is strictly stronger for smaller positive \(\gamma\), approaches \(11/377\) above the classical voter probability, and disappears at \(\gamma=1\).
