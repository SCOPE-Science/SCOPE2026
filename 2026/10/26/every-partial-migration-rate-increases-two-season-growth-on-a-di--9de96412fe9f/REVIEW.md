# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The periodic population map is defined unambiguously with reproduction followed by a single fixed column-stochastic migration kernel. The complete claim is equivalent to a spectral radius inequality for \(Q=M D^{-1}M D\). The determinant of \(I-Q\) factors exactly as \(-d^3(1-d)H\), with \(H\) a sum of three positive ratio-square defects. The strict sign yields a real eigenvalue above one, hence a Perron root above one; the zero- and complete-migration endpoints each have Perron root one. Uniform patch fitness gives a column-stochastic square and equality. The dimension-two boundary follows from positive diagonal symmetrization and convexity of the operator norm (or triangularity in reducible cases). The standalone Laurent-polynomial checker verifies the nonstandard determinant identity symbolically and performs exact rational diagnostics. Finite checks are not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS with bibliographic residual risk
The closest primary work, Schreiber and Li (2010), Appendix A final remark, already gives a three-cycle and one \(d=1/2\) numerical counterexample to a reversible monotonicity statement. The existence of such an example is **not original**. The present quantified statement covers every positive nonconstant reciprocal growth diagonal and every \(0<d<1\), supplies an exact symmetric ratio discriminant and complete equality classification, and is not implied by that single example or the paper's symmetric-similarity theorem. Kleinhans and Jonsson (2010) analyze a stochastic occupancy model, not this spectral product. Artzy-Randrup and Stone (2010) study directed dispersal cycles with a different persistence matrix; no two-season all-rate ratio identity appears in the material inspected. The search results did not reveal a stronger published all-parameter theorem, but search is not a proof of novelty. A possible uncatalogued matrix-analysis inequality or close later extension is the principal risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The prior literature demonstrates that nonreversible dispersal can violate familiar monotonicity expectations, but an isolated example does not establish robustness. The theorem resolves the natural robustness question completely for the minimal directed cycle: strict benefit holds at *every* positive but incomplete dispersal rate and for *every* nonuniform reciprocal seasonal profile, and fails exactly in the homogeneous case and at the two endpoints. The minimum-dimension observation clarifies why directed cycles, rather than merely asymmetric two-site exchange, are structurally responsible. This is an exact family classification; no large downstream theory or quantitative ecological field conclusion is asserted.
