# Exact 4-kernel rank for a family of Lucas-sequence valuations

## Finding

Let \(a,b\in\mathbb Z\), with \(a\ne0\) even and \(b\) odd. Let \((U_n)_{n\ge0}\) be a nondegenerate Lucas sequence defined by

\[
U_0=0,\qquad U_1=1,\qquad U_{n+2}=aU_{n+1}+bU_n.
\]

For a nonzero integer \(m\), write \(\nu_k(m)=\max\{e\ge0:k^e\mid m\}\). Put \(t=\nu_2(a)\) and \(s(n)=\nu_4(U_{n+1})\) for \(n\ge0\). The \(4\)-kernel is

\[
\ker_4(s)=\bigl\{(s(4^e n+i))_{n\ge0}:e\ge0,\ 0\le i<4^e\bigr\}.
\]

The \(\mathbb Z\)-module generated by this kernel has exact rank

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z}\langle\ker_4(s)\rangle=
\begin{cases}
2,&t\in\{1,2\},\\
3,&t\ge3.
\end{cases}
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The recurrence is nondegenerate: its characteristic-root ratio is not a root of unity. The coefficient \(a\) is nonzero and even, while \(b\) is odd, so \(\gcd(4,b)=1\). The claim concerns only the composite base \(4\) and this coefficient class; it does not classify odd \(a\) or other bases.

## Proof

Write \(t=\nu_2(a)\). Modulo \(2\), the recurrence is \(U_{n+2}\equiv U_n\), because \(a\) is even and \(b\) is odd. Hence \(U_n\) is even exactly when \(n\) is even, and the rank of apparition of \(2\) is \(2\). The discriminant \(a^2+4b\) is divisible by \(4\).

The 2-adic Lucas-sequence valuation formula of Murru and Sanna (Theorem 1.5 in the cited paper) therefore gives, for every \(m\ge1\),

\[
\nu_2(U_m)=
\begin{cases}
0,&m\text{ odd},\\
\nu_2(m)+t-1,&m\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]

Since \(\nu_4(x)=\lfloor\nu_2(x)/2\rfloor\), define

\[
f_t(m)=\begin{cases}
0,&m\text{ odd},\\
\left\lfloor\dfrac{\nu_2(m)+t-1}{2}\right\rfloor,&m\text{ even},
\end{cases}
\qquad s(n)=f_t(n+1),
\]

and also define \(h(n)=\left\lfloor(\nu_2(n+1)+t-1)/2\right\rfloor\). Put \(c=\lfloor(t-1)/2\rfloor\). For odd \(n+1\), \(h(n)=c\) and \(s(n)=0\); for even \(n+1\), \(h(n)=s(n)\). Thus

\[
h(n)-s(n)=c\,\mathbf 1_{\{n\text{ even}\}}.
\]

We now determine the kernel module. Fix \(e\ge1\) and \(0\le i<4^e\). If \(i+1\) is odd, then \(4^e n+i+1\) is odd for every \(n\), so \(s(4^e n+i)=0\). If \(i+1\) is even and \(i+1<4^e\), then \(\nu_2(4^e n+i+1)=\nu_2(i+1)\), so \(s(4^e n+i)\) is constant in \(n\). In the remaining case \(i=4^e-1\),

\[
s(4^e n+4^e-1)=\nu_4(U_{4^e(n+1)})=e+h(n).
\]

The constant sequence \(\mathbf 1\) belongs to the kernel module: subtract the special kernel subsequence at level \(e=1\) from the one at level \(e=2\). Consequently every kernel subsequence lies in the \(\mathbb Z\)-span of \(s,h,\mathbf 1\), so the rank is at most \(3\).

If \(t=1\) or \(t=2\), then \(c=0\), hence \(h=s\). The module is generated by \(s\) and \(\mathbf 1\). These are linearly independent because \(s(4^j-1)=j\) for every \(j\ge1\), so the rank is exactly \(2\).

If \(t\ge3\), then \(c\ge1\). Suppose \(A s+B h+C\mathbf 1=0\) for integers \(A,B,C\). Evaluating at every even \(n\) gives \(Bc+C=0\). Evaluating at \(n=4^j-1\) gives \((A+B)(j+c)+C=0\) for all \(j\ge1\). Since \(j+c\) is unbounded, \(A+B=0\) and \(C=0\); then \(Bc=0\), so \(B=A=0\). Thus \(s,h,\mathbf 1\) are linearly independent, and the rank is exactly \(3\). This also proves 4-regularity in the stated family.

## Verification

The proof reduces the claim to the published 2-adic valuation formula and then describes every 4-kernel subsequence explicitly as zero, constant, or \(h+e\). The included standard-library script checks the valuation identity for 15 sample coefficient pairs through index \(500\); this is a consistency check only and is not used as an infinite proof. The rank statement follows from the symbolic spanning and independence arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work

Murru and Sanna prove that \(\nu_k(U_{n+1})\) is \(k\)-regular when \(\gcd(k,b)=1\), give the valuation formula used above, and determine exact ranks when \(k\) is prime. Their concluding remarks explicitly identify the exact rank for composite \(k\), especially prime powers, as an open direction. The present result supplies the exact rank for \(k=4\) throughout the stated even-\(a\), odd-\(b\) family. Medina and Rowland's exact-rank results concern prime-base valuations of the Fibonacci sequence and do not give this composite-base family classification.

## Limitations

No rank formula is claimed for odd \(a\), even \(b\), degenerate recurrences, or bases other than \(4\). The finite script checks examples only. Independent audit and independent validation have not been performed.
