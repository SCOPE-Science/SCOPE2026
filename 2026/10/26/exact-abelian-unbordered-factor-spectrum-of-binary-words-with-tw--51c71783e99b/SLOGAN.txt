For every binary word \(0^a1\,0^b1\,0^c\) with at least one zero, the number of distinct nonempty Abelian-unbordered factors is exactly \(2+2\max\{a,b,c,a+c-b\}\). The full finite count distribution has an explicit one-dimensional sum, and uniformly chosen weight-two words obey a nondegenerate piecewise-quadratic limiting law with mean \(4n/3+O(1)\) and variance \(n^2/9+O(n)\).
