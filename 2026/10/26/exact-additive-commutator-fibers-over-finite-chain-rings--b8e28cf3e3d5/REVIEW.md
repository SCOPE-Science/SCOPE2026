# Review of exact additive commutator fibers over finite chain rings

**Correctness — PASS.** A linear invertible identification reduces the additive two-by-two commutator to a wedge map on a free rank-three module. For a nonzero target of valuation \(s\), stratifying the first vector by its valuation \(a\le s\) yields exactly \(q^{s-a}(q^{2(m-a)}-q^{2(m-a-1)})\) admissible first vectors and \(q^{m+2a}\) second-vector choices each. The resulting geometric sum gives the asserted law. The zero target uses the same kernel sizes and a separate zero first-vector case. A standalone program enumerates all targets over distinct finite chain-ring families for small parameters. No finite enumeration is treated as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with priority risk.** Stasinski's primary text (arXiv:1211.6872, introduction and Corollary 6.4) gives surjectivity of the additive commutator map over principal ideal rings but no complete fiber counts. Yuen's rank-one determinantal jet geometry controls the zero fiber, not every nonzero target; Britnell--Wildon treats class pairs over fields, not arbitrary local-ring target multiplicities. Multiple alias, valuation, cross-product, and commutator-fiber searches identified no direct implication covering this exact all-depth all-chain-ring distribution. Search failure is not a priority certificate; an implicit earlier cross-product or representation-density computation remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** The complete target-dependent distribution is a natural strengthening of a classical surjectivity problem. It characterizes the arithmetic probability measure of additive commutators, shows sharp uniformity on valuation strata, and establishes universality across rings with the same residue cardinality and nilpotence length. The exact nonzero fibers, rather than known surjectivity or the zero-fiber special case, motivate this claim.

**Scientific boundaries.** Only two-by-two additive commutators over finite commutative chain rings are covered; no assertion about higher ranks, multiplicative commutators, or general nonprincipal local rings follows.
