# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The source claim is an infinite-family classification for the exact grounded-Laplacian inverse trace on a unit-weight clique corona. Its proof starts from an explicit four-status decomposition of each core–leaf pair and proves the grounded trace formula using a Schur complement and a rank-one inverse. A strict local leader exchange eliminates any configuration containing both an empty and a fully grounded pair. The remaining one-variable optimization is solved by an exact positive second derivative, a strict discrete comparison, and a polynomial sign test; a modular congruence rules out all integer ties. The higher-budget case follows from a separate strictly negative derivative. All parameter endpoints, optimizer counts, and objective normalization are specified. Exact symbolic identities, rational budget enumeration, exact matrix inverses, and exhaustive small-graph tests were independently replayed from the supplied program. Finite checks support but do not establish the universal assertion.

## Originality
**PASS, subject to bibliographic uncertainty.** Lin–Fardad–Jovanović (arXiv:1302.0450, §II and §IV) establishes the noise-free grounded-trace optimization and relaxations, not the explicit all-budget solution for this family. Fitch–Leonard (arXiv:1407.1569, §V) gives joint-centrality principles and special solutions for cycle and path families. Du–Zhu–Gao–Li (DOI:10.3389/fphy.2022.948247, §§2–6) examines weighted-corona leaderless network coherence from full Laplacian spectra, not optimization over grounded leader sets. Sharma–Adhikari–Mishra (arXiv:1507.05335, §§1–4) treats corona structural spectra, not grounded-coherence leader selection. Searches under both corona and pendant-leaf terminology did not identify equivalent or stronger coverage. General leader-selection formulas do not by themselves imply the quadratic optimizer-type threshold, no-overlap exchange, or exact counts without the additional optimization proved here. Bibliographic coverage is not exhaustive; alternative terminology and nonindexed special-graph literature remain risks.

## Value
**PASS.** The noise-free leader problem is intrinsically combinatorial, and the graph has two sharply different vertex roles in each clique–leaf pair. A complete, natural all-budget solution reduces every leader-set search to one quadratic sign test, determines the exact minimum and all optimizing sets, excludes redundant fully selected pairs at low budgets, and yields a sharp transition at asymptotically \((\sqrt3-1)n\) leaders. This clarifies how hub-like core coverage competes with removal of noisy pendant followers. It is a mathematical classification rather than an unmotivated numerical instance.

## Closest literature and limitations
The most directly related formulations are the original noise-free leader-selection optimization and the joint-centrality criterion. The most directly related graph-family study derives **leaderless** weighted-corona coherence; it does not impose an arbitrary grounded leader set. The theorem is restricted to unweighted complete cores with one private leaf per core and perfect grounding; weighted edges and other core graphs remain unproved. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
