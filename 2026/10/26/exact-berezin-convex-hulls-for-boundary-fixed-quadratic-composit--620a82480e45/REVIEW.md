# Scientific review: quadratic composition Berezin hulls

## Correctness — PASS
The normalized Hardy kernel gives \(F_\mu(re^{i\theta})=(1-r^2)/(A-Be^{i\theta})\), with \(A=1-(1-\mu)r^2\) and \(B=\mu r^3\). Both the circle center/radius and the limit disk are derived directly. Two exact factorizations establish \(a<c_r<1\) and \(\rho_r/(1-c_r)=r/(1+\mu r^2)<1/(1+\mu)\), which puts every finite-radius circle inside a strict homothetic disk in the stated convex hull. The origin image and limit circle force the opposite inclusion. Compactness of the radius interval proves omission of open tangent segments. Standard tangent-disk area geometry and an elementary radius-one Hausdorff estimate prove the remaining claims. This is an analytical proof for every real \(0<\mu<1\); finite checks are corroborative only.

## Originality — PASS (conditional on historical priority)
The 2026 Bourdon–Le–Thompson paper provides the circular-image mechanism in Proposition 3.7 but no optimized global convex-hull formula or identity-limit discontinuity for the nonmonomial quadratic segment. Earlier Cowen–Felder and Augustine–Garayev–Shankar papers treat different symbol families or general questions, not the stated one-parameter exact hull and area. Recorded related results for monomial compositions cover a different parameter stratum and cannot be specialized to \(0<\mu<1\). A targeted search for the exact quadratic family, its tangent-disk hull, aliases, and identity discontinuity disclosed no implication-equivalent prior statement. Older unindexed literature or an unstated corollary may still affect priority; absence from search is not a proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The convex hull is a canonical operator-geometric invariant; its complete exact determination over a natural one-parameter boundary-fixed quadratic family is more than a selected evaluation. Most importantly, the hull remains maximally separated from that of the identity under uniformly vanishing quadratic perturbations and has a strictly positive limiting area, a nonuniform boundary-layer phenomenon. Two missing exposed segments show that convexification loses real geometric information. General quadratic and higher-degree extensions remain open.
