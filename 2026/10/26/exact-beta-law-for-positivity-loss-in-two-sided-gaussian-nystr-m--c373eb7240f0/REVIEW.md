# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** QR reduction of the Gaussian two-sided Nyström estimator gives an exact oblique block with a single Gaussian least-squares coefficient vector. Direct singular-value decomposition proves that the symmetrized rank-one output has negative eigenvalue \((1-\sqrt{1+\|b\|^2})/2\). The independently derived Wishart/multivariate-Student law for \(b\), followed by the beta-ratio transformation, proves the exact CDF, sharp polynomial tail, all finite-moment thresholds, and mean formula. Boundary cases \(x=0\), positive integer oversampling and almost-sure uniqueness are addressed. The included verifier independently checks actual two-sided projections/SVDs and sampling identities (812 assertions), but finite checks are not substituted for a distributional proof.

**Originality — PASS, subject to publication-priority uncertainty.** The full accessible primary formulation [1] and generalized Nyström analysis [2] establish the operator and generic Frobenius error guarantees; those are not new. The PSD-streaming approach [3] is different, and [4] supplies an expected-error formula, not the claimed signed-spectrum law. Searches for oblique-projection aliases, Gaussian regression, truncation-induced indefiniteness, and relevant published mathematical records did not identify the exact codimension-one beta law or its moment phase boundary. An equivalent specialized principal-angle or random oblique-projector theorem could exist. Another PSD-focused streaming paper [5] was inspected at abstract level only, leaving a bounded residual risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The full-rank isotropic identity is the canonical positive-definite reference matrix and makes a clean, parameter-dependent stress test of a randomized matrix-compression primitive already used in Lyapunov-equation solvers. The law quantifies exactly how independent left/right sketching plus singular truncation can destroy covariance positivity, including a nontrivial oversampling-controlled heavy-tail exponent and moments, rather than just showing an arbitrary negative entry. These structural diagnostics can inform when a PSD-aware compressor is necessary. The theorem makes no assertion about low-rank approximation accuracy for this high-rank input or convergence of any entire differential equation discretization.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Lam–Ceruti–Kressner (arXiv:2409.06384v1, Sec. 2.3), Nakatsukasa (arXiv:2009.11392, Secs. 1–3), Tropp et al. (arXiv:1609.00048, PSD sections), and the expected-error identity in DOI:10.1002/nla.2576 were compared. Qualitative obliqueness and Gaussian/Wishart statistics are prior knowledge. The exact law holds for rank-one truncation of identity inputs with a codimension-one sketch range in exact arithmetic; general matrices and numerical time stepping are not covered.
