# Scientific assessment: Gaussian oblique tangent normal leakage

## Correctness — PASS

The result is defined for a nonzero real rank-one state, one unit Frobenius normal rank-one forcing, and two independent Gaussian sketches of size \(s\ge2\). The one-sided oblique operators match the canonical projectors in Carrel–Grigori, Lemma 4.2. Matrix multiplication gives the exact leakage block \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-ab&a\\b&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and squared norm \(a^2+b^2+a^2b^2\). Orthogonality of the input directions makes the two Gaussian numerator vectors independent of their denominator vectors; conditioning proves independent normal-over-chi slopes. The resulting beta ratios yield the exact finite-sample law and moment threshold. A convolution argument with an integrable boundary correction proves the sharp logarithmic coefficient, not merely a proportional tail bound. Identity-side specialization establishes the precise one-sided contrast. All boundary conditions (almost-sure nonzero denominators, \(s=2\) infinite mean square, \(s>2\) finite mean square, moment endpoints) are explicit. A separate standalone checker passed 3,221 assertions with a fixed seed. Simulations are supporting checks, not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, with residual priority risk

Carrel and Grigori (arXiv:2607.03402, §§2 and 4, Lemma 4.2, Remark 4.3, Proposition 4.4) give the exact operator and a qualitative large-perpendicular-residual failure mechanism, together with conditional sketch-embedding bounds. They do not directly state the independent reciprocal-beta product law, complete finite-sample tail CDF, exact moment cutoff, or squared-vs-norm logarithmic extreme-tail comparison. Balabanov and Nouy (arXiv:1803.02602, Galerkin error analysis) address embedding-controlled approximation, not this unconditional rank-one tangent projector law. Niu et al. (arXiv:2605.24955, oblique subsampled OLS/CUR) address different oblique projectors and statistical limits. Dereziński et al. (arXiv:2006.10653v3, §§1–2 and theorem statements, appendix proof structure) analyze expected **orthogonal** residual projections and do not imply this oblique two-sided distribution. Stewart's 2011 deterministic projector paper was available at abstract level only. Searches across mathematical research records for exact aliases, reciprocal beta variables, two-sided Gaussian oblique tangent leakage, inverse-Wishart projection tails, and one-sided/two-sided comparisons revealed no matching stronger result. Familiar beta-prime and independent-product asymptotic facts are fully acknowledged, and the novelty claim is limited to their joint exact application to the current numerical operator. Full historical priority remains uncertain and should be independently checked.

## Value — PASS

The underlying numerical-analysis paper identifies the importance of large normal forcing in oblique dynamical low-rank projections. The theorem supplies its first minimal nontrivial, fully quantified unconditional error model: a complete error law, sharp power-and-log tail with coefficient, exact oversampling threshold for finite mean square, and comparison of one- and two-sided sketch choices. At \(s=2\), every individual Gaussian sketch remains injective on the rank-one state almost surely, yet the projection error has infinite mean square. This distinguishes almost-sure well-posedness and high-probability embeddings from unconditional moment control and offers a concrete sketch-risk diagnostic. The analysis does not claim full ODE instability, finite-time errors, correlated-sketch extensions, or finite-precision behavior.
