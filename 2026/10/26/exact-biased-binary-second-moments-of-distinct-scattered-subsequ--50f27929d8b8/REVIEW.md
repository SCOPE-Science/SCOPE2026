# Research review: biased-binary scattered-subsequence second moments

**Correctness — PASS.** Each distinct nonempty subsequence has a unique final symbol. The exact two-counter word update is proved from this disjoint partition, with no dependence on how many embeddings realize an output. The first-moment matrix, quadratic-moment matrix, and initial moment values determine every asserted equation for arbitrary length. The spectral inequality is strict for every interior bias and is supported by a direct polynomial sign argument. An independent enumerator counts full subsequence sets and verifies the resulting rational moments through length 10 for five probabilities. Finite enumerations are confirmation, not infinite certification.

**Originality — PASS (qualified).** The central comparison is the published 2017–18 study of biased means by Biers-Ariel, Godbole and Kelley, especially the explicit further question about concentration. Collins gives the unbiased mean. Stanley's 2019 Stern-array power-sum recurrence substantially overlaps the *unbiased* second-moment mechanism, so the symmetric case is not treated as separately novel. The claim instead concerns the complete biased family, its parameterized cubic law, and a strict all-bias relative-fluctuation exponent; these statements were not located in the inspected primary texts or semantic database results. Unindexed weighted Stern-product treatments are a residual risk, as are any uninspected alternate presentations of the biased second moment.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Distinct scattered-subsequence counts are an intrinsic statistic of words and deletion channels. The 2017 paper explicitly calls concentration widths an open direction. This result settles an exact nontrivial moment statistic for every Bernoulli bias and proves an unusual exponential divergence of squared relative fluctuations, an obstacle for naive second-moment concentration around the mean. It is not merely a finite table or a uniform substitution: the bias changes the moment recurrence to a genuine cubic and requires a strict Perron-growth comparison.

Scientific limits: no quenched typical-value or tail theorem follows from the moment calculation, and higher moments and Markov inputs remain open here. Comparisons against broadly formulated weighted Stern power-sum results remain a literature risk.
