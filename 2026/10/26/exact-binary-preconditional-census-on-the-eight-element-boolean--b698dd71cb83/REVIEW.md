# Scientific review: binary preconditionals on the eight-element Boolean algebra

## Correctness — PASS
The normal form is an equivalence, not merely a necessary restriction: P1--P4 force the top row, dependence on the intersection with the antecedent, extensivity and monotonicity, and P5 is precisely the displayed family of row-coherence inclusions. Conditional identity and semicomplementation give the remaining row boundaries. For three atoms, doubleton rows have an exhaustive list of at most eighteen raw candidates, with the only cross-antecedent constraints supplied by the two singleton sub-antecedents. This proves the factorization of the census over three doubletons. The sixteen relabelling orbits exhaust the sixty-four singleton-row choices, yielding \(7820\). For \(\mathsf F\), self-adjointness of the antitone negation forces a symmetric loop-free three-atom relation, and each of its four edge-count types gives the asserted exact fiber sizes. The finite checker reconstructs all \(7820\) operations and tests all five original axioms directly; it finds exactly \(1021\) satisfying the additional \(\mathsf F\) condition. The small-carrier uniqueness boundary follows from explicitly forced rows.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
The principal antecedents are Chen, arXiv:2607.20221, especially Sections 3.1--3.2 defining the two subclasses, and Holliday, arXiv:2402.02296, especially Sections 2.1--2.5. Holliday's Proposition 7 concerns a strictly more constrained selection-frame subclass rather than all operations counted here; his general representations are not an explicit binary enumeration. Chen establishes proof theory, finite model property and modal translations, not the three-atom binary census. Public published-finding corpus work on finite chains describes different lattice carriers, and unary-negation classifications supply only reduct counts \(125\) and \(8\), not the binary fibers \(7820\) and \(1021\). Targeted semantic and text searches did not identify a published equivalent enumeration. This absence is not proof of uniqueness: an overlooked general counting theorem or previously unpublished table could imply the same numbers.

## Value — PASS
An exact smallest-carrier classification clarifies a natural structural issue in an actively studied conditional logic: to what extent does the negation induced by a conditional determine the binary connective? For Boolean carriers of size at most four it determines the connective; at size eight it ceases to do so, strikingly with \(729\) fundamental-logic-compatible extensions of a single unary negation. The exact binary censuses are not consequences of knowing the number of unary reducts; the complete three-atom finite cutoff and graph-indexed fibers distinguish genuinely different conditional enrichments of the same Boolean algebra. The theorem makes no claim of a full census at higher cardinalities or of any general downstream theorem.
