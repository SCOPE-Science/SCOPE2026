# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The digit-coordinate proof uses exact nonnegative exponent indices and handles both the top-block zero padding and the one-block boundary. For high columns, the combined two reversal symmetries imply a reflected-column recurrence between adjacent blocks; zero high columns propagate from the outer block. Low columns then become individually symmetric. Both directions of the characterization are established. Independent block choices give an exact formula with an explicit nonzero top digit. Summing two-to-one exponential block-length layers proves the rational generating function and both cutoffs. The two normalized constants differ strictly by arithmetic-geometric mean, excluding a universal leading constant. Direct finite digit-reversal tests are auxiliary evidence only.

## Originality

**PASS with residual literature risk.** Bérczes–Ziegler explicitly identify infinitely many solutions for dependent-power bases, but treat their more substantive estimates only for multiplicatively independent bases. The exact all-length structure, counting formulas, rational series, parity-dependent sparsity exponent, and unequal cutoff constants are not implied merely by those exhibited examples. Bašić varies the bases and fixes prescribed digit strings, whereas the result fixes a dependent pair and counts all its intersections. Phunphayap–Pongsriiam compare separate marginal totals, which do not determine an intersection. Targeted literature and semantic-prior comparisons found no matching or dominating statement. A theorem with equivalent combinatorial content may nevertheless be present in unlocated sources.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The 2014 simultaneous-palindrome investigation separates dependent powers as the elementary infinite regime but leaves its quantitative intersection structure undeveloped. This theorem identifies a complete natural family (all bases, all integral exponents, all lengths) rather than a selected numerical slice. It shows the intersection is sparse of exact order \(X^{1/4}\) for every even exponent and of order \(X^{(k+1)/(4k)}\) for odd exponents, with genuinely distinct growth-normalized constants. These baseline counts distinguish existence from frequency and support precise quantitative comparisons with the difficult independent-base regime.
