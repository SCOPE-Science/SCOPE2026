# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The exact theorem is proved by Rayleigh quotients of factor-only test vectors, aggregation of independently weighted parallel fibers, and attainable Kronecker-sum designs. The grid refinement is independently pinned down by centered coordinate Rayleigh bounds, polynomial-preserving weighted-path operators with eigenvalues \(\ell r(r+1)/2\), and an equality-case recurrence that forces **every** fiber's rates. This proves existence, optimal value, complete spectrum, Fiedler multiplicity and uniqueness for all side lengths and dimensions; no finite simulation substitutes for an infinite-family proof. The supplied standalone checker passed eight grid configurations, twenty coordinate eigenvector identities, eight spectrum comparisons, 1,440 asymmetric competitor comparisons and an exact non-path product case.

## Originality — PASS, with specific residual risk

The single-path parabola is not new: Sun, Boyd, Xiao and Diaconis (2006), Section 5.2, explicitly derive it. The ordinary Cartesian-product spectrum in de Abreu (2006/2007) likewise is prior art. The relevant 2026 paper by Indrani Y. R. L. and collaborators already balances *constant directional multipliers*; its full discussion explicitly excludes individual-edge optimization. The stated unrestricted-fiber theorem, the rectangular-grid global **unique** optimizer, and its full spectrum are strictly broader in the edge-rate decision space and are not stated in that restricted paper. Wan et al. (2008) offer general edge-design structure without this classification; Shakeri et al. (2015) tune interlayer edges with other edges fixed. Searches for absolute algebraic connectivity, fastest-mixing processes, Cartesian products, grid optimal weights, parameter equivalence and spectral-gap resource allocation found no same unrestricted classification among the inspected sources and indexed claims. This is evidence of noncoverage of these checked materials, not a uniqueness proof. Residual risk: an overlooked specialized grid optimization theorem or a direct corollary not explicitly highlighted in general semidefinite design literature.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The precise optimization problem is natural for diffusion, consensus and continuous-time random walks under a limited edge-rate budget. Tensorization gives an infinite-class exact reduction **even for competitors lacking Cartesian symmetry**. Rectangular grids give a fully explicit design without any unknown factor solver, strict uniqueness for an entire family containing many cycles, the full optimized spectrum and exact multiplicity of the slow mode; the dimension-independent cubic-grid improvement approaches \(12/\pi^2\), a nontrivial resource-efficiency boundary. It is not a numerical sweep, parameter renaming, or a restatement of the known one-dimensional path case.

## Scope and unresolved comparisons

The objective is combinatorial spectral gap, not normalized or discrete-time mixing. No claim is made that the general-factor optimum is unique. The result assumes unit cost per unit edge conductance. The closest relevant restricted-directional prior is the 2026 generalized Cartesian-products paper; its per-edge exclusion was inspected in full-text material. The older underlying path and product-spectral ingredients are correctly attributed. Possible specialized prior coverage remains; no independent audit has been performed.
