# Exact census and ordinarity of genus-nineteen space-filling quartic sections over the binary field

## Finding

Let \(k=\mathbb F_2\) and consider the smooth cubic surface
\[
S=V(G)\subset\mathbb P^3_k,\qquad
G=X^2Y+XY^2+Z^2W+ZW^2.
\]
Let \(J\) be the homogeneous ideal of the \(15\) points of \(\mathbb P^3(k)\), and let \(\mathcal V=J_4/(G\cdot k[X,Y,Z,W]_1)\) denote the space of quartic sections of \(S\) containing every rational point. Then \(\dim_k\mathcal V=16\). Among its \(2^{16}=65536\) elements, precisely \(288\) yield complete intersections that are nonsingular at all rational points; precisely \(144\) yield geometrically smooth curves. **All \(144\) geometrically smooth sections are ordinary smooth geometrically integral curves of degree \(12\) and genus \(19\).**

Thus, within this entire finite linear system, geometric smoothness implies maximal \(2\)-rank of the Jacobian. The claim is an exact census of sections on the *fixed* cubic \(S\), not a census modulo automorphisms of \(S\) or of all cubics in \(\mathbb P^3_k\).

## Assumptions and scope

Everything is defined over \(k=\mathbb F_2\). A quartic \(F\) must vanish at each of the \(15\) rational projective points. Two quartics give the same section of \(S\) if they differ by \(G L\) with \(L\) homogeneous linear; over \(\mathbb F_2\) there is no additional nontrivial scalar identification. The zero class is included among \(65536\) quartic classes but never counted among smooth curves. Smoothness means geometric smoothness over \(\overline{k}\), checked in all four standard affine charts. The statements about ordinarity and genus pertain only to geometrically smooth curves.

## Proof

Set \(\phi_{ij}=X_i^2X_j-X_iX_j^2\) for \(0\le i<j\le3\). The ideal of rational projective points is generated by these six cubic Frobenius binomials (see the primary reference). Its degree-four part \(J_4\) is spanned by the \(24\) products \(X_\ell\phi_{ij}\). Compute these as vectors of coefficients of the \(35\) degree-four monomials. Binary Gaussian elimination gives rank \(20\). The four vectors \(GX_\ell\) are linearly independent, because \(G\ne0\), hence \(\dim_k\mathcal V=20-4=16\). The accompanying checker constructs a canonical \(16\)-vector complement: first inserts the four vectors \(GX_\ell\), then adds each independent \(X_\ell\phi_{ij}\) in lexicographic loop order. Every class is represented uniquely by a \(16\)-bit mask. Its polynomial is the sum of the corresponding basis vectors.

At all \(15\) rational points the gradient of \(G\) is nonzero. For each of the \(65536\) masks the checker evaluates the gradients of \(G\) and \(F\) directly in \(k\) and asks that their binary vectors be linearly independent. The resulting list has exactly \(288\) masks. This is an exhaustive check over all sections, not a random sample.

For each of the \(288\) surviving masks and each chart \(X_i=1\), the checker forms the affine ideal generated by the dehomogenized \(G,F\) and the three \(2\times2\) minors of their \(2\times3\) Jacobian matrix. Over an algebraic closure the chart has no singular point if and only if this ideal is the unit ideal. An exact Gröbner-basis computation over \(k\) tests precisely this condition. The computation exhausts the \(288\) masks in five consecutive nonoverlapping intervals, and yields \(144\) masks with the unit ideal on *all four* charts, and \(144\) with a nonempty geometric singular locus. The attached `CENSUS.json` supplies all masks and their results; `verify_filling_census.py` independently regenerates the masks and Gröbner checks. The source of completeness is that all \(65536\) classes are enumerated and the Jacobian ideal is solved by exact ideal membership, not by checking finitely many extension-field points.

For a smooth complete intersection \(C=V(G,F)\) of types \((3,4)\) in \(\mathbb P^3\), the degree is \(12\) and adjunction gives \(\omega_C\cong\mathcal O_C(3+4-4)=\mathcal O_C(3)\), so \(2g-2=36\), hence \(g=19\). The complete-intersection connectedness theorem and geometric smoothness imply geometric integrality. Every such \(C\) contains all \(15\) rational points by construction.

For ordinarity, apply the Koszul resolution
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O(-7)\longrightarrow
\mathcal O(-3)\oplus\mathcal O(-4)\longrightarrow
\mathcal O\longrightarrow\mathcal O_C\longrightarrow0.
\]
Its cohomology gives \(H^1(C,\mathcal O_C)\cong\ker(H^3(\mathbb P^3,\mathcal O(-7))\xrightarrow{\cdot G}H^3(\mathbb P^3,\mathcal O(-4)))\), a \(19\)-dimensional vector space. The source admits the \(20\) Laurent-monomial Cech basis \(X^{-a_0}Y^{-a_1}Z^{-a_2}W^{-a_3}\) with all \(a_i\ge1\) and \(\sum a_i=7\). The target is one-dimensional, and multiplication by \(G\) gives one linear constraint. In characteristic two, the Frobenius action on this kernel, transported through the Koszul comparison, is represented on the ambient Cech basis by multiplying the squaring map by \(GF\): the coefficient from basis vector \(a\) to \(b\) is the coefficient of \(X^{2a_0-b_0}Y^{2a_1-b_1}Z^{2a_2-b_2}W^{2a_3-b_3}\) in \(GF\) (zero for a negative exponent). The checker evaluates this exact coefficient formula and restricts it to an explicit \(19\)-element kernel basis. Every one of the \(144\) geometrically smooth masks has Frobenius matrix rank \(19\) (and stable rank \(19\)); equivalently its Jacobian is ordinary, of \(2\)-rank \(19\).

## Verification

The standalone `verify_filling_census.py` uses Python 3 and SymPy and has no data-file prerequisites. Running it without arguments exhausts all \(65536\) quartic masks, applies all \(288\) four-chart Gröbner computations, and outputs the per-class results. For bounded replay one may call the script with consecutive slice arguments \((0,45)\), \((45,105)\), \((105,165)\), \((165,225)\), and \((225,288)\), then combine its resulting JSON files. Those five actual replay intervals have smooth-counts \(23,30,30,29,32\), respectively, and all \(144\) accepted smooth curves have computed Hasse–Witt rank \(19\) and stable rank \(19\). They also have nonsmooth-counts \(22,30,30,31,31\). `CENSUS.json` records all \(144\) smooth masks, their Hasse–Witt ranks, and all \(144\) excluded masks. All arithmetic in the checker is exact in characteristic two; the Gröbner computations use SymPy over \(\mathbb F_2\).

## Relationship to prior work

Campbell, Dedvukaj, McCormick, Moon and Morales construct smooth space-filling complete-intersection curves in \(\mathbb P^3_{\mathbb F_q}\) and give the cubic-plus-quartic construction for \(q=2\), including the displayed symplectic cubic. Their paper concerns existence, degree and examples, rather than a complete enumeration of all quartic sections of this fixed cubic or the ordinarity of all smooth sections. Cheng develops the broader geometry of \(q\)-bic hypersurfaces but does not treat this exact finite-field quartic-section census in the inspected portions. The contribution here is the **complete exact \(144\)-curve enumeration and uniform maximal Hasse–Witt rank**, not the original construction of these curves or the general Frobenius-binomial ideal.

## Limitations

The count concerns embedded quartic sections on this fixed cubic over \(\mathbb F_2\), without identifying curves up to projective automorphism or isomorphism. No classification is asserted for other ambient cubics or larger finite fields. The finite computation is independently reproducible from a disclosed algorithm but has not received independent external audit. A residual literature-overlap risk remains for follow-up work or ancillary computational tables not found in the inspected sources. The proof relies on an exact finite enumeration and Gröbner certificate generation, not on a closed-form conceptual enumeration of the \(144\) curves.
