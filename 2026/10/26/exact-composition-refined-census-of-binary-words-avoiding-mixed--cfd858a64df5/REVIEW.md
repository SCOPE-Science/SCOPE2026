# Scientific review of the exact binary mixed-Abelian-square avoidance census

## Correctness — PASS
A five-run obstruction, a three-run interior-parity characterization, and an exact four-run necessity/sufficiency argument together cover **all** finite binary words. Counting the resulting positive integer solutions proves the fixed-composition formula, and summing geometric series proves the rational generating function and cubic leading term. The parity and ordering claims are checked directly rather than inferred from finite examples. An independent direct-factor verifier checked every word of lengths 1–16 and all corresponding compositions, with randomized longer tests; finite tests do not prove the theorem.

## Originality — PASS within examined sources
The 2026 paper by Fazekas, Mammoliti, Mercaş and Simpson provides the five-run obstruction and an effective four-run construction, both credited. The present all-word if-and-only-if classification and its exact fixed-composition enumeration are not stated there as a result. The 2014 Fici–Saarela treatment and 2018 Simpson survey address occurrence minima; Balado–Silvestre's general run enumeration does not impose these avoidance constraints. Relevant prior mathematical records and the available local claim history were compared for implication-level overlap. A prior unindexed equivalent enumeration remains possible and no publication-priority guarantee is asserted.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The theorem gives a complete composition-refined census and rational generating function for a natural zero/nonzero phenomenon in abelian-square avoidance, as well as the sharp cubic asymptotic. This yields the exact number of candidates possessing no mixed abelian-square factors at every fixed word length and composition, an invariant directly relevant to the 2026 study of words with few abelian squares. The full binary classification is not an arbitrary short instance and does not depend on an unproved global minimization conjecture.

## Scope and remaining risk
Only contiguous factors in linear binary words are considered; monochromatic abelian squares are allowed. A closest source supplies substantial ingredients but does not state the refined exact count. An unindexed equivalent result cannot be ruled out; no independent validation was commissioned.
