For every normal complex \(3\times3\) matrix, the exact Haar mean-squared error of predicting a second numerical evaluation from the first is classified by the spectrum: three noncollinear points reveal all diagonal masses, whereas three collinear distinct points leave precisely \(|D|^2/24\) of diagonal error. The optimal nonlinear prediction can beat the best affine prediction by exactly 25%, and the loss can jump from \(3/8\) to zero under an arbitrarily small noncollinear spectral perturbation.
