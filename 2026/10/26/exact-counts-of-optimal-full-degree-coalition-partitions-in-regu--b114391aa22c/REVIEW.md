# Review: exact counts of optimal full-degree coalition partitions in regular graphs

**Correctness (PASS).** The key vertex-cover equivalence is immediate for \(k\)-regular graphs. The partner-graph degree bound of two, the existence of a matching of two partners, and two disjoint cover unions prove the four-block obstruction. Complete construction proves the numerical dichotomy. The novel enumeration accounts for every global bipartition orientation and every pair of nontrivial color-class splits, then corrects exactly for partitions supporting two distinct partner matchings. No partition can support three such matchings when the graph has an edge. Finite crosschecks count all 174 labeled regular graphs through six vertices and larger disconnected families using independent routines; they are not substituted for proof.

**Originality (PASS; limited bibliographic certainty).** Jafari–Alikhani–Bakhshesh (arXiv:2407.09332) originated \(k\)-coalitions and bounds. Brešar–Henning–Samadi (arXiv:2507.18306, Theorem 4.2) already determined the *numerical* full-degree invariant in cubic graphs, using a partner-degree argument. Kaneshiro (arXiv:2608.05403, Theorem 3.7) records \(3\le C_k(G)\le4\) for regular graphs. The numerical extension alone is a close adaptation and is not represented as an independent major advance. The assessed additional result is the exact enumerator of *all* optimal four-block partitions for arbitrary regular bipartite graphs, with a nonzero correction in the disconnected case; it is not stated by these sources, and targeted record queries found no matching counting claim. An unindexed prior enumeration remains a risk.

**Value (PASS).** The theorem determines a natural complete optimum-partition census, not just existence or a numerical maximum. It exposes a shape-invariant count across an infinite class and a component pairing correction invisible in the connected case: \(F_4(C_4\sqcup C_4)=97\), versus naive \(98\). The result applies for all degrees at least two and any number of connected components. Its scope excludes irregular graphs and partition orbits under automorphism.
