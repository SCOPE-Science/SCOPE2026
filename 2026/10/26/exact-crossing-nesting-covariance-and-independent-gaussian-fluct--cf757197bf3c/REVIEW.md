# Scientific review: crossing–nesting joint fluctuations

## Correctness — PASS

The asserted law on ordered uniform matchings is exactly represented by ranking independent continuous endpoints of individually labeled chords. Conditional crossing and nesting probabilities for a first chord with endpoints \(u<v\) have been independently integrated using the correct density \(2\) on the ordered triangle. Their centered first projections have the positive-definite diagonal covariance \(I_2/45\). The canonical order-two remainders are conditionally mean-zero, yielding the exact finite covariance matrix and an \(O(n^2)\) mean-square remainder. The ordinary multivariate iid central limit theorem then yields independent Gaussian limits at the \(n^{3/2}\) scale. Exact-rational exhaustive small matching enumerations and symbolic integration confirm the formulas without serving as the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS for the joint limit, with historical-priority risk

The 2005 Klazar result and 2006 Kasraoui–Zeng bivariate continued fraction provide substantially stronger *finite distributional information* than an isolated finite covariance formula. The finite covariance is explicitly acknowledged as extractable from those predecessors, so it is not treated as a standalone priority claim. The 2017 Khare–Lorentz–Yan moment machinery likewise concerns exact matching moments. Existing crossing-number CLTs (Féray, Paguyo, and Arenas-Velilla–Arizmendi–Paguyo) address a single coordinate, not the bivariate crossing–nesting law. The result reviewed here is the independent two-coordinate Gaussian limit and the cancellation of the \(n^3\)-order cross-covariance through projection orthogonality. No inspected full-text source states or immediately entails that explicit independence assertion without additional calculations. A broader theorem or an unlocated derivation may affect ultimate historical priority.

## Value — PASS

Joint fluctuations are natural because every pair of chords is exactly one of crossing, nesting, and alignment. Their finite negative dependence makes asymptotic independence nonobvious: the covariance is of order \(n^2\) even though both variances are of order \(n^3\). The orthogonal-projection mechanism resolves this structural question and gives a full three-coordinate Gaussian limit with the unavoidable conservation constraint.

## Scientific limitations

The theorem applies to uniformly random perfect matchings, not to general set partitions or nonuniform models. The limit is Gaussian only after centering and scaling, and the two counts remain dependent at any fixed size. Finite symbolic and exhaustive computations supplement but do not substitute for the analytic argument. Existing exact generating functions dominate the finite distribution; independent external audit and definitive bibliographic priority are not claimed.
