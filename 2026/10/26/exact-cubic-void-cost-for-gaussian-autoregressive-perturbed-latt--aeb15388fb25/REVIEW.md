# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The upper bound converts interval emptiness into an explicit lower bound on Gaussian displacement energy, then controls its probability through an exact determinant moment formula and a Toeplitz operator-norm bound. The lower bound constructs a finite, two-direction evacuation profile. An exact discrete energy identity gives the same cubic coefficient; the Gaussian autoregressive density supplies its probability, and conditional Gaussian tails control all unconditioned exterior lattice sites. The integer-to-real interval extension is monotonic. The 15,353 standard-library exact-rational tests support, but do not replace, these universal arguments.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Flimmel's 2026 mixing result gives general bounds without the cubic coefficient; the independent-displacement work of Elboim–Spinka–Yakir gives only order-level Gaussian hole asymptotics; Gabrielli's correlated displacement study and Flimmel's Gaussian fitting work focus on correlation and second-order structure rather than rare empty intervals. Targeted mathematical-record and literature searches found no implication that already fixes this AR(1) dependence-sensitive constant. An implicit version in a general Gaussian obstacle or large-deviation theorem remains possible. The independent special case is not singled out as the new contribution.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Void probabilities are a fundamental geometric characteristic of point processes. This exact sharp decay rate goes beyond general mixing upper bounds and identifies how positive spatial dependence changes the leading cost of evacuating an increasingly long region. The result applies to a natural continuous family of dependent Gaussian perturbation fields and expresses its coefficient using the field's long-run covariance.
