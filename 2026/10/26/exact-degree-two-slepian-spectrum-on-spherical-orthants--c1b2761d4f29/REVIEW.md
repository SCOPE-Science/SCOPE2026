# Scientific review: degree-two orthant concentration

**Correctness — PASS.** The precise domain is the degree-exactly-two complex harmonic eigenspace on the probability-normalized sphere, with open coordinate orthant and integer ambient dimension at least three. The proof is a full operator calculation, not a numerical extrapolation. The gamma/Dirichlet fourth-moment formula yields an orthogonal diagonal/off-diagonal basis and the entire restricted Gram matrix. The unsigned incidence matrix decomposes the edge modes into constant, standard, and kernel sectors; the standard sector is an explicitly repeated symmetric two-by-two block. The eigenvalues, multiplicities, equality case, and spectral gap follow without unstated regularity assumptions. The inequalities comparing the top branch with the standard branch hold for every dimension. A separate determinant computation orders the two lower branches; its strict sign uses a classical rational enclosure for \(\pi\). The two asymptotic limits follow from explicit leading coefficients. Recorded independent numerical matrix constructions and quadrature agree in all checked dimensions, but do not substitute for the general proof.

**Originality — PASS (best knowledge, not independent certification).** Full textual sections of Michel–Plattner–Seibert (arXiv:2103.14650, Sections 3 and 5) describe the general Slepian concentration matrix and commuting operators for spherical caps, not the explicit all-dimensional orthant quadratic spectrum. The manuscript of Bates–Khalid–Kennedy (arXiv:1608.03031 and DOI 10.1109/TSP.2016.2646668; title, analytical formulation, and conclusion inspected) treats arbitrary colatitude/longitude patches on the two-sphere, so its method **does** apply to the three-dimensional octant as an implicit spectral problem. It does not give the dimension-uniform result, the general orthant optimizer, or the exact gap and \(n^2\) concentration amplification. Aslam–Khalid (arXiv:2010.07266, Sections II-D and IV) is another general numerical operator framework. Published findings on global fourth norms for quadratic harmonics and on hemispherical parity laws address different objectives. Earlier formulas for degree-two spherical lunes address a different region. A published formula for a general patch matrix could imply a fixed-dimension instance after additional computation; the scope of originality claimed here is the explicit complete orthant family, its optimizer and spectral structure. Unindexed equivalent formulas, including in signal-processing literature or representation-theoretic accounts of spherical orthants, remain possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** An orthant is a canonical non-axisymmetric spherical region with exponentially small area. This theorem solves its exact degree-two spectral concentration problem in every ambient dimension, classifies every eigenspace and multiplicity, and exhibits a unique zonal extremizer despite the absence of rotational symmetry. The sharp stability gap and universal \(2n^2/\pi^2\) enhancement over the volume fraction are motivated quantities for localized quadratic spherical data. A small single-octant calculation alone would have limited value; the all-dimensional closed spectrum, exact spectral ordering, and quantitative asymptotic distinction are the substantive content.

**Limitations and residual risks.** The analysis is confined to one homogeneous degree, coordinate orthants, and uniform spherical measure. The verification code checks finitely many dimensions and does not prove absence of older equivalent publications. The 2017 patch paper supplies an exact general matrix recipe for the two-sphere, so the dimension-three specialization should not be described as an independent conceptual solution of the original concentration problem. The present result is not a solution for higher degrees, general cones, or arbitrary spherical polygons. No outside mathematical audit has been performed.
