# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The sample-path identity follows by induction on coupled departure recursions, including ties and sharp failure below the service floor. Its stationary mean follows from the two Pollaczek–Khinchine identities. For the zero-duration boundary the published Borel-group IPA/mass-transport identity is valid without downstream exponential memorylessness, as explicitly checked term by term; a compound-Poisson batch comparison uniformly bounds the remainder, leaving the two-customer group with coefficient \(-\lambda\). The station-two positivity and exponential-moment assumptions support the limiting and differentiation steps. The exact finite verifier confirms the deterministic identity but is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
**PASS with residual risk.** The relevant 2026 full text proves downstream waiting monotonicity and obtains a group derivative, not the stated floor-dependent exact sample-path law or a universal second-order boundary coefficient. The pre-existing tandem Lindley identity is insufficient by itself to imply the all-coupling stationary expansion without additional bounds. Dedicated record searches for reversed groups, exact shift, tandem queue service floor, and zero-duration curvature found no stronger implication. Older queuing literature could contain the special floor law implicitly; the boundary asymptotic under a general continuous service law remains the key independent contribution.

## Value
**PASS.** Nonincrease alone does not identify the response order near a zero-duration upstream server. The quadratic coefficient exactly calibrates that sensitivity for an entire distributional class and separates universal behavior from the fixed-duration service-floor regime. The deterministic transfer also gives a checkable equality family for the classic tandem comparison, not a mere isolated parameter evaluation.
