# First correction to three-regime concentration for outward-star endomorphisms

## Finding

Let \(r\ge1\). Let \(H_r\) be the monoid generated by a center \(c\) and leaves \(u_1,\dots,u_r\), with \(c^2=c\), \(u_i^2=u_i\), \(u_i u_j=u_j u_i\) for distinct leaves, and \(cu_ic=u_icu_i=cu_i\). Write \(E_r\) for the number of identity-preserving endomorphisms. Then
\[
\frac{E_r}{2^{r(r+1)}}=3+(3r+1)2^{-r}+O(r^2 4^{-r}).
\]
In particular, for a uniform random endomorphism, the probability of lying outside the three previously enumerated regimes is
\[
\left(r+\tfrac13\right)2^{-r}+O(r^2 4^{-r}).
\]
The three regimes are: the center and all leaves map to idempotents containing \(c\); the center, but no leaf, maps to one containing \(c\); and neither the center nor any leaf maps to one containing \(c\). Here “containing” means membership in the support of a word; the defining relations preserve support. Each of these regimes has size \(2^{r(r+1)}\). The new quantitative claim is the first explicit correction and its error bound; the exact finite census and leading three-regime concentration are inputs, not novelty claims.

## Counting input

Let \(u_X=\prod_{i\in X}u_i\) for \(X\subseteq[r]\). The idempotents are \(u_X\) (type 0) and \(cu_X\) (type 1). To see this, the identity \(cu_Xc=cu_X\) follows inductively from \(cu_ic=cu_i\); hence a word with a center reduces to \(u_Xcu_Y\). If \(X\subseteq Y\), successive relations \(u_i c u_i=cu_i\) remove the left factors and give \(cu_Y\). Otherwise retract onto the center and a single leaf in \(X\setminus Y\). The resulting \(u_i c\) is not idempotent: the presentation is realized by maps on \(\{1,2,3\}\), with composition from right to left, taking \(c\) to the map \(2\mapsto1\) and \(u_i\) to the map \(3\mapsto2\), all other points fixed. The two orders of composition differ and \((u_i c)^2=cu_i\).

Products of two idempotents have union support. The only order in which they can fail to be idempotent is type 0 followed by type 1, and that product is idempotent exactly when the type-0 leaf support is contained in the type-1 leaf support. It follows that same-type idempotents commute, and mixed-type ones commute precisely under this containment. For idempotents \(P,Q\), the arrow relations \(PQP=QPQ=PQ\) hold if and only if \(PQ\) is idempotent: necessity follows by squaring \(PQ\); for sufficiency, use the product rule to see that \((PQ)P\) and \(Q(PQ)\) are both idempotents of the same union support (when \(Q\) has type 0 and \(PQ\) has type 1, its leaf support is contained in that union).

For a fixed type pattern with \(s\) leaves of type 0 and \(t=r-s\) of type 1, each of the \(r\) target-leaf coordinates permits \(2^s+2^t-1\) membership patterns: either all type-0 bits are zero or all type-1 bits are one, with one overlap. If the center image has type 1, its support is unrestricted, adding a factor \(2^r\); if it has type 0, its bit is another lower bit, changing the pattern count to \(2^{s+1}+2^t-1\). Thus
\[
\frac{E_r}{2^{r(r+1)}}=
\sum_{s=0}^r\binom rs\left(\frac{2^s+2^{r-s}-1}{2^r}\right)^r+
\sum_{s=0}^r\binom rs\left(\frac{2^{s+1}+2^{r-s}-1}{2^{r+1}}\right)^r.
\]
The unit terms are \(s=0,r\) in the first sum and \(s=r\) in the second, giving the three regimes above. This finite census is also stated in [1] (reindex its center-type-0 sum by the number of type-0 leaves).

## Proof of the correction

For sufficiently large \(r\), in the first sum the terms \(s=1,r-1\) total \(2r\,2^{-r}(1+2^{1-r})^r\); in the second sum the terms \(s=r-1,0\) total \((r+1)2^{-r}(1+2^{-r})^r\). Each factor of the form \((1+O(2^{-r}))^r\) equals \(1+O(r2^{-r})\). These contributions therefore total \((3r+1)2^{-r}+O(r^24^{-r})\).

For the remaining terms let \(m=r\), \(a=s\) in the first sum and \(m=r+1\), \(a=s+1\) in the second. Put \(k=\min(a,m-a)\); all remaining terms have \(k\ge2\). The base of the power is at most \(2^{-k}(1+2^{2k-m})\). If \(k\le m/4\), the power is at most \(2^{-kr}\exp(r2^{-m/2})\). Each \(k\) arises at most twice in each sum; in either sum \(\binom rs\le\binom mk\). The corresponding total is \(O(r^2 4^{-r})\), since \(\sum_{k\ge2}\binom mk z^k\le (mz)^2e^{mz}\) with \(z=2^{-r}\). For \(k>m/4\) the base is at most \(2^{1-m/4}\), and summing the binomial weights gives at most \(2^{2r-rm/4}\) in either sum, which is \(o(r^24^{-r})\). This proves the expansion. If \(M_r=E_r/2^{r(r+1)}\), the exceptional probability is \((M_r-3)/M_r\), giving the stated refinement by expansion of the denominator.

## Scope and verification

The result concerns the outward star, the indicated arrow convention, and uniform counting of identity-preserving maps. It does not classify composition, image size, or other graph families. The finite support-count checks in `verify.py` are illustrative; the error bound above is analytic. The general admissible-image framework in [2] explains why an enumeration is possible; the small-distance terms and the tail estimate identify the first correction, which is not supplied by that framework or by the leading error estimate in [1].
