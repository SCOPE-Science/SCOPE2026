# Scientific review: complementary two-subset graph projection norms

## Correctness — PASS

The proof defines the canonical incidence-matrix projections and computes their induced endpoint norms as absolute electrical-current column sums. The two explicit Laplacian potential patterns solve the full source-sink equations, including each low-order boundary; the full nonzero potential-drop orbit counts lead to the four rational formulas. Symmetry equates all edge columns. The complementary projection identity and two exact consecutive-difference factorizations prove every stated global maximum and limiting value. A separately constructed, exact-rational incidence-matrix implementation checked 753 edge columns on ten graphs, plus 32 edge-multiplicity tables. The infinite-range result uses the proved symbolic identities, not a finite extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS with residual bibliographic uncertainty

The closest directly inspected work is Dilworth–Kutzarova–Ostrovskii (arXiv:2305.12582), whose definitions and invariant-projection framework are used, but whose explicit endpoint computations cover tori and Hamming graphs rather than the two-subset complementary pair. Schild–Rao–Srivastava (arXiv:1708.01632) supplies the transfer-current interpretation and general growth estimates, but not these graph-family exact expressions or distinct maximizing orders. A previously documented general electrical-resistance formula relating complementary column norms is acknowledged; this is not claimed new. Likewise, primitive Johnson-scheme idempotent norms act on vertex functions and do not give the edge Hodge projection constants. Indexed searches found no covering statement. Unindexed or inaccessible exact calculations remain possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The complementary triangular and Kneser graphs form a canonical rank-three association-scheme pair, while the norm of edge electrical transfer currents is a natural stability invariant for Banach-space projections and electrical flow localization. The result is a complete infinite-family computation, with a sharp and unexpected contrast: triangular norms increase to limits, whereas the Kneser cut and cycle norms have different finite maximizing orders, respectively \(8,9\) and \(12\). These exact finite cutoffs and the \(1000/297\) peak are not arbitrary numerical slices; they globally classify a natural invariant across all eligible orders.
