# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. Prime-power Fibonacci ranks and least-common-multiple compatibility give the explicit three-coordinate recurrence, with the fifth-prime exponent invariant. The case split proves the first exact time the exponent state reaches a fixed point; the eight exceptional states and the 7-exponent dropout are explicitly checked. The least-input argument is exact for every integer height at least five. The standalone finite checks independently compare modular Fibonacci ranks with the state transitions and test the closed formula, but are not substitutes for the proof.

## Originality
PASS with residual uncertainty. Luca–Tron (2014), Theorem 2.2, establishes eventual rank stabilization and derives the 2/3/5 exponent recurrence as part of a different self-Fibonacci-divisor problem; it does not give the full 7-smooth hitting-time formula. FitzGibbons–Miller–Verga (2023), Lemmas 4.1–4.2, compute heights of large powers of two, and their other results establish heights for certain constructed powers of ten and existence statements. These special cases and general rank identities are **not** claimed new. The new statement simultaneously handles arbitrary exponents of 2, 3, 5, and 7, its precise boundary exceptions, and the natural sharp constrained least-input problem. Semantic search cannot establish absolute novelty: unknown equivalent literature remains a risk.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The literature explicitly studies the number of iterations required to reach Fibonacci rank-map fixed points. The 7-smooth stratum is the first standard smooth-number family beyond the stable support \(\{2,3,5\}\), where the prime 7 introduces a distinct exit-delay mechanism through \(z(7)=8\). The result yields an exact four-exponent height law and a sharp infinite least-input theorem for every height at least five, rather than a sample census or arbitrary parameter specialization.
