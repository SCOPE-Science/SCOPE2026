For an adjoint-free random Rayleigh–Ritz search on a two-level one-spike operator, every adaptive \(s\)-direction step multiplies the squared spectral gap by an independent \(\operatorname{Beta}((d-1-s)/2,s/2)\) factor, giving exact finite-horizon moments and almost-sure rates. With a fixed budget of forward operator evaluations, one full block strictly beats any nontrivial sequence of smaller blocks in expected spectral gap, although it entails higher storage and reduced-eigenproblem costs.
