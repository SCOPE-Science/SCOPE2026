# Exact finite-\(n\) area–perimeter covariance for random inscribed polygons

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge3\), take \(n\) independent uniform points on the unit circle, ordered counterclockwise. Let \(Y_i\) be the consecutive angular gap divided by \(2\pi\), so \(Y=(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)\sim\mathrm{Dirichlet}(1,\ldots,1)\). The polygon perimeter and area are
\[
P_n=2\sum_{i=1}^n\sin(\pi Y_i),\qquad A_n=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n\sin(2\pi Y_i).
\]
For \(m\ge0\), define
\[
J_m(t)=\int_0^1u^m\sin(tu)\,du,\qquad K_m(t)=\int_0^1u^m\cos(tu)\,du.
\]
Set
\[
s_n=(n-1)J_{n-2}(\pi),\qquad a_n=-(n-1)J_{n-2}(2\pi),
\]
\[
h_n=\frac{n-1}{2}\bigl[-K_{n-2}(\pi)+K_{n-2}(3\pi)\bigr],
\]
\[
d_n=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{3\pi}\bigl[2J_{n-3}(\pi)+J_{n-3}(2\pi)\bigr].
\]
Then the exact finite-sample covariance is
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(P_n,A_n)=nh_n+n(n-1)d_n-n^2s_na_n.}
\]
The right-hand side is finite and exactly evaluable for every integer \(n\ge3\).

## Assumptions and scope

The points are independent and uniform on a circle of radius one. The polygon is the convex hull in circular order. The formula concerns only the covariance of perimeter and area; it does not assert a full joint distribution or a sign theorem for every \(n\). Scaling the circle radius by \(r\) multiplies perimeter by \(r\), area by \(r^2\), and this covariance by \(r^3\).

## Proof

The normalized gaps have constant density \((n-1)!\) on the simplex \(y_i>0\), \(\sum_i y_i=1\). Hence \(Y_1\sim\mathrm{Beta}(1,n-1)\), giving
\[
\mathbb E\sin(\pi Y_1)=s_n,\qquad \mathbb E\sin(2\pi Y_1)=a_n.
\]
The product-to-sum identity yields
\[
\mathbb E[\sin(2\pi Y_1)\sin(\pi Y_1)]=h_n.
\]
For distinct gaps, let \(T=Y_1+Y_2\) and \(U=Y_1/T\). The Dirichlet factorization gives \(T\sim\mathrm{Beta}(2,n-2)\), while \(U\) is uniform on \([0,1]\) and independent of \(T\). Direct integration of the product-to-sum identity gives, for \(0<t<1\),
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[\sin(2\pi Y_1)\sin(\pi Y_2)\mid T=t\right]
=\frac{2\sin(\pi t)-\sin(2\pi t)}{3\pi t}.
\]
Integrating against the beta density and substituting \(u=1-t\) gives
\[
\mathbb E[\sin(2\pi Y_1)\sin(\pi Y_2)]=d_n.
\]
Exchangeability now implies
\[
\mathbb E[P_nA_n]=nh_n+n(n-1)d_n,\quad
\mathbb E[P_n]\mathbb E[A_n]=n^2s_na_n,
\]
which proves the boxed identity.

For exact evaluation, integration by parts gives
\[
J_0(t)=\frac{1-\cos t}{t},\quad J_1(t)=-\frac{\cos t}{t}+\frac{\sin t}{t^2},
\]
\[
J_m(t)=-\frac{\cos t}{t}+\frac{m\sin t}{t^2}-\frac{m(m-1)}{t^2}J_{m-2}(t)\quad(m\ge2),
\]
\[
K_0(t)=\frac{\sin t}{t},\quad K_m(t)=\frac{\sin t}{t}-\frac{m}{t}J_{m-1}(t)\quad(m\ge1).
\]
At \(t\in\{\pi,2\pi,3\pi\}\), these recurrences terminate in exact rational combinations of powers of \(\pi\).

## Verification

The recurrence gives \(\operatorname{Cov}(P_3,A_3)=10/(3\pi^2)\). For \(n=4\), it gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(P_4,A_4)=\frac{4(216-19\pi^2)}{3\pi^4},
\]
which is the exact quadrilateral special-case value. Numerical integration of the defining one- and two-gap integrals for \(n=3,4,5,6,7,8\) agrees with the formula to floating-point precision. The formula is exact; numerical checks are corroboration, not its proof. The leading asymptotic covariance \(80\pi^6n^{-5}\) is consistent with the published power-sum expansions and is not claimed as a new asymptotic result.

## Relationship to prior work

Steven Finch's 2016 paper *Random Cyclic Quadrilaterals* gives detailed side and angle laws for four points and explicitly leaves the joint perimeter–area analysis open. The present formula gives an exact all-\(n\) cross-moment calculation; the quadrilateral case is recovered as a consistency check. Bélisle's 2011 paper studies asymptotic area and perimeter behavior separately; only its abstract was accessible here, so it remains a priority risk. Wang, Li, and Xu (2023) develop Dirichlet-gap power-sum expansions and asymptotic extrapolation rules. Those results explain the leading asymptotic covariance, but the inspected text does not state the exact finite-\(n\) covariance recurrence above.

## Limitations

No full joint density for \((P_n,A_n)\) is derived, and no all-\(n\) positivity proof or finite-\(n\) optimal estimator is claimed. The exact formula is a finite integral-reduction identity; independent audit and independent validation have not been performed. The inaccessible full text of Bélisle (2011) leaves a residual historical-priority risk.
