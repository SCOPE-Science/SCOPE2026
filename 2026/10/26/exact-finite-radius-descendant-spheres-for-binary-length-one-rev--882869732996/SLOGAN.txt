For binary length-one reverse-complement duplication, the exact number of distinct descendants after any \(t\) insertions depends only on the seed length \(n\) and its first constant-run length \(r\): \(\sum_{j=0}^{t}\binom{n+t-1}{j}-\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\binom{n+t-r-1}{j}\). The resulting all-radius counting law gives sharp extremal seeds and limiting descendant density despite the many-to-one nature of duplication histories.
