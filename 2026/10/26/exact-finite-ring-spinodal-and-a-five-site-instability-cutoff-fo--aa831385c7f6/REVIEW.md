# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The result is a theorem about the source's two-orientation mean-field ODE rather than a claim about exact finite stochastic dynamics. Direct linearization in density and polarization gives a two-by-two Bloch matrix with strictly negative trace and a determinant whose sign is affine in \(1-\cos k\) after division by the positive factor \(1-\cos k\). This proves the exact full-spectrum criterion, the shortest-mode onset, and the five-site cutoff. The quadratic activity boundary, density optimizer, and finite-size correction follow by exact algebra. An independent spatial-Jacobian checker, symbolic simplifications, and 318 finite assertion groups corroborate the calculations.

Its finite lattice discussion describes numerical stability analysis, whereas the present statement is an all-parameter one-dimensional finite-ring classification, including a universal sharp minimum ring size and an optimal activity/density pair. The hydrodynamic active-lattice-gas papers of Kourbane-Houssene et al., Mason et al., and Neville establish long-wave and related-model physics, not this discrete all-mode cutoff. The source's Bloch formula does imply the present result after a nontrivial two-by-two determinant inequality; unrecognized earlier exact finite-mode analysis remains a priority risk.

## Value

PASS. A finite simulation or mean-field computation can miss an infinite-volume spinodal when the lowest nonconstant mode is prohibited by system size. Here the exact cutoff is unexpectedly absolute for three- and four-site rings, while at five sites linear instability is achievable, with computable density/activity thresholds. The quantified inverse-square shift gives a practical finite-size benchmark. This is not merely a reparametrization of the thermodynamic spinodal because it classifies all discrete modes and determines the minimum possible periodic system size.
