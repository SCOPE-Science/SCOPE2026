**Correctness.** The jump-coordinate inverse reduces output order to entrywise positivity. Factoring it exactly certifies the first parameter cutoffs, including the separate exclusion intervals needed at six samples. Nonnegative jumps summing to at most one give the sharp four-point defect. The small-parameter step witness and the affine-projector limit handle arbitrary fixed lengths. The large-length comparison follows from the negative kernel coefficient and convergence of finite slopes to the bilateral step slopes.

**Originality.** The order cutoffs through length six and the sharp four-point defect are the distinct claim. Prior weight formulas concern data-to-fit filters, not the signs and extrema of the induced jump-to-slope map. Prior range preservation is a different property, with the distinction visible already at length three. Bilateral negative weights and convergence to the bulk kernel in the de Jong–Sakarya work supply the nonuniformity obstruction; it is included as context, not claimed as new.

**Value and scope.** The first exact thresholds give shape-preservation guarantees for an unconstrained smoother without changing the objective, and the sharp defect quantifies the violation when the first nontrivial cutoff fails. There is no full classification above length six and no asserted asymptotic rate for the restoring strength.
