# Scientific comparison and review

## Correctness — PASS
The unrestricted complex degree-one minimization is reduced to a real one-parameter problem by conjugation, uniqueness, and coefficient reversal symmetry. Exact aperiodic autocorrelations yield the objective without numerical approximation. Strict convexity and explicit endpoint derivative signs force the unique cubic root, and the rescaled cubic proves the sharp boundary rate. The accompanying standalone checker independently implements convolution and numeric bisection for several degrees; it does not replace the analytical proof.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk
The closest foundation is Centner–Cheng–Felder, *Optimal Polynomial Approximants in \(H^p\)* (arXiv:2305.16068), Theorem 5.16. Its general positive exclusion radius is not the present all-degree geometric-sum cubic or exact approach to radius one. Cheng–Felder, arXiv:2310.16010, gives additional global Hardy-space properties without a matching stated family formula in inspected sections. Related indexed mathematical findings on Dirichlet polydisks and Hardy bidisk approximation work in different objects. Possible unindexed special-case formulas remain a historical-priority risk; non-detection cannot prove priority.

## Value — PASS
The question whether the universal degree-one Hardy \(H^4\) root lower bound can be improved to one motivates a sharp obstruction: these explicit roots exceed one yet tend to one at a computed inverse-degree rate. This result rules out any stronger universal bound and identifies an exact algebraic family useful for testing general conjectures.

## Scope and limitations
The proof concerns only the geometric-sum inputs and degree-one approximants, not all \(H^4\) functions or arbitrary approximation degrees. Numerical checks are finite corroboration; independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
