# Review of the exact five-support binary lunar prime census

**Correctness — PASS.** Binary lunar multiplication is identified exactly with the Minkowski sum of digit supports. The elementary one-dimensional minimal-sumset characterization reduces every five-element composite to a two-by-three factorization; counting its one-point translate overlap yields two structural families, their exact intersection, and the degree formula. The proof handles all boundary cases, and a standalone exhaustive verifier independently checks 127 degrees and all factor size shapes through degree fourteen. Computation corroborates, but the universal claim depends on the written combinatorial proof.

**Originality — PASS subject to residual priority risk.** OEIS A087636 (2003-10-26) records a general lunar prime counting problem; Applegate–LeBrun–Sloane (2011) develops the underlying arithmetic and broad prime counts; Gross (2019) proves extremal sumset-divisor results; Baily et al. (2025) proves an almost-everywhere indecomposability theorem. None of the examined relevant passages gives the exact fixed-five-support count or the two explicit complete support families. The broad asymptotic result does not imply the degree-specific periodic correction terms. Targeted mathematical record comparisons returned no equivalent fixed-weight count. A different previous result on a specific sparse block family computes lunar divisor counts, not the set-wide prime census. Unindexed or alternate-language fixed-support counts remain a genuine residual risk.

**Value — PASS.** Fixed binary digit support weight is a natural arithmetic refinement of existing lunar-prime counting questions, and weight five is the first level in this argument at which two noncongruent composite-support families overlap, generating an additional divisibility-by-four correction. The result is a complete classification over all degrees, exposes a non-obvious period-twelve correction and sharp quadratic decay of the exceptional proportion within this fixed-weight stratum, and supplies an explicit formula to compare against broader density-one results.

**Limits.** The claim does not enumerate factorization multiplicities, varying binary weights, or larger-base lunar primes. Independent validation and definitive bibliographic priority review remain outstanding.
