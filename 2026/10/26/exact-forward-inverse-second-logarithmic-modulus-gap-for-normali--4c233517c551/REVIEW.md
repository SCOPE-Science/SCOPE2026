# Scientific review: fixed-coefficient forward–inverse logarithmic mismatch

**Correctness (C): PASS.** The proof explicitly derives the Taylor coefficients of a normalized convex map from its Schwarz function, then derives direct/inverse logarithmic coefficients through exact order-three series inversion. Its elementary two-sided norm estimate yields the conditional envelope, and an explicit degree-two Schwarz realization attains both endpoints for every permitted second coefficient. The intermediate-value argument gives the full interval. The unique crossing at \(r=2/5\), the equality conditions for complex absolute values, and the Schwarz–Pick equality case classify all global extremizers. An executable symbolic checker independently confirms the derived identities and sampled cases; its finite checks are not treated as an infinite proof.

**Originality (O): PASS on inspected evidence, with residual priority risk.** The most relevant accessible manuscript, Ali–Raza–Raza, arXiv:2610.20645v1, studies \(\bigl||\gamma_2|-|\gamma_1|\bigr|\) and \(\bigl||\Gamma_2|-|\Gamma_1|\bigr|\), comparing their sharp constants. Dhara–Ghosh, arXiv:2606.11325v1, and Allu–Shaji, arXiv:2403.10031v1, likewise control consecutive-index functionals and inverse estimates. Those statements neither imply the same-index, same-function conditional interval nor classify its cross-direction cancellation extremals. Direct searches of external mathematical sources and a published-results database produced no stronger covering theorem. This is evidence-based novelty, not proof of universal priority; literature in other terminology and abstracts-only sources remain risks.

**Value (V): PASS.** Forward and inverse logarithmic coefficient comparisons are motivated by the current invariance literature, yet agreement of separately optimized sequential bounds does not quantify the actual mismatch for the same function. The present result completely determines a natural continuous family of conditional sharp ranges, identifies a nontrivial transition at \(|a_2|^2=2/5\), and proves exact inverse/direct cancellation rigidity. Its value is a precise classification for the first nontrivial common index, not a claim of consequences for all coefficients or function classes.

**Limits.** The theorem treats the classical convex class and index two only. The literature comparison was limited to accessible primary material and discoverable records; independent external validation has not occurred.
