# Scientific review: exact Fourier concentration on nonbinary Hamming one-balls

**Correctness — PASS.** Character orthogonality makes every concentration matrix entry exactly \(q^{-d}(N-q\operatorname{dist}_H(x,y))\), independent of the finite abelian alphabet. Orthogonal decomposition into within-axis contrasts, between-axis contrasts, and one radial \(2\)-by-\(2\) block produces all \(N\) eigenvalues with correct multiplicities. Its determinant is \(q^2\); its trace is \(T\), so the extreme roots are exact. The strict order follows from \(d\ge3\), and no perfect Fourier localization follows from the existence of two other coordinates. Equalities and the optimal stability gap follow from the spectral theorem. The standalone checker passed 1,875 counted assertion units, including independent cyclic and noncyclic finite Fourier reconstructions. The checks are finite evidence, not an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS (best knowledge).** The closest result on Boolean Hamming balls explicitly assumes an alphabet of two symbols, with no nontrivial within-axis contrast modes. The closest nonbinary Fourier concentration theorem covers a *single shell* of fixed Hamming weight; its first shell has one forced zero singular value, whereas addition of the ball center couples the radial null direction, yields a strictly positive smallest eigenvalue and sharp condition number, and yields a different top profile. A split-quadric result covers a two-coordinate cross \(d=2\), which has perfect localization and therefore does not cover the strict \(d\ge3\) theorem. General uncertainty inequalities and induced-ball adjacency spectral results have different conclusions and operators. Published result-record searches by the exact four eigenlevels, terms “q-ary radius-one ball” and “first Fourier band,” and the joint Hamming ball mask returned no theorem with identical claim. Stronger consequences hidden in older Hamming association-scheme/Krawtchouk literature remain a residual priority risk; a negative search cannot certify uniqueness.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The exactly solved object is the most elementary nontrivial two-sided finite Fourier sampling geometry (a full radius-one ball and its matching frequency band), important as a baseline for sampling, discrete concentration, and condition-number design. The addition of a single central point qualitatively removes the shell's nullspace, while dimension \(d=2\) allows perfect concentration and every \(d\ge3\) strictly forbids it. The result gives the full nonbinary spectrum, every extremizer, an optimal quantitative stability constant, and large-alphabet and large-dimension asymptotics, not merely one numerical instance or a routine restatement of the binary result. The theorem makes no claim about higher radii, and independent priority or independent audit has not been established.

**Closest literature and risk.** Hogan–Lakey, arXiv:1812.08905v1 (2018-12-21), develops Boolean Hamming-ball spatio-spectral limiting. A later exact Boolean first-band ball result covers only \(q=2\); an exact nonbinary first-band shell result covers weight-\(k\) shells, not balls; a finite-field split-quadric first-band theorem includes a special two-coordinate cross. These comparisons were made against their actual mathematical statements, including the full accessible result files, rather than title matching. Residual risk: an older association-scheme compression result might implicitly contain this particular radius-one two-sided spectrum.
