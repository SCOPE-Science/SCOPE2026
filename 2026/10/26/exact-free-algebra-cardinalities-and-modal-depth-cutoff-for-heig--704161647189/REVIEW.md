# Mathematical review

## Correctness (PASS)
The ambient class, direction of accessibility, and meaning of height are fixed. Every strict successor of a nonmaximal point is maximal; the pointed colour-and-strict-successor-set invariant is complete under bisimulation, with the degenerate case collapsing to a terminal colour. Characteristic formulas distinguish every remaining type at depth two. The depth-zero and depth-one signatures are directly characterized, and two explicit rooted structures show that depth one cannot suffice for any positive variable count. The resulting powerset cardinality follows without extrapolating finite experiments. The canonical representative satisfies antisymmetry and height two.

## Originality (PASS, qualified)
Agnew (2026) treats local finiteness qualitatively, and Bezhanishvili–Ghilardi–Jibladze (2014) treat the general iterative construction of unrestricted \(\mathsf{S4}\) free algebras. The finite-height local-tabularity result likewise does not supply the exact three-stage size filtration. No exact height-two partial-order cardinality or equivalent sharp collapse was identified in the available direct searches or in the closest retrieved published records. This is a best-knowledge originality judgment, not a proof of universal priority; older specialized formulas might subsume the calculation.

## Value (PASS)
The exact sizes of the free algebras of a natural finite-height logic are structural invariants controlling normalization and finite exhaustive semantic testing. The characterization of all pointed types, explicit minimal fixed-colouring representative, and sharp depth-two stabilization give more information than qualitative local finiteness. The unrestricted free-\(\mathsf{S4}\) constructions do not by themselves provide the closed finite spectrum given here.

## Limits
The distinction between a fixed coloured representative and an uncoloured frame admitting varied valuations matters. The general proof is independent of, but corroborated by, finite code for \(k=1,2,3\). Historical exact-formula priority may require specialist review.
