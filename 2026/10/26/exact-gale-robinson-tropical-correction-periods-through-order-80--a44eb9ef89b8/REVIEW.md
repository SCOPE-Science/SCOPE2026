# Mathematical assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The integer recurrence and normalization agree with Can's Conjecture 1. The finite affine-shift lemma converts the exact \(m\)-identity certificate at indices \(N+1\) through \(N+m\) into an infinite-time theorem. All 11,052 parameter triples were enumerated twice and the required integer equalities were checked by the included standalone verifier. Least-period minimality follows from integrality, not from a finite period scan.

**Originality — PASS under examined evidence.** Can's 2026 article gives the conjecture, proves only the infinite \((p,q)=(1,2)\) family, and uses the affine-shift proof mechanism; those antecedents are fully credited. The present bounded-order certification covers 10,977 triples outside that family, with exact full-index proofs and the least-period refinement. Galashin–Pylyavskyy prove general quadratic-growth estimates rather than the same all-parameter explicit periodicity result. Older specific Somos and tropical recurrence cases do not establish the full enumerated domain. One relevant older full text was inaccessible, so undiscovered equivalent coverage remains a priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem establishes a sharp, motivated finite-order frontier for an explicit open conjecture on discrete integrable rational maps. The finite domain contains 11,052 distinct systems; 10,977 are outside the source's proved family. Each verification is promoted by a structural lemma to an all-iterations statement, and the exact least correction period adds information beyond the conjectured existence of a period. This remains meaningful bounded progress without claiming resolution of the unbounded conjecture.
