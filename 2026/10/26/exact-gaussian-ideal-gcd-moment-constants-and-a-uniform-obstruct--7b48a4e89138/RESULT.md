# Exact Gaussian-ideal gcd-moment constants and a uniform obstruction to the proposed zeta formula

## Finding
Let \(R=\mathbb Z[i]\), and let \(\mathfrak a,\mathfrak b\) be independently and uniformly chosen from the nonzero integral ideals of \(R\) with norm at most \(X\). Their ideal gcd is \(\mathfrak a+\mathfrak b\). Define
\[
M_n(X)=\mathbb E\big[\mathrm N(\mathfrak a+\mathfrak b)^n\big].
\]
For an integer \(k\ge1\), let \(a(k)=\#\{\mathfrak a:\mathrm N\mathfrak a=k\}\), and set \(A(k)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}a(j)\), \(A(0)=0\). Write \(Z(s)=\zeta_{\mathbb Q(i)}(s)=\sum_{k\ge1}a(k)k^{-s}\). **For every fixed integer \(n\ge2\)**,
\[
\boxed{M_n(X)\sim C_n X^{n-1},\qquad
 C_n=\frac{4}{\pi(n+1)Z(n+1)}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}
 \frac{A(k)^2-A(k-1)^2}{k^{n+1}}.}
\]
The series is absolutely convergent. Moreover the coefficient is *strictly smaller* than the coefficient proposed in Conjecture 1 of Bradley--Cheng--Luo (arXiv:1502.02148v1; Conjecture 4 in the journal typesetting):
\[
\boxed{\frac4{\pi(n+1)}\left(\frac{2Z(n)}{Z(n+1)}-1\right)-C_n
\ge\frac{8}{\pi(n+1)4^{n+1}Z(n+1)}>0.}
\]
Thus the proposed asymptotic coefficient fails for every exponent in the conjecture's stated domain; the corrected coefficient above exists for all those exponents.

## Assumptions and scope
All ideals are nonzero **integral** ideals of \(\mathbb Z[i]\); ideals are counted once, not multiplied by the four unit associates of a generator. The parameter \(X\ge1\) tends to infinity, and the moment exponent \(n\ge2\) is a *fixed integer*. Norms of nonzero ideals are positive rational integers. Sampling nonzero Gaussian integers instead of ideals gives the same gcd-norm distribution: every nonzero ideal has exactly four generator associates, so the ordered sample has a constant multiplicity of sixteen. The greatest common ideal divisor means the ideal sum, equivalently the ideal generated by a Gaussian-integer gcd in this principal ideal domain. We use only the classical Gaussian circle estimate \(A(y)=(\pi/4)y+o(y)\) and elementary convergence of \(Z(s)\) for \(s>1\). No uniformity in \(n\) is asserted.

## Proof
**1. A sharp elementary count.** Each nonzero integral ideal has a unique generator \(a+bi\) with integers \(a\ge1\), \(b\ge0\). Hence \(A(k)\) counts pairs \((a,b)\) with \(a^2+b^2\le k\) in this quadrant. Put \(h=\lfloor\sqrt{k}\rfloor\) and \(r=k-h^2\). The pairs with \(b<h\) number at most \(h^2\), since \(1\le a\le h\); the pairs with \(b=h\) number \(\lfloor\sqrt r\rfloor\le r\). Thus
\[
A(k)\le h^2+r=k \quad(k\ge1).
\]
In particular \(a(k)=A(k)-A(k-1)\) and
\[
A(k)^2-A(k-1)^2=a(k)(A(k)+A(k-1))\le(2k-1)a(k).
\]
At \(k=4\), the actual counts are \(a(4)=1\), \(A(3)=2\), \(A(4)=3\). The left side is \(5\), whereas \((2k-1)a(k)=7\); this inequality is strict by \(2\) at that index.

**2. A convergent coprime-pair max-norm series.** For real \(s>2\) define
\[
U_s=\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{A(k)^2-A(k-1)^2}{k^s},
\qquad
T_s=\sum_{\substack{\mathfrak u,\mathfrak v\ne0\\
\mathfrak u+\mathfrak v=R}}
\max(\mathrm N\mathfrak u,\mathrm N\mathfrak v)^{-s}.
\]
The first series is the sum over all ordered ideal pairs with weight \(\max(\mathrm N\mathfrak a,\mathrm N\mathfrak b)^{-s}\), grouped by their maximum norm. From step 1,
\[
0<U_s\le 2Z(s-1)-Z(s)<\infty.
\]
Each ordered pair factors *uniquely* as \((\mathfrak a,\mathfrak b)=(\mathfrak d\mathfrak u,\mathfrak d\mathfrak v)\), where \(\mathfrak d=\mathfrak a+\mathfrak b\) and \(\mathfrak u+\mathfrak v=R\); the maximum norm is multiplicative under this common factor. Tonelli's theorem therefore gives
\[
U_s=\left(\sum_{\mathfrak d\ne0}\mathrm N\mathfrak d^{-s}\right)T_s=Z(s)T_s.
\]
There is no regularity assumption on the maximum norm beyond integrality.

**3. The moment limit.** Set
\[
W_n(y)=\sum_{\substack{\mathfrak d\ne0\\\mathrm N\mathfrak d\le y}}
(\mathrm N\mathfrak d)^n.
\]
The quadrant circle estimate and partial summation give
\[
W_n(y)\sim\frac{\pi}{4(n+1)}y^{n+1}\qquad(y\to\infty).
\]
The elementary bound of step 1 also gives \(0\le W_n(y)\le y^{n+1}\) for every \(y\ge1\) (and \(W_n(y)=0\) for \(0\le y<1\)). Classifying the two sampled ideals by their gcd and coprime quotients yields the exact finite identity
\[
S_n(X):=\sum_{\substack{\mathrm N\mathfrak a,\mathrm N\mathfrak b\le X}}
\mathrm N(\mathfrak a+\mathfrak b)^n
=\sum_{\mathfrak u+\mathfrak v=R}
W_n\!\left(\frac{X}{\max(\mathrm N\mathfrak u,\mathrm N\mathfrak v)}\right).
\]
For \(s=n+1>2\), each summand divided by \(X^s\) is bounded by \(\max(\mathrm N\mathfrak u,\mathrm N\mathfrak v)^{-s}\). Their sum is \(T_s<\infty\). Dominated convergence consequently shows
\[
\frac{S_n(X)}{X^{n+1}}\longrightarrow\frac{\pi}{4(n+1)}T_{n+1}.
\]
There are exactly \(A(\lfloor X\rfloor)^2\sim\pi^2 X^2/16\) ordered sampled ideal pairs. Division yields
\[
M_n(X)\sim\frac{4T_{n+1}}{\pi(n+1)}X^{n-1}
=\frac{4U_{n+1}}{\pi(n+1)Z(n+1)}X^{n-1},
\]
proving the first formula, including the endpoint exponent \(n=2\).

**4. Strict conflict with the proposed coefficient.** The proposed coefficient equals
\[
C_n^{\mathrm{proposed}}=\frac4{\pi(n+1)Z(n+1)}
\big(2Z(n)-Z(n+1)\big).
\]
For every index \(k\) the difference between the coefficient of \(k^{-n-1}\) in \(2Z(n)-Z(n+1)\) and that in \(U_{n+1}\) is nonnegative by step 1. At \(k=4\) this difference is exactly \(2\). Hence
\[
(2Z(n)-Z(n+1))-U_{n+1}\ge\frac{2}{4^{n+1}},
\]
and multiplication by \(4/[\pi(n+1)Z(n+1)]\) gives the displayed positive gap.

The exact-integer checker confirmed \(A(k)\le k\) and the coefficient inequalities for \(1\le k\le10000\), independently reconstructed ideal-norm counts via \(\zeta(s)L(s,\chi_4)\), enumerated every ordered Gaussian-ideal pair for cutoff norms \(8,15,24,40\), and checked direct Gaussian Euclidean gcd moments \(n=2,3,4,5\) against a separate ideal-Möbius convolution. It emitted `VERIFY_OK`. These finite tests support the exact definitions and grouped identities; the infinite asymptotic and strict inequality follow from the proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Bradley, Cheng and Luo, *On the Distribution of the Greatest Common Divisor of Gaussian Integers* (arXiv:1502.02148v1, 2015; journal version 2016), prove higher-moment existence results and explicitly conjecture the coefficient \(C_n^{\mathrm{proposed}}\) for \(n\ge2\). Their data for exponents two through five notably favor coefficients approximately \(0.63952,0.37018,0.27238,0.21914\), consistent with the newly identified max-norm expression rather than their proposed coefficient. The proposed zeta factor has the same form as the integer-model coefficient, but the strict gap at Gaussian norm \(4\) proves that this factor cannot give the Gaussian leading term. The analogous classical rational-integer gcd moment factor is indeed derived from its one-dimensional count \(A(k)=k\), and cannot be transferred unchanged to Gaussian ideals. Collins--Johnson address Gaussian coprimality probabilities, a different statistic. Search of closely related published research records and inspected original statements found no identical corrected coefficient or universal strict inequality; an equivalent formula in unlocated literature remains a risk.

## Limitations
The result is an asymptotic for fixed integer \(n\ge2\), without an effective error term, uniform growing-exponent estimate, or treatment of higher tuple moments. The method uses unique ideal factorization and a special quadrant counting inequality in \(\mathbb Z[i]\); extensions to other number fields are not established. Numerical calculations cannot certify novelty or replace the analytic proof. Bibliographic coverage is necessarily finite, and independent external validation has not been performed.
