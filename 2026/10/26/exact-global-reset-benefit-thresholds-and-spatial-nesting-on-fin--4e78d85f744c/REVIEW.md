# Scientific assessment

**Correctness: PASS.** The target-hitting generating function solves the two-boundary recurrence and renewal mean exactly; the reset comparison is reduced to a reciprocal polynomial with at most two positive roots by explicit coefficient signs. The endpoint root multiplicities and strict convexity settle all parameter regimes, including the vanishing linear-response boundary. An independently replayed exact-rational script checks special cases but does not replace the general proof.

**Originality: PASS, with priority uncertainty.** The finite-ring mean for reset walks already appears in Riascos et al., arXiv:1910.13568, Section III; this is not claimed as new. Pal--Prasad, arXiv:1812.03009, treats the analogous continuous-time Brownian interval and gives its spatial criterion. Lauber Bonomo--Pal, arXiv:2102.00895, gives the local derivative/variance test and confined discrete-walk formulas. The complete finite-discrete, global-in-reset-probability speedup window and strictly nested target-distance threshold are not stated by these inspected works. Their general formulas could be used to derive parts of the result, and unindexed prior results remain a priority risk.

**Value: PASS.** A local small-reset test does not generally rule out reentrant finite-rate improvement. Here a palindromic two-sign-change certificate proves that the local test is *globally decisive*, while strict spatial nesting gives an exact architecture-dependent search-priority ordering. The finite correction term matters at small cycle sizes, distinguishing the theorem from its continuum limit.

**Scientific limitations:** The theorem concerns unbiased simple cycle walks, fixed-origin update-consuming resets and comparison of *mean* hitting times; it does not establish an optimal reset rate or stochastic dominance. Historical priority needs independent evaluation.
