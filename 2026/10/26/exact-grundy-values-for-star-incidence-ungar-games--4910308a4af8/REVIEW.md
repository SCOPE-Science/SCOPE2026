# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The independent-chain mex recurrence is exact, because removing selected chain tops creates singletons. The high/low induction proves the closed \(F(a,b)\) formula by showing all nimbers below the candidate occur and the candidate does not. A second mex argument establishes the full star-incidence formula, including every boundary at triangular numbers. A separately implemented labelled-poset recurrence agrees on eight complete stars. The latter checks are finite corroboration, not the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Hashimoto's complexity classification, Paltrowitz's graded-poset outcome classification, and Defant–Kravitz–Williams' foundational work do not imply the full nimbers. Gurvich–Ho's studied slow-\(k\)-Nim cases and their separate ordinary Moore-Nim discussion do not settle arbitrary numbers of piles of heights one or two. Searches of research records for star-incidence Ungar games, equivalent slow-Nim rules, the triangular cutoff, and the full two-parameter recurrence found no claim that dominates this one. An equivalent formula under less common selective-compound terminology remains a priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** A family with unbounded size and width receives a fully explicit Sprague–Grundy invariant rather than only a winning-player decision. Its triangular transition points and independent-chain normal form explain behavior under disjunctive sums and expose a mathematically natural graded-poset class beyond the small-pile slow-Nim cases.

**Scope and limitations.** The theorem is restricted to incidence posets of stars and the stated residual classes, not all graph-incidence posets. Originality assessment is a substantive literature comparison, not a guarantee of global uniqueness. Independent mathematical auditing has not been performed.
