# Scientific review: Rudin–Shapiro first-repeat law

## Correctness — PASS
The proof specifies the one-sided sequence, its zero-based indexing, the definition of the first-collision index, and every length range. A four-state lift gives a complete parity-8 factor certificate and a general synchronizing congruence lemma. The independent mathematical bit-shift identity yields four pairwise distinguishable dyadic adjacent-block windows and a collision exactly at \(4B\). The short lengths are exhaustively checked. The bundled checker rederives the critical finite certificate and compares the symbolic formula with direct factor collisions, while unrestricted correctness follows from the proof rather than a finite prefix.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority risk
Nicholson and Rampersad, arXiv:1507.08206, define the invariant and prove word-specific formulas for Thue–Morse, Fibonacci and Tribonacci; the inspected theorem statements and argument do not classify Rudin–Shapiro. Existing Rudin–Shapiro dyadic string-attractor results concern hitting sets, not first repeated-factor positions. Sun and Winterhof, DOI:10.2478/udt-2019-0012, analyze maximum order complexity, which has a different quantifier: two equal contexts with different successors. Its full text was not accessible here, so an incidental stronger lemma cannot be ruled out from an abstract alone. Targeted mathematical-record and literature searches found no equivalent full formula; this is positive overlap assessment with an expressly unresolved priority risk, not a proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The initial non-repetitive complexity is a standard origin-anchored complexity invariant. The finding resolves its entire value sequence for a canonical automatic word and exposes its exact sharp oscillation between normalized limits \(2\) and \(4\). The synchronization and dyadic-block argument supplies structural information beyond a table or a routine substitution of parameters. It does not classify later returns or arbitrary shifted factors.
