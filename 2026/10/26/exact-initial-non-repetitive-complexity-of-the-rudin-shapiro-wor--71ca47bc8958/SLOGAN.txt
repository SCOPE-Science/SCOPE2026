The initial non-repetitive complexity of the binary Rudin–Shapiro word is exactly \(1\) for lengths \(1,2\), \(4\) for lengths \(3,4,5\), and \(4\cdot2^{\lfloor\log_2(n-1)\rfloor}\) for every \(n\ge6\). A dyadic synchronization argument pins down the first repeated factor and yields sharp normalized lower and upper limits \(2\) and \(4\).
