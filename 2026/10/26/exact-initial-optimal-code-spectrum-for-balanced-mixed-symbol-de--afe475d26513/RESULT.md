# Exact initial optimal-code spectrum for balanced mixed-symbol deletions

## Finding

Consider a binary word of even length \(n=2m\) with exactly \(m\) zeros and \(m\) ones. A channel deletes exactly one zero and exactly one one, without revealing their positions, and returns the remaining \(n-2\) symbols in order. A zero-error code is a subset of these balanced source words with pairwise disjoint sets of channel outputs. Let \(A_m\) be its maximum size, and \(M_m\) the number of labeled maximum codebooks (codebooks are sets of words; their word order is immaterial).

**Exact finite classification.** For \(m=1,2,3,4,5\), respectively,
\[
(A_m)_{m=1}^{5}=(1,2,2,6,10),\qquad
(M_m)_{m=1}^{5}=(2,1,40,3,666).
\]
Under the group generated by coordinate reversal and global bit complementation, the numbers of maximum-codebook orbits are, respectively, \(1,1,14,2,174\). More precisely, the numbers of orbits of sizes \(1,2,4\) are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
n&1&2&4\\\hline
2&0&1&0\\4&1&0&0\\6&2&5&7\\8&1&1&0\\10&0&15&159
\end{array}.
\]
The size-\(10\) optimum at length \(10\) has an explicit representative:

```text
0001101110  0011000111  0011111000  0101010101  1000001111
1000111001  1100100011  1101011000  1110001010  1111100000
```

The phenomenon differs from correcting two arbitrary deletions: with the same constant-weight source constraint but allowing any pair of symbols to be deleted, direct exact optimization yields maxima \(1,1,2,4,8\) for \(n=2,4,6,8,10\), respectively. This comparison is contextual; it is not a claim about longer lengths.

## Assumptions and scope

Words are linear, not circular. Coordinates are labeled and source words have exactly half their symbols equal to one. The channel always deletes two *different-valued* symbols, with no substitutions or insertions. The receiver knows this model, not the deleted positions. For any source \(w=w_1\cdots w_n\), define
\[
D_{01}(w)=\{w_1\cdots\widehat{w_i}\cdots\widehat{w_j}\cdots w_n:1\le i<j\le n,\ w_i\ne w_j\},
\]
where hats indicate omitted symbols. The set removes duplicate outputs from different position pairs. A code \(C\) is valid if \(D_{01}(u)\cap D_{01}(v)=\varnothing\) whenever \(u,v\in C\) are distinct. The result covers exactly the five even lengths \(2\le n\le10\); it makes no assertion for larger blocks or unbalanced inputs.

## Proof

**Channel graph reduction.** Let \(W_m\) be all \(\binom{2m}{m}\) balanced words. Every output lies in the \(\binom{2m-2}{m-1}\)-element set \(Y_m\) of balanced words of length \(2m-2\). Conversely, every length-\(2m-2\), weight-\(m-1\) subsequence of a word in \(W_m\) necessarily results from deleting one zero and one one. Construct a graph \(H_m\) on \(W_m\) in which two distinct words are adjacent if and only if their \(D_{01}\)-sets do not intersect. Thus the zero-error codebooks are exactly the cliques of \(H_m\). This equivalence involves no approximation or assumption about the number of distinct outputs of one word.

**Exhaustive search with a rigorous upper bound.** `check.py` builds \(H_m\) by enumerating every possible pair of deleted positions and each resulting distinct output. Each vertex's neighborhood is represented by an integer bitset. The `exact_codes` recursion keeps a clique \(R\) and a candidate set \(P\). It computes a *proper greedy coloring* of the induced graph on \(P\). Because any clique contains at most one vertex from each color class, the assigned color index bounds the number of additional clique vertices in every prefix of the resulting ordered candidate list. Processing this order in reverse and permanently removing each processed vertex partitions all possible extensions without duplication. It discards a branch only when \(|R|\) plus that valid color upper bound is less than the target size.

For each \(m=1,2,3,4,5\), the checker enumerates every clique of the asserted size \(A_m\) and proves no clique of size \(A_m+1\) exists. Hence the computed maxima are exact; their complete enumeration simultaneously proves the \(M_m\) values. In particular, the length-ten graph has \(252\) vertices and \(70\) possible outputs. Its size-ten search visits \(59{,}384\) recursion nodes and finds exactly \(666\) codebooks, while its size-eleven search visits \(12{,}006\) recursion nodes and finds none. The same conclusion follows from an independently formulated integer-program optimization on the matrix of distinct channel outputs.

**Symmetry classes.** Both reversal and global complement preserve balance and channel confusability; they commute and have order two. For every enumerated maximum codebook, the checker forms its images under the identity, reversal, complement, and their composition. It verifies all images occur among the enumerated codebooks, and partitions the collection into the stated orbit sizes. Since the group has order four, no other orbit sizes are possible.

## Verification

Execute `python3 check.py` with Python 3 and no third-party libraries. It recomputes every graph, lists every extremal codebook internally, certifies the nonexistence of larger ones using the proved coloring bound, checks all orbit memberships, and directly reconstructs all output sets to verify the disjointness of every enumerated maximum codebook. The expected terminal line is `BALANCED_MIXED_DELETION_EXACT_PASS`. The finite exhaustive proof is restricted to the displayed parameters. No computational timeout is interpreted as nonexistence.

## Relationship to prior work

Cullina, Kulkarni and Kiyavash studied the graph formulation of arbitrary deletion correction and constant-weight source layers, including computational constructions for two deletions; their work supplies the broader graph-theoretic setting, not this mixed-deletion optimal-codebook census. Their two-deletion confusability relation checks all possible deleted-symbol compositions, whereas \(H_m\) checks only the output layer containing equal remaining numbers of zeros and ones. Guruswami and Håstad constructed asymptotically efficient unrestricted two-deletion-correcting codes and analyzed recovery when the two deleted symbols differ; their theorems do not determine this exact finite-length optimum or multiplicity. The result here therefore concerns a restricted conditional channel, not a strengthening of unrestricted two-deletion coding bounds.

## Limitations

The theorem resolves only the five balanced lengths through ten; it gives neither an asymptotic formula nor an efficient general-purpose encoder. It assumes the *type* of deleted pair is guaranteed; if the channel can delete two equal symbols, the stated codes need not decode correctly. The proof of exact maxima is finite and relies on a complete, auditable search with a proved branch bound. No independent external audit has been performed. An unindexed equivalent calculation cannot be excluded by literature search alone.
