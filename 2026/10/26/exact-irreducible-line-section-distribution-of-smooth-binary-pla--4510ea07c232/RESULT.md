# Exact irreducible-line-section distribution of smooth binary plane quartics

## Finding

For each geometrically smooth plane quartic \(C\subset\mathbf P^2_{\mathbf F_2}\), let \(I(C)\) be the number of its seven \(\mathbf F_2\)-rational lines whose intersection divisor on \(C\) is a single closed point of degree four. Equivalently, on any such line the homogeneous binary quartic restriction of an equation of \(C\) is irreducible over \(\mathbf F_2\); its four geometric intersection points form one Frobenius orbit (cycle type \((4)\)).

Among **all 32,767 nonzero** ternary quartic forms over \(\mathbf F_2\), exactly **10,920** define geometrically smooth plane quartics. The complete distribution of \(I(C)\), counted by equations in fixed projective coordinates, is:

| Number of irreducible rational line sections | Smooth quartic equations |
|---:|---:|
| 0 | 4,666 |
| 1 | 3,444 |
| 2 | 1,722 |
| 3 | 504 |
| 4 | 420 |
| 5 | 84 |
| 6 | 56 |
| 7 | 24 |
| **Total** | **10,920** |

Thus 4,666 smooth equations have **no** rational line whose degree-four intersection is irreducible, while 6,254 have at least one. Every possible count from zero to seven occurs. Counting a pair consisting of a smooth equation and a qualifying rational line gives exactly **11,004** pairs. This is an equation-weighted distribution, **not** a count of projective isomorphism classes; the separate orbit certificate contains 78 smooth projective orbits.

## Assumptions and scope

All coefficients and coordinate transformations lie in \(\mathbf F_2\). A plane quartic means a reduced geometrically smooth degree-four hypersurface in the projective plane, not an affine equation or a possibly singular quartic. Each nonzero quartic polynomial is its own scalar-normalized equation since \(\mathbf F_2^\times=\{1\}\). Each of the seven rational lines is counted at most once. Irreducibility refers to its degree-four *binary form*, not irreducibility of the plane quartic itself. Smoothness is geometric, i.e. excludes singularities over any algebraic extension.

The weight of an isomorphism class is the number of coefficient equations in its \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_2)\)-orbit. Neither the general \(q\)-distribution nor a statement about random abstract genus-three curves is asserted.

## Proof

**Lemma 1 (arithmetic line test).** A homogeneous degree-four binary form over \(\mathbf F_2\) is irreducible if and only if it has no zero in \(\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_4)\). Indeed, any reducible degree-four form has a homogeneous factor of degree one or two, and that factor has a root over \(\mathbf F_4\). Conversely an \(\mathbf F_4\)-root has minimal polynomial of degree one or two over \(\mathbf F_2\), furnishing a proper factor. This also treats forms vanishing identically on a line as non-irreducible. Every rational projective line contains five \(\mathbf F_4\)-points. The seven nonzero linear forms over \(\mathbf F_2\) enumerate the seven rational lines.

**Lemma 2 (exact geometric-smoothness test).** A homogeneous quartic \(F\) is geometrically smooth precisely when, in each of the three standard affine charts obtained by setting one of \(X,Y,Z\) to one, the ideal generated by the dehomogenization \(f\) and its two partial derivatives is the unit ideal over \(\mathbf F_2\). If the reduced lexicographic Gröbner basis is \(\{1\}\), the affine singular scheme is empty even after algebraic closure. Conversely, a non-unit ideal has a maximal ideal over the algebraic closure, hence a geometric singular point. The three charts cover projective space. The executable `census.py` makes all 279 orbit-representative tests explicitly over \(\mathbf F_2\); this is a zero-dimensional/empty-ideal computation, not a finite rational-point smoothness surrogate.

**Lemma 3 (orbits and invariance).** The coordinate permutation cycle, coordinate swap and elementary shear specified in `census.py` generate all 168 elements of \(\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbf F_2)=\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_2)\). The order is independently checked by closure of the 3-by-3 matrix products over \(\mathbf F_2\). Pullback by any projective linear automorphism preserves geometric smoothness and permutes rational lines while preserving their restricted binary-form irreducibility. Orbit enumeration therefore legitimately applies Lemmas 1 and 2 to a single representative per orbit, weighted by its exact orbit size.

**Exact finite enumeration.** Fix the 15 monomials \(X^aY^bZ^c\) with \(a+b+c=4\) in the order defined by the attached program. Iterate all integer coefficient bitmasks from 1 through 32,767. Orbit closure under the three generators partitions them into exactly 279 disjoint orbits of total size 32,767, as the attached certificate lists. For each orbit representative, compute the seven line tests of Lemma 1 and the Gröbner bases of Lemma 2. Exactly 78 orbits (weight sum 10,920) are geometrically smooth. Grouping their orbit weights by the line-test result gives the table above. The first script asserts the total, all orbit sizes and all eight weights. Summation verifies \(4666+3444+1722+504+420+84+56+24=10920\), \(10920-4666=6254\), and \(\sum_{i=0}^7i\,N_i=11004\).

A *separate restriction implementation* in `factor_check.py` computes each binary form via binomial expansion on two rational line-basis points and compares it with the three monic irreducible degree-four forms, bit patterns \(19,25,31\). It independently recovers all 279 line-test counts and the eight weighted bins from the accompanying certificate. This second checker shares the first program's certified orbit weights and smoothness labels; it is **not** an independent geometric-smoothness proof. The complete main program and the explicit orbit certificate make the smoothness checks reproducible rather than assumed.

## Verification

With Python 3 and SymPy 1.14, place `census.py` and `factor_check.py` in the same directory as \(`orbit_certificate.csv`\), then run the exact commands:

```sh
python census.py > census_output.txt
python factor_check.py > factor_check_output.txt
```

The programs rerun from the source files, raise an assertion on any changed census, and generate/verify the accompanying 279-row certificate. The exact stdout files supplied here were produced from those commands. All computations are over exact finite fields or polynomial rings, with no floating point, probabilistic cutoff or network access. Re-executing the Gröbner computation requires SymPy; the counts are not inferred from sample points or unbounded experiments.

## Relationship to prior work

Entin studies Frobenius cycle-type statistics of transverse hyperplane sections **as field size grows**; the present fixed \(q=2\), fixed-degree, **complete equation-weighted distribution** is not given by that asymptotic statement. Oyono–Ritzenthaler treat lines whose four quartic intersection points are **all rational** for sufficiently large fields, a distinct splitting type and range. Lercier–Ritzenthaler–Rovetta–Sijsling–Smith study point counts/traces of genus-three curves, rather than the distribution of irreducible line restrictions over all seven lines for each embedded quartic.

The extreme bin \(I(C)=7\) is **not claimed as a new isomorphism-class classification**: Lario's published genus-three diophantine-stability table already records the relevant pointless-over-\(\mathbf F_4\) nonhyperelliptic example. The full eight-bin incidence distribution, including nonextreme bins and the exact 4,666/6,254 absence/existence partition, is the claim assessed here. Dickson's early complete quartic classifications are relevant historical comparators, but a page-by-page comparison with their complete original tables was not available; prior indexing of this exact statistic therefore remains a priority risk.

## Limitations

No theorem over arbitrary finite fields is claimed. Smoothness labels are established by one Gröbner implementation, while the second checker independently validates the finite-field line-restriction logic. The certificates determine exact finite enumerations, not independent expert verification or priority of discovery. The distinction between *equation-weighted* and *isomorphism-class-weighted* distributions is essential. Earlier historical tables and unindexed code could contain these values; absence from searches alone cannot establish publication novelty.
