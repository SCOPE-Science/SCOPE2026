# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof's lossless compression is justified because vertices starting with the same colour within one bipartition part have identical update predicates forever. Each excitation predicate only depends on whether the sum of at most three class sizes exceeds the threshold two; truncating the six initial sizes at two preserves it exactly. Enumerating all 676 nonempty capped-type patterns therefore exhausts all graph sizes, not merely sampled sizes. Direct type-colour updates prove that preperiod five occurs exactly for thin–fat patterns. The independent labelled implementation checks 560124 configurations. Counting the two disjoint thin–fat orientations gives \(2(P_aQ_b+Q_aP_b)\). No infinite assertion rests on an unjustified finite cutoff.

## Originality — PASS (bounded)

The exact maximal-depth population stratification is not supplied by the closest inspected literature or recoverable earlier claims. Earlier general-threshold bipartite results establish periodic-state totals and the five-step cutoff, including sharpness when both sides are at least four; they do not enumerate the initial states achieving the cutoff, characterize the thin–fat pattern, or close the asymmetric \((3,4)\) sharpness boundary. The published arbitrary-graph results by Doba and by Gravner–Lyu–Sivakoff explicitly use the different threshold-one rule. The historical 1993 paper examines planar lattice spiral dynamics. Searches did not locate a stronger formula, although unindexed overlap remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

First-entry-time strata are a natural measure of transient relaxation and are not determined by cycle counts or a global time bound. The result identifies a previously uncounted sharp terminal stratum in an infinite graph family, supplies its simple exact binomial form, and reveals a structural contrast: the five-step maximum already occurs on the nonoscillating \(K_{3,4}\), but its probability vanishes exponentially along balanced growing graphs. The exact conditional population description enables reproducible basin-size and finite-size relaxation comparisons.
