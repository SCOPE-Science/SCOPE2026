# Review

**Correctness (C): PASS.** The Fourier transform is reduced to a fifth divided difference at the six integer barycentric nodes. The proof treats every multiplicity partition of six, derives the only possible repeated-node equations, and proves their necessity and sufficiency. The domain and normalization are explicit.

**Originality (O): PASS, with residual risk.** The closest inspected primary sources provide general simplex/polytopal transform formulas or discuss different zero-set questions; none states the complete integer-lattice classification or the two Diophantine gap families. Searches included the equivalent divided-difference and uniform-Dirichlet formulations. This is a bounded literature comparison, not proof that no unindexed source exists.

**Value (V): PASS.** The claim is a complete classification of a natural spectral invariant for the standard five-simplex. It isolates the only repeated-node multiplicity pattern that can vanish and turns it into two exact, infinite Diophantine families; this is more informative than isolated examples or a routine numerical census.

Closest literature: arXiv:1602.08593v1; arXiv:1608.02417v1; arXiv:2404.03405v1. The 2008 discrete Fourier/cubature paper on simplices is a near miss because its operator is not the continuous Fourier transform of the simplex indicator.

Limitations: only integer frequencies in dimension five are classified; the general real/complex zero set and higher-dimensional analogues remain outside the claim.
