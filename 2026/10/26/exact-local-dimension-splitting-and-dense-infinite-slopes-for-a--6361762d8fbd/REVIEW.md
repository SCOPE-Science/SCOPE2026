## Correctness — PASS
The proof verifies the two full expanding inverse branches, constructs the Bernoulli maximal-entropy law, obtains exact branch mass-scaling, and uses the analytic fixed-point inverse germ to prove two-sided endpoint power bounds. The cusp mass identity yields exact exponent halving. Local diffeomorphic pullbacks preserve the exponent on every precritical point, whose density follows from uniform cylinder shrinking. The measure's ergodicity, regular absolutely continuous invariant density, and preservation of both Lebesgue-decomposition types establish singularity. Six thousand and thirty finite numerical identities support the algebra but are not used as infinite-scale proofs.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The critical square-root singularity halves the maximal-entropy pointwise dimension at the cusp, forcing a dense set of infinite derivative points of the conjugacy, while the same dynamical system has a twice weakly differentiable physical invariant density. The exact exponent depends on the finite repelling endpoint multiplier and provides an explicit structural contrast between invariant measures with fundamentally different regularity. This resolves a natural dimension/regularity question for the newly analyzed critical-cusp family, rather than merely recomputing its known transfer-operator estimates.
