# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Canonical leftmost embeddings uniquely count distinct exact-two-deletion descendants for arbitrary read alphabets; the identity \(|\mathcal D_2(y)|=\binom{R(y)}2+H(y)-T(y)\) holds at the boundary \(m=2\) as well. For overlapping \(\ell\)-mers, constant-adjacency events correspond to constant length-\(\ell+1\) letter blocks. Their pair probabilities are \(q^{-\ell-\min(d,\ell)}\), and the extra run and alternation expectations cancel exactly. The finite-dependence central limit theorem with an explicit covariance sum and an \(L^2\) remainder estimate proves the asymptotic variance and normal law. Direct replay of the standalone verifier checked 294,061 entire source words over 48 grids, without extrapolating finite checks to an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS with stated residual risk.** The January 2026 nanopore paper develops code redundancy and constructions rather than this two-deletion output-volume distribution. Chee et al. enumerate locally constrained read strings, a different statistic. The published binary and an earlier all-alphabet double-deletion moment calculation concern unconstrained uniformly sampled words of independent symbols; neither implies the correlated-read covariance law. Mixed-edit DNA error-ball results likewise concern a different channel. Public mathematical-record and primary-literature comparisons found no equivalent statement or dominating formula. The classical run-count identity is treated as a proof ingredient, not itself claimed new. A differently indexed general formula may have appeared elsewhere; non-detection is not proof of global priority.

**Value — PASS.** The number of distinct possible read outputs is a natural ambiguity statistic in deletion-based sequencing, and neighboring sliding reads are heavily dependent. The theorem gives the exact finite-length mean for every alphabet/window/length and a nondegenerate sharp Gaussian fluctuation scale, with a positive explicit overlap correction. In the quaternary two-read setting the limiting variance constant is \(4725/65536\). This is useful as a quantitative baseline for average-case deletion ambiguity; it makes no assertion about better decoders, rates, or experimental hardware.
