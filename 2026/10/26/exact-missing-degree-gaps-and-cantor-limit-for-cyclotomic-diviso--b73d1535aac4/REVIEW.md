# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The theorem is for every \(a,b\ge1\) and odd prime \(p\). The degree set follows directly from cyclotomic factorization and completeness of subset sums of the weights \(1,1,2,\ldots,2^{a-1}\). In the noncomplete range, a modular argument and base-\(p\) uniqueness prove that all coefficient tuples are different. A strict place-value domination gives lexicographic ordering, hence every gap and its multiplicity. The Cantor limit follows from coupled independent digits and a separated iterated-function system. Independently calculated totient subset sums match all formulas in 77 finite parameter cases. No computational finiteness claim is used as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS under inspected evidence.** Thompson's primary paper [1], §4, establishes the prime-adjunction criterion and thereby the *completeness threshold*. Pollack–Thompson [2] analyze attainable-degree sets and global counting, not this exact missing-degree gap profile or the limiting Cantor measure. OEIS A260653 [3] records complete moduli, not the internal structure of incomplete sets. The nearest indexed mathematical findings address multiplicities of monic divisors in the complete two-prime family and ordinary integer-divisor subset sums for two-prime powers; neither implies these exact cyclotomic-degree gaps. These distinction checks do not prove global novelty. Unindexed treatments of finite subset-sum gap spectra remain a residual publication-priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** The established theory distinguishes complete and incomplete polynomial degree sets; this result describes *every gap*, its frequency at each prime-power scale, the exact fraction of absent degrees and the limiting fractal geometry for a natural two-prime family on the noncomplete side. The infinite exact formulas and singular limiting law capture information absent from the yes/no \(\varphi\)-practicality criterion.

**Limits.** This result does not count polynomial divisor multiplicities per degree, concern finite-field factors or settle a general three-prime-support problem. The verification program checks small cases only; no independent scientific audit has been performed.
