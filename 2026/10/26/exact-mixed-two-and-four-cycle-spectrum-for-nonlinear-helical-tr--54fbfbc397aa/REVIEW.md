# Scientific review: nonlinear helical transposition orbit census

## Correctness — PASS

The proof explicitly defines the carrier-inclusive state, the quotient by the swapped-pair subgroup, and the indicator controlling each site update. Quotient dynamics yields the exact time-indexed indicator, proving \(T^4=I\) and the full-state fixed-point counts of \(T\) and \(T^2\). Fixed-count subtraction yields the primitive cycles. The characteristic-two functional equation is proved directly without assuming the source proposition, and nonlinearity follows from the size of an affine rank-one fiber. Exhaustive finite configurations for all 504 prescribed \(q=8\) cases and 16 selected \(q=16\) cases agree with the theorem. Finite runs corroborate, rather than prove, the all-parameter result.

## Originality — PASS under inspected evidence

The primary 2026 Araoka–Tokihiro preprint provides the transposition solution family, helical update, and qualitative period-two/four observations, but its inspected results do not give the carrier-inclusive fixed-point formulas or an exponential mixed-period phase-space proportion for every \(n\ge3\). A directly related publicly available exact census for **additive-affine** rules was inspected in full; it does not subsume the present nonlinear maps (whose perturbation has two-point rather than affine half-space support for \(q\ge8\)). Other exact matching results were not located in the targeted published-record and primary-literature searches. Residual risk: wider combinatorial Yang–Baxter literature or a later journal version might have a stronger orbit-count statement. No independent historical priority verification is claimed.

## Value — PASS

The motivating question is distribution rather than merely maximum possible period of an integrable automaton. The theorem resolves the full dynamical zeta data for a natural nonlinear family at all lattice lengths, and quantifies an unusual regime where both primitive period-two and period-four trajectories occur with exact exponentially varying weights. This distinguishes genuinely nonlinear rules from the already-classified additive-affine family; the exact fraction is a natural phase-space statistic, not an arbitrary isolated parameter computation.
