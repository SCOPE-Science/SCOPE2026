# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The stationary process is a rigorously convergent Gaussian scale mixture. Its random variance satisfies a positive affine perpetuity equation with a nonarithmetic multiplicative factor; explicit moment calculations verify the hypotheses of Goldie's tail theorem, and the Gaussian-mixture product result supplies the strictly positive physical tail coefficient. The approximate density is reconstructed from the source Fokker–Planck equation, and Taylor's theorem at exponent two proves the complete strict index comparison. Generator calculations identify exact variance equality, the fourth-moment wedge and strict fourth-moment ordering, while an implicit-function expansion proves the weak-reset ratio. The accompanying deterministic checker passes all reported finite checks. General heavy-tail theorems are used with stated hypotheses rather than presented as original proofs.

**Originality — PASS, with explicit risk.** The motivating source (Galla, arXiv:2602.21635v1, full HTML including supplementary S1) already derives the diffusion approximation and its stationary density. Goldie's 1991 implicit-renewal theorem (bibliographic record/abstract examined) and Alsmeyer–Behme (arXiv:2601.09314, primary introduction and relevant results examined) cover the general affine-perpetuity power-law mechanism. These are explicitly excluded from the new contribution. The mathematical contribution assessed is the *comparison*: exact finite-reset sign reversal pinned to the shared second-moment boundary, the fourth-moment qualitative mismatch wedge, and the singular nonunit weak-reset index ratio. Tal-Friedman et al. (arXiv:2205.03363, primary PDF inspected) treat constant drift rather than the expanding linear drift; Zanette–Manrubia (arXiv:2105.11679, primary PDF sections II and IV inspected) treat full reinjection to a fixed value rather than proportional contraction and Gaussian diffusion approximation. Published mathematical-record searches and primary-literature comparisons revealed no equivalent comparison. Since these expressions combine established techniques, possible unlocated equivalent applications are a real residual originality risk; independent originality review is still required.

**Value — PASS.** High-reset diffusion approximations are used to describe systems with frequent small shocks. This result identifies a natural reliability boundary at which even the *direction* of tail-index bias changes, and an explicit open wedge where the approximation predicts an infinite fourth moment despite the actual system having a finite one. This remains possible when the second moments are identical, so variance agreement cannot certify rare-event accuracy. The exact fourth-moment criterion, nonunit weak-reset exponent ratio and explicit parameter bounds offer concrete diagnostics in the published model rather than arbitrary numerical benchmarks.

**Scientific limits and risks.** The only nontrivial external proof dependency is standard Goldie renewal theory; finite checks are not a proof of regular variation. The source's exact generalized Ornstein–Uhlenbeck extensions might indirectly imply parts of the result. This is a matched linear specialization, not a general theorem for nonlinear resetting, state-dependent rates or simultaneous spatial/weak-reset limits.
