# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** A natural basis of a nondegenerate rank-one-square evolution algebra is exactly an orthogonal basis of its nondegenerate symmetric form. The proof counts diagonal presentations of each discriminant class, uses Witt transitivity to pass to coordinates of the fixed generator of the square, separates proper and full coordinate supports, and evaluates all resulting character sums. The normalization and the sharp exceptional regimes follow algebraically. A separate standard-library enumerator checked 128 feasible parameter types over four finite fields, and explicit orthogonal bases were enumerated in four fixed examples. A finite check is not an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with limitations.** The October 15, 2026 survey poses the question of basis-independent natural-basis graph information; its graph discussion and statement of Question 27 do not give the present quantitative rank-one graph-support distribution. The 2025 one-dimensional-square classification establishes the form-theoretic classification on which the theorem builds, but not the finite-field profile or exact exceptional support regimes. A related two-dimensional natural-basis orbit calculation counts different equivalence classes, not graph-type probabilities for all dimensions. Published-record searches for rank-one evolution algebras, finite-field orthogonal bases, and graph support distributions revealed no stronger or equivalent exact result. The unavailable full text of *Natural families in evolution algebras*, older form-enumeration articles, and unindexed sources remain possible overlap risks; novelty is not proved by search failure.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A natural infinite family in which graph type depends on natural basis now has a complete, exact graph-realization and frequency invariant, directly motivated by the open problem in the October 2026 survey. The closed probability vector distinguishes bilinear norm and discriminant regimes, exhibits unexpectedly sharp small-field graph obstructions, and gives exact counts of all natural bases realizing each support size. This is more than the customary classification of the underlying quadratic form and does not rely on an arbitrary finite slice.

**Scope.** Odd finite fields, dimension at least two, zero annihilator, one-dimensional square, ordered natural bases. The result neither solves the general graph-invariance question nor gives orbit-weighted distributions.
