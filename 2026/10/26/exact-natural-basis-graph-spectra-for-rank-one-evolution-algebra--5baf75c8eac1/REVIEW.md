# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** Starting from the bilinear realization with zero radical, unique square roots normalize each natural basis. The characteristic vector is fixed by every isometry of the dot product in characteristic two. Odd-dimensional isometries reduce to a symplectic action; even-dimensional isometries are explicitly parametrized by one symplectic isometry, one vector, and one scalar. These facts determine the orbits, stabilizers and full group order. A nonzero-coordinate-sum recursion determines every support frequency, and the free permutation action gives the exact basis-class multiplicities. The general proof covers all stated parameters; the executable checker enumerates only bounded finite examples, including \(\mathbb F_4\) and \(\mathbb F_8\). Risk: the theorem is limited to nondegenerate rank-one evolution algebras and characteristic-two extension fields.

**Originality (PASS, qualified).** The October 15, 2026 survey arXiv:2610.22248v1 asks about counting natural bases modulo scaling and reordering and about graph change under basis selection, but does not give the present all-parameter characteristic-two extension-field spectrum. The foundational full-text 2025 one-dimensional-square classification (DOI 10.1007/s00009-025-02812-7) describes the algebras through bilinear forms without these counts. The 2020 natural-family article arXiv:2006.14460v1 provides structural change-of-basis criteria, not the present support and multiplicity formulas. Related finite-field computations cover the excluded prime field or odd characteristic. Actual semantic searches of mathematical findings and primary-literature checks revealed no implication covering the combined claim. Residual risk: an equivalent specialized orthogonal-frame weight-enumerator may exist in older or unindexed literature; a search is not a proof of novelty.

**Mathematical value (PASS).** This is a natural complete classification, not a selected numerical sample: for every dimension and field degree in an infinite family it determines all basis-graph types and gives their exact multiplicities. It resolves a concrete enumerative instance of open questions about natural-basis and graph variability, and exhibits a sharp difference between the prime-field parity restrictions and characteristic-two extension fields. It does not settle those open questions for general evolution algebras.
