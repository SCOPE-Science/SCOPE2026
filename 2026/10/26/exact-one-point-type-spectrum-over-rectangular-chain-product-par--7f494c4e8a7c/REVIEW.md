# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The type-to-ideal/filter translation is an established structural premise, independently reconstructed for this parameter family. Partitioning a nonempty lower ideal by its two maxima leaves an arbitrary interior ideal; the full rectangle is the only configuration that forbids its upper-right corner. Exactly one upper filter is subtracted in that case. The resulting binomial convolution is counted by marked monotone paths, with both terminal boundaries removed and their overlap restored. The algebraic correction, boundary cases, fixed-width degree and square-grid asymptotic were checked. A standalone implementation directly enumerates finite ternary poset extensions in addition to ideal/filter checks. No finite experiment is used in place of the all-size proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The closest inspected prior result, *Finite-parameter 1-type counting in the random poset is #P-complete*, establishes the generic ideal/filter characterization and overall #P-completeness but does not evaluate the product-of-two-chains family. Dolinka and Mašulović already give the one-point L/U description, which is acknowledged as prior; Droste and Kuske establish the homogeneous random poset framework. These facts alone do not provide the evaluated all-parameter expression or its contrasting growth regimes. Pretzel's earlier insertion-position construction (1980) is a plausible specialized prior source whose full text was unavailable; an equivalent unindexed formula may exist. The specific formula is the claim, not the general mechanism or the numerical table.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Rectangular products of chains give natural finite parameter sets with interacting chains and interior diamond relations, unlike a lone chain. The exact formula is a uniform two-variable classification with a positive fixed-width leading coefficient and a square-grid exponential asymptotic. It therefore identifies an interpretable tractable family within a general #P-complete type-enumeration problem; the lattice-path mechanism is reusable for comparable grid extensions. The result is more than a finite census or a routine reindexing of the chain case.

## Closest literature, scope and limits
The closest fully inspected work is the 2026 general #P-completeness theorem (public repository record), Dolinka–Mašulović (arXiv:1009.2892, §3.1), and Droste–Kuske (JCTA 2003). Pretzel (Discrete Mathematics 1980) was reviewed only at abstract level because full text was unavailable. The claim covers complete one-types over nonempty two-chain products in the generic poset; it does not cover arbitrary finite posets or higher tuple arity. Its originality remains subject to independent primary-literature examination, and numerical tests are not infinite proofs.
