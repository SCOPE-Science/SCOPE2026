# Exact open-neighborhood VC dimension of every finite cycle power

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(n\ge3\), \(r\ge1\) be integers, and let \(G_{n,r}\) be the graph on \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) in which distinct vertices \(u,v\) are adjacent precisely when their cyclic distance \(d_n(u,v)=\min\{(u-v)\bmod n,(v-u)\bmod n\}\) satisfies \(1\le d_n(u,v)\le r\). Let \(\mathcal N_{n,r}=\{N_{G_{n,r}}(v):v\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\}\) consist of **open** neighborhoods. Then

\[
\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal N_{n,r})=
\begin{cases}
1,&n\le2r+2,\\
2,&n=2r+3,\\
3,&(n,r)=(8,2),\\
3,&r\ge3\ \text{and}\ n\ge2r+4,\\
2,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

Equivalently, among noncomplete cycle powers the maximal value is three; it occurs for all radii at least three once the graph has at least four vertices more than twice the radius, and in the isolated exceptional square of the eight-cycle. In particular the square of the cycle loses VC dimension when its order increases from eight to nine, whereas all higher powers have a stable dimension-three range.

## Assumptions and scope

The neighborhoods are open, so the center is omitted even if all nearby points are adjacent. The power is the ordinary **undirected** graph power. Cyclic distance is measured on the original cycle, and \(r\) may exceed the diameter; in that event the graph is complete. The VC dimension is the maximum cardinality of a set \(S\) for which every subset \(T\subseteq S\) equals \(N(v)\cap S\) for some graph vertex \(v\). The ground set and the centers are both the full cyclic vertex set. The theorem does not claim the same values for closed neighborhoods, arbitrary circulant graphs, or arbitrary induced subgraphs.

## Proof

**1. Complete and almost-complete cases.** If \(n\le2r+1\), every two distinct vertices are adjacent, hence every open neighborhood is the complement of its center. The VC dimension is one: every singleton is shattered, but no two-point set has the empty trace. If \(n=2r+2\), the graph is a complete graph with an antipodal perfect matching deleted. The complement of each open neighborhood is its antipodal pair. To obtain the empty trace on a two-point set \(S\), the set \(S\) must be exactly one antipodal pair; then no singleton trace is possible. Thus the dimension is again one.

If \(n=2r+3\), the complement of \(N(v)\) is \(\{v,v+(r+1),v-(r+1)\}\). Since \(\gcd(n,r+1)=1\), under the cyclic relabeling generated by addition of \(r+1\), these complementary neighborhoods are exactly the consecutive three-vertex windows of an \(n\)-cycle. Complementing all ranges preserves shattering. No three-element set is shattered by consecutive three-vertex windows: a full trace forces the set itself to be three consecutive vertices \(\{a,b,c\}\); no three-consecutive window can contain both ends \(a,c\) while omitting the middle vertex \(b\), as \(n\ge5\). A two-element set *is* shattered by the original open neighborhoods (as shown below). Hence the dimension is two.

**2. A universal three-point ceiling.** Suppose \(n\ge2r+3\). Fix four distinct sample points \(S\). As the center \(v\) runs around the cycle, for each \(x\in S\) the bit \(\mathbf1_{x\in N(v)}\) changes exactly four times: at the starts of the two radius-\(r\) blocks surrounding \(x\) and at the two boundaries adjoining the omitted center \(x\). More explicitly, the changes occur when the new center equals \(x-r,x,x+1,x+r+1\) (modulo \(n\)). Thus the cyclic trace sequence \(N(v)\cap S\) has at most sixteen changes.

If all sixteen subsets of \(S\) occurred, there would be at least sixteen distinct maximal constant runs. Hence there would be exactly sixteen change positions and **only one bit could change at each position**. Choose any \(x\in S\). At the consecutive transitions into centers \(x\) and \(x+1\), only the bit for \(x\) would change. It changes from one to zero and back to one, so the traces at centers \(x-1\) and \(x+1\) coincide. These positions belong to different constant runs, contradicting the necessary sixteen distinct trace runs. Consequently no four-point set is shattered. The VC dimension is at most three for all \(n\ge2r+3\).

**3. A universal shattered pair.** For \(n\ge2r+3\), use \(S=\{0,r+1\}\). Centers \(0,1,r+2,n-1\) produce respectively the traces \(\varnothing,S,\{r+1\},\{0\}\). All distances are cyclic and the inequalities remain valid at the endpoint \(n=2r+3\). Thus the dimension is at least two in every case not covered by the first paragraph.

**4. The radii one and two.** At radius one each neighborhood has only two vertices, so it cannot contain a shattered three-set. For radius two, the nontrivial dense boundary \(n=7\) was dealt with in paragraph 1. When \(n=8\), \(S=\{0,1,4\}\) is shattered: the centers realizing the bit strings \(000,001,010,011,100,101,110,111\), with bits ordered as \(0,1,4\), are respectively \(4,5,0,3,1,6,7,2\).

For \(r=2,n\ge9\), assume a three-set \(S\) is shattered. In particular its full trace occurs at some center; by rotation take that center to be \(0\). Thus \(S\) is one of the four triples from \(N(0)=\{-2,-1,1,2\}\). The following table exhibits a missing trace in each case (a bit string lists membership in the displayed order):

| Triple | Missing trace | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| \((-2,-1,1)\) | \(011\) | The unique common neighbor of \(-1,1\) is \(0\), which also neighbors \(-2\). |
| \((-2,-1,2)\) | \(101\) | The unique common neighbor of \(-2,2\) is \(0\), which also neighbors \(-1\). |
| \((-2,1,2)\) | \(101\) | The unique common neighbor of \(-2,2\) is \(0\), which also neighbors \(1\). |
| \((-1,1,2)\) | \(110\) | The unique common neighbor of \(-1,1\) is \(0\), which also neighbors \(2\). |

The claimed uniqueness of each common neighbor follows directly from the two radius-two neighborhoods and holds for \(n\ge9\). Thus no triple is shattered for radius two at orders nine or above.

**5. A shattered triple for every higher radius.** Suppose \(r\ge3\), \(n\ge2r+4\). The following tables give, for each of the eight desired three-bit traces, an actual center. This supplies a uniform all-parameter existence proof.

If \(2r+4\le n\le3r+2\), choose \(S=(0,1,r+2)\). In increasing binary-string order the centers are:

| Trace | \(000\) | \(001\) | \(010\) | \(011\) | \(100\) | \(101\) | \(110\) | \(111\) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Center | \(r+2\) | \(r+3\) | \(0\) | \(r+1\) | \(1\) | \(n-r\) | \(n-1\) | \(2\) |

If \(n\ge3r+3\), choose \(S=(0,2,r+1)\). The analogous centers are:

| Trace | \(000\) | \(001\) | \(010\) | \(011\) | \(100\) | \(101\) | \(110\) | \(111\) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Center | \(2r+2\) | \(r+3\) | \(0\) | \(r+2\) | \(n-r\) | \(2\) | \(n-1\) | \(1\) |

For example, the potentially delicate first-table trace \(101\) at center \(n-r\) works because its cyclic distance from \(0\) is \(r\), from \(1\) is \(r+1\), and from \(r+2\) is \(n-2r-2\), between two and \(r\) in the stipulated range. In the second table, center \(2r+2\) has distance greater than \(r\) from all three sample vertices because \(n\ge3r+3\). The remaining entries follow immediately from the same distance inequalities; the included verification script checks all eight entry types across many boundary and interior instances. Therefore the dimension is at least three in this entire parameter range. Combining the upper bound, low-radius exclusions, and witnesses proves the formula. \(\square\)

## Verification

The accompanying independent standard-library program `verify.py` constructs the graph directly from cyclic distances, calculates traces by bit masks, and tests the formula exhaustively for all \(3\le n\le22\) and every \(1\le r\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) (120 parameter pairs), enumerating all vertex subsets up to size four. Separately it tests both explicit three-trace witness tables at 2,717 parameter pairs over \(3\le r\le40\), and radius-two obstruction templates for 142 orders. The program prints `VERIFY_OK` on success. These finite checks are not used as an infinite proof; the infinite argument is the cyclic-change budget and the explicit witness/obstruction tables above.

## Relationship to prior work

Adler, Benediktsson and Macpherson (arXiv:2007.16042, first public 2020-07-31) study open-neighborhood VC dimension for the **Johnson and Hamming graphs**, obtaining tight uniform bounds and a Hamming graph bound of three. Their graph families are not the powers \(G_{n,r}\) of cycles. In particular the result here is not a specialization of their Hamming graph theorem: the cyclic power graph with arbitrary radius is not generally an induced Hamming graph. Coudert, Csikós, Ducoffe and Viennot (arXiv:2405.07588, first public 2024-05-13) give algorithms for graph VC dimension and emphasize the distinction between open and closed neighborhoods; their paper does not supply a closed form for the present two-parameter family. A separately published study of topological neighborhood complexes for the third cycle power concerns homotopy rather than shattering traces; neither problem mathematically implies the other. Closely related earlier exact shattering results for Johnson/Hamming or triangular graphs do not establish the cyclic-radius transition or radius-two exception.

## Limitations

This is a statement about open neighborhoods only. A literature search, including semantic published-record searches and inspection of two primary manuscripts, found no prior equivalent formula; this is not a proof of publication priority. A result using alternative terms such as fixed-length circular arcs with a puncture, circulant neighborhood hypergraphs, or cyclic translates could cover the theorem and should be checked by an independent subject specialist. No independent external scientific audit has been performed. The computational checker only validates finitely many cases; the proof of unbounded parameter ranges is mathematical and appears above.
