# Exact pairwise sumsets of the regular paperfolding sign classes

## Finding

For each nonnegative integer \(n\), let \(u(n)\) be the odd part of \(n+1\), and put
\[
A=\{n\ge0:u(n)\equiv1\pmod4\},\qquad
B=\{n\ge0:u(n)\equiv3\pmod4\}.
\]
These are the one-positions and zero-positions, respectively, of the regular paperfolding sequence A014577, indexed from \(n=0\). Every integer belongs to exactly one of the two classes. Their three pairwise ordinary integer sumsets satisfy
\[
\boxed{A+A=\mathbb N_0},\qquad
\boxed{\mathbb N_0\setminus(A+B)=\{0,1,4\}},
\]
\[
\boxed{\mathbb N_0\setminus(B+B)
=\{1,3,5,9\}\cup\{2^k-2:k\ge1\}}.
\]
In particular, \(B+B\) misses infinitely many integers, but only logarithmically many up to a threshold. For every real \(X\ge0\), the exact exceptional counting function is
\[
\#\{n\in[0,X]\cap\mathbb N_0:n\notin B+B\}
=\lfloor\log_2(X+2)\rfloor+
\mathbf1_{X\ge1}+\mathbf1_{X\ge3}+\mathbf1_{X\ge5}+\mathbf1_{X\ge9}.
\]
Hence the complement of \(B+B\) has natural density zero, and all three sumsets have density one. The integer exceptions of \(B+B\) begin \(0,1,2,3,5,6,9,14,30,62,126,\ldots\).

## Assumptions and scope

The notation \(\mathbb N_0\) means \(\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\), the notation \(X+Y\) means the ordinary sumset \(\{x+y:x\in X,\ y\in Y\}\), and summands may be equal and may include zero where the class permits it. The odd part of a positive integer is the quotient by its largest power-of-two divisor. We use the zero-based paperfolding convention of OEIS A014577: its initial terms are \(1,1,0,1,1,0,0,1,\ldots\). In particular, \(0\in A\) and \(0\notin B\). All displayed set identities hold for every nonnegative integer; no eventual-periodicity or finite-cutoff hypothesis is imposed.

## Proof

**Digit decomposition.** The odd-part definition immediately yields the disjoint recursions
\[
A=4\mathbb N_0\ \sqcup\ (2A+1),\qquad
B=(4\mathbb N_0+2)\ \sqcup\ (2B+1). \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, \(u(4m)=4m+1\equiv1\pmod4\), \(u(4m+2)=4m+3\equiv3\pmod4\), and \(u(2m+1)=u(m)\). Thus the even elements of \(A\) are exactly the multiples of four, the even elements of \(B\) are exactly those congruent to two modulo four, and an odd element \(2m+1\) is in either class exactly when \(m\) is.

**The sumset \(A+A\).** Strongly induct on \(n\ge0\). If \(n\equiv0\pmod4\), then \(n=n+0\) with both summands in \(A\). If \(n\equiv1\pmod4\), then \(n=(n-1)+1\) with both summands in \(A\). If \(n\equiv3\pmod4\), then \(n=(n-3)+3\) with both summands in \(A\) (noting \(3\in A\)). For \(n=4m+2\), apply the induction hypothesis to \(2m<n\): write \(2m=a+b\) with \(a,b\in A\). Then
\[
n=(2a+1)+(2b+1),
\]
and both lifted odd summands are in \(A\) by (1). This proves \(A+A=\mathbb N_0\).

**The mixed sumset \(A+B\).** We give a representation for all \(n\notin\{0,1,4\}\). For \(m\ge0\),
\[
4m+2=(4m)+2,\qquad 4m+3=1+(4m+2),
\]
where the first summand is in \(A\) and the second in \(B\). For \(m\ge1\),
\[
4m+1=4(m-1)+5,
\]
with \(5\in B\). Finally, for \(m\ge2\), the odd integer \(q=2m-1\ge3\) is covered by one of the preceding odd cases; write \(q=a+b\) with \(a\in A\), \(b\in B\). By (1),
\[
4m=(2a+1)+(2b+1)
\]
is a mixed representation. The remaining indices \(0,1,4\) have no mixed representation: no element of \(B\) is smaller than \(2\), the only decomposition of \(1\) using nonnegative summands uses numbers in \(A\), and the possible \(B\)-summands at total \(4\) are \(2\), which would require \(2\notin A\). Thus the mixed complement is exactly \(\{0,1,4\}\).

**The sumset \(B+B\).** For \(m\ge1\),
\[
4m=(4m-2)+2,
\]
with both even summands in \(B\). For \(m\ge1\),
\[
4m+3=(4m-2)+5,
\]
with \(5=2\cdot2+1\in B\). For \(m\ge3\),
\[
4m+1=(4m-10)+11,
\]
with \(11=2\cdot5+1\in B\). The absent indices in these three residue classes are consequently exactly \(0,3\), and \(1,5,9\), respectively. (Nonrepresentation for these small values follows directly from the least elements \(2,5,6,10,11,\ldots\) of \(B\).)

It remains to classify numbers congruent to two modulo four. For every \(m\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{4m+2\in B+B\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2m\in B+B}. \tag{2}
\]
For the forward direction, two even summands from \(B\) have sum divisible by four, while an even and an odd summand have odd sum. Hence any representation of \(4m+2\) must have two odd summands, say \(2a+1\) and \(2b+1\) with \(a,b\in B\), yielding \(a+b=2m\). Conversely, any \(2m=a+b\) with \(a,b\in B\) lifts via (1) to \(4m+2=(2a+1)+(2b+1)\).

The only even integers outside \(B+B\) that are not already covered by the positive multiples of four are generated by repeatedly applying (2) starting with \(0\notin B+B\). Indeed, for an even \(n\ge2\) with \(n\equiv2\pmod4\), the reduced integer \((n-2)/2\) is smaller than \(n\) and remains even; every reduction chain terminates either at \(0\) or at a positive multiple of four. The latter case is representable, the former exceptional. Solving the exceptional backward recurrence \(n\mapsto2n+2\) from \(0\) gives exactly \(n=2^k-2\) for integers \(k\ge1\). Together with the odd exceptional indices \(1,3,5,9\), this proves the last sumset identity.

**Counting.** The increasing sequence \(2^k-2\), \(k\ge1\), has \(\lfloor\log_2(X+2)\rfloor\) terms not exceeding \(X\). None of the four additional odd exceptions belongs to it. Adding their four indicator terms proves the exact formula and the density conclusions.

## Verification

The standalone file `verify.py` checks the membership recurrences and constructive representations at every integer \(0\le n<100000\), using explicit recursive witness builders. It separately enumerates the three sumsets by Boolean-integer convolution for every \(0\le n<8192\), comparing membership against the claimed exact exceptional families. These bounded computations independently catch normalization and small-index mistakes; they are not used to infer an infinite theorem. Equations (1) and (2) and the complete residue-class proof above provide the universal argument. Run with `python3 verify.py`; only the Python standard library is required.

## Relationship to prior work

The regular paperfolding sequence and its exact two-adic membership rule are classical and recorded as OEIS A014577. Bunder, Bates, and Arnold (2024) study the associated sequence of run lengths and its connection to continued fractions, rather than two-term sumsets of the sign positions. Allouche and Shallit (2021/2022) analyze representations using evil and odious integers, a different automatic partition. Bell, Hare, and Shallit (2017/2018) give a general algorithm for assessing automatic additive bases, but their theorem does not explicitly assert these exact paperfolding exception sets. These works supply the motivation and methods; none of the material inspected gives the displayed three formulas. The exact identities have not undergone independent publication-priority verification.

## Limitations

The classification concerns precisely the regular (constant-fold-instruction) paperfolding sequence with zero-based indexing and unrestricted two-summand representations. No analogous statement is established for arbitrary generalized paperfolding sequences, for ordered representation multiplicities, or for three or more summands. A potentially relevant monograph, Shallit's *The Logical Approach to Automatic Sequences* (2022), has adjacent chapters on additive number theory and paperfolding; full chapter text was not accessible in the sources inspected. Although its accessible descriptions provide no identical claim, the uninspected chapter content is a specific residual originality risk. Mathematical correctness is proved here but there has been no independent audit.
