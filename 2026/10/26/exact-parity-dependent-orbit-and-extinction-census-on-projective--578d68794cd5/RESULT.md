# Exact parity-dependent orbit and extinction census on projective-plane incidence graphs

## Finding

Let \(\Pi\) be any finite projective plane of order \(q\ge2\) (not necessarily Desarguesian). Let \(v=q^2+q+1\), let \(N\) be its \(v\times v\) point–line incidence matrix over \(\mathbb F_2\), and let \(\rho=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}N\). On the \(2v\) vertices of its bipartite incidence graph, update every binary state to the XOR of the states of all its neighbors, synchronously. Writing \(F\) for this update, \(F^4=F^2\), and the first hitting time of the periodic subspace is at most two for every configuration.

When \(q\) is even, **there are precisely four periodic configurations**, two fixed configurations and a single primitive two-cycle. The counts of initial configurations entering periodic dynamics for the first time at times \(0,1,2\), respectively, are
\[
4,\qquad 2^{2v-2\rho+2}-4,\qquad 2^{2v}-2^{2v-2\rho+2}.
\]
The time-two bound is sharp. The nilpotent Jordan blocks of \(F\) have multiplicities \(2\rho-2\) of size two and \(2v+2-4\rho\) of size one, together with one size-two block of eigenvalue one.

When \(q\) is odd, the periodic subspace has dimension \(2v-2\) and hence \(2^{2v-2}\) configurations. There are exactly \(2^{v-1}\) fixed configurations and
\[
\frac{2^{2v-2}-2^{v-1}}{2}
\]
primitive two-cycles. Exactly \(3\cdot2^{2v-2}\) other configurations enter the periodic subspace after one update. The Jordan decomposition consists of \(v-1\) size-two blocks of eigenvalue one and two size-one zero blocks. The one-step bound is sharp.

## Assumptions and scope

A finite projective plane of order \(q\) has \(v=q^2+q+1\) points and equally many lines, with \(q+1\) points per line and exactly one line through any two distinct points. The existence of such a plane is assumed; no plane existence is asserted for arbitrary integers \(q\). Coordinates need not be available. Binary states at points and lines are independent. There are no self-neighbor contributions, asynchronous updates or majority thresholds. An initial state has *entry time* \(t\) if \(t\) is the least nonnegative integer for which its image under \(F^t\) is periodic.

## Proof

Put \(J=\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\), the \(v\times v\) all-ones matrix over \(\mathbb F_2\). Distinct incidence rows meet in precisely one position; each row meets itself in \(q+1\) positions. Consequently,
\[
NN^{\mathsf T}=N^{\mathsf T}N=(q\bmod2)I+J.
\]
Here \(v\) is odd, so \(J^2=J\). With point coordinates first, line coordinates second,
\[
F=\begin{pmatrix}0&N\\N^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
F^2=\operatorname{diag}(P,P),\qquad P=(q\bmod2)I+J.
\]
Both \(P=J\) and \(P=I+J\) are idempotent. Thus \(F^4=F^2\). For an idempotent endomorphism \(F^2\), a state is periodic for \(F\) exactly when it is in \(\operatorname{im}(F^2)\): on that image \(F^2\) acts as the identity, while any periodic state \(x=F^m x\) also satisfies \(F^2x=x\) because \(F^4=F^2\) makes its eventual orbit have period dividing two. This proves the uniform bound.

If \(q\) is even, \(P=J\) has rank one. Moreover \(q+1\) is odd, so \(N\mathbf1=N^{\mathsf T}\mathbf1=\mathbf1\). Hence the recurrent subspace is the span of the all-one point state and all-one line state, on which \(F\) exchanges the two generators. There are four periodic configurations; the diagonal two are fixed. The exchange map over \(\mathbb F_2\) has exactly one size-two Jordan block at eigenvalue one. As \(\operatorname{rank}F=2\rho\), the restriction of \(F\) to the invariant nullspace of \(F^2\) has rank \(2\rho-2\) and square zero. Its dimension is \(2v-2\); hence the stated zero-eigenvalue block numbers follow. A state maps to the periodic subspace in one update precisely when its nullspace component lies in the kernel of this square-zero restriction, a subspace of dimension \((2v-2)-(2\rho-2)=2v-2\rho\). Therefore the count with entry time at most one is \(2^{2v-2\rho+2}\); subtracting earlier counts yields the displayed exact distribution. Finally \(\rho>1\): every row of \(N\) is nonzero, but distinct rows have different supports because they each contain \(q+1>1\) points and share exactly one point. Rank one would force all nonzero rows to coincide. Thus the time-two class is nonempty and the bound is sharp.

If \(q\) is odd, \(P=I+J\) has rank \(v-1\) with kernel generated by \(\mathbf1\). Here each incidence row has even weight, so \(N\mathbf1=N^{\mathsf T}\mathbf1=0\). Hence \(F^3=F\), and its image equals the recurrent subspace of dimension \(2(v-1)\). The zero-component has dimension two and is killed by \(F\). A fixed state \((a,b)\) obeys \(a=Nb\) and \(b=N^{\mathsf T}a\), equivalently \(a=(I+J)a\) and \(b=N^{\mathsf T}a\). Thus \(a\) is an arbitrary even-parity vector, giving exactly \(2^{v-1}\) fixed states. Since \(F^2=I\) on the recurrent subspace, all other recurrent states make two-cycles. The recurrent endomorphism's fixed-space dimension is \(v-1\), so its Jordan form is \(v-1\) size-two blocks with eigenvalue one. Every nonrecurrent state enters the recurrent subspace after exactly one update. The displayed population counts and sharpness follow.

## Verification

The standalone file `verify.py` constructs the Desarguesian point–line incidence matrices for \(q\in\{2,3,4,5,7,8,9\}\), working over explicitly implemented finite fields, and independently checks all stated rank and power identities as well as fixed/recurrent dimensions and exact entry-count formulas. It exhaustively simulates all \(2^{14}=16384\) binary initial states for the order-two plane and uses additional deterministic sample trajectories at every other tested order. The accompanying `verify_output.txt` contains the exact recorded result. Execute `python verify.py` with Python 3.10 or later and no third-party dependencies. These finite tests corroborate, but do not replace, the general algebraic proof.

## Relationship to prior work

The point–line incidence identities and the associated binary ranks are classical finite-geometry and code-theory objects. Lavrauw, Storme and Van de Voorde study the code generated by point–hyperplane incidences and its dual, including related orthogonality identities, but do not give the neighbor-XOR time dynamics or its complete orbit/entry census. Key, McDonough and Mavron show that binary ranks of even-order Hall planes matter in coding theory, making the parameter \(\rho\) a meaningful invariant rather than a cosmetic variable. This theorem expresses the exact one-step population through that invariant without presuming a closed rank formula for all non-Desarguesian planes. The parity split, complete Jordan forms and sharp entry-time distribution are the additional dynamical conclusions, not claims that the underlying incidence identity or ranks are new.

## Limitations

The result concerns synchronous linear neighbor XOR only, not nonlinear threshold, probabilistic, self-inclusive or asynchronous updates. For even-order planes, the full numeric entry-time census requires the binary incidence rank \(\rho\) of the chosen plane; an order-only universal formula is not claimed. The proof applies to any existing finite projective plane, but does not resolve the existence or rank classification of projective planes. Exhaustive state verification was performed only at order two; other finite-field tests are nonexhaustive. No independent audit or priority certification is claimed; historical equivalence outside examined sources remains possible.
