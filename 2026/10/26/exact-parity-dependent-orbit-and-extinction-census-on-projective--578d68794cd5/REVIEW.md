# Review of the projective-plane parity-dynamics classification

**Correctness — PASS.** The projective-plane incidence Gram identity over \(\mathbb F_2\) gives \(F^4=F^2\), with exact rank and fixed-space computations in both parity regimes. The proof treats all existing finite projective planes, rather than only coordinate examples, and derives sharp first-entry counts and Jordan blocks. The verifier independently corroborated the complete finite-field cases \(q=2,3,4,5,7,8,9\), including all 16,384 labelled order-two configurations. Those tests are supporting checks only, not a substitute for proof.

**Originality — PASS, bounded.** The closest fully inspected paper (Lavrauw, Storme and Van de Voorde, arXiv:1201.3290) uses the identical incidence linear-algebra machinery in coding theory but gives neither dynamics nor a first-entry distribution. Other retrieved records on identical graphs concern graph topology or real spectra. The abstract of the Hall-plane coding paper (Key, McDonough and Mavron, DOI:10.1007/s00022-013-0189-8) supports genuine rank dependence but its full text was inaccessible, so an implicit earlier equivalent is a documented risk. Standard incidence identities and rank results are not claimed as new.

**Value — PASS.** The complete infinite-family classification exposes a sharp geometric parity dichotomy, distinguishes recurrent population scales of four versus \(2^{2v-2}\), and interprets the binary incidence rank via an exact transient-entry population. This is a natural dynamical invariant on an important family of graphs, not a selected numerical coincidence.

Limits: existing planes only, binary synchronous open-neighborhood XOR only; numeric even-order entry distributions depend on the specific plane's rank; no independent validation or historical priority guarantee.
