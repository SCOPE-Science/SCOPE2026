# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The local update for a zero hub is neighbour conjunction, and for a one hub it is neighbour disjunction. Their two-step identities give necessary and sufficient conditions for a fixed-hub or switching-hub two-cycle. The run decomposition enumerates all switching configurations and distinguishes the two parity components when the rim length is even. The Goles–Olivos theorem excludes longer periods. The proof handles the two-vertex step-two cycles, the exceptional four-rim wheel, both unanimity states, and labelled-versus-unlabelled counts. Exhaustive enumeration for rim sizes 3 through 14 independently confirms the stated classifications. Risk: the independent audit has not been performed; finite checks cannot replace the infinite combinatorial proof.

## Originality — PASS (bounded assessment)

The 1980 Goles–Olivos period-two theorem and its 2014 treatment by Benjamini et al. imply the absence of longer recurrent periods, but do not supply the wheel-specific count. The 2014 primary HTML source was inspected through its random-graph and threshold-dynamics sections, the 2014 Epperlein–Siegmund–Stehlík paper was inspected at its exact wheel analysis (Section 8), and a later study discussing wheel stabilization was compared. The 2014 wheel paper concerns a different imitation-based update and thus does not imply the majority census. Exact wheel-specific hub-switching classifications or the circular-run count were not located. Risk: the full 1980 paper was not available in the inspected material, and equivalent results could exist elsewhere under alternate graph-dynamics terminology. Failure to find a result is not proof of originality.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A wheel adds one globally coupled hub to a local cycle, making the simplest nonregular test graph for majority-periodicity phenomena. The theorem classifies every periodic configuration for all sizes, with an exact integer polynomial coefficient extraction, an explicit distinction between hub switching and non-switching, and a sharp exceptional size. These data are not implied by the general period-two theorem and offer a tractable benchmark for state-space enumeration on heterogeneous networks. The result does not claim a general graph-periodicity improvement.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
