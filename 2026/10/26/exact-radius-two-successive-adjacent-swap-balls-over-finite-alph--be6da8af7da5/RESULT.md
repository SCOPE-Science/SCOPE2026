# Exact radius-two successive adjacent-swap balls over finite alphabets

## Finding

For a length-\(n\) word \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\) over an alphabet of size \(q\ge2\), write \(\mathcal B_2(x)\) for the distinct words reachable from \(x\) by **at most two consecutive-symbol swaps performed successively**, with overlapping swaps permitted. Define
\[
U=\{i:1\le i<n,\ x_i\ne x_{i+1}\},\quad b=|U|,\quad s=|\{i:i,i+1\in U\}|,
\]
\[
h=\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}\mathbf1[x_i\ne x_{i+2}],\qquad
 d=\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}\mathbf1[|\{x_i,x_{i+1},x_{i+2}\}|=3].
\]
Then for every \(q\ge2\) and \(n\ge1\), with empty sums zero,
\[
\boxed{|\mathcal B_2(x)|=1+b+\binom b2-s+h+d.}
\]
For a uniformly chosen word, the exact mean when \(n\ge2\) is
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E|\mathcal B_2(x)|=1+\frac{(n-1)(q-1)}q+
\frac{(n-2)(n+1)(q-1)^2}{2q^2};}
\]
for \(n=1\), the mean is \(1\).

For \(q\ge3\) and \(n\ge2\), the **sharp maximum** and number of maximizing words are respectively
\[
\boxed{\max_x|\mathcal B_2(x)|=\binom{n+1}{2}-1,\quad
\#\operatorname{argmax}=q(q-1)(q-2)^{n-2}.}
\]
These maximizers are exactly the words satisfying \(x_i\ne x_{i+1}\) at each adjacent pair and \(x_i\ne x_{i+2}\) at each distance-two pair (equivalently, every consecutive triple is pairwise distinct when \(n\ge3\)). For \(n=1\), every word has ball size \(1\).

For \(q=2\), the sharp maxima are \(1,2,3,6\) for \(n=1,2,3,4\), and
\[
\boxed{\max_x|\mathcal B_2(x)|=\frac{n^2-3n+6}{2}\quad(n\ge5).}
\]
For \(n\ge6\), the only two maximizing binary words are the fully alternating words. At \(n=5\) exactly six maximizing words exist: \(01001,01010,01101,10010,10101,10110\); at \(n=4\), only \(0110\) and \(1001\) maximize. At \(n=3\), the six nonconstant words maximize; at \(n=2\), the two words with unequal letters maximize; at \(n=1\), both words maximize.

## Assumptions and scope

All words have length \(n\ge1\) over a fixed \(q\)-element alphabet, and \(q\) is any integer at least \(2\). An adjacent swap at site \(i\) exchanges positions \(i\) and \(i+1\). The radius counts the **minimum number of sequential swaps**; the unchanged word is included. Swapping equal letters leaves the word unchanged and is immaterial. The result concerns the *successive* model, not the radius-two set generated by one simultaneous batch of at most two disjoint swaps.

In the notation of Kovačević--Goyal--Kiah [1], \(\mathcal B_2(x)=B^{\mathrm{(s)}}(x;2)=\bar B(x;2)\). The last equality is special to radius two: each pair of successive swaps is either disjoint (one allowed simultaneous batch) or overlapping (two single-swap batches); conversely, two batches of total size at most two expand into at most two successive swaps. No extension of this equality to larger radii is asserted.

## Proof

**1. Classification by changed support.** If the two effective swaps use the same site, they undo each other. If the second site is at distance at least two from the first, the swaps are disjoint; both sites must belong to \(U\), and their output changes precisely four original positions. Such choices contribute exactly \(\binom b2-s\) words, since \(s\) removes the adjacent pairs of eligible sites. Different choices have distinct four-position changed supports, and no such output is in the zero- or one-swap classes.

A single effective swap changes exactly two adjacent positions, and each of the \(b\) eligible sites gives one distinct word. Together with the unchanged word, the zero- and one-swap contributions are \(1+b\).

It remains to count **overlapping** swaps on the unique affected triple \((a,b,c)\). The two possible two-swap orders yield cyclic rotations \((b,c,a)\) and \((c,a,b)\). Relative to \((a,b,c)\), the first is genuinely new beyond zero and one swaps exactly when \(a\ne c\) and \(a\ne b\), or, more generally, among the two rotations the exact number of genuinely new words is
\[
\mathbf1[a\ne c]+\mathbf1[|\{a,b,c\}|=3].
\]
Indeed, if all three are pairwise distinct, both rotations change all three positions; if precisely two distinct symbols occur with \(a\ne c\), exactly one rotation is a new end-to-end displacement whose changed positions are the two ends of the triple; if \(a=c\), neither rotation is new. (This includes triples with a repeated adjacent pair.) Every genuinely new rotation either changes all three positions or changes the first and third positions of its triple; therefore its original changed support determines the triple. These outputs cannot coincide across different triples, with the one-swap class, or with a disjoint two-swap output. Summing over all \(n-2\) triples proves the center-specific formula.

**2. Mean.** For uniform independent letters, each eligible boundary has probability \(p=(q-1)/q\). Two distinct boundaries, including neighboring ones, are jointly eligible with probability \(p^2\) (condition on the shared letter in the neighboring case). There are \(\binom{n-2}{2}\) pairs of nonadjacent sites. On each triple, the two bonus indicators have expectations \((q-1)/q\) and \((q-1)(q-2)/q^2\), summing to \(2p^2\). Linearity of expectation gives
\[
1+(n-1)p+\left(\binom{n-2}{2}+2(n-2)\right)p^2,
\]
which simplifies to the displayed exact mean. This proof does not require independence of the entire family of boundary indicators.

**3. Nonbinary maximum.** Each \(x\) obeys \(b\le n-1\), at most \(\binom{n-2}{2}\) nonadjacent site pairs, and at most \(2(n-2)\) new overlapping outputs. Thus
\[
|\mathcal B_2(x)|\le1+(n-1)+\binom{n-2}{2}+2(n-2)
=\binom{n+1}{2}-1.
\]
All three inequalities are equalities precisely when each adjacent pair differs and every triple has three pairwise distinct symbols. For \(q\ge3\), such words exist at every length: choose \(x_1\) in \(q\) ways, \(x_2\) in \(q-1\) ways, and each later symbol in \(q-2\) ways. This also counts *all* maximizers, including \(n=2\).

**4. Binary maximum and rigidity.** Put \(N=n-1\) and define boundary bits \(a_i=\mathbf1[x_i\ne x_{i+1}]\) for \(1\le i\le N\). Writing \(b=\sum a_i\) and \(s=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}a_ia_{i+1}\), binary symbols satisfy \(d=0\) and \(h=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}|a_i-a_{i+1}|=2b-a_1-a_N-2s\). Hence the ball size is
\[
F(a)=1+3b+\binom b2-a_1-a_N-3s.
\]
Let \(z=N-b\) be the number of zero boundary bits, \(E=(1-a_1)+(1-a_N)\) their endpoint count, and \(C_0=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}(1-a_i)(1-a_{i+1})\) their adjacent-pair count. Direct expansion gives the exact deficit from the all-one boundary pattern:
\[
\boxed{F(a)-\left(2+\binom N2\right)
=\frac{z(z+7-2N)}2-2E-3C_0.}
\]
For \(N\ge8\), every \(z\ge1\) has \(z+7-2N<0\), hence loses strictly. For \(N=7\), the factor is strictly negative unless \(z=7\), when \(C_0=6\) makes the deficit negative. For \(N=6\), it is strictly negative when \(z\le4\); for \(z=5,6\) we have \(C_0\ge4,5\), respectively, and the displayed deficit is negative. For \(N=5\), it is strictly negative for \(z\le2\); for \(z=3\) the first term vanishes but \(E+C_0>0\), because three zero positions cannot be both interior and pairwise nonadjacent; for \(z=4,5\), we have \(C_0\ge2,4\), respectively, again giving strict negativity. Thus for all \(N\ge5\), the only maximizers have \(a_1=\cdots=a_N=1\), i.e. the two fully alternating binary words, and their ball size is \(2+\binom{n-1}{2}=(n^2-3n+6)/2\).

For \(N=4\), the deficit formula permits exactly the all-one boundary pattern and the two patterns with one *interior* zero, giving six binary words. For \(N=3\), the unique maximizing boundary pattern is \((1,0,1)\), giving \(0110,1001\). For \(N=1,2\) or \(n=1\), direct evaluation of the same local formula gives the stated cases.

## Verification

The standalone `verify_adjacent_radius_two.py` independently builds balls by repeated swaps and compares them word-by-word with the local closed formula. It exhausts all binary words of lengths \(1\) through \(11\), ternary words of lengths \(1\) through \(8\), and four-symbol words of lengths \(1\) through \(6\), totaling \(19{,}394\) center words. The checker also verifies exact rational means, maxima, complete finite extremizer lists, and the binary boundary-polynomial identity through \(13\) boundary positions. The included `verification_output.txt` records its successful run. These are *finite consistency tests*; the all-length results are established by the preceding proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Kovačević, Goyal and Kiah [1] distinguish one-batch disjoint adjacent swaps, two-batch generalized balls \(\bar B\), and the successive-swaps metric. Their Proposition 5 gives inequalities for \(|\bar B(x;r)|\), while Proposition 6 evaluates the mean of the *single-batch* exact-error spheres. Neither implies the local exact count for \(\bar B(x;2)\), its different mean, nor the above sharp extremizer classifications. In particular, the two-swap overlap terms \(h+d\) are absent from the single-batch disjoint formula. Cicirello [2] defines the Kendall tau sequence distance on multisets and provides efficient distance-computation algorithms, without giving a radius-two center-specific volume formula or extremizers. Both original sources were compared at the statement and assumption level.

## Limitations

This theorem treats exactly radius two, not radius three or higher. Alphabet-independent support counting fails to be sufficient at higher radius because interacting overlapping swaps can revisit positions. The result supplies exact volumes, averages, and extremizers; it does not establish new optimal code cardinalities by itself. The literature comparison covers inspected available sources and searched indexed findings; an unindexed equivalent calculation remains a possible originality risk. Independent scientific validation has not been performed.
