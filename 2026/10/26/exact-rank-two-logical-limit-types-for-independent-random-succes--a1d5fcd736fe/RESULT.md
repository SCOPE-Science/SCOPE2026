# Exact rank-two logical limit types for independent random successor orders

## Finding
Fix an integer \(r\geq1\). Independently choose \(r\) uniformly random permutations of an \(n\)-element labelled set, regarding each as a linear ordering, and retain only its directed immediate-successor relation \(S_i\). In the first-order vocabulary \(S_1,\ldots,S_r,=\), let \(q\leq2\) denote quantifier rank, not the number of distinct variable names. Set \(K=r(r-1)\). For each \(i<j\), write
\[
C^{+}_{ij}=\#\{(a,b):S_i(a,b)\wedge S_j(a,b)\},\qquad
C^{-}_{ij}=\#\{(a,b):S_i(a,b)\wedge S_j(b,a)\}.
\]
Then the \(K\) random counts \((C^+_{ij},C^-_{ij})_{i<j}\) converge jointly to independent Poisson random variables, each of mean one. In addition, the resulting structures have exactly \(2^K\) asymptotically positive complete first-order quantifier-rank-at-most-two theories. These theories are distinguished precisely by the Boolean vector indicating which of the \(K\) counts are positive. The limiting probability of a particular vector with \(h\) positive coordinates is
\[
(1-e^{-1})^h e^{-(K-h)}.
\]
For every first-order sentence \(\varphi\) of quantifier rank at most two, its limiting probability exists and is an effectively calculable polynomial in \(e^{-1}\) with integer coefficients. For \(r=1\), one limiting theory occurs; for \(r=2\), four occur with limiting weights \(e^{-2}\), two copies of \(e^{-1}(1-e^{-1})\), and \((1-e^{-1})^2\). This is a convergence law at rank two, not a claim of convergence for arbitrary quantifier rank.

## Assumptions and scope
The number of successor relations \(r\) is fixed as \(n\to\infty\), although any positive finite \(r\) is permitted. All \(r\) orders are independent and uniform. The underlying linear-order relations, constants, additional predicates, and counting quantifiers are absent. Successor pairs are ordered; \(C^+\) counts same-direction common adjacencies and \(C^-\) opposite-direction adjacencies. The \(K\) coordinates are indexed by the \(2\binom r2\) distinct signed unordered pairs. A complete rank-two theory means truth values of all first-order sentences of quantifier rank at most two, with no restriction on formula size or number of variable names.

## Proof
**1. Joint factorial moments.** In a uniformly random Hamiltonian path on \(n\) labelled vertices, \(s\) prescribed directed arcs occur together with probability \((n-s)!/n!=1/(n)_s\) if their union is a vertex-disjoint family of directed chains; otherwise the probability is zero. For a fixed mixed factorial moment of degree \(H\) of the \(K\) collision counts, expand it over ordered collections of \(H\) distinct signed collision motifs (within each count). Each motif prescribes two coloured arcs on one ordered vertex pair. Collections using \(2H\) distinct vertices contribute \((n)_{2H}\,n^{-2H}(1+O_r(1/n))=1+O_r(1/n)\), since every path colour contributes its assigned independent probability and the total number of prescribed coloured arcs is \(2H\). Here the implied constant may also depend on the fixed moment indices.

Any other collection has a connected component involving more than one motif. Consider its underlying simple graph of \(v\) vertices, with an edge for each unordered pair carrying a motif; every edge prescribes at least two coloured arcs. If \(v\ge3\), connectedness requires at least \(v-1\) underlying edges, hence at least \(2(v-1)>v\) prescribed coloured arcs. If \(v=2\), two distinct motifs on the same pair require at least three distinct coloured arcs (or are incompatible), likewise exceeding \(v\). Thus the sum over embeddings of each overlapping motif configuration is \(O(n^{v-e})=O(1/n)\) after accounting for the number \(e\) of prescribed coloured arcs; disjoint one-motif components contribute order one. There are only finitely many configurations for a fixed mixed moment. Every mixed falling-factorial moment therefore tends to one, proving joint convergence to \(K\) independent \(\operatorname{Pois}(1)\) variables by the multivariate factorial-moment criterion.

**2. Asymptotically clean local geometry.** The same overlapping-motif estimates imply that the probability that distinct collision motifs meet at a vertex tends to zero. The initial and final vertices of all \(r\) orders are mutually distinct with probability tending to one, since \(r\) is fixed. A collision touching any such endpoint also has probability tending to zero: condition on the relevant order's initial/final endpoint and its unique adjacent arc, then an independent order repeats its directed edge with probability \(1/n\), with the analogous estimate for an endpoint from a third order. A union bound over finitely many order pairs suffices. We work on the event on which these three properties hold. The total collision count is tight by Step 1, so the fraction of vertices involved in any collision or endpoint tends to zero.

**3. Rank-two back-and-forth invariant.** For distinct \(a,b\), define the mask
\[
\mu(a,b)=\{i^+:S_i(a,b)\}\cup\{i^-:S_i(b,a)\}.
\]
Define the signature \(T(a)=\{\mu(a,b):b\ne a\}\) and let \(\Sigma(M)=\{T(a):a\in M\}\), the *set*, not multiset, of vertex signatures. For finite loopless structures in this binary signature, equality \(\Sigma(M)=\Sigma(N)\) implies elementary equivalence through quantifier rank two: in the two-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game, answer the first chosen vertex with one having the same signature, and answer the second with a vertex realizing exactly the requested mask; if it repeats the first vertex, repeat its mate. All atomic relations and equality are preserved. The converse also holds: unequal signature supports permit a two-round Spoiler win, choosing an unmatched signature then one distinguishing mask. This is a purely finite binary-vocabulary statement (here loops are uniformly absent).

**4. Enumerating signatures.** Each vertex has at most \(2r\) distinct adjacent neighbours. For \(n>2r+1\), the empty mask belongs to every \(T(a)\). On the clean event in Step 2, a generic non-endpoint/non-collision vertex has signature consisting of the empty mask and all \(2r\) singletons \(\{i^+\},\{i^-\}\). Each of the \(2r\) endpoints is missing exactly one corresponding singleton. Each same-direction \((i,j)\) collision produces at one vertex the merged mask \(\{i^+,j^+\}\), replacing two outgoing singletons, and at the other vertex \(\{i^-,j^-\}\), replacing two incoming singletons. Each reverse-direction collision similarly produces paired merged masks \(\{i^+,j^-\}\) and \(\{i^-,j^+\}\), replacing the corresponding two singletons. By cleanliness, no other abnormal signatures arise, and all endpoint and generic signatures are present for sufficiently large \(n\) as the number of collisions stays bounded in probability. Each signed pair of merged masks is distinct from the masks for every other pair and sign. Therefore \(\Sigma(M)\) is determined exactly by the collision-positivity bit vector, and conversely that vector is recoverable from \(\Sigma(M)\).

The distinguishing bits themselves have rank-two sentences \(\exists x\exists y\,[x\ne y\wedge S_i(x,y)\wedge S_j(x,y)]\) and \(\exists x\exists y\,[x\ne y\wedge S_i(x,y)\wedge S_j(y,x)]\). Steps 3 and 4 show that no further rank-two distinctions occur with asymptotically positive probability. Step 1 gives independent positivity bits with probability \(1-e^{-1}\), establishing the stated number of classes and all their probabilities. Every rank-two sentence is a finite Boolean union of complete rank-two classes, so its probability has the stated effectively computable limit. To compute its limit, evaluate it on one sufficiently large clean finite representative for each bit vector, or equivalently run the finite two-round back-and-forth state procedure; multiply the resulting indicator values by the explicit class weights.

## Verification
The standalone Python 3 program `check_rank2_successors.py` enumerates all possible second orders relative to a fixed first order for \(n=5,\ldots,9\). It checks exact common/reverse adjacency moments and verifies that the observed row-signature support equals the predicted collision-bit signature support whenever the explicit clean-event hypotheses and available generic-vertex size are satisfied. It also samples independently generated families of \(2\), \(3\), and \(4\) random orders on \(150\) vertices, checking the same local characterization on every clean sample. The recorded successful final line is `RANK2_SUCCESSOR_PROFILES_OK`. The program tests finite consequences only; the Poisson limit and logical completeness are proved in Steps 1–4.

## Relationship to prior work
Jeż, Michaliszyn, and Witkowski [1] established finite satisfiability and finite-model bounds for two-variable logic with arbitrarily many designated successors; Sections 1–3 discuss pairwise successor masks and overlapping arcs, but do not give a random-structure joint-Poisson law or a rank-two limiting-theory distribution. Michaliszyn and Witkowski [2] treat satisfiability for multiple successors; their investigated quantities are not these limiting probabilities. Müller, Skerman, and Verstraaten [3] establish logical limit laws for random permutations in the one-bijection/two-full-orders vocabularies; neither is this random family of independent successor-only relations. A rank-two EF characterization by local pair diagrams is standard logic; the contribution here is its exact interaction with the independent-order Poisson collision geometry, including all limit-type weights. The classical distribution of common adjacencies for two permutations is related and is not independently claimed as new.

## Limitations
The result is for fixed \(r\), quantifier rank at most two, pure directed successors, and independent uniform orders. It does not establish a full first-order convergence law, higher-rank description, or growing-\(r\) limit. Standard asymptotic Poisson methods and two-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé games are used; historical priority of the complete combined classification is not certified, and a broader earlier logical limit theorem could subsume it. The finite checker is a consistency check, not a certificate of the infinite statement.
