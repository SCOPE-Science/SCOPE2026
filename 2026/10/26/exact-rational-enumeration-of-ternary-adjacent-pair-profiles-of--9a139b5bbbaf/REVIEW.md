# Scientific review: ternary adjacent-pair profile enumeration

**Correctness (PASS).** The equivalence of realizability and the connected directed-Euler-trail conditions is standard and used with exactly the positive-degree vertices. Each nonzero profile is counted once by one-, two-, or three-active-vertex class. Nonloop three-vertex graphs have exactly seven possible degree-imbalance patterns; separating all balanced matrices from six endpoint patterns and then removing exactly the disconnected configurations proves the formula. Loop generating factors and the unique zero profile cover all boundary cases. Independent direct word enumeration through length 10 and direct connected-flow enumeration through length 8 agree with the formal series. These tests support, but do not replace, the all-length proof.

**Originality (PASS, with residual priority risk).** Jacquet–Knessl–Szpankowski explicitly derive the rational balanced-matrix enumerator for cyclic Markov types. That term does not count open Euler trails nor account for the disconnection correction in the present linear-word formula. Kiah–Puleo–Milenkovic define general substring-profile equivalence and, crucially, state Theorem 4.5 giving the \(q^2-q+1\) asymptotic ratio for unrestricted versus closed length-two profiles. Their Table I already contains the closed ternary leading constant \(1/8640\); hence the claimed unrestricted leading constant \(7/8640\) is **covered by prior work** and is not the novelty basis. Chang–Chrisnata–Ezerman–Kiah present exact short-word formulas and lower bounds; their short-word regime with pair length two does not determine the entire all-length profile series. The published binary exact profile classification does not settle labeled ternary profiles. No inspected source supplies the stated rational function, though an unindexed prior equivalent remains possible; this is not an assertion of priority certification.

**Value (PASS).** Noiseless reconstruction from adjacent-pair counts has a natural exact zero-error code size, namely the number of distinct realizable profiles. The all-length rational function and explicit seventh-order pole give complete finite-length and asymptotic information for the first nonbinary alphabet, and computes every finite-length correction to the previously established leading asymptotic. The all-length exact counting distinction is structural rather than an arbitrary finite table, and it is useful for understanding endpoint loss in DNA-profile channels.

The most relevant prior exact counting theorem has a different (cyclic) scope. Potential unindexed equalities and generalizations to longer factors remain limitations. All proof steps were reviewed in the stated scope; no external independent validation was performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
