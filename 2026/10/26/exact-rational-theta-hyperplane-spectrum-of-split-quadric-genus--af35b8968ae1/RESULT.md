# Exact rational theta-hyperplane spectrum of split-quadric genus-four curves over \\(\\mathbf F_2\\)

## Finding
Every smooth \\((3,3)\\) curve on the split quadric has at most four \\(\\mathbf F_2\\)-rational theta-hyperplanes, and the bound is attained. More precisely, for each integer \\(k\\) between zero and four, the number of smooth fixed-coordinate equations admitting exactly \\(k\\) such hyperplanes is

| \\(k\\) | Number of equations |
|:---:|---:|
| 0 | 5,208 |
| 1 | 8,076 |
| 2 | 2,556 |
| 3 | 120 |
| 4 | 60 |
| **Total** | **16,020** |

The 60 extremal equations form three orbits under \\(\\operatorname{Aut}_{\\mathbf F_2}(Q)\\), of sizes 18, 36 and 6. The respective least numerical representatives for the coefficient encoding below are `0x29e3`, `0x2de6`, `0x7889`. Their four qualifying hyperplanes split into (smooth-conic section, union-of-rulings section) counts \\((2,2),(3,1),(4,0)\\).

## Assumptions and scope
Fix \\(\\mathbf F_2\\) and the Segre embedded split quadric \\(Q=\\mathbf P^1\\times\\mathbf P^1\\subset\\mathbf P^3\\) with Segre coordinates \\((z_{00}:z_{01}:z_{10}:z_{11})=(u_0v_0:u_0v_1:u_1v_0:u_1v_1)\\). The homogeneous coordinate ring of sections of \\(\\mathcal O_Q(3,3)\\) has the 16-monomial basis \\(u_0^{3-i}u_1^i v_0^{3-j}v_1^j\\) for \\(0\\le i,j\\le3\\). A nonzero \\((3,3)\\) equation is recorded by a 16-bit integer `mask` with bit \\(4i+j\\) its coefficient. A curve is counted if its scheme is geometrically smooth. Adjunction yields \\(K_C=\\mathcal O_C(1,1)\\) and genus \\(g=(3-1)(3-1)=4\\); geometric smoothness and ampleness ensure connectedness and irreducibility, and the Segre hyperplane series gives its canonical embedding.

A **rational theta-hyperplane** is one of the 15 \\(\\mathbf F_2\\)-hyperplanes \\(H\\subset\\mathbf P^3\\) such that every intersection multiplicity in the length-six geometric divisor \\(H\\cdot C\\) is even. Equivalently \\(H\\cdot C=2D\\) for an effective degree-three geometric divisor \\(D\\). This is the characteristic-two definition of a theta-hyperplane; it does not assert that the three contact points are distinct. In particular, a reducible conic section is permitted. The statistic \\(t(F)\\) counts rational hyperplanes satisfying this condition. All frequencies refer to equations in fixed \\(Q\\)-coordinates, not abstract isomorphism classes.

## Proof
**1. Finite action and smoothness.** There are \\(2^{16}-1=65,535\\) nonzero equations. The split-quadric group \\(G=(\\operatorname{PGL}_2(\\mathbf F_2)\\times\\operatorname{PGL}_2(\\mathbf F_2))\\rtimes C_2\\) has order \\(6\\cdot6\\cdot2=72\\): two independent fractional-linear substitutions and optional exchange of the two factors. `tri_orbits.py` explicitly constructs the induced linear action on the 16 coefficients, enumerates the orbit of the smallest unseen mask, and partitions all nonzero forms into 1,075 disjoint orbits. The sum of the recorded orbit sizes is exactly 65,535. On each of the four standard affine charts of \\(Q\\), a local polynomial \\(f(x,y)\\) defines a smooth curve iff \\((f,\\partial_x f,\\partial_y f)=(1)\\) in the polynomial ring over \\(\\mathbf F_2\\). The equivalence holds after extension to an algebraic closure by the weak Nullstellensatz. `tri_smooth.py` verifies this with exact lexicographic Gröbner bases in characteristic two. The certificate marks 252 smooth orbits, whose orbit sizes sum to 16,020.

**2. The 15 hyperplanes.** Write a rational hyperplane as \\(a z_{00}+b z_{01}+c z_{10}+d z_{11}=0\\), where \\(a,b,c,d\\in\\mathbf F_2\\) are not all zero. Its section on the quadric is a divisor of type \\((1,1)\\). If \\(ad+bc=1\\) (six choices), the section is a smooth rational conic. Parametrize it by \\((u_0:u_1)\\) and \\((v_0:v_1)=(b u_0+d u_1:a u_0+c u_1)\\). Restricting \\(F\\) produces a binary sextic \\(B(U,V)\\). In characteristic two, a binary sextic has an even geometric zero divisor exactly when it is the square of a binary cubic, equivalently the coefficients of \\(U^5V,U^3V^3,UV^5\\) vanish. The independent checker evaluates these three coefficient conditions exactly.

If \\(ad+bc=0\\) (nine choices), the section is the union of one \\(u\\)-ruling and one \\(v\\)-ruling through a rational point \\((P,R)\\). For a smooth \\(C\\), no entire ruling lies in \\(C\\), so the two restrictions are nonzero binary cubics. Their combined zero divisor is even precisely when each cubic has divisor \\(L\\cdot Q^2\\), where \\(L\\) is the linear factor vanishing at the ruling intersection \\((P,R)\\), and \\(Q\\) is a nonzero rational binary linear form. Necessity follows because each cubic has odd degree, so any odd-multiplicity point must be the shared point, and all other contributions must be even; conversely two such odd contributions at the shared point sum to even. This test is exact over the perfect field \\(\\mathbf F_2\\).

**3. Exact frequency computation.** `tri_probe.py` computes each hyperplane-incidence test by a precomputed binary-sextic substitution or two binary-cubic restrictions. `tritangent_check_independent.py` independently reconstructs the bilinear hyperplanes, graph parametrizations and cubic factor tests. For every one of the 1,075 orbit representatives, both return the identical pair (number of theta-hyperplanes, exact 15-plane bitset), and agree with `orbit_certificate.json`. For all 72 group transformations of each representative, the primary computation confirms that the theta-hyperplane count is orbit invariant; its transformed orbit size agrees with the recorded size. The exact smooth-orbit weighted histogram is the five-row table in the finding.

**4. Extremal cases.** No smooth certificate row has \\(t(F)>4\\). The three smooth certificate rows with \\(t(F)=4\\) have least masks `0x29e3`, `0x2de6`, `0x7889` and orbit sizes 18, 36 and 6 (stabilizer orders 4, 2 and 12). Testing the six smooth and nine reducible rational quadric sections gives the respective splits \\((2,2),(3,1),(4,0)\\), so the bound is attained in 60 equations and three projective orbits. Exhaustive coverage follows from the complete group-orbit partition, not statistical extrapolation.

## Verification
Run `python verify_all.py` in one directory containing `verify_all.py`, `tri_probe.py`, `tri_orbits.py`, `tri_smooth.py`, `tritangent_check_independent.py` and `orbit_certificate.json`; Python 3 and SymPy are required. The script reconstructs every orbit, redoes all exact characteristic-two smoothness tests, computes each 15-hyperplane bitset by both implementations, checks every orbit member for incidence-count invariance, and asserts the full histogram and three extremal representatives. `verification_output.txt` records successful execution. All data are finite; the enumerated 1,075 classes cover the entire 65,535-form domain.

## Relationship to prior work
Harris and Len (2017) and Kulkarni et al. (2017) study tritangents of canonical space sextics over characteristic-zero/real settings. Pacini and Testa (2020, Definition 3.10) formulate the even-intersection-multiplicity definition of theta-hyperplanes and investigate characteristic-two geometric counts for general curves over algebraically closed fields, rather than weighted frequencies among all 16,020 smooth binary split-quadric equations. Xarles (2020) classifies abstract genus-four curves over \\(\\mathbf F_2\\), tabulating point-count and zeta-related invariants; the inspected paper does not state the five-bin rational theta-hyperplane distribution or its three maximizer orbits. An isomorphism-class census can provide equations from which one could derive this table by new incidence and orbit-weight calculations, but its stated tables do not logically imply these frequencies. The enumerated rational hyperplane conditions are distinct from ordinary tritangent counts in characteristic zero, and unlike the tropical count of 15 are field-rational and characteristic-two arithmetic.

## Limitations
This result classifies rational theta-hyperplanes only among geometrically smooth \\((3,3)\\) equations on the split quadric over \\(\\mathbf F_2\\). It does not classify genus-four curves on nonsplit or singular quadrics, nor all abstract genus-four isomorphism classes. It does not claim three distinct geometric tangency points per hyperplane; conic sections and reducible sections may have concentrated intersection multiplicities. The implementations share the orbit enumeration and Gröbner smoothness labelling, while the hyperplane tests are independently constructed; no independent human audit has been performed. Indirect recoverability from unpublished or accompanying data to the older genus-four census and unindexed historical tables remains a priority risk.
