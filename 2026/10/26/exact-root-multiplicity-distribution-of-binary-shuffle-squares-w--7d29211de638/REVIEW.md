# Scientific review: exact root-multiplicity law for sparse binary shuffle squares

## Correctness — PASS

The quantifiers are all integers \(m\ge0\), \(t\ge1\), and all labeled linear binary output words of length \(2m+2\) with exactly two ones. Each distinct root has the form \(0^q1\,0^{m-q}\). The four inequalities \(q\le A\le2q\) and \(m-q\le C\le2(m-q)\) are proved necessary by the two copies' order constraints and sufficient by explicit distribution of both copies' zeros. For at least \(t=s+1\) roots, the four endpoint differences are at least \(s\); this forces the three gaps to contain \(2s\) zeros each, and subtraction produces precisely a smaller shuffle square. The invertible gap map establishes the tail count, hence the stated exact multiplicity distribution for every \(m\), with all boundary cases included. The limiting density follows from the tail and is conditional on a positive root count. The code independently verified 2,856 cases with a two-copy state algorithm, 715 constructive root-output incidences, and the formulas through \(m=100\). The finite checks are corroboration, not a proof for arbitrarily large \(m\).

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority uncertainty

The baseline scalar count of two-one shuffle squares and their **membership characterization were already proved** by Grytczuk, Pawlik and Pleszczyński [arXiv:2308.13882, Section 2.5, Theorem 3 and Corollary 1]; the same combinatorial interpretation explicitly appears in OEIS A005448. These are prior art and **not claimed new**. Datko and Pawlik [doi:10.3390/sym17020305, Proposition 1] counted outputs of an individual fixed root and studied unrestricted multiplicities, including selected unique-root cases. These facts determine neither the number of distinct roots of each two-one output nor the full inverse distribution of overlapping root-generated sets. The present additional content is the exact integer-root interval, three-gap peeling bijection, all-length \(N_m(t)\), bivariate generating function, sharp maximum multiplicity, and conditional limiting law. Direct statement-and-implication comparison with inspected primary material and accessible mathematical search records did not locate that joint distribution. An unindexed equivalent analysis, or a simple but previously unpublished consequence of earlier membership criteria, remains a historical-priority risk.

## Value — PASS

Root ambiguity, not merely recognition, is a natural reconstruction invariant: a word with one root has a unique shuffle-square explanation, whereas words with multiple roots are ambiguous. The earlier exact membership total is insufficient to determine how ambiguous its members are. The result completes the full multiplicity spectrum at every size in the first nonconstant binary composition layer, gives a structural inverse-count bijection and a limiting law, and distinguishes abundance of valid outputs (asymptotic density \(3/4\)) from rarity of uniquely rooted outputs among them (vanishing relative frequency). This constitutes an exact classification of a motivated invariant, beyond re-evaluating an existing numerical sequence.

## Closest literature and limitations

[1] Buss–Soltys, *Unshuffling a Square is NP-Hard*, arXiv:1211.7161v1 (30 November 2012), supplies the general self-shuffle framework. [2] Guégan–Ochem (2016), doi:10.1051/ita/2016007, deals with avoidance. [3] Grytczuk–Pawlik–Pleszczyński, arXiv:2308.13882, Section 2.5, **already gives the two-one membership test and total count**. [4] Datko–Pawlik, *Roots of Binary Shuffle Squares*, doi:10.3390/sym17020305 (2025), proves fixed-root output counts and studies root multiplicities in the unrestricted class. [5] OEIS A005448 credits the scalar counting interpretation in 2023. All five were examined for implication or overlap. The theorem concerns only two-one linear words; there is no claim about unrestricted alphabets, unrestricted composition or an independently verified publication-priority determination.
