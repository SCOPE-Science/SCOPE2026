Binary shuffle squares with exactly two ones admit a complete all-length root-multiplicity law: the number with exactly \(t\) distinct roots is \(S(m-3t+3)-S(m-3t)\), where \(S(j)=(3j^2+3j+2)/2\) for \(j\ge0\) and vanishes otherwise. A bijection removes two zeros from each of the three gaps per additional root, yielding a sharp maximum \(\lfloor m/3\rfloor+1\) and a triangular limiting law for normalized root multiplicity.
