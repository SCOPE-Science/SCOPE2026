# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** For every zero-labelled vertex in a partial part, each colour absent from all full parts must occur in at least two distinct partial parts. This supplies a global lower bound, independent of the colour distribution within full parts. Comparing the possible number of full parts gives the exact minimum. Both candidate constructions meet the required neighbourhood condition; equality analysis identifies every optimum and inclusion-exclusion/falling factorials count them. The included independent literal-neighbourhood checker verifies both minima and labelled optimum counts on 29 finite cases. The infinite theorem is justified by the proof, not by the finite tests.

**Originality: PASS with publication-priority risk.** The October 2026 regular-graph study uses the same singleton definition but develops exact values for cycles, ladders, prisms and generalized Petersen graphs, not a balanced multipartite all-optima classification. The 2019 regular-graph study uses the less restricted, set-valued model. A published complete-multipartite two-rainbow result does cover the low-palette case, so the new assessed claim expressly concerns \(k\ge3\). Database and primary-literature searches found no statement implying the residue formula together with the full equality classification and optimum census. This does not prove global priority.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** Balanced complete multipartite graphs are a natural unbounded-degree regular family for testing singleton facility-colouring constraints. The theorem gives a sharp all-palette phase diagram, a structural dichotomy for every minimum assignment, and the exact number of optimum named assignments. The switch at half a residual part's size is not supplied by generic regular-graph bounds. The result is restricted to equal part sizes, and no claim is made for the full weight enumerator.
