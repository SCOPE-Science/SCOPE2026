# Exact slow-winding area law for the integrable Ostrovski square maps

## Finding

For every integer \(K\ge 3\), let \(F=F_{\theta_K}\) be the area-preserving, piecewise-affine map of the unit square in Ostrovski [1] at the special parameter of his Theorem 5.1. Write
\[
s=\sin\theta_K\cos\theta_K,\quad t=\sin^2\theta_K,\quad \delta=1-2s>0.
\]
Let \(\Gamma_K^N\) (\(N=0,1,2,\ldots\)) denote that theorem's nested, rotationally symmetric invariant polygon, with circle rotation number \(\rho_N=1/(4(K+N))\). Let \(\mathcal A_K\) be the closed invariant annulus between the unit-square boundary and \(\Gamma_K^0\), and let \(\rho(z)\) be the circle rotation number of a point in this annulus, supplied by the invariant-circle foliation of Corollary 5.4 of [1]. Area means ordinary Lebesgue area (the whole square has area one). Then
\[
\operatorname{area}(\mathcal A_K)=2t(1+K\delta)
\]
and, **for every integer \(N\ge0\)**,
\[
\operatorname{area}\{z\in\mathcal A_K:0<\rho(z)\le\rho_N\}
=\frac{4t}{1+N\delta}
 -\frac{2t(1-K\delta)}{(1+N\delta)^2}. \tag{1}
\]
For a general small \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
\operatorname{area}\{z\in\mathcal A_K:0<\rho(z)\le\varepsilon\}
=\frac{16t}{\delta}\varepsilon+O_K(\varepsilon^2). \tag{2}
\]
In particular, if \(z\) is distributed uniformly by normalized area on \(\mathcal A_K\), then
\[
\mathbb P\{1/\rho(z)>R\}
\sim\frac{8}{\delta(1+K\delta)}\frac1R \quad(R\to\infty),
\qquad\mathbb E[1/\rho(z)]=+\infty. \tag{3}
\]
Here \(1/\rho\) is the number of iterates per complete winding in the rotation-number sense, **not** the period of a typical orbit (which may have irrational rotation number).

## Assumptions and scope

The parameter family, its maps, and the existence and foliation of the indicated invariant polygons are precisely those of [1, Sections 2–5]. In the first corner of the square, set \(E_1=(0,0)\), \(P_1=(s,t)\), \(P_4=(t,1-s)\). The corner linear map \(B\) sends \(P_4\) to \((t,s)\), has determinant one, and satisfies \(B^K P_4=P_1\). The adjacent bottom-triangle shear is
\[
A(x,y)=(x+\delta y/t,y).
\]
The quarter-turn about the square center is \(R(x,y)=(1-y,x)\). The intended claims are limited to \(K\ge3\), these special parameters, and the **outer** invariant annulus. No claim is made about rotation statistics of the region interior to \(\Gamma_K^0\) or generic parameter angles.

## Proof

**Step 1 (exact boundary-orbit scaling).** By the published construction of [1, Theorem 5.1 and Lemmas 5.2–5.3], \(\Gamma_K^N\) is generated by a quarter orbit starting on the segment \(E_1P_4\): \(K\) successive applications of \(B\), then \(N\) successive applications of \(A\), end at the quarter-turn of the starting point, and four rotated copies form a simple invariant polygon. Write its starting point as \(X_N=r_NP_4\). By linearity, the point after \(K\) steps is \(Y_N=r_NP_1\). After \(N\) shears it becomes
\[
A^N(Y_N)=(r_N(s+N\delta),r_Nt).
\]
Since \(R(X_N)=(1-r_N(1-s),r_Nt)\), equality of these endpoints forces
\[
r_N(1+N\delta)=1,\qquad r_N=\frac1{1+N\delta}. \tag{4}
\]
This also proves directly the location of the break-line intersection \(Y_N\) implicit in [1].

**Step 2 (the exact polygon area).** Put \(Z=(1/2,1/2)\), \(P_j=F^j(X_N)\) for \(0\le j\le K+N\), and \(z_j=P_j-Z\). The fourfold-rotated, simple counterclockwise polygon has signed area
\[
\operatorname{area}(\operatorname{int}\Gamma_K^N)
=2\sum_{j=0}^{K+N-1}\det(z_j,z_{j+1}). \tag{5}
\]
The determinant of the two consecutive corner vectors \(P_4\) and \(BP_4=(t,s)\) is
\[
\det(P_4,BP_4)=\det((t,1-s),(t,s))=-t\delta.
\]
Because \(\det B=1\), each of the \(K\) corner steps contributes \(-r_N^2t\delta\) to \(\det(P_j,P_{j+1})\). On the bottom shear segment, consecutive points have equal ordinate \(r_Nt\) and abscissas differing by \(r_N\delta\), so each of the \(N\) shear steps contributes the same \(-r_N^2t\delta\). Consequently
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{K+N-1}\det(P_j,P_{j+1})
=-(K+N)r_N^2t\delta. \tag{6}
\]
Translating coordinates to the center telescopes the mixed terms:
\[
\sum_j\det(P_j-Z,P_{j+1}-Z)
=\sum_j\det(P_j,P_{j+1})+\det(Z,P_0-P_{K+N}).
\]
As \(P_{K+N}=R(P_0)\) and \(P_0=r_N(t,1-s)\), the last determinant is \((1-2r_Nt)/2\). Combining this with (5)–(6) yields
\[
\operatorname{area}(\operatorname{int}\Gamma_K^N)
=1-2tr_N-2(K+N)t\delta r_N^2
=1-4tr_N+2t(1-K\delta)r_N^2. \tag{7}
\]
The outer region's area is therefore \(4tr_N-2t(1-K\delta)r_N^2\). At \(N=0\), \(r_0=1\), giving \(\operatorname{area}(\mathcal A_K)=2t(1+K\delta)\).

**Step 3 (rotation-number distribution).** [1, Corollary 5.4, with its preceding annular-foliation construction] establishes that the outer annulus is foliated by invariant circles, with the rotation number varying continuously and strictly between successive boundary polygons. It decreases toward the square boundary and equals \(\rho_N\) on \(\Gamma_K^N\). Thus the area of the sublevel set at the exact threshold \(\rho_N\) equals the area exterior to \(\Gamma_K^N\), up to area-zero boundary circles. Equation (7) gives (1).

To infer (2), observe that \(\rho_N\sim1/(4N)\) while \(r_N\sim1/(N\delta)\), so (1) equals \((16t/\delta)\rho_N+O_K(\rho_N^2)\). If \(\rho_{N+1}\le\varepsilon\le\rho_N\), monotonicity sandwiches the cumulative area between its values at these consecutive thresholds. Since \(\rho_N-\rho_{N+1}=O_K(\rho_N^2)\), the same coefficient and the \(O_K(\varepsilon^2)\) remainder hold throughout. Dividing by \(\operatorname{area}(\mathcal A_K)\) and setting \(\varepsilon=1/R\) gives the tail in (3). Finally the tail-integral identity \(\mathbb E[U]=\int_0^\infty\mathbb P(U>R)\,dR\) for the nonnegative random variable \(U=1/\rho\) shows that its mean diverges.

## Verification

The standalone `check.py` reconstructs the corner linear map from its vertex correspondences, builds the four quarter-turn copies of each polygon, and evaluates the shoelace area. It checks \(K=3,4,5,6\) and \(N\in\{0,1,2,3,10,100\}\) against (7), using the exact angle \(\theta_3=\pi/8\) and approximate numerical solutions of the special-parameter relation for the other three cases. These are numerical spot checks **only**; Steps 1–3 are the proof for all admissible integers.

## Relationship to prior work

Ostrovski [1] proves the existence of these particular polygons, their rotation numbers, and a complete invariant-circle foliation of the outer annulus. Those are *inputs*, not claimed new here. The additional information is the all-level closed area identity (1), the quantitative small-winding area asymptotic (2), and its reciprocal-winding heavy tail (3). The explicit area observable uses both the hyperbolic corner and the shear contributions; the qualitative foliation theorem alone does not state this measure distribution. Bullett [2] studies invariant circles of a different piecewise-linear twist map on an annulus, rather than this square-boundary area law. Comparisons were also made with other piecewise-linear area-preserving plane-map literature [3].

## Limitations

This does not establish properties of generic \(\theta\), of the center region, or statistical mixing; the large-\(R\) tail describes a distribution of invariant-circle rotation numbers under area measure, not random temporal escape from one invariant curve. The published existence theorem of [1] is used for the initial orbit geometry and foliation. Novelty is a bounded literature assessment, and no independent audit has been performed.
