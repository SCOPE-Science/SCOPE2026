# Exact special-automorphism orbits on an invariant torus-cylinder divisor

## Finding

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, and let \(n\geq1\). In the smooth affine \((n+2)\)-fold \(Y_n\) constructed by Nikitina from the torus quotient
\[
X_n=\{x^2y+z^3w^2u_1^2\cdots u_n^2=1\}\subset\mathbb A_k^{n+4},\qquad Y_n=X_n/\!/\mathbb G_m,
\]
with weights \((1,-2,2,-2,-1,0,\ldots,0)\) on \((x,y,z,w,u_1,u_2,\ldots,u_n)\), denote by \(\Delta_n\) the image of \(\{x=0\}\). The divisor \(\Delta_n\) is invariant under the special automorphism group \(\operatorname{SAut}(Y_n)\), as established in the cited source.

The **new conclusion** concerns the action on the invariant divisor. With weight-zero coordinates \(B=zw\), \(C=yz\), and \(U_i=u_i\) for \(2\le i\le n\), the boundary has the known torus-cylinder presentation \(\Delta_n\cong\mathbb A^1_C\times(\mathbb G_m)^n_{B,U_2,\ldots,U_n}\). Its **entire special-automorphism orbit partition** is exactly
\[
\bigl\{\rho^{-1}(b,u_2,\ldots,u_n): (b,u_2,\ldots,u_n)\in(k^\times)^n\bigr\},
\qquad
\rho:\Delta_n\longrightarrow(\mathbb G_m)^n,
\quad(C,B,U_2,\ldots,U_n)\mapsto(B,U_2,\ldots,U_n).
\]
Every one of these orbits is a closed affine line, and no point of the divisor is fixed. In particular, its set of geometric orbits is naturally parametrized by the torus and its exact invariant algebra is
\[
k[\Delta_n]^{\operatorname{SAut}(Y_n)}=k[B^{\pm1},U_2^{\pm1},\ldots,U_n^{\pm1}].
\]
The boundary product presentation and affine-space complement are background geometric inputs, not originality claims. The theorem makes no assertion about the orbit decomposition on the complement.

## Assumptions and scope

The base field is algebraically closed of characteristic zero, \(n\) is an arbitrary positive integer, and \(\operatorname{SAut}\) is the subgroup generated by **all algebraic** \(\mathbb G_a\)-actions. The torus action is given by the displayed integral weights and the affine hypersurface has its reduced scheme structure. Quotients are affine categorical quotients, which in this example are geometric quotients because the source proves that the torus action is free with closed orbits. The divisor has the reduced induced closed-subvariety structure; its displayed coordinate ring is determined directly below.

## Proof

**Boundary coordinates.** Restrict the defining equation to \(x=0\). Then
\[
 z^3w^2u_1^2\prod_{i=2}^n u_i^2=1,
\]
so all \(z,w,u_1,\ldots,u_n\) are units there, whereas \(y\) is a free affine-line coordinate. Write \(P=\prod_{i=2}^n u_i^2\), with empty product equal to one. The weight-zero functions
\[
 B=zw,\quad E=zu_1^2,\quad C=yz,\quad U_i=u_i
\]
satisfy \(EB^2P=1\). To show that they generate the *entire* invariant ring, take any invariant Laurent monomial
\[
 M=y^a z^b w^c u_1^d\prod_{i=2}^n u_i^{e_i},
\]
where \(a\geq0\) and the remaining exponents are integers. Weight zero says \(-2a+2b-2c-d=0\). Consequently \(t=b-a-c=d/2\) is an integer and
\[
 M=C^a B^c E^t\prod_{i=2}^nU_i^{e_i}
  =C^aB^{c-2t}\prod_{i=2}^n U_i^{e_i-2t}.
\]
Every invariant polynomial is a sum of these monomials. The characters \(B,U_2,\ldots,U_n\) are independent: a Laurent monomial relation among them would, in the torus character lattice, be a multiple of \((3,2,2,2,\ldots,2)\), but its \(u_1\)-exponent is zero, forcing that multiple to vanish; the other exponents then vanish. The coordinate \(C\) is algebraically independent over this Laurent ring because \(y\) is free and \(z\) invertible. Hence
\[
k[\Delta_n]=k[C,B^{\pm1},U_2^{\pm1},\ldots,U_n^{\pm1}].
\]
This also establishes smoothness, irreducibility and the exact product description.

**All boundary orbits.** The source proves that \(\Delta_n\) is preserved by every \(\mathbb G_a\)-action on \(Y_n\). Any locally nilpotent derivation on an integral domain of characteristic zero annihilates every unit: the exponential action maps an invertible regular function to a unit in the polynomial extension, whose units have no dependence on the additive parameter. Thus the restrictions of all such actions annihilate the invertible functions \(B,U_2,\ldots,U_n\); all boundary orbits lie within fibers of \(\rho\).

Conversely, the following explicitly defined derivation of \(k[X_n]\) is locally nilpotent and has torus weight zero:
\[
\delta(z)=x^2,\qquad
\delta(y)=-3z^2w^2\prod_{i=1}^n u_i^2,
\qquad\delta(x)=\delta(w)=\delta(u_i)=0.
\]
It preserves the hypersurface equation by direct differentiation and is triangular in \((z,y)\); therefore it descends to a \(\mathbb G_a\)-action on \(Y_n\). On \(\Delta_n\), its action on the specified coordinates is
\[
\delta(B)=x^2w=0,\quad\delta(U_i)=0,\quad
\delta(C)=x^2y-3z^3w^2\prod_{i=1}^n u_i^2=-3.
\]
Since \(-3\ne0\) in \(k\), this one-parameter subgroup translates the coordinate \(C\) transitively along each fiber of \(\rho\). No other orbit is possible. Each fiber is closed in the closed subset \(\Delta_n\), hence in \(Y_n\). Invariants under this translation alone are precisely the Laurent polynomials independent of \(C\), and every special automorphism fixes those Laurent coordinates. This proves the stated invariant ring and orbit classification.

## Relationship to prior work

Nikitina, arXiv:2610.05344v1 (4 October 2026), proves that these \(Y_n\) are smooth and generically flexible, provides their invariant coordinate presentation and a suitable locally nilpotent derivation, and proves that the divisor \(\Delta_n\) is invariant. The boundary product and affine complement follow from already available explicit quotient-coordinate analysis and are **not claimed as new** here. The cited paper does not state the exhaustive orbit classification or torus-valued orbit quotient. The new assertion identifies the action-theoretic equivalence relation between every pair of boundary points, not merely the boundary topology.

Gaifullin, arXiv:2507.14745v1, classifies finitely many special-automorphism orbits in a different, generally singular normal affine family with a boundary of four prime divisors. Its orbit theorem neither specializes to nor implies the present family of infinitely many closed affine-line orbits on a **smooth** boundary. More general theorems about additive-group actions and units supply a proof ingredient, not the explicit boundary description for this family.

## Limitations

The full orbit decomposition on the open affine-space complement is **not** determined. Neither the boundary product structure nor the affine-complement structure is submitted as an originality claim. The source's assertions about Cox realization, free closed torus orbits, and invariance of the image of \(\{x=0\}\) are explicitly used; the new algebraic arguments independently prove the boundary coordinates and orbit fibers assuming those documented premises. The assertion is about geometric \(k\)-point orbits and the ordinary invariant coordinate ring, not stabilizer group schemes or the structure of the full infinite-dimensional automorphism group. Search-based priority assessment is not an exhaustive historical uniqueness proof.
