# Scientific review

**Correctness (C): PASS.** Geometric smoothness follows from the explicit partial derivatives, and the projection is finite of degree three at every exterior center. Two exhaustive and independently coded exact finite-field calculations agree **pointwise** on all \(4161\) centers. Cardinality, incidence-moment and subfield-extremal assertions are checked; the finite algorithm is included and reproducible without external packages. There is no extrapolation to unsurveyed fields.

**Originality (O): PASS, with residual risk.** The 2015 completeness census explicitly covers the cubic and therefore the general existence of at least one three-secant through each exterior point is prior art. The 2023 Hermitian-arcs work studies split intersections algebraically, and the 2025 paper treats sixth-degree extension families. Their inspected results do not list the exact six-valued per-center splitting-fiber profile or characterize its twelve extremal centers. Accessible prior research and semantic record searches did not yield an equivalent distribution, although an unindexed old fine-incidence table remains possible. Distinguishing a global line-character census from a vertex-degree histogram is essential.

**Value (V): PASS.** Completely split fibers of degree-three morphisms from projection of an elliptic cubic form a natural arithmetic invariant. The full finite-field stratification, including a uniquely recognizable subfield-defined exceptional locus, is substantially more precise than completeness or global line characters. It is finite but exhaustive and mathematically motivated, rather than an arbitrary coordinate sample.

**Limits:** This is the marked-center census over precisely \(\mathbb F_{64}\); no general extension-field theorem or orbit classification is asserted. A possible unseen stronger formula remains an originality risk.
