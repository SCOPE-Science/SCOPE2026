# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The residual identity follows from base-\(b\) decomposition of \(c+u\). Distinct offset-residue pairs are separated by arbitrary high-position maximal digits and the growing run of maximal digits in \(b^K-\delta\). For bases \(b\ge3\), positive quotients at separate depths cannot overlap except quotient one, which is counted once. The proof gives all reachable states and proves minimality. Boundary cases \(c=0\), \(r=0\), empty carry intervals, and redundant leading zeroes have explicit treatments. Independent finite partition-refinement and direct-output computations passed; these tests do not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS within checked evidence

The latest motivating full text, arXiv:2610.03837 (2026-10-02), studies abelian complexity of the base-four digit-three residue sequence, not state counts after additive shifts. The full relevant sections of arXiv:2512.10017 (2025-12-10) provide generic bounds and state complexity for different transformations, not this all-shift exact formula. arXiv:1910.08543 (2019-10-18) computes the state complexity of scaled-and-translated images of the Thue–Morse set, an operation that does not imply the inverse-translation formula here. A previously recorded result treats translated binary digit sums; it cannot be substituted for maximum-digit counts in bases at least three, whose quotient-level separation is decisive. Semantic database searches identified no covering claim. Unindexed or unpublished equivalences remain a priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a complete description of a natural exact invariant for every shift of the recently introduced automatic word (and a broader infinite family). Beyond a construction, it proves that all reachable carry-and-residue states are intrinsically inequivalent. The adjacent-power identity exhibits arbitrarily large downward jumps across unit shifts, a meaningful nonmonotonic phenomenon absent from the generic existence and upper-bound statements. It is not claimed to solve arbitrary digit-pattern automata.
