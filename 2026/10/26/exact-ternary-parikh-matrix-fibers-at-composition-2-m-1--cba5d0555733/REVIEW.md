# Review of the fixed-composition Parikh-matrix result

## Correctness — PASS

The fixed Parikh vector leaves exactly the scattered counts \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=(|w|_{ab},|w|_{bc},|w|_{abc})\). Deleting the \(b\)'s produces exactly three disjoint letter skeletons; explicit \(b\)-gap parametrizations derive the three exact summands of each fiber. Intersection cardinalities, checked also in the edge cases \(\beta=0\) and \(h=0\), give the image polynomial; bounds on the triangular counting function and explicit witnesses establish the exact maximum. Direct enumeration through \(m=35\) matched every fiber, not just the totals. The all-integer proof is independent of finite tests.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical-priority risk

Teh's 2015 primary article provides the Parikh-matrix definition and ternary rewriting-system framework, including the signature identity, but its inspected text does not tabulate or determine all fibers for the composition \((2,m,1)\). The 2020 primary treatment of \(M\)-unambiguity concerns singleton classes. published-finding corpus comparisons with its global singleton sparsity record and neighboring composition-reconstruction records did not reveal this slice's complete fibers, cardinality polynomial, or maximal ambiguity law; those other claims do not imply the present exact three-term formula. The 2023 Hahn–Cheon–Han article was available as abstract only and concerns unambiguity; its complete contents could not be checked. An uncatalogued equivalent result remains possible; failed searches alone cannot prove uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

For a natural first sparse ternary family containing all three letters when \(m\ge1\), the result determines the *entire reconstruction-fiber law*, not only particular ambiguous examples. It supplies an exact zero-error image/code-size polynomial and a sharp contrast between asymptotically constant occupancy averaged over observations and linearly growing worst-case ambiguity. These properties address exact information loss in Parikh-matrix reconstruction. The fixed-composition restriction, alphabet order, and historical-priority uncertainty are explicit limits.
