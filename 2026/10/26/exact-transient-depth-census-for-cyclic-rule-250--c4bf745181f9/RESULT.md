# Exact transient-depth census for cyclic Rule 250

## Finding

Let \(n\ge3\) and let \(F_n\) be synchronous elementary cellular automaton Rule 250 on a labelled binary ring, with
\[
 (F_nx)_i=x_{i-1}\lor x_{i+1},\qquad i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z.
\]
The **preperiod** \(\tau_n(x)\) is the least nonnegative integer \(t\) such that \(F_n^t(x)\) belongs to a periodic orbit. Thus already-periodic states have preperiod zero. For integers \(q\ge1\), \(h\ge1\), define the polynomial
\[
 B_{q,h}(z)=1+\sum_{k=1}^{q}\frac qk\,[u^q](u+u^2+\cdots+u^h)^k\,z^k.
\]
All coefficients are nonnegative integers. Write \(E_{n,t}(z)=\sum_{x:\,\tau_n(x)\le t}z^{\#\{i:x_i=1\}}\). **For every \(t\ge0\)** and \(n\ge3\),
\[
 \boxed{E_{n,t}(z)=
 \begin{cases}
 B_{n,t+1}(z),&n\text{ odd},\\
 B_{n/2,t+1}(z)^2,&n\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}}
\]
Consequently the exact number of configurations of weight \(k\) with *preperiod exactly* \(t\) is the coefficient of \(z^k\) in \(E_{n,t}-E_{n,t-1}\), taking \(E_{n,-1}=0\). Expanding the coefficients requires only finite binomial sums:
\[
 [z^k]B_{q,h}(z)=\frac qk\sum_{j=0}^k(-1)^j\binom kj\binom{q-jh-1}{k-1}\quad(1\le k\le q),
\]
with out-of-range binomial coefficients interpreted as zero.

For odd \(n\), exactly two states are periodic, both fixed: all-zero and all-one. For even \(n\), exactly four states are periodic: the same two fixed states and the two alternating checkerboards forming one primitive two-cycle. In particular, no other primitive periods occur.

The sharp maximum preperiod and the number of initial configurations attaining it are
\[
 \boxed{\max_x\tau_n(x)=
 \begin{cases}n-1,&n\text{ odd},\\n/2-1,&n\text{ even},\end{cases}}
 \qquad
 \boxed{\#\{x:\tau_n(x)=\max\tau_n\}=
 \begin{cases}n,&n\text{ odd},\\n2^{n/2}-(n/2)^2,&n\text{ even}.\end{cases}}
\]
The even-size extremizers are precisely the configurations with exactly one occupied site in at least one of the two parity classes. Odd-size extremizers are exactly the singleton configurations.

## Assumptions and scope

All statements concern simultaneous update, periodic spatial boundaries, binary states, and labelled positions. The assertion includes rings of size three and four; neither fixed boundary conditions nor sequential update is covered. Each preperiod is measured in whole synchronous updates, with the initial state at time zero. An empty initial support has preperiod zero.

Write \(S(x)=\{i:x_i=1\}\). The map \(i\mapsto i+2\) has one orbit of length \(n\) for odd \(n\), and two parity orbits of length \(n/2\) for even \(n\). In one such orbit, list occupied sites in cyclic \(+2\) order; the **gap** from one occupied site to the next counts the number of steps of \(+2\) (a single site has a gap equal to the whole orbit length). Let \(\Delta\) be the largest gap, and take the maximum over all nonempty parity orbits when there are two. For empty support set \(\tau_n(0)=0\).

## Proof

**1. Reachability.** Boolean OR distributes over itself, so induction on \(t\) gives
\[
 S(F_n^t x)=S(x)+W_t,\quad W_t=\{-t,-t+2,\ldots,t-2,t\}\pmod n.
\]
Indeed \(W_0=\{0\}\) and \(W_t+\{-1,1\}=W_{t+1}\). The displayed set is a length-\(t+1\) interval in the cyclic order generated by the \(+2\) step, followed by a common translation by \(-t\). For odd \(n\), the whole ring is one such orbit; for even \(n\), the two parity classes are distinct orbits, possibly exchanged under the common translation.

**2. Exact absorption time.** On a cyclic orbit of length \(q\), let \(U\ne\varnothing\) be a set of sites with maximum cyclic gap \(\Delta(U)\). The union of \(t+1\) successive translates of \(U\) covers that whole orbit if and only if every gap between successive occupied sites is at most \(t+1\), equivalently \(\Delta(U)\le t+1\). Therefore the first full occupation of every active step-two orbit is exactly \(\max_U\Delta(U)-1\) updates.

To see that this is the dynamical *preperiod*, not just a saturation time, observe from the reachability formula that \(S(F_n^2x)\supseteq S(x)\). A periodic state must satisfy equality under two steps, since iterating a proper containment would contradict eventual return. Equality means its support is closed under both \(+2\) and \(-2\); hence its support is a union of entire step-two orbits. Conversely any union of these orbits is periodic because one application of \(F_n\) simply exchanges the parity classes when they are distinct, or preserves the occupied full ring. Thus the preperiod is precisely the first time all active step-two orbits have saturated. This also classifies the periodic states and their periods.

**3. Enumeration for one orbit.** Suppose a cyclic orbit has \(q\) labelled sites and exactly \(k\ge1\) occupied sites. Mark an occupied start and record the \(k\) positive gaps to successive occupied sites. They sum to \(q\). With each gap at most \(h\), the number of gap compositions is \([u^q](u+\cdots+u^h)^k\). There are \(q\) choices for the marked starting site, but each unmarked subset is represented exactly \(k\) times. Hence the number of valid unmarked subsets is \(q/k\) times this coefficient. The empty set contributes the constant coefficient one. Inclusion-exclusion applied to \(u^k(1-u^h)^k/(1-u)^k\) yields the binomial formula in the finding.

**4. Full census.** For odd \(n\), there is one step-two orbit, so its admissible subsets are counted by \(B_{n,t+1}\). For even \(n\), the two labelled parity orbits can be selected independently and their occupied-site counts add, giving \(B_{n/2,t+1}^2\). Taking successive differences yields every preperiod and fixed-density census. As \(B_{q,h}=(1+z)^q\) for \(h\ge q\), all preperiods are bounded by \(n-1\) for odd rings and \(n/2-1\) for even rings.

**5. Sharpness and extremizers.** If an orbit has size \(q\ge2\), a gap equal to \(q\) occurs if and only if its support is a singleton. Hence odd-ring maximal preperiods are attained exactly by its \(n\) singleton supports. For even rings, maximal preperiod requires a singleton in at least one parity orbit. There are \(2(n/2)2^{n/2}\) configurations from choosing the singleton's parity class, site, and an arbitrary subset of the other class. Configurations with singletons in both classes were counted twice; their number is \((n/2)^2\). Inclusion-exclusion gives the second extremizer formula. Every assertion follows.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` from the directory containing this document's accompanying checker. The checker independently implements the local Boolean rule, identifies eventual periodicity by explicit state repetition, computes the step-two gap statistic, and compares the binomial-polynomial predictions by occupancy weight and preperiod. It exhausts all binary configurations on all rings \(3\le n\le16\), then tests 4,800 seeded examples on larger rings; these finite checks corroborate but are not substituted for the unrestricted proof.

For example, the complete unrestricted formula gives for \(n=8\) the transient-depth histogram \(4,60,80,112\) at times \(0,1,2,3\), respectively, summing to \(256\). At \(n=6\) the histogram is \(4,21,39\) at times \(0,1,2\).

## Relationship to prior work

Wolfram introduced and studied elementary cellular automata in 1983. Rule 250 is the generalized additive Boolean-OR rule of two neighbors (Wolfram/MathWorld). Wolfram's 1984 formal-language theory concerns reachability languages, their regularity, and periodic configurations, not the present fixed-density transient-depth counts. Stricker's 2023 complete finite-size numerical cycle-spectrum classifications focus on attainable *cycle lengths*, not how many initial states enter those cycles at each time. Zhu and Richard's 2026 Koopman-spectrum classification considers the same finite periodic-boundary family in aggregate but does not give these explicit Rule-250 preperiod generating polynomials. The new assertion is the full all-ring, all-density exact relaxation histogram, including sharp extremizer counts, rather than the known local rule or the qualitative fact that many trajectories simplify.

The counting method itself uses classical cyclic compositions and inclusion-exclusion; those tools are not claimed as new. The synthesis into the stated complete Rule-250 transient census is the contribution being assessed. Previous exact results may have escaped the accessible search, and historical priority is not independently certified.

## Limitations

The claimed classification applies only to synchronous Rule 250 on finite binary rings. It does not classify arbitrary Boolean-semiring cellular automata, random update schedules, or nonperiodic initial configurations on the infinite line. The coefficient formulas are finite sums; no claim of a newly discovered binomial identity is made. Independent review and historical-priority confirmation have not been performed.
