# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For every fixed integer \(r\ge3\), \(k\ge3\), and \(b\ge1\), the exact finite sum over admissible labelled tuple graphs follows from free amalgamation and ultrahomogeneity of the universal \(K_{r+1}\)-free graph. The common-neighbour criterion is equivalent to forbidding a \(K_{r+1}\) in a single parameter clique. Inclusion–exclusion establishes the first three bases, and each additional internal edge strictly eliminates a concrete admissible one-clique assignment, proving a strict gap to every remaining base. The triangle correction removes precisely 13 admissible external patterns per chosen \(r-2\)-element common core. Dividing by the leading exponential term yields the rarity and conditional two-incident-edge concentration statements. Direct finite-graph and independent common-neighbour checks pass 294,912 local assignments, 4,800 two-block tests and nine exact triple checks. The general proof does not rely on the finite checks.

A published one-type Henson counting result treats neighborhood subsets and counting complexity rather than ordered triple or higher tuple types. Earlier enumerations of triangle-free higher-arity tuples and four-clique-free pairs do not imply the present \(K_{r+1}\)-free \(r\ge3\), \(k\ge3\) formulas. ## Mathematical value — PASS
The family of named disjoint maximal parameter cliques is a natural controlled stress test for counting complete higher-arity types in a non-free, forbidden-clique homogeneous graph. The exact four-base formula for ordered triples and the sharp all-fixed-arity three-base hierarchy separate four possible triples and the first three large-deviation strata. Most distinctively, conditional on observing at least two edges among the tuple, the two edges almost surely share a vertex as parameter size grows; disjoint two-edge patterns are exponentially suppressed. These are exact structural consequences, not arbitrary finite table entries or routine restatement of one-point neighborhood counting.

This review concerns nonalgebraic ordered injective tuple types over a disjoint union of \(K_r\) cliques, not all finite parameter graphs, varying arity, or random-vertex distributions.
