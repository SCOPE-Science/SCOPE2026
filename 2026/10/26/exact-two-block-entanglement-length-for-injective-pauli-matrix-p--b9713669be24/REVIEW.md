# Scientific review: exact two-block entanglement length for injective Pauli matrix-product states

## Correctness — PASS
The statement quantifies over strictly positive Pauli weights, all finite nonempty contiguous block lengths, and every positive integer separator length. The Pauli transfer channel is explicitly bistochastic and primitive. Each block tensor map is injective at length one and at every greater length because the four Pauli matrices occur as nonzero word products. The proof identifies the **exact** two-region density matrix as the image of the output-first Choi operator tensored with two separable spectator factors. Left inverses of the physical embeddings recover that operator: both separability and PPT are consequently preserved in both directions. The two-qubit PPT equivalence and the standard unital-qubit entanglement-breaking criterion yield the sharp octahedron inequality, including its equality boundary. The isotropic ceiling and limit follow algebraically. Fifty-seven numerical cases cross-check direct contractions, recovered Choi factors, normalization, and PPT signs; the universal claim relies on the proof rather than finite testing.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge)

Ruskai's 2003 octahedral threshold is an existing result for unital qubit **channels**, not a theorem about all physical matrix-product-state block marginals; the full local-filter equivalence supplies the nontrivial bridge. Sauerwein–Molnár–Cirac–Kraus provide injective tensor-map principles used here; Rahaman–Jaques–Paulsen study the channel power notion. No statement of global novelty is inferred from an empty search. ## Value — PASS
The exact finite-length threshold replaces a qualitative existence guarantee by a complete parameter-resolved separability/NPT phase classification for a canonical primitive qubit transfer family. Its universality over **both arbitrarily sized physical blocks** makes it stronger than testing two individual physical sites; every subthreshold pair remains NPT. The near-product boundary gives a natural family with a provably diverging length \(L_*(s)\sim\log3/(1-s)\), despite local convergence to a product state. The sharp answer is directly usable as a controlled benchmark for quantitative sudden-death bounds and illustrates why finite entanglement length need not be uniform over tensor parameters.
