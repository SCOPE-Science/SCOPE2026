# Review

## Correctness

The rowwise conflicts between second-one positions differing by one give an upper bound \(\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor\). The odd-gap code saturates each row. Comparing the parity profiles of the three possible weight-two descendants and the two possible weight-one descendants proves that distinct odd-gap words share at most one deletion output. The full proof applies to all \(n\ge2\); the finite checker was also executed through \(n=18\).

## Originality

The nearest older papers survey single-deletion-correcting codes and optimal colorings of constant-weight one-read deletion graphs. Their inspected statements do not establish this distinct two-read optimal cardinality. One earlier general multiple-read reconstruction paper was available only as an abstract and remains a substantive residual risk; absence of a direct match is not a guarantee of priority.

## Value

The two-read minimum-read setting is intrinsic to sequence reconstruction. The weight-two restriction defines a natural sparse family of words, and the exact all-length optimum reveals a quadratic capacity achieved by a simple parity-restricted family. This is a structural classification of the optimum size, not a finite numerical census.
