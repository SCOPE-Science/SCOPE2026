# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claimed strict period reversal reduces to two exact Collatz–Wielandt spectral bounds after positive scalar matrix shifts. The attached executable performs the crucial inequalities entirely with integers, including its Taylor tail enclosure and the strict final fourth-power comparison. The minimal patch claim reduces by fixed diagonal similarity to the established symmetric-dispersal period monotonicity principle; one-season period independence is immediate. The precise claim requires no unverified infinite calculation.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Benaïm et al. (arXiv:2311.04706) formulated monotonicity for fixed asymmetric migration. Monmarché, Schreiber, and Strickler (arXiv:2408.11179) had **already exhibited nonmonotonicity** using three environmental regimes; this finding does not claim otherwise. Their three-regime witness does not mathematically imply a two-regime witness. The present contribution is an explicit two-regime, three-patch integer certificate together with a sharp simultaneous lower bound on both counts. Comparable literature and published mathematical result summaries were checked, without finding this exact two-regime structural boundary.

## Value — PASS
The number of independent seasonal regimes is a natural complexity measure for an environmental-switching population model. Resolving its least possible value sharpens understanding of the mechanism behind period-dependent growth with irreversible dispersal, and the tiny fully explicit network provides a reproducible benchmark not dependent on numerical spectral plots.

## Closest literature, limits and risks
The nearest published counterexample has three patches **and three regimes**, rather than the two here. The result only proves a strict pairwise inequality at two environmental periods. Literature coverage is not exhaustive, particularly for equivalent positive-switched-system language; it makes no assertion of independent audit or publication priority beyond the inspected sources.
