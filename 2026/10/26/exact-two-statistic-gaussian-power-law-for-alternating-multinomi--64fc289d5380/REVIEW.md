# Research review

**Correctness — PASS.** The fixed-dimension multinomial Gaussian reduction, one-dependent joint central limit theorem, exact folded-normal mean expansion, and Gaussian linear-score optimization establish the result in the explicitly iterated \(N\)-then-even-\(n\) limit. The independent normal variables are merely a representation of the fixed-\(n\) multinomial Gaussian limit. The raw covariance identity uses the conditional moment \(\mathbb E[G_1^2-1\mid G_2-G_1=w]=w^2/4-1/2\). Numerical checks reproduce the constants but prove no limit.

**Originality — PASS, with residual literature risk.** The full motivating 2026 papers give empirical power and then uniform-null moments; they do not calculate this alternative two-statistic joint law. Beirlant–Györfi (1994) compares \(\ell_p\) *cellwise errors*, not neighboring differences. Hoel–Kamat normal null variance is pre-existing and not claimed as original. Some null cross-moment theory may likewise be classical, and an unknown alternative-power theorem could overlap; these risks are recorded in AUDIT.json.

**Value — PASS.** This gives a sharp, interpretable analytic power comparison for the natural comb alternatives of a recently proposed test, plus the surprising sign reversal of the best two-statistic linear score. The coefficient ratio is expressly a ratio of squared limiting Gaussian shifts, not a blanket Pitman or global sample-efficiency statement.

No uniform simultaneous \(N,n\) limit, no alternative-shape universality, no oracle-optimality, and no independent validation are asserted.
