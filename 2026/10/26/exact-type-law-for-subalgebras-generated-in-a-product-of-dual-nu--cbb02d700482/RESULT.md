# Exact type law for subalgebras generated in a product of dual-number rings

## Finding

Let \(q\) be a prime power and \(R_q=\mathbb F_q[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\). For \(r,\ell\ge1\), choose \(\ell\) independent uniform elements \(X_1,\ldots,X_\ell\) of \(A_{q,r}=R_q^r\), and let \(B=\mathbb F_q[X_1,\ldots,X_\ell]\) be the unital subalgebra they generate.

For each integer \(s\) with \(0\le s\le\ell\), let \(A_s=\mathbb F_q\oplus V_s\), where \(V_s\) is an \(s\)-dimensional vector space and \(V_s^2=0\). In particular, \(A_0=\mathbb F_q\). There are uniquely determined nonnegative integers \(C_0,\ldots,C_\ell\) such that
\[
B\cong\prod_{s=0}^{\ell} A_s^{C_s}.
\]
The joint distribution of these multiplicities is exactly
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\sum_{r\ge1}q^{2\ell r}\,
\mathbb E\!\left[\prod_{s=0}^{\ell}z_s^{C_s}\right]\frac{t^r}{r!}
=\left(1+\sum_{s=0}^{\ell}z_sF_{\ell,s}(t)\right)^{q^\ell}-1,
}
\]
where
\[
N_{\ell,m,s}(q)=
\begin{cases}
{\ell\brack s}_q\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}(q^m-q^j),
&0\le s\le\min(\ell,m),\\[4pt]
0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\qquad
F_{\ell,s}(t)=\sum_{m\ge\max(1,s)}N_{\ell,m,s}(q)\frac{t^m}{m!}.
\]
Here \({\ell\brack s}_q\) is a Gaussian binomial coefficient. Thus, for any nonnegative \(c_0,\ldots,c_\ell\) with a feasible total dimension,
\[
\Pr(C_0=c_0,\ldots,C_\ell=c_\ell)
=\frac{r!}{q^{2\ell r}}
[t^r z_0^{c_0}\cdots z_\ell^{c_\ell}]
\left(1+\sum_{s=0}^{\ell}z_sF_{\ell,s}(t)\right)^{q^\ell}.
\]
The coefficient is zero when the requested type cannot occur.

## Assumptions and scope

The base field is any finite field \(\mathbb F_q\); no restriction is placed on its characteristic. The ambient algebra is exactly the direct product of \(r\) copies of the dual-number ring \(R_q\). The \(\ell\) sampled elements are independent and uniform, and “generated subalgebra” means the unital \(\mathbb F_q\)-subalgebra.

Write the \(i\)-th coordinate of \(X_j\) uniquely as \(a_{ij}+\varepsilon b_{ij}\), with \(a_{ij},b_{ij}\in\mathbb F_q\). For coordinate \(i\), its residue vector is \(a_i=(a_{i1},\ldots,a_{i\ell})\in\mathbb F_q^\ell\), and its tangent vector is \(b_i=(b_{i1},\ldots,b_{i\ell})\in\mathbb F_q^\ell\). For a residue vector \(a\), let \(C_a=\{i:a_i=a\}\). If \(C_a\) is nonempty, let \(s_a\) be the rank over \(\mathbb F_q\) of the \(\ell\)-by-\(|C_a|\) matrix whose columns are the \(b_i\) for \(i\in C_a\). Then \(C_s\) counts the nonempty residue classes with \(s_a=s\).

The theorem determines the abstract isomorphism type of \(B\), not its embedding, the number of distinct embedded copies, or the automorphism group of \(B\). It concerns a square-zero radical only; no formula is asserted for higher nilpotency length or unequal local factors.

## Proof

**1. Residue classes split the generated algebra.** Let \(B\) be generated by the \(X_j\), and reduce modulo the square-zero ideal \(\varepsilon A_{q,r}\). The image is the subalgebra of \(\mathbb F_q^r\) generated by the residue vectors. Polynomial functions on the finite set of distinct points in \(\mathbb F_q^\ell\) separate those points, so this image contains the indicator idempotent of each nonempty class \(C_a\). Idempotents lift across nilpotent ideals. Any idempotent in \(R_q^r\) has every coordinate equal to \(0\) or \(1\), because \(R_q\) is local; consequently the lifted idempotent is exactly the coordinate indicator \(e_a\) of \(C_a\). The algebra therefore decomposes as the product of its factors \(e_aB\).

**2. The factor is determined by one matrix rank.** Write \(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_\ell)\). In \(e_aB\), the generator \(X_j\) gives
\[
e_aX_j-a_je_a=\varepsilon(b_{ij})_{i\in C_a}.
\]
All products of these nilpotent vectors vanish, since \(\varepsilon^2=0\). Conversely, every polynomial in the generators within this factor is a scalar multiple of \(e_a\) plus a linear combination of those vectors. Their span has dimension \(s_a\). Hence
\[
e_aB\cong \mathbb F_q\oplus V_{s_a},\qquad V_{s_a}^2=0.
\]
The factors \(A_s\) are local and have pairwise distinct dimensions, so uniqueness of the decomposition of a finite-dimensional commutative algebra into local factors makes the multiplicities \(C_s\) an isomorphism invariant.

**3. Count a block of a fixed residue.** Fix a nonempty block of \(m\) labeled coordinates and a residue vector \(a\). Its tangent data form an \(\ell\)-by-\(m\) matrix. To count matrices of rank \(s\), first choose their image subspace in \({\ell\brack s}_q\) ways. For a fixed \(s\)-dimensional image, the corresponding linear map \(\mathbb F_q^m\to\mathbb F_q^s\) must be surjective; there are \(\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}(q^m-q^j)\) such maps. Thus the number is \(N_{\ell,m,s}(q)\).

**4. Form the exponential generating function.** There are \(q^\ell\) possible residue vectors, and coordinates sharing a residue vector form one block. For a fixed residue vector, the contribution is either \(1\) if its block is empty, or \(z_sN_{\ell,m,s}(q)t^m/m!\) for a nonempty block of size \(m\) and tangent rank \(s\). Summing over \(m,s\) gives \(1+\sum_s z_sF_{\ell,s}(t)\). The \(q^\ell\) residue vectors contribute independently, giving its \(q^\ell\)-th power. Exponential generating functions assign labels to the \(r\) coordinates, so multiplying the coefficient of \(t^r\) by \(r!\) counts labeled coordinate data. Each coordinate has \(q^{2\ell}\) possible residue/tangent data, hence there are \(q^{2\ell r}\) equally likely generator tuples. Removing the empty-coordinate constant term and dividing by this total proves both displayed formulas.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` uses only the Python 3 standard library. It compares direct enumeration of all residue/tangent assignments with exact rational coefficient extraction from the formula over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\), for four small parameter sets. It checks the total number \(q^{2\ell r}\) of tuples in each case. These finite tests are consistency checks only; the all-parameter result follows from the proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Sercombe and Shalev study probabilities that random elements generate an entire finite algebra, bounds on minimal generator counts, and related generation questions. First, Reichstein, and Salazar determine minimal generator counts for separable algebras, including products of finite fields. Those results motivate the random-generation setting but do not state the joint isomorphism-type distribution of the proper subalgebra generated inside a product of dual-number rings. The rank-zero block count reduces to the familiar residue-class partition mechanism for products of fields; the additional rank strata record the square-zero tangent directions.

No priority claim is made beyond the sources and comparisons listed here. A broader treatment of random subalgebra types in finite local algebras could imply or subsume this formula, and the present result does not settle that general problem.

## Limitations

The result is restricted to \(R_q^r\), where the nilpotent ideal has square zero and every residue field is \(\mathbb F_q\). It does not handle \(\mathbb F_q[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^d)\) for \(d>2\), mixed residue fields, noncommutative algebras, or classification of embedded generated subalgebras. The coefficient formula is exact but does not by itself give a simplified asymptotic law as \(r\) or \(\ell\) grows. Independent mathematical review has not been performed.
