# Exact unmarked colour-refinement depth on Cartesian powers of paths

## Finding

Let \(P_n\) be the loopless path on \(n\ge2\) vertices \(0,1,\ldots,n-1\), and let \(G_{n,d}=P_n^{\square d}\) be its \(d\)-fold Cartesian power for \(d\ge1\). Begin standard synchronous, counting, one-dimensional colour refinement with the uniform colouring \(C^0\). Define

\[
r=\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor,\qquad
\delta_i(x)=\min\{x_i,n-1-x_i\},\qquad
c_j(x)=|\{i:\delta_i(x)=j\}|\quad(0\le j\le r).
\]

**Theorem.** For each integer \(0\le t\le r\), the colours \(C^t(x)\) and \(C^t(y)\) coincide if and only if

\[
(c_0(x),\ldots,c_{t-1}(x))=(c_0(y),\ldots,c_{t-1}(y)).
\]

At \(t=0\), this means the empty tuple. The first stable round is **exactly** \(r\), and its classes are precisely the vertex-automorphism orbits. Hence the number of stable classes is

\[
\binom{d+r}{r}.
\]

For each nonnegative integer profile \((c_0,\ldots,c_r)\) with \(\sum_jc_j=d\), the associated class has exactly

\[
\frac{d!}{\prod_{j=0}^{r}c_j!}\prod_{j=0}^{r}a_j^{c_j},\qquad
a_j=\begin{cases}1,&j=r\text{ and }n\text{ odd},\\2,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

Thus the stabilization depth depends only on side length, not the dimension of the Cartesian power. For \(n=2\), the power is a transitive hypercube and the stable round is \(0\).

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite, simple, undirected, and loopless; \(n\ge2\) and \(d\ge1\) are arbitrary integers. Two vertices in the Cartesian power are adjacent exactly when they agree in all but one coordinate and differ by one in that coordinate. No vertices are marked. Counting refinement retains the previous colour: \(C^{t+1}(v)=(C^t(v),\{\!\{C^t(w):w\sim v\}\!\})\), with colours identified up to canonical renaming. A *strict refinement round* is one in which the partition becomes finer, so the first stable round has no further split on the next update.

## Proof

**1. Orbits.** A nontrivial Cartesian product of two connected graphs with edges contains a four-cycle. Consequently each path \(P_n\) is prime under Cartesian product. The Sabidussi–Vizing factorization and its automorphism consequence imply that automorphisms of \(P_n^{\square d}\) are generated by independent coordinate reflections and permutations of its \(d\) identical path factors. The reflection-orbit of coordinate \(x_i\) is exactly its nearest-end distance \(\delta_i(x)\). Hence two vertices are in one automorphism orbit exactly when their full histograms \((c_0,\ldots,c_r)\) agree. There are \(\binom{d+r}{r}\) such histograms. Each coordinate in a distance category \(j\) has \(a_j\) possible positions, which gives the stated multinomial orbit-size formula.

**2. Exact colours at all intermediate rounds.** The assertion at \(t=0\) is immediate. If \(n=2\), the graph is regular and vertex-transitive, so the theorem follows. Assume \(n\ge3\), hence \(r\ge1\). The degree of a vertex is \(2d-c_0\). Thus round one distinguishes exactly \(c_0\), proving the assertion for \(t=1\).

Inductively suppose \(1\le t<r\) and the round-\(t\) colour is exactly the truncated histogram \(H_t=(c_0,\ldots,c_{t-1})\). Every neighbour is obtained by a single coordinate move on a path. A coordinate at nearest-end distance \(j\) has one outward and one inward neighbour when \(0<j<r\); distance \(0\) has a single neighbour at distance \(1\). At the central distance \(r\) the two neighbours either both have distance \(r-1\) (odd \(n\)) or one has distance \(r-1\) and the other has distance \(r\) (even \(n\)).

Because \(t<r\), the multiset of neighbour \(H_t\)-profiles depends only on \(H_{t+1}=(c_0,\ldots,c_t)\): each of the \(c_j\) coordinates for \(j\le t\) gives an explicitly determined collection of changes to \(H_t\); every coordinate of distance above \(t\) contributes two neighbours with unchanged \(H_t\), except that the distance-\(t\) coordinates contribute one neighbour of a special changed profile. The number of coordinates of distance above \(t\) is \(d-\sum_{j\le t}c_j\), so no finer information is needed.

Conversely, among neighbours whose truncated histogram preserves \(c_0,\ldots,c_{t-2}\) and increases \(c_{t-1}\) by exactly one, there are **exactly \(c_t\)**: they are produced precisely by moving one coordinate from distance \(t\) to distance \(t-1\). (For \(t=1\), the condition on earlier entries is vacuous.) The retained old colour determines \(H_t\), and that particular neighbour-colour multiplicity determines \(c_t\). Hence round \(t+1\) distinguishes exactly \(H_{t+1}\). Induction reaches \(t=r\), when the omitted final count \(c_r=d-\sum_{j<r}c_j\) is determined as well, so this is already the automorphism-orbit partition and is stable.

**3. Sharpness.** For \(r\ge1\), choose a vertex whose every coordinate is in the central category \(r\), and a second vertex in which one coordinate is in category \(r-1\) and every other coordinate remains in category \(r\). Such vertices exist for every \(d\ge1\). Both have zero entries in every category below \(r-1\), so they share a colour at round \(r-1\), but their orbit profiles differ and they split at round \(r\). For \(r=0\), no strict refinement is needed. This proves the exact stabilization round in all cases. \(\square\)

## Relationship to prior work

Kersting–Mladenov–Garnett–Grohe (2014) analyze general colour refinement and report a square-grid benchmark with one million vertices, but do not give the general sharp depth or exact orbit histograms here. Arvind–Köbler–Rattan–Verbitsky (2015) define *refinable graphs* and establish relations between colour refinement, fractional automorphisms, and structural graph classes, without this Cartesian-power profile theorem. Boiko–Cuno–Imrich–Lehner–van de Woestijne (2014) supply the underlying Cartesian-prime factorization theory, not the refinement-depth result.

Related specific results include a known exact unmarked refinement theorem for two-dimensional rectangular grids and a known unmarked orbit-classification result for Cartesian products of stars. The present statement concerns all dimensions and gives every intermediate partition and a sharp dimension-independent bound. Neither the two-dimensional theorem nor the star-factor theorem covers all powers of paths of arbitrary size \(n\ge4\). Root-marked grid results use different initial colours and do not imply the unmarked claim. More general literature could cover this theorem under another formulation; no publication-priority or independence claim is made.

## Limitations

This theorem is about within-graph automorphism orbits, not identification of the graph against all nonisomorphic graphs. It does not address rectangular products with unequal path lengths, individually coloured vertices, coloured edges, graph isomorphism algorithms, or variants of colour refinement. The proof is all-parameter; the finite checks alone would not establish an infinite family. Earlier unindexed special or general product results remain a literature risk.
