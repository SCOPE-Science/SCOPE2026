# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is exact for each prime \(p\), exponent \(r\ge1\), and integer multiplier \(m\ge1\) satisfying \(p\nmid m\). Legendre's digit-sum identity yields the full binomial block valuation including both boundary types; summing two adjacent binomial valuations minus \(r\) gives three complete cases. The shell counts exhaust \(p^r-2\) interiors with correct multiplicities. Rational summation and the \(p\)-adic coupling prove the two global extensions. The standalone verifier confirms \(202\) independent exact rows; finite verification is corroboration, not a replacement for the proof. The proven domain excludes multiples \(m\) of \(p\).

## Originality — PASS, subject to literature risk

Bóna–Sagan's Theorem 1.2 is a prime-divisibility criterion, not a closed histogram for all prime-power dilations. Checcoli–D'Adderio's prime-power row discussion covers relevant special valuations when \(m=1\), not the full parameterized carry-mixture polynomial or a quantitative distributional limit. OEIS A001263 tabulates individual entries. Search comparisons against these implications, semantic research-index records and terminology variants did not locate the full joint result. A derivation buried in earlier Kummer-theoretic work or an unindexed generalization remains possible; a negative search is not proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result answers a naturally posed valuation-strengthening direction for the Narayana triangle: every row in a two-parameter prime-power-dilation family has an exactly enumerable spectrum, an explicit rational exponent generating function, and a quantitative universal geometric-tail limit. The multiplier-dependent carry mixture is genuine, as demonstrated by \(m=p+1\), rather than a routine restatement of the prime-power-only row. These exact distributions permit prime-divisibility depth questions for entire rows without enumerating exponentially many entries, while the general formula avoids proposing unsupported applications.
