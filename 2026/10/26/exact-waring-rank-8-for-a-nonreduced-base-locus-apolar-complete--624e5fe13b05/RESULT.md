# Exact Waring rank \(8\) for a nonreduced-base-locus apolar complete intersection

## Finding
Over \(\mathbb C\), the sextic
\[
F=6x^5z+y^6+15y^4z^2+15y^2z^4+z^6
\]
has complex symmetric Waring rank **exactly \(8\)**. Its annihilator under differentiation is the complete-intersection ideal
\[
F^\perp=(q_1,q_2,g)=(Y^2-Z^2,\,XY,\,X^5-Z^5).
\]
This closes the published numerical interval \(4<\operatorname{rk}(F)\le 8\) for this Macaulay dual.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over the algebraically closed field \(\mathbb C\); points are reduced distinct points of \(\mathbb P^2\). Waring rank is the smallest number of sixth powers of linear forms needed to express \(F\), with complex scalar coefficients. The apolarity lemma identifies it with the minimum cardinality of a reduced projective zero-dimensional set whose homogeneous ideal is contained in \(F^\perp\). Capital variables act by differentiation in the corresponding lower-case variables.

## Proof
**Annihilator.** Direct differentiations give \((\partial_y^2-\partial_z^2)F=0\), \(\partial_x\partial_yF=0\), and \((\partial_x^5-\partial_z^5)F=0\). The three displayed generators have no common projective zero and degrees \((2,2,5)\), hence generate an Artinian complete intersection with socle degree \(2+2+5-3=6\). Its one-dimensional degree-six socle dual is spanned by \(F\), so \(F^\perp=I\).

**An eight-point apolar set.** Set
\[
J_8=(XY,\;Y(Y^2-Z^2),\;X^6+Z^4(Y^2-Z^2)).
\]
The inclusion \(J_8\subseteq I\) follows from the first two generators and the explicit polynomial identity
\[
X^6+Z^4q_1=Xg+YZ^3q_2+(Z^4-XZ^3)q_1.
\]
The three generators form a homogeneous Gröbner basis for graded lexicographic order \(X>Y>Z\), with leading monomials \(XY,Y^3,X^6\). The quotient is free over \(\mathbb C[Z]\) on \(1,X,\ldots,X^5,Y,Y^2\), so the projective scheme has degree \(8\) and no component at infinity. In the chart \(Z=1\), it has exactly the eight simple points \([\zeta:0:1]\) with \(\zeta^6=1\), together with \([0:1:1]\) and \([0:-1:1]\); consequently \(J_8\) is their radical saturated ideal. Apolarity gives \(\operatorname{rk}(F)\le8\).

**Exclusion of seven or fewer.** Were there a reduced apolar set with fewer than seven points, adjoin arbitrary distinct points to obtain an apolar set \(S\) of exactly seven, with \(J=I(S)\subset I\). Put \(Q=(q_1,q_2)\). In every degree below \(5\), one has \(J_d\subset Q_d\). The projective scheme \(B=V(Q)\) has length four: two simple points \(P_\pm=[0:\pm1:1]\) and one doubled point \(P_0=[1:0:0]\), whose local ideal in the chart \(X=1\) is \((y,z^2)\). Note that \(g\ne0\) at each of these three supports.

Suppose first **no five points of \(S\) are collinear**. Fix \(p\in S\). Among the other six points, no four lie on one line through \(p\); therefore the six can be paired into three pairs, none of whose joining lines passes through \(p\). The product of these three lines is a cubic vanishing on \(S\setminus\{p}\}\), not at \(p\). Thus cubics interpolate arbitrary values at \(S\). Choose a linear form \(L\) nonzero at all points and cubic Lagrange separators \(h_p\). For each coordinate linear form \(V\), the quartic \((V-V(p)L/L(p))h_p\) vanishes at all seven points. Let \(K\) be the ideal generated by \(J_3\) and these quartics (and all lower-degree elements of \(J\)). Each degree-\(d\) polynomial, \(d\ge3\), reduces modulo \(K\) to a linear combination of \(L^{d-3}h_p\): write its monomials as products of degree-three and degree-\((d-3)\) forms, use the cubic interpolation basis modulo \(J_3\), and then the stated quartic relations. Evaluating shows that a polynomial vanishing on \(S\) has all the resulting coefficients zero. Hence \(J=K\) is generated in degrees at most four. Since \(J_d\subset Q_d\) for those degrees, \(J\subseteq Q\). This would force the nonreduced scheme \(B\) to be a closed subscheme of the reduced seven-point scheme \(S\). That is impossible: every closed subscheme of a finite reduced \(\mathbb C\)-scheme is reduced.

Now suppose **at least five points lie on a line \(L\)**. Six collinear points are impossible: two independent products of \(L\) with lines through the remaining point would lie in \(J_2\subseteq I_2=\langle q_1,q_2\rangle\), but \(q_1,q_2\) have no common linear factor. Seven collinear points are impossible because \(I_1=0\). Therefore exactly five lie on \(L\) and two points \(p,q\) lie off it. The unique conic through these seven is \(LL'\), where \(L'\) is the line joining \(p,q\), and \(LL'\in I_2\). The determinant of the ternary quadratic \(aXY+b(Y^2-Z^2)\) is a nonzero scalar multiple of \(a^2b\), so its only reducible members are \(q_1\) and \(q_2\). Let \(q'\) denote the other generator of the pencil. Every quartic through the five collinear points is divisible by \(L\), so \(J_4=L\,I(\{p,q}\})_3\). Because \(\gcd(L,q')=1\), \((LL',q'):L=(L',q')\), and \(J_4\subset Q_4\) implies \(I(\{p,q}\})_3\subset(L',q')_3\). Restrict to \(L'\cong\mathbb P^1\). The binary cubic subspace vanishing at \(p,q\) is generated by their squarefree binary quadratic times linear forms. Containment in the binary cubics divisible by \(q'|_{L'}\) forces their squarefree quadratic to be proportional to \(q'|_{L'}\) (both spaces have dimension two). Thus \(p,q\) must be the two distinct zeros of \(q'|_{L'}\), both lying off \(L\).

If \(LL'=q_1=(Y-Z)(Y+Z)\), one of the two zeros of \(q'=XY\) on \(L'\) is \(P_0=L\cap L'\), which is not off \(L\). If \(LL'=q_2=XY\) and \(L=X\), then \(q'|_{L'}=-Z^2\) is a double zero, not two distinct points. The sole remaining case is \(L=Y\), \(L'=X\), and \(\{p,q}\}=\{P_+,P_-}\}\).

Let \(P_5(X,Z)\) be the squarefree binary quintic vanishing at the five distinct points of \(S\cap\{Y=0}\}\). The quintic
\[
H=P_5(X,Z)-P_5(0,1)Y^2Z^3
\]
vanishes on all seven points, so \(H\in J_5\subset I_5\). Write \(H=U_3q_1+V_3q_2+c g\). Evaluating at \(P_+\), where \(q_1=q_2=0\), \(g=-1\), and \(H=0\), forces \(c=0\). Hence \(H\in Q_5\). Restricting to \(Y=0\) forces \(Z^2\mid P_5(X,Z)\), contradicting squarefreeness. This exhausts all configurations of seven distinct apolar points and proves the lower bound \(\operatorname{rk}(F)\ge8\).

Combining the bounds gives \(\boxed{\operatorname{rk}(F)=8}\).

## Verification
The standalone file `verify_rank8.py` uses exact integer polynomial arithmetic to verify the three apolar differential identities, the explicit upper-bound inclusion identity, and all three Buchberger \(S\)-pairs for the eight-point ideal. The geometric seven-point dichotomy above is the universal lower-bound proof; no finite computation is used in its place.

## Relationship to prior work
Bhat–Carlini–Masuti–Sebastian, *Waring rank of certain complete intersection forms* (arXiv:2610.18346v1, §6, Example 6.1), introduce this particular complete-intersection apolar ideal and establish only \(4<\operatorname{rk}(F)\le8\). Bhat–Sebastian, *On the variety of sum of powers of certain complete intersection forms* (arXiv:2610.21544v1, §4, Example 4.1), use the same ideal to establish that its minimal reduced apolar configurations are not complete intersections; they continue to state the interval rather than the exact value. The present lower-bound argument rules out both possible ranks six and seven (and smaller ranks); the upper bound is included for completeness. The argument is specific to the nonreduced four-point base-locus configuration, not a general theorem for all complete intersections.

## Limitations
The result is over \(\mathbb C\), using reduced distinct apolar points. It does not classify all rank-eight decompositions, calculate the variety of sums of powers, or address rank over \(\mathbb R\). Unindexed earlier derivations of this same exact invariant cannot be excluded by searchable literature alone.
