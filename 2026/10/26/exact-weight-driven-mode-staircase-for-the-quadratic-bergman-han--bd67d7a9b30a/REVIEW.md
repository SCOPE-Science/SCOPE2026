# Scientific assessment: quadratic Bergman Hankel norm staircase

## Correctness — PASS

The normalized moment identity \(m_n=n!\Gamma(\beta)/\Gamma(n+\beta)\) follows from the weighted area measure. Angular orthogonality makes the big Hankel operator's squared singular channels diagonal with \(s_n^2=m_{n+2}/m_n-\mathbf1_{\{n\ge2\}}m_n/m_{n-2}\). Direct algebra gives the closed rational formula. The adjacent-mode difference has the positive-denominator sign of \(\beta^2-(n+3)\beta-2n^2-3n+2\), decreasing in the integer mode. This proves every maximizer and crossing, not merely sampled cases. The comparison with the linear-symbol norm uses an explicitly nonnegative polynomial certificate; the asymptotic coefficient follows by uniform substitution at the maximizing degree. The standalone exact rational checker passed 29,904 test identities/comparisons; these tests do not replace the algebraic proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical-priority risk

Keshavarzi–Wu (arXiv:2210.06896) gives Schatten membership/mean-oscillation criteria, not a sharp quadratic-monomial norm as a function of weight. Bourass–El-Fallah–Marrhich–Naqos (arXiv:2102.01743) derives singular-value asymptotics, not the maximal channel staircase at every weight. Janson (1988) gives operator-ideal and boundedness criteria. Relevant public mathematical-result summaries concern harmonic Bergman circle traces or related weighted operators, neither of which implies the quadratic Hankel threshold. The original Arazy–Fisher–Peetre (1988) full text was not accessible for a decisive comparison; its possible exact-norm material remains a priority risk. An indexed-search absence alone is not novelty proof.

## Value — PASS

The natural operator norm problem is resolved for an entire continuum of Bergman weights, identifying infinitely many sharp mode switches and two-dimensional threshold eigenspaces. The linear-versus-quadratic norm reversal gives a distinct structural consequence that is not supplied by compactness or Schatten membership. This is a complete family classification, not a single chosen finite example.
