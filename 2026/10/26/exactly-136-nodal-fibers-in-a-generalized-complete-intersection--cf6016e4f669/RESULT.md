# Exactly 136 nodal fibers in a generalized-complete-intersection K3 fibration

## Finding
For a general complex generalized-complete-intersection Calabi–Yau threefold \(X\) of configuration \([\mathbb P^1\mid 3,-1;\ \mathbb P^4\mid 2,3]\), the projection \(\pi:X\to\mathbb P^1\) is a Lefschetz K3 fibration: there are exactly \(136\) singular fibers, all over distinct base points, and each is an irreducible quadric–cubic complete-intersection surface with precisely one ordinary double point. In particular the reduced discriminant on \(\mathbb P^1\) has degree \(136\), and each local monodromy on the smooth fiber's second cohomology is an ordinary Picard–Lefschetz reflection.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbb C\). Choose four general quadratic homogeneous forms \(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3\in H^0(\mathbb P^4,\mathcal O(2))\) and three linearly independent linear forms \(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\in H^0(\mathbb P^4,\mathcal O(1))\). On \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^4\), write \(P=u^3f_0+u^2vf_1+uv^2f_2+v^3f_3\). Along \(P=0\), define the three quadrics \(q_1,q_2,q_3\) uniquely by the exact sequence \(0\to\mathcal O(-3)\xrightarrow{(u^3,u^2v,uv^2,v^3)}\mathcal O^{\oplus4}\to\mathcal O(1)^{\oplus3}\to0\), equivalently by solving the rank-three matrix equation
\[
\begin{pmatrix}-v&0&0\\u&-v&0\\0&u&-v\\0&0&u\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}q_1\\q_2\\q_3\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}f_0\\f_1\\f_2\\f_3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The second equation of \(X\) is \(C=\ell_1q_1+\ell_2q_2+\ell_3q_3=0\). The equations describe a threefold, and \(\pi\) is the first projection. “General” means outside a proper Zariski-closed locus of coefficient space; the results do not claim that every special coefficient choice is Lefschetz.

## Proof
**1. Freedom of the fiber equations.** Fix \([u:v]\). The four-dimensional vector space of coefficient quadrics maps invertibly to the four quadrics \((Q,q_1,q_2,q_3)\), where \(Q=P(u,v,-)\), by the displayed exact sequence and a choice of splitting. Thus at every fixed base point the quadric \(Q\) and the three auxiliary quadrics can be varied independently. The fiber is \(\{Q=C=0\}\subset\mathbb P^4\), a degree-six polarized K3 surface whenever smooth. Let \(L\subset\mathbb P^4\) be the line \(\ell_1=\ell_2=\ell_3=0\).

**2. Exclusion of special-point singularities.** The incidence \(Q(x)=dQ(x)=0\) for \(([u:v],x)\in\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^4\) has codimension five in the quadric coefficients. Adding \(C(x)=0\) away from \(L\) has codimension at least one, whereas the test space has dimension five. Along \(L\), the test space has dimension two, so the five quadric conditions suffice. Hence all points of all fibers have \(dQ\ne0\) for general coefficients. At points of \(L\) where \(Q=0\), the first derivative of \(C\) is \(\sum_i q_i(x)d\ell_i\). If \(dQ\) is not normal to \(L\), singularity of \(\{Q=C=0\}\) forces three independent conditions \(q_i(x)=0\). If \(dQ\) is normal to \(L\), that condition on \(Q\) plus proportionality of \((q_1(x),q_2(x),q_3(x))\) to the three normal coefficients of \(dQ\) again imposes at least three conditions beyond \(Q(x)=0\). Either way, the incidence dimension is strictly smaller than the parameter space after varying the two-dimensional test space \(\mathbb P^1\times L\). Thus no singular fiber point belongs to \(L\).

**3. Isolated ordinary double points.** At a point \(x\notin L\), some \(\ell_j(x)\ne0\). On the smooth quadric \(Q=0\), varying \(q_j\) prescribes an arbitrary first jet of \(C\) at \(x\), since \(\ell_j\) is a local unit and global quadrics separate first jets. The condition that \(C\) and all three derivatives tangent to \(Q\) vanish gives four independent linear equations, in addition to \(Q(x)=0\). Since \(\dim(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^4)=5\), general coefficient choices yield only isolated critical points. At any such point, varying \(q_j\) by quadrics vanishing to second order there realizes all six second-order tangent coefficients of \(C\) on \(Q\). Therefore the singular Hessian locus imposes another proper equation, and all critical points are ordinary nodes. Generic smoothness of the full threefold follows from the same jet transversality: the fiber singularity is smoothed to first order by the base parameter, because a singular total space would impose one further independent condition. The fiber remains a connected, reduced complete intersection; with isolated singularities it is irreducible (distinct surface components would meet along a curve).

**4. No two nodes above one base point.** Consider \(t\in\mathbb P^1\) and distinct \(x,x'\in\mathbb P^4\). The two equations \(Q(x)=Q(x')=0\) are independent. When the nonzero vectors \((\ell_i(x))\) and \((\ell_i(x'))\) are independent, one can choose linear combinations of the \(q_i\) that contribute independently to arbitrary first jets of \(C\) at the two points. Indeed, global quadrics realize arbitrary first jets at either point, and a quadratic vanishing to first order at one prescribed point can be used to separate those jets. There are eight independent \(C\)-jet conditions, so together with the two \(Q\)-value conditions the incidence has codimension ten, exceeding the dimension nine of \((t,x,x')\). In the exceptional case where the two \(\ell\)-vectors are proportional and nonzero, proportionality imposes two independent conditions on the point-pair. Global quadrics on \(\mathbb P^4\) realize a nine-dimensional space of the ten first-jet coordinates at two distinct points (the single shared coefficient is the coefficient of the product of the two distinguished homogeneous coordinates). Restricting to the two smooth quadric tangent spaces removes at most two coordinates, so at least seven independent \(C\)-jet conditions remain. The total codimension is at least \(2+2+7=11\), again exceeding nine. Thus no two singularities occur in one fiber. Reducedness of the discriminant follows from the ordinary nondegenerate smoothing of each node.

**5. Exact count.** For a smooth Calabi–Yau threefold, \(e(X)=2(h^{1,1}(X)-h^{2,1}(X))\). The Hodge numbers \((2,46)\) calculated for this family give \(e(X)=-88\). A smooth K3 fiber has Euler characteristic \(24\), whereas a K3 surface with one ordinary double point has Euler characteristic \(23\). Stratified additivity over \(\mathbb P^1\) therefore gives \(-88=48-N\), so \(N=136\). A check independent of the reported Hodge numbers uses the birational smooth flop phase \(X_-\subset\mathbb P^2\times\mathbb P^4\), the zero locus of \((\mathcal Q\boxtimes\mathcal O(2))\oplus\mathcal O(1,1)\), where \(c(\mathcal Q)=1+2h+3h^2\). With \(c_3(\mathcal E)=7h^2H+8hH^2+4H^3\) and \(c_3(T_{\mathbb P^2\times\mathbb P^4}-\mathcal E)=3h^2H-9hH^2-4H^3\), extracting the coefficient of \(h^2H^4\) in their product gives \(-88\). This matches the Hodge computation. Standard Picard–Lefschetz theory supplies the final local-monodromy assertion.

## Verification
The standalone \(verify.py\) performs exact truncated polynomial arithmetic to independently check the Chern-class Euler value \(-88\), the resulting fiber count \(136\), and the dimension-nine first-jet evaluation matrix for quadrics at two distinct points. It does not substitute finite arithmetic for the generic incidence argument or prove the global genericity claims by sampling. All geometric and transversality statements are established by the dimension arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work
Kanazawa's 2026 paper introduces the specific generalized complete-intersection Calabi–Yau threefold, the Hodge numbers, its toric variation-of-GIT flop and related fibrations. Garbagnati–van Geemen's earlier paper explains generalized-complete-intersection equations and the corresponding K3-fiber framework. The new assertion here is the full generic Lefschetz profile of the \(\mathbb P^1\)-projection, particularly the reduced degree-\(136\) discriminant, isolated one-node fibers and corresponding local monodromies; it does not claim new Hodge numbers, the original Calabi–Yau construction, or the standard Picard–Lefschetz theorem.

## Limitations
Special nongeneral members can have more complicated or coincident singular fibers. This proves neither a global monodromy-group identification nor arithmetic point-counting predictions. While the cited papers were compared for equivalent statements, the possibility of a less obvious prior proof of the precise Lefschetz-fiber count is a historical-priority risk.
