# Exactly 136 nodal fibers in a generalized-complete-intersection K3 fibration

## Finding

For a general complex generalized-complete-intersection Calabi–Yau threefold \(X\) of configuration \([\mathbb P^1\mid 3,-1;\ \mathbb P^4\mid 2,3]\), the projection \(\pi:X\to\mathbb P^1\) is a Lefschetz K3 fibration. There are exactly \(136\) singular fibers over distinct base points. Each is an irreducible quadric–cubic complete-intersection surface with precisely one ordinary double point. The reduced discriminant has degree \(136\), and each local monodromy on the smooth fiber's second cohomology is a Picard–Lefschetz reflection.

## Assumptions and scope

Work over \(\mathbb C\). Fix linearly independent linear forms \(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\) on \(\mathbb P^4\); their common zero set is a line \(L\). Let \(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3\) be general quadrics on \(\mathbb P^4\), and put \(P=u^3f_0+u^2vf_1+uv^2f_2+v^3f_3\). Set \(Y=\{P=0\}\subset\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^4\). The matrix
\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}-v&0&0\\u&-v&0\\0&u&-v\\0&0&u\end{pmatrix}
\]
is the first map in the exact sequence \(0\to\mathcal O(-1)^3\xrightarrow{M}\mathcal O^4\xrightarrow{(u^3,u^2v,uv^2,v^3)}\mathcal O(3)\to0\) on \(\mathbb P^1\). On \(Y\) the column of the \(f_i\) lies in the kernel of the last map, after twisting by \(\mathcal O(0,2)\) and restricting. There are therefore unique components \(q_j\) of bidegree \((-1,2)\) on \(Y\) with \(M(q_1,q_2,q_3)^{\mathsf T}=(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3)^{\mathsf T}\). Then \(X\subset Y\) is the zero locus of the section \(C=\sum_j\ell_jq_j\) of \(\mathcal O_Y(-1,3)\). “General” means outside a proper Zariski-closed subset of coefficients.

## Proof

**1. Fiber equations and the smooth quadric locus.** At a fixed base point \(t\), choose a local splitting of the displayed sequence. In this splitting the four coefficient quadrics give four independent quadrics \((Q,q_1,q_2,q_3)\), with \(Q=P(t,-)\), and the fiber is \(\{Q=C=0\}\) in \(\mathbb P^4\). Changing the splitting changes the cubic extension from \(Q=0\) only by a multiple of \(Q\). For fixed \((t,x)\), the condition that the value and the full projective first derivative of \(Q\) vanish at \(x\) imposes five independent linear conditions. Off \(L\), the extra equation \(C(x)=0\) is independent, giving codimension six over the five-dimensional space of \((t,x)\). On \(L\), that space has dimension two, so the five quadric conditions suffice. Thus for general coefficients \(d_xQ\ne0\) on every fiber of \(X\).

**2. No fiber singularities on the fixed line.** On \(L\), \(d_xC=\sum_j q_j(x)d_x\ell_j\) is an arbitrary conormal covector for fixed \((t,x)\), obtained by varying the three values \(q_j(x)\). If \(d_xQ\) is not conormal, dependence of \(d_xQ,d_xC\) requires all three values to vanish. If \(d_xQ\ne0\) is conormal, this imposes one additional restriction on the first jet of \(Q\), and dependence then imposes two restrictions on the three \(q_j(x)\). Together with \(Q(x)=0\) this is codimension at least four over the two-dimensional test space \(\mathbb P^1\times L\). No such fiber point occurs for general coefficients.

**3. Isolated nodes and smooth total space.** Off \(L\) choose a nonvanishing \(\ell_j(x)\). For \(Q(x)=0\), \(d_xQ\ne0\), varying \(q_j\) gives any first jet of \(C|_{Q=0}\) at \(x\): multiply by a local unit and restrict the arbitrary ambient first jet of a quadric to the smooth quadric tangent space. Vanishing of this jet imposes four independent conditions beyond \(Q(x)=0\). The critical-point incidence therefore has dimension at most the dimension of coefficient space, so general coefficients have at most finitely many critical points. Keeping the first jet fixed, quadrics whose value and first derivatives at \(x\) are zero realize arbitrary quadratic terms on the quadric tangent space. Indeed, in homogeneous coordinates with \(x=[1:0:0:0:0]\), monomials in the four other coordinates give all ambient quadratic terms, from which one restricts to the tangent space; multiplication by \(\ell_j\) multiplies this quadratic term by a nonzero scalar. Degeneracy of the resulting three-variable Hessian thus imposes an additional condition. Its incidence is not dominant, and every critical point of a general member is an ordinary double point of its fiber.

For smoothness of the total threefold, use the universal incidence of the two value equations off \(L\). These equations are independent linear conditions on the quadrics at every \((t,x)\) off \(L\) by the independent variation in step 1. In local splittings they define a vector subbundle of the trivial coefficient bundle over \(\mathbb P^1\times(\mathbb P^4\setminus L)\); changing the cubic lift by a multiple of \(Q\) preserves the subbundle. This universal incidence is smooth. Generic smoothness over the coefficient space in characteristic zero gives a smooth total threefold off \(L\) for general coefficients. Along \(L\) it follows from the independence of the vertical derivatives in step 2. At a nodal fiber point, the nonzero total derivative after restricting to \(P=0\) is therefore in the base direction. The holomorphic Morse lemma gives a Lefschetz critical point.

**4. Distinct critical values.** Consider \((t,x,x')\) with \(x\ne x'\), both off \(L\), and both on the smooth locus of \(Q=0\); the space of test triples before imposing \(Q(x)=Q(x')=0\) has dimension nine. The two quadric values are independent. If the nonzero vectors \(\ell(x)=(\ell_1(x),\ell_2(x),\ell_3(x))\) and \(\ell(x')\) are independent, choose constants \(a_j\) such that \(\ell\cdot a\) is nonzero at \(x\) and zero at \(x'\). Vary the cubic by \((\ell\cdot a)q\), where \(q\) is a quadric with \(q(x')=0\). The cubic first jet at \(x'\) then vanishes. The value condition \(q(x')=0\) does not obstruct arbitrary first jets at \(x\): the square of a linear form zero at \(x\) but not at \(x'\) adjusts the value at \(x'\) without altering the first jet at \(x\). Reverse the two points to obtain independent jets at both. Vanishing of the two first jets restricted to \(Q=0\) gives eight independent conditions, for total codimension ten over a space of dimension nine.

If the two evaluation vectors are proportional, the locus of such point pairs has codimension two, since the linear projection \(\mathbb P^4\setminus L\to\mathbb P^2\) has fibers of dimension two. Some \(\ell_j\) is nonzero at both points. Varying \(q_j\) gives nine independent coordinates in the space of the two ambient first jets of \(C\): the two ambient quadric jets have rank nine, since, with the two points as coordinate points, the one shared coefficient is that of the product of their corresponding homogeneous coordinates, and multiplication by \(\ell_j\) acts invertibly on the jet spaces. Restriction to the two quadric tangent spaces loses at most two dimensions, so the two vanishing tangent jets give at least seven conditions. Together with the two independent quadric values this exceeds the dimension seven of the restricted test space. The incidence of two singularities in one fiber is therefore not dominant in either case. The one-parameter Morse smoothing at each critical point gives a simple local discriminant point.

**5. Fiber type and count.** The projective intersection is nonempty. Every fiber is a codimension-two complete intersection: a common hypersurface component would give a positive-dimensional critical locus. Such a complete intersection is connected and Cohen–Macaulay. Its only possible singularity is an isolated node, so it is regular in codimension one, hence normal by Serre's criterion and irreducible by connectedness. A smooth fiber is K3 by adjunction and the vanishing of its first structure-sheaf cohomology from the complete-intersection resolution. It has degree six.

For a direct Euler calculation, let \(a,b\) denote the two ambient hyperplane classes, so \(a^2=b^5=0\) and \(\int a b^4=1\). The hypersurface \(Y\) is smooth for general \(P\) by Bertini for the full \((3,2)\) system. The successive divisor classes are \(D_1=3a+2b\), \(D_2=-a+3b\), with the latter taken as a class restricted to \(Y\). Adjunction gives \(c_1(TX)=0\), and the two normal exact sequences give
\[
 c(TX)=\left.\frac{(1+a)^2(1+b)^5}{(1+D_1)(1+D_2)}\right|_X,
 \qquad [X]=D_1D_2=7ab+6b^2.
\]
Since \(c_1(TX)=0\), the third Newton sum is \(3c_3(TX)\); here it is \(2a^3+5b^3-D_1^3-D_2^3\). Hence \(c_3(TX)=-3ab^2-10b^3\) on \(X\), and integration gives \(e(X)=-88\). A smooth fiber has Euler characteristic \(24\); a fiber with one ordinary double point has Euler characteristic \(23\), since smoothing replaces a point by a Milnor fiber with the homotopy type of a two-sphere. With \(N\) singular fibers, Euler additivity in the proper stratified fibration gives \(-88=24(2-N)+23N\), or \(N=136\). Picard–Lefschetz theory gives the local reflection, in the vanishing class of square \(-2\), on the smooth fiber's second cohomology.

## Verification

The companion `verify.py` checks the Euler value by truncated Chern-class arithmetic in the alternative flop model, the conditional count \(136\), and the rank-nine evaluation map of ambient quadratic first jets at two points. The direct Chern calculation above also gives the Euler value without the flop model or the reported Hodge numbers. Neither finite check substitutes for the incidence and smoothness arguments establishing the generic singularity profile.

## Relationship to prior work

Anderson–Apruzzi–Gao–Gray–Lee gave this configuration and its Hodge numbers \((h^{1,1},h^{2,1})=(2,46)\) [1]. Garbagnati–van Geemen described the equations and the projection with quadric–cubic K3 fibers [2]. Kanazawa discusses this previously studied threefold and its VGIT flop and mirror construction [3]. For broader gCICY and K3-fibration context see [4]. The claim here is the generic Lefschetz profile of this specific \(\mathbb P^1\)-projection. The Euler characteristic gives the numerical count once the one-node and distinct-critical-value assertions have been proved; it does not by itself give them. The Calabi–Yau configuration, its Hodge numbers, the K3 fibration, and the general Picard–Lefschetz theorem are not claimed as new.

## Limitations

Special coefficient choices can have worse singularities or coincident critical values. No global monodromy-group identification or arithmetic point-counting result is claimed. The historical comparison does not exclude unindexed or differently formulated proofs of this profile.
