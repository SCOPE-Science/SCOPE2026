# Extension-field zeta profiles of three degree-two singular del Pezzo surfaces

## Finding

Let \(X_{A_1}\), \(X_{3A_1}\), and \(X_{D_4}\) be the three degree-two singular del Pezzo surfaces over \(\mathbf F_2\) displayed by Uppal in Lemmas 9.16–9.18 of *Singular del Pezzo surfaces over finite fields*. Their geometric singularity types are respectively \(A_1\), \(3A_1\), and \(D_4\), and all their singular points are \(\mathbf F_2\)-rational. Write \(U_X=X\setminus\operatorname{Sing}(X)\), and put \(q=2^m\) for \(m\geq1\). Define the integer-valued periodic term

\[
\epsilon_m=2\cos(\pi m/3),
\]

whose values for \(m\equiv1,2,3,4,5,0\pmod6\) are respectively \(1,-1,-2,-1,1,2\). Then the exact smooth-locus point counts are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\#U_{A_1}(\mathbf F_q)&=q^2+q+1+q\bigl(4(-1)^m+\epsilon_m\bigr)-1,\\
\#U_{3A_1}(\mathbf F_q)&=q^2+q+1+q\bigl(1+3(-1)^m\bigr)-3,\\
\#U_{D_4}(\mathbf F_q)&=q^2+q+1+3q(-1)^m-1.
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular, all three surfaces have no smooth \(\mathbf F_2\)-point, while their smooth-locus counts over \(\mathbf F_4\) are respectively \(32\), \(34\), and \(32\). Thus the least extension degree \(m\geq1\) for which each surface has a smooth \(\mathbf F_{2^m}\)-point is exactly \(m=2\).

The corresponding zeta functions, with \(Z(X,T)=\exp\bigl(\sum_{m\geq1}\#X(\mathbf F_{2^m})T^m/m\bigr)\), are

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z(X_{A_1},T)^{-1}&=(1-T)(1-4T)(1-2T)(1+2T)^4(1-2T+4T^2),\\
Z(X_{3A_1},T)^{-1}&=(1-T)(1-4T)(1-2T)^2(1+2T)^3,\\
Z(X_{D_4},T)^{-1}&=(1-T)(1-4T)(1-2T)(1+2T)^3.
\end{aligned}
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The surfaces are exactly the weighted-projective hypersurfaces in \(\mathbf P(1,1,1,2)_{\mathbf F_2}\) specified in [1, Lemmas 9.16–9.18]:

\[
\begin{aligned}
X_{A_1}:\quad&w^2+w(y^2+yz+z^2)+x^2yz+xyz^2+y^4+y^2z^2+z^4=0,\\
X_{3A_1}:\quad&w^2+wx^2+x^4+x^2yz+xy^2z+xyz^2+y^2z^2=0,\\
X_{D_4}:\quad&w^2+w(y^2+yz+z^2)+xy^2z+xyz^2+y^4+y^2z^2+z^4=0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(x,y,z\) have weight one and \(w\) has weight two. The count is for rational points of the scheme-theoretic smooth locus, not for abstract isomorphism classes, resolutions, or points on a different model. The stated geometric singularity types are taken from the cited source; the point enumeration below independently verifies the finite-field counts but does not replace the source's geometric resolution arguments.

## Proof

**1. Point-count formula and finite data.** Uppal's Theorem 3.3 gives, for a degree-two singular del Pezzo surface with rational double points,

\[
\#X(\mathbf F_q)=q^2+q+1+q\,t_m,
\]

where \(t_m\) is the trace of the \(m\)-th Frobenius power on the \(E_7\) root-lattice representation after subtracting the root sublattice generated by the exceptional \((-2)\)-curves. The three singular sets have cardinalities \(1,3,1\), respectively, and all their geometric points are rational over \(\mathbf F_2\). Hence the smooth-locus count is the total count minus \(1,3,1\).

For each \(m\), represent the points of \(\mathbf P^2(\mathbf F_q)\) by the disjoint normalized charts \((1,y,z)\), \((0,1,z)\), and \((0,0,1)\), and let \(w\) range over \(\mathbf F_q\). This gives every point of each weighted-projective hypersurface exactly once: the remaining weighted-projective point \([0:0:0:1]\) is not on any equation. Evaluating each equation and its four partial derivatives gives the following exhaustive totals for \(m=1,\ldots,6\):

| Surface | \(\#X(\mathbf F_2)\) | \(\#X(\mathbf F_4)\) | \(\#X(\mathbf F_8)\) | \(\#X(\mathbf F_{16})\) | \(\#X(\mathbf F_{32})\) | \(\#X(\mathbf F_{64})\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(A_1\) | 1 | 33 | 25 | 321 | 961 | 4545 |
| \(3A_1\) | 3 | 37 | 57 | 337 | 993 | 4417 |
| \(D_4\) | 1 | 33 | 49 | 321 | 961 | 4353 |

Subtracting \(q^2+q+1\) and dividing by \(q\) gives the trace sequences

\[
\begin{aligned}
(t_1,\ldots,t_6)_{A_1}&=(-3,3,-6,3,-3,6),\\
(t_1,\ldots,t_6)_{3A_1}&=(-2,4,-2,4,-2,4),\\
(t_1,\ldots,t_6)_{D_4}&=(-3,3,-3,3,-3,3).
\end{aligned}
\]

**2. Recover the Frobenius polynomials.** The quotient trace representation has ranks \(6,4,3\) for the three types, respectively: the \(E_7\) rank is seven, and the root ranks are one, three, and four. Newton's identities applied to the first \(r\) traces of a rank-\(r\) operator determine its characteristic polynomial. The coefficients obtained from the displayed exact traces are

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{A_1}(s)&=(s+1)^4(s^2-s+1),\\
P_{3A_1}(s)&=(s+1)^3(s-1),\\
P_{D_4}(s)&=(s+1)^3.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the trace of the \(m\)-th Frobenius power is, respectively,

\[
t_{A_1}(m)=4(-1)^m+\epsilon_m,\qquad
t_{3A_1}(m)=1+3(-1)^m,\qquad
t_{D_4}(m)=3(-1)^m.
\]

These identities hold for every positive integer \(m\), since they are the power traces of the displayed characteristic polynomials, not extrapolations from a finite point-count table. Substituting them into the point-count formula and subtracting the fixed singular-point counts proves the three smooth-locus formulas.

**3. Zeta functions.** The zeta-function formula for singular del Pezzo anticanonical models in Blache–Hallouin [2, Theorem 1] contributes the factors \((1-T)(1-4T)\) and the determinant on the geometric Picard group. The canonical class is fixed by Frobenius, contributing an additional factor \((1-2T)\); the remaining characteristic factors are those of \(P_{A_1},P_{3A_1},P_{D_4}\). Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
Z(X_{A_1},T)^{-1}&=(1-T)(1-4T)(1-2T)(1+2T)^4(1-2T+4T^2),\\
Z(X_{3A_1},T)^{-1}&=(1-T)(1-4T)(1-2T)^2(1+2T)^3,\\
Z(X_{D_4},T)^{-1}&=(1-T)(1-4T)(1-2T)(1+2T)^3.
\end{aligned}
\]

The least-extension statement follows because the formulas give zero smooth points for \(m=1\) and positive counts \(32,34,32\) for \(m=2\).

## Verification

Run `python3 code/verify_point_counts.py` and `python3 code/verify_f4_independent.py` from the extracted package directory. The first program exhaustively enumerates the normalized weighted-projective representatives over \(\mathbf F_{2^m}\), for \(1\leq m\leq6\), evaluates the equations and Jacobian gradients, reproduces all three total and smooth-point sequences, computes the traces, and checks the Newton-identity characteristic polynomials. The second program uses a separate explicit multiplication table for \(\mathbf F_4\) and directly recomputes the \(33/32\), \(37/34\), and \(33/32\) total/smooth counts.

The recorded output ends with `ALL_EXHAUSTIVE_CHECKS_PASS` and `INDEPENDENT_F4_CHECK_PASS`. The exhaustive computations establish the finite trace inputs. The all-extension formulas additionally use the cited Frobenius trace theorem and Newton identities; they are not inferred from a finite sample alone.

## Relationship to prior work

Uppal [1] constructs the three degree-two counterexamples and proves that each has no smooth \(\mathbf F_2\)-point. The paper does not tabulate their extension-field point-count sequences, the three quotient Frobenius characteristic polynomials, or the resulting explicit zeta functions. Blache and Hallouin [2] give a general zeta-function formula and classify arithmetic types for singular del Pezzo surfaces of degrees three through six; their type tables do not give these degree-two examples. Tamanoi [3] studies unirationality and field-size thresholds for degree-two rational-double-point del Pezzo surfaces, not these exact extension-field counts. The current statement is a concrete arithmetic refinement for the three named examples, not a new general point-count theorem for all degree-two surfaces.

## Limitations

The result covers only the three explicit surfaces listed above; it does not classify all degree-two singular del Pezzo surfaces over finite fields, all possible Frobenius actions, or all geometric singularity types. The characteristic polynomials are recovered from exact counts in ranks six, four, and three, using the cited point-count theorem. The finite enumeration alone would not prove the extension-uniform formulas. Targeted literature-record searches found no exact matching profile, but search absence is not a proof of global priority. No independent external audit has been performed.
