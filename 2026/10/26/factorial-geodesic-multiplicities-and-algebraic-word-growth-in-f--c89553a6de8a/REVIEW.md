# Scientific review: factorial geodesic multiplicities and algebraic word growth

## Correctness — PASS

For every finite-rank free inverse monoid, Munn-tree cut parity proves the minimum length \(2|E(T)|-d_T(s,t)\). Equality forces ordered depth-first off-trunk excursions and one-way trunk passage, giving a bijection with the vertex factorial product. Endpoints at eccentricity or diameter give Green-class counts, with inversion handling \(\mathcal L\). The formal subtree equation with falling factorials then gives the algebraic series. The standalone program enumerates every word of length at most eight in rank two, testing 87,381 words and comparing shortest counts in all four Green types; an independent formal-series calculation agrees through degree eight, with additional independent word-count checks in ranks one and three. The proof, not the finite test, establishes the unrestricted claim.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The closest primary paper is Bodart, Carvalho and Monteiro, arXiv:2609.35251 (2026-09-28), on Green languages and their complexity. Kambites, Nyberg-Brodda, Szakács and Webb, arXiv:2407.10489, give exact minimum lengths and count distinct Munn-tree *elements* using Fuss–Catalan numbers, not all geodesic *words*. Existing accessible mathematical claims on deterministic logspace Green-class minima do not determine their attaining-word multiplicities. Normal-form work selects representatives. The paper *Counting word-trees* (1974) was available as metadata and abstract only; full text could contain related old counting ideas. Similar formulas could exist in unindexed literature or in uninspected material; the negative searches are not a proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a natural quantitative refinement of recent geodesic-language work, determining shortest-representative ambiguity for individual elements and each Green class, then a genuinely different growth series counting words rather than the known series counting elements. Algebraicity follows uniformly in rank; no general explicit asymptotic or other generating-set claim is made.

## Closest literature and scientific limitations

The proof depends on established Munn-tree representations and their shortest-length criterion. It applies only to standard symmetric generators and finite-rank **free** inverse monoids. Numerical checks stop at length eight in rank two. Historical enumeration and incomplete full-text access impose residual originality risks; external validation remains outstanding.
