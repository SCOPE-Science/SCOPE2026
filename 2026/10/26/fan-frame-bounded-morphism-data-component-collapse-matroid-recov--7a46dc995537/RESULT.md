# Fan-frame bounded-morphism data: component collapse, matroid recovery, and sharp thresholds

## Finding
Let \(G=(V,E)\) be any nonempty finite simple graph, with \(v=|V|\), \(e=|E|\), and \(c=c(G)\) connected components (isolated vertices count). Define the **edge-below-vertices incidence poset** \(Q_G=V\sqcup E\), where \(e_0<x\) exactly if \(x\) is an endpoint of edge \(e_0\). The direction of this incidence order matters. Equip all posets with reflexive accessibility. For \(m\ge2\), let \(F_m\) be the height-two *fan*: a least world \(\bot\) with exactly \(m\) incomparable maximal worlds \(1,\ldots,m\). Count all bounded morphisms \(Q_G\to F_m\), **without** a surjectivity requirement, and with every point of the source and target distinguished (no quotient by automorphisms).

**Theorem (complete scalar and edge-marked information).** The total number \(b_m(G)\) of such morphisms is
\[
 b_2(G)=2^v,\qquad b_m(G)=m^{c}\quad(m\ge3).
\]
Thus the entire infinite scalar fan spectrum \((b_m(G))_{m\ge2}\) determines *exactly* the pair \((v,c(G))\), and no other graph structure. For \(m=2\), mark each edge-world sent to \(\bot\) by its own commuting variable \(z_e\) and form
\[
 B_G(\mathbf z)=\sum_{f:Q_G\to F_2\ \mathrm{bounded}}\ \prod_{e\in E:\ f(e)=\bot}z_e.
\]
Then, with \(\mathcal B(G)=\{\delta(S):S\subseteq V\}\) the binary cut space,
\[
 \boxed{B_G(\mathbf z)=2^{c(G)}\sum_{D\in\mathcal B(G)}\prod_{e\in D}z_e.}
\]
The edge-labelled polynomial determines the binary cut space and its orthogonal complement, the binary cycle space. In particular, for graphs having the same number of vertices, two such polynomials agree after a bijective relabelling of edges **if and only if** the corresponding cycle matroids are isomorphic. Without fixing the vertex count, equality requires both the component counts and the labelled cut spaces to agree.

**Sharp reconstruction thresholds within graph-incidence posets.** Among nonisomorphic \(Q_G\) and \(Q_H\) with the same number of worlds, the first collision of all scalar fan counts is at **six worlds**: take \(G=2K_2\) and \(H=P_3\sqcup K_1\). Both have \(v=4,e=2,c=2\) and thus \(b_2=16\), \(b_m=m^2\) for every \(m\ge3\). Their edge-marked polynomials coincide as well, namely \(4(1+z_1)(1+z_2)\). Among pairs agreeing on all scalar counts but separated by the edge-marked polynomial, the first examples occur at **eight worlds**: \(C_4\) versus the triangle with one pendant edge. Both have \(v=e=4,c=1\), but the former has a four-element graphic circuit and the latter a three-element circuit. Exhaustive classification of the graphs having \(|V|+|E|\le8\), together with the formula above, proves both thresholds. The first *connected* scalar collision occurs at seven worlds, between \(P_4\) and \(K_{1,3}\); their marked polynomials still agree. Indeed every forest satisfies \(B_G=2^c\prod_{e\in E}(1+z_e)\), so even the complete marked data cannot recover tree branching.

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, simple, and nonempty in vertices; isolated vertices are allowed. The incidence poset places edge-elements strictly **below** their two endpoint vertices. A bounded morphism \(f:(P,\le)\to(R,\le)\) is a function satisfying: (forth) \(x\le y\Rightarrow f(x)\le f(y)\); (back) whenever \(f(x)\le t\) in \(R\), some \(y\ge x\) satisfies \(f(y)=t\). No surjectivity, root preservation, or extra valuations are imposed. Edge-marked equality means equality up to relabelling the edge variables, and statements about cycle-matroid recovery from it assume equal vertex cardinality, unless component count is separately retained. Scalar-count reconstruction concerns the infinite family \(m\ge2\), not arbitrary target frames.

## Proof
**Step 1: images of upper vertices.** If \(v\in V\) is maximal in \(Q_G\), its only accessible point above itself is itself. It cannot map to \(\bot\), since the back condition would then require distinct successors mapped to all \(m\ge2\) top worlds. Thus its image is a color in \([m]\). An edge-element \(a=\{u,v\}\) has exactly three accessible points above itself: \(a,u,v\). Forth forces \(f(a)\) to equal the common endpoint color when these agree; otherwise \(f(a)=\bot\). If endpoints have different colors, back at \(a\) can hold precisely when \(m=2\), in which case the two endpoint images witness both tops. If endpoints agree, bottom cannot satisfy back, and the common endpoint color uniquely determines \(f(a)\).

**Step 2: scalar formulas.** For \(m=2\), every \(2\)-coloring of the \(v\) upper vertices extends uniquely, giving \(2^v\). For \(m\ge3\), an extension exists precisely when every edge is monochromatic, equivalently when the vertex coloring is constant on each connected component. There are \(m^c\) such colorings. Consequently \(b_2\) determines \(v\), \(b_3\) determines \(c\), and no other graph property influences the family.

**Step 3: marked cut-space formula.** For \(m=2\), an edge-element is sent to \(\bot\) exactly when its endpoints have different colors. Therefore the set of marked edges is the cut \(\delta(S)\) defined by the vertices colored \(1\). The map from subsets \(S\subseteq V\) to their cuts has kernel of size \(2^c\): independently complement the choice on each connected component. Each cut hence occurs with coefficient \(2^c\). In the binary vector space \(\mathbb F_2^E\), the cut space is the row span of the vertex-edge incidence matrix, and the cycle space is its orthogonal complement. Minimal nonzero cycle supports are the circuits of the graphic matroid. Hence the marked polynomial, with its edge variables, determines exactly the corresponding cycle-matroid information, and the converse follows from the same orthogonal-complement relation together with the component coefficient.

**Step 4: sharpness.** For a graph with \(v+e\le5\), there is, up to graph isomorphism, at most one graph for each fixed pair \((v,e)\): for \(v\le3\), the permitted \(e\) are at most \(2\) (except when \(v\le2\), where the statement is immediate), and for \(v\ge4\) the permitted \(e\) are at most \(1\). These possibilities are unique. Therefore no same-size nonisomorphic scalar collision is possible below six worlds. At six worlds the two graphs displayed above are nonisomorphic (one has adjacent edges; the other has disjoint edges), and both have \((v,e,c)=(4,2,2)\). At seven worlds the connected star and four-vertex path both have \((v,e,c)=(4,3,1)\). For \(v+e\le7\), whenever graphs of the same \((v,e,c)\) are nonisomorphic, they are forests, so their marked spectra coincide. At eight worlds \(C_4\) and the triangle-plus-pendant have \((v,e,c)=(4,4,1)\), equal scalar profiles and inequivalent cycle spaces under every edge bijection: the former has a circuit of size four while the latter has one of size three. This establishes the sharp first separation of scalar and marked data. Finally, any two nonisomorphic trees of the same order share all scalar and marked fan data by Step 3, yielding unbounded same-size nonisomorphic examples.

It compares 22 graph/target configurations with the formulas, checks full edge-marked supports, enumerates graph isomorphism classes up to eight incidence-poset worlds, and confirms both thresholds. ## Relationship to prior work
Bounded morphisms and Kripke frame semantics are classical; the basic definition is not claimed as new. Whitney's theorem and the cut/cycle-space relation are likewise classical: Katz (2012), §1, explicitly discusses the cycle-space determination up to 2-isomorphism. Almeida (2024), Proposition 5.2, gives a more general *maximal-point color extension into nonempty-subset posets*. That result supplies a closely related conceptual mechanism, and is an important implicit-coverage risk for the local coloring step. Neither that general extension nor the classical cycle-space theorem itself states the full scalar fan profile of edge-below-vertex incidence frames, the marked p-morphism/cut-space identity, and the sharp six- and eight-world information thresholds assessed together here. The contribution is this exact modal-invariant information hierarchy; not the previously known general definitions or Whitney theory.

## Limitations
This is a same-author mathematical derivation and computational cross-check, not independently audited. The strongest residual originality risk is that the composite theorem may already appear as an implicit easy corollary of a maximal-point lifting construction and standard graphic-matroid identities. No novelty is claimed for the underlying cut-space theory or the general maximal-color extension into powerset targets. The result does not classify bounded morphisms to arbitrary finite frames, and the marked polynomial never distinguishes trees of the same order.
