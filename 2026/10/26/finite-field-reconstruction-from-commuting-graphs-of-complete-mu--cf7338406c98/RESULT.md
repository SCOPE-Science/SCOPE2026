# Reconstructing the field and parts from a full commuting graph

## Finding

Let \(q\) be an odd prime power and \(G=K_{n_1,\ldots,n_s}\), where \(s\ge2\) and each \(n_i\ge1\). Form the two-step graphical Lie algebra \(L(G,q)\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) with a separate central basis vector for every edge. Form its **full commuting graph**, with every element of \(L(G,q)\) a vertex, and distinct vertices adjacent when their bracket is zero. Within this family the abstract graph determines both \(q\) and the unordered multiset \(\{n_1,\ldots,n_s\}\). Neither the order of the field nor the number of parts is given to the reconstruction.

## Proof

Put \(N=\sum_i n_i\), \(E=\sum_{i<j}n_i n_j\), and \(V_i=\mathbb F_q^{n_i}\). The algebra is \((\bigoplus_i V_i)\oplus Z\), with \(Z=\bigoplus_{i<j} V_i\otimes V_j\) central and bracket components \(x_i\otimes y_j-y_i\otimes x_j\). Every element with some nonzero \(x_i\) has nonzero bracket with a suitable vector supported in another part, so the center is exactly \(Z\). The universal vertices therefore number \(c=q^E\); delete them.

For a nonzero quotient vector supported only in part \(i\), the bracket equations force a commuting quotient vector to lie in \(V_i\), with no further restriction. For a vector \(x\) supported in at least two parts, the equation for two active parts forces \(y_i=\lambda x_i\) and \(y_j=\lambda x_j\) for the same \(\lambda\): apply linear functionals taking value one on the respective \(x_i,x_j\). Comparing a fixed active part with each remaining part then gives \(y=\lambda x\), including zero coordinates in all inactive parts. Thus each part gives a component clique on \(c(q^{n_i}-1)\) vertices. Every mixed projective line gives a separate component clique on \(c(q-1)\) vertices. Mixed lines exist since there are at least two parts. This gives the forward graph description used for decoding.

Let \(b\) be the least order of a connected component after the universal vertices are removed. All part cliques have order at least \(c(q-1)\), and the mixed lines attain it. Hence \(q=1+b/c\). The original graph order is \(cq^N\), so it now gives \(N\). Each component of order \(h>b\) comes from exactly one part of size \(a\ge2\), recovered by \(q^a=1+h/c\). A smallest clique cannot itself distinguish a singleton part from a mixed line. Instead, subtract the sum of the recovered nonsingleton part sizes from \(N\) to get the number of singleton parts. This determines the multiset, even when all parts are singletons. Conversely, the recovered field order and multiset determine the Lie algebra and thus its full commuting graph.

## Scope and relationship to known results

This is an inverse classification within the specified independent-edge family, not a recognition theorem among all Lie algebras. It relies on the center being present in the observed graph. For example, the projective graph for two singleton parts is edgeless, and it is not in general safe to treat singleton part components in a projective census as distinguishable from mixed lines.

The support-centralizer calculation and the underlying projective component counts are known in more generality; see *Projective commuting components of graphical Lie algebras and the connected-domination enumerator*, Finding and proof steps 1–2 ([record](https://github.com/Resultary/2026/tree/main/2026/10/26/SCOPE-projective-commuting-components-of-graphical-lie-algebras-and-th--6e8bed3ac269)). In this specialization each part induces an edgeless graph, so its projective points form a clique and every mixed point is isolated. The full forward description amounts to replacing each projective vertex by a clique of size \(q^E(q-1)\) and adjoining the universal center clique. The claim here is the inverse: the full graph itself gives that fiber size via a minimum component, gives the ambient quotient dimension after decoding the field, and thereby resolves the otherwise indistinguishable singleton parts. The forward census and its rank or probability consequences are not claimed as separate contributions.

## Verification

The proof is valid over the stated field orders without enumeration. For a check with a nonprime field, over \(\mathbb F_9=\mathbb F_3[t]/(t^2+1)\) and parts \((2,1)\), enumeration of the two bracket coordinates on the quotient gives one nonzero component of order 80 and 81 of order 8. Thus the full graph has 81 universal vertices, one component of order 6480 and 81 of order 648 after their removal, and 59049 vertices in all. The decoding gives \(1+648/81=9\), \(9^3=59049/81\), and the unique size-two part leaves one singleton. This finite check is not a proof for other fields or multipartitions.
