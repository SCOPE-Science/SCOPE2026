# Scientific review: Boolean-frame quotients and one-factorizations

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is limited to \(m\ge2\), the minimum source dimension, and the first dimension above it. The proof uses the maximal bottom-fibre frontier to force at least \(m\) distinct immediate Boolean successors, giving a sharp dimension lower bound. When the dimension has just one excess coordinate, bottom-valued singleton worlds form the whole nontrivial bottom fibre. Their incident rank-two worlds require each intermediate target label once, yielding a bijection with labelled proper edge-colourings of complete graphs. The remaining rank-three and higher images are forced to the top. Conversely each described assignment satisfies forth, back, and surjectivity. An independent monotone-map/back-lifting enumerator verifies \((n,m)=(2,2),(3,2),(4,3)\), and a separate complete-graph colour enumerator confirms the small factorization coefficients. No finite computation is offered as proof for unbounded \(m\).

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The predecessor chain-target finding concerns a unique intermediate point and does not imply the \(m\)-branch first-excess classification for \(m\ge3\). A predecessor diamond-target result treats \(m=2\), which is acknowledged as covered; neither that instance nor the standard back-lifting definition supplies the all-parameter parity/factorization decomposition. The accessible 2014 primary one-factorization article defines and studies precisely the edge-colouring objects used here but does not count Kripke-frame morphisms. The inspected 2015 Kripke filtration paper addresses finite-height frame partitions rather than labelled Boolean-frame fan quotient enumeration. Research-record searches under equivalent names and implication comparisons found no direct all-\(m\) statement. Unindexed prior modal-logic enumeration, or an inaccessible specialized source, could still duplicate some portion: absence of a hit is not proof of priority.

## Value — PASS
The exact first-excess count is a natural next invariant after the sharp threshold of a branching quotient problem, not a selected small numerical instance. The structural bijection explains why a frame-quotient enumeration contains the classical one-factorization counts of \(K_{2s}\), including a parity obstruction and exact labelled multiplicities for each fibre slice. It connects bounded-morphism geometry with combinatorial design enumeration and gives an all-parameter method, while leaving the harder higher-excess dimensions open.

## Comparison and limitations
Kaski et al. (2014), *Switching in One-Factorisations of Complete Graphs*, DOI: 10.37236/3606, pages 1–3 and the computational framing of section 5, supplies known edge-factorization definitions and counts, not the quotient bijection. Kudinov and Shapirovsky (2015), arXiv:1511.09092, introductory sections concern modal filtrations of finite-height frames. Existing Boolean-to-chain and Boolean-to-four-point-diamond results cover smaller/related targets but not the full \(m\ge3\) family. Known one-factorization counts are not claimed as new. The finite executable confirms limited instances, and the mathematical proof has not been independently audited.
