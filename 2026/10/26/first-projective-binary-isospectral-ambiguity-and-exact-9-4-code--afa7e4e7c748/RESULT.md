# First projective binary isospectral ambiguity and its exact code distortion

## Finding
For a binary linear code, **projective** means that some (hence every) generator matrix has nonzero, pairwise distinct columns; equivalently its dual minimum distance is at least three. Let \(W_C(z)=\sum_{c\in C}z^{\operatorname{wt}(c)}\) be its ordinary Hamming weight enumerator. Among binary projective linear codes of equal length and dimension, every two codes sharing \(W_C\) are permutation equivalent whenever either their dimension is at most four or their common length is at most seven. Both restrictions are sharp: there are projective binary \([8,5,2]\) codes \(C_A,C_B\) with identical enumerators but which are not permutation equivalent.

The first such pair below has exact minimum linear code distortion
\[
\boxed{D(C_A,C_B)=\frac94.}
\]
Here the minimization and distortion are as defined by Bennett, Fox and Morrell (2026): for every invertible linear map \(T:C_A\to C_B\), let \(r_T(c)=\operatorname{wt}(Tc)/\operatorname{wt}(c)\) for \(c\ne0\), and set \(D(C_A,C_B)=\min_T\max_{c\ne0}r_T(c)/\min_{c\ne0}r_T(c)\).

Write an integer from 0 to 31 as its five-coordinate binary vector, least significant bit first. Define two \(5\times8\) generators by their ordered integer column labels
\[
G_A=[1,2,4,8,16,3,5,30],\qquad
G_B=[1,2,4,8,16,3,12,21].
\]
The associated codes have the common weight enumerator
\[
\boxed{W_{C_A}(z)=W_{C_B}(z)=1+3z^2+8z^3+7z^4+8z^5+5z^6.}
\]
The spans of their three weight-two codewords have dimensions two and three respectively; hence they cannot be permutation equivalent.

## Assumptions and scope
All fields are \(\mathbb F_2\). The word "same enumerator" compares codes with the same length and dimension; in fact equality determines dimension from the total coefficient sum and determines length within the projective class when the codes have positive dimension. The claimed sharp threshold ranges over **all** projective binary linear codes, without a restriction on minimum distance other than what their parameters force. The explicit distortion value is for the displayed pair, not an extremum over every length-eight pair. The census assumes standard exact integer and binary arithmetic and counts unlabeled codes up to coordinate permutation.

## Proof
**1. Coding-theoretic reduction.** A projective \([n,k]\) binary code is represented by a rank-\(k\) subset \(S\subseteq\mathbb F_2^k\setminus\{0\}\) of cardinality \(n\). Coordinate permutation and change of generator basis identify precisely the orbits of the natural \(\mathrm{GL}(k,2)\) action on these subsets. For every \(u\in\mathbb F_2^k\), its codeword has weight \(w_S(u)=|\{v\in S:u\cdot v=1\}|\). Thus the sorted list \((w_S(u):u\ne0)\) determines its Hamming enumerator.

**2. Exhaustive small-dimension implication.** For each \(1\le k\le4\), take every subset of the \(2^k-1\) nonzero binary columns and compute its orbit by repeatedly applying elementary transvections \(v\mapsto v+(v_j)e_i\) for \(i\ne j\). These generate \(\mathrm{GL}(k,2)\). Retain only rank-\(k\) orbits and compare their sorted weight lists. The exact number of such orbits is \(1,2,6,36\) for dimensions \(1,2,3,4\), respectively; **no two distinct orbits share a sorted weight list**. For dimension four, the orbit counts at lengths \(4,5,\ldots,15\) are
\[
1,3,4,5,6,5,4,3,2,1,1,1.
\]
The standalone exhaustive verifier implements every loop, transformation, rank test, and collision assertion, including all \(2^{15}=32768\) potential four-dimensional column subsets. Consequently equality of Hamming enumerators implies equivalence for projective codes of dimension at most four.

**3. Exact length-seven cutoff in dimension five.** Every rank-five projective column set has five linearly independent members. Changing basis makes these the five unit columns, so every code with at most seven columns is represented by \((e_1,\ldots,e_5)\) plus at most two distinct members of the remaining 26 nonzero vectors. There are exactly \(1,26,325\) normalized candidates for lengths \(5,6,7\). A canonical orbit key is obtained by running through **all** ordered independent five-column bases, converting all columns to their coordinates in each basis, and taking the least resulting 31-bit point-set mask. Direct enumeration gives respectively \(1,4,8\) equivalence classes; there are exactly \(1,4,8\) distinct weight lists. The verifier enumerates all bases, not a sample. Hence no dimension-five ambiguity occurs below length eight.

**4. Larger dimensions below length eight.** If the common length is at most seven and the common dimension exceeds five, then the code has codimension zero or one. Codimension zero gives the unique full-space code. In codimension one, the dual is generated by one nonzero binary check vector; its Hamming weight uniquely determines the code up to permutation. The MacWilliams identity determines the dual weight enumerator from the primal weight enumerator, hence fixes the check-vector weight. This proves the claimed global length-seven cutoff.

**5. Inequivalent length-eight witnesses.** The two column sets above each consist of eight distinct nonzero vectors and contain a basis, so their codes are projective and have dimension five. Directly evaluating the 31 nonzero information vectors gives the common enumerator displayed above. The preimages of their weight-two words, in integer coordinates, are respectively \(\{8,16,24\}\) and \(\{2,8,16\}\). The first triple has binary rank two and the second has rank three. Because a coordinate permutation maps every weight-two word to a weight-two word by a linear isomorphism, this rank discrepancy proves inequivalence.

**6. Exact distortion lower bound.** In any invertible linear map between the two codes, the three weight-two words of the first code span a two-dimensional subspace. Their images cannot all be weight-two words of the second code, because the latter's three weight-two words are linearly independent. Thus some source word of weight two maps to weight at least three: \(\max r_T\ge3/2\). Conversely, the three independent weight-two words of the second code cannot all have weight-two preimages because the source's three weight-two words have rank two. Hence some target word of weight two has a preimage of weight at least three: \(\min r_T\le2/3\). Therefore \(D_T\ge(3/2)/(2/3)=9/4\).

**7. Exact distortion upper bound.** Define a binary linear isomorphism on information coordinates by mapping the five standard basis vectors to the integer vectors \((1,4,7,8,20)\), in that order. They are independent. For every one of the 31 nonzero information vectors, direct evaluation gives all weight pairs \((w_A(u),w_B(Tu))\), with the multiplicities
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrrrrrrr}
\text{pair}&(2,2)&(2,3)&(3,2)&(3,3)&(3,4)&(4,3)&(4,4)&(4,5)&(5,4)&(5,5)&(5,6)&(6,5)&(6,6)\\
\text{count}&1&2&2&4&2&2&3&2&2&4&2&2&3
\end{array}
\]
Their counts total 31, and every corresponding ratio lies in the closed interval \([2/3,3/2]\), with both endpoints attained. Consequently this isomorphism has distortion \(9/4\), matching the universal lower bound.

## Relationship to prior work
Bennett, Fox and Morrell (2026) introduce and formalize the code-distortion problem, analyze approximation/hardness, and describe general algorithms; their paper does not derive this minimum projective isospectral threshold or the exact distortion of these two codes. Bouyukliev (2006) **already classifies all projective binary codes of dimension up to six**: in particular, its table lists the numbers \(36\) (summed over dimension-four lengths), \(1,4,8\) (dimension-five lengths five through seven), and \(15\) dimension-five classes of length eight. These class counts themselves are **not claimed as new**. The asserted contribution is the sharper collision-free/collision threshold for identical full Hamming enumerators, an explicit attaining pair distinguished by minimum-word span rank, and its exact code-distortion value. Ding (2020) provides the general projective-code/Walsh-spectrum correspondence, without this sharp collision and distortion statement. Dodunekov and Simonis (1998) describe the projective-multiset correspondence; this underlying formalism is also prior work.

## Limitations
No claim is made about shortest nonprojective isospectral examples; those are known at smaller lengths. The distortion \(9/4\) pertains to the explicit pair, not to all pairs with the same parameters. The small-rank and short-length exclusions use a fully specified finite enumeration, not a conceptual classification of every projective point set. Absolute originality cannot be guaranteed: a numerical table or unpublished classification may imply this spectral collision threshold implicitly, and Bouyukliev's complete historic code catalogue was not individually inspected beyond its published paper. The findings have not received independent mathematical validation.
