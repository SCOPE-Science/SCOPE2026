# Review of the sharp projective isospectral threshold and distortion example

**Correctness (C): PASS.** The projective-code correspondence identifies exact matrix-subset equivalence with \(\mathrm{GL}(k,2)\)-orbit equivalence. The standalone finite orbit enumeration verifies injectivity of the weight spectrum for every rank-full point set at dimensions at most four, and independent-basis canonicalization exhausts all dimension-five cases through length seven. The higher-dimensional short-length cases follow from the MacWilliams identity for codimension one. The length-eight examples give identical 32-word Hamming spectra but weight-two subspaces of ranks two and three. That rank discrepancy yields an analytic \(9/4\) distortion lower bound, while the explicit invertible coefficient map has ratios exactly between \(2/3\) and \(3/2\). No finite samples are used to justify an infinite unsupported extrapolation.

**Originality (O): PASS, subject to catalogue risk.** Bouyukliev (2006) already classifies projective binary codes through dimension six and reports relevant equivalence-class counts; these are acknowledged and not presented as discoveries. Its inspected published table provides minimum-distance and automorphism-group data, not the exact earliest Hamming-isospectral collision and its distortion. Bennett, Fox and Morrell (2026) formalize distortion and give complexity results, but not this sharp finite threshold. Ding (2020) gives a Walsh-spectrum representation of all projective binary codes but not the specific threshold. Searches of mathematical research records and related papers found no direct implication of the combined theorem. The unexamined complete historic catalogue could encode an implicit equivalent pair; the originality assessment is therefore qualified and does not certify absolute priority.

**Value (V): PASS.** The question asks when the ordinary Hamming spectrum first fails to identify a projective binary code, a natural sharp boundary that constrains how meaningful spectral comparison is for the newly formalized distortion problem. The result combines a global low-dimensional exclusion, a global short-length exclusion, an explicit smallest witness, a strong algebraic inequivalence invariant, and an exact nontrivial distortion calculation. It does not purport to improve code rate, minimum distance, or coding algorithms.
