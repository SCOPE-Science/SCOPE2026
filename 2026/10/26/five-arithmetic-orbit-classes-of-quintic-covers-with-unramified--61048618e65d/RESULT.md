# Five arithmetic orbit classes of quintic covers with unramified rational fibers

## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbf F_2\), and let \(\varphi:\mathbf P^1_k\to\mathbf P^1_k\) be a finite morphism of degree five. Require that \(\varphi\) be unramified above **each** of the three rational target points. With source and target projective coordinates both fixed, precisely **132** such morphisms exist. Modulo target-coordinate transformations, they give **22** two-dimensional spaces of binary quintics. Under independent source and target changes of coordinates, these form exactly **five** equivalence classes.

Write a binary quintic as \(H_m(X,Y)=\sum_{i=0}^{5}\epsilon_i X^iY^{5-i}\), where \(m=\sum_{i=0}^{5}\epsilon_i2^i\) is the coefficient mask and \(\epsilon_i\in\mathbf F_2\). A representative \((a,b,c)\) denotes the pencil with nonzero members \(H_a,H_b,H_c=H_a+H_b\). The five equivalence classes are:

| Nonzero coefficient masks | Target-unlabelled pencils in orbit | Sizes of the three rational fibers | Critical-source Frobenius orbit(s) | Geometric ramification indices | Branch-value Frobenius orbit(s) |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| (18,37,55) | 3 | (0,0,3) | one orbit of degree 4 | four points of index 2 (wild) | one orbit of degree 4 |
| (19,33,50) | 1 | (1,1,1) | one orbit of degree 2 | two points of index 5 (tame) | one orbit of degree 2 |
| (19,41,58) | 6 | (0,1,2) | one orbit of degree 4 | four points of index 2 (wild) | one orbit of degree 4 |
| (22,35,53) | 6 | (0,1,2) | one orbit of degree 4 | four points of index 2 (wild) | one orbit of degree 4 |
| (22,43,61) | 6 | (0,1,2) | one orbit of degree 4 | four points of index 2 (wild) | one orbit of degree 2, with **two** ramification points above each branch point |

Thus the 22 target-unlabelled covers split into **one** with tame quadratic branch orbit, **six** with a wild quadratic branch orbit, and **fifteen** with a wild quartic branch orbit. The fifth orbit exhibits the collision of four wild geometric critical points into only two geometric branch values. The unique class with rational fiber profile \((1,1,1)\) is the nonsplit cyclic (Rédei-type) case; its known construction is not a separate originality claim.

## Assumptions and scope
Here finite morphisms are defined over \(\mathbf F_2\), the degree is their geometric degree, and a target point is a branch point exactly when some geometric point in its fiber has ramification index greater than one. The condition refers to **all three** target points over \(\mathbf F_2\), not merely the rational source points. Isomorphism of covers permits independent automorphisms of source and target over \(\mathbf F_2\); the intermediate count of 22 permits target transformations only. The branch data in the table concern geometric ramification, not just rational points.

## Proof
**Finite parameterization.** Every degree-five morphism is represented by an ordered pair of homogeneous binary quintics \((F,G)\) with no common zero on \(\mathbf P^1_{\overline{k}}\). It is unramified over all three rational target points if and only if all three binary quintics \(F,G,F+G\) are squarefree as homogeneous forms. Indeed these are exactly the scheme-theoretic fibers of the three rational target points. A basis change of the pencil permutes its three nonzero members, so the pencil identifies precisely one orbit under target automorphisms. Each such pencil has six ordered bases (because \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbf F_2)\) has order six).

**Exact finite criterion.** In the affine chart \(Y=1\), the form encoded by \(m\) is \(h_m(t)=\sum_i\epsilon_it^i\). A nonzero homogeneous quintic is squarefree precisely if \(\gcd(h_m,h'_m)=1\) in \(\mathbf F_2[t]\) and its coefficients in degrees five and four are not both zero (the latter is the simple-root test at infinity). Two forms have no projective common root precisely if their affine polynomials are coprime and their degree-five coefficients are not both zero. These are exhaustive exact tests of the 63 nonzero masks; they yield 24 squarefree quintics and 22 qualifying three-element pencils. The complete 22-pencil list and two executable implementations of this enumeration are supplied.

**Orbit completeness.** There are precisely six invertible binary coordinate substitutions \((X,Y)\mapsto(aX+bY,cX+dY)\) with coefficients in \(\mathbf F_2\). Their images of the 22 pencils form disjoint orbits of sizes \(3,1,6,6,6\), represented exactly by the five triples in the table. In particular, their sum is 22, so no orbit is omitted. Each pencil has six target coordinate bases, proving the count \(22\cdot6=132\) of maps with both coordinate systems fixed.

**Critical divisor and its field of definition.** For degree five, write uniquely \(F=XA^2+YB^2\) and \(G=XC^2+YD^2\), with homogeneous binary quadratics \(A,B,C,D\). In affine coordinates the Wronskian numerator of \(\varphi=F/G\) is \((AD+BC)^2\). Hence the reduced critical-point divisor is controlled by the quartic \(R=AD+BC\). Across the five representatives, the coefficient masks of \(R\), in table order, are \(19,21,31,31,19\). Masks 19 and 31 encode irreducible degree-four forms. Mask 21 encodes the square of \(X^2+XY+Y^2\). The zeros of the former comprise four points conjugate over \(\mathbf F_{16}\); the latter has two points conjugate over \(\mathbf F_4\).

**Ramification indices and branch orbits.** In the residue field \(k(\alpha)\) of each irreducible critical factor, evaluate the Hasse coefficients in \(t-\alpha\) of \(F(t)G(\alpha)-G(t)F(\alpha)\). The first nonzero positive coefficient occurs at order two for the four representatives with irreducible critical quartic, and at order five for the quadratic critical pair. Thus those geometric ramification indices are respectively two and five; characteristic two makes the former wild and the latter tame. Computing the Frobenius degree of \(F(\alpha)/G(\alpha)\) gives branch-value degrees \(4,2,4,4,2\). An independent symbolic resultant calculation gives, in the same order, the branch-polynomial masks \(25,7,25,19,21\), with mask 21 a squared irreducible quadratic and the other nonquadratic masks irreducible quartics. This proves both the branch-collision statement and the complete table. Local Hasse calculations avoid the invalid inference that a double Wronskian zero alone determines the ramification index in characteristic two.

## Relationship to prior work
The arithmetic study of low-degree covers and branch cycles is part of Hurwitz-space geometry. Karemaker–Marques–Sijsling (2020, arXiv:2003.06673) treats **cubic** function fields with specified ramification and bi-isomorphism. Ding–Zieve (2021, arXiv:2103.07921) gives a relative Riemann–Hurwitz formula for general curve maps under stated hypotheses; it does not enumerate the degree-five binary-field locus. Canning–Larson (2021, arXiv:2110.01059) studies Chow rings of low-degree Hurwitz spaces, not this small-field branch-avoidance count. Ding–Zieve (2020, arXiv:2010.15657) and Sze (2023, University of South Florida dissertation 9934) study **permutation** rational functions; the permutation subcase is only one of the 22 pencils and is not claimed independently. Their broader results do not, on the inspected material, give the complete nonpermutation wild-branch classification above. The Sze dissertation's full PDF was not accessible during this review, which leaves a concrete overlap risk.

## Limitations
This is an exact classification over \(\mathbf F_2\) at degree five, not a formula for general finite fields or degrees. It is a genus-zero Hurwitz-locus count, not a new general Riemann–Hurwitz theorem. The result may be implicit in an unindexed low-field cover database or the unavailable full dissertation, so bibliographic priority remains qualified. The computation has received self-review only; no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
