# Review of the five-equilibrium three-agent sigmoidal confidence theorem

## Correctness (PASS)
For each fixed \(1<\delta<4\) and all sufficiently large \(\gamma\), the exact scalar equilibrium function is odd and has one positive root in each of two distinct transition layers. Uniform sign control excludes other positive roots; strict convexity in the interior transition layer and strict decrease near opinion \(1\) establish uniqueness and stability. Positive interaction weights, inward endpoint drift and one-dimensional flow monotonicity establish all attraction basins. Exact logarithmic equilibrium identities give the \(1/\gamma\) basin correction and exponentially small displacement of the outer attractors. The standard-library decimal checker independently corroborates signs and asymptotics at six parameter pairs; it does not establish the infinite-parameter theorem.

## Originality (PASS, subject to publication-priority uncertainty)
The principal prior source is Brooks--Chodrow--Porter, arXiv:2209.07004 (initially available 2022-09-15), SIAM Journal on Applied Dynamical Systems 23 (2024), 1442--1470. Its published Theorem 6 establishes stability of the zero harmonic equilibrium under balanced exposure. Its general steep-confidence results constrain possible limiting states, and its Conjecture 1 requests converse approximation of hard-threshold interaction patterns. None of these statements yields the number of nonlinear equilibria, their global basins, or their exact transition-layer scales for a single persuadable node with two fixed opposing zealots. The paper's figures concern larger interacting persuadable populations and do not furnish the present analytic census. A search of mathematical research records, quoted-formula variants and related opinion models yielded no directly covering result, but searches cannot establish global publication priority. The distinction from stochastic discrete-state zealot-voter models is material.

## Value (PASS)
This is a complete, motivated singular-limit classification on the smallest balanced-exposure graph with one persuadable individual. It isolates coexistence of compromise and two polarized attractors even though the compromise is linearly stable, and computes the unstable opinions separating their basins. The exact barrier \(\sqrt\delta-1\), including its first finite-steepness correction, is a useful structural benchmark and gives a rigorous small-system instance of the equilibrium-pattern approximation problem posed in the motivating paper. Its scope is deliberately limited to one continuous opinion and fixed \(1<\delta<4\).

## Closest literature and limitations
The source's harmonic-stability theorem, numerical polarization illustrations and general limiting-state theorem must not be mistaken for the present full scalar phase portrait. Its proposed general converse remains unproved here. No explicit sharp minimum steepness is obtained, and critical boundary values, noisy dynamics, asymmetric zealots, and multivertex systems are excluded. No exhaustive priority claim or independent scientific validation is made.
