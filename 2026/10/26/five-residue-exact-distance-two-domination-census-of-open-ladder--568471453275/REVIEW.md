# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The path metric gives exactly two disjoint distance-two adjacency components, each isomorphic to the square of a path. Every minimum path-square dominating set is encoded bijectively by endpoint deficits at most two and inter-center deficits at most four, with total deficit \(5\lceil n/5\rceil-n\). This proves both cardinality and exact count for every \(n\ge1\), including the boundary case \(n=1\). Direct graph enumeration through \(n=9\) and independent path center counts through \(n=18\) corroborate the proof; neither finite run substitutes for it.

**Originality — PASS on currently accessible evidence.** Primary-source inspection of Das–Das–Sadhukhan (arXiv:2607.29648) finds square-grid asymptotics rather than optimum-solution multiplicities for open two-row strips. Henning–Jafari Rad (2017) provides general hop-domination results; the 2026 honeycomb/butterfly article studies different networks. Targeted publication searches, published mathematical-claim searches and historical-claim comparisons found no equivalent all-order ladder census, including by reduction to optimal domination of a path square. The path-square decomposition itself is elementary and not asserted to be novel; complete priority cannot be guaranteed.

**Value — PASS.** Counting **every** minimum exact-distance configuration on a canonical narrow strip sharpens an optimization problem to an exact multiplicity theorem. The slack composition establishes the precise five-residue phase diagram, with unique solutions at multiples of five and polynomial solution entropy of degrees \(2,4,6,8\) in the other classes. The constrained cardinality is a natural invariant motivated by exact-distance grid coverage, not an arbitrary numerical slice.
