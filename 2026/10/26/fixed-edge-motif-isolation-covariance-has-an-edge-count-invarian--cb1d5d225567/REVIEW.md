# Review of the exact motif–isolation covariance claim

## Correctness
PASS. Counting the allowed host edges outside an isolated vertex gives the exact mixed moment, and standard hypergeometric factorial cancellation yields the claimed edge-count-independent sign. Both two-edge sign factorizations and the five-vertex cubic threshold follow algebraically; all domain and degeneracy boundaries are specified. The standalone exact-rational program exhaustively compares 32,380 fixed-edge graphs and 436 arithmetic checks to the stated formula. Exhaustive tests supplement, but do not substitute for, the all-parameter proof.

## Originality
PASS on inspected sources, with nonzero historical-priority risk. The full mathematical presentations of Dembo–Lubetzky (arXiv:1804.11327, Sections 1–2) and Goldschmidt–Griffiths–Scott (arXiv:1902.06830, Sections 1–2 and 7) were checked for the same mixed covariance and sign classification. The latter gives exact non-induced motif means, not the present mixed isolation covariance or the sharp all-density sign differences. Related independent-edge formulas and a geometric-graph isolation covariance do not imply fixed-edge signs. Standard factorial identities could conceal an earlier implicit derivation; independent priority review remains warranted.

## Value
PASS. Under a fixed-edge null model, the sign of how isolates interact with small subgraph counts can reverse from the independent-edge model and remain fixed at every admissible edge count. The wedge-versus-matching dichotomy distinguishes edge concentration from dispersal, while the sharp \(n=11\) transition for a natural disconnected motif is a substantive structural boundary. This directly informs interpretation of finite-sample graph motif diagnostics, rather than adding an arbitrary single parameter value.

## Residual limitations
Only non-induced motifs without isolated vertices and nondegenerate edge counts are covered. The result is finite exact covariance information, not a full joint distribution or a stochastic ordering. Priority is not independently established.
