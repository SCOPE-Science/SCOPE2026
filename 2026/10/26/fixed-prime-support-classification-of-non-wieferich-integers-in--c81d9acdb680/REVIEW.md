# Review of the fixed-support non-Wieferich classification

## Correctness
The odd-prime condition comes from the exact lifted valuation \(\nu_p(b^{L_b(n)}-1)=\max\{a_p,s_p+h_p\}\). For the binary prime the two valuation cases for odd bases and the order modulo powers of \(2\) give the three cases in the classification, including the isolated exponent \(1\). The count follows from weighted-simplex lattice points and a lower-dimensional exceptional face. The standalone verifier checks exact modular-order congruences against the criterion without using the classification to calculate the order.

## Originality
Falk–Harrington–Jones classify monogenicity conditions using base-\(b\) Wieferich primes, not composite exact-support exponents. Franco–Pomerance establish global properties of the base-two composite criterion and a local obstruction, but not this all-support theorem. Siegel's later asymptotic work (October 19, 2026) provides a sufficient squarefree criterion and global asymptotic, not a classification of all powers in each support or the even-\(n\) phenomenon. Searches of published research records and related literature identified no equivalent statement. Classical lifting and CRT give significant conceptual overlap, so an unindexed stronger theorem remains a priority risk.

## Mathematical value
The exact positive-exponent description is a complete classification for each natural fixed prime radical and arbitrary base, revealing that every such radical has infinitely many non-Wieferich multiples despite their global rarity. The dyadic exception exhibits a genuine failure of upward-closure, and the formula yields a sharp fixed-support logarithmic main term. This is not a numerical table or merely a single example.

## Scope and limitations
The theorem does not address the global density as supports vary, bases not coprime to the integer, or the infinitude of Wieferich primes. Finite checking is evidence for local identities, not a proof of the asymptotic. No independent audit or external priority certification is implied.
