# Four components and the first dimension jump in flagged one-sided zero-product pairs

## Finding
Let \(k\) be any field, let \(\mathfrak n_n(k)\) denote the associative algebra of strictly upper-triangular \(n\times n\) matrices, and let \(\mathcal Z_n\) be the affine scheme of ordered pairs \((A,B)\in\mathfrak n_n(k)^2\) satisfying \(AB=0\). For \(n=4\), the defining scheme is **geometrically reduced**, and its support has **exactly four geometrically integral, rational irreducible components**, of dimensions \(10,9,9,9\). Thus this natural flagged one-sided variety is non-equidimensional even though \(\mathcal Z_2\) is an affine space and \(\mathcal Z_3\) has two equidimensional components.

Write \(a_{ij}=A_{ij}\), \(b_{ij}=B_{ij}\), and abbreviate \(x=a_{12}\), \(y=a_{13}\), \(z=a_{23}\), \(p=b_{23}\), \(h=b_{24}\), and \(r=b_{34}\). The four components are the zero loci of the following prime ideals in the full twelve-coordinate ring:
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_1&=(x,r),\\
P_2&=(p,h,r),\\
P_3&=(x,y,z),\\
P_4&=(z,p,xh+yr).
\end{aligned}
\]
All six other coordinates are free. The fourth component is a six-dimensional affine factor times the irreducible quadric cone \(xh+yr=0\) in four variables.

For every finite field \(\mathbb F_q\), the number of ordered pairs is exactly
\[
\boxed{\#\mathcal Z_4(\mathbb F_q)=q^{10}+3q^9-4q^8+q^7.}
\]
This counts points of the reduced scheme and, because the defining scheme is reduced, of the original scheme. No restriction on characteristic is imposed.

## Assumptions and scope
The field \(k\) is arbitrary for the prime decomposition and geometric assertions; geometric integrality means integrality after extension to an algebraic closure. The count concerns each prime power \(q\), including characteristic two. Multiplication is **one-sided**: \(AB=0\) is required, but neither \(BA=0\) nor \(AB=BA\) is assumed. The flag is fixed by requiring each matrix to be strictly upper triangular in the displayed basis; no quotient by simultaneous conjugation is taken.

## Proof
Direct multiplication gives exactly three potentially nonzero entries of \(AB\):
\[
(AB)_{13}=xp,\qquad (AB)_{24}=zr,\qquad (AB)_{14}=xh+yr.
\]
Consequently the defining ideal is \(I=(xp,zr,xh+yr)\) in the six active coordinates, extended by the six free coordinates.

**Set-theoretic decomposition.** When \(x=0\), the equations reduce to \(yr=zr=0\), so either \(r=0\) (the locus \(P_1\)) or \(y=z=0\) (the locus \(P_3\)). When \(x\ne0\), one has \(p=0\), and \(zr=0\) forces either \(r=0\) (then \(h=0\), the locus \(P_2\)) or \(z=0\) (the locus \(P_4\)). Conversely each listed locus satisfies all equations. Thus the set of geometric points is the union of these four closed sets.

**The original ideal is radical.** Work in \(S=k[x,y,z,p,h,r]\). We establish the polynomial-ideal identity \(I=P_1\cap P_2\cap P_3\cap P_4\), not merely equality of zero sets. Direct monomial-ideal intersections yield
\[
P_1\cap P_2=(xp,xh,r),\qquad
J:=P_1\cap P_2\cap P_3=(xp,xh,rx,ry,rz).
\]
Certainly \(I\subseteq J\cap P_4\). Conversely, let \(f\in J\cap P_4\). Using the generators of \(J\) and the relations \(xp=rz=0\), \(xh=-yr\) modulo \(I\), there are polynomials \(U,V\) with \(f\equiv r(xU+yV)\pmod I\). Since \(P_4\) is prime and \(r\notin P_4\), \(xU+yV\in P_4\). The elementary variable-separation identity
\[
P_4\cap(x,y)=(p,z)(x,y)+(xh+yr)
\]
follows because \(xh+yr\in(x,y)\), while the ideals \((p,z)\) and \((x,y)\) are generated by disjoint variable sets. Multiplying its generators by \(r\) puts them in \(I\): \(rpx\in(xp)\), \(rpy=p(ry)=p(-xh)=-h(xp)\), \(rzx,rzy\in(rz)\), and \(r(xh+yr)\in I\). Therefore \(f\in I\), proving the identity. Every \(P_i\) is prime: \(P_1,P_2,P_3\) are variable ideals, and \(P_4\) is prime because \(xh+yr\) is an irreducible primitive linear polynomial in \(h,r\) over \(k[x,y]\). The intersection is therefore radical over every field and stays radical over any extension.

**Components and dimensions.** The variable primes have codimensions \(2,3,3\). The fourth prime has codimension \(3\) because it consists of two coordinate equations and one irreducible equation. Hence their dimensions in twelve variables are \(10,9,9,9\). None contains another: \(P_1,P_2,P_3\) have different unrestricted-variable patterns, and \(P_4\) admits points with \(x,r\ne0\), \(y\ne0\) and \(h=-yr/x\), so it is not contained in any of the others; the same open-pattern tests separate the other inclusions. All four are rational, including \(P_4\), since its dense open set \(x\ne0\) allows elimination of \(h\). The \(n=2\) algebra has identically zero multiplication. For \(n=3\), \(AB=0\) is the single equation \(a_{12}b_{23}=0\) in six variables, giving two five-dimensional affine components. Thus \(n=4\) is the first size for failure of equidimensionality.

**Finite-field count.** The six free coordinates contribute \(q^6\). Among the six active coordinates, the case \(x=0\) has \(q^2(q^2+q-1)\) solutions: \(p,h\) are free and the triples \((y,z,r)\) satisfy \(yr=zr=0\). If \(x\ne0\), then \(p=0\), \(h\) is determined by \(x,y,r\), and \((z,r)\) has \(2q-1\) possibilities. That case contributes \((q-1)q(2q-1)\). Adding produces \(q^4+3q^3-4q^2+q\); multiplying by \(q^6\) gives the stated count.

## Verification
The included `verify_counts.py` exhaustively enumerates the six active coordinates over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_4,\mathbb F_5\), independently tests all three product equations, and confirms active counts \(26,129,388,905\) (full counts \(1664,94041,1589248,14140625\)). Its \(\mathbb F_4\) arithmetic uses the quotient by \(t^2+t+1\). It prints `VERIFY_OK_FLAGGED_ZERO_PRODUCT_N4`.

The included `verify_ideals.py` uses SymPy's Gröbner-basis elimination to compare the original ideal with the four-prime intersection over \(\mathbb Q\) and characteristics \(2,3,5\). It prints `VERIFY_OK_PRIMARY_INTERSECTION_N4`. This is a finite computational cross-check; the preceding ideal identity is the characteristic-independent proof.

## Relationship to prior work
The classical variety of complexes treats unrestricted linear maps with consecutive product zero; its rank-stratified components do not give the component decomposition of the **fixed complete-flag, strict-triangular coordinate fiber**. Schröer's `math/0211293` describes components of nilpotent pairs subject to **both** \(AB=0\) and \(BA=0\), and Bondarenko--Gerasimova--Sergeichuk's `0812.2155` classifies mutually annihilating pairs up to simultaneous similarity; neither statement implies this one-sided fixed-flag prime decomposition. Chen--Wang's 2016 zero-product-preserver paper concerns bijections preserving the zero-product relation on the same strict-triangular algebra; its full text was not accessible during the comparison and is an explicit historical-priority risk. Prior finite-field and component formulas for zero products in the **full** upper-triangular algebra concern a different ambient scheme and do not supply the decomposition of the nilradical's diagonal-zero fiber. This statement is not claimed as a new general theory of complexes.

## Limitations
Only the first non-equidimensional size \(n=4\) is classified; no formula for the components for arbitrary \(n\ge5\) is asserted. The ideal identity is for one-sided product zero, not for commuting or mutually annihilating pairs. Historical priority remains subject to comparison with inaccessible relevant preserver literature. No external independent mathematical review has been conducted.
