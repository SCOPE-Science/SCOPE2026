# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

For arbitrary real habitat vital rates, common positive symmetric migration, and positive season lengths, the symmetric exponential factors have strictly positive entries and positive determinant. Cyclic and transpose invariance explain all order invariance up to three factors. An explicit traceless matrix expansion proves the quartic trace difference and comparison, while fixed determinant plus a strictly positive matrix proves the Perron ordering. The three four-season permutation classes follow from dihedral orbits and the same relabeled nonzero factorization. The extinction-versus-growth witness is established with exact rational enclosures of positive matrix exponentials and the scalar Perron threshold, not from numerical eigensolvers. The proof does not use or imply nonlinear persistence. A Python standard-library standalone checker verifies the strict finite inequalities and sample symbolic identities. Residual correctness risk: failure of an independent review of the symbolic argument or the interpretation of the demographic rates; neither appears in the reconstructed proof.

## Value — PASS

Season order is an interpretable ecological variable when total climatic exposure, mean local vital rates and mobility are held fixed. The four-phase minimality theorem rules out an entire class of putative ordering effects, and the signed law gives an exact test without Floquet eigensolver runs. The rationally certified pair of opposite-growth schedules shows that exposure totals alone can be insufficient even when both patches are sinks in isolation. This is a structural theorem and a meaningful boundary case for ecological schedule design, rather than an arbitrary finite-parameter recomputation.
