# Four forbidden threshold graphs for depth-three neighborhood shattering

## Finding

For a finite simple threshold graph \(G=(V,E)\), let \(\mathcal N^\circ(G)=\{h_v:v\in V\}\), where \(h_v(x)=1\) exactly when \(vx\in E\). Consider its ordinary realizable Littlestone dimension (maximum depth of a completely shattered binary query tree). Then

\[
\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal N^\circ(G))\le 2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
G\text{ contains none of the four graphs }F_1,F_2,F_3,F_4\text{ as an induced subgraph}.
\]

Define the four graphs explicitly by a threshold-graph creation word. Start with vertex \(0\); for each successive vertex \(j\ge1\), a word bit \(b_j=0\) makes it isolated from previous vertices, while \(b_j=1\) joins it to **every** previous vertex. The four words and sorted ordinary degree sequences are:

| Graph | Creation word | Vertices | Sorted degrees |
|---|---|---:|---|
| \(F_1\) | `1101010` | 8 | `(0,1,2,4,4,4,5,6)` |
| \(F_2\) | `1010110` | 8 | `(0,2,3,4,4,5,6,6)` |
| \(F_3\) | `1010101` | 8 | `(1,2,3,4,4,5,6,7)` |
| \(F_4\) | `11011011` | 9 | `(2,4,6,6,6,7,7,8,8)` |

Each graph has Littlestone dimension exactly three; each proper induced subgraph has dimension at most two. Consequently, **eight vertices is the sharp first order at which a threshold graph can have neighborhood Littlestone dimension three**. For every threshold graph, the ordinary VC dimension of this same open-neighborhood class is at most two; hence each \(F_i\) gives a strict adaptive-versus-static shattering separation.

## Assumptions and scope

Graphs are finite, simple, undirected, possibly disconnected, and may include isolated or dominating vertices. A threshold graph is one obtainable from a singleton by iteratively adding an isolated or dominating vertex; equivalently, it has no induced \(P_4\), \(C_4\), or \(2K_2\). An induced subgraph always retains *all* edges between its selected vertices. All queried points and all hypothesis indices range over the same vertex set \(V\). If distinct vertices have identical neighborhood rows, the concept class counts their Boolean functions only once. The primary logical object is the adjacency formula \(E(x;y)\) and its Shelah/Littlestone tree rank (classification-theoretic MSC 03C45). The theorem concerns **open**, not closed, neighborhoods. It characterizes the condition \(\operatorname{Ldim}\le2\), not the exact dimension of all threshold graphs with larger rank.

## Proof

**1. Three-round splitting criterion.** Given a finite nonempty family \(\mathcal H\) of *distinct* Boolean functions on a finite query domain \(X\), define \(\mathcal H_{q,b}=\{h\in\mathcal H:h(q)=b\}\). The family shatters a full binary tree of depth one exactly when it contains at least two distinct functions: two such functions disagree on some query. Therefore it shatters depth two exactly when some query \(q\) leaves at least two distinct functions in *each* of \(\mathcal H_{q,0}\) and \(\mathcal H_{q,1}\). It shatters depth three exactly when some query leaves two families that both satisfy the depth-two criterion. These are if-and-only-if statements, proved by splitting at the root and using the two subtrees. They apply unchanged to graph-neighborhood rows after duplicate rows are discarded.

**2. A universal size cutoff.** Any depth-three shattered binary tree has at most seven internal query labels and eight root-to-leaf binary strings. Choose one hypothesis realizing each leaf string. The eight chosen hypotheses are necessarily distinct, since any two leaf strings separate at a node. Keep the at most seven queried vertices and at most eight witnessing hypothesis vertices. Restricting the graph to these at most fifteen vertices preserves the complete shattered tree, since every retained center-query adjacency is unchanged. Threshold graphs are induced-hereditary. Thus every threshold graph with dimension at least three has an induced threshold subgraph of order at most fifteen with the same property. Among all such induced subgraphs, choose one minimal under vertex deletion. It too has order at most fifteen.

**3. Finite exhaustive reduction.** Every \(n\)-vertex threshold graph is isomorphic to one generated by exactly one of the \(2^{n-1}\) binary strings of length \(n-1\). For each string, construct the symmetric adjacency rows by adding an edge \(ij\) for each \(i<j\) with \(b_j=1\). Remove duplicate Boolean rows and apply the depth-three criterion of step 1. When it succeeds, delete each vertex in turn, *recompute the rows on the smaller query domain*, and test whether the criterion fails after **every** deletion. This exactly characterizes minimal depth-three graphs; it does not assume distinct creation strings correspond to distinct isomorphism classes.

The enumeration checks the complete range \(1\le n\le15\), comprising \(32767\) creation words. The numbers of depth-three-positive words for \(n=1,\ldots,15\) are

`0,0,0,0,0,0,0,3,23,101,337,954,2426,5734,12878`.

Exactly three words at order eight pass the minimality test, and exactly one word at order nine does; no word at any other order up to fifteen passes. Their literal creation words are precisely those in the table under Finding. Each has a different sorted degree sequence, so the four graphs are pairwise nonisomorphic. The included C++17 and Python implementations independently enumerate all the words and apply logically equivalent formulations of the criterion; both reproduce the identical classification. The algorithms are exhaustive finite proofs because the creation-word generation is complete, the splitting test is equivalent to depth-three shattering, and the fifteen-vertex cutoff covers every minimal obstruction. This is not an extrapolation from sampled instances.

**4. Minimality and completeness.** Each listed word has a complete depth-three shattered-tree certificate in `witnesses.json`; `witness_checker.py` reconstructs the graph, checks all query labels and all eight leaf hypothesis paths, and checks that the leaf witnesses are distinct. All one-vertex deletions of each listed graph fail the rank-three test; this implies all proper induced subgraphs fail it by heredity. Conversely, suppose an arbitrary threshold graph has rank at least three. By step 2 it has a minimal depth-three induced subgraph on at most fifteen vertices; by step 3 this subgraph must be isomorphic to one of the four listed graphs. If it contains any of them, rank at least three follows from the certificates and monotonicity under induced subgraphs. This proves both directions. Since an \(n\)-hypothesis class shatters depth at most \(\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor\), each listed eight- or nine-vertex example has dimension exactly three.

**5. Static VC contrast.** In every threshold graph there is a split into a clique \(C\) and independent set \(I\) such that the neighborhoods of vertices from \(I\), restricted to \(C\), are nested. The traces of clique-centered neighborhoods on \(I\) are nested as well. No three-point query set is shattered. If it contains three points from \(I\), traces form a chain (independent centers contribute only the empty trace). If it contains two from \(I\), the two singleton traces on those coordinates cannot both arise from the nested clique-centered family, while independent centers give only zeros on \(I\). If it contains one from \(I\) and two from \(C\), the trace that is one at the independent point and zero at both clique points is impossible: a center adjacent to an independent point must lie in \(C\), and every clique vertex is adjacent to at least one of the two other clique vertices. If it contains three from \(C\), clique centers give only the all-one trace or traces with one zero, and independent centers give a chain, which cannot supply the three different singleton traces. Thus \(\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal N^\circ(G))\le2\). Direct trace enumeration gives equality for the four displayed examples.

## Relationship to prior work

Adler, Benediktsson, and Macpherson (2020) examine VC dimension of the edge predicate on Johnson and Hamming graphs and their induced subgraphs; the objects and static invariant differ. Ghazi, Golowich, Kumar, and Manurangsi (2021) give general closure inequalities for Littlestone dimension, not exact graph-neighborhood obstructions. Coudert, Csikós, Ducoffe, and Viennot (2024) analyze the algorithmic computation of **closed-neighborhood VC dimension** on graphs, not adaptive Littlestone depth for threshold-graph open neighborhoods. Classical threshold-graph creation sequences and their induced heredity are used as existing structural tools. No priority claim is made beyond the searches and primary-source inspections documented in the review; an earlier equivalent classification might remain undiscovered.

## Limitations

The theorem classifies whether the dimension is at most two; it does not give a closed formula for values above two. The computation needs the finite cut-off lemma and the exact exhaustive code to establish the four-object list; the numerical transcript alone would not prove completeness. The VC contrast is specific to open neighborhoods of threshold graphs. Priority has not been independently certified, and no independent scientific audit has been performed.
