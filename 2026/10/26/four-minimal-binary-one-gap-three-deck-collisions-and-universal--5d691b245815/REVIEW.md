# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proposed exact observation statistic is defined for every subsequence length one, two and three with index separation at least two. A self-contained 14-coordinate integer census of every one of the \(8192\) length-thirteen binary words is run by two separate algorithms and agrees on all signatures; only the four displayed doubletons survive. The boundary-puncture requirements are directly checked. The arbitrary-context argument partitions selections by fixed external indices, which proves every required equality for all contexts; it is not extrapolated from tests. The class partition proves the optimum code sizes, and an explicit finite symmetry calculation verifies the orbit count. Residual correctness risk is restricted to independent review of the finite exhaustive computation and the mathematical argument, not an unresolved premise.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The published first-collision threshold and the example of one doubleton are credited to Golm et al., not presented as new. Their Lemma 4 uses stronger full three-deck puncture hypotheses and a padded higher-order substitution, whereas these pairs violate those stronger hypotheses; the weaker boundary-two-deck context lemma is separately proved. Rigo et al. explain noncongruence and present two-deck, not three-deck, local rules. Related indexed findings address other channels. No accessible inspected source implies the complete four-pair census, sixteen codebooks or unrestricted context-stable three-deck substitutions. Unindexed exact precedents remain possible.

**Value — PASS.** Complete optimum decoding classes at a natural minimal informative length are intrinsically useful to finite gapped-subsequence reconstruction. More importantly, all four nontrivial pairs permit arbitrary unpadded local substitution despite general noncongruence, generating at least \(2^m\) confusable sequences for every length \(13m\); the result supplies a structural channel ambiguity mechanism beyond isolated numeric examples.

**Scope and closest literature.** Binary alphabet, one-index gap, subsequence orders up to three, full finite partition only at length thirteen. The exponential ambiguity statement is a lower bound. Primary references: Golm et al., arXiv:2201.12671 (2022), §III and Lemma 4; Rigo–Stipulanti–Whiteland, ISIT 2023, §§II and VI; Chrisnata et al., AMC 2023 (ordinary ungapped deck).
