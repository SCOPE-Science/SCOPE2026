# Review of four-point file-retrieval Pareto theorem

## Correctness
**PASS.** The proof identifies every recovery-set count in a rank-three four-column system using the unique dependence support (sizes \(2,3,4\)); it proves the small-second-coordinate obstruction \(E_2<4\Rightarrow E_1\ge4\) and supplies universal finite-field constructions for all four boundary points. The coupon-drawing Markov recurrence independently agrees with the analytic subset formula in complete binary and ternary projective censuses. All strict and non-strict inequalities and the role of zero columns are addressed.

## Originality
**PASS (self-review, with residual literature risk).** The originating 2026 block-retrieval paper gives the subset formula and dedicated-code examples. The subsequent September 2026 paper develops a mixed-column hyperbolic bound and explicitly leaves finite-length Pareto optimality of dedicated MDS codes as a question. A published one-mixed-column result proves a scalar inequality and does not classify the finite Pareto set. The four boundary points and all-code domination statement are not logically implied by those statements. Exact equivalence in unindexed material remains possible.

## Value
**PASS.** This is the full sharp tradeoff at the first redundancy level for two unequal-sized files, for every finite field. It answers a stated finite-length structural question and shows the four different undominated operating points beyond merely proving a broad reciprocal-time bound.
