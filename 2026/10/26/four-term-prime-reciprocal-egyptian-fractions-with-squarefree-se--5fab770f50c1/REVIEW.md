# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The argument is a complete classification rather than extrapolation from computations. The squarefree-semiprime condition gives a simple prime-edge graph. Reduction modulo each vertex rules out non-target leaves and forces one of two four-edge topologies. Direct algebra classifies the four-cycle uniquely and yields an iff prime-divisor parametrization of the paw. The exceptional \(p=2,3\) cases and positivity, distinctness, and uniqueness are proved. Exact fractions independently verify every four-edge set over prime vertices at most \(31\) and all constructed examples for target primes up to \(997\). The infinite claim rests on the proof, not the finite enumeration.

## Originality — PASS with residual bibliographic risk

Li's 2026 paper establishes existence for arbitrary squarefree rational targets, but not cardinality-four classification; its graph viewpoint is an input, not the derived two-topology and divisor-parameter classification. The nearest indexed three-summand result has triangle condition \(p+1=(q-1)(r-1)\), whereas the four-summand theorem requires a paw or a unique cycle and entails \((2q-p)(2r-p-2)=p(p+6)\). The 2025 few-prime paper concerns restricted prime-factor sets, not four squarefree-semiprime denominators. Exact mathematical-phrase and related-parameter searches found no four-term equivalent. A prior result under alternative terminology, an implicit corollary of a broader classification, or unread portions of adjacent literature remains a risk. The indexed three-term record was not read as a complete full manuscript.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The number of summands is a natural complexity parameter in Egyptian-fraction representations, and Li's 2026 theorem does not constrain it. The present complete four-term classification gives a usable finite prime-factor/divisor test for every prime target, exhibits a unique cyclic obstruction, and separates two natural graph topologies. It is not an arbitrary fixed numerical computation or a restatement of the existence theorem.

## Limits and nearest sources

All novelty claims are provisional. Source 1 is the squarefree-semiprime existence theorem; source 2 studies few allowed primes; source 3 is the three-term prime-reciprocal classification. No density or uniform counting theorem is asserted, and computer checks do not certify infinitely many cases.
