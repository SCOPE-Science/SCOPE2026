# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The claim concerns all eleven closed reduct groups of the countable generic digraph and ordered tuple orbits. The proof reduces each orbit calculation to its explicitly defined finite state action. The switching formulas use exact Burnside fixed-set counts, rotation uses the rank-\(n-1\) coboundary action over \(\mathbf F_3\), and graph switching uses the corresponding binary cut space with its triangle obstruction. The exceptional one- and two-vertex boundaries are handled explicitly. Independently generated finite orbits confirm all eleven formulas through five distinct tuple positions; the general theorem rests on the proofs, not solely on enumeration.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Agarwal’s 2014 preprint and 2016 paper classify the eleven groups and the elementary changes they induce; those are established premises. The exact numerical orbit profile of each group, their complete formula and the sharp four-tuple distinguishing threshold are the assessed contribution. McKay and Schweitzer analyze switching reconstruction rather than these group-orbit counts. Published profile studies for random bipartite graphs and random posets address different finite structures. Targeted semantic, alias and numerical searches did not reveal a covering theorem; a residual possibility of prior unindexed switching enumeration or an implicit presentation in longer sources remains.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The generic digraph reduct lattice is a standard and natural test case for how finitely many tuple orbits distinguish closed permutation groups. The result determines this invariant at every arity and shows that exactly four coordinates suffice to distinguish all eleven groups by a single number. Its binary/ternary cut-space explanation makes the statement structural rather than a list of sporadic computer counts.

## Scope and limitations

The count is for ordered, position-labelled tuples, either injective or allowing equality according to the stated formulas; it does not count unlabeled finite digraph isomorphism classes. The group lattice itself is used from Agarwal’s theorem, not reclassified here. Originality has residual literature-access uncertainty. Direct finite testing does not certify the infinite statement.
