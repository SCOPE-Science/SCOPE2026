# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The complete Fourier multiplication table follows by character orthogonality. For left multiplication, its matrix is a permutation-diagonal matrix whose rank is the support size of the left factor. Preservation of this rank forces every algebra automorphism to permute one-dimensional Fourier coordinate axes. Comparing all zero and nonzero multiplication entries yields the exact commuting-permutation and root-scaling conditions. Orbit analysis and the centralizer theorem for finite abelian group actions prove the entire automorphism-group formula. The prime-order numerical examples follow by arithmetic; the exact verifier checks the structural equations and small cases. This does not purport to use finite verification as a proof of the infinite family.

## Originality — PASS, with residual literature risk

Sangare–Ta (arXiv:2608.01550v2, Proposition 4.6) gives the automorphism group of \(\mathbb Z[R_n]\) for odd \(n\), not of \(\mathbb C[R_n]\); its §4.1 arbitrary-coefficient result applies to trivial quandles only. In particular, its integral affine group order \(110\) at \(n=11\) is strictly smaller than the proved complex order \(330\), so the statements are not equivalent. Elhamdadi–Ta–Virgin (arXiv:2609.03799v1) studies Fourier transforms and integral idempotents, providing a prior method but not this automorphism group. Zhang (arXiv:2610.08204v1) develops augmentation and Peirce information, neither of which implies the monomial-automorphism classification. Actual indexed searches, including equivalent Fourier/scaling terminology, did not identify a dominating theorem. The original authors' broad abstract wording and possibly unindexed complex-coefficient extensions remain risks; failed searches are not evidence of universal novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result answers a natural automorphism question suggested by the 2026 integral-quandle-algebra work, for all finite odd abelian cores simultaneously. It upgrades previously available Fourier tools to a structural and exact finite-group classification and detects genuinely new algebra symmetries: the smallest displayed difference occurs at \(R_{11}\), with a sharp index \(3\), and \(R_{17}\) has index \(136\). The wreath-product description treats all abelian-group structures, not only cyclic prime orders. This is a classification of a natural operator-invariant algebra family rather than an arbitrary numeric slice.

## Scientific limitations

The complete theorem is restricted to complex coefficients and odd finite abelian cores; it does not assert an extension to integral quandle rings or even orders. No independent scholarly audit has been carried out. Accessible records cannot exclude unindexed equivalent statements.
