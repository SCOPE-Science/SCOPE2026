# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness
**PASS.** Both base arcs admit translation coordinates and their two shared endpoints are fixed. The fiber cocycle is continuous because its oscillatory amplitude vanishes at each endpoint. The invariant lift has horizontal displacement bounded by one and vertical Birkhoff sums bounded uniformly by \(C_\beta n^{1-\beta}\), giving a singleton rotation set. For upper Bowen growth, the \(O(n)\) central time strips require \(O(n^{1-\beta})\) phase samples apiece, while far-left tails are covered by monotone terminal-phase binning and the right tail and inactive arc are cheaper; hence the upper exponent is at most \(2-\beta\). For the lower bound, \(O(n)\) different visit times separate in the base, each admitting \(O(n^{1-\beta})\) phase choices that separate by a first crossing in the fiber, rather than an unjustified unwrapped final displacement. This proves both the exact exponent and vanishing ordinary entropy. The finite script checks sampled finite identities and monotonicity but makes no infinite certification claim.

## Originality
**PASS under examined evidence, with bibliographic risk.** Corrêa (2026), Section 5.1, Problem 1 explicitly asks for zero-topological-entropy torus homeomorphisms with singleton rotation set realizing a prescribed polynomial-entropy exponent; no construction answering its open interval \((1,2)\) appears in that inspected paper. Published frozen-base torus translation examples realize fractional values on \(\mathbb T^3\) but their rotation sets are non-singleton. The rational-base scalar-skew zero–one result has entropy only zero or one and cannot imply the present moving-base example. The Morse–Smale surface integer theorem assumes isolated hyperbolic basic sets and does not cover two fixed circles. Search results may miss equivalent work on slow entropy or parabolic skew extensions; this is a specific remaining historical risk, not a claim of universal priority.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** This construction settles an entire nontrivial interval of values in an explicit 2026 open question and distinguishes fractional orbit-separation complexity from asymptotic rotation diversity on the natural minimal ambient manifold \(\mathbb T^2\). The sharp two-sided polynomial growth law arises from two independently resolvable dynamical scales (visit time and phase), rather than parameter renaming of an existing frozen-base formula. It does not claim minimal or smooth realizations.
