# Full symmetric Galois groups for generic evolution-algebra idempotents

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(n\ge2\), and let \(K=\mathbb Q(u_{ij}:1\le i,j\le n)\) be the rational-function field in \(n^2\) algebraically independent parameters. Give the \(K\)-vector space with natural basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\) the evolution-algebra multiplication
\[
e_ie_j=0\quad(i\ne j),\qquad e_i^2=\sum_{j=1}^{n}u_{ij}e_j.
\]
The Galois group acting on its \(2^n-1\) nonzero geometric idempotents is
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(K(\{x\in E\otimes_K\overline K:x^2=x\})/K)
\cong S_{2^n-1}.
\]
In particular, the generic-idempotent Galois-group assertion of Kaygorodov, Martín González and Viruel, Conjecture 2 (2026), holds in **every** dimension \(n\ge2\), not just the previously known two-dimensional case.

**Stronger specialization.** Set \(N=2^n-1\) and \(M=2^{n-1}-1\). The monic trinomial
\[
 f_{n,a}(T)=T^N+aT^M-1\in\mathbb Q(a)[T]
\]
already has geometric (and arithmetic) Galois group \(S_N\). It is the exact nonzero-idempotent polynomial of an explicit one-parameter family of **perfect** evolution algebras.

## Assumptions and scope

The characteristic is zero. The parameters of the generic algebra are algebraically independent over \(\mathbb Q\). The Galois group means the automorphism group of the splitting field, with its natural faithful permutation action on the distinct geometric idempotents. The term “nonzero” excludes the identically defined zero idempotent. No claim about a fixed arbitrary specialization or positive characteristic is made.

For \(n=2\), the special family below means \(e_1^2=e_2+ae_1\), \(e_2^2=e_1\). For \(n\ge3\), put
\[
e_i^2=e_{i+1}\ (1\le i\le n-2),\qquad
 e_{n-1}^2=e_n+ae_1,\qquad e_n^2=e_1.
\]
All products of distinct basis elements vanish. Its structure matrix has determinant \((-1)^{n-1}\), independently of \(a\), so the family is perfect. Here \(N=2M+1\) and \(\gcd(N,M)=1\).

## Proof

**1. The idempotent system in the special family.** Write an idempotent as \(x=\sum_{i=1}^n x_ie_i\). The equations in coordinates \(2,\ldots,n\) give successively
\[
x_i=x_1^{2^{i-1}}\qquad(2\le i\le n).
\]
The first equation is \(x_1=ax_{n-1}^2+x_n^2\). Thus every nonzero solution is uniquely determined by \(t=x_1\ne0\) satisfying
\[
t=t^{2^n}+at^{2^{n-1}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t^N+at^M-1=0.
\]
The zero solution is separate. Since \(\det(M_E)\ne0\), the family remains perfect even at branch parameters.

**2. The trinomial has full symmetric geometric monodromy.** Introduce the rational map of projective lines
\[
\alpha:\mathbb P^1_t\longrightarrow\mathbb P^1_a,
\qquad a=\alpha(t)=t^{-M}-t^{N-M}.
\]
Its degree is \(N\): the rational function has coprime numerator and denominator and pole orders \(M\) at \(t=0\) and \(N-M\) at \(t=\infty\), summing to \(N\). Its finite critical points solve
\[
\alpha'(t)=-t^{-M-1}\bigl(M+(N-M)t^N\bigr)=0,
\qquad t^N=-\frac{M}{N-M}.
\]
There are exactly \(N\) such points. None is zero or infinite, all are simple critical points, and their critical values are
\[
\alpha(t)=\frac{N}{N-M}\,t^{-M}.
\]
Two such values coincide only if the corresponding quotient of critical points is simultaneously an \(N\)-th and \(M\)-th root of unity; \(\gcd(N,M)=1\) forces the quotient to be \(1\). Hence the \(N\) finite branch values are distinct, and each finite branch point has **exactly one simple ramification**, with branch permutation a transposition. All other branch behavior lies over \(a=\infty\), with two source points of ramification indices \(M\) and \(N-M\) (a ramification index \(1\) is harmless).

The covering of the complex sphere with its finitely many branch values removed has connected source: it comes from the connected projective line with finitely many points deleted. Therefore its monodromy group on the \(N\) sheets is transitive. In the fundamental group of the punctured sphere, a positively oriented loop around infinity is the inverse of the product of loops around **all** finite branch values. Its monodromy is consequently contained in the subgroup generated by the \(N\) finite transpositions; that subgroup already equals the full monodromy group. A subgroup of \(S_N\) generated by transpositions is a product of symmetric groups on the connected components of its transposition graph. Transitivity forces that graph to be connected. Thus the monodromy group is \(S_N\). The characteristic-zero correspondence between a branched cover and the Galois group of its function-field normal closure identifies this group with the geometric Galois group of \(f_{n,a}\) over \(\mathbb C(a)\). The arithmetic group over \(\mathbb Q(a)\) contains that group and is contained in \(S_N\), so it also equals \(S_N\).

**3. Passing from one parameter to independent structure constants.** For a structure matrix \(U=(u_{ij})\), the idempotent equations are \(F_j(x)=\sum_i u_{ij}x_i^2-x_j=0\). On the open set where \(\det U\ne0\), the simultaneous homogenization \(\sum_i u_{ij}x_i^2-zx_j=0\) has no projective solutions on \(z=0\): invertibility yields \(x_i^2=0\) for every \(i\). Thus the affine intersection is finite, of total scheme-theoretic length \(2^n\) by Bézout. The zero point is simple because the Jacobian of \((F_j)_j\) at zero is \(-I_n\). Removing that section leaves a finite family of length \(N\).

There is a nonempty Zariski-open subset \(\mathcal U\) of the invertible-matrix locus over which the \(N\) remaining solutions are all simple: the sparse family at \(a=0\) has distinct nonzero roots of \(T^N-1\), so such a point exists. Over \(\mathcal U\), the nonzero-idempotent solutions form a finite étale cover of degree \(N\), hence a monodromy representation into \(S_N\). Pulling this cover back to the sparse one-parameter line, away from the finitely many critical values, gives exactly the degree-\(N\) cover from Step 2. Every permutation induced by a loop on that parameter line is induced by its image loop in \(\mathcal U(\mathbb C)\). Therefore the generic geometric monodromy **contains** the full \(S_N\) already obtained on the line, and so equals \(S_N\). Again the arithmetic Galois group over \(K\) cannot be larger, and must equal \(S_N\). This proves the theorem. \(\square\)

## Verification

The independent finite-field splitting tests in `verify.py` check the three-dimensional rational specialization \(a=3\), whose polynomial is \(T^7+3T^3-1\). Modulo \(2\) it is irreducible. Modulo \(7\) it splits into irreducible factors of degrees \(2\) and \(5\):
\[
(T^2+T+3)(T^5-T^4-2T^3-2T^2-3T+2).
\]
Both reductions are squarefree. These imply Frobenius elements with cycle types \((7)\) and \((2)(5)\); the fifth power of the latter is a transposition. Since the group is transitive of prime degree \(7\), the conjugates of that transposition by a \(7\)-cycle generate \(S_7\). This yields a separate exact rational example with Galois group \(S_7\). The checker also tests \(\gcd(2^n-1,2^{n-1}-1)=1\) for \(2\le n\le24\). The all-dimensional proof uses Euclid’s algorithm and complex covering monodromy, not finite testing.

Run `python3 verify.py` from a directory containing the file; expected last line begins `VERIFY_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work

Kaygorodov, Martín González and Viruel (arXiv:2610.22248v1, 15 October 2026), Conjecture 2, formulate precisely the full \(S_{2^n-1}\) generic Galois-group problem and establish the case \(n=2\), but leave \(n=3\) and higher open. Their Theorem 18 covers existence and the \(2^n\) scheme-length framework, neither of which establishes full monodromy. García-Martínez and Pérez-Rodríguez (2026) investigate generic quadratic-system solutions to deduce existence and subalgebra consequences, not a Galois-group classification. The result here adds a sparse perfect family with full monodromy and a specialization argument resolving the generic group in all dimensions. General facts about monodromy of rational functions and the transposition-graph lemma are classical ingredients, not claimed as new.

## Limitations

This is a characteristic-zero generic statement and an explicit rational-function-field family. It does not assert that every individual rational structure matrix has maximal Galois group; specializations can have smaller groups. The finite-field experiments are supplementary, not a proof of infinite-dimensional generality. Earlier or concurrent monodromy results outside the inspected evolution-algebra literature could imply this result; this is a residual literature risk.
