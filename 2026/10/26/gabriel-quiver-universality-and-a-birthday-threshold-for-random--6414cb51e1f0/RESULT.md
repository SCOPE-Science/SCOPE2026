# Gabriel-quiver universality and a birthday threshold for random generators of finite split basic algebras

## Finding

Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\), where \(q\) is any prime power. For a finite-dimensional unital **split basic associative** \(k\)-algebra \(A\), let \(J\) be its Jacobson radical and let \(A/J\cong k^v\), with \(v\ge1\). Choose a complete family of primitive orthogonal idempotents \(e_1,\ldots,e_v\), and set
\[
m_{ij}=\dim_k e_i(J/J^2)e_j\qquad(1\le i,j\le v).
\]
For integers \(m\ge0\) and \(d\in\mathbb Z\), define
\[
\beta_q(m,d)=\begin{cases}1,&m=0,\\ \displaystyle\prod_{h=0}^{m-1}(1-q^{h-d}),&1\le m\le d,\\0,&m>0\text{ and }d<m.\end{cases}
\]
For any integer \(r\ge0\), sample \(r\) independent uniform elements of \(A\); the subalgebra they generate is required to contain the identity and the prescribed copy of \(k\). **The exact generation probability** is
\[
\boxed{\Pr(\langle X_1,\ldots,X_r\rangle_k=A)
= \frac{(q^r)_{\underline v}}{q^{rv}}
\prod_{i=1}^v\beta_q(m_{ii},r)
\prod_{i\ne j}\beta_q(m_{ij},r-1),}
\qquad (Q)_{\underline v}=\prod_{a=0}^{v-1}(Q-a).
\]
The expression is interpreted as zero when \(v>q^r\). This law depends only on \(q\), the number of simple summands, and the loop and nonloop arrow multiplicities of the Gabriel quiver; it is **independent of all relations in the second and higher radical layers**. In fact the product depends only on the two multisets of multiplicities (loops and ordered nonloops), not their placement.

The least number of algebra generators is
\[
\boxed{d(A)=\max(\rho,\lambda_0,\lambda_1),}
\]
where \(\rho\) is the least nonnegative integer satisfying \(q^\rho\ge v\), \(\lambda_0=\max_i m_{ii}\), and \(\lambda_1=1+\max_{i\ne j:m_{ij}>0}m_{ij}\) when any nonloop arrow exists, or zero otherwise.

**Birthday window.** Fix \(q\) and integer \(t\). For each integer \(k\to\infty\), let \(A_k\) be any such algebra with \(v_k=q^k\) simple summands, all arrow multiplicities at most a fixed \(M\), and total arrow multiplicity \(\sum_{i,j}m_{ij}=O(v_k)\). For \(r_k=2k+t\) (eventually nonnegative), uniformly across these algebras with a common sparsity constant,
\[
\boxed{\lim_{k\to\infty}\Pr(\langle X_1,\ldots,X_{r_k}\rangle_k=A_k)
=\exp(-\tfrac12q^{-t}).}
\]

## Assumptions and scope

All algebras are finite-dimensional, associative, unital, and split basic over \(\mathbb F_q\); no commutativity, acyclicity or bound on radical nilpotence length is assumed. The \(r\) samples are independent and uniform in the additive set of \(A\). The listed \(m_{ij}\) are measured in the first radical layer only. Empty products equal one. The zero-generator case is the unital ground-field subalgebra.

## Proof

**1. Reduction to the first radical layer.** Let \(B\subseteq A\) be the unital subalgebra generated by given elements. If its image in \(A/J^2\) is all of \(A/J^2\), then \(A=B+J^2\). Because the semisimple images span \(A/J\), for every \(x\in J\) there is \(y\in B\cap J\) with \(x-y\in J^2\), hence \(J=(B\cap J)+J^2\). Write \(I=B\cap J\). In each product of \(s\) terms from \(J=I+J^2\), terms involving a factor from \(J^2\) lie in \(J^{s+1}\); thus \(J^s\subseteq I^s+J^{s+1}\), and \(I^s\subseteq I\) for positive \(s\). Starting with \(J\subseteq I+J^2\), substitution gives \(J\subseteq I+J^3\subseteq\cdots\subseteq I+J^N=I\) for sufficiently large \(N\), since \(J\) is nilpotent. Therefore \(J\subseteq B\), and \(A=B\). The converse is immediate. Every generation event is thus determined by the uniform images in \(A/J^2\).

**2. Square-zero model and vertex collisions.** By separability of \(A/J\cong k^v\) and the Wedderburn–Malcev theorem, \(A/J^2\cong S\oplus W\), with \(S=k^v\), \(W=J/J^2\), and \(W^2=0\). Its \(S\)-bimodule decomposes as the direct sum of \(W_{ij}=e_iWe_j\), of dimension \(m_{ij}\). Let \(a_i=(a_{1i},\ldots,a_{ri})\in k^r\) be the diagonal-coordinate vectors of the \(r\) samples. These vectors are independent uniform. Their generated subalgebra is all of \(k^v\) precisely when all \(a_i\) are distinct: polynomial interpolation on the finite set of distinct points gives the primitive coordinate projections, while equal points cannot be separated. This has probability \((q^r)_{\underline v}/q^{rv}\).

**3. The one-direction loss for a nonloop arrow space.** Condition on distinct \(a_i\). The subalgebra \(B\) generated in \(S\oplus W\) maps onto \(S\). Its radical is \(B\cap W\), and \(B/(B\cap W)\cong S\). Separability supplies an embedded complement \(S'\subseteq B\); square-zero complement conjugacy provides \(z\in W\) such that conjugation by \(1+z\) takes \(S'\) to canonical \(S\). For each ordered pair \((i,j)\), conjugating all generators changes their \(W_{ij}\)-components by a common vector \(z_{ij}\in W_{ij}\) multiplied at sample \(\ell\) by the scalar \(a_{\ell i}-a_{\ell j}\), up to a uniform overall sign. Thus the gauge subspace of the \(r\)-tuple of \(W_{ij}\)-components has dimension zero when \(i=j\), and one when \(i\ne j\), because the distinctness assumption makes \(a_i-a_j\ne0\). After an invertible coordinate change among the \(r\) sample positions, exactly \(r\) independent uniform vectors in \(W_{ii}\), or \(r-1\) independent uniform vectors in \(W_{ij}\) for \(i\ne j\), govern the span.

Once the conjugated \(B\) contains all primitive idempotents, its radical part in \(W_{ij}\) is exactly the span of those vectors: multiplying by idempotents isolates each component, whereas \(W^2=0\) generates no additional radical vectors. Consequently the conditional event \(B=S\oplus W\) is precisely full row rank in all these independent blocks. Equivalently, for a nonloop pair the classes of the sample radical vectors in the quotient of \(W_{ij}^r\) by the common difference-label direction determine generation; this quotient is uniformly distributed even when a chosen conjugating element depends on the samples. The full-rank probability of \(m\) vectors drawn independently in \(k^d\) (equivalently an \(m\)-by-\(d\) random matrix) is \(\beta_q(m,d)\). Conditioning on vertex labels preserves independence across all \(W_{ij}\) blocks. Multiplication of these rank probabilities and the vertex-separation probability gives the claimed formula. The generator threshold follows by requiring each factor to be positive.

**4. Birthday limit.** Set \(v=q^k\), \(r=2k+t\) and \(Q=q^r\). Uniformly for fixed \(q,t\), the logarithm of the diagonal-separation factor is
\[
\sum_{a=0}^{v-1}\log(1-a/Q)
=-\frac{v(v-1)}{2Q}+O\left(\frac{v^3}{Q^2}\right)
=-\tfrac12q^{-t}+o(1).
\]
If every \(m_{ij}\le M\), the failure probability for a rank factor of type \(\beta_q(m,d)\) is at most \(\sum_{h=0}^{m-1}q^{h-d}\le m q^{M-1-d}\), once \(d\ge M\). Applying the union bound to all loops (\(d=r\)) and nonloops (\(d=r-1\)), and using \(\sum_{ij}m_{ij}=O(v)\), bounds the combined arrow failure by \(O(vq^{M-r})=O(q^{-k})\). Multiplying with the vertex factor proves the birthday limit and its claimed uniformity.

## Verification

The standalone \(\texttt{verify_generation.py}\) enumerates the complete generating tuples in thirteen small examples over prime fields by iterating all elements, computing their unital subalgebra closure from the multiplication table of a truncated path algebra, and comparing the exact count with the product formula. The samples include loops, parallel arrows, a two-step path with and without its length-two products, different arrow orientations, and two finite field orders. All thirteen tests pass. These experiments do not prove arbitrary dimension or prime-power characteristic; the proof above does.

## Relationship to prior work

Sercombe and Shalev treat random generation of finite associative algebras, including general bounds on generator numbers and probabilities, but their inspected introduction and sections on maximal subalgebras and minimal generators do not give the displayed exact probability for arbitrary split basic algebras or a sparse-quiver birthday limit [1]. Iovanov and Sistko classify maximal subalgebras of pointed/basic algebras using merged vertex pairs and codimension-one subspaces of arrow spaces (Theorem 4.1, Proposition 4.2), supplying important structural context; those results do not themselves provide a conditional independent-rank product or birthday window [2]. Kravchenko, Mazur and Petrenko give generator probabilities and related counts for simple and semisimple matrix-algebra settings, not the present radical-dependent graph universal law [3]. Previously described finite étale birthday phenomena cover the vertex-distinctness factor alone, and square-zero local-algebra rank laws address one simple residue block; the proposed theorem links these phenomena across arbitrary split basic quivers and all their admissible relations.

## Limitations

The theorem is restricted to split basic algebras with residue field equal to the coefficient field at every simple vertex; division-field residue factors and matrix simple blocks are not covered. The asymptotic limit assumes bounded individual arrow multiplicity and total multiplicity linear in the number of vertices; denser quivers can have additional rank-failure transitions. The finite verifier covers only listed small prime-field instances. The theorem uses standard semisimple-complement conjugacy and nilpotent-filtration facts, not a computational certification of infinite families. An equivalent expression derived from maximal-subalgebra incidence or Frattini-type enumeration under different terminology remains a publication-priority risk.
