## correctness

The exact Weibull reduction xF_p(x)/2=E sin(xU^(1/p)) for U~Exp(1) yields Q_p(ps)=Gamma(1+is)+is Gamma''(1+is)/(2p)+O_C(p^-2). Integrability of logarithmic exponential moments justifies uniformity for s in compact [0,C]. Nonvanishing of Gamma and |theta'_p|=O(1/p) imply strictly increasing phase h_p=x+theta_p and hence the first floor(Cp/pi) positive zeros are simple and exhaustive in their interval. The negative second-order correction -s Re(Gamma''/Gamma)/(2p) and the Phi Phi' term follow from implicit phase expansion. The digamma series gives Phi''(s)>0 for s>0 and a unique positive gamma-phase zero s*=1.805547071605. Centering the Weibull law with log-Exp third cumulant -2 zeta(3) yields fixed-k correction -zeta(3)(k pi)^3/(3p^3) after mean scaling. Independent quadrature and root finding at (p,k)=(24,1),(24,5),(48,15),(80,24) had deviations 3.69e-5,5.44e-4,1.29e-4,3.34e-5 from the two-term prediction. The analytic phase monotonicity, not finite roots, establishes the theorem.

## originality

PASS

Paris (2012) and Senouf (1995/1996) treat large-frequency zeros at fixed super-Gaussian exponent; they do not directly imply the uniform simultaneous p→infinity and k~p Gamma-phase quantization. Equivalent Weibull characteristic-function and Fourier/Wright formulations were compared. The full Ki–Kim (2007) source could not be read because authorized institutional retrieval reported disconnected VPN; possible prior uniform asymptotics are retained as genuine priority risk. Resultary searches found only adjacent Fourier zero results, not a demonstrated dominating theorem.
