# Scientific review: Gamma-phase crossover for super-Gaussian Fourier zeros

## Correctness — PASS
The exact Weibull integration-by-parts identity, the uniform Gamma expansion including its first correction, and the strictly increasing total phase prove that the first linearly many positive roots exist, are unique in their phase cells, and are simple. The digamma derivative sum yields strict convexity and uniqueness of the phase crossing. The centered Weibull third cumulant yields the fixed-index \(\zeta(3)\) coefficient. Direct Fourier integrals and the phase predictions have been numerically cross-checked, but no infinite assertion depends on sampling.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Senouf's 1995/1996 fixed-order asymptotic theory and Paris's 2012 Wright-function treatment of the same integrals were checked against the candidate statement, including parameters and the direction of limits. They treat large frequency at fixed exponent and do not imply a uniform simultaneous large-exponent / linearly growing index law. Targeted research-record and literature searches found no covering statement. Ki and Kim (2007), and older material in probabilistic Weibull language, are residual priority risks because the former full text was not inspected and search coverage is not exhaustive.

## Value — PASS
The finding quantifies the first \(O(p)\) Fourier zeros while the generalized Gaussian approaches a rectangular cutoff, a natural and nontrivial shape/frequency regime. Its unique phase-sign reversal and universal gamma function distinguish it from elementary pointwise sinc convergence; the third cumulant identifies the first correction not absorbed by mean scaling.

## Scientific limitations
The theorem is confined to the positive real zeros in the linearly growing index range for each fixed window coefficient. No remote or complex zero census and no independent priority certification are asserted.
