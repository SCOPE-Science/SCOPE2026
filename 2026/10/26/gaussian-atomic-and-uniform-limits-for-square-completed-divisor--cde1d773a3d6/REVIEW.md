# Scientific assessment: Gaussian, atomic, and uniform limits for square-completed divisor ratios

## Correctness — PASS

The single accepted theorem is the joint weak limit of the Gaussian logarithmic divisor-ratio fluctuation, the prime-power correction, and the fractional distance to the next square, including two exact atoms for the correction. Exact block sizes, the local identity \(D(p^{2a})=(2a+1)/2\), and the deterministic correction decomposition were reconstructed. The infinite joint law follows from fixed-progressions Erdős–Kac, finite-prime valuation convergence, an explicit uniform \(L^1\) tail \(2\sum_{p>B}1/(p(p-1))\), and conditional equispaced square-gap grids. The full-square cutoff is extended to arbitrary \(X\) by discarding at most \(2\lfloor\sqrt X\rfloor+1\) observations. The two atom calculations come from independent geometric valuations and have no unproved independence assumption. Exact-integer finite verification independently reproduced the defining arithmetic through the stated finite ranges. The Gaussian and Euler limits are supported by proof rather than by finite sampling.

## Originality — PASS, with bibliographic risk

Mihoub, arXiv:2602.20808v1 (February 24, 2026), defines the same shifted-square statistic and gives a summatory-mean assertion; its sections 1–2 do not establish the joint law or the two correction atoms. The accepted proof does not rely on that source's asymptotic or its analytic estimates. Levy–Squillace, arXiv:2405.06860, covers scalar Erdős–Kac convergence under perturbed sampling but not a joint Gaussian/atomic/square-phase limit. Classical Erdős–Wintner and Tenenbaum–Verwee's effective version establish the existence and structure of additive marginal distributions, accounting for the correction coordinate **alone**. Tenenbaum–Verwee's Theorem 1.3 is specifically for strongly additive functions with continuous limiting distribution; the present correction vanishes at primes, is nonzero at their squares, and has an atomic law, so that result does not directly imply the conditional independence asserted here. The source does discuss possible extensions, which is a genuine originality risk. No equivalent source or covering scientific statement was found in the targeted literature and indexed-record comparison, but this does not prove global uniqueness or replace independent priority assessment.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The investigated quantity was recently introduced for its summatory behavior. A first moment alone does not characterize its typical order, fine additive correction, or the location of the original integer inside a square-completion block. The joint theorem establishes a product limit combining three different types of behavior—normal, discrete, and uniform—with **two explicit intrinsic arithmetic atoms** of masses \(6/\pi^2\) and \((6/\pi^2)\sum_p1/(p(p+1))\). The product structure provides conditional predictions that separate genuinely local squarefull effects from bulk prime-count fluctuations and the geometric shift phase. This is more information than a scalar central-limit or scalar additive-limit assertion. It is a natural structural classification of the limiting behavior of the entire observable, not an arbitrary finite sample statistic.

## Limitations and nearest antecedents

The general Erdős–Kac and Erdős–Wintner components are established mathematics; novelty is not claimed for those marginal theorems. Stronger unindexed joint additive limit theorems might subsume the first two coordinates, and an extension of the conditional theorem in Tenenbaum–Verwee could reduce the proof burden. None of the inspected primary statements provides the entire three-way square-shift limit or its two first atoms. The result establishes no effective convergence rate. Its numerical verifier does not validate infinite asymptotics. No independent research audit or priority determination has been performed.
