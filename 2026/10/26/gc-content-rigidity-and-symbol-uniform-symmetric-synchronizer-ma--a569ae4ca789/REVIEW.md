# Scientific assessment: GC-balanced synchronization marker tables

## Correctness — PASS
The two-letter complement has exactly eight two-element orbits on the sixteen ordered input pairs. The proof lists all permitted pairs of output values on each orbit, and the inequality constraints are independent between orbits. The resulting eight-factor polynomial is therefore the exact complete generating function, not a heuristic. Replacing all C/G variables by one indeterminate proves that the only GC occupancies are seven, eight, and nine, with multiplicities 512, 1280, and 512. Exact coefficient extraction gives 80 symbol-uniform tables and 16 symbol-uniform transpose-symmetric tables. A second calculation independently enumerates all 2304 admissible lookup tables, checks all defining inequalities, and agrees with every coefficient. The supplied symmetric balanced witness meets all constraints. No result about individual codeword GC balance is inferred from a uniform-input calculation.

## Originality — PASS, with stated residual risk
Choi's 2026 paper specifies the lookup-table admissibility conditions in Theorem 10 and provides one quaternary table in Definition 11. It does not report the GC occupancy law, full output-symbol balance, or the transpose-symmetric balanced count in the inspected technical sections. A closely related 2026 public mathematical record, *Exact enumeration and alphabet threshold for Choi synchronizer maps* (identifier 406f1f4dc0ca), proves the unrestricted 2304 total by an orbit/cycle-coloring argument. Its complete RESULT text was compared: this total does not imply the refined three-class GC histogram, the 80 exact output-uniform designs, or the 16 simultaneously transpose-symmetric designs. The new contribution is restricted to these refinements, not the already known total or map existence threshold. GC-balanced polar coding literature concerns whole codes, not these sixteen-entry tables. Unindexed equivalent weighted enumerators remain a risk, as does the methodological proximity to the earlier unweighted enumeration.

## Value — PASS
The precise C/G marker bias and the availability of lookup rules with uniform nucleotide frequencies are directly motivated by DNA storage GC-content constraints. The central marker is inserted twice in the underlying construction, so lookup-symbol frequency is a meaningful design statistic under a stated flank distribution. The universal one-entry deviation bound and the exact 80/16 design counts supply a complete answer for the canonical four-symbol alphabet, including a natural input-order symmetry appropriate for considering reversed flanks. The result is not presented as a stronger decoder, a biochemical experiment, or a guarantee for arbitrary nonuniform input symbols. The known unweighted 2304 count cannot select a balanced marker design.
