# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** The monomiality lemma follows directly from invertibility of the structure matrix. For a nontrivial diagonal monomial map, the multiplication constraints force at least one zero in each matrix column; for a permutation moving \(k\) indices, the induced action on ordered matrix coordinates reduces the number of free entries by at least \(k(2n-k)/2\). These counts, the finite-field invertibility proportion, Burnside's lemma, and the two-range sum estimate prove all quantitative bounds. Finite exhaustive computations and exact rational inequalities independently corroborate the low-dimensional cases. The proof, not the experiment, handles arbitrary finite fields and all dimensions in its domain.

**Originality (PASS against inspected sources, with residual risk).** Wei–Zou's full paper provides exact Burnside orbit enumeration and individual low-dimensional class counts, but does not state the fixed-field, growing-dimension concentration of monomial stabilizers or its quantitative asymptotic class count. Sriwongsa–Zou study automorphism-group structure and special highly symmetric families. The later evolution-algebra survey explicitly poses broader counting and random-automorphism questions, but not an equivalent settled fixed-field perfect-algebra theorem. Nearby previous calculations for the binary-field growing-dimension regime and for growing fields at fixed dimension do not imply the \(q\ge3\) fixed-field conclusion. A hidden or differently phrased asymptotic in other literature remains possible.

**Value (PASS).** The result gives a leading asymptotic for a natural isomorphism-class count that previous work expressed as an expensive exact orbit sum and computed in small dimensions. It also proves typical automorphism rigidity under both labeled-matrix and uniform-class sampling, explicitly separating the two measures. This resolves a motivated dense perfect-algebra special case of the broader random-evolution-algebra and counting-asymptotics directions.

**Limitations and risk.** The bound is not a sharp asymptotic for the exceptional nonrigid probability. It is for fixed \(q\ge3\), not simultaneously growing \(q\), and excludes singular structure matrices. The cited primary paper's full relevant sections and the survey's pertinent statements were inspected; no exhaustive publication-priority guarantee follows from literature retrieval.
