# Review of the graded automorphism fixed-space census

## Correctness

The free class-two Lie algebra has automorphisms uniquely parametrized by a three-dimensional invertible linear map and an arbitrary map from generators to their exterior square. The action on the derived ideal is its exterior square. In dimension three, exterior duality changes the derived fixed-space dimension into the eigenspace dimension at the determinant. The determinant-fiber subspace incidences, including the exceptional full-dimensional compatibility condition, admit two exact Gaussian-binomial inversions. The closed polynomial follows by finite algebraic simplification. The independent direct finite-field enumerator reproduces the full bivariate profile over fields of orders \(2\), \(3\), and \(4\).

## Originality

Full text of Benito–Roldán-López (arXiv:1911.01897) supplies the automorphism decomposition in characteristic zero but does not give finite-field fixed-space statistics. Full text of Burde–Dekimpe (arXiv:2604.27916) studies characteristic-zero existence conditions; no rank-three finite-field bivariate enumeration appears. The closely related accessible published-finding corpus Heisenberg record gives a different, three-dimensional, two-generator case. Searches also covered the exterior-square, determinant-eigenvalue, derangement and \(\operatorname{GL}_3\) formulations, and published mathematical sources. The remaining priority risk is that a known representation-theoretic matrix enumeration, or unavailable full text of the Heisenberg record, subsumes the result in another language; a global novelty proof is not asserted.

## Value

The two characteristic graded factors of the first non-Heisenberg free class-two Lie algebra are natural automorphism modules. Their joint fixed-space law detects when the entire automorphism is fixed-point-free, supplies an exact count for all finite fields, and exhibits a genuine odd/even-field correction caused by the \(-I\) determinant stratum. At field order two the nonzero count contrasts with the smaller free algebra. This is a complete, interpretable distribution of a structural invariant rather than an arbitrary isolated matrix statistic. The actual fixed-space dimension for non-free cases, and higher free ranks, remain outside scope.
