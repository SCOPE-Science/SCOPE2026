# Graph-Tutte census of rank-one Rota–Baxter operators on radical-square-zero quiver algebras

## Finding
Let \(Q=(V,E)\) be any finite quiver, including loops and parallel arrows, with \(n=|V|\geq1\) and \(m=|E|\). Let \(A=\mathbb F_qQ/J^2\), where \(J\) is the ideal generated by the arrows and \(q\) is any prime power. A weight-zero Rota–Baxter operator is a linear map \(R:A\to A\) satisfying
\[
R(x)R(y)=R(R(x)y+xR(y))\quad(x,y\in A).
\]
We count operators of **vector-space rank exactly one**, without identifying them under algebra automorphisms.

Make the bipartite *separated-support graph* \(G_Q\) with a left copy and a right copy of \(V\), and for each arrow \(e:s\to t\) an edge between the left copy of \(s\) and the right copy of \(t\). For an arrow subset \(S\subseteq E\), let \(r(S)\) be the graphic-matroid rank of \(S\) in \(G_Q\), namely the number of incident vertices minus the number of nonempty connected components of the support subgraph. For a vertex subset \(B\subseteq V\), put
\[
u(B)=\#\{e:s\to t:s\in B,\ t\notin B\},\qquad
v(B)=\#\{e:s\to t:s\notin B,\ t\in B\},\qquad c(B)=u(B)+v(B).
\]
Then the exact count of nonzero rank-one weight-zero Rota–Baxter operators is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal R_Q(q)=
\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq E}
(q-1)^{|S|}\big(q^{n+m-r(S)}-1\big)
+\sum_{B\subseteq V}q^{c(B)}\big(q^{u(B)}+q^{v(B)}-2\big).}
\tag{1}
\]
The first summand is determined by a single evaluation of the Tutte polynomial of \(G_Q\). More precisely, if \(r=r(E)\), then
\[
\sum_{S\subseteq E}(q-1)^{|S|}q^{-r(S)}
=\left(\frac{q-1}{q}\right)^r
T_{G_Q}\!\left(\frac{2q-1}{q-1},q\right).
\tag{2}
\]
The second summand is a two-spin, directed-cut partition function. Thus (1) separates a matroidal square-zero contribution from an orientation-sensitive idempotent contribution.

For the linearly oriented path with \(n\geq1\) vertices, \(1\to2\to\cdots\to n\), define polynomials
\[
F_1(a,b)=2,\qquad F_2(a,b)=2+a+b,\qquad
F_{j+2}(a,b)=2F_{j+1}(a,b)+(ab-1)F_j(a,b)\ (j\geq1).
\]
For \(n=1\), interpret the arrow contribution as zero. For every \(n\geq1\),
\[
\boxed{\mathcal R_n(q)=
\frac{q^n(2q-1)^{n-1}-q^{2n-1}-q^{n-1}+1}{q-1}
+2\big(F_n(q^2,q)-F_n(q,q)\big).}
\tag{3}
\]
The statistic detects orientation: for the three-vertex linearly oriented path and the outward fork \(2\leftarrow1\to3\), respectively,
\[
\boxed{\mathcal R_{\mathrm{fork}}(q)-\mathcal R_{\mathrm{line}}(q)
=2q^2(q-1)^2>0.}
\tag{4}
\]
Both quivers have the same underlying undirected tree and the same number of arrows; their nilpotent-image contributions in (1) coincide. The difference comes entirely from directed idempotent cuts.

## Assumptions and scope
All fields in the enumeration are finite fields; there is no characteristic restriction. The quiver may have loops, oriented cycles, and parallel arrows, because all products of two arrows vanish in \(A\). Formula (3) concerns the radical-square-zero quotient of the path algebra, not the full upper-triangular matrix algebra when \(n\geq3\). The counted maps have rank one as linear operators on the entire \((n+m)\)-dimensional vector space, and the zero operator is excluded.

## Proof
**1. A rank-one structural reduction.** Write a nonzero rank-one linear map as \(R(x)=f(x)a\), with \(0\ne f\in A^*\) and \(0\ne a\in A\). Substitution into the Rota–Baxter identity gives
\[
f(x)f(y)a^2=\big(f(x)f(ay)+f(y)f(xa)\big)a.
\]
Choose \(x_0\) with \(f(x_0)=1\). Inserting elements of \(\ker f\) in either argument shows that \(f\circ L_a=\lambda f\) and \(f\circ R_a=\mu f\) for scalars \(\lambda,\mu\), where \(L_a(x)=ax\), \(R_a(x)=xa\). Then necessarily \(a^2=(\lambda+\mu)a\). Conversely, these three conditions imply the identity. This reduction is standard for rank-one maps; the new work here is the all-quiver enumeration and its Tutte/cut structure.

**2. Two disjoint types.** If \(a^2=0\), then \(L_a\) and \(R_a\) are square-zero and hence \(\lambda=\mu=0\). The allowed functionals are exactly the nonzero elements of \((aA+Aa)^\perp\). If \(a^2=\tau a\) for \(\tau\ne0\), normalize uniquely to the nonzero idempotent \(p=a/\tau\). Because the dual left and right multiplication maps by \(p\) are commuting idempotents, the eigenvalues must be \((1,0)\) or \((0,1)\). The functional then lies in the dual of exactly one of the Peirce spaces \(pA(1-p)\) and \((1-p)Ap\), annihilating the other three Peirce spaces. The two nonzero functional sets are disjoint. Scaling \((a,f)\mapsto(ta,t^{-1}f)\) for \(t\in\mathbb F_q^\times\) represents the same operator, and these are its only nonzero rank-one presentations.

**3. The square-zero contribution.** The semisimple quotient of \(A\) is \(\mathbb F_q^V\), hence \(a^2=0\) forces \(a\in J\); conversely every radical element squares to zero. For \(a=\sum_{e\in E}a_ee\), let \(S=\{e:a_e\ne0\}\). The space \(aA+Aa\) is spanned by the weighted outgoing and incoming arrow sums indexed by source and target vertices. Its rank is the rank of the vertex-edge incidence map for the bipartite graph \(G_Q[S]\); dividing each nonzero arrow coordinate by \(a_e\) shows this rank is \(r(S)\), in every characteristic. For each support \(S\ne\varnothing\), there are \((q-1)^{|S|}\) choices of \(a\), and then \(q^{n+m-r(S)}-1\) nonzero choices of \(f\). Dividing by the \(q-1\) rescalings yields exactly the first sum in (1). The standard subset expansion of the Tutte polynomial, with \(x-1=q/(q-1)\) and \(y-1=q-1\), gives (2), including graph loops and parallel edges after they become ordinary edges or multiedges of \(G_Q\).

**4. The idempotent contribution.** The diagonal coefficients of an idempotent \(p\) are \(\varepsilon_v\in\{0,1\}\). In \(p^2=p\), the coefficient on \(e:s\to t\) is \((\varepsilon_s+\varepsilon_t-1)a_e=0\), so it is free exactly when the endpoints have different labels. Therefore there are \(q^{c(B)}\) idempotents having diagonal-one set \(B\). Every such idempotent is conjugate by a unit \(1+j\), \(j\in J\), to its diagonal part: on each crossing arrow the conjugation changes the coefficient by a nonzero sign times the corresponding coordinate of \(j\). Thus \(\dim pA(1-p)=u(B)\) and \(\dim (1-p)Ap=v(B)\), regardless of its off-diagonal coefficients. The allowable nonzero functionals number \((q^{u(B)}-1)+(q^{v(B)}-1)\), giving the second sum in (1). Idempotents and square-zero nonzero image generators cannot overlap.

**5. The path evaluation.** For a linearly oriented path, the bipartite graph \(G_Q\) is a matching with \(n-1\) edges, so \(r(S)=|S|\). The first sum in (1) reduces by the binomial theorem to the first term in (3). A binary vertex labeling has \(u\) transitions \(1\to0\) and \(v\) transitions \(0\to1\); summing \(a^ub^v\) is \(F_n(a,b)=(1,1)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&b\\a&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)^{n-1}(1,1)^T\). Cayley–Hamilton yields the displayed recurrence. Complementing the binary labels interchanges \(u\) and \(v\), giving the second term of (3). For three vertices, direct cut enumeration gives idempotent contributions \(4q(q-1)(q+1)\) in the line and \(2q(q-1)(q^2+q+2)\) in the outward fork. Their difference simplifies to (4), while their first sums coincide.

## Verification
The attached standalone program `rb_quiver_checker.py` enumerates all nonzero rank-one tensors \(a\otimes f\), uniquely normalizing the first nonzero coefficient of \(f\) to one. For each pair, it directly evaluates the Rota–Baxter identity on all basis-element pairs using the full multiplication table; this enumeration does not use the classification or the graph formula. It independently evaluates (1) by subsets and (3) by recurrence. Thirteen test configurations passed, including fields of orders \(2\), \(3\), and \(4\), path lengths up to four, different orientations, parallel arrows, loops, and a bipartite four-cycle. Expected example counts are \(\mathcal R_2(2)=7\), \(\mathcal R_3(2)=61\), \(\mathcal R_4(2)=421\), \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{fork}}(2)=69\), \(\mathcal R_3(3)=308\), and \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{fork}}(3)=380\). Run `python3 rb_quiver_checker.py` with Python 3.9 or later. Finite checks do not replace the proof for arbitrary quivers or fields.

## Relationship to prior work
The weight-zero Rota–Baxter identity and small-dimensional operator classifications are established; Guo–Rosenkranz–Zheng (2014), arXiv:1402.3702, classifies semigroup algebras of dimensions two and three and covers at most low-dimensional boundary instances, not the arbitrary-quiver count or its Tutte specialization. Gubarev (2024), arXiv:2404.00289, classifies operators on the full \(3\times3\) upper-triangular algebra in characteristic zero; that algebra is not the radical-square-zero path quotient, and its theorem does not state (1)–(4). Previous rank-one Rota–Baxter classifications for full matrix algebras already supply the generic square-zero/idempotent reduction; that reduction is not claimed as an originality contribution. The contribution evaluated here is the exact graph-polynomial enumeration for **all** radical-square-zero quiver algebras, its closed path recurrence, and the quantitative orientation distinction.

## Limitations
The counts concern rank-one weight-zero maps and do not classify higher-rank Rota–Baxter operators, automorphism orbits, or nonreduced scheme structures. Formula (1) is a finite combinatorial sum over arrow and vertex subsets; for dense quivers it is not a polynomial-time algorithm. Its Tutte-factor observation does not imply a new formula for the Tutte polynomial itself. Publication-priority comparison is subject to the possibility of unindexed or implicit consequences in broader quiver-operator classifications; independent mathematical validation has not occurred.
