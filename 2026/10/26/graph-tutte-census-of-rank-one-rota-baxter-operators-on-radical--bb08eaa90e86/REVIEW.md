# Scientific review: Graph-Tutte census of rank-one Rota–Baxter operators

## Correctness — PASS
The rank-one reduction follows directly from the defining bilinear identity; its kernel argument is reversible. In a radical-square-zero quiver algebra all square-zero elements have zero vertex coordinates, and the sum of left and right ideals generated by a radical element is the weighted incidence image of the separated bipartite support graph. Its dimension is a graphic-matroid rank, unaffected by nonzero field coefficients. An idempotent with a specified diagonal is unipotently conjugate to that diagonal, so its two relevant Peirce dimensions are exactly the two directed crossing-arrow counts. Counting scalar presentations exactly once yields the formula without characteristic restrictions. The path recurrence follows from a two-state transfer matrix. A separate exhaustive tensor-and-basis checker confirmed 13 finite cases, including a four-cycle of the separated graph and a nonprime finite field; the general result rests on the proof, not on extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS, subject to literature-priority risk
The rank-one Rota–Baxter dichotomy is background, not claimed new. Guo–Rosenkranz–Zheng (arXiv:1402.3702) treats two- and three-dimensional semigroup algebras and potentially implies the single-arrow boundary case. Gubarev (arXiv:2404.00289) classifies all weight-zero operators on the full upper-triangular algebra of size three in characteristic zero; it does not formulate an exact arbitrary-quiver radical-square-zero census and is not a theorem about the quotient family. Earlier exact distributions of individual left and right ideals for square-zero quiver algebras do not determine their *sum* or the rank-one Rota–Baxter count. Published mathematical-record comparisons did not identify formulae equivalent to the arbitrary-quiver Tutte-plus-cut expression or its all-field path recurrence. Implicit deductions from broader classifications remain a genuine risk, not a claimed proof of uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
Rank-one Rota–Baxter operators are the first nontrivial stratum of an operator-classification problem, and radical-square-zero quiver algebras are a natural representation-theoretic family. The theorem converts the count for every finite quiver into established graph invariants, including a graphic-matroid Tutte evaluation and an orientation-sensitive two-spin cut sum. It yields closed formulas simultaneously for infinitely many path lengths and all finite-field sizes. The exact positive difference \(2q^2(q-1)^2\) between the two orientations of the three-vertex tree demonstrates that this operator count detects a structural orientation distinction invisible to the first (square-zero) term. These are substantive natural invariants, not a single isolated small computation.

## Scope and residual risks
The theorem concerns rank-one weight-zero maps on \(\mathbb F_q Q/J^2\), counts labeled operators rather than automorphism orbits, and makes no higher-rank or scheme-theoretic assertion. A full historical survey of all implicit consequences has not been completed; in particular the upper-triangular classification could conceal special cases after additional arguments. The exact graph formula is a finite subset sum, not an efficient algorithm for arbitrary dense quivers. The computation verifies representative finite instances but is not a substitute for the symbolic proof.
