# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof provides an exact integrated Wronskian excluding imaginary-axis crossings for asymmetric beta weights, a Bessel-identity calculation showing the sign of the departure of *each* symmetric zero, and a parameter-uniform endpoint/Rouché localization to control possible branches escaping to infinity. The two-regime argument is essential: finite-index perturbation alone does not establish an all-zero claim. Verification code checks actual Kummer zeros and the independently derived Wronskian and parameter derivative; all-dimensional conclusions rest on the analytical proof.

**Originality — PASS, with historical priority risk.** Wynn (1973) treats only two one-dimensional subfamilies, while Boussaada–Mazanti–Niculescu (2021), Corollary 1, assumes the total Kummer parameter at least two. The DLMF asymptotic enumeration by itself cannot exclude finitely many opposite-half-plane zeros. The open subcritical positive-beta parameter strip is therefore not implied by the closest explicitly inspected theorems. Older Tricomi, Tsvetkov, Saff–Varga and related zero-location literature may contain an equivalent theorem and warrant specialist comparison before publication.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Half-plane control of **all complex zeros** is a strong global property of a canonical continuous family of Fourier/Laplace transforms. It distinguishes beta asymmetry by a robust analytic zero invariant and fills a parameter gap between classical one-dimensional cases and a proven \(s\ge2\) theorem. This is not merely a numerical census, nonvanishing on the real frequency axis, or a reformulation of asymptotic zero positions.

**Verification and scope.** All parameters and normalizations are explicit. The proof is not asserted for complex parameters or beyond positive beta shapes. Automated tests are finite diagnostics; external specialist review and independent verification have not occurred. A potential older-source equivalence remains a qualified priority risk.
