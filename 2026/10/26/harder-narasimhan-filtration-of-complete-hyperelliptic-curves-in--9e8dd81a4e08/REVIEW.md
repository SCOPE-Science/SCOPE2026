# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For every smooth hyperelliptic curve of genus at least three and general complete degree-\(g+4\) polarization, the balanced cubic scroll exists. The regular normal sequence and the explicit cubic-scroll normal resolution reduce the rank-two quotient to the dual of a complete evaluation kernel. Pullback of the evaluation of \(\mathcal O\oplus\mathcal O(1)\) exhibits its canonical line subbundle and quotient. The three locally free quotients have degrees \(4g+4\), \(2g+6\), and \(g+8\), strictly decreasing for \(g\ge3\). Their determinant equals \(L^5A^{g-1}\) as required by adjunction. The checker independently confirms the degree identities on an extensive finite range; the infinite result rests on the sheaf-theoretic proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual uncertainty
Liu (2026) proves the instability threshold and identifies the first scroll line; its explicit refined scroll-core lemma has a rank hypothesis excluding our projective-four-space case for \(g\ge3\). Coskun–Jovinelly–Larson (2026) already compute the genus-two projective-four-space HN and classify generic Brill–Noether stability; the genus-two case is explicitly excluded, and the general-curve theorem does not supply the HN polygon on the hyperelliptic locus. A search for equivalent graded slopes, normal-quotient flags, and broader tables identified no prior matching result. The basic scroll geometry is known and acknowledged. There remains a risk of an implicit older derivation from standard scroll normal sequences.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The problem is not merely whether the normal bundle is unstable: its canonical HN filtration controls the precise numerical instability and distinguishes the hyperelliptic locus within families of projective-four-space curves. The new three-line flag gives all three slopes for an infinite range of genera, including the previously undetermined rank-two intermediate step, rather than an arbitrary isolated numerical example. It complements the recent sharp stability classification and the established genus-two exceptional HN example.
