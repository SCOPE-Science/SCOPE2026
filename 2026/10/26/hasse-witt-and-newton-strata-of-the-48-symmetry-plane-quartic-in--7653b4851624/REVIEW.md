# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The smoothness proof checks all projective singular possibilities for every \(p\geq5\). The Hasse–Witt coefficient formula follows from the plane-quartic Cech basis; the three exponent conditions give an exhaustive nonnegative-integer classification for each of the four possible residues modulo 12. Every surviving multinomial is nonzero modulo \(p\). All four matrix shapes have stable rank equal to initial rank. The Cartier \(a\)-number and Newton polygon deductions use standard polarisation symmetry, slope denominator/height constraints, and the superspecial criterion \(a=g\). The bundled code compares two independently implemented coefficient constructions through prime 109. Its finite range does not certify the infinite statement by itself.

## Originality — PASS, subject to explicit residual risk
Janasz (arXiv:2406.07015) treats the complex geometry of the same curve, not its characteristic-varying Newton polygons. Ohashi (arXiv:2605.29624) cites Brock's previously known superspecial criterion for the isomorphic equation, so the \(p\equiv11\pmod{12}\) endpoint is excluded from the novelty assertion. In the inspected material, the genus-three arithmetic work of Moriya–Kudo (arXiv:2209.02926) does not state the three remaining residue-class ranks or their Newton polygons. Searches for both equation variants, their automorphism-group alias, Hasse–Witt and Newton terminology, and comparable existing research records identified no matching classification. This is not a uniqueness proof. Brock's full 1993 thesis was unavailable, and general Delsarte/Jacobi-sum theory may imply portions after specialization.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a complete arithmetic-reduction classification for a distinguished maximally symmetric genus-three quartic, not an arbitrary isolated example. The two intermediate \(p\)-rank and Newton strata sharpen the known binary superspecial/non-superspecial criterion. It gives exact genus-three Newton polygons for infinitely many primes, with a short structural explanation in terms of Frobenius support. No new general Hasse–Witt algorithm, zeta-function formula, or isogeny decomposition is claimed.

## Limits
The claim is specific to one plane quartic and primes at least five; the possible literature overlap with Brock's thesis remains a material risk. Full independent checking has not been performed.
