# Hidden one-dimensional fairness in reversible weighted cycle cover tours

## Finding

Let \(n\ge3\). A nearest-neighbour walk on the cycle with arbitrary positive edge conductances can be **exactly fair about its last newly visited vertex from a fixed starting site even when the conductances are nonconstant**. More precisely, after normalizing the \(n\) edge resistances to sum to \(n\), the weights giving last-vertex probability \(1/(n-1)\) from vertex \(0\) form, in a neighbourhood of equal weights, a real-analytic one-dimensional submanifold. Its tangent space at equal weights is generated by the alternating binomial profile

\[
 h_j=(-1)^j\binom{n-1}{j},\qquad 0\le j\le n-1.
\]

The result is stronger than fairness existence: **every last-vertex probability vector sufficiently close to uniform can be realized by positive, nonconstant conductance profiles near equal weights, and its local inverse fiber is one-dimensional**. By contrast, the last newly visited vertex is uniform among all other vertices **from every starting site** if and only if all edge conductances are equal. Thus one-source coverage data leave one locally unidentifiable weight parameter, whereas all-source fairness is rigid.

For four vertices the entire positive fixed-source fairness class, up to positive scaling, is

\[
 (r_0,r_1,r_2,r_3)=(a,2d-a,2a-d,d),
 \qquad a>0,\quad a/2<d<2a.
\]

In particular \((1,2,1/2,3/2)\) is nonhomogeneous yet gives last-coverage probabilities exactly \((1/3,1/3,1/3)\) from site \(0\). The four-cycle class contains profiles with arbitrarily large conductance contrasts as either interior resistance approaches zero.

## Assumptions and scope

The vertices of \(C_n\) are \(0,1,\ldots,n-1\), with indices interpreted modulo \(n\). Edge \(\{i,i+1\}\) has conductance \(c_i>0\) and resistance \(r_i=c_i^{-1}\). At vertex \(i\), the discrete-time walk jumps to \(i+1\) with probability \(c_i/(c_{i-1}+c_i)\) and to \(i-1\) with probability \(c_{i-1}/(c_{i-1}+c_i)\). These are reversible transition probabilities. The same last-discovery statements hold for a continuous-time walk with these positive edge jump rates, since holding times do not change visit order.

For each starting site \(s\), write \(L_s\) for the unique vertex first discovered upon completion of the vertex cover tour. The finite irreducible chain covers the cycle almost surely, so \(L_s\) is defined almost surely and is never \(s\). All fairness statements refer to the exact distribution of \(L_s\), not its expectation, the stationary law, or the cover time.

Only common positive rescaling of all edge resistances is immaterial. The local manifold assertion uses \(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}r_j=n\); a neighbourhood and analytic structure are relative to this open positive simplex. It does not classify all globally fair profiles when \(n>4\).

## Proof

**1. A weighted path identity for last discovery.** Fix distinct sites \(s,k\). Delete \(k\) from the cycle. The remaining path has endpoints \(a=k-1\) and \(b=k+1\), in that order when traversed counterclockwise from \(a\) to \(b\). Let \(x\) be the sum of resistances on this path between \(a\) and \(s\), and let \(y\) be the sum between \(s\) and \(b\). Thus

\[
 H=x+y=\sum_{j\notin\{k-1,k\}}r_j>0,
 \qquad R=H+r_{k-1}+r_k.
\]

The elementary series-resistance (inhomogeneous gambler's ruin) rule states that on a weighted path, the chance of hitting its left endpoint before its right endpoint is the right-side resistance divided by total resistance. This follows by solving the harmonic equation: the voltage increments along each edge are proportional to the edge resistance because the current is constant at each interior vertex.

Before the walk can discover \(k\), it must reach either \(a\) or \(b\). Its first such endpoint is \(a\) with probability \(y/H\) and \(b\) with probability \(x/H\). On first reaching \(a\), all vertices along the path from \(s\) to \(a\) have been visited; the vertex \(k\) is discovered last precisely if the walk subsequently reaches \(b\) before \(k\). The two absorbing endpoints \(k,b\) are separated by the path with resistances \(r_{k-1},H\), so this event has probability \(r_{k-1}/(r_{k-1}+H)\). The analogous probability from \(b\) is \(r_k/(r_k+H)\). The strong Markov property therefore gives the exact identity

\[
 \boxed{\mathbb P(L_s=k)=
 \frac{y}{H}\frac{r_{k-1}}{H+r_{k-1}}
 +\frac{x}{H}\frac{r_k}{H+r_k}.}
 \tag{1}
\]

This path identity is an intermediate tool, **not a claim of the first general formula for nonuniform cycle walks**: earlier work of Mosbah and Saheb already provides termination probabilities for weighted nonuniform cycles. The results below concern the inverse image of the fair distribution and its local geometry.

**2. The exact fair tangent and local classification.** Set \(s=0\). For \(1\le k\le n-1\), equation (1) has \(x=\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}r_j\) and \(y=\sum_{j=k+1}^{n-1}r_j\), with empty sums zero. Let \(r_j(t)=1+t h_j\), where \(\sum_jh_j=0\). Then \(x(0)=k-1\), \(y(0)=n-k-1\), \(H(0)=n-2\), and \(\mathbb P(L_0=k)=1/(n-1)\) at \(t=0\).

Differentiate (1) at zero. Both fractions \(r_{k-1}/(H+r_{k-1})\) and \(r_k/(H+r_k)\) equal \(1/(n-1)\) at zero, hence the derivatives of the two prefactors \(y/H\) and \(x/H\) cancel. The remaining terms, using \(\dot H=-h_{k-1}-h_k\), give

\[
 \boxed{\left.\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb P_{r(t)}(L_0=k)\right|_{t=0}
 =\frac{(n-k)h_{k-1}+k h_k}{(n-1)^2}.}
 \tag{2}
\]

Consequently an infinitesimal zero-change direction must satisfy the recursion

\[
 k h_k=-(n-k)h_{k-1},\qquad 1\le k\le n-1.
\]

Its solutions are exactly the multiples of \(h_j=(-1)^j\binom{n-1}{j}\), and the alternating binomial sum vanishes, so these directions are genuinely tangent to the normalized positive simplex. The derivative of the vector \((\mathbb P(L_0=k))_{k=1}^{n-2}\), restricted to the \((n-1)\)-dimensional hyperplane \(\sum r_j=n\), therefore has a one-dimensional kernel and rank \(n-2\). The target probabilities are rational functions of positive resistances and thus real analytic. The real-analytic submersion/implicit-function theorem gives real-analytic local coordinates in which this probability map is projection onto the first \(n-2\) coordinates. Therefore it is locally onto a neighbourhood of the fair probability vector, and **every** nearby probability vector has a one-dimensional analytic local fiber of positive resistances. This includes the fair fiber, whose tangent line is as displayed. Since that line is nonzero in the normalized simplex, the fair fiber contains nonconstant positive profiles arbitrarily close to the equal-resistance profile. The same local coordinate description ensures a nonconstant realization for every nearby target vector, including a target itself equal to the fair law.

**3. Four-vertex global example and classification.** For \(n=4\), applying (1) from site \(0\) to the two adjacent targets yields

\[
 \mathbb P(L_0=1)=\frac{r_0}{r_0+r_2+r_3},
 \qquad
 \mathbb P(L_0=3)=\frac{r_3}{r_3+r_0+r_1}.
\]

Both equal \(1/3\) exactly when \(2r_0=r_2+r_3\) and \(2r_3=r_0+r_1\). Since the three nonstarting probabilities sum to one, those two equations are necessary and sufficient for all three to be \(1/3\). Put \(a=r_0\) and \(d=r_3\), obtaining the claimed complete class \((a,2d-a,2a-d,d)\); positivity is equivalent to \(a>0\) and \(a/2<d<2a\). For \((1,2,1/2,3/2)\), equation (1) gives all three probabilities exactly \(1/3\), whereas from site \(1\) they are \(2/7,4/9,17/63\) at sites \(0,2,3\), respectively.

**4. All-start rigidity.** Assume for every starting vertex \(s\) and every \(k\ne s\) that \(\mathbb P(L_s=k)=1/(n-1)\). Starting at the two neighbours \(a=k-1\) and \(b=k+1\), equation (1) gives, respectively,

\[
 \frac{r_{k-1}}{R-r_k}=\frac{1}{n-1},
 \qquad
 \frac{r_k}{R-r_{k-1}}=\frac{1}{n-1}.
\]

Subtracting the resulting linear equations yields \((n-2)(r_{k-1}-r_k)=0\). Since \(n\ge3\), consecutive resistances are equal. Letting \(k\) vary proves that all resistances, hence all conductances, are equal. Conversely if all resistances are equal, (1) reduces to \(1/(n-1)\) for all distinct \(s,k\). This proves the equivalence.

## Relationship to prior work

Idnani and Salem (arXiv:2610.15115v1, first submitted 2026-10-10) calculate last-coverage laws for spatially homogeneous symmetric and biased walks and deterministic time-dependent bias. Their Section 6.2 identifies position-dependent probabilities as a further direction. The present findings specialize to reversible position-dependent probabilities and study their **inverse fair-distribution geometry**, rather than just computing another last-coverage distribution.

Mosbah and Saheb, *Formal rational fractions and random walks on cycles* (2000), already supply a general termination-probability formula for nonuniform weighted cycle walks; this prior coverage is explicitly acknowledged. Formula (1) is a simpler resistance representation used as an input. The claimed addition is the all-length local inverse-design submersion and one-parameter identifiability defect, its alternating-binomial tangent, the complete positive four-cycle fair family, and rigidity under simultaneous fairness for every start. An inaccessible complete comparison against the older paper leaves residual uncertainty about implicit or explicit prior inverse characterizations.

Lovász and Winkler (1993) classify **unweighted** undirected graphs with uniform last-visited-site distributions from all starts. Buchanan, Horn and Rombach (2023) extend graph-structure classification to unweighted directed walks. Neither graph-topology theorem directly characterizes which conductance assignments on a fixed cycle preserve the uniform law from one or every start.

## Limitations

Only reversible nearest-neighbour Markov chains with fixed strictly positive undirected edge conductances are treated. Position-dependent nonreversible drift, vanishing conductances, random time-varying environments, and general graphs are excluded. The one-dimensional fair manifold is a **local** result around equal conductances for each fixed \(n\); no global parametrization is claimed beyond \(n=4\). Earlier work already provides formulas for general weighted-cycle termination probabilities, so formula (1) by itself is not new. The older Mosbah–Saheb article's full journal text was not completely accessible in this review; an equivalent inverse characterization implicit there remains an originality risk. No independent scientific audit has been performed.
