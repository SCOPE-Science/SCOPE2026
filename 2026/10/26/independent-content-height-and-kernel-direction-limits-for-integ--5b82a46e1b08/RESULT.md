# Independent content, height, and kernel-direction limits for integral square-zero matrices

## Finding

For an integer \(T\ge2\), let \(\mathcal N_T\) be the set of all nonzero integer \(2\times2\) matrices \(M\) satisfying \(M^2=0\) and \(\|M\|_\infty\le T\), where \(\|M\|_\infty\) is the largest absolute entry. Put \(c(M)=\gcd\{|M_{ij}|:1\le i,j\le2\}\). Every \(M\in\mathcal N_T\) has a one-dimensional kernel generated by a unique primitive integer vector \((u,v)\) with \(u>0\), or \((u,v)=(0,1)\). Define its projective slope \(z(M)=v/u\) if \(u>0\), and \(z(M)=\infty\) otherwise.

Choose \(M\) uniformly from \(\mathcal N_T\). As \(T\to\infty\), the three random quantities

\[
X_T=\frac{\log c(M)}{\log T},\qquad Q_T=\frac{\|M\|_\infty}{T},\qquad Z_T=z(M)
\]

converge **jointly to independent variables**: \(X\) and \(Q\) are each uniform on \([0,1]\), while \(Z\) is real-valued with density

\[
f_Z(z)=\frac14\min\{1,|z|^{-2}\}\qquad(z\in\mathbb R),
\]

interpreting the minimum as \(1\) at \(z=0\). In particular, the apparent singularity is removable and \(Z\) assigns probability zero to the vertical direction \(\infty\).

Quantitatively, for every fixed bounded interval \(I\subset\mathbb R\), \(0<q\le1\), and \(0\le\alpha<1\), write \(\mu(I)=\tfrac14\int_I\min\{1,|z|^{-2}\}\,dz\). Then

\[
\Pr(X_T\le\alpha,\ Q_T\le q,\ Z_T\in I)
=\alpha q\,\mu(I)+O_{I,q}\!\left(\frac1{\log T}+T^{-(1-\alpha)/2}\right).
\]

The error is uniformly \(O_{I,q,\delta}(1/\log T)\) for \(0\le\alpha\le1-\delta\) and each fixed \(\delta>0\). The limiting statement also holds at \(\alpha=1\), without the displayed uniform rate there. The exact unrefined count is recorded in earlier literature and is **not** claimed as original:

\[
\#\mathcal N_T=8\sum_{m\le\sqrt T}\varphi(m)\left\lfloor\frac{T}{m^2}\right\rfloor
=\frac{24}{\pi^2}T\log T+O(T).
\]

## Assumptions and scope

All matrices have integral entries and size \(2\times2\); the sampling is uniform among **nonzero** square-zero matrices in the entrywise supremum-norm box. The parameter \(T\) tends to infinity through positive integers. The content \(c(M)\) is positive. Kernel direction is projective, so \((u,v)\) and \((-u,-v)\) represent the same line. The zero matrix is excluded because its kernel is not a line. The quantitative joint estimate concerns bounded real intervals and \(\alpha<1\).

## Proof

**1. Unique primitive-line parameterization.** A nonzero \(2\times2\) square-zero matrix has rank one and its image equals its kernel. Let \(w=(u,v)^t\) be the canonical primitive generator of that line. The integer row \((-v,u)\) annihilates \(w\), so the matrix is necessarily a scalar multiple of \(w(-v,u)\):

\[
M=t\begin{pmatrix}-uv&u^2\\-v^2&uv\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Since \(\gcd(u,v)=1\), the gcd of \(u^2,uv,v^2\) is exactly \(1\). The scalar \(t\) must therefore be a nonzero integer, uniquely determined; its magnitude is the entry content \(c(M)=|t|\). Writing \(h=\max\{u,|v|\}\), the entrywise norm satisfies \(\|M\|_\infty=|t|h^2\). Conversely, every such triple \((u,v,t)\) gives a distinct nonzero square-zero integer matrix. This covers all axes and all matrix signs.

**2. Primitive projective directions.** For any fixed bounded interval \(I\), let \(L_I(x)\) count the primitive canonical vectors of height at most \(x\) with slope in \(I\). The planar region

\[
\Omega_I=\{(r,s):0<r\le1,\ |s|\le1,\ s/r\in I\}
\]

has area \(\tfrac12\int_I\min\{1,|z|^{-2}\}\,dz=2\mu(I)\); this follows from \((r,s)=(r,rz)\), with Jacobian \(r\) and \(0<r\le\min\{1,1/|z|\}\). Boundary lattice-point counting in this fixed bounded polygon gives \(\#(x\Omega_I\cap\mathbb Z^2)=2\mu(I)x^2+O_I(x)\). Möbius inversion for primitive pairs and \(\sum_{d\ge1}\mu(d)/d^2=6/\pi^2\) then give, uniformly for \(x\ge1\),

\[
L_I(x)=\frac{12}{\pi^2}\mu(I)x^2+O_I\bigl(x\log(2x)\bigr).
\]

The excluded vertical direction consists of one primitive line and has no effect on this formula.

**3. The normalizing count.** For every integer \(m\ge1\), the number of canonical primitive projective lines with \(\max\{u,|v|\}=m\) is \(4\varphi(m)\). For \(m\ge2\), there are \(2\varphi(m)\) vectors with \(u=m\) and \(\varphi(m)\) with each of \(v=\pm m\); for \(m=1\) there are four lines directly. The two signs of \(t\) therefore give

\[
\#\mathcal N_T=8\sum_{m\le\sqrt T}\varphi(m)\lfloor T/m^2\rfloor.
\]

Using \(\sum_{m\le y}\varphi(m)/m^2=(6/\pi^2)\log y+O(1)\) and \(\sum_{m\le y}\varphi(m)=O(y^2)\), this equals \((24/\pi^2)T\log T+O(T)\). Both the exact sum and its univariate asymptotic are explicitly known (OEIS A120486, A211422); they are included only to normalize the new joint law.

**4. The joint three-variable count.** Fix \(0<q\le1\), a bounded interval \(I\), and \(0\le\alpha<1\). For sufficiently large \(T\), the primitive parameterization gives exactly

\[
\#\{M\in\mathcal N_T:c(M)\le T^\alpha,\ \|M\|_\infty\le qT,\ z(M)\in I\}
=2\sum_{1\le t\le T^\alpha}L_I\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{qT}{t}}\right).
\]

Inserting the preceding estimate for \(L_I\), and using \(\sum_{t\le T^\alpha}t^{-1}=\alpha\log T+O(1)\) as well as \(\sum_{t\le T^\alpha}t^{-1/2}=O(T^{\alpha/2})\), yields

\[
\frac{24}{\pi^2}\,q\mu(I)\alpha T\log T
+O_{I,q}\bigl(T+T^{(1+\alpha)/2}\log(2T)\bigr).
\]

Dividing by the normalizing count proves the quantitative probability estimate for \(\alpha<1\). Its two error terms are uniform when \(\alpha\le1-\delta\).

**5. Boundary cases and weak convergence.** At \(\alpha=1\), the event \(X_T\le1\) is automatic. For fixed \(\alpha<1\), the total proportion with \(X_T>\alpha\) tends to \(1-\alpha\): the content-only count is obtained from step 4 with the unrestricted primitive-line estimate \(L_{\mathrm{all}}(x)=4\sum_{m\le x}\varphi(m)=12x^2/\pi^2+O(x\log(2x))\). Squeezing the probabilities at \(\alpha=1\) between those at \(\alpha<1\) and an additional mass at most \(1-\alpha\), then letting \(\alpha\uparrow1\), establishes the asserted factorization at the endpoint. Similarly, \(q=0\) has probability zero, and the vertical line accounts for only \(2\lfloor T\rfloor=O(T)\) matrices. Since \(\int_{\mathbb R}\min\{1,|z|^{-2}\}\,dz=4\), the limiting angular law is a probability measure. The rectangle probabilities determine the joint weak limit on \([0,1]^2\times\mathbb R\), which is the product of two Lebesgue-uniform laws and \(\mu\).

## Verification

The standalone Python program `verify.py` performs integer-only, independently reconstructed finite checks. For nineteen integer height cutoffs through \(T=50\), it compares a direct enumeration using \(a^2+bc=0\) for \(M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&-a\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) against the full primitive-vector parameterization and the totient formula. It checks 175 exact joint content, norm, and slope-window counts (slope between \(-1\) and \(1\)) for distinct content and norm cutoffs. For all \(1\le T\le299\), a separate content-first finite sum agrees with the totient-first count. The program returns `VERIFY_OK` on success. These are finite checks, **not** proofs of distributional convergence; the proof is steps 1–5.

## Relationship to prior work

Browning, Gundlach, and Sawin (arXiv:2610.18226v1, first public 2026-10-13) study the count of integer matrices with a prescribed characteristic polynomial, including nonsquarefree cases, and prove a uniform upper bound. For \(2\times2\) square-zero matrices this object has characteristic polynomial \(X^2\), but the cited results do not state the present joint asymptotic independence law for entry content, normalized height, and projective kernel direction.

Martin and Wong (2008) establish an asymptotic for \(2\times2\) matrices with a given integer eigenvalue and bound the repeated-eigenvalue stratum (their Lemma 7). Requiring **both** eigenvalues to be zero is a smaller, differently normalized subfamily. Neither their eigenvalue-count result nor their repeated-root bound gives the claimed joint distribution.

The total unrefined counts and the totient identity are **already recorded** in OEIS A211422 and A120486; A120486 also gives the leading \(T\log T\) asymptotic. Those facts are cited, not presented as discoveries. The additional assertion here is the full three-factor joint law with an explicit angular density and a quantitative truncation estimate, neither of which follows from an unrefined counting sequence alone. It is possible that a general equidistribution theorem for primitive points on homogeneous cones implies the same distribution after specialization; no exact such implication was established by the comparisons performed.

## Limitations

The theorem is limited to uniform box sampling of nonzero square-zero \(2\times2\) integral matrices; no higher-dimensional nilpotent claim or other matrix norm is made. The error bound is stated away from the content endpoint \(\alpha=1\). Finite verification does not certify any limit, and unindexed broader coverage remains a bibliographic risk.
