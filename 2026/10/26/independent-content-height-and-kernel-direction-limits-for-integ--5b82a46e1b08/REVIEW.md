# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

Every nonzero integral square-zero matrix in dimension two admits a unique primitive projective kernel generator and nonzero integral scalar. The representation gives exact content and height identities. Primitive sector counts follow from elementary lattice-point counting and Möbius inversion; summing over the scalar gives the stated three-variable asymptotic, with a uniform error away from the content endpoint. The endpoint follows from squeezing and tightness. The included integer-only verifier independently enumerates full small cases and joint sector/content/height windows. Its finite checks are corroboration, not a replacement for the asymptotic proof.

## Originality — PASS, with bibliographic qualification

The original exact enumeration is **not new**: OEIS A211422 and A120486 give the same unrefined count, and A120486 records its leading asymptotic. Martin–Wong's 2008 paper treats matrices with one given integer eigenvalue and bounds repeated eigenvalues. Browning–Gundlach–Sawin (2026) treat characteristic-polynomial counting, including nonsquarefree cases, but give general upper bounds rather than the present joint disintegration. The new assertion is specifically the asymptotic independence of normalized logarithmic content, radial height, and projective kernel slope with the explicit nonuniform slope law and sector-uniform quantitative estimate. Relevant research-record comparisons identified no equivalent assertion. Strong general equidistribution theorems may imply this specialization and remain a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Uniformly sampled square-zero integral matrices form a natural and singular characteristic-polynomial stratum. Their content records arithmetic primitivity, their normalized height records geometry inside the matrix box, and their kernel line records rational projective direction. Knowing the total counting sequence does not determine the three joint observables. The theorem identifies a product limit with two uniform factors and a heavy-tailed projective factor, and permits asymptotic counts subject to simultaneous restrictions. It is a structural distribution theorem, not a restatement of an old count, arbitrary finite slice, or unsupported application.

## Limits and closest literature

The proof uses a two-dimensional rank-one parameterization unavailable without modification in higher dimensions. The probability law depends on the supremum norm and on sampling from nonzero integral square-zero matrices. The displayed quantitative error is restricted to bounded slope intervals and content cutoffs strictly below one on the logarithmic scale. The OEIS entries give the prior exact total; the relevant Martin–Wong lemma only bounds repeated-eigenvalue matrices; the 2026 characteristic-polynomial paper gives a uniform upper bound. An unindexed specialized distribution theorem or an applicable broader homogeneous-variety equidistribution theorem cannot be excluded.
