# Review: independent-domination rigidity

## Correctness (C): PASS
The unique-completion lemma forces the synthemes in every independent dominating set once its duads are fixed, and the matching-cover condition characterizes which choices dominate the remaining duads. All \(2^{15}\) duad subsets are checked, not sampled; the minimum and all \(90\) attainers are determined exactly. A second implementation independently enumerates maximal independent sets from a symplectic construction with an explicit checked incidence-graph isomorphism. Both report \(3264\) maximal independent sets, \(90\) optimal ones, and exactly \(45\) unique token-slide edges. The \(45\) four-cycle pairings and \(S_6\) symmetry are checked explicitly.

## Originality (O): PASS, with remaining literature uncertainty
The historical duad–syntheme and \(S_6\) description, general independent-domination bounds, and independent-set enumeration are prior results. The closest same-graph published computation identifies the integral homotopy type of its independence complex; that theorem does not imply the minimum maximal-independent-set classification or exact optimum exchange graph. Searches covered Tutte's 8-cage, Cremona–Richmond, generalized quadrangle, independent dominating sets and token-jump/slide aliases. No inspected source states the pairwise \(45K_2\) optimum reconfiguration theorem. Unindexed equivalent work is a residual publication-priority risk, not a reason to claim universal uniqueness.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS
This is a complete classification of natural extremal independent-dominating configurations in the smallest cubic girth-eight graph. The exact token-slide connectivity obstruction is strong: the apparent \(90\)-state optimum space splits into \(45\) separate two-state components, and each optimum has one and only one legal local replacement. This transforms an extremal cardinality problem into a precise rigidity theorem at fixed optimum cardinality, not a routine count or an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Boundaries
The theorem is finite, exact, and confined to the stated graph and optimum-state exchange rules. No claim is made about paths that leave the minimum layer, arbitrary cubic graphs, or priority against inaccessible publications.
