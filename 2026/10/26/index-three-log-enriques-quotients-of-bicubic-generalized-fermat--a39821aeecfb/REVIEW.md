# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Each coefficient hypothesis is used to show the two cubic gradients have rank two, and the fixed-point locus is checked in both projective eigenspaces. The normal representation is scalar of weight one on a rank-two normal bundle, allowing an explicit blowup-chart resolution and discrepancy computation. The order-three action on middle cohomology is reconstructed from the topological fixed-point trace, and the Hodge summands in the blowup are geometrically fixed. Independent Euler calculations agree, and `verify.py` reproduces the exact integer arithmetic. The resulting Hodge computation is restricted to the ordinary smooth resolution, not to any crepant or age-shifted theory.

**Originality — PASS with residual risk.** Billi--Palomino--Sarti (arXiv:2610.21752v1) explicitly treat empty-fixed-locus quotients and mark nonfree automorphisms as future work. The present action has a fixed genus-ten curve, is outside their hypothesis, and the paper does not give its resolution or \((2,42,0)\) Hodge result. Hidalgo--Hughes--Leyton--Álvarez (arXiv:2010.04628) analyze the automorphism group and fixed loci, so the existence of the fixed curve itself is not original. Constantin--Gray--Lukas (arXiv:1607.01830) and Braun (arXiv:1003.3235) discuss free complete-intersection quotients; their tables cannot directly imply the singular quotient's resolution invariants. Directed mathematical-record and bibliographic searches did not identify an equivalent nonfree computation. An unindexed equivalent calculation remains possible; a failed search is not a mathematical uniqueness proof.

**Value — PASS.** Removing freeness is the motivating paper's explicitly stated natural next question. The result determines, uniformly in the admissible three-parameter family, the canonical torsion index, precise transverse singularity, discrepancy, concrete resolution and its Hodge numbers. In particular, it exhibits an odd-dimensional index-three Log Enriques quotient, a phenomenon excluded for *free* non-symplectic quotients by the holomorphic Lefschetz argument, while distinguishing ordinary resolution invariants from hypothetical crepant ones. This is a structural classification of a natural example rather than an arbitrary numerical slice.

**Scope and risks.** The proof uses classical Lefschetz, blowup and transfer theorems, not independently new versions of them. The source checks do not certify exhaustiveness of all possible unindexed literature. No claim about a crepant resolution is made (the stated one has discrepancy \(-1/3\)), and no independent audit has been performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
