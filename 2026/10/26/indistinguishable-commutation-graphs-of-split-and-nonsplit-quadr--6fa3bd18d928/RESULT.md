# Indistinguishable commutation graphs of split and nonsplit quadratic skew truncations

## Finding

Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\) have odd order and \(m\ge1\). Equip \(E_{\mathrm{s}}=k\times k\) with factor swapping and \(E_{\mathrm{f}}=\mathbb F_{q^2}\) with Frobenius conjugation. The \(k\)-algebras \(A_{\mathrm{s}}=E_{\mathrm{s}}[t;\tau]/(t^{2m+1})\) and \(A_{\mathrm{f}}=E_{\mathrm{f}}[t;\tau]/(t^{2m+1})\) are nonisomorphic, but a \(k\)-linear bijection between them preserves and reflects commutation of every pair. Both have center order \(q^{m+2}\); exactly \(q^{m+2}\) elements have centralizer order \(q^{4m+2}\), and for each \(0\le j<m\), exactly \(q^{m+2}(q^{3(m-j)}-q^{3(m-j-1)})\) elements have centralizer order \(q^{2m+2+2j}\). Their commuting probability is \(q^{-3m}+(1+q^{-1}+q^{-2})q^{-2m}(1-q^{-m})\). In particular, their commuting and noncommuting graphs are isomorphic although their semisimple quotients are different. Moreover, if \(q\) has odd characteristic \(p\) and \(2m+1=p\), the split algebra \(A_{\mathrm{s}}\) is isomorphic to the modular dihedral group algebra \(k[D_{2p}]\), providing the exact centralizer spectrum of that group algebra.

## Assumptions and scope

For an involutive \(k\)-algebra \((E,\tau)\), the Ore/skew-polynomial ring \(E[t;\tau]\) is defined by \(ta=\tau(a)t\) for \(a\in E\). The ideal generated by \(t^{2m+1}\) is two-sided. We count centralizers of **all algebra elements** as additive \(k\)-subspaces, not centralizers of the unit group and not only centralizers of group elements. The two graphs on the noncentral elements join distinct commuting, respectively noncommuting, pairs. The graph correspondence also preserves the commuting predicate on the entire underlying set. Throughout, \(q\) is odd; no claim is made for characteristic two.

## Proof

**Étale coordinates.** Choose \(d\in k^\times\) and write \(E_d=k[z]/(z^2-d)\) with involution \(z\mapsto-z\). If \(d\) is a square, \(E_d\cong k\times k\); if \(d\) is a nonsquare, \(E_d\cong\mathbb F_{q^2}\), and the involution is Frobenius. Put \(A_d=E_d[t;\tau]/(t^{2m+1})\) and \(u=t^2\). Its normal basis is \(z^bt^i\), \(b\in\{0,1\}\), \(0\le i\le 2m\), so \(\dim_kA_d=4m+2\). As a vector space it has the unique presentation
\[
 A_d=S_{m+1}\cdot1\;\oplus\;S_{m+1}\cdot z\;\oplus\;S_m\cdot t\;\oplus\;S_m\cdot zt,
 \quad S_r=k[u]/(u^r).
\]
Reduction of coefficients modulo \(u^m\) is denoted by a bar. For \(h=z\), \(e=t\), and \(f=zt\), direct multiplication gives
\[
[h,e]=2f,\qquad [h,f]=2de,\qquad [e,f]=-2uh.
\]
An element \(x\) is therefore uniquely \(x=s+a h+b e+c f\), with \(s,a\in S_{m+1}\) and \(b,c\in S_m\). For another \(y=S+A h+B e+C f\), coefficient comparison in the displayed normal basis gives
\[
[x,y]=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \bar a B-b\bar A=0,\qquad
 \bar a C-c\bar A=0,\qquad bC-cB=0\quad\text{in }S_m.
\]
Here \(d\ne0\), \(2\ne0\), and multiplication by \(u\) injects \(S_m\) into \(S_{m+1}\). These are precisely the three exterior minors of \((\bar a,b,c)\) and \((\bar A,B,C)\) over \(S_m\), independent of the square class of \(d\). Consequently the coefficient-wise map \(z_{d_1}^bt^i\mapsto z_{d_2}^bt^i\) is a \(k\)-linear bijection preserving and reflecting commutation for every pair. It carries central to central elements and yields the stated graph isomorphisms.

**Center and centralizers.** All three minors vanish against every vector exactly when \((\bar a,b,c)=0\). Thus
\[
 Z(A_d)=S_{m+1}\cdot1\oplus k\,u^m h,\quad |Z(A_d)|=q^{m+2},\quad A_d/Z(A_d)\cong S_m^3
\]
(as additive \(k\)-spaces). For a nonzero \(v\in S_m^3\), let \(j\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\) be the minimum \(u\)-adic valuation of its coordinates. An invertible matrix in \(\operatorname{GL}_3(S_m)\) sends \(v\) to \((u^j,0,0)\): first move a least-valuation coordinate into position one, scale its unit coefficient to one, and eliminate the other coordinates. Exterior minors vanish exactly when the last two coordinates of the partner are annihilated by \(u^j\); the first coordinate is arbitrary. This leaves \(q^{m+2j}\) possible partner vectors, hence
\[
 |C_{A_d}(x)|=q^{m+2}q^{m+2j}=q^{2m+2+2j}.
\]
There are \(q^{3(m-j)}-q^{3(m-j-1)}\) quotient vectors with valuation exactly \(j\), each with \(q^{m+2}\) lifts. Zero quotient vectors correspond to central elements, with full centralizer order \(q^{4m+2}\). This establishes all multiplicities, which sum to \(q^{4m+2}\).

**Commuting probability.** The average centralizer cardinality, divided by \(q^{4m+2}\), is
\[
 q^{-3m}+q^{-6m}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}
 (q^{3(m-j)}-q^{3(m-j-1)})q^{m+2j}
 =q^{-3m}+(1+q^{-1}+q^{-2})q^{-2m}(1-q^{-m}).
\]
One may check the sum by substituting \(i=m-j\) and using the geometric series.

**Not isomorphic as rings.** The ideal \((t)\) is nilpotent and the semisimple quotient \(A_d/(t)\cong E_d\). Thus \((t)\) is the Jacobson radical. The split member has semisimple quotient \(k\times k\), while the nonsplit member has quotient \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\). Their radicals' quotients are not isomorphic, so neither are the algebras.

**Modular dihedral realization.** Let \(p=2m+1\) be an odd prime equal to the characteristic of \(k\), and \(D_{2p}=\langle r,s:r^p=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle\). In \(k[C_p]\), the element \(1+r\) is a unit, since its augmentation is \(2\ne0\) and \(k[C_p]\) is local. Put \(t=(r-1)/(r+1)\). Then \(t^p=0\), \(sts=-t\), and \(r=(1+t)/(1-t)\). Hence \(k[D_{2p}]\) is generated by \(s,t\) with \(s^2=1\), \(st=-ts\), \(t^p=0\). Its \(2p\)-dimensional basis and multiplication identify it with \((k\times k)[t;\tau]/(t^p)\), completing the claim.

## Verification

The standalone script `dihedral_etale_check.py` constructs multiplication and adjoint matrices directly from the normal basis, then computes their finite-field nullities independently of the exterior-minor argument. It exhaustively checks all \(3^6=729\) elements for \(m=1\) over \(\mathbb F_3\), for both square and nonsquare \(d\), giving centralizer dimension multiplicities \(\{6:27,4:702\}\) and \(76{,}545\) commuting ordered pairs in each. It checks independently sampled elements for \(m=2\) over \(\mathbb F_3\) and \(\mathbb F_5\), for both square classes. These computations are finite consistency checks, not the proof for arbitrary \(q,m\).

## Relationship to prior work

The established theory of finite-ring commuting probabilities and centralizer counts is developed by Dutta–Basnet–Nath, while Juráš–Ursul compare the possible commuting probabilities of rings with those of nilpotent groups. Sahai–Ansari study units in dihedral and related group algebras. These works motivate the naturality of commuting probability and the group-algebra specialization, but the inspected material does not give the nonisomorphic split/nonsplit skew-truncation pair with a commutation-preserving linear bijection or its all-element centralizer spectrum. The nonsplit Ore quotient's centralizer enumeration alone is not claimed as a new result: the new content is the split counterpart, the simultaneous linear graph isomorphism, and the dihedral group-algebra realization.

## Limitations

The result requires odd characteristic, uses a quadratic étale coefficient algebra with its nontrivial involution, and truncates in odd length. It does not identify the multiplication structures, only the pairwise commutation predicate. The published literature check is extensive but not exhaustive; full texts of some older classical treatments of ring isoclinism were not fully inspected, and a general isoclinism principle may imply graph equivalence once an appropriate isoclinism is observed. The explicit pair and its formulas are proved here independently. No independent audit or validation is claimed.
