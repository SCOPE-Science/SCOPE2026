# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. On the domain \(H^2(\mathbb R)\), point evaluations yield a closed lower-bounded form. The inverse of \(D^4+1\) has the explicitly checked Green function with the correct unit third-derivative jump. A rank-two form inertia reduction rigorously counts all strictly negative eigenvalues; the oscillatory off-diagonal Green entry has exactly one absolute maximum in each noninitial lobe. Its exponentially decreasing peak heights give the exact integer component count and include a separate neutral-mode boundary check. The standalone Fourier-quadrature and finite-difference checker returned 10,079 passing comparisons, but the continuum theorem is proved analytically, not numerically.

## Originality
PASS, with documented historical-priority uncertainty. An accessible complete single-softener preprint [4], a complete single finite-width defect article [3], and the full finite-beam multiple-spring study [5] were compared against the actual statement. The first gives no two-defect coupling; the second varies one defect's width; the third supplies first-order, finite-interval vibration and critical-load results. None implies the exact nonperturbative count of disconnected unstable separation windows on the infinite line. The earlier relevant complete Coman and Wang articles were not accessible; priority against material not read cannot be excluded. General finite-rank resolvent machinery itself is established, and is not claimed as new.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The oscillatory Green function produces an exact hierarchy of spacing-driven loss and restoration of stability, even though either isolated defect is stable. The theorem determines the number of all unstable separation components for every \(\sqrt2<a<2\sqrt2\), and exact threshold strengths for an arbitrary number of components. This is a natural spectral-stability/placement problem with meaningful design interpretation, not a restatement of the single-defect threshold.
