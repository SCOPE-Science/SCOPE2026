# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For each nonnegative target there is exactly one fundamental rectangle coordinate pair \((u,v)\), and the layer index is at least \(-1\). The proof enumerates all two-denomination decompositions and their exact \(1+\min(U,V)\) refinements into three labeled denominations. The \(t\ge2\) lower bound excludes all low-multiplicity cases; the remaining rectangle and triangle are counted exactly, including \(k=1\) and \(k=a+1\). Maximization compares every remaining layer and includes the zero-representation boundary. A standalone independent coefficient computation corroborated 575 coprime pairs and 952360 integer values. Finite verification does not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The motivating Aldrich–Gulbrandsen–Keddy article (arXiv:2610.20899, version 2, inspected introduction, §§2–4, Theorem C) derives bounds and existence, not the uniform exact first multiplicity phase or first slope change. Komatsu–Ying (2023) treats arithmetic-progression triples; in the coprime \((a,b,a+b)\) setting with \(a\ge2\), that shape is impossible. Komatsu–Laishram–Punyani (2023) and Komatsu–Mu (2024) give formulas for recurrence-constrained triples, not arbitrary coprime pairs. Searches also considered published result databases, numerical semigroup terminology, and exact denumerant aliases. None of the inspected statements entails the uniform classification without a new all-residue/layer argument. Residual risk is implicit coverage by broad generating-function machinery or an unindexed older special case; absence of a search hit is not proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The third coin is redundant for the support of representable integers but changes their representation multiplicities, making the first multiplicity phase a natural structural invariant rather than a restatement of the two-generator Frobenius theorem. The result simultaneously specifies every count of exactly \(k\) representations and the associated \(k\)-Frobenius and \(k\)-genus, up through the first universal slope change at \(k=a+1\). The endpoint is structurally forced by when a second fundamental-rectangle layer becomes active, and the exact breakpoint differs for each smaller generator. No claim is made that this covers arbitrary three-coin systems or later phases.
