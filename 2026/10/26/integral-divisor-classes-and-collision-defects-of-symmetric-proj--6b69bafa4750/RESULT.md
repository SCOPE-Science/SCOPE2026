# Integral divisor classes and collision defects of symmetric projective powers

## Finding
Let \(n,d\ge2\) be integers and set
\[
M=(\mathbb P^n_{\mathbb C})^d,\qquad X=M/\mathfrak S_d=\operatorname{Sym}^d(\mathbb P^n_{\mathbb C}),\qquad N=nd.
\]
Write \(L\) for the line bundle on \(X\) descending from \(\boxtimes_{j=1}^{d}\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^n}(1)\) with the natural permutation linearization, and \(\tau\) for the Weil class extending the sign-character line bundle on the open set of distinct unordered points. There are isomorphisms of abelian groups, with the cycle map itself an isomorphism,
\[
\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\mathbb Z[L],\quad
\operatorname{Cl}(X)=\operatorname{CH}_{N-1}(X)=\mathbb Z[L]\oplus(\mathbb Z/2)[\tau],\quad
\operatorname{cl}_{N-1}:\operatorname{CH}_{N-1}(X)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }H_{2N-2}(X,\mathbb Z).
\]
The nonzero class \(\tau\) is not Cartier. Thus every integral homology class of the indicated degree is algebraic, including the unique nonzero torsion class.

For a complex point \(x\in X\), let its unordered support have distinct points with multiplicities \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_s\), summing to \(d\), and put \(r=\#\{i:\lambda_i\ge2\}\). Then
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{X,x})\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^r,
\qquad
\operatorname{im}\left(\operatorname{Cl}(X)\to\operatorname{Cl}(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{X,x})\right)
=\begin{cases}0,&r=0,\\ \langle(1,\ldots,1)\rangle,&r\ge1.\end{cases}
\]
In particular, when \(r\ge1\) the cokernel is \((\mathbb Z/2)^{r-1}\), and local divisor classes invisible globally appear precisely when at least two distinct support points are each repeated.

The canonical class and quotient singularities are determined by the same sign character:
\[
K_X=-(n+1)[L]+(n\bmod2)[\tau].
\]
The variety \(X\) is normal, \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial and Fano; it is Gorenstein precisely for even \(n\), while for odd \(n\) its canonical divisor has Cartier index two. Its singular locus is the collision locus. All singularities are canonical for \(n=2\) and terminal for \(n\ge3\).

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\); \(n\) and \(d\) are arbitrary integers at least two. Chow groups are graded by complex dimension and homology has integral coefficients. The variety \(X\) is the coarse projective quotient, not the quotient stack. The completed local class group refers to the completed complex local ring at a closed point. The claimed cycle isomorphism is only in divisor degree \(N-1\); no statement of codimension-two surjectivity is made.

Let \(\Delta\subset M\) be the union of the pairwise diagonals, let \(F=M\setminus\Delta\), and set \(U=F/\mathfrak S_d\subset X\). Every component of \(\Delta\) has complex codimension \(n\), so \(X\setminus U\) also has codimension \(n\ge2\). The finite covering \(F\to U\) is étale with group \(\mathfrak S_d\).

## Proof
**1. Global Weil divisors and line bundles.** The complement of \(F\) in smooth \(M\) has codimension at least two. Consequently \(\operatorname{Pic}(F)=\operatorname{Pic}(M)=\mathbb Z^d\), and the regular invertible functions on \(F\) are just \(\mathbb C^*\). Since \(F\to U\) is a finite étale Galois covering, line bundles on \(U\) are the \(\mathfrak S_d\)-linearized bundles on \(F\). The equivariant Picard exact sequence gives
\[
0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak S_d,\mathbb C^*)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(U)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(F)^{\mathfrak S_d}\longrightarrow H^2(\mathfrak S_d,\mathbb C^*).
\]
Here \(\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak S_d,\mathbb C^*)\cong\mathbb Z/2\) is generated by the sign character, and \(\operatorname{Pic}(F)^{\mathfrak S_d}\cong\mathbb Z\) is generated by the diagonal tuple \((1,\ldots,1)\). The external tensor product admits its evident symmetric permutation linearization, so this last generator lifts to \(L|_U\) and the sequence splits. Hence
\[
\operatorname{Pic}(U)\cong\mathbb Z[L|_U]\oplus(\mathbb Z/2)[\tau|_U].
\]
Normality of the finite quotient and codimension at least two of its complement yield \(\operatorname{Cl}(X)\cong\operatorname{Cl}(U)=\operatorname{Pic}(U)\). The class \(L\) extends as an actual line bundle because the natural permutation linearization acts trivially on each stabilizer fiber. The sign-character linearization acts by \(-1\) at a transposition fixing a repeated pair. The descent criterion for finite coarse quotients therefore excludes \(\tau\) from \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)\), and similarly excludes every class with odd sign component. Thus \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\mathbb Z[L]\) and \(\tau\) is non-Cartier.

**2. Exact integral divisor cycle map.** The product \(M\) is simply connected, and removing the diagonals of real codimension \(2n\ge4\) preserves its fundamental group. Consequently \(F\) is simply connected. Removal in this codimension also gives
\[
H^1(F,\mathbb Z)=0,\qquad H^2(F,\mathbb Z)\cong H^2(M,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^d.
\]
Apply the integral Cartan--Leray spectral sequence of the free covering \(F\to U\). In total degree two, the only potentially nonzero terms are \(H^2(\mathfrak S_d,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/2\) and \(H^2(F,\mathbb Z)^{\mathfrak S_d}\cong\mathbb Z\). The first injects, because \(H^1(F,\mathbb Z)=0\); the second survives completely because \(c_1(L|_U)\) restricts to its primitive generator. It follows that
\[
H^2(U,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2.
\]
The torsion summand is the first Chern class of the sign-character line bundle: the classifying map of the covering pulls back the nonzero element of \(H^2(\mathfrak S_d,\mathbb Z)\), the integral Bockstein of the sign homomorphism. The other summand is generated by \(c_1(L|_U)\). Thus \(c_1:\operatorname{Pic}(U)\to H^2(U,\mathbb Z)\) is an isomorphism.

Let \(B=X\setminus U\), of complex dimension at most \(N-n\). Algebraic localization for divisors and the Borel--Moore homology localization sequence, respectively, give compatible isomorphisms
\[
\operatorname{CH}_{N-1}(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{CH}^{1}(U)=\operatorname{Pic}(U),
\qquad H_{2N-2}(X,\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{2}(U,\mathbb Z).
\]
For the latter, the two adjacent Borel--Moore groups of \(B\) vanish because \(2N-2>2(N-n)+1\) for \(n\ge2\); use integral Poincaré duality only on smooth \(U\). These isomorphisms commute with cycle classes. The cycle map is therefore exactly the above first Chern class isomorphism, proving both its injectivity and surjectivity and the asserted integral groups.

**3. Local collision groups and global restriction.** At a point with support multiplicities \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_s\), the stabilizer in \(\mathfrak S_d\) is \(G_x=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathfrak S_{\lambda_i}\). Finite-group formal linearization identifies the completed local ring with the invariants of a formal power series ring on the tangent representation. Any nonidentity permutation has nonfixed normal directions of complex rank at least \(n\ge2\), hence the representation has no pseudo-reflections. The finite-quotient class-group character theorem (Benson--Nakajima) then gives
\[
\operatorname{Cl}(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{X,x})\cong\operatorname{Hom}(G_x,\mathbb C^*)
\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda_i\ge2}\mathbb Z/2.
\]
The restriction of a Cartier line bundle is locally trivial. By contrast, the global sign character restricts to the product of the signs of all the repeated blocks, which corresponds to \((1,\ldots,1)\). This proves the image and the stated cokernel, including the case \(r=0\).

**4. Canonical class and singularities.** A transposition of two tangent blocks of dimension \(n\) acts on their top exterior power by \((-1)^n\). More generally a permutation acts by the \(n\)th power of its sign. Comparing the naturally permuted external anticanonical line bundle with the canonical linearization gives
\[
K_X=-(n+1)L+(n\bmod2)\tau
\]
as a Weil divisor class. At every collision there is a stabilizer transposition, so the local character calculation makes the canonical class Cartier exactly when \(n\) is even; if \(n\) is odd its Cartier index is two. Finite quotients are normal and locally \(\mathbb Q\)-factorial; ampleness of the pullback of \(-K_X\) proves the Fano assertion.

For a permutation \(g\in G_x\) with \(c(g)\) cycles among its \(d\) letters (fixed letters included), the local tangent age is
\[
\operatorname{age}(g)=\frac n2\bigl(d-c(g)\bigr).
\]
Indeed a cycle of length \(\ell\) contributes \(n(\ell-1)/2\). The smallest nonzero age occurs for a transposition and is \(n/2\). There are no pseudo-reflections, so the Reid--Tai criterion gives canonical quotient singularities for \(n=2\), with an age-one transposition excluding terminality along a generic double collision, and terminal singularities for \(n\ge3\). The Chevalley--Shephard--Todd criterion identifies the singular locus as the collision locus.

## Verification
The argument is uniform for all integer pairs \(n,d\ge2\), not an extrapolation from numerical examples. The critical checks are: (i) the diagonal complement has codimension \(n\); (ii) the invariant Picard lattice is rank one and its generator has a genuine symmetric linearization; (iii) \(H^1(F,\mathbb Z)=0\) and \(H^2(F,\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^d\); (iv) the finite-group cohomology \(H^2(\mathfrak S_d,\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/2\) injects and the primitive free generator survives; (v) the Borel--Moore localization inequality holds already for \(n=2\); and (vi) local stabilizers have no pseudo-reflections. None of these requires an unverified infinite computation. As small checks, a single double collision has completed local class group \(\mathbb Z/2\); two distinct repeated pairs give \((\mathbb Z/2)^2\) with only the diagonal globally induced class.

## Relationship to prior work
Chen--Hu, *Chow groups of symmetric products of projective space*, arXiv:2610.20239v1, Theorem 1.1 proves split injectivity and a factorial annihilator for the finite cokernel in all dimensions. Their Proposition 5.7 explicitly constructs sign-character two-torsion for \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(\mathbb P^2)\) and tracks its stabilization in fixed cycle degree three; Theorem 1.2 detects nonalgebraic torsion in codimension two in another range. The present theorem neither repeats those results nor asserts the torsion first appears here: it computes **all** divisor classes and both integral groups for **every** \(n,d\ge2\), proves zero cokernel precisely in codimension one, and records the full collision-stratum completion map and canonical parity. The character-group theorem for local linear finite quotients is classical; Schmitt's 2024 Section 2.1 recalls it explicitly. The global integral comparison combines quotient descent, Cartan--Leray and Borel--Moore localization; neither source states this complete universal combination. A separate construction for the alternating-group quotient gives distinct local divisor groups and does not itself compute the cycle map for the symmetric-group quotient.

## Limitations
The computation concerns coarse symmetric powers of projective space over \(\mathbb C\), not arbitrary varieties or integral cohomology in other degrees. The stated groups are in degree \(2N-2\) only; codimension-two classes can fail integral algebraicity. The local groups are for completed local rings and should not be substituted for class groups of other local objects without comparison. The proof uses established quotient and topological spectral-sequence theorems. Earlier implicit presentations of the same all-parameter calculation are a remaining historical-priority risk; a targeted literature search is not a proof of uniqueness. No independent external validation is asserted.
