# Integral low-degree cohomology of Enriques complete-intersection quadrics

## Finding
Let \(m\ge2\) be an integer. A smooth Calabi–Yau complete intersection of \(2m+1\) quadrics in \(\mathbb P^{4m+1}_{\mathbb C}\), each invariant under the sign involution on the first \(2m+1\) homogeneous coordinates, admits a free such involution for generic defining quadrics. Write \(Y_m\) for one of these smooth invariant complete intersections and \(X_m=Y_m/\langle\iota\rangle\) for its quotient.

There are classes \(h,t\in H^2(X_m,\mathbb Z)\), with \(h\) the first Chern class of an equivariant descent of the hyperplane bundle and \(t=c_1(\omega_{X_m})\), for which the graded homomorphism
\[
\mathbb Z[h,t]/(2t)\longrightarrow H^*(X_m,\mathbb Z),\quad |h|=|t|=2
\]
is an isomorphism in **every cohomological degree strictly less than \(2m\)**. This is a truncated-ring statement; it makes no assertion about the middle degree or higher.

In particular, for \(1\le j<m\),
\[
H^{2j}(X_m,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\oplus(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^j,
\qquad H^{2j+1}(X_m,\mathbb Z)=0\quad(2j+1<2m).
\]
Moreover \(t^j\ne0\) whenever \(1\le j<m\), and
\[
\operatorname{Pic}(X_m)\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]
Consequently the cup-power survival length of the canonical torsion class grows without bound in this natural family, although all these cup powers disappear with rational coefficients.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbb C\), using singular cohomology with integral coefficients. The ambient coordinates are \([x_0:\cdots:x_{2m}:y_0:\cdots:y_{2m}]\). Take \(2m+1\) general quadrics of the form \(Q_a(x)+\widetilde Q_a(y)\), defining \(Y_m\). Their common zero locus is smooth of dimension \(2m\). The involution \(\iota([x:y])=[-x:y]\) preserves these quadrics. Its fixed locus in the ambient projective space is the union of the two coordinate \(\mathbb P^{2m}\)'s. The restriction of \(2m+1\) general independent quadrics to either fixed \(\mathbb P^{2m}\) has no common projective zero, so \(\iota\) acts freely on \(Y_m\). These are the complete-intersection Enriques manifolds described in the cited originating literature; the generalized Fermat members are special examples.

The degree cutoff is strict: all assertions concern \(H^q\) with \(q<2m\). The substantial primitive middle cohomology of \(Y_m\) is not analyzed.

## Proof
**(1) Integral Lefschetz range.** The usual integral Lefschetz hyperplane theorem, iterated for the smooth complete intersection \(Y_m\subset\mathbb P^{4m+1}\), gives an isomorphism of integral cohomology
\[
H^q(\mathbb P^{4m+1},\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^q(Y_m,\mathbb Z)
\quad\text{for all }q<2m.
\]
In this range the odd groups vanish and the degree-\(2j\) group is \(\mathbb Z\) generated by the \(j\)-th hyperplane power. The ambient sign action is projective linear and preserves the hyperplane class, hence the induced \(C_2\)-action is trivial on each of these cohomology groups.

**(2) Borel equivariant comparison.** Let \(G=C_2=\langle\iota\rangle\). The inclusion \(Y_m\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^{4m+1}\) induces a morphism between the integral Serre spectral sequences for the Borel fibrations over \(BG\). Their second pages are \(H^p(G,H^q(-,\mathbb Z))\). By (1), these pages agree in every row \(q<2m\). In total degrees below \(2m\), the only nonzero entries have \(p\) and \(q\) even; there are no possible nonzero differentials between these entries, since a cohomological differential increases total degree by one. A differential leaving total degree \(2m-1\) has zero source. Therefore the inclusion induces an isomorphism of integral graded rings below degree \(2m\):
\[
H^q_G(\mathbb P^{4m+1},\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^q_G(Y_m,\mathbb Z)
\quad(q<2m).
\]
Because the action on \(Y_m\) is free, its Borel construction is homotopy equivalent to \(X_m\); thus the target equals \(H^q(X_m,\mathbb Z)\).

**(3) Explicit ambient equivariant ring.** Let \(\lambda\) be the nontrivial complex character line over \(BC_2\), and put \(t=c_1(\lambda)\). Integral group cohomology gives \(H^*(BC_2,\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[t]/(2t)\). The ambient Borel construction is the projective bundle associated with the representation \(\mathbb C^{2m+1}\oplus\lambda^{\oplus(2m+1)}\). With \(h\) the first Chern class of its relative \(\mathcal O(1)\), the integral projective-bundle formula yields
\[
H^*_G(\mathbb P^{4m+1},\mathbb Z)
\cong\mathbb Z[h,t]\big/\bigl(2t,\;h^{2m+1}(h+t)^{2m+1}\bigr).
\]
If the alternate linearization convention for \(h\) is adopted, one replaces \(h\) by \(h+t\); this does not affect the asserted low-degree ring. The projective-bundle relation is homogeneous of cohomological degree \(8m+4\), far beyond the cutoff. Combining (2) and (3) gives the claimed truncated-ring isomorphism.

**(4) Canonical torsion and Picard group.** The quotient is an Enriques manifold of index two, so its canonical bundle is the nontrivial order-two character line associated to the principal \(C_2\)-cover \(Y_m\to X_m\). Thus \(t=c_1(\omega_{X_m})\), which is nonzero by the degree-two part of (3). The degree-\(2j\) monomials \(h^j,t h^{j-1},\ldots,t^j\) consist of one free generator and exactly \(j\) independent classes of order two for every \(j<m\). This also proves \(t^j\ne0\).

Finally, \(H^1(X_m,\mathcal O_{X_m})=H^2(X_m,\mathcal O_{X_m})=0\): these vanish on the Calabi–Yau covering complete intersection and remain zero on the finite quotient. The analytic exponential sequence therefore identifies \(\operatorname{Pic}(X_m)\) with \(H^2(X_m,\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\). The hyperplane descent supplies the free generator up to a possible torsion shift.

## Verification
The construction and freeness are checked directly via the two fixed coordinate linear spaces; integral Lefschetz supplies the precise strict cutoff; the Borel–Serre page has nonzero entries only in even total degree within that cutoff; the integral projective-bundle formula supplies an explicit closed presentation, avoiding unverified additive-extension assumptions. A finite monomial-count sanity check for dimensions \(4\) through \(16\) reproduced the count of independent torsion monomials in each below-middle even degree. That finite check is not used as proof of the theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Billi–Palomino–Sarti describe the Enriques generalized-Fermat quotients, their origin as complete intersections of quadrics, and distinguish their rational second Betti numbers from Hilbert-scheme examples. Boissière–Camere–Sarti give the earlier general free-quadric construction, including the fixed-coordinate-space argument. Frassineti–Rizzo–Tufo–Verni develop Cartan–Leray methods for Brauer groups of known **hyper-Kähler-cover** Enriques manifolds. The statements of those works read for this comparison do not supply the full below-middle **integral cohomology ring of the Calabi–Yau complete-intersection quotient** or its unbounded nonzero canonical cup powers. The construction, the spectral sequence, and Picard torsion alone are not novelty claims.

## Limitations
The strict degree bound \(q<2m\) is essential; primitive middle cohomology, its involution action, higher group cohomology differentials, and the complete integral cohomology ring are not determined. No independence, independent audit, exhaustive literature priority, or general theorem about all Enriques manifolds is asserted. An earlier implicit derivation from equivariant Lefschetz and projective-bundle methods remains possible.
