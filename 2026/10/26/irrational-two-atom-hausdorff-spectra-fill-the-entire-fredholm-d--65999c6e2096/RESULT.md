# Irrational two-atom Hausdorff spectra fill the entire Fredholm disk

## Finding
For positive real parameters \(a,b,u,v\) with \(u/v\notin\mathbb Q\), set \(\mu=a\delta_{e^{-u}}+b\delta_{e^{-v}}\). The Hausdorff matrix generated by this measure acts boundedly on BMOA and on the analytic Bloch space \(\mathscr B\). On **each** space its ordinary and Fredholm essential spectra coincide and are exactly
\[
\sigma(\mathcal H_\mu)=\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathcal H_\mu)
=\{\lambda\in\mathbb C:|\lambda|\le ae^u+be^v\}.
\]
On the corresponding preduals, every nonzero point in the open disk is an eigenvalue of infinite geometric multiplicity. No assertion about the point spectrum of \(\mathcal H_\mu\) is needed.

## Assumptions and scope
For a finite positive measure on \((0,1]\), write \(\mu_n=\int t^n\,d\mu(t)\) and
\[
(\mathcal H_\mu f)(z)
=\sum_{n\ge0}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk\int t^k(1-t)^{n-k}\,d\mu(t)\;\widehat f(k)\Big)z^n.
\]
Here \(\widehat f(k)\) is the \(k\)-th Taylor coefficient; the operator is interpreted through its bounded extension. BMOA carries its standard analytic bounded-mean-oscillation norm; \(\mathscr B\) the standard Bloch norm. The preduals are the Hardy space \(H^1\) and the Bergman space \(A^1\), respectively. The auxiliary Dirichlet space \(D^1\) has norm \(|f(0)|+\|f'\|_{A^1}\), and the bounded isomorphism \(J:D^1\to A^1\) is \(Jf=(zf)'\). Both measures have support strictly inside \((0,1)\), and hence satisfy \(\int t^{-1}\,d\mu<\infty\). Fredholm essential spectrum means failure of \(\lambda I-\mathcal H_\mu\) to be Fredholm.

## Proof
**1. Transfer to affine composition.** Define
\[
T(t)f(z)=f(1-e^{-t}+e^{-t}z),\quad t\ge0.
\]
On \(H^1\), the preadjoint of \(\mathcal H_\mu\) is \(A=aT(u)+bT(v)\). On \(A^1\), its preadjoint is similar, through \(J\), to the same formula on \(D^1\); explicitly it is \(JAJ^{-1}\). The boundedness and identifications follow from the coefficient pairing and the affine-semigroup formulas proved for BMOA and Bloch Hausdorff matrices in Galanopoulos–Mahillo–Oliva-Maza–Siskakis, Sections 3 and 4. The parameter restriction ensures those statements apply even though the cited paper's additional regularity assumption for its spectral mapping theorem does not.

**2. Uniform power bound.** On \(H^1\) and \(D^1\), the affine-composition semigroup obeys a uniform growth estimate \(\|T(t)\|\le C e^t\) for all \(t\ge0\). For \(H^1\) this follows from the Hardy composition-semigroup bound. For \(D^1\), use the Bergman change of variables \(\|(T(t)f)'\|_{A^1}\le e^t\|f'\|_{A^1}\) and the point-evaluation estimate \(|f(1-e^{-t})|\le C e^t\|f\|_{D^1}\). As the semigroup commutes,
\[
A^n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk a^k b^{n-k}T(ku+(n-k)v),
\qquad \|A^n\|\le C(ae^u+be^v)^n.
\]
The spectral-radius formula gives \(\sigma(A)\subseteq\{\lambda:|\lambda|\le R\}\), where \(R=ae^u+be^v\). Similarity and duality give the same spectral upper bound for the two Hausdorff operators.

**3. Interior eigenvalue multiplicity.** Fix \(\lambda\in\mathbb C\) with \(0<|\lambda|<R\). Put \(r=|\lambda|\). The strictly increasing function \(x\mapsto ae^{ux}+be^{vx}\) ranges from zero to \(R\) on \(( -\infty,1)\); let \(x_0<1\) be the unique point where its value is \(r\). Since both summands at \(x_0\) are positive, \(|ae^{ux_0}-be^{vx_0}|<r\). For a sufficiently small increase \(x\in(x_0,1)\), strict triangle inequalities hold:
\[
|ae^{ux}-be^{vx}|<r<ae^{ux}+be^{vx}.
\]
Therefore there exist *non-collinear* unimodular numbers \(\zeta_1,\zeta_2\) such that
\[
\lambda=ae^{ux}\zeta_1+be^{vx}\zeta_2.
\]
For \(G(z)=a\zeta_1e^{uz}+b\zeta_2e^{vz}\), we have \(G(x)=\lambda\), and \(G'(x)\ne0\): cancellation of the two derivative terms would force \(\zeta_1/\zeta_2\) to be a negative real number, contrary to non-collinearity.

Irrationality of \(u/v\) makes the one-parameter orbit \(y\mapsto(e^{iuy},e^{ivy})\) dense on the two-torus. Hence there are arbitrarily separated real numbers \(y_j\to+\infty\) for which this orbit converges to \((\zeta_1,\zeta_2)\). The analytic functions
\[
F(z+iy_j)=ae^{u(z+iy_j)}+be^{v(z+iy_j)}
\]
converge to \(G(z)\) uniformly on compact subsets. Choose a small disk centered at \(x\), lying strictly in \(\{\operatorname{Re}z<1\}\), with no zero of \(G-\lambda\) on its boundary and exactly one zero inside. Rouché's theorem yields a zero \(z_j\) of \(F(z+iy_j)-\lambda\) inside this disk for all sufficiently large \(j\). After taking a separated subsequence, \(s_j=z_j+iy_j\) are distinct, satisfy \(\operatorname{Re}s_j<1\), and obey \(F(s_j)=\lambda\).

For every \(s\) with \(\operatorname{Re}s<1\), the analytic function \(f_s(z)=(1-z)^{-s}\) lies in both \(H^1\) and \(D^1\). The latter follows from \(|f_s'(z)|\asymp_s|1-z|^{-\operatorname{Re}s-1}\), which is area-integrable near \(1\) precisely when \(\operatorname{Re}s<1\) for nonconstant \(f_s\). Since \(T(t)f_s=e^{st}f_s\), each \(f_{s_j}\) is an eigenvector of \(A\) with eigenvalue \(\lambda\). Distinct complex exponentials are linearly independent when evaluated at points \(z=1-e^{-h}\), \(h>0\); thus the eigenspace is infinite-dimensional on each predual.

**4. Essential spectrum and equality.** Infinite-dimensional kernel excludes Fredholmness. Therefore every \(\lambda\) with \(0<|\lambda|<R\) lies in the Fredholm essential spectrum on \(H^1\) and \(D^1\). The Fredholm essential spectrum is closed, so it contains the entire closed disk, including zero and its boundary. It is contained in the ordinary spectrum, which Step 2 bounds by the same disk. Similarity transfers this equality to the Bloch preadjoint, and adjoint duality transfers Fredholmness and spectrum to the Hausdorff operators on Bloch and BMOA. This completes the proof.

## Verification
The core identities are directly reproducible: substitute \(1-(1-e^{-t}+e^{-t}z)=e^{-t}(1-z)\) into \(f_s\), expand the commuting operator binomial, and check the triangle inequalities and derivative noncancellation in Step 3. The infinite-dimensional conclusion uses exact Rouché/Kronecker arguments, not finite enumeration. The source's independent affine-semigroup growth and predual-identification lemmas are stated with their precise space hypotheses.

## Relationship to prior work
Galanopoulos, Mahillo, Oliva-Maza and Siskakis (arXiv:2610.22554v1) establish spectral formulas on both spaces when a Mellin transform belongs to the extended Dunford–Riesz class \(\mathcal E\). The present atomic Mellin transform is a positive sum of exponential functions and is unbounded on sectors wider than a right half-plane; therefore it does **not** satisfy their \(\mathcal E\)-hypothesis. Their affine-composition estimates and duality machinery *do* apply, and are used here. Galanopoulos and Papadimitrakis (2006) treated boundedness on multiple analytic spaces. Abadias and Oliva-Maza (2024) developed generalized Hausdorff spectral theory on several spaces, including weighted Dirichlet spaces; their complete applicability to two incommensurable atoms could not be resolved from the accessible abstract, and remains a priority risk.

The irrationality hypothesis is substantive. With commensurable rates, the same preadjoint is a polynomial in a single affine composition operator; its spectral image need not be a disk. For instance, the polynomial image of \(w\mapsto w+w^2\) on \(|w|\le e\) omits the point \(-e-e^2\), whereas irrationally related rates give the whole disk of the corresponding radius. This contrast is context, not a separate originality claim for the commensurable case.

## Limitations
The proof requires two strictly positive masses and an irrational logarithmic-rate ratio. It does not classify general atomic, signed, or continuous measures; it does not characterize the Hausdorff operators' own point spectra; and it does not claim independent verification. The full text of the closely related Abadias–Oliva-Maza 2024 paper was unavailable during this comparison, so a broader theorem there may affect priority.
