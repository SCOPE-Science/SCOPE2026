# Isolated spoke-weight resonances for hub-to-rim absorption on quantum wheels

## Finding

**Theorem.** Fix an integer \(n\ge 3\). Let \(W_{n+1}(\tau)\) be the weighted wheel with rim vertices \(0,1,\ldots,n-1\) in cyclic order, hub \(h\), unit hopping on every rim edge, and hopping \(\tau>0\) on each spoke. Let \(A_{n,\tau}\) be its symmetric weighted adjacency matrix and let
\[
K_{n,\tau,\gamma}=A_{n,\tau}-i\gamma |0\rangle\langle 0|,\qquad \gamma>0.
\]
The excitation starts at \(|h\rangle\). Define its surviving norm at time \(t\) by \(P(t)=\|e^{-itK_{n,\tau,\gamma}}|h\rangle\|^2\), and its eventual survival by \(S_n(\tau,\gamma)=\lim_{t\to\infty}P(t)\).

For every integer \(k\) with \(\lfloor n/4\rfloor<k\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), define
\[
\lambda_{n,k}=2\cos(2\pi k/n),\qquad
\tau_{n,k}=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{n,k}(\lambda_{n,k}-2)}{n}},\qquad
 d_{n,k}=\begin{cases}1,&n\text{ even and }k=n/2,\\2,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]
All these spoke weights are positive and pairwise distinct, and they form the **complete** set of spoke weights supporting nonzero eventual survival from the hub. Specifically,
\[
S_n(\tau,\gamma)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{d_{n,k}(\lambda_{n,k}-2)}{(2d_{n,k}+1)\lambda_{n,k}-2d_{n,k}},
&\tau=\tau_{n,k}\text{ for some eligible }k,\\[6pt]
0,&\tau\notin\{\tau_{n,k}\}.
\end{cases}
\]
The nonzero values are independent of \(\gamma\). The number of resonant spoke weights is
\[
\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac n4\right\rfloor.
\]
The **bright subspace**, the direct sum of the one-dimensional trap-visible direction in each distinct eigenspace of \(A_{n,\tau}\), has dimension \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor+2\) off resonance and \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1\) at resonance. Its orthogonal complement, the dark subspace, has dimension \(n+1\) minus the corresponding bright dimension.

**Unweighted corollary.** At spoke weight \(\tau=1\), survival from the hub is nonzero for exactly two wheel sizes:
\[
S_n(1,\gamma)=\begin{cases}2/3,&n=3,\\1/2,&n=8,\\0,&n\ge3,\ n\notin\{3,8\}.\end{cases}
\]

## Assumptions and scope

All Hilbert spaces are \(\mathbb C^{n+1}\) with the standard orthonormal vertex basis; time and hopping energies use units with \(\hbar=1\). Rim edges, including the closing edge \(n-1\leftrightarrow0\), have positive hopping \(1\); all hub-to-rim spokes have positive hopping \(\tau\). The walker obeys continuous-time **adjacency** dynamics, not graph-Laplacian dynamics. The sole absorbing rim vertex is \(0\), with a constant positive loss parameter \(\gamma\). There is no decoherence or other source/sink. Survival denotes remaining Hilbert-space norm, not the probability of occupying the hub.

The rank of the bright subspace is equivalently the dimension of the Krylov space generated by the trap vector, \(\operatorname{span}\{|0\rangle,A|0\rangle,\ldots,A^n|0\rangle\}\). All results are for fixed finite \(n\) with arbitrarily large observation time. There is no assertion of uniformity in the limits of detuning and time.

## Proof

**1. Why the dark eigenspace determines ultimate survival.** For every real-symmetric finite matrix \(A\) and unit vector \(|w\rangle\), set \(K=A-i\gamma|w\rangle\langle w|\) with \(\gamma>0\). At each real eigenvalue \(\lambda\), let \(E_\lambda\) be the orthogonal spectral projector of \(A\), and let \(D_\lambda=\{v\in\operatorname{ran}E_\lambda:\langle w|v\rangle=0\}\). The space \(D=\bigoplus_\lambda D_\lambda\) reduces both \(A\) and the rank-one loss term; the evolution on it is unitary. Its orthogonal complement is invariant under \(K\). If \(Kv=zv\) there with \(\operatorname{Im}z=0\), taking the imaginary part of \(\langle v|Kv\rangle\) gives \(\langle w|v\rangle=0\), whence \(Av=zv\), putting \(v\in D\), a contradiction. Dissipativity makes every eigenvalue of \(K|_{D^\perp}\) have negative imaginary part, so its finite-dimensional semigroup converges to zero (also with Jordan blocks). Therefore the surviving norm tends exactly to \(\|P_D|h\rangle\|^2\). For each eigenspace \(E_\lambda\), the trap-visible subspace has dimension one if \(E_\lambda|w\rangle\ne0\), and zero otherwise.

**2. Invariant spectral sectors.** Put \(|r\rangle=n^{-1/2}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}|j\rangle\). The hub-uniform sector \(U=\operatorname{span}\{|r\rangle,|h\rangle\}\) is invariant under \(A=A_{n,\tau}\), where in the ordered basis \((|r\rangle,|h\rangle)\) it is represented by
\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}2&\tau\sqrt n\\\tau\sqrt n&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\mu_\pm=1\pm\sqrt{1+n\tau^2}.
\]
The orthogonal complement of \(U\) has no hub amplitude. Fourier diagonalization of the rim gives its eigenvalues \(\lambda_{n,k}=2\cos(2\pi k/n)\), \(1\le k\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), of multiplicity \(d_{n,k}\) as defined above. Each such eigenspace projects onto \(|0\rangle\) with squared norm \(d_{n,k}/n\), which is strictly positive. Each \(\mu_\pm\) eigenvector has nonzero rim-uniform component and is likewise visible from \(|0\rangle\).

**3. Exhaustive resonance classification.** Since \(\tau>0\), \(\mu_+>2\), whereas all nonuniform rim eigenvalues are below \(2\). The only possible hub-sector/rim-sector collision is
\[
\mu_-=\lambda_{n,k}<0.
\]
The characteristic equation for \(B\) gives \(\mu_-\,(\mu_--2)=n\tau^2\), hence the stated \(\tau_{n,k}\). Because cosine strictly decreases on \((0,\pi]\), the eligible negative rim energies are precisely the indices \(\lfloor n/4\rfloor<k\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). The function \(x\mapsto x(x-2)\) strictly decreases on \(( -2,0)\); as \(k\) increases, the corresponding resonance weights strictly increase. There is at most one collision at a given \(\tau\). All other eigenspaces remain trap-visible. Hence the number of distinct trap-visible energies is \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor+2\) off resonance and one fewer at resonance.

**4. Resonant survival coefficient.** Fix one collision with energy \(\lambda<0\), multiplicity \(d\). Write the normalized \(\mu_-\) eigenvector as \(|u\rangle=a|r\rangle+b|h\rangle\) with real positive magnitudes \(a,b\). The eigenvector equation and resonance relation give
\[
a^2=\frac{\lambda}{2(\lambda-1)},\qquad
b^2=\frac{\lambda-2}{2(\lambda-1)}.
\]
The trap projection onto the rim Fourier sector is a vector of squared norm \(d/n\), while the trap projection onto \(|u\rangle\) has squared norm \(a^2/n\). The hub overlaps only \(|u\rangle\) in this common eigenspace, with squared overlap \(b^2\). Projecting \(|h\rangle\) orthogonally away from the one trap-visible direction in that eigenspace leaves dark mass
\[
b^2\left(1-\frac{a^2}{a^2+d}\right)=\frac{b^2d}{a^2+d}
=\frac{d(\lambda-2)}{(2d+1)\lambda-2d}.
\]
The other hub-sector eigenvalue \(\mu_+\) is nondegenerate and visible, so there is no other surviving hub weight. When the parameters are off resonance, both hub-sector eigenvectors are in distinct trap-visible eigenspaces and no hub mass is dark. This proves the main formula.

**5. Only two unweighted exceptional sizes.** At \(\tau=1\), resonance requires \(n=\lambda(\lambda-2)\), where \(-2\le\lambda<0\). Therefore \(n\le8\). For \(n=3\) the value \(\lambda=-1\) gives equality, and for \(n=8\) the value \(\lambda=-2\) does likewise. For \(n=4,6\), the negative rim energies are only \(-2\) or \(-1\), yielding right-hand sides \(8\) and \(3\), respectively. For \(n=5\), the negative rim energy has irreducible polynomial \(\lambda^2+\lambda-1=0\), which is incompatible with \(\lambda^2-2\lambda-5=0\). For \(n=7\), the negative rim energies are roots of the irreducible cubic \(\lambda^3+\lambda^2-2\lambda-1=0\) (no rational roots \(\pm1\)), and so cannot satisfy the quadratic \(\lambda^2-2\lambda-7=0\). Thus no further \(n\) qualifies. Substitution into the general survival formula yields \(2/3\) for \(n=3\) and \(1/2\) for \(n=8\).

## Verification

The standalone file `wheel_verify.py` constructs the full weighted adjacency matrix independently from the Fourier proof. For every \(3\le n\le39\), it diagonalizes that matrix and computes the surviving hub component as the orthogonal projection onto the spectral trap-kernel in each eigenspace. It checks every eligible resonance, two nearby detunings, and several nonresonant weights, including exact Krylov-space ranks and rational survival masses for all unweighted wheels with \(3\le n\le13\). Execution with NumPy, SciPy and SymPy completed with 1,438 passing assertions. The independent numerical checks are finite corroboration; the preceding algebra establishes the infinite parameterized theorem.

Run `python wheel_verify.py` in an environment meeting `requirements.txt`. The numerical eigenspace grouping uses a prescribed tolerance; the finite exact SymPy calculations do not. No computation is used to infer novelty or to extend finite verification to all \(n\).

## Relationship to prior work

Anishchenko, Blumen and Mülken (2011), arXiv:1111.7065, examine spreading as edges are added to star graphs, using Laplacian-type connectivity matrices and averaged unitary spreading observables; this motivates the sensitivity of hub-based networks but does not specify an absorbing rim trap, spoke-weight resonance set, or eventual survival classification on weighted wheels. Razzoli, Paris and Bordone (2020), arXiv:2011.13794, prove the general trap-visible invariant-subspace method and evaluate several graph families (complete bipartite, strongly regular, joined complete, and simplex-like examples); their general principle underlies Step 1 but does not state the wheel-specific resonance classification. Razzoli, Bordone and Paris (2022), arXiv:2202.13824, give an adjacency reduction for a universal hub on graphs regular away from that hub (their Proposition 1) and derive wheel-based statements for hub-observed motion, alongside Laplacian transport results for marked **fully connected** vertices. Their hub reduction supplies the two-dimensional sector in Step 2, but the present lossy **rim** detector, Fourier collision conditions, resonance survival coefficients, and arbitrary positive spoke weights require the additional argument here. These comparisons do not establish exhaustive historical priority.

## Limitations

The formula is for a single excitation, fixed finite \(n\ge3\), uniform positive spoke couplings, and one lossy rim vertex. The discontinuous long-time survival as \(\tau\) crosses an isolated resonance is an order-of-limits phenomenon: no uniform finite-time discontinuity is claimed. The theorem does not determine absorption-time distributions, decoherence stability, arbitrary trap locations, nonuniform spoke weights, or thermodynamic limits. It does not imply that nonresonant wheels lack dark states: rather, the specified hub source has no overlap with them. Historical coverage beyond the explicitly inspected work may remain.
