# Isomorphism rigidity, fan symmetries, and asymptotic Kähler–Einstein scarcity for root-twisted toric Fano fibrations

## Finding
Let \(A_k\) denote the number of isomorphism classes of nontrivially root-twisted \(\mathrm{dP}_3\)-fibrations with \(k\ge 2\) base factors and nonzero, zero-sum \(A_2\)-root twists, as constructed by Wuebben [1]. Let \(E_k\) count those classes that are Kähler–Einstein. Isomorphism here is *abstract algebraic-variety isomorphism over* \(\mathbb C\), not merely equality of twist presentations. The exact series are
\[
\sum_{k\ge2}A_kz^k=\frac1{(1-z^2)(1-z^3)(1-z^4)(1-z^6)}-1,
\]
\[
\sum_{k\ge2}E_kz^k=\frac1{(1-z^2)(1-z^4)(1-z^6)}-1+\frac{z^3}{(1-z^3)(1-z^6)}.
\]
In particular, \(A_k\sim k^3/864\), while \(E_k=O(k^2)\), so the Kähler–Einstein fraction tends to zero among these varieties as dimension grows. All varieties counted have anticanonically stable tangent bundles by [1]; this stability assertion is an input, not a new result.

The proof also gives a complete isomorphism invariant and the full finite fan symmetry group. Choose \(\gamma_0,\gamma_1,\gamma_2\) with \(\gamma_0+\gamma_1+\gamma_2=0\) and root hexagon \(R=\{\pm\gamma_j:0\le j\le2\}\). Every zero-sum nonempty multiset has, uniquely up to the dihedral hexagon action, multiplicities
\[
m_{\gamma_j}=d+q_j,\qquad m_{-\gamma_j}=q_j,\qquad d\ge0,\quad0\le q_0\le q_1\le q_2,
\]
with \(k=3d+2(q_0+q_1+q_2)\), excluding the all-zero tuple. Two members of the family are isomorphic precisely when their quadruples \((d,q_0,q_1,q_2)\) coincide.

For a specific twist list \(t=(t_1,\ldots,t_k)\), write \(m_\rho\) for the multiplicity of root \(\rho\). If \(D_6\) denotes the 12-element automorphism group of the root hexagon and \(s(m)=|\operatorname{Stab}_{D_6}(m)|\), then the group of lattice automorphisms preserving its fan has order
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{fan}}(\Sigma(t))|=2^k\,s(m)\prod_{\rho\in R}m_\rho!.
\]
More precisely, with \(\Gamma_t=\{(\phi,\sigma)\in D_6\times S_k:\phi(t_i)=t_{\sigma(i)}\ \text{for all }i\}\), this finite group is \((\mathbb Z/2)^k\rtimes\Gamma_t\). It is **not** the full positive-dimensional algebraic automorphism group of the variety.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(\mathbb C\). Let \(N_f=\mathbb Z\gamma_0\oplus\mathbb Z\gamma_1\), with \(\gamma_2=-\gamma_0-\gamma_1\), and let \(N=N_f\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb Z f_i\). Every twist \(t_i\in R\) is nonzero and \(\sum_i t_i=0\). Define the fan \(\Sigma(t)\) with six fiber rays \(R\), two rays \(u_i^+=f_i+t_i\), \(u_i^-=-f_i\) per base factor, and maximal cones generated by adjacent fiber roots together with one ray from each base pair. Wuebben [1, Definition 7.1 and Lemma 7.4] proves these fans are smooth, projective, and Fano; [1, Theorems 7.2–7.3] supplies stability and the exact Kähler–Einstein symmetry criterion. We classify only this **zero-sum, nonzero-root** construction, not all toric Fano manifolds or unstable/zero-twist members.

## Proof
**1. Intrinsic fiber sublattice.** A pair of opposite primitive generators of rays must have vanishing sum in \(N\). For the six fiber roots there are exactly three antipodal pairs. Every base pair sums to \(u_i^++u_i^-=t_i\ne0\). Distinct base indices have linearly independent nonzero images in \(N/N_f\); no root–base pair is opposite. Thus the only opposite ray pairs are the three root pairs, and the rank-two lattice \(N_f\) that they generate is intrinsic to the fan. Any lattice isomorphism carrying \(\Sigma(t)\) to \(\Sigma(t')\) restricts on \(N_f\) to a hexagon automorphism \(\phi\in D_6\).

**2. Rigidity of twist multiplicities.** In the quotient \(N/N_f\), the remaining rays map to \(\pm f_i\). Their antipodal pairs must be permuted by the induced quotient automorphism. The sum of the two primitive lifts in the \(i\)-th pair is the intrinsic fiber vector \(t_i\). Consequently a fan isomorphism yields a permutation \(\sigma\) satisfying \(\phi(t_i)=t'_{\sigma(i)}\). Conversely, for any such \(\phi,\sigma\), the unimodular map \(\phi|_{N_f}=\phi\) and \(f_i\mapsto f'_{\sigma(i)}\) sends every ray, and by the explicit maximal-cone rule every cone, to a cone of the second fan. Hence the multiset modulo \(D_6\) is a complete **toric** isomorphism invariant.

The same conclusion holds for unmarked abstract isomorphisms: in a complete toric variety the acting algebraic torus has dimension equal to the variety, so it is a maximal torus of the identity component of its algebraic automorphism group. If an abstract isomorphism transports one torus action to the other variety, conjugacy of maximal tori in that connected algebraic group makes it torus-equivariant after composing with an automorphism of the target. An automorphism of a torus action can include a translation; composing with a torus element makes the resulting isomorphism preserve identity on the dense torus and therefore correspond to a fan isomorphism. This uses the standard conjugacy theorem for maximal tori, not any claim that all automorphisms are toric.

**3. Finite fan symmetry group.** Fix \(t\). For each \(i\), the map acting identically on \(N_f\) and the other base coordinates but taking \(f_i\mapsto-f_i-t_i\) exchanges \(u_i^+\) and \(u_i^-\), and preserves the fan. These \(k\) commuting involutions form \((\mathbb Z/2)^k\). Every fan symmetry induces \((\phi,\sigma)\in\Gamma_t\) as above; composing with pair-flips removes any base-pair sign changes, leaving the standard lift \(f_i\mapsto f_{\sigma(i)}\). The lifts form a subgroup \(\Gamma_t\), splitting the exact sequence. For each stabilizing \(\phi\), the number of compatible index permutations equals \(\prod_{\rho\in R}m_\rho!\). Hence the asserted group and order formula.

**4. Unique parameters.** Put \(a_j=m_{\gamma_j}\), \(b_j=m_{-\gamma_j}\). Since \(\gamma_0+\gamma_1+\gamma_2=0\) and \(\gamma_0,\gamma_1\) are linearly independent, \(\sum_i t_i=0\) exactly when \(a_0-b_0=a_1-b_1=a_2-b_2=:\delta\). Global hexagon inversion replaces \(\delta\) by \(-\delta\); set \(d=|\delta|\) and \(q_j=\min(a_j,b_j)\). The residual hexagon symmetry permutes the three \(q_j\), giving a unique ordered triple. The tuple determines the multiset and has total length \(k=3d+2\sum_jq_j\). This proves both necessity and sufficiency of equality of normal-form parameters.

**5. Exact generating series.** A weakly ordered triple of nonnegative integers contributes the partition generating factor \(1/[(1-z^2)(1-z^4)(1-z^6)]\). The free integer \(d\ge0\) contributes \(1/(1-z^3)\). Remove the empty tuple to obtain the formula for \(A_k\). By Wuebben's criterion [1, Theorem 7.3], the variety is Kähler–Einstein exactly when \(d=0\) (negation symmetry) or \(d>0\) with \(q_0=q_1=q_2\) (threefold rotation symmetry). The first set contributes \(1/[(1-z^2)(1-z^4)(1-z^6)]-1\); the second contributes \(z^3/[(1-z^3)(1-z^6)]\). These cases are disjoint, proving the second generating series.

**6. Asymptotic conclusion.** The unrestricted series has its unique fourth-order unit-circle pole at \(z=1\), with leading term \(1/[144(1-z)^4]\). All other unit-circle poles have smaller order. Coefficient extraction gives \(A_k\sim k^3/864\). The Kähler–Einstein series has poles of order at most three, so \(E_k=O(k^2)\). Their ratio therefore tends to zero. All claims follow for integer \(k\ge2\), with dimension \(k+2\).

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` directly enumerates all multiplicity vectors on the six nonzero \(A_2\)-roots with zero sum through \(k=14\), computes their full 12-element dihedral orbits, and compares them with the proposed ordered-parameter classification. It independently compares the predicted generating functions with exact integer coefficients through \(k=99\), computes sample fan symmetry orders, and tests the Kähler–Einstein symmetry criterion. Run `python3 verify.py`; the script uses only the Python standard library. Finite verification illustrates the theorem; all-\(k\) conclusions are proved above.

## Relationship to prior work
Wuebben [1] defines the fans and proves smoothness, Fano property, stable tangent bundles, and the precise Kähler–Einstein symmetry criterion. It uses a normal form for root multiplicities in the proof of its Kähler–Einstein theorem and tabulates the nine hexagon-symmetry classes through dimension eight. All these results and the use of hexagon symmetry are **prior work**. The added statements are the intrinsic opposite-ray rigidity and abstract-isomorphism classification, the exact finite fan-symmetry groups, the closed all-dimension isomorphism-class enumerators, and the asymptotic rarity of Kähler–Einstein classes in this specific stable family. Standard maximal-torus conjugacy and formal partition enumeration are tools, not novel general theorems. This is a literature-supported originality assessment rather than a guarantee of historical priority.

## Limitations
The root-twist fan isomorphism theorem depends on every twist being nonzero. Allowing a zero twist introduces additional antipodal ray pairs and invalidates the intrinsic-subfan argument. The Fano and tangent-stability properties, and the Kähler–Einstein criterion, are imported from [1], not reproved here. The automorphism formula concerns the discrete lattice/fan symmetry group, not the full automorphism group or its unipotent radicals. The isomorphism classification is limited to the described root-twist family; further isomorphisms outside the family are irrelevant to the count. The series enumerate abstract varieties, not marked toric presentations, and their asymptotics do not assert anything about all stable toric Fano manifolds.
