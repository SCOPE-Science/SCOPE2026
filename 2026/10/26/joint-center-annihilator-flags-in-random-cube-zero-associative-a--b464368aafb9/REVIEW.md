# Scientific review

## Correctness
The nested-center-and-annihilator dimensions are reduced to independent linear constraints on a bilinear map. The first containment count has dimension \(D_r(a,b)\), and independence of the output-coordinate forms exactly expresses surjectivity. Two subspace-lattice Möbius inversions count **exact** flags, yielding a nonnegative joint distribution summing to \(F_q(r^2,s)\). Independent exhaustive coefficient comparisons in seven prime-field cases passed. For the fixed-\(s\) asymptotics, one-line first moments and two-line Bonferroni bounds have different exponents for \(s\ge2\); the conditional law follows from inclusion of the annihilator in the center. Risk: the proof does not cover output dimension increasing with the input dimension, nor does finite verification establish an infinite statement.

## Originality
The exact annihilator marginal is already known for this type of algebra and is not represented as new. A joint nested flag contains genuinely more information: the marginal alone does not determine the center–annihilator correlation. Direct comparison with the inspected sections of Bahturin–Olshanskii (arXiv:2406.15631), Ignatyev–Kaygorodov–Popov (arXiv:2103.01036), and Verhulst's 2020 algebra-orbit counts found no stronger statement implying the full joint formula. Published database and thematic searches for common alternating radicals and finite-field nilpotent algebra centers likewise returned no equivalent; an unindexed equivalent Möbius-inversion enumeration remains a residual priority risk.

## Value
The pair of nested ideals \(\operatorname{Ann}(A_B)\subseteq Z(A_B)\) is a canonical algebraic invariant. Its **joint**, dimension-refined census resolves how noncentrality and annihilation interact in a natural complete family, beyond either one-dimensional marginal; the fixed-output-size threshold between a forced center kernel at \(s=1\) and generic vanishing at \(s\ge2\) has a precise conditional decay exponent. This provides a reusable benchmark for finite-field algebra statistics without claiming an isomorphism classification or a larger theorem.
