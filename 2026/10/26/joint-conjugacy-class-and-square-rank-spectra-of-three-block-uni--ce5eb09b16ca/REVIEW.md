# Review: three-block unipotent radical class-size and square-rank census

## Correctness (PASS)
The commutator image is the sum of the column-constrained and row-constrained matrix subspaces, of dimensions \(cr\) and \(as\), with intersection dimension \(rs\). This gives each class size \(q^{cr+as-rs}\). Fixing the kernel of the first adjacent block, the rank-of-product condition is an intersection-dimension condition for an image subspace; a graph-map count and a surjection count give every joint coefficient. The full-row-rank coupling gives the stated fixed-end limit. A standalone independent linear-map enumeration agrees coefficient by coefficient in eleven finite cases. The proof, rather than those finite checks, establishes the infinite family.

## Originality (PASS against inspected sources, with residual risk)
Hennecart–Perry (2022), §4, establish the three-block **total commuting-pair count**, which aggregates centralizer sizes; the present claim determines each joint size/square-rank stratum. Goodwin–Röhrle (2009) prove polynomiality and give centralizer structures via parabolic-orbit methods, without the explicit joint distribution in the inspected text. Diaconis–Malliaris (2021) treat the Heisenberg one-dimensional-end-block case, and existing order-two/order-four counts for the ordinary \(3\)-by-\(3\) Heisenberg group are acknowledged as prior special cases. Local records on three-block total commutativity and four-block class-size spectra were compared by implication: neither asserts a joint class-size/square-rank law for all three positive block dimensions. Residual risk: an equivalent incidence/refined conjugacy enumeration might appear under random-matrix product or parabolic-unipotent terminology.

## Value (PASS)
For a natural infinite family of class-two algebra groups, the formula counts **conjugacy classes**, not only group elements, simultaneously by a group-theoretic invariant and a matrix-theoretic nilpotence invariant. It shows by exact counts that the same class-size stratum can contain elements with three different square ranks and, in characteristic two, two different element orders. The fixed-end limiting law isolates a genuine distinction between the concentration of class size and persistent randomness of square rank.

## Limits and verification
No individual conjugacy class representatives or ambient-parabolic orbit classification are claimed. Arithmetic enumeration over small prime fields and exact coefficient identities for several prime powers supplement, not replace, the proof. The novelty statement is bounded by the primary texts inspected; no independent priority certification has been performed.
