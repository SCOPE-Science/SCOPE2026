The mixed-test estimate follows by deleting the priority neighborhood of a rare-gap event, applying the Poisson Stein equation, and using the exact neighboring joint probabilities. The finite verifier confirms selected small-size identities but does not establish the limit.

The 2019 original model paper gives earlier root and gap results, but no joint rare-gap result. General mixed Gaussian–Poisson methods exist; an unlocated general permutation-pattern theorem might imply this statement. That is a priority risk, not positive evidence of coverage.

A joint limit determines whether uncommon large voids bias macroscopic density fluctuations and allows simultaneous probabilistic inference. The quantitative mixed error addresses precisely the dependence that marginal limit laws leave unresolved.
