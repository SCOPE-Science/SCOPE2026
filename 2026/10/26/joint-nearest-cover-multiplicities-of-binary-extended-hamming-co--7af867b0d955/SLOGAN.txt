For binary extended Hamming codes, the rank and first row of a joint syndrome determine both its exact minimum number of accessed coordinates and the number of *all* optimal joint corrections. Zero-first-row syndromes require one extra access and have \(2^m|\mathrm{GL}_r(2)|/(r+1)!\) optimal corrections; at fixed query count, a probability approaching \(2^{-t}\) produces exponentially many minimizing solutions.
