# Mathematical review

## Correctness
PASS. With zero-scalar vertex set \(S\) and internal supported-arrow set \(F\), the exact basis multiplication equations show that the right principal ideal dimension is \(\sum_{v\notin S}(1+d^+(v))+|T(F)|\), while the left principal ideal dimension is \(\sum_{v\notin S}(1+d^-(v))+|U(F)|\). The residual vertex columns for different targets/sources have disjoint arrow supports. Every coefficient of the bivariate polynomial is obtained by an explicit disjoint-stratum count with multiplicity \((q-1)^{n-|S|+|F|}q^{m-|E[S]|}\). The formula also treats loops, parallel arrows and isolated vertices. It is not inferred from small examples.

## Originality
PASS on the accessible scientific comparison, with residual historical-priority risk. Męcel's 2016 classification concerns finiteness of conjugacy classes of one-sided ideals over algebraically closed fields, not counts of generators stratified simultaneously by the two dimensions. Lanzilotta--Marcos--Mata (2015) concern Igusa--Todorov homological functions; Nasr-Isfahani (2016) concerns singular equivalence under quiver moves. Published commutator-rank and commuting-tuple formulas for radical-square-zero quivers concern different images: for the three orientations of the same three-vertex path the commuting-tuple conditions agree, whereas the new right-ideal rank-one coefficients differ. Searches for finite-field principal-ideal size enumerators and the exact quiver/algebra alias did not reveal a statement subsuming this bivariate formula. This is not proof of historical uniqueness.

## Value
PASS. The object is a standard family in finite-dimensional representation theory, and the invariant gives the complete element-weighted joint dimensions of its principal one-sided ideals over every finite field, rather than only an upper bound or one special rank. The exact formula treats all quivers using combinatorial induced-arrow support data. Its sharp three-orientation corollary separates algebras that their commuting-equation invariants cannot distinguish. The actual distinction is a statement about information content, not a claim of a new classification of arbitrary modules.

## Limitations and prior literature
The published conjugacy-class finiteness theorem is not the present enumeration. Existing singular-equivalence and homological results are not claimed as new. Earlier work on commutator distributions is acknowledged. An older calculation expressed in other terminology or implicit in a detailed quiver-ideal classification remains possible.
