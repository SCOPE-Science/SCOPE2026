# Scientific assessment

## Correctness

PASS. The proof classifies every partial isometry of rank at least two by a unique affine extension; separates nonzero translations into arithmetic progressions; and establishes that a nilpotent reflection must omit every fixed midpoint and at least one endpoint of each mirror pair. The sole overlapping nonzero cases are \(n(n-1)\) rank-one maps, corrected coefficientwise. This yields an exact finite-state recurrence for the full joint distribution. The hockey-stick identity and explicit parity multiplicities of reflection pairs yield the rank and total formulas, including \(n=1,2\). The independent program enumerated all relevant partial injections through \(n=8\), and checked rank/total identities through \(n=100\), with every comparison passing. Enumeration is supporting evidence, not an infinite proof.

## Originality

PASS, with residual historical uncertainty. The full texts of the 2010 chain partial-isometry precursor and the 2014 combinatorial sequel establish affine orientations, rank/height counts, fixed-point counts and the ambient monoid size, but not exact nilpotency-index distribution. In particular the fixed-point-free locus is strictly larger than the nilpotent locus because of reflection two-cycles. The 2024 partial-contraction analysis covers a different ambient monoid and only selected nilpotent classes according to its accessible abstract. The 2004 symmetric-inverse-semigroup study concerns all partial injections rather than the metric-preserving submonoid. Public sequence indexes and relevant mathematical research records were searched for equivalent or stronger joint enumerators, without a directly covering statement found. Search absence alone does not establish global priority; an implicit equivalent formula remains a risk.

## Value

PASS. The rank/step-to-zero profile is an intrinsic finite-semigroup invariant: nilpotency length governs successive powers, while rank describes the size of the partial action. Prior known height and fixed-point enumerations cannot retrieve this joint law, since fixed-point-free reflections may contain cycles of length two. The theorem gives a reproducible transfer scheme for every coefficient, a simple parity-resolved rank polynomial, a closed total and an asymptotic density of \(1/3\) across an infinite natural family, including sharp extremal nilpotency-index multiplicities.
