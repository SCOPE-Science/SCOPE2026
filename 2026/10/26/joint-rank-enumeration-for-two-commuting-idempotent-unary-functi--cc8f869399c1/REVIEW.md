# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The composite of commuting idempotents is a retraction onto their common fixed points. Its canonical fibers have an exhaustive partition into the root, elements fixed by the first operation only, elements fixed by the second operation only, and elements fixed by neither. The latter elements independently choose images in the two fixed-point sets. This is a bijective model classification, and the labeled-set exponential formula yields exactly the displayed trivariate generating function and recurrence for all finite domain sizes. A direct brute-force checker also compared every rank-profile coefficient through five labels and unweighted coefficients through ten. Finite tests alone would not prove the theorem; the constructive bijection does.

**Originality — PASS within examined sources.** The closest primary source is Kuželka, arXiv:2608.30580v1. Its one-function tractability and two-function existential hardness theorems do not count commuting-idempotent pairs or provide their joint rank distribution. Paulista's arXiv:2511.09495 concerns extremal commutative subsemigroups, not the number of all ordered idempotent pairs. The accessible small-graph descriptions of commuting idempotents are insufficient to imply the general formula. Searches included the aliases commuting retractions, commuting set projections, and finite actions of a four-element semilattice. Priority remains conditional: historical finite semilattice-action enumeration may cover this specialization, and full text was unavailable for two related sources.

**Value — PASS.** The result computes a natural three-dimensional invariant of finite models of a fixed one-variable sentence inside a syntactic fragment that admits hard model-count problems. The exact joint-rank formula, together with the canonical-fiber proof and effective recurrence, provides structural information beyond a small numerical census and has clear applications to conditioned counting of commuting retractions.

**Limitations.** Only labeled ordered pairs of total idempotent functions are addressed; there is no theorem about all two-function sentences, no unlabeled enumeration, and no asymptotic claim. An equivalent specialization of general semigroup-action enumerators is the main unresolved originality risk.
