# Review of the leaf-sibling Palm theorem

## Correctness: PASS
The event that two specified leaves share a specified neighbor bijects with all trees on the other \(n-2\) labels. The conditional Prüfer code proves the complete Palm generating function. A two-chain birth coupling proves genuine first-order stochastic dominance; the exact mean and limiting covariance follow algebraically. Exact exhaustive checks independently decoded 280388 trees for orders four through eight; the nonstandard monotone-coupling argument was separately reconstructed at its equality and strictness boundaries.

## Originality: PASS, with acknowledged historical priority risk
Erdős–Rényi (1963), pp. 312–313, already give the single-cherry indicator probability and mean cherry count, so these are not claimed as new. The inspected original text does not give the complete conditional leaf law, stochastic ordering or mixed covariance. Junk–Schmidt–Weber (2020), §3, require a degree-three center and establish a different cherry-existence result. Choi–Kaur–Wu (2021), §§1–2, use binary phylogenetic models with a fixed number of leaves, not uniform Cayley trees. No equivalent statement was identified in the inspected primary literature; unindexed literature can still affect priority. The same-object Erdős–Rényi enumeration is explicitly recognized and not re-presented as novel.

## Mathematical value: PASS
The count-biased Palm law is a natural joint-statistic question when leaf-pair motifs are used to detect tree symmetries. The theorem upgrades a previously known expectation into a complete conditional distribution and stochastic ordering, with a positive linear covariance constant. These are useful for testing how local symmetry motifs select globally leaf-rich trees and are not determined by the separate means of cherry and leaf counts.

## Limitations
No assertion is made for a degree-three-restricted cherry, uniform phylogenetic models, general tree ensembles, conditioning only on the existence of a cherry, or independent audit. The asserted stochastic comparison is count-size-biased, and older unindexed equivalents are a residual risk.
