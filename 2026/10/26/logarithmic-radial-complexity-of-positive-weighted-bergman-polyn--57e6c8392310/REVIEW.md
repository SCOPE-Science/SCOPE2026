## Correctness — PASS
The proof begins from the exactly normalized beta moments of weighted Bergman monomials. A two-sided comparison with \((k+1)^{-\beta}\) and a fully quantified dyadic exponential-peak lemma imply that one positive radial atom contributes at most a bounded amount to the sum of degree-normalized monomial tests. Hence the lower bound counts distinct radii, independent of how angular sites are arranged. A dyadic partition in squared radius with the lower endpoint of each cell, combined with \(N+1\) roots of unity, yields all monomial eigenvalues between an explicit positive constant times their true beta moments and those moments. ## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge assessment)
The 2020 Gröchenig–Ortega-Cerdà preprint establishes Bergman polynomial sampling via existing sampling sequences and geometric kernel estimates, including a general Carleson-measure upper condition. It does not state a lower bound for the number of distinct radii under arbitrary positive weights, nor the matching radial-count construction. Their 2022 Fock-space paper treats Gaussian radial geometry; Gaussian factorial moments do not imply the beta-moment logarithmic count. The closest visible mathematical-result records on radial Bergman circle traces address one-circle operator norms, not multiring frame complexity. McLean (2016) already gives positive exponential-sum approximations with logarithmic node complexity on finite intervals; that anticipates the construction, but the inspected full text does not state the universal *lower bound* for arbitrary positive weighted point samplers or the sharp order of physical radii. A hidden implication from broader optimal-approximation theory remains a risk; absence of a search match does not establish priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The number of independent radial levels is a natural acquisition resource in concentric-circle sampling, distinct from total point count. The theorem supplies a necessary resource lower bound against every positive weighted configuration and a constructive matching order simultaneously for all power weights. In particular, it distinguishes the logarithmic Bergman boundary-scale hierarchy from the different radial hierarchy of Gaussian Fock sampling. This is an exact asymptotic order for a natural geometric invariant, not a cosmetic constant improvement.

## Limits
The numeric constants are not optimal. No statement is made for negative/complex sampling weights, optimal total sample counts, or other domains. Equivalence to broad, classical positive exponential-sum approximation theorems cannot be excluded without additional specialist historical review.
