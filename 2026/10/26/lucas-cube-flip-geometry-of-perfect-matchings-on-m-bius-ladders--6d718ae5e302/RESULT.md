# Lucas-cube flip geometry of perfect matchings on Möbius ladders

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge4\), let \(M_{2n}\) be the Möbius ladder formed from the cycle on \(2n\) vertices by adjoining the \(n\) antipodal rungs. Define \(\mathcal F_n\) to have one vertex for each perfect matching of \(M_{2n}\), with two vertices adjacent when their symmetric difference is the edge set of a four-cycle (equivalently, a local switch replaces the two opposite matched edges of that four-cycle by the other two).

Write \(\Lambda_n\) for the Lucas cube: its vertices are the independent sets of the \(n\)-cycle, and two such sets are adjacent exactly when one cycle vertex is added or removed. Then

\[
\boxed{\mathcal F_n\cong\begin{cases}\Lambda_n,&n\text{ even},\\\Lambda_n\sqcup2K_1,&n\text{ odd}.\end{cases}}
\]

In particular, with \(F_0=0,F_1=1,F_{j+2}=F_{j+1}+F_j\) and \(L_0=2,L_1=1,L_{j+2}=L_{j+1}+L_j\), the number of perfect matchings is \(L_n+2\mathbf1_{\{n\text{ odd}\}}\), and the number of switch edges is \(nF_{n-1}\). The unique nontrivial component has diameter \(2\lfloor n/2\rfloor\); the graph has exactly two isolated vertices for odd \(n\) and none for even \(n\). For any matchings \(P,Q\) in the Lucas component with associated circular binary words \(b(P),b(Q)\), the exact switch distance is \(d(P,Q)=\operatorname{wt}(b(P)\oplus b(Q))\).

## Assumptions and scope
Take vertices \(u_i,v_i\), indexed by \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). The rungs are \(u_iv_i\). For \(0\le i<n-1\), the horizontal edges are \(u_iu_{i+1}\) and \(v_iv_{i+1}\); at the seam the two horizontal edges are \(u_{n-1}v_0\) and \(v_{n-1}u_0\). Their union is the single boundary cycle of length \(2n\). The canonical square \(Q_i\) has the two rungs at columns \(i,i+1\) as one pair of opposite edges and the two corresponding horizontal edges as its other pair. For \(n\ge4\), these \(n\) squares are all four-cycles; in particular no four-cycle uses exactly one rung, since opposite endpoints on the boundary are distance \(n\ge4\). The result concerns four-cycle switches, not arbitrary alternating-cycle switches, and excludes the exceptional six-vertex \(M_6\cong K_{3,3}\), which has additional four-cycles.

## Proof
**1. Construction of a complete main family.** For any independent set \(S\subseteq E(C_n)\), place both horizontal edges of \(Q_i\) into a matching whenever the edge \(\{i,i+1\}\) belongs to \(S\); at each remaining column place its rung. Nonadjacency of edges in \(S\) ensures that no column is covered twice. Every vertex is matched exactly once, so this defines a perfect matching \(P(S)\). Distinct \(S\) give distinct \(P(S)\): the paired horizontal edges record precisely the members of \(S\).

**2. Exhaustiveness when a rung is present.** Let \(P\) be any perfect matching containing a rung. Cut the twisted strip at an occupied rung. Between successive occupied rungs, each maximal run of columns with no rungs must be covered solely by horizontal edges. Reading from either occupied-rung boundary, the first remaining upper and lower vertices must be matched into the next column, and subsequently the same is forced in alternating adjacent column pairs. Thus the run has even length and is tiled uniquely by pairs of parallel horizontal edges. Across the twisted seam the labels upper/lower are exchanged, but this forced pairing is unchanged. The resulting horizontal pairs select pairwise vertex-disjoint edges of \(C_n\), giving a unique independent \(S\) such that \(P=P(S)\).

**3. Rung-free matchings.** With no rung, a perfect matching is a perfect matching of the single length-\(2n\) boundary cycle. There are precisely two, obtained by alternating its edges. The two horizontal edges of a square are located \(n\) boundary-edge positions apart. If \(n\) is even, both have the same alternating parity; hence either both are selected or neither is. These two matchings equal \(P(S)\) for the two perfect matchings \(S\) of the even cycle \(C_n\). If \(n\) is odd, exactly one horizontal edge of each square is selected in either boundary matching, so neither equals \(P(S)\). Both have no rungs and no paired horizontal edges, hence admit no square switch. They are two additional isolated configurations.

**4. Adjacency.** A square switch in \(P(S)\) replaces two rungs by a horizontal pair exactly when its edge can be added to \(S\) while retaining independence. The reverse switch removes that edge. Hence \(P(S)\sim P(T)\) precisely when \(S\triangle T\) is one edge of \(C_n\). This is the Lucas-cube adjacency, proving the graph decomposition. Its main component is connected because every \(S\) can be reduced to the empty set by single deletions.

**5. Counts and distances.** Independent sets of \(C_n\) are enumerated by the Lucas number \(L_n\). To count Lucas-cube edges, designate the unique newly inserted cycle edge position \(i\); the two adjacent positions are absent and the remaining \(n-3\) positions form a path, with \(F_{n-1}\) independent subsets. Summing over \(n\) positions yields \(nF_{n-1}\) edges. A switch modifies one binary coordinate, so \(d(P(S),P(T))\ge |S\triangle T|\). Deleting all elements of \(S\setminus T\) and then adding all of \(T\setminus S\) gives a valid sequence of exactly that many switches. Therefore equality holds. The maximum possible symmetric-difference size is \(2\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), achieved by opposite alternating independent sets (when \(n\) is odd, leave one cycle position unused). This is the diameter of the connected component. The frozen matchings are exactly the two additional rung-free matchings from step 3.

## Verification
Two standalone programs and their exact recorded outputs accompany the result. `check_direct.py` enumerates every perfect matching of the \(2n\)-vertex graph by vertex-first recursion, builds every legal four-cycle switch, and computes component sizes for \(4\le n\le16\). `check_construct.py` instead enumerates cyclic binary independent sets, reconstructs the graph matchings, checks both directions of each single-square transition, constructs the two exceptional boundary matchings for odd \(n\), and verifies the Lucas counts, switch counts and Hamming diameters on the same range. Both passed when executed from these package contents. These finite checks independently test the symbolic classification but are not the proof for unrestricted \(n\).

## Relationship to prior work
Lucas cubes, their vertex counts, edge counts and metric structure predate this result: see Ashrafi et al. (2014) and Codara–D'Antona (2014). The 2026 analysis by Agarwal–Basak describes the perfect-matching *simplicial complex* of Möbius ladders, including the presence of two special perfect-matching facets at odd width; it does not state or derive a four-cycle switch-graph identification. Therefore the Lucas-cube structure itself and the existence of the two exceptional matchings are not individually asserted to be new. The claimed contribution is the explicit all-width **matching-to-circular-independent-set isomorphism preserving local four-cycle switches**, including identification of the complete frozen sector and exact switch metrics. Matching-complex homotopy groups are not interchangeable with switch-graph components.

## Limitations
The decomposition concerns \(n\ge4\) and local four-cycle switches. The six-vertex case with additional square cycles, and transformations along longer alternating cycles, are excluded. The Lucas-cube counts and its well-known properties are used rather than rediscovered. A published but unindexed treatment of the identical Möbius-ladder local-switch graph may exist. All scientific review reported here is internal self-review; independent scientific audit remains unperformed.
