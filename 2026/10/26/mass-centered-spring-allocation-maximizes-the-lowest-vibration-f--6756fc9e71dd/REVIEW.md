# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** The centered affine test mode gives a universal sharp Rayleigh upper bound; the positive cumulative-moment construction attains it. The oscillation theorem identifies it as the lowest nonzero mode, and equality fixes all springs uniquely. The harmonic-number compliance comparison follows from the exact springs and series force balance. The independently run checker passed 14,854 assertion groups; numerical testing is not used as an infinite-dimensional proof.

**Originality (PASS within bounded examined literature).** The central graph-Laplacian spectral design method and equal-mass parabolic profile are known through Boyd (2006) and Wan et al. (2008). The assessed distinct proposition concerns arbitrary positive inertial masses in a *generalized* eigenproblem, its unique cumulative mass-center spring law, and an explicit engineering interpretation of the compliance trade-off. Ordinary symmetric-Laplacian results do not immediately state the mass-metric conclusion. The source is close and may suggest the adaptation; a stronger unlocated treatment of reversible weighted birth–death chains or structural vibration optimization could subsume the claim. The harmonic-number penalty alone is not asserted as priority-bearing new science.

**Value (PASS).** Optimizing a physical fundamental vibration under a finite spring-material budget is a natural complete allocation problem, and unequal inertias make an index-centered design nonoptimal. The formula specifies every bond for arbitrary system size, exhibits which side of the physical mass center receives greater stiffness, and exposes a quantitatively growing static-versus-dynamic design cost. This is a complete classification rather than an arbitrary numerical slice. The proof and novelty risk are documented, and experimental robustness is not claimed.

**Closest work and limits.** Wan et al., IEEE CDC 2008, gives full graph edge designs of ordinary Laplacians and equal-difference Fiedler modes; Boyd, ICM 2006, gives general Laplacian optimization methods. Liu et al. (2015) addresses mass configurations of a ring, not adjustable positive chain springs. The result assumes free ends, discrete masses, nearest-neighbor linear Hookean springs, and a sum-of-stiffnesses resource, with no damping or frequency-dependent material response.
