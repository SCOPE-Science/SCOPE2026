# Matroidal apartment compatibility for paired orthogonal generators

## Finding
Let \(m\ge4\). In a complex split quadratic space \((V,\langle-,-\rangle)\) of dimension \(2m\), fix a maximal isotropic \(F=\operatorname{span}(f_1,\ldots,f_m)\), where the displayed vectors form a basis. Let \(E,E'\) be *maximal isotropic complements* of \(F\), meaning \(V=E\oplus F=E'\oplus F\). Put \(K=E\cap E'\) and for \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\) define \(\epsilon_I:K\to\mathbb C^I\) by \(\epsilon_I(k)=(\langle k,f_i\rangle)_{i\in I}\).

**Theorem.** An apartment of the type-\(D_m\) spherical building contains all the vertices represented by \(E,E'\), and \(\mathbb Cf_i\) for \(i\in I\), if and only if \(\epsilon_I\) is surjective. Thus the entire family of compatible subsets is the independence complex of the column matroid of the restriction of the bilinear pairing to \(K\times F\). If \(E'\) is the graph of an alternating matrix \(A\) over \(E\), this matroid has rank \(m-\operatorname{rank}A\). Hence exactly \(\operatorname{rank}A\) line constraints must be removed at minimum, an even integer. For distinct \(E,E'\), no apartment can accommodate both and any \(m-1\) of the \(m\) prescribed lines.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\); in particular its characteristic is not two. The quadratic form is nondegenerate, symmetric, split, and bilinear. Both maximal isotropics are required to be transverse to the *same* fixed \(F\); no assertion is made without this assumption. An apartment is the collection of coordinate totally isotropic subspaces for one hyperbolic frame \((u_1,v_1),\ldots,(u_m,v_m)\), satisfying \(\langle u_i,v_j\rangle=\delta_{ij}\) and all same-family pairings zero. Simultaneous containment means all stated subspaces are spanned by subsets of this frame. This is exactly simultaneous containment of their stabilizer parabolics in a maximal torus, by the standard building correspondence.

## Proof
**Necessity.** Suppose one hyperbolic frame makes \(E,E'\) and the lines \(\mathbb Cf_i\) for \(i\in I\) coordinate. Each \(f_i\) occupies a frame axis, and its opposite axis \(u_i\) is its unique hyperbolic partner. Since \(E\) and \(E'\) meet \(F\) trivially, neither contains \(f_i\). Since each is a coordinate maximal isotropic, each must therefore contain the opposite axis \(u_i\). Thus \(u_i\in K\), and the pairings \(\langle u_i,f_j\rangle\), for \(i,j\in I\), form a nonsingular diagonal matrix after rescaling. Therefore \(\epsilon_I\) is onto.

**Sufficiency.** If \(\epsilon_I\) is onto, select \(k_i\in K\) with \(\langle k_i,f_j\rangle=\delta_{ij}\), for \(i,j\in I\). Both \(\{k_i\}\) and \(\{f_i\}\) are totally isotropic collections, so the \(2|I|\)-dimensional space \(H=\operatorname{span}(k_i,f_i:i\in I)\) is a nondegenerate direct sum of hyperbolic planes. Split \(V=H\perp H^{\perp}\). Since \(k_i\) belongs to both \(E,E'\), the residual subspaces \(E\cap H^{\perp}\) and \(E'\cap H^{\perp}\) are maximal isotropics in \(H^{\perp}\). Any two maximal totally isotropic subspaces of a split quadratic space are coordinate in a common hyperbolic frame: let \(J\) be their intersection; choose a basis for \(J\), extend to complementary isotropic vectors by Witt decomposition, and in the nondegenerate quotient \(J^{\perp}/J\), pair the transverse residual isotropics into dual bases. Lifting gives a hyperbolic frame of \(H^{\perp}\) containing both subspaces as coordinate spans. Appending the selected pairs \((k_i,f_i)\) proves the desired apartment exists. The construction works also when \(I\) is empty or when \(E=E'\).

**Matroid and parity.** Select a basis \(b_1,\ldots,b_d\) of \(K\). The \(d\times m\) pairing matrix \(B_{ai}=\langle b_a,f_i\rangle\) represents a column matroid, and \(\epsilon_I\) has rank \(|I|\) exactly when the columns indexed by \(I\) are independent. The nondegenerate pairing between \(E\) and \(F\) shows \(B\) has full row rank \(d\); hence some \(d\) columns are independent, and no larger set is. Relative to \(V=E\oplus F\), an isotropic complement \(E'\) is the graph of a linear map \(E\to F\). The condition that its graph be isotropic means that its matrix \(A\) is skew-symmetric. The intersection with \(E\) is \(\ker A\), so \(d=m-\operatorname{rank}A\). Over characteristic zero the rank of an alternating matrix is even. If \(E\ne E'\), then \(\operatorname{rank}A\ge2\), and \(d\le m-2\): any set of \(m-1\) prescribed lines fails the apartment test. The existence of an independent \(d\)-column set proves the bound sharp for each such pair.

**Sharp profiles.** For each feasible even \(2r\le m\), choose an alternating form of rank \(2r\) with a generic kernel \(K\) of dimension \(m-2r\). Genericity of \(K\) means its Plücker coordinates for maximal column minors are all nonzero, a nonempty Zariski-open condition on the Grassmannian. Every \(|I|\le m-2r\) then passes the apartment criterion and none larger does: the compatibility matroid is uniform. More generally, degenerations yield other representable matroid profiles without changing the parity cutoff.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library script `verify.py` carries out rational row reductions on four explicit alternating matrices of sizes four and five. For each it constructs the kernel matrix, checks rank parity, enumerates all subsets of coordinate lines, and verifies that the largest compatible size equals the kernel dimension. It reproduces four distinct compatibility profiles, including the all-compatible case, the rank-zero profile, and nonuniform intermediate profiles. These examples verify computations but are **not** offered as a proof of the universal apartment equivalence, which is proved above.

## Relationship to prior work
Tsvelikhovskiy, arXiv:2610.11340 (first public October 8, 2026), proves the general rank-plus-two Helly bound, classifies many simple types, and leaves \(h(D_m)\) between \(m+1\) and \(m+2\). The source constructs sharp symplectic and odd-orthogonal families using \(m\) isotropic lines and two maximal isotropic subspaces (its Proposition 4.4), but does **not** classify all compatible line-subsets for two transverse maximal isotropic generators of type D. The present theorem gives an exact matroid of valid deletion patterns and explains the alternating-rank obstruction to transferring that construction to even orthogonal groups. It does not improve the unrestricted Helly bound. The standard polar-frame and Witt-decomposition ingredients are classical; see Pankov (2011) for general apartment descriptions. An earlier line-only Helly classification concerns only stabilizers of isotropic lines, without either maximal-isotropic generator, and therefore does not imply the mixed-parabolic matroid criterion.

## Limitations
No unrestricted determination of \(h(D_m)\) or \(h(E_6)\) is claimed. The result concerns two maximal isotropic complements to a common basis-bearing maximal isotropic \(F\), not arbitrary partial flags, more than two maximal generators, or positive-characteristic fields. The matroid profile is relative to a chosen ordered basis of \(F\). Priority within broader building-theoretic literature, including possible implicit versions in apartment-frame extension theory, remains a residual risk.
