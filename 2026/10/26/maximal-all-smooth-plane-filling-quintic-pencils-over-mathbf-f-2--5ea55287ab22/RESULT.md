# Maximal all-smooth plane-filling quintic pencils over \(\mathbf F_2\)

## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbf F_2\) and let \(I_5\) be the \(k\)-vector space of homogeneous degree-five forms in \(k[X,Y,Z]\) vanishing at every point of \(\mathbf P^2(k)\). Call \(U\subset I_5\) *all-smooth* if every nonzero form in \(U\) cuts out a geometrically smooth plane curve.

The maximum possible vector-space dimension of an all-smooth subspace \(U\) is **two**. There are **exactly 16,800** two-dimensional all-smooth subspaces, counted in fixed projective coordinates. They split into **108** orbits under \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(k)\): **96 orbits of size 168** and **12 of size 56**. Thus the stabilizers have orders one and three, respectively.

The scheme-theoretic base intersection of the two quintics defining a pencil has length 25. All seven \(k\)-points are reduced and transverse in that intersection, leaving a residual degree-18 closed subscheme with no \(k\)-points. The entire base is geometrically reduced for **16,128** pencils (102 projective orbits), and is nonreduced for the remaining **672** pencils (six projective orbits). The orbit counts refined by base reducedness are: reduced base, 93 orbits of size 168 and nine of size 56; nonreduced base, three orbits of size 168 and three of size 56.

## Assumptions and scope
All forms have exactly degree five and are defined over \(k=\mathbf F_2\). A two-dimensional \(k\)-linear system has precisely three nonzero \(k\)-members. Each such member is required to be geometrically smooth over \(\overline{k}\); no claim is made that all members after scalar extension are smooth. The group acts by projective linear substitutions in the fixed ambient plane, so the 108 classes are classes of embedded pencils, not classes of abstract genus-six curves or of their fibrations.

All three members of every counted pencil are geometrically integral genus-six plane quintics and contain all seven rational points of the plane. The pencil has a zero-dimensional base scheme; it does not define a morphism from the entire plane without blowing up the base.

## Proof
**(1) Finite first-jet parameterization.** Order the 21 monomials \(X^aY^bZ^c\) with \(a+b+c=5\) lexicographically in descending \(a,b\). Order the seven nonzero binary triples \((x,y,z)\) lexicographically. At each rational point choose the first coordinate equal to one as the projective affine chart, and retain the two remaining partial derivatives. Form the \(21\)-by-\(21\) binary matrix consisting of the seven values and 14 transverse first derivatives of the 21 monomials. Exact row reduction (implemented in `verify_census.py`) gives rank 21. Thus degree-five forms are uniquely specified by their seven values and 14 first-derivative coordinates. The vanishing subspace \(I_5\) consequently has dimension 14. A filling quintic is smooth at a rational point precisely when its two-component first derivative there is nonzero. There are therefore \(3^7=2,187\) candidates nonsingular at every rational point, with no repetition.

**(2) Geometric smoothness census.** For any degree-five form \(F\) in characteristic two, Euler's identity gives \(F=X F_X+Y F_Y+Z F_Z\). Hence its geometric singular locus is the common projective zero set of the three partial derivatives. In each of the charts \(X=1\), \(Y=1\), and \(Z=1\), the three derivatives generate the unit ideal over \(\mathbf F_2\) exactly when there is no singular point over an algebraic closure. This is tested by exact characteristic-two Gröbner bases, not by bounded field-extension point searches. The 2,187 locally smooth forms split into 18 orbits under the 168 matrices in \(\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbf F_2)\); exactly 10 orbits survive the geometric test, comprising 1,512 forms. Independently, `verify_independent.py` applies the Gröbner test to each of the 2,187 forms, obtaining precisely the same 1,512.

**(3) Pencil count and projective classification.** Let \(S\subset I_5\) denote the 1,512 geometrically smooth forms. A two-space \(\langle F,G\rangle\) is all-smooth if and only if \(F,G,F+G\in S\). The exact count of unordered distinct pairs \(\{F,G\}\subset S\) with \(F+G\in S\) is 50,400. Every two-space contributes exactly three such pairs, giving 16,800 pencils. A separate 14-variable Walsh–Hadamard cubic-moment computation yields 100,800 ordered pairs and the same 16,800 pencils. Exhaustive action of the 168 projective coordinate substitutions on these pencils gives 96 orbits of size 168 and 12 of size 56. Every representative and its orbit size is recorded in `census_output.json`.

**(4) Base-scheme geometry.** Distinct smooth plane quintics share no geometric component: a reducible plane curve of positive degree would be singular at an intersection of its components. Bézout therefore makes every base a length-25 zero-dimensional complete intersection. At a rational point \(P\), if the differentials of \(F,G\) were dependent, the nonzero form among \(F,G,F+G\) having zero differential would be singular at \(P\). Thus the seven rational base points are simple, and the remaining degree is 18. A finite complete intersection of two planar curves is reduced over \(\overline{k}\) if and only if the two affine differentials are independent at every base point. On each affine chart this is equivalent to the ideal generated by the two dehomogenized equations and their two-by-two Jacobian determinant being the unit ideal. Applying this exact test to the 108 projective representatives gives 93 size-168 and nine size-56 reduced-base orbits; the remaining three size-168 and three size-56 orbits have nonreduced base. Orbit invariance then gives the coordinate-fixed counts 16,128 and 672. The `reduced_base` field in `census_output.json` records all 108 outcomes.

**(5) Sharp upper bound.** For any linear system of filling quintics, the first-derivative map at a fixed rational point has a two-dimensional target. A three-dimensional subspace has a nonzero form in its kernel, singular at that point. Hence any all-smooth subspace has dimension at most two. Existence of the counted two-spaces proves sharpness.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_census.py` and `python3 verify_independent.py` with SymPy installed; the deterministic reference outputs are `census_output.json` and `independent_output.json`. The first program constructs the 21-by-21 jet matrix, enumerates all 18 form orbits, performs geometric smoothness and base-Jacobian Gröbner tests, and classifies all 108 pencil orbits. The second tests all 2,187 forms individually and checks the pencil count both by direct XOR pairs and an exact Walsh–Hadamard transform. On Python 3.13.5 and SymPy 1.14.0 both programs reproduce the stated counts. No output from a different mathematical instance is used.

The first two generators of a representative with reduced base have monomial coefficient masks `213034` and `593124`; a representative with nonreduced base has masks `213034` and `595238`. The monomial order is explicitly defined above and included in `census_output.json`.

## Relationship to prior work
Asgarli and Ghioca, *Smoothness in pencils of hypersurfaces over finite fields* (arXiv:2203.07169, first public 2022-03-14), study existence and large-field bounds for all-smooth pencils, including conic examples; the text does not provide the present binary degree-five filling-pencil census. Asgarli and Ghioca, *Plane-filling curves of small degree over finite fields* (arXiv:2307.03072, first public 2023-07-06), study individual plane-filling curves of degree one more than the minimum and a special parameterized pencil; they do not count all three-member all-smooth filling quintic pencils or their base schemes. Huang, Kedlaya and Lau, *A census of genus 6 curves over \(\mathbf F_2\)* (arXiv:2402.00716, first public 2024-02-01), enumerate smooth genus-six curves, including plane quintics, but not linear incidence among their equations. The present classification concerns three-member families and their scheme-theoretic intersections, which are not consequences stated in those sources.

## Limitations
This classification is restricted to \(\mathbf F_2\), degree five, and projective-linear changes of plane coordinates. Base reducedness is computed exactly, but the Frobenius decomposition of the residual degree-18 scheme is not classified. The 108 classes are not abstract genus-six curve isomorphism classes. Searches and examination of accessible primary texts cannot exclude every unindexed earlier enumeration; publication-priority claims remain subject to independent literature review. The numerical classification has been checked by exact algorithms, not by an independent external audit.
