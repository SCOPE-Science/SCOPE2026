# Minimal one-pass implications and exact Boolean trace rank for cycle maximal-independent-set contexts

## Finding
For each \(n\ge3\), consider the formal context with attributes \(V(C_n)\), objects all maximal independent vertex sets of the simple cycle, and membership incidence. Let \(c_n(S)\) be the intersection of maximal independent sets containing \(S\), with the empty-intersection convention \(c_n(S)=V(C_n)\). The globally smallest cardinality of any sound and complete multi-conclusion implication basis is
\[
B_n=\begin{cases}3&n=3,\\4&n=4,\\5&n=5,\\12&n=8,\\2n&n\ge6,\ n\ne8.\end{cases}
\]
Every one of these minimum bases is realizable in **one simultaneous forward-chaining round**. Independently, the largest set of attributes exhibiting every membership pattern among the objects (Boolean trace dimension) is exactly
\[
D_n=\left\lfloor\frac{n+1}{3}\right\rfloor.
\]
Thus these explicit cycle contexts have linear-size minimum direct logical descriptions and unbounded linear Boolean trace dimension.

## Assumptions and scope
Number the vertices cyclically by the integers modulo \(n\); all subscripts are modulo \(n\). A maximal independent set is an independent vertex subset dominating every vertex outside it. For an implication \(P\to Q\), all of \(Q\) is inferred if \(P\) is included in the initial set. A one-pass basis computes closure by union of all conclusions whose premises are contained in the **original** input, not by sequential iteration. Multi-attribute conclusions are permitted; a rule with empty or already-contained conclusion is omitted. The statement concerns complete bases without restrictions on rule-premise size.

## Proof
**Closure lemma.** If \(S\) contains adjacent vertices, no maximal independent set contains it and \(c_n(S)=V\). If \(S\) is independent, it has a maximal independent extension. For a vertex \(v\notin S\), if either neighbor \(u\in\{v-1,v+1\}\) can be appended to \(S\), then an extension of \(S\cup\{u\}\) to a maximal independent set omits \(v\). Conversely if neither neighbor can be appended, every maximal independent extension of \(S\) must contain \(v\), since it must dominate \(v\). Consequently, for \(n\ge5\),
\[
c_n(S)=S\cup\{v:\{v-2,v+2\}\subseteq S\},\qquad\text{when \(S\) is independent},
\]
where the displayed forcing pair is relevant only when its two members are nonadjacent. For \(n=3\), every maximal independent set is a singleton; for \(n=4\), every maximal independent set is an opposite pair.

**Optimal bases.** At \(n=3\), use the three adjacent-edge premises, each concluding all vertices; at \(n=4\), use the four singleton implications \(\{i\}\to\{i+2\}\); at \(n=5\), use the five adjacent-edge premises. At \(n\ge6\), use all \(n\) rules \(\{i,i+1\}\to V\), and one rule \(\{i-2,i+2\}\to\{i\}\) for each distinct forcing premise, combining conclusions if two forcing premises coincide. All forcing premises are different except that at \(n=8\) the premise for \(i\) equals that for \(i+4\), producing four such rules instead of eight. By the closure lemma, this basis computes closure in one round.

To prove global cardinality optimality, observe that any nonclosed premise \(P\) whose proper subsets are closed must occur as the *exact premise of a nontrivial rule* in every complete basis: otherwise forward chaining from \(P\) could not begin, because a rule with a proper-subset premise cannot add any new attribute without contradicting soundness and closure of that subset. For \(n\ge5\), all singleton subsets are closed. The inclusion-minimal nonclosed sets are precisely adjacent pairs and, when \(n\ge6\), independent pairs \(\{i-2,i+2\}\). (At \(n=5\) these would be adjacent and are already counted.) For \(n=4\) the four singleton sets are the minimal nonclosed premises; for \(n=3\) they are the three adjacent pairs. Thus the displayed bases attain a lower bound valid even against arbitrarily iterative complete bases.

**Boolean trace upper bound.** Suppose \(S\) of size \(k\ge2\) is shattered by the maximal independent sets. The all-ones trace forces \(S\) independent. Write the cyclic spacings between consecutive members of \(S\) as positive integers \(d_1,\ldots,d_k\ge2\), summing to \(n\). No two spacings equal to \(2\) can be joined in cyclic order by a sequence containing only spacings equal to \(3\). Indeed, label the selected endpoint preceding the first short spacing \(1\), the endpoint following the second short spacing \(1\), and all intermediate selected vertices \(0\). Starting across the first spacing of length \(2\), maximality at the first selected zero forces the neighbor immediately to its right into the independent dominating set: its left neighbor is excluded by the preceding selected one. Across each intermediate length-three spacing the forced occupied vertex excludes the next selected zero's left neighbor and therefore forces its right neighbor. At the final length-two spacing this neighbor would be adjacent to the final selected one, contradicting independence. For two or more short spacings, there must therefore be at least as many spacings of length at least \(4\) as short ones. If there are no short spacings then \(n\ge3k\); with exactly one, \(n\ge3k-1\); with at least two, the long spacings offset the deficits and \(n\ge3k\). Thus always \(n\ge3k-1\), or \(k\le\lfloor(n+1)/3\rfloor\). The case \(k=1\) also obeys the bound for \(n\ge3\).

**Boolean trace lower bound.** Put \(k=\lfloor(n+1)/3\rfloor\). When \(k=1\), every singleton is shattered: there is a maximal independent set containing that vertex and another maximal independent set omitting it. For \(k\ge2\), choose \(k\) vertices with all cyclic gaps at least \(3\), except possibly one gap of length \(2\). This is possible since \(n\ge3k-1\). To realize any trace \(T\subseteq S\), start with the selected vertices of \(T\) and supply a neighboring helper vertex for each \(s\in S\setminus T\), thereby excluding \(s\) from all maximal independent extensions. Ordinarily take the clockwise successor \(s+1\) as helper. This starting set is independent because the gaps are at least \(3\), except possibly if the single length-two gap has its left endpoint omitted and right endpoint retained. In that exceptional case instead use the counterclockwise predecessor \(s-1\) for **every** omitted selected vertex. With at most one short gap, this alternative is independent as well. Extend the resulting independent set greedily to a maximal independent set. Each omitted selected vertex already has a neighboring helper, so it remains omitted, whereas each retained selected vertex remains present. Every trace occurs, giving \(D_n\ge k\) and equality.

## Verification
The supplied standalone program directly enumerates maximal independent sets by checking nonadjacency and domination, then independently computes intersection closures from objects and one-round consequences from the displayed implication families. It exhaustively compares every premise subset and computes shattering dimensions for cycle orders \(3\) through \(15\). It confirms the exact basis-rule counts, closure, and Boolean trace dimensions over these finite ranges. The general proof above, not this finite enumeration, establishes the infinite family results.

## Relationship to prior work
Cho, Kim, Kim, and Oum study sizes of independent dominating sets in graphs with bounded degree, providing the independent-domination/maximal-independent-set equivalence but not the attribute implication systems and traces here. Rawson and coauthors study hypergraph intersection complexes as formal concept lattices, without the explicit cycle maximal-independent-set classification. Baixeries and Napoli investigate minimum versus direct implication bases in general closure systems, without these exact parameters. The present claim is a **family-specific** exact result; no novelty claim is made for these general theories. Published published-finding corpus overlap searches returned graph cover-free-family numbers for paths/cycles and graph-independent-domination results for other families; neither implies the stated cycle maximal-independent-set closure and Boolean traces.

## Limitations
The Boolean dimension is for traces of maximal independent sets **on vertices**, not the ordinary independence number, open-neighborhood VC dimension, or the edge-ground-set cycle hypergraph. The minimality result assumes general sound complete multi-conclusion implications, and distinguishes total number of rules from number of individual concluded literals. Novelty is best knowledge after listed searches and material inspections; equivalent formulations under maximal-set hypergraph or transversal nomenclature remain possible. Independent external validation has not been undertaken.
