# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)
The minimum-component disintegration has an explicit Jacobian factor and a uniform residual Dirichlet law independent of the minimum. The coupling is a convex interpolation with the uniform barycenter, which decreases each ranked partial sum, proving majorization without an asymptotic argument. The finite extreme covariance is derived from the conditional-maximum expectation and beta variance, and checked separately by exponential order-statistic increments. Rational exact-arithmetic checks cover 2 through 29 components and 71 conditional CDF comparisons, not a substitute for the general proof.

## Originality (PASS, qualified historical priority)
Inspection of Barthe et al. (2010) §§2.1–2.2, Monti et al. (2011) §§1–3, Lin (2016) Ch. 2 and Reznik et al. (2014) §§1–2 identified standard Dirichlet facts, compositional perturbations, and scanning results, but not the same conditional whole-vector majorization law. The claim is not originality of Dirichlet normalization or maximum-marginal formulas. Classical spacings work (Mauldon 1951, Weiss 1959, Pyke 1965) is relevant; parts were not accessible in full, so full historical precedence is not established. The accessible material supplies no decisive stronger implication. Query and source comparison summaries are recorded in AUDIT.json.

## Mathematical value (PASS)
Random spacings in interval partitions control both minimum segment safety and global heterogeneity. Majorization under fixed minimum provides a single pathwise ordered coupling for infinitely many dispersion statistics rather than an isolated expectation. It yields nonpositive covariance of the minimum with maximum, sum of squared gaps and negative entropy (reverse for Shannon entropy), plus exact finite-n gap covariance and a conditional sampler.

## Limitations
Only the uniform Dirichlet law is proved; nonuniform concentration changes the conditional geometry. The order covers Schur-convex/concave functions, not all statistics. The proof is mathematical, checks are finite, and full historical priority remains uncertain until classical sources can be inspected.
