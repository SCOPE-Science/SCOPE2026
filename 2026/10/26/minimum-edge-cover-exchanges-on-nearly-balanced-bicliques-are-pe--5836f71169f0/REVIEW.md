# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** A minimum cover of \(K_{n+1,n}\) is proved equivalent to a surjection from its larger class to the smaller. Replacing one duplicate position by a blank provides a bijection from cover states to edges of the permutation star graph. The exact equivalence of one-edge exchanges and incidence of root edges is proved in both directions. Standard graph arguments give counts, clique, coloring and independence numbers. A cycle-splitting proof reconstructs the star-transposition distance formula, and an explicit diameter pair of root edges establishes the precise line-graph diameter. The balanced obstruction is immediate once all optima are perfect matchings. Independent enumerations and graph checks agree for \(2\le n\le7\), with all adjacencies compared for \(2\le n\le5\). All-parameter proofs, rather than finite data, support the claims.

**Originality: PASS with residual risk.** The underlying permutation star network and its diameter are established, so these are explicitly treated as prior results. The contribution is the equivalence between minimum-edge-cover exchanges of \(K_{n+1,n}\) and the star-network line graph, and the resulting sharp balanced/unbalanced connectivity transition with exact exchange distances and full structural counts. Targeted searches for edge-cover reconfiguration, surjection Hamming adjacency and line-graph/permutation network equivalences did not identify coverage. Irving and Rattan's examined theorem and the inspected star-network orientation introduction address different claims. Relevant prior text not read in full and possibly unindexed surjection/network literature remain risks; a failed search is not a novelty certificate.

**Value: PASS.** Minimum edge-cover reconfiguration asks whether feasible optimal solutions are mutually reachable without increasing cover size. A one-vertex imbalance is the first nontrivial case beyond isolated perfect matchings and has a large natural, connected solution space. Its exact permutation-network identification gives a constructive geometric framework, an optimal worst-case exchange distance, and exact structural invariants; this is substantively stronger than simply evaluating the minimum cover cardinality or counting covers.

**Scientific and access limits.** The proof does not cover arbitrary bipartite graphs, greater imbalance, or changed move rules. The foundational IEEE article was located bibliographically but its complete text was not read. The reviewed statements are not independently validated.
