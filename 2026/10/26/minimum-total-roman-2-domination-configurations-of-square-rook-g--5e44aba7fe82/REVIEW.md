# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** For the stated family, the row-column dichotomy gives the exact optimum and removes weight-two labels at the optimum. The incidence graph then has no isolated vertices or one-edge components, and the edge/component inequality forces an acyclic spanning graph with exactly the maximal possible component count. The labeled bipartite-tree exponential formula and the complete size-excess cases prove each count, independently checked against small exhaustive enumerations and rational recurrence values through order ten.

**Originality: PASS, subject to priority risk.** An existing binary total Roman \(\{2\}\) function is a double dominating set, which is acknowledged rather than relabeled as novelty. The earlier rook \(k\)-tuple domination paper is a close potential predecessor; available bibliographic material indicates bounds, not the complete minimum-set census, while its inaccessible full text leaves genuine residual risk. Work on total open two-domination imposes a strictly stronger requirement at selected vertices; the ordinary rook domination polynomial imposes a weaker condition at unselected vertices. Neither directly yields the component-forest census or residue-dependent counts. Targeted literature and research-record comparisons found no statement implying that complete census; failed search alone does not establish priority.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** The exact number of minimum double-domination/total Roman \(\{2\}\) configurations on every labeled square rook graph is a natural extremal-enumeration question. Its complete forest classification describes every optimum, and its three congruence classes yield large, nontrivial exact counts beyond the scalar domination value. This structural census is not a numerical curiosity or a routine evaluation of an existing known table.

**Closest work and limitations.** Cabrera García et al. (arXiv:2101.02537) define the function class and its relation to double domination; Burchett--Lane--Lachniet (2009) directly study rook \(k\)-tuple domination, but its full text was not available. Carballosa--Wisby (arXiv:2001.07850) study the stronger total two-domination property; Mertens (arXiv:2401.00716) counts ordinary rook domination. Computational tests are finite and do not certify uniqueness or publication priority.
