# Scientific review: cubed broom zero forcing

## Correctness

PASS. The theorem has exact integer ranges \(t\ge2\), \(\ell\ge2\), and distinguishes the complete-graph boundary at \(\ell=2\). The Gram matrix has off-diagonal support exactly equal to the ordinary cube and rank \(\ell-1\), establishing a maximum-nullity lower bound \(t+2\). The explicit forcing schedules establish the opposite inequality. For \(\ell\ge3\), twin-neighbor obstruction and an exhaustive examination of every possible initial force classify all sets of size \(t+2\). The 32 finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the proof. Risk: the standard bound \(M\le Z\) is cited rather than re-proved.

## Originality

PASS on the inspected literature, subject to publication-priority risk. The 2026 graph-power study proves a path-power equality and an equal-arm-spider bound, neither a census of minimum forcing sets in unequal-arm broom cubes nor the present \(4t+3\) classification. Primary minimum-rank papers address path powers, strict powers, and ordinary squares of trees; none of those results enumerates the present minimum zero forcing sets. Semantic mathematical-record searches using broom, true-twin, cube, graph-power, multiplicity and zero-forcing aliases did not yield an equivalent. A general true-twin argument could provide a short alternate derivation of the rank equality, but not, without further work, this exact enumerative classification. Unindexed equivalent results remain possible.

## Mathematical value

PASS. A current graph-power zero-forcing program motivates sharpness questions beyond paths and cycles. This natural asymmetric spider class has arbitrarily long handles and unbounded branching, but its minimum-set multiplicity stabilizes exactly at \(4t+3\). The result combines a complete structural classification with sharp ordinary zero forcing and real maximum nullity; it is neither a finite table nor an arbitrary parameter slice. It does not claim to resolve equal-arm or general-spider conjectures.
