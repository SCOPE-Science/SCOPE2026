# Mixed-dimensional singular support of the cube-graph edge toric sixfold

## Finding

Let \(k\) be any field. Let \(Q_3\) be the graph on \(V=\{0,1\}^3\), with edges joining binary strings at Hamming distance one; denote its twelve edges by \(E\). Grade the edge ring \(R=k[t_ut_v: \{u,v\}\in E]\) by assigning degree one to each edge monomial, and put \(X=\operatorname{Proj}(R)\subset\mathbb P^{11}_k\), with homogeneous coordinate \(z_e\) for each \(e\in E\).

For a cube vertex \(v\), let \(S_v\subset E\) be the three incident edges, and let \(\mathbb P(S_v)\) denote the coordinate projective plane on \(\{z_e:e\in S_v\}\). The antipodal involution \(\iota\) sends \(u\in\{0,1\}^3\) to its bitwise complement; for an edge \(e=\{u,v\}\), write \(e^*=\{\iota(u),\iota(v)\}\). The twelve edges partition into six pairs \(\{e,e^*\}\). Write \(\mathbb P(e,e^*)\) for the coordinate projective line spanned by these two edge coordinates.

**Theorem.** The projective variety \(X\) is a normal sixfold. Its entire reduced singular locus is
\[
\operatorname{Sing}(X)_{\mathrm{red}}
=\bigcup_{v\in V}\mathbb P(S_v)
\ \cup\!\!\bigcup_{\{e,e^*\}}\mathbb P(e,e^*).
\]
These are exactly fourteen irreducible components: eight linear planes and six linear lines. In particular, the singular support is **not pure-dimensional**. Two distinct components intersect only at torus-fixed coordinate points. Every one of the twelve such points lies on exactly two of the planes and one of the lines; there are no other component intersections. The non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial locus equals the singular support, set-theoretically.

For \(Y=\operatorname{Sing}(X)_{\mathrm{red}}\), in the given embedding,
\[
H_{k[Y]}(T)=\frac{1+9T+9T^2-11T^3}{(1-T)^3},\qquad
P_Y(n)=4n^2+18n-10.
\]
Here \(H\) is the homogeneous-coordinate Hilbert series and \(P_Y\) the Hilbert polynomial; the Hilbert-function formula equals the polynomial for every integer \(n\ge1\).

## Assumptions and scope

The field \(k\) is arbitrary, including positive characteristic. The coordinate edge ring is the subalgebra generated by the twelve distinct squarefree degree-two monomials, *regraded* to degree one, not the edge ideal of the cube. All statements about the singular locus are about its reduced support, not the full Jacobian/Fitting scheme. The toric fan is the **normal fan** of the edge polytope, not the fan of cones over the faces of the ordinary geometric cube. The Hilbert series is for the reduced singular support in \(\mathbb P^{11}\), not for \(X\).

## Proof

**(1) The correct lattice and normality.** Number the binary vertices \(000,001,010,011,100,101,110,111\) by \(0,\ldots,7\). The bipartition is \(\{0,3,5,6\}\sqcup\{1,2,4,7\}\). In the edge-incidence vectors, the coordinate sums on each part are each one. Deleting coordinates \(6\) and \(7\) identifies the affine lattice with \(\mathbb Z^6\); the edge differences span it (the gcd of their maximal minors is one). Their convex hull \(P\) has dimension six. Since the vertex-edge incidence matrix of a bipartite graph becomes a directed incidence matrix upon changing the signs in one part, it is totally unimodular. Consequently every lattice point of \(mP\), for an integer \(m\ge1\), is an integral sum of \(m\) edge vectors: an existing nonnegative fractional flow with integral vertex-degree demands has an integral feasible flow. Thus \(P\) has the integer decomposition property, the homogeneous edge ring is normal, and \(\dim X=6\).

**(2) Complete exact facet and face census.** Put \(y_0,\ldots,y_5\) for the retained incidence coordinates. Exactly twelve primitive facet inequalities cut out \(P\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
&y_i\ge0 \quad(0\le i\le5),\\
&1-y_1-y_2-y_4\ge0,\quad 1-y_0-y_3-y_5\ge0,\\
&-y_0+y_1+y_2+y_4\ge0,\quad y_0-y_1+y_3+y_5\ge0,\\
&1-y_3-y_4\ge0,\quad 1-y_2-y_5\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]
An exhaustive exact-integer argument certifies completeness: any facet contains six affinely independent among the twelve edge points, so enumerate the \(\binom{12}{6}=924\) six-subsets, compute their integer cofactor hyperplanes, retain precisely those supporting all twelve points, and identify coincident equality sets. This gives the displayed twelve inequalities, without numerical polyhedral approximation. Repeated intersections of their equality vertex-sets enumerate **every** nonempty face. In increasing face dimension \(0,1,2,3,4,5,6\), the counts are
\[
(12,54,110,108,52,12,1),
\]
a total of \(349\) nonempty faces including \(P\).

**(3) Which torus orbits are singular.** The normal cone at a face \(F\) of dimension \(d\) has dimension \(6-d\), and its primitive rays are exactly the inward primitive normals to the facets containing \(F\). A torus orbit is smooth precisely when these rays extend to a basis of the intrinsic dual lattice. For every face, the enclosed exact checker determines the ray count and the gcd of its maximal minors. If there are more than \(6-d\) rays the cone is nonsimplicial; if there are exactly \(6-d\) rays the gcd is one, so the cone is smooth. The exhaustive singular face counts by dimensions \(0,1,2\) are \((12,30,8)\); no face of dimension three or higher is singular. The eight singular two-faces are exactly the triangles with edge-index sets \(S_v\), one for each cube vertex. The thirty singular one-faces split into the twenty-four sides of these triangles and the six additional edges of the edge polytope with vertex sets \(\{e,e^*\}\). The zero-faces are all contained in these. Therefore the maximal faces carrying singular normal cones are exactly those eight triangles and six segments.

The closure of the torus orbit of a face \(F\) is its projective face toric variety, supported on the coordinates of the vertices of \(F\). Each of the fourteen maximal singular faces is an affinely independent set of respectively three or two edge points; its face-coordinate semigroup has no binomial relations. Thus the orbit closures are literally the asserted coordinate \(\mathbb P^2\)'s and \(\mathbb P^1\)'s. The toric orbit criterion proves the full singular-locus equality and irreducibility. In this calculation every nonsmooth cone is nonsimplicial and every simplicial cone is regular, proving the non-\(\mathbb Q\)-factorial assertion.

**(4) Intersections and Hilbert data.** The twelve edge coordinates are in bijection with the cube edges. Each belongs to exactly two star triples and one antipodal pair. Two distinct star triples have a common edge coordinate precisely when their cube vertices are adjacent; opposite-edge pairs partition the twelve coordinates. Since the singular components are coordinate linear subspaces, their intersections are exactly the twelve coordinate points, each incident to three components as stated.

The radical homogeneous ideal of their union is the Stanley–Reisner ideal of the simplicial complex whose facets are the eight three-element stars and six two-element antipodal pairs. Its nonempty face numbers are exactly \(f_0=12\), \(f_1=30\), \(f_2=8\). Hence its homogeneous Hilbert series is \((1+9T+9T^2-11T^3)/(1-T)^3\). Alternatively, a degree-\(n\) monomial with nonempty squarefree support of size \(j\) occurs in \(\binom{n-1}{j-1}\) ways; for \(n\ge1\), counting supports gives \(12+30(n-1)+8\binom{n-1}{2}=4n^2+18n-10\). This proves both formulas.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` using only the Python standard library. The checker constructs all twelve edges from Hamming distance, proves the intrinsic lattice index is one, computes exact facets using integer cofactors, closes the face lattice under facet intersections, tests simpliciality and lattice indices of all cones, identifies all maximal singular faces, and recomputes the coordinate-union face numbers. Its successful output is:

`VERIFY_OK polytope_dimension=6 facets=12 faces=349 singular_faces=(12,30,8) maximal=(8_P2,6_P1) f_complex=(12,30,8)`

The enumeration has a fixed finite search space: 924 candidate six-subsets and 349 nonempty faces. It is exhaustive because every facet contains six affinely independent vertices, every face is an intersection of facets, and toric smoothness is determined by primitive normal rays and their lattice index. No floating-point operations or probabilistic tests enter the checker. The computation concerns this fixed six-dimensional polytope only.

## Relationship to prior work

Ohsugi and Hibi (1998) establish the general normality and facets framework for graph edge polytopes; their normality criterion already covers bipartite graphs. Ohsugi and Hibi (2008, arXiv:0804.4287) classify globally simple/smooth graph edge polytopes, which implies that this non-complete-bipartite cube edge polytope is not globally smooth, but does not identify its maximal singular strata. Kahle, Kubjas, Kummer and Rosen (2016, arXiv:1605.01678) discuss partial rank-one matrix projections and completion; the cube graph is also the four-by-four crown bipartite graph \(K_{4,4}\) with a perfect matching removed. Their completion analysis does not supply the component-wise reduced singular-support classification or the Hilbert series calculated here. Standard toric orbit criteria reduce the question to an exact lattice-face computation; the explicit mixed-dimensional answer is the assertion of the theorem.

## Limitations

The full nonreduced Jacobian singular scheme, completed transverse local equations, and singularity multiplicities are not computed. No assertions are made about edge varieties of higher-dimensional hypercubes or general crown graphs. The exact finite enumeration and toric orbit criterion establish this specific cube case; they do not constitute a theorem about arbitrary graph edge polytopes. Equivalent tables or descriptions under different graph/partial-matrix terminology may exist and would affect originality, not the stated proof.
