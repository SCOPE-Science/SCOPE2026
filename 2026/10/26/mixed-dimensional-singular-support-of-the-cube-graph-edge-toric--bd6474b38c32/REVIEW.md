# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The edge ring is normal by total unimodularity of a signed bipartite incidence matrix. The complete 924-subset integer facet enumeration produces twelve primitive facet inequalities, and the facet-intersection enumeration produces 349 nonempty faces. Full primitive-normal cone and lattice-index checks show exactly eight maximal singular two-faces (the vertex-star triangles) and six maximal singular one-faces (antipodal edge pairs); all simplicial cones are unimodular. The orbit closures are the stated coordinate linear spaces. Their Stanley–Reisner face complex gives the Hilbert polynomial and series. The standard-library exact checker supplies a self-contained reproducible finite certificate. No characteristic hypothesis is needed.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Ohsugi–Hibi (1998) cover normality and a general facet method, while Ohsugi–Hibi (2008) classify when the whole edge polytope is smooth; neither inspected text gives this cube's fourteen maximal singular orbit closures or the non-pure Hilbert data. Kahle–Kubjas–Kummer–Rosen (2016) concern geometry of rank-one completion for partial matrices rather than the reduced singular support of this particular projected Segre variety. The general toric smoothness criterion does not state the required exhaustive face classification. Searches for the cube/hypercube and equivalent four-by-four crown-graph or deleted-diagonal Segre formulation did not identify an equivalent published component census. Residual risks are a hidden table of normal-fan orbit singularities, an implicit special case of a broader partial-Segre theorem, or undocumented coverage under a different name. Absence from a search is not proof of priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The cube graph is the smallest regular bipartite three-dimensional hypercube graph and also the four-by-four crown graph; its edge toric sixfold is a natural sparse Segre projection. The theorem replaces a global nonsmoothness verdict by the complete reduced singular-support geometry. Its coexistence of maximal singular planes and separate maximal singular lines establishes non-purity, and the explicit coordinate-incidence complex yields a nontrivial Hilbert polynomial. These data distinguish the variety's boundary geometry and are not implied by normality, degree or Hilbert-function information about the ambient sixfold alone.
