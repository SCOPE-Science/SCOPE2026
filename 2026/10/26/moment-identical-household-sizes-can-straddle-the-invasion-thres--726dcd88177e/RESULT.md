# Moment-identical household sizes can straddle the invasion threshold under path transmission

## Finding

For the explicit household epidemic model defined below, the first two moments of household size do not determine the household reproduction number. Let the household-size law be either

\[
q^A_1=q^A_3=\frac12,
\]

or

\[
q^B_1=\frac13,\qquad q^B_2=\frac12,\qquad q^B_4=\frac16.
\]

Both laws have mean \(\mathbb E[M]=2\) and variance \(\operatorname{Var}(M)=1\). With independent within-household transmission probability \(p=1/2\) along each edge of a path-shaped household, their household reproduction numbers are

\[
\mathcal R_*^A=\frac{13\beta}{8},\qquad
\mathcal R_*^B=\frac{77\beta}{48}.
\]

Consequently, for every \(\beta\in(8/13,48/77)\), law \(q^A\) is supercritical while law \(q^B\) is subcritical. In particular, at \(\beta=31/50\), the exact values are \(403/400>1\) and \(2387/2400<1\). Thus moment-matched household-size summaries can give opposite invasion classifications when within-household contact topology is explicitly modeled.

## Assumptions and scope

Households are sampled independently from a finite-support law \(q_m=\Pr(M=m)\). A household of size \(m\) has contact graph equal to the path \(P_m\). One individual is initially infected, chosen uniformly among its \(m\) vertices. Each path edge is independently open with probability \(p=1/2\); infection reaches exactly the connected component of the seed in the open-edge subgraph. Each infected individual independently generates a Poisson number of successful external infections with mean \(\beta\in(8/13,48/77)\); each external infection targets a uniformly random individual in the population. The concrete rational witness uses \(\beta=31/50\). In the large-population early-outbreak branching approximation, collisions between external infections and previously infected households are negligible. The household reproduction number \(\mathcal R_*\) is the mean number of newly infected households generated by one infected household in this approximation.

This is an exact calculation for the stated model, not a claim about every within-household SIR mechanism or an empirical population.

## Proof

For a path of size \(m\), let \(Z_m\) be the number of vertices in the open-edge component containing the uniformly chosen seed, and write \(H_m=\mathbb E[Z_m]\). A vertex at distance \(d\) from the seed is reached exactly when the unique path of length \(d\) between them has all its edges open, an event of probability \(p^d\). There are \(2(m-d)\) ordered seed-target pairs at distance \(d\). Averaging over the \(m\) possible seed positions gives

\[
H_m=1+\frac{2}{m}\sum_{d=1}^{m-1}(m-d)p^d.
\]

At \(p=1/2\), direct finite sums yield

\[
H_1=1,\qquad H_2=\frac32,\qquad
H_3=\frac{11}{6},\qquad H_4=\frac{33}{16}.
\]

If a new external infection chooses a uniformly random individual, its household size has the size-biased law \(\Pr(M^*=m)=m q_m/\mathbb E[M]\); conditional on that size, the seed vertex is uniform. A household with \(Z_m\) infected individuals produces mean \(\beta Z_m\) external infections. Therefore

\[
\mathcal R_*=\frac{\beta}{\mathbb E[M]}\sum_m q_m m H_m.
\]

For law \(q^A\),

\[
\sum_m q^A_m mH_m
=\frac12(1)+\frac12\left(3\cdot\frac{11}{6}\right)
=\frac{13}{4},
\]

so \(\mathcal R_*^A=13\beta/8\). At \(\beta=31/50\), this is \(403/400>1\).

For law \(q^B\),

\[
\sum_m q^B_m mH_m
=\frac13(1)+\frac12\left(2\cdot\frac32\right)
+\frac16\left(4\cdot\frac{33}{16}\right)
=\frac{77}{24},
\]

so \(\mathcal R_*^B=77\beta/48\). At \(\beta=31/50\), this is \(2387/2400<1\).

The moments agree exactly: for both laws, \(\mathbb E[M]=2\) and \(\mathbb E[M^2]=5\), hence \(\operatorname{Var}(M)=5-2^2=1\). For any \(\beta\in(8/13,48/77)\), the inequalities \(13\beta/8>1\) and \(77\beta/48<1\) follow directly from the interval endpoints. The selected value lies in that interval because \(31/50>8/13\) is equivalent to \(403>400\), and \(31/50<48/77\) is equivalent to \(2387<2400\). This proves the claim.

## Verification

All household-size probabilities are nonnegative and sum to one. The mean and second moment were evaluated directly from the stated finite distributions. The path-component expectation follows by summing reachability indicators over all seed-target pairs; no simulation or asymptotic approximation is used for \(H_m\). The only branching approximation is the stated large-population interpretation of external infections, where repeated hits to an already infected household vanish in the limit. The displayed reproduction numbers are exact rational values under that model.

## Relationship to prior work

Household epidemic models commonly summarize within-household transmission through an attack-rate law or through an expected household epidemic size. Dönges et al. derive a size-only model in which a household of size \(m\) has expected infected count \(1+a(m-1)\), leading to a reproduction-number expression determined by the first two household-size moments. That expression relies on the stipulated size-independent attack rate. Barlow, Adams, and Penington instead use frequency-dependent, complete-mixing contacts within each household and calculate the household reproduction number from a within-household Markov process. Classical household branching-process analyses likewise make explicit the role of within-household epidemic size. The present calculation does not contradict those results: it isolates a different, path-structured contact law and gives an exact moment-matched pair for which the invasion verdict changes.

## Limitations

The result concerns path-shaped households, independent open-edge transmission with \(p=1/2\), and a Poisson external-infection mechanism with \(\beta\in(8/13,48/77)\); \(\beta=31/50\) is the concrete rational witness. It does not assert that the first two moments are insufficient under every household model; in particular, the constant-attack-rate model above has a different dependence. It does not estimate real-world parameters, calculate final epidemic size, or prove a finite-population threshold theorem. The numerical separation is exact but model-specific. A broader literature search may uncover related moment-matched examples; the current comparison establishes a distinct explicit witness, not a universal novelty theorem.
