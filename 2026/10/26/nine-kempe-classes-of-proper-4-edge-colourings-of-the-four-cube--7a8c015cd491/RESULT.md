# Nine Kempe classes of proper \(4\)-edge-colourings of the four-cube

## Finding

Write \(Q_4\) for the graph on \(\{0,1\}^4\) in which two words are adjacent when they differ in one coordinate. A proper four-edge-colouring uses each of four colours exactly once at every vertex. We identify two colourings differing only by a permutation of the four colour names. A Kempe exchange interchanges two colours on **one entire connected component** of their two-colour subgraph. Every such component is an alternating even cycle.

**Theorem.** The Kempe reconfiguration graph \(\mathcal K(Q_4,4)\) has exactly \(1840\) vertices, \(8808\) edges, and nine connected components: one on \(1712\) vertices and eight on \(16\) vertices. The eight smaller components are each isomorphic to the Möbius–Kantor graph \(GP(8,3)\), and the automorphism group of \(Q_4\) acts transitively on this family of eight components. The large component has radius \(6\), diameter \(8\), and eccentricity distribution \(133,1342,237\) at eccentricities \(6,7,8\), respectively.

Let \(\varepsilon(\mathcal F)\in\{+1,-1\}\) be the product of the permutation signs of the four perfect matchings of an edge-colouring, regarded as bijections from the eight even-weight vertices to the eight odd-weight vertices. This sign is independent of the chosen orderings of the two bipartition classes and of colour names. It is invariant under all Kempe exchanges. Precisely \(1712\) colourings have positive sign and form the large component, while the \(128\) negative-sign colourings make up the eight small components. Every negative-sign colouring has exactly nine square faces carrying all four colours. Exactly two colour-name-equivalence classes have all \(24\) square faces rainbow, and both lie in the large component.

## Assumptions and scope

The vertex labels used by the verifier are the integers \(0,\ldots,15\), interpreted as binary four-vectors. An edge is an unordered pair \(\{u,u\oplus2^i\}\), where \(i\in\{0,1,2,3\}\). Perfect matchings are eight-edge spanning subgraphs, and a factorization is an **unordered** four-element set of pairwise disjoint perfect matchings whose union is the full edge set. Every proper four-edge-colouring corresponds bijectively to such a factorization. A nontrivial Kempe edge is present only when two distinct factorizations result from a single alternating-cycle exchange. There is no quotient by automorphisms of the underlying four-cube.

## Proof

**Exhaustive factorization lemma.** For each subset \(U\) of vertices already matched, take the least vertex \(u\) outside \(U\). Recur over all currently unmatched neighbours \(v\) of \(u\), adding \(uv\). Every perfect matching has exactly one edge at \(u\), and so the recursion produces every matching exactly once; it produces \(272\) matchings. Order the matchings by their recursion indices. For every index quadruple \(i<j<k<\ell\), include it precisely when its four matchings are disjoint and cover all \(32\) edges. Instead of scanning all quadruples, the algorithm selects the first three disjoint matchings and tests their forced complement as the fourth. This is exhaustive and duplicate-free, and yields \(1840\) factorizations. The independently implemented edge-colouring backtracking search fixes the four colours at vertex zero, assigns every remaining edge one allowed colour, and yields exactly the same \(1840\) canonical factorizations; fixing the colour permutation at a degree-four vertex is unique.

**Exact Kempe graph lemma.** For every unordered pair of distinct factors, their union is a spanning two-regular graph. Its connected components are exactly the alternating even cycles on which a Kempe exchange is allowed. For each such component \(C\), replacing the two matching edge masks \(M,N\) by \(M\mathbin{\triangle}E(C),N\mathbin{\triangle}E(C)\) is precisely the required interchange. Exhausting all six factor pairs and every component therefore constructs the entire reconfiguration graph, with no omitted adjacency. The accompanying verifier performs this construction twice: from matching masks and independently from literal edge-colour assignments with fresh connected-component searches. The neighbour sets agree for all \(1840\) colourings. Exact breadth-first traversal gives component orders \(1712\) and eight copies of \(16\), with \(8808\) undirected adjacency edges.

**Structural certificates.** The file `mk_isomorphism_certificates.json` contains eight explicit bijections from the vertices \(0,\ldots,15\) of \(GP(8,3)\) to the colouring indices in each small component. The verifier checks every one of the \(\binom{16}{2}\) adjacency/nonadjacency statements for each map against the graph whose edges are \(u_i u_{i+1}\), \(u_i v_i\), and \(v_i v_{i+3}\), all subscripts modulo eight. All eight maps satisfy the equivalence, proving the component isomorphisms. Acting on one small-component representative by every coordinate permutation and every binary translation (\(24\cdot16=384\) cube automorphisms) reaches each of the eight components exactly \(48\) times, proving transitivity. Exhaustive shortest-path searches from every colouring in the large component establish its stated metric parameters. The full unordered-pair distance enumerator in that component has coefficients \((8616,54708,220188,496780,490860,181602,11258,604)\) at distances one through eight; their sum is \(\binom{1712}{2}\).

**Sign invariant.** Each perfect matching \(M\) gives a bijection from the even-weight to the odd-weight vertices. On an alternating cycle of length \(2r\), exchanging two matching colours composes **each of their** bijections with a permutation of parity \((-1)^{r-1}\) on the involved vertices. Thus the product of their two signs, and hence the product of all four signs, is unchanged. Independent reorderings of the two bipartition classes multiply the four matching signs by the same common sign four times, so the product is well-defined. This gives an abstract proof of Kempe invariance; exhaustive enumeration determines its fibres to have sizes \(1712\) and \(128\) and shows the exact component split.

**Square-face census.** Every square is indexed uniquely by two coordinate directions and a base vertex with both corresponding bits zero. There are \(\binom42 2^2=24\) faces. For every factorization, examining the four coloured edges of each face yields exactly nine rainbow faces on each negative-sign colouring. The positive-sign colourings have rainbow-face counts \(0,4,6,8,10,12,16,24\) with respective frequencies \(371,768,304,132,96,24,15,2\). This is a **finite exhaustive claim**, checked within the same complete domain; no all-dimensional parity law is asserted.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` in a directory containing `mk_isomorphism_certificates.json`. The code needs only Python's standard library. It regenerates the \(32\) edges, all \(272\) perfect matchings, all \(1840\) factorizations, and every Kempe adjacency; an independent backtracking method enumerates the same colourings and independently generates their Kempe neighbours. It checks all component counts, the sign invariant, all \(24\) square faces, all eight Möbius–Kantor adjacency certificates, the full \(384\)-element cube symmetry action on the small components, and all shortest-path distances from every graph vertex. Finite exhaustive checking here is the proof of the finite census; it is not a sample or a claim concerning any other dimension.

## Relationship to prior work

McDonald, Mohar and Scheide (2010) introduced the Kempe-equivalence viewpoint for edge-colourings and settled connectivity with extra colours for subcubic and subquartic graphs. Asratian and Casselgren (2014) proved that all **five**-edge-colourings of four-regular Class 1 graphs are Kempe equivalent; that statement does not cover the minimum four-colour Kempe graph studied here. Faudree, Gyárfás, Lesniak and Schelp (1993) constructed rainbow edge-colourings of hypercubes, including \(Q_4\), without determining the number or Kempe geometry of all four-colourings. Behague (2018) defined a sign for hypercube one-factorizations and proved its invariance under square-face switches. The sign and the existence of special factorizations are prior observations; the exhaustive nine-class census, the eight Möbius–Kantor components, and the exact full-Kempe distance data are distinct claims, not consequences of those results as inspected.

## Limitations

All enumeration, isomorphism and distance conclusions concern the finite graph \(Q_4\) only. The proof does not provide corresponding counts for higher-dimensional cubes. Literature comparison is limited to accessible targeted primary sources and research-record searches; an unindexed computation may already contain some of these numbers. The finite verification is reproducible but is not an independent mathematical audit or a claim of global novelty certification.
