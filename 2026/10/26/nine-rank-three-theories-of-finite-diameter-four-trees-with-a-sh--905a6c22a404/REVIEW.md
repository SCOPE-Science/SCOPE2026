# Review: rank-three diameter-four tree classification

**Correctness (PASS).** Unique-center decomposition, truncation of pendant-leaf counts and branch multiplicities at three, and a three-round partial-isomorphism strategy cover all finite trees of diameter four. The reduction leaves exactly 240 canonical vectors. A full Hintikka-type enumeration, a separately implemented literal-diagram recursion, and 81 direct three-round games establish nine classes and minimum representative sizes of 5, 6, 6, 7, 7, 7, 8, 9, 10. The reduction never increases order, making ten sharp for arbitrary members. A residual reproducibility risk is code or environment error; literal second recursion and game comparison reduce, but do not logically eliminate, implementation risk.

**Originality (PASS, provisional).** The closest full-text study by Bohman et al. (2007) computes asymptotic defining depth of individual general trees rather than the rank-three quotient of all diameter-four trees. Heuter (1991) studies definability for ordered ranked labelled trees with named successor relations; that is a distinct signature and does not establish this exact quotient. Libkin gives the general game/type framework, not the nine-class computation. Related prior exact rank-three spectra of star forests and linear forests concern different graph classes: branching diameter-four trees are not members of those classes, and the nine-class result does not follow as a special case. Actual targeted semantic-record and literature searches found no stronger statement implying the exact count and ten-vertex sharp bound. The significant residual risk is older or unindexed exact low-rank tree classifications.

**Value (PASS).** This is a complete natural finite-model-theoretic classification for the full unbounded-order class of diameter-four trees, rather than an arbitrary bounded-size census. It sharpens qualitative bounded-depth finite-kernel existence to a nine-element testing set with an optimal 10-vertex bound for all rank-three sentences on that class. The precise logical threshold and representative size are intrinsically useful for satisfiability, validity, and finite-model test-suite reduction; no claim of broader algorithmic or paper-sized consequences is needed.
