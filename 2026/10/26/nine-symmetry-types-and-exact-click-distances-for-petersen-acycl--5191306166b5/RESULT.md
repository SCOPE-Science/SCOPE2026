# Nine symmetry types and exact click distances for Petersen acyclic orientations

## Finding

Let \(P=\operatorname{KG}(5,2)\) be the Petersen graph: its ten vertices are the two-element subsets of \(\{0,1,2,3,4\}\), and disjoint subsets are adjacent. Form \(\mathcal K(P)\), the graph whose vertices are *acyclic orientations* of all fifteen edges of \(P\). Two orientations are adjacent when one is obtained from the other by reversing all three edges incident to a source, thereby making it a sink. Adjacency is undirected: reversing a sink is the inverse move.

The 16,680 acyclic orientations form **704 connected components**, with the following complete component-size and diameter census. A component of size \(s\) appears \(c\) times in the row shown; \(d\) is its graph diameter. The last column is the number of component orbits under \(\operatorname{Aut}(P)\), followed by their orbit sizes.

| \(s\) | \(c\) | \(d\) | Component orbits under \(\operatorname{Aut}(P)\) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 12 | 120 | 5 | one orbit of size 120 |
| 16 | 120 | 5 | one orbit of size 120 |
| 21 | 120 | 5 | two orbits of size 60 each |
| 22 | 120 | 5 | one orbit of size 120 |
| 28 | 120 | 6 | one orbit of size 120 |
| 38 | 60 | 6 | one orbit of size 60 |
| 50 | 24 | 6 | one orbit of size 24 |
| 66 | 20 | 6 | one orbit of size 20 |

Consequently the 704 components form **nine symmetry orbits**, and every component has diameter either 5 or 6; both occur. Precisely 480 components have diameter 5 and 224 have diameter 6. The two inequivalent sixty-component orbits of twenty-one-state components are interchanged by reversing every edge: they have identical intrinsic distance and degree distributions but cannot be exchanged by any automorphism of the underlying Petersen graph.

For completeness, the number of *unordered pairs of distinct* click-equivalent acyclic orientations at each distance from 1 to 6 is, respectively,
\[
(28140,\ 50400,\ 63040,\ 60960,\ 30500,\ 2040).
\]
These add to 235,080 unordered pairs within components. Of the 16,680 orientations, 13,880 have component eccentricity 5 and 2,800 have component eccentricity 6.

The bare totals 16,680 acyclic orientations and 704 click-equivalence classes follow from previously known chromatic and Tutte polynomials; they are included as normalization checks, **not** claimed as new. The contribution is the component-size/diameter stratification, its nine-orbit symmetry refinement, the reversal-paired exception, and the full finite distance census.

## Assumptions and scope

All orientations are of the *labeled* ten-vertex Petersen graph, with each of its fifteen undirected edges oriented exactly once. No directed cycles are allowed. A legal elementary move at vertex \(v\) requires either indegree zero or outdegree zero, and reverses all incident edges. The undirected move graph agrees with the equivalence relation generated by source-to-sink conversions, since a sink-to-source flip is a reversed source-to-sink conversion. Distances are shortest numbers of these **single-vertex** moves, not shortest sequences of arbitrary directed-cut reversals or transpositions of orientations.

The symmetry group acts by permuting the five underlying symbols, hence on the ten two-subsets, the fifteen graph edges, their orientations, and the click components. *Reversal* means reversing all fifteen oriented edges simultaneously; this is not a Petersen graph automorphism and is studied as an additional involution. The distinguished pairs of twenty-one-state component orbits are inequivalent under the graph automorphism group, although reversal is an isomorphism between their move graphs.

The statement is a complete finite computation for \(P\) only; there is no extrapolation to the graphs \(\operatorname{KG}(n,2)\) for larger \(n\).

## Proof

**1. Exhaustive orientation representation.** Order the two-subsets of \(\{0,1,2,3,4\}\) lexicographically as graph vertices \(0,\ldots,9\). Order its fifteen undirected edges \((i,j)\), \(i<j\), lexicographically. An integer mask from 0 through 32,767 orients edge \(k\) from \(i\) to \(j\) when bit \(k\) is zero, and from \(j\) to \(i\) when bit \(k\) is one. This is a bijection from the masks to *all* \(2^{15}\) orientations, with no isomorphism quotient or sampling. A topological-peeling test computes whether the orientation is acyclic: repeatedly remove a zero-indegree vertex and its outgoing arcs, accepting precisely when all ten vertices are removed. This test is equivalent to the absence of directed cycles because every finite nonempty directed acyclic graph has a source, while a remaining directed subgraph with no source contains a directed cycle. The executable enumerates exactly 16,680 accepted masks, independently confirmed by a depth-first directed-cycle test and by the published chromatic polynomial evaluated at \(-1\).

**2. Exact click edges and connected components.** For every accepted mask, inspect the three incident edges at every vertex. If all three point out or all three point in, toggle exactly those three bits. Such a move preserves acyclicity: replacing a source by a sink cannot create a directed cycle involving it, and directed cycles avoiding it are unchanged; the sink case is symmetric. Conversely, the definition of the move admits *no other edges* between states. The verifier checks every generated endpoint for acyclicity and checks adjacency symmetry. Breadth-first traversal of the complete resulting finite graph partitions the 16,680 states into 704 components and produces the component-size multiplicities in the table. The check \(\sum_s sc_s=16680\) certifies full partition coverage. Independently, the 704 count agrees with both the count of acyclic orientations having a specified unique source (the known click-class transversal theorem) and the already published evaluation \(T_P(1,0)=704\). This agreement is a check, not the originality claim.

**3. Exact shortest-path distances.** For each of the 16,680 states \(a\), breadth-first search within its complete click component assigns distance zero to \(a\) and one plus the minimum predecessor distance to every subsequently discovered state. Since every legal move is a unit-cost edge and all neighbors are generated, breadth-first search gives the exact distance between \(a\) and every vertex in its component. The program asserts that each such search visits *the full component*. Maximizing its distances gives \(a\)'s eccentricity, then maximizing eccentricities gives each component's exact diameter. All-pairs distances give the stated global pair histogram after dividing nonzero ordered-pair counts by two, and the program verifies the independent identity \(\sum_s c_s\binom{s}{2}=235080\). The same checks produce diameters 5 and 6, with the component counts given above. These are exhaustive shortest paths, not lower-bound estimates or incomplete searches.

**4. Full symmetry-group orbits.** The five star families of two-subsets containing a fixed symbol are precisely the independent sets of size four in \(P\): a pairwise-intersecting family of at least four two-subsets necessarily shares a common symbol (without one, such a family has at most the three pairs of a triangle). An automorphism therefore permutes these five families, and each graph vertex is their pairwise intersection, so the automorphism acts faithfully as a permutation of the five symbols. Every such symbol permutation is an automorphism, proving \(\operatorname{Aut}(P)\cong S_5\), of order 120. The verifier explicitly constructs all 120 induced vertex permutations, maps the directed endpoints of each oriented edge, and therefore acts on masks and connected-component IDs. Taking each remaining component representative and all its 120 images partitions the 704 components into exactly nine orbits of lengths \(120,120,60,60,120,120,60,24,20\), organized by sizes as in the table. The two orbits with size-21 components are distinct because their sets of component IDs are disjoint under the **complete** automorphism group; reversing all bits exchanges these two orbits and preserves each of the seven other orbit types. This establishes the stated reversal pairing without assuming that matching numerical invariants imply symmetry equivalence.

To reproduce the census, run `python3 verify_click_petersen.py` in this directory using Python 3. The program contains the entire graph specification and asserts all claimed counts. Its output ends in `VERIFY_OK` when every exhaustion, distance and group-action assertion succeeds.

## Verification

The packaged standard-library verifier was executed on its source path and returned `VERIFY_OK`. Its independently implemented depth-first acyclicity test agrees with topological peeling on all 32,768 masks. For **every reachable ordered pair** it also compares the BFS distance to an independently computed integer-potential median distance from the prior general flip theorem. Further checks compare the orientation total with the established Petersen chromatic polynomial, the class count with the unique-source transversal, all component sizes against full-state coverage, all unordered-pair distances against component binomial counts, the previously known 168 automorphism orbits on individual orientations, and the nine component orbits against the explicit action of all 120 vertex automorphisms. The proof is an exhaustive finite calculation using a completely specified transition graph; this establishes neither an infinite-family result nor a complexity bound for larger graphs.

## Relationship to prior work

Macauley and Mortveit's 2007 paper established source-to-sink click equivalence and the identity \(\kappa(G)=T_G(1,0)\) for any graph. Their 2011 paper gave a complete general invariant of click-equivalence classes and a bijection with acyclic orientations with a fixed unique source. Therefore the Petersen total of 704 classes is already implied by the published Tutte polynomial and is **not** claimed new. The cited papers do not give Petersen class-size distributions, component diameters, the nine automorphism orbits or the reversal-paired twenty-one-state classes.

Cameron's 2015 discussion already computes 16,680 acyclic orientations of the Petersen graph and 168 ordinary automorphism orbits on the *individual orientations*. Those 168 orbits are **not** the nine orbits of click-equivalence components: the latter are orbits on the set of connected components of a separate move graph.

Aichholzer and collaborators' general vertex-flip theory, and a later published graph-flex cycle-signature/potential geodesic theorem, concern shortest flips for general orientation systems. For acyclic orientations the latter gives pairwise reachability and a median-potential distance formula; it is potentially stronger at the abstract metric level. Neither supplies the Petersen-specific exact enumeration, component-size strata, automorphism orbit split or reversal action. The present result specializes to one canonical graph and reports those precise values without claiming to improve the general theory.

## Limitations

All new numerical claims depend on exhaustive computation, whose completeness is supplied by the explicit finite mask enumeration and verifier. They have not been independently audited. The class count and the orientation total themselves are prior knowledge. The literature search cannot rule out a previously unindexed census, thesis appendix or external dataset containing the same nine-orbit or distance profile. The symmetry orbit table distinguishes components under graph automorphisms, not necessarily all intrinsic graph-isomorphism types. No results about full higher-dimensional flex complexes or generalized Kneser graphs are asserted.
