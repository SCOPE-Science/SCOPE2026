# No extra affine automorphisms at CM parameters for the four-punctured elliptic \(C_d\) family

## Finding
For every algebraically closed field \(k\) of any characteristic and \(d\in k\) with \(d(1-16d)\ne0\), the smooth affine elliptic curve
\[
C_d:\quad (u^2+u)(v^2+v)=d
\]
has **exactly eight** regular automorphisms. They are the transformations generated by
\[
\iota_u(u,v)=(-1-u,v),\qquad \iota_v(u,v)=(u,-1-v),\qquad \sigma(u,v)=(v,u),
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_k(C_d)=\langle\iota_u,\iota_v,\sigma\rangle\cong D_8.
\]
Here \(D_8\) denotes the dihedral group of order eight. This equality remains true when the smooth projective elliptic completion has extra origin-preserving automorphisms, including the complex-multiplication cases. Moreover
\[
k[C_d]^{\operatorname{Aut}_k(C_d)}=k[J],\qquad J=(u^2+u)+(v^2+v).
\]
Thus the quotient of this **affine open curve** by its complete regular automorphism group is the affine line. The displayed eight-element subgroup and its rational invariant field are known from Wu; the new classification is the equality with the **full** regular automorphism group.

## Assumptions and scope
All automorphisms are regular \(k\)-automorphisms of the affine curve, not all abstract automorphisms of its smooth projective completion. Let \(E_d\subset\mathbb P^1_k\times\mathbb P^1_k\) be the smooth projective closure. Smoothness is equivalent to \(d(1-16d)\ne0\); in characteristic two this reduces to \(d\ne0\), and in characteristic three to \(d(d-1)\ne0\). The complement has four distinct points
\[
H=E_d\setminus C_d=\{O,T,R,-R\},\quad O=(0,\infty),\quad T=(-1,\infty),\quad R=(\infty,0),\quad -R=(\infty,-1).
\]
Take \(O\) as the elliptic origin. The four geometric points are understood as a reduced divisor; the theorem is about the group of geometric automorphisms rather than the possibly subtler automorphism group scheme in positive characteristic.

## Proof
**1. The boundary is a cyclic subgroup of order four.** The coordinate projections have degree two. In a reciprocal local parameter \(w=1/v\) near \(O\), the equation becomes
\[
(u^2+u)(1+w)=dw^2,
\]
so \(u\) vanishes to order two at \(O\). The analogous charts at \(T,R,-R\) give the principal divisors
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{div}(u)&=2O-R-(-R),\\
\operatorname{div}(u+1)&=2T-R-(-R),\\
\operatorname{div}(v)&=2R-O-T,\\
\operatorname{div}(v+1)&=2(-R)-O-T.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(-R\) in a divisor denotes the point named \(-R\), not a negative coefficient. Interpreting degree-zero linear equivalence using the Abel--Jacobi group law on \(E_d\) with origin \(O\), these relations imply \(R+(-R)=O\), \(2T=O\) and \(2R=T\). Thus \(R\) has exact order four, \(T=2R\), and \(H=\langle R\rangle\cong\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\) in every characteristic.

**2. A regular affine automorphism preserves the four-point subgroup.** Each regular automorphism \(f:C_d\to C_d\) extends uniquely to an isomorphism of the smooth projective completion \(E_d\): a rational map between smooth projective curves extends at every missing point by the valuative criterion, and the inverse extends similarly. The extension permutes the omitted points. Consequently \(f(O)=h\in H\) and
\[
f=t_h\circ\alpha,\quad h\in H,\quad\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(E_d,O),\quad\alpha(H)=H.
\]
As \(H\) is cyclic of order four, \(\alpha(R)\in\{R,-R\}\). Composing with inversion if necessary reduces the calculation to origin-preserving automorphisms fixing \(R\).

**3. The only origin-preserving automorphism fixing \(R\) is the identity.** We spell out the small-characteristic cases. For an elliptic curve over an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from two and three, the classical classification gives an origin stabilizer of order two, four, or six, cyclic, with its unique involution equal to inversion \([-1]\). Every even-order element has a power equal to \([-1]\), which cannot fix a point of exact order four. A remaining possible nonidentity element of odd order has order three. Such an automorphism \(\alpha\) satisfies \(\alpha^2+\alpha+1=0\) in the integral endomorphism ring, and
\[
(1-\alpha)(1-\alpha^2)=[3].
\]
The two factors have the same degree because \(1-\alpha^2=-\alpha^2(1-\alpha)\), so each has degree three. An isogeny of degree three cannot have four distinct geometric points in its kernel, and so cannot fix the subgroup \(\langle R\rangle\).

In characteristic three, the origin stabilizer is either of order two or, at the supersingular elliptic curve, the standard order-twelve dicyclic group, again with unique involution \([-1]\). Every nonidentity element either has a power \([-1]\) or has order three; the preceding degree-three argument applies also in characteristic three (degree counts scheme length, so inseparability only strengthens the bound on distinct geometric kernel points).

In characteristic two, \(E_d\) has a geometric point of order four, hence is ordinary: a supersingular elliptic curve in characteristic two has no nontrivial geometric two-power torsion. The origin-preserving automorphism group of an ordinary elliptic curve in characteristic two is precisely \(\{1,[-1]\}\). Its nontrivial element does not fix \(R\). Therefore, in all characteristics,
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(E_d,O;H)=\{1,[-1]\}.
\]
The affine automorphism group has exactly \(|H|\cdot2=8\) elements.

**4. The eight visible transformations exhaust the group.** Each of \(\iota_u,\iota_v,\sigma\) preserves the equation and the affine open subset, and their restrictions to the ordered boundary \((O,T,R,-R)\) are respectively the permutations \((O\ T)\), \((R\ {-R})\) and \((O\ R)(T\ {-R})\). These generate eight distinct permutations, with relations \(\iota_u^2=\iota_v^2=\sigma^2=1\), \(\iota_u\iota_v=\iota_v\iota_u\), and \(\sigma\iota_u\sigma=\iota_v\). This is \(D_8\), and the preceding upper bound proves completeness.

**5. The full affine quotient.** Put \(A=u^2+u\) and \(B=v^2+v\), so \(AB=d\) and \(A\) is invertible in \(k[C_d]\). The coordinate ring is finite free with basis \(1,u,v,uv\) over \(k[A,A^{-1}]\), using the monic relations \(u^2+u=A\) and \(v^2+v=d/A\). An element fixed by both \(\iota_u\) and \(\iota_v\) has zero coefficients on \(u,v,uv\), since subtracting its transforms gives factors \(2u+1\) and \(2v+1\), both nonzero functions even in characteristic two. Thus the joint fixed subring is \(k[A,A^{-1}]\). Coordinate exchange sends \(A\mapsto d/A\), and the invariant Laurent-polynomial ring is \(k[A+d/A]=k[J]\), by the symmetric-Laurent-polynomial recurrence. All eight transformations are the full automorphism group, yielding the claimed affine categorical quotient. In particular this conclusion concerns **regular functions**, not just invariant rational functions.

## Relationship to prior work
Wu's *A New Characteristic-Uniform Model for Elliptic Curves* (arXiv:2608.01675, Theorem 4.6 and Remark 4.7) establishes the smooth model, cyclic boundary subgroup and an eight-element intrinsic dihedral **subgroup**, while noting that the abstract elliptic curve can have extra automorphisms at complex-multiplication parameters. Wu's later *Direct Group Laws on Classical Genus-One Models* (arXiv:2610.00029, §4; primary MSC 14H52) proves the direct projective group law in all characteristics. Andryc and Langer (2026) discuss symmetry subgroups of Edwards curves and full projective genus-one automorphisms, but do not supply the full regular automorphism group of this punctured model. The present step distinguishes the group preserving its four-point complement from the larger automorphism group of the **complete** elliptic curve. The visible subgroup and its invariant function are not presented as newly discovered.

## Limitations
The theorem assumes the affine equation is smooth and \(k\) algebraically closed. It classifies actual geometric regular automorphisms, not automorphism group schemes or arbitrary automorphisms of the projective elliptic curve. Historical priority beyond inspected primary sources remains uncertain, especially because the final upper bound uses standard elliptic-curve automorphism theory. It does not claim a new elliptic group law, a new eight-element subgroup, or a new formula for the subgroup invariant field.
