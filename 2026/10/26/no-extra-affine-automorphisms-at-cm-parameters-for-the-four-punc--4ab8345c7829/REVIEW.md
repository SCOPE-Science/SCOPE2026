# Self-review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The four boundary points form a cyclic order-four subgroup via local principal divisors. Automorphisms of the affine open necessarily stabilize this subgroup. Origin automorphisms acting nontrivially at elliptic complex-multiplication parameters cannot fix a primitive order-four point; the argument includes wild characteristic-three and ordinary characteristic-two cases. This gives a sharp upper bound of eight, attained by the three explicit symmetries. The invariant ring follows from the four-term free basis and Laurent inversion. Exact symbolic checks corroborate coordinate identities but do not prove the group-theoretic upper bound.

## Originality
**PASS, with historical-priority risk.** Wu (arXiv:2608.01675v5, §4.4) establishes the intrinsic subgroup of order eight and its invariant rational field, and explicitly distinguishes it from the potentially larger automorphism group of the abstract projective elliptic curve. Wu (arXiv:2610.00029v1, §4) supplies the direct all-characteristic group law. Andryc--Langer (2026, §3) studies Edwards-model symmetries but does not classify the complete group of regular maps of this four-punctured native affine model in the inspected material. This finding proves the converse group containment, rather than renaming the known subgroup. The classification might already be implicit in older elliptic-curve literature.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The full automorphism group is a natural unknown invariant of a curve whose intrinsic eight symmetries were isolated earlier; the distinction matters at exceptional complex-multiplication parameters. The exact answer is uniform across all characteristics and identifies the quotient by the *full* regular group, not just a selected subgroup. It is a natural complete classification rather than an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Limits
The claim is about geometric regular automorphisms, not automorphism group schemes, and excludes singular parameters. Full historical priority is not independently established. The order-eight subgroup, boundary torsion and its invariant function are all credited to earlier literature.
