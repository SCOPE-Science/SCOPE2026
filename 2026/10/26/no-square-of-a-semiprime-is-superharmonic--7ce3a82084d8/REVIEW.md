# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** Every universal step is proved without relying on enumeration. For \(n=p^2q^2\), \(\tau(n)=9\) gives the necessary support restriction \(\operatorname{rad}(\sigma(n))\mid3pq\). The factors \(\Phi_3(p)\), \(\Phi_3(q)\) have precise three-adic valuations and their other prime factors are \(1\pmod3\). With \(p<q\), the smallest-prime factor must be \(13\) in the generic case; explicit factorizations eliminate the three possible pairs. The auxiliary checker is not used as a proof of universal nonexistence.

## Originality

**PASS under accessible evidence, with residual risk.** Cohen (2008) introduced superharmonicity, established the prime-support necessity, and excluded odd squarefree numbers, but left the broader two-prime factor question open. The square-of-a-semiprime layer is not squarefree and is not excluded by that theorem. Pollack and Pomerance (2012) establish global counts, not a complete local classification of this exponent pattern. The closest accessible mathematical record dealing with superharmonic numbers and a prime odd part concerns \(2^a p\), not \(p^2q^2\). Luca (2004) concerns prime support of \(\sigma(n)\) contained in that of \(n\), whereas here the extra factor \(3\) is allowed. Luca's full text was not accessible and may pose a priority risk; no claim of exhaustive priority certification is made.

## Value

**PASS.** A central open-ended question asks whether odd superharmonic numbers exist and how few distinct prime factors they might have. The minimal square exponent profile on two primes is a natural first nonsquarefree layer, and this theorem rules out the entire infinite family, rather than merely supplying examples or a search cutoff. Its key cyclotomic-support obstruction is reusable in adjacent small-support exponent problems. Its scope is deliberately narrow and not represented as a solution of the general question.

## Remaining limits

The general two-prime case, other exponent patterns, odd superharmonic existence, and definitive independent priority verification remain open or unperformed. Finite testing through prime \(2000\) is corroboration only.
