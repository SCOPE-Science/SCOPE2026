# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Matrix-unit substitution decomposes the entire Hermitian operator space into one weighted graph-Laplacian block and independent symmetric/antisymmetric coherences, yielding the exact gap without relying on a finite test. The sum-of-rates identity gives a universal upper bound; the equality equations show rigidity for every \(N\ne4\), and the four-vertex Laplacian eigenvalues prove a genuine exceptional continuum. An independently assembled dense-superoperator checker matches the formulas on 157 cases. The theorem does not assert any claim about coherent terms or finite-switching errors.

**Originality — PASS (best knowledge, not independently certified).** The October 2026 fluctuation-topology paper already supplies the Lindblad construction and analyzes correlated ensembles including a uniform limit. This finding only claims the arbitrary-independent-edge spectral decomposition and the budget-constrained equality/ridigity law, specifically the four-site nonuniqueness. Full-text comparison with that paper and an earlier random-network study, published-finding semantic searches, and prior-record claim checks identified no statement covering the result. Broader semigroup-optimization literature may still imply a portion of it.

**Value — PASS.** For a fixed decoherence resource, the theorem solves a natural network-design problem and shows that the seemingly obvious uniform optimum is rigid except at a physically small, exceptional system size; the nonuniform four-site family provides multiple equivalent designs. This is stronger information than merely calculating the gap for one graph.

**Risks and limits.** No hardware realizability theorem for arbitrary nonnegative rates, no coherent Hamiltonian, and no independent priority review. General spectral concavity might give another route to the inequality but does not, without equality analysis, settle the four-vertex exception.
