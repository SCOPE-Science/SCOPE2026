# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The closed geometric-sum formula, strict fixed-separation convexity, exponential deficiency bounds and uniform long-range expansion prove the stated theorem with its quantifiers. The continuum product-distance functional has the unique quarter-pair minimum. Exact finite polynomial factorization proves the seven-site cubic switch; the standalone rational verifier checks the arithmetic independently but is not used as a substitute for the asymptotic proof.

## Originality
PASS on the inspected literature, with residual risk. Dhillon, Chakrabarty and Iyengar (2002, DOI:10.1109/ICIF.2002.1021005) use the same exponential observation model and independent misses but solve a threshold feasibility/count problem using a greedy algorithm. Sun, Fuller and Royle (2014, DOI:10.1371/journal.pone.0088025) compare spatial capture–recapture *estimation* designs using half-normal detection. Frasca et al. (arXiv:1404.7711) optimize expected maximum distance to nonfailed sensors. None of their inspected statements implies the specific average-detection optimization law or noncommuting limits. Equivalent probabilistic-facility or sensor-design formulations outside inspected sources remain possible.

## Mathematical value
PASS. A complete fixed-gap centering theorem and asymptotic contradiction between thirds and quarters clarify two natural, but different, trap-design scaling regimes. The first finite regime change occurs sharply at seven sites. The finding does not claim empirical sampling efficiency, general nonlinear detection or universal sensor placement.
