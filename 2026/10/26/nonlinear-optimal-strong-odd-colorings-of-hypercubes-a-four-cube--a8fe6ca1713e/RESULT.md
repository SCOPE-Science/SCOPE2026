# Nonlinear optimal strong odd colorings of hypercubes: a four-cube census and even-dimensional persistence

## Finding
A strong odd coloring of a graph is a proper vertex coloring such that, in every open neighborhood, each color which occurs there occurs an odd number of times. Write \(Q_d\) for the graph on \(\mathbb F_2^d\) joining binary vectors at Hamming distance one. Colors are four named elements of \(\mathbb F_2^2\), encoded as \(0,1,2,3\), with bitwise XOR denoted \(\oplus\).

**Theorem.** Exactly \(1920\) strong odd colorings of \(Q_4\) use this named four-color palette. They define \(80\) color partitions after forgetting the four color names. Exactly \(96\) of the named colorings (\(4\) of the partitions) are affine maps \(\mathbb F_2^4\to\mathbb F_2^2\); the remaining \(1824\) named colorings (\(76\) partitions) are nonlinear. In \(Q_2\), every strong odd four-coloring is affine. Every even-dimensional cube \(Q_d\) with \(d\ge4\) possesses a nonlinear optimal strong odd four-coloring.

More generally, the number of affine optimal strong odd four-colorings of \(Q_d\) for every even \(d\ge2\) is \(3\cdot2^{d+1}\), or \(2^{d-2}\) after forgetting color names. The fact that four colors are optimal in even dimension is a previously established result, not a new claim; the contributions here are the exact four-cube census, its affine/nonlinear separation, and the indefinite nonlinear extension.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple, undirected, and have vertex-distinguished binary coordinates. Different color names are counted separately unless explicitly stated otherwise. An affine coloring has the form \(f(x)=b\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d}x_iv_i\), with \(b,v_i\in\mathbb F_2^2\). This definition is invariant under any permutation of the four named colors, because every such permutation is an affine permutation of \(\mathbb F_2^2\). Optimality means using the least number of colors admitted by a strong odd coloring. There is no assertion about the total number of optimal colorings in dimensions six and higher.

## Proof
**1. Affine maps and optimum.** A proper affine map must have \(v_i\ne0\) for every coordinate. In an open neighborhood of \(x\), the colors are \(f(x)\oplus v_i\) over all \(i\), hence the strong odd property is equivalent to every direction among \(v_1,\ldots,v_d\) occurring an odd number of times. For even \(d\), the number of different used nonzero directions must therefore be even; it cannot be zero and is at most three, so it is exactly two. There are three choices of the unordered pair of directions, \(2^{d-1}\) ways to choose a nonempty odd-size support for the first direction (its complement also has odd size), and four translations \(b\). This gives \(3\cdot2^{d+1}\) named affine colorings. Two distinct nonzero directions span \(\mathbb F_2^2\), ensuring all four colors occur. Each orbit under renaming the four colors has size \(24\), yielding \(2^{d-2}\) affine partitions.

For completeness, the known lower bound of four in even dimension follows directly from parity linear algebra. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), the cube adjacency operator \(A=\sum_{i=1}^dT_i\), where \(T_i\) flips coordinate \(i\), satisfies \(A^2=dI=0\) when \(d\) is even. A proper strong odd coloring using at most three colors must use exactly three: the even degree requires an even number of distinct neighbor colors, necessarily exactly two, and these must be the two colors other than the center color. Thus each of the three color indicators \(h_j\) would satisfy \(Ah_j=\boldsymbol1-h_j\), since both non-center colors occur an odd number of times at each vertex, while the center color cannot occur in its own neighborhood. Thus \((A+I)h_j=\boldsymbol1\). But \((A+I)^2=I\), giving \(h_j=(A+I)\boldsymbol1=\boldsymbol1\) because \(A\boldsymbol1=d\boldsymbol1=0\). This is incompatible with a proper coloring on a graph with edges. Therefore at least four colors are required, and the affine construction attains four.

**2. Full four-cube census.** A degree-four vertex in a strong odd four-coloring must see exactly two colors, with multiplicities three and one: neither one, three nor four distinct colors can sum to four using positive odd multiplicities. Every coloring of \(Q_4\) consequently has a unique initial-star datum consisting of the root color (four possibilities), the singleton-neighbor color (three), the triple-neighbor color (two), and the location of the singleton among four neighbors. These \(96\) initial data are equivalent for counting by an affine permutation of the palette and a coordinate permutation.

Normalize the root \(0\) and its neighbors by \(f(0)=0\), \(f(1)=2\), and \(f(2)=f(4)=f(8)=1\), with binary vertex codes \(0,\ldots,15\). Of the six vertices at Hamming weight two, the vertices \(3,5,9\) must have colors in \(\{0,3\}\), and the vertices \(6,10,12\) colors in \(\{0,2,3\}\). All \(2^3 3^3=216\) such assignments are examined by the explicit, self-contained `verify.py` layered enumerator. It discards assignments violating any fully exposed open-neighborhood parity constraint at a weight-one vertex, leaving \(11\) assignments. Each of the four weight-three vertex colors is then exhaustively chosen among colors unlike the already colored adjacent weight-two vertices, and the unique weight-four vertex is exhaustively assigned one of four colors; complete properness and every neighborhood parity are checked for each result. Exactly \(20\) normalized complete colorings survive. Their histogram classified by numbers of zero-colored weight-two vertices of the two specified types is \((0,0)\mapsto3\), \((0,3)\mapsto2\), \((2,1)\mapsto6\), \((2,2)\mapsto9\). It follows that there are precisely \(96\cdot20=1920\) labeled colorings.

A separately structured exhaustive checker in the same standalone verifier assigns colors first to all eight even-weight vertices (fixing root color zero) and then to all eight odd-weight vertices. Of \(4^7=16384\) even-layer assignments, precisely \(54\) have odd multiplicities in every odd-vertex neighborhood; their allowed proper odd-layer assignments total \(12288\), with precisely \(480\) also meeting even-vertex parity. Translation in the palette gives \(4\cdot480=1920\), independently reproducing the first count. Both enumeration methods test the original vertex-neighborhood definition and exhaust every legal assignment in their stated partitions, rather than relying on random samples. `verify.py` is the finite counting certificate; its full execution output is `verification_output.txt`.

Among the twenty normalized complete colorings, exactly one obeys all affine identities \(f(x)=f(0)\oplus\bigoplus_i x_i(f(e_i)\oplus f(0))\). Thus the number of affine named colorings is \(96\), and the nonlinear count is \(1920-96=1824\). Every four-coloring actually uses all four colors (a three-coloring is impossible), so renaming acts freely, dividing these three counts by \(24\) to get \(80,4,76\) partitions.

**3. Nonlinear seed and extension.** Give \(Q_4\) this color word, indexed in increasing binary vertex order:
\[ (f_4(0),\ldots,f_4(15))=(0,2,1,0,1,0,0,1,1,3,3,1,3,1,2,3). \]
Direct inspection of the sixteen neighbor lists shows that every color in each open neighborhood has odd multiplicity and none equals the center color. Moreover, for each vertex \(x\), the color \(f_4(x)\oplus1\) appears at least once among the neighbors. It is not affine: \(f_4(0)=0\), \(f_4(1)=2\), \(f_4(2)=1\), yet \(f_4(3)=0\ne2\oplus1=3\).

For every even \(d=4+2k\), write a vertex as \((x,z)\in\mathbb F_2^4\times\mathbb F_2^{2k}\) and set \(f_d(x,z)=f_4(x)\oplus\bigl(\sum_i z_i\bmod2\bigr)\), where the final bit is understood as the first coordinate of \(\mathbb F_2^2\). The four neighbors obtained by changing \(x\) retain exactly the shifted seed's odd neighbor-color multiplicities. The additional \(2k\) neighbors all have color \(f_d(x,z)\oplus1\), which is already present in the original four-neighbor multiset; adding an even number preserves its odd multiplicity. All such neighbors have colors different from the center's color, so the extended labeling is proper and strong odd. It remains nonaffine on the slice \(z=0\), and uses four colors, hence is optimal. For \(d=2\), \(Q_2\) has four vertices with all mutually at distance at most two, so any strong odd four-coloring uses four different colors, and every bijection \(\mathbb F_2^2\to\mathbb F_2^2\) is affine.

## Verification
The provided `verify.py` contains two exhaustive finite algorithms with different search orders for the \(Q_4\) count, validates the affine census independently by enumerating direction vectors for dimensions two, four, six and eight, and verifies the explicit seed and its lifted colorings directly on \(Q_d\) for \(d=4,6,8,10,12\). Run `python3 verify.py` with standard Python 3; the checked output is supplied as `verification_output.txt`. Finite enumeration proves the fixed \(Q_4\) claim because the search space is exhaustively partitioned. The all-even-dimensional extension rests on the algebraic argument above, not on finite tests.

## Relationship to prior work
Kwon and Park introduced and investigated strong odd coloring in the 2024 preprint cited below. Caro, Petruševski, Škrekovski and Tuza study strong odd colorings, including Cartesian products; their subsequent paper on odd independence already proves that \(\chi_{\mathrm{so}}(Q_d)=4\) for even \(d\) (and two for odd \(d\)). The present result does **not** claim to discover those chromatic numbers. It distinguishes affine from genuinely nonlinear optimal colorings, counts every optimum on the four-cube, identifies the first even dimension supporting nonlinear optimums, and constructs them in all higher even dimensions. The inspected primary source statements and relevant mathematical research records did not provide this count or an equivalent nonlinear-persistence classification. Search failure is not proof of priority; unindexed equivalent enumerations remain a possibility.

## Limitations
The census of **all** optimal colorings is proved only for \(Q_4\). For even dimensions at least six, an affine count and the existence of nonaffine colorings are proved, but no total count or nonlinear classification is claimed. The \(Q_4\) exact finite enumeration depends on reproducible exhaustive Python code, not a compact hand enumeration. The result has undergone self-review only; no independent audit or independent validation is claimed. Bibliographic priority is subject to inaccessible or unindexed equivalent results.
