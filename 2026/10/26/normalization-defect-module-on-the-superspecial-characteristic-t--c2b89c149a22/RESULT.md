# Normalization-defect module on the superspecial characteristic-two Kummer partial resolution

## Finding

Let \(k\) be algebraically closed of characteristic \(2\), let \(A=E\times E\) be the superspecial abelian surface in Schröer’s model, let \(T=A/\{\pm1\}\), and let \(T'=\operatorname{Bl}_{(x^2,y^2,z)}T\) be the crepant partial resolution at its singular point. If \(\nu:S\to T'\) is the normalization and \(F\cong\mathbb P^1\oplus\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)\) is the exceptional fiber, then
\[
\nu_*\mathcal O_S/\mathcal O_{T'}\cong i_*\pi^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1),
\]
where \(i:F\hookrightarrow T'\) and \(\pi:F\to F_{\mathrm{red}}\cong\mathbb P^1\) is the split-ribbon projection. Thus the normalization defect is a line bundle on the nonreduced exceptional fiber, not merely a sheaf supported set-theoretically on it. Its nilpotent filtration is
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-2)\longrightarrow\pi^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)\longrightarrow0,
\]
and \(h^1(F,\pi^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1))=1\).

## Assumptions and scope

The claim is local to the superspecial characteristic-two case studied by Schröer. The quotient surface has completed local equation
\[
z^2+x^2y^2z+xy^4+yx^4=0,
\]
and \(T'\) is the blow-up of the primary ideal \((x^2,y^2,z)\). The exceptional fiber \(F\) is the split infinitesimal extension \(\mathbb P^1\oplus\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)\). The statement concerns the sheaf quotient of the normalization map; it does not assert that \(T'\) is normal or that this model is a full resolution.

## Proof

Work first on the \(x^2\)-chart of the blow-up. Put \(q=y^2/x^2\) and \(r=z/x^2\). Its ring is
\[
R_x=k[x,y,q,r]\big/\big(y^2-x^2q,\ r^2+x^2qr+xq^2+y\big).
\]
The normalization chart is generated by \(x,t=y/x,r\), with \(q=t^2\) and \(y=xt\), and relation
\[
r^2+x^2t^2r+xt^4+xt=0.
\]
Thus \(S_x=R_x[t]\), with \(t^2=q\), and \(S_x/R_x\) is generated as an \(R_x\)-module by the class of \(t\).

The class of \(t\) is annihilated by \(x\), since \(xt=y\in R_x\). It is also annihilated by \(r^2\): multiplying the normalization relation by \(t\), and using \(xt=y\) and \(t^2=q\), expresses \(r^2t\) as an element of \(R_x\). Hence there is a surjection
\[
R_x/(x,r^2)\longrightarrow S_x/R_x,\qquad f\longmapsto ft.
\]
For injectivity, reduce modulo \(x\). The source becomes \(k[q,r]/(r^2)\), embedded in \(S_x/(x)=k[t,r]/(r^2)\) by \(q\mapsto t^2\). Multiplication by \(t\) sends a polynomial in \(t^2\) to an odd polynomial in \(t\); it can lie in the even subring only when its class is zero. Therefore
\[
S_x/R_x\cong R_x/(x,r^2)\cong\mathcal O_F
\]
on this chart, with local generator the class of \(t\).

The \(y^2\)-chart is symmetric, with \(s=x/y\) as the added normalization generator. On the overlap, \(s=t^{-1}\) and \(q=t^2\), so the local generators of the quotient satisfy
\[
[s]=q^{-1}[t].
\]
The transition \(q^{-1}\) is precisely the transition for \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)\) on the reduced exceptional \(\mathbb P^1\). The exceptional fiber does not meet the \(z\)-chart: substituting the blow-up ratios into the defining equation gives a constant \(1\) on the exceptional locus there. Away from the exceptional fiber the blow-up is an isomorphism and the original quotient surface is normal. These charts therefore glue to
\[
\nu_*\mathcal O_S/\mathcal O_{T'}\cong i_*\pi^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1).
\]

Because \(F\) is the split ribbon with square-zero ideal \(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)\), tensoring its defining filtration by \(\pi^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}(-1)\) gives the stated filtration with subquotients \(\mathcal O(-2)\) and \(\mathcal O(-1)\). Since \(H^1(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(-2))\cong k\), \(H^1(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(-1))=0\), and both \(H^0\) groups vanish, the long exact sequence gives \(h^1(F,\pi^*\mathcal O(-1))=1\).

## Verification

The two chart presentations and the transition of the added normalization generator were checked against the displayed equations. The quotient module is determined by the annihilator \((x,r^2)\) on the \(x^2\)-chart and the symmetric calculation on the \(y^2\)-chart; the \(z\)-chart contributes no exceptional points. The cohomology calculation uses only the standard cohomology of line bundles on \(\mathbb P^1\). No computer algebra or finite enumeration is used as a substitute for the ring argument.

## Relationship to prior work

Schröer proves that the two natural blow-ups in the superspecial case are nonnormal, have isomorphic normalizations, and share conductor \(\mathcal O_S(-S_t)\). He also identifies the conductor scheme’s schematic image on \(T'\) with the nonreduced exceptional fiber and states that the map on reduced fibers is Frobenius. The present claim refines those results by identifying the full quotient module \(\nu_*\mathcal O_S/\mathcal O_{T'}\) and its nilpotent filtration; it is not a claim that the blow-up, its normalization, or its conductor is new.

## Limitations

The result is specific to the superspecial characteristic-two model and does not classify normalization-defect modules for arbitrary wild involutions or other abelian surfaces. The literature comparison found no exact statement of this quotient-module isomorphism in the inspected primary text, but a complete priority check of every cited historical source was not possible; an equivalent calculation elsewhere remains a residual risk. The claimed cohomology dimension is a consequence of the sheaf identification, not an independent global invariant of the whole surface.
