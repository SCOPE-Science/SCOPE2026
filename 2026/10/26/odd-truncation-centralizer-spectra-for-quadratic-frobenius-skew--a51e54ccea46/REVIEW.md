# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The Ore-product commutator is independently expanded in even and odd powers. Writing a quadratic extension in the basis \(1,\theta\) turns the commutator into precisely three two-by-two minors in the local chain ring \(S=\mathbb F_s[z]/(z^a)\). The central quotient, valuation-dependent annihilators, exact class sizes, and geometric sum were re-derived. Complete brute-force field calculations over \(\mathbb F_4\) and \(\mathbb F_9\), supplemented by \(\mathbb F_{16}\) centralizer tests, agree with the result. The infinite-family statement follows from the proof, not sampling.

**Originality — PASS within investigated literature.** Dutta–Basnet–Nath's finite ring centralizer classification addresses small numbers of *distinct centralizers*; its exact results do not determine the present odd Ore-quotient centralizer-size law. Juráš–Ursul's commuting-probability spectrum inclusion is global, not a ring-specific calculation. Martínez-Peñas' skew polynomial classification concerns isomorphisms; Boulagouaz–Deajim's finite skew-code quotient analysis concerns generator and duality matrices; Richter–Silvestrov use a different noninvertible-endomorphism polynomial coefficient setting. Searches of published research records, these primary sources, and accessible historical claims found no implication proving the displayed family formula. Absence from search is not itself a uniqueness proof; a possible implicit reduction or older specialist computation remains a residual priority risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Commuting probability and element-centralizer spectra are intrinsic invariants of finite noncommutative rings. The result does not merely substitute parameters into a standard identity: its three-minor isomorphism simultaneously resolves every centralizer-size stratum over an unbounded family of nonsemisimple Ore quotients, handles the characteristic-two case, and identifies the exponential decay constant along the radical-filtration length. The local-chain-ring exterior geometry also supplies a concrete comparison point for commutation in other truncated skew algebras.
