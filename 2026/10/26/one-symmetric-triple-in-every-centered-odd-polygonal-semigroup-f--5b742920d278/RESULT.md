# One symmetric triple in every centered odd-polygonal semigroup family

## Finding

For each odd integer \(k\ge3\), write \(C_k(t)=1+\frac{k}{2}t(t-1)\) for the centered \(k\)-gonal numbers. The numerical semigroup generated by any three consecutive terms of indices at least \(k\) is symmetric at **exactly one** starting index:
\[
\boxed{\langle C_k(n),C_k(n+1),C_k(n+2)\rangle\text{ is symmetric}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n=\frac{3k+7}{2},\qquad n\ge k.}
\]
At the exceptional index its Frobenius number and genus are
\[
\boxed{F=\frac{27k^5+432k^4+2058k^3+3912k^2+2779k+328}{64},\qquad g=\frac{F+1}{2}.}
\]
Examples: centered triangular order \(k=3\) gives \(n=8\), generators \(85,109,136\), \(F=2203\); centered pentagonal order \(k=5\) gives \(n=11\), generators \(276,331,391\), \(F=11307\); centered heptagonal order \(k=7\) gives \(n=14\), generators \(638,736,841\), \(F=37631\).

## Assumptions and scope

Throughout, \(k\) is an odd integer at least \(3\), and \(n\) is an integer at least \(k\). Set
\[
a=C_k(n),\quad b=C_k(n+1),\quad c=C_k(n+2),\quad K=3k+8,\quad N=2n+1.
\]
Thus \(0<a<b<c\) and \(\gcd(a,b,c)=1\). A numerical semigroup is symmetric when its complement in the nonnegative integers is reflected about its Frobenius number, equivalently when its genus \(g\) and Frobenius number \(F\) satisfy \(2g=F+1\). The Frobenius number is the largest excluded integer. All generators and quantifiers are ordinary integers.

## Proof

**1. Pairwise gcd identities.** Since each centered number is congruent to \(1\) modulo \(k\), and
\[
b-a=kn,\quad c-b=k(n+1),\quad c-a=k(2n+1),
\]
we have
\[
\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(a,n)\le2,\qquad \gcd(b,c)=\gcd(b,n+1)\le2.
\]
For example, if \(n\) is odd then \(a\equiv1\pmod n\). If \(n\) is even, \(a\equiv1+k n/2\pmod n\), so its gcd with \(n\) is either \(1\) or \(2\); the same argument works for \(b,n+1\). Also \(8a\equiv3k+8\pmod N\), and \(N\) is odd. Therefore
\[
 d:=\gcd(a,c)=\gcd(N,K).
\]
In particular, \(d\) is odd, and the three generators have gcd one.

**2. The gluing criterion.** For three positive integers of overall gcd one, the associated numerical semigroup is symmetric exactly when there is a pair \(x,y\), with \(d_0=\gcd(x,y)\), such that the third generator \(z\) belongs to \(\langle x/d_0,y/d_0\rangle\) and \(\gcd(d_0,z)=1\). This is the classical three-generator symmetry criterion; if an original generator is redundant, the same criterion applies with the redundant generator as \(z\). We show that only the pair \((a,c)\) can satisfy it.

**3. Eliminate \((b,c)\) and \((a,b)\).** Suppose \(a\in\langle b/d_0,c/d_0\rangle\) for \(d_0=\gcd(b,c)\le2\). Then \(d_0 a=u b+v c\), with nonnegative integers \(u,v\). Setting \(s=u+v\), we obtain \(s b\le d_0 a<d_0 b\), hence \(s<d_0\). Reducing modulo \(k\) gives \(s\equiv d_0\pmod k\), impossible because \(0\le s<d_0\le2<k\).

Suppose instead \(c\in\langle a/d_0,b/d_0\rangle\), where \(d_0=\gcd(a,b)\le2\). Writing \(d_0 c=u a+v b\), we have \(s=u+v>d_0\), since \(s b\ge d_0 c>d_0 b\). Again \(s\equiv d_0\pmod k\), so \(s\ge d_0+k\). As \(s a\le d_0 c\), it follows that
\[
(d_0+k)a\le d_0 c,\qquad a\le d_0(2n+1).
\]
For \(n\ge4\) and \(k\ge3\), \(a\ge1+3n(n-1)/2>2(2n+1)\), contradicting this inequality. The only case with \(n=3\) is \(k=3\), where \(a=10\), \(d_0=1\), and \(10>2n+1=7\). Hence both pairings fail.

**4. Reduce \((a,c)\) to one divisibility equation.** Assume \(b\in\langle a/d,c/d\rangle\), and write \(d b=u a+v c\) with \(u,v\ge0\). Set \(s=u+v\); since the three generators are \(1\) modulo \(k\), \(s\equiv d\pmod k\). If
\[
dn<a\qquad\text{and}\qquad d(n+1)<c,
\]
then from \(s a\le d b\le s c\) and the consecutive differences,
\[
 d-k<s<d+k.
\]
Therefore \(s=d\) and
\[
 d(b-a)=v(c-a),\qquad v=\frac{dn}{2n+1}.
\]
The coprimality \(\gcd(n,2n+1)=1\) forces \(N=2n+1\mid d\). Because \(d=\gcd(N,K)\mid N\), this gives \(d=N\), so \(N\mid K\). Since \(n\ge k\), we have \(N\ge2k+1\). Moreover, \(K=3k+8<3(2k+1)\), and both \(K,N\) are odd. Thus the positive integer quotient \(K/N\) is odd and less than \(3\), so it equals \(1\). We conclude \(N=K\), equivalently \(n=(3k+7)/2\).

**5. Verify the two inequalities, without hidden asymptotics.** For odd \(k\ge5\), the odd integer \(K=3k+8\) satisfies either \(d<K\), in which case its proper positive divisor \(d\le K/3<k(k-1)/2\le a/n\), or \(d=K\), in which case \(K\mid N\), so \(n\ge(K-1)/2=(3k+7)/2\) and
\[
\frac an>\frac{k(n-1)}2\ge\frac{k(3k+5)}4>K=d.
\]
In both cases \(d<c/(n+1)\) as well: for a proper divisor \(d\le K/3<k(n+2)/2<c/(n+1)\), while for \(d=K\) the bound \(k(n+2)/2\ge k(3k+11)/4>K\) holds. Thus the inequalities of Step 4 hold for \(k\ge5\).

For \(k=3\), \(K=17\) is prime. If \(d=1\), then \(a<b<2a<c+a\) for all \(n\ge3\); hence \(b\notin\langle a,c\rangle\). If \(d=17\), then \(2n+1\) is a multiple of \(17\), so \(n=8+17t\), \(t\ge0\). At \(n=8\), we have \(N=K\). For \(t\ge1\), \(n\ge25\) and \(a/n>17\), \(c/(n+1)>17\), so Step 4 again forces \(N=K\), contradicting \(n>8\). This finishes necessity in every allowed case.

**6. Sufficiency and invariants.** Conversely, put \(n=(3k+7)/2\); then \(N=K=d\). The identity
\[
(n+1)a+n c=(2n+1)b=K b
\]
shows
\[
 b=(n+1)\frac aK+n\frac cK.
\]
Further, \(\gcd(K,b)=1\): modulo \(K=2n+1\), \(b\equiv kn\), and \(\gcd(n,K)=\gcd(k,K)=1\), the latter because \(k\) is odd and \(K=3k+8\). The gluing criterion therefore proves symmetry. The coprime pair \(a/K,c/K\) has Sylvester Frobenius number \((a/K)(c/K)-a/K-c/K\), so the standard gluing formula gives
\[
F=\frac{ac}{K}-a-c+(K-1)b.
\]
Substituting
\[
\frac aK=\frac{(k+1)(3k+1)}8,\quad
\frac cK=\frac{3k^2+12k+1}8,\quad
 b=\frac{9k^3+48k^2+63k+8}8
\]
and simplifying produces the displayed quintic \(F\). Symmetry gives \(g=(F+1)/2\). All denominators divide the indicated numerators because \(k\) is odd. This proves the theorem.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` reproduces all three exact gluing orientations for 7,550 parameter pairs (odd \(k\) from \(3\) through \(101\), each with the first 151 indices \(n\ge k\)). It then independently computes Apéry-set Frobenius numbers and genera for the first ten symmetric examples, checks the closed quintic and gluing coefficients, and verifies five nonsymmetric examples by Apéry sets. The symbolic proof above—not a finite scan—establishes all infinitely many cases.

## Relationship to prior work

The three-generator gluing criterion is classical and is presented in Fel (2008). Robles-Pérez and Rosales (2017) study triples of ordinary triangular numbers and quadruples of tetrahedral numbers; their figurate generators differ from centered odd-polygonal numbers. Yin, Mu, and Komatsu (2024) treat triples \(a n(n-1)+1\) for integer \(a\ge4\), i.e. centered **even** \(2a\)-gonal numbers, and give generalized Frobenius formulas. Their stated integer-\(a\) domain does not include the half-integer \(a=k/2\) for odd \(k\); their formulas therefore cannot simply be applied to the present theorem. The centered heptagonal sequence is documented in OEIS A069099. The gluing theorem, centered-number definition, and neighboring figurate-semigroup literature are prior work; novelty is claimed only for the odd-order, stable-index symmetry classification and its exact Frobenius specialization.

## Limitations

The hypothesis \(n\ge k\) is essential to the argument; smaller starting indices have sporadic additional symmetric cases. The theorem does not classify those smaller indices, does not treat even centered polygon orders, and does not determine Frobenius numbers for nonsymmetric triples. A published equivalent result in unlocated literature cannot be excluded without independent review. Finite verification is corroborative rather than a proof of the infinite classification.
