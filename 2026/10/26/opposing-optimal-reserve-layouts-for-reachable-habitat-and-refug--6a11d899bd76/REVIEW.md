# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The one-gap law follows from disjoint prefix/suffix survival runs, and products of its probability-generating functions yield the complete reachable-patch distribution. The coefficientwise balancing certificate displayed in the proof is nonnegative for every real survival probability strictly between zero and one, proving every tail inequality rather than extrapolating from computation. Mutual-refuge connectivity follows by classifying the event that all but at most one inter-refuge gap is completely functional. The universal coverage ceiling and its exact feasibility cutoff follow from a pathwise upper bound and a positive-probability isolated-survivor obstruction. Exact rational enumeration and independent recovery of the stated formulas corroborate the algebra. Non-strictness of the exceptional \((2,0)\) move is explicitly retained.

## Originality — PASS with residual bibliographic risk

The closest inspected ecological reserve-spacing study, Wagner–Ross–Possingham (arXiv:math/0309255), treats two reserve populations under dynamic Markov colonization and catastrophes rather than many always-functional refuges and finite-cycle site failures. The full 2014 Frasca–Garin–Gerencsér–Hendrickx arXiv study minimizes expected maximum geometric coverage distance for *unreliable sensors*, not the entire distribution of reachable live patches when the relays fail and source refuges persist. Hof–Flather (1996) treats a continuous wildlife-placement optimization with correlation; Blowes–Connolly (2012) addresses risk spreading but has only partially accessible detail. No inspected statement proves, dominates, or reduces to both the every-threshold first-order stochastic coverage extremum and the reverse protected-connectivity extremum. Results under alternative network-reliability or percolation terminology remain a real residual risk. No independence or bibliographic completeness is claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The derived order compares *every* capacity threshold, rather than only expected rescued habitat, for a natural conservation placement problem; simultaneously it establishes a conflicting reliability criterion, thus preventing conflation of maximizing local reachable habitat with keeping all reserves interconnected. The exact limiting placement feasibility \(n\le3k\), the one-gap law, and the exceptional equal-distribution balancing move distinguish structural regimes. These precise facts can inform model-based placement tradeoffs without presuming they generalize to ecological field systems.

## Remaining limitations

Static independent homogeneous site failures, guaranteed protected patches, and cycle geometry are essential. No population dynamic, colonization or actual extinction probability is evaluated. Full-text prior-literature coverage and independent verification remain pending.
