# Review: hidden-target geometric resetting on vertex-transitive graphs

## Correctness — PASS

The statement requires a target drawn once from a uniform prior, an initial position and every relocation drawn from the same law, unit-duration reset steps, and absorption at time zero when starting on the target. With those hypotheses, the hitting-time generating function is the resolvent column divided by its target diagonal. The geometric-restart renewal equation gives the exact targetwise mean, including target hits by relocation. The row-sum identity for the resolvent, strict arithmetic–harmonic inequality, and its exact variance-like identity give the unique uniform optimal relocation law and regret coefficient; invertibility rules out other equality cases. The minimum-resolvent entry gives the independent worst-target result. The spectral expression, strict convexity and rate criterion follow under symmetric transitive dynamics, and the cycle cutoff follows from the exact second- and fourth-cosecant sums. A Taylor expansion with a uniformly bounded remainder establishes the large-cycle limits. Independent absorbing-chain solutions and symbolic checks in `verify.py` executed successfully. Finite computations do not prove the all-graph/all-size quantifiers; those follow from the displayed algebra and analytic identities.

## Originality — PASS, with explicit boundary

The sources already derive network resetting resolvents and first-passage formulas. In particular, the uniform Google-reset spectral formula is stated in González–Riascos–Boyer, arXiv:2104.00727, Eq. (76), and the reset-law-independent Kemeny constant, its strict convexity and its rate derivative criterion are explicit in Michelitsch–D'Onofrio–Polito–Riascos, arXiv:2409.08394, Eqs. (47), (51)–(52). **None of those background identities is asserted as new.** Riascos–Boyer–Mateos, arXiv:2110.15437, analyzes rate optimization when the searcher resets to its fixed starting node and seeks a specified target. The assessed new statement instead fixes an *exogenous uniform target prior*, optimizes over **every reset distribution** on a vertex-transitive graph, proves simultaneous mean and worst-target optimality with an exact strictly positive penalty, and identifies the exact all-cycle transition and fifth-cycle optimum. The stationarity-weighted Kemeny functional from prior work cannot replace the uniform-prior objective: under nonuniform resetting its target weights change with the reset law, making it invariant while the present objective has an exact nonzero heterogeneity penalty. Published formulas make the present analysis accessible but do not by themselves state these strict optimization and sharp classification consequences. Residual risk remains of equivalent formulations in graph-search or generalized mean-access-time literature.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The optimization is motivated by a searcher that does not know which vertex is the target. Exact nonuniformity regret quantifies the penalty for preferential relocation, while the simultaneous minimax result shows fairness and average performance coincide under vertex transitivity. The distinction between uniform target priors and stationary-weighted random target functionals prevents an apparently natural but incorrect optimization substitution. The criterion gives a complete all-graph prescription at fixed rate and, for cycles, a sharp integer cutoff, an exact five-site optimum and an asymptotic scale. This is a natural design classification, not a relabeled fixed-target example or an arbitrary finite parameter slice.
