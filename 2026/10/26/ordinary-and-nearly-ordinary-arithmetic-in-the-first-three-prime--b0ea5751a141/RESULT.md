# Ordinary and nearly ordinary arithmetic in small prime-field plane-filling curves

## Finding
For \(p\in\{3,5,7\}\) prime and \(k\in\mathbb F_p\), set
\[
F_{p,k}(x,y,z)=x^2(x^py-xy^p)+y^2(y^pz-yz^p)+(z^2+kx^2)(z^px-zx^p),\qquad C_{p,k}=V(F_{p,k})\subset\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb F_p}.
\]
This is the degree-\(p+3\) plane-filling family introduced by Asgarli and Ghioca. All geometrically smooth members for these three primes and their Jacobian invariants are:

| Prime | Parameters giving geometric smoothness | Genus | \(p\)-rank | \(a\)-number |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \(3\) | \(k=2\) | 10 | 10 | 0 |
| \(5\) | \(k=4\) | 21 | 21 | 0 |
| \(7\) | \(k=1\) or \(k=3\) | 36 | 35 | 1 |

Consequently, both smooth characteristic-seven curves are nonordinary with \(7\)-rank exactly one below genus, whereas all smooth members of the preceding two prime-field families are ordinary. The characteristic-seven Jacobians have Newton slopes \(0\) with multiplicity \(35\), \(1/2\) with multiplicity \(2\), and \(1\) with multiplicity \(35\).

## Assumptions and scope
All curves are projective over the indicated prime field. A curve is geometrically smooth when it has no singular point over an algebraic closure. The \(p\)-rank is the dimension of the geometric \(p\)-torsion group of the Jacobian over \(\mathbb F_p\); the \(a\)-number is the dimension of homomorphisms from \(\alpha_p\) to the Jacobian. The result concerns exactly the displayed Asgarli–Ghioca one-parameter family, not all plane-filling curves in these degrees. It does not claim a pattern for other primes.

## Proof
**Smooth parameters.** Theorem 1.2 of Asgarli–Ghioca shows that a member is smooth at all rational points precisely when \(t^7+kt^3-1\) has no root in \(\mathbb F_p\). Direct evaluation at each \(t\in\mathbb F_p\) gives the complete lists \(\{2\}\), \(\{4\}\), and \(\{1,3\}\) for \(p=3,5,7\). Therefore every parameter outside these lists yields a singular curve. For every listed parameter, substitute each coordinate successively equal to \(1\). In each affine chart, the ideal generated by the dehomogenized equation and both of its affine partial derivatives has reduced Gröbner basis \(\{1\}\) over \(\mathbb F_p\). Since scalar extension to an algebraic closure preserves this unit ideal, all four listed curves are geometrically smooth. The exact finite computations are regenerated by `verify.py`; its script also independently expands the defining polynomial and checks all rational projective points lie on the curve.

**Cohomological matrix.** For a smooth plane curve of degree \(d=p+3\), adjunction gives genus \(g=(d-1)(d-2)/2\). Index the standard basis of \(H^1(C,\mathcal O_C)\) by triples \(u=(u_1,u_2,u_3)\) with positive integer entries and \(u_1+u_2+u_3=d\). Write \(c_e\) for the coefficient of monomial exponent vector \(e\) in \(F_{p,k}^{p-1}\), with \(c_e=0\) if any entry of \(e\) is negative. In this basis, Frobenius on \(H^1(C,\mathcal O_C)\) is represented by the Hasse–Witt matrix
\[
H_{u,v}=c_{pu-v}\pmod p.
\]
This coefficient formula is the classical Hasse–Witt hypersurface formula; it is also the \(\mu=0\) case discussed by Adolphson–Sperber. The ordering used below is increasing first index, then increasing second index.

**Finite exact certificates.** For the unique admissible \(p=3\) curve, the \(10\times10\) matrix has determinant \(1\pmod3\). For the unique admissible \(p=5\) curve, the \(21\times21\) matrix has determinant \(4\pmod5\). Hence both are ordinary. For \(p=7\), both \(36\times36\) Hasse–Witt determinants vanish. Deleting the first row and first column from the matrix for \(k=1\) gives a \(35\times35\) minor of determinant \(4\pmod7\); deleting the first row and second column for \(k=3\) gives determinant \(4\pmod7\). Thus both matrices have rank exactly \(35\). The corresponding minors of their squares have determinants \(3\pmod7\) and \(4\pmod7\), respectively. Thus \(\operatorname{rank}(H^2)=35\), and since \(\operatorname{rank}(H)=35\), the images stabilize; all higher Frobenius powers have rank \(35\).

**Geometric interpretation.** Over the prime fields in question, Frobenius acts linearly on the basis coefficients because every coefficient belongs to \(\mathbb F_p\). The stable rank of its iterate equals the \(p\)-rank; the rank defect of the first Hasse–Witt matrix equals the \(a\)-number. This proves the table. For genus \(36\) and \(p\)-rank \(35\), Newton-polygon duality leaves exactly two slopes between zero and one. Their required equal denominator multiplicities force the remaining pair to be \(1/2,1/2\). This proves the stated slopes.

## Verification
Run `python verify.py` with Python 3.10+ and SymPy. The script uses two separate coefficient-expansion algorithms: sparse dictionary multiplication and SymPy polynomial expansion. It performs modular Gaussian elimination, independent determinant/minor checks, square-rank checks, Gröbner-unit-ideal tests in all three charts, and enumeration of the rational projective points. In the checked environment (SymPy 1.14.0), execution ends with `VERIFY_OK` and exactly four case records. All computations are exact in the designated finite fields. The finite case enumeration is exhaustive because the theorem quantifies only over three explicitly fixed primes and their finitely many parameters.

## Relationship to prior work
Asgarli–Ghioca introduced the family and supplied the rational-point smoothness criterion; their article focuses on existence and smoothness rather than Jacobian arithmetic. Duran Cunha proved ordinarity for *Tallini curves*, which have degree \(p+2\), not the present degree-\(p+3\) family. Adolphson–Sperber give the general Hasse–Witt coefficient formula, and Kudo–Harashita give general Cartier-matrix algorithms; neither of these general methods supplies the four numerical ranks in the present family without the finite calculations. The observed arithmetic change at the first three odd prime fields is not a deduction from the number of rational curve points or from genus alone.

## Limitations
This is an exact finite classification within one named family and only for \(p=3,5,7\). It does not establish an infinite-prime trend, a characterization of all smooth plane-filling curves, or an isogeny decomposition of the Jacobians. The Newton-polygon computation gives slopes but not a canonical decomposition. General algorithms predate this calculation, and an unindexed arithmetic table could overlap the specific ranks; no absolute novelty is claimed beyond the completed comparisons.
