# Scientific review: outer-root parity resonance and harmonic valence

## Correctness
**PASS.** The imaginary equation restricts every nonzero zero of the harmonic polynomial to one of \(n\) real lines. For fixed \(j\), its real equation is a degree-\(n\) perturbation of a real Kostlan polynomial. The rescaling \(x=\varepsilon^{-1/(n-m)}y\) identifies all escaping roots as the nonzero roots of \(y^{n-m}=-b_{m,j}\); the finite roots persist by simple-root continuity. The phase-grid argument counts the escaping real roots, including its greatest-common-divisor resonance cases. Gaussian root-intensity integration yields \(\sqrt m\) real roots on each line in expectation, while the mean outer contribution equals \(n\). Uniform boundedness by \(n^2\) validates passage of expectation through the limit. Finally, exact real-root parity converts the mean into the stated integer existence bound. The critical test cases have been verified by exact rational Sturm counts, but those tests are corroborative only.

## Originality
**PASS under accessible-source comparison, with residual priority risk.** Lundberg's arXiv:2201.00788 supplies the same random model and proves the weaker bound \(\lceil n\sqrt m\rceil\) by preservation in bounded intervals; its Sections 3–4 do not claim or count the additional escaping roots in expectation. The 2018 Khavinson–Lee–Saez construction explicitly exploits root creation by higher-degree perturbations, so the elementary escape mechanism itself is prior art. Their lower bounds \(3n-2\) and \(m^2+m+n\), however, do not imply the arithmetic outer-count law or the \(n(1+\sqrt m)\) inequality. The 2015 counterexample and numerical-experiment literature uses distinct constructions. The 2023 iid-coefficient asymptotics does not treat this perfectly coupled analytic–coanalytic ensemble. Targeted published-record searches did not reveal the combined statement, but this is not a proof of historical novelty. Unseen equivalent phase-grid or harmonic-valence treatments are a genuine residual risk.

## Value
**PASS.** The improvement is of exact order \(n\) over the preceding Kostlan construction, not a normalization change. It extends the range of fixed coanalytic degrees covered by the preceding **Kostlan probabilistic bound** from \(m>9\) to all \(m\ge5\), with a concrete degree pair \((77,5)\). Other constructions already supply counterexamples at some smaller degrees, and no first-ever fixed-degree counterexample is claimed. The cosine-grid resonance law gives an additional precise distributional feature: a complete arithmetic criterion for deterministic versus symmetric two-point escaping-root counts. These are structural features of the harmonic valence problem, not merely an arbitrary small-parameter computation.

## Limitations and literature relationship
The claim is existential; no explicit coefficient vector or optimal maximal-valence classification is supplied. The new bounds may be dominated at particular degrees by unrelated constructions. The proof requires the precise Gaussian coupling and the small-positive-perturbation limit. Standard degree-raising root escape and the real Kostlan \(\sqrt m\) formula are acknowledged as existing ingredients. Closest sources: Lundberg (arXiv:2201.00788, full text Sections 2–4); Khavinson–Lee–Saez (doi:10.1186/s40627-018-0012-2, full text Sections 1–2.4); Lee–Lerario–Lundberg (arXiv:1308.6474); Hauenstein–Lerario–Lundberg–Mehta (2015); and the 2023 iid-coefficient work (arXiv:2308.10333).
