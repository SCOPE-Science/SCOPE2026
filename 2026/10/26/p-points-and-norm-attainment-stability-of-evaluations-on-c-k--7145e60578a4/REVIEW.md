## Correctness

PASS. The \(P\)-point implication constructs a common positive minorant of a norming sequence by intersecting the neighborhoods on which its members equal one. The converse uses closed complements of a witnessing neighborhood sequence, proves \(\|f\|_\infty\le\|f\|_*\le2\|f\|_\infty\), computes \(\|\delta_p\|_{(C(K),\|\cdot\|_*)^*}=1\), and shows every candidate with \(|f(p)|=1\) has a positive restriction penalty.

## Originality

PASS. The closest primary results are the \(\omega_1\) example, global renorming theorem, and sequential-accumulation construction in arXiv:2502.10165v1, especially Theorem 3.1, Proposition 5.1, and Example 3.4. Those results do not state the \(P\)-point equivalence or the closed-set restriction norm for non-\(P\)-points that need not be sequential accumulation points. Search results found related norm-attainment records but no exact \(P\)-point classification; search failure alone is not treated as proof of novelty.

## Value

PASS. The result gives a complete pointwise criterion for a natural stability question and a constructive counter-renorming whenever the criterion fails. It explains why the ordinal endpoint example is robust while extending the nonattainment construction beyond sequentially accessible points.

## Closest literature and limits

The theorem is limited to real \(C(K)\) with \(K\) compact Hausdorff and equivalent lattice norms. The 2025 primary paper's sequence construction and ordinal example were inspected directly. A search cannot rule out an unindexed earlier formulation of the same topological criterion.
