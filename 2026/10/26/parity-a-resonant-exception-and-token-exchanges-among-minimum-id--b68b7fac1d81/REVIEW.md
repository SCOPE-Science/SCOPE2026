**Originality: PASS, with residual risk.** Foundational and later papers already determine numerical identifying-code minima of cycles; those numbers are not claimed as new. The full publicly accessible Bertrand–Charon–Hudry–Lobstein paper was inspected, with comparisons to its cycle theorems. Its stated results do not entail the full count of minimum configurations and the one-token exchange graph. Indexed abstracts of Gravier–Moncel–Semri, Xu–Thulasiraman–Hu, and Junnila–Laihonen emphasize optimal cardinalities but their full texts were not completely available: they remain explicit risks. Searches for code counts, extremal cyclic-word equivalences, exchange connectivity and known finite databases found no decisive covering theorem. Absence of a search result is not a novelty certificate.

**Value: PASS.** A complete classification of optimal configurations in the canonical cyclic graph family is mathematically motivated independently of the scalar extremal number. Exact solution counts, the connected \(3n\)-edge graph for odd orders, rigid even orders, and the isolated five-configuration resonance at order fifteen distinguish several natural types of optimum-solution space that the cardinality result cannot determine. The orbit templates enable efficient enumeration and explicit optimum-preserving transformations.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Bertrand et al. (2004), Gravier et al. (2006), Xu et al. (2008), and Junnila–Laihonen (2012) treat identifying codes of cycles and their numerical minima. An earlier exhaustive characterization within inaccessible full texts remains possible. The present claim covers only even \(n\ge8\) and odd \(n\ge13\) with ordinary closed-neighborhood identifying codes and one-token exchanges. Independent external validation has not been performed.
