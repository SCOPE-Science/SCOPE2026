# Scientific review: parity-paired first-order spectra of random alternating forms

## Correctness — PASS
The mathematical claim is supported by the exact Gaussian-binomial times general-linear/symplectic count, an independent first-moment tightness bound, and an explicit Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé response strategy. The strategy preserves both linear dependence and the form predicate, and uses enough unused dimensions in the symplectic quotient; it gives stabilization at quantifier rank \(d\) once the half-rank is at least \(d\). The zero-radical and fixed-radical predicates are definable in the specified finite signature. The finite enumeration checks the finite counting identities, not the infinite theorem.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
The key published comparison is Fulman–Goldstein (arXiv:1211.0504v4, Section 5): they already give even/odd rank/nullity limit laws and convergence bounds. The present work does not claim those as new; it adds a precise first-order EF stabilization and the entire joint paired probability spectrum across both parities. Koponen (arXiv:2610.16163v1, especially introduction and finite-structure expansion discussion) gives convergence-law context but not the exact alternating-form spectrum. Demin–Zhukovskii (arXiv:2402.02567) addresses other random structures. Related algebraic/model-theoretic generalizations may be stronger and remain a priority risk. A query with no exact match is not proof of historical novelty.

## Value — PASS
The unrestricted probability of a first-order statement can fail to converge solely through a parity-dependent radical obstruction. The theorem describes not only the pointwise radical laws but the entire attainable *coupled* pair spectrum under ordinary first-order definability, with a finite algorithm for each sentence. This answers a natural logical convergence question for a canonical finite-field random-structure model, and separates logical observability from raw random-matrix rank statistics.

## Scope and verification limits
Fixed finite field, uniformly chosen alternating forms, standard first-order language with all scalar multiplications and exact-value predicates. No assertion for other form classes or logics with counting. The checker exhausts small prime fields and cannot establish the Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé or limit statements beyond the proof. No independent scientific validation has occurred. Historical equivalences in general theories of finite form spaces may not have been fully located.
