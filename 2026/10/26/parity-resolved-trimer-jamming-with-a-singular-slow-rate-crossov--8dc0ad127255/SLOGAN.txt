Alternating the deposition rates of three-site segments on an infinite chain admits an exact two-component vacant-interval closure. As one starting parity becomes arbitrarily slow but remains active, its delayed deposits fill every residual vacancy of the other parity: the eventual mean coverage approaches \(\tfrac34(1+e^{-2})\), whereas setting that rate exactly to zero gives only \(\tfrac34(1-e^{-2})\).
