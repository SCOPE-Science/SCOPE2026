# Partition-selective weak-dephasing clock on trapped complete bipartite quantum networks

## Finding

Let \(m,n\ge 2\) be integers and let \(K_{m,n}\) have vertex classes \(A=\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}\) and \(B=\{m,\ldots,m+n-1\}\). Vertex \(0\) is an irreversible trap. All cross-class hopping amplitudes are the same fixed real number \(J>0\); there are no within-class hoppings or site-energy terms. The one-excitation density matrix evolves according to

\[
\mathcal L_\varepsilon(X)=-i[H,X]-\gamma\{E_0,X\}+\varepsilon\sum_v r_v\left(E_vXE_v-\tfrac12\{E_v,X\}\right),
\]

where \(E_v=|v\rangle\langle v|\), \(\gamma>0\), and \(\varepsilon>0\). The dephasing weights equal \(\alpha>0\) on \(A'=A\setminus\{0\}\), \(\beta>0\) on \(B\), and an arbitrary \(\eta\ge0\) at the trap. These weights are fixed while \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).

Put \(k=m-1\), \(u_A=\sum_{a\in A'}|a\rangle\), and \(u_B=\sum_{b\in B}|b\rangle\). Define the orthogonal projections

\[
Q_A=\sum_{a\in A'}E_a-\frac{|u_A\rangle\langle u_A|}{k},\qquad
Q_B=\sum_{b\in B}E_b-\frac{|u_B\rangle\langle u_B|}{n}.
\]

The convention for \(k=1\) gives \(Q_A=0\). For every fixed initial density matrix \(\rho\ge0\) with \(\operatorname{tr}\rho=1\), define its integrated survival time by

\[
T_\varepsilon(\rho)=\int_0^\infty\operatorname{tr}\!\left(e^{t\mathcal L_\varepsilon}\rho\right)\,dt.
\]

For every \(\varepsilon>0\), complete eventual absorption occurs and \(T_\varepsilon(\rho)<\infty\). Its exact leading small-dephasing law is

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\varepsilon T_\varepsilon(\rho)
=\frac{k}{\alpha}\operatorname{tr}(Q_A\rho)
+\frac{n}{\beta}\operatorname{tr}(Q_B\rho).}
\]

Equivalently, the difference between \(T_\varepsilon(\rho)\) and the displayed coefficient divided by \(\varepsilon\) is bounded as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), uniformly over all density matrices at fixed model parameters.

For a particle localized at the trap, a different vertex of \(A\), or a vertex of \(B\), respectively, the three limiting coefficients are

\[
\boxed{0,\qquad\frac{m-2}{\alpha},\qquad\frac{n-1}{\beta}.}
\]

For a pure initial state \(|\psi\rangle\), the coefficient has the coherence-geometric form

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\frac1\alpha\sum_{\substack{a<a'\\a,a'\in A'}}|\psi_a-\psi_{a'}|^2
+\frac1\beta\sum_{\substack{b<b'\\b,b'\in B}}|\psi_b-\psi_{b'}|^2.}
\]

Consequently the lifetime stays bounded in the weak-dephasing limit **if and only if** the initial density matrix is supported on the three-dimensional span of the trap site and the two class-uniform vectors. No large-graph limit or nonzero background recombination is taken.

## Assumptions and scope

All parameters \(m,n,J,\gamma,\alpha,\beta,\eta\) are fixed while \(\varepsilon\) tends to zero. The factors are normalized so that, when \(\alpha=\beta=\eta=1\), every off-diagonal density-matrix element has direct dephasing rate exactly \(\varepsilon\). The sink term \(-\gamma\{E_0,\cdot\}\) gives population-loss rate \(2\gamma\) at vertex \(0\). The geometry is the complete bipartite **adjacency** Hamiltonian, not a graph Laplacian.

The claim applies to arbitrary fixed mixed or coherent initial states, including states with amplitude on both classes. The \(O(1)\) bound is not uniform as \(m,n\to\infty\), \(\gamma\to0\), or either nontrap dephasing weight tends to zero.

## Proof

**1. Bright and dark decomposition.** Both \(Q_A\) and \(Q_B\) project onto vectors of zero sum within a class. The complete bipartite adjacency matrix annihilates them: \(HQ_A=HQ_B=0\), and the trap projector annihilates them as well. Set \(P=Q_A+Q_B\). The orthogonal complement of \(\operatorname{ran}P\) is the three-dimensional space generated by \(|0\rangle\), \(u_A/\sqrt{k}\), and \(u_B/\sqrt n\). In this ordered orthonormal basis the Hamiltonian is

\[
H_{\mathrm{br}}=J\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&\sqrt n\\
0&0&\sqrt{kn}\\
\sqrt n&\sqrt{kn}&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Write \(K=-iH-\gamma E_0\). Every eigenvalue of its bright block has strictly negative real part. Indeed, if an eigenvector had zero real decay, its amplitude at \(0\) would vanish by taking the real part of \(\langle z,Kz\rangle\). The first row of its eigenvalue equation would then force its \(B\)-uniform coefficient to vanish, and the third row would force its \(A'\)-uniform coefficient to vanish. The supposed eigenvector is zero. Since the dark block of \(K\) is exactly zero and there are no cross blocks, the unperturbed density-matrix generator \(\mathcal L_0(X)=KX+XK^\dagger\) has a semisimple zero eigenspace exactly \(\{X=PXP\}\). All complementary eigenvalues lie strictly in the open left half-plane.

**2. Strict decay for every positive dephasing strength.** In the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product,

\[
\operatorname{Re}\langle X,\mathcal L_\varepsilon X\rangle
=-\gamma\big(\|E_0X\|_2^2+\|XE_0\|_2^2\big)
-\frac\varepsilon2\sum_{i\ne j}(r_i+r_j)|X_{ij}|^2.
\]

An eigenoperator with zero real decay must have zero trap row and column and be diagonal, since all nontrap rates are positive. Its commutator with \(H\) must also vanish. Connectivity forces every diagonal entry to be the same, and the trap entry is zero, so it vanishes. Thus every eigenvalue of \(\mathcal L_\varepsilon\) has strictly negative real part. In finite dimension this gives exponential decay and convergence of the survival integral.

**3. Effective slow generator.** Let \(\Pi(X)=PXP\). Write \(\mathcal L_\varepsilon=\mathcal L_0+\varepsilon\mathcal D\) and \(M=\Pi\mathcal D\Pi\) restricted to \(\{X=PXP\}\). The dephasing map is Hilbert-Schmidt self-adjoint. On the dark operator space it is strictly negative definite: equality in the displayed dissipativity identity would require an operator diagonal in the site basis; no nonzero diagonal matrix satisfies \(X=PXP\), because such a matrix must annihilate the uniform vector of each class.

Its action on the class projections is especially simple. Each diagonal element of \(Q_A\) on \(A'\) equals \(1-1/k\), and each on \(B\) equals \(1-1/n\). Therefore

\[
M(Q_A)=-\frac\alpha k Q_A,\qquad M(Q_B)=-\frac\beta n Q_B.
\]

The first identity is vacuous when \(k=1\). Cross-class dark coherences do not contribute to the trace of either projection, regardless of their initial values.

**4. Resolvent singularity and trace.** By the semisimple zero/complementary stable decomposition in step 1 and invertibility of \(M\), the finite-dimensional Schur-complement identity gives, in operator norm,

\[
-\mathcal L_\varepsilon^{-1}=
\frac1\varepsilon(-M)^{-1}\Pi+O(1)
\qquad(\varepsilon\downarrow0).
\]

Because \(\int_0^\infty e^{t\mathcal L_\varepsilon}\,dt=-\mathcal L_\varepsilon^{-1}\), and \(M\) is self-adjoint, the trace functional on its slow domain is represented by

\[
(-M)^{-1}P=\frac{k}{\alpha}Q_A+\frac{n}{\beta}Q_B.
\]

Taking the trace against \(\rho\) proves the central formula. Direct evaluation of the diagonal entries of the two projections proves the localized-state coefficients. The identity \(l\sum_i|z_i|^2-|\sum_i z_i|^2=\sum_{i<j}|z_i-z_j|^2\) proves the pure-state expression. Positivity of the two projection terms shows that a zero coefficient is equivalent to support entirely in \(\operatorname{ker}P\), proving the bounded-versus-divergent dichotomy.

## Verification

Run `python verify_bipartite.py` with NumPy installed. The program reconstructs the complete Lindblad superoperator independently in column-major vectorization, solves the integrated survival equation numerically, and compares the resulting weak-noise coefficients to the theorem. It also performs exact rational projector checks and numerical tests of strict slow-space dissipation. With NumPy 2.0-compatible operations, the supplied run recorded 648 or more exact rational assertions, 80 projected/spectral checks and 160 finite-parameter small-dephasing comparisons; the largest absolute discrepancy between \(\varepsilon T_\varepsilon\) at \(\varepsilon=0.001\) and the predicted limit was approximately \(0.042418482\). Such differences are expected at finite \(\varepsilon\); neither these observations nor finite enumeration establishes the limit. Steps 1–4 above constitute the all-size proof.

## Relationship to prior work

The Haken–Strobl framework and the relevance of decoherence to quantum-network trapping long predate this theorem. Gaab and Bardeen (2004) studied connectivity, coherence and trapping in finite light-harvesting network models; their accessible abstract addresses topology and trapping times qualitatively, but does not establish or exclude the precise claim here. Kassal and Aspuru-Guzik (2012) analyzed environment-assisted transport on ordered chains with an additional competing recombination channel and trapping efficiency as the primary observable. Marais and coauthors (2014) studied fully connected networks with spin-bath environments and transition probabilities, not the present partition-weighted Lindblad lifetime. Behzadi and coauthors (2014) examined selective dephasing through constructed multi-sink networks, not a two-orbit complete-bipartite lifetime law. Razzoli, Paris and Bordone (2020/2021) analyzed trap-visible spectral subspaces without decoherence; the zero-noise dark-subspace concept alone does not imply the present coefficient of a singular mean lifetime.

The quantified contribution is the exact \(\varepsilon^{-1}\) coefficient for **all** complete bipartite sizes, **all** initial density matrices, and two independently selectable nontrap dephasing weights, including its pairwise-coherence characterization. This is not a claim of inventing the dark-subspace method, the Haken–Strobl model, or noise-assisted transport. Earlier work may have a more general implicit theorem; historical priority has not been independently certified.

## Limitations

The statement concerns fixed finite complete bipartite networks, identical cross-edge hopping, site-basis Markovian dephasing, one trap, and an infinite-time integrated observable. It does not give a time-resolved absorption distribution, a uniform large-size/noise scaling, a theorem for arbitrary multipartite or disordered networks, or an exact formula for finite positive \(\varepsilon\). An especially relevant historical trapping paper by Gaab and Bardeen could only be checked through its abstract; substantive full-text overlap remains an originality risk.
