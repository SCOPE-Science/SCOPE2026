The step response is differentiated explicitly. The pulse response is obtained by superposition. The logarithmic derivative-ratio function has one turning point after cessation and one zero, so the pulse response has exactly one maximum. The bounds follow from the unique mode of the response derivative, and implicit differentiation proves the duration sensitivity lies strictly between zero and one.

The closest literature includes Glover and Balmer's classical step response, Jenkins's treatment of intermittent pumping and residual effects, and Zlotnik's maximum-rate analysis for continuously pumped leaky aquifers. The inspected statements do not imply the exact unique finite-pulse peak-time bracket and comparative-statics formula. The full text of Jenkins (1968) and Hunt's 2014 review was not available through the consulted route, so coverage is not ruled out absolutely.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The time of maximum stream depletion is a natural operational quantity: the stream impact can peak after pumping ceases. The result turns this qualitative management warning into a strict, parameter-explicit time bracket and a comparative-statics bound, supporting monitoring-window selection under the classical model.

## Limitations

The result does not extend without additional proof to leaky or heterogeneous aquifers, partial stream penetration, finite stream width, changing stage, or multi-pulse schedules. The peak is characterized implicitly, not in elementary closed form.
