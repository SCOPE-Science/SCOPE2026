# Review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The proof uses the path-length grading of the completed path algebra. A degree-\(L\) cyclic-derivative term must come from a cycle of length \(L+1\), hence requires \(n\mid L+1\); when \(L+1=nr\), the coefficient of each length-\(L\) path is a scalar multiple of \(r\). Conversely, \(c^r\) generates exactly \(\mathfrak m^L\) when \(r1_K\ne0\). The hyperpotential relations \(p_i\) satisfy the commutator identity and generate the same ideal whenever \(n\mid L+1\).

## Originality

**PASS, with residual risk.** The closest primary source gives the single case \(n=3,L=5,\operatorname{char}K=2\), whereas this statement gives a complete criterion for all \(n\ge3\), \(L\ge2\), and fields. Searches of the published-result index using the exact divisibility condition, aliases involving truncated cyclic Nakayama algebras, and potential-versus-hyperpotential formulations returned no directly matching statement. This is not treated as proof that no unindexed prior result exists.

## Value

**PASS.** The criterion isolates a structural distinction important in positive-characteristic quiver representation theory: the endpoint/length condition determines when a hyperpotential exists, while a separate field-characteristic condition determines whether the same ideal can be generated by an ordinary potential. It generalizes a motivating characteristic-two example to a natural complete family.

## Closest literature and limitations

The most relevant source is Geiß–Hartung–Labardini-Fragoso–Schröer, arXiv:2610.18805v1, Examples 6.2–6.3 and Theorem 6.6. Trepode–Valdivieso-Diaz, arXiv:1207.1917, treats finite-dimensional Jacobian algebras for other cyclically oriented quivers and primitive potentials. Chang–Tang, arXiv:2609.11200v1, studies Jacobi-finiteness obstructions on different quiver families. The claim is about the canonical ideal on a fixed cycle; abstract presentations on other quivers are outside scope.
