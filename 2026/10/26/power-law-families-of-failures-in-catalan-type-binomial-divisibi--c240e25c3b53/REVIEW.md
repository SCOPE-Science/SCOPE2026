# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The conditions \(p\mid a\), \(p\nmid b\) ensure the multiplicative order \(s\) exists and \(w=(p^s-1)/(a+b)\) is integral. A residue computation gives \(p\mid bw+1\). The \(s\)-digit numerator block is all \(p-1\), so its Lucas residue is a unit. Independent block concatenation preserves digitwise Lucas products and divisibility of the denominator by \(p\); injectivity and the sharp finite count follow. The Hausdorff dimension is supplied by both a disjoint-cover upper bound and a Bernoulli-mass lower bound. Reproducibility checks are included.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The qualitative infinite-existence result and special seed originate in Guo–Krattenthaler (2013/2014). This finding claims neither as new. The inspected full source does not give a uniform power-law count or an explicit positive-dimensional \(p\)-adic witness family. The direct binary-block quantification is not a logical corollary of infinitude alone. Broad digital concatenation methods may imply analogous results, so unlocated equivalent coverage is a residual risk. Published-archive and relevant-research-index searches did not identify these two statements.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The open-ended qualitative divisibility question is strengthened uniformly across all parameter pairs having a missing prime factor: the number of failures is bounded below by an explicit positive power. The theorem constructs self-similar, nontrivial witness sets and identifies their exact Hausdorff dimension. It does not just extend an isolated table or add a single witness. It leaves sharpness and true failure-set density open.

## Closest literature and limitations
Guo–Krattenthaler, arXiv:1301.7651v1, Theorem 2.1 and Section 4: explicit single seed and infinitely many nondivisibility witnesses, not the binary-block census. Sun (2012) formulated the motivating divisibility problem. The search also considered contemporary treatments of related binomial congruences, which did not address the stated quantitative measure. Generality does not imply optimality; no independent external validation is available.
