# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The height-two parity representation gives a unique path from each subset of even-zero heights. The prefix-normal inequality is shown to be an additive closure condition by considering odd factor lengths, even factor lengths with endpoint height two, and even factor lengths with endpoint height zero at both parities of starting index. Truncated addition closure is equivalent to the numerical-semigroup axiom after adjoining the tail at semilength. Explicit inverse maps prove the bijection; identified gaps and returns prove area, genus, multiplicity and conductor formulas. Pairing complementary integers below the Frobenius gap proves the conductor bound and the two-generator semigroup construction proves its sharpness. A standalone direct-factor checker covers all 16,383 height-two paths to semilength 14, including an independently enumerated semigroup count. The infinite claims are proved symbolically, not extrapolated.

## Originality — PASS (residual risk acknowledged)

The foundational prefix-normal inequality in Fici–Lipták, arXiv:1805.12405, is a **stronger general statement about words**, but does not explicitly encode numerical semigroups. Ferrari–Giannini–Pinzani, DOI:10.2478/puma-2022-0029, discuss numerical-semigroup injections into genus-length Dyck paths, not height-two prefix-normal paths indexed by a conductor cutoff. The known OEIS A326083 sum-closed-subset counts already imply the unrefined totals once the bridge is given and are not claimed new. Statement-level searches for aliases, numerical-semigroup/Dyck equivalences, and stronger published coverage did not find the full restriction + bijection + statistic transport. A concise equivalent consequence of the general prefix-normal inequality might exist elsewhere; novelty is best-knowledge, not absolute uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact bijection identifies the language-theoretic prefix-normal condition with a natural additive closure axiom in a canonical Catalan subfamily. It transfers several **structural** semigroup invariants simultaneously—genus/area, conductor/last elevated height, and multiplicity/first return—and yields an all-genus exact conductor cutoff with sharp stabilization at semilength \(2g\). These are motivated classifications with mathematical use for either subject, not a numerical table alone. No unsupported downstream consequence or new closed form is asserted.
