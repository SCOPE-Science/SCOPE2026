# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For every integer \(N\ge2\), the real planar system with rotation angle \(\pi/(2N)\), one active unit coordinate sensor at each of \(N\) times, equal independent Gaussian noise and no process noise has information eigenvalues exactly \((N\pm|P_s(e^{i\pi/N})|)/(2\sigma^2)\). The proof uses the Gramian's exact doubled-angle representation. The telescoping switch identity transforms any isotropic schedule with \(k\) switches into a zero sum of \(k\) or \(k+1\) distinct non-antipodal \(2N\)-th roots; the cited Lam–Leung theorem gives \(k\ge p-1\), no solution for a power of two, and regular-polygon rigidity at equality. Alternating blocks of length \(N/p\) attain the bound. For \(N=2^a p^b\), the exact minimal polynomial \(\Phi_{2N}\) yields disjoint coefficient blocks, proving both completeness and the count \(2^{N/p}\). All information-optimality claims follow from fixed Gramian trace and strict eigenvalue balancing. The verifier independently checks cyclotomic identities by exact integer arithmetic for all binary schedules through \(N=16\), with additional finite construction and floating-point checks; these tests do not substitute for the general proof.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, residual risk)

The sensor-scheduling source of Dutta–Wilde–Smith (arXiv:2304.02692) and the periodic-scheduling treatment of Liu–Fardad–Masazade–Varshney (arXiv:1305.5601) were read in available full text. Both optimize or characterize broader sensing objectives but neither was found to assert the quarter-turn binary cyclotomic equivalence, exact least-switch theorem, full prime-power sensor-schedule census, or simultaneous information optimality of those same schedules. The abstract minimal vanishing-sum theorem is **known**, explicitly attributed to Lam–Leung (arXiv:math/9511209, Theorems 3.3 and 4.8), and is not claimed as a new lemma. Queries to mathematical research records for time-indexed coordinate sensors, root-of-unity tight observation, switch-count bounds, cyclotomic binary schedules, and optimal finite-horizon rotating sensors returned distinct results in spectral graph demixing, symbolic observation or nonmatching observation-horizon optimization. Accessible prior local claims concerned planar oscillator *controllability* sampling resonances or random cyclic actuator controllability, not this sensing statement. Residual risk: an earlier paper on rotational sensing, signed cyclotomic words, or tight binary measurement designs could independently contain an equivalent specialization; no universal uniqueness or independent priority certification is asserted.

## Value — PASS

One-sensor-per-time scheduling has a concrete setup/switching cost and several competing information objectives. The theorem resolves when the absolute information-theoretic optimum is attainable and finds its exact minimum switching cost, as determined by the least odd prime divisor of the observation horizon. It proves rigidity of every minimum-switch solution and an explicit exponential count of all optimal patterns in the prime-power family. The surprising obstruction for power-of-two horizons and the arithmetic phase transition are directly actionable for this carefully delimited sensor model, not mere recomputation of a previously tabulated invariant.
