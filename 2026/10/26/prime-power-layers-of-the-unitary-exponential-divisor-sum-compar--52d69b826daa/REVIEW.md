# Supported mathematical review

**Correctness (PASS).** The exact multiplicative identities yield a strict comparison threshold \(q\Delta_a(p)\le p^a\). Its leading cutoff is \(p^{a-d(a)}\). The two prime ranges cut at \(X^{1/(a+b)}\) give contributions \(1/[b(b+1)]\) and \(1/[b(a-1)]\) in the rescaled logarithmic units. The error from replacing the exact cutoff, the diagonal \(p=q\), and both tails is lower order. Exact finite tests match the cutoff for five exponents and three search bounds. The proof needs only the classical prime number theorem; finite tests do not prove asymptotics.

**Originality (PASS with residual risk).** Trudgian discusses the ambient density and equality question, not fixed-exponent asymptotic strata. A known finite equality classification concerns a different logical condition; an ambient-density lower bound cannot imply an asymptotic for the specified zero-density family. Decker–Moree counts differently constrained two-prime products. Searches of prime-product, divisor-comparison, and published research records did not find the same exponent-dependent constant. A general lattice-point prime theorem may subsume the analytic counting component, and unlocated treatments may cover the combined arithmetic predicate.

**Value (PASS).** The problem separates the first non-squarefree two-prime strata of the comparison asked in the motivating work. For **all** fixed higher prime-power exponents, it identifies an exact threshold and a nontrivial exponent-dependent asymptotic; the least prime divisor of the exponent changes the power law. The relative rarity contrasts with the positive ambient density and gives a quantitative rather than merely qualitative description. It does not settle the ambient-density question.

**Closest literature and limitations.** The complete motivating arXiv HTML text and two highly relevant published equality/density findings were inspected. The prime-pair counting method is standard. No independent audit was performed; equivalent coverage outside the inspected material remains possible.
