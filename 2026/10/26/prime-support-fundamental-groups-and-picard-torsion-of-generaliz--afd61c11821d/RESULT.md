# Prime-support fundamental groups and Picard torsion of generalized Fermat quotients

## Finding
Let \(k\ge 4\) be composite and let \(t\ge 1\) be an integer with \(d=t(k-1)-1\ge2\). Let \(M\subset\mathbb P^{kt-1}_{\mathbb C}\) be a smooth Calabi–Yau generalized Fermat complete intersection of \(t\) degree-\(k\) hypersurfaces, in the sense of Billi–Palomino–Sarti (arXiv:2610.21752v1, §§3–4). Let \(\varphi\) be their distinguished order-\(k\), fixed-point-free diagonal automorphism, whose eigenvalues \(1,\zeta,\ldots,\zeta^{k-1}\) each occur \(t\) times in the ambient coordinates. Write \(G=\langle\varphi\rangle\) and \(Y=M/G\), the **underlying coarse complex algebraic variety**, not the quotient stack or its smooth locus.

If \(k=p^e\) is a proper prime power with \(e\ge2\), then
\[
\pi_1(Y^{\mathrm{an}})\cong \mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z,\qquad
\operatorname{Pic}(Y)_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong \mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z,\qquad
H^2(Y^{\mathrm{an}},\mathbb Z)_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z.
\]
If \(k\) has at least two distinct prime divisors, **all three groups vanish**. Independently of the prime factorization, the torsion Weil divisor class group is \(\operatorname{Cl}(Y)_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\mathbb Z/k\mathbb Z\). Thus the torsion Cartier-to-Weil gap \(\operatorname{Cl}(Y)_{\mathrm{tors}}/\operatorname{Pic}(Y)_{\mathrm{tors}}\) is cyclic of order \(k/p\) for prime powers \(k=p^e\), and of order \(k\) if \(k\) has at least two prime divisors. For every divisor \(s\) of \(k\) with \(1<s\le k\), the intermediate quotient \(M/\langle\varphi^s\rangle\) is simply connected and has torsion-free Picard group and torsion-free integral second cohomology.

In particular, whenever \(k\) is even with at least two distinct prime divisors and \(t\) is odd, \(Y\) is a simply connected log-Enriques variety with no torsion line bundles but with a non-Cartier canonical rank-one reflexive sheaf of order two. This includes \(k=6,t=1\), the quotient of the Fermat sextic fourfold by the diagonal order-six automorphism.

## Assumptions and scope
Only composite cyclic orders \(k\ge4\) are assessed in this finding; prime orders and the known order-two free quotient are boundary comparisons. The complete intersections and actions are exactly the smooth, general-position Calabi–Yau generalized Fermat pairs studied in arXiv:2610.21752v1. The dimension restriction \(d\ge2\) is essential for simple connectedness of \(M\). Quotients are coarse projective normal varieties with their classical complex topology. \(\operatorname{Pic}\) means isomorphism classes of invertible algebraic sheaves; it does **not** mean the Weil divisor class group or the reflexive Picard class group. The last canonical-sheaf assertion uses the index-two statement in the originating paper, not a new computation of canonical characters.

## Proof

**1. Fixed-point exponents.** In the cited family, \(M\) is simply connected by the Lefschetz theorem for smooth complete intersections in complex projective space, since \(d\ge2\). For an integer \(a\), \(0<a<k\), write \(s=\gcd(a,k)\). The eigenspaces of \(\varphi^a\) group the ambient coordinates into \(k/s\) blocks of size \(st\). The general-position complete-intersection equations have no solutions inside each eigenblock if \(s=1\), while for \(s>1\) each fixed component is nonempty, of dimension \(t(s-1)-1\). These are precisely the fixed-locus statements of Billi–Palomino–Sarti, Proposition 4.3 and Remark 4.4. Consequently,
\[
\operatorname{Fix}_M(\varphi^a)\ne\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \gcd(a,k)>1\qquad(0<a<k).
\]

**2. The topological fundamental group.** Armstrong's orbit-space theorem states that if a finite group \(H\) acts on a simply connected, locally compact manifold, then \(\pi_1(M/H)\cong H/N_H\), where \(N_H\) is the normal subgroup generated by elements possessing fixed points. Its hypotheses hold here; in particular finite group actions on compact complex manifolds are discontinuous. For \(H=G\), \(N_G\) is generated by \(\varphi^s\) for proper divisors \(s\) of \(k\) with \(s>1\). For the composite \(k=p^e\) with \(e\ge2\), they generate \(\langle\varphi^p\rangle\), giving \(G/N_G\cong C_p\). When \(k\) has at least two distinct prime factors \(p,q\), both \(\varphi^p\) and \(\varphi^q\) fix points, and these generate all of \(G\), because \(\gcd(p,q)=1\); the quotient group is trivial.

For an intermediate group \(H=\langle\varphi^s\rangle\) with \(1<s<k\), its generator itself fixes points, so \(N_H=H\) and \(\pi_1(M/H)=1\). For \(s=k\) the quotient equals the simply connected \(M\). This establishes every fundamental-group assertion.

**3. Torsion in the Picard group.** The simply connected smooth complete intersection \(M\) has \(H^1(M,\mathcal O_M)=0\) and \(H^2(M,\mathbb Z)_{\mathrm{tors}}=0\). The latter follows from the universal coefficient theorem and \(H_1(M,\mathbb Z)=0\); the former is a standard complete-intersection vanishing. The exponential sequence then gives \(\operatorname{Pic}(M)_{\mathrm{tors}}=0\).

Let \(\pi\colon M\to M/H\) be any of the finite quotients considered above. If \(L\in\operatorname{Pic}(M/H)\) is torsion, its pullback is trivial on \(M\). A trivialization transports the canonical \(H\)-linearization to an action on the trivial line bundle by constants, because \(\mathcal O_M(M)^*=\mathbb C^*\); therefore it is a character \(\chi\in\operatorname{Hom}(H,\mathbb C^*)\). Since the linearized bundle descends from \(M/H\), the stabilizer of each point must act trivially on its fiber. Thus \(\chi\) is trivial on \(N_H\).

Conversely, a character of \(H/N_H\) defines a linearization of \(\mathcal O_M\) that is trivial on every geometric stabilizer. The finite-group line-bundle descent criterion then provides a unique line bundle \(L_\chi\) on the coarse quotient. It is torsion because its pullback character has finite order. These constructions are inverse (the descent of the trivial character is the trivial line bundle), so
\[
\operatorname{Pic}(M/H)_{\mathrm{tors}}
\cong\operatorname{Hom}(H/N_H,\mathbb C^*).
\]
Substitution of part 2 proves the Picard assertions, including for singular quotients. Neither étaleness nor smoothness of \(M/H\) is assumed.

**3a. Weil versus Cartier torsion.** The action of \(G\) is quasi-étale (Billi–Palomino–Sarti, Proposition 4.7), so the exact class-group sequence for a finite cyclic quasi-étale quotient (Boissière–Camere–Sarti, Proposition 3.2) applies:
\[
0\longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}(G,\mathbb C^*)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Cl}(Y)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(M)^G\longrightarrow0.
\]
As \(M\) is smooth, \(\operatorname{Cl}(M)=\operatorname{Pic}(M)\), which is torsion-free by part 3. Therefore the torsion subgroup of \(\operatorname{Cl}(Y)\) is precisely the character subgroup \(\operatorname{Hom}(G,\mathbb C^*)\cong C_k\). The natural injection \(\operatorname{Pic}(Y)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(Y)\) identifies torsion Cartier classes with the subgroup of characters trivial on \(N_G\) from part 3. Since a cyclic group has a unique subgroup of each order, the quotient of torsion Weil classes by torsion Cartier classes has order \(k/p\) for a composite prime power and \(k\) for an integer with at least two prime factors. This comparison also explains why a reflexive canonical torsion class can exist without a torsion line bundle.

**4. Integral degree-two torsion.** A projective complex algebraic variety admits a finite CW homotopy type. The universal coefficient theorem gives \(H^2(M/H,\mathbb Z)_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\operatorname{Ext}^1(H_1(M/H,\mathbb Z),\mathbb Z)\). Each group in part 2 is finite cyclic (or trivial), hence its abelianization is itself and its Ext group has the same cyclic order. This proves the integral-cohomology statement.

**5. The canonical non-Cartier boundary.** If \(k\) is even and \(t\) odd, then \(d\) is even. Billi–Palomino–Sarti, Proposition 4.8, proves the quotient has nontrivial order-two reflexive canonical sheaf (log-Enriques index two). If \(k\) has an odd prime divisor, part 3 gives \(\operatorname{Pic}(Y)_{\mathrm{tors}}=0\). Were the canonical sheaf invertible, it would be a nontrivial order-two line bundle, contradiction. Therefore it is not Cartier, although its second reflexive power is trivial. The assertion applies to every such \(k,t\), including the sextic fourfold case.

## Verification
The supplied `verify.py` exhaustively checks the relevant subgroup arithmetic over a range of orders: it forms each stabilizer-generated subgroup from the condition \(\gcd(a,k)>1\), computes its index, and checks all intermediate subgroups and the prime-power dichotomy. This is an arithmetic consistency check, not a replacement for Armstrong's theorem, the geometric fixed-locus theorem, or line-bundle descent.

## Relationship to prior work
Billi–Palomino–Sarti (2026) establish the family, the simply connected covers, the precise fixed loci, the prime-order free quotients, and the log-Enriques canonical-index statement. The order-two free quotient has separately known integral Picard and cohomological invariants; it is excluded from the assessed composite-order claim. In Example 2.14 they explicitly note \(\pi_1\cong C_5\) for a free quintic quotient; they do not give the full prime-support classification or the torsion Picard classification across singular composite-order quotients. Armstrong (1965, 1968) supplies the general fundamental-group formula for a group orbit space. The finite-group descent criterion is classical. Boissière–Camere–Sarti (2024), §5, discuss how singular quotient fundamental groups can differ from covering-group orders, without specializing to this complete family or computing its torsion Picard groups. The asserted contribution is the combined all-parameter classification of coarse topology and Cartier-versus-Weil torsion (using the known class-group exact sequence) and the distinction between Cartier and non-Cartier reflexive canonical torsion, not a new orbit-space theorem or a new fixed-locus computation.

## Limitations
The conclusion concerns coarse quotient spaces, not their smooth loci or orbifold fundamental groups; in particular the latter can retain the full cyclic covering group. The argument does not compute the free part of any Picard or cohomology group, the entire Weil divisor class group, the stringy Hodge numbers, or the geometry of resolutions. It relies on published fixed-locus and canonical-index results and on classical topology and descent. An implicit previous statement of the prime-support and torsion-Picard formulas may exist in broader quotient literature; priority has not been independently certified.
