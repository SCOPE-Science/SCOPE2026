# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The complete residue-minimum formula follows from a proved greedy coin lemma and strict monotonicity of the minimum representation as the coin amount increases. The pseudo-Frobenius criterion is equivalent to three explicit generator-membership tests. The seven boundary residues are exhausted by a deterministic table for the two claimed congruence classes; differences of their Apéry values prove the stated sets and types. A separate shortest-path computation confirms both the Apéry sets and exact pseudo-Frobenius elements for all exponents from five to thirteen. Experiments do not establish the infinite claim; the written integer inequalities and residue classification do.

## Originality — PASS within available evidence
The Apéry and Frobenius representations are not new: Komatsu's arXiv:2209.13118 describes four-variable Apéry geometry, and Liu–Xin–Ye–Yin's arXiv:2306.03459 gives general greedy formulas. The latter's Theorem 3.4 gives the pseudo-Frobenius classification only when the initial generator is a multiple of seven (the excluded residue class here). The full Mersenne semigroup is larger and has different type. No equivalent classification for the remaining two congruence classes appeared in the targeted literature or mathematical-record searches. The new part is specifically the exact two-branch pseudo-Frobenius set and type transition. Prior implicit derivations and unindexed sources remain risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Pseudo-Frobenius elements and Cohen–Macaulay type are natural invariants of semigroup rings. This theorem determines all such elements for two infinite arithmetic branches of a familiar four-generator numerical-semigroup family and reveals an exact change from three consecutive pseudo-Frobenius gaps to a separated pair. These are genuinely different structural patterns rather than new labels for already tabulated Frobenius numbers. The arithmetic restrictions and dependence on exactly four generators matter.

## Scope and limits
The claim excludes the already-covered exponent branch divisible by three; does not claim new Apéry or genus results; and does not classify other truncated geometric families. Independent validation and publication-priority checks have not been performed.
