# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The claimed object is the middle squared-frequency branch of a precisely stated positive, conservative two-resonator mass-in-mass chain. Schur complement and positivity of its derivative give strict interlacing and monotonicity. Unit-eigenvector normalization gives the core-motion slope identity. Convex reciprocal-square minimization gives the explicit all-detuning bound. The equal-frequency dark eigenvalue is uniformly isolated on a compact Brillouin zone, justifying the uniform Taylor remainder; expansion to second order gives the claimed exact coefficient. `CHECK.py` provides 20 numerical comparisons without substituting finite computations for the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with priority risk.** The archived granular-chain model (Bonanomi–Theocharis–Daraio 2014) establishes physical locally resonant wave propagation. The closest direct forward source (Stein–Nouh–Singh 2021, §2.1.1–2.1.3) already identifies multi-resonator flat dark bands and their narrow dispersive inter-gap counterparts; neither fact is new here. The inspected sections do not state the positive universal second-order bandwidth coefficient for unequal local masses, the exact host-fraction/slope identity in this perturbative context, or the global finite-detuning flattening envelope. Relevant indexed magnetic and quantum-graph band claims are on different operators. A specialized earlier perturbation derivation could still overlap; the earlier multiscale-resonator paper of Jones–Movchan–Gei (2011) was available only in abstract at the checked repository.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** An exact bandwidth opening law and mode-energy leakage rate quantify the stability of an experimentally relevant flat acoustic resonance; the coefficient is explicit across a complete natural positive-mass parameter family. The finite-detuning slope bound is a complementary physically interpretable guarantee. Experimental finite-chain attenuation, fabrication tolerance and dissipation are not proved.
