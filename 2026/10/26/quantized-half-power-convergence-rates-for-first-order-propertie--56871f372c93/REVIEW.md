# Scientific evaluation

## Correctness
**PASS.** In the fixed-prime, uniformly unlabelled model, Baur–Monk pp-invariant elimination reduces each sentence to finitely many additive cyclic-summand index tests. Smith normal forms make their supports effectively finite or cofinite. Cofinite-support failures are superpolynomially negligible because any arbitrarily large fixed set of marked part sizes would have to have bounded multiplicity. Exact partition generating functions and the first Rademacher term yield a uniform first nonzero finite-difference contribution proportional to \(n^{-r/2}\). A finite capped multiplicity decision table gives the positive integer coefficient and prevents cancellation, since it counts disjoint truth-error events. The realization formula using the pp quotient \((p^{j-1}G)[p]/(p^jG)[p]\) establishes sharpness for every exponent. Code checks finite identities and is not misrepresented as proof of the infinite theorem.

## Originality
**PASS with historical-priority risk.** The underlying zero-one law, finite-module first-order elimination and partition expansions are classical, and are expressly excluded from the claimed advance. Bell–Burris's 2003 article was read beyond its abstract: it develops ratio-class sufficient criteria for logical limits, but does not provide a universal integer-quantized rate classification for these groups. The accessible Burris–Compton–Odlyzko–Richmond 1997 abstract similarly establishes zero-one criteria, not the stated sharp hierarchy. Marström–Räty (2026) and Koponen (2026) investigate logically different random families. Direct mathematical comparison with published-record searches did not find the exact joint rate/coefficient/effectivity theorem. The 2001 Burris monograph and full 1997 paper were not examined in full, so an older theorem implying this one remains a residual originality risk; failed searching alone is not used as novelty proof.

## Value
**PASS.** A classical all-sentence zero-one law tells which eventual truth value is obtained but does not quantify the speed of approach. Here the possible leading exponents and their lattice of constants are sharply classified and algorithmically extractable from a formula. Every exponent is actually realized by simple first-order conditions on small cyclic summands. This is a natural refinement of logical asymptotic theory for the canonical integer-partition ensemble of finite abelian groups, not an arbitrary finite-table statistic.

## Scientific limitations
Fixed prime, isomorphism-class rather than labelled sampling, and no complexity bound for the elimination procedure. The infinite argument uses classical pp-elimination and partition asymptotics; the finite program only checks sample cases. Historical-priority risk remains, especially among comprehensive treatments of arithmetic partition systems.
