# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The exact determinant is reduced to \(\beta\gamma(\gamma^2-\beta^2)=\alpha^2\xi^4\). At a simple root \(S'(k_0)>0\), the implicit-function expansion gives \(\beta=(\xi^4/\gamma_0^3)\alpha^2+O(\alpha^6)\). Substitution into \(\beta^2=\xi^2-S(k)\) gives the stated quartic phase-speed detachment with the displayed coefficient and sign.

## Originality

PASS, with a stated literature-access risk. The 2015 primary paper proves the existence threshold and gives the exact determinant but does not state the fixed-wavenumber \(\alpha\to0\) detachment law. The 2019 comparison abstract reports qualitatively slow decay but no such coefficient. Semantic literature searches returned no direct equivalent; that absence is not treated as proof of novelty. The 2019 full text was inaccessible, so coverage by a detailed result in that text cannot be excluded absolutely.

## Value

PASS. The coupling-zero limit is where the surface mode ceases to exist. The distinct quadratic decay-exponent and quartic phase-speed scales quantify how the localized mode merges into the bulk-shear branch, a natural parameter-threshold observable for surface-wave propagation and microstructure identification.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest primary source is Gourgiotis–Georgiadis (2015), which gives the exact Toupin–Mindlin surface-wave determinant and numerical dispersion curves. Eremeyev–Rosi–Naili (2019) compare decay in strain-gradient and surface-stress models; only their abstract was accessible. The theorem assumes a simple bulk-shear threshold and is not uniform at multiple roots or under simultaneous limits.
