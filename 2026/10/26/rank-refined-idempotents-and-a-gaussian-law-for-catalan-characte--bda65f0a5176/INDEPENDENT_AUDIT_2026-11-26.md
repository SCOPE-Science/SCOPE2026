# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

An idempotent block character has fixed set S containing 1. For i in a run starting at s, monotonicity forces its image to be at least s and regressivity and idempotency force it to be a fixed point at most i, hence exactly s. On a run of m blocks the r image points in the target block must be fixed and the other dm−r points independently choose an image among them. The rank therefore adds over runs and the sequence construction gives 1/(1−Q), including the empty map. The simple positive root lies below d^(−d); positivity with consecutive t powers excludes any other root on its circle. On a slightly larger fixed circle still below d^(−d), the gap persists for z near 1 by analyticity and the argument principle. Subtracting the analytic pole term and applying Cauchy's estimate there proves the required uniform exponential error. The tilted law satisfies E exp(sR−u(s)M)=1; its two derivatives give the stated linear cumulants and its Taylor expansion gives the CLT. Positive masses at (1,1) and (2,1) rule out zero variance, also for d=1. Independent enumeration of all point maps at selected sizes recovers the frozen explicit polynomial and E_(3,2)(1)=81. For d=2 the unmarked denominator is 1−8t+13t², and differentiating its marked recurrence at k=2000 corroborates the mean and variance coefficients (0.651084740 and 0.804523671).

## originality

PASS

Inspected sources supply the idempotent character/local-image criterion or enumerate unrestricted uniform-partition transformations. The strongest indexed match includes point-rank marks but sums over unrestricted characters even at full domain. This does not provide the ordered predecessor-run generating function for d>1 or the fixed-d rank-law constants. Quasi-powers and the d=1 case are not credited as new methods or main claims.

## value

PASS

The ordered-character restriction is a natural lift of Catalan idempotents, and the point-rank series answers a question that an ambient total or an image-block count cannot. The explicit run law quantifies the typical rank and its nonzero fluctuation for d>1, not just a low-order count. The use of standard analytic tools is appropriate and does not by itself constitute the contribution.
