# Rank-resolved idempotent–square-zero factorizations of rank-one finite-field matrices

## Finding
For a prime power \(q\), integer \(n\ge3\), and nonzero rank-one \(A\in M_n(\mathbb F_q)\), define \(F_{n,r}(A)\) to be the number of ordered pairs \((E,N)\) satisfying \(EN=A\), \(E^2=E\), \(\operatorname{rank}E=r\), \(N^2=0\), and \(\operatorname{rank}N=1\). Here \(1\le r\le n\). Set
\[
 C_{n,r}=q^{r(n-r)-1}{n-2\brack r-1}_q\quad(1\le r<n),\qquad C_{n,n}=0,
\]
\[
 D_{n,r}=q^{r(n-r)}{n-1\brack r-1}_q\quad(1\le r\le n).
\]
The bracket is the Gaussian binomial coefficient, with value zero when its lower argument is out of range. **Theorem:**
\[
F_{n,r}(A)=\begin{cases}
(q^{n-1}-1)C_{n,r},&\operatorname{tr}A\ne0,\\
(q^{n-1}-q)C_{n,r}+D_{n,r},&\operatorname{tr}A=0.
\end{cases}
\]
Consequently the entire idempotent-rank profile, and its sum, depend only on whether the trace of \(A\) vanishes. In every dimension \(n\ge3\) and over every finite field, the zero-trace stratum has strictly more minimal-rank factorizations. For \(n=3\), the totals are \(3q^3+1\) and \(2q^3-2q\), respectively, for zero and nonzero trace. The two-dimensional specialization is already covered by earlier work and is not claimed to be new.

## Assumptions and scope
The coefficient field is exactly \(\mathbb F_q\), with \(q\) an arbitrary prime power; no restriction on characteristic. All matrices are labeled, and no quotient by conjugation is taken. A rank-one map is expressed \(A=v\psi\), where \(v\in\mathbb F_q^n\setminus\{0\}\) and \(\psi\in(\mathbb F_q^n)^*\setminus\{0\}\). The right factor is **required to have rank exactly one**, the smallest possible rank because \(EN=A\) is nonzero. The left factor may have any positive rank. Multiplication order is \(E\) followed by \(N\) (the matrix product \(EN\)).

## Proof
**1. Unique right-factor parametrization.** Fix one representation \(A=v\psi\). If a rank-one \(N\) obeys \(EN=v\psi\), then the image of \(EN\) is generated by \(Eu\) for any generator \(u\) of \(\operatorname{im}N\), while its row functional is proportional to \(\psi\). Therefore it has a unique representation \(N=u\psi\) normalized by \(Eu=v\). Because \((u\psi)^2=\psi(u)u\psi\) and \(u\psi\ne0\), the square-zero condition is exactly \(\psi(u)=0\). Moreover, idempotence implies \(Ev=E^2u=Eu=v\).

**2. Partition of right factors.** Write \(\alpha=\psi(v)=\operatorname{tr}A\). If \(\alpha\ne0\), all admissible nonzero \(u\in\ker\psi\) are independent of \(v\); there are \(q^{n-1}-1\) choices. If \(\alpha=0\), there are \(q^{n-1}-q\) admissible vectors outside the line \(\mathbb F_qv\). For those inside the line, \(Eu=v\) and \(Ev=v\) force \(u=v\), yielding exactly one exceptional choice.

**3. Projections for independent vectors.** Given independent \(u,v\), put \(w=u-v\). The conditions on a rank-\(r\) projection are equivalent to \(Ev=v\) and \(Ew=0\). Thus its image \(U\) must contain \(v\) and avoid \(w\), and its kernel \(W\) must contain \(w\), with \(\mathbb F_q^n=U\oplus W\). The number of rank-\(r\) image spaces containing \(v\) but not \(w\) is
\[
{n-1\brack r-1}_q-{n-2\brack r-2}_q
=q^{r-1}{n-2\brack r-1}_q.
\]
For each such \(U\), choose a reference complementary space containing \(w\), and express all complements as graphs of linear maps from that reference complement to \(U\). Requiring the graph to contain \(w\) imposes \(r\) independent scalar conditions, leaving \(q^{r(n-r-1)}\) possibilities. The product is \(q^{r(n-r)-1}{n-2\brack r-1}_q=C_{n,r}\); at \(r=n\) no such complement exists.

**4. Exceptional vector.** When \(\alpha=0\) and \(u=v\), only \(Ev=v\) is required. Choose the rank-\(r\) image \(U\) containing \(v\) in \({n-1\brack r-1}_q\) ways; every \(U\) admits exactly \(q^{r(n-r)}\) complements. This yields \(D_{n,r}\). In particular the full-rank projection \(E=I\) contributes precisely once, and only for a square-zero target.

**5. Counts and strictness.** Multiply the independent-vector count from step 2 by step 3, then add the exceptional step-4 count when \(\alpha=0\). The displayed formula follows. For \(r<n\), the ratio \(D_{n,r}/C_{n,r}\) is \(q{n-1\brack r-1}_q/{n-2\brack r-1}_q\ge q\), so the zero-trace minus nonzero-trace multiplicity equals \(D_{n,r}-(q-1)C_{n,r}>0\). At \(r=n\) this difference is one. This proves strictness in every rank. No step divides by the field characteristic or assumes that \(q\) is prime.

## Verification
The included Python program `factorization_checker.py` independently enumerates idempotents and tests actual matrix multiplication, idempotence and nilpotence, together with all rank strata. It covers \((n,q)=(2,2),(2,3),(2,4),(2,5),(3,2),(3,3),(4,2)\); the dimension-two cases are regression tests of previously known instances. The dimension-three counts are \((25,12)\) at \(q=2\) and \((82,48)\) at \(q=3\), with the zero-trace count first. The dimension-four binary counts are \((369,224)\). The script reports `MINIMAL_RANK_FACTOR_EXHAUSTIVE_OK`. These finite checks are not the proof of the general theorem; steps 1–5 are.

## Relationship to prior work
Alahmadi, Jain, Leroy and Sathaye [1] study products of idempotents, with an additional nonnegative-real rank-one problem; their work motivates finite-field factorization questions but not the present idempotent–square-zero count. Botha [2] gives factorability conditions and possible factor ranks for products involving idempotents and square-zero matrices; its abstract is accessible, but its full text was not available for comparison. Hattingh [3] supplies a subsequent complete structural treatment of singular quadratic-matrix products and does not present this Gaussian-binomial finite-field fiber census in the inspected material. An earlier published two-dimensional result explicitly determines \(n=2\), and that case is excluded from the new claim. No general-\(n\) equivalent was located in available mathematical-record searches; this does not establish absolute priority.

## Limitations
Only rank-one targets and **rank-one square-zero right factors** are counted. Arbitrary higher-rank square-zero right factors can produce additional factorizations. This is a labeled-pair count, not a similarity-orbit or algebraic-scheme classification. One directly relevant historical full text [2] remained inaccessible; it creates a residual risk of earlier implicit coverage. All conclusions have self-review only and require independent validation.
