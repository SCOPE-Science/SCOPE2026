# Rank-stratified algebra retractions of elementary abelian modular group algebras

## Finding
Let \(p\) be any prime, \(k\) any field of characteristic \(p\), and \(m\geq1\). Put
\[
A_{m,p}(k)=k[x_1,\ldots,x_m]/(x_1^p,\ldots,x_m^p),\qquad
\mathfrak m=(x_1,\ldots,x_m).
\]
This is also the group algebra \(k[C_p^m]\) under \(x_i=g_i-1\). Every unital \(k\)-algebra endomorphism \(\pi\) satisfying \(\pi^2=\pi\) is conjugate by an algebra automorphism to exactly one of the coordinate retractions \(P_r\), \(0\leq r\leq m\), defined by \(P_r(x_i)=x_i\) for \(i\leq r\) and \(P_r(x_i)=0\) for \(i>r\). Here \(r=\operatorname{rank}_k(\bar\pi)\) for the induced linear map on \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\), and \(\dim_k\pi(A_{m,p})=p^r\). Thus there are precisely \(m+1\) automorphism-conjugacy classes of unital algebra retractions over every field of characteristic \(p\).

For \(k=\mathbb F_q\), where \(q=p^e\) and \(e\geq1\), let \({m\brack r}_q\) be the Gaussian binomial coefficient. The number of **distinct embedded retraction images** of cotangent rank \(r\) is
\[
I_{m,r}(q)={m\brack r}_q\,q^{r(p^m-p^r-m+r)}.
\]
Every such image admits exactly \(q^{(m-r)(p^r-1)}\) algebra retractions onto it. Consequently, the number of **distinct idempotent unital algebra endomorphisms** of cotangent rank \(r\) is
\[
\boxed{R_{m,r}(q)={m\brack r}_q\,
q^{r(p^m-p^r-m+r)+(m-r)(p^r-1)}.}
\]
The total number is \(\sum_{r=0}^m R_{m,r}(q)\); the extreme ranks each contribute exactly one map (augmentation and identity). The counts distinguish embedded maps, not their conjugacy classes.

There is a sharp asymptotic reversal between the two highest proper cotangent-rank strata. For fixed \(p\), their orders as \(q=p^e\to\infty\) have degrees
\[
\delta_{m,r}=r(p^m-p^r)+(m-r)(p^r-1).
\]
The rank-\((m-1)\) stratum has strictly greater degree than rank \((m-2)\) exactly when \(m\leq p+2\), while rank \((m-2)\) has strictly greater degree exactly when \(m\geq p+3\). This compares those two strata, and does not assert which rank maximizes the count among all proper ranks.

## Assumptions and scope
All ring maps are unital and \(k\)-linear. A retraction means an algebra endomorphism \(\pi\) with \(\pi\circ\pi=\pi\), not an idempotent *element* of the group algebra. The maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) is the nilradical. The structural conjugacy statement holds over arbitrary fields of characteristic \(p\). Cardinal formulas require the finite field \(\mathbb F_q\) and include all prime powers \(q=p^e\). The rank is measured on the \(m\)-dimensional cotangent space, not on the full \(p^m\)-dimensional underlying vector space. For \(r=0\) use \({m\brack0}_q=1\) and the convention that \(A_{0,p}=k\).

## Proof
**Step 1: maps and automorphisms.** Every \(u\in\mathfrak m\) has \(u^p=0\): expand \(u\) in nonconstant monomials and use Frobenius; the \(p\)th power of each such monomial is divisible by at least one \(x_i^p\). Conversely, nilpotent elements of this local ring lie in \(\mathfrak m\). Therefore any \(m\)-tuple in \(\mathfrak m\) determines a unique unital algebra endomorphism by giving the images of the generators. An endomorphism is an automorphism precisely when its induced linear map on \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\) is invertible: the forward implication is immediate, and the reverse follows by successively lifting generators through the nilpotent filtration (or finite-dimensional Nakayama and surjectivity).

**Step 2: conjugacy.** Suppose \(\pi^2=\pi\), and \(\bar\pi\) has rank \(r\). Choose a basis \(\bar a_1,\ldots,\bar a_r\) for its image and \(\bar b_{r+1},\ldots,\bar b_m\) for its kernel, with representatives in \(\mathfrak m\). Define \(u_i=\pi(a_i)\) for \(i\leq r\), and \(v_j=b_j-\pi(b_j)\) for \(j>r\). Their linear classes are the chosen basis, and each lies in \(\mathfrak m\), so each has \(p\)th power zero. The assignment sending the first \(r\) generators to the \(u_i\) and the others to the \(v_j\) extends to an algebra automorphism \(g\). By idempotence, \(\pi(u_i)=u_i\) and \(\pi(v_j)=0\); hence \(\pi g=gP_r\), proving \(\pi=gP_rg^{-1}\). Ranks are conjugacy invariant, so these are exactly the \(m+1\) distinct orbits, and their images have dimension \(p^r\).

**Step 3: distinct images.** Every embedded image of rank \(r\) is generated by an ordered \(r\)-tuple of elements of \(\mathfrak m\) linearly independent modulo \(\mathfrak m^2\); conversely such a tuple extends to an algebra automorphism as in Step 1, so generates an embedded \(A_{r,p}\). The number of ordered tuples is
\[
\left(\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(q^m-q^i)\right)
q^{r(p^m-1-m)}.
\]
Two tuples generate the same embedded algebra precisely when they differ by an automorphism of \(A_{r,p}\). Its automorphism group has size
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}(A_{r,p})|
=|\mathrm{GL}_r(q)|\,q^{r(p^r-1-r)}.
\]
The action is free on generating tuples. Division and the standard product formula for \({m\brack r}_q\) yield
\[
I_{m,r}(q)={m\brack r}_q q^{r(p^m-p^r-m+r)}.
\]

**Step 4: splittings over an image.** Conjugacy lets us fix the image \(A_{r,p}\subseteq A_{m,p}\). A retraction restricting to the identity on this subalgebra must send \(x_i\) to \(x_i\) for \(i\leq r\); for each \(j>r\), its image may be any element of the augmentation ideal \((x_1,\ldots,x_r)\) of \(A_{r,p}\). Every such choice is permitted by the \(p\)th-power relations, and fixing the subalgebra makes the resulting map idempotent. These choices are independent, giving precisely \(q^{(m-r)(p^r-1)}\) retractions onto each fixed image. Multiplying by the number of images gives the displayed formula for \(R_{m,r}(q)\).

**Step 5: degree boundary.** The Gaussian coefficient \({m\brack r}_q\) is a monic polynomial in \(q\) of degree \(r(m-r)\). Thus the count \(R_{m,r}(q)\) has leading degree \(\delta_{m,r}\) as stated. Direct simplification gives
\[
\delta_{m,m-1}-\delta_{m,m-2}
=1+p^{m-2}\bigl((p-1)(p-m+3)-1\bigr).
\]
For \(m\leq p+2\) this is positive (including \(p=2,m=p+2\)); for \(m\geq p+3\) it is negative. This proves the stated sharp comparison.

## Verification
Run `python3 check_retractions.py` using Python 3 and only its standard library. It exhaustively tests every algebra retraction over \(\mathbb F_2\) for \(m=2,3\), by enumerating all generator tuples whose necessary cotangent projection condition holds and explicitly checking composition. The resulting rank counts are \((1,12,1)\) and \((1,448,3584,1)\), respectively. It separately enumerates all \(5832\) rank-one embeddings into \(\mathbb F_3[x,y]/(x^3,y^3)\), finding exactly \(972\) distinct image subalgebras; direct generator parametrization gives nine retractions per image and hence \(8748\) rank-one retractions. Additional randomized ternary polynomial identities are checked. Computations verify finite samples only; the general statement follows from Steps 1–5.

## Relationship to prior work
Carlson, Friedlander and Pevtsova study rank-\(r\) truncated polynomial subalgebras of elementary abelian modular group algebras, and Proposition 1.1 identifies linear independence in the cotangent space as the flatness criterion. That substantial predecessor is used and acknowledged: the present statement instead classifies **unital algebra idempotent endomorphisms up to conjugacy** and computes the distinct-image and per-image splitting multiplicities over every finite field. Ebrahim and Facchini study idempotent *pre*-endomorphisms in general nonassociative categories; their decomposition theorem is structurally broader but not the present numerical classification of ordinary algebra maps. Chakraborty, Dasgupta, Dutta and Gupta investigate retracts of untruncated polynomial domains, which are distinct from the finite nonreduced rings here. Literature priority beyond the materials inspected is not established.

## Limitations
No claim is made about idempotent \(k\)-linear operators that do not preserve algebra multiplication; no claim covers characteristic different from the truncation exponent's prime, mixed truncation exponents, or arbitrary finite \(p\)-groups. The number of algebra automorphisms is used only in a counting argument; there is no independent external verification of the result and unlocated descriptions of finite endomorphism monoids may imply the formula. The asymptotic rank comparison is between two specified strata, not a global rank maximization theorem.
