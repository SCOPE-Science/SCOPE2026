# Rank-stratified elementary \(2\)-group homomorphisms into even-characteristic Heisenberg groups

## Finding
Let \(q=2^e\), \(e\ge1\), and let \(b,r\ge1\). For the Heisenberg group \(H_b(q)\) defined below, the number of homomorphisms from the rank-\(r\) elementary abelian \(2\)-group is
\[
\boxed{\#\operatorname{Hom}((C_2)^r,H_b(q))
=q^r\sum_{d=0}^{\min(b,r)}{r\brack d}_q
\left(\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(q^b-q^j)\right)
q^{r(b-d)+\binom d2}.}
\]
Here \({r\brack d}_q\) is the Gaussian binomial coefficient, the empty product equals \(1\), and the summand indexed by \(d\) counts precisely those homomorphisms for which the matrix of first horizontal coordinates of the \(r\) generator images has rank \(d\) over \(\mathbb F_q\). Thus the theorem supplies an exact rank distribution, not only a total count.

For fixed \(q\) and \(r\), a consequence is the limiting probability
\[
\lim_{b\to\infty}\frac{\#\operatorname{Hom}((C_2)^r,H_b(q))}{|H_b(q)|^r}
=q^{-\binom{r+1}{2}}.
\]
All counts include homomorphisms with nontrivial kernels and the trivial homomorphism.

## Assumptions and scope
Write \(k=\mathbb F_q\). The group \(H_b(q)\) has elements \((x,y,z)\), where \(x\in k^{1\times b}\), \(y\in k^{b\times1}\), and \(z\in k\). Multiplication is
\[
(x,y,z)(x',y',z')=(x+x',\,y+y',\,z+z'+xy'),
\]
where \(xy'\) is the ordinary row-column product. Equivalently it is the group of upper block-unitriangular matrices with successive block sizes \(1,b,1\). Its order is \(q^{2b+1}\). The domain \((C_2)^r\) is generated by \(r\) commuting elements, each of order dividing \(2\). The assertion is restricted to characteristic \(2\), all even prime powers \(q\), and all positive integers \(b,r\). Rank is measured over \(k\), not over \(\mathbb F_2\).

## Proof
Let \(g_i=(x_i,y_i,z_i)\) be the image of the \(i\)-th standard generator for \(1\le i\le r\). Multiplication in characteristic \(2\) yields
\[
g_i^2=(0,0,x_i y_i),\qquad
g_i g_j=g_jg_i\Longleftrightarrow x_i y_j=x_j y_i.
\]
Consequently, a tuple defines a homomorphism exactly when \(x_i y_i=0\) for every \(i\), and \(x_i y_j=x_j y_i\) for every \(i,j\). The \(z_i\) are arbitrary, supplying the factor \(q^r\).

Form \(X\in k^{r\times b}\) with \(i\)-th row \(x_i\), and \(Y\in k^{b\times r}\) with \(j\)-th column \(y_j\). The equations say that \(S=XY\in k^{r\times r}\) is alternating, meaning \(S=S^{\mathsf T}\) with zero diagonal. Let \(d=\operatorname{rank}X\) and \(W=\operatorname{im}(X:k^b\to k^r)\).

Fix \(X\) of rank \(d\). The possible products \(S=XY\) are exactly the alternating matrices with column image contained in \(W\): one direction is immediate, while the converse holds since every column of such an \(S\) lies in \(\operatorname{im}X\). To count these matrices, give \(k^r\) its standard nondegenerate coordinate pairing. Because \(S\) is symmetric and has image in \(W\), it kills the annihilator \(W^\perp\): for \(v\in W^\perp\) and any \(u\), the pairing \(u^{\mathsf T}Sv=(Su)^{\mathsf T}v\) vanishes. Therefore the associated alternating bilinear form on \((k^r)^*\) factors through the \(d\)-dimensional quotient \((k^r)^*/W^\perp\cong W^*\). Conversely every alternating bilinear form on \(W^*\) pulls back to a unique such \(S\). The space of alternating forms on a \(d\)-space has dimension \(\binom d2\) even in characteristic \(2\), so there are \(q^{\binom d2}\) possible \(S\).

For every allowed \(S\), each of its \(r\) columns has exactly \(q^{b-d}\) preimages under \(X\); hence the number of lifts \(Y\) is \(q^{r(b-d)}\). The rank-\(d\) matrix \(X\) is specified by a \(d\)-dimensional image subspace \(W\le k^r\), which has \({r\brack d}_q\) choices, and a surjection \(k^b\twoheadrightarrow W\), which has \(\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(q^b-q^j)\) choices. Multiplication of these independent factors, followed by summation over \(0\le d\le\min(b,r)\), proves the stated rank distribution and total formula.

For the limit, divide each summand by \(|H_b(q)|^r=q^{r(2b+1)}\). Once \(b\ge r\), the full-rank term \(d=r\), after this division, equals
\[
q^{-\binom{r+1}{2}}\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(1-q^{j-b}).
\]
For fixed \(q,r\), every term with \(d<r\) is \(O(q^{-(r-d)b})\) relative to the full-rank term, because \({r\brack d}_q\) is fixed and \(\prod_{j<d}(q^b-q^j)=O(q^{bd})\). It tends to zero, whereas the displayed product tends to \(1\), giving the asserted limit.

## Verification
The standalone `check.py` enumerates all elements with square identity and all mutually commuting ordered tuples for \(q\in\{2,4\}\) and a range of \(b,r\). It computes the first-coordinate matrix rank independently by finite-field elimination and compares each observed rank fiber with the closed formula. The direct check passed all \(14\) documented cases, including \((q,b,r)=(2,1,2)\), where the total count is \(28\), and \((4,2,2)\), where the total is \(34816\). The reproducibility command is `python3 check.py`; no external Python packages are needed. Finite enumerations are checks of special cases, not proofs of the unbounded parameter statement; the latter is supplied above.

## Relationship to prior work
Jezernik's work on Schur multipliers of unitriangular groups establishes structural context for finite unitriangular groups, including characteristic two, but computes second homology and covering groups, not elementary abelian homomorphism counts. Existing commuting-tuple theory studies \(\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z^r,G)\) and related probabilities. In particular, Levit--Shwartz's Heisenberg-type hierarchy in arXiv:2605.17171 assumes an odd prime and counts unrestricted commuting elements; neither restriction matches square-one commuting tuples for even-characteristic Heisenberg groups.

A published computation of element orders in \(UT_3(\mathbb F_{2^e})\) gives \(2q^2-q-1\) elements of *exact* order two; with the identity included, this agrees with the special case \(b=r=1\) of the formula and is not claimed as new. The contribution here is the uniform \(b,r\) rank-resolved homomorphism count and its large-\(b\) probability limit. These comparisons do not constitute independent publication-priority certification.

## Limitations
This is a count of ordered homomorphisms, not a conjugacy-class count, and does not classify distinct image subgroups or faithful actions. It assumes characteristic two and does not assert an odd-characteristic analogue. The asymptotic requires \(q,r\) fixed as \(b\to\infty\); other joint limits are not asserted. Indirect implication by broader subgroup-counting results, especially for extraspecial groups when \(q=2\), remains a possible literature-priority risk. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
