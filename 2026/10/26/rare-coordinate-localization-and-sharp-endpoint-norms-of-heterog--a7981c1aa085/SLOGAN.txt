The sharp \(L^1\) and \(L^\infty\) norms of the affine least-squares projector for an arbitrarily biased Bernoulli product are attained at a coordinatewise rare vertex and equal \(\mathbb E|\sum_i B_i/r_i-(n-1)|\). Componentwise fair probabilities uniquely minimize amplification, while rare coordinates drive its unattained supremum to \(2n-1\); even at fixed mean bias, heterogeneous coordinates can move amplification in either direction, defeating both Schur-convexity and Schur-concavity.
