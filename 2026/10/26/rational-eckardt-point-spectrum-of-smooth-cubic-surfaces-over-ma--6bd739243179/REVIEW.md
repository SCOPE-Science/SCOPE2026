# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** The argument establishes geometric smoothness by the exact degree-five full-rank criterion for the gradient ideal, not a bounded finite-extension test. The complete \(2^{20}\)-vector enumeration gives \(322560\) smooth forms and an explicit 24-profile joint incidence table. A separate \(\mathrm{GL}_4(\mathbf F_2)\)-orbit enumeration yields \(36\) smooth orbits, weighted to the same global distribution; it uses a second Eckardt criterion based on squarefree residual tangent-plane binary cubics. Exact Gröbner computations on all \(36\) smooth representatives provide a further check. The conditions \(Q=0\) and \(ad+bc=1\) distinguish three concurrent geometric lines from nonreduced tangent sections, including Frobenius-nontrivial triples.

**Originality: PASS, with residual publication risk.** Das's arithmetic-statistics tables describe rational points and lines and do not state the joint rational Eckardt spectrum; their marginals alone do not imply it. McKean studies rational lines, Kadyrsizova and collaborators study geometric characteristic-two cubic surfaces, Brosowsky and collaborators exceptional Eckardt geometries, and Vikulova maximal symmetry. These are closest inspected literature. They neither state nor directly imply the complete distribution for all \(322560\) smooth binary forms. Semantic research-record searches found only tangential complex Eckardt and Fermat-surface claims, not the present finite-field joint census. Historical incidence catalogues or unindexed machine tables could contain the same statistic, and the lack of such a match is not priority proof.

**Value: PASS.** The invariant is not arbitrary: a rational Eckardt point detects the splitting of the tritangent tangent section, and partitioning it by Frobenius orbit of the three lines refines precisely the arithmetic statistics of rational points and lines already studied. The 24-profile distribution, gap spectrum, and forced-Eckardt consequence for small point counts give complete structural information for the smallest finite base field, not a routine renaming of a published table.

**Scope and limitations.** Counts refer to coefficient vectors in fixed \(\mathbf F_2\)-coordinates. The proof does not assert a formula over other fields or rule out older unindexed tabulations. Independently authored mathematical validation and publication-priority verification remain open.
