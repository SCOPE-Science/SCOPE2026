# Rational smooth-conic eight-contacts on plane quartics over \(\mathbf F_2\)

## Finding

Let \(C\subset\mathbf P^2_{\mathbf F_2}\) be a geometrically smooth plane quartic. Set \(h(C)\) equal to the number of points \(P\in C(\mathbf F_2)\) for which there exists a geometrically smooth conic \(Q\) defined over \(\mathbf F_2\) with intersection multiplicity \(I_P(C,Q)=8\). Then the complete frequency distribution, among the \(10,920\) geometrically smooth nonzero ternary quartic forms in fixed homogeneous coordinates, is

| \(h(C)\) | Number of quartic forms |
|---:|---:|
| 0 | 9,849 |
| 1 | 840 |
| 2 | 210 |
| 4 | 21 |

No other values occur. In particular \(h(C)\le4\); equality holds for one \(\mathrm{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_2)\)-orbit, of size \(21\), with stabilizer of order \(8\). A representative is
\[
C_0:\;X^4+X^2YZ+XY^3+XZ^3+Y^2Z^2=0.
\]
Its four qualifying rational points and respective smooth conics are
\[
\begin{aligned}
(0:0:1)&:\;XZ+Y^2=0,\\
(0:1:0)&:\;XY+Z^2=0,\\
(1:0:1)&:\;XZ+Y^2+YZ+Z^2=0,\\
(1:1:0)&:\;XY+Y^2+YZ+Z^2=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
All counts are for forms in fixed coordinates, not abstract curve-isomorphism classes. Here scalar normalization is automatic because \(\mathbf F_2^\times\) is trivial.

## Assumptions and scope

Throughout, ground field is exactly \(\mathbf F_2\), and *geometrically smooth* means smooth after extending to an algebraic closure. A conic is *geometrically smooth* when its plane projective conic scheme remains nonsingular over the algebraic closure. Every quartic \(C\) considered is a smooth geometrically integral canonical genus-three curve. The local intersection multiplicity \(I_P(C,Q)\) is the length of the local intersection algebra, equivalently the vanishing order of the conic equation on any formal uniformizer of \(C\) at \(P\). Bezout's theorem gives \(I_P(C,Q)\le8\), since a geometrically integral smooth quartic and a conic share no component. Thus an eight-contact is maximally osculating. The claim deliberately requires **smooth** conics and does not count singular double-line or line-pair contacts.

## Proof

Write \(F=\sum_{i=1}^{15}a_i m_i\), where \(m_i\) runs through the fifteen degree-four ternary monomials in the explicit lexicographic order in `census_octactic.py`. There are exactly \(2^{15}-1=32,767\) nonzero forms. Generate all \(168\) invertible binary \(3\)-by-\(3\) coordinate matrices, expand their monomial substitutions over \(\mathbf F_2\), and take each orbit. These transformations partition the nonzero forms into \(279\) disjoint orbits. Smoothness is constant on each orbit. To test a representative, form its three partial derivatives and compute the ideal generated by \(F\) and these derivatives on each of the three standard affine charts. The ideal is the unit ideal on every chart exactly when the projective curve has no geometric singularity. Exact Groebner bases over \(\mathbf F_2\) yield \(78\) smooth orbits, whose orbit sizes sum to \(10,920\).

For a rational point \(P\in C(\mathbf F_2)\), choose a chart with one projective coordinate nonzero. Since \(C\) is smooth at \(P\), one of the remaining coordinates is a local uniformizer \(t\) and the other has a unique formal expansion \(s(t)\), modulo \(t^8\), that solves \(F=0\). The implementation constructs \(s(t)\) coefficient by coefficient in the ring \(\mathbf F_2[t]/(t^8)\), checking the resulting substitution exactly. For each of the \(63\) nonzero binary ternary quadratic forms \(Q\), substitute \(s(t)\). Vanishing modulo \(t^8\) is precisely the condition \(I_P(C,Q)\ge8\); Bezout makes this equality. Only geometrically smooth \(Q\) are counted.

In characteristic two, write the quadratic cross-term coefficients of \(XY,XZ,YZ\) as \(A,B,D\). The common zero of its three partial derivatives is \((D:B:A)\). A quadratic form defines a geometrically smooth conic if and only if \((A,B,D)\ne(0,0,0)\) and \(Q(D,B,A)\ne0\). Checking all \(63\) nonzero forms yields exactly \(28\) smooth conics. The first program applies these explicit criteria to every smooth quartic-orbit representative. The positive values occur on exactly eight smooth quartic orbits: five orbits of size \(168\) with one eight-contact point, two orbits of sizes \(168\) and \(42\) with two points, and one orbit of size \(21\) with four. Their weighted counts give \(840\), \(210\), and \(21\); the remaining \(9,849\) smooth forms have none. There cannot be omitted values because all \(279\) quartic orbits have been exhausted.

An independently implemented check reverses the incidence search: it enumerates all \(28\) smooth conics, their \(3\) rational points each (\(84\) conic-point flags), solves each conic formally modulo \(t^8\), and tests the resulting \(15\)-monomial evaluation vectors against **all \(32,767\) nonzero quartic forms**. It independently generates binary projective matrices and rechecks geometric smoothness by Groebner bases on all \(279\) supplied orbit representatives, while preserving no previously accepted smoothness label without retesting. It reproduces the same four frequency bins, the same \(78\) smooth orbits, and the \(21\) maximizers. Orbit completeness, geometric smoothness and the two opposite contact-search methods certify the finite classification.

For the displayed representative, direct substitution of each listed smooth conic into the local quartic uniformizer has zero coefficients through degree seven. The decoded `conic_census.json` explicitly records all four point-conic incidences. The equivalence class has \(21\) forms because the full projective group has \(168\) elements and the orbit representative has stabilizer size \(168/21=8\).

## Verification

Run `python census_octactic.py` followed by `python verify_octactic.py` in the same directory, with Python 3 and SymPy (Groebner bases) installed. Both are standalone and use exact integer bitmask and finite-field arithmetic; they do not depend on internet services. `conic_census.json` contains the complete \(279\)-orbit table, orbit sizes, smoothness labels, and all detected contact events on representatives. `octactic_verified.json` lists the independently checked distribution and every extremal form. The first program regenerates the first JSON certificate; the second independently checks it and regenerates the second. The second program uses the first certificate to supply orbit representatives and claimed orbit sizes, but independently reconstructs their projective orbits and reruns their smoothness tests. Its contact tests instead sweep all \(32,767\) forms by conic-first incidence, and do not use the first program's contact values. Both programs assert the critical census totals and terminate with errors on disagreement.

## Relationship to prior work

Maugesten and Moe (public preprint dated 2017-09-06) treat the classical \(2\)-Hessian and sextactic points, with characteristic-zero curve geometry. Kamel and Elshareef (author-posted 2018 manuscript) study generalized Weierstrass point orders and conic contact of smooth quartics, with examples over characteristic zero. Their statements do not determine the characteristic-two, seven-point-plane distribution above. Nart and Ritzenthaler classify smooth genus-three nonhyperelliptic curves over fields of characteristic two; such a classification of objects does **not by itself** tabulate smooth-conic maximal contact flags in fixed projective coordinates. The present theorem is the finer equation-weighted conic-incidence census. Nearby previously recorded small-field quartic calculations concern rational bitangents, rational line sections, hyperflexes, or inflection lines; none of those invariants logically determines the eight-contact *smooth-conic* histogram.

## Limitations

No claim is made about larger binary fields, characteristic-zero osculation counts, moduli-space weights, abstract isomorphism classes, or all higher Weierstrass weights. The conics must be geometrically smooth. The enumeration is exact and exhaustive for \(\mathbf F_2\), not a proof for arbitrary finite fields. Earlier papers or unindexed auxiliary tables might implicitly or explicitly contain the same distribution; novelty has been checked against the specifically identified accessible works but cannot be certified solely from negative search results. There has been no independent external audit.
