# Rationally separated frequency spheres detect every nonconstant spherical harmonic

## Finding

For every integer \(\ell\ge1\), distinct positive zeros of the spherical Bessel function \(j_\ell\) have **irrational ratio**. The exception \(\ell=0\) is sharp because \(j_0(z)=\sin(z)/z\), whose positive zeros are integer multiples of \(\pi\).

Let \(p,q\) be distinct coprime positive integers and \(r>0\). On \(\mathbb S^2\subset\mathbb R^3\), observe the Fourier transform of a finite complex Borel measure on the two concentric frequency spheres of radii \(r\) and \(pr/q\). The restrictions determine the measure uniquely **if and only if** \(2r\notin q\mathbb Z_{>0}\). At \(2r\in q\mathbb Z_{>0}\), the complete nullspace is precisely the one-dimensional space generated by area measure on \(\mathbb S^2\). Thus, on the subspace of measures having total mass zero, *every* pair of distinct rationally proportional radii is injective.

## Assumptions and scope

All scalar measures may be complex, finite, and singular with respect to surface area. The Fourier convention is
\[
\widehat\mu(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb S^2} e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot\eta}\,d\mu(\eta).
\]
The frequency spheres are centered at the origin. The unit source sphere is fixed; a source sphere of arbitrary positive radius reduces by scaling. The two positive observation radii must be distinct and have rational ratio \(p/q\). Write \(\sigma\) for unnormalized area measure and \(\mathcal H_\ell\) for the degree-\(\ell\) spherical harmonics.

## Proof

**1. An algebraic phase identity at each Bessel zero.** For every integer \(\ell\ge0\), define the reverse Bessel polynomial
\[
Q_\ell(w)=\sum_{k=0}^\ell\frac{(\ell+k)!}{2^k k!(\ell-k)!}\,w^k.
\]
The standard elementary spherical Hankel formulas (reference [4]) read, for real \(x>0\),
\[
h_\ell^{(1)}(x)=\frac{(-i)^{\ell+1}e^{ix}}{x}Q_\ell(i/x),\quad
h_\ell^{(2)}(x)=\frac{i^{\ell+1}e^{-ix}}{x}Q_\ell(-i/x).
\]
Since \(2j_\ell=h_\ell^{(1)}+h_\ell^{(2)}\), a positive zero \(x\) of \(j_\ell\) satisfies
\[
e^{2ix}=R_\ell(x),\qquad R_\ell(z)=(-1)^\ell\frac{Q_\ell(-i/z)}{Q_\ell(i/z)}.
\]
Both denominator and numerator are nonzero at such a zero: for real positive \(x\), the first and second spherical Hankel functions are conjugate and cannot vanish because the first- and second-kind spherical Bessel functions have nonzero Wronskian. This avoids division through a pole.

For \(\ell\ge1\), \(R_\ell\in\mathbb Q(i)(z)\) extends analytically across \(z=0\), with \(R_\ell(0)=1\), and is nonconstant. Indeed, the two highest consecutive coefficients of \(Q_\ell\) are nonzero; therefore \(Q_\ell(-w)\ne(-1)^\ell Q_\ell(w)\). For \(\ell=0\), \(R_0=1\).

**2. No rational ratio of distinct positive zeros when \(\ell\ge1\).** Suppose distinct positive zeros occur at \(x=pt\) and \(y=qt\), with positive coprime integers \(p\ne q\) and \(t>0\). Taking powers of the phase identities gives
\[
R_\ell(pt)^q=R_\ell(qt)^p.
\]
The rational function \(D(z)=R_\ell(pz)^q-R_\ell(qz)^p\) is not identically zero. Otherwise, near \(z=0\) take the analytic logarithm \(F(z)=\log R_\ell(z)\) with \(F(0)=0\). Continuity at zero removes the possible multiple of \(2\pi i\) and yields \(qF(pz)=pF(qz)\). If \(F(z)=\sum_{k\ge1}a_kz^k\), comparison of coefficients gives \((qp^k-pq^k)a_k=0\). For \(k\ge2\) the factor is nonzero, so \(F(z)=a_1z\). Then the nonconstant rational function \(R_\ell(z)\) agrees near zero with an exponential \(e^{a_1z}\), impossible: no nonconstant exponential is rational, and the constant case contradicts nonconstancy of \(R_\ell\).

Consequently \(t\) is a root of the nonzero rational function \(D\), whose coefficients lie in the algebraic number field \(\mathbb Q(i)\). Hence \(t\) is algebraic. But \(e^{2ipt}=R_\ell(pt)\) is then algebraic, contradicting the Hermite–Lindemann theorem, since \(2ipt\) is nonzero algebraic. Thus distinct positive zeros cannot have a rational ratio.

**3. Exact two-radius nullspace.** For every \(Y\in\mathcal H_\ell\), the Funk–Hecke identity gives
\[
\int_{\mathbb S^2}e^{-2\pi it\omega\cdot\eta}Y(\omega)\,d\sigma(\omega)
=4\pi(-i)^\ell j_\ell(2\pi t)Y(\eta),\qquad t>0.
\]
If the two Fourier restrictions of \(\mu\) vanish, integrating each angular restriction against a spherical harmonic implies
\[
j_\ell(2\pi r)\int_{\mathbb S^2}Y\,d\mu=0,\qquad
j_\ell(2\pi pr/q)\int_{\mathbb S^2}Y\,d\mu=0.
\]
For \(\ell\ge1\), both coefficients cannot vanish, by Step 2: their arguments are distinct in rational ratio. Thus every nonconstant harmonic moment of \(\mu\) vanishes. As finite combinations of spherical harmonics are uniformly dense in \(C(\mathbb S^2)\), the Riesz uniqueness theorem gives \(\mu=c\sigma\). Such a measure is invisible on both frequency spheres precisely if \(j_0(2\pi r)=j_0(2\pi pr/q)=0\). Since \(j_0(z)=\sin z/z\), this occurs if and only if \(2r\in q\mathbb Z_{>0}\). Conversely, when this condition holds, every multiple of \(\sigma\) indeed has vanishing observations. Requiring \(\mu(\mathbb S^2)=0\) forces \(c=0\) in all cases. This proves the exact classification.

## Verification

The companion `rational_bessel_zero_checker.py` performs exact symbolic checks that \(R_\ell(0)=1\), \(R_\ell\) is nonconstant, and \(D\not\equiv0\) for eight degrees and four distinct rational slopes. It also numerically verifies the phase formula against positive spherical Bessel roots and checks those numerically determined roots against low-denominator rational approximations. The finite check passed **1,636 assertions**. These experiments are diagnostics only: the universal irrationality assertion uses Hermite–Lindemann and the rational-function argument, not enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work

González Vieli [1] established the Bessel-multiplier criterion for **one** frequency sphere and surface-absolutely-continuous measures. Chattopadhyay–Ghosh–Giri–Srivastava [2, Theorem 13] characterized failure for **two or more** observation spheres by common zeros of one Bessel order. That general characterization does not explicitly resolve whether a fixed positive rational dilation admits common spherical-Bessel zeros. The extra assertion here is the all-degree arithmetic exclusion of such pairs for degrees \(\ell\ge1\), including the precise surviving degree-zero nullspace and the formulation for arbitrary finite measures. Liu–Zou [3] give interlacing and positivity results for Bessel/spherical-Bessel zeros, but not the ratio theorem in the inspected text. No claim is made to priority for spherical-harmonic diagonalization or the abstract two-sphere criterion.

## Limitations

This is an injectivity theorem, **not** a uniform stable-inversion estimate; high-degree Bessel multipliers can be small. Rationality of the ratio is essential to this proof. Irrational ratios, displaced frequency centers, and other ambient dimensions are not classified here. Independently established priority is not claimed: unindexed arithmetic literature on half-integer Bessel zeros may contain an equivalent assertion.
