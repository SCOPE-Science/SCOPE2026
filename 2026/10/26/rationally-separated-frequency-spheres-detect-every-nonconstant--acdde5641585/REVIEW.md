# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The zero-ratio proof uses the exact spherical-Hankel phase identity, establishes nonconstancy of the rational elimination function by comparing the analytic logarithm at zero, and applies Hermite–Lindemann only after deriving algebraicity of a putative common zero. It treats nonvanishing of the Hankel denominators and excludes degree zero separately. The Fourier consequence follows from Funk–Hecke harmonic multipliers, uniform density of spherical harmonics, and the explicit zeros of \(j_0\). The checker confirms finite algebraic identities and sampled zeros; no infinite conclusion is inferred from those samples.

## Originality — PASS under bounded comparison

The one-sphere Bessel condition of González Vieli (2011 online) and the union-of-spheres common-Bessel-zero criterion of Chattopadhyay et al. (2020, Theorem 13) are acknowledged as established stronger *structural frameworks*. Their inspected results leave the arithmetical comparison of positive zeros under a fixed rational dilation unevaluated. Liu and Zou (2007) cover interlacing/positivity rather than ratios. The rational-dilation exclusion and precise one-dimensional residual mode do not follow from these criteria without a separate zero-ratio theorem. A prior equivalent in unindexed transcendence/special-function literature remains possible; priority is not certified.

## Value — PASS

An infinite-dimensional Fourier-reconstruction family has an exact, arithmetic criterion: rationally linked observation radii eliminate every nonconstant blind spherical harmonic, and the only remaining obstruction is an explicitly characterized one-dimensional mass mode. This resolves the common-zero ambiguity in a natural structured family of observation geometries and extends the analysis to singular finite measures. It does not claim numerical stability.
