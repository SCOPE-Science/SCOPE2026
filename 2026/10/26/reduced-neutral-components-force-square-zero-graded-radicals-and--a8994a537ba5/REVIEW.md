# Mathematical assessment

The reduced neutral algebra is a product of finite separable fields. Nilpotence of the graded radical and reducedness force the radical into degree one. Bimodule semisimplicity supplies a complement there and the grading ensures closure. The Peirce proof identifies the summands by explicitly showing that the relevant annihilator subspaces would be nilpotent graded ideals; nonzero reverse pairings produce matrix units, and isolated vertices give the two crossed-product possibilities or a purely even field. Unitization and intersection handle algebras without a unit. The finite checker is illustrative only.

The reducedness hypothesis is weaker than the neutral identity in the closest cited graded splitting theorem. The listed building blocks, including the quadratic neutral field grading, are prior structures. The contribution is their completeness together with graded splitting under the weaker reducedness condition, not priority for constructing the individual gradings. The scope is finite fields of characteristic two.
