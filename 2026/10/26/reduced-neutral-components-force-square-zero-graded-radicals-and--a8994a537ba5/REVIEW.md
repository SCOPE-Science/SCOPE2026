# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** The reduced finite-dimensional neutral component is a product of finite separable fields. The graded radical therefore has zero degree-zero part and square zero. Since the enveloping bimodule algebra A₀⊗_F A₀ᵒᵖ is semisimple, the radical has an A₀-bimodule complement in A₁; this yields a graded subalgebra complement. The Peirce-component argument in RESULT.md classifies the possible graded-simple summands. The included checker verifies the explicit F₄-neutral grading of M₂(F₂); it is illustrative, not a substitute for the universal proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The closest recent primary source proves the analogous decomposition when every neutral element satisfies \(x^q=x\). That condition forces each neutral field factor to be F_q and does not cover a merely reduced neutral component. The general graded-division classification supplies relevant simple building blocks, while the ungraded Wedderburn–Mal’tsev theorem supplies a different, ungraded complement result. Neither inspected statement gives the reduced-neutral-component radical-splitting theorem for arbitrary finite-dimensional graded algebras. A broader graded Wedderburn–Mal’tsev result or an equivalent older formulation may exist; priority is not certified.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Reducedness is a natural structural condition weaker than the recent polynomial identity. The theorem gives a clean sufficient condition for a square-zero graded radical and a graded semisimple complement in characteristic two, and identifies the extension-field component types that appear beyond the prime-field case.

**Limitations.** The result is restricted to finite fields of characteristic two and Z/2Z-gradings. The component classification uses associativity and does not assert an analogue for nonassociative algebras.
