# Scientific review: weighted Boolean-cube cut–cycle norms

**Correctness — PASS.** The square-root-conductance incidence convention defines an actual Euclidean orthogonal projection, and the signed product heat kernel proves the precise sign of every transfer-current entry. Summation gives one unit of same-direction absolute current and exactly \(\sqrt{w_iw_j}/(w_i+w_j)\) from each other direction. The diagonal is a finite positive Walsh/Laplace sum. The complementary projector's absolute column formula follows from its diagonal and off-diagonal entries. For geometric conductances, a strictly decreasing scalar kernel proves the cut maximizer classification. A monotone diagonal comparison reduces the four-dimensional cycle maximizer to two directions; the explicitly checked rational factorization and exact Sturm variation counts show exactly two switches. All eight independently assembled test graphs agree column by column with the analytical formulas (188 columns). In particular, the computer does **not** replace the heat-kernel argument for arbitrary dimension and weights; it certifies a finite polynomial root-count calculation and corroborates the full formulas.

**Originality — PASS, with identified residual risks.** Dilworth–Kutzarova–Ostrovskii (arXiv:2305.12582v1, Section 2.7, Theorem 45 and Corollary 60) treats the unweighted Hamming cube, where all direction columns have the same norm. The present weighted formula reduces to that case but neither its arbitrary-conductance formula nor the reentrant dimension-four classification follows merely from that numerical specialization. Gurel-Gurevich–Nachmias–Sachdeva (arXiv:2605.24130v1, Section 1.1, Theorem 1.3) studies precisely the symmetric weighted transfer-current matrix but establishes a universal spectral-norm upper bound for its entrywise absolute value, not exact endpoint column sums or maximizing directions. Relevant searches for anisotropic Hodge projection, weighted Hamming cubes, conductance-dependent transfer currents, and reentrant coordinate maximizers did not identify an equivalent result. Exact implicit results in older network literature or unpublished notes cannot be excluded; failed retrieval is not novelty proof. Comparison is of operator and parameter statements, not just titles.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The object is the standard symmetric weighted transfer-current projection in electrical-network analysis and real Banach-space endpoint operator geometry. The result calculates both complementary norms for all positive coordinate-conductance assignments without enumerating exponentially many edges. For geometric conductances, its exact cut maximizer theorem holds in every dimension, while its dimension-four cycle counterpart exhibits a *provable reentrant switch* with exactly two thresholds. The latter is a structural effect not visible in unweighted, fully direction-symmetric cubes and provides concrete adversarial examples for intuitive monotonicity claims about weighted projection extremizers. The geometric condition and dimension-four boundary arise from the intrinsic cube symmetries rather than arbitrary data slicing.

**Scientific limitations.** The theorem concerns positive conductances constant along each direction, the real symmetric square-root-conductance normalization, canonical orthogonal projections, and endpoint norms; it makes no nonorthogonal minimality assertion. The two-switch classification is only for four coordinates; the finite maximum formula remains valid for every dimension. The originality assessment has not received independent expert validation. The checker depends on SymPy 1.14.0 for rational polynomial arithmetic.
