# Regular-element profiles separate the smallest tree-shaped incidence-algebra twins

## Finding

Let \(P\) be any finite poset of height at most two, \(G\) its undirected strict-comparability graph, \(n=|P|\), and \(m=|E(G)|\). For every prime power \(q\), write \(A_P=I(P,\mathbb F_q)\), and call \(a\in A_P\) **von Neumann regular** when some \(b\in A_P\) satisfies \(aba=a\). If \(e_G(T)\) denotes the number of edges with both endpoints in \(T\subseteq P\), then the exact regular-element count is

\[
R_G(q)=\#\{a\in A_P:\exists b\in A_P,\ aba=a\}
=\sum_{T\subseteq P}(q-1)^{n-|T|}q^{m-e_G(T)}.
\]

Equivalently, letting \(V(F)\) be the union of the endpoints of an edge set \(F\), the regular-element probability has the exact subgraph expansion

\[
\frac{R_G(q)}{q^{n+m}}=
\sum_{F\subseteq E(G)}(-1)^{|F|}
\frac{(q-1)^{|F|}}{q^{|F|+|V(F)|}}.
\]

In particular, with \(h(G)=\sum_{v\in P}\binom{\deg_G(v)}2\), for fixed \(G\) and increasing prime powers \(q\),

\[
\frac{R_G(q)}{q^{n+m}}
=1-\frac{m}{q^2}+\frac{m+h(G)}{q^3}+O_G(q^{-4}).
\]

Thus the finite-field regularity profile detects the number of incident edge pairs, which is not determined by a tree's cycle matroid. This distinction occurs already for the connected four-vertex tree shapes: the outward star \(S\) with edges \(0<1,0<2,0<3\), and the alternating path \(H\) with edges \(0<1,2<1,2<3\). Their exact counts are

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_H(q)&=q^7-3q^5+5q^4-4q^3+3q^2-2q+1,\\
R_S(q)&=q^7-3q^5+6q^4-7q^3+6q^2-3q+1,\\
R_S(q)-R_H(q)&=q(q-1)^3>0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Nevertheless, for every \(t\ge1\) and prime power \(q\), the numbers of ordered pairwise-commuting \(t\)-tuples of idempotents in \(A_H\) and \(A_S\) are both exactly

\[
2^t\bigl(1+(2^t-1)q\bigr)^3.
\]

The regularity invariant therefore separates two incidence algebras that every such commuting-idempotent tuple count fails to distinguish, in every characteristic and every arity.

## Assumptions and scope

A height-at-most-two poset has no strict chain of three elements. Consequently the strict-interval basis of \(I(P,k)\) has square-zero multiplication, while diagonal basis elements act on each edge coordinate by its source or target coefficient. The comparability graph is simple and bipartite; isolated vertices are allowed. All claims hold for every finite field, including characteristic two. The two concrete posets have four vertices, three strict comparabilities and algebra dimension seven.

## Proof

Write an element \(a\) uniquely as its vertex coordinates \(d_v\in k\) and edge coordinates \(x_{uv}\in k\) for \(u<v\). Multiplication is

\[
(dc)_{v}=d_vc_v,\qquad (ab)_{uv}=d_u y_{uv}+x_{uv}c_v
\]

when \(b\) has vertex coordinates \(c_v\) and edge coordinates \(y_{uv}\). With \(b\)'s corresponding coordinates \(c_v,y_{uv}\), the equation \(aba=a\) requires \(d_v^2c_v=d_v\) and, at an edge \(u<v\),

\[
(d_uc_u+d_vc_v)x_{uv}+d_ud_vy_{uv}=x_{uv}.
\]

At a nonzero diagonal coordinate choose \(c_v=d_v^{-1}\); at a zero coordinate choose \(c_v=0\). If both \(d_u,d_v\) are zero, the edge equation forces \(x_{uv}=0\). Conversely, if all such coordinates vanish, choose \(y_{uv}=-x_{uv}/(d_ud_v)\) when both endpoints are nonzero, and \(y_{uv}=0\) otherwise. This solves all equations, including in characteristic two. Therefore **regularity is equivalent to vanishing of exactly those edge coordinates whose two diagonal endpoints are both zero**.

For the zero set \(T=\{v:d_v=0\}\), the \(n-|T|\) nonzero diagonal entries have \((q-1)^{n-|T|}\) choices; precisely \(m-e_G(T)\) edge coordinates are free. These disjoint strata prove the first formula.

Alternatively, choose a uniformly random element. For each edge \(e=uv\), let \(B_e\) be the event that both endpoint diagonals vanish but the edge coordinate is nonzero. Regularity is the complement of the union of all \(B_e\). For each \(F\subseteq E(G)\), the probability of the simultaneous bad events is \(q^{-|V(F)|}((q-1)/q)^{|F|}\), because the vertex and edge coordinates are independent. Inclusion-exclusion gives the second formula. A single edge contributes \(-q^{-2}+q^{-3}\). The only edge pairs contributing at order \(q^{-3}\) are those sharing a vertex; there are \(h(G)\) such pairs. Since \(G\) is simple and triangle-free, every edge set of size at least three uses at least four vertices and contributes \(O_G(q^{-4})\). This proves the asymptotic expansion.

For the two four-vertex graphs, direct evaluation of the 16 vertex-zero sets in the exact formula gives the two displayed polynomials and their positive difference. The star has \(h(S)=3\), whereas the path has \(h(H)=2\); both have three edges and tree cycle matroid freely generated by those edges.

For completeness, the commuting-idempotent comparison follows independently. A commuting \(t\)-tuple of idempotents yields mutually orthogonal joint spectral idempotents indexed by its diagonal bit colors \(c(v)\in\{0,1\}^t\). These idempotents can be simultaneously conjugated to their diagonal projections by a unit whose diagonal is one: if \(e_\alpha\) are the joint spectral projections and \(D_\alpha\) their diagonal supports, then \(U=\sum_\alpha e_\alpha D_\alpha\) is such a unit and satisfies \(e_\alpha U=UD_\alpha\). For a fixed coloring, conjugation by the \(q^m\)-element unitriangular group is transitive, and its stabilizer has one free edge coordinate precisely at edges joining equal colors. Hence the number of tuples at that coloring is \(q^{\#\{uv\in E(G):c(u)\ne c(v)\}}\). On any tree with three edges, choose the root color in \(2^t\) ways, and along each edge choose either its parent's color with weight one or one of the \(2^t-1\) different colors with weight \(q\). This gives the common formula.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` using only Python's standard library. The verifier independently builds multiplication in both seven-dimensional algebras, and for every element solves the **literal linear system** for an inner inverse \(b\) satisfying \(aba=a\). It compares the results with the subset formula for \(q=2,3,4\), including field arithmetic in \(\mathbb F_4\). It also exhaustively constructs the idempotents in both algebras and verifies the common one-idempotent and commuting-pair counts in each field. The observed regular-element counts are, respectively, \((89,91)\), \((1777,1801)\), and \((14377,14485)\), listed as (path, star). The verifier prints `VERIFY_OK_INCIDENCE_REGULARITY_SEPARATION` only after all tests pass. This finite verification is independent of the counting criterion but does not replace the proof for arbitrary fields and posets.

## Relationship to prior work

Khripchenko and Novikov establish diagonal conjugacy for idempotents and two-sided unit equivalence of von Neumann regular elements to diagonal idempotents in the more general finitary incidence algebra. These structural classification theorems are prior work. The present claim is the all-finite-field exact **regular-element cardinality and induced-edge expansion** for height-at-most-two posets, together with the degree-sensitive asymptotic and the explicit smallest tree-shape separation against all-arity commuting-idempotent counts. The simultaneous diagonalization argument is included only to establish the comparator; it is not separately claimed as new. The field-independent structural facts alone do not report the displayed graph-dependent finite-field profiles or their separation.

## Limitations

The regularity criterion and enumerator exploit the radical-square-zero condition and are not asserted for posets of height three or more. The asymptotic statement fixes the finite graph while the field size tends to infinity. The exact counting and separation are mathematically derived and finitely checked, but historical priority beyond the inspected sources and independent validation have not been established. The older general unit-equivalence theorem creates a residual possibility that the enumerator is already implicit in earlier work; no claim of absolute priority is made.
