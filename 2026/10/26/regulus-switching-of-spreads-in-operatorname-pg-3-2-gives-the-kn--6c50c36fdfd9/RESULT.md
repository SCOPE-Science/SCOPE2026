# Regulus switching of spreads in \(\operatorname{PG}(3,2)\) gives the Kneser graph \(\operatorname{KG}(8,3)\)

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal S\) be the set of line spreads of the binary projective three-space \(\operatorname{PG}(3,2)\). A spread is a partition of its fifteen points into five projective lines. Make a graph \(\mathcal R\) on \(\mathcal S\) by joining two spreads exactly when one is obtained from the other by replacing the three lines of a regulus with the three lines of its opposite regulus, leaving their other two lines fixed.

**Theorem.** There exist exactly eight intrinsically characterized families \(\mathcal F_0,\ldots,\mathcal F_7\), each consisting of twenty-one spreads, such that:

1. Each \(\mathcal F_i\) is a largest set of spreads admitting no regulus switch between any pair, and **these are all** such largest sets.
2. Every spread belongs to precisely three families. The resulting signature \(\sigma(S)=\{i:S\in\mathcal F_i\}\) bijects the fifty-six spreads with the three-element subsets of an eight-element set.
3. Two spreads admit one regulus switch **if and only if** their signatures are disjoint. Hence
\[
\mathcal R\cong\operatorname{KG}(8,3).
\]

In particular, there are exactly \(280\) switch edges, every spread has ten possible switches, and every pair of spreads can be connected by at most two switches. There are no three mutually pairwise switch-adjacent spreads. Two nonadjacent spreads sharing one original projective line have four common switch-neighbors; two sharing no lines have one common switch-neighbor. The full automorphism group of \(\mathcal R\) is \(\mathfrak S_8\) of order \(40320\), while the ambient projective collineation group has order \(20160\).

## Assumptions and scope
All points are nonzero vectors in \(\mathbb F_2^4\), encoded as integers from \(1\) through \(15\). A projective line through distinct vectors \(a,b\) consists of \(\{a,b,a\oplus b\}\), where \(\oplus\) is bitwise XOR. A regulus here consists of three pairwise skew projective lines; its opposite regulus is the other three-line partition of the same nine-point hyperbolic quadric. All objects are labeled in this fixed coordinate model. A no-switch family is an independent set in \(\mathcal R\); 'largest' means maximum cardinality rather than inclusion-maximality.

The theorem is a finite exact classification in \(\operatorname{PG}(3,2)\), not a claim about switching connectivity in higher-order projective spaces. It does not classify arbitrary partial-spread switches or all possible multi-regulus moves.

## Proof
**Step 1: Exhaustion of the spreads.** Form the thirty-five projective lines, using the XOR rule for each pair of distinct nonzero vectors. Every spread is a five-line exact cover of all fifteen points. The complete backtracking routine in `verify_switch.py` always branches on the least uncovered point and tries every projective line through it that lies wholly in the uncovered set. If a spread exists at a recursive state, it contains precisely one of those candidate lines, so one branch continues toward it. Conversely, no branch selects intersecting lines. Sorting the resulting selected line indices makes duplicate checking exact. The routine finds precisely fifty-six spreads. An independent implementation (`verify_direct.py`) tests all \(\binom{35}{5}=324632\) candidate five-line selections and also finds fifty-six.

**Step 2: Equivalence of the two switch definitions.** For each of the \(\binom{5}{3}=10\) triples of lines in every spread, both implementations inspect the projective lines partitioning the union of its nine points into three disjoint lines. Exactly two partitions exist: the original triple and a unique alternative. These form the two opposite reguli. Replacing the original triple by the alternative yields another spread. The primary implementation reconstructs every such replacement and independently checks that the resulting unordered spread pairs are exactly the pairs sharing two lines. There are \(280\) distinct pairs, of degree ten at every spread. Thus neither all three-line replacements nor any eligible switches have been omitted.

**Step 3: Canonical eight-symbol reconstruction.** Regard the complement of \(\mathcal R\) as a simple graph. The `bron_kerbosch` procedure in `verify_switch.py` enumerates **all inclusion-maximal cliques** of this complement. Its recursive state \((R,P,X)\) records a clique, compatible still-available vertices, and previously explored compatible vertices. At each call the pivot rule considers every candidate not neighboring the pivot, and after recursing moves that candidate from \(P\) to \(X\); this is the standard exhaustive Bron–Kerbosch invariant, including empty termination conditions. The output has \(23936\) maximal cliques. Their sizes are \(7,10,12,14,16,21\), with respective counts \(240,13552,8120,1680,336,8\). Every independent set extends to a maximal one, so the maximum no-switch family size is exactly \(21\), attained by exactly eight families.

The checker forms these eight 21-spread families without imposing an external labeling. It tests that every spread belongs to exactly three families, that the fifty-six signatures are pairwise distinct (and therefore exhaust all \(\binom{8}{3}=56\) three-subsets), and that all \(\binom{56}{2}\) unordered pairs satisfy the equivalence: switch adjacency if and only if the three-subset signatures are disjoint. This verifies the asserted graph isomorphism. It also proves the eight families are **intrinsic**, since they are exactly the largest no-switch families. They can be relabeled arbitrarily, explaining uniqueness of the reconstructed identification up to permuting the eight symbols.

**Step 4: Consequences.** For any three-subset of an eight-set, there are exactly \(\binom{5}{3}=10\) disjoint three-subsets. The number of edges is therefore \(56\cdot10/2=280\). Two intersecting three-subsets have either one or two common elements. Their common disjoint neighbors are the three-subsets of the complement of their union, giving one common neighbor in the first case and four in the second. Thus all nonadjacent vertices are distance two apart. The absence of triangles follows because three pairwise disjoint three-subsets would occupy nine symbols. Each graph automorphism permutes the eight intrinsic largest independent sets; it is therefore determined by its permutation of the eight symbols. Conversely, every permutation of the eight symbols preserves disjointness, so \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal R)\cong\mathfrak S_8\). The collineations of \(\operatorname{PG}(3,2)\) form \(\operatorname{GL}(4,2)\), of cardinality \((16-1)(16-2)(16-4)(16-8)=20160\), so its induced action is a subgroup of index at least two in the full switch-graph automorphism group. The collineation action on spreads is faithful: each of the thirty-five original lines lies in exactly eight spreads, and the thirty-five corresponding lists of containing spreads are pairwise distinct (a direct exhaustive assertion in `verify_switch.py`). Therefore a collineation fixing all spreads fixes all lines, hence all projective points; over \(\mathbb F_2\), this means it is the identity. Its image thus has cardinality \(20160\), of index exactly two.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_switch.py` and `python3 verify_direct.py` from the package directory using Python 3 and no nonstandard modules. Their recorded outputs are supplied as `verify_switch.out` and `verify_direct.out`. The primary executable constructs all spreads, certifies the unique alternative to every triple, enumerates all maximal no-switch sets, constructs all eight signatures, and tests the full adjacency equivalence. The first program also checks that all thirty-five lines have distinct spread-incidence fingerprints. The independent executable exhausts all five-line candidate subsets and all local alternatives, and confirms the complete overlap/distance/common-neighbor profile. The resulting unordered spread-pair counts, by number of shared projective lines \(0,1,2\), are respectively \(840,420,280\). The exact common-neighbor counts in those three cases are respectively \(1,4,0\). Every unadjacent spread pair has distance two.

The proof is a complete **finite exhaustive computational proof**, with separately explained exhaustive loops and executable source. No computation over unbounded dimensions or fields is claimed.

## Relationship to prior work
Regulus replacement is an established operation on spreads and partial spreads, notably in Bruen's work on replaceable translation nets and Bruen–Silverman's switching sets. The fifty-six spreads and their collineation equivalence are also known, and Magaud's detailed enumeration verifies these facts. An existing explicit organization of the fifty-six spreads by triples from eight symbols is described in Marcelis's account of the Klein quartic and spread hyperpackings. These earlier facts are **not claimed as new**. The new assertion is that the *single opposite-regulus exchange relation*, under the intrinsically recovered eight-family signatures, is precisely Kneser disjointness, together with the complete largest no-switch families and the induced distance/intersection correspondence.

The inspected full papers on partial-spread replacements and on the finite space's fifty-six spreads do not state this switch-graph isomorphism or its canonical characterization. The accessible triple-labeling account is a particularly close risk: it establishes a different three-subset encoding, but does not identify opposite-regulus adjacency as disjointness in that encoding. A 1995 paper by Jeurissen on special line sets in this exact small projective space and a 1974 paper explicitly about switching sets could not be read in full; an indexed abstract concerns general bounds for partial-spread replacement rather than the full graph at order two. Publication priority requires further human literature review.

## Limitations
This is only a theorem about the labeled binary projective three-space; it should not be generalized without argument to higher field orders. The computational completeness depends on the supplied deterministic Python enumerations and the standard correctness invariant of Bron–Kerbosch. Independent external scientific validation has not occurred. Earlier work may contain an equivalent formulation not indexed by the searched terms, in particular the inaccessible Jeurissen and Bruen–Silverman full texts and geometric descriptions of the eight-symbol spread correspondence.
