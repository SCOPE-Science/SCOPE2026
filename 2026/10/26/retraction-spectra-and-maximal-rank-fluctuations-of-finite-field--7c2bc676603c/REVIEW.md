# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The bracket-preserving description of a group endomorphism follows from the class-two, odd-exponent additive reconstruction. The nonzero-center branch of the idempotence equations forces the identity; the zero-center branch forces an isotropic image, which in the two-coordinate determinant space must lie in a unique field line. Complement counting and central-graph counting give the exact formula. A separate Gaussian-product calculation gives a dominated-convergence proof of the rank-defect limit. The standalone verifier enumerates all prime-field generator-image maps in three small fields, all permitted quotient projections for the field of order nine, and checks direct multiplication for 20,000 sampled extension-field cases. No finite check is substituted for an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS against the inspected evidence

Anil Kumar–Pradhan's Theorem Omega (arXiv:1908.00331) explicitly covers extraspecial groups with center order p and hence absorbs the prime-field calibration case. Its Example 6.2 discusses Heisenberg groups over general finite extension fields as a further setting, not as a proved theorem. The claimed domain has center order greater than p and supplies the complete rank-refined retraction census, retract-subgroup count, normality restriction, and extension-degree limit. Searches for aliases involving idempotent endomorphisms, retractions, unitriangular groups, and Heisenberg groups did not locate these exact conclusions. Public centralizer-lattice findings concern a different invariant, and the literature on strong retracts has a different universal quantifier. These distinctions are comparisons of implications, not a proof of absolute novelty.

## Value — PASS

Retracts encode the splitting idempotents of an endomorphism semigroup, and determine which subgroups appear as split quotients. For this natural finite-group family, the theorem classifies every proper retract, gives every image-size multiplicity and the multiplicities of retract subgroups, and proves normal-retract rigidity. The tight nontrivial rank-defect limit explains which retract sizes dominate as the field extension grows. The nonprime-field restriction is mathematically intrinsic: it distinguishes center rank greater than one from the already analyzed extraspecial case.

## Closest literature, limitations, and remaining risks

Closest: C. P. Anil Kumar and S. S. Pradhan, *On the Endomorphism Semigroups of Extra-special p-groups and Automorphism Orbits*, arXiv:1908.00331, Theorem Omega, Section 5, Example 6.2 (first posted August 1, 2019). Their parametrization is acknowledged for the prime-field boundary. The 2023–2024 paper on nilpotent strong retracts addresses a different property. More broadly equivalent extension-field retract classifications under alternative terminology cannot be excluded definitively. The result does not address characteristic two, higher-dimensional Heisenberg groups, or arbitrary endomorphisms.
