# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For two agents, if the tops differ both rules give each agent her top, and if the tops coincide both split each object equally. For three agents, a fresh exact-rational calculation over all 216 profiles compared the eating process to serial dictatorship averaged under the proposed law. It found equality everywhere, ten relabeling orbits, and exactly two exceptional classes of 36 profiles each, represented by 012 021 102 and 012 021 120. The exceptional-law matrices coincide with the listed PS matrices; the uniform-law row distances in the canonical ordering are 1/12, 0, and 1/12. The four permitted orders are reversal-closed and equiprobable, as are the six ordinary orders. Reversal gives probability 1/2 to either relative order of two agents, and an agent choosing first between the two cannot envy the later chooser. A Pareto improvement of a realized serial-dictatorship assignment would have a first agent in priority order whose allocation changes; the agent had a free choice among exactly the objects left by the unchanged earlier agents, so cannot be improved. The four-agent obstruction was independently confirmed on all 12 reversal pairs: PS gives agent 3 probability 5/12 of a or b while any such law gives 1/2.

PASS

The supported distinct contribution is the explicit reversal-symmetric priority law implementing PS for all strict three-agent profiles. The uniform-law 144/72 split, the same exceptional-profile sets in a prior envy census, the general Dec-EF results, and the four-agent negative example are verified coverage and are not credited as new. PASS

The construction links a fractional eating allocation to a lottery over efficient integral priority outcomes with a simple pairwise fairness guarantee ex post in probability. Unlike an unrestricted existence theorem or a count of uniform-law failures, it gives implementable weights from the submitted rankings. The established four-agent obstruction makes three agents a meaningful sharp boundary for the representation.
