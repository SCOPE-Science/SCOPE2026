# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The statement is about nonempty subset semigroups of the matrix-unit Brandt family. The proof identifies all central idempotents and the characteristic image of multiplication by the nonabsorbing one, then reconstructs the full automorphism group of the binary-relation semigroup from its least nonzero Green \(\mathscr J\)-class of rectangles. A zero-adjoining fiber argument proves no extra automorphism is present. Burnside's lemma and explicit support-size bounds give the exact orbit formula and asymptotic. The standalone verifier checks full central-idempotent conditions and product tables in the small cases, direct orbit enumerations and values through degree six. Finite tests are corroboration, not proofs for infinite families.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Magill (1966) already proves the automorphism group of all binary relations; this is explicitly treated as prior input, and its proof is reconstructed. The new conclusion concerns the strictly larger nonempty-subset semigroup of matrix units, requiring intrinsic identification of the relation subsemigroup and a trivial-lift-kernel theorem. Tringali and Wen (2026) treat a different numerical-semigroup family, while Yang and collaborators (2025) study power graphs, not power semigroups. Tamura (1986) studies power semigroups of the broader completely zero-simple class: its full text was not accessible, and a possible stronger overlapping theorem remains an important priority risk. Targeted indexed mathematical-literature searches found no equivalent statement; a failed search is not evidence of absolute novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The classification gives the complete automorphism group for every finite matrix-unit Brandt semigroup of degree at least two, distinguishes it from large hidden-symmetry power semigroups, and calculates the full subset-orbit census and its leading asymptotic. These are intrinsic and reproducible algebraic invariants across a natural infinite family, not an arbitrary finite exercise.

## Closest sources and limitations

The closest structural source is Magill's full binary-relation theorem, and the most important unresolved historical comparison is Tamura's 1986 article about isomorphisms of completely zero-simple power semigroups. This work does not address Brandt semigroups over nontrivial groups, general Rees matrices, or infinitely indexed orbit enumeration.
