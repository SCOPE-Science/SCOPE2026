# Review: Rigidity of intersecting triple systems at positive pair codegree three

## Correctness

**PASS.** The hypotheses are explicit: finite simple 3-uniform, nonempty, pairwise intersecting, and every pair appearing in an edge has codegree at least three. The proof forces the only edge avoiding a chosen pair \(ab\) to be \(cde\), confines every edge to \(S=\{a,b,c,d,e\}\), and then uses the codegree condition to force all ten triples on \(S\). The converse verifies intersection and exact codegree three.

## Originality

**PASS, with residual literature risk.** The closest primary papers are Balogh–Lemons–Palmer (arXiv:2005.04282; Theorem 3 and Proposition 4) and Spiro (arXiv:2110.10317; Theorem 1.3 and Lemma 2.1). They classify maximum-size admissible families for sufficiently large order and provide general feasibility bounds; neither inspected statement gives the stronger claim that every admissible triple system is forced to be \(K_5^{(3)}\) for all orders. A nearby indexed result concerned vertex-degree/diversity, which is a different invariant. Search failure is not treated as proof of novelty.

## Value

**PASS.** This is a complete, all-orders rigidity classification at the sharp endpoint of the positive pair-codegree bound for intersecting triple systems. It distinguishes the equality case from the general maximum-size results and immediately rules out positive pair codegree four or larger.

## Closest literature and limitations

The result is restricted to finite simple intersecting 3-graphs. It does not establish analogous statements for higher uniformity, t-intersection with \(t\ge2\), or positive degrees of smaller faces. An unindexed prior source may contain the same special case; independent audit has not been performed.
