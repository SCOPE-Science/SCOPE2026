# Review of root–leaf covariance in degree-proportional attachment trees

## Correctness: PASS

The degree-proportional growth model and initial edge are fixed explicitly. The event that an initial vertex never receives a new edge gives the central-binomial probability \(p_n\). Conditional expansion of the degree and leaf increments shows that the potentially difficult mixed products cancel; the sole overlapping event is attachment to the initial vertex while it is a leaf. The remaining recurrence solves to the exact mixed moment, which yields the covariance after subtracting independently proved means. A strict ratio inequality establishes the complete sign classification, and verified variance recurrences plus Stirling's formula yield the correlation constant. The bundled exact-rational verifier enumerates full finite distributions, but the infinite proof is analytic.

## Originality: PASS, with priority risk

Targeted searches for the exact covariance, its central-binomial form, alias descriptions (initial hub degree versus degree-one population), and stronger coverage found no equivalent result. The closest available record on covariance of root degree with total leaves is for a *uniform* random recursive tree; its attachment law is different and its covariance stays bounded, so it does not imply the present square-root-order formula. Brightwell–Łuczak treats preferential-attachment degree profiles, Peköz–Röllin–Ross fixed collections of early degrees, Hartle–Krapivsky global leaf/degree-class counts, and Mahmoud–Smythe–Szymański the related but different plane-oriented recursive model. The directly relevant material of these primary works was read and compared at the level of model and implication. Historical priority remains uncertain because an older unindexed joint-moment identity is possible; a failed search alone was not used as proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value: PASS

Preferential attachment creates simultaneously a distinguished age-dependent high-degree vertex and an extensive population of low-degree vertices. Their positive all-size covariance, growth on the \(\sqrt n\) scale, and asymptotic disappearance of correlation form a natural dependence law not inferable from either separate degree profile. The exact finite-size sign and the contrast with the known bounded-covariance uniform recursive-tree case make the finding structurally informative, rather than a mere tabulation of an arbitrary statistic.

## Limits

The assertion is restricted to the standard one-edge, undirected-degree-proportional tree started from an edge. It does not imply monotone regression, independence, or a full bivariate central limit theorem. Previously inaccessible or unindexed equivalent statements could affect historical attribution.
