# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The first-step second-moment recursion holds for joint holding-time and destination laws; no independence between them is assumed. The mean-hitting equation and its stationary target-row correction are checked explicitly, and the fundamental-matrix argument gives a genuine necessity proof using all targets. The loop-free condition forces the sole scalar obstruction to vanish. The three-state lognormal construction realizes the moment constraints with strictly positive waiting times. Exact rational replay covers multiple irreducible chains but is not treated as infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Voits (2026), Theorem 15, provides general higher-moment expressions and explicitly matches means to pseudo-rate chains, but the examined full text does not supply the all-target inverse iff, zero first-order destination-correlation criterion, or explicit destination-dependent lognormal mimic family. Silvestrov–Manca (2017) develops related recursive moment algorithms; the accessible full text's Section 2 provides the building blocks but the examined discussion does not state the all-target rigidity. The closest mathematical-record result on stationary alternating-renewal two-time aliasing studies different observables; its implications do not cover the claimed inverse theorem. Earlier general recursion methods could yield a reformulation; a residual implicit-coverage risk remains.

## Mathematical value — PASS

For semi-Markov inference, matching the first two moments of every passage-time experiment is a natural observational equivalence question, not an arbitrary slice. The theorem exactly identifies all local first/second holding-time information that those experiments recover, and gives a non-Markovian destination-dependent family indistinguishable by these statistics. This exposes a concrete second-order identifiability boundary useful for model validation and inverse semi-Markov problems; it requires all-target inversion, not merely another moment formula.

## Limits

Only finite irreducible loop-free networks, entry-epoch passage times, and finite second holding moments are covered. Higher moments and full distributions can distinguish the examples. Prior general recursions make implicit antecedents a live bibliographic risk.
