# Seven-edge rigidity for weighted variation under cyclic heat flow

## Finding

Let \(n\ge3\), let \(\pi_0,\ldots,\pi_{n-1}>0\), and use indices modulo \(n\). On the unit-conductance cycle define
\[
 (Lf)_i=\frac{2f_i-f_{i-1}-f_{i+1}}{\pi_i},\qquad H_t=e^{-tL},\qquad
 V_w(f)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}w_i|f_{i+1}-f_i|,
\]
where the variation weights \(w_i\) are independently chosen positive numbers. Define
\[
 N_w(t)=\sup_{V_w(f)>0}\frac{V_w(H_tf)}{V_w(f)}.
\]

**Rigidity threshold.** For every choice of positive vertex masses,
\[
 \boxed{n\ge7:\quad N_w(t)\le1\text{ for all }t\ge0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad w_0=\cdots=w_{n-1}.}
\]
For any nonconstant \(w\) on such a cycle, a real, two-level arc indicator satisfies \(V_w(H_tf)>V_w(f)\) for every sufficiently small \(t>0\). Conversely, for each \(3\le n\le6\) and for **every** choice of positive vertex masses, some nonconstant positive \(w\) satisfy \(N_w(t)\le1\) for every \(t\ge0\).

There is also an **exact finite test and sharp initial amplification rate** in every dimension. Set \(r_i=1/\pi_i\) and form the symmetric cyclic matrix
\[
 M_{ii}=-(r_i+r_{i+1}),\quad M_{i,i-1}=r_i,\quad M_{i,i+1}=r_{i+1},
\]
with all other entries zero. For \(i<j\), put \(b^{ij}=M(e_i-e_j)\) and
\[
 F_{ij}(w)=w_i b_i^{ij}-w_j b_j^{ij}+
 \sum_{k\notin\{i,j\}}w_k|b_k^{ij}|,\qquad
 \mu(w)=\max_{i<j}\frac{F_{ij}(w)}{w_i+w_j}.
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{N_w(t)=1+\mu(w)t+O(t^2),\qquad
 N_w(t)\le e^{\mu(w)t},\qquad
 N_w(t)\le1\ (t\ge0)\iff \mu(w)\le0.}
\]
Here \(O(t^2)\) is for the specified finite cycle, masses, and weights. The exact test has \(\binom n2\) explicit inequalities \(F_{ij}(w)\le0\), with no numerical approximation.

For uniform vertex masses, a complete local description follows. For every cyclic index \(i\), write
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 A_i&:\quad w_{i-1}+w_{i+2}\le3(w_i+w_{i+1}),\\
 B_i&:\quad w_{i-1}+w_{i+3}\le2(w_i+w_{i+2}).
 \end{aligned}
\]
For \(n=3\), every positive weight vector is admissible. For \(n=4\), exactly the \(A_i\) conditions are needed; for \(n=5\), exactly all \(A_i\) and \(B_i\) conditions are needed. For \(n=6\), exactly all \(A_i\), all \(B_i\), and
\[
 w_0+w_3=w_1+w_4=w_2+w_5
\]
are needed. For \(n\ge7\), exactly constant weights are admissible. As a sharp nonconstant six-cycle example, \(w_i=1+\varepsilon(-1)^i\) is contractive exactly for \(|\varepsilon|\le1/3\).

## Assumptions and scope

All signals are real-valued functions on the vertices of a finite simple cycle \(C_n\), \(n\ge3\). Vertex masses are strictly positive; every graph edge has conductance one. Variation weights are positive and independent of the vertex masses. The semigroup is generated by the vertex Laplacian displayed above; its time parameter is arbitrary nonnegative real. The variation functional is a seminorm, so the operator norm is taken over signals with nonzero variation (equivalently modulo constants). Two-input differences are covered because the heat operator is linear. No statement about non-unit edge conductances or infinite graphs is made.

## Proof

**1. The edge-gradient semigroup.** Let \(g_i=f_{i+1}-f_i\). Exactly the vectors satisfying \(\sum_i g_i=0\) occur as gradients of cyclic vertex data. Direct subtraction of adjacent heat equations gives
\[
 \dot g_i=r_i g_{i-1}+r_{i+1}g_{i+1}-(r_i+r_{i+1})g_i=(Mg)_i.
\]
The matrix \(M\) is symmetric, has nonnegative off-diagonal entries, and has all row and column sums zero. Moreover
\[
 -u^{\mathsf T}Mu=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}r_i(u_i-u_{i-1})^2,
\]
so \(\ker M\) consists exactly of constants, and the image of \(M\) is the zero-sum subspace.

**2. Sharp two-point norm formula.** Write \(\|g\|_{1,w}=\sum_iw_i|g_i|\). Every nonzero zero-sum vector decomposes as a nonnegative sum of vectors \(e_i-e_j\), by matching its positive and negative coordinate masses. Its weighted norm is the sum of the corresponding weights \(w_i+w_j\), counted with the same coefficients. It follows, with equality obtainable from a single pair, that
\[
 N_w(t)=\max_{i<j}\frac{\sum_k w_k|(e^{tM})_{ki}-(e^{tM})_{kj}|}{w_i+w_j}.
\]
Each \(e_i-e_j\) is the gradient of an actual two-level vertex signal: an indicator of the oriented arc from edge \(i\) to edge \(j\). Thus the pairwise supremum is attained in the operator norm at each fixed \(t\).

**3. Exact infinitesimal criterion.** For each fixed pair, expand \(e^{tM}(e_i-e_j)=e_i-e_j+t b^{ij}+O(t^2)\). The two initial nonzero coordinates retain their signs for small positive \(t\); all other absolute values contribute \(t|b_k^{ij}|+O(t^2)\). Hence its quotient is
\[
 1+t\frac{F_{ij}(w)}{w_i+w_j}+O(t^2).
\]
Taking a maximum over finitely many pairs proves the sharp expansion for \(N_w(t)\). The induced norm is submultiplicative, since the zero-sum gradient space is invariant. Applying \(N_w(t)\le N_w(t/m)^m\), expanding at zero, and sending \(m\) to infinity gives \(N_w(t)\le e^{\mu(w)t}\). Conversely, if \(\mu(w)>0\), a maximizing pair gives an actual arc indicator whose variation strictly increases for all sufficiently small positive \(t\). This proves the exact finite criterion.

**4. Far-pair rigidity.** Put \(\rho=Mw\); its coordinates are
\[
 \rho_i=r_i(w_{i-1}-w_i)+r_{i+1}(w_{i+1}-w_i),
 \qquad\sum_i\rho_i=0.
\]
When two edge indices have cyclic separation at least three, the two columns \(Me_i\) and \(Me_j\) have disjoint off-diagonal supports, and the formula in step 3 simplifies to
\[
 F_{ij}(w)=\rho_i+\rho_j.
\]
Let \(G_n\) join precisely such far-separated index pairs. For \(n\ge7\), this graph is regular of degree \(n-5\), connected, and nonbipartite. Specifically, \(G_7\) is the odd seven-cycle of step three; \(G_8\) is connected by step-three edges and contains the five-cycle \(0,3,6,1,4,0\); for \(n\ge9\), steps three and four generate the cyclic group, while \(0,3,6,0\) is a triangle. If all \(F_{ij}(w)\le0\), then summing the far-pair inequalities yields
\[
 0=(n-5)\sum_i\rho_i
  =\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(G_n)}(\rho_i+\rho_j).
\]
Each summand is nonpositive, so each is zero. Along each edge of the connected graph \(G_n\), \(\rho_j=-\rho_i\), and an odd cycle forces \(\rho=0\). The energy identity from step 1 now gives \(w\) constant. Conversely, if \(w\) is constant then \(e^{tM}\) is a nonnegative column-stochastic matrix and contracts the full unweighted \(\ell^1\) norm, hence \(N_w(t)\le1\). The same argument shows that nonconstant \(w\) produces a far-separated pair with \(F_{ij}>0\), proving the explicit two-level witness claim.

**5. Sharpness of seven and complete low-dimensional descriptions.** At constant \(w\), the derivative for a pair satisfies
\[
 F_{ij}(\mathbf1)=-2\left(M_{ij}+M_{ji}+
 \sum_{k\ne i,j}\min(M_{ki},M_{kj})\right).
\]
It is strictly negative whenever the indices have cyclic separation one or two, because adjacent indices communicate directly and distance-two indices have a common neighbor. For \(3\le n\le5\), every distinct pair is at distance at most two, so all inequalities remain valid for sufficiently small nonconstant positive perturbations of constant weights, for every fixed positive mass vector.

For \(n=6\), the only far pairs are the three antipodal pairs. Their inequalities sum to zero and therefore must all be equalities, i.e. \(\rho_{i+3}=-\rho_i\). Choose the nonzero, zero-sum vector \(v_i=(-1)^i\). Since \(\operatorname{im}M\) is the zero-sum subspace, choose \(u\) with \(Mu=v\). For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), \(w=\mathbf1+\varepsilon u\) is positive and nonconstant, satisfies every far-pair equality, and preserves the strictly negative near-pair derivatives. This supplies contractive nonconstant weights for arbitrary masses on the six-cycle.

If every mass equals one, direct substitution into step 3 gives \(F_{ij}=-3(w_i+w_j)\) on the triangle; \(F_{i,i+1}=-3(w_i+w_{i+1})+w_{i-1}+w_{i+2}\) for \(n\ge4\); and \(F_{i,i+2}=-2(w_i+w_{i+2})+w_{i-1}+w_{i+3}\) for \(n\ge5\). On a four-cycle, antipodal derivatives are automatically negative. On a six-cycle the far equalities say that \(M(w_i+w_{i+3})=0\), giving the displayed equality of three antipodal weight sums. These exhaust all pair distances for \(n\le6\), proving the classification. Substitution of alternating six-cycle weights yields the bound \(|\varepsilon|\le1/3\), including its endpoints.

## Verification

The standalone `verify.py` uses exact rational arithmetic to recompute the edge generator, its symmetry and conservation identities, the full pairwise derivative formula, and the far-pair simplification for randomly generated positive masses and weights. It separately checks the explicit uniform-mass formulas; graph connectivity and odd-cycle conditions in a range of finite dimensions; nonconstant six-cycle weights even with unequal vertex masses; and an explicit positive-time first-order amplification witness for a seven-cycle. Run with \`python3 verify.py\`. Its tests support finite identities and examples; the proof above, rather than finite enumeration, establishes the claims for all dimensions, masses, weights, and times.

## Relationship to prior work

The weighted two-point coefficient in step 2 belongs to the established Dobrushin/Doeblin contraction framework, including Gaubert–Qu's general Markov-operator treatment and the weighted version of Mao–Zhang–Zhang. It is **not** claimed as a newly invented generic coefficient. The new graph-specific result asserted here is the exact seven-edge rigidity cutoff with arbitrary positive vertex masses, the complete low-dimensional inequality classification, and the constructive infinitesimal witnesses. Bauer–Horn–Lin–Lippner–Mangoubi–Yau study Li–Yau-type gradients and heat-kernel inequalities on graphs, not this independent-weight variation-contraction design problem. An earlier tree-specific edge-variation criterion concerns unrestricted tree gradients and does not imply the codimension-one cyclic obstruction.

## Limitations

Only real scalar signals, finite simple cycles, positive vertex masses, unit graph-edge conductances, and fixed positive variation weights are included. In particular, the seven-edge result need not carry over verbatim to nonunit conductances, directed heat generators, graphs with chords, or infinite cycles. The generic Dobrushin weighted-pair mechanism is prior mathematics; only its graph-specific consequences here are assessed as potentially original. A hidden equivalent statement in older finite-state weighted-ergodicity literature cannot be excluded by search alone. Independent validation has not been performed.
