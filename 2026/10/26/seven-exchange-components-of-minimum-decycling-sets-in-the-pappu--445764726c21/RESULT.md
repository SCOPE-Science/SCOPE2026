# Seven exchange components of minimum decycling sets in the Pappus graph

## Finding
Let \(P\) be the Pappus graph, in the explicit LCF representation \([5,7,-7,7,-7,-5]^3\) defined below. A *decycling set* is a vertex set whose deletion leaves a forest. Let \(\mathcal R(P)\) have as vertices the minimum-cardinality decycling sets, with two sets adjacent exactly when one deleted vertex is replaced by one previously undeleted vertex.

**Theorem.** The decycling number of \(P\) is \(5\); there are exactly \(252\) minimum decycling sets. All are independent sets, and their complements induce thirteen-vertex trees. Their single-replacement graph is \(5\)-regular and has \(630\) edges. It has seven connected components: six copies of \(K_6\) and one component \(H\) on \(216\) vertices. Thus
\[
\mathcal R(P)\cong H\sqcup6K_6.
\]
The components \(K_6\) contain precisely the \(36\) minimum decycling sets lying entirely on one side of the bipartition of \(P\); all \(216\) mixed-side minimum decycling sets belong to \(H\). The latter has \(540\) edges, radius \(6\), and diameter \(7\). Exactly \(108\) vertices of \(H\) have eccentricity \(6\), and the remaining \(108\) have eccentricity \(7\). There are \(108\) unordered pairs at distance \(7\).

An explicit subgroup of \(\operatorname{Aut}(P)\), generated by the two permutations in the certificate, has order \(216\) and three orbits on minimum decycling sets, of sizes \(36,108,108\). Its two mixed-side orbits have distance distributions, indexed by distances \(0,1,\ldots,7\), respectively
\[
(1,5,14,37,56,71,32,0),\qquad (1,5,14,36,52,69,37,2).
\]
The pure-side orbit has distance distribution \((1,5)\) within its six-vertex component.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple, and undirected. Label the vertices of \(P\) by integers \(0,\ldots,17\). Include each cycle edge \(\{i,i+1\}\), with indices reduced modulo \(18\). For the periodic sequence \((d_i)_{i=0}^{17}=(5,7,-7,7,-7,-5)^3\), include every undirected chord \(\{i,i+d_i\}\), again modulo \(18\), taking repeated descriptions only once. This gives \(18\) vertices, \(27\) edges, and degree \(3\) at each vertex. The natural bipartition consists of even and odd labels. No vertex relabeling is identified in \(\mathcal R(P)\): distinct vertex subsets give distinct states. A replacement is legal only if its result is again a *minimum* decycling set; no intermediate larger deletions are permitted.

## Proof
Write \(S\) for a decycling set of size \(k\), \(e(S)\) for the number of edges internal to \(S\), and \(c\ge1\) for the number of components of the induced forest \(P-S\). The induced forest has \(18-k\) vertices and \((18-k)-c\) edges. Because \(P\) is cubic, exactly \(3k-e(S)\) edges of \(P\) have at least one endpoint in \(S\); therefore
\[
27-3k+e(S)=18-k-c,\quad\text{hence}\quad 2k=9+e(S)+c\ge10.
\]
Thus every decycling set has at least \(5\) vertices. If \(k=5\), equality forces \(e(S)=0\) and \(c=1\), so every such set is independent and its complement induces a tree. Existence, exact enumeration, and the exchange-graph claims are established by the following complete finite certificate.

The certificate `verification/complete_certificate.json` lists in increasing bitmask order all \(252\) five-element vertex sets passing the acyclicity test and all \(630\) unordered legal replacement pairs as indices into that list. The two standalone programs described below recreate the entire certificate independently. The first program enumerates every one of the \(\binom{18}{5}=8568\) candidate bitmasks, checks the complement by disjoint-set union, and generates **every** exchange edge by all vertex replacements. The second program instead constructs the nine-point/nine-line Pappus incidence graph, enumerates the same number of candidates, checks forests by iterative leaf deletion, constructs exchange edges from four-element intersections, and translates its results through the explicitly verified incidence-to-LCF isomorphism. The programs compare the full sorted mask and exchange-pair lists, not merely numerical totals.

For the exchange-graph decomposition, ordinary breadth-first search from each as-yet-unvisited state gives exactly seven components with orders \((216,6,6,6,6,6,6)\) and edge counts \((540,15,15,15,15,15,15)\). Every vertex has degree \(5\), so each six-vertex component is \(K_6\). Of the \(252\) minimum sets, exactly \(36\) use only one bipartition class; they are precisely those in the small components. Therefore the \(216\) mixed-side sets form the large connected component. Breadth-first search from each large-component state gives the two distance distributions displayed above, with \(108\) states of each type. The maximum distance is \(7\) and the minimum eccentricity is \(6\). Since each eccentricity-seven state has exactly two other vertices at distance seven, the number of unordered diametral pairs is \(108\cdot2/2=108\).

Finally, the two generator permutations recorded in the certificate each preserve every edge of the LCF graph; their closure under composition contains exactly \(216\) permutations. Applying these permutations to minimum decycling sets gives the three orbits of sizes \((36,108,108)\) stated above. This cross-checks the two large-component distance distributions and their multiplicities independently of any assumed full automorphism-group classification.

## Relationship to prior work
Bau's 2007 decycling survey [1] gives general cubic lower bounds and the induced-forest viewpoint; these imply the five-vertex lower bound here but not the number of solutions or their replacement graph. The later structural work of Nedela, Seifrtová, and Škoviera [2] describes existence and types of optimal decycling partitions in broad cubic classes, without determining the labeled Pappus solution space. Bousquet, Hommelsheim, Kobayashi, Mühlenthaler, and Suzuki [3] use *the same one-vertex-exchange definition* of feedback-set reconfiguration. Their general connectivity/disconnectivity results do not logically specify the seven-component and exact-metric classification for this named graph. In particular their family includes a disconnected eighteen-vertex cubic example at parameter five; whether it coincides with the Pappus graph was not established from the accessible material, so that potential overlap remains a priority risk. The Pappus graph's standard eighteen-vertex LCF representation and its incidence interpretation are recorded in [4].

## Limitations
This is a complete finite computation-assisted classification for the specific Pappus graph, not a general formula for cubic graphs. The finite certificate is exhaustive because its tested domain and adjacency relation are explicitly complete; it makes no infinite-family inference. The stated automorphism group is a verified subgroup of order \(216\); proving that it exhausts every automorphism is not needed. The literature comparison does not establish absolute publication priority: an equivalent classification could appear in unindexed computational tables, and the complete full text of [3] was not obtained. No independent external scientific audit is claimed.
