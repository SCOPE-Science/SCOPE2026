# Review of the rank-three halved-cube classification

**Correctness — PASS.** All dimensions are covered by a case-complete two-point extension-cell lemma derived from Hamming-weight formulas. Equality and adjacency of the named pair are retained when comparing pointed types. The four large-dimension pair profiles enable an explicit three-round Duplicator strategy; six rank-at-most-three sentences separate the seven dimension regimes. A standalone exact logical-type recurrence and an independent coordinate-orbit implementation agree through dimensions nine and thirteen, respectively. The computation is supplementary to the uniform proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Krotov's paper on perfect colorings of the halved 24-cube describes the same graphs but studies equitable partitions, not ordinary rank-three logical theories. Chepoi–Labourel–Ratel analyze densities and clique VC-dimensions in these graphs. Wen–Huang determine Terwilliger algebra modules; their exact intersection data could support some combinatorics, but do not themselves state the finite-rank logical stabilization and sharp cutoff. Relevant index queries and previously published results on binary hypercubes, Hamming graphs, and strongly regular graphs were compared by implication rather than by titles. Strongly regular graph classifications account for some small dimensions only, not the entire distance-regular halved-cube family. No equivalent or stronger result was located; unindexed or inaccessible prior work remains a risk, and database coverage is not a uniqueness certificate.

**Value — PASS.** Halved cubes are a classical distance-regular family; the exact bounded-rank theory and smallest universal representative are natural logical invariants. The sharp dimension-seven phase follows from the disappearance of the six-dimensional antipodal two-point extension pattern, not merely from counting vertices. Ordinary first-order logic collapses all subsequent dimensions despite strictly increasing valency; this creates a concrete comparison with counting logic and motivates the exact invariant.

**Limitations.** Only ordinary first-order logic in pure adjacency/equality language is treated, and only through rank three. The representative bound is family-relative. The uniform argument, not the bounded verifier, proves the infinite tail. The literature comparison does not exclude every inaccessible or unindexed formulation.
