**Correctness — PASS.** The gain identity \(B_j(q)=\sum_{i\sim j}q^{d_i}\) follows by conditioning on the one protected pollinator. The small-guild lemma is a direct dominance argument valid for all \(q\in(0,1)\). Exactly at seven total species, all remaining graphs have four plants and three pollinators. The complete finite classifier covers every incidence matrix, with integer quadratic positivity (including a possible interior minimum) rather than numerical sampling; its two exceptional graph types have independently checked exact expected-value formulas. The packaged verifier and actual certificate reproduce the counts \(1795\), \(108\), \(72\), and \(36\). Its finite census is used only for orders at most seven, not as an infinite-graph proof.

**Originality — PASS, with bounded remaining risk.** Earlier ecological network studies concern removing pollinators, estimating coextinction, and identifying influential species. None of the inspected primary material supplies the exact seven-total-species minimum or the two complete exceptional graph types for this independent-survival one-protected-pollinator objective. A distinct existing result about **two** changes of the optimal target counts repeated switches and does not imply the first-change minimum or identify the two first-layer isomorphism classes. Searches included equivalent conservation intervention language, score-polynomial comparisons, and graph-reliability interpretations. An unlocated graph-polynomial result or a fuller comparison with some ecological sensitivity literature could conceivably imply part of the classification; independent bibliographic review remains outstanding.

**Value — PASS.** The exact threshold identifies the smallest ecological interaction complexity where the optimal protection decision is genuinely environment-dependent. The two complete seven-species classes specify precisely how specialist/generalist dependencies cause that change, rather than merely furnishing a numerical anecdote. This supplies a structural benchmark for evaluating conservation-target robustness under extinction-risk uncertainty. This is a self-contained finite classification, not a claim of empirical predictivity or a broad theorem about weighted mutualisms.

**Closest literature and limits.** Memmott–Waser–Price (2004) analyze random/systematic pollinator removal and secondary plant extinction; Kaiser-Bunbury et al. (2010) extend robustness analysis, including behavior; Traveset–Tur–Eguíluz (2017) model stochastic coextinction and keystone pollinators. The current theorem is limited to homogeneous independent pollinator survival, one guaranteed pollinator, connected bipartite networks, and expected surviving plant richness. Further review of equivalently phrased exact finite rankings would strengthen originality confidence.
