In a synchronous single-seed SIR epidemic on a rooted tree, the expected number infected by the earliest prevalence peak increases with transmissibility on every tree of at most six vertices; seven vertices are the sharp onset of failure, with exactly two rooted exceptional types. Their complete exact polynomial laws have unique interior maxima, so making every transmission edge more reliable can slightly *decrease* expected cumulative cases measured at the sample-path peak, even while final outbreak size increases.
