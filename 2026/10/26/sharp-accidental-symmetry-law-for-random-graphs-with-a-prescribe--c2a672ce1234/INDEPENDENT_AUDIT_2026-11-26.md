# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Uniformity gives m independent within-fiber bits and two independent fair bits per unordered pair of fibers. A named-involution automorphism that fixes all fibers can flip them only with identical flip status across a disagreement edge; conversely those constraints suffice. The kernel is therefore exactly the componentwise flips of an independent G(m,1/2). For an independent check of the projection bound, any orbit permutation must preserve the pair color A+B, with independent color probabilities (1/4,1/2,1/4). On a pair cycle of length L>=2 the probability of this constraint is at most (3/8)^(L/2), by the monotonicity of vector p-norms. If k fibers move, the k(m-k) pairs joining moved to fixed fibers all move. For k=2 this gives O(m^2(3/8)^(m-2)); for 3<=k<=m/2 the sum is bounded by a geometric series starting at the cube of m(3/8)^(m/4), which is o(m 2^(-(m-1))). For k>m/2 the only fixed pairs are two fixed fibers or a 2-cycle, so there are quadratically many moved pairs and even the m! union bound is exponentially negligible on the target scale. Finally the isolated-vertex union differs from m 2^(-(m-1)) by at most binom(m,2) 2^(-(2m-3)); a nontrivial component cut of size s in [2,m/2] has union bound sum binom(m,s) 2^(-s(m-s)), also negligible (separate s=2 and use a geometric bound for s>=3). Apart from these lower-order events, the disagreement graph has one isolated fiber and one connected remainder, and the group is generated by f and the single isolated-fiber flip.

## originality

PASS

Twin swaps and connected-component flips in switching models are simple ingredients. The distinct contribution is the exact kernel in this marked graph ensemble together with the sharp probability of excess symmetry and the proof that an isolated prescribed pair accounts for the leading term. The closest inspected cover, asymmetry and rare-symmetry results use different sample spaces or different target quantities.

## value

PASS

A marked free involution is a natural way to impose one permanent symmetry while asking how often others survive. The leading exponential coefficient answers that question much more precisely than asymptotic rigidity, and the exact component kernel explains which prescribed fibers can flip together in each sample. The isolation mechanism separates local accidental symmetry from whole-fiber permutations on the scale that matters for the leading term.
