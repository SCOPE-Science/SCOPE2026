# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The coefficient identity follows from finite trigonometric frequency support, Gegenbauer orthogonality, moment cancellation and a large-parameter asymptotic that fixes the numerator. The logarithmic derivative expresses the affine correction exactly. Strict increase in each parity, an explicit degree-one versus degree-zero bound, and the two parity limits prove the sharp full-degree interval. Boundary strictness uses the standard infinitely-many-even-and-odd-positive-coefficients theorem. The dimension limit uses a convergent coth partial-fraction identity with an explicit tail expansion. Finite-precision and exact symbolic spot checks corroborate the formulas but are not a substitute for the infinite argument.

## Originality
PASS under bounded literature and overlap review. The known one-dimensional signed affine-Laplace interval does not imply positive definiteness in higher sphere dimensions. Gneiting (arXiv:1111.7077) gives general Gegenbauer positivity criteria and geodesic Matérn restrictions; Beatson–zu Castell–Xu (arXiv:1110.2437) give spherical Pólya sufficient criteria, and Ziegel (arXiv:1201.5833) gives convolution/differentiability results. The inspected passages do not contain this all-odd-dimensional free-slope interval, its dimension-independent upper endpoint or the exact \(-2/\pi\) intersection limit. Feng–Ge (arXiv:2604.11187) concerns compactly supported transfer criteria instead. Searches and comparisons are bounded, and an equivalent uncatalogued classification remains possible.

## Mathematical value
PASS. A motivated question in spherical covariance design is whether reducing the fixed Matérn linear slope can recover positive definiteness, and how any repair behaves under increasing dimension. The result settles that question exactly for all odd dimensions, supplies sharp failure witnesses, proves strict interpolation admissibility at both endpoints and identifies a universal high-dimensional negative boundary with a concrete random-hyperplane interpretation. It is not merely the known circle bound restated on another space.

## Limitations
The theorem does not give individual thresholds for even dimensions. It does give their shared all-dimensional admissibility range through equatorial inclusions. Numerical verification is finite, literature coverage is bounded, and independent scientific validation remains outstanding.
