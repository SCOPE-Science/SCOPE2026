# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact moment/Gram-matrix calculation gives the kernel \(1+s_{d,p}^{-2}\langle a,y\rangle\). The generalized-Gaussian representation, weighted central limit theorem, and variance-based uniform integrability yield a uniform leading norm profile; a concavity-defect argument gives the complete iff characterization of near-norming points. The accompanying finite Monte Carlo computations check normalization and consistency but do not replace these analytical arguments.

**Originality — PASS with acknowledged uncertainty.** The directly inspected 2005 Barthe--Guédon--Mendelson--Naor paper supplies the sampling representation and estimates first moments only up to multiplicative factors; the directly inspected 2016 Figueroa paper concerns Sobolev errors on Euclidean balls rather than the sharp endpoint norm on finite superquadratic balls. Comparisons against available published research records and previous exact affine-projection computations on several other bodies did not identify a stronger theorem implying the sharp constant, uniform profile, and iff flatness criterion. Sündermann's 1984 article on unit-ball polynomial projection constants could not be read in full and remains an unresolved literature risk. This is a best-knowledge noncoverage assessment, not a proof of unique priority.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The theorem supplies an explicit sharp amplification constant for a natural family of approximation domains and classifies every asymptotically worst evaluation sequence through normalized coordinate flatness. This is useful for distinguishing stable from worst-case geometry in high-dimensional affine least-squares approximation and is not merely a finite numerical instance. The proof applies only for fixed finite \(p>2\); neither the finite-dimensional exact extremizers nor parameter-uniform endpoint limits are established.
