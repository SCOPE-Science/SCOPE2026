# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The explicit rightmost-terminal-variable induction proves that both sides of any equation are true on at least one quarter of valuations. The recursively constructed truth-count lemma covers each integer in its interval, including the endpoint and tautological cases. Four concrete implication-only equation schemes span four adjoining intervals and realize all admissible agreement counts; the two-variable base case is checked separately. Their truth-table identities have been reconstructed, and the standalone checker confirms 651 constructive witnesses and exhaustively all term functions at arities one through four. The proof, not these bounded checks, establishes arbitrary arity. There is no primitive constant in any construction.

## Originality — PASS, with priority qualifications
Carles Cardó, *Probabilistic equational spectrum, primality and approximation in finite algebras*, arXiv:2604.08446v1 (2026-04-09), Table 1, lists the material-implication table \((1,1,0,1)\) among **unknown** two-element groupoid spectra. The present theorem gives its exact spectrum and the stronger sharp all-arity agreement-count characterization. Cardó's semilattice spectrum, Sheffer-bar spectrum and clone/primality generalities do not imply the four constructive agreement-count intervals. His separate minimal-spectrum work applies to groupoids with spectra \(\{1/2,1\}\), not this nonminimal dense spectrum. Searches of relevant published mathematical-result records under material implication, implication-only equations, equational spectrum and agreement-count aliases did not identify a covering theorem. Search failure is not a uniqueness proof: a prior result phrased in Boolean-clone or probabilistic logic terminology may have been missed.

## Value — PASS
This is the exact completion of a natural open cell in a systematic classification of two-element binary-algebra equational spectra, not an arbitrary numerical evaluation. The surprising coexistence of a sharp strictly positive probability floor with a dense spectrum provides a structural counterpoint to the full dyadic spectrum of primal two-element algebras and the nondense spectrum of the Boolean semilattice. The exact arity-by-arity interval and uniform recursive witnesses explain the mechanism rather than merely approximating a spectrum.

## Known limits and closest literature
The result is restricted to implication-only terms over two Boolean values, without nullary symbols. It neither covers richer Boolean signatures nor larger algebras. The independent historical-priority search is necessarily incomplete. All finite checker outcomes have bounded scope.
