# Review of the tracer-arrival comparison finding

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof starts from the normalized absorbing first-passage inverse-Gaussian density and derives the exact log-density ratio \(A/t+Bt+C\). Its monotone and convex regimes, together with equality of total probability masses, establish strict stochastic ordering or a unique transversal CDF crossing for every pair of admissible positive parameters. The strict mean-time bound uses the Gaussian Mills inequality. The singular scaling follows from a locally uniform survival expansion, a single density-ratio crossing of its derivative, and bracketing the unique finite-speed crossover. Executable numerical checks support the exact formulas. The zero-drift boundary is handled separately, and resident concentration is not included.

**Originality — PASS, subject to the risks below.** Molini et al. (arXiv:1009.0557), Bodin (doi:10.1002/2014WR015910), and Russian et al. (doi:10.1002/2015WR018511) give or apply passage/transport formulas. Their inspected core mathematical and transport sections do not assert the exact necessary-and-sufficient comparison criterion and its singular crossing-time scaling. Existing related research on transport inversion and finite-release peak lags addresses different observables: point-concentration identifiability or optimization of a source-convolved flux. The underlying inverse-Gaussian formula and ordinary likelihood-ratio manipulations are prior art; the claim concerns the complete parameter boundary together with the singular constant and localization, not the density formula.

**Value — PASS.** Comparing cumulative recovery fractions, rather than only mean passage times, is a natural monitoring question when a tracer or contaminant has different advective and dispersive regimes. The sharp inequalities decide precisely when one breakthrough curve is uniformly earlier; the complementary regime has exactly one change in early/late ranking. At equal velocity, even arbitrarily close-to-zero positive drift creates a distant crossover absent at zero drift, quantified by a nontrivial \(v^{-2}\) law. The result is restricted to a clearly motivated physical observable and supplies an exact diagnostic not reducible to merely computing one numerical crossing.

**Closest literature and residual risks.** The first-passage density is classical; the accessible full-text accounts by Molini et al., Bodin, and Russian et al. supplied the main physical comparison. The complete Kreft–Zuber 1978 original was not inspected. General statistical comparison results for inverse-Gaussian families could cover the order classification, and a general singular perturbation theorem might imply the scaling. These are priority risks, not claims of independent validation. The assumptions exclude nonabsorbing resident concentrations, finite-duration pulses and heterogeneous media.
