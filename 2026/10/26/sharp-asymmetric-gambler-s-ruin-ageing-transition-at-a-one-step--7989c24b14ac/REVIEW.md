# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact grouped survival law is derived from killed two-step kernels for upper bounds two through four. The sign of the eigenvalue decomposition's log-concavity determinant is computed for all ages, including the golden-ratio boundary. The universal obstruction at all larger upper barriers uses only the first four raw steps and has strictly negative first hazard increment. The finite-support case is treated with hazards only when defined. Six rational bias settings and seven upper barriers were replayed with exact fractions; they confirm the stated formulas without replacing the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS with literature risk

Liang's 2026 full-text primary result proves IFR for symmetric gambler’s ruin barriers and explicitly identifies asymmetric boundaries as future research. The two inspected related exit-time ordering papers are about symmetric barriers and stochastic comparisons as drift changes; those statements do not imply this complete fixed-drift asymmetric IFR phase. The same concepts were searched under first passage, ageing, log-concavity, absorbing-chain spectra, and gambler’s ruin terminology. An unlocated general finite birth–death ageing theorem remains a priority risk, and full text for one plausibly related stochastic-domination source was inaccessible.

## Value — PASS

The theorem answers a motivated geometry-specific part of a declared extension problem. It distinguishes a trivial/constant region, a half-drift cutoff, a sharp golden-ratio cutoff, and total IFR failure for large asymmetric buffers. The exact cutoff and global negative regime are useful structural information unavailable from a generic matrix-survival representation.

## Closest literature and limitations

Primary: Liang, arXiv:2609.13000 (full accessible text, particularly Sections 2 and 7). Context: Evans–Peköz–Righter, arXiv:2301.08328; Geng–Markowsky, DOI 10.1080/15326349.2025.2515964. The result assumes a one-step lower absorbing boundary, iid nearest-neighbor increments, starting at zero, and grouping the actual first-exit time as a ceiling divided by two. Broader bounds, random drift, and alternative grouping are not covered.
