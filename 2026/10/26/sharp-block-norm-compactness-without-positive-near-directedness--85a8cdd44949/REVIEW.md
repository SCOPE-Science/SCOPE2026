# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The anchored-block norm identity follows by independent top-coordinate selection within each block. The average-of-largest-entries inequality implies the optimal \(1/2\) comparison with \(\ell_1\) and yields a uniform tail estimate at a maximizing anchor. That estimate reconstructs the norm-plus-coordinate Kadec proof and the bounded equinormed compactness equivalence. Uniform counting measures on two separated full blocks establish failure of every stated positive near-directedness parameter. A standalone checker passed 693 exact finite assertions; the infinite-dimensional statements depend on the displayed proof rather than the enumeration.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The closest source, Gulgowski--Sawicki, arXiv:2610.09810v1, Theorems 12--13, proves sufficient compactness conditions requiring uniformly positive weighted near-directedness, and its Example 1 shows that mere \(\ell_1\)-equivalence is insufficient. The present anchored-block norm fails all these positive classes near zero in the first parameter and still has the compactness property. The 2021 abstract seminorm theorem uses equinorming of differences; the 2024 combinatorial-space survey supplies distinct equivalent \(\ell_1\) norms without this separation. An older stronger norm-plus-coordinate criterion applied to a similar construction remains a possible priority risk; no independence or exhaustive historical search is claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The example answers a natural necessity question raised by the recent positive sufficient condition: the sufficient directedness hypothesis can fail uniformly in the most relevant small-loss range without destroying the one-set compactness criterion. Its sharp norm constant and explicit tightness/Kadec mechanism give a concrete geometric explanation not supplied by isomorphism to \(\ell_1\) alone.

## Scope

There is no general classification of all hereditary families, no result for large first directedness parameter, and no answer to the entire limiting-\((0,0)\) question. The exact tests cover only finite blocks; an independent scientific audit has not been conducted.
