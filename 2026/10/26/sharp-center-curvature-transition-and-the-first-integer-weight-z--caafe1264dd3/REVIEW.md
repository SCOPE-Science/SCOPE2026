# Scientific review: Euclidean-distance Bergman curvature transition

## Correctness
PASS. The normalized monomial moment expansion yields exact coefficients through \(s^2\); differentiating \(\log K_t\) twice gives the rational center curvature for every real \(t>-1\). The strict positive parameter derivative, boundary behavior and center-versus-boundary comparison were checked algebraically and analytically. The weight-four expression reduces curvature to a degree-twelve polynomial divided by a strictly positive denominator; a direct coefficient-dominance argument proves its derivative negative throughout \((0,1)\). Exact rational bounds isolate its unique zero. The checker reproduces the algebra but does not replace the boundary asymptotic proof.

## Originality
PASS, with a substantial dependency and priority risk. The sharp off-diagonal zero transition and an explicit closed formula for this same weighted disk kernel are already published; they are cited as prior input. This result does not repackage that zero theorem. The new invariant is the curvature of the associated Kähler metric, with a sharp all-real-exponent center transition and a global sign classification at the first integer exponent. The inspected kernel, parameter-dependence, and weighted-metric sources do not provide this curvature statement, nor the exact unique radius. The kernel closed form makes much of the new calculation accessible in principle; older unindexed curvature computations or the evaluation being regarded as an elementary corollary remain substantial originality risks.

## Value
PASS. A parameterized analytic family shows **two different geometric phase boundaries**: zero-freeness of its kernel breaks at exponent \(2\) (prior result), whereas local positive curvature does not appear until \(t=(\sqrt{61}-1)/2\). The curvature remains negative near the boundary for every exponent. At the first integer exponent in the positive-center phase, an exact single sign-changing circle and a strict algebraic root enclosure give a complete finite geometric classification. These statements concern natural metric invariants rather than arbitrary selected coefficients.

## Closest sources and scientific limitations
The full previously published exact kernel result is the closest source, and the center-curvature formula follows by differentiating that known expression; no novelty is claimed for the kernel. Marinescu--Xing proves parameter regularity, not the curvature transition. Yoo's weighted Bergman geometry papers develop distinct boundary and invariant-metric settings, not the same Euclidean-distance radial weight phase diagram. Uniqueness of a curvature-zero circle is shown only for exponent \(4\), not for every positive exponent. No independent review has been undertaken.
