For a complete bipartite network with one pendant leaf on the \(p\)-vertex side, \(p\ge4\), the best continuous-time mixing spectral gap reaches the universal budget ceiling \(B/(p+4)\) exactly when the opposite side has at most \(p+2\) vertices. A positive three-class rate allocation attains this plateau, while a dual Gram certificate proves that no asymmetric allocation can extend it beyond the sharp imbalance threshold.
