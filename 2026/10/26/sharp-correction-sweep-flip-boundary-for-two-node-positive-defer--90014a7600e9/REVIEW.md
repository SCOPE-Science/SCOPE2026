# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** Direct reduction of the published two-node exponential correction equation yields the decreasing map \(u\mapsto c e^{-u}\). Its monotone second iterate has exactly one fixed point below the threshold and exactly three above it; a globally monotone two-subsequence argument identifies the limits. The cycle parametrization is one-to-one because its logarithmic derivative is strictly positive by \(2\sinh(d/2)>d\). At criticality, the cubic term in the second iterate rigorously yields the alternating \(\sqrt{{k}}\) rate. The strict separation between the sweep threshold and the carrying-capacity threshold follows from \(-\log a>1-a\). Deterministic numerical tests supplement, but do not establish, the infinite-parameter proof.

**Originality — PASS (qualified).** The primary source introduces positivity-preserving finite corrections and studies local error and collocation contractivity, but does not give the fixed-step logistic correction-count phase diagram or prove this strict invariant-box gap. Generic fixed-point theory and other published limit-cycle claims explain related mechanisms but have different maps or conclusions. The closest related prior scientific calculation studies *one* correction of the same two-node method on a *two-component conservative exchange equation*; it does not imply the scalar logistic infinite-sweep criterion. No exact published equivalent was found in targeted searches, though older specialized Picard/Volterra sources remain a historical-priority risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** This is a complete nonlinear sweep-convergence criterion for a natural minimal member of a positive time-integrator family. It differentiates guaranteed *positivity of each sweep* from convergence as the correction budget grows, proves a critical slowdown, and exhibits an open step-size range where additional corrections fail to converge even though the desired collocation endpoint is physically bounded. The exact \(h_c(a)\) and uniform \(h=2\) frontiers are actionable limits when choosing correction budgets and large step sizes.

**Closest literature and limits.** Pezzella, arXiv:2606.29523v1, Eq. (3) and Sections 4–5, supplies the method and finite-sweep analysis. Micalizzi–Torlo, arXiv:2608.23919, concerns a distinct correction design. Historical-priority uncertainty remains for exact scalar exponential Picard-iteration results; a positive cone alone does not imply the carrying-capacity bound. The result is for fixed-step two-node Gauss–Lobatto sweeps only, and has not been independently audited.
