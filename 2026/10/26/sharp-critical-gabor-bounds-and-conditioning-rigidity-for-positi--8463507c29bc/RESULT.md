# Sharp critical Gabor bounds and conditioning rigidity for positive Cauchy mixtures

## Finding

For a nonzero finite positive Borel measure \(\mu\) supported in \([u_0,\infty)\), \(u_0>0\), define \(g_\mu(t)=\int(t+iu)^{-1}\,d\mu(u)\). On every critical rectangular lattice with translation step \(\alpha>0\) and modulation step \(1/\alpha\), the **optimal squared Gabor frame bounds** are
\[
 A_\alpha={4\pi^2\over\alpha}\left(\int {r(u)\over1+r(u)}\,d\mu(u)\right)^2,\qquad
 B_\alpha={4\pi^2\over\alpha}\left(\int {1\over1-r(u)}\,d\mu(u)\right)^2,
 \quad r(u)=e^{-2\pi u/\alpha}.
\]
These constants are respectively the exact largest lower and smallest upper frame bounds; both are positive and finite. They hold for discrete, infinite, or continuous positive mixtures, with no restriction on their number of distinct poles.

Writing the analysis-frame condition number as \(\kappa_\alpha=\sqrt{B_\alpha/A_\alpha}\), define \(r_*=\sqrt2-1\) and \(\kappa_*=3+2\sqrt2\). An **exact rigidity formula** is
\[
\boxed{\ \kappa_\alpha-\kappa_*=
\kappa_*\frac{\displaystyle\int\frac{(r(u)-r_*)^2}{1-r(u)^2}\,d\mu(u)}
{\displaystyle\int\frac{r(u)}{1+r(u)}\,d\mu(u)}\ \ge0.\ }
\]
Consequently \(\kappa_\alpha\ge3+2\sqrt2\), with equality **if and only if** \(\mu\) is concentrated at the single pole height \(u_*=\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\log(1+\sqrt2)\). Equivalently \(B_\alpha/A_\alpha\ge17+12\sqrt2\). Thus no positive mixing of pole heights improves the best-conditioned single Cauchy pole, and deviation of the pole-height distribution has an exact positive quadratic cost.

## Assumptions and scope

The Fourier transform is \(\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(t)e^{-2\pi it\xi}\,dt\). The Gabor family is \(\{e^{2\pi i mt/\alpha}g_\mu(t-\alpha n):m,n\in\mathbb Z\}\) in the usual \(L^2(\mathbb R)\), with the frame inequalities \(A_\alpha\|f\|_2^2\le\sum_{m,n}|\langle f,e^{2\pi im\cdot/\alpha}g_\mu(\cdot-\alpha n)\rangle|^2\le B_\alpha\|f\|_2^2\). The measure is positive (not signed or complex), finite, nonzero, and supported a positive distance from height zero. No boundedness of its support above is needed. The claimed condition number is \(\sqrt{B_\alpha/A_\alpha}\), not the squared operator-frame ratio.

## Proof

**1. Justification and dilation.** Since \(|t+iu|^{-1}\le(t^2+u_0^2)^{-1/2}\), the measure integral defines a continuous \(L^2(\mathbb R)\) window by domination. Under the unitary dilation \(Uf(x)=\sqrt\alpha f(\alpha x)\), the critical Gabor system is mapped, up to unimodular factors and reindexing, to the unit lattice generated by \(\alpha^{-1/2}h(x)\), where \(h(x)=\int(x+i u/\alpha)^{-1}d\mu(u)\). Hence squared frame bounds of the physical system are \(\alpha^{-1}\) times the corresponding bounds of \(h\). We can calculate at unit period.

**2. Exact Zak symbol.** For \(v>0\), the boundary-value Fourier transform \(\widehat{(t+iv)^{-1}}(\xi)=-2\pi i\,\mathbf1_{\xi>0}e^{-2\pi v\xi}\) follows by a lower-half-plane contour for the positive-frequency Fourier convention. On a Zak fundamental cell \(0\le x<1\), \(0<\omega<1\), the Fourier-series form of the unit Zak transform gives, almost everywhere,
\[
 Z h(x,\omega)=-2\pi i\,e^{2\pi ix\omega}
 \int\frac{r(u)^\omega}{1-r(u)e^{2\pi ix}}\,d\mu(u).
\]
The formula first holds for atoms and extends to arbitrary finite \(\mu\) by the displayed uniform integrable denominator bound and dominated convergence, or by \(L^2\) approximation of the window. The usual Zak-Plancherel identity and critical-density diagonalization say the optimal squared frame bounds of \(h\) are exactly \(\operatorname{ess\,inf}_{x,\omega}|Zh|^2\) and \(\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{x,\omega}|Zh|^2\).

**3. Exact common maximizing corner.** Put \(z=e^{2\pi ix}\). The inequalities \(r(u)^\omega\le1\) and \(|1-r(u)z|\ge1-r(u)\) give
\[
\biggl|\int\frac{r(u)^\omega}{1-r(u)z}\,d\mu(u)\biggr|
\le \int\frac{1}{1-r(u)}\,d\mu(u).
\]
The limit \((x,\omega)\to(0,0^+)\) achieves the right side by dominated convergence. Continuity inside and up to the relevant one-sided boundary implies it is the **essential** supremum, not merely an isolated-point value.

**4. Exact common minimizing corner.** For \(r\in(0,1)\), \(z=e^{i\theta}\),
\[
 \Re\frac1{1-rz}=\frac{1-r\cos\theta}{1-2r\cos\theta+r^2}
 \ge\frac1{1+r},
\]
with numerator difference after clearing positive denominators equal to \(r(1-r)(1+\cos\theta)\ge0\). Moreover \(r^\omega\ge r\) for \(0<\omega<1\). Therefore
\[
 \biggl|\int\frac{r(u)^\omega}{1-r(u)z}\,d\mu(u)\biggr|
 \ge\Re\int\frac{r(u)^\omega}{1-r(u)z}\,d\mu(u)
 \ge\int\frac{r(u)}{1+r(u)}\,d\mu(u).
\]
At \((x,\omega)\to(1/2,1^-)\), the integrand becomes positive real and tends to \(r(u)/(1+r(u))\). Uniform domination proves the exact essential infimum. Combining steps 1–4 gives the stated optimal bounds and their positivity.

**5. Universal optimum and equality.** Let \(L=\int r/(1+r)\,d\mu>0\) and \(U=\int1/(1-r)\,d\mu\). For \(r_*=\sqrt2-1\), the elementary identity
\[
 \frac1{1-r}-\kappa_*\frac{r}{1+r}
 =\kappa_*\frac{(r-r_*)^2}{1-r^2}
\]
holds for all \(0<r<1\), because \(\kappa_*r_*^2=1\) and \(2\kappa_*r_*=\kappa_*-1\). Integration and division by \(L\) give the exact deficit formula since \(\kappa_\alpha=U/L\). The integrand in the deficit vanishes exactly at \(r=r_*\); positivity of \(\mu\) makes equality equivalent to support concentrated on that single height. The map \(u\mapsto e^{-2\pi u/\alpha}\) is injective, proving the stated unique height. Squaring yields the frame-operator condition barrier.

## Verification

The included standalone Python checker `verify.py` uses the standard library only and executes 3,231 deterministic assertions. It compares formula-based Zak amplitudes against the proposed exact bounds for randomly generated positive atomic mixtures and variable \(\alpha\), tests the two sharp Zak corners, verifies the exact rigidity identity and equality height, and independently checks the Fourier-series Zak expression against a slowly converging symmetric spatial Zak sum. Run from a directory containing the file using `python verify.py`; the reproduced stdout is embedded in `verification_output.txt`. The finite tests check implementation and normalization only; the proof of arbitrary positive measures is analytic and does not rely on numerical sampling.

## Relationship to prior work

Belov–Kulikov–Lyubarskii, *Gabor frames for rational functions* (arXiv:2103.08959, first public 2021-03-16; later Inventiones mathematicae, DOI below), establishes the all-critical-and-denser **frame existence** criterion for positive finite Cauchy-pole sums, after reflection of the time axis to match the pole-half-plane convention. Its Theorem 1.1 does not give the optimal pair of critical-density frame constants, an exact formula for general positive measures, or global optimal choice of pole-height mixtures. Its concluding infinite-pole theorem addresses a significantly restricted rapidly decaying discrete configuration.

Belov–Kulikov–Lyubarskii, *Gabor frame operator for the Cauchy kernel* (arXiv:2212.08915, first public 2022-12-17), gives sharp critical frame bounds for **one Cauchy pole** and a frame-operator formula. Specializing the displayed theorem here to a point mass reproduces that known single-pole case; neither the single-pole statement nor the all-frame-existence theorem itself implies that arbitrary positive mixtures retain precisely the **same** sharp Zak extremal corners, or the full measure-valued quadratic rigidity identity. The present claim is that positive mixtures have these jointly sharp constants and that their optimal frame conditioning is attained only by a single specified pole height. Its originality is evaluated relative to those statements; independent priority confirmation is not claimed.

## Limitations

No claim is made for signed or complex measures, poles in both half-planes, supercritical or oversampled lattice densities, irregular lattices, simultaneous optimization over lattice shape, or a uniform noise model beyond the frame condition number. The reference single-pole estimate is prior work, not new. The constant and deficit are for the analysis-frame condition number with the specified \(L^2\) and Fourier normalization. Although extensive source comparison was performed, an equivalent result in another guise in older Zak-transform, positive-kernel, or Gabor frame-bound literature remains a possible bibliographic risk. The work has not undergone independent scientific audit.
