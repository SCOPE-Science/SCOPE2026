Euclidean projection onto a probability simplex always decreases the cyclic variation of an individual input, yet its sharp two-input cyclic-variation Lipschitz multiplier is exactly \(\lfloor(n+2)^2/8\rfloor/\lceil(n+2)/2\rceil\), attained by explicit input pairs. The multiplier grows like \(n/4\) and has a precise four-residue arithmetic defect.
