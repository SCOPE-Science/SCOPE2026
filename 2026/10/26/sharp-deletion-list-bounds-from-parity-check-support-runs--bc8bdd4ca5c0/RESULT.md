# Sharp deletion-list bounds from parity-check support runs

## Finding
Let \(n\ge2\) and \(0\ne h\in\mathbb F_2^n\). The binary hyperplane \(C_h=\{x\in\mathbb F_2^n:\langle h,x\rangle=0\}\) has a worst-case **single-deletion list size** \(L(h)=\max_{y\in\mathbb F_2^{n-1}}|\{x\in C_h:y\text{ is obtained by deleting one symbol of }x\}|\). Write the support of \(h\), in its given coordinate order, as \(r\ge1\) maximal, pairwise separated intervals of ones of lengths \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_r\).

**Theorem.** Every such hyperplane satisfies \(L(h)\le n-r+1\). Equality holds whenever \(r\) is even or at least one \(\ell_j\) is odd. If \(r=1\) and \(\ell_1\) is even, then the exact value is \(L(h)=n-1\). Thus the exact list size is determined for every mask except possibly those with an odd number \(r\ge3\) of ones-runs, all of even length. At most \(F_{n+1}-1\) of the \(2^n-1\) nonzero masks fall in that unclassified class, where \(F_1=F_2=1\) denotes the Fibonacci numbers. In particular the theorem is exact for a fraction \(1-O((\varphi/2)^n)\) of all binary parity-check hyperplanes, where \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\).

The bound counts *distinct parent codewords*, not deletion positions. It applies to the output length \(n-1\), not to one-insertion decoding at output length \(n+1\).

## Assumptions and scope
Coordinates are labeled and ordered from \(1\) to \(n\), and arithmetic is over \(\mathbb F_2\). A ones-run of \(h\) is a maximal nonempty consecutive interval of coordinates on which \(h_i=1\). No Hamming-distance condition is imposed: the hyperplane can have zero-coordinate parity checks and hence weight-one codewords. The statement concerns the exact worst-case list for one deletion and holds for every integer \(n\ge2\).

## Proof
**Insertion tree and universal upper bound.** Fix a received word \(y=(y_1,\ldots,y_{n-1})\). For each \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\) and \(b\in\{0,1\}\), let \(x_i^b\) be the word formed by inserting \(b\) at position \(i\) in \(y\). Form a graph with the distinct \(n+1\) words \(x_i^b\) as vertices and an edge \(e_i=x_i^0x_i^1\) labeled \(i\). There are \(n\) distinct edges. Consecutive edges share the vertex \(x_i^{y_i}=x_{i+1}^{y_i}\), so the graph is connected and is a tree. For each vertex \(x\), the set \(D_y(x)\) of deletion positions producing \(y\) is exactly one nonempty interval: those positions in the run of equal symbols containing the inserted symbol.

Color a vertex \(x\) odd when \(\langle h,x\rangle=1\). The edge \(e_i\) joins vertices whose syndromes differ by \(h_i\); hence, modulo two, \(h_i\) equals the number of odd vertices whose deletion interval contains \(i\). Therefore the binary word \(h\) is the coordinatewise symmetric difference of the incidence-interval indicators for all odd vertices. A symmetric difference of \(t\) intervals has at most \(t\) runs of ones, since its boundary has at most \(2t\) transitions (counting the two outer boundaries). Thus at least \(r\) vertices are odd. Exactly \(n+1\) vertices exist, so \(L(h)\le n+1-r\).

**Explicit equality witnesses.** Denote the ones-runs by \([a_j,b_j]\) and their lengths by \(\ell_j=b_j-a_j+1\). Choose bits \(t_j\) satisfying \(\sum_{j=1}^r\ell_jt_j\equiv r\pmod2\). This is possible when \(r\) is even (take every \(t_j=0\)) or some \(\ell_j\) is odd (set one corresponding bit appropriately). Construct a word \(y\) of length \(n-1\) by assigning \(y_i=t_j\) for \(a_j\le i<b_j\), \(y_{a_j-1}=1-t_j\) when \(a_j>1\), and \(y_{b_j}=1-t_j\) when \(b_j<n\). All these assignments are compatible because distinct ones-runs are separated by at least one zero coordinate; assign all remaining symbols arbitrarily, for example zero.

For each \(j\), insert \(t_j\) into \(y\) at position \(a_j\), obtaining a distinct vertex \(X_j\) whose deletion-position interval is precisely \([a_j,b_j]\). Color only \(X_1,\ldots,X_r\) odd and every other insertion-tree vertex even. On edge \(e_i\), this tentative coloring changes exactly when \(i\) lies in the support of \(h\). It therefore differs from the actual syndrome coloring by at most a global complement, by connectivity of the tree. The actual syndrome of \(X_1\) is
\[
\langle h,X_1\rangle\equiv \ell_1t_1+\sum_{j=2}^{r}\bigl(1+\ell_jt_j\bigr)\equiv (r-1)+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\ell_jt_j\equiv1\pmod2.
\]
Consequently the colorings agree, exactly the \(r\) selected vertices are odd, and \(L(h)\ge n+1-r\). Together with the upper bound this proves equality in the stated regimes.

**One even run.** Suppose \(r=1\) and \(\ell_1\) is even. If only one insertion-tree vertex \(X\) were odd, the interval identity would force its deletion interval to be exactly the support of \(h\). All symbols of \(X\) on that interval would be equal to one bit \(b\), so \(\langle h,X\rangle=\ell_1b=0\), a contradiction. There are at least two odd vertices, giving \(L(h)\le n-1\). To attain it, for the unique run \([a,b]\) of even length \(\ell\), take \(y\) to be zero everywhere except for \(\ell-2\) consecutive ones starting at position \(a\). Inserting a one inside the check interval gives exactly two distinct odd-parent words (one with the inserted one before the final zero in the interval and one with it at the last coordinate). Every insertion outside the check interval, and every insertion of zero, yields even syndrome. Thus exactly two insertion-tree vertices are odd and \(L(h)=n-1\). This includes \(\ell=2\), when \(y\) is the all-zero word.

**Size of the unclassified class.** A binary word with every ones-run of even length has a unique tiling by singleton zero blocks and pairs \(11\). The number of such words of length \(n\) is \(F_{n+1}\), by the Fibonacci tiling recurrence. This includes the all-zero mask, so at most \(F_{n+1}-1\) nonzero masks, and hence at most this many of the stated exceptional type, remain outside the exact formula. The standard Fibonacci estimate proves the exponential density assertion.

## Verification
The standalone file `verify_parity_runs.py` independently enumerates each codeword in \(C_h\), deletes each coordinate, and counts distinct parents by received word. It checks every nonzero check mask through length \(9\): \(1{,}012\) hyperplanes in total, including every applicable mask and all remaining exceptions in that range. The independently constructed equality witnesses were then verified for every applicable check mask through length \(14\), with \(32{,}230\) verified witness instances across the tested lengths. The executable output records `PARITY_RUNS_VERIFY_OK`. These finite tests corroborate, but do not replace, the all-length proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Verma, Hollanti, and Tajeddine (arXiv:2610.23270v1, submitted October 16, 2026), Remark 9, give a binary alternating-parity-coordinate hyperplane of length \(2L-1\) whose single-deletion list size is at most \(L\), thereby exhibiting an exception to their nonbinary list-decoding bound. Its parity-check mask has \(r=L\) singleton ones-runs, so the present theorem recovers the list size \(L\) and extends the calculation to arbitrarily located parity checks with many runs and many different support lengths. Their general rate bounds do not imply the mask-run formula. The earlier insdel-distance work of Ji, Zheng, Chen, and Wang (arXiv:2206.00287) and linear-code constructions of Con, Shpilka, and Tamo (arXiv:2201.06130) concern different code parameters or existence rather than this exact parent-list invariant. The basic deletion/insertion-ball construction is classical; only the stated run-sensitive sharp bound and its broad equality criteria are asserted as a new contribution.

## Limitations
For an odd number of at least three ones-runs all of even length, the theorem proves \(L(h)\le n-r+1\) but does not universally assert equality. Some masks in that class may satisfy a stricter bound. No two-deletion, mixed-edit, general-dimension, or insertion-output classification is claimed. The literature comparison cannot rule out an equivalent result in older or unindexed synchronization-coding work; independent priority assessment remains outstanding.
