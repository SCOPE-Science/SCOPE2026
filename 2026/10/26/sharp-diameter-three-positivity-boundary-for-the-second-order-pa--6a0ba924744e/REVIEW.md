# Review of the Padé tree-positivity boundary

**Correctness — PASS.** The Padé coefficient at order four is exactly \(-1/4\), while lower powers cannot connect vertices four edges apart in a tree. All positive cases follow by classification of trees of diameter at most three. The star formula uses strict convexity. Double stars are handled by a complete exact-rational coefficient-sign certificate for the six distinct cross-class entry types and two within-leaf-class off-diagonal types. The reconstructed certificate, matrix positivity samples, and direct path obstruction passed. Numerical experiments were not used as proof of universal statements.

**Originality — PASS, with bounded residual uncertainty.** Schatz–Thomée–Wahlbin (2010) already analyze the *same* \([0/2]\) Padé function on a Dirichlet tridiagonal finite-element discretization and show a negative fourth-distance Taylor entry. This path obstruction is expressly attributed and not counted as novel. The new assessed statement is the complete classification for **zero-row-sum combinatorial tree Laplacians**, especially global, all-step positivity of all nontrivial and arbitrarily unbalanced double stars. Targeted published-claim searches and earlier mathematical results inspected did not reveal the same stronger classification, though specialist sources can remain unindexed. The earlier Thomée–Wahlbin parabolic maximum-principle source was bibliographically and abstract-checked, not claimed read in full.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Unconditional nonnegativity is a structural physical constraint on a second-order time discretization; this infinite-family sharp if-and-only-if criterion separates exactly those tree topologies guaranteeing it. The positive half is not a simple scalar stability check and is certified uniformly in arbitrarily large branch counts. The conclusion also determines all-step \(\ell_\infty\)-contractivity for these mass-preserving Laplacian steps.

**Limitations.** Only finite simple unweighted undirected trees with unnormalized Laplacian; failure for longer trees is guaranteed at small steps, not all steps. No independent mathematical audit or uniqueness certification. The published fourth-distance mechanism is not novel.

**Closest literature.** Schatz, Thomée, Wahlbin, 2010, DOI 10.2478/cmam-2010-0025, Sections 2 and 6; Thomée, Wahlbin, 2007 electronic release, DOI 10.1090/S0025-5718-07-02021-2. Competing Padé \([2/2]\), Crank–Nicolson and backward-Euler-filtered CN records have different update maps.
