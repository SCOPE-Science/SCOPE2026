# Scientific comparison and review

**Correctness — PASS.** Convex averaging over translations and coordinate permutations reduces a globally symmetric nonnegative stochastic matrix to its two permitted edge orbits. The exact character spectrum is derived from the third binary Krawtchouk polynomial. Two strict positive signed-spectral dual certificates establish the small exceptions \(d=5,6\), with every Fourier eigenvalue checked at the matching feasible weights. For \(d\ge7\) the first and last Fourier modes provide a universal sharp lower bound; a factored quadratic ordering of all intermediate Krawtchouk eigenvalues proves exact attainment. Every unrestricted optimizer then has zero distance-one edge probabilities because its nonnegative group average has zero weight on that entire orbit. Independent row-enumerated character spectra were also checked by included exact rational code. The proof, not its finite tests, supports the infinite domain.

**Originality — PASS, subject to a named historical-priority risk.** Boyd, Diaconis, and Xiao (2004) establish the general spectral-norm optimization framework; Boyd, Diaconis, Parrilo, and Xiao (2009) establish symmetry reduction and solve the distance-one hypercube case. Neither of the inspected primary sections states the optimum of the graph allowing both one-bit and three-bit jumps, let alone its sharp deletion transition and all-optimizer consequence. The potentially close Karlin–Lindqvist–Yao (1993) work discusses hypercube spectral majorization, but only its published abstract was accessible; the full paper could contain a stronger result, and noncoverage of that paper is **not** asserted. Publicly indexed Hamming-radius Turán records concern different positive-definiteness extremal problems, and were not treated as whole-text exclusions. No historical-priority certification is claimed.

**Value — PASS.** The exact integer boundary at dimension seven is structurally surprising: for all higher dimensions, even unrestricted optimizers assign zero probability to every allowed *nearest-neighbor* move and instead use three-coordinate flips and holding. This full phase classification goes beyond basic automorphism averaging and records a practical support-reduction law for optimizing symmetric finite-state chains. The complete-bipartite low-dimensional cases are acknowledged as already reducible from existing general work; they are included for precise domain boundaries rather than independent significance.

**Limitations.** The theorem concerns symmetric nonnegative, row-stochastic chains and SLEM, not nonreversible or finite-time total-variation optimization. The exact invariant optimizer is unique in each dimension, but unrestricted-optimizer uniqueness is not asserted. Specialized literature might dominate the result, particularly the uninspected complete text of Karlin–Lindqvist–Yao (1993). Numerical experiments alone do not establish the theorem.
