# Sharp edge-gradient amplification on anisotropically weighted products of cliques

## Finding
For every integer \(d\ge3\), every tuple of integer alphabet sizes \(q_1,\ldots,q_d\ge2\), and every tuple of conductances \(c_1,\ldots,c_d>0\), consider the Cartesian product of cliques \(G=K_{q_1}\square\cdots\square K_{q_d}\), with conductance \(c_i\) on every edge varying coordinate \(i\). Let \(B\) be any oriented vertex-edge incidence matrix and \(W\) the diagonal edge-conductance matrix. The orthogonal edge-gradient projector in energy-normalized edge coordinates is
\[
P=W^{1/2}B^{\mathsf T}(BWB^{\mathsf T})^{\dagger}BW^{1/2}.
\]
Its exact induced coordinatewise and summation norms coincide:
\[
\boxed{\|P\|_{\ell^\infty\to\ell^\infty}=\|P\|_{\ell^1\to\ell^1}=\max_{1\le i\le d}\left\{\frac{2(q_i-1)}{q_i}+2\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{(q_j-1)\sqrt{c_i c_j}}{q_i c_i+q_j c_j}\right\}.}
\]
Every row indexed by an edge varying coordinate \(i\) has the displayed value \(R_i\) as its absolute row sum. Sharpness for \(\ell^\infty\) is attained by the sign vector of a maximizing row; sharpness for \(\ell^1\) is attained by a coordinate-unit input.

Among all positive conductance choices with alphabet sizes fixed, the norm has the unique maximizing ratio
\[
\boxed{q_1c_1=\cdots=q_dc_d.}
\]
The maximizing norm equals
\[
\boxed{\max_i F_i,\qquad F_i=\frac{2(q_i-1)}{q_i}+\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{q_j-1}{\sqrt{q_iq_j}}.}
\]
Precisely the output directions with the smallest alphabet size attain this global optimized maximum. Thus the higher-factor response contains **only pairwise contributions** despite the full product graph having exponentially many vertices and edges. When the edge conductances are all equal, the most sensitive output directions likewise have smallest \(q_i\). When all alphabet sizes equal \(q\), the exact norm is \((d+1)(q-1)/q\), independently of the common conductance; for the \(d\)-cube the value is \((d+1)/2\).

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is finite, simple, and connected; \(d\ge3\), each \(q_i\ge2\), and each factor's edges share a **positive** conductance \(c_i\). Orientation is arbitrary and affects signs but not row absolute sums. The projector acts on \(\mathbb R^{E(G)}\) with the usual Euclidean inner product after multiplying physical edge gradients by \(\sqrt{c_e}\); its induced norms refer to the ordinary \(\ell^1\) and \(\ell^\infty\) norms of those normalized coordinates. The dagger denotes the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse. Common rescaling of all conductances does not change \(P\). The maximum over conductances does not claim a corresponding minimum or a result for heterogeneous weights inside a factor.

## Proof
**Step 1: heat-kernel representation.** For an edge \(e\) joining the vertices with coordinate \(i\) equal to symbols \(0\) and \(1\), hold every other coordinate equal to \(0\). The product Laplacian is \(L=BWB^{\mathsf T}\), a sum of commuting coordinate Laplacians. The \(j\)-th one-coordinate heat kernel is
\[
 p_{j,t}(a,b)=q_j^{-1}+(\mathbf{1}_{a=b}-q_j^{-1})e^{-q_jc_jt}.
\]
Its entries are nonnegative and each row sums to one. Since the dipole \(\delta_{e^-}-\delta_{e^+}\) has zero sum,
\[
 L^\dagger(\delta_{e^-}-\delta_{e^+})=\int_0^\infty e^{-tL}(\delta_{e^-}-\delta_{e^+})\,dt.
\]
Consequently the potential generated by the energy-normalized edge impulse is
\[
\phi(x)=\sqrt{c_i}(\mathbf{1}_{x_i=0}-\mathbf{1}_{x_i=1})h_i(x_{-i}),
\quad h_i(z)=\int_0^\infty e^{-q_ic_it}\prod_{j\ne i}p_{j,t}(0,z_j)\,dt.
\]
Here \(h_i(z)>0\) everywhere and, summing over all other coordinates,
\[
\sum_z h_i(z)=\frac{1}{q_i c_i}.
\]

**Step 2: exact absolute transfer-current mass.** The projected edge-vector \(Pe\) is \(W^{1/2}B^{\mathsf T}\phi\). On edges varying coordinate \(i\), for each other-coordinate word \(z\), the unique \(0\)-to-\(1\) edge contributes absolute value \(2c_ih_i(z)\); the \(2(q_i-2)\) edges from these two symbols to other symbols each contribute \(c_ih_i(z)\); all remaining edges contribute zero. The total mass on coordinate-\(i\) edges is therefore \(2c_i(q_i-1)\sum_z h_i(z)=2(q_i-1)/q_i\).

For another direction \(j\ne i\), the only nonzero flows occur in layers \(x_i\in\{0,1\}\), with opposite signs. For fixed coordinates other than \(i,j\), there is one group of \(q_j-1\) edges between symbol \(0\) and each other symbol in coordinate \(j\). By cancellation in the coordinate heat kernel,
\[
 p_{j,t}(0,0)-p_{j,t}(0,a)=e^{-q_jc_jt}\qquad(a\ne0).
\]
Each corresponding difference of \(h_i\) is nonnegative. Summing over the remaining coordinates removes their heat kernels, and gives
\[
\sum_{z_{-i,-j}}\big(h_i(0,z_{-i,-j})-h_i(a,z_{-i,-j})\big)
=\int_0^\infty e^{-(q_ic_i+q_jc_j)t}\,dt
=\frac{1}{q_ic_i+q_jc_j}.
\]
Taking account of two \(i\)-layers and \(q_j-1\) nonzero symbols, the total mass along direction \(j\) equals \(2(q_j-1)\sqrt{c_ic_j}/(q_ic_i+q_jc_j)\). Summing these *disjoint* directional masses proves the formula for \(R_i\). Automorphisms act transitively on edges of each direction, so these are all row sums. A matrix's induced \(\ell^\infty\) norm is its largest absolute row sum. Since \(P\) is symmetric, its induced \(\ell^1\) norm is the same. The sign and coordinate-unit extremizers give actual equality.

**Step 3: optimization of all weights.** For every two distinct indices, arithmetic–geometric mean gives
\[
\frac{2\sqrt{c_ic_j}}{q_ic_i+q_jc_j}\le\frac{1}{\sqrt{q_iq_j}},
\]
with equality precisely if \(q_ic_i=q_jc_j\). Thus \(R_i\le F_i\); at balanced rates all these bounds are equalities simultaneously. Conversely, for the maximum norm to reach \(\max_iF_i\), at least one row \(i\) must reach \(F_i\), which forces balanced rates for **every** \(j\ne i\). Hence the maximizing conductance ratios are unique.

For \(q_i=a<q_j=b\), direct subtraction gives
\[
 F_i-F_j=(b-a)\left(\frac1{\sqrt{ab}}-\frac2{ab}\right)
+\left(\frac1{\sqrt a}-\frac1{\sqrt b}\right)
\sum_{k\notin\{i,j\}}\frac{q_k-1}{\sqrt{q_k}}>0.
\]
Indeed \(ab>4\) for distinct integers \(a,b\ge2\), making the first summand positive and the others nonnegative. Equal sizes give equal values by symmetry. This proves the maximizing output directions under optimized rates.

**Step 4: equal-conductance classification.** For common \(c_i\), the row formula becomes \(R_i=2(q_i-1)/q_i+2\sum_{j\ne i}(q_j-1)/(q_i+q_j)\). For \(a=q_i<b=q_j\), subtracting yields
\[
 R_i-R_j=2(b-a)\left[\frac{ab-a-b}{ab(a+b)}
+\sum_{k\notin\{i,j\}}\frac{q_k-1}{(a+q_k)(b+q_k)}\right]>0,
\]
because \(ab-a-b>0\) for distinct integers \(a,b\ge2\). Equal \(q_i\) again tie. Substituting \(q_i=q\) completes the homogeneous formula.

## Verification
A standalone Python-standard-library script, `verify.py`, reconstructs the full weighted graph Laplacian, inverts an exact rational gauge Laplacian, and independently builds every transfer-current matrix entry. It checks the predicted row sums and the expected projection-matrix symmetry and idempotent diagonal identities in ten finite graph/weight instances, including three and four factors with unequal alphabets and unequal weights. Additional exact tests cover homogeneous dimensions and small alphabet sizes; auxiliary numerical tests check strict ordering of optimizer row values. Run `python verify.py` in the directory containing this document. Finite checks support the formulas but do **not** replace the universal heat-kernel and inequality proof.

## Relationship to prior work
The graph-gradient orthogonal projector and its relation to combinatorial Hodge theory are standard; see Jiang–Lim–Yao–Ye. The transfer-current kernel is an established edge projection; see Kassel–Wu. Cartesian products and their spectral methods are classical, including work of Bollobás–Brightwell and Hibino–Lee–Obata. An exact **two-factor** weighted rook-graph row formula was previously recorded and is a specialization of the present expression; no novelty is claimed for that two-factor case. The present result determines the arbitrary higher-factor row decomposition, exact global positive-weight optimizer, smallest-alphabet extremizing directions, and the resulting homogeneous high-dimensional law. The literature inspected does not establish that complete higher-factor classification; historical priority is provisional.

## Limitations
Conductance must be constant within each clique direction; no assertion is made for arbitrary edge weights or general Cartesian products. The endpoint norms use energy-normalized edge measurements and would differ under a different coordinate normalization. The result proves the **maximum**, not the minimum, over positive conductance ratios. No claim of historical uniqueness, external mathematical certification, or independent validation is made.
