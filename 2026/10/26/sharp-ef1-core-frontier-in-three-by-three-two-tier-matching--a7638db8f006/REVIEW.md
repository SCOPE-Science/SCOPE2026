# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof covers every real quality level \(0<\delta\le1\) and every normalized two-level valuation matrix with three workers and three firms. The high-edge matching number is one, two, or three. In every case there is an EF1 one-worker-per-firm allocation and an explicit feasible normalized wage vector; the firm-financing identities imply nonnegative locally financed profits and every coalition's approximate-core constraint. The all-high-star family has forced welfare ratio \((1+2\delta)/3\) under EF1, which proves sharpness. The bundled exact checker exhausts the \(343\) incidence matrices at five rational quality levels and checks every relevant coalition. These checks corroborate the continuum proof rather than replace it.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The closest work is Qin (2026), which gives the alpha-core financing equivalence and star-family upper bound, and closes the unrestricted three-firm bound only through \(\delta=1/2\). Its stated lower and upper formulas differ for \(1/2<\delta<1\). The new result is strictly restricted to three workers and common two-tier values; its maximum-high-edge-matching classification provides an analytic tight lower bound not supplied by the general source. Actual semantic research-record searches and accessible own-claim comparisons found no implication-equivalent item. Unindexed earlier work remains a residual priority risk. Full primary text was inspected; the 2025 related paper was assessed from abstract only and was not characterized as fully excluded.

## Value — PASS

This is a natural smallest-square-market classification for quality-tier team assignments, not an arbitrary numeric instance. Its all-quality-ratio exact frontier identifies exactly when fair one-worker-per-team assignments require a stability concession, supplies payment certificates, and distinguishes a high-edge matching deficiency mechanism from general continuous-valued complications. It does not claim a solution to the unrestricted three-firm open interval.

## Closest literature and limitations

Primary source: Genjie Qin, *Fairness–Stability Trade-offs in Many-to-One Matching*, arXiv:2608.17295, §2–3, Theorems 8–9, Appendix A.
