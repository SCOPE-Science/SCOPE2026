# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS (self-review).** The beta–Gaussian convolution is an established representation. The exact Kummer identity gives a unique transverse central-curvature crossing; Gaussian strict total positivity bounds the number of derivative zeros, including multiplicities; the heat-semigroup identity determines peak splitting. Beta endpoint perturbation yields the explicit linear coefficient; a uniform Laplace-endpoint balance determines the subleading logarithmic correction and constant. Numerical checks are corroborative only.

**Originality — PASS (self-review; priority risk remains).** The exact stationary law, implicit critical line and leading endpoint limits are already in arXiv:2602.08436v1, Section V.1.1, so none is presented as new. The August 2026 convolution paper is another direct predecessor. Specific new statements are the first correction at zero tumbling, the log-log and constant near the uniform beta limit, and the quantitative onset of two peaks. Searches for those precise refinements yielded no decisive covering result. A mathematically equivalent 2024 telegraphically driven trap was only examined in abstract form and may contain an implicit stronger result.

**Value — PASS (self-review).** The established logarithmic leading boundary converges exceptionally slowly when persistence approaches its critical value. The subleading logarithm and explicit constant improve finite-parameter phase calibration, while the opposite-end linear coefficient and mode-splitting law give measurable location and onset predictions. This is a quantitative finite-parameter refinement, not a new universality class or a new stationary measure.

**Scientific limitations.** One-dimensional linear trap, constant-rate telegraph flipping, additive thermal noise. The method does not address nonlinear confinement, interactions, or simultaneous parameter scaling. Priority requires independent checking of the full earlier telegraphically driven trap literature.
