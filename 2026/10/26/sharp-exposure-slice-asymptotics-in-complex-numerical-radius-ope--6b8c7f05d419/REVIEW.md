# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** The claim is reconstructed for complex \(\ell_p^d\), \(d\ge2\), fixed \(1<p<\infty\), and the entire complex-linear numerical-radius dual ball. The first-row and first-column dual norms follow from phase-isometry Fourier projections and direct Hölder maximization. Uniform pure-state estimates bound all errors. At \(p=2\), the pair of off-diagonal state functionals has exact dual norm \(2t\), and the two-by-two test matrix has verified numerical radius one. Concavity plus a large-deficit tail bound transfers the sharp asymptotic from individual states to the whole dual ball. Neither finite numerics nor the qualitative exposure theorem substitutes for these steps.

**Originality (PASS, subject to bibliographic residual risk).** Pal--Roy--Sain (2026) explicitly describe extreme states and establish qualitative exposure, but do not calculate the limiting dual-slice modulus, its non-Hilbert exponent, or the coefficient jump at \(p=2\). Martín--Merí--Quero--Roy--Sain (2024, Section 3.6) treat numerical-radius orthogonality and qualitative smoothness, not that modulus. Nearby known rank-one or mixed-exponent numerical-radius computations concern operator radii, not quantitative exposure inside the entire operator-dual ball. Recoverable pre-existing quantitative results on other Banach-space balls do not imply this statement. A residual risk is an unindexed sharp norming-face error-bound theorem covering the same dual slice under a different name; that was not located in the inspected literature.

**Value (PASS).** This is a sharp *metric-stability* refinement of a newly studied exposed-point phenomenon. The exact exponent identifies which off-diagonal perturbation controls stability, while the coefficient discontinuity at \(p=2\) reveals a genuine three-mode interaction unavailable in non-Hilbert exponents. The dimension independence and use of the full dual unit ball (not merely the state manifold) make the exact constant mathematically natural for dual geometry and robust norming problems.

**Closest comparisons and scope.** Source (1), Theorems 2.16 and 2.20, implies exposure and yields the state representation but not the rate; source (2), Section 3.6, describes orthogonality and norming conditions without sharp stability constants. The result is limited to complex finite dimensions, one coordinate exposure, fixed exponent, and leading asymptotics. Claims of absolute priority or external certification are not made.
