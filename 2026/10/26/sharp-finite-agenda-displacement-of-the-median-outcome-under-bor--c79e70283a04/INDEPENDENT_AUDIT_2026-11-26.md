# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

With an odd electorate, at least r+1 peaks lie on the median's side of each challenger; monotone within-side rankings make the median peak the unique Condorcet winner. If w<h, those with peaks at or right of h place the d−1 intervening alternatives strictly between h and w, imposing a Borda loss of at least d=h−w. The other at most r voters contribute at most m−1 in the reverse direction. Reflection handles w>h. A unique winner has strictly positive score margin over h for d>0, giving (r+1)d≤r(m−1)−1 by integrality; the bound is nonnegative even at m=2. In the construction a=1+floor((m−1)/(r+1)) lies in the agenda. The two ballots are single-peaked, their median peak is m−1, and all voters prefer a to y<a. For y>a the majority ballot gives y a score advantage of y−a and the minority ballot ranks y exactly y ranks below a; the aggregate gap in favor of a is (r+1)a−y>0. Thus the matching winner is unique, even when the two ballots coincide at small m. The loss of one unit in the integer numerator is necessary for uniqueness, and the maximum m−2 for m≥3 is first achieved when r≥m−1. The first disagreement counts follow by substitution.

## originality

PASS

The distinct claim is the exact maximum displacement on a finite candidate axis with a unique Borda winner for every m and every odd electorate 2r+1 with r≥1. Feld and Grofman explicitly exhibit finite ordinal single-peaked disagreement, indeed an attaining example for m=3, r=2; they then prove a different continuous-domain coincidence result. Neither they nor the closest checked catalogue or probabilistic results give the all-parameter sharp bound and threshold. This does not claim priority for disagreement or for the median-voter fact.

## value

PASS

The standard median outcome controls all pairwise majorities, but finite-agenda scoring can move the selected outcome along the axis. Existence examples give no scale for that movement. The bound and saturating profiles quantify the largest possible shift as a joint function of agenda and electorate size, distinguishing the unique-winner regime and giving a sharp threshold for near-end-to-end movement. This is meaningful finite-agenda information beyond a restatement of the median-voter theorem or Borda disagreement.
