# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS
The physical coupling is exact: each anchor's first proposal fixes whether its footprint ever contributes to coverage. The cyclic forbidden-run event is represented by a finite transfer matrix. A simple Perron root yields the series \(\log\Lambda=-q^\ell+q^{\ell+1}+O(q^{2\ell})\), and the remaining eigenvalues are \(O(q)\); substitution at the critical observation time gives the stated leading, positive, and sharp \(n^{-1/\ell}\) term. Exact rational enumeration and independent kinetic simulation support the proof, but do not replace its analytic steps. Scope is fixed \(\ell\), finite rings and a compact normalized-time window.

## Originality: PASS, with qualified priority risk
The Gumbel leading-order statement is an acknowledged earlier theorem of Campos and Teixeira. Krapivsky's original ring calculations concern accepted deposits and dynamics, not the evaluated finite-size time distribution. Full primary texts by Krapivsky and Campos–Teixeira, and the general structured-coupon paper of Falgas-Ravry et al., were inspected for the same parameters and stronger implications. No inspected result evaluates this sharp \(n^{-1/\ell}\) coefficient. Janson's 1988 discrete-circle coverage paper could not be read in full despite bounded lawful access attempts; its abstract addresses asymptotic distributions and it remains a specific second-order priority risk. A negative search is not proof of uniqueness.

## Scientific value: PASS
The finite-size completion window is a natural kinetic observable of irreversible random deposition, distinct from the expected number of successfully retained particles. The positive coefficient quantifies spatial overlap of rare uncovered sites, identifies the sharp slow correction \(n^{-1/\ell}\), and provides a concrete refinement of the established Gumbel law for finite substrates. The result is an exact all-length family, not an arbitrary finite enumeration or a renaming of an established formula. It does not claim extension to other deposition protocols or growing segment lengths.
