# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The total-energy eigenspaces comprise two one-dimensional endpoints and exactly \(d-1\) independent two-dimensional blocks. Each interior output excited population is a Rayleigh quotient bounded by the greater of two explicit incoming populations; energy-block swaps attain all maxima simultaneously. Symmetry of the input and energy-conserving unitary guarantees a diagonal qubit output. The temperature threshold, exact minimum dimension, all-input condition, and finite-truncation penalty follow by algebra from that bound. The asymptotic follows by expanding the unique root of \(q^d=2q-1\). The included program gave 1484 passing checks and does not stand in for the analytic argument.

## Originality

PASS, qualified best-knowledge. Singh, Saha and Sen (arXiv:2609.04457v1) show the thermal-qubit no-go and an activating thermal-qutrit example, establishing the three-level boundary case. Hu (arXiv:1807.02942v1) proves the infinite-bosonic-mode qubit reachability and optimal ideal \(eta\)-swap bound. The evaluated claim is neither of these ingredients: it is the exact optimum *at fixed finite ladder size*, the full input-temperature-dimensional activation phase diagram, and the minimum physical ladder size. Direct searches for equivalent finite-reservoir theorems and stronger implications did not identify coverage. A more general unidentified finite-dimensional thermal-operation bound remains a priority risk; the evidence does not prove exhaustive uniqueness.

## Value

PASS. Minimal finite environment dimension is a motivated resource requirement in the source problem, rather than an arbitrary matrix-size slice. The closed transition curve and its asymptotic quantify the finite thermal bath needed at any prescribed temperature, and the exact truncation penalty measures the operational difference from the ideal infinite reservoir. The finite-dimensional closed form and boundary are meaningful even without a broader general-reservoir theorem.

## Boundaries and independent status

The result is limited to a passive diagonal qubit, gap-matched finite Gibbs ladder, and one energy-preserving interaction. All-parameter proof is analytic; no finite test certifies priority or the infinite statement. No independent audit has been performed.
