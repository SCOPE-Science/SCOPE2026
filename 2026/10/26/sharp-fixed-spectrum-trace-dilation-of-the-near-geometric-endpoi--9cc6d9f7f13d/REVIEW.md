# Scientific review: sharp near-geometric trace dilation

## Correctness — PASS

The endpoint \(A\star_1B\) is exactly \(XX^*\) for \(X=A^{1/2}B^{1/2}A^{-1/2}\), whose square is similar to \(B\). The trace difference is therefore the squared Frobenius norm of its skew-adjoint part. For \(A=aP+b(I-P)\), this is precisely the off-diagonal Frobenius energy of \(B^{1/2}\) times \((a-b)^2/(ab)\). Ky Fan weak majorization bounds that energy by the paired spectral spread. The explicit balanced-pair construction attains equality for all \(n\) and ranks; connectedness of the unitary orbit fills the interval. The two-by-two commutator formula follows from Cayley–Hamilton. The standalone rational checker passed 2,950 exact comparisons, which are corroboration rather than proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Ibrahim–Huang, arXiv:2610.23736v1 (2026-10-16), establishes relevant qualitative log-majorization for the near-spectral endpoint and an explicit determinant-one planar near-spectral formula. The known endpoint \(A\star_1B\) is isospectral to that near-spectral endpoint. Neither the inspected statement nor its relevant proof supplies the proposed *exact commutator trace deficit and sharp full fixed-spectrum orbit trace interval*. Li–Mathias (1999), Theorem 2, already gives the optimal off-diagonal Hermitian-block majorization; this theorem claims no new block majorization, and credits that bound. Rather, it combines an exact quantitative identity for the newer matrix mean with the sharp known block inequality to determine the trace-dilation envelope. The result is a specific quantitative synthesis, not a claim that each component is independently new.

Only the opening page and abstract of Franco–Gan–Kim (2025), the introductory paper, were accessible. An equivalent trace identity or exact orbit claim might appear in its uninspected body or elsewhere. The priority conclusion is made under that explicit residual risk and does not certify uniqueness of discovery.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The nonlinear near-geometric endpoint has a qualitative log-majorization relation to its input. The result replaces that qualitative statement with a noncommutativity-sensitive **exact trace stability quantity in all dimensions** and a **complete, sharp unitary-orbit trace range** for two-level first factors. The optimal spectral matching consists of extreme pairs across opposing eigenspaces and works with unequal multiplicities. The result determines the exact worst-case endpoint trace expansion at fixed input spectra rather than a generic uniform estimate, and it has nontrivial dimension and rank dependence. No broad matrix-mean classification or infinite-dimensional theorem is claimed.

## Limitations and closest literature

The sharp optimization is for two-level first factors, not general multilevel factors. Li–Mathias (1999) covers the spectral-spread block bound, which is not independently new. The complete introductory 2025 paper was inaccessible; residual priority risk remains. Finite rational checks are not exhaustive, and independent external review was not undertaken.
