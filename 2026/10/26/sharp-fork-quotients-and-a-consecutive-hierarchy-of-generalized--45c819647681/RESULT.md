# Sharp fork quotients and a consecutive hierarchy of generalized Medvedev logics

## Finding
For each integer \(b\ge1\), let \(D_b\) be the finite rooted poset with bottom \(o\), top \(t\), and exactly \(b\) pairwise incomparable elements \(a_1,\ldots,a_b\) strictly between them. Write \(H_k\) for the rooted \(k\)-fork obtained from \(D_k\) by deleting \(t\), with \(k\ge2\). For \(m\ge1\), let \(P_{b,m}=D_b^m\setminus\{(t,\ldots,t)\}\), ordered coordinatewise, and let \(\mathcal L_b=\bigcap_{m\ge1}\operatorname{Log}(P_{b,m})\) be the corresponding generalized Medvedev logic.

**Sharp fork-quotient theorem.** For each \(b\ge1\), the largest \(k\ge2\) for which some generated subframe of some \(P_{b,m}\) admits an onto order-preserving back-lifting (\(p\)-)morphism to \(H_k\) is exactly \(\max\{b,2\}\). In particular, for every \(b\ge2\) and \(k>b\), the Jankov--de Jongh characteristic formula \(\chi(H_k)\) belongs to \(\mathcal L_b\), but does not belong to \(\mathcal L_k\).

**Consecutive strict hierarchy.** For every integer \(b\ge2\),
\[
\mathcal L_{b+1}\subsetneq\mathcal L_b.
\]
The already known finite-distributive-lattice characterization gives \(\mathcal L_1=\mathcal L_2=\mathsf{Med}\). Hence the complete order along this natural rooted-fan family is
\[
\mathsf{Med}=\mathcal L_1=\mathcal L_2\supsetneq\mathcal L_3\supsetneq\mathcal L_4\supsetneq\cdots.
\]
This resolves pairwise inclusion and equality within this family; it does **not** classify all generalized Medvedev logics.

## Assumptions and scope
An intuitionistic Kripke frame is a nonempty finite poset. For a poset \(P\), \(\operatorname{Log}(P)\) consists of all intuitionistic propositional formulas valid at every point under every persistent valuation. A generated subframe is an upward-closed subposet. A \(p\)-morphism \(f:P\to Q\) is order preserving and has the back-lifting property: for every \(x\in P\), \(z\in Q\) with \(f(x)\le z\), some \(y\ge x\) satisfies \(f(y)=z\). Every onto \(p\)-morphism considered below has nonempty domain and codomain. The statements concern all integers \(b,m\ge1\) and every fork arity \(k\ge2\), not just the tested finite cases.

## Proof
**1. The maximal-colour criterion.** Fix a finite poset \(P\) and a target fork \(H_k\) with \(k\ge2\). Write \(\operatorname{Max}(P)\) for the maximal points and \(M(x)=\{z\in\operatorname{Max}(P):x\le z\}\), always nonempty. An onto \(p\)-morphism \(P\to H_k\) exists exactly when one can colour \(\operatorname{Max}(P)\) with \(k\) colours so that for every \(x\in P\), the set of colours appearing in \(M(x)\) has either one element or all \(k\) elements, and at least one \(x\) sees all \(k\) colours.

For necessity, an order-preserving map sends every maximal point to a maximal point of the target: if the image were the fork root, back-lifting would require an extension with each maximal target value, contradicting maximality. If \(f(x)\) is a fork arm, order preservation forces all maximal extensions to have that arm's colour. If \(f(x)\) is the root, back-lifting requires a maximal extension of each colour. Conversely, given such a colouring, send \(x\) to the unique arm if \(M(x)\) is monochromatic, and to the root otherwise. Since \(M(y)\subseteq M(x)\) whenever \(x\le y\), this map preserves order. If its value is the root, all target arms occur on maximal extensions, so back-lifting holds; if its value is an arm, every extension has that same arm. The point seeing all colours establishes surjectivity. This proves the criterion without an algorithmic assumption.

**2. Geometry of punctured products.** A maximal point of \(P_{b,m}\) has exactly one coordinate equal to an arm \(a_j\) and every other coordinate equal to \(t\). For \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_m)\), define its active set \(S(x)=\{i:x_i\ne t\}\). Thus \(M(x)\) splits into \(|S(x)|\) coordinate groups, each containing at most \(b\) maximal points.

Suppose \(|S(x)|\ge2\), and choose any maximal extensions \(z,z'\ge x\) belonging to **different** coordinate groups. There is a point \(w\ge x\) with the two selected arm coordinates equal to those of \(z,z'\) and all other coordinates \(t\). Its maximal extensions are **exactly** \(z,z'\). Consequently, for an onto \(p\)-morphism to a fork with \(k\ge3\), those two maximal extensions must have the same arm colour: otherwise \(w\) would see exactly two distinct colours, contradicting Step 1. Fixing an extension from one group and comparing it with every extension in every other group shows that all maxima above \(x\) have one colour. Therefore no \(x\) with two or more active coordinates can map to the fork root when \(k\ge3\).

If instead \(|S(x)|=1\), there are at most \(b\) maximal extensions of \(x\). Thus, when \(k>\max\{b,2\}\), no point \(x\) can map to the root: points with at least two active coordinates see one colour, and points with one active coordinate see fewer than \(k\) maxima. Every nonempty upward-closed generated subframe \(S\subseteq P_{b,m}\) containing \(x\) also contains the entire upset \(x\uparrow\). If it admitted an onto \(p\)-morphism to \(H_k\), choose \(x\) in the preimage of the root; its restriction to \(x\uparrow\) would still be onto by back-lifting. The preceding obstruction gives a contradiction. This proves the upper bound uniformly in \(m\) and over all generated subframes.

**3. Sharpness.** If \(b\ge2\), then \(P_{b,1}=H_b\), so the identity map attains \(k=b\). When \(b=1\), \(D_1\) is the three-point chain. In \(P_{1,2}\), let \(a\) denote its middle point. Map points whose second coordinate is \(t\) to the first arm of \(H_2\), points whose first coordinate is \(t\) to the second arm, and all remaining points to the fork root; the removed pair \((t,t)\) prevents ambiguity. This is an onto \(p\)-morphism: every point assigned root lies below both maximal points \((a,t)\) and \((t,a)\), and points assigned either arm have only successors with that same image. Hence the sharp capacity is \(\max\{b,2\}\).

**4. From capacity to strict logical inclusion.** For \(c>b\ge2\), choose any surjection from the \(c\) middle arms of \(D_c\) onto the \(b\) arms of \(D_b\), preserving bottom and top. It is an onto \(p\)-morphism, and the top has exactly one preimage. Its coordinatewise power therefore restricts to an onto \(p\)-morphism \(P_{c,m}\to P_{b,m}\) for every \(m\ge1\). Formula validity is preserved under onto \(p\)-morphic images, giving \(\mathcal L_c\subseteq\mathcal L_b\).

For strictness, take the characteristic formula \(\chi(H_c)\). By the finite Jankov--de Jongh theorem, a frame refutes this formula exactly if it has a generated subframe with an onto \(p\)-morphism to \(H_c\). Step 2 rules this out in every \(P_{b,m}\), so \(\chi(H_c)\in\mathcal L_b\). But \(P_{c,1}=H_c\), whose identity witnesses refutation, so \(\chi(H_c)\notin\mathcal L_c\). In particular every consecutive pair \(c=b+1\) is strict. The equalities \(\mathcal L_1=\mathcal L_2=\mathsf{Med}\) follow from the established theorem that topless powers of any nontrivial finite distributive lattice characterize Medvedev logic, since \(D_1\) is a chain and \(D_2\) is the four-element Boolean lattice. This completes the proof.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_fan.py` with Python 3 from the same extracted directory. The checker independently constructs punctured products and their principal generated upsets, enumerates all possible colours on maximal points in small cases, applies the maximal-colour condition, and verifies each positive colouring directly against order preservation and the back condition. It also enumerates **all** point maps in five tiny cases rather than presupposing the colour characterization, and matches the two methods. For \(1\le b\le3\) and \(1\le m\le3\), the observed maximal achievable fork arities are \(0,2,2\) for \(b=1\) as \(m=1,2,3\), and \(b\) for all tested \(m\) when \(b\ge2\). Here a reported capacity \(0\) means no quotient to a fork of arity at least two. The checker prints `FAN_PRODUCT_CAPACITY_CHECK_OK`. The proof above, not finite testing, establishes the unbounded claims.

## Relationship to prior work
Han Xiao's *Non-finite Axiomatizability of Generalized Medvedev Logics* (arXiv:2606.31893, initially public 2026-06-30), Theorem 5.4, already constructs a strictly descending sequence of generalized Medvedev logics using the **geometrically spaced** fork widths \(1,4,16,\ldots\). Its Lemma 4.6 gives the general estimate \(\text{branching}<b2^{\text{depth}}\) for suitable \(p\)-morphic image obstructions; that estimate does not distinguish consecutive widths \(b\) and \(b+1\). The newly established exact fork-quotient capacity detects **every adjacent** width and determines inclusion across all members of this rooted-fan family. The paper explicitly asks for finer comparison criteria. Xiao's 2024 dissertation, Chapter 4.3, contains the same spaced-width construction. Grilletti's earlier characterization of Medvedev logic is used only for the known equality at widths one and two. The Jankov--de Jongh correspondence is classical and not claimed as new.

## Limitations
This establishes a sharp invariant and the full chain classification **within the single-parameter rooted-fan family**, not an antichain of generalized Medvedev logics, the full inclusion order for arbitrary finite rooted frames, or finite axiomatizability. The characteristic formulas are existence witnesses; no bounds on their symbol counts are derived. The calculation uses finite posets and ordinary intuitionistic Kripke semantics, not unrelated notions of Medvedev degrees. An older general theorem about finite-poset bounded morphisms could imply the capacity lemma; historical priority is not certain. Some external source documents were only partially accessible. Independent validation is not claimed.
