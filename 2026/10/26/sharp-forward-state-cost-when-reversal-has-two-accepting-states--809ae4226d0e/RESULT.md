# Sharp forward-state cost when reversal has two accepting states

## Finding

For a regular language \(L\), write \(\operatorname{sc}(L)\) for the state count of its minimal complete deterministic finite automaton (DFA), \(\operatorname{asc}(L)\) for its number of accepting states, and \(L^R\) for word reversal. A **permutation DFA** is a complete DFA in which every letter permutes the states. For every integer \(m\geq2\),

\[
\min\{\operatorname{sc}(L): L\text{ is recognized by a permutation DFA},\ \operatorname{asc}(L)=m,\ \operatorname{asc}(L^R)=2\}
=\binom{m+1}{2}.
\]

Moreover, equality holds precisely for the following class of minimal DFAs, up to relabeling states and alphabet: choose a set \(V\) of \(m+1\) vertices and a group \(H\leq\operatorname{Sym}(V)\) transitive on unordered pairs of distinct vertices (a **2-homogeneous group**). Use the edges \(\binom{V}{2}\) as states; the input letters act on edges via permutations of \(V\) generating \(H\); the initial state is any edge; and the final states are exactly the edges incident to one fixed vertex. In every equality case the reversed minimal DFA has exactly \(m+1\) states. The bound is already attained over a binary alphabet.

## Assumptions and scope

All automata are finite, deterministic and complete; the alphabet is finite and nonempty. When a regular language is recognized by a permutation automaton, its unique minimal complete DFA is itself a permutation automaton (minimization gives a permutation action on state-equivalence classes). Accepting-state complexity means the number of final states of this **minimal** DFA, not the number in an arbitrary nonminimal presentation. The theorem concerns the joint requirements \(\operatorname{asc}(L)=m\) and \(\operatorname{asc}(L^R)=2\), for every integer \(m\geq2\), without fixing the alphabet size in the lower bound.

## Proof

**Orbit incidence and the lower bound.** Let \(A=(Q,\Sigma,\delta,q_0,F)\) be the minimal permutation DFA for a qualifying language, with \(N=|Q|\) and \(|F|=m\). The finite group \(G\) generated by the letter permutations acts transitively on \(Q\), because every state is reachable from \(q_0\); inverses of letters are positive powers of those letters.

Let \(\mathcal B=\{gF:g\in G\}\) be the orbit of the \(m\)-element final set, and put \(t=|\mathcal B|\). Reversing the transitions and applying the subset construction gives as its reachable states exactly the members of \(\mathcal B\): the reversal letters act by inverse images and generate the same group on subsets. These states are pairwise distinguishable. Indeed, if \(B\neq B'\), choose \(q\in B\triangle B'\), then choose a positive word \(w\) taking \(q_0\) to \(q\). Reading the reversed word \(w^R\) from subset states \(B,B'\) makes exactly one accepting, since reversal acceptance tests membership of \(q_0\). Thus this reachable subset automaton is already the minimal DFA of \(L^R\).

The number of reverse accepting states is the number of blocks containing \(q_0\), hence is \(2\) by hypothesis. Transitivity of \(G\) implies that **every** \(q\in Q\) belongs to exactly two blocks of \(\mathcal B\). Define its incidence signature

\[
I(q)=\{B\in\mathcal B:q\in B\}\in\binom{\mathcal B}{2}.
\]

The map \(q\mapsto I(q)\) is injective: equal signatures imply that for each \(g\in G\), either both states or neither belong to \(g^{-1}F\), so no input word can distinguish them in \(A\), contradicting minimality. Counting point-block incidences now yields

\[
2N=tm,\qquad N\leq\binom{t}{2},\qquad m\leq t-1,\qquad
N\geq\frac{m(m+1)}2.
\]

**Rigidity at equality.** If \(N=\binom{m+1}{2}\), the incidence equation gives \(t=m+1\), and the injective signature map is a bijection \(Q\longrightarrow\binom{\mathcal B}{2}\). It carries \(F\) exactly to the pairs containing the vertex \(F\in\mathcal B\), because \(q\in F\iff F\in I(q)\). For \(g\in G\), one has \(I(gq)=gI(q)\); hence the action on \(Q\) becomes the edge action of the permutation group induced by \(G\) on \(\mathcal B\). Since \(G\) is transitive on \(Q\), the induced group is transitive on all edges: it is 2-homogeneous. It is faithful, since a permutation fixing every vertex of \(\mathcal B\) fixes every signature and hence every state of \(Q\). The initial state becomes some edge, and \(\mathcal B\) comprises exactly \(m+1\) reverse states. This proves necessity of the stated form.

**Converse and attainment.** Conversely, let \(V\) have \(m+1\ge3\) vertices, let \(H\) be 2-homogeneous, and construct the edge-action DFA with any initial edge \(e_0\) and a star \(F\) centered at \(v_0\) as its accepting set. All edges are reachable. The group is vertex-transitive, and for distinct edges \(e,e'\), choosing \(v\in e\setminus e'\) and sending \(v\) to \(v_0\) distinguishes \(e,e'\); thus the forward DFA is minimal and has \(|F|=m\) accepting states. The orbit of the star \(F\) is the family of all \(m+1\) vertex-stars, which are distinct. Precisely two stars contain the initial edge \(e_0\). They are distinguishable in the reversed DFA: for different centers \(v,w\), choose an edge containing \(v\) but not \(w\), and use edge transitivity to map \(e_0\) to this edge. Consequently \(\operatorname{asc}(L^R)=2\) and the reversed minimal DFA has \(m+1\) states. Taking \(H=\operatorname{Sym}(V)\), generated by a full cycle and one adjacent transposition, gives an explicit binary-alphabet witness for every \(m\ge2\).

## Verification

The standalone file `verify.py` constructs these two-letter edge-action automata, minimizes both forward and reversed reachable DFAs by partition refinement, and checks \(m=2,\ldots,12\). It additionally exhausts all ordered binary-letter permutation-DFA transition pairs and final sets at state counts below the bound for \(m\in\{2,3\}\), after fixing the initial state. Its expected terminal line is `VERIFY_OK`. This finite verification is corroborative only; the incidence proof establishes the result for all \(m\ge2\).

## Relationship to prior work

Rauch and Holzer (2022, arXiv:2208.14731v1, Section 3.6) introduced the accepting-state spectrum for reversal of permutation automata, excluded the reverse accepting-state count one when the forward count exceeds one, and posed the full attainable-spectrum conjecture. German (2026, arXiv:2605.13385v1, Section 3) subsequently proved that conjecture, using a construction with \(\binom{m+\alpha-1}{\alpha}\) forward states for prescribed forward and reversed accepting-state counts \(m,\alpha\ge2\). At \(\alpha=2\) this supplies the sharp **upper** bound \(\binom{m+1}{2}\). The result here establishes the complementary **universal lower bound** and classifies every equality case by 2-homogeneous edge actions. Existence of a witness alone does not imply that no smaller witness exists or that all optimizers have this form.

## Limitations

The exact minimum and optimizer classification are for reverse accepting-state count precisely two. The proof's incidence method gives necessary inequalities for larger reverse counts but not a general sharp formula. The theorem does not classify individual 2-homogeneous groups or their generating sets, and it does not optimize input-word lengths. The primary literature comparison does not rule out unknown unindexed treatments of this minimum-state refinement. No independent external audit has been performed.
