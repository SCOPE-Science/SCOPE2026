Uniformly retaining \(k\ge6\) of \(n\) taxa from any noncaterpillar unrooted binary phylogeny reveals its noncaterpillar shape with probability at least \((k)_4/(n)_4\). For every \(k<n\) this worst case is attained by exactly one tree shape, a four-leaf side fork attached to a long comb, giving sharp six-taxon detection costs of order \(n^4\).
