# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. Clebsch–Gordan decomposes every character product into a multiplicity-one step-two progression. Three overlap counts and the two single-character fourth moments yield an exact complex-coefficient quartic identity. For odd differences, elementary maximization of a one-variable quadratic covers both endpoints and all equality phases; for even differences, phase convexity forces alignment, and Descartes' sign-change rule proves exactly one positive critical root and no boundary optimizer. The standalone verifier separately assembles fusion coefficients and symbolically verifies the derivative. Finite enumerations do not replace the analytic arguments.

## Originality
PASS under current accessible evidence, not independent priority certification. A directly relevant published high-weight parity-packet result supplies the leading asymptotic for fixed offsets, and explicitly limits its assertion to leading order; it neither evaluates arbitrary finite character pairs nor identifies the finite-dimensional dimension-ratio threshold or exact same-parity root. The primary 2019 compact-group Fourier paper and the 1996 noncentral SU(2) norm paper do not imply those statements. published-finding corpus semantic searches by group, pair, fourth norm, parity, and equivalent Chebyshev parametrization did not return a stronger exact theorem. The possibility of an older equivalent two-Chebyshev formulation remains open.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The sharp \(\ell^2\to L^4\) norm of a two-mode central character subspace is a basic extremal norm invariant, and the answer applies to every ordered pair of weights rather than a selected example. It exposes a qualitative representation-theoretic parity dichotomy: for odd separations a sharp dimension-ratio threshold removes the lower mode, while for even separations a nonzero lower mode is always forced regardless of spacing. The result additionally classifies all complex extremizers. No general multi-mode conclusion is claimed.
