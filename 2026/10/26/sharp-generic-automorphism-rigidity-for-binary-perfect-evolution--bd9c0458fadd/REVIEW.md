# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
A matrix-invertible evolution algebra has the squaring products as a basis. Orthogonal images force every automorphism to be monomial, hence a permutation over the two-element field. An explicit centralizer calculation proves the exact transposition fixed probability. The ordered-pair orbit codimension bound proves that all nontranspositions have negligible combined probability; pairwise transposition intersections are negligible. This establishes both the sharp asymptotic and the conditional cyclic-group law. The accompanying independent implementation exhaustively checks matrices through dimension four; finite checks are not the asymptotic proof.

## Originality — PASS within investigated coverage
Elduque–Labra establish the graph-automorphism reduction; Sriwongsa–Zou establish structural automorphism-group facts; Wei–Zou establish Burnside orbit enumerations for isomorphism classes; Costoya–Mayorga–Viruel address realizable permutation representations. Those broader structural inputs are prior work. Examined statements did not already identify the probability for uniform invertible labelled binary matrices or the transposition-dominated sharp law. General orbit enumeration may implicitly encode some moments, and inaccessible or unexamined matrix-asymmetry literature remains a substantive priority risk.

## Value — PASS
The sharp first-order exceptional probability gives an intrinsic generic-rigidity measurement of perfect evolution algebras. Conditional identification of the unique symmetry provides structural information rather than a mere event bound, and conditioning on invertibility changes the leading coefficient from the unconstrained matrix model.

## Limitations
The field is precisely the two-element field, sampling is matrix-uniform on invertibles, and the statements are asymptotic in dimension. Nothing is claimed about uniformly distributed isomorphism classes or arbitrary finite fields. Independent historical-priority checking remains open.
