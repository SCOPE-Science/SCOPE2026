# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

With the Fourier normalization in the proof, Hermite modes \(0,4,8\) have eigenvalue \(+1\). Origin-zero data reduce exactly to \(u[A(u-3)+Bp(u)]\), and positivity of the highest term forces \(B>0\) or the simpler \(B=0,A>0\) case. The cubic \(2z^3-23z^2+84z-105\) has one real root in \((11/2,6)\), by its negative discriminant and endpoint signs. The exact factorization provides one simple boundary and one double outer contact. Strict negative certificates at the boundary for \(a>\alpha_*\) and at the outer contact for \(a<\alpha_*\) prove global sharpness and uniqueness. A positive Gaussian shift strictly decreases the last-sign radius for strict-negative-origin data. The packaged SymPy checker verifies the critical identities; it does not replace these logical arguments.

## Originality — PASS (best knowledge)

Full-text inspection of Cohn–Dong–Gonçalves (arXiv:2210.01684, Theorems 1.2 and 2.1) shows asymptotic dimension growth and a general Laguerre lower bound, not an exact \(d=1\) three-mode last-sign radius or equality classification. Full-text inspection of Gonçalves–Oliveira e Silva–Steinerberger (arXiv:1602.03366, Section 4.2) identifies a four-mode numerical construction; the stronger larger-packet upper bound does not determine the smaller-packet sharp optimum. Targeted searches of Hermite sign-uncertainty aliases and mathematical-result records found no equivalent stated theorem. Absence from search is not a priority proof; unindexed equivalent work remains a residual risk.

This is an exact optimization of the first nontrivial three-mode Fourier-invariant sign-uncertainty cutoff, with all extremizers.
