# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof computes the product fibers \(bc=z\) over each prime-power ring, obtains the exact root-count identity, and independently evaluates the normalized limit by a closest-root valuation integral. The odd-prime and dyadic proximity cases are verified explicitly, including repeated roots, nonsquare unit discriminants, all parity boundaries, and the strict criteria for each extremum. The standalone direct enumerator agrees in 500 finite configurations, with 22 additional rational limit checks. Finite checks do not substitute for the general valuation proof.

**Originality — PASS, with risk.** Browning–Gundlach–Sawin address global counts and Fourier estimates over finite fields; Guria addresses a global weighted quadratic matrix count; Reiner's theorem enumerates matrices over finite fields. At primes of squarefree reduction, the corresponding local density follows from finite-field counts and smooth lifting and is not claimed new. The present contribution is the full prime-power root formula and complete ramified and dyadic limiting classification, with sharp splitting and rootlessness criteria. Searches for equivalent local matrix-fiber densities identified no directly covering result. An older local-ring enumeration or a general quadratic-form local-density formula may imply the conclusion and remains the most material risk.

**Value — PASS.** These fixed-characteristic-polynomial matrix fibers are the natural local congruence data for matrix counting. The result determines both extremal possible local masses and their precise structural equality cases, with a complete exceptional prime-two stratification rather than just residue-field statistics. All exponents and all quadratic coefficients are covered, so it is a general local input rather than a sparse numerical example.

**Limitations.** No global matrix-counting asymptotic is asserted. A potential implication from more general local-density work remains unresolved; the earlier finite-field regime is explicitly excluded from novelty claims.
