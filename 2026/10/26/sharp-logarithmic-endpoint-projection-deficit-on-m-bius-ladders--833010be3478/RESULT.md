# Sharp logarithmic endpoint-projection deficit on Möbius ladders

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge4\), let \(M_{2n}\) be the unit-edge Möbius ladder, formed by adjoining the \(n\) antipodal chords to the cycle on \(2n\) vertices. Let \(Q_n\) be the Euclidean orthogonal projection onto the real cut space in edge coordinates and let \(C_n=I-Q_n\) be the projection onto the cycle space. Both are viewed on real \(\ell_1^{3n}\); their \(\ell_\infty\) norms agree with their \(\ell_1\) norms by matrix symmetry.

Define
\[
q=2-\sqrt3,\qquad g_j=\frac{q^j-q^{n-j}}{2\sqrt3(1+q^n)}\quad(0\le j\le n),\qquad h=\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor,
\]
\[
S_n=\sum_{j=1}^{h}\left(g_j-\frac{1}{2n}\right)_+,\qquad (t)_+=\max(t,0).
\]
Then the exact endpoint norms are
\[
\boxed{\|Q_n\|_{1\to1}=\|Q_n\|_{\infty\to\infty}=2-\frac2n+2g_0+4S_n,}
\]
\[
\boxed{\|C_n\|_{1\to1}=\|C_n\|_{\infty\to\infty}=2-\frac1n+2g_1+4S_n.}
\]
For every \(n\ge4\), the maximum absolute column sum of each projector is attained exactly at the rim-edge columns, and strictly exceeds every chord-edge column. Moreover, the finite-size corrections have the **sharp logarithmic limits**
\[
\boxed{\|Q_n\|=3-\frac{2\log n}{n\log(2+\sqrt3)}+O(n^{-1}),}
\qquad
\boxed{\|C_n\|=2+\frac1{\sqrt3}-\frac{2\log n}{n\log(2+\sqrt3)}+O(n^{-1}).}
\]
In particular, both leading deficits are logarithmic despite exponential decay in the antipodal-odd Green kernel. The cut projection has the larger norm throughout the family.

## Assumptions and scope

Vertices are \(0,\ldots,2n-1\) modulo \(2n\). Rim edges join \(i\) to \(i+1\), and chords join \(i\) to \(i+n\) for \(0\le i<n\). Give every edge unit resistance, choose an arbitrary orientation, and let \(B\) be the vertex-edge incidence matrix. Write \(L=BB^{\mathsf T}\) and let \(L^+\) be its Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse. The canonical orthogonal cut projection is \(Q_n=B^{\mathsf T}L^+B\). Edge orientations can change matrix signs but not absolute column sums. No assertion is made about minimum norms over arbitrary nonorthogonal projections.

The \(n=3\) graph is \(K_{3,3}\), already part of extensively studied complete-bipartite graph families. The formulas remain algebraically valid there but the two edge orbits tie; the strict-orbit theorem starts at \(n=4\).

## Proof

**1. Antipodal Green kernel.** Put \(\alpha=(2\sqrt3)^{-1}\) and extend \(g_j\) from the displayed formula to all integer indices by \(g_{j+n}=-g_j\) and \(g_{-j}=g_j\). Direct substitution using \(q+q^{-1}=4\) shows
\[
4g_0-2g_1=1,\qquad 4g_j-g_{j-1}-g_{j+1}=0\quad(1\le j<n),\qquad g_n=-g_0.
\]
Hence \(g\) is the antipodal-odd potential generated by the unit chord source \(\delta_0-\delta_n\). The sequence is strictly decreasing from \(g_0>0\) to \(-g_0\), and \(g_{n-j}=-g_j\). For \(1\le j\le h\), \(g_j>0\), and any remaining middle term is zero.

**2. A rim source.** Drive unit current into vertex \(0\) and out of vertex \(1\). Decompose that source into antipodal-even and antipodal-odd parts. In the even sector, the rim currents in each copy of the length-\(n\) quotient cycle are \((n-1)/(2n)\) on the source edge and \(-1/(2n)\) on all other quotient edges; chords carry no even current. In the odd sector, the vertex potential is \((g_j-g_{j-1})/2\), and the current on rim edge \(j\to j+1\) is \((2g_j-g_{j-1}-g_{j+1})/2\). This equals \(g_0-g_1\) for \(j=0\) and \(-g_j\) for \(1\le j<n\). The antipodal rim currents have opposite odd parts. For \(a,b\in\mathbb R\), \(|a+b|+|a-b|=2\max(|a|,|b|)\). Since \(g_0-g_1<g_0<1/3\le(n-1)/(2n)\), the sum of absolute currents along all rim edges is
\[
\frac{2(n-1)}n+4\sum_{j=1}^h\left(g_j-\frac1{2n}\right)_+.
\]
The chords carry the currents \(g_j-g_{j-1}\) for \(0\le j<n\), with telescoping absolute sum \(2g_0\), proving the stated rim-column cut norm.

The original rim edge carries current \(R_{\mathrm{rim}}=(n-1)/(2n)+g_0-g_1\). For any real orthogonal projection \(Q\) and its complement \(I-Q\), the absolute column sums are related by \(\|C_{\cdot e}\|_1=1+\|Q_{\cdot e}\|_1-2Q_{ee}\) provided \(0\le Q_{ee}\le1\). Applying this identity gives the rim-column cycle expression \(2-1/n+2g_1+4S_n\).

**3. Chord columns and strict orbit comparison.** A chord unit source has potential \(g_j\). Along the two opposite rim paths its total absolute current is \(4g_0\). The chords themselves contribute \(2\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}|g_j|=2g_0+4\sum_{j=1}^h g_j\). Consequently, writing \(T_n=\sum_{j=1}^{h}g_j\), the two absolute chord-column sums are exactly
\[
Q_{\mathrm{chord}}=6g_0+4T_n,\qquad C_{\mathrm{chord}}=1+2g_0+4T_n.
\]
Using \(0<g_j<\alpha q^j\) gives \(Q_{\mathrm{chord}}<1+2/\sqrt3\) and \(C_{\mathrm{chord}}<2\). For \(n\ge5\), the rim cut expression is at least \(2(n-1)/n+2g_0\ge359/165>1+2/\sqrt3\), because \(g_0\) increases with \(n\). At \(n=4\) the exact cut-column values are \(29/14\) (rim) and \(2\) (chord).

The quantity \(g_1\) increases with \(n\) and \(g_1\) at \(n=7\) equals \(19/246>1/14\). Hence for \(n\ge7\) the first positive-part term is active, and the rim cycle expression is at least \(2+6g_1-3/n>2>C_{\mathrm{chord}}\). The remaining cases are exact rational: at \(n=4\), cycle columns give \(53/28>13/7\); at \(n=5\), \(322/165>64/33\); at \(n=6\), \(155/78>51/26\). Rotations preserve each edge orbit. Thus both global norms are exactly the rim formulas. Further, \(g_0-g_1> (1-1/\sqrt3)/2>1/(2n)\), giving \(R_{\mathrm{rim}}>1/2\) and \(\|Q_n\|>\|C_n\|\).

**4. Sharp logarithmic defect.** Let \(L_n\) be the largest index \(1\le j\le h\) for which \(g_j>1/(2n)\), with \(L_n=0\) when none exists. Strict decrease of \(g_j\) makes the positive part an initial interval, so
\[
\|Q_n\|=2-\frac2n+2g_0+4\sum_{j=1}^{L_n}g_j-\frac{2L_n}{n}.
\]
For \(j=O(\log n)\), the exact formula gives \(g_j=\alpha q^j(1+O(q^{n-2j}))\); comparison with \(1/(2n)\) yields
\[
L_n=\frac{\log n}{\log(1/q)}+O(1).
\]
Furthermore \(q^{L_n}=O(1/n)\), and the geometric-series identities \(2\alpha+4\alpha q/(1-q)=1\) and \(q+q^{-1}=4\) show
\[
2g_0+4\sum_{j=1}^{L_n}g_j=1+O(n^{-1}).
\]
Since \(\log(1/q)=\log(2+\sqrt3)\), the asserted cut norm asymptotic follows. Finally \(R_{\mathrm{rim}}=1/2+(1-1/\sqrt3)/2+O(n^{-1})\), so the complementary-column identity gives the cycle norm asymptotic with the **same** logarithmic coefficient. All \(O(\cdot)\) constants here are absolute.

## Verification

The attached standalone `verify_mobius.py` builds the incidence matrix and reduced graph Laplacian, computes the exact-rational cut projection column by column, checks orthogonal-projector identities, and compares all rim and chord columns with the independent recurrence definition of \(g_j\). It passed for each \(n\) from \(3\) to \(13\): **264 edge columns** in total. A finite check is not a proof of the all-parameter statement, which instead follows from the Green-kernel identities and orbit inequalities above.

The real sequence \(q^j\) in the closed form is algebraic; the checker uses a rational recurrence so no floating-point approximation enters the exact comparisons. An additional floating-point printout illustrates convergence of the logarithmic ratio but is not used to certify it.

## Relationship to prior work

Dilworth, Kutzarova, and Ostrovskii (arXiv:2305.12582, first posted 2023-05-21; primary MSC 46B04) studied invariant projections onto graph cycle spaces, with detailed examples centered on tori and Hamming graphs. Their framework motivates the operator norms studied here. Azimi and Farrokhi (arXiv:2306.10497, first posted 2023-06-18) **already determined the full Moore–Penrose inverses for Möbius ladders**, including every incidence-pseudoinverse entry and all effective resistances (Section 5). The Green-kernel data and hence the canonical projector itself are therefore not claimed to be newly discovered. What is derived here beyond those underlying formulas is a global absolute-column-sum maximizer classification, an exact positive-part norm expression, and the sharp \((\log n)/n\) defect with explicit leading coefficient. This requires tracking a moving absolute-value sign threshold rather than directly reading off the preexisting resistance formula. No uniqueness or independence relative to all possible prior sources is asserted.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns canonical Euclidean orthogonal cut/cycle projections acting with real \(\ell_1\) or \(\ell_\infty\) edge norms. It is not a theorem about minimal projection constants over all bounded projections, nor about weighted ladders or their Lipschitz-free Banach–Mazur distances. The prior pseudoinverse theorem formally contains sufficient data to recover these endpoint sums after additional analysis. Therefore equivalent norm conclusions in other graph/electrical-network literature remain a genuine residual bibliographic risk. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
