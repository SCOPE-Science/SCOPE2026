# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof explicitly covers every integer \(q\ge4\) and every induced class of minimum degree at least \(q\). The star-neighbourhood double count yields the optimal lower bound and rigid equality case; the constructed pair-and-triple lifted partition attains the matching upper bound. All nonstandard inequalities and the rectangle equality case have been verified. The finite checker tests partitions and rectangle counts for \(4\le q\le14\) and fully enumerates small subsets in the separate \(q=3\) boundary case. Enumeration is not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk
Bujtás et al., arXiv:2608.27669v2, Proposition 14 and Open problem 2, provide a general product construction and explicitly ask for imbalanced 3D values, rather than this formula. Lu, arXiv:2609.31674v1, Theorem 1, determines balanced odd-dimensional Hamming values and §7 calls for unequal-order results. Lu’s Lemma 4 **already implies the 2q minimum-size lower bound** by embedding the imbalanced graph in the balanced q-ary three-cube; this antecedent is credited, and originality is limited to the attaining imbalanced partition and the rigid minimum-core equality classification/census. Six actual research-record searches and comparison to accessible earlier claims showed no equivalent \((3,q,q)\) theorem for \(q\ge4\). The 2012 alliance-partition publication was inspected only at abstract level, so alternate historical coverage remains a risk. Lack of a search result is not itself a proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS
This is an exact infinite-family answer to a directly motivated unequal-factor problem. The exact imbalanced partition and the sharp equality characterization/count of minimum cores provide information not stated or implied by the cited known size lower bound alone.
