# Sharp moment rates for multi-step forecast averages

## Finding

Let \(K_N\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\) be deterministic with \(K_N/N\to0\). For each \(N\), let \((\mathcal F_{N,n})_{n\in\mathbb Z}\) be a filtration and let \(Y_{N,1},\ldots,Y_{N,N}\) be adapted, integrable real random variables. Set
\[
T_N=\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N\left(Y_{N,n}-\mathbb E[Y_{N,n}\mid\mathcal F_{N,n-K_N}]\right).
\]
If the family \(\{Y_{N,n}:N\ge1,\ 1\le n\le N\}\) is uniformly integrable, then \(T_N\to0\) in \(L^1\). More quantitatively, for every \(p>1\), if \(\sup_{N,n}\mathbb E|Y_{N,n}|^p\le M<\infty\), then
\[
\|T_N\|_p\le C_p M^{1/p}\left(\frac{K_N}{N}\right)^{\alpha_p},
\qquad
\alpha_p=\min\left\{\frac12,1-\frac1p\right\},
\]
where \(C_p\) depends only on \(p\). The exponent \(\alpha_p\) is sharp in order over this moment-bounded class: for \(N=K_Nm\), symmetric sparse-spike blocks attain fluctuations of order \(m^{-(1-1/p)}\) with probability tending to \(e^{-1}\) when \(1<p<2\), and Rademacher blocks attain order \(m^{-1/2}\) with probability bounded away from zero when \(p\ge2\). A uniform first-moment bound alone does not ensure convergence: sparse-spike blocks with amplitude \(m\) give \(|T_N|=1\) with probability tending to \(e^{-1}\).

The conclusion is an \(L^1\) law of large numbers under uniform integrability, together with the sharp order of the \(L^p\) rate when a uniform \(p\)-moment is available. Uniform integrability is a sufficient condition here; no claim is made that it is necessary for every individual sequence.

## Assumptions and scope

- \(K_N\) is deterministic, integer-valued, and \(1\le K_N\le N\); the regime of interest is \(K_N/N\to0\).
- The filtration is indexed over all integers so \(\mathcal F_{N,n-K_N}\) is defined even for \(n\le K_N\).
- The upper bounds require no independence or identical-distribution assumptions.
- The quantitative statement assumes \(p>1\) and a common bound \(M\) on all \(p\)-th moments. Constants \(C_p\) are finite but not optimized.
- Sharpness is an order statement over triangular arrays, demonstrated along subsequences with \(N=K_Nm\).

## Proof

Write
\[
D_{N,n}=Y_{N,n}-\mathbb E[Y_{N,n}\mid\mathcal F_{N,n-K_N}].
\]
Then \(D_{N,n}\) is \(\mathcal F_{N,n}\)-measurable and
\(\mathbb E[D_{N,n}\mid\mathcal F_{N,n-K_N}]=0\). Fix \(N\) and abbreviate \(K=K_N\). For each residue \(r\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\), the sequence
\[
D_{N,r},D_{N,r+K},D_{N,r+2K},\ldots
\]
(up to index \(N\)) is a martingale-difference sequence with respect to the subsequence filtration \(\mathcal F_{N,r+jK}\), because the conditioning sigma-algebra for each term is the preceding sigma-algebra in that subsequence.

For \(p>1\), conditional Jensen and the triangle inequality give
\[
\|D_{N,n}\|_p\le \|Y_{N,n}\|_p+
\|\mathbb E[Y_{N,n}\mid\mathcal F_{N,n-K}]\|_p
\le 2M^{1/p}.
\]
If \(1<p\le2\), the martingale \(L^p\) inequality yields, for a residue class containing \(m_r\) terms,
\[
\left\|\sum_jD_{N,r+jK}\right\|_p
\le C_p M^{1/p}m_r^{1/p}.
\]
If \(p\ge2\), the martingale Rosenthal inequality yields
\[
\left\|\sum_jD_{N,r+jK}\right\|_p
\le C_p M^{1/p}\bigl(m_r^{1/2}+m_r^{1/p}\bigr)
\le C'_p M^{1/p}m_r^{1/2}.
\]
Every \(m_r\le\lceil N/K\rceil\le2N/K\). Summing the \(K\) residue-class sums by Minkowski's inequality and dividing by \(N\) proves
\[
\|T_N\|_p\le C_pM^{1/p}(K/N)^{1-1/p}\quad(1<p\le2),
\]
and
\[
\|T_N\|_p\le C_pM^{1/p}(K/N)^{1/2}\quad(p\ge2).
\]

For the uniform-integrability conclusion, clip each variable at level \(B\):
\[
Y_{N,n}^{(B)}=\max\{-B,\min(Y_{N,n},B)\},\qquad
R_{N,n}=Y_{N,n}-Y_{N,n}^{(B)}.
\]
Let \(T_N^{(B)}\) be the corresponding centered forecast average formed from \(Y^{(B)}\). The bounded multi-step law of large numbers of Vovk applies after scaling by \(B\), so \(T_N^{(B)}\to0\) in probability for each fixed \(B\), and then in \(L^1\) because \(|T_N^{(B)}|\le2B\). Moreover,
\[
\mathbb E|T_N-T_N^{(B)}|
\le \frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N\left(\mathbb E|R_{N,n}|+
\mathbb E\left|\mathbb E[R_{N,n}\mid\mathcal F_{N,n-K}]\right|\right)
\le2\sup_{N,n}\mathbb E|R_{N,n}|.
\]
The last bound tends to zero as \(B\to\infty\) by uniform integrability. First taking \(N\to\infty\), then \(B\to\infty\), proves \(T_N\to0\) in \(L^1\).

To show sharpness, take \(N=Km\), independent centered block variables \(X_1,\ldots,X_m\), and set \(Y_{(j-1)K+r}=X_j\) for \(1\le r\le K\). With the filtration generated by past blocks, each \(Y_n\) is independent of \(\mathcal F_{n-K}\), so the conditional forecast is zero and
\[
T_N=\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m X_j.
\]
For \(1<p<2\), choose \(\Pr(X_j=m^{1/p})=\Pr(X_j=-m^{1/p})=1/(2m)\) and \(\Pr(X_j=0)=1-1/m\). Then \(\mathbb E|X_j|^p=1\), and the event of exactly one nonzero block has probability \((1-1/m)^{m-1}\to e^{-1}\); on that event \(|T_N|=m^{-(1-1/p)}\). For \(p\ge2\), choose Rademacher \(X_j\); the central limit theorem gives \(\sqrt m\,T_N\Rightarrow N(0,1)\), so the \(m^{-1/2}\) order cannot be improved uniformly. For only a first-moment bound, use the same sparse construction with amplitude \(m\); then \(\mathbb E|X_j|=1\) and \(|T_N|=1\) on the exactly-one-spike event, whose probability again tends to \(e^{-1}\).

## Verification

The proof uses the martingale-difference property on residue classes, standard martingale \(L^p\)/Rosenthal inequalities, truncation under uniform integrability, and explicit block examples. All bounds are analytic; no finite computation is being used as evidence for an infinite statement. The result does not optimize \(C_p\), establish necessity of uniform integrability, or address strong laws, iterated-logarithm laws, central limit theorems, or random prediction horizons.

## Relationship to prior work

Vovk's *A law of large numbers for predicting several steps ahead* proves the bounded-variable version and explicitly lists removal of the uniform-boundedness assumption as a direction for further research. The result here supplies an \(L^1\) extension under uniform integrability and a sharp \(p\)-dependent rate with matching lower examples. The martingale inequalities used in the proof are classical ingredients; they are not claimed as new.

## Limitations

The constants \(C_p\) are unspecified and not optimized. Uniform integrability is sufficient, not asserted to be necessary for each sequence. The lower examples establish sharpness of the rate exponent and failure under a uniform first-moment bound, not exact minimax tail probabilities at every finite \(N\).
