# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The finite generator is explicitly column-conservative and reversible. The Dirichlet inverse exists by connectivity. The harmonic extension formula follows by applying detailed balance to the Schur extension equations. The Gram matrix is positive definite, so its determinant is positive. Its negative inverse off-diagonal is exactly the stated moment quotient. Cauchy–Schwarz establishes the constant-harmonic-data optimizer, the one-variable derivative has one positive optimum, and an explicit connected conductance network attains it. The strong maximum principle establishes the sign boundary. Rare-state divergence follows from the attained closed formula. A separate exact-rational implementation checks the identities, but is not substituted for their universal proofs.

## Originality — PASS (subject to independent priority verification)
The primary arXiv paper, Iwata–Oki–Sakaue, Sections 3.1–3.3 and Appendix C, **already defines Type A/B**, identifies the Type B weighted orthogonal projection, and states that it can map a probability vector outside the simplex; these are cited, not claimed as new. Its error and spectrum results do not provide the explicit harmonic-moment cross-terminal entry, an attained sharp stationary-mass bound over all detailed-balance networks, or the rare-state asymptotic divergence. The subsequent arXiv computational implementation emphasizes Type A rather than sharp Type B coefficient extrema. General graph Dirichlet/Kron reduction theory is related and may conceal equivalent formulations. Targeted mathematical-record searches found no corresponding result; this is evidence rather than proof of publication priority. The 2026 journal rendering and wider specialized matrix-positivity literature remain residual risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The originating algorithm explicitly needs a nonlinear simplex projection because the raw Type B output can become negative; quantitatively diagnosing exactly how severe this can be is a motivated structural question. The attained bound depends on only three stationary masses, is valid uniformly across all reversible kinetic topologies and rates, and exhibits an unbounded rare-state amplification even for rank-two retained subspaces and 3-state systems. This separates weighted-Hilbert stability from entrywise probability validity and supplies a sharp stress test for RCMC preprocessing and probability correction. It is not merely a rephrasing of the known abstract projector formula.
