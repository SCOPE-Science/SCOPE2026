# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The trace argument separates scalar-rank idempotents from rank-one idempotents. The rank-one fiber is explicitly and bijectively parameterized by a conic. A vanishing-gradient calculation proves smoothness for nonzero determinant in every characteristic; a rational point yields exactly \(q+1\) projective points, and rational points at infinity biject to the characteristic-polynomial roots. For zero determinant the two affine lines meet at one point. Conjugacy-class sizes and finite-field quadratic splitting counts yield every histogram coefficient. The standalone checker exhaustively tests all matrices over eight stated finite fields. No finite enumeration is offered as proof for other fields.

## Originality — PASS with residual risk
Basnet–Bhattacharyya (2014), Proposition 4.4, gives the lower bound \(2q-1\), and computes the binary case, but not the uniform sharp upper bound, full fiber stratification, complete global histogram or smooth-versus-reducible conic model. Breaz et al. (2013), *Nil-clean matrix rings*, characterizes when whole matrix rings are nil-clean but not these fibers. Cîmpean–Danchev (2017) calculates the related **weakly** nil-clean index in examples, not the same invariant across all fields. Related published results about torsion-unit-plus-nilpotent **sums** and idempotent–nilpotent **products** do not imply additive idempotent-plus-nilpotent fiber numbers. Special cases may be known and are not claimed as novel. The 2018 dissertation *Clean Indices of Common Rings* was accessible only at abstract level; it could contain more results on prime-field cases, and the complete historical literature has not been exhausted.

## Value — PASS
The nil-clean index was introduced to quantify ambiguity of ring-theoretic decompositions. An earlier explicit lower bound motivates the precise sharpness question. The answer is uniform over every finite-field size, including nonprime fields, and enhances the maximum with a complete decomposition-fiber classification. The conic's split/non-split behavior explains the exact arithmetic multiplicities and isolates the exceptional reducible fiber. These are natural structural and quantitative invariants, not a chosen unmotivated parameter slice.

## Limitations and sources
The result does not classify higher-size matrix rings, and it does not address uniqueness or nil-clean decomposition existence for every matrix in the ring. Independent mathematical validation and exhaustive priority verification remain outstanding. Closest inspected sources: Breaz–Călugăreanu–Danchev–Micu, arXiv:1310.0416 (2013); Basnet–Bhattacharyya, *Nil clean index of rings* (2014), Proposition 4.4; Cîmpean–Danchev, *Weakly nil-clean index and uniquely weakly nil-clean rings* (2017), Example 2.26. The Schoonmaker (2018) thesis abstract was seen, but no full text could be inspected.
