# Review of sharp nonmodal amplification in an active lattice gas

**Correctness — PASS.** The stated physical \(L^2\) norm is exactly twice the unweighted Euclidean norm of the two species-density fluctuations. Fourier linearization of the displayed hydrodynamic PDE gives the matrix in the proof. Its Hermitian part has maximal eigenvalue \(-k^2-1+\sqrt{1+(\mathrm{Pe}\,kr/2)^2}\); its sign gives both necessity and sufficiency for strict transient gain, without a finite-mode assumption. Independently, its characteristic polynomial gives the spectral spinodal. The squared-threshold difference is the nonnegative perfect square \(Q(3r-2)^2/[r^2(1-r)(2r-1)]\). At half filling the triangular exponential yields both sides of the gain estimate uniformly over every mode and time. Symbolic identities and selected finite-mode matrix exponentials were replayed using the packaged checker. No finite sampling is used as proof of the PDE-wide claims.

**Originality — PASS, subject to the stated residual literature risk.** Kourbane-Houssene et al. (arXiv:1801.08952) already establish the nonlinear hydrodynamics and ordinary spinodal, including finite-length onset; those are not treated as discoveries. Agranov et al. (arXiv:2104.14650) provide the exact deterministic Fourier matrix as the drift of their fluctuation equations and compute correlation functions, while Jose, Dandekar and Ramola (arXiv:2301.10680) use the related Fourier propagator to study current statistics. The checked statements concern eigengrowth, noise covariances, and current cumulants, not the all-mode operator norm separating transient from asymptotic stability. A published abstract for a general triangular-semigroup contractivity theorem covers the half-filling norm-contractivity *threshold* as a special case; this component is acknowledged as prior coverage, not attributed independent novelty. The distinct result is the complete physical active-gas phase portrait, its unique self-adjoint density \(2/3\), and the sharp optimized linear-in-activity gain coefficient, none of which is supplied by that triangular contractivity theorem. Searches by objects, aliases, singular growth and phase-boundary comparisons returned no dominating application to the self-review; this is evidence of non-detection, not proof of uniqueness.

**Value — PASS.** A spinodal eigenvalue diagram can label an active lattice-gas state linearly stable while physical species perturbations transiently gain norm. The exact transient boundary shows precisely where this happens, its distinction from modal instability collapses only at an intrinsic density, and the half-filling law makes spectral predictions maximally misleading by giving arbitrarily large gain without changing any decay eigenvalue. The norm is fixed by species densities, rather than designed to manufacture growth. These precise criteria identify where nonmodal response may matter for finite perturbations and motivate subsequent stochastic response analysis, without claiming nonlinear or microscopic theorems.

**Closest literature and limits.** Primary hydrodynamic reference: arXiv:1801.08952; relevant linearized covariance reference: arXiv:2104.14650; current propagator reference: arXiv:2301.10680. General triangular matrix contractivity describes one strict subset (the half-filling contraction threshold) and is not an independent novelty claim here. Inaccessible/unindexed studies of transient response of the same active-matter hydrodynamics remain an originality risk. The theorem is for the deterministic linearized finite-torus equations and the specified unweighted species norm; it is not a nonlinear or stochastic finite-particle assertion.
