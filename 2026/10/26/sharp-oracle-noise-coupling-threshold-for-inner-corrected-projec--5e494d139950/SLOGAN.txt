For the published four-stage inner-corrected projective Euler scheme on a scalar stable mode, independent stochastic derivative evaluations create a stationary variance that grows like \(h^2\sigma^2/(2\lambda^2)\) as the inner-to-outer step ratio \(\lambda\) vanishes, even though the deterministic multiplier tends to zero. Uniform mean-square control is possible exactly when the final two oracle errors have correlation deficit \(1-\rho_\lambda=O(\lambda^2)\); uncoupled averaging instead requires batch size \(\Omega(\lambda^{-2})\), while ordinary uncorrected projective Euler has a bounded variance floor.
