# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The standard finite-depth two-layer dispersion law is reproduced directly from the potential-flow boundary conditions and independently checked against Mikaelian (1996). On the full positive unstable wavenumber interval, the normalized derivative of the squared growth rate has strictly negative derivative and changes sign from positive to negative exactly once. The peak's stationary equation uniquely determines the tension ratio from the single dimensionless depth variable. A strictly increasing bijective peak-height function then proves existence, uniqueness, and the strict data-realizability interval. The edge expansions and all strict inequalities follow from the same formulas. The standalone forward maximizer and inverse checker test finite parameter sets but do not substitute for the universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority uncertainty
The full relevant text of Mikaelian (1996) supplies finite-layer forward growth-rate expressions and physical discussion of thickness corrections; it does not present the sharp two-observable inverse feasibility theorem or global reconstruction. Mikaelian (1990) already treats the identical inviscid dispersion geometry and therefore strongly precedes the forward framework; only its abstract and bibliographic record were accessible, leaving unresolved whether its body or appendices include a peak-data inverse argument. Targeted published-source and research-index searches did not locate an equivalent two-observable sharp feasible-region statement, though negative search results cannot prove novelty. The claimed contribution is narrowly the inverse theorem, not the known forward relation. Related data from stable-wave inverse dispersion belong to other forward and inverse physical settings and do not yield this growing-peak classification.

## Mathematical value — PASS
In a physically standard model, one ordinarily observes a rapidly amplified spectral peak rather than an entire exact dispersion curve. The theorem identifies the precise set of compatible measured peak pairs and reconstructs exactly the two unknown physical parameters without other wavelength measurements. Its nontrivial universal upper feasibility bound \(2/3\), unique peak proof, and exponential loss of deep-layer conditioning are actionable structural information, rather than an arbitrary special value or unmotivated parameter sweep.

## Risks and boundaries
The theorem applies only to linear, inviscid, equal-depth, rigid-wall dynamics with known density contrast and gravity and positive tension. Deep-layer recovery is poorly conditioned; statistical uncertainty is not analyzed. Historical priority remains uncertain because the complete 1990 primary article was inaccessible. No claim of independent validation is made.
