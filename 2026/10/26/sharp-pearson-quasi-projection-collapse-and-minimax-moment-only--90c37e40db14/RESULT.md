# Sharp Pearson quasi-projection collapse and minimax moment-only repair on three atoms

## Finding

For the uniform three-state reference law and the one-moment Pearson minimum-divergence family, the **exact optimal multiplicative quasi-projection constant** at any fixed mean \(0<m<2/3\) is
\[
c(m)=\frac{4-9m^2}{4-6m^2}.
\]
It decreases to zero as \(m\uparrow2/3\). Therefore no uniform positive constant compares the Pearson divergence of the moment-matching reconstruction with the best Pearson divergence inside the associated family, even for just three atoms and one moment. This is a sharp obstruction to a proposed non-KL quasi-projection property.

There is nevertheless an **exact minimax repair based only on that mean**: the unique family member minimizing the worst-case *additive excess Pearson divergence* over all strictly positive truths with the observed mean has parameter
\[
t_*(m)=\frac{2m}{3(1+m)},\qquad
\inf_{|t|<1/3}\sup_{m<s<1}\bigl[D(P_{m,s}\Vert Q_t)-\inf_uD(P_{m,s}\Vert Q_u)\bigr]
=\frac{3m^2(1-m)}{4(1+3m)}.
\]
The minimax parameter need not match the observed mean; it coincides with moment matching precisely at \(m=1/3\) in the indicated range.

## Assumptions and scope

The alphabet is \(X=\{-1,0,1\}\), the reference is \(\lambda=(1/3,1/3,1/3)\), and the sole nonconstant statistic is \(T(x)=x\). Use **oriented** Pearson divergence, including the normalizing factor,
\[
D(P\Vert Q)=\frac12\sum_{x\in X}\frac{(P(x)-Q(x))^2}{Q(x)}
=\frac12\left(\sum_{x\in X}\frac{P(x)^2}{Q(x)}-1\right).
\]
This is the \(f\)-divergence for \(f(u)=(u-1)^2/2\). Since \((f')^{-1}(z)=1+z\), the positive normalized \(f\)-exponential family generated by \(1,T\) is exactly
\[
Q_t=(1/3-t,\ 1/3,\ 1/3+t),\qquad -1/3<t<1/3.
\]
Fix a known mean \(0<m<2/3\). All strictly positive target laws on this alphabet with this mean can be uniquely written
\[
P_{m,s}=((s-m)/2,\ 1-s,\ (s+m)/2),\qquad m<s<1.
\]
The reconstruction matching the mean is \(Q_{m/2}\). The divergence infima in this note are taken over the **open** positive family; all individual oracle minimizers are interior. The supremum over targets in the additive minimax game is generally not attained by a strictly positive target; it equals the limit at an endpoint of its allowed \(s\)-interval.

## Proof

**1. Oracle projection.** Let \(s=P_{m,s}(-1)+P_{m,s}(1)\). Every model member puts total mass \(2/3\) on the two outer atoms. For fixed \(s\), the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality shows
\[
\frac{P(-1)^2}{Q(-1)}+\frac{P(1)^2}{Q(1)}
\ge\frac{s^2}{2/3}=\frac{3s^2}{2},
\]
with unique equality when the ratios of the two outer model masses equal their target ratios. Because the target is strictly positive, this condition specifies the unique interior parameter
\[
t_{\mathrm{or}}=\frac{m}{3s}\in(0,1/3).
\]
Adding the central atom yields the exact oracle divergence
\[
D(P_{m,s}\Vert Q_{t_{\mathrm{or}}})=\frac{(3s-2)^2}{4}.
\]
Thus the oracle loss vanishes exactly when \(s=2/3\), equivalently the truth itself belongs to the model family.

**2. Exact multiplicative quasi-projection constant.** Substituting the mean-matching parameter \(t=m/2\) into the same elementary rational divergence gives
\[
D(P_{m,s}\Vert Q_{m/2})
=\frac{4-6m^2}{4-9m^2}\frac{(3s-2)^2}{4}.
\]
Consequently, for every target in the specified domain and every \(u\in(-1/3,1/3)\),
\[
D(P_{m,s}\Vert Q_u)\ge
\frac{4-9m^2}{4-6m^2}\,D(P_{m,s}\Vert Q_{m/2}).
\]
For any \(s\ne2/3\), equality holds at \(u=m/(3s)\), proving this is the **largest** admissible constant at fixed \(m\). Taking \(s=5/6\) and \(m\uparrow2/3\) keeps \(P_{m,s}\) strictly positive and fixes the oracle divergence at \(1/16\), while the mean-matching divergence grows without bound. Hence no constant \(c>0\) works uniformly over all these targets and all permitted means.

**3. Exact excess-divergence formula.** Directly subtracting the oracle loss (or retaining the equality remainder in the preceding Cauchy--Schwarz inequality) gives, for every \(m<s<1\) and every \(|t|<1/3\),
\[
R_{m,s}(t):=D(P_{m,s}\Vert Q_t)-D(P_{m,s}\Vert Q_{t_{\mathrm{or}}})
=\frac{3(m-3st)^2}{4(1-9t^2)}.
\]
For fixed \(m,t\), the squared numerator is convex in \(s\). Thus its supremum over the open interval \(s\in(m,1)\) is the larger of the two endpoint limits:
\[
W_m(t)=\sup_{m<s<1}R_{m,s}(t)
=\frac{3}{4(1-9t^2)}\max\left\{m^2(1-3t)^2,\ (m-3t)^2\right\}.
\]

**4. Unique minimax rule.** If \(t<0\), the first term already yields \(W_m(t)>3m^2/4\), whereas the rule below has smaller risk. For \(0\le t<1/3\), the first branch of \(W_m\) equals \(3m^2(1-3t)/[4(1+3t)]\) and strictly decreases in \(t\). The second branch is \(3(m-3t)^2/[4(1-9t^2)]\); it is strictly increasing once \(t>m/3\), because its derivative has the sign of \((3t-m)(1-3mt)\). Comparing absolute values shows that the first branch dominates until
\[
t_*=\frac{2m}{3(1+m)}>\frac m3,
\]
and the second dominates thereafter. Their intersection is unique, so \(W_m\) decreases up to \(t_*\) and increases afterward. Substitution gives the stated sharp value \(3m^2(1-m)/[4(1+3m)]\). Finally \(t_*=m/2\) holds precisely when \(m=1/3\) in \((0,2/3)\). The minimax value tends to \(1/27\) at the upper-mean endpoint, in contrast with unbounded worst-case moment-matching excess loss.

## Relationship to prior work

Bec, Le Corre and Garivier (2026) define the same generalized \(f\)-exponential family, characterize the uniqueness of KL for exact moment-matching projection, and explicitly leave open whether a weakened multiplicative quasi-projection constant may exist beyond KL. Their Pearson examples demonstrate nonzero projection gaps but do **not** establish the exact conditional constant, its collapse to zero, or the minimax moment-only replacement rule derived here. Nielsen and Nock (2013) calculate chi-square divergences for parametric exponential-family members, but do not investigate this moment-conditioned robust decision problem.

## Limitations

The negative result disproves a **universal**, parameter-independent positive quasi-projection constant for Pearson divergence, not the existence of nonzero constants under bounded-away-from-zero means or probabilities, nor quasi-projection inequalities for other divergences. The sharp minimax rule uses a uniform three-point reference, a single first moment, full-support truths, and the specifically oriented Pearson divergence. It does not solve general finite-alphabet or multiple-moment minimax reconstruction. No independent audit of this finding has been performed.
