# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact factorial moments of the intersection of independent uniform missing-type sets follow by choosing ordered marked types. The normalized factorial moments factor into products bounded by one. A uniform fourth-degree remainder estimate valid for every factorial-moment index, including indices beyond the finite support, is established directly from logarithmic inequalities and a separate large-index bound. Binomial inversion is absolutely summable after multiplication by \(2^j\), giving the explicit signed \(\ell^1\) error with constant \(K_t\). The sharp total-variation limit is a consequence of a reverse triangle inequality, \(r_n\delta_n\to t\), and Poisson moment continuity. Finite exact-rational enumeration and numerical checks agree with these steps. Limits: the coefficient can be uninformative for growing \(\lambda\); the corrected law is signed.

## Originality — PASS, with historical priority risk

The closest source is Goldstein and Reinert's 2005/2006 study of exactly the same random-subset intersection variable, which derives a first-order Poisson limit and a one-sided total-variation upper bound, but not the signed second-Charlier correction, its all-index quadratic-order error estimate, or the sharp constant. Betken and Thäle's 2022 preprint and 2023 paper concern the same coupon-group model but fixed package sizes and logarithmically many rounds, not nearly complete packages and a fixed number of rounds. Existing Charlier approximation theory for Poisson-binomial sums is methodologically close, yet the dependent intersections and explicit \(\delta\) correction are not stated in material examined. The 2026 close-to-complete package study could not be read in full; its accessible abstract addresses mean completion time, not the present signed law. General dependent Poisson-expansion literature may still subsume this theorem, a real priority limitation that cannot be eliminated by a negative search.

## Value — PASS

In the naturally defined threshold \(r_n\asymp n^{1-1/t}\) for covering all coupons within exactly \(t\) nearly complete packages, merely knowing a Poisson limit does not quantify the finite-population calibration error. The theorem gives an explicit uniform nonasymptotic signed approximation, an exact positive first-order total-variation constant and the sign and size of the complete-collection correction. Retaining the \(1/n\) term is mathematically relevant for \(t>2\), where it can dominate the quadratic remainder. The proof's uniform factorial-moment control makes these improvements more than a renaming or a routine restatement of a known bound.
