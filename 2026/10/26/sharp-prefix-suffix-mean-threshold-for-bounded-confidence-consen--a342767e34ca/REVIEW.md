# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof establishes all quantifiers for finite \(n\ge3\), strictly increasing ordered data, fixed positive edge-ring rates and compromise \(0<\mu\le1/2\). The key identity is the exact decrease of each prefix–suffix centroid gap when its unique boundary edge updates. Every adjacent gap is bounded by the corresponding centroid separation, which itself never exceeds its initial value. For confidence below the maximum initial separation, an explicit finite edge-selection word must create an inactive cut. The borderline distinction follows by classifying finite homogenization of monotone blocks, not by extrapolating finite tests. The bundled rational checker cross-checks the identities and constructive cases.

## Originality — PASS (subject to independent priority checking)
The motivating 2022 Deffuant mean-field article studies density evolution, cluster emergence and linear stability; it does not state this finite-path probability-one criterion. The finite-graph Deffuant study by Lanchier and Li gives a general probability lower bound from i.i.d. data, with an inclusive confidence convention; that is neither an equivalent statement nor a stronger coverage of a deterministic-profile sharp cutoff and strict-equality exceptions. The clique-mean bounded-confidence work considers different group-update operators. Available mathematical research records and the accessible earlier path/Deffuant research claims were compared for implication, not merely for matching titles. A differently named general result in finite-dimensional state-dependent consensus theory remains a plausible residual priority risk.

## Value — PASS
A computable \(O(n)\) cut-centroid statistic gives an exact all-population-size criterion for a natural path-network opinion model. The constructive subcritical failure is stronger than a one-sided sufficient condition, and the exceptional 3/4-versus-5-agent equality phenomenon reveals sensitivity to finite-time averaging and to the strict confidence convention. The distinction is relevant to simulation calibration and to separating inevitable eventual consensus from positive-probability fragmentation of an initially connected confidence path.

## Scope and unresolved limitations
The theorem does not give the numerical probability of fragmentation below threshold, and does not cover nonmonotone initial opinions, nonpath graphs or biased/unilateral compromise. The published finite-graph theorem with equality-allowing confidence is deliberately not treated as the same boundary model. No independent novelty review or audit has been performed.
