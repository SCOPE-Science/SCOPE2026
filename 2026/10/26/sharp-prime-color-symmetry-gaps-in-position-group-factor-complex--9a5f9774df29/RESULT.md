# Sharp prime-color symmetry gaps in position-group factor complexity

## Finding

Let \(p\ge3\) be prime. Suppose an infinite word \(x\) over a finite alphabet \(A\) has a factor language invariant under a fixed-point-free alphabet permutation \(\rho\) of order \(p\). Here *fixed-point-free* means that every nonidentity power of \(\rho\) fixes no alphabet letter. For \(n\ge1\), let \(F_n\) be the distinct length-\(n\) factors, let \(P(n)=|F_n|\), and let \(P^H(n)\) count equivalence classes of \(F_n\) under coordinate permutations from any \(H\le S_n\). Define the *position-group deficit* \(\Delta_H(n)=P(n)-P^H(n)\).

**Theorem (sharp symmetry gap).** For every \(n\ge1\) and \(H\le S_n\):

1. Either \(\Delta_H(n)=0\) or \(\Delta_H(n)\ge p-1\).
2. If \(p\nmid n\), then \(\Delta_H(n)\equiv0\pmod p\) and \(P^H(n)\equiv0\pmod p\). Thus every positive deficit is at least \(p\).
3. Equality \(\Delta_H(n)=p-1\) holds if and only if precisely one equivalence class within \(F_n\) is nonsingleton, that class is a complete \(\rho\)-orbit of size \(p\), and all other classes are singletons. Necessarily \(p\mid n\).
4. Both positive lower bounds are sharp among periodic infinite words with prime-color-invariant factor languages: deficit \(p-1\) occurs at length \(p\), and deficit \(p\) occurs at length \(2\).

In particular, if the usual abelian complexity \(P^{S_n}(n)\) is strictly below \(P(n)\), none of the integers \(P(n)-1,\ldots,P(n)-(p-2)\) is realized by a coordinate-permutation subgroup (restricted to integers strictly above the abelian complexity); when \(p\nmid n\), even \(P(n)-(p-1)\) is excluded. This gives a conditional, sharp family of gaps in the group-complexity spectrum.

## Assumptions and scope

A factor is a contiguous finite block. Alphabet symmetry is **not** itself an allowed group action in \(P^H\); the groups \(H\) permute **positions** only. Two factors \(u,v\in F_n\) are equivalent when \(v=h(u)\) for some \(h\in H\), regardless of whether every \(h(u)\) remains in \(F_n\). Equivalence classes are therefore intersections of full \(H\)-orbits in \(A^n\) with \(F_n\). Invariance means \(\rho(F_n)=F_n\) for each \(n\), and \(\rho\) acts letterwise on words. The alphabet may contain several disjoint cycles of length \(p\); no aperiodicity assumption is needed.

## Proof

**Commutation and class orbits.** Letterwise \(\rho\) commutes with every position permutation. Thus it permutes the equivalence classes \(\mathcal C_n(H)\) of \(F_n\), preserving their cardinalities. Because \(p\) is prime, a class either is fixed by \(\rho\), or belongs to a class orbit of size \(p\). Since every nontrivial power of \(\rho\) fixes no letter, it fixes no nonempty word.

Write
\[
\Delta_H(n)=\sum_{C\in\mathcal C_n(H)}(|C|-1).
\]
An orbit of \(p\) nontrivial classes each contributes at least \(p\) to this nonnegative sum. A class fixed setwise by \(\rho\) is a disjoint union of full \(p\)-element word orbits, so its size is divisible by \(p\). A nontrivial fixed class therefore contributes at least \(p-1\). This establishes statement 1, and also shows that total deficit \(p-1\) arises exactly from one fixed class of size \(p\), with all remaining classes singleton. Such a fixed class is the full letterwise \(\rho\)-orbit of any of its words, proving the equivalence in statement 3 except the divisibility of \(n\).

**Length-divisibility obstruction.** If an \(H\)-class \(C\) is fixed by \(\rho\), then for any \(u\in C\), the word \(\rho(u)\) is a coordinate permutation of \(u\); consequently their Parikh vectors coincide. Since \(\rho\) is a product of disjoint \(p\)-cycles on \(A\), equality of Parikh vectors implies that the counts of all \(p\) letters in each alphabet cycle are equal. Summing counts gives \(p\mid n\). Thus when \(p\nmid n\), no class is fixed; all classes occur in free \(p\)-orbits. Their numbers are multiples of \(p\), and the contributions to \(\Delta_H(n)\) also come in \(p\)-multiples. This proves statement 2 and the final assertion of statement 3.

**Sharpness at divisible length.** On \(A=\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\), let \(x=(0\,1\,\cdots\,p{-}1)^\omega\), with \(\rho(a)=a+1\pmod p\). Letter augmentation translates the period. At length \(n=p\), the factor set has exactly \(p\) cyclic rotations of the period. The cyclic group generated by rotating the \(p\) coordinates has one equivalence class, giving \(P(p)=p\), \(P^H(p)=1\), and \(\Delta_H(p)=p-1\).

**Sharpness at nondivisible length.** For \(p\ge3\), set
\[
V=(0,1,2)(1,2,3)\cdots(p-1,0,1),
\]
where all entries and indices are reduced modulo \(p\), and let \(y=V^\omega\). Adding one to each letter rotates \(V\) by one three-letter block; hence the factor language is \(\rho\)-invariant. At length two, the factors are precisely the \(2p\) distinct directed pairs \((j,j+1)\) and \((j+1,j)\), for \(j\in\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\). Under the two-position swap group, these form exactly \(p\) classes. Therefore \(P(2)=2p\), \(P^{S_2}(2)=p\), \(\Delta_{S_2}(2)=p\). Since \(p\ge3\), this is a nondivisible length, completing statement 4.

**Missing values.** Position subgroups have \(P^{S_n}(n)\le P^H(n)\le P(n)\). The nonzero-deficit bounds already proved exclude the stated integers in the attainable interval. No assertion of universal nonattainability is made when ordinary complexity equals abelian complexity.

## Verification

The proof covers all primes \(p\ge3\), all invariant factor languages, all lengths, and all position subgroups without computational assumptions. The accompanying standard-library program `check.py` independently enumerates every \(\rho\)-invariant subset of \(A^n\) and every subgroup of \(S_n\) for \((p,n)=(3,2),(3,3),(5,2),(7,2)\), and verifies the deficit bound, congruence, and equality classification. It also checks both periodic sharpness examples at \(p=3,5,7\). Run `python3 check.py`; the expected final line is `FINITE_STRESS_TESTS_OK`. These finite checks are stress tests, not substitutes for the proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Launer–Puzynina–Voloshinova [1] study attainable group complexities for infinite words and provide an odd-length parity obstruction for the **binary** complement-invariant setting. The result above differs both in hypothesis and conclusion: for prime-color actions \(p\ge3\), it yields a sharp forbidden interval adjacent to ordinary complexity, a length-divisibility congruence, an exact equality characterization, and two uniform periodic extremizers. Its point is the interaction of commuting letter symmetries and coordinate-group equivalence classes; it does not claim the binary parity mechanism as new. Machacek [2] studies simultaneous letter and coordinate group actions, a different quotient from the position-only invariant here. Charlier–Puzynina–Zamboni [3] establish Morse–Hedlund-type lower bounds on the absolute group complexity, not endpoint deficit spectra. The cited sources do not state these combined sharp prime-symmetry conclusions in the sections inspected.

## Limitations

The theorem requires a **free** prime-order letter action; it does not extend automatically to composite orders or permutations with fixed letters. It describes deficits near the ordinary endpoint, not the full spectrum for every word. The examples are periodic; they establish sharpness for the stated class but do not show the same exact endpoints for aperiodic words. No independent scholarly audit is claimed. Unindexed or historically overlooked equivalent arguments remain a possible originality limitation.
