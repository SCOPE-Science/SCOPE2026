# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For three odd primes the square sum is 3 modulo 4; when one is 2, half the square sum is 3 modulo 4. The resulting odd prime divisor l congruent to 3 modulo 4 cannot be a supported prime, since reducing modulo l would make the quotient of the other two squares equal to -1 in the field. For two primes the sum is coprime to n and exceeds one. For odd n the square sum is congruent to the support size modulo 2, so it can divide n only at odd support. Recomputed by trial division and exact arithmetic: 46206 = 2*3^2*17*151 and its sum is 23103 (quotient 2); 72105 = 3*5*11*19*23 and its sum is 1045 (quotient 69). These verify sharpness under the explicit exclusion of prime powers. The packaged 300000-integer scan was inspected, not executed; it is not used for the all-prime result.

## originality

PASS

The witnesses, sequence and squarefree case are prior. On the compared evidence, what survives is the exponent-independent exclusion of three distinct supported primes in the missing cases and the resulting sharp unrestricted and odd support minima. No verified comparison establishes that full claim. This is a bounded priority judgment, not a claim that the modular tools or witnesses are new.

## value

PASS

For a pre-existing divisibility sequence, this identifies which minimal sizes of prime support are possible even when prime powers are arbitrary. The stronger existence of a specific residue-class outsider gives a uniform local obstruction to realization of any three-prime support, rather than an observation about the first terms or a repeat of the squarefree result.
