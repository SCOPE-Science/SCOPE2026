# Review of sharp prime-support thresholds

**Correctness — PASS.** For any three distinct primes, their square sum is either \(3\pmod4\) or twice a number that is \(3\pmod4\). Therefore it contains a prime \(\ell\equiv3\pmod4\), which cannot belong to the original triple because \(-1\) is a quadratic nonresidue modulo \(\ell\). This proves the exponent-independent three-support exclusion. Two-support exclusion uses coprimality; parity handles the odd four-support case. Two explicit factorized witnesses prove sharpness. The verifier independently checks 3654 triples and all \(n\le300000\); it is supplementary rather than a substitute for the universal proof.

**Originality — PASS under the inspected record.** The full seven-page De Koninck–Luca paper establishes existence of divisibility examples but not minimal \(\omega(n)\). OEIS A190882 records a weaker necessary bound \(\omega(n)\ge3\) and the same sharp witnesses, which are not presented as new numerical examples. MathPages proves the distinct-prime product obstruction only in the squarefree setting; because exponents change divisibility, that statement does not imply the all-exponent three-support theorem or the exact four-support threshold. The full nine-page related paper on partial sums concerns equality with a selected subset, a different condition. No equivalent result surfaced in targeted searches of published mathematical research records. Residual priority uncertainty remains for older unindexed equivalent proofs.

**Value — PASS.** The question concerns a natural arithmetic divisibility function whose OEIS record states a nonsharp prime-support bound. The new external-prime obstruction proves the exact uniform lower bound over all exponents; explicit examples settle attainability in both parity classes. This is a motivated exact support classification boundary rather than an isolated numerical experiment or a squarefree-table recounting. It does not classify every qualifying higher-support number.
