# Sharp quantifier-rank visibility and existential collapse for triangular graphs

## Finding
For every integer \(q\ge 1\) and all integers \(n,m\ge2\), consider the simple triangular graph \(T_n=J(n,2)=L(K_n)\). Its vertices are the two-element subsets of \([n]\); distinct vertices are adjacent if they intersect. In the first-order graph language consisting only of equality and adjacency, let \(T_n\equiv_q T_m\) mean agreement on all sentences of quantifier rank at most \(q\). Then
\[
T_n\equiv_q T_m\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\min\{n,2q\}=\min\{m,2q\}.
\]
Consequently these nonempty graphs have exactly \(2q-1\) rank-\(q\) theories: singletons represented by \(T_2,\ldots,T_{2q-1}\), and the final class containing every \(T_n\) with \(n\ge2q\). For \(2\le n<m\), the least distinguishing quantifier rank is exactly \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1\). More strongly, **agreement on existential sentences of rank at most \(q\) already implies agreement on all first-order sentences of rank at most \(q\) within this family.**

## Assumptions and scope
All structures have nonempty finite vertex domains (hence \(n\ge2\)), no distinguished vertices, no order and no counting quantifiers. Quantifier rank is the maximum number of nested first-order quantifiers; writing \(\exists x_1\cdots\exists x_r\) gives rank \(r\). The equivalence is *within the triangular-graph family*, not a claim that these formulas define the graphs among all graphs. The variable \(q\) is any positive integer, with no finite-size cutoff assumed beyond that proved.

## Proof
**Upper equivalence.** Suppose \(n,m\ge2q\). In a \(q\)-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game on \(T_n,T_m\), maintain a partial bijection between the underlying ground sets \([n]\) and \([m]\) that sends every previously selected two-set on either side to its chosen counterpart on the other. Initially it is empty. At round \(r\le q\), the newly selected two-set contains at most two ground elements not yet in the domain or range of this bijection. Before the round at most \(2(r-1)\) ground elements on either side have been used, leaving at least \(2q-2(r-1)\ge2\) available on the responding side. Extend the bijection to the new ground elements and answer with the image (or preimage) two-set. Repeated choices are answered consistently. Equality and nonempty intersection of the selected two-sets are preserved exactly. Thus Duplicator wins every \(q\)-round game, proving equivalence.

**A root-size lemma.** Let \(F\) be an abstract simple graph that is a vertex-disjoint union of \(c\ge1\) path graphs, with a total of \(r\ge c\) vertices (a single vertex counts as a path). A family of \(r\) distinct two-element subsets has intersection graph isomorphic to \(F\) if and only if the underlying ground set has at least \(r+c\) elements. To prove the lower bound, a path component on \(s\) vertices must be represented by \(s\) successive edges of a simple underlying path and therefore uses \(s+1\) ground points: the two endpoints of successive intersecting edges are forced to differ, and edges at distance at least two in the intersection graph must be disjoint. This also covers \(s=1\) and \(s=2\) directly. Different components use disjoint ground points because their selected two-sets are pairwise disjoint. Adding the requirements gives \(r+c\) points. Conversely, take \(c\) vertex-disjoint simple paths with a total of \(r\) edges; their edges have intersection graph \(F\) and use exactly \(r+c\) points. (The familiar exceptional alternative line-graph root of a triangle does not apply because the components of \(F\) are paths, not triangles.)

**Lower separation.** It is enough to separate \(T_n\) from every \(T_m\) with \(m>n\) whenever \(n<2q\). Put \(s=n+1\), so \(3\le s\le2q\). We exhibit an existential graph sentence of rank at most \(q\) that holds in every \(T_m\) with \(m\ge s\) and fails in \(T_n\).

If \(s=3\), use existence of two distinct graph vertices (rank two). If \(s=4\), use existence of two nonadjacent vertices (rank two). Both ranks are at most \(q\), since their cases require \(n<2q\).

If \(5\le s\le q\), use existence of \(s-1\) pairwise adjacent, distinct vertices (rank \(s-1\)). Indeed a pairwise-intersecting family of \(r\ge4\) distinct two-element sets necessarily has a single common point: choose \(ab,ac\); any further set not containing \(a\) would have to be \(bc\), after which no fourth distinct set intersects all three. Thus a clique of size \(s-1\ge4\) needs at least \(s\) ground points and is realized by a star on \(s\) points.

Finally, if \(q<s\le2q\), set \(c=s-q\), so \(1\le c\le q\). Partition \(q\) into \(c\) positive parts and let \(F\) be the disjoint union of corresponding vertex-paths. The existential rank-\(q\) sentence requiring \(q\) pairwise distinct vertices with **exactly** the adjacency/nonadjacency pattern of \(F\) holds in \(T_m\) exactly when \(m\ge q+c=s\), by the root-size lemma.

We have thus separated every two orders on opposite sides of a missing visible order, and the upper equivalence proves there are no further separations. When \(n<m\), the first rank for which \(n<2q\) is \(q=\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1\), establishing the exact distinguishing rank. All the separating witnesses just constructed are existential, so agreement on the existential fragment is equivalent to full rank-\(q\) agreement on this family. The number of classes follows by counting the \(2q-2\) visible orders starting at two and adding the common tail.

## Verification
The symbolic EF strategy applies simultaneously to every positive \(q\) and every \(n,m\ge2q\), while the existential separation handles every smaller \(n\). The root-size lemma is the only family-specific reconstruction step. As an independent **finite sanity check**, `check_triangular.py` enumerates canonical induced adjacency patterns on up to four selected graph vertices, in triangular graphs of ground orders \(2\) through \(9\). It verifies for ranks \(1\) through \(4\) that adjacent ground orders are existentially separable before \(2q\) but not at or above \(2q\). The exact recorded output is in `check_triangular_output.txt`; these tests are supplementary and do not establish the unrestricted result.

## Relationship to prior work
Triangular graphs \(T_n=J(n,2)=L(K_n)\) are standard Johnson/line graphs. Jones's study of automorphism groups and regular embeddings of merged Johnson graphs gives this graph-family representation but concerns different invariants. Brown and Hoshino (2007) analyze Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé games on paths and cycles rather than line graphs of complete graphs. Pikhurko and Verbitsky (2010) survey first-order graph logical depth and Ehrenfeucht games and motivate exact graph-family visibility calculations. Neither inspected relevant statement supplies the particular linear threshold \(2q\), the count \(2q-1\), or the existential-fragment collapse above. Similar exact-depth statements for other graph families do not entail this result without a distinct representation and sharp separation argument.

## Limitations
No claim is made about Johnson graphs \(J(n,k)\) for \(k>2\), about arbitrary line graphs, about first-order logic with built-in order, or about counting logic. The proof does not assert an upper bound for formulas defining \(T_n\) among all finite graphs. The literature search does not establish absolute novelty: an equivalent triangular-graph calculation could exist under another terminology, in unindexed literature, or as folklore. A relevant 2005 Johnson-graph study was accessible in indexed sections but not as a fully retrievable publisher document; its stated theorem topics are automorphism groups and embeddings.
