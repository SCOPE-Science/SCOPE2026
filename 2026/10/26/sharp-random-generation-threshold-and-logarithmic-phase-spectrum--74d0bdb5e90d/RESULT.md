# Sharp random-generation threshold and logarithmic phase spectrum for finite S5 modal algebras

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge1\), choose an equivalence relation \(R_n\) uniformly among all \(B_n\) set partitions of \([n]\). Independently choose \(k\ge1\) subsets \(P_1,\ldots,P_k\subseteq[n]\), each uniformly and independently. Let \(A_n=(\mathcal P([n]),\cap,\neg,\Diamond)\), where \(\Diamond X\) is the union of all \(R_n\)-classes meeting \(X\). Put \(q=2^k\), and let \(G_{n,k}\) denote the event that the modal subalgebra generated by the \(P_i\) is all of \(A_n\).

**Exact law.** For every \(n\ge1\) and \(k\ge1\),
\[
\Pr(G_{n,k})=\frac{n!}{B_n q^n}[z^n]\prod_{s=1}^{q}(1+z^s)^{\binom qs}.
\]
In particular, full generation is possible if and only if \(n\le q2^{q-1}\).

**Sharp threshold.** Let \(\alpha_n>0\) be the unique solution of \(\alpha_n e^{\alpha_n}=n+1\). If \(k=k_n\), \(q_n=2^{k_n}\) and \(q_n/(n\alpha_n)\to c\in(0,\infty)\), then
\[
\Pr(G_{n,k_n})\longrightarrow\exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2c}\right).
\]
The limit is \(0\) when \(q_n/(n\alpha_n)\to0\), and is \(1\) when that ratio tends to infinity. More precisely, for every fixed \(a>0\), uniformly along any integer sequence with \(q_n\ge a n\alpha_n\),
\[
\Pr(G_{n,k_n})=\exp\!\left(-\frac{n\alpha_n}{2q_n}\right)+o(1).
\]

**Integer-observation phase.** At \(k_n=\lfloor\log_2(n\alpha_n)\rfloor\), for sufficiently large \(n\) so \(k_n\ge1\), the probability generally has no limit. Its complete set of subsequential limits is exactly
\[
\left[e^{-1},e^{-1/2}\right].
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The frame has exactly one \(\mathsf{S5}\) accessibility equivalence relation on a labelled \(n\)-element universe. The frame distribution is uniform over set partitions, *not* over all binary relations or over partitions with a prescribed number of blocks. A generator is an independently uniform random subset. Every Boolean operation and both dual modal operations are available in the generated subalgebra; the unary \(\Diamond\) alone suffices. The probability is over both the frame and the random generators. The exact formula holds for finite \(n\), including small or impossible regimes; the threshold and phase statements hold as \(n\to\infty\). Here \(B_n\) is the \(n\)-th Bell number, with \(B_0=1\), and \([z^n]\) extracts a formal generating-function coefficient.

## Proof
**1. Modal observability criterion.** Each world receives one of \(q=2^k\) colors recording the truth values of all generators; all \(q\) colors are equiprobable and independently assigned. In each equivalence class (block) \(C\), define its *support* \(S(C)\subseteq[q]\) as the set of colors present. We prove the exact equivalence:
\[
G_{n,k}\iff \text{every block has pairwise distinct colors and distinct blocks have distinct supports.}
\]
If two worlds have the same color inside a block, the transposition of those worlds preserves \(R_n\) and all generators, hence fixes all definable subsets, preventing singleton separation. When all colors inside each block are distinct but two blocks have the same support, the color-preserving swap of these two blocks is a nontrivial frame automorphism fixing all generators, with the same obstruction. Conversely, for each color \(c\), the Boolean color atom \(A_c\) is definable from the generators. Each block has constant Boolean vector \((\Diamond A_c)_{c\in[q]}\), recording its support. For any support \(S\subseteq[q]\), the Boolean conjunction specifying exactly these truth values isolates the unique block with support \(S\), if one exists. Intersecting it with \(A_c\) isolates its unique color-\(c\) point. Thus all singleton subsets are definable, so the generated algebra is the full powerset algebra.

**2. Exact enumeration.** For each nonempty support \(S\subseteq[q]\), the criterion permits at most one block using precisely the colors \(S\); such a block has \(|S|\) labelled vertices, one per color. Its labelled exponential generating-function contribution is \(z^{|S|}\), because the \(|S|!\) possible assignments of its distinct colors cancel the \(|S|!\) denominator for the unordered set of vertices. This support is either unused or used once. Multiplication across all nonempty supports gives \(\prod_{S\ne\varnothing}(1+z^{|S|})\), which equals the displayed product grouped by cardinality. The numerator of the probability counts exactly \(n!\) times its \(n\)-th coefficient; the denominator is \(B_n q^n\). The polynomial has nonnegative coefficients at every integer degree from \(0\) to \(q2^{q-1}\): include supports one by one, whose available sizes include \(1\), and for every larger degree one can first choose all supports of a prescribed maximum total weight and omit appropriate chosen supports (equivalently, the subset-sum weights include all integers because the first \(q\) singleton supports furnish weights \(1\) and each later support has size \(s\le q\), so consecutive coverage extends at every stage). Its degree is \(\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}|S|=q2^{q-1}\). This proves the finite existence threshold.

**3. Exact urn representation.** Define a positive-integer variable \(M\) by
\[
\Pr(M=m)=\frac{m^n}{e\,m!\,B_n}\quad(m\ge1).
\]
Dobinski's identity normalizes the law. Given \(M=m\), place all \(n\) labelled worlds independently and uniformly into \(m\) numbered boxes and discard empty boxes. Any particular partition into \(b\) blocks results with probability
\[
\sum_{m\ge b}\frac{m^n}{e\,m!B_n}\frac{(m)_b}{m^n}
=\frac1{B_n e}\sum_{j\ge0}\frac1{j!}=\frac1{B_n}.
\]
Thus this exactly samples the prescribed uniform random equivalence relation. The independent color choices assign each world uniformly to one of \(Mq\) *box/color* cells conditionally on \(M\). Known saddle-point estimates for Stam's \(M\), stated in Chern--Diaconis--Kane--Rhoades (2015, Theorem 2.1, building on Hwang), give
\[
\mathbb E M\sim\frac n{\alpha_n},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(M)=O\!\left(\frac n{\alpha_n^2}\right),\qquad
\frac{M}{n/\alpha_n}\xrightarrow{\Pr}1.
\]
The last follows by Chebyshev's inequality (\(n/\alpha_n\to\infty\)).

**4. Birthday collisions.** Let \(C_n\) be the event that no two worlds occupy the same box/color cell. Then \(G_{n,k}\subseteq C_n\) and, conditionally on \(M=m\),
\[
\Pr(C_n\mid M=m)=\frac{(mq)_n}{(mq)^n}
=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(1-\frac j{mq}\right),
\]
interpreted as zero for \(mq<n\). If \(q_n\ge a n\alpha_n\), then with probability tending to one \(M\in[(1-\delta)n/\alpha_n,(1+\delta)n/\alpha_n]\) for any fixed \(\delta\in(0,1)\), and there \(Mq\ge a(1-\delta)n^2\). Expanding logarithms uniformly gives
\[
\log\Pr(C_n\mid M)=-\frac{n(n-1)}{2Mq}+O\!\left(\frac{n^3}{(Mq)^2}\right)
=-\frac{n\alpha_n}{2q_n}+o_{\Pr}(1).
\]
The error is uniform for such sequences, since the concentration relative error of \(M\) tends to zero and \(n\alpha_n/q_n\) is bounded. Bounded convergence in probability yields \(\Pr(C_n)=\exp(-n\alpha_n/(2q_n))+o(1)\). If \(q_n/(n\alpha_n)\to0\), use the elementary upper bound \(\Pr(C_n\mid M)\le\exp[-n(n-1)/(2Mq)]\) on typical \(M\); it tends to zero. If \(q_n/(n\alpha_n)\to\infty\), the displayed asymptotic tends to one.

**5. Different-box support aliases vanish in the critical/upper window.** We show \(\Pr(C_n\setminus G_{n,k})=o(1)\) whenever \(q_n\ge a n\alpha_n\). Fix any assignment of worlds to boxes, and condition on \(C_n\). Given the box sizes, each occupied box then receives independently a uniformly random set of distinct colors of its prescribed size. Take \(\delta=1/5\) and restrict to \(M\le (6/5)n/\alpha_n\), an event of probability tending to one. Since \(q_n\ge a n\alpha_n>2n\) eventually, for any two distinct boxes of equal size \(s\ge2\), their support collision probability is \(\binom{q_n}s^{-1}\le\binom{q_n}2^{-1}\). There are at most \(M^2/2\) such pairs, so their total conditional probability is at most \(M^2/[2\binom{q_n}2]=O(\alpha_n^{-4})\), uniformly on the typical event. For pairs of singleton occupied boxes, let \(L\) count singleton boxes. Conditional on \(M=m\), ordinary occupancy gives the exact second factorial moment
\[
\mathbb E[L(L-1)\mid M=m]
=n(n-1)\left(1-\frac1m\right)\left(1-\frac2m\right)^{n-2}
\]
for \(m\ge2\). On the typical event \(M\le(6/5)n/\alpha_n\), the last factor is at most \(\exp[-(5/3+o(1))\alpha_n]\), so the expected number of singleton-support collisions is at most
\[
\frac{n^2}{2q_n}\exp[-(5/3+o(1))\alpha_n]=o(1),
\]
because \(\alpha_n\sim\log n\) and \(q_n\ge a n\alpha_n\). Different box sizes cannot have equal supports under \(C_n\). A union bound finishes. Thus \(\Pr(G_{n,k})=\Pr(C_n)+o(1)\), proving the full sharp-threshold statement.

**6. Integer-observation phase.** For \(k_n=\lfloor\log_2(n\alpha_n)\rfloor\), put \(\theta_n=\{\log_2(n\alpha_n)\}\in[0,1)\). Then \(q_n=n\alpha_n 2^{-\theta_n}\), so the threshold estimate gives
\[
\Pr(G_{n,k_n})=\exp(-2^{\theta_n-1})+o(1).
\]
The sequence \(t_n=\log_2(n\alpha_n)\) is eventually strictly increasing to infinity with \(t_{n+1}-t_n\to0\) (since \(\alpha_n\) increases smoothly and \(\alpha_n\sim\log n\)). Hence every \(\theta\in[0,1]\) is approached modulo one by a subsequence of \(t_n\), with \(\theta=1\) attained as a limit from below. The continuous phase map \(\theta\mapsto\exp(-2^{\theta-1})\) therefore has precisely the cluster interval \([e^{-1},e^{-1/2}]\). This also proves genuine nonconvergence.

## Verification
The included standalone Python checker directly enumerates all set partitions and all color assignments for \(1\le n\le5\) and \(2\le q\le4\); its independent polynomial-coefficient calculation agrees with every enumerated success count. For selected small cases it computes the actual Boolean/modal closure and confirms the support criterion. The output is `S5_RANDOM_GENERATION_EXACT_OK 7536`. These are finite consistency tests, not proofs of the infinite asymptotics. The asymptotic argument uses the exact uniform-partition urn representation and the cited concentration estimate for \(M\).

## Relationship to prior work
Alfred Horn's *Free S5 algebras* (1978) studies finite-variable free algebras, so it is pertinent deterministic structural background; the present result does not claim that colored support types or S5 local finiteness are new. De Berardinis--Ghilardi (2025) describe finite S5 algebras and equivalence-frame structure via essentially algebraic theory. Almeida--De Berardinis (2026) study definability and descent for S5. Zantedeschi (2026) proves representation results for modal-exchangeable probabilities of valuations, not the probability of full modal generation on a Bell-uniform random frame. Stam's urn representation (1983) and the block-count estimates assembled by Chern--Diaconis--Kane--Rhoades (2015) supply known probabilistic ingredients. The result here is the joint, exact random-*generation* probability for Bell-uniform frames, its sharp \(n\log n\) threshold, and its integer-generator logarithmic phase interval. This claim is not a first proof of Stam's representation, the birthday paradox, or the free-S5-algebra characterization.

## Limitations
No claim is made for nonuniform random partitions, multiple accessibility relations, or non-Boolean/nonstandard modal operations. The concentration estimate for Stam's auxiliary random number of boxes is cited rather than reproved. The historical coverage search is not a proof of publication priority: the complete text of Horn (1978) was inaccessible during comparison, and an equivalent random-generation formulation may exist in another field. The finite checker does not certify infinite asymptotics or independent validation.
