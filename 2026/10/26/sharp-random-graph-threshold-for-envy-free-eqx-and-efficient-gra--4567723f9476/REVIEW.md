# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For graphical binary edge goods with simple G and unnormalized additive endpoint value 1, every fPO allocation assigns each edge to an endpoint (welfare m). Such orientations are EF iff every nonisolated vertex receives >=1 edge; EQX iff recipient indegrees differ by at most 1. A balanced indegree interval [q,q+1], q=floor(m/n), exists iff e(S)<=(q+1)|S| and b(S)>=q|S| for every vertex subset by integral lower-bound max flow. Independently exhaustively compared these conditions to direct orientations across all 1,098 labeled graphs on 2..5 vertices. For p=c log n/n and c>2/(1-log2), concentration of q about mean-degree/2 and Chernoff union bounds over all small/large S establish both cut inequalities with high probability. Below threshold, a second-moment calculation of vertices with degree <q forbids any balanced orientation with high probability. The critical c=c* case is explicitly excluded and no finite enumeration is substituted for the asymptotic proof.

## originality

PASS

Misra–Sethia develops EF orientations for graphical valuations, but not the simultaneous EQX-balanced/fPO random threshold. Peled–Peleg already has precisely the same constant 2/(1−log2), via the minimum-degree versus half-average-degree crossover in a different rigidity problem; this constant and necessary degree obstruction are credited prior work. The distinct claim is that *all* orientation cuts hold above the threshold and hence joint allocation exists. Hosseini et al. 2026 full text unavailable; more general load-balancing literature may imply this result under translation.

## value

PASS

Provides an exact sharp phase transition for simultaneous EF, EQX and efficiency in a natural random graphical-goods model. It separates easily detected local degree obstruction from the global circulation inequalities and establishes that no further cut obstruction changes the leading threshold, a substantive random-combinatorial result beyond the previously known constant.
