# Scientific review: finite partition-lattice rank-three classification

## Correctness — PASS

The asserted equivalence is proved for all positive ground-set sizes, not by extrapolating an enumeration. The proof provides a complete comparison-pattern table for every ordered pair of partitions and derives the pointed rank-two types from this table. For ground-set size at least five, these pointed types are classified by truncated block count and rank. Their realized set stabilizes at seven ground points. Six explicitly defined rank-three order sentences separate the exceptional sizes. The argument yields the optimal 877-element representative bound. The accompanying exact checker reproduces the pair-pattern table exhaustively through seven points and the rank-three theory through eight points; its eighth-dimension pair-table check is explicitly sampled. No computation is used as a substitute for the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to the recorded literature-access limitation

Jenča–Sarkoci's accessible full text treats order-preserving partitions and their order-complex homotopy type, not a quantifier-rank equivalence classification. Idziak's inspected 1987 discussion of hereditary undecidability for the class of finite partition lattices addresses unbounded rank and does not imply a seven-ground-point cutoff at rank three. Previously identified counting-colour-refinement results recover the automorphism orbit of a partition inside one lattice by counting neighbors; this is neither the same logical language nor an implication to cross-size equivalence for pure first-order logic with three nested quantifiers. Primary sources and mathematical-index results were checked for equivalent, dual or stronger coverage. No covering statement was identified, but absence from a search index is not a proof of originality. Burris–Sankappanavar's relevant full text was unavailable, so potential older coverage remains a residual risk.

## Value — PASS

The entire natural sequence of partition lattices has a sharply classified bounded-rank theory. The result separates the logical visibility of ground-set size from the much richer integer-partition orbit structure, exhibits six explicit low-rank threshold sentences, and provides the smallest possible universal representative within the family. Its exact value lies in a complete invariant and optimal finite-model testing cutoff, not in computation of another Bell number.

## Limits

Only the ordinary refinement-order-and-equality signature at quantifier rank three is classified. A stronger language, an increased quantifier rank or counting logic may distinguish larger members. Independent scientific audit is outstanding, and one historically relevant full text was not completely accessible.
