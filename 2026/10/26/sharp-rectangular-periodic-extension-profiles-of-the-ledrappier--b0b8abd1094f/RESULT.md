# Sharp rectangular periodic-extension profiles of the Ledrappier three-dot shift

## Finding

Consider the binary three-dot subshift \(X\) of arrays \(x:\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb F_2\) obeying
\[
x_{i,j}+x_{i+1,j}+x_{i,j+1}=0
\quad\text{for all }(i,j)\in\mathbb Z^2.
\]
For every integer \(n\ge 1\), let \(P_n\) be the subgroup of configurations fixed by both coordinate shifts raised to the \(n\)-th power. Define, in \(\mathbb F_2[t]\),
\[
M_n(t)=t^n-1,\qquad B_n(t)=(1+t)^n-1,\qquad
 d_n=\deg\gcd(M_n,B_n).
\]
Let \(\mathcal L_{w,h}\) be the set of all \(w\times h\) binary rectangles satisfying the three-dot rule at each triangle wholly contained in the rectangle, for positive integers \(w,h\). Write \(L=w+h-1\). The restriction of \(P_n\) to this rectangle has the **exact linear rank**
\[
\operatorname{rank}(P_n\to\mathcal L_{w,h})=\min(d_n,L).
\]
Consequently, \(|P_n|=2^{d_n}\), precisely \(2^{\min(d_n,L)}\) distinct locally legal rectangles arise, and every realized rectangle has \(2^{\max(d_n-L,0)}\) distinct square-periodic extensions. The proportion of locally legal rectangles admitting such extension equals \(2^{-\max(L-d_n,0)}\). **All** locally legal \(w\times h\) rectangles extend to an \(n\times n\)-periodic global configuration if and only if \(w+h-1\le d_n\). These conclusions hold without requiring \(w\le n\) or \(h\le n\).

There is also a sharp arithmetic classification. If \(n=2^a m\), with \(a\ge0\) and odd \(m\ge1\), then
\[
d_n=2^a d_m\le n-2^a,
\]
with equality **if and only if** \(m=2^k-1\) for some integer \(k\ge1\). In particular, when \(n=2^k-1\), \(d_n=n-1\), and all locally legal rectangles with \(w+h\le n\) extend to \(n\)-square-periodic configurations, with exactly \(2^{n-w-h}\) extensions each. When \(n\) is a power of two, \(P_n\) contains only the zero configuration.

The all-n extension threshold, image counts, fiber multiplicities, and arithmetic extremality are the claim. The Mersenne-period cardinality alone was previously computed and is not claimed as new.

## Assumptions and scope

All arithmetic is in the field \(\mathbb F_2\). A *locally legal rectangle* satisfies only those three-site equations for which all three cells lie in the rectangle; no boundary wrapping is imposed on its definition. In particular \(\mathcal L_{1,1}\) has two elements. The square-periodic requirement is simultaneous period \(n\) in both lattice directions, not least period exactly \(n\). The location of the rectangle is immaterial by translation symmetry. The degree of the constant polynomial \(1\) is zero. A fraction of patterns is relative to the uniform counting measure on \(\mathcal L_{w,h}\).

## Proof

**1. First-row coding and square-periodic dimension.** Write \(u_i=x_{i,0}\). The local rule uniquely determines every positive-height row by
\[
x_{i,j}=(1+T)^j u_i,\qquad (Tu)_i=u_{i+1}.
\]
For bi-infinite configurations, every \(u\) has an extension to negative heights because \(1+T\) is surjective on the full two-sided binary shift: given \(v\), a preimage \(u\) can be constructed by fixing \(u_0\) and solving \(u_i+u_{i+1}=v_i\) in both directions. For horizontally \(n\)-periodic configurations we instead work on \(\mathbb F_2^n\), where the vertical-period condition is exactly \(((1+T)^n-I)u=0\), and this condition automatically makes the vertical evolution invertible on its solution space (since \((1+T)^n u=u\)); hence it defines a global doubly periodic configuration.

Identify the cyclic shift module \(\mathbb F_2^n\) with \(R_n=\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^n-1)\) in the orientation for which \(T\) acts by multiplication by \(t\). Then the row solution space is \(C_n=\ker(B_n(t):R_n\to R_n)\). Let \(g_n=\gcd(M_n,B_n)\) and \(h_n=M_n/g_n\). The divisibility condition \(M_n\mid B_n f\) is equivalent to \(h_n\mid f\) in \(\mathbb F_2[t]\); thus each solution has a unique representative \(h_n q\) with \(\deg q<d_n=\deg g_n\). Therefore \(\dim C_n=d_n\), proving \(|P_n|=2^{d_n}\).

**2. Consecutive-coordinate information-set lemma.** More generally, let \(C\subset\mathbb F_2^n\) be a cyclic code of dimension \(d\) represented by the ideal generated by a monic divisor \(h\) of \(t^n-1\) with \(\deg h=n-d\). Since \(h(0)=1\), the \(d\) codewords \(h,th,\ldots,t^{d-1}h\) have a lower-triangular \(d\times d\) matrix of their first \(d\) polynomial coefficients, with diagonal entries all equal to one. Consequently projection to the first \(d\) cyclic coordinates is an isomorphism. Rotating coordinates, which preserves \(C\), proves the same for every \(d\) consecutive coordinates. For any \(L\ge1\), projecting to \(L\) consecutive coordinates (repeating cyclically if \(L>n\)) has rank exactly \(\min(d,L)\). The coordinate orientation used to realize \(T\) as multiplication by \(t\) may reverse the order of the physical \(u_i\), but a reversed consecutive interval is still a cyclic consecutive interval; the rank conclusion is unaffected.

**3. Locally legal rectangles are first-row windows.** Set \(L=w+h-1\). Arbitrarily prescribing \(L\) consecutive binary values \(u_0,\ldots,u_{L-1}\) determines a legal rectangle via \(x_{i,j}=(1+T)^j u_i\) for \(0\le i<w\), \(0\le j<h\). Conversely the bottom row gives the first \(w\) values, and the upper-right-edge entries determine \(u_w,\ldots,u_{L-1}\) in increasing order, because the coefficient of \(u_{w+j-1}\) in \(x_{w-1,j}\) is one. This triangular recovery shows that \(\mathbb F_2^L\to\mathcal L_{w,h}\) is a linear isomorphism; the total number of legal rectangles is \(2^L\). For a square-periodic row, the \(L\) seed coordinates are simply read cyclically from \(C_n\). By the preceding lemma, their rank is \(\min(d_n,L)\), proving the image count. Rank-nullity gives constant fiber size \(2^{d_n-\min(d_n,L)}\), yielding the exact fraction and surjectivity threshold.

**4. Arithmetic extremality.** Factor \(n=2^a m\) with \(m\) odd. The Frobenius identity in characteristic two gives
\[
M_n=M_m^{2^a},\quad B_n=B_m^{2^a},\quad g_n=g_m^{2^a},
\]
so \(d_n=2^a d_m\). When \(m\) is odd, \(M_m=(t+1)H_m\) with \(H_m=1+t+\cdots+t^{m-1}\). The factor \(t+1\) does not divide \(B_m\), since \(B_m(1)=1\). Therefore \(g_m\mid H_m\) and \(d_m\le m-1\). Equality means \(H_m\mid B_m\). Because \(B_m\) has degree \(m\), is monic, and has zero constant coefficient while \(H_m(0)=1\), equality forces \(B_m=tH_m\), or
\[
(1+t)^m=1+t+t^2+\cdots+t^m.
\]
By the binomial parity criterion (equivalently Lucas's theorem modulo two), every binomial coefficient \(\binom mj\) is odd precisely when the binary expansion of \(m\) contains only ones: \(m=2^k-1\). This establishes necessity and sufficiency. Taking \(m=1\) proves \(d_{2^a}=0\). Taking \(a=0,m=2^k-1\) gives \(d_n=n-1\), completing the proof.

**5. A dynamical consequence.** For each finite locally legal rectangle, choose a sufficiently large Mersenne period \(n=2^k-1\) with \(n\ge w+h\). Then that rectangle extends to an \(n\)-square-periodic point. Therefore configurations with equal horizontal and vertical Mersenne periods form a dense subset of the three-dot subshift. This is an explicit, quantitative version of periodic-point density, not a claim that such density was previously unknown.

## Verification

`ledrappier_verify.py` is a standalone pure-Python exact arithmetic verifier. Run `python3 ledrappier_verify.py` using Python 3 without third-party libraries. It performs GF(2) polynomial division, gcd and modular exponentiation, independently computes the cyclic-code basis, and evaluates rectangular restrictions from the cellular recurrence. It checks all \(1\le n\le34\); for \(n\le16\), it checks a range of rectangular dimensions including widths/heights greater than \(n\); and for \(n\le10\), it also enumerates all seed vectors and directly compares their images with the predicted rank. The successful terminal output is `VERIFY_OK: n=1..34; widths/heights through min(8,n+3) for n<=16; exhaustive n<=10 tests`. These finite checks test formulas and corner cases but do not replace the universal proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Salo (2026), *Periodic points and residual finiteness of automorphism groups of subshifts* (arXiv:2603.16591v1; publicly posted 2026-03-17), studies the structural significance of dense totally periodic points for automorphism groups; it does not supply the all-period, rectangle-by-rectangle extension profile calculated here. Salo (2025), *Avoshifts, Unishifts and Nondeterministic Cellular Automata* (arXiv:2504.11071), discusses the three-dot subshift as a cellular-automaton spacetime and establishes broader periodic-point density mechanisms.

Miles and Ward (2015), *The dynamical zeta function for commuting automorphisms of zero-dimensional groups* (arXiv:1504.04973), explicitly compute the Mersenne-square-period cardinality in their Ledrappier example, agreeing with \(2^{n-1}\) for \(n=2^k-1\). That **cardinality is prior work**, not a novelty claim; neither the local restriction ranks for all \(w,h,n\), nor the uniform extension fraction, nor the iff arithmetic extremality classification follow merely from the isolated Mersenne counts. The cyclic-module gcd description relates to established algebraic-dynamical and coding-theoretic methods. Kari and Moutot (2018), *Nivat's Conjecture and Pattern Complexity in Algebraic Subshifts* (arXiv:1806.07107), analyze low-complexity configurations but do not classify rectangular square-periodic extension images.

## Limitations

The result concerns the standard binary three-dot relation, equal prescribed horizontal/vertical periods \(n\), and axis-parallel rectangles. It is not a classification for arbitrary sublattices, unequal periods, other prime characteristics, or arbitrary algebraic shifts. Period \(n\) is not least period \(n\). The proof is a direct finite-field argument; finite verification does not establish the infinite quantifiers. Prior algebraic-dynamics literature establishes general periodic-point algebra, and an undiscovered equivalent formulation of the all-rectangle rank theorem in cyclic-code terminology remains a historical-priority risk.
