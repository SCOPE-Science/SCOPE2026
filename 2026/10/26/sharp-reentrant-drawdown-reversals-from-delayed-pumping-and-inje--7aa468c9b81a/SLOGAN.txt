In the ideal Theis aquifer model, a delayed injection well that replaces less water than a continuously pumping well can make monitored drawdown rise, fall, then rise again. When the injection well is closer to the monitor, there is an exact reentrant delay law: for injection-rate ratio \(0<\lambda<b/a\), two turning points occur at short and long injection delays but disappear for a nonempty intermediate delay interval, with the critical maximum \(b/a\) attained at delay \(a-b\).
