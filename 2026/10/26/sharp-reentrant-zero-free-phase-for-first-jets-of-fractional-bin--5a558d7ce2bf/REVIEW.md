# Scientific assessment: sharp reentrant zero-free phase for fractional first jets

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is a complete if-and-only-if zero-set classification for all coefficient pairs and every real \(0<\beta\le1\). The Gram representation is obtained by differentiation of the binomial kernel. Its boundary torus numerator splits into two explicit real equations; elimination gives the exact factor \(\beta(1-\beta)(t^2-4/(1-\beta))(t^2+4/\beta)\). Below threshold the angular Jacobian is nonzero, and the real implicit function theorem moves boundary zeros strictly into the disk. Above threshold uniform large-ratio nonvanishing and two uses of the argument principle exclude *all* interior zeros; Rouché's theorem includes equality. The pure jet cases and Hardy endpoint are checked separately. The packaged exact-arithmetic and complex checks verify key algebra, but the analytic proof carries the infinitely many parameters.

## Originality — PASS, with remaining risk
The 2026 Gehlawat–Jain–Sarkar source provides the general Gram idea and classical Bergman examples. The closest full published results are an exact **classical disk Bergman** first-jet dichotomy, a classical **upper-half-plane** two-jet coefficient phase, and a classical-disk **pure-jet** all-orders count. All were compared at the full mathematical statement and proof level. Their kernels, parameter spaces and conclusions do not imply the fractional reentrant phase at \(0<\beta<1\), its explicit threshold, or the Hardy endpoint. Targeted searches for aliases and stronger coverage found no direct matching theorem; unindexed equivalent work remains a priority risk. This is a best-knowledge originality judgment, not an assertion that absence from search proves novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The object is a natural family of complete Pick analytic kernels, and the question is whether point-plus-derivative Riesz Gram matrices can vanish off the diagonal. The sharp threshold reveals a structural effect not seen in the familiar classical Bergman case: nonvanishing survives for both pure jets, fails for intermediate mixtures, and returns for sufficiently derivative-dominated mixtures. The finite critical ratio and its divergence at the Hardy endpoint precisely describe the boundary between two regimes. This is an all-parameter classification, not an arbitrary selected invariant or numeric slice.

## Limits and residual risks
The classification is restricted to fixed constant first-order coefficients on the binomial-kernel Hilbert spaces with \(0<\beta\le1\). No result is claimed for arbitrary complete Pick kernels, higher derivatives, or classical Bergman exponents \(\beta>1\). The source-comparison process cannot exclude inaccessible or unindexed equivalent prior formulas. Numerical checks are not infinite proofs.
