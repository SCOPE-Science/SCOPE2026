# Scientific review: sharp reservoir-contrast threshold for stationary two-qubit entanglement

## Correctness (C): PASS

The claim concerns the unique unconditional stationary state of a specified finite-dimensional completely positive Lindblad generator with both local excitation and relaxation strictly positive. The proof derives the four stationary expectation identities explicitly and reconstructs all four populations and the only surviving coherence. Wootters' exact concurrence formula reduces to the strict positivity of a concave quadratic in a dimensionless coupling variable. The quadratic's unique interior maximum, negative boundary values, and explicit roots yield a necessary-and-sufficient contrast threshold and precisely one bounded coupling window. The balanced-reservoir factorization yields an exact square-root interval. A separately constructed Liouvillian and spin-flip concurrence computation independently test the formulas on hundreds of parameters; those finite checks support, but do not replace, the algebraic proof. The full-rank uniqueness follows from the full local matrix algebra generated by strictly positive raising and lowering channels and the irreducibility of the finite-dimensional quantum Markov semigroup.

## Originality (O): PASS, with specified literature risk

The most closely related source is Wang et al., arXiv:2302.00976v1, whose main text and relevant supplementary discussion were inspected. That work establishes the equal-ratio no-correlation result and computes spin-flip coherence, not the present globally optimized entanglement-feasibility criterion for unequal ratios. Wang, Wu and Wang, arXiv:1812.04799, examine different nonsecular Bloch–Redfield local environments and system eigenenergy-dependent rates; Tacchino et al., arXiv:1710.01377, use collective instead of local jump operators. No inspected primary source or queried mathematical record established an equivalent all-occupation threshold for this specific generator. The 2017 Triana–Fonseca conference abstract considers a related one-pump/one-loss endpoint and poses a residual risk; its full text was unavailable, and an equivalent theorem elsewhere cannot be categorically excluded. A failed database search is not proof of absolute novelty.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS

Coherence and stationary entanglement differ substantially in an open quantum system, and quantitative reservoir engineering needs a parameter criterion rather than an isolated numerical example. The result supplies a complete feasibility boundary over two arbitrary reservoir occupations, a unique finite admissible coupling interval, a precise no-entanglement region even with tunable coherent exchange and detuning, and an exact square-root opening at complementary-bath contrast. This clarifies when nonequilibrium gain–loss imbalance can generate the strictly stronger quantum resource of entanglement. The statement is not a claimed extension to unequal total dissipation rates, collective baths, general Hamiltonians, or dynamical stability.
