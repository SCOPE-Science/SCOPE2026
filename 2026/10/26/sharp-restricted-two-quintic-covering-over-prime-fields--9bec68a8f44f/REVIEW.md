# Scientific review: restricted two-quintic covering

## Correctness

PASS. A complete proof separates the bijective fifth-power case from the index-five subgroup case. For sufficiently large primes, the smooth genus-six Fermat quintic has more rational points than the at most forty points excluded by a zero coordinate, infinity, or equal fifth-power values. The exact Hasse–Weil cutoff is checked arithmetically. For the remaining eleven primes congruent to one modulo five, two independent constructions of the fifth-power set and two restricted-sum algorithms agree on every missing residue. These are exhaustive finite calculations supplementing, rather than replacing, the infinite point-count argument. The two very small primes are handled separately.

## Originality

PASS relative to accessible comparisons; publication priority is not independently certified. Demiroğlu Karabulut's inspected 2017 paper proves ordinary Waring results and its exceptions do not determine nonzero unequal-value sums. Wu, Wei and Li's inspected 2023 paper asks whether the power-residue set itself admits a decomposition, a different statement. The later Karabulut paper is supported only by its abstract and remains a residual coverage risk. Targeted record searches for restricted fifth-power sumsets, sharp characteristic thresholds, Fermat-quintic pair restrictions and the six exceptional primes found no corresponding full-field restricted spectrum; a related graph two-sum theorem is not a dominating scalar-value result. Unindexed or uninspected sources may still exist.

## Mathematical value

PASS. Exact restricted Waring thresholds are natural additive invariants of multiplicative subgroups; imposing nonzero unequal summands changes the answer even at primes where ordinary Waring number two is known. The result gives every exceptional prime, precise missing-residue spectra, a sharp eventual threshold and a geometric proof of uniform termination; none is supplied by the cited ordinary Waring classification alone.

## Limits

The theorem applies only to prime fields, quintic residues and exactly two distinct nonzero fifth-power *values*. It does not characterize higher-extension fields or quantify all representations. Full text of the later alternative Waring paper was not available. The proof is supported by standard Hasse–Weil theory and reproducible exact finite checks, not an external independent audit.
