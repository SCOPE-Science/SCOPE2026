# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The finite-cycle Laplacian squared is inverted explicitly on the zero-mean space by the displayed quartic radial Green kernel. Real \(\ell^1\) duality reduces the sharp inequality to an optimization over sign vectors. Vasilev's published cycle-power theorem applies independently to every radial layer, proving a contiguous subset is optimal at fixed cardinality. The exact block summation and strict increase in \(m(N-m)\) give the real constant. Fourier spectral-gap equality proves the complex constant and the exact real--complex separation. Exact-rational finite diagnostics support the arithmetic but do not establish the universal combinatorial statement.

## Originality
PASS with residual historical-priority risk. The closest focal primary work, Hindov--Nitzan--Olsen--Rydhe (arXiv:2610.19010v1), solves zero-extended interval differences rather than cyclic zero-mean differences. Vasilev (arXiv:2601.06912v1) provides the cycle-power rearrangement ingredient, not the Sobolev norm evaluation. Available related findings on first-order cycle energies involve \(\|Dx\|_2\) and do not imply the biharmonic constants. Semantic and formula-oriented searches found no exact matching second-difference result. The possibility of an older formulation as a periodic spline or inverse bi-Laplacian norm remains.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The endpoint \(\ell^1\)-to-\(\ell^2\) discrete Sobolev problem changes substantially when the difference is periodic and constants form its kernel. The exact all-size second-order solution exhibits polynomial parity dependence, constructs optimizers by an inverse bi-Laplacian, and distinguishes real from complex scalars, including the unique finite-size equality and the asymptotic gap. The use of a nontrivial cycle-power rearrangement theorem rather than a direct textbook norm estimate shows this is not merely a normalization change from the interval theorem.

## Remaining limitations
The analysis establishes at least one family of real extremizers and does not classify all equality sign patterns. The order of the difference is fixed at two. Historical priority for alternate periodic-Sobolev terminology has not been independently audited.
