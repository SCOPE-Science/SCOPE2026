# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Exact path-event inclusion–exclusion, strict discrete concavity of a geometric partial sum, a closed adjacent-distance difference, uniqueness of a nontrivial crossing from the derivative's single turning point, and strict ordering of adjacent crossing functions together prove the complete claim. The accompanying standalone exact-rational verification corroborates the formula in 483 exhaustive open-edge cases and 26,946 parameter-placement comparisons. These finite tests are not substituted for proof of the infinite theorem.

**Originality — PASS (self-review only).** Existing surveillance and influence work motivates the objective but does not appear to imply the exact all-order two-sentinel staircase. Bajardi et al. (2012) use directed livestock temporal networks; Takaguchi et al. (2014) use immunizing observers; Holme (2017) computes SIR time objectives on at most seven nodes; Holme (2018) compares time and eventual detection frequency on empirical data; Haldar et al. (2023) offers broader optimization machinery without the specific closed phase diagram in the accessible material. A 2013 unreliable-facility paper was accessible only in summary. The residual risk of an equivalent network-reliability or facility-location special case remains, and independent bibliographic verification has not occurred.

**Value — PASS.** Placing a pair of monitors to maximize the probability of detecting *any* outbreak is a concrete epidemic surveillance problem. The result identifies a global optimum for every path length and transmissibility, including all strict transitions and ties. Neither an arbitrary small graph calculation nor a generic assertion of optimal spread covers these structural consequences.

**Scope.** Uniform single-seed source outside fixed sentinels, perfect detection, homogeneous independent single-attempt edges, fixed path; no finite-time detection or heterogeneous/transient disease model. Only the scientific contribution has been reviewed.
