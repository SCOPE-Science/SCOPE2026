## correctness

The nine F4 points of H: X³+Y²Z+YZ²=0 form the reduced transverse cubic–cubic intersection V(H,G), G=X(Y²+YZ+Z²). The ideal cubic part is the two-dimensional pencil <H,G>; every non-H projective pencil member factors into three F4 lines. Cayley–Bacharach forces any cubic through eight of the nine points to pass through all nine, so geometrically integral distinct C meets at most seven. The displayed C0 has nonsimultaneously vanishing gradient over the algebraic closure and exactly seven common F4 points. New independent exact GF4 enumeration of all (4^10−1)/3=349525 projective cubic equations gave 576 with exactly seven common points, none with eight, five with nine, and exactly 180 seven-point forms without F4-linear factors. For each of 36 omitted pairs, these split two with seven total F4 points and one tangent, three with eight points and two tangencies (72/108 overall). A no-F4-line-factor cubic with at least seven F4 points is geometrically integral: three nonrational conjugate line components support at most three F4 points; a rational line factor was explicitly excluded. A singular integral plane cubic would have at most six F4 points by its genus-zero normalization, hence these are smooth. The Hasse–Weil maximality of H yields #H(F16)=9 and excludes degree-two H-points; Bezout then forces exactly the (3,1^6) or (2^2,1^5) contact patterns as measured by tangencies. Every count here is a complete finite computation, and the geometric implications are separate symbolic arguments.

## originality

PASS

The previously published Beelen–Datta–Montanucci–Niemann paper identifies the exceptional impossibility of nine common rational points for q=2,d=3, and the classical nine-point Cayley–Bacharach mechanism is prior art. It does not provide the complete 180 smooth seven-point companions or their 36-by-five incidence and 72/108 contact stratification; its purported q=2 Theorem 2.2 specialization is reducible, not a competing integral curve. Homma classifies cubic curves with nine total F4 points, not fixed-H seven-point intersections. Kaplan–Matei give an exact number of ordered pairs of cubic equations meeting in k rational points over Fq, but this aggregate does not condition on the fixed Hermitian H, smoothness, total F4 point counts, or tangent multiplicities. These concrete prior statements do not imply the full refined census. The field-specific sharp classification survives as best-of-knowledge original, with an explicit risk of older unindexed tables or reductions.
