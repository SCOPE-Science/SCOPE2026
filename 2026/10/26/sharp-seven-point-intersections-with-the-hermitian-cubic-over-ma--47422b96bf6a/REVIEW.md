# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The upper bound is proved by a reduced transverse \((3,3)\) complete intersection, Cayley–Bacharach, and an explicit factorization of every degree-three equation through the nine points. The smooth witness has an algebraically verified Jacobian criterion and exactly seven common rational points. Two separately constructed exact enumerators confirm 180 extremizers, five per missing pair, and the contact/point-count split. The proof explains why the finite condition of no rational linear factor suffices for geometric smoothness in this high-point stratum.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Beelen–Datta–Montanucci–Niemann (arXiv:2407.13521, 2024, introduction) identifies the exceptional \((q,d)=(2,3)\) full-intersection failure but does not give the sharp seven bound or refined 180/72/108 classification in the inspected text. Their Theorem 2.2 specialized at \(q=2\) produces the explicitly reducible equation \(H+G\), not a competing integral nine-point example. Homma's 2024 maximal-cubic classification determines types of nine-rational-point cubics, not the exact intersection profile relative to this fixed \(H\). Classical Cayley–Bacharach is not claimed as original. Searched mathematical research records and accessible earlier claims disclose no equal or stronger covering classification. Unindexed earlier small-field incidence tables remain a novelty risk.

## Value — PASS
This resolves the sharp extremal threshold and all attaining configurations for an explicitly isolated small-field exception of a published Hermitian-intersection problem. The five-per-pair refinement, rational-point splits and geometric contact distributions provide a natural complete classification beyond simply observing failure of the Bézout bound.

## Limits
Counts are of equations modulo scalar for one fixed embedding, not projective or curve-isomorphism classes. Exact finite computations support the classification; external independence and validation have not been performed.
