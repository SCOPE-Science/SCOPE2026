# Review of the cross-polytope sixth-moment theorem

## Correctness

PASS. The proof gives a complete coefficient-by-coefficient enumeration of the four possible types in the cubic Fourier convolution. Its exact polynomial sixth-moment identity yields a manifestly nonnegative, fully explicit deficit decomposition. All normalization, boundary and equality cases are accounted for, including the one-coordinate case and the finite-order aliasing threshold. The reproducible Gaussian-integer checker completed 2923 exact comparisons, with direct convolution independently compared to the symbolic identity and deficit. These finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the written argument.

## Originality

PASS under currently inspected literature, with residual priority uncertainty. The closest full mathematical arguments inspected were Havrilla--Nayar--Tkocz (arXiv:2102.09500, Gaussian comparison for type-L variables), Norvidas (arXiv:2209.12294, large-sieve sampling of trigonometric powers), and Hubert--Metzlaff--Moustrou--Riener (arXiv:2303.09487, pointwise optimization of reflection-invariant trigonometric polynomials). Their statements do not entail this exact finite-dimensional complex-coefficient sixth-moment constant or its positive deficit. Nearby published finite-spectrum sixth-moment results concern different spectra and collision structures. A formula expressed as an arcsine-sum moment or a stronger coefficient-majorization theorem could already cover this result; independent exhaustive bibliographic confirmation is absent.

## Value

PASS. A standard universal Gaussian moment comparison is not sharp for a fixed number of antipodal coordinate pairs. The theorem supplies the sharp dimension correction, all complex equality cases and a coefficientwise positive certificate quantifying three separate failure modes. It also identifies the exact finite-grid nonaliasing threshold and gives an explicit counterexample at the boundary. The claim does not rely for value on a conjectured downstream theorem.

## Limitations

The theorem is restricted to sixth moments and coordinate-cross spectra; it does not classify the optimal moment at finite cyclic order six or all higher even moments. Numerical verification is finite, while validity in arbitrary dimension follows from the symbolic proof. Literature priority remains subject to independent examination.
