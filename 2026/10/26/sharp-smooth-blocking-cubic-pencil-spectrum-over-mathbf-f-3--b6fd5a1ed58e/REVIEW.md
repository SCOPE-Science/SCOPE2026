# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The proof fixes exact finite domains and a unique normal form for every pencil through the distinguished cubic. The basepoint-free condition is checked at all six rational points, and each rational pencil member is tested for geometric smoothness. Complete C++ enumeration and independently implemented exact Gröbner-basis computations agree on the full 312-vector list, on the global 936 smooth blocking cubics, and on the four point-count profiles. The incidence partition and Hasse–Weil give the at-most-one blocking-member bound. Crucially, the Gröbner checks address singularities over the algebraic closure rather than extrapolating from selected extensions.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
The closest sources concern (i) existence of all-smooth pencils, (ii) isolated smooth blocking cubic curves, and (iii) bounds on blocking proportions for pencils. They do not state the joint small-field smoothness/blocking census, the sharp three-nonblocking statement in this restricted class, or the four exact profile multiplicities. Published bounds do not imply these exact masses. An older small-field catalogue or unpublished computation may implicitly encode the count; this is a publication-priority risk, not a proved uniqueness result based on failed searches.

## Value — PASS
The question couples two existing natural restrictions on pencil geometry—having no rational base points and having all rational fibers smooth—with the exceptional blocking property at the smallest odd field that admits smooth blocking cubics. The global theorem improves the general small-prime nonblocking guarantee under a meaningful geometric hypothesis. Its exact equality mass and four distinct rational-point profiles describe a complete, motivated finite incidence locus, not an arbitrary coefficient slice.
