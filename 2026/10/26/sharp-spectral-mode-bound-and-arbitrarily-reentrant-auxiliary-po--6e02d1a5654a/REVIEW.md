# Mathematical assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The positivity denominator is positive by a symmetric positive-definite resolvent identity. The signed auxiliary numerator reduces to a rational function with at most one pole per active positive eigenvalue, producing the sharp polynomial-degree upper bound. The rational interpolation weights match all pole residues and the value at zero, so they realize every prescribed set of simple positive zeros. The quadratic potential is strictly convex and everywhere positive with consistent initial auxiliary value. Exact rational and independent direct-update calculations verify the two-dimensional example; finite tests are not substituted for the universal proof.

**Originality — PASS, subject to residual bibliographic risk.** Huang–Liu–Wang–Yan, arXiv:2610.01063v1, Section 2, explicitly provides the exact one-step positivity test and scalar sign-loss example; neither is new here. The claimed result is instead a sharp bound on the number of positivity intervals **as the stepsize varies** and a constructive converse realizing any prescribed sign-change thresholds while retaining a positive convex energy. The source's fixed-stepsize iff test, single-mode example, and sufficient stepsize condition do not state or establish these global topological and interpolation assertions. Zhang–Shen, arXiv:2606.18551v1, deals with a modified SAV correction and does not imply them. Search comparisons with published work on other integrators showed no directly subsuming statement. The pointwise criterion makes the new spectral deduction accessible; unindexed earlier special cases or an implicit equivalent remain possible.

**Value — PASS.** A practical step-size restriction is commonly expected to be an initial safe interval. This result proves that the safe set for an energy-stable SAV discretization may consist of arbitrarily many disjoint intervals even for strongly convex quadratic gradient-flow energies. The exact modal complexity limit and realization theorem give a mathematically natural classification of this nonmonotonicity, and the two-mode example provides a minimal diagnostic for stepsize controllers.

The theorem is exact-arithmetic, finite-dimensional, and specific to the original SAV-BE method. It does not establish state positivity, multi-step trajectory behavior, or the same phenomenon for the new positivity-preserving methods in the source.
