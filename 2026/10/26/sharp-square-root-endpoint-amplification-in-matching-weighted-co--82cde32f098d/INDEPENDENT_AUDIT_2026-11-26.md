# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The weighted vertex Laplacian acts with eigenvalues 0, 2d, n and h on the constant, opposite-constant and two mean-zero symmetry sectors. Inverting those sectors on the incidence columns gives the stated transfer currents; multiplying by the two edge square-root weights gives Q. There are two source types. For a matching source the incident off-diagonal voltages are positive and the nonincident ones negative. For a nonmatching source, the two classes of n−2 incident nonmatching targets have opposite signs and their magnitudes add to 1/n per pair, and the reversed target contributes |W|. Its numerator decreases from n−2 at x²=0 to −∞. Summing each class gives C_D and C_O. Symmetry gives the same row and column maxima, and replacing just the diagonal by 1−Q_ee gives Z_D and Z_O since 0≤Q_ee≤1. The nonmatching columns and the own-weight part of the matching sum are uniformly bounded. The other term divided by √n converges uniformly to F(α) for α=x²/n: convergence is uniform on compacts, with uniform O(√α) and O(1/√α) tails. The derivative changes sign at the unique positive zero of the stated strictly increasing cubic. At that fixed α, F_n(α)−F(α)=O(1/n), giving the stated O(1) error in the unscaled norm; the cut and cycle norms differ by at most one. Direct substitution gives the x=1 formulas. Independent rational grounded-matrix inversion of 370 columns in 15 graphs confirmed both sets of sums and the signed W entry.

## originality

PASS

The qualitative √n law for conductance tuning on balanced bicliques already occurs in the one-edge Resultary result, and the electrical projection machinery is standard. The supported distinct claim is the exact all-parameter classification for a full equal-weight matching, including the W sign transition and its own sharp optimized coefficient. The closest rank-one formula and the spectral-norm theorem do not give that classification.

## value

PASS

Equal conductance on an entire perfect matching is a natural uniform symmetry class of a weighted biclique. The all-parameter column sums give a precise endpoint amplification diagnostic, show the sign switch that an unsigned or spectral estimate misses, and distinguish this collective perturbation's sharp coefficient from the known one-edge regime. The result quantifies a size-dependent effect not captured by the uniformly bounded norms at equal weights.
