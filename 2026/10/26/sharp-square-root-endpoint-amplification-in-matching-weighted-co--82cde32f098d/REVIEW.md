# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
A four-eigenspace inverse of the weighted vertex Laplacian yields explicit matching/nonmatching transfer-current columns. Every entry sign except one is fixed by the allowed parameters; the remaining absolute value is retained rather than assumed. Summing the signed classes gives the exact four column norms. Uniform upper bounds and a one-variable stationary cubic prove the sharp common square-root asymptotic. A separate exact-rational grounded-Laplacian reconstruction checks all 516 columns across 24 configurations. Neither the computation nor finite sampling is used to prove the infinite-parameter theorem.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The closest primary literature on cycle-space projections treats discrete tori, Hamming graphs and recursively generated graph families; the closely related weighted electrical-flow localization theorem bounds a different spectral operator norm. Searches of mathematical research records using matching-weighted bicliques, transfer currents, projection column norms and square-root amplification returned no covering result. Comparisons with existing weighted complete-graph matching, deleted-matching biclique and two-community clique calculations show no logical implication for this weighted biclique family. Standard Laplacian inversion is prior technique and is not claimed new. The main residual risk is a less-indexed network calculation equivalent to the exact formulas.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The exact full-parameter classification solves a natural perturbation of the highly symmetric balanced biclique, while its conductance-optimized norm grows at a provably sharp square-root rate with a cubic-algebraic constant. This identifies substantial ℓ1/ℓ∞ amplification that disappears at uniform conductance and is invisible to generic spectral-norm transfer-current bounds. These conclusions provide motivated operator invariants rather than an arbitrary parameter sample.

## Limitations
Only real canonical Euclidean-orthogonal projections in square-root-conductance coordinates are covered; no minimum over other projections or extension to arbitrary edge weights is asserted. Novelty cannot be established by failed searches alone. The scientific review is not independent.
