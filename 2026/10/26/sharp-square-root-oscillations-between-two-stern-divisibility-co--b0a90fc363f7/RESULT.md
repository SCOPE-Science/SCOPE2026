# Sharp square-root oscillations between two Stern divisibility counts

## Finding
For Stern's diatomic sequence \(s(0)=0\), \(s(1)=1\), \(s(2n)=s(n)\), and \(s(2n+1)=s(n)+s(n+1)\), let
\[T_r(d)=\#\{0\le n<2^r:d\mid s(n)\},\qquad E_r=T_r(8)-T_r(6).\]
For **every integer** \(r\ge0\), the exact ordinary generating function is
\[\boxed{\sum_{r\ge0} E_rz^r=\frac{2z^5(1-z)}{L(z)}}\]
where
\[L(z)=(1+z)(1-2z+2z^2)(1-z+2z^2)(1+z+2z^2)(1-z^2+4z^4).\]
Furthermore,
\[\boxed{\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{E_r}{2^{r/2}}>0,\qquad \liminf_{r\to\infty}\frac{E_r}{2^{r/2}}<0.}\]
Hence neither sign eventually dominates, and the exact discrepancy oscillates at a sharp square-root scale even though the two congruence classes have the same leading density \(1/12\).

## Assumptions and scope
All indices \(r\) are nonnegative integers, and all counted Stern values include \(s(0)=0\). No primality restrictions are imposed; the moduli are specifically \(6\) and \(8\). The two-sided limit statements are limsup/liminf inequalities, not claims of a limiting periodic function or an exact best amplitude constant.

## Proof
**1. Reduction to finite residue dynamics.** For a modulus \(m\in\{6,8\}\), let \(\mathcal P_m\) be the primitive residue pairs \((a,b)\in(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^2\), satisfying \(\gcd(a,b,m)=1\), modulo simultaneous multiplication by units of \(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\). Each \(\mathcal P_m\) has **exactly 12 elements**. Let \(v_m\) be the unit basis vector for \([1,1]\), \(w_m\) the indicator vector for the class \([0,1]\), and \(M_m\) the nonnegative integer transfer matrix defined, with multiplicities, by
\[M_m[a,b]=[a,a+b]+[a+b,b].\]
The right side denotes a sum of basis vectors, not equality of residue pairs. Every Stern pair in the dyadic shell \(2^k\le n<2^{k+1}\) is obtained from \((1,1)\) by \(k\) applications of these two child maps. Therefore, writing
\[D_m(k)=\#\{2^k\le n<2^{k+1}:m\mid s(n)\},\]
we have \(D_m(k)=w_m^{\mathsf T}M_m^kv_m\) for every \(k\ge0\), and \(T_r(m)=1+\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}D_m(k)\). The initial \(1\) comes from \(s(0)=0\).

**2. Exact polynomial actions.** In \(\mathbb Z[x]\), define
\[P_6(x)=(x-2)(x+1)(x^2-2x+2)(x^2+x+2),\]
\[P_8(x)=(x-2)(x+1)(x^2-x+2)(x^4-x^2+4).\]
A finite exact integer calculation on all 12 projective states in each modulus verifies
\[\boxed{P_6(M_6)v_6=0,\qquad P_8(M_8)v_8=0.}\]
The standalone verifier builds states by enumerating all primitive residue pairs, canonically quotients by all units, applies the two explicit transitions, and computes the above integer vector identities. It checks the full vector equalities, not merely a finite run of output values. These identities are independently reconstructible from the definitions without external numerical data. The first \(\deg P_m\) shell counts then give the exact rational functions
\[\sum_{k\ge0}D_6(k)z^k=\frac{4z^5}{(1+z)(1-2z)(1-2z+2z^2)(1+z+2z^2)},\]
\[\sum_{k\ge0}D_8(k)z^k=\frac{2z^4(1-z+4z^3)}{(1+z)(1-2z)(1-z+2z^2)(1-z^2+4z^4)}.\]
The initial counts and polynomial-action identities imply these fractions **for all indices**, since they supply exact linear recurrences. Direct subtraction and cancellation yields
\[\sum_{k\ge0}(D_8(k)-D_6(k))z^k=\frac{2z^4(1-z)^2}{L(z)}.\]
Multiplication by \(z/(1-z)\), corresponding to summing previous shells and cancelling the shared \(s(0)\) contribution, gives the stated generating function for \(E_r\).

**3. Sharp two-sided oscillations.** Each of the ten roots of the four higher-degree factors of \(L(z)\) has absolute value \(2^{-1/2}\), is nonreal, is simple, and is distinct from the others. This follows directly from their displayed quadratic and quartic equations (the quartic has \(z^2=(1\pm i\sqrt{15})/8\)). The only other root is \(-1\). The numerator \(2z^5(1-z)\) vanishes at none of these roots. Thus partial fractions give
\[\frac{E_r}{2^{r/2}}=\sum_{j=1}^{10}c_j e^{ir\theta_j}+O(2^{-r/2}),\]
where every \(c_j\ne0\), the \(e^{i\theta_j}\) are distinct, nonreal unit complex numbers, and the sum is real. The normalized discrepancy is bounded. Elementary Cesàro orthogonality of distinct unit complex exponentials shows that its limiting Cesàro mean is zero, while its limiting Cesàro mean square is \(\sum_j|c_j|^2>0\) (the conjugate roots pair). A bounded real sequence with these two properties necessarily has strictly positive limsup and strictly negative liminf: otherwise one-sided eventual bounds arbitrarily close to zero, together with zero mean, would force the mean square to vanish. This proves both inequalities.

## Verification
Run \(\texttt{python3 verify.py}\) with the Python standard library. It checks the state counts and both exact integer polynomial-action certificates, reconstructs both rational functions and their exact difference by polynomial convolution, and independently compares 14 complete dyadic shells against Stern values generated by the defining recurrence. The resulting output is \(\texttt{VERIFY_OK}\). The all-index proof depends on the vector annihilation identities, not on extrapolation from the finite shell enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Reznick's *Regularity Properties of the Stern Enumeration of the Rationals*, originally public in October 2006 and published in 2008, proves general modular equidistribution and explicitly observes that \(T_r(8)\) and \(T_r(6)\) are not identical even though their asymptotic densities are both \(1/12\). The present result supplies an exact simplified rational generating function for their difference and proves sharp two-sided oscillations at scale \(2^{r/2}\), which the inspected result does not state. The standard transfer-matrix method for Stern's sequence and related sequences is not in itself claimed to be new.

## Limitations
The claim concerns two fixed moduli; no optimal sign-extremum constants or analogous classification for all modulus pairs is claimed. The finite exact calculations certify recurrence relations; the infinite conclusions additionally require the given algebraic and asymptotic arguments. Research priority beyond the inspected sources remains subject to independent verification; unpublished course notes and unlocated stronger general modular-distribution results are residual risks.
