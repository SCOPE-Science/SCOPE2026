# Research review: critical backward-shift lower moments

## Correctness

The proposed limit is established by two matching inequalities. All vectors are represented exactly by coordinate-energy probability measures. For each fixed time fraction the reciprocal-kernel estimate is uniform over all probability measures, including infinite support. Jensen's inequality handles the initial time interval, and concavity tangents handle the complementary interval. The tangent multiplier is explicitly optimized, and the shrinking cutoff gives the claimed upper constant. The specified positive finite-support vectors supply the lower constant by a bulk-uniform harmonic-sum approximation, followed by a shrinking endpoint cutoff. No numerical inference is used as an infinite-dimensional proof. The domain includes \(p=1\) and \(q<1\) without reliance on triangle inequalities for the \(q\)-moment.

## Originality

The closest focal primary paper is Abbar–Arnold–Coine (2026), Theorem 3.4 and Example 3.10: it treats boundedness and boundary failure of power-law weighted shifts, including an if-and-only-if test for higher moments. It does not state the sharp finite-horizon logarithmic coefficient for \(0<q<p\). Bernardes–Bonilla–Pinto (2025), Corollary 58, reduces the \(p\)-moment bound to basis tests and does not imply the lower-moment sharp coefficient. A related indexed mathematical result gives an exact harmonic profile for a possibly different extremal weighted shift in the \(p\)-moment case. That case gives only the nonsharp upper coefficient one for strictly lower moments by Jensen, whereas the derived coefficient is strictly smaller. Arnold (arXiv:2610.00271v1) derives a logarithmic moment estimate under an ACB assumption, which fails for the shift here. The indexed abstract of the harmonic profile was accessible; its full public repository text was not retrieved. Older equivalent formulations involving concave Hardy kernels remain a priority risk, and the search record is not an independent proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value

At the canonical exponent boundary where absolute Cesàro boundedness fails, the result computes the precise leading rate for every subcritical moment instead of merely establishing unboundedness. Its diffuse witness separates the nonlinear lower-moment optimization from the basis-vector geometry of the previously treated \(p\)-moment, and the sharp coefficient varies nontrivially with the ratio of exponents. Any coordinate basis vector has bounded lower-moment averages at every horizon, so the extremal growth genuinely requires diffuse energy. It yields a reusable variational and kernel estimate rather than an arbitrary finite numerical constant.

## Limits

The limit holds for fixed exponents and this exact critical weight, without an exact finite-horizon formula, uniqueness classification, or uniform near-endpoint expansion. Priority risk persists from possible unindexed equivalent Hardy inequalities. Finite diagnostic calculations do not provide a proof or publication-priority certification.
