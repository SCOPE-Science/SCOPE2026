# Scientific self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The claim concerns one completely specified 16-vertex graph, 32 named color slots and finitely many functions. Feasibility is an if-and-only-if neighborhood test. All \(4{,}514{,}873\) assignments with at most seven tokens are exhausted; the two independent source programs agree that precisely \(320\) feasible functions have weight seven and none have smaller weight. The affine-group action and optional global color exchange exhaust the minimum set in two orbits; all \(51{,}040\) pairs are checked, and all-source breadth-first searches establish the precise exchange geometry. Risks are limited to implementation error despite structurally different checkers and to the explicitly stated single-graph scope.

**Originality — PASS, subject to priority uncertainty.** The 2013 open-access toroidal-grid paper characterizes equality in a lower bound, while Shao et al. (2019) establish the scalar 2-rainbow domination numbers of the relevant cycle products. Because \(Q_4\square K_2\cong Q_5\), the full minimum-function set is also a set of optimal length-five binary covering codes and falls within Östergård–Weakley’s 2000 classification up to code equivalence. Consequently the number and isomorphism types of optimal functions are treated as established or computable background, not the novelty. The new assertion is precisely the sharp three-token exchange graph, its regular connected diameter-three geometry and two-class equitable neighbor matrix, not the scalar minimum. Mathematical-record searches under the precise object, slot encoding, orbit counts, token distance and exchange metric found no covering abstract or stated theorem. The subscription-only 2019 article and the 2000 covering-code full text could not be checked in full. Either might subsume the exact exchange statement; this is an unusually material residual priority risk rather than a proven noncoverage. Publication uniqueness is not certified by a negative search.

**Value — PASS.** The natural question of changing optimal covering configurations while preserving their size exposes a sharp local-repair barrier for a canonical graph, not a loose collection of samples: all \(320\) optimal functions are separated by at least three token movements, yet the first admissible exchange radius yields a connected diameter-three, regular state graph. Two naturally distinct double-label classes form an equitable partition with different intra-/inter-class exchange degrees. Neither phenomenon follows from the known optimum weight, and the finite classification is directly usable in reconfiguration and local-search analyses.

**Scientific limits.** The result is restricted to the binary four-cube, with color-token rearrangements permitted to change colors and only endpoints constrained; intermediate feasibility at smaller changes is impossible between distinct minimum states. Priority against inaccessible published supplementary material remains uncertain.
