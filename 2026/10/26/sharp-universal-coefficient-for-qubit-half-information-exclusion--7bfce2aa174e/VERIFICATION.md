The ensemble identity is obtained by expanding the Holevo information and the two binary mutual informations in Bloch coordinates. The Hessian is evaluated on the open unit ball; the positive-definite radial term is reduced by a Schur complement. The determinant identity is expanded exactly, and the bound \(\delta(s)\ge1/3\) follows from the power series for \(\operatorname{artanh}(\sqrt{s})/\sqrt{s}\). The determinant threshold is bounded above by \(s(1-2\delta)/\delta\), and the family \((X,Y,Z)=(s-Z,0,Z)\) as \(Z\downarrow0\) proves that this threshold is sharp. If a larger coefficient were used, the determinant becomes negative at an interior point and a small symmetric two-state ensemble violates the proposed inequality.

The numerical estimate \(c_*\approx0.7906755\) is illustrative only. The exact theorem uses the supremum formula, and no uniqueness of the numerical maximizer or certified decimal enclosure is asserted. The boundary extension uses continuity of binary entropy. The two-qubit corollary follows from the sharpened ensemble inequality in both orientations and relative-entropy data processing under local dephasing.

Scope limits: finite qubit ensembles, complementary Pauli projective measurements, and base-two entropies. No claim is made for higher dimensions, nonorthogonal axes, arbitrary POVMs, or experimental finite-sample corrections.
