# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** The metric admissibility inequalities enumerate every triangle type. Finite-dimensional symmetry averaging is contractive and leaves an arbitrary linear extension equivariant, and constant preservation is forced by boundedness in the Lipschitz seminorm. Zero-sum equilateral coefficient norms are exactly one half their total variation. Clipping the remaining scalar parameter, solving the monotone two-line minimax, and checking that the target-pair term is redundant by \(s\ge|a-b|\) give a complete all-parameter proof. The explicit kernels are nonnegative and constant-preserving, yielding the claimed universal real Banach-valued sharpness through transportation and one-dimensional norming functionals. A separate unrestricted LP and rational coefficient checker test fourteen finite rational cases; these are corroboration, not an infinite proof.

**Originality: PASS (best-knowledge, bibliographic risk).** Bruè–Di Marino–Stra's 2018 Theorem 4.1 concerns existence with logarithmic doubling bounds, not exact paired-layer norms. Cavagnari–Savaré–Sodini's 2023 Theorem 2.13 concerns equivariant Lipschitz maps between Hilbert spaces rather than a linear operator minimizing the Banach-valued extension factor. The closest published summary involving independently shortened matching pairs concerns faces of Lipschitz-free balls, not simultaneous sharp linear extensions, and the record's full text was not accessible at review time. Nearby equilateral-source one-point and double-star extension statements have different numbers and geometries of new points. Equivalent older or sparsely indexed finite-metric extension computations remain a residual priority risk.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** The result is a complete, natural, exact classification of unrestricted real-linear extension constants on a three-continuous-parameter metric family in every \(n\ge3\), simultaneously for all nonzero real Banach targets. Two features need the exact analysis: the entire family is insensitive to the within-target distance despite that distance entering the operator norm, and the optimal *positive* kernel loses its matched-source support at a sharp ratio before entering a nontrivial plateau. The first strictly expansive plateau occurs already with four paired points. This is not only a recomputation of a known scalar coefficient norm.

**Scope and risks.** The formula does not address general asymmetric point configurations, nonlinear extensions, complex targets, or uniqueness outside the permutation-equivariant class. No mathematical priority claim should be inferred solely from failed searches.
