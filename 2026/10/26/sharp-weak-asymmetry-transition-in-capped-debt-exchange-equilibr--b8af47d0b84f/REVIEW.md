# Scientific review: capped-debt weak-asymmetry transition

## Correctness — PASS
The stationary measure follows by checking detailed balance of the explicit positive and nonpositive transfer weights. A sector with aggregate debt \(d\) and \(j\) debtor sites has the exact stars-and-bars count stated in the result. Uniform Stirling estimates reduce its joint logarithmic weight to a strictly concave, explicit two-variable entropy. The unique saddle yields both the critical debt parameter and the debtor-site fraction; conditioning on these counts reduces the coordinates to uniform compositions, with exact tail ratios converging to independent exponentials. This establishes stationary fixed-marginal and empirical weak limits. The independent checker exercises 278 exact identities and 12 numerical critical-curve checks as well as finite-size distributions; its numerical asymptotic comparisons are corroboration, not a replacement for the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Sakagawa's Proposition 1.1 supplies the broader finite-dimensional stationary product formula and is credited. His Theorem 1.4 assumes a fixed \(N\)-independent transfer function and classifies its fixed-rate asymptotic regimes; the weak-asymmetry scaling \(g_N^+/g_N^-=e^{\tau/N}\) lies outside that theorem. It displays an intermediate unsaturated but positive-debt law and an explicit kink at \(\tau_c=1/[\kappa\sqrt{r(1+r)}]\), not identified in the inspected material. Cao–Reed (2025) Lemma 2.3 and Theorem 2.4 already establish the general finite-state reversible product law, so the detailed-balance ingredient is not claimed as new. Their inspected Sections 2–3 specialize to a different nonlinear rate and a formal fixed-money-per-agent ODE; neither computes the simultaneous inverse-wealth weak-bias crossover or its debt-saturation threshold. Cao–Motsch (2023) treat the unbiased mean-field problem, and Lanchier–Reed’s accessible statements cover uniform transaction rules and fixed-rule wealth limits. Multiple semantic searches against published mathematical records and review of the accessible existing ledger identified no implication covering the new statement. The full text of one older debt model and indirect consequences of general Gibbs-conditioning theorems remain priority risks; an unsuccessful search does not establish uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The question is forced by a genuine discontinuity between the two fixed-rate limits: what happens if the rate contrast shrinks at the natural inverse-wealth scale? The answer is a sharp, analytically characterized bank-saturation transition, with an open interval of strictly positive but nonbinding debt densities and an explicit family of limiting wealth laws. The finite-size combinatorics turns qualitative competing regimes into a complete quantitative crossover theorem that applies to any symmetric connected interaction graph. Its significance does not depend on a claim of universal mixing rates or on numerical experiments.
