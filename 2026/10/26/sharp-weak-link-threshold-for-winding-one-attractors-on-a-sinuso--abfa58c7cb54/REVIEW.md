# Scientific review

## Correctness
The fixed-point condition is precisely equal edge current because the update per vertex cannot wrap by a full turn for the stated step size. The reduced potential Hessian is a weighted cycle Laplacian. The proof rules out any attracting winding-one state with a strong edge on the negative-cosine branch, reduces all remaining candidates to the scalar equation \(w=\sin((2\pi-\beta)/m)/\sin\beta\), proves this equation has one nondegenerate minimum, and uses the exact constrained Hessian inertia to determine stability. The asymptotic law follows from an implicit-function expansion at \(\tan t_*=\pi+t_*\). Independent finite-size calculations of physical Hessian eigenvalues agree with the signs predicted by the proof. The checker is not a proof of the infinite-parameter claim.

## Originality
The 2026 discrete Huygens papers provide the graph potential, discrete phase model and unweighted repulsive cycle classification. The 2016 single-loop Kuramoto study establishes loop-current and one-obtuse-edge restrictions, mostly in a common-edge-coupling framework. The 2017 cycle-flow study covers arbitrary edge capacities in its broad formalism and characterizes normal-operation states; its general method could be specialized, but the inspected text does not give this exact weak-link saddle-node threshold or its \(1/(n-1)\) expansion. Published research record searches of edge-defect thresholds, aliases and equivalent formulas did not identify the same theorem. No general priority or uniqueness certificate is claimed; a separate human literature comparison remains worthwhile.

## Mathematical value
The exact edge-weight threshold identifies where persistent winding-one lockings are lost even with identical intrinsic frequencies; it gives the complete linearly stable count in these winding sectors, a nondegenerate saddle-node mechanism, and an explicit finite-size law with a cubic correction. This is a natural structural invariant of defective oscillator rings, not an arbitrary numerical slice. More general weighted-cycle fixed-point formalisms are acknowledged rather than claimed as new.

## Limitations and residual priority risk
Only a single exceptional edge, attractive sinusoidal coupling, winding \(\pm1\), and \(0<h<1/4\) are covered. Nonlinear critical-fold attraction, other windings, basins, nonstationary attractors and general graphs are not classified. Earlier weighted-cycle-flow methods might imply a portion of the result through specialization; the absence of a matching explicitly stated threshold in inspected texts does not establish absolute novelty.
