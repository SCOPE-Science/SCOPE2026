# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem has precisely quantified hypotheses \(\beta>-1\) and \((\beta+2)/3<q<(\beta+2)/2\), specifies the unrooted moment and area normalization, and keeps the full complex harmonic modulus. The shear identity follows directly by differentiating \(h_k-g_k\). Differentiation in the stretched coordinate and a real-basepoint integral determine the holomorphic limit \(H\), including its normalization; conjugation converts it to \(2\operatorname{Re}H\). The global bound \(O(|1-z|^{-2}(1-k+|1-z|)^{-1})\), established by a straight-segment integral, gives an integrable rescaled majorant exactly in the stated open window. The positive coefficient is therefore an actual limit, not a fit from finite computations. Boundary and endpoint claims outside the theorem are expressly excluded.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
The original Koebe shear and explicit coefficients are known from Wang–Wang–Rasila–Qiu (arXiv:2405.19852), and the generalized family is known from Wang–Qiu–Rasila (arXiv:2505.12562). Parashar–Ponnusamy–Kaliraj (arXiv:2608.28121) prove integral mean and fixed-distortion Bergman membership estimates; Das–Rasila (arXiv:2507.19158) prove other Bergman membership assertions. The present exact \(k\uparrow1\) profile and positive leading constant require a uniform singular-perturbation analysis absent from the inspected sections of those works. The named sources are not novelty certificates: an unindexed asymptotic treatment or an equivalent reformulation might exist, and one related older Bergman paper was inspected only through its abstract and publisher summary.

## Value — PASS
The known family interpolates between an order-two finite-distortion boundary singularity and an order-three limiting harmonic Koebe singularity. The two natural membership thresholds leave a nontrivial interval where every finite-distortion moment is finite but the limiting moment diverges. The theorem supplies both the exact rate and a reproducible, finite, strictly positive coefficient on this entire interval, making that phase change quantitative. This is more than a routine substitution into fixed-parameter bounds: it resolves the nonuniform limit and its full angular cancellation geometry.

## Remaining limitations
There is no classification of arbitrary harmonic mappings, no claim at either exponent endpoint, no coefficient expressed through elementary constants in general, and no independent review. Published priority requires additional external scrutiny.
