# Sharp width-controlled online-shattering thresholds for finite poset ideals

## Finding
Let \(P\) be a finite nonempty partially ordered set, and let \(\mathcal J(P)\subseteq\{0,1\}^{P}\) comprise the characteristic functions of all lower order ideals, including the empty ideal. Its width \(w(P)\) is the largest cardinality of an antichain. For integers \(w\ge1\) and \(K\ge w\), write \(K=qw+r\), where \(q\ge1\) and \(0\le r<w\), and set
\[
N_w(K)=(w-r)(2^q-1)+r(2^{q+1}-1)=2^q(w+r)-w.
\]
**Sharp threshold and rigidity.** Every finite poset of width \(w\) with \(\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal J(P))\ge K\) has at least \(N_w(K)\) elements. Equality in order is possible, and if \(|P|=N_w(K)\) and \(\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal J(P))\ge K\), then \(P\) is, up to isomorphism, **exactly** the disjoint union of \(w-r\) chains of length \(2^q-1\) and \(r\) chains of length \(2^{q+1}-1\). Conversely, this poset has Littlestone dimension exactly \(K\).

**Connected threshold.** For \(w\ge2\), the smallest cardinality of a poset of width \(w\), connected comparability graph, and Littlestone dimension at least \(K\) is \(N_w(K)+1\). One sharp construction adjoins a new global minimum to the above disjoint union of chains.

**Fixed-size maximum.** For every \(n\ge w\ge1\),
\[
\max_{|P|=n,\,w(P)=w}\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal J(P))
=\max\{K\in\mathbb Z:K\ge w,\ N_w(K)\le n\}.
\]
Moreover \(\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal J(P))=w(P)\), so subtracting \(w\) gives the maximum possible adaptive-minus-static shattering gap for fixed \((n,w)\). All maxima and thresholds use vertex-membership queries, not queries about order relations or lattice elements.

## Assumptions and scope
The domain of each Boolean hypothesis is the ground set of the poset; labeling a query \(x\in P\) by one means \(x\) belongs to the ideal. A depth-\(d\) complete binary query tree is shattered when every binary root-to-leaf path is realized by at least one fixed ideal. \(\operatorname{Ldim}\) is the maximum such depth and \(\operatorname{VCdim}\) is the ordinary largest shattered *fixed* query set. A chain of length \(\ell\) has \(\ell\) elements, not \(\ell\) covering relations. The comparability graph connects two distinct elements whenever they are comparable; connectivity here is equivalent to connectivity of the undirected Hasse diagram. We require \(K\ge w\), since \(\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal J(P))=w\) already forces \(\operatorname{Ldim}\ge w\). No restriction to naturally labeled posets, ranked posets, or trees is made in the theorem.

## Proof
**Lemma 1 (ideals of a chain).** The \(\ell+1\) ideals of a chain are its prefixes. Therefore their Littlestone dimension is at most \(\lfloor\log_2(\ell+1)\rfloor\), since a depth-\(d\) shattered tree has at least \(2^d\) distinct hypotheses. Conversely, for \(d=\lfloor\log_2(\ell+1)\rfloor\), choose any \(2^d\) successive distinct prefix hypotheses and recursively query a chain position separating their first and second halves. This yields a shattered complete tree of depth \(d\), proving equality.

**Lemma 2 (disjoint-domain additivity).** If nonempty classes \(H_i\) are defined on pairwise disjoint query domains, their Cartesian-product hypothesis class has Littlestone dimension \(\sum_i\operatorname{Ldim}(H_i)\). Lower bound: run a shattered tree for one factor and append independent shattered trees for the other factors at all leaves. For the upper bound, assign the potential \(\sum_i\operatorname{Ldim}(H_i)\) to any product version space. A query belongs to one domain, and at least one of its two nonempty conditional factor classes has Littlestone dimension at most one below that factor's previous dimension; otherwise the original factor itself shatters one additional round. Induction on the potential shows that the whole product cannot shatter deeper than the sum. If a query has an empty branch, it cannot start a shattered tree. This argument applies equally to conditional version spaces, not just the initial product.

**Lemma 3 (width as static shattering).** If a selected query set contains comparable elements \(a<b\), the label pattern \((h(a),h(b))=(0,1)\) is impossible for every lower ideal, so a shattered set must be an antichain. Conversely, for any antichain \(A\) and any \(S\subseteq A\), the ideal \(\downarrow S\) contains exactly \(S\) among the elements of \(A\). Hence the VC dimension equals the width.

**Lemma 4 (exact chain-partition upper bound).** By Dilworth's theorem, a width-\(w\) poset \(P\) admits a partition into \(w\) nonempty chains of lengths \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_w\), summing to \(|P|\). Every lower ideal intersects each chain in a prefix. Thus \(\mathcal J(P)\) is a *subclass* of the Cartesian product of those \(w\) chain-prefix classes (additional cross-chain comparabilities can only remove tuples). Monotonicity of shattering under subclass inclusion, Lemmas 1 and 2, imply
\[
\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal J(P))\le\sum_{i=1}^{w}\lfloor\log_2(\ell_i+1)\rfloor.
\]
Put \(k_i=\lfloor\log_2(\ell_i+1)\rfloor\). Since \(\ell_i\ge1\), every \(k_i\ge1\) and \(\ell_i\ge 2^{k_i}-1\). To support dimension at least \(K\), we must have \(\sum_i k_i\ge K\). For a fixed sum, the quantity \(\sum_i2^{k_i}\) is minimized by distributing the integers \(k_i\) as evenly as possible: replacing \((a,b)\), when \(b\ge a+2\), by \((a+1,b-1)\) strictly decreases that sum. The minimum is strictly increasing with the prescribed total. Hence
\[
|P|=\sum_i\ell_i\ge\sum_i(2^{k_i}-1)\ge N_w(K).
\]

**Rigidity at equality.** Suppose \(|P|=N_w(K)\) and \(\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal J(P))\ge K\). Equality holds at *every* step of the preceding inequalities. Consequently \(\sum_i k_i=K\), the \(k_i\) are balanced (\(w-r\) equal to \(q\), \(r\) equal to \(q+1\)), and every length is exactly \(\ell_i=2^{k_i}-1\). The product of the corresponding chain-prefix classes has exactly \(\prod_i(\ell_i+1)=2^K\) hypotheses. A depth-\(K\) shattered tree requires at least \(2^K\) **distinct** hypotheses, so every prefix tuple must occur as an ideal of \(P\). If two elements in different partition chains were comparable, there would be a prefix tuple including the larger but omitting the smaller; it would not be an ideal. Therefore no cross-chain comparabilities exist. The stated disjoint union is forced. Conversely, that union has width \(w\), order \(N_w(K)\), and Littlestone dimension \(\sum_i k_i=K\) by Lemmas 1 and 2.

**Connected threshold and fixed-size formula.** For \(w\ge2\), the unique order-minimizing extremizer has at least two disconnected chain components; hence a connected poset needs at least \(N_w(K)+1\) elements. Adjoin a new global minimum to the extremal union of chains. This makes the poset connected, leaves its width \(w\), and preserves a copy of the original concept class on all old-coordinate queries by taking ideals containing the new minimum. Thus its Littlestone dimension is at least \(K\), proving the connected threshold. For the fixed-size maximum, take the disjoint-chain extremizer for the largest \(K\) with \(N_w(K)\le n\), and add \(n-N_w(K)\) new elements, each above every existing element and arranged in a chain. All original ideals remain available with new coordinates zero, the width stays \(w\), and the Ldim cannot decrease. The general upper bound excludes the next \(K\). This proves the formula and completes the finding.

## Verification
The accompanying self-contained `verify_poset_threshold.py` independently enumerates each transitive order relation consistent with a natural labeling on up to six elements. Every finite poset admits a linear extension, so this family includes every poset on at most six elements up to relabeling. It computes lower ideals by closure constraints, width by all antichains, and Littlestone dimension by an exact memoized two-branch recurrence, independent of the proof's chain-decomposition argument. For each order it checks \(\operatorname{VCdim}=w\) indirectly via width, \(w\le\operatorname{Ldim}\le\lfloor\log_2|\mathcal J(P)|\rfloor\), the universal order threshold, and rigidity at equality. It also checks the threshold arithmetic for \(1\le w\le10\) and \(w\le K<30\). The computed numbers of *distinct naturally labeled posets* for orders \(1\) through \(6\) are \(1,2,7,40,357,4824\); these are not asserted to be counts of unlabeled isomorphism classes. The output is supplied in `verification_output.txt`. Run with `python3 verify_poset_threshold.py`. The unrestricted theorem is proved above; finite enumeration is corroboration, not its justification.

## Relationship to prior work
The relevant classical ingredients are Dilworth chain decomposition and the standard threshold interpretation of a chain-prefix concept class. Liu and Weselcouch (arXiv:1807.02865, Section 4.1) represent width-two ideal lattices in a product of two chains, but study \(P\)-partition generating functions rather than adaptive shattering or exact minimum-order extremizers. Ghazi, Golowich, Kumar, and Manurangsi (ALT 2021) study bounds for pointwise Boolean aggregations of hypothesis classes; their closure theorem is on a common query domain and does not determine this disjoint-domain poset threshold or its rigidity. Guingona, Kolesnikov, Nierwinski, and Soucy (arXiv:2109.04805) determine VC and Littlestone dimensions of zero sets of linear combinations; their theorem concerns a different concept family. The exact fixed-width threshold and unique-onset rigidity were not found in the checked sources. This negative comparison is not an assertion of exhaustive publication priority.

## Limitations
The formulas apply to all finite nonempty posets with *membership queries to order ideals*. They do not apply to all distributive-lattice query languages, to membership queries about elements of the ideal lattice, or to online presentation of poset elements. The uniqueness statement holds precisely at the minimum number of elements for a specified \((w,K)\); it does **not** classify all extremizers at larger orders. The connected result gives the minimum size, not all connected extremal isomorphism types. Publication priority remains subject to specialist literature review; independent external validation has not been performed.
