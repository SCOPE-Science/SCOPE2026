# Scientific review: induced-edge shattering theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** A triangle or a three-edge path forces an extra selected edge in a trace, so cannot be shattered. Absence of both patterns characterizes star forests, and choosing centers together with leaves of requested edges realizes every trace. Minimum dominating sets and spanning star forests provide two opposite inequalities proving the VC-dimension identity, including isolated vertices. The labeled component weights are \(1,1,r\), so the exponential formula gives the claimed complete shattering polynomial. For a fixed-size random edge set, the three-edge path is strictly balanced; factorial moments yield Poisson parameter \(4c^3\), and triangles occur with vanishing probability in the critical window. The proof deals separately with zero and diverging critical-scale ratios.

**Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, qualified).** The lead 2020 paper by Adler, Benediktsson, and Macpherson calculates VC dimension/density for single-vertex Johnson/Hamming open-neighborhood ranges, not the rank-two Boolean principal-ideal or vertex-induced-edge range system. The relevant Sections 1–2 and 7 of Kranakis et al. (1994) were inspected: the edge ranges there require connectedness, trees, paths, or stars, whereas the present ranges are all edges induced by a vertex subset, with no connectivity condition. Coudert et al. (2024) concern closed-neighborhood graph VC dimension; only their publicly available abstract was inspected and no full-text noncoverage claim is made. Targeted checks of the published mathematical-record index and accessible earlier claims produced no dominating statement under the checked aliases. A work using monotone quadratic conjunction, Boolean rank-two downset, or star-forest-complex vocabulary could nevertheless be equivalent; independent priority verification remains outstanding.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The exact graph-wide VC dimension becomes a classical invariant, domination number, providing both a structural explanation and a direct complexity classification. For complete graphs, the formula enumerates all shattered subset sizes. The Poisson theorem identifies the first obstruction and its exact critical constant, converting a qualitative structural description into a sharp random-phase law. This is a motivated complete-invariant and threshold result for a natural rank-selected Boolean range system, not a small arbitrary finite case.

**Proof and verification limits.** The Python checker reconstructs induced-edge traces for 33,867 edge sets, compares an independent labeled recurrence, and verifies domination identity for all 1,099 labeled graphs with at most five vertices. Its 3,586 further host-graph cases are supplementary, not exhaustive beyond those sizes. The asymptotic statement follows from the written factorial-moment argument, not numerical simulation.
