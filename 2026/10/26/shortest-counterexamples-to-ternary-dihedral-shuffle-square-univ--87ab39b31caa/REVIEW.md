# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** Every shorter even ternary word splits into two equal-Parikh halves, and dihedral permutations exhaust all permutations of at most three positions. In the proposed eight-letter word, all 24 balanced position partitions yield only the incompatible four-position patterns `0012` and `0102`, distinguished by adjacency of their zeros on a cycle. The finite classification is checked by complete enumeration of all 6561 labeled eight-letter words and all 70 half-position subsets, with a separate structural four-cycle test, and independently identified with the 48-element symmetry orbit. The proof never extrapolates this census to larger lengths.

**Originality: PASS, subject to literature risk.** Conjecture 2 of Grytczuk–Pawlik–Pleszczyński, *Variations on shuffle squares* (arXiv:2308.13882, August 2023), explicitly asserts the opposite of the witness statement; the inspected 2024 revision retains it. Its proven binary cyclic-shuffle theorem cannot imply a ternary dihedral decomposition. Grytczuk–Pawlik–Ruciński (arXiv:2503.22043, 2025) discusses ordinary shuffle squares, not the claimed ternary dihedral family. Searches of available mathematical research records and publication indexes found no directly covering counterexample or complete first-failure count. An equivalent counterexample in unindexed or subsequently published work remains possible.

**Mathematical value: PASS.** The claim decisively resolves a specific conjectured structural property of generalized shuffle squares in the negative and identifies the sharp minimum word length and all minimum obstructions in a single orbit. Its relevance is to the stated conjecture and to the distinction between ordinary, cyclic and dihedral shuffle decompositions, not to an arbitrary isolated tuple.

**Limits.** The enumeration and symmetry classification apply only to the shortest obstructions. No independent audit has been performed.
