# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The two probability models admit one coupled nonfocal-species sampling order, with unconditional richness \(R_T+\mathbf1_{\{T<n\}}\) and singleton-conditioned richness \(1+R_{n-1}\). Monotonicity of discovered species makes the latter at least as large samplewise. This proves the infinite-parameter stochastic order, the nonnegative covariance with singleton count, and the independent-draw strictness at threshold two. Explicit positive-probability counterfamilies at all higher multiplicities establish sharpness. Exact finite verification reproduces 718 cases and 5,140 threshold comparisons without floating point.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, literature risk retained)

The inspected full occupancy study of Barbour–Gnedin treats joint counts and limit covariances rather than conditional stochastic order in two sampling schemes. A published exact finite-uniform covariance study identifies sign changes and explicitly does not assert heterogeneous or conditional stochastic order; its result is implied at singleton frequency by the present statement, not conversely. Empirical singleton-richness literature motivates the statistic without proving the coupling. A preexisting general occupancy monotone coupling is an unresolved possibility and requires independent bibliographic evaluation.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Singleton counts are used as richness and sampling-completeness diagnostics, yet conditional changes in the entire richness law can matter beyond covariance. The result is distribution-wide and applies without equal-abundance assumptions, including exact finite censuses. The proof identifies the structural reason for singleton-only ordering, while positive counterexamples show that all higher multiplicities fail this robustness. It does not model biased detection or correlated sampling.
