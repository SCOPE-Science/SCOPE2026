# Six concurrent lines and exceptional six-point symmetry in the characteristic-five Bring curve

## Finding
Let \(X\) be the coefficientwise special fibre over \(\mathbb F_5\) of the integral Bring model in \(\mathbb P^4_{\mathbb Z}\)
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{5}X_i=\sum_{i=1}^{5}X_i^2=\sum_{i=1}^{5}X_i^3=0.
\]
Over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_5\), it is a **reduced union of exactly six lines**, all defined over \(\mathbb F_5\), concurrent at \(E=[1:1:1:1:1]\), and pairwise disjoint away from \(E\). The six line directions are the six rational points of a smooth projective conic; the ambient coordinate permutation group \(S_5\) acts through the full \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb F_5)\) in its exceptional six-point action.

The local singularity at \(E\) has six branches, delta invariant \(9\), and complete local graded Hilbert function \((1,3,5,6,6,\ldots)\). Its normalization conductor has exponent three along every branch. The normalization of \(X\) is the disjoint union of six rational projective lines. For all integers \(r\geq1\),
\[
\#X(\mathbb F_{5^r})=6\cdot5^r+1,\qquad Z(X/\mathbb F_5,T)=\frac1{(1-T)(1-5T)^6}.
\]
This is a statement about the **specified integral model**; reductions of other birational plane sextic models are not asserted to agree.

## Assumptions and scope
Work over \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_5\), retaining the rational structure over \(\mathbb F_5\). Define the power sums \(p_j=\sum_{i=1}^{5}X_i^j\) for \(j=1,2,3\). The scheme is \(\operatorname{Proj}(k[X_1,\ldots,X_5]/(p_1,p_2,p_3))\). All reductions are coefficientwise from this integral presentation. The delta invariant refers to the local ring of this reduced curve at its unique nonnormal point, not to an ordinary plane sixfold point.

## Proof
**1. Cone reduction.** Set \(t=X_5\), \(Y_i=X_i-X_5\) for \(1\leq i\leq4\), and \(Y_5=0\). As \(5=0\) in \(k\), binomial expansion gives \(p_1(X)=p_1(Y)\), \(p_2(X)=p_2(Y)+2tp_1(Y)\) and \(p_3(X)=p_3(Y)+3tp_2(Y)+3t^2p_1(Y)\). Thus the three ideals generated by the respective power sums coincide. After eliminating \(Y_4=-Y_1-Y_2-Y_3\), the affine cone in three variables is cut out by homogeneous forms \(Q=p_2(Y)\) and \(C=p_3(Y)\) of degrees two and three. Its projectivization is a length-six intersection in \(\mathbb P^2_k\), provided there is no common component, as established next. The coordinate \(t\) is free: \(X\) is its projective cone with vertex \(E\).

**2. All directions, scheme-theoretically.** Newton identities, valid since \(2,3\) are units, show \(p_1=p_2=p_3=0\) if and only if \(e_1=e_2=e_3=0\), with \(e_j\) the elementary symmetric functions in \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_5\). Because \(Y_5=0\), we have
\[
\prod_{i=1}^{5}(T-Y_i)=T^5+e_4(Y)T.
\]
If \(e_4(Y)=0\), all five coordinates are zero; this is excluded projectively. Hence \(e_4(Y)\ne0\) and the derivative of the displayed polynomial is the nonzero constant \(e_4(Y)\); its five roots are distinct. Over \(k\) they form a one-dimensional \(\mathbb F_5\)-vector space, so the unordered coordinate values are \(\lambda\mathbb F_5\) for some nonzero \(\lambda\). Conversely, permutations of \((0,1,2,3,4)\) have their first three power sums zero modulo five. Projectivization removes \(\lambda\), while translation of the five entries fixes the \(Y_i\) and rescales no direction; permuting all five entries yields exactly \(5!/(5\cdot4)=6\) different projective directions. Each is \(\mathbb F_5\)-rational. The plane quadric and cubic have six distinct common geometric points; Bézout's theorem forces intersection length exactly six, so they have no common component and their complete-intersection scheme is reduced. Their homogeneous ideal is saturated and radical (a dimension-one homogeneous complete intersection is Cohen–Macaulay and has no embedded vertex prime). Thus the full three-dimensional affine cone is reduced and consists exactly of the six direction lines. Projectivizing with \(t\) free gives the six asserted projective lines through \(E\).

**3. The invariant conic and symmetry.** The quadratic form \(Q\) has invertible polar matrix on the three-dimensional quotient by the diagonal; in the coordinates \(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3\), direct calculation gives polar determinant \(2\) in \(\mathbb F_5\). Thus \(V(Q)\) is a nonsingular conic with precisely six \(\mathbb F_5\)-points, and they are the directions above. The five-coordinate permutation group fixes \(E\), preserves all power sums, and preserves this conic. Its action on the six points is transitive with direction stabilizer the twenty affine permutations \(z\mapsto az+b\) of \(\mathbb F_5\). The kernel is a normal subgroup of \(S_5\) contained in an order-twenty stabilizer, hence trivial (every nontrivial normal subgroup of \(S_5\) contains \(A_5\), of order sixty). The action therefore gives a subgroup of size 120 of the conic automorphism group \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb F_5)\), also of size 120: it is the whole group. In particular the six branches carry the exceptional degree-six permutation representation of \(S_5\).

**4. Local normalization, delta and conductor.** The completed local vertex ring \(A\) is \(k[[u,v,w]]/(Q,C)\). Each of its six distinct line branches has normalization \(k[[t_i]]\), so the normalization is \(B=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{6}k[[t_i]]\). Since the homogeneous ideal of the six direction points is a complete intersection of types \((2,3)\), its graded affine coordinate ring has Hilbert series
\[
\frac{(1-z^2)(1-z^3)}{(1-z)^3}=\frac{1+2z+2z^2+z^3}{1-z},
\]
with degree dimensions \(1,3,5,6,6,\ldots\). The grading on \(B\) has dimension six in every nonnegative degree. In degrees at least three, evaluation on the six distinct points is an isomorphism onto \(k^6\) (the ring has degree dimension six). Thus \(B/A\) has length \((6-1)+(6-3)+(6-5)=9\): this is \(\delta_E\). The branchwise conductor contains \(\bigoplus_i t_i^3k[[t_i]]\). It contains no \(t_i^2\): a plane conic different from \(Q\) cannot vanish on five distinct points of the smooth conic \(V(Q)\), by Bézout's bound four, so degree-two evaluation cannot isolate one of the six branches. The same conclusion holds at degrees zero and one. Therefore the conductor is exactly \(\bigoplus_i t_i^3k[[t_i]]\).

**5. Arithmetic.** Each of the six lines has \(q+1\) rational points over \(\mathbb F_q\), for \(q=5^r\), and they share only the rational vertex. Hence \(\#X(\mathbb F_q)=6(q+1)-5=6q+1\). Exponentiating \(\sum_{r\ge1}\#X(\mathbb F_{5^r})T^r/r\) gives the stated zeta function. The arithmetic genus is \(4\) from the \((2,3)\) complete-intersection formula in \(\mathbb P^3\), consistent with \(\delta_E-6+1=4\).

## Verification
The standalone Python 3 checker `verify.py` uses integer arithmetic modulo five and no third-party packages. It exhausts the \(31\) projective rational points of \(X\), determines the six base directions and twenty-element stabilizer, verifies that the six-point permutation action has 120 elements, computes the nonsingular conic polar determinant, and obtains the evaluation-rank sequence \((1,3,5,6,6)\). Run `python3 verify.py` from the extracted package. These exact finite calculations corroborate the proof; geometric completeness over \(\overline{\mathbb F}_5\) follows from the root-polynomial argument and Bézout, not from finite-field enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Braden–Northover (2012) gives precisely the symmetric integral equations as its starting classical model (Introduction, first page) and discusses complex period geometry, not this characteristic-five special fibre. Korchmáros–Lia–Timpanella (2021, later revisions) investigates the power-sum family explicitly under \(p\ge7\) and \(m\le p-1\), excluding the case \(p=m=5\) treated here. Braden–Disney-Hogg (2023/2024) further studies the characteristic-zero complex curve and its modular quotients. None of these inspected sources states this six-line degeneration, its exceptional six-point action, or the local conductor. A lack of located coverage is not proof of absolute historical novelty: earlier treatments of the Bring–Jerrard root configuration in characteristic five may imply part of it.

## Limitations
The finding applies to the displayed integral power-sum model, not arbitrary equivalent characteristic-zero presentations. It does not compute a semistable model, monodromy, or the full deformation theory. The claim is algebraically reconstructed and computationally checked but has not undergone independent scientific audit. The originally published work of Edge (1978) was available only through references, not read in full; historical overlap remains possible.
