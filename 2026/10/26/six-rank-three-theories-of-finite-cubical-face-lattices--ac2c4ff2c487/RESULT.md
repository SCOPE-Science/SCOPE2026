# Six rank-three theories of finite cubical face lattices

## Finding
For each integer \(n\geq0\), let \(\mathcal F_n\) be the lattice of all faces of the \(n\)-dimensional cube, including the empty face. Use the **pure order language** containing just binary face inclusion \(\leq\) and equality; no constants, operations, rank predicates, or coordinate labels are named. Write \(\equiv_3\) for agreement on every first-order sentence of quantifier rank at most three. Then
\[
\mathcal F_m\equiv_3\mathcal F_n\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\min(m,5)=\min(n,5),\qquad m,n\geq0.
\]
Hence exactly six rank-three theories occur. Every theory has a representative of size at most \(3^5+1=244\), and this bound is optimal **among the cubical face lattices**. The stabilized theory is already represented by the five-cube, although the higher-dimensional lattices have unbounded sizes and ranks.

## Assumptions and scope
A nonempty face is encoded by a word in \(\{0,1,*\}^n\): a numeric symbol fixes a cube coordinate, while \(*\) leaves it free. An additional point \(\bot\) denotes the empty face. On nonempty faces, \(x\leq y\) means that, in each coordinate, either the two symbols coincide or \(x\) has a fixed symbol and \(y\) has \(*\); and \(\bot\leq x\) for every \(x\). In dimension zero there are two faces, \(\bot\) and the unique point. Only finite dimensions and the indicated language are covered. Equivalence does not assert isomorphism or elementary equivalence at unbounded quantifier rank.

## Proof
**1. Exact rank-three type recurrence.** For two selected faces \(x,y\), define \(j(x,y)\in\{0,1,2,3\}\) using bit zero for \(x\leq y\) and bit one for \(y\leq x\). For each third face \(z\), define the four-bit mask \(b(z;x,y)\) with bits zero through three equal to the truth values of \(z\leq x\), \(x\leq z\), \(z\leq y\), and \(y\leq z\), respectively. Put
\[
 s(x,y)=\sum_{b\in\{b(z;x,y):z\in\mathcal F_n\}}2^b,
 \qquad t_1(x,y)=(j(x,y),s(x,y)).
\]
These signatures are precisely the complete one-move extensions of a two-point quantifier-free type. The complete two-move type of a selected face \(x\) is consequently the set \(t_2(x)=\{t_1(x,y):y\in\mathcal F_n\}\), and the complete rank-three sentence type is the set \(t_3(\mathcal F_n)=\{t_2(x):x\in\mathcal F_n\}\). The equivalences follow inductively because Boolean combinations are determined by constituent types and each existential quantifier records exactly which successor types occur. All equality information is included: in a partial order, \(x=y\) if and only if \(x\leq y\) and \(y\leq x\).

**2. Exhaustive, symmetry-reduced exact evaluation.** Cube-coordinate permutations and bit-flips act as order automorphisms. Thus every nonempty selected \(x\) is represented, up to automorphism, by \(f\) fixed zero coordinates and \(u=n-f\) free coordinates. Given a second nonempty face \(y\), its coordinate-pair orbits are exhausted by \((0,0),(0,1),(0,*),(*,0),(*,*)\), with multiplicities \(a,b,c,h,g\) satisfying \(a+b+c=f\) and \(h+g=u\). For each such coordinate pair, try all three symbols of \(z\) and collect the resulting four-bit local relation masks; the global relation mask is the bitwise AND of one allowed local mask in every coordinate. A finite dynamic program computes the **exact** set of global masks, and adds the empty third face separately. The case where \(x\) or \(y\) is \(\bot\) is computed explicitly. This enumerates every possible pair orbit and every third-face extension, not a sample.

The attached deterministic standard-library programs `logical_types.py` and `verify.py` compute this recurrence for every \(0\leq n\leq27\). Its six distinct theory signatures occur for dimensions \(0,1,2,3,4,5\), and the signature at dimension five agrees *exactly* with every signature from dimension six through twenty-seven. An independent implementation directly enumerates all faces and all ordered pairs, and agrees with the orbit reduction for all \(0\leq n\leq5\). These are exhaustive finite checks; their reproducibility does not by itself prove stability for unbounded dimension.

**3. Uniform large-dimension lemma.** For every \(m,n\geq27\), Duplicator wins the three-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game on \(\mathcal F_m,\mathcal F_n\). Empty faces are paired with empty faces, and ordinary faces with ordinary faces. After \(r\) chosen pairs of ordinary faces, partition coordinates in each cube by their \(r\)-tuples of selected symbols, so there are at most \(3^r\) cells. Maintain that corresponding cell sizes are either equal and smaller than \(3^{3-r}\), or both at least \(3^{3-r}\). Initially there is one cell of sizes \(m,n\geq27\).

For an ordinary new face, each current cell splits into at most three new symbol cells. Let \(t=3^{2-r}\) be the next-round threshold. If a parent size is less than \(3t\), it is exactly matched by the other parent; copy the three subcell sizes. If both parent sizes are at least \(3t\), copy each Spoiler-created subcell of size less than \(t\), and assign at least \(t\) to each remaining non-small subcell. This is feasible: there are at most three subcells, the total contribution from small ones is at most \(2(t-1)\) if any large subcell exists, and the available parent size is at least \(3t\). For a parent whose three subcells all are small, the total is at most \(3(t-1)<3t\), impossible; therefore at least one non-small subcell exists. All respective new cells meet the updated invariant. For selection of the empty face, simply select the empty face opposite; no coordinate refinement is needed, and decrease the threshold with the round count. After three rounds, corresponding cells are simultaneously empty or nonempty. Since all order relations among selected ordinary faces are determined coordinatewise by which tuple cells are nonempty, all binary order and equality facts agree; the empty face has been preserved. This proves the lemma for all sufficiently large dimensions, without computation.

**4. Combining the bounds and sharpness.** The exact finite recurrence connects dimension five with dimension twenty-seven and distinguishes dimensions zero through four from each other and from dimension five. The uniform lemma connects every dimension at least twenty-seven. Together they give the claimed iff statement for all nonnegative dimensions. The face lattice of dimension \(n\) has exactly \(3^n+1\) elements. Since its rank-three class is unique for each \(0\leq n\leq4\), while the stable class first appears at \(n=5\), the largest least representative has \(3^5+1=244\) elements.

An explicit rank-three witness for the stable class uses the signatures \((1,17653)\) and \((2,12067)\). For a pair signature \((j,s)\), let \(\vartheta_{j,s}(x,y)\) assert the two indicated order bits, and for **every** mask \(b\in\{0,\ldots,15\}\), assert or negate \(\exists z\,\chi_b(z,x,y)\) according to whether the \(b\)-th bit of \(s\) is present. Here \(\chi_b\) is the conjunction of the four positive or negative atomic order literals specified above. Then
\[
\exists x\bigl[(\exists y\,\vartheta_{1,17653}(x,y))\land(\exists y\,\vartheta_{2,12067}(x,y))\bigr]
\]
has quantifier rank three, and holds exactly in \(\mathcal F_n\) for \(n\geq5\). In dimension five a witness has three fixed and two free coordinates. The exhaustive orbit computation verifies the stated truth values through dimension twenty-seven; the uniform lemma extends them beyond. Distinctness of the other five classes follows from their different exact Hintikka type sets in the finite certificate.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` from the directory containing both accompanying `.py` files, using Python 3 and only the standard library. `output.txt` records the actual execution. The independent direct oracle evaluates \(3^n+1\) concrete faces and all ordered pairs for \(n\leq5\), while the symmetry-reduced recurrence scans every integer dimension through twenty-seven. It confirms six theory sets, the threshold-five explicit signature witness, and the bound \(244\). `logical_types.py` separately implements the exact local-mask dynamic program. The uniform large-dimension lemma is a mathematical proof above, **not a computational result**.

## Relationship to prior work
Metropolis and Rota, *On the lattice of faces of the n-cube* (1978), study exactly these objects through face-lattice structure, axioms, and symmetric-chain questions, but their described results do not classify rank-three first-order theories. Bailey and Oliveira (2009) study automorphism actions and cube derangements via Möbius inversion, not logical quantifier-rank equivalence; Szajewska (2012) studies high-dimensional regular-polytope face enumerations. Generic Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé techniques are established background rather than new claims. Known bounded-rank classifications of Boolean inclusion posets or vector-subspace inclusion lattices concern different structures and do not imply this six-class cubical-face result by renaming or reduct. Research-index comparisons did not reveal the identical classification, but absence from search results is not proof of priority.

## Limitations
The proof is restricted to first-order quantifier rank at most three in the pure order language. The finite portion depends on the exact, independently cross-checked program enumerations; the uniform tail uses the explicit combinatorial EF proof. Optimality of the size bound is only **within** the natural family of cubical face lattices. Full text of Leont'ev's 2008 geometric work on \(n\)-cube faces, and some older sources, was unavailable for definitive overlap assessment; unidentified prior logical classifications remain an originality risk. No independent external scientific audit has been conducted.
