# Scientific review: finite cubical face lattices at rank three

**Correctness — PASS.** The face-inclusion relation has an exact three-valued coordinate description, and the quantified 3-types have a complete extension-mask recurrence. Two different implementations agree on all concrete cubical face lattices through dimension five; an exhaustive orbit-census covers every dimension through 27. A separate explicit three-round coordinate-color Duplicator strategy proves stability for all dimensions at least 27. The finite and infinite arguments together establish the asserted iff statement, and the number of elements is exactly \(3^n+1\). Risk: the finite certificate and its extraction should be replayed by an independent auditor; the proof does not establish any higher-rank statement.

**Originality — PASS, subject to residual literature risks.** Metropolis–Rota (1978) analyzes the identical face-lattice objects but focuses on axiomatization/chain decompositions, not low-quantifier-rank theory. Bailey–Oliveira (arXiv:0902.0937) studies automorphism and derangement enumeration; Szajewska (arXiv:1204.1875) studies face counts. None of the inspected results has a statement that implies the six-theory rank-three classification. Earlier Boolean subset and vector-subspace inclusion logical classifications concern different families, neither of which entails the cubical lattice result by mere syntactic substitution. The full 2008 Leont'ev text was not obtained, so a possible unindexed or inaccessible prior logical classification remains a specifically acknowledged risk.

**Value — PASS.** Face lattices of hypercubes are canonical finite partially ordered structures, whereas pure-order bounded-rank logic can hide their growing dimensions. A complete natural dimension-stabilization classification, explicit distinguishing rank-three formula, and sharp least representative bound provide a reusable finite testing principle; this is not simply a routine count of faces. The 27-dimensional tail cutoff is a proof device, not the asserted sharp stabilization threshold; the sharp threshold is five.
