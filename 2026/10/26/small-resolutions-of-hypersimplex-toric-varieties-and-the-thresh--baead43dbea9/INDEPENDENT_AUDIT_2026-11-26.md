# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The normal cone at a k-subset B has the primitive signed coordinate rays in the stated quotient lattice. Its unique relation has coefficient +1 on B and −1 on the complement; any n−1 signed coordinate rays (one per index) form an integral basis. Both signs occur at least twice, so all n rays are extremal and every proper face of the cone is smooth, while the maximal cone is not simplicial. Its dual semigroup is generated by e_j−e_i (j in B and i outside B): distribute the nonnegative row totals among the equal total of the column demands. This is the rank-one matrix semigroup. Smallness forbids new rays, so local smooth subdivisions are circuit triangulations. In nonnegative coefficient coordinates, varying a representation along the one signed relation gives an interval whose two endpoints eliminate a ray on opposite sides. Omitting each ray on a specified side gives a triangulation; mixing single omissions from opposite sides gives full-dimensional overlaps, and covering requires every omission on the chosen side. Both fans have unimodular full cones, fix the boundaries and glue independently. The two matrix incidence resolutions exhibit the fibers P^(k−1) and P^(n−k−1), both of codimension at least two in the source. The count is up to equivariant isomorphism over the fixed target: the induced torus map of a birational refinement identifies its lattice with that of the target. For heights h on the rays of one cone, let L_j interpolate the heights on the basis omitting ray j, and c_j be its signed circuit coefficient. Then h_j−L_j(v_j)=c_j δ_B with δ_B=sum c_i h_i. This corrects the proof's misleading phrase 'unique interior wall': several walls can occur, but the same circuit controls them all. Under the convex support-function convention, requiring strict inequality at each omitted ray gives exactly one strict sign of δ_B per triangulation. Heights w_i=a_i+b_i and t=sum b_i parametrize every sign test simultaneously; a realizable strict system admits rational heights and hence integral heights after scaling. Adding a large pullback of a globally strictly convex parent function preserves the strictly convex local bends and makes all other wall bends strict. Conversely a projective toric refinement admits a strictly convex support function, so necessarily realizes the signs. The canonical support vector must be +1 on the B coordinates and −1 on the others in the sum-zero dual lattice; even rational solvability is equivalent to n=2k. In that case a small morphism is crepant by comparison in codimension one. Independently generating all labeled threshold graphs by successive isolated or universal vertex additions (python3 /workspace/review_checks.py 5) gives 46 and 332. For a strict pair-sum graph the sum of minimum and maximum weights identifies such an isolated or universal vertex; conversely choose a sufficiently extreme weight for each addition. These counts check the k=2 examples, not the all-rank fan proof.

## originality

PASS

The supported distinct claim is the all-rank fixed-target count and precise threshold-arrangement characterization of its projective subfamily. The normal fan, determinantal local models, standard circuit triangulation theory, and numerical k=2 chamber counts are not credited as new. Neither a covering prior classification nor a stronger inspected statement implying it was established.

## value

PASS

For a natural labeled family of projective toric targets, the result classifies smooth small models, including nonprojective ones, and turns projectivity into an exact, checkable strict-sign problem. The threshold-graph case gives a useful combinatorial interpretation and concrete instances where dozens or hundreds of smooth models are not projective. This is more than recomputing published arrangement counts or recognizing the local determinantal singularity.
