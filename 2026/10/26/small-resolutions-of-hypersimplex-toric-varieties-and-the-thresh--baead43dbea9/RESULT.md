# Small resolutions of hypersimplex toric varieties and the threshold-arrangement projectivity criterion

## Finding

Let \(K\) be an algebraically closed field and let integers \(n\ge4\) and \(2\le k\le n-2\) be fixed. Consider the hypersimplex
\[
\Delta(k,n)=\{(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in[0,1]^n:\ \sum_{i=1}^n x_i=k\}
\]
with its saturated affine lattice, and let \(X_{k,n}\) be the projective toric variety associated with its normal fan. The following classification is over the **fixed target** \(X_{k,n}\), and counts torus-equivariant smooth proper birational morphisms with no exceptional divisor, not abstract isomorphism types.

**Theorem.** Precisely \(\binom nk\) torus-fixed points of \(X_{k,n}\) are singular. At each, there are exactly two smooth small toric resolutions, and their choices glue independently. Therefore the exact number of smooth toric small resolutions over \(X_{k,n}\) is
\[
\boxed{2^{\binom nk}}.
\]
Each exceptional fiber is either \(\mathbb P^{k-1}\) or \(\mathbb P^{n-k-1}\). The target is Gorenstein exactly for \(n=2k\), in which case all these small resolutions are crepant.

**Projectivity theorem.** Let \(\mathcal A_{k,n}\) be the real central hyperplane arrangement
\[
\sum_{i\in B}w_i=t\qquad (B\subseteq[n],\ |B|=k)
\]
in \(\mathbb R^{n+1}\). The toric small resolutions that are projective are in canonical bijection with chambers of \(\mathcal A_{k,n}\): each chamber prescribes which of the two local resolutions to select at every singular fixed point. A choice is projective **if and only if** its sign prescription has the strict linear realization above.

When \(k=2\), substituting \(u_i=w_i-t/2\) identifies this with the classical threshold arrangement \(u_i+u_j=0\), so projective resolutions correspond to labeled threshold graphs. In particular, the numbers of projective and all smooth small resolutions, respectively, are \(46\) versus \(64\) at \((n,k)=(4,2)\), and \(332\) versus \(1024\) at \((n,k)=(5,2)\).

## Assumptions and scope

The ground field may have arbitrary characteristic. The cocharacter lattice of the normal fan is \(N=\mathbb Z^n/\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1)\). Its dual is \(M=\{(m_i)\in\mathbb Z^n:\sum m_i=0\}\). The notion of projectivity concerns the resolution as a variety (equivalently, projectivity of its proper morphism to the already projective target); it does not require all small resolutions to be projective. No result about nontoric birational maps, uniqueness modulo automorphisms, or enumeration of nonprojective isomorphism classes is asserted.

## Proof

**1. Cones and singular support.** Vertices of \(\Delta(k,n)\) are indexed by \(k\)-subsets \(B\subseteq[n]\). The maximal normal cone \(\sigma_B\) is generated by the \(n\) rays
\[
r_j=\overline e_j\quad(j\in B),\qquad s_i=-\overline e_i\quad(i\notin B),
\]
where the bar is the image in \(N\). They satisfy the unique primitive signed circuit
\[
\sum_{j\in B}r_j-\sum_{i\notin B}s_i=0.
\]
Every proper face of \(\sigma_B\) is generated by a proper subset of these \(n\) rays, hence by part of an integral basis of \(N\). In particular all non-maximal cones are smooth. The \(\binom nk\) maximal cones are nonsimplicial, and their torus fixed points are singular. Equivalently, each affine chart is the rank-at-most-one determinantal cone of \(k\)-by-\((n-k)\) matrices; this local description and the singular-point count are established structural context, not claimed as novel by themselves.

**2. The two small resolutions at each vertex.** A pointed full-dimensional cone whose generators form one circuit, with at least two generators on each side, has exactly two triangulations using only its original rays. For \(\sigma_B\) they are the fans whose full-dimensional cones omit, respectively, one ray from \(\{r_j:j\in B\}\) or one ray from \(\{s_i:i\notin B\}\). Each full-dimensional cone has \(n-1\) generators, one signed coordinate ray omitted, and its primitive generators form an integral basis of \(N\). Both subdivisions are therefore nonsingular. Each proper face of the original maximal cone is already simplicial, so the two local refinements restrict identically to every overlap with neighboring maximal cones. Thus all \(\binom nk\) binary choices glue independently to smooth complete fans, and every smooth toric small resolution arises in this way: a new ray would define an exceptional toric divisor and violate smallness. These facts yield the claimed \(2^{\binom nk}\) count. The standard two incidence resolutions of an affine rank-one matrix cone have exceptional fibers \(\mathbb P^{k-1}\) or \(\mathbb P^{n-k-1}\); both have codimension at least two in the smooth source because \(k,n-k\ge2\).

**3. Projective choices are precisely coherent circuit choices.** Assign arbitrary real heights \(a_i\) to the ray \(+\overline e_i\) and \(b_i\) to the ray \(-\overline e_i\). A support function on a smooth refinement is linear on every refined cone and determined by these \(2n\) heights. The signed bending quantity across the unique interior wall refining \(\sigma_B\) is
\[
\delta_B=\sum_{j\in B}a_j-\sum_{i\notin B}b_i
=\sum_{j\in B}(a_j+b_j)-\sum_{i=1}^n b_i.
\]
The two circuit triangulations require opposite strict signs of \(\delta_B\) for relative strict convexity. Put \(w_i=a_i+b_i\) and \(t=\sum_i b_i\). Every \((w_1,\ldots,w_n,t)\) is obtained from some ray heights: choose \(b_i\) summing to \(t\), then set \(a_i=w_i-b_i\). Hence a prescribed family of triangulations supports a strictly convex function over each original maximal cone if and only if its corresponding signs occur in a chamber of \(\mathcal A_{k,n}\). The parent fan is projective, so adding a sufficiently large multiple of the pullback of a strictly convex parent support function makes any such relatively ample function globally ample without changing its local circuit signs. Conversely an ample divisor on a projective refined toric variety supplies all the required strict local signs. This proves the if-and-only-if statement.

**4. The balanced case.** A rational Gorenstein support vector evaluating to \(1\) on all rays of \(\sigma_B\) would have coordinates \(m_j=1\) for \(j\in B\) and \(m_i=-1\) for \(i\notin B\). The defining lattice equation \(\sum m_i=0\) holds exactly if \(n=2k\). Thus \(X_{k,n}\) is (\(\mathbb Q\)-)Gorenstein precisely then. Since a small resolution is an isomorphism in codimension one, every such smooth small resolution is crepant in that balanced case.

**5. Exact small cases.** For \(k=2\) the change \(u_i=w_i-t/2\) turns all bending hyperplanes into \(u_i+u_j=0\). This is the classical threshold-graph arrangement; its numbers of chambers \(46\) at \(n=4\) and \(332\) at \(n=5\) are already known in arrangement theory. The standalone exact-arithmetic checker independently reproduces both numbers from Whitney's subset formula and Zaslavsky's chamber formula, and verifies the unimodularity determinants of the two local triangulations in four sample families. These numerical enumerations support, but do not replace, the all-parameter argument.

Run `python3 verify.py` using Python 3 standard library. It checks the primitive signed ray circuits and all maximal refined-cone determinants for \((n,k)=(4,2),(5,2),(6,2),(6,3)\). For the first two families it also evaluates the exact rank inclusion-exclusion expression \(\sum_{S\subseteq\mathcal A}(-1)^{|S|+\operatorname{rank}(S)}\), giving chamber counts \(46\) and \(332\). The general theorems follow from the fan and support-function proofs, not from finite tests.

## Relationship to prior work

Feichtner--Sturmfels introduce the polyhedral and toric framework of matroid polytopes. Wang--Yu study the normal fan of the hypersimplex, its generic Grassmannian torus orbit closure and cohomological invariants, but do not give a classification of toric smooth small resolutions or their projectivity patterns. The isolated rank-one determinantal local singularities and the Gorenstein criterion are treated as known structural inputs, not as new claims. Deshpande--Menon--Singh study the threshold arrangement and already give the values \(46\) and \(332\); these integers are not claimed as newly discovered. The contribution here is the global resolution classification and the geometrically motivated identification of projective choices with threshold-arrangement chambers in every permitted dimension and rank.

## Limitations

The count is over the fixed labeled target and among **torus-equivariant smooth small resolutions**. Non-toric resolutions, abstract isomorphism classes, and birational transformations between resolutions are not classified. Except in the balanced case \(n=2k\), the target is not \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein, so no crepancy claim is made. The proof is combinatorial and works in arbitrary characteristic. General toric circuit theory makes some constituent steps standard, so a possible undiscovered formulation in secondary-fan or GIT literature remains an originality risk; independent validation has not occurred.
