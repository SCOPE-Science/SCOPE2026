# Smooth macroscopic inversion fluctuations of random butterfly pivot permutations

## Finding

Let \(N=2^n\), with \(n\geq1\), and let \(\pi_n\) be uniform in the standard binary-tree automorphism subgroup \(B_n\subset S_N\). Equivalently, by the cited butterfly-permutation uniformity theorem, \(\pi_n\) is the pivot permutation from Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting on a **general (nonsimple) scalar butterfly matrix** whose recursion angles are independent uniform angles. Define its inversion count by
\[
 I_n=\#\{(a,b):1\leq a<b\leq N,\ \pi_n(a)>\pi_n(b)\}.
\]
Then, with independent Bernoulli\((1/2)\) random variables \(B_{j,k}\),
\[
 I_n\overset{d}{=}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}4^{n-j-1}\sum_{k=1}^{2^j}B_{j,k},\qquad
 \mathbb E z^{I_n}=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1+z^{4^{n-j-1}}}{2}\right)^{2^j}.
\]
In particular,
\[
 \mathbb E I_n=\frac{N(N-1)}4,\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(I_n)=\frac{N^4}{56}\left(1-8^{-n}\right).
\]
Under an explicit common coupling, the normalized counts \(X_n=I_n/N^2\) converge almost surely and in every \(L^p\), \(1\leq p<\infty\), to
\[
 X=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}4^{-j-1}\sum_{k=1}^{2^j}B_{j,k},
 \qquad 0\leq X-X_n\leq2^{-n-1}\ \text{almost surely}.
\]
The limit has expectation \(1/4\), variance \(1/56\), symmetry \(X\overset d=1/2-X\), and **full topological support** \([0,1/2]\). Although the finite laws are discrete, the limit has a compactly supported \(C^\infty\) probability density. More precisely, writing \(\phi(t)=\mathbb E e^{itX}\),
\[
 \phi(t)=e^{it/4}\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}
 \left[\cos\left(\frac{t}{2\cdot4^{j+1}}\right)\right]^{2^j},
 \qquad |\phi(t)|\leq e^{-c\sqrt{|t|}}\quad(|t|\geq4),
\]
where \(c=-\frac14\log(\cos(1/2))>0\). Thus the fraction of discordant pairs, \(I_n/\binom N2\), converges to the nondegenerate smooth law of \(2X\), with variance \(1/14\), rather than concentrating at \(1/2\) as for a uniform permutation of \(S_N\).

## Assumptions and scope

Use the usual ordering of leaves \(1,\ldots,2^n\) of the complete ordered binary tree of depth \(n\). The subgroup \(B_n\) is generated by independently swapping the two children at each internal vertex and acts on the ordered leaves. Its cardinality is \(2^{2^n-1}\). Uniformity in \(B_n\) is equivalent to fair independent choices of these internal swaps. The matrix interpretation concerns the **general** scalar butterfly ensemble with iid continuous uniform recursion angles, not the smaller simple/Kronecker or diagonal butterfly ensembles. The uniformity theorem for this ensemble is due to Peca-Medlin and Zhong (2024), and is reiterated by Gunawan, Peca-Medlin and Zhong (2026); it is a cited premise, not a new conclusion.

The finite-distribution statements hold for each integer \(n\geq1\). The density and support statements concern the single infinite random variable \(X\) constructed from an infinite independent family. No claim is made about normalized inversion processes indexed by continuous time or nonuniform tree-swap laws.

## Proof

**1. Independent inversion increments.** Each internal vertex at level \(j\), where the root has level zero, controls two subtrees of \(2^{n-j-1}\) leaves each. A swap at this vertex exchanges the two contiguous sets of output values. If it is not swapped, all pairs with one leaf in each child block are noninversions; if swapped, all \(4^{n-j-1}\) such pairs are inversions. A pair of leaves has a unique lowest common ancestor, so no pair is counted at any other vertex. Inversions entirely within child blocks obey the identical rule recursively. The unique wreath-product coordinates of a uniform group element are independent fair swaps, proving the distributional identity and generating polynomial.

**2. Exact moments and a simultaneous limit coupling.** Independent Bernoulli means and variances yield
\[
 \mathbb E I_n=\frac12\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}2^j4^{n-j-1}
 =\frac{4^n-2^n}{4},
\]
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(I_n)=\frac14\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}2^j16^{n-j-1}
 =\frac{16^n}{56}(1-8^{-n}).
\]
On one probability space draw all the independent \(B_{j,k}\) and use the stated finite partial sums to realize \(X_n\). Their nonnegative remainder is bounded by
\[
 \sum_{j=n}^{\infty}2^j4^{-j-1}=2^{-n-1}.
\]
This deterministic error implies almost-sure and every finite \(L^p\) convergence. Summing the limiting mean and variance gives \(\mathbb EX=1/4\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(X)=1/56\). Replacing every bit by its complement maps \(X\) into \(\sum_{j\geq0}2^j4^{-j-1}-X=1/2-X\), proving symmetry.

**3. Full interval support.** List the individual weights \(4^{-j-1}\), each repeated \(2^j\) times, in nonincreasing order. Their sum equals \(1/2\), and every weight does not exceed the sum of all later weights. Indeed even the last weight at level \(j\) is at most the sum of the entire deeper-level tail, \(2^{-j-2}\). The elementary greedy subsum lemma therefore represents every real \(x\in[0,1/2]\) as a sum of a subset of these weights. More explicitly, write the weights in order as \(a_1,a_2,\ldots\), with tail \(T_k=\sum_{i>k}a_i\). Starting at residual \(r_0=x\), select weight \(a_k\) if \(r_{k-1}\geq a_k\) and subtract it; otherwise leave the residual unchanged. Inductively \(0\leq r_k\leq T_k\): if a weight is selected this follows by subtraction, while if skipped it follows from \(r_k<a_k\leq T_k\). As \(T_k\to0\), the residual vanishes. For every such representing sequence, each finite prefix has positive Bernoulli probability. Since the remaining sum after a sufficiently long prefix can be made arbitrarily small, every neighborhood of \(x\) has positive probability. Values outside \([0,1/2]\) are impossible by the total-weight bound, establishing exact support.

**4. Smoothness, with an explicit Fourier bound.** For a fair bit \(B\) of weight \(a\), \(\mathbb E e^{itaB}=e^{ita/2}\cos(ta/2)\). Independence gives the stated characteristic-function product, which converges because the sum of all weights is finite. Fix \(|t|\geq4\) and put \(m=\lfloor\log_4|t|\rfloor\geq1\). Retain just the modulus of the factor at level \(j=m-1\); all other factors have modulus at most one. Its cosine argument has absolute value in \([1/2,2)\). Throughout this interval, \(|\cos u|\leq q:=\cos(1/2)<1\). Hence
\[
 |\phi(t)|\leq q^{2^{m-1}}
 \leq\exp\left(-\frac{-\log q}{4}\sqrt{|t|}\right),
\]
using \(2^{m-1}>\sqrt{|t|}/4\). Consequently \(t\mapsto |t|^r\phi(t)\) is integrable for every nonnegative integer \(r\). Fourier inversion and differentiation under the integral produce a bounded continuous density with bounded continuous derivatives of every order. This proves the \(C^\infty\) assertion. It is supported on \([0,1/2]\) by step 3.

Finally, \(I_n/\binom{2^n}{2}=(2N/(N-1))X_n\to2X\) almost surely in the coupling. Its limiting variance is \(4/56=1/14\). For comparison, the classical uniform-permutation inversion variance is \(N(N-1)(2N+5)/72=O(N^3)\), so the corresponding normalized fraction concentrates at \(1/2\).

## Verification

The standalone `checker.py` independently enumerates all \(2^{2^n-1}\) binary-tree automorphisms for \(n=1,2,3,4\), computes inversion histograms by directly comparing positions, and checks exact agreement with polynomial-product coefficients, inversion symmetry, and both moment formulas. At \(n=4\) it checks all \(32,768\) permutations on \(16\) leaves. It also tests \(80\) rational-truncation identities and selected numerical consequences of the explicit Fourier bound. The observed output is preserved in `checker_output.txt`: **359 passed assertions**. These finite checks are corroboration, not a proof of the infinite-series smoothness or support conclusions; those are established in the analytic steps above.

## Relationship to prior work

Peca-Medlin and Zhong's 2024 paper provides the recursive tree-automorphism group and uniform induced butterfly law, including its Theorem 5, and studies longest increasing subsequences and cycles. Gunawan, Peca-Medlin and Zhong's 2026 Theorem 5 reiterates the same group law and relates it to Gaussian elimination; both are **assumed prior results**. Neither inspected account states the inversion-count product, its nonconcentrating macroscopic scaling, or the smooth limit. Their recursively ordered group is distinct from a uniform element of the whole symmetric group.

Cai, Holmgren, Janson, Johansson and Skerman study *tree inversions* arising from uniformly randomly assigning numerical labels to **vertices** and comparing ancestor-descendant pairs. Their independent per-vertex summands are uniform on integer intervals, unlike the fair independent internal swaps of an ordered tree's **leaves** here. Mendes and Remmel's work concerns inversion-type statistics on standard reflection or colored wreath-product permutation groups; its accessible abstract does not provide the present iterated-binary-tree leaf-action limit. The inversion count here is the ordinary one-line inversion statistic, not a colored descent or major index. These comparisons do not establish exhaustive historical uniqueness.

## Limitations

The limit is proved for fair independent binary-tree swaps and therefore for the specific general uniform-angle scalar butterfly ensemble. It does not extend automatically to general random matrices, arbitrary 2-Sylow embeddings in a fixed coordinate ordering, nonuniform swaps, simple butterfly matrices, or other inversion conventions. The smooth density is characterized by its convergent Fourier product, not by a closed elementary expression. The proof establishes full topological support, **not strict pointwise positivity** of its density. A more general earlier theorem for inversion statistics of iterated wreath-product actions could subsume the result; historical priority remains qualified.
