# Sparse fixed-weight paths: a hierarchy of Poisson first-order spectra

## Finding
Fix an integer \(r\geq 3\) and a real \(c>0\). Let \(k_n\) be integers with \(0\leq k_n\leq n\) and
\[
\frac{k_n}{n^{1-1/r}}\longrightarrow c.
\]
Choose \(U_n\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\) uniformly among the \(k_n\)-element subsets, and consider the first-order structure \(\mathcal P_n=(\{1,\ldots,n\},S,U_n)\), where \(S(x,y)\) holds exactly when \(y=x+1\). The vocabulary has equality, directed immediate successor, and the unary predicate; it does **not** contain a global linear-order relation.

For every finite increasing integer pattern \(a=(0=a_1<a_2<\cdots<a_r)\), write
\[
X_{a,n}=\#\{i:1\leq i\leq n-a_r,\ \{i+a_1,\ldots,i+a_r\}\subseteq U_n\}.
\]
Then, for every finite collection of **distinct** such patterns, the joint vector of their counts tends in distribution to independent Poisson variables, each with mean \(\lambda=c^r\). Moreover, every first-order sentence \(\varphi\) has a limiting probability computable as a finite symbolic expression in \(c\). The full set of such limiting probabilities is precisely
\[
\mathcal S_{r,c}=\bigl\{\mathbb P(E): E\text{ is in the finite Boolean algebra generated by events }\{Z_a=j\},\ a\in\mathcal A_r,\ j\in\mathbb N_0\bigr\},
\]
where \(\mathcal A_r\) is the countable family of normalized increasing \(r\)-point integer patterns and the \(Z_a\) are independent \(\operatorname{Poisson}(c^r)\) variables. Here "finite Boolean algebra generated" means that each event uses only finitely many generators; this is a countable algebra. In particular, \(\mathcal S_{r,c}\) is **countable and dense** in \([0,1]\).

For comparison, if \(k_n\sim c n^\alpha\) with \(0<\alpha<1\) and \(\alpha\) is not \(1-1/s\) for any integer \(s\geq2\), then every first-order sentence in this same successor-only vocabulary has limiting probability \(0\) or \(1\). Thus there are nontrivial Poisson phases at every higher collision power \(1-1/r\) for \(r\geq3\), while all noncritical powers obey a zero-one law; the first collision power \(1/2\) is an established antecedent, not claimed as new here.

## Assumptions and scope
All limits are as \(n\to\infty\); \(r,c\), every tested sentence, and every pattern mentioned in a joint limit are fixed independently of \(n\). Each of the \(\binom n{k_n}\) marked words has equal probability. Pattern occurrences only require marked positions, not zeros at the other sites. Distinct normalized patterns are distinct subsets up to translation. Empty boundary segments are interpreted naturally. No claims are made for arbitrary drifting, oscillating, or supercritical marking sequences, nor for the vocabulary containing a global order relation.

## Proof
**1. Exact sampling formula and Poisson patterns.** For any set \(T\) of \(v\) distinct sites,
\[
\mathbb P(T\subseteq U_n)=\frac{(k_n)_v}{(n)_v},
\]
where \((t)_v=t(t-1)\cdots(t-v+1)\) and the probability is zero if \(v>k_n\). For fixed \(v\), this equals \((c+o(1))^v n^{-v/r}\) at the critical exponent. Consider any fixed mixed factorial moment of finitely many pattern counts, with a total of \(h\) selected occurrences. Expand it over ordered selections of **pairwise distinct occurrences of each given pattern**, as required by falling factorials. An occurrence is an \(r\)-element translated site set. Group all selected occurrences into connected overlap clusters. A singleton cluster has exactly \(r\) sites. Any cluster with at least two distinct occurrences has at least \(r+1\) sites, even when occurrences have different patterns. A fixed cluster shape has \(O(n)\) translations; a selection with \(g\) clusters and \(v\) total distinct required sites therefore contributes \(O(n^{g-v/r})\). This is \(o(1)\) unless every cluster is a singleton. For disjoint singleton occurrences there are \(n^h+O(n^{h-1})\) admissible ordered translations and their probability is \((c+o(1))^{rh}n^{-h}\). The mixed factorial moment hence tends to \((c^r)^h\), exactly the mixed factorial moments of independent \(\operatorname{Poisson}(c^r)\) variables. Standard factorial-moment determinacy gives the joint convergence.

**2. The local-neighborhood dichotomy.** Fix a radius \(R\). On a path, a radius-\(R\) neighborhood is a colored directed interval of bounded size, possibly truncated by an endpoint. The endpoints and their fixed-radius windows are unmarked with probability tending to one, since \(k_n/n\to0\). The expected number of local intervals realizing a prescribed configuration with exactly \(s\) marked sites and finitely many specified unmarked sites has order \(n(k_n/n)^s\). For \(s<r\), this diverges; the corresponding occurrence count divided by its mean tends to one in probability. Indeed, the count of positive required-site patterns has variance at most its mean plus \(O(n(k_n/n)^{s+1})\): disjoint site requirements are negatively correlated under sampling without replacement, and overlapping bounded-interval placements have at least \(s+1\) required sites. Requiring finitely many additional sites to be unmarked removes an expected \(O(n(k_n/n)^{s+1})\) occurrences, negligible relative to the mean. For \(s\geq r+1\), the expected count is \(O(n(k_n/n)^s)=o(1)\); such neighborhoods disappear in probability.

For a neighborhood type with exactly \(r\) marks, and ignoring a probability-vanishing event involving \(r+1\) marks in a uniformly bounded window, every occurrence is supplied by precisely one translated \(r\)-point configuration. Hence the count of each such rooted type is a fixed finite integer linear combination of \(X_{a,n}\) for patterns with bounded diameter, plus a term that vanishes in probability. This applies simultaneously to any fixed finite set of local types.

**3. From local counts to all first-order sentences.** Effective Hanf locality on structures with Gaifman degree at most two supplies, for each first-order sentence \(\varphi\), a computable radius \(R\), threshold \(t\), and a Boolean decision rule on the counts of radius-\(R\) rooted neighborhood types, each count truncated at \(t\). By step 2, types with fewer than \(r\) marks are eventually saturated, types with more than \(r\) marks disappear, and endpoint contributions stabilize. The remaining finite vector of truncated counts is a deterministic function of finitely many \(X_{a,n}\). Step 1 gives its distributional limit; probabilities of integer-valued finite vectors converge at every point, so the rule has a limit expressible as a finite Boolean combination of the events \(\{Z_a=j\}\). The finite normal form and finite Poisson probabilities make the limit effectively computable given \(r\), a description of \(c\) permitting evaluation of its exponential, and the sentence; for arbitrary real \(c\) this is an effective symbolic expression in \(c\).

**4. Converse and density.** For each fixed pattern \(a\), a first-order formula \(\theta_a(x)\) says that \(x\) and its specified fixed successor offsets are marked. This uses a bounded chain of existentially quantified intermediate successors, not global order. For any fixed nonnegative integer \(j\), a first-order sentence says that precisely \(j\) distinct starting sites satisfy \(\theta_a\); its asymptotic probability is \(\mathbb P(Z_a=j)\). Finite Boolean combinations realize every event in the stated algebra, proving **equality** of the two spectra. Infinitely many distinct patterns are available for each \(r\); the zero-count events \(\{Z_a=0\}\) are independent Bernoulli events of common probability \(e^{-c^r}\in(0,1)\). A partition by the first \(d\) such events has maximum atom probability at most \(\max\{e^{-c^r},1-e^{-c^r}\}^{d}\to0\). Selecting unions of atoms approximates any target probability in \([0,1]\) arbitrarily closely. There are only countably many sentences, hence the spectrum is countable and dense.

**5. Off-critical zero-one law.** Put \(p_n=k_n/n\sim c n^{\alpha-1}\). Every local configuration with exactly \(s\geq1\) marks has expected count of order \(np_n^s\sim c^s n^{1-s(1-\alpha)}\). If \(\alpha\) avoids all \(1-1/s\), this tends either to zero or to infinity. The first-moment and negative-correlation/overlap estimates of step 2, applied at each fixed radius, make all local-type count truncations deterministic in probability. The endpoint windows again contain no marks with probability tending to one. Hanf locality yields a deterministic limiting truth value for each sentence, proving the zero-one law.

## Relationship to prior work
Shelah and Spencer (1994) studied independently marked unary predicates on finite linear and circular orders, established zero-one regimes, and identified special critical exponents. Their Theorem 1.1 is an **off-critical** zero-one result for a stronger cyclic order language, not the present conditional fixed-weight model and full Poisson spectrum of successor-only paths. St. John (1997) classified first-order limiting probabilities at the separate sparse scale \(p_n=c/n\) for an independently marked **globally linearly ordered** word. Spencer and St. John (1998) go further at the adjacency threshold \(p_nsymp n^{-1/2}\): they characterize all first-order limiting probabilities of independently marked globally ordered words using ordered rare-pair occurrences. In particular, rare pairs and their Poisson occurrence counts at the first collision threshold are established background, not part of the claimed higher-threshold novelty. The zero/one phase hierarchy and use of first-order locality have substantial antecedents. Koponen (2026) studies requirements for convergence of random expansions of finite structures. Markström and Räty (2026) treat regular languages and permutation classes under their natural uniform models, not growing prescribed Hamming-weight slices. The proposed distinction is the **simultaneous independent Poisson family and exact countable dense first-order spectrum under uniform fixed-cardinality marking at every higher collision threshold from \(r=3\) onward**. The supplied literature comparison does not constitute a proof of historical priority.

## Limitations
The successor-only vocabulary is essential; adding global order can detect ordering among widely separated rare patterns. The model is uniformly conditioned on exactly \(k_n\) marks, not independent Bernoulli marking. The method gives effective symbolic probability expressions but no asserted uniform rate over all sentences or efficient complexity bound for converting to Hanf normal form. No independent audit has been performed. The 1998 full-order Bernoulli adjacency-threshold result is a close antecedent at \(r=2\); the present claim excludes that threshold. Stronger higher-threshold or de-Poissonization results in sparse unary-predicate logic remain a material historical-priority risk.
