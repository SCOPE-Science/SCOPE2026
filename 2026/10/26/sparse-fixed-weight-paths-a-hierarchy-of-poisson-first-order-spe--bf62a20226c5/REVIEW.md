# Scientific review: sparse fixed-weight successor paths

## Correctness (C): PASS
The exact hypergeometric formula for prescribed marks and the overlap-cluster factorial-moment argument identify an independent Poisson limit for every finite collection of distinct \(r\)-point gap patterns. The structural proof separately treats the abundant \(s<r\) local patterns, the disappearing \(s>r\) patterns, and the critical \(s=r\) patterns. Bounded-degree Hanf locality then makes the first-order limit a finite Boolean function of Poisson pattern counts. The converse is furnished by explicit first-order sentences counting occurrences, and independent zero-count events establish density. The finite checker independently verifies the exact finite moment identities but is not an infinite proof. No unjustified claim of a full-order-language theorem is made.

## Originality (O): PASS, with documented priority risk
Shelah and Spencer, *Random Sparse Unary Predicates* (1994), obtain first-order zero-one regimes in independently marked linear/circular languages and identify critical powers; they do not state this conditioned, fixed-cardinality model's all-pattern joint Poisson convergence and exact successor-only probability spectrum. St. John, *Limit Probabilities for Random Sparse Bit Strings* (1997), analyzes independent marking at scale \(c/n\) in a globally ordered language, not the hierarchy \(k_n\sim c n^{1-1/r}\) of fixed-cardinality successor words. Koponen (2026) studies the finite-model property and random expansions; Markström and Räty (2026) study regular languages and permutation classes. The relevant distinction is mathematical, not merely terminological: the sampling measure, logic vocabulary, rare-object indexing, and spectrum characterization all differ. Spencer and St. John (1998) already give a substantially stronger, globally ordered Bernoulli limit-law analysis at the pair-collision threshold, and that \(r=2\) threshold is excluded from the new claim. Their full text was inspected. A stronger result at \(r\ge3\) or one transferring their method under conditioning could still cover the higher-threshold theorem; that priority question remains open. A failed search by itself is not a priority guarantee.

## Value (V): PASS
This is a complete classification across all fixed exponents \(0<\alpha<1\) for a natural successor-only finite-model probability experiment, with explicit exceptional exponents and an unusual countable dense spectrum at each exception. The result distinguishes the structural effects of a global cardinality constraint from independent-marking limit laws and gives a symbolic calculation of every first-order limit. The exact domain and boundary are mathematically motivated by local collisions of \(r\) marks rather than an arbitrary finite statistic.
