Uniformly marked successor paths at exactly \(k_n\sim c n^{1-1/r}\) marks for each integer \(r\geq3\) exhibit independent Poisson counts for every fixed \(r\)-point gap pattern, and their complete first-order probability spectrum is countable and dense in \([0,1]\). At exponents strictly between zero and one that avoid every collision power \(1-1/s\) with integer \(s\geq2\), the successor-only language satisfies a zero-one law.
