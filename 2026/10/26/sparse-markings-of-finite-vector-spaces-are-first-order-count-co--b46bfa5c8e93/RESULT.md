# Sparse markings of finite vector spaces are first-order count-complete

## Finding
Fix a finite field \(\mathbb F_s\), with \(s\) a prime power, and a real \(c>0\). Independently mark every point of the \(d\)-dimensional vector space \(V_d=\mathbb F_s^d\) with probability \(c/s^d\). Regard it as an \(L_s\)-structure, where \(L_s=\{0,+,(\lambda\cdot)_{\lambda\in\mathbb F_s},P\}\) includes the vector-space operations and a unary marking predicate. For any fixed first-order sentence \(\varphi\), the limit \(\lim_{d\to\infty}\Pr(V_d\models\varphi)\) exists and can be calculated by inspecting finitely many explicitly bounded vector spaces.

Let \(R\) be the quantifier rank of \(\varphi\), and put \(T=2s^R\). Write \(B_{k,h}\) for the vector space of dimension \(k+h\) with predicate consisting of \(k\) independent standard basis vectors. Define \(b_k=\mathbf1_{\{B_{k,R}\models\varphi\}}\) for \(0\le k\le T\). Then
\[
\lim_{d\to\infty}\Pr(V_d\models\varphi)
=e^{-c}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}b_k\frac{c^k}{k!}
+b_T\left(1-e^{-c}\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}\frac{c^k}{k!}\right).
\]
In particular, the exact set of attainable first-order limiting probabilities is
\[
\mathcal S_c=\left\{e^{-c}\sum_{k\in F}\frac{c^k}{k!}:
 F\subseteq\mathbb N_0\text{ finite or cofinite}\right\},
\]
where the sum for a cofinite set is convergent. Its closure is the factorial-subsums set
\[
\overline{\mathcal S_c}=\left\{e^{-c}\sum_{k\in A}\frac{c^k}{k!}:A\subseteq\mathbb N_0\right\},
\]
a perfect nowhere-dense compact set of Hausdorff dimension zero. Although the topology of this probability set follows from the already known Poisson-subsums form in the *different* ordered sparse-bit-string model, the substantive assertion here is that the vector-space addition, all scalar multiplications and arbitrarily nested first-order quantifiers detect no further limiting information beyond the finite-or-cofinite cardinality test of the sparse marking.

## Assumptions and scope
The field \(\mathbb F_s\) and \(c>0\) are fixed as \(d\to\infty\). For sufficiently large \(d\), \(c/s^d\in[0,1]\). Markings of distinct vectors are independent. The probability space is over predicates only; the underlying vector-space operations are deterministic. The language contains no order, basis names, arbitrary relation variables or dimension-dependent symbols. Quantifier rank is the maximum nesting depth of first-order quantifiers. In the finite reference structure \(B_{k,h}\), the complement to the marked independent basis has dimension \(h\). Effectivity means a finite algorithm for a symbolic expression in the real parameter \(c\); numerical evaluation of an arbitrary noncomputable \(c\) requires its value as input.

## Proof
**Step 1: a uniform finite-rank game lemma.** Let \(R\ge0\), \(T_r=2s^{R-r}\) for \(0\le r\le R\). Consider two vector spaces with predicates consisting of respective independent sets of basis vectors, with at least \(R\) complementary dimensions. If the numbers of marked vectors are equal, or are both at least \(T_0\), the structures agree on all sentences of quantifier rank at most \(R\). The first alternative is immediate for the marked coordinate blocks, but the second requires a simultaneous coefficient-pattern argument.

For clarity, write a vector space as the direct sum of the span of its marked basis \(E=\{e_i\}\) and an unmarked complementary space \(W\). After \(r\) pebbles \(v_1,\ldots,v_r\), write \(v_j=\sum_i a_{ij}e_i+w_j\). For each row pattern \(\alpha\in\mathbb F_s^r\), count marked coordinates \(i\) with \((a_{i1},\ldots,a_{ir})=\alpha\). The invariant is that each pair of corresponding counts is either **equal and below \(T_r\)** or **both at least \(T_r\)**. In addition the kernels of the two linear maps \(\mathbb F_s^r\to W\), defined by the tuples of complementary components, are equal. For \(r=0\), the invariant follows from the hypothesis on marked-set sizes.

Suppose the invariant holds at \(r<R\). A new pebble specifies one additional coefficient in \(\mathbb F_s\) for each existing marked basis coordinate, splitting every old row-pattern cell into \(s\) child cells. A small old cell has equal size on both sides, so the opponent copies its exact \(s\)-way split. A large old cell has size at least \(T_r=sT_{r+1}\) on each side. Among its child cells at least one has size at least \(T_{r+1}\). Copy every small child count exactly, and distribute the remaining elements among the large children, with each receiving at least \(T_{r+1}\). This is possible on the opponent's side: if \(j\ge1\) child cells are large, the other \(s-j\) children contain at most \((s-j)(T_{r+1}-1)\) elements, while the parent has at least \(sT_{r+1}\). Thus at least \(jT_{r+1}\) elements remain. This preserves the invariant after adding the pebble. Independently, choose its complementary component to have exactly the same linear-dependence relations with preceding complementary components; the complement has dimension at least \(R>r\), so dependent choices and an independent choice are both reproducible. The constructed marked and complementary coordinates determine an actual response vector.

After \(R\) rounds every coefficient-pattern cell is either equal of size zero or one, or has at least two elements in both structures. Every atomic equation between vector-space terms in the pebbles reduces to zero of a linear combination, which holds exactly when (a) the corresponding linear combination of complementary components is zero and (b) every marked coefficient-pattern row evaluates to zero. Every atomic marking assertion \(P(t)\) holds exactly when the complementary component of the value of \(t\) vanishes and the vector has precisely one marked basis coefficient equal to \(1\), all others zero. These conditions depend only on the common complementary kernel and on whether each terminal row-pattern cell has zero, one or at least two elements. Hence the pebble correspondence preserves **all atomic formulae**, including nested linear terms and scalar multiplications, and the duplicator wins the Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game. This proves the lemma, including \(R=0\) (vacuous game).

**Step 2: independence of a sparse marking.** Let \(K_d=|P|\). Since \(K_d\sim\operatorname{Bin}(s^d,c/s^d)\), the classical binomial-to-Poisson limit gives \(K_d\Rightarrow\operatorname{Poisson}(c)\). Conditional on \(K_d=k\), the marked set is a uniformly chosen \(k\)-subset of \(V_d\). Exposing its elements one at a time, after \(j\) independent elements their span has \(s^j\) points; consequently for every fixed \(k\) the probability of linear dependence is at most \(\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}s^j/(s^d-j)\), which tends to zero. All independent \(k\)-subsets are in one orbit under \(\mathrm{GL}(d,s)\). For fixed \(k\) and sufficiently large \(d\), the conditional structure therefore has the same truth of \(\varphi\) as \(B_{k,d-k}\), except on an event of vanishing probability. By Step 1, for \(d-k\ge R\) this truth is \(b_k\) if \(k<T\) and \(b_T\) if \(k\ge T\). Truncating the tight Poisson count at any fixed cutoff and then allowing the cutoff to grow proves the displayed limiting-probability formula. Brute-force finite-model evaluation on the \(T+1\) structures \(B_{k,R}\) makes the formula effective.

**Step 3: exact image of first-order probability limits.** Step 2 shows every limit is a finite or cofinite sum of Poisson point masses. Conversely, for each fixed \(k\), the statement that the unary predicate has exactly \(k\) elements is first-order expressible using \(k\) distinct existential witnesses and one universal clause. Finite disjunctions and their negations realize every finite or cofinite set of counts. No extra probabilities occur.

**Step 4: closure and dimension.** Put \(w_k=e^{-c}c^k/k!\) and \(R_m=\sum_{k>m}w_k\). Finite-subset sums are dense among all subset sums because \(R_m\to0\), so the closure is the continuous image of the compact binary sequence space under \((\varepsilon_k)\mapsto\sum_k\varepsilon_k w_k\). It is compact and perfect: each point has distinct arbitrarily close approximants obtained by flipping one sufficiently late digit (possibly for nonunique coding). For sufficiently large \(k\), \(w_k>\sum_{j>k}w_j\), since the successive weight ratio is \(c/(k+1)\) and becomes less than one half; thus the tail coding is injective and each point has arbitrarily close *distinct* points, not just distinct codes. For every \(m\), at most \(2^{m+1}\) intervals of length \(R_m\) cover the set. Because \(R_m\le C_c c^{m+1}/(m+1)!\) for all sufficiently large \(m\), \(2^{m+1}R_m^\delta\to0\) for every \(\delta>0\). All positive-dimensional Hausdorff measures vanish, giving Hausdorff dimension zero, and the set has empty interior. Compactness makes it nowhere dense.

## Verification
The standalone script `check_vector_fo.py` independently enumerates marked-independent subsets of binary vector spaces up to dimension five and compares 19 counts with the exact sequential-span product. It also implements a full two-move Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game on small binary marked-vector-space structures, including all atomic linear combinations of pebbled points, and verifies three test comparisons (one equivalent and two distinguishable). Finally, it checks six numerical factorial-weight gap inequalities at \(c=1\). Running `python check_vector_fo.py` yields `VECTOR_SPARSE_FO_FINITE_OK 19 EF2_3 POISSON_GAPS6`. These finite checks do not certify the infinite-rank game lemma or limits; Steps 1–4 provide their proofs.

## Relationship to prior work
Katherine St. John's 1997 Theorem 1 (*Limit Probabilities for Random Sparse Bit Strings*, DOI 10.37236/1308) **already establishes the same finite/cofinite Poisson-subsums probability formula for a different model**, namely an independent sparse predicate on a finite *linearly ordered* set. The present theorem makes no novelty claim for the numerical subset-sum formula alone: its mathematical distinction is the explicit bounded-rank equivalence theorem showing that all vector-space operations fail to distinguish the sparse independent marked configurations beyond their truncated number. The different underlying signatures prevent an immediate logical implication from the ordered-bit-string theorem; no first-order interpretation transferring vector addition from the order was identified. The zero-dimensional topology follows simply from the previously known factorial-weight spectrum once that spectrum is established here.

Shelah and Spencer's *Random sparse unary predicates* (1994, arXiv math/9401214) studies sparse predicates in a circular-order language; its published abstract does not state the vector-space EF lemma. Berarducci, Mamino and Mennuni's *Vector spaces with a union of independent subspaces* (arXiv:2209.03867, especially Section 4) studies model theory of unions of independent **subspaces** over both infinite and finite fields, rather than growing finite-dimensional finite-field spaces with a randomly chosen *finite set of independent individual points*; its finite-field case treats near-model-completeness, not this probability law. Koponen's *A characterization of the finite model property for ω-categorical theories* (arXiv:2610.16163v1, first posted 2026-10-12), in particular the discussion of random expansions in Remark 2.15, supplies contemporary logical motivation but not these asymptotic probability results. Larrauri, Müller and Noy (2021) give a contrasting analysis of limiting-probability closures for sparse random graphs/hypergraphs rather than deterministic vector-space addition with sparse unary markings. General old statements on vector-space EF games or exchangeability could conceivably imply the game lemma; historical priority is not independently settled.

## Limitations
The conclusion requires a **fixed finite field**, a **fixed positive intensity**, sparse independent point-markings and **ordinary first-order** logic without externally named basis elements or order. No claim is made for growing fields, denser marking regimes, monadic second-order logic, or predicates marking affine subspaces. The spectrum itself coincides with a previously known spectrum from the ordered-bit-string model; only its occurrence in the finite-vector-space model and explicit finite-rank collapse constitute the assessed new result. No independent mathematical audit or priority certification has occurred.
