# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** Independent Brownian resetting gives the exact stationary Laplace displacement law. Conditioning on fixed anchors produces a deterministic conditional mean count and a sum of independent particle covariances; Campbell's theorem evaluates their mean for any stationary anchor point process of the stipulated intensity. The nonstandard kernel \(K_x\) follows from the reset semigroup's initial-state-independent renewal term, the exponential-time Brownian resolvent, and two explicitly integrated Laplace probabilities. Exponential covariance domination establishes absolute convergence and the \(O(T^{-2})\) remainder. The shifted-lattice and Poisson plateaus follow by direct integration and exact series. A separate executable checker reproduces 25 algebraic, numerical-quadrature, and random-sampling assertions. The proof, not simulation, supports the infinite result.

**Originality: PASS (self-review, subject to independent priority checking).** Di Bello et al., arXiv:2302.06696, study one-time current distributions for independent resetting and uniform initial positions; their conclusion identifies correlated initial conditions as an extension. Banerjee, Jack and Cates, arXiv:2206.08739, already establish a general initial-condition variance decomposition and hyperuniformity effects for unreset diffusion; those ingredients and single-time spatial variances are not presented as new. The distinct evaluated result here is the long-time *temporal average* under individual resetting, with a universally identical Green--Kubo coefficient across all stationary fixed-intensity anchor processes and a sharp lattice-versus-Poisson plateau comparison. The earlier Poisson-only resetting results cannot establish the spatially correlated ensemble assertion without further argument. Possible equivalent specialization of other renewal or time-covariance theory remains a priority risk.

**Value: PASS.** An exact time-average decomposition is directly informative for measurement precision in a resetting gas whose spatial preparation is uncertain but fixed over each experiment. It sharply separates persistent configuration noise from temporal fluctuations and proves the latter remains unchanged when the initial anchor structure becomes hyperuniform. The explicit shifted-lattice case produces an order-one plateau while the Poisson plateau diverges with dense anchors, a substantive contrast in experimentally meaningful observables.

**Limits.** Stationary Brownian displacement, independent per-particle Poisson resets, a fixed stationary anchor process of finite intensity, and a finite-second-moment anchor-weighted sum are required. The time and dense-density limits have a specified order. The result does not cover synchronized resets or interacting particles.
