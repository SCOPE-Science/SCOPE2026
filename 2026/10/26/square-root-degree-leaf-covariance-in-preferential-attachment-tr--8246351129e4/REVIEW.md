# Mathematical review

## Correctness
**PASS.** Conditional Bernoulli attachment indicators give exact recurrences for the marked vertex degree, whole-tree leaf count, and their overlapping event. The nonstandard covariance identity \(C_{n+1}-C_n=a_n^2d_n\ell_n-a_nq_n\) is derived by multiplying the actual increments and retaining the case when the marked vertex is itself a leaf. Algebraic substitution gives a positive increment for every \(n\ge3\). Central-binomial asymptotics and a telescoping leaf-variance recurrence justify both stated growth and correlation constants. The packaged rational-history checker agrees through order nine; infinite conclusions use the proof, not enumeration.

## Originality
**PASS on the inspected evidence, with residual historical uncertainty.** The nearest published results address (i) the initial degree martingale of a BA tree and root recovery; (ii) leaf-count means and global degree-count covariances; (iii) general split-tree representations; and (iv) analogous but genuinely different recursive/increasing-tree models. None of the inspected statements furnishes the finite age-marked degree/global-leaf covariance or the \(\sqrt n\) sign-and-scaling law. The proof's scalar-moment recurrences could possibly be subsumed by an unlocated historical marked-vertex treatment; further historical verification is appropriate before a priority claim.

## Value
**PASS.** An oldest vertex is a canonical hub marker, while the global leaf count measures terminal network mass. The exact all-size sign and nonvanishing ratio \(C_n/\mathbb E D_n\to1/3\) identify a persistent order-\(\sqrt n\) local-to-global dependence in a standard network null model. In contrast, the comparable uniform-attachment root–leaf covariance is bounded. The simultaneous vanishing correlation clarifies why absence of a constant Pearson coefficient cannot be interpreted as absence of a substantial covariance.
