# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact normalized Bergman weights yield matrix (3). The holomorphic rank-one trace-class family has integrable boundary singularity bounded by \((1-|z|)^{-1+\theta}\). The dyadic intervals stay uniformly far from the analytic singularity under a complex parameter circle of radius two; Cauchy bounds and exact positive Gaussian quadrature give the trace-norm error at rank \(Jm\). The Eckart–Young/min–max characterization then gives \(s_n=O(e^{-c\sqrt n})\) and \(\mathcal S_p\) for every \(p>0\). The main boundary exponent and quadrature factors were reconstructed, and finite-section calculations with the accompanying script are consistent with the argument. The finite tests are not used as proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to residual priority risk

The closest direct source is Li–Zhou, arXiv:2610.05672v1, especially Theorem 4.2 and its proof. It establishes the same rank-one decomposition and trace-class membership, but states Schatten membership only for \(p\ge1\); its theorem does not yield any decay rate beyond summability. The new quantitative approximation and its \(p<1\) consequence are strictly stronger and not present in the inspected full text. Peláez–Seco address generalized Hilbert operators on a single Dirichlet-type scale, rather than this precise two-weight map. Beckermann–Townsend's finite-section spectral estimates lose uniformity in matrix dimension. Searches for equivalent weighted Hilbert-matrix and differential-Hilbert spectral rates did not identify a matching statement. Priority risk remains from broad analytic-kernel approximation theory and potentially unindexed literature.

## Value — PASS

The 2026 characterization naturally leaves open whether compact differential Hilbert maps lie in the strictly finer quasi-Schatten ideals. Membership for every \(p>0\), together with constructive trace-norm rank-complexity \(O((\log(1/\varepsilon))^2)\), settles that quantitative approximation question for the entire strict supercritical family. The rate is not sharp and no exact singular-value sequence is given.
