# Scientific review of strict birthday–coverage anticorrelation

**Correctness — PASS.** The conditioning event \(B_m>b\) fixes exactly \(b\) observed labels, permitting an exact geometric-stage conditional expectation. The tail-sum identity for \(B_m\), including the rare \(B_m=m+1\) case, derives the strictly negative covariance as a sum of positive terms with a minus sign. The stochastic-order coupling uses identical remaining geometrical increments and handles the last pair of conditioning indices separately. An explicit Gaussian envelope and exponentially small far-tail bound justify the \(\sqrt m\) asymptotic; Rayleigh moments and the exact coupon-completion variance justify the correlation normalization. Exact-rational independent recurrences confirm all sizes \(2\le m\le60\) but do not establish the infinite result.

**Originality — PASS on the inspected evidence, with an explicit priority risk.** Targeted queries of relevant mathematical records and primary literature were compared by implication. Flajolet–Gardy–Thimonier treat both classical stopping problems, but the materially inspected birthday and coupon sections establish marginal facts rather than a cross-stopping-time covariance. Pósfai discusses coupon waiting-time distributions; O'Neill describes occupancy-threshold geometric sums. Those tools can be used in this proof, but none of the inspected results gives the exact mixed covariance sum and sharp \(m^{-1}\) correlation law. Holst (1986) presents a particularly relevant unified birthday/coupon-collector theory; its original text could not be accessed, so the available abstract does not support a full-text non-coverage finding. An equivalent statement in that work or other unexamined occupancy literature remains a residual historical risk, not resolved by a negative search alone.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The birthday collision time and complete coverage time are two canonical observables of the same sampling trajectory, with very different scales: \(\sqrt m\) and \(m\log m\). The all-size negative dependence, complete conditional stochastic ordering and surprisingly small, explicit \(m^{-1}\) correlation illuminate joint behavior not determined by their classical marginal Rayleigh and Gumbel limits. The proof supplies a reusable tail-conditioning approach to mixed stopping-time moments.

**Limitations.** The theorem assumes uniform independent labels; the proof does not identify the complete bivariate limiting distribution, and historical priority has not been independently certified. The supporting finite code is reproducibility evidence rather than proof.
