# Scientific review

**Correctness (C): PASS.** Every acyclic spanning edge subset of \(K_{3,n}\) belongs to one of three disjoint high-degree leaf patterns. Explicit counts of each marked-edge and marked-pair class give rational joint probabilities; three coefficientwise identities yield strictly negative covariances for every integer \(n\ge3\). Independent brute-force union-find enumeration for \(1\le n\le6\), checking every edge pair, plus polynomial identities and exact sign tests, passed 4,825 assertions. No inference of an infinite theorem from enumeration is used.

**Originality (O): PASS (best-knowledge, residual priority risk).** Huang (arXiv:2311.00965v1, full text inspected) analyzes general arboreal gas with adjacent-edge conclusions only for large fugacity and complete-graph conclusions in restricted regimes. Erickson (arXiv:1008.3660v3, full text inspected) proves a stronger weighted I-Rayleigh property only for series-parallel graphs; the present host graph has a \(K_4\) minor. Stark (Discrete Mathematics 313 (2013), complete accessible text inspected) studies unweighted bipartite forests, but its adjacent-edge asymptotic theorem assumes both sides diverge and explicitly does not settle nonadjacent edges. Speyer (2019, full web answer inspected) establishes the unweighted forest-count normalization already, not edge covariance; that normalization is not claimed as new. Grimmett–Winkler inspected from abstract and Erickson's summary only, finite small-order enumeration. Searches of prior research records for the exact host graph, arboreal gas, negative correlation and edge-pair types found no direct or stronger match. Originality cannot be certified against all literature, and an overlooked equivalent statement is possible.

**Value (V): PASS.** Pairwise edge negative correlation of uniform forests is a recognized open structural problem; this result establishes strict sign and exact constants for *all three* orbit types on an infinite family of 3-connected, non-series-parallel graphs not covered by the known series-parallel theorem. This is not a mere recomputation of the known forest total: it resolves previously untreated disjoint-edge dependence at every size, quantifies its distinct asymptotic scale and provides explicit examples outside sufficient-condition classes.

**Limits:** uniform edge weights only; pairwise rather than full negative association; priority not definitively established; independent mathematical audit outstanding.
