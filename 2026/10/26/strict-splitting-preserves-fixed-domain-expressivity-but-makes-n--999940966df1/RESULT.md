# Strict splitting preserves fixed-domain expressivity but makes nonemptiness-team model checking NP-complete

## Finding
Consider propositional team logic with atoms \(p,\neg p,\bot,\top,\mathrm{NE}\), conjunction, and split disjunction, where \(\mathrm{NE}\) holds exactly on nonempty teams. Lax splitting permits overlapping witnesses \(T=S\cup U\); strict splitting requires \(T=S\mathbin{\dot\cup}U\), with empty witnesses allowed.

**Theorem (joint semantic and computational separation).** For every fixed finite propositional alphabet \(X\), the team properties over the exact domain \(X\) definable by strict splitting are precisely the convex, union-closed families of subsets of \(2^X\). These are the same properties definable by lax splitting. In fact every such family has a formula with exactly the same semantics under both conventions. Nevertheless, combined model checking for the strict variant is NP-complete, even on formulas \(\Phi\vee\Phi\) in which \(\Phi\) is a conjunction of positive tests \(\Diamond\alpha\), with \(\alpha\) classical and \(\Diamond\alpha:=((\mathrm{NE}\wedge\alpha)\vee\top)\). Combined model checking for the lax variant is in P by Anttila--Kontinen--Yang (2025).

## Assumptions and scope
A team is a finite **set of distinct valuations** on its declared finite domain. A formula is evaluated on a team whose domain includes its variables. The expressive-completeness assertion fixes the *whole declared domain* \(X\) and permits formulas to mention any variable in \(X\); it does **not** assert locality for strict splitting when the domain is subsequently extended. For complexity, both formula and explicit team are inputs, formulas are syntax trees, and partitions may have empty parts. The classical language allows negation of propositional variables and the two Boolean constants, not contradictory or arbitrary team negation.

A family \(\mathcal F\subseteq\mathcal P(2^X)\) is union closed if the union of any nonempty collection of its members is in \(\mathcal F\); it is convex if \(A,B\in\mathcal F\) and \(A\subseteq C\subseteq B\) imply \(C\in\mathcal F\). The empty family qualifies vacuously.

## Proof
**1. Closure under strict splitting.** Put \(U=2^X\), a finite set. A family \(\mathcal F\subseteq\mathcal P(U)\) is convex and union closed if and only if it is empty, or there exists a top team \(B\subseteq U\) such that \(B\in\mathcal F\) and \(\mathcal F\) is upward closed within \(\mathcal P(B)\). Indeed, the top is \(B=\bigcup\mathcal F\), which belongs to \(\mathcal F\) by finite union closure, and convexity supplies every intermediate superset. Conversely, upward closure within \(B\) yields both properties.

Flat classical formulas and \(\mathrm{NE}\) have this form. Two such families are closed under intersection: if the intersection is nonempty, its top is the intersection of their tops; any existing common witness extends to that top, and every intermediate superset remains common. For strict split, let \(\mathcal H=\{A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B:A\in\mathcal F,\ B\in\mathcal G\}\). If \(\mathcal H\) is nonempty, fix a witness partition \(T=A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B\). Each element of \((B_{\mathcal F}\cup B_{\mathcal G})\setminus T\) belongs to at least one of the two tops; assign it to a permitted side. Upward closure there gives a witness for the enlarged team. This works for every enlarged team between \(T\) and \(B_{\mathcal F}\cup B_{\mathcal G}\). The union of the two tops itself has a witness, so it is the maximum of \(\mathcal H\); any member can be enlarged to any team between it and that maximum. Thus \(\mathcal H\) has the same form. Structural induction completes the closure proof.

**2. Normal form, valid for both splitting conventions.** Every Boolean function on valuations in \(U\) is represented by some classical formula \(\alpha_C\) whose satisfying valuations are exactly \(C\subseteq U\) (use finite disjunctions of complete literals). Classical formulas are flat under either split convention. Hence \(\Diamond\alpha_C:=((\mathrm{NE}\wedge\alpha_C)\vee\top)\) is true on \(T\) under either semantics precisely when \(T\cap C\ne\varnothing\): select a nonempty witness in \(C\), and assign the rest to \(\top\).

For a nonempty convex union-closed family \(\mathcal F\), let \(B=\bigcup\mathcal F\). The nonmembers of \(\mathcal F\) within \(\mathcal P(B)\) form a downward-closed family; let \(M_1,\ldots,M_s\) be its maximal members. Then the formula
\[
\alpha_B\ \wedge\ \bigwedge_{i=1}^s\Diamond\alpha_{B\setminus M_i}
\]
holds exactly on \(\mathcal F\), simultaneously under lax and strict splitting. This is because, for \(T\subseteq B\), membership in \(\mathcal F\) is equivalent to \(T\not\subseteq M_i\) for every \(i\). If \(\mathcal F=\varnothing\), use \(\bot\wedge\mathrm{NE}\). Empty conjunctions are \(\top\). The lax expressive completeness itself is also established in Anttila--Knudstorp (2025); the above establishes a common normal form and the strict counterpart.

**3. Strict model checking belongs to NP.** Give, for each occurrence of a strict disjunction in the syntax tree, the subset of the current team sent to its left child; the right child receives its complement. There are at most \(|\varphi|\) occurrences and at most \(|T|\) row-membership bits at each occurrence. Verify literals, \(\mathrm{NE}\), conjunctions and disjunction witnesses in polynomial time. This yields an NP certificate.

**4. NP-hardness by hypergraph property B.** Let \(H=(V,E)\) be an arbitrary finite 3-uniform hypergraph. Testing whether its vertices admit a red--blue coloring with no monochromatic edge (monotone not-all-equal 3-SAT) is NP-complete. Assign distinct binary valuations \(v_i\) of \(k=\lceil\log_2|V|\rceil\) variables to vertices \(i\in V\) and set \(T=\{v_i:i\in V\}\). For each edge \(e\), let \(\alpha_e\) be the classical disjunction of the three complete minterms for its vertices. Its size is \(O(k)\). Set
\[
\Phi_H=\bigwedge_{e\in E}\Diamond\alpha_e,\qquad
\Psi_H=\Phi_H\vee\Phi_H.
\]
On any subteam \(S\subseteq T\), \(S\models\Phi_H\) under either split semantics precisely when \(S\) meets every edge. Under **strict** splitting, \(T\models\Psi_H\) exactly when \(T=S\mathbin{\dot\cup}(T\setminus S)\), with both sides meeting every edge. That is exactly a proper red--blue hypergraph coloring. The reduction is polynomial in the input size; combined with NP membership, strict model checking is NP-complete. Under lax splitting, the same formula is true whenever each hyperedge is nonempty, since both disjuncts can use the full team. The cited 2025 complexity theorem proves polynomial-time lax model checking for *all* formulas of the language.

## Verification
The standalone `checker.py` implements recursive strict and lax team semantics with flat classical tests; the classical tests used in hardness are interchangeable with their explicit minterm formulas by flatness. It enumerates **all** 3-uniform hypergraphs on 2, 3, 4 and 5 vertices (respectively 1, 2, 16 and 1,024 hypergraphs), and verifies that strict truth of \(\Psi_H\) agrees with exhaustive two-coloring whereas lax truth holds. It also checks the 7-point Fano hypergraph (strict false, lax true), and independently builds the complete strict-definable closure and convex union-closed family census for ambient valuation sets of cardinality 1, 2 and 3 (respectively 4, 11 and 42 families). These finite checks support, but do not replace, the general proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Anttila and Knudstorp's *Convex Team Logics* (first public March 27, 2025; revision October 14, 2026) proves convex union-closed expressive completeness for **lax** propositional nonemptiness-team logic. Anttila, Kontinen and Yang's *Complexity Results in Team Semantics: Nonemptiness Is Not So Complex* (first public October 9, 2025) explicitly defines **overlapping** subteam splitting and proves polynomial-time combined model checking. The new contribution is the **strict/disjoint** variant and its contrasting complexity, together with a semantics-invariant fixed-domain normal form. Earlier work of Yang on strict variants of other union-closed team logics describes failure of locality or union closure in different languages, not this combined strict nonemptiness result. Hypergraph 2-colorability is a classical NP-complete problem, used as a reduction source rather than claimed as new.

## Limitations
Expressive equivalence is *relative to a fixed declared finite domain*, not a statement that every formula preserves truth under changing the domain. Strict splitting can fail locality: \((\mathrm{NE}\wedge p)\vee(\mathrm{NE}\wedge p)\) holds on the two distinct valuations \((p,q)=(1,0),(1,1)\) but fails on their one-element projection to \(\{p\}\). NP-completeness is a combined-complexity result with an explicit input team, not a data-complexity or succinct-team result. The proof does not establish a strict/lax polynomial-time formula translation or a polynomial-time algorithm for strict model checking; the expressivity normal form can be exponentially long. Literature equivalences under unexamined terminology remain a priority risk. Independent audit has not been performed.
