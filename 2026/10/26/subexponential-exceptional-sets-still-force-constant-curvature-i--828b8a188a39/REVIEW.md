# Self-review

## Correctness

PASS. The unstable exponent of a periodic orbit is the time average of the infinitesimal unstable expansion potential. If the centered potential were not a flow coboundary, Livšic would supply a periodic orbit with nonzero average. Specification then combines that orbit with positive-entropy orbit segments to create exponentially many periodic orbits whose averages stay away from zero. They would all lie in the exceptional set, contradicting its subexponential counting bound. The cited surface rigidity theorem then forces \(K_g\equiv-\alpha^2\).

## Originality

PASS, with priority risk noted. The inspected 2024 source assumes a constant unstable exponent on every periodic orbit. The present claim identifies a sufficient exceptional-set condition that forces that global hypothesis. The inspected subset-rigidity literature concerns marked lengths or approximate metric control, not this Lyapunov-potential statement. The proof uses established thermodynamic tools, so the novelty is the precise geometric corollary rather than a new specification or Livšic theorem.

## Value

PASS. This gives a sharp, interpretable weakening of an all-periodic-orbit rigidity hypothesis: exceptions may exist, but they cannot have positive exponential counting growth. It clarifies what a large but incomplete periodic-orbit family can imply, without claiming an effective finite cutoff.
