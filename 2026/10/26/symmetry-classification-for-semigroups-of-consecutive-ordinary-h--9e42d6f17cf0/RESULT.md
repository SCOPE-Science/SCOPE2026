# Symmetry classification for semigroups of consecutive ordinary hexagonal numbers

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge2\), put \(H(n)=n(2n-1)\) and
\[
S_n=\langle H(n),H(n+1),H(n+2)\rangle\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\]
Then \(S_n\) is a symmetric numerical semigroup if and only if
\[
\boxed{n\in\{2,3,6,7,8,18\}.}
\]
Equivalently, for its genus \(g_n\) and Frobenius number \(F_n\), one has \(2g_n=F_n+1\) precisely at these six indices. Their exact invariants are:

| \(n\) | Generators | \(F_n\) | \(g_n\) |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 2 | 6, 15, 28 | 65 | 33 |
| 3 | 15, 28, 45 | 377 | 189 |
| 6 | 66, 91, 120 | 1589 | 795 |
| 7 | 91, 120, 153 | 6029 | 3015 |
| 8 | 120, 153, 190 | 3347 | 1674 |
| 18 | 630, 703, 780 | 35357 | 17679 |

## Assumptions and scope
A numerical semigroup is a cofinite additive submonoid of \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\). It is symmetric if, for every integer \(j\), exactly one of \(j\) and \(F-j\) belongs to the semigroup; equivalently \(2g=F+1\). We use *ordinary*, not centered, hexagonal numbers. The index begins at \(n=2\); \(H(1)=1\) produces the trivial semigroup \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), not part of the classification. The generators have gcd one for each \(n\ge2\).

## Proof
Write
\[
a=H(n)=2n^2-n,\quad b=H(n+1)=2n^2+3n+1,\quad c=H(n+2)=2n^2+7n+6.
\]
Thus \(b-a=4n+1\) and \(c-a=8n+6\). Elementary Euclidean calculations give
\[
\gcd(a,b)=\begin{cases}3,&n\equiv2\pmod3,\\1,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
\qquad
\gcd(b,c)=\begin{cases}3,&n\equiv1\pmod3,\\1,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]
The two congruences are mutually exclusive, so \(\gcd(a,b,c)=1\). Also
\[
\gcd(a,c)=2^{[2\mid n]}3^{[3\mid n]}5^{[n\equiv3\pmod5]}\le30,
\]
where an exponent in square brackets is one when its condition holds and zero otherwise. Indeed factor \(a=n(2n-1)\) and \(c=(n+2)(2n+3)\). The four cross-gcds of those factors are respectively \(\gcd(n,n+2)=\gcd(n,2)\), \(\gcd(n,2n+3)=\gcd(n,3)\), \(\gcd(2n-1,n+2)=\gcd(n+2,5)\), and \(\gcd(2n-1,2n+3)=1\). Since the two factors within either product are coprime, the formula follows.

For \(n\ge4\), the three generators are minimal. Clearly \(b<2a\), and for \(n\ge6\) also \(c<2a\), preventing any generator from being generated by the other two. At \(n=4,5\), \(c<a+b\), \(c\ne2a\), and \(c\ne2b\), so minimality follows separately. At \(n=2\), the minimal set is \(\{6,15,28\}\). At \(n=3\), \(c=3a\), and \(\langle15,28,45\rangle=\langle15,28\rangle\), which is symmetric.

For a three-minimally-generated numerical semigroup, the classical gluing characterization states that symmetry holds exactly when some two generators \(u<v\), with \(d=\gcd(u,v)>1\), have the remaining generator \(z\) in \(\langle u/d,v/d\rangle\). We now reduce this infinite disjunction to twenty-four exact arithmetic tests.

Every pairwise gcd \(d\) divides 30. Suppose \(a>d(c-a)\) and \(dz=su+tv\) for nonnegative integers \(s,t\). Since \(a\le u,v,z\le c\), setting \(k=s+t\) yields \(k=d\): if \(k\le d-1\), then \(su+tv\le(d-1)c<da\le dz\); if \(k\ge d+1\), then \(su+tv\ge(d+1)a>dc\ge dz\). If \(k=d\), the remaining generator must lie between \(u\) and \(v\). Therefore \((u,v)=(a,c)\) and \(z=b\). Then
\[
d(b-a)=t(c-a),\qquad \gcd(4n+1,8n+6)=1,
\]
forcing \(8n+6\mid d\). This is impossible: at \(n=2,3\) the endpoint gcd is 2 and 15 respectively, and for \(n\ge4\), \(8n+6>30\ge d\). Hence **a necessary condition for any gluing is**
\[
\boxed{a\le d(c-a).}
\]
In particular, \(a>30(c-a)\) excludes every index. This inequality is
\[
2n^2-241n-180>0,
\]
which holds at \(n=122\) and increases strictly thereafter; therefore no \(n\ge122\) is symmetric.

Using the explicit gcd formulas, the necessary inequality leaves the following *complete* candidate sets for \(2\le n\le121\):

| Pair \((u,v)\), remaining \(z\) | Candidate indices \(n\) |
| --- | --- |
| \((a,b)\), \(z=c\) | 2, 5, 8, 11 |
| \((a,c)\), \(z=b\) | 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 12, 13, 18, 24, 28, 33, 38, 48, 78, 108 |
| \((b,c)\), \(z=a\) | 4, 7, 10, 13 |

For each listed triple let \(d=\gcd(u,v)\), \(x=u/d\), \(y=v/d\). Since \(\gcd(x,y)=1\), define \(s_0\) as the unique integer in \([0,y-1]\) satisfying \(s_0x\equiv z\pmod y\), and put \(t_0=(z-s_0x)/y\). Then \(z\in\langle x,y\rangle\) **if and only if** \(t_0\ge0\): every other nonnegative solution for the first coefficient is \(s_0+jy\), where \(j\ge0\), and cannot improve a negative \(t_0\). The following table is a complete, independently reproducible exact certificate. Entries give \((n:s_0,t_0)\).

| Pair | Candidate certificates |
| --- | --- |
| \((a,b)\) | (2: 4, 4); (5: 9, -2); (8: 43, -30); (11: 9, -4) |
| \((a,c)\) | (2: 5, 0); (3: 1, 9); (4: 15, -5); (6: 1, 4); (8: 8, 3); (9: 46, -28); (12: 55, -35); (13: 54, -36); (18: 5, 23); (24: 137, -111); (28: 159, -129); (33: 106, -80); (38: 107, -87); (48: 104, -67); (78: 217, -177); (108: 735, -679) |
| \((b,c)\) | (4: 18, -11); (7: 1, 1); (10: 18, -13); (13: 105, -89) |

The nonnegative second coordinates occur exactly for \(n=2,3,6,7,8,18\). Explicit gluing identities include
\[
28=4\cdot2+4\cdot5,\quad28=1\cdot1+9\cdot3,\quad
91=1\cdot11+4\cdot20,\quad91=1\cdot40+1\cdot51,
\]
\[
153=8\cdot12+3\cdot19,\qquad703=5\cdot21+23\cdot26.
\]
Together with the two-generator case \(n=3\), these identities establish all six symmetric cases, and all others are excluded.

For each symmetric triple with witness pair \(u,v\), remaining generator \(z\), and \(d=\gcd(u,v)\), the usual complete-intersection Frobenius formula gives
\[
F=uv/d+dz-(a+b+c).
\]
For \(n=3\), use the two-generator formula \(F=15\cdot28-15-28\). Substituting the witnesses gives the six values listed in the finding. Symmetry then gives \(g=(F+1)/2\).

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` using only Python's standard library. It reproduces the twenty-four reduced candidate tests and all six exceptional indices; for every \(n\in[2,121]\), an independent shortest-path computation of the Apéry set modulo \(a\) calculates exact \(F\), \(g\), and the symmetry condition. It additionally matches all 29 published OEIS A069758 Frobenius values at indices 2 through 30. The script checks the gcd formulas over an extended finite range and the base and monotonicity of the symbolic cutoff, but these computational checks are not substitutes for the preceding infinite proof.

## Relationship to prior work
OEIS A069758 (Victoria A. Sapko, April 8, 2002) identifies the ordinary three-consecutive-hexagonal Frobenius sequence and tabulates early Frobenius numbers; it does not state the symmetry indices or a complete genus/symmetry classification. Leonid G. Fel, *Symmetric Numerical Semigroups Generated by Fibonacci and Lucas Triples* (arXiv:0803.1606, first posted March 11, 2008), recalls the general three-generator gluing criterion. The present specialization requires the explicit hexagonal gcd calculation, uniform exclusion, and exact modular candidate resolution; the general criterion alone gives neither the exceptional list nor its completeness. Research on consecutive **centered** polygonal numbers concerns different generators and does not directly imply this classification.

## Limitations
This is a classification for triples of **ordinary hexagonal** numbers only, not for arbitrary polygonal orders, centered hexagonal numbers, or more than three generators. The original index \(n=1\) gives a trivial semigroup containing 1 and is excluded by convention. The proof uses the standard published gluing criterion and finite exact arithmetic; originality is assessed relative to inspected sources, not guaranteed against every unindexed publication. Independent validation has not been performed.
