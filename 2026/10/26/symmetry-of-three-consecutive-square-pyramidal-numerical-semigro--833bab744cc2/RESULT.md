# Symmetry of three consecutive square-pyramidal numerical semigroups

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge 1\), put
\[
Q(n)=\sum_{j=1}^n j^2=\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}6,\qquad
S_n=\langle Q(n),Q(n+1),Q(n+2)\rangle.
\]
All three generators have gcd one. The complete symmetry classification is
\[
\boxed{S_n\text{ is symmetric}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ n\in\{1,2\}\text{ or }n\equiv0,4\pmod6.}
\]
Here a numerical semigroup is *symmetric* when for its Frobenius number \(F\), exactly one of \(z,F-z\) lies in the semigroup for each integer \(z\), equivalently its genus \(g\) satisfies \(2g=F+1\). We use \(F(\mathbb N_0)=-1\).
For \(n\equiv0,4\pmod6\), its exact Frobenius number is
\[
\boxed{F(S_n)=\frac{4n^5+32n^4+101n^3+175n^2+102n-36}{36}},\qquad
\boxed{g(S_n)=\frac{F(S_n)+1}{2}}.
\]
The two exceptional small semigroups are \(S_1=\mathbb N_0\), with \((F,g)=(-1,0)\), and \(S_2=\langle5,14\rangle\), with \((F,g)=(51,26)\). Thus the symmetric-index set has natural density \(1/3\).

## Assumptions and scope
The index is an integer \(n\ge1\); the generators are sums of ordinary squares, not consecutive squares themselves and not tetrahedral, triangular, or hexagonal numbers. All generated semigroups are submonoids of \(\mathbb N_0\), with Frobenius number the greatest excluded nonnegative integer. For \(n\ge3\), the three generators form a minimal generating set.

## Proof
Write \(a=Q(n)\), \(b=Q(n+1)\), and \(c=Q(n+2)\). Their successive differences are \(b-a=(n+1)^2\) and \(c-b=(n+2)^2\). The identity
\[
\boxed{(n+1)c=(n+2)a+5b}\tag{1}
\]
is obtained by substituting the defining cubic polynomial. For any integer \(u\ge2\), the two factors \(u-1\) and \(2u-1\) are coprime to \(u\); comparing valuations at the primes dividing \(u\) in \(Q(u-1)=u(u-1)(2u-1)/6\) gives
\[
\gcd(Q(u-1),Q(u))=\gcd(Q(u-1),u^2)
=\frac{u}{\gcd(u,6)}.
\]
In particular, put \(q=\gcd(n+1,6)\), \(r=\gcd(n+2,6)\), \(d=(n+1)/q\), and \(e=(n+2)/r\). Then
\[
\gcd(a,b)=d,\qquad \gcd(b,c)=e.
\]
Since \(d\mid(n+1)\) and \(e\mid(n+2)\), the gcd of \(a,b,c\) is one. From (1), \(\gcd(a,c)\mid5b\); coprimality of the triple therefore forces \(\gcd(a,c)\mid5\). Thus \(\gcd(a,c)\in\{1,5\}\).

For \(n\ge8\), the strict inequality \(c<2a\) follows from
\[
2a-c=\frac{2n^3-9n^2-35n-30}{6}>0;
\]
the numerator is positive at \(n=8\) and increasing thereafter. Because \(a<b<c\), this ensures \(c\notin\langle a,b\rangle\). For the remaining \(3\le n\le7\), the five explicit triples \((14,30,55),(30,55,91),(55,91,140),(91,140,204),(140,204,285)\) give the same nonmembership directly. Hence the triple is minimal for all \(n\ge3\).

We invoke the classical three-generator gluing criterion: a minimally three-generated numerical semigroup of gcd one is symmetric exactly when, after permuting generators, two have gcd \(D>1\) and the third belongs to the semigroup generated by the two quotients after division by \(D\). This is the standard three-generator complete-intersection characterization; it is stated with its associated Frobenius formula as Lemma 1 in Fel's 2016 paper (see References).

**Pair \(a,b\).** Put \(A=a/d\) and \(B=b/d\), so \(\gcd(A,B)=1\). Equation (1) becomes
\[
q c=(n+2)A+5B.
\]
If \(c=\alpha A+\beta B\) with \(\alpha,\beta\ge0\), coprimality gives an integer \(t\) such that
\[
q\alpha=n+2+tB,\qquad q\beta=5-tA.
\]
For \(n\ge3\) one has \(A>5\) and \(B>n+2\). If \(t>0\), the second equation is negative, and if \(t<0\), the first is negative. Thus \(t=0\), requiring \(q\mid n+2\) and \(q\mid5\). Since \(q\mid6\), this is equivalent to \(q=1\), or \(n\equiv0,4\pmod6\). Conversely, in this case \(d=n+1>1\) and \(c=(n+2)A+5B\), a valid gluing. In particular these cases are symmetric.

**Pair \(b,c\).** Put \(B=b/e\), \(C=c/e\). Because \(e=(n+2)/\gcd(n+2,6)\) and \(c=(n+2)(n+3)(2n+5)/6\), one has \(\gcd(e,C)=1\): a prime dividing \(n+2\) divides neither \(n+3\) nor \(2n+5\), and cancellation of the factor \(6\) introduces no such prime. If \(a=\alpha B+\beta C\) with nonnegative coefficients, (1) implies
\[
((n+2)\alpha+5e)B=((n+1)e-(n+2)\beta)C.
\]
Thus \((n+2)\alpha+5e=tC\), \((n+1)e-(n+2)\beta=tB\) for an integer \(t>0\). The first equation and \(\gcd(e,C)=1\) imply \(e\mid t\). The second then gives \(B\le n+1\), contradicting \(B=(n+1)(2n+3)r/6>n+1\) for all \(n\ge3\). No symmetry arises from this pair.

**Pair \(a,c\).** Their gcd is at most five, so a relevant gluing would require \(\gcd(a,c)=5\). Put \(A=a/5\) and \(C=c/5\). If \(b=\alpha A+\beta C\), (1) yields
\[
(5(n+2)+5\alpha)A=(5(n+1)-5\beta)C.
\]
Coprimality of \(A,C\) gives an integer \(t>0\) for which \(5(n+2+\alpha)=tC\), \(5(n+1-\beta)=tA\); these two equations force \(5\mid t\). Writing \(t=5s\), one obtains \(n+1-\beta=sA\), and hence \(A\le n+1\). But \(a/5=n(n+1)(2n+1)/30>n+1\) for every \(n\ge4\). At \(n=3\), \(\gcd(a,c)=1\). Therefore this pair cannot provide symmetry for \(n\ge3\).

These cases exhaust the classical criterion and prove the classification for \(n\ge3\). At \(n=1\), the first generator is one. At \(n=2\), \(c=30=6a\) and \(S_2=\langle5,14\rangle\), which is symmetric with Frobenius number \(5\cdot14-5-14=51\).

For \(n\equiv0,4\pmod6\), \(d=n+1\), \(A=n(2n+1)/6\), \(B=(n+2)(2n+3)/6\). The two-generator Sylvester formula and gluing give
\[
F(S_n)=d(AB-A-B)+(d-1)c
=\frac{4n^5+32n^4+101n^3+175n^2+102n-36}{36}.
\]
Symmetry implies the stated genus. Of the six residue classes modulo six, exactly two qualify for all sufficiently large indices, proving density \(1/3\).

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify.py` implements an Apéry-set shortest-path calculation, independent of the gluing test, for the forty indices \(1\le n\le40\). It checks the Frobenius number, genus, symmetry, and the special cases. Separate exact-arithmetic checks for \(3\le n\le10000\) verify pairwise gcd identities, recurrence (1), and the three gluing tests. Expected output begins with `VERIFY_OK`. These finite tests are independent spot checks; they are not substitutes for the preceding universal argument.

## Relationship to prior work
The elementary semigroup gluing and Frobenius formulas are classical, and this work does not claim them as new. Fel (2016) classifies analogous three-generator semigroups formed from the squares, cubes, and quartics of three consecutive integers; the values \(Q(n)\) studied here are cubic *sums of squares* and are not a special case of those pure-power triples. Robles-Pérez and Rosales (2017) derive Frobenius results for consecutive triangular and tetrahedral numbers, rather than these three consecutive square-pyramidal numbers. OEIS A000330 contains the underlying square-pyramidal sequence, not a symmetry classification of its consecutive triples. Literature comparisons found no direct statement or stronger theorem implying this particular infinite classification; this is not proof of global novelty.

## Limitations
No Frobenius or genus formula is asserted for nonsymmetric indices \(n\ge3\); the classification determines symmetry, not their full gap distributions. The result is limited to three consecutive square-pyramidal numbers. An equivalent characterization may exist in additional or unindexed literature. Independent validation has not been performed.
