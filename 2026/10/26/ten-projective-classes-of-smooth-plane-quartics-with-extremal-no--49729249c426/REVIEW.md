# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The finite domain and all normalizations are explicit: 13 rational plane points; 13 four-point lines; 234 six-point nontrivial blocking sets; 15 quartic monomials; a rank-six vanishing matrix and its nine-dimensional kernel; 567 equations with exact prescribed rational locus. The degree-seven Jacobian-rank criterion is justified by Euler's identity and the Hilbert series of a homogeneous regular sequence of three cubics. Independent Gröbner computations find exactly the same 222 geometrically smooth projective equations, not just the same total. The full 5,616-element projective group and the 24-element stabilizer give ten orbit sizes and the exact 51,948 census. Source code and reference outputs are embedded.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The closest blocking-curves literature (Asgarli–Ghioca–Yip, arXiv:2208.13299) addresses general degree thresholds and larger-field quartic obstructions rather than the complete smooth degree-four, field-three extremal incidence classification. Their arithmetic-statistics follow-up (arXiv:2211.08523, Section 7 Example 7.2) presents the exact field-three density only for degrees at least five, leaving the current smooth degree-four orbit problem outside its hypotheses. The genus-three plane-quartic classification literature (Lercier–Ritzenthaler–Rovetta–Sijsling, arXiv:1403.0562) does not provide an identical incidence count for this field. Finite-geometric work classifies blocking point subsets rather than embedding smooth quartic equations with such subsets. Unindexed tables or earlier computer searches might nonetheless encode the same ten classes implicitly; absence from inspected sources is not a proof of world-first priority.

## Value — PASS
The six rational points realize the smallest possible nontrivial blocking configuration in the 13-point ternary projective plane, a sharp and intrinsic incidence boundary. Determining which smooth genus-three canonical plane curves realize these configurations, and their arithmetic projective automorphism classes, addresses a distinct moduli and incidence question not decided by the point-set classification or asymptotic blocking-statistics bounds. The unique order-four stabilizer class supplies a meaningful symmetry distinction, and the exhaustive exact answer is reusable as a small-field benchmark.

## Limits and nearest comparators
Only the field-three extremal six-point stratum is classified; larger blocking loci and other fields are outside the claim. Closest primary sources were inspected in full for relevant hypotheses, tables, and sections. The finite-geometric classification article was available in abstract form but not full text, so unindexed supplemental counts are a residual bibliographic risk. All verification is exact but computer-assisted, and no independent external audit has been performed.
