# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed component scheme follows from an exact coefficientwise identity in characteristic three. Smoothness and the four rational points of the conic are established by the gradient and the squares in \(\mathbb F_3\). Polarization identifies every component line with one of the four conic tangents. Bézout and the dual conic exclude additional contacts and triple-line points over the algebraic closure. The ten resulting singularities have explicit local models \(uv\) (six nodes) and \(u(u-v^2)\) (four tacnodes); their normalization quotients and Jacobian ideals give \(\delta=(1,2)\), \(\tau=(1,3)\), totals \(14\) and \(18\). The arithmetic genus check independently agrees with a degree-six plane curve. Inclusion–exclusion among the five rational components and ten rational double-branch intersections establishes the point count over every finite extension and its zeta function. The bundled checker reproduces the exact polynomial identity and finite incidence data. The geometric classification is proved rather than inferred from finite enumeration.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The full Wiman plane equation and classical genus-six geometry are established in Inoue–Kato; their treatment explicitly excludes characteristic three. Kawakita (2015) assumes primes above five for the relevant curve-counting and isogeny statements. Giulietti–Kawakita–Lia–Montanucci, Remark 3.1 (2018), already states that this same polynomial is reducible in characteristics two and three; that antecedent is explicitly credited. The complete factorization as the conic plus exactly its four rational tangent lines, its ten-point singularity basket, local lengths and all-singular rational locus are not supplied by the inspected primary passages. A direct implication comparison shows that generic reducibility does not determine the irreducible factors, contact orders, or local conductor. Literature searches and mathematical-record comparisons found no matching full classification; this absence alone is not a proof of uniqueness. Risk: older modular/projective treatments may have obtained the same factorization without indexing the singularity data.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a complete natural special-fibre classification at the exceptional prime of a classical genus-six curve, rather than an arbitrary parameter slice. It identifies the degeneration with the entire rational tangent arrangement of a smooth conic over the smallest odd field, explains the four tangencies and six additional line crossings, and quantifies the normalization and Jacobian defects and computes the complete zeta function of the singular special fibre. These data are needed to understand the boundary and local models of the classical Wiman equation and provide a base for further arithmetic-model calculations. No claim about semistable/stable reduction or a general moduli theorem is made.
