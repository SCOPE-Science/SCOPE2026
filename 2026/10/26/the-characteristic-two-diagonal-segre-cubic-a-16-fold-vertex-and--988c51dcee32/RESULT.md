# The characteristic-two diagonal Segre cubic: a 16-fold vertex and a jump from 15 to 27 planes

## Finding
Let \(R=\mathbb Z_{(2)}\). In \(\mathbb P^5_R\), let \(\mathscr D\) be defined by \(\sum_{i=1}^6X_i=0\) and \(\frac13\sum_{i=1}^6X_i^3=0\). Its characteristic-zero geometric fiber is the classical Segre cubic with 10 nodes and 15 planes. Its geometric characteristic-two fiber \(D_2\) is a projective cone over a smooth Hermitian cubic surface \(C\), and the intrinsic projective Jacobian singular scheme is supported at the cone vertex with local algebra \(k[\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3,\epsilon_4]/(\epsilon_1^2,\epsilon_2^2,\epsilon_3^2,\epsilon_4^2)\), of length \(16\). The special fiber has exactly \(27\) geometric planes: exactly \(15\) descend to \(\mathbb F_2\) and are the distinct specializations of the generic 15 pairing planes; the remaining \(12\) are defined over \(\mathbb F_4\) but not \(\mathbb F_2\), forming six Frobenius pairs. Consequently the relative Hilbert scheme parametrizing planes in this flat cubic family is **not flat** at \(2\): the lengths of its geometric fibers are \(15\) and \(27\). The dual of the special cone is the dual cubic surface inside the hyperplane of hyperplanes through the cone vertex, and the Gauss map of its base surface is purely inseparable of degree \(4\).

## Assumptions and scope
The base is the discrete valuation ring \(R=\mathbb Z_{(2)}\), so \(3\) is a unit. Geometric fibers are taken over algebraically closed fields of characteristic zero or two, respectively. Planes mean closed linear copies of \(\mathbb P^2\) in the specified \(\mathbb P^4\), not abstract surfaces. A plane is said to be defined over \(\mathbb F_q\) when its corresponding Grassmannian point descends to \(\mathbb F_q\). This is the **naive diagonal integral model** of the classical Segre cubic, and is **not** the modular/GIT Segre-cubic integral model whose characteristic-two fiber can retain ten nodes. The relative Fano/Hilbert scheme is the usual closed subscheme of the Grassmannian, representing contained projective planes.

## Proof
**Flatness and characteristic-zero fiber.** The ambient hyperplane \(H_R=V(\sum X_i)\) is \(\mathbb P^4_R\). The cubic \((1/3)\sum X_i^3\) is a relative effective Cartier divisor, hence flat over \(R\), since its reduction is a nonzero cubic. Over characteristic zero its diagonal model is the classical Segre cubic with ten nodes and fifteen distinct pairing planes; this also follows by the standard singular-locus calculation and the classical classification (Kondō, §2; Dolgachev, §2). For each partition of six indices into three pairs, the three linear pair-sum equations specify one plane in every fiber.

**Special cone and Jacobian thickness.** In characteristic two set \(s=X_6\) and \(u_i=X_i+s\) for \(1\le i\le5\). The linear equation becomes \(\sum_{i=1}^5u_i=0\), and the cubic becomes \(\sum_{i=1}^5u_i^3=0\), independent of \(s\). Hence \(D_2\) is a projective cone with vertex \(v=[1:1:1:1:1:1]\) over \(C=V(\sum u_i,\sum u_i^3)\subset\mathbb P^4\). Let \(V=\{(u_i)\in k^5:\sum u_i=0\}\). On \(V\), the gradient of the cubic vanishes exactly when the vector \((u_i^2)\) lies in the span of the all-ones vector. Over a field, all the \(u_i\) must then be equal, and because \(5\ne0\) in characteristic two and \(\sum u_i=0\), all vanish. Thus \(C\) is smooth and the unique singular point of the cone is \(v\). In linear coordinates \(z_1,\ldots,z_4\) on \(V\), write \(t=\sum_{i=1}^4z_i\); the cubic is \(F=\sum_{i=1}^4 z_i^3+t^3\) and the four partial derivatives are \((z_i+t)^2\). The change of variables \(w_i=z_i+t\) has matrix \(I+J\), whose square equals \(I\) over \(\mathbb F_2\), so the \(w_i\) form a coordinate system. The Euler identity places \(F\) in its derivative ideal, and the Jacobian ring near \(v\) is exactly \(k[w_1,\dots,w_4]/(w_i^2)\), length \(16\).

**Hermitian surface and the \(27\) planes.** On the four-dimensional vector space \(V\) over \(\mathbb F_4\), the Hermitian form \(h(u,v)=\sum u_i v_i^2\) is nondegenerate: its orthogonal complement in \(k^5\) is the all-ones vector, which is not in \(V\) since five is odd. Thus \(C\) is the nonsingular Hermitian cubic surface. Every plane in the cone contains \(v\), because projecting any plane avoiding \(v\) would put a projective plane into the smooth cubic surface \(C\), which is impossible. Planes through \(v\) correspond bijectively to lines on \(C\). The classical 27-line theorem for a smooth cubic surface gives at most and exactly 27 geometric lines. The complete finite-field enumeration in the checker exhibits all 27 over \(\mathbb F_4\), 15 fixed by the \(\mathbb F_2\) Frobenius. Independently, lines defined over \(\mathbb F_2\) are the maximal totally isotropic subspaces of the 4-dimensional symplectic \(\mathbb F_2\)-space \(V(\mathbb F_2)\) under its restricted dot product. Their number is \((2+1)(2^2+1)=15\). Specializing the 15 pair-partition planes produces precisely these 15 distinct rational planes: the checker constructs the corresponding spans and verifies equality. The other 12 geometric planes therefore form six nontrivial Frobenius pairs.

**Reducedness and nonflat relative Hilbert scheme.** For a plane \(\Pi\) through \(v\), corresponding to a line \(\ell\subset C\), the normal deformation sequence is \(0\to N_{\Pi/D_2}\to\mathcal O_\Pi(1)^2\xrightarrow{(q_1,q_2)}\mathcal O_\Pi(3)\). Here \(q_1,q_2\) are binary quadratics in the coordinates of \(\ell\), and have no common zero because \(C\) is smooth. Their syzygy module is generated in polynomial degree 2, so no degree-1 syzygy exists, giving \(H^0(N_{\Pi/D_2})=0\). Thus all 27 geometric points in the special plane Hilbert scheme are reduced. In characteristic zero the classical 15 planes are also reduced: at a pairing plane the same normal-map calculation reduces to two relatively prime quadratic forms, again yielding no first-order deformation. The geometric scheme lengths are therefore \(15\) and \(27\). A finite-type scheme over a discrete valuation base, flat near a zero-dimensional proper fiber with no new positive-dimensional fiber components, would have constant geometric length; equivalently, a proper quasi-finite relative Hilbert scheme is finite near the closed fiber, so its length would be constant if flat. The jump proves nonflatness.

**Dual degeneration.** The Gauss map of the smooth cubic \(C\) is the projective squaring map \(u\mapsto[u_1^2:\ldots:u_5^2]\) on its four-dimensional linear span, after the identification of \(V\) with its dual via the nondegenerate dot form. It is relative Frobenius of a surface, hence purely inseparable of degree \(2^2=4\), with image the Frobenius-twisted smooth cubic dual surface. All tangent hyperplanes of the cone contain \(v\). Accordingly \(D_2^\vee\) is a cubic surface in that hyperplane, unlike the characteristic-zero Igusa quartic hypersurface.

## Verification
Run `python3 diagonal_segre_char2_checker.py` with Python 3 and no external packages. It checks exact polynomial gradients and the invertible Jacobian coordinate change over \(\mathbb F_2\), enumerates all \(85\) points of \(\mathbb P^3(\mathbb F_4)\), finds the \(45\) points on the smooth cubic, and exhaustively determines the \(27\) lines, their \(15\) rational descents and the six conjugate pairs. It also checks the fifteen pairing-plane specializations against the fifteen rational lines. Finite-field exhaustion alone does not establish the absence of lines defined only over extensions; that step relies on the classical 27-line theorem and smoothness proved above. The relative Hilbert-fiber scheme structures and full Jacobian thickening follow from the written general algebraic arguments, not the finite enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Kondō (2011, §2) and Dolgachev (2015, §2) provide the diagonal classical Segre cubic, its ten ordinary nodes, fifteen planes and its Igusa-quartic dual in characteristic zero. Ren--Sam--Sturmfels (2013, §2) study the same classical configuration in characteristic zero. Dolgachev (2024, §3) explicitly warns that a characteristic-two GIT model of the Segre cubic is **not** projectively equivalent to the standard diagonal equations; its ten-node model is a different integral extension. These results motivate the choice of the diagonal model but do not state the scheme length 16 at its special vertex, the exact \(15+12\) arithmetic plane decomposition, or the relative plane Hilbert-scheme length jump. The 27 lines of a smooth cubic and standard Hermitian-surface facts are classical, and are *not* claimed separately as discoveries. The claimed contribution is their explicit arithmetic and scheme-theoretic interaction in the specified degenerating diagonal integral family.

## Limitations
No flat or canonical modular interpretation of the diagonal integral model is asserted. This result does not identify the characteristic-two GIT moduli fiber with this cone; indeed that identification is false. A general Frobenius assertion for the Gauss map of the entire cone is not made: that map has positive-dimensional rulings; the degree-four statement concerns its smooth cubic base. The Fano count covers planes, not lines, and it does not classify all embedded flat limits of other subschemes. Equivalent earlier treatment under modular or Hermitian-cubic terminology is possible; no independent scientific audit has been completed.
