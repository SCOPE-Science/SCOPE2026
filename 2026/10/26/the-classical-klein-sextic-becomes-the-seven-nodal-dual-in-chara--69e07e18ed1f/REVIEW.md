# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
Smoothness of the quartic and the exact Gauss substitution identity are proved directly. The displayed gradient equations exhaust the dual sextic's geometric critical support. Its seven singularities have nondegenerate tangent quadrics, and irreducibility follows from Bézout and the noncollinearity argument. The genus, Gauss degree, Frobenius factorization and bitangent correspondence follow from the stated standard results. The bundled exact checker reruns the polynomial identity and seven-point incidence.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Elkies (1998) already gives the integral sextic and the classical quartic; Müller–Ritzenthaler (2004) explicitly records the characteristic-two seven-bitangent criterion. These are credited as antecedents. The characteristic-zero Hessian formula has a factor of \(54\) in the denominator, so it cannot imply the characteristic-two duality. The apparently additional result is the exact coincidence of the reduced sextic invariant with the dual, together with the node coordinates and explicit Frobenius normalization. Pellikaan (1998) supplies finite-field incidence context, not an identified equation for the projective dual. Historical equivalent coverage remains a substantial risk.

## Value — PASS
This is a complete geometric classification of a canonical exceptional-characteristic dual curve, giving its equation, singularities, normalization and all geometric bitangent lines. The explicit identification with a classical integral invariant is independently motivated, beyond the known number seven.

## Limits and sources
Prior general characteristic-two bitangent theory accounts for the count but not the displayed sextic identification. The result does not treat the integral family of dual schemes in mixed characteristic; historical mathematical literature and independent validation remain incomplete. Main primary sources: Elkies, *The Klein Quartic in Number Theory* (MSRI Publications 35, 1998); Müller–Ritzenthaler, arXiv:math/0405052v1 (2004); Pellikaan, *Codes, Curves and Signals* (1998).
