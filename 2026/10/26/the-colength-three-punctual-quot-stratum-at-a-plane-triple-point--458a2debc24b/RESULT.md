# The colength-three punctual Quot stratum at a plane triple point

## Finding
Let \(R=\mathbb C[[x,y]]/(f)\) have order-three reduced defining series and write \(f_3\) for its initial cubic. Put \(r=|V(f_3)(\mathbb C)\cap\mathbb P^1|\), so \(1\le r\le3\). For each integer \(d\ge1\), let \(Q_d\) parameterize \(R\)-submodules \(N\subset\mathfrak m R^d\) for which \(\operatorname{length}(\mathfrak mR^d/N)=3\). With \(\mathbb L=[\mathbb A^1]\), its class in \(K_0(\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb C})\) is
\[
[Q_d]=[\operatorname{Gr}(3,2d)]+\mathbb L^{2d-2}\frac{\mathbb L^{3d}-(\mathbb L+1)\mathbb L^d+\mathbb L}{\mathbb L-1}
 +(\mathbb L+1)\mathbb L^{2d-2}[\mathbb P^{d-1}][\mathbb P^{2d-2}]+r\mathbb L^{3d-1}[\mathbb P^{d-1}].
\]
The apparent denominator divides the numerator in \(\mathbb Z[\mathbb L]\). For \(d=1\) the answer specializes to \(1+\mathbb L+(r+1)\mathbb L^2\); the new information is the complete all-rank formula, including the interaction of the two generators of the maximal ideal with \(d\) framing vectors.

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is \(\mathbb C\), and \(f\in(x,y)^3\setminus(x,y)^4\) is reduced; its leading form may have repeated linear factors. No assumption is made about its degree-four and higher terms. The stratum consists of quotients of \(R^d\) with length \(d+3\) requiring exactly \(d\) generators, equivalently quotients of \(\mathfrak mR^d\) of length three. Every quotient is supported at the closed point. Classes of constructible strata are taken in the ordinary Grothendieck ring of complex varieties, so only reduced underlying parameter varieties enter the identity.

## Proof
Set \(E=\mathfrak mR^d\), and write \(U=E/N\). Because \(\operatorname{length}(U)=3\), one has \(\mathfrak m^3U=0\). Its possible radical Hilbert functions are \((3)\), \((2,1)\), and \((1,1,1)\). In the first case \(U\cong\mathbb C^3\), and quotients correspond to three-dimensional quotients of \(E/\mathfrak mE\cong\mathbb C^{2d}\), giving \(\operatorname{Gr}(3,2d)\).

For Hilbert function \((2,1)\), choose a splitting of the two-dimensional top and one-dimensional socle. Multiplication by \(x,y\) is a pair of linear forms \(\alpha,\beta\) on the top, not both zero. Up to isomorphism either they are independent, or proportional. An \(R\)-map \(E\to U\) is specified by the images \(X_i,Y_i\) of \(xe_i,ye_i\), subject to \(xY_i=yX_i\); the relation deriving from \(f\) has degree at least three and acts trivially here. In the independent case fix \(\alpha=(1,0)\), \(\beta=(0,1)\). The top coordinates of \(X_i,Y_i\) have the form \((a_i,b_i)\), \((b_i,c_i)\); hence there are three rows \(a,b,c\in\mathbb C^d\). The surjectivity condition is that the resulting \(2\times2d\) matrix has rank two. The deficient locus consists of the zero triple and the nonzero triples of the form \((s^2v,stv,t^2v)\), parameterized by \([s:t]\in\mathbb P^1\) and a nonzero vector \(v\in\mathbb A^d\). Its class is \(1+(\mathbb L+1)(\mathbb L^d-1)\). The socle components of all \(X_i,Y_i\) contribute \(\mathbb A^{2d}\). The automorphism group of the target module is \(\mathbb G_m\ltimes\mathbb G_a^2\), of class \((\mathbb L-1)\mathbb L^2\). This special group acts freely on surjections; its torsors are Zariski locally trivial. The independent stratum therefore contributes
\[
\mathbb L^{2d-2}\frac{\mathbb L^{3d}-(\mathbb L+1)\mathbb L^d+\mathbb L}{\mathbb L-1}.
\]

When \(\alpha,\beta\) are proportional, their common direction \([a:b]\) ranges through \(\mathbb P^1\). Normalize \(\alpha=(a,0)\), \(\beta=(b,0)\). The top coordinates are \(X_i=(av_i,s_i)\), \(Y_i=(bv_i,t_i)\), and surjectivity says \(v\ne0\) and \((s,t)\notin\mathbb C(av,bv)\). Thus top-coordinate surjections have class \((\mathbb L^d-1)(\mathbb L^{2d}-\mathbb L)\). Accounting for the \(2d\) arbitrary socle coordinates and quotienting by the special automorphism group \((\mathbb G_m)^2\ltimes\mathbb G_a^3\), of class \((\mathbb L-1)^2\mathbb L^3\), gives for each \([a:b]\) the class \(\mathbb L^{2d-2}[\mathbb P^{d-1}][\mathbb P^{2d-2}]\). This family trivializes Zariski locally on \(\mathbb P^1\), giving its claimed factor \(\mathbb L+1\).

Finally, Hilbert function \((1,1,1)\) yields cyclic length-three targets \(U\cong\mathbb C[t]/(t^3)\). After choosing \(t\), write \(x=at+bt^2\), \(y=ct+dt^2\), with \((a,c)\ne(0,0)\). Modulo changes \(t\mapsto ut+vt^2\), the isomorphism types have parameters a tangent direction \([a:c]\in\mathbb P^1\) and one affine curvature coordinate. The relation \(f\) holds precisely when \(f_3(a,c)=0\), since \(t^3=0\). There are therefore \(r\) allowed tangent directions and an \(\mathbb A^1\)-family over each. For each fixed target, the \(R\)-module maps \(E\to U\) are parameterized by \(d\) nonzero top coefficients, \(d\) arbitrary next coefficients and \(2d\) arbitrary final coefficients. Dividing by \(\operatorname{Aut}_R(U)\cong\mathbb G_m\ltimes\mathbb G_a^2\) gives \(\mathbb L^{3d-2}[\mathbb P^{d-1}]\) per curvature value. The resulting contribution is \(r\mathbb L^{3d-1}[\mathbb P^{d-1}]\). Automorphism torsors and curvature charts trivialize Zariski locally. All three Hilbert functions have now been exhausted, proving the formula.

## Verification
The accompanying pure-Python script verifies cancellation of the displayed denominator, checks the rank-one specialization symbolically for integers \(1\le r\le3\), and checks independent class-by-class finite-field counts at selected field sizes and framing ranks; its computations are checks of finite identities, not an independent proof of the motivic statement. As an additional direct enumeration, the length-three submodule count for \(d=1\), \(f_3=xy(x+y)\) over \(\mathbb F_2\), was \(19\), matching \(1+2+4(1+3)\). The proof itself uses algebraic stratification and locally trivial special-group torsors over \(\mathbb C\).

## Relationship to prior work
Huang and Jiang's 2023 work on punctual Quot schemes of the cusp (arXiv:2305.06411) and their 2023 motivic Quot-zeta treatment (arXiv:2312.12528) address cusp-type singularities through much deeper generating series. They do not imply this tangent-direction-sensitive all-rank formula for arbitrary multiplicity-three plane-curve germs. Huang, Jiang and Oblomkov (arXiv:2608.16086) study Quot schemes of modules over torus-knot singularities, in particular Quot of the normalization module, not this framing of all reduced plane triple-point germs. A previously determined rank-one multiplicity-three length-four classification and a multiplicity-at-least-three all-rank colength-two formula are both proper lower-rank or shorter-length specializations, not this result.

## Limitations
This is a motivic identity for the reduced underlying constructible Quot stratum in characteristic zero, not a claim of isomorphism of whole schemes or equivalence of nonreduced structures. It determines the class, not all singularities or cohomological cup products. The polynomial identity has finite-field analogues when the tangent roots are counted over the ground field, but the theorem as stated is over \(\mathbb C\). No independent audit has been performed, and unindexed related literature could still contain an equivalent result.
