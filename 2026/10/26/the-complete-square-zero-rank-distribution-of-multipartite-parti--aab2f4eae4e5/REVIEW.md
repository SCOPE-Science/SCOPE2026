# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The exact domain–image disjointness criterion for a square-zero partial injection, the classification of distance-preserving maps by injective assignments of active source parts to target parts, and the independent binomial choices of source subsets and destination subsets together give a complete combinatorial proof for every finite complete multipartite graph. The two possible assignments of bipartite parts produce the stated closed double sum with one empty-map correction. Feasible-domain inequalities in the two cases yield the exact maximum rank and the parity obstruction; rank restriction establishes all intermediate ranks. Independently, direct enumeration of disjoint domain/image bijections checked nineteen graph configurations and all coefficients.

## Originality — PASS within examined literature
The closest source, Fernandes–Paulista's finite-star partial-isometry article (arXiv:2203.05504), studies monoid structure, cardinality and generator rank, but does not state the complete square-zero element-rank distribution over arbitrary multipartite graphs. Its companion cycle article (arXiv:2205.02196) covers cycles rather than multipartite families. Searches for square-zero, nilpotent and partial-automorphism counts over complete multipartite graphs yielded no equivalent or stronger distribution in accessible examined sources. The graph \(K_{2,2}\) coincides with the four-cycle, and complete graphs reduce to standard partial injections: these are covered special cases, not claims of priority. Residual priority risk includes a possible reduction from general partial-automorphism semigroup enumeration or unpublished literature; a missing search hit cannot prove uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A square-zero rank profile is a natural structural statistic of a transformation inverse monoid. The theorem calculates every coefficient for a full infinite graph family and distinguishes a sharp obstruction to the unrestricted maximal rank that is invisible from vertex number alone. For instance, both \(K_{3,5}\) and \(K_{4,4}\) have eight vertices, but their largest attainable square-zero ranks are respectively \(3\) and \(4\). The multipartite formula is not merely one numerical instance or an arbitrary slice.
