# The complete two-colour wild-number spectrum of a connected graph

## Finding

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be any finite, connected, simple graph with \(n=|V|\ge3\) (hence at least two edges). For a partition \(\mathcal P\) of \(V\), write \(e_G(\mathcal P)\) for the number of edges whose endpoints lie in different parts, and define
\[
D(G)=\max_{\mathcal P}\bigl(2(|\mathcal P|-1)-e_G(\mathcal P)\bigr).
\]
Let \(b(G)\) be the number of edge blocks: equivalence classes of edges generated by the relation that two edges lie on the same simple cycle. In particular, each bridge is one edge block. Then, over all surjective red/blue edge-colourings,
\[
\left\{\operatorname{wld}_{\chi}(G):\chi:E\twoheadrightarrow\{\mathrm R,\mathrm B\}\right\}
=\{D(G),D(G)+1,\ldots,U(G)\},
\qquad
U(G)=\begin{cases}n-2,&b(G)=1,\\n-1,&b(G)\ge2.\end{cases}
\]
Every integer in this interval occurs. Moreover, the number of **labelled** surjective red/blue colourings attaining \(n-1\) is exactly \(2^{b(G)}-2\) when \(b(G)\ge2\), and is zero when \(b(G)=1\). The minimum is zero precisely when \(G\) contains two edge-disjoint spanning trees.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph is finite, connected, simple and has \(n\ge3\) vertices. No equitability or proper-edge-colouring condition is imposed: each colour must occur on at least one edge and may occur on adjacent edges. The wild number \(\operatorname{wld}_{\chi}(G)\) is the smallest size of an edge set made available to both colours so that a spanning tree exists in each colour. Component counts always include isolated vertices. The count \(2^{b(G)}-2\) distinguishes red from blue.

## Proof

**1. The component and rank identities.** Write \(E_{\mathrm R}=S\) and \(E_{\mathrm B}=E\setminus S\). For each \(X\subseteq E\), let \(\kappa(X)\) be the number of components of the spanning graph \((V,X)\), and let \(r(X)=n-\kappa(X)\) be its graphic-matroid rank. The two-colour component identity of Anders et al. gives
\[
\operatorname{wld}_{\chi}(G)=\kappa(S)+\kappa(E\setminus S)-2
=2(n-1)-r(S)-r(E\setminus S).
\tag{1}
\]
Because \(G\) is connected, \(r(E)=n-1\). Thus, with the connectivity function
\[
\lambda(S)=r(S)+r(E\setminus S)-r(E),
\]
Equation (1) becomes \(\operatorname{wld}_{\chi}(G)=n-1-\lambda(S)\). Since a basis of \(S\) together with one of \(E\setminus S\) spans \(E\), rank subadditivity yields \(\lambda(S)\ge0\).

**2. The exact minimum.** Let \(r_2(E)\) denote the maximum number of edges in a union of two edge-disjoint forests of \(G\). For any two-colouring, maximal forests within the two colour classes have total size \(r(S)+r(E\setminus S)\le r_2(E)\). Conversely, take two edge-disjoint forests whose union has size \(r_2(E)\) and assign them opposite colours. As \(G\) has at least two edges, \(r_2(E)\ge2\); if one forest is empty, move one edge from the other forest into it. Color the unassigned edges arbitrarily. The resulting surjective colouring satisfies \(r(S)+r(E\setminus S)\ge r_2(E)\), so equality holds. Therefore
\[
\min_{\chi}\operatorname{wld}_{\chi}(G)=2(n-1)-r_2(E).
\tag{2}
\]
The Edmonds–Nash-Williams matroid-union rank theorem, applied to two copies of the graphic matroid, states
\[
r_2(E)=\min_{A\subseteq E}\bigl(|E\setminus A|+2r(A)\bigr).
\]
Substitution into (2) gives
\[
\min_{\chi}\operatorname{wld}_{\chi}(G)
=\max_{A\subseteq E}\bigl(2(\kappa(A)-1)-|E\setminus A|\bigr).
\tag{3}
\]
To translate (3) into the partition formula, let \(\mathcal P_A\) be the connected-component partition of \((V,A)\). Every crossing edge of \(\mathcal P_A\) belongs to \(E\setminus A\), so the expression at \(A\) in (3) is at most \(2(|\mathcal P_A|-1)-e_G(\mathcal P_A)\). Conversely, for any partition \(\mathcal P\), take \(A\) to consist of all edges internal to its parts. Then \(\kappa(A)\ge|\mathcal P|\) and \(|E\setminus A|=e_G(\mathcal P)\). The expression at this \(A\) is at least the partition deficiency. Hence the two maxima are equal and the minimum is exactly \(D(G)\). The one-part partition gives \(D(G)\ge0\). The spanning-tree-packing criterion gives \(D(G)=0\) exactly when two edge-disjoint spanning trees exist.

**3. The exact maximum and its multiplicity.** Work over the binary cycle space \(Z(G)\). Its dimension is \(|E|-n+1\). For an edge subset \(X\), its monochromatic cycle subspace \(Z(X)\) has dimension \(|X|-r(X)\). The two monochromatic cycle spaces have disjoint edge supports; consequently
\[
\lambda(S)=\dim Z(G)-\dim Z(S)-\dim Z(E\setminus S).
\tag{4}
\]
If a simple cycle contains both colours, then the red edges of that cycle have odd degree at a vertex where its edge colours change. Its cycle vector therefore cannot be a sum of a red Eulerian cycle vector and a blue Eulerian cycle vector. Hence the inclusion \(Z(S)\oplus Z(E\setminus S)\subseteq Z(G)\) is proper and \(\lambda(S)>0\). Conversely, if every simple cycle is monochromatic, those cycles span \(Z(G)\), so (4) gives \(\lambda(S)=0\). Thus
\[
\operatorname{wld}_{\chi}(G)=n-1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{every simple cycle is monochromatic.}
\tag{5}
\]
The latter condition is equivalent to assigning a constant colour to each edge block. If \(b(G)\ge2\), there are exactly \(2^{b(G)}-2\) surjective assignments to blocks, proving the maximum \(n-1\) and the multiplicity. If \(b(G)=1\), a surjective colouring cannot make all cycles monochromatic; thus the maximum is at most \(n-2\). In this case no edge is a bridge: deleting one edge \(e\) leaves \(G-e\) connected. Colour just \(e\) red, and everything else blue. The red spanning subgraph has \(n-1\) components and the blue one has one, so (1) yields \(n-2\). This proves the upper endpoint.

**4. There are no gaps.** The graph of all nonempty proper subsets of the \(m=|E|\) edges, with adjacency given by a single-edge change, is connected when \(m\ge3\). Indeed, one can move any nonempty proper subset to any other without passing through the empty or full set (keep a witness element when adding or deleting). Each such move changes one graphic rank by either zero or one and the other rank by zero or one in the opposite direction. Equation (1) therefore changes by at most one. A path from a colouring attaining \(D(G)\) to one attaining \(U(G)\) must pass through every intermediate integer. When \(m=2\), connected simplicity forces \(G\) to be the three-vertex path: its two surjective colourings have the same wild number \(2\), so the asserted interval is still correct.

Together, steps 1–4 establish the theorem and the exact maximum-colouring count.

## Verification

The standalone `verify_wild_spectrum.py` computes components directly from edge lists, enumerates every surjective colouring and every vertex partition, independently tests two-vertex-connectivity, and obtains edge blocks by unioning edges of each simple cycle. It exhaustively checks all connected labelled simple graphs of orders \(3,4,5\), respectively \(4,38,728\) graphs, plus five deliberately varied six-vertex graphs. It compares the entire observed wild spectrum, both predicted endpoints, and the number of colourings attaining \(n-1\). The output is stored verbatim in `verify_wild_spectrum_output.txt` and ends with `VERIFY_OK`. Exhaustion at finite orders corroborates but does not establish the universal claim; the proof above establishes all orders.

## Relationship to prior work

Anders, Foster-Greenwood, Garcia and Krawzik introduce the wild number and prove the two-colour component formula (Theorem 6.4(2)), and develop results for trees, cycles and coloured families. Dawkins, Nappo, Tong and Zimbizi investigate complete and complete bipartite graphs, proving the complete-graph unrestricted two-colour spectrum (Theorem 3.3) and studying equitable and proper colourings. The minimum formula here uses the classical graphic-matroid union theorem, and its zero case is the classical two-spanning-tree packing criterion. Those ingredients are credited rather than claimed as new. The contribution assessed here is their complete all-connected-graph synthesis: both endpoints, the block dichotomy and exact extremal-colouring count, and gap-free attainability for every graph without any density or connectivity restriction. An earlier case-specific result determines the unrestricted two-colour spectrum for complete bipartite graphs; that is subsumed as a special case and is not counted as independent supporting novelty.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns surjective colourings with **exactly two** colours; it does not classify spectra of proper or equitable colourings, nor three or more colours. The proof relies on the established two-colour wild identity and the standard matroid-union rank theorem. No priority claim is made for those classical inputs or for an individual extremal corollary implied by them. An equivalent all-graph formulation under matroid-connectivity terminology may exist outside the inspected bibliography and indexed records; independent literature review and mathematical auditing remain to be performed.
