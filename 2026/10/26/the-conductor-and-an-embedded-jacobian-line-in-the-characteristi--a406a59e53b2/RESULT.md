# The conductor and an embedded Jacobian line in the characteristic-two binary-quartic discriminant

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(2\), and consider the projective space of binary quartics
\[
f(X,Y)=aX^4+bX^3Y+cX^2Y^2+dXY^3+eY^4,
\qquad [a:b:c:d:e]\in\mathbb P^4_k.
\]
Put \(Q=ad^2+bcd+eb^2\). The classical integral discriminant reduces to \(Q^2\) modulo \(2\) (the squareness phenomenon is known). The *reduced* discriminant \(X=V(Q)\subset\mathbb P^4\) is an integral, nonnormal cubic threefold whose reduced singular locus is the plane \(P=V(b,d)\simeq\mathbb P^2\). Its intrinsic Jacobian ideal is exactly
\[
J_X=(d^2,cd,bd,bc,b^2)=(b,d)(b,c,d)=(b,d)\cap(b,c,d)^2.
\]
Thus the Jacobian scheme has an embedded line \(L=V(b,c,d)\simeq\mathbb P^1\) inside its reduced singular plane; its Hilbert function in degree \(n\geq0\) is
\[
h(n)=\binom{n+2}{2}+2n,
\qquad H_{k[a,b,c,d,e]/J_X}(T)=\frac{1+2T-2T^2}{(1-T)^3}.
\]
The normalization is the finite birational morphism
\[
\nu:\mathbb P^1_{[u:v]}\times\mathbb P^2_{[A:B:C]}\longrightarrow X,
\qquad ([u:v],[A:B:C])\longmapsto[uB:uA:vB+uC:vA:vC].
\]
Its conductor on \(X\) is exactly the ideal \(\mathcal I_{P/X}=(b,d)\). The normalization quotient is the invertible sheaf on \(P\)
\[
\nu_*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^2}/\mathcal O_X\cong i_*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^2}(-1),\quad i:P\hookrightarrow X.
\]
The inverse image of the conductor plane is a finite flat double cover defined by \(av^2+cuv+eu^2=0\) inside \(P\times\mathbb P^1\), generically separable, with non-étale locus supported over \(c=0\). Conversely, the *usual marked-double-root incidence* maps generically purely inseparably with degree \(2\) onto \(X\), and is **not** this normalization.

## Assumptions and scope
All geometry is over an algebraically closed field of characteristic exactly \(2\). The coordinate names \(a,b,c,d,e\) denote degree-one homogeneous coordinates on \(\mathbb P^4\), and the Jacobian singular scheme refers to the reduced hypersurface \(Q=0\), not to the nonreduced double scheme defined by the specialized integral discriminant \(Q^2\). A conductor is the largest ideal in both \(\mathcal O_X\) and its normalization sheaf. Schemes and ideal equalities are scheme-theoretic; the associated Hilbert formula applies to every \(n\geq0\).

## Proof
**Discriminant and reduced cubic.** The discriminant of the generic integral binary quartic, calculated as the polynomial discriminant of \(f(X,1)\), reduces modulo \(2\) to \((ad^2+bcd+eb^2)^2\). This also follows from the general characteristic-two squareness theorem for discriminants. The polynomial \(Q\) is linear in \(a\), with leading coefficient \(d^2\) relatively prime to \(bcd+eb^2\) in \(k[b,c,d,e]\). Gauss's lemma shows \(Q\) is irreducible.

**Intrinsic Jacobian scheme.** Direct differentiation in characteristic \(2\) gives \((\partial_aQ,\partial_bQ,\partial_cQ,\partial_dQ,\partial_eQ)=(d^2,cd,bd,bc,b^2)\). Euler's homogeneous identity shows \(Q\) belongs to this ideal, since its degree is odd. Monomial inspection proves the displayed product and intersection equalities; the radical is \((b,d)\), and \((b,c,d)\) is its unique embedded associated prime. Modulo \(J_X\), the nonzero monomials are precisely those in \(a,c,e\), together with \(b\) or \(d\) multiplied by arbitrary monomials in \(a,e\). Hence the degree-\(n\) dimension is \(\binom{n+2}{2}+2n\), including \(n=0\); summing the series yields the asserted rational expression.

**Normalization.** Substituting \((a,b,c,d,e)=(uB,uA,vB+uC,vA,vC)\) makes \(Q\) vanish identically. For a point of \(X\) outside \(P\), the relation \(bv=du\) uniquely determines \([u:v]=[b:d]\). The incidence equations are
\[
bv-du=0,\qquad av^2+cuv+eu^2=0.
\]
For a fixed \([u:v]\), these equations are two independent linear forms in the five coefficients, and their kernel is a three-dimensional vector space. The displayed bilinear parametrization identifies the resulting projective plane bundle with \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^2\). Above \(P\), the remaining quadratic equation is nonzero at each projective \([a:c:e]\), so every fiber is a finite length-two scheme. Consequently the proper incidence morphism is quasi-finite, therefore finite. It is birational over \(X\setminus P\), and its smooth source is normal. It is the normalization.

**Conductor and quotient.** On the affine chart \(e=1\), the normalization has coordinates \(c,d,t\), with
\[
a=t^2+ct,\qquad b=dt.
\]
The normalized ring is \(B=k[c,d,t]\) and the original ring is \(A=k[a,b,c,d]/(ad^2+bcd+b^2)\). As an \(A\)-module, \(B/A\) is generated by the class of \(t\). The relations \(dt=b\) and \(bt=ad+bc\) show \((b,d)\) annihilates it. On \(b=d=0\), the monic relation \(t^2+ct+a=0\) shows \(1,t\) form a basis over \(k[a,c]\); hence the class of \(t\) has *exactly* annihilator \((b,d)\). On the complementary chart \(a=1\), the same calculation uses \(s=v/u\) and \(e=s^2+cs,\ d=bs\). On the remaining chart \(c\ne0\) of the conductor plane, the quadratic \(av^2+cuv+eu^2=0\) has nonvanishing differential along each geometric fiber; the finite cover is locally étale, and the same quotient is locally free of rank one. Thus the global quotient is a line bundle on \(P\), annihilated precisely by its ideal. The homogeneous normalization is the full Segre coordinate ring with six bilinear generators. Its missing degree-one generator is \(w=vB\), satisfying \(w^2+cw+ae=0\), \(bw=ad\), \(dw=cd+be\). These identities show that the graded quotient is \(k[a,c,e](-1)\) as a graded module. Sheafifying gives \(i_*\mathcal O_P(-1)\), while the exact annihilator makes \((b,d)\) the conductor on \(X\).

**Conductor cover and the usual root incidence.** Over \(P\), the normalization's incidence equation is \(av^2+cuv+eu^2=0\), a family of nonzero binary quadratics and hence finite flat of degree two. On \(c\ne0\) it is étale, while the non-étale locus lies over \(c=0\). For the usual marked-double-root incidence, differentiating \(f(x,1)\) gives \(f'(x)=bx^2+d\). On the generic point of \(X\), \(x^2=d/b\). On the affine chart \(e=1\), the function field of \(X\) is \(k(c,d,t)\), with \(b=dt\), and \(d/b=1/t\) is not a square in that rational function field. The marked-root incidence therefore induces a purely inseparable extension of degree two, unlike the finite birational normalization.

## Relationship to prior work
Chipalkatti's 2001 account describes coincident-root loci in characteristic zero. Kurmann's 2011 normalization and singularity results also explicitly assume characteristic zero. Bhargava--Shankar discuss the integral binary-quartic invariants and discriminant. The fact that characteristic-two discriminants are squares is independently established in general, e.g. Dolgachev--Kond\=o; this finding does not claim that known fact as novel. The claim here is the explicit conductor, the nonreduced embedded Jacobian line *of the reduced cubic*, and the contrast between its normalization and the inseparable root-incidence map. Equivalent accounts under Segre-projection or modular invariant-theory language remain possible.

## Limitations
The result does not characterize all integral moduli models or arbitrary perfect/nonperfect base fields. It is not a claim of independent validation. The nonreduced hypersurface \(Q^2=0\) has identically vanishing ordinary Jacobian derivatives, and must not be conflated with the reduced discriminant cubic. The claimed originality is conditional on the inspected literature and may be limited by equivalent presentations in classic nonnormal cubic-threefold literature.
