# Review

## Claim

For \(1\le p<\infty\), on \(P=\{0\}\cup\bigcup_{n\ge1}[2^{-2n-1},2^{-2n}]\) with weighted lattice norm \(\|f\|_{X_\alpha}^p=\sum_n\int_{I_n}|f(x)|^p x^{-\alpha}\,dx\), every element of \(N^1X_\alpha(P)\) has a continuous representative exactly when \(\alpha\ge1\).

## Correctness: PASS

On each interval, the upper-gradient inequality supplies a continuous representative. For \(\alpha\ge1\), the oscillation estimate tends to zero, while every component has weighted mass bounded below; finite weighted norm therefore forces the component values to tend to zero. For \(\alpha<1\), the component masses are summable and alternating constants give a finite-norm function with upper gradient zero but no continuous representative. The proof treats \(p=1\) separately and includes the endpoint \(\alpha=1\).

## Originality: PASS

The closest inspected primary source is Björn–Björn–Malý, arXiv:2503.21665v1, Example 7.2(a), which treats the critical weight \(\alpha=1\) on this geometry. It does not state the all-real-\(\alpha\) classification or the explicit subcritical obstruction. published-finding corpus searches returned a power-weighted Campanato interval result, but that concerns a different space and invariant. Search coverage is not exhaustive; an unindexed treatment of this exact family remains a residual risk.

## Value: PASS

The result gives a complete, sharp parameter threshold for a natural model in which upper gradients cannot connect the components. It separates the existence of a continuous representative from weaker quasicontinuity notions and shows that the critical exponent is independent of \(p\). The subcritical counterexample is explicit and explains necessity rather than relying only on a positive estimate.

## Limitations

The conclusion is specific to the stated interval family and power weights. No broader classification of metric spaces or capacities is claimed.
