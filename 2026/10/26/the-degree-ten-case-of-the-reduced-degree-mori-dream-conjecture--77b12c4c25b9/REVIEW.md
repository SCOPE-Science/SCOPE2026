# Review

## Correctness

The proof reduces the degree-ten case to a finite partition of the counts \(2,\ldots,9\). The included integer-only checker exhausts all 163 partitions up to left/right reflection and retains exactly three shapes. The two nonempty shapes are contradicted by the displayed floor/ceiling equations. The remaining shape is precisely the González–Karu configuration, for which the cited theorem gives non-finite generation of the Cox ring and hence failure of the Mori dream property under the stated hypotheses.

## Originality

He's published theorem covers \(d'\le9\) and formulates the conjecture beyond that range. The present claim is the next case, \(d'=10\), and is not a restatement of the general conjecture: the new content is the exhaustive elimination of all degree-ten column shapes except the already-known González–Karu family. Searches of the published literature and the closest retrieved mathematical records found related work on toric surfaces and weighted-projective-plane blow-ups, but no statement covering this exact all-slope degree-ten case. The later weighted-plane extremal-curve paper was only abstract-read and remains a limited residual risk for that subclass.

## Value

The degree-ten case is the first boundary value not covered by He's finite verification through degree nine. Resolving it tests the next exact case of a stated conjecture and rules out two otherwise admissible combinatorial patterns, rather than merely recomputing an example or a known table entry.
