# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The q−1 nonbottom alpha-cut relations are reflexive crisp preorders exactly when the fuzzy relation is max-min transitive. Monotonicity of cuts yields a bijection with weakly nested chains of length q−1 in the finite preorder lattice. Reducing to strict chains gives F4(q)=Σ_{k=0}^9 a_k binom(q−1,k); a0..a9=[1,354,8331,63730,235344,488760,604752,444192,179328,30720]. An independent fresh enumerator checks all 2^12=4096 directed off-diagonal masks and all 64 transitivity triples, finds 355 reflexive preorders, then counts every strict chain by its immediate-subset recurrence and matches all coefficients, q=1..5 and exact polynomial arithmetic. Rank function comparability-count +4−equivalence-class-count bounds strict chain length by 9, so exhaustive finite enumeration and binomial polynomial hold for every q, not merely tested q.

## originality

PASS

The classical threshold-cut equivalence and finite preorder chain-height theory are acknowledged prior methods. Moerman 2022 proves longest-chain lengths but not number of each chain length on four labelled points. Li–Yu–Liu 2025 obtain probability distributions and expected values for transitive closures of random matrices, not the probability that an input is already a fixed point. Targeted direct exact-coefficient and database searches did not find the ten strict-chain counts or F4(q) polynomial. A separately tabulated multichain order polynomial remains a priority risk.

## value

PASS

The labelled four-world model is the first nontrivial compact finite system with a rich preorder lattice, and independent membership levels are natural in Gödel/fuzzy-accessibility models. An exact all-q acceptance polynomial and sharp q^−3 probability provide a reusable finite-poset invariant and sampling baseline, beyond a fixed q numerical exercise; no broad n-world formula is claimed.
