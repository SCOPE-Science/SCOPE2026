# Scientific review: exact single-substring-edit ball spectrum

## Correctness: PASS
The edit definition is the same-position replacement of one block of at most \(k\) input letters by at most \(k\) arbitrary letters. The proof explicitly separates output lengths; identifies the maximal fixed replacement window in each length; counts positive-insertion outputs by their rightmost feasible window; and handles pure length-\(k\) deletion by the classical period-\(k\) run decomposition. The residue-class path argument proves the exact joint distribution of equality indicators, including \(n=k\), every \(q\ge2\), and all integer \(k\ge1\). The mean, variance, extrema and central limit statement then follow. The standalone exhaustive checker reconstructed all edit sets and their full histograms for 44 parameter triples (17,413 centers); finite tests are corroboration, not the infinite proof.

## Originality: PASS, with residual risk
The 2026 motivating paper treats the same general error channel but emphasizes code constructions, code-size bounds and localization; its inspected formal model and main results do not present the complete received-length ball census or the binomial population theorem. Lu–Zhang's earlier binary fixed-pair burst balls are exactly relevant stronger **within a fixed pair**; their center-independence is explicitly recognized rather than misattributed. Xi–Sun–Ge state the already known pure-burst deletion cardinality and analyze a broader yet different channel allowing separated insertion and deletion sites. Neither fixed-pair nor separated-site results supplies the stated entire at-most-\(k\), all-length same-site union and its population law directly. Search of available mathematical records and source/parameter/alias variations did not identify an equivalent statement. These are concrete best-knowledge comparisons, not proof of global uniqueness; an unindexed or hidden equivalent remains possible.

## Value: PASS
The exact size distribution of a channel error ball is a natural uncertainty/packing diagnostic. The recent substring-edit model treats many removal/insertion lengths simultaneously, so adding fixed-pair cardinalities would overcount across edit descriptions. The maximal-window reduction resolves that overlap and shows a striking qualitative split: all shells with nonempty insertion are rigid, while the **only** source-dependent component has a binomial law. The result gives all exact frequencies and sharp extremizers over every alphabet and all finite lengths, as well as the variance scaling; these finite-length channel statistics are not a cosmetic substitution of a known fixed-pair formula. No improved code design or decoding efficiency is claimed.
