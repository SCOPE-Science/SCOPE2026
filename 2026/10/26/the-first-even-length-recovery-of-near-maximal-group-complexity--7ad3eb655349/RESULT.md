# The first even-length recovery of near-maximal group complexity in the Thue–Morse word

## Finding
Let \(t\) be the infinite Thue–Morse word. Write \(F_n(t)\) for its distinct factors of length \(n\), and \(p_t(n)=|F_n(t)|\). For \(G\leq S_n\), define \(p_t^G(n)\) to be the number of equivalence classes of \(F_n(t)\), where two factors are equivalent when one is obtained from the other by some coordinate permutation belonging to \(G\).

The *next-to-largest* value \(p_t(n)-1\) is attainable at even length \(n=2\), is **unattainable** at both \(n=4\) and \(n=6\), and is **attainable again** at \(n=8\). More precisely:

\[
p_t(4)=10,\qquad p_t(6)=16,\qquad p_t(8)=22.
\]
No subgroup of \(S_4\) gives \(p_t^G(4)=9\), and no subgroup of \(S_6\) gives \(p_t^G(6)=15\). But the order-two subgroup generated by the coordinate permutation

\[
\sigma=(1,0,7,4,3,6,5,2)
\]
(in zero-based one-line notation) yields \(p_t^{\langle\sigma\rangle}(8)=21\). This is the first even-length recovery after the two forbidden even lengths.

## Assumptions and scope
The fixed point starts in \(0\) and is defined using \(\mu(0)=01\) and \(\mu(1)=10\). Coordinate permutations act on words of length \(n\) as \(\sigma\cdot w=w_{\sigma(0)}\cdots w_{\sigma(n-1)}\), using zero-based subscripts. Equivalence is measured in the entire binary-word space before restriction to the factor set; a group orbit is counted if it intersects \(F_n(t)\). Thus group elements are not required to preserve the factor language.

The finite classification is restricted to the even lengths \(2,4,6,8\). It makes no claim for lengths greater than \(8\), arbitrary binary words, or all even lengths.

## Proof
**Exact factor extraction.** All four binary factors of length two occur in \(t\). Because \(t=\mu^5(t)\), the infinite word partitions into successive blocks \(\mu^5(a)\) of length \(32\). Every factor of length \(n\leq8\) is contained in the concatenation of two consecutive blocks. Consequently, its exact factor set is obtained by scanning the four finite words \(\mu^5(ab)\) for \(a,b\in\{0,1\}\). Each of those four block concatenations occurs in \(t\), so the extraction has no spurious factors. The bundled checker calculates \(p_t(4)=10\), \(p_t(6)=16\), and \(p_t(8)=22\) in this mathematically exhaustive manner, then cross-checks all sets against a length-\(4096\) prefix. The prefix cross-check is only a consistency test; exhaustive validity follows from the two-block argument.

**Reduction to individual permutations.** Let \(F\) be a finite set of words of the same length with cardinality \(m\). If a coordinate-permutation subgroup \(G\) has exactly \(m-1\) distinct orbits intersecting \(F\), then its partition of \(F\) consists of exactly one two-element block \(\{u,v\}\) and \(m-2\) singletons. Some \(\tau\in G\) sends \(u\) to \(v\). The cyclic subgroup \(\langle\tau\rangle\) cannot merge any different pair in \(F\), since its orbits refine the \(G\)-orbit partition, and it does merge \(u,v\). Thus \(\langle\tau\rangle\) already realizes \(m-1\) classes. Contrapositively, to rule out \(m-1\) for all groups, it suffices to check the cyclic subgroup generated by **each individual coordinate permutation**.

**The forbidden lengths.** The checker enumerates all \(4!=24\) permutations at length four and all \(6!=720\) permutations at length six, computing the orbit partition on the *ambient* binary words and then intersecting with the exact factor set. None of the \(744\) cyclic subgroups tested produces the next-to-largest orbit count. The reduction lemma consequently rules out **every** subgroup of \(S_4\) and \(S_6\), not merely those generated by one permutation.

**The recovery at eight.** Direct substitution shows \(\sigma^2\) is the identity, and among the \(22\) factors of length eight the only two distinct factors mapped to one another by \(\sigma\) are

\[
01001011\quad\text{and}\quad10110100.
\]
Every other factor maps either to itself or to a word outside \(F_8(t)\). Hence exactly one pair coalesces and the other \(20\) factors remain singleton intersections; therefore \(p_t^{\langle\sigma\rangle}(8)=21\). At length two, swapping the two coordinates joins only \(01\) and \(10\), attaining \(4-1=3\). This establishes the first recovery at \(8\) after the gaps at \(4\) and \(6\).

The finite computations are an exhaustive certificate **only because** the proof establishes exact factor extraction and the cyclic-subgroup reduction.

## Relationship to prior work
Launer, Puzynina, and Voloshinova study the set of values obtainable by permutation-group complexity for an infinite word, including a known odd-length parity obstruction for the Thue–Morse word. The present statement concerns a different, finite even-length phenomenon: the nonattainment of the next-to-largest value at two consecutive even lengths and its explicit recovery at the next even length. The prior odd-length obstruction by itself neither excludes the value at length six nor provides the permutation at length eight. Generalized Morse–Hedlund results of Charlier, Puzynina, and Zamboni provide contextual lower bounds and definitions rather than this finite next-to-largest spectrum.

## Limitations
The result proves **no** general asymptotic assertion, monotonicity statement, or classification beyond the displayed finite lengths. It is not an independent audit. The complete text of the most relevant 2026 primary manuscript could not be examined during this literature comparison; therefore an equivalent statement hidden in that work or unindexed literature remains a meaningful priority risk. The impossibility claims do not rest on an experimental sampling cutoff: all required permutations and all exact factor sets at the stated lengths are exhausted.
