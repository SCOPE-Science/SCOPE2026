**Correctness — PASS.** The sharp operator seminorm reduces by Markov-kernel duality to a total-variation integral. The fourth-order difference factors into an explicit quadratic in the cosine coordinate. Its sign-changing roots partition the entire parameter range into three exact rational branches. Differentiation, a unique quintic-root sign argument, and four exact Bernstein-basis positivity/negativity certificates establish the unique global maximum; order two and three are also checked. `CHECK.py` validates rational identities, sign certificates and independent numerical quadrature. Numerical integration alone is not used as a global proof.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The closest general result is Gaubert–Qu's abstract Dobrushin coefficient, which yields the variational reduction but not the explicit fourth-Fejér optimum or angular symmetry-breaking. Weisz's 2012 full survey gives Fejér kernels and approximation theory but not a sharp translation total-variation constant. Targeted literature and mathematical-record searches of aliases and exact claims found no dominating evaluated result. An unindexed prior equivalent may remain; no claim of exhaustive novelty is made.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The exact contraction constant is the optimal one-step stability factor on the quotient of real continuous circle functions by constants. The first non-antipodal maximizer is a structural boundary: the worst pair changes geometry at the first Fejér kernel with three positive Fourier frequencies. The quintic exact answer, uniqueness, and complete three-branch certification are not a restatement of the general Dobrushin formula. These bounds are directly relevant to sharp error propagation of positive Fourier means.

**Scientific limitations.** Scope is strictly the real one-step oscillation problem for Fejér orders two through four; no extension to larger orders or repeated iterates. The continuous-function operator norm is a supremum; a measurable sign witness may not be continuous. The possible presence of unindexed earlier evaluations is the principal originality risk.
