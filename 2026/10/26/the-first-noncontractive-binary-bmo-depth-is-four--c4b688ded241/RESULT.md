# The first noncontractive binary BMO depth is four

## Finding

For a complete rooted binary tree of depth three and every real terminal datum \(x=(x_0,\ldots,x_7)\), define its mean-centered local \(L^1\)-BMO seminorm by
\[
F_3(x)=\max_{I\in\mathcal T_{<3}}\frac1{|I|}\sum_{i\in I}|x_i-\bar x_I|,\qquad \bar x_I=\frac1{|I|}\sum_{i\in I}x_i,
\]
where \(I\) ranges over the root, the two four-leaf children and the four two-leaf grandchildren. Then
\[
\boxed{F_3(|x|)\le F_3(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb R^8.}
\]
The constant is exactly \(1\), as any nonconstant nonnegative datum gives equality. In the standard notation for the folding constant, \(c_3(2)=1\). Combined with the known contractivity for depths one and two and the checked 16-leaf counterexample at depth four, **the first noncontractive binary depth is exactly four**.

## Assumptions and scope

Every split has two children of equal relative measure, and functions are real-valued and constant on the eight depth-three leaves. The oscillation is relative to the *arithmetic mean* on each node, with exponent \(1\), not median-centered BMO or the \(L^2\) oscillation norm. Depth zero has zero seminorm. The tree may be placed as a subtree of any homogeneous binary filtration, but the claim is about functions of depth at most three on a fixed root, not an unrestricted infinite-depth function.

## Proof

**1. Reduction to root oscillation.** For a two-leaf node, taking absolute values cannot increase oscillation, by the reverse triangle inequality. For a four-leaf node the sharp equal-atom folding inequality (Theorem 4.1 of Mohajer, reference [1]) has constant \(\max\{1,2-4/4\}=1\). Thus only the eight-leaf root inequality requires proof. By positive homogeneity we may assume \(F_3(x)\le1\); the zero case is immediate.

**2. Exact finite linearization.** Label the terminal leaves \(0,\ldots,7\) in left-to-right order and let \(\mathcal N\) be the seven internal node-index sets
\[
\{0,\ldots,7\},\quad\{0,\ldots,3\},\quad\{4,\ldots,7\},\quad\{0,1\},\{2,3\},\{4,5\},\{6,7\}.
\]
Choose two signs per leaf: \(\varepsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}\) with \(\varepsilon_i x_i=|x_i|\), and \(s_i\in\{-1,1\}\) for the dual formula for the mean absolute deviation. Put \(S=\sum_i s_i\) and \(C_i=\varepsilon_i(8s_i-S)\). Then
\[
64D_{\mathrm{root}}(|x|)=\max_{s\in\{-1,1\}^8}\sum_{i=0}^7 C_i x_i.
\]
Here \(D_I(x)=|I|^{-1}\sum_{i\in I}|x_i-\bar x_I|\).

For each \(I\in\mathcal N\) of cardinality \(m\) and each \(i\in I\), introduce a nonnegative auxiliary variable \(u_{I,i}\). The constraints \(D_I(x)\le1\) are *equivalent* to the existence of these variables satisfying, for both \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
 m\sigma x_i-\sigma\sum_{j\in I}x_j-mu_{I,i}\le0,\qquad
 \sum_{i\in I}u_{I,i}\le m.
\]
Add \(-\varepsilon_i x_i\le0\) for each leaf. These form an integer system \(A(x,u)\le b\) with 63 inequalities and 32 variables: eight unrestricted \(x\)'s and 24 nonnegative \(u\)'s.

For each sign pair \((\varepsilon,s)\), the needed assertion is that the linear objective \(\sum_iC_ix_i\) is at most \(64\) on this polyhedron. It is enough to exhibit a dual vector \(y\ge0\) such that
\[
(A^Ty)_{x_i}=C_i,\quad (A^Ty)_{u_{I,i}}\ge0,
\quad b^Ty\le64.
\]
Indeed \(C\cdot x\le y^TA(x,u)\le y^Tb\le64\) for every feasible \((x,u)\), because \(u\ge0\).

**3. Exhaustion modulo tree symmetry.** Each leaf is colored by the ordered pair \((\varepsilon_i,s_i)\), giving four colors. The full automorphism group of the complete rooted binary tree is generated by independent swaps of the two children at each internal vertex, and leaves the constraints and objective invariant. Canonical subtrees are unordered pairs of canonical child types. Their counts at heights \(0,1,2,3\) obey \(T_0=4\) and \(T_{k+1}=T_k(T_k+1)/2\), hence are \(4,10,55,1540\). Therefore exactly 1540 simultaneous sign-pair orbits exhaust the \(4^8\) original possibilities.

The companion file `DUAL_CERTIFICATES.json` supplies a sparse *rational* dual vector for every one of these 1540 representatives. The independent standard-library script `VERIFY.py` reconstructs all representatives and the entire integer constraint matrix from these definitions, then verifies each displayed dual equality, nonnegative constraint and upper objective bound using exact fractions. Every case satisfies \(b^Ty\le64\) (the largest certified value is exactly \(64\)). This proves the root inequality and therefore \(F_3(|x|)\le F_3(x)\).

**4. First failure at depth four.** For the 16 leaves, the datum
\[
(0,-8,-12,-12,\ 0,0,0,0,\ 6,1,1,1,\ 1,10,14,14)
\]
has \(F_4(x)=5\) and \(F_4(|x|)=41/8\), as a direct rational node-by-node computation confirms. This depth-four example was already given by Mohajer [1]; it is used here only to translate the new depth-three result into the first-failure classification. Thus depths one, two and three are contractive, whereas depth four is not.

Save the two attached files in the same directory and run `python3 VERIFY.py DUAL_CERTIFICATES.json`. The verifier uses no floating-point calculations, linear-programming packages or network access; it checks all 1540 orbit representatives against integer matrices and rational dual vectors. Its output is `PASS: 1540 orbit-complete cases; all exact dual equalities and bounds; largest scaled upper bound 64.` The source's depth-four datum can be independently checked by averaging on the root and its 14 proper nonterminal descendants; its exact norm ratio is \(41/40\). The computed dual witnesses are an exact finite proof, not a numerical extrapolation to other depths.

## Relationship to prior work

Mohajer [1], Section 4.2, proves the equal-atom folding bound and records \(c_1(2)=c_2(2)=1\); Section 4.4 constructs a depth-four failure; Section 6 explicitly lists the unknown \(c_3(2)\) and the least noncontractive depth as an open problem. The present exact depth-three inequality closes that *specific* gap. Slavin and Vasyunin [2] establish sharp comparisons of first and second BMO oscillation norms via Bellman functions, but those comparisons do not settle the nonlinear eight-leaf folding inequality. Dafni and Gibara [3] treat absolute value and rearrangement inequalities across averaging families but do not evaluate this rooted depth-three constant. The theorem concerns neither the classical interval BMO contractivity nor an \(L^2\)-BMO claim.

## Limitations

The proof of the eight-leaf bound is finite, computer-assisted and certificate-based rather than a short symbolic classification of extremizers. The certificate is fully supplied, independently rationally checkable, and exhaustive **only for depth three**. No exact value of \(c_4(2)\), of the global constant \(c(2)\), or of other arities is asserted. A mathematically equivalent earlier, presently unindexed finite-depth result cannot be ruled out solely by database search.
