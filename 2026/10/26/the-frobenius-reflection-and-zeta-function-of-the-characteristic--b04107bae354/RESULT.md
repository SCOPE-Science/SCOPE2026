# The Frobenius reflection and zeta function of the characteristic-three Clebsch cubic

## Finding
Let \(e_i\) denote the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree \(i\) in five variables, and define over \(\mathbb F_3\) the projective surface
\[
X=V(e_1,e_3)\subset\mathbb P^4_{\mathbb F_3}.
\]
Then \(X\) is a smooth cubic surface. Its 27 geometric lines consist of 15 lines defined over \(\mathbb F_3\) and six pairs of lines defined over \(\mathbb F_9\) and exchanged by Frobenius. Frobenius acts on the geometric Picard group with characteristic polynomial
\[
(T-1)^6(T+1).
\]
For every integer \(n\geq 1\),
\[
\#X(\mathbb F_{3^n})=1+3^{2n}+3^n\bigl(6+(-1)^n\bigr),
\qquad
Z(X/\mathbb F_3,T)=\frac{1}{(1-T)(1-9T)(1-3T)^6(1+3T)}.
\]
In particular, \(\#X(\mathbb F_3)=25\) and \(\#X(\mathbb F_9)=145\). The Frobenius class on the \(E_6\) root lattice is the reflection class \(A_1\).

## Assumptions and scope
The base scheme is the explicit elementary-symmetric model \(V(e_1,e_3)\), not the naive reduction of \(V(e_1,\sum_i x_i^3)\). Newton's identity gives \(\sum_i x_i^3=e_1^3-3e_1e_2+3e_3\); after reduction modulo three the naive equations define the whole hyperplane \(V(e_1)\). The primitive, torsion-free cubic \(e_3\) retains the intended surface. The formulas describe geometric lines, finite-field rational points, the geometric Picard group, and the Hasse–Weil zeta function of this specific smooth surface.

## Proof
**Smoothness.** On the hyperplane \(e_1=0\), differentiation of \(e_3\) gives \(\partial e_3/\partial x_i=e_2+x_i^2\). A projective singular point would have \(x_i^2=x_j^2\) for all \(i,j\). Since the point is nonzero, rescaling over an algebraic closure makes every coordinate \(+1\) or \(-1\). The equation \(e_1=0\) in characteristic three forces multiplicities \((4,1)\) or \((1,4)\). For the first pattern, the degree-three coefficient of \((1+t)^4(1-t)\), hence \(e_3\), equals \(-2=1\) in \(\mathbb F_3\); the other has opposite nonzero value. Thus \(X\) is smooth.

**Lines and the Frobenius splitting.** Fifteen lines arise by permuting the two-dimensional span of
\[
(1,-1,0,0,0),\quad (0,0,1,-1,0).
\]
To specify the remaining twelve, write \(\mathbb F_9=\mathbb F_3[s]/(s^2+s-1)\), and put
\[
u=(2,s,s^2-2,s^2-2,s),\qquad d=(0,1,s,-s,-1).
\]
Permute the five coordinates of \(\langle u,d\rangle\). Each line lies on \(e_1=e_3=0\): its two generating vectors satisfy \(e_1=0\), and the cubic \(e_3(u+td)\) vanishes for every \(t\in\mathbb F_9\) as well as at \(t=\infty\); since this polynomial has degree at most three, this is an exact polynomial identity. Row reduction over \(\mathbb F_9\) of all \(120\) permutations, as implemented in the standalone finite checker, gives exactly twelve distinct such lines, disjoint from the fifteen coordinate-difference lines. The same exhaustive finite check proves that no one of these twelve is fixed by the cubing Frobenius and that they form six conjugate pairs. All fifteen coordinate-difference lines are Frobenius fixed. By the theorem of 27 lines on a smooth cubic surface, these 27 distinct lines are all geometric lines. This enumeration is an exhaustive finite calculation, not an inference from partial sampling.

**The initial rational-point count.** A nonzero vector over \(\mathbb F_3\) has \(a\) coordinates equal to \(1\), \(b\) equal to \(-1\), and the remaining coordinates zero. Requiring \(e_1=e_3=0\) forces \((a,b)=(1,1)\) or \((2,2)\): the additional possibilities allowed by \(e_1=0\), namely \((3,0),(0,3),(4,1),(1,4)\), have \(e_3\ne0\). There are \(5\cdot4=20\) vectors of the first kind and \(\binom52\binom32=30\) of the second. Each projective point has two nonzero representatives, so \(\#X(\mathbb F_3)=(20+30)/2=25\).

**Picard eigenvalues and all-extension counts.** The Picard group of a smooth cubic surface over an algebraic closure has rank seven and is generated by classes of its 27 lines. Since every line is defined over \(\mathbb F_9\), the square of arithmetic Frobenius acts trivially on this Picard group; its eigenvalues are \(\pm1\). The standard smooth rational-surface trace formula is
\[
\#X(\mathbb F_{3^n})=1+3^{2n}+3^n\operatorname{tr}(F^n\mid\operatorname{Pic}(X_{\overline{\mathbb F}_3})\otimes\mathbb Q_\ell).
\]
The count \(25=1+9+3\operatorname{tr}(F)\) gives trace five, hence multiplicities six and one for the eigenvalues \(+1\) and \(-1\). This yields the point formula, the claimed characteristic polynomial, and the zeta function by exponential generating functions. On the rank-six orthogonal complement of the anticanonical class, the resulting involution has one negative direction and is a root reflection, the \(A_1\) Weyl class.

## Verification
The accompanying `clebsch_f3_verifier.py` requires only Python's standard library. Run `python clebsch_f3_verifier.py`. It performs exact operations in \(\mathbb F_9\), exhausts all \(120\) permutations for both line families, tests cubic containment on all nine affine parameters and at infinity, checks the six Frobenius pairs, exhausts all projective points over \(\mathbb F_3\) and \(\mathbb F_9\), and independently checks the sign-pattern count. Its verified output reports 27 distinct lines, 15 split lines, six conjugate pairs, 25 rational points over \(\mathbb F_3\), and 145 over \(\mathbb F_9\). The uniform formula for all extension degrees follows from the preceding geometric proof, rather than from a finite set of numerical tests.

## Relationship to prior work
The classical Clebsch surface and its 15-plus-12 geometric-line decomposition are established mathematics; see Baez's dated exposition and its earlier references. Vikulova studies the correct elementary-symmetric Clebsch model, including characteristic three, and develops the order-two possibilities for Galois action within the geometric symmetry framework. Banwait–Fité–Loughran classify possible finite-field cubic-surface Frobenius traces. Neither inspected treatment directly identifies the Frobenius class or zeta function of this particular characteristic-three integral model. The additional claim here is that *this model* realizes the nontrivial reflection, with explicit six conjugate line pairs and the resulting full rational-point series; smoothness and the existence of 27 lines are not asserted as new.

## Limitations
The identification is for the indicated model over the prime field of characteristic three, not for all twists of the Clebsch cubic or all integral models. The proof uses the standard smooth-cubic 27-line theorem and smooth-rational-surface trace formula. The reproducibility code proves only finite exact identities and counts; it does not serve as an independent audit or prove general cohomological results. Search coverage cannot establish absolute historical novelty.
