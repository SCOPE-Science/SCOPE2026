# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The unrestricted proof is a rank-\(t\) resolvent identity on the zero-sum Laplacian subspace of \(K_{s,s}\). Its deletion Gram matrix has exactly two eigenvalues, with positive inverse for every \(s\ge3\). The resulting complete transfer-current formula is simplified for disjoint edges to a three-case reciprocal-pair count; all signs follow from explicit rational expressions. Kirchhoff conservation and the maximum principle give the absolute cut- and cycle-column sums. Four symbolic orbit comparisons, each strictly positive on its applicable parameter range, prove the maximizing-edge classification. A separate calculation at perfect matching saturation and two exact positive difference identities prove monotonicity through the final step. An independent grounded-matrix *calculation* (not an independent mathematical audit) checked all 1,370 columns in 39 small graphs, including 60,990 transfer entries. The proof does not treat general deletion patterns.

## Originality — PASS, with bibliographic risk

Dilworth–Kutzarova–Ostrovskii, arXiv:2305.12582, analyzes invariant cycle-space projections principally on Hamming graphs and tori, not the matching-deleted biclique interpolation. Kassel–Wu, arXiv:1312.2946, establishes the general transfer-current framework but not the signed four-orbit absolute-current sums. Earlier exact boundary evaluations at one deleted edge and at the complete deletion of a perfect matching do not imply formulas at \(2\le t<s\), the complete maximizer count, or the order-\(s^{-2}\) final jump. Searches of matching-deleted bicliques, resistance distances, signed transfer currents, equivalent projection formulations, and public mathematical records found no stronger covering statement; no claim of exhaustive coverage is made. An undiscovered older current table or unindexed graph-projection calculation remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a complete, natural interpolation between a highly symmetric biclique and a crown graph: its edge-current sign inversion starts at the second deletion, its maximum-absolute-column location is exactly characterized without transitivity, and the last deletion restores transitivity while changing the norms by one higher asymptotic order than the entire earlier sequence. These quantitative mechanisms go beyond restating the familiar Laplacian inverse formula or calculating a single isolated graph. The theorem is limited to unweighted, balanced, matching-deleted bicliques and canonical orthogonal endpoint projections.
