# The modular rational curves in Picard modular compactifications have normal bundle \(\mathcal O(-1)^{\oplus(n-1)}\)

## Finding

Fix an integer \\(n\\ge 2\\) and a prime \\(p\\equiv1\\pmod{12}\\). Let \\(X_{n,p}\\), its smooth projective toroidal compactification \\(\\overline X_{n,p}\\), and the embedded modular curve \\(C_{n,p}\\subset\\overline X_{n,p}\\) be those of Memariansorkhabi–Yeung's level-three Picard modular construction [1, Sections 2–3]. Then \\(C_{n,p}\\simeq\\mathbb P^1_\\mathbb C\\) and its **ordinary holomorphic/algebraic normal bundle** splits exactly as

\\[
N_{C_{n,p}/\\overline X_{n,p}}\\cong\\mathcal O_{\\mathbb P^1}(-1)^{\\oplus(n-1)}.
\\]

Therefore \\(H^0(C_{n,p},N)=H^1(C_{n,p},N)=0\\): the curve defines an isolated, reduced point in the Hilbert scheme of the compactification. In dimension three the splitting is \\(\\mathcal O(-1)\\oplus\\mathcal O(-1)\\), while its canonical degree is zero [1, Proposition 4.1]. No assertion of a global contraction or flop is made.

## Assumptions and scope

Everything is over \\(\\mathbb C\\). The source [1] fixes \\(K=\\mathbb Q(i\\sqrt p)\\), a Hermitian form \\(H=J\\oplus(-I_{n-1})\\) with \\(J=\\begin{psmallmatrix}0&i\\sqrt p\\\\-i\\sqrt p&0\\end{psmallmatrix}\\), and the principal level-three congruence subgroup \\(\\Gamma\\subset\\operatorname{SU}(H,\\mathcal O_K)\\). The modular geodesic comes from the positive lines in the span of the first two basis vectors. Its stabilizer is precisely \\(\\Gamma(3)\\subset\\operatorname{SL}_2(\\mathbb Z)\\), embedded by \\(M\\mapsto\\operatorname{diag}(M,I_{n-1})\\) [1, Lemma 2.1]. The closed curve is embedded and meets the boundary transversely at the four distinct cusps \\(S=s_1+\\cdots+s_4\\) [1, Proposition 3.3]. These group and boundary properties, rather than an arbitrary totally geodesic curve, are hypotheses of the computation.

## Proof

**1. Split transverse descent on the open modular curve.** Write a point of the ambient ball near the geodesic as \\([\\tau:1:\\xi]\\), where \\(\\tau\\in\\mathbb H\\) and \\(\\xi=(\\xi_1,\\ldots,\\xi_{n-1})\\). The geodesic is \\(\\xi=0\\). For \\(\\gamma=\\begin{psmallmatrix}a&b\\\\c&d\\end{psmallmatrix}\\in\\Gamma(3)\\), the block-diagonal action is

\\[
(\\tau,\\xi)\\longmapsto
\\left(\\frac{a\\tau+b}{c\\tau+d},\\frac{\\xi}{c\\tau+d}\\right).
\\]

Set \\(j_\\gamma(\\tau)=c\\tau+d\\). The normal derivative at \\(\\xi=0\\) is exactly \\(j_\\gamma(\\tau)^{-1}I_{n-1}\\), with no off-diagonal mixing. Thus the ordinary normal bundle restricted to \\(Y(3)=C\\setminus S\\) is the direct sum of \\(n-1\\) copies of a single automorphy line bundle \\(L^\\circ\\) with weight-one normal multiplier \\(j_\\gamma^{-1}\\). The derivative of the Möbius map along the geodesic is \\(j_\\gamma^{-2}\\), since \\(ad-bc=1\\). Therefore the tensor square \\((L^\\circ)^{\\otimes2}\\) has exactly the tangent multiplier and is isomorphic to \\(T_{Y(3)}\\).

**2. Compare the extensions at every cusp.** At infinity the embedded curve's positive parabolic generator acts by \\(\\tau\\mapsto\\tau+3\\). Source [1, Lemma 3.1] identifies the ambient toroidal coordinate with the curve cusp coordinate:

\\[
q=\\exp(2\\pi i\\tau/3),\\qquad(\\zeta,q)=(\\xi,q),\\qquad C:\\zeta=0.
\\]

The coordinate vectors \\(\\partial/\\partial\\zeta_j\\) define nonvanishing frames of each ordinary normal summand at \\(q=0\\); the logarithmic tangent \\(q\\,\\partial/\\partial q=(3/(2\\pi i))\\,\\partial/\\partial\\tau\\) extends nonvanishingly across the curve cusp. The normal multipliers square to the tangent multipliers on the punctured disc; here the parabolic multipliers are both one, and the two just-displayed frames extend their identification holomorphically and invertibly at \\(q=0\\). For the three remaining cusps, [1, Lemma 3.1] transports this coordinate calculation by \\(\\operatorname{diag}(g,I_{n-1})\\) for a suitable \\(g\\in\\operatorname{SL}_2(\\mathbb Z)\\). The same transverse frames and logarithmic tangent calculation apply. In particular the \\(n-1\\) normal directions stay unmixed through all four boundary extensions.

Consequently a line bundle \\(L\\) on \\(C\\simeq\\mathbb P^1\\) extends \\(L^\\circ\\), and

\\[
N_{C/\\overline X}\\simeq L^{\\oplus(n-1)},\\qquad
L^{\\otimes2}\\simeq T_C(-S).
\\]

The second identity uses the **logarithmic** tangent extension, not the ordinary tangent: the cusp frame is \\(q\\partial_q\\), rather than \\(\\partial_q\\).

**3. Determine the integer degree.** Since \\(C\\simeq\\mathbb P^1\\) and \\(\\deg S=4\\), one has \\(T_C(-S)\\simeq\\mathcal O_{\\mathbb P^1}(2-4)=\\mathcal O_{\\mathbb P^1}(-2)\\). The Picard group of \\(\\mathbb P^1\\) is freely generated by \\(\\mathcal O(1)\\), so the only line bundle with this square is \\(L\\simeq\\mathcal O(-1)\\). This proves the complete splitting, for **every** allowed \\(n,p\\).

**4. Deformations and numerical check.** On \\(\\mathbb P^1\\), both \\(H^0(\\mathcal O(-1))\\) and \\(H^1(\\mathcal O(-1))\\) vanish. The Hilbert tangent space at the smooth embedded curve is \\(H^0(N)\\); hence that local Hilbert point has zero tangent dimension and is reduced and isolated. Standard embedded-deformation obstructions lie in \\(H^1(N)\\) and also vanish. The normal degree equals \\(1-n\\). Independently, [1, Proposition 4.1] gives \\(K_{\\overline X}\\cdot C=n-3\\), so tangent-normal adjunction yields \\(\\deg N=-K_{\\overline X}\\cdot C-\\deg T_C=3-n-2=1-n\\), as required.

## Verification

An exact standalone calculation in `artifacts/check_automorphy.py` verifies the Möbius derivative and scalar normal transformation for explicit integral determinant-one matrices, verifies the level-three parabolic at the cusp, and checks the adjunction degree identity for representative dimensions. The universal conclusion does **not** follow from that finite sample: it follows from the symbolic derivative for arbitrary determinant-one \\(\\gamma\\) and from the four-cusp extension argument above. Reproduce with `python3 artifacts/check_automorphy.py`.

## Relationship to prior work

The construction, identification of \\(\\Gamma(3)\\), four distinct transverse boundary crossings, and canonical degree are established in [1, Lemmas 2.1 and 3.1, Propositions 3.3 and 4.1]. They are not new here. The new claim is the exact **ordinary normal-bundle splitting** and the resulting Hilbert-scheme rigidity. In the available full text of [1], the normal-bundle splitting and deformation cohomology are not calculated. General complex-geodesic normal-degree phenomena motivate a search for stronger coverage, but numerical normal degree alone does not imply this vector-bundle splitting, especially in higher rank. Searches for Picard modular curve normal bundles, weight-one automorphy, the logarithmic tangent square, and the specific construction identified no prior statement covering the complete result. The priority assessment remains literature-dependent, not an assertion of absolute uniqueness.

## Limitations

The result applies only to the explicit level-three Picard modular varieties and their particular embedded \\(X(3)\\) curves, not to all toroidal compactifications or all geodesic curves. The normal splitting yields the local Hilbert-scheme statement, not a global analytic contraction, flop, or description of a component of the full moduli space of compactifications. The proof relies on the correct matching of **ambient** and **curve** cusp periods, established in [1, Lemma 3.1]. The classification has been reviewed mathematically within this package; independent validation has not been performed. An earlier or unindexed general normal-bundle theorem could affect priority.
