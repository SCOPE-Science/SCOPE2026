## Correctness

PASS. Conditioning on the specified vertex having no later children leaves independent uniform parent choices after deleting that leaf and closing the birth-label gap. This proves the exact distributional reduction, harmonic conditional mean and covariance. The adjacent-covariance difference is monotone, and a short parity argument shows that a nonempty consecutive reciprocal sum starting at an integer at least two cannot equal one. This rules out ties, proves the unique threshold and gives the limiting extremal birth fraction. All finite boundary cases in the stated theorem have been checked against exact exhaustive enumeration through nine vertices; the proof applies for arbitrary finite order.

It does not retain the birth index and does not supply the marked deletion law, exact age profile or unique \(e^{-1}\) extremum. Published related research records on root attachments and on root-degree/leaf-count covariance treat different conditioning events. ## Value

PASS. Specified birth age is a meaningful intrinsic parameter of recursive growth, whereas leaf count alone suppresses it. The exact law exhibits how terminal status of a vertex affects global path length and yields an unusual nonmonotone age profile: the earliest leaf has a larger conditional mean penalty, but the strongest covariance occurs at a unique intermediate birth position approaching \(1/e\) of the total arrival order. The complete finite-order maximally negative age classification goes beyond a routine unmarked total covariance calculation.

## Limitations

The result is for the uniform attachment model, leaves are zero-child vertices, and only covariance—not correlation—is optimized. Aggregate covariance is covered by earlier unmarked generating-function techniques. Priority risk remains for uncatalogued formulations of marked deletion or age-indexed leaf conditioning.
