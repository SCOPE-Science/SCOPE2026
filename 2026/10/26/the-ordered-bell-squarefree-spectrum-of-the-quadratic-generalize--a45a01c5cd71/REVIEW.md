# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The recurrence is normalized at \(s(1)=1\), and adjacent-index subtraction gives the divisor convolution \(s*g=g-u+\delta\), with \(g(m)=2m-1\). Its reduction modulo two proves the entire nonsquarefree branch. For squarefree \(n\), a finite Boolean-lattice convolution inversion, followed by an exact Stirling-to-Fubini generating-function calculation, gives the displayed closed formula. Positivity of each \(F_j\) and \(p_i-1\) establishes the iff and sign boundaries. Full integer calculations through \(n=40000\) corroborate but do not prove the universal statement.

## Originality — PASS (residual risk disclosed)

The motivating source arXiv:2609.25628v1 defines the construction, but supplies no squarefree formula in elementary symmetric polynomials with Fubini coefficients. Its literal all-\(f\) primality statement would formally include the present prime criterion, but that blanket statement has a demonstrated fixed-constant counterexample and its proof only establishes nonidentity as a polynomial in unconstrained parameters. Therefore the present mathematically valid quadratic coefficient law is not established by the motivating source. A published constant-function obstruction addresses \(f(m)=1\), not \(f(m)=m^2\). An earlier fixed-affine specialization of the same recurrence has a different, prime-factor-count structure; it does not imply a formula depending on individual \(p_i-1\). Meaning-based mathematical-record searches and searches of the motivating full text and relevant literature found no exact implication. Searches are not a proof of novelty; an implicit broader identity remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This supplies the first located fully explicit squarefree magnitude-and-sign spectrum for a nonlinear polynomial specialization of a recently introduced generalization. The exact Fubini coefficient structure records arithmetic information about each prime divisor, gives complete sharp magnitude bounds for every squarefree composite, and independently clarifies a natural pointwise primality question after a published universal assertion proved insufficient. The result is a natural infinite classification, not a merely empirical table or renaming.

## Limits and relevant literature

The formula does not classify the magnitudes at nonsquarefree integers; it gives evenness there. The result does not claim algorithmic competitiveness.
