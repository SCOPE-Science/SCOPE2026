# Scientific review: sharp almost-sure Poisson sampling frontier

**Correctness — PASS.** The scalar holding-interval flow gives the exact random multiplier; logarithmic integrability and renewal strong laws give the sample-time exponent and the continuous-time *upper* exponent. A beta-density transformation converts existence of a stabilizing gain into the sign of a one-parameter logarithmic potential. Its differentiated principal-value representation has a strictly positive derivative at intensity ratio \(0<\alpha\le1/2\). The cotangent expansion, the direct \(\alpha=1\) formula, and the finite-inverse-moment expansion prove negative potential at small positive gain offset when \(\alpha>1/2\). The critical boundary is included on the non-stabilizable side. Full-time *ordinary* logarithmic convergence is not claimed because an interval trajectory can cross zero. The standalone verification program independently checks the key finite identities and integral evaluations; analytical arguments, not computational sampling, prove the theorem.

**Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, residual risk).** Tanwani, Chatterjee, and Liberzon (2018, §6.3) study the exact scalar model and already establish the mean-square stabilizability threshold \(\lambda>2a\). That threshold is credited as prior work. Huff, Fiacchini, and Gomes da Silva Jr. (2022) characterize mean-exponential stability through convex conditions, and Basu, Ferrante, and Fiacchini (2023) treat stochastic sampled output regulation. Beutler (1972) gives a general logarithmic almost-sure stability bound; its available abstract states no sharp Poisson scalar intensity frontier. The new scientific assertion is specifically the *if and only if* gain-existence threshold \(\lambda>a/2\) for pathwise stability, including the universal no-gain half of the theorem and its sharp separation from mean square. No covering exact threshold was identified by targeted publication and mathematical-record searches. The complete 1972 article was inaccessible, so its possible inclusion of an equivalent scalar calculation remains a material priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** For the same physically natural unstable sampled-data plant, the existence of pathwise-stabilizing and mean-square-stabilizing fixed gains has sharply different sampling-rate requirements. The fourfold intensity gap is an exact control-design boundary and clarifies why stable observed trajectories may coexist with failure of second-moment guarantees. The result is not a statement about multidimensional plants or arbitrary stochastic sampling laws.

**Limitations and priority risks.** The theorem assumes scalar dynamics, exact zero-order hold, independent exponential intervals, no measurement noise, and fixed gain. The original state can have isolated intersample zeros, hence only a physical-time upper exponent is asserted. Almost-sure stabilization and mean-square stabilization are different notions; the older mean-square threshold cannot be relabeled as new. An early logarithmic stability paper was accessible only by abstract, and independent scientific/priority audit remains necessary.
