# The sharp singleton obstruction to universal common knowledge in random S5 frames

## Finding

Fix an integer \(r\ge 2\). On \(W_n=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), independently sample \(r\) equivalence relations \(R_1,\ldots,R_r\), each uniformly among the \(B_n\) equivalence relations, where \(B_n\) is the \(n\)-th Bell number. Let \(J_n\) be the equivalence relation generated by \(R_1\cup\cdots\cup R_r\), i.e. the common-knowledge accessibility relation. Write \(\rho_n>0\) for the unique solution of \(\rho_n e^{\rho_n}=n\).

**Theorem.** For each fixed \(r\ge2\), as \(n\to\infty\),

\[
\Pr(J_n\ne W_n\times W_n)
\sim n\left(\frac{B_{n-1}}{B_n}\right)^r
\sim\frac{\rho_n^r}{n^{r-1}}
\sim\frac{(\log n)^r}{n^{r-1}}.
\]

Moreover, conditioned on \(J_n\ne W_n\times W_n\), the partition into \(J_n\)-classes has exactly two blocks, one a singleton and the other containing all remaining \(n-1\) worlds, with probability tending to one. The singleton is a singleton in **every** independently selected agent partition.

For classical S5 semantics, the common-knowledge operator \(C\) is the global box on all propositional valuations precisely when \(J_n=W_n\times W_n\). Thus the theorem provides the sharp failure probability of global common knowledge for this specific uniform-equivalence-frame model. It does not assert a rate for general intuitionistic birelational frames.

## Assumptions and scope

All worlds are labelled; relations are chosen independently and uniformly from the set of equivalence relations, **not** by independently sampling possible edges. Every agent has full equivalence accessibility. The agent count \(r\ge2\) is fixed while \(n\to\infty\). Common-knowledge reachability is the reflexive transitive closure of the union of agent relations; equivalently it is the join of the associated set partitions. The underlying intuitionistic information order, if one chooses to view the structures in a birelational framework, is equality. \(B_0=1\).

An unordered world-partition with more than one block has a block of size at most \(n/2\); that fact is used only as a union bound, not as an independence assumption.

## Proof

**1. Singleton lower bound.** For each world \(i\), let \(E_i\) denote the event that \(\{i\}\) is a block in all \(r\) sampled partitions. Deleting this common singleton leaves an arbitrary partition of the other \(n-1\) worlds for each agent, hence

\[
\Pr(E_i)=q_n^r,\qquad q_n=\frac{B_{n-1}}{B_n}.
\]

For two distinct worlds, \(\Pr(E_i\cap E_j)=(B_{n-2}/B_n)^r\). The classical Bell-ratio asymptotic gives \(q_n\sim\rho_n/n\) and \(B_{n-2}/B_n=O((\log n/n)^2)\). With \(\lambda_n=nq_n^r\), for fixed \(r\ge2\) we have \(\lambda_n\to0\), and Bonferroni therefore gives

\[
\Pr\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^nE_i\right)
=\lambda_n+O\left(n^2(B_{n-2}/B_n)^r\right)
=\lambda_n(1+o(1)).
\]

A common singleton is necessarily an isolated component of the union-accessibility graph, hence the join is not universal whenever it exists (for \(n\ge2\)).

**2. Uniform exclusion of non-singleton small components.** For a fixed set \(A\subseteq W_n\) with \(|A|=k\), the event that no agent's partition block crosses the cut \(A\mid A^c\) has probability

\[
Q_{n,k}^r,\qquad Q_{n,k}=\frac{B_kB_{n-k}}{B_n}.
\]

Let \(T_n=\sum_{k=2}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\binom nkQ_{n,k}^r\). Every disconnected join without a singleton component contributes to a cut counted by \(T_n\). We prove \(T_n=o(\lambda_n)\).

For \(2\le k\le n^{1/3}\), the Bell-number ratios and their monotonicity imply, uniformly on this range,

\[
\frac{B_{n-k}}{B_n}\le\left(\frac{C\log n}{n}\right)^k
\]

for some absolute constant \(C>0\). Also \(B_k\le k^k\) and \(\binom nk\le(en/k)^k\). Therefore, with \(C\) enlarged if necessary,

\[
\binom nkQ_{n,k}^r\le
\left(\frac{eC^r k^{r-1}(\log n)^r}{n^{r-1}}\right)^k
\le\varepsilon_n^k,
\quad
\varepsilon_n=\frac{eC^r(\log n)^r}{n^{2(r-1)/3}}\to0.
\]

Summing the geometric majorant from \(k=2\), we obtain \(O(\varepsilon_n^2)=o(\lambda_n)\), since \(\varepsilon_n^2/\lambda_n=O((\log n)^r/n^{(r-1)/3})\to0\).

For \(n^{1/3}\le k\le n/2\), let \(s_m=B_m/B_{m-1}\), and let \(H_{n,k}=\log\binom nk\). The standard Bell-ratio estimate is

\[
\log s_m=\log m-\log\log m
+O\left(\frac{\log\log m}{\log m}\right)
\quad(m\to\infty).
\]

Telescoping the Bell numbers yields

\[
\log Q_{n,k}
=\sum_{j=1}^k\log\frac{s_j}{s_{n-k+j}}.
\]

The same sum with \(s_m\) replaced by \(m\) equals \(-H_{n,k}\). We establish that the replacement changes the sum by \(o(H_{n,k})\), uniformly over the stated range. For the first \(\lfloor\sqrt{k}\rfloor\) terms the difference is at most \(O(\sqrt{k}\log\log(n+3))=o(k)\). In the remaining terms, the Bell-ratio error totals \(O(k\log\log k/\log k)=o(k)\), while the contribution from differences of \(\log\log\) is at most

\[
\frac{2}{\log k}\sum_{j=\lfloor\sqrt{k}\rfloor+1}^k
\log\frac{n-k+j}{j}
\le\frac{2H_{n,k}}{\log k}.
\]

Since \(H_{n,k}\ge k\log(n/k)\ge k\log2\), each error is \(o(H_{n,k})\), uniformly. Consequently

\[
\binom nk Q_{n,k}^r
=\exp\left(-(r-1+o(1))H_{n,k}\right)
\le\exp(-c_r n^{1/3})
\]

for some constant \(c_r>0\). Summing at most \(n\) such terms remains \(o(\lambda_n)\). Combining both ranges gives \(T_n=o(\lambda_n)\).

**3. Exact dominant topology.** The two events 'at least one jointly singleton world' and 'disconnected join' differ by an event of probability at most \(T_n=o(\lambda_n)\). The probability of two or more common singletons is \(O(n^2(B_{n-2}/B_n)^r)=o(\lambda_n)\). Further, a disconnected join with precisely one singleton and more than one other component has a component of size between \(2\) and \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) (for sufficiently large \(n\)), and so is covered by \(T_n\). Thus conditional on disconnection, exactly one singleton component and one complementary component appear with asymptotic probability one.

Finally the standard asymptotic \(B_{n-1}/B_n\sim \rho_n/n\), together with \(\rho_n\sim\log n\), transforms \(\lambda_n\) into the two announced equivalent forms. This concludes the proof.

## Verification

An independent finite enumeration generates every restricted-growth string representation of a partition of \(W_n\), constructs the union-equivalence components for each ordered tuple of partitions, and compares the result against the exact connected-count recurrence. It checks all tuples for \(r=2\) and \(r=3\) on \(1\le n\le5\).

With \(C_{n,r}\) denoting the number of connected \(r\)-tuples, the finite recurrence is

\[
C_{1,r}=1,\qquad
C_{n,r}=B_n^r-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k-1}C_{k,r}B_{n-k}^r.
\]

This follows by selecting the connected component containing world \(1\), and partitioning its complement independently. The included `verify.py` checks this recurrence against exhaustive direct enumeration. The probability calculations for finite \(n\) are sanity checks, not proofs of the asymptotic uniform estimate above.

## Relationship to prior work

Zenger, *Intuitionistic Common Knowledge* (arXiv:2605.00523v1, first publicly posted 2026-05-01), provides a recent semantic and proof-theoretic treatment of common knowledge, including S5 settings; it does not give the present random-frame rate. The theorem here is restricted to the classical/equality-information-order S5 subclass, not the full intuitionistic models.

Pittel, *Where the Typical Set Partitions Meet and Join* (Electronic Journal of Combinatorics 7 (2000), R5, Theorem 5), treats **exactly** the random join model, proving that disconnection has probability \(O((\log n)^{r+1}/n^{r-1})\). Thus the random-join question itself and its probability-one connectivity conclusion are established prior results. The result proved here supplies a sharp equivalent of strictly smaller order by a factor proportional to \(\log n\), including the leading constant expressed in Bell ratios, and identifies the conditional shape of failures.

Bell, Bender, Cameron and Richmond, *Asymptotics for the Probability of Connectedness and Distribution of Number of Components* (Electronic Journal of Combinatorics 7 (2000), R33), gives general labeled decomposable-structure machinery. The finite connected-count recurrence above is an instance of that classical component principle, not an independently new general enumeration formula.

## Limitations

The leading asymptotic requires the number of agents fixed and at least two; it is not established uniformly as the agent count grows with the number of worlds. Sampling equivalence relations uniformly is essential. The theorem concerns global equivalence of common knowledge with the universal box, rather than validity probabilities of particular formulas. The proof is self-contained **conditional on standard Bell-number ratio asymptotics**, for which classical references are given. Exhaustive finite enumeration does not establish the infinite statement. Priority relative to every possible unpublished or more general asymptotic result is not established.
