# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** Every nonzero product corresponds to a positive-index pair with an untruncated exponent sum. Distinct rows and columns force the sum of all selected output exponents to be at least \(t(t+1)\); distinct outputs bounded by \(n-1\) force it to be at most \(tn-t(t+1)/2\). This proves the upper bound. Two explicit parity-specific permutations yield the matching number of distinct, nonzero products, and a single zero-product cell completes the construction. The \(n=2\) boundary is included. Exact search for sizes up to 12 and witness tests through 70 corroborate but do not establish the infinite assertion.

**Originality — PASS, subject to residual literature risk.** The closest available original source concerns *full* transversals and existence of complete mappings; its regularity obstruction is not an upper-and-lower quantitative formula for *partial* transversals. A prior talk covering complete mappings was read, and the principal newer abstract was inspected. The classical Latin-square literature assumes a Latin property absent here. A complete examination of the newer 63-page manuscript and of all equivalent truncated-sum/rainbow-matching formulations was not possible; possible prior equivalence remains a substantive risk, not a claim of universal novelty.

**Value — PASS.** For a natural nonregular family ruled out by the complete-mapping theory, the theorem identifies the exact optimal deficit from a full transversal, for every size, and supplies explicit attaining configurations. The answer distinguishes a sharp quantitative extremal invariant from the previously known qualitative no-complete-mapping obstruction.

**Limits.** The result neither classifies maximizers nor extends to non-monogenic nilpotent semigroups. No independent audit has been performed.
