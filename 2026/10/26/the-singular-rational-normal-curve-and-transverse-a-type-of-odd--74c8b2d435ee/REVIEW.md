# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For r=2p+3≥7, Massri’s hypothesis p>N=1 holds, giving the integral codimension-three scheme and its three-quadric ideal. In odd degree the invariant pairing is alternating and the binary-quadratic summand of Sym²(V) occurs once, so the transvectant and nonzero moment map have the same coordinate ideal. The differentiated pairing identifies rank failure with a nonzero infinitesimal stabilizer. A semisimple generator has no zero weight; a nilpotent generator has a pure-power kernel. Pure powers are zeros. Homogeneity at a nonzero zero makes the radial direction a kernel direction, so the affine rank test also gives the projective one. On a₀=1 the unipotent substitution x↦x+sy changes a₁ to a₁+s, giving an actual product of the a₁=0 slice with a smooth coordinate, not just a tangent-space complement. Directly differentiating in binomial coordinates gives q₀=2a_(r−1)+P₀ and q₁ linear in a_r with coefficient 2. The two terms of q₂ involving a_(r−1) add to −2(r−1)a₂a_(r−1), yielding the printed reduced equation. Its Hessian in a₃,…,a_(r−2) is invertible at t=0. Uniqueness of the stationary section makes it equivariant under the diagonal weights, hence the critical value is a single power c t^((r+1)/2). If c=0, the actual product chart would have slice singularities off the pure-power curve, contradicting the rank test. Completing squares, taking analytic units locally, and rescaling gives the asserted germ; the transverse Jacobian and Tjurina algebras are both C[t]/(t^((r−1)/2)). As a normalization check, at r=7 the reduced equation is 180t²a₄−120ta₃²+30a₃a₅−20a₄², with stationary value 405t⁴.
