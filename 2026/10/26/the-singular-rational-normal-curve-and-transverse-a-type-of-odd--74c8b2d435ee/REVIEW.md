# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The three defining quadrics and integrality are provided by Massri's 2026 Theorem 1.1 in the strict range \(r=2p+3\), \(p>1\). The odd symmetric-power representation is symplectic; its moment-map differential loses rank if and only if an infinitesimal \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) stabilizer exists. Semisimple stabilizers have no zero-weight vectors, and nilpotent stabilizers fix only pure powers. This gives the reduced singular locus exactly. Substituting two local equations yields an explicit weighted polynomial \(F_r\); the Hessian in all variables except \(a_2\) is nondegenerate. Analytic completion of the square yields an \(A\)-type form with the sole coefficient proved nonzero using the exact singular-support result. The finite script independently reproduces the algebra at seven small odd degrees, not all degrees.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Massri (2013), Theorem 12, identifies the same three-quadric family and proves **inclusion** of the rational normal curve in the singular locus; it does not give equality or an analytic transverse normal form. Massri (2026), Sections 2–6, treats integrality, tangent cones and their normality, not this global singular-locus equality or suspended \(A_{(r-1)/2}\) type. Searches of published findings for the source, moment maps, transvectants, rational normal curves and all-degree \(A\) types did not return the same object/statement. Herbig–Schwarz–Seaton's cotangent-lift moment shells do not cover this self-symplectic representation. A different-language published equivalence remains possible; no claim of certified novelty is made.

## Value — PASS
The integral complete-intersection theorem left the precise singular support and its analytic severity unspecified. This result identifies the only singular stratum, proves uniform transverse analytic equisingularity, and computes the exact increasing Milnor number. It distinguishes the variety's genuine singular structure from the weaker assertion that the curve is merely contained in the singular locus. These are natural invariants relevant to studying its vanishing cycles, stratification, and possible resolutions.
