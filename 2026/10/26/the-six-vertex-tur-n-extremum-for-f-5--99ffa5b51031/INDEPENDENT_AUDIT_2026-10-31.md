# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

There are exactly 20 possible edges on six vertices. All five-vertex injections of F5 produce precisely 180 distinct forbidden 3-edge masks. An independently written complete traversal of all 2^20=1,048,576 labeled hypergraphs tested each candidate for containing a forbidden mask and found maximum 10 and precisely six labeled maximizers; all six are stars centered at each vertex. The explicit star is F5-free because a star center would have to be in the shared pair of the first two F5 edges, but the third edge excludes that pair. The finite check covers the *entire* domain n=6; no random sampling, timeout inference or asymptotic extrapolation is used.

## originality

PASS

The classical eventual Turán theorem gives a balanced complete tripartite extremum only for sufficiently large n; the available modern sources explicitly discuss eventual n thresholds, not the six-vertex star exception and unique extremizers. Search did not locate a precise earlier n=6 table. This qualified novelty is limited by uninspected older Frankl–Füredi full text or possible small-hypergraph catalogs.

## value

PASS

This is the first small-order regime immediately beyond the five-vertex F5 pattern where the extremal family is a genuine star, unlike the balanced tripartite 6-vertex construction with only eight triples. The exact smallest nontrivial endpoint and uniqueness classification are natural base-case extremal hypergraph invariants that a later small-order classification or stability argument can cite. The small size and lack of a general n-theorem limit significance, but do not make this unknown exact natural finite case mathematically arbitrary.
