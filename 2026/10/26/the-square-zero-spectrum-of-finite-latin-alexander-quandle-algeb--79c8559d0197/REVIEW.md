# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The character Fourier transform is invertible under the stated field assumptions. Direct multiplication gives \(\widehat{u*v}(\chi)=\widehat u(\Phi\chi)\widehat v(\Psi\chi)\); both maps are bijective for latin Alexander quandles. A universal-product-zero subspace is therefore supported on an independent set of \(T=\Psi\Phi^{-1}\). The argument quantifies over *all ordered pairs*, including distinct vectors, and correctly handles loops and two-cycles. Cycle independence numbers, extremizer counts, and independence polynomials follow by explicit counting. Character separation proves the commutative-versus-square-zero boundary. A direct finite-field checker corroborates the product identity, numerical formulas and two exhaustive element censuses, without substituting finite tests for proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The central Fourier multiplication identity belongs to Elhamdadi–Ta–Virgin (arXiv:2609.03799) and Ta (arXiv:2610.20306), and the transported group-ring product to Zhang (arXiv:2610.08204). These are acknowledged as prior results. The inspected main manuscripts concern idempotents, augmentation, group-ring filtrations and qualitative zero divisors. Churchill et al. (arXiv:2608.09149) studies nilpotence for trivial quandles and selected zero-divisor graphs; Sangare–Ta (arXiv:2608.01550v2) investigates idempotents, automorphisms and commutator widths. No inspected text states the complete mutually annihilating subspace classification, exact independence-polynomial square-zero counts, or commutativity rigidity boundary. Mathematical-record and literature searches did not identify equivalent coverage. The remaining risk is that a more general established theorem about twisted coordinate products could imply these formulas.

## Value — PASS

The study of zero divisors and nilpotents is explicit motivation in the recent quandle-ring literature. A complete geometric description of the square-zero locus and all square-zero linear subspaces is a natural structural invariant, not a prescribed slice of a large enumeration. The result translates nonassociative zero products into a computable permutation-cycle invariant, with exact finite-field point counts and a sharp characterization of when nonzero square-zero elements cannot exist. The dihedral-prime specialization connects the invariant to the multiplicative order of \(-2\) modulo \(p\).

## Limits

The theorem assumes finite latin Alexander quandles over splitting nonmodular fields. It does not classify modular or arbitrary quandle algebras, and does not assert a new Fourier multiplication identity. Finite checks are illustrative. A possible implicit consequence of broader structural literature remains a scientific priority risk.
