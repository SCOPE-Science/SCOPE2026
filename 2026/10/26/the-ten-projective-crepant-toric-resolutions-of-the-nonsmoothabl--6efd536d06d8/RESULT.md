# The ten projective crepant toric resolutions of the nonsmoothable pentagon and their thirteen flops

## Finding

For the lattice pentagon
\[
Q=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(-2,-1),(-3,-2),(-2,-3),(-1,-2)\},
\]
let \(U_Q=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C[\sigma^\vee\cap M]\), where \(\sigma\) is the strongly convex cone in \(\mathbb R^3\) generated by \((v,1)\) for \(v\in Q\cap\mathbb Z^2\). The smooth **toric**, **crepant** resolutions that are **projective over** \(U_Q\), each equipped with its specified morphism to \(U_Q\), are in bijection with ten coherent unimodular triangulations of \(Q\). Every unimodular triangulation of \(Q\) is coherent. The elementary flop graph \(G_Q\) has
\[
|V(G_Q)|=10,\qquad |E(G_Q)|=13,\qquad
\operatorname{rad}(G_Q)=3,\quad \operatorname{diam}(G_Q)=5.
\]
Its degree multiplicities, for degrees one through four, are \((1,3,5,1)\). The numbers of unordered phase-pairs at distances one through five are respectively \((13,16,11,4,1)\), so exactly one pair of marked resolutions requires five elementary toric flops.

## Assumptions and scope

All varieties are over \(\mathbb C\). The base \(U_Q\) is the isolated affine toric Gorenstein threefold singled out by the pentagon in Wuebben's September 2026 construction. Resolution phases are **not** quotiented by lattice symmetries or abstract isomorphism: an isomorphism of resolutions must commute with their given maps to the **fixed identity** of \(U_Q\). Only toric crepant morphisms that are projective over this affine base are counted. Each graph edge is a single toric flop represented by an empty convex lattice quadrilateral diagonal exchange. No claim is made about non-toric resolutions or the global birational geography of the projective threefold in which this singularity occurs.

The seven lattice points, with the indicated immutable indices, are
\[
\begin{aligned}
&p_0=(0,0),\ p_1=(-2,-1),\ p_2=(-3,-2),\ p_3=(-2,-3),\ p_4=(-1,-2),\\
&p_5=(-2,-2),\quad p_6=(-1,-1).
\end{aligned}
\]
The first five are vertices, and the last two are interior. All five boundary edges are primitive, and \(Q\) has normalized area seven.

## Proof

**1. Toric correspondence.** Because every boundary edge of \(Q\) is primitive, the lattice points shown are its entire lattice point set. A toric crepant subdivision of the height-one cone uses rays \((p_j,1)\) only. Smoothness requires each maximal three-dimensional cone to be unimodular, equivalently each triangle of the induced subdivision has determinant of absolute value one. Projectivity relative to \(U_Q\) is equivalent to coherence, or the existence of a strictly convex piecewise-linear support function. Thus smooth projective crepant toric resolutions over \(U_Q\) are exactly coherent unimodular triangulations of \(Q\).

**2. Exhaustive triangulations.** For any three distinct indices \(a,b,c\), put
\[
\delta(a,b,c)=\det(p_b-p_a,p_c-p_a).
\]
There are exactly seventeen unordered triples with \(|\delta|=1\). Because the normalized area of \(Q\) is seven, every unimodular triangulation contains exactly seven such triangles. Two distinct unit triangles have disjoint interiors precisely when they share no properly crossing edges and, if they share an edge, their third vertices lie on opposite sides of that edge. Here every triangle is primitive and has area one half, so there are no hidden lattice points on any triangle edge or in its interior. Enumerate all seven-element pairwise-compatible subsets of the seventeen candidates. The supplied independently runnable integer checker enumerates exactly ten. Their total area is exactly that of \(Q\), so the compatible triangles fill \(Q\), with no gaps. Conversely, any unimodular triangulation must occur among these subsets, proving completeness.

For an especially short exact certificate, index the seventeen unit triangles lexicographically by their three vertex indices as in `verify.py`. The ten resulting index sets are

```
(0,2,3,4,11,12,15)
(0,2,5,11,12,13,16)
(0,2,5,11,12,14,15)
(1,3,4,6,10,11,15)
(1,3,4,7,11,12,15)
(1,5,6,8,9,11,13)
(1,5,6,10,11,13,16)
(1,5,6,10,11,14,15)
(1,5,7,11,12,13,16)
(1,5,7,11,12,14,15)
```

**3. All phases are projective.** For each of the ten subdivisions, `verify.py` supplies an explicit integral height vector \((h_0,\ldots,h_6)\). On every interior edge shared by triangles \((u,v,w)\) and \((u,v,z)\), it verifies the strict inequality saying that the lifted point \((p_z,h_z)\) lies above the affine plane through the other lifted three vertices. All eight interior edges are checked for each triangulation; the smallest positive integer support-function margins are \((1,1,1,1,2,1,1,1,3,3)\). Strict convexity on all interior edges gives a coherent subdivision. The ten certificates prove coherence without assuming that arbitrary planar triangulations are regular.

**4. Flops and the graph.** A single elementary toric flop here replaces the two unimodular triangles of an empty convex four-point lattice quadrilateral with its other two unimodular triangles, without changing the other five triangles. Thus two enumerated triangulations are adjacent exactly when their seven-element triangle-index sets have a symmetric difference of cardinality four, corresponding to two triangles on each side. A direct exact comparison yields the following adjacency lists for vertices numbered from zero in the order above:

```
0: 2,4
1: 2,8
2: 0,1,9
3: 4,7
4: 0,3,9
5: 6
6: 5,7,8
7: 3,6,9
8: 1,6,9
9: 2,4,7,8
```

Every exchanged quadrilateral contains exactly the same four lattice vertices, as checked in the certificate. The degrees sum to twenty-six, proving the thirteen-edge total. Exhaustive breadth-first shortest-path search from each of the ten vertices establishes connectivity; the distance histogram is \((13,16,11,4,1)\). The minimum eccentricity is three, the maximum is five, and the sole pair at distance five is \(\{0,5\}\). The adjacency lists are independently reproducible from the exact triangle sets.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` in a clean directory containing that file. It uses only the Python standard library and exact integer operations: orientation determinants, primitive triangle tests, pairwise-disjointness constraints, integer strict-convexity determinants, graph edges, and all-pairs shortest paths. A successful run ends in `QB_EXACT_VERIFICATION_OK` and prints the triangulations, ten height certificates, adjacency lists, degree histogram and distance histogram. This is a *complete finite certificate* because the algorithm enumerates every possible unimodular triangle from every lattice point of \(Q\), then every possible compatible seven-triangle set. It does not attempt to enumerate non-toric resolutions.

## Relationship to prior work

Wuebben (2026, Theorem 1.2 and Example 2.3) constructs the non-smoothable local singularity from this explicit pentagon in a projective Calabi--Yau threefold and studies its deformation theory; he does not tabulate this toric flop graph. Saxena (2020, Section 3, especially Figure 3 and the discussion of the ten isolated rank-two toric singularities) gives an extensive toric-resolution and gauge-phase framework, with detailed analysis emphasizing seven gauge-theoretic types; the exact ten-phase, thirteen-edge graph of the specified coordinate pentagon is not presented in the inspected material. Bao, Beaney Colverd and He (2020) classify quiver and toric-diagram information for polygons with two interior lattice points; their quiver phases are not, by themselves, an exact listing of these marked crepant resolutions or of graph distances. General correspondences between coherent triangulations, relative projectivity and crepant toric resolutions are standard toric geometry; the asserted contribution is their complete exact application to this recently used local singularity, including the explicit adjacency and distance profile.

## Limitations

The singularity and its lattice-point data are known, as are general toric methods. The exact finite graph may be independently recoverable from earlier physics, secondary-fan or quiver databases not exhaustively indexed; the claim is scoped to an explicit checked enumeration rather than priority over every possible representation. Flop graph edges are toric and over the fixed affine base, and no correspondence with projective resolutions of the global compactification is asserted. This is a self-review mathematical computation and argument; independent audit has not been performed.
