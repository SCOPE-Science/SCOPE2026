# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The stated all-positive-integer domain is explicit. After excluding \((s,t)=(2,1)\), the real roots satisfy \(\alpha\ge2\), \(0<\beta\le1\), and \(\lambda\ge2\) apart from \((s,t)=(3,2)\), where \(\lambda=\beta=1\). Exact Binet expansion and a geometric-tail estimate give the strict two-sided inequality \(D-1<S^{-1}<D\). The exceptional case is handled rather than inferred from limiting cases. The proof includes \(n=r=1\); no extrapolation from finite computation is needed. An exact-rational standalone verifier independently tests all \(7600\) specified integer quadruples.

## Originality — PASS with residual risk

Amdeberhan--Chen--Moll--Sagan [1] stated the precise identity as the first part of Conjecture 4.6. Park [2, Theorem 18] proved it only for sufficiently large \(s,t\), leaving uniform small-parameter coverage unresolved in that theorem. Direct inspection of relevant open primary texts found no stronger covering result. Kuhapatanakul [4] and Holliday--Komatsu [3] use positive second recurrence coefficients; Yuan--He--Zhuo [5] principally address a third reciprocal power. Equivalent formulations and independently obtained earlier proofs may exist outside the checked literature; the result is not claimed to be independently validated or absolutely unique.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact reciprocal-tail floor for a natural entire two-parameter family was explicitly conjectured in archival work. Removing the qualitative large-parameter hypothesis from Park's theorem resolves every permitted small exceptional parameter, all index progressions and every tail cutoff at once. The real-root comparison bound also supplies a direct quantitative explanation of why the reciprocal always lies in its asserted unit interval, including the delicate \(\lambda=1\) boundary. This is a genuine complete conjectural identity, not a normalization, renamed formula, arbitrary finite slice or known table recomputation.
