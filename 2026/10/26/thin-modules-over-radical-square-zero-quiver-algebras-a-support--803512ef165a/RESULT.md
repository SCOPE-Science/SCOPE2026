# Thin modules over radical-square-zero quiver algebras: a support-graph enumerator

## Finding

Let \(Q=(Q_0,Q_1,s,t)\) be any finite quiver, allowing loops and parallel arrows, let \(\mathbb F_q\) be a finite field, and let \(A=\mathbb F_q Q/J^2\), where \(J\) is the ideal generated by all arrows. Consider finite-dimensional \(A\)-modules \(M\) whose dimension at every vertex is exactly one.

Call a subset \(S\subseteq Q_1\) admissible when it contains no composable pair of arrows. Let \(\underline S\) be the undirected multigraph with vertex set \(Q_0\) and one edge for each arrow in \(S\), with orientations forgotten but parallel edges retained. Define \(e(S)=|S|\), \(v=|Q_0|\), \(c(S)=\kappa(\underline S)\) (including isolated vertices), and \(\beta(S)=e(S)-v+c(S)\). Then the exact isomorphism-class enumerator, graded by the dimension of the endomorphism algebra, is

\[
\boxed{\mathcal P_Q(q,z)=\sum_{S\subseteq Q_1\atop S\text{ admissible}}(q-1)^{\beta(S)}z^{c(S)}}.
\]

For every module in the stratum indexed by \(S\),

\[
\operatorname{End}_A(M)\cong \mathbb F_q^{\,c(S)},\qquad
|\operatorname{Aut}_A(M)|=(q-1)^{c(S)}.
\]

In particular, the coefficient of \(z^c\) counts exactly the isomorphism classes with endomorphism-algebra dimension \(c\); the coefficient of \(z\) counts the indecomposable modules of dimension vector \(\mathbf 1\). The formula is a finite polynomial for every finite quiver, and treats parallel arrows as distinct edges.

## Assumptions and scope

The dimension vector is fixed to \(\mathbf 1\), so every vertex space is one-dimensional over \(\mathbb F_q\). The algebra is the ordinary path algebra quotient by the square of the arrow ideal, not a quotient with additional relations. The count is for isomorphism classes of modules, not for labeled arrow assignments, and it does not count modules with larger vertex dimensions. Loops cannot belong to an admissible support because a nonzero loop scalar would have nonzero square in a field. Isolated vertices are retained when computing \(c(S)\).

## Proof

**1. The possible supports.** A representation with dimension vector \(\mathbf 1\) assigns a scalar \(x_\alpha\in\mathbb F_q\) to each arrow \(\alpha\). The relation \(J^2=0\) requires \(x_\beta x_\alpha=0\) whenever \(t(\alpha)=s(\beta)\). Since \(\mathbb F_q\) is a field, two composable arrows cannot both receive nonzero scalars. Thus the support \(S=\{\alpha:x_\alpha\ne0\}\) is admissible. Conversely, every admissible support, with arbitrary nonzero labels on its arrows and zero labels elsewhere, satisfies all length-two relations.

**2. Isomorphism classes at fixed support.** The base-change group is \(G=(\mathbb F_q^\times)^{Q_0}\). It acts on arrow labels by
\[
(g\cdot x)_\alpha=g_{t(\alpha)}x_\alpha g_{s(\alpha)}^{-1}.
\]
For a fixed support \(S\), there are \((q-1)^{e(S)}\) labelings. A vertex-scaling tuple stabilizes a labeling exactly when its coordinates agree across every edge in \(\underline S\), hence are constant on each connected component. The stabilizer therefore has size \((q-1)^{c(S)}\), and each orbit has size \((q-1)^{v-c(S)}\). The number of isomorphism classes with support exactly \(S\) is
\[
\frac{(q-1)^{e(S)}}{(q-1)^{v-c(S)}}=(q-1)^{e(S)-v+c(S)}=(q-1)^{\beta(S)}.
\]

**3. Endomorphisms and automorphisms.** An endomorphism of a dimension-vector-\(\mathbf 1\) representation is a tuple of scalars \((\lambda_u)_{u\in Q_0}\) satisfying \(\lambda_{t(\alpha)}x_\alpha=x_\alpha\lambda_{s(\alpha)}\) for every arrow. On each active edge, this forces equality of the endpoint scalars; on inactive arrows it imposes no condition. Thus the scalars are independently constant on the \(c(S)\) connected components, and multiplication is componentwise. Hence \(\operatorname{End}_A(M)\cong\mathbb F_q^{c(S)}\), whose unit group has order \((q-1)^{c(S)}\). Summing the fixed-support orbit counts with weight \(z^{c(S)}\) proves the boxed formula.

## Verification

The included standard-library script `verify.py` exhaustively enumerates all arrow assignments for four small quivers over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\). It checks the length-two relations, computes vertex-scaling orbits, and verifies the predicted number of orbits for each support as well as the endomorphism and automorphism counts. It reports `VERIFY_OK: 8 exhaustive finite-field/quiver cases`. These finite checks corroborate the calculation for those examples only; the all-quiver statement follows from the proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Helleloid and Rodriguez-Villegas, *Counting Quiver Representations over Finite Fields Via Graph Enumeration* (arXiv:0810.2127, first public version 2008-10-12), study counts of absolutely indecomposable representations of an unbound quiver through Hua's formula and connected-graph enumeration. Their object and statistic differ: the present formula counts all modules of one-dimensional vertex spaces subject to the additional relations \(J^2=0\), and records the endomorphism-algebra dimension and automorphism-group order in each support stratum. The graph-orbit calculation here is explicit and does not follow merely from the unbound-quiver count.

Nasr-Isfahani, *Singular equivalence of finite dimensional algebras with radical square zero* (arXiv:1603.04191, first public version 2016-03-14), studies quiver operations preserving singular equivalence of the associated square-zero algebras. It provides adjacent structural context, but does not state this thin-module orbit enumerator. No absolute priority claim is made; a similar support-graph formula may appear in older literature under thin representations or torus-orbit terminology.

## Limitations

The result is restricted to the single dimension vector \(\mathbf 1\) and to the square-zero arrow ideal. It does not give counts for arbitrary dimension vectors, higher radical powers, or isomorphism classes under additional relations. The support admissibility condition depends on arrow orientation even though \(\beta(S)\) uses the underlying undirected multigraph. The finite checker is not a proof for all quivers or fields. The originality assessment remains provisional because the underlying base-change orbit argument is elementary and older equivalent formulations cannot be ruled out exhaustively.
