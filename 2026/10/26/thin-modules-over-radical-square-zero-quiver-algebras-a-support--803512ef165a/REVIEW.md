# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For each dimension-vector-\(\mathbf 1\) representation, the arrow scalars satisfy the square-zero relations exactly when the nonzero support has no composable pair. The vertex-scaling action preserves support. Its stabilizer consists of one nonzero scalar per connected component, so the orbit size is \((q-1)^{|Q_0|-c(S)}\) and the orbit count is \((q-1)^{|S|-|Q_0|+c(S)}\). The endomorphism equations force vertex scalars to be constant on each active component, giving the claimed product algebra and unit-group order. This proves the formula for every finite quiver and every finite field; the finite checks are supplementary.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

The full relevant sections of Helleloid–Rodriguez-Villegas (arXiv:0810.2127) were compared. That paper counts absolutely indecomposable representations of unbound quivers and studies graph-enumeration coefficients; it does not provide the all-isomorphism-class distribution for the bound algebra \(\mathbb F_q Q/J^2\) by endomorphism-algebra dimension. Nasr-Isfahani (arXiv:1603.04191) studies singular equivalences induced by quiver operations, not this orbit count. Targeted searches for thin representations, support-graph cycle-rank weights, and radical-square-zero finite-field orbit counts did not locate an equal statement. Because the underlying torus-orbit argument is elementary, an older equivalent formula under different terminology remains a real priority risk.

## Value — PASS

Thin modules are a canonical dimension-vector stratum in quiver representation theory. The formula gives the entire finite-field isomorphism-class count and refines it by endomorphism-algebra dimension; equivalently, it gives each stratum's automorphism-group order. This refinement is useful in finite-field representation enumeration and automorphism-weighted module sums, and it isolates the precise role of the support graph's cycle rank. The result is a complete all-quiver formula, not a recomputation of a single finite table.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest inspected source is Helleloid–Rodriguez-Villegas, arXiv:0810.2127, which treats absolutely indecomposable representations of an unbound quiver. The new formula instead imposes the relations \(J^2=0\) and includes decomposable modules and automorphism weights. Nasr-Isfahani, arXiv:1603.04191, supplies related context on square-zero quiver algebras but concerns singular equivalence. Older thin-representation and torus-orbit literature may contain an equivalent formulation; novelty is provisional, not a uniqueness theorem. The result does not cover higher dimension vectors or higher radical powers.
