# Scientific review: finite Grassmann adjacency graphs

## Correctness — PASS

The exact domain is all prime-power field orders \(q\ge2\), dimensions \(n\ge2\), and proper positive subspace ranks. The proof first reduces by annihilator duality, then uses a three-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé profile lemma with its necessary diagonal information. Explicit, dimension-checked subspace witnesses realize each claimed profile flag; a dimension inequality excludes the one impossible flag for subspace distance at least three. Each distance occurs, giving exactly the three theories. Concrete rank-three distinguishing sentences and Gaussian-binomial monotonicity prove sharpness. The two standalone verifiers reproduce orbit profiles and independent direct logical types in finite examples. The proof, not the finite computations, covers arbitrary finite fields.

## Originality — PASS, with qualifications

Closest inspected primary literature: Pankov (arXiv:1003.3329, full accessible text) classifies isometric Johnson-to-Grassmann embeddings; it does not equate Grassmann rank-three theories. Naimi–Shaw (arXiv:1008.0595, complete accessible HTML) analyzes Johnson-induced subgraphs. Pikhurko–Verbitsky (arXiv:1003.4865, accessible full text) provides general finite-graph logical methods, not this Grassmann classification. The existing rank-three classification of strongly regular graphs covers the diameter-two subfamily in substance; it is not being counted as a wholly new component. The existing classification of ordinary Johnson graphs concerns different ambient objects and does not imply uniform first-order equivalence of Grassmann graphs. The new contribution is the finite-field-wide classification, especially all diameter-at-least-three cases and the sharp Grassmann representative bound. Relevant research-index and title/alias searches found no directly dominating result. The possibility of unindexed or inaccessible equivalent work remains a risk, and absence from a search is not proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS

This is a complete classification of a canonical infinite family from finite geometry under an exact, motivated logical invariant, with no restriction on field characteristic or size. The structural witness lemma explains why nontrivial field and dimension parameters vanish at rank three. The threshold between subspace distances two and three is sharp in quantifier rank, and the internal \(1395\)-vertex testing bound is optimal; cross-family Johnson representatives provide a substantially smaller external logical kernel. The claim concerns a natural family and a natural full invariant, not an arbitrarily selected finite computation or a small numerical improvement.

## Verification status and remaining limitations

Algebraic proof and finite-field examples were checked in this review. The verifier is not an independent proof for all field orders. No claim is made about logics with counting, named incidence relations, or quantifier rank four or higher. Relevant literature could remain unavailable or unindexed.
