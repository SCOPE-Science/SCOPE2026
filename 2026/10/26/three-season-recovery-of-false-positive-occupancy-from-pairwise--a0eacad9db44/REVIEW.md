# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** Stationary two-state Markov covariance is \(\pi(1-\pi)\lambda^{j-i}\); conditionally independent Bernoulli observation at each season scales that covariance by \((d_i-f)(d_j-f)\). Equality of two adjusted consecutive correlations gives a unique unknown false-positive rate when \(d_0\ne d_2\). The remaining parameters follow from two covariance ratios; direct substitution proves the converse. Continuous non-identifiability at equal outer-season detection and at two visits is proved by explicit open-parameter perturbation, not by parameter counting alone. The exact-rational verification checks both signs of the latent eigenvalue and a concrete pair of equal-moment distinct models.

**Originality — PASS, with unresolved bibliographic risk.** Royle–Link (2006) treats false-positive occupancy mixtures, not this dynamic three-wave moment inverse. Fukaya–Royle (2013) estimates Markov ecological transitions using a different mislocation observation model rather than the common false-positive and variable-true-positive scheme. Cole (2019) addresses HMM identifiability from full distributions with homogeneous emissions. Liu–Culpepper (2025) considers heterogeneous HMMs but gives general sufficient full-law identifiability conditions with repeated-emission requirements, not the exact pairwise-moment boundary or inverse here. An earlier specialized covariance identification formula may exist; the Royle–Link primary full text was not materially accessible in this session, so its abstract is not treated as proof of noncoverage. Accessible historical claims concern the zero-false-positive case or show nonidentification in an unconstrained false-positive class, neither of which implies nor contradicts the present restricted-design theorem.

**Value — PASS.** False-positive records are a significant issue in wildlife surveys. The sharp design rule identifies a minimal way to recover transition and detection rates with only three single visits using first- and second-order summaries, while the exact failure boundary warns against relying on unchanged effort at the two outer seasons. The result is not merely a generic identifiability assertion: explicit rational inversion and model-feasibility conditions are provided. This does not establish low estimator variance or a preferred field protocol under finite sample sizes.

The closest materials are Royle–Link (2006), Fukaya–Royle (2013), Cole (2019), and Liu–Culpepper (2025). Inaccessibility of one plausible full text and possible alternative terminology leave residual novelty risk; independent bibliographic and mathematical evaluation remain necessary.
