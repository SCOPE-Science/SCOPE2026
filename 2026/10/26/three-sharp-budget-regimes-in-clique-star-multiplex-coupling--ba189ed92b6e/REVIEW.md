Concavity permits exact symmetry averaging. The full spectrum reduces to two explicit two-by-two blocks plus \(0,n\); monotonicity yields the unique active crossing and the two rational budget thresholds. A separate test-subspace average proves hub-link extinction and uniqueness for **all** nonsymmetric designs in the final regime. Exact characteristic identities and full-matrix spectra were replayed with `verify_multiplex.py` (707 finite checks). The finite checks are supporting evidence, not an infinite proof.

Network Science article covers general interlink optimization but does not derive these formulas for the center-aligned clique–star case. Shakeri et al. (arXiv:1903.01073, 2019; Phys. Rev. E 102, 022302, 2020) is a material residual risk: the preprint, public OA routes, and institutional retrieval did not yield full text. Search and the absence of matching titles do not establish universal novelty.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The motivating design problem is exactly the established one-to-one resource-constrained network coupling problem, not an arbitrary graph statistic. The second sharp transition reveals the non-obvious global optimality of deleting an apparently useful center-to-center coupling as the budget grows. The explicit all-budget solution, the global rigidity of the final phase, and the exceptional \(n=3\) boundary provide a structural classification absent from the generic linear-regime statement. No application performance beyond the stated Laplacian spectral gap is claimed.

**Limitations.** Only the specified clique, star, alignment, and fixed unit intralayer weights are addressed. Arbitrary-design uniqueness in the interior regime remains open here. Full-text comparison with the 2020 article remains incomplete; independent auditing and external confirmation are not claimed.
