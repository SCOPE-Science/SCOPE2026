# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The graph convention is explicit: distinct edges of the cube share an endpoint. The incidence identity \(F=B^{\mathsf T}B\), group-algebra relation \(L=BB^{\mathsf T}=\times\sum y_i\), and augmentation-ideal argument prove \(F^3=0\) and both power ranks for every integer \(d\ge2\). In particular, the nonstandard second-rank step requires that the all-ones vertex vector is in the image \(sI\), and this is explicitly witnessed by \(s\prod_{i=2}^dy_i=\prod_{i=1}^dy_i\). Rank-nullity and Jordan block identities then supply exact time and block populations. Finite direct-adjacency tests cover dimensions two through eight; the first two dimensions receive exhaustive state tests. No claim of an infinite computational proof is made.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Fish–Key–Mwambene's 2010 primary work on binary codes of these exact line graphs contains the **already known** adjacency rank and code parameters; this first rank is explicitly excluded from the originality claim. Muthivhi's accessible 2013 thesis Chapter 5, Sections 5.1–5.3, revisits the same rank and incidence identity, but the inspected chapter does not give a second-power rank, nilpotent Jordan decomposition, or time-resolved extinction census. General linear cellular automata and published distance-two-cube parity results do not imply this operator-specific refinement. The 2010 article's complete text remained inaccessible, leaving a specified historical coverage risk; failure to access that paper is not treated as proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The line graph of the hypercube is a canonical edge-interaction network already studied in binary coding theory. Distinguishing an adjacency code's dimension from its **iterated image filtration** is substantive: the rank \(2^d-2\) does not determine the second rank \(2^{d-1}-2\), fixed length-two Jordan multiplicity two, sharp three-step nilpotence, or the proportion of edge states requiring that final update. These formulas provide an infinite-dimensional family-wide dynamical classification and a transparent bridge between coding parameters and finite-state extinction, not a narrow arbitrary numerical slice.

## Closest literature and scope limits
Most closely related: Fish, Key and Mwambene (2010), DOI 10.1016/j.jsc.2010.03.012; and Muthivhi (2013), University of the Western Cape, Chapter 5. The former is an unresolved full-text-access limitation; the latter was inspected in relevant full sections. Comparisons also considered published parity dynamics on distance-two Boolean cubes, a different operator. The proof is for binary XOR on simple undirected line graphs. Computational tests are finite, not an exhaustive test of all dimensions.
