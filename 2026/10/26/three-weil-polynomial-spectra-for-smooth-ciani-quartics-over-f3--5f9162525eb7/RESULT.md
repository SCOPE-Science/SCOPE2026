# Three Weil-polynomial spectra for smooth Ciani quartics over \(\mathbf F_3\)

## Finding
Consider the following naturally marked Ciani linear system over \(k=\mathbf F_3\):
\[
C_{a,b,c,d,e,f}:\quad aX^4+bY^4+cZ^4+dX^2Y^2+eX^2Z^2+fY^2Z^2=0,
\qquad (a,b,c,d,e,f)\in k^6\setminus\{0\}.
\]
Equations differing by a common nonzero scalar are counted once. Exactly **16** scalar-normalized equations define geometrically smooth curves. They represent exactly **three** projective-isomorphism classes over \(k\), distinguished by their rational-point counts and Weil polynomials:

| Representative | Number of scalar-normalized equations | \(\#C(k)\) | Jacobian \(3\)-rank | Weil numerator \(L_C(T)\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| \(C_0: X^4+Y^4+Z^4=0\) | 4 | 4 | 0 | \((1+3T^2)^3\) |
| \(C_+: X^4+Y^4-Z^4+Y^2Z^2=0\) | 6 | 2 | 1 | \((1-2T+3T^2)(1+3T^2)^2\) |
| \(C_-: X^4+Y^4-Z^4-Y^2Z^2=0\) | 6 | 6 | 1 | \((1+2T+3T^2)(1+3T^2)^2\) |

Here \(L_C(T)=\det(1-T\operatorname{Frob}_3\mid H^1_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(C_{\overline{k}},\mathbf Q_\ell))\), for any \(\ell\ne3\). Thus the Jacobian of every smooth curve in this six-monomial family is nonordinary: **the \(3\)-rank is at most one**, and its Weil polynomial has two supersingular elliptic factors. These are statements about the marked six-monomial family, not all plane quartics or all possible twists of Ciani curves.

## Assumptions and scope
Geometric smoothness means nonsingularity over an algebraic closure of \(k\). A nonzero scalar multiple defines the same embedded curve. Projective equivalence is under \(\mathrm{PGL}_3(k)\), with transformations not required to preserve the displayed monomial subspace. The family is characterized by the simultaneous sign involutions of the coordinates and their fixed eigencoordinates over \(k\); other forms with a geometrically defined but non-split Klein-four symmetry are not classified here. A smooth plane quartic has genus three.

## Proof
**1. Exact geometric-smoothness criterion.** Write \(F\) for the displayed quartic. Because the characteristic is three, Euler's identity gives \(F=XF_X+YF_Y+ZF_Z\). Consequently a projective point is singular precisely when all three partial derivatives vanish there. These derivatives are
\[
F_X=X(aX^2-dY^2-eZ^2),\quad
F_Y=Y(bY^2-dX^2-fZ^2),\quad
F_Z=Z(cZ^2-eX^2-fY^2).
\]
If any of \(a,b,c\) vanishes, the corresponding coordinate vertex is singular. Hence \(a,b,c\in\{1,-1\}\). At a possible singular point with nonzero coordinates supported on \(S\subseteq\{X,Y,Z\}\), the squares of the coordinates form an all-nonzero kernel vector for the principal submatrix indexed by \(S\) of
\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}a&-d&-e\\-d&b&-f\\-e&-f&c\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Every nonzero element of the algebraic closure has a square root, so this condition is also sufficient for a singularity. On a two-coordinate support, nonzero off-diagonal coefficient \(d\) gives a singularity if and only if \(ab=d^2=1\), namely if \(a=b\). Thus a smooth form can have a mixed coefficient only between indices with opposite diagonal signs. Among three signs, at most two such pairs exist. If both corresponding coefficients are nonzero, after multiplying by a common scalar and permuting coordinates we have \(a=b=1\), \(c=-1\), \(d=0\), and \(e,f\in\{1,-1\}\). Then \(B(e,f,1)^t=0\), giving a singular point with all coordinates nonzero. Conversely, if at most one mixed coefficient is nonzero and it connects opposite signs, every principal submatrix of \(B\) is invertible: the only nontrivial two-by-two determinant is \((-1)-1=1\in k\), and the three-by-three determinant is its product with the remaining nonzero diagonal entry. Hence there is no singular point.

The necessary-and-sufficient criterion is therefore: \(a,b,c\ne0\), and either \(d=e=f=0\) or **exactly one** among \(d,e,f\) is nonzero, joining opposite diagonal signs. There are eight coefficient vectors of the first type and 24 of the second: the latter choose one of the six sign triples having one exceptional sign, one of two opposite-sign coordinate pairs, and one of two nonzero coefficients. Dividing by the two scalars gives \(4+12=16\) equations.

**2. Projective equivalence.** Permutations and multiplication of \(F\) by a scalar reduce each one-mixed-coefficient form to exactly one of \(C_+\) and \(C_-\). For diagonal forms, all-equal coefficients already give \(C_0\), while the two-equal-one-opposite case reduces to \(X^4+Y^4-Z^4\). In characteristic three,
\[
(U+V)^4+(U-V)^4+W^4=-(U^4+V^4-W^4),
\]
and the linear substitution on the left is invertible, so this case is equivalent to \(C_0\). Distinctness of the three classes follows from their different rational-point counts computed in step 3.

**3. Weil polynomials and rational points.** All three prototypes admit the Klein-four action generated by coordinate sign changes. The quotient by the entire group is the smooth conic obtained by setting \(U=X^2,V=Y^2,W=Z^2\) in the defining equation; it has genus zero. Each of the three index-two quotients is a smooth genus-one curve. Because the group order is prime to three, the usual character-space decomposition of \(H^1\) (equivalently, the standard decomposition by the three elliptic quotients) gives
\[
L_C(T)=L_{E_X}(T)L_{E_Y}(T)L_{E_Z}(T).
\]
For \(C_\sigma: X^4+Y^4-Z^4+\sigma Y^2Z^2=0\), where \(\sigma\in\{1,-1\}\), weighted-projective models of these quotients are
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_X:&\quad U^2=-Y^4+Z^4-\sigma Y^2Z^2,\\
E_Y:&\quad U^2=-X^4-Z^4,\\
E_Z:&\quad U^2=X^4-Y^4.
\end{aligned}
\]
These follow by taking the square of the sign-flipped variable as a weight-two coordinate and completing the square where necessary. Each has a degree-two map to \(\mathbf P^1_k\). Since a nonzero element of \(k\) is a square exactly when it is \(1\), direct evaluation at its four rational base points gives
\[
(\#E_X(k),\#E_Y(k),\#E_Z(k))=
\begin{cases}(2,4,4)&\sigma=1,\\
(6,4,4)&\sigma=-1.\end{cases}
\]
For \(C_0\), all three quotients have four rational points. A genus-one curve over this field has a rational point, and the associated elliptic Weil factor is \(1-(4-\#E(k))T+3T^2\). This proves all three displayed Weil polynomials, from which \(\#C(k)=4-\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{Frob}_3\mid H^1)\) follows. Finally an elliptic curve over \(\mathbf F_3\) with trace divisible by three is supersingular, whereas traces \(\pm2\) are ordinary. Isogeny invariance of \(p\)-rank gives exactly ranks \(0,1,1\).

## Verification
Two standalone programs are included. `check_smoothness.py` uses exact Gröbner bases over \(\mathbf F_3\) on all three projective affine charts to test the complete set of **728 nonzero coefficient vectors**, and confirms the algebraic criterion for each. `check_zeta.py`, written using only the Python standard library, independently evaluates every smooth sextuple over \(\mathbf F_3\), \(\mathbf F_9\), and \(\mathbf F_{27}\), reconstructs each genus-three Weil polynomial from three point counts and the functional equation, and reproduces the three table rows. Run `python check_smoothness.py` and `python check_zeta.py` in a directory containing those scripts. The exact recorded outputs are in `verification_output.txt`; the 16 scalar-normalized vectors are recorded in `smooth_coefficients.json`. The displayed proof establishes infinite-field geometric smoothness and the zeta factors independently of finite-point experimentation.

## Relationship to prior work
Ohashi, *On the maximality of genus-3 nonhyperelliptic curves of Ciani type* (arXiv:2201.12996v1, 31 January 2022), gives a smoothness criterion for the standard form with all three diagonal coefficients equal to one, constructs elliptic quotients and establishes maximality/minimality under a **superspecial** hypothesis. The present classification permits arbitrary diagonal coefficients in \(\mathbf F_3^\times\) and includes the non-superspecial \(3\)-rank-one cases; it also supplies their exact \(\mathbf F_3\)-rational equivalence classes and Weil factors. The elliptic-quotient method itself is not new.

Meagher and Top, *Twists of genus three curves over finite fields* (2010), study twists of the Fermat quartic and characterize their arithmetic, but their explicit Fermat-twist theorem in Section 7 assumes characteristic different from three. Their work concerns a broader twist framework and does not state this fixed six-monomial, characteristic-three classification. Lercier, Ritzenthaler, Rovetta and Sijsling (2014), *Parametrizing the moduli space of curves and applications to smooth plane quartics over finite fields*, work with characteristic exceeding seven for their main parametrization and experimentally list primes starting at eleven. Neither result directly implies the coefficient counts and non-superspecial alternatives here without additional analysis.

## Limitations
This is an exact classification **inside the displayed coefficient family**, not a classification of arbitrary smooth plane quartics, arbitrary \(\mathbf F_3\)-forms of the Fermat quartic, or all genus-three curves with a geometric Klein-four action. The numerical counts are for fixed-coordinate equations modulo scalar, although the result also identifies three projective-isomorphism classes among them. The proof makes no assertion of publication priority; comprehensive historical and supplementary-database comparison remains desirable. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
