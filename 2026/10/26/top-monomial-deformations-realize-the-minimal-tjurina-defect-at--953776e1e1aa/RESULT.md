# Top-monomial deformations realize the minimal Tjurina defect at the 1-liminal boundary

## Finding

Let \(m\ge 6\), and let integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_m\ge 2\) satisfy
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{a_i}=2.
\]
Set
\[
f_0=\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_i^{a_i},\qquad g=\prod_{i=1}^{m}x_i^{a_i-2},\qquad F=f_0+g.
\]
Then the germ \(F^{-1}(0)\subset(\mathbb C^m,0)\) is an isolated hypersurface singularity of dimension \(m-1\), with minimal exponent \(\widetilde\alpha_F=2\). Consequently it is \(1\)-Du Bois but not \(1\)-rational; this is what is meant here by \(1\)-liminal. Its Milnor and Tjurina numbers are
\[
\mu(F)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(a_i-1),\qquad
\tau(F)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(a_i-1)-1.
\]
Thus \(\mu(F)-\tau(F)=1\), the smallest possible positive Tjurina defect for a non-quasihomogeneous isolated hypersurface singularity.

This family is nonempty in every dimension \(m-1\ge5\): take \(a_1=a_2=a_3=2\) and \(a_4=\cdots=a_m=2(m-3)\). In dimension five, this gives the explicit germ
\[
F=u^2+v^2+w^2+x^6+y^6+z^6+x^4y^4z^4=0,
\]
for which \(\widetilde\alpha_F=2\), \(\mu(F)=125\), and \(\tau(F)=124\).

## Assumptions and scope

All germs are complex analytic. The exponent tuple consists of integers at least two, has length \(m\ge6\), and satisfies the reciprocal-sum condition above. The claim concerns this top-monomial positive-weight deformation, not arbitrary deformations and not a classification of all \(1\)-liminal singularities.

## Proof

Give \(x_i\) weight \(w_i=1/a_i\). Then \(f_0\) has weighted degree one and \(\sum_iw_i=2\). The monomial \(g\) has weighted degree
\[
 d=\sum_i\frac{a_i-2}{a_i}=m-2\sum_i\frac1{a_i}=m-4>1.
\]
Thus \(F\) is a positive-weight deformation of the isolated weighted-homogeneous singularity \(f_0\). Its Newton boundary is unchanged by \(g\), since the exponent vector of \(g\) lies strictly above the compact face \(\sum_i e_i/a_i=1\). The face polynomials are sums of pure powers and are nondegenerate. The Newton-spectrum formula therefore gives \(\widetilde\alpha_F=\sum_iw_i=2\). Equivalently, the positive-weight-deformation extension of the weighted-homogeneous criterion in Friedman–Laza applies. For isolated hypersurfaces, \(k\)-Du Bois is equivalent to \(\widetilde\alpha_F\ge k+1\), whereas \(k\)-rational is equivalent to \(\widetilde\alpha_F>k+1\). At \(k=1\), equality gives the claimed \(1\)-Du Bois but not \(1\)-rational conclusion. The semiquasihomogeneous theorem also ensures that the germ remains isolated.

The associated graded Milnor algebra of \(F\), for the weight filtration, is
\[
Q_{f_0}=\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_m]/(x_1^{a_1-1},\ldots,x_m^{a_m-1}).
\]
It has dimension \(\prod_i(a_i-1)\), so semiquasihomogeneous invariance gives \(\mu(F)=\prod_i(a_i-1)\). Its top weighted degree is \(d=m-4\), and its one-dimensional socle is generated by the monomial \(g\). Hence the class \([g]\) is nonzero in the Milnor algebra \(Q_F=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^m,0}/(\partial_1F,\ldots,\partial_mF)\) and is annihilated by the maximal ideal: this follows from the associated graded identification and the absence of graded pieces above degree \(d\).

The weighted Euler identity is
\[
\sum_i w_i x_i\,\partial_iF=f_0+d g.
\]
Modulo the Jacobian ideal, it yields \([f_0]=-d[g]\), and therefore
\[
[F]=[f_0+g]=(1-d)[g]=(5-m)[g].
\]
Since \(m\ge6\), the scalar \(5-m\) is nonzero. Thus \([F]\) generates exactly the one-dimensional socle ideal of \(Q_F\). The Tjurina algebra is \(Q_F/([F])\), so its dimension is \(\tau(F)=\mu(F)-1\). In particular \(\mu(F)>\tau(F)\), and the singularity is not analytically quasihomogeneous by Saito's \(\mu=\tau\) criterion. Since a non-quasihomogeneous isolated hypersurface singularity has positive integer defect, the value one is minimal.

For the displayed dimension-five example, the reciprocal sum is \(3/2+3/6=2\), the top monomial is \(x^4y^4z^4\), and the Milnor number is \((2-1)^3(6-1)^3=125\). The accompanying Gröbner-basis verifier computes the Tjurina quotient dimension as \(124\).

## Verification

The proof establishes the general family and both invariant formulas. The included `artifacts/verify.py` independently replays the finite Tjurina-algebra computation for the six-variable example by computing a lexicographic Gröbner basis for the reduced three-variable Tjurina ideal and counting its standard monomials. The script reports pure leading-monomial bounds \((5,6,6)\), a standard-monomial count of \(124\), and `VERIFY_OK`. The Milnor number \(125\) is obtained from the principal weighted-homogeneous part and semiquasihomogeneous invariance. The script does not purport to certify the general theorem or replace the Newton-spectrum and singularity-theory inputs.

## Relationship to prior work

Friedman–Laza characterize higher Du Bois and higher rational isolated hypersurface singularities and state the weighted-homogeneous criterion in terms of the sum of weights; their discussion also explains its extension to positive-weight deformations. That theory supplies the qualitative \(1\)-liminal boundary used here. It does not state this top-monomial family or the exact Tjurina-defect formula \(\tau=\mu-1\). The new calculation is the socle argument showing that the defining equation represents a nonzero socle generator in the Milnor algebra. Existing nearby Milnor–Tjurina calculations concern different hypersurfaces and do not imply this formula.

## Limitations

The result gives a construction, not a classification of all \(1\)-liminal singularities or of all isolated hypersurface singularities with defect one. The minimal-exponent conclusion uses the standard Newton-spectrum formula for convenient Newton-nondegenerate germs and the higher-singularity criteria cited below. The explicit Tjurina computation is replayed for one representative; the general formula follows from the weighted-filtration and Euler-identity proof. Independent audit has not been performed.
