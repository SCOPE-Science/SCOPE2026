# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim applies to every integer \(n\ge2\). The complete proof starts from the cited distance-two characterization of total mutual visibility in Hamming graphs. Each layer permits at most one vertex from each binary parity class; labels in distinct layers obey an exact complement constraint. Encoding this as a bipartite graph gives exactly four assignments per component. The connected and two-component cases are exhaustive, leading to the displayed polynomial with nonoverlapping corrections. Its highest-degree terms give the maximum and rigidity, with the \(n=2\) exception stated separately. Independent distance-two subset enumeration for \(1\le n\le6\), and sampled direct geodesic checks for \(1\le n\le3\), passed. The finite computations support, but do not replace, the proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified priority assessment)

The closest primary source [1] proves that total visibility in Hamming graphs equals distance-two avoidance. That theorem is used openly here. Its exact maximum formulas concern three-factor products, and its general higher-dimensional statements are bounds/asymptotics, not the four-factor \(Q_3\square K_n\) polynomial or the threshold rigidity. The later visibility-polynomial study [2] defines the invariant and an abstract inclusion–exclusion identity but gives no identified equivalent formula for this family. Search comparisons considered total mutual visibility, distance-two avoiding codes, Hamming graph product aliases, and published nearby graph-polynomial results. The remaining priority risk is an unindexed or differently named distance-two code enumeration; it is not represented as excluded with certainty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This gives the full size distribution of allowable geodesic-obstacle configurations for an infinite four-factor Hamming family, refining prior focus on maximum size and making visible a nontrivial threshold: all sufficiently large configurations concentrate in one of four antipodal cylinders. The exact exceptional count on \(Q_4\) and explicit coefficients are reproducible. The family is natural as the first mixed-alphabet extension of a binary three-cube in the published Hamming-visibility setting, rather than an arbitrary finite parameter slice.
