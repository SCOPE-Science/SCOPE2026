# Triple-orbit recovery and numerical-profile collisions in reversal–exchange reducts of the random permutation

## Finding

Let \(\Pi=(D;<_{1},<_{2})\) be the countable homogeneous structure whose age consists of all finite sets carrying two linear orders. Let \(G=\operatorname{Aut}(\Pi)\). There are natural transformations of its finite labelled two-order types: \(R_1\) reverses the first order, \(R_2\) reverses the second, and \(S\) exchanges the orders. They generate the eight-element dihedral group \(W=\langle R_1,R_2,S\rangle\), with \(R_1R_2=R_2R_1\) and \(SR_1S=R_2\). For each of the ten subgroups \(H\le W\), let \(G_H\) be the corresponding closed permutation supergroup of \(G\), obtained by adjoining the indicated order symmetries. Define \(s(H)=|H\cap\{S,R_1R_2S\}|\).

For every integer \(n\ge2\), the number \(a_H(n)\) of \(G_H\)-orbits on injective ordered \(n\)-tuples is exactly
\[
a_H(n)=\frac{(n!)^2+s(H)n!}{|H|}.
\]
For a singleton tuple, \(a_H(1)=1\). For ordered tuples with repetitions, \(b_H(0)=1\) and, for \(m\ge1\),
\[
b_H(m)=\sum_{n=1}^{m}\left\{\begin{matrix}m\\n\end{matrix}\right\}a_H(n),
\]
where braces denote a Stirling number of the second kind.

The ten subgroup actions have exactly **six** different numerical orbit profiles, and these are already distinguished by injective triples. The complete collision and arity table is:

| Subgroup \(H\) | \(|H|\) | \(s(H)\) | \(a_H(2)\) | \(a_H(3)\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(\{1\}\) | 1 | 0 | 4 | 36 |
| \(\langle R_1\rangle\) | 2 | 0 | 2 | 18 |
| \(\langle R_2\rangle\) | 2 | 0 | 2 | 18 |
| \(\langle R_1R_2\rangle\) | 2 | 0 | 2 | 18 |
| \(\langle S\rangle\) | 2 | 1 | 3 | 21 |
| \(\langle R_1R_2S\rangle\) | 2 | 1 | 3 | 21 |
| \(\langle R_1,R_2\rangle\) | 4 | 0 | 1 | 9 |
| \(\langle R_1S\rangle\) | 4 | 0 | 1 | 9 |
| \(\langle S,R_1R_2\rangle\) | 4 | 2 | 2 | 12 |
| \(W\) | 8 | 2 | 1 | 6 |

Although orbit **counts** fail to identify four of the ten groups even when every arity is known, the actual orbit **partition** on injective triples uniquely identifies every subgroup. This is sharp: injective pairs yield only seven different orbit partitions. Thus the least arity recovering all ten groups from their full orbit equivalence relations is \(3\), whereas no collection of scalar orbit counts can recover all ten.

## Assumptions and scope

All tuples are ordered by their coordinate labels, not quotiented by permutations of tuple positions. The two orders of \(\Pi\) are independent dense orders without endpoints. Group actions are on the fixed countable domain; \(H\) acts only on the finite set of two-order types after quotienting by \(G\). The theorem concerns the ten reversal–exchange supergroups, not the entire lattice of 39 closed supergroups classified by Linman and Pinsker.

Each of \(R_1,R_2,S\) has a domain-permutation lift because reversing either dense order or exchanging the two orders produces another copy of \(\Pi\). These lifts normalize \(G\), and the group \(K\) generated by the lifts and \(G\) has quotient \(K/G\cong W\): an element acting trivially on both order relations belongs to \(G\). Since \(K\) is the union of eight closed cosets of \(G\), it is closed. Every inverse image \(G_H\) is therefore a distinct closed intermediate group.

## Proof

**Finite type reduction.** By homogeneity and the Fraïssé extension property, a labelled injective \(n\)-tuple is characterized under \(G\) by its pair of strict total orders on its \(n\) coordinate labels. Every pair occurs. Writing \(\mathcal L_n\) for the \(n!\) labelled orders, the orbit-type set is \(X_n=\mathcal L_n\times\mathcal L_n\). Passing from \(G\) to \(G_H\) merges precisely the types in the \(H\)-orbits of \(X_n\).

**Fixed types and the numerical formula.** For \(n\ge2\), no strict total order on \(n\) labelled elements equals its reverse. The identity in \(W\) fixes all \((n!)^2\) pairs. A nonidentity transformation without exchanging the orders reverses at least one order, hence fixes no pair. A transformation exchanging orders can be written \((L_1,L_2)\mapsto(R^aL_2,R^bL_1)\) with \(a,b\in\{0,1\}\) and \(R\) reversing an order. A fixed pair satisfies \(L_1=R^aL_2\), \(L_2=R^bL_1\). For \(n\ge2\), this is possible exactly when \(a=b\), giving \(n!\) fixed pairs in either of the two cases \(S\) and \(R_1R_2S\). Burnside's lemma gives the boxed formula. Enumerating the ten subgroups of \(W\) gives the table and the six pairs of invariants \((|H|,s(H))\): \((1,0),(2,0),(2,1),(4,0),(4,2),(8,2)\).

**Why the six numerical profiles are genuinely different.** If two expressions coincide for all \(n\ge2\), divide the equality by \((n!)^2\) and let \(n\to\infty\) to recover equal subgroup orders. Substituting back recovers equal \(s(H)\). Conversely, equal pairs \((|H|,s(H))\) give equal expressions for every \(n\). The six distinct values at \(n=3\) shown in the table already distinguish the six classes. The Stirling transform preserves these exact coincidences for unrestricted tuples.

**Recovering the subgroup from a triple orbit.** Consider the element \(x\in X_3\) whose first labelled order is \(1<2<3\) and whose second is \(1<3<2\). The two orders are neither equal nor reverses of one another. No nonidentity order-reversal element can fix \(x\), and no order-exchanging element can fix it either (the only potentially fixing exchanges require equality or reversal of the two orders). Hence the \(W\)-orbit of \(x\) has eight elements and is regular. The orbit \(H\cdot x\) determines \(H\) itself by the free identification \(g\leftrightarrow g\cdot x\). Thus distinct subgroups induce distinct orbit equivalence relations on triples.

**Sharpness at pairs.** \(X_2\) consists of four states, the two signs of the relative order of the two labelled coordinates in each order. The \(W\)-action is the ordinary symmetry action on the four vertices of a square. Its ten subgroups induce exactly seven set partitions: \(\langle R_1R_2\rangle\) and \(\langle S,R_1R_2\rangle\) induce the same pair partition; \(\langle R_1,R_2\rangle\), \(\langle R_1S\rangle\), and \(W\) each act transitively on the four states; the five remaining subgroup partitions are pairwise distinct and distinct from these two. Consequently pairs cannot recover all ten groups. Singletons provide only one type.

**Repeated coordinates.** Partition the \(m\) coordinate positions according to equality. For each partition into \(n\) blocks, the tuple is equivalent to an injective ordered \(n\)-tuple, and distinct equality partitions never mix under domain permutations. There are \(\left\{\begin{matrix}m\\n\end{matrix}\right\}\) equality partitions. Summation proves the full-tuple formula.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` generates all eight signed order transformations, closes all one- and two-generator subgroups, checks that exactly ten subgroups occur, independently enumerates their type orbits for tuple lengths \(2,3,4,5\), checks every Burnside formula and the multiplicity of six numeric profiles, and confirms that exactly seven pair partitions and ten triple partitions occur. It also verifies a free triple witness and the impossibility of a free pair witness. Run `python3 verify.py` using the standard library. The mathematical proof above establishes the all-arity assertions; finite checks do not substitute for it.

## Relationship to prior work

Cameron (2002) introduced the two-linear-order homogeneous-permutation viewpoint and classified its Fraïssé ages. Linman and Pinsker (first public preprint 2014; journal 2015) determined all 39 closed reduct groups and explicitly identified the natural dihedral reversal–exchange sublattice with ten subgroups. Those facts, and the existence of the ten groups, are prior results. Their paper exhibits finite-arity relations that separate the larger 39-group lattice, but does not present the all-arity numerical character, the six exact profile-equivalence classes, or the sharp distinction between numerical non-recovery and triple orbit-partition recovery proved here. Burnside's lemma and the finite dihedral subgroup classification are classical tools rather than new results.

## Limitations

No orbit formulas for the other 29 reduct groups are asserted. The result concerns tuple orbits of closed groups, not isomorphism classes of unlabelled finite permutation patterns. An undiscovered source that already derives the six specific numerical profile classes and sharp triple recovery would negate the originality assessment; the searched literature does not establish that such a source does not exist. Independent external auditing has not been performed.
