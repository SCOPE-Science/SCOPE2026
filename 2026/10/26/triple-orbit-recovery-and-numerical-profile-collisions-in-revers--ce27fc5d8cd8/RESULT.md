# Triple-orbit recovery and numerical-profile collisions in reversal–exchange reducts of the random permutation

## Finding

Let \(\Pi=(D;<_{1},<_{2})\) be the countable homogeneous structure whose age comprises all finite sets equipped with two linear orders. Write \(G=\operatorname{Aut}(\Pi)\). On the finite labelled two-order types, let \(R_i\) reverse order \(i\), and let \(S\) exchange the orders. They generate the order-eight dihedral group \(W=\langle R_1,R_2,S\rangle\). For \(H\le W\), let \(G_H\) be the corresponding closed supergroup of \(G\), and put \(s(H)=|H\cap\{S,R_1R_2S\}|\).

For \(n\ge2\), the number of orbits on injective ordered \(n\)-tuples is
\[
a_H(n)=\frac{(n!)^2+s(H)n!}{|H|}.
\]
Also \(a_H(1)=1\). The number of orbits on unrestricted \(m\)-tuples is \(b_H(0)=1\) and, for \(m\ge1\),
\[
b_H(m)=\sum_{n=1}^m \left\{\begin{matrix}m\\n\end{matrix}\right\}a_H(n),
\]
where the braces denote Stirling numbers of the second kind. The ten groups give exactly six all-arity numerical profiles of either kind, with the six profiles distinguished by \(a_H(3)\). The class sizes are \(3,2,2,1,1,1\): **seven groups share their numerical profile with at least one other group**, while three have unique profiles.

| \(H\) | Order of \(H\) | \(s(H)\) | \(a_H(2)\) | \(a_H(3)\) |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| \(\{1\}\) | 1 | 0 | 4 | 36 |
| \(\langle R_1\rangle\) | 2 | 0 | 2 | 18 |
| \(\langle R_2\rangle\) | 2 | 0 | 2 | 18 |
| \(\langle R_1R_2\rangle\) | 2 | 0 | 2 | 18 |
| \(\langle S\rangle\) | 2 | 1 | 3 | 21 |
| \(\langle R_1R_2S\rangle\) | 2 | 1 | 3 | 21 |
| \(\langle R_1,R_2\rangle\) | 4 | 0 | 1 | 9 |
| \(\langle R_1S\rangle\) | 4 | 0 | 1 | 9 |
| \(\langle S,R_1R_2\rangle\) | 4 | 2 | 2 | 12 |
| \(W\) | 8 | 2 | 1 | 6 |

For comparison, Linman and Pinsker's preservation table already implies that the **full** orbit partitions on injective triples distinguish these ten groups. The full pair partitions distinguish only seven. Below we count the pair partitions and exhibit a single labelled triple type whose orbit recovers \(H\) directly, to contrast orbit-partition recovery with the loss of information in the six numerical profiles.

## Assumptions and scope

Tuple positions are labelled and not quotiented by permutations of positions. Each element of \(W\) admits a lift to a domain permutation: reversing either order or exchanging them produces another copy of the same homogeneous structure. The lifts normalize \(G\), and the subgroup \(K\) generated by the lifts and \(G\) is the union of eight closed cosets of \(G\). Indeed, two lifts with the same action on the order relations differ by an automorphism of both orders, and the eight transformations are distinct on the realized labelled types. Thus \(K/G\cong W\) and \(G_H\) is the inverse image of \(H\). The result is confined to this ten-group family, not the entire lattice of 39 closed supergroups of \(G\).

## Proof

**Types and the fixed-point count.** If \(\mathcal L_n\) denotes the \(n!\) strict total orders on the coordinate labels, the \(G\)-types of injective tuples are exactly \(X_n=\mathcal L_n\times\mathcal L_n\), by homogeneity and realization of every pair of finite orders. The \(G_H\)-orbits are exactly the inverse images of \(H\)-orbits on \(X_n\). For \(n\ge2\) no order equals its reverse. Thus only the identity among non-exchanging transformations fixes types, and it fixes \((n!)^2\) of them. A swap with reversals, \((L_1,L_2)\mapsto(R^a L_2,R^b L_1)\) with \(a,b\in\{0,1\}\), can fix types only when \(a=b\); then choosing the first order determines the second and gives exactly \(n!\) fixed types. These are the contributions of \(S\) and \(R_1R_2S\). Burnside's lemma gives \(a_H(n)\). For repeated coordinates, choose the equality partition of the positions and label its blocks by order of first occurrence. Permutations of the domain cannot merge equality partitions; on each partition with \(n\) blocks the orbit problem is that for \(n\) labelled distinct entries. This proves the Stirling transform.

**Numerical collisions.** The sign flips commute and \(SR_1S=R_2\). There are five involutions, two mutually inverse elements of order four and the identity. The three subgroups of order four are the cyclic group generated by \(R_1S\), the sign-flip group, and the group generated by \(S\) and the double flip. Together with the order-two groups, the trivial group and \(W\), these are the ten subgroups displayed. Equal pairs \((|H|,s(H))\) give identical formulas at every arity; the six distinct pairs give six different triple counts, as displayed. The Stirling transform preserves all these coincidences and distinctions: it is triangular with diagonal one, so the full \(b_H\) sequence determines the full \(a_H\) sequence.

**Pair partitions and a single triple orbit.** On pairs use the four types \(x=(+,+), y=(+,−), z=(−,+), w=(−,−)\). Besides the discrete partition for \(\{1\}\) and the indiscrete partition common to \(\langle R_1,R_2\rangle,\langle R_1S\rangle,W\), the partitions are:

- \(\langle R_1\rangle\): \(xz\mid yw\);
- \(\langle R_2\rangle\): \(xy\mid zw\);
- \(\langle R_1R_2\rangle\) and \(\langle S,R_1R_2\rangle\): \(xw\mid yz\);
- \(\langle S\rangle\): \(x\mid yz\mid w\);
- \(\langle R_1R_2S\rangle\): \(xw\mid y\mid z\).

They follow by flipping or exchanging the signs and give seven distinct pair partitions. For a triple take the first order \(1<2<3\) and the second \(1<3<2\). These orders are neither equal nor reversed copies of one another. The fixed-point test shows that the type has trivial \(W\)-stabilizer. If \(x_3\) denotes this type, the bijection \(W\to W\cdot x_3\) given by \(g\mapsto g\cdot x_3\) takes \(H\) exactly to \(H\cdot x_3\); knowing that single orbit therefore identifies the group. In particular the recovery fails at arities one and two and succeeds at three, while no number of scalar counts can distinguish two groups in a common numerical profile class.

## Verification

The accompanying standard-library `verify.py` enumerates the ten subgroup actions on labelled types for arities 2 through 5, verifies the orbit counts against the formula, and checks the pair/triple partition counts and the triple witness. `verification_output.txt` records that finite check. The proof above, rather than this finite test, justifies the formula for unrestricted arity.

## Relationship to prior work and limitations

Cameron introduced the homogeneous-permutation framework. Linman and Pinsker identify the ten-group dihedral family and classify the full lattice of 39 closed supergroups. In particular, the ten groups considered here have distinct preservation signatures on the ternary entries of their Figure 3. Every preserved ternary relation is a union of tuple orbits and repeated-coordinate triples contribute only information already carried by injective triples. Thus full triple-orbit separation is a consequence of their table, not a new separation theorem here. The profile calculation measures a different invariant: which groups have equal **numbers** of orbits at every tuple length. The pair count and single-orbit argument make the comparison explicit, using the already known finite type action. Neither the group classification nor Burnside's lemma is claimed as new. This result does not count orbits of the other 29 reduct groups, nor of unlabelled finite patterns. The exact numerical profile computation is distinct on inspected evidence; unindexed coverage remains possible.
