# Review: Tutte-controlled conjugacy-class spectra of radical-square-zero quiver unit groups

## Correctness — PASS
The diagonal plus square-zero-arrow normal form is unique. At fixed nonzero diagonal labels, radical conjugation removes exactly the nonresonant arrows; their free conjugation parameters contribute a power of the field size to class sizes. The surviving edge-support stabilizer is the group of vertex labels constant along support edges, proving both the power of the multiplicative-group size and the orbit multiplicity. The spanning-subset expansion and Tutte specialization are exact; the tree leaf recurrence and cyclic transfer matrix give the advertised special families. Every class-size coefficient was corroborated by direct conjugation-orbit enumeration in 17 finite field/quiver configurations, including loops and parallel edges.

## Originality — PASS within checked coverage
Can, Casimiro and Malheiro, arXiv:2208.01073, study general torus/unipotent semidirect structure and qualitative conjugacy for bipartite incidence monoids; their inspected theorems do not give these finite-field elementwise class-size distributions. Rossmann, DOI:10.1112/blms.12665, computes conjugacy enumerations of different class-two graphical groups, not the unit groups here. Triangular-unitriangular finite group enumerations offer related methods but not the entire radical-square-zero quiver class-size polynomial. The classical Tutte identity is not claimed as a discovery. The complete two-variable class-size distribution and its graph-matroid invariance are the assessed finding. Priority risk remains from potentially equivalent finite torus-by-vector orbit enumerations outside the inspected literature; a search does not prove absolute uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Conjugacy-class sizes control basic finite-group representation and commuting statistics. The complete rank-graded distribution, beyond just the number of classes, provides explicit spectra for every forest or unicyclic quiver and shows that edge orientations are invisible to this invariant. The first-cycle closed expression isolates exactly what is added beyond forest cases. These are structural finite-family conclusions, not isolated table entries.

## Limitations
No class-degree distribution or full quiver classification is asserted. When the field has two elements, different formal multiplicative-group exponents yield identical integer class sizes and must be summed. Finite computations test small cases only; the displayed algebraic proof establishes the general statement. The result does not apply to an unrestricted path algebra with paths of length two.
