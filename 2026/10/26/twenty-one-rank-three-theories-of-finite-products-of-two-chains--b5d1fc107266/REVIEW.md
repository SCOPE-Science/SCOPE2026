# Scientific review

## Correctness (C) — PASS

The quantified theorem concerns every pair of nonempty finite chains in the single reflexive binary-order language. A three-round interval-invariant strategy reduces each chain coordinate to at most seven points, and a coordinatewise strategy preserves all atomic equality and order relations of a product. The 28 canonical posets are exhaustively classified by the exact complete rank-three extension-type recurrence, using actual atomic diagrams rather than sampled formulas or numeric signatures. The two implementations give the identical 21-class partition; direct game recursion agrees in 64 comparisons. The twenty-one representatives and sharp 30-point bound follow from the complete table. The infinite tail is justified separately by the EF proof. Risk: the substantial finite certificate and product proof have not been independently audited; a code defect or a misapplication of the game invariant could affect the claim.

## Originality (O) — PASS, with residual risks

The exact 21-class classification and the sharp within-family bound were compared with indexed findings, accessible historical claims and primary literature. The closest classical tools are the Feferman–Vaught product theorem, Makowsky's survey of its algorithmic uses, and the standard Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé finite-type argument explained by Otto. These provide general methods; the material inspected does not state the exact 21 rectangular-poset theories or the sharp 30-point representative bound. Existing claims about numbers of maps from the same product posets and rank-bounded graph theories of rook graphs do not imply equality of rank-three theories in the inclusion/order language. Divisor-poset aliases and larger product theorems were considered. Risk: the Feferman–Vaught 1959 primary full text was not accessible, and unindexed exact historical tabulations may exist; absence in a search is not a proof of novelty.

## Value (V) — PASS

The family consists of the canonical two-dimensional distributive grid lattices, equivalently all divisor posets of integers with at most two distinct prime factors. The result determines their complete depth-three observable structure, including unexpectedly large identifications between geometrically different rectangles. The 30-point theorem is a sharp finite witness bound for every model in the natural two-parameter class, and the table identifies all exceptional small shapes. This is a finite-model-theoretic invariant, not a routine cardinality count or an arbitrary parameter slice. The bound's significance is within the specified pure-order family and rank, not a claim about arbitrary posets or stronger languages.
