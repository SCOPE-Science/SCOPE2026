# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact coordinate split proves the cone decomposition for all characteristic-two fields; gradient equations give one isolated triple vertex and smooth cubic base. The plane/line correspondence is a projective-geometric bijection, and the classical 27-line theorem supplies the geometric count. Vikulova's uniqueness and quadratic-extension results, plus explicit exhaustive binary-cubic coefficient tests over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_4\), establish the arithmetic splitting. Frobenius-trace computation yields the finite-field formula. Limits: reduced geometric planes only; no relative Hilbert scheme scheme structure is claimed.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The closest sources address the characteristic-zero Segre cubic and its 15 planes (Dolgachev; Cheltsov–Tschinkel–Zhang; Eklund), and the maximally symmetric characteristic-two cubic surface separately (Vikulova). Neither read source establishes the particular special-fiber cone identification and jump to 27 planes together with Frobenius splitting. Relevant database and external literature searches also did not reveal a dominating statement. A 2026 Segre-plane construction was found by abstract but its full text was unavailable; it does not visibly address modular reduction. Absence of a search hit is not a proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The exact jump of the plane locus at prime two is a natural degeneration invariant of a classical rational cubic threefold, not an arbitrary finite-field count. It connects two distinct high-symmetry geometries and gives the six quadratic Galois pairs and a uniform zeta-count formula. It is a structural boundary for compactifications and Fano-plane incidence, and the explicit reduced-plane cardinality remains informative without a study of the Hilbert-scheme infinitesimals.
