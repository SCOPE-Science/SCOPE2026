# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The proof first reconstructs each blown-up twin block intrinsically as a maximal rank-one multiplication subspace, using independence of the base graph's neighborhood vectors. Any isomorphism permutes these blocks. Comparing the squares and the images of block sums forces a base-graph automorphism and the identity \(s_i^2=\lambda_i d_i\) with \(s_j=\lambda_i\) along edges. Propagation along even walks yields either constant degree or bipartite biregularity. Explicit positive diagonal maps establish the converse. The finite verifier checks 117 blow-ups and 3480 independent rank calculations; it is supplementary to the proof, not exhaustive evidence for all graphs.

## Originality

PASS with residual risk. The 2017/2019 graph/random-walk paper proves the iff statement for nonsingular graphs; the 2024/2026 singular-graphs paper obtains stronger absence-of-homomorphism results for selected caterpillars and other families, but does not claim the unrestricted invertible-quotient blow-up classification. A 2026 survey explicitly leaves the singular-graph converse open as Question 26. Some known nonsingular and double-star instances are logically subsumed and are not counted as new. Published-index and local claim comparisons did not reveal the same all-blow-ups theorem. Equivalent unindexed results are possible.

## Mathematical value

PASS. This is a uniform theorem for an explicitly motivated, structurally natural class of singular graphs, with arbitrarily large kernel of the original adjacency matrix, rather than a finite census. The complete-multipartite classification includes nonbipartite graphs with unequal part sizes and arbitrary nullity. The intrinsic rank-one-locus method offers a reusable approach to isomorphism problems for rank-deficient evolution algebras. It does not settle all singular graphs or classify all homomorphisms.

## Closest literature and limitations

Closest primary texts: Cadavid–Rodiño Montoya–Rodríguez, arXiv:1710.10516, Theorem 2.3; Cadavid–Rodiño Montoya–Rodríguez–Vidal, arXiv:2405.12341, especially Section 3; Kaygorodov–Martín González–Viruel, arXiv:2610.22248, Question 26. Proof is for real algebras and invertible base adjacency, not for arbitrary fields or false-twin quotients that remain singular. Previous nullity-one special cases and selected caterpillars must not be treated as novel subclaims. Independent validation remains outstanding.
