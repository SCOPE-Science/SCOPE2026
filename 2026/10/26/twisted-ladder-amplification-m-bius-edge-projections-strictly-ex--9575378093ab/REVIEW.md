# Scientific review: Möbius versus prism edge-flow stability

## Correctness — PASS
The graph-gradient projector is the usual incidence/Laplacian Moore–Penrose orthoprojector. The decomposition under antipodal symmetry produces periodic and antiperiodic massive one-dimensional Green functions, whose recurrences are checked explicitly. Their entrywise differences give exactly two absolute row sums, and a sign-valued edge input attains each sum. A uniform analytic inequality establishes cycle-edge dominance from four rungs onward; separate exact cases cover three and four. For every size the comparison with the prism bounds the possible loss in the massive periodic kernel by a geometric sum smaller than the gain caused by the twisted closure. This yields strict comparison, followed by two parity-specific asymptotic coefficients. The geometric cutoff proof supplies the separate sharp leading logarithmic deficit. The accompanying rational checker independently constructs full edge projections and verifies 713 exact assertions for ten sizes; these finite tests are not presented as proof of the infinite family.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, with residual risk
The graph-Hodge least-squares projection is standard (Jiang et al., arXiv:0811.1067). The transfer-current viewpoint is standard (Kassel–Wu, arXiv:1312.2946). Resistance formulas for ordinary ladders are known (Cinkir, arXiv:1503.06353). The most directly relevant prior result is Azimi–Farrokhi, arXiv:2306.10497, Theorem 5.1 and Corollary 5.4, which explicitly determine incidence and Laplacian pseudoinverses for Möbius ladders. Those formulas are fully acknowledged and could be used to calculate individual projection entries. The inspected source does not state the maximum-coordinate norm classification, the strict topology comparison across every size, the eight-vertex threshold, or the parity-dependent asymptotic excess. The comparison uses threshold-aware absolute row sums and a nontrivial positivity bound. Database searches for Möbius/Moebius ladder endpoint norms, transfer-current projections and twisted circulant Green functions returned no result implying the complete comparison. An equivalent sharp stability statement may exist with different terminology; mere failure to locate it does not certify priority.

## Value — PASS
This is a natural robustness comparison between two standard three-regular families with exactly the same numbers of nodes and observations: changing only the ladder closure affects worst-case coordinate amplification. The strict inequality holds at every size, the difference has distinct sharp even/odd exponential coefficients, and the minimal size for amplification above two differs from the ordinary prism. The result is a complete invariant and geometry-sensitive boundary rather than an arbitrary single numerical example. This adds concrete quantitative information to the already known explicit pseudoinverse formulas.
