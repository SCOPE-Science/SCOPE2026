# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The two patches are conditionally independent given the common two-sided environmental history. The conditional occupancy mean obeys the strictly stable recursion \(M_t=\lambda M_{t-1}+(1-\lambda)aS_t\). Applying the Markov covariance of the weather gives the strictly positive cross-sectional covariance and the lag-one cross covariance. The one-patch lag-one covariance differs by exactly \(\lambda[p(1-p)-C_0]\), because the Bernoulli copy operation alone transports conditional residual variance from one census to the next. The residual-variance denominator is positive under the strict assumptions, and \(1-\lambda q>0\). These identities reconstruct all four parameters without ambiguous square roots after fixing positive weather contrast. One-date failure is demonstrated by two exact admissible parameter settings producing the identical four-cell marginal table. The independent `verify.py` computes exact stationary laws for 12 settings and matches 48 moments plus 12 reconstructed parameter sets. Finite checks are not used as proof of universal claims.

## Originality — PASS, with qualified scope

This concerns *two-site, two-date exact identification* of the shared binary Markov forcing memory and local copy probability. Kendall et al. (2000; DOI 10.1086/303350) analyze dispersion and environmental spatial correlation in linearized population fluctuations, whereas the present object is a stationary Bernoulli patch-renewal model with **temporal** common-environment memory and an inverse from just four observed moments. MacKenzie et al. (2003; DOI 10.1890/02-3090) establish imperfect-detection dynamic occupancy methods, not the displayed shared-weather inversion. Ripa (2000, DOI 10.1034/j.1600-0706.2000.890119.x) analyzes the Moran effect and census timing in linearized population models, but the accessible abstract does not contain this theorem. Massie et al. (2015, DOI 10.1038/ncomms6993) treat autocorrelated weather in population synchronization, not a documented exact two-site discrete Bernoulli inverse. Heaton and Solo (2004, DOI 10.1111/j.1368-423X.2004.00146.x) prove general identification of dynamic factor models, but the accessible abstract does not demonstrate coverage of the specific binary-renewal parameter bounds, minimal two-census sufficiency, or one-census nonidentifiability witness. These sources were compared at the level of objects, observations and implications; the author-accessible detailed treatment of Kendall and the complete MacKenzie source were inspected. Relevant general state-space identification theory is a residual originality risk; no claim of exhaustive priority or independent verification is made.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Environmental autocorrelation and patch-state persistence can confound interpretations of ecological spatial synchrony. The exact two-census result separates them, using the within-patch minus cross-patch lag-one covariance to isolate local persistence, then a cross-lag ratio to isolate the unobserved weather memory. This establishes a sharp minimum temporal observation horizon for **two** permanently monitored patches and supplies admissibility bounds. The precise fact matters for assessing whether short ecological panel data can, in principle, recover shared versus local persistence without observing weather. It is not asserted that four noisy sample statistics can reliably estimate the parameters.
