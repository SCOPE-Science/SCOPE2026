# Review of the two-coset odd-rational Gabor theorem

## Correctness
**PASS.** The exact Zak kernel is obtained by partial fractions, Fourier transformation, and absolutely convergent geometric series; its finite zero set follows from an explicit factored elimination identity and sign tests at the two real spatial phases and the frequency boundary. The critical-lattice Zak Parseval identity then turns the union of two modulation cosets into a scalar frame symbol. At the only common-zero offset, the local spatial and frequency derivatives are nonzero and orthogonal in the complex plane. The exact two-dimensional quadratic minimum gives the stated limiting constant, while compactness and a nondegenerate first-derivative lower bound exclude other minimizers. `check.py` independently checks central algebra, source normalizations, and finite-offset trends. These calculations do not replace the proof of the infinite statements.

## Originality
**PASS under the inspected evidence, with remaining historical-priority risk.** Ghosh's arXiv:2608.10568 investigates this same rational odd window but frames along *single rectangular lattices* through a polynomial-matrix condition; an arbitrary two-frequency-coset configuration is not a rectangular lattice unless the offset is the exceptional half-shift. Ulanovskii–Zlotnikov arXiv:2606.31450 provides parity obstructions for selected periodic sets, explaining the negative critical instance but offering neither sufficiency for every other offset nor the exact lower-bound degeneration. Kloos–Stöckler arXiv:1311.7359 concerns Zak zeros of totally positive functions and splines, not this rational window. The corresponding even Poisson-window situation has a different, even window and is optimized rather than obstructed by half-frequency staggering. The important new component here is the complete three-point Zak-zero classification, which gives the all-offset phase diagram and the sharp local spectral coefficient. The possibility of an implicit equivalent in historical Zak-transform analysis remains.

## Value
**PASS.** The motivating rational window has a subtle lattice frame set; at the fixed integer translation scale, a natural two-coset density-two perturbation of the parity-obstructed rectangular lattice resolves its frame question for *every* stagger parameter, and quantifies its loss of stability to second order with a sharp computable coefficient. The affirmative/nonaffirmative transition is qualitatively opposite to the natural even Poisson window, showing that frequency staggering can destabilize as well as regularize oversampled Gabor systems. The exact finite zero-set computation carries mathematical information not recoverable from general density criteria. The result is specialized to one physically natural rational window and does not determine a full off-critical frame-bound formula.
