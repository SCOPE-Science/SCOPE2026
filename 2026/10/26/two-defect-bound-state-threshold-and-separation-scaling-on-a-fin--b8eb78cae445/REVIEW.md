# Scientific assessment

**Correctness.** The rank-two resolvent separates into symmetric and antisymmetric channels. The antisymmetric edge limit is \(d(N-d)/(2N)\). The linear interpolant with opposite values on the two defects gives a nonzero edge eigenfunction precisely at the reciprocal threshold. The scaled threshold decreases strictly with the folded separation. At limiting equality a second strictly above-edge level thus requires the finite folded fraction to exceed the limiting fraction; this is impossible at the antipodal limit, and a second level along any such critical subsequence has zero scaled edge shift. The monotonicity and bracketing arguments give the remaining scaled energy limits. The numerical checker is a bounded corroboration only.

**Originality.** Exner's circle-resolvent determinant already gives the exact continuum two-point secular equations and, on taking the antisymmetric edge limit, the continuum threshold. The scientific distinction is the finite-cycle lattice classification and its proven connection to that continuum limit. The open-chain imaginary-defect transition of Joglekar et al. is not a threshold for real levels above the periodic-chain band edge. Bierman's many-impurity abstract does not supply the finite pair calculation; its full text was not inspected. Equivalent formulations elsewhere in the lattice-defect literature are not excluded.

**Value and scope.** The finite-cycle result distinguishes one and two above-edge levels at every coupling and separation, and identifies which finite sizes are on each side of the threshold at critical scaling. The limiting equation describes the energy scale and relates it to a standard continuum model. The proof does not claim a new general finite-rank method or extend the scaling to bounded separation, unequal defects, or different lattices.
