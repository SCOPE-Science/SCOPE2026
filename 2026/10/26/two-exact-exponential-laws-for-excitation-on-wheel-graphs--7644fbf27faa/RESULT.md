# Two exact exponential laws for excitation on wheel graphs

## Finding

For the synchronous three-state Greenberg–Hastings rule on a labelled wheel \(W_n=K_1\vee C_n\), for each integer \(n\ge3\), every orbit eventually reaches either the all-rest fixed point or a primitive three-cycle. Let \(C_n\) count distinct primitive three-cycles (a cyclic shift in time does not create another cycle). Define \(p_0=3\), \(p_1=2\), \(p_2=4\) and \(p_{n+3}=2p_{n+2}+p_n\). Then

\[
C_n=p_n+2\cos(2\pi n/3)-1.
\]

The number \(E_n\) of **labelled initial configurations** whose orbits eventually reach the all-rest fixed point is exactly

\[
E_n=3\left((1+\sqrt2)^n+(1-\sqrt2)^n+1\right).
\]

Consequently, for a uniformly chosen initial three-colouring of all \(n+1\) vertices, the exact extinction probability is

\[
\mathbb P(\mathrm{extinction})=\frac{(1+\sqrt2)^n+(1-\sqrt2)^n+1}{3^n}\sim\left(\frac{1+\sqrt2}{3}\right)^n.
\]

Let \(\lambda\) be the largest real root of \(t^3-2t^2-1=0\), approximately \(2.205569\). The fraction of configurations **already periodic** is \((1+3C_n)/3^{n+1}\sim(\lambda/3)^n\), even as the probability of **eventually oscillating** tends to one. The two exponentially small proportions measure different phenomena: immediate recurrence versus eventual extinction.

## Assumptions and scope

The labelled wheel has a distinguished hub \(h\) and labelled rim \(r_0,\ldots,r_{n-1}\), with every rim vertex adjacent to \(h\) and to its two cyclic neighbours. All vertices take values in \(\{0,1,2\}\): state \(0\) is resting, state \(1\) excited, state \(2\) refractory. In parallel at each discrete time, state \(1\) becomes \(2\), state \(2\) becomes \(0\), and state \(0\) becomes \(1\) iff a neighbour was in state \(1\), otherwise it remains \(0\). No quotient by graph automorphisms is taken. Counts are exact for every integer \(n\ge3\); randomization is only in the stated initial-distribution corollary.

## Proof

**1. Cycle-space obstruction and eventual periods.** For an oriented edge \((u,v)\) and initial colouring \(X\), introduce the integer antisymmetric form

\[
dX(u,v)=\begin{cases}1,&X(u)=0,\ X(v)=1,\\-1,&X(u)=1,\ X(v)=0,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]

Gravner–Lyu–Sivakoff's finite-graph theorem [2, Theorem 3] states that the three-state Greenberg–Hastings trajectory synchronizes precisely when all cycle integrals of this form vanish; if they do not, the eventual excitation frequency at every vertex is the maximal directed-cycle integral divided by its length. The wheel's integer cycle space is generated by the \(n\) triangles \((h,r_j,r_{j+1})\). An oriented such triangle has nonzero integral exactly when its three vertices carry distinct colours; the integral is then \(+1\) or \(-1\). Thus the initial condition is irrotational exactly when **no hub triangle is trichromatic**. In that case the general theorem gives synchronization; a synchronized configuration passes through the all-rest state in at most two further updates and then stays there.

If a trichromatic triangle exists, orient it to obtain integral \(+1\), so the general theorem gives excitation frequency at least \(1/3\) for every vertex. Because a vertex excited at time \(t\) must be refractory at time \(t+1\) and resting at time \(t+2\), it cannot be excited again sooner than time \(t+3\); hence that frequency is at most \(1/3\). All excitation frequencies therefore equal \(1/3\). Finiteness makes every trajectory eventually periodic. On its periodic tail, every inter-excitation gap is at least three, with average exactly three; hence every gap is **exactly three**. Each vertex then advances \(0\to1\to2\to0\) on every step, giving a global primitive three-cycle. The only other possible eventual orbit is the all-rest fixed point.

**2. Enumerating primitive three-cycles.** In a nonzero periodic point, all states advance by one in each step. Fix the hub state to \(0\) at a chosen time. Then the hub advances iff some rim vertex is in state \(1\). For a rim state \(b\), with cyclic neighbours \(a,c\), the vertex advances exactly when

\[
b=2\quad\text{or}\quad(b=0\ \wedge\ (a=1\ \vee\ c=1))\quad\text{or}\quad(b=1\ \wedge\ (a=2\ \vee\ c=2)).
\]

Index the nine ordered pairs \((a,b)\in\{0,1,2\}^2\). Define the integer transfer matrix \(M\) by \(M_{(a,b),(b,c)}=1\) exactly when this local condition holds, and zero otherwise. Its trace \(\operatorname{tr}(M^n)\) counts all cyclic rim strings satisfying the rim rules. The sole such rim string without any state \(1\) is the constant string \(2\), which fails the hub's requirement. Hence exactly \(\operatorname{tr}(M^n)-1\) states with hub \(0\) lie in genuine three-cycles. Each genuine cycle visits hub states \(0,1,2\) once, so this is also the number \(C_n\) of **cycles**.

Exact integer linear algebra yields

\[
\det(tI-M)=t^4(t^2+t+1)(t^3-2t^2-1).
\]

The third-degree factor contributes the power-sum sequence \(p_0=3,p_1=2,p_2=4\), \(p_{n+3}=2p_{n+2}+p_n\) by Newton identities. The other two nonzero eigenvalues are primitive cube roots of unity, contributing \(2\cos(2\pi n/3)\) to the \(n\)-th trace. This proves the cycle formula for every positive \(n\) relevant here.

**3. Exact extinction basin.** By step 1, an initial condition becomes all-rest if and only if no triangle \((h,r_j,r_{j+1})\) is trichromatic. Fix hub state \(0\); this means no cyclically consecutive rim pair is \((1,2)\) or \((2,1)\), in either order. The adjacency matrix of allowed pairs is

\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\1&1&0\\1&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The number of labelled cyclic rim strings is \(\operatorname{tr}(B^n)\), not an open-path count. The spectrum of \(B\) is exactly \(1,1+\sqrt2,1-\sqrt2\). The same count holds for each of the three hub colours, establishing \(E_n=3\operatorname{tr}(B^n)\).

**4. Asymptotic comparison.** The non-Perron roots of \(t^3-2t^2-1\) have modulus strictly smaller than the Perron root \(\lambda\), so \(p_n\sim\lambda^n\). As \(1+\sqrt2<3\), extinction probabilities decay at ratio \((1+\sqrt2)/3\). Since each primitive three-cycle contains exactly three configurations and there is one fixed configuration, immediate periodicity probability is \((3C_n+1)/3^{n+1}\sim(\lambda/3)^n\). These exponential laws are distinct.

## Verification

The companion standalone script `verify_gh_wheel.py` implements the update map, transfer matrices, exact characteristic-polynomial calculations and exhaustive functional-graph decomposition. Run `python3 verify_gh_wheel.py` with standard Python. The exact exhaustive tests cover **all** \(3^{n+1}\) labelled initial states for every \(n=3,\ldots,10\), matching the counts \(C_n=12,22,50,116,252,558,1236,2722\) and \(E_n=45,105,249,597,1437,3465,8361,20181\). The infinite-family proof does not depend on finite enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work

The foundational Greenberg–Hastings rule and its spatial patterns are described in [1]. The graph-independent circulation and excitation-frequency criterion of [2] provides the decisive qualitative dynamical input; its statements do not supply the wheel-specific transfer-matrix characteristic polynomial or the two exact exponential enumerations proved here. The related general finite-graph analysis of [3] addresses excitation dynamics but not the explicit wheel cycle and basin counts. These comparisons distinguish a finite-family exact census from the general existence and synchronization criteria; they do not establish priority against every unpublished or unindexed computation.

## Limitations

The model has exactly three states, synchronous parallel updating and excitation threshold one, and the counts are for labelled wheels, not isomorphism classes. No claim is made for four-state variants, noisy dynamics, random update schedules, or transient hitting-time distributions. The general synchronization theorem [2] is used as a cited prior result rather than reproved. A possibly relevant 1978 founding journal article could not be reviewed in full through accessible sources, so the novelty search is bounded. Independent scientific verification has not been performed.
