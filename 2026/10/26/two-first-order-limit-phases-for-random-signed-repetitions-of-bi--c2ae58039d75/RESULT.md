# Two first-order limit phases for random signed repetitions of binary words

## Finding
Let \(m\ge 2\) be fixed. Choose \(U_n\) uniformly from binary words of length \(n\). For a fixed sign template \(\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_m)\in\{+,-\}^m\), concatenate \(m\) copies of \(U_n\), reversing the copy wherever the sign is \(-\), to obtain \(W_{n,\varepsilon}\). Regard this as a finite structure in the signature consisting of immediate successor \(S\) and one unary predicate \(P\) for bit 1.

There are exactly two asymptotic first-order probability laws, classified by whether \(\varepsilon_1=\varepsilon_m\). For every fixed first-order sentence \(\varphi\), its probability has an effectively computable limit and the difference from that limit is exponentially small in \(n\). All these limits are dyadic rationals; conversely every dyadic rational in \([0,1]\) is attained in either phase. If the two endpoint signs agree, the limiting theory is exactly that of a uniformly random binary word. If they disagree, the limiting theory is different: the formula asserting equality of the first and last bits has probability one, whereas it has limiting probability one half in the agreeing-sign phase.

For every fixed quantifier rank \(q\), the total variation distance between the distributions of rank-at-most-\(q\) first-order theories from any two templates in the same phase decreases exponentially in \(n\); this permits different fixed numbers of copies. In contrast, for each fixed template the total variation distance **between the full word distributions** of \(W_{n,\varepsilon}\) and a uniform binary word of length \(mn\) is exactly \(1-2^{-(m-1)n}\).

## Assumptions and scope
All words are over the fixed alphabet \(\{0,1\}\); \(m\) and the sign template are independent of \(n\). The vocabulary is precisely \(\{S,P\}\), with \(S(i,j)\) meaning \(j=i+1\) on positions \(1,\ldots,mn\), and \(P(i)\) meaning bit 1. Equality is available. No nonlocal order, position arithmetic, copy boundary marker or mirror relation is in the signature. A formula is fixed before \(n\) grows. The value \(n\) tends through positive integers, and all probability bounds hold for sufficiently large \(n\).

Write \(U_n^+=U_n\) and \(U_n^-\) for its reversal, and set \(W_{n,\varepsilon}=U_n^{\varepsilon_1}\cdots U_n^{\varepsilon_m}\). Denote the random uniformly distributed length-\(mn\) word by \(Z_{mn}\). The two phases are A (equal first and last signs) and B (opposite first and last signs).

## Proof
**1. Effective local reduction.** Fix \(q\). Effective bounded-degree Hanf locality gives an integer neighbourhood radius \(r\) and truncation threshold \(t\), computable from \(q\) and the signature, such that two finite successor-coloured paths with the same number of rooted radius-\(r\) neighbourhoods of each isomorphism type, up to cap \(t\), satisfy the same sentences of quantifier rank at most \(q\). For a sufficiently long binary path, the only neighbourhoods having an endpoint within radius \(r\) occur at the first and last \(r+1\) vertices. Their types and exact counts are completely determined by the first and last \(b=2r+1\) bits. Every other rooted radius-\(r\) type is an interior window of length \(b\).

**2. Interior saturation.** Let \(B\) be any binary pattern of length \(b\), and consider disjoint blocks of length \(b\) lying inside the middle portion of the seed \(U_n\), leaving \(2b\) or more bits at each end unused. For \(n\) sufficiently large these blocks are independent, and each is equal to \(B\) with probability \(2^{-b}\). A binomial lower-tail bound implies that the probability that fewer than \(t\) blocks equal \(B\) is at most \(C_{b,t}e^{-c_b n}\), with positive \(c_b\) depending only on \(b\). Taking the union over all \(2^b\) possible \(B\), with probability at least \(1-Ce^{-cn}\) **all** interior patterns occur at least \(t\) times in the seed. Every signed copy contains either the seed or its reversal, so the full repeated word also has all interior neighbourhood types at least \(t\) times. The same argument applies to \(Z_{mn}\). Call such a word saturated.

Consequently, among saturated paths sufficiently long, rank-\(q\) truth depends solely on the pair of boundary words \((L,R)\) of length \(b\): interior counts all truncate to \(t\), while endpoint types depend on \((L,R)\). More formally, if two saturated words have identical \((L,R)\), they agree on every rank-\(q\) sentence. The estimates in this paragraph are uniform over the finitely many templates of a fixed \(m\).

**3. Exact boundary laws.** Suppose \(n>2b\). If the first and last signs agree, the first \(b\) bits of \(W_{n,\varepsilon}\) depend on one end of \(U_n\), and its last \(b\) bits depend on the other end. These disjoint seed bits are independent and uniformly random. Thus \((L,R)\) is uniform on \(\{0,1\}^b\times\{0,1\}^b\), exactly as for \(Z_{mn}\). If the first and last signs disagree, the two windows use the same seed end in opposite orientations: \((L,R)=(B,\operatorname{rev}(B))\) for a uniformly random \(B\in\{0,1\}^b\).

It follows by coupling the boundary variables and applying the saturation estimate that any two members of one phase have rank-\(q\) theories agreeing with probability \(1-O_q(e^{-c_q n})\), including comparison to \(Z_{mn}\) in phase A. The probability of a fixed sentence approaches the average of its eventually constant truth value for each admissible boundary pair, with exponentially small error. There are finitely many equally likely boundary assignments; their total number is a power of two. Hence each limit is dyadic.

**4. Effectivity and complete spectrum.** Given \(\varphi\), compute its quantifier rank and a valid pair \((r,t)\) from effective Hanf locality. Enumerate boundary words of length \(b\). For each permissible pair construct one sufficiently long binary word with the specified boundary blocks and at least \(t\) copies of every interior block of length \(b\), for example by inserting a long enough concatenation of all binary length-\(b\) strings between the boundaries. Evaluate \(\varphi\) on these finitely many representatives, and average over the boundary pairs using the uniform independent or reversal-coupled law. This computes the limit exactly. For any integer \(k\ge1\), the first \(k\) positions of \(W_{n,\varepsilon}\) are independent fair bits for \(n\ge k\), since the first signed copy is a uniformly random word even if reversed. A first-order sentence can locate the unique first point and its next \(k-1\) successors and test membership of its binary prefix in any prescribed set of \(a\) of the \(2^k\) possible prefixes. Its probability is exactly \(a/2^k\). Together with the dyadic upper bound, this gives the full spectrum \(\mathbb Z[1/2]\cap[0,1]\) in each phase.

**5. Sharp separation.** The sentence
\[
\exists x\,\exists y\,\bigl[\neg\exists z\,S(z,x)\ \wedge\ \neg\exists z\,S(y,z)\ \wedge\ (P(x)\leftrightarrow P(y))\bigr]
\]
says the first and last bits agree. Under opposite endpoint signs these two bits are the same seed bit and the sentence is certain; under equal signs they are independent fair bits, making its probability exactly one half for every \(n\ge2\). Thus the two limiting theories differ. Finally, the map \(U_n\mapsto W_{n,\varepsilon}\) is injective, since its first block recovers the seed (up to reversal), and its image has size \(2^n\). The uniform distribution on this image has total-variation distance \(1-2^n/2^{mn}=1-2^{-(m-1)n}\) from the full uniform measure. This proves the remaining claim.

## Relationship to prior work
Markström and Räty, *Logical isomorphism and logical limit laws for small permutation classes and regular languages*, arXiv:2610.22218v1 (public 15 October 2026), develop logical limit laws for regular languages and small permutation classes. The signed-power and mirror-copy languages considered here have long-range equality constraints and are generally nonregular; their two-phase limit classification is not stated in the inspected paper. The proof uses effective bounded-degree Hanf locality as a standard tool, as developed for example by Kuske and Schweikardt (arXiv:1703.01122v1). The novelty claim is restricted to the particular signed-repetition ensemble classification and its sharp contrast between first-order and total-variation distances, not to Hanf locality or to convergence of ordinary independent random words.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns only the immediate-successor vocabulary and fixed templates and formulas. It does not hold as stated if the signature includes full global order, a predicate distinguishing copy boundaries, a reversal relation or random templates varying with \(n\). No claim is made for monadic second-order logic, growing quantifier rank, growing number of copies, or universal concentration across arbitrary word distributions. Broader historical results about locally indistinguishable correlated words could subsume the classification; independent literature validation has not been performed.
