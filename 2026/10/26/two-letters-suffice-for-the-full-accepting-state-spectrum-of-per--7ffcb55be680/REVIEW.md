# Review of the binary right-quotient spectrum theorem

## Correctness: PASS

The high-range construction is entirely explicit, uses two letters, and is valid for all positive parameters. The subgroup generated by the two letter actions equals \(S_k\times\mathbb Z/h\mathbb Z\) because the projections generate \(S_k\) and the identity-permutation words \(a^k\), \((ab)^{k-1}\) move the counter by coprime increments. The minimality proofs and the right-quotient final-set computation then give exactly \(m,n,\alpha\). The remaining low range is supplied by the published unary theorem. Direct finite group enumeration and DFA minimization corroborate 75 cases.

## Originality: PASS within examined evidence

German (arXiv:2605.10852v1, 2026) gives the same unrestricted output spectrum with a ternary high-range construction. It does not establish the fixed binary-alphabet assertion. Rauch and Holzer (arXiv:2208.14731, 2022) determine the unary spectrum but not the binary one. Hospodár and Mlynárčik (2020) examine total-state complexity rather than accepting-state complexity. Searches of mathematical research records, terminology variants, and retrieved published mathematical statements found no logically covering assertion. An unindexed binary witness or a relevant inaccessible full text remains a priority risk.

## Value: PASS

The exact minimum alphabet size is a natural unresolved quantitative refinement of a theorem explicitly phrased as a unary-to-nonunary transition. The two-generator group construction explains *why* a separately controlled third symbol is unnecessary, while the unary interval demonstrates optimality. This is a structural alphabet-threshold result, not a rewording of the unrestricted spectrum.

## Limitations

No minimal total-state witness size or left-quotient spectrum is claimed. Finite computational checks are corroborative. Historical priority is not guaranteed, and independent validation has not occurred.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
