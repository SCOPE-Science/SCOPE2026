# Scientific review: cubic harmonic concentration on antipodal caps

## Correctness

**PASS.** The claim is an all-dimensional optimization over the entire pure degree-three spherical-harmonic space, not a numerical experiment or a restriction to zonal harmonics. Four explicit, pairwise-orthogonal harmonic subspaces exhaust that space by their dimension sum. Rotation symmetry makes each a scalar eigenspace of the concentration operator. Their normalized beta-energy integrals yield four exact factorizations of eigenvalue differences; signs give precisely the two transitions and all equality cases. The scalar normalizations and differential factorizations were separately checked by exact rational arithmetic for 104 dimensions, and independent quadrature at the transition locations for four dimensions. The all-dimensional proof rests on displayed symbolic identities, not on these finite checks.

## Originality

**PASS on the inspected evidence, with residual risk.** Simons–Dahlen (arXiv:math/0603271v1) studies the *same double-cap geometry* in dimension three and supplies general harmonic-bandwidth matrix formulas and numerical eigenfunction examples. Simons–Dahlen–Wieczorek (arXiv:math/0408424v1) supplies the single-cap Slepian framework; Bates–Khalid–Kennedy (arXiv:1608.05479) supplies numerical methods for large bandlimits in dimension three. None of the inspected sections and appendices states the dimension-uniform fixed-degree-three sharp thresholds, three-way eigenvalue collision, or full optimizing-space classification. The general matrix formulations implicitly allow calculations of individual two-sphere instances, but neither their dimensions nor their statements imply the universal result without a new reduction and four exact comparisons. A related accessible degree-two calculation has only one transition and does not imply the degree-three classification. Published-record similarity searches found adjacent hemisphere, spherical-curvature and cap-concentration topics rather than equivalent results. A specialized unindexed or implicit formula remains possible; search cannot prove uniqueness.

## Value

**PASS.** The pair \(t=1/(d+2),3/(d+2)\) resolves an intrinsically motivated operator invariant: exactly which cubic harmonic concentrates best in the two observation gaps of a sphere. The first nontrivial three-stage change among inequivalent harmonic types has a large triple collision, with a precisely determined multiplicity \(d(d+1)/2-1\), and a second collision of multiplicity \(d\). The answer is dimension-uniform and yields sharp complete optimizer spaces rather than only a few numerical eigenvalues. This is not a mere reparametrization or a one-off arbitrary example.

## Closest literature and limits

The closest same-region source is *Spherical Slepian functions and the polar gap in geodesy* (Simons and Dahlen; arXiv:math/0603271v1), especially §5.2 and Appendix A/B. General all-degree numerical Slepian matrices do not by themselves state these phase transitions. Results on hemispherical mask eigenvalues concern a different region. A related degree-two double-cap result is a lower-degree calculation and does not decide the cubic competition. The theorem is for pure degree three, equal antipodal caps, and \(d\ge3\); it does not cover mixed degrees, unequal caps, or higher harmonics. Independent scientific review remains outstanding.
