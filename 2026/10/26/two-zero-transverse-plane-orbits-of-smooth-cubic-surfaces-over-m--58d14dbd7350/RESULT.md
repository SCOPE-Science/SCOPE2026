# Two zero-transverse-plane orbits of smooth cubic surfaces over \(\mathbf F_2\)

## Finding

Let \(S\subset\mathbf P^3_{\mathbf F_2}\) be a geometrically smooth cubic surface, defined by a nonzero homogeneous cubic. Write \(t(S)\) for the number of \(\mathbf F_2\)-planes \(H\) for which \(S\cap H\) is geometrically smooth. Precisely \(1708\) of the \(322560\) geometrically smooth cubic equations have \(t(S)=0\). These exceptional surfaces form exactly two \(\operatorname{PGL}_4(\mathbf F_2)\)-orbits, represented by
\[
S_0:\;xw(x+w)+yz(y+z)=0,\qquad
S_1:\;xw(x+w)+yz(y+z)+yzw=0.
\]
The orbit sizes are respectively \(28\) and \(1680\), and their projective stabilizers have orders \(720\) and \(12\).

The complete transverse-plane spectrum, counted by nonzero coefficient vectors (equivalently projective equations, since \(\mathbf F_2^*=\{1\}\)), is:

| \(t(S)\) | Number of smooth cubic surfaces |
|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1708 |
| 4 | 11340 |
| 6 | 68320 |
| 7 | 10080 |
| 8 | 94500 |
| 9 | 60480 |
| 10 | 64512 |
| 11 | 10080 |
| 12 | 1540 |
| **Total** | **322560** |

Thus a smooth binary cubic surface either has no geometrically smooth rational hyperplane section or has at least four; both alternatives occur. In particular, the obstruction to finite-field Bertini smoothness is not confined to the \(28\) space-filling cubics in the first orbit.

## Assumptions and scope

The ground field is exactly \(\mathbf F_2\). All smoothness assertions mean smoothness after base change to an algebraic closure, not merely regularity at rational points. A rational plane is a codimension-one projective linear subspace of \(\mathbf P^3\) defined over \(\mathbf F_2\); there are \(15\) such planes. Counts refer to fixed homogeneous coordinates, with no scalar multiplicities beyond the unique nonzero binary scalar. No claim is made over larger fields.

## Proof

**1. Exhaustive parameterization.** The space of cubic forms in four variables has monomial basis \(x^ay^bz^cw^d\) with \(a+b+c+d=3\), hence dimension \(20\) over \(\mathbf F_2\). Enumerating all \(2^{20}-1\) nonzero coefficient vectors is exhaustive. The exact deterministic implementation is `check_sections.cpp`.

**2. Criterion for geometric smoothness of a surface.** Let \(F\) be a ternary-degree-three homogeneous form in four variables and let \(J_F\) be the ideal generated by its four homogeneous quadratic partial derivatives. By Euler's identity in characteristic two, a geometric singular point of \(V(F)\) is exactly a projective common zero of these four derivatives. They have no projective common zero precisely when they form a homogeneous system of parameters, equivalently a regular sequence in the polynomial ring of four variables. In that case the graded quotient has Hilbert series \((1+t)^4\) and vanishes in degree \(5\). Conversely, vanishing of the degree-five graded component implies that the quotient is Artinian, so there is no projective common zero. The program tests that the eighty products of a partial derivative with a cubic monomial span all \(\binom{8}{3}=56\) quintic monomials over \(\mathbf F_2\), by exact binary row reduction. This condition is therefore necessary and sufficient for geometric smoothness. It gives \(322560\) smooth equations, independently of any finite-extension point-search cutoff.

**3. Criterion for smooth plane cubic sections.** Restrict each surface cubic to each of the fifteen rational planes, choosing any linear basis of the plane's three-dimensional vector space. One obtains one of \(2^{10}\) ternary cubic forms. A geometrically singular plane cubic over \(\mathbf F_2\) has a geometric singular point of residue degree at most \(3\): if it is geometrically irreducible, its arithmetic genus is \(1\), so there is at most one geometric singular point and Frobenius fixes it; if geometrically reducible, intersections of geometric components provide singular points, and the degree-three total degree permits either a line and a conic (intersection degree at most \(2\)) or at most three lines (Frobenius orbits of length at most \(3\)). A multiple component has a singular point in one of these same extensions. Hence checking partial derivatives at all points of \(\mathbf P^2(\mathbf F_{2^r})\) for \(r=1,2,3\) is an exact criterion, not a finite approximation. For a homogeneous cubic, Euler again makes the equation redundant when all derivatives vanish. Exact arithmetic using \(\mathbf F_4=\mathbf F_2[u]/(u^2+u+1)\) and \(\mathbf F_8=\mathbf F_2[u]/(u^3+u+1)\) finds \(336\) smooth ternary cubic forms, including no zero form.

**4. Enumeration and group orbits.** Computing those two exact predicates for every cubic form and all fifteen restrictions gives the ten-row spectrum above, including \(1708\) smooth equations with no smooth section. To classify the exceptional locus, `check_orbits.py` applies all elementary coordinate shears and adjacent transpositions; these generate \(\operatorname{GL}_4(\mathbf F_2)=\operatorname{PGL}_4(\mathbf F_2)\). Breadth-first orbit enumeration of the actual \(1708\) exceptional coefficient vectors gives orbit sizes \(28\) and \(1680\), with respective least-index representatives \(S_0\) and \(S_1\). The projective group has order \(20160\), yielding stabilizers \(20160/28=720\) and \(20160/1680=12\).

**5. Direct geometric witnesses.** Both displayed cubics are geometrically smooth: for \(S_0\) the partial derivatives are \((w^2,z^2,y^2,x^2)\), while for \(S_1\) they are \((w^2,z^2+zw,y^2+yw,x^2+yz)\), in the order \((x,y,z,w)\). Either tuple can vanish only at the origin. `check_representatives.py` independently restricts each polynomial to all fifteen planes and uses exact Gröbner bases over \(\mathbf F_2\) in each affine chart; each restricted cubic has a geometric singular point. The first example contains every rational point of projective three-space, but the second does not, as witnessed at \([0:1:1:1]\). Therefore the two exceptional classes have a meaningful geometric difference.

## Verification

Recompile `check_sections.cpp` with a C++17 compiler using `-O3` and run it in the directory containing the source. It writes `zero_cubics.txt` containing the \(1708\) exceptional masks, and reports the full ten-row spectrum. Run `python check_orbits.py` to re-enumerate the two complete orbits, and `python check_representatives.py` with SymPy to repeat the independent Gröbner tests of all thirty sections of the two representatives. Compare output with the three included `.out` files. All three programs were executed from the included source files; all checks completed successfully. The enumeration is finite and exhaustive only for binary cubic hypersurfaces; it says nothing by itself about other characteristics or degrees.

## Relationship to prior work

Asgarli–Duan–Lai, *Transverse linear subspaces to hypersurfaces over finite fields* (first available 2020), establish general sufficient inequalities for transverse sections, not this complete \(q=2\), degree-three census. Vikulova (first available 2023) proves that \(S_0\) is the unique maximally symmetric binary cubic surface with automorphism group \(S_6\) of order \(720\), but does not classify the zero-transverse-section property or identify the second orbit. Earlier studies of rational lines, rational points, and Dickson's binary cubic surface types do not, in the material inspected, give the above hyperplane-section distribution. The existence and representation of the \(28\)-class is not itself asserted to be new.

## Limitations

The novelty assessment is conditional on the accessible texts: a full copy of Karaoğlu (2021) was not accessible through the journal download (HTTP 403), and older Dickson tables or unindexed classifications may encode equivalent statements. The explicit new comparison is the incidence property of all fifteen rational hyperplanes and the second orbit, not the previously known maximally symmetric orbit. No independent audit has been performed.
