# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The renewal formula is derived from a first-reset decomposition. The scale-normalized logarithmic derivative at the two endpoints of each predicted window is forced to have opposite signs by an explicit uniform bound less than \(0.01\), independent of the number of reset locations. Each disjoint interval therefore contains a strict local minimum, with strict local maxima between them. The checker confirms 1,287 finite assertions; the all-\(N\) theorem rests on analytic inequalities, not enumeration.

**Originality — PASS (self-review; priority risk remains).** Julián-Salgado, Dagdug and Boyer derive the N-site mean formula and demonstrate two-site metastability. Besga et al. demonstrate a metastable Gaussian-reset optimum. Méndez et al. cover other distributed-reset questions and fluctuations. The explicit uniform all-\(N\) lower bound on distinct rate minima does not follow from the inspected two-site phase diagram without a new constructive argument. Available prior claims and semantic research records were searched for equivalent/dominating results. The possibility of a stronger unlocated general theorem remains a priority risk.

**Scientific value — PASS.** The theorem reveals unbounded local-optimum complexity in a one-parameter physical search mechanism and identifies the geometric separation that produces its distinct competing timescales. It rules out any universal finite bound on local reset-rate minima across all finitely supported resetting distributions.

**Limitations.** The minima are local, not asserted to be simultaneous global minima. The proof applies to constant-diffusivity Brownian motion and instantaneous Poisson resetting with a fixed finite mixture of sites. No independent validation or exhaustive scholarly priority assessment is claimed.
