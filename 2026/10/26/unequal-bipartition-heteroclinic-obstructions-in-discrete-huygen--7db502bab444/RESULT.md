# Unequal-bipartition heteroclinic obstructions in discrete Huygens trees

## Finding

Consider a finite unit-weight tree \(T\), with its bipartition \(A\sqcup B\), edge set \(E\), and the reduced repulsive sinusoidal Huygens map
\[
G_h(z)=z+hK\sin z\pmod{2\pi},\qquad K=B_T^{\mathsf T}B_T,
\]
where all edges are oriented consistently from \(A\) to \(B\), \(B_T\) is the oriented incidence matrix, and sine acts coordinatewise. Let \(m=|E|=|V(T)|-1\).

**Signed-cut classification.** Fix positive integers \(s,t\) satisfying \(|A|s=|B|t\). For every edge \(e\), delete \(e\) and let \(C_e\) be the resulting component containing the endpoint of \(e\) in \(A\). Set
\[
D_e=s|A\cap C_e|-t|B\cap C_e|.
\]
There exists a sign vector \(d\in\{-1,1\}^{E}\) for which the incident-sign sum equals \(s\) on every \(A\)-vertex and \(t\) on every \(B\)-vertex **if and only if** \(|D_e|=1\) for every edge. In that event \(d_e=D_e\), uniquely. Moreover, **every** sign vector with \(Kd=c\mathbf1\) for a positive constant \(c\) arises this way, with \(c=s+t\).

**Dynamical obstruction.** If these cuts produce at least one negative edge, then for **every** \(0<h<1/\lambda_{\max}(K)\) the map \(G_h\) has a heteroclinic arc along which the stable and unstable tangent spaces coincide and are proper subspaces; hence \(G_h\) is not Morse–Smale. If there are \(r\ge1\) negative edges, both tangent spaces have dimension \(m-r\).

**Sharp unequal-class onset.** Among trees with such a mixed-sign obstruction and **unequal** incident sums \(s\ne t\), the smallest order is exactly **nine vertices**. At nine vertices the signed tree is unique up to signed graph isomorphism. It has bipartition cardinalities \((6,3)\), incident sums \((1,2)\), eight edges, one negative edge, and coincident seven-dimensional stable/unstable tangent spaces along a nontransverse arc in the eight-dimensional reduced phase space.

There are infinitely many further examples with unequal sums: for every odd integer \(b\ge3\) and integer \(k\ge2\), one can construct a unit-weight tree on \(b(k+1)\) vertices with \((s,t)=(1,k)\), \((b-1)/2\) negative edges, and scalar heteroclinic dynamics \(u\mapsto u+(k+1)h\sin u\).

## Assumptions and scope

All trees are finite, simple, connected, unit-weight and have at least two vertices. The phase-difference space is the full \(m\)-torus because a tree has no cycle relations. The step bound is open and strict. An "unequal-class obstruction" refers precisely to a mixed-sign diagonal invariant circle generated by a binary critical-point sign vector, not to every conceivable heteroclinic intersection. The nine-vertex minimality and uniqueness are **within this mechanism**, not a claim that nine is the first size with arbitrary nontransversality; a six-vertex balanced-sign obstruction is known.

## Proof

**1. Exact signed-cut criterion.** With the consistent bipartition orientation, \(K_{ee}=2\), and two distinct edges have \(K_{ef}=1\) exactly when they share a vertex. Write \(S_v=\sum_{e\ni v}d_e\). Then
\[
(Kd)_{uv}=S_u+S_v\quad (uv\in E).
\]
Thus \(Kd=c\mathbf1\) exactly when \(S_u+S_v=c\) at each edge. Connected bipartiteness forces \(S_v=s\) throughout \(A\) and \(S_v=t\) throughout \(B\), with \(c=s+t\). Summing over either vertex class yields
\[
|A|s=|B|t=\sum_e d_e=:q.
\]
When \(c>0\), both \(s,t\) must be positive: since \(q\) has the same sign as both, \(s+t>0\) excludes nonpositive \(q\).

Orient each edge toward \(B\), but choose the signed incidence convention \(+1\) at its \(A\)-endpoint and \(-1\) at its \(B\)-endpoint. The signed vertex equations become \(Jd=f\), where \(f=s\) on \(A\) and \(f=-t\) on \(B\). A tree's incidence matrix has rank \(|V|-1\), and the linear system has a unique real solution whenever \(\sum_v f_v=|A|s-|B|t=0\). Summing \(Jd=f\) over \(C_e\) cancels every internal edge and leaves exactly \(d_e=D_e\). Conversely, those cut values solve \(Jd=f\) by uniqueness (or repeated leaf elimination). The solution consists of signs precisely when every \(|D_e|=1\).

**2. Heteroclinic arc and transversality.** Suppose the cuts give \(r\ge1\) negative edges. Let \(\Delta=\operatorname{diag}(d_e)\), let \(p_e=0\) for \(d_e=1\) and \(p_e=\pi\) for \(d_e=-1\), and let \(q=p+\pi\mathbf1\) on the torus. Since \(\sin(p+u\mathbf1)=d\sin u\) and \(Kd=(s+t)\mathbf1=c\mathbf1\),
\[
G_h(p+u\mathbf1)=p+[u+ch\sin u]\mathbf1.
\]
The matrix \(K\) is positive definite because the tree incidence has independent columns. The matrix \(K\Delta\) is similar to the symmetric \(K^{1/2}\Delta K^{1/2}\), has \(m-r\) positive and \(r\) negative real eigenvalues, and every eigenvalue has magnitude at most \(\lambda_{\max}(K)\). Since \(K\Delta\mathbf1=c\mathbf1\), this also implies \(0<ch<1\) under the asserted step restriction. Hence the scalar map \(u\mapsto u+ch\sin u\) is strictly increasing on \([0,\pi]\), fixes only its endpoints, and advances strictly inside. Every orbit with \(0<u<\pi\) tends to \(0\) backward and to \(\pi\) forward.

Along this heteroclinic arc, the derivative is \(I+h\cos u\,K\Delta\). All derivatives preserve the same positive-eigenvalue subspace \(E_+\); every spectral factor is positive. At \(p\), exactly \(E_+\) is unstable; at \(q\), exactly \(E_+\) is stable. Forward and backward linear asymptotics along the orbit and the stable-manifold theorem give
\[
T_zW^u(p)=T_zW^s(q)=E_+,\qquad \dim E_+=m-r<m.
\]
Their sum therefore is not the full ambient tangent space, proving nontransversality. The step bound also guarantees the reduced map is a diffeomorphism.

**3. The sharp size nine.** With \(n=|V(T)|\) and \(r\ge1\),
\[
q=\sum_e d_e=n-1-2r>0,
\qquad |A|s=|B|t=q\le n-3.
\]
When \(s\ne t\), \(|A|\ne|B|\), and \(\operatorname{lcm}(|A|,|B|)\) divides \(q\). For each \(2\le n\le8\), every pair of unequal positive integers with sum \(n\) has least common multiple greater than \(n-3\). The smallest such least common multiples for \(n=3,4,5,6,7,8\) are respectively \(2,3,4,4,6,6\), whereas the corresponding upper bounds are \(0,1,2,3,4,5\). Thus no smaller tree works.

At \(n=9\), the only possible unordered class sizes are \((3,6)\), with \(q=6\), \(r=1\), and signed sums \((2,1)\). Take a class of six vertices with signed sum one and a class of three with signed sum two. Their respective degree sequences must be \((3,1,1,1,1,1)\) and \((4,2,2)\): parity, positivity and each side's total degree eight force these. The unique negative edge must join their unique degree-three and degree-four vertices. Every remaining edge is positive. Connectivity now forces that degree-three vertex to attach to the two degree-two vertices, each of which has one leaf; the degree-four vertex attaches to the remaining three leaves. This determines the signed tree uniquely up to isomorphism.

Explicitly, let \(a_0\) meet \(b_1,b_2,b_3\); attach one leaf to each of \(b_1,b_2\) and three to \(b_3\). Place a negative sign only on \(a_0b_3\) and positive signs on the other seven edges. Then \(S_v=1\) on the class consisting of \(a_0\) and five leaves, and \(S_v=2\) at all three \(b_i\), so \(Kd=3\mathbf1\). There is one negative edge, and the seven-dimensional common tangent space is proper in dimension eight.

**4. Arbitrarily large unequal-class examples.** Choose odd \(b\ge3\) and \(k\ge2\). Attach \(b\) branch vertices to one central vertex; declare \((b+1)/2\) spokes positive and \((b-1)/2\) negative. At a positive-spoke branch attach \(k-1\) positive leaf edges, and at a negative-spoke branch attach \(k+1\) positive leaf edges. The central signed sum is one, each leaf sum is one, and every branch sum is \(k\). The tree has \(b(k+1)\) vertices and exactly \((b-1)/2\) negative edges. Parts 1–2 therefore apply with \(c=k+1\).

## Verification

The included standalone standard-library script `verify.py` checks signed sums, the exact matrix identity \(Kd=(k+1)\mathbf1\), every tree-edge cut and the invariant-circle update on 15 constructed trees. It also checks the strict necessary divisibility exclusion for every smaller bipartition, and the unique feasible numeric parameter pair at size nine. The script prints `VERIFY_OK` on success. The finite tests support the explicitly proved universal results; they do not establish the infinite claims by enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work

Buescu, D’Aniello and Oliveira, *Morse–Smale dynamics in discrete Huygens networks*, arXiv:2610.10993v1 (first public October 7, 2026), Proposition 5.11, establish nontransverse heteroclinics when **every vertex** has signed incident sum one. Their resulting trees have \(4r+2\) vertices and equal bipartition sizes; Proposition 5.12 gives their six-vertex example. The present argument credits and adapts their invariant-line and tangent-space mechanism. What is added is the exact signed-cut characterization, the unequal-class family, and the sharp minimum and uniqueness at nine vertices, which do not satisfy their vertexwise-one hypothesis. Earlier studies of small Huygens rings and path phase maps concern different networks or dynamical questions.

## Limitations

The criterion is for this particular all-ones-direction invariant-circle obstruction. Failure of its signed-cut test does **not** establish Morse–Smale dynamics; other nontransverse mechanisms may exist. The result assumes unit weights and the sinusoidal odd law, and only the open invertibility interval is analyzed. The nine-vertex minimum is restricted to unequal-class diagonal obstructions, not all nontransverse Huygens trees. The method extends an explicit prior proof, and undiscovered related signed-flow literature remains a bibliographic-priority risk. No independent audit has been performed.
