# Uniform Gaussian leaf fluctuations in regular complete multipartite spanning trees

## Finding
Let \(n=rk\ge4\), with integers \(r\ge2\), \(k\ge1\). Draw a uniform spanning tree \(T\) of the graph \(K_{k,\ldots,k}\), consisting of \(r\) parts of size \(k\), and write \(L\) for its number of leaves. Put \(d=n-k\), and define
\[
\begin{aligned}
p&=(1-1/n)^{r-2}(1-1/d)^{(r-1)(k-1)},\\
s&=(1-2/n)^{r-2}(1-2/d)^{(r-1)(k-1)},\\
t&=(1-2/n)^{r-2}(1-1/d)^{2(k-1)}(1-2/d)^{(r-2)(k-1)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here an exponent zero denotes the factor one. The exact first and second moments are
\[
\mathbb E L=np,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(L)=np(1-p)+n(k-1)(s-p^2)+n(n-k)(t-p^2).
\]
**Theorem (uniform multipartite leaf CLT).** For every sequence \((r_n,k_n)\) of positive integers with \(r_n\ge2\), \(n=r_nk_n\to\infty\), without any further restriction on the number of parts,
\[
\mathbb E L=n/e+O(1),\quad
\operatorname{Var}(L)=\left(e^{-1}-2e^{-2}\right)n+O(1),\quad
\frac{L-\mathbb E L}{\sqrt n}\ \xrightarrow{d}\ N\!\left(0,e^{-1}-2e^{-2}\right).
\]
The implied constants are absolute and uniform over all equal-part factorizations. The leading variance \(e^{-1}-2e^{-2}\approx0.0972088747\) is universal from complete graphs to complete balanced bipartite graphs.

## Assumptions and scope
All trees are labeled, unweighted, and uniformly selected. Degrees are ordinary graph degrees; a leaf has degree one. The graph must have at least two nonempty equal-sized parts and \(n\ge4\). The theorem is uniform over integer factorizations, including fixed \(r\), fixed \(k\), and diverging \(r,k\). No assertion is made for unequal part sizes or edge-weighted tree measures.

## Proof
**1. Independent allocation representation (previously known enumeration).** The multipartite degree enumerator of Eğecioğlu and Remmel [1] implies the normalized multivariate probability generating polynomial
\[
\mathbb E\prod_{v}z_v^{\deg_T(v)-1}
=\left(\frac{\sum_v z_v}{n}\right)^{r-2}
\prod_{i=1}^r\left(\frac{\sum_{v\notin V_i}z_v}{n-k}\right)^{k-1}.
\]
Thus \(\deg_T(v)-1\) has the joint law of occupancy counts generated by \(r-2\) independent uniform choices among all \(n\) vertices and, independently for each part \(V_i\), \(k-1\) independent choices among the \(n-k\) vertices outside \(V_i\). This exact representation, not the new result, is taken from [1]. A vertex is a leaf precisely when its occupancy count is zero.

**2. Exact two-site laws.** For a fixed vertex, the probability of no occupancy is \(p\). For two distinct vertices in the same part, the probability that both are empty is \(s\), since every permitted draw avoids two vertices. For distinct vertices in different parts, draws sourced from either marked part avoid one vertex, and all other draws avoid two, yielding \(t\). There are \(n(k-1)\) ordered same-part distinct pairs and \(n(n-k)\) ordered different-part pairs. Expanding \(L^2\) proves the exact expectation and variance claimed.

**3. A uniform variance computation.** The total number of draws is \(n-2\), each draw has a choice set of cardinality at least \(n/2\). The total first-order load on any vertex is exactly
\[
\frac{r-2}{n}+\frac{(r-1)(k-1)}{n-k}=\frac{n-2}{n}.
\]
Taylor's formula for \(\log(1-x)\) therefore yields \(p=e^{-1}+O(n^{-1})\) uniformly. Put \(h(a)=\log[(1-2/a)/(1-1/a)^2]=-a^{-2}+O(a^{-3})\). Direct cancellation gives
\[
\log\frac{s}{p^2}=(r-2)h(n)+(r-1)(k-1)h(n-k),\qquad
\log\frac{t}{p^2}=(r-2)h(n)+(r-2)(k-1)h(n-k).
\]
Both logarithms are \(O(n^{-1})\) uniformly because each exponent is at most \(n\) and each denominator at least \(n/2\). Inserting \(e^x-1=x+O(x^2)\) in the variance expression gives
\[
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(L)}n=p(1-p)-p^2 B_{n,k}+O(n^{-1}),
\]
where
\[
 B_{n,k}=\frac{(r-2)(n-1)}{n^2}
 +\frac{(k-1)[(r-1)(k-1)+(r-2)(n-k)]}{(n-k)^2}
 =1-\frac{(3n-2k)(n-1)}{n^2(n-k)}.
\]
As \(1\le k\le n/2\), we have \(B_{n,k}=1+O(n^{-1})\) uniformly. Consequently \(\operatorname{Var}(L)/n=e^{-1}-2e^{-2}+O(n^{-1})\), proving the second moment and its strictly positive limiting coefficient.

**4. Joint cumulant bound and Gaussian limit.** We prove the necessary weak-dependence bound rather than treating numerical data as a CLT. Let \(I_v\) indicate that site \(v\) receives zero draws. For any fixed \(j\ge2\) and any \(j\) distinct vertices \(v_1,\ldots,v_j\),
\[
 |\kappa(I_{v_1},\ldots,I_{v_j})|\le C_j n^{1-j},
\]
with \(C_j\) independent of the part sizes. Indeed, for subsets \(A\) of these sites, exact independence of the underlying draws gives
\[
 q(A):=\mathbb E\prod_{v\in A}I_v
 =\prod_{g}\left(1-\frac{|A\cap S_g|}{|S_g|}\right)^{m_g},
\]
where \(S_g\) is a draw's allowed vertex set and \(m_g\) its multiplicity; \(\sum_gm_g=n-2\) and every \(|S_g|\ge n/2\). For \(|A|\le j\) and large \(n\), define the Möbius interactions
\[
 \theta(B)=\sum_{A\subseteq B}(-1)^{|B|-|A|}\log q(A).
\]
Expanding the logarithm into its absolutely convergent power series shows \(|\theta(B)|\le C_j n^{1-|B|}\): for a set of \(b\) distinct sites the first noncanceling term in the finite difference has polynomial degree at least \(b\), and every denominator is at least \(n/2\). In particular
\[
 q(A)=\prod_{v\in A}q(\{v\})
 \prod_{\substack{B\subseteq A\\|B|\ge2}}(1+w_B),\quad
 w_B=e^{\theta(B)}-1=O_j(n^{1-|B|}).
\]
Expand the standard moment–cumulant partition sum using these factors. Every disconnected hypergraph of selected interactions cancels, by the partition-lattice Möbius identity. A connected hypergraph covering \(j\) distinct sites has \(\sum_B(|B|-1)\ge j-1\), so each surviving term is \(O_j(n^{-(j-1)})\). This proves the bound. If labels repeat, the idempotence \(I_v^a=I_v\) creates interactions of bounded size between copies of the same site; the same connected-partition argument then gives \(O_j(n^{1-d})\) for a cumulant with \(d\) distinct underlying sites.

For any fixed \(j\), summing over all ordered \(j\)-tuples of vertices according to their \(d\) distinct sites now gives \(\kappa_j(L)=O_j(n)\). The second cumulant satisfies \(\kappa_2(L)/n\to\sigma^2=e^{-1}-2e^{-2}>0\). For every fixed \(j\ge3\),
\[
 \kappa_j\bigl((L-\mathbb EL)/\sqrt n\bigr)=O_j(n^{1-j/2})\longrightarrow0.
\]
The moment–cumulant formula hence yields convergence of all centered moments to the corresponding normal moments. Since the normal distribution is moment-determinate, the method of moments proves the claimed CLT for every changing sequence \((r_n,k_n)\). The bounds throughout depend on \(j\), not on the factorization.

## Verification
The accompanying `check.py` independently enumerates all spanning trees of representative balanced multipartite graphs on four to seven vertices and compares exact rational first and second moments to the stated formulas; it also prints finite-size variance coefficients for complete, matching-complement, and balanced bipartite host graphs. The exhaustive checks establish only those small instances. The unrestricted covariance and Gaussian statements depend on the analytic arguments in Steps 1–4.

## Relationship to prior work
Eğecioğlu and Remmel [1] proved the multipartite degree generating function and the limiting probability that a uniformly marked vertex is a leaf; they explicitly obtain \(e^{-1}\) for equal parts. That generating function is used as an input, not claimed as new. Their Section 6 computes one-vertex leaf probabilities, not a centered total-leaf Gaussian law uniformly over varying numbers of parts. Fita Sanmartín, Schnörr, and Hamprecht [2] study degree moments of specified vertices in general Gibbs spanning-tree measures; their degree formulas do not assert the uniform total-leaf CLT or its universal second-order constant. The contribution here is the all-factorizations Gaussian limit, its uniform second-order law, and explicit finite second moments. The previously known degree enumerator necessarily contains the finite leaf distribution implicitly, so priority is claimed for the extracted joint fluctuation theorem rather than for the raw enumeration.

## Limitations
The Gaussian theorem concerns the scalar total number of leaves, not the joint law of degrees or the count of leaves by part. It requires exactly equal part sizes and unweighted, uniform spanning trees. The proof establishes asymptotic convergence, not a Berry–Esseen bound or large-deviation principle. The historical-priority assessment is based on the inspected literature and searches, not an exhaustive mathematical bibliography; independent validation has not been performed.
