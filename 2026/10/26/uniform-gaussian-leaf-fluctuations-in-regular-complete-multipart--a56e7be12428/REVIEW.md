# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The multipartite degree-allocation polynomial is an explicit prior theorem, and empty-occupancy events give exact one- and two-leaf probabilities. The asymptotic variance calculation has uniform error bounds since all drawing sets have size at least half the number of vertices. The nontrivial Gaussian step is supplied by the connected-hypergraph cumulant estimate, with derivation via discrete logarithmic finite differences, rather than by finite checks. Exact enumeration independently verifies representative small cases. The proof covers changing part numbers and part sizes.

**Originality — PASS, with a priority risk.** The 1994 multipartite degree enumerator implicitly determines the entire finite degree distribution, and its single-vertex leaf probability is not a new result. The new statement is the uniform total-leaf central limit theorem and the universal nonzero second-order coefficient over all equal-part factorizations, which require a multi-site fluctuation analysis not included in the inspected leaf-probability results. The 2024 random-spanning-tree degree-moment work concerns individual node degrees. Mathematical-record searches for the exact leaf CLT and alternate terms did not identify direct coverage. The exact enumerator might support an unlocated earlier asymptotic extraction; this is the main residual risk.

**Value — PASS.** A total-leaf Gaussian law invariant under arbitrarily changing multipartite density connects the dense bipartite and complete-tree regimes and quantifies fluctuations beyond the classical universal first moment. The invariant is the natural global leaf count, the asymptotic variance is strictly positive, and the result does not rely on choosing arbitrary parameter slices.

**Limits.** Uniform unweighted spanning trees with equal nonempty parts only; neither a local limit nor a concentration rate is established. Historical priority cannot be certified from a bounded search.
