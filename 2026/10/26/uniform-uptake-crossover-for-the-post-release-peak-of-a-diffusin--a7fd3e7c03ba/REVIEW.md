# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The Gaussian loss kernel is derived from the homogeneous three-dimensional diffusion Green function. Its strictly unimodal impulse response establishes the unique finite-pulse maximum. Equating the post-pulse response slopes leads exactly to the scalar equation \(z-\log z=B_T+E_T(z)\), and the explicit error bound \(0<E_T(z)\le2a/(pT)\) proves the stated two-term asymptotic *uniformly in uptake rate*. The submitted script checks representative parameter ranges, but the proof—not the samples—supports the infinite-domain claim.

**Originality — PASS, narrowly qualified.** The 1997 iontophoresis paper explicitly reports delayed maxima after finite pulses; the 2008 brain-diffusion review gives the constant point-source response with and without uptake. Both premises and the qualitative delay are acknowledged. Related prior work using the same loss-free mathematical kernel already establishes finite-pulse peak uniqueness and its time brackets; those are not claimed as new. The inspected materials did not state a uniform two-term large-duration expansion for the *whole uptake interval* or its sharp crossover between inverse-log and inverse-duration scaling. This is a bounded originality conclusion, not independent certification: uninspected sections and general asymptotic inversion literature pose specific residual coverage risks.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The crossover distinguishes two qualitatively different late-sampling regimes of finite point-source pulses. A uniform bound makes the statement valid when the physical removal rate and experimental pulse length vary jointly, rather than treating only two fixed-parameter cases. It directly identifies the relevant dimensionless competition between uptake exposure and the logarithm of pulse duration. This is a substantive asymptotic boundary of a motivated transport model, without suggesting that the underlying Green function is new.

**Scope and limits.** The result needs an infinite homogeneous isotropic medium, linear uptake, fixed strictly positive source-detector separation, and constant point-source strength. It neither accounts for heterogeneous brain tissue nor demonstrates identifiability of a biological uptake rate. An open online posting date of 2006-03-27 was verified for the 1997 article; the day of its 1997 print issue was not verified.
