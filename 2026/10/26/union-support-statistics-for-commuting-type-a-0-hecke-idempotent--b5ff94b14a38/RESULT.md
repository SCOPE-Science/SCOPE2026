# Union-support statistics for commuting type-A \(0\)-Hecke idempotents

## Finding
For every integer \(m\ge0\), the idempotents of the type-A \(0\)-Hecke monoid \(H_0(S_{m+1})\) are uniquely indexed by subsets \(J\subseteq[m]\) as parabolic-longest elements \(e_J\). For subsets \(J,K\subseteq[m]\), the following are equivalent:

1. \(e_Je_K=e_Ke_J\).
2. Every maximal consecutive interval of \(J\cup K\) is contained entirely in \(J\) or entirely in \(K\).

When they hold, \(e_Je_K=e_{J\cup K}\). Let
\[
P_m(z)=\sum_{J,K\subseteq[m]\,:\,e_Je_K=e_Ke_J}z^{|J\cup K|}.
\]
The full union-support-size distribution satisfies
\[
\boxed{\sum_{m\ge0}P_m(z)t^m=\frac1{1-(1+3z)t+2z^2t^2}.}
\]
Thus \(P_0(z)=1\), \(P_1(z)=1+3z\), and for \(m\ge2\),
\[
P_m(z)=(1+3z)P_{m-1}(z)-2z^2P_{m-2}(z).
\]
The ordered-pair totals \(C_m=P_m(1)\) obey \(C_0=1\), \(C_1=4\), and \(C_m=4C_{m-1}-2C_{m-2}\), so
\[
\boxed{C_m=\frac{(2+\sqrt2)^{m+1}-(2-\sqrt2)^{m+1}}{2\sqrt2}.}
\]
The numerical totals are already recorded as OEIS A007070; novelty is not claimed for their numerical recurrence. The contribution assessed here is the union-support refinement and its limiting law; the commutation criterion is proved for self-containment, without a claim of priority for the previously studied noncommuting graph.

For a uniformly selected ordered commuting pair and \(U_m=|J\cup K|\),
\[
\mathbb E U_m=\left(1-\frac{\sqrt2}{4}\right)m+O(1),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(U_m)=\frac{\sqrt2}{8}m+O(1),
\]
and \(U_m/m\) converges in probability to \(1-\sqrt2/4\). For independently uniform idempotents, the exact commutation probability is \(C_m/4^m\), exponentially decreasing with base \((2+\sqrt2)/4\).

## Assumptions and scope
Fix \(m\ge0\). The finite monoid \(H_0(S_{m+1})\) has generators \(s_1,\ldots,s_m\), with \(s_i^2=s_i\), commuting relations at distance at least two, and braid relations \(s_is_{i+1}s_i=s_{i+1}s_is_{i+1}\). An element is represented by a permutation; right multiplication by \(s_i\) swaps positions \(i,i+1\) if their entries are increasing and otherwise leaves them unchanged. The product is the Demazure product, *not* the ordinary symmetric-group product.

For \(J\subseteq[m]\), the longest element \(e_J\) in the parabolic submonoid generated by \(J\) reverses each maximal block of positions connected by edges of \(J\). The empty-set element is the identity. All counts use **ordered** pairs, with no conjugacy identification. A connected component of \(J\cup K\) means a maximal interval of consecutive simple-generator indices; isolated excluded indices separate components.

## Proof
**1. Idempotent classification and support.** The idempotent classification is the standard parabolic-longest lemma, proved for Coxeter monoids in Elias–Ko, Lemma 2.9. It also follows directly: an idempotent \(w\) satisfies \(w=w^2\), so multiplying \(w\) on the right by any generator appearing in a reduced word for \(w\) cannot increase length; all these generators lie in the right descent set of \(w\), forcing \(w\) to be the longest element of its standard parabolic support. The presentation relations preserve the set of generator indices appearing in any word, hence \(\operatorname{supp}(uv)=\operatorname{supp}(u)\cup\operatorname{supp}(v)\).

**2. Necessity of the interval condition.** If \(e_J,e_K\) commute, then their product \(h=e_Je_K\) is idempotent; its support is \(J\cup K\), so the classification gives \(h=e_{J\cup K}\). Consider one component \(C=\{a,a+1,\ldots,b-1\}\) of \(J\cup K\), so \(e_{J\cup K}\) reverses all entries at positions \(a,\ldots,b\). Assume \(C\) is contained in neither \(J\) nor \(K\). Pick \(p\in C\setminus J\) and \(q\in C\setminus K\). A product realizing \(e_J\) acts only through adjacent swaps across indices in \(J\), followed by a product realizing \(e_K\) using only indices in \(K\). If \(p\le q\), the item initially at position \(a\) cannot cross edge \(p\) in the first stage, remains at or before \(p\), and then cannot cross edge \(q\) in the second stage; hence it cannot end at \(b\). If \(q<p\), the item initially at position \(b\) cannot cross edge \(p\) in the first stage, remains beyond \(p\), and then cannot cross edge \(q\) in the second stage; hence it cannot end at \(a\). Both contradict reversal. So each component lies in one of \(J,K\).

**3. Sufficiency and product identification.** If a component \(C\) lies wholly in \(J\), then \(e_J\) restricts to the full longest element there. This element absorbs the \(e_K\) factor on either side, by the elementary descent/absorption identity \(e_Cs_i=s_ie_C=e_C\) for all \(i\in C\). The argument is symmetric if \(C\subseteq K\). Distinct components act on disjoint, separated position blocks, hence commute with one another. Thus both ordered products equal \(e_{J\cup K}\).

**4. Exact count.** Assign each generator index one of the four statuses \(0\) (in neither), \(A\) (only \(J\)), \(B\) (only \(K\)), and \(D\) (in both). The criterion precisely says that any maximal run of nonzero statuses cannot contain both \(A\) and \(B\). A nonzero run of length \(r\ge1\) has \(2^{r+1}-1\) allowed statuses: \(\{A,D\}^r\cup\{B,D\}^r\) with the all-\(D\) word counted once. Its bivariate generating function is
\[
R(t,z)=\sum_{r\ge1}(2^{r+1}-1)(zt)^r
=\frac{4zt}{1-2zt}-\frac{zt}{1-zt}.
\]
An admissible full word is a sequence of nonzero runs separated by one or more zero statuses, allowing zeros at both ends. Accordingly its generating function equals
\[
F(t,z)=\frac{1+R(t,z)}{1-t(1+R(t,z))}.
\]
Since \(1+R(t,z)=1/[(1-2zt)(1-zt)]\), this reduces to \(F(t,z)=1/[1-(1+3z)t+2z^2t^2]\), proving the claimed identity and recurrence.

**5. Asymptotics.** The characteristic roots of the recurrence are
\[
\lambda_\pm(z)=\frac{1+3z\pm\sqrt{1+6z+z^2}}2.
\]
Near \(z=1\), \(\lambda_+(z)\) strictly dominates \(\lambda_-(z)\); the coefficient \(P_m(z)\) therefore equals a nonvanishing analytic factor times \(\lambda_+(z)^m\) plus an exponentially smaller term, uniformly in a neighborhood of \(z=1\). Taking the first and second derivatives of \(\log(P_m(e^s)/P_m(1))\) at \(s=0\) gives
\[
\left.z\frac{d}{dz}\log\lambda_+(z)\right|_{z=1}=1-\frac{\sqrt2}4,
\qquad
\left.z\frac{d}{dz}\left(z\frac{d}{dz}\log\lambda_+(z)\right)\right|_{z=1}=\frac{\sqrt2}8.
\]
The stated expectation, variance, and convergence in probability follow from these formulas and Chebyshev's inequality. The total-count closed form follows by specializing \(z=1\), with initial values \(1,4\).

## Verification
The included standalone \(\texttt{check_hecke.py}\) implements the Demazure product by bubble-sort reduced words independently of the interval criterion, constructs every permutation for \(m+1\le8\), identifies every idempotent, checks all ordered idempotent pairs and each union-support-rank coefficient. It examined \(46{,}233\) permutations for idempotency and \(21{,}845\) ordered idempotent pairs. Its successful completion prints \(\texttt{ALL\_TYPE\_A\_HECKE\_IDEMPOTENT\_CHECKS\_PASSED}\). The exact polynomial recurrence and its first eight total values are checked against independent enumeration. These finite tests corroborate but cannot replace the proof for unbounded \(m\).

## Relationship to prior work
Elias and Ko (2021 preprint, 2023 publication), Lemma 2.9, supply the prior classification of Coxeter-monoid idempotents as parabolic-longest elements. Li, Oh, Richmond, Yan, and You (2023 preprint) study efficient algorithms for arbitrary type-A and type-B Demazure products, rather than this commutation criterion. Denton (2011) studies orthogonal idempotents in the monoid *algebra*, a different notion from commuting idempotent elements of the underlying monoid. Berenstein, Greenstein, and Li (2026 preprint) investigate homomorphisms and certain abelian submonoids of special parabolic elements; their distinguished elements \(w_{J;K}\) are not the family of all parabolic-longest idempotents indexed by \(J\).

The unrefined integer sequence \(1,4,14,48,164,\ldots\) is OEIS A007070, with a previously known recurrence and numerous other interpretations; this work does **not** claim those integers, their recurrence, or their closed form as previously unknown in isolation. The mathematical claim assessed for originality is the union-support-rank refinement and resulting distributional information; the pairwise criterion is a self-contained proof ingredient, not an assertion of priority over earlier idempotent noncommuting-graph literature.

## Limitations
The rank-distribution theorem is about the type-A \(0\)-Hecke **monoid**, not arbitrary Hecke-algebra linear combinations, nor other Coxeter types. Existing abstract four-state word enumerations could give equivalent unrefined sequences without proving the same semigroup commutation statement. Sentinelli (2020) previously studied the noncommuting graph of this exact family, as cited by Sentinelli (2021, Remark 4.17). The 2020 paper's full text was inaccessible, so its implications for refined union-support counts remain a material priority risk. Absence from searches cannot establish uniqueness. Independent mathematical audit is not performed. The computational experiments cover only \(m\le7\).
