# Scientific review — optimal fixed-height Rayleigh–Taylor interface allocation

## Correctness (C): PASS

The result is an all-positive-parameter analytic classification in the inviscid, heavy-over-light, two-rigid-wall problem. The already-established forward relation is explicitly separated from the new constrained optimization theorem. For fixed wavenumber, a hyperbolic-cotangent addition identity reduces the density-weighted denominator to a positive constant plus \((at+b/t)/\sinh(kL)\); strict arithmetic–geometric mean equality yields exactly one interface location. For the globally optimized dispersion, a positive two-term cotangent decomposition makes the logarithmic elasticity a weighted mean of decreasing \(2z/\sinh(2z)\) functions. The derivative is the sum of strictly negative terms minus a nonnegative variance, proving strict decrease. A strictly increasing rational capillary term therefore intersects it exactly once. Monotonic height dependence and sharp shallow/deep limits follow directly from the same equations. There is no dependence on unverified infinite sampling, symbolic black boxes, or numerical-only sign evidence.

A standalone numerical checker passed 826 random fixed-wavenumber, 681 random full-optimum, 400 increasing-height, and six endpoint checks. Random samples outside well-conditioned verification ranges were skipped and are not counted as executed checks. The proof, rather than those finite tests, certifies all continuous parameter ranges. The model does not include viscosity, nonlinear effects, or any finite horizontal wavelength lattice.

## Originality (O): PASS, with a specific unresolved historical-priority risk

The closest foundational source is K. O. Mikaelian, *Physical Review A* **42**, 7211 (1990), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.42.7211: its detailed publisher abstract explicitly covers multilayer capillary Rayleigh–Taylor instability and the two finite layers between fixed boundaries. This supplies the classical **forward** operator and is stronger than an unrelated semi-infinite model. Its complete text could not be inspected; only the publisher abstract was available. As a result, an earlier explicit solution of the same height-budget optimization is possible and would defeat the priority claim; it has not been declared impossible.

The author's accessible full text of K. O. Mikaelian, *Physical Review E* **54**, 3676 (1996), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevE.54.3676, was examined from its introduction through the end of its references, including the exact inviscid two-layer relation as equation (2), Sections II and III, and its discussion of growth-rate peaks and finite-thickness trends. This reproduces the forward relation and reports numerical/approximate growth curves, but does not furnish the fixed-total-depth optimization, explicit optimal split, or global two-variable uniqueness classification. The classical infinite-depth maximizing wavenumber also gives only a limiting case; it does not imply the thickness-budget allocation theorem. Targeted scientific database, indexed-literature, and terminology/equivalence searches did not reveal the combined constrained optimization, while fixed-depth and equal-depth inverse problems do not themselves optimize the internal interface position. Search failure is not treated as proof of novelty.

The prior-claim implication comparison is therefore limited to the following defensible distinction: *given* a depth pair, one may evaluate the growth rate from old dispersion relations; *choosing* the partition of a fixed total depth to maximize it, identifying the unique optimizer for every wavenumber, and proving a unique global optimal wavenumber under that chosen partition are additional variational statements. There is no claim that their derivation requires new hydrodynamic equations. The 1990 full-text limitation remains the principal residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS

The parameter \(h/L\) describes how a fixed-height physical container is divided between fluids. Maximizing a dangerous growth rate over this placement and over wavelength is a natural sharp-design/worst-case problem, not an arbitrary parameter slice. The result is nontrivial even before wave optimization: the exact unique allocation follows a square-root-density balance involving hyperbolic sines, giving a rigorously quantified and strictly positive gain over the intuitive equal-height split whenever the densities differ. The weighted cotangent reduction then proves global uniqueness with a sharp dimensionless wavelength window and exact shallow/deep limiting placement laws. It distinguishes a finite offset of the optimal interface in the deep-container limit from a fixed fractional asymmetry in a thin container. These statements offer interpretable placement and spectral predictions not inherent in merely evaluating the forward dispersion relation.

## Limitations and follow-up risks

The claim concerns a continuous wavenumber spectrum, positive surface tension, inviscid two-layer potential flow, and a freely allocated interface at fixed **geometric** total height; it does not impose a fixed-mass constraint and does not extrapolate to viscosity or nonlinear breakup. A complete human-reviewed comparison with the 1990 primary paper could alter the priority assessment. No independent audit has been performed.
