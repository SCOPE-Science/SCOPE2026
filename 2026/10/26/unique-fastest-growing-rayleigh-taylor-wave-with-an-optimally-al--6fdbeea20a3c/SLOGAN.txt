When the total height of two inviscid Rayleigh–Taylor fluid layers is fixed, the fastest instability has a unique jointly optimal wavelength and interface placement: the heavier layer is thicker, and the depth split is exactly determined by a square-root-density hyperbolic-sine law. Optimizing placement reduces the dispersion denominator to a positive cotangent mixture, proving sharp wavelength bounds, strict superiority to equal placement, and shallow/deep limits.
