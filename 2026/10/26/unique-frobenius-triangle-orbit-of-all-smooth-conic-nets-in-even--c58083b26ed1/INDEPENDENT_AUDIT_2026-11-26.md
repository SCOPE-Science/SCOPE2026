# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

In characteristic two, a nonzero zero-polar quadratic is a double line over the closure, so the map of an all-smooth 3-space onto the three cross-term coefficients is an isomorphism. For a nonzero coefficient parameter u the alternating polar matrix has rank two and radical ku. The only candidate singular point is [u], so evaluating there gives the displayed cubic C, nonzero at every rational projective point. An absolutely irreducible cubic has a rational point: in the smooth case Hasse–Weil gives q+1−2 sqrt(q)>0, and in the singular case there is exactly one geometric singular point by the genus bound, so it is Frobenius fixed. In the reducible case, a Frobenius-fixed line is impossible, and a Frobenius-fixed conic component leaves a fixed line. Taking multiplicities into account, the only remaining decomposition of degree three is three distinct lines permuted transitively, and concurrence would again give a fixed point. The gradient of C in the identified parameter and point coordinates gives the three net generators. In line coordinates C is a nonzero scalar times XYZ and its gradient ideal is (XY,XZ,YZ), the reduced vertices; this identification is scheme-theoretic. Conversely any such triangle yields the three cross-term monomials in its vertex coordinates, with cyclic action up to nonzero scalars, so a nonzero rational member has all three coefficients nonzero and is smooth. For the descent detail compressed in the source's orbit argument, an ordered triple is not a projective frame: choose actual representatives w,w^q,w^(q²) in F_(q³)^3 and similarly v,v^q,v^(q²). Their basis matrices B(w),B(v) are rotated on the right by the same cyclic permutation matrix by Frobenius. Thus B(v)B(w)^(-1) is fixed entrywise and gives the required rational projectivity. One must choose this equivariant map; an arbitrary map taking one ordered triple to another need not descend. Subtracting collinear closed points from all degree-three closed points gives the stated count: each collinear orbit is on exactly one rational line. Alternatively a normalized point (1,a,b) yields a triangle precisely when 1,a,b are k-linearly independent, giving (q³−q)(q³−q²)/3 orbits. Orbit–stabilizer yields 3(q²+q+1). Independent finite-field counts from the latter criterion at q=2 and 4 give 8 and 960 (python /workspace/review_checks.py, check 1). The argument establishes the all-q claim; these finite computations do not. Here the three geometric points constitute one degree-three closed point, as clarified in the frozen assumptions, and no assertion of smoothness of every extension-field member is used.
