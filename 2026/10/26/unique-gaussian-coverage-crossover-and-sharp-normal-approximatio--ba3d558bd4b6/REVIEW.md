# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The stationary characteristic-function equation follows from the generator with Poisson resets; its convergent product yields the independent-Laplace and exponential Gaussian-variance representations. The variance-mixing weights sum to one, have squared sum \(s_a\), and have explicitly computed fourth central moment. Strict convexity of the normal-mixture density ratio, strict Jensen inequality and positive mass above variance one imply precisely two density intersections with the standard normal, yielding a unique *central-coverage* crossing. A second-order Taylor expansion in mixture variance is controlled uniformly in density \(L^1\) by fourth moments and an exponentially small small-variance event. Hermite integration gives both exact metric coefficients and the limiting crossover radius. This proof covers all \(0<a<1\); its weak-reset error bounds concern only \(a\uparrow1\). The 62 packaged computations check supporting identities and finite examples rather than substitute for infinite-domain arguments.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Pierce's arXiv:2204.07215 and Tal-Friedman–Roichman–Reuveni's arXiv:2205.03363 derive the stationary distributions and Gaussian limit; those are not claimed. Pierce obtains an unspecified first-order approximation error, not a total-variation or Kolmogorov sharp coefficient. Assefa–Keller-Ressel–Palmowski (arXiv:2610.01363v1, earliest public 2026-10-01) obtain general affine-reset moment formulas but not metric-accurate Gaussian calibration. Tong–Trafimow–Chen (DOI 10.1111/anzs.70042) study a broad class of Gaussian scale mixtures but not the present resetting-family calibration constants. The new claim combines an exact all-contraction interval-crossover classification, two explicit optimal near-Gaussian distance coefficients, and the 91.6735% limiting central-calibration boundary. No mathematically equivalent or stronger result was identified in the accessible primary texts and compared research records. Residual risk: uncatalogued general normal-variance-mixture crossing principles or specialized resetting asymptotics may partly subsume these statements.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A Gaussian weak limit by itself does not predict whether a variance-normalized confidence region overcovers or undercovers at a given level, nor the quantitative metric error. The proved one-switch structure fixes the sign for **every** contraction and **every** interval radius, while the sharp total-variation and Kolmogorov constants precisely measure weak-reset normal-approximation accuracy. The exact limiting switch at \(\sqrt3\) identifies the practical boundary between central overcoverage and tail undercoverage. The theorem is natural for the smallest nontrivial partial-reset Brownian model and complements, rather than rephrases, its known stationary density and kurtosis.
