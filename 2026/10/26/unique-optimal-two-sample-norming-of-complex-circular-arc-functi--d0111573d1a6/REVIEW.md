# Scientific review: unique optimal two-sample norming of complex circular-arc function planes

## Correctness — PASS
For every \(0<\theta<\pi\) and every distinct admissible node pair, two complex Lagrange values parametrize the whole function plane, so their freely chosen phases attain the sum of coefficient moduli. The angle split into an interior interval and two exterior intervals gives the displayed exact sampling profile, including boundary-node and more-than-semicircular cases. Minimizing its universal lower bound balances the strictly increasing \(\sec x\) term against the strictly decreasing \(\sin(\theta/2)/\sin x\) term at \(x=\arctan(\sin(\theta/2))\). A centered pair attains both terms; any off-center midpoint makes the exterior term strictly larger, proving uniqueness. The proof is analytic, uniform in the arc aperture, and does not extrapolate finite tests.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority risk
The primary source, Ray, arXiv:2610.04608v1, was inspected in HTML, including its Section 4 nonisometry theorem. It does not give a two-evaluation inverse norm or optimal node locations. A published result on exactly this family determines a unique best Hermitian quadratic norm and Hilbertian Banach–Mazur distance; that does not imply the present constrained \(\ell_\infty^2\) evaluation-coordinate optimum. The general interval Lebesgue-constant survey and the real-bivariate unit-disk degree-one optimal-node analysis use distinct objects and domains. Searches for complex degree-one arc interpolation, two-node Lebesgue constants, sampling norms, and aliases did not locate a covering statement. Unindexed older complex-arc interpolation formulas remain a realistic priority risk; absence from search results alone is not a novelty proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This solves the complete natural two-point norming problem for a newly prominent family of complex Banach planes, specifying not only a sharp constant but every pair's loss and the unique minimizing positions. The result quantitatively distinguishes endpoint sampling (loss \(\sec(\theta/2)\), divergent for almost-full arcs) from optimally positioned sampling (loss always below \(\sqrt2\)). Its strictly monotone constant also encodes the arc aperture. The fact is a motivated exact finite-dimensional invariant, not an arbitrary parameter slice or a general claim about arbitrary embeddings.

## Scope of review
The proof concerns complex affine functions on a single proper unit-circle arc and exactly two point evaluations. A finite checker supplies corroboration and no universal proof. No claim is made about the full Banach–Mazur distance to \(\ell_\infty^2\), higher-degree functions or independent literature certification.
