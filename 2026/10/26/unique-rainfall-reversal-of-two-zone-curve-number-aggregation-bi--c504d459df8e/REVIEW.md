# Scientific review: Unique rainfall reversal of two-zone curve-number aggregation bias

**Correctness (PASS).** The theorem fully specifies curve-number, storm, weight and abstraction domains, with the zero-runoff case separated. The three wetness stages, the monotone runoff ratio, and the Descartes polynomial sign count establish one and only one nontrivial positive-to-negative crossing. The exact weighted reciprocal identity produces the stated cubic without relying on numerical fitting. Explicit curvature signs establish the sharp lower infimum, and a composite-below-onset construction proves lack of a finite global upper bound. The deterministic program separately checked exact benchmark fractions and many parameter configurations; finite samples are supplementary, not proof.

**Originality (PASS, qualified by source risk).** The 2009 Paudel–Nelson–Scharffenberg full paper already describes a distributed-versus-composite-CN sign reversal and numerical crossing ranges. The theorem does not claim that phenomenon as original; rather, it supplies a complete all-parameter two-zone uniqueness result, explicit fully wet cubic, sharp lower endpoint and unbounded upper endpoint, none stated in the examined full text. The 2012 Jena–Tiwari–Pandey–Mishra case study covers the comparison empirically but was not available in full, so it remains a residual priority risk. Bartlett et al. (2016) provides a full-text probabilistic runoff upscaling framework with a different result and no matching exact two-zone classification. A broader mathematical implication not found in the searched sources remains possible.

**Value (PASS).** An area-averaged curve number and an area-averaged runoff represent competing practical computations for heterogeneous catchments. Knowing there is exactly one rainfall threshold, together with a computable cubic and sharp limits on universal inference, answers a physically motivated sign-classification question rather than merely showing numerical nonlinearity. The curve-number 40/50 example demonstrates that a reported high-rainfall crossover window cannot be treated as a universal mathematical cap. This is an exact ideal-model result, not a forecast, calibration statement, or general multi-zone theorem.

**Closest sources and limits.** Paudel et al., Journal of Hydrologic Engineering 14 (2009), DOI 10.1061/(ASCE)HE.1943-5584.0000100 (full PDF inspected, including discussion and conclusions); Jena et al., Journal of Hydrologic Engineering 17 (2012), DOI 10.1061/(ASCE)HE.1943-5584.0000651 (abstract and metadata only); Bartlett et al., Water Resources Research 52 (2016), DOI 10.1002/2015WR018439 (full HTML inspected). The positive-parameter model has no demonstrated empirical validity for extreme storm depths, and the result is restricted to two land-cover types.
