# Review: cyclic modular Latin Alexander quandle-algebra ideal chains

## Correctness
PASS. Both group-ring multipliers are automorphisms because \(p\nmid t(1-t)\). Closure under multiplication by the basis element indexed by zero forces every one-sided ideal to be invariant under the corresponding multiplier. Finite-dimensionality upgrades inclusion to equality. Surjectivity of the other multiplier then makes it an ordinary group-ring ideal; the group ring is exactly \(K[z]/(z^{p^s})\). The converse and exact ideal-product spans follow from invariance of the ordinary powers. The finite verifier checks the predictions in orders three, five and nine and exhaustively enumerates all 28 subspaces in the order-three case. These checks supplement rather than replace the general proof.

## Originality
PASS on examined evidence, subject to unindexed-corollary risk. Zhang's 2026 paper proves transport and augmentation-power formulas, not exhaustion of one-sided ideals. The 2019 semisimple representation-theoretic decomposition of dihedral quandle ideals is over real/complex fields in the relevant proposition; it does not give the modular chain classification. Bardakov–Elhamdadi (2026) compute mainly integral augmentation-power series, not the modular left/right ideal lattice. The relevant semisimple Fourier decomposition applies when the coefficient characteristic is prime to the underlying group order, disjoint from the present characteristic. Independent comparison of exact implication coverage was used; missing full coverage from possible other sources is a residual risk.

## Mathematical value
PASS. Complete classifications of *both* one-sided ideal lattices and their multiplication for a natural infinite family of nonassociative algebras are structural data, not merely a calculation of one augmentation power. The uniserial modular behavior contrasts with the semisimple Boolean decompositions and identifies the exact nilpotence length and simple successive ideal layers. The statement has a meaningful reason to specify precisely prime-power Latin quandles: cyclic modular group rings are local chain rings and both multipliers are invertible. No claim is made that the elementary constituent observations are new.

## Closest literature and limits
Main primary source: Zhang, arXiv:2610.08204, Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 2.2. Earlier dihedral one-sided representation results: Elhamdadi–Fernando–Tsvelikhovskiy, arXiv:1805.05908, Section 4.2. Integral augmentation powers: Bardakov–Elhamdadi, arXiv:2601.07057. The algebra is not claimed to be associative; ideal products mean spans. No statement is made for non-Latin parameters or noncyclic groups. An equivalent modular specialization may occur in older representation-theoretic literature and requires independent expert checking.
