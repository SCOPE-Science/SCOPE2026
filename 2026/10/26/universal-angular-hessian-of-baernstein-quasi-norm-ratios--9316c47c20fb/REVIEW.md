# Scientific review: Universal angular Hessian of Baernstein quasi-norm ratios

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is a local asymptotic with fixed \(n\ge2\) and fixed finite exponents \(0\le s<t\), for monic polynomials whose simple unimodular roots are individually perturbed from a regular \(n\)-gon. The distance is the sum of squared angular offsets after subtracting their mean. Common rotation leaves every circle norm unchanged. In the mean-zero slice, the first polynomial variation has only intermediate monomials, with coefficients \(i\sum_jv_j\zeta_j^{-k}\); discrete Parseval gives squared coefficient mass \(n\sum_jv_j^2\). The weighted second-variation identity for exponents \(r>1\), rotational orthogonality and the exact beta-integral ratio \(A_{r-2}/A_r=r/[4(r-1)]\) give the Hessian coefficient \(nr/8\). To reach \(0<r\le1\), the proof first moves integration coordinates with each simple boundary zero and obtains the holomorphic dependence of both derivatives on \(r\) for \(\operatorname{Re}r>-1\), then applies the identity theorem. This is not an illegal integral of \(|Q_n|^{r-2}\) at small exponents. The zero exponent follows separately from Jensen's formula. Polarization and parameter smoothness give the full Hessian and multivariate little-o; the fixed-direction limit establishes optimality of the coefficient. The standalone computation on the packaged path passed (`CHECK_OK`); its finite tests are explicitly not treated as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with remaining priority uncertainty

Zhang's October 2026 theorem (arXiv:2610.02009v1, especially §1.2, §§6–7) proves global monotonicity of the normalized \(L^r\) ratio and identifies a quantitative root-discrepancy refinement as distinct; the material read does not provide the exact local second-variation tensor. Lei and Zhang's May 2026 entropy theorem (arXiv:2605.03949v1) treats an equality problem at exponent two, not an angular Hessian across arbitrary exponent pairs. The full published scientific results *Strict Baernstein monotonicity and quantitative binomial rigidity* and *Equality rigidity and a quantitative defect for Baernstein monotonicity* were inspected. Both prove rigidity and nonoptimal positive coefficient or Hardy–Dirichlet deficits, which do not determine the exact isotropic coefficient \(n(t-s)/8\) on the physical root-displacement space. Actual indexed mathematical-result searches for the angular Hessian, Fourier modes and discrepancy were checked. The absence of a matched record is not uniqueness proof; earlier unindexed root-perturbation calculations remain possible.

## Value — PASS

This result supplies the sharp *local quantitative answer* for the natural squared-angular deviation of roots of a unimodular-root polynomial from the regular polygon—the type of refinement explicitly motivated in the 2026 proof of Baernstein's conjecture. It determines an entire quadratic form, not merely strict inequality or a nonoptimal coefficient estimate. The fact that every transverse Fourier mode has the same sharp coefficient, including for \(0<r<1\), is substantive information about the extremizer's local geometry. It is not a global stability theorem; local control is useful as an exact benchmark for any proposed global root-discrepancy improvement.
