# Review of the conductance-cost frontier theorem

## Correctness

PASS. The grounded weighted Laplacian decomposes into independent rank-one clique blocks, yielding exact hub–leaf and leaf–leaf resistances and the separable Kirchhoff-index identity. Convexity and injectivity of the weighted graph Laplacian establish uniqueness of the unrestricted optimizer, not merely of symmetric allocations. The two constrained Lagrange regimes are exhaustive, including potentially negative auxiliary cost coefficient, and the boundary directional derivative factors exactly into the stated size-independent cost threshold. The budget-normalization identity and objective are checked algebraically. Exact rational matrix comparisons and mixed-cost numerical tests are additional corroboration; they are not substituted for infinite-family proof.

## Originality

PASS, with literature uncertainty. The closest full text is Ghosh–Boyd–Saberi (2008), Sections 2, 4, 5 and 8: it supplies the general convex resistance-design framework and solved examples for trees, wheels and a different two-clique barbell, but not the heterogeneous common-vertex clique family or its cost-dependent active-edge classification. Haritha–Chithra (arXiv:2309.02704), Sections 1, 3 and 4, treats unit-weight resistance distances of coalescences and windmills but not conductance-cost optimization. Targeted mathematical-record and literature searches for aliases, weighted bouquet optimization, exact tables and broader results found no covering claim. Search absence cannot rule out unindexed work. The 2006 shorter proceedings version of the same Ghosh–Boyd–Saberi paper was identified, though the detailed inspected text was its accessible journal version.

## Value

PASS. The mathematical design problem is global, with one conductance variable per edge, not an artificially restricted two-parameter exercise. The result identifies the exact set of graph edges removed by an optimal heterogeneous-cost design, with a cost threshold independent of local clique size, and gives all remaining unique optimal weights and the exact optimum for arbitrary bouquet sizes and costs. The nontrivial mixed regime occurs on a natural hub-and-community network; the theorem generalizes the resistance-allocation use case beyond previously worked symmetric examples. It does not claim results for arbitrary networks.
