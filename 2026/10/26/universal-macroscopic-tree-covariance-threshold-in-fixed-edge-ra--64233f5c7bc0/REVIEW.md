# Review of the macroscopic-tree covariance classification

## Correctness — PASS

The exact factorial-moment ratio follows by conditioning on a specified ordered set of isolated vertices, observing that the remaining graph has the uniform fixed-edge law, and canceling the tree automorphism factor and the edge-count factorial. All denominators are positive on the stated domain. The consecutive-ratio numerator simplifies to a linear polynomial of tree order with strictly negative slope; together with the exact level-one value at tree size two, this proves the finite unimodality and the possible single sign crossing. Uniform Taylor expansion on compact macroscopic-size intervals and the nondegenerate positive root establish the asymptotic transition and sharp location. The standalone verifier independently performed 69 exhaustive mixed-moment comparisons over 33,856 labeled graphs, 39,711 exact-rational sign/unimodality comparisons, and eight threshold calculations. Finite computation is corroboration, not a replacement for proof. No assertion is made outside the admissible parameter domain.

## Originality — PASS, subject to stated priority risk

The exact hypergeometric inclusion probability itself is classical and not claimed as new. The proposed contribution is the joint classification: shape-and-density-free finite covariance signs for all tree sizes and isolated-vertex factorial orders, a nontrivial universal macroscopic cutoff, and its order-one threshold correction. Full inspection of Janson–Ruciński (2009) finds upper-tail and rooted fixed-subgraph results, not this joint fixed-edge dependence theorem. Janson (1990) concerns fixed-pattern subgraph fluctuations and ensemble differences according to its accessible abstract. Tomescu (1994) concerns overlaps between random subtrees according to its accessible abstract. Targeted searches of formulas, equivalences, stronger coverage, and existing result summaries identified no statement implying this classification. The inaccessible historical full texts could contain additional relevant material; publication priority remains open to human verification. A failed search alone is not taken as proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The question distinguishes an important random-graph ensemble constraint: fixing the number of edges can **reverse** the natural negative dependence between isolated vertices and a tree-subgraph count, even though the same statistic is negatively correlated under independent edges. The first positive region, the universal \(0.7968\ldots\) macroscopic cutoff, and its shape- and edge-budget independence form a natural complete dependence classification. The exact order-one correction resolves the transition for integer tree sizes. These are structurally motivated effects beyond recomputing a known marginal expectation.
