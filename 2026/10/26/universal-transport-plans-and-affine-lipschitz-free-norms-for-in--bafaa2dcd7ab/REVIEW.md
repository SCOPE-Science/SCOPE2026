# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The exact norm for an arbitrary real zero-mass vector follows from a quantitative complete-multipartite Hall condition. Because the total cluster variations sum to twice the positive mass, at most one part can be overloaded. A cross-part flow with exactly the necessary leftover intrapart transport gives the upper bound. For a non-overloaded vector, the positive-support indicator is a dual norming potential. For a uniquely overloaded part, assigning source potentials \(t_i\), \(1\) and sink potentials \(0\), \(t_i-1\) inside and outside that part gives an explicit one-Lipschitz dual witness attaining the upper bound. The argument handles arbitrary part sizes, arbitrary real masses, and all independent parameter values including endpoints. A single coupling built solely from the mass vector is optimal for every parameter vector and implies affine interpolation.

The standalone exact-rational transport enumerator re-computes primal assignment minima and independently verifies the formula and midpoint-affinity statement for 745 zero-sum test vectors across five nonuniform multipartite configurations. This finite check corroborates but cannot replace the mathematical proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual bibliographic uncertainty

The strongest directly adjacent published classification enumerates all extreme dual potentials for multipartite metrics with one common intrapart distance. In the common-parameter slice, an exact norm statement may follow from that earlier stronger classification; no separate novelty is claimed for that slice. The new assertion concerns independently variable parameters, **one optimal transport plan for the whole parameter cube**, and global pointwise affine norm interpolation. Neither the inspected common-parameter classification, its bipartite variant, the classical complete-multipartite symmetric-edge facet theorem at the graph endpoint, nor a different clustered-space projection-constant result states or implies this full multi-parameter joint-plan theorem without additional transport analysis. Older polyhedral and optimal-transport literature could nevertheless contain an equivalent weighted-Hall formulation, so noncoverage cannot be certified independently by search.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The parameter vector describes a full natural metric family rather than an artificially selected finite example. Ordinarily a transportation optimum is a concave piecewise-affine minimum over plans when costs vary; here the entire norm is affine in all independent intrapart costs for every vector, because one plan is simultaneously optimal. The exact closed form identifies the only possible overloaded part and eliminates a transport linear program for arbitrarily large finite metrics, while supplying a universal optimal-coupling construction. This is a structural classification with plausible uses in weighted metric interpolation and sensitivity, not a routine numerical improvement.

## Closest literature and limits

Higashitani–Jochemko–Michałek (2018) classify facets at the all-length-two graph endpoint, not parametric transport. Aliaga–Pernecká (2018) characterize extreme molecules, while Alexander–Fradelizi–García-Lirola–Zvavitch (2019) treat broader finite-metric convex geometry. Published equal-parameter bipartite/multipartite facet classifications and a clustered relative-projection theorem were also compared by mathematical implication. The formula is restricted to real finite metrics with interpart distance one and intrapart distances in \([1,2]\). Bibliographic uncertainty remains for unindexed transportation-formula variants; no external independent audit was performed.
