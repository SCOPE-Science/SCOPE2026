# Universal weak-potential diffusion suppression for thermally diffusing run-and-tumble particles

## Finding
For a one-dimensional run-and-tumble particle with *strictly positive independent Brownian diffusion*, every nonconstant smooth periodic potential suppresses the long-time effective diffusivity to second order in its amplitude. Moreover, the suppression per unit Fourier-potential intensity is strictly increasing with spatial frequency whenever the self-propulsion speed is nonzero.

More precisely, put \(q_n=2\pi n/L\), let \(u_n=L^{-1}\int_0^L U(x)e^{-iq_nx}\,dx\), and set \(t>0\). Define
\[
K(t)=\frac{D^3\gamma t^2+4D^2\gamma^2t+D^2v^2t^2+4D\gamma^3+D\gamma v^2t+2\gamma^2v^2+v^4t}{\gamma(D^2t+2D\gamma+v^2)^2}.
\]
Then, as the real potential amplitude \(\varepsilon\to0\),
\[
D_{\mathrm{eff}}(\varepsilon)=D+\frac{v^2}{2\gamma}-2\varepsilon^2\sum_{n\ge1}|u_n|^2K(q_n^2)+O(\varepsilon^3).
\]
The stationary transport velocity is \(V(\varepsilon)=O(\varepsilon^3)\). For \(v>0\),
\[
K'(t)=\frac{v^2(5D^3\gamma t+2D^2\gamma^2+D^2v^2t+3D\gamma v^2+v^4)}{\gamma(D^2t+2D\gamma+v^2)^3}>0.
\]
In particular, all nonzero Fourier modes contribute strictly negatively, and at fixed Fourier amplitude shorter wavelengths have strictly greater quadratic suppression. The endpoint values are \(K(0)=1/(D+v^2/(2\gamma))\) and \(\lim_{t\to\infty}K(t)=1/D+v^2/(\gamma D^2)\). For \(v=0\) the factor is the constant \(1/D\), the classical passive weak-potential limit (not a novelty claim).

## Assumptions and scope
Fix any \(L,D,v,\gamma>0\) and any real, nonconstant, infinitely differentiable \(L\)-periodic function \(U\). Let \(B_t\) be standard Brownian motion and let \(\sigma_t\in\{-1,1\}\) flip at independent exponential times of rate \(\gamma\). Consider the unwrapped process
\[
dX_t=\big(v\sigma_t-\varepsilon U'(X_t)\big)\,dt+\sqrt{2D}\,dB_t.
\]
We use the unique invariant distribution of the periodized process on \([0,L)\times\{-1,1\}\). The long-time effective diffusivity is \(\lim_{T\to\infty}\operatorname{Var}(X_T-X_0)/(2T)\); the long-time velocity is \(\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathbb E(X_T-X_0)/T\). Both are independent of initial conditions after the long-time limit. The asymptotic error \(O(\varepsilon^3)\) is for fixed \(L,D,v,\gamma,U\) and does not claim uniformity in a zero-noise, infinite-frequency, or vanishing-flip limit. The theorem applies to smooth asymmetric ratchets as well as symmetric periodic potentials.

## Proof
**1. Floquet generator and cumulants.** The elliptic periodized diffusion has a unique positive invariant density. Its real tilted backward generator acting on \(L\)-periodic two-component functions is
\[
\mathcal L_{\varepsilon,k}=D(\partial_x+k)^2+v\sigma(\partial_x+k)+\gamma(S-I)-\varepsilon U'(x)(\partial_x+k),
\]
where \(S\) flips the spin. The simple principal eigenvalue \(\lambda_\varepsilon(k)\) is analytic for \((\varepsilon,k)\) close to the origin by isolated-eigenvalue elliptic perturbation theory. Its first derivative at \(k=0\) is the asymptotic velocity and half its second derivative is the asymptotic diffusivity. At \(\varepsilon=0\), the spatially constant block yields
\[
\lambda_0(k)=Dk^2-\gamma+\sqrt{\gamma^2+v^2k^2},
\]
so \(\lambda_0'(0)=0\) and \(\lambda_0''(0)/2=D+v^2/(2\gamma)\).

**2. Explicit second-order resolvent.** Switch from spin components to their symmetric and antisymmetric sums. In spatial Fourier mode \(q\), the unperturbed block is the complex-symmetric matrix
\[
A_q(k)=D(k+iq)^2I+\begin{pmatrix}0&v(k+iq)\\v(k+iq)&-2\gamma\end{pmatrix}.
\]
A right and transpose-left eigenvector of the \(q=0\) principal block is \(r(k)=(1,b(k))^\top\), where \(b(k)=vk/(\gamma+\sqrt{\gamma^2+v^2k^2})\). Writing \(z=k+iq\), \(m=\lambda_0(k)-Dz^2\), the normalized diagonal resolvent matrix element is
\[
H_q(k)=\frac{m+2\gamma+2b(k)vz+b(k)^2m}{[1+b(k)^2][m(m+2\gamma)-v^2z^2]}.
\]
Because the mean of \(U'\) is zero, the first-order eigenvalue perturbation vanishes. The second-order term is exactly
\[
\lambda_2(k)=k\sum_{q\ne0}(k+iq)q^2|u_q|^2H_q(k).
\]
This follows by multiplying the two Fourier transitions from mode \(0\) to mode \(q\) and back, each generated by \(-U'(x)(\partial_x+k)\), and applying the reduced resolvent. No mode-zero term arises because \(U'_0=0\). For all \(q\ne0\) and real \(k\) near zero, the resolvent exists: the nonzero spatial modes have strictly negative spectral real parts relative to the isolated principal eigenvalue, ensured by \(D>0\).

**3. Taylor extraction.** Directly setting \(k=0\) and differentiating once yields
\[
H_q(0)=\frac{Dq^2+2\gamma}{q^2(D^2q^2+2D\gamma+v^2)}.
\]
A symbolic or elementary rational differentiation of the preceding explicit \(H_q\) gives the exact identity
\[
-q^2\big[H_q(0)+iqH'_q(0)\big]=K(q^2).
\]
Pairing modes \(q\) and \(-q\) cancels the term linear in \(k\), so \(\lambda_2'(0)=0\), and the coefficient of \(k^2\) in \(\lambda_2(k)\) is \(-2\sum_{n\ge1}|u_n|^2K(q_n^2)\). This proves both displayed transport expansions. Absolute and locally uniform convergence of the differentiated Fourier sums follows from smoothness of \(U\) and the elliptic resolvent estimates, justifying termwise expansion.

**4. Signs, strictness, and limits.** Every monomial in the numerator of \(K(t)\) has positive coefficient when \(D,\gamma,v,t>0\). Differentiation gives the stated strictly positive formula for \(K'(t)\). The limits follow by evaluating at \(t=0\) and taking leading coefficients at infinity. Since a real nonconstant \(U\) has at least one nonzero Fourier coefficient, its quadratic term is strictly negative. Analyticity then gives \(D_{\mathrm{eff}}(\varepsilon)<D+v^2/(2\gamma)\) for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\ne0\). For \(v=0\), direct cancellation in \(K(t)\) yields \(K(t)=1/D\), consistent with the second-order expansion of the Lifson–Jackson formula.

## Verification
Run the standalone `check_weak_ratchet.py` with Python, NumPy and SymPy. It symbolically re-derives the Bloch-resolvent coefficient, strictly positive derivative numerator, endpoints, and passive limit. Independently, it builds the full spin-resolved truncated Fourier–Bloch generator and numerically differentiates its principal eigenvalue for four combinations of \(D,\gamma,v,U\). Results agree with the exact quadratic predictions at decreasing amplitudes, with finite-difference/truncation tolerance. These computations corroborate rather than replace the infinite-dimensional perturbation proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Roberts and Zhen, *Run-and-tumble motion in a linear ratchet potential* (arXiv:2303.07880, first public March 14, 2023), study positive thermal diffusion with an active ratchet, deriving stationary currents, energetics and passage times; they do not give this weak-potential long-time dispersion kernel. Le Doussal, Majumdar and Schehr (arXiv:2003.08155) derive exact drift and diffusion for periodic force with telegraphic propulsion **without** independent Brownian diffusion; their zero-thermal-noise model is therefore not the positive-\(D\) theorem claimed here. Pavliotis (*A multiscale approach to Brownian motors*, arXiv:cond-mat/0504403) derives general Markov-forcing homogenization equations, applicable as a broad methodological foundation, and an explicit *small correlation-time* expansion for Ornstein–Uhlenbeck forcing. Those cell problems do not state the present arbitrary-waveform, finite-flip-rate spectral sign-and-wavelength law. Spagnolo and Dubkov (arXiv:cond-mat/0510170) find effective diffusion for Brownian particles in a potential that *changes sign* under dichotomous noise; this is multiplicative force switching, whereas the present model has additive dichotomous propulsion and a fixed potential. The passive \(v=0\) case is classical and is claimed only as a consistency check.

## Limitations
The result is a weak-potential expansion and is not a claim of diffusion suppression at arbitrary potential amplitudes. Strict wavelength ordering compares Fourier modes of equal potential amplitude, not equal force amplitude or experiments at fixed force strength. The proof assumes \(D>0\), \(\gamma>0\), and smooth periodic \(U\); singular limits are not covered. A broad previously known perturbation identity could conceivably reproduce the coefficient by specialization, so independent scholarly priority assessment is still required. No experimental detectability claim follows.
