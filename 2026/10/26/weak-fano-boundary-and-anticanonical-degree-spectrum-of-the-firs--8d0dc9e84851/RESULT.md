# Weak-Fano boundary and anticanonical degree spectrum of the first unexpected toric Richardson fourfold

## Finding
Let \(X=\overline{R}_{v,w}\subset\mathrm{Fl}(\mathbb C^4)\) be the closure of the Richardson variety for the permutations \(v=1324\) and \(w=4231\), expressed in one-line notation. The smooth, projective fourfold \(X\) is toric for a nonstandard four-dimensional torus. Its anticanonical line bundle is nef and big but **not ample**. More precisely, its \(32\) torus-invariant curves have anticanonical degree spectrum
\[
\#\{C:(-K_X)\cdot C=0\}=4,\quad
\#\{C:(-K_X)\cdot C=2\}=24,\quad
\#\{C:(-K_X)\cdot C=4\}=4,
\]
and the exact anticanonical volume is
\[
(-K_X)^4=320.
\]
The four anticanonical-degree-zero curves correspond to the four unordered Bruhat-cover endpoint pairs
\[
(1342,1432),\quad(3124,3214),\quad(2341,2431),\quad(4123,4213).
\]
Consequently \(X\) is a weak-Fano but non-Fano Bott fourfold, and its anticanonical contraction is birational and contracts each of these four invariant curves.

## Assumptions and scope
All varieties are over \(\mathbb C\). Bruhat intervals and Richardson varieties are taken in the full flag variety of \(\mathrm{GL}_4\). Permutations are written in one-line notation and \(\ell\) denotes inversion length. A torus-invariant curve means a one-dimensional orbit closure for the intrinsic four-dimensional torus of \(X\); the smaller diagonal torus need not have the same collection of orbit closures. The set of sixteen fixed-point vertices is \([1324,4231]\); the standard diagonal torus acts on \(X\) as a subtorus of the intrinsic torus. The moment-polytope edge graph is the Bruhat Hasse diagram of this Boolean rank-four interval, by the prior results cited below.

## Proof
**1. Toric geometry and the complete fixed-point graph.** The interval \([v,w]\) is Boolean of rank four; in fact \(w=s_1s_3s_2s_1s_3\) admits a magic word for \(v=s_2\). The cited geometry identifies the closed Richardson fourfold with a smooth projective Bott tower and a toric variety whose moment-polytope edge graph is the Bruhat-cover graph. For permutations \(u,z\in S_4\), the Bruhat rank-matrix criterion is
\[
u\leq z\iff
\#\{t\leq k:u(t)\geq j\}\leq
\#\{t\leq k:z(t)\geq j\}
\quad\text{for every }1\leq k,j\leq4.
\]
This criterion enumerates exactly sixteen permutations \(u\) with \(v\leq u\leq w\), with rank counts \((1,4,6,4,1)\). An edge joins \(u\) and \(u(i\ j)\) when \(1\leq i<j\leq4\) and \(|\ell(u(i\ j))-\ell(u)|=1\). Within this interval each of the sixteen vertices has four neighbors, hence there are \(16\cdot4/2=32\) undirected invariant edges. The complete enumeration is also reproduced by the enclosed standalone verifier.

**2. Equivariant tangent weights.** Restrict to the standard diagonal torus \(T\subset\mathrm{SL}_4\), with formal characters \(\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_4\) considered modulo the common diagonal character. Let \(u' = u(i\ j)\) denote a Bruhat-cover neighbor of \(u\). The character of its tangent line at the flag fixed point \(u\) is
\[
\alpha_{u,u'}=\epsilon_{u(j)}-\epsilon_{u(i)}.
\]
These four invariant lines are the four tangent coordinate axes of smooth \(X\) at \(u\). Therefore
\[
S(u)=\sum_{u'\sim u}\alpha_{u,u'}=
\left.c_1^T(TX)\right|_u.
\]
For the invariant projective line joining \(u\) and \(u'\), equivariant restriction of a line bundle gives the integer \(d_{u,u'}=(-K_X)\cdot C_{u,u'}\) determined by
\[
S(u)-S(u')=d_{u,u'}\alpha_{u,u'}.
\]
This identity is symmetric under reversing the edge, as then both the difference and \(\alpha\) change sign. Exhausting the \(32\) pairs gives the multiset of degrees
\[
\{0^{\times4},\,2^{\times24},\,4^{\times4}\}.
\]
The four degree-zero pairs are precisely those displayed in the finding. All degrees are nonnegative. On a complete projective toric variety, the Mori cone is generated by invariant curve classes, so \(-K_X\) is nef. It is not ample, because it has degree zero on a nonzero effective curve. Its semiampleness follows from toric nefness.

**3. Anticanonical self-intersection by exact localization.** Choose the integral diagonal one-parameter subgroup with weights \(\lambda=(0,1,2,3)\); translation by a constant changes no root weight, so this defines a cocharacter of the projective diagonal torus. All four tangent weights are nonzero at each fixed point. For \(a_{u,u'}=\langle\alpha_{u,u'},\lambda\rangle\), the equivariant fixed-point formula in complex dimension four yields
\[
(-K_X)^4
=\sum_{u\in[v,w]}
\frac{\bigl(\sum_{u'\sim u}a_{u,u'}\bigr)^4}{\prod_{u'\sim u}a_{u,u'}}=320.
\]
This is an exact rational identity, not a numerical approximation; the verifier also repeats the calculation for three other generic integral cocharacters and obtains \(320\) every time. Since \(-K_X\) is nef and its fourth self-intersection is positive, it is big. The morphism defined by sufficiently divisible anticanonical multiples is therefore birational. Its zero-degree invariant curves are contracted by that morphism.

## Verification
The file `verify.py` is a dependency-free Python program using rational integer arithmetic only. It exhaustively enumerates all \(24\) elements of \(S_4\), independently compares the Bruhat upper ideal with the subword criterion for \(s_1s_2s_3s_2s_1\), constructs all \(32\) cover edges and their \(64\) oriented tangent characters, verifies the equivariant edge identities exactly, and computes the localization sum for four distinct generic cocharacters. To reproduce, run `python verify.py` from the extracted package. The terminal result is `anticanonical_volume=320` and the complete degree spectrum \((4,24,4)\) at degrees \((0,2,4)\). The finite enumeration is exhaustive because \(S_4\) contains exactly \(24\) permutations, while the geometric identification and equivariant formulas are mathematical theorems used in the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Gorsky–Kim–Scroggin–Simental (2025, Example 5.17) introduces the specific open-Richardson example. Gorsky–Kim–Sherman-Bennett (2026, Example 5.2 and the toric interval-polytope results) establishes its toric coordinates and polyhedral combinatorics. Elek (2026, Example 2.4 and the magic-word theorem) explains its Bott-tower geometry. These sources establish the variety and methods used here, not the anticanonical degree distribution, its weak-Fano-but-not-Fano status, or the exact volume \(320\) as inspected. General Mori-cone/Fano criteria for Bott towers, including Chary (2018) and Suyama (2018), are foundational tools rather than claims that this particular Richardson example has the stated numerical profile.

## Limitations
This theorem treats only the specified four-dimensional Richardson variety, not all hypercube Richardson varieties or Bott towers. The four listed invariant curves need not exhaust **all** curves contracted by the anticanonical morphism. The argument establishes a birational anticanonical contraction but does not determine the singularities of its image. Full literature priority, particularly implicit consequences of previously published toric polytopes, is not independently certified. The finite script proves the finite character and localization arithmetic when combined with the cited geometric identifications; it does not prove the general external theorems from first principles.
