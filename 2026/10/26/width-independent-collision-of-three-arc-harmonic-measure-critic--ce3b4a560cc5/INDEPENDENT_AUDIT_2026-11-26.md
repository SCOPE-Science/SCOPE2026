## correctness

PASS

The harmonic extension of an arc uses Fourier coefficients sin(nβ)/(πn); summing the differentiated holomorphic series gives the three rational summands, with poles only on the boundary. For c in {1,exp(iα),exp(−iα)}, their three elementary symmetric functions are A=1+2cosα, A, 1. After clearing denominators the constant and quartic coefficients are A, the linear and cubic coefficients are −4bA, and the middle coefficient is A²−3+12b², with b=cosβ. The reciprocal reduction t=z+1/z gives discriminant 16A(1−x)(1+x−2b²), x=cosα. The last factor is strictly negative since α>2β. If A>0 each nonreal conjugate t gives one simple disk root, necessarily with positive real part since Re(t)=2b=Re(z)(1+|z|^(−2)). If A<0, the downward quadratic takes strictly positive values at ±2 by the two stated factorizations and strict bounds −b<x<2b²−1; its real roots lie outside [−2,2] on opposite sides. The reciprocal quadratics give one interior root of each sign. At A=0 the numerator is exactly 3(4b²−1)z², nonzero except for a double zero at 0. For the local scale, the critical-point equation bounds |B||z|²≤10|A| for |z|≤1, so the stated O(sqrt(|δ|)) substitution is justified. A=−sqrt(3)δ+O(δ²) then yields z²=δ/[sqrt(3)(4b²−1)]+O(|δ|^(3/2)), giving the claimed signed branches with O(|δ|) errors. A simple or double derivative zero gives respectively the real part of a nonzero quadratic or cubic as the first nonconstant harmonic term. The strict admissible range includes 2π/3 for every allowed β.
