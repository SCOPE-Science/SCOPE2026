# Wild fixed-point thickening equals the Weddle Jacobian scheme in characteristic two

## Finding

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(2\). For every four pairwise distinct nonzero scalars \(a,b,c,d\in k\), consider the Weddle quartic \(W\subset\mathbb P^3_k\) defined by the determinant below and the Hutchinson involution \(T\) on the coordinate torus. Write \(\alpha^2=a\), \(\beta^2=b\), \(\gamma^2=c\), and \(\delta^2=d\), and set \(P=[\alpha:\beta:\gamma:\delta]\).

The scheme of fixed points of \(T\) on the coordinate torus and the Jacobian singular scheme of \(W\) have **identical completed local ideals at \(P\)**. More precisely, in local coordinates \(x=\alpha(1+u),\ y=\beta(1+v),\ z=\gamma(1+t),\ w=\delta\), both ideals are \((u^2,v^2,t^2)\). Thus
\[
\widehat{\mathcal O}_{\operatorname{Sing}(W),P}\simeq k[[u,v,t]]/(u^2,v^2,t^2),
\]
and the local singular scheme has length \(8\), embedding dimension \(3\), and Hilbert function \((1,3,3,1)\). This equality of a wild fixed-point scheme and an infinitesimal hypersurface singular scheme holds uniformly across the full pairwise-distinct four-parameter locus.

## Assumptions and scope

Use homogeneous coordinates \([x:y:z:w]\) and define \(F\) by
\[
F=\det\begin{pmatrix}
ayzw&x&1&a\\
bxzw&y&1&b\\
cxyw&z&1&c\\
dxyz&w&1&d
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Let \(W=V(F)\). On the torus \(U=\{xyzw\ne0\}\), define the regular involution
\[
T([x:y:z:w])=[a/x:b/y:c/z:d/w].
\]
All equality statements concern the completed local rings of \(W\) and \(U\) at \(P\), not the fixed locus on a resolution or possible additional boundary fixed points of a rational extension.

The Jacobian singular scheme is defined on the chart \(w=\delta\) by the ideal generated by the local equation \(f\) and its three first partial derivatives, rather than by the reduced singular support. In characteristic \(2\), square roots exist uniquely in the algebraically closed field, and distinct parameters have distinct square roots.

## Proof

**Step 1 (fixed-point scheme).** In the chart \(w=\delta\), the torus involution sends \((1+u,1+v,1+t)\) to \(((1+u)^{-1},(1+v)^{-1},(1+t)^{-1})\). Its fixed-point equations are \((1+u)^2=(1+v)^2=(1+t)^2=1\), hence exactly \(u^2=v^2=t^2=0\). Denote this ideal in \(k[[u,v,t]]\) by \(I\). Its support is the single point \(P\) and its quotient has basis \(1,u,v,t,uv,ut,vt,uvt\).

**Step 2 (Jacobian inclusion).** Substitute \(x=\alpha(1+u)\), \(y=\beta(1+v)\), \(z=\gamma(1+t)\), \(w=\delta\), and \(a=\alpha^2\), etc., into the displayed determinant to obtain \(f\). Direct polynomial expansion over \(\mathbb F_2[\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta,u,v,t]\) shows that every coefficient of a squarefree monomial in \(u,v,t\) vanishes. Equivalently \(f\in I\). Differentiating a monomial divisible by a square preserves membership in \(I\) in characteristic two: differentiating the exponent two kills the term, while a higher exponent still leaves a square, or a derivative in a different variable leaves the square unchanged. Therefore \(J=(f,f_u,f_v,f_t)\subseteq I\).

**Step 3 (three independent quadratic jets).** Write \([u^i v^j t^k]f\) for a coefficient, viewed as a polynomial in \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\). Consider the matrix whose rows consist of the \(u^2,v^2,t^2\) coefficients of \(f\), \(f_v\), and \(f_t\):
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}
[u^2]f &[v^2]f &[t^2]f\\
[u^2v]f &[v^3]f &[vt^2]f\\
[u^2t]f &[v^2t]f &[t^3]f
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The universal determinant identity is
\[
\det M=(\alpha\beta\gamma\delta)^4
(\alpha+\beta)(\alpha+\gamma)(\alpha+\delta)^2
(\beta+\gamma)^2(\beta+\delta)(\gamma+\delta).
\]
This identity follows by expanding the four-by-four defining determinant over the polynomial ring in characteristic two; the embedded standalone exact checker reconstructs it without external dependencies. Its right side is nonzero for every set of pairwise distinct nonzero \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\).

**Step 4 (Nakayama).** Since \(f\in I\) and all its first derivatives lie in \(I\), the three chosen elements \(f,f_v,f_t\in J\) have invertible coefficient matrix in the three-dimensional vector space \(I/\mathfrak m I\), where \(\mathfrak m=(u,v,t)\). Hence \(I=J+\mathfrak m I\). Nakayama's lemma for the finitely generated module \(I/J\) over \(k[[u,v,t]]\) yields \(I=J\). The monomial basis of Step 1 proves the asserted local length and Hilbert function.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` is a self-contained Python 3 standard-library checker. It performs polynomial arithmetic over \(\mathbb F_2[\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta,u,v,t]\), expands the actual Weddle determinant, verifies that **all eight squarefree local coefficients vanish**, and computes the above universal jet determinant exactly. The checker returned `VERIFY_OK: Weddle determinant; Frobenius-fixed thickening; Jacobian jet minor`. This is a symbolic polynomial-identity computation valid for arbitrary coefficients subject to the stated nonvanishing conditions, not extrapolation from finitely many field examples.

## Relationship to prior work

Bateman studied the classical Weddle quartic [1]. Dolgachev [2, §2] supplies the determinant presentation, identifies \(P\) as the additional characteristic-two singularity of type \(D_4\), and states the existence of a unique geometric fixed point of the Hutchinson involution on \(W\). These established assertions concern singularity type and fixed-point **support**. The finding here is the stronger local **scheme equality**, including the explicit Jacobian algebra and the uniform length-eight thickening. A known \(D_4\) label, without a specified characteristic-two local equation, is not itself a computation of the Tjurina scheme; nor does an isolated geometric fixed point specify its infinitesimal structure. Catanese–Schütt [3] treats characteristic-two normal quartic singularities in broader context but does not, on the abstract material consulted, resolve this particular local comparison.

## Limitations

The claim is local at \(P\). It does not classify the full singular scheme of \(W\), the scheme of fixed points on the minimal resolution, or the associated quotient singularities. It does not assert that the computed length is a deformation-invariant Milnor number. Classical source [1] was identified through publication metadata but its paywalled full text was unavailable; a historical equivalent calculation cannot be excluded from that source alone. The exact polynomial certificate independently establishes the algebraic identity used in the proof. Independent scientific validation has not been performed.
