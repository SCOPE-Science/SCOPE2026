# A connectivity transition for minimum zero-forcing sets of rook graphs

## Finding

For \(m,n\ge2\), let \(\mathcal E_{m,n}\) have as vertices the minimum ordinary zero-forcing sets of the labeled rook graph \(R_{m,n}=K_m\square K_n\). Join two sets when one selected cell can be exchanged for another. Then \(\mathcal E_{m,n}\) is connected for \(m,n\ge3\), and also for \((m,n)=(2,2),(2,3),(3,2)\). For \(\{m,n\}=\{2,t\}\) with \(t\ge4\), it has exactly two components, each isomorphic to
\[
K_1\vee L(K_{t,t}-M_t),
\]
where \(M_t\) is a perfect matching and \(\vee\) denotes graph join. Each component has \(1+t(t-1)\) vertices and \(t(t-1)^2\) edges.

## Assumptions and scope

Cells are adjacent when sharing a row or a column. Blue cells repeatedly force their unique white neighbor under the ordinary zero-forcing rule. Minimum refers to cardinality, not merely inclusion-minimality. The exchange graph allows any one-for-one exchange preserving a minimum set, not only exchanges along rook-graph edges. Row and column labels are retained. The known minimum cardinality and earlier classification of the minimum sets are background, not part of the contribution.

## Proof

**White-tree model (known).** Identify \(R_{m,n}=L(K_{m,n})\) and let \(H\) contain the bipartite edges for the white cells. At a blue cell \((r,c)\), the number of white neighbors is \(d_H(r)+d_H(c)\). A force therefore deletes a leaf edge using an isolate on the opposite side. A cycle cannot lose its first edge. Conversely, the first such force in a nonempty forest gives isolates on both sides, so further leaf-edge deletions can exhaust it. This recovers the earlier all-set criterion. A nonempty successful white forest has at most \(m+n-2\) edges because it has an isolate. That bound is achieved by isolating a row, joining the other rows to one column, and attaching every other column by a single edge. Therefore for minimum blue sets the white graph consists exactly of one isolate and a spanning tree on all remaining vertices with an opposite-side leaf. A blue exchange is precisely a white-edge exchange, so work in this known model.

**Both dimensions at least three.** Fix an isolated row and a column constrained to be a leaf. All such trees are connected under exchanges: deleting the leaf gives a spanning tree on the remaining complete bipartite graph; its spanning trees are connected by graphic-matroid basis exchange. Independently, changing just the leaf edge changes the attachment. Any two choices of constrained leaf have intersecting families: join all nonisolated rows to a third column and attach each other column by one edge. Thus the entire graph for a fixed isolated row is connected. To exchange the isolated row \(a\) with row \(b\), take a tree in which \(b\) is a leaf and some column leaf attaches elsewhere. Such a tree is obtained by joining all nonisolated rows to one column and attaching every other column to a row distinct from \(a,b\). Replace \(b\)'s edge by the corresponding edge at \(a\); the opposite-side leaf remains. This connects all row-isolate families, and the transpose argument connects all column-isolate families. To pass between the types, delete a leaf column's edge in a row-isolate tree and attach the previously isolated row to any column other than the newly isolated one. This gives a column-isolate tree with a leaf row in one exchange.

**Two columns.** By transpose write \(m=t,n=2\). For \(t\ge3\), the white-tree model leaves either a whole column \(c\) (the apex) or a dominant column \(c\) with \(t-1\) edges plus one edge in the other column. The latter states are indexed by distinct rows \(a,b\): omit row \(a\) from \(c\) and add row \(b\) in the other column. Indeed, isolating a column gives its opposite entire column, and isolating a row leaves a tree on \(t-1\) rows and two columns. Such a tree has an opposite-side leaf exactly when one column has degree one and the other degree \(t-1\). The apex is adjacent to every state for its column; among nonapex states with that column, adjacency means the same omission or the same addition. This is incidence of the edges indexed by \((a,b)\), \(a\ne b\), in the crown graph \(K_{t,t}-M_t\). For \(t\ge4\) an exchange cannot change the dominant column, since at least \(t-2\ge2\) edges in that column remain, but a state with the other dominant column has at most one. Thus the two cones are the components. The crown has \(t(t-1)\) edges and its line graph has degree \(2(t-2)\); adding the apex gives exactly the claimed vertex and edge counts.

When \(t=3\), choose the two nonisolated rows \(b,d\) and start with white edges \((b,0),(d,0),(b,1)\). Exchanging \((d,0)\) for \((d,1)\) switches the dominant column, connecting the two families. When \(t=2\), the four possibilities are the two whole rows and two whole columns, with each row intersecting each column in one cell; the exchange graph is a four-cycle.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify_rook.py` checks the exchange graph on finite instances using the ordinary forcing rule. It also checks the previously known all-set and counting formulas, which are included in the script as cross-checks, and the explicit thin component description for \(4\le t\le12\). The general proof above, not the bounded enumeration, establishes the transition.

## Relationship to prior work and limitations

Montazeri–Soltankhah, DOI [10.55016/ojs/cdm.v16i1.68557](https://doi.org/10.55016/ojs/cdm.v16i1.68557), Theorem 2.1 at \(k=1\), give the scalar minimum. The earlier [rook zero-forcing polynomial result](https://github.com/Resultary/2026/tree/main/2026/10/12/SCOPE-zero-forcing-polynomials-of-rook-graphs-via-bipartite-forests--dd424d573692) gives the all-set forest criterion, the tree-plus-isolate extremizers, and the labeled Stirling count. Geneson–Haas–Hogben, arXiv:2009.00220, develop the general exchange-graph framework; their tree result concerns the underlying graph being forced, not the white bipartite tree in this proof. A same-day triangular-graph result treats \(L(K_n)\), a different family. The later [complete-prism reconfiguration result](https://github.com/Resultary/2026/tree/main/2026/11/22/SCOPE-crown-line-geometry-and-frozen-universal-states-in-complete-pris--72025585cc5d) also states the two-cone description of the token-jump graph on \(K_t\square K_2\), without addressing the full two-parameter transition. No exchange diameter is asserted, and earlier coverage under different terminology is not ruled out.
