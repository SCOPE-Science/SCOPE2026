For rook graphs \(K_m\square K_n\), ordinary zero forcing is equivalent to the initially white cells forming a bipartite forest with a leaf on the side opposite an isolated vertex; this gives an exact minimum-set count with a Stirling-number correction. The minimum-set exchange graph is connected whenever both dimensions exceed two, but for \(K_2\square K_t\) with \(t\ge4\) it splits into two explicit joins of a vertex with the line graph of a crown graph.
