# A 76-orbit classification of smooth bidegree-\((3,5)\) filling curves over \(\mathbf F_2\)

## Finding

Put \(Q=\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf F_2}\times\mathbf P^1_{\mathbf F_2}\), with ordered factors. Exactly **2,304** nonzero bihomogeneous equations of bidegree \((3,5)\) define geometrically smooth curves containing all nine points of \(Q(\mathbf F_2)\). Here equations are identified up to nonzero scalar, which is immaterial over \(\mathbf F_2\). These curves form exactly **76** orbits under \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_2)\times\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_2)\). The orbit distribution is:

| Orbit size | Number of orbits | Stabilizer order | Equations |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 36 | 55 | 1 | 1980 |
| 18 | 15 | 2 | 270 |
| 12 | 3 | 3 | 36 |
| 6 | 3 | 6 | 18 |
| **Total** | **76** | | **2304** |

Every such curve is geometrically integral and of genus \(8\). These are embedded/projective-equivalence classes relative to the ordered quadric factors, **not** abstract isomorphism classes of genus-eight curves.

## Assumptions and scope

Use homogeneous coordinates \(([X_0:X_1],[Y_0:Y_1])\). A bidegree-\((3,5)\) form has the unique coefficient expansion
\[
F=\sum_{i=0}^{3}\sum_{j=0}^{5}c_{ij}X_0^{3-i}X_1^iY_0^{5-j}Y_1^j,
\qquad c_{ij}\in\mathbf F_2.
\]
Define its coefficient bitmask as \(\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}2^{6i+j}\). Count all forms, not only those supplied by a special ansatz. Smoothness means geometric smoothness after extending to an algebraic closure. A curve is *filling* when it contains every \(\mathbf F_2\)-point of \(Q\).

## Proof

**Step 1: Complete coefficient space.** Write
\[
S_X=X_0^2X_1+X_0X_1^2,\qquad
S_Y=Y_0^2Y_1+Y_0Y_1^2.
\]
These cut out the three rational points of each respective projective line, scheme-theoretically. Because the nine points are their Cartesian product, the bihomogeneous ideal of the rational points of \(Q\) is \((S_X,S_Y)\). This is also a specialization of the rational-point-ideal description in Homma–Kim. Thus all filling bidegree-\((3,5)\) forms, with no omission, are
\[
F=S_XA(Y_0,Y_1)+S_YB(X_0,X_1;Y_0,Y_1),
\]
where \(A\) has degree \(5\) in \(Y\), and \(B\) has bidegree \((3,2)\). The first summand has dimension \(6\), the second dimension \(4\cdot3=12\), and the intersection is \(S_XS_Y\) times binary quadratics in \(Y\), of dimension \(3\), because \(S_X,S_Y\) is a regular sequence. Consequently the filling linear system has dimension \(6+12-3=15\), and exactly \(2^{15}=32,768\) coefficient vectors, including zero.

A concrete basis used by an independent check is \(S_X\) times each of the six degree-\(5\) monomials in \(Y\), together with \(S_Y\) times \(X_0^{3-i}X_1^iY_0^{2-j}Y_1^j\) for \(i\in\{0,1,3\}\) and \(j\in\{0,1,2\}\). Its fifteen vectors are independent and span the same space.

**Step 2: Rational-point screening.** At each of the nine rational points, evaluate the two derivatives in affine local coordinates. A curve already vanishing at the point is smooth there exactly when at least one derivative is nonzero. Full enumeration of all \(32,768\) forms leaves exactly \(5,832\) forms smooth at the nine rational points. This necessary screen does not claim geometric smoothness.

**Step 3: Complete projective orbits.** Each \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_2)\) has order \(6\), generated by exchanging the two homogeneous coordinates and replacing the second by the sum of the two. Apply these generators independently to the ordered \(X\)- and \(Y\)-factors, expanding coefficients modulo \(2\). All \(5,832\) screened forms partition into \(206\) orbits: \(135\) of size \(36\), \(40\) of size \(18\), \(13\) of size \(12\), \(15\) of size \(6\), and \(3\) of size \(2\). The sum \(135(36)+40(18)+13(12)+15(6)+3(2)=5,832\) verifies the partition size.

**Step 4: Geometric singularities (not extension-field sampling).** In each of the four standard affine charts of \(Q\), dehomogenize the curve to a polynomial \(f(u,v)\) over \(\mathbf F_2\). By the Jacobian criterion and the weak Nullstellensatz, that chart has no geometric singularity exactly when the ideal
\[
(f,\partial f/\partial u,\partial f/\partial v)
=\mathbf F_2[u,v].
\]
This is decided exactly by a Gröbner basis equaling \(\{1\}\). Since coordinate automorphisms preserve smoothness, one representative per orbit suffices. Of the \(206\) representatives, exactly \(76\) are smooth, with orbit sizes as tabulated in the finding. Their sizes sum to \(2304\).

**Step 5: Integral genus-eight curves.** A smooth effective divisor of bidegree \((a,b)\), with both degrees positive, on \(\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\) cannot split into disjoint nonzero geometric effective divisors: the intersection number of two complementary component sums is \(a_1b_2+a_2b_1>0\), apart from decompositions entirely in a single ruling, impossible when both total degrees are positive. A reducible smooth divisor would have disjoint components, contradiction. Hence all counted curves are geometrically integral. Adjunction yields genus \((3-1)(5-1)=8\).

## Verification

The accompanying programs reproduce the classification with exact arithmetic. Run from the `verification` directory:

```text
python enumerate_orbits.py
python smooth_classify.py
python check_groebner.py
python independent_basis.py
python check_all_forms.py
```

The first route constructs the complete filling vector space via nine rational evaluation constraints, screens derivatives at all rational points, computes all \(206\) orbits, and tests geometric smoothness by computer-algebra Gröbner bases over \(\mathbf F_2\). It writes `classification.json` with every representative and its smoothness result. A separate implementation of Buchberger reduction over \(\mathbf F_2\), without reliance on the computer-algebra Gröbner routine, verifies the orbit representatives. Another check constructs the linear space from \((S_X,S_Y)\) and tests the exact singularity ideals for **all** \(5,832\) rationally smooth equations without using orbit reduction, obtaining the same \(2304\) count. `orbit_representatives.csv` lists the 76 final coefficient masks and their exact orbit sizes. The enumerators and both geometric-smoothness tests were executed from these reproducibility paths and agreed with the reference data.

## Relationship to prior work

Homma and Kim, *Filling curves for \(\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\)* (arXiv:2203.11595, initial public version March 22, 2022), establish the rational-point ideal and minimum irreducible filling bidegrees, including \((3,4)\) and \((4,3)\) for \(\mathbf F_2\); they do not give a census of smooth bidegree-\((3,5)\) equations or the resulting \(76\) orbit classes. Ahammed, Campbell, and Moon (arXiv:2512.02796, December 2025) study abundant smooth minimal-degree filling curves, principally of bidegree \((q+1,q+1)\) for odd \(q\), and do not present this binary bidegree-\((3,5)\) census. Huang, Kedlaya, and Lau (arXiv:2402.00716) enumerate curves of genus six over \(\mathbf F_2\); these are a different genus and equivalence relation. Campbell and colleagues (arXiv:2310.09293) study filling complete-intersection curves in projective three-space, not this split-quadric embedded orbit classification.

## Limitations

This is a finite embedded classification with exact certificates of exhaustion. It does not classify abstract genus-eight curves over \(\mathbf F_2\), nor claim that 76 is the number of abstract isomorphism classes. The count depends on the ordered bidegree and on coordinate equivalence under the indicated order-36 group. The literature comparison cannot exclude an unindexed previous computation or table. No independent scientific audit has been performed.
