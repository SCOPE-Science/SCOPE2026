# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The full cycle-space lift identifies the projector as a cycle Gram inverse, and the one-coordinate Sherman–Morrison update makes every absolute column sum a convex-over-positive-affine function. The elementary quasiconvex maximum inequality and coordinate iteration give the corner theorem. The diamond identity follows from its explicit two-cycle Gram matrix. An independently constructed incidence-Laplacian checker verified 28 expansions, 216 midpoint inequalities, 2,052 box instances and 17 diamond lengths. Only the analytical derivation establishes the all-graph theorem.

**Originality — PASS, with coverage risk.** Dilworth, Kutzarova and Ostrovskii (arXiv:2305.12582) treat invariant and orthogonal cycle projections on tori and Hamming graphs. A known general nonuniform-subdivision Gram identity does already determine individual endpoint norms. The additional claim is a simultaneous, graph-independent *optimization across subdivision parameters*: coordinatewise quasiconvexity and the exact corner-maximum principle for both complementary projections, with a strict intermediate dip. These are not pointwise formulas or a family-specific restatement and require a separate rank-one/convexity comparison. The existing pointwise formula remains the principal implication/novelty risk. Nowak and Stachowiak's weighted exactness treatment does not establish these endpoint maximum comparisons. Searches cannot exclude all prior statements.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Independent edge subdivisions create a potentially large finite design grid whose projector dimension changes with every length. The universal theorem reduces exact worst-case norm control to the corners *without enumerating interior choices*, while the diamond shows monotonicity is false. This is a structural global optimization result, not the recomputation of a particular projection norm. The result concerns canonical Euclidean orthogonal projectors only.
