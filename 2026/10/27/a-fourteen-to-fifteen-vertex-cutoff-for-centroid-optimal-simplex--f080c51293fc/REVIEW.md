# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is about the entire nonempty-face barycentric point set. For every integer from three through fourteen, the constructive positive extension is explicitly specified by rational coefficients and tested on every symmetry class of unordered point pairs. There are exactly 1,489 nonidentical classes across these dimensions; all squared norm inequalities and probability normalizations were checked using integer fractions, not floating-point tolerances. The bound for every larger integer follows from two explicit one-Lipschitz vertex data witnesses applied after permutation averaging, and the strict threshold reduces to the rational comparison \(n>672/47\). Signed and asymmetric operators are included in the lower argument. The source of both inequalities, and the exact metric normalization, are stated in the result.

## Originality — PASS, with risk

Bruè–Di Marino–Stra (2018) provide a general random-projection construction and Basso (2021) provide quantitative finite-space extension/projection machinery, not this exact face-subdivision threshold. Related fixed-rank simplex-face formulas do not imply a common operator for all ranks. Relevant mathematical-record searches using full-barycentric, face-centroid, interpolation and simplex phrases did not locate an equivalent statement or one with broader implications. This absence is not definitive proof; unpublished and less-indexed equivalent computations remain a risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact cutoff separates compatibility of many individually controlled extension problems from failure of a common centroid-optimal operator. The fourteen-to-fifteen-vertex change is the first sharp obstruction in this canonical hierarchy of simplicial barycentric refinement, and the proof supplies both a reproducible rational construction on the positive side and a two-witness global obstruction on the negative side. The result does not require a downstream theorem to make the threshold meaningful.

## Limits

For \(n\ge15\) the exact operator norm remains unknown. The affirmative side is a finite exact certificate rather than a closed all-dimensional formula. Only real scalar-valued data and the inherited Euclidean metric are covered. Independent review remains outstanding.
