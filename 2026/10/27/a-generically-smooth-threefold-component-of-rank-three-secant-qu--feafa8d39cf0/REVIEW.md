# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The entire claim is reconstructed from restriction of conics to a line, the kernel \(\mathcal O_C(1)\oplus\mathcal O_C(2)\) of the quotient, and the first-order determinantal condition \(a\langle1,t\rangle\subseteq\langle1,t,t^2\rangle\), \(b\langle1,t,t^2\rangle\subseteq\langle1,t,t^2\rangle\). Because \(m\ge4\), multiplication by \(t\) of degree-at-most-two representatives cannot wrap modulo the degree-\(m\) polynomial; hence \(a\) has degree at most one and \(b\) is constant. Tangent dimension equals the exhibited parameter dimension three, proving generic regularity and the component claim. The \(m=3\) boundary and conditions for the optional numerical comparison have been separately checked. The finite exact-arithmetic script corroborates but does not prove the infinite claim.

The relevant full primary manuscript is Hitching, arXiv:2302.04328v1, especially Example 2.4, Propositions 2.9, 2.12 and 5.2, and Conjecture 2.13. These establish the general definitions, Hilbert–Quot correspondence, a splitting construction, and abstract tangent formulas; they do not identify the all-degree plane-curve line-section threefold as a generically smooth component or compute its exact tangent dimension. Searches for rank-three split scroll Quot secant loci, Veronese plane-curve sections and the specific plane-conic construction returned no equivalent statements in the queried corpus. Residual risk: this component may be implicit in literature on secant varieties of Segre–Veronese scrolls or in an unindexed specialization of the published general tangent theorem. Lack of search matches is not itself a novelty certificate.

## Value — PASS

This is a natural and complete identification of a generically smooth irreducible component in a determinantal Quot secant locus for every plane-curve degree at least four, despite strongly negative naive determinantal expected dimension. The explicit multiplier lemma explains the uniform local geometry. In degrees six through eight it reveals a concrete obstacle to a naturally dual-normalized Martens-type bound, while properly respecting the literal published formulation. The result is not a random finite instance or a routine invariant recomputation.
