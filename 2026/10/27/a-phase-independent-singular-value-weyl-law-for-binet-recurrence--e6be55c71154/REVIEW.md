# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof determines the exact normalized monomial shift entries on the classical Gaussian Fock space. The supercritical obstruction uses the nonzero mean square of distinct maximal-modulus modes; the subcritical regime has geometric Schatten decay. In the critical regime, compact torus equidistribution and the proved layer-cake counting lemma yield \(k s_k^2\to c_U\) even when infinitely many input weights vanish. The harmonic trace remainder follows from uniformly bounded off-diagonal geometric harmonic sums. The standalone finite checker only corroborates normalization and examples.

**Originality — PASS with explicit historical-priority limits.** Orozco López, arXiv:2610.20593v1, defines the Binet recurrence derivative and gives algebraic/Leibniz identities rather than the standard-Fock singular-value law. Alpay–Cerejeiras–Kähler, arXiv:2112.07883, use generalized Fock norms to study sampling, not this fixed Gaussian Weyl coefficient. General Dixmier trace results offer potential broader methods but do not state the rank-by-rank law for this family. Searches of related research records found no covering statement. A general older almost-periodic rearrangement theorem could subsume the counting argument; priority has not been independently certified.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** This gives a natural complete boundedness/compactness/Schatten trichotomy for a recurrence calculus and an exact, phase-independent leading singular-value constant across rational, irrational and cancellation-rich characteristic spectra. A concrete Jackson derivative corollary shows a factor-two jump at the unit spectral boundary.

**Closest source and limitations.** Orozco López, arXiv:2610.20593v1 (2026-10-14), establishes the recurrence derivative's monomial action. The present result requires finite distinct nonzero Binet modes with zero initial term and the standard Gaussian Hilbert norm. No second-order rate or exact finite-rank singular values are claimed, and a broader abstract rearrangement theorem may have historical precedence.
