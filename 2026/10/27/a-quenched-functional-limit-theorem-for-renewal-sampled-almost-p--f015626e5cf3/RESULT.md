# A quenched functional limit theorem for renewal-sampled almost-periodic covariances

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \((X_t)_{t\in\mathbb R}\) be a jointly measurable, real-valued stochastic process with \(|X_t|\le K\) almost surely, simultaneously for all \(t\), where \(K<\infty\). Assume it is \(m\)-dependent for some finite \(m>0\): the sigma fields generated by observations in sets separated by a distance greater than \(m\) are independent. Write \(\xi_t=X_t-\mathbb E X_t\) and, for \(u\ge0\), \(c_u(t)=\mathbb E[\xi_t\xi_{t+u}]\). Assume every \(c_u\) is Bohr almost periodic in \(t\), and the map \(u\mapsto c_u\) is continuous in the uniform norm on \([0,\infty)\). In particular \(c_u=0\) for \(u>m\).

Let \(\psi_1,\psi_2,\ldots\) be i.i.d. strictly positive random variables, independent of \(X\), with \(|\mathbb E e^{i\lambda\psi_1}|<1\) for every nonzero real \(\lambda\). Set \(Y_k=\sum_{j=1}^k\psi_j\). Then there is a probability-one set of sampling trajectories such that, **for each fixed trajectory in this set**, under the conditional law of \(X\),

\[
\left(n^{-1/2}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\xi_{Y_k}\right)_{0\le t\le1}
\ \Longrightarrow\ (\sigma B_t)_{0\le t\le1}
\quad\text{in }D([0,1])\text{ with the }J_1\text{ topology},
\]

where \(B\) is standard Brownian motion, including the degenerate case \(\sigma=0\), and

\[
\sigma^2=M(c_0)+2\sum_{h=1}^{\infty}
\mathbb E_{\psi}\big[M(c_{\psi_1+\cdots+\psi_h})\big].
\]

Here \(M(f)\) denotes the Bohr mean of an almost-periodic function. The series converges absolutely. This is a **quenched functional** theorem: conditioning on the entire random observation schedule occurs before taking the limit; it is not merely a scalar central limit theorem averaged over schedules. Only almost periodicity of the covariance functions, not of all finite-dimensional distributions, is required.

## Assumptions and scope

The environment \((\psi_j)\) and process \(X\) are independent on a product space. The statement concerns the regular conditional law given the **whole** schedule \((Y_j)_{j\ge1}\), which for independent factors equals the fixed law of \(X\) evaluated at those deterministic times. The uniform-norm continuity assumption includes the boundary \(u=m\); it ensures integrals of covariance functions remain Bohr almost periodic. No positive lower bound on an increment and no finite moment assumption on \(\psi_1\) are imposed. The paths of the rescaled partial sums are cadlag step functions. All limits in the theorem are as the integer \(n\to\infty\).

## Proof

**1. Sparse collisions have exponential tails.** Fix \(a>0\) and put \(\rho=\mathbb E e^{-a\psi_1}<1\), using strict positivity. For \(S_h=\psi_1+\dots+\psi_h\), Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{-aS_h}\) gives
\[
\mathbb P(S_h\le m)\le e^{am}\rho^h.
\]
Thus \(\sum_{h\ge1}\mathbb P(S_h\le m)<\infty\), and the proposed variance series is absolutely summable because \(|c_u(t)|\le4K^2\) and \(c_u=0\) for \(u>m\).

**2. A pathwise almost-periodic ergodic mean.** For each fixed \(\lambda\ne0\), let \(\varphi_\lambda=\mathbb E e^{i\lambda\psi_1}\) and \(D_k=e^{i\lambda Y_k}-\varphi_\lambda e^{i\lambda Y_{k-1}}\), with \(Y_0=0\). The \(D_k\) are bounded complex martingale differences, so \(n^{-1}\sum_{k\le n}D_k\to0\) almost surely. The identity
\[
(1-\varphi_\lambda)\sum_{k=1}^n e^{i\lambda Y_k}
=\sum_{k=1}^nD_k+\varphi_\lambda(1-e^{i\lambda Y_n})
\]
shows that the empirical average of every nonzero character tends to zero. Finite trigonometric polynomial approximation then gives, for each specified Bohr almost-periodic \(f\),
\[
 n^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^n f(Y_k)\longrightarrow M(f)
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
The probability-one set is chosen simultaneously for the countable family of functions used below.

**3. A strong law for every covariance lag.** For fixed \(h\ge1\), define \(F_{k,h}=c_{Y_{k+h}-Y_k}(Y_k)\), and let
\[
g_h(t)=\mathbb E_\psi[c_{S_h}(t)].\]
The map \(u\mapsto c_u\) is norm-continuous with compact support in \([0,m]\); approximating its integral by finite sums proves \(g_h\) is Bohr almost periodic. Conditional on \(\psi_1,\dots,\psi_k\), \(F_{k,h}\) has conditional mean \(g_h(Y_k)\). For each fixed residue class modulo \(h+1\), the variables \(F_{k,h}-g_h(Y_k)\) form bounded martingale differences with respect to the filtration at the ends of their respective \(h\)-step lookahead windows. Their empirical average therefore tends to zero almost surely. Applying step 2 to \(g_h\) yields
\[
 n^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^nF_{k,h}
\longrightarrow\gamma_h:=M(g_h)=\mathbb E_\psi[M(c_{S_h})]
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
The equality follows by norm-continuous integration and continuity of \(M\). The same argument with \(h=0\) treats \(n^{-1}\sum c_0(Y_k)\).

For all \(H\), the absolute contribution of lags \(h>H\) to the empirical covariance is at most
\[
4K^2 n^{-1}\sum_{k\le n}\sum_{h>H}
\mathbf1\{\psi_{k+1}+\cdots+\psi_{k+h}\le m\}.
\]
For each \(H\), the inner sum is an integrable stationary function of the one-sided i.i.d. shift; Birkhoff's ergodic theorem makes its empirical mean converge almost surely to \(\sum_{h>H}\mathbb P(S_h\le m)\). This vanishes as \(H\to\infty\). Consequently there exists a single full-measure set of schedules on which, writing \(A_\ell=\sum_{k=1}^\ell\xi_{Y_k}\) and taking variance only over \(X\),
\[
\sup_{0\le t\le1}\left|n^{-1}\operatorname{Var}_X(A_{\lfloor nt\rfloor})-t\sigma^2\right|\longrightarrow0.
\]
The uniformity follows from the prefix form of convergent Cesàro sums and the nonnegative collision-tail envelope. This also proves \(\sigma^2\ge0\).

**4. Almost-sure logarithmic dependence range.** For an integer \(r_n=\lceil C\log(n+1)\rceil\), the union bound and step 1 give
\[
\mathbb P\left(\exists k\le n:\ Y_{k+r_n}-Y_k\le m\right)
\le ne^{am}\rho^{r_n}.
\]
Choose \(C>3/|\log\rho|\). The bound is summable in \(n\), so Borel–Cantelli gives, on another probability-one set, for all sufficiently large \(n\), all pairs of sampled indices among the first \(n\) separated by more than \(r_n\) indices correspond to times farther apart than \(m\). Therefore the first \(n\) centered observations form an \(r_n\)-dependent sequence under the conditional law of \(X\), even though the original sampling increments can be arbitrarily small.

**5. Block-gap conditional functional limit.** Work at a schedule in the intersection of the full-measure sets above. Set \(b_n=\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor\). Divide the indices into consecutive main blocks of length \(b_n\), with \(r_n+1\) deleted indices separating main blocks (also delete any incomplete tail). The main-block sums are independent under \(X\). There are \(O(nr_n/b_n+b_n+r_n)\) deleted indices. Splitting the deleted sequence by residue modulo \(r_n+1\) and using Doob's \(L^2\) maximal inequality on the independent centered variables in each class shows
\[
\frac1n\mathbb E_X\left[
\max_{\ell\le n}\left|\sum_{k\le\ell,\,k\text{ deleted}}\xi_{Y_k}\right|^2\right]
\le C_K\frac{(r_n+1)(nr_n/b_n+b_n+r_n)}{n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The same residue splitting and the fourth-moment formula for independent bounded centered summands imply for each main block \(J\)
\[
\mathbb E_X\left|\sum_{k\in J}\xi_{Y_k}\right|^4
\le C_K b_n^2(r_n+1)^2.
\]
Summing over \(O(n/b_n)\) blocks and dividing by \(n^2\) gives the Lyapunov bound \(O(b_n(r_n+1)^2/n)\to0\). Their cumulative conditional variance profile, normalized by \(n\), converges uniformly to \(t\sigma^2\): this follows from step 3, the deleted-terms maximal \(L^2\) bound, and the fact that a partial last block has variance at most \(C_Kb_n(r_n+1)=o(n)\). The functional Lindeberg–Feller theorem for independent triangular-array blocks gives convergence of their step partial-sum process to \(\sigma B\). Restoring the deleted terms changes the process by a quantity tending to zero in conditional probability in uniform norm. This proves the asserted quenched weak convergence in \(D([0,1])\).

## Verification

A reproducible standard-library script, `verify.py`, checks an explicit nonstationary example. Take a two-sided Brownian motion \(W\), let \(\varepsilon\in[0,1)\), and set \(X_t=(1+\varepsilon\cos(2\pi t))\operatorname{sgn}(W_{t+1}-W_t)\). This is bounded, \(1\)-dependent, periodic in distribution, and has norm-continuous lag covariance
\[
 c_u(t)=(1+\varepsilon\cos2\pi t)(1+\varepsilon\cos2\pi(t+u))
 \frac2\pi\arcsin((1-u)_+)\quad(u\ge0).
\]
For independent exponential rate-one sampling, \(\sum_{h\ge1}\mathbb P(S_h\in du)=du\) on positive \(u\), yielding
\[
\sigma_\varepsilon^2=1+\varepsilon^2/2+
\frac4\pi\int_0^1(1+\varepsilon^2\cos(2\pi u)/2)\arcsin(1-u)\,du.
\]
At \(\varepsilon=0\), this is exactly \(3-4/\pi\). The script evaluates the conditional covariance sum for 90,000 seeded sampled times and checks its agreement with the theoretical value for \(\varepsilon=0\) and \(\varepsilon=1/2\), plus twelve deterministic trigonometric mean identities. Finite numerical checks do **not** prove the almost-sure or infinite-dimensional limit.

## Relationship to prior work

Berger and Strobel (2026, arXiv:2610.23669, primary MSC 60G10) prove a **scalar, annealed** central limit theorem for finite-range-dependent almost-periodic processes under i.i.d. positive nonarithmetic random sampling. Their covariance lemmas and summability regime motivate this result, but their Theorem 4.5 does not state almost-sure conditional convergence given the sampling trajectory or process-level convergence. The present theorem assumes bounded observations and uniform lag-covariance continuity and obtains both stronger conclusions, while only requiring almost periodicity of covariances. Berger and Mohamed (2022) treat central limit behavior for almost-periodic processes without independent random sampling. Brandes and Curato (2018) examine renewal-sampled Lévy moving averages, and Brandes, Curato and Stelzer (2020) study preservation of mixing properties under renewal sampling for stationary inputs. These theorems address different objects or annealed/second-order quantities; none of the inspected statements entails the quenched functional result under the current assumptions. General blocking and independent-array invariance principles are classical ingredients, not claimed as new.

## Limitations

Uniform boundedness and uniform-norm continuity of the lag covariance are additional restrictions compared with Berger–Strobel's integrability hypotheses. The conclusion is conditional weak convergence for almost every sampling schedule; no uniform rate over schedules is claimed. No Gaussian joint law is assumed. Novelty has been assessed against identified primary sources and research records, not certified exhaustively; an implicit formulation in unindexed limit-theorem literature remains possible. No independent audit has been performed.
