# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
Every proper subalgebra is one of two exhaustive types: a centre-containing lift of a proper quotient subspace, or a functional graph above a nonzero isotropic quotient subspace. The Lie-generation criterion follows from the nondegenerate bracket. The exact completion sum and Lagrangian symmetric-graph count give three disjoint edge types. The only potentially subtle asymptotic case is rank three: the leading exponents and coefficients are checked explicitly, and complement probabilities for the surviving dimension-three types tend to one. Independent direct Lie-bracket closure over five small fields/ranks reproduces every predicted count. The proof covers characteristic two; the enumeration is finite verification, not a substitute for the general proof.

## Originality — PASS
Relevant comparisons include the 2026 finite-Lie-algebra comaximal-graph paper and its low-dimensional antecedent, Das's subspace-sum graph, and previously established five-dimensional degrees and arbitrary-rank centre-free Lagrangian complement adjacency. The weighted all-dimensional mixed centre-containing/centre-free contribution and the exact rank-three limiting probability are not supplied by the inspected results. A separate exact Heisenberg **subalgebra commutativity degree** concerns permuting subalgebras, not Lie-generation. The broad Frattini blow-up perspective could make the full finite sum readily derivable, so its value is assessed primarily together with the unexpected asymptotic rank-three discontinuity. Equivalent unindexed literature remains possible.

## Value — PASS
The comaximal graph is a natural invariant for understanding pairs of proper subalgebras generating a finite-dimensional Lie algebra. The result supplies the complete all-rank census and a sharp structural phase classification, separating the seven-dimensional Heisenberg algebra from every other odd dimension as the field grows. This is a motivated infinite-family answer beyond the previously treated low-dimensional cases, not an arbitrary slice.

## Residual limitations
The result does not concern other nilpotent Lie algebra families, does not classify triangles, and does not control joint growth of rank and field size. The low-dimensional and noncentral complementary-Lagrangian subresults are previously covered. Broad graph-blow-up principles and unindexed finite-geometry references pose remaining coverage risks.
