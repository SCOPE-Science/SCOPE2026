# A residue criterion extends a positive-characteristic logarithmic foliation theorem

## Finding

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(p>0\), let \(n\ge3\), and let \(r\ge3\). Choose positive degrees \(1\le d_1\le\cdots\le d_r\). Suppose exactly two indices \(a,b\) satisfy \(p\nmid d_a,d_b\), and assume \(p\nmid(d_a+d_b)\). Then \(\operatorname{Log}_{(d_1,\ldots,d_r)}(\mathbb P_k^n)\) is an irreducible component of the support of \(\operatorname{Fol}^{1}_{d}(\mathbb P_k^n)\), where \(d=\sum_i d_i-2\).

## Assumptions and scope

Write \(M=d+2\) and \(S=\{i:p\mid d_i\}\). The logarithmic locus is the closure of the foliations given by \(\omega=\sum_i\lambda_i\,df_i/f_i\) with \(\sum_i d_i\lambda_i=0\), where \(f_i\) are homogeneous of degrees \(d_i\) defining a simple normal crossing divisor. Multiplying by \(\prod_i f_i\) gives a twisted projective 1-form of twist \(M\). The locus is irreducible: the hypersurface parameter spaces and the projective residue hyperplane are irreducible, and the locus is the closure of the image of a dense open of their product. We work with the underlying reduced spaces and make no assertion about generic reducedness of the foliation scheme.

## Proof

### 1. A point with a reduced p-curvature divisor

Set \(\lambda_b=1\), \(\lambda_a=-d_b/d_a\in\mathbb F_p^\times\). These are distinct by the hypothesis on the sum. Choose the other residues in distinct nonzero \(\mathbb F_p^\times\)-cosets, none in the coset of \(1\); there is at least one such residue because \(S\ne\varnothing\). All residues are distinct, the weighted relation holds, and every \(i\) has a partner \(j\) with \(\lambda_i/\lambda_j\notin\mathbb F_p\).

Choose the \(f_i\) generally so that \(D=\sum_i(f_i=0)\) is simple normal crossing. The Cartier transform is \(\eta=C\omega=\sum_i\lambda_i^{1/p}\,df_i/f_i\), also projective because \(\sum_i d_i\lambda_i^{1/p}=0\). For any tangent vector field \(v\), the closed-form Cartier identity gives \(\omega(v^p)=\eta(v)^p\): the term \(v^{p-1}(\omega(v))\) is zero. This is the evaluation identity used in [2, §4.6]. The two residue rows \((\lambda_i)_i\) and \((\lambda_i^{1/p})_i\) are independent, because a partner pair has a nonzero two-by-two minor. At any point away from the poles, independent first jets of two corresponding \(f_i\) allow the pair \((\omega,\eta)\) to take any prescribed pair of covectors at that point: fix the two nonzero values, and vary their differentials. First jets of sections of \(\mathcal O(d_i)\) are surjective for every \(d_i\ge1\). Dependence of the two covectors has codimension \(n-1\ge2\) in the space of pairs of covectors. Applying the incidence dimension bound to the open set where all \(f_i\) are nonzero shows that for general \(f_i\) the zero locus of \(\omega\wedge\eta\) away from \(D\) has codimension at least two. In particular, the saturated foliation defined by \(\omega\) has degree \(d\) and nonzero p-curvature: the twisted numerator is nonzero at the generic points of the poles, and has no divisorial zero elsewhere. For a codimension-one foliation, nonzero p-curvature is equivalent to p-denseness [2, §4].

At the generic point of \(H_i=(f_i=0)\), a local generator of the twisted defining form is \(x\omega\) up to a unit, with \(x\) a local equation of \(H_i\). The form \(\eta-(\lambda_i^{1/p}/\lambda_i)\omega\) is regular there. Its restriction to the tangent bundle of \(H_i\) is nonzero at the generic point: it has a nonzero logarithmic residue along a transverse intersection with any partner \(H_j\), at a general point of that intersection outside the other components. The tangent space of the foliation reduces to that of \(H_i\) there at the generic point because \(x\omega\) reduces to \(\lambda_i\,dx\). Consequently the p-curvature values of \(x\omega\) on tangent fields have valuation at least one, and equal to one for a tangent field with nonvanishing Cartier evaluation on reduction. Away from \(D\) the covector independence in codimension one ensures that no divisor is in the p-curvature degeneracy divisor. Thus \(\Delta_{\mathcal F}=D\), reduced. This proves the needed consequence of [1, Lemma 6.1] at the chosen general point without requiring nonvanishing of \(\omega\wedge\eta\) at every crossing. In particular, its leading wedge term at \(H_a\cap H_b\) need not be nonzero.

### 2. Realizing nearby degeneracy divisors in a fixed linear system

Let \(\Sigma\) be an irreducible component of the reduced foliation space through \(\mathcal F\). Work on an open neighborhood \(U\subset\Sigma\) where the twisted form \(\Omega_t\) is represented by algebraic coefficients, and is saturated in each fiber. By [2, Lemma 6.3], on a nonempty open of \(U\) the foliations are p-dense and the canonical degree of the kernel of p-curvature is constant, equal to that at \(\mathcal F\). The normal line bundle of the codimension-one foliation is \(\mathcal O(M)\) throughout. By [2, Proposition 4.12] this gives \(\deg\Delta_t=M\) on that nonempty open. No assertion that the open in Lemma 6.3 contains \(\mathcal F\) is needed.

Here is a relative divisor construction. In each standard affine chart write the dehomogenized defining form as \(\alpha=\sum_j A_j\,dx_j\). Use the tangent derivations \(v_{ij}=A_i\partial_j-A_j\partial_i\), taking the p-fold iterate relative to the base. The polynomials \(\alpha(v_{ij}^{p})\) have coefficients algebraic in \(t\). At every codimension-one point of each saturated fiber the \(v_{ij}\) generate the tangent module, because some \(A_i\) is a unit there. By Frobenius linearity of p-curvature its divisorial vanishing orders are the minima of the valuations of these polynomials. Homogenize this list on each chart to a fixed sufficiently large degree. A projective hypersurface component can be tested in a chart where it is not at infinity; any additional homogeneous factors introduced at infinity in one chart do not affect the minimum over all charts. Thus for each p-dense fiber the homogeneous common divisor of the entire list is exactly the equation of \(\Delta_t\), up to scalar.

The incidence of \((t,[F])\in U\times\mathbb P H^0(\mathcal O(M))\) such that \(F\) divides all listed polynomials is closed: for a nonzero homogeneous \(F\), multiplication by \(F\) in the relevant degrees is injective of constant rank. The incidence dominates \(U\) by the generic degree equality. Choose an irreducible closed subset of the incidence dominating \(U\). Its projection is proper, hence contains the central point, over which the only possibility is \([F]=[D]\). On this dominating family the universal divisor is a relative degree-\(M\) Cartier divisor. Reducedness and normal crossings in codimension two hold on an open containing the central pair by [1, Corollary 5.4]. On a dense open of this family the universal divisor is also exactly \(\Delta_t\), because both have degree \(M\) and the incidence gives divisibility.

### 3. Logarithmic forms and constancy of degrees

At a p-dense point of that dense open, there exists a closed rational defining 1-form \(\xi\) by [2, Proposition 4.4]. By [2, Proposition 4.14], the signed polar divisor of \(\xi\) agrees with \(\Delta_t\) modulo \(p\). Since \(\Omega_t\) has no codimension-one zero, the signed polar divisor of \(\Omega_t/F\) is exactly \(\Delta_t\). The quotient between these defining forms is a rational function whose divisor is divisible by \(p\). On \(\mathbb P^n\) such a function is a p-th power: the divisor divided by \(p\) is principal by torsion-freeness of the Picard group, and constants in \(k\) are p-th powers. Thus \(\Omega_t/F\) is closed. It has only simple poles at the poles of the normal crossing divisor in codimension two. It is logarithmic there, with constant residue on every irreducible component, and is a sum of logarithmic derivatives as in [1, Corollary 5.2]. Equivalently, the residues extend across codimension at least two on the proper normalizations of the components, so they are constant. Restriction to a general line gives the weighted residue relation, and after subtracting the resulting sum of logarithmic derivatives there is a global regular 1-form on \(\mathbb P^n\), hence zero.

On a fixed general line, the central \(D\) cuts out \(M\) distinct points, transverse to the smooth locus of each component and away from their intersections. Choose an affine coordinate containing these points. The line intersections stay simple on an open of the divisor family; after étale localization their points can be labeled. The residues of \(\Omega_t/F\) on the line vary regularly there, since the residue of \(A(z)\,dz/F(z)\) at a simple root \(z_0\) is \(A(z_0)/F'(z_0)\). Use local scalar representatives for \(F\) and \(\Omega_t\), so the central residues are the originally chosen distinct values up to a common nonzero scalar.

To track factor degrees, write the geometric generic divisor as a product of its irreducible factors of degrees \(e_1,\ldots,e_s\). The factorization can be specialized to the central divisor: the incidence adding ordered projective polynomials of those degrees with product \([F]\) is proper over the divisor family, and the closure of a generic factorization dominates it. Each specialized factor is a nonempty product of distinct central components, since their full product is the reduced central divisor. Its line roots specialize to the corresponding roots on those components. All roots of a given generic factor have the same residue, hence so do their specializations by the regular residue formula. Distinct central components have distinct residues, so a specialized factor contains only one central component. The factors therefore match the central components bijectively, with precisely the degrees \(d_1,\ldots,d_r\).

It follows that a general point of \(\Sigma\) lies in \(\operatorname{Log}_{(d_1,\ldots,d_r)}\). A rational logarithmic expression with the prescribed degrees belongs to this closure even if its divisor is only normal crossing in codimension two: the polynomial residue parametrization is defined at that nonzero numerator and the simple normal crossing parameters are dense. Hence \(\Sigma\) is contained in the logarithmic locus. Since that locus is closed and irreducible and \(\Sigma\) is a maximal irreducible closed subset of the foliation space, they coincide.

## Relationship to prior work and limitations

Theorem A (Theorem 6.2) of [1] explicitly omits the stratum with exactly two degrees prime to \(p\) when \(r>2\). The per-component residue condition in its Lemma 6.1 is weaker than requiring every pairwise ratio to lie outside \(\mathbb F_p\). Distinctness, not pairwise avoidance of \(\mathbb F_p\), is what is needed for the residue comparison along a line. The result fills the nonzero-sum subcase using this distinction and the deformation strategy of [1]. It does not treat \(p\mid(d_a+d_b)\), which forces these two residues to coincide, or establish generic reducedness. In characteristic two the stated degree hypothesis is impossible.
