The proof is geometric, not computational. The residue equation and the nonzero Cartier minors give the required residue conditions. A first-jet incidence argument excludes divisorial zeros away from the poles for the chosen general hypersurfaces, and the tangent Cartier evaluations at the poles give exactly the reduced p-curvature divisor. The relative fixed-degree divisor is obtained by closed divisibility incidence; the degree result of Mendson–Pereira (arXiv:2207.08957, Lemma 6.3 and Proposition 4.12) is used only at general points. Proper specialization of factorizations and regular residues on a transverse line then identify the degrees. There is no claim about formal verification, an exhaustive priority search, or generic reducedness of the foliation scheme.
