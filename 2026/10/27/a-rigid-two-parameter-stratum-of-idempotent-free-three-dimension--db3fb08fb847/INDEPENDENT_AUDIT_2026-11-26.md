In W, (xu+yw)^2=(xy+y²)u+y²w, so the only idempotent is zero, W²=W, and the square-zero locus is exactly ku. Automorphisms preserve that line, and uw and w² successively force the coefficient of w in the image of w and the scaling of u to be 1. Thus the only freedom is w↦w+bu. Thus any natural basis is monomial. The unique relation among the three squares has all entries nonzero. Evaluating u²=0 shows that all coordinates of u are nonzero; normalizing them to 1 yields square coefficients summing to zero. The Vandermonde system in 1,2t_i,t_i² has a unique solution, giving precisely the displayed squares. An isomorphism fixes the intrinsic u in square coordinates, so it can only permute the normalized basis, and it must translate the unordered w-coordinate triple. Conversely the coordinate permutation for a translating triple respects the multiplication by uniqueness of the decomposition of the square map of W. An automorphism translates a finite triple to itself, so summing gives b=0 and distinctness gives the identity. Every idempotent of the ambient algebra lies in W, and its derived series stays W. These finite checks supplement, not replace, the exhaustion argument.
