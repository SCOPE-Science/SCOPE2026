# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The result follows from an exact Vandermonde decomposition of the square map of the two-dimensional algebra \(W\), a simultaneous-diagonalization argument ensuring uniqueness of natural square-lines, and an explicit computation of \(\operatorname{Aut}(W)\). The normal-form exhaustion, translation equivalence and trivial ambient automorphism group are proved for every unordered distinct complex triple. The dependency-free rational verifier passed all stated sample identities; no finite test is substituted for the general argument.

## Originality — PASS, subject to bounded-literature limitations

Hu–Wen (arXiv:2609.25023) analyze an exceptional proportional-square-line counterexample, rather than the pairwise-nonproportional stratum or its intrinsic two-parameter moduli. The October 2026 survey (arXiv:2610.22248, Section 2.1.8 and Question 9) reports numerical evidence for two essential parameters but does not present this stratum classification or its automorphism rigidity as a theorem. Earlier general classification tables of all three-dimensional evolution algebras (Cabrera Casado–Siles Molina–Velasco, 2017; Celorrio–Velasco, 2019) pose the main remaining priority risk: the tables' full texts were inaccessible in this inspection and may imply part or all of the normal-form statement after reparametrization. The new assertion is restricted to intrinsic square-algebra geometry, an explicit discriminant classifying map and trivial automorphism groups; no general priority or complete global classification is claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The natural problem is to understand the as-yet-unclassified three-dimensional idempotent-free non-solvable locus. A natural open stratum has an explicit complete pair of invariants, a geometrically meaningful discriminant boundary, and automorphism rigidity. These are structural results for an entire two-dimensional family, not an arbitrary finite sample or parameter substitution. The excluded proportional-square-line boundary remains a separate problem.

## Limitations

The result is only over \(\mathbb C\), only when \(A^2\cong W\), and only in the distinct-natural-square-line stratum. Full older classification tables could not be compared formula by formula, so the remaining overlap risk requires human review. The classifier is a set of ordinary isomorphism classes, not a fine moduli scheme.
