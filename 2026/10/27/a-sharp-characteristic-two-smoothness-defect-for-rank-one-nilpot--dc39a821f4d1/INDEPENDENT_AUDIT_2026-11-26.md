# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Over any base ring, multiplying by each e_i detects its coefficient, so the annihilator is exactly Rz; invertible block maps give precisely A^T A=cI. Linearization gives X^T+X=hI, with h=0 and X symmetric in characteristic two; away from two the differentials of the n(n+1)/2 symmetric Gram equations are independent. For n=1 the multiplier equation simply eliminates c. For n≥2, a direct-sum decomposition of ideals would put kz in the summand with nonzero square and force the other summand into the annihilator, a contradiction. Over an algebraic closure in characteristic two, isometries fix w=(1,…,1). For odd n, split off kw, leaving Sp on w-perp. For even n, a pair (w,t) with b(w,t)=b(t,t)=1 leaves an alternating complement; the map on the complement is determined by a symplectic matrix and its w-coefficient is prescribed by the complement component of g(t). That component and the w-coefficient of g(t) are free, giving n−1 additional affine dimensions. These are regular parameterizations of the reduced groups, with inverses by projections. For similitudes over the algebraic closure, the scalar square root s of c is also the first coordinate of Aw on geometric points, so the parameterization by scalars and reduced isometries gives the stated dimension and connectedness without making a claim about splitting the nonreduced scheme. Including n shear directions, subtracting dimensions gives n−1. The reduced group preserves the line kw; the diag(t_1,…,t_{n−1},1) tangent directions send w to a vector with last entry zero, so they meet the reduced tangent space trivially. The same odd/even parameterizations count points over F_q: first choose a symplectic frame (successive pair counts (q^{2j}−1)q^{2j−1}), then add q^{n−1} choices in even dimension, q−1 scalars and q^n shears. Independently counted orthonormal frames recursively over F_2 in ranks 1–4 and F_4 in ranks 1–3, choosing the next norm-one vector orthogonal to all previous choices: counts (1,2,6,48) and (1,4,60), respectively. This is a bounded arithmetic cross-check, not an all-rank argument.

## originality

PASS

The supported distinct claim is the exact scheme-level smoothness boundary and tangent defect in this nilpotent family, with a concrete complementary infinitesimal subgroup. The algebra, ordinary automorphism blocks, bilinear isometry geometry and symplectic order formulas are not credited as new. Earlier inspected results on ordinary point groups do not give the all-base-change scheme comparison and exact infinitesimal excess; the connectedness and count are applications of that ordinary geometry, not stand-alone novelty claims. No earlier equivalent scheme-level result was established in the bounded comparison.

## value

PASS

The rank-one-square family is a natural test case for the survey's non-perfect smoothness question. The exact excess and its complementary infinitesimal subgroup quantify a failure that cannot be inferred from finite-field automorphism counts; the same analysis explains why the reduced connected geometry and the ordinary order do not reveal it. The contribution is bounded to this family, not a solution of the general survey question.
