# Scientific review: characteristic-two nilpotent evolution automorphisms

## Correctness

**PASS.** Annihilator preservation holds functorially, yielding a complete matrix presentation. Linearizing the defining identities computes the derivation dimension. Orthogonal geometry in characteristic two produces explicit odd/even-dimensional symplectic parameterizations and the reduced geometric dimension, giving the exact smoothness boundary. The finite-field formula follows by counting symplectic bases. Separate exhaustive calculations checked seven small finite-field parameters and 44 tangent systems. The infinite result does not follow solely from tests.

## Originality

**PASS, with residual priority risk.** The 2026 survey's Question 37 motivates group-scheme smoothness for non-perfect evolution algebras. The known annihilator-extension matrix form (Hegazi–Abdelwahab 2015) and bilinear-form similarity classification (Elduque–Labra 2016, characteristic not two) are expressly excluded from the originality claim. The contribution is the exact all-rank characteristic-two smoothness defect, infinitesimal directions, reduced-group geometry and finite-field count. No equivalent new statement was located by searches and full-text comparisons. A classical modular orthogonal-group presentation might imply an equivalent result; no absolute historical priority is asserted.

## Value

**PASS.** The result resolves an infinite, natural one-square family of the smoothness question raised in a current evolution-algebra survey. It explains why field-valued automorphism counts miss a persistent \(n-1\)-dimensional infinitesimal defect and gives uniform closed arithmetic and geometric formulas. Its domain is deliberately restricted to this non-perfect rank-one nilpotent family, not all evolution algebras.

## Scientific limitations

The characteristic-two finite-field counts measure rational points, not scheme length; earlier bilinear-form techniques are foundational. Potential stronger implicit coverage in classical characteristic-two isometry-group literature remains a residual risk. Independent validation is not yet available.
