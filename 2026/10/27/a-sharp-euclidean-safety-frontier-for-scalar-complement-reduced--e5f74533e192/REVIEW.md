## Correctness — PASS
The claim is a fixed-operator statement about a real SPD star matrix. The Schur complement gives \(k^2<ab\). Orthogonal reduction yields one coupled two-by-two mode and uncoupled identical modes. The two eigenvalues give the spectral interval and unique optimum, while positive definiteness of the explicitly calculated Gram deficit gives the entire Euclidean-safe interval and its exclusion endpoints. Transposition transfers the induced-norm result to the residual. The large-transient family has an exact two-step matrix identity. The symbolic rational checker passes 12,756 determinant identities; a seeded 14,000-case numerical test supports the algebra. Numerical samples are not used as proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The Type-II scalar complement is not an arbitrary slice: it is precisely a tunable parameter in a newly proposed reduced-basis preconditioner. The theorem determines exactly when a uniformly decreasing Euclidean error or true residual is possible, distinguishes this from asymptotic convergence, and quantifies how spectral-optimal scaling can fail. The three regimes yield operationally different choices. The family with a fixed spectral contraction factor and unbounded first-step amplification is a substantial, exact boundary phenomenon rather than a minor improvement in a numeric bound. The result is dimension-independent within the natural isotropic complement model and does not claim a general guarantee for all reduced spaces.
