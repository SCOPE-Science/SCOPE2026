# Scientific assessment of the finite-perturbation laws

**Correctness.** The two scalar balance identities give the stated small positive center responses with nonzero coefficients. Since each response is smaller than the leaf amplitude in order, the active arm yields exactly twice the center response divided by that amplitude as the amplification deficit.

**Coverage.** The global sharp star Lipschitz modulus and its nonattainment are consequences of the positive-weight star classification (`169c7e389bc1`). Its proof gives a superquadratic tangent witness and a subquadratic two-parameter family at regular centers. The claim here is confined to the two higher-order deficit laws at one fixed base datum. The distinction is mathematical, not a claim about historical priority. An equivalent singular-location calculation in other literature is possible.

**Value and limitations.** The exact coefficients and unequal power laws tell how quickly the sharp modulus is nearly realized at finite perturbation amplitude, including the dependence on the number of tied leaves in the subquadratic law. No optimality over arbitrary perturbation paths, unequal arm claims, or uniformity as p tends to 2 is asserted. Finite checker evaluations are corroborative only.
