A displayed scalar proof establishes the requisite spectral cosine bound. Rank is characterized by the degree-two polynomial satisfied on active eigenvalues. **Originality — PASS with residual historical risk.** The primary 2026 extended-Krylov paper studies split/mixed channels and loss-of-orthogonality ratios, not this first-block minimax conditioning or its unattained three-point limit. Classical Kantorovich and Bauer–Householder inequalities imply the *nonstrict spectral-cosine ingredient*; that ingredient is expressly credited and excluded from the novelty claim. The new proposition adds the projected strictness, sharp constrained realization, and two-mode/three-mode boundary. Known block Gram–Schmidt conditioning results do not provide those specializations in the material inspected. Searches of mathematical finding records, the first manuscript and related literature found no claim that directly covers this entire proposition; unindexed or inaccessible earlier equivalents are a genuine risk.

**Value — PASS.** Split-channel extended Krylov constructs both \(Ab\) and \(A^{-1}b\) after removing the starting vector. The theorem gives the exact best possible conditioning of the corresponding normalized Gram–Schmidt input at any prescribed spectral condition number, a concrete barrier relevant to block orthogonalization and rank decisions. It separates genuine two-mode breakdown from arbitrarily sharp but nonzero three-mode conditioning, rather than offering only a generic upper bound or an arbitrary numerical example.

The sharp extremal input approaches an eigenvector, at which the normalized projected block ceases to exist. General Kantorovich theory is essential prior art and could entail related specializations.
