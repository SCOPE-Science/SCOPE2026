# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** For an explicitly stationary symmetric two-state sampler and two strictly convex scalar quadratic components, conditioning on the current sample produces exact finite-dimensional first- and second-moment recursions. Their coefficient maps are contractive in column \(\ell^1\) norm, so stationary limits exist uniquely. Solving those systems yields an exact rational stationary risk. Its factorization and derivative give all strict intervals, boundary ties, and the unique maximum; exact-rational path enumeration independently corroborates the recursions. The robustness statement follows from continuous dependence of nonsingular moment systems on parameters, not from finite experimentation.

**Originality: PASS (qualified).** The published MC-SGD analysis in Even (arXiv:2302.14428) gives general convergence rates under Markov sampling, not this stationary risk comparison. Beznosikov et al. (arXiv:2305.15938) derive optimization bounds, including two-state lower-bound models, without the present exact risk phase for vanilla MC-SGD. Kühn and Rosenow (arXiv:2306.05300) treat anti-correlations generated by epoch reshuffling under a different noise process. The contribution is a fully specified robust risk reversal and exact correlation threshold, not the known moment-recursion method or the general value of correlated-noise analysis. There is residual risk of a logically covering identity in the extensive Markov-jump autoregression literature or uncatalogued algorithmic examples; the search is not an originality proof.

**Value: PASS.** Markovian gradient sampling is motivated by random-walk optimization, token methods, and correlated data streams. This example gives exact opposing outcomes from *equal-cost* sampling rules: improving Markov mixing toward independence can worsen stationary last-iterate squared error. The exact crossover and open-parameter robustness distinguish a structural counterexample from a one-off rounding artifact. This is a deliberately scoped complete example, not a universal criterion or a claim about averages.

**Scientific limits.** Two components, one scalar, constant step, stationary Markov sampling, exact arithmetic, last-iterate squared error only; a nonzero stationary bias exists away from independence. Closest relevant literature and the residual priority risk are detailed above and in the structured assessment.
