# Scientific review: A sharp mixed-degree spherical-harmonic fourth moment

## Correctness
PASS. Probability normalization is explicit. For real functions, the degree-one vector and degree-two trace-free symmetric matrix have exact sphere moments, with the mixed sixth-degree moment controlled by the sharp traceless eigenvalue inequality. The displayed deficit is an identity with two nonnegative terms; its simultaneous equality conditions force the stated zonal profiles and degree-one energy fraction. For complex functions, the real/imaginary Cauchy–Schwarz reduction preserves the sharp constant and real-analyticity rules out additional genuinely complex equality cases. The 1008 exact checker assertions test independent matrix inputs and zonal moments; they are supplementary, not a replacement for the universal proof.

## Originality
PASS on available evidence. The closest published degree-two-only fourth-moment result does not include degree-one contributions, and its pure-degree bound of \(15/7\) is strictly weaker than \(375/112\). Kwon–Lee (arXiv:1709.09795) concerns single-degree projection estimates; Germain (arXiv:2306.16981) surveys broad spectral-window norms. Their inspected theorems and relevant discussion do not present the exact two-degree constant or all extremizers. Equivalent-language searches include mean-zero spherical quadratics, mixed spectral clusters, kurtosis, and zonal linear-quadratic combinations. The full text of the closest published degree-two record was inaccessible; this remains a priority risk, though its available claim is restricted to pure degree two.

## Value
PASS. Exact constants for low spectral bands quantify nonlinear interaction between separate spherical-harmonic levels. The two-degree band strictly improves on both isolated levels, and the theorem identifies the unique distribution of spectral energy and complete rotational/phase rigidity of the extremizers. The positive two-component deficit is usable as a quantitative certificate of off-optimum profiles.

## Scientific limits
Only the mean-zero degrees one and two on \(S^2\), fourth moments, and complex scalar-valued functions are covered. There is no theorem for adding degree zero, changing dimension, or increasing the bandwidth or exponent. Independent validation and independent priority assessment remain outstanding.
