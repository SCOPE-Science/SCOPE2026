# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The logarithmically normalized Gaussian pulse model is an exact bijection between positive physical parameters and the transformed variables \(A\in\mathbb R\), \(p>0\), \(q>0\). The three-reading inverse reduces without omitted roots to \(\gamma=\kappa p-2ab-b^2/p\) together with \(ap+b>0\). Strict increase for \(\kappa>0\), exact paired positive roots for \(\kappa<0\), and a full positive family on \(\kappa=0\) certify both directions of the classification. The fourth reading gives explicit unique linear recovery of \(p,q\), then of \(A\). The independently reconstructed rational witness and the accompanying exact-rational executable reproduce each required equality. This is a finite-dimensional algebraic proof, not an extrapolation from testing.

**Originality — PASS, with a specified residual risk.** The 2005 El Badia–Ha-Duong–Hamdi inverse-source theorem assumes known transport coefficients and continuous-time monitoring to infer an unknown source location and release history. The 1961 Ogata–Banks study supplies a different semi-infinite forward boundary formulation. The 2014 Han et al. source-inversion study (abstract inspected) and the 2020 Zeunert–Meon full article address monitoring and uncertain source parameters, not jointly unknown mass, drift and diffusivity in a three-point L-design. Therefore those results do not directly imply or supersede the sharp geometric condition. A 2013 study of spatially varying transport coefficients likewise estimates time-dependent source strength/location from monitoring histories, rather than these three constant physical parameters. A broader inverse interpolation theorem might still subsume the algebraic criterion. Database silence is not evidence of novelty; the exact 2014 paper and complete 2013 article were not materially inspected.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Two-station intermittent sampling is a physically motivated sparse monitoring design. The theorem determines, for *all* positive unknown parameter values, whether that design is globally identifiable; it classifies double solutions, critical infinite ambiguity and the exact fourth-sample remedy. This distinction between local recoverability and global uniqueness is relevant before applying likelihood or optimization methods, and the sharp geometric boundary is a nontrivial constraint coupling station placement to sampling times. Its importance is structural and limited to the stated idealized model; no field-operation guarantee is asserted.
