# Review of the paired bond-stiffness theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The operator is a nonnegative rank-two perturbation of the discrete spring Laplacian, with its essential band equal to \([0,4]\). The exact resolvent gives \(B=2q/(1-q)\) and \(C=q^d(1+q)/(1-q)\). The smaller Birman--Schwinger eigenvalue has sharp edge limit \(2\alpha\beta(2d-1)/(\alpha+\beta)\); the larger one diverges. Strict resolvent monotonicity and the absence of decaying free solutions within the band prove the complete count. Monotonicity in the stiffness product proves the allocation theorem at fixed sum. The exact polynomial \(F_d(q)\) gives the quadratic edge coefficient. Fifty-six finite computations independently check formulas and comparisons; they do not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with historical priority risk
Montroll and Potts (1955) developed general defect Green functions and pair-interaction physics. The original papers by Bacon, Dean and Martin (1962), and Poon and Bienenstock (1966), address lattice defects and impurity springs, but the inspected material did not establish this paired bond-strength threshold and optimal budget law. Mannheim (2005) treats cubic-crystal substitutional force-constant changes, Luo (2016) treats a single mass impurity, and the accessible multi-defect analysis by Ramakrishnan and Matlack (2024) excludes interacting proximal defects from its quantitative treatment. Relevant broader results were compared at the level of assumptions and claim implications, not only titles. Full texts of two historical works were inaccessible and could alter priority; no claim of complete literature uniqueness is made.

## Mathematical and physical value — PASS
The physically motivated problem is whether a fixed excess spring-stiffness budget can sustain two independent exponentially localized vibrational branches. An exact budget barrier \(S>2/(2d-1)\), its unique balanced optimizer and a sharp edge-onset coefficient together give complete design information for the two-bond model. A single defect cannot display the two-mode feasibility transition, while arbitrary ad hoc parameter examples do not establish this global result.

## Limits
Only strictly positive stiffenings of two distinct springs in an infinite, undamped, ungrounded nearest-neighbor harmonic chain are covered. A threshold resonance is not a square-summable eigenmode. Independent audit has not been carried out, and historical equivalence cannot be entirely excluded.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
