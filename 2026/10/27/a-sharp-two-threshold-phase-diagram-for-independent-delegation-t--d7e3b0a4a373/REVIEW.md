# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Exact conditioning on the number of independent delegations gives three possible conditional accuracies and a degree-four reliability polynomial. Direct differentiation yields an affine nontrivial derivative factor, with opposite endpoints determined exactly by the two competence thresholds. The proof verifies uniqueness and all equality boundaries, plus minimality among odd one-expert electorates. An independently organized enumeration using exact rational numbers confirms 225 five-voter parameter/rate cases, boundary conditions, the three-voter law and an exact interior witness. No infinite claim is inferred from finite enumeration.

## Originality — PASS, with specific residual risk

The nearest source is Campbell et al. (2022), which already analyzes five-voter single-expert delegation and establishes qualitative gains from selective, incomplete delegation for heterogeneously precise ordinary agents. That qualitative gain is not claimed new. Their stated welfare theorem and five-voter example depend on dispersed individual precision and threshold-on-precision choices; they do not establish the precise two posterior-odds thresholds for four **identically** competent voters using a common signal-independent Bernoulli delegation rate. Kahng et al. (2018/2021) prove distinct global/local delegation guarantees; other inspected work analyzes random delegation at scale, strategic behavior or proxy weights. Published-result searches did not identify an exact covering phase theorem. Equivalent unpublished work, existing Bayesian inference folklore and the asymmetric expert-weight theory mentioned by Campbell et al. remain residual risks. A negative search is not proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact phase diagram answers a concrete welfare-design question for the minimal odd electorate in which the anonymous independent delegation policy can have a strict interior optimal rate. Two explicit posterior-odds thresholds characterize when retaining independent information dominates delegation, when partial pooling strictly improves upon both extremes, and when a single expert should hold the majority. This is a natural calibrated boundary refining existing qualitative studies; it is not a claim of a general social choice breakthrough.

## Scope and closest literature

Only fixed-confidence independent binary signals, one compulsory expert, four identical potential delegators, strict majority and common signal-independent randomized rates. Campbell et al.'s heterogeneous-precision five-voter experiment and local partial-delegation existence theorem are closely related; Kahng et al.'s local mechanisms provide the base model. A more general optimizing mechanism can attain higher accuracy, and no strategic incentive statement follows.
