# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The finite-tree innovation identities and recurrence are independently checked for 6,104 parent-choice configurations under 35 rational attachment-probability sequences. The proof establishes the nonstandard needed step \(Q<\infty\Rightarrow\sum v_j/j^2<\infty\), then uses martingale convergence and Kronecker's lemma for the almost-sure limit. Fatou's lemma forces divergence of scaled variances when \(Q=\infty\). For regularly varying attachment fractions, rigorous recurrence barriers yield the local-innovation constants; weighted Riemann sums and a logarithmic boundary sum yield the global variance constants. Finite experiments cannot replace these arguments.

## Originality — PASS, with risks
The closest work, Gałązka–Wdowicka (arXiv:2610.00614v1, §§5, 6, 10), contains the exact martingale and variance recurrences and assumes \(p_j=O(1/j)\) for its limit theorem; it explicitly asks for an extension. Our criterion is necessary and sufficient for arbitrary positive deterministic weights, and the stretched-exponential phase diagram is outside its proven power-weight regimes. Searches of mathematical research records for equivalent formulations, parameters \(1/2\), \(2/3\), and broad path-length coverage found no direct covering theorem. Other relevant literature on weighted-tree profiles (Sénizergues, arXiv:1904.07115) and fading-memory recursive-tree depth (Mailler–Uribe Bravo, arXiv:1810.02735) was examined at accessible abstract/summary level only; equivalent unstated corollaries remain the main originality risk. Novelty is a qualified assessment, not independent verification.

## Mathematical value — PASS
An explicit open question asks which general deterministic attachment weights support the global linear-scale limit. The theorem gives a complete, computable innovation-energy criterion; its necessity prevents mistaking a sufficient martingale estimate for a classification. The two critical exponents separate changes in individual depth innovations from the onset of globally summable fluctuations. This is structural information for a natural family with rapidly growing cumulative weights, rather than a routine parameter substitution.

## Limitations and closest sources
The theorem does not classify non-square-integrable almost-sure limits, distributional limits under superquadratic variance normalization, or random/adaptive weights. It uses pre-existing exact fluctuation identities; stronger implicit results in other weighted-profile literature remain a documented risk.
