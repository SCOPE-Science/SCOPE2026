# Review of the two-stratum Cartier-rank law

## Correctness
PASS. The normalized Artin–Schreier cover has exactly one pole of order seven, giving genus six and 3-rank zero by established genus and Deuring–Shafarevich formulas. The valuation at infinity provides six independent regular differentials. Explicit Cartier identities show the image rank is three if the fifth-degree coefficient is nonzero and two otherwise; another iterate always has rank one and the third is zero. The finite-field verifier checks all 81 coefficient tuples over the ternary field and all 6561 over the field of nine elements, confirming but not replacing the universal proof.

## Originality
PASS within the inspected literature, with residual duplication risk. Farnell–Pries's main constant-rank theorem requires a pole-order congruence absent here. Zhou gives only necessary restrictions for genus six and a-number four. Pries–Zhu classifies p-rank, which is constant zero throughout this family. A longer preexisting or unindexed computation of this exact special case may exist, and unsuccessful search does not establish absolute priority. The statement is the full coefficientwise degree-seven/characteristic-three one-pole classification, not a claimed new Cartier formalism.

## Value
PASS. This is a complete two-stratum arithmetic invariant classification on a natural genus-six wild cyclic-cover component, not a sporadic parameter specialization. It identifies a genuine codimension-one jump of the a-number and simultaneously determines all higher Cartier image ranks; the constant 3-rank cannot distinguish the strata. No statement is made about full Newton or Ekedahl–Oort types.
