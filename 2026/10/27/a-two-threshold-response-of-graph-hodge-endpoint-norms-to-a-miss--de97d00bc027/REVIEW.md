# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The assertion is an exact pair of all-order induced endpoint norms for Euclidean-orthogonal graph cut/cycle projections. On the zero-sum subspace, the deleted-clique Laplacian has a rank-one inverse; applying it to each of the two surviving edge types gives closed rational column sums and diagonals. Their comparisons factor as \((N-4)/(N(N-2))\) and \((N-6)/(N(N-2))\). A separate tree case handles \(N=3\). Direct grounded-Laplacian rational calculations reproduced every actual projector column for orders 3 through 11 (210 columns), without being relied upon for the infinite statement.

## Originality — PASS, subject to literature limits
The closest inspected primary text, Dilworth–Kutzarova–Ostrovskii (arXiv:2305.12582, 2023), develops cut/cycle projection methods and exact results on discrete tori and Hamming graphs; its Section 2.1 does not state the deleted-clique norm thresholds. Their arXiv:2007.07949 treats diamond/Laakso graphs. Related published research records on complete multipartite symmetric-edge polytopes or clustered metric projection constants concern different invariants and do not imply this specific two-threshold claim. Finite graph results covering at most six vertices cannot establish the global transition at seven. Exact generic transfer-current formulas are acknowledged, not passed off as new. Risk remains from specialized or inaccessible electrical-network results that might already calculate this case.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A one-edge deletion of the maximally symmetric graph is a natural stress test for cut and cycle projection norms. The two invariants react at distinct sharp orders: one increases starting at five vertices, the other only at seven, with explicit norm changes and extremizing-edge localization. This is a complete structural phase classification across an infinite family, rather than a table of small graphs or a numerical gain claimed significant in isolation.

## Scope and reservations
No claim is made for nonorthogonal minimal projections, weighted/multiple deletions, or arbitrary graphs. Finite checks do not replace the algebraic all-order proof. Independent mathematical validation has not been carried out.
