# Scientific review: exact sentinel selection on stars

**Correctness — PASS.** Conditional detection laws for the two star-automorphism classes are reconstructed by independent geometric waits. Their exact difference factors as a strictly positive time-dependent multiplier times \(r-S_k(q)\), with \(r=n-k\), so the all-deadline sign is rigorous. Summing tails yields the expected delay formula; strict monotonicity of \(S_k\) gives sharp switching. Independent exponential convolutions prove the continuous-time result. An independent exact-rational transition simulator passed 5,797 finite checks; the infinite-parameter theorem rests on the analytic argument.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The epidemic-sensor allocation literature establishes generic objectives, conditional detection calculations and submodularity or sensor heuristics. Full primary texts of Batlle et al., both Holme articles and Heavey et al. were inspected for equivalent statements. Relevant indexed findings and accessible prior records were compared by claim, not merely title. No inspected source explicitly establishes the same complete all-horizon star classification or its sharp contrast with continuous infection clocks. First-passage and convolution identities are not claimed new; a broader implicit coverage theorem remains a realistic residual risk.

**Value — PASS.** Uniform-source star detection is a motivated exact stochastic sensor-placement benchmark. The theorem identifies a single optimal configuration for every finite decision deadline, a sharp transmissibility switch at fixed budget, and the absence of the switch under continuous-time transmission. These properties affect the placement choice, rather than changing a normalizing convention or checking a minor example.

**Scientific limitations.** Homogeneous perfect-monitor SI on a star, uniform index case, fixed sensor budget and no recovery. The theorem does not cover heterogeneous graph networks, limited-sensitivity tests, source priors, SIR recovery or adaptive monitoring. Literature-priority certainty is not claimed.

**Closest literature.** Batlle et al., *Adaptive Test Allocation for Outbreak Detection and Tracking in Social Contact Networks* (2020 arXiv version); Holme, *Three faces of node importance in network epidemiology* (2017); Holme, *Objective measures for sentinel surveillance* (2018); Heavey et al., *Provable Sensor Sets for Epidemic Detection over Networks with Minimum Delay* (2022).
