# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
Every nonzero idempotent is uniquely represented by a fixed point of the projective squaring–linear map. The homogeneous fixed-point polynomial is a nonzero cubic, hence has at most three projective roots. For uniform \(M\in\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_q)\), sharp three-transitivity of \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb F_q)\) evaluates all required factorial moments; the only exceptional projective square fibers are pairs \(\{t,-t\}\) in odd characteristic. Inclusion–exclusion yields the exact \(1/3\) law and all four probabilities. Eight exhaustive finite-field examples corroborate, but do not establish, the general argument.

## Originality — PASS, with explicitly limited coverage
The closest recent survey [1] reports an expected idempotent count and a two-dimensional existence criterion. Neither an expectation nor the two-moment information for the same sampling model determines the odd-characteristic full distribution; a third factorial moment is essential. The 2023 classification [2] treats moduli of two-dimensional perfect evolution algebras, not the sampling law, and [3] enumerates isomorphism classes under a different weighting. Indexed mathematical comparisons revealed an existence theorem for idempotent-free examples but no exact random-matrix histogram. Risk remains that equivalent fixed-point counts appear in classical projective-map or cubic-polynomial literature; the most relevant portion of [1] on average idempotents could not be re-opened during this review, although introductory material was read.

## Value — PASS
The theorem settles all four probabilities for a canonical, finite-field family, and the exact \(1/3\) probability is independent of both field size and characteristic. It quantifies how commonly a perfect evolution algebra has no nonzero idempotents—a structural existence issue central to [1]—and distinguishes a genuine odd-characteristic double-root stratum. This information is not recoverable from the previously available mean/variance alone.
