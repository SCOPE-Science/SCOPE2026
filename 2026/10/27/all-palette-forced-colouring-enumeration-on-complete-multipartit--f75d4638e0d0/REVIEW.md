# Scientific review: all-palette forcing on complete multipartite graphs

## Correctness — PASS
A proper completed colouring uses disjoint colour palettes in distinct multipartite parts. Incompletely seeded successful colourings must use the entire palette: otherwise no first force is possible. A part using at least two final colours cannot have an initially uncoloured vertex, because a forcing vertex sees all but one available colour outside its part. Two completely seedless monochromatic parts make two colours absent initially, preventing the first force. Conversely, with all multicoloured parts fully seeded and at most one seedless monochromatic part, forcing finishes in at most two simultaneous rounds. This proves the necessary-and-sufficient classification. The Stirling-number count chooses colour palettes and surjections exactly once, and removes the already-counted fully seeded term. The seed-minimization follows by emptying one monochromatic part and allocating the palette surplus across fully seeded multicoloured parts. Seventeen direct exhaustive coefficient comparisons and four exhaustive structural comparisons passed. Finite enumeration is corroboration, not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with source-access risk
The closest published findings treat the chromatic palette \(\lambda=r\) for arbitrary complete multipartite graphs, the single case \((r,\lambda)=(2,3)\) for complete bipartite graphs, and three-colour paths and cycles. Their exact statements do not entail the present result for every \(\lambda>r\). The earlier chromatic-palette proof relies on all parts being monochromatic, whereas the present regime allows multicoloured parts and proves their full-seeding rigidity. Farr and Morgan's accessible 2024 primary full text introduces the forcing process and the probability polynomial but does not provide the present enumeration. Farr's 2026 initiating paper was confirmed by its arXiv metadata and abstract, but its complete PDF was unavailable; therefore possible stronger coverage in that full text remains the principal historical-priority risk. Neither a result-search miss nor partitioning parameters establishes novelty by itself.

## Value — PASS
The forced-colouring function is a new graph-polynomial statistic with difficult general evaluation. Exact enumeration simultaneously for every complete multipartite part profile and every palette beyond the chromatic number resolves the principal extra-palette dimension left open by the closest explicit family formulas. The sharp classification and two-round bound expose why multicoloured parts differ from chromatic-palette forcing. The minimum-seed subset-sum formula gives a separate natural extremal statistic. The explicit \(K_{2,2,2}\) four-colour polynomial illustrates nontrivial intermediate coefficients not obtained by the previously published \(\lambda=r\) formula.
