# An infinite square-zero center in the Reynolds operator ring of the dual numbers

## Finding
Let \(k\) be any field, \(R=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\), and \(P(a+b\varepsilon)=a\). For the classical Reynolds operator of weight \(-1\), the associated operator ring \(\mathcal U_{R,P}\) has center
\[
Z(\mathcal U_{R,P})=k\cdot1\oplus\bigoplus_{m\geq1}k\cdot(\varepsilon Q^m\varepsilon).
\]
The second summand is a nonzero, countably infinite-dimensional square-zero ideal annihilated on both sides by the two algebra generators \(Q\) and \(\varepsilon\). In particular, the center is non-Noetherian, although the operator ring is a finitely presented algebra on two generators.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field \(k\) is arbitrary, including characteristics \(2\) and \(3\). The underlying Reynolds algebra is the unital two-dimensional dual-number algebra \(R=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\), with the unital idempotent projection \(P(a+b\varepsilon)=a\) and classical weight \(-1\). The operator ring follows the defining presentation of He–Peng–Zhang–Zhang (2026): adjoining an operator symbol \(Q\) to \(R\), with relations \(P(r)Q=QP(r)+Q(r-P(r))Q\) for every \(r\in R\). We compute the algebraic (finite-sum) center, without completing the ring.

## Proof
**Presentation.** The projection \(P\) is an averaging operator and hence a Reynolds operator of classical weight \(-1\): indeed, for \(a,b,c,d\in k\), the Reynolds identity reduces to \(ac=ac+ac-ac\). Substituting \(r=a+b\varepsilon\) into the operator-ring relation yields just \(Q\varepsilon Q=0\), since scalar terms commute with \(Q\). Therefore
\[
\mathcal U_{R,P}\cong k\langle Q,\varepsilon\rangle/(\varepsilon^2,Q\varepsilon Q).
\]
**Monomial normal forms.** The defining ideal is generated by two monomials. Hence distinct words avoiding both forbidden contiguous subwords \(\varepsilon\varepsilon\) and \(Q\varepsilon Q\) are a \(k\)-basis: the ideal has as basis precisely the words containing at least one forbidden subword. The normal words are exactly
\[
Q^a\ (a\geq0),\quad Q^a\varepsilon\ (a\geq0),\quad \varepsilon Q^a\ (a\geq1),\quad \varepsilon Q^a\varepsilon\ (a\geq1).
\]
Indeed, at most two occurrences of \(\varepsilon\) are possible: three distinct occurrences would make the middle one bordered by \(Q\)'s. Two occurrences force the word to start and end with \(\varepsilon\), separated by a positive power of \(Q\). A single \(\varepsilon\) cannot have positive powers of \(Q\) on both sides.

**Centralizer of \(Q\).** Each \(z_m=\varepsilon Q^m\varepsilon\), \(m\geq1\), is annihilated on both sides by \(Q\) and \(\varepsilon\). Every polynomial in \(Q\) commutes with \(Q\). Conversely, consider a finite linear combination in the displayed normal basis. For \(a\geq1\),
\[
[Q,Q^a\varepsilon]=Q^{a+1}\varepsilon,\qquad [Q,\varepsilon Q^a]=-\varepsilon Q^{a+1},\qquad [Q,\varepsilon]=Q\varepsilon-\varepsilon Q.
\]
All resulting normal words are independent. Hence every coefficient of a one-\(\varepsilon\) normal word must vanish if the element commutes with \(Q\). The centralizer of \(Q\) is therefore \(k[Q]\oplus\bigoplus_{m\geq1}kz_m\).

**Centralizer of \(\varepsilon\) and the center.** The elements \(z_m\) also commute with \(\varepsilon\). If \(f(Q)\in k[Q]\) commutes with \(\varepsilon\), then
\[
[f(Q),\varepsilon]=\sum_{a\geq1}c_a(Q^a\varepsilon-\varepsilon Q^a)=0.
\]
Independence of the two families of normal words implies \(c_a=0\) for every \(a\geq1\). Therefore \(f\) is constant. Since \(Q,\varepsilon\) generate the ring, this gives the asserted center.

Finally, \(z_mz_n=0\) because the product contains \(\varepsilon^2\), and \(z_m\neq0\) are linearly independent normal words for all \(m\geq1\). The center's ideal \(\bigoplus_{m\geq1}kz_m\) is not finitely generated as a center-module: every generator is supported on finitely many basis vectors, and multiplying by central elements only rescales these vectors. The ascending chain generated by the first \(n\) basis vectors never stabilizes. Thus the center is non-Noetherian.

## Verification
The standalone script `verify.py` checks the forbidden-word normal-form criterion up to word length nine, checks all listed central candidates through degree nine, and reports `VERIFY_OK`. The unrestricted theorem is proved by the word-basis argument, not by the finite check.

## Relationship to prior work
He, Peng, Zhang and Zhang, *Modules and operator rings of Reynolds algebras* (first submitted 15 October 2026), construct \(\mathcal U_{R,P}\) generally and determine its positive-degree pieces for idempotent Reynolds operators fixing the identity. Their presentation and structural theorem are the starting point, not new claims here. The added statement is the exact center and its infinite-dimensional square-zero behavior in the natural dual-number projection example; the inspected text has no center classification. Standard monomial-ideal methods underlie the proof.

## Limitations
This is an exact calculation for one naturally defined operator ring, not a classification of all Reynolds operator rings or a statement about their module categories in general. Possible equivalent coverage in unindexed monomial-algebra literature remains a priority risk. No independent mathematical audit has been conducted.
