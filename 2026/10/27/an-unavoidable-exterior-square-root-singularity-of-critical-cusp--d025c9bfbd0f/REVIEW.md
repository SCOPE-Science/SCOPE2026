# Review: unavoidable exterior inverse-square-root singularity

## Correctness — PASS
The inverse-branch radical and transfer weight follow by solving the quadratic inverse equation and differentiating. Both inverse branches are strict contractions in an analytic neighborhood of the closed target interval. Their compact weighted-composition operator on bounded holomorphic functions has spectral radius exactly one, by intertwining with the spectral gap and convergence of iterates to the unique nonzero Sobolev invariant density established in arXiv:2610.23180v1. A unit eigenvector then gives the holomorphic density. Full-branch cylinders force strict positivity in the open interval. Analytic continuation of the transfer identity to just outside the interval produces a nonzero coefficient times an inverse square root at the first discriminant collision. The argument proves a nonremovable exterior branch point, irrational-to-meromorphic exponent, and Taylor-radius upper bound.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence
The closest 2026 critical-cusp source proves Sobolev density regularity and analyticity with respect to a Bernoulli mixing parameter, whereas the present result concerns holomorphic regularity in the spatial variable and a precise forced exterior singularity. The 2025 related cusp paper concerns Sobolev linear response; the 2026 August predecessor concerns a different cusp exponent and was only accessible by abstract. Searches for the precise family, radical branch point, nonrationality, spatial analyticity, and analytic-continuation aliases yielded no covering published-finding corpus record or inspected published theorem. This is best-knowledge priority, not certified uniqueness or publication novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The singularity is an exact obstruction to extending the invariant density holomorphically, with an explicit location for a natural full continuum of critically singular yet uniformly expanding maps. It explains why the spectral-gap regularity does not imply global entire/rational formulas, gives an explicit rigorous Taylor convergence-radius ceiling, and separates spatial regularity from parameter response. This boundary invariant is useful when approximating stationary densities by high-order Taylor expansions.

## Closest literature and limitations
Oltiboev–Vafoyev, arXiv:2610.23180v1, Sections 3–6, supplies the map and Sobolev inputs but not the stated exterior singularity. Oltiboev, arXiv:2505.17462v1, supplies Sobolev cusp response context. The leading coefficient is not an evaluated elementary constant, and the limit parameter θ=2 is excluded. Generic analytic transfer-operator methodology predates this work.
