# Scientific review: Antipodal absorption resonances on Möbius quantum ladders

## Correctness — PASS

The Fourier spectrum \(2\cos(\pi k/n)+(-1)^k\) follows from the circulant adjacency operator. The cyclotomic sign lemma, with proof by Galois conjugates, primitive-root trace and algebraic norm, forces all cross-parity coincidences into two explicit size congruence classes. In each class the Fourier multiplicities and the spectral-projection overlap prove the survival fraction. All bright modes decay for positive loss because their generator eigenvalues have strictly negative imaginary part. The standalone verifier passed 78 finite numerical spectral comparisons, eight exact rational Krylov rank comparisons, and ten independent dissipative spectral comparisons. These checks corroborate, rather than substitute for, the all-size proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources

The nearest inspected sources are Solenov–Fedichkin (2005), Krapivsky–Luck–Mallick (2013), and Razzoli–Paris–Bordone (2020). The first treats quantum transport and decoherence on ordinary rings, the second gives surviving states for trapped ordinary rings, and the third establishes the general spectral-subspace framework on selected graph families. None of the relevant inspected sections establishes this Möbius-ladder parity-collision or rung-source absorption law. Searches for the circulant alias, Möbius/ladder names, trapping language, Fourier eigenvalue collisions and stronger rank classifications did not expose a covering statement. Absence from search is not a novelty proof; an unknown stronger spectral-observability result remains a genuine priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result completely classifies a nontrivial source-specific absorbing observable on an infinite family of nonorientably twisted cubic graphs. It exhibits rare arithmetic failures of certain absorption even when the dark subspace is nearly half the total state space; the Möbius twist alters interference relative to a simple ring. The nontrivial cyclotomic obstruction and exact spectral weights go beyond a routine normalization or one finite graph table.

## Restrictions and verification status

The theorem covers one finite unweighted graph family, a single positive loss, adjacency dynamics and one special source. It is not a claim about finite-time absorption, all trap placements or altered hopping. Historical-priority checks were broad but not exhaustive; independent verification of science remains outstanding.
