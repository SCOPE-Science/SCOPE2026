# Arithmetic dark-state resonance for a lossy junction of two quantum cycles

## Finding
Let \(G_{m,n}=C_m\vee C_n\) be the simple graph obtained by identifying exactly one vertex \(v\) of two cycles with lengths \(m,n\ge3\). Write \(d=\gcd(m,n)\), and let \(H\) be either its adjacency matrix or its combinatorial Laplacian. For any positive sink strength \(\gamma>0\), evolve a single excitation with the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian \(H_\gamma=H-i\gamma|v\rangle\langle v|\). The dark subspace \(\mathcal D\) consists of vectors that never lose norm. Its dimension is
\[
\dim\mathcal D=\left\lfloor\frac{m-1}{2}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor+\chi\left\lfloor\frac d2\right\rfloor,
\qquad \chi=\mathbf1_{\{m/d\ \mathrm{odd},\ n/d\ \mathrm{odd}\}}.
\]
For an initial unit excitation at the \(j\)-th vertex of the first cycle, numbered \(1\le j<m\) after the junction, the asymptotic survival probability is exactly
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}\|e^{-itH_\gamma}e_j\|^2=
\frac2m\sum_{\substack{2\le k<m\\k\ \mathrm{even}}}\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi k j}{m}\right)
+\frac{2\chi}{m+n}\sum_{\substack{1\le r<d\\r\ \mathrm{odd}}}\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi rj}{d}\right).
\]
For a vertex on the second cycle interchange \(m,n\). At the junction the survival probability is zero. An incoherent uniform initial vertex has survival probability \(\dim\mathcal D/(m+n-1)\).

In particular, adjoining the second loop **strictly increases** a localized initial state's survival compared with the isolated first cycle if and only if \(m/d\) and \(n/d\) are odd, \(d\ge2\), and \(d\nmid j\). This arithmetic resonance is independent of \(\gamma\) and occurs for both choices of \(H\). For two triangles joined at one vertex, the first-cycle nonjunction survival increases exactly from \(1/2\) to \(3/4\).

## Assumptions and scope
All edges are simple, undirected, and have unit weight. The cycles have distinct vertex sets except for their unique common hub. The Hamiltonian is exactly the real symmetric adjacency matrix \(A\) or combinatorial Laplacian \(L=D-A\), with unitary convention \(e^{-itH}\) and absorbing perturbation \(-i\gamma|v\rangle\langle v|\). Every statement holds for integers \(m,n\ge3\), all real \(\gamma>0\), and every indicated initial site; no weak-loss or large-graph limit is taken. Survival is the nonnormalised squared Hilbert-space norm at time \(t\).

## Proof
**1. Lossless invariant sector.** Along the dissipative evolution \(\psi(t)=e^{-itH_\gamma}\psi(0)\),
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\|\psi(t)\|^2=-2\gamma|\langle v,\psi(t)\rangle|^2.
\]
The subspace \(\mathcal D\) spanned by all eigenvectors of \(H\) vanishing at \(v\) is invariant under \(H\) and annihilated by the loss projector; because \(H\) is self-adjoint, its orthogonal complement is also invariant under both terms. An eigenvector of \(H_\gamma\) with a real eigenvalue has zero hub amplitude by the imaginary part of its Rayleigh quotient, and is therefore an eigenvector of \(H\) in \(\mathcal D\). On \(\mathcal D^\perp\), every eigenvalue of \(H_\gamma\) has strictly negative imaginary part, so its finite-dimensional semigroup tends to zero (possible Jordan-polynomial factors are dominated by exponential decay). Consequently
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\psi(t)\|^2=\|P_{\mathcal D}\psi(0)\|^2.
\]
In particular the limit is independent of \(\gamma>0\).

**2. Exact Dirichlet eigenspaces.** Delete vertex \(v\). The remaining graph is the disjoint union of paths of lengths \(m-1\) and \(n-1\). On a path cut from a cycle of length \(q\), the normalized Dirichlet modes are
\[
\phi_{q,k}(j)=\sqrt{\frac2q}\sin\left(\frac{\pi k j}{q}\right),\quad 1\le k<q,\quad1\le j<q.
\]
Their eigenvalues are \(2\cos(\pi k/q)\) for the adjacency Hamiltonian and \(2-2\cos(\pi k/q)\) for the Laplacian. Each spectrum is strictly monotone in \(k/q\) on \((0,1)\). Extending the mode by zero at the hub is a full-graph eigenvector precisely when its two endpoint contributions cancel:
\[
\phi_{q,k}(1)+\phi_{q,k}(q-1)=\sqrt{\frac2q}\sin\left(\frac{\pi k}{q}\right)\bigl(1+(-1)^{k+1}\bigr)=0.
\]
Therefore every even \(k\) independently produces one dark state confined to its own loop, accounting for the first two dimension terms.

**3. Cross-loop cancellation.** An odd \(k\) on the first path can share its Dirichlet eigenvalue with an odd \(\ell\) on the second precisely if \(k/m=\ell/n\). Writing \(m=dm'\) and \(n=dn'\) with \(\gcd(m',n')=1\), all coincidences have \(k=m'r\), \(\ell=n'r\), and \(1\le r<d\). Both indices are odd exactly when \(m',n',r\) are all odd. Each such two-dimensional common eigenspace has a one-dimensional hub-cancellation kernel, generated by the normalized vector
\[
\Psi_r=\sqrt{\frac m{m+n}}\,\phi_{m,m'r}\ \oplus\
\left(-\sqrt{\frac n{m+n}}\,\phi_{n,n'r}\right),
\]
with zero hub component; equality of the endpoint sines follows from \(k/m=\ell/n=r/d\). Distinct values of \(r\) give distinct eigenvalues. No further dark vectors exist: at any fixed Dirichlet eigenvalue the two paths contribute at most one mode each, and the hub equation is the only remaining condition. Even-index modes have zero hub contribution individually; in the odd/odd case exactly one linear combination cancels. There are \(\lfloor d/2\rfloor\) odd integers in \(1,\ldots,d-1\). This proves the dimension formula.

**4. Localized survival and strictness.** All listed dark modes are orthonormal (either supported on different eigenvalues or different loops). Each pure even mode contributes \(2\sin^2(\pi kj/m)/m\) to the diagonal entry \(\langle e_j,P_\mathcal D e_j\rangle\). On the first loop each normalized cross-loop \(\Psi_r\) has amplitude \(\sqrt{2/(m+n)}\sin(\pi rj/d)\), yielding the second sum. The same reasoning gives the formula on the other loop. Relative to an isolated lossy \(C_m\), the difference is this nonnegative cross-loop sum. If the cross-loop condition fails it vanishes; otherwise it is strictly positive exactly when \(d\nmid j\), since \(r=1\) is present and all summands are nonnegative. Finally, \(\operatorname{tr}P_\mathcal D=\dim\mathcal D\) proves the uniform-mixture statement. For \(m=n=3\) and \(j=1\), the pure even contribution is \(1/2\) and the cross-loop contribution \(1/4\).

## Relationship to prior work
Anishchenko, Blumen and Mülken studied quantum-walk survival and trapping on random two-dimensional structures with absorbing sites, motivating a precise topological trapping question; their examples do not give this figure-eight arithmetic law. He and van Dam established spectral characterization of rose graphs; their full-text arguments concern global graph isospectrality, not the dark projection or local survival probability of a hub absorber. Thiel and coauthors established general spectral relationships between dark and bright states for stroboscopic first-detection protocols; we use continuous imaginary-potential absorption instead and classify a specified graph family. Work on Grover-coin walks with sinks also addresses trapping in a different (coined discrete-time) dynamics. The arithmetic formula is an explicit application of familiar finite-dimensional spectral techniques, not a claim that the general bright/dark principle itself is new.

## Limitations
This is a finite, single-particle, noninteracting result for one absorbing junction, with uniform edge weights and exactly two cycles. It does not classify general cactus graphs, arbitrary sinks or edge weights, many-particle transport, mean conditional absorption time, or stroboscopic detection at resonant sampling intervals. Priority with respect to potentially unlocated graph-observability results remains an originality risk. Independent scientific validation has not been performed.
