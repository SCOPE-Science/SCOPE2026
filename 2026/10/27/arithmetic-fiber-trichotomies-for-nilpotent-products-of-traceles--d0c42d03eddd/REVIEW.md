# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The domain is \(V_q=\{A\in M_2(\mathbb F_q):\operatorname{tr}A=0\}\) for every prime-power \(q\). Nilpotency of the product requires both vanishing trace and determinant; the determinant factors, permitting exact inclusion–exclusion of two trace-hyperplane slices over the size-\(q^2\) nilpotent cone. Direct conjugation to \(E_{12}\) proves the critical nonzero-cone incidence size \(q\). Independent invertible-fiber calculations separate odd-characteristic split/nonsplit types and even-characteristic scalar/nonscalar types. The separate zero-product count follows from the right annihilator of each rank stratum. Exhaustive program tests in six fields, including extensions, corroborate all formulas.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks
The motivating paper of Sarma–Subedi (arXiv:2610.07892, full HTML reviewed) treats the whole finite ring and triangular subrings, not both traceless factors; the restriction changes the admissible matrix pairs, so its general estimates do not imply the conditional table. Lopatin (arXiv:2310.00477, complete HTML sections 1 and 3 inspected) studies pairs of individually nilpotent matrices up to conjugacy, not the full census with invertible traceless factors. Zaid–Sarmin–Khasraw (J. Indones. Math. Soc. 2025, PDF inspected) study zero products of ordinary matrix rings rather than this conditioned nilpotency event. Specific related mathematical-record and literature searches found no equivalent table or probability. Standard quadratic-form methods might independently yield the formulas, and unindexed prior derivations remain possible; this review does not certify uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
The trace-zero matrix space is a canonical three-dimensional Lie-algebra vector space inside a matrix ring; its matrix multiplication is nonclosed, so finite-ring nilpotent-product probabilities do not transfer automatically. The full conditional law describes a characteristic-dependent singularity and the surprisingly characteristic-independent total, while exactly separating zero and nonzero square-zero products. These are concrete arithmetic invariants, not arbitrary parameter substitutions, and directly test how ring-level random-product laws change under a natural linear constraint.

## Scientific boundaries
The exact formulas are proved for all finite fields, but their novelty was assessed against inspected accessible sources rather than all possible sources. The bundled tests do not replace the proof. No claim is made about higher matrix dimensions.
