# Self-review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The signed evaluation measure has explicitly computed weights on all distinct Bernstein nodes, giving an exact pointwise variation norm and attainable finite extremizers. Binomial-to-Poisson convergence proves the boundary profile; uniform local central limits and controlled tails prove the Gaussian bulk constant. A rigorously enclosed exponential witness proves the strict separation, and a compactness argument yields the global operator-norm limit and boundary localization. The standalone checker supplements rather than proves the infinite statements.

## Originality
**PASS, subject to documented risk.** Butzer (1953, DOI 10.4153/CJM-1953-063-7) explicitly uses the same accelerated Bernstein operator, but its inspected theorem and proof address approximation orders rather than the exact \(C[0,1]\) operator norm. Lu–Zhang (arXiv:1007.1044, publicly posted 2010-07-07) analyze modified weighted combinations for singular functions, with direct/inverse approximation results rather than this sharp unweighted stability asymptotic. The 2022 two-Bernstein publication (DOI 10.3934/mfc.2022061) was accessible only by abstract; mathematically equivalent unindexed evaluations remain possible. Search failure is not proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** This identifies a strict, nontrivial boundary-vs-interior stability gap for an important quadratic-reproducing extrapolation operator. It reduces the exact limiting sharp norm to a transparent Poisson variation problem and locates extremizers in a shrinking boundary layer. The pointwise and asymptotic conclusions materially improve on the trivial norm bound of three.

## Limitations
The scalar maximizer of the Poisson profile is not established unique and no elementary exact numerical value is claimed. The relevant 2022 paper was not fully inspected. The included finite checks do not prove the asymptotic steps.
