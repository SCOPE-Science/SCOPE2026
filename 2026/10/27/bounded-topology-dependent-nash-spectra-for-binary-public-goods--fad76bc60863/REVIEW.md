# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The strict parity best-response rule is exactly the system \((I+A)x=\mathbf1\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) on these maximum-degree-three graphs. Two-column elimination yields a deterministic order-two recurrence whose fourth iterate is the identity. The proof explicitly evaluates all sixteen possible ordered initial pairs under each open, cylindrical, and Möbius boundary condition, producing each residue-class count. The standalone checker separately generates graphs and exhaustively tests literal action profiles for all permitted sizes through eight rungs; the program also checks the recurrence through one hundred rungs. The finite tests are corroboration, not a substitute for the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with bibliographic risk.** Gilboa–Nisan (2022), pages 2–3, and Klimm–Stahlberg (2023), pages 1–2, already explain the general mod-two linear-system reduction and polynomial-time solvability of parity responses. That earlier result is expressly excluded from the novelty claim. The work of Sánchez Muñoz–Mc Gettrick (2021), Sections 1–2, concerns the same two underlying ladder graph geometries but a *different additive anti-coordination game* and exponential rather than bounded counts; it cannot imply these counts for the parity public-goods game. No inspected paper or searchable closely related record states the three exact mod-four spectra or their common four-periodic construction. The existing general parity algorithm makes these formulas computable on each individual graph but does not directly give the uniform three-family classification. Equivalent unpublished or differently indexed enumerations remain possible.

**Value — PASS.** This is an exact complete classification across three standard infinite graph families, not a finite sample or arbitrary chosen graph size. The universal four-rung periodicity proves the striking absence of exponential equilibrium proliferation despite linear growth in the number of players, and shows how one untwisted or twisted pair of end edges changes the periodic equilibrium counts. Such model-dependent contrasts are motivated by published work reporting exponential equilibrium counts on the same ladder geometries for other neighbor interactions. The known parity reduction alone does not describe those graph-specific sensitivities without additional analysis.

**Scientific limitations.** This concerns homogeneous strict response and degree at most three, full labeled-profile counts, and static pure Nash equilibria only. The general parity-solvability fact is not independently novel; no dynamic or welfare statement follows. Independent checking by another researcher has not been performed.
