# Scientific review

## Correctness
The derivation conditions were reconstructed directly from the Leibniz equations. Block separation is valid for every number of parts at least two. In characteristic two the blocks are exactly symmetric matrices annihilating the all-ones vector. Under the stated odd-characteristic nondivisibility assumptions they are exactly alternating matrices annihilating that vector. The rank counts use the unique radical and independently established nondegenerate-form enumerations. Summable uniform corank bounds prove total-variation limits. The standalone finite-field checker independently reconstructs the derivation equations and matches all tested exact histograms. Scientific limitation: the excluded odd-characteristic resonance cases are not covered.

## Originality
The closest primary paper, Reis and Cadavid, arXiv:2006.11934, explicitly establishes the all-graph derivation-space characterization, including the twin-block criterion. The 2018 paper arXiv:1807.02493 treats related characteristic-zero structure. Classical symmetric and alternating matrix rank counts are also pre-existing. The assessed contribution is the new explicitly rank-refined complete-multipartite specialization and joint asymptotic corank law, not a claim that the derivation-space parametrization or finite-field form counts are new. None of the inspected source statements provides the asserted characteristic-dependent rank generating functions and corank limits. A residual risk is unlocated equivalent rank-enumeration work and the fact that the result uses strong existing structural input.

## Value
The distribution of ranks of all algebra derivations is a natural structural census. The result covers arbitrarily many multipartite graph classes and all sizes in characteristic two, supplies all rank multiplicities without enumerating operators, and detects a sharp parity change and two different families of universal limiting corank laws. This is a complete infinite-family statistic, not a small numerical improvement or a single ad hoc finite instance.
