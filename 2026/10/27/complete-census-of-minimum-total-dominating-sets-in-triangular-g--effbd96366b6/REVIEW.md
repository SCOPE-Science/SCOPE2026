# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is proved by a necessary-and-sufficient translation from total domination in \(J(n,2)\) to complete-graph edge sets with at most one isolated vertex and no isolated edge component. A sharp component/edge inequality and an exhaustive three-residue equality analysis force forests with precisely the listed component orders. Prüfer enumeration then gives the factorial expressions without duplicate counting. Direct exhaustive verification evaluated every candidate at the minimum size for \(4\le n\le10\), and a separately coded forest enumerator checked every \(4\le n\le100\). The infinite conclusion rests on the written arguments, not those experiments.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk

Cornet–Dravec–Torres (arXiv:2610.04675v1, Theorem 14, pp. 11–12) establish the minimum cardinality, not the exact census or equality-case classification in their relevant theorem and proof. Pan–Yang–Li–Xu (arXiv:1910.02431v2) supply the equivalent total-edge-domination setting and study complexity and trees. Literature checks considered the aliases triangular graph, the line graph of a complete graph, and total edge domination of complete graphs, as well as database results for domination enumerators. No stronger result implying the census was located. This search is not proof of priority; older literature indexed under another name may exist.

## Value — PASS

The work determines every optimum solution and its multiplicity for a natural infinite family whose optimum size was recently determined. It identifies a mod-three structural phase distinction, including a five-vertex-tree versus two-four-vertex-tree exception when the underlying order is divisible by three, and quantifies an asymptotically overwhelming isolated-vertex phenomenon. These data are not implied by the known cardinality theorem and constitute a natural extremal-configuration classification, rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest source is arXiv:2610.04675v1, Theorem 14. The earlier arXiv:1910.02431v2 supplies equivalent terminology and general context. Scope is \(J(n,2)\) for \(n\ge4\) and minimum sets only. Historical-priority checking and independent mathematical assessment are incomplete.
