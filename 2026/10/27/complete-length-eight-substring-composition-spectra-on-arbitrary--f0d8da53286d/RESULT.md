# Complete length-eight substring-composition spectra on arbitrary alphabets

## Finding

Let \(\Sigma\) be a finite alphabet of cardinality \(q\ge 2\). For a word \(w\in\Sigma^8\), let \(\mathcal C(w)\) be the **multiset**, with multiplicities, of all \(36\) Parikh vectors of nonempty contiguous factors of \(w\). The equivalence fiber of \(w\) consists of all words \(v\in\Sigma^8\) satisfying \(\mathcal C(v)=\mathcal C(w)\).

All fibers have cardinality \(1\), \(2\), or \(4\). Exactly \(q^4\) fibers have cardinality \(1\), exactly \(\binom{q}{2}^{2}\) have cardinality \(4\), and exactly
\[
\frac{q^8-q^4-4\binom{q}{2}^{2}}{2}
\]
have cardinality \(2\). Thus the number of distinguishable observations, equivalently the maximum cardinality of a zero-error code under this exact observation channel, is
\[
\boxed{N_q(8)=\frac{q^8+q^4}{2}-\binom{q}{2}^{2}.}
\]

The four-word fibers are **precisely** the following classes: choose two unordered two-element subsets \(\{a,b\}\) and \(\{c,d\}\) of \(\Sigma\), which may intersect, and take the four words
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathtt{abcabdab},\quad \mathtt{abdabcab},\\
\mathtt{bacbadba},\quad \mathtt{badbacba}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Equivalently these are the reversal/interleaving class generated by \(ab\,c\,ab\,d\,ab\). The letters within each unordered pair are distinct, but the two pairs need not be disjoint. Every other fiber consists of a word and its reversal, with one member if the word is palindromic.

For example, the observation counts are \(135\) for \(q=2\), \(3312\) for \(q=3\), and \(32860\) for \(q=4\). For every finite alphabet, length eight is the first blocklength at which distinct nonreversal words have the same complete composition observation.

## Assumptions and scope

Alphabets are labeled and arbitrary except that \(q\) is a finite integer at least two. A Parikh vector specifies each symbol's multiplicity in a contiguous, nonempty factor; different factors giving the same vector are counted repeatedly. Equality of complete observations includes the length-eight factor, and hence forces equality of the source words' overall symbol multiplicities. Reversal is not identified at the input level; each word is counted separately. The code-size statement assumes a noiseless, exact observation of all such vectors and one representative per equivalence fiber.

## Proof

**1. Known constructions yield the stated four-element classes.** For commuting indeterminates \(X_s\), let \(P_w=\sum_{j=0}^{|w|}\prod_{i=1}^j X_{w_i}\) be the prefix monomial polynomial. In \(P_w(X)P_w(X^{-1})\), the coefficients of monomials with nonnegative exponents (excluding the constant diagonal term) count the nonempty substring compositions. This is the usual prefix-product representation of the composition spectrum.

For \(s=ab\) put \(L=X_aX_b\) and \(S=1+X_a+L\). The two interleavings \(w=ab\,c\,ab\,d\,ab\) and \(w'=ab\,d\,ab\,c\,ab\) satisfy
\[
P_w=S(1+LX_c+L^2X_cX_d),\qquad
P_{w'}=S(1+LX_d+L^2X_cX_d).
\]
The bracketed factors are reciprocal up to the same monomial \(L^2X_cX_d\). They have identical products with their Laurent reciprocals, so \(\mathcal C(w)=\mathcal C(w')\). Reversal also preserves every composition spectrum. If \(a\ne b\) and \(c\ne d\), these operations yield four distinct words. This construction and its general interleaving principle appear in Acharya et al.; no new priority is claimed for interleaving itself.

**2. An exhaustive, alphabet-independent finite classification.** Every eight-letter word has at most eight distinct symbols. Moreover, the complete spectrum determines the total number of appearances of every symbol (from the unique length-eight vector). Any two words sharing an observation thus have the same positive symbol-multiplicity partition \(\lambda\vdash 8\), after a common bijective relabeling of the used alphabet. Conversely, a common relabeling preserves equality of spectra and reversal/interleaving structure. Hence it suffices to examine, for each of the exactly \(22\) partitions \(\lambda\) of eight, all multiset permutations of one canonical alphabet whose symbol multiplicities are the parts of \(\lambda\).

The self-contained file `check_length8.py` carries out **all** these cases: \(95{,}503\) canonical words in total. It constructs each exact multiset of \(36\) Parikh vectors, groups words by exact equality (no probabilistic hashes), and for **every** group compares its full membership with the explicitly predicted set consisting of a chosen word, its reversal, and—if positions \(1,2\), \(4,5\), and \(7,8\) repeat the same two-symbol block—the two versions obtained by exchanging positions \(3\) and \(6\) and reversing. Each fiber agrees exactly. The only four-element fiber types have sorted positive multiplicities \((4,4)\), \((4,3,1)\), and \((3,3,1,1)\), each occurring once on its canonical alphabet. This is an exhaustive finite proof for **every** alphabet size, not extrapolation from fixed small \(q\): all words of any larger alphabet still reduce to one of those \(22\) cases.

**3. Count fibers.** A four-word class uniquely determines the unordered pair \(\{a,b\}\) appearing in the three repeated two-symbol blocks and the unordered pair \(\{c,d\}\) appearing in the two inserted positions. Each independent choice of these two distinct-element pairs yields exactly one four-word fiber, so their number is \(\binom{q}{2}^{2}\). Every palindrome of even length eight is a singleton fiber, and the exhaustive classification excludes any further singleton; there are \(q^4\) palindromes. Every remaining word belongs to a two-element reversal fiber. Subtracting the words in singleton and quadruple fibers from \(q^8\) gives the displayed two-element count and the total number of observations.

**4. First ambiguity.** The same canonical-multiplicity procedure checks every source length from one through seven (\(13{,}390\) additional canonical words) and finds only reversal fibers, agreeing with the previously published first-ambiguity boundary. The four-word construction above exists at length eight for every \(q\ge2\), establishing that boundary for all finite alphabets.

## Verification

Run `python3 check_length8.py` with Python 3 and only its standard library. It prints the 22-partition/95,503-word exhaustive classification, the complete absence of nonreversal collisions for source lengths one through seven, and independent direct full-alphabet checks for \(q=2,3,4\), ending with `VERIFY_OK`. All comparisons use exact integer Parikh vectors and complete group membership; no timeouts or floating point approximations are involved. The file is part of this result and contains everything needed to replay the finite certificate.

## Relationship to prior work

Acharya, Das, Milenkovic, Orlitsky, and Pan established the complete substring-composition model, reversal equivalence, polynomial representation, interleaving collision construction, and the first binary nonreversal collision at length eight. Their 2010 table contains the binary length-eight collision, and the 2014 full-text treatment characterizes more general binary ambiguities by polynomial factors. Those constructions are essential starting points here. The present claim is the complete **all-alphabet**, **length-eight** fiber census and its exact \(q\)-parameter code-size polynomial, obtained by a finite alphabet-independent reduction and an explicit exhaustive certificate. It does not claim the interleaving identity or the first binary counterexample as new.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns the full multiset of *all* nonempty substring compositions at **length eight**, not a restricted window length, noisy composition channel, or general blocklength. Its infinite quantifier ranges over finite alphabet sizes \(q\); the finite-certificate reduction is exact for this range. The historical-priority search did not locate an earlier identical all-alphabet census, but an unindexed equivalent enumeration or one recoverable by specialization of the established polynomial-characterization framework remains possible. Independent mathematical review has not been performed.
