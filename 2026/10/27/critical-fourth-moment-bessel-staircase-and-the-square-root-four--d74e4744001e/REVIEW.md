# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The Bessel recurrences yield the exact differentiated-square identity without approximation; its zero-boundary term and absence of simultaneous positive zeros prove strict finite-radius ordering for every admissible index and radius. Fixed-order Hankel asymptotics give the endpoint constants. Uniform-in-exponent integration by parts of zero-mean periodic oscillations justifies exchange of limit and subtraction as the Bessel integrals separately diverge. The pole residue, mean-value scaling, and Fourier-normalization constants have been checked explicitly. The standalone symbolic/numerical program provides corroboration, not a substitute for the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS with residual priority risk
The direct 2026 source proves sharp Fourier-extension extremality and stability for exponents strictly above four, not an exact finite-radius Bessel fourth-power hierarchy or the fixed-mode square-root coefficient at the excluded endpoint. The 2018/2019 foundational article supplies harmonic decomposition, and the 2025 article discusses exponent ranges, not the stated finite-part staircase. General classical Bessel recurrence/antiderivative formulas might imply part of the finite-radius statement; this priority risk is explicitly retained. Indexed results concerning finite character fourth moments differ in objects and imply neither the Bessel endpoint constants nor the mixed-norm limit. The new full compound claim is not shown covered by the inspected sources.

## Value — PASS
The result identifies an exact, strictly ordered hierarchy at the singular endpoint of an actively studied sharp mixed-norm Fourier-extension problem, and calculates a fixed-mode quantitative stability-gap constant inaccessible from a bare positive-stability statement. It is a family of identities for all cutoff radii and modes, plus a justified singular limit, not an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Limits and independence
The theorem does not identify the globally optimal nonconstant Fourier mode at finite exponents, gives no uniformity in growing degree, and asserts no settled historical priority for the underlying recurrence identity. Classical Bessel handbooks remain a literature-overlap risk.
