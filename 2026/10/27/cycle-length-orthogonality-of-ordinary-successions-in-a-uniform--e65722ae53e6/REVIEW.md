# Scientific review: cycle-length orthogonality of ordinary successions

## Correctness — PASS
The exact domain is uniform permutations of \([n]\) with \(n\ge2\), ordinary one-line ascents of size exactly one, and the cycle-count coordinates of the same mapping. The proof partitions the two prescribed arrows \(i\to b\) and \((i+1)\to(b+1)\) into two loops, one two-edge path, or two disjoint edges. Each completion has a uniform permutation on \(n-2\) contracted objects. The marked-cycle expectations give the displayed table, including all long-cycle boundary terms. Weighted sums prove both covariance formulas. The special sizes \(n=2,3\) are fully enumerated in the proof/checker. Replaying `python verify.py` yielded 46,232 exact permutation enumerations, 183 marginal/positionwise comparisons, and 1,136,245 exact category/aggregation comparisons. Finite checks do not replace the analytic argument. The proof does not assert independence from cycle counts.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical-priority risk
The closest existing ordinary-succession result establishes the random succession set's marginal distribution (Diaconis–Evans–Graham, Berkeley Technical Report 822, 2013). A previously recorded adjacent-jump conditional law establishes the scalar covariance with *total* cycle count, which is explicitly excluded from the novelty claim: a scalar sum does not determine the length-resolved vector or its positionwise zero band. Reilly–Tanny (1979) compute ordinary-succession counts, not their same-permutation cycle-length cross-moments. Mansour–Shattuck (2016) compute the cycle-successions/cycle-count joint distribution in *cycle notation* and explain why the bijective correspondence with ordinary successions does not preserve arbitrary joint statistics. Ma–Qi–Yeh–Yeh (2024) study ordinary successions with Eulerian and other statistics and use the number of cycles in transformed polynomial identities, not as the same-permutation length-resolved response. Relevant research-record searches for aliases, length counts, and dominant implications found no equivalent. No search proves universal novelty; further historical checking remains appropriate.

## Mathematical value — PASS
An ordinary succession is a classic finite-permutation shuffling diagnostic; cycle counts measure a distinct decomposition of exactly the same permutation. The finding gives the complete first-order coupling of the local diagnostic to all cycle lengths and proves a surprising *entire middle range of exact zero covariances, separately at each position*. The four surviving boundary lengths have explicit, alternating signs and expose information that neither the marginal succession law nor a total-cycle conditional law can recover. This is a structural orthogonality theorem, not an arbitrary new statistic or a numeric improvement. It makes no claim about joint independence or practical testing power.
