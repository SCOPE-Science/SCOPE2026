# Cyclic radical braces: the exact joint flip–component law and a sharp valuation threshold

## Finding
For every prime \(p\), integer \(n\ge2\), and integer \(s\) with \(1\le s<n\), let \(B_{p,n,s}=\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z\) be the commutative radical brace with addition modulo \(p^n\) and circle multiplication
\[
a\circ b=a+b+p^sab\pmod{p^n}.
\]
Put \(k=n-s\). Let \(r\) denote its canonical involutive nondegenerate set-theoretic Yang–Baxter solution. An *indecomposable component* means an orbit under the group generated by all left and right component maps of \(r\). Let \(F\) be the event that \(r\) acts as the flip on an independently uniform ordered pair \((a,b)\), and let \(C\) be the event that \(a,b\) lie in the same indecomposable component.

**Theorem (exact orbit and joint law).** The component-size multiset consists of exactly \(p^s\) singleton components and, for every \(j=1,\ldots,k\), exactly \((p-1)p^{s-1}\) components of size \(p^j\). Each component is internally *flip-complete* or *flip-free*: on a component of size \(p^j>1\), every ordered pair is flipped precisely when \(2j\le k\), while no ordered pair is flipped when \(2j>k\). Every singleton is internally flip-complete.

In particular,
\[
\Pr(F)=\frac{p+k(p-1)}{p^{k+1}},\qquad
\Pr(C)=\frac{p^s+(p-1)p^{s-1}\sum_{j=1}^k p^{2j}}{p^{2n}},
\]
\[
\Pr(F\cap C)=\frac{p^s+(p-1)p^{s-1}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}p^{2j}}{p^{2n}}.
\]
The four flip/triviality probabilities defined in Ferrara–Trombetti–Tsang all coincide with \(\Pr(F)\) for this brace. The number of indecomposable components is \(p^s+k(p-1)p^{s-1}\), and exactly the fraction \(1-1/p\) of the underlying elements belong to maximum-size components.

For fixed \(p,s\) and \(n\to\infty\),
\[
\Pr(F)\to0,\qquad
\Pr(C)\to\frac{p^{1-s}}{p+1}>0,\qquad
\Pr(F\mid C)\to0.
\]
Thus the two natural notions of probabilistic triviality separate sharply on a single explicit family of nontrivial finite braces. For example, with \(p=2,s=1\), the same-component probability tends to \(1/3\) even though the flip probability tends to zero.

## Assumptions and scope
All primes \(p\), all integers \(n\ge2\), and all \(1\le s<n\) are included, including characteristic two. No assertion is made about arbitrary braces or the independently constructed indecomposable solutions having these braces as permutation braces. The probability space is the uniform product \(B_{p,n,s}\times B_{p,n,s}\), not a random brace. The event \(F\) denotes literal flip action, and not merely involutivity (the entire solution is involutive).

## Proof
1. The multiplication \(a\cdot b=p^sab\pmod{p^n}\) is an associative, commutative, nilpotent ring multiplication on the additive group. Consequently \(a\circ b=a+b+a\cdot b\) is a two-sided brace. For each \(a\), its left action is
\[
\lambda_a(b)=-a+a\circ b=(1+p^sa)b.
\]
Every factor \(1+p^sa\) is a unit modulo \(p^n\). As \(a\) ranges over the brace, these factors exhaust the principal unit subgroup \(U_s=1+p^s\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z\). The canonical brace solution has the form
\[
r(a,b)=(c,\lambda_c^{-1}(a)),\qquad c=\lambda_a(b).
\]
In particular, every right component map sends each \(a\) to some \(U_s\)-multiple of \(a\), and the group generated by left and right component maps has exactly the \(U_s\)-orbits. This argument uses the actual right component, not an assumption that it equals a fixed left multiplication.

2. If \(a\ne0\) has \(p\)-adic valuation \(v\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\), then its orbit is
\[
U_sa=a+p^{s+v}\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z,
\]
where for \(s+v\ge n\) the coset is a singleton. For \(v<n-s=k\) the orbit size is \(p^{n-s-v}\). There are \((p-1)p^{n-v-1}\) elements of valuation \(v\), so at this valuation there are exactly \((p-1)p^{s-1}\) orbits. For \(v\ge k\), together with zero there are precisely \(p^s\) fixed points. Reindexing by \(j=k-v\) gives the asserted full component-size multiset. Summing the orbit sizes yields \(p^n\), confirming exhaustiveness.

3. Because \(a\cdot b=p^sab\) is symmetric, the formula for \(r\) gives
\[
r(a,b)=(b,a)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p^sab=0\pmod{p^n}.
\]
For the forward direction, the first component must fix \(b\); for the converse, \(\lambda_a(b)=b\) and \(\lambda_b(a)=a\), so the second component equals \(a\). Involutivity also gives the reverse flip simultaneously. Since both brace group laws are abelian, the four flip/triviality events of the cited 2026 paper reduce to this same zero-product condition.

4. Every non-singleton orbit of size \(p^j\) consists entirely of elements of valuation \(v=k-j\). For any two such elements, \(p^sab\equiv0\pmod{p^n}\) holds exactly when \(s+2v\ge n\), equivalently \(2j\le k\). In a singleton orbit the sole element is divisible by \(p^k\), so it also satisfies the zero-product condition with itself. This proves the componentwise all-or-none flip threshold.

5. Uniform \(a,b\in\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z\) reduce to independently uniform residues modulo \(p^k\), and \(F\) occurs precisely when their product vanishes modulo \(p^k\). For the first residue zero there are \(p^k\) choices of the second. For each valuation \(i=0,\ldots,k-1\), there are \((p-1)p^{k-i-1}\) first residues and \(p^i\) compatible second residues. Thus there are \(p^k+k(p-1)p^{k-1}\) successful pairs modulo \(p^k\), proving the displayed \(\Pr(F)\).

6. An orbit of length \(L\) contributes \(L^2\) ordered pairs to \(C\). By Step 4 it contributes all of them to \(F\cap C\) precisely for \(L=1\) or \(L=p^j\) with \(2j\le k\). The two corresponding sums are the displayed \(\Pr(C)\) and \(\Pr(F\cap C)\), after division by \(p^{2n}\). The number of components is immediate. The largest components have size \(p^k\) and number \((p-1)p^{s-1}\), so their total relative mass is \((p-1)p^{s-1+k}/p^n=1-1/p\).

7. With \(p,s\) fixed and \(k=n-s\to\infty\), the first probability is \(O(kp^{-k})\). The geometric sum gives
\[
\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{(p-1)p^{s-1}\sum_{j=1}^k p^{2j}}{p^{2(s+k)}}
=\frac{p^{1-s}}{p+1}.
\]
Finally \(\Pr(F\cap C)\le\Pr(F)\to0\), while \(\Pr(C)\) has a strictly positive limit, proving the conditional-probability claim.

## Verification
The included `verify_cyclic_brace_orbits.py` independently builds the left actions, computes their orbit closure and the actual right maps, and compares directly enumerated flip, same-component and joint pair events to all three formulas. It verifies 25 parameter triples across \(p\in\{2,3,5,7\}\) at the documented finite ranges. In the smallest cases it separately checks the Yang–Baxter braid relation and involutivity on all triples. It exits with `VERIFY_OK` on success. Exhaustive finite checks corroborate the proof but do not establish the universal quantifiers; those are proved in Steps 1–7.

## Relationship to prior work
Ferrara, Trombetti and Tsang (2026, Sections 1, 3–5) introduced the four flip probabilities and the same-indecomposable-component probability, proved general bounds, and gave finite example families. Their general identity \(\Pr(C)=\sum_O|O|^2/|B|^2\) is used here, not claimed as new. The contribution is the complete \(p,n,s\) orbit-size distribution, the componentwise sharp internal flip threshold, the exact joint probability \(\Pr(F\cap C)\), and the persistent-versus-vanishing probability separation for a natural family of cyclic radical braces. The known radical-ring construction of braces is not new. Darlington and Wiertel (2026) classify *indecomposable solutions with specified permutation braces* in small order, rather than the canonical solution on every element of \(B_{p,n,s}\); these are distinct objects, and their 2026 full text was not available for a decisive full-text novelty comparison. Earlier classification of cyclic braces and actions remains a potential source of implicit formulas. Ramírez (2022) treats indecomposable solutions with permutation groups of size \(pq\) or \(p^2q\), not the family and joint probabilities stated here.

## Limitations
This is an explicit-family theorem, not an extremal statement about all Yang–Baxter solutions. Only elementary properties of radical braces, the canonical associated solution, and valuation strata are used. Earlier work may implicitly contain some of the orbit calculations, especially for fixed small prime powers; the combined all-length joint threshold and asymptotics have not been independently certified as new. The Python tests are finite and the literature search is not a proof of global uniqueness of the result.
