# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The defining weights have a nonzero periodic product, the diagonal entries follow a primitive cyclotomic orbit, and the commutation relation \(DB=\zeta^{-1}BD\) gives the exact \(q\)-power identity. Residue-wise permutation transforms the power to a direct sum of explicit scalar shifts. The eigenvalue recurrence proves the strict point-spectrum lemniscate and, with spectral mapping, the closed spectrum. A bounded diagonal gauge conjugates every two tuples with the same weight-product modulus. Analytic eigenvector totality, the Godefroy–Shapiro and Grivaux theorems, and norm bounds prove the sharp linear-dynamics threshold. The shadowing interval is obtained from the exact general characterization of [1]. Finite matrix checks corroborate only finite algebraic identities, not the universal claims.

## Originality — PASS, subject to residual priority risk
The focal paper [1], read in full at Theorems 3.1–3.6 and proofs, treats the entire perturbation class but states only sufficient frequent-hypercyclicity conditions; its shadowing theorem already implies our shadowing answer after substitution, and that ingredient is credited. The combination with cyclotomic power collapse, exact spectral classification, one-invariant diagonal similarity classification, and necessary-and-sufficient frequent hypercyclicity/mixing/chaos conditions is not stated there. Grivaux [3] gives the implication from perfectly spanning unimodular eigenvectors; Godefroy–Shapiro [2] gives mixing, but neither identifies these periodic perturbation threshold regions. Older operator-polynomial equivalences remain a realistic residual priority risk; absence of a search hit is not independent proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The results classify an entire natural periodically perturbed operator family rather than one parameter slice. They turn large-weight sufficient hypotheses into exact conditions valid for all nonzero complex weight tuples, identify rigid similarity classes, and isolate sharp critical boundaries where dynamics and shadowing separate. For instance, at \(a=1\) arbitrarily tiny nonzero weights already produce frequent hypercyclicity, whereas shadowing needs product modulus greater than \(2\). The sharpness and the simultaneous presence/absence of shadowing supply a motivated structural distinction.

## Limitations and closest literature
The shadowing criterion itself is not new; the finite diagnostic has no infinite-dimensional force. Relevant earlier general results could subsume some reductions or classifications; independent historical-priority verification is still necessary. No claims about arbitrary nonperiodic weights or independent audit are made.
