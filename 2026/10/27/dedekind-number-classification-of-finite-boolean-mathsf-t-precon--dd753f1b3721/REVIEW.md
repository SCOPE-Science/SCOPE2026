# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The necessity direction follows from the published antitonicity theorem for preconditional-derived negation, together with identity and semicomplementation. For the converse, the explicit difference-lift is checked individually against each of (P1)–(P5), including the critical semicomplementation argument in (P5): \(D\setminus C\subseteq A\setminus(D\Rightarrow C)\). Identity and exact negation recovery follow directly. The independent per-atom coordinate parametrization produces \((M(n-1)-1)^n\). The graph comparison is also derived, though it is explicitly prior work. Small Boolean algebras were exhaustively checked. This is a proof for all finite positive atom counts, not an inference from tests.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical-priority risk

Chen (2026) defines the \(\mathsf T\) and \(\mathsf F\) algebra classes; Holliday (2024/2025) gives the preconditional axioms, antitone negation lemma, Sasaki/ortholattice cases, and related representation results. Earlier Boolean-polarity work already covers \(\mathsf F\)-negation reducts and uses the displayed lift under stronger reciprocal hypotheses. Finite-chain classifications are not Boolean-classification theorems. The distinguishing theorem here is the *all* normalized antitone semicomplementations extend to \(\mathsf T\), without the extra reciprocal property, and the resulting unrestricted coordinate-wise Dedekind census. There is no implication from the known \(\mathsf F\) theorem to the larger \(\mathsf T\) class. Literature and semantic overlap searches did not uncover a direct previous treatment of the lifted \(\mathsf T\) class. The existing weak-negation literature may nevertheless contain a reformulation of the extension lemma; this remains a nonzero priority risk.

## Value — PASS

This gives a complete classification of a canonical derived operation across all finite Boolean carriers, distinguishing the logical strength of two natural axiomatic systems. The same lift recovers the entire weaker class; the Dedekind census makes the sharp expressive gap quantitative, and the four-element witness locates its first occurrence. These are motivated structural results about logical negation, not arbitrary small calculations; full binary-operation enumeration and non-Boolean generalization remain outside scope.
