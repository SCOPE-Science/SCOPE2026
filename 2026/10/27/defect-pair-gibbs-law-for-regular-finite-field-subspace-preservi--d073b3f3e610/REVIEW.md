# Review: defect-pair Gibbs law for regular subspace-preserving maps

**Correctness — PASS.** For \(T=\begin{psmallmatrix}A&B\\0&D\end{psmallmatrix}\), the regularity condition in the subspace-preserving monoid is \(\operatorname{im}T\cap U=\operatorname{im}A\). The displayed proof reconstructs the right inverse respecting \(U\), translates the condition to \(B(\ker D)\subseteq\operatorname{im}A\), and obtains exactly \(q^{ab-ij}\) allowed off-diagonal blocks for diagonal coranks \((i,j)\). Standard rank counts give the complete joint distribution. The infinite-field-size parameter \(q\) is fixed in the limiting argument; elementary product estimates give the stated \(O_q(q^{-a}+q^{-b})\) bound. A decreasing likelihood ratio verifies negative dependence. An independent finite enumeration checked all defect strata in six configurations; full generalized-inverse witness searches were performed in three configurations.

**Originality — PASS within inspected coverage.** The subspace-preserving regularity criterion is established by Sarkar and Singh, Theorem 4.2 (arXiv:2303.03861). Fulman and Goldstein (arXiv:1211.0504) establish the random finite-field matrix-corank law and quantitative convergence. Those two ingredients are expressly not new; the exact parabolic *joint* regularity/defect mass \(p_{q,a}(i)p_{q,b}(j)q^{-ij}\), its limiting conditional Gibbs law, and strict negative association are the assessed claims. Searches of the checked papers, indexed results, and accessible historical claim titles did not reveal the same quantified joint spectrum. This is not a claim of exhaustive priority; specialized enumerations of parabolic regular elements could cover the result.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Regularity of an entire transformation monoid is a coarse yes/no property, and finite subspace-preserving monoids generally contain both regular and nonregular elements. The theorem replaces that binary property with the complete defect-resolved probability measure, explains the interaction between two otherwise independent diagonal kernels, and proves a nontrivial large-dimension failure probability and strict negative dependence. These are structural quantitative invariants of a natural infinite family of finite algebraic semigroups, not just one small table or a change of variables.

**Limitations and risk.** There is no claim for longer flags, arbitrary finite rings, or varying field order in the limit. Prime-field finite cases corroborate but do not substitute for all-field proof. The semigroup criterion and matrix-nullity law are prior results. Publication-equivalent derivations in unindexed or otherwise unexamined specialized literature remain possible.
