# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The independent exponential-clock representation yields an exact triangular Bernoulli array. At the moving mean root, its variance tends to the positive, finite integral \(V\); the summed third absolute moments after \(N^{3/2}\) scaling vanish at order \(N^{-1/2}\). Lyapunov convergence and inversion of the monotone mass process establish the claimed Gaussian time law. A separate Euler-summation expansion and a uniformly regular undiscovered-mass integral yield the zeta coefficient for the deterministic root. The two results together justify the continuum-centering transition. The accompanying program checks representative finite roots and variance expressions.

**Originality — PASS (qualified).** Müller–Šebek's full primary text, especially Theorem 5.3 and Sections 2–5, yields first-order diffuse convergence, not this Gaussian, second-order finite-normalizer theorem. Chebunin–Zuyev's full primary text treats stationary infinite inventories of regularly varying urn probabilities rather than the moving finite Zipf weights and inverse weighted threshold. A related harmonic \(s=1\) result is a distinct endpoint with different scale. Record and literature searches did not locate an implication covering the explicit \(0<s<1\) crossing displacement and sharp \(1/2\) boundary. Residual risk: general triangular-array inverse-process expansions may imply the Gaussian component, but no located result supplies the explicit zeta displacement.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** At finite \(N\), the continuum mean root may be biased relative to the sample's exact deterministic mean crossing. The first nonvanishing bias scales as \(N^{s-1}\), which directly competes with the random \(N^{-1/2}\) fluctuations at \(s=1/2\). The theorem therefore specifies when first-order prediction can or cannot be used to center Gaussian confidence intervals, while supplying exact, computable variance and bias coefficients for a natural quorum stopping problem.

**Limitations and priority risks.** The result is fixed-parameter and does not cover \(s=0\), \(s=1\), \(s>1\), growing thresholds, or exceptional vanishing leading bias beyond the stated conclusions. Broader inverse empirical-process or missing-mass results are plausible potential reformulations; independent priority analysis is necessary.
