# Dihedral subquandle filtrations: universal ungraded collapse and a graded torus-knot formula

## Finding

Let \(n\geq 2\), and let \(R_n=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) be the dihedral quandle with \(x\triangleright y=2y-x\). Use the *complete* endomorphism set \(S=\operatorname{End}(R_n)\). For every nonempty oriented link \(L\) and every filtration \(U_0\subseteq\cdots\subseteq U_m=R_n\) by nonempty subquandles, the ungraded disrespectful polynomial introduced in [2] is
\[
\mathbf{dis}_{U_i}^{S}(L;t)
=\#\operatorname{Col}_{U_i}(L)\;t^{n(n-|U_i|)},\qquad 0\leq i\leq m.
\]
Thus the entire sequence of these ungraded polynomials contains exactly the same link-dependent information as the corresponding sequence of subquandle coloring counts. The statement holds for every \(n\), including even \(n\).

For the two-stage filtration \(U_0=\{0\}\subset U_1=R_n\), let \(k\geq1\) be odd, let \(L=T(2,k)\) denote the oriented closure of the positive two-strand braid with \(k\) crossings, and put \(D=\gcd(n,k)\). For \(r\mid D\) define
\[
M_r=t_{0,1}^{n/r}\,t_{1,1}^{n(n-1)/r}
 \prod_{\substack{s\mid r\\s>1}}
 t_{1,s-1}^{(n/r)\varphi(s)s}
 t_{1,s}^{(n/r)\varphi(s)(n-s)}.
\]
Here \(\varphi\) is Euler's totient, and factors using the same variable are combined by adding exponents. Then the *graded* disrespectful polynomials of [2] are
\[
G\mathbf{dis}_{U_1}^{S}(T(2,k))
=\sum_{r\mid D}n\varphi(r)M_r,
\qquad
G\mathbf{dis}_{U_0}^{S}(T(2,k))=M_1.
\]
In particular the full-stage graded polynomial has precisely \(\tau(D)\) distinct monomials, with coefficients \(n\varphi(r)\) for \(r\mid D\). Setting every variable to \(1\) yields \(nD\) colorings. The ungraded full-stage polynomial is the constant \(nD\), while the ungraded initial-stage polynomial is \(t^{n(n-1)}\).

## Assumptions and scope

An \(R_n\)-coloring of \(L\) means a quandle map from its fundamental quandle to \(R_n\). At stage \(U_i\), the source vertices are exactly the colorings with images contained in \(U_i\). For every source coloring \(f\), the ungraded exponent is the number of endomorphisms \(\phi\in S\) such that \(\phi\circ f\) does not color into \(U_i\). For the graded polynomial, define \(B_0=U_0\), \(B_1=U_1\setminus U_0\); the target index \(j\) of \(\phi\circ f\) is the smallest \(j\) with image contained in \(U_j\), and its defect weight is \(|\operatorname{im}(\phi\circ f)\cap B_j|\). A monomial counts each endomorphism once in the factor \(t_{j,w}\) associated to its target index and defect weight. These are the conventions of Definitions 3.5–3.11 of [2].

The general collapse statement allows every nonempty oriented link and every nonempty nested subquandle filtration; no assertion is made for a proper subset of endomorphisms. The exact graded formula is for the specified two-level filtration and positive \((2,k)\) torus knots with odd \(k\), including the unknot at \(k=1\). It uses only \(n\ge2\) and integer arithmetic; no primality assumption is imposed.

## Proof

**1. Endomorphisms and subquandles.** Every affine function \(\phi_{u,v}(x)=ux+v\) on \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) preserves \(x\triangleright y=2y-x\). Conversely, the identity \(j\triangleright(j+1)=j+2\) implies that an endomorphism's values satisfy \(\phi(j+2)=2\phi(j+1)-\phi(j)\) for all residues \(j\). Starting at \(\phi(0)\) and \(\phi(1)\), induction gives \(\phi(j)=j\phi(1)+(1-j)\phi(0)\), hence affine form. The \(n^2\) pairs \((u,v)\) are distinct.

Every nonempty subquandle \(U\) of \(R_n\) is a coset of a subgroup. Translate so that \(0\in U\) and let \(H\) be the additive subgroup generated by \(U\). Right reflection \(R_y:x\mapsto 2y-x\) is an involution; its restriction to the finite subquandle is a permutation. Compositions \(R_yR_z\) translate by \(2(y-z)\), so \(U\) is invariant under translation by the generated group \(2H\). Therefore \(U\) is a union of cosets of \(2H\) inside \(H\). Since \(H\) is cyclic, \(H/2H\) has at most two elements. If there are two, the fact that \(U\) generates \(H\) implies it meets the nonzero coset, and \(0\in U\) supplies the zero coset. Thus \(U=H\). Translating back gives \(U=c+H\). Every such coset is plainly closed under the quandle operation, completing the classification.

**2. Universal ungraded collapse.** Fix \(U=c+H\) and any coloring \(f\) into \(U\). Choose a point \(x\in\operatorname{im}f\). For every \(y\in\operatorname{im}f\), the difference \(y-x\in H\), hence \(\phi_{u,v}(y)-\phi_{u,v}(x)=u(y-x)\in H\). Thus \(\phi_{u,v}\circ f\) has image inside \(U\) if and only if \(\phi_{u,v}(x)\in U\). For each of the \(n\) choices of \(u\), exactly \(|H|=|U|\) values of \(v\) satisfy this condition. Exactly \(n|U|\) endomorphisms are respectful and \(n(n-|U|)\) are disrespectful, independently of the coloring. Summing the identical monomial over the \(U\)-colorings proves the first formula simultaneously for every filtration stage.

**3. Torus-knot coloring images.** With two starting arc colors \((a,b)\), write \(\delta=b-a\). One positive braid crossing transforms the ordered pair to \((b,2b-a)=(a+\delta,b+\delta)\), preserving \(\delta\). After \(k\) crossings closure is equivalent to \(k\delta=0\pmod n\). The coloring image is exactly the coset \(a+\langle\delta\rangle\). Let \(r=\operatorname{ord}(\delta)\). The closure condition is \(r\mid\gcd(n,k)=D\). For each such divisor \(r\), there are \(\varphi(r)\) residues of additive order \(r\) (including the unique zero residue for \(r=1\)) and independently \(n\) starting values \(a\): exactly \(n\varphi(r)\) colorings have image size \(r\).

**4. Graded image census.** Fix one of these colorings, with image a coset of the subgroup \(\langle\delta\rangle\) of size \(r\). Under \(\phi_{u,v}\), the image is a coset of \(\langle u\delta\rangle\), whose order is some \(s\mid r\). The multiplication map \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\langle\delta\rangle\), \(u\mapsto u\delta\), has all fibers of size \(n/r\). Exactly \(\varphi(s)\) elements in the target subgroup have order \(s\), so exactly \((n/r)\varphi(s)\) coefficients \(u\) produce image size \(s\).

For each such \(u\), as \(v\) ranges over the \(n\) residues, precisely \(s\) choices make the output image coset contain \(0\), and \(n-s\) do not. When \(s=1\), those choices have target/defect \((0,1)\) and \((1,1)\), respectively. For \(s>1\), even a coset containing \(0\) cannot fit inside \(U_0=\{0\}\); its target/defect is \((1,s-1)\), whereas a coset not containing \(0\) has target/defect \((1,s)\). Multiplying one variable for each \((u,v)\) yields the stated monomial \(M_r\). It is independent of the translate \(a\) and the chosen generator of order \(r\).

Each of the \(n\varphi(r)\) colorings with image size \(r\) therefore contributes \(M_r\). This proves the full-stage graded formula. The only coloring with image in \(U_0\) is the constant-zero coloring, which has \(r=1\) and contributes \(M_1\), proving the initial-stage formula. The exponent of \(t_{0,1}\) in \(M_r\) is \(n/r\); these values are pairwise distinct for distinct divisors \(r\) of \(D\), hence there are exactly \(\tau(D)\) terms. Finally, \(\sum_{r\mid D}\varphi(r)=D\), so the coefficient sum is \(nD\), as required.

## Verification

The standalone file `verify.py` uses only Python's standard library. Run `python3 verify.py`. On successful execution it reports `ALGEBRA_OK`, `GRADED_OK`, and `VERIFY_OK`. It independently enumerates every map on \(R_n\) for \(2\le n\le5\), all nonempty subsets for \(2\le n\le10\), and direct endomorphism-respect counts on one- and two-element coloring images. It then explicitly simulates braid coloring, determines each image, enumerates all \(n^2\) affine endomorphisms, and compares the graded monomials and their multiplicities with the formulas for the listed small moduli and positive odd crossing numbers. The computations are finite reproducibility checks; the preceding steps are the proof for all parameter values.

For \(n=k=9\), the three monomials are
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_1&=t_{0,1}^{9}t_{1,1}^{72},\\
M_3&=t_{0,1}^{3}t_{1,1}^{24}t_{1,2}^{18}t_{1,3}^{36},\\
M_9&=t_{0,1}t_{1,1}^{8}t_{1,2}^{6}t_{1,3}^{12}t_{1,8}^{54}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The full graded polynomial is \(9M_1+18M_3+54M_9\), a three-term refinement of the ungraded constant \(81\).

## Relationship to prior work

The 2026 paper [2] defines subquandle filtrations and both disrespectful polynomials, including target indexes and defect weights, and evaluates illustrative finite examples. The present theorem evaluates these new definitions for the complete endomorphism monoid of *every* cyclic dihedral target: first for all links at all ungraded stages and then for a full infinite family of filtered torus knots at the graded level. The 2026 finite spherical-subquandle classification [1] motivates why the dihedral family deserves attention but neither supplies this polynomial nor evaluates the filtration. The 2024 torus-link quiver study [3] predates the new graded invariant and computes unfiltered quiver structure; its known affine endomorphisms and knot coloring counts are used as background, not claimed as discoveries. The outcome is compatible with the earlier quiver enumeration but not identical to it: the output tracks *defect-weight distributions of all endomorphisms* for each coloring image.

## Limitations

The collapse theorem is only for the *complete* dihedral endomorphism monoid, and does not apply automatically to restricted endomorphism sets or arbitrary finite quandles. The explicit graded formula requires the particular singleton/full filtration and positive two-strand torus knots. In general a graded polynomial can retain information absent from the ungraded one; no all-link graded collapse, universal knot discrimination improvement, or classification of other target quandles is asserted. Standard affine endomorphism and basic torus-link coloring computations are not novel. The originality comparison includes accessible publications and mathematical-record searches, but cannot exclude a coincidentally equivalent older or unindexed formula.
