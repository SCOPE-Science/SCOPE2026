## correctness

Both curves scale by a^(1−t)b^t, so separate determinant-one normalization preserves the ordering. Cayley–Hamilton yields M=(A⁻¹+B)/K and N=(A⁻¹+D)/K, with determinants one and tr(AM)=tr(AN)=K>0. In a unitary eigenbasis of A, pairing the ij and ji terms in tr(D)−tr(B) yields the squared commutator sum; it vanishes exactly when A commutes with B^(1/2), equivalently with B. Thus the rapidities satisfy v≥u, strictly when AB≠BA. The two-point interpolation of T^(2t) on e^(±w) gives the displayed two-term trace difference, valid by continuity also at w=0. Strict increase of x coth x gives decreasing sinh(pw)/sinh(w) for 0<p<1 and increasing for p>1. The two coefficients of the trace difference thus have the same sign in the open intervals. At t=1 the first term is zero, not strictly increasing as stated for the combined range in Step 4; the F_2 term alone gives strictness. Both terms yield zero difference at t=0 and t=1/2. Equal positive determinants make largest eigenvalue strictly increasing with trace, giving the claimed spectral comparisons, not a Löwner-order claim.
