# Mathematical review

## Correctness (C) — PASS

The claim concerns every complex Hermitian positive-definite \(2\times2\) pair and every indicated real parameter, not a sample. After determinant normalization, the Cayley–Hamilton square-root identity yields common trace \(\operatorname{tr}(AM)=\operatorname{tr}(AN)\) and determinant-one comparison matrices. An exact squared-commutator energy gives \(\operatorname{tr}N\ge\operatorname{tr}M\), strictly except for commuting inputs. The two-eigenvalue hyperbolic power formula converts the global spectral comparison into monotonicity of \(\sinh(pw)/\sinh w\), including the midpoint and endpoint cases. Since determinants match, this proves log-majorization and strictness. The exact 896-assertion checker was replayed; it supplements but cannot replace the universal proof.

## Originality (O) — PASS, subject to independent priority audit

Ibrahim–Huang (arXiv:2610.23736v1, posted 16 October 2026) explicitly states the comparison as Conjecture 5.6 and proves only the endpoint order, the midpoint isospectrality, and a two-dimensional representation. Its relevant primary sections and results were inspected; no parameter-global two-dimensional sign proof was found. Gan–Kim's 2022 and 2023 full-text articles compare the ordinary spectral mean to other means, not to this later near-spectral interpolation. The foundational Franco–Gan–Kim (2025) reference is plausible but its full text was inaccessible: only the abstract and preview were inspected. No covering statement was identified in the additional published-record searches, but this does not establish absolute novelty; alternative terminology remains a residual risk.

## Mathematical value (V) — PASS

The complete dimension-two resolution of an explicit recent matrix-mean conjecture is a natural nontrivial classification. The theorem determines the exact sign on both sides of the midpoint for **every** parameter, proves a rigidity/equality criterion, and supplies a reusable quantitative trace identity. It does not claim the unrestricted higher-dimensional conjecture.
