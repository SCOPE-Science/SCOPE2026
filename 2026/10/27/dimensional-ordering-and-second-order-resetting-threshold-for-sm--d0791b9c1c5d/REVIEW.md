# Review of dimensional optimal-reset coefficient

**Correctness (PASS).** The governing objective and Bessel-root condition are exactly those of Evans–Majumdar [1], equations (71), (73), and (77). Differentiation gives the explicit Riccati identity and a unique monotone level-two crossing; the K-order log-convexity and recurrence give strict dimensional ordering and the square bracket. The Gamma integral expansion of the ratio has a controlled uniform remainder, and its coefficients are independently reproduced by symbolic recurrence algebra. The proof establishes the correction for all sufficiently large integer dimensions, not just ten numerical samples.

**Originality (PASS, qualified).** The 2014 originating study [1] already established the equation for the small-target optimum, including dimension-two and dimension-three values; these are excluded from the novel claim. Segura [2] establishes general Bessel ratio properties that can imply part of the monotonicity and brackets; those ingredients are properly credited. The combined physical dimension-ordering/bracketing result and, centrally, the explicit \(8/(d-2)\) correction for this continuum small-target reset optimizer were not found in the examined papers, related lattice analysis [4], or semantic research-record searches. General special-function asymptotic treatments may contain equivalent implicit-root expansion results, and that priority risk is retained.

**Mathematical value (PASS).** The dimensionless optimal reset rate is the natural measurable control parameter for this classical non-equilibrium search model. The exact finite-dimension bracket limits tuning without numerical Bessel solving; strict ordering resolves how the optimal rate moves as spatial dimension changes; the first nonzero relative finite-d correction quantifies how quickly the leading linear regime is reached. The correction is not a routine numerical table lookup, and the physical object was fixed before calculating it. The statement remains limited to the iterated small-target/high-dimension limit.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Primary source [1] supplies the model and optimization equation; [2] supplies stronger general ratio techniques; [3] treats gating; [4] concerns a lattice with a different dimension scaling. NIST DLMF [5] catalogs large-order expansions. A directly equivalent high-dimensional continuum implicit-root asymptotic may have escaped the searches. No uniform joint limit or finite-target optimizer is proved.
