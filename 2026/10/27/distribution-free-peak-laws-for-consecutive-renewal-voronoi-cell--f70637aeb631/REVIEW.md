# Review of the peak-count law

## Correctness
**PASS.** The shared-gap cancellation gives an indicator of two opposite strict comparisons of disjoint parity variables. Rank symmetry establishes one-point probability, the two independent three-chains at lag two, the common minimum at lag three, and independence from lag four onward. Covariance aggregation gives exact finite variance and bounded 3-dependence yields the stated CLT. The included exact-rational enumerator independently checks all finite pairwise cases and window sizes one to six. Infinite statements rest on the proof rather than simulation.

## Originality
**PASS, with historical-priority risk.** The 2004 public preprint by Járai-Szabó–Néda gives the marginal one-dimensional Poisson-Voronoi distribution; the 2004 Gabrielli–Torquato paper derives two-cell correlations for a different superhomogeneous construction. Neither inspected relevant full-text section presents the complete distribution-free peak-count covariance profile. The 1994 Ku–Seneta Gaussian-ARMA peak analysis is substantially closer in statistic and may cover the Gaussian specialization; Bandt–Shiha 2007 is likewise relevant on Gaussian/ARMA ordinal patterns. Their full texts were not examined, so we cannot rule out a previously observed universal identity. However Gaussian-specific MA(1) formulas do not by themselves imply a result for all atomless, possibly nonsymmetric, positive gap distributions. The inspected statements have not established a covering theorem with that quantifier.

## Value
**PASS.** Local maxima of Voronoi cell length locate spatially dominant gaps in one-dimensional renewal tessellations. The exact negative-negative-positive-zero lag pattern, the finite-sample variance, and especially its universality across gap distributions are meaningful dependence information beyond marginal cell-length laws and generic stationary-peak counting principles. The correct asymptotic variance is only \(5/27\) of a falsely independent model's value.

## Limitations
Dependent gap processes, ties, spatial-window (rather than cell-index-window) counts, and higher-dimensional cells are excluded. Historical priority, especially in older peak-count literature, requires independent inspection.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
