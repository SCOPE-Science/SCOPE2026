# Scientific review: dyadic first-order limits for uniform Dyck words

## Correctness — PASS
For a fixed prefix and suffix, the reflection-principle count is the difference of two binomial coefficients for a nonnegative walk between fixed heights. At total length \(2n\), Stirling asymptotics give the stated boundary mass \((h+1)(h'+1)/2^{a+b}\), with a fixed-window \(O(n^{-1})\) error. Under the unconditioned fair-word measure, disjoint blocks provide linearly many independent opportunities for every fixed interior factor; conditioning on a Dyck word costs only a factor \(\Theta(n^{3/2})\). Hence all complete neighbourhood types are exponentially likely to exceed any fixed multiplicity threshold. Effective Hanf locality for directed successor paths then reduces any fixed sentence to endpoint cylinders. Every endpoint cylinder has positive dyadic limiting mass, proving both the dyadic upper bound and exponential decay when the limit is zero or one. The sorted odd-weight coin lemma proves that every dyadic mass is a finite-prefix event; all quantifiers, parity restrictions and the permitted signature are explicit. The standalone check independently tested \(1083\) exact bridge identities, six even-prefix complete subset sums, and \(121\) large-size boundary ratios.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk
Relevant exact-query searches in mathematical records and public literature found no claim establishing the full successor-and-letter first-order probability spectrum for *uniform Dyck words*, or the joint boundary law plus exponential zero-limit dichotomy. Markström and Räty (arXiv:2610.22218v1) treat small permutation classes and regular encodings and mention Dyck bijections, but the result studied here is the nonregular Dyck language in its own successor signature. Albert, Bouvel, Féray and Noy (arXiv:2210.05537) prove logical limits for 231-avoiding permutations in TOTO's two-total-order signature, not for the Dyck word's successor signature. Effective bounded-degree Hanf locality and Catalan reflection asymptotics are classical and are not themselves claimed new. Koponen (arXiv:2610.16163v1) addresses the finite model property of omega-categorical theories and is a motivation rather than an originality exclusion based on its unread full text. No absence-of-search-result is treated as a uniqueness certificate: a general conditional-word limit theorem or uninspected work could imply this special case.

## Value — PASS
The counting constraint defining a Dyck language is global and nonregular, unlike the regular word classes featured in recent logical-limit-law work. The result nevertheless gives a complete effective classification for every sentence in a precise natural observational language, a quantitative convergence rate, an exponentially sharp criterion for vanishing events, and an explicit construction of every attainable limit. It identifies the boundary Doob-transform mechanism responsible for the law; the numerical set of dyadics also occurs in simpler word models, so its appearance alone is not credited as novel.
