# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** Exact graph definitions, the equivalence between edge-resolution and covering all \(861\) edge-pair constraints, exhaustive traversal of all \(3276\) triples and \(20475\) quadruples, and full all-root breadth-first searches prove the theorem. Two independent, self-contained implementations reproduce the identical counts, exchange edge number and eccentricity census. Limits: the calculation proves only the finite case \(J_7\).

**Originality (PASS, residual risk).** Kelenc–Tratnik–Yero (arXiv:1602.00291v1) introduced the invariant and its general set-cover characterization. Milivojević Danas (DOI:10.1515/math-2021-0065), on inspecting the primary text, explicitly leaves the exact flower-snark edge metric dimension open, supplying only bounds \(3\le\operatorname{edim}(J_n)\le4\) for odd \(n\ge7\). These earlier statements cannot determine whether the lower or upper endpoint occurs at \(J_7\), nor can they determine its \(1932\) bases or exchange eccentricities. Searches for flower snark, edge metric, aliases and reconfiguration did not locate coverage; an unindexed finite table remains possible, so publication priority is not independently established.

**Value (PASS).** This is the **first unsettled case** in a mathematically motivated explicitly published question about the entire flower-snark family. It supplies both the exact missing landmark number and a complete, nontrivial description of how all optimal placements transform by one-landmark replacement, including a sharp five-move maximum. This is not presented as a solution of the infinite-family problem.
