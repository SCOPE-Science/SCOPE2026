# Embedded cyclic submonoids of symmetric inverse monoids: a stretched-exponential collision law

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge1\), let \(I_n\) be the symmetric inverse monoid of all injective partial self-maps of the labeled set \([n]\). For \(f\in I_n\), denote the embedded, ambient-identity-containing cyclic submonoid by \(\langle f\rangle_1=\{1_n,f,f^2,\ldots\}\). Two submonoids are distinct when their subsets of \(I_n\) are distinct, not merely when their abstract monoids are nonisomorphic.

Write \(o(\sigma)\) for the order of a permutation \(\sigma\), \(\varphi\) for the Euler totient (with \(\varphi(1)=1\)), and \(S_0\) for the trivial group. Define
\[
Q_n=\sum_{r=0}^n\binom nr^2r!,\qquad H_n=\sum_{m=0}^n\binom nm m!.
\]
The exact number \(M_n\) of embedded cyclic submonoids is
\[
\boxed{M_n=Q_n-H_n+\sum_{m=0}^n\binom nm\sum_{\sigma\in S_m}\frac1{\varphi(o(\sigma))}.}
\]
In particular, \(M_1,\ldots,M_6=2,7,33,198,1448,12526\).

If \(f\) is uniform in \(I_n\), let \(E_n\) denote the event that some distinct \(g\in I_n\) satisfies \(\langle g\rangle_1=\langle f\rangle_1\). Then
\[
\boxed{Q_n-M_n\sim e\,n!,\qquad\Pr(E_n)\sim\frac{Q_n-M_n}{Q_n}\sim2e^{3/2}\sqrt\pi\,n^{1/4}e^{-2\sqrt n}.}
\]
Furthermore, for every fixed integer \(j\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\Pr(n-|\operatorname{dom}f|=j\mid E_n)=\frac1{e\,j!}.}
\]
Thus the domain defect on the exceptional event converges to \(\operatorname{Poisson}(1)\).

## Assumptions and scope
The monoid identity in \(\langle f\rangle_1\) is the *full* identity map \(1_n\), not the partial identity on \(\operatorname{dom}f\). A cyclic submonoid is generated by nonnegative powers alone; it is not the inverse submonoid generated by \(f\) and its partial inverse. The empty partial map is included. All conclusions hold for integer \(n\ge1\). The asymptotics concern \(n\to\infty\), with uniform distribution over elements of \(I_n\), not over submonoids.

## Proof
**1. Generator multiplicities.** For an element \(a\) in any finite monoid, let \(t\ge0\) and \(p\ge1\) be its minimal index and eventual period: its distinct powers are \(a^0,\ldots,a^{t+p-1}\), and \(a^{t+p}=a^t\). If \(t\ge2\), no power \(a^k\) with \(k\ge2\) generates \(a\), since no positive power of \(a^k\) equals \(a\); hence \(a\) is the sole generator of \(\langle a\rangle_1\). The identity \(a^0\) generates only the singleton monoid, also with one generator. If \(t=1\), the powers \(a^1,\ldots,a^p\) form a cyclic group with identity \(a^p\), and the other generators of \(\langle a\rangle_1\) are precisely \(a^k\) for \(1\le k\le p\) with \(\gcd(k,p)=1\): there are \(\varphi(p)\). The case \(t=0\) is a group (in the ambient monoid), and its generator multiplicity is again \(\varphi(p)\). This argument counts generators of an embedded submonoid rather than isomorphism types.

**2. Partial-injection threshold.** Every partial bijection decomposes uniquely into disjoint directed cycles, nontrivial directed paths, and vertices omitted from its domain. Any path having at least one directed edge makes the power index at least two; conversely, when there are no directed edges outside cycles, all non-cycle vertices are omitted and the power index is at most one. Equivalently, this occurs precisely when \(\operatorname{dom}f=\operatorname{im}f\), so \(f\) is a permutation of its domain, extended as an undefined map elsewhere. For a chosen domain of size \(m\), there are \(m!\) such maps, indexed by \(\sigma\in S_m\), with eventual period \(o(\sigma)\).

**3. Exact enumeration.** There are \(Q_n\) partial bijections, by choosing the domain, image, and bijection for each rank \(r\). Exactly \(H_n\) have no nontrivial path components. Partitioning all generators into classes with the same embedded cyclic submonoid, the unique-generator classes contribute one apiece, while a locally permutational class of period \(p\) contributes \(1/\varphi(p)\) for each of its \(\varphi(p)\) generators. This gives the displayed expression for \(M_n\).

**4. Almost all local permutations have large generator multiplicity.** If \(\sigma\) is uniform in \(S_m\), set \(P=o(\sigma)\) and let \(L\) be the length of its largest cycle. For \(1\le J\le m\), the expected number of points lying in cycles of length at most \(J\) is exactly \(J\), because each cycle length \(k\) has expected count \(1/k\). Therefore Markov's inequality gives \(\Pr(L\le J)\le J/m\). Since \(P\ge L\) and the elementary bound \(\varphi(v)\ge\sqrt{v/2}\) holds for every positive integer \(v\),
\[
\mathbb E\frac1{\varphi(P)}\le\frac Jm+\sqrt{\frac2J}.
\]
Choosing \(J=\lfloor m^{2/3}\rfloor\) shows this expectation tends to zero. The same bound implies \(\Pr(\varphi(P)=1)\to0\).

**5. Asymptotic exceptional mass and domain defect.** Put \(j=n-m\). The locally permutational count at domain defect \(j\) is
\[
\binom n{n-j}(n-j)!=\frac{n!}{j!},
\]
and hence \(H_n=n!\sum_{j=0}^n1/j!\sim e\,n!\). The normalized total \(\sum_{m}\binom nm\sum_{\sigma\in S_m}1/\varphi(o(\sigma))\) is \(o(n!)\): for fixed \(j\), Step 4 makes the coefficient relative to \(n!/j!\) tend to zero, and the tail in \(j\) is uniformly bounded by \(\sum_{j>J}1/j!\). Thus \(Q_n-M_n\sim e\,n!\).

The maps with a different generator are exactly those local permutations whose \(\varphi(o(\sigma))>1\). For each fixed defect \(j\) they number \((n!/j!)(1-o(1))\); summing over \(j\) by the same dominated-tail argument gives \(|E_n|\sim e\,n!\) and conditional mass \(1/(e\,j!)\) at defect \(j\).

**6. Precise rare-event scale.** Elementary manipulation yields
\[
Q_n=n!\sum_{k=0}^n\frac{\binom nk}{k!}=n!L_n(-1),
\]
where \(L_n\) is the standard Laguerre polynomial. To obtain its asymptotic without assuming a special-function result, compare the expansion to \(I_0(2\sqrt n)=\sum_{k\ge0}n^k/(k!)^2\). By Stirling's formula, the latter sum is exponentially concentrated where \(k/\sqrt n\to1\). Uniformly there, the factor \((n)_k/n^k\) equals \(e^{-1/2+o(1)}\): expand its logarithm as \(-k(k-1)/(2n)+O(k^3/n^2)\). The tails remain negligible by the coefficient ratio \(n/(k+1)^2\), and hence \(L_n(-1)\sim e^{-1/2}I_0(2\sqrt n)\). Finally, Fourier expansion and Laplace's method give
\[
I_0(2\sqrt n)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{2\sqrt n\cos\theta}\,d\theta\sim\frac{e^{2\sqrt n}}{2\sqrt\pi\,n^{1/4}}.
\]
Therefore \(Q_n\sim n!e^{-1/2}e^{2\sqrt n}/(2\sqrt\pi\,n^{1/4})\). Combining with Step 5 proves the asserted collision probability asymptotic, including its constant.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `CHECKER.py` enumerates all embedded cyclic submonoids of \(I_n\) for \(1\le n\le5\) by direct composition and hashes the resulting subsets of partial maps. Independently it evaluates the exact formula with rational arithmetic for \(1\le n\le6\), and verifies the predicted counts \(2,7,33,198,1448,12526\). The exhaustive finite checks test the formula at those sizes only; the proof above establishes all dimensions and asymptotics.

## Relationship to prior work
Lipscomb (1986) develops the cycle/path representation and abstract structure of cyclic subsemigroups of symmetric inverse semigroups. The cycle/path decomposition used here is prior work, not newly claimed. Elliott, Levine, and Mitchell (2023) enumerate isomorphism types of monogenic submonoids of the full transformation monoid and of *inverse-generated* monogenic submonoids of the symmetric inverse monoid, distinct from counting embedded ordinary cyclic submonoids. The present theorem studies a labeled embedded counting problem and its uniform-element collision probability and conditional defect distribution. The analogous full-transformation-monoid embedded count concerns all functions, not injective partial maps: it has a different graph decomposition and a different asymptotic scale. Neither that result nor the cited isomorphism classifications directly supplies the present exact count or Poisson defect law.

## Limitations
The contribution is about embedded subsets, not abstract isomorphism classes, and the Poisson law is conditioned on a rare event. The 1986 Lipscomb article is very close in its structural subject; its abstract, not its complete text, was accessible during this literature comparison, so an unrecognized equivalent enumeration remains a substantive priority risk. The exact count is a corollary of the elementary generator-multiplicity principle and the standard path/cycle decomposition; the distinctive additional results are the sharp stretched-exponential scale and the Poisson domain-defect limit. Finite enumeration is not a proof of the large-size assertions.
