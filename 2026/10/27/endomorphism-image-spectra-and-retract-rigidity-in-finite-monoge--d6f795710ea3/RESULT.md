# Endomorphism image spectra and retract rigidity in finite monogenic inverse monoids

## Finding
For integers \(n\ge 2\) and \(k\ge 1\), let \(S_{n,k}\) be the inverse monoid generated by the disjoint union \(x\) of a forward partial chain on \(n\) points and one cyclic permutation on \(k\) points. Endomorphisms throughout mean identity-preserving inverse-monoid homomorphisms (equivalently, unital monoid homomorphisms). Write
\[
B(r)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}j^2=\frac{r(r+1)(2r+1)}6,
\qquad d_h=\frac{k}{\gcd(k,h)},
\qquad r_{b,h}=1+\left\lceil\frac{n-b}{h}\right\rceil.
\]
The *endomorphism image-cardinality polynomial*, counting each endomorphism once, is
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathcal R_{n,k}(z)
&=\sum_{F\in\operatorname{End}(S_{n,k})}z^{|F(S_{n,k})|}\\
&=z+\left(\frac{n(n+1)}2-1\right)z^2
+\sum_{d\mid k}\varphi(d)z^{d+1}\\
&\quad+\sum_{h=1}^{n-1}\sum_{b=h}^{n-1}
2(b-h+1)z^{B(r_{b,h})+d_h}.
\end{aligned}}
\]
Here \(\varphi\) is Euler's totient function, with \(\varphi(1)=1\). The formula includes any collisions between the displayed exponents by coefficient addition. In particular,
\[
\boxed{|\operatorname{End}(S_{n,k})|=B(n)+k,\quad
|\operatorname{Idem}(\operatorname{End}(S_{n,k}))|
=\frac{n(n+1)}2+2^{\omega(k)}+1,\quad
\operatorname{Aut}(S_{n,k})\cong C_2,}
\]
where \(\omega(k)\) is the number of distinct prime divisors, including \(\omega(1)=0\).

**Retract rigidity.** Every proper retract of \(S_{n,k}\) (the image of an idempotent unital endomorphism distinct from the identity endomorphism) is commutative. Proper retracts are singleton monoids, two-element semilattices, or a cyclic group \(C_d\) with a *new* identity adjoined, where \(d\mid k\) is a unitary divisor: \(\gcd(d,k/d)=1\). Such a proper retract can have a nonidentity idempotent, so the adjoined identity and the identity of \(C_d\) are distinct. In particular, no proper noncommutative inverse submonoid can be a retract. The only identity-preserving automorphisms send \(x\) to \(x\) or \(x^{-1}\).

For fixed \(k\), the proportion of idempotent endomorphisms is asymptotic to \(3/(2n)\) as \(n\to\infty\).

## Assumptions and scope
Choose disjoint sets \(P=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\) and \(Q=\{0,\ldots,k-1\}\). The generator \(x\) sends \(i\in P\) to \(i+1\) for \(i<n-1\), is undefined at \(n-1\), and acts on \(Q\) by addition of one modulo \(k\). The inverse monoid is the subset of the symmetric inverse monoid on \(P\sqcup Q\) generated by \(x,x^{-1}\) and the ambient identity. For a monoid map \(F\), its image cardinality is the size of its image as a set, not its rank as a partial permutation. No assumption of primality on \(k\) is made. Only \(n\ge2\) is asserted here; the group/one-link boundary is intentionally outside the statement.

The classical presentation (Elliott--Levine--Mitchell, Theorem 10) is
\[
S_{n,k}=\operatorname{InvMon}\langle x\mid
x^nx^{-n}=x^{n+1}x^{-(n+1)},\quad
x^nx^{-n}=x^nx^{-n}x^k\rangle.
\]
This presentation and the classification of its inverse submonoids generated by one element are prior results, not new claims.

## Proof
**Step 1: Concrete normal forms and size.** The identity is represented by the empty word. For every integer \(0\le b<n\) and \(0\le a,c\le b\), the Munn word \(w_{a,b,c}=x^{-a}x^bx^{-b}x^c\) gives a partial bijection with two parts: (i) on \(P\), the translation by \(t=c-a\) from the interval
\[
I_{a,b}=[a,n-1-b+a]\cap\mathbb Z
\]
(which has \(n-b\) points); and (ii) on \(Q\), rotation by \(t\) modulo \(k\). Two such maps coincide precisely when their triples coincide: the nonempty chain domain recovers \(a,b\), and translation recovers \(c\). Words whose Munn width reaches \(n\) have empty chain component; exactly \(k\) distinct such maps remain, forming a group \(C_k\) supported on \(Q\), with its identity \(e\ne1\). Hence the element set consists of the \(\sum_{b=0}^{n-1}(b+1)^2=B(n)\) nonterminal forms and the \(k\) bottom-group elements. This proves \(|S_{n,k}|=B(n)+k\).

**Step 2: Every element is an admissible endomorphism image.** For any \(y\in S_{n,k}\), its chain part is either an idempotent partial identity or a union of finite, directed, noncyclic links on at most \(n\) points. Consequently its range idempotents \(y^ny^{-n}\) and \(y^{n+1}y^{-(n+1)}\) are equal. Their common restriction to \(Q\) is the identity on \(Q\), and the cyclic action of \(y\) there has order dividing \(k\). Thus \(y^ny^{-n}=y^ny^{-n}y^k\). The displayed defining relations hold with \(x\) replaced by \(y\), so there is a unique unital inverse-monoid endomorphism \(F_y\) taking \(x\) to \(y\). Conversely every endomorphism is determined by its value on \(x\). Therefore \(y\mapsto F_y\) is a bijection, proving the first cardinality identity.

**Step 3: Image sizes.** For the identity \(y=1\), the image is the singleton \(\{1\}\). If \(y=w_{a,b,a}\ne1\) is a nonterminal idempotent, its image is the two-element semilattice \(\{1,y\}\). There are \(\sum_{b=1}^{n-1}(b+1)=n(n+1)/2-1\) of these. If \(y\) is a bottom-group element of order \(d\), its image is \(\{1\}\sqcup C_d\), of size \(d+1\); the number of such elements is \(\varphi(d)\) for each \(d\mid k\).

The remaining normal forms have \(h=|c-a|\ge1\), \(h\le b\le n-1\). Their chain part is translation by \(\pm h\) with a consecutive domain of \(L=n-b\) points. The maximum finite-link length, measured in vertices, is
\[
r_{b,h}=1+\left\lceil L/h\right\rceil.
\]
The group component has order \(d_h=k/\gcd(k,h)\). By the known classification of finite monogenic inverse monoids, the image inverse monoid \(\langle y,y^{-1},1\rangle\) is isomorphic to \(S_{r_{b,h},d_h}\), hence has size \(B(r_{b,h})+d_h\). For fixed \(b,h\), the ordered pairs \((a,c)\) with \(|c-a|=h\) number \(2(b-h+1)\). The four disjoint cases sum to the asserted image polynomial.

**Step 4: Idempotent endomorphisms.** Since \(F_y^2(x)=F_y(y)\), the condition \(F_y^2=F_y\) is equivalent to \(F_y(y)=y\). Every nonterminal idempotent \(y=w_{a,b,a}\) satisfies this (as does \(y=1\)), giving \(n(n+1)/2\) choices. Write a bottom-group element as \(y=g^u\), where \(g\) generates \(C_k\) and \(u\in\mathbb Z/k\mathbb Z\). Direct substitution gives \(F_y(y)=g^{u^2}\), so this case is idempotent precisely when \(u^2\equiv u\pmod{k}\). The Chinese remainder theorem gives exactly \(2^{\omega(k)}\) solutions.

Finally let \(y=w_{a,b,c}\) have nonzero chain translation \(t=c-a\). In the chain component, \(F_y(y)\) is a partial translation by \(t^2\), whereas \(y\) translates by \(t\); equality requires the integer equation \(t^2=t\), hence \(t=1\). For \(t=1\), the chain of \(y\) consists of \(L+1=n-b+1\) vertices, and the Munn word for \(y\) traverses a span of \(b\) edges. Upon replacing its generator by \(y\), the chain domain has at most \(\max(L+1-b,0)\) points. Equality with the original domain size \(L\) is therefore possible only if \(b=1\). The constraints then force \((a,b,c)=(0,1,1)\), i.e. \(y=x\), and \(F_x\) is the identity. Summing these disjoint cases gives the stated count of idempotent endomorphisms.

**Step 5: Automorphisms and retracts.** A surjective \(F_y\) must have image size \(B(n)+k\). By the classification above, the only possible nonterminal images with chain length \(n\) and group order \(k\) come from \(b=1\), \(|c-a|=1\), hence \(y=x\) and \(y=x^{-1}\). These two assignments do give automorphisms, and the second is an involution; therefore \(\operatorname{Aut}(S_{n,k})\cong C_2\). The list in Step 4 shows that every idempotent endomorphism other than \(F_x\) has a commutative image of the types specified in the theorem. For group-type images, the congruence \(u^2\equiv u\pmod{k}\) selects exactly full prime-power factors, giving the unitary divisors of \(k\). Conversely those congruence solutions realize every unitary-divisor type. As \(x x^{-1}\ne x^{-1}x\) on the chain for \(n\ge2\), the whole monoid is noncommutative; all other retractions are proper. Finally the idempotent-endomorphism count divided by \(B(n)+k\) yields the asserted fixed-\(k\) asymptotic.

## Verification
A standalone exact-arithmetic checker, `verify.py`, enumerates the inverse submonoid of partial bijections generated by \(x\), attaches to each element a representative word, and evaluates the putative endomorphisms by word substitution. It computes actual image sets, tests \(F_y^2=F_y\) through the image of \(x\), and identifies automorphisms through surjectivity. It compares all results against the polynomial and arithmetic formulas for \(2\le n\le6\) and \(k\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}\). For \(n\le4\), it additionally verifies preservation of multiplication for every pair of elements under every enumerated \(F_y\). The program returns `VERIFY_OK 25 exact configurations`. These finite tests corroborate but do not replace the all-parameter proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Elliott, Levine and Mitchell (2023, Theorem 10 and Lemmas 6--9) give the defining presentation and classify the isomorphism types of finite monogenic inverse submonoids of symmetric inverse monoids. Preston (1986) classifies monogenic inverse semigroups and their faithful partial-bijection representations. These results are essential structural input, and the counts or classifications of the monoids themselves are not claimed as new. The new assessed assertion concerns their unital self-endomorphisms: an exact image-size spectrum, the complete retraction/idempotent endomorphism census, and the absence of proper noncommutative retracts. Cho and Ruškuc (2024) study finite presentability of subsemigroups of the *free* monogenic inverse semigroup; the problem and domain differ from the finite self-endomorphism classification here.

## Limitations
This theorem treats the standard finite \(S_{n,k}\) with \(n\ge2\), not arbitrary finite inverse semigroups, infinitely generated inverse monoids, or non-unital semigroup homomorphisms. The proof assumes the established inverse-monoid presentation and the classical monogenic classification; it does not establish them anew. Exhaustive publication priority is not asserted: an equivalent retraction census could occur in literature using alternate finite-link or Gluskin-quotient terminology. The checker is finite and cannot establish the universal theorem by enumeration.
