# Scientific review: monogenic inverse-monoid endomorphism spectrum

## Correctness
PASS. The published presentation shows that an element of the finite monoid defines an endomorphism precisely by satisfying two relations; all concrete partial bijections do so. Distinct interval/rotation normal forms supply the exact enumeration. Partial-link lengths and cyclic orders determine image sizes. An independent substitution criterion classifies idempotent endomorphisms, with a Chinese-remainder count on the bottom cyclic group. The proper-retract classification follows by enumerating the images of those idempotents. Exact finite computations include independent verification of multiplication preservation in all configurations with at most four chain vertices.

## Originality
PASS against the inspected literature and available overlapping descriptions. Elliott--Levine--Mitchell (arXiv:2303.12387, Sections 3--4) count *isomorphism classes of monogenic inverse submonoids* and provide their presentations, rather than a self-endomorphism image-size polynomial or the retract-rigidity theorem. Preston (1986, especially Sections 4--5) gives the finite-link representation and type classification, not the stated self-map census. Cho--Ruškuc (arXiv:2404.05244, Sections 1--2 and conclusion) handle finite presentation of subsemigroups of the free object. Generic partial-interval-translation counts alone omit the group component, the substitution criterion, and the retract classification. These comparisons do not prove exhaustive publication priority; an unindexed equivalent result is a residual risk.

## Value
PASS. Images, idempotent endomorphisms, automorphisms and retracts are intrinsic invariants of the monoid. The formula gives the entire image-size distribution in an infinite two-parameter family; the stronger structural result excludes every proper noncommutative retract although proper noncommutative inverse submonoids exist (for instance, a shorter chain generated by a power in sufficiently long cases). The count separates link growth from prime-power choices in the cyclic component, while yielding a concrete asymptotic scarcity of retractions.

## Limitations
The finite-generator presentation and monogenic inverse-submonoid classification are prior inputs. Only unital self-maps of \(S_{n,k}\) for \(n\ge2\) are assessed, not arbitrary inverse semigroups or nonunital homomorphisms. The verification program is a finite corroboration, not an infinite proof. Independent publication-priority review remains necessary.
