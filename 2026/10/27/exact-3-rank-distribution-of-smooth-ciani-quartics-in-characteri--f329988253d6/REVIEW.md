# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS
The claim is a complete enumeration for all \(q=3^m\), not merely an extrapolation from experiments. The geometric smoothness criterion is proved via the associated symmetric ternary quadratic, including coordinate-line and dense-torus singularities. The cohomological Hasse--Witt construction makes its matrix diagonal with entries proportional to \(f+de\), \(be+df\), and \(cd+ef\), so their nonzero count equals stable Frobenius rank, while the \(a\)-number is its codimension. A direct quadratic-character inclusion--exclusion obtains the total \((q-1)^2(q^3-4q^2+6q-5)\), and counts for prescribed vanishing diagonal entries give all four ranks. The independent coefficient expansion and exact brute-force computations over \(\mathbb F_3\) and \(\mathbb F_9\) return the two predicted vectors. The proof, not the computations, establishes the infinite family.

## Originality: PASS (qualified)
Ohashi, *On the maximality of genus-3 nonhyperelliptic curves of Ciani type*, arXiv:2201.12996v1, proves structural results about superspecial Ciani quartics and three elliptic quotient factors. Its smoothness lemma and isogeny are prior and are explicitly used as comparison points; the source does not state the marked all-\(q\), all-rank enumeration. Lercier, Ritzenthaler, Rovetta and Sijsling, arXiv:1403.0562, study isomorphism-class representatives and trace distributions with characteristic assumptions that exclude characteristic three, and their reported numerical sampling concerns primes at least eleven. The previously determined total number of smooth marked Ciani equations does not imply the distribution of Jacobian ranks. The new count is a further, nontrivial partition by Frobenius invariants.

Search comparisons included the full claim and aliases based on Cartier--Manin rank, Hasse--Witt diagonal coefficients, superspeciality, polynomial counts, and finite-field Ciani quartics; they returned no exact or stronger relevant theorem. Negative searches alone are not a proof of originality. Possible equivalent computations in inaccessible classical or unpublished tables remain a residual risk.

## Mathematical value: PASS
The object is the natural six-coefficient Ciani locus of smooth plane quartics; the invariant is the geometric Jacobian \(3\)-rank, a central arithmetic stratification. The result supplies a simultaneous, exact, all-extension-field counting law for all four possible ranks, plus the special behavior that \(\mathbb F_3\) has no ordinary marked members despite larger characteristic-three extension fields having many. The character-sum argument and rank-stratum restrictions are substantive steps beyond either a genus calculation or an individual Hasse--Witt test. The count is not promoted to a claim about moduli isomorphism classes or arbitrary plane quartics.

## Closest literature and limitations
The superspecial portion relates to R. Ohashi's arXiv:2201.12996v1; the method of organizing quartic moduli relates to Lercier--Ritzenthaler--Rovetta--Sijsling, arXiv:1403.0562. The literature comparisons do not guarantee that every historical table has been indexed. The verification script enumerates only \(\mathbb F_3\) and \(\mathbb F_9\); the quantifier over all \(m\ge1\) is discharged in the written proof.
