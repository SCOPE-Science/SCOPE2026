## correctness

PASS

A vanishing pure fourth-power coefficient makes the corresponding vertex singular. Thus A=1 is a unique scalar representative. On a coordinate line a nonvertex singularity corresponds to a null vector of its binary principal block; with nonzero diagonal a singular block has a null vector with both coordinates present. If the binary blocks are nonsingular, any nonzero null vector of the full symmetric matrix has all coordinates nonzero. Over the algebraic closure its coordinates lift by square roots, and the gradient of Q(X²,Y²,Z²) vanishes exactly by the supported block equations. This proves the geometric criterion. For the Cartier/Hasse–Witt calculation the precise semilinear operation cubes the Laurent representative and then multiplies by the equation squared. Mixed basis transitions require an odd exponent, while the three even diagonal target exponents give twice f+de, be+df and cd+ef, respectively. Perfection and diagonality keep the support unchanged under Frobenius iteration, so r is the number of nonzero entries and a=3−r. For the total count there are (q−1)^4 admissible choices of b,c,d,e before f; each of the two excluded f-square equations removes (q−1)^4+(q−1)^2, since the one-pair character sums are −(q−1) and −(q−1)χ(−1) and the double sums factor. Their intersection has (q−1)^2 cases with d=e=0, none with exactly one nonzero, and (q−1)^2(q−2) with both nonzero: normalization by d and e gives w=u+v and (u−v)²=0 in characteristic 3. For prescribed j diagonal zeros, T₁=(q−1)^2(q²−3q+3) since f=−de and with d,e nonzero one takes u,v nonzero, not 1, with uv≠1. T₂=(q−1)^3 because e(b−d²)=0 forces e=f=0, and T₃=(q−1)^2 because d must then vanish. Coordinate permutations followed by rescaling make the stated inclusion-exclusion valid for each prescribed set of j positions. This recovers all four polynomials including 3-rank zero through three over F₃ and F₉. It returned (4,12,0,0) and (64,1344,8064,19584). No uploaded code was run; the finite replay is not a replacement for the all-q proof.
