# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS (self-review).** The spherical addition theorem reduces the exact-degree evaluation rank to a seven-point Legendre Gram matrix. Octahedral parity decomposes that Gram into explicit scalar and two-by-two blocks. A standalone exact-fraction recurrence and an independent rational Gaussian elimination agree on all twenty-nine required finite degrees; the degree-fourteen null functional is verified algebraically. The classical Bernstein–Legendre estimate gives strict diagonal dominance for all degrees at least twenty-nine, so finite tests are not being substituted for the infinite argument.

## Originality

**PASS within bounded literature and indexed-record comparison.** The fourteen-node Lebedev cubature rule and its degree-five integration exactness are established mathematics, not claimed as new. Rogers (arXiv:1411.0011) discusses quadrature and finite-band harmonic representations, while Beentjes (2015) surveys spherical quadrature; the inspected passages do not state this fixed-node, single-high-degree rank classification. Related exact-degree sampling results at other node configurations and indexed finite-field spectral work do not logically imply the present fourteen-node spectrum. A full 1976 historical Lebedev article was not available for inspection, and an equivalent unindexed result remains a residual novelty risk.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The smallest nontrivial two-orbit Lebedev integration grid is a canonical harmonic sampling system. Its exact-degree rank behavior is relevant to spectral aliasing beyond ordinary integration accuracy. The isolated resurgence of an interpolation defect at degree fourteen, its exact missing axis/corner functional, and the sharp all-high-degree threshold are motivated structural facts rather than arbitrary numerical slices.
