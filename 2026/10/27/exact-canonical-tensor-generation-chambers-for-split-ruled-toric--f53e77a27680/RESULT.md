# Exact canonical tensor-generation chambers for split ruled toric threefolds

## Finding
Let \(m\ge0\) and \(a,b\in\mathbb Z\). On the Hirzebruch surface \(B=\mathbb F_m\), let \(S\) be a section with \(S^2=-m\) and \(F\) a ruling fiber. Put \(L=\mathcal O_B(aS+bF)\) and let \(Y=\mathbb P_B(\mathcal O_B\oplus L)\), in the convention that the projective bundle parametrizes one-dimensional quotients. The canonical bundle \(\omega_Y\) is tensor-generating (its positive and negative tensor powers generate \(\operatorname{Perf}(Y)\)) if and only if
\[
 (a-2)(b-m-2)>0,\qquad (a+2)(b+m+2)>0,
\]
\[
 |b-am|>m-2,\qquad |b-am|\ne |m-2|.
\]
In particular the last condition is redundant for \(m\ne1\), while for \(m=1\) it excludes exactly the additional wall \(|b-a|=1\). This yields a complete integer-parameter classification for every smooth threefold of the displayed split-bundle form.

The first genuinely rank-two phenomenon outside the anti-ampleness hypothesis of Ito–Zeng–Zeng, Proposition 6.10, occurs, for instance, for \(m=2,a=3,b=5\). Here \(\omega_Y\) tensor-generates, although neither presentation of the bundle obtained by interchanging \(\mathcal O_B\) and \(L\) satisfies the paper's sufficient anti-ampleness condition.

## Assumptions and scope
All varieties are over an algebraically closed field (the fan proof works over any field for which the cited toric criterion holds). The numerical parameters are arbitrary integers subject only to \(m\ge0\). Tensor-generating means that the collection of all integral tensor powers of the invertible sheaf classically generates the perfect derived category. We do not classify arbitrary non-split rank-two bundles or non-toric threefolds.

## Proof
We apply the invariant-orbit-closure criterion for tensor-generation proved in Ito–Zeng–Zeng, Theorems 3.1 and 3.3: for a smooth complete toric variety, an invertible sheaf is tensor-generating exactly when its restriction to every torus-invariant orbit closure is big or anti-big. We check every orbit dimension, not merely the invariant curves.

Let \(\pi:Y\to B\) be the projection and \(\xi=c_1(\mathcal O_Y(1))\). The quotient convention gives
\[
 K_B=-2S-(m+2)F,\qquad K_Y=-2\xi+\pi^*(K_B+L).
\]
The two invariant section divisors \(E_0,E_L\), associated respectively to the quotient maps onto \(\mathcal O_B\) and \(L\), are each isomorphic to \(B\). Their restricted canonical divisor classes are \(K_B+L\) and \(K_B-L\). The pseudoeffective cone of \(B\) is generated by \(S,F\); therefore \(uS+vF\) is big (respectively anti-big) precisely when \(u,v>0\) (respectively \(u,v<0\)). Applying this to the two restrictions produces exactly
\[
 (a-2)(b-m-2)>0,\qquad(a+2)(b+m+2)>0.
\]

For each of the four torus-invariant curves \(C\subset B\), the vertical invariant surface is \(V_C=\pi^{-1}(C)=\mathbb P_{\mathbb P^1}(\mathcal O\oplus\mathcal O(\ell))\), where \(k=K_B\cdot C\) and \(\ell=L\cdot C\). On it, \(K_Y=-2\xi+(k+\ell)f\), with \(f\) the fiber divisor. This restriction is never big, because its degree on a ruling fiber is negative. To detect bigness of its inverse, use the projection formula for every \(n>0\):
\[
 h^0(V_C,\mathcal O(-nK_Y))
 =\sum_{j=0}^{2n}\max\bigl(0,j\ell-n(k+\ell)+1\bigr).
\]
This grows quadratically in \(n\) exactly when \(|\ell|-k>0\), since the maximum summand's linear coefficient is \(|\ell|-k\), while all other summands differ from it by at most \(2n|\ell|\). Equivalently, if \(|\ell|-k>0\), a positive-length interval of indices contributes order-\(n\) terms; if \(|\ell|-k\le0\), all nonconstant coefficients are nonpositive and the sum grows at most linearly. For the two fiber curves \(C=F\), the pair is \((k,\ell)=(-2,a)\). For the negative section \(C=S\), it is \((m-2,b-am)\). For the positive section \(C=S+mF\), it is \((-m-2,b)\). Thus the only additional divisorial condition is \(|b-am|>m-2\).

The invariant curves in \(Y\) are the four \(\pi\)-fibers over invariant fixed points of \(B\), with canonical degree \(-2\), and the eight section curves lying over the four invariant curves of \(B\). The latter have respective canonical degrees \((K_B+L)\cdot C\) and \((K_B-L)\cdot C\). Along the two fibers of \(B\) these are \(-2\pm a\); along the positive section they are \(-m-2\pm b\); and along the negative section they are \(m-2\pm(b-am)\). A line bundle on a complete curve is big or anti-big exactly when its degree is nonzero. The first two groups of nonvanishing conditions are already imposed by the strict two-product inequalities. The last group gives exactly \(|b-am|\ne|m-2|\).

Finally, \(-K_Y\) is big: for the complete smooth fan of \(Y\), the anticanonical polytope \(\{u:\langle u,v_\rho\rangle\ge-1\ \text{for all rays}\}\) contains a neighborhood of the origin, so it has full dimension. The fixed torus points impose no restriction (every line bundle on a point is big). Thus the displayed four inequalities are necessary and sufficient by the orbit-closure criterion. When \(m=0\), the curve condition follows from \(b\ne\pm2\); when \(m\ge2\), it follows from the strict vertical-surface condition. The sole surviving extra wall occurs at \(m=1\), as asserted.

For the concrete \((m,a,b)=(2,3,5)\), the products are \(1\) and \(45\), and \(|b-am|=1>0\). Yet \(K_B+L=S+F\) is not anti-ample, while in the swapped presentation \(K_B-L=-5S-9F\) is not anti-ample because \(5S+9F\) intersects the negative section by \(-1\). This proves the claimed strict enlargement beyond the source's sufficient range even after interchanging the summands.

## Verification
The attached dependency-free Python 3 program `verify_rank2.py` builds the six-ray smooth complete fan, checks the exact rational strict-polytope bigness test for all six invariant surfaces, and evaluates wall relations for all twelve invariant curves. It compares this independent fan implementation to the closed-form inequalities for every \(0\le m\le4\), \(-6\le a\le6\), \(-9\le b\le9\) (1,235 triples) and tests the witness \((2,3,5)\). The final output begins with `VERIFY_OK`. This is a finite diagnostic check, not the proof for all parameters; the preceding orbit-closure argument establishes the universal statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Ito–Zeng–Zeng (2026), Theorems 3.1 and 3.3, give the general toric orbit-closure criterion used as an input. Their Proposition 6.10 supplies a sufficient criterion for \(\mathbb P_X(\mathcal O_X^{\oplus n}\oplus L)\), but explicitly asserts its converse only when \(n\ge2\); Example 6.11 computes its sufficient conditions over Hirzebruch surfaces for general \(n\), and Example 6.12 exhibits a separate rank-two example. The result above gives a complete necessary-and-sufficient closed parameter classification specifically for the unresolved \(n=1\) class over all \(\mathbb F_m\), including a witness not satisfying either orientation of the earlier anti-ampleness sufficient condition. It is a specialized consequence of their general combinatorial criterion, not a claim to a new general tensor-generation criterion. Ito–Olander (2025) proved the general derived Nakai–Moishezon theorem and classified the canonical case on ruled *surfaces*; their surface classification does not directly give this threefold parameter chamber.

## Limitations
The theorem uses the general tensor-generation criterion from the originating paper and standard toric divisor computations. Its originality is the closed all-parameter rank-two classification and the failure of the special anti-ampleness characterization, not a new criterion for arbitrary toric varieties. The embedded finite verification cannot independently certify all integer triples or historical priority. We did not prove any statement about non-split projective bundles or general deformations. Earlier unpublished computations may overlap with the closed formula.
