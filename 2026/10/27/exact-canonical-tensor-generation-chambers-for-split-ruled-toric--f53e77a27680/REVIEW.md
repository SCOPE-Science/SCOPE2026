## Correctness
PASS. The two distinguished sections have canonical restrictions \(K_B\pm L\), whose big/anti-big cones give the first two inequalities. The four vertical surfaces have anticanonical section growth governed exactly by \(\lvert L\cdot C\rvert-K_B\cdot C\), yielding one new strict wall. The twelve invariant curves have explicitly computed canonical degrees; their nonvanishing contributes only the remaining negative-section wall. The full anticanonical bundle is big by the toric polytope argument. A dependency-free exact fan calculation checks local restriction-polytope feasibility and all wall degrees against the theorem on 1,235 parameter triples. The finite grid is corroboration only.

## Originality
PASS with priority uncertainty. Ito–Zeng–Zeng Theorems 3.1/3.3 imply an algorithm for all toric fans, and their Proposition 6.10 explicitly stops asserting the converse at rank two. Their Example 6.11 discusses sufficient inequalities over Hirzebruch surfaces, not the necessary-and-sufficient threefold region. The resulting complete four-wall parameter classification and the example outside the anti-ampleness range even after swapping summands are not stated there. Ito–Olander 2025 classifies a different dimension (ruled surfaces). Published-record searches disclosed no equivalent explicit classification. The classification is a new specialization of a stronger general theorem, not a new general tensor criterion; unlocated prior calculations remain possible.

## Value
PASS. The rank-two converse is an explicit boundary of Proposition 6.10, and its failure cannot be diagnosed from that proposition's sufficient condition. The closed necessary-and-sufficient parameter formula classifies a natural infinite threefold family without running per-fan linear programs; it explains a codimension-one wall unique to \(\mathbb F_1\), and supplies a concrete threefold missed by both summand orientations of the sufficient criterion. These are intrinsic classification data for the canonical reconstruction property, not arbitrary integer slices.
