# Scientific review: exact census of rank-two recovery balance

**Correctness — PASS.** For a nonzero target projective direction, the event of nonrecovery in a Poissonized sample is exactly the disjoint union of the events with zero or one *other* occupied direction observed. Integrating the resulting exponential survival function gives a strictly multiplicity-dependent coordinate expectation. Balance therefore forces all occupied direction multiplicities to agree; zero columns are excluded. A free change-of-basis action produces the exact code counts, and Stirling asymptotics yield the divisor-dependent rarity. The standalone verifier cross-checks independent subset-based expectations and exhaustively enumerates all small prime-field generator matrices. Finite experiments are supporting checks, not the proof for arbitrary prime powers.

**Originality — PASS, with priority risk.** The August 2026 generalized random-access source motivates finite-geometric expectation methods, and the subsequent October 2026 matroidal source studies coordinate-recovery balance and the proportion of sparse-paving matroids. Its general expectation formula, and earlier coordinate-recovery formulas, imply the elementary rank-two local expectation identity. They do not give the exact enumeration of dimension-two balanced linear **code subspaces**, the probability under either natural code ensemble, or the divisor-dependent asymptotic dichotomy. An accessible mathematical record on rank-two coverage depth addresses optimizing complete-message recovery, not balanced coordinate recovery. Precise claim and alias searches of public mathematical research records did not identify an equivalent population theorem. The arithmetic census could conceivably be implicit in older rank-two matroid or coding enumerations; no priority certification is asserted.

**Value — PASS.** Recent 2026 geometric and matroidal work motivates prevalence of recovery balance and observes that matroid-wide counting need not address representable objects. The exact count resolves that frequency question completely for the first nontrivial code dimension over every finite field. The alternating polynomial, exponential, and identically zero behaviors with length give a structural answer unavailable from a yes/no balance test or a full-recovery optimum alone. No claim is made about high-dimensional families or practical speedups.
