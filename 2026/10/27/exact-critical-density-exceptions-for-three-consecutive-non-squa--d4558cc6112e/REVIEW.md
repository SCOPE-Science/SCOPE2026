# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** The critical-density condition forces \(q-1=2^a3^b\), making the non-square non-primitive set a single sixth-power coset. The reconstructed three-character expansion and Weil bound establish existence for every \(q>186624\). The embedded standalone exact checker exhaustively enumerates all \(31\) eligible prime powers at or below the cutoff, including all three quadratic extension fields, with an independent coset-membership test. Twelve and only twelve fail.

**Originality (PASS, subject to residual literature risk).** Cohen's 2026 paper classifies consecutive pairs at density \(1/3\); Zhou and Wu's 2026 triple theorem treats density less than or equal to \(4/15\) and has a different exceptional list. Neither implies the exact twelve exceptions at density \(1/3\). The 2024 Cohen paper counts nonsquare triples, not triples with the additional non-primitive condition. Direct searches of research records and indexed literature did not find an identical statement, though non-indexed cyclotomic tables could carry the data implicitly.

**Mathematical value (PASS).** The critical value \(1/3\) is the natural sharp boundary of the recent theorem for pairs, and understanding precisely how that boundary changes for triples is an explicit structural gap left by the two subsequent theorems. The complete twelve-exception classification, including extension fields, shows seven additional triple obstructions beyond the five pair obstructions; it is not an arbitrary numerical cutoff.

**Scope and risk.** This is a classification only for fields whose multiplicative-order prime support is \(\{2,3\}\). It does not strengthen the general sharp \(4/15\) density threshold. A hidden equivalent older cyclotomic-number table remains possible.
