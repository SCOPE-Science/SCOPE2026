# Review of the exact one-marker DFA separation theorem

**Correctness (PASS).** The theorem precisely specifies the word class, marker positions, complete DFA and minimization. The three upper-bound automata give explicit distinguishing terminal states. The lower bound exhausts the only two cases for the zero-transition orbit: transient at most the number of states minus two, or maximal transient, which forces a constant sink after the required suffix length. The global threshold equivalence follows from elementary divisibility and optimal placement. The attached exhaustive checker independently confirms small state budgets and sharp finite thresholds; all-integer correctness is proved by the symbolic argument, not by enumeration.

**Originality (PASS, subject to priority risk).** Full primary text of Robson (1989), Demaine–Eisenstat–Shallit–Wilson (2011), and Ebrahimnejad (2016), and full HTML of Nicol (2026) were compared for exact hypotheses and implications. The 2011 paper provides the left/right boundary upper constructions separately and logarithmic hard instances, but neither the minimum with the least nondivisor nor the sharp exact separation for arbitrary marker positions. The later all-word bounds and reversal constructions do not imply the equality. Search against published mathematical research records and equivalent search strings found no direct match. Possible unindexed or older equivalent work, especially Goralčík–Koubek (1986), remains a residual priority risk; search absence alone is not evidence of originality.

**Value (PASS).** This completely solves a natural sparse but nontrivial subfamily of the classical DFA-separation problem. It isolates three genuinely different mechanisms, characterizes precisely when each dominates, and derives the first blocklength needed to force any given number of states via an arithmetic lcm threshold. The result extends beyond known asymptotic logarithmic examples into a transparent all-parameter structural classification.

**Scope and limitations.** No claim is made for words with multiple markers or for arbitrary pairs; full classification of optimal automata is not attempted. Finite checker output is corroborative. Publication priority and the proof should receive an independent expert audit before being represented as independently validated.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
