# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

In the nondominant regime, the polygon construction is available precisely because the longest length u_i² is at most the sum of the others. In the induction proving that lemma the attainable resultant radii form a connected interval when a segment is added; the minimum is the distance of its length to the previous attainable interval, which gives the claimed formula. The resulting Gram matrix H is PSD with trace one, diagonal u_i²/S and Hu=0. For every positive diagonal P the tests with u and H give a common denominator W=Σu_i²p_i in the bounds S(1+S)/W and S/W, proving the ratio bound 1+S attained at identity. In the dominant regime with R>0 the restricted Rayleigh problem on the dominant coordinate and the normalized tail is two-dimensional, and the restricted P is diagonal even for asymmetric tail entries. The off-diagonal correlation magnitude is invariant. The trace-squared/determinant inequality makes equal scaled diagonals optimal on this plane and is strict at identity. The displayed P_* balances them and leaves the orthogonal complement at eigenvalue 1; the lower planar eigenvalue is less than 1 by M>R and the upper is greater than 1, so the full condition number equals the lower bound. The diagonal R=0 and zero-vector cases, threshold equality and signs check directly. In the Jacobi example the lesser planar eigenvalue exceeds 1/2, so the stated ratio uses the correct minimum eigenvalue.

## originality

PASS

Pass restricted to the distinct all-real-vector, arbitrary-tail theorem, especially the nonsymmetric nondominant certificate. The equal-tail one-spike specialization, including its exact optimum and one-coordinate design, is already covered by an earlier Resultary theorem. The general Jacobi-worsening phenomenon is also not new. The earlier one-spike theorem does not extend by a substitution or admissible symmetry to arbitrary tails, and no inspected statement supplies that extension mechanically.

## value

PASS

Solves a natural conditioning problem over independently adjustable diagonal entries, without requiring numerical optimization. In particular, determining whether a scale can help at all, and finding an optimal one-entry change otherwise, is actionable for general real rank-one updates with heterogeneous tails. The polygon certificate explains why even strongly asymmetric scale choices cannot help below the threshold. This is value beyond the already-known one-spike formula and the illustration about Jacobi.

The dated certificate retains the supplied scientific assessment, sources and limitations.
