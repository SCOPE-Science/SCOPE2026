# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact distance lower bound counts each necessary target-edge insertion and, separately, a first non-target insertion for each original alternating cycle. The construction realizes the bound by flipping the unique alternating path and then switching each cycle using the exposed vertex as a temporary pivot. The generating function uses disjoint labeled matching blocks; ordering and orientation of path blocks gives \(2^k k!\), and cycles of \(j\) blocks have \(2^{j-1}(j-1)!\) possibilities. The extremal degree follows because each nontrivial cycle consumes at least two blocks. Two genuinely different finite computations confirm all five smaller instances. Risks: the scripts are finite evidence; they are not substituted for the unrestricted proof.

(2025). Exact complete-graph distance distribution is not the same assertion as their general connectedness and linear upper bound, nor as Bousquet et al. (2018)'s general matching-distance complexity results. Brenner et al. (2026) explicitly study this same complete-graph flip graph and prove Hamilton-connectedness; its full 44-page open-access preprint was inspected, including the stated combinatorial Hamilton-connectedness theorem and related definitions; the exact distance polynomial is not established there. Publication-priority uncertainty remains for differently indexed results. Search did not establish uniqueness. The new claim is the full labeled polynomial and its antipodal enumeration, not merely rederiving connectivity or the cycle lower bound. The comparison distinguishes graph-invariant implication from coincident keywords.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Exact distance layers give the complete geodesic profile of a natural highly symmetric reconfiguration graph studied in current combinatorial graph theory. The coefficient formula is an infinite-family labeled enumeration with an explicit decomposition into alternate cycles and the exposed-vertex path. It yields sharply the number of farthest configurations, rather than only a diameter upper bound. These are natural invariants for optimization of flip sequences and reconfiguration navigation; no unsupported downstream theorem is claimed.
