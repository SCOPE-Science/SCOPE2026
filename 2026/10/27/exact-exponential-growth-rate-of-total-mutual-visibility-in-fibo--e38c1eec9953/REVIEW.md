# Scientific review: Fibonacci-cube total mutual visibility

**Correctness — PASS.** The claim uses the exact definition of all-pairs shortest-path visibility. The rank-two midpoint lemma is proved by a length-preserving path surgery, and the Fibonacci cube's isometric embedding justifies restricting distance-two common-neighbor sets to one or two vertices. The two-flip analysis proves the singleton zero-run obstruction in both directions. The explicit square-diagonal conflict graph therefore classifies all admissible sets. A positive rational generating function counts exactly the eligible words; a greedy independent set gives the lower bound and a pole calculation gives the asserted exponential rate. The standalone verifier checks every candidate subset by direct geodesics for dimensions \(2\) through \(6\), plus independently checks conflict polynomials through dimension \(14\). Finite computations are corroboration, not an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS on accessible evidence, with a material priority risk.** The closest established full-text theorem, Bujtás–Klavžar–Tian (arXiv:2307.05168, Theorem 6), characterizes total visibility in full Hamming graphs. It cannot simply be transferred to Fibonacci cubes, whose deleted hypercube vertices create unique distance-two intermediates. Cicerone et al. (2025) study ordinary visibility in Fibonacci cubes but leave total visibility open. An announced Podrug–Bujtás–Klavžar–Tan talk on *Total mutual visibility in Fibonacci cubes* explicitly promises bounds and small-dimensional values; its accessible abstract gives neither the forbidden-run enumerator nor the exponential root, and the underlying proof is not accessible. This is a specific unresolved priority risk: the announced bounds could imply or subsume the present theorem. No publication novelty certification or independent priority validation is asserted.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The exact exponential rate \(1.465571231876768\ldots\) answers a natural asymptotic question for an explicitly identified, previously open network family; the rate is strictly below the golden-ratio growth of the whole Fibonacci cube. The analysis identifies all individually feasible vertices and reduces the global geodesic condition to independent sets in an explicit sparse conflict graph. The method gives quantitatively matching exponential upper and lower bounds, not merely a small-dimensional census.

**Scope and risks.** The polynomial factor between the bounds remains unresolved; no all-dimensional exact total mutual-visibility number is claimed. The 2026 talk is an overlap risk; the 2025 publisher PDF was inaccessible, although indexed article sections, the introduction, Fibonacci section, and conclusion were examined. Exact finite values are verification output rather than a separate priority assertion. Peer review and independent audit have not been performed.
