# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The claim applies to every integer dimension \(n\geq2\), every conjugate-linear coefficient matrix, and every rank \(1\leq k\leq n\). The diagonal trace depends only on the complex-symmetric coefficient part. Takagi factorization converts its modulus to a quadratic form in an orthonormal frame; projection row-norm bounds and the decreasing-rearrangement inequality establish the exact upper radius, attained by the top singular directions. Explicit orthonormal zero-trace frames cover rank one, even ranks, odd ranks including the full-rank case, and singular degeneracies. Path-connectedness of unitary frames plus rotation fills every closed disk. Consecutive radius differences give the inverse singular-spectrum formula. Computational checks provide supplementary tests, not a substitute for these infinite-quantifier arguments.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Majumdar and Müller, arXiv:2610.16823v1, Definition 7.1 and Theorem 7.2, give the underlying normalized trace range and an infinite-dimensional centered-disk theorem. Their stated result lacks the full finite-dimensional rank profile, exact radii, and inverse Takagi reconstruction. Kołaczek and Müller (2024) cover the ordinary single-vector disk only; their 2025 survey treats unnormalized ordinary numerical ranges, not multi-frame trace profiles. Chan--Li--Poon's \(k\)-numerical theory concerns complex-linear operators, where the top-rank range is a singleton; it is not a dominating formulation of the antilinear top-rank disk. Targeted index searches and recovery of available earlier claims did not reveal a covering result. There remains a legitimate risk that standard matrix congruence theory or unindexed literature already records an equivalent combined formula; no uniqueness or independence is claimed.

## Value — PASS
This gives a complete, naturally parameterized **exact invariant profile** rather than an arbitrary slice: it determines the trace disks at all ranks of a construction introduced in recent literature and supplies an inverse problem with a closed-form solution. Rank-dependent radii separate symmetric coefficient spectra that the ordinary numerical range cannot distinguish (for example, the common maximum \(s_1\) does not reveal \(s_2\)). The disk at top rank, unlike the singleton of ordinary linear \(k\)-numerical theory, makes the distinction sharp, and the skew-invisibility theorem delineates the maximal recoverable information. The result requires only finite-dimensional Takagi/Ky Fan tools but the all-rank zero-frame construction and exact information classification are substantive, motivated facts. No broader theorem or priority beyond this scope is claimed.
