# Scientific review: cyclic Bergman kernel spectral staircase

## Correctness
**PASS.** The stated normalization gives the analytic Bergman kernel \((1-z\overline w)^{-2}\). Absolute power-series convergence and cyclic orthogonality yield the finite Fourier eigenvalue formula. The strictly decreasing adjacent eigenvalue ratios identify every maximum and its possible two-mode tie. The substitution \(h=-\log q\), together with strict hyperbolic-sine superlinearity, proves that every transition is unique. Strict logarithmic concavity and a factored endpoint comparison determine the unique least mode. Uniform critical-scale expansions justify passage from exact finite spectra to the continuous profile and to sharp limiting Riesz bounds. The standalone checker passed 177 independently evaluated finite instances. Its samples are corroboration, not the infinite proof.

## Originality
**PASS, subject to residual bibliographic risk.** The most closely related 2022 Bergman sampling paper treats interpolation and density geometry, without finite regular-polygon Gram spectra. A published Szegő/Hardy circular-kernel result has an explicit geometric spectrum but no Bergman interior maximal mode. A related two-ring Bergman result compares relative angular placements, not the radius-driven single-ring extremal-mode staircase or its fixed-critical-parameter full spectral limit. A weighted harmonic Bergman circle-trace frequency staircase concerns a different continuous functional. No equivalent complete phase diagram or explicit critical condition-number limit was identified in the searched and inspected works. Because standard kernel expansions can produce the finite eigenvalue formula, the original scientific claim rests on the complete comparison and limiting classification; earlier unindexed calculations remain a priority risk.

## Value
**PASS.** Finite normalized reproducing-kernel Gram matrices directly determine interpolation stability. The result specifies, for every node count and radius, both sharp Riesz bounds and the exact noise-amplifying Fourier mode, including all transition degeneracies; the radius parameter induces a genuinely nonmonotone optimizing-mode phenomenon absent for Hardy kernels. It additionally determines a complete uniform spectral profile and a closed critical-scale conditioning law, including a mode fraction and a nontrivial finite positive limiting condition number. These are intrinsic answers to the natural cyclic sampling problem, not arbitrary parameter slices or merely numerical values.

## Closest literature and limitations
Aadi–Cruz–Hartmann–Kellay, *Multiple sampling and interpolation in Bergman spaces*, arXiv:2202.07263 (2022), provides the normalized Bergman-kernel and interpolation setting. Publicly available results on Szegő orbit frames, harmonic Bergman circle traces, and two concentric point rings provide meaningful comparisons but do not imply the simultaneous staircase and critical profile without additional work. Standard power-series and circulant matrix calculations are classical ingredients. The theorem excludes irregular polygons, different kernel powers, infinite multiring sampling, and nonfixed critical parameters. Neither the checker nor the literature search establishes historical uniqueness.
