For normalized unweighted Bergman kernels at the vertices of a regular disk polygon, the largest Gram eigenmode climbs through an exact, gap-free staircase as the radius grows, while the smallest mode is always the highest Fourier residue. At the critical scale \(r_N^2=1-c/N\), the entire spectrum converges uniformly to an explicit one-hump profile and its sharp condition number tends to \(\frac{e^c-1}{c}\exp(-1+c/(e^c-1))\).
