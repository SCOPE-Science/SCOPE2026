# Exact generator rank of mixed singleton–doubleton regular centralizers

## Finding
For each integer \(b\ge1\), let \(X_b=\{0\}\sqcup\bigcup_{i=1}^{b}\{(i,0),(i,1)\}\), and let \(e:X_b\to X_b\) be the idempotent with \(e(0)=0\) and \(e(i,0)=e(i,1)=(i,0)\). The centralizer monoid \(C_b=\{f:X_b\to X_b:fe=ef\}\) has minimum semigroup generating-set cardinality
\[
\operatorname{rank} C_b=\begin{cases}2,&b=1,\\4,&b=2,\\5,&b\ge3.\end{cases}
\]
The cardinality is \(|C_b|=(b+1)(2b+1)^b\). Explicit optimal generating sets are given below, including a structurally necessary trio of nonunit types.

## Assumptions and scope
All maps are *full* maps on \(X_b\); multiplication is function application from left to right, so \(fg\) means first \(f\), then \(g\). Rank means the least cardinality of a semigroup generating set (not image rank). Let \(B=\{0,1,\ldots,b\}\) index the kernel blocks. The distinguished section contains \(0\) and \((i,0)\) for positive \(i\). These definitions apply for every integer \(b\ge1\), and the generated monoid includes its identity as an ordinary nonempty word.

## Proof
**1. Normal form.** Commutation with \(e\) is equivalent to preserving its kernel blocks and sending distinguished representatives to distinguished representatives. Thus every \(f\in C_b\) is uniquely encoded by a map \(\sigma:B\to B\) and bits \(\varepsilon_i\in\{0,1\}\), one for each positive source block, constrained by \(\varepsilon_i=0\) whenever \(\sigma(i)=0\). The action is \(f(0)=\sigma(0)\) as a distinguished representative, \(f(i,0)=(\sigma(i),0)\), and \(f(i,1)=(\sigma(i),\varepsilon_i)\), interpreting a target index of zero as the singleton point \(0\). Extend each bit vector by \(\varepsilon_0=0\). For successive maps \((\sigma,\varepsilon)\) and \((\tau,\delta)\), the index map is \(\tau\circ\sigma\) and its positive-source bits are \(\varepsilon_i\delta_{\sigma(i)}\). There are \(b+1\) choices of \(\sigma(0)\); independently each positive source index has one singleton-target choice or two choices for each positive target, giving \((2b+1)^b\) possibilities. The units are exactly the permutations of positive block indices, with index \(0\) fixed and all bits one; their group is \(\operatorname{Sym}(b)\).

**2. Explicit generators.** For \(b\ge2\), take a transposition of positive indices; for \(b\ge3\), additionally take a positive-index \(b\)-cycle. These generate all units. Define three nonunits by their index maps and bits: \(t\) swaps indices \(0\) and \(1\), with bit \(\varepsilon_1=0\) and every other positive bit one; \(z\) sends index \(0\) to \(1\), fixes all positive indices and has all positive bits one; and, when \(b\ge2\), \(c\) sends index \(2\) to \(1\), fixes the remaining indices and has all positive bits one. The proposed set contains \(2\), \(4\), or \(5\) elements in the three stated cases.

Conjugation by units relocates all positive-index labels in \(t,z,c\). In particular, \(w_j=z_jt_j\) fixes index zero and sends positive index \(j\) to zero; and \(d_j=t_j^2\) fixes every index and deletes only the bit at positive source \(j\). Collapses \(c_{j\to k}\) together with positive permutations generate every transformation of the positive index set. Deletions \(w_j\), applied before collapses, therefore generate every index map \(h\) fixing zero, with all nonzero-target bits one.

It remains to realize an arbitrary index map \(\sigma\) taking zero to a positive index \(a\), while keeping all possible positive bits one. If some positive index \(j\) satisfies \(\sigma(j)=a\), first apply \(z_j\), then a zero-fixing map \(h\) satisfying \(h(j)=a\) and \(h(i)=\sigma(i)\) on the other positive indices. If no such \(j\) exists, but some positive \(j\) satisfies \(\sigma(j)=0\), first apply \(t_j\), then choose \(h(0)=0\), \(h(j)=a\), and \(h(i)=\sigma(i)\) otherwise. The single bit destroyed by \(t_j\) was destined for zero, hence is required to vanish. Finally, if no positive index maps to either \(0\) or \(a\), the pigeonhole principle supplies distinct positive indices \(j,k\) with \(\sigma(j)=\sigma(k)\). Apply \(c_{j\to k}t_j\), then a zero-fixing \(h\) with \(h(j)=a\), \(h(k)=\sigma(k)\), and the prescribed images on the other positive indices. Because the collapse first vacates index \(j\), the subsequent swap loses no positive-source bit. All three cases yield the maximal permissible bit vector. Precomposing with selected \(d_j\) deletes exactly the remaining bits required for any desired \(f\). Thus the listed generators produce every element of \(C_b\).

**3. Lower bound.** In a finite transformation monoid, a product can be a unit only when all its factors are units. Consequently at least \(0,1,2\) unit generators are needed for \(b=1,2,\ge3\), respectively. For \(b\ge2\), three further nonunit types are independently indispensable. To produce \(t\), one factor must induce a permutation on \(B\) moving index zero; units cannot do that, and no nonbijective index map can occur in such a product. To produce \(z\), all positive-source blocks must retain distinct positive targets and their full bits; hence a factor moving zero into the positive indices while preserving these \(b\) distinct positive indices and bits is needed. This cannot be the same type as the permutation-moving-zero factor \(t\), which necessarily sends a positive block to zero and destroys its bit. To produce \(c\), zero must remain fixed and all positive-source bits must survive, yet two distinct positive indices must merge. Consider the first factor in a word where distinct positive-source indices merge: before that factor all these indices are distinct, nonzero and their bits survive; this factor therefore collapses two positive indices without sending either to zero. It is neither of the previous two types. Thus at least three nonunits are necessary. For \(b=1\), the constructed pair generates \(C_1\) and a one-generated monoid would be commutative, whereas \(t\) and \(z\) do not commute. This proves the claimed rank in all cases.

## Verification
An independently implemented full-map enumerator checks the centralizer commutation condition, enumerates its members, and closes the asserted generator set under multiplication for \(1\le b\le4\). The observed cardinalities are \(6,75,1372,32805\), matching \((b+1)(2b+1)^b\). For \(b=2\), all \(2628\) sets consisting of a unit generator and two nonunit generators are also tested and fail to generate. Execute `python verify.py` with Python 3 and no dependencies. These finite checks corroborate the universal constructive argument; they do not replace it.

## Relationship to prior work
Araújo, Bentz, Mitchell and Schneider (2014 preprint, 2015 journal) explicitly pose determining the rank of regular section-and-partition-preserving transformation semigroups in Problems 5.1–5.3. Their preceding classification concerns all partition-preserving full maps, which is larger than the section-preserving centralizer here. The all-equal-two-element-block case has separate wreath-product relative-rank coverage in subsequent research and is not included in the novelty assertion: the present theorem specifically requires one singleton block together with an arbitrary number of doubletons. The rank formula is therefore not a substitution into the uniform wreath-product theorem.

## Limitations
The result addresses only the mixed kernel type \((1,2,\ldots,2)\), not arbitrary idempotent centralizers or all regular section-preserving semigroups. The strongest historical-priority risk is an uninspected equivalent specialization in earlier work on idempotent centralizers (especially Araújo and Konieczny, 2004, and André–Araújo–Konieczny, 2011). Their accessible descriptions concern structural properties and idempotent generation; their full relevant content was not obtained. The 2014 open-problem statement and other search comparisons support, but cannot definitively establish, priority. Independent mathematical auditing has not occurred.
