# Review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The Artin–Schreier genus formula gives genus four. The absolute-trace criterion proves that the affine equation has no points over either \(\mathbf F_4\) or \(\mathbf F_{64}\), and the smooth projective model has one point above infinity. Thus \(N_1=N_3=1\), whence \(B_3=0\). Aubry–Herbaut–Monaldi's general estimate gives \(\rho(4,4)\le4\); the witness gives the reverse inequality.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to the stated literature-search limits.** The primary paper tabulates exact values only for genera one through three and does not state \(\rho(4,4)\). published-finding corpus queries for the exact threshold, the equation, and equivalent DS-curve formulations returned no matching claim. Accessible own ledgers were searched for the same equation and threshold. Lario's genus-four DS resource is a relevant comparison, but its overview says that for base fields other than \(\mathbf F_2\) it lists candidate Weil polynomials rather than a complete list of explicit models; no inspected source directly states this curve or the exact threshold. This does not certify uniqueness.

## Value

**PASS.** The invariant \(\rho(q,g)\) is posed explicitly in the literature, and the paper's exact table stops at genus three. The example closes the specific \((q,g)=(4,4)\) case by matching a general upper bound with a concrete, trace-certified obstruction at degree three. It is a sharp threshold result, not merely a point-count table or a claim about an arbitrary finite search.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest primary source is Aubry–Herbaut–Monaldi, arXiv:2310.11218v1, which supplies the definition and upper bound but not this genus-four value. Lario's DS-curves resource is relevant to curves with no points gained over a cubic extension; the inspected overview does not provide an explicit model for this case. A fuller search of all candidate genus-four Weil polynomials over \(\mathbf F_4\) could reveal an earlier equivalent example.
