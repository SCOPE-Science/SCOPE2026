# Mathematical review

## Correctness
The normalized Hardy kernel overlap forces real collinearity and yields exactly two Möbius-related companion kernels for a fixed output parameter. The real geometric-series expression is monotone in their product, so one branch wins globally. Its one-variable derivative has quartic numerator with a derivative of fixed negative sign throughout the open unit interval; the sole critical root is the unique maximum, and radial continuity plus covariance yields exactly the closed disk. The endpoint \(\beta=1\) follows from the multiplication operator identity, and the two asymptotic laws follow from the exact stationarity equation and an explicit envelope bound. All endpoints and quantifiers were checked. The exact rational witness and 41,412 script tests supplement, rather than replace, the proof.

## Originality
Bhattacharya–Patra (arXiv:2601.02334; §3.3, Corollary 3.19, Remark 3.20) already proves disk geometry and gives two unevaluated branch formulae and conditional critical-point candidates. The new contribution is the unconditional global quartic-root radius, unique branch/extremizers, closed-boundary status, and sharp square-root cutoff law. An exact admissible witness exceeds the numerical value quoted in Remark 3.20. Mahapatra et al. (arXiv:2608.21723) work with squared Bergman kernels, not the present Hardy kernel. A query-level comparison with relevant indexed operator-radius findings found no implication-equivalent statement. Unindexed literature can still contain an equivalent result.

## Mathematical value
Sharp Berezin radii for explicitly given weighted shifts are natural quantitative geometric invariants; the focal article leaves the optimization at a conditional stationary stage. The result resolves the entire two-parameter geometric-weight model, including all exact norming pairs and an endpoint transition from attained to unattained maximal values, with a distinctive nonanalytic square-root deficit. It improves on a conditional stationary prescription and supplies a checkable counter-witness to a published numerical estimate.
