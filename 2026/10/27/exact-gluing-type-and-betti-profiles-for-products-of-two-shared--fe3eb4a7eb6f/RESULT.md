# Betti and almost-symmetry dichotomy for common-generator products of numerical semigroups

## Finding

**Theorem (one complete product-family classification).** Fix integers \(m\ge3\), \(1\le a<b<m\) satisfying \(\gcd(m,a)=\gcd(m,b)=1\). Let
\[
A=\langle m,m+a\rangle,\qquad B=\langle m,m+b\rangle,
\qquad H=\langle m,m+a,m+b\rangle,\qquad d=(m+a)(m+b).
\]
Multiplication \(AB\) means ideal multiplication in the semiring of nonnegative integers: the additive submonoid generated by products of one element of \(A\) and one element of \(B\). Put \(C=AB\). Then:

1. \(C=\langle m^2,m(m+a),m(m+b),d\rangle=mH+d\mathbb N_0\) has embedding dimension four. Every element of \(C\) has a unique expression \(mh+jd\) with \(h\in H\) and \(0\le j<m\). In particular, the Hilbert series and pseudo-Frobenius numbers obey
\[
\sum_{c\in C}z^c=\biggl(\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}z^{jd}\biggr)\sum_{h\in H}z^{mh},
\qquad \operatorname{PF}(C)=m\operatorname{PF}(H)+(m-1)d.
\]
Consequently, with \(F\), \(g\), and \(t\) denoting Frobenius number, genus, and Cohen--Macaulay type,
\[
F(C)=mF(H)+(m-1)d,\quad
 g(C)=mg(H)+\frac{(m-1)(d-1)}2,\quad t(C)=t(H)\in\{1,2\}.
\]
2. Set \(e=\gcd(m+a,m+b)\). The following criterion is necessary and sufficient for \(C\) to be symmetric:
\[
e>1\quad\text{and}\quad
m\in\left\langle\frac{m+a}{e},\frac{m+b}{e}\right\rangle.
\]
If this holds, \(K[C]\) is a complete intersection and has total minimal Betti vector \((1,3,3,1)\) over \(K[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]\), for every field \(K\). Otherwise \(t(C)=2\), the Betti vector is \((1,4,5,2)\), and \(C\) is *not* almost symmetric.
3. More sharply, writing \(\Delta(S)=2g(S)-F(S)-t(S)\), one has the exact defect-amplification identity
\[
\Delta(C)=m\Delta(H)+(m-1)(t(H)-1).
\]
In particular, whenever \(C\) is nonsymmetric, \(\Delta(C)\ge m-1\); the bound is attained, for example, at \((m,a,b)=(3,1,2)\).

**Explicit boundary subfamily.** For \(a=1\), \(b=m-1\), symmetry occurs if and only if \(m\equiv2\pmod3\). Writing \(q=\lfloor(2m-1)/3\rfloor\), the Apéry set of the intermediate three-generator semigroup \(H=\langle m,m+1,2m-1\rangle\) is given exactly by
\[
\operatorname{Ap}(H;m)=\{w_r:0\le r<m\},\qquad
w_r=\min\{r(m+1),(m-r)(2m-1)\}.
\]
Let \(P=q(m+1)\), \(Q=(m-q-1)(2m-1)\). Its pseudo-Frobenius set is \(\{P-m,Q-m\}\) if \(m\not\equiv2\pmod3\), and \(\{P-m\}\) otherwise. Therefore the preceding formulas give *all* pseudo-Frobenius numbers and the Frobenius number and genus of \(AB\) explicitly for every \(m\) in this boundary family; in particular its type has the sharp period-three pattern \(2,2,1\).

## Assumptions and scope

All semigroups contain zero and have finite complement in \(\mathbb N_0\). The inequalities \(0<a<b<m\) ensure the stated four generators of \(C\) are minimal; coprimality gives \(\gcd(m,d)=1\) and guarantees that the two input semigroups and \(H\) are numerical semigroups. The claim concerns *multiplication of ideals in* \(\mathbb N_0\), not the Minkowski sum of the two original semigroups. \(K[S]\) means the one-dimensional affine semigroup algebra with its natural positive grading. The Betti vector is over a polynomial ring with one variable for each of the four minimal generators of \(C\).

## Proof

**Product-to-gluing identity.** Since \(A\) and \(B\) have the displayed generators, their ideal product is generated by the four pairwise products
\(m^2\), \(m(m+a)\), \(m(m+b)\), and \(d\). This gives \(C=mH+d\mathbb N_0\). The integer \(d\) lies in \(H\) since it is a positive integer multiple of \(m+a\), and \(d\equiv ab\pmod m\) is coprime to \(m\). In any expression \(mh+jd\) we may reduce \(j\) modulo \(m\), absorbing multiples of \(md\) into \(mH\). Uniqueness follows by reduction modulo \(m\) and then cancellation. The three generators of \(mH\) are minimal, since the underlying three generators of \(H\) lie strictly between \(m\) and \(2m\). The fourth generator \(d\) is not a multiple of \(m\), and it exceeds each of the other three generators, so no redundant generator appears. This proves the embedding dimension and Hilbert-series factorization.

**Pseudo-Frobenius and genus formulas.** Each integer has a unique representation \(mh+jd\) with \(h\in\mathbb Z\) and \(0\le j<m\). Its membership in \(C\) is equivalent to \(h\in H\). If a gap with \(j<m-1\) were pseudo-Frobenius, adding \(d\in C\) would leave it a gap; hence a pseudo-Frobenius number must have \(j=m-1\). Adding the positive elements \(mH\) shows that its \(h\) must lie in \(\operatorname{PF}(H)\). Conversely, if \(h\in\operatorname{PF}(H)\), then adding an arbitrary nonzero element of \(C\) to \(mh+(m-1)d\) gives an element of \(C\): a positive \(mH\) contribution works by the pseudo-Frobenius property, and a positive \(d\) contribution causes a carry by \(md\), with \(d\in H\). This proves the pseudo-Frobenius identity and the type formula. Taking the maximum gives the Frobenius formula. Finally, let \(u_0,\ldots,u_{m-1}\) be the least members of \(H\) in the respective classes modulo \(m\). The least members of \(C\) in classes modulo \(m^2\) are \(mu_i+jd\), \(0\le i,j<m\); their residues are distinct because \(d\) is a unit modulo \(m\). Selmer's Apéry genus formula, or the Hilbert-series identity and its first derivative at \(z=1\), yields \(g(C)=mg(H)+(m-1)(d-1)/2\).

**Sharp type and homological dichotomy.** The standard three-generator numerical-semigroup theorem (Herzog) says that every minimally three-generated numerical semigroup has type one or two; it is symmetric exactly when its semigroup ring is a complete intersection. The standard classification of symmetric three-generator semigroups says that symmetry holds exactly when some pair of generators has gcd \(e>1\) and the remaining generator belongs to the semigroup generated by the pair divided by \(e\). Here \(\gcd(m,m+a)=\gcd(m,m+b)=1\), so the only possible pair is \((m+a,m+b)\), giving precisely the stated arithmetic criterion.

For the resolution assertion, write \(K[H]=K[x_1,x_2,x_3]/I_H\) and choose \(d=r m+s(m+a)+t(m+b)\) with nonnegative integers \(r,s,t\). The presentation of \(K[C]\) is
\[
K[C]\cong K[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]/(I_H,\ x_4^m-x_1^rx_2^sx_3^t),
\]
where the variable degrees on the right are \(m^2,m(m+a),m(m+b),d\). This follows from the unique normal-form decomposition above. The added binomial is monic in \(x_4\), hence regular modulo \(I_H\), and lies in the square of the homogeneous maximal ideal. Thus the mapping cone on the minimal resolution of \(K[H]\) is minimal, multiplying its Betti polynomial by \(1+z\). The three-generator Betti vectors are \((1,2,1)\) for a symmetric complete intersection and \((1,3,2)\) otherwise (the non-symmetric case is the classical codimension-two Hilbert--Burch presentation). Hence the displayed four-generator vectors follow.

**Defect and boundary.** Substitute the preceding Frobenius/genus/type formulas directly into \(\Delta(C)=2g(C)-F(C)-t(C)\) to obtain \(\Delta(C)=m\Delta(H)+(m-1)(t(H)-1)\). For every numerical semigroup \(\Delta(H)\ge0\); if \(t(H)=2\), this gives the sharp bound and disproves almost symmetry. To verify its sharpness, \(H=\langle3,4,5\rangle\) has \((F,g,t)=(2,2,2)\) and \(C=\langle9,12,15,20\rangle\) has \((F,g,t)=(46,25,2)\), so \(\Delta(C)=2=m-1\).

For the boundary family, put \(b=m-1\). Then \(\gcd(m+1,2m-1)=\gcd(m+1,3)\). If \(m\equiv2\pmod3\), the reduced pair \(((m+1)/3,(2m-1)/3)\) sums to \(m\); otherwise its gcd is one. Hence the symmetry criterion is exactly the claimed congruence. To find the Apéry set, write an element built from \(m+1\) and \(2m-1\) as \(u(m+1)+v(2m-1)\) with \(u,v\ge0\). Its residue modulo \(m\) is \(u-v\), so for residue \(r\), the smallest representative among \(u-v=r+km\) arises either from \(u=r,v=0\) or from \(u=0,v=m-r\); all other choices increase its value. This proves the displayed minimum formula. Its two monotone branches have the only possible maximal Apéry elements \(w_q=P\) and \(w_{q+1}=Q\), giving \(\operatorname{PF}(H)\) after subtracting \(m\). When \(m\equiv2\pmod3\), the tie at \(q=(2m-1)/3\) makes \(Q\) dominated by \(P\) in the Apéry order, leaving a single maximal element. The genus of the boundary family can equivalently be calculated without enumeration from
\[
g(H)=\frac{(m+1)q(q+1)+(2m-1)(m-q-1)(m-q)}{2m}-\frac{m-1}{2},
\]
then the general genus formula above applies to \(C\). This completes the classification.

## Relationship to prior work

Chen, Hyde, Laurens, Piermarini and Simons (2026) introduce multiplicative arithmetic for numerical semigroups as ideals of \(\mathbb N_0\); their Proposition 4.9 gives a sufficient symmetry criterion when two two-generator factors have a common generator and their other generators are congruent modulo that generator. The present full family assumes *distinct* residues \(0<a<b<m\), treats both symmetric and nonsymmetric products, and determines exact Betti vectors, pseudo-Frobenius sets, defects and a sharp arithmetic symmetry test. The proofs use classical gluing, Apéry and three-generator results: the *algebraic tools* are not claimed as new. The recent work of Eto et al. (2026) supplies a broader almost-symmetric almost-complete-intersection obstruction at even embedding dimension; the direct positive quantitative defect formula here is additional and its known obstruction is not reclaimed as new. No claim is made that the isolated gluing/Betti theorems are original.

## Limitations

The multiplicative-product classification is restricted to two-generator factors sharing the same multiplicity and to the stated coprime, distinct interval offsets. It does not classify products with no common generator or arbitrarily many generators. Most ingredients are standard theorems; the new element, to the extent not already contained in unindexed literature, is their explicit unified application to this recently studied ideal-product family and the associated sharp arithmetic/quantitative profiles. This is a self-review only, not independent validation. Search does not establish uniqueness in the literature.
