# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is for every positive-length record ballot obtained from any strict latent ranking, any presentation permutation, and at least one unknown nonzero cutoff. Necessity follows because every retained alternative is an actual running maximum, whereas every unretained alternative is below the most recent retained maximum or, before the first retained candidate, below the first retained candidate. Sufficiency explicitly constructs a cutoff for each linear extension: full acceptability when the first arrival is retained, or one rank above the best unretained prefix candidate otherwise. Rooted-tree hook counting gives the exact compatible-ranking cardinality; the extension theorem for incomparable poset elements gives precisely the forced comparisons. When the presentation order is hidden, the reverse-presentation construction proves both the count and identified pair statements. Standard independent random-permutation record indicators yield the benchmark generating function, expectation, variance, and translated Dickman limit; the last is acknowledged prior theory.

The independent-of-proofs finite checker exhausts 243 fixed-order nonempty record sets with lengths 3–7, and every submitted ballot from all strict rankings, cutoffs, and presentation orders with lengths 3–5 (15, 64, and 325 ballots). It checks actual ranking fibers and logically forced pairwise relations using integer/rational arithmetic. The proof—not those tests—establishes the unrestricted quantifiers. The theorem does not assert actual latent-preference uniqueness, or treat the unknown cutoff as identified.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge comparison)

Fioravanti–Terzopoulou's full ranked-ballot preprint introduces the anchoring observation rule and proves a characterization of order-invariant voting rules (tops-only), explicitly identifying order-aware analysis as an open direction. Their earlier approval-ballot work studies similar anchor-dependent behaviors but not the precise all-compatible-rankings identification set. The fully inspected relevant primary texts do not state or imply the present tree fiber, exact sharp count, and difference in identifiable comparisons conditional on recording the order. The classical record-process identities and Dickman limit are already covered by Louchard (2014), and are **not** offered as independent novelty. Mathematical-record queries on order revelation, anchoring, latent ranking, tree posets, and equivalent terms returned results on distinct voting rules, rankings in allocation, and marginal-order identification; no equivalent characterization was identified. Search incompleteness and priority in unexamined sequential-choice econometrics remain real risks; this review is not independent priority certification.

## Value — PASS

The paper motivating the result explicitly asks how presentation-order information can be used in voting designs. Exact partial identification, rather than pretending to infer a single hidden preference, answers a concrete inference question for the same deterministic sequential-anchoring model. The two exact feasible-set cardinalities, exact information difference, and an explicit construction of every compatible ranking quantify what collecting order data does and does not buy. The gain can be strict and is not a standard comparison of two voting outcomes. The distributional appendix is useful as a benchmark, with prior probabilistic results expressly attributed.
