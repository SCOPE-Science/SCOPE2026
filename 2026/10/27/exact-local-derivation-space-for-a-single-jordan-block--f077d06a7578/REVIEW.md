# Review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The derivation identity forces every derivation to map into the abelian ideal and to commute with the single Jordan block; its centralizer is the polynomial algebra in the nilpotent shift. The pointwise local condition is equivalent to preserving each tail subspace in the Jordan flag. The converse constructs an appropriate derivation for every vector, including vectors with zero complementary coordinate. The dimension formulas then follow from the triangular-matrix and centralizer dimensions.

## Originality

**PASS, best-knowledge.** The primary source proves the diagonalizability dichotomy and supplies a single non-derivation local map for a nontrivial Jordan block. It does not characterize every local derivation or give the dimension of the local-derivation space. Searches for the exact flag-preserving characterization and dimension formula did not return a matching result. Related work on solvable Leibniz algebras treats a different algebraic identity. These searches do not prove universal novelty.

## Value

**PASS.** The complete space \(\operatorname{LocDer}(L)\) is a natural refinement of the known yes/no rigidity dichotomy. The exact excess dimension \(k(k-1)/2\) measures how the failure of rigidity grows with the length of the canonical Jordan block and identifies all, rather than one, of the non-derivation directions.

## Closest literature and limitations

Ayupov and Khudoyberdiyev, arXiv:1803.06668v1, Theorem 3.2 and the derivation matrices in Section 3, are the closest direct source. Ayupov, Khudoyberdiyev, and Yusupov, arXiv:1911.03733v1, give related results for solvable Leibniz algebras, not the same Lie algebra. The result is limited to characteristic zero and a single Jordan block with nonzero eigenvalue; multiple-block and positive-characteristic cases remain outside scope.
