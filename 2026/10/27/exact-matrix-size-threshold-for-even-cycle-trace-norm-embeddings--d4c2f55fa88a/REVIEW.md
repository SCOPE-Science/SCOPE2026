# Review of the even-cycle trace-norm embedding threshold

## Correctness — PASS
The half-cycle inequality is exactly sharp when every edge norm is equal and each consecutive half-cycle is trace-geodesic. The proof of a trace-norm support lemma uses two norming contractions with conflicting polar signs. Its application produces positive halves with identical sums and a full-rank off-diagonal-orthogonal pairing, enforcing \(d\ge k\). A diagonal construction attains equality for every larger matrix size; compactness makes the subthreshold inequality strict. Direct numerical diagnostics passed 5,018 assertions and were not used to infer the infinite family.

## Originality — PASS, with qualification
Jiao, Luo and Wu (arXiv:2610.21445v1) prove dimension-independent trace-class metric cotype bounds, not this sharp one-dimensional equality threshold. Rigobert and Laurent (arXiv:2607.07939) give exact dimensions into binary Cayley hosts, but a matrix with nuclear distance need not be a binary host. published-finding corpus comparisons and own-ledger claim searches located no implication-complete precursor; older alternate formulations remain a priority risk. This is a literature comparison, not proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The finite-matrix size needed to realize even-cycle graph geometry is motivated intrinsically by finite-dimensional approximants to trace class. The exact threshold is robust under all complex matrices, including noncommuting and nonnormal cases, and is not implied by the sharp inequality without the structural support lemma.

## Limits and source accessibility
No sharp numerical constant below the threshold is asserted; no odd cycles or higher torus dimensions are classified. Jiao–Luo–Wu full arxiv text and the relevant Cayley work were inspected directly. The Mendel–Naor source was used for the original framework without claiming exhaustive full-text priority exclusion. Historical priority and correctness have not undergone an independent audit.
