# Scientific assessment: exact mixed chord statistics and independent Poisson–Gaussian limits

## Correctness — PASS

Fixing any disjoint collection of adjacent chords removes exactly those endpoints and preserves every crossing among the others. Counting these forced collections establishes the finite joint polynomial and, upon differentiation, the factorial Palm identity. Removing a uniformly selected existing chord from a uniform matching gives a coupled smaller uniform matching without increasing the crossing count, proving the sign inequality. The known exact crossing mean/variance then gives the displayed covariance and correlation. For the joint limit, each fixed factorial coefficient tends to one and is uniformly at most one; the published crossing central limit theorem, together with a dominated binomial-series argument, proves joint independence. The attached verifier independently checked 2,945 exact assertions, including all matchings through six chords. Finite checks were not substituted for the proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical priority

The closest inspected literature includes Flajolet–Noy on the crossing generating polynomial and marginal central limit theorem; Cameron–Killpatrick on the marginal linear short-chord statistic; Paguyo on separate crossings and circular-simple-chord limit laws; Marmor on short-chord Schur positivity within classes of fixed crossing count; and Pilaud–Rué on fixed-small-crossing enumeration and its asymptotics. Marmor's refined Schur-positive family may encode aspects of finite bivariate enumeration, but the inspected theorem is a positivity statement, not the deletion identity, all-n increasing-function covariance inequality, or independent joint limit. No directly covering result was located in the inspected sources or the research-index comparison. ## Mathematical value — PASS

The two observables are natural but distinct: adjacent-pair chords describe isolated microscopic structure, whereas crossing pairs aggregate global intersection geometry. Exact Palm reduction controls all mixed factorial moments with an arbitrary crossing functional, gives a stringent finite-size negative-dependence statement, and lifts known separate marginal limits to an independent bivariate law. The transformation thus supplies motivated structural information beyond univariate enumeration or a routine moment substitution.

## Limitations

General Schur-positive generating-function encodings and older combinatorial identities present plausible priority pathways and warrant independent historical assessment.
