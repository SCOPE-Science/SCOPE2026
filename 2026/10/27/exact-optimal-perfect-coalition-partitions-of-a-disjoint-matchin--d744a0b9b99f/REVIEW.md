# Scientific review: optimum perfect-coalition partitions of a matching

## Correctness — PASS
The claimed exact enumeration has a complete reduction: partition labels on the endpoints of each original matching edge form a graph of edge types, and two partition classes are partners exactly when their labels cover all edge types. This gives a four-class upper bound, three possible type-support graphs, and a surjective-assignment census divided by \(4!\). The proof treats loops, non-dominating conditions, and all three graph types explicitly. The accompanying verifier independently evaluates the original vertex-level conditions for \(2\le n\le5\), including each partner-graph type; these finite tests are supplementary, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with stated limits
The original 2024 perfect-coalition article introduces the parameter and bounds but does not present this matching-partition count in the inspected full text. Earlier ordinary coalition work, applicable because the matching is one-regular, likewise concerns broader bounds and different families in its inspected sections. A later transversal-coalition paper **already** establishes the abstract optimal partner-graph options \(C_4,P_4,2K_2\) for two-uniform hypergraphs: these types and the scalar four-class ceiling are not claimed as new. The proposed original fact is the matching-specific, complete exact census \(2^n(\tfrac14 S(n,2)+3S(n,3)+3S(n,4))\) and its three separate type counts, which do not follow from the mere list of possible graphs. No equivalent calculation appeared in targeted literature and mathematical-record searches. Full exhaustive priority is not provable, and access to some possible related texts was incomplete.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The disjoint matching is the canonical nontrivial one-regular graph, on which ordinary and perfect coalition constraints coincide. The theorem goes beyond determining its maximum coalition number: it counts **all** optimum set partitions of \(2n\) labeled vertices, distinguishes three partner-graph types, and gives their realization multiplicities for every \(n\). The Stirling-number profile shows when each type first occurs and gives a reusable correspondence between domination coalitions and surjective edge-type assignments. The exact optimum census is not a known-bound substitution or a single small-case calculation.

## Literature and limitations
Closest sources: Mojdeh–Samadzadeh, *Perfect coalition in graphs*, arXiv:2409.10185 (first public 2024-09-16); Haynes and collaborators (2020), *Introduction to coalitions in graphs*, DOI:10.1080/09728600.2020.1832874; Bakhshesh–Henning–Pradhan, *On the coalition number of trees*, arXiv:2111.08945; Blázsik, *General sharp upper bounds on the transversal coalition number*, DOI:10.1016/j.dam.2025.08.019. The latter is strongest on the structural three-type classification but does not itself entail the matching realization counts. Residual priority risk includes unindexed enumeration work and not-fully-inspected publications. The checker covers only \(2\le n\le5\), and the claim does not cover smaller nonoptimal partitions or unlabeled matching endpoints.
