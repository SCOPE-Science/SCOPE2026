# Exact pair-synchronization-radius distribution of five-state binary automata

## Finding

Consider all deterministic complete automata \(\mathcal A=(Q,\{a,b\},\delta)\) on \(Q=\{0,1,2,3,4\}\), with the two input letters distinguished. Each ordered pair of maps \(Q\to Q\) is counted once, for \(5^{10}=9{,}765{,}625\) automata. For distinct \(i,j\in Q\), set
\[
 d_{\mathcal A}(i,j)=\min\{\lvert w\rvert:\delta(i,w)=\delta(j,w)\},
\]
with the minimum equal to \(\infty\) when the pair cannot be merged. Define the **pair-synchronization radius**
\[
 \rho(\mathcal A)=\max_{\{i,j\}\subseteq Q,\ i\ne j}d_{\mathcal A}(i,j).
\]
Exactly \(8{,}063{,}385\) automata have finite radius, equivalently are synchronizing. Their complete distribution is:

| \(\rho\) | Number of labeled automata |
| ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 31,225 |
| 2 | 1,852,520 |
| 3 | 3,544,680 |
| 4 | 1,798,320 |
| 5 | 613,920 |
| 6 | 175,440 |
| 7 | 38,160 |
| 8 | 7,440 |
| 9 | 1,440 |
| 10 | 240 |

The remaining \(1{,}702{,}240\) automata have infinite radius. The exact mean radius conditional on synchronizability is \(5{,}205{,}565/1{,}612{,}677\), approximately \(3.22790304568\).

The radius-ten class contains exactly \(240\) labeled automata and is one orbit under simultaneous relabeling of states and optional interchange of \(a,b\). Thus, the only five-state binary automata attaining the general upper bound \(\binom{5}{2}=10\), up to these symmetries, are the Černý automaton:
\[
 a=(1,2,3,4,0),\qquad b=(0,1,2,3,0).
\]
The existence of a pair requiring ten steps in this classical automaton and the general bound are not claimed as new. The reported contribution is the complete labeled radius distribution and the exhaustive uniqueness of its extremal orbit.

## Assumptions and scope

Automata are complete and deterministic; there are exactly five labeled states and exactly two distinguished input letters. A word acts on both states simultaneously, and the empty word cannot merge distinct states. All statements about uniqueness concern isomorphism under the group generated by state permutations and the swap of the two letters. No statement concerns automata on more states or with a different alphabet size.

## Proof

There are exactly ten unordered pairs of distinct states. For every pair \(e=\{i,j\}\) and letter \(c\in\{a,b\}\), its successor is either an absorbing merged state when \(\delta(i,c)=\delta(j,c)\), or the unordered pair \(\{\delta(i,c),\delta(j,c)\}\). Thus the shortest merge length is the shortest directed distance from \(e\) to the absorbing merged state in this ten-vertex pair graph.

Define \(D_0=\varnothing\) and recursively
\[
 D_{t+1}=D_t\cup\{e:\text{some letter merges }e\text{ immediately or sends }e\text{ into }D_t\}.
\]
Then \(e\in D_t\) if and only if \(d_{\mathcal A}(e)\le t\), by induction on \(t\). Every shortest successful pair path visits distinct nonmerged pairs: otherwise the intervening cycle can be deleted. Hence every finite merge length is at most ten. The automaton is synchronizing precisely when every pair is mergeable: merging an arbitrary pair in the current image strictly decreases the image cardinality, and repetition produces a reset word.

The accompanying `forward_levels.cpp` loops over all \(5^5\) possibilities for the first letter and all \(5^5\) for the second, constructs the ten successors, computes the ten finite distances by successive predecessor levels, and increments exactly one radius counter if all ten pairs merge. The independent `reverse_bfs.cpp` also visits every ordered transition pair, but instead builds the reversed pair graph and performs a multi-source breadth-first search from directly mergeable pairs. Both yield the table in `pair_radius_counts.csv`. Their equality provides a reproducible check on the exhaustive computation, not a substitute for the inductive correctness argument above.

For the representative Černý automaton, the distances for pairs in lexicographic order \(\{0,1\},\{0,2\},\ldots,\{3,4\}\) are exactly \((5,10,7,1,4,9,6,3,8,2)\). In particular \(a^4ba^4b\) merges \(\{0,2\}\) in ten steps; no shorter word does. The reverse-graph implementation stores all 240 automata having radius ten. The orbit of its first such automaton under the 120 state permutations and both letter orders consists of 240 distinct ordered transition pairs and agrees exactly with that exhaustive set. An explicit state relabeling \((0,2,3,1,4)\) carries the displayed Černý representative to the first extremizer printed by the forward program. This proves the single-orbit classification.

## Verification

Run `sh reproduce.sh` in the directory containing the embedded artifacts. This compiles two standalone C++17 programs and checks both against `pair_radius_counts.csv`, including the number of nonsynchronizing automata, the extremal orbit size, and complete agreement of the extremal set. The script prints `VERIFY_OK` upon success. The raw two-algorithm totals are \(8{,}063{,}385\) synchronizing, \(1{,}702{,}240\) nonsynchronizing, and a radius-ten count of \(240\).

The weighted radius sum is \(26{,}027{,}825\), giving the exact conditional mean above. Both exhaustive programs were compiled and run from the actual artifact directory; every reported count agreed. The finite enumeration is exhaustive because each of the two nested base-five loops covers all \(5^{10}\) ordered maps, not because of random sampling.

## Relationship to prior work

Szykuła's 2026 survey, *Synchronizing Automata: Open Problems*, Section 2.4, records the tight general bound \(n(n-1)/2\) for synchronizing a prescribed pair and states that Černý automata attain the associated subset bound. Sections 2.3 and 2.6 distinguish merging a prescribed pair from the easier problem of merging a fixed state with some other state, and explain the central role of pair mergeability in synchronizability testing. These bounds, definitions, and the Černý witness are established background, not new contributions.

De Bondt, Don, and Zantema's *Slowly synchronizing automata with fixed alphabet size* (2016 preprint; Section 4) tabulates automata by **reset length** and explicitly uses the pair-merging criterion for synchronizability. A distribution by global reset threshold does not determine the present radius distribution: for example, both \(a=(0,0,0,0,4), b=(3,4,2,1,4)\) and \(a=(0,0,0,0,4), b=(4,0,0,0,1)\) have reset threshold four, but their pair radii are four and three respectively. The first is therefore not a restatement of the earlier reset-length census.

## Limitations

This result is an exact finite computation with an elementary exhaustive proof, not an asymptotic law or an extension of the general prescribed-pair upper bound. No unexamined historical enumeration can be ruled out solely from negative search results; possible unindexed tables of pair distances are the main remaining priority risk. No independent external scientific audit has been performed.
