# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The ten-state position lift of the nonuniform morphism is checked state by state against a five-uniform six-state morphism. Its eight admissible adjacent letter pairs are generated by \(g^2(0)\), and all eight pair images preserve that language. For each length through 25, factors of the **infinite** coded word equal the union of factors of eight explicit projected 50-letter words. Exhaustive palindromic-factor extraction yields the exact spectrum with 37 distinct nonempty palindromes and maximum length 23. Absence of palindromes of lengths 24 and 25 excludes all longer ones by their centers. Each of six projected \(g^4\)-images has length 625 and contains all 37 palindromes, so every length-at-least-1249 factor does as well. The separate standard-library verifier reconstructs and checks these critical finite identities and an independently generated prefix. All universal quantifiers have a finite-language closure argument, not an inference from a long prefix. Residual correctness risk is ordinary implementation or exposition error; no independent verification has been obtained.

## Originality — PASS, with bounded residual risk
The focal primary paper (Rigo, Stipulanti, Whiteland, *Automaticity and Parikh-collinear Morphisms*, WORDS 2023; public May 11, 2023) presents exactly the nonuniform morphism and six-state representation while investigating abelian complexity; the inspected full conference article does not state a palindrome spectrum or factor defect. The 2024 full article *Automatic Abelian Complexities of Parikh-Collinear Fixed Points* develops the automaticity of abelian complexity, not the present reversal-symmetric invariant. Frid, Laborde, Peltomäki, *On prefix palindromic length of automatic words* (2020), proves the existing general implication from finite palindrome language to regular prefix palindromic length, but does **not** establish the hypothesis for this specific word. Its conclusion is acknowledged as prior, not claimed as new. Searches also compared the exact morphism, palindrome aliases, factor defect and general bounded-palindrome results against available mathematical records; the closest located results concern different words, code channels, closed-factor maxima and string attractors. An unindexed equivalent classification remains possible; database nonretrieval is not a proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The paper's published canonical example has aperiodic abelian complexity; establishing its **complete**, unexpectedly finite palindromic language addresses a different natural structural invariant of the exact same infinite word. The finite spectrum alone would leave arbitrary-position factors unresolved, whereas the all-factor saturation theorem determines a global palindromic-defect law for every sufficiently long factor, a strong quantitative statement. Finite-palindrome verification also makes the general regularity theorem on prefix palindromic length applicable to this previously untreated example. No paper-sized contribution or optimal cutoff is claimed.

The 2023 and 2024 primary papers and the 2020 prefix-palindromic-length primary paper were inspected in full-text form for the relevant statements and definitions; their scopes and implied corollaries were compared, not merely their titles. The 2023 morphism and automatic coding are acknowledged, and the earlier regularity implication is explicitly excluded from novelty.
