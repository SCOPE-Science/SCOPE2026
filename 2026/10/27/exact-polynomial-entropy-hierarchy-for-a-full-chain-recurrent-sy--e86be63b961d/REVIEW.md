# Scientific review: exact entropy hierarchy of a two-transition quotient

## Correctness — PASS

The result has exact hypotheses, quantifiers and a complete proof. The language of the compact binary shift has exactly \(L^2-L+2\) words of length \(L\); this yields an \(O(N^{2n})\) bound on all length-\(N\) orbit profiles of \(F_n(g)\) after passing through the finite-to-one quotient. The lower bound constructs \(n\) independent finite pulses, each with two freely placed interfaces, and shows that distinct \(2n\)-parameter choices remain uniformly Bowen-separated. For \(n\ge2\), the marker configuration is genuinely two-point near each passage, so separation survives collapsing singleton configurations. The theorem covers every finite \(n\) and all sufficiently large observation times, not just checked instances. The nested invariant finite hyperspaces yield infinite polynomial entropy on the full hyperspace.

## Originality — PASS under inspected evidence

The directly motivating preprint by Đorić, arXiv:2610.23058v1, gives this precise quotient system, its nonwandering and chain-recurrence properties, and a full description of the second symmetric-product nonwandering set; its main results do not state the exact polynomial-entropy values. The earlier Đorić article arXiv:2507.10126v2 proves \(h_{\mathrm{pol}}(F_n(f))=h_{\mathrm{pol}}(\mathcal SF_n(f))=n h_{\mathrm{pol}}(f)\) when the chain recurrent set is finite, and a more general lower bound under a wandering-point hypothesis. The current system has infinite chain recurrent set equal to all of \(Y\), so that equality theorem does not apply; its general lower bound is strictly weaker than the established \(2n\) conclusion. Earlier symmetric-product recurrence work is about nonwandering, recurrence and periodicity properties, not an exact spectrum for this canonical quotient. Available published mathematical index records were also compared for equivalent or broader implications, without locating a covering theorem.

## Value — PASS

The exceptional example was expressly constructed to show that the base nonwandering set cannot control the nonwandering dynamics of symmetric products. It is therefore natural to ask whether the accompanying polynomial-entropy amplification remains computable once the finite-chain-recurrence hypothesis of the known entropy theorem fails. The exact answer \(2n\) identifies two independently observable transition passages for each of \(n\) particles, and remains unchanged under collapsing singleton subsets. It separates the effects of full chain recurrence and infinite induced nonwandering sets from the exact polynomial complexity. This is a structural classification of a canonical natural counterexample, not a computation of an arbitrary truncation.
