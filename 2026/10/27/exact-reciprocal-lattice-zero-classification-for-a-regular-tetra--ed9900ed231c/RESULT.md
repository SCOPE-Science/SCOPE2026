# Exact reciprocal-lattice zero classification for a regular tetrahedral shell

## Finding

Let \(T\subset\mathbb R^3\) be a regular tetrahedron with vertices \(v_0=0,v_1,v_2,v_3\). Let
\[
L=\sum_{j=1}^3\mathbb Z v_j,\qquad
L^*=\{\xi\in\mathbb R^3:\langle \xi,v_j\rangle\in\mathbb Z\text{ for }j=1,2,3\}.
\]
Write \(\lambda_0=0\) and \(\lambda_j=\langle \xi,v_j\rangle\) for \(\xi\in L^*\). For the Fourier transform of Euclidean surface measure,
\[
\widehat{\sigma_T}(\xi)=\int_{\partial T}e^{-2\pi i\langle \xi,x\rangle}\,dS(x),
\]
the complete zero classification on \(L^*\) is:
\[
\widehat{\sigma_T}(\xi)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\{\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\}
\text{ has type }1+1+1+1,\ 2+2,\ \text{or }2+1+1
\]
where in the \(2+1+1\) case the repeated value \(r\) and singleton values \(s,t\) satisfy \(s+t=2r\). Thus all-distinct phases and two-pair phases always cancel; a single repeated pair cancels precisely when its two singleton phases are symmetric about the repeated value.

## Assumptions and scope

The tetrahedron is regular, so all four facets have the same Euclidean area. The lattice is the reciprocal of the edge lattice generated by \(v_1,v_2,v_3\), with the \(2\pi\) Fourier convention displayed above. The values \(\lambda_j\) are therefore integers. The claim concerns the unweighted surface-area measure on the boundary, not volume measure on \(T\), normal-weighted boundary integrals, or frequencies outside \(L^*\).

A multiplicity type such as \(2+1+1\) records the multiplicities among the four integers \(\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\). In that type, \(r\) denotes the value occurring twice and \(s,t\) the two distinct singleton values.

## Proof

Let
\[
\Delta_2=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb R^2:u\ge0,\ v\ge0,\ u+v\le1\}
\]
and define the face integral
\[
G(a,b,c)=\int_{\Delta_2}
e^{-2\pi i(a(1-u-v)+bu+cv)}\,du\,dv
\qquad(a,b,c\in\mathbb Z).
\]
Since every node is integral, translating all three nodes by the same integer does not change \(G\). Put \(p=b-a\) and \(q=c-a\), and write
\[
F_2(p,q)=\int_{\Delta_2}e^{-2\pi i(px+qy)}\,dx\,dy.
\]
For integers \(p,q\) with \(q\ne0\), integrating first in \(y\) gives the exact identity
\[
F_2(p,q)=\frac{1}{2\pi i q}
\left(\mathbf 1_{\{p=0\}}-\mathbf 1_{\{p=q\}}\right),
\]
because \(\int_0^1 e^{-2\pi i n x}\,dx=\mathbf 1_{\{n=0\}}\) for integer \(n\). If \(q=0\) and \(p\ne0\), symmetry gives \(F_2(p,0)=-i/(2\pi p)\); if \(p=q=0\), the integral is the area \(1/2\). These formulas yield the complete three-node rule
\[
G(a,b,c)=
\begin{cases}
0,&a,b,c\text{ pairwise distinct},\\[2pt]
\frac12,&a=b=c,\\[2pt]
-\dfrac{i}{2\pi(s-r)},&\{a,b,c\}=\{r,r,s\},\ r\ne s.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, \(G(0,0,q)=-i/(2\pi q)\) and \(G(q,q,0)=i/(2\pi q)\) for nonzero integer \(q\).

Let \(A_f\) be the common Euclidean area of a facet. Each facet's affine barycentric parametrization by \(\Delta_2\) has constant area Jacobian \(2A_f\). The four facets are obtained by omitting one vertex phase at a time. Consequently,
\[
\widehat{\sigma_T}(\xi)
=2A_f\sum_{j=0}^3
G(\lambda_0,\ldots,\widehat{\lambda_j},\ldots,\lambda_3),
\]
where the hat over a node means that it is omitted. This identity uses equal facet areas; it is the step that distinguishes the regular tetrahedron from a general tetrahedron.

It remains to classify the five possible multiplicity partitions of four nodes.

* Type \(1+1+1+1\): every three-node face has distinct nodes, so all four terms vanish.
* Type \(2+2\): two faces have nodes \(\{r,r,s\}\) and two have nodes \(\{r,s,s\}\). Their contributions cancel in equal and opposite pairs.
* Type \(2+1+1\): if \(r\) is repeated and \(s,t\) are the singletons, the two faces containing three distinct nodes contribute zero. The remaining sum is
  \[
  -\frac{i}{2\pi}\left(\frac1{s-r}+\frac1{t-r}\right).
  \]
  Since \(s\ne r\) and \(t\ne r\), this is zero exactly when \(s+t=2r\).
* Type \(3+1\): one face contributes \(1/2\), while the other three contribute the same purely imaginary number \(-i/(2\pi(s-r))\). The real part of the sum is \(1/2\), so it cannot vanish.
* Type \(4\): all four faces contribute \(1/2\), so the sum is \(2\), not zero.

These cases are exhaustive, and the common factor \(2A_f>0\) does not affect zeros. This proves both necessity and sufficiency.

## Verification

The proof is an exact finite case analysis after evaluating one triangular integral; it does not infer an infinite statement from a numerical search. The included `check.py` independently represents the real part and the coefficient of \(i/(2\pi)\) using rational arithmetic, enumerates all \(9^3=729\) integer triples with \(-4\le\lambda_j\le4\) for \(j=1,2,3\), and checks the stated classification. That bounded test finds 396 zeros and no disagreement with the criterion. The finite test is only a consistency check; the proof above establishes the result for every \(\xi\in L^*\).

## Relationship to prior work

Fourier analysis of polyhedral shapes and their face decompositions is classical. Gallatin's 2012 treatment uses Gauss's law to express a *volume* Fourier transform as a boundary integral weighted by the outward normal; that vector-valued identity is not the scalar, unweighted surface transform studied here. Diaz, Le, and Robins give general Fourier formulas for polytope indicator functions and their face-poset/Stokes decompositions, but their stated objective is the transform of the indicator and lattice-point/solid-angle sums. Those general formulas are relevant tools, not the zero classification above. Senesi and Lee study polyhedral scattering functions, an adjacent setting; the precise scalar surface-measure lattice-zero statement is not established from the material inspected for this report.

The present claim is confined to a regular tetrahedron and the reciprocal edge lattice. It does not assert a corresponding criterion for volume measure, arbitrary tetrahedra, other polyhedra, or continuous frequencies.

## Limitations

The result classifies only lattice frequencies whose pairings with the three edges are integers. The equality of the four facet areas is essential to the cancellations in the \(2+2\) case. The literature comparison is not exhaustive: an equivalent lattice-zero classification may occur in work on thin-shell diffraction or polyhedral surface form factors. The full text of one closely related polyhedral-scattering article was not available for statement-by-statement comparison, so priority is not certified. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
