# Scientific review: exact second jets of commuting three-by-three matrices

## Correctness
PASS. The result follows from the exhaustive algebraic classification of scalar, rank-one-shift, and cyclic matrices of size three. The five-dimensional centralizer of a rank-one matrix has a two-dimensional center and rank-two inner commutator maps away from its center; both semisimple and nilpotent rank-one cases are handled explicitly. Trace duality converts the second-order lifting condition into that rank calculation. The smooth stratum contributes \(q^{24}\) lifts, the non-scalar non-smooth stratum contributes \(q^{23}(q^3+q^2-1)\), and scalar pairs contribute \(q^{18}T\) per base. Exact Fq counts and numerical checks over four field orders support, but do not substitute for, the proof. No assumption on the characteristic is needed.

## Originality
PASS with meaningful residual historical-priority risk. The inspected jet-scheme paper of Sethuraman–Šivic (arXiv:0902.3467, especially introduction and sections 2, 4, 5) proves geometric irreducibility for all jet orders in size three; it does not state the all-field second-jet enumerator. The inspected three-by-three branching section of Sharma (arXiv:1409.2698, section 4) already classifies the common-centralizer types; this known component is explicitly excluded from the originality claim. The second-jet obstruction map, its uniform rank over the two distinct rank-one types, and the resulting exact arithmetic lift counts require an additional quadratic lifting analysis. Popov (arXiv:0801.3074) supplies antecedents on codimension-four irregular loci in characteristic zero but not finite-field second-jet fibers. Further literature on matrix rings over finite local rings might contain an equivalent count and should be checked independently before asserting absolute priority.

## Value
PASS. Finite-order jets encode infinitesimal geometry of commuting-matrix equations. Whereas geometric irreducibility alone says little about arithmetic thickness, the theorem gives every rational fiber size of the second-jet projection and a uniform polynomial point count in every characteristic. The rank-two obstruction detects the non-smooth rank-one directions and distinguishes them from both regular and scalar bases. These are natural invariants of an important fixed-size commuting scheme, rather than arbitrary restricted calculations.

## Limits
The theorem is restricted to size three, jets modulo \(t^3\), and field-rational points. It does not prove reducedness of the commuting scheme, determine scheme multiplicities, or extend the formulas to arbitrary orders. Exhaustive tests are finite, and an independent audit has not yet been performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
