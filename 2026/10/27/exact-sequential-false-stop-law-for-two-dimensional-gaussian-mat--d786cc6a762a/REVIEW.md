# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof fixes the entire domain \(a\ge b>0\), \(0<\tau<b\), independent standard Gaussian probes, and the exact empty-initial-state decision convention. Orthogonal invariance and polar independence give the first gate; an exact perpendicular-vector identity gives the rank-one residual. The second gate is conditioned on the first probe's direction, so its probability is integrated with the first-gate survival factor rather than treated independently. The residual CDF and density include the equal-singular-values boundary; dominated convergence proves the strictly positive anisotropic limit. Eckart–Young proves that either premature stop misses the Frobenius tolerance. The extracted standalone numerical check applies direct matrix updates on 400,000 seeded paths and a separate CDF diagnostic on 100,000 paths. The proof is analytic and does not rely on stochastic trials.

**Originality — PASS under accessible mathematical-record and literature comparison, with historical-priority risk.** Smith–Do–Chen (arXiv:2607.06758v1, §3.1, Algorithm 2) define the precise Gaussian stopping/basis-growth primitive and justify its norm estimator, but inspected sections do not derive the dependent two-probe stopping distribution. Gorman et al. (arXiv:1810.04125v2, §2.5 and Appendix A) give generalized chi-square laws and concentration for a fixed residual, which do not by themselves determine the coupled updated-basis risk. Pearce et al. (arXiv:2310.09417) study LU-based interpolative decomposition; Epperly–Webber (arXiv:2605.19096) study sketched minimax bounds. published-finding corpus and current accessible claim comparisons found no formula entailing the exact probability law; absence of a database hit is not proof of priority. Other specialized random-projection literature may overlap, and complete appendices of some comparators remain uninspected.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The exact failure probability is a direct measure of a randomized adaptive numerical stopping mechanism. A strictly positive high-condition-number failure floor is especially useful because no rank-one approximation could meet the proposed tolerance even in the best case; the issue is with one-probe stopping, not truncation quality. Explicit constants and formulas allow calibration and comparison with repeated validation. This is not presented as a general-dimensional or other-block theorem.

**Limits and risks.** Only real invertible two-by-two matrices, one-column Gaussian look-ahead, exact arithmetic, a strict absolute tolerance, and the defined empty-rank convention are covered. The rank-zero branch is a mathematical stop-decision convention, not a claim of implementation safety for empty-block pruning. Broader historical priority and external reproduction remain unverified.
