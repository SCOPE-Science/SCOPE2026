# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The inverse-incidence factorization \(I^{-1}=B\) gives a grounded graph quadratic form. Explicit radial and sibling-contrast spaces are pairwise orthogonal, invariant under \(B^*B\), and account for every vertex; the Chebyshev determinant formula therefore covers every singular value with multiplicity. Positivity and the Perron eigenvector show that the radial block supplies the sharp norm. A weighted Cauchy–Schwarz lower bound and a matching radial trial profile establish the fixed-branching leading constant. Direct finite-matrix spectral comparisons and separate large-depth checks support, but do not replace, these arguments.

**Originality — PASS, with a substantive literature limitation.** An actual search for the same ancestor operator, its discrete tree zeta/grounded-Laplacian form, and the complete singular spectrum found no equivalent formula. Arcozzi–Rochberg–Sawyer (2002, Theorem 3) gives tree-Hardy embeddings, not finite spectra. Evans–Harris–Lang (2000) studies metric-tree operators. Rojo–Robbiano (2007) is the closest structural comparator: it obtains tridiagonal blocks for tree adjacency and ungrounded graph Laplacians. The grounded rank-one boundary alteration in this problem changes the eigenvalues, including elimination of the zero eigenvalue; the abstract does not state the current formula or asymptotic. That article's full text could not be inspected, so a general tridiagonal method covering this case remains a meaningful risk. Inability to locate an equivalent paper is not a proof of uniqueness.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The unweighted counting \(\ell^2\) ancestor primitive is a natural discrete Hardy embedding and an exact inverse of a rooted graph difference operator. Its full singular spectrum determines every finite-rank optimal approximation error, not merely a selected small-parameter example. The sharp exponentially growing norm with an explicit branching constant and its exact path contrast quantify a genuine structural effect of branching in tree integration.
