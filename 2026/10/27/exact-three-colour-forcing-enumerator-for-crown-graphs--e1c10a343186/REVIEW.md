# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The classification of proper three-colourings exhausts disjoint side palettes and the unique permitted shared matched pair for \(n\ge4\). Within these cases the necessary and sufficient seed conditions are proved by examining the two distinct neighbour colours required to force a vertex; the shared-pair case has a constructive three-stage forcing sequence using the crucial bound \(n-2\ge2\). A successful partial assignment has a unique proper forced extension, so terminal-colouring enumeration causes no duplicate counting. The coefficient formula, sharp minimum and comparison with \(K_{n,n}\) then follow. A separate exhaustive simulator agrees at \(n=4,5\); finite checks are auxiliary, not proof of all orders.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, qualified)
Farr [1] establishes the local forcing rule, polynomial interpretation and small-graph examples, not the infinite crown-graph enumerator. The closest published complete-bipartite three-colour forcing formula concerns \(K_{n,n}\), and cannot be transplanted across deletion of a perfect matching because that deletion permits a third terminal-colouring type. A published complete-multipartite all-palette classification likewise assumes every cross-edge is present. Maximum-degree-two results cover the cycle \(\operatorname{Cr}_3\) only; other crown colouring results employ different constraints. Targeted searches for crown, matching-deleted bipartite, forced three-colouring and equivalent partial-colouring terms did not identify an implication covering the exact formula. Residual risk is an unindexed earlier equivalent precolouring/forcing enumerator.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A complete cardinality polynomial is determined for a natural dense bipartite family, including every successful partial precolouring and a complete three-seed extremal classification. The minimum forcing seed number drops from \(n\) to \(3\) after deleting just \(n\) edges from \(K_{n,n}\), establishing an unbounded instability for this explicitly motivated new graph polynomial. The result is not solely a routine computation or an unsupported conjectural extension.
