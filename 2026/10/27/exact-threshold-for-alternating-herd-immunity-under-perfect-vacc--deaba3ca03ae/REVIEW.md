# Scientific assessment: alternating herd immunity under perfect efficacy

**Correctness — PASS.** The theorem is for \(s=1\), \(R>1\), and \(0<r<1\), with the high state at or above \(p_c=1-1/R\). The cycle equation reduces to a strictly decreasing scalar function with a negative right endpoint. The inverse of the SIR final-size relation has rigorously signed first, second, and third derivatives. An integral formula gives a strictly decreasing threshold function, whose endpoints identify the exact \(R=5/2\) change and the unique positive cost threshold for larger \(R\). The outbreak final-size derivative lies strictly between one and two, forcing the strict-crossing two-step multiplier into \((-1,0)\). The boundary touching case is excluded from the smooth stability assertion. The packaged symbolic and numerical checker reproduces the analytic identities and sample branches; numerical tests are not the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with literature uncertainty.** The inspected original arXiv:2101.07926v1 contains the annual vaccination map and a qualitative, numerically illustrated transition to biennial herd immunity for positive cost; those observations are credited. Its analytical appendix concerns zero cost only. No sharp formula for the positive-cost perfect-efficacy crossing threshold, no \(R=5/2\) universal divider, and no all-parameter negative-multiplier bound was located in the original paper. Broader SIR final-size and vaccine-allocation literature does not directly yield the threshold for the repeated decision heuristic. Indexed-record and targeted phrase searches found no dominating statement. Residual risk: an equivalent analysis could exist in a differently indexed or later work; full independent bibliographic validation is not claimed.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A publication's previously numerical regime diagram is given an exact analytic phase boundary on the natural maximal-efficacy slice. The theorem explains when disease-induced vaccination can sustain an alternating outbreak/herd-immunity pattern, establishes uniqueness and attraction for strict crossings, and shows the mathematically nontrivial \(R=5/2\) curvature transition. It does not assert global attraction or behavior for imperfect vaccines.
