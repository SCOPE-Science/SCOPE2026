# Scientific review: adjacent marked-queue escape clustering

## Correctness — PASS

The claim specifies every probabilistic conditioning event: the queue is stationary, the two customers are consecutive among all arrivals, and their two original marks are conditioned susceptible. Its exponential/Poisson assumptions permit two independent derivations of the equal marginals and a separate joint embedded-arrival transform. The central nonstandard step is the conditional law \(Q_1=(Q+1-G)_+\), with \(G\) geometric for potential service completions before the next arrival, and the resulting nonnegative double-series evaluation of \(\mathbb E[u^Qz^{Q_1}]\). This is reconstructed explicitly in the proof. All series converge for \(0<\rho,p<1\); every rational denominator is positive on this domain. Algebraically subtracting the square of the common marginal gives a strictly positive covariance and then the simple exact correlation. Heavy-traffic limiting probabilities follow by continuity of the rational formulas at fixed \(p>0\). The included exact-rational, finite-tail-bound, and independently simulated checks corroborate, but do not replace, the analytic proof. No positive exposure threshold or secondary infection mechanism is established.

## Originality — PASS, qualified by historical risk

The motivating 2026 Newman–Pender paper supplies the single-tag risk and the queue infection mechanism, but not the conditional **two-tag** joint infection-status law: knowing a single tagged customer's infection probability does not determine correlation with its successive arrival. The inspected 2026 Boxma–Pender Theorem 4 is a joint transform of **two contact-duration components for one tagged customer**, not the bivariate infection status of two tags; their Theorem 3 is likewise one-tag overlap information. The 2023 Palomo–Pender article studies overlap *between adjacent customers*, but not their joint contagion from all other marked customers. Kang et al. supply population-level overlap-risk motivation rather than this exact two-conditioned-tag probability. Topical searches, comparisons with published research-index results and accessible local research claims found no matching covered formula; this is evidence of no identified overlap, not proof of historical uniqueness. Prior general marked-queue reward processes or unpublished paired-contact calculations remain a possible priority risk. The exact pair law is not presented as a newly invented queueing mechanism.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The instantaneous-contact boundary is a natural baseline of the new queue-infection model, rather than an arbitrary numerical slice. The full bivariate law reveals positive dependence, and the unexpectedly simple correlation \(2\rho/(1+\rho+2p\rho)\) translates immediately into an exact two-customer infection-count variance inflation factor relative to independent outcomes. Its nonvanishing heavy-traffic limit gives a precise rare-escape clustering mechanism in a capacity-sensitive service setting. This motivated dependence statistic cannot be recovered from the already known one-customer infection rate alone. The theorem deliberately does not treat longer lags, positive contact-time thresholds, general service times or infectiousness acquired after entry.
