# Review: Exact two-sensor D-optimality and a parity gap in cyclic heat observability

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem defines the full two-sensor observation Gramian for the symmetric combinatorial-cycle heat semigroup at exactly \(r=\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1\) times. Its spectral factorization separates the \(r\) distinct temporal Vandermonde columns from two spatial sensor channels. The Fourier sine blocks give the exact full-rank conditions for odd and even cycles. A proved rectangular Gram determinant identity, the finite sine product, and the constant/Nyquist nullspace complement give both determinant formulas. The strict even-parity gap follows from the nonzero cross-entry of the inverse Vandermonde Gram matrix, established coefficientwise via Lagrange polynomials at positive distinct spectral nodes; strict positive definiteness yields the gap interval. Exact integer Bareiss verification covers 96 instances with two positive spectral shifts, and a direct high-precision heat-kernel replay covers 35 instances. These checks are supplemental to the general proof.

## Originality — PASS, with specified literature limits
Parlangeli and Notarstefano, *On the reachability and observability of path and cycle graphs* (arXiv:1109.3556v1, 2011), provide qualitative cycle observability analysis but no full observation-information determinant or even-parity D-optimal gap in the inspected results. Summers, Cortesi and Lygeros (arXiv:1404.7665v1, 2014) study general submodularity of log-determinant Gramian criteria, not this closed-form placement classification. Alaeddini and Morgansen (arXiv:1609.02654v1, 2016) motivate Gramian-determinant sensor location for an epidemic-network model without this cyclic Fourier calculation. A closely related publicly available *Sharp spectral-demixed two-sensor frame bounds on finite cycles* (2026) explicitly treats the spectrally demixed low-pass output norm, not the determinant of untreated finite-time trajectories; its frame bounds do not imply the parity correction from the inverse temporal Gramian. *Universal endpoint optimality for dynamical sampling on normalized paths* addresses a single sensor and a path graph. The common Fourier, Vandermonde, and Schur-complement tools are not claimed as new. The asserted contribution is the fully evaluated untreated time-trajectory determinant and its strict parity classification; an exhaustive historical survey and independent expert check have not occurred.

## Value — PASS
The D-optimal placement problem is natural for finite-horizon state reconstruction with equal independent sensor noise. The result gives a complete exact answer for every cycle size and diffusion time, including the non-obvious equal-determinant degeneracy for every coprime spacing on odd cycles, and a strict objective distinction between observable even and odd spacings on even cycles. The six-cycle is a concrete instance where full rank does not mean D-optimality. Those features matter for sensor deployment and distinguish determinant optimization from the qualitative observability problem. The exact parity gap also identifies the role of constant/Nyquist temporal interactions, which is absent from a spectrally demixed sensor objective.

## Risks and limitations
The general graph control setting and qualitative placement theorems are well established, so their ingredients are not novel. The spectral assumptions and noise/measurement normalization are essential. There is no independent audit.
