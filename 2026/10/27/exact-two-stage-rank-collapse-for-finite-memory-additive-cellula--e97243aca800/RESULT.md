# Exact two-stage rank collapse for finite-memory additive cellular automata

## Finding
Let \(p\) be prime, \(a\ge1\) and \(k\ge1\) be integers, \(L=p^a\), and \(q=(L-1)/(p-1)\). On a periodic ring of \(L\) sites over \(\mathbb F_p\), consider the \(k\)-layer cellular automaton
\[
u_j^{n+1}=\sum_{h=0}^{k-1}\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}u_{j-r}^{n-h}\quad(\bmod p),\qquad j\in\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z.
\]
Its global one-step map \(M\) acts on all \(k\) initial layers together, a vector space of dimension \(kL\) over \(\mathbb F_p\). Its exact nilpotent Jordan form is
\[
M\sim J_{k(q+1)}(0)\oplus J_{kq}(0)^{\oplus(p-2)}.
\]
The second summand is empty for \(p=2\). Thus for every integer \(t\ge0\), the exact image dimension is
\[
\operatorname{rank}(M^t)=
\begin{cases}
kL-(p-1)t,&0\le t\le kq,\\
k(q+1)-t,&kq\le t\le k(q+1),\\
0,&t\ge k(q+1).
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, the **least** time by which every initial configuration is zero equals
\[
\tau=k(q+1)=k\left(1+\frac{p^a-1}{p-1}\right).
\]
For \(p>2\), rank falls by \(p-1\) dimensions per step until time \(kq\), but by only one per step in the final \(k\)-step interval. Every one of the \(p^{kL}\) initial layered configurations has a well-defined exact extinction time \(T\); putting \(d_t=kL-\operatorname{rank}(M^t)\), their exact counts are \(\#\{T=0\}=1\) and
\[
\#\{T=t\}=p^{d_t}-p^{d_{t-1}},\quad 1\le t\le\tau.
\]
Consequently a fraction exactly \(1-1/p\) of *all* configurations extinguish at the final possible step \(\tau\).

## Assumptions and scope
All additions are in \(\mathbb F_p\). The spatial shift \(S\) acts by \((Sv)_j=v_{j-1}\), with indices modulo \(L=p^a\). The initial state consists of the arbitrary \(k\) layers \((u^0,u^{-1},\ldots,u^{1-k})\); the updating rule is applied for successive nonnegative times. For \(p=2\), the second Jordan summand is absent and the formula gives \(\tau=kL\). The requirement \(a\ge1\) is essential to this stated block-multiplicity description.

## Proof
Write \(V=\mathbb F_p^{L}\) and \(T_0=I+S+\cdots+S^{p-1}\) for the spatial neighborhood operator. The group-algebra module \(V\), generated by a unit basis vector under the cyclic shift, is \(\mathbb F_p[S]/(S^L-1)\). Because \(L=p^a\), the characteristic-\(p\) identity gives \(S^L-1=(S-1)^L\). Put \(z=S-1\). Then \(V\cong\mathbb F_p[z]/(z^L)\), and the geometric-sum identity gives
\[
T_0=\frac{S^p-1}{S-1}=z^{p-1}
\]
as a polynomial identity (the quotient is interpreted in \(\mathbb F_p[S]\), before substituting the nilpotent shift).

As \(L=(p-1)q+1\), the residue-class subspaces
\[
W_r=\operatorname{span}\{z^{r+(p-1)j}:0\le j<\ell_r\},\quad 0\le r\le p-2,
\]
with \(\ell_0=q+1\) and \(\ell_r=q\) for \(1\le r\le p-2\), give \(V=\bigoplus_r W_r\). Multiplication by \(T_0=z^{p-1}\) is one nilpotent Jordan block of size \(\ell_r\) on each \(W_r\).

The layered update on \(V^k\) is the block companion map
\[
M(v_0,\ldots,v_{k-1})=(T_0(v_0+\cdots+v_{k-1}),v_0,\ldots,v_{k-2}).
\]
It preserves each \(W_r^k\). In the commutative ring \(\mathbb F_p[T_0]\), the companion-polynomial identity states
\[
M^k=T_0(I+M+\cdots+M^{k-1}).
\]
If \(T_0^{\ell_r}=0\) on \(W_r\), this immediately implies \(M^{k\ell_r}=0\) on \(W_r^k\), since \(M\) commutes with the diagonal action of \(T_0\). Thus the restricted map is nilpotent. Its kernel consists **exactly** of vectors \((0,\ldots,0,w)\) with \(T_0w=0\), and hence has dimension one. A nilpotent linear map with one-dimensional kernel has precisely one Jordan block. Since \(\dim W_r^k=k\ell_r\), that block has size \(k\ell_r\). This proves the claimed Jordan decomposition without a spectral or infinite-dimensional limiting assumption.

A nilpotent Jordan block of length \(m\) has \(\operatorname{rank}J_m(0)^t=\max(m-t,0)\). Summing the one long and \(p-2\) short blocks yields the two-stage rank formula and the sharp extinction index. Finally, \(\dim\ker M^t=kL-\operatorname{rank}M^t\), so the number of states extinguished by time \(t\) is \(p^{d_t}\). Subtracting successive counts gives the exact-time histogram. At \(t=\tau-1\), the long Jordan block contributes rank one and all others contribute zero, so exactly \(p^{kL}-p^{kL-1}\) configurations remain nonzero until the final step.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` constructs the actual block companion matrices over \(\mathbb F_p\), computes ranks of *all* powers through the nilpotency index by modular Gaussian elimination, and compares them with the claimed formula for 17 parameter triples with \(p\in\{2,3,5\}\), \(a\in\{1,2\}\) and \(k\in\{1,2,3\}\) wherever the total matrix size is at most 50. Output is in `verification_output.txt`. For example, \(p=3\), \(L=9\), \(k=2\) gives Jordan sizes \(10\) and \(8\) and sharp extinction time \(10\), rather than the general bound \(18\). The finite calculations are diagnostics, not the proof for arbitrary parameters.

## Relationship to prior work
Endo's 2026 paper defines the same multivalued, higher-order additive rule and establishes an upper extinction-time bound of \(kp^a\). The present result computes the complete nilpotent Jordan partition, the rank of **every** power, sharp extinction time, and exact histogram of extinction times. Its structural difference from a mere improvement of a bound is the universal two-stage image-dimension profile, with \(p-1\) directions lost per iteration followed by a final one-direction phase. At \(p=2\), the original upper bound is sharp; for \(p>2\) it need not be. The classical one-step algebraic analyses by Martin–Odlyzko–Wolfram and Lettieri–Stevens–Thomas provide methods and context but do not, in the inspected material, state this memory-layer block decomposition. Guan–He's 1986 paper treats higher-order additive automata at a much broader level; only its abstract could be inspected, and its potential coverage of the stated closed formulas remains an explicit priority risk.

## Limitations
Only the specified prime-field, \(p\)-consecutive-neighbor, equal-memory-weight rule on a prime-power ring is classified. Arbitrary coefficients, non-prime-power rings, different memories, and nonlinear cellular automata are not covered. The originality assessment is limited by unavailable complete historical sources, particularly Guan–He (1986). The proof establishes a finite-dimensional algebraic result; no claim of independent mathematical validation is made.
