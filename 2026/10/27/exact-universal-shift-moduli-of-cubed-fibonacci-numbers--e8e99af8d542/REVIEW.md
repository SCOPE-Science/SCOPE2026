# Assessment of exact universal shift moduli of cubed Fibonacci numbers

**Correctness — PASS.** The degree-three Binet roots give a fourth-order recurrence. The determinant of four consecutive Fibonacci-pair evaluations is of absolute value \(2\), and a separate parity witness removes the extra-factor ambiguity. Cassini and doubling identities force the three even cases, including the precise \(3\)-adic correction when \(8\mid k\). The standalone exact-integer verifier covers shifts through \(500\); it does not substitute for the all-shifts proof.

**Originality — PASS under inspected evidence.** The closest sources concern ordinary Pisano periods, transformations of recurrence sequences, or gcds of consecutive square sums. Actual research-record searches for cubic Fibonacci modular shifts and aliases returned related but nonequivalent findings. Ordinary Pisano periods cannot cover the period-shortening witness modulo \(9\). A potentially equivalent unindexed theorem remains an unresolved priority risk; originality is not certified by the absence of hits.

**Value — PASS.** This is a sharp all-parameter determination of a natural modular periodicity invariant of Fibonacci cubes, rather than a bounded enumeration. It separates four arithmetic regimes and detects a nontrivial cubic-period phenomenon.

**Closest literature and limits.** Wall (1960), Barbero–Cerruti–Murru (arXiv:1105.2143), Čerin (2013), and Mbirika–Spilker (arXiv:2204.12626) were compared; the full text of Wall was not read. The theorem does not classify the least period for each separate modulus without additional inversion, and the computation is finite. Independent mathematical audit and exhaustive publication-priority review remain outstanding.
