# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
Every binary word of weight three has a unique nonnegative four-gap representation. The three-deck fixes the relevant shorter counts, giving an integral rank-three system with one-dimensional kernel. The quadratic \(001\) count has exactly the two possible shifts stated in the proof; identities for all remaining length-three patterns verify sufficiency, while nonnegative-gap boundaries verify feasibility. The involution has only singleton and doubleton orbits. Enumerating the unique representative of each doubleton by \(b=c+3s\), \(d=e+s\) yields the conjectured rational series for all lengths. Independent scattered-subsequence dynamic programming checks 52,360 words of lengths 3–34 and a second explicit three-index enumeration checks 495 words; neither finite test is treated as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Rigo–Salimov (2013) defines binomial equivalence but addresses complexity of infinite words; Lejeune–Rigo–Rosenfeld (2020) investigates classes of finite words primarily under 2-binomial equivalence and general language-growth constraints. The highly relevant full text by Chrisnata et al. contains the same weight-three deck model and the one-dimensional affine kernel (Lemma 24), plus a lower bound (Theorem 27), but expressly labels the precise generating-function identity *Conjecture 1* (p. 17) and reports checking only twenty coefficients. The present all-length sufficiency and exact collision-pair orbit count closes that specific stated gap. Searches for exact formulas, equivalent terminology, broader coverage and overlap found no proof already supplying this result. An unindexed later proof is a residual possibility; a failed search is not a mathematical priority certificate.

## Value — PASS
The work proves an explicit unresolved conjecture in the primary literature about scattered-subsequence reconstruction. It determines the exact number of distinguishable observations for an infinite fixed-composition family and classifies all ambiguity fibers, yielding an exact reconstruction-code capacity as well as limiting ambiguity density. The theorem is not merely a finite-table extension: its principal new content is the all-length enumeration and the structural equivalence criterion.

## Scientific boundaries
The result covers precisely binary weight-three words and three-decks with multiplicity. It does not resolve unrestricted-weight deck counts, general alphabets or general deck length. Existing affine-kernel observations receive explicit credit; no independent audit has been conducted.
