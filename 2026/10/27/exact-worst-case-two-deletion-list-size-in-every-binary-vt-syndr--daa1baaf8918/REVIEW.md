# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For one insertion the increments are 0 through w for inserting a zero and w+1 through n for inserting a one, with repetitions exactly the equivalent positions across constant runs. Thus the n distinct intermediate words over a fixed read map to unique parents in each fixed syndrome, and every two-deletion parent is reached. If two deleted coordinates were in different runs, their two individual deletions give different intermediates, so a singleton fiber requires a doubled symbol in one gap. The doubled-zero and doubled-one checksums are the two step-2 progressions in the proof, with opposite parity. Each has fewer than N terms for N=n+1: for odd N each can hit a residue at most once, and for even N at most one progression can hit that residue and at most twice. At most two singleton fibers imply 2L≤n+2. For sharpness, the all-zero read uses complementary pairs, or for odd n and odd a the fixed-point-free residue involution with its zero-incident pair removed. The odd-n even-a cases use the stated short supports and triples; for a≥6 the pairs incident to 0 and 1 are distinct and remove exactly two pairs. For even n the five triple branches have sums N+r, and the retained one is respectively at r, r+2, r, r+1, r+2, shifted back by 0,2,0,1,2 positions. They have distinct below/equal/above-r patterns except the second and last branches, distinguished by r−1; each branch recovers its original pair. The short supports and reversal finish the construction. Direct parent-word enumeration also confirms the n=2 exception (2,1,1).

## originality

PASS

The supported distinct statement is the sharp all-syndrome maximum for exactly two arbitrary deletions from ordinary binary VT classes, with achievable reads and explicit parents. The inspected closest results concern averages, lower bounds, candidate generation, deletion balls in the opposite direction, or different code/channel families.

## value

PASS

A decoder for a specified VT class needs the worst possible number of distinct candidates, not a class-averaged typical list size. The sharp improvement over the immediate n-candidate bound is substantial, and the proof explains it through the one-deletion intermediate fibers and supplies worst-case reads for every class rather than a single favorable syndrome.
