# Exact zeta function and period spectrum for ternary cyclic short-square avoidance

## Finding

Let \(c_n\) be the number of *labeled* ternary circular words \(w=w_0\cdots w_{n-1}\in\{0,1,2\}^n\) for which the bi-infinite periodic extension \(w^\infty\) has no adjacent equal blocks of lengths \(1\), \(2\), or \(3\). Here the positions \(0,\ldots,n-1\) are distinguished; repetitions of a shorter primitive word are allowed, provided they obey the stated avoidance. Then, as a formal power series,

\[
\boxed{\exp\!\left(\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{c_n}{n}z^n\right)
=\frac{1}{(1+z)(1-z-z^3)(1+z^2+z^3+z^4)(1-z^4+z^5+z^7-z^8)^2}.}
\]

Moreover, \(c_n>0\) if and only if \(n\) is even with \(n\ge4\), or \(n\) is odd with \(n\ge11\). Thus precisely the positive lengths \(1,2,3,5,7,9\) have no such periodic ternary word. The numbers of necklace classes under rotation, \(N_n\), and of primitive necklace classes, \(P_n\), are
\[
N_n=\frac1n\sum_{d\mid n}\varphi(d)c_{n/d},\qquad
P_n=\frac1n\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)c_{n/d}.
\]
In the latter expression, \(P_n\) counts primitive rotation orbits with **minimal** period \(n\). The first twenty labeled counts, starting at length one, are
\[
(0,0,0,12,0,18,0,36,0,60,66,174,78,210,210,612,612,1134,1140,2232).
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The alphabet consists of three **distinguishable** letters. The forbidden pattern at block length \(k\in\{1,2,3\}\) consists of \(2k\) consecutive letters with the first and second \(k\)-blocks identical; the starting position may be anywhere in the periodic extension, even across arbitrarily many circular boundaries when \(n<2k\). This periodic-extension convention differs from some finite-once circular avoidance conventions. The counts are of positioned words, not just words up to relabeling or rotation. No restriction is made on the minimal period of a positioned word.

## Proof

**An exact finite directed graph.** Let \(V\) consist of the five-letter ternary words that avoid squares of periods \(1\) and \(2\) internally. There are precisely \(30\) such states. Put a directed edge from \(x_0x_1x_2x_3x_4\) to \(x_1x_2x_3x_4a\) when the resulting six-letter word \(x_0x_1x_2x_3x_4a\) contains no square of periods \(1,2,3\). The adjacency matrix \(A\), indexed lexicographically by \(V\), has size \(30\times30\) and exactly \(42\) directed edges; it is unweighted, and each allowed transition is uniquely specified by its source and added symbol.

For every \(n\ge1\), a labeled periodic word satisfying avoidance determines the length-\(n\) closed state walk whose state at time \(i\) is the five-letter window beginning at \(i\). Conversely a length-\(n\) closed walk determines the \(n\) successive leading symbols and hence exactly one labeled periodic word. The overlap requirements ensure consistency even for \(n<5\). The six-letter edge rule excludes all the forbidden squares in both directions. Therefore
\[
\boxed{c_n=\operatorname{tr}(A^n),\quad n\ge1.}
\]

**The exact determinant.** Form the matrix by iterating all \(3^5\) possible states and all three possible added symbols under the preceding entirely explicit tests. Exact integer calculations yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\det(I-zA)={}&(1+z)(1-z-z^3)(1+z^2+z^3+z^4)\\
&\times(1-z^4+z^5+z^7-z^8)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is an exact polynomial identity, not a fit to a finite initial sequence. To check it independently, the accompanying standalone verifier constructs \(A\) from the definition and calculates the entire monic characteristic polynomial \(\det(\lambda I-A)\) by each of the integer Newton power-sum identities and the Faddeev--LeVerrier recurrence. Both give, coefficient for coefficient,
\[
\lambda^6(\lambda+1)(\lambda^3-\lambda^2-1)
(\lambda^4+\lambda^2+\lambda+1)
(\lambda^8-\lambda^4+\lambda^3+\lambda-1)^2.
\]
Reversing polynomial coefficients gives the displayed determinant. The formal determinant identity
\[
-\log\det(I-zA)=\sum_{n\ge1}\operatorname{tr}(A^n)\frac{z^n}{n}
\]
proves the claimed generating function for **every** positive length. In particular, the exact traces at lengths \(1,2,3,5,7,9\) are zero.

**All other lengths exist.** The following four words have admissible periodic extensions, and their associated graph walks all begin and end at the **same five-symbol state** `01210`:

| Walk length | Circular word written from this common state |
| ---: | --- |
| 4 | `0121` |
| 6 | `012102` |
| 11 | `01210120102` |
| 13 | `0121012010212` |

Each row is checked directly by examining all length-two, length-four, and length-six factors in the periodic extension; the shared-state property can be checked by comparing its first five periodic symbols with `01210`. Concatenation of these closed directed walks produces admissible closed walks of lengths formed by their sums. The positive even integers at least four are exactly the additive semigroup generated by \(4\) and \(6\), and the odd integers at least eleven are exactly \(11\) or \(13\) plus a nonnegative combination of \(4\) and \(6\). Therefore all other lengths occur, establishing the sharp existence classification.

**Rotation orbits.** A rotation by \(j\) positions of a length-\(n\) word fixes exactly the admissible words whose minimal period divides \(\gcd(n,j)\), namely \(c_{\gcd(n,j)}\). Burnside's lemma gives the formula for \(N_n\). Möbius inversion on the possible minimal periods gives \(nP_n=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)c_{n/d}\). Both are exact for all \(n\ge1\).

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_periodic_square_avoidance.py` using a standard Python 3 installation. The program is self-contained: it generates the state graph, computes both integer characteristic-polynomial identities, checks the determinant factorization, exhaustively enumerates all \(3^n\) positioned ternary words for \(1\le n\le11\), compares independent necklace counts for \(1\le n\le9\), computes traces through length thirty, and checks all four constructive closed walks. It prints `TERNARY_PERIODIC_SHORT_SQUARE_PASS` only after every assertion succeeds. The exhaustive short-length checks are cross-checks; the finite graph/determinant identity plus constructive loops prove the infinite statements.

## Relationship to prior work

Kovačević's 2019 preprint on optimal nonconfusable codes under short tandem duplications identifies **linear** words avoiding squares of block lengths at most three and proves capacity results. Jain and collaborators earlier formulated the associated constrained-string graph and studied asymptotic rates. The present contribution is the **exact periodic closed-walk zeta function**, its complete all-length spectrum, and the resulting exact necklace enumerations, not the known irreducibility criterion or graph construction. Shur's ternary *fully* square-free circular words are different: they exclude squares of every possible block length within a finite circular word, while this theorem excludes only lengths one to three in the infinite periodic extension. For example, this theorem has \(c_{10}=60\), whereas full circular square-freeness has no examples at length ten.

## Limitations

Only three letters and forbidden equal-block lengths \(1,2,3\) are treated. The proof does not provide the analogous determinant for other alphabets or other forbidden lengths; the result also does not assert full square-freeness. The factorization is checked by transparent exact integer algorithms, not by a separately published hand-derived factor proof. There may be equivalent unindexed accounts of this specialized periodic spectrum; absence from consulted texts alone does not establish priority. No independent mathematical audit has been conducted.
