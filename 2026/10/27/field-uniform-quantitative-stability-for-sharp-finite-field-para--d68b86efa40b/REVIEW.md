# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

For every finite field of characteristic greater than three, the published sharp inequality supplies the sixth-moment benchmark. A direct quadratic Gauss-sum calculation, Fourier expansion of an arbitrary complex mean-zero perturbation, and orthogonality establish exact amplitude and phase half-Hessian coefficients, including their multiplicities. With normalized source norm the two positive levels are \(3-6/q+12/q^2\) and \(3-6/q\), and all complex scalar directions have zero curvature. The six-factor Hölder estimate bounds all cubic-and-higher terms by \(46C_qr^3\), giving the claimed field-independent radius \(r\le1/64\). Arithmetic at the endpoint uses \(q\ge5\), \(C_q\le29/25\), and \(3-6/q\ge9/5\). Direct finite checks at five field sizes, including an extension field, passed 1069 assertions. These checks are not the universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, best knowledge

The closest primary source, González-Riquelme–Oliveira e Silva (arXiv:2405.16647, Theorem 1.3, Section 6), proves the global sharp parabolic extension inequality, not the transverse half-Hessian or a field-size-uniform finite-radius remainder. The published finite-parabola characteristic-three calculation `158763dab4f3` proves analogous local curvature only in the disjoint excluded characteristic, without a nonasymptotic uniform neighborhood. The anisotropic paraboloid calculation `80ecd3989428` treats a fourth moment and a different surface; finite-field moment-curve endpoint work uses different exponents. Explicitly checked public mathematical records and accessible own historical claims revealed no covering assertion. Residual risk: differently formulated older local-stability estimates may exist outside indexed or inspected sources; a self-review cannot establish global bibliographic uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The sharp global constant alone gives no quantitative control on the deficit under complex-valued perturbations. The result identifies a meaningful phase-amplitude curvature split and proves an explicit field-independent interval of coercivity near each true parabolic extremizer. It makes near-extremizer robustness usable even as the field size grows. This is a local statement, not a purported global stability classification or an optimal neighborhood.
