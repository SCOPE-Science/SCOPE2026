## correctness

PASS

On the normalized circle a nonzero Hilbert transform of a real trigonometric polynomial has mean zero, attains zero, and has no positive-measure absolute-value levels. Its W quantity is therefore the supremum of E(lambda)/D(lambda), with E the positive-part integral and D the strict superlevel measure. At every lambda below the maximum, Osękowski's shifted obstacle-majorant difference at r=|Hf|-lambda>0 is strictly positive where |f|<1, since the arctangent sum is zero at A=0 and has strictly positive derivative for A>0. This set has positive measure in each nonempty superlevel set of a normalized nonconstant polynomial. The source mean-value inequality gives E-D<0; the ratio is continuous and tends to zero at the maximum since it is bounded by max|Hf|-lambda. This makes the supremum strictly below one, rather than merely each level ratio. Uniform perturbations by epsilon change W by at most 2 epsilon, so compactness gives an attained strict gap on each unit sphere. Constants give C_0=0 and nesting gives weak monotonicity. In the global sharpness witness, a rearrangement value with positive excess gives a tail ratio at least as large since D(lambda) is at most the chosen rearrangement measure. Moving the level to a nearby regular level from above preserves the ratio up to arbitrary error by right continuity. Poisson means converge in L1 for the transform (bounded input gives an L2 transform), giving convergence of the two tail quantities there. Fixed-radius truncations of input and transform converge uniformly, and the input norms converge to a value at most one. This yields finite-band ratios arbitrarily close to one.
