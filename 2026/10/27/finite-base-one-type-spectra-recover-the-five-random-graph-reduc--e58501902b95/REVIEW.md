# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The nonalgebraic complete one-types of an \(\omega\)-categorical reduct over a finite individually named base are exactly the pointwise-stabilizer orbits on remaining vertices. Rado extension realizes all binary adjacency vectors. The finite local complement/switch description yields the simultaneous equations \(s_i+s_j+c=0\) inside the base and translations \(d_i=s_i+s_x+c\) to the new vertex. The proof checks every case \(m=0,1,2\), and for all larger \(m\) the triple sum forces \(c=0\). The sharp prefix and exact tail equalities then follow algebraically. Finite exhaustive computations for \(m\le7\) and independent tuple edge-action checks for \(n\le5\) pass; these are corroborative only.

A claim merely classifying those groups is fully covered and is not presented as new. Their accessible full texts do not state a complete finite-base one-type count table or compare the sharp three-parameter reconstruction against all-tail collisions. Semantic database searches for these quantities and their synonyms returned neighboring results on random-poset, random-tournament, and bipartite-graph reducts, none of which implies the exact five-group random-graph spectrum. No dominating published formula was located. The original Thomas 1991 full paper remained inaccessible beyond its abstract, and the finite-bit derivation is short, so implicit publication elsewhere remains a significant residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The pointwise-stabilizer one-type profile is a canonical model-theoretic numerical invariant, not an arbitrary graph slice. The result completes that invariant for every member of the classical five-reduct classification and proves a sharp quantitative inverse statement: finite profile prefixes recover all groups at exactly three named parameters, whereas arbitrarily long tails can fail to distinguish distinct reducts. This distinction between finite information and eventual asymptotic information is a structurally meaningful boundary of a natural invariant, despite the elementary finite-field calculation.
