Two equally weighted strictly convex quadratic losses can make the fixed-parameter non-negative Gauss–Newton conditional-mean map have five fixed points even though their average has a unique minimizer; the five-root regime becomes possible at the sharp threshold \(\sigma>4+4\sqrt2\). At \(\sigma=11\) an explicit instance has three locally attracting mean-map equilibria, including two strictly suboptimal ones; this is a statement about the conditional-mean map, not convergence of random trajectories.
