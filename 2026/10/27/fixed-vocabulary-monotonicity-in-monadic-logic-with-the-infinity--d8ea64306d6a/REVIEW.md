# Review

## Correctness

PASS. The reduction to satisfiability preserves the definition of monotonicity exactly. For fixed vocabulary size, the rank-\(q\) types are determined by the exact counts below \(q\), the finite-at-least-\(q\) category, and the infinite category. The recursive evaluator stores only polynomially many bits along a branch. The QBF reduction forces exactly two elements and maps Boolean quantification to first-order quantification without using the infinity quantifier.

## Originality

PASS. The closest primary source is Carreiro, Facchini, Venema and Zanasi, arXiv:1809.03262 and DOI:10.1007/s00153-021-00797-0. It proves effective normal forms and decidability for monotonicity but explicitly leaves complexity questions unexplored. Targeted searches for fixed-vocabulary ME-infinity monotonicity complexity found adjacent but non-equivalent results. Search failure is not treated as proof of novelty; the residual risk is that a relevant result may be indexed under a different formulation.

## Value

PASS. The theorem gives a precise fixed-vocabulary boundary and establishes hardness even for one unary predicate. This distinguishes the fixed-signature regime from combined-complexity results in which the vocabulary grows with the input.
