Summation by parts controls the reciprocal match stage, and a coupling to independent uniform orientation bits gives the displayed finite-size total-variation inequality. The argument covers every fixed integer \(r\ge2\), all \(n\ge2r+2\), and every common positive adsorption rate. Independent exact rational graph-level and three-count calculations agree in finite cases; these checks are corroborative rather than infinite certificates.

A previously recorded tripartite adsorption theorem concerns the opposite asymptotic regime of one dominant class with two growing minority classes and a Poisson count. None of the inspected stated results directly implies the fixed-impurity law. Historical priority is not exhaustively certified.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The result explains a persistent finite vacancy distribution caused by a bounded impurity population in an otherwise perfectly matchable bipartite substrate. Its complete limiting law holds for every fixed impurity count with a uniform-in-\(n\), explicit rate bound. Already at \(r=2\), the asymptotic chance of imperfect jamming is \(1/2\), despite the impurity proportion tending to zero. The object, observable, and boundary regime are natural in irreversible deposition and random greedy matching.
