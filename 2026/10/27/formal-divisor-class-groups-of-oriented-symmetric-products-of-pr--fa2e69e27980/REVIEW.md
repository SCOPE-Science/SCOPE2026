# Scientific review: formal class groups of oriented symmetric powers

## Correctness (PASS)
The claimed scope is all complex dimensions \(n\geq2\), all \(d\geq5\), and every complex closed point, indexed by its coincidence partition. The argument identifies codimension-two-or-higher diagonals and the equivariant Picard group of the free quotient. Perfectness of \(A_d\) eliminates character ambiguity, while the naturally symmetric tensor product of the hyperplanes descends as an ample invertible sheaf, so the entire Weil group is Cartier. Completion at a collision point is a formal invariant ring of the parity-kernel stabilizer; no tangent pseudoreflections occur. Benson's classical character formula reduces the result to the explicitly proved abelianizations: \(0\), \(\mathbb Z/3\) for exactly one block of size three/four, or \((\mathbb Z/2)^{r-1}\) for multiple nontrivial blocks. Reid–Tai ages give terminality, and determinant character gives the Gorenstein and anticanonical claims. The exact enumeration checker corroborates ten group profiles; it does not establish the universal claim. Source foundations: Chen–Hu arXiv:2610.20239v1; Schmitt 2024 Theorem 2.1 (Benson); Geraschenko–Satriano 2015 section 5.1.

## Originality (PASS; limited priority claim)
Closest **dominant ingredients** are classical: the character formula for finite small quotients is due to Benson, and Geraschenko–Satriano already study factorial quotients by perfect groups and supply related projective examples. Neither of these classical mechanisms is claimed as new. Chen–Hu's October 2026 symmetric-power article treats the *full symmetric quotient's Chow groups*, not the oriented quotient's pointwise formal divisor class groups. The assessed distinction is the explicit, exhaustive collision-partition classification for the natural orientation double cover, including the algebraic-versus-formal factoriality contrast, and the precise \(2\)- versus \(3\)-primary switch. Inspected literature and statement-level searches did not identify this complete geometric assertion, though an unindexed earlier derivation from the general character theorem remains possible. This is a self-review priority assessment, not an independent certification.

## Value (PASS)
The oriented cover of symmetric products is a natural geometric quotient, not an arbitrary invariant-ring slice. The formal class group is a local divisor invariant that controls whether Weil classes on analytic germs are principal and highlights the distinction from algebraic factoriality. An exact classification at every collision stratum is a complete structural invariant with a sharp formal-factoriality criterion, rather than a small numerical extension. The result gives explicit terminal Fano examples whose Zariski local rings are all UFDs but whose formal local rings carry \(2\)- or \(3\)-torsion classes. No classification of higher Chow groups or resolutions is implied.

## Limitations and priority risk
All statements are characteristic zero, closed-point, \(n\geq2,d\geq5\). The invariant-ring and Picard principles are standard; a prior paper could implicitly contain this exact partition formula. Related earlier factoriality and group quotient constructions limit the novelty claim to the specific complete geometric classification. Computational tests span only a finite selection of stabilizer types. The proof provides the actual universal statement.
