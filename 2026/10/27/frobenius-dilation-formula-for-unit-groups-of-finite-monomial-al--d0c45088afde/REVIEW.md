# Review

## Correctness

The proof reduces the torsion equation to \(z^{p^r}=0\) in the maximal ideal. Frobenius sends each standard monomial to its exponent vector multiplied by \(p^r\), or to zero if that monomial is in the ideal. Distinct exponent vectors do not collide, so the kernel count is exactly \(q^{N-s_r}\). The second-difference formula for invariant factors follows from \(\log_p|U[p^r]|=\sum_i\min(r,a_i)\) for a finite abelian \(p\)-group.

## Originality

published-finding corpus searches for unit groups of finite monomial algebras and for their \(p^r\)-torsion profiles returned adjacent results on torsion units in local rings, monomiality of short local algebras, and group-ring units, but no matching standard-monomial formula. The full text of Zhuravlev (2017) was inspected: its theorem treats a single radical-layer profile with \(J^4=0\), not arbitrary zero-dimensional monomial ideals. Zhuravlev (2014) is a plausible adjacent source; only its abstract and metadata were accessible, and that abstract describes another fixed layer profile. These comparisons support a broader scope, but do not prove global novelty.

## Value

The unit group is a natural invariant of a finite local algebra and is used in distinguishing ring structures. The formula turns its complete abstract group structure into a finite combinatorial calculation from the standard monomial set, across arbitrary embedding dimension and radical length, instead of a separate multiplication-table classification for each Hilbert profile.

## Limitations

The result is restricted to commutative finite monomial quotients over finite fields. The general proof, rather than the finite experiments, establishes the theorem. A broader theorem in the unit-group or Witt-vector literature may imply the same profile; independent review remains outstanding.
