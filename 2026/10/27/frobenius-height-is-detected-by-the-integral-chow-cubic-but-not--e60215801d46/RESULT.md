# Frobenius height is detected by the integral Chow cubic but not by any prime-field reduction

## Finding

Fix a prime \(p\). For every integer \(m\geq1\), put \(q=p^m\) and define the smooth projective threefold
\[
X_m=\left\{([x_0:x_1:x_2],[y_0:y_1:y_2])\in\mathbb P^2_{\overline{\mathbb F}_p}\times(\mathbb P^2_{\overline{\mathbb F}_p})^\vee:\ \sum_{i=0}^2x_i^q y_i=0\right\}.
\]
Writing \(h\) and \(u\) for the hyperplane classes from the two projective factors, there is an **integral**, graded-ring presentation
\[
\operatorname{CH}^{*}(X_m)\cong\frac{\mathbb Z[h,u]}{(h^3,u^2-qhu+q^2h^2)},\qquad\deg h=\deg u=1.
\]
In particular, the top-intersection binary cubic is
\[
F_m(a,b)=\deg(ah+bu)^3=3ab(a+qb),\quad\operatorname{disc}(F_m)=81q^2.
\]
Therefore, for all \(m,m'\geq1\), the graded integral Chow rings are isomorphic **if and only if** \(m=m'\). In contrast, for every prime \(\ell\), their graded Chow rings after tensoring with \(\mathbb F_\ell\) are all mutually isomorphic as \(m\) varies; the same holds over \(\mathbb Q\). Over \(\mathbb F_p\), all the varieties have the same zeta function
\[
Z(X_m,T)=\frac{1}{(1-T)(1-pT)^2(1-p^2T)^2(1-p^3T)}.
\]
Thus an integral multiplicative intersection invariant distinguishes every Frobenius height despite the inability of rational Chow rings, all prime-field reductions, and zeta functions to do so.

## Assumptions and scope

Here \(p\) is an arbitrary rational prime, \(m\) is a positive integer, the varieties and Chow rings are taken over the algebraic closure of \(\mathbb F_p\), and \(\operatorname{CH}^{*}\) is the ordinary codimension-graded Chow ring with **integral** cycle coefficients. Comparison of rings is abstract and respects grading but need not preserve the two named divisor generators, the canonical class, or a polarization. The point-count and zeta assertions refer to the displayed models over \(\mathbb F_p\). The integral-ring classification concerns the three-dimensional member \(n=2\) of the higher-dimensional Frobenius-incidence family; no classification for \(n>2\) is asserted.

## Proof

**(1) Smoothness and a projective-bundle presentation.** On \(B=\mathbb P^2_{\overline{\mathbb F}_p}\) the three sections \(x_0^q,x_1^q,x_2^q\) of \(\mathcal O_B(q)\) have no simultaneous zero. Consequently
\[
0\longrightarrow E_q\longrightarrow\mathcal O_B^{\oplus3}\xrightarrow{(x_0^q,x_1^q,x_2^q)}\mathcal O_B(q)\longrightarrow0
\]
is exact with \(E_q\) locally free of rank two. A point of \(X_m\) over \([x]\) is exactly a one-dimensional subspace of \((E_q)_{[x]}\), so \(X_m\cong\mathbb P_{\mathrm{lines}}(E_q)\). In particular it is smooth and the first projection is a Zariski-locally-trivial \(\mathbb P^1\)-bundle.

**(2) Integral ring and top products.** Let \(h=c_1(\mathcal O_B(1))\) and let \(u=c_1(\mathcal O_{\mathbb P(E_q)}(1))\), the restriction of the hyperplane class of the second projective factor. The exact sequence gives
\[
c(E_q)=(1+qh)^{-1}=1-qh+q^2h^2.
\]
The integral projective-bundle formula for the lines convention yields
\[
\operatorname{CH}^{*}(X_m)=\mathbb Z[h,u]/(h^3,u^2+c_1(E_q)u+c_2(E_q)),
\]
which is the stated presentation. It is free as a module over \(\mathbb Z[h]/(h^3)\) on \(1,u\), and hence has free graded pieces of ranks \(1,2,2,1\).

The relative hyperplane push-forward is \(\pi_*(u)=1\); therefore \(\deg(h^2u)=1\). The relation gives \(\deg(hu^2)=q\), while \(h^3=0\) and \(u^3=qhu^2-q^2h^2u=0\). The displayed cubic follows by expansion.

**(3) Integral-ring classification without a marked basis.** Suppose an isomorphism of graded integral Chow rings exists between \(X_m\) and \(X_{m'}\). In degree one it is an element of \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)\). In degree three, since both groups are \(\mathbb Z\) generated by \(h^2u\), it is multiplication by \(\pm1\). Thus the binary cubic intersection polynomials differ by an integral unimodular linear substitution and a global sign. The discriminant of a binary cubic changes by the sixth power of the substitution determinant and the fourth power of any global scalar. Both factors here equal one. The binary-cubic formula gives
\[
\operatorname{disc}(3a^2b+3qab^2)=(3)^2(3q)^2=81q^2.
\]
Hence \(q=q'\) because both are positive. With the same fixed prime \(p\), this forces \(m=m'\). Conversely equality of \(m\) gives the identical presentation. An equivalent elementary argument factors the cubic as \(3a\cdot b\cdot(a+qb)\); the unordered absolute determinants of primitive coefficient vectors of its three linear factors are \(\{1,1,q\}\), invariant under unimodular substitutions.

**(4) Prime-field and rational blindness.** For \(\ell=p\), \(q\equiv0\pmod p\) for every \(m\ge1\), so the reduction is always
\[
\mathbb F_p[h,u]/(h^3,u^2).
\]
For a prime \(\ell\ne p\), \(q\) is invertible in \(\mathbb F_\ell\). The change of degree-one generator \(z=q^{-1}u\) puts the relation into
\[
\mathbb F_\ell[h,z]/(h^3,z^2-hz+h^2),
\]
independent of \(m\). The same change works over \(\mathbb Q\), proving the asserted abstract ring isomorphisms. These coefficientwise isomorphisms are not claimed to be compatible integral lifts.

**(5) Point counts.** Every fiber over an \(\mathbb F_{p^r}\)-rational point of \(\mathbb P^2\) is \(\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb F_{p^r}}\). For each \(r\ge1\), therefore,
\[
\#X_m(\mathbb F_{p^r})=(1+p^r+p^{2r})(1+p^r)=1+2p^r+2p^{2r}+p^{3r}.
\]
Exponentiating the generating series of these point counts proves the given zeta function, with no dependence on \(m\).

## Verification

The included standalone script `verify_chow.py` performs exact integer checks of the cubic coefficients, discriminant, intersection products, reductions at six small primes, and invariance of the discriminant under 104 unimodular changes of coordinates for nine sample positive prime powers. It prints `VERIFY_OK heights=9 unimodular_changes=104 prime_reductions=54`. These finite computations are regression checks, not a substitute for the preceding all-prime and all-height proof. Reproduce with `python3 verify_chow.py` using the Python standard library; the code has no external dependencies.

## Relationship to prior work

Brion–Maccan–Srimathy (2026) introduce and study the same Frobenius-incidence homogeneous varieties, determine their Picard generators, and obtain pairwise geometric nonisomorphism by parabolic/Wenzel-function arguments. Their geometric nonisomorphism does **not** imply that their unmarked graded integral Chow rings are nonisomorphic. The additional statement here is a complete classification **by the integral multiplicative Chow ring**, together with its contrast against all prime-field reductions and point counts.

Srinivasan (2015/2017) studies motives of pseudo-homogeneous varieties and explicitly includes the height-one incidence example; the cited equivalences of Chow motives with finite coefficients are equivalences of motives as objects, not identifications of their Chow **multiplication**. Indeed the multiplicative distinction exhibited here is consistent with the same underlying graded additive groups. Shimada (2011) studies a related but different, generally codimension-two Frobenius incidence defined using *two* reciprocal incidence equations, so the intersection pairing studied there is not the present single-equation threefold calculation. The integral projective-bundle formula and binary cubic discriminant theory are standard inputs, not claimed as new.

## Limitations

No statement is made about integral Chow-motive isomorphism, integral cycle rings in higher dimension, canonical compatibility of the residue-field ring identifications, or independent historical priority. This is a short structural calculation for a natural complete family rather than a new general projective-bundle theorem. The earlier Haboush–Lauritzen treatment of varieties of unseparated flags (1993) was located bibliographically but its full text could not be examined, leaving a specific risk of an implicit earlier calculation. No independently conducted research audit has been performed.
