# Functional-cover spectrum and Poisson transition for expansive rooted-star relations

## Finding

Let \(P_k=\{r,a_1,\ldots,a_k\}\) and \(Q_\ell=\{s,b_1,\ldots,b_\ell\}\), for integers \(k,\ell\ge1\), be rooted-star posets with reflexive orders generated by \(r\le a_i\) and \(s\le b_j\), respectively. A total relation \(R\subseteq P_k\times Q_\ell\) is **expansive** when it satisfies both confluence conditions: for every \(x\le y\), \(xRu\) extends to some \(yRv\) with \(u\le v\), and every \(yRv\) lifts from some \(xRu\) with \(u\le v\). Here total means every source point has at least one successor.

Write \(S=R(r)\), \(U_i=R(a_i)\), and \(C=S\cap\{b_1,\ldots,b_\ell\}\), with \(c=|C|\). All such relations, and only such relations, have one of two forms:

1. \(s\in S\), \(S=\{s\}\cup C\), and every \(U_i\) contains \(C\) and is nonempty; or
2. \(s\notin S\), \(S=C\ne\varnothing\), and \(U_i=C\) for every \(i\).

Every expansive \(R\) is a union of graphs of isotone maps \(f:P_k\to Q_\ell\). Let \(\rho(R)\) be the *minimum* number of maps in such a union. The exact rank is
\[
\rho(R)=\begin{cases}
c+\max\bigl(1,\max_{1\le i\le k}|U_i\setminus C|\bigr),&s\in S,\\
c,&s\notin S.
\end{cases}
\]
The sharp uniform range is \(1\le\rho(R)\le\ell+1\); its maximum is attained for every \(k,\ell\ge1\).

There are exactly
\[
N_{k,\ell}=(2^{\ell+1}-1)^k+\sum_{c=1}^{\ell}\binom\ell c\,2^{k(\ell+1-c)}+(2^\ell-1)
\]
labelled expansive relations. Their full *functional-cover rank distribution* has cumulative counts \(A_{k,\ell}(0)=0\) and, for every integer \(t\ge1\),
\[
\begin{split}
A_{k,\ell}(t)={}&\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\min(t,\ell+1)}\binom{\ell+1}{j}\right)^k\\
&+\sum_{c=1}^{\min(\ell,t-1)}\binom\ell c
 \left(\sum_{j=0}^{\min(t-c,\ell+1-c)}\binom{\ell+1-c}{j}\right)^k\\
&+\sum_{c=1}^{\min(\ell,t)}\binom\ell c.
\end{split}
\]
Thus the number with rank exactly \(t\) is \(A_{k,\ell}(t)-A_{k,\ell}(t-1)\). In particular, at \((k,\ell)=(2,2)\), the ranks \(1,2,3\) occur \(11,46,31\) times respectively, among \(88\) relations.

If \(R\) is chosen uniformly among the \(N_{k,\ell}\) expansive relations, the exact size distribution of the **common target-leaf core** \(C\) is
\[
\Pr(|C|=0)=\frac{(2^{\ell+1}-1)^k}{N_{k,\ell}},\qquad
\Pr(|C|=c)=\frac{\binom\ell c\left(2^{k(\ell+1-c)}+1\right)}{N_{k,\ell}}
\quad(1\le c\le\ell).
\]
For any sequence with \(\ell\to\infty\) and \(\ell 2^{-k}\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)\), the size \(|C|\) converges in law to \(\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda)\), while \(\Pr(s\in S)\to1\). The scale \(k\approx\log_2\ell\) therefore marks a nondegenerate Poisson window for globally forced target leaves.

## Assumptions and scope

The orders on \(P_k\) and \(Q_\ell\) are reflexive, with the root below each leaf and no comparison between distinct leaves. Source and target leaves are *labelled*. Expansivity uses both forward and backward confluence for the same source comparison; relation rows are nonempty. Isotone maps preserve these orders. No claim is made for arbitrary posets, empty rows, or distinct semantics for the two confluence directions. The parameter \(k\) is the number of source leaves and \(\ell\) the number of target leaves.

## Proof

**Normal form.** The only nontrivial comparisons in \(P_k\) are \(r\le a_i\). If \(s\in S\), forward confluence from \(s\) is automatic for every nonempty \(U_i\), since \(s\) lies below every target; forward confluence from \(b_j\in C\) forces \(b_j\in U_i\), as a target leaf has no larger element. Backward confluence is automatic because \(s\in S\) lies below all targets. Conversely these conditions suffice. If \(s\notin S\), every root successor is a leaf, so forward confluence forces \(C\subseteq U_i\); backward confluence forces each \(u\in U_i\) to be a leaf in \(C\). Thus \(U_i=C\), and \(C\) is nonempty by totality. Reflexive comparisons add no constraints.

**Functional generation and rank.** Any isotone map with root image \(b_j\) is necessarily constant \(b_j\); a map with root image \(s\) may take independent arbitrary values on the source leaves. If \(s\notin S\), there is exactly one necessary constant map for each of the \(c\) root edges. If \(s\in S\), the \(c\) root edges to members of \(C\) require \(c\) distinct constant maps. Those maps cover \(C\) in each leaf row but cannot cover any member of \(U_i\setminus C\). Each root-\(s\) map covers at most one such extra value per source leaf, and at least one such map is necessary for the root edge to \(s\). Hence the stated lower bound. For equality choose the required constant maps and set the number of root-\(s\) maps to \(t=\max(1,\max_i|U_i\setminus C|)\). Independently in each leaf row, assign the extra values injectively to those \(t\) maps, padding unassigned maps with any element of the nonempty \(U_i\). This is possible because root image \(s\) is below every target. The union of the constructed map graphs is exactly \(R\). Since \(|U_i\setminus C|\le\ell+1-c\), the rank never exceeds \(\ell+1\); equality occurs for \(S=\{s\}\) and \(U_i=Q_\ell\) for all \(i\).

**Counts.** With \(S=\{s\}\), each of the \(k\) leaf rows may be any of the \(2^{\ell+1}-1\) nonempty subsets of \(Q_\ell\). With \(s\in S\) and \(c\ge1\) selected members of \(C\), each \(U_i\) consists of mandatory \(C\) and an arbitrary subset of the other \(\ell+1-c\) targets. There are \(\binom\ell c\,2^{k(\ell+1-c)}\) such relations. Without \(s\in S\), each nonempty \(C\) gives one relation. Summing yields \(N_{k,\ell}\). To count rank \(\le t\), in the \(C=\varnothing\) case select an arbitrary nonempty subset of at most \(t\) of the \(\ell+1\) target points independently per leaf; for \(c\ge1\) and \(s\in S\), the number of extras in each leaf must not exceed \(t-c\), and \(t\ge c+1\); for \(s\notin S\), rank is exactly \(c\). These three disjoint cases yield \(A_{k,\ell}(t)\) and its differences. Notice in particular that \(A_{k,\ell}(\ell+1)=N_{k,\ell}\).

**Poisson limit.** Let \(z\) be a probability-generating variable. The weight polynomial of the core size, by the three cases, is
\[
W_{k,\ell}(z)=(2^{\ell+1}-1)^k+2^{k(\ell+1)}\bigl((1+z2^{-k})^\ell-1\bigr)+\bigl((1+z)^\ell-1\bigr).
\]
The total weight is \(W_{k,\ell}(1)=N_{k,\ell}\). On dividing by \(2^{k(\ell+1)}\), the first term tends to \(1\), the middle term tends to \(e^{\lambda z}-1\), and the last term tends to zero whenever \(\ell2^{-k}\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)\). Therefore \(W_{k,\ell}(z)/W_{k,\ell}(1)\to e^{\lambda(z-1)}\) for \(0\le z\le1\), the probability-generating function of the claimed Poisson law. The negligible last term is precisely the family of root rows without \(s\), proving its probability vanishes.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library checker `checker.py` enumerates all nonempty row-tuples for \(1\le k,\ell\le3\), tests forward and backward confluence directly, compares each relation with the normal form, constructs minimal covers by isotone map graphs, and recomputes all rank counts from the displayed formulas. For \(k,\ell\le2\), an independent dynamic-programming set-cover search over *all* admissible isotone maps checks the claimed minimal rank. The same program tests finite-size approximations in the critical Poisson window. Its output ends with `ALL_CHECKS_OK 88784`. These finite checks are diagnostics, not the proof for all sizes; the displayed arguments prove the unrestricted claims.

## Relationship to prior work

Fernández-Duque, *Fine Selection for Intuitionistic Modal Logic* (arXiv:2609.27078, first posted 2026-09-22), defines expansive confluence and studies the finite-model property for Fischer Servi-style intuitionistic modal logic; its Proposition 2.4 establishes union closure of expansive relations. This union-closure theorem **alone does not assert decomposition into functional graphs**, a sharp number of component maps, or a labelled rank distribution, and is acknowledged as motivating background. The construction here is special to rooted stars and is proved directly.

Published related classifications treat **strict chains** (published-finding corpus record `03b1c1fd9c86`, endpoint normal form for expansive relations) and map counts between **finite weak orders** (record `1cee64311a06`); neither reported statement implies this multivalued branching-star functional-cover spectrum or its Poisson threshold. Counting isotone maps and deriving Poisson limits from independent rare events are standard ingredients, not independently claimed inventions.

## Limitations

The conclusion depends on two-height rooted-star geometry, both confluence axioms and row-totality. No result is asserted for arbitrary finite posets, for proof-theoretic completeness of a modal logic, or for random frames sampled under other distributions. The priority assessment is limited by full-text access to some related archived classifications and by possible older equivalent formulations in multivalued order-preserving map literature. Independent expert verification has not been performed.
