# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
Yuan's Section 3.2 fixes an isometric holomorphic-form basis, and the cyclic automorphism orthogonalizes it. A two-sheet covering substitution followed by an explicitly derived complex beta integral yields every coefficient of the polynomial \(p_d\). The Bergman form is the pullback of \(i\partial\bar\partial\log p_d(|x|^2)\). A variance identity proves that its rank is one off the branch locus and zero precisely at the branch locus. The local curvature expansion is exact. The gamma-series identity follows from Taylor expansions at \(1\) and \(1/2\), and the genus-three lower bound and all-generations upper bound reduce to checked strict rational inequalities. The 2007 canonical/Theta metric is explicitly distinguished from this derived form; differing curvature signs would not constitute a contradiction. Diagnostic numerics are not substituted for the all-genus proof.

## Originality — PASS with residual risk
Yuan's complete Section 3.2 establishes the domains, their Hilbert space dimensions and bases, but neither the normalized gamma kernel coefficients nor an all-genus transverse curvature phase diagram. Castorena's complete available article studies zeros of the curvature of the canonical/Theta metric, which supplies the classical branch mechanism but does not determine the curvature of the distinct positive transverse Bergman form or its genus threshold. Huang's older abstract concerns canonical metrics and degenerations, not these 2026 product domains. Targeted published-finding semantic searches for the curve, kernel, aliases and genus threshold surfaced generic or unrelated material. It remains possible that historical specialized computations on these cyclic superelliptic curves imply the same gamma sign transition without matching title or vocabulary; this unresolved priority risk warrants independent literature review, not a claim of exhaustive global uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
The 2026 counterexamples make degenerate Bergman geometry unusually concrete; the sign of the curvature of the surviving complex direction is not determined by Bergman-space dimension or the standard canonical map alone. The result identifies an exact threshold at genus four, proves it for all genera rather than finitely many examples, and makes the metric and its degeneration strata explicit. Its scientific use is as a geometric benchmark for finite-dimensional pseudoconvex Bergman spaces, not a resolution of positive-definite curvature conjectures.

## Limits
The branch rank-drop is independently known from hyperelliptic canonical-map theory and is background rather than the central novelty. Only the special family and the chosen regular points \(x=0\) have sign classification; complete curvature profiles elsewhere remain open. Historical priority and geometric interpretation require independent audit.
