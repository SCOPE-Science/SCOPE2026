# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The extremal-corner-geodesic characterization follows from the Cartesian-product metric and supplies all \(2n\) inclusion-maximal shortest paths. The endpoint inequalities are necessary and sufficient, including when either row is empty. The inclusion-exclusion polynomial uses two disjoint failure events. The minimality classification proves that every transversal has a two- or three-element transversal subset, and counts these subsets without duplication. Direct graph-metric exhaustive tests for ladder orders \(2\) through \(8\) reproduced both polynomials.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks
The foundational 2021 paper (arXiv:2101.08042) introduces maximal-geodesic transversals but treats complexity, trees, cactus graphs and scalar examples rather than all ladder transversals. The 2022 preprint arXiv:2210.15325 focuses on packing and scalar comparisons (including rook graphs), not this enumeration. Targeted index and literature searches found no ladder counterpart; ladder matching-complex homology and domination are about different set families. Searches cannot exclude all unindexed results or independently establish research priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The explicit full polynomial gives all feasible monitoring-placement counts by selected cardinality. The inclusion-minimal polynomial reveals a sharp six-rung threshold at which nonminimum irredundant solutions emerge, with a cubic exact count; this phenomenon is invisible to the constant minimum transversal number. The new boundary criterion also gives a concise finite-width structural representation.

## Limitations
Only \(P_n\square K_2\), \(n\ge2\), is covered. Computational verification through \(n=8\) is supplementary to the written universal proof. Independent evaluation of the mathematics and the literature priority has not yet been performed.
