# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

An idempotent partial transformation is a retraction onto its image, and its domain either omits the star center, fixes that center, or moves it. These three disjoint cases give exactly the stated leaf-choice counts for each graph-morphism convention; all six rank polynomials are derived with no probabilistic assumptions. The exponential generating functions are formal binomial resummations. The asymptotic limits follow from elementary coefficient bounds that force \(B_m(1)/A_m(1)\to\infty\) and \(m2^m/A_m(1)\to0\). Independent code enumeration of all partial transformations for \(1\le m\le6\), assessing each defining predicate rather than using the proof's classification, reproduces every coefficient. Finite enumeration is corroboration, not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS against inspected sources, with residual risk

Dimitrova–Fernandes–Koppitz, arXiv:2401.11741 (2024), is the closest and most important primary precedent: it defines these same monoids, establishes the element classifications, and computes monoid sizes, Green relations, regularity and generating ranks. Its Section 3 already notes the rank-one restriction on extra strong-weak maps; this piece is acknowledged as prior work. The later arXiv:2410.21084 develops presentations. Searches through their accessible full texts and targeted literature/database queries did not locate the six image-rank-refined idempotent polynomials or either asymptotic limit. The familiar \(A_m\) polynomial for partial transformations and the partial-identity result for the inverse monoid are standard background and not independently new. A possible unindexed treatment through graph retracts or fixed-point set enumeration remains the principal priority risk. Presentations and elementwise criteria can be used to derive the counts, but the observed weak/strong idempotent dichotomy is not among the inspected source statements.

## Value — PASS

The five distinct graph-morphism conditions studied in the 2024 source produce a natural, comparable family of finite monoids. Their **idempotent rank polynomials**, rather than their total cardinalities, determine the distribution of retractions by image size, a meaningful semigroup invariant complementary to the known ranks and Green relations. The formulas expose two otherwise hidden structural facts: equality of strict/strong idempotent sets despite distinct monoids, and the fact that strong-weak idempotents are almost all strict while weak idempotents are overwhelmingly not strong. The result therefore supplies a complete, reusable rank-refined invariant across the entire family and an exact asymptotic boundary, not an isolated small-parameter calculation.
