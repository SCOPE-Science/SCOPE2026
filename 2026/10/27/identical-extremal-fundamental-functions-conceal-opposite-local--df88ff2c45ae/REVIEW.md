# Review

## Correctness — PASS
The weighted \(L^2\) and \(L^1\) spaces are valid Banach function spaces because all weights are bounded below by one and their excess masses are integrable. A measure-preserving interval swap preserves each target extremal fundamental function. The maxima ratio has an explicit geometric-series upper bound. Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz identifies the exact localized norm as the square root of the maximal integral of the joint quotient \(v_i^2/w\). The aligned quotient equals \(n^2\) on the spike intervals and yields \(\mathcal A_0(a)\sim\sqrt a\log(1/a)/(4\log2)\). In the separated case arbitrarily many disjoint spikes fit inside any positive measure budget, each contributing one unit to the integral, so \(\mathcal A_1(a)=\infty\). These are infinite-quantifier analytic arguments, not extrapolations from finite checks.

## Originality — PASS with residual priority risk
The closest direct literature is Mihula–Pándy (arXiv:2511.19000, Definitions 1.1 and 3.1, Proposition 4.1(ii), Section 6). Its necessary ratio is satisfied by both members of this constructed pair and cannot decide the result. Edmunds–Gogatishvili–Nekvinda (arXiv:2101.00182) addresses variable-exponent almost-compact embeddings, not identical marginal extrema of two spatially rearranged weights on infinite measure. The foundational Slavíková 2012 article concerns general almost-compact embeddings; its complete text was not inspected, so an overlapping older example remains a concrete priority risk. Semantic searches of published mathematical research records for extremal-function, rearrangement, and weighted local embedding aliases returned no dominating result. Absence of a hit is not a proof of global novelty.

## Value — PASS
This is a motivated boundary of the recently proposed extremal fundamental-function machinery: retaining **all** individual extremal functions and the sharp necessary vanishing quotient still loses a binary localized-decay property. The positive arrangement supplies an exact logarithmic scale and coefficient, the negative arrangement has no finite local constant at any scale, and both use standard Banach function spaces. The example identifies the need for a joint spatial weight invariant in non-rearrangement-invariant problems. No new all-space compactness theorem is asserted.
