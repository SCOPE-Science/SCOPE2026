# Independent coalition partitions of one-leaf coronas: a bipartite criterion and exact census

## Finding
Let \(G\) be an arbitrary finite simple graph with \(n\ge2\) vertices and \(c\ge1\) connected components, and let \(W(G)=G\circ K_1\) denote the graph formed by adjoining one private leaf to every vertex of \(G\). An independent coalition partition is an *unlabelled set of nonempty disjoint vertex classes* covering the whole graph in which every class is either a singleton dominating set or belongs to a pair of classes whose union is an independent dominating set, neither constituent being one. Write \(\operatorname{icp}(H,k)\) for the number of such partitions into \(k\) classes and \(\operatorname{ICP}(H;z)=\sum_{k\ge1}\operatorname{icp}(H,k)z^k\). A graph with no such partitions has the zero polynomial and independent coalition number \(0\).

**Theorem.** If \(G\) is nonbipartite, then \(W(G)\) admits no independent coalition partition. If \(G\) is bipartite, every independent coalition partition of \(W(G)\) has exactly four classes, so \(\operatorname{IC}(W(G))=4\), and
\[
\operatorname{ICP}(W(G);z)=\left[2^{c-1}(2^{n-1}-1)^2-\begin{cases}0,&c=1,\\2^{c-2}(2^{c-1}-1),&c\ge2\end{cases}\right]z^4.
\]
In particular, when \(G\) is connected and bipartite, the number is \((2^{n-1}-1)^2\), regardless of the edges within \(G\). The formula includes disconnected graphs, isolated vertices of the base, and all bipartite cycles. It counts vertex-labelled graphs' partitions as sets of *unlabelled* classes; no division by graph automorphisms is made.

For completeness, if \(n=1\) then \(W(G)=K_2\) has exactly one independent coalition partition (the singleton partition), and its polynomial is \(z^2\).

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple, undirected. For every base vertex \(v_i\), write \(r_i\) for the corresponding original vertex and \(\ell_i\) for its private leaf. The edges of \(W(G)\) are the original edges \(r_ir_j\) together with \(r_i\ell_i\). No edges join different leaves. The graph is bipartite exactly when \(G\) is bipartite, and \(W(G)\) has the same number \(c\) of connected components as \(G\). We use the independent-coalition definition with singleton dominating blocks as the only individually dominating classes; for \(n\ge2\), none exist in \(W(G)\).

## Proof
**Lemma 1 (forced transversals).** Every independent dominating set \(D\) of \(W(G)\) contains exactly one vertex of each adjacent pair \(\{r_i,\ell_i\}\). Indeed, independence forbids both; domination of \(\ell_i\) forbids neither. Conversely, choosing one from each pair gives an independent dominating set exactly when the chosen root vertices form an independent set of \(G\). In particular, every independent dominating set of \(W(G)\) has cardinality \(n\), and no proper subset of one is dominating.

**Lemma 2 (only four classes).** In an independent coalition partition \(\pi\) of \(W(G)\), every class is independent (there are no singleton dominating vertices). For a class \(B\), define its *index support* \(\sigma(B)=\{i:r_i\in B\text{ or }\ell_i\in B\}\). Independence ensures at most one member from each root–leaf pair in a class. Since each original pair has both endpoints included somewhere in the partition, every index occurs in supports of exactly two *different* classes. By Lemma 1, coalition partners have disjoint complementary supports.

Choose any partner pair \(B,C\) with \(\sigma(B)=S\), \(\sigma(C)=[n]\setminus S=T\). Both sets are nonempty. Deleting these two classes leaves classes whose nonempty supports partition \([n]\), since each index occurred twice overall and once in \(B\cup C\). If there were at least three remaining classes, no two of their supports could be complements of each other: their pairwise unions omit at least one nonempty remaining support. Thus every remaining class would have to find its complementary-support partner among \(B,C\). Its support would then equal either \(S\) or \(T\). But at most one residual disjoint support can equal each of the nonempty complementary sets \(S,T\), so there cannot be three remaining classes. Thus \(|\pi|\le4\). Exactly one remaining class cannot have a complementary support anywhere in the partition, and zero remaining classes cannot cover the second copy of each index. Hence \(|\pi|=4\). More precisely, the four class supports are \(S,T,S,T\).

**Lemma 3 (a complementary independent-dominating pair).** Let \(A_1,A_2\) be the two classes with support \(S\) and \(B_1,B_2\) the two with support \(T\). All coalition pairs join an \(A\)-class to a \(B\)-class. Each of the four classes has a partner, so its coalition graph is a bipartite graph on two plus two vertices without isolated vertices. Every such bipartite graph has a perfect matching. Its two disjoint coalition pairs have unions \(D\) and \(V(W(G))\setminus D\), both independent dominating sets. They therefore form the two parts of a proper 2-coloring of \(W(G)\). Consequently, \(G\) is bipartite.

Conversely, if \(G\) is bipartite, each proper two-coloring of \(W(G)\) splits its vertices into complementary independent dominating sets \(D_0,D_1\), both of size \(n\). For \(n\ge2\), split each set into two nonempty unlabelled classes. Each proper subset fails to dominate by Lemma 1, and the two classes coming from the same \(D_i\) form an independent coalition. This gives four-class independent coalition partitions. Lemmas 2 and 3 prove the existence and exact-size assertions.

**Counting partitions before multiplicity correction.** The number of proper 2-colorings of \(W(G)\), when \(G\) is bipartite, is \(2^c\): each component has two possible orientations. Since each color class has size \(n\), there are \(2^{c-1}\) *unordered* complementary independent-dominating pairs. Each of the two sets of size \(n\) has \(2^{n-1}-1\) unordered splits into two nonempty blocks. Thus the number of presentations consisting of an unordered complementary pair and a split of each member equals
\[
T=2^{c-1}(2^{n-1}-1)^2.
\]

**Exactly when a partition is presented twice.** Fix a four-class partition with supports \(S,S,T,T\), where \(S,T\) partition \([n]\). The only two possible pairings into disjoint coalition pairs match its \(S\)-blocks with its \(T\)-blocks, either straight or crossed. Suppose the straight pairing gives complementary independent dominating sets. On the indices in \(S\), the two blocks assign the root vertices opposite colors; the same holds on \(T\). The crossed pairing reverses all root colors on \(T\) but not on \(S\). It also yields two independent sets **if and only if** no edge of the base graph runs between \(S\) and \(T\). The necessity follows from any crossing edge, whose endpoints change from different colors to equal colors under the reversal; sufficiency is immediate from the absence of crossing edges, since independence within each side is unchanged. Thus a partition has exactly two presentations precisely when \(S,T\) are nonempty unions of whole connected components of \(G\); otherwise it has one presentation. There cannot be a third presentation because same-support blocks cannot be coalition partners.

There are \(2^{c-1}-1\) unordered splits of the \(c\) components into two nonempty sides. If those sides contain \(a\) and \(c-a\) connected components, then the two blocks on the first side are determined by a proper two-coloring of its corona, up to interchange, in \(2^{a-1}\) ways; the two blocks on the other side likewise have \(2^{c-a-1}\) possibilities. Their product is \(2^{c-2}\). The number of double-counted partitions is therefore exactly
\[
E=2^{c-2}(2^{c-1}-1)\quad(c\ge2),
\]
and it is zero for \(c=1\). Subtracting \(E\) from \(T\) gives the asserted coefficient. This completes the proof.

## Verification
The accompanying executable `check_corona.py` computes the formula using graph-component 2-coloring and independently enumerates all vertex partitions of small coronas by an adjacency-based predicate. Every labelled base graph on \(1\le n\le4\) vertices was checked, comprising 75 graphs, along with seven deliberately varied five-vertex bases (path, cycle, clique, edge-plus-isolates, two disjoint edges plus isolate, edgeless base, triangle plus isolates). All 82 checks agree exactly, not only on maximum cardinality but on the complete partition-size distribution. The log `check_corona.log` contains reproducible results. These finite checks are not used as proof of the theorem for unbounded \(n\).

## Relationship to prior work
Samadzadeh and Mojdeh introduced independent coalition partitions and explicitly posed the existence-characterization problem; their arXiv manuscript dates from June 2023 (arXiv:2306.02079). Their Proposition 4.8 already proves the broader necessary implication that a four-class independent coalition partition of an isolate-free graph without a full vertex makes that graph bipartite. The present necessity argument may be viewed as a specialization of that implication once four-class rigidity is proved, and is not claimed as independent novelty. Their path, cycle, multipartite, and extremal results do not address all one-leaf coronas. Alikhani, Bakhshesh, Golmohammadi, and Klavžar studied independent coalition graphs and extremal cardinalities (DOI:10.7151/dmgt.2543); its available abstract does not establish this corona characterization, while its full PDF could not be read. Asadzadeh and Alikhani's 2026 counting manuscript provides formulas for several families including trees; in particular, its tree formula already implies the connected-tree-base special case of the enumerative result here, which is not claimed as an independent novelty. Its discussed graph operations and tree cases do not state this arbitrary-base bipartiteness criterion and disconnected-component correction. This theorem treats arbitrarily large bipartite base graphs with cycles and all nonbipartite bases, and the correction term counts nonuniqueness of complementary colorings among disconnected bases.

## Limitations
The result applies to precisely one attached private leaf per vertex, not to general coronas with larger attachments or to other domination variants. A verification of priority against all inaccessible or unindexed literature is not claimed. The 2025 journal paper's PDF was unavailable to inspection despite examination of available public descriptions and attempted lawful download access; its precise coverage is a residual originality risk. The published 2026 preprint was read through its available full-text representation, but this is not a statement of formal independent certification. The computation is finite corroboration only.
