# Integral Smith factors of prime-square gcd-difference circulants

## Finding
For any prime \(p\), write \(b=p-1\), and define the \(p^2\)-by-\(p^2\) integer matrix
\[
G_p=(\gcd(i-j,p^2))_{0\le i,j<p^2},\qquad \gcd(0,p^2)=p^2.
\]
Its complete integral Smith normal form, with entries listed by value and multiplicity, is
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
\text{invariant factor}&1&b&pb&p^2b&2p^2b&2p^2b(3p-2)\\
\text{multiplicity}&1&p-1&p(p-2)&1&p-2&1.
\end{array}
\]
Entries with zero multiplicity are omitted. In the displayed order, every invariant factor divides the next, including at \(p=2\). Consequently this also determines the finite abelian group \(\mathbb Z^{p^2}/G_p\mathbb Z^{p^2}\) exactly, not just its order.

## Assumptions and scope
The index set is the cyclic group \(\mathbb Z/p^2\mathbb Z\), with indices represented by integers from \(0\) through \(p^2-1\). The greatest common divisor is positive and invariant under changing the representative modulo \(p^2\). The coefficient ring for the Smith form is \(\mathbb Z\). The theorem includes the prime \(2\), for which the invariant factors are \(1,1,4,32\). No claim about moduli other than prime squares is made.

## Proof
**Reduction to a block matrix.** Arrange the residues in \(p\) groups of \(p\) elements according to their residue modulo \(p\). Put \(c=p(p-1)=pb\), write \(J_s\) for an \(s\)-by-\(s\) all-ones matrix, and put \(B=I_p\otimes J_p\). Checking the three possibilities (equal residues modulo \(p^2\), distinct but congruent modulo \(p\), and incongruent modulo \(p\)) gives
\[
G_p=cI_{p^2}+bB+J_{p^2}.
\]
Present the cokernel with generators \(x_{ij}\) for \(1\le i,j\le p\), and relations
\[
cx_{ij}+b S_i+T=0,\qquad S_i=\sum_{j=1}^p x_{ij},\quad T=\sum_{i=1}^p S_i.
\]
Set \(z_i=x_{i1}\) and \(y_{ij}=x_{ij}-x_{i1}\) for \(j>1\). Subtracting the relation at \(j=1\) from the one at \(j>1\) gives \(cy_{ij}=0\). The remaining relation in block \(i\) reads
\[
2cz_i+p\sum_{\ell=1}^p z_\ell+b\sum_{j>1}y_{ij}
+\sum_{\ell=1}^p\sum_{j>1}y_{\ell j}=0.
\]
For each \(i\), \(Y_i=\sum_{j>1}y_{ij}\) is a primitive linear coordinate in the free group generated by \(y_{ij}\). Complete it to an integral basis; the other \(p-2\) coordinates in each block split off \(c\)-torsion independently. On the remaining \((Y_i,z_i)\), the presentation matrix is
\[
M_p=\begin{pmatrix}cI_p&0\\bI_p+J_p&2cI_p+pJ_p\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Thus \(G_p\) is integrally equivalent to \(cI_{p(p-2)}\oplus M_p\).

**Integral two-dimensional reduction.** Over \(\mathbb Z\), the all-ones operator \(J_p\) has a primitive two-dimensional invariant direct summand on which its matrix is
\[
J_2=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
and vanishes on a complementary rank-\(p-2\) direct summand. For an explicit unimodular basis, take \(e_1\), \(v=\sum_{i=2}^p(e_i-e_1)\), and extend \(v\) to a basis of the kernel of coordinate summation. Then \(J_pe_1=pe_1+v\) and \(J_pv=0\). Apply this same integral change of coordinates to both \(Y\) and \(z\) components of \(M_p\). The matrix splits into \(p-2\) copies of
\[
B_p=\begin{pmatrix}c&0\\b&2c\end{pmatrix}
\]
and one four-dimensional block
\[
K_p=\begin{pmatrix}
c&0&0&0\\
0&c&0&0\\
2p-1&0&p(3p-2)&0\\
1&b&p&2c
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

**Exact Smith factors of the small blocks.** The gcd of the entries of \(B_p\) is \(b\), while \(\det B_p=2c^2\). Hence its Smith factors are \(b,2p^2b\). For \(K_p\), pivot at the entry \(1\) in its last row and first column, then use integer row/column operations to remove that row and column. The remaining three-by-three matrix has an overall factor \(b\). After exchanging its first two rows and choosing harmless signs, the quotient is
\[
T_p=\begin{pmatrix}
p&0&0\\
-pb&-p^2&-2p^2b\\
-(2p-1)&p&-2p(2p-1)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its entries have gcd \(1\) since \(\gcd(p,2p-1)=1\). Every two-by-two minor is divisible by \(p^2\), while the minor with rows \(1,3\) and columns \(1,2\) is exactly \(p^2\). Moreover \(\det T_p=2p^4(3p-2)\). The determinantal-divisor characterization of Smith normal form therefore gives the three factors
\[
1,\quad p^2,\quad 2p^2(3p-2).
\]
The four Smith factors of \(K_p\) are consequently
\[
1,\quad b,\quad p^2b,\quad2p^2b(3p-2).
\]
Combining the \(p(p-2)\) copies of \(c=pb\), the \(p-2\) small blocks, and the four-dimensional block produces exactly the six groups of factors stated in the theorem. Because
\[
1\mid b\mid pb\mid p^2b\mid2p^2b\mid2p^2b(3p-2),
\]
this combined diagonal matrix is itself in Smith form. This proves the claim for every prime, including the zero-multiplicity boundary case \(p=2\).

## Verification
The accompanying standalone Python file `verify.py` uses only the standard library. It computes the complete integer Smith determinantal divisors for the full matrices at \(p=2,3\), and computes those of \(K_p\) and \(B_p\) for ten primes through \(29\). It checks the complete divisibility chain and determinant against the independently known Fourier eigenvalues. Its output is:

```text
VERIFY_OK exhaustive_full_SNF p=2,3; exact_core_SNF 10 primes; factor_chains_and_determinants 10 primes
```

The exact integral basis changes and small-minor computation above, rather than finite experiments or Fourier diagonalization, constitute the proof for all primes. As a separate consistency check, the Fourier eigenvalues are \(p(3p-2)\) once, \(2p(p-1)\) repeated \(p-1\) times, and \(p(p-1)\) repeated \(p(p-1)\) times. Their product agrees with the product of the Smith factors.

## Relationship to prior work
Van der Kamp explicitly computed the discrete Fourier transform of the greatest-common-divisor function, which supplies the spectral and determinant comparison but not the integral cokernel or integral Smith form. Ilmonen and Haukkanen determine Smith forms for gcd matrices indexed by factor-closed divisibility sets, rather than cyclic differences. Telloni and Williams investigate parameter-polynomial Smith matrices, and Noferini and Williams study more general companion-matrix methods; their inspected results do not state the concrete prime-square gcd-circulant formula or yield it without the additional integral decomposition supplied here. The problem requires \(\mathbb Z\)-unimodular transformations, not just diagonalization over a field. The smallest prime-power modulus with nontrivial nested residue blocks, \(p^2\), is the natural first test of this distinction.

## Limitations
This is an exact classification only for moduli that are squares of primes. The formula is not asserted for higher prime powers or composite moduli with multiple prime factors. A finite checker cannot certify the universal statement on its own. Absence of a matching formula from indexed literature is not a proof of publication priority: an unindexed or differently described prior Smith-form computation may exist. No independent external validation has been undertaken.
