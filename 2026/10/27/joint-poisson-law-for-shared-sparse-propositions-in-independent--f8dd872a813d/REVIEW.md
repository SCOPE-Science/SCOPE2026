# Research review: Joint Poisson law for shared sparse propositions in independent finite S5 frames

## Correctness — PASS
The exact conditional mixed factorial moments are obtained by ordered disjoint block selection. The key bound uses the log-concavity of \(B_n/n!\), and classical Bell asymptotics supply the fixed-size limit. Exact exchangeability proves finite-size cross-agent independence under cardinality-conditioned marking; concentration of binomial marked cardinality establishes the sparse-independent labeling case. Factorial-moment domination controls the entire countable-array tail. The standalone finite checker passed 406 direct comparisons. Finite enumeration corroborates rather than replaces the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
The strongest relevant comparison is Arratia–Tavaré's 1994 one-partition independent-process approximation. Its classical single-partition Poisson approximations are not claimed as novel; the new scientific statement combines several independently uniform equivalence relations with one shared sparse predicate and proves a joint marked-class total-variation limit, an exact fixed-size finite independence identity, and a simultaneous first-order witness probability. Koponen's 2026 work motivates random finite logical expansions but does not give this specific rare-marking classification. Indexed studies of random partition overlaps, Bell block counts, and deterministic modal covers concern different observables. Broad older multicolored-assembly literature might subsume the new combined statement; independent priority review remains advisable.

## Value — PASS
The theorem identifies the actual asymptotic distribution of every fully marked information-cell size for finitely many agents, shows independence despite shared semantics, computes the probability of a natural conjunction of first-order knowledge-witness sentences, and identifies the \(1/W(n)\) critical marking scale. These are structural/probabilistic classifications rather than an arbitrary finite table.

## Limitations
This is for fixed numbers of independently uniform finite equivalence relations; labels are independent of the partitions. It proves annealed limits, not quenched behavior or growing-agent uniformity. Sources were inspected within their relevant sections, not exhaustively all adjacent literature.
