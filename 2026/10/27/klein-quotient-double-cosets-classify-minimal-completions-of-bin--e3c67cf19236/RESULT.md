# Klein-quotient double-cosets classify minimal completions of binary cellular automata

## Finding

Let \(G\) be **any finite group**, \(A=\{0,1\}\), \(X=A^G\), and \(E=\mathrm{CA}(G;A)=\mathrm{End}_G(X)\). Let \(U=\mathrm{ICA}(G;A)\), let \(L\) consist of all single-active-pattern lazy cellular automata, and set \(M=\langle U\cup L\rangle\). Put
\[
\mathcal K(G)=\{H\triangleleft G:G/H\cong C_2\times C_2\}.
\]
For each \(H\in\mathcal K(G)\), exactly two \(G\)-orbits of binary configurations have stabilizer \(H\); denote them \(O_H^+\) and \(O_H^-\). Choose an equivariant bijection between them and define \(\varepsilon_H\in E\) to send every point of \(O_H^-\) to its corresponding point in \(O_H^+\), fixing all other configurations. The following criterion holds for **every** set \(S\subseteq E\):
\[
\boxed{\ \langle M\cup S\rangle=E\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 S\cap U\varepsilon_H U\ne\varnothing\quad\text{for every }H\in\mathcal K(G).\ }
\]
The double-cosets \(U\varepsilon_H U\) are disjoint for distinct \(H\). Consequently, the exact number of additional (not necessarily lazy) cellular automata required to complete \(M\) to \(E\) is
\[
\boxed{\ \operatorname{rank}(E:M)=\lvert\mathcal K(G)\rvert
=\frac{(2^r-1)(2^r-2)}6,\quad
r=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}G/([G,G]G^2).\ }
\]
For \(r=0,1\) the number is zero. All minimum-size completing sets are **precisely** the transversals containing one element from each \(U\varepsilon_H U\). In particular, the required numbers for \(G=(C_2)^r\), \(r=2,3,4,5\), are \(1,7,35,155\). Distinct quotient kernels require genuinely distinct auxiliary generators: no single added arbitrary cellular automaton can replace two of them.

## Assumptions and scope

The group \(G\) and binary alphabet \(A\) are finite. The group acts on configurations by the usual shift. Every equivariant transformation of \(X\) is a cellular automaton, because \(X\) is finite. A lazy rule is one whose local map changes the symbol at the identity only for one active finite-neighborhood pattern; we include all such rules of all allowed neighborhoods. Composition is right-to-left. Here \(G^2\) denotes the subgroup generated by squares, and \([G,G]\) the commutator subgroup. The displayed rank is relative to the **entire submonoid** \(M\), not to \(U\) alone and not to a finite preselected set of lazy rules. The criterion concerns generation of the entire finite monoid, not a bound on factorization lengths or memory radii.

## Proof

**Step 1: two obstruction orbits for each quotient.** Fix \(H\in\mathcal K(G)\). The configurations with stabilizer containing \(H\) identify with binary subsets of \(G/H\cong(C_2)^2\). In the four-element quotient, the subsets with trivial translation stabilizer are exactly the four singletons and their four three-point complements. The four singletons form one free translation orbit, and the four complements form another. The empty set and whole set have full stabilizer; all six two-point sets have nontrivial translation stabilizer. Thus precisely two configuration orbits have stabilizer exactly \(H\). As \(G/H\) has order four, the chosen collapse \(\varepsilon_H\) has precisely four nontrivial kernel pairs, all between these two orbits.

**Step 2: these collapses cannot be lazy-generated.** Restrict an equivariant endomorphism of \(X\) to the configurations fixed by \(H\), producing a cellular automaton on \(G/H\). The restriction homomorphism takes units to units and any lazy automaton to a lazy automaton or the identity (Alcalá-Arroyo and Castillo-Ramirez, Proposition 3.3). It takes \(\varepsilon_H\) to the elementary collapse of the two free binary orbits on \(C_2\times C_2\). Corollary 3.11 of that work proves this elementary collapse is not in the monoid generated by units and lazy maps on the four-element quotient, using the collision/constant-collapse obstruction. Hence \(\varepsilon_H\notin M\).

**Step 3: a first nonunit factor is rigid.** Suppose \(\varepsilon_H=f_k\cdots f_1\), with each factor in \(U\cup L\cup S\). The first noninvertible factor from the right, \(w\), is preceded only by units; call their product \(v\in U\). Since \(w\circ v\) is noninjective and its kernel is contained in \(\ker\varepsilon_H\), every nontrivial kernel pair of \(w\circ v\) is one of the four corresponding pairs between \(O_H^-\) and \(O_H^+\). By equivariance, merging one corresponding pair forces the merger of **all four** pairs. Hence its kernel equals \(\ker\varepsilon_H\). The two images are isomorphic finite \(G\)-sets, and the isomorphism of images extends to a unit of \(E\), because each complement has the same orbit types. Thus \(\varepsilon_H=u\,w\,v\) for some \(u,v\in U\). This reconstructs the essential content of Lemma 3.9 of the motivating paper. If \(w\in L\), this representation would place \(\varepsilon_H\) in \(M\), contrary to Step 2. Therefore \(w\in S\), and \(S\cap U\varepsilon_H U\ne\varnothing\).

**Step 4: the double-cosets are disjoint.** Every equivariant unit preserves each exact point stabilizer: \(G_{u(x)}=G_x\). Thus, under left and right multiplication by units, a map whose only nontrivial kernel pairs join the two orbits of stabilizer \(H\) retains that stabilizer label. The labels for \(H\ne H'\) differ, proving \(U\varepsilon_H U\cap U\varepsilon_{H'}U=\varnothing\). Steps 3–4 prove necessity and give a separate compulsory auxiliary map per \(H\).

**Step 5: one auxiliary map per quotient suffices.** Conversely, if \(S\) meets every indicated double-coset, multiply each selected element by units to recover \(\varepsilon_H\). By Lemma 3.7(i) of Alcalá-Arroyo and Castillo-Ramirez (based on Castillo-Ramirez and Ruiz-Medina), units and all elementary \(G\)-orbit collapses generate \(E\). Their Lemma 3.14(1) proves that every elementary collapse already lies in \(M\), except possibly a collapse between two distinct orbits with the same stabilizer \(H\in\mathcal K(G)\). Step 1 shows the only such source-target orbits are \(O_H^\pm\). Both orientations and every corresponding equivariant bijection are obtained from \(\varepsilon_H\) by conjugation with equivariant units (Lemma 3.7(ii)). Therefore the selected maps, together with \(M\), generate **all** elementary collapses, hence all of \(E\).

**Step 6: arithmetic count.** Every epimorphism from \(G\) to \((C_2)^2\) factors through \(V=G/([G,G]G^2)\cong (\mathbb F_2)^r\). Choosing its two independent coordinate functionals gives \((2^r-1)(2^r-2)\) ordered choices. Two epimorphisms have the same kernel exactly when they differ by one of the six elements of \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_2)\). Division by six therefore counts the distinct kernels \(H\). This proves the rank formula and the characterization of every minimum completing set.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` using the Python standard library. The checker enumerates **all** surjective linear maps \((\mathbb F_2)^r\to(\mathbb F_2)^2\) up to their kernels for \(2\le r\le5\), obtaining exactly \(1,7,35,155\) distinct kernels, and independently examines every binary configuration of \((\mathbb F_2)^r\) for \(r=2,3\), confirming that every index-four kernel occurs as the exact stabilizer of precisely two orbits of size four, represented by quotient weights one and three. Output: `VERIFY_OK`. These finite checks are demonstrations; Steps 1–6 are the proof for arbitrary finite \(G\).

## Relationship to prior work

Alcalá-Arroyo and Castillo-Ramirez (arXiv:2610.18480v1, October 13, 2026), Theorem 1.2, establish the **qualitative** equality criterion \(M=E\); their Lemmas 3.7, 3.9, 3.14 and Corollary 3.11 provide the orbit-collapse methods used and explicitly credited above. Alcalá-Arroyo, Castillo-Ramirez, Ma, and Zhang (arXiv:2610.20329v1, October 14, 2026), Proposition 5.1, determine the **single-quotient** relative rank \(1\) for \(G=C_2\times C_2\), as well as a specific maximal-submonoid structure. The present theorem instead gives the exact hitting-set criterion for **every finite group** and **every supplementary generating set**, and counts multiple independent obstructions for groups such as \((C_2)^3\). Castillo-Ramirez and Ruiz-Medina (arXiv:2203.12158v3, originally March 2022) determine relative rank modulo **units alone**, not modulo all lazy rules. The new criterion is deduced by combining these published structural ingredients with an all-quotient separation argument; it is not a claim to have invented those ingredients.

## Limitations

No statement is made for infinite groups, alphabets of size greater than two, restricted-memory lazy families, shortest words, or the size of \(M\). The general result is a synthesis of recent orbit-collapse lemmas, and its bibliographic independence from other algebraic semigroup formulations is not fully settled. Finite verification does not certify the general argument. No independent audit has been performed.
