# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Equation (1) is a finite-ring identity derived by counting interval overlaps. The logarithmic-window Gaussian limit follows from a uniform absolute covariance bound and an independent-block Lindeberg argument. Cramér–Wold and Slutsky give multitime shifts and positivity probabilities. Finite exhaustive checks test the exact formula but do not constitute the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS with explicit residual priority risk.** Hiura's 2021 paper contains the fixed-time annealed covariance and analyzes fixed-horizon fluctuation laws. De Roeck and collaborators analyze Kac-ring relaxation using a different scatterer ensemble and sequential limits. The additional claim here is the simultaneous logarithmic-time triangular-array limit, its cutoff profile, and lattice-phase qualification. Full-text comparison with Cardoen's 2004 thesis could not be completed; the indexed abstract and chapter structure do not resolve whether that thesis contains a similar growing-time theorem. Consequently absolute historical priority is not claimed.

**Value — PASS.** The theorem quantifies a physical signal-to-noise threshold in a reversible microscopic dynamical system with deterministic macroscopic relaxation. It identifies a non-Markovian Gaussian temporal profile and the phase structure of its critical window, not merely a finite moment or a renaming of a known fixed-time central limit theorem.

Scientific limitations: the theorem requires independent static scatterers with fixed density strictly below one-half, fixed initially positive spins, logarithmically growing times, and finitely many fixed offsets. It does not prove microscopic equilibration, cover quenched fixed-scatterer behavior, or eliminate the prior-work uncertainty noted above.
