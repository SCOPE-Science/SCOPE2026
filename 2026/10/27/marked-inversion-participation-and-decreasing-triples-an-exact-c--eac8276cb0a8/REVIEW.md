# Scientific review: marked inversion participation and decreasing triples

## Correctness

PASS. The marked covariance is reconstructed from the exact four-index overlap classification for one pairwise inversion comparison and one decreasing-triple indicator, with disjoint-index independence, two-shared-index covariance \(1/12\), and one-shared-index covariances \(0,\pm1/24\). Summation yields the stated finite \(n\), finite \(i\) identity. The degree variance follows separately from three-index comparison covariances. The variance \(13n^5/7200+O(n^4)\) for decreasing triples is corroborated by the cited fixed-length subsequence theorem and an independent bounded-kernel projection/Riemann-sum proof. Exact permutation enumeration for \(3\le n\le8\), overlap identities, and exact polynomial integration passed. Enumeration is not substituted for the unrestricted argument.

## Originality

PASS within the inspected sources, with priority risk. Işlak–Özdemir give the triple-count variance, not its mixed covariance with marked inversion participation. Bhattacharya–Mukherjee study degree-process limits, and Gürerk–Işlak–Yıldız give marginal degree and clique-count moments rather than their mixed moment. The published full pair-inversion covariance/precision result supplies only pairwise moments and does not imply the mixed third-order order-statistic interactions. No equivalent location-specific covariance or \(\sqrt n\) correlation window was found in the recorded targeted searches. This is not a claim of exhaustive historical uniqueness.

## Value

PASS. A natural basic local statistic (inversion participation/graph vertex degree) and a natural global statistic (decreasing-triple/graph triangle count) have a non-obvious all-positive finite covariance whose magnitude is largest at the endpoints, while their *normalized* correlation has the opposite localization behavior: only the central \(\sqrt n\) positions retain nonvanishing correlation. The continuous limiting profile exhibits an intrinsic scale, not an arbitrary parameter slice. It provides a benchmark for dependence of local and global permutation-pattern fluctuations.
