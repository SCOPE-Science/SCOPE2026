# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Every claimed necessity and sufficiency condition follows from the exact four-point Fourier decomposition of the real Hermitian lifted measurement map. The possible nullspace is explicitly one-dimensional and generated by a full-rank diagonal matrix. Differences of two rank-one positive matrices have rank at most two, proving universal phaseless injectivity. The displayed failing geometries admit explicit distinct polynomials with identical measurements. A determinant-sign argument establishes that seven complex rank-one intensity measurements never suffice in dimension three. For the eight Gaussian-integer positions, an independent exact-arithmetic checker reconstructs and diagonalizes the whole lifted matrix. The stability inequality follows from its smallest positive singular value and the least singular value of the kernel generator.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A complete minimal-sample characterization for a natural finite Fourier/Vandermonde measurement geometry is independently motivated by phaseless interpolation and structured sensing. The simplest successful configuration is a memorable set of eight nearest nonzero Gaussian integers. Complete exact singular values and the quantitative lift stability bound distinguish this result from an isolated finite rank computation; the geometric failure cases delimit feasibility exactly.
