# Scientific review: nilpotency indices in restricted-range semigroups

## Correctness
**PASS.** Every restricted-range operator is an upper block matrix with diagonal blocks \(A\) and zero. The power identity \(X^j=(A^j,A^{j-1}B;0,0)\) proves the conditional index dichotomy exactly. The crucial rank of \(A^{d-1}\) equals the number of longest Jordan blocks, giving \(q^{(k-r)c}\) precise index-preserving lifts for each \(A\). Counting nilpotent Jordan classes by their explicit centralizer orders proves the finite index enumerator for arbitrary prime powers. At the top edge, decomposing \(\lambda=(k-s,\mu)\) for \(2s<k\), then summing the centralizer reciprocals, gives the stated exact defect probabilities. The \(q\)-binomial product normalizes the limiting masses, and pointwise mass convergence to a mass-one limit yields total-variation convergence. Seven direct matrix-power enumerations independently agree with the formula. The computations are corroborative, not a proof of infinite families.

## Originality
**PASS against the inspected material, with meaningful residual risk.** The foundational paper by Grau (arXiv:2206.12526v1), read in its open full text, treats Green relations and ideals of precisely the restricted-range semigroup, but does not state a nilpotency-index enumerator. Sullivan (2008, DOI 10.1017/S0004972708000385) likewise studies its Green relations, ideals and regular locus; the accessible full PDF was read. Sommanee and Sangkhanan (2017, DOI 10.1017/S144678871600080X) classify regular-subsemigroup phenomena, not the entire nonregular nilpotent index law in the accessible abstract. The classical nilpotent Jordan orbit count is acknowledged as an input, not claimed. Fulman's affine-group cycle-index work (arXiv:math/0009182) provides especially close one-column precedent; the present claim's arbitrary codimension, all-index enumerator and codimension-dependent total-variation law are not present in the inspected statements. Fulman--Guralnick (arXiv:2111.09433) recount ordinary nilpotent Jordan classes and mutually annihilating pairs, but does not impose the same restricted-range block geometry. The closest indexed nilpotent Jordan-lift result treats lifts over dual numbers rather than arbitrary fixed-range endomorphisms. Search coverage does not prove priority.

## Value
**PASS.** Nilpotency index is a natural semigroup invariant, and the restricted-range semigroup is studied for its nonregular strata. The result is not merely a known matrix-count substitution: it classifies the index of every nonregular lift as well as regular lifts and gives exact uniform counts across all finite fields, dimensions and codimensions. The top-edge distribution reveals a structural phase: a random nilpotent has a tight bounded index defect even as the proportion of regular nilpotents approaches zero with codimension. The Bernoulli dependence on the codimension is a compact, usable law for probabilistic and structural comparisons. No broad downstream theorem is asserted or needed for the complete natural enumerator to be valuable.

## Closest literature and limitations
The one-column affine-matrix setting is related and may imply that special case by elementary reductions. General parabolic matrix-counting literature might imply broader formulas that were not identified; this is the principal remaining originality risk. The 2017 regular-subsemigroup paper's full text was not assessed in this review. The proof uses classical finite-field Jordan centralizer and nilpotent total identities, independently identified and appropriately credited. No full ambient Jordan-type distribution or semigroup-conjugacy classification is claimed. Independent external validation remains outstanding.
