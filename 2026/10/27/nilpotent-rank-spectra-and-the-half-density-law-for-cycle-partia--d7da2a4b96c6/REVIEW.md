# Scientific review: nilpotent rank spectra for cycle partial-isometry monoids

## Correctness — PASS
The graph-metric inverse monoid equals the set of dihedral restrictions (Fernandes–Paulista, Lemma 1.1). A restriction is nilpotent exactly when its domain omits a point from each entire permutation cycle. This supplies the displayed rotation and reflection generating functions. The low-rank extension duplications are explicitly enumerated and subtracted. The top-rank count follows from primitive rotations; the logarithmic cutoff argument proves the half-density bound. Exhaustive independent enumeration for every cycle order from three through ten matched every rank coefficient. The proof, rather than the finite enumeration, supports the unbounded claims.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected scientific evidence
The closest primary full text, arXiv:2205.02196 (Fernandes–Paulista), establishes extension/overlap facts and total monoid size, not the complete nilpotent rank polynomial. The original 1987 nilpotents paper of Gomes–Howie concerns all symmetric partial bijections and its unrestricted path classification does not supply the cycle-restriction enumeration. Current published-record semantic searches returned no equivalent rank formula. A closely related Mills (1990) polygon inverse-semigroup paper was identifiable by abstract but inaccessible in complete text; potential overlap is a material residual risk, not claimed away. No exhaustive uniqueness certificate is asserted.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The nilpotent rank distribution is a natural structural invariant of a previously studied finite inverse monoid. It includes both parity-dependent exceptional fibers and an exact high-rank totient count, while showing a nontrivial asymptotic half-density law across all polygon orders. This is a complete natural family classification, not a single small-sample numerical count.
