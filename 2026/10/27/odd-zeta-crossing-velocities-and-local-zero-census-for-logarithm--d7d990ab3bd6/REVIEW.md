## correctness

PASS

Normalized polar integration gives squared monomial norms (n+1)^(-s), giving the stated kernel and normalization P_s(0)=1. In the replacement, an integral for P_(s−N), with N an integer larger than the local parameters, followed by (1+t∂_t)^N establishes joint analyticity off [1,∞), in particular at (2m,−1). The alternating continuation gives P_s(−1)=η(−s). Differentiating there in the order gives −η′(−2m); the recurrence in the scalar variable gives ∂_tP_(2m)(−1)=−η(−2m−1), which is nonzero. The unique implicit branch is real by conjugation, and its derivative is −η′(−2m)/η(−2m−1). Expanding the sine factor in the zeta functional equation at −2m and evaluating at −2m−1 gives the positive ratio π²ζ(2m+1)/[(2m+1)ζ(2m+2)], multiplied by the ratio of the two eta prefactors. This gives the stated m=1 value and large-m asymptotic; equal moduli give an inward radial speed v_m/2. Eulerian reciprocity and simplicity give exactly m−1 roots in (−1,0) at s=2m, and the simple root −1. The prior negative-real zero result reduces exhaustion for real s to the compact interval [−1,0], where continuity and local uniqueness of each zero exclude any other arrivals. As a separate check on the frozen proof's boundary exclusion, the zeta functional equation bounds the coefficients of the regular part of the Jonquière series uniformly by a polynomial in the index times (2π)^(-index), locally in s, so that proof also excludes zeros near t=1. ## originality

PASS

The original bundle overstates the novelty of the real-order simple negative zero and local census claims: Jurkat–Peyerimhoff already give stronger slit-plane zero location and simplicity for the real power series, and the local census follows by combining that theorem with the integer reciprocal roots and the sign change at −1. The replacement's claim is the explicit velocity of the parameter-dependent zero at every even parameter and its quantitative asymptotic, not the known reality, simplicity, or census. The comparison sources do not give this parameter derivative; its closed value requires comparing the order and argument derivatives and using the trivial-zero derivative, not reading a count or location from a table.

## value

PASS

The velocity is a quantitative invariant of a natural boundary transition already singled out by the integer reciprocal symmetry. Knowing only a zero count or sign change gives no distance of the new zero from the unit circle for a small perturbation. The exact slope determines that distance to first order, also for symmetric choices of the two kernel arguments, and its asymptotic describes weakening sensitivity at successive even orders without claiming a uniform perturbation neighborhood.
