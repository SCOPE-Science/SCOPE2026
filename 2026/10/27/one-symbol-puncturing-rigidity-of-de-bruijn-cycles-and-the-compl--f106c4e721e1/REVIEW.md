# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The de Bruijn directed graph has balanced in/out degrees, so the unique missing edge of a closed trail of all but one edge is necessarily a loop. The loop forces a missing constant window. Restoring that loop at the \(q-1\) visits to its vertex gives distinct full-cycle extensions; the absence of rotational symmetry makes the multiplicity exact. Conversely, only deletion inside a constant length-\(n\) window can preserve window distinctness. The classical full-cycle count then yields the enumerator. Boundary case \(n=1\) is treated with the single empty-word vertex. The standalone program exhaustively checks seven finite parameter pairs; the graph proof supplies all quantifiers.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The classical punctured construction and its \(q-1\) insertion sites are explicit in Goresky–Klapper (2011), Section 8.2.4; Cameron–Gündoğan–Sawada (2022/2023) construct one-symbol cuts in their general cut-down algorithm. These prior claims are not represented as novel. The inspected texts do not state that *every* one-deficit window-distinct cycle must have a constant missing window, nor that the successful indexed deletions of *every* full de Bruijn cycle are precisely its \(qn\) homogeneous-window positions, nor the census of all such cycles without fixing a punctured construction. Search comparison therefore supports novelty of that complete classification, while unindexed equivalent treatments of pseudonoise and modified sequences remain a real risk. A brief proof alone is not evidence of established prior coverage.

**Value — PASS.** Exact single-symbol puncture robustness and classification of all near-complete universal cycles are natural invariants of a central universal-cycle construction. The statement is independent of which de Bruijn cycle is chosen; it settles the only-one-omitted-window boundary case, identifies the exact permitted edit locus, and counts all outputs rather than merely constructing examples. The loop-balance obstruction is the motivating structure; it is not an arbitrary numerical slice.

**Closest literature and scientific limitation.** Section 8.2.4 of *Algebraic Shift Register Sequences* gives the classical forward puncture and inverse insertion construction, while Section 4.2 of *Cut-Down de Bruijn Sequences* handles single-loop cutting. The omitted-edge conservation converse and global enumeration are the restricted present claims. Possible prior coverage via an unlocated stronger characterization remains unresolved, but no inspected primary treatment was found to assert the full claim. The result is only for cyclic windows, exactly one deletion, and labeled alphabets. Exhaustive small cases cannot replace the proof; no independent external audit is asserted.
