# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The stationary generator has the four explicitly stated nearest-neighbor transition rates. Its affine first-moment balance and three linear second-moment relations uniquely give the rational covariances; independent stationary probabilities computed using exact rational elimination agree for all nine integer parameter pairs \(1\le a,b\le3\). For the limit, the generator makes the difference of the two partwise means vanish in stationary mean square. The accelerated generator of their average converges on polynomials to the Jacobi generator with beta parameters \(2\theta,2(1-\theta)\). The stationary moment recurrence uniquely characterizes the asserted weak limit on \([0,1]\). Exact checks verify algebra but are not infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS, with residual uncertainty
Mori–Hisakado–Nakayama (arXiv:1810.05643) use internally interacting population nodes and mutation/innovation in a multivariate beta framework; their joint-diffusion setting does not supply the exclusive two-part microscopic copier, its rational distinct-agent moments, and the specific opposing-zealot beta shape. Ben-Ari–Panzo–Speegle–VandenBerg (arXiv:2004.10187) study quasi-stationarity without fixed opposing zealots in a different one-part-fixed asymptotic regime. Braha–de Aguiar (arXiv:2601.09816) derive fully mixed multi-zealot stationary laws, not the complete-bipartite exclusive-neighbor law. These are statement/assumption comparisons; similar reductions in unindexed network/voter literature remain possible. Generic beta-diffusion and coalescing-walk techniques are not asserted new.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The model isolates influence from a single frozen pro-red agent facing a single frozen pro-blue agent in segregated, exclusively cross-linked social groups. The exact cross- and within-group stationary correlations and the nondegenerate beta limit show persistent system-wide stochastic polarization even in dense large networks. The exact uniform limiting law for balanced communities is a substantive no-concentration result, not a finite-table curiosity or an obvious consequence of first-moment analysis.

## Limitations and reproducibility
The theorem excludes extreme community-size ratios, additional fixed opinions, nonuniform copying and general graphs. The exact stationary joint distribution and mixing time are not classified. The verifier solves finite count-chain stationary equations in fractions, independently of the paper's pair-moment derivation, for nine small sizes. Independent scientific validation has not occurred.
