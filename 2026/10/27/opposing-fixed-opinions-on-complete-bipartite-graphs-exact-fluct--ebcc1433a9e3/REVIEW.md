# Scientific review: opposite-class fixed opinions on complete bipartite graphs

## Correctness — PASS

The finite-state process is fully specified by four transition rates on the pair of positive free-vertex counts. Solving stationary one-vertex and two-vertex generator identities produces the displayed all-size mean and variance, including absorbing star boundaries. For the joint-size limit, the two class proportions have stationary mean-square difference tending to zero. Rescaling their mean yields the Wright–Fisher generator with drift towards the partition ratio and diffusion coefficient consistent with beta parameters \(2\theta\) and \(2(1-\theta)\); stationary polynomial moments identify the limit without assuming prior convergence. The independent exact-rational checker reproduced 25 finite Markov-chain means and variances and 55 beta-moment recursions. These computations are supporting tests, not infinite-volume proofs.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected prior work

General stationary covariance methods for voter networks with fixed-opinion agents are already present in Yildiz and coauthors' 2011 manuscript and are explicitly credited. Mobilia, Petersen and Redner develop related complete-graph and lattice zealot models. The inspected sources do not evaluate the stationary opposite-class \(K_{m,n}\) variance across all sizes, prove a joint beta stationary limit for its free-part fractions, or isolate the balanced uniform-law boundary. The result is assessed as the *combined evaluated finite-graph and distributional classification*, not as a new general moment method. A stronger unlocated theorem on multiple-population voter diffusions or a later exact bipartite calculation could imply some or all of the claim; this remains a priority risk. An exact match was not found in the searched mathematical research records or inspected primary sources, which alone does not establish absolute novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

With only two nonchanging vertices, the stationary fluctuations of a macroscopic voter fraction remain order one on dense two-community graphs. Exact finite-size variance ordering isolates balanced bipartitions as the most fluctuating architecture; the limit specifies the *whole law*, not just two moments. The surprising balanced uniform stationary fraction contrasts with concentration predicted by naive independent-voter averaging. These are natural observables for binary-opinion dynamics with persistent competing influences and identify a genuine structural large-population transition.

## Boundaries and related literature

The result is limited to symmetric complete bipartite connectivity, rate-one neighbor-copying, and one positive and one negative fixed vertex placed in different parts. The primary antecedents are Mobilia–Georgiev (2004), Mobilia–Petersen–Redner (2007), and Yildiz et al. (2011), with Ben-Ari et al. (2020) relevant for a *different*, no-zealot quasi-stationary bipartite process. No independent audit or exhaustive bibliography is claimed.
