# Opposite typical compatibility geometries in finite quantum-modal forcing frames

## Finding

Fix \(n\geq 1\) and a labelled nonempty set \(W=\{1,\ldots,n\}\). A *compatibility relation* \(Q\subseteq W^2\) is reflexive and symmetric. A *modal relation* \(M\subseteq W^2\) satisfies the forcing condition relative to \(Q\) if
\[
M(u,w)\land Q(u,v)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad M(v,w)
\qquad (u,v,w\in W).
\]
There are two natural finite ensembles: all admissible ordered relation pairs \((Q,M)\), and the subclass with \(M\) an equivalence relation (the equivalence-modal ensemble). In each ensemble choose a pair uniformly among the labelled admissible pairs. The labels and the full relation pair, not valuations or models up to isomorphism, are counted.

Write \(c(Q)\) for the number of connected components of the undirected compatibility graph with the reflexive loops suppressed, and \(\Pi_n\) for the set of set partitions of \(W\). The exact cardinalities of the two ensembles are respectively
\[
A_n=\sum_{Q\,\mathrm{reflexive,symmetric}}2^{n c(Q)},
\qquad
B_n=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_n}2^{\sum_{C\in\pi}\binom{|C|}{2}}.
\]
In the equivalence-modal ensemble the second formula is equally the exponential generating function identity, interpreted as a *formal* series,
\[
\sum_{n\ge0}B_n\frac{z^n}{n!}
=\exp\!\left(\sum_{j\ge1}2^{\binom j2}\frac{z^j}{j!}\right),
\]
and the exact recurrence \(B_0=1\) and
\[
B_n=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{j-1}2^{\binom j2}B_{n-j}.
\]
The two ensemble sizes have sharply different leading terms:
\[
\boxed{A_n\sim 2^{n^2},\qquad B_n\sim 2^{\binom n2}.}
\]
Their **typical compatibility geometries are opposite**. In the unrestricted ensemble, \(Q\) is the identity (no off-diagonal compatibility edges) with probability tending to one. In the equivalence-modal ensemble, \(M=W^2\) with probability tending to one, and \(Q\) is connected with probability tending to one. Indeed, conditional on \(M=W^2\), the off-diagonal edges of \(Q\) are independent fair bits; hence the entire equivalence-modal pair law is asymptotically, in total variation, the uniform compatibility graph together with universal \(M\). Conversely, the unrestricted pair law is asymptotically the identity compatibility relation together with a uniform unconstrained binary modal relation.

## Assumptions and scope

The world set is finite, nonempty and labelled. Compatibility is exactly a reflexive symmetric binary relation, not necessarily transitive. Modal accessibility is either arbitrary or an equivalence relation, as explicitly stipulated. Forcing is the implication above with no extra restrictions. No requirement is imposed on propositional valuations, on representability by Hilbert-space orthogonality, or on isomorphism-reduced counting. The randomness is uniform over admissible *ordered pairs of relations*, not generated by first sampling the two relations uniformly and then conditioning on admissibility in a way that changes their weights. The resulting distribution is the same as conditioning a uniform independent pair on the forcing event, but not the same as first selecting a uniformly random admissible compatibility graph and then a modal relation.

## Proof

**Component-row lemma.** The forcing condition and symmetry of \(Q\) imply that if \(Q(u,v)\), then \(M(u,-)=M(v,-)\). By traversal of compatibility paths, successor rows are constant on connected components. Conversely such constancy gives forcing directly. Thus for a fixed \(Q\) with \(c(Q)\) components, every component independently chooses an arbitrary subset of the \(n\) target worlds as its common modal successor row. There are exactly \(2^{n c(Q)}\) choices. Summing gives \(A_n\). This component invariant is part of the established 2026 theory; the new work here is the finite-ensemble enumeration and asymptotic contrast.

**Equivalence-modal lemma.** If \(M\) is an equivalence relation, equality of its rows at \(u\) and \(v\) is equivalent to \(M(u,v)\). Hence the forcing condition is equivalent to \(Q\subseteq M\), viewing both as relations. Partition \(W\) into the equivalence classes \(\pi\) of \(M\). Every off-diagonal edge of \(Q\) must remain within one class, and each such unordered pair can be chosen independently. This yields \(2^{\sum_{C\in\pi}\binom{|C|}{2}}\) possibilities for \(Q\) for a fixed partition, proving \(B_n\). The exponential formula for weighted partitions gives the formal generating function. Distinguishing the class containing world \(1\) yields the stated recurrence.

**Unrestricted asymptotic.** The identity compatibility relation has \(n\) singleton components and contributes exactly \(2^{n^2}\) admissible pairs. To bound all other contributions, group compatibility graphs by \(k=n-c(Q)\), where \(1\le k\le n-1\). Each graph in this class has a spanning forest with exactly \(k\) edges. There are at most \(\binom{\binom n2}{k}\) choices for that forest. If its component sizes are \(s_1,\ldots,s_{n-k}\), then every graph edge lies within a forest component and the number of possible unordered edges is at most
\[
\sum_i\binom{s_i}{2}
\le \frac n2\sum_i(s_i-1)=\frac{nk}{2}.
\]
For each chosen forest, the number of compatible graphs is consequently at most \(2^{nk/2}\). Overcounting is harmless. Their contribution, divided by the identity graph's contribution, is bounded by
\[
\frac{A_n-2^{n^2}}{2^{n^2}}
\le\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\binom{\binom n2}{k}2^{-nk/2}
\le(1+2^{-n/2})^{\binom n2}-1=o(1).
\]
Thus the identity graph has probability \(2^{n^2}/A_n\to1\). Conditional on that graph, *every* modal relation is admissible and is uniformly chosen, proving the claimed total-variation approximation for the unrestricted ensemble.

**Equivalence-modal asymptotic.** Put \(N=\binom n2\). The single-block partition \(\pi=\{W\}\) contributes exactly \(2^N\) pairs, all with universal \(M=W^2\). For any other partition with blocks of sizes \(s_i\), define the number of forbidden between-block pairs
\[
L(\pi)=\sum_{i<j}s_i s_j
=\frac12\left(n^2-\sum_i s_i^2\right).
\]
The relative weight of this partition is \(2^{-L(\pi)}\). Split partitions according to whether they have a block exceeding \(n/2\). In that case its block size is \(n-k>n/2\) for a unique \(k\in\{1,\ldots,\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\}\), and \(L(\pi)\ge k(n-k)\ge nk/2\). The number of such partitions is \(\binom nk\mathrm{Bell}(k)\le n^{2k}\). Their combined relative weight is at most
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}n^{2k}2^{-nk/2}=o(1).
\]
If every block has size at most \(n/2\), then \(\sum_i s_i^2\le n^2/2\) and hence \(L(\pi)\ge n^2/4\). There are at most \(n^n\) set partitions, contributing at most \(n^n2^{-n^2/4}=o(1)\). Therefore
\[
\frac{B_n}{2^N}=1+o(1),
\qquad
\Pr(M=W^2)=\frac{2^N}{B_n}\longrightarrow1.
\]
Conditional on \(M=W^2\), each of the \(N\) possible non-loop compatibility edges is a fair independent bit: the compatibility graph is exactly \(G(n,1/2)\). For completeness, the probability that this graph is disconnected is bounded by choosing the vertex set of one component of size at most \(n/2\):
\[
\Pr(G(n,1/2)\text{ disconnected})
\le \sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\binom nj2^{-j(n-j)}=o(1).
\]
The last assertion follows by bounding \(\binom nj\le n^j\) and \(n-j\ge n/2\). Combining it with \(\Pr(M=W^2)\to1\) proves connectivity with high probability and the second total-variation statement. ∎

## Relationship to prior work

Tokuo's *Quantum Modal Logic* (2024/2025), Section 2, defines the forcing condition on a reflexive symmetric compatibility relation and arbitrary modal relation. Tokuo's *Component Modalities of Quantum Logic* (first available July 16, 2026), Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 3.2, proves successor-row constancy on compatibility components; Section 2.5 additionally treats equivalence modal relations. Those qualitative structural results are used rather than claimed anew. The complete accessible preprints, including the 2026 theorem proof and subsequent modal results, were inspected for count and typical-case statements. The precise labelled-pair enumerations and their opposite high-probability geometries are not asserted in those papers. The proof above derives the quantitative laws from their structural setting and elementary finite graph and partition bounds. Older graph-pair enumeration theorems could potentially imply parts of these identities; no absolute priority claim is made.

## Limitations

These are counts of finite abstract relation pairs, not of valuations or physically representable quantum states. The conclusions concern two specific uniform measures, so changing the way relations are sampled may change the typical geometry. The generating function for \(B_n\) is formal, not asserted convergent at nonzero complex arguments. The asymptotic comparison does not give optimal finite-error constants or isomorphism-class counts. Novelty is assessed against inspected sources and searched records, not independently certified; an older graph-enumeration result could subsume some formulas.
