# Optimal connected-dominating backbones and their exchange geometry in the four-cube

## Finding

Let \(Q_4\) have vertex set \(\{0,1\}^4\), with edges joining strings that differ in exactly one coordinate. A **connected dominating set** is a set \(S\) for which \(Q_4[S]\) is connected and every vertex outside \(S\) has a neighbor in \(S\). Call two such sets of the same size *exchange-adjacent* if one vertex of the first is replaced by one vertex of the second.

**Theorem.** The connected domination number is \(\gamma_c(Q_4)=6\). Its **entire set of minimum connected dominating sets** consists of the \(192\) six-vertex paths generated by choosing a starting vertex and four distinct coordinate directions \(a,b,c,d\), then flipping coordinates in the order \(a,b,c,d,a\); duplicate paths from reversing direction are identified. Each of these vertex sets induces a path on six vertices. They form one orbit under the \(384\) cube automorphisms.

On these \(192\) optimal sets, the graph \(\mathcal X\) of single-vertex exchanges is connected, vertex-transitive and four-regular. It has \(384\) edges and diameter **seven**. From each vertex the numbers of optimal sets at distances \(0,1,\ldots,7\) are exactly

\[
(1,4,10,23,45,76,30,3).
\]

Thus the unordered-pair distance counts at distances \(1,\ldots,7\) are \((384,960,2208,4320,7296,2880,288)\). Equivalently, every maximum-leaf spanning tree of \(Q_4\) has \(10\) leaves and its set of nonleaf vertices is one of these \(192\) path backbones; every listed backbone can be realized by such a spanning tree. Every pair of optimum backbones is connected by at most seven optimum-preserving exchanges.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite, simple and undirected. Vertices of the cube are the integers \(0,\ldots,15\) interpreted as four-bit words; adjacency means the bitwise XOR has Hamming weight one. A vertex of \(\mathcal X\) is an *unlabeled six-element subset* of the cube, not an ordering of a path. The exchange move may replace any one cube vertex, but both endpoint sets must satisfy minimum connected domination. Graph distances refer to \(\mathcal X\). No assertion concerns higher-dimensional cubes or arbitrary induced six-vertex paths.

## Proof

**Minimum size.** The cube has degree four. Suppose a connected dominating set \(S\) has five vertices, with \(e\) edges in its induced graph. Connectivity gives \(e\ge4\); there are \(20-2e\) boundary edges between \(S\) and its eleven-vertex complement. Domination requires at least eleven such edges, so \(e=4\); the induced subgraph is a tree, and there are exactly twelve boundary edges.

Every five-vertex tree has at least three distinct length-two paths (this is \(\sum_{v\in S}\binom{\deg_{Q_4[S]}(v)}2\ge3\)). The two endpoints of each such path have exactly two common neighbors in \(Q_4\): the path's middle vertex and a different vertex outside \(S\) (otherwise \(Q_4[S]\) contains a four-cycle). The twelve boundary edges would allow only one incidence in excess of the eleven needed to dominate all outside vertices. Hence at most one outside vertex could have two neighbors in \(S\), and no outside vertex could have three. This would account for at most one length-two path, a contradiction. If a smaller connected dominating set existed, it could be enlarged to a connected five-set, so all sizes below six are excluded. The path on bitstrings \(0,1,3,7,15,14\) has five internal edges and dominates the cube, proving \(\gamma_c(Q_4)=6\).

**Exhaustiveness and structure of optimizers.** Enumerate all \(\binom{16}{6}=8008\) six-subsets by ascending integer vertex tuples. Compute connectedness by exact graph search and domination by the union of closed neighborhoods. Exactly \(1952\) of the six-subsets are connected. Generate the full action of \(C_2^4\rtimes S_4\) by all 16 bitwise translations followed by all 24 coordinate permutations; the 1952 connected subsets form precisely **eleven** orbits. In the following complete orbit certificate, the columns are an orbit representative, orbit size, number of cube vertices in its closed neighborhood, and the induced degree multiset:

The orbit sizes sum to 1952, and the last is the *unique* orbit that dominates all sixteen vertices. Its degree sequence is that of an induced six-vertex path. The canonical path \(0,1,3,7,15,14\) lies in this orbit and has edge-coordinate word \(a,b,c,d,a\). Translating and permuting coordinates yields exactly the last orbit. Counting \(16\cdot4!/2=192\) unordered paths proves the explicit description. Note that **not every induced six-vertex path is dominating**; for example the induced path \((0,1,3,7,6,14)\) misses one cube vertex.

**Reconfiguration metric.** Form an edge between two of the exactly 192 optimum sets precisely when the cardinality of their symmetric difference is two. Each optimum has four such neighbors, yielding 384 edges. A complete breadth-first search from one optimum produces the shell vector \((1,4,10,23,45,76,30,3)\); every shell is accounted for, so the exchange graph is connected and has diameter seven. Cube automorphisms act transitively on the optima, preserving exchanges, so every optimum has the same shells. As an additional exhaustive check, breadth-first search from **all** 192 optima gives that same vector. Double-counting ordered pairs gives the claimed unordered-pair counts.

**Maximum-leaf interpretation.** For any graph of order at least three, the nonleaf vertices of a spanning tree form a connected dominating set. Conversely, every connected dominating set supports a spanning tree with every outside vertex attached as a leaf. Hence maximum leaves equals graph order minus minimum connected-domination size. Applied to \(Q_4\), this gives ten leaves and precisely the backbone classification above.

## Relationship to prior work

Connected domination and its complementarity with maximum-leaf spanning trees are classical. Camby and Schaudt (2013) analyze connected-domination bounds in graphs excluding long induced paths or cycles, not the exact optimal-backbone exchange geometry of this cube. Mane and Waphare (2012) study different hypercube domination invariants. Chen and Syu (2012) directly study minimum connected domination in hypercubes, making their inaccessible full text an important overlap risk; no assertion is made that the *scalar minimum* itself is new. Griggs (2021) treats maximum-leaf spanning trees and connected domination in hypercubes, including asymptotic bounds and the backbone correspondence, not this complete minimum-layer exchange metric. Existing database counts of all connected dominating subsets do not by themselves give the exchange-graph metric. The contribution is the **structural description of all optimum backbones together with their exact optimum-preserving exchange graph and distances**.

## Limitations

This is a small, complete finite classification of the four-cube only. Its numerical invariants come from exhaustive deterministic enumeration, accompanied by a reproducible checker, and no claim is made of a closed all-dimensional family. The full 2012 Chen–Syu hypercube paper was not available for a page-by-page originality comparison; historical/unindexed databases could also contain fragments of the optimum enumeration. Neither that gap nor keyword searches establish global uniqueness, and independent validation has not been performed. The separate exchange-graph metric is not implied by the known connected-domination value.
