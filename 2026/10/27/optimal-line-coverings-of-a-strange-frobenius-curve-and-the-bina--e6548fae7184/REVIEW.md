# Scientific review: optimal coverings of the strange Frobenius curve

## Correctness — PASS
The proof explicitly identifies rational points, all maximal secant blocks via \(\gcd(p^2-1,p^m-1)=p-1\), the exact permissible line intersections, and the rigorous incidence capacity bound. When \(p\) is odd, a first-moment identity excludes an optimal family of maximal secants that leaves one uncovered affine point and doubles another, completing the complete-cover characterization. Independent choices of directions in disjoint affine 2-planes establish the exponential lower bound. Pairing all remaining points completes the binary counterfamily for every odd \(m\ge3\). Finite-field checks cover \(q=27\), \(125\), and \(8\) but are not relied upon for the general statements.

## Originality — PASS, with priority uncertainty
Asgarli et al. (arXiv:2610.13042v1, full Section 4) prove the exact optimal **number**, and exhibit one pencil, but neither the classification of **all** minimum line covers, the Steiner secant realization, the exponential multiplicity of nonpencils, nor the sharp binary non-spread boundary. Gupta--Pavese (2024, complete introduction and Section 2) discusses affine spreads in a different setting, without assigning them to algebraic curve line-cover minimizers. Blokhuis--Brouwer--Szőnyi (2010, introductory statements and near-cover bound) covers an important *general obstruction* implicitly consistent with one ingredient; that earlier theorem does not imply the full curve-specific classification absent the Frobenius incidence identification, multiplicity, and binary construction. No matching mathematical-record claims were returned by targeted semantic searches; search absence is not proof of novelty. An unpublished or differently indexed equivalent statement remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The original 2026 paper motivates determining the *minimum covering number* of the explicit geometrically irreducible but nonreflexive curve. Distinguishing existence of one minimum cover from a complete description of all minimizers is natural in the same incidence-geometry problem. The theorem connects the minimizers with a canonical affine Steiner structure, produces a super-polynomial lower bound in the field size by genuinely nonparallel configurations, and pinpoints a characteristic-dependent rigidity boundary. It is more than a fixed-parameter recalculation; it describes the geometry of every optimal object for all odd primes and odd extension degrees.

## Limitations and priority risk
The precise count of affine line spreads, and hence exact number of optimal covers, is not determined. The binary case is an existence counterexample, not a full classification. General near-cover obstructions are prior art. Independent mathematical validation and definitive historical-priority adjudication remain outstanding.
