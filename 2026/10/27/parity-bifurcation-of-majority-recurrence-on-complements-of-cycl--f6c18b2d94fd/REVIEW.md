# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The update is reconstructed from the complement-cycle neighbourhood of degree \(n-3\). For odd \(n\), outer weights collapse to consensus, and the two central weight layers are analysed by cyclic-run counts: exactly the maximal independent-set words and their complements are fixed, all other states reach consensus within two updates. For even \(n\), the flanking layers are either absorbed or make explicit half-turn two-cycles, while the balanced layer reduces exactly to the complement of cyclic local majority. Persistence of adjacent equal pairs excludes any other recurrent cyclic-majority states. The run-composition formula counts balanced no-singleton words without assuming they are newly enumerated. Exhaustive, independent vertex-neighbourhood checks for \(5\le n\le18\) reproduce the structural sets and formulas. The finite verification is corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (bounded)
The oldest relevant full primary enumerative source, Munarini–Zagaglia Salvi (2003), *already* counts balanced no-zigzag words \(H_m\) and gives their asymptotics, so neither is claimed as new. Goles–Olivos (1980) establishes general eventual period at most two; Benjamini and collaborators (2014) cover this general setting and random/infinite graph results. Kaaser and collaborators (2015) give upper bounds on voting time, not an exact complement-cycle attractor census. Searches for complement-of-cycle majority recurrent states, dual anti-majority rules, balanced-word aliasing, and published exact tables found no source covering the whole dynamical parity classification. Related exact dynamics for the ordinary cycle, crown graphs and Möbius ladders concern different neighbourhood operators and cannot imply this census merely by relabelling; the new proof must establish the three global weight regimes and the odd-size tie mechanism. Residual risk is an unindexed exact complement-cycle classification.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The natural dense but minimally defective family \(\overline{C_n}\) changes its recurrent-state count from linear for odd sizes to exponential for even sizes, despite differing by a single vertex and having near-complete neighbourhoods. The theorem specifies exactly which configurations oscillate, gives the odd-size sharp small-time collapse, and identifies a concrete local-to-global mechanism behind the parity transition. This is a dynamical classification, not a restatement of the known balanced-word integer sequence. Even-size attraction-basin sizes and sharp transient depths remain open in this statement.
