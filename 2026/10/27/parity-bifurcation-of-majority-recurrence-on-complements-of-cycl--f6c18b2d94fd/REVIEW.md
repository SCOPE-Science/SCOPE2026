# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The open-neighbour one count is the total weight minus the centred triple. In odd order the outer layers go straight to consensus. At weight m in order 2m+1, the new ones count isolated ones and 000 centres. With r one-runs, the second count is at most m-r and the first is at most r-1 unless all ones are isolated. In the latter case r=m and the sole 00 gap determines a fixed word, with n labelled choices; complementing gives the other middle layer. A block of m ones witnesses sharpness. In even order, outside the balanced layer the only nonconsensus recurrent configurations are at weight m±1 with a single run of each colour: at m-1 the new ones are the 000 centres, attaining m-1 iff the zeros form one run. The run then moves by m and back, giving n distinct cycles across the two flanks. No trajectory leaving balance can return. Within balance the update is the complement of three-site majority, and its square along a balanced orbit is majority squared. Equal adjacent pairs are permanent for majority; their neighbouring sites either already lie in a permanent pair or join one in a step. Thus noncheckerboard majority orbits become fixed, so the only balanced recurrent words are the two checkerboards and the no-singleton words. The latter are exchanged with their complements. Counting positive runs of each colour by compositions with parts at least two, rooting at a one-run start and dividing by the number of roots gives H_m. This yields all counts and excludes longer periods. An exponential lower bound already follows by taking r=floor(m/3) in the sum and bounding the central binomial term below by 4^(r-1)/(2r-1).
