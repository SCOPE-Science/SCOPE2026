# Scientific review: clique-windmill minority dynamics

## Correctness (C): PASS

The claim's graph, closed-neighbourhood tie rule, and quantifiers are explicit. A direct energy-difference identity, complete local spin-count classification for both parities, connectivity of subset-inclusion graphs, and exact stationary boundary flux prove the recurrent census and mixing obstruction for all integers \(s,r\ge1\). The monotone-energy drift gives the stated expected entrance bound. The supplied exhaustive checker independently enumerates transition-graph terminal components in 21 small parameter instances; these checks supplement rather than replace the proof.

## Originality (O): PASS, bounded to accessible evidence

The closest primary antecedent, Regnault–Rouquier–Thierry, *Stochastic Minority on Graphs*, gives the model and general energy lemma and already classifies one clique. Its full accessible 2010 text (sections 2, 3 and 4, including theorem 4.1 and appendices) has been inspected; it does not assert the gluing-family all-parameter class count or the hub-colour conductance bound. A predecessor April 2008 version is verified by metadata but not full-text accessible. Targeted mathematical literature and indexed research searches under `friendship`, `windmill`, `clique-sum`, `zero-temperature antiferromagnetic`, `Glauber`, `ground state`, `attractor`, and `mixing` found no dominating statement, with residual risk from unindexed or inaccessible coverage. The complete-graph \(r=1\) corollary is explicitly credited, not claimed as new.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS

A block graph built entirely from cliques provides a natural solvable test of whether local spin minimization reaches global maximum cuts and whether rapid entry implies rapid equilibration. The result answers both: no nonground recurrent classes for any block multiplicity, a complete parity-sensitive communicating-class classification, and polynomial absorption versus exponential mixing due to an exact rare hub transition. The distinction is structural and holds for an unbounded two-parameter family; the numerical checks are not themselves the reason for value.

## Residual scientific limits

The mean absorption bound is not sharp, no upper mixing theorem is claimed, and model conventions are essential. The literature comparison is bounded, particularly for the inaccessible older preprint and possible unpublished descriptions of clique-sum antiferromagnetic chains. These do not supply an identified stronger implication in the material inspected.
