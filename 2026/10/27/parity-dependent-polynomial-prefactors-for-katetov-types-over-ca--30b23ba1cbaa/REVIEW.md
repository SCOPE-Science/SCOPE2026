# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The one-point extension principle gives an exact bijection to Katětov distance words. Nearest-neighbor differences characterize the Lipschitz side, while all noncritical low labels have strictly smaller tail growth. The odd critical label can occur only within a bounded span; an exact first-to-last-defect decomposition gives a finite convolution. Perron–Frobenius decomposition yields the constants and the even/odd polynomial-prefactor dichotomy. Independent finite-memory recurrence and direct enumeration agree on the tested configurations. The claim is pointwise in fixed distance bound; finite checks do not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS (with residual risk)
The closest primary prior literature establishes finite-distance Urysohn type functions (Sauer), the general Katětov one-point extension correspondence (Mbombo–Pestov), and an even/odd structural difference for *global* labelled finite metric-space counts (Mubayi–Terry). None of these statements implies the asserted asymptotic one-point type count over a fixed capped geodesic. A published diameter-three one-type polynomial covers related special cases and may imply the illustrative diameter-three formula; that specialization is not claimed new. The all-distance-bound spectral constants and linear paired-diameter asymptotic were not found in the checked material. The related published diameter-three type-polynomial record was examined in full, and specialized unindexed coverage remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
One-point type spectra are natural invariants of homogeneous metric structures. The result gives a sharp, all-bound family of asymptotics with explicit constants and shows a structural phase change between **linear** and **constant** prefactors at equal exponential entropy. The mechanism is the bounded spatial cluster of critical distance symbols; it cannot be read off from unconstrained alphabet size or from equilateral-base formulas. It supplies a quantitative benchmark for metric type enumeration rather than merely a numerical experiment.

## Scientific limits
Neither uniqueness of the result in all literature nor uniformity as the distance bound increases is claimed. No independent validation has been obtained; the computational tests are finite.
