# Mathematical review

## Correctness

**PASS.** For a partial injection, square-zero is equivalent to disjoint domain and image, giving the half-size upper bound. In dimensions congruent to two modulo four, the two parity classes have odd cardinality, so they cannot each be divided equally between a complementary domain and image. Explicit even interval translations attain the permitted bounds in each residue class, and restriction fills in every lower rank. At dimensions divisible by four, the equality case is a complementary binary word. The cited source/image adjacency criterion forces paired runs, and the parity condition forces each paired length to be even. Their ordered-composition count is exactly \(2^k\). The finite checker directly checks both fence relations without using the paper's gap test.

## Originality

**PASS, with priority risk.** The exact same monoid's established element characterization [1] is acknowledged; the published generating-rank theorem is about minimal generating sets, not maximal square-zero element rank. The inspected 2023 presentation [2] and 2024 ideal/maximal-subsemigroup classification [3] do not assert this residue-four bound or the exponential paired-run classification. The larger inverse fence semigroup [4] has different hypotheses and studies Green's relations/generators. Searches for square-zero, nilpotent, rank spectra, and extremal parity-preserving fence injections did not locate an equivalent statement in inspected records. A direct proof is included, but an implicit specialization of other comprehensive enumeration results remains possible.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The maximum attainable rank of square-zero elements is a natural intrinsic element invariant in finite partial-transformation monoids. Its sharp \(n\bmod4\) parity obstruction, gap-free rank support, and complete exponential family of extremizers demonstrate a nontrivial difference from unconstrained partial injections. The paired-run description is a reusable structural classification, not a computation on a chosen finite \(n\). This does not claim a full census.
