# Partial inner automorphisms of completely zero-simple semigroups depend only on their idempotents and local inner group

## Finding
For every group \(G\), every pair of nonempty index sets \(I,\Lambda\), and every regular Rees zero-matrix \(P=(p_{\lambda i})\) over \(G\) (each row and column has a nonzero entry), let \(S=M^0(G;I,\Lambda;P)\). Put
\[
E=\{(i,\lambda):p_{\lambda i}\ne0\},\qquad m=|E|,\qquad H=\operatorname{Inn}(G)\cong G/Z(G).
\]
With the definition of partial inner automorphisms in Araújo–Bentz–Kinyon–Konieczny–Malheiro–Mercier, the following are isomorphisms of inverse monoids:
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Inn}(S)\cong
\begin{cases}
H^0,&m=1,\\
B_E(H)^1,&m\ge2.
\end{cases}}
\]
Here \(H^0\) means \(H\) with an absorbing zero adjoined, and \(B_E(H)=(E\times H\times E)\cup\{0\}\) is the Brandt semigroup with \((e,u,f)(f,v,k)=(e,uv,k)\) and mismatched indices multiplying to zero. A superscript \(1\) denotes an additional identity element. The assertion allows infinite \(G,I,\Lambda\); the cardinal \(m=1\) exception uses regularity of \(P\).

In particular, for finite \(S\) with \(m\ge2\),
\[
|\operatorname{Inn}(S)|=m^2|G/Z(G)|+2,
\]
and for \(m=1\) it has \(|G/Z(G)|+1\) elements. Thus the entire sandwich matrix, beyond its number of nonzero cells, disappears from this invariant.

There exist nonisomorphic seven-element completely zero-simple semigroups with isomorphic eighteen-element partial inner automorphism monoids: use the trivial group and the two regular sandwich matrices
\[
P_A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\1&0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
P_B=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\1&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both have four nonzero entries. Their multisets of numbers of idempotents in nonzero \(\mathcal L\)-classes are respectively \(\{2,2\}\) and \(\{3,1\}\), so the semigroups cannot be isomorphic, whereas both partial inner automorphism monoids are \(B_4(1)^1\).

## Assumptions and scope
Write \(G^0=G\cup\{0\}\), and represent the nonzero elements of \(S\) by triples \((i,a,\lambda)\) with \(i\in I\), \(a\in G\), and \(\lambda\in\Lambda\). Multiplication is
\[
(i,a,\lambda)(k,b,\nu)=
\begin{cases}(i,a p_{\lambda k}b,\nu),&p_{\lambda k}\ne0,\\0,&p_{\lambda k}=0.\end{cases}
\]
The external zero annihilates every element. For \(g,h\in S^1\), define
\[
D_{g,h}=\{x\in S:(gh)x=x=x(gh)\},\qquad
\phi_{g,h}:D_{g,h}\longrightarrow D_{h,g},\quad x\longmapsto hxg.
\]
The inverse monoid \(\operatorname{Inn}(S)\) is generated by these partial maps under partial-map composition. These definitions, including the generated-monoid convention, are exactly those of the motivating paper. Note that every \(D_{g,h}\) contains the external zero; the absorbing element of the resulting inverse monoid is the identity map on \(\{0\}\), not the empty partial map.

## Proof
**1. Every nonzero domain is one maximal group and zero.** Let \(g,h\in S\). If \(gh=0\), the only element fixed on both sides by \(gh\) is zero. Otherwise write \(gh=(i,t,\lambda)\). For a nonzero \(x=(j,a,\mu)\), equality \((gh)x=x\) forces \(j=i\), \(p_{\lambda i}\ne0\), and \(t p_{\lambda i}=1_G\). Equality \(x(gh)=x\) forces \(\mu=\lambda\), \(p_{\lambda i}\ne0\), and \(p_{\lambda i}t=1_G\). These equations are equivalent to \(t=p_{\lambda i}^{-1}\), so \(gh\) must be the idempotent
\[
e_{i\lambda}=(i,p_{\lambda i}^{-1},\lambda).
\]
Conversely, when \(gh=e_{i\lambda}\), every element of the maximal subgroup
\[
G_{i\lambda}=\{(i,a,\lambda):a\in G\}
\]
is fixed on both sides by \(gh\), and no other nonzero element is. Thus \(D_{g,h}=\{0\}\cup G_{i\lambda}\). If either conjugator is the adjoined identity, the same argument applies to \(gh\in S\); if both are identities, then \(D_{1,1}=S\) and \(\phi_{1,1}=\mathrm{id}_S\).

**2. Every bridge between maximal groups is inner conjugation.** Choose \(e=(i,\lambda)\in E\), \(f=(k,\nu)\in E\), and abbreviate \(p=p_{\lambda i}\), \(q=p_{\nu k}\). The identifications
\[
\tau_e(x)=(i,xp^{-1},\lambda),\qquad
\tau_f(x)=(k,xq^{-1},\nu)
\]
are group isomorphisms \(G\to G_{i\lambda}\) and \(G\to G_{k\nu}\). Every nonzero generator between these groups can be written using
\[
g=(i,a,\nu),\qquad h=(k,q^{-1}a^{-1}p^{-1},\lambda),\qquad a\in G.
\]
Indeed \(gh=e_{i\lambda}\) and \(hg=e_{k\nu}\); conversely the multiplication formula forces precisely these parameters for any nonzero generator. Direct multiplication yields
\[
\tau_f^{-1}\bigl(h\tau_e(x)g\bigr)
=q^{-1}a^{-1}xaq=(aq)^{-1}x(aq).
\]
As \(a\) varies, \(aq\) runs through all of \(G\). Therefore the distinct maps from \(\{0\}\cup G_{i\lambda}\) onto \(\{0\}\cup G_{k\nu}\) are indexed exactly by \(H=G/Z(G)\), with zero fixed. The equality of two conjugations is equivalent to their parameters differing by an element of the group center, since they agree on every \(x\in G\).

**3. Multiplication is Brandt multiplication.** Suppose one such map takes \(G_{i\lambda}\) to \(G_{k\nu}\), and another takes \(G_{i'\lambda'}\) to \(G_{k'\nu'}\). When the middle groups agree, their composite is the conjugation determined by the product in \(H\). Otherwise their only shared intermediate point is external zero, so the composite is the partial identity on \(\{0\}\), which is absorbing for every generator. This gives exactly the Brandt multiplication on \(B_E(H)\). The partial identities and inverses belong to the stated maps, so this is the entire inverse semigroup generated by the non-global maps.

**4. The global identity.** The remaining generator is \(\mathrm{id}_S\). If \(m\ge2\), there are at least two nonzero maximal subgroup blocks, so \(\mathrm{id}_S\) is not any local partial map and must be adjoined as a new identity. If \(m=1\), regularity of \(P\) forces \(|I|=|\Lambda|=1\), whence \(S\cong G^0\), and the identity is already the local identity. This proves the two cases.

**5. The example.** Both displayed matrices have no zero row or column, have four nonzero entries, and define seven-element semigroups. In a Rees zero-matrix semigroup, a nonzero \(\mathcal L\)-class consists of fixed third-coordinate triples, and its number of idempotents equals the number of nonzero entries in the corresponding sandwich row. The row-degree multisets above differ, an isomorphism invariant. The classification yields the same \(B_4(1)^1\) for both, completing the example.

## Verification
The standalone file `verify_rees_inner.py` computes the defining products and the exact domains \(D_{g,h}\) for every pair of conjugators. It constructs all resulting partial maps, checks closure under every pairwise composition, and compares their count with the proposed classification. Executed finite cases include trivial, cyclic of orders two and three, and symmetric of degree three groups; one-by-one and all four standard two-by-two zero-pattern cases; and two nontrivially labelled regular two-by-three patterns. The resulting direct checks return `VERIFY_OK`. This finite testing does not substitute for the unrestricted proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Araújo, Bentz, Kinyon, Konieczny, Malheiro and Mercier (2026), Definition 3.8 and Theorem 4.2, introduce \(\operatorname{Inn}(S)\) and describe its generators for **completely simple** (zero-free Rees matrix) semigroups; their Problem 7.5 specifically asks for **Rees zero-matrix** semigroups. The present classification solves that problem for every regular Rees zero-matrix semigroup, including matrices with genuinely zero entries, and determines the resulting inverse monoid up to isomorphism, with the one-idempotent boundary and explicit information-loss examples. It uses their partial-map definition, not a new definition of inner automorphism.

The earlier companion work *Conjugacy in Abstract Semigroups, Transformation and Diagram Monoids, and Conjugacy Growth* (2023/2026), Theorem 2.19, determines natural-conjugacy classes in completely zero-simple semigroups. Its statement is a relation on pairs of elements, not a multiplication and isomorphism classification of the monoid of all partial inner maps. The prior theory of inverse semigroups covers Brandt input semigroups; here the input semigroup is allowed to be noninverse, as in the explicit seven-element example.

## Limitations
This theorem concerns **completely zero-simple** semigroups (regular sandwich matrices), not arbitrary zero-simple semigroups without primitive idempotents, nor arbitrary semigroups with a zero. It classifies the *abstract partial inner automorphism monoid*, not the full partial automorphism monoid, the automorphism group, or conjugacy classes of all semigroup elements. Finite direct checks cover only the examples described. No independent mathematical audit has been performed. Earlier equivalent statements hidden in classical semigroup literature remain a bibliographic possibility; the cited 2026 work itself presents the Rees zero-matrix case as an open problem.
