# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The domain calculation is exact: a nonzero element fixed on both sides by a Rees product must lie in the group block whose identity equals that product. The change of coordinates \(\tau_e\) and \(\tau_f\) gives precisely the conjugation \(x\mapsto(aq)^{-1}x(aq)\), with arbitrary \(aq\in G\). Multiplication of the resulting maps is Brandt multiplication, because distinct group blocks intersect only in the external zero. The identity case is distinct precisely when there are at least two nonzero sandwich entries. Direct exhaustive generation and composition checks for the stated small cases agree with every predicted cardinality.

## Originality — PASS, provisional literature assessment
The 2026 paper (arXiv:2605.00041v1) poses characterization of these very monoids for Rees zero-matrix semigroups as Problem 7.5; its Theorem 4.2 treats the zero-free Rees case. The earlier companion arXiv:2301.04252v3 provides natural-conjugacy classes, not the inverse-monoid multiplication law. Search comparisons over equivalent terminology, reductions to Brandt quotients, and the published mathematical-record index found no covering statement. The inverse-semigroup Munn-type quotient covers only the Brandt-input subcase and does not imply the noninverse case by an immediate restriction. Residual risk: an overlooked equivalent older construction of partial inner maps or a result implicit in more general semigroup-category literature.
