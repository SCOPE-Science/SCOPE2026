# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The theorem takes fixed \(k\ge2\), \(m_n\le n^B\), and independent \(p_n=1/k+\lambda/\sqrt n\) approval edges. JR fails precisely when an unelected candidate has at least \(\lceil n/k\rceil\) supporters outside the union of elected supporters. Conditioning on the exact-quota elected columns bounds the uncovered set by \(n-\lceil n/k\rceil\); independence and Hoeffding's inequality give an explicit exponentially small probability per unselected candidate. A polynomial union bound makes **all** eligible committees JR at once. Independent binomial column counts, a binomial local limit, and Poisson convergence then prove the full committee-count and existence laws. Fixed off-critical approval probabilities are ruled out by an exponential large-deviation estimate. The standalone program verifies the JR equivalence exhaustively in three finite markets and tests the local asymptotics; its calculations are not treated as a proof of the infinite statement.

**Originality — PASS, subject to literature risk.** The directly relevant 2026 source defines DR and proves worst-case hardness, while its empirical analysis addresses alternative local criteria; it does not state an independent-approval \(\sqrt n\)-candidate transition, the quantitative all-committee JR event, or the \(\binom{\mathrm{Poisson}(\mu)}k\) feasible-committee limit. The 2025 AJR study obtains probability transitions under constant candidate supply for a different axiom, without DR's exact-support condition. Elkind et al. explicitly prove all size-\(k\) committees satisfy JR with high probability when the number of candidates is fixed (Proposition 1); this is stronger JR coverage than mere existence, but it lacks both growing candidate supply and exact-quota DR requirements. The result does not claim new binomial or Poisson technology; its new object is the complete DR feasibility/count law in the specified stochastic scaling. Relevant full texts and targeted topical searches were compared by implication, not title alone. Unindexed independent proofs remain possible.

**Value — PASS.** A newly introduced, worst-case NP-hard and sometimes unsatisfiable diversity axiom has an exact, nontrivial random-instance phase diagram: with fixed \(k\), availability of exactly supported candidates switches on at \(m\asymp\sqrt n\), the approval-probability critical window is explicitly Gaussian, and the simultaneous JR theorem shows which component of the axiom actually controls typical feasibility. The count law is stronger than mere existence and gives the limiting expected number of valid committees. This directly addresses how candidate availability alters the apparent impossibility of a natural fairness requirement, rather than an arbitrary finite parameter slice.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Chingoma et al. (arXiv:2609.29332v1, Section 6 and Appendix D), Han et al. (arXiv:2510.18718v1, Sections 2–3), and Elkind et al. (arXiv:2108.12949, JR random-model background). Main risks: publication priority and extensions hidden in unindexed writing. The theorem does not assert coverage for growing \(k\), correlated approvals, or exponential candidate supply. The explicit upper bound is asymptotic and can be numerically vacuous at small \(n\).
