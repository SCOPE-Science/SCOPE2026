# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** For the standard integral \(\mathbb Z^7\) Picard lattice and the twenty-seven listed line classes, six root reflections generate the complete \(W(E_6)\) action of order \(51840\). An exhaustive conjugation calculation gives \(25\) types. For each type, taking orbit sums and the integer Smith invariants computes the lattice-saturation index; a separate Hermite-form/maximal-minor calculation yields the same result. The proof explains why these minors compute \([P^w:T]\), using the primitivity of \(P^w\) and rational spanning. Explicit bases explain the three exceptional types. The finite computation is exhaustive in the finite Weyl group, and standard cubic-surface descent supplies the scope over arbitrary finite fields.

**Originality — PASS (provisional historical assessment).** Banwait–Fité–Loughran (arXiv:1606.00300, especially their class tables and cohomology discussion) cover Frobenius types, line orbit structures, ranks and a distinct skew-line index; they do not present the integral orbit-sum trace-cokernel as defined here. Swinnerton-Dyer (DOI 10.1017/S0305004100076106) studies \(H^1(G,P)\), different from the cohomology of relations among line classes controlling the present cokernel. published-finding corpus searches using line norms, lattice saturation, Picard fixed modules, twenty-seven lines and Weyl \(E_6\) did not identify the same theorem. This is not a proof of worldwide priority: a calculation of \(H^1\) of the relation lattice could imply it, and nearby classical Weyl tables contain all necessary ingredients.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The arithmetic Picard lattice and its natural orbit-trace sublattice govern integral descent of divisor classes on finite-field cubic surfaces. The complete classification sharply identifies three and only three genuine saturation failures, with a concrete order-nine example where all rationally effective line traces lie in \(3\mathbb ZH\) although \(H\) is integral and effective. This is a natural structural invariant not recoverable from Picard rank or rational line-orbit counts alone; it matters for integral descent and orbit-norm generation questions.

**Sources and limitations.** Elsenhans–Jahnel, arXiv:1006.0721; Banwait–Fité–Loughran, arXiv:1606.00300; Swinnerton-Dyer, DOI 10.1017/S0305004100076106. Scope excludes exact finite-field realizability by field size and does not claim full descriptions of effective monoids. The proof's finite enumeration is reproducible, but independent audit and comprehensive historical priority certification have not been performed.
