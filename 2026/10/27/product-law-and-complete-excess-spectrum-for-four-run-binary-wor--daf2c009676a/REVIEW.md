# Review of the four-run closed-factor product law

**Correctness (PASS).** Each nonempty factor crosses at most three letter changes. The exhaustive run-count classification in `RESULT.md` leaves no missing form: one-run powers; no two-run closed factors; symmetric three-run complete returns; and four-run complete returns exactly when both occurrences of the border \(0^i1^j\) fit. The four families are disjoint and their counts add to \(n+1+pq\). A constructive converse yields every admissible product \(pq\), and balancing proves the fixed-length maximum. The separate all-border enumerator in `verify.py` matched every actual factor set for 1296 words with run lengths up to six and all attainable spectra for lengths four to eighteen. The proof, not the finite experiment, covers every positive integer parameter.

**Originality (PASS, residual priority risk).** The full foundational article by Badkobeh, Fici, and Lipták establishes the zero-excess characterization and one weaker four-block lower-bound construction, not the complete positive four-run defect law. Parshina and Puzynina determine unrestricted asymptotic maxima, and Mieno and collaborators give algorithms for arbitrary supplied strings, neither entailing this symbolic classification or spectrum. The published exact binary closed-rich record determines unrestricted global maxima only at lengths 25–27. Targeted searches of complete-return / four-run / bitonic-defect / exact-factor spectrum aliases and the published mathematical records did not identify a covering claim. An unindexed equivalent statement remains possible; this review does not certify priority.

**Value (PASS).** The four-run family is the first binary class outside the sharp minimum-characterization of closed-factor richness. Exactly quantifying its first defect is a motivated structural question, especially since earlier four-block words provided only quadratic-existence witnesses. The product law and nonconsecutive attainable spectrum distinguish the exceptional minimum class from a sharply classified class with quadratic excess; the result is an unrestricted-parameter theorem rather than a finite table or a routine substitution of a known general formula.
