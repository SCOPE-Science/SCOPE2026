# Scientific review: logarithmic volume laws for limited-permutation balls

## Correctness — PASS

The result uses the exact radius-one simultaneous-disjoint-swap channel, with every input word drawn independently and uniformly. The proof establishes the injectivity of effective swap selections using their leftmost differing edge, the independent Bernoulli distribution of adjacent inequalities, and the resulting Fibonacci-product volume. Conditioning on the first terminating zero gives an exact all-real-moment recurrence and generating function. A unique strictly dominant positive simple pole gives locally analytic pressure asymptotics. Differentiating the pressure root gives explicit strictly positive Gaussian variance; analytic cumulant expansions and Gärtner–Ellis establish the finite-variance CLT and full LDP, respectively. Binet expansion establishes the stated large-alphabet coefficients. Separate exhaustive and rational finite checks reconstruct the low-dimensional channel and moment recurrence. The asymptotic statements are analytic proofs, not extrapolations from those tests.

## Originality — PASS, with disclosed uncertainty

The closest literature is M. Langberg, M. Schwartz and Y. Yaakobi (2017), *Coding for the \(\ell_\infty\)-Limited Permutation Channel*, Theorems 16 and 18, accessible full PDF at https://www.ece.mcmaster.ca/~schwam10/publications/MSjrnl35.pdf . The antirun Fibonacci-product formula and **arithmetic mean** radius-one ball count are already theirs. They are credited and not claimed as new. Their exact formulas do not state the logarithmic/quenched typical exponent, exact Gaussian variance, real-moment pressure, LDP, or a strict quantitative arithmetic/geometric exponent separation; these are different distributional assertions requiring further arguments. The July 21, 2026 work of N. Ben Shimon and A. L. Zabokritskiy, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19566 , Sections 1–2, 5 and 8, concerns code construction, extinction, capacity covers and detection; its Fibonacci all-distinct calculation is a distinct extremal case and does not imply a typical-center law for fixed alphabet. Searches of public mathematical research records for aliases including random antirun, quenched/annealed entropy, disjoint-swap ball CLT, and Fibonacci renewal pressure did not reveal the same theorem. There remains a risk of an unindexed stochastic-process formulation or a derivation implicit in historical work; absence of search hits does not prove uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

The ball volume under this channel can vary exponentially with the center. The earlier arithmetic mean therefore does not answer the natural coding-geometric question of how many outcomes a *typical* center produces. The new strictly smaller quenched exponent proves an order-exponential average/typical discrepancy; the Gaussian variance and LDP quantify both central and rare variation across centers; and the positive explicit \(q^{-1}\) coefficient identifies how this discrepancy disappears as the alphabet grows. The full real-moment recurrence and analytic pressure unify these properties, rather than supplying an isolated routine moment or a fixed small instance. The result neither changes zero-error code capacity nor asserts an operational decoding performance bound, and those applications would require separate work.

## Limitations and comparison priorities

The proof assumes independent uniform symbols, fixed finite alphabet in the long-block limit, and displacement radius one with simultaneous nonoverlapping swaps. Related nonuniform models, sequential swaps and code capacity are not settled. The 2017 product and mean results are stronger in their own direction (exact center-by-center balls and arithmetic mean) but do not cover the probabilistic distribution laws. Generic renewal and complex-analytic limit theorems are standard mathematical ingredients. An equivalent result may exist in literature under another stochastic matching-polynomial description despite the searches and primary-text comparisons.
