# Rank-support lattice and exact rank-weight enumerator of Frobenius-consistent Fourier codes

## Finding

The rank metric of a finite-field Fourier spectrum records the base-field dimension of the span of **its field-valued spectral coordinates**, not the number of nonzero coordinates. For every finite abelian group and every splitting-field extension, the Frobenius-consistent code has an exact rank-support lattice and a closed product formula for the number of codewords at each rank. The result includes repeated-root Frobenius modules when the characteristic divides the relevant extension degree.

Write \(q\) for a prime power of characteristic \(p\), \(m\ge1\), \(A\) for a finite abelian group whose exponent \(N\) divides \(q^m-1\), and \(d=\operatorname{ord}_N(q)\), setting \(d=1\) if \(N=1\). The \(q\)-power map on the character group \(\widehat A\) has orbits indexed by \(i=1,\ldots,s\), of lengths \(\ell_i\mid d\). Define \(\mathcal F_A\subseteq\mathbb F_{q^m}^{\widehat A}\) by the equations \(V(\chi^q)=V(\chi)^q\). These are precisely the spectra of \(\mathbb F_q\)-valued functions on \(A\); a codeword has one independent seed \(a_i\in\mathbb F_{q^{\ell_i}}\) per orbit. Its **rank support** and **rank weight** are
\[
S(V)=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_q}\{V(\chi):\chi\in\widehat A\},\qquad \rho(V)=\dim_{\mathbb F_q}S(V).
\]
Let \(\sigma(x)=x^q\) on \(\mathbb F_{q^d}\). For a monic divisor \(f\mid T^d-1\), put \(W_f=\ker f(\sigma)\). **The occurring rank supports are precisely all** \(W_f\) with \(f\mid T^d-1\), each occurring with strictly positive multiplicity; in particular these are all \(q\)-Frobenius-invariant \(\mathbb F_q\)-subspaces of \(\mathbb F_{q^d}\), and \(\dim W_f=\deg f\).

Factor \(T^d-1=\prod_P P^{r_P}\) over \(\mathbb F_q\), with distinct monic irreducible factors \(P\). Define \(\delta_P=\deg P\), \(a_{iP}=v_P(T^{\ell_i}-1)\), and
\[
 b_P(j)=\sum_{i=1}^s\min\{j,a_{iP}\},\qquad
 L_{P,0}=1,\qquad
 L_{P,j}=q^{\delta_P b_P(j)}-q^{\delta_P b_P(j-1)}\quad (1\le j\le r_P).
\]
Then **exactly** \(\prod_P L_{P,j_P}\) codewords have the particular rank support \(W_f\), where \(f=\prod_P P^{j_P}\). Consequently the exact rank-weight enumerator is
\[
\boxed{\sum_{V\in\mathcal F_A}z^{\rho(V)}
 =\prod_{P\mid T^d-1}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{r_P}L_{P,j}z^{j\delta_P}\right).}
\]
The number of *distinct occurring subspaces* is \(\prod_P(r_P+1)\). For a uniformly random input function (equivalently, a uniformly random codeword), the probability that its rank support is all of \(\mathbb F_{q^d}\) is
\[
\boxed{\Pr[\rho(V)=d]=\prod_{P\mid T^d-1}\bigl(1-q^{-\delta_P t_P}\bigr),\quad
 t_P=\#\{i:a_{iP}=r_P\}.}
\]
All coefficients and supports are exact; there is no assumption that \(T^d-1\) is square-free.

## Assumptions and scope

There is no restriction on the finite abelian group beyond \(N\mid q^m-1\); consequently \(p\nmid|A|\) and finite-field Fourier inversion holds. The extension \(\mathbb F_{q^d}\) embeds canonically in \(\mathbb F_{q^m}\). A rank support is the subspace of **field values**, considered inside \(\mathbb F_{q^d}\), rather than the positional support in \(\widehat A\). The code is \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear and in general not \(\mathbb F_{q^m}\)-linear. The orbit decomposition, and hence all multiplicities above, refer to complete Fourier spectra, not to truncated observations, arbitrary residuals, or an unspecified random ensemble.

The order \(d\) is attained by the orbit of a character of order \(N\), because the dual of a finite abelian group of exponent \(N\) has such a character. Thus at least one orbit has length \(d\). The trivial group is admitted with \(N=1\), \(d=1\) and one singleton orbit. The factorization and enumeration are over arbitrary prime-power fields, even though the accompanying small-case executable uses prime-base fields.

## Proof

**Step 1 (descent and conjugate spanning).** The Fourier descent theorem identifies every member of \(\mathcal F_A\) with independent seeds \(a_i\in\mathbb F_{q^{\ell_i}}\), and expands orbit \(i\) to \(a_i,a_i^q,\ldots,a_i^{q^{\ell_i-1}}\). Hence \(S(V)=\sum_i\mathbb F_q[\sigma]a_i\), a sum of cyclic Frobenius modules inside \(\mathbb F_{q^d}\).

**Step 2 (submodule lattice, including inseparability).** The normal basis theorem gives an \(\mathbb F_q[T]\)-module isomorphism
\[
 \mathbb F_{q^d}\simeq\mathbb F_q[T]/(T^d-1),\quad T\cdot u=\sigma(u).
\]
This remains valid when \(p\mid d\); no semisimplicity is invoked. For each monic \(f\mid T^d-1\), the submodule \(\ker f(\sigma)\) corresponds to the ideal generated by \((T^d-1)/f\) modulo \(T^d-1\). It is the unique submodule with annihilator \(f\) and has dimension \(\deg f\). Every invariant subspace is one of these kernels. If \(f_i\) is the monic polynomial of least degree annihilating seed \(a_i\) under \(\sigma\) (set \(f_i=1\) when \(a_i=0\)), then \(f_i\mid T^{\ell_i}-1\) and its cyclic module is precisely \(W_{f_i}\). The sum of the corresponding ideals is the ideal for \(\operatorname{lcm}_i f_i\), hence
\[
S(V)=W_{\operatorname{lcm}_i f_i}.
\]
This proves the exact-domain and rank part of the assertion without finite enumeration.

**Step 3 (primary-component counting).** The Chinese remainder theorem decomposes the cyclic module \(\mathbb F_{q^d}\) and each subfield \(\mathbb F_{q^{\ell_i}}=\ker(T^{\ell_i}-1)(\sigma)\) into independent \(P\)-primary factors. In the \(P\)-factor of seed space \(i\), the possible annihilator exponent is between \(0\) and \(a_{iP}\). For any \(j\ge0\), exactly \(q^{\delta_P\min(j,a_{iP})}\) elements have exponent at most \(j\), because this set is the kernel of \(P^j(\sigma)\) within that factor. Independent seeds therefore produce \(q^{\delta_P b_P(j)}\) tuples whose maximum \(P\)-exponent is at most \(j\). Subtracting consecutive cumulative counts yields \(L_{P,j}\), with the zero case \(L_{P,0}=1\). Independence between different irreducible factors makes the multiplicities multiply. Substituting \(\dim W_f=\sum_P j_P\delta_P\) gives the rank-weight enumerator.

**Step 4 (nonzero multiplicities and full support).** A character of order \(N\) has orbit length \(d\), so for every \(P\) there is a seed with \(a_{iP}=r_P\). Thus \(b_P(j)>b_P(j-1)\) for each \(1\le j\le r_P\); all \(L_{P,j}\) are positive, realizing every \(W_f\). The top exponent occurs with probability
\[
\frac{L_{P,r_P}}{q^{\delta_P b_P(r_P)}}
=1-q^{-\delta_P(b_P(r_P)-b_P(r_P-1))}
=1-q^{-\delta_P t_P}.
\]
Multiplying this expression across \(P\) establishes the full-rank probability. Every case, including \(p\mid d\), is covered.

## Verification

The executable `verify_rank_profile.py` implements finite fields as polynomial quotients and checks multiplicative inverses exhaustively, computes independent \(q\)-Frobenius orbits and their admissible seed subfields, enumerates every seed tuple, computes the span of all spectral coordinates by modular Gaussian elimination, and compares its rank distribution and actual subspace count against the factorization formula. Six complete cyclic cases were checked: \((q,m,n)=(2,2,3),(2,3,7),(2,4,5),(2,4,15),(3,2,8),(5,2,6)\), totaling \(55{,}122\) actual codewords. (Here \(n\) is the cyclic-group order.)

A separate independent check enumerates the complete \(2^9=512\)-word input space on the *noncyclic* group \(A=C_3\times C_3\), computes the finite-field character transform directly into \(\mathbb F_4\), and compares the observed rank distribution \(1,31,480\) and three rank-support subspaces with the formula. The output ends with `RANK_PROFILE_VERIFY_OK` and `NONCYCLIC_DFT_VERIFY_OK`. Both the executable and its captured stdout are included. Computations are finite checks; the unrestricted theorem is proved by Steps 1–4.

For \(q=2\) and \(A=C_{15}\) with splitting field \(\mathbb F_{16}\), the exact rank enumerator is
\[
1+31z+480z^2+3584z^3+28672z^4.
\]
Its coefficients total \(2^{15}\); there are exactly five possible rank-support subspaces and a full-rank fraction \(7/8\). This example includes a repeated-root polynomial \(T^4-1=(T+1)^4\) in characteristic two.

## Relationship to prior work

Kumallagov, Sizikov and Zarubin (arXiv:2605.20062, first submitted 2026-05-19), Theorem 3.1 and Proposition 8.1, establish the general finite-abelian Fourier descent and the independent-orbit seed description used here. Their Proposition 8.2 computes the **positional symbol-Hamming** weight enumerator by multiplying independent orbit contributions. It does not calculate the \(\mathbb F_q\)-span of spectral *values*, its invariant-subspace support lattice, or the rank-weight populations; the positional support and field-value span are different invariants and do not determine one another.

Maithri K. and coauthors (arXiv:2601.09201, Section 2.2) and earlier normal-element literature establish the \(\mathbb F_q\)-order of a *single* field element and its relation to conjugate-span dimension; those single-seed facts are explicitly used rather than claimed as new. The step involving a least common multiple across independent seeds of different subfields, the primary-component cumulative counts, and the resulting exact enumerator for the Fourier code are the claimed contribution. Prior full text and mathematical research-record searches (including Frobenius rank enumerators, q-order aliases, cyclotomic spectral codes, and equivalent formulations) did not identify a theorem implying the full result. This is evidence of limited checked coverage, not a proof of priority.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns *complete* Frobenius-consistent Fourier spectra of arbitrary finite abelian groups with all roots of unity in the splitting field. It does not characterize the rank distribution of arbitrary corrupted spectra, rank-metric covering radii, shortened spectra, or decoding complexity. Its rank-support definition must not be confused with the coefficient-basis Hamming support of a single symbol. The exact formulas depend on factoring \(T^d-1\) over \(\mathbb F_q\), and the provided verifier covers only small prime-base cases. Full priority relative to every older finite-field module-enumerator formulation, including implicit corollaries in unindexed literature, has not been independently established.
