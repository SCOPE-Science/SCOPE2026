# Scientific review

## Correctness (C): PASS

The published independent-searcher survival representation is rewritten as an intrinsic survival term plus an initial-mortality term. A common-process coupling proves the total-variation estimate; the rare-survival calculation uses exponential bare-search tails and two direct dominated-convergence limits. Conditional convergence follows by conditioning on initial-searcher death. For all real positive moment orders a fixed positive-mass subset of promptly completed bare searches gives an exponential domination bound, allowing the limit to be exchanged with an unbounded time integral. Two independent exact-law examples passed 24 computational comparisons. Risks are restricted to the stated exponential-moment and independent-clock hypotheses; they are explicitly included.

## Originality (O): PASS with residual priority risk

The exact survival probability and the discontinuous small-turnover **first moment** are already published in arXiv:2602.11095v1. arXiv:2601.07096v1 independently gives the same survival formula and analyzes the opposite, high-turnover coefficient-of-variation regime. The reviewed addition is a quantified low-turnover rare-event profile, a conditional limiting clock, and the full real-order moment phase diagram. Targeted searches produced no identical stated result. Since the new laws are derived from a known exact expression by asymptotic reasoning, their priority might be implicit in broader first-passage or rare-event literature. Full access to every supplementary section of the second source could not be confirmed, leaving that comparison as a residual risk.

## Value (V): PASS

The source's unexpected mean jump becomes quantitatively intelligible through the exact distribution of a vanishingly rare subpopulation of long-delayed searches. This yields a robust reliability consequence: arbitrarily close first-passage distributions in total variation can have variance growing as the reciprocal turnover rate, and higher moments have different divergence exponents. The criterion concerns a natural stochastic-detection model and a physically meaningful distinction between typical delay and failure-induced tail risk.
