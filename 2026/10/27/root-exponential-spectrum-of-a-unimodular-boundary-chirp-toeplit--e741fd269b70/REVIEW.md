# Review of the inverse-boundary chirp spectral law

## Correctness

PASS. The radial weighted-ball monomial quotient is an exact beta moment with degree shift \(N=k+d-1\). The substitution \(x=(1-t)/t\) exposes a beta-prime integral. Gamma mixing and the Abel-limited Macdonald integral reduce its asymptotics to a standard sectorial large-argument expansion; the gamma central-limit correction contributes the essential factor \(e^{a/2}\). Power normalization and geometric multiplicities were checked separately. The exact \(s\downarrow0\) counting coefficient follows by a threshold sandwich, not any unproved monotonicity. Numerical calculations are corroborative, not a proof.

## Originality

PASS with residual priority risk. The full relevant sections of Cao–He–Zhang (arXiv:2609.24614) establish qualitative compactness, not the sharp spectral law. Looi (arXiv:2603.10879, §§3–4) treats logarithmic boundary chirps with persistent oscillatory eigenvalues, whereas the inverse-gap chirp has root-exponential decay. Rozenblum (DOI:10.4171/JST/13, §5) studies fast oscillation in decaying symbols on Bargmann spaces, not the weighted-ball, modulus-one phase. Dedicated search for the exact phase, Bessel/gamma formulation, and counting coefficient yielded no equivalent statement; absence from search is not proof of global novelty. A 2020 radial Toeplitz/Schatten paper was not available in full text and remains a material historical-priority uncertainty.

## Value

PASS. The complete complex asymptotic and dimension-dependent counting coefficient give a quantitatively sharp spectral model directly tied to qualitative compactness of Toeplitz operators with nonvanishing symbol modulus. The result also separates rapid boundary cancellation from the slowly oscillating radial examples with nontrivial essential spectra. Its sharpness is in the exponential scale and full prefactor, not merely an observation of compactness.

## Limitations and literature

Fixed phase and parameters; no general classification. An accessible primary text establishes only qualitative compactness, a second establishes a different oscillatory regime, and a 2011 primary article deals with different domains/symbols. The 2020 radial Schatten article was abstract-accessible but full-text unavailable. There is no independent review or certified formal proof.
