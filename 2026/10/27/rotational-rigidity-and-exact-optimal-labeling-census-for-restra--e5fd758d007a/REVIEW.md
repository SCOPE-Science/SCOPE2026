# Scientific review of the optimal-labeling census

## Correctness

PASS. The proof first derives the unavoidable zero-pair structure and a nonnegative block-excess decomposition. Equality and excess-two cases are fully classified; the disjoint no-zero case is bounded by domination of the cycle and checked explicitly at orders four and five. This settles all integer orders at least three. Two independent finite implementations, raw label enumeration through order nine and weighted cyclic transfer through order 120, agree with the theorem. The infinite claim is justified by the structural proof, not inferred from finite data.

## Originality

PASS under the inspected evidence, with residual priority uncertainty. Mojdeh–Masoumi–Volkmann (arXiv:2106.08501, Theorem 5; later journal Theorem 3.3) already established the minimum weight and exhibited suitable functions. The new assertion is the exhaustive classification, exact optimizer counts, symmetry-orbit census, and associated generating series, none implied solely by the scalar minimum formula or a construction. Related primary work on general bounds and graph operations was inspected for stronger implications. No matching exact cycle census was located in the searches described in `AUDIT.json`. Unindexed prior work cannot be ruled out by search failure alone.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The natural multiplicity question for an optimized graph-labeling invariant distinguishes a complete set of optimal defense deployments from a single exhibited one. The zero-pair excess lemma explains a global rotational rigidity phenomenon for every cycle of length at least six, as well as the precise small-order symmetry exceptions. The exact all-orders sequence and rational series give reproducible enumerative information not contained in the known minimum-weight identity.

## Limitations

Only simple labeled cycles are covered; near-minimizers and general graphs are not classified. The independent numerical checks cover finite lengths, and priority remains subject to fuller bibliographic review.
