# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The classification treats separately zero-only factors, factors containing one marked symbol (impossible for an Abelian square), and factors containing both marked symbols. For the latter it derives the exact midpoint condition, including the integer parity boundary; each admissible pair of flank lengths determines exactly one distinct factor. The diagonal sum counts these pairs without multiplicities. The sharp asymptotic follows from a uniformly bounded lattice-to-area error, an exhaustive case split for a continuous band area, and a unique equality case. This establishes both directions of asymptotic rigidity. Independent direct-substring enumeration matched the explicit factor sets and diagonal count on 2,197 parameter triples (nonnegative coordinates 0 through 12); two known or derived exact specializations each matched 25 parameter values. This is finite corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS with explicit residual risk

Fici–Mignosi–Shallit (arXiv:1701.00948, Proposition 1) exhibit the equal-run member \(0^k1\,0^k1\,0^k\) as a quadratic-order lower-bound witness; the exact all-parameter classification, optimal \(1/16\) coefficient, and rigidity are not stated there. Simpson (arXiv:1802.04481, pp. 3–4) explicitly counts the asymmetric subfamily \(0^k1\,0^k1\,0^{2k}\); that result is prior and is reproduced by the general formula. Kociumaka et al. (arXiv:1604.02238) give an unrestricted quadratic-order theorem, not a reported sharp constant for the two-marked-letter class. Their full text was not retrievable, so an unrecognized specialization remains a risk. The OEIS A262249 table is for the unrestricted maximum. published-finding corpus comparisons did not surface a stated equivalent claim, but this absence cannot establish absolute novelty.

## Value — PASS

Exactly two occurrences of one symbol form the smallest marked-letter class that can have quadratically many *distinct* Abelian-square factors; at most one marked letter allows only unary squares. An exact count for every placement, the best possible \(1/16\) density, and a unique asymptotic run-shape refine the classical construction: equal zero runs yield only \(1/18\). This is a natural complete sparse-class extremal classification, not a numerical census or a cosmetic rewriting of a known example. The absolute optimum for unrestricted binary words and exact finite-length two-marked maxima remain outside scope.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
