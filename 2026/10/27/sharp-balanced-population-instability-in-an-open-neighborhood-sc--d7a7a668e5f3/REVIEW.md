# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The critical cross-class exchange inequalities follow by following individual agents to their destinations. Both strict gains factor as \((k-h-1)(b-n-1)<0\) and \((k-h-1)(b-n+1)>0\); integrality forces \(b=n\), and the reverse swap gives the other side of the sharp balance window. Same-class exchanges never help; all feasible cases and endpoints are covered. The embedded checker enumerates every coloring for \(2\le n\le6\) against direct best-response comparisons, without treating enumeration as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to bibliographic risk
The two closest full-text sources separate the model's ingredients: arXiv:1909.02421 uses open neighborhoods with monotone preference, while arXiv:2204.12599 uses symmetric single-peaked preference with **closed** neighborhoods. Neither's result asserts the open-neighborhood balanced-population equilibrium census. Published semantic-search hits concerning majority graph coloring, bipartite graph homology, and other social games have different objects. Relevant previous research reports concern cycle graphs and vacant-vertex Schelling games; they do not cover this statement. An equivalent classification in unindexed literature remains possible, but no direct stronger implication was found in the inspected scientific material.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The finding is an all-parameter classification on an infinite, naturally symmetric family of interaction networks, and it detects an unusually sharp composition discontinuity: switching just one agent's color from exact balance makes *every* placement stable, while at exact balance the stable fraction vanishes asymptotically. The precise fact distinguishes open from closed neighborhoods in a behavioral model, rather than merely plugging in a number or applying a known universal symmetry theorem. Its exact counting formulas quantify the stability phase transition.

## Limits
Symmetric strictly single-peaked utility, two colors, equal bipartitions, and strictly jointly improving swaps are necessary restrictions; no general graph conclusion is implied. The algebraic proof is complete, but external priority risk is not completely eliminable by literature search. Independent validation remains outstanding.
