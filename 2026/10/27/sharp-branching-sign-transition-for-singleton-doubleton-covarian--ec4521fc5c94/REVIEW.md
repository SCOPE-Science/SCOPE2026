# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem follows from the event-by-event local covariance identity on arbitrary trees: an isolated vertex at either endpoint is incompatible with a two-vertex component, a vertex adjacent to exactly one endpoint receives the single forced-closed-edge correction, and other vertices use disjoint independent edge events. Substituting the two edge types yields the displayed polynomials. Strict monotonicity gives the star root; factorization gives the five-vertex path threshold; the inequality \(B_k(q)/q^2<-1+1/2+e^{-1}<0\) proves the all-parameter \(k\ge3\) result. Independent exact rational enumeration supports, but does not replace, the infinite-parameter proof.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence

The closest inspected published sources concern asymptotic giant-cluster sizes in random split trees (Berzunza–Holmgren, 2020) and rooted finite-graph component-size distributions (Allard et al., 2015); neither states or directly implies the sharp covariance sign classification between global singleton and doubleton counts under full arm subdivision. A published related covariance result considers total component count versus triangle count, while an accessible earlier result uses isolated vertices versus the number of all retained edges; neither resolves the present dependence. A historical priority risk from general enumeration methods or unlocated specialized work remains.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The cluster-size-one and cluster-size-two counts are the first nontrivial coordinates in the percolation component-size profile. An unsubdivided branching hub permits positive correlation at high retention, while a two-edge buffer on every arm makes positive correlation impossible as soon as a third arm is present. The five-vertex path is the exact exceptional transition case. The theorem identifies an all-parameter, structurally meaningful boundary rather than a numerical example or a fixed-order table entry.

## Limits

The classification covers stars, their uniform single-edge subdivisions, and the general local formula on any tree; it does not establish a sign theorem on all trees. The search cannot exclude every earlier equivalent derivation, and no independent validation is claimed.
