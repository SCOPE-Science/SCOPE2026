# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

With a uniform booster phase the stationary mean of clipped-linear susceptibility is F(x)=1-x/2 for 0<=x<=1 and F(x)=1/(2x) for x>=1, where x=tau r and F(0)=1. After normalization of the unvaccinated reproduction number, pure cross mixing has the off-diagonal entries F(x),F(y) and squared Perron root F(x)F(y). Put B=x+y=2w. For B<=1 the positive correction to the endpoint product is xy/4. For 1<B<2 the low–low segment has xy>=B-1 and hence product at least (3-B)/4, strictly above 1/(2B); on a mixed segment the derivative with respect to the smaller argument is (1-B/2)/(2(B-y)^2)>0, so only the endpoint attains 1/(2B). At B=2 the same mixed product cancels to 1/4 identically, including x=y=1. For B>2 the mixed segment minimizes at y=1 with value 1/[4(B-1)]>1/B²; when both arguments exceed or equal 1, maximizing xy yields the unique equal split and product 1/B². These checks verify all rates including zeros, both kinks and the displayed positive square roots. The archived exact rational grid was inspected, not rerun; it is redundant to the continuum argument. The result concerns a stationary disease-free invasion factor under the specified phase and contact assumptions, not whole-epidemic or empirical vaccine outcomes.

## originality

PASS

Foutel-Rodier–Charpentier–Guérin's framework contains the budget, immunity renewal average and spectral matrix, including pure cross mixing. Those are prior ingredients. Their fairness theorem is restricted to alpha<=1, while the proposed model is alpha=2. The distinct addition is the full constrained global lower envelope and its optimizer set for the clipped-linear deterministic case, not the spectral reduction or the general idea of a fairness reversal.

## value

PASS

This is a natural extreme-mixing benchmark just outside an established fairness theorem. The exact lower envelope separates the waning-saturation kink from the allocation reversal and identifies a complete interval of neutral splits at one specific budget. That distinction is useful as a check on numerical global allocation and qualitative extensions; the result does not recommend unequal allocation in real populations.
