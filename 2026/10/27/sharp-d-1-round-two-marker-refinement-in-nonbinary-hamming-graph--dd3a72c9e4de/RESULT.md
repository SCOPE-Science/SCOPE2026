# Exact two-marker color-refinement time in nonbinary Hamming graphs

## Finding

Let \(H(d,q)\) be the simple graph on \([q]^d\) in which two words are adjacent exactly when they differ in one coordinate, with \(d\ge1\) and \(q\ge3\). Color two distinct vertices \(A,B\) with separate singleton colors and give all remaining vertices one common color. Synchronous counting color refinement retains the old color and adjoins the multiset of neighbor colors; the initial partition has index zero. The first stable partition is reached at **round \(d-1\)**, for every marker separation \(1\le d_H(A,B)\le d\). In particular, the initial partition is already stable for \(d=1\).

## Proof

Put \(t=d_H(A,B)\), let \(I=\{i:A_i\ne B_i\}\), and let \(J=[d]\setminus I\). For a vertex \(x\), write \(a=|\{i\in I:x_i=A_i\}|\), \(b=|\{i\in I:x_i=B_i\}|\), and \(s=|\{i\in J:x_i\ne A_i\}|\). We use the orbit coordinates and simultaneous-distance-decrement invariant of the prior [two-root orbit-exactness result](https://github.com/Resultary/2026/blob/main/2026/10/18/SCOPE-two-root-counting-refinement-of-hamming-graphs-orbit-exactness-a--63c3d45d7be9/RESULT.md), Proof steps 1–3. Coordinate permutations within \(I\) and \(J\), with accompanying relabelings aligning the corresponding marked symbols, together with independent permutations of unmarked symbols, preserve the initial colors and act transitively on each feasible triple \((a,b,s)\). Thus no refinement round splits a triple. This is enough for the upper bound: a round that distinguishes all triples gives an equitable partition, since the triples are the orbits of this subgroup.

At round \(r\), the color of \(x\) determines its two distances to the marks, separately truncated at \(r+1\). At round zero it distinguishes each distance zero from all positive distances. Inductively, among vertices still at unresolved distance from a mark, a neighbor at the previous exact distance \(r\) characterizes those at distance \(r+1\). Retention of the old color and the neighbor multiset give the induction for both marks.

For \(d\ge2\), round \(d-1\) therefore gives the exact pair \(u=d_H(x,A)=t-a+s\), \(v=d_H(x,B)=t-b+s\). If either distance is \(d\), the bound \(s\le d-t\) forces \(s=d-t\). Otherwise the simultaneous-decrement target pair \((u-1,v-1)\) has entries at most \(d-2\). If an entry is negative there are no such neighbors; if both are nonnegative, the round-\((d-2)\) colors already identify the neighbors with that pair, since both entries are below the truncation threshold \(d-1\). Their multiplicity is exactly \(s\): the only qualifying move changes a coordinate in \(J\) from a nonmarked symbol to the common marked symbol, once for each such coordinate. In both cases the round-\((d-1)\) color determines \(s\), and then \(a=t+s-u\), \(b=t+s-v\). Thus this round is stable, without requiring an additional round after the full distance pair becomes available. For \(d=1\), the two singleton colors and the common color of the unmarked symbols already give equitable classes in the complete graph.

For the lower bound with \(d\ge2\), take \(x\) whose symbols differ from both markers in every coordinate (possible because \(q\ge3\)). Replace one coordinate in \(I\) by the symbol of \(A\) there, obtaining \(y\). The two distance pairs are \((d,d)\) and \((d-1,d)\), so the round-\((d-1)\) colors differ. Neither vertex sees a mark within distance \(d-2\). In a regular graph all vertices at distance greater than \(r\) from both marks have the same color at round \(r\): the assertion holds at round zero and each such vertex at the next round has itself and all its neighbors in the common previous-round color. Hence \(x,y\) agree at round \(d-2\). An equitable partition cannot split later, so the first stable round is exactly \(d-1\).

## Prior work and scope

The stable pointwise-stabilizer orbit partition and its class counts and sizes were already given in *Two-root counting refinement of Hamming graphs: orbit exactness and the metric-partition boundary* ([published result](https://github.com/Resultary/2026/blob/main/2026/10/18/SCOPE-two-root-counting-refinement-of-hamming-graphs-orbit-exactness-a--63c3d45d7be9/RESULT.md)). That result does not determine the stopping round from the original three colors; its one-round assertion concerns refinement **starting** from the full distance partition. Here the boundary case of a distance equal to \(d\) removes the potential extra round and the nonbinary background witness supplies the sharp lower bound. The formula is not a dimension-only law for binary graphs, as illustrated by the known [binary two-marker classification](https://github.com/Resultary/2026/blob/main/2026/10/25/SCOPE-exact-two-marker-colour-refinement-depth-on-boolean-hypercubes--37798fff7d40/RESULT.md): on \(H(3,2)\) with antipodal marks, shell colors at round one already form a stable partition. The theorem makes no claim about three or more markers, alternate initial colors, or cross-graph identification.
