# Scientific review: finite-chain partial-uphill cutoff

## Correctness — PASS

The statement specifies a three-state hard-core chain, admissible rates and reservoirs, and a particular finite-difference constitutive current including the two reservoir links. The published exact one-point closure, with the finite-chain Dirichlet boundary equations, decouples into an affine total-density mode and a hyperbolic colour-difference mode. Substitution gives the exact edge-current formula. The largest colour-difference increment is at both boundary links, and the monotone length functional has derivative of strictly positive sign. The resulting if-and-only-if length classification and sign-changing regime therefore follow for all finite sizes, without extrapolation. Exact rational results for 3 and 4 sites and independent solutions of the full stationary Markov generator for 1–4 sites confirm normalization. The 1,445 executed checks support, but are not substitutes for, the universal analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS (same mathematical model literature comparison; priority risk remains)

Casini, Giardinà and Vernia, arXiv:2210.01514v1, derive this microscopic generator and the mean closure and establish the qualitative finite-system existence of partial uphill currents and their hydrodynamic disappearance. Their continuum stationary solution is not the same as the finite-difference solution, and their main results do not state the sharp chain-size threshold, exact monotonicity, or all-link sign-change classification. The new result is explicitly derived from their closure, not presented as an independent model invention. Floreani and colleagues' earlier two-layer switching process proves global uphill transport and boundary-layer behavior in a different dynamics. A 2026 study by Casini and colleagues instead concerns weakly asymmetric exclusion and hydrodynamic drift-diffusion. Targeted literature searches for equivalent discrete-chain size thresholds found no dominating result. Search limitations and possible unnamed equivalences leave independent priority assessment necessary.

## Scientific value — PASS

The motivating finite-chain particle model exhibits partial uphill behavior that is lost in its macroscopic limit. Determining precisely how many sites can exhibit the countergradient effect across **every link** is a natural unresolved finite-size question. The result supplies a complete, sharp cutoff for a physically admissible anti-aligned gradient family, a rigorous monotonic size dependence, and a nontrivial transition from countergradient current throughout the chain to currents that reverse near both boundaries. The exact 3-to-4-site example is a reproducible witness, not the main claim. The mathematical extension is confined to a symmetric two-species system and to the defined constitutive species current, not arbitrary currents or microscopic tracer crossing counts.
