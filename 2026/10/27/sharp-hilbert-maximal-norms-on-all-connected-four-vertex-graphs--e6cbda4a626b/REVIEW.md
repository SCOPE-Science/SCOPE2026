# Scientific review: four-vertex centered maximal norms

## Correctness

PASS. The reduction from a pointwise maximum of graph-ball averages to the maximum singular value of finite nonnegative averaging matrices is exact for all real and complex inputs. The pure-Python rational certificate reconstructs all 385 row selections and proves strict positive definiteness for all 374 losing matrices via Sylvester's criterion, checks the eleven winning patterns and their exact characteristic polynomials. Perron eigenvectors establish the stated equality cases, with strict comparisons of distinct averaging balls ruling out unlisted maximizers. No extrapolation from finite tests is made: the object domain itself consists of the six four-vertex graphs.

## Originality

PASS within inspected evidence, subject to historical priority risk. Soria and Tradacete (arXiv:1410.6204v1, Example 2.6) explicitly compute the four-vertex \(\ell^1\) constants, not the \(\ell^2\) constants. González-Riquelme and Madrid (arXiv:2005.03146 and arXiv:2011.02630) address complete and star graphs; those two contributions are acknowledged and excluded from priority claims. Related published work on path-graph variation bounds concerns a different seminorm. Direct database and literature searches did not find the four-class Hilbert classification or its nonisomorphic coincidences. Such searches cannot establish global uniqueness.

## Value

PASS. This is a natural complete classification at the smallest graph order with six connected isomorphism types, extending the exact-constant program beyond complete and star graphs. The algebraic values, all maximizing data and permutation equivalences of winning matrices explaining the cross-graph equalities supply reproducible benchmarks for nonlinear maximal inequalities and show that equality of sharp Hilbert constants does not distinguish nonisomorphic graphs. The latter fact alone is not new at the \(\ell^1\) endpoint; the present value lies in the Hilbert classification and extremizers.

## Scientific risks and boundaries

The theorem does not classify larger graphs, arbitrary weights, or other exponents. Although highly relevant primary text was inspected, an equivalent unindexed calculation remains possible. The rational enumeration covers precisely the declared graph family; the supplied checker and paper proof are not an independent mathematical audit.
