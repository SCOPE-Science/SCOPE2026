# Sharp moment invisibility of first-branch size in random recursive trees

## Finding

In a uniformly random recursive tree on the increasing labels \(1,\ldots,n\), let \(B_n\) be the number of vertices descending from vertex \(2\), counting vertex \(2\), and let \(L_n\) be the number of outdegree-zero vertices. Thus a singleton tree has one leaf. For every \(n\ge2\), \(B_n\) is uniform on \(\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\).

**Exact conditional splitting law.** Let \(L_a'\) and \(L_b''\) be independent copies of the leaf counts in uniform recursive trees of sizes \(a\) and \(b\), with \(L_1=1\). For every \(1\le s<n\),
\[
  L_n\mid\{B_n=s\}\ \stackrel{d}=\ L_s'+L_{n-s}''-\mathbf 1_{\{n-s=1\}}.
\]
The subtracted term reflects that the original root is not a leaf even when its complementary induced tree consists only of that root.

**Sharp hierarchy of indistinguishable moments.** Let \(h\ge1\) be any integer, and suppose \(n\ge4h+2\) and \(2h+1\le s\le n-2h-1\). Then the conditional and unconditional distributions of \(L_n\) have *identical first \(2h\) ordinary moments and identical first \(2h\) cumulants*. More precisely, with \(\kappa_r\) denoting the \(r\)-th cumulant and \(\beta_r\) the conventional Bernoulli numbers \(\beta_2=1/6\), \(\beta_4=-1/30\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E[L_n\mid B_n=s]&=n/2,\\
 \kappa_r(L_n\mid B_n=s)&=\kappa_r(L_n)
    =\frac{n\beta_r}{r}\qquad(2\le r\le 2h).
 \end{aligned}
\]
For every \(h\ge1\) and every \(n\ge4h+1\), the boundary \(s=2h\) **does detect** the split at order \(2h\):
\[
 \kappa_{2h}(L_n\mid B_n=2h)-\kappa_{2h}(L_n)=-\beta_{2h}\ne0,
\]
although every lower-order cumulant (and hence every lower-order ordinary moment) agrees at this boundary. The mirrored boundary \(s=n-2h\) has the same defect. Thus the stated cutoff is sharp for even-order detection.

In particular, for \(n\ge6\), conditioning on any \(3\le s\le n-3\) leaves both \(\mathbb E L_n=n/2\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(L_n)=n/12\) unchanged. At \(s=2\), with \(n\ge5\), the conditional variance is lower by \(1/6\). Across the full range of branch sizes, the exact covariance is
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(B_n,L_n)=-\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)},\qquad n\ge2.
\]
These statements constitute one conditional-moment hierarchy, with the covariance as a boundary-effect consequence.

## Assumptions and scope

The tree is generated by independent parent choices: for each \(t=2,\ldots,n\), vertex \(t\) chooses one parent uniformly from \(\{1,\ldots,t-1\}\). Its root is vertex \(1\). A *descendant* includes the vertex itself; a *leaf* means a vertex of outdegree zero (and the one-vertex tree has one leaf). No asymptotics or scaling assumptions enter. The hierarchy applies to every finite integer \(h\ge1\) with the size bounds stated above. All cumulants exist because the state space is finite.

## Proof

**1. Uniform first-branch size and conditional factorization.** After vertex \(2\) attaches to vertex \(1\), color the descendants of \(2\) red and all remaining vertices blue. Each arrival chooses the color of its parent, so when the current sizes are \((a,b)\), the next color is red with probability \(a/(a+b)\). Starting at \((1,1)\), the elementary two-color Pólya-urn formula makes the final red size uniform on \(1,\ldots,n-1\).

Fix a particular possible set \(S\subseteq\{2,\ldots,n\}\) of descendants of vertex \(2\). Membership in \(S\) is equivalent to requiring that each parent pointer of a vertex in \(S\setminus\{2\}\) lies in \(S\) and that each parent pointer of a vertex outside \(S\cup\{1\}\) lies outside \(S\). Since the original parent pointers are independent and uniform, conditional on this event their restricted choices remain independent and uniform in the respective earlier-label groups. Thus the tree induced by \(S\) and the one induced by its complement are independent uniform recursive trees, after preserving their relative increasing-label orders. In the full tree the only connecting edge is \(1\to2\). Their leaf totals add, except that the complementary singleton root (when \(n-s=1\)) is not a leaf of the full tree. Averaging over all admissible \(S\) proves the asserted conditional distribution.

**2. A finite Eulerian leaf law.** In a recursive tree of size \(d\ge2\), adding a vertex either replaces one existing leaf by another, or increases the leaf total by one. Among the \(d-1\) possible parents, exactly the current number of leaves are leaves. The resulting recurrence is the ordinary Eulerian recurrence. Consequently, if \(D_m\) denotes the number of descents of a uniformly random permutation of size \(m\), then
\[
   L_d\stackrel d=1+D_{d-1},\qquad d\ge2.
\]
Write \(A_m(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\left\langle {m\atop k}\right\rangle z^k\). The standard Eulerian generating identity, which also follows by induction from the recurrence, is
\[
 \sum_{j\ge1}j^m z^j=\frac{zA_m(z)}{(1-z)^{m+1}},\qquad |z|<1.
\]
For \(u>0\), differentiation of the geometric series gives
\[
 \sum_{j\ge1}j^m e^{-ju}=\frac{m!}{u^{m+1}}+O(1)
 \qquad (u\downarrow0),
\]
because \(1/(e^u-1)-u^{-1}\) is analytic near zero. Inserting this into the Eulerian identity with \(z=e^{-u}\), and multiplying by \(e^{-u}\) to pass from \(D_{d-1}\) to \(L_d\), yields the formal Taylor expansion
\[
 \log\mathbb E e^{t L_d}
   =d\log\!\left(\frac{e^t-1}{t}\right)+O(t^d)
   \quad (t\to0),\qquad d\ge2.
\]
The expression at \(t=0\) is defined by continuity. The remainder is analytic of order at least \(d\); hence derivatives of orders below \(d\) agree exactly. With the Bernoulli expansion
\[
 \log\!\left(\frac{e^t-1}{t}\right)
     =\frac t2+\sum_{r\ge2}\frac{\beta_r}{r\,r!}t^r,
\]
we obtain \(\kappa_1(L_d)=d/2\), and
\[
 \kappa_r(L_d)=\frac{d\beta_r}{r}\qquad(2\le r<d).
\]
Odd Bernoulli numbers beyond the first vanish. This derivation is exact for every admissible finite order; it uses no large-size limit.

**3. Boundary coefficient.** For even \(r\ge2\), the more precise Laurent expansion
\[
 \sum_{j\ge1}j^{r-1}e^{-ju}
   =\frac{(r-1)!}{u^r}-\frac{\beta_r}{r}+O(u)
\]
follows from the same convergent expansion of \(1/(e^u-1)\), by differentiating \(r-1\) times. For \(d=r\), the corresponding cumulant generating function therefore has a first omitted term \(-\beta_r t^r/r!\). It follows that
\[
  \kappa_r(L_r)=\frac{r\beta_r}{r}-\beta_r=0.
\]
This identity is used only at even orders; it does not assert vanishing of arbitrary cumulants of a finite Eulerian law.

**4. Moment matching and sharpness.** When \(s,n-s\ge2\), the conditional sum has independent summands and no root correction. Cumulants add. For every \(1\le j\le 2h\), if \(s,n-s\ge2h+1\), each summand has its cumulant formula from Step 2. Their sizes sum to \(n\), yielding exactly the unconditional cumulant \(n\beta_j/j\) for \(j\ge2\) and mean \(n/2\) for \(j=1\). The universal polynomial relations between moments and cumulants give equality of the first \(2h\) ordinary moments.

At \(s=2h\) and \(n-s\ge2h+1\), Steps 2 and 3 show the \(2h\)-th cumulant difference is \(-\beta_{2h}\), while every lower-order cumulant agrees. Since \(\beta_{2h}\ne0\), this proves sharpness. The law is symmetric under exchanging the two non-singleton block sizes, proving the mirrored statement.

Finally, for \(n\ge3\), the conditional mean is \(n/2\) at \(2\le s\le n-2\), is \((n+1)/2\) at \(s=1\), and is \((n-1)/2\) at \(s=n-1\). Multiplying by the uniform branch-size weights and subtracting \(\mathbb E B_n\,\mathbb E L_n=n^2/4\) gives \(\operatorname{Cov}(B_n,L_n)=-(n-2)/(2(n-1))\). At \(n=2\), the branch size is deterministic and the same formula gives zero. This completes the proof.

## Verification

The included standalone `verification.py` uses only the standard Python library and can be executed with `python3 verification.py`. It independently enumerates every labeled increasing-parent tree through nine vertices, checks conditional branch-size and leaf-count laws against Eulerian convolutions, computes exact rational cumulants, verifies all admissible moment identities on a finite grid, and tests the sharp even-order boundary. The executed script reported **6,586 passing exact rational assertions**, including independent exhaustive parent-pointer enumeration through size nine. These checks are not offered as a proof for all sizes; Steps 1–4 are the infinite-parameter proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Goldschmidt and Martin [1, Section 2.2] explicitly relate recursive-tree root branches to tables/cycles in a uniform random permutation. That establishes the classical branch representation used in Step 1, but does not give leaf-count laws conditional on a specified branch size or cumulant-matching cutoffs. Bryc, Minda, and Sethuraman [2, Sections 1 and 1.2] treat the number of leaves as a Markov chain and derive asymptotic large-deviation and normal results; they do not condition on vertex \(2\)'s subtree size or establish exact finite-order moment matching. A published finite covariance result [3] concerns **root degree** and total leaves, not the **size of vertex \(2\)'s descendant subtree** or conditional cumulants. The positive root-degree/leaf covariance there is not the negative branch-size/leaf covariance here.

The elementary ingredients (Pólya splitting and Eulerian numbers) are classical. The asserted new synthesis is the exact, *arbitrarily high finite order* cutoff for how many leaf-count moments can reveal a distinguished root-branch size, including the sharp nonzero Bernoulli defect at the first detectable even order. An earlier formulation of this synthesis could exist under permutation-descents or random-split-tree language; the priority conclusion is restricted to the accessible comparisons stated here.

## Limitations

Only the uniform parent-attachment recursive-tree model is covered. The result matches finitely many conditional moments, **not** full conditional distributions or independence of \(B_n\) and \(L_n\). Full distributions change with the split. The theorem makes no claim for preferential-attachment models, plane-oriented recursive trees, or conditioning simultaneously on several branches. The moment cutoff is proved sharp at even orders; no statement of a sharp odd-order detection threshold is made. Literature priority may be affected by older work on permutation descent cumulants or recursive-tree split-tree decompositions not identified in the checked sources. The verification code supplies finite checks only.
