# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The sharp formula follows from a full three-function nodal Gram kernel (determinant \(a(1-a)/36\)), exact endpoint/knot absolute row integrals, and separate convexity in each output subinterval. The sign witnesses and the probability-range defect use \(\int K_a(x,y)\,dy=1\), not a numerical approximation. The boundary strong-convergence argument uses the rank-one residual of the shrinking hat function. The standalone checker replays exact symbolic identities and 1485 rational row comparisons. Real-knot quantifiers rest on the proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence
Shadrin's general theorem bounds all order-two spline orthoprojectors by the known uniform sharp constant \(3\), not the exact two-cell profile. Passenbrunner--Shadrin treat convergence and kernel decay, neither of which implies this profile. The closest previously available spline findings treat infinite grids, knot insertion on a full line, or periodic meshes, with different boundary conditions and no implication to a three-node finite-interval projector. A directly relevant older piecewise-linear-projections article was identified but its full text was inaccessible, leaving priority risk. An equivalent elementary calculation may exist under a different term such as a three-hat Lebesgue function.

## Value — PASS
Exact amplification and the \(0\)-to-\(1\) positivity defect are intrinsic robustness invariants for the smallest nontrivial fitted linear spline. The uniquely optimal knot location and the sharp unattained boundary value quantify the effect of mesh skewness. Particularly useful is the exact incompatibility between strong \(L^2\) convergence under boundary knot collapse and discontinuity of sharp maximum-norm constants. The result provides a tractable sharp test problem within spline approximation; it does not claim to solve the general multiknot problem.
