# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The cycle constraint is exactly the hyperplane of path-constant flows with sum zero; any projection can be averaged over edge-coordinate and equal-arm permutations without enlarging its real edge-ℓ1 norm. The resulting linear map has the necessary and sufficient form \(s\mapsto s-w\sum_i s_i\), with one real free parameter. Direct column sums are computed for both edge classes, the minimax problem is solved on all of \(\mathbb R\), including the two outer intervals, and the phase-boundary equality is included. A distinct weighted-Euclidean least-squares argument constructs the canonical graph Hodge projector. Substitution gives the sharp unbounded ratio. Independent exact-rational tests of 28 small graphs check the projector matrices, range, idempotence and Gram reconstruction; they supplement rather than establish the infinite theorem.

## Originality — PASS, residual bibliographic risk
Inspected primary full texts: Dilworth–Kutzarova–Ostrovskii 2023 (arXiv:2305.12582v1, sections 1.1, 1.4 and 2) treats averaging, invariant projections and other graph families, and their 2018 work (arXiv:1807.03814v1, sections 1 and 5–6) treats diamond graph constructions including symmetric equal arms. Neither inspected material implies a two-length sharp phase transition or the unbounded separation between the canonical Euclidean graph projector and the best edge-ℓ1 cycle projector. Older Blatter–Cheney 1974 and Shekhtman–Skrzypek 2014 hyperplane projection research supplies potentially broader background; their full papers were not inspected, and a general hyperplane theorem may recover the minimax portion. The genuinely compared claim is the whole graph incidence reduction, parameter transition, and ratio in simple unweighted planar graphs, not priority over generic hyperplane methods. Semantic research-database queries also returned cactus basis constants, unrelated theta-graph gnarledness and other distinct projection problems, not an implication of this theorem. Neither failed search nor a graph-category partition proves uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
The result gives a sharp global minimum over *all* real projections, not just graph-invariant or orthogonal projections, for a natural two-terminal graph family. A genuine minimizer phase change explains why long subdivisions concentrate the optimal correction into the short branch. The resulting ratio grows as \(r/2\), proving canonical graph Hodge projections can be arbitrarily inefficient relative to the true edge-ℓ1 minimum even for simple planar series-parallel graphs. It is a structural infinite-family separation rather than isolated constants or routine reparameterization.

The broader 1974 and 2014 minimal-hyperplane works must be checked in a subsequent independent literature audit to settle residual priority risk, and their unseen contents are not characterized as excluding this claim. This result is limited to cycle projections on the specified graph family and the real edge-ℓ1 norm.
