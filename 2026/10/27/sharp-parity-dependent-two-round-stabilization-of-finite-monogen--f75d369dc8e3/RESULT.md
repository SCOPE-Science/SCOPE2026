# Sharp parity-dependent two-round stabilization of finite monogenic semigroups

## Finding

For integers \(m,p\ge1\), let \(S_{m,p}\) be the finite monogenic semigroup generated by \(a\), with elements \(a,a^2,\ldots,a^{m+p-1}\), where \(a^{m+p}=a^m\), and with no earlier repetition. Regard it as a relational structure in the language consisting of equality and the **ternary** relation \(R(u,v,w)\) meaning \(uv=w\). The generator is **not** named; there is no identity constant or binary multiplication function symbol.

Fix the eventual period \(p\), and write \(L=\operatorname{lcm}(p,2)\). The **exact** stabilization threshold for first-order quantifier rank two is

\[
T(p)=2L+1=\begin{cases}4p+1,&p\text{ odd},\\2p+1,&p\text{ even}.\end{cases}
\]

For all \(m,n\ge T(p)\), the semigroups \(S_{m,p}\) and \(S_{n,p}\) satisfy exactly the same first-order sentences of quantifier rank at most two. Moreover, if \(m<T(p)\le n\), the two semigroups are distinguishable by a **single rank-two sentence**, given explicitly below. Thus \(S_{T(p),p}\) is the smallest member of the stabilized rank-two class **within the fixed-period family**, with exactly \(2L+p\) elements. This claim does not classify pairs with distinct periods, or the equivalence classes entirely below the cutoff.

## Assumptions and scope

All indices and periods are positive integers; the period \(p\) is fixed when comparing indices. The operation in exponent coordinates \(1,\ldots,m+p-1\) is

\[
 u\cdot v=\rho_{m,p}(u+v),\qquad
 \rho_{m,p}(t)=\begin{cases}t,&t<m,\\m+((t-m)\bmod p),&t\ge m.\end{cases}
\]

The transient part is \(\{1,\ldots,m-1\}\); the periodic tail is \(\{m,\ldots,m+p-1\}\). Equality of two tail elements is equality of their exponents modulo \(p\). The tail is a cyclic group of order \(p\), with its unique idempotent represented by the exponent divisible by \(p\).

The relation symbol \(R\) is interpreted by the multiplication graph, not as a function. Quantifier rank is maximum nesting depth, so conjunctions of separate one-quantifier conditions within one outer existential have rank two.

## Proof

**1. Reduction of the entire rank-two theory to pointed extension profiles.** For ordered elements \(x,y\), let \(D(x,y)\) be their complete atomic diagram: equality, and the truth values of every substitution from \(\{x,y\}\) into \(R\). Set

\[
 E(x)=\{D(x,y):y\in S_{m,p}\},\qquad
 \mathcal E(S_{m,p})=\{E(x):x\in S_{m,p}\}.
\]

Two structures in this fixed finite relational language agree on all rank-two sentences if and only if their sets \(\mathcal E\) coincide. Indeed, in the two-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game, an initial choice \(x\) can be answered by \(x'\) exactly when the sets of atomic diagrams of the possible second moves agree, in both directions. Conversely, equality of these sets supplies Duplicator's two-move strategy. No multiplicities need to be preserved beyond equality, which is part of \(D\).

**2. The transient pointed profiles.** Suppose \(1\le x<m\). For a transient second element \(1\le y<m\), neither element is idempotent and the product \(xy\) cannot equal either factor. The only special relations are \(x=y\), \(x^2=y\) (which requires \(2x<m\) and \(y=2x\)), and \(y^2=x\) (which requires \(x\) even and \(y=x/2\)). Every other transient \(y\) gives the generic diagram with all these relations false. Whenever \(m\ge5\), such a generic \(y\) exists: there are at least four transient elements and at most three excluded choices \(x,2x,x/2\).

For a tail second element \(y\ge m\), we always have \(x\ne y\), \(y^2\ne x\), and \(xy\ne x\). The remaining possible special relations are exactly

\[
 y^2=y\iff y\equiv0\pmod p,\quad
 xy=y\iff x\equiv0\pmod p,\quad
 x^2=y\iff 2x\ge m\ \text{and}\ y\equiv2x\pmod p.
\]

Every residue of \(y\bmod p\) occurs exactly once in the tail. Therefore, once \(m\ge5\), the full profile \(E(x)\) of a transient \(x\) depends only on

\[
 (x\bmod p,\ x\bmod2,\ \mathbf1_{2x<m}),
\]

and on the fixed period \(p\), not otherwise on \(m\) or \(x\).

**3. The tail pointed profiles.** Suppose \(x\ge m\). If \(y\) also lies in the tail, all atomic relationships among \(x,y\) depend only on their residues modulo \(p\). In particular, \(x=y\) iff their residues coincide, \(x^2=y\) iff \(2x\equiv y\pmod p\), \(y^2=x\) iff \(2y\equiv x\pmod p\), and \(xy=x\) or \(xy=y\) iff \(y\equiv0\pmod p\) or \(x\equiv0\pmod p\), respectively.

For transient \(y\), the relations \(x=y\), \(x^2=y\), and \(xy=y\) are false, as is idempotence of \(y\). The two possibly true mixed relations are

\[
 xy=x\iff y\equiv0\pmod p,\qquad
 y^2=x\iff 2y\ge m\ \text{and}\ 2y\equiv x\pmod p.
\]

Split the transient range into

\[
 I_{\mathrm{low}}=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor(m-1)/2\rfloor\},\qquad
 I_{\mathrm{high}}=\{\lceil m/2\rceil,\ldots,m-1\}.
\]

For \(m\ge2L+1\), each interval has at least \(L\) consecutive integers and thus contains a complete set of residues modulo \(p\). In the low interval \(2y<m\); in the high interval \(2y\ge m\). Consequently every possible mixed diagram depends only on \(x\bmod p\), and is realized for every \(m\ge2L+1\). Thus the tail profile \(E(x)\) depends only on \(x\bmod p\).

**4. Uniform realization of all pointed profiles.** Each of the low and high intervals above contains at least \(L=\operatorname{lcm}(p,2)\) consecutive integers. Therefore each contains every residue modulo \(L\). These residue classes simultaneously encode \(x\bmod p\) and parity. All transient profile parameters identified in step 2 occur in both halves for every \(m\ge2L+1\); all \(p\) tail residues also occur. By steps 2 and 3, the entire set \(\mathcal E(S_{m,p})\) is independent of \(m\) in this range. Step 1 proves rank-two equivalence throughout the range.

**5. A sharp rank-two sentence.** Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi:=\exists x\bigl[&\neg R(x,x,x)
\land\exists y\,R(y,y,x)\\
&\land\exists z\,(R(z,z,z)\land R(x,z,z))\\
&\land\exists w\,(R(x,x,w)\land\neg R(w,w,w))\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\]

The three inner existential quantifiers occur in separate conjuncts, so \(\Phi\) has quantifier rank exactly two. An \(x\) satisfying the formula cannot belong to the periodic tail: the only idempotent tail element is its group identity, and \(xz=z\) for that idempotent forces a tail \(x\) to be idempotent, contradicting the first conjunct. Thus \(x<m\). The square-root conjunct forces \(x\) to be even, the absorbing-idempotent conjunct forces \(p\mid x\), and the non-idempotent-square conjunct forces \(2x<m\) (since a tail square of a multiple of \(p\) is the idempotent). Conversely, any positive even multiple of \(p\) with \(2x<m\) satisfies all four requirements, with the unique tail idempotent as \(z\). The smallest positive even multiple of \(p\) is \(L\). Hence

\[
 S_{m,p}\models\Phi\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m>2L
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m\ge T(p).
\]

This separates every smaller index from the stabilized class, proving the sharp threshold and smallest representative size.

## Verification

The included `verify_mono.py` constructs finite multiplication tables directly from exponent reduction, enumerates **every** atomic substitution on two selected elements, builds the exact two-round extension profiles, and checks their equality at different indices. It separately evaluates \(\Phi\) by direct existential search. The supplied `verify_output.txt` records successful tests for periods \(1\) through \(30\), including 1,500 direct threshold cases, four post-threshold samples per period, signature saturation checks, and independent two-move back-and-forth checks. Run `python3 verify_mono.py` from the extracted package directory. These finite checks test the proof but do not replace the argument covering arbitrary \(p\) and \(m\).

## Relationship to prior work

Finite monogenic semigroups' index-period decomposition and eventual cyclic group are standard. Cabessa's thesis, Section 9.3.4 and Lemma 9.15, spells out the index-period decomposition, the terminal subgroup, and a distinct invariant for finite cyclic \(\omega\)-semigroups. Distler's thesis studies classification and enumeration of finite semigroups. He's work develops games for quantifier hierarchies over finite structures. None of the compared statements implies the displayed **rank-two first-order** threshold for these relational semigroups. In particular, classification of semigroups up to isomorphism does not determine the collapse under two rounds, and a hierarchy for \(\omega\)-semigroup Wadge degrees does not determine this elementary-equivalence question.

## Limitations

The theorem fixes the eventual period and varies only the index. It does not classify equivalences between different periods or all equivalences below the threshold. It is specific to the ternary relational multiplication language without constants; naming the generator, adding an identity, or replacing the relation with a multiplication function changes quantifier rank. The computations are finite checks only. Equivalence with an unindexed historical result cannot be categorically excluded.
