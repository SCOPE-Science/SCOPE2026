# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact logarithmic-odds equilibrium identity reduces the maximum contrast problem to a uniquely minimized secant of the logit function; the minimizing interval is centered at one half. A scalar Dini-derivative comparison proves the all-stimulus contraction threshold and the sharp critical \(t^{-1/2}\) envelope, with an invariant centered symmetric example realizing the coefficient. Direct Jacobian diagonalization and exact stimulus subtraction establish the stability and inverse formulas. The standalone verifier independently recomputes diverse specializations. The results state all admissibility and nonhyperbolic limitations.

**Originality — PASS (self-review, provisional).** The primary full-text article arXiv:2609.10580v1 analyzes logistic examples and symmetric two-block cusps but not the asymmetric, field-optimized sharp difference cap and two-stimulus inverse problem. A previously established symmetric tanh reduction does cover the symmetric fixed-stimulus subcase; this is acknowledged rather than claimed anew. Searched threshold-network records and related scalar/mean-field references disclose no broader implication-equivalent theorem. Some portion might still be recoverable from general coupled-sigmoid theory, an explicit historical-priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** The optimization answers the natural measurable question of how far two interacting communities can diverge as the common input is varied, sharply distinguishes attainable stationary polarization from local stability, and makes the two extremizing stimulus locations sufficient to recover directed cross-community asymmetry. The critical contrast bound also describes the otherwise missing nonhyperbolic boundary. This is a structural classification, not a merely renamed fixed-point calculation.

The limitations include possible saddle-type maximizers, no finite-noise statistical inverse theorem, and no claim of global convergence of both activity coordinates to a unique equilibrium.
