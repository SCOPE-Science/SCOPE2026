# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is limited to two delta absorbers at \(-a\) and \(2a\) with reset-to-origin diffusion. The killed-free-excursion resolvent has the specified two-by-two linear system and strictly positive determinant; its exact solution gives the capture-odds ratio. Renewal makes the one-excursion flux ratio equal to the ultimate capture ratio. Rearrangement yields the scalar majority envelope. Its Taylor coefficient has the sign of \(B-1\); the strictly negative affine test \(K(B,t)\) for \(B\le1\) uses an exact hyperbolic inequality. A strict rational-strength witness has two crossings. The zero-reset endpoint and finite-capture conditions are justified separately. The executable checker reconstructs the matrix solve independently; finite checks do not establish the universal theorem.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk
The exact forward two-defect Green-function framework is **already** in Giuggioli–Gupta–Chase (2019), Sec. 3 and Appendix B. The claim does not attribute novelty to the underlying ratio or generic reset renewal. Their Sec. 5 deals with two perfectly absorbing targets and does not present an iff criterion for reentrant capture-majority selection for two unequal partial sinks. The asserted additional mathematical fact is precisely the global sharp reactive threshold \(B>1\) and the full open \(A\)-window, proved by a nonlocal envelope inequality. Schumm–Bressloff (2021) treats extended partially absorbing regions and general competition, not this threshold. The current comparison finds no stronger prior statement implying the iff boundary without new inequality arguments, but incomplete indexing and specialized prior publications remain a priority risk.

## Value — PASS
Which imperfect target ultimately captures a diffusive searcher is a natural transport selectivity observable. The result exhibits and precisely classifies a three-regime majority reversal even though the nearer target dominates at both vanishing and very rapid resetting. The sharp \(B=1\) feasibility threshold and exact \(A\)-interval provide a structural phase diagram, rather than an arbitrary numerical witness or a routine evaluation of a known forward formula.

## Closest literature and limits
The 2019 two-defect paper already provides the forward linear-algebraic toolkit; only the sharp reentrant phase theorem is asserted as additional. In the 2018 interval paper only abstract-level comparison was feasible; no claim of full-text exclusion is made. More specialized sources could have been missed. No uniqueness or exact number of crossings is claimed; numerical tests verify samples, not the continuum.
