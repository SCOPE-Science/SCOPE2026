# Review of the sharp subnormal tail-weight envelope

## Correctness — PASS

The Berger-measure moment identities reduce arbitrary positive subnormal weighted shifts and any starting index to a probability measure with prescribed first two moments on the unit interval. The explicitly constructed two-atom comparator matches those moments. The nonnegative quadratic Hermite certificate proves the sharp upper ratio at every subsequent index, and inverse tilting supplies actual equality examples. Endpoint and near-flatness calculations are treated separately. The exact-rational checker is supplementary; the proof uses no finite enumeration as an infinite certification.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The directly inspected December 2025 full open-access propagation paper establishes qualitative equality propagation and matrix-valued decomposition, not a cap-conditioned sharp scalar future-weight envelope. The December 2025 open-access signed-charge paper focuses on positive and signed representing measures and related classifications, without the stated ratio extremal criterion. The inspected abstract and substantive introductory excerpt of Lee–Yoon 2016 address two-atomic completions and two-variable recursion, but no universal first-two-weight-plus-cap extremal upper bound was visible. Semantically similar published mathematical-index findings concern different weighted-shift geometry or norm problems. A complete inspection of the 2016 paper and exhaustive classical truncated-moment literature was not achieved; equivalent earlier formulations remain a priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a natural quantitative analogue of flatness propagation: it classifies all possible *upper* tail behavior under two directly observable neighboring weights and a norm cap, sharply at every horizon. Its extremal examples show that small local variation does not force uniformly small distant variation; the exact logarithmic crossover is a structurally interpretable effect, rather than an arbitrary finite computation. This supplies rigorous sharp bounds and extremizers for a standard infinite-dimensional operator class.
