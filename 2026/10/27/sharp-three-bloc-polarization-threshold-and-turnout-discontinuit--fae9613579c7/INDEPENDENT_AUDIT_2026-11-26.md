# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For the unit-mass electorate, expected vote totals per unit mass and shares of all eligible voters coincide; neither is share of votes cast nor winning probability. The two endpoint blocs jointly contribute e|x−y|^q to each candidate's own payoff. On each half of the action interval the center-bloc contribution is a positive-part affine function raised to power q; convexity and strict convexity of the endpoint term for q>1 exclude all non-anchor best replies. Comparing the center and distant endpoint against opponent 0 gives (1−e)2^(−q) versus e, hence the exact threshold. For q=1 and 0<y<1/2, the slopes in increasing action order on [0,y], [y,1/2], [1/2,1−y], [1−y,1] are −e, 1−e, 3e−1, e. Action 0 is worse than 1, and if e=1/3 the flat middle-right interval is also strictly worse than action 1 for y>0. Thus no non-anchor best reply occurs against an interior opponent. Against 0 the only flat maximizing segment is [1/2,1] at e=1/3, and for any z in that segment 0 is a best reply to z (by reflection and the strict payoff advantage y/3 against an opponent y=1−z in (0,1/2)). At y=1/2 both endpoints maximize. This proves the linear-case continuum and absence of further equilibria without relying on a grid. The turnout calculations follow from advantages 1/2 for all blocs in half-center profiles, 1/0/1 in extremes, and z/1−z/z in the critical segment (0,z). The source's verifier and verification notes were inspected, not executed; the finite tests have the stated grid/integer-q scope only.
