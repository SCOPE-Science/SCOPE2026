# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The explicit maximal formulas follow from the radius-zero, radius-one, and radius-two graph balls. Translation equivariance on the nonnegative cone makes the entire two-input variation problem an exactly equivalent weighted \(\ell^1\) Lipschitz problem for an edge-gradient map on all of \(\mathbb R^m\). Each affine selector's weighted column sum has the stated upper bound, including at ties by continuity, and a strictly positive two-signal pair lies inside a cell realizing that column norm. The two-vertex exception and the signed-domain obstruction have explicit formulas. The reproducibility script performs exact arithmetic, but the all-parameter proof is analytic.

## Originality

PASS, with residual historical risk. The earlier finite-star maximal-operator literature determines \(\ell^p\) one-input norms and \(p\)-variation ratios of one output to one input. The closest one-input result is \(\operatorname{Var}_1(Mf)\le m/(m+1)\operatorname{Var}_1(f)\) with equality, whereas the new two-data equal-weight constant is \(2m/(m+1)\). The earlier inequalities do not imply a seminorm inequality for differences of two *nonlinear* outputs. The arbitrary weighted-edge formula, its attaining perturbations, and the unique optimal weighting were not present in the inspected primary mathematical statements. Unindexed equivalent treatments cannot be excluded.

## Value

PASS. This gives an exact sharp perturbation modulus for a standard nonlinear graph averaging operator, not a recomputation of its known one-input variation constant. The theorem identifies an unavoidable two-data expansion on every genuine star, uniqueness of the balanced-variation design, unbounded sensitivity to a vanishing edge weight, and the boundary at which allowing signed inputs makes the seminorm problem ill-posed. These structural comparisons motivate the precise weighted formula.

## Literature limits

The mathematical content of arXiv:1410.6204, arXiv:2005.03146, and Section 4 of arXiv:2011.02630 was compared. Later related directed-graph variation work was accessible only at abstract level. A general theorem outside these sources may contain an implicit equivalent; this has not been independently audited.
