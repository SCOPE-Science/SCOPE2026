# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem quantifies over every integer \(q\ge3\) and every real \(t>0\), with unit internal weights and two distinct noise-free leaders. The seven orbit classes exhaust all unordered pairs. A block-orthogonal decomposition yields exact rational grounded-inverse traces; five comparisons have strictly positive factorizations for the full domain, while the sixth has one crossing at \(t=(q+2)/(q-2)\). The supplied exact-arithmetic program independently performs symbolic block inversion and direct rational grounded inversions for sample cases; finite checks are not treated as an infinite proof. The variance normalization is explicitly one-half the inverse-trace objective.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Full accessible manuscript sections of Lin–Fardad–Jovanović (2013), Fitch–Leonard (2014), Patterson–McGlohon–Dyagilev (2014), and Patterson–Yi–Zhang (2017) were compared at the level of objective, graph family and implication. They give general leader-placement methods, and explicit results for paths, cycles or trees, but the inspected statements do not directly supply the bridge-barbell pair classification or sharp threshold. Searches for barbell/clique-bridge coupling and equivalent grounded-Laplacian objectives, including mathematical research records, found no covering statement. This is not a proof of historical uniqueness: broader formulas may make the calculation accessible, and specialized work outside the accessible indexed material could cover it.

## Value — PASS
Two communities linked by a single adjustable tie provide a natural benchmark for stochastic leader placement. The theorem gives a complete, non-asymptotic placement phase diagram for every large-community size, a unique exact conductance switch and all tie multiplicities. It also shows a counterintuitive robust feature: the small-clique gateway is never selected, while strengthening the bridge eventually forces the large-clique gateway into every optimum. The exact size/coupling threshold is useful for checking algorithms beyond merely calculating one instance.

## Limits and closest literature
The graph contains one two-vertex clique, one homogeneous larger clique and a single weighted bridge. Different noise, extra links, or arbitrary two-clique orders remain outside scope. Closest primary source: Lin–Fardad–Jovanović arXiv:1302.0450 (general optimization), with Fitch–Leonard arXiv:1407.1569 and Patterson–Yi–Zhang arXiv:1708.06873 (general resistance/joint-centrality methods and special graph classes). General algorithmic solvability is credited, not claimed to be new.
