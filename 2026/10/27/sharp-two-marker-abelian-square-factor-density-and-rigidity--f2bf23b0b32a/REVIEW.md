## Originality — PASS, with priority limitations
Two closely related primary texts were inspected in their available full-text presentation, not merely titles or abstracts. Kociumaka–Radoszewski–Rytter–Waleń, *Maximum Number of Distinct and Nonequivalent Nonstandard Squares in a Word* (DLT 2014; arXiv:1604.02238), Section 2, prove an unrestricted \(\Theta(n^2)\) bound using a two-marker example; this does not imply the exact all-gap formula, sharp fixed-weight-two leading coefficient, or asymptotic optimizer rigidity. Fici–Mignosi, *Words with the Maximum Number of Abelian Squares* (2015; arXiv:1506.03562), Proposition 1, prove quadratic existence using \(0^t1\,0^t1\,0^t\). That family has a fixed-weight-two coefficient \(1/18\), below the sharp \(1/16\) here. General full-alphabet finite maxima, e.g. OEIS A262249, optimize over a larger domain and do not imply the constrained result. Searches for the exact three-gap/marker claim and its equivalent asymptotic proportions did not reveal a dominating assertion in the checked material; search absence is not proof of priority. Residual risks include uncatalogued finite-weight literature and later papers or supplementary results not inspected in full.

## Value — PASS
This exact constrained extremum fills a natural gap directly adjacent to the sparse two-marker construction used in the foundational Abelian-square literature. It sharpens that construction to the optimal normalized constant, gives an exact formula for all degeneracies, and identifies the only asymptotically competitive placement of the two markers. The point is the **sharp coefficient and rigidity**, not the previously known observation that two markers already permit quadratic growth. No optimality over unrestricted binary words is claimed.
