# Sharp two-seat exactness of local distinctive representation and a three-seat interval obstruction

## Finding
In an approval-based committee election with at least one voter, let \(k\) be the number of seats and \(q=\lceil n/k\rceil\). Distinctive representation (DR) means justified representation (JR) plus exactly \(q\) approving voters for every elected candidate. Local DR is the exchange axiom of Chingoma, Grossi, Lindeboom, and Maly (Definition 27 in the cited source).

**Theorem.** If \(k\le 2\) and a DR committee exists, every local-DR committee is DR. More constructively, from any JR committee with \(k=2\), a DR committee can be reached in at most two strictly improving, JR-preserving, admissible single-candidate exchanges. For \(k=1\), at most one exchange suffices.

The seat threshold is sharp. With \(k=3\), \(n=3\), and five linearly ordered candidates \(a,b,c,d,e\), take the three approval ballots \(\{a,b\}\), \(\{b,c\}\), and \(\{d,e\}\). Every ballot is a candidate interval. The committee \(W=\{b,d,e\}\) satisfies local DR but not DR, while \(D=\{a,c,d\}\) satisfies DR. Five candidates are necessary for this separation with three seats: whenever \(m\le4\), feasibility of DR implies that every local-DR three-seat committee is DR.

## Assumptions and scope
There are integers \(n\ge1\), \(m\ge k\ge1\), a candidate set \(C\) of size \(m\), and arbitrary approval sets \(A_i\subseteq C\), including potentially empty ballots. Let \(S(c)=\{i:c\in A_i\}\) and \(s(c)=|S(c)|\). A voter is represented by \(W\) if their ballot intersects \(W\). JR requires that no set of at least \(n/k\) unrepresented voters jointly approves any candidate. Equivalently, with \(U(W)\) the represented voters, \(W\) satisfies JR precisely when \(|S(c)\setminus U(W)|<q\) for all \(c\in C\).

A swap \(W\rightsquigarrow (W\setminus\{c\})\cup\{d\}\), with \(c\in W\), \(d\notin W\), is **improving and admissible** when the new committee is JR, \(|s(d)-q|<|s(c)-q|\), and the source's exclusion does not apply: it is not the case that \(s(c)\ge n/k\) and \(s(d)<n/k\). Local DR means JR with no improving admissible swap. All constructive swaps below bring in a candidate with support exactly \(q\), so the exclusion never applies. We do not claim polynomial-time recognition of DR on arbitrary larger-seat instances or that local DR always implies DR when DR is infeasible.

## Proof
**Two-seat insertion lemma.** Fix a two-seat JR committee \(W=\{x,y\}\) and any candidate \(d\) with \(s(d)=q=\lceil n/2\rceil\), where \(d\notin W\). At least one of \(\{d,x\}\) and \(\{d,y\}\) satisfies JR. For odd \(n\), \(S(d)\) has more than half the voters, so fewer than \(q\) voters are left unrepresented by any committee containing \(d\); either replacement is JR. For even \(n=2q\), suppose both pairs violate JR. A violation for \(\{d,x\}\) means that at least \(q\) voters outside \(S(d)\) remain unrepresented; there are exactly \(q\) such voters, so \(S(x)\setminus S(d)=\varnothing\), and a candidate \(h\) is approved by all voters outside \(S(d)\). Similarly \(S(y)\setminus S(d)=\varnothing\). Thus \(W\) leaves all \(q\) voters outside \(S(d)\) unrepresented even though they jointly approve \(h\), contradicting JR. This proves the lemma, including when approval ballots are empty.

**A quota-supported elected candidate completes in one swap.** Let \(W=\{z,x\}\) be JR, with \(s(z)=q\) and \(s(x)\ne q\). Let \(D=\{u,v\}\) be a DR committee; thus \(s(u)=s(v)=q\) and \(D\) is JR. We show that some \(d\in D\setminus W\) has \(\{z,d\}\) JR. When \(n\) is odd, every committee containing the quota-supported \(z\) is JR, and \(D\setminus W\) is nonempty because \(x\) is not quota-supported. When \(n=2q\), if one of \(u,v\) has a different support set from \(z\), the union with \(S(z)\) has at least \(q+1\) voters, leaving at most \(q-1\) unrepresented, so \(\{z,d\}\) is JR. This \(d\) is not \(x\) because \(x\) is off quota. If instead both \(S(u)\) and \(S(v)\) coincide with \(S(z)\), the JR committee \(D\) has exactly the same voter coverage as \(\{z,d\}\) for any \(d\in D\setminus W\); hence \(\{z,d\}\) is JR. In either case, swapping \(x\) for \(d\) strictly reduces \(|s(x)-q|\) to zero and is admissible.

**Completion from any two-seat JR committee.** If both members have degree \(q\), it is DR. If exactly one does, use the preceding paragraph. Otherwise choose a member \(d\) of any feasible DR committee. The insertion lemma produces a JR-preserving exchange replacing an off-quota elected member by \(d\). This strictly improves the local objective and is admissible. Now one elected member is at quota; one further exchange yields DR. Thus every JR two-seat committee has an improving path of length at most two to DR, and a non-DR JR committee cannot be locally optimal. With \(k=1\), a DR candidate has support \(n\); replacing any off-quota singleton with it is JR and strictly improves the deviation.

**Sharpness with three seats.** For the stated three-voter instance, \(q=1\) and candidate support counts are \(s(a)=s(c)=s(d)=s(e)=1\) and \(s(b)=2\). The committee \(D=\{a,c,d\}\) covers all three voters and has every support count equal to \(q\), so is DR. The committee \(W=\{b,d,e\}\) also covers all voters, so is JR, but is not DR because \(s(b)=2\). The only swaps that can strictly reduce its support deviation remove \(b\) and introduce \(a\) or \(c\). The former leaves the voter approving only \(b,c\) unrepresented, and the latter leaves the voter approving only \(a,b\) unrepresented; because \(q=1\), either violates JR. Therefore \(W\) is local DR. This proves a strict separation even on the candidate-interval domain.

Finally, for any \(k\), if \(m\le k+1\), any two distinct size-\(k\) committees differ by exactly one exchange. If \(D\) is DR and \(W\) is a non-DR JR committee, \(D\) exchanges an off-quota selected candidate for a quota-supported unselected candidate. The swap is JR, strictly improving, and admissible. Hence \(W\) is not local DR. For \(k=3\), the witness with \(m=5\) meets this lower bound exactly.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify_local_dr_threshold.py` checks Definition 27 literally using integer support counts, JR's equivalent uncovered-support criterion, and the precise forbidden-swap test. It exhaustively verifies the absence of two-seat local traps when DR is feasible for all approval profiles with \(1\le n\le4\) and \(2\le m\le4\), as well as the absence of three-seat traps when \(m=4\) and \(1\le n\le4\). It separately checks every relevant property of the explicit three-voter candidate-interval witness. All checks use integer arithmetic; the unrestricted two-seat assertion follows from the proof above, not from enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Chingoma, Grossi, Lindeboom, and Maly (2026), *Diverse Representation in Approval-Based Committee Voting*, introduce DR (Definition 22), the restricted improvement rule local DR (Definition 27), and a larger counterexample in their Proposition 29 and Appendix E, involving four seats, twenty-four voters, and seven candidates. Their result establishes that local DR need not recover a feasible DR committee, but does not prove a general two-seat guarantee or exhibit a three-seat candidate-interval obstruction. The current theorem locates the exact seat threshold for such traps, gives a two-swap completion guarantee, and makes the three-seat obstruction minimal in the number of candidates.

Standard JR dates to Aziz and collaborators (2017). Neither the standard JR existence theorem nor the separate DR-existence results imply the local-search exchange guarantee without the two-seat insertion argument.

## Limitations
The existence of a DR committee is necessary for the positive implication: local DR can be well-defined when exact DR is infeasible. The negative witness uses three seats, three voters, five candidates, and candidate-interval ballots; no claim is made about minimal electorate size among all three-seat traps or about any approximation factor for larger committees. The historical novelty of the precise boundary is subject to the possibility of unpublished or differently phrased equivalent results.
