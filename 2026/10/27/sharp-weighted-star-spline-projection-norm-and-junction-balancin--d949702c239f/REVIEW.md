# Scientific review: weighted-star linear-spline projection

**Correctness — PASS.** For positive weighted masses, the nodal Gram matrix is positive definite and its Schur complement is exactly one quarter of total mass. The displayed inverse was checked by exact block multiplication. Each reproducing-kernel row is affine on each arm; triangle integration gives the complete central and leaf row norms. Convexity reduces the global norm to nodal rows, and sign-valued inputs make the bound exact. The extremal range and balancing condition follow from monotonicity of the explicit formula. An independent rational implementation checked 225 variable-weight cases and additional balancing and degeneracy instances. The proof, not the test sample, covers all real positive data.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The closest two-cell interval formula gives the two-arm specialization and is explicitly excluded from the new scope. The full texts of Kayumov (arXiv:math/0408418), Passenbrunner--Shadrin (arXiv:1308.4824), and Passenbrunner (arXiv:1608.06720) treat projections on intervals or circles and establish other estimates, not an exact weighted mass law on junctions of valence three or more. The 1997 Ciesielski--Kamont interval-spline article could be identified bibliographically but not read in full. A previous exact two-cell interval formula does not itself imply the high-valence star kernel calculation, which requires the junction Gram-block coupling. An unindexed metric-network result remains possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The space is the canonical lowest-order continuous finite-element space on a star-shaped metric network. The complete sharp constant for arbitrary valence and positive edgewise uniform weights establishes degree-independent stability, identifies the shortest *weighted mass* as the sole source of worst-case amplification, and proves the optimal way to allocate arm sampling weight. It also yields a valence-dependent, strictly increasing optimal floor and a sharp but nonattaining universal envelope. This is a natural full classification for a fundamental junction geometry, not an arbitrary finite slice or numerical recomputation.

**Limits.** One affine element per arm; strictly positive constant density on each arm. Results about arbitrary further subdivision or irregularly varying densities are not asserted. Database search and partial access cannot prove absolute historical novelty.
