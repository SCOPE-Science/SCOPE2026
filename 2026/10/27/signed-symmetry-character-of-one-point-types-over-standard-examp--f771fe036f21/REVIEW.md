# Scientific review: signed one-point type symmetry of standard examples

**Correctness — PASS.** Every nonalgebraic type corresponds to one admissible ideal/filter pair, by the homogeneous-poset one-point extension property. For \(S_n\) the lower sets admit three disjoint forms, and each one-sided type contributes the corresponding invariant set; mixed types are exactly two nonempty, disjoint index subsets. Permutations act independently on index cycles, giving the first character. For the duality-twisted action, invariance under the square of a permutation and disjointness permit precisely three states per even cycle and one state per odd cycle, yielding the second. Explicit cardinality and orbit formulas follow by complete orbit classification. The standalone checker exhaustively confirms all permutations through \(n=6\). Its output is corroborative, not the general proof.

**Originality — PASS (with residual risks).** Full-text inspection of the 2013 standard-example paper establishes the object and order-dimension motivation but reveals no equivariant one-type character; the inspected portions and targeted full-text searches in the 2014 dimension paper likewise do not cover this result. Earlier general height-two counting determines the scalar labelled count and is expressly acknowledged; it does not classify cyclewise fixed types or parity-twisted duality. Searches of relevant indexed research and primary literature found no equivalent signed character or orbit classification. More specialized or unindexed literature, including an incompletely accessible homogeneity paper, remains a real residual risk; failed searches alone are not used as proof of priority.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The dimension-extremal standard examples are a canonical, highly symmetric family. The result is a complete group-equivariant classification of their finite-parameter one-type space, revealing an even-cycle-only order-duality law and a quadratic orbit spectrum despite exponential labelled growth. The result refines the established nonequivariant general counting theory rather than just recomputing a scalar value.

**Closest literature and limits.** Biró–Hamburger–Pór (2013, arXiv:1311.6518v1) and Biró–Hamburger–Pór–Trotter (2014, arXiv:1402.5113) concern standard-example order dimension. Droste–Kuske (2003) and Cameron–Lockett (2010) are general homogeneous-poset background. General ideal/filter descriptions and height-two scalar count formulas are not new. The statement does not extend to higher-arity types or to an order-preserving duality automorphism; publication-priority uncertainty and lack of independent audit remain.
