# Scientific review: one-rewire threshold cascade cutoff

## Correctness

**PASS.** The claim has two independently checkable components: a complete lower-order obstruction through five vertices and an explicit six-vertex tree witness. The exhaustive checker enumerates all connected labeled simple graphs of orders two through five, every nonempty proper initial seed set, every allowed ordered rewire, and exact rational breakpoints/midpoints covering every real homogeneous strict fractional threshold. Its 213,990 nontrivial rewiring comparisons pass, and it verifies that the displayed six-vertex tree changes a three-adopter closure to full adoption at threshold one third. The proof identifies the complete finite-discontinuity set so this is not statistical sampling or an extrapolation to real thresholds. A separate argument checks the full threshold interval and extends the witness to every larger order.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to publication-priority limits.** The 2023 Min–San Miguel paper established both suppression and possible enhancement of macroscopic threshold cascades under coevolving homophilic link rewiring. The present claim is not that qualitative mechanism: it fixes a **single** permitted rewiring before deterministic closure and proves a **sharp universal six-vertex finite cutoff** over all connected simple graphs, thresholds, seeds and legal rewires. The 2018 Eckles–Mossel–Rahimian–Sen work concerns noisy or absolute-neighbor-count contagion and edge rewiring on large lattice/random networks. A 2025 blocking-set paper concerns integer thresholds on static networks. These do not imply the exact one-event homogeneous fractional-threshold cutoff. Relevant research-record searches returned no directly covering statement. No assertion of absolute publication priority is made.

## Value

**PASS.** A published model exhibits competing effects of adaptive rewiring on cascades but does not identify the smallest interaction network that can display a positive net effect from a single permitted escape rewire. The theorem isolates that mechanism, sharply distinguishes local one-round suppression from later network-mediated reinforcement, and shows the enhancement is possible on trees at every order from six onward. This is a natural complete finite-size existence classification, not an arbitrary numerical instance.

## Closest literature and limitations

Eckles et al., *Long ties accelerate noisy threshold-based contagions* (2018), arXiv:1810.03579, especially the comparison to related work and results. The single-rewire closure theorem does not quantify expected cascade size under an ongoing stochastic adaptive process.
