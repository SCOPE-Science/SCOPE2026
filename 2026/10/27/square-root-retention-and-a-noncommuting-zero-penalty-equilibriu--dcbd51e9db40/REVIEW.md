# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

A geometric-series calculation reconstructs the exact utility at every positive penalty and at every nonsingular zero-penalty strategy profile. Its unilateral derivative has a sign independent of the acting agent's strategy; the complete zero-penalty support case split (zero, one, two, or three positive retentions) excludes all equilibria. The symmetric positive-penalty sign function is strictly decreasing with explicitly checked endpoint values, giving the unique symmetric branch and threshold at \(\varepsilon=1/2\). Direct expansion proves \(q_\varepsilon\sim\sqrt{3\varepsilon}\), and the exact mass-flow equations give the limiting voting-weight discontinuity. Symbolic and finite tests corroborate the identities but are not used to establish the infinite assertions.

## Originality — PASS, with explicitly bounded bibliographic risk

Bersetche (arXiv:2209.14128), Sections 2–4, already defines the absorbing Markov-type delegation model, proves positive-penalty existence, and permits network-restricted strategy faces. **Its Appendix A.3 already provides zero-penalty nonexistence for a different two-agent fractional example using negative utilities.** Escoffier–Gilbert–Pass-Lanneau (arXiv:1904.05775), Example 2 and Observation 1, already give a binary three-agent cyclic no-equilibrium example. These premises and qualitative antecedents are not claimed as novel. The inspected primary texts do not cover the present simultaneous **nonnegative-preference continuous-cycle obstruction plus exact symmetric retention branch, square-root asymptotic, sharp \(\varepsilon=1/2\) boundary, and noncommuting mass limit**. Searches on fractional delegation equilibrium, cyclic delegates and singular vanishing-penalty retention identified no equivalent quantitative calculation; absence in a search is not a priority guarantee. Potentially relevant later treatments of regularized Markov delegation remain a residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result quantifies an otherwise hidden singularity in the penalty remedy for cyclic delegation: tiny leakage induces only square-root-sized individual retention while recovering asymptotically all three counted votes, even though the limiting profile would invalidate every vote. This is a concrete mathematical limitation of interpreting vanishing-regularization equilibria as equilibria of the unpenalized mechanism. It also identifies an exact strategic phase boundary and distinguishes equilibrium-profile convergence from voting-power convergence, questions motivated directly by the source's zero-penalty approximation.

## Scope and limitations

Uniqueness is restricted to symmetric positive-penalty equilibria. Directed trust restriction, payoff values, and the absorption rule are structural hypotheses; no results are claimed for arbitrary complete strategy simplices, stochastic learning, all cardinal preferences, or election outcomes. Closest literature includes the author's own two-agent counterexample and the earlier ordinal three-cycle. Global priority and independent validation are not established.
