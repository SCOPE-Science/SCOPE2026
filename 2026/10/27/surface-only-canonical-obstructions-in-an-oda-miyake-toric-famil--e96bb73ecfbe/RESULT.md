# Surface-only canonical obstructions in an Oda–Miyake toric family

## Finding
For every integer \(d\geq2\), consider the smooth complete toric threefold
\[
 X_d=Z''_{13}(-2,2,0,d)
\]
of Oda–Miyake and Fujino–Sato. Its canonical bundle \(\omega_{X_d}\) is **not tensor-generating**, although
\[
 K_{X_d}\cdot C\ne0
\]
for **every torus-invariant irreducible curve** \(C\subset X_d\). The obstruction occurs on the torus-invariant surface \(D_7\) corresponding to the ray \(v_7=(0,0,1)\). This surface is \(\operatorname{Bl}_p(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1)\), and if \(F_x,F_y\) are the two ruling pullbacks and \(E\) is the exceptional curve, then
\[
 K_{X_d}|_{D_7}=(d-1)F_x-2F_y+3E.
\]
Neither this divisor nor its negative is big. Moreover, for every \(d\ge2\) there is a **smooth, non-torus-invariant** curve \(C_d\subset D_7\) satisfying
\[
 K_{X_d}\cdot C_d=0,\qquad g(C_d)=d-2.
\]
Thus one obtains canonical-trivial curves of unbounded genus in threefolds with no canonical-trivial invariant curve. In particular, the assertion that all canonical tensor-generation failures in the four-parameter \(Z''_{13}\) family arise from invariant \((-1,-1)\)-curves is false. This supplies a negative answer to the second part of Question 6.6 in [1]; it is not a complete classification of that four-parameter family.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\). For the indicated integer \(d\ge2\), take the lattice \(N=\mathbb Z^3\) and eight rays
\[
\begin{array}{llll}
 v_1=(1,1,2),&v_2=(1,0,0),&v_3=(0,-1,0),&v_4=(0,0,-1),\\
 v_5=(-1,-2,d),&v_6=(0,1,0),&v_7=(0,0,1),&v_8=(-1,0,d+1).
\end{array}
\]
The maximal cones are precisely
\[
124,234,345,456,146,167,127,237,358,568,378,678,
\]
where, for example, \(124\) means the cone generated by \(v_1,v_2,v_4\). This is Construction 6.1 of [1], which follows [2]. The fan is smooth and complete for all integral parameters by those sources. As \(b=2\ne0\) and \(a=-2\ne c=0\), its variety is non-projective [2, §4].

For a line bundle \(L\) on a proper variety, *tensor-generating* means that the tensor powers \(\{L^{\otimes n}:n\in\mathbb Z\}\) generate the perfect derived category. The criterion used below is [1, Theorem 1.1/Corollary 3.6]: for a smooth complete toric variety, the canonical bundle is tensor-generating only if each ray generator can be strictly separated from the other ray generators in its star by an affine hyperplane passing through that generator. A convex-hull inclusion obstructs the condition.

## Proof
**1. The complete invariant-curve calculation.** There are eighteen two-dimensional cones (walls). For a wall \(\tau=\langle v_i,v_j\rangle\) with adjacent maximal cones involving opposite rays \(v_k,v_l\), write the unique integral relation
\[
 v_k+v_l=\alpha v_i+\beta v_j.
\]
Smoothness gives integral coefficients, and the toric wall intersection formula is
\[
 (-K_{X_d})\cdot V(\tau)=2-\alpha-\beta.
\]
The following table enumerates **all** walls; each entry is \((-K_{X_d})\cdot V(ij)\).

| Wall | Degree | Wall | Degree | Wall | Degree |
|:--|--:|:--|--:|:--|--:|
| \(12\) | \(2\) | \(14\) | \(-1\) | \(16\) | \(2\) |
| \(17\) | \(3\) | \(23\) | \(2\) | \(24\) | \(3\) |
| \(27\) | \(-1\) | \(34\) | \(d\) | \(35\) | \(3\) |
| \(37\) | \(1-d\) | \(38\) | \(-1\) | \(45\) | \(2\) |
| \(46\) | \(d+5\) | \(56\) | \(-1\) | \(58\) | \(2\) |
| \(67\) | \(-d-2\) | \(68\) | \(3\) | \(78\) | \(2\) |

This table follows by substitution of the eight displayed ray generators into the wall relations; each wall is shared by exactly two of the listed maximal cones. For \(d\ge2\), none of the eighteen expressions vanishes. Since all torus-invariant irreducible curves on a smooth complete toric threefold arise from walls, this establishes the first assertion.

**2. An obstruction at a divisor, not at a wall.** The four rays \(v_2,v_8,v_3,v_6\) are adjacent to \(v_7\) in the fan. Directly,
\[
 v_7=\frac{v_2+v_8}{d+1}
       +\frac{d-1}{2(d+1)}(v_3+v_6).
\]
All four coefficients are positive and their sum is one for every \(d\ge2\). Thus \(v_7\) lies in their convex hull and cannot be strictly separated by an affine hyperplane through it. The canonical ray criterion [1, Corollary 3.6] proves that \(\omega_{X_d}\) is **not tensor-generating**. By the wall calculation, this failure has no invariant-curve witness.

**3. Identification of the obstructing surface and canonical restriction.** Let \(D_7=V(v_7)\). Projecting its star fan to \(N/\mathbb Zv_7\cong\mathbb Z^2\), its rays in cyclic order are
\[
 (1,0),(1,1),(0,1),(-1,0),(0,-1).
\]
Its two-dimensional cones are those formed by successive rays. This is the fan of \(\operatorname{Bl}_p(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1)\) at a torus fixed point. Denote by \(F_x,F_y\) the pullbacks of the two ruling classes and by \(E\) the exceptional divisor. They have intersection products
\[
 F_x^2=F_y^2=F_xE=F_yE=0,\quad F_xF_y=1,\quad E^2=-1.
\]
The five torus boundary curves on \(D_7\), indexed by their other rays, are \(E\) (index \(1\)), \(F_x-E\) (index \(2\)), \(F_y-E\) (index \(6\)), \(F_x\) (index \(8\)), and \(F_y\) (index \(3\)). Their canonical degrees, negating the five appropriate entries in the wall table, are respectively
\[
 -3,\quad 1,\quad d+2,\quad -2,\quad d-1.
\]
These five numbers uniquely give
\[
 K_{X_d}|_{D_7}=(d-1)F_x-2F_y+3E.
\]
In particular,
\[
 K_{X_d}|_{D_7}\cdot F_x=-2<0,
 \qquad K_{X_d}|_{D_7}\cdot F_y=d-1>0.
\]
Both ruling pullbacks are nef on the projective surface. An effective divisor has nonnegative intersection with a nef divisor, and the same inequality holds for pseudo-effective divisor classes by taking limits. Consequently neither the displayed restricted canonical class nor its negative is pseudo-effective, hence neither is big. The invariant surface is therefore directly obstructing in the sense of [1, Theorem 1.1].

**4. Smooth zero-degree curves of unbounded genus.** In \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\), a general member of the basepoint-free complete linear system of bidegree \((d-1,2)\) is smooth and irreducible and avoids the designated blowup center \(p\). Let \(C_d\) be its strict transform on \(D_7\), whose class is \((d-1)F_x+2F_y\), with no exceptional component. Intersecting with the restricted canonical class,
\[
 K_{X_d}\cdot C_d
 =[(d-1)F_x-2F_y+3E]\cdot[(d-1)F_x+2F_y]
 =2(d-1)-2(d-1)=0.
\]
The genus formula for a smooth curve of bidegree \((r,s)\) on \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) gives
\[
 g(C_d)=(r-1)(s-1)=d-2.
\]
These curves are not torus-invariant: every invariant curve on \(X_d\) was proved to have nonzero canonical degree. The genera grow without bound as \(d\to\infty\). This completes the theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Ito–Zeng–Zeng [1, §§3–4] prove the general fan and curve criteria used here, and already show by a separate \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) example that non-invariant zero-degree curves can exist. Their Remark 7.6 gives a general construction of surface-only obstructions on other toric threefolds. Neither general phenomenon is claimed as new.

The more specific question [1, Question 6.6] asks whether all failures inside the named four-parameter Oda–Miyake family arise from invariant \((-1,-1)\)-curves. Their two computed one-parameter subfamilies have \(c=a+2\) and \(d=a+1\) or \(d=a\), whereas here \(a=-2,c=0,d\ge2\): the new subfamily lies outside both computed loci. This theorem identifies an explicit obstruction **inside that same four-parameter family**, with all eighteen invariant-curve degrees nonzero and with zero-degree curves of unbounded genus. Fujino–Sato [2] provide the family and its non-projectivity criterion, but not these canonical tensor-generation calculations. This is an explicit motivated counterexample to the suggested invariant-curve-only pattern; the general criteria and the underlying toric variety are prior work.

## Limitations
This theorem does not classify every parameter quadruple, all obstructing divisors or all canonical-trivial curves. It does not claim an example of the **general** surface-only obstruction phenomenon for the first time: such examples already occur in [1, Remark 7.6]. It establishes the stated phenomenon only for the displayed infinite subfamily, over \(\mathbb C\). The computations alone do not establish historical priority; an implicit prior evaluation of this particular subfamily remains possible.
