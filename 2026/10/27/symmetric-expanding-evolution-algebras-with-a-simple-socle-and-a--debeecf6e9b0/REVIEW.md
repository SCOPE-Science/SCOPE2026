# Review

## Correctness — PASS

The general proof gives an explicit natural basis, proves invertibility of the shifted adjacency matrix, identifies a simple half-dimensional square ideal, establishes its essentiality and consequently classifies **every** ideal. The graph and Laplacian are computed by exact fiber decomposition. A finite-field checker independently enumerates all 1120 subspaces in the smallest nontrivial test, finding exactly nine ideals. The finite test is not used as an infinite proof. Fields of characteristic two are excluded.

## Originality — PASS (self-review)

The 2026 expander evolution algebra paper introduces the support-graph setting and contains a claimed symmetric-simplicity theorem; its Section 9.2 already gives Kronecker-product constructions. The 2016 structure paper explains why connectedness differs from true simplicity. The 2024 ideals paper already proves all subspaces containing the square are ideals, and supplies a dimension-two equal-square example. None of these component facts is claimed as new. The distinctive assertion here is a complete ideal-lattice classification **for every** regular-base two-clique expander construction, proved by the essentiality of its simple square, together with a complete spectrum and fixed-degree expansion. Full relevant published texts were inspected, alongside literature and scientific-record searches. Residual risk remains for implicit treatment of this exact clone/tensor family by general theory.

## Value — PASS

The construction gives arbitrarily large, bounded-degree, uniformly expanding support graphs with a unique simple socle and all remaining ideals classified by a subspace lattice. The exact spectral-gap identity quantifies expansion and the finite-field Gaussian polynomial counts every ideal, not just maximal ideals. These features furnish a structural boundary to transferring graph expansion into algebraic simplicity.

## Scientific limitations

The graph is associated with the stated natural basis; the algebra structure matrix is singular, and no claim is made of perfectness or of a spectral bound on multiplication as a linear operator. Finite fields require an invertible parameter. The originality conclusion is not independently verified and may be weakened by broader unlocated ideal-lattice literature.
