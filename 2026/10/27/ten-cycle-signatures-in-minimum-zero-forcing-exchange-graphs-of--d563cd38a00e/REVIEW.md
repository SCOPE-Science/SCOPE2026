# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof starts from an exact first-force condition, proves all successful three-white-vertex configurations, counts them via the distance-two graph, and classifies all reconfiguration exchanges through the common-pair multiplicity table. Its degree-class exception at ten-cycles is explicitly justified by antipodal coincidence. The constructive three-step exchange path and the disjoint-triple witness establish exact diameter. The stand-alone checker builds the complement and runs ordinary color-change forcing independently of the characterization; it verifies every parameter listed in `VERIFICATION.md`. Finite testing supports but does not replace the general proof.

## Originality — PASS, with stated residual risks
Eroh–Kang–Yi's cycle-complement minimum number and an earlier exact single-cycle complement polynomial/classification cover numerical and single-cycle forcing-set counts. Geneson–Haas–Hogben introduce the general reconfiguration operation and prove results for ordinary cycles and several other graph families, not the complements of arbitrary two-factors. The new contribution is the general long-cycle-union exchange graph's exact degree spectrum, ten-cycle detector, and exact diameter. Relevant published mathematical-result searches and review of accessible earlier records found no stronger covering statement. Bibliographic incompleteness, equivalent terminology, and unindexed earlier computations remain risks; search absence alone is not claimed as proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS
The ten-cycle multiplicity is a natural invariant of a spanning two-factor, and the theorem recovers it from an optimal-solution reconfiguration graph even though order, edges, and diameter do not detect it. This separates coarse and local reconfiguration data on an infinite family, and supplies an exact intrinsic detector rather than merely another numerical zero-forcing optimum.
