# Scientific review: ternary quotients of four consecutive integers

## Correctness — PASS
The integer-interval description is exact because sums of a fixed number of consecutive generators fill a complete integer interval. Its nested offset intervals give exactly one minimal representative per residue modulo the quotient's multiplicity, yielding the displayed Apéry formula. The explicit positive/negative branches give the four-generator descriptions; Selmer's Apéry identities give the exact genus and Frobenius formulas, and the terminal-branch comparisons give every pseudo-Frobenius element. All boundary cases, including \((r,x)=(2,3)\), are treated. The standalone verifier successfully tested 1596 parameter pairs for Apéry arithmetic and 176 parameter pairs by an independent direct membership enumeration. Computation is not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to priority risks
The 2018 arithmetic-progression quotient problem explicitly permits four consecutive generators and denominator three; its published formulas cover different cases. Liu's 2023 treatment arXiv:2312.06096 requires the denominator to divide the first generator, which the stated family excludes. Liu's 2023 general generator construction arXiv:2312.10889 and his 2026 GCNS-quotient formulas were read and compared; neither states or immediately implies this two-family, modulus-three Apéry staircase or the resulting complete invariants. Targeted mathematical-record, source, and exact-generator searches did not locate dominating coverage. An unindexed specialization of a general semigroup theorem and non-explicit consequences of general generating-function methods remain risks; no universal priority guarantee is asserted.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The 2018 authors singled out the Frobenius/genus problem for quotient semigroups generated by finite arithmetic progressions as an open direction. The present proof determines both natural Frobenius invariants for an infinite non-divisible-denominator subfamily, including a clean genus law and an exact residue-class transition for the Frobenius number. It further determines the full pseudo-Frobenius set and type, explaining why almost-equal quotient presentations can have distinct factorization obstructions. This is not simply a restatement of the general Apéry formula, since the contribution is its uniform evaluation with a new two-branch staircase.

- Liu, arXiv:2312.06096, §1–3: denominator divides first generator; excludes the current \(3\nmid a\) cases.

- Liu, arXiv:2604.26415, §1–3: different GCNS family and divisor hypothesis.
