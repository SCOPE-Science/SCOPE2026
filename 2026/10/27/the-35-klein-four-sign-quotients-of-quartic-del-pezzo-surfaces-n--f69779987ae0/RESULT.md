# The 35 Klein-four sign quotients of quartic del Pezzo surfaces: node baskets and smooth-locus fundamental groups

## Finding

Let \(\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_4\in\mathbb C\) be pairwise distinct and set
\[
X=\left\{\sum_{i=0}^4 x_i^2=\sum_{i=0}^4\lambda_i x_i^2=0\right\}\subset\mathbb P^4.
\]
Then \(X\) is a smooth degree-four del Pezzo surface. Let \(A=(\mathbb Z/2)^5/\langle(1,1,1,1,1)\rangle\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^4\) act by coordinate signs. For each rank-two subgroup \(H\le A\), let \(Y_H=X/H\) be the normal projective quotient. The following table classifies *all* \(35\) distinct such subgroups, not abstract isomorphism classes of the quotients:

| Nonidentity sign types in \(H\) | Number of \(H\) | Basket of \(Y_H\) | \(K_{Y_H}^2\) | \(\pi_1(Y_H^{\mathrm{sm}})\) |
|:--|--:|:--|--:|:--|
| single, single, double | 10 | smooth | 9 | trivial |
| single, double, double | 15 | \(4A_1\) | 4 | \(\mathbb Z/2\) |
| double, double, double | 10 | \(6A_1\) | 1 | \((\mathbb Z/2)^2\) |

Here “single” and “double” mean respectively one and two flipped coordinates, modulo the projectively trivial all-five flip. Every \(Y_H\) is Gorenstein with ample anticanonical class; the smooth case is isomorphic to \(\mathbb P^2\). In the third case, the quotient \(X\to Y_H\) is the universal quasi-étale Galois cover with group \((\mathbb Z/2)^2\).

Moreover, for \(H\) generated by the flips of coordinate pairs \(\{0,1\}\) and \(\{0,2\}\), let \(s=x_3\), \(t=x_4\), \(u=x_2^2\), \(v=x_0x_1x_2\), and
\[
L_0=\frac{(\lambda_1-\lambda_2)u+(\lambda_1-\lambda_3)s^2+(\lambda_1-\lambda_4)t^2}{\lambda_0-\lambda_1},\qquad
L_1=\frac{(\lambda_2-\lambda_0)u+(\lambda_3-\lambda_0)s^2+(\lambda_4-\lambda_0)t^2}{\lambda_0-\lambda_1}.
\]
Then
\[
Y_H\simeq \left\{v^2=uL_0L_1\right\}\subset\mathbb P(1,1,2,3)_{s,t,u,v}.
\]
The three branch components \(u=0\), \(L_0=0\), and \(L_1=0\) are pairwise transverse, with two intersections per pair and no triple intersection, giving six ordinary nodes.

## Assumptions and scope

The ground field is \(\mathbb C\), and the \(\lambda_i\) are pairwise distinct. The acting group is the canonical projective sign subgroup, not the full automorphism group or an arbitrary subgroup of the Cremona group. Only normal quotients are considered; the fundamental groups refer to the ordinary topological fundamental groups of their smooth complex loci (and hence have the indicated finite étale profinite completions). Quotients from distinct subgroups are not asserted pairwise nonisomorphic.

## Proof

**1. The geometric input.** Smoothness of \(X\) follows from the rank-two Jacobian criterion: dependence of the gradients of the diagonal quadrics at a projective point forces all its nonzero coordinates to have a common \(\lambda_i\), impossible for a point of \(X\) when the parameters are distinct. Adjunction gives \(-K_X=\mathcal O_X(1)\), \(K_X^2=4\), and \(X\) is simply connected, since it is the blowup of \(\mathbb P^2\) at five points.

For an involution reversing a *single* coordinate \(x_i\), the fixed locus on \(X\) is the smooth elliptic anticanonical curve \(E_i=X\cap\{x_i=0\}\). No point of \(X\) lies on the complementary coordinate point. For an involution reversing precisely two coordinates \(x_i,x_j\), its fixed set is \(X\cap\{x_i=x_j=0\}\), comprising exactly four distinct points, by transverse intersection of two diagonal conics in the complementary \(\mathbb P^2\). There are no points on the complementary \(\mathbb P^1\). At each isolated fixed point the action on the tangent surface is multiplication by \(-1\), so a stabilizer of order two produces an \(A_1\) quotient singularity. Along a single-flip elliptic fixed curve the tangent action is a complex reflection and its quotient is smooth.

**2. Complete subgroup census and local stabilizers.** The \(15\) nontrivial projective sign vectors comprise five singles and ten doubles. Enumerate all two-dimensional subspaces of the four-dimensional vector space \(A\); their number is \(\binom{4}{2}_2=35\). Two distinct singles span a subgroup of type (single,single,double), giving \(\binom 52=10\) subgroups. A single \(i\) together with a double not containing \(i\) gives type (single,double,double), with exactly \(5\binom 42/2=15\) groups; division by two accounts for the two doubles in the group. The remaining ten have three doubles, necessarily the three coordinate-pair flips on one three-element index set.

For two singles, the four points in \(E_i\cap E_j\) have the full rank-two stabilizer acting as two independent reflections, so their quotients are smooth. All other stabilizers are reflections or trivial. For the type with one single and two doubles, the two double flips each have four isolated fixed points, and these sets are disjoint because a point fixed by both would also be fixed by their single product but no double fixed point lies on its single fixed curve. Their total eight points have stabilizer order two and form four orbits, hence four \(A_1\) nodes. For the all-double type, the three fixed sets (four points each) are pairwise disjoint: a point with stabilizer containing two independent double flips would require simultaneous vanishing of at least three coordinate entries, leaving at most two nonzero coordinates, which cannot solve both diagonal quadrics for distinct parameters. Thus twelve fixed points form six orbits and give six \(A_1\) nodes. These exhaust all singularities.

**3. Anticanonical degrees.** Write \(h=\mathcal O_X(1)\) and let \(\pi:X\to Y_H\). The Hurwitz relation reads \(K_X=\pi^*K_{Y_H}+R\), where \(R\) is the sum of single-flip elliptic fixed divisors. There are respectively \(2,1,0\) such divisors, each linearly equivalent to \(h\). Consequently
\[
\pi^*(-K_{Y_H})=(1+r)h,\qquad K_{Y_H}^2=\frac{(1+r)^2}{4}\,h^2=(1+r)^2
\]
for \(r=2,1,0\), giving \(9,4,1\). The local quotients are smooth or \(A_1\), so \(K_{Y_H}\) is Cartier. The displayed pullback is ample, hence \(-K_{Y_H}\) is ample. In the first case the smooth degree-nine del Pezzo is \(\mathbb P^2\).

**4. Fundamental groups.** Let \(F\subset X\) be the finite union of points at which a non-reflection (double-flip) stabilizer occurs, and set \(U=X\setminus F\). Removing finitely many points from a simply connected complex surface leaves \(U\) simply connected. Its quotient is precisely \(Y_H^{\mathrm{sm}}\), and all remaining nontrivial stabilizers are single-flip reflections. By the elementary orbit-space fundamental-group formula (equivalently, covering-space theory away from branch divisors followed by van Kampen at the smooth branch curves),
\[
\pi_1(U/H)\cong H/\langle\text{single-flip stabilizers}\rangle.
\]
In the three cases the subgroup generated by these reflections has order respectively \(4,2,1\), giving the stated groups. In the all-double case \(U\to Y_H^{\mathrm{sm}}\) is free and simply connected of degree four; the original finite map \(X\to Y_H\) is therefore its unique normal extension and the universal quasi-étale cover.

**5. Weighted-sextic model.** For an all-double group relabel indices so that it is generated by flips \(\{0,1\}\), \(\{0,2\}\). A homogeneous polynomial is invariant precisely when the exponents of \(x_0,x_1,x_2\) have equal parity. Hence the invariant coordinate ring before imposing the two quadrics is generated by \(x_0^2,x_1^2,x_2^2,x_0x_1x_2,x_3,x_4\), with the relation \(v^2=x_0^2x_1^2x_2^2\). The defining quadrics eliminate \(x_0^2=L_0\) and \(x_1^2=L_1\), proving the weighted-sextic equation above. The three degree-two branch sections in \(\mathbb P(1,1,2)\) are graphs \(u=q(s,t)\) or \(u=0\), each isomorphic to \(\mathbb P^1\). Distinctness of all \(\lambda_i\) makes every pair intersect in two distinct transverse points and forbids triple intersections. No branch component meets the ambient weighted vertex. Each branch crossing induces a single node \(v^2=ab\); no additional singularities occur.

## Relationship to prior work

Smith's 2023 quartic del Pezzo automorphism classification provides the diagonal sign-action setting. Trepalin's 2013 study of quartic del Pezzo quotients already gives the degree-nine case with two elliptic reflections (Lemma 5.7) and, crucially, identifies four fixed points per double flip and the six-node all-double quotient (Lemma 5.8). Those individual baskets and the construction of the all-double quotient are **not** claimed as new here. Xu's 2007/2008 paper classifies possible smooth-locus fundamental groups for log del Pezzo surfaces in general and considers actions with isolated fixed points. The contribution claimed is the **complete 35-subgroup basket/degree/topological-fundamental-group table**, including the one-reflection middle case and explicit universal quasi-étale covering and weighted equation, not the general group, quotient, or log del Pezzo theory. Exact literature overlap outside the inspected sources remains possible.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns smooth diagonal intersections over \(\mathbb C\) and the coordinate-sign group only. It does not classify all quartic del Pezzo automorphism subgroups, quotient isomorphism classes, étale fundamental groups in positive characteristic, or singularity deformations. Some geometric components are direct consequences of Trepalin's general quotient machinery; no priority is claimed for them. The exact combined 35-subgroup table and the fundamental-group profiles have not been independently audited.
