# The 36 embedded collision defects of the cubic system through nine Veronese tangent planes

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, let \(V\) be a three-dimensional \(k\)-vector space, and let \(C_1,C_2\in\operatorname{Sym}^3(V^*)\) be a regular pencil of plane cubics whose scheme-theoretic intersection consists of nine distinct reduced points \(q_1,\ldots,q_9\). In \(\mathbb P(\operatorname{Sym}^2V)\cong\mathbb P^5\), write \(\Pi_i=\mathbb P(q_iV)\) for the plane tangent to the quadratic Veronese surface at \([q_i^2]\).

Let \(W\) be the complete vector space of cubic hypersurfaces containing all nine \(\Pi_i\), and let \(B=V(W)\) be their **scheme-theoretic** common zero locus. Then \(\dim W=4\), and the base scheme \(B\) has support precisely \(\bigcup_{i=1}^{9}\Pi_i\). It is reduced away from the 36 distinct pairwise intersections \(p_{ij}=\Pi_i\cap\Pi_j=[q_iq_j]\), \(i<j\). At each of these 36 points, the local nilpotent/embedded defect has length one: if \(B_{\mathrm{red}}\) denotes the reduced union of planes, then \(\mathcal I_{B_{\mathrm{red}}}/\mathcal I_B\) is a direct sum of 36 skyscraper sheaves \(k(p_{ij})\).

This is a statement about the four-dimensional **ambient cubic system** in \(\mathbb P^5\). It does not assert the same scheme structure for the anticanonical base scheme of a subsequently blown-up cubic fourfold.

## Assumptions and scope
All schemes are over an algebraically closed field \(k\) of characteristic zero. The nine points are the transverse complete intersection of two cubics: equivalently, the pencil has nine distinct basepoints and the differentials of \(C_1,C_2\) are linearly independent at each point. In particular, every cubic passing through the nine points is in \(\langle C_1,C_2\rangle\), since their homogeneous ideal is the complete-intersection ideal \((C_1,C_2)\). Products \(qv\in\operatorname{Sym}^2 V\) are symmetrized, and the line \([qv]\) represents a rank-at-most-two ternary quadratic form.

## Proof
**Step 1: the four cubic generators.** Let \(D\subset\mathbb P(\operatorname{Sym}^2V)\) be the cubic determinant hypersurface of rank-at-most-two symmetric matrices. The multiplication map \(\mathbb P(V)\times\mathbb P(V)\to D\), \(([q],[v])\mapsto[qv]\), identifies \(D\) with the symmetric square of \(\mathbb P(V)\); away from the rank-one locus, either ordered local sheet is étale and gives regular coordinates. Restriction of cubics induces
\[
\operatorname{Sym}^3((\operatorname{Sym}^2V)^*)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sym}^2(\operatorname{Sym}^3(V^*)),\qquad F\longmapsto((q,v)\mapsto F(qv)).
\]
It is surjective, with one-dimensional kernel generated by the determinant cubic. Indeed, a cubic on the symmetric-matrix space vanishes on \(D\) precisely if divisible by the irreducible determinant, and the dimensions are \(56\) and \(55\). The image of a cubic \(F\) vanishing on each \(\Pi_i\) vanishes as a cubic in \(q\) at the nine points for every fixed \(v\), so each coefficient is in \(U=\langle C_1,C_2\rangle\). By symmetry between \(q\) and \(v\), its image lies in \(\operatorname{Sym}^2U\), a three-dimensional space. Conversely, every element of \(\operatorname{Sym}^2U\) lifts and vanishes on each \(\Pi_i\). Thus \(W\) is four-dimensional: one determinant and lifts of
\[
C_1(q)C_1(v),\quad C_1(q)C_2(v)+C_2(q)C_1(v),\quad C_2(q)C_2(v).
\]

**Step 2: set-theoretic base locus.** The determinant is one of the generators, so every point of \(B\) has rank at most two. Let \(a=(C_1(q),C_2(q))\) and \(b=(C_1(v),C_2(v))\). The three displayed generators vanish simultaneously precisely when the symmetric product \(a\odot b\) is zero. Since the symmetric algebra of a two-dimensional vector space is a domain, this means \(a=0\) or \(b=0\). Thus one factor is among the nine basepoints. The support is exactly the union of the tangent planes \(\Pi_i\).

**Step 3: points of rank two.** At a point \([q_i v]\) with \(v\) not another basepoint, use local coordinates \((u,v)\) for \((C_1,C_2)\) at the first factor. The second factor's value vector is nonzero, and multiplication by that vector is an injective map into its symmetric square. The three equations therefore locally generate the regular ideal \((u,v)\) of \(\Pi_i\). At \(p_{ij}=[q_iq_j]\), with distinct \(i,j\), use \((u,v)=(C_1,C_2)\) near \(q_i\) and \((x,y)=(C_1,C_2)\) near \(q_j\); transversality makes them regular local coordinates. The discriminant hypersurface is smooth at rank two and the two ordered sheets are étale, so the completed local ideal of \(B\) inside it is
\[
I=(ux,\ uy+vx,\ vy)\subset k[[u,v,x,y]].
\]
Its radical is \(J=(u,v)\cap(x,y)=(ux,uy,vx,vy)\). The module \(J/I\) is generated by the class of \(uy=-vx\), which is nonzero and annihilated by each of \(u,v,x,y\): for example, \(u(uy)=-uvx=0\), and similarly for the other variables. Hence \(J/I\cong k\), a length-one embedded defect at each pair intersection.

**Step 4: rank-one points.** Write \([q^2]\) with \(q=q_i\), and choose affine coordinates \(s_1,s_2\) for the average of the two factors \(q+\alpha,q+\beta\) and \(t_1,t_2\) for half their difference. Locally the symmetric square has completed coordinate ring \(k[[s_1,s_2,a,b,c]]/(ac-b^2)\), where \(a=t_1^2,b=t_1t_2,c=t_2^2\). Since \((C_1,C_2)\) is an étale coordinate system at \(q_i\), one can make this same coordinate calculation using the values of \((C_1,C_2)\) on the two factors. The three base equations are a basis of the three relations \(s_1^2-a,\ s_1s_2-b,\ s_2^2-c\) (up to nonzero constants). Their quotient is \(k[[s_1,s_2]]\), because the relation \(ac=b^2\) becomes an identity. This is the reduced smooth local ring of \(\Pi_i\). At rank-one points not equal to a basepoint, at least one cubic generator is nonzero, so there is no basepoint. The result follows.

## Verification
A standalone script verifies that the difference between the radical \(J\) and the three-generator local ideal \(I\) is a nonzero one-dimensional socle killed by all four parameters. It checks the polynomial identity underlying the rank-one quotient. The infinite-family and projective claims follow from the proof, not from finite testing. The number of distinct pair intersections is \(\binom{9}{2}=36\).

## Relationship to prior work
Secci, *The blow-up of the cubic fourfold along eight planes is Fano* (arXiv:2610.25532v1, first public October 18, 2026), constructs precisely the Veronese-tangent configuration, proves that the cubic system through the first eight planes also contains the ninth (Proposition 6.1), and obtains its four-dimensional space of sections. Secci leaves a related scheme-theoretic anticanonical base-locus equality for the blown-up fourfold as a hypothesis (Corollary 6.5). The theorem here identifies the **ambient** cubic base scheme and its 36 transverse embedded defects, a different invariant not asserted in the inspected source. Dolgachev's *Classical Algebraic Geometry* supplies the classical description of the Veronese discriminant, and Dolgachev--Markushevich (arXiv:1906.01445) examines distinct configurations of ten mutually meeting planes and cubics; neither inspected discussion supplies this local ambient base-ideal computation.

## Limitations
This theorem assumes nine transverse complete-intersection points and characteristic zero. It does not address nontransverse pencils, arbitrary nine points, characteristic two, or the blow-up's anticanonical scheme. The priority comparison is limited to the cited texts and searched records; older unindexed sources could contain the same local statement. The small symbolic computation is only a corroboration of the explicit commutative-algebra step.
