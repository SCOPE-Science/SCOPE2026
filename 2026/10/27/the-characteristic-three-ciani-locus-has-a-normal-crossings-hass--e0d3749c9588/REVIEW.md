# Scientific review of the characteristic-three Ciani Hasse stratification

## Correctness — PASS
The smoothness condition follows from the complete projective Jacobian equations. The plane-quartic Cartier coefficient extraction gives the three stated factors; the prime-to-three elliptic-quotient splitting determines rank, a-number and slopes. Graph descriptions of each divisor component and its intersections prove the simple-normal-crossings assertion. The character-sum count of excluded discriminant parameters proves the four rank-stratum formulas for every finite field of characteristic three. The standalone enumerator checks exact finite fields of orders \(3\), \(9\), and \(27\) and separately confirms the polynomial coefficient identities. Infinite conclusions rely on the proof rather than enumeration.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Howe–Leprévost–Poonen (arXiv:math/9809210, §4.1) already give the genus-three biquadratic constructions and isogenies. Auer–Top (arXiv:math/0201320) study a symmetric specialization. Ohashi (arXiv:2201.12996, Lemma 2.1, §3) already supplies the smoothness test and elliptic quotients, and treats the superspecial extremum. None of the materially inspected portions gives the joint description of the three-parameter normal-crossings nonordinary divisor together with the exact counts of all four rank strata for every \(q=3^n\). The new claim relies substantially on classical quotient formulas; the possibility of an equivalent unpublished or uninspected literature census remains a genuine risk. The conductor literature was inspected only in its publicly visible portions, not asserted to lack the result throughout.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The stratification describes the complete arithmetic rank behavior of a natural dimension-three genus-three family, including the geometry of degeneracy intersections and an all-extension exact parameter enumeration. These counts distinguish rank-one, rank-two and ordinary regions and are neither one-off point computations nor arbitrary parameter slices. They are useful inputs for arithmetic statistics on the marked Ciani locus, though not moduli isomorphism-class counts.

## Scope and remaining risks
The assertion is restricted to smooth marked plane quartics in characteristic three. Familiar elliptic isogenies, supersingular examples and a basic Hasse calculation are not credited as new. Full historical uniqueness and independent mathematical validation remain open.
