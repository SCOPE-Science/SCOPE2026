# The characteristic-three Jacobian defect of the Igusa quartic

## Finding

For the characteristic-three reduction of the integral Igusa-quartic equation, the projective Jacobian singular scheme has a length-\(6\) embedded component at \(P=(1:\cdots:1)\), the common vertex of the \(15\) pair-partition lines.

## Assumptions and scope

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(3\), let \(H\subset\mathbb P^5_k\) be the hyperplane \(\sum_{i=0}^{5}x_i=0\), and let \(X_3\subset H\cong\mathbb P^4_k\) be defined by
\[
F=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{5}x_i^2\right)^2-4\sum_{i=0}^{5}x_i^4=0.
\]
Write \(S_2=\sum_i x_i^2\). Let \(J\) be the Jacobian ideal of the restricted equation on \(H\), equivalently generated after eliminating \(x_5\) by the five derivatives \(\partial F/\partial x_i-\partial F/\partial x_5\), for \(0\le i\le4\). Let \(I\) be the radical ideal of the reduced union of the \(15\) lines indexed by partitions of \(\{0,\ldots,5\}\) into three unordered pairs.

The claim concerns this explicit special-fibre equation and its Jacobian scheme. It does not assert that the characteristic-three fibre has the same moduli interpretation as the characteristic-zero Igusa quartic.

## Proof

In characteristic \(3\),
\[
\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_i}=S_2x_i-x_i^3.
\]
A point of \(X_3\) is singular precisely when these six ambient partial derivatives are all equal, say to \(\lambda\), because the normal space of \(H\) is generated by \((1,\ldots,1)\).

Thus every coordinate \(x_i\) is a root of
\[
T^3-S_2T+\lambda=0.
\]
If \(S_2=0\), the equation \(T^3+\lambda=0\) has only one root over \(k\), so all coordinates are equal. This gives \(P=(1:\cdots:1)\).

Suppose \(S_2\ne0\). The cubic has derivative \(-S_2\ne0\), hence three distinct roots \(a,b,c\). Its quadratic coefficient gives \(a+b+c=0\), and its linear coefficient gives \(S_2=a^2+ab+b^2\). If \(r,s,t\) count the occurrences of \(a,b,c\), then \(r+s+t=6\) and \(ra+sb+tc=0\). Modulo \(3\), the possible multiplicity residues are \((0,0,0)\), \((1,1,1)\), \((2,2,2)\), or a permutation of \((0,1,2)\). The first forces \(S_2=0\). The second gives \(S_2=a^2+b^2+c^2=2(a^2+ab+b^2)=-S_2\), again forcing \(S_2=0\). In each mixed-residue case, \(ra+sb+tc=0\) forces two of \(a,b,c\) to be equal, a contradiction. Therefore \(r\equiv s\equiv t\equiv2\pmod3\); since the counts sum to \(6\), each count is exactly \(2\).

Conversely, fix any partition into pairs and take coordinates \((a,a,b,b,c,c)\) with \(a+b+c=0\). In characteristic \(3\), \(S_2=2(a^2+b^2+c^2)=a^2+ab+b^2\), so \(S_2a-a^3=S_2b-b^3=S_2c-c^3=-abc\). Hence all six partial derivatives agree and the line is singular. This proves that the reduced support is exactly the \(15\) pair-partition lines. Since \(1+1+1=0\) in \(k\), every such line contains \(P\); two distinct lines intersect only at \(P\).

It remains to determine the scheme-theoretic excess. At the point with coordinates \((0,0,1,1,2,2)\), the Hessian of the restricted equation has rank \(3\) over \(\mathbb F_3\), the codimension of the affine cone over a line. Thus the Jacobian scheme is generically reduced along each line; the coordinate-permutation symmetry is transitive on the lines. Consequently \(I/J\) is supported in dimension \(0\).

An exact Gröbner-basis computation over \(\mathbb F_3\), reproducible by `verify_char3_igusa.py`, gives
\[
\operatorname{Hilb}(R/J;t)=
\frac{1+3t+6t^2+6t^3+3t^4-3t^5-t^6}{(1-t)^2},
\]
where \(R=k[x_0,\ldots,x_4]\) after eliminating \(x_5\). For the reduced union, the \(15\) line directions at \(P\) form \(15\) points in \(\mathbb P^3\). Exact evaluation-matrix ranks in degrees \(0,1,2,3\) are \(1,4,10,15\); the rank remains \(15\) in all higher degrees. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Hilb}(R/I;t)=
\frac{1+3t+6t^2+5t^3}{(1-t)^2}.
\]
The difference is
\[
\operatorname{Hilb}(R/J;t)-\operatorname{Hilb}(R/I;t)
=\frac{t^3(1+4t+t^2)}{1-t},
\]
whose Hilbert polynomial is the constant \(6\). Because \(I/J\) is zero-dimensional, its sheaf has length \(6\). The excess is invariant under \(S_6\). Any point on one of these lines other than \(P\) has three distinct coordinate values, each occurring twice, so its \(S_6\)-orbit has at least \(15\) points. A zero-dimensional invariant subscheme of length \(6\) therefore cannot be supported away from \(P\). Thus the embedded excess has length \(6\) and is supported at \(P\), as claimed.

## Relationship to prior work

The standard equation and the reduced singular locus of \(15\) pair-partition lines are described in the literature on the Castelnuovo–Igusa–Richmond (Igusa) quartic. The line-support description, cone geometry, and concurrency are not claimed as new; these are described in prior work. The additional claim here is the length-\(6\) embedded component of the projective Jacobian singular scheme at the common vertex. No located source stated this characteristic-three Jacobian-scheme calculation.

## Limitations

The result determines the support and length of the embedded excess, not the full isomorphism class of its local Artin algebra. It applies to the displayed integral equation reduced modulo \(3\); no claim is made about a modular interpretation of that fibre. The full text of the original 1962 paper was not obtained, so a historical treatment of this exact scheme structure remains a residual literature risk.
