# The complete metabelian-solubilizer profile of \(A_6\)

## Finding

Let \(G=A_6\). For \(k\geq 0\) define
\[
\operatorname{Sol}_{G,k}(x)=\{y\in G:\operatorname{dl}(\langle x,y\rangle)\leq k\},
\]
where \(\operatorname{dl}\) is the derived length (the trivial group has length zero, while a nonsoluble group has infinite derived length). Let \(\operatorname{Sol}_G(x)\) denote the unrestricted soluble solubilizer. The following table gives an *exhaustive* classwise profile. A 5-cycle has two conjugacy classes in \(A_6\), each of size 72, with identical entries.

| Cycle type in the natural degree-six action | Number of elements | \(|C_G(x)|\) | \(|N_G(\langle x\rangle)|\) | \(|\operatorname{Sol}_{G,2}(x)|\) | \(|\operatorname{Sol}_G(x)|\) |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Identity | 1 | 360 | 360 | 360 | 360 |
| One 3-cycle | 40 | 9 | 18 | 63 | 90 |
| Two disjoint 3-cycles | 40 | 9 | 18 | 63 | 90 |
| Two disjoint 2-cycles | 45 | 8 | 8 | 136 | 168 |
| One 5-cycle (two classes, 72 each) | 144 | 5 | 10 | 10 | 10 |
| Disjoint 4-cycle and 2-cycle | 90 | 4 | 8 | 72 | 104 |

Here \(\operatorname{Sol}_{G,3}(x)=\operatorname{Sol}_G(x)\) for every \(x\in G\). The divisibility
\[
|N_G(\langle x\rangle)|\;\bigm|\;|\operatorname{Sol}_{G,2}(x)|
\]
fails **precisely** on the two order-three cycle types: in each case the two orders are \(18\) and \(63\). For independent uniform \(X,Y\in A_6\), the exact derived-length census is
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrr}
\operatorname{dl}(\langle X,Y\rangle)&0&1&2&3&\infty\\\hline
\#(X,Y)&1&2519&16920&6480&103680.
\end{array}
\]
In particular,
\[
\Pr(\operatorname{dl}(\langle X,Y\rangle)\le 2)=\frac3{20},\qquad
\Pr(\langle X,Y\rangle\text{ soluble})=\frac15.
\]
Every ordered pair at derived length exactly three generates a subgroup isomorphic to \(S_4\). There are exactly 30 such subgroups in \(A_6\), and each is generated by exactly 216 ordered pairs.

## Assumptions and scope

All permutations are even permutations of six letters. Products are composition, with the right-hand factor applied first. The normalizer refers to the cyclic subgroup generated by the specified element, rather than its centralizer. The finite classification makes no assertion about alternating groups of larger degree or about universal bounded-derived-length normalizer divisibility.

## Proof

The finite exhaustive proof is a terminating integer algorithm, fully specified and independently replayable in `verify.py` (Python standard library only). The following explains why the verified enumeration exhausts *all* possibilities rather than sampling a subset.

1. Enumerate the \(6!=720\) permutations of six letters and select even parity, obtaining exactly \(360\) elements. Encode every permutation as a six-tuple and form a \(360\times360\) composition table; invert each element using that table. These are all elements and all products of \(A_6\) by the definition of an alternating group.
2. For any \(a,b\in A_6\), begin with the identity and repeatedly multiply discovered elements on the right by \(a,a^{-1},b,b^{-1}\). The process ends since there are only 360 possible elements. Its output is exactly \(\langle a,b\rangle\): all generated words are reached by induction on word length, and every reached element belongs to the generated subgroup.
3. For every enumerated subgroup \(H\), form all commutators \([u,v]=u^{-1}v^{-1}uv\) with \(u,v\in H\) and apply the same finite generator-closure procedure to that set. The result is exactly \(H'=[H,H]\), by definition. Iterating until the trivial group gives derived length. If \(H'=H\neq1\), the derived series cannot terminate and \(H\) is nonsoluble. No timeout or cutoff is used to classify a subgroup.
4. Partition the 360 elements into all seven conjugacy classes by explicitly computing \(gxg^{-1}\) for each \(g\in G\). Both \(\operatorname{dl}(\langle x,y\rangle)\) and the cardinalities of the bounded solubilizer sets are invariant under conjugating \(x\), because conjugation bijects the possible partners \(y\). Therefore it suffices to run the complete 360-partner census for *each of the seven representatives*. In the same finite loops the cyclic normalizer and element centralizer are counted directly by their definitions. These computations give the displayed classwise table. Multiplying the partner count for each class representative by the verified class size yields the whole ordered-pair distribution, totaling \(360^2=129600\) pairs.
5. Every soluble two-generated subgroup reached in this enumeration has derived length at most three, and every length-three subgroup has exactly 24 elements. Each such subgroup has, by its explicit element multiplication table, the structure of \(S_4\). To cross-check structurally, the maximal-subgroup classification of \(A_6\) lists two conjugacy classes of \(S_4\), each with index 15, hence 30 distinct \(S_4\)'s. Every proper subgroup of \(S_4\) is metabelian. The number of ordered generating pairs in \(S_4\) is \(216\): for the six transpositions, eight partners each; the eight 3-cycles, nine partners each; and six 4-cycles, sixteen partners each. The identity and the three double transpositions cannot belong to a generating pair. Thus \(6\cdot8+8\cdot9+6\cdot16=216\), and \(30\cdot216=6480\). The checker independently enumerates the 30 conjugate subgroups and independently verifies the 216 count.
6. In the 3-cycle and double-3-cycle rows the cyclic normalizer order is 18 while the metabelian-solubilizer cardinality is 63, and \(18\nmid63\). In every other class row the displayed normalizer order divides the metabelian-solubilizer cardinality. Summation of the exact classwise counts gives the two probabilities, including their difference \(1/20\).

The reasoning in steps 1–4 proves completeness of the exhaustive finite procedure. The provided source and its actual successful replay constitute the explicit certificate of the finite arithmetic claimed here, while the group-theoretic argument in step 5 supplies an independent structural explanation of the precise length-three contribution.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` using Python 3 with no additional packages. The program asserts the seven conjugacy classes and every table row, tests all partner elements for each representative, verifies that no soluble subgroup has derived length above three, verifies the exact overall distribution, and independently checks all 30 \(S_4\) subgroups and 216 generating pairs per subgroup. Its successful completion prints `VERIFY_OK`. This is an exhaustive finite certificate, not evidence for any infinite family.

## Relationship to prior work

Sabatini, *Arithmetic properties of solubilizers* (arXiv:2610.03151, first public version October 2, 2026), establishes cyclic-normalizer divisibility for unrestricted solubilizers and explicitly notes that a derived-length-two analogue fails in \(A_6\). The present finite theorem identifies *exactly which classes fail*, all corresponding cardinalities, the full distribution of two-generator derived lengths, and the structural \(S_4\) explanation for the gap between metabelian and general solubility. The work of Akbari, Delizia and Monetta studies unrestricted solubilizers; Akbari, Chuharski, Sharan and Slonim treat minimal simple groups, a class that does not include \(A_6\) because it has an \(A_5\) subgroup. The ATLAS maximal-subgroup record verifies the two \(S_4\) conjugacy classes. The known qualitative failure and the maximal-subgroup classification are not claimed as novel.

## Limitations

This is an exact result for the single natural group \(A_6\). It does not establish a bound or classification for arbitrary alternating groups. A previous equivalent census could exist outside the inspected literature; no literature search proves priority or uniqueness. No independent external mathematical audit has been performed. The computations have been reproduced only with the included finite checker, and there is no claim of formal proof-assistant certification.
