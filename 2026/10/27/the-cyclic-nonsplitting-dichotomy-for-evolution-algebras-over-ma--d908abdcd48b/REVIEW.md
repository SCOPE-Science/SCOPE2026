# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof independently derives monomial rigidity from invertibility of the structure matrix, obtains the exact scalar recurrence for every rotation, closes the recurrence modulo three, and analyzes each parity and twist case. The displayed degree-two rotation \(\phi\) is an explicit automorphism with \(\phi^{n/2}\) equal to a nontrivial order-three diagonal map. Simplicity and idempotent counts follow directly from the multiplication. The packaged checker exhaustively compares all coefficient vectors for dimensions one through seven and directly enumerates small idempotent fibers. No finite experiment is treated as the universal proof.

## Originality — PASS with residual literature risk
The closest 2026 survey already proves a nonsplit cyclic order-nine example with a different, noncycle support graph (Proposition 21), so this is not a priority claim for nonsplitting in general. Its Question 33 seeks splitting criteria; the present claim gives a complete answer for every weighted single directed cycle over the minimal four-element field supporting the required cubic torsion. The 2025 permutation-representation paper provides the general automorphism sequence and rigidity but no equivalent all-dimensional classification. Earlier permutation-algebra and idempotent papers give related normal-form and fixed-point computations, not an identified cyclic group-extension dichotomy. A universal orbit-classification theorem could in principle subsume the narrow classification when specialized, and not all older full texts were accessible. Searches found no statement asserting the exact split/nonsplit cutoff, but absence from searches alone is not a novelty proof.

## Value — PASS
The result solves a natural infinite family within a live automorphism-extension splitting question. It exhibits nonsplitting for arbitrarily large **simple** evolution algebras with minimal single-cycle support, proves the sharp arithmetic threshold \(6\mid n\), and relates the extension type to an exact idempotent dichotomy. The prime-power field restriction is mathematically motivated: \(\mathbb F_4^\times\) is the smallest multiplicative group containing nontrivial cubic torsion, while squaring acts by inversion.

## Limitations and closest literature
The construction is over \(\mathbb F_4\), not over arbitrary fields; the claim concerns rational linear automorphisms, not group schemes. The survey's earlier nonsplit six-dimensional example is fully credited. General monomial rigidity and permutation-algebra idempotent methods are prior. Historical equivalence or implications from broad classification results remain possible and merit independent bibliographic review.
