# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The claim specifies all quantifiers and distinguishes a compact-intensity uniform error from its sequential boundary checks. The crucial weighted \(O(n^{-2})\) Poisson correction follows from the exact \(k\)-cell multinomial mass formula and uniform tail bounds. The disjoint pair first-order covariance is a negative Poisson score projection; the exact count of pairs gives the claimed variance coefficient. An independent representation by orthogonal Poisson edge kernels shows strict positivity. The executable verifies 90 finite or numerical assertions, not a substitute for the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Banić–Elezović, arXiv:2610.17046v1, computes exact finite-sample variance and fixed-bin high-sample expansions, explicitly excludes the jointly growing-bin/sampling window and already gives the Gaussian large-intensity coefficient (which is credited). This finding specifically evaluates the previously untreated finite-positive-occupancy intensity and controls its uniform \(O(1)\) variance remainder. The closest matching own historical records address fixed-bin large-sample or iterated Gaussian limits, not the simultaneous proportional regime. Searches of available mathematical findings and primary literature found no exact matching formula. An equivalent consequence of earlier occupancy functional theory or later parts of the same series is a genuine unresolved priority risk; an unsuccessful search is not a proof of uniqueness.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The joint proportional regime is a natural calibration problem for ordered histograms: ignoring the weak global conditioning yields a wrong leading variance despite correct finite-dimensional Poisson limits. The coefficient is a convergent one-dimensional Poisson expectation with explicit Bessel correction, strict positivity and a sparse-onset law. This fills a mathematical gap between fixed-number-of-bins Gaussian asymptotics and fixed-sample sparse asymptotics rather than changing notation in a known limit.

The paper's own exact finite variance formulas, its nonuniform and cyclic extensions in preparation, and broad occupancy functional theory remain relevant to historical priority. No central limit theorem or extrapolation of uniformity to vanishing/diverging cell intensity is established.
