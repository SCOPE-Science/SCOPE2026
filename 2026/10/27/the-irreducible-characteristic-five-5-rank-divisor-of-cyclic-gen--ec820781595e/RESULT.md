# The irreducible characteristic-five 5-rank divisor of cyclic genus-four quintics

## Finding

Fix an algebraically closed field \(k\) of characteristic \(5\). For every squarefree monic polynomial
\[
f(x)=x^5+a_4x^4+a_3x^3+a_2x^2+a_1x+a_0\in k[x],
\]
let \(C_f\) be the smooth projective model of \(y^3=f(x)\), a cyclic genus-four curve. Set
\[
\alpha_i=[x^{4+5i}]f(x)^3\quad(0\le i\le 2),\qquad
H=a_4^5\alpha_0+a_3^5\alpha_1+a_2^5\alpha_2.
\]
Then \(H\) is a nonzero, **absolutely irreducible** polynomial in \(k[a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4]\) of weighted degree \(16\) for weights \(\operatorname{wt}(a_i)=5-i\). For every such squarefree \(f\), the \(5\)-rank of the Jacobian obeys the exact dichotomy
\[
f_5(\operatorname{Jac}(C_f))=\begin{cases}0,&H(a_0,\ldots,a_4)=0,\\2,&H(a_0,\ldots,a_4)\ne0.\end{cases}
\]
Consequently the explicitly defined hypersurface \(V(H)\), restricted to the discriminant-nonzero space of monic quintics, is a **nonempty geometrically irreducible divisor** describing precisely the rank-zero locus of this marked family. No intermediate \(5\)-rank occurs.

An expanded expression, with coefficients interpreted in characteristic five, is
\[
\begin{aligned}
H={}&3a_0^2a_4^6+a_0a_1a_3a_4^5+3a_0a_2^2a_4^5+a_0a_3^5a_4\\
&+3a_1^2a_2a_4^5+a_1a_3^6+3a_1a_3^5a_4^2\\
&+3a_2^5a_4+3a_2^2a_3^5+a_2a_3^6a_4+a_3^8.
\end{aligned}
\]
The claimed absolute irreducibility is for this **concrete polynomial in the complete affine coefficient space**, not a claim that irreducibility of the abstract genus-four cyclic-cover moduli stratum is new (that abstract result was established previously).

## Assumptions and scope

The field \(k\) is algebraically closed of characteristic exactly \(5\); the quintic is monic and squarefree; the degree-three cover is tamely ramified because \(5\ne3\). The five parameters \(a_0,\ldots,a_4\) are independent. \(V(H)\) refers to the reduced hypersurface defined by the explicitly displayed polynomial; no unproved scheme-theoretic identification with an independently defined moduli-stack stratum is asserted. The value \(f_5\) means the p-rank, equivalently the stable rank of the Cartier operator on regular differentials.

The projection \(C_f\to\mathbb P^1\) has six tame branch points (the five simple zeros of \(f\) and infinity). Riemann–Hurwitz gives \(2g-2=-6+12=6\), hence \(g=4\). At the unique point above infinity, \(\operatorname{ord}_{\infty}(x)=-3\), \(\operatorname{ord}_{\infty}(y)=-5\), and \(\operatorname{ord}_{\infty}(dx)=-4\). Thus the four regular independent forms
\[
u=\frac{dx}{y},\quad v_0=\frac{dx}{y^2},\quad v_1=\frac{x\,dx}{y^2},\quad v_2=\frac{x^2\,dx}{y^2}
\]
have pairwise different nonnegative orders \(1,6,3,0\) at infinity and form a basis of \(H^0(C_f,\Omega^1)\). At finite points regularity follows from the smooth superelliptic equation or using the standard local parameter at simple roots of \(f\).

## Proof

**1. Cartier action.** For a polynomial \(G(x)=\sum_{j\ge0}c_jx^j\), the characteristic-five Cartier identities are
\[
\mathcal C\bigl(G(x)\,dx\bigr)
=\sum_{j\ge0}c_{4+5j}^{1/5}x^j\,dx,
\qquad
\mathcal C(h^5\omega)=h\,\mathcal C(\omega).
\]
Because \(y^3=f(x)\), there are identities of rational differentials
\[
\frac{dx}{y}=\left(\frac1{y^2}\right)^5 f(x)^3\,dx,
\qquad
\frac{x^i\,dx}{y^2}=\left(\frac1y\right)^5x^if(x)\,dx.
\]
Writing \(\beta_0=a_4\), \(\beta_1=a_3\), and \(\beta_2=a_2\), the coefficients \(\alpha_i=[x^{4+5i}]f^3\) satisfy
\[
\mathcal C(u)=\sum_{i=0}^2\alpha_i^{1/5}v_i,\qquad
\mathcal C(v_i)=\beta_i^{1/5}u\quad(0\le i\le2).
\]
The exponents \(4,9,14\) are the only indices in degree \(\le15\) contributing to \(\mathcal C(u)\); for \(x^if\) with \(0\le i\le2\), only index \(4\) contributes. The perfectness of \(k\) makes the displayed fifth roots unambiguous.

**2. Stable rank.** Apply semilinearity twice:
\[
\mathcal C^2(u)=\left(\sum_{i=0}^2\alpha_i\beta_i^5\right)^{1/25}u
=H^{1/25}u.
\]
If \(H=0\), the composition kills \(u\) and therefore \(\mathcal C^3\) kills each basis vector; \(\mathcal C\) is nilpotent and stable rank is zero. If \(H\ne0\), the two one-dimensional directions generated by \(u\) and \(\mathcal C(u)\) form a stable two-dimensional semilinear image on which \(\mathcal C\) is bijective; every other direction maps into that subspace after at most one step. Hence stable rank is exactly two. For smooth projective curves, stable Cartier rank equals the p-rank of the Jacobian.

**3. The polynomial.** Multiply the monic quintic three times in \(\mathbb F_5[a_0,\ldots,a_4][x]\), select coefficients of exponents \(4,9,14\), and form \(H\). This yields precisely the eleven terms displayed above. Every monomial has weighted degree \(16\) when \(\operatorname{wt}(a_i)=5-i\).

**4. Absolute irreducibility.** Regard \(H\) as a quadratic in \(a_0\) over \(R=k[a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4]\). Its leading coefficient is \(3a_4^6\); its constant coefficient has the nonzero term \(a_3^8\) when \(a_4=0\). Consequently \(H\) is primitive in \(R[a_0]\): any common factor of its coefficients would divide \(a_4^6\), yet the constant coefficient is not divisible by \(a_4\).

The discriminant of this quadratic lies in \(R\). Under the specialization \(a_1=a_2=0\), \(a_4=1\), the quadratic becomes
\[
3a_0^2+a_3^5a_0+a_3^8
\]
and its discriminant becomes
\[
a_3^{10}-4\cdot3a_3^8=a_3^8(a_3^2+3).
\]
This is **not a square in \(k[a_3]\)**: \(a_3^2+3\) has two distinct simple roots over the algebraically closed field \(k\), since \(3\ne0\) and \(2\ne0\). Because \(R\) is a unique factorization domain and \(k\) is algebraically closed, any element of \(R\) that is a square in \(\operatorname{Frac}(R)\) is a square in \(R\). Therefore the unspecialized discriminant is not a square in \(\operatorname{Frac}(R)\); the quadratic is irreducible over this fraction field (characteristic \(\ne2\)), and Gauss's lemma makes \(H\) irreducible in \(R[a_0]\). The argument works over **every** algebraically closed field of characteristic five, proving absolute irreducibility.

**5. Smooth points on both sides.** The quintic \(x^5+x^2+x\) is squarefree (its derivative is \(2x+1\), whose unique zero is \(x=2\), where the quintic has value \(3\)), and \(H=0\). The quintic \(x^5+x^3+x\) is squarefree (its derivative is \(3x^2+1\), and the quintic is nonzero at either derivative root), and \(H=2\ne0\). Thus \(V(H)\) meets the smooth locus nontrivially and its complement also meets it. The smooth locus is an open subset of affine five-space, so the irreducible hypersurface restricts to a nonempty geometrically irreducible divisor.

## Verification

The complete standalone pure-Python verifier `verify.py` symbolically constructs \(f^3\), extracts \(\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2\), reconstructs the eleven-term polynomial, tests its weighted homogeneity, the quadratic specialization and smooth examples. It then exhaustively examines all \(5^5\) coefficient vectors in \(\mathbb F_5^5\), classifies the \(2500\) squarefree quintics, and independently counts \(\mathbb F_{25}\)-points to test the Cartier/Frobenius trace congruence
\[
\#C_f(\mathbb F_{25})-1+2H\equiv0\pmod5.
\]
For \(\mathbb F_5\)-defined coefficients the two nonzero eigenvalues of \(\mathcal C^2\) reduce modulo five to \(H\) in the semilinear-square trace computation, giving the stated congruence. All \(2500\) smooth models pass; \(700\) have \(H=0\), and \(1800\) have \(H\ne0\). The two example point counts are \(36\) and \(42\), respectively. The symbolic derivations and commutative-algebra discriminant proof—not finite enumeration—establish the universal statement.

Reproduce with `python3 verify.py` using Python's standard library, no optional dependencies.

## Relationship to prior work

Baldwin–Eilbeck–Gibbons–Ônishi analyze precisely the same genus-four cyclic trigonal quintic family and provide its holomorphic differential basis, but their work on complex Abelian functions does not supply the characteristic-five coefficient polynomial or its irreducibility. Bouw develops general p-rank theory for cyclic prime-to-characteristic covers; generic ranks and their bounds are prior knowledge.

**Substantial prior coverage:** Özman–Pries–Weir, Proposition 5.8, prove that the abstract p-rank-zero stratum for the genus-four signature \((3,1)\) trielliptic moduli problem is irreducible when \(p\equiv2\pmod3\); their Proposition 5.9 also treats codimension-one p-rank drops. The present result **does not claim** those moduli facts or the existence of ranks zero and two as new. Its specific contribution is an independently proved coefficient-level polynomial criterion and **absolute irreducibility of its pullback hypersurface in the full monic-quintic coefficient space**, an assertion not obtained merely from irreducibility after quotienting by changes of coordinates and branch-point marking.

Sohn (2025) studies genus-four superelliptic Cartier–Manin matrices and a-numbers. Only the bibliographic abstract could be checked, not the complete text. That creates a material risk that the same matrix or a related coefficient expression has been printed before. In any case, identical first Cartier matrices do not, without further computation, imply the present stable-rank and coefficient-hypersurface irreducibility statements.

## Limitations

The theorem is confined to algebraically closed characteristic-five fields, monic squarefree degree-five polynomials and p-rank. It neither classifies Ekedahl–Oort types nor determines every Newton polygon. The polynomial is an explicit defining equation on a marked coefficient space, not a proposed canonical equation on a coarse moduli space. Abstract moduli-stratum irreducibility is previously known and excluded from the originality claim. The full Sohn (2025) paper was not inspectable, so possible overlap with its displayed matrices remains unresolved. No independent scientific validation is asserted.
