For the Gaussian randomized Rayleigh–Ritz method on a canonical Hermitian interior-eigenvalue example, even an oracle picking the better of both Ritz vectors has squared-angle risk \(\varepsilon^2\log(1/\varepsilon)+O(\varepsilon^2)\), rather than the best-subspace risk \(\varepsilon^2\). The normalized error converges to a Pareto-tail law with exact moments \(2/(2-p)\) for \(0<p<2\), exposing a sharp gap between high-probability accuracy and mean-square performance.
