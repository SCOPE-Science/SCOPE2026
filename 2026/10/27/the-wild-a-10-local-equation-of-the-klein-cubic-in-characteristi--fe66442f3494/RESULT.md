# The wild \(A_{10}\) local equation of the Klein cubic in characteristic eleven

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(11\). For the projective Klein cubic threefold
\[
X=V\!\left(\sum_{i\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z}x_i^2x_{i+1}\right)\subset\mathbb P^4_k,
\]
its only geometric singular point is \(P=[1:3:9:5:4]\). At that point its completed local hypersurface ring is isomorphic to
\[
\widehat{\mathcal O}_{X,P}\simeq
 k[[u,y_1,y_2,y_3]]/(u^{11}+y_1^2+y_2^2+y_3^2).
\]
This is the characteristic-eleven (wild) \(A_{10}\) hypersurface equation, understood by the displayed formal ring rather than by a characteristic-zero singularity convention. Its intrinsic Tjurina algebra is \(k[[u]]/(u^{11})\), of length exactly \(11\). The statement is about this coefficientwise special fiber and its complete local ring, not a model with good reduction.

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is algebraically closed of characteristic \(11\); indexing of coordinates is cyclic modulo five. Formal equivalence of local hypersurface rings permits coordinate changes and multiplication of a defining equation by a unit. The Tjurina algebra is the completed local ring of the intrinsic hypersurface Jacobian (generated by the equation and its four affine partial derivatives); its dimension over \(k\) is the Tjurina length. All local calculations use the chart \(x_0=1\), with \((a,b,c,d)=(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)\).

## Proof
Put
\[
f(a,b,c,d)=a+a^2b+b^2c+c^2d+d^2.
\]
The affine partial derivatives in the indicated order are
\[
 f_a=1+2ab,\quad f_b=a^2+2bc,\quad
 f_c=b^2+2cd,\quad f_d=c^2+2d.
\]
No projective singular point has a zero coordinate: if \(x_i=0\), the homogeneous partial derivative in coordinate \(x_i\) forces \(x_{i-1}^2=0\), and induction forces all five coordinates to vanish. Thus all singular points occur in the displayed chart with \(a\ne0\).

The first three affine gradient equations give, successively and **exactly**,
\[
 b=-\frac1{2a},\qquad c=a^3,\qquad d=-\frac1{8a^5}.
\]
The fourth becomes
\[
 f_d=a^6-\frac1{4a^5}=\frac{4a^{11}-1}{4a^5}.
\]
In characteristic \(11\), \(4^{-1}=3\), so its zero scheme on this chart is \(a^{11}-3=0\). Frobenius gives \(a^{11}-3=(a-3)^{11}\). Substitution yields \(b=9,c=5,d=4\) at the reduced point. Hence the affine gradient algebra is
\[
k[a,b,c,d]/(f_a,f_b,f_c,f_d)\simeq k[a,a^{-1}]/((a-3)^{11}),
\]
of length \(11\), supported at \(P\). To incorporate the defining equation, substitution along the first three gradient equations gives
\[
 f\left(a,-\frac1{2a},a^3,-\frac1{8a^5}\right)
 =\frac{5a}{8}+\frac1{64a^{10}}.
\]
This is zero modulo \(a^{11}-3\), since it equals \(2a+5a^{-10}\) in characteristic \(11\) and \(2a^{11}+5=11=0\) in the quotient. Thus including \(f\) does not change the length-\(11\) gradient algebra, proving the claimed Tjurina length.

It remains to establish the formal local *equation*, not just a numerical invariant. On the critical curve \(f_a=f_b=f_c=0\), the parameter \(a\) gives the formulas above, and \(d'(a)=5/(8a^6)\) is nonzero at \(a=3\). Hence this curve can be parametrized locally by the fourth coordinate \(d\). The \(3\times3\) Hessian block of \(f\) in the transverse variables \((a,b,c)\), evaluated at \(P\), is
\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}7&6&0\\6&10&7\\0&7&8\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det H=6\ne0\ \text{in }k.
\]
The formal Morse lemma with parameter \(d\) (valid because \(2\) is invertible and \(H\) is nonsingular) therefore gives a local equation \(Q(y_1,y_2,y_3)+g(u)\), where \(Q\) is nondegenerate and \(g\) is the restriction of \(f\) to the critical curve, in any formal parameter \(u\) on that curve. Choose \(a=3(1+u)\). As \(3^{10}=1\) in \(k\), direct substitution gives the exact identity
\[
 g(u)=6(1+u)+\frac{5}{(1+u)^{10}}
 =6u^{11}\frac{1+u}{1+u^{11}}.
\]
The last factor is a unit in \(k[[u]]\), with constant term \(6\). Over the algebraically closed field \(k\), diagonalize \(Q\) and, after multiplying the equation by that unit's inverse and rescaling the transverse variables by its square root, obtain \(u^{11}+y_1^2+y_2^2+y_3^2\). This proves the displayed completed-local-ring isomorphism. Its Tjurina quotient sets the three \(y_i\) to zero (as \(2\) is invertible); the derivative of \(u^{11}\) vanishes in characteristic \(11\), and the equation then imposes \(u^{11}=0\). The Tjurina length is exactly \(11\), agreeing with the original coordinate calculation.

## Relationship to prior work
Roulleau's study of the Klein cubic and its Fano surface establishes the classical cyclic model. Debarre–Laface–Roulleau, *Lines on cubic hypersurfaces over finite fields*, Section 4.5.2, explicitly identify the unique characteristic-\(11\) singular point, so **that identification is not new here**. The same section labels its type \(A_2\) without an accompanying local analytic normal-form calculation. In Section 4.4, footnote 4, that paper explicitly defines \(A_j\) by the local analytic equation \(t^{j+1}\) plus a nondegenerate sum of squares. Thus its \(A_2\) label would imply Tjurina length \(2\) in characteristic \(11\), whereas the exact gradient quotient and formal equation above give length \(11\). The new assertion is the explicit completed local ring and intrinsic length, not the uniqueness of the singular point. The contrast with the published label is flagged as a source-comparison issue, not presented as independently adjudicated historical provenance.

General classification literature on hypersurface singularities in positive characteristic supplies context for the characteristic-dependent distinction between contact and right equivalence; it does not by itself evaluate this particular cubic. The cubic's exceptional prime is natural: away from characteristic \(3\), the product of its five homogeneous gradient equations forces \(2^5=-1\), so characteristic \(11\) is the only possible singular reduction with \(3\) invertible.

## Limitations
This does not classify the Fano scheme of lines, the global resolution, or a semistable integral model. The computed local ring establishes contact equivalence, not a separate right-equivalence assertion about the original cubic polynomial. The characteristic-\(11\) description conflicts with the brief \(A_2\) label in a highly relevant earlier publication; that source comparison is a material risk requiring independent specialist assessment. No exhaustive search of historical modular singularity tables is claimed.
