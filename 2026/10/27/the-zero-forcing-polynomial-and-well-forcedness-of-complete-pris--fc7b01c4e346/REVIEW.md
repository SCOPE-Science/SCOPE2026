# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For each initially blue clique vertex, the count of white clique neighbors is exactly the number of white vertices on its own layer, with at most one additional matched white neighbor. The case analysis distinguishes all possibilities and proves the full polynomial. Every successful configuration contains an explicit size-\(n\) successful subset, establishing the well-forced statement. Independent direct rule enumeration over all subsets for \(3\le n\le8\) reproduces every polynomial coefficient and the inclusion-minimal census. Boundary scope is \(n\ge3\); finite checks are not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to priority uncertainty
Boyer et al. (arXiv:1801.08910) introduce the polynomial; Pelayo–Abara (arXiv:2202.04997) prove graph-product failed-number results; Grood et al. (arXiv:2312.14298) define well-forcedness and primarily classify trees. None of these primary sources states the all-cardinality matched-clique-prism formula. A pre-existing complete classification of minimal forts of rook graphs is closely relevant: via fort-transversal duality, it determines zero-forcing feasibility in principle, but a separate enumeration of every successful subset is needed to establish this polynomial and the well-forced census. The accessible indexed searches found no prior equivalent counting statement. Unindexed literature and this strong fort-based overlap are explicit residual risks.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The closed formula enumerates all forcing initial configurations, proves all inclusion-minimal forcing sets have minimum size and counts them, and makes an exact, asymptotically decaying fair-random success probability available. These are natural structural questions that scalar zero-forcing or failed-zero-forcing numbers do not settle.
