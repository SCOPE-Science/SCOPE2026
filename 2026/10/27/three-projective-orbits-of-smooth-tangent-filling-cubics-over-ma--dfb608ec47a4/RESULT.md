# Three projective orbits of smooth tangent-filling cubics over \(\mathbf F_3\)

## Finding

Call a plane curve over \(\mathbf F_3\) **tangent-filling** if the union of the rational tangent lines at its smooth rational points equals \(\mathbf P^2(\mathbf F_3)\). Exactly three \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_3)\)-orbits of **geometrically smooth plane cubic curves** are tangent-filling. Representatives, with homogeneous coordinates \([X:Y:Z]\), are
\[
\begin{aligned}
f_{(4)}&=YZ^2-Y^3+X^2Z,\\
f_{(7)}&=YZ^2-Y^3+XZ^2+X^2Z,\\
f_{(6)}&=YZ^2-Y^2Z+XY^2+X^2Z.
\end{aligned}
\]
The subscripts give the respective numbers of rational points, \(4,7,6\). Their projective equation-orbit sizes are, respectively, \(936,936,1872\), and their projective stabilizer orders are \(6,6,3\). Therefore there are exactly \(3744\) scalar-normalized projective cubic equations, or \(7488\) nonzero coefficient vectors, defining smooth tangent-filling cubics in fixed coordinates.

These three configurations have distinct rational tangent-line incidence types. Writing \(m(R)\) for the number of distinct rational tangent lines through \(R\in\mathbf P^2(\mathbf F_3)\), the distributions of \(m(R)\) are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\#C(\mathbf F_3)& \#\{R:m(R)=j\}\text{ for nonzero }j\\\hline
4&(j=1:12,\ j=4:1)\\
7&(j=1:2,\ j=2:9,\ j=4:2)\\
6&(j=1:6,\ j=2:3,\ j=3:4).
\end{array}
\]
All three smooth tangent-filling orbits are **nonblocking** in the original plane: a rational line disjoint from the rational curve exists. The respective numbers of such disjoint lines are \(2,1,1\).

In particular, the minimum degree of an **irreducible tangent-filling plane curve over \(\mathbf F_3\)** is exactly \(3\), answering the \(q=3\) instance of Question 1.5 of Asgarli--Ghioca (2023). The claim is about this field alone, not a uniform bound for general \(q\).

## Assumptions and scope

Everything is over the prime field \(k=\mathbf F_3\). Projective equivalence means the action of \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(k)\) by linear coordinate substitutions; scalar-proportional equations describe the same embedded curve. Geometric smoothness is over an algebraic closure, not just at rational points. The tangent at a smooth point \(P\) of \(F=0\) is the line with coefficient vector \(\nabla F(P)\), up to nonzero scalar. For the census, all ten coefficients of a homogeneous ternary cubic run independently over \(k\), excluding the zero form.

## Proof

**Complete incidence sieve.** There are \(3^{10}-1=59048\) nonzero ternary cubic coefficient vectors. Enumerate the \(13\) projective points \(P\in\mathbf P^2(k)\) by normalizing their first nonzero coordinate to \(1\). Fix the monomial order
\[
(Z^3,YZ^2,Y^2Z,Y^3,XZ^2,XYZ,XY^2,X^2Z,X^2Y,X^3).
\]
For each vector \(c\in k^{10}\setminus\{0\}\), form its polynomial \(F_c\). For each of the thirteen rational points on \(F_c=0\), evaluate the three partial derivatives. Reject the vector if a rational singular point occurs. For each remaining rational curve point, normalize its nonzero gradient to a dual projective point (a tangent line), then take the union of its four rational points. Accept precisely when that union consists of all thirteen points. This tests the definition directly, using exact residues modulo \(3\), without any geometric-smoothness shortcut.

The exhaustive loop returns \(35388\) forms smooth at every rational point, and among these \(7488\) whose rational tangents cover the plane. By rational-point count the tangent-filling forms number \(1872\) with four points, \(3744\) with six points, and \(1872\) with seven points. Independent implementations in `verify_incidence.cpp` and `verify_numpy.py` agree on all \(7488\) coefficient codes; the enumeration file `candidate_codes.txt` lists exactly those codes in increasing order. In this calculation, a code is \(\sum_{i=0}^{9}c_i3^i\) in the displayed monomial order, so no random sampling occurs.

**Projective-orbit exhaustion.** Normalize each accepted coefficient vector by multiplying it by \(1\) or \(2\) until its first nonzero coefficient is \(1\). This produces exactly \(3744\) distinct equations. Use coordinate substitutions generated by
\[
\begin{gathered}
(X,Y,Z)\mapsto(X+Y,Y,Z),\qquad (X,Y,Z)\mapsto(X,Y+Z,Z),\\
(X,Y,Z)\mapsto(Y,X,Z),\qquad (X,Y,Z)\mapsto(X,Z,Y),\\
(X,Y,Z)\mapsto(-X,Y,Z).
\end{gathered}
\]
These are elementary transvections, coordinate swaps and one diagonal scaling, and generate \(\operatorname{GL}_3(k)\) (hence the full projective action). Exact orbit-closure traversal with the induced linear substitution on the ten cubic coefficients gives three disjoint orbits, represented by integer codes \(2244,2325,2937\), with sizes \(936,936,1872\). Their sizes sum to all \(3744\) candidates; consequently **no other** rationally nonsingular tangent-filling cubic remains. Since
\[
|\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_3)|
=\frac{(27-1)(27-3)(27-9)}{2}=5616,
\]
the corresponding stabilizer orders are \(6,6,3\). `verify_orbits.py` independently implements the substitution algebra and breadth-first orbit closures; it asserts closure, disjointness, and complete coverage of the input set. The three representative coefficient lists are printed with the output.

**Geometric smoothness of each representative.** For the first two forms, \(\partial f/\partial Y=Z^2\). A hypothetical geometric singularity therefore has \(Z=0\), and \(\partial f/\partial Z=X^2\) forces \(X=0\); however \(f(0,Y,0)=-Y^3\ne0\) at any projective point. Thus both are smooth over \(\overline k\).

For \(f_{(6)}\), the derivatives in characteristic three are
\[
(f_X,f_Y,f_Z)=(Y^2-XZ,\ Z^2+YZ-XY,\ X^2-YZ-Y^2).
\]
If a singular point had \(Z=0\), the first and third derivatives would force \(Y=X=0\), impossible. Thus set \(Z=1\). The first two derivatives imply \(X=Y^2\) and \(Y^3=Y+1\). Substituting into the equation gives
\[
f_{(6)}(Y^2,Y,1)=Y-Y^2+2Y^4=Y^2
\]
using \(Y^4=Y^2+Y\). A singularity on the curve would force \(Y=0\), contrary to \(Y^3=Y+1\). Hence this representative is geometrically smooth too. Smoothness is invariant under projective substitutions, proving that every enumerated candidate is smooth; no inference from tests over finitely many extension fields is needed.

**Sharp minimum degree.** A line has only itself as its tangent and cannot be tangent-filling. A smooth conic over \(k\) has four rational points and four distinct rational tangent lines; its dual rational points form a four-point conic, no three collinear. Such a four-point set cannot meet every dual line: counting incidences of the four dual points gives \(4\cdot4-\binom42=10<13\) distinct incident lines. Thus the tangent lines do not cover the original plane. A singular irreducible conic over \(k\) consists geometrically of two conjugate lines, whose only rational intersection is singular; it has no smooth rational point from which to generate a tangent. Therefore irreducible tangent-filling curves have degree at least \(3\). The explicit smooth cubic representatives attain degree \(3\).

**Incidence refinements.** Direct substitution at all thirteen rational projective points computes the rational point sets of the three representatives, their distinct tangent lines, and the tangent multiplicity distributions in the Finding. The three tangent-line sets have respectively one complete four-line pencil; two complete four-line pencils sharing one line; and a six-line dual blocking set with no complete rational pencil. Checking the original curve-point sets against the thirteen rational lines produces the disjoint-line counts \(2,1,1\). All these finite incidence statements use the same exact point/line definitions and can be reproduced from the displayed equations.

## Verification

Compile and run `verify_incidence.cpp` with a C++17 compiler: `g++ -O3 -std=c++17 verify_incidence.cpp -o incidence && ./incidence > incidence.out`. This writes `candidate_codes.txt` and reports \(35388\) rationally nonsingular forms and \(7488\) tangent-filling forms. Run `python3 verify_orbits.py > orbits.out` to certify three orbits and the representatives. As an independent polynomial-evaluation implementation, run `python3 verify_numpy.py > numpy.out` (NumPy needed). The packaged `incidence.out`, `orbits.out` and `numpy.out` record the observed reference outputs. The three explicit geometric-smoothness arguments above do not rely on software, while the finite exhaustive orbit and incidence counts do rely on the fully disclosed deterministic programs. `candidate_codes.txt` is an included complete intermediate certificate; its derivation is reproducible by the C++ checker.

## Relationship to prior work

Asgarli and Ghioca introduced tangent-filling curves, gave uniform nonexistence results in sufficiently large characteristic/field size, a square-field Hermitian construction, and posed their minimum-degree question (arXiv:2302.13420, Question 1.5). Neither their large-field exclusions nor their constructions with other field assumptions imply the three-orbit classification for \(\mathbf F_3\). The present result answers their question for exactly this field and supplies an exhaustive embedded cubic classification.

Asgarli, Ghioca and Yip (arXiv:2208.13299) studied **blocking by curve points**, including explicit small-field smooth cubics. That property differs from blocking in the **dual plane by rational tangent lines**. In particular, each of the three cubics above is nonblocking as a set of original curve points, so the blocking-cubic constructions do not subsume the present characterization. Complete historical tables of elliptic curves over tiny fields could potentially permit a secondary reconstruction of the answer, but no examined source states the three-orbit tangent-filling theorem with its dual incidence spectra.

## Limitations

The claim is restricted to the prime field \(\mathbf F_3\), smooth embedded plane cubics, and equivalence under rational **projective** transformations. It does not enumerate arbitrary genus-one models, prove an analogous result over extension fields, or determine all higher-degree tangent-filling curves. The orbit counts depend on reproducible finite enumeration; they are not consequences of a uniform symbolic classification for all finite fields. Publication priority against unindexed computational databases or historical small-field elliptic-curve catalogues remains uncertain; neither originality nor correctness is independently audited here.
