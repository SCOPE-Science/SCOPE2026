# Review: Uniformly randomized quasioptimal alternating projections

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact Euclidean admissibility interval gives independent scalar gains at every projection. Their exact second and general positive moments yield the moment criterion; integrable logarithmic gains yield the almost-sure criterion via the strong law. A standard iterated-logarithm argument excludes convergence at the critical pathwise exponent. The derivative analysis proves uniqueness of its root, and strict Jensen separates it from the mean-square root. `verify.py` passes a finite exact-rational and numeric consistency test, supplemental to the proof.

**Originality — PASS, best knowledge.** The primary Budzinskiy preprint and later published full text were inspected, including the deterministic quasioptimal convergence condition and its explicitly published perpendicular-axis divergence example. Neither deterministic claim is asserted new. Targeted published-literature and published-finding corpus searches did not identify a uniform-arclength-random selection analysis giving the angle-independent mean-square threshold, pathwise Lyapunov boundary, or their strict separation. A search is not proof of global uniqueness; the abstract-only access to Diaconis–Khare–Saloff-Coste remains a specific risk.

**Value — PASS.** Different definitions of numerical stability respond sharply differently to the same randomized approximate-projection budget. The uniform mean-square tolerance \(\sigma=2\) for every line angle and the strict gap to pathwise stability are exact benchmarks for algorithms with random projection error, although the uniform-interval oracle is an idealized model rather than a claim about all implementations.

**Closest literature and scope.** Budzinskiy's original article proves deterministic convergence under geometric restrictions and already contains an orthogonal-line divergence example. Diaconis–Khare–Saloff-Coste studies iterated conditional-expectation projections, based on the publicly accessible abstract. The present theorem requires independent uniform arclength selection in the entire quasioptimal segment, equal tolerances, planar lines, and exact arithmetic. No claim is made for biased or dependent selectors, higher-dimensional sets, or random exact projection schedules.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
