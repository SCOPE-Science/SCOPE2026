# Three single-visit seasons identify stationary occupancy despite drifting detection

## Finding
Consider one species and a panel of independently replicated sites monitored in exactly three consecutive seasons, each site visited only once per season. In a stationary, homogeneous two-state Markov occupancy model with zero false-positive detections and arbitrary season-specific detection probabilities, **the three observed means and three pairwise covariances uniquely determine all five biological/observation parameters whenever the latent chain has nonzero temporal memory**. A two-season joint distribution cannot generally determine them, and no number of seasons resolves the ambiguity when temporal memory is zero and detection remains unrestricted. Three seasons are thus the sharp minimum for structural identification under these assumptions. There is also an exact pairwise-moment feasibility criterion and a testable equality between the two normalized adjacent covariances.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(X_0,X_1,X_2\in\{0,1\}\) be a strictly stationary Markov chain representing actual occupancy. Its colonization probability is \(\gamma=\Pr(X_{t+1}=1\mid X_t=0)\in(0,1)\), and its extinction probability is \(\varepsilon=\Pr(X_{t+1}=0\mid X_t=1)\in(0,1)\). Set stationary occupancy \(\pi=\gamma/(\gamma+\varepsilon)\in(0,1)\) and persistence eigenvalue \(\lambda=1-\gamma-\varepsilon\in(-1,1)\). Each season produces one observed binary detection \(Y_t=X_tD_t\). The \(D_t\) are independent Bernoulli variables, independent of the entire latent process, with \(d_t=\Pr(D_t=1)\in(0,1)\). Thus sensitivity may vary freely by season, but false positives are impossible. Parameters are common across sites; infinitely many independent sites or exact population moments are required for the stated *structural* inverse problem. Assume \(\lambda\ne0\).

Write \(m_t=\mathbb E[Y_t]\), \(C_{ij}=\operatorname{Cov}(Y_i,Y_j)\), and \(r_{ij}=C_{ij}/(m_im_j)\) for \(i<j\) in \(\{0,1,2\}\). Every valid model satisfies
\[
 r_{01}=r_{12}=\frac{1-\pi}{\pi}\lambda\ne0,\qquad
 r_{02}=\frac{1-\pi}{\pi}\lambda^2>0.
\]
The uniquely recovered parameters are
\[
 \lambda=\frac{r_{02}}{r_{01}},\qquad
 \pi=\frac{r_{02}}{r_{01}^2+r_{02}},\qquad
 d_t=\frac{m_t}{\pi},\qquad
 \gamma=\pi(1-\lambda),\qquad
 \varepsilon=(1-\pi)(1-\lambda).
\]
Conversely, six proposed moments \((m_0,m_1,m_2,C_{01},C_{12},C_{02})\) arise from *some* model in the stated class with nonzero memory if and only if \(m_t>0\), \(r_{01}=r_{12}\ne0\), \(r_{02}>0\), and the reconstructed \(\pi,d_0,d_1,d_2,\gamma,\varepsilon\) all lie strictly between zero and one. This is an exact pairwise-moment compatibility test, not a characterization of an arbitrary supplied eight-cell observed joint distribution.

## Proof
**Covariance identity.** Conditional expectation under the stationary chain gives \(\mathbb E[X_{t+k}-\pi\mid X_t]=\lambda^k(X_t-\pi)\). Therefore for \(k\ge1\), \(\operatorname{Cov}(X_t,X_{t+k})=\pi(1-\pi)\lambda^k\). The independence of the season-specific detection coins yields \(m_t=\pi d_t\) and \(C_{ij}=d_id_j\pi(1-\pi)\lambda^{j-i}\). Dividing by positive observed means cancels *both* unknown detection probabilities and gives the displayed normalized covariance identity.

**Reconstruction and uniqueness.** From \(r_{01}=a\lambda\) and \(r_{02}=a\lambda^2\), where \(a=(1-\pi)/\pi>0\) and \(\lambda\ne0\), it follows that \(\lambda=r_{02}/r_{01}\) and \(a=r_{01}^2/r_{02}\). Hence \(\pi=(1+a)^{-1}=r_{02}/(r_{01}^2+r_{02})\), giving every detection and transition parameter by the formulas above. All quantities are uniquely forced. For sufficiency, assume the stated moment inequalities and reconstructed parameter ranges. Build the stationary two-state chain from \(\gamma,\varepsilon\) and independent detection variables with probabilities \(d_t\). The covariance identity proves that this model has exactly the proposed three means and three covariances.

**Sharp two-season boundary.** Two distinct interior models give precisely the same *full two-season observed distribution*: \((\pi,\lambda,d_0,d_1)=(2/5,1/3,1/2,3/4)\) and \((1/2,1/2,2/5,3/5)\). Both yield, respectively in the order observed outcomes \((0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\), the probabilities \((59/100,21/100,11/100,9/100)\). Their transition parameters differ, since \((\gamma,\varepsilon)=(4/15,2/5)\) in the first model and \((1/4,1/4)\) in the second. Thus a third season is genuinely necessary for identification from unrestricted seasonal detection without covariates or within-season replication.

**Zero-memory boundary.** If \(\lambda=0\), the latent states become independent stationary Bernoulli variables, so the observations are independent with means \(m_t=\pi d_t\). For any finite or infinite set of seasons whose detection probabilities are arbitrary and satisfy \(\sup_t m_t<1\), every \(\pi\in(\sup_t m_t,1)\) with \(d_t=m_t/\pi\) generates the same complete joint observation law. No finite number of seasons can remove this ambiguity in the unrestricted detection model. The exact three-season certificate also gives a concrete pair of such models.

**False-positive boundary.** The no-false-positive assumption is necessary for this six-moment recovery. Let an alternative observation law have false-positive rates \(f_t\) and sensitivities \(s_t\), with \(\mathbb E[Y_t\mid X_t]=f_t+(s_t-f_t)X_t\). This implies \(m_t=f_t+(s_t-f_t)\pi\) and \(C_{ij}=(s_i-f_i)(s_j-f_j)\pi(1-\pi)\lambda^{j-i}\). Compare the zero-false-positive model \(\pi=1/2,\lambda=1/3,(d_0,d_1,d_2)=(1/2,3/4,2/3)\) with the second model \(\pi'=9/25,\lambda'=1/3,f_t=d_t/8,s_t=7d_t/6\). All sensitivities and false-positive rates are strictly between zero and one. The two models share the three means \((1/4,3/8,1/3)\) and the three covariances \((C_{01},C_{02},C_{12})=(1/32,1/108,1/24)\), but have different occupancy. Their full three-way laws need not agree: for instance, \(\Pr(Y_0=Y_1=Y_2=1)\) is respectively \(1/18\) and \(199/3456\). This establishes pairwise-moment, *not full-joint-law*, nonidentifiability when false positives are admitted.

**Near-independence sensitivity.** Writing \(A=r_{01}\), \(B=r_{02}\), the inverse prevalence formula has partial derivatives \(\partial\pi/\partial B=A^2/(A^2+B)^2=\pi^2/\lambda^2\) and \(\partial\pi/\partial A=-2AB/(A^2+B)^2=-2\pi^2/\lambda\), with the other normalized moment held fixed. Hence the inverse can be highly sensitive as \(\lambda\to0\). This is a *local algebraic sensitivity statement*, not a finite-sample error guarantee.

## Relationship to prior work
MacKenzie and collaborators (2003) developed dynamic site-occupancy analysis allowing imperfect detection using within-season replication. Later single-visit methods by Peach, Cohen and Frair (2017) exploited observation-process covariates and survey effort, while Doser and Stoudt (2024, publicly available online in 2023) evaluated identifiability and practical risks of fractional replication in multi-season models. These sources motivate the *absence* of within-season replication; this theorem instead imposes stationarity and a common Markov transition to recover the hidden quantities from only three seasonal single visits despite unrestricted season-specific sensitivities. The two approaches are not interchangeable under assumption violations. Huang, Fu and Sidiropoulos (2018) studied pairwise co-occurrence recovery of stationary *homogeneous-emission* hidden Markov models under sufficiently scattered emissions; the varying emissions and constrained no-false-positive observation channel here are different. None of the specifically inspected results was found to imply the exact three-season, pairwise-only inverse, its feasibility criterion, and the sharp failure modes. An equivalent parameterization in older hidden-Markov literature cannot be ruled out.

## Limitations
The theorem assumes exact population moments, common across-site parameters, strict stationarity, homogeneous colonization/extinction rates, conditionally independent observation coins, zero false positives and nonzero temporal memory. It is not a finite-sample estimator guarantee, and the inverse is unstable near zero memory. With transient initial occupancy, heterogeneous site parameters, correlated observation errors or false positives, the formulas can fail. The reviewed 2003 foundational paper was accessible only at abstract level and was not certified as non-covering in its full text. Independent bibliographic and scientific review has not been performed.
