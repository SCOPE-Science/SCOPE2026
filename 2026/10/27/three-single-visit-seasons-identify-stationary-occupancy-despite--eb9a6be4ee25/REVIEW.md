# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The stationary binary chain identity \(\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j)=\pi(1-\pi)\lambda^{j-i}\) follows from the conditional expectation eigenvalue. Independent season-specific detections rescale each cross-covariance by \(d_id_j\). Normalizing by observed means cancels the unknown sensitivities, yielding \(r_{01}=r_{12}=a\lambda\) and \(r_{02}=a\lambda^2\). Nonzero memory then forces the unique reconstructed prevalence, memory, three sensitivities and transition probabilities. The converse constructs the corresponding stationary chain when all stated strict probability bounds hold. Rational enumeration independently confirms three inversions, both complete two-season law collisions, complete zero-memory observational collision, and false-positive six-moment collision. Two-season and zero-memory counterexamples concern full observable joint laws; the false-positive counterexample is restricted to six moments. Infinite claims use algebra, not finite verification.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
MacKenzie et al. (2003, DOI 10.1890/02-3090) founded dynamic imperfect-detection occupancy inference using within-season replication; its full text could not be inspected, so the abstract is not treated as conclusive noncoverage. Peach, Cohen and Frair (2017, DOI 10.1111/1365-2664.12925) offer single-visit inference based on survey-effort/detection covariates and a distinct dynamic design. Doser and Stoudt (2024, DOI 10.1111/2041-210X.14275) examine fractional replication and weak practical identifiability for spatiotemporal occupancy effects. Huang, Fu and Sidiropoulos (2018, arXiv:1802.06894) prove stationary homogeneous-emission HMM recovery under sufficient scattering, not the same freely time-varying emission model. Published mathematical-research record searches found models of two-visit N-mixtures and perfectly observed aggregated occupancy but no statement implying the three-season second-moment inverse. The generic Markov covariance identity is not claimed as new. A hidden equivalent in the older HMM/capture-recapture literature remains possible and independent bibliographic confirmation is outstanding.

## Value — PASS
The theorem gives a sharply minimal single-visit design, an explicit five-parameter inverse, a falsifiable cross-season normalized-covariance equality, necessary-and-sufficient moment feasibility inequalities, and concrete boundaries under zero memory or false positives. This concerns a natural gap between perfect detection and unattainable repeated field visits. It does not assert high finite-sample precision: sensitivity worsens sharply near zero memory and all biological/measurement assumptions must hold.

## Limitations
Exactly three seasons and exact population moments are the structural setting. Assumptions: independent replicated sites, stationary homogeneous transitions, no false positives, no temporal detection dependence conditional on hidden states, common latent and detection parameters across sites, nonzero memory. The source dates do not certify publication of this result. No independent scientific audit was conducted.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
