# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The six plane quartics are smooth because the branch quartic has simple roots and the unique point at infinity has nonzero \(Z\)-derivative. The published classification shows the unique rank-deficient geometric cyclic-six class at \(p=13\), and explicit multinomial evaluation reproduces Hasse–Witt diagonal \((0,h_A,0)\) with \(h_A\ne0\) for each model. The verifier exhaustively counts \(13^m\) affine coordinate values for \(m=1,2,3\) and adds the unique point at infinity. Newton identities and the Weil functional equation yield all three full zeta numerators; proof extends to all \(m\ge1\). The six distinct forms follow from the full geometric automorphism group \(\mu_6\) and the finite-field Kummer twist classification.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Ohashi–Kudo–Harashita (arXiv:2111.09777, Theorems A/B) cover the exceptional geometric class and its Hasse–Witt type; these are not claimed as new. Laga–Shnidman (arXiv:2308.15297, §2.4) cover the general six-fold sextic twist classification; that is not claimed as new either. Meagher–Top (2010) treat explicit quartics with automorphism groups of order at least sixteen; their general twist method applies but their worked spectra are not for these six-automorphism models. The novel claim is the exact correspondence assigning **three** Weil polynomials to all **six** forms, two per polynomial. No matching assignment was identified in inspected literature and mathematical claim searches. A previously unindexed finite-field table or class-specific computation cannot be ruled out; the individual isogeny polynomials may have entries in broader databases without model identification.

**Value — PASS.** The exceptional geometric \(a\)-number-two locus is a unique curve at the smallest prime \(p\equiv1\pmod6\) for which it occurs. A complete classification of its six arithmetic descents, including Frobenius isogeny types and the zeta-invisible quadratic twist distinction, is a natural finite-field invariant not fixed by the prior geometric classification, \(p\)-rank or Newton polygon. The exact zetas distinguish an elliptic-square Prym and quartic simple Prym isogeny types. The claim is precise and self-contained; no general-prime classification is asserted.

**Scope and risks.** The models are fixed over \(\mathbb F_{13}\), and the results concern curve isomorphisms and unpolarized Jacobian isogenies over the ground field, not geometric uniqueness beyond the published theorem, nor classification of principal polarizations. The main residual originality risk is an unindexed earlier computation for this exact curve, not missing correctness evidence. Finite enumeration is complete for three extension degrees only; the general point-count conclusion additionally depends on the Weil functional equation.
