For a single perfect vaccine on a path with independent-edge Reed–Frost transmission, the exact optimal location for limiting outbreaks of at least \(s\) cases is generally \(s-1\) unvaccinated vertices from an end, whereas minimizing the expected outbreak size requires vaccinating the center. The complete threshold classification holds at every transmission probability \(0<p<1\); already on five vertices the two objectives have disjoint optimal placements and no first-order stochastically best placement exists.
