# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

For every pair of positive integers \(m,n\) with \(N=m+n\ge3\), the two-particle symmetric simple exclusion process on \(K_{m,n}\) has the stated three-class lumped generator and uniform stationary measure. The tilted generator for every successful jump is \(Q_z=e^zW-\Lambda\). Its second eigenvalue derivative is the stationary mean escape rate plus twice the reversible Poisson-equation energy. Explicit solution of that equation yields the stated rational \(F_{m,n}\). Positivity follows from its two-square Dirichlet form; the only exact Poisson case is \(K_{1,3}\), with constant jump intensity two. The sign of \(N^2-6N+4\) gives the topology-order reversal between five and six vertices. Full-configuration calculations for 35 graph parameters, two exact symbolic algebra checks and 28 finite shape-comparison tests passed; general claims are analytical.

## Originality — PASS within inspected sources

The established general counting-generator methods are not claimed as novel. Appert-Rolland et al. (arXiv:0804.2590, Sec. II) develop cumulants of exclusion processes on rings. Mendonça (arXiv:1207.4106, Sec. 4) resolves the unmarked exclusion-spectrum on complete bipartite graphs, not the successful-hop Fano ratio and its shape ordering. Vanicat et al. (arXiv:2011.02202, Secs. 2–4) work with current/activity correspondences for one-dimensional exclusion models. A covered \(K_{2,2}\) special case is acknowledged. Actual searches examined the bipartite hop-count variance, exact stationary Fano ratios, Poisson exception and topology reversal under alternate formulations. No exact all-size result was identified. This conclusion is best-knowledge originality within the inspected literature, not a claim of exhaustive priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result evaluates a natural dynamical-noise statistic for a broad infinite family of finite exclusion networks, rather than just rederiving a stationary measure or a known benchmark. The sharp change from stars minimizing at orders four and five to maximizing from six, together with a unique exact Poisson graph, gives a structural and testable distinction between hub-dominated and balanced transport networks. It provides exact finite-graph benchmarks for activity fluctuations.

## Sources and residual limitations

Primary sources: arXiv:0804.2590v1 (first public 2008-04-16), arXiv:1207.4106, and arXiv:2011.02202. The specific graph and jump-count observable could conceivably be covered by an unlocated specialization of a more general activity-cumulant result; searches and inspected full text do not establish global uniqueness. Only stationary, unit-rate, two-particle exclusion and all successful hops on connected complete bipartite graphs are asserted. Experimental checks are finite corroboration, not a proof for arbitrary parameters.
