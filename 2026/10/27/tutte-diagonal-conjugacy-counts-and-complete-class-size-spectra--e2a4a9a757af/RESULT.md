# Tutte-diagonal conjugacy counts and complete class-size spectra for square-zero quiver unit groups

## Finding
Let \(Q=(V,E)\) be an arbitrary finite directed multigraph with \(n=|V|\ge 1\) vertices and \(m=|E|\) arrows; loops and parallel arrows are permitted. Fix any prime-power \(q\), and let \(A_Q=\mathbb F_qQ/J^2\), with \(J\) the ideal generated by the arrows. The group of units is \(G_Q=A_Q^\times\). Write \(M\) for the cycle matroid of the *underlying undirected multigraph*, including loops, and put \(c=n-r_M(E)\). Thus isolated vertices count toward \(c\).

For each flat \(H\) of \(M\), write \(\chi_{M/H}(z)\) for the characteristic polynomial of the contraction and \(r(S)=r_M(S)\). (A flat contains every loop and is closed under adding edges whose endpoints are already connected within it.) Define the two-parameter conjugacy-class enumerator by attaching exponents \((a,b)\) to each conjugacy class \(C\), where its size is \(q^a(q-1)^b\), and where the exponents are the canonical graph strata constructed in the proof. Then

\[
\boxed{\mathcal K_Q^{(q)}(U,W)=
(q-1)^c\sum_{H\in\operatorname{Flats}(M)}
\chi_{M/H}(q-1)U^{m-|H|}
\sum_{S\subseteq H}(q-1)^{|S|-r(S)}W^{r(S)}.}
\tag{1}
\]

For a numerical conjugacy-class size \(s\), the number of classes of that size is obtained by summing coefficients of the monomials \(U^aW^b\) in (1) satisfying \(s=q^a(q-1)^b\); for \(q=2\), distinct \(W\)-degrees may describe the same size and must be merged. All coefficients are nonnegative integers, including when characteristic two occurs.

Most strikingly, the **total** number of conjugacy classes has the compact formula

\[
\boxed{k(G_Q)=(q-1)^c\,T_M(q,q),}
\tag{2}
\]

where \(T_M\) is the standard Tutte polynomial of the graphic matroid. Both the complete class-size spectrum (1) and its total (2) depend only on \(M\) and \(c\), not on arrow orientations or the positions of isolated vertices. In particular, for a forest of \(m\) nonloop arrows and \(c\) components, \(k(G_Q)=(q-1)^c q^m\). Loops contribute central additive direct factors; their effect is included without any special convention in (1)–(2).

## Assumptions and scope
The coefficient field is finite of arbitrary prime-power order. The quiver may be disconnected, with directed cycles, reversed arrows, multiple parallel arrows and loops. The path algebra is **truncated after paths of length one**, so that the radical is square zero. The group considered is the full group of algebra units, not only the principal unipotent subgroup and not a general parabolic matrix group. No acyclicity or characteristic restriction is used.

For an edge subset \(S\), \(r(S)=n-c(V,S)\), with loops ignored by connectivity and with isolated vertices counted. The characteristic polynomial of a loopless matroid \(N\) of rank \(r_N\) is defined as \(\chi_N(z)=\sum_{F\subseteq E(N)}(-1)^{|F|}z^{r_N-r_N(F)}\). The contraction \(M/H\) is loopless whenever \(H\) is a flat; consequently this definition applies in (1), even if \(M\) itself has loops. The Tutte polynomial is defined by \(T_M(x,y)=\sum_{S\subseteq E}(x-1)^{r(E)-r(S)}(y-1)^{|S|-r(S)}\).

## Proof
For each vertex \(i\), let \(e_i\) be its trivial path; for each arrow \(a:i\to j\), let \(z_a\) be its basis arrow. The multiplication relations are \(e_ie_j=\delta_{ij}e_i\), \(e_i z_a=\delta_{i,s(a)}z_a\), \(z_a e_j=\delta_{j,t(a)}z_a\), and \(z_az_b=0\). Every unit is uniquely \(g=(d,x)\), with \(d=(d_i)\in(\mathbb F_q^\times)^V\) and \(x=(x_a)\in\mathbb F_q^E\). Hence \(|G_Q|=(q-1)^n q^m\). Multiplication is

\[
(d,x)(b,y)=\left((d_ib_i)_i,\; (d_{s(a)}y_a+x_ab_{t(a)})_a\right).
\tag{3}
\]

**Centralizers and individual class sizes.** Equation (3) gives \(gh=hg\) precisely when

\[
(d_{s(a)}-d_{t(a)})y_a=(b_{s(a)}-b_{t(a)})x_a
\quad\text{for every arrow }a.
\tag{4}
\]

For a fixed \(g=(d,x)\), put \(H=H(d)=\{a:d_{s(a)}=d_{t(a)}\}\). For each \(a\notin H\), equation (4) determines \(y_a\) uniquely once \(b\) is chosen. For each \(a\in H\), the value \(y_a\) is free, while nonzero \(x_a\) forces equality \(b_{s(a)}=b_{t(a)}\). Set \(S=\{a\in H:x_a\ne0\}\). Thus \(b\) must be constant on connected components of \((V,S)\), so there are exactly \((q-1)^{n-r(S)}\) choices of \(b\); there are \(q^{|H|}\) choices of \(y\). Therefore

\[
|C_{G_Q}(g)|=q^{|H|}(q-1)^{n-r(S)},\qquad
|g^{G_Q}|=q^{m-|H|}(q-1)^{r(S)}.
\tag{5}
\]

A conjugate of \(g\) keeps its vertex-diagonal \(d\). On the arrows of \(H(d)\), diagonal conjugation only rescales coordinates by nonzero factors, and conjugation by the additive arrow subgroup leaves these coordinates unchanged. Hence \(S\) is constant on the conjugacy class, and the two exponents in (5) are intrinsically defined by that class.

For a fixed \(d\) and fixed support \(S\subseteq H(d)\), there are exactly \((q-1)^{|S|}q^{m-|H(d)|}\) elements with this support: coordinates on \(S\) are nonzero, on \(H(d)\setminus S\) zero, and off \(H(d)\) arbitrary. By (5), their number of conjugacy classes is

\[
(q-1)^{|S|-r(S)}.
\tag{6}
\]

**Matroid-flat aggregation.** The set \(H(d)\) is a matroid flat: equality of vertex colors propagates along a path of edges in \(H(d)\), so every edge in its graphic closure is also in \(H(d)\). Conversely, the number of \(d\in(\mathbb F_q^\times)^V\) with \(H(d)=H\), for a fixed flat \(H\), is the chromatic polynomial of the contracted multigraph \(Q_{\rm undirected}/H\), evaluated at \(q-1\). Contracting \(H\) preserves the number \(c\) of components. The standard subset inclusion–exclusion definition of the chromatic polynomial gives

\[
\#\{d:H(d)=H\}=(q-1)^c\chi_{M/H}(q-1).
\tag{7}
\]

Combining (5), (6), and (7), and grouping by the pair of exponents, proves (1).

**Tutte evaluation.** There is also an independent class-number proof. The class equation/Burnside lemma gives \(k(G_Q)=\#\{(g,h):gh=hg\}/|G_Q|\). For fixed vertex diagonals \(d,b\), each arrow equation (4) has \(q^2\) solutions \((x_a,y_a)\) when \(d_{s(a)}=d_{t(a)}\) and \(b_{s(a)}=b_{t(a)}\), and \(q\) solutions otherwise. View \((d_i,b_i)\) as a vertex color chosen from a palette of size \((q-1)^2\), and let \(e_0(d,b)\) be the number of monochromatic edges. Thus

\[
k(G_Q)=(q-1)^{-n}\sum_{(d,b)}q^{e_0(d,b)}
=(q-1)^{-n}\sum_{S\subseteq E}(q-1)^{|S|}((q-1)^2)^{n-r(S)}.
\tag{8}
\]

The second equality expands \(q^{e_0}=\prod_{a\in E}(1+(q-1)\mathbf1_{\text{monochromatic }a})\) and counts colorings constant along each selected edge. The exponent of \(q-1\) in the summand of (8) simplifies to \(n+|S|-2r(S)=c+[r(E)-r(S)]+[|S|-r(S)]\). This is precisely the subset definition of \((q-1)^cT_M(q,q)\), proving (2). The argument includes loops: a loop is automatically monochromatic and gives the correct factor of \(q\).

## Verification
A standalone Python verifier, included as `verify.py`, reconstructs the algebra multiplication (3) and computes group-element centralizers by literal two-way multiplication. It checks the predicted conjugacy-class multiset against the independent centralizer enumeration, against the flat/contracted-chromatic expression, and against the Tutte subset expression. Tests cover characteristics two and three, the nonprime field \(\mathbb F_4\), isolated vertices, forests with reversed orientations, a directed cycle, parallel arrows, reverse parallel arrows and loops. The verifier prints `VERIFY_OK_QUIVER_UNIT_GROUP_CONJUGACY` only after every assertion succeeds. Finite checks do not establish the theorem for arbitrary graphs; the proof above does.

For example, with \(q=3\) and a directed triangle, the pairs (conjugacy class size, number of classes) are \((1,2),(2,6),(4,10),(9,6),(18,6)\), summing to \(30\) classes and \(216\) group elements. With \(q=3\) and a two-edge tree, the corresponding list is \((1,2),(2,4),(3,4),(4,2),(6,4),(9,2)\), summing to \(18\) classes in a group of order \(72\).

## Relationship to prior work
Evseev (2008) develops conjugacy/orbit-count reductions for general parabolic matrix groups and groups attached to preordered sets, including cases describable by quiver representations. These orbit problems are broader in some directions but the inspected results do not state or immediately imply a full class-size formula for arbitrary square-zero quiver unit groups or the Tutte-diagonal identity (2). They remain an important possible route to an alternative derivation.

Rossmann (2021) studies graph-polynomial class-size enumeration for *graphical groups* arising from class-two nilpotent commutator relations. Those groups have underlying additive coordinates and central edge commutators; the present unit groups instead have a split multiplicative vertex torus acting on an abelian arrow radical. Thus agreement of graph-indexed language does not identify the groups or transfer the cited polynomial. General literature on basic quiver algebras supplies the algebraic context.

Some specialized earlier calculations of total conjugacy-class counts for height-two incidence-algebra unit groups are compatible with (2), but such total counts do not cover (1), and the unrestricted matroid formula is the subject here. Likewise, rank distributions of inner derivations on the entire algebra do not determine group conjugacy-class sizes without the additional unit constraint and centralizer analysis (4)–(7). The contribution is the class-size spectrum, not the general semidirect-product method or standard Tutte identities.

## Limitations
The theorem does not treat quiver algebras in which length-two paths survive, nor nonbasic vertex algebras with matrix blocks, nor arbitrary local coefficient rings. Class-size enumerators distinguish conjugacy orbit sizes, not the full group isomorphism type; matroid-invariant spectra need not imply isomorphic groups. Exact historical novelty is not independently established: an equivalent formulation may exist under incidence-group, split metabelian, or torus-action terminology. The direct checks cover only small finite instances, and no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
