# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness
The statement follows from explicit multiplication in the arrow-square-zero algebra. Its units have nonzero diagonal coefficients, and commutation on each arrow is the single linear equation displayed in (4) of the result. For each fixed diagonal, edges with unequal endpoint coefficients contribute uniquely solved arrow variables, while edges with equal coefficients impose graph-component equalities precisely on the nonzero support. This gives exact centralizer orders and class sizes. A flat/chromatic aggregation yields the full spectrum; an independent commuting-pair count yields the Tutte evaluation. Loops, parallel arrows, disconnected graphs and characteristic two are included in the proof. A standalone verifier corroborates the result in finite test configurations.

## Originality
The result differs from the parabolic conjugacy reductions in Evseev (2008), which handle different matrix groups and do not give the stated matroid-flat class-size enumerator. Rossmann's (2021) graph-indexed class spectra concern class-two nilpotent graphical groups, not units of square-zero path algebras. Prior special total class counts for some incidence-algebra unit groups and adjoint-rank formulas for quiver algebras do not themselves establish the full unit-group class-size spectrum. Direct source comparisons therefore support originality, but priority remains uncertain because general orbit-counting methods or split metabelian group literature might implicitly contain the result.

## Value
The exact size-resolved conjugacy classification covers arbitrary quiver multigraphs and all finite fields. Its compact identity \\(k(G)=(q-1)^cT_M(q,q)\\) relates a group-theoretic invariant to a natural matroid polynomial and explains orientation invariance, including the forest specialization. This is more informative than a numerical sample or a renamed standard identity, and gives uniform access to every conjugacy-class multiplicity.

## Scientific limitations
The radical is required to have square zero, and the vertex semisimple quotient is a product of copies of the base field. Full group isomorphism classification and independent priority certification are not asserted.
