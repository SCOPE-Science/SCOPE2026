# Two conjugacy orbits of generating trace-zero planes in \(M_2(\mathbb F_q)\)

## Finding

Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\), \(L=\ker(\operatorname{tr}:M_2(k)\to k)\), and let \(\mathcal G_q\) consist of the two-dimensional subspaces \(U\le L\) that generate \(M_2(k)\) as a unital associative algebra. Conjugation by \(\operatorname{GL}_2(k)\) has exactly two orbits on \(\mathcal G_q\), of sizes

\[
\frac{q(q+1)}2\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{q(q-1)}2.
\]

In particular, \(|\mathcal G_q|=q^2\) for every prime power \(q\). The orbit invariant changes form in characteristic two: it is a square class of a quadratic-form value for odd \(q\), and an absolute field trace for even \(q\).

## Assumptions and scope

All vector spaces and algebras are over the finite field \(k=\mathbb F_q\). “Generated” means generated as a unital associative \(k\)-subalgebra. The group acts by simultaneous conjugation on subspaces. The result concerns only two-dimensional subspaces of the canonical trace-zero subspace \(L\), not all subspaces of \(M_2(k)\).

Use
\[
E=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
F=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
H=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad I=I_2.
\]

## Proof

### Odd characteristic

Assume \(q\) is odd. The bilinear form \(B(X,Y)=\operatorname{tr}(XY)\) is nondegenerate on \(L=\mathfrak{sl}_2(k)\). For \(N=\begin{psmallmatrix}a&b\\c&-a\end{psmallmatrix}\), put \(Q(N)=a^2+bc=-\det N\); then \(N^2=Q(N)I\). Every plane \(U\le L\) has a unique projective normal line \([N]=U^\perp\).

We first show that \(U\) generates \(M_2(k)\) if and only if \(Q(N)\ne0\). If \(Q(N)=0\), then \(N\) is a nonzero nilpotent matrix. After conjugation, \(N=E\), so \(U=N^\perp=\langle H,E\rangle\), the traceless upper-triangular plane. It generates only upper-triangular matrices.

If \(Q(N)\ne0\), choose a basis \(X,Y\) of \(U\). The identities \(\operatorname{tr}([X,Y]X)=\operatorname{tr}([X,Y]Y)=0\) show that \([X,Y]\in U^\perp=kN\). It is nonzero: the centralizer in \(L\) of a nonzero element of \(L\) is one-dimensional in odd characteristic. Since \(B(N,N)=2Q(N)\ne0\), the line \(kN\) is not contained in \(U\), so \(U\) and \([X,Y]\) span \(L\). The algebra generated by \(U\) therefore contains \(L\). The restriction of \(Q\) to \(U\) cannot vanish identically: otherwise polarization gives \(B|_U=0\), forcing \(U\subseteq U^\perp\), impossible by dimensions. Thus some \(X\in U\) has \(Q(X)\ne0\), and \(X^2=Q(X)I\) puts \(I\) in the generated algebra. It is consequently all of \(M_2(k)\).

Conjugation preserves the square class of \(Q(N)\), and scaling \(N\) changes \(Q(N)\) only by a square. If \(Q(N)\) is a square, scale to \(Q(N)=1\); the matrix is conjugate to \(H\), and its normal plane is \(\langle E,F\rangle\). If \(Q(N)\) is a nonsquare, scale to a fixed nonsquare \(\nu\); its irreducible characteristic polynomial \(t^2-\nu\) gives the representative \(N_\nu=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1\\\nu&0\end{psmallmatrix}\), whose normal plane is \(\langle H,E-\nu F\rangle\). These are exactly two orbits. The stabilizers in \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(k)\) are the normalizers of the split and nonsplit tori, of orders \(2(q-1)\) and \(2(q+1)\). Since \(|\operatorname{PGL}_2(k)|=q(q^2-1)\), the orbit sizes are \(q(q+1)/2\) and \(q(q-1)/2\).

### Characteristic two

Assume \(q=2^m\). Then \(L=\langle I,E,F\rangle\). Any plane containing \(I\) is generated by \(I\) and one matrix \(X\), so its generated algebra is the commutative algebra \(k[X]\), of dimension at most two; it is not \(M_2(k)\). Every plane avoiding \(I\) has a unique expression
\[
U_{\alpha,\beta}=\langle E+\alpha I,F+\beta I\rangle,\qquad (\alpha,\beta)\in k^2.
\]
For \(X=E+\alpha I\) and \(Y=F+\beta I\), direct multiplication gives \(XY+YX=I\) and \(\operatorname{tr}(XY)=1\). Hence the generated algebra contains \(I,X,Y,XY\); the first three span \(L\), while \(XY\notin L\), so these four matrices span \(M_2(k)\). Thus the generating planes are exactly the \(q^2\) complements to \(kI\).

Conjugation by diagonal, upper-unitriangular and lower-unitriangular matrices acts on the parameter pair by
\[
(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto(\alpha/t,t\beta),\qquad
(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto(\alpha,\beta+s+s^2\alpha),\qquad
(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto(\alpha+s+s^2\beta,\beta),
\]
where \(t\in k^\times\) and \(s\in k\). These matrices generate \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(k)\). The absolute trace \(\operatorname{Tr}_{k/\mathbb F_2}(\alpha\beta)\) is invariant: each unipotent move changes \(\alpha\beta\) by \(z+z^2\), whose absolute trace is zero, and diagonal moves leave the product unchanged.

If \(\alpha\ne0\), a diagonal move makes \(\alpha=1\), and an upper-unitriangular move changes \(\beta\) by any element of trace zero, because the image of \(s\mapsto s+s^2\) is exactly the trace-zero subspace. Therefore every trace-zero pair with \(\alpha\ne0\) is conjugate to \((1,0)\), and every trace-one pair is conjugate to \((1,\delta)\) for any fixed \(\delta\) of trace one. If \(\alpha=0\), an upper move sends \((0,\beta)\) to \((0,0)\), and a lower move then sends it to \((1,0)\). Thus there are exactly two orbits.

For trace zero, there are \(q\) pairs with \(\alpha=0\), and for each of the \(q-1\) nonzero values of \(\alpha\), exactly \(q/2\) choices of \(\beta\) give trace zero. The orbit therefore has size \(q+(q-1)q/2=q(q+1)/2\). The trace-one orbit has size \((q-1)q/2=q(q-1)/2\). Orbit-stabilizer gives stabilizer orders \(2(q-1)\) and \(2(q+1)\). This completes the proof in every characteristic.

## Verification

The accompanying standard-library script `verify.py` checks the orbit decomposition directly in \(\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_5,\mathbb F_7\) and checks the trace-parameter action in \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_4,\mathbb F_8\). It returns `VERIFY_OK`. These finite checks are sanity checks only; the proof above is uniform in \(q\).

## Relationship to prior work

Reineke proves polynomial counting for generating subspaces of matrix algebras over finite fields and gives \(s_2^{(2)}(q)=q^4\) for all two-dimensional generating subspaces of \(M_2(\mathbb F_q)\) (Theorem 3.3 and Example 3.7 of the cited preprint). That total count does not isolate the canonical trace-zero slice or classify its conjugacy orbits. The present theorem gives this exact refinement, including the characteristic-two orbit invariant.

## Limitations

The result does not classify generating subspaces of other dimensions or of \(M_n(k)\) for \(n>2\). The finite verifier is not used as proof of the all-fields statement. The literature search did not establish that no earlier equivalent formulation exists; the orbit theorem is stated with a direct proof so that any such equivalence can be checked precisely.
