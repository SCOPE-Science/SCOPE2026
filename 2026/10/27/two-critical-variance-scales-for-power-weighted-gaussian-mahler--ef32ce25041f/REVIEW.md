# Scientific review: two critical variance scales for power-weighted Gaussian Mahler means

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is fully quantified for \(n\ge1\) and \(\alpha\ge0\). The covariance identity follows by an explicit Laguerre expansion and its bivariate Gaussian generating function. Fourier Parseval isolates the first chaos exactly; summation by parts bounds the residual uniformly on the two moving-exponent windows. Weighted Riemann sums plus an integrable majorant prove the low-exponent constant, and convergence of the coefficient series proves the positive high-exponent limit. The compact-uniform crossover limits follow by integral comparison. All critical assumptions and boundary cases appear in the proof. Numerical FFT, finite Fourier identities and independent root-based samples corroborate, but do not constitute the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS with stated historical risk
Kiro–Nishry (arXiv:1911.13051, Theorem 1.4, Section 3 and Appendix) treats variances of zero counts for analytic Gaussian series and supplies useful Gaussian covariance machinery, not the exact truncation-index phase diagram for boundary Jensen means. Kabluchko–Khoruzhenko–Marynych (arXiv:2511.12302, Sections 1 and 4.4) treats local zeros of regularly varying random polynomials and self-inversive zeros, not these logarithmic Mahler variances. The exact unweighted \(\alpha=0\) specialization is covered by earlier work and is explicitly not claimed new. The genuinely new statement concerns the other parameter regimes and the two independent \(1/\log n\) transition profiles. Searches under Jensen mean, Mahler mean, dilogarithm covariance and power-weighted random polynomial did not identify a covering statement; absence from search is not proof of priority. The recent work on radial zeros using Jensen averages could not be compared in full text and remains a residual risk.

## Value — PASS
The two critical exponents separate different mechanisms in a standard random-analytic observable: at small exponent the entire Gaussian-chaos series survives, in the intermediate region only the first chaos controls leading variance, and above exponent one the randomness of a convergent boundary series persists. The two explicit critical-window functions quantify how the changes occur, rather than merely giving rate bounds. The result can guide fluctuation and concentration analysis of weighted random Taylor truncations and Jensen zero-count approximations. This is not a generic reweighting of the unweighted constant: the distinct transition laws require simultaneous changes in angular Fourier localization and coefficient square-summability.
