# Scientific review — two-distance visibility in three-factor Hamming graphs

## Correctness

**PASS.** The proof covers all integer factor orders at least two. It distinguishes necessary diameter-two anticodes from sufficient visibility, then proves a complete three-carrier classification. The cardinality and extremal-set count follow from disjointness of full balls, optimal planes, and the two exceptional binary tetrahedra. The small square Zarankiewicz values follow from an explicit row-pair upper bound and exhibiting matrices. A three-shift circulant construction gives the strict planar advantage for every equal factor order at least seven. The standalone scripts check every admissible diameter-two subset in six small Hamming graphs and explicit finite constructions; no finite test is substituted for the general proof.

## Originality

**PASS, with priority risk.** The original two-factor correspondence with the rectangle-free matrix problem is credited to Cicerone, Di Stefano, and Klavžar. The 2024 introduction of \(k\)-distance visibility gives a general isometric-subgraph lower bound but no three-factor converse. The 2023 three-factor Hamming paper addresses total, not selected-pair, visibility. The previously studied binary instance is explicitly excluded from claims of novelty. A semantic comparison with accessible prior mathematical records found no statement implying the nonbinary three-carrier classification, the rectangular maximum formula, or the equal-factor transition. Undiscovered publications may still overlap, and optimal plane counts remain expressed through the established matrix census.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The full structural classification identifies the only three mechanisms supporting two-distance visibility in three-factor Hamming products. It reduces an infinite three-dimensional graph invariant and its optimizer census to the classical bipartite \(C_4\)-free extremal problem, with a sharp, proved phase change at alphabet size six/seven. This exposes the point at which a classical unsolved extremal matrix problem takes over from local radius-one geometry, without pretending to solve that matrix problem.

Cera López et al., arXiv:2408.03976 (2024); Cicerone et al., arXiv:2112.13024 (2021); Bujtás et al., arXiv:2307.05168 (2023).
