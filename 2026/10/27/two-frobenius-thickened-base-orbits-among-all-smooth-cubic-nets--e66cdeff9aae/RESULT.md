# Two Frobenius-thickened base orbits among all-smooth cubic nets over \(\mathbf F_2\)

## Finding
Let \(V=H^0(\mathbf P^2_{\mathbf F_2},\mathcal O(3))\), the ten-dimensional space of ternary homogeneous cubics. Call a three-dimensional \(\mathbf F_2\)-subspace \(W\subset V\) *all-smooth* when every one of its seven nonzero elements cuts out a geometrically smooth plane cubic.

**Theorem (geometric-base refinement).** Of the \(864\) previously classified coordinate-fixed all-smooth binary cubic nets, exactly \(16\) have nonempty geometric base schemes. They form precisely two of the eight projective orbits, each of size \(8\); each exceptional base scheme has length \(6\) and is a doubled closed point of degree \(3\). The other \(848\) nets are geometrically basepoint-free and the associated cubic triples define finite morphisms \(\mathbf P^2_{\mathbf F_2}\to\mathbf P^2_{\mathbf F_2}\) of degree \(9\). The geometric-base classification is the new assertion; the existing ambient count and orbit decomposition are not claimed as new.

The following table reproduces representatives of the already-classified eight projective orbits to make the new geometric-base computation reproducible; its last column is the refinement proved here. To encode a cubic, the integer \(b\in\{0,\ldots,1023\}\) is its binary coefficient vector in the fixed monomial order
\[
(X^3,\ X^2Y,\ X^2Z,\ XY^2,\ XYZ,\ XZ^2,\ Y^3,\ Y^2Z,\ YZ^2,\ Z^3).
\]
Thus the three integers in a row encode three basis cubics; the entry is their linear span, not an ordered triple of curves. The point profile counts rational points on the seven distinct members.

## Assumptions and scope
All forms and linear spaces are defined over \(\mathbf F_2\); smoothness is geometric, over an algebraic closure. A net is a vector subspace of dimension exactly three, or a projective linear system of dimension two. Counts are for actual coordinate-fixed subspaces of \(V\), whereas orbit counts identify subspaces by changes of homogeneous coordinates over \(\mathbf F_2\). The base scheme is the scheme-theoretic common zero locus of the three generating cubic forms over the algebraic closure. It is not assumed to be a complete intersection.

## Proof
**1. Exact geometric smoothness test.** Write a cubic as a ten-bit coefficient vector. There are exactly \(2^{10}=1024\) vectors. In characteristic two, Euler's relation for degree three reads
\[
F=XF_X+YF_Y+ZF_Z.
\]
Consequently, a geometric singularity occurs exactly at a common projective zero of the three quadratic partial derivatives. The three standard affine charts can be covered without losing points by the normalizations \(X=1\), \(X=0,Y=1\), and \((X,Y,Z)=(0,0,1)\). On the first two charts, a common zero over the algebraic closure exists if and only if the ideal generated by the specialized partials is proper. The attached exact Gröbner-basis computation over \(\mathbf F_2\) checks whether each such ideal is the unit ideal, and evaluates the final point explicitly. Precisely \(336\) of the \(1023\) nonzero cubic forms pass all tests.

**2. Exhaustive net enumeration.** Encode addition of cubics by bitwise XOR. First enumerate every ordered-independent pair \((a,b)\) from the \(336\) smooth vectors for which \(a+b\) is also smooth; reducing the two rows to a fixed row-echelon form gives exactly \(5544\) distinct all-smooth two-dimensional subspaces. For each such pair, test every smooth vector \(c\) for which \(c+a\), \(c+b\), and \(c+a+b\) are smooth. The four requirements, combined with the original three smooth vectors, are equivalent to all seven nonzero members of the span being smooth. Reduce the three rows to unique row-echelon form and remove duplicates. The resulting set contains exactly \(864\) distinct three-dimensional subspaces. The attached C++ finite-field enumeration independently reproduces the smooth-form, pencil, and net counts.

**3. Projective orbit indexing (reproduced, not new).** Enumerate all \(2^9\) binary matrices of size three and retain the \(168\) invertible ones. For each such matrix, substitute its three linear forms into each of the ten cubic monomials, expand modulo two, apply the resulting ten-bit action to each net basis, and row-reduce. An exhaustive orbit walk partitions the \(864\) nets into the eight displayed orbits, of sizes \(8,8,8,168,168,168,168,168\). The stabilizers follow from orbit-stabilizer. The computation also evaluates each of the seven cubics at the seven rational projective points to obtain the displayed point profiles.

**4. Base-scheme refinement.** No all-smooth net has a rational common base point. Otherwise, at a rational base point, the differential of the three-dimensional space of cubic equations would map to a two-dimensional cotangent space; a nonzero element of its kernel would define a member singular at that point. For the eight orbit representatives, exact elimination on the charts used above shows that six have empty geometric base scheme. For the two exceptional representatives \((522,395,101)\) and \((579,271,229)\), the affine chart \(X=1\) has Gröbner bases respectively
\[
(Y+Z^4+Z^3+Z^2,\quad (Z^3+Z^2+1)^2)
\]
and
\[
(Y+Z^5+Z^2,\quad (Z^3+Z^2+1)^2).
\]
Neither has a base point on \(X=0\). Since \(Z^3+Z^2+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbf F_2\), each quotient ring is of length six, consisting of a doubled closed point of degree three. Orbit-invariance yields \(16\) such nets and \(864-16=848\) geometrically basepoint-free nets. For the six basepoint-free representatives, the three cubic forms have no common zero over the algebraic closure, hence define morphisms to a projective plane; since their common pullback of a hyperplane is \(\mathcal O(3)\), ampleness excludes positive-dimensional fibers, and the degree is \((3H)^2=9\). This proves the asserted geometric-base stratification and finite-morphism consequence.

## Relationship to prior work
Asgarli, Ghioca and Reichstein (2022, arXiv:2207.00918, Theorem 2) proved existence and the maximal projective dimension of smooth cubic systems. The finite binary net count \(864\), the eight projective orbits and rational point spectra are available from an earlier exact enumeration and are **not** part of the new claim. That enumeration settles only common *rational* base points; it does not determine the geometric base scheme over an algebraic closure. The present result computes the base schemes of the eight representatives by exact elimination and proves that precisely two size-eight orbits are supported at doubled Frobenius degree-three points, while the other six orbits are basepoint-free and yield degree-nine finite morphisms. The geometric distinction does not follow from absence of rational base points. Asgarli and Ghioca (2024, arXiv:2407.18104) study geometrically irreducible cubic systems, not the geometric base-scheme classification of all-smooth nets.

## Limitations
Only the binary base field and *rational members* of the parameter plane are classified; geometrically varying members over extensions need not all be smooth. The count \(864\) is not the number of abstract elliptic curves or nets up to arbitrary extension-field coordinate changes. The computation is finite and exhaustive in the explicitly described coefficient space, rather than a statement over all finite fields. The eight-orbit ambient classification is known; publication-priority risk for the geometric-base refinement comes from potentially unindexed computations of common zero schemes of those exact representatives.
