# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The absorbing/reflecting backward resolvent produces an explicit positive substochastic two-state attempt kernel. Its characteristic polynomial is negative at the stationary one-attempt survival probability and has two positive roots below one. A complete Markov-renewal recurrence then yields a positive two-geometric mixture. The resulting strict hazard monotonicity and closed-form overdispersion are exact algebraic consequences. The independent finite-difference boundary calculation and symbolic/numerical checker agree with these derivations; finite sampling is not offered as a continuum proof.

## Originality
PASS, with a residual literature priority risk. Mercado-Vásquez–Boyer (arXiv:2107.02148) treats the identical physical gated-search model but primarily derives hitting-*time* transforms and their first moments. Bressloff (arXiv:2009.02739) resets the gate as well as the searcher, whereas the present model preserves the target's gate state. Bressloff (arXiv:2310.20502) examines finite numbers of resets in an ungated search. These results provide the physical model, a contrasting renewal protocol, and the motivation for reset budgets, respectively, but no equivalent two-geometric positive mixing theorem or all-parameter strict dispersion identity was identified in the examined material. Standard two-state phase-type algebra and broad Markov-modulated stopping literature pose a residual priority risk; absence from searches is not proof of novelty.

## Scientific value
PASS. Reset counts are operationally meaningful when repeated interventions have a cost or finite budget. The exact two-mode law and decreasing success hazard quantify survival bias caused by preserving the hidden reactive state at resets, and the positive excess variance states how much a naive independent-attempt geometric approximation underestimates uncertainty. The result is structural across all strictly positive parameters rather than a selected numerical slice.
