Differentiation yields an explicit derivative ratio on the entire interval between the branch modes, whose logarithmic derivative has the stated cubic numerator. The exact discriminant factorization was checked symbolically and through independent exact rational evaluation. Möbius substitutions and Descartes' rule prove that the quartic has exactly two positive roots. Convexity and monotonicity arguments locate the two interior cubic zeros in each extreme-rate regime. Horizontal-line intersection counting proves all strict peak counts, including equality cases. The standalone checker ran successfully, including direct derivative-grid checks; numerical tests are not substituted for the all-parameter proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The branch response formulas and their use in parallel cascades are old. Došlá's 2009 full-text Propositions 6 and 7 solve gamma-mixture modality with a common shape or a common rate, and explicitly leave unequal shapes and unequal rates untreated. The general derivative-ratio framework gives a route to this calculation but does not supply the two nontrivial algebraic thresholds, physical interval root localization, or weight boundaries for shapes two and three. Evin et al. (2011) use gamma mixtures statistically in hydrology, not the stated exact peak-count classification. Comparable prior published findings recovered through mathematical-record search concern gamma *sums*, not weighted density mixtures, or translated storm responses rather than simultaneous unequal-cascade impulses. Material from Diskin et al. (1978) could not be read in full and broader mixture-modality literature may imply pieces; those priority risks remain open.

**Value — PASS.** Multiple runoff peaks can arise from watershed heterogeneity even with a single impulse. The sharp global result specifies when a minimal, physically motivated parallel-cascade model can and cannot produce them, and gives a reproducible complete mixing-weight interval when it can. This distinguishes mixture-induced double peaks from shifted-storm double peaks in a way an individual example does not. The theorem is not an empirical rainfall–runoff prediction; integer reservoir depths, ideal impulses, and linear empty-state dynamics are essential restrictions.
