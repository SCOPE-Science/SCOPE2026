# Review: two-versus-three component transition for square-zero centralizers

## Correctness — PASS

The square equations are obtained from the full endomorphism algebra of the truncated modules, with no omitted block. A valuation cutoff reduces them to a small even/odd polynomial system, including the exceptional linear-coordinate shift when \(n=4\). The even nontrivial component is an irreducible complete intersection: its open set \(B\ne0\) is irreducible of dimension four, and the boundary has dimension at most three. In odd length, each displayed component is an affine space or irreducible quadratic cone times a free affine factor. Direct finite-field fibers give the two announced polynomials. Both coefficient enumeration and direct ordinary-matrix squaring reproduce the stated small examples; the symbolic proof, rather than finite samples, establishes all sizes and fields.

## Originality — PASS, with residual uncertainty

Košir--Oblak (`arXiv:0712.2813`) analyze dense generic commuting nilpotent types. Britnell--Wildon (`arXiv:1001.0811`) analyze commuting similarity-type existence, including two-block cases. Bogdanić et al. (`arXiv:2212.08425`) treat **equal** two-block nilpotent centralizers. Their inspected results do not supply the geometric decomposition or exact count for the fixed unequal type \((n,2)\), \(n\ge4\). Earlier fixed-hook and consecutive-block results address different Jordan types and do not imply this result as written. A general Hall-polynomial method might imply some of the counts indirectly; uninspected wider literature could defeat publication priority. Search absence is not a uniqueness certificate.

## Value — PASS

Square-zero commuting fibers classify two-step differentials respecting a chosen nilpotent module filtration. The result determines an entire infinite family's reduced component geometry, a uniform codimension-one common intersection, and exact finite-field counts in all characteristics. It identifies a parity-dependent component-number transition, rather than an arbitrary small numerical gain.

## Limits

Fixed \((n,2)\), \(n\ge4\); reduced varieties only, not possible embedded nonreduced structure, operator conjugacy or full rank spectra. The checker is a finite consistency test, not a theorem prover. Absolute publication priority is not certified.
