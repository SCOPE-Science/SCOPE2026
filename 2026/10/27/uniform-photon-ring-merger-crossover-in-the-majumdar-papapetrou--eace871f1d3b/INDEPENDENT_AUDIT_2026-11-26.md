Log differentiation gives V_ρ/V=-2g/(ρη³U) with η=√(1+ρ²) and g=η³-2Mη²+4M. At the double root, g=g_ρ=0, g_M=-8 and g_ρρ=5√6/2. This gives the stated a and -6b, while U_*=5/2 gives V_*=16/3125 and 2√V_*/ρ_*=8/125. The gap factors as b(Y-X)(Y²+XY+X²-3c²). For X>c the quadratic is increasing in Y≥X and positive at X; for X<-2c its global minimum is 3(X²-4c²)/4>0. This last argument avoids the overbroad intermediate comparison in the frozen proof: Φ(c) need not bound Φ(Y) for Y just above a sufficiently negative X, but Φ(X)>Φ(Y) still holds. The scaled gap converges in C¹ on compact intervals, so since -Φ'(X)>0 it admits a uniform positive multiple of Y-X near the singular lower limit, and a positive minimum away from it. For the intermediate tail, V_ρ=aδ-3bu²+O(u³+δu+δ²) with u=ρ-ρ_*≥R₀√δ, and is bounded above by -K u² for R₀ large and u small. Integrating over [u/2,u] for u≥2R₀√δ bounds the potential gap from below by a positive multiple of u³, giving an O(R^-1/2) scaled tail at Y≥R. At u≥ε>0 the gap is bounded below independently of small δ: the potential is decreasing there by the circular-orbit polynomial and already has a positive gap at ε. The numerator in the angular integrand then decays as ρ^-2, so the far tail vanishes after scaling. These bounds also ensure a true outermost turning point on either branch for sufficiently small δ at each fixed X. The quadratic factor yields a logarithm as its zero approaches the lower endpoint at X=c or its interior minimum vanishes at X=-2c; rescaling by |X| gives the |X|^-1/2 edges. The certified limit is for each fixed X on the two open branches, not uniform up to their boundaries. No computational verification is required by this proof.
