# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The inverse-branch transfer formula is exact. A uniform full-branch BV Lasota--Yorke bound follows from uniform expansion, a fixed partition, and uniformly bounded branch reciprocal-slope variations; BV compactness and the uniqueness of the tent-map invariant density imply \(L^1\) convergence. The cylinder-variation inequality supplies interior pointwise convergence without interchanging limiting derivatives. The right scale is an exact substitution, and the left scale follows a finite recursive expansion whose remainder is uniformly geometrically bounded. Endpoint slope limits use exact identities instead of differentiating convergence.

## Originality — PASS
The full primary paper arXiv:2610.23180v1 covers existence, regularity, mixing and endpoint derivative for fixed parameter, but not the singular \(\vartheta\uparrow2\) two-layer density profile. Its probability-law linear response does not imply this parameter degeneration. The earlier cusp-response paper treats exponent strictly below the critical value. The separately recorded complex continuation of the same invariant densities does not imply the spatially resolved real parameter limit. Searches for the exact profiles and their equivalent tent-degeneration formulation did not locate a covering theorem. The absence of a database hit does not prove priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The density converges to the tent invariant density in integral but cannot converge uniformly on the closed interval because both finite-parameter endpoint densities vanish. Both singular boundary regions have the same precise shrinking width yet different nontrivial limiting shapes and slopes. This describes a natural and otherwise unresolved statistical-stability boundary effect in the motivating full-branch family, beyond the already known endpoint trace alone.

## Closest literature and limitations
The cusp-image derivative formula by itself is prior work (Proposition 5.1, arXiv:2610.23180v1); the newly claimed result is the coupled pair of *singular parameter profiles*. The proof uses standard BV compactness theory, and a still-undiscovered equivalent statement in general full-branch perturbation literature remains a priority risk.
