## Originality (O): PASS under self-review
The closest paper, Biroli–Majumdar–Schehr (arXiv:2512.00440v1), establishes the exact stationary renewal/conditionally independent density, **not** the claimed fixed-\(N\), far-negative multivariate tail dependence limit or sharp stationary moment threshold. Its discussed limiting regime is primarily growing particle number and typical positions. De Mauro–Biroli–Majumdar–Schehr (arXiv:2509.06658v2) prove power-law one-point density tails for **independently prescribed Pareto reset intervals**, with free propagators. The present particle-triggered reset mechanism instead requires an absorbed propagator and produces the explicit sum-of-squared-thresholds factor. Biswas–Majumdar–Pal (arXiv:2504.13501v1) analyze threshold-triggered resetting for target-search times, not these stationary joint extremes. Public mathematical-result searches for aliases and implication-equivalent statements did not locate this particular coefficient or moment classification. Existing general renewal-mixture and regular-variation results could still provide a shorter derivation or implicit coverage; exhaustive priority is not claimed.

## Value (V): PASS
The multi-particle first-passage-reset NESS is an established physically motivated model of emergent correlations. The theorem quantifies a qualitatively stronger phenomenon than a covariance: even at arbitrarily deep negative excursions, distinct particles have a strictly positive conditional probability of co-extremes. The universal multivariate threshold norm distinguishes event-triggered resetting from a free Gaussian renewal mixture. It also resolves which stationary observables have finite moments, showing that a normalized NESS at three particles can have infinite absolute mean and at four particles infinite second moment. Those exact thresholds matter when interpreting or estimating fluctuations in correlated resetting experiments.
