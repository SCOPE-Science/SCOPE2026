# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof identifies two possible outer-colour triple types and shows that the repeated/missing-colour state cannot close around an odd number of triangles. Each of the three bicoloured two-factors then has exactly two strands whose cyclic order is reversed at every triangle column; for odd \(k\) this forces a single Hamiltonian cycle. For even \(k\) the inner triangles expand a balanced bipartite cubic graph with even side size; the signs of the three relative matching permutations preclude a perfect one-factorization. The edge-colouring bijection and preservation of bicoloured component counts are explicit. Two different finite enumerations reproduce the known counts and the new parity dichotomy through \(k=8\) and \(k=10\), respectively. The computation is not substituted for the general proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Bonvicini–Mazzuoccolo already establish existence of one perfect factorization for odd \(k\), and Bruhn–Gellert–Günther enumerate all factorizations; neither inspected result implies the universality of perfection for odd \(k\), the absence of perfection for even \(k\), or the full resulting Kempe rigidity. The Goedgebeur–Östergård edge-Kempe study covers separate classes and bounded-size enumerations. Mathematical record searches and accessible historical claims identified no covering implication. An older source on Kotzig or cycle-exchange rigidity, or an unindexed factorization catalogue, could still contain an equivalent statement; publication priority is not independently certified.

**Value — PASS.** The property that every factorization is perfect is much stronger than a single perfect factorization, and the even side is a complete impossibility theorem despite many existing proper three-edge-colourings. These facts give a sharp all-parameter classification and show exponentially many edge-colourings cannot undergo nontrivial Kempe transformations. They address the same named cubic family as the earlier existence and enumeration papers, rather than an arbitrary numerical slice.

The scope is the family \(GP(3k,k)\); the factorization counts and older existence direction are explicitly credited. Computational checks through finite \(k\) supplement, but do not prove, the infinite claim. Equivalent unindexed literature remains the principal originality risk.
