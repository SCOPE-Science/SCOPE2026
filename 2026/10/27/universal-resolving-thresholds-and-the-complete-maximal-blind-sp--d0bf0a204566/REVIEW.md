# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The failure-equivalence lemma gives an exact reduction of resolving number to the maximal equal-distance fiber. For two target words differing in \(r\) coordinates, a direct coefficient calculation yields the full fiber size. Central-binomial monotonicity settles the binary alphabet, and a proved symmetric-unimodal convolution argument settles every nonbinary alphabet, including the unique ternary tie. Full equality cases are described by coordinate parity relations, hyperplanes, permutation graphs or excluded-symbol cylinders. Distinctness of the resulting subsets yields the exact count. A directly executed independent pair-enumeration checker confirms each claimed family for 15 small Hamming graphs; finite checks are not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to priority risk

The 2013 Garijo–González–Márquez proposition supplies the **general** threshold/equalizer equivalence, already credited. The 2019 Laird–Tillquist–Becker–Lladser Hamming theorem characterizes **individual** resolving sets through a constrained linear system. Their theorem is capable of deciding any particular set, but the inspected text does not derive the all-set cardinality threshold or the complete list and count of largest failures. The 2021 survey treats Hamming metric dimension and the resolving-number concept separately. Directly relevant 2012 results concern minimum doubly resolving sets and strong metric dimension, not the universal resolving threshold. A newly accessible equidistant-dimension study concerns the reversed equal-distance covering quantifier, not maximum nonresolving landmark sets. Record searches found no equivalent or dominating theorem. Unindexed or differently described priority remains possible; unsuccessful searches alone do not prove novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

For symmetric finite-coordinate network models, the distinction between *some* small resolving set and *every* large resolving set is a natural robustness question. This theorem solves the latter for all dimensions and alphabet sizes and describes/counts every worst-case blind spot. The ternary transition—two genuinely different types of optimal obstruction tied only at one alphabet size—is structural information not contained in a generic resolving-set decision criterion. The result is an infinite-family classification rather than a numerical lookup or routine specialization.
