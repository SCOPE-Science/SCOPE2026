# Scientific review: regular-polygon midpoint extension

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof covers every integer \(n\ge3\), every nonzero real Banach target, and every real-linear operator without positivity or symmetry assumptions. Its lower certificate uses the full vertex-distance column sum and signed coefficients. The upper construction explicitly inverts the Euclidean chord-distance matrix; Ptolemy makes the weights positive, a differential identity and circular rearrangement make them radially monotone, and a nonnegative convolution with adjacent-vertex midpoint measures makes all midpoint-to-midpoint pairs contractive. The conclusion is an exact norm rather than a finite extrapolation. Numerical transport LPs for \(3\le n\le30\) support the calculations only.

**Originality — PASS, with qualification.** The 2018 linear-extension source treats general doubling subsets with nonsharp bounds; Basso's 2021 work gives a projection-constant framework and computational approaches, while the 2023 exposition gives general explicit upper bounds. Existing finite calculations already determine \(n=4,5,6\): those are not independently claimed. No located result from primary full-text inspections and targeted statement/alias searches gives the **all-order** exact ratio, Ptolemy-positive kernel and convolution proof. The all-order generalization, particularly \(n\ge7\), is assessed as distinct. Residual risk: less-indexed distance-matrix or interpolation literature might contain an equivalent result; search absence is not proof of absolute novelty.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The natural simultaneous finite-metric extension problem is solved uniformly across the unbounded family of regular Euclidean polygons. A central algebraic lower bound is matched by a nontrivial, globally valid positive operator. The constructive link between Ptolemy's inequality, a discrete chord-kernel inverse and Wasserstein contraction is structural, and explains why all midpoint-pair constraints are harmless. This adds a uniform theorem beyond three previously solved isolated low-order instances, rather than a routine larger-n computation.

The operator is not claimed optimal for complex-linear extension, arbitrary polygons or higher-dimensional face centers. Independent verification has not been performed.
