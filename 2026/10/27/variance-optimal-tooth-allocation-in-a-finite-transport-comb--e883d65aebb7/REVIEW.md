# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The edge-rate convention and absorbing boundary are explicit. The tooth hitting-time moment equations, renewal transform, strong-Markov factorization and cumulant recurrence yield the exact variance formula without approximation. Hölder supplies a sharp cubic lower bound, integer rounding supplies a matching upper bound, strict convexity gives convergence of all optimizers, and a monotone nonzero integer forward difference proves the exact two-tooth threshold. An exact-rational killed-generator checker confirms 169 small models; exhaustive two-tooth optimization agrees through budget 40. The asymptotic proof does not rely on finite experiments.

**Originality — PASS (qualified).** Relevant primary full text concerns comb propagators, tooth waiting-time statistics and homogeneous finite teeth; general tree work gives previously known moment formulae. The new contribution assessed is not a general moment formula: it is the constrained minimum of the absorption-time *variance* over heterogeneous integer tooth allocations, the unique asymptotic inverse-square-root design, and the finite two-tooth phase boundary. published-finding corpus and local claim comparisons did not yield a dominating statement. The 2011 general-tree moment paper and some more recent generalized-walk works could not be materially checked in full, so unrecognized implication or priority remains a real risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A fixed tooth-site budget is a natural geometric constraint in branched transport. Mean-first-passage optimization concentrates the entire budget at one end, whereas suppressing its variability requires a distributed and quantitatively unique profile. The threshold \(M=3\) already forces this design conflict in the minimal nontrivial comb, and the universal large-budget coefficient determines the sharp achievable dispersion reduction for every fixed backbone length. These are structural optimization results rather than arbitrary enumerated examples.

**Scope and limitations.** The walk has unit rate on each edge, finite reflecting teeth, one absorbing endpoint and fixed backbone length in the asymptotic. No stochastic ordering, general-rate extension, growing-backbone joint limit, or independent validation is claimed. The listed priority risks do not replace direct comparison against any later discovered stronger theorem.
