# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Induced forests of a rook graph are exactly edge sets of bipartite linear forests; direct maximality analysis proves saturation on the smaller shore in the unbalanced case and spanning alternating paths in the balanced case. The component exponential generating function and the maximum-set count follow from explicit labeled path counts. Direct exhaustive adjacency checks on ten small boards confirm the formulas and inclusion-maximality. The infinite statement rests on proof, not experiments.

**Originality — PASS, subject to priority uncertainty.** The 2021 foundational paper by Jafarpour-Golzari defines well-f-coveredness and proves a result for complete bipartite graphs themselves, but does not treat their line graphs. Known well-coveredness of rook graphs addresses maximal independent sets, not maximal induced forests. Searches for the line-graph, linear-forest, feedback-vertex and rook-graph formulations found no prior claim implying the complete result. An unindexed enumeration of maximal linear forests in complete bipartite graphs could be equivalent; priority is not independently certified.

**Value — PASS.** This gives a natural infinite well-f-covered family with arbitrary factor imbalance, exact all-weight forest counts, all minimal feedback-vertex-set cardinalities, exact numbers of minimum feedback vertex sets, and a sharp path-component spectrum; it is not just an instance of a generic path counting exercise.

**Closest primary literature:** R. Jafarpour-Golzari, *Graphs whose all maximal induced forests are of the same order*, arXiv:2105.14339 (2021), especially Definitions 3.1 and Theorem 6.15/Corollary 6.16; the objects in that corollary are complete bipartite graphs, not rook graphs. Other relevant literature discusses general forest optimization, lexicographic graph products, and rook-graph domination, none implying this statement in the inspected material.

**Limitations:** The proof is confined to \(L(K_{p,q})\), \(2\le p\le q\). Finite verification is exhaustive only on the ten listed boards. Undiscovered equivalent results are a residual originality risk. Independent mathematical validation is not performed.
