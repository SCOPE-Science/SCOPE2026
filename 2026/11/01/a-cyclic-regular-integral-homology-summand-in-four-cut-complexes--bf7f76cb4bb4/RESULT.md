# A cyclic regular integral homology summand in four-cut complexes of cubed cycles

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(n\ge 12\), let \(G_n=C_n^3\) be the third power of the cycle on \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), and let \(\Delta_n=\Delta_4(G_n)\) be its ordinary four-cut complex. Write \(K_n=\Delta_n^{\vee}\) for the combinatorial Alexander dual on the full \(n\)-element vertex set. Then:

1. For every \(n\ge 13\), \(H^3(K_n;\mathbb Z)\) contains an **equivariantly split** copy of the integral regular representation \(\mathbb Z[\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z]\) under cyclic vertex rotation. In particular,
   \[
   \widetilde H_{n-6}(\Delta_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^n\oplus A_n
   \]
   for some finitely generated abelian group \(A_n\). The cyclic action on the displayed summand in the homology of \(\Delta_n\) may be chosen regular as well.
2. At the exceptional size \(n=12\), \(\widetilde H_6(\Delta_{12};\mathbb Z)\) has a split \(\mathbb Z\) summand.

All these nonvoid complexes are pure of dimension \(n-5\), so they are nonshellable. The new assertion is the **linear-multiplicity, cyclic-regular integral summand** (and the explicit size-twelve integral witness), not nonshellability alone.

Exact supplementary computations give
\[
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\widetilde H_{6}(\Delta_{12};\mathbb F_2)=2,
\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\widetilde H_{7}(\Delta_{13};\mathbb F_2)=14.
\]
No equality for the entire integral homology group at arbitrary \(n\) is asserted.

## Assumptions and scope

Vertices of \(G_n\) are residues \(0,\ldots,n-1\). Two distinct vertices are adjacent exactly when their circular distance is at most \(3\). The ordinary \(4\)-cut complex \(\Delta_n\) is generated by complements of four-vertex sets inducing disconnected subgraphs. Its Alexander dual has the elementary characterization
\[
K_n=\{S\subseteq\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z:\text{every four-element subset of }S\text{ induces a connected subgraph of }G_n\}.
\]
Every set with at most three vertices is therefore a face of \(K_n\). We use oriented integral simplicial chains and cochains, and reduced homology for cut complexes. All topological assertions concern ordinary complexes, not total-cut complexes.

## Proof

**1. A translated family of three-spheres.** Partition eight consecutive vertices into four disjoint pairs
\[
(0,1),\quad(2,3),\quad(4,5),\quad(6,7).
\]
For each binary four-tuple \(\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_0,\ldots,\varepsilon_3)\), put
\[
T_\varepsilon=[\varepsilon_0,\,2+\varepsilon_1,\,4+\varepsilon_2,\,6+\varepsilon_3].
\]
Define the oriented three-chain
\[
z_0=\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^4}(-1)^{\varepsilon_0+\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2+\varepsilon_3}T_\varepsilon.
\]
It is the fundamental chain of the boundary of the four-dimensional cross-polytope, hence \(\partial z_0=0\). Every summand is a face of \(K_n\): its four vertices in increasing order have successive differences at most three, and thus form a connected path in \(G_n\). This holds for every \(n\ge12\), including possible wrap edges. Let \(\rho\) be cyclic vertex rotation, with the natural orientation signs on simplicial chains. Set \(z_j=\rho^j z_0\), for all residues \(j\).

**2. A primitive cochain for \(n\ge14\).** Let \(Q=[0,2,4,7]\). It is a face of \(K_n\), with coefficient \(-1\) in \(z_0\). It belongs to **no four-dimensional simplex** of \(K_n\). Indeed, for a putative added vertex \(v\), a disconnected four-subset of \(Q\cup\{v\}\) is displayed in the following exhaustive cases:

| Added vertex \(v\) | Disconnected four-subset |
|---|---|
| \(1\) | \(\{0,1,2,7\}\) |
| \(3\) | \(\{0,2,3,7\}\) |
| \(5\) | \(\{0,4,5,7\}\) |
| \(6\) | \(\{0,2,6,7\}\) |
| \(8,9,10\) | \(\{0,2,4,v\}\) |
| \(11\le v\le n-1\) | \(\{0,4,7,v\}\) |

Each indicated graph is disconnected whenever \(n\ge14\), by the circular-distance adjacency criterion. Thus the oriented characteristic cochain \(\omega_0=-e_Q\), taking value \(-1\) on \(Q\) and zero on all other tetrahedra, is an **integral cocycle**. Its evaluation is \(\langle\omega_0,z_0\rangle=1\).

**3. The wrapped exception \(n=13\).** The singleton \(e_Q\) is no longer a cocycle. Instead put
\[
\omega_0=e_{[0,3,4,7]}-e_{[3,4,7,10]}.
\]
The **only** possible five-vertex face of \(K_{13}\) containing either listed tetrahedron is \(\{0,3,4,7,10\}\). Both tetrahedra occur in its signed boundary with coefficient \(+1\), so \(\delta\omega_0=0\). Exactly \([0,3,4,7]\) is present in the support of \(z_0\), with coefficient \(+1\); therefore \(\langle\omega_0,z_0\rangle=1\).

**4. The full cyclic pairing.** For each residue \(j\), set \(\omega_j=\rho^j\omega_0\). In either case \(n\ge13\), we claim
\[
\langle\omega_i,z_j\rangle=\delta_{ij}.
\]
For \(n\ge14\), the four vertices \(0,2,4,7\) have cyclic gaps \(2,2,3,n-7\). Their unique eight-consecutive-vertex block is \(\{0,1,\ldots,7\}\): the complementary six or more skipped vertices must lie in the one gap of size \(n-7\). Consequently \(Q\) occurs in the support of only \(z_0\), and in no other translate. For \(n=13\), \(\{0,3,4,7\}\) also lies in only the block \(\{0,\ldots,7\}\); the other cochain tetrahedron \(\{3,4,7,10\}\) lies in the unique block \(\{3,\ldots,10\}\), but it chooses **both** vertices from its first opposite pair \((3,4)\), so it occurs in none of the translated cross-polytope chains. In both cases the diagonal pairing was computed in Steps 2–3. Cyclic translation now proves the full identity matrix.

It follows that the homomorphism
\[
E:H^3(K_n;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z},
\qquad E([\eta])=(\langle\eta,z_j\rangle)_j,
\]
is a well-defined cyclic-equivariant surjection. The map sending the \(j\)-th standard basis vector to \([\omega_j]\) is a cyclic-equivariant **section**, since \(E([\omega_i])\) is the \(i\)-th basis vector. Thus \(H^3(K_n;\mathbb Z)\) contains an equivariantly split regular integer module, not merely \(n\) independent rational classes.

**5. The size-twelve integral boundary case.** For \(n=12\), define a degree-three integral cochain \(u\) on the following tetrahedra, with the listed coefficients; on all remaining tetrahedra put zero:

| Tetrahedron | Coefficient | Tetrahedron | Coefficient |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| \((0,3,4,7)\) | \(-1\) | \((1,3,5,10)\) | \(-1\) |
| \((1,3,6,10)\) | \(-1\) | \((1,3,7,10)\) | \(-1\) |
| \((1,3,8,10)\) | \(-1\) | \((1,6,9,10)\) | \(+1\) |
| \((3,4,7,9)\) | \(+1\) | \((3,4,7,10)\) | \(+1\) |
| \((4,5,7,9)\) | \(-1\) | \((4,6,7,9)\) | \(-1\) |
| \((4,6,9,10)\) | \(+1\) | \((4,7,8,9)\) | \(+1\) |

There are exactly \(168\) allowed five-vertex faces of \(K_{12}\). Direct substitution in each signed boundary gives \(\delta u=0\) over \(\mathbb Z\). The evaluation on \(z_0\) is \(-1\); thus \([u]\) spans a split \(\mathbb Z\)-summand of \(H^3(K_{12};\mathbb Z)\). The exact exhaustive checker implements this finite integral proof independently from the displayed table.

**6. Passage to cut complexes.** Integral combinatorial Alexander duality on \(n\) ambient vertices gives
\[
\widetilde H_{n-6}(\Delta_n;\mathbb Z)\cong H^{3}(K_n;\mathbb Z).
\]
For \(n\ge13\) the right side has the split rank-\(n\) summand from Step 4; for \(n=12\) it has the primitive rank-one summand from Step 5. The duality is natural up to the orientation-sign action of vertex permutations, so its sign twist carries the regular cyclic summand to another integral regular cyclic summand. The four-set \(\{0,1,6,7\}\) is disconnected for \(n\ge12\); hence \(\Delta_n\) is nonvoid and generated in pure dimension \(n-5\). The nonzero homology in degree \(n-6\), one below top dimension, contradicts the homology concentration of a pure shellable complex. This proves the theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Chauhan and Shukla, arXiv:2603.23155 (public 24 March 2026), establish shellability for **three-cut** complexes of powers of cycles and explicitly discuss nonshellability questions for higher cuts. Bayer et al., arXiv:2304.13675, establish the definition, induced-subgraph inheritance for shellability, and earlier results on ordinary cycles and **squared** cycles. A published cubic-**path** result proves only one split integral next-to-top homology class for \(\Delta_4(P_n^3)\), and combined with induced-subgraph inheritance it already implies **nonshellability** for sufficiently large cubic cycles. That nonshellability is therefore **not** the new contribution. The cyclic regular rank-\(n\) direct summand, its exact identity pairing, and the integral wrapped-cycle certificates do not follow from the cited statements: nonshellability inheritance does not induce an injection of the relevant whole-complex homology. The cited three-cut cyclic-character work treats a different cut size and degree.

## Limitations

Only an explicit direct summand of reduced integral homology is asserted: other summands, torsion, complete homotopy types, and exact integral Betti numbers for general \(n\) are not determined. The regular-module assertion concerns cyclic rotations, not the full dihedral action. The two stated mod-two ranks are exact only for \(n=12,13\). The infinite proof specializes to cycle third powers and ordinary four-cut complexes.
