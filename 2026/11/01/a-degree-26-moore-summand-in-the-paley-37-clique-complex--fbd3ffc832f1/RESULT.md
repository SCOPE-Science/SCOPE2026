# A degree-26 Moore summand in the Paley-37 clique complex

## Finding

Let \(P_{37}\) have vertex set \(\mathbb F_{37}\), with two distinct elements adjacent when their difference is a nonzero quadratic residue modulo \(37\). Put \(K=\operatorname{Cl}(P_{37})\), the abstract clique complex. Define the Moore space \(M(\mathbb Z/26,2)\) as the cofiber of the degree-\(26\) map \(S^2\to S^2\). There is a homotopy equivalence

\[
K\simeq\bigvee^{36}S^2\vee M(\mathbb Z/26,2).
\]

In particular the complete integral homology is

\[
H_i(K;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&i=0,\\
\mathbb Z^{36}\oplus\mathbb Z/26,&i=2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

The vertex shortest-path metric on \(P_{37}\) has diameter two, so its inclusive Vietoris–Rips filtration has a discrete 37-point stage below radius one, the homotopy type above from radius one to below radius two, and a contractible stage at every radius at least two. No integral persistence-module splitting at intermediate non-integer radii is needed.

## Assumptions and scope

The field is the prime field of exactly \(37\) elements; the graph is simple and unweighted. Adjacency is computed directly as \(j-i\) belonging to \(\{s^2\bmod37:1\le s\le36\}\). Faces of \(K\) are all complete vertex sets, oriented by increasing vertex labels \(0,\ldots,36\). Homology is ordinary integral simplicial homology of its geometric realization. The statement is about this particular finite graph, not a uniform theorem for all Paley graphs or a claim about continuous metric realizations.

## Proof

**1. Complete simplicial model and fundamental group.** Enumerating all increasing cliques gives the face vector \((37,333,888,555)\) in dimensions zero through three, with no higher faces. A breadth-first spanning tree rooted at \(0\) has \(36\) edges and collapses in the one-skeleton. The remaining \(333-36=297\) edges generate the fundamental group, subject to the attaching words of the \(888\) triangles. Repeatedly select an attaching triangle whose edge word contains only one currently surviving non-tree generator; the other edge letters have already been eliminated. The triangle relation then kills that generator even in the nonabelian group. An explicit complete sequence of \(297\) such unit eliminations is generated and checked by the accompanying program. Therefore \(\pi_1(K)=0\). Moreover, the same sequence is a unimodular elimination in the abelian cellular boundary, so \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_2=297\), and its image is the full primitive subgroup \(\ker\partial_1\). Also \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_1=36\).

**2. Exact integral Smith reduction of the tetrahedral boundary.** The matrix \(\partial_3:C_3\to C_2\) has \(555\) columns indexed by increasing tetrahedra and \(888\) rows indexed by increasing triangles, with incidence coefficient \((-1)^j\) when the \(j\)-th tetrahedron vertex is deleted. Exactly \(666\) rows meet two tetrahedra and the other \(222\) meet four. Regard the first set as a signed incidence graph on the tetrahedra. Its three connected components each have \(185\) vertices. Propagating signs on a spanning forest consistently orients all three components and verifies that every two-coface row annihilates the sign vector on its component. Consequently this \(666\)-row submatrix is equivalent by integral unimodular operations to an ordinary graph incidence matrix of rank \(555-3=552\); it has precisely \(552\) unit Smith pivots and a **primitive**, three-dimensional kernel.

Restrict the remaining \(222\) rows to this kernel lattice using its three component-wise sign vectors. Each of the six rows

\[
(-3,0,1),\quad(-1,-3,0),\quad(0,-1,-3),\quad(0,1,3),\quad(1,3,0),\quad(3,0,-1)
\]

appears exactly \(37\) times. The gcds of all minors of sizes one, two and three of this reduced \(222\)-by-\(3\) matrix are \(1,1,26\), respectively. Its Smith invariants are thus \((1,1,26)\). The full matrix \(\partial_3\) consequently has \(554\) unit invariants, one invariant \(26\), and full column rank \(555\). Hence \(H_3(K;\mathbb Z)=0\) and \(\operatorname{tors}\operatorname{coker}\partial_3\cong\mathbb Z/26\). Since \(\operatorname{im}\partial_2\) is free, the exact sequence \(0\to H_2(K)\to C_2/\operatorname{im}\partial_3\to\operatorname{im}\partial_2\to0\) splits as abelian groups. Therefore

\[
H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{888-555-297}\oplus\mathbb Z/26
=\mathbb Z^{36}\oplus\mathbb Z/26.
\]

**3. Upgrade to integral homotopy.** A simply connected three-dimensional CW complex is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of two-spheres with three-cells attached: collapsing a maximal tree and eliminating the one-cells using the triangle relations gives an equivalent simply connected two-skeleton, a wedge of two-spheres. Since \(\pi_2\) of that two-skeleton is freely generated by the sphere summands, integral basis changes in its free second-homotopy group are realized by homotopy equivalences. Applying the Smith diagonalization to the attaching degrees of its three-cells yields one degree-\(26\) attachment, \(36\) unattached two-spheres, and no surviving three-sphere attachments because \(H_3=0\). Equivalently, choose a homology generator for each free summand and the order-\(26\) class in \(\pi_2(K)\cong H_2(K)\), extend a degree-\(26\) two-sphere map across a three-cell using the torsion relation, and apply the homology Whitehead theorem to the resulting map from the stated wedge. Either construction establishes the claimed homotopy equivalence.

**4. Final filtration stage.** Every pair of distinct nonadjacent vertices has a common graph neighbor, as exhaustively checked from quadratic residues. Thus graph diameter is two. At radius two all pairs form edges of the Rips complex, which is therefore the full \(36\)-simplex and contractible.

## Relationship to prior work

Lim and Praeger (2006) give the finite-field graph definition and automorphism-group setting, but no asserted integral clique-complex topology. Cioabă, Guo, Ji and Mim (2025 preprint/2026 journal article), Theorem 5.21 and its examples, explicitly establish vanishing first cohomology for the 37-vertex Paley graph; this already covers the corresponding first rational homology assertion and is not novel here. Cioabă and Mim (2026) study first clique homology of strongly regular graphs and higher homology for a distinct Latin-square family. Chandler, Sin and Xiang (2014) analyze Smith invariants of Paley graph matrices, **not** the tetrahedron-to-triangle boundary matrix. Related published indexed computations for the 29-vertex Paley graph report order-two torsion in its clique/independence complex; order 29 is a different object, and that statement does not imply the present order-26 Moore summand.

The specific proposed contribution is the complete **integral homotopy type** for the 37-vertex Paley clique complex, and especially its \(13\)-primary component. No absolute claim of first discovery is made.

## Limitations

This is a finite exact calculation with a fully specified graph; it proves no statement for other field orders. The character of automorphism actions on homology and a canonical geometric set of torsion carriers are not determined. The characterization uses homotopy equivalence, not homeomorphism or an explicit minimal triangulation of the Moore space. Earlier unindexed or inaccessible equivalent calculations remain a publication-priority risk. No independent mathematical audit is asserted.
