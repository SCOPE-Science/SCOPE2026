# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. For binary \(f\), \(h=1-Mf\) equals the minimum zero density over centered balls. Its height vanishes on one vertices and is at most the global zero density on zero vertices. A zero run of length one or two contributes exactly its maximum height times the number of incident transition edges, including boundary pairs because their two maximal values coincide. This proves the exact global defect, the ballwise if-and-only-if equality condition and the infinitely many single-zero sharp examples. The three-zero local counterexample is checked by exact fractions. No finite enumeration is invoked as proof of all \(n\).

## Originality
PASS on the inspected literature and mathematical-record comparisons, with normal publication-priority uncertainty. The relevant 2026 primary paper poses the unrestricted finite-path constant and does not furnish this exact support-density/zero-run defect. Bilz--Weigt's 2021 preprint handles \(\mathbb Z\) at constant one; Hagelstein--Kosz--Stempak's 2024 preprint concerns different endpoint terms and infinite-domain settings. The prior finite-path unimodal and monotone records do not imply this class of arbitrarily many separated indicator components. No equivalent or stronger runwise finite-path statement appeared in targeted searches. An unindexed formulation in terms of block oscillation or ball densities remains possible.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The unsettled path-graph variation conjecture motivates natural positive classes. The theorem covers genuinely nonunimodal binary data of unbounded oscillation count; gives a density-strengthened coefficient, an exact nonnegative quantitative deficit and complete equality certificate; and identifies a sharp local-combinatorial boundary at the first gap length that permits within-gap re-oscillation. The theorem is not the unrestricted conjecture, and its proof is intentionally elementary.
