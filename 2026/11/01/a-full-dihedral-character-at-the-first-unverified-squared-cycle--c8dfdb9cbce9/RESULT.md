# A full dihedral character at the first unverified squared-cycle cut complex

## Finding
Let \(W_{21}=C_{21}^{2}\) be the simple graph with vertex set \(\mathbb Z/21\mathbb Z\) and edges at cyclic distances one or two. For any graph \(G\), the *ordinary* \(k\)-cut complex \(\Delta_k(G)\) is generated by vertex complements of disconnected induced \(k\)-vertex subgraphs. Put \(X=\Delta_{16}(W_{21})\). Then the following **integral homotopy equivalence** holds:
\[
 X\simeq S^3\vee\bigvee^{637}S^4.
\]

The complete rational homology action of the natural dihedral group \(D_{21}=\langle r,s\mid r^{21}=s^2=1,\;srs=r^{-1}\rangle\), where \(r(i)=i+1\) and \(s(i)=-i\), is as follows. The one-dimensional \(\widetilde H_3(X;\mathbb Q)\) is trivial. On \(\widetilde H_4(X;\mathbb Q)\), the character \(\chi_4\) satisfies
\[
 \chi_4(1)=637,\qquad
 \chi_4(r^7)=\chi_4(r^{14})=7,\qquad
 \chi_4(r^j)=0\ (j\notin\{0,7,14\}),\qquad
 \chi_4(r^js)=-7\ (0\le j<21).
\]
Consequently the dihedral quotient has reduced rational Betti numbers \(\widetilde\beta_3(X/D_{21};\mathbb Q)=1\) and \(\widetilde\beta_4(X/D_{21};\mathbb Q)=12\), all others zero. No quotient integral-homology or quotient homotopy classification is asserted.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are simple and unlabeled up to the displayed canonical labels; the calculation fixes the 21 labeled vertices for reproducibility. A facet of \(X\) is a five-set \(F\subset\mathbb Z/21\mathbb Z\) for which the graph induced on \(V\setminus F\) is disconnected. Thus \(X\) has dimension four. Every operation below uses integer simplicial chains and their standard orientations: each face is oriented by increasing vertex label. The 42 dihedral elements are specified by \(r^j\) and \(r^js\), with \(0\le j<21\).

This is an exact theorem for \(k=16\), \(n=21\). It is **not** an all-parameter proof of the conjectured \(\Delta_k(W_{k+5})\) homotopy shape, and no inference from finite sampling to all integers is made.

## Proof
**1. Complete face model.** Enumerate each of the \(\binom{21}{5}=20349\) five-subsets \(F\), and test connectivity of the induced graph on the 16-vertex complement using the explicit adjacency rule. Exactly 2520 subsets disconnect the complement. Taking all subfaces gives precisely 7106 augmented faces (counting the empty face), with ordinary face vector
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4)=(21,210,1330,3024,2520).
\]
The attached executable `verify.py` performs this complete enumeration from graph adjacency, without hard-coded facets.

**2. Exact acyclic matching.** Maintain the active set of all faces. For each pivot \(j=0,\ldots,20\), simultaneously pair every currently active nonempty face \(A\) not containing \(j\) with \(A\cup\{j\}\), whenever both are present. Remove both faces from the active set. Leave the empty face unpaired, and never match the resulting vertex \(\{0\}\). A pair is recorded in orientation low-to-high. The matching contains 3220 disjoint pairs; its ordinary critical cells are exactly one vertex, 14 three-simplices, and 650 four-simplices.

Reverse every matched Hasse arrow and retain the ordinary downward orientation of the other codimension-one face incidences. A complete topological sort of this directed graph traverses all 7105 nonempty faces and 29106 arrows, so there is no directed cycle. Discrete Morse theory therefore gives a CW complex with exactly these critical cells, with no positive cells in dimensions one or two.

**3. Signed integral Morse boundary.** Order the 14 critical three-simplices as
\[
 A_i=\{i+2,i+3,i+4,i+6\}\quad(0\le i\le12),
 \qquad B=\{1,2,4,20\}.
\]
For a four-vertex face \(Y\), let \(N(Y)\in\mathbb Z^{14}\) be its normal-form chain: a critical three-cell maps to its basis vector, an upper member of a matched two/three pair maps to zero, and a lower member of a matched three/four pair, paired to \(Z\), maps to
\[
 N(Y)=-\varepsilon_{Z,Y}
 \sum_{T\in\partial Z,\ T\ne Y}\varepsilon_{Z,T}N(T),
\]
where \(\varepsilon_{Z,T}\in\{\pm1\}\) is the ordinary signed simplicial incidence. Acyclicity makes this recursion well-defined. The integral Morse boundary of every critical four-cell \(Q\) is \(\sum_{Y\in\partial Q}\varepsilon_{Q,Y}N(Y)\).

The complete 14-by-650 computation has **exactly 13 nonzero columns**. They are indexed by
\[
 Q_i=\{i+2,i+3,i+4,19,20\}\quad(0\le i\le12),
\]
and each such column is \(e_i-e_{13}\), where \(e_{13}\) denotes the basis vector indexed by \(B\). Every other column is exactly zero over the integers. The first 13 rows of these 13 columns form the identity matrix, so the boundary has rank 13 and primitive image: these are integral unit pivots, not merely field ranks. Hence 13 degree-one cellular cancellations leave one three-cell and 637 four-cells with zero attaching degrees. Since there are no one- or two-cells, \(\pi_3(\bigvee^{14}S^3)\cong\mathbb Z^{14}\) by Hurewicz, so those degree-one pivots genuinely cancel homotopy cells. It follows that \(X\simeq S^3\vee\bigvee^{637}S^4\) integrally.

**4. A primitive invariant three-cycle.** The following signed sum of oriented three-simplices is an explicit integral cycle, written \([a,b,c,d]\) for the increasing orientation:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 z={}&+[0,1,2,4]+[0,2,3,4]-[0,1,2,6]-[0,2,3,6]\\
     &+[0,1,4,6]+[1,2,4,6]-[0,3,4,6]+[2,3,4,6].
\end{aligned}
\]
Direct simplicial boundary expansion gives \(\partial z=0\). Define the integral simplicial three-cochain \(c\) by \(c(Y)=\sum_{i=0}^{13}(N(Y))_i\) on each oriented three-simplex. The same integer normal-form recursion gives \(c(\partial Q)=0\) on **every** 2520 four-simplex, and \(c(z)=1\). Thus \(z\) is a primitive generator of the already established \(H_3(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\). For each of the 42 elements \(g\in D_{21}\), direct signed vertex permutation gives \(c(gz)=1\). Therefore every group element acts by \(+1\) on the integral three-dimensional homology generator.

**5. Entire rational character and quotient.** For any simplicial automorphism \(g\), the reduced Lefschetz number is computed directly on the finite chain model:
\[
 L_{\mathrm{red}}(g)=-1+\sum_{\substack{\varnothing\ne F\in X\\gF=F}}(-1)^{|F|-1}
 \operatorname{sgn}(g|_F),
\]
where the sign is the permutation sign on the ordered vertices of a setwise fixed simplex. Exhaustive signed counts for the full group give \(L_{\mathrm{red}}(1)=636\), \(L_{\mathrm{red}}(r^7)=L_{\mathrm{red}}(r^{14})=6\), \(L_{\mathrm{red}}(r^j)=-1\) for the 18 other nonidentity rotations, and \(L_{\mathrm{red}}(r^js)=-8\) for every reflection. Because only reduced homology degrees three and four survive and the degree-three trace is one, \(L_{\mathrm{red}}(g)=-1+\chi_4(g)\). This yields the stated character.

For the finite group action, rational homology of the quotient agrees with the invariant part of rational homology (by transfer or the invariant chain model after barycentric subdivision). Averaging the fourth-degree character gives
\[
 \dim H_4(X;\mathbb Q)^{D_{21}}
 =\frac{637+2\cdot7+18\cdot0+21\cdot(-7)}{42}=12.
\]
The degree-three invariant rank is one.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` using Python 3 and the standard library. It builds the graph and complex, checks all faces and matching pairs, topologically sorts every reversed Hasse incidence, evaluates **all 650 exact signed Morse-boundary columns**, verifies the 13 unit pivots, constructs and checks the primitive eight-term integral cycle and cocycle, computes all 42 signed Lefschetz values and cycle-pairings, and independently computes the mod-two boundary ranks \(\operatorname{rank}(\partial_3)=1140\) and \(\operatorname{rank}(\partial_4)=1883\) on the original simplicial complex. The output must agree byte-for-byte with `verification_output.txt` and begin with `SQUARED_CYCLE_21_16_EXACT_OK`.

The complete finite enumeration is exhaustive for this precisely specified graph, rather than a sampling argument. Exact algebraic topology and discrete-Morse arguments, not the numerical values alone, establish the homotopy equivalence. The complete dihedral character is proved by signed face enumeration and the independently exhibited primitive cycle.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer and collaborators [1, §7.4] conjectured the two-dimension sphere-wedge shape for all \(\Delta_k(W_{k+5})\), and reported a fourth-homology multiplicity formula supported computationally only for \(3\le k\le15\). The present \(k=16\) result is the first integer beyond that stated range; it supplies a full **integral homotopy proof**, rather than just another rational Betti count, as well as the hitherto undetermined rational action of **all** dihedral symmetries and quotient ranks. Their published cyclic symmetry result, Proposition 7.29, concerns \(k=2\) and the distinct complex \(\Delta_k(W_{k+4})\), not the present \(k=16\), \(n=k+5\) instance.

A prior public all-parameter face-vector calculation [4] implies the Euler characteristic and conditional numerical prediction \(\beta(16)=637\) but **does not prove** a homotopy equivalence, integral torsion-freeness, the three-dimensional symmetry character, the fourth-degree dihedral character, or quotient ranks. Results [2,3] treat ordinary **three-cut** complexes, rather than the sixteen-cut complex. Results for *total* cut complexes instead impose independent-set complement conditions, an inequivalent definition, and do not cover this theorem.

## Limitations
Only \(n=21\), \(k=16\) is proved. The character is over the rationals in degree four; the integral \(D_{21}\)-representation lattice, integral homology of the quotient, and the quotient homotopy type are not determined. This does not settle the infinite-family Conjecture 7.23. Although primary sources, the closest public face-vector result, indexed mathematical records, and publicly accessible related findings were compared, unindexed equivalent results cannot be excluded absolutely. No independent external audit has been performed.
