# A hidden index-two relation in the seven-vertex complete-graph two-particle space

## Finding
Let \(X=\operatorname{UConf}_2(|K_7|)\) be the space of two distinct unordered points on the ordinary geometric complete graph with seven labeled vertices. Its integral second homology is freely generated by classes of products of two vertex-disjoint triangles, subject to explicit relations. There are \(70\) natural oriented triangle-pair classes and \(35\) elementary four-term relations, one for each three-subset of the seven vertices. **Those elementary relations do not give the complete integral presentation:** their lattice has index exactly \(2\) in the lattice of all relations. Adding one explicitly oriented ten-term relation from any six-vertex subgraph gives the complete presentation. Precisely,
\[
\mathbb Z^{70}/\langle\text{35 four-term relations}\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^{35}\oplus\mathbb Z/2,
\qquad H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{35},
\]
and the nonzero order-two element on the left is annihilated by the added ten-term relation. Thus integral presentation by local four-term identities fails even though those identities span the rational relation space.

## Assumptions and scope
The complete graph has vertices \(V=\{0,\ldots,6\}\), edges oriented from smaller to larger vertex, and each ordered three-subset \(a<b<c\) defines the oriented triangle \([a,b]+[b,c]-[a,c]\). For each pair of disjoint three-subsets \(A,B\), orient the product of their cycles in the order determined by lexicographic comparison \(A<B\). The product represents a class \(z_{A,B}\) in the **unordered, collision-free** two-point configuration space. There are \(7\binom63/2=70\) such classes. No assertion is made for particles with permitted collisions or for arbitrary larger graphs.

For every three-subset \(A\), write its four-element complement as \(B=(b_0<b_1<b_2<b_3)\), and set \(C_j=B\setminus\{b_j\}\). Define \(s(A,C)=+1\) for \(A<C\) lexicographically and \(-1\) otherwise. The local relation is
\[
 r_A=\sum_{j=0}^3(-1)^j s(A,C_j)z_{\min(A,C_j),\max(A,C_j)}=0.
\]
For the six-vertex subset \(U=\{1,\ldots,6\}\), define \(r_U\) to be the sum over its ten unordered partitions \(U=A\sqcup B\) into triples with \(A<B\), weighted by the parity of the permutation \((A,B)\) relative to increasing \(U\):
\[
 r_U=\sum_{U=A\sqcup B,\ A<B}\operatorname{sgn}(A\mathbin\Vert B)z_{A,B}=0.
\]
These definitions specify all coefficients without choice of orientation beyond the stated ordering.

## Proof
Use the standard discrete unordered two-particle cubical configuration model for a graph. For \(K_7\), the model is a deformation retract because every simple cycle has at least three edges and any edge path between distinct vertices of valence at least three has at least one edge; these are the two-particle subdivision conditions. Its cubical second chain group has one generator for each unordered pair of vertex-disjoint edges, hence rank \(105\). Its first chain group has one generator for each edge paired with a nonincident vertex, also rank \(105\). A second cube \((e,f)\), ordered lexicographically with edges oriented increasingly, has boundary \((f,b)-(f,a)-(e,d)+(e,c)\) for \(e=(a,b), f=(c,d)\).

Let \(D\) be this \(105\)-by-\(105\) integer boundary matrix and \(T\) the \(105\)-by-\(70\) matrix whose columns are the oriented product-torus cycles. The columns are two-cycles since \(DT=0\). Exact normal-form computation on these integer matrices yields: \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_3}(D)=70\); the column Hermite basis of \(T\) has rank \(35\); all \(35\) nonzero Smith entries of this basis are \(1\). Consequently \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}(D)\ge70\), while \(DT=0\) and \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}(T)=35\) force \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}(D)\le70\). Thus \(\ker D\) has rank exactly \(35\). The column lattice of \(T\) is primitive in \(\mathbb Z^{105}\), so \(\operatorname{im}T=\ker D\), since both are saturated sublattices of the same rank. No nonzero cubic boundaries exist in this two-dimensional model, so \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\ker D\cong\mathbb Z^{35}\).

Form a \(70\)-by-\(35\) matrix \(R\), with column \(r_A\) as above. A direct integer multiplication yields \(TR=0\). Exact Smith reduction gives the nonzero invariant factors of \(R\) as \(34\) copies of \(1\) and one copy of \(2\). Thus \(\operatorname{rank}R=35\), its image has index \(2\) in the saturated rank-\(35\) lattice \(\ker T\), and \(\mathbb Z^{70}/\operatorname{im}R\cong\mathbb Z^{35}\oplus\mathbb Z/2\). The ten-term column \(r_U\) satisfies \(Tr_U=0\). The augmented matrix \([R\mid r_U]\) still has rank \(35\), but its nonzero Smith entries are all \(1\). Hence its image is saturated in \(\mathbb Z^{70}\), and since it is contained in \(\ker T\) with equal rank, it equals \(\ker T\). This is the asserted complete integral presentation.

## Relationship to prior work
Barnett and Farber's 2009 intersection theory and Barnett's 2010 thesis provide foundational calculations for two-point graph configuration spaces, including complete graphs and the cellular model. Ramos and Yun (2026), Theorem 3.10, already establish the **rational** \(S_n\)-representation of second homology of unordered two-point configurations of complete graphs; the rank \(35\) and rational span of torus classes are therefore not claimed as independently new. The present finding concerns the **integral index-two obstruction in the explicit local relation lattice** for the first seven-vertex case, and the concrete ten-term integral correction; neither statement was located in the inspected literature or the available published mathematical research records.

## Limitations
The conclusion is a finite, exact integral presentation only for \(K_7\). It does not determine the corresponding relation lattices for every complete graph or classify arbitrary minimal relation supports. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed. Equivalent unindexed integral presentations may exist. The computation needs SymPy's exact integer normal forms, and no independent proof avoiding finite matrix reduction is asserted.
