# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** The reduction from a uniformly distributed point in the top dyadic cell to a geometric-reset birth–death hitting time is exact. The bounded solution of the hitting-time recurrence gives the displayed algebraic transform. Singularities at both branchpoints must be included below the threshold; the sign at the negative branchpoint supplies the nontrivial parity term. At resonance the positive pole merges with the positive branchpoint, and above resonance the pole residue is explicit. Exact-fraction coefficients and separate finite-state evolution agree for five parameter choices and 26 time coefficients each. Numerical asymptotic ratios are supporting checks, not the infinite proof.

**Originality (PASS on inspected comparisons; residual risk acknowledged).** Yan et al. establish the model, density and autocorrelations. Monthus already gives the reset-chain structure and exact mixed-absorption transform, and even reports the \(p=4/5\) rate crossover for *position correlations*. These facts are not claimed as new. However her Appendix B.3 formula (296) extrapolates the \(n^{-3/2}\) absorbing-excursion law over all \(p>1/2\), while her preceding summation (291) requires \(p<4/5\). The explicit sharp first-return coefficients across this boundary, their critical \(n^{-1/2}\) exponent, and the two-parity constants were not found stated in inspected sources. The closed-form transform means an independent equivalent singularity analysis is a genuine priority risk; no uniqueness is inferred from the database search.

**Value (PASS).** Return-time tails measure recurrence and rare long excursions, not simply mean recurrence or correlation eigenvalue. A change in both exponential scale and polynomial prefactor alters excursion probabilities by unbounded factors, so the boundary distinguishes different first-return statistical mechanisms. The exact parity constants are necessary for a correct pointwise discrete-time asymptotic. This is a natural sharp classification for a fixed binary recurrence set, not an arbitrary parameter sample.

The comparisons include Yan et al. (arXiv:2308.09269), Monthus (arXiv:2409.19999, Sections IV and VI, Appendix B), and relevant portions of Brevitt–Klages (arXiv:2607.19084). The original 1984 paper was not fully accessible. The asymptotics apply at each fixed switching parameter, not uniformly in a shrinking neighborhood of the collision.
