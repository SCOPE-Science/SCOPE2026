# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The centered variance is strictly convex on the quotient by constants, so each maximizer is a vertex of the finite Lipschitz polytope. A vertex must have a connected graph of tight distance constraints. For two clusters at mutual distance \(1\) and internal distance \(0<s<1\), all tight cross constraints share an orientation and force a four-level potential. A pairwise exchange preserving the total sum strictly increases squared mass when both clusters split, and a direct strict inequality eliminates splitting of the smaller cluster. This yields the explicit integer maximum. Completing the square proves nearest-integer extremizers and the exact jump law. The equilateral endpoint is checked separately. The packaged checker independently enumerates all Lipschitz polytope vertices for 27 small parameter instances and gives exact rational diagnostics for 600 cases, plus 122 slope crossings. The universal theorem relies on the proof, not finite tests.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Actual topical searches for sharp variance of one-Lipschitz functions on finite two-level ultrametrics, optimal Lipschitz-to-Hilbert centering norms, and finite sharp Poincaré constants did not locate the stated two-cluster formula or the integer-phase ladder. Barthe–O'Connell (DOI:10.1051/ps:2008018, sections 1–3) evaluates continuous Gaussian and Euclidean-ball laws. Cúth–Doucha (arXiv:1411.2434v1, sections 1–3) studies ultrametric free-space structure, not this fixed centered norm. Nakajima–Shioya (arXiv:1801.01302v1, variance and isoperimetric-rigidity sections) imposes geometric hypotheses not met by these discrete ultrametrics. The inspected published four-point Hilbert Banach–Mazur and clustered-projection results concern different operator optimizations and do not imply this variance law. An exact fixed-profile variance-to-range law (record cdf5b2d6b4a3) gives a global fixed-mass range inequality without within-cluster oscillation constraints and therefore does not calculate the present constrained supremum. A hidden equivalent formula in a concentration or hierarchical-metric paper remains possible; database nonappearance is not a uniqueness proof.

## Value — PASS
The classical sharp Poincaré variance problem for Lipschitz observables is a natural fixed-function-space invariant, independent of any arbitrary enumeration choice. The theorem settles every cluster size and every scale in an infinite ultrametric family and gives exact optimizer geometry rather than a bound. The transition threshold \(s=2L/(N-1)\), integer-phase changes, and universal nonzero cusp increment \(2L/N\) distinguish strong imbalance from balanced clusters and reveal arithmetic sensitivity not implied by the cited structural Banach-space results. These features can serve as exact benchmarks for hierarchical concentration and sharp finite metric inequalities.

## Closest antecedents and residual limitations
The closest conceptual antecedent is Barthe–O'Connell's maximization of variance of Lipschitz functions; its explicit solved distributions are continuous. The closer metric-space antecedents Cúth–Doucha and the inspected clustered free-space projection calculation share the domain but not the invariant. Scope is finite uniform two-level ultrametrics and real \(L^2\) observables; this is not a general sharp variance theorem for arbitrary ultrametrics. Independent audit has not been performed.
