# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The two-independence condition is evaluated exhaustively on every one of the 2,047 nonempty subsets of a precisely specified 11-vertex graph. The complete 105-pair relative collapse is checked step by step against the current face poset, verifying both true freeness and unchanged target. The independent 50-pair acyclic matching on the target is checked on all 255 Hasse incidences, leaving one critical vertex and one critical two-cell, which proves an integral sphere homotopy type by the discrete-Morse CW theorem. Independent mod-two boundary reductions reproduce the expected Betti groups. Critical numerical data are derived from the actual embedded certificates, not inferred from a log alone.

## Originality — PASS against inspected evidence

The closest exact published results are the higher-independence classifications in Deshpande–Singh (arXiv:2001.05448) and the chordal graph theorem of Deshpande–Shukla–Singh (arXiv:2001.06775), neither of which covers the nonchordal Grötzsch graph. The ordinary Mycielskian independence results of Goyal–Shukla–Singh (arXiv:1905.06926) and Carnero Bravo (arXiv:2601.13432) concern only cutoff one and do not give the cutoff-one to cutoff-two inclusion collapse. Source-text searches and accessible research-record comparisons under the aliases Grötzsch/Mycielski–Grötzsch, two-independence/dissociation, and simple-homotopy collapse found related but nonidentical claims. No statement inspected implies the 105-pair relative collapse. An unindexed equivalent calculation remains possible.

## Value — PASS

The 11-vertex Grötzsch graph is the canonical smallest triangle-free four-chromatic graph, and cutoff two is the first new induced-component restriction beyond the standard independence complex. A verified inclusion-preserving **simplicial collapse** is stronger than a coincidental equality of homology ranks or abstract homotopy types. It establishes an exact stability phenomenon for a natural graph with high chromatic complexity and supplies an explicit finite benchmark for proposed broader Mycielski/clustered-independence statements. The claim does not pretend to settle an infinite family.

## Scientific limitations

The certificate proves the assertion only for this graph and these two cutoffs. More general graph-class implications are not asserted. Stronger published results not found in accessible searches could still subsume the finite case; independent scholarly review has not been performed.
