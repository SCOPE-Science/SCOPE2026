# A sharp all-dimension cubic sampling gap for the third hypersimplex

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge6\), let \(\mathcal X_n\subset S^{n-2}\) be the centered and normalized vertices of the third hypersimplex,
\[
 x_S=\frac{\mathbf1_S-(3/n)\mathbf1}{\sqrt{3(n-3)/n}},\qquad S\subset\{1,\ldots,n\},\quad |S|=3,
\]
inside the hyperplane \(\sum_i x_i=0\). Let \(\mathcal H_3(S^{n-2})\) be the real spherical harmonics of **exact** degree three, and let \(E_n:\mathcal H_3(S^{n-2})\to\mathbb R^{\binom n3}\) evaluate them at the indicated points. With \(P_{n,3}(1)=1\), form the **unscaled** normalized-zonal Gram matrix
\[
 K_n(S,T)=P_{n,3}(\langle x_S,x_T\rangle),\qquad
 P_{n,3}(z)=\frac{(n+1)z^3-3z}{n-2}.
\]
Its complete eigenvalue list consists of the following four rational numbers, indexed by \(r=0,1,2,3\), with multiplicities \(\binom nr-\binom n{r-1}\), where \(\binom n{-1}=0\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_0(n)&=\frac{n(n-6)^2(n+1)}{18(n-3)(n-2)},\\
\lambda_1(n)&=\frac{n^2(n^2-17n+54)^2}{54(n-3)^3(n-2)},\\
\lambda_2(n)&=\frac{2n^2(n-6)^2(n+1)}{9(n-3)^3(n-2)},\\
\lambda_3(n)&=\frac{2n^3(n+1)}{9(n-3)^3(n-2)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
At \(n=6\), the evaluation rank is exactly \(10\), because the ten complementary pairs of triple-subsets give antipodal vectors. For **every** integer \(n\ge7\), the evaluation is onto the complete \(\binom n3\)-dimensional data space; degree three is the *smallest exact harmonic degree* for which surjectivity is possible.

The sharp **dimension-uniform lower sampling eigenvalue** over all integer \(n\ge7\) is
\[
 \boxed{\inf_{n\ge7}\lambda_{\min}(K_n)=\frac{169}{148500}},
\]
and equality holds **only at \(n=13\)**. At that dimension the minimum eigenvalue belongs to the \(r=1\) Johnson component and has multiplicity \(12\). This is a sharp, positive lower frame bound in the normalization \(P_{n,3}(1)=1\), with no rescaling by the number of vertices or by harmonic dimension. In particular, the integer dimension \(13\), rather than a small-dimensional endpoint or a large-dimensional limit, is the uniquely least stable member of this family in absolute minimal-eigenvalue terms.

## Assumptions and scope
All coordinates and inner products are Euclidean. There is exactly one point for each unordered three-subset; no antipodal representatives are discarded. The ambient Euclidean dimension is \(n-1\), so the normalized Gegenbauer parameter is \((n-3)/2\). Sampling means evaluation of **exact degree three**, not a sum of harmonic degrees. Gram eigenvalues are those of the kernel with diagonal entries equal to one. The lower bound is across integer dimensions, not a minimization over continuous noninteger parameters. No assertion is made about other harmonic degrees, other ranks of hypersimplices, or optimal conditioning after rescaling individual data points.

## Proof
**Step 1: geometry and reproducing kernel.** If \(c=|S\cap T|\), direct calculation gives
\[
 \langle x_S,x_T\rangle=\frac{nc-9}{3(n-3)}.
\]
The spherical-harmonic addition theorem shows that the positive-semidefinite evaluation Gram is a strictly positive scalar multiple of the matrix of the normalized degree-three zonal kernel \(P_{n,3}\) above. In particular, its rank equals that of \(E_n\), and the stated Gram normalization is precise.

**Step 2: Johnson incidence decomposition.** Let \(V_t\) be the subspace of functions on three-subsets generated by indicators \(S\mapsto\mathbf1_{\{U\subseteq S\}}\), where \(|U|=t\). For \(n\ge6\), the nested filtration \(V_0\subset V_1\subset V_2\subset V_3\) has successive orthogonal layers \(H_r=V_r\cap V_{r-1}^{\perp}\) (with \(V_{-1}=0\)); their dimensions are \(\binom nr-\binom n{r-1}\). Define \(M_t(S,T)=\binom{|S\cap T|}{t}\), so \(M_t=W_tW_t^{\mathsf T}\) for the triple-to-\(t\)-subset inclusion matrix. Counting shared subsets and subtracting successive incidence layers shows that \(M_t\) acts on \(H_r\) as zero for \(t<r\) and otherwise as the scalar
\[
 \mu_t(r)=\binom{3-r}{t-r}\binom{n-t-r}{3-t}.
\]
This is the classical Johnson-scheme inclusion-matrix identity; it may also be checked directly by applying \(M_t\) to an alternating \(r\)-fold inclusion indicator and counting completions of prescribed subsets.

**Step 3: exact Gram spectrum.** Write the cubic function of \(c\) in the Newton binomial basis,
\[
 P_{n,3}\!\left(\frac{nc-9}{3(n-3)}\right)
 =\sum_{t=0}^{3}\alpha_t(n)\binom ct.
\]
Its four forward-difference coefficients are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_0&=\frac{9(n^2-9n+6)}{(n-3)^3(n-2)},\\
\alpha_1&=\frac{n^2(n^2-53n+378)}{27(n-3)^3(n-2)},\\
\alpha_2&=\frac{2n^2(n-9)(n+1)}{9(n-3)^3(n-2)},\\
\alpha_3&=\frac{2n^3(n+1)}{9(n-3)^3(n-2)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(K_n=\sum_t\alpha_tM_t\), and \(\lambda_r=\sum_{t=r}^3\alpha_t\mu_t(r)\). Simplifying this last expression gives precisely the four factored eigenvalues stated in the Finding. Every step is an exact rational polynomial identity; there are no numerical eigenvalue approximations in the proof.

**Step 4: rank and minimal degree.** The \(r=1\) eigenvalue can vanish at integer \(n\) only if \(n^2-17n+54=0\); its discriminant is \(73\), not a square. The other three eigenvalues have their only allowable zeros at \(n=6\). Hence \(\operatorname{rank}E_6=5+5=10\) and \(\operatorname{rank}E_n=\binom n3\) for all integer \(n\ge7\). When \(n\ge7\), the dimension of the exact-degree-two harmonic space is \(\binom n2-1<\binom n3\), and lower degrees are smaller, so degree three is the sharp first possible surjective degree.

**Step 5: uniform lower bound and its unique equality.** For integers \(n\ge7\), the first eigenvalue is larger than \(1/18\), because \(n(n+1)>(n-3)(n-2)\) and \((n-6)^2\ge1\). The second nonstandard eigenvalue obeys \(\lambda_2(n)>1/18\), from
\[
 \frac{(n-6)^2}{n-3}\ge\frac14,\qquad
 \frac{n^2(n+1)}{(n-3)^2(n-2)}>1.
\]
Also \(\lambda_3(n)>2/9\). Each of these bounds is strictly above \(169/148500\).

For the remaining \(\lambda_1(x)\), viewed temporarily as a real function for \(x\ge7\), differentiation yields
\[
 \lambda_1'(x)=\frac{x(x^2-17x+54)R(x)}{54(x-3)^4(x-2)^2},
\quad R(x)=2x^4-21x^3+115x^2-462x+648.
\]
The identity
\[
 R(7+u)=2u^4+35u^3+262u^2+805u+648>0\qquad(u\ge0)
\]
shows that \(\lambda_1\) decreases up to the larger real root \((17+\sqrt{73})/2\in(12,13)\), and increases thereafter. The integer minimizer must therefore be \(12\) or \(13\). Direct exact evaluation gives
\[
 \lambda_1(12)=\frac{16}{1215}>\frac{169}{148500}=\lambda_1(13).
\]
This proves the claimed sharp, uniquely attained dimension-uniform lower bound; its eigenspace dimension is \(\binom{13}{1}-\binom{13}{0}=12\).

## Relationship to prior work
The harmonic-index design literature supplies the zonal addition-theorem framework but does not identify the exact cubic sampling gap of the third hypersimplex or its sharp integer-dimension minimizer. Johnson association-scheme theory (including Krebs--Shaheen) supplies the general primitive-eigenspace and Eberlein-polynomial machinery, which is used explicitly here rather than claimed as new. In particular, the general eigenmatrix formulas imply that **a fixed** polynomial of subset-intersection size has a decomposable spectrum; they do not themselves state or settle the **sharp minimization across all integer dimensions** of this specifically normalized spherical cubic Gram. The present original target is the sharp \(169/148500\) lower bound and unique \(n=13\) equality, supported by the explicit four-level formula. The rank-three slice is distinct from rank-two simplex-edge midpoint configurations.

## Limitations
This is an exact-degree cubic interpolation theorem for the rank-three hypersimplex only. The spectrum is normalized at \(P_{n,3}(1)=1\); altering this normalization changes the numerical frame bound. A standard Johnson eigenvalue theorem makes the fixed-dimensional spectral decomposition accessible; no independence from classical association-scheme identities is claimed. Full equivalence with every historical treatment of hypersimplex sampling has not been independently ruled out. The result has undergone internal self-review, not independent audit or validation.
