# Mathematical review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The graph distance really takes values \(0,1,2\) on wheel vertices. The rim block and hub sector give a full orthogonal spectral splitting. Strict convexity gives a unique nonunit rim obstruction root; strict concavity gives a unique hub obstruction root from order six. Explicit rational inequalities prove the crossover at order nine, including the exceptional seven-cycle comparison. The threshold nullities and asymptotic expansion follow from the same complete decomposition. The standalone checker passed its exact rational sign checks and 351 direct matrix eigenvalue checks. The proof, not finite tests, establishes arbitrary order.

## Originality

**PASS under bounded comparison.** The closest wheel-specific papers compute conditional negative definiteness of the unsquared distance \(d\), not the positive-semidefinite parameter set of \(\exp(-t d^2)\). The broad geodesic-kernel reference characterizes global positivity under different hypotheses. Published-database search results about wheel edge polytopes, graph distance matrices, and cyclic Gaussian kernels do not imply the identified order-nine mode switch or the logarithmic bandwidth asymptotic. Residual risk: a general diameter-two matrix-kernel theorem could contain this case after specialization, though none was identified in the inspected material.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** This is a complete classification of a natural family of Gaussian covariance matrices generated by a canonical graph metric. It distinguishes two spectral obstructions and pinpoints where the controlling mechanism switches as graph size grows; the sharp scale diverges as half the logarithm of the rim size. In graph-based interpolation, positivity is a prerequisite for a valid covariance matrix, so the precise admissible bandwidth interval and threshold nullspace have a natural interpretation. This is an exact finite-geometry result rather than an unmotivated sample or a generic spectral computation.
