# A sharp inverse-chi moment frontier for Gaussian-sketched Arnoldi Hessenberg noise

## Finding

**Theorem (three-step Hessenberg coefficient, all sketch sizes).** Let \(A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1&0\\1&2&1\\0&1&2\end{pmatrix}\), let the initial Krylov vector be \(e_1\in\mathbb R^3\), and let \(\Omega\in\mathbb R^{\ell\times3}\) have independent \(N(0,1/\ell)\) entries, where \(\ell\ge3\) is an integer. In exact arithmetic, perform sketch-inner-product modified Gram–Schmidt Arnoldi for three columns, using the same sketch throughout, and record the above-tridiagonal Hessenberg coefficient \(h_{13}\) when processing the third column, before any terminal normalization. Then the following conclusions hold, without conditioning on a subspace-embedding event.

1. For every real \(p>0\), \(\mathbb E|h_{13}|^p<\infty\) if and only if \(p<\ell-2\). In particular, \(\mathbb E|h_{13}|=\infty\) for \(\ell=3\), and \(\mathbb E[h_{13}^{2}]=\infty\) for \(\ell=3,4\), whereas \(\mathbb E[h_{13}^{2}]<\infty\) for all \(\ell\ge5\). The law is symmetric and nondegenerate.
2. Let \(G=\sqrt\ell\,\Omega=QR\) be the positive-diagonal thin QR decomposition. Put \(r_{ij}=R_{ij}\), \(k=\ell-2\), and
   \[
   C_\ell=-\frac{r_{11}^{2}r_{23}-r_{11}r_{12}r_{22}+r_{11}r_{13}r_{23}-r_{12}^{2}r_{23}+r_{12}r_{13}r_{22}}{r_{11}r_{22}}.
   \]
   Then \(h_{13}=C_\ell/r_{33}\), and the exact nonzero two-sided tail constant is
   \[
   \lim_{t\to\infty}t^{k}\mathbb P(|h_{13}|>t)
   =\frac{\mathbb E|C_\ell|^k}{2^{k/2}\Gamma(k/2+1)}\in(0,\infty).
   \]
3. As the sketch size grows, \(\sqrt\ell\,h_{13}\) converges in distribution to a centered Gaussian with variance \(2\):
   \[
   \sqrt\ell\,h_{13}\ \xrightarrow{d}\ N(0,2).
   \]
   Thus Gaussian central fluctuations coexist with a sharp finite-sketch inverse-chi moment obstruction.

## Assumptions and scope

The sketch is **one fixed Gaussian matrix** across the three exact-arithmetic Arnoldi steps, not independently redrawn at each inner product. All normalizations use Euclidean norms of the sketched vectors, and inner products use the sketched images. The positive-diagonal QR convention removes signs. The third column can be processed before the Krylov process terminates in dimension three; the claim does not require a fourth basis vector. The matrix is symmetric positive definite, with eigenvalues \(2-\sqrt2\), \(2\), and \(2+\sqrt2\), and its unsketched Arnoldi Hessenberg is tridiagonal. All statements are unconditional over the Gaussian sketch. The primary mathematical problem is an exact coefficient-distribution question in an iterative numerical linear-algebra algorithm; no convergence assertion for a linear-system solve is made.

## Proof

**1. Identify the sketched Arnoldi basis.** Since \(e_1,Ae_1,A^2e_1\) are a Krylov basis and have successive leading coordinates equal to one, the coefficient vectors generated by sketch-modified Gram–Schmidt agree with the ordered \(\Omega\)-orthogonalization of \(e_1,e_2,e_3\): subtracting lower-index columns produces the next coordinate direction modulo their span. The corresponding sketched orthonormal basis has matrix \(Q\). Because \(G=QR\), the unsketched basis matrix is \(V=\sqrt\ell R^{-1}\). Consequently its three-column representation is \(H=V^{-1}AV=RAR^{-1}\). This is a similarity representation, not an assertion that \(V\) is ordinarily orthogonal. The observed \(h_{13}\) is the \((1,3)\) entry of \(H\).

**2. Compute the coefficient.** Matrix multiplication gives
\[
h_{13}=-\frac{r_{11}^{2}r_{23}-r_{11}r_{12}r_{22}+r_{11}r_{13}r_{23}-r_{12}^{2}r_{23}+r_{12}r_{13}r_{22}}{r_{11}r_{22}r_{33}}=\frac{C_\ell}{r_{33}}.
\]
The shift \(2I\) contributes no above-diagonal entry; only the two unit neighbor couplings contribute. This identity is also independently verified by the directly replayed sketched Arnoldi recurrence in `verify.py`.

**3. Apply the Gaussian QR distribution.** The Bartlett decomposition of a standard Gaussian \(\ell\times3\) matrix gives independent diagonal variables \(r_{11}\sim\chi_\ell\), \(r_{22}\sim\chi_{\ell-1}\), \(r_{33}\sim\chi_{\ell-2}\), with \(r_{12},r_{13},r_{23}\) independent standard normals, jointly independent of the diagonal. In particular \(C_\ell\) is independent of \(r_{33}\). The alternate expression
\[
C_\ell=r_{12}-\frac{r_{11}r_{23}}{r_{22}}-\frac{r_{13}r_{23}}{r_{22}}+\frac{r_{12}^{2}r_{23}}{r_{11}r_{22}}-\frac{r_{12}r_{13}}{r_{11}}
\]
shows that \(\mathbb E|C_\ell|^{p}<\infty\) for \(0<p\le\ell-2\), since \(\chi_m\) has finite inverse \(p\)-moment when \(p<m\), and all Gaussian positive moments exist. This is true even for \(p=\ell-2\) because the denominator degrees are \(\ell\) and \(\ell-1\). The numerator is not identically zero, and the continuous Bartlett density implies \(\mathbb P(C_\ell\ne0)=1\), so its \((\ell-2)\)-moment is strictly positive. The simultaneous sign change \((r_{12},r_{23})\mapsto(-r_{12},-r_{23})\), fixing the other variables, changes \(C_\ell\) to \(-C_\ell\); hence the law is symmetric.

For \(Z\sim\chi_k\), its density at zero behaves as a positive multiple of \(z^{k-1}\), so \(\mathbb E Z^{-p}<\infty\) exactly for \(p<k\). Independence and the preceding properties of \(C_\ell\) prove the claimed if-and-only-if moment criterion. When \(p\ge k\), the expectation diverges by Tonelli's theorem, not by a finite numerical experiment.

**4. Obtain the sharp tail.** The small-ball expansion of a chi variable gives
\[
\mathbb P(\chi_k\le u)\sim\frac{u^k}{2^{k/2}\Gamma(k/2+1)}\qquad(u\downarrow0).
\]
The same CDF is bounded above by a constant times \(u^k\) for every \(u\ge0\). Conditioning on \(C_\ell\) and using the integrable dominating function proportional to \(|C_\ell|^k\), dominated convergence yields the stated exact tail limit.

**5. Identify the Gaussian center.** Standard chi concentration gives \(r_{jj}/\sqrt\ell\to1\) in probability for each fixed \(j\in\{1,2,3\}\). The numerator representation in Step 3 therefore shows
\[
\sqrt\ell\,h_{13}=r_{12}-r_{23}+o_{\mathbb P}(1).
\]
Here the off-diagonal variables remain independent standard normals for every \(\ell\). Slutsky's theorem proves convergence to \(N(0,2)\).

## Verification

The independent script `verify.py` uses symbolic algebra to reconstruct the exact \((1,3)\) similarity coefficient, checks positive definiteness of \(A\), numerically reproduces the coefficient both by QR similarity and by explicitly applying the sketch-inner-product modified Gram–Schmidt Arnoldi recurrence to hundreds of independently seeded Gaussian matrices across several sketch sizes, and checks the small-chi-tail constant against the integral definition. All continuous-distribution and all-dimensional claims above are proved analytically; finite computational tests are regression checks only. To reproduce: `python verify.py` with the packages in `requirements.txt`.

## Relationship to prior work

Timsit, Grigori and Balabanov's randomized orthogonal projection methods develop sketched Krylov Arnoldi-type solvers, discuss above-superdiagonal Hessenberg coefficients, and use a Gaussian-noise modeling assumption motivated by numerics [1]. The present theorem tests that issue on the minimal nontrivial symmetric positive-definite tridiagonal matrix and gives an unconditional finite-sketch moment/tail classification. The randomized Gram–Schmidt method [2], randomized Cholesky QR work [3], and later randomized Krylov recurrence analyses [4] establish algorithms and/or stability guarantees but, in the relevant inspected sections, do not provide this exact \(h_{13}\) tail and moment law. Gaussian QR/Bartlett facts and elementary chi inverse moments are established mathematical ingredients, not claimed innovations. This comparison is not a certification of publication priority.

## Limitations

The theorem applies only to this three-dimensional path matrix, a fixed Gaussian sketch, exact arithmetic and the specified single coefficient; it does not assert identical moments for general matrices, other sketch distributions, later Arnoldi columns, conditional embedding events or floating-point implementations. The moment divergence arises from arbitrarily ill-conditioned sketch realizations of small probability and does not contradict high-probability embedding theorems. The Gaussian center is a weak-convergence statement and does not imply Gaussian tails or uniform moment bounds. Specialized publications on Gaussian QR ratio statistics or randomized Arnoldi coefficient distributions may already contain equivalent special cases; no independent research audit has been performed.
