For every convex planar body \(B\), the four-dimensional Cartesian square \(B\times B\) has four real negative Steiner roots exactly when its isoperimetric quotient \(4\pi A/P^2\) lies below the universal threshold \(0.8766744290\ldots\), with a single double-root boundary. In particular, Cartesian squares of regular polygons have four real Steiner roots for three through five sides, but only two for six or more sides.
