## Correctness — PASS

The group-theoretic reduction is reconstructed from Sylow orders and counts in \(A_5\) and from the unique Sylow subgroups of a finite nilpotent direct factor. It yields exact old-prime defects and new-prime reciprocal gains. The worst-case new-prime sums through seven and eight terms are verified as exact fractions. The known nine-prime witness attains the bound. The additional necessary old-prime structure at equality follows from the eight-new-prime sum bound. `CHECK.py` reproduces every finite arithmetic comparison, but the algebraic proof does not rely on empirical enumeration.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Zhang and Yang, arXiv:2605.20976, prove an exact necessary-and-sufficient compensation criterion for arbitrary nilpotent direct factors and exhibit nine-prime witnesses. Their full available text, including the introductory statement, Sections 3–5 and 11–13, was examined. It does not assert a minimum number of prime divisors, nor rule out *all* seven-term prime-power Egyptian solutions. The new extremal obstruction is more than substituting a value into the criterion: exact dominance inequalities force the first five possible new primes and leave an exhaustive two-case arithmetical contradiction. Their nine-prime construction and their general compensation formula remain explicitly credited as prior work. Anabanti and Asboei's earlier simple-group characterization is inapplicable to nilpotent extensions. Related research records on Egyptian fractions, Sylow flags and Frobenius groups concerned different objects or invariants. No equivalent minimum-support theorem was identified. Historical priority is not independently established.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The motivating paper gives a surprisingly large nine-prime nilpotent factor to invalidate a proposed characterization of \(A_5\), but construction alone does not explain whether fewer primes would suffice. The new bound shows that nine is **forced** for the *entire* natural nilpotent direct-product family, including noncyclic Sylow groups and arbitrary prime powers. It identifies the rigidity of every extremal old-prime component. This is a sharp, motivated arithmetic complexity threshold for a group-theoretic counterexample, not a table recomputation or a routine enlargement of the witness.
