# Review of the odd-prism maximal-section theorem

## Correctness
**PASS.** The exact count of crossed vertical and base edges proves that the maximal section has \(n+2\) sides if and only if precisely one projected base vertex lies above and one below the normal threshold. The odd regular-polygon order statistics provide the two exact angular cutoff curves. Integrating the explicit one-dimensional orientation density yields the inverse-sine law. The strict derivative-sign argument combines concavity of sine, convexity of tangent and strict convexity of odd powers of sine on the stated angular interval; it proves a unique optimum rather than relying on plots. A uniform Taylor expansion establishes the sharp asymptotic and optimizer limit. An independent numerical checker tests the geometry and integrations on multiple instances but is not the proof.

## Originality
**PASS, with residual historical-priority risk.** The inspected general treatment of hyperplane section chambers (Brandenburg–De Loera–Meroni, arXiv:2304.14239v1) does not give this probability law. Two close pre-existing records concern even-sided prisms and the three-sided base. The former uses paired antipodal extreme projected vertices, whereas the odd family has offset maxima; the latter does not imply the formula across all larger odd orders or the parity coefficient. Searches for the odd family, its inverse-sine probability and its asymptotic constants identified no equivalent or dominating checked statement. This is not proof that the result has never appeared elsewhere.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** This is a natural complete answer for the most complex central section of an infinite geometric family. The exact three-term formula, uniquely maximizing aspect ratio and precise factor-two odd/even asymptotic transition form a structural geometric probability phenomenon. The result fills the parity gap left by the available even-base analysis; it is not an arbitrary numerical case or a bare Crofton expectation.

## Scope and remaining risks
The theorem treats generic centroidal planes, odd \(n\ge5\), and unit-circumradius bases; it does not provide the full section-type law. It is possible that a less directly titled source contains a dominating equivalent, requiring external scholarly verification. No independent audit has been conducted.
