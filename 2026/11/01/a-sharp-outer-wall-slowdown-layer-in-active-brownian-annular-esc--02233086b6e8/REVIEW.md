# Self-review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The precise claim is the uniform-heading mean-exit-time weak-propulsion coefficient on a two-absorber annulus, followed by a sequential high-rotational-diffusion limit. The backward elliptic equation is reduced through two exact Dirichlet radial equations. A homogeneous modified-Bessel boundary-value solution yields exact identities for both boundary slopes. Its two rapidly decaying endpoint solutions give a nonzero negative slope of order \(\beta^{-3/2}\), while its outer second derivative is of order \(\beta^{-1/2}\). Their competition gives the sharp quadratic profile after scaling by \(\beta^{5/2}\) across the \(\beta^{-1}\) layer. The sign inequality \(\sinh t>t\) establishes strict positivity of the controlling constant at every annulus ratio. The local root is simple. The packaged verifier passed 171 independent finite checks, including direct comparison of the Bessel representation against a numerical boundary-value solver. Such checks support, but do not replace, the analytic proof. No fixed-nonzero-propulsion high-rotation assertion is made.

## Originality
**PASS, qualified.** The closest paper specifically concerning the annular absorbing geometry, Iyaniwura and Peng (arXiv:2506.15813v1), derives the mean-first-passage PDE, gives the first-order weak-activity term, and notes the uniform-heading correction begins at second order. It does not calculate that second-order term or classify its boundary signs and sharp fast-rotation layer. Di Trapani, Franosch and Caraglio (arXiv:2306.12702v1) study the fully absorbing disk using a broad propagator method, not the two-boundary annulus and its narrow positive weak-activity skin. The related disk-only susceptibility theorem is restricted to a disk and does not imply the annular reversal because the annular passive exit profile has a sign-changing radial derivative. Known general elliptic perturbation theory and Bessel asymptotics are used, not claimed as innovations. A wider unindexed absorbing-boundary perturbation theorem or a computation hidden in supplementary literature remains a plausible publication-priority risk; no comprehensive literature uniqueness guarantee is asserted.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** At first sight the large-rotation limit suggests effective enhanced diffusion and a uniformly decreased escape time. The exact two-wall boundary calculation reveals a qualitatively different sign in an extremely thin outer-wall region for an initially isotropic ensemble, with a nontrivial limiting profile and a sharp width proportional to \(\beta^{-1}\). The finding isolates a genuine nonuniformity of fast-angular-mixing approximations in a natural escape geometry and provides a testable analytic benchmark for boundary-resolved numerics. The positive effect is small, and its smallness and sequential-limit character are disclosed rather than overstated.
