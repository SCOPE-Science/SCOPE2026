Smooth threefold-symmetric constant-width ovals need not gain area monotonically when averaged with a rotated copy, unlike the Reuleaux triangle. In the natural two-harmonic family \(h=1+\varepsilon(a\cos3\phi+b\cos9\phi)\), the area increases strictly through the half-turn of the symmetry period exactly when \(a^2\ge30b^2\); otherwise two interior turning points appear at explicitly determined angles.
