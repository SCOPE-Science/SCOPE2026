Disk rounding of any convex Euclidean polygon has a universal exact self-Cheeger threshold \(t_*=\sqrt{A/\pi}\), but the Cheeger profile is only once differentiable there. Its sharp second-derivative jump is \(8\pi^2[\sum_i\cot(\alpha_i/2)-\pi]/(P+2\sqrt{\pi A})^3\), revealing an angular corner invariant and identifying equiangular polygons through an optimal inequality.
