# A sharp three-regime Fourier conditioning law for uniformly proper-coloured regular trees

## Finding
For integers \(q\ge3\), \(b\ge2\) and \(h\ge1\), let \(T_{b,h}\) be the rooted complete \(b\)-ary tree of height \(h\), with vertex set \(V\) of size \(N=(b^{h+1}-1)/(b-1)\). Uniformly choose a proper \(q\)-colouring \(X\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}^{V}\). In the normalized counting Hilbert space of these colourings, take the constant function and all one-coordinate additive characters \(\psi_{v,k}(X)=\exp(2\pi i kX_v/q)\), where \(v\in V\) and \(1\le k\le q-1\). These \(1+(q-1)N\) functions are linearly independent.

Write \(a=1/(q-1)\), \(t=\sqrt b\,a\), and \(u=t^2\). Define the real tridiagonal matrix \(B_h\) of order \(h+1\) by off-diagonal entries \(-t\) and diagonal entries
\[
1+(b-1)a^2,\quad\underbrace{1+t^2,\ldots,1+t^2}_{h-1\text{ entries}},\quad1.
\]
For \(1\le m\le h\), define \(D_m\) of order \(m\) with off-diagonal entries \(-t\) and diagonal entries \(1+t^2\) repeated \(m-1\) times, followed by \(1\). Every one of these matrices is strictly positive definite. The probability-normalized Gram spectrum of the stated functions is exactly the multiset formed by \(1\) once and, for each \(1\le k\le q-1\), the values
\[
\left\{\frac{1-a^2}{\beta}:\beta\in\operatorname{Spec}(B_h)\right\}
\quad\sqcup\quad
\bigsqcup_{\ell=0}^{h-1}
\left\{\frac{1-a^2}{\delta}:\delta\in\operatorname{Spec}(D_{h-\ell})\right\}^{(b-1)b^\ell}.
\]
Here the superscript on a multiset means repetition by that integer multiplicity, and all eigenvalues within a matrix are counted with multiplicity. The smallest and largest eigenvalues of the **nonconstant-coordinate** Gram operator are, respectively, \((1-a^2)/\lambda_{\max}(B_h)\) and \((1-a^2)/\lambda_{\min}(B_h)\). In particular its squared singular-value condition number is
\[
\kappa_h^2=\frac{\lambda_{\max}(B_h)}{\lambda_{\min}(B_h)}.
\]
For fixed \(b,q\), its complete sharp depth-asymptotic trichotomy is
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{lll}
0<t<1:&\displaystyle\kappa_h^2\longrightarrow\left(\frac{1+t}{1-t}\right)^2,&\text{bounded};\\[6pt]
t=1:&\displaystyle\frac{\kappa_h^2}{h^2}\longrightarrow\frac{16}{\pi^2},&\text{quadratic};\\[8pt]
t>1:&\displaystyle\frac{\kappa_h^2}{t^{2h}}\longrightarrow
(1+t)^2\frac{t^2(t^2-a^2)}{(1-a^2)(t^2-1)^2},&\text{exponential}.
\end{array}}
\]
Thus the condition-number transition occurs precisely at \(b=(q-1)^2\). The location matches the classical Kesten--Stigum broadcasting threshold; the claimed contribution is the full finite-depth Fourier sampling spectrum and the *sharp constants and growth laws* for the all-vertex coordinate frame, not the location of that threshold by itself.

## Assumptions and scope
All vertices, including the root and internal vertices, are sampled in the coordinate band. Every proper colouring is equiprobable, so there are exactly \(q(q-1)^{N-1}\) possible samples. Inner products use the mean over that finite set, not its unnormalized sum. Frequencies \(k\) are understood modulo \(q\), and the assertion is over the complex scalar field. The stated \(\kappa_h^2\) is the ratio of extreme Gram eigenvalues on the nonconstant-coordinate subspace; it is not asserted to be the whole-system condition number after adjoining the constant eigenvalue \(1\). Parameters \(b\) and \(q\) remain fixed in all limits as \(h\to\infty\).

## Proof
**Character covariance.** A uniformly proper colouring is generated by choosing the root colour uniformly from \(q\) colours and then independently giving each child a uniformly chosen colour distinct from its parent's. The single-edge transition kernel has eigenvalue \(-a\) on every nonconstant cyclic character. Global colour-shift symmetry makes characters with unequal frequencies orthogonal. Conditional independence of the branches from the lowest common ancestor gives
\[
\langle\psi_{v,k},\psi_{w,k}\rangle=(-a)^{d(v,w)}.
\]
The constant is orthogonal to these characters. Conjugation by the diagonal sign matrix \(\operatorname{diag}((-1)^{\operatorname{depth}(v)})\) changes this same-frequency Gram block to \(K=(a^{d(v,w)})_{v,w\in V}\).

**Precision matrix.** Let \(A_T\) and \(D_T\) be the adjacency and vertex-degree matrices of \(T_{b,h}\). Define \(Q=I+a^2(D_T-I)-aA_T\). For any vertices \(v,w\), direct multiplication along the unique tree path gives \(KQ=(1-a^2)I\): when \(v=w\), all \(\deg(w)\) neighbours are at distance one, while for \(v\ne w\), exactly one neighbour of \(w\) lies one step nearer \(v\) and every other neighbour one step farther. Consequently \(K=(1-a^2)Q^{-1}\).

**Complete block decomposition.** Decompose vectors on vertices orthogonally into the radial subspace constant on each level and, at every branching vertex, the \(b-1\) child-contrast subspaces, extended radially down each child subtree. The levelwise orthonormal basis contains factors \(b^{-j/2}\), so adjacency between consecutive levels has coefficient \(\sqrt b\). The radial restriction of \(Q\) is \(B_h\). Contrasts based at any depth \(\ell\) have restriction \(D_{h-\ell}\) and multiplicity \((b-1)b^\ell\). Their total dimensions sum to \(N\). Equivalently, this constructs an orthogonal similarity between \(Q\) and their stated direct sum. Each \(D_m\) is a trailing principal submatrix of \(B_h\), so strict Cauchy interlacing (all off-diagonal entries are nonzero) places the two extreme eigenvalues of \(Q\) in \(B_h\). Taking reciprocals yields the complete Fourier spectrum and exact frame bounds.

**Boundary quadratic form and positivity.** For \(x\in\mathbb C^{h+1}\),
\[
x^*B_hx=(1-a^2)|x_0|^2+\sum_{j=1}^{h}|x_j-tx_{j-1}|^2.
\]
This proves \(B_h>0\) and \(\det B_h=1-a^2\). For a vector \(y\) of length \(m\), the exact identity \(y^*D_my=|y_0|^2+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}|y_j-ty_{j-1}|^2\) similarly yields \(D_m>0\) and \(\det D_m=1\). Hence all the stated Fourier characters are linearly independent.

**Largest eigenvalue.** For every fixed \(t>0\), the maximum absolute row-sum bound on \(B_h\) is at most \((1+t)^2\). Alternating trial vectors supported on a long run of interior levels have Rayleigh quotients tending to \((1+t)^2\). Thus \(\lambda_{\max}(B_h)\to(1+t)^2\).

**Subcritical minimum.** If \(t<1\), the diagonal-dominance bound on all rows of \(B_h\) gives \(\lambda_{\min}(B_h)\ge(1-t)^2\). At the root the excess above the Gershgorin threshold is \(t-a^2>0\); at the leaf it is \(t-t^2>0\). A positive, slowly varying trial vector supported on many interior levels has Rayleigh quotient tending to \((1-t)^2\). Hence \(\lambda_{\min}(B_h)\to(1-t)^2\).

**Critical minimum.** If \(t=1\), interior eigenvectors with eigenvalue \(2-2\cos\theta\) can be written \(x_j=\cos((h+\tfrac12-j)\theta)\), automatically satisfying the leaf condition \(x_{h+1}=x_h\). The root condition is
\[
\cos((h+\tfrac32)\theta)=a^2\cos((h+\tfrac12)\theta).
\]
It has a root \(0<\theta_h<\pi/(2h+1)\); the corresponding eigenvector is strictly positive, so irreducible tridiagonal Perron/Sturm theory identifies the associated eigenvalue as the smallest. The root relation is equivalent to \(\tan((h+\tfrac12)\theta_h)=(\cos\theta_h-a^2)/\sin\theta_h\), which gives \(h\theta_h\to\pi/2\). Consequently \(h^2\lambda_{\min}(B_h)\to\pi^2/4\), and the critical conditioning coefficient follows.

**Supercritical minimum.** If \(t>1\), put \(u=t^2\). The bidiagonal factorization above yields the *exact* finite-depth identity
\[
\operatorname{tr}(B_h^{-1})
=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^h u^k}{1-a^2}
+\sum_{m=0}^{h-1}(h-m)u^m.
\]
Thus \(u^{-h}\operatorname{tr}(B_h^{-1})\to A=u(u-a^2)/((1-a^2)(u-1)^2)\). Let \(C\) be the \(h\times(h+1)\) first-difference matrix with rows \(x_j-tx_{j-1}\). Because \(B_h=C^*C+(1-a^2)e_0e_0^*\), rank-one interlacing implies \(\lambda_2(B_h)\ge\lambda_{\min}(CC^*)\ge(t-1)^2\), using the Toeplitz tridiagonal form of \(CC^*\). Therefore \(\operatorname{tr}(B_h^{-1})-1/\lambda_{\min}(B_h)=O(h)\), negligible compared with \(u^h\), and \(u^h\lambda_{\min}(B_h)\to1/A\). Combining with the largest-eigenvalue limit proves the exponential formula.

## Relationship to prior work
The single-edge broadcast eigenvalue and the location of the threshold \(b\lvert\lambda_2\rvert^2=1\) are classical; see Mossel and Peres. The inverse \(Q=(1-a^2)K^{-1}\) is a known general-tree exponential-distance/q-Laplacian identity, given by Bapat, Lal and Pati and discussed in Nagar. Orthogonal radial/child-contrast constructions for adjacency spectra of homogeneous trees are developed by DeFord and Rockmore. None of these ingredients, considered separately, is asserted to be new. The result here is the exact all-vertex *colour-character* Gram spectrum and its finite-depth Riesz extrema, including the three sharp asymptotic condition-number constants. The primary material inspected did not give this combined theorem.

## Limitations
No claim is made about nonlinear root reconstruction, total variation, recovery solely from leaves, nonuniform colouring laws, growth with varying \(b\) or \(q\), or independent audit. The trichotomy applies to the nonconstant Fourier coordinate band, with exactly the specified normalization. The general exponential-distance inverse and threshold location were previously known. Historical equivalent formulations in Bethe-tree Jacobi spectral theory or broadcast-model covariance literature are possible and warrant independent review.
