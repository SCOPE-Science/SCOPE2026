# Scientific review of a balanced-biclique cut variation theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The statement concerns the supremum over nonconstant indicator functions, not the full real-valued bounded-variation operator norm. The proof reduces every indicator to two occupancy integers, classifies the degenerate and equal-occupancy cases, and obtains the sole remaining upper bound from two explicit algebraic identities with positive denominators. The nonnegative comparisons are valid for all integers \(r\ge2\). The singleton case attains the first branch, and the \((1,2)\) case attains the second when it can dominate, namely for \(r\ge6\). Edge and ball counting were reconstructed independently with exact-rational code; finite computations are corroboration only. The strict threshold and all-order extremizer classification follow algebraically.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The novelty claim is deliberately the balanced-biclique *uncentered indicator* constant and its transition, not the classical uncentered interval contraction theorem or any known centered graph maximal result. The closest accessible primary works are Aldaz–Pérez Lázaro (continuous intervals, 2006), Bober–Carneiro–Hughes–Pierce (discrete integer line, 2010), Soria–Tradacete (centered finite-graph \(\ell^p\) bounds, 2014), González-Riquelme–Madrid (centered finite-graph variation, 2020), and González-Riquelme–Kovač–Madrid (centered variation on complete graphs and graphs of at most five vertices, 2026). None of their inspected theorems implies the claimed uncentered infinite balanced-biclique cut classification. The related Liu–Xue finite-graph variation article could be inspected only at abstract level; institutional full-text access was unavailable, which leaves a publication-priority risk rather than proof of absence. Published-record searches and accessible prior-claim comparisons also found no dominating implication.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The precise question is whether uncentered maximal averaging contracts edge variation for cuts on regular, diameter-two graphs; a uniform yes would parallel the classical uncentered interval result. The theorem gives an all-order complete answer for a natural highly symmetric family, locates the first amplifying graph at \(K_{9,9}\), identifies the abrupt change of extremizer shape at \(K_{6,6}\), and calculates the finite-to-infinite limit. The amplification is exhibited by actual indicator data, hence also disproves global contraction on the same graphs without claiming the sharp global norm. This is a motivated structural boundary, not an arbitrary one-off calculation.

## Closest literature and limitations

The principal distinction is centered versus uncentered averaging, followed by the restriction to indicators versus arbitrary inputs. The theorem neither settles unrestricted real-input variation norms nor covers unbalanced parts, weighted metrics, or arbitrary graph subfamilies. Search incompleteness, unindexed equivalent computations, and the unavailable full Liu–Xue text remain residual originality risks. A same mathematical model proof check does not substitute for independent mathematical review.
