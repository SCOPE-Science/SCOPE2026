# Review of the missing-edge biclique resistance theorem

## Correctness (PASS)

The claimed metric is effective resistance, not graph distance. A rank-one Laplacian downdate gives the four-class distance formula. The zero-sum quadratic form separates into two strictly positive within-class blocks and a three-dimensional matrix. The explicitly stated first principal minor is positive, and the second is a positive-coefficient polynomial for all integer part sizes except the four-vertex path. The factored determinant has sign positive for balanced orders at least three and negative for unequal orders. Sylvester's criterion and inertia additivity give the asserted rank, Euclidean criterion and unique obstruction. The four-vertex path is checked separately. Exact electrical inversions and centered Gram eigenvalues have been checked across all 25 size pairs from 2 through 6; finite enumeration is not the all-orders proof.

## Originality (PASS, qualified)

Full-text material from Sánchez, arXiv:1108.0451 (introduction, finite-space criteria, Section 3), and Ge--Dong, arXiv:1904.07766 (introduction and full Section 5, including Theorem 5.7 and Kirchhoff-index application), has been inspected. The former applies the machinery to shortest-path biclique distances. The latter gives effective-resistance values for the *same nearly-complete biclique objects*, a strong antecedent that must not be confused with a new resistance formula. Its recorded conclusions do not perform the centered *squared*-resistance Euclidean/inertia classification. An earlier mathematical result on complete (undeleted) bicliques establishes a different balance window, so it does not subsume this deletion result. published-finding corpus queries for the exact objects, missing-link and nearly-complete synonyms, negative-type formulations, and centered Gram equivalents did not surface an equivalent evaluated classification. These are evidence-based noncoverage comparisons, not a guarantee of global uniqueness. Unindexed computations and the partially accessible 2016 multipartite paper are residual risks.

## Mathematical value (PASS)

The deletion experiment is a canonical stability question for resistance-metric Hilbert embeddings: the balanced complete bipartite metric survives a missing edge, but the one-vertex balance tolerance of the undeleted case disappears. The all-order if-and-only-if theorem, exact one-negative-eigenvalue obstruction and exceptional rank-one case constitute a structural classification. They cannot be inferred from the presence of a pseudoinverse formula without the determinant-sign analysis. No assertion is made that the result has publication-level scope.

## Remaining limitations

Only one missing cross-edge, unit electrical resistances and integer part sizes at least two are treated. The precise supremal exponent is not determined away from the four-vertex path. Relevant full-text antecedents give distances, not this sign classification; an unindexed equivalent could exist.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
