# A sphere necklace and integral dihedral homology for closed neighborhoods of Möbius ladders

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge4\), let \(M_{2n}\) be the Möbius ladder with vertex set \(\mathbb Z/(2n)\), edges \(\{i,i+1\}\) and \(\{i,i+n\}\) (indices modulo \(2n\)). Let \(K_n=\mathcal N[M_{2n}]\) be the *closed-neighborhood complex*: a simplex is a set of graph vertices contained in the closed neighborhood of one vertex. Then exactly \(4n\) elementary simplicial collapse pairs reduce \(K_n\) to the union of the \(n\) tetrahedral boundaries
\[
B_i=\partial\Delta\bigl(\{i,i+1,i+n,i+n+1\}\bigr),\qquad i\in\mathbb Z/n,
\]
which form a cyclic necklace, with successive spheres sharing a rung edge and all other pairwise intersections empty. In particular,
\[
\boxed{|K_n|\simeq S^1\vee\bigvee^{n}S^2.}
\]
Thus \(\pi_1(K_n)\cong\mathbb Z\), \(\widetilde H_1(K_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), \(\widetilde H_2(K_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^n\), and all other reduced integral homology vanishes.

There is also a canonical integral symmetry refinement. The dihedral group acting on labels by \(\rho(i)=i+1\) and \(\tau(i)=-i\) acts trivially by \(\rho\) and by \(-1\) for \(\tau\) on \(H_1(K_n;\mathbb Z)\). On \(H_2(K_n;\mathbb Z)\), the fundamental cycles
\[
z_i=\partial[i,i+n,i+1,i+n+1],\qquad i\in\mathbb Z/n,
\]
form an integral basis with \(\rho z_i=z_{i+1}\) and \(\tau z_i=z_{-i-1}\). Thus the latter is the unsigned permutation lattice of the \(n\) square faces, and the central half-turn acts trivially on both nonzero homology groups.

## Assumptions and scope
The ladder is a finite simple graph with \(2n\) vertices; \(n=3\) is excluded because the extra four-cycles of \(K_{3,3}\) invalidate the stated local collapse. A face of \(\mathcal N[G]\) is contained in \(N_G[v]=N_G(v)\cup\{v\}\) for at least one vertex \(v\). This is the radius-one *closed* intrinsic graph Čech complex, not the open Lovász neighborhood complex, the Vietoris–Rips complex, or a matching or independence complex. The homotopy equivalence is nonequivariant; the group representation statements concern the natural action on integral homology, not an equivariant wedge model.

All indices in the following proof are computed modulo \(2n\), unless explicitly reduced modulo \(n\). An elementary simplicial collapse deletes a simplex and its unique remaining immediate coface. The square boundary \(B_i\) is the simplicial boundary of an abstract three-simplex on four vertices; its three-simplex interior is *not* present in the residual complex.

## Proof
**Step 1 (free tetrahedral faces).** For each vertex \(v\), its closed neighborhood is
\[
A_v=\{v-1,v,v+1,v+n\}.
\]
The complex \(K_n\) is generated by the \(2n\) tetrahedra \(\Delta(A_v)\). For \(n\ge4\), the only generating tetrahedron containing the triangle \(T_v=\{v-1,v+1,v+n\}\) is \(\Delta(A_v)\). Indeed, a distinct closed-neighborhood center containing all three would have to be a second common center for the two rim neighbors and the antipodal neighbor. From the adjacency rule \(\pm1,n\), their only common closed-neighborhood center is \(v\); alternatively, checking the three possible neighbor steps of the three vertices gives this directly. There are no triangles in this cubic graph for \(n\ge4\). Hence deleting \((T_v,A_v)\) is an elementary collapse for every \(v\); the pairs are disjoint and each remains free under deletion of the others.

**Step 2 (free rim diagonals).** Once these \(2n\) pairs have been deleted, every two-dimensional face contains its generating center and two graph neighbors. The triangle \(R_v=\{v-1,v,v+1\}\) now has a unique remaining coface over its rim-diagonal edge \(d_v=\{v-1,v+1\}\). For \(n\ge4\) this vertex pair has exactly one common graph neighbor, \(v\) (an additional center would give an extra length-four cycle using two rim steps, which is excluded by the explicit \(\pm1,n\) adjacency). Consequently, the \(2n\) pairs \((d_v,R_v)\) are elementary collapses. Exactly \(4n\) collapse pairs have now been removed.

**Step 3 (identify every remaining face).** The remaining triangles are exactly
\[
\{v-1,v,v+n\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{v,v+1,v+n\},\qquad v\in\mathbb Z/(2n).
\]
For each \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\), these are the four triangular faces of \(B_i\), and every remaining edge and vertex lies in one of those faces. This yields the exact simplicial identity
\[
K_n\searrow Z_n:=\bigcup_{i=0}^{n-1}B_i.
\]
The initial face vector (in dimensions zero through three) is \((2n,7n,8n,2n)\), and the residual face vector is \((2n,5n,4n)\). Consecutive \(B_i\) and \(B_{i+1}\) intersect in the rung edge \(\{i+1,i+1+n\}\), including the cyclic wraparound; nonconsecutive boundaries are disjoint, and no three boundaries intersect, for \(n\ge4\).

**Step 4 (integral homotopy type).** Let \(F\subset Z_n\) be the graph consisting of the \(n\) rung edges \(\{i,i+n\}\) for \(0\le i<n\), together with the \(n\) consecutive rim edges \(\{i,i+1\}\) for \(0\le i<n\). It is connected with \(2n\) vertices and \(2n\) edges, so \(|F|\simeq S^1\). Every \(B_i\cap F\) is the three-edge path that joins the four vertices of its square using the two rungs and the chosen rim edge; in particular it is contractible. Moreover \(B_i\cap B_j\subset F\) for distinct indices. Thus, regardless of attachment order, each new \(B_i\) meets the previously constructed subcomplex in exactly the contractible path \(B_i\cap F\). The pushout of finite simplicial complexes along a contractible subcomplex is a homotopy pushout, and this attachment has the homotopy type of wedging on \(B_i\simeq S^2\). Iterating gives \(|Z_n|\simeq S^1\vee\bigvee^nS^2\). This also establishes the fundamental group and *integral*, not merely field-valued, homology claims.

**Step 5 (dihedral action in degree two).** Each missing three-simplex with ordered vertices \([i,i+n,i+1,i+n+1]\) has all four boundary triangles in \(Z_n\); its oriented boundary is an integral two-cycle \(z_i\). The cycles lie in different tetrahedral-boundary summands, so Step 4 proves that they are an integral homology basis. Rotation by one takes \(z_i\) to \(z_{i+1}\). At the wraparound from \(i=n-1\) to \(i=0\), both rung pairs reverse, an even permutation, hence the sign is still positive. Reflection takes \(z_0\) to \(z_{n-1}\) through the even permutation reversing the ordered four-tuple, and the relation \(\tau\rho=\rho^{-1}\tau\) gives \(\tau z_i=z_{-i-1}\) for every index. In particular \(\rho^n\) fixes every basis vector.

**Step 6 (dihedral action in degree one).** Orient rim edges by \(e_i=[i,i+1]\) and rungs by \(r_i=[i,i+n]\), where \(r_{i+n}=-r_i\). In the square \(B_i\), the one-cycle \(e_i+r_{i+1}-e_{i+n}-r_i\) bounds a two-chain. The one-cycle
\[
\gamma=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}e_i-r_0
\]
is the unique cycle of \(F\), hence generates \(H_1(Z_n;\mathbb Z)\). Rotation gives \(\rho\gamma-\gamma=e_n-e_0-r_1+r_0\), which is null-homologous by the first square relation. Summing the \(n\) square relations yields \(\sum_{i=n}^{2n-1}e_i=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}e_i-2r_0\). Reflection fixes \(r_0\) and sends the upper rim path to the negative lower rim path, hence \(\tau\gamma=-\gamma\). All claimed integral actions follow.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library program `verify.py` reconstructs the full closed-neighborhood face set for every \(4\le n\le100\), checks every individual free-face pair at its actual deletion stage, and proves in each tested case that the residue is exactly the proposed tetrahedral-boundary union. It checks the face counts, precise pairwise sphere intersections, the base graph's intersections with each sphere, and the signs of the rotation, reflection and half-turn on oriented boundary cycles. For \(4\le n\le16\) it independently computes the original simplicial mod-two boundary ranks, obtaining reduced Betti numbers \((0,1,n,0)\) in degrees zero through three. The expected terminal line is `ALL_VERIFY_OK`. These finite tests corroborate the six-step all-parameter proof; they are not substituted for it.

## Relationship to prior work
Matsushita [1] establishes the general closed-neighborhood framework, its fundamental-group correspondence and links to canonical double covers and neighborhood hypergraphs. The present collapse is not a corollary of the specific product-of-complete-graphs example there, and supplies an explicit integral homotopy and dihedral module for the different Möbius-ladder family.

A published classification [2] identifies the *open* Lovász neighborhood complex of these same Möbius ladders as an annulus for even \(n\ge4\), or two Möbius bands for odd \(n\ge5\). Those are not the closed-neighborhood complex studied here: the closed construction has a connected \(S^1\)-and-\(n\)-spheres type in either parity, and a new integral second-homology permutation action. Another published result [3] treats closed-ball Čech nerves of **untwisted prisms** \(H\square K_2\) over graphs without triangles or four-cycles, producing analogous tetrahedral sphere boundaries; setting \(H=C_n\) gives a circular ladder, not the Möbius ladder. Its domain and the resulting incidence graph therefore do not establish the present twisted-ladder assertion. Agarwal and Basak [4] classify independence and perfect-matching complexes of Möbius ladders, not their closed-neighborhood complexes. These comparisons concern mathematical implication, not merely title or terminology.

## Limitations
No classification is asserted at \(n=3\), for larger graph-metric Čech radii, or for arbitrary cubic graphs. The collapse need not be equivariant step by step; only the induced homology representations are claimed. The work does not prove that no equivalent unindexed calculation exists; the latest full manuscript of [4] was not accessible for complete direct inspection. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
