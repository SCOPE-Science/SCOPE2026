# Review of the closed-neighborhood Möbius-ladder theorem

## Correctness
**PASS.** For every \(n\ge4\), the local neighborhood formula gives explicit free faces of distinct tetrahedra and then free rim-diagonal edges. The remaining faces are exactly a cyclic union of tetrahedral boundaries. Choosing the rungs and one consecutive half-rim produces a graph with circle homotopy type that meets each tetrahedral sphere along a contractible three-edge tree; all other sphere intersections lie in that graph. The homotopy-pushout argument is integral and establishes \(S^1\vee\bigvee^nS^2\). The explicit square-cycle relations and even vertex-permutation signs prove the two dihedral representations. The standalone checker independently confirms the identities for \(4\le n\le100\), including raw mod-two homology for \(n\le16\). Its finite checks are corroboration only.

## Originality
**PASS on accessible evidence, with a stated residual risk.** Searches used the closed-neighborhood, Čech, circulant, reflection-equivariant, and sphere-necklace formulations and compared the statements of the closest indexed findings and primary literature. Matsushita's general closed-neighborhood theorems do not identify the Möbius-ladder homotopy type or its modules. The published open-neighborhood Möbius-ladder surface classification uses a genuinely different complex, and the high-girth prism theorem applies to untwisted Cartesian prisms, not this half-twisted family. Agarwal–Basak address independence and perfect matching, not closed neighborhoods. A complete examination of the latter manuscript was unavailable; an equivalent unindexed result remains a risk, rather than a claim of absolute uniqueness.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** This is an exact integral all-width classification for a standard cubic, highly symmetric family. The local simplicial collapse explains why the homotopy type has one circle and linearly many two-spheres even though the originating complex has three-simplices. The explicit integral dihedral module refines Betti counts, and the contrast with the parity-dependent open-neighborhood surface types isolates a structurally meaningful distinction. It is not a numerical slice or an automatic consequence of the known untwisted-prism theorem.
