# A tetrahedral hypergraph sphere invisible to three normalized cubical homologies

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal T\) have vertex set \(\{0,1,2,3\}\) and exactly the four three-element subsets as hyperedges. For the normalized \(\Gamma\)-, \(\Box\)-, and \(\times\)-cubical hypergraph complexes of Zaidi and Jamil,
\[
H_2^{\Gamma}(\mathcal T;\mathbb Z)=H_2^{\Box}(\mathcal T;\mathbb Z)=H_2^{\times}(\mathcal T;\mathbb Z)=0,
\qquad H_2^{\mathrm{emb}}(\mathcal T;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z.
\]
Here the last group is the infimum-chain embedded homology of the same hypergraph. In contrast to the three normalized cubical constructions, it detects the fundamental class of the tetrahedral boundary.

More generally, the normalized \(\Box\)- and \(\times\)-cubical homology complexes of any finite hypergraph are naturally determined by its *two-section graph* (with the same vertices and an edge whenever a hyperedge contains both endpoints). Whenever this graph is complete, both theories have zero positive-degree homology. Consequently, for each \(m\ge3\), the hypergraph whose hyperedges are the \(m\)-element subsets of \(m+1\) vertices has embedded homology \(\mathbb Z\) in degree \(m-1\), but vanishing positive-degree \(\Box\)- and \(\times\)-homology. The nontrivial new three-theory comparison is the exact integral \(\Gamma\)-calculation for \(\mathcal T\) in degree two.

## Assumptions and scope
A hypergraph morphism sends each source hyperedge into some target hyperedge; target edges need not be equal for different source hyperedges. Singular \(n\)-cubes are morphisms from the standard \(n\)-dimensional source \(\Gamma\)-, \(\Box\)-, or \(\times\)-hypergraph into the target. For \(\Gamma\), each source hyperedge is a closed radius-one star in the binary cube; for \(\Box\), source hyperedges are graph edges of the cube; for \(\times\), the source has every two-element subset as a hyperedge. The free cubical chains are normalized by quotienting cubes independent of at least one coordinate. The cubical differential is the alternating sum of opposite faces, with the same convention used consistently in the verifier. Embedded homology uses the integral infimum subcomplex \(\mathrm{Inf}_n=D_n\cap\partial^{-1}(D_{n-1})\), where \(D_n\) is generated by \((n+1)\)-vertex hyperedges in the ambient simplicial chain complex. All coefficients here are integral.

## Proof
**The two-section lemma.** For \(\Box\) and \(\times\), every source hyperedge has two vertices. Thus a vertex-valued cube is admissible precisely when each corresponding source pair maps either to one point or to an edge of the target two-section. The normalized chains and face maps consequently depend only on that two-section, naturally for hypergraph morphisms. If the two-section is complete, every map from the binary cube to the vertices is admitted in both theories. Fixing a vertex \(v\), the cubical prism taking a cube at its last coordinate \(0\) and the constant map to \(v\) at \(1\) gives the standard cubical contraction of the augmented chain complex. Hence all strictly positive homology groups vanish.

**Exact \(\Gamma\)-calculation.** Enumerate the normalized directed one-cubes of \(\mathcal T\): there are \(12\) ordered pairs of distinct vertices. The normalized two-cubes number \(4^4-2\cdot4^2+4=228\). All are admissible because a radius-one star of the square has three vertices, and any triple of target vertices is contained in a hyperedge. The boundary \(\partial_1:\mathbb Z^{12}\to\mathbb Z^4\) has rank \(3\). A direct independent binary elimination on the actual \(\partial_2\)-columns has rank \(9\) modulo two; since \(\mathrm{im}\,\partial_2\subseteq\ker\partial_1\), its rational rank is at most \(12-3=9\), hence exactly \(9\). Thus \(\ker\partial_2\), a subgroup of the free abelian group \(\mathbb Z^{228}\), has rank \(219\).

An admissible nondegenerate three-cube is an eight-tuple of target vertices in which each of the eight binary radius-one stars has at most three distinct labels. Exhaustively checking \(4^8\) tuples produces exactly \(32868\) such cubes. The accompanying certificate `gamma_minor.json` specifies \(219\) admissible nondegenerate three-cubes and \(219\) distinct normalized two-cube basis rows; the selected boundary-column minor of size \(219\times219\) has integer determinant \(-1\). Its coefficients are recomputed from cube faces, not supplied as a precomputed matrix. Since \(\partial_2\partial_3=0\), this exhibits a primitive rank-\(219\) subgroup of \(\ker\partial_2\). More precisely, the unit minor makes this image a direct summand of \(\mathbb Z^{228}\). The quotient \(\mathbb Z^{228}/\mathrm{im}\,\partial_3\) is therefore torsion-free, so its subgroup \(\ker\partial_2/\mathrm{im}\,\partial_3\) is torsion-free; this subgroup also has rank zero. It vanishes. Hence \(H_2^{\Gamma}(\mathcal T;\mathbb Z)=0\).

**Embedded homology.** The four oriented triple facets form the oriented boundary of the three-simplex. The simplicial boundary matrix on their span has kernel generated by this alternating sum, and there are no four-element hyperedges, so the embedded infimum complex has \(H_2\cong\mathbb Z\). The same boundary-sphere argument applies to the \(m\)-subset hypergraph on \(m+1\) vertices in degree \(m-1\).

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_gamma.py` with `gamma_minor.json` in the same directory, using only Python's standard library. It regenerates normalized cube lists in degrees one and two, establishes the mod-two rank-nine lower bound, enumerates admissible nondegenerate three-cubes, checks that all \(219\) certificate cubes are distinct and admissible, verifies \(\partial_2\partial_3=0\) for each selected cube, and computes the integer unit determinant of the selected \(219\times219\) boundary minor using exact fraction-free elimination. This is a finite exhaustive certificate for the decisive integral lattice condition, not extrapolation from finite sampling to an unbounded family.

## Relationship to prior work
Zaidi–Jamil (2026) define the three normalized cubical constructions and calculate introductory examples, but the inspected text does not compute this tetrahedral-boundary degree-two comparison. Bressan–Li–Ren–Wu (2016) introduced embedded hypergraph homology; its infimum-chain definition directly recovers the fundamental class. The present finite calculation addresses their mathematically different theories and does not assert that embedded homology is cubical homology. The two-section factorization explains the \(\Box\)- and \(\times\)-phenomena; it does not by itself imply the \(\Gamma\)-conclusion, because \(\Gamma\)-source hyperedges contain more than two vertices.

## Limitations
The explicit \(\Gamma\) vanishing claim concerns only degree two of the four-vertex tetrahedral-boundary hypergraph; it does not establish higher-degree vanishing or corresponding results for all higher-dimensional sphere boundaries. The two-section lemma concerns only \(\Box\) and \(\times\), not \(\Gamma\). The certificate is a transparent finite integral computation; an independent mathematical audit has not been performed. Implicit equivalent calculations in unindexed sources cannot be ruled out completely.
