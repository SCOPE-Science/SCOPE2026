The boundary of a tetrahedron, viewed as a four-edge three-uniform hypergraph, has integral second embedded homology \(\mathbb Z\), yet its second homology vanishes in all three normalized cubical hypergraph theories \(\Gamma\), \(\Box\), and \(\times\). An exact \(219\times219\) unit-minor certificate establishes the non-obvious \(\Gamma\) vanishing, while a general two-section lemma explains why the other two theories cannot detect the fundamental classes of any simplex-boundary hypergraph.
