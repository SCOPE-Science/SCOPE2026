# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The result is an exact finite classification, not an extrapolation. Gale's evenness criterion specifies each facet without heuristic filtering; perfect-pairing enumeration generates all faces of the double. For every one of the ten parameters, an exhaustive reversed-Hasse-DAG check proves the discrete-Morse matching acyclic, signed gradient-path reduction constructs the integral Morse differential, and exact Hermite/Smith normal forms yield the listed groups. Independent unreduced mod-two boundary ranks agree. The differential has all unit nonzero Smith factors except the stated single torsion factor in each relevant case.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The directly relevant full primary text, Lesh–Schreiner–Wahl's *The double of a simplicial complex* (arXiv:2506.10436v1, 2025-06-12), gives a general connectivity theorem but no exact integral homology for the doubles of cyclic-polytope boundaries. Complete-graph matching complexes such as Jonsson's fourteen-vertex example are different complexes, because the union of disjoint pairs must also be a face of the cyclic-polytope boundary. Searches for this construction, the Gale-evenness description, double complexes, Smith invariants and five-torsion did not uncover a theorem implying this table. Indexed related results were compared by their objects and conclusions rather than title. Unindexed equivalent calculations and unexamined specialized work remain risks; negative searches cannot certify uniqueness.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Doubles are a newly studied construction where connectivity is established but the integral torsion behavior of natural input spheres is not described by the cited theorem. Six-dimensional cyclic polytopes are the canonical neighborly simplicial six-polytopes, and the complete first ten-member integral classification through the first order-five class supplies a nonroutine, motivated structural benchmark. The exact contrast between alternating free groups and torsion \(2,3,4,5\) is mathematically informative, but it does not justify an unproved infinite pattern.
