# A tripartite period collapse and exact basins in three-color cyclic automata

## Finding
Consider synchronous three-color cyclic cellular automata on a labelled complete multipartite graph with at least three nonempty vertex classes. Each vertex of color \(c\in\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\) advances to \(c+1\) precisely if a neighbor has the successor color. If \(F\) is the synchronous map, then for every initial coloring,
\[
F^5=F^2.
\]
Consequently each orbit enters a fixed point or primitive period-three orbit by time two; these are the only possible eventual periods. The time-two bound is sharp already on \(K_{1,1,2}\).

For a complete tripartite graph \(K_{a,b,c}\), with arbitrary integers \(a,b,c\ge1\), all fixed states are the three uniform colorings, and the exact number of initial colorings eventually reaching one of them is
\[
B(a,b,c)=3\left(2^{a+b+c}+3^a+3^b+3^c-2^{a+1}-2^{b+1}-2^{c+1}+2\right).
\]
All other initial states eventually enter primitive three-cycles. To enumerate *distinct primitive three-cycle orbits* (each temporal orbit counted once), index the three shore sizes by \(n_1=a,n_2=b,n_3=c\), set \(u_i=2^{n_i}-2\) and \(r_i=3^{n_i}-3\cdot2^{n_i}+3\), and define
\[
\begin{aligned}
P={}&r_1r_2r_3+6+6\sum_i u_i+9\sum_{i<j}u_iu_j+15u_1u_2u_3\\
&+3\sum_i r_i\sum_{j\ne i}u_j+3\sum_{i<j}r_ir_j\\
&+6\sum_i r_i\prod_{j\ne i}u_j+3\sum_i u_i\prod_{j\ne i}r_j.
\end{aligned}
\]
Then the exact number is \(P/3\). For equal shore sizes \(a=b=c=n\), the consensus-basin probability under uniformly independent initial colors satisfies
\[
\Pr(\text{eventual consensus})=\frac{3(8^n+3\cdot3^n-6\cdot2^n+2)}{27^n}\sim3(8/27)^n,
\]
while the probability of not being periodic initially is asymptotic to \(9(2/3)^n\). Thus the rare nonperiodic initial states are overwhelmingly attracted to three-cycles rather than consensus.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is the simple, loopless, labelled complete multipartite graph; all shore sizes are positive integers. Every state is a vertex assignment in \(\{0,1,2\}\). Updates are simultaneous, deterministic and depend on the existence (not number) of successor-colored neighbors. The consensus formula and primitive-orbit polynomial concern exactly three shores; the global identity \(F^5=F^2\) holds for any number of shores at least three. Periodicity is temporal; a primitive three-cycle consists of three distinct full colorings.

## Proof
**1. A bounded exact reduction for every number of shores.** Within any shore only the set of colors present matters to all inter-shore existence tests. A shore's nonempty color support is one of the seven subsets of \(\{0,1,2\}\). Shores with the same support see the same sets of colors in their other shores and evolve to the same next support. For each of the seven possible initial support types, replacing its number of shores by \(0\), \(1\), or \(2\) (where \(2\) represents any multiplicity at least two) preserves the neighbor-color availability seen by every initial support type. This remains true at each subsequent time: shores from one type evolve identically, and merging types preserves the truncated multiplicities. Each individual initial color class in a shore therefore follows the same color trajectory as its representative color in the truncated system.

There are \(3^7-36=2151\) possible truncated multiplicity vectors representing at least three nonempty shores (subtract the one zero vector, seven single-one vectors, seven single-two vectors and 21 vectors with two ones). The included self-contained verifier explicitly constructs each representative shore with one vertex of every initially present color and applies the original color update five times. It checks equality of the complete representative configurations at times two and five in all 2151 cases. Because the truncation preserves every initial color-class trajectory, this finite exhaustive identity proves \(F^5=F^2\) on *all* complete multipartite graphs with at least three nonempty shores. No bounded shore-size assumption is made. The initial supports \((\{0\},\{0\},\{0,1\})\) on \(K_{1,1,2}\) reach consensus at exactly time two, proving sharpness.

**2. Consensus basins.** On a complete tripartite graph the cycle space is generated by triangles: a four-cycle involving two shores can be subdivided into triangles using a vertex of the third shore. For three colors, the oriented color-increment one-form has nonzero integral over a triangle exactly when the triangle is rainbow (one vertex of each color). The general finite-graph synchronization criterion for this cellular automaton, cited below, therefore says that a state eventually reaches consensus if and only if it has no rainbow triangle with one vertex in each shore. The diameter-two synchronization bound also gives consensus by the second update; the only fixed colorings are monochrome because any non-monochrome connected coloring has an edge with a color-successor relation.

A three-color assignment has no rainbow transversal of the three shores precisely when either (i) the union of its used colors contains at most two colors, or (ii) two shores are monochromatic in the same color. To see this, apply Hall's condition to the bipartite incidence between the three colors and the three shores. The first event has \(3\cdot2^{a+b+c}-3\) assignments. Outside that event, each choice of two monochrome shores and their common color yields \(3^{n_i}-2\cdot2^{n_i}+1\) possibilities on the remaining shore, where \(n_i\) is its size. These nine events are disjoint outside the first event. Summing gives exactly \(B(a,b,c)\) above.

**3. Exact primitive-period-three orbit polynomial.** For three shores, there are only \(7^3=343\) nonempty support triples. The verifier evaluates the microscopic color dynamics on a representative containing every present color once. It computes the first periodic-entry time and least period for every support triple. The six resulting classes \((T,p)\), with their *support-triple multiplicities*, are \((0,1):3\), \((0,3):121\), \((1,1):66\), \((1,3):90\), \((2,1):27\), \((2,3):36\). A support with \(k=1,2,3\) colors is realized on a labelled shore of size \(n\) in respectively \(1\), \(u_n\), \(r_n\) ways (onto-coloring counts). Summing the products of these weights over the 121 microscopic primitive-period-three support triples gives the displayed polynomial \(P\). Each primitive temporal orbit contributes three periodic configurations, hence \(P/3\) orbits. This is a finite exhaustive calculation over all support patterns, not extrapolation from bounded graph sizes.

**4. Asymptotic separation.** For balanced tripartite shore size \(n\), the formula for \(B\) gives the first asymptotic. In the same 343-support census, all nonperiodic classes \((T=1\text{ or }2)\) have leading total \(9u_nr_n^2\), whereas every other term is of smaller exponential order. Since \(u_n\sim2^n\) and \(r_n\sim3^n\), dividing by \(3^{3n}=27^n\) gives \(9(2/3)^n\).

It prints `CCA_TRIPARTITE_CERTIFICATE_OK`. The machine computation is an exhaustive finite proof only because the preceding seven-support truncation lemma reduces arbitrary shore sizes to these instances; ordinary small-graph simulations alone would not establish the result.

## Relationship to prior work
Gravner, Lyu and Sivakoff (arXiv:1610.07320v1, October 24, 2016), Theorem 3, established the general criterion for synchronization of three-color cyclic automata using a cycle-integral one-form, and the diameter bound for synchronization. Those are used as prior theorems here and are **not** claimed as new. Their general results do not explicitly give the universal five-versus-two iterate identity, the all-shore tripartite transient-orbit census, or the closed basin and primitive-orbit formulas. For the corresponding two-shore dynamics, primitive period-four behavior can occur; this case has a different periodic-state structure and does not imply the three-shore formulas. The exact formulas here quantify a natural combinatorial and dynamical transition when a third interacting shore is present.

## Limitations
The tripartite orbit-count formula does not assert a complete basin-size distribution *by individual periodic orbit*, nor a formula for the number of period-three orbits for arbitrary numbers of shores above three. The statement \(F^5=F^2\) is a dynamical identity for the stated synchronous three-color rule, not for other color counts, asynchronous updates or inhibitory rules. The finite truncation check is inspectable but not a formal proof-assistant certification; independent review remains open. An unindexed prior enumeration could reduce the originality assessment.
