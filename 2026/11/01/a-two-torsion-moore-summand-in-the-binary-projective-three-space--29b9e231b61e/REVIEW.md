# Scientific assessment: binary projective three-space cap complex

## Correctness — PASS

All \(15\) points and \(35\) lines of \(\operatorname{PG}(3,2)\) are reconstructed from the bit-vector definition. The \(3048\) cap faces are exhaustively enumerated; the supplied \(1484\)-pair discrete-Morse matching covers every noncritical face, and exhaustive topological sorting verifies acyclicity of every Hasse incidence. The remaining CW complex has one zero-cell, one three-cell, and seventy-eight four-cells. Signed integral Morse reduction yields a degree row with gcd two, so attaching-map classification gives \(M(\mathbb Z/2,3)\vee\bigvee^{77}S^4\). Independent finite-field matrix calculations match its homology, but are not used to replace the integral proof. This is a finite exact certificate, not a general theorem for other geometries.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks

Forbes–Grannell–Griggs (2004) study independent sets of Steiner triple systems, the same combinatorial faces, but establish enumeration and coloring rather than this integral homotopy type. Lew (2017) defines and computes the topology of several other cap complexes in Section 6.2, notably ternary projective-plane caps, without tabulating this binary projective three-space case. Targeted prior-result searches for \(C_p(3,2)\), no-collinear triple systems, the 15-point binary projective cap complex, and a two-torsion Moore summand did not identify a mathematically covering source. Searches are not proof of novelty; unindexed equivalent calculations and gaps in inspection remain possible.

## Value — PASS

The natural cap complex of the smallest binary projective three-space has integral two-torsion, invisible to its face vector and rational homology. The result identifies the precise Moore-space obstruction and 77 top spheres, resolving the complete integral homotopy type for a canonical finite geometric incidence system. The result is self-contained and reproducible, rather than an arbitrary numerical slice of a larger census.

## Scope and limits

No automorphism representation, equivariant splitting, minimal Morse-certificate size, or extension to higher-dimensional projective spaces is claimed. Definitive publication priority and independent verification remain open.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
