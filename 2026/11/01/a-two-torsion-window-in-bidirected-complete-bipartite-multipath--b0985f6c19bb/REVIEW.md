# Review of the finite bidirected complete-bipartite classification

## Correctness
**PASS.** The exact object uses all two-way arcs of the specified bipartite graph and excludes every directed cycle. Every allowed face has at most four arcs. The complete finite face-poset enumeration agrees between subset and incremental constructions. A full directed Hasse acyclicity test verifies the staged discrete-Morse matching, and a signed gradient-path recursion yields the exact critical-cell incidence matrices. The determinant-two and unit-maximal-minor certificates determine the integral Smith factors. The Hurewicz theorem upgrades those two- and three-cell Morse CW complexes to the claimed sphere wedges and Moore summands. Separate full simplicial boundary reductions over the two-element field agree. Critical limitations are restricted to the six explicitly listed values.

## Originality
**PASS against examined evidence.** The one-way bipartite digraphs of Caputi–Collari–Di Trani–Smith (arXiv:2208.04656) have different arrow sets and their multipath complexes are matching complexes; that formula does not imply the bidirected torsion window. The Caputi–Collari–Ramos blow-up theorem (arXiv:2401.01248) requires no oriented cycles, which fails here. Caputi–Collari–Smith (arXiv:2601.05670) treat bidirectional paths and polygons, not bidirected complete bipartite graphs. The closest identified online summaries concern bidirected double-star trees or other matching complexes, not these objects and attaching degrees. An unindexed equivalent calculation remains possible; the accessible online summaries of some related records did not permit a full-document exclusion.

## Value
**PASS.** This determines exact *integral homotopy*, not just mod-two Betti numbers or Euler characteristics, for the first substantial six members of a natural bidirected complete-bipartite family. The two adjacent nontrivial Moore-space cases demonstrate degree-two torsion absent in the one-way matching-complex picture, followed by torsion-free three-sphere wedges at the next three natural parameters. The transition makes the finite classification structurally informative even without an all-parameter theorem. It neither claims permanent stabilization nor assumes independent verification.
