# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is a pointwise statement, for every fixed \(a\ge1\) and \(x\in(-1,1]\). The killed symmetric kernel has complete, explicitly normalized Dirichlet–Neumann eigenbasis with eigenvalues \(4(-1)^m/[\pi(a+1)(2m+1)]\). Conjugating by its positive principal eigenfunction gives the Markov transition density and the full spectrum. The relative density after one step is square integrable for every fixed interior start, so the spectral expansion and the remainders \(O_x(5^{-n})\) and \(O_x(7^{-n})\) follow from Parseval and Cauchy–Schwarz. Exact sign-split integrations give both leading constants. The first nonconstant eigenfunction has one and only one zero, at \(x=1/3\), where the next eigenfunction is nonzero. The strictly positive two-step transition density proves uniqueness of the invariant law. The companion program produced 233 finite checks; finite tests are not treated as proof of the infinite claim.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The direct 2026 source establishes the conditioned Markov kernel and principal harmonic function in this parameter region, which are taken as known. Its Theorems 1.1–1.2 and supporting proofs do not state the *pointwise total-variation two-speed asymptotics, exact leading constants, or unique accelerated start*. The earlier Aurzada–Raschel paper studies persistence generating functions, not conditioned-chain relaxation. Majumdar–Dhar (2001) studies persistence rates of stationary-integer-shift processes and does not provide this Doob-chain relaxation theorem. General quasi-stationary ergodicity theory yields qualitative or bounded convergence, not the model-specific sharp rates. The underlying reflection operator and mixed-boundary sine eigenfunctions are classical; they are not claimed to be novel. Residual priority risk: another presentation of this exact symmetric kernel may already imply these constants implicitly, particularly in alternating-permutation or Q-process literature. No independent historical-priority assessment has occurred.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The conditional model has a natural observable: mixing of the innovation state after imposing long-term persistence. The exact spectral cancellation shows a qualitative distinction among initial conditions that is absent from the existing persistence exponent: all but one fixed initial state have sharp base \(1/3\), while exactly one has sharp base \(1/5\). Explicit constants and full parameter independence distinguish an observable structural consequence rather than a routine alternative notation. No claim of a separate paper-sized advance is needed.

## Limits

The result does not address parameter values outside the indicated region, arbitrary innovation laws, or uniform-in-start mixing near the excluded endpoint. Computation supplements a self-contained spectral proof and does not certify novelty or independent verification.
