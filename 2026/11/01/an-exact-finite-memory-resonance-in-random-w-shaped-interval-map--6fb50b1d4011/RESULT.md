# An exact finite-memory resonance in random W-shaped interval maps

## Finding

For the two W-shaped maps defined below, selected independently at each time with probabilities \(1-p\) and \(p\), the stationary natural four-cell coding \((Q_n)_{n\ge0}\) has a **unique, exact one-dependence resonance**:

\[
Q\text{ is finitely dependent}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p=p_*(a):=\frac{a-1}{a},\qquad a>2,\quad0<p<1.
\]

At this parameter, the coding is strictly one-dependent: any blocks separated by at least one intervening symbol are independent, but consecutive symbols are dependent. In fact, with \(P\) the four-symbol transition matrix and \(\pi\) its stationary row vector,

\[
P^2=\boldsymbol{1}\pi,\qquad P\ne\boldsymbol{1}\pi.
\]

Furthermore the indicator \(H_n=\boldsymbol1_{\{Q_n=2\text{ or }3\}}\) is then an *independent and identically distributed* Bernoulli sequence with success probability \((a-2)/a\). Away from the resonance, the exact autocovariance is nonzero at every lag and changes from alternating sign to positive sign as \(p\) crosses the resonance.

## Assumptions and scope

Choose arbitrary real parameters \(a>2\) and \(0<p<1\). For \(0\le x\le 1/2\), set

\[
W_{a,0}(x)=
\begin{cases}
1-ax,&0\le x\le 1/a,\\
\dfrac{2(x-1/a)}{a-2},&1/a<x\le 1/2,
\end{cases}
\]
\[
W_{a,1}(x)=
\begin{cases}
1-ax,&0\le x\le 1/a,\\
\dfrac{2(a-1)(x-1/a)}{a-2},&1/a<x\le1/2.
\end{cases}
\]

Extend each map to the right half by \(W_{a,j}(x)=W_{a,j}(1-x)\). These are exactly the random W-map branches introduced in [1]. Choose the index \(j\in\{0,1\}\) independently at each step, taking \(j=1\) with probability \(p\).

Partition the unit interval into four cells with endpoints \(0,1/a,1/2,(a-1)/a,1\), in increasing order, and call them \(I_1,I_2,I_3,I_4\). All endpoint assignments are immaterial for the absolutely continuous distribution below. Let \(Q_n=i\) when \(X_n\in I_i\). Start \(X_0\) from the **specific stationary absolutely continuous law** described in the proof. No assertion about arbitrary initial distributions, quenched trajectories, or independence of the full real-valued states is intended.

Introduce the parameters

\[
\theta=\frac{a-2}{a},\quad
\eta=\frac{p(a-2)}{a-1},\quad
r=\eta-\theta=\frac{(a-2)(ap-a+1)}{a(a-1)},\quad
\alpha=\frac{\theta}{1-\eta+\theta}.
\]

For all stated parameters, \(0<\theta,\eta,\alpha<1\) and \(-1<r<1\). A second equivalent form of the finite-memory statement is that for every \(k\ge0\), the sigma-algebra generated by \(Q_0,\ldots,Q_k\) is independent of the sigma-algebra generated by \(Q_{k+2},Q_{k+3},\ldots\) exactly at \(p=p_*(a)\).

## Proof

**1. Branch images and symbolic Markov property.** Both outer branches \(I_1,I_4\) of either map have image \([0,1]\). Each central branch \(I_2,I_3\) has image \([0,1/a]\) under \(W_{a,0}\), and image \([0,(a-1)/a]\) under \(W_{a,1}\). Each of these images is a union of complete partition cells. On any branch, the map is affine and injective in the branch interior.

A probability density constant on each cell therefore remains constant on each target cell after applying either map's transfer operator. Indeed, on a target cell, the contribution from each preimage branch is the same constant input density divided by that branch's constant absolute slope. More strongly, conditional on a specified finite history of visited cells and on the present cell, the position within that cell is uniform: this holds initially for a cellwise-constant law and propagates by the preceding full-image affine property. Independent map choices preserve it under mixing. Hence the cell itinerary is a homogeneous Markov chain with transition matrix

\[
P=\begin{pmatrix}E\\ C\\ C\\ E\end{pmatrix},\qquad
E=\left(\frac1a,\frac{a-2}{2a},\frac{a-2}{2a},\frac1a\right),
\]
\[
C=\left(1-p+\frac{p}{a-1},\frac{p(a-2)}{2(a-1)},\frac{p(a-2)}{2(a-1)},0\right).
\]

Here an outer cell has uniformly distributed image over \([0,1]\) and therefore next-symbol distribution \(E\). A central cell maps to the first cell with probability \(1-p\), or uniformly onto the first three cells with probability \(p\), giving \(C\). This establishes the Markov property for all finite histories, not merely the one-step marginal.

**2. Stationarity and the two-mode operator.** The center/outer indicator \(H_n\) is Markov with two-state transition matrix (outer state first)

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}1-\theta&\theta\\1-\eta&\eta\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its stationary center probability is \(\alpha=\theta/(1-\eta+\theta)\). As a result, the row vector

\[
\pi=(1-\alpha)E+\alpha C
\]

satisfies \(\pi P=\pi\), all four entries are positive, and they sum to one. Define a density equal to \(\pi_i/|I_i|\) on each \(I_i\), which is a genuine stationary probability density by the affine transfer calculation. This recovers the density given in [1], so the stationarity premise is explicit and not assumed without justification. Since the rows of \(P\) depend only on the outer/center class, direct multiplication (or the rank-one spectral decomposition of \(K\)) gives, for every integer \(n\ge1\),

\[
P^n=\boldsymbol1\pi+r^{n-1}(P-\boldsymbol1\pi).
\]

Equivalently \((P-\boldsymbol1\pi)^2=r(P-\boldsymbol1\pi)\). There is no unproved spectral-limit argument in this identity: the claimed polynomial is verified by direct matrix multiplication and induction.

**3. Exact resonance and strict one-dependence.** Since \(a>2\), the equality \(r=0\) occurs exactly when \(ap-a+1=0\), or \(p=(a-1)/a\). Then the preceding identity yields \(P^2=\boldsymbol1\pi\), hence any cell at time \(k+2\) is stationary independently of the cells through time \(k\). By the Markov property, the *entire future tail* from time \(k+2\) is also independent of the entire prior cell history. Nevertheless \(P\ne\boldsymbol1\pi\), since the probability of transition to cell \(4\) is \(1/a>0\) from an outer cell but is zero from a central cell. Thus adjoining symbols are not independent. At the resonance,

\[
\pi=\left(\frac{2(a-1)}{a^2},\frac{a-2}{2a},\frac{a-2}{2a},\frac{2}{a^2}\right).
\]

In addition \(\eta=\theta\), so the two rows of \(K\) coincide. Therefore \(H_0,H_1,\ldots\) are iid Bernoulli with parameter \(\theta\), not just uncorrelated.

**4. Off-resonance obstruction and all lag covariances.** For every integer \(n\ge0\), the two-state Markov transition kernel gives

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(H_0,H_n)=\alpha(1-\alpha)r^n.
\]

If \(p\ne p_*(a)\), then \(r\ne0\) and \(0<\alpha<1\). This covariance is nonzero at every nonnegative lag, so no finite dependence radius can hold for the full four-state chain. For \(p<p_*(a)\), \(r<0\) and its signs alternate with lag; for \(p>p_*(a)\), \(r>0\) and all covariance lags are positive. This proves necessity, sufficiency, and uniqueness of the resonance. As a check, for \(a=4\), \(p_*=3/4\), and \(\pi=(3/8,1/4,1/4,1/8)\), while \(H_n\) are iid fair Bernoulli.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify_random_w.py` uses exact symbolic rational functions and rational parameter samples. It checks that \(P\boldsymbol1=\boldsymbol1\), \(\pi P=\pi\), \(P^2=\boldsymbol1\pi+r(P-\boldsymbol1\pi)\), and at the unique resonance \(P^2=\boldsymbol1\pi\ne P\). It separately checks affine-branch endpoint images and the transition identities at 24 rational parameter pairs. Exact symbolic identities together with the proof establish all real-parameter statements; finite samples do not substitute for infinite quantifiers or for the full sigma-algebra argument. The source program uses SymPy and can be invoked with `python verify_random_w.py`.

## Relationship to prior work

Kalle and Maggioni [1, Section 6.2, printed pp. 21–22] introduced exactly this random two-map W family and derived the invariant cellwise-constant density. Their work supplies the source map and the measure; this finding does **not** claim either as new. The additional statement here is a sharp parameter-wide temporal-memory classification: the four-symbol Markov kernel, its full powers, the unique exact two-step mixing resonance, strict one-dependence, and its iid central-indicator factor.

Li and Góra [2] investigate isolated transfer-operator spectral instability and metastability for a different *deterministic* family of W-shaped maps. Góra and Boyarsky [3] study Ulam approximations/stochastic stability for W-shaped maps. The corresponding theorems do not yield the indicated probability-tuned two-step annihilation for this random family without new case-specific calculation. Finite-state Markov algebra itself is standard; the notable structural fact is that this geometrically defined random map has exactly one nontrivial finite-memory switching curve.

## Limitations

The dependence classification applies to the **stationary four-cell symbolic sequence**, not to real-valued states, arbitrary nonstationary initial measures, or pathwise (quenched) behavior. At the critical curve, the four symbols are *one-dependent*, not iid, although their center/outer indicator is iid. The original paper gives parameter conditions ensuring average expansion for its general transfer-operator theory; the present stationary cellwise-constant density is also obtained directly from the finite-state affine calculation for all \(a>2\), \(0<p<1\), without a claim of global uniqueness of the full-state stationary measure outside those conditions. Literature comparison is not a proof of publication priority, and equivalent unindexed calculations may exist. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
