For a normal finite-dimensional matrix \(A\), every Haar numerical-range measurement is optimally predicted by an affine function of \(\langle Ax,x\rangle\) exactly when the distinct eigenvalues of \(A\) form an affine simplex in the complex plane; precisely then the best squared prediction error is the Hilbert–Schmidt distance to \(\operatorname{span}\{I,A,A^*\}\), divided by \(n(n+1)\). A collinear three-level spectrum is the first obstruction: its canonical central spectral projector gains exactly 25% in squared error from a nonlinear predictor.
