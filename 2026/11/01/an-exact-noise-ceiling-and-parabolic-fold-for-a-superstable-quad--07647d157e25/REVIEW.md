# Scientific assessment: exact noise ceiling for a quadratic two-band trap

## Correctness — PASS

The universal statement is about the set-valued scalar quadratic map \(\mathcal F_\varepsilon\), not a randomly selected sample orbit. A necessary inequality \(p_\varepsilon(u)\le0\) is derived from the endpoints of **any** separated interval trap, irrespective of slack in its invariance conditions. At the explicit radical threshold, exact polynomial factorization proves the comparison polynomial is nonnegative on the entire allowable endpoint interval; strict monotonicity in the noise parameter rules out all larger noises through \(1/20\). For smaller noise, a sign-and-convexity argument gives both exact-image interval pairs and distinguishes their boundary stability. Local fold expansion establishes the square-root branch splitting; a reciprocal increment argument gives the critical two-return inverse-time law. The analytic proof supports the unbounded quantifiers; finite code diagnostics corroborate but do not replace it. The smallest-pair claim is proved only among forward-invariant compact sets containing the deterministic orbit.

## Originality — PASS, historical priority provisional

The 2025 Lamb–Rasmussen–Tey manuscript studies noisy **two-dimensional** Hénon maps and identifies boundary folds numerically; it is not a computation of the scalar superstable two-cycle's exact noise ceiling. Sato–Doan–Lamb–Rasmussen (2018) establish a generic composition-and-tangency criterion for a different random logistic model with a period-three attractor, a mathematically close antecedent whose contribution is explicitly acknowledged. The 2025 Tey–Olicón-Méndez–Lamb–Aihara paper treats one-dimensional **order-preserving diffeomorphisms**, so its extremal-boundary fold is related but does not cover this noninvertible quadratic map. The assessed contribution is the explicit algebraic constant, the **necessary-and-sufficient all-interval-trap** inequality and the quantitative parabolic boundary law; neither generic fold existence nor noisy-logistic models are claimed new. Searches for the exact constant, conjugate logistic formulation, band aliases and broader mechanisms did not locate an identical statement. Exact historical priority remains uncertain against specialized older calculations; the complete 2025 Hénon manuscript was not accessible in one continuous view, although its abstract, model, method, and initial sections were inspected.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The bounded-noise threshold is a natural resilience quantity for a superstable quadratic period-two orbit. It separates rigorous robustness of two distinct alternating reachable bands from impossibility of **any** separated interval trap containing the orbit, and does so while the two bands are still more than \(0.58\) apart. This demonstrates that a loss of robust phase separation need not occur through spatial band collision. The exact radical threshold and inverse-time endpoint law give a reproducible benchmark for set-valued boundary bifurcations and stochastic logistic dynamics. The problem is not a generic smooth saddle-node exercise: its global necessity requires handling arbitrary intervals and a folding quadratic map. The result does not imply a full classification of random attractors, sample-path exponents or two-dimensional Hénon dynamics.
