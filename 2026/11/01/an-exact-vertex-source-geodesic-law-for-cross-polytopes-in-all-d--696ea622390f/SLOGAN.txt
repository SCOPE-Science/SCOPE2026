For the surface of the regular \(n\)-cross-polytope, the squared intrinsic distance from a vertex equals the squared Euclidean chord plus exactly \(4(-p_1)_+/(n-1)\), for every target point and every \(n\ge2\). The formula yields a uniquely sharp geodesic stretch \(\sqrt{n/(n-1)}\) at the opposite vertex and a complete polynomial probability law for the squared-geodesic excess under uniform surface sampling.
