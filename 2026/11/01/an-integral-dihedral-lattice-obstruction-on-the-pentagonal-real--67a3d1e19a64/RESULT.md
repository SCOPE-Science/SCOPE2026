# An integral dihedral lattice obstruction on the pentagonal real moment-angle surface

## Finding

Let \(K=C_5\) be the five-cycle with vertex set \(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z\), and let \(X=(D^1,S^0)^K\) be its real moment-angle surface. Its cyclic coordinate permutation \(r(i)=i+1\) and reflection \(s(i)=-i\) generate \(D_5\) (order ten). Write \(H=\langle s\rangle\) and denote by \(\mathbb Z_+\) and \(\mathbb Z_-\) the trivial and sign rank-one integral \(H\)-lattices. Then the natural coordinate-permutation action on first integral homology has the complete integral decomposition

\[
H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\ \cong_{\mathbb Z[D_5]}\ \operatorname{Ind}_H^{D_5}(\mathbb Z_+)\ \oplus\ \operatorname{Ind}_H^{D_5}(\mathbb Z_-).
\]

It has rank ten and its rationalization is the regular \(\mathbb Q[D_5]\)-module, yet **the integral module is not isomorphic to the regular integral group ring**. More precisely, for every reflection \(t\in D_5\), reduction of the integral matrices modulo two gives

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}(t-I\mid H_1(X;\mathbb F_2))=4,
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}(t-I\mid \mathbb F_2[D_5])=5.
\]

In the left-hand expression the coefficient reduction is the homology of \(X\) with \(\mathbb F_2\)-coefficients, identified with \(H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\otimes\mathbb F_2\) because integral first homology is free and \(H_0(X;\mathbb Z)\) has no torsion. This characteristic-two obstruction is invisible over the rationals.

## Assumptions and scope

The polyhedral product \((D^1,S^0)^K\subseteq[-1,1]^5\) consists of cubical cells whose set of varying coordinates is a face of \(C_5\); the empty face is allowed. The \(D_5\)-action is **only** the canonical permutation of coordinates, without additional sign changes of those coordinates. Its generators satisfy \(r^5=s^2=1\) and \(srs=r^{-1}\). The assertion classifies the integral first-homology module of this fixed surface and action; it makes no claim for arbitrary actions on genus-five surfaces.

## Proof

**Integral equivariant cochain decomposition.** Give \(D^1=[-1,1]\) its two endpoint 0-cells and oriented 1-cell. Let \(1\) denote the sum of the two endpoint 0-cochains, \(t\) the positive-endpoint 0-cochain, and \(u\) the oriented 1-cochain. They form an integral basis with \(d1=0\), \(dt=u\), \(du=0\). Tensoring over coordinates and retaining cubical cells admissible for \(K\), every cochain has a unique expansion into monomials \(u_\sigma t_\tau\), where \(\sigma\in K\), \(\tau\cap\sigma=\varnothing\), and other coordinates carry \(1\). The differential preserves the union \(W=\sigma\cup\tau\). For each fixed \(W\subseteq\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z\), this summand is the shifted augmented simplicial cochain complex of the induced subcomplex \(K[W]\): the degree-\(p\) cochains correspond to its \((p-1)\)-simplices, with the usual simplicial orientation signs. This splitting holds **over integers at chain level** and is natural under permutation of coordinates; hence

\[
H^1(X;\mathbb Z)\cong_{\mathbb Z[D_5]}
\bigoplus_{W\subseteq\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z}\widetilde H^0(K[W];\mathbb Z).
\]

**Which subsets contribute.** The full list of \(W\) with disconnected induced subgraph consists of (i) the five unordered nonadjacent vertex pairs and (ii) the five three-vertex subsets that induce an edge together with an isolated vertex. Each such \(K[W]\) has precisely two connected components and contributes one copy of \(\mathbb Z\). The remaining subsets are connected or empty, yielding zero reduced degree-zero cohomology. The five pairs form one transitive \(D_5\)-orbit, as do the five triples, each with order-two stabilizer generated by a reflection. For a pair, the stabilizing reflection interchanges its components and acts as \(-1\) on reduced degree-zero cohomology. For a triple, its stabilizing reflection exchanges the two endpoints of the edge component but preserves the isolated component, and therefore acts as \(+1\). Every reflection subgroup of \(D_5\) is conjugate to \(H\). The orbit-sum modules are consequently \(\operatorname{Ind}_H^{D_5}\mathbb Z_-\) and \(\operatorname{Ind}_H^{D_5}\mathbb Z_+\), respectively.

**Passage to homology.** The cubical complex is connected and has no first-homology torsion: the displayed cohomology and the universal-coefficient theorem for the closed orientable surface \(X\) identify \(H^1\) with the integral dual \(\operatorname{Hom}(H_1,\mathbb Z)\), and the surface first homology is free. Each signed coset-permutation lattice is isomorphic to its integral dual, by declaring its signed coset basis orthonormal. Thus the same induced-summand decomposition holds in \(H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\).

**The nonisomorphism certificate.** The restriction of either five-coset lattice to \(H\) has one fixed coset and two two-element coset orbits. On the fixed coset, \(s\) acts by the inducing sign, while each swapped pair affords the integral regular \(\mathbb Z[H]\)-lattice after a basis sign change. Consequently the restriction of the direct sum is

\[
\mathbb Z_+\oplus\mathbb Z_-\oplus4\,\mathbb Z[H].
\]

Modulo two, \(s-I\) vanishes on both one-dimensional summands and has rank one on each of four regular two-dimensional summands: rank four. By contrast, \(\mathbb Z[D_5]\) restricts to five copies of \(\mathbb Z[H]\), so its mod-two rank is five. Integral module isomorphisms commute with reduction modulo two, giving the obstruction. Rationally, however, \(\mathbb Q_+\oplus\mathbb Q_-\cong\mathbb Q[H]\); inducing up proves \(H_1(X;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q[D_5]\). Every reflection has the same rank by conjugacy. This concludes the proof.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library script `verify.py` exhaustively enumerates the ten disconnected induced subsets; checks the two stabilizer signs, dihedral matrix relations and the full rational character; and computes \(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}(s-I)=4\) on the actual signed cohomology basis. Independently, it builds the exact cubical cellular differentials (32 vertices, 80 edges, 40 squares), checks \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\), and finds mod-101 boundary ranks 31 and 39, giving Betti numbers 10 and 1 in degrees one and two. Its preserved output ends with `VERIFY_OK`. These finite checks corroborate the infinite-free group-module argument for this **fixed finite object**, but the algebraic proof, not field-rank extrapolation, establishes the integral claim.

## Relationship to prior work

Panov's lecture notes (2010) discuss moment-angle topology and integral cohomological methods. Yu's 2014 arXiv preprint develops Hochster-type decompositions for moment-angle quotient constructions; the natural integral decomposition above is a standard special-purpose tool, not claimed as original. Kamiyama (2012) analyzes the dihedral action and computes **integral homology of the orbit spaces** of the real moment-angle polygon surface; his Theorems A/B concern quotient groups, not an integral representation of the covering surface's first homology. Kamiyama's 2013 companion paper studies necklace sequences and orbit-space homology. The finding claimed here is specifically the **two induced integral summands together with their index-two-characteristic obstruction relative to the regular lattice**. Previous non-equivariant genus and Betti numbers, as well as the rational regular character alone, do not imply this lattice nonisomorphism.

## Limitations

Only the canonical \(D_5\)-action on the pentagonal real moment-angle surface is treated. The assertion does not classify full homeomorphism-group representations, arbitrary polygon sizes, or integral intersection pairings. A companion paper on necklace sequences and orbit-space homology could not be inspected in full; an equivalent unpublished or unindexed group-module computation remains a possible originality risk. Rational equivalence alone does not imply integral equivalence, and this result does not claim independent verification beyond the accompanying reproducibility checks.
