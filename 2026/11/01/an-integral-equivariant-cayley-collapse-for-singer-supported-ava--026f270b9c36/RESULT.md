# An integral equivariant Cayley collapse for Singer-supported avalanche complexes

## Finding
Let \(D\subset\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) be a cyclic perfect difference set of cardinality \(k\ge3\): every nonzero residue has **exactly one ordered** representation \(d-d'\) with distinct \(d,d'\in D\). Thus \(n=k(k-1)+1\). On the consistently oriented directed \(n\)-cycle, place one chip precisely at the positions of \(D\), and let \(K(D)\) be the simplicial avalanche complex generated by the simultaneous-firing sets. The translation group \(G=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) acts on \(K(D)\) by rotating vertex labels.

Choose \(a\in D\) with \(a+1\in D\); such an \(a\) exists and is unique by the perfect-difference property. There is a **translation-equivariant sequence of elementary simplicial collapses** from \(K(D)\) onto the graph \(L(D,a)\) with vertex set \(G\) and undirected edges
\[
\{x,x+d\}\quad(x\in G,\ d\in (D-a)\setminus\{0\}),
\]
where every edge carries its specified orientation from \(x\) to \(x+d\). The \(k-1\) translation-orbits of these edges are distinct, and the step-one orbit forms a Hamiltonian \(n\)-cycle. Moreover, as a \(G\)-CW complex, \(L(D,a)\) is \(G\)-equivariantly homotopy equivalent to that \(n\)-cycle with \(k-2\) freely translated loop-orbits, each orbit contributing one loop at every vertex. Consequently, **over the integers**, with the natural translation action,
\[
\widetilde H_1(K(D);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z_{\mathrm{triv}}\oplus\mathbb Z[G]^{\oplus(k-2)},
\qquad \widetilde H_i(K(D);\mathbb Z)=0\quad(i\ne1).
\]
The invariant summand is generated by the oriented Hamiltonian cycle of the collapsed graph; the remaining summands admit an explicitly translated basis of integral chord-minus-arc cycles. For a nonidentity rotation the first-homology trace is \(1\), whereas at the identity it is \(1+n(k-2)\).

By Singer's construction, for **every prime power** \(q\ge2\) one may take \(n=q^2+q+1\) and \(k=q+1\). Thus this family has
\[
\widetilde H_1(K(D);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z[\mathbb Z/(q^2+q+1)\mathbb Z]^{\oplus(q-1)},
\qquad\operatorname{rank}\widetilde H_1=q^3,
\]
with all higher reduced homology zero. The equivariant collapse and integral lattice identification, rather than the previously accessible nonequivariant projective-plane cycle rank, are the specific contribution.

## Assumptions and scope
The digraph has vertex set \(G\), one directed edge \(x\to x+1\) from each vertex, out-degree one, and no sinks. In every parallel firing round, each occupied vertex fires once, losing its single chip and sending it to its successor. The initial configuration is binary with \(k<n\) occupied sites; there are no extra chips. The avalanche simplices are all subsets of vertex sets that fire together at some round, including the initial round. The rotation action is on the **entire** complex of all firing rounds, not on the chosen initial state.

A perfect difference set is assumed to exist in the first part; no non-prime-power existence assertion is made. The prime-power existence statement uses Singer's classical theorem. The equivariant homotopy equivalence is for geometric realizations as \(G\)-CW spaces. The displayed decomposition concerns the full integral group-ring lattice and is noncanonical: choosing the distinguished ordered unit difference determines a basis.

## Proof
**Firing orbit.** For a binary initial state, the parallel firing recurrence is exactly \(c^{(t+1)}_x=c^{(t)}_{x-1}\). Every configuration remains binary and the fired support at round \(t\) is \(D+t\). All supports repeat after \(n\) rounds, so
\[
K(D)=\bigcup_{t\in G}\Delta(D+t).
\]
For distinct \(t,u\), an intersection point satisfies \(t+d=u+d'\), hence \(d-d'=u-t\). By uniqueness of perfect differences there is exactly one such ordered pair. Therefore distinct facets \(\Delta(D+t)\) and \(\Delta(D+u)\) meet in exactly one vertex and no edge belongs to two facets.

**Translation-equivariant simplicial collapse.** Fix the unique \(a\in D\) with \(a+1\in D\). In the facet \(B_t=D+t\), choose root \(r_t=t+a\). Pair every nonempty face \(\sigma\subset B_t\setminus\{r_t\}\) of cardinality at least two with \(\sigma\cup\{r_t\}\). Remove these face-coface pairs in descending order of \(|\sigma|\). At each step \(\sigma\) is a free face: within its unique original facet all strictly larger cofaces, except the displayed companion, were previously removed. Because distinct facets share no edge, operations in separate facets do not interfere. Rotating by \(g\in G\) sends each pair in facet \(t\) to the corresponding pair in facet \(t+g\); hence every cardinality stage is a disjoint invariant family of elementary collapses. The surviving faces are the original vertices and, for every \(t\in G\), the \(k-1\) edges from \(t+a\) to \(t+d\) with \(d\in D\setminus\{a\}\). Writing \(x=t+a\) gives exactly \(L(D,a)\). These edges are all distinct: a repeated unordered point-pair in two different facets would contradict unique block intersections. Thus the collapse is a genuine translation-equivariant simple-homotopy reduction, not just a homology equivalence.

**Equivariant graph model.** Since \(1\in D-a\), the edges \(e_{x,1}:x\to x+1\) form a spanning \(n\)-cycle. For each other step \(d\in(D-a)\setminus\{0,1\}\), let \(e_{x,d}:x\to x+d\) be the corresponding oriented chord (using the unique representative \(2\le d\le n-1\)). Construct \(Y\) by retaining the spanning \(n\)-cycle and adding a loop \(\ell_{x,d}\) at each vertex \(x\), for every such \(d\), with translations carrying \(\ell_{x,d}\) to \(\ell_{x+g,d}\). Map each \(\ell_{x,d}\) to the path consisting of \(e_{x,d}\) followed by the backward unit-edge arc from \(x+d\) to \(x\); conversely map each chord \(e_{x,d}\) to \(\ell_{x,d}\) followed by the forward unit-edge arc from \(x\) to \(x+d\). Fix the spanning cycle in both maps. These maps and their edge-path cancellation homotopies commute with translations, establishing \(G\)-equivariant homotopy equivalence between \(L(D,a)\) and \(Y\).

**Exact integral homology and no hidden extension.** Set
\[
N=\sum_{x\in G}e_{x,1},\qquad
z_{x,d}=e_{x,d}-\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}e_{x+j,1}
\quad(2\le d\le n-1,\ d\in D-a).
\]
Both \(N\) and each \(z_{x,d}\) are integral one-cycles. Every integral one-cycle \(c\) can first be stripped of its chord coefficients by subtracting \(\sum_{x,d}c_{x,d}z_{x,d}\). The remainder is supported only on the unit edges. Its boundary vanishes precisely when all remaining unit-edge coefficients are the same integer, so it is an integral multiple of \(N\). Uniqueness holds because each \(z_{x,d}\) has coefficient \(1\) on its own chord and \(0\) on all other chords, while \(N\) contains no chord. Thus these cycles form a **\(\mathbb Z\)-basis** of \(H_1\), not merely a rational or mod-two spanning set. Translation sends \(N\) to itself and \(z_{x,d}\) to \(z_{x+g,d}\). There are \(k-2\) extra step-orbits, proving the stated integral group-ring decomposition. As \(Y\) is a connected graph, all other reduced homology vanishes.

Finally \(1+n(k-2)=1+(q^2+q+1)(q-1)=q^3\), and Singer's theorem supplies such a \(D\) for every prime power \(q\).

## Relationship to prior work
Riihimäki and Smith (2026) define avalanche complexes for parallel sandpile dynamics and prove special classifications on directed cycles, notably for consecutive occupied blocks and reduced binary configurations. Their accessible full manuscript does not study cyclic planar difference-set supports, a rotation-equivariant simplicial collapse or integral translation lattices. The discrete orbit rule is elementary and is not claimed as new.

Singer's theorem and subsequent finite-geometry literature supply the perfect difference sets; their existence and projective-plane incidence interpretation are classical. Existing topology results for projective-plane incidence and related Čech/neighborhood complexes already imply the familiar nonequivariant wedge-of-\(q^3\)-circles phenomenon. The **new assertion is the connection to the avalanche firing complex together with the equivariant simplicial collapse and integral (not merely rational) \(G\)-lattice basis**. General finite graph-cover homology theory, including Gaschütz's theorem, already predicts the corresponding **complex** character after one identifies the graph cover. It does not itself supply this specific equivariant collapse or the displayed integral generators; no originality is claimed for the general rational covering-space character formula.

## Limitations
The claim requires a cyclic planar difference set and hence does not classify arbitrary binary supports. The existence of a cyclic planar difference set for non-prime-power order is neither assumed nor asserted. No assertion is made about filtration-level persistent avalanche modules, variants with sequential instead of simultaneous firing, or noncyclic digraphs. The isomorphism of integral translation modules depends on choosing the unique ordered difference of one. The family includes a \(q=2\) case whose nonequivariant three-grain homotopy type was previously accessible; its integral equivariant coordinates are included for uniformity. An equivalent statement in unindexed design or graph-cover literature cannot be ruled out without broader independent review.
