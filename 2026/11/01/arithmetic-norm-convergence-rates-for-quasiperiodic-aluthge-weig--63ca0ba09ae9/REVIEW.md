# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact weighted-shift polar decomposition and binomial iterates give a direct norm formula. Fourier multiplier decay and density prove norm convergence; the Diophantine Fourier split proves the \(k^{1/3}\) upper rate; a continued-fraction convergent gives a uniform Fourier-coefficient lower barrier. The recursive Liouville denominator construction yields analytic nonzero weights and a rigorous lower bound on infinitely many chosen iterates. The independent finite numerical checks supplement rather than establish these universal claims. Boundary assumptions include positive bounded weights, mean-zero continuous logs, irrational rotation, and integer iterate indices.

**Originality — PASS, subject to literature risk.** Claim-level comparisons with the accessible full text of the 2026 composition-operator Aluthge paper, the 2026 compact-operator study, and the 2022 fixed-window Birkhoff averaging paper show no direct implication of the present binomial-kernel arithmetic phase diagram. The closest directly comparable older weighted-shift paper provides qualitative strong-limit classifications in the accessible abstract, but its full text could not be inspected; novelty is therefore a best-knowledge assessment, not a complete publication-priority guarantee. Comparisons of published Aluthge, weighted-shift, quasiperiodic, and Diophantine statements identified adjacent but not dominating results.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The first natural nonconstant analytic profile, the centered Poisson kernel, exhibits a proven arithmetic-optimal exponent and a universal lower speed barrier. The analytic Liouville counterweight shows that regularity alone cannot deliver a uniform norm rate despite unconditional convergence. This refines operator-topology convergence into a meaningful quantitative arithmetic distinction for a noncompact operator family, rather than a routine finite test.

**Closest prior work and residual limits.** Rion's 2016 paper is a substantive but incompletely accessible priority risk. The upper bound is not an exact leading asymptotic coefficient. The lower barrier is specifically for Poisson profiles, and arbitrarily slow convergence is subsequential; no claim is made for arbitrary rotations uniformly or arbitrary analytic log symbols.
