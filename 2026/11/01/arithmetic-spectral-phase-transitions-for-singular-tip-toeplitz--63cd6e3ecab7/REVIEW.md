# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Decomposition under the two angular rotations gives the Laurent monomial basis, with nonnegative z-degree by regularity on the z-axis. Polar integration gives squared norm pi²/((m+1)t) for t=n+1+(m+1)/gamma>0, and divergence otherwise. Both compressions are diagonal by angular orthogonality; their entries are expectations against 2t r^(2t-1)dr, giving t/(t+beta/2) and R^(2t). At gamma=P/Q reduced, invert Q mod P to obtain every t=k/P, and translate (m,n) by (P,-Q) to obtain infinitely many occurrences at fixed k. For irrational gamma, density modulo one of (m+1)/gamma, together with integer shifts, gives density throughout the positive half-line; equality of two parameters would contradict irrationality unless both indices agreed. The ordinary spectra are the closures of these lists. At each spectral point there is either an infinite orthonormal eigensystem or an orthonormal approximate eigensystem, proving equality with the Fredholm essential spectra. The endpoint eigenvalue exclusions, inverse norm, and norm attainment statements follow from the diagonal entries; in the irrational power case they also give zero kernel and dense, nonclosed range. With b=P beta/2 the first power value is 1/(1+b) and each interior gap b/((k+b)(k+1+b)) is smaller. For s=R^(2/P) the top omission is 1-s and each interior gap is at most s(1-s). Thus the endpoint omissions give the exact Hausdorff distances. On approaching a positive irrational parameter, bounded reduced numerators would force bounded denominators and hence a finite set of rationals; the distances therefore vanish. Irrational approximants prove discontinuity at each rational.

## value

PASS

The value lies in the exact arithmetic discontinuity and spectral-type contrast, not the scalar moment integrals. At rational exponents one has an exact inversion bound for the power observation and a strict, attained concentration ceiling for the tip, whereas the irrational parameter has no bounded inversion and unit concentration supremum without attainment. The endpoint-controlled Hausdorff defect identifies precisely how fast rational spectra fill the interval as their reduced numerators grow, and why continuity fails at rational parameters despite dense approximation from both regimes.
