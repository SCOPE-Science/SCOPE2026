# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** Weighted Bergman monomials give the exact beta-moment formula. The change of variables \(s=t/(1-t)\) gives an absolutely convergent boundary-value integral for Tricomi's \(U\). The fixed-parameter formula DLMF 13.8.11, principal branch \(z=-ic\), and fixed-order \(K\)-Bessel asymptotic yield the stated complex relative \(O(n^{-1/2})\) expansion, including the crucial \(e^{ic/2}\) phase. Weyl's criterion follows from the square-root phase's exponential sums \(O(\sqrt N)\). The two-sided logarithmic cutoffs and sign densities yield the coefficient \(1/(4c)\); the exponentially damped eigenvalues yield all Schatten memberships. All boundary assumptions and the difference between relative complex and absolute real remainders are explicit. Separate finite exponential-integral identities and large-index checks pass, but are not substituted for the proof.

## Originality

**PASS, limited to best-knowledge assessment.** Li–Wang's compactness characterization does not determine quantitative signed spectral laws. Looi's oscillatory radial models retain nonvanishing eigenvalue oscillation and have different boundary phase scales; his theorem does not dominate the root-exponentially compact model. Rozenblum's compactly supported disk results and Bargmann oscillation example do not establish the two-sided signed logarithmic-square law. Rozenblum–Vasilevski's trace-class theory concerns broader symbol classes but no explicit signed counting constant. The prior large-parameter Tricomi asymptotic and radial diagonalization are credited as standard, and no originality is claimed for them. Equivalence and stronger-coverage searches found no directly covering theorem. There remains a plausible risk of an older oscillatory Mellin or Toeplitz spectral-counting theorem implying this specialization.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** A bounded boundary symbol that never settles to zero can nevertheless generate an operator in *every* Schatten class through oscillatory cancellation. The complete positive/negative eigenvalue counting law gives an exact natural invariant and exposes a balanced distribution with a coefficient independent of the Bergman weight and chirp phase. This refines qualitative compactness and distinguishes rapid phase cancellation from the logarithmic-frequency oscillation of earlier essential-positivity counterexamples. It does not purport to solve general radial-symbol classification.
