# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The closed-neighborhood rule is precisely \(F(S)=E([n]\setminus U(S))\), not the open-neighborhood NOR rule. Direct case analysis establishes all cycles and their one-step attraction fibers. Inclusion–exclusion counts exact-support graphs; a labelled injection proves unique basin dominance; Bonferroni bounds and the empty-graph correction yield the sharp asymptotic. Two independently structured enumerators reproduce all individual basin formulas for \(n=4,5,6\), with the base-support implementation also covering \(n=7\). The infinite assertions follow from the proof, not the finite computations.

## Originality

**PASS, with residual literature risk.** General eventual period-two results and a general method for counting periodic points of self-including NAND/NOR systems are known, and the special periodic-point count for \(T_4\) is covered by a 2020 regular-graph corollary. These are excluded from the novelty claim. Exact individual basins for the entire \(L(K_n)\) family and their sharp asymptotic concentration are not established by the accessible primary texts inspected. The 2012 paper on maxterm/minterm orbit structure was available only in bibliographic and abstract form, leaving a relevant but nondecisive uninspected-source risk.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The triangular graphs constitute a canonical infinite family, and attraction-basin sizes are intrinsic, mathematically motivated robustness invariants. The theorem distinguishes exponentially many recurrent orbits from a single overwhelmingly dominant basin and quantifies the exponentially rare exceptional initial configurations with a sharp constant and exponent. It provides the complete exact finite basin census, not an arbitrary numerical slice. This need not be interpreted as a general theorem for arbitrary Boolean networks.

## Closest literature and limitations

The 2015 survey and the 2017 period theorem cover the periodicity framework. The 2020 periodic-point census covers broad background and can imply part of the orbit-count component, but does not supply the exact triangular-graph basin distribution or its asymptotic. The 2014 AND–OR–NOT fixed-point work addresses a different invariant. The 2012 orbit-structure paper remains an inaccessible plausible overlap source. Results require closed self-inclusive NOR and \(n\ge4\); no claims are made for the open-neighborhood rule.
