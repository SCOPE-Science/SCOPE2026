# Scientific review: complete Tietze higher-independence homotopy classification

## Correctness
PASS. Both independent vertex-subset membership algorithms agree on all \(2^{12}\) masks. For every integer cutoff from \(1\) through \(12\), the standalone checker reconstructs and replays all matching pairs, performs exhaustive acyclicity verification on the entire directed Hasse diagram, recomputes every signed critical-cell differential, verifies explicit determinant-one maximal-rank minors, and independently reduces the full simplicial chain complex modulo two. Across all cases the total is 33,248 nonempty faces, 16,345 discrete-Morse pairs, and 184,650 Hasse incidences. Correctness is not inferred from the saved execution output: the verifier and saved finite certificate were actually replayed. Since the two-level critical-cell complexes start above dimension one, primitive integer attachments cancel and prove the stated integral homotopy wedge types. All claims are restricted to the precisely enumerated twelve-vertex graph and the explicitly described cutoff complex.

## Originality
PASS against the sources inspected, with bibliographic residual risk. The original higher-independence family calculations of Deshpande–Singh, arXiv:2001.05448, cover multipartite/whiskered, cycle and regular-tree classes, not Tietze's cubic nonchordal graph. Deshpande–Shukla–Singh, arXiv:2001.06775, proves general connectivity bounds and a homotopy-wedge theorem for chordal graphs, not a numerical classification of this nonchordal graph. Abdelmalek et al., arXiv:2106.10863, focus on chordal graphs and trees. The indexed Petersen second higher-independence computation uses a different graph; no published homotopy-preserving transformation from the Petersen graph to the Tietze graph was located. Searches for Tietze, vertex-to-triangle Petersen expansion, the dissociation complex, and exact sphere-multiplicity signatures found no covering statement. No assertion of absolute novelty is justified solely by negative searches; unindexed results remain possible.

## Value
PASS. The Tietze graph is a natural 12-vertex cubic test graph obtained by a canonical vertex-to-triangle expansion of the Petersen graph. Rather than one small isolated Betti number, the claim supplies an entire natural integral homotopy classification of the higher-independence family for the graph. The sequence has a genuine mixed-dimensional transition at \(r=5\), rises to 193 six-spheres at \(r=7\), and returns through systematic high-cutoff wedges to a simplex. The exact change of homotopy type across cutoffs is relevant to graph-complex structure, with no unsupported assertion that it proves a broader theorem.

## Closest literature and boundaries
The specifically inspected papers are arXiv:2001.05448, arXiv:2001.06775, and arXiv:2106.10863. A related indexed calculation of the Petersen \(2\)-independence complex has different vertex and edge sets and cannot imply the Tietze sequence without an unproved transfer theorem. Ordinary graph independence calculations, generic chordal bounds, and homology over a single field would not determine the stated sequence of integral homotopy types. The full-simplex endpoint \(r\ge12\) is standard; it is reported for completeness rather than novelty. The output does not determine filtration maps, naturality, group actions or a universal graph-family result.
