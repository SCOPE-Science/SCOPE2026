# Scientific assessment: wheel matching-complex dihedral fixed spaces

**Correctness — PASS.** The graph is the wheel on a rim of \(n\geq5\) vertices, not a wheel with \(n\) total vertices. The orbit-barycenter lemma proves actual PL identifications for geometric fixed points. The spoke, rim-edge, and conflict calculations cover nonidentity rotations and both even-rim reflection conjugacy classes, including primitive rotations with no admissible orbit. The homotopy deductions use the standard path-fold recurrence and the published cycle-independence homotopy formula. The self-contained checker established 4,080 exact orbit-graph template comparisons, 105 independently generated fixed matching-face comparisons, and 28 boundary-rank checks. No finite check is presented as an infinite proof. Risk: mathematical arguments and implementation were not independently reviewed.

**Originality — PASS, subject to residual discovery risk.** The closest full-text primary source, Vega's *2-Matching Complexes*, Section 5 and Theorem 5.2, proves nonequivariant wheel matching-complex homotopy types and the cycle formula; it does not classify any geometric fixed loci. Bayer–Jelić Milutinović–Vega's *Matching Complexes of Outerplanar Graphs* proves a general nonequivariant result for outerplanar graphs and names wheel graphs as a separate planar example. The closest published searchable records concern cycle matching characters and **wheel discrete-Morse matching complexes**, distinct from the ordinary matching complex and its actual fixed-point spaces. Statements, not titles alone, were compared: neither homology characters nor the non-equivariant equivalences imply the fixed loci. No directly covering theorem surfaced in the inspected full texts and targeted searches. An unindexed equivalent treatment remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** The result solves the naturally complete, infinite-family problem of fixed-space homotopy under every nontrivial standard dihedral symmetry of a fundamental planar graph family whose ordinary homotopy type was already determined. The classification contains both empty and nonempty fixed spaces, arbitrarily high-dimensional spheres, two-sphere wedges, and two combinatorially different even-reflection triangulations that share one homotopy type. These are invariants of actual fixed spaces, not consequences of sphere multiplicities or alternating homology traces. The orbit-conflict classification is a structural result, not an arbitrary finite slice or parameter substitution.
