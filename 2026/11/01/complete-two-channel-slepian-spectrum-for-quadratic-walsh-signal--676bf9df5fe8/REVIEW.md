# Scientific review: quadratic Walsh concentration on majority halfcubes

## Correctness — PASS
The exact operator is the compression of multiplication by a strict homogeneous majority indicator onto the full Walsh band of degrees zero, one and two. Antipodal symmetry makes the diagonal parity blocks exactly \(I/2\). Conditioning on one and three signs gives \(\alpha=a_d\) and \(\beta=-a_d/(d-2)\); the pair-incidence identity then gives the *entire* two-channel singular spectrum and its multiplicities. Explicit normalized Walsh polynomials attain both sharp concentration bounds. The strict inequalities distinguish the degenerate \(d=3\) case from all odd \(d\ge5\). The self-contained exact checker tests all Gram entries through \(d=13\) in six odd dimensions and thousands of analytic ratio identities. These computations do not replace the proof over all odd dimensions.

## Originality — PASS (priority provisional)
The related general Boolean-cube Slepian construction and qualitative rank consequences of Hogan–Lakey (2018/2019) are prior work. The published general homogeneous-halfspace result already determines the \(1/2\)-eigenspace multiplicity and symmetric pairing for arbitrary bandwidth; neither is claimed as a separate discovery. The exact first-Walsh-band Hamming-ball theorem concerns only degrees zero and one. A quadratic-band shell theorem concerns concentration on *one shell*, not on the strict-majority union of shells. These full text materials and source-equivalence searches did not produce the present two *noncentral* singular levels, all extremizers and sharp condition number. General implicit block decompositions could still imply this explicit formula after additional computation, and a historical priority review remains outstanding.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The quadratic-majority setting is a natural low-bandwidth extension of linear Walsh Slepian concentration at half cube density. The theorem identifies all five spectral levels and their representation-theoretic multiplicities, gives exact sharp energy and sampling bounds, and isolates two different limiting channels. In contrast, antipodal parity alone fixes only the center of the spectrum, not the extremal energy fractions or optimizing polynomials. The dimension-three exception and lack of noise stability or higher-band generalization are explicit.
