# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The claim fixes the unscaled-projector normalization and the exact domain \(\gcd(N,6)=1\). Expanding the cubic ambiguity reduces the local calculation to a quadratic Gauss sum; its exact magnitude follows by double character orthogonality without an unproved evaluation of the Gauss-sum phase. Prime-power valuation counting gives every local multiplicity, and the Chinese remainder tensor identification gives all composite moduli. Weyl diagonalization identifies the entire operator kernel. The odd-cycle ratio argument establishes full-support uniqueness, and a cycle Poincaré inequality proves the explicit flat-state stability bound. Executed finite checks at seven moduli and direct intensity checks support, but do not replace, the proof.

## Originality
**PASS under bounded comparison.** Goldberger–Kang–Okoudjou already prove the general Gabor projector-Gram eigenvalue formula and the exact **prime** Alltop rank. Li (2026), Proposition 5.1, already gives **one direction** of the prime-power zero rule. The present all-modulus exact multiplicity product, complete Weyl-kernel classification, and stable flat-signal recovery are stronger and were not found verbatim or as explicit special cases in the inspected sections. The 2015 Bojarovska–Flinth sufficient full-support theorem requires nonzero zero-delay modulation ambiguities, which the constant-modulus cubic chirp fails; it does not directly imply the present full-support recovery. Similar exact results for other windows are not equivalent. Residual risk: elementary Gauss-sum and CRT machinery could make an unlocated earlier result cover the quantitative theorem, and the prime-modulus part is explicitly prior art.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The exact rank, nonzero conditioning tiers, and identifiable Weyl directions answer a natural tomography design question for the classical cubic phase window across all admissible composite dimensions. The sharp contrast between a rank fraction tending to zero and retained stable nonlinear recovery on flat signals is structurally relevant: linear operator identifiability and restricted signal identifiability behave differently. The explicit arithmetic multiplicities quantify rather than merely exhibit the known prime-power obstruction.

## Boundaries
The source date is the earliest verified first-posting date among the selected archival motivating preprints, 2013-04-29. The original 1980 Alltop article is cited as historical background without inventing a day-level first-public date. Moduli divisible by two or three, nonunit coefficients, unrestricted stability, and sharpness of the flat-state constant are outside the proved scope. The bibliographic comparison remains bounded, and independent validation has not been performed.
