# Critical spectral-gap synchronization: scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For every finite connected unweighted undirected graph, the centered energy identity, strong monotonicity identity for the cubic, and Poincaré estimate give a differential inequality with a negative quadratic term **even when its linear coefficient vanishes**. Integration yields the stated endpoint inverse-time upper bound for all complex initial conditions. Dissipativity gives forward completeness. The analytic-gradient convergence theorem gives pointwise limits, and the center-stable manifold theorem makes the origin basin null. The two-node antisymmetric invariant subspace has an explicit solution whose centered energy is asymptotic to inverse time. A standalone standard-library numerical checker passed the finite diagnostics specified in VERIFICATION.md. Limit: finite sampling is not an infinite proof; the main inequalities and classical dynamical theorems furnish that proof.

## Originality — PASS under inspected literature, with a priority risk
The assessed new quantitative item is **the endpoint** \(c\lambda_2=\mu\) all-initial-state inverse-time inequality together with its graph-uniform order-sharp two-site witness. Strictly above-threshold exponential synchronization and almost-global asymptotic synchronization are not claimed as novel. The 2010 Chung–Slotine paper directly addresses almost-global synchronization for positive coupling and an exponential strong-coupling threshold. Its publicly accessible abstract does **not** imply an endpoint inverse-time estimate, but its complete text was inaccessible, so an earlier equivalent endpoint estimate cannot be ruled out definitively. The 2026 Chen–Hsiao paper works with positive amplitude barriers and semicircle data under a small-coupling condition, so its Theorem 3.1 cannot imply the endpoint all-state result. The 2025 Millán et al. paper concerns sectorial data and real reductions; Gallo et al. supply local master-stability rather than endpoint global convergence estimates. No equivalent indexed statement was identified in actual semantic record searches. This is a best-knowledge same mathematical model finding, not a certified historical priority determination.

## Value — PASS
The endpoint is a natural spectral-gap boundary where ordinary linear contraction ceases to supply decay. The theorem supplies a finite-time all-state bound, proves that nonlinear dissipation still forces synchronization at equality, and exhibits an exact trajectory with the optimal inverse-time **order**. The boundary result is useful for distinguishing a true loss of synchronization from a change in relaxation law in amplitude-inclusive oscillator networks. Its content is about a natural entire parameter hypersurface, not an arbitrary small numerical special case. It does not claim the prefactor is sharp on each graph or that the threshold is necessary for each fixed graph.
