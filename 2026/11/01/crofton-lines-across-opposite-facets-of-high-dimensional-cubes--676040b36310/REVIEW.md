# Scientific review: Crofton lines across opposite facets of high-dimensional cubes

## Correctness — PASS

The invariant line measure induces projected-area directional weighting and facet-specific flux \(|u_i|\). Its Gaussian homogeneous ratio correctly yields the Rayleigh-weighted factor \(F\), and the proof controls saddlepoint tails as well as compact-limit expansions. The chi radial law and reciprocal expansion produce the conditional chord-length theorem. The independent Crofton face-tracing implementation reproduces exact low-dimensional integrals within sampling error; saddlepoint and moment checks reproduce the predicted limiting constants. The finite checks are corroboration, not a substitute for the analytic tail bounds.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)

The closest inspected cube high-dimensional reference, Kabluchko–Prochno–Thäle (2019, arXiv:1910.02676), develops random orthogonal projection laws and projected-product large deviations, not Crofton affine-line exit-face probability. Akopyan–Edelsbrunner–Nikitenko (2020, arXiv:2007.07783) use Crofton sampling for spherical random polytopes. Egan's *Pick Two Points* gives an opposite-facet probability of \(2/(d+1)\) but **only** for the line generated by two volume-uniform points; in dimension two this differs sharply from \(\sqrt2-1\). Published indexed results on cube widths and central hyperplane sections are not equivalent. Coleman's 1969 abstract covers multiple chord sampling mechanisms, but the full article was inaccessible, and unknown historical duplication remains possible. These unresolved risks are recorded without treating a missing search hit as uniqueness proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem identifies a precise geometric rare-event law for the entire cube family: a sharp stretched exponential of order \(\exp[-\Theta(d^{2/3})]\), a nontrivial subleading factor, and conditional long-chord Gaussian fluctuations at the unusual scale \(d^{1/6}\). It clarifies how line-sampling convention changes even the qualitative large-dimensional probability behavior. It is not presented as a theorem for arbitrary bodies or for uniformly sampled point pairs.

## Verification and limitations

The attached `verify.py` checks the exact integral against independently traced Crofton lines and tests numerical saddlepoint convergence. The proof in `RESULT.md` establishes limits analytically. The sampling model must remain explicitly Crofton-affine-line sampling, and a hidden historical equivalent cannot be excluded solely by indexed searches.
