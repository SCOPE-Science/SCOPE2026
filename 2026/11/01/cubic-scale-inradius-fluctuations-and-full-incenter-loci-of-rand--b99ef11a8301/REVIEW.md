# Self-review

## Correctness (PASS)
The exact intrinsic ball criterion is \(|z_i|+r\sqrt{1-u_i^2}\le1\) for every cube coordinate. Central symmetry permits a ball of the same radius at the origin, proving the global radius \((1-\min_i u_i^2)^{-1/2}\) without an appeal to finite experiments. At a normal with a single smallest absolute coordinate, only the corresponding coordinate inequality is active at the zero incenter, so the exact incenter locus has affine dimension \(n-2\). Haar-sphere squared coordinates are Dirichlet with parameter \(1/2\); the translated-simplex Jacobian gives the entire survival law, including both sharp endpoint coefficients. The minimum of independent half-normal coordinates is exponentially scaled; normalizing by the chi-square sum and proving an exponential uniform tail bound yields the squared-exponential cubic-scale limit and all positive fixed moments. All steps, including endpoints and normalization, were reconstructed and compared with independently generated section polygons. The standalone checker passed 400 literal 3-cube section-radius checks, six 2D law quadratures and high-dimensional distribution diagnostics. A separately constructed local linear-program verification over dimensions two, three, four, five, eight, and fifteen also confirmed the radius formula in 300 independent cases. This local numerical check is not an independent mathematical audit.

## Originality (PASS, qualified by the stated priority risk)
The motivating full-text paper of Guédon, Litvak, Tatarko and Vritsiou (arXiv:2609.24131v1, 2026-09-21) studies outer radii/diameters of random convex-body sections, not maximal *inscribed* Euclidean balls in the cube sections. Brandenburg and Meroni (arXiv:2510.09265v1, 2025-10-10) give bounded-dimensional combinatorial-type and vertex-count classifications, and their exact results do not fix within-type Euclidean inradius. Ambrus (arXiv:2107.14778) treats volume criticality. Böröczky–Henk (1997/2000) determine expected cross-polytope projection face counts, not the circumradius of the projected body. Published related records titled “Cube-section incenter loci of Boolean signed-chain polytopes” concern an entirely different Boolean-poset polytope, while “Direction-resolved half-normal fluctuations of random central cube sections” concerns directional section vertex counts. The published “Exact inradius law for random planar projections of the regular octahedron” computes the *projected cross-polytope's own inradius* (the reciprocal of cube-section circumradius), not the cube section's inradius (the reciprocal of projected cross-polytope circumradius). Both genuine polar-dual formulations were searched and compared; neither implies the stated incenter classification, full law, sharp endpoints, and moment limit. Searches of indexed results and available full primary statements did not identify a covering claim. This is evidence for originality, not proof of historical uniqueness; unindexed earlier special-case formulae or a stronger work remain possible.

## Mathematical value (PASS)
The exact intrinsic inradius is a natural geometric invariant of random sections of one of the principal model convex bodies. The proof gives a complete fixed-dimensional probability law and an unexpectedly small cubic-scale high-dimensional excess with non-Gaussian squared-exponential fluctuations and convergence of every positive moment. It also characterizes all maximizing ball centers, which generically form an \((n-2)\)-dimensional continuum rather than one point, and determines two distinct endpoint exponents. These precise, motivated model facts are not just the known cubical section face counts or a trivial renaming of random diameters; the result is a reproducible benchmark rather than a claim of a general convex-body inequality.

## Limits and comparison
A central hyperplane is essential, and random directions are Haar-uniform. The displayed Dirichlet integral does not simplify to an elementary expression for all dimensions. The real-parameter positive-moment result is asymptotic and has no assertion about large deviations outside the fixed-scale limit. Historically unindexed stronger results remain a residual priority risk. The finite checks verify selected cases but do not establish the universal proof or independent certification.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
