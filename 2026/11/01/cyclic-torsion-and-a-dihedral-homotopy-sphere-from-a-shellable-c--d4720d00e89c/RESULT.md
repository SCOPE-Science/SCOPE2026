# Cyclic torsion and a dihedral homotopy sphere from a shellable circulant flag complex

## Finding

Let \(G=C_{16}(1,4,8)\) be the circulant graph with vertex set \(\mathbb Z/16\mathbb Z\), where vertices are adjacent precisely when their circular distance is \(1\), \(4\), or \(8\). Let \(K=\operatorname{Ind}(G)\) be its simplicial independence complex. The vertex permutations \(r(j)=j+1\) and \(s(j)=-j\) generate a natural cyclic subgroup \(C_{16}=\langle r\rangle\) and dihedral group \(D_{16}=\langle r,s\rangle\) of order \(32\), acting simplicially on \(K\).

**Theorem.** The cyclic action on \(|K|\) is free. The two orbit spaces have integral homology

\[
H_i(|K|/C_{16};\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&i=0,3,\\
\mathbb Z/16,&i=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\qquad
H_i(|K|/D_{16};\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&i=0,3,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

Moreover, their fundamental groups and the homotopy type of the dihedral quotient are

\[
\pi_1(|K|/C_{16})\cong C_{16},\qquad
\pi_1(|K|/D_{16})=1,\qquad
|K|/D_{16}\simeq S^3.
\]

The cyclic quotient is thus an integral *homology lens space* in the limited sense that its homology groups equal those of a three-dimensional lens space. No homeomorphism to a lens space, manifold classification, or lens-space linking form is asserted.

## Assumptions and scope

The spaces above are geometric realizations of finite abstract simplicial complexes, divided by the indicated finite simplicial group actions. Quotients are ordinary topological orbit spaces, **not** Borel/homotopy quotients. \(D_{16}\) denotes the dihedral group of order \(32\) (symmetries of a sixteen-gon). The statement is specific to the listed canonical circulant, not an assertion about all well-covered circulants.

## Proof

**1. Sphere before quotient.** Direct enumeration of independent subsets gives \(16,80,144,80\) nonempty faces in dimensions \(0,1,2,3\), and no larger face. The attached `verify_quotients.py` contains an explicit sequence of the \(80\) facets (quadruples of vertices), verifies that it exhausts all facets without duplication, and checks the shelling condition for each successive facet: its intersection with the previous union is nonempty and is a pure union of boundary triangles. The same certificate thereby verifies purity and shellability without relying only on the earlier published shelling claim. A pure shellable three-complex is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of three-spheres. The reduced Euler characteristic is \(16-80+144-80-1=-1\), hence exactly one sphere occurs: \(|K|\simeq S^3\). In particular \(|K|\) is simply connected.

**2. The cyclic action is free.** If a nonidentity rotation \(r^k\) fixes a point of a simplicial complex, it preserves a nonempty simplex setwise (the support of the point). A rotation orbit in \(\mathbb Z/16\mathbb Z\) has size a divisor of \(16\), and any orbit contained in an independent set must have size at most \(4\). A two-element orbit is an antipodal pair, with circular step \(8\); a four-element orbit has step \(4\). Both contain an edge of \(G\), so neither can be independent. A singleton orbit is impossible for a nonidentity rotation. Therefore no nontrivial rotation fixes any point; the quotient map is a regular \(16\)-fold covering. Since \(|K|\) is simply connected, its deck group identifies \(\pi_1(|K|/C_{16})\) with \(C_{16}\).

**3. Fundamental group of the dihedral quotient.** The reflection \(s_0(j)=-j\) fixes vertex \(0\). The reflection \(s_1(j)=1-j\) interchanges vertices \(2\) and \(15\); because their circular distance is \(3\), they span a simplex of \(K\), and its midpoint is fixed by \(s_1\). Since \(s_0\) and \(s_1\) generate the entire dihedral group, its normal subgroup generated by elements having fixed points is all of \(D_{16}\). Armstrong's orbit-space fundamental-group theorem [2], applied to the simply connected finite simplicial realization (after subdivision where needed), gives \(\pi_1(|K|/D_{16})=1\).

**4. Exact quotient homology.** The barycentric subdivision \(\operatorname{sd}K\) has vertices indexed by nonempty faces of \(K\) and simplices indexed by strictly increasing flags of faces. The dihedral action preserves face cardinality; consequently a group element that preserves a flag simplex fixes each of its barycentric vertices. Therefore there are no simplex inversions, and group-orbits of oriented flags form valid cellular bases of the ordinary orbit spaces. The code constructs every flag directly, identifies its orbit under the specified action, and builds the signed cellular boundary by deleting successive entries of a flag, retaining multiplicities when distinct boundaries have the same orbit.

For the cyclic quotient, the resulting cellular dimensions are \((20,134,234,120)\). The three boundary matrices have nonzero Smith factors, respectively,

\[
\partial_1:\ 1^{[19]},\qquad
\partial_2:\ 1^{[114]},16^{[1]},\qquad
\partial_3:\ 1^{[119]}.
\]

For the dihedral quotient, the cellular dimensions are \((16,73,117,60)\), with nonzero Smith factors

\[
\partial_1:\ 1^{[15]},\qquad
\partial_2:\ 1^{[58]},\qquad
\partial_3:\ 1^{[59]}.
\]

The verifier checks both boundary composites are zero. These Smith factors and the cellular ranks give exactly the displayed integral homology groups: \(\mathbb Z/16\) survives in the cyclic quotient's first homology and neither quotient has second homology. In the dihedral case, the simply connected CW complex has the integral homology of \(S^3\); the Hurewicz theorem yields a map \(S^3\to |K|/D_{16}\) inducing all homology isomorphisms. The simply connected homology form of Whitehead's theorem then makes it a homotopy equivalence.

## Verification

Run `python verify_quotients.py` with Python 3 and SymPy installed. The program reconstructs \(320,2144,3744,1920\) barycentric faces, validates the eighty-facet shelling certificate, checks rotation freeness and two reflection-fixed witnesses, tests both boundary-square-zero identities, and calculates the six exact integral Smith factor multisets from matrices it builds itself. The companion `verification_output.txt` records the expected actual output, ending in `VERIFY_OK`. Integer normal forms here certify integral torsion, not merely ranks over finite fields.

## Relationship to prior work

Earl, Vander Meulen and Van Tuyl [1, Theorem 6.1(iii), Section 6] singled out precisely this graph as a smallest well-covered circulant with shellable but non-vertex-decomposable independence complex, and established a shelling. **That foundational shellability statement is not claimed as new.** Their inspected article studies combinatorial regularity and does not give the two specified orbit-space fundamental groups, the Smith forms of their integral boundary complexes, or the dihedral quotient's homotopy type. Armstrong's classical orbit theorem [2] supplies the fundamental-group principle, not the graph-specific fixed-point verification or integral quotient homology calculation.

The point of the theorem is the contrast for the same distinguished simplicial complex: a free cyclic action produces exact order-sixteen first-homology torsion, while adjoining reflections yields a simply connected homotopy three-sphere. The result does not follow from shellability or the unquotiented face vector alone.

## Limitations

The cyclic quotient's classification beyond its fundamental group and integral homology is open here. In particular, no three-manifold structure, lens-space homeomorphism or linking form is established. The equivariant comparison is for the explicitly displayed cyclic and dihedral subgroups only. The exact matrix certificates are computational and should be independently reviewed; the standalone script is sufficient to reconstruct all claimed finite matrices, with no dependency on a data file other than the Python standard library and SymPy. Publication-priority checks cannot eliminate unindexed equivalent computations.
