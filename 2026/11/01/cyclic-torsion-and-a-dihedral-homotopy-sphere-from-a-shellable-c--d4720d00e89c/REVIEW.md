# Review of the cyclic and dihedral orbit-space theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is confined to the natural actions on the independence complex of the explicitly defined sixteen-vertex circulant. An independent, fully included shelling certificate reconstructs its \(80\) facets and proves \(|K|\simeq S^3\). The rotation-freeness argument checks all possible rotation-orbit sizes without relying on sampling. Two explicit fixed-point witnesses generate the entire dihedral group; Armstrong's theorem therefore determines the ordinary orbit-space fundamental group. Barycentric quotient-chain matrices are constructed directly without inversions, with both boundary composites verified zero. Their exact Smith forms give \(H_1=\mathbb Z/16\) for the cyclic quotient and no positive homology below degree three for the dihedral quotient. Since the latter is simply connected and has the homology of a three-sphere, Hurewicz and Whitehead imply the asserted homotopy equivalence. The finite computation is exhaustive and reproducible; it is not an extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Earl–Vander Meulen–Van Tuyl (arXiv:1505.02837v1, 2015) treat exactly \(C_{16}(1,4,8)\) in their Section 6 and prove shellability/non-vertex-decomposability. The complete material inspected contains no cyclic/dihedral quotient homotopy calculation or orbit-space integral homology Smith form. Armstrong (1965) gives only the general orbit-space fundamental-group principle, which does not determine this example's integral quotient homology. Additional comparisons included equivalent formulations as orbit homology, graph-independence equivariance, lens-space homology, and general simplicial group quotients. Available published mathematical research records nearest in meaning concern different complexes (such as the Möbius–Kantor independence complex) or general representation questions, with no directly covering assertion. A search cannot establish universal absence: non-indexed theses, unpublished notes and hidden graph isomorphisms remain residual risks. The old source's shelling is correctly credited rather than marketed as a new result.

## Value — PASS

This is a natural topology problem forced by symmetries of a canonical literature example, not a fabricated parameter slice. The identical shellable flag complex produces sharply different integral and homotopy orbit-space behavior: a cyclic fundamental group with genuine order-sixteen homology torsion versus a simply connected sphere homotopy quotient. This concretely illustrates how fixed-point strata introduced by reflections can erase torsion without destroying top-dimensional homology. The phenomenon is invisible to the known unquotiented shellability result and invites comparison with orbit spaces of other shellable-but-non-vertex-decomposable complexes.

## Limitations and closest sources

The cyclic quotient is not proved to be homeomorphic to any lens space, and no manifold/quotient linking-form claim is made. The exact Smith certificates are finite and require independent verification before broader reliance. The strongest same-object predecessor is Earl–Vander Meulen–Van Tuyl, Theorem 6.1 (arXiv:1505.02837v1); the general fundamental-group mechanism is due to Armstrong, *On the fundamental group of an orbit space* (1965).
