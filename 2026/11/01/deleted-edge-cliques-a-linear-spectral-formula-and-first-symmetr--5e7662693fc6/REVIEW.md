# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The finite graph-ball alternatives are stated with the correct vertex counts and denominators. The complete-graph comparison uses a positive maximizing profile with at least two low coordinates; its nonadjacent low coordinates strictly improve their neighborhood averages after one edge deletion. This proves the extremizer exclusion needed for the all-size four-by-four compression. The decomposition into constant-on-groups, singleton-group contrasts and whole-group contrasts is orthogonal and yields the stated exact norm, not merely a bound. The five-vertex cubic, active row selections, simple top eigenvalue and three extremizing rays follow from exact polynomial algebra and a 72-selection integer positive-definiteness certificate. Finite checks do not substitute for the analytic all-size proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The original finite-graph maximal framework and complete-graph pointwise minimum are from Soria–Tradacete (arXiv:1410.6204). González-Riquelme–Madrid (arXiv:2005.03146) explicitly solve the centered strong Hilbert norms for complete graphs and stars, not the one-edge-deleted clique family; their sequel (arXiv:2011.02630) addresses star exponents and variation. The same graph family appears elsewhere in a different negative-type invariant, which does not imply Hilbert maximal norms. Searches for deleted-edge complete graphs, almost-complete graphs and equivalent spectral cubics found no covering result. These finite and literature searches cannot establish absolute priority; unindexed equivalent computations are a residual risk.

## Value — PASS

Near-complete graphs provide a natural test of how a single missing edge changes a nonlinear averaging norm. The result proves strict change for every order, reduces an exponentially large family of ball selections to linearly many low-dimensional spectral problems, and detects the first nonuniform universal-clique equality profile on five vertices. These conclusions have structural content independently of the particular cubic constant.

## Scope and limitations

Only centered, unweighted, counting-measure strong Hilbert norms are asserted. The all-size exact formula need not identify a closed-form winning count; the extremizer classification is complete only in the specifically stated five-vertex case. Independent scientific validation has not been performed.
