# Dicyclic fundamental group of the four-colour pentagon quotient

## Finding

Let \(X=\operatorname{Hom}(C_5,K_4)\) be the ordinary Lovász graph-multimorphism complex. Let the order-ten dihedral group \(D_5\) act by rotations and reflections of the five input positions, leaving the four colours fixed. Then the action is free, the ordinary quotient \(Y=|X|/D_5\) is a connected closed orientable three-manifold with universal covering space \(S^3\), and
\[
\boxed{\pi_1(Y)\cong\operatorname{Dic}_5\cong C_5\rtimes C_4,}
\]
where the generator of \(C_4\) inverts \(C_5\). Equivalently, \(\operatorname{Dic}_5\) has order twenty and presentation
\[
\langle a,b\mid a^{10}=1,\ b^2=a^5,\ bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\rangle.
\]
Its complete integral singular homology is
\[
\boxed{H_i(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z,&i=0,3,\\
\mathbb Z/4,&i=1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}}
\]
These assertions concern the genuine orbit space rather than the homotopy quotient.

## Assumptions and scope

Index the pentagon vertices by \(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z\) and the colours by \(\{0,1,2,3\}\). An open cell of \(X\) is determined by a tuple \(A=(A_0,A_1,A_2,A_3,A_4)\), where each \(A_i\) is a nonempty subset of the colours and \(A_i\cap A_{i+1}=\varnothing\) (indices modulo five). The corresponding closed cell is \(\prod_{i=0}^4\Delta(A_i)\), a product of simplices. A rotation sends index \(i\) to \(i+1\), and a reflection sends \(i\) to \(-i\). The source complex is given its standard finite regular CW topology; the quotient has the quotient topology. The dihedral group has order ten (not twenty).

## Proof

**1. The dihedral action is free.** Every nonidentity rotation acts transitively on the five positions; a rotation-invariant cell would have all five colour sets equal, contradicting adjacent disjointness. Every reflection of an odd pentagon interchanges the endpoints of some edge. A reflection-invariant cell would therefore assign equal nonempty, disjoint colour sets to adjacent positions, also impossible. A fixed point of a cellular automorphism must lie in an open cell preserved by that automorphism. Thus no nonidentity element fixes a point, and \(X\to Y\) is a regular ten-sheeted covering.

**2. The covering manifold and a lifted reflection.** Csorba and Lutz proved \(|X|\cong V_{3,2}\cong SO(3)\cong\mathbb{RP}^3\) (Theorem 13 of [1]). Dover and Özaydın established that for any *one chosen pentagon reflection* this homeomorphism can be chosen equivariant, with the reflection on \(V_{3,2}\) acting by \((x,y)\mapsto(y,x)\) (Main Theorem of [3]). Under the frame identification \((x,y)\mapsto[x\ y\ x\times y]\) with \(SO(3)\), this is right multiplication by
\[
U=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}\in SO(3).
\]
This is a rotation through angle \(\pi\). In the double cover \(SU(2)\to SO(3)\), each lift of a nonidentity half-turn has square equal to the nontrivial central deck involution. Consequently **every** lift of the chosen reflection to the universal cover \(S^3\to X\) has order four and square equal to its nontrivial deck transformation. This conclusion uses only single-reflection equivariance, not a full-dihedral equivariant identification.

**3. The full quotient fundamental group.** Put \(E=\pi_1(Y)\). Since \(X\to Y\) is a regular covering and \(\pi_1(X)\cong C_2\), the universal covering action gives an exact sequence
\[
1\longrightarrow\langle z\rangle\cong C_2\longrightarrow E
\longrightarrow D_5\longrightarrow1.
\]
The kernel is central, since it is of order two. The preimage of the rotational subgroup \(C_5\) has order ten and contains an element \(\rho\) of order five mapping to a rotational generator. Choose a lift \(\tau\) of the selected reflection. Step 2 shows \(\tau^2=z\), so \(\tau\) has order four. Conjugation by \(\tau\) inverts the unique order-five subgroup, giving \(\tau\rho\tau^{-1}=\rho^{-1}\). These relations generate all twenty elements, hence
\[
E=\langle\rho,\tau\mid \rho^5=1,\ \tau^4=1,\
\tau\rho\tau^{-1}=\rho^{-1}\rangle\cong C_5\rtimes C_4.
\]
Taking \(a=\rho\tau^2\) and \(b=\tau\) gives the standard dicyclic presentation above. The universal covering space of \(Y\) is the universal cover of \(X\), namely \(S^3\).

**4. Orientability and integral homology.** The reflection corresponds to a right translation on the connected Lie group \(SO(3)\), so it preserves orientation. Every other reflection is conjugate to it, and rotations are products of reflections. Thus every element of \(D_5\) preserves orientation, making \(Y\) a connected closed orientable three-manifold. Abelianizing the presentation of \(E\), the equation \(\tau\rho\tau^{-1}=\rho^{-1}\) gives \(\rho^2=1\). Together with \(\rho^5=1\) this forces \(\rho=1\) in the abelianization, leaving \(C_4\) generated by \(\tau\). Thus \(H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)=C_4\). Poincaré duality and the universal coefficient theorem give \(H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong H^1(Y;\mathbb Z)=0\), while \(H_0\) and \(H_3\) are \(\mathbb Z\). All higher homology vanishes.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone Python program `verify_pentagon_four_color_quotient.py` provides a second, integral cellular derivation of the homology. It enumerates every product-of-simplices cell, checks all ten-position symmetries and the absence of cell stabilizers, verifies the Koszul orientation signs against cellular differentials, constructs integer quotient boundary matrices, checks both boundary-square identities, and computes exact Smith invariant factors. The original cell counts are \((240,780,840,300)\), and the quotient counts are \((24,78,84,30)\), in degrees zero through three. The nonzero Smith factors of the three quotient boundary matrices are \(1^{\times23}\), \(1^{\times54},4\), and \(1^{\times29}\), respectively, giving integral homology \((\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z/4,0,\mathbb Z)\). The verifier was executed successfully with SymPy 1.14.0; exact output is embedded in `verification_output.txt`. The group-theoretic proof is independent of the enumerated matrices.

## Relationship to prior work

Kozlov [4] computed the cohomology of the unquotiented cycle-colouring complexes. Csorba and Lutz [1] already proved that \(\operatorname{Hom}(C_5,K_4)\) is \(\mathbb{RP}^3\), and Schultz [2] gave a general Stiefel-manifold homeomorphism. Neither underlying-space assertion is claimed here. Dover and Özaydın [3] proved equivariance under **one** pentagon reflection and explicitly posed the full \(D_5\)-equivariance problem in Question 5.6. The present calculation extracts the order-twenty covering group from that one-reflection information together with the full dihedral cellular action; it does not claim to resolve Question 5.6. The resulting dicyclic fundamental group and complete integral homology of the full orbit space were not found in the inspected sources. That absence is not proof of publication priority.

## Limitations

The calculation is specific to four colours and the five-cycle. It does not establish a full-dihedral equivariant homeomorphism with the standard Stiefel action, determine a unique metric realization of the quotient as a spherical space form, classify other colour counts, or include the action of permutations of the four colours. The historical coverage search cannot exclude unindexed equivalent work. Independent mathematical review has not been performed.
