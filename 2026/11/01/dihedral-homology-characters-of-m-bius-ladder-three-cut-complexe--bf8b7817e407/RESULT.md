# Dihedral homology characters of Möbius-ladder three-cut complexes

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge5\), let \(G_n=M_{2n}\) be the graph on \(V=\mathbb Z/(2n)\) with cycle edges \(\{i,i+1\}\) and antipodal edges \(\{i,i+n\}\). Let \(\Delta_n=\Delta_3(G_n)\) denote the **ordinary three-cut complex**: its facets are the complements of three-vertex subsets whose induced graph is disconnected. Its distinguished dihedral action is generated by \(r(i)=i+1\) and \(s(i)=-i\).

**Theorem.** The integral homotopy type and the complete rational dihedral character of the only nonzero reduced homology are
\[
\Delta_n\simeq\bigvee^{2n^2-8n+1}S^{2n-4},\qquad
\chi_n(g)=\operatorname{tr}\!\left(g\mid\widetilde H_{2n-4}(\Delta_n;\mathbb Q)\right),
\]
with \(\chi_n(1)=2n^2-8n+1\), and, for \(1\le a<2n\),
\[
\chi_n(r^a)=(-1)^a.
\]
For \(0\le a<2n\),
\[
\chi_n(r^a s)=
\begin{cases}
 n-3,&n\text{ even},\ a\text{ even},\\
 3-n,&n\text{ even},\ a\text{ odd},\\
 4-n,&n\text{ odd},\ a\text{ even},\\
 n-2,&n\text{ odd},\ a\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular the full-dihedral invariant subspace has dimension
\[
\dim_{\mathbb Q}\widetilde H_{2n-4}(\Delta_n;\mathbb Q)^{\langle r,s\rangle}
=\left\lfloor\frac{n-3}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]
The group \(\langle r,s\rangle\) has order \(4n\), and the character determines the complete rational representation (finite-group semisimplicity).

## Assumptions and scope
All graph edges are simple and undirected; \(n\ge5\) excludes exceptional additional short cycles. A triple is *disconnected* when its induced graph is not connected; the ordinary three-cut complex is distinct from the total three-cut complex defined by independent triples. All geometric complexes use the standard augmented simplicial-chain convention. The representation is for the explicitly specified dihedral action, not necessarily an exceptional larger automorphism group.

## Proof
**1. The Alexander dual.** Write \(N=2n\), and let \(K=\Delta_n^\vee\) be the combinatorial Alexander dual on \(V\). A face of \(K\) is precisely a vertex set all of whose three-subsets induce connected graphs. Every pair is a face. The graph \(G_n\) is triangle-free and cubic for \(n\ge5\), so its connected triples are exactly induced paths of length two, of which there are \(N\binom32=6n\). Every four-face of \(K\) must induce a square: counting each edge in its three-vertex subsets forces at least four graph edges, and a triangle-free four-vertex graph has at most four. Conversely every square qualifies. The squares are exactly
\[
Q_i=\{i,i+1,i+n,i+n+1\}\quad (i\in\mathbb Z/n),
\]
so there are \(n\) four-faces. There cannot be any five-face: if all its four-subsets were squares, counting graph edges across these five four-subsets would give \(5\cdot4=3e\), which is impossible. Consequently the numbers of nonempty faces of \(K\) in cardinalities one through four are \(2n,\binom{2n}{2},6n,n\), with no larger faces.

**2. Exact integral boundary and homotopy.** Orient the simplices consistently. Among the \(6n\) triangle faces of \(K\), exactly \(2n\) are three consecutive vertices on the long cycle; the other \(4n\) are the four triples in each square. For every three-consecutive-vertex path \(\{i-1,i,i+1\}\), the boundary contains the distance-two chord \(\{i-1,i+1\}\), which appears in no other triangle (since \(n\ge5\)). This supplies an integral diagonal unit block of size \(2n\) in \(\partial_2\). For each \(Q_i\), write its cycle vertices in order \((A,B,C,D)=(i,i+1,i+n+1,i+n)\). On its triangle columns \(ABC,BCD,CDA\), select the edge rows \(AC,BD,AB\). Their signed \(3\)-by-\(3\) boundary matrix has determinant \(\pm1\). These rows occur in no *other square’s mixed-path triangles*; a selected cycle-edge row may occur in a cycle-path triangle, but the selected distance-two-chord rows are zero on all mixed-path triangle columns. Ordering the cycle-path columns and chord rows first therefore gives a block-lower-triangular matrix with unit diagonal blocks, hence a signed **unimodular maximal minor** of size \(5n\). In particular, \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_2\ge5n\) and its image is primitive in the integral edge-chain lattice.

Each square-tetrahedron boundary is a two-cycle; the \(n\) such cycles are linearly independent and generate a primitive sublattice because their triangle supports are pairwise disjoint. Thus \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_2\le6n-n=5n\). The primitive rank-\(n\) lattice of square boundaries is all of \(\ker\partial_2\) (both are primitive sublattices of the triangle-chain lattice), so \(\operatorname{im}\partial_3=\ker\partial_2\) and \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)=H_3(K;\mathbb Z)=0\). As the one-skeleton is complete, \(H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\) is free of rank
\[
\binom{2n}{2}-(2n-1)-5n=2n^2-8n+1.
\]
Combinatorial Alexander duality transfers this cohomology to \(\widetilde H_{2n-4}(\Delta_n;\mathbb Z)\), with no other reduced homology. Since \(K\) has no five-face, \(\Delta_n\) contains the entire \((2n-6)\)-skeleton of the simplex. Therefore it is \((2n-7)\)-connected, and homology vanishing in the next two degrees upgrades this to \((2n-5)\)-connectivity by Hurewicz. Since \(\Delta_n\) has dimension \(2n-4\), Hurewicz and Whitehead give the asserted integral wedge homotopy type.

**3. The equivariant character.** On augmented rational chains of \(K\), the chain-level Euler character identity and the preceding homology concentration give
\[
\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H^1(K;\mathbb Q))
=1-c_0(g)+c_1(g)-c_2(g)+c_3(g),
\]
where \(c_j(g)\) denotes the signed oriented trace on \(j\)-simplices. Equivariant combinatorial Alexander duality contributes the sign of the permutation of all \(2n\) vertices:
\[
\chi_n(g)=\operatorname{sgn}_V(g)\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H^1(K;\mathbb Q)).
\]
For every nonidentity rotation, \(c_0=c_2=0\). Except for the antipodal half-turn, \(c_1=c_3=0\); for the half-turn they are \(-n\) and \(+n\), respectively. Thus the degree-one cohomology trace is \(1\) for every nonidentity rotation. Since the rotation of a \(2n\)-cycle is odd, its ambient-vertex sign is \((-1)^a\).

For a reflection \(r^a s\), if \(a\) is even there are two fixed graph vertices and \(n-1\) swapped vertex pairs, yielding \(c_0=2\), \(c_1=2-n\), \(c_2=-2\). If \(a\) is odd, there are no fixed vertices and \(n\) swapped pairs, giving \(c_0=c_2=0\), \(c_1=-n\). Square \(Q_i\) is mapped to \(Q_{a-i-1}\), and a setwise-fixed square has positive orientation sign. Hence \(c_3\) is the number of solutions to \(2i\equiv a-1\pmod n\): it is \(1\) for odd \(n\), \(0\) for even \(n\) and even \(a\), and \(2\) for even \(n\) and odd \(a\). Multiplying by the vertex-permutation sign \((-1)^{n-1}\) (even \(a\)) or \((-1)^n\) (odd \(a\)) gives the four displayed reflection values. Summing the character over the \(4n\) group elements proves the invariant-rank formula.

## Relationship to prior work
Agarwal and Basak, arXiv:2608.30601v1 (public 2026-08-31), revised v2 (2026-09-14), study independence and perfect-matching complexes of Möbius/circular ladders; their Section 6 expressly raises cut complexes as a future direction, but their main theorems do not calculate ordinary three-cut homology or its symmetry character. The previously published circular-ladder ordinary three-cut result (published-finding corpus identifier `2026/10/11/SCOPE-sphere-wedge-topology-of-three-cut-complexes-of-circular-ladders--4fc5f7521449`) establishes a closely related nonequivariant rank and wedge classification for the **untwisted** circular ladder, not the present dihedral action on the twisted Möbius ladder. Its Alexander-dual argument motivates the integral boundary strategy here; that related rank formula is not claimed as independent novelty. The graph-wide edge-disjoint-square theorem (published-finding corpus `2026/10/11/SCOPE-three-cut-complexes-of-triangle-free-graphs-with-edge-disjoint-s--8ca830284420`) does not apply because every spoke edge of a Möbius ladder is in two squares. Existing dihedral results for Möbius **independence** or **perfect-matching** complexes concern other complexes and cannot determine the stated three-cut homology character. The 2025 study arXiv:2512.04486 treats related cut-complex calculations for other graph families. The original contribution emphasized here is the all-width **dihedral character, including reflection-axis distinctions and invariant ranks**.

## Limitations
No claim is made for \(n<5\), the total three-cut complex, integral group-ring decompositions, or symmetry outside the specified dihedral action. The homotopy rank shares a pattern with the previously treated untwisted ladder and is not asserted to be a new general graph-wide principle. Searches cannot rule out uncatalogued equivalent equivariant calculations, and no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
