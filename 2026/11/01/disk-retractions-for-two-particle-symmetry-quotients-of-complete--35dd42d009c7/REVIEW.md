## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Barnett and Farber's *Topology of Configuration Space of Two Particles on a Graph, I* (arXiv:0903.2180, first public 2009-03-12) analyzes ordered two-particle graph configurations and particle interchange, including the surface case of \(K_{3,3}\), but not the orbit by the full graph-automorphism group or its disk model. Prue and Scrimshaw's *Abrams's Stable Equivalence for Graph Braid Groups* (arXiv:0909.5511) supplies subdivision criteria for ordinary graph-configuration models, not automorphism quotient classifications. Maciążek and Sawicki's *Non-abelian Quantum Statistics on Graphs* (DOI:10.1007/s00220-019-03583-5) addresses graph configuration homology and braid statistics rather than the claimed full orbit-space PL disk models. The corresponding publicly indexed calculations for complete bipartite graph homology/representations and for related graph quotients have distinct statements; rational acyclicity does not imply contractibility, let alone an explicit disk deformation retract.

Targeted searches using terms for unordered collision-free configurations, full automorphism orbit spaces, deleted products, complete bipartite graphs, equivariant subdivisions, and disk retracts did not identify a statement implying this theorem. Missing or unindexed treatments, especially theses or notes on symmetry quotients, remain a genuine priority risk. This is a best-knowledge comparison rather than proof of absolute novelty.

## Value — PASS

The family \(K_{m,n}\) is a standard laboratory for graph braid groups and configuration topology. Its unquotiented two-particle spaces exhibit nontrivial homology and can vary with both parameters, whereas the full symmetry quotient uniformly has a disk model. The two explicit PL models show how the extra part-exchange symmetry changes the finite triangulation, and their certified collapse sequences are constructive input for studying graph-symmetry orbit spaces. A full homotopy classification of a natural infinite family is mathematically more informative than a small-case numerical invariant or a rational-Betti-number computation.
