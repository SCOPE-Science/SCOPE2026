# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

An invariant simplex of the ordinary graph discrete Morse complex consists of whole arrow orbits. Such an orbit is admissible precisely for two distinct permutation cycles with target length dividing source length, with a number of possible phases equal to the target length. The projection-and-lifting argument proves the equivalence of original and quotient directed acyclicity. A minimum cycle length dividing all others supplies a complete source after reversal, so the published Kozlov–Jojić shellability theorem applies; the published positive-integer expansion theorem preserves shellability when the phase choices are restored. The directed matrix–forest identity yields the exact face polynomial, and pure shellability turns the reduced Euler characteristic into the claimed integral sphere-wedge homotopy type. Fourteen independently enumerated finite cases corroborate the orbit model and reduced mod-two homology, without substituting for the unrestricted proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks

The closest published prior result is *Equivariant matrix–forest character formula for graph Morse complexes*, which already establishes the complete orbit-divisibility quotient and face-character determinant. This review does **not** claim those ingredients as new. The prior statement determines alternating homological traces, not shellability or geometric fixed-space homotopy types. The present theorem supplies the latter for the natural divisor-rooted class, including incomparable nonminimal cycle lengths. The complete-source shellability theorem, the expansion theorem, and the directed matrix–forest theorem were checked as known inputs; each alone lacks the identification and final complete-graph fixed-space homotopy statement. Existing involution-only calculations are a proper special case. This is a literature-bounded novelty assessment: an unindexed combination or implicit corollary of known statements is the principal originality risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The classification determines a geometric fixed-point homotopy invariant of an infinite, natural family of permutation actions, with an exact integral sphere multiplicity and no unproved torsion hypotheses. It distinguishes non-chain divisor patterns, yields explicit topology for symmetric actions of arbitrarily high order, and explains the precise arithmetic condition enabling a shellability argument. These homotopy conclusions are not furnished by a face-character or Euler-character formula alone; they answer a motivated fixed-point question rather than tabulating arbitrary numerical examples. The result does not extend to arbitrary non-divisor-rooted cycle types, nor determine the complete equivariant homotopy type.
