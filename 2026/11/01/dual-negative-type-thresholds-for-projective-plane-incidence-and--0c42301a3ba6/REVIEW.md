# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The same stated theorem is checked in every component. The projective-plane incidence identity yields exactly three types of eigenvalues of the powered-distance quadratic form on the total zero-sum subspace. All multiplicities add to \(2v-1\). The incident-design part-contrast eigenvalue is always negative, while the nonincidence complement changes obstruction from a singular sector at \(q=2\) to a part-contrast vector at all \(q\ge3\). For the latter, an explicit root in \((0,1/q)\) is proved to exist uniquely and to precede the other obstruction. The endpoint Hilbert dimensions follow from centered-Gram rank. The asymptotic estimates follow from small-exponent expansions with proved root localization; the finite checker is only corroboration.

De Winter–Lazebnik–Verstraëte's 2008 published abstract treats a distinct extremal cycles question; its full text was not checked for the present comparison and no whole-paper exclusion is claimed. The indexed distance-regular graph table lists both Heawood and its distance-three graph but no negative-type threshold. Search records on projective-plane Rips complexes and design-neighborhood topology likewise address different invariants. Residual risk: a classical association-scheme transform or older generalized-roundness source may already contain the exact specialization; no originality claim is an independent certification.

## Value — PASS

The invariant is an intrinsic and natural sharp Schoenberg exponent, not an arbitrary finite statistic. Passing from incidence to nonincidence on the *same* projective-plane vertex set switches the obstruction type precisely between orders two and three, changes the endpoint nullity from \(v-1\) to one, and separates asymptotic exponents by order \(q^{3/2}\). The theorem applies to every existing plane, independently of the existence of coordinates. This supplies a direct quantitative description of kernel positivity and squared-Hilbert embeddability for two classical design metrics.
