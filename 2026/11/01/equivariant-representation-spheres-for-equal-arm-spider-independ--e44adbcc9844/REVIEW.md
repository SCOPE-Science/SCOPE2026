The open-neighborhood fold is a contiguous retraction: whenever a face contains the source vertex, it can also contain the target. An independent reconstruction applies this from the tips, simultaneously sending depth L−2 to depth L on every arm for L≥3. The requisite containment of neighborhoods follows because the tip has just the neighbor at depth L−1; all replacements are compatible across arms. For L≥4 the remaining graph is the spider with length L−3 plus m disjoint two-vertex paths, so on independence realizations the recurrence is an equivariant join with S(V_m). For L=3 the center is isolated after the fold, so the complex is an invariant cone. For L=2, the link of the center is the simplex on the tips: replace the center by its invariant barycenter linearly on the cone, fixing the center-deletion complex, which is the join of the m two-point path complexes. For L=1 the center and the leaf simplex give a trivial two-point model. Iterating and using the weight-square-root homeomorphism from a join of unit spheres to the unit sphere of their direct sum verifies all three formulas and their equivariance. Actual H-fixed points have constant barycentric coordinate on each vertex orbit. Such an orbit is independent (all vertices at one depth on distinct arms), and two allowed orbits are incompatible exactly when their corresponding vertices are adjacent in the smaller spider. Summing orbit coordinates and dividing by orbit size on the inverse are affine on the relevant cells and give the asserted PL homeomorphism. The fixed subspace of V_m has one coordinate per arm orbit and its determinant on a permutation is the sign, which gives all dimensions and orientation characters, including trivial S^0 at L=1.
