# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The ball formula is exact on a complete bipartite graph because centered radii give singleton, a vertex together with the opposite class, and all vertices. The upper bound (1) is obtained by a sum-of-squares bound and Cauchy–Schwarz; the displayed constants are verified with the explicit large-class threshold. Compactness supplies a maximizing modulus. Its output energy forces \(\ell^2\)-mass concentration on the smaller class and then proximity to the constant smaller-class vector. This strictly fixes all maximizing selectors. The resulting two-by-two block has trace \(\tau_{m,n}\), determinant \((m+1)^{-2}\), and unique positive leading eigenvector; the claimed expansion is obtained by the quadratic formula. Finite checker experiments corroborate these identities but are not used as infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Soria–Tradacete (arXiv:1410.6204, sections 2–3) derive general finite-graph bounds, primarily strong estimates for \(0<p\le1\) and stars. González-Riquelme–Madrid (arXiv:2005.03146, introduction and section 3) give exact \(\ell^2\) results for complete and star graphs, not the unbalanced complete-bipartite family. Previous exact \(K_{2,3}\) and \(K_{3,3}\) calculations and the balanced-class lower bound overlap the candidate selector, but do not imply eventual all-input optimality for each fixed \(m\) as \(n\to\infty\). Searches of published mathematical claims did not identify equivalent coverage, but unindexed or differently expressed work remains a priority risk. No claim of independent review is made.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The problem of evaluating the strong norm of a nonlinear maximal operator is a natural norm problem, not a discretization of an arbitrary parameter slice. The theorem supplies an exact infinitely continuing high-imbalance regime, proves that all optimizing functions become rigid two-level functions, and determines the constant and first inverse-size correction beyond leading growth. The explicit threshold is conservative rather than a claim of optimal finite cutoff; this restricts, but does not erase, the structural result.

## Limits and closest literature
The theorem is not an all-size complete-bipartite classification and does not cover unequal masses or uncentered operators. The closest statements are the 2014 Soria–Tradacete finite-graph estimates, the 2020 González-Riquelme–Madrid complete/star formulas, and earlier finite complete-bipartite calculations; no independent audit has been performed.
