# Research assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The set of allowed radius-\(R\) disk centers is exactly the intersection of two explicit disks. Their intersection-circle endpoints determine two shorter \(R\)-spindle arcs; a support-disk argument exposes the surviving arc of the original disk. Green's formula gives the global area identity, and nested center sets prove strict monotonicity. Symbolic Taylor coefficients, series inversion, and the terminal asymptotic were reconstructed and replayed from `verify.py`. The radius and illumination domains, boundary endpoints, and singular \(\(r=R\)\) exclusion are explicit. Numerical circle intersections independently corroborate the geometric picture but are not a proof.

**Originality — PASS under the inspected literature, with priority risk.** Oliva–Werner–Yalikun (arXiv:2607.13714, online full text) establish the \(R\)-ball illumination construction and first-order surface-area limit; neither their results nor their disk and lens examples supply the present exact global \(r<R\) disk area profile, its order-\(4/3\) coefficient, or its terminal saturation law. The first coefficient is credited. Schütt–Werner–Yalikun (arXiv:2504.15488) treat inward floating bodies; Werner–Yalikun (arXiv:2512.19836) treat relative surface area and weighted inward floating. Closely retrieved published mathematical records on local relative-affine-area Hessians and hyperbolic disk illumination concern different operators or curvatures, not implied instances of this claim. The older literature on planar spindles was checked at the level of accessible definitions/searches, not exhaustively; unindexed equivalent formulas remain possible.

**Value — PASS.** The disk is the canonical calibration object for the recently defined outward ball-convex illumination operator. The exact full-range formula resolves its inverse-radius map, and two independently sharp endpoint exponents explain how the process both starts and saturates. The explicitly changing sign of the first new correction is a natural curvature-ratio transition, not a numerical fit to an arbitrarily chosen family.

**Limits.** Planar Euclidean disk only, strict \(\(0<r<R\)\), fixed-parameter asymptotics, residual priority uncertainty; no claim of a higher-dimensional or uniform degenerating-radius result.
