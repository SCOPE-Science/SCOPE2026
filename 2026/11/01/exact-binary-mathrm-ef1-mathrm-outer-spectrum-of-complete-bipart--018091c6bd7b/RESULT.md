# Exact binary \(\mathrm{EF1}_{\mathrm{outer}}\) spectrum of complete bipartite graphs

## Finding
Let \(a\ge b\ge1\) and \(1\le k\le a+b\) be integers, and let the items be the vertices of the complete bipartite graph \(K_{a,b}\). The \(k\) agents have **one identical additive valuation**, assigning each vertex value zero or one. An allocation is a partition into \(k\) nonempty connected induced vertex sets. It satisfies \(\mathrm{EF1}_{\mathrm{outer}}\) when every comparison can be made envy-free by removing at most one vertex of the compared bundle without disconnecting that bundle (an empty remainder is permitted).

**Theorem.** Every common binary valuation profile on \(K_{a,b}\) admits such a connected \(\mathrm{EF1}_{\mathrm{outer}}\) allocation if and only if
\[
\boxed{k\le b\quad\text{or}\quad k\ge a.}
\]
When \(b<k<a\), the valuation that assigns one to *every* vertex obstructs the guarantee. For the two positive ranges the proof gives a constructive polynomial-time algorithm. Consequently the exact spectrum in the agent count has a contiguous initial yes interval, a (possibly empty) no interval, and a final yes interval; for \(a\le b+1\), all admissible entries are yes.

## Assumptions and scope
All parameters are integers in the ranges above; each vertex is an indivisible good. Commonness means all \(k\) agents agree on each zero/one item value. In this model a subset of at least two vertices is connected precisely when it meets both bipartition sides. A vertex is removable as an *outer* vertex if its removal leaves a connected induced subgraph, including the empty subgraph for a singleton. No statement is made for heterogeneous or nonbinary valuations. The number of agents is at most the number of vertices so every bundle is required to be nonempty.

Write \(A,B\) for the two bipartition sides, with \(|A|=a\), \(|B|=b\); \(u\) and \(v\) are the numbers of one-valued vertices in \(A\) and \(B\). Put \(W=u+v\).

## Proof
We first note an elementary sufficient condition. Suppose all bundle values are either \(q\) or \(q+1\), and every bundle of value \(q+1\) contains an *outer one-valued vertex*. Then the allocation is \(\mathrm{EF1}_{\mathrm{outer}}\): comparisons against bundles of value \(q\) involve no envy (a connected bundle has some removable outer vertex), while deleting the specified vertex from any higher-valued bundle lowers its value to \(q\). This argument also covers \(q=0\).

**Case 1: \(k\le b\).** Both sides contain at least \(k\) vertices. Number the bundles \(0,\ldots,k-1\). In order, send the \(u\) one-valued vertices of \(A\), then the \(v\) one-valued vertices of \(B\), cyclically to bundles numbered by the item's global position modulo \(k\). Therefore each bundle receives either \(q=\lfloor W/k\rfloor\) or \(q+1\) one-valued vertices. Further, for either side containing \(s\) one-valued vertices, these vertices meet exactly \(\min(s,k)\) distinct bundles: they occupy consecutive positions in a cyclic order. Hence at most \(\max(0,k-s)\) bundles lack a one-valued vertex of that side. Since the side has at least \(k\) vertices in total, its zero-valued vertices suffice to supply **one vertex of that side to each such bundle**. After performing this operation on both sides, all bundles are connected. If the remainder \(r=W\bmod k\) is positive, place every as-yet-unallocated zero-valued vertex in bundle \(r\), whose value is \(q\); if \(r=0\), any bundle receives all the remaining zeros. Thus no higher-valued bundle receives a surplus zero connector.

When \(r=0\) all bundle values are equal, which proves the condition. Otherwise a higher-valued bundle contains at least one one-valued vertex, and has only one vertex on a side if that side initially had no one-valued vertex. If it has a one-valued vertex on each side, then either it is a two-vertex edge (both vertices removable), or one side contains at least two one-valued vertices and removing one of them preserves mixed-side connectivity. If it has one-valued vertices only on one side, the other side has exactly one zero-valued connector. If at least two valued vertices are on the first side, remove one and retain a mixed connected remainder; if exactly one, the bundle is a two-vertex edge and removal leaves a singleton. Thus every higher-valued bundle contains an outer one-valued vertex and the sufficient condition applies. This is an explicit algorithm that takes linear time given the two labeled sides and their valuations.

**Case 2: \(k\ge a\).** Put \(p=a+b-k\). Then \(0\le p\le b\). Partition the graph into exactly \(p\) disjoint cross-side edges (two-vertex bundles) and \(k-p\) singleton bundles. We can choose the matched edges so that all bundle values lie in an interval of length at most one.

If \(W\le k\), put \(z_A=a-u\), \(z_B=b-v\); the number of zeros is \(z_A+z_B=a+b-W\ge p\). Consider the bipartite graph obtained from \(K_{a,b}\) by forbidding edges whose *both* endpoints are valued one. It has a matching of size at least \(p\). Indeed its maximum matching cardinality is
\[
\min\{b,z_A+z_B\};
\]
this follows directly by separating vertices into the four value-side classes, or by the bipartite vertex-cover theorem: all vertices of \(B\) form a cover of size \(b\), the zero vertices on both sides form a cover of size \(z_A+z_B\), and examining the two complete rectangular subgraphs \(A_0\!\times B\), \(A_1\!\times B_0\) shows that no smaller vertex cover exists (using \(a\ge b\)). Choose any \(p\) edges of this matching. No two-vertex bundle has value two; all bundles have value zero or one, and a valued edge or singleton has a removable valued vertex.

If \(W>k\), let \(z=z_A+z_B=a+b-W\), so \(z<p\). Each zero-valued \(A\)-vertex can be matched to a distinct one-valued \(B\)-vertex, because \(z_A\le z\le p-1\le b-1\) implies \(z_A\le b-z_B=v\). Similarly each zero-valued \(B\)-vertex can be paired to a distinct one-valued \(A\)-vertex, since \(z_B\le a-z_A=u\). After these \(z\) zero–one edges have been chosen, each side has at least \(p-z\) unused one-valued vertices: these counts are \(a-z\ge p-z\) and \(b-z\ge p-z\). Add \(p-z\) one–one edges. Every remaining vertex is valued one and is a singleton. Thus all bundles have value one or two, and valued two-vertex bundles permit removal of either endpoint. This concludes Case 2.

**Case 3: \(b<k<a\).** Set the common value of *every* vertex to one. Each nonsingleton connected bundle must contain at least one \(B\)-vertex, so there are at most \(b<k\) nonsingleton bundles; at least one bundle is a singleton and has value one. In an \(\mathrm{EF1}_{\mathrm{outer}}\) allocation, every other bundle must have at most two vertices, since removing one vertex from a larger bundle still leaves value at least two, exceeding the singleton's value. Yet \(a>k\) forces some bundle to contain at least two \(A\)-vertices. To be connected, this bundle also needs a \(B\)-vertex and therefore has at least three vertices, a contradiction. This proves necessity and sharpness. \(\square\)

## Verification
The standalone Python 3 standard-library script `verify.py` implements the constructive algorithms above, checks connectivity and removability, and independently searches feasible bundles by exact integer counts of valued and zero-valued vertices in each side. It checked **3,362** complete small parameter/value profiles using the independent feasibility oracle, **5,451** positive-regime profiles using the explicit construction, and **55** negative-regime parameter cells through the all-ones obstruction, over \(1\le b\le\min(a,5)\), \(1\le a\le8\), \(1\le k\le a+b\). The exact oracle is applied for \(a\le7\), \(b\le4\), and each pair of counts \((u,v)\). Run `python3 verify.py` from the extracted artifact directory. Finite computations test implementations only; the proof handles every permissible finite parameter value.

## Relationship to prior work
Bei, Igarashi, Lu and Suksompong (arXiv:1908.05433, Proposition 4.8) proved ordinary connected EF1 existence on \(K_{a,b}\) when both sides have at least as many vertices as there are agents, for arbitrary agent valuations. Their statement is **ordinary EF1**, which does not impose the outer-removal restriction, and it does not give the high-agent segment \(k\ge a\) as an exact biclique spectrum. Chen and Zwicker (arXiv:2402.05884) defined and studied such spectra and obtained high-agent guarantees for arbitrary graphs, including \(k\ge |V|-1\) and a broad \(k=|V|-2\) theorem. These cover subranges here but not the entire high-agent segment for arbitrary bicliques. Bousquet, Connor, Totschnig and Zeitoun (arXiv:2608.29902v1, first public August 30, 2026) study \(\mathrm{EF1}_{\mathrm{outer}}\) universal guarantees and binary valuations, obtaining a spectrum result on trees and a separate family of nontraceable graphs; their stated results do not supply the biclique characterization above.

## Limitations
All agents must share the same binary additive valuation. The theorem does not address heterogeneous valuations, arbitrary additive weights, or common monotone nonadditive utilities. The negative portion follows already for all-one values; the positive constructions exploit binary parity and matching. Prior ordinary-EF1 existence results and general graph results entail special positive subranges; no independent priority or correctness validation has been performed. An unindexed equivalent classification or an unstated implication in other literature remains possible.
