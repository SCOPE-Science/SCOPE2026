# Scientific review: pentagonal-wheel maximal norms

## Correctness
PASS. The six-vertex metric and all admissible graph balls are specified. Independent row selection linearizes the nonlinear positive maximal operator exactly, and positivity of each averaging matrix justifies replacing the positive-cone maximization by an ordinary singular norm. Exact fraction-free integer Sylvester tests examine every legal selection (486 centered and 21,875 uncentered), leaving respectively one and five exceptional matrices. Positive eigenvectors, cubic root isolation, and strict ball-selection inequalities establish attainment and exhaust equality. The executable certificate has been run successfully using the embedded standalone Python source. The finite exhaustive computation proves the finite-graph bounds; it is not extrapolated to other graphs.

## Originality
PASS, with a residual unindexed-literature risk. Soria–Tradacete (arXiv:1410.6204) study the finite-graph centered maximal operator and its general sharp inequalities, but their strong-type discussion treats the star rather than the present wheel. González-Riquelme–Madrid (arXiv:2005.03146) study sharp Hilbert norms on complete graphs and stars, not the uncentered six-vertex wheel. Searches by wheel aliases, the cubic, graph-ball definitions and equality patterns did not locate an equivalent result. Separate published wheel facts about distance matrices or edge projections concern distinct operators and do not imply the maximal-operator norm. Search failure alone does not prove absolute priority.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The six-vertex wheel \(K_1\vee C_5\) is the first wheel with an odd nontriangular rim, a natural small planar graph with a universal hub and a five-cycle of peripheral vertices. The result exhibits a strict, exactly quantified centered–uncentered Hilbert-norm gap, while the equality structure changes from one fully rim-symmetric positive ray to five symmetry-broken positive rays. This comparison supplies a natural benchmark for understanding how mobile averaging centers affect nonlinear strong estimates on graphs; it is not a mere normalization or one additional decimal value.

## Limits and sources
The classification is exact for this graph only. A potentially unindexed equivalent finite-graph calculation remains a priority risk. Sources inspected include the introductory definitions and main norm sections of Soria–Tradacete, arXiv:1410.6204, and both González-Riquelme–Madrid articles, arXiv:2005.03146 and arXiv:2011.02630. The proof relies on the accompanying exact certificate, not on absence of indexed search hits.
