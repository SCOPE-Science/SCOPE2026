# Exact centered Walsh stability for uniform labelled unicyclic graphs

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge4\), let \(\mathcal U_n\) be the set of connected simple graphs on the labelled vertex set \([n]\) having exactly \(n\) edges. Give \(\mathcal U_n\) the uniform probability measure. Write \(E=\binom n2\), let \(I_e(G)=\mathbf1_{\{e\in E(G)\}}\) for every unordered pair \(e\), and let \(w_e(G)=(-1)^{I_e(G)}-\mathbb E[(-1)^{I_e}]\). Set
\[
S_n=\sum_{j=0}^{n-3}\frac{n^j}{j!},\qquad z_n=\frac{n^{n-3}}{(n-3)!S_n},
\]
\[
A_n=\frac{n-3z_n}{(n-1)(n-2)},\qquad
B_n=\frac{2\bigl(n(n^2-6n+7)+3(n-1)z_n\bigr)}{n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)}.
\]
The probability-normalized Gram matrix \(\Gamma_n=(\mathbb E[w_e w_f])_{e,f}\) has the **complete spectrum**
\[
\operatorname{spec}(\Gamma_n)=
\{0^{[1]},\;(4A_n)^{[n-1]},\;(4B_n)^{[n(n-3)/2]}\},
\]
where superscripts in square brackets denote multiplicities. In particular, the only linear dependence among the centered degree-one Walsh characters is \(\sum_e w_e=0\), and the constant function together with all uncentered edge Walsh characters has exactly one linear relation, namely \(\sum_e(-1)^{I_e}=E-2n\).

For \(n=4\), \(A_n=B_n=4/15\), so the nonzero sector is exactly tight. For every \(n\ge5\), \(0<A_n<B_n\). Consequently the sharp squared singular-value condition number on the quotient by that one relation is
\[
\kappa_n^2=\frac{B_n}{A_n},\qquad
1\le \kappa_n^2<2,\qquad \sup_{n\ge4}\kappa_n^2=2,
\]
with equality in the lower bound only at \(n=4\), while the upper supremum is not attained. Moreover,
\[
 z_n\sim\sqrt{\frac2{\pi n}},\qquad
 \kappa_n^2=2-\frac6n+O(n^{-3/2}).
\]
The eigenvalue \(4A_n\) acts on the vertex-star contrast space of dimension \(n-1\); \(4B_n\) acts on the orthogonal cycle/edge-interaction space of dimension \(n(n-3)/2\).

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are undirected, simple, vertex-labelled and connected. A unicyclic graph has a unique cycle of length \(\ell\in\{3,\ldots,n\}\); each remaining vertex lies in a tree attached to a cycle vertex. The Walsh characters are real functions on \(\{0,1\}^{\binom n2}\) restricted to the unicyclic sample support; all inner products are averages over the **uniform** measure on that support. The normalizations are not the counting-measure Gram or the individual unit-norm rescaling of the centered coordinates. The degenerate \(n=3\) singleton support is excluded.

## Proof
**1. Enumeration and exact cycle moment.** Fix \(\ell\) cycle vertices and a cyclic ordering up to reversal. For a specified root set \(R\) of cardinality \(\ell\), the weighted enumerator of spanning forests oriented toward those roots, with \(x_v\) marking each child of \(v\), is
\[
F_R(\mathbf x)=\left(\sum_{r\in R}x_r\right)\left(\sum_{v=1}^n x_v\right)^{n-\ell-1}\quad(\ell<n).
\]
Indeed the directed matrix-tree principal minor for the nonroot vertices is \(X I-\mathbf1(x_v)_{v\notin R}^{\mathsf T}\), where \(X=\sum_v x_v\), and the determinant lemma gives \(X^{n-\ell-1}\sum_{r\in R}x_r\). In particular there are \(\ell n^{n-\ell-1}\) such forests. Including the case \(\ell=n\) (empty attached forest) gives
\[
N_{n,\ell}=\binom n\ell\frac{(\ell-1)!}{2}\ell n^{n-\ell-1}
=\frac{(n-1)!}{2}\frac{n^{n-\ell}}{(n-\ell)!}.
\]
Thus \(|\mathcal U_n|=(n-1)!S_n/2\). Conditional on \(\ell<n\), the child counts can be generated by \(n-\ell-1\) independent uniform vertex selections plus one independent uniform cycle-root selection. Each root contributes base degree \(2\); each nonroot contributes base degree \(1\). Direct factorial-moment evaluation yields
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_v\deg(v)(\deg(v)-1)\mid\ell\right]
=3n+1-\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}n.
\]
The identity also holds at \(\ell=n\), where every degree is \(2\). Put \(j=n-\ell\). From \(\sum jn^j/j!=nS_{n-4}\) and \(\sum j(j-1)n^j/j!=n^2S_{n-5}\) (with empty sums interpreted as zero), one obtains
\[
\mathbb E[\ell(\ell+1)]=n(1+3z_n),\qquad
\mathbb E\sum_v\deg(v)(\deg(v)-1)=3(n-z_n).
\]

**2. Exact two-edge inclusion moments.** Symmetry gives \(p=\mathbb P(e\in G)=2/(n-1)\). For distinct edges sharing one vertex, let the common probability be \(a\). Summing ordered adjacent edge pairs gives
\[
a=\frac{3(n-z_n)}{n(n-1)(n-2)}.
\]
Let \(b\) be the corresponding probability for disjoint edges. The deterministic identity \(\sum_f I_f=n\) gives
\[
(n-1)p=2(n-2)a+\binom{n-2}{2}b,
\]
whence
\[
b=\frac{4\bigl(n(n-4)+3z_n\bigr)}{n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)}.
\]
These formulas apply for every \(n\ge4\), including the smallest instance.

**3. Spectral decomposition.** The covariance matrix \(C=(\mathbb E[(I_e-p)(I_f-p)])\) belongs to the Johnson two-subset algebra:
\[
C=(p-b)I+(a-b)\mathsf A+(b-p^2)J,
\]
where \(\mathsf A\) is the adjacency matrix of the graph joining two unordered pairs exactly when they intersect in one vertex, and \(J\) is all ones. The decomposition of edge-coordinate space is the constants (dimension \(1\)), star contrasts \(\{x_{ij}=u_i+u_j:\sum_i u_i=0\}\) (dimension \(n-1\)), and their common orthogonal complement (dimension \(n(n-3)/2\)). The eigenvalues of \(\mathsf A\) on these sectors are \(2(n-2),n-4,-2\). Therefore the eigenvalues of \(C\) are \(0,p+(n-4)a-(n-3)b,p-2a+b\), which simplify to \(0,A_n,B_n\). Since \(w_e=-2(I_e-p)\), \(\Gamma_n=4C\).

**4. Strict positivity and extremal sectors.** Since \(0<z_n<1\), \(A_n>0\). Direct subtraction gives
\[
a-b=-\frac{n(n-7)+3(n+1)z_n}{n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)},\quad B_n-A_n=-(n-2)(a-b).
\]
At \(n=4\), \(z_4=4/5\), so \(a=b\). At \(n=5\), \(z_5=25/37\); at \(n=6\), \(z_6=36/61\); substitution yields \(a<b\). For \(n\ge7\), the numerator preceding the minus sign is strictly positive. Hence \(B_n>A_n>0\) exactly for \(n\ge5\).

To bound the condition number, the positive first two terms of \(S_n\) in descending order imply \(z_n\le n/(2n-3)\le4/5\). Algebra gives
\[
B_n-2A_n=\frac{2\left[3(n^2-2n-1)z_n-n(3n-7)\right]}{n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)}.
\]
For every \(n\ge4\), \(n(3n-7)/[3(n^2-2n-1)]>4/5\), because \(3n^2-11n+12>0\). Thus \(B_n<2A_n\), establishing the strict universal bound.

**5. Asymptotics.** Reversing the defining sum for \(z_n^{-1}\) shows
\[
 z_n^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-3}\prod_{h=0}^{k-1}\left(1-\frac{3+h}{n}\right).
\]
After setting \(k=t\sqrt n\), the product converges to \(e^{-t^2/2}\). The upper bound by \(e^{-k(k+5)/(2n)}\), together with Riemann-sum convergence, yields \(z_n^{-1}/\sqrt n\to\sqrt{\pi/2}\). Finally the displayed rational formulas give \(B_n/A_n=2-6/n+O(n^{-3/2})\). In particular, the unattained squared-condition-number supremum is \(2\).

## Verification
The standalone script `check_unicyclic.py` exhaustively enumerates every size-\(n\) subset of the \(\binom n2\) possible edges for \(n=4,5,6,7\), retains exactly the connected graphs, and checks the sample counts, every exact one-edge and two-edge inclusion probability, and explicit star and four-edge cycle eigenvector identities using rational arithmetic. Verified counts are \(15\), \(222\), \(3660\), and \(68295\), respectively. The computation is finite corroboration only: the foregoing proof establishes all integer \(n\ge4\).

## Relationship to prior work
Cayley's tree/forest enumeration and the classical two-subset Johnson association algebra provide the underlying counting and symmetry tools. Bell–Burris–Yeats (preprint made public 2005-12-19) discuss recursive tree enumeration and cycle constructions, but do not present the constrained first-Walsh-band Gram classification here. The relevant published research records concern Fourier sampling on transpositions of symmetric groups, graph-supported harmonic bases, or edge-coordinate Fourier problems on different sample spaces; the inspected summaries do not assert a general theorem that implies this probability-normalized unicyclic result. No priority claim is made for the underlying forest determinant or Johnson eigenvalues.

## Limitations
This theorem concerns only the first edge-Walsh band, only the uniform distribution on labelled connected unicyclic graphs, and a condition number after quotienting the unique fixed-edge-count dependence. It does not give higher-degree Walsh spectra, nonuniform unicyclic models, or conditioning when coordinates are separately standardized. A historical equivalent in random-graph covariance/association-scheme literature cannot be excluded by bounded searches. Independent validation has not been performed.
