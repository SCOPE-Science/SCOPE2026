# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The precise probability ensemble is uniformly weighted rooted ordered full binary trees with \(n\) leaves (shape-equivalent to the uniform PDA model). The mixed generating-function recurrence follows by expanding the product of cherry count and total leaf depth at every root split, including the special two-leaf cherry contribution. Its solved form yields four explicit coefficient sequences, exact first/mixed moments and the covariance formula. Comparing consecutive ratios establishes strict negativity for every \(n\ge4\), not merely in an enumerated range. Cherry variance comes from the exact second cherry-marker derivative; the correlation coefficient uses the published uniform-model Sackin variance. The standalone checker reports 868 independent deterministic exact and asymptotic checks. Risk: the external Sackin variance coefficient is cited rather than re-proved, and these finite checks do not replace the general proof.

## Originality — PASS with residual priority risk
The inspected primary literature separates the two statistics: Blum–François–Janson treats Sackin with Colless and gives the necessary asymptotic variance; Chang–Fuchs and Janson treat fringe-pattern counts; Wu–Choi treats cherries with pitchforks. The later Norton framework lists both variables in one formal multivariate series but does not evaluate their mixed coefficient or prove the uniform finite-size sign and sharp correlation constant. Search for an exact cherry–Sackin covariance under aliases and a separate mathematical-record comparison did not identify an implication-dominating theorem. Related prior findings concern different tree models or other random-tree observables. Residual risk: prior combinatorial generating-function work could encode or state an equivalent mixed moment under different terminology, and independent historical priority review remains outstanding.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Cherry count records the most local rooted two-tip topology, whereas Sackin summarizes global total evolutionary depth; both are standard and separately studied phylogenetic shape indices. The full finite-size sign law and the explicit \(n^{-1/2}\) decorrelation constant determine their quantitative nonredundancy under an established null model. The zero/negative boundary, exact covariance, and asymptotic rate constitute a motivated structural result rather than a routine recomputation of known marginal moments.

## Comparison and limitations
The uniform PDA/Catalan law is not the Yule–Harding law; results for cherry/Sackin covariance in Yule trees do not imply the statement here. The correlation is undefined at \(n=2,3\), and the theorem does not claim statistical independence or complete historical priority. Some literature may have an unrecognized equivalent formulation.
