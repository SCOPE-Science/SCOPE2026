# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS

The assertion is exactly a rank assertion for the evaluation of individual spherical-harmonic degrees on a fixed thirty-point antipodal orbit. The addition formula reduces the claim to a fifteen-by-fifteen Legendre Gram matrix. Exact quadratic-field elimination gives all ranks through degree \(14\), with null constant data exactly at \(8\) and \(14\). Nonzero modular determinants modulo \(1009\) settle every integer degree from \(15\) through \(262\), and a standard Legendre Bernstein inequality yields strict diagonal dominance for every degree at least \(263\). These ranges partition all integers \(\ell\ge0\). No finite computation is interpreted as an infinite proof.

## Originality: PASS, subject to bounded source coverage

The 2013 harmonic-index-design paper gives the icosahedral degrees \(8\) and \(14\) as known zero-sum cases, not this thirty-vertex evaluation rank sequence. The 2014 spherical sampling article studies cumulative-bandlimit latitude grids. A later article on hybrid spherical designs uses the icosidodecahedral orbit for cubature and describes an invariant degree-six polynomial, but does not state the rank of its single-degree vertex-evaluation map. Closely related sampling results for the twelve icosahedral vertices and twenty dodecahedral vertices treat other sampling domains and cannot supply the midpoint evaluation ranks simply by reindexing. The especially distinguishing statements here are rank ten at degree \(6\), the rank-fourteen losses at degrees \(8\) and \(14\), and full rank in every degree from \(15\) onward. An unindexed earlier representation-theory specialization remains a residual risk.

## Mathematical value: PASS

This is a natural complete interpolation invariant of the rectified icosahedron, rather than a chosen parameter slice. It distinguishes the parity-forced fifteen-dimensional upper bound from the additional geometric failures, including five missing data directions at degree \(6\), a first full interpolation degree \(7\), isolated later losses, and a provably permanent degree-fifteen threshold. The exact rank sequence determines precisely which single-frequency spherical-harmonic spaces can realize a prescribed antipodally compatible signal on this symmetric orbit, a useful contrast to cumulative-degree cubature and to the two dual Platonic vertex orbits.

## Limits

The proof certifies rank, not the condition number of the nonzero singular spectrum. Older polyhedral harmonic tables have not been exhaustively indexed, and the mathematical result remains without independent validation.
