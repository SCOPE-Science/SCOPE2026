# Review of the exact-degree two-orbit interpolation rank law

## Correctness
The evaluation rank is exactly the rank of a sixteen-by-sixteen Legendre Gram matrix by the addition theorem and antipodal parity. Exact Gaussian elimination over the quartic field generated by the golden ratio and a common dual-face normalization handles degrees zero through forty-four; explicit nonzero modular determinants handle forty-five through 249; and strict diagonal dominance proves positive definiteness from degree 250 onward. `verify.py` independently reconstructs the arithmetic. The proof is infinite in scope and does not extrapolate from finite numerical experiments.

## Originality
The nearest directly examined results are Bannai–Okuda–Tagami (arXiv:1308.5101), concerning harmonic-index designs including the icosahedron alone, and Zhu–Bannai–Bannai–Kim–Yu (arXiv:1507.05373), concerning harmonic-index design bounds and extremizers. The known isolated harmonic cancellations are acknowledged. Neither examined source gives the complete rank law for the common dual icosahedron–dodecahedron union. General addition formulas and icosahedral symmetry alone do not settle the cross-orbit evaluation rank. Bounded literature and database searches do not eliminate the possibility of an earlier specialized character calculation.

## Value
The exact first-surjectivity degree \(17\), the repeated loss of surjectivity, and the last defect at degree \(44\) jointly determine a natural interpolation invariant of a canonical two-orbit polyhedral sampling set. This distinguishes exact-degree interpolation from cumulative band-limited interpolation and describes which parity-compatible data can be fitted by individual eigenspaces.

## Scientific limitations
The rank law applies to unit radial directions rather than the unequal-length polyhedral vertices. Conditioning and robustness are not classified. Independent mathematical validation remains outstanding.
