# Exact finite-volume onset of the discrete Hardy instability in every dimension

## Finding

Let \(d\ge3\) be an integer, \(C_d=(d-2)^2/4\), and \(S_{d,R}=\{x\in\mathbb Z^d:1\le |x|^2\le R\}\) for each integer \(R\ge1\). Define
\[
 \mathcal E_d(u)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z^d}\sum_{j=1}^d|u(x+e_j)-u(x)|^2,\qquad
 \mathcal H_d(u)=\sum_{x\ne0}\frac{|u(x)|^2}{|x|^2},
\]
where \(u\) is finitely supported, zero at the origin and outside \(S_{d,R}\). Let \(R_*(d)\) be the smallest such \(R\) for which some nonzero \(u\) satisfies \(\mathcal E_d(u)<C_d\mathcal H_d(u)\), or \(+\infty\) if no such finite radius exists.

**The exact first-violation radii are**
\[
 R_*(d)=\begin{cases}
 +\infty,&3\le d\le8,\\
 24,&d=9,\\
 3,&d=10,\\
 2,&d=11,\\
 1,&d\ge12.
 \end{cases}
\]
Thus, in the first dimension where the continuum inverse-square coefficient fails on the lattice, the entire punctured Euclidean ball through squared radius \(23\) remains strictly positive, but squared radius \(24\) admits a genuine negative-energy test. This radius is **minimal among every complex-valued lattice function**, not merely among symmetric trials or a chosen Ritz family.

## Assumptions and scope

The nearest-neighbor Laplacian has diagonal \(2d\) and off-diagonal \(-1\) on lattice edges. Each edge is counted once in \(\mathcal E_d\); edges leaving the support, including those leading to the origin, contribute to the diagonal. The finite-volume comparison is Dirichlet, with support exactly constrained by the Euclidean squared radius \(R\). The assertion concerns the fixed coefficient \(C_d\), not the globally optimal discrete Hardy constant. All sums are finite and all finite certificates use exact integers.

## Proof

**1. Full signed-permutation quotient.** For a fixed \(d,R\), every nonzero lattice point in \(S_{d,R}\) has a unique orbit representative \(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_d)\), with \(0\le a_1\le\cdots\le a_d\) and \(1\le\sum a_j^2\le R\). Let \(\mathcal O_{d,R}\) be these representatives, \(r_a=\sum a_j^2\), \(s_a\) the number of lattice points in the signed-permutation orbit, and \(n_{ab}\) the number of neighbors of a representative of orbit \(a\) belonging to orbit \(b\). These integers are computed by the exact enumeration in `verify_certificate.py`. The orbit multiplicity is
\[
 s_a=\frac{2^{\#\{j:a_j>0\}}d!}{\prod_{m\ge0}(\#\{j:a_j=m\})!},
 \qquad s_an_{ab}=s_bn_{ba}.
\]
For any invariant test function taking value \(v_a\) on orbit \(a\), extension by zero gives the exact identity
\[
 4\mathcal E_d(v)-(d-2)^2\mathcal H_d(v)
 =\sum_{a\in\mathcal O_{d,R}}
 \frac{s_a}{r_a}
 \left((8dr_a-(d-2)^2)v_a^2
 -4r_av_a\sum_b n_{ab}v_b\right).
 \tag{1}
\]
This identity follows directly by expanding each nearest-neighbor square. Formula (1) is used for a **negative witness only**; positivity for arbitrary nonsymmetric functions is proved separately.

**2. Exact positive certificate at dimension nine, squared radius 23.** Read the 90 positive integers \(h_a\) in `certificate.json` as values constant on the signed-permutation orbits of \(S_{9,23}\), in the canonical order explicitly generated by the accompanying checker. For each orbit set
\[
 p_a=(72r_a-49)h_a-4r_a\sum_b n_{ab}h_b.
 \tag{2}
\]
The complete exact enumeration proves \(\min_a p_a=100963>0\). No numerical eigenvalue estimate enters this statement. Let \(L_{23}\) be the full Dirichlet lattice Laplacian on \(S_{9,23}\), and \(W\) the diagonal multiplier \(W(x)=|x|^{-2}\). Equation (2) states
\[
 (L_{23}-\tfrac{49}{4}W)h(x)=\frac{p_a}{4r_a}>0
 \qquad (x\in\text{orbit }a).
\]
For every complex function \(f\) on this finite set, the ground-state identity is
\[
 \langle (L_{23}-\tfrac{49}{4}W)f,f\rangle
 =\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset S_{9,23}\atop |x-y|=1}
 h(x)h(y)\left|\frac{f(x)}{h(x)}-\frac{f(y)}{h(y)}\right|^2
 +\sum_{x\in S_{9,23}}\frac{(L_{23}-\tfrac{49}{4}W)h(x)}{h(x)}|f(x)|^2.
 \tag{3}
\]
This is verified by expansion of the two sides and includes the Dirichlet boundary in \(L_{23}\). Both terms are nonnegative and the second is strictly positive for nonzero \(f\). Therefore **every** complex test supported in \(S_{9,23}\) satisfies a strict Hardy comparison. The same holds at all smaller radii by extension by zero.

**3. Exact negative certificate at squared radius 24.** The 98 strictly positive integers \(w_a\) in `certificate.json` specify an orbit-constant function on \(S_{9,24}\). Substitution in (1), multiplying by \(D=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,24)=5354228880\), gives the **exact integer identity**
\[
 D\left(4\mathcal E_9(w)-49\mathcal H_9(w)\right)
 =-3740776448633821897328<0.
 \tag{4}
\]
The checker independently regenerates every orbit, multiplicity and adjacent-orbit count and evaluates (2) and (4) using only Python integers. Equations (2)–(4) prove \(R_*(9)=24\) without reliance on finite-precision spectral experiments.

**4. Other dimensions.** For the squared-radius-two balls, the nonzero punctured lattice graph is connected whenever \(d\ge2\): any two signed coordinate vertices can be linked via a point supported on two coordinates. The symmetrized generalized eigenproblem for the energy over Hardy mass has strictly negative edge off-diagonals and positive Hardy weights. Its smallest eigenvalue is simple with an everywhere-positive eigenvector by the finite irreducible Perron–Frobenius theorem (apply it to a sufficiently large scalar identity minus the weighted Laplacian). Since coordinate sign changes and permutations commute with the weighted problem, uniqueness forces this eigenvector to be orbit-constant. Thus the two-orbit minimum computed below is the **full finite-volume minimum**, not merely a symmetric trial upper bound. At squared radius one every lattice vertex is isolated inside the punctured ball, so the full quotient equals \(2d\).

For \(3\le d\le8\), the established full-lattice Hardy inequality \(\mathcal E_d\ge C_d\mathcal H_d\) gives \(R_*(d)=+\infty\). For \(d=10\), the one- and two-shell Dirichlet comparisons are positive: the first-shell quotient is \(2d\), while the least two-shell quotient is \(3d-\sqrt{d^2+8d-8}\), which at \(d=10\) exceeds \(16\). On the three signed-permutation orbits of the squared-radius-three ball, the orbit values \((17,4,1)\) have \(4\mathcal E_{10}-64\mathcal H_{10}=-960\), as witnessed by an exact scaled defect of \(-5760\) with multiplier \(6\). Hence \(R_*(10)=3\).

For \(d=11\), the first-shell quotient \(22\) exceeds \(81/4\), while the two-orbit function with values \((4,1)\) has scaled negative defect \(-2332\) with multiplier \(2\), proving \(R_*(11)=2\). Finally, the single-coordinate support orbit at squared radius one has quotient \(2d\), strictly below \((d-2)^2/4\) for every integer \(d\ge12\); there is no positive squared radius below one. This completes the classification.

## Relationship to prior work

Lizama's 2026 work proves strict failure of the global continuum coefficient in dimensions nine and ten, using a multiscale Gaussian and a coordinate-support Ritz ladder. The coordinate ladder already exhibits the first three dimensional crossings, but its complete dimension-nine version remains positive, and the Gaussian proof gives only an unspecified sufficiently large finite truncation. The exact **minimum Euclidean support radius** for dimension nine is not implied by those statements. The new positive certificate at radius 23 is essential: an explicit negative example at radius 24 alone cannot prove minimality. Alpay's 2026 paper settles the missing full-lattice dimensions five through eight, providing the infinite-radius portion of this classification. Standard finite-dimensional ground-state transforms and signed-permutation orbit reductions are used, not claimed as new techniques.

## Limitations

The result gives **the first radius of positivity failure**, not the exact global sharp constant for dimensions nine or above, not the minimizing eigenfunction, and not the first violation for other shapes of support such as cubes. The radius convention is squared Euclidean distance; using ordinary radius changes the numbers to their square roots. The finite certificate is exact, but no independent mathematical audit or publication-priority certification has been performed. The literature comparison is best-knowledge and can miss older equivalent results under alternative terminology.
