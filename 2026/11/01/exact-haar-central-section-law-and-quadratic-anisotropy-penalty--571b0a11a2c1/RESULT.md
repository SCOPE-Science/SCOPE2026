# Exact Haar central-section law and quadratic anisotropy penalty for rhombic dodecahedra

## Finding
Let
\[
K=\bigl\{x\in\mathbb R^3:\ |x_i|+|x_j|\le2\text{ for every }i<j\bigr\}
\]
be the regular rhombic dodecahedron. For arbitrary \(s_1,s_2,s_3>0\), put \(K_s=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,s_2,s_3)K\). Let \(U\) be uniform on the unit sphere and let \(N_s\) be the number of sides of the polygon \(K_s\cap U^\perp\). Set, for each \(i\in\{1,2,3\}\),
\[
q_i=\operatorname{arsinh}(s_j/s_i)+\operatorname{arsinh}(s_k/s_i),
\qquad \{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}.
\]
Then the **complete Haar polygon-type law** is
\[
\boxed{\Pr\{N_s=8\}=\frac4\pi\sum_{i=1}^3\arctan(e^{-q_i}),\qquad
\Pr\{N_s=6\}=1-\Pr\{N_s=8\}.}
\]
No other side count has positive probability. The formula is unchanged if all three scale factors are multiplied by the same positive number.

The exact orientation criterion, valid outside measure-zero exceptional directions, is
\[
\boxed{N_s=8\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \max_i(s_i|U_i|)>\sum_{j\ne i_*}s_j|U_j|,}
\]
where \(i_*\) is the maximizing index. Otherwise \(N_s=6\). In the regular case,
\[
\Pr\{N_{(1,1,1)}=8\}=\frac{12}{\pi}\arctan(3-2\sqrt2)
=0.6490406878163556\ldots,
\]
\[
\mathbb E N_{(1,1,1)}=6+\frac{24}{\pi}\arctan(3-2\sqrt2)
=7.298081375632711\ldots .
\]

There is a **strict local rigidity statement for anisotropy**. For any real \(h_1,h_2,h_3\), write \(\bar h=(h_1+h_2+h_3)/3\). Then, as \(\varepsilon\to0\),
\[
\boxed{\Pr\bigl\{N_{(e^{\varepsilon h_1},e^{\varepsilon h_2},e^{\varepsilon h_3})}=8\bigr\}
=\Pr\{N_{(1,1,1)}=8\}
+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{\pi\sqrt2}\sum_{i=1}^3(h_i-\bar h)^2
+O_h(\varepsilon^3).}
\]
Consequently the regular member is a strict local minimizer among positive diagonal stretches, modulo overall scale; no claim of **global** optimality is made. In the anisotropic limit \(t\to\infty\),
\[
\boxed{\Pr\{N_{(t,1,1)}=6\}
=\frac{4(2-\sqrt2)}{\pi t}+O(t^{-2}).}
\]

## Assumptions and scope
All spaces are Euclidean. Scale factors are strictly positive and fixed for the exact law. A random plane means an unoriented two-dimensional linear subspace sampled from the rotation-invariant probability measure. A plane meeting any vertex has probability zero, and the side count is defined almost surely. The quantity is the number of polygon edges of the *intersection*, not the number of sides of an orthogonal projection. The local statement is only for a neighborhood of the equal-scale ray in the three-parameter diagonal deformation family. The asymptotic regime expands one axis while keeping the other two fixed.

## Proof
**1. The edges of the base solid.** From the twelve facet inequalities \(\pm x_i\pm x_j\le2\) for \(i<j\), the polytope has exactly fourteen vertices:
\[
\{\pm2e_1,\pm2e_2,\pm2e_3\}\ \cup\ \{(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3):\sigma_i\in\{-1,1\}\}.
\]
For a fixed ordered pair \(i<j\) and fixed signs, the supporting plane \(\sigma_i x_i+\sigma_j x_j=2\) contains four vertices: the two signed axial vertices \(2\sigma_i e_i,2\sigma_j e_j\) and the two cube vertices whose \(i\)- and \(j\)-signs agree. Their convex hull is a rhombus. The twelve rhombic facets show that the solid's exactly twenty-four edges are the segments joining a cube vertex \(\sigma\) to the three signed axial vertices \(2\sigma_i e_i\), one for each \(i\). A generic central plane meets an edge in a section vertex precisely when the two edge endpoints lie on opposite sides of the plane.

**2. Side-count chamber criterion.** A plane normal \(u\) after the diagonal change of variables has effective coordinates \(m_i=s_i u_i\). Independent sign changes and coordinate permutations, applied to the base polytope, let us assume \(m_1\ge m_2\ge m_3>0\). The three positive axial vertices have positive dot products and the opposite three have negative ones. Each cube vertex \(\sigma\) has dot-product sign equal to the sign of \(m_1\sigma_1+m_2\sigma_2+m_3\sigma_3\). If \(m_1>m_2+m_3\), this sign always equals \(\sigma_1\): across all eight cube vertices, exactly eight cube-to-axial edges cross the plane. If \(m_1<m_2+m_3\), only the signs at \((1,-1,-1)\) and its antipode change relative to that case; each change lowers the number of crossing incident edges from two to one, hence exactly six edges cross. Since the only boundaries are coordinate-zero, ties, and \(m_1=m_2+m_3\), all of measure zero, this gives the claimed almost-sure dichotomy and criterion.

**3. Exact solid angles.** By the coordinatewise sign symmetry of a uniform sphere normal, work in the positive octant and multiply the area by eight. The octagonal event is the disjoint union of the three triangular cones
\[
C_i(s)=\{u\in\mathbb R^3_{\ge0}:s_i u_i>s_j u_j+s_k u_k\}.
\]
For fixed \(i\), the cone is generated by the rays
\[
e_i,\qquad (s_j/s_i)e_i+e_j,\qquad (s_k/s_i)e_i+e_k.
\]
The elementary solid-angle formula for the spherical triangle spanned by three rays \(v_1,v_2,v_3\) is
\[
\Omega=2\arctan\frac{|\det(v_1,v_2,v_3)|}{\|v_1\|\|v_2\|\|v_3\|+(v_1\cdot v_2)\|v_3\|+(v_2\cdot v_3)\|v_1\|+(v_3\cdot v_1)\|v_2\|}.
\]
Here the numerator is one and the denominator factors into
\[
\left(\sqrt{1+(s_j/s_i)^2}+s_j/s_i\right)
\left(\sqrt{1+(s_k/s_i)^2}+s_k/s_i\right)=e^{q_i}.
\]
Thus \(\Omega_i=2\arctan(e^{-q_i})\). The whole sphere has solid angle \(4\pi\); the eight octants contribute \(8\sum_i\Omega_i\), giving
\[
\Pr(N_s=8)=\frac{8}{4\pi}\sum_i\Omega_i=\frac4\pi\sum_i\arctan(e^{-q_i}).
\]
At equal scales, \(q_i=2\operatorname{arsinh}(1)\) and \(e^{-q_i}=(\sqrt2-1)^2\), which gives the stated exact regular-case value. The mean side count follows because \(N_s=6+2\mathbf1_{\{N_s=8\}}\) almost surely.

**4. Exact quadratic stability.** Let \(h\in\mathbb R^3\). Common additions to all components of \(h\) do not change the probabilities, so assume \(\sum_i h_i=0\). Define
\[
Q(x)=\operatorname{arsinh}(e^x),\qquad F(y)=\arctan(e^{-y}),\qquad a=\operatorname{arsinh}1.
\]
The probability is \(4\pi^{-1}\sum_iF(q_i(\varepsilon))\), where
\[
q_i(\varepsilon)=Q(\varepsilon(h_j-h_i))+Q(\varepsilon(h_k-h_i)).
\]
Direct differentiation gives
\[
Q'(0)=1/\sqrt2,\quad Q''(0)=1/(2\sqrt2),
\quad F'(2a)=-1/6,\quad F''(2a)=\sqrt2/9.
\]
Using \(\sum h_i=0\),
\[
q_i'(0)=-3h_i/\sqrt2,\quad
\sum_i[q_i'(0)]^2=\frac92\sum_i h_i^2,\quad
\sum_iq_i''(0)=\frac3{\sqrt2}\sum_i h_i^2.
\]
Consequently
\[
\frac{d^2}{d\varepsilon^2}\Pr(N_{e^{\varepsilon h}}=8)\bigg|_{\varepsilon=0}
=\frac4\pi\left(\frac{\sqrt2}{9}\frac92-\frac16\frac3{\sqrt2}\right)\sum_i h_i^2
=\frac{\sqrt2}{\pi}\sum_i h_i^2.
\]
The first variation is zero by permutation symmetry, and Taylor's theorem produces the claimed second-order coefficient. Analyticity near equality gives a remainder uniformly bounded by a constant times the cubed centered log-stretch norm. The Hessian is positive definite transverse to the common-scale direction, so the regular member is a **strict local minimizer**.

**5. One-axis expansion.** For \(s=(t,1,1)\), the exact formula simplifies to
\[
\Pr(N_s=8)=\frac4\pi\left[F(2\operatorname{arsinh}(t^{-1}))
+2F(\operatorname{arsinh}t+\operatorname{arsinh}1)\right].
\]
As \(t\to\infty\), the first \(F\) term equals \(\pi/4-t^{-1}+O(t^{-2})\); each of the other two equals \((\sqrt2-1)/(2t)+O(t^{-2})\). Substitution yields
\[
\Pr(N_s=8)=1-\frac{4(2-\sqrt2)}{\pi t}+O(t^{-2}),
\]
and hence the hexagon probability has the stated asymptotic.

## Verification
The included standard-library Python program `verify.py` reconstructs all fourteen vertices and twenty-four edges from integer coordinates, compares direct crossing-edge counts to the exact chamber criterion on 150,000 independent Gaussian normal samples across five positive stretch triples, independently compares the sampled octagon frequencies with the exact solid-angle formula, and tests the quadratic coefficient by centered finite differences along three independent logarithmic perturbations. It reports `VERIFY_OK 150006 checks`. These finite diagnostics support the proof but do **not** certify all directions or independently validate the mathematical statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Brandenburg, De Loera, Luo and Meroni (first posted March 26, 2026) establish a general chamber method for computing section volumes and moments of polyhedral norm balls; their displayed applications concern cubes, not a complete Haar section-*type* distribution for a diagonally stretched rhombic dodecahedron. Brandenburg and Meroni (2025) study combinatorial types of cube sections and discuss random central slices, but their stated census is for cubes, not the two-class rhombic-dodecahedral angular law. The exact solid-angle identity is classical and is explicitly credited to van Oosterom and Strackee. Prior radial-distance and average-area results for the regular rhombic dodecahedron concern functions of a single ray's boundary distance, not the number of edges crossed by a random central plane. A search of the available geometric research index using rhombic-dodecahedral central sections, Haar polygon types, anisotropic stretch, and triangle-inequality chamber aliases found no equivalent exact three-parameter probability formula or local logarithmic Hessian. This does not establish exhaustive historical priority.

## Limitations
The theorem is about central sections of the *diagonal affine family* only. It does not establish a global minimizer over arbitrary anisotropies, classify off-center sections, or provide an area/perimeter distribution. Existing general chamber algorithms could reproduce the side-count criterion for this polyhedron, but do not by themselves state the evaluated uniform angular measure or its anisotropic stability coefficient. A historical probability result under crystallographic or stereological terminology may exist outside the inspected sources. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
