The lift-parity characterization follows from the simply connected antipodal double cover. The bounded spherical sign kernel is Hilbert–Schmidt, so its cyclic integral is the trace of its trace-class powers. Funk–Hecke and the Legendre endpoint identities give the exact Catalan eigenvalues and multiplicities. The Hilbert–Schmidt identity certifies convergence and supplies an explicit inequality bounding all nonleading spectral terms. Separate spherical-lune integration yields both short-loop constants. The standalone, separately implemented computations corroborate critical identities and sampling conventions. Numerical tests do not claim to prove the infinite statements.

## Originality — PASS, historical priority provisional
Glorieux–Yarmola (2020, arXiv:2003.08190) determine the probability for random shortest-geodesic **triangles on flat tori**. Their result and its Dirichlet-domain convolution formulation do not imply a law on \(\mathbb{RP}^{2}\). Hubard–Kaluža–de Mesmay–Tancer (2016, arXiv:1602.06778) prove shortest-path embedding existence on the round projective plane, not probability laws for randomly chosen loops. An all-length flat-torus homotopy distribution is likewise not a projective-space statement. The underlying hemisphere sign law and spherical harmonic diagonalization are classical tools, not being presented as new. The comparison found no exact prior statement yielding the **complete projective-plane spectral law plus strict increase and explicit two-eigenvalue error bound**. Remaining risk: a differently formulated spherical sign-cycle or integral-operator result might already imply the theorem.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The model is natural in geometric probability and global geodesic topology: each edge is metric-minimizing, yet a randomly closed polygon may represent the nonzero projective fundamental-group class. The result resolves the whole sequence of loop lengths rather than a single special case. The strict increase, two exact short-loop constants, and signed second eigenmode show how topology loses memory of the number of vertices, including a quantitative oscillation invisible to a first-order limit. This is an exact, motivated classification, not an arbitrary numerical slice. No broader theorem for general Riemannian surfaces or new harmonic-analytic technique is claimed.
