# Mathematical review: orthogonal spherical-lune great-circle sections

## Correctness — PASS

For generic normal \((a,b,c)\), the two true meridian endpoints have dot product \(ab/[\sqrt{a^2+c^2}\sqrt{b^2+c^2}]\); the spherical arc between them lies in the lune. The Haar disintegration \(|a|\sim\operatorname{Uniform}(0,1)\), independent sign and azimuth, yields the exact conditional threshold integral. Its antiderivative, reflection symmetry, derivative and singularity are explicitly reconstructed. A separately generated normal sample, angular clipping, numerical quadrature and exact identities were replayed with the packaged script. No finite numerical enumeration is presented as proof of an infinite law.

## Originality — PASS, historical priority provisional

Moseeva–Tarasov–Zaporozhets (arXiv:2007.07297, first posted 2020-07-14) provide spherical chord methods and an explicit spherical-cap distribution, not the right-lune law. The lune contains antipodal points and is not a spherical convex body generated by a line-free cone under that paper's convention. Besau and collaborators (arXiv:2203.07916, first posted 2022-03-15) describe the same right-angle wedge and its section geometry in Lemma 4.1, but use that description for random-polytope facet asymptotics, without evaluating its Haar section-length CDF. Gille's 2005 wedge and hemisphere article abstracts concern solid Euclidean straight-chord distributions rather than intrinsic spherical arcs. A prior result about squared-distance moments between two independently sampled lune points and another about random-hull perimeter deficits concern different statistics and do not imply the present complete distribution. Searches and primary comparisons are recorded in the accompanying structured scientific review. Literature coverage is necessarily incomplete; Gille full texts were not inspected.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The orthogonal spherical lune is the basic two-hemisphere wedge, and the complete probability law for a random great-circle section is a natural section metric. The logarithmic peak at the median, endpoint density, central-band asymptotic and exact CDF resolve substantially more than its immediate Crofton mean; the formula enables arbitrary quantile and moment computation. The proof is self-contained and does not claim a general lune-angle theorem.

## Limitations

The theorem is for the curvature-one sphere, right-angle lune, Haar-uniform unoriented great circles, and intrinsic arclength. It does not establish a general-angle law, an offset-plane law, or a point-pair distance law. Historical novelty requires subsequent independent comparison.
