# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For the stated symmetric intersection family, the radial function in the fundamental eighth of the plane yields \(|K_t|=4t\arctan(1/t)\). Polarity converts the intersection to the convex hull of two polar ellipses. Green's area formula on its two elliptical arcs and their common supporting segment gives \(|K_t^\circ|=4\arctan(1/t)/t+2(1-t^{-2})\); the supporting points and cross determinant have been independently reconstructed. Differentiating \(16(\phi^2+\phi\cot(2\phi))\) gives a product of positive terms for \(0<\phi<\pi/4\), so strict decrease with \(t\) follows. Taylor expansions and the square limit have explicitly controlled endpoints. An independent support-function polar quadrature and symbolic derivative checker passed 649 tests. These tests are corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The first relevant full text is Hughes–Chraibi (arXiv:1106.3787), whose ellipse-sector algorithm supplies overlap areas but not polar-volume products. Böröczky–Makai–Meyer–Reisner (arXiv:1507.01481) establishes global inequalities and stability, not parameterwise strict ordering. Meyer–Reisner (arXiv:math/0606305) proves a shadow-system reciprocal-polar-volume convexity theorem; its hypothesis and conclusion do not directly supply the claimed exact area-product profile. Searches for perpendicular/orthogonal ellipse intersections, polar convex hulls, Mahler products, and the closed arctangent formula did not identify a matching statement or stronger implication. The absence of an identified equivalent result is a targeted-search conclusion, not a certificate of worldwide priority. Earlier unindexed derivations remain possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is the exact value of a natural affine-invariant geometric functional on a complete smooth-to-polyhedral interpolation produced by intersecting two congruent, perpendicular ellipses. The object is motivated by classical ellipse-overlap geometry, and the invariant is motivated by planar Blaschke–Santaló/Mahler extremal theory. Both primal and polar areas change nontrivially and the polar develops support segments, so the strict product ordering is not an endpoint inequality or a mere reparameterization of a circle/disk formula. The sharp near-disk and near-square coefficients give quantitative benchmarks for anisotropic intersections. It is a specialized family result, not a general Mahler conjecture or arbitrary-intersection theorem.
