# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The finite dyadic Haar vectors form an orthogonal basis on every interval. For binary data, local Parseval forces each subtree sum to be either its observed sum or its complement. At fixed parent sum, a nonzero contrast admits a second child-sum orientation exactly for a balanced parent; complementation bijects the two orientations, establishing the exact product recurrence. Induction proves the fiber count and Hamming-weight upper bound, and explicit separated-pair constructions attain the latter. Equality at the global maximum forces zero upper-level contrasts and one occupied site per bottom pair. An exhaustive exact checker verifies the finite cases through length 16; the all-length claims rely on the induction, not the checker.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

The literature comparison included the full HTML theorem and introduction of Alaifari–Bartolucci–Wellershoff (2023), and the full HTML introduction and theorems of Alaifari–Daubechies–Grohs–Thakur (2016). Those papers address uniqueness from redundant wavelet systems on continuous real signals, not exact multiplicities of nonredundant discrete Haar magnitude fibers on binary signals. Searches also considered bandlimited wavelet recovery, binary phase retrieval, and closely related mathematical-record claims about Fourier, Walsh and cyclic STFT fibers. No equivalent theorem or stronger implication was located. Older combinatorial reconstructions of hierarchical binary data remain a specific priority risk; absence from searches is not a uniqueness proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A complete quantitative ambiguity law for an elementary multiscale transform is directly motivated by quantized wavelet reconstruction: it predicts the entire fiber size from a linear-time dyadic statistic, proves all nonconstant binary vectors are ambiguous, gives a sharp bound at every fixed Hamming weight, and classifies the unique maximally ambiguous measurement. The exact weight-dependent exponent is not supplied by general failure-of-phase-retrieval statements.

## Limitations

Known binary/two-symbol alphabet, full labelled Haar magnitudes, signed total, and exact measurements are essential. Extensions to other transforms, unknown labels, missing coefficients or measurement noise are unproved. The result has no independent validation.
