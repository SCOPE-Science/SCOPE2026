# Exact polygon-type law for Haar central sections of the regular truncated octahedron

## Finding

Let \(T\subset\mathbb R^3\) be the regular truncated octahedron whose vertices are the 24 distinct signed coordinate permutations of \((0,1,2)\). For a plane \(H_u=\{x:u\cdot x=0\}\) with normal \(u\) chosen uniformly from the sphere, let \(N(u)\) count the sides of the polygon \(T\cap H_u\).

**The exact polygon-type law** is supported on three values:

\[
\Pr\{N=6\}=\frac{12}{\pi}[\Omega(A,B,C)+\Omega(B,E,F)+\Omega(B,F,D)]\approx0.4984506899177035,\]
\[
\Pr\{N=8\}=\frac{12}{\pi}[\Omega(C,B,D)+\Omega(G,D,F)]\approx0.31610885558019075,\]
\[
\Pr\{N=10\}=\frac{12}{\pi}\Omega(C,D,G)\approx0.18544045450210572.
\]

Here the seven rays are
\[
A=(1,1,1),\ B=(2,2,1),\ C=(2,1,1),\ D=(4,2,1),\ E=(1,1,0),\ F=(2,1,0),\ G=(1,0,0),
\]
and, for any nonzero triple of vectors spanning an acute spherical triangle, its positive spherical area is
\[
\Omega(x,y,z)=2\arctan\frac{|\det(x,y,z)|}{\|x\|_2\|y\|_2\|z\|_2+(x\cdot y)\|z\|_2+(y\cdot z)\|x\|_2+(z\cdot x)\|y\|_2}.
\]

A stronger pointwise classifier explains this law. If \(a>b>c>0\) are the decreasing absolute coordinates of a generic normal \(u\), then
\[
\boxed{N(u)=6+2\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{a>2c}+\mathbf{1}_{a>2b}-\mathbf{1}_{b>2c}\bigr).}
\]

## Assumptions and scope

The body is the three-dimensional regular Archimedean truncated octahedron with edge length \(\sqrt2\), centered at the origin. The plane always passes through its center; the normal is Haar-uniform on \(S^2\), with \(u\) and \(-u\) defining the same plane. Probabilities concern the planar **intersection**, not the orthogonal projection. The inequalities \(a>b>c>0\), \(a\ne2b\), \(a\ne2c\), and \(b\ne2c\) exclude only a finite union of great circles, hence no probability mass. No assertion about the number of sides at these exceptional orientations is needed.

## Proof

**(1) Exact polyhedron and edge data.** The body is the intersection of the fourteen supporting halfspaces
\[
|x_i|\le 2\quad(1\le i\le3),\qquad \varepsilon_1x_1+\varepsilon_2x_2+\varepsilon_3x_3\le3\quad(\varepsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}).
\]
Their 24 vertices are exactly the signed permutations of \((0,1,2)\). Two vertices are joined by an edge if and only if they lie simultaneously on two supporting planes with linearly independent normals: enumerating these exact integer equalities gives 36 edges, three at each vertex. Equivalently, in these coordinates the edges are the 36 unordered vertex pairs at squared Euclidean distance \(2\); the two descriptions agree exactly. Every such edge has just its two endpoints as vertices of the polyhedron.

For a generic central plane, each polygon vertex arises at exactly one edge of \(T\) with endpoints on opposite sides of the plane, and each such edge gives a distinct polygon vertex. Therefore the number of sides equals
\[
N(u)=\sum_{\{v,w\}\in E(T)}\mathbf{1}_{(u\cdot v)(u\cdot w)<0}.
\]
This is an exact finite combinatorial formula, not a sampling approximation.

**(2) Exhaustive chamber classification.** Signed coordinate permutations act transitively on 48 equal-area spherical chambers. Within the positive ordered fundamental chamber \(a>b>c>0\), the signs of all \(u\cdot v\) can change only on \(a=2b\), \(a=2c\), or \(b=2c\). The strict regions cut out by these three planes are precisely the following five convex cones; their labels, side counts, and spherical polygon boundaries are:

| Region | Defining inequalities in the ordered chamber | Sides | Boundary rays |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| \(R_0\) | \(a<2c\) | 6 | \(A,B,C\) |
| \(R_1\) | \(2c<a<2b\), \(b<2c\) | 8 | \(C,B,D\) |
| \(R_2\) | \(a>2b\), \(b<2c\) | 10 | \(C,D,G\) |
| \(R_3\) | \(b>2c\), \(a<2b\) | 6 | \(B,E,F,D\) |
| \(R_4\) | \(b>2c\), \(a>2b\) | 8 | \(G,D,F\) |

The list is exhaustive because \(a>2b\) and \(b>2c\) each force \(a>2c\). Each region is connected, and all endpoint signs are constant in it. Hence evaluating the preceding edge-crossing sum once in each region is rigorous for **every** generic direction. Exact representatives \((13,11,8),(18,12,8),(30,12,8),(18,11,4),(30,11,4)\), respectively, give the side counts in the table by direct integer comparisons over all 36 edges. The embedded, dependency-free checker reconstructs the edges directly from independent active facets and reproduces these exact comparisons. This proves the stated indicator identity.

**(3) Exact Haar probability.** The ordered positive normal chamber has spherical area \(4\pi/48=\pi/12\). The cone of \(R_0\) is the spherical triangle \(ABC\); \(R_1\) is \(CBD\); \(R_2\) is \(CDG\); \(R_4\) is \(GDF\). The spherical quadrilateral \(R_3=BEFD\) is divided along \(BF\) into \(BEF\) and \(BFD\). These five spherical regions tile the ordered positive chamber with disjoint interiors. For a spherical triangle generated by three rays in a common hemisphere, the determinant-and-dot-product formula for \(\Omega\) above is the standard exact solid-angle identity; in the six triangles here its denominator is positive. Summing their areas by side count and dividing by \(\pi/12\) gives exactly the displayed three probabilities. Their sum is one because the five regions tile the fundamental chamber. This completes both the pointwise and distributional claims.

## Relationship to prior work

Cesaroni and Novaga (first publicly posted March 28, 2026) study the regular truncated octahedron as the BCC Voronoi cell in the isoperimetric problem for parallelohedra; their metric and surface-area results do not derive random central-section polygon-type probabilities. Their subsequent paper proves the global isoperimetric minimization over parallelohedra. Standard polyhedron descriptions supply the 24 vertices, 36 edges, and regular faces, but not the spherical probabilities. Closest related random-slicing literature gives face-vector laws for other polytopes, including four-cubes and regular icosahedra; these do not imply the present normal-chamber areas. **Central section** is essential: polygonal silhouettes of orthogonal projections are not the same invariant.

## Limitations

This theorem concerns one fixed, regularly scaled three-dimensional truncated octahedron and Haar-uniform planes through its center. It does not give the distribution for noncentral planes, perturbed parallelohedra, planar shadows, or arbitrary orientations with vertices on the cutting plane. Search of accessible sources does not establish historical uniqueness; an equivalent older computation under zonotope or foam-cell terminology remains possible. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
