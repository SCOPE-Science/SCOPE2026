# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The factorization of the saddle energy gives all three separatrix contours with no missing bounded lobe. Exact quadratures give the two-to-one action ratio. The positive \(b_0\)-derivative of a well-defined local homoclinic splitting function proves existence and uniqueness of the smooth persistence curve by the implicit-function theorem. The independent symbolic script reproduces the integral ratios and the Hopf separation. Smooth dependence of hyperbolic stable/unstable manifolds, and reversibility of the central connections, provide the nonalgebraic parts of the proof.

**Originality — PASS on inspected evidence.** Cao–Sadri–Tang–Wang (arXiv:2610.26699v1) establishes the qualitative homoclinic surface \(\mathrm{HL}_4\) in its five-equilibrium regime, and its Section 6.13 gives the qualitative bifurcation strips. It does not report the area partition, the equal-depth specialization's first derivative \(-19a^2/21\), or the leading Hopf interval width. The source's other Melnikov slope pertains to a different equilibrium configuration. Siewe et al. (Physica A 357, 2005) analyze the same degree of potential with even-position Van der Pol damping plus forcing, rather than the reversible odd-position damping here. Related published mathematical results and the searched scientific records did not identify those coefficients; a negative search does not establish historical priority.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The source leaves the quantitative separation of the Hopf and homoclinic thresholds implicit; the exact \(2/21\) window gives a sharp asymptotic measure of the parameter region carrying the unstable outer-well limit cycle. The conserved-action partition simultaneously quantifies the geometry of the three coexisting separatrix regions at the symmetric equal-depth reference state. The selection of the equal-depth potential is canonical within sextic symmetric three-well potentials, rather than an arbitrary numerical parameter slice.

**Limits.** No global continuation formula, higher-order coefficient, or independent validation is claimed. The classical Melnikov method and the existence/uniqueness of the qualitative five-equilibrium homoclinic curve are credited to prior mathematics.
