# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The exact perpendicular-bisector segment parameterization recovers all reachable nonvertex distances for each of the four sides. Its sole special endpoint is a polygon vertex exactly when the projected adjacent-vector parameter reaches the opposite vertex. The shared interval and exact shear feasibility condition follow from the intersection of the two distinct edge-type intervals. The perimeter inequality is an explicit algebraic consequence of the long-side maximal strut distance, and the two sharpness families realize equality at nonvertices. The near-square rates follow from the nonnegative terms of the inequality and explicit rectangle and rhombus perturbations. Independent rational-intersection checks corroborated all key formulas; finite computations are not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, historically provisional

The source-defined strut notion and universal bound are due to Nikonorov--Nikonorova (arXiv:2209.05940, full PDF inspected); that work does not give the exact parallelogram shared-radius interval, shear threshold, or sharp two-mode stability. Nikitenko--Nikonorov (arXiv:2301.03218, complete HTML scientific material inspected) study a different minimax self-Chebyshev radius. They identify the numerical square factor \(8/\sqrt5\) in that context, so the constant as a number is not claimed new. Their quadrilateral theorem has a magic-kite optimizer, not the full parallelogram strut classification. Targeted published-finding corpus and related literature searches did not retrieve the compound theorem, but unindexed equivalent analysis remains possible. No priority certification is asserted.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The natural four-sided centrally symmetric specialization of the established \(\Delta(\ell)\) polygon problem admits a complete phase classification with a geometric shear threshold, including a subtle excluded endpoint caused by the nonvertex requirement. The sharp defect inequality quantifies two independent shape departures (length imbalance versus shear), provides optimal separate constants and the exact rigidity rate near squares, and distinguishes it structurally from the previously known general polygon bound.

## Limits and overlap risk

The square constant appears in prior self-Chebyshev work, but under a different invariant, and prior polynomial-geometry techniques could conceivably give an unindexed equivalent. No claim is made about all quadrilaterals or non-Euclidean metrics. The checker verifies exact rational test configurations and is not an exhaustive classification of all real configurations. The universal proof is analytic.
