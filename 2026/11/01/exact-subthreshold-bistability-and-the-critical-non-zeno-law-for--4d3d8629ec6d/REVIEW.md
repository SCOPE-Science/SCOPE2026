# Mathematical review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The exact free-flight solution defines one and only one positive return for each launched kick. Its speed return map is increasing, the explicit integral equation is necessary and sufficient for genuine periodic flights, and the unique sign-change argument for the integral derivative proves the complete orbit census. The rest/cycle basins follow from the sign of the speed increment, and a local cubic-in-flight-period calculation yields the sharp boundary \(n^2v_n/(g\tau)\to128/3\), \(nT_n/\tau\to16\), and a finite-constant logarithmic clock. The included finite numerical checks passed but do not replace these global arguments.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to the identified historical-priority risk.** The full accessible main text of Saldi and Morin's arXiv:2608.02926v1 introduces this reset oscillator but states a long-flight approximation and a phase-locking theory, not the exact subgravity saddle-node and Zeno threshold. Luck and Mehta's 1993 paper was inspected for its impact and chattering mathematics; its sinusoidally moving platform produces a different clock-dependent return map. Naylor, Sánchez and Swift's air-damping paper has a different driven-table formulation in its accessible abstract. Comparisons of the actual propositions, not just titles, and published record searches found no stronger statement covering the exact theorem. Supplementary material and some specialized full texts were not accessible; priority is not independently certified.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The case \(A/g<1\) is natural because it distinguishes spontaneous liftoff from persistence after an external kick; a sharp wedge supports both a stable resting contact and a stable bouncing cycle with a repelling separatrix. The critical restitution exactly \(1/2\) yields a quadratic cusp and a non-Zeno impact clock with coefficient \(16\tau\), offering a testable structural boundary between two qualitatively distinct reset regimes. The theorem is not a general claim about all impact oscillators or finite-height electrode cells.

## Closest literature and limitations

The model is taken from Saldi and Morin (2026, arXiv:2608.02926v1). The closest general precedent for impact chatter is Luck and Mehta (1993), which instead has an oscillating lower wall. Other air-damped examples do not automatically reduce to exponential force recharging. The theorem assumes a stationary ground, exponential drive renewal, velocity-independent \(0<\eta<1\), and no upper-wall impacts. The contact continuation after finite-time Zeno is an explicit modeling convention; no independent scientific verification or global literature priority proof has been performed.
