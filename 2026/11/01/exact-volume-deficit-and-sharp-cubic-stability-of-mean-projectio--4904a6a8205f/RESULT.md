# Exact volume deficit and sharp cubic stability of mean-projection bodies of rectangular boxes

## Finding
Let \(Q(a,b,c)=[-a,a]\times[-b,b]\times[-c,c]\subset\mathbb R^3\), where \(a,b,c>0\), and let \(\mathcal M_2\) be the probability-normalized two-plane projection transform defined below. Put \(s=(abc)^{1/3}\) and \(Q_\square=Q(s,s,s)\). Then the following **exact normalized-volume deficit** holds:

\[
\boxed{\frac{\operatorname{Vol}_3(\mathcal M_2Q(a,b,c))-\operatorname{Vol}_3(\mathcal M_2Q_\square)}{\operatorname{Vol}_3(Q(a,b,c))^2}
=\frac{16}{\pi^2}\left[\frac{(a-b)^2}{ab}+\frac{(b-c)^2}{bc}+\frac{(c-a)^2}{ca}\right].}
\]

Thus, among all three-dimensional Euclidean rectangular boxes of a fixed positive volume, the cube is the **unique minimizer** of the three-dimensional volume of its mean-projection body. The identity is a global, sharp, aspect-ratio stability formula, not merely a local expansion. If \(x_1=\log a\), \(x_2=\log b\), and \(x_3=\log c\), then

\[
\frac{\operatorname{Vol}_3(\mathcal M_2Q)-\operatorname{Vol}_3(\mathcal M_2Q_\square)}{\operatorname{Vol}_3(Q)^2}
\ge \frac{16}{\pi^2}\sum_{i<j}(x_i-x_j)^2
=\frac{48}{\pi^2}\sum_{i=1}^3(x_i-\bar x)^2,
\qquad \bar x=\tfrac13(x_1+x_2+x_3).
\]

The coefficient \(16/\pi^2\) in the first logarithmic stability inequality is optimal for the displayed sum of squared log-differences: the ratio of its two sides tends to one along any nontrivial family approaching a cube.

## Assumptions and scope
All volumes are ordinary Euclidean volumes. The three axes are mutually perpendicular, and all half-side lengths \(a,b,c\) are strictly positive. For each unit vector \(u\in S^2\), let \(\nu_u\) denote the invariant **probability** measure on the Grassmannian of two-planes \(E\subset\mathbb R^3\) containing \(u\). The convex body \(\mathcal M_2K\) is specified by its support function

\[
h(\mathcal M_2K,u)=\int_{E\supset\mathbb Ru}\operatorname{Area}_2(K|E)\,d\nu_u(E).
\]

Here \(K|E\) is the orthogonal projection to \(E\). This is the projection-Radon-transform normalization of Brauner, Ortega-Moreno, and Schuster, not an unnormalized affine-plane section integral. The comparison cube \(Q_\square\) has exactly the same volume \(8abc\) as \(Q\). No assertion is made for general parallelepipeds with nonorthogonal edges or for arbitrary convex bodies.

## Proof
**1. The image is a three-disc Minkowski sum.** For a unit normal \(v\) of a two-plane, the ordinary projected area of \(Q\) is

\[
\operatorname{Area}_2(Q|v^\perp)=4\bigl(bc|v_1|+ac|v_2|+ab|v_3|\bigr).
\]

The invariant planes containing \(u\) are indexed by their unoriented normals in the circle \(S^2\cap u^\perp\). Parameterize this circle uniformly. Its average satisfies \(\mathbb E|v_i|=(2/\pi)\sqrt{1-u_i^2}\). Thus

\[
h(\mathcal M_2Q,u)=\frac8\pi\Bigl[bc\sqrt{1-u_1^2}+ac\sqrt{1-u_2^2}+ab\sqrt{1-u_3^2}\Bigr].
\]

Let \(D_i=\{x:\|x\|_2\le1,\ x_i=0\}\) be the unit circular disk in the coordinate plane orthogonal to \(e_i\). Since \(h(D_i,u)=\sqrt{1-u_i^2}\), equality of support functions gives

\[
\mathcal M_2Q=\frac8\pi\bigl(bcD_1+acD_2+abD_3\bigr).
\]

**2. Mixed-volume polynomial.** For arbitrary strictly positive radii \(r_1,r_2,r_3\), Minkowski's volume polynomial for the three (possibly lower-dimensional) convex disks is

\[
\operatorname{Vol}_3(r_1D_1+r_2D_2+r_3D_3)
=2\pi\sum_{i\ne j}r_i^2r_j+C\,r_1r_2r_3,\tag{1}
\]

where the sum is over the six *ordered* distinct index pairs, and \(C=6V(D_1,D_2,D_3)>0\) is a universal constant. The single-disk cubic terms vanish because each disk is two-dimensional. Here is a self-contained computation of the coefficients, avoiding any unstated smoothness assumption. For a unit vector \(v_i(t)\) rotating uniformly in \(e_i^\perp\), each disk has the exact zonoid representation

\[
h(D_i,u)=\frac14\int_0^{2\pi}|\langle u,v_i(t)\rangle|\,dt.
\]

The volume formula for a three-dimensional zonotope generated by segments \([-w_j,w_j]\) is \(8\sum_{j<k<\ell}|\det(w_j,w_k,w_\ell)|\). Approximate each circular integral by positive finite sums of segments and pass to the limit in volume (Hausdorff continuity). It follows that

\[
V(D_i,D_j,D_k)=\frac1{48}\int_{[0,2\pi]^3}|\det(v_i(t),v_j(s),v_k(q))|\,dt\,ds\,dq.
\]

For \(i\ne j\), choose coordinates so \(i=1,j=2\). With two vectors in \(e_1^\perp\) and one in \(e_2^\perp\), the determinant magnitude is \(|\sin(s-t)\cos q|\). Its triple integral is \((8\pi)(4)=32\pi\). Hence \(3V(D_i,D_i,D_j)=3(32\pi/48)=2\pi\), proving the coefficient in (1). For completeness, parameterizing the three different coordinate disks yields the exact finite constant

\[
C=\frac12\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
\sqrt{\cos^2t\sin^2s+\sin^2t\cos^2s}\,dt\,ds
\approx13.372779524499367.
\]

The value of \(C\) will cancel from the fixed-volume difference, so no numerical approximation is used in the theorem's proof.

**3. Subtract the equal-volume cube.** Set \(p=abc\), \(r_1=bc\), \(r_2=ac\), \(r_3=ab\). Then \(r_1r_2r_3=p^2\), and

\[
\sum_{i\ne j}r_i^2r_j
=p^2\left(\frac ab+\frac ba+\frac bc+\frac cb+\frac ca+\frac ac\right).
\]

The cube with half-side \(s=p^{1/3}\) has all three disk coefficients equal to \(s^2\), so the corresponding sum is \(6p^2\) and its triple product is also \(p^2\). Equation (1) and the prefactor \((8/\pi)^3\) give

\[
\operatorname{Vol}_3(\mathcal M_2Q)-\operatorname{Vol}_3(\mathcal M_2Q_\square)
=\left(\frac8\pi\right)^3 2\pi p^2\left(\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}\left(\frac ab+\frac ba\right)-6\right).
\]

The parentheses are \(\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}(a-b)^2/(ab)\). Dividing by \(\operatorname{Vol}_3(Q)^2=(8p)^2\) proves the boxed identity. All summands are nonnegative and vanish simultaneously only for \(a=b=c\), proving strict cubic rigidity.

**4. Optimal logarithmic stability.** The elementary identity \((a-b)^2/(ab)=2(\cosh(\log a-\log b)-1)\), together with \(2(\cosh t-1)\ge t^2\), gives the lower bound. If \((a,b,c)=(e^t,1,e^{-t})\), then the quotient of the exact deficit and the logarithmic lower bound tends to one as \(t\to0\). Thus the coefficient for the stated sum of squared log-differences is best possible; equality in the logarithmic inequality holds only at the cube.

## Verification
A standalone program `verify.py` verifies the six-pair algebra over 125 positive rational triples without floating-point operations, independently evaluates the universal triple-disk integral by two finite midpoint quadratures, and checks the predicted three-disk volumes against inscribed convex hulls of numerically sampled exposed points at three anisotropic radius triples. It also checks the sharp logarithmic limit. All checks passed. These finite checks cannot replace the integral/zonoid argument or prove claims for infinitely many real parameter triples; the proof above supplies those universal steps.

## Relationship to prior work
Brauner, Ortega-Moreno and Schuster introduced the probability-normalized Radon-transform formulation and established general log-concavity inequalities for intrinsic volumes of mean-projection bodies; their all-convex-body statements do not evaluate \(\operatorname{Vol}_3(\mathcal M_2Q)\) for orthotopes or produce this exact fixed-volume defect. The geometry of Minkowski sums of three mutually perpendicular disks (the “dice” discotope) is studied in Gesmundo–Meroni and Mathis–Meroni, primarily through algebraic boundary and fiber-body structure. The present argument uses only the classical zonoid/Minkowski mixed-volume machinery from that setting and claims originality for the **exact normalized deficit and optimal quantitative cubic rigidity**, not for the discotope or its support representation. Earlier published evaluations of this specific deficit, if any, would affect historical priority.

## Limitations
The result is limited to Euclidean rectangular boxes in three dimensions, including all their positive aspect ratios. The universal constant \(C\) is specified by a convergent exact integral, not by a claimed elementary closed form. The inequality does not assert universal minimization among all convex bodies, and it does not establish the corresponding statement for rotated-edge nonorthogonal parallelotopes or in other dimensions. The scientific argument has not received independent mathematical validation; historical-priority assessment is limited to sources explicitly compared.
