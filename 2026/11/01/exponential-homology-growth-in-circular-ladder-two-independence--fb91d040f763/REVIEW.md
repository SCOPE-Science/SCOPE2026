# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The local induced-degree condition gives a bijection between faces and admissible cyclic sixteen-state walks; the sign of each walk is the face parity. The exact characteristic polynomial is independently computable by the included standard-library integer-matrix verifier and corroborated by exhaustive face enumeration for three through eight rungs. Rational root brackets and a one-point monotonic growth comparison establish nonvanishing for all larger rungs, with no extrapolation. The Euler--Poincaré inequality then proves the nonzero rational homology and exponential lower bound. The scope is restricted to this signed Euler obstruction; no claims about individual degrees or full homotopy type are made.

**Originality — PASS on inspected evidence.** Taylan, arXiv:1501.06754, treats \(P_k\)-devoid paths, cycles and forests, not Cartesian prism products. Deshpande–Singh, arXiv:2001.05448, explicitly enumerates its treated higher-independence graph families; the prism is not among them. The 2023 cubic-graph dissociation extremal theorem of Tu–Xiao–Lang assumes positive activities and therefore does not imply an exact signed value at \(-1\). Indexed related prism results apply to other topological graph complexes or ordinary independence, not induced-degree-one faces. The available preceding claims contain no matching cyclic transfer or nonvanishing statement. Exact unindexed equivalence remains a publication-priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** The circular ladder is a standard width-two periodic graph, and the two-independence complex is the first nontrivial relaxation beyond ordinary independence. The theorem settles a uniform, infinite-family noncontractibility question and proves an explicit exponential lower bound on rational Betti growth, rather than merely evaluating a graph-counting polynomial. The recurrence is a structural topological invariant and a reproducible benchmark for future homotopy/persistence classifications. The natural invariant, not arbitrary parameters, motivates the numerical algebraic constant.
