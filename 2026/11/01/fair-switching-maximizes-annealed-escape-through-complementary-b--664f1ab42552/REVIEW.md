# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. Binary coding turns survival into a run-weighted word count with exact finite-horizon normalization. The unique positive Perron root follows from the primitive truncated-run transfer and alternating-run generating function. The strict monotonicity calculation differentiates the **product** of run generating functions at fixed spectral parameter, and the uniform large-depth estimate expands the same exact root equation. The packaged checker returned 160 exact finite-horizon equalities and 36 independent eigenvalue agreements. Dyadic boundaries have zero measure; the endpoint biases are included.

## Originality

PASS within the inspected evidence. The closest general averaged-transfer formula is Section II of Georgiou--Dettmann--Altmann (2012). It supplies the appropriate framework, but not the explicit all-depth optimization or the sharp 4/3 gain. Cheriyath--Agarwal (2019) analyzes fixed forbidden cylinders, while Atnip--Froyland--González-Tokman--Vaienti (2021) gives a quenched pressure formalism. None of these inspected sections gives the present bias-ordering theorem. This is not a claim that finite-matrix reduction itself is new. A residual risk is that another weighted-pattern renewal treatment already implies the same optimization and constant.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The location-selection bias is a natural design parameter when each available escape hole has the same measure. The result identifies the globally optimal iid choice at **every** cylinder depth, rather than for selected numerical examples, and gives a nontrivial 4/3 gain over permanently opening one hole in the rare-event limit. Both the finite-depth strict inequality and the sharp gain resolve quantitative questions not answered by a general existence theorem for escape rates.

## Limits and closest literature

This is an **annealed** statement; its rate cannot be substituted for a typical-schedule quenched rate. Optimization excludes periodic or correlated schedules. The existing depth-two deterministic all-schedule analysis is not subsumed or contradicted. Earlier general Markov-hole transfer theory may give another route to the eigenvalue, and unindexed weighted-word literature may contain a stronger implication. Key primary sources: arXiv:1207.7000v1 (Section II), arXiv:1905.11767v1 (Sections 2--4), and arXiv:2103.04712v1 (Sections 13, 15).
