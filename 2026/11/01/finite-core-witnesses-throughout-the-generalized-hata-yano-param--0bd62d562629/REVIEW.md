# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The finite core parameter is chosen strictly between \(a\) and \(\min\{2a,4\delta\}\), so the reflection-paired endpoint atoms lie strictly inside the half-open core strips. The upper/lower image inequalities prove that the full core maps are defined, not merely their restrictions to the exhibited atoms. Every selected core branch moves to an adjacent vertex on a finite path; exact detailed balance yields a normalized, irreducible stationary probability with positive mass in both outer strips. The delicate endpoint \(\delta=1/6\) is included by verifying the source's branch conventions. The established source theorem supplies the arcsine corollary only after these premises are met. Rational sample checks agree; no infinite proof relies on those samples.

**Originality — PASS on inspected evidence.** Nakamura--Nakano--Toyokawa--Yano [1] proves a conditional arcsine law but asks in Problem 1.7 whether the requisite core probabilities exist for all \(\delta>0\). Its Section 1.2.2 explicitly constructs deterministic-core measures for \(\delta\geq1/8\), not for all \(\delta<1/8\). The present finite path gives witnesses for the entire previously unsettled interval. Hata--Yano [2] treats \(\delta=0\) and Inoue--Toyokawa [3] addresses different Lebesgue-equivalent invariant measures, so neither implies the new atomic-core existence. Database and literature searches did not identify an equivalent construction; broad priority remains subject to external review.

**Value — PASS.** The result closes a specifically posed and natural existence problem for an entire parameter family. It supplies explicit finitely supported invariant probabilities and an irreducible Markov mechanism even when the source's preferred deterministic-core method is unavailable. It makes a published intermittent limit law available for explicit initial points at arbitrarily small positive perturbations. The separate Gharaei--Homburg part is not settled.
