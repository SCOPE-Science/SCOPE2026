# Finite-scale stabilization of polyhedral translate depth and its sharp box cutoff

## Finding

For any full-dimensional convex polytope \(P\subset\mathbb R^d\), \(d\ge2\), the depth generated by translates of sufficiently large homothetic copies of \(P\) becomes **exactly**, not merely asymptotically, a finite-direction halfspace depth. The scale is explicit, uniform over all query points, and requires no continuity or density assumption on the probability measure.

Write an irredundant facet description
\[
P=\{x:\langle a_i,x\rangle\le b_i,\ 1\le i\le m\},
\]
where each \(a_i\ne0\). Select \(p_i\) in the relative interior of the \(i\)-th facet. Let \(\mu\) be any Borel probability measure with nonempty compact support \(S\). Put
\[
W_j=\max_{z\in S}\langle a_j,z\rangle-\min_{z\in S}\langle a_j,z\rangle,
\qquad
T(P,S;p)=\max_{i\ne j}\frac{W_j}{b_j-\langle a_j,p_i\rangle}.
\]
The denominators are strictly positive. For every \(t>0\) with \(t\ge T(P,S;p)\), and simultaneously for **every** \(y\in\mathbb R^d\),
\[
\boxed{\mathsf d_{tP}(y;\mu)=1-\max_{1\le i\le m}\mu\bigl(\{z:\langle a_i,z\rangle<\langle a_i,y\rangle\}\bigr).}
\]
Here \(\mathsf d_{tP}(y;\mu)=1-\sup\{\mu(tP+x):y\notin tP+x\}\), with all tested translates closed. The strict inequality in the finite-direction formula is essential when \(\mu\) has atoms.

For \(0<\alpha\le1\), define
\[
q_i(1-\alpha)=\sup\{s\in\mathbb R:\mu(\langle a_i,X\rangle<s)\le1-\alpha\},\qquad X\sim\mu.
\]
At every such stabilized scale, every upper depth region is the (possibly empty) polytope
\[
\boxed{D_\alpha^{tP}(\mu)=\bigcap_{i=1}^m\{y:\langle a_i,y\rangle\le q_i(1-\alpha)\}.}
\]
The facet normals therefore do not depend on the distribution or the level. For a discrete law, all depth values and regions can be computed from finitely many one-dimensional projected order statistics.

**Sharp parallelotope threshold.** If \(P=\{x:|\ell_k(x)|\le1,\ 1\le k\le d\}\) for linearly independent linear forms \(\ell_k\), write \(w_k=\max_S\ell_k-\min_S\ell_k\). The same exact formula and region description hold already at
\[
\boxed{t\ge t_{\rm box}:=\tfrac12\max_k w_k.}
\]
This is the smallest scale guaranteed uniformly over probability laws with a specified full rectangular support of those coordinate widths: for a measure uniform on that full box and every \(0<t<t_{\rm box}\), a query point at the midpoint of a longest-coordinate end facet has limiting facet depth zero but positive \(tP\)-depth.

## Assumptions and scope

The facet system is irredundant, so each selected facet-interior point satisfies \(\langle a_j,p_i\rangle<b_j\) for all \(j\ne i\). The support of \(\mu\) is compact but may be lower-dimensional or purely atomic. The scale \(t\) is a dilation factor of the fixed testing polytope, and the probability measure does not change with \(t\). Affine changes of coordinates transform the statement covariantly; the displayed threshold is unchanged by positive rescaling of individual facet inequalities.

The box-cutoff sharpness is distribution-uniform for full rectangular supports, not a claim that every individual probability measure needs this much dilation. The general threshold \(T(P,S;p)\) is sufficient, not claimed optimal for every polytope.

## Proof

**1. Universal facet upper bound.** If a translate \(tP+x\) misses \(y\), at least one of its defining inequalities is violated:
\[
\langle a_i,y\rangle>tb_i+\langle a_i,x\rangle.
\]
Every point \(z\in tP+x\) satisfies \(\langle a_i,z\rangle\le tb_i+\langle a_i,x\rangle<\langle a_i,y\rangle\). Consequently
\[
\sup_{x:y\notin tP+x}\mu(tP+x)
\le \max_i\mu\{z:\langle a_i,z\rangle<\langle a_i,y\rangle\}.
\]
No regularity condition on \(\mu\) is needed.

**2. An explicit matching translate.** Fix a facet index \(i\), a query \(y\), and a scalar \(a<\langle a_i,y\rangle\). If the halfspace \(H_i(a)=\{z:\langle a_i,z\rangle\le a\}\) has zero mass, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise replace \(a\), if necessary, by the upper endpoint of the projected support interval; this leaves \(\mu(H_i(a))\) unchanged and retains the strict separation from \(y\). Thus we may assume \(a\) belongs to the projection interval of \(S\). Choose \(z_i\in\operatorname{conv}S\) with \(\langle a_i,z_i\rangle=a\). Such a point exists by the intermediate-value property on the compact convex hull.

Let \(Q=z_i+t(P-p_i)\), a translate of \(tP\). Its \(i\)-th facet lies in \(\langle a_i,z\rangle=a\), so \(y\notin Q\). For every \(z\in S\cap H_i(a)\), the \(i\)-th inequality for membership in \(Q\) holds. For any other facet \(j\ne i\), both \(z\) and \(z_i\) belong to \(\operatorname{conv}S\), hence
\[
\langle a_j,z-z_i\rangle\le W_j\le t\bigl(b_j-\langle a_j,p_i\rangle\bigr).
\]
All other inequalities also hold. Therefore \(S\cap H_i(a)\subset Q\), and \(\mu(Q)\ge\mu(H_i(a))\). Taking the supremum over \(a<\langle a_i,y\rangle\) gives exactly \(\mu\{\langle a_i,X\rangle<\langle a_i,y\rangle\}\), by continuity from below. Taking the maximum over \(i\) proves the first boxed equality for every query simultaneously.

**3. Depth regions.** Each function \(s\mapsto\mu(\langle a_i,X\rangle<s)\) is nondecreasing and left-continuous. With compact support and \(0\le1-\alpha<1\), its sublevel set at \(1-\alpha\) is the closed ray ending at \(q_i(1-\alpha)\). Taking all \(m\) inequalities proves the second boxed equality.

**4. Improved threshold for parallelotopes.** The linear map \(x\mapsto(\ell_1(x),\ldots,\ell_d(x))\) identifies \(P\) with \([-1,1]^d\). For any coordinate \(i\) and strict threshold below \(\ell_i(y)\), an interval of length \(2t\ge w_i\) can cover all support points on the selected side of that threshold while leaving \(y\) outside the interval. The other coordinate intervals, independently translated, can each cover the entire support projection because \(2t\ge w_j\). Their Cartesian product is an admissible translate of \(tP\). The opposite facet directions are treated identically; this reaches the facet upper bound at the claimed half-width threshold.

For sharpness, let \(\mu\) be uniform on a full affine-coordinate box with positive widths \(w_1,\ldots,w_d\). Select an index \(i\) with \(w_i=\max_k w_k\), and a query \(y\) at the midpoint of the box face where \(\ell_i\) is minimal. The strict halfspace \(\{z:\ell_i(z)>\ell_i(y)\}\) has mass one, so the stabilized depth is zero. But any translate of \(tP\) intersects the box in a set of volume fraction at most \(2t/w_i\); therefore its depth at \(y\) is at least \(1-2t/w_i>0\) whenever \(0<t<w_i/2\). This proves optimality of the distribution-uniform threshold.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify_polyhedral_depth.py` uses only the Python standard library and exact rational arithmetic. It constructs facet-interior representatives and computes the sufficient threshold for squares, triangles, diamonds, cubes, and tetrahedra. For deterministic weighted empirical measures and query points (including data points), it explicitly builds every necessary facet-anchored admissible translate and checks that it captures all support points in the associated strict-side halfspace. It also checks exact inequalities witnessing sharpness of the box half-width threshold. Execute `python verify_polyhedral_depth.py`. Successful replay reported **32,823 exact rational checks**.

These finite checks test the construction but are not a proof for all measures or dimensions; that universal conclusion follows from Steps 1–4.

## Relationship to prior work

Molchanov and Nagy, *Depth based on translates of a convex body: geometry, floating bodies, and empirical theory* (arXiv:2610.26108, first public 2026-10-19), define translate depth in their Section 2 and prove in their Section 5 that dilation converges to tangent-cone depth under a boundary-null assumption. Their Section 7.4 gives a general arrangement algorithm for empirical polytope depth. The present statement instead gives **finite-scale exact stabilization**, a computable facet/slack threshold, and a reduction to only facet-direction quantiles even for atomic measures. It does not claim to have introduced translate depth or its general asymptotic framework.

Fraiman and Pateiro-López, *Quantiles for finite and infinite dimensional data* (2012), discuss directional quantiles and their connection to depth regions. The mere construction of polyhedral contours from finitely many directional quantiles is therefore not claimed as new; the asserted addition is their exact finite-dilation identification with the 2026 translate-depth functional.

An earlier evaluated simplex/uniform-measure dilation calculation establishes a special family of stabilization statements but does not supply the all-polytope, all-probability-law facet/slack theorem. The general theorem is compatible with that special case and does not re-claim its explicit simplex depth profile.

## Limitations

The proof assumes compact support. Without compactness there is no guarantee of a finite common scale. The explicit general-polytope threshold is sufficient, not asserted optimal. For measures charging facet hyperplanes, the strict-halfspace formula must not be replaced by closed-halfspace tangent-cone formulas without checking boundary mass. No assertion is made for arbitrary nonpolyhedral testing bodies or for finite-scale equality with unrestricted Tukey depth. Historical priority cannot be conclusively established from search results alone; independent mathematical assessment has not been performed.
