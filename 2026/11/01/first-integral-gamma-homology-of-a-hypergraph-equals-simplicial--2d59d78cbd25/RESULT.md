# First integral \(\Gamma\)-homology of a hypergraph equals simplicial first homology of its downward closure

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal H=(V,\mathcal E)\) be a finite hypergraph in which each vertex belongs to a nonempty hyperedge. Let \(K(\mathcal H)\) be the smallest abstract simplicial complex containing all hyperedges, and let \(H_i^{\Gamma}(\mathcal H;\mathbb Z)\) denote the normalized singular cubical homology defined using the radius-one-ball \(\Gamma\)-hypercubes of Zaidi and Jamil [1]. Then there are **natural integral isomorphisms**
\[
H_i^{\Gamma}(\mathcal H;\mathbb Z)\cong H_i(K(\mathcal H);\mathbb Z),\qquad i=0,1.
\]
They commute with every hypergraph morphism that sends each hyperedge into a hyperedge. In particular, the first \(\Gamma\)-homology need not be torsion-free: the three-uniform hypergraph of the ten triangular facets of the six-vertex projective-plane triangulation has \(H_1^{\Gamma}\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\). More generally, every finitely generated abelian group occurs as the first \(\Gamma\)-homology of a finite hypergraph.

## Assumptions and scope
A hypergraph here is finite, has nonempty hyperedges, and has no unsupported isolated vertices. Its simplicial downward closure \(K(\mathcal H)\) comprises all nonempty subsets of hyperedges. The normalized \(\Gamma\)-chain complex uses morphisms from \(J_n^\Gamma\), where \(J_n^\Gamma\) has vertex set \(\{0,1\}^n\) and hyperedges the closed Hamming-radius-one balls, modulo cubes constant in at least one coordinate. Boundary signs are those of [1], and all groups use integer coefficients. For \(n=2\), label a cube \((a,b,c,d)\) at \((00,10,01,11)\); its normalized boundary is
\[
\partial_2(a,b,c,d)=[a,b]+[b,d]-[a,c]-[c,d],
\]
where \([u,u]=0\). This statement asserts no equivalence in degrees greater than one and no invariance under the hypergraph-homotopy relation of [1].

## Proof
**1. Comparing generators.** Every supported vertex gives the same degree-zero generator in both complexes. A nondegenerate degree-one \(\Gamma\)-cube is an ordered pair \([u,v]\) of *different* vertices that lie together in some hyperedge. These pairs are precisely the two orientations of the edges of \(K(\mathcal H)\). Let \(q:C_1^{\Gamma}\twoheadrightarrow C_1(K)\) send an ordered pair to its oriented simplicial edge. It is surjective. Its kernel is generated by the backtracks \([u,v]+[v,u]\). The square \((u,v,u,u)\) is a valid, nondegenerate \(\Gamma\)-cube: each radius-one ball has image contained in \(\{u,v\}\). Its boundary is exactly \([u,v]+[v,u]\). Thus
\[
\ker(q)\subseteq\operatorname{im}(\partial_2^{\Gamma}).
\]

**2. Every cubical two-boundary becomes a simplicial boundary.** For a valid \(\Gamma\)-square \((a,b,c,d)\), the radius-one balls centered at \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\) imply that \(\{a,b,d\}\) and \(\{a,c,d\}\), respectively, each lie inside a hyperedge of \(\mathcal H\). Hence these are simplices in \(K(\mathcal H)\). Using the standard oriented simplicial convention, including zero for repeated vertices,
\[
q\bigl(\partial_2^{\Gamma}(a,b,c,d)\bigr)
=\partial_2^{K}[a,b,d]-\partial_2^{K}[a,c,d].
\]
It follows that \(q(\operatorname{im}\partial_2^{\Gamma})\subseteq\operatorname{im}\partial_2^K\).

**3. Every simplicial triangular boundary lifts.** If \(\{a,b,c\}\) is a two-simplex of \(K(\mathcal H)\), then its three vertices lie together in some hyperedge. The square \((a,b,a,c)\) is a valid nondegenerate \(\Gamma\)-cube and
\[
q\bigl(\partial_2^{\Gamma}(a,b,a,c)\bigr)= [a,b]+[b,c]-[a,c]=\partial_2^K[a,b,c].
\]
As the triangle boundaries generate \(\operatorname{im}\partial_2^K\), equality holds:
\[
q(\operatorname{im}\partial_2^{\Gamma})=\operatorname{im}\partial_2^K.
\]
Combined with Step 1 and the first isomorphism theorem, this gives a canonical isomorphism between the two degree-one chain groups modulo their two-boundaries. Both degree-one boundary maps to the common degree-zero group send \([u,v]\) to \(v-u\), so their kernels correspond. Taking kernels proves the degree-one homology isomorphism. Since degree-zero generators and degree-one boundaries agree, degree-zero homology also agrees. All constructions use only vertex maps and therefore commute with hypergraph morphisms.

**4. Realizations and torsion.** The complex whose two-faces are
\[
\{012,013,024,035,045,125,134,145,234,235\}
\]
(using the digits to denote vertices) is the classical six-vertex triangulation of \(\mathbb{RP}^2\): each edge occurs twice and every vertex link is a five-cycle. Its Euler characteristic is one. Explicit integral boundary reduction gives \(H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\), so the theorem gives the stated \(\Gamma\)-torsion example. Any finitely generated abelian group is the first homology of a finite connected simplicial complex (triangulate a finite presentation two-complex); taking that complex's maximal simplices as hyperedges leaves its downward closure unchanged. Hence the general realization assertion follows.

## Verification
Run the included standalone `verify_gamma.py` with Python 3 and SymPy. It enumerates all nondegenerate degree-two \(\Gamma\)-cubes for seven independently constructed hypergraphs, calculates their integer boundary lattices, and compares them via Hermite normal form against the lattices generated by triangle and edge-reversal relations. It independently computes simplicial first homology with Smith normal form. The cases include a triangle graph (one free class), a square graph (one free class), two nonuniform examples, a tetrahedral boundary, and the six-vertex projective plane (one order-two torsion class). Every exact integral comparison passed. These are finite consistency checks; the proof in Steps 1–3 establishes the theorem for all finite hypergraphs.

## Relationship to prior work
Zaidi and Jamil [1, Section 3] introduce \(\Gamma\)-homology and compute individual degree-one examples, including a nonuniform cycle; they do not identify its first homology with the ordinary simplicial homology of the hypergraph's downward closure or exhibit integral torsion. Bressan–Li–Ren–Wu [2] study *embedded* hypergraph homology and separately define the associated simplicial complex: embedded homology and \(\Gamma\)-homology are different, even in degree one [1, Example 3.11]. Barcelo–Greene–Jarrah–Welker [3] study a different graph cubical construction (the \(\Box\)-type construction in [1]), which kills ordinary graph triangles; a triangle graph has \(H_1^{\Gamma}\cong\mathbb Z\) by [1, Example 3.6], so this result is not a corollary of the graph cubical comparison. Previously tabulated two-torsion of the six-vertex simplicial projective plane is used as known input, not claimed new. The contribution is its universal transfer to the recently defined \(\Gamma\) theory and the natural structural identification in degrees zero and one.

## Limitations
No claim is made for higher \(\Gamma\)-homology, for nonsupported vertices, or for agreement with embedded or \(\Box\)-homology. The result does not contradict the failure of \(\Gamma\)-homotopy invariance proved in [1], since the corresponding simplicial downward closures of hypergraph-homotopy-equivalent objects need not be homotopy equivalent. The literature comparison cannot exclude all unpublished or unindexed equivalent formulations. Independent mathematical assessment remains outstanding.
