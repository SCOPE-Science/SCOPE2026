# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof gives an explicit surjection from oriented degree-one \(\Gamma\)-cubes onto simplicial one-chains. Its kernel consists of backtracks realized by \(\Gamma\)-square boundaries. Every singular square boundary maps to a difference of two allowable triangular boundaries, and every simplicial triangular boundary lifts via a specific admissible square. The two degree-one boundaries agree, proving a natural isomorphism in dimensions zero and one. The six-vertex projective-plane torsion illustration is separately certified by combinatorial manifold links and integral Smith invariants. The included checker corroborates the integer boundary-lattice equalities on seven hypergraphs, but is not substituted for the general argument.

## Originality — PASS, qualified by residual literature risk
The paper defining \(\Gamma\)-homology (Zaidi–Jamil, arXiv:2610.08196v1, October 6, 2026) was inspected through Sections 2–5, including definitions, examples and structural statements; no natural first-homology identification with the downward closure or torsion realization was found. The earlier embedded-homology paper of Bressan–Li–Ren–Wu examines a different invariant while defining the associated simplicial complex. The Barcelo–Greene–Jarrah–Welker graph homology uses a different cubical model and is distinct already on a triangle graph. Searches for equivalence under the terms Γ, hypergraph simplicial closure, first integral homology, and torsion found no stronger result; search absence alone is not proof of originality. The projective-plane torsion of classical simplicial homology is independently known and is not presented as new. The residual risk is an implicit equivalence in unindexed or unpublished literature.

## Value — PASS
The exact natural identification turns a new homology theory's nontrivial degree-one chain calculation into ordinary simplicial homology for all finite hypergraphs, without uniformity assumptions. It simultaneously establishes the possibility of integer torsion (previous examples in the new theory supply only free groups), gives a repeatable computation method, and shows that every finitely generated abelian group can occur. The naturality strengthens isolated Betti-number observations and clarifies precisely which higher-order hyperedge filling relations survive in first homology. The result does not purport to determine higher homology.

## Limits and related sources
The comparison applies only in degrees zero and one. Primary source [1] is Zaidi–Jamil, arXiv:2610.08196v1; related sources are Bressan–Li–Ren–Wu, arXiv:1610.00890, and Barcelo–Greene–Jarrah–Welker, arXiv:1803.07497. The latter is a distinct graph cubical theory. No external independent audit has been performed.
