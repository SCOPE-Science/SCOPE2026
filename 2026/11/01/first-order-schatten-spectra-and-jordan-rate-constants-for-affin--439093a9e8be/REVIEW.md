# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The Gaussian monomial identity bounds the Hilbert–Schmidt norm of the holomorphic operator pencil uniformly on a disk of radius inversely proportional to the matrix size. Cauchy coefficient estimates, together with polynomial rank growth for degree-m homogeneous Fock output, sum to a trace-class second-order remainder. The first differential factors as \(VB^{\mathsf T}W\); the exact Gram factors \(V^*V=I+\bar u u^{\mathsf T}\) and \(WW^*=e^{|u|^2}(I+\bar u u^{\mathsf T})\) yield the stated singular spectrum. Strictly contracting affine powers insert \(B=A^n\), while finite-dimensional Jordan asymptotics yield the sharp positive limit. The singular-spectrum and trace-norm estimates hold in arbitrary fixed finite dimension. The standalone finite checker passes; finite checks do not certify the infinite-dimensional analytic statements.

## Originality — PASS, subject to priority risk

The closest dated source, Bayart–Wang–Yao (2026, arXiv:2610.26349), gives qualitative operator-norm convergence for affine Fock composition iterates without the explicit trace-norm first variation or the rank-d singular spectrum and Jordan-rate coefficient. Chalendar–Partington (2021 preprint, arXiv:2106.00427) treat qualitative single-variable iteration. Carroll–Gilmore (arXiv:1911.07254) treat weighted orbit behavior. Stochel–Stochel (2017) provide a linear-symbol Fock model, acknowledged rather than claimed as new. Choe–Izuchi–Koo (2010) give boundedness, compactness and Schatten-membership results for linear sums; the full work could not be inspected and therefore remains a priority risk. Tien–Khoi (2017) address boundedness/compactness/essential norms of differences. Claim-level searches of published mathematical records found no statement implying the claimed displacement-weighted singular formula with a uniform trace-class quadratic remainder and sharp nonnormal iteration constants. Search nonretrieval does not establish uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The motivating work raises the natural question of **how fast** nonnormal Fock composition iterates approach their evaluation projection, which the qualitative semigroup theorem does not quantify. The theorem identifies all first-order singular values simultaneously, provides a uniform trace-class error instead of merely an operator-norm estimate, and reveals the exact polynomial multiplier from a dominant Jordan block. The two-dimensional defective case supplies a concrete, optimally normalized rate with dependence on displacement and nonnormal coupling. These are natural operator-theoretic quantities rather than arbitrary finite parameters.

## Scope and residual risks

The result assumes strict Euclidean contraction, finite-dimensional Gaussian Fock space and Schatten exponents at least one. A single dominant spectral value is needed for the normalized scalar Jordan limit. More general analytic symbol calculus could implicitly recover parts of the first variation. Unavailable full texts of older Fock linear-sum articles prevent a definitive literature-priority assertion. An independent audit is not included.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
