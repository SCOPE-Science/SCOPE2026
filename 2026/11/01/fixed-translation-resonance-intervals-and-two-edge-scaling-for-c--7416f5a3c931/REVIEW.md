# Review: fixed-translation contracted-rotation resonance intervals

## Correctness — PASS
The rational-tongue inequality is quoted as a known theorem from Laurent–Nogueira. Monotonicity of the nondecreasing degree-one lift, followed by analyticity, proves strict endpoint ordering and invertibility in the contraction rate. A finite floor-sum identity establishes the derivatives at the weak-contraction edge. The first missing coefficient and a mean-value argument establish the two strong-contraction regimes. The proof covers every reduced rational \(1\le p<q\), including \(q=2\). The standalone checker supports, but does not replace, these arguments.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence
Laurent–Nogueira and Janson–Öberg already provide the exact rational plateaus in the translation coordinate, and Gaivão–Nogueira explicitly identify the fixed-translation contraction-endpoint problem. The present claim is not a new rational rotation criterion: it is the unique polynomial-root inversion with universal \(q\)-dependent weak-edge width and arithmetic fractional-power strong-edge behavior. The inspected primary sources and targeted external-record comparisons do not state these fixed-translation asymptotic laws. Failure of a search is not uniqueness evidence.

## Mathematical value — PASS
For every reduced rational rotation number, the theorem determines the full nonempty fixed-translation resonance set and quantifies both extremal boundaries; the weak-edge aperture is numerator-independent, whereas the opposite edge exhibits a sharp arithmetic change for \(p=q-1\). This addresses an explicit interval-endpoint question and yields a natural complete parameter classification rather than an isolated numerical example.
