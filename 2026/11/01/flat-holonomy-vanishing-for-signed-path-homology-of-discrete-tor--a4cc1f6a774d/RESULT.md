# Flat-holonomy vanishing for signed path homology of discrete tori

## Finding
Let \(d\ge2\), let \(m_i\ge5\) be integers, and let \(G=C_{m_1}\square\cdots\square C_{m_d}\), with every edge present in both arrow directions. Assign a common sign \(s(e)\in\{+1,-1\}\) to its two arrows. Assume the product of the four signs on *every elementary coordinate square* is \(+1\). The sign product along an \(i\)-coordinate circle is independent of the values of all other coordinates; denote it by \(\chi_i\in\{\pm1\}\). For canonical signed GLMY homology with real coefficients (as defined by Bai, Li and Yau), the complete dimension formula is
\[
\dim_{\mathbb R} H_p^{\mathrm{sgn}}(G,s)=
\begin{cases}
\binom{d}{p},&\chi_1=\cdots=\chi_d=+1,\\
0,&\text{some }\chi_i=-1,
\end{cases}\qquad p\ge0.
\]
The binomial coefficient is zero for \(p>d\). The formula also holds for \(d=1\) and \(m_1\ge5\), included only as a base case. The new higher-dimensional assertion is that *one nontrivial flat sign holonomy annihilates every degree simultaneously*. The balanced value is an ordinary path-homology product calculation rather than a new claim.

## Assumptions and scope
The Cartesian product is the ordinary graph Cartesian product, not the categorical graph product. Thus its vertices are tuples of residues and edges change precisely one coordinate by one cyclic step. Each cycle length is at least five, ensuring that distinct two-step routes between distinct nonadjacent endpoints are precisely the two routes around an elementary coordinate square. A signing is **square-flat** when the product of its signs around every such square is positive. The coordinate-circle signs are \(\chi_i\), well-defined by square-flatness. We use unreduced, nonaugmented path homology over \(\mathbb R\) and the standard *canonical signed GLMY theory for undirected signed graphs*, obtained from bidirected completion. In particular, by the defining double-cover theorem of Bai--Li--Yau, its chain complex is the \((-1)\)-eigenspace under the deck involution on ordinary GLMY chains of the signed double cover. The statement concerns all integers \(d\ge2\), all \(m_i\ge5\), all square-flat signings, and every homological degree; no probabilistic or asymptotic assumption is present.

## Proof
**1. Holonomy normal form.** Regard the graph as the one-skeleton of the standard cubical decomposition of the \(d\)-torus, whose two-cells are exactly the elementary coordinate squares. Square-flatness says that the \(\mathbb Z/2\)-valued edge signing is a cellular one-cocycle. Two cocycles with the same coordinate-circle values differ by a coboundary because the cellular first cohomology of the torus is \((\mathbb Z/2)^d\), detected on the coordinate loops. The corresponding vertex switching induces an isomorphism of the canonical signed GLMY complexes (Bai--Li--Yau, Corollary 4.2). It suffices to take signs \(+1\) on every edge except the wraparound seam in direction \(i\), whose sign is \(\chi_i\). An elementary alternative proof of the normal form gauges edges on coordinate spanning trees to \(+1\) and propagates the remaining signs across squares.

**2. Square-faithful descent for path chains.** Consider a finite regular covering \(q:\widetilde X\to X\) of loopless bidirected graphs which is locally bijective on adjacent vertices and has this property: any two length-two routes in \(X\) between the same ordered pair of *distinct nonadjacent* vertices have lifts with the same endpoint when begun at the same lifted vertex. Suppose also that backtracks lift to backtracks. Then, over \(\mathbb R\), pullback by the orbit-sum of path lifts identifies the ordinary GLMY chain complex \(\Omega_*(X)\) with the deck-invariant subcomplex \(\Omega_*(\widetilde X)^{\mathrm{Deck}}\), provided every two-step shortcut which is allowed in the base lifts as an allowed shortcut (the latter is vacuous for the triangle-free graphs considered here).

Here is the degreewise verification, including the nontrivial issue of forbidden faces. Allowed \(p\)-paths lift uniquely once their first vertex is lifted, so allowable chains on the base correspond to invariant allowable chains upstairs. The regular path boundary is formed by deleting vertices. Deleting an endpoint is always allowable and respects the correspondence. Deleting an interior vertex from an allowable path creates either a regular allowable shortcut, a nonregular backtrack (recorded as zero), or a regular *forbidden* shortcut at exactly that position. In the forbidden case the resulting path contains one nonedge, connecting the endpoints of a length-two walk; all other consecutive pairs are graph edges. For a fixed starting lift, the lift of that resulting path across its one nonedge is independent of the choice of length-two bridge **exactly because of the stated two-step property**. Consequently every coefficient-cancellation relation for a forbidden regular face downstairs lifts to an identical relation at every upstairs starting vertex, and conversely invariant forbidden-face cancellation upstairs descends to the downstairs relation. Hence a chain has an allowable boundary downstairs if and only if its orbit-lift has an allowable boundary upstairs, proving \(\Omega_p(X)\cong\Omega_p(\widetilde X)^{\mathrm{Deck}}\) for every \(p\), compatibly with \(\partial\). Characteristic zero makes invariants exact on homology. For products of cycles of lengths at least five, all distinct two-step routes between distinct endpoints form an elementary coordinate square (or coincide). Their lifts to the longer coordinate-cycle products close, so the hypotheses apply. The same remains true for any quotient by coordinate half-turns, since a coordinate square is closed in every such quotient.

**3. The signed double cover as a quotient of a longer torus.** Let \(I=\{i:\chi_i=-1\}\). If \(I\neq\varnothing\), form the unsigned product graph
\[
P=\mathop{\square}_{i=1}^d C_{\ell_i},\qquad
\ell_i=\begin{cases}2m_i,&i\in I,\\m_i,&i\notin I.\end{cases}
\]
Let \(D\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^I\) act by independent translations through \(m_i\) in the doubled factors. Let \(K\le D\) be the kernel of the sum-of-coordinates homomorphism \(D\to\mathbb Z/2\). The quotient \(P/K\) is the signed double cover \(\widetilde G_s\). An explicit covering isomorphism sends a vertex \(y\in V(P)\) to
\[
\left( (y_i\bmod m_i)_{i=1}^d,\quad \sum_{i\in I}\lfloor y_i/m_i\rfloor\bmod2 \right).
\]
Indeed the sheet coordinate flips exactly when traversing a negative wraparound seam, matching the defining signed-double-cover edge lifts. Fibers are precisely the \(K\)-orbits and the residual deck involution is the nontrivial element of \(D/K\). All four-cycles lift closed, so Step 2 gives a \(D/K\)-equivariant identification
\[
H_p(\widetilde G_s;\mathbb R)\cong H_p(P;\mathbb R)^K.
\]

**4. The entire covering action is homologically trivial.** By the *chain-level* Cartesian-product Künneth theorem for ordinary path homology (Grigor'yan--Muranov--Yau, Theorem 4.7),
\[
H_*(P;\mathbb R)\cong\bigotimes_{i=1}^dH_*(C_{\ell_i};\mathbb R).
\]
Each bidirected simple cycle of length at least five has one copy of \(\mathbb R\) in degrees zero and one and zero in higher degrees (Asao, Remark 8.15, with the unreduced degree-zero group added). A generator of first homology is the forward oriented sum of the cycle edges; every cyclic translation fixes this representative, and it fixes the degree-zero generator as well. The Künneth isomorphism is natural under graph translations, because its shuffle-cross-product map commutes with the factorwise translations. Thus **every element of \(D\) acts as the identity on \(H_p(P;\mathbb R)\) for every \(p\)**. In particular, \(H_p(P)^K=H_p(P)\) as real vector spaces; the remaining nontrivial element of \(D/K\) also acts trivially. The deck \((-1)\)-eigenspace in \(H_p(\widetilde G_s)\) is therefore zero. By the signed GLMY double-cover theorem (Bai--Li--Yau, Proposition 3.4 and its homological consequence), \(H_p^{\mathrm{sgn}}(G,s)=0\) in all degrees.

**5. Balanced case.** If \(I=\varnothing\), the signing is switching equivalent to all-positive; its signed double cover is the disjoint union of two copies of \(G\). Its deck anti-invariant homology equals the ordinary path homology of \(G\). The preceding cycle calculation and the Künneth theorem give \(H_p(G;\mathbb R)\cong\mathbb R^{\binom dp}\) for \(0\le p\le d\), and zero otherwise. This also covers \(d=1\). The theorem follows.

## Verification
The accompanying self-contained `verify.py` tests the distinct ingredients without substituting finite cases for the general proof. It computes signed cubical local-system boundary ranks over two primes, checks square-flatness and connectedness of the signed covers, and verifies the explicit longer-product quotient map. It covers 22 signed torus cases, with \(d\in\{1,2,3\}\), including all eight holonomy patterns for the three-dimensional five-cycle torus; 8,030 cubical cell columns were processed. It **also constructs the actual signed GLMY chain matrices in degrees zero, one, and two** for 12 one- and two-dimensional signed tori, retaining sheet labels of forbidden shortcuts; the resulting degree-zero and degree-one homology agrees with the theorem. These checks are finite corroboration: the all-degree conclusion uses the chain-level descent lemma, published Cartesian Künneth theorem, and the translation-action argument.

## Relationship to prior work
Bai, Li and Yau (2026, arXiv:2609.25557) introduced the canonical signed GLMY theory through the deck anti-invariant part of a signed double cover, established switching invariance, and gave low-dimensional sign-sensitive examples including an unbalanced directed three-cycle. **These definitions, the anti-invariant identification, and the balanced reduction are prior results, not novel here.** Their full accessible text, including Sections 3--4 and 6--7, does not state a general all-degree flat discrete-torus vanishing theorem. Bai et al. (2026, arXiv:2608.23187) give higher-degree nonvanishing ordinary path homology on Cartesian powers of five-cycles; that is compatible with and motivates the contrast with the signed case. The path Künneth theorem and long-cycle homology used in the proof are established by Grigor'yan--Muranov--Yau (2017) and Asao (2022/2023). The new content is the use of square-faithful chain descent and holonomy-quotient symmetry to prove simultaneous vanishing in **all degrees** for arbitrary nontrivial flat signings and arbitrary high-dimensional cycle products. The elementary result for one cycle and the ordinary balanced product ranks are not independently claimed as new.

## Limitations
The statement requires square-flat signs, coincident signs on antiparallel arrows, real coefficients, and all coordinate cycles of length at least five. It does not address non-flat signings, unequal opposite-arrow signs, shorter cycle factors, torsion in integral signed theories, or more general graph products. The proof supplies vector-space dimensions and vanishing, not a canonical cycle-level splitting for balanced products. There is residual novelty risk from related literature not identified in the searched sources, although the relevant full text of arXiv:2609.25557 has been inspected.
