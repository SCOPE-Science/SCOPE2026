# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem quantifies over arbitrary dimension \(d\ge2\), all factor lengths at least five, and every square-flat signing. A complete proof reduces such signs by switching to seam holonomies, realizes the signed double cover as an even-parity quotient of a longer Cartesian-cycle product, proves that two-step square lifting identifies GLMY chains under finite covering invariants, and applies the published Cartesian Künneth theorem. Every half-turn generator acts trivially on the full product path homology because it fixes the directed-cycle generator, so the residual deck sign eigenspace vanishes degree by degree. In the balanced case, the Künneth ranks are the familiar binomial coefficients. Asao establishes long-cycle homology (Remark 8.15); Grigor'yan--Muranov--Yau establish the path-chain Künneth theorem (Theorem 4.7); Bai--Li--Yau establish signed double-cover anti-invariance and switching. The computational checker independently confirms low-degree signed GLMY ranks in 12 cases and cubical analogues in 22 cases. The experiments do not certify infinitely many instances; the argument does. The only substantial new lemma (square-faithful descent) is explicitly proved from the local form of the forbidden-path boundary equations.

## Originality — PASS, residual risk
The accessible full text of Bai--Li--Yau (arXiv:2609.25557), particularly Sections 3--4 and 6--7, gives the signed double-cover construction, switching invariance, and small sign-sensitive examples, **not** this flat-torus all-degree vanishing classification. Their directed cycle example and the balanced reduction are acknowledged as previously known; the present claim asserts the new higher-dimensional holonomy criterion. The established path Künneth and ordinary long-cycle computations are credited and combined with a new quotient-descent argument. Relevant actual searches for signed GLMY flat products, anti-invariant torus path homology, sign local-system holonomy, and higher-dimensional unbalanced cycle products revealed no covering theorem. An implicit or independently known adaptation of cellular local-system acyclicity remains the main novelty risk, and search non-detection is not proof of originality.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The source paper introduces a new signed graph homology theory and emphasizes sign sensitivity, while earlier work exhibits nonzero ordinary path homology in all degrees for high-dimensional cycle products. A complete **all-degree**, **all-flat-signings**, and **all-dimensional-products** vanishing criterion gives a structural answer on a canonical test family: exact coordinates of the change from binomial Betti numbers to full acyclicity. The controlling invariant is the natural \(\mathbb Z/2\) torus holonomy class rather than an arbitrary selected parameter, and the proof establishes a reusable local two-step square-descent mechanism. This is more than a textbook application of ordinary cellular local systems because the equivalence with GLMY path chains needs a separate nonstandard lifting argument.

## Scope and risks
The formula concerns real homology, direction-compatible bidirected signs, all elementary squares flat, and cycle lengths at least five. It does not assert integral torsion properties, generic signings, or signed products outside those hypotheses. No independent external audit has been performed.
