# Four integral product-torus generators of octahedral two-particle homology

## Finding
For the octahedral graph \(O=K_{2,2,2}\), let \(X=\operatorname{UConf}_2(|O|)\) be the space of two unordered, distinct points on the geometric graph. Its integral second homology is freely generated by **four explicitly embedded product tori**, one for each pair of opposite triangular faces. In addition, under \(G=\operatorname{Aut}(O)\cong S_4\times C_2\),
\[
H_2(X;\mathbb Q)\cong (\mathbf1\oplus S^{(3,1)})\boxtimes\varepsilon,
\qquad
H_1(X;\mathbb Q)\cong (\operatorname{sgn}_{S_4}\oplus S^{(2,1,1)})\boxtimes(\mathbf1\oplus\varepsilon).
\]
Here \(\varepsilon\) denotes the nontrivial representation of the central \(C_2\), and \(S^\lambda\) denotes a rational Specht module. The rational Betti vector is \((1,8,4)\). Thus \(\widetilde H_*(X/G;\mathbb Q)=0\), although \(X\) has nontrivial first and second homology.

## Assumptions and scope
Represent the six vertices by \(\{\pm e_1,\pm e_2,\pm e_3\}\), with edges connecting every nonantipodal pair. The group of signed coordinate permutations has order \(48\). It acts on the four opposite-face pairs as \(S_4\); the parity of the number of coordinate sign changes gives the central \(C_2\)-character. In particular, the kernel of this parity character is a subgroup isomorphic to \(S_4\). We take ordinary singular homology with the indicated coefficients and use the geometric (not sink) collision-free configuration space.

## Proof
**Finite cubical model.** The standard discrete two-particle cubical model is homotopy equivalent to \(X\): the graph is simple, hence the two-particle subdivision conditions hold [1,2]. Its \(0\)-cells are unordered pairs of distinct vertices; its \(1\)-cells are pairs \((v,e)\) with vertex \(v\) disjoint from graph edge \(e\); and its oriented \(2\)-cells are unordered pairs of disjoint graph edges, ordered to orient their product. There are exactly \(15,48,30\) cells in dimensions \(0,1,2\).

Orient each graph edge \(e=(i,j)\) from the lower to the higher vertex index. The cubical boundary of a square \((e,f)\), written with \(e<f\), is
\[
\partial_2(e,f)=(j_e,f)-(i_e,f)-(j_f,e)+(i_f,e).
\]
The boundary of \((v,e)\) is \((v,j_e)-(v,i_e)\). Exact rational elimination gives \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_1=14\) and \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_2=26\), with \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\). Thus \(\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_2=4\) and \(\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_1=8\). The complete reconstructible elimination is in `verify.py`; the result does not rely on a floating-point computation.

**The four primitive tori.** Consider the four sign triples \(f=(f_1,f_2,f_3)\in\{\pm1\}^3\) satisfying \(f_1f_2f_3=1\). The triangle on vertices \(f_1e_1,f_2e_2,f_3e_3\) is disjoint from its antipodal triangle. Let \(z_f\) be the product of their oriented three-edge cycles, viewed as a cubical \(2\)-chain in the unordered configuration space. Since both factors are cycles, \(\partial_2z_f=0\). The four columns \(z_f\) have a \(4\times4\) coordinate minor of determinant \(\pm1\): each opposite-face-pair has a square coordinate used by none of the other three pairs (the independently reconstructed square coordinates are documented and checked in `verify.py`). Therefore the four cycles span a primitive rank-four sublattice of the ambient free group \(C_2\). Since \(\ker\partial_2\subseteq C_2\) is saturated and has rank four, they form an **integral basis** of \(H_2=\ker\partial_2\cong\mathbb Z^4\); no claim about the torsion of \(H_1\) is required.

**Equivariance.** The four positive-product sign triples represent the four opposite-face pairs. A signed coordinate permutation with even sign-change parity preserves this preferred choice of one face per pair, and acts by an unsigned permutation of the four product tori: any coordinate permutation changes each triangular orientation by the same sign, whose square is positive. An odd-parity sign-change maps each preferred triangle to the *opposite* of a preferred triangle, interchanging the two product factors and reversing their torus orientation. Thus the integral action on the displayed basis is the four-point permutation action tensored with \(\varepsilon\). Over \(\mathbb Q\), the four-point permutation representation is \(\mathbf1\oplus S^{(3,1)}\).

**First homology.** The equivariant cellular Euler identity gives \([H_1]=[C_1]-[C_0]-[C_2]+[\mathbf1]+[H_2]\) in the rational representation ring, since the complex is connected. Signed fixed-cell enumeration for all \(48\) automorphisms yields the following characters, indexed by the cycle type in \(S_4\): for the positive central parity, \(\chi_{H_1}=(8,-4,0,2,0)\) and \(\chi_{H_2}=(4,2,0,1,0)\); for negative central parity, \(\chi_{H_1}=(0,0,0,0,0)\) and \(\chi_{H_2}=(-4,-2,0,-1,0)\). The cycle-type order is \((1^4),(2,1,1),(2,2),(3,1),(4)\). Comparison with the elementary \(S_4\) character table gives the stated \(H_1\) decomposition. Neither positive-degree module has a full-group invariant vector; rational transfer applied to the barycentric subdivision therefore gives \(\widetilde H_*(X/G;\mathbb Q)=0\).

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` (standard library only). It constructs the complete integer matrices, performs rational row reduction, checks \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\), verifies the four torus cycles and their independent columns, and calculates the second-homology character for every signed coordinate permutation. The output is recorded in `verification_output.txt`. The proof above establishes the integral torus basis via a unit minor and saturation; numerical experiments are not being extrapolated to an infinite family.

## Relationship to prior work
The discrete graph-configuration cubical model and the broader graph-braid homology framework are classical [1,2]. Sawicki [3] explicitly discusses how the octahedral graph supports position-dependent Aharonov–Bohm phases, without giving the four top-dimensional integral torus generators or their complete octahedral representation. Farber and Hanbury [2] study ordered configuration spaces and general intersection forms; their formulas do not state the unordered octahedral four-torus symmetry basis. The new claim concerns that integral top-homology basis and full symmetry decomposition, not the known existence of the configuration model or generic rank techniques.

## Limitations
This gives the second integral homology and the first rational homology representation, but does not determine the integral lattice structure of first homology or the fundamental group. Publication-priority coverage outside the accessible indexed and inspected sources remains possible. Scientific comparison is not an independent mathematical audit.
