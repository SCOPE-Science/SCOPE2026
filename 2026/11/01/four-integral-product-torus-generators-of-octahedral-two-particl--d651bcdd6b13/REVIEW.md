# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The complete cubical model has 15 vertices, 48 edges and 30 squares. The boundary matrices satisfy \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\) and have exact ranks 14 and 26. Four opposite-triangle product cycles are explicitly closed, and each has a square support coordinate missing from the other three; this gives a unit integral coordinate minor. With top homology rank four and saturated kernel, the cycles form an integral basis. Symmetry is verified for all 48 automorphisms and also proved geometrically using exchange of complementary face factors.

## Originality — PASS
Sawicki's octahedral example discusses position-dependent Aharonov–Bohm phases; it does not present the top integral homology basis or its full symmetry action. Farber–Hanbury's ordered two-point intersection framework and the cubical model are acknowledged. The closest indexed same-symmetry result concerns the cuboctahedron (different graph), while the octahedron matching complex is a different functor. Equivalent unindexed work remains a risk, as does an unexamined part of the relevant Durham thesis.

## Value — PASS
The top-homology basis is realized by four concrete embedded product tori and is proven integrally primitive, strengthening a plain Betti-number computation. Their unexpectedly simple twisted permutation action explains why a nontrivial four-dimensional top homology disappears after quotienting by full octahedral symmetry.

## Limits
The integral structure of first homology, the fundamental group, and absolute publication priority are not established.
