# Four Lyapunov inflections with only three distinct expansion slopes
## Finding
There exists a four-branch expanding interval cookie-cutter with exactly three distinct branch slopes and **at least four distinct Lyapunov inflections**. Its slopes are \(2,2^{17},2^{17},2^{5761}\). In the full class of finite-branch affine cookie-cutters, the minimum possible number of distinct slopes supporting four inflections is therefore **three**; the minimum total number of branches is **four**.
## Assumptions and scope
Take four pairwise interior-disjoint closed intervals inside \([0,1]\) with respective lengths \(2^{-1},2^{-17},2^{-17},2^{-5761}\). Their total length is less than one. Map each interval affinely and orientation-preservingly onto \([0,1]\). Restrict the resulting expanding map to the compact repeller. For a Lyapunov exponent \(\alpha\) in the interior of its attainable range, \(L(\alpha)\) denotes the Hausdorff dimension of points having exponent \(\alpha\). We call a point a Lyapunov inflection when \(L''\) changes sign there.
## Proof
Let \(c=16\log2\), \(b=\log2\), \(\beta=b/c=1/16\), and let the multiplicity-weighted slope levels be \((d_0,d_1,d_2)=(0,1,360)\) with multiplicities \((m_0,m_1,m_2)=(1,2,1)\). The logarithms of the four expansion slopes are \(b+cd_j\), each repeated \(m_j\) times. The pressure for the potential \(-t\log|T'|\) is
\[P(t)=-bt+\log\!\left(1+2e^{-ct}+e^{-360ct}\right).\]
Put \(w=-ct\), \(Z(w)=1+2e^w+e^{360w}\), and introduce the probabilities \(p_0=1/Z\), \(p_1=2e^w/Z\), \(p_2=e^{360w}/Z\). Define \(\mu(w)=p_1+360p_2\) and \(V(w)=p_1+360^2p_2-\mu(w)^2\). Since the three slopes differ, \(V(w)>0\) at every finite \(w\). Direct differentiation gives the Lyapunov exponent \(\alpha(t)=-P'(t)=c(\beta+\mu(w))\) and the pressure variance \(P''(t)=c^2V(w)\).
The standard Legendre/entropy identity for a full-branch affine repeller is \(L(\alpha(t))=t+P(t)/\alpha(t)\). Differentiating twice *with respect to \(\alpha\)* yields
\[L''(\alpha(t))=\frac{2P(t)P''(t)-\alpha(t)^2}{\alpha(t)^3P''(t)}.\]
Thus its sign equals the sign of the following explicit real-analytic function:
\[G(w)=2\bigl(\beta w+\log Z(w)\bigr)V(w)-(\beta+\mu(w))^2.\]
At the five strictly increasing rational arguments \(w=-2,-7/10,-1/5,-3/100,1/50\), respectively, rigorous outward-rounded interval evaluations give the signs \(-,+,-,+,-\). Since \(G\) is continuous, each of the four disjoint intervening open intervals contains a zero at which the sign changes. Because \(d\alpha/dt=-c^2V<0\), the resulting four exponent values are distinct and interior. Hence the spectrum has at least four inflections.
For minimality in the number of distinct slopes, a map with only one distinct slope has constant Lyapunov exponent. For two distinct slopes, the rigorous two-essential-slope theorem of Ma (2020, Theorem 4.2) bounds the number of inflections by two, regardless of multiplicities. Ma (2020, Theorem 2.2) likewise bounds three physical branches by two, while this construction uses four. Therefore three distinct slopes and four physical branches are both minimal.
## Verification
Run `python verification.py`. It evaluates \(G\) at the five rational arguments using 75-digit arbitrary-precision interval arithmetic, checks that the lower and upper endpoints have the stipulated strict signs, and prints each outward enclosure. The values are approximately \(-0.037503598176\), \(0.00850269141738\), \(-0.0162669781777\), \(1.88672587615\), and \(-124856.224015\). These finite evaluations certify four continuous sign crossings; they are not substituted for the analytic pressure derivation or Ma's two-slope upper bound. The verification code uses the openly available `mpmath` package.
## Relationship to prior work
Jenkinson, Pollicott and Vytnova (2020) give a seven-branch, three-slope example exhibiting four inflections. Ma (2020) proves four branches are minimal, gives a four-branch construction with four different slopes, and proves a general upper bound of six inflections for any system with three distinct slopes. Those results separately minimize physical branch count and exhibit three-slope examples, but do **not** jointly supply a four-branch/three-distinct-slope example. The present witness makes both minima simultaneous using just one duplicated middle slope. The thermodynamic criterion employed here is standard; the specific simultaneous-minimality witness is the claim.
## Limitations
We prove **at least four**, not exactly four, for the exhibited map; Ma's three-distinct-slope general upper bound allows up to six. No classification of all slope vectors, bifurcations, or locations of further possible inflections is asserted. The novelty assessment is conditional on literature accessible in the stated comparisons and is not an independent priority certification.
