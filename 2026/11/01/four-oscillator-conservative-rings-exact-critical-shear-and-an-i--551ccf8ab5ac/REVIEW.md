## Originality — PASS (priority provisional)

The general conservative lattice equation and its Hamiltonian interpretation are previously established: Pikovsky (2026) writes the nearest-neighbor equations explicitly, while Ahnert–Pikovsky (2008) studies their traveling waves. Burylko–Mielke–Wolfrum–Yanchuk (2018) treats four-oscillator invariant foliations in a special skew-symmetric phase-ring regime. The current claim is not the rediscovery of integrability; it isolates the two exceptional mixed cosine–oriented-sine leaves and computes a sharp probability law for *periods of their individual trajectories*, with finite-mean contrast everywhere else and a transverse square-root divergence. Inspected relevant sections of the primary sources did not give an equivalent or stronger statement. Absence from accessed documents or search results is not proof of first discovery; more obscure specialized period statistics remain a priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Exact period statistics can differ qualitatively between neighboring invariant leaves even though trajectories are almost surely periodic on every leaf. The complete identification of the singular two-leaf geometry, its universal heavy tail and unbounded mean, and its explicit crossover in small-amplitude periods provide a sharp finite-network dynamical boundary that a general volume-preservation statement alone cannot quantify. The assertion is restricted to conditional Haar distributions on invariant leaves and does not generalize automatically to arbitrary network sizes.
