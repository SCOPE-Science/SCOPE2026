# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The operator normalization is fixed and the trace-class coherent-state energy formula follows by rank-one integration. The coordinate \(u=r^2/(1-r^2)\) makes area preservation exact. Twice differentiating the integrated kernel gives a boundary convolution and one diagonal potential term. Disk-automorphism invariance forces the first-harmonic Hessian to vanish and thereby fixes that potential term without an unverified integral identity. Fourier diagonalization yields the stated deficit with the specified factor \(\pi^2\). The strict ordering follows from the Gamma-mixture representation and a proved Riccati differential inequality for modified Bessel ratios. An independent finite-dimensional quadrature check supports the normalization and signs, but the infinite-dimensional theorem relies on the analytical proof.

## Originality

PASS, with historical-priority limitations. Ramos–Tilli (arXiv:2205.07998, 2022) treats wavelet concentration in operator norm, not the Hilbert–Schmidt purity Hessian. Nicola–Riccardi (arXiv:2401.04659, 2024) proves the global hyperbolic rearrangement result for Hilbert–Schmidt wavelet localization but does not determine its Fourier-mode Hessian or the sharp least-stable harmonic. Gómez–Kalaj–Melentijević–Ramos (arXiv:2411.16010, 2024) gives quantitative operator-norm stability and does not determine the squared-Hilbert–Schmidt Hessian. Euclidean nonlocal isoperimetric variation formulas are structurally related but neither supply the hyperbolic Cauchy-kernel coefficient nor imply the stated Fourier spectrum. Searches of relevant mathematical records did not identify an equivalent statement. Absence from such searches is not a proof of priority.

## Mathematical value

PASS. A global disk-maximization inequality does not determine the mode-resolved quadratic loss of localization purity. This theorem provides the entire angular curvature spectrum, proves that only geometric translations are flat, and identifies the universally least-stable quadrupole deformation with a sharp coefficient for every wavelet parameter and every disk area. These data specify local conditioning and near-optimal shape design for a natural wavelet localization functional. No downstream theorem or global quantitative-stability bound is asserted.

## Scope and risks

The expansion is for each fixed smooth perturbation and gives no uniform nonlinear remainder for highly oscillatory families. Historical-priority uncertainty remains, especially regarding unpublished shape calculus in hyperbolic rearrangement theory. Numerical experiments cover finitely many parameters and cannot replace the analytical Bessel comparison or shape invariance argument. Independent audit and validation have not been performed.
