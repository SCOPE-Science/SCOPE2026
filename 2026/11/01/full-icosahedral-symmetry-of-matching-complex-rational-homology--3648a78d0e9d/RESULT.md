# Full icosahedral symmetry of matching-complex rational homology

## Finding
Let \(I\) be the ordinary icosahedral graph, \(X=\mathcal M(I)\) its matching complex, and \(G=\operatorname{Aut}(I)\cong A_5\times C_2\). The full rational representation on its only positive-dimensional reduced rational homology is
\[
\widetilde H_3(X;\mathbb Q)\cong
(2V_6\oplus2V_4\oplus4V_5)\boxtimes\mathbf1
\;\oplus\;
(V_6\oplus2V_4\oplus2V_5)\boxtimes\varepsilon.
\]
Here \(V_4,V_5\) denote the irreducible rational \(A_5\)-modules of dimensions four and five, and \(V_6\) is its six-dimensional rational irreducible obtained by combining the two Galois-conjugate three-dimensional complex characters. The inversion factor \(C_2\) has trivial representation \(\mathbf1\) and sign \(\varepsilon\). All other reduced rational homology vanishes. The inversion-even and inversion-odd summands have dimensions \(40\) and \(24\). Equivalently, traces on identity, order-two, order-three and each order-five *rotation* class are \((64,0,-2,-1)\), and traces after multiplying by the central inversion are \((16,0,-2,1)\). Thus
\[
\dim\widetilde H_3(|X|/C_2;\mathbb Q)=40,\qquad
\widetilde H_*(|X|/A_5;\mathbb Q)=\widetilde H_*(|X|/G;\mathbb Q)=0.
\]
The established integral homotopy calculation of \(X\), including its three-torsion, is prior work and is not a new claim here.

## Assumptions and scope
Use vertices \(0,11\) and \(u_j=1+j\), \(v_j=6+j\), for \(j\) modulo five. The thirty undirected edges are the five orbit families \(0u_j\), \(11v_j\), \(u_ju_{j+1}\), \(v_jv_{j+1}\), \(u_jv_j\), and \(u_jv_{j-1}\), where the first two families together contribute ten edges, for a total of thirty. The vertices of \(X\) are these thirty graph edges; its simplices are all nonempty collections of pairwise endpoint-disjoint graph edges, along with the empty face in augmented chains. Every assertion is for this precisely specified finite complex and its natural action induced by automorphisms of \(I\). The coefficient field for all new representation claims is \(\mathbb Q\).

## Proof
**Finite homology concentration.** Exhaustive edge-by-edge generation gives the simplex face vector \((30,315,1400,2535,1482,125)\) in dimensions zero through five. An independent sparse modular elimination of each signed oriented simplicial boundary over \(\mathbb F_{101}\) has ranks \((29,286,1114,1357,125)\) for the boundaries from dimensions one through five. The reduced homology over that field therefore has dimension \(64\) only in degree three. For any integer matrix, its rational rank is at least its rank modulo a prime, hence rational Betti numbers cannot exceed those over \(\mathbb F_{101}\). The reduced Euler characteristic computed directly from the face vector is \(-64\). These two facts force \(\widetilde H_3(X;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q^{64}\) and all other reduced rational homology zero. The pre-existing integral homotopy classification independently agrees with this conclusion.

**All graph automorphisms.** The verifier backtracks through adjacency-preserving vertex permutations and finds precisely \(120\), each checked against all adjacencies. The subgroup generated by its elements of orders three and five has \(60\) elements and is the icosahedral rotation group \(A_5\). The unique vertex-antipodal involution \(z\), sending every vertex to its graph-distance-three opposite, commutes with the entire group and lies outside the rotations. Hence the full group is \(A_5\times\langle z\rangle\).

**Exact character.** For a graph automorphism \(g\), let its induced permutation on the thirty edges act on the simplicial chains with the usual orientation sign. The reduced homological Lefschetz number equals the alternating signed count of fixed oriented simplices. Since the rational reduced homology is concentrated in degree three, the required third-homology trace is minus this reduced Lefschetz number. A second direct computation partitions the thirty edges into orbits under \(g\) and sums \((-1)^{\#\mathrm{selected\ orbits}}\) over families of pairwise vertex-disjoint edge orbits. For each invariant simplex, its alternating chain sign equals that orbit-family sign, so this second method is algebraically identical to, but algorithmically independent from, the oriented-simplex computation. Every one of the \(120\) automorphisms agrees in both computations. The resulting complete class counts and traces are:

| Rotation factor order | Number of rotation factors | Trace without \(z\) | Trace with \(z\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 1 | 64 | 16 |
| 2 | 15 | 0 | 0 |
| 3 | 20 | −2 | −2 |
| 5 | 24 | −1 | 1 |

There are two order-five conjugacy classes of size twelve; the traces agree on them. Thus the table indeed specifies the character on all ten conjugacy classes of \(A_5\times C_2\).

**Rational irreducibles.** On the four merged rotation orders \((1,2,3,5)\), the rational irreducible character values are \(\mathbf1:(1,1,1,1)\), \(V_6:(6,-2,0,1)\), \(V_4:(4,0,1,-1)\), and \(V_5:(5,1,-1,0)\). The half-sum of the two coset characters is \((40,0,-2,0)=2V_6+2V_4+4V_5\), and the half-difference is \((24,0,0,-1)=V_6+2V_4+2V_5\). These identities also hold separately on the two order-five conjugacy classes. Character independence over \(\mathbb Q\) establishes the claimed full decomposition; no trivial \(A_5\) constituent appears. For finite group actions on a finite simplicial complex, rational homology of the orbit space is the invariant rational homology, using barycentric subdivision and rational averaging. This proves the quotient claims.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with standard Python 3 from a directory containing the embedded verifier; no external packages or database files are needed. It constructs the labeled graph, enumerates \(5887\) nonempty simplices, independently enumerates \(120\) automorphisms, checks all boundary ranks modulo \(101\), compares two independent homological character algorithms on all automorphisms, and checks the complete rational decomposition. Its last output line must be `icosahedral_matching_symmetry_PASS`. The exact stdout is embedded in `verification_output.txt`. The modular ranks alone would *not* certify an arbitrary infinite-family theorem or integral torsion claim: the rational conclusion also uses the exact Euler characteristic, and the result concerns one finite complex only.

## Relationship to prior work
Shareshian and Wachs studied torsion and representation theory for matching complexes of complete graphs and chessboard complexes (2004); those graphs are not the icosahedral graph. Engström studied independence complexes of claw-free graphs (2005), a broad framework since a matching complex is the independence complex of a line graph; the cited general framework does not evaluate this particular icosahedral symmetry character. Bayer, Jelić Milutinović and Vega (2024) studied matching complexes of outerplanar graphs and conjectured a planar extension, but the icosahedron is not outerplanar. The closest exact previous calculation on this same simplicial complex determines its integral homotopy type \(\bigvee^{64}S^3\vee M(\mathbb Z/3,3)\); it gives the rational dimension and three-torsion but **does not specify the natural action of any graph automorphism on homology**. The new claim is therefore the *full symmetry representation and quotient-invariant conclusions*, not the already known homotopy type. An equivalent unindexed character calculation remains a publication-priority risk.

## Limitations
The result determines a rational representation, not an integral equivariant lattice or the action on the integral \(\mathbb Z/3\) torsion. Quotient homology is rational only and does not identify quotient homotopy types or fundamental groups. The exhaustive programs independently reconstruct the finite input but are not an externally verified formal certificate. The source comparisons cannot rule out all unindexed equivalent work.
