# Full reflection-fixed spaces of three-row grid matching complexes

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge 2\), let \(G_n=P_3\square P_n\) be the simple nearest-neighbor rectangular grid with vertex set \(\{0,1,2\}\times\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\). Let \(X_n=\mathcal M(G_n)\) be its ordinary matching complex (vertices are graph edges; simplices are sets of pairwise endpoint-disjoint edges). Let \(R(r,c)=(2-r,c)\) exchange the outer rows and \(C(r,c)=(r,n-1-c)\) reverse the columns, and set \(K=\langle R,C\rangle\cong C_2\times C_2\).

Define the nonnegative integer
\[
q_n=\begin{cases}n/2,&n\text{ even},\\(n-3)/2,&n\text{ odd}.\end{cases}
\]
Writing \(P_0\) for the graph with no vertices, **the geometric common fixed-point space** has a canonical piecewise-linear triangulation
\[
\boxed{|X_n|^K\cong_{\rm PL}|\operatorname{Ind}(P_{q_n}\sqcup P_{q_n})|.}
\]
In particular, \(|X_3|^K=\varnothing\), whereas for every \(n\ge 2\) with \(q_n\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{|X_n|^K\simeq
\begin{cases}
S^{2q_n/3-1},&q_n\equiv0\pmod3,\\
\{\mathrm{pt}\},&q_n\equiv1\pmod3,\\
S^{(2q_n-1)/3},&q_n\equiv2\pmod3.
\end{cases}}
\]
The first sphere case requires \(q_n\ge3\). Every asserted sphere has reduced integral homology \(\mathbb Z\) only in the indicated degree; the contractible cases have trivial reduced integral homology. Thus the fixed-point spheres have unbounded dimension despite the fact that the ambient spaces \(|X_n|\) are \((n-2)\)-connected for \(n\ge3\) [1].

## Assumptions and scope
These are actual geometric fixed points of the **whole** four-element subgroup generated by both reflections, not homology classes invariant under \(K\), the orbit-space quotient \(|X_n|/K\), or points fixed under only one reflection. The graph is the Cartesian-product grid, not the categorical product. A geometric fixed point of a simplicial action may lie at the barycenter of an orbit of vertices, so taking only individually fixed edges would be incorrect. The PL identification uses normalized barycenters of admissible edge orbits. All homology is integral, and \(S^0\) means two points. We treat \(\operatorname{Ind}(P_0)=\{\emptyset\}\) as the augmented empty-face complex, with empty geometric realization.

## Proof
**Step 1 (fixed-point orbit triangulation).** For a finite simplicial complex \(X\) acted upon simplicially by a finite group \(H\), form one prospective vertex \(b_O=|O|^{-1}\sum_{v\in O}v\) for each orbit \(O\) of vertices that is itself a face of \(X\). Declare a set of such vertices a simplex precisely when the union of the corresponding orbits is a face of \(X\). A point \(x=\sum_v\lambda_v v\in|X|\) is fixed by \(H\) exactly when \(\lambda_v\) is constant on each orbit. Its support is a face, so it has the unique expression \(x=\sum_O t_O b_O\), with \(t_O=|O|\lambda_v\ge0\) and \(\sum_Ot_O=1\). This gives a bijective simplex-wise affine map from the displayed orbit complex to \(|X|^H\). Compactness and the Hausdorff property make it a PL homeomorphism onto the fixed locus.

**Step 2 (all admissible four-element-group edge orbits).** Denote horizontal grid edges by \(h_{r,j}=\{(r,j),(r,j+1)\}\), with \(0\le j\le n-2\), and vertical edges by \(v_{r,j}=\{(r,j),(r+1,j)\}\), with \(r=0,1\). Reflection \(R\) exchanges \(v_{0,j}\) and \(v_{1,j}\), which share \((1,j)\). Therefore no \(K\)-orbit of a vertical edge is a matching, and no such orbit can support a fixed-point vertex.

Every horizontal edge orbit is either of **outer type**, using row \(0\) together with row \(2\), or **middle type**, using only row \(1\). Column reversal maps index \(j\) to \(n-2-j\). If \(n=2m+1\), the orbit at \(j=m-1\) contains adjacent horizontal edges at indices \(m-1,m\), so it is inadmissible in each row type. The remaining permissible indices have representatives \(j=0,\ldots,m-2\): exactly \(q_n=m-1\) orbits per row type. If \(n=2m\), there are \(m-1\) reflected pairs of nonadjacent horizontal edges, indexed \(j=0,\ldots,m-2\), and one central singleton-index orbit at \(j=m-1\); thus \(q_n=m\) orbits per row type. Each of the stated orbits individually consists of mutually disjoint grid edges, including the central outer-type orbit (two edges on different rows).

**Step 3 (the orbit-conflict graph).** Two admissible edge orbits are compatible if and only if their unions are still matchings. Distinct orbit types never conflict because one uses rows \(0,2\) and the other uses row \(1\). Within either type, orbits with representatives \(i,j\in\{0,\ldots,q_n-1\}\) conflict exactly when \(|i-j|=1\): this follows by inspecting the adjacent left-half horizontal edges and their reversed counterparts, including the central singleton-index orbit for even width. Therefore the graph of incompatible admissible edge orbits is exactly the disjoint union \(P_{q_n}\sqcup P_{q_n}\). The orbit triangulation from Step 1 is its independence complex, proving the claimed PL homeomorphism. If \(n=3\), then \(q_n=0\) and no admissible orbit exists, so the fixed space is empty.

**Step 4 (integral homotopy classification of path independence complexes).** Put \(I_q=\operatorname{Ind}(P_q)\). The initial cases are \(|I_1|\simeq\{\mathrm{pt}\}\), \(|I_2|\simeq S^0\), and \(|I_3|\simeq S^0\). For \(q\ge4\), cover \(I_q\) by its induced subcomplex on all vertices except vertex \(2\), which is a cone at vertex \(1\), and the closed simplicial star of vertex \(2\), another cone. Their intersection is the independence complex on vertices \(4,\ldots,q\), namely \(I_{q-3}\). Because both pieces are contractible and their inclusions form a simplicial-cofibration cover, the union has homotopy type \(\Sigma|I_{q-3}|\). Induction gives
\[
|I_q|\simeq\begin{cases}
S^{k-1},&q=3k\text{ or }q=3k-1,\quad k\ge1,\\
\{\mathrm{pt}\},&q=3k+1,\quad k\ge0.
\end{cases}
\]
(The case \(q=0\) has empty realization and is treated separately.) Finally, independence complexes take graph disjoint unions to simplicial joins; and \(S^d*S^d\cong S^{2d+1}\). The join of a nonempty contractible complex with a nonempty complex is contractible. Substituting the three residue classes gives the asserted homotopy types and integral homology.

## Verification
The standalone `verify_fixed.py` independently constructs each grid's actual graph edges, computes the orbits generated by both vertex reflections, discards edge orbits that fail the matching condition, and tests every pair of surviving orbits against the predicted disjoint-path conflict graph. It passed all widths \(2\le n\le90\), including \(6,119\) orbit classes; for \(2\le n\le13\) it also independently compared every one of \(6,825\) candidate orbit-subsets to the predicted simplicial faces. For \(2\le n\le12\), it constructs all faces and their mod-two simplicial boundary matrices and checks reduced homology against the formula. Run `python3 verify_fixed.py` and expect `VERIFY_OK`. These are bounded computations, not a substitute for Steps 1--4's proof for all widths or for integral homology.

## Relationship to prior work
Chauhan–Sharma–Shukla [1] study nonequivariant topology of \(\mathcal M(P_3\square P_n)\), prove its \((n-2)\)-connectivity and formulate an ambient wedge-of-spheres conjecture. Its full accessible preprint was inspected, including the main theorems and concluding discussion; it contains no symmetry-fixed-space classification. The published reflection-character calculation [2] uses the two independent horizontal-row orbit families to compute a **signed count** for row reversal, and therefore already supplies an important part of the underlying orbit observation; the latter is not claimed as a new contribution. The full-group fixed-space theorem additionally imposes **both** reflections, classifies column-reversal orbits and their compatibility, obtains a PL model for the common fixed-point set, and determines its integral homotopy type, none of which follows from the scalar row/column Lefschetz numbers. The full-group quotient-Euler calculation [3] determines a quotient's rational Euler characteristic using a half-turn character; it does not determine the common geometric fixed locus. The path-complex recurrence in Step 4 is classical and is proved here for completeness, not presented as independently novel.

## Limitations
The formula concerns only the subgroup generated by row reversal and column reversal; it does not classify arbitrary actions or the homotopy of the ambient quotient. It does not establish the open ambient wedge conjecture. The claim is structural rather than a new result about path independence complexes themselves. Highly relevant published equivariant results [2,3] make the deduction relatively short, and a previously unnoticed statement or immediate published corollary giving the same full fixed-point space remains a residual originality risk. The accompanying finite checks do not establish infinite assertions. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
