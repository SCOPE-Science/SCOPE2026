# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The free translation action on the two-dimensional progression complex gives a regular prime-degree covering; the quotient two-cell words are exactly \(e_d^2=e_{2d}\). Elimination along signed-doubling cycles leaves \(q_p\) cyclic factors of order \(N_p\). The monodromy surjects on each factor, and the Bass–Serre quotient graph has \(p+q_p\) vertices, \(pq_p\) edges and cyclic stabilizers of order \(m_p\). Abelianization and Euler characteristic give every integral homology group. The separate exact Smith-normal-form checker agrees on twelve primes, including nontrivial torsion and nonzero free first homology. Boundary restriction: no claim about composite moduli or higher homotopy.

## Originality — PASS, with risks
The 2016 linear van der Waerden facet complex is not the cyclic prime complex; the 2017 shellability paper still addresses the linear object. Liber's accessible full 2025 cyclic-hypergraph paper studies **independent subsets**, chromatic bounds and cyclic van der Waerden numbers, not the facet complex's fundamental group or integral homology. The relevant historical 2011 cyclic Ramsey construction defines the underlying hypergraph rather than these topological invariants. Searches for cyclic progression facet complexes, signed-doubling quotient presentations, finite cyclic torsion, and potential general covering implications produced no covering primary result. Residual risk: unindexed or equivalently phrased work could have established the same group calculation, which search absence cannot disprove.

## Value — PASS
The arithmetic invariant measuring powers of two modulo a prime precisely controls free-product factors, torsion orders, and integral Betti numbers in a canonical topology built from modular progressions. The previously studied non-wrapping facet complex and cyclic independence-number problem do not provide these invariants. Cases \(p=11\) and \(p=41\) exhibit respectively pure finite and mixed free/finite fundamental groups, an intrinsic distinction that an ordinary face-vector census alone misses.
