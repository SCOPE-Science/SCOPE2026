# Geometric fixed-point homotopy types for every circular-ladder symmetry

## Finding
For an integer \(n\ge5\), let \(G_n=C_n\square K_2\), with vertices \((i,j)\), where \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), \(j\in\{0,1\}\). Put \(X_n=\operatorname{Ind}(G_n)\), and consider its natural \(D_n\times C_2\)-action generated by
\[
r(i,j)=(i+1,j),\qquad s(i,j)=(-i,j),\qquad t(i,j)=(i,1-j).
\]
For every element \(g\) of this group, its **geometric realization's actual fixed-point space** \(|X_n|^{\langle g\rangle}\) has the homotopy type specified below. This is not a statement merely about fixed simplices, homology characters, or quotient spaces.

Write \(L_k=P_k\square K_2\) for the \(k\)-rung *open ladder* (\(k\ge1\)); write \(M_{2k}\) for the \(2k\)-vertex Möbius ladder (\(k\ge2\)), with \(M_2=K_2\) by convention. In the rotational cases set \(d=\gcd(n,a)\), interpreting \(\gcd(n,0)=n\), and \(\ell=n/d\).

**(A) Rotations without layer exchange.** For \(g=r^a\), the fixed-point space is empty if \(d=1\); otherwise it is homeomorphic to the realization of the independence complex of \(C_d\square K_2\), where \(C_2=K_2\). This includes the identity \(a=0\) as a boundary consistency check.

**(B) Rotations with layer exchange.** For \(g=r^at\), the fixed-point space is empty if \(\ell\) is odd. If \(\ell\) is even it is homeomorphic to \(|\operatorname{Ind}(M_{2d})|\). In particular, when \(d=1\) this is the two-point space \(S^0\), and when \(d=2\) this is four points, equivalently \(\bigvee^3S^0\).

**(C) Reflections, with or without layer exchange.** For \(g=r^ast^c\), with \(c\in\{0,1\}\), the fixed-point space is always a single sphere
\[
|X_n|^{\langle g\rangle}\simeq S^{\lceil k/2\rceil-1},
\]
where \(k\) is specified by the following complete table:

| Width and symmetry | \(k\) |
| --- | --- |
| \(n=2m+1\) odd, all \(a,c\) | \(m\) |
| \(n=2m\) even, \(a\) even, \(c=0\) | \(m+1\) |
| \(n=2m\) even, \(a\) odd, \(c=0\) | \(m-2\) |
| \(n=2m\) even, \(a\) even, \(c=1\) | \(m-1\) |
| \(n=2m\) even, \(a\) odd, \(c=1\) | \(m\) |

These exhaust all \(4n\) elements of the specified symmetry group. For cases (A) and (B), the complete homotopy type is obtained by the explicit formulas for the circular and Möbius ladder independence complexes in Agarwal--Basak, arXiv:2608.30601v2; the small conventions \(C_2=K_2\) and \(M_2=K_2\) are handled directly. No unproved classification of graph independence complexes is needed.

**Character-invisible distinction.** At \(n=12\), the two nonconjugate reflections \(s\) and \(rs\) have fixed-point spaces homotopy equivalent to \(S^3\) and \(S^1\), respectively. Both have Euler characteristic zero, hence the same fixed-point Lefschetz number; the rational homology character alone does not determine their geometric fixed-point homotopy types.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite and simple. An independence complex has faces the independent vertex sets of its graph; its geometric realization has no point corresponding to the empty face. The cyclic graph \(C_n\), for \(n\ge3\), is the ordinary simple cycle. For \(d=2\), the quotient's cyclic adjacency is the single edge of \(K_2\). The claimed action is the explicit natural \(D_n\times C_2\) subgroup, not any extra automorphisms of exceptional prisms. The result is valid uniformly for every \(n\ge5\) and every indicated group element, including the empty fixed-space cases. A zero-dimensional wedge \(\bigvee^pS^0\) means a discrete set of \(p+1\) points.

The classification uses the existing full homotopy theorems for \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_d\square K_2)\) and \(\operatorname{Ind}(M_{2d})\). Its new content is the uniform *geometric fixed-point* reduction and all-conjugacy-class orbit-graph identification, especially the combined reflection/layer-swap cases; the known nonequivariant classifications are not being relabeled as new results.

## Proof
**1. Orbit-conflict lemma with the correct geometric meaning.** Let a finite cyclic group \(H=\langle g\rangle\) act on a finite simple graph \(G\). Partition \(V(G)\) into \(H\)-orbits. Discard each orbit containing an internal graph edge. Let \(Q_g\) have the remaining orbits as vertices, with two joined when some edge of \(G\) joins their constituent vertices. A geometric point of \(|\operatorname{Ind}(G)|\) is fixed by \(g\) precisely when its barycentric coordinates are constant along each vertex orbit. An orbit with an internal edge cannot have positive coordinate, and two conflicting orbits cannot simultaneously have positive coordinates. Writing the total barycentric weight on each permitted orbit gives a piecewise-linear homeomorphism
\[
|\operatorname{Ind}(G)|^{H}\cong |\operatorname{Ind}(Q_g)|.
\tag{1}
\]
The map sends a vertex of \(Q_g\) to the barycenter of the corresponding orbit simplex, and its inverse reads off total orbit weights. This proves an actual fixed-point statement and handles the empty complex with no permitted orbits.

**2. Pure rotations.** The group generated by \(r^a\) shifts each layer by multiples of \(d=\gcd(n,a)\). If \(d=1\), each of its two full-layer orbits contains a horizontal edge, so no vertex remains. If \(d\ge2\), the orbits are precisely the two layers indexed by residues modulo \(d\). Horizontal edge conflicts form \(C_d\) in each layer and rung conflicts connect equal residues across the two layers. For \(d=2\) there is one horizontal edge per layer, not parallel edges. Equation (1) gives (A).

**3. Layer-swapping rotations.** On a column orbit of length \(\ell=n/d\), the transformation \(r^at\) applies a row swap at every step. If \(\ell\) is odd, its \(\ell\)-th power fixes column positions but exchanges the two rows, so every vertex orbit contains a rung edge. The fixed space is therefore empty. If \(\ell\) is even, writing \(a=dh\) gives \(\gcd(h,\ell)=1\), hence \(h\) is odd. An odd power of the transformation advances exactly \(d\) columns and exchanges rows; consequently the orbit identification is \((i+d,j)\sim(i,1-j)\). Select \(d\) consecutive representative columns. Horizontal conflicts join neighboring same-row representatives, except across the seam, where they interchange rows; vertical rung conflicts join each column's two representatives. These are exactly the twisted cyclic sides and rungs of \(M_{2d}\). For \(d=1\), the quotient is \(K_2\); for \(d=2\), it is \(K_4\). Equation (1) proves (B).

**4. Reflections: endpoints account for every difference.** Rotating the axis conjugates the reflection exponent \(a\) by an even integer, so for odd \(n\) all \(a\) are equivalent; for even \(n\) it suffices to analyze the two parities \(a=0,1\), each with \(c=0,1\). A reflection orbit consists of one fixed column or one pair of columns. With \(c=0\), an orbit that straddles a reflection axis through a horizontal edge is forbidden (its two same-row vertices are adjacent). With \(c=1\), an orbit at a fixed column is forbidden (it is a rung edge), while paired columns with the row reversal remain independent. Every surviving paired column contributes one upper and one lower orbit vertex, every surviving fixed column contributes two singleton orbit vertices, and the quotient conflicts consist exactly of vertical rungs and horizontal edges between consecutive surviving column positions. These observations give the explicit orbit lists:

- If \(n=2m+1\): with \(c=0\), retain the fixed column and the \(m-1\) paired columns away from the opposite edge-axis; with \(c=1\), discard the fixed column and retain all \(m\) paired columns. Either produces \(L_m\).
- If \(n=2m\) and \(a\) is even: with \(c=0\), retain both fixed columns and all \(m-1\) paired columns, giving \(L_{m+1}\); with \(c=1\), discard both fixed columns and retain the \(m-1\) pairs, giving \(L_{m-1}\).
- If \(n=2m\) and \(a\) is odd: with \(c=0\), discard the two pairs meeting the opposite edge-axes, leaving \(m-2\) pairs, giving \(L_{m-2}\); with \(c=1\), all \(m\) paired columns survive, giving \(L_m\).

The quotient has no extra edges: any original edge is a rung or a unit horizontal step, and both have already been represented. Equation (1) therefore gives the five graph identifications in (C).

**5. Homotopy type of an open ladder.** For \(k=1,2\), the complex \(\operatorname{Ind}(L_k)\) has the homotopy type \(S^0\). For \(k\ge3\), label the first three columns' vertices \(u_i,v_i\) (upper/lower). The graph neighborhoods satisfy \(N(v_1)=\{u_1,v_2\}\subset N(u_2)=\{u_1,v_2,u_3\}\). The standard independence-complex fold removes \(u_2\) without changing homotopy type. In the remaining graph, \(N(u_1)=\{v_1\}\subset N(v_2)=\{v_1,v_3\}\); a second fold removes \(v_2\). The graph is now the disjoint union of the rung \(K_2\) at column one and \(L_{k-2}\), so the independence complex is a join with \(S^0\), hence a suspension. Thus
\[
\operatorname{Ind}(L_k)\simeq \Sigma\operatorname{Ind}(L_{k-2})
\simeq S^{\lceil k/2\rceil-1}.
\tag{2}
\]
Combining with step 4 proves the sphere formula in (C). The named existing classifications give the exact homotopy types in (A)--(B), completing the proof.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone Python 3 program `verify.py` uses only the standard library and can be executed using `python3 verify.py`. It independently builds each prism graph, computes the complete vertex-orbit conflict graph from the transformation, and checks exact graph isomorphism against the asserted path, circular, Möbius or empty graph. All \(800\) group elements for widths \(5\le n\le20\) passed. Independent exact augmented mod-two simplicial boundary elimination agrees with the expected homology of open ladders for \(1\le k\le6\), Möbius ladders for \(1\le d\le5\), and circular ladders for \(2\le d\le5\). The actual completion marker is `VERIFY_OK: orbit_isomorphisms=800, ladder_mod2=6, mobius_mod2=5, circular_mod2=4`. These finite tests check endpoint and degeneracy cases but do not stand in for the uniform proof or the cited existing infinite-family homotopy results.

## Relationship to prior work
Agarwal and Basak's arXiv:2608.30601v2 supplies the nonequivariant homotopy types of the Möbius and circular ladders (Main Theorems 1--2; Theorems 12 and 15); its first public version, dated 2026-08-31, treated Möbius ladders, while the first source version covering circular ladders was publicly posted on 2026-09-14. Neither version states a classification of geometric fixed-point spaces under all symmetry elements.

The separate published *Complete dihedral and layer-swap homology representations of circular ladders* determines the complete rational symmetry representation using orbit counting and, for the layer-preserving reflection generator, explicitly identifies an open-ladder quotient. That covers part of the combinatorial setup here. It does **not** identify the actual homotopy type of the fixed-point space, classify all mixed reflection/layer-swap quotients, or state the complete fixed-locus theorem. Its signed Lefschetz counts cannot distinguish the \(S^3\) and \(S^1\) fixed spaces of two reflections at width twelve. Another published result on Möbius ladders determines rational dihedral homology but not these geometric fixed points. The present theorem treats the more delicate orbit-facet geometry and supplies an all-elements topological classification, rather than merely recalculating known character values.

## Limitations
The statement is restricted to the explicit natural \(D_n\times C_2\) action and \(n\ge5\); it does not address extra graph automorphisms in exceptional cases or claim an equivariant wedge splitting of the ambient complex. The large-family non-equivariant homotopy formulas invoked in steps 2--3 are prior results, not independently re-proved here. Their correctness for arbitrary widths is a premise of this corollary-like combination; the newly proved orbit identities and reflection folding argument are independent. The result does not claim rational-character novelty. Equivalent unindexed geometric fixed-space work remains a publication-priority risk.
