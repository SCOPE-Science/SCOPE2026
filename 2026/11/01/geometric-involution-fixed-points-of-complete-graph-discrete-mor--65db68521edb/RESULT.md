# Geometric involution fixed points of complete-graph discrete Morse complexes

## Finding

Let \(K_n\) be the complete graph on \(n\) labeled vertices, regarded as a one-dimensional simplicial complex, and let \(\mathfrak M(K_n)\) denote its **ordinary** Chari--Joswig discrete Morse matching complex. Its vertices are the incident vertex--edge pairs of \(K_n\), and its faces are the acyclic Hasse-diagram matchings. Permutations of graph vertices induce simplicial automorphisms.

**Theorem.** Let \(\sigma\in S_n\) be a nonidentity involution with exactly \(f\ge0\) fixed vertices and \(t\ge1\) disjoint transpositions, so \(n=f+2t\). Write \(Y_{f,t}=|\mathfrak M(K_n)|^{\langle\sigma\rangle}\) for the actual **geometric** fixed-point space. Then

\[
Y_{f,t}\simeq\begin{cases}
\varnothing,&f=0,\ t=1,\\
\ast,& f=1,\\
\displaystyle\bigvee^{(2t-1)^{t-1}}S^{t-2},&f=0,\ t\ge2,\\
\displaystyle\bigvee^{(f-1)^f(f+2t-1)^{t-1}}S^{f+t-2},&f\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]

The nonempty fixed-point complexes admit **pure shellable triangulations**. In particular their reduced integral homology is torsion-free and concentrated in the single displayed dimension; in the contractible case it vanishes. A precise simplicial model and face enumerator are supplied below.

Examples: \(Y_{0,2}\) consists of four isolated points; \(Y_{0,3}\simeq\bigvee^{25}S^1\); \(Y_{1,3}\) is contractible; \(Y_{2,2}\simeq\bigvee^5 S^2\); and \(Y_{3,2}\simeq\bigvee^{48}S^3\).

## Assumptions and scope

Here \(\mathfrak M(K_n)\) is the **ordinary** complex of all discrete Morse matchings of the graph as a one-dimensional simplicial complex, not the separate strong Morse complex, not the ordinary graph-edge matching complex, and not a quotient under a group action. A point in the geometric realization is fixed when its barycentric coordinates are invariant under \(\sigma\), even if the containing simplex is not fixed pointwise. The integral homotopy classification concerns the full realization of \(\mathfrak M(K_n)\) under the subgroup generated by exactly one nontrivial involution. The trivial permutation is outside scope.

For the proof, identify a vertex--edge pair \((v,\{v,w\})\) with the arrow \(w\to v\). This reverses the more usual arrow \(v\to w\) pointing away from the matched vertex: reversal is a simplicial and permutation-equivariant identification, because it preserves acyclicity and exchanges the conditions of at most one outgoing arrow and at most one incoming arrow. Thus faces can be viewed as rooted directed forests whose arrows point **away from each root**, with at most one arrow entering any vertex and no directed cycle.

## Proof

**1. Geometric fixed points and the orbit-barycenter triangulation.** For any finite simplicial complex carrying a simplicial involution, a fixed geometric point has equal barycentric coordinates on the vertices of each orbit of the involution. Consequently its fixed locus is PL homeomorphic to the complex whose vertices are nonempty involution-orbits of simplex vertices that themselves span faces, and whose faces are sets of such orbits with simultaneously admissible union. This uses all setwise invariant simplices; merely retaining individually fixed vertices would be incorrect.

Let the graph-label orbits of \(\sigma\) be \(f\) singleton blocks \(F_1,\ldots,F_f\) and \(t\) doubleton blocks \(P_1,\ldots,P_t\). An orbit of directed arrows is admissible in the inward-degree-at-most-one forest model exactly in the following cases:

* \(F_i\to F_j\) for \(i\ne j\): one arrow orbit, of size one.
* \(F_i\to P_j\): one arrow orbit consisting of the two arrows from the fixed source to both members of the paired target.
* \(P_i\to P_j\) for \(i\ne j\): exactly **two** arrow orbits, the aligned and crossed bijections between the two pairs. Each consists of two arrows.

No arrow orbit \(P_i\to F_j\) is admissible, since it contains two incoming arrows to the same fixed target. An arrow orbit within a paired block gives a two-cycle, and a singleton cannot point to itself. This classification exhausts all arrow orbits.

The union of admissible arrow orbits is a forest if and only if the resulting **uncolored** quotient directed graph is a rooted directed forest with at most one arrow entering each block. One direction follows because a directed cycle upstairs projects to a closed directed walk downstairs. Conversely, a quotient directed cycle cannot mix singleton and paired blocks (there is no arrow from a paired block into a singleton). A singleton-block cycle is already a cycle upstairs. A paired-block cycle composes bijections of two-element sets and hence returns to its starting element after at most two circuits, producing an upstairs cycle. Thus the quotient criterion is exact, including both allowed parallel arrow colors.

Therefore the fixed locus is PL homeomorphic to the directed-forest complex \(\Delta(D_{f,t})\) of a directed **multigraph** on \(f+t\) blocks, with multiplicity one on arrows out of a singleton into any other block, multiplicity two on arrows from one paired block to another paired block, and no other arrows.

**2. Shellability and purity.** Forget the distinction between the two parallel colors on each paired-to-paired arrow to obtain a simple directed graph \(D^0_{f,t}\). If \(f\ge1\), any singleton block is a complete source: it has an arrow to every other block. If \(f=0,t\ge2\), every paired block is a complete source. The complete-source shellability theorem for directed-forest complexes (Kozlov, as stated and proved in Jojić [3], Section 2) shows that \(\Delta(D^0_{f,t})\) is shellable, and that its maximal faces are directed spanning trees with exactly \(f+t-1\) arrows. Restoring the two parallel colors is the vertex-expansion construction of Moradi and Khosh-Ahang [4, Definition 2.1]. The expansion replaces each paired-to-paired arrow vertex with two mutually exclusive clones, independently for each such vertex. Theorem 2.12 of [4] proves that expansion preserves shellability; it also preserves facet sizes. Thus \(\Delta(D_{f,t})\) is a **pure shellable** complex of dimension \(d=f+t-2\). The usual shelling attachment argument then shows that it has the homotopy type of a bouquet of \(d\)-spheres (possibly a contractible bouquet with zero spheres). In the one remaining case \(f=0,t=1\), there are no admissible arrow orbits and the geometric fixed space is empty.

**3. All faces from a determinant.** Let \(\Phi_{f,t}(z)=\sum_{k\ge0}a_kz^k\), with \(a_k\) the number of orbit-forest faces containing exactly \(k\) directed arrow-orbits and \(a_0=1\). The matrix--forest theorem for directed multigraphs [5, Theorem 3] gives \(\Phi_{f,t}(z)=\det(I+zL)\), where \(L\) has row index equal to the target block, its diagonal equal to the total number of arrows *entering* that block, and its off-diagonal entry the negative number of arrows entering from the indicated source.

For \(f\ge1\), ordering the singleton blocks first and the paired blocks second yields

\[
L=\begin{pmatrix}
fI_f-J_f&0\\
-J_{t\times f}&(f+2t)I_t-2J_t
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The singleton block has eigenvalues \(0\) once and \(f\) with multiplicity \(f-1\). The paired block has eigenvalue \(f\) once and \(f+2t\) with multiplicity \(t-1\). Consequently

\[
\boxed{\Phi_{f,t}(z)=(1+fz)^f(1+(f+2t)z)^{t-1}\quad(f\ge1).}
\]

For \(f=0,t\ge1\), the Laplacian is \(L=2tI_t-2J_t\), giving

\[
\boxed{\Phi_{0,t}(z)=(1+2tz)^{t-1}.}
\]

The weight variable counts edges, not graph vertices, and the empty face is counted once. This determines the entire face vector, not just Euler characteristic.

**4. From Euler characteristic to integral homotopy.** The reduced Euler characteristic of a nonempty simplicial complex with face polynomial \(\Phi\) is \(\widetilde\chi=-\Phi(-1)\). Since the complex is pure shellable of dimension \(d=f+t-2\), all reduced integral homology, if nonzero, is concentrated in dimension \(d\), so its rank is \((-1)^d\widetilde\chi=(-1)^{d+1}\Phi(-1)\). Substituting the two products gives precisely \((2t-1)^{t-1}\) for \(f=0,t\ge2\) and \((f-1)^f(f+2t-1)^{t-1}\) for \(f\ge1\). The case \(f=1\) gives rank zero and hence contractibility by shellability. The theorem follows in every stated case. The coefficients are integers and shellability gives genuine integral sphere wedges, not merely rational homology computations.

## Verification

The standalone file `verify.py` constructs the full graph-label involution, enumerates orbits of its original directed arrows, tests admissibility by checking **original** incoming degrees and directed cycles, and computes exact mod-two simplicial boundary ranks of the resulting orbit-barycenter complex. It does not take the quotient classification or determinant formula as input when building faces. It compares the resulting Betti numbers and Euler characteristic against the theorem for ten parameter pairs with \(f+t\ge2\), and separately checks the empty case \((f,t)=(0,1)\). The output `VERIFY_OK` was obtained with Python 3 using only its standard library. In particular the computation detects ranks \(3,25,5,48\) for \((f,t)=(0,2),(0,3),(2,2),(3,2)\), respectively, in the predicted degrees. The shellability and determinant arguments are symbolic and prove the unrestricted parameter statement independently of finite experiments.

To reproduce, place the included `verify.py` next to this text and execute `python3 verify.py`.

## Relationship to prior work

Chari and Joswig [2, Proposition 3.1] identify the ordinary discrete Morse complex of a graph with its rooted-forest complex; this identification is prior knowledge. The September 2026 preprint [1] renews study of Morse matching complexes, but primarily treats **strong** discrete Morse matchings, a different complex. Its introductory discussion and the graph and simplex sections do not classify geometric involution fixed spaces of \(\mathfrak M(K_n)\). The shellability theorem for a directed graph with a complete source [3], expansion-shellability theorem [4], and directed matrix--forest theorem [5] are used as established results; none alone supplies the orbit geometry and integral homotopy classification above.

Most importantly, the preexisting *Equivariant matrix--forest character formula for graph Morse complexes* [6] determines individual automorphism **chain characters** and an alternating homological Lefschetz number. Its formula, specialized to the involution, covers the Euler-characteristic part of this computation, and **that part is not claimed as original**. The existing result expressly does not determine geometric fixed-space homotopy types. The additional geometric orbit complex, complete-source shellability after parallel-edge expansion, and exact integral sphere-wedge classification are the contribution here.

A previously obtained geometric fixed-point result for the same directed-forest complex under a **product of independent transitive block actions** is also distinct: it fixes each pair independently, whereas the present involution swaps all pairs **simultaneously**. Its stronger symmetry condition excludes paired-to-paired arrow orbits and yields different fixed spaces. For example, with no fixed graph vertices and at least two pairs, the product-action fixed space is empty, while the present diagonal involution fixed space is a nonempty sphere bouquet.

## Limitations

The classification covers involutions of the complete graph and their individual geometric fixed loci, not arbitrary permutations or joint fixed spaces of arbitrary subgroups. It does not determine the maps among fixed loci or the full equivariant homotopy type. It does not extend to the strong discrete Morse complex without a separate argument. The Euler polynomial is within the coverage of previously known equivariant character formulas; novelty concerns the stronger **integral homotopy type**. Although the most relevant accessible primary sources and indexed results were compared, an unindexed statement of the same fixed-locus homotopy theorem is an unresolved originality risk. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
