# Geometric subgroup fixed spaces and normalizer characters of uniform multiclique complexes

## Finding
For integers \(n,q\ge2\), let \(X_{n,q}\) be the geometric multiclique complex of the complete loopless multigraph with \(q\) distinct parallel edges between each pair of its \(n\) vertices. Its nonempty cells are indexed by pairs \((S,\kappa)\), where \(\varnothing\ne S\subseteq[n]\) and \(\kappa:\binom S2\to[q]\); restriction defines their incidences. The vertex-permutation group \(S_n\) acts by relabeling vertices without changing the parallel-edge colors.

**Theorem (all subgroup fixed spaces and their residual symmetry).** For any subgroup \(H\le S_n\), let \(V_1,\ldots,V_c\) be its orbits on \([n]\), with \(c\ge1\). Let \(\mathcal E_H\) be the set of \(H\)-orbits on unordered pairs of distinct original vertices. Each \(E\in\mathcal E_H\) has a support \(s(E)\subseteq[c]\) of size one or two, consisting of the orbit indices containing the endpoints. For \(J\subseteq[c]\), put
\[
 e_H(J)=\big|\{E\in\mathcal E_H:s(E)\subseteq J\}\big|.
\]
Then the actual geometric fixed-point set is homotopy equivalent, **integrally**, to
\[
 \boxed{X_{n,q}^{H}\simeq\bigvee^{B_H(q)}S^{c-1},\qquad
 B_H(q)=(-1)^c\sum_{J\subseteq[c]}(-1)^{|J|}q^{e_H(J)}.}
\]
The zero-fold wedge is a point. When \(c=1\), the statement is interpreted literally: the fixed space has exactly \(q^{e_H([c])}\) isolated points, and \(B_H(q)=q^{e_H([c])}-1\). More strongly, the fixed space has an explicit triangulation as a **homotopically Cohen–Macaulay simplicial poset of dimension \(c-1\)**.

The normalizer \(N_{S_n}(H)\) preserves this fixed space, with its action factoring through \(N_{S_n}(H)/H\). Given \(g\in N_{S_n}(H)\), let \(T_1,\ldots,T_d\) be the cycles of its induced permutation of the \(c\) orbit indices. If \(K\subseteq[d]\), write \(J_K=\bigcup_{i\in K}T_i\) and let \(a_{H,g}(K)\) be the number of cycles of the induced permutation of \(\mathcal E_H\) **entirely supported** on \(J_K\). The full rational top-homology character is
\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{tr}\big(g\mid\widetilde H_{c-1}(X_{n,q}^{H};\mathbb Q)\big)
 =(-1)^c\sum_{K\subseteq[d]}(-1)^{|K|}q^{a_{H,g}(K)}.}
\]
For \(c=1\), this is the character of reduced degree-zero homology. There is no reduced homology in other degrees and no integral torsion.

For illustration, the cyclic rotation of four vertices has four fixed points when \(q=2\), while the full symmetric group has two: these are wedges of three and one zero-spheres. The subgroup generated by \((12)(34)\) in \(S_4\) has a fixed space homotopy equivalent to a wedge of thirteen circles for \(q=2\). For the five-cycle subgroup in \(S_5\), the fixed space is four isolated points at \(q=2\), but the normalizing multiplication-by-two permutation has trace \(1\), not \(3\), on reduced degree-zero homology. Hence the topological classification and the residual normalizer action contain distinct information.

## Assumptions and scope
All groups are finite because they lie in \(S_n\). Edge labels are fixed pointwise under the permutation group; no simultaneous color permutation is included. The geometric fixed-point space means fixed points in the **realization**, not the combinatorial quotient and not the collection of pointwise fixed original vertices. A cell corresponds to a colored copy of an abstract simplex. Every face restriction remembers the colors on the retained unordered pairs. The normalizer formula uses the natural orientation associated to an ordering of the \(H\)-orbits; final traces are independent of this order.

## Proof
**1. Identify geometric fixed cells without losing orbit barycenters.** Barycentrically subdivide the cellular simplicial poset \(\mathcal P_{n,q}\). Its vertices are nonempty colored faces, and its simplices are strict chains of such faces. The group action preserves the rank \(|S|\) of each colored face. A group-invariant simplex in this subdivision therefore fixes each of its vertices individually, because a chain contains at most one face of each rank. On every simplicial simplex, the action consequently has no internal vertex permutation. Therefore the actual fixed-point set is the realization of the order complex of the fixed-face subposet \(\mathcal P_{n,q}^{H}\); no point that is geometrically fixed is omitted.

An original face \((S,\kappa)\) is fixed by the entire subgroup precisely when \(S\) is a union of complete vertex orbits \(V_j\) and \(\kappa\) is constant on every \(H\)-orbit of unordered pairs lying inside \(S\). Put \(S=\bigcup_{j\in J}V_j\). Thus above each nonempty \(J\subseteq[c]\) lie exactly \(q^{e_H(J)}\) fixed faces. Their restrictions to \(J'\subseteq J\) simply forget those labels whose edge-orbit support is not contained in \(J'\). In particular, the fixed-face poset is **isomorphic**, as an ordered set, to the generalized inflation of the face poset of the \((c-1)\)-simplex with fibers
\[
 D_H(J)=[q]^{\{E\in\mathcal E_H:s(E)\subseteq J\}}.
\]
This is a *model of the fixed space*, rather than a guess based on Euler characteristics. For each \(J\subseteq J'\), all restriction maps \(D_H(J')\to D_H(J)\) are coordinate projections and are surjective. The associated finite-set sheaf is inhabited and flabby: any coherent partial assignment extends by independently choosing labels for the unassigned edge orbits.

**2. Apply the full-dimensional inflation theorem and count spheres.** Ayzenberg–Khoroshavkina's Theorem 5.1 applies to this inhabited flabby inflation of a simplex and gives a wedge of \((c-1)\)-spheres. Their Theorem 5.3 establishes the stronger homotopically Cohen–Macaulay property. The original inflation is a finite regular CW complex with \(q^{e_H(J)}\) faces of dimension \(|J|-1\) above each nonempty \(J\). Its reduced Euler characteristic therefore equals
\[
 -\sum_{J\subseteq[c]}(-1)^{|J|}q^{e_H(J)}.
\]
Comparing with the reduced Euler characteristic of a wedge of \((c-1)\)-spheres gives exactly \(B_H(q)\). This proves nonnegativity, the integral homotopy classification and torsion-freeness. For \(c=1\), the inflation consists of \(q^{e_H([c])}\) vertices and the statement is immediate.

**3. Determine the normalizer character.** If \(g\in N_{S_n}(H)\), it permutes both the vertex-orbit indices and the \(H\)-edge-orbit set, preserving edge-orbit supports. Hence it induces an automorphism of the explicit inflated CW model. A cell above \(J\subseteq[c]\) can be fixed setwise by \(g\) only if \(J\) is a union of cycles of the orbit-index permutation. Once \(J\) is selected, invariant color assignments correspond bijectively to assignments constant on each cycle of \(g\) acting on the edge orbits supported within \(J\). Therefore the number of fixed cells above \(J\) is \(q^{a_{H,g}(K)}\). The map's local orientation degree on a fixed cell is the sign of the induced permutation of \(J\); the color-coordinate choices carry no extra orientation. If \(J\) is a union of \(K\) cycles, \((-1)^{|J|-1}\operatorname{sgn}(g|_J)=(-1)^{|K|-1}\). The reduced cellular Lefschetz number (including augmentation \(-1\)) is consequently
\[
 -\sum_{K\subseteq[d]}(-1)^{|K|}q^{a_{H,g}(K)}.
\]
By the Hopf trace formula and the already proved concentration of reduced rational homology in degree \(c-1\), this equals \((-1)^{c-1}\) times the claimed homological trace. Rearranging proves the formula. Elements of \(H\) fix all points of the geometric fixed locus and hence act trivially on it; the normalizer action thus factors through the quotient.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. It first constructs the original subgroup closure on vertices and directly enumerates every colored face invariant under every subgroup element; independently it computes the vertex- and edge-orbit inflation model and compares the exact cells. It computes simplicial-poset cellular boundary ranks over \(\mathbb F_2\), checks the predicted concentrated Betti ranks, and directly calculates signed traces of normalizer elements on the cellular chains. The separate orbit-cycle trace formula is checked against these direct traces. Executing `python3 verify.py` produced `VERIFY_OK cases= 41 invariant_cells= 3014 normalizer_characters= 218`. These are bounded tests and are **not** a proof of the unrestricted theorem, which depends on the preceding structural identification and the cited general inflation theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Ayzenberg and Rukhovich (2020 preprint) established the nonequivariant topology of complete-multigraph clique complexes and general simplex inflations. Ayzenberg and Khoroshavkina (2026) proved the inhabited-flabby inflation wedge theorem and homotopically Cohen–Macaulay preservation; both are used directly, and neither is claimed as a new theorem here. The contribution is the identification of **actual geometric fixed spaces for every permutation subgroup** with their orbit-support inflations, and the simultaneous computation of all their **normalizer characters**.

A previously published result entitled *Joint vertex-and-edge-label homology characters of uniform multiclique complexes* determines the ambient rational \(S_n\times S_q\) character. In particular, for a **cyclic** subgroup generated by one permutation, its Lefschetz character already constrains the numerical fixed-space Euler characteristic and, using the present sphere-concentration theorem, yields the same numerical sphere count. Thus the cyclic multiplicity by itself is **not** asserted as new. Ambient homology characters, however, do not determine the fixed-space integral homotopy type, its homotopically Cohen–Macaulay structure, or the residual normalizer representation on fixed-space homology. These are the asserted additions. An unindexed equivalent theorem remains a historical originality risk.

## Limitations
The action is by vertex permutations leaving the \(q\) parallel-edge labels unchanged; color-twisting symmetries and arbitrary actions on the sheaf are not classified. The theorem supplies a homotopy type and a rational homology character, not a canonical equivariant wedge decomposition or an integral decomposition of the normalizer module. It uses the published general inflation theorem for the sphere-wedge and Cohen–Macaulay conclusions. The checker is finite and cannot independently verify all subgroup orders and graph sizes. Exhaustive literature novelty cannot be mathematically guaranteed.
