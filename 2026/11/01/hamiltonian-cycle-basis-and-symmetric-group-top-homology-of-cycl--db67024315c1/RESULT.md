# Hamiltonian-cycle basis and symmetric-group top homology of cycle-free chessboard complexes

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge3\), let \(\overleftrightarrow{K_n}\) be the loopless directed complete graph on a labeled set \(V\) of size \(n\). Define \(X_n\) as the simplicial complex whose vertices are directed arcs \(a\to b\) with \(a\ne b\), and whose faces are arc sets that are disjoint unions of directed simple paths, including isolated vertices. This is the cycle-free chessboard complex \(\Omega_n\). A Hamiltonian directed cycle is identified with its cyclic rotation, but not with its reversal.

For every directed Hamiltonian cycle \(C\), write \(E(C)\) for its \(n\) distinct arcs in cyclic order and let \(z_C=\partial[E(C)]\) be the alternating oriented boundary of the formally absent \((n-1)\)-simplex on those arcs. Every proper subset of \(E(C)\) is a face of \(X_n\), so \(z_C\) is an integral cycle in simplicial degree \(n-2\). Then
\[
\boxed{\widetilde H_{n-2}(X_n;\mathbb Z)=\bigoplus_{C}\mathbb Z[z_C]\cong\mathbb Z^{(n-1)!}.}
\]
The natural action of \(S_n\) by relabeling the underlying graph satisfies
\[
\boxed{\widetilde H_{n-2}(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong_{\mathbb Z[S_n]}\operatorname{Ind}_{C_n}^{S_n}\epsilon_n,\quad \epsilon_n(r)=(-1)^{n-1},}
\]
where \(C_n\) is the stabilizer of a fixed cyclic order and \(r\) is its generating one-step rotation. The first isomorphism is an actual integral lattice isomorphism, not only an equality of rational characters.

The top reduced rational Betti number of the orbit space \(|X_n|/S_n\) equals one for odd \(n\), zero for even \(n\). For \(|X_n|/A_n\), it equals two for odd \(n\), one for even \(n\). No statement about other rational homology degrees of these orbit spaces is implied.

## Assumptions and scope
The digraph is finite, loopless, bidirected and complete; the simplicial vertices are directed arcs, not original graph vertices. A face may have components of any positive path length, but directed cycles of every length and branching indegrees or outdegrees above one are forbidden. Every face has at most \(n-1\) arcs, so \(\dim X_n=n-2\). The symbols \(S_n\) and \(A_n\) act on \(V\) by relabeling. All homology without explicit coefficients is simplicial homology over \(\mathbb Z\). The topological orbit spaces use the geometric realization of the complex.

## Proof
**Top-dimensional chains.** Every face with \(n-1\) arcs is a directed Hamiltonian path on all \(n\) vertices, hence corresponds to exactly one linear ordering \((v_0,\ldots,v_{n-1})\). There are \(n!\) such oriented facets. Each path uniquely closes, using its arc \(v_{n-1}\to v_0\), to a directed Hamiltonian cycle \(C\). Thus the oriented top-chain basis is partitioned into disjoint blocks of \(n\) facets indexed by the \((n-1)!\) directed Hamiltonian cycles.

**Explicit generators.** For any cycle \(C\), the full set \(E(C)\) is not a simplex because it is a directed cycle; every proper subset is a multipath. Its simplicial boundary \(z_C\) therefore consists precisely of the \(n\) top facets obtained by deleting one arc, with coefficients each \(+1\) or \(-1\). The identity \(\partial^2=0\) proves that \(\partial z_C=0\). Distinct cycles have disjoint supports among top facets, so the classes \([z_C]\) are integral-linearly independent: there are no chains in degree \(n-1\) that could make such a linear combination a boundary.

**Exhaustion over integers.** Let \(c\) be any integer top cycle, with coefficient \(a(P)\) at a Hamiltonian path \(P\). For a fixed cyclic order \(C=(v_0,\ldots,v_{n-1})\), take an \((n-2)\)-arc face consisting of the consecutive path \(v_i\to v_{i+1}\to\cdots\to v_{i+n-2}\) and the isolated remaining vertex \(v_{i+n-1}\), subscripts interpreted cyclically. In the complete bidirected graph this face belongs to exactly two top facets: one obtained by appending the remaining vertex and one by prepending it. No insertion at an interior vertex respects the directed path condition. These two top facets close to the *same* Hamiltonian cycle \(C\). The coefficient of the face in \(\partial c=0\) gives a signed equality between the two corresponding top coefficients. As \(i\) runs cyclically, these equations connect all \(n\) facets in the block for \(C\), and their solution space over \(\mathbb Z\) has rank at most one, generated by the primitive coefficient vector of \(z_C\). Because this holds separately in every block, every integral top cycle is an integral sum \(\sum_C b_C z_C\). Thus the displayed basis is complete. The argument is integral: a coefficient of \(+1\) in every \(z_C\) establishes primitiveness without any field or rank extrapolation.

**Exact symmetry module.** Relabeling the original vertices permutes the cycles and their oriented simplex boundaries. Fix one cycle \(C_0\). Its stabilizer in \(S_n\) is the cyclic group generated by its order-\(n\) vertex rotation \(r\). On the ordered list of its \(n\) directed arcs, \(r\) acts by an \(n\)-cycle, with simplicial orientation sign \((-1)^{n-1}\). The orbit basis is therefore precisely the induced rank-one integral module \(\operatorname{Ind}_{C_n}^{S_n}\epsilon_n\).

**Rational symmetry quotients.** Transfer for finite group actions after barycentric subdivision identifies top rational homology of a geometric orbit space with the invariants of top rational homology of the original complex. By Frobenius reciprocity, the trivial \(S_n\)-representation occurs in the induced module once exactly when \(\epsilon_n\) is trivial, i.e., when \(n\) is odd. The sign \(S_n\)-representation occurs once for every \(n\), since its restriction to the generating \(n\)-cycle equals \(\epsilon_n\). Finally \(\operatorname{Ind}_{A_n}^{S_n}(\mathbf1)=\mathbf1\oplus\operatorname{sgn}\), so the \(A_n\)-invariant multiplicity is the sum of the two multiplicities: \(2\) when \(n\) is odd and \(1\) when \(n\) is even.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` constructs every Hamiltonian-path top simplex and its complete signed integer boundary for each \(n\in\{3,4,5,6\}\). It checks all forbidden-simplex oriented boundaries, the disjoint supports of the cycle generators, and the two-coface incidence equations linking every rotation. An independently implemented binary boundary rank confirms \(\operatorname{rank}(\partial_{n-2})=n!-(n-1)!\). It also computes the signed trace of every element of \(S_n\) for each listed \(n\), then checks both invariant dimensions. No external modules are needed. The universal conclusion is proved above independently of this finite test.

## Relationship to prior work
Vrećica and Živaljević introduced the *same object*, the cycle-free chessboard complex \(\Omega_n\), and proved its connectivity bound in Theorem 10, with sharpness for certain congruence classes in Theorem 15. Their Section 2 identifies \(\Omega_n\) as the collection of cycle-free partial permutations. Those results concern connectivity and lower homology; the inspected paper does not state the integral top homology basis or the induced \(S_n\)-module above.

Caputi, Collari, Di Trani and Smith treat multipath complexes and their homotopy theory. Their 2022 preprint explicitly identifies complete digraph multipath complexes with the earlier chessboard construction; the complete directed graph is background, whereas their classifications concern different graph families. The inspected 2026 study of bidirectional paths and polygons again cites the connectivity bound for complete digraphs but supplies no corresponding all-\(n\) top homology representation.

The basic idea of taking the boundary of a missing simplex is classical. The new mathematical content claimed here is the completeness of this explicit integral cycle basis for every \(n\), its exact induced integral symmetry module, and the resulting parity-dependent orbit-space top homology; no general homotopy classification is claimed. Relevant graph-complex and representation searches did not locate a stronger implication, but undiscovered equivalent work remains possible.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns only the highest possible reduced homology degree. It does not determine lower homology groups, torsion in lower degrees, the fundamental group, or an entire wedge-of-spheres decomposition. Its free-top-homology proof relies on every Hamiltonian path closing in the fully bidirected complete digraph and does not assert that equality for sparse digraphs. Rational orbit-space results say nothing about integral torsion or contractibility. Bibliographic originality remains conditional on the inspected literature and mathematical-record searches; the proof itself is fully explicit.
