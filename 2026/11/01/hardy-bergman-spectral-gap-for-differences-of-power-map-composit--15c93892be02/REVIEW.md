# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The normalized monomial norms give exact composition coefficients whose limits are the positive constants \(c_p,c_q\). Each composition operator is a compact perturbation of a scaled multiplicative isometry. The adjoint product of two such isometries decomposes into coprime arithmetic Jacobi paths: infinite half-lines when one reduced exponent is one, finite paths of every length with infinite multiplicity otherwise. This establishes the entire Fredholm essential spectrum, including its endpoints. The distinction between the two positive products follows from the infinite missing-degree cokernel versus the identity-minus-strict-contraction Fredholm case. The Hardy case is an exact isometry model and proves nonclosed range. The finite checker confirms the normalization and path combinatorics but is not an infinite proof.

## Originality

**PASS on accessible best-knowledge comparisons, with explicit residual risk.** The original 2026 Li–Wang–Zhao work gives compactness characterizations of linear combinations. Kriete–Moorhouse provide compact Calkin relations and essential-norm obstructions. Hu–Yang give essential-norm estimates and Schatten criteria for differences; Leng–Zhao treat cross-adjoint products. These are neither statements of the entire essential singular interval nor implications giving the Hardy–Bergman closed-range transition. An accessible earlier claim for Hardy-space cross-adjoint products contains related multiplicative graph paths, but the cross-product's disk spectrum does not itself imply this two-sided spectral-gap and Fredholm classification. The complete 2015 Abdollahi–Mehrangiz–Roientan single-monomial self-commutator article was not available, so an implicit generalization remains a risk; no whole-document noncoverage claim is made for it.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Compactness and essential-norm estimates are natural established problems for differences of composition operators. The finding determines the full positive essential spectrum, including a nonzero lower edge that forces closed range even for non-Fredholm differences; it also determines exactly when the cokernel is finite. The Hardy boundary destroys that gap and closed range for all distinct powers, an explicit qualitative transition. This is a natural complete family classification, not merely a single finite computation or a relabeling of compactness.

## Limits and prior literature

The result concerns only two power-map symbols on Hilbert spaces. Ordinary Bergman singular spectra are not claimed. A broader equivalent theorem could be hidden in weighted-Hardy literature or in the unavailable 2015 full text. The supplementary program is deterministic finite verification, not independent proof.
