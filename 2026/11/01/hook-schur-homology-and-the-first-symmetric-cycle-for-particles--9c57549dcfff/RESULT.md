# Hook-Schur homology and the first symmetric cycle for particles on star graphs

## Finding

Let \(S_m\), for \(m\ge3\), be the cone on an \(m\)-element set and let \(B_n(S_m)=\operatorname{UConf}_n(|S_m|)\) be the space of unordered configurations of \(n\ge2\) distinct particles on its geometric realization. The automorphism group \(\mathfrak S_m\) permutes its arms. Let \(E_m=\mathbb Q^m\) be the arm-permutation module and \(A_m=\ker(E_m\xrightarrow{\sum}\mathbb Q)\) its augmentation submodule.

**Theorem.** For every pair of integers \(m\ge3\), \(n\ge2\), the complete rational arm-permutation module of the first homology is
\[
H_1(B_n(S_m);\mathbb Q)\cong_{\mathbb Q[\mathfrak S_m]}
\bigoplus_{q=1}^{n-1}\mathbb S_{(q,1)}(A_m).
\]
Here \(\mathbb S_{(q,1)}\) is the ordinary Schur functor associated with the hook partition \((q,1)\) evaluated on \(A_m\); the \(\mathfrak S_m\)-action is the restriction of the resulting general-linear-group action. **These summands need not be irreducible as \(\mathfrak S_m\)-representations.** The formula therefore specifies a representation and its character, without asserting a Specht-irreducible decomposition.

For an arm permutation \(\sigma\) fixing \(f(\sigma)\) arms and having \(c_j(\sigma)\) cycles of length \(j\), write \(\chi_{m,n}(\sigma)\) for the trace on \(H_1\), setting \(\chi_{m,0}=\chi_{m,1}=0\). Then
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\sum_{n\ge0}\chi_{m,n}(\sigma)t^n
=\big((f(\sigma)-1)t-1\big)
\prod_{j\ge1}(1-t^j)^{-c_j(\sigma)}+\frac1{1-t}.}
\]
Consequently, \(H_1(B_n(S_m);\mathbb Z)\) is free abelian, all higher integral homology vanishes, and
\[
\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_1(B_n(S_m);\mathbb Q)^{\mathfrak S_m}=0
\quad(2\le n\le4),\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_1(B_5(S_m);\mathbb Q)^{\mathfrak S_m}=1.
\]
Thus **five is the least number of particles producing a nonzero first-homology class invariant under every arm permutation**, independent of the arm count.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite topological graphs and all configurations are **unordered**, collision-free configurations. The symmetric group acts by permuting the **arms**, not by relabeling particles. The theorem is uniform for every integer \(m\ge3\), \(n\ge2\). The integral homology is ordinary singular homology; rational representations are ordinary linear actions. The claim uses the natural, permutation-equivariant reduced Świątkowski chain model from the graph-configuration comparison theorem. No claim is made about integral decomposition into irreducible representations or about ordered configuration spaces.

## Proof

**1. The equivariant two-term complex.** Let \(E_{\mathbb Z}=\mathbb Z^m\) with permutation basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_m\), \(A_{\mathbb Z}=\ker(E_{\mathbb Z}\xrightarrow{\sum}\mathbb Z)\), and \(R=\operatorname{Sym}(E_{\mathbb Z})=\mathbb Z[e_1,\ldots,e_m]\), graded by polynomial degree. Denote the degree-\(r\) part by \(R_r\). The reduced Świątkowski comparison theorem identifies the integral configuration homology in particle weight \(n\) with the homology of
\[
0\longrightarrow A_{\mathbb Z}\otimes R_{n-1}
\xrightarrow{\ d_1\ }R_n\longrightarrow0,
\qquad d_1(a\otimes P)=aP,
\]
where elements \(a\in A_{\mathbb Z}\) are viewed as degree-one polynomials. Each leaf vertex contributes only a degree-zero unit to the reduced vertex factor; the central vertex contributes the half-edge-difference lattice \(A_{\mathbb Z}\). The correspondence is natural under permuting the arms, so the displayed chain complex is genuinely \(\mathfrak S_m\)-equivariant, not merely an isomorphism of underlying groups. This is the previously established chain construction, not an asserted new topological comparison theorem.

Let \(I\subset R\) be the ideal generated by all \(e_i-e_j\). The map \(d_1\) has image \(I_n\), and
\[R/I\cong\mathbb Z[z],\qquad e_i\longmapsto z.\]
Hence its degree-\(n\) cokernel is \(\mathbb Z\), carrying the trivial arm action. The nonzero homology of the two-term complex in positive degree is exactly \(\ker d_1\); there can be no homology in degrees at least two. As a subgroup of a finite-rank free abelian group, \(\ker d_1\) is free. The exact integral sequence is therefore
\[
0\longrightarrow H_1(B_n(S_m);\mathbb Z)
\longrightarrow A_{\mathbb Z}\otimes R_{n-1}
\xrightarrow{d_1}R_n\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0.
\]

**2. An integral syzygy presentation.** Choose the differences \(e_i-e_m\), for \(1\le i<m\), as a basis of \(A_{\mathbb Z}\). They form a regular sequence in \(R\), since substituting these differences and \(e_m\) gives a polynomial ring over \(\mathbb Z\). Exactness of its Koszul complex, expressed basis-free, yields
\[
H_1(B_n(S_m);\mathbb Z)\cong
\operatorname{coker}\left(
\bigwedge^3 A_{\mathbb Z}\otimes R_{n-3}
\xrightarrow{d_3}
\bigwedge^2 A_{\mathbb Z}\otimes R_{n-2}
\right),
\]
where the negative-degree polynomial pieces are zero, and the Koszul map sends \(a\wedge b\wedge c\otimes P\) to \(b\wedge c\otimes aP-a\wedge c\otimes bP+a\wedge b\otimes cP\). The isomorphism sends a two-form to its Koszul boundary in \(A_{\mathbb Z}\otimes R_{n-1}\). Both maps are arm-permutation equivariant; the presentation is not an integral splitting into rational Schur summands.

**3. Uniform representation by Pieri's rule.** Over \(\mathbb Q\), averaging over the arms gives the canonical equivariant splitting \(E_m\cong\mathbb Q\mathbf1\oplus A_m\). Consequently,
\[
\operatorname{Sym}^{n-1}(E_m)
\cong\bigoplus_{q=0}^{n-1}\operatorname{Sym}^q(A_m),
\]
where the omitted degrees are filled by powers of the trivial vector. Under this splitting, \(d_1\) is the direct sum of multiplication maps
\[
A_m\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^q(A_m)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sym}^{q+1}(A_m),
\qquad 0\le q\le n-1.
\]
These maps are surjective for every \(q\); for \(q=0\) the map is the identity. For \(q\ge1\), the characteristic-zero Pieri decomposition of general-linear-group modules is
\[
A_m\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^q(A_m)
\cong \operatorname{Sym}^{q+1}(A_m)
\oplus\mathbb S_{(q,1)}(A_m),
\]
with multiplication projecting onto the first summand. The kernel is the second summand. Restrict the natural action to \(\mathfrak S_m\) and sum over \(q\) to prove the asserted representation formula. This is an isomorphism for all \(m\ge3\), including \(\dim A_m=2\).

**4. Closed character and known rank.** Since the exact integral sequence splits as vector spaces over \(\mathbb Q\), taking traces gives
\[
\chi_{m,n}(\sigma)
=(f(\sigma)-1)h_{n-1}(\sigma)-h_n(\sigma)+1,
\quad h_r(\sigma)=\operatorname{tr}(\sigma\mid\operatorname{Sym}^r E_m).
\]
Every permutation with \(c_j\) cycles of length \(j\) has symmetric-power trace generating function
\[
\sum_{r\ge0}h_r(\sigma)t^r
=\det(I-t\sigma\mid E_m)^{-1}
=\prod_{j\ge1}(1-t^j)^{-c_j(\sigma)}.
\]
Summing the trace identity proves the closed character series, including the vanishing coefficients at particle weights zero and one. Evaluating the identity permutation recovers the **previously known** first Betti rank
\[
\operatorname{rank}H_1
=(m-1)\binom{m+n-2}{n-1}-\binom{m+n-1}{n}+1.
\]
Its recovery is a consistency check, not a separate novelty claim.

**5. The first invariant class.** Put \(p_s(r)=[t^r]P_s(t)\) with \(P_s(t)=\prod_{j=1}^{s}(1-t^j)^{-1}\). The \(\mathfrak S_m\)-invariant subspace of \(\operatorname{Sym}^r(E_m)\) has basis the orbits of exponent vectors \((a_1,\ldots,a_m)\) with \(\sum a_i=r\); hence its dimension is \(p_m(r)\). Similarly, averaging the product of the fixed-arm character \(f(\sigma)\) with \(\operatorname{Sym}^r E_m\) counts orbits of a *marked arm and an exponent vector*. Fixing the marked arm leaves \(r'\) particles there and a partition of \(r-r'\) among the remaining \(m-1\) arms. Its generating function is \(P_{m-1}(t)/(1-t)\). Averaging the character series gives the closed invariant generating function
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\sum_{n\ge0}\dim H_1(B_n(S_m);\mathbb Q)^{\mathfrak S_m}t^n
=\frac{t}{1-t}P_{m-1}(t)-(1+t)P_m(t)+\frac1{1-t}.}
\]
For each \(m\ge3\), direct coefficient extraction using integer partitions of weights through five yields coefficients \(0\) at weights zero through four and coefficient \(1\) at weight five. To make uniformity explicit, the necessary partition values split into three cases: \(m=3\), \(m=4\), and \(m\ge5\). At weight five, the respective quantities \(\sum_{r=0}^4p_{m-1}(r)\) are \(9,11,12\), whereas \(p_m(4)+p_m(5)\) are \(9,11,12\). The formula therefore gives one invariant. At weights two, three and four, the same coefficient formula gives zero in each of these cases. Since the action is by a finite group in characteristic zero, these invariant dimensions also give first rational Betti numbers of the full arm-symmetry quotient.

## Verification

The package includes `verify_star.py`, which constructs the complete Abrams cubical discretization of the unordered configuration space after subdividing each arm into \(n+1\) edges. Its cells are sets of graph cells with pairwise disjoint closures. For representatives of every arm-permutation conjugacy class, it computes a signed cubical Lefschetz number by counting setwise fixed cubes, multiplying each by the orientation sign on its moving edges. The independent radial-tree theorem that \(B_n(S_m)\) has the homotopy type of a connected graph then gives \(\operatorname{tr}(\sigma\mid H_1)=1-L(\sigma)\). These traces are compared against the closed character formula above.

The script exhaustively checked every conjugacy class for \(m=3,4,5\) and the eleven pairs \((m,n)\) recorded in `verification_output.txt`; the final output is `VERIFY_OK`. These finite tests check signs, normalizations, and specializations but are **not an infinite proof**. The uniform conclusion is established by the algebraic argument in the previous section.

## Relationship to prior work

Farley and Sabalka's original 2004 preprint established radial-tree graph-braid freeness and Betti-number calculations, not an arm-permutation character formula. Maciążek and Sawicki's 2016 preprint establishes tree-configuration Betti ranks and a cycle-based framework, not full arm-symmetry representations. Most directly, An, Drummond-Cole, and Knudsen's 2017 preprint supplies the natural Świątkowski comparison and explicitly treats star classes and their relations in Section 5.1. That **previously known two-term chain model is used here**. The contribution is the uniform Schur-functor kernel identification, the exact all-conjugacy-class character series, and the five-particle symmetry-invariant threshold. Lütgehetmann studies representation stability for ordered graph configurations, not the present finite arm-permutation representation of the unordered spaces.

A broad preexisting chain-level theory can in principle compute the desired representation, so the theorem is a concrete closed-form specialization rather than a new general comparison construction. No equivalent explicit statement was located in the inspected primary sources or the targeted research index; this is not a proof of historical priority. The Schur-functor formulation specifies the rational module without claiming its irreducible Specht multiplicities individually.

## Limitations

This result applies to one-branch-point **stars** with unordered particles, not arbitrary trees or ordered configurations. It gives a complete rational character and an integral presentation, but not a canonical integral splitting of Schur modules or all integral automorphism matrices. Published chain models and Betti formulas substantially predate it; novelty rests on the explicit equivariant decomposition and its new consequences. Mathematical correctness and publication priority have not undergone independent assessment, and unindexed equivalent literature may exist.
