# Icosahedral matching homology: its complete rational character and antipodal three-torsion

## Finding
Let \(I\) denote the ordinary twelve-vertex, thirty-edge icosahedral graph, let \(X=\mathcal M(I)\) be its ordinary matching complex (faces are sets of edges having no common endpoints), and let \(G=\operatorname{Aut}(I)\cong A_5\times C_2\). Write \(\tau\) for the central antipodal graph involution. As a rational \(G\)-module, its only nonzero reduced rational homology is
\[
\widetilde H_3(X;\mathbb Q)\cong
 2V_4^{+}\oplus4V_5^{+}\oplus2V_6^{+}
 \oplus2V_4^{-}\oplus2V_5^{-}\oplus V_6^{-}.
\]
Here \(V_4,V_5,V_6\) are the rational irreducible representations of \(A_5\) of degrees \(4,5,6\), respectively; the superscripts \(+\) and \(-\) denote the eigencharacter of \(\tau\). In particular, the \(\tau\)-even and \(\tau\)-odd rational summands have dimensions \(40\) and \(24\), and the rotation and full-automorphism invariant rational homology subspaces are zero.

The complete degree-three rational character, evaluated on \(\tau^e h\) for rotations \(h\in A_5\), is

| \(e\) | \(\operatorname{ord}(h)=1\) | \(\operatorname{ord}(h)=2\) | \(\operatorname{ord}(h)=3\) | \(\operatorname{ord}(h)=5\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(0\) | 64 | 0 | -2 | -1 |
| \(1\) | 16 | 0 | -2 | 1 |

Moreover, the **previously known** torsion subgroup \(\operatorname{Tor}\widetilde H_3(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/3\) carries the nontrivial antipodal sign: \(\tau\) acts as multiplication by \(-1\) on it. Every rotation acts trivially on that torsion subgroup. Thus the theorem specifies the rational representation and the action on the integral torsion subgroup, but not the entire integral representation lattice.

## Assumptions and scope
The twelve graph vertices are \(0,1,\ldots,11\); the thirty edges, written as increasing endpoint pairs, are exactly those generated by the Python list `E` in `verify_torsion.py`. Equivalently, `networkx.icosahedral_graph()` with its standard twelve-vertex labeling gives the same explicit graph, used by `verify_character.py`. The complex has one vertex for each graph edge and a simplex for each matching. A simplex with \(k\) chosen graph edges has simplicial dimension \(k-1\); the empty face is retained in the augmented chain complex. The graph has 120 automorphisms; its rotation subgroup of order 60 is isomorphic to \(A_5\), and antipodal inversion generates the central complementary \(C_2\).

For clarity, the rational irreducible character values on rotations of orders \((1,2,3,5)\) are \(\chi_{V_4}=(4,0,1,-1)\), \(\chi_{V_5}=(5,1,-1,0)\), and \(\chi_{V_6}=(6,-2,0,1)\). The last degree-six rational representation complexifies into the two Galois-conjugate degree-three irreducibles; consequently its rational character has complex inner product two with itself. These conventions completely specify the asserted representation without choosing matrices.

## Proof
**Exact rational homology ranks.** Exhaustively enumerate matching subsets of the explicit graph's thirty edges. The face numbers in dimensions zero through five are
\[
(30,315,1400,2535,1482,125).
\]
For increasing edge-index orientations, the signed simplicial boundary deletes one edge at a time with coefficient \((-1)^j\). The executable checker performs sparse Gaussian elimination modulo the prime \(1000003\) and returns the augmented boundary ranks, in degrees zero through five,
\[
(1,29,286,1114,1357,125).
\]
These are *also the exact rational ranks*, not merely guesses from reduction modulo a prime. Indeed a modular rank is a lower bound for rational rank, and adjacent rational boundary images have disjoint interiors because \(\partial^2=0\). The two adjacent displayed modular ranks already sum to the full chain dimension in dimensions zero, one, two, and four; the final boundary has full column rank. Therefore all those rational ranks are forced to coincide with their modular lower bounds. The displayed rank of the degree-three boundary is forced by the saturated degree-two chain dimension, and the degree-four boundary is forced by the saturated degree-four chain dimension. It follows that reduced rational homology vanishes outside degree three and has dimension \(2535-1114-1357=64\) in degree three.

**Full automorphism character.** The checker enumerates all graph automorphisms as vertex permutations, verifies their cardinality \(120\), finds the rotation subgroup generated by odd-order elements (order \(60\)), and finds the antipode as the unique graph-distance-three partner of each vertex. Every induced graph-edge permutation acts on oriented matching simplices with its permutation sign. For each group element \(g\), the signed fixed-simplex sums yield the simplicial Lefschetz number \(L(g)\). Since only ordinary \(H_0\) and reduced \(H_3\) survive over \(\mathbb Q\), \(L(g)=1-\operatorname{tr}(g\mid\widetilde H_3)\). For the eight rotation-order/antipodal cases this gives exactly the displayed character, checked individually across all \(120\) elements, with class sizes \(1,15,20,24\) for each antipodal parity. Both order-five rotation conjugacy classes have the same value, so combining them causes no information loss.

**Irreducible decomposition.** Decompose first into the two \(\tau\)-eigenspaces using the central involution. The character-inner-product formulas for \(A_5\) applied to the preceding complete character give multiplicities \((0,2,4,2)\) in the even sector and \((0,2,2,1)\) in the odd sector, indexed by irreducible rational degrees \((1,4,5,6)\). For the rational degree-six constituent divide its complex character inner product by two, because it is the sum of two conjugate complex irreducibles. The dimensions check as \(8+20+12=40\) and \(8+10+6=24\). Both trivial multiplicities are zero. This proves the rational statement.

**Action on the known three-torsion.** The independent `verify_torsion.py` constructs the same thirty-edge complex from a literal edge list, obtains the antipodal edge involution directly by graph distances, and works over \(\mathbb F_3\). Since \(2\) is invertible in \(\mathbb F_3\), the augmented simplicial chain complex splits into \(\pm1\) antipodal eigensubcomplexes. Their degree-zero-through-five chain dimensions are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
 &0&1&2&3&4&5\\\hline
+&15&150&700&1290&741&55\\
-&15&165&700&1245&741&70
\end{array}
\]
with respective boundary ranks
\[
(1,14,136,564,686,55)\quad\text{and}\quad
(0,15,150,550,670,70).
\]
Consequently the only nonzero reduced \(\mathbb F_3\)-homology is dimension \(40\) in degree three in the plus sector, and dimensions \(25\) in degree three and \(1\) in degree four in the minus sector. The earlier independent integral homotopy classification proves \(\widetilde H_4(X;\mathbb Z)=0\) and \(\operatorname{Tor}\widetilde H_3(X;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/3\). By the *natural*, hence automorphism-equivariant, universal coefficient short exact sequence, \(\widetilde H_4(X;\mathbb F_3)\cong\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathbb Z}(\widetilde H_3(X;\mathbb Z),\mathbb F_3)\). The one-dimensional degree-four group lies entirely in the minus eigenspace, proving antipodal multiplication by \(-1\) on the torsion subgroup. Since \(A_5\) is perfect, its action on this cyclic torsion subgroup factors trivially through \(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb Z/3)\cong C_2\). This completes the claimed torsion action.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_character.py` and `python3 verify_torsion.py` with Python 3; the first also requires the standard external Python package `networkx`. `verify_character.py` reconstructs all six nonempty simplicial dimensions, verifies boundary ranks over \(\mathbb F_{1000003}\), enumerates all 120 graph automorphisms, and verifies every signed Lefschetz trace. Its success sentinel is `VERIFY_CHARACTER_OK`. `verify_torsion.py` uses only the Python standard library, a literal independent graph edge list, and exact \(\mathbb F_3\) antipodal eigenspace elimination; it ends with `VERIFY_TORSION_OK`. The checked executions are preserved in `character_output.txt` and `torsion_output.txt`.

These exact finite calculations establish the finite group character and eigenspace ranks, rather than extrapolating an infinite claim. The logical steps converting these ranks to rational homology and integral torsion action are given above. The preexisting integral torsion/homotopy theorem is a stated dependency, not independently reproved here.

## Relationship to prior work
Shareshian and Wachs analyze torsion and group representations of matching complexes of **complete** graphs and chessboard complexes; the present graph is the proper, highly symmetric 30-edge icosahedral subgraph of the complete graph, and their listed results do not specify its automorphism character or torsion action. Bayer, Jelić Milutinović and Vega prove an outerplanar sphere-wedge theorem; the icosahedron is not outerplanar (it has thirty edges, exceeding the \(2(12)-3=21\) edge bound). A previously available exact computation of this **same** icosahedral matching complex proves \(\bigvee^{64}S^3\vee M(\mathbb Z/3,3)\); the known homotopy type and homology ranks are explicitly not asserted as novel here. Those nonequivariant invariants do **not** determine the 120-element rational character or the automorphism of its distinguished \(\mathbb Z/3\) torsion subgroup. Related representations of the icosahedral *neighborhood* complex concern a different simplicial complex and do not imply this result.

## Limitations
This is a finite-complex-specific equivariant computation and does not describe matching complexes of all planar graphs. The full *integral lattice* automorphism representation is not classified; only the rationalization and the action on the characteristic three-torsion subgroup are. The degree-four mod-three-to-torsion identification depends on the published same-object integral homotopy result. The explicit computations provide repeatable exact arithmetic, not a machine-checked formal mathematical proof. Independent review has not been performed. An unindexed equivalent equivariant calculation could still affect publication priority.
