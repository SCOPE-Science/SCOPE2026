# Scientific review: icosahedral matching representation and torsion character

## Correctness — PASS
The two actual standalone programs reconstruct 5,887 matching simplices from independent graph input methods and execute in exact finite fields. The augmented boundary ranks, the 120 signed Lefschetz traces, and the antipodal mod-three eigenspace ranks are checked, not inferred from sample cases. Adjacent boundary-rank saturation proves the rational Betti concentration and rules out erroneous modular-to-rational inference. Orthogonality against the rational \(A_5\) characters gives exact nonnegative integral multiplicities; the degree-six rational character is correctly normalized for its pair of conjugate complex components. The torsion-sign conclusion follows from an equivariant universal coefficient sequence using the published same-object integral homotopy type. The latter remains an explicit dependency rather than a newly certified fact.

## Originality — PASS, residual risk stated
The full publicly indexed same-object homotopy computation already gives the 64 rational free generators and the order-three torsion, but not any graph-automorphism action. The present decomposition and antipodal torsion character are not consequences of that nonequivariant result. Matching complexes of complete graphs and chessboard complexes, the icosahedral *neighborhood* complex, and the outerplanar result concern distinct objects or assumptions; exact named comparisons and published-finding corpus searches are documented in AUDIT.json. No prior covering equivariant assertion was identified. Indexing omissions and uninspected equivalent theses remain possible.

This is a symmetry refinement of a canonical icosahedral complex with a distinguished rare torsion phenomenon. The complete 120-element character and the nontrivial involution on three-torsion answer a structurally motivated question not recoverable from the known homotopy type, while distinguishing rational from integral representations. This is not a gratuitous variation of a graph parameter.
