# Integral automorphism modules for higher-independence complexes of double stars

## Finding

Let \(T_{a,b}\) be the double-star tree with adjacent hubs \(u,v\), with \(a\geq1\) leaves adjacent to \(u\) and \(b\geq1\) leaves adjacent to \(v\). Let \(L\) be its \(m=a+b\) leaves. For an integer \(r\geq1\), the higher-independence complex \(X_r=\operatorname{Ind}_r(T_{a,b})\) consists of vertex sets whose induced connected components contain at most \(r\) vertices.

Write \(V_L=\mathbb Z^L\) for the leaf permutation lattice, and \(W_L=\ker(\epsilon:V_L\to\mathbb Z)\), where \(\epsilon(e_\ell)=1\) for each leaf. Let \(\chi_{uv}\) be the one-dimensional integral character of \(\operatorname{Aut}(T_{a,b})\) given by the sign of its induced permutation of the two hubs. **The new assertion is a natural identification of integral automorphism modules**: for every \(2\le r\le m+1\),

\[
\widetilde H_{r-1}(X_r;\mathbb Z)\ \cong_{\mathbb Z[\operatorname{Aut}(T_{a,b})]}\ \chi_{uv}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\bigwedge^{r-2} W_L,
\]

and every other reduced integral homology group vanishes. The same other-degree vanishing holds when \(r=1\) or \(r\ge m+2\), in which cases all reduced homology vanishes. When \(a\ne b\), the group is \(S_a\times S_b\) and \(\chi_{uv}\) is trivial; when \(a=b\), the group is \((S_a\times S_a)\rtimes C_2\) and the block-exchange involution acts by the extra minus sign.

In particular, the complete rational character is explicit. If an automorphism \(g\) acts on the leaves with cycle lengths \(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_t\), and \(\eta(g)=+1\) when it fixes both hubs and \(-1\) when it interchanges them, then

\[
\operatorname{tr}\left(g\mid\widetilde H_{r-1}(X_r;\mathbb Q)\right)
=\eta(g)\,[z^{r-2}]\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{t}(1-(-z)^{\ell_i})}{1+z}.
\]

The coefficient is an integer. For example, with \(a=b=3\), \(r=4\), the rank is \(10\), but the full part-interchange involution has trace \(2\), and hence its rational \(+1\) and \(-1\) eigenspaces have respective dimensions \(6\) and \(4\). This representation-theoretic information is not determined by the previously known homotopy type.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph is an ordinary finite, simple, undirected double star. Each hub has at least one pendant leaf, so these hubs are exactly the non-leaf vertices. The definition of higher independence refers to sizes of induced connected components, not ordinary independent sets for \(r>1\). Homology is reduced simplicial homology with integral coefficients and the usual oriented-simplex action. The exterior power of degree zero is \(\mathbb Z\). Graph automorphisms permute hubs and leaves but never interchange a hub with a leaf. For \(r=1\) and \(r\ge m+2\), the representation is the zero module.

For orientation, the ordinary homotopy type

\[
X_r\simeq\bigvee^{\binom{m-1}{r-2}}S^{r-1}\quad (2\le r\le m+1)
\]

was already covered by Deshpande and Singh (2020/2021), Theorem 3.10 and Corollary 3.13, for leaf-whiskered graphs and caterpillars. **That homotopy classification and its Betti count are not claimed to be new.** The contribution here is the complete **integral** group-module identification, including the hub-exchange twist, for every threshold and every double-star size.

## Proof

**1. Faces without both hubs form a contractible subcomplex.** Set \(X=X_r\) and let \(A\subseteq X\) contain all faces not containing both \(u\) and \(v\). Let \(\Delta^L\) be the full simplex on the leaves. For \(r\ge2\), the faces of \(A\) are precisely those in \(\Delta^L\), in the cone \(u*C_u\), or in the cone \(v*C_v\), where

\[
C_u=\{S\subseteq L:|S\cap L_u|\le r-1\},\quad
C_v=\{S\subseteq L:|S\cap L_v|\le r-1\},
\]

and \(L_u,L_v\) are the disjoint sets of leaves incident with the corresponding hubs. Each \(C_u\) contains every subset of \(L_v\) freely and is therefore the join of a truncated simplex on \(L_u\) with the nonempty simplex \(\Delta^{L_v}\); hence it is contractible. Likewise \(C_v\) is contractible. Attaching \(u*C_u\) to \(\Delta^L\) along \(C_u\), then attaching \(v*C_v\) along \(C_v\), preserves contractibility: all three spaces and the two successive intersections are contractible CW complexes. Thus \(|A|\) is contractible. The same argument with \(C_u=\Delta^{L_v}\), \(C_v=\Delta^{L_u}\) proves that \(X_1=A\) is contractible.

**2. Canonical relative chains.** A face containing both hubs has the unique form \(\{u,v\}\cup S\) with \(S\subseteq L\), \(|S|\le r-2\), because the entire induced component is then connected. For \(r\ge2\), the relative simplicial chain group \(C_j(X,A;\mathbb Z)\) is therefore freely generated by oriented faces \([u,v,s_1,\ldots,s_k]\), with \(k=j-1\) and \(k\le r-2\). Fix the hub ordering \(u,v\) and order the remaining vertices consistently. The relative boundary removes only leaf vertices, because removing either hub lands in \(A\). Since the hubs are the first two entries, the leaf-removal signs are exactly those of the **augmented** simplicial boundary on leaf subsets. Hence there is a chain isomorphism

\[
C_\bullet(X,A;\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde C_{\bullet-2}(K;\mathbb Z)\otimes \mathbb Z_{\chi_{uv}},
\quad K=\{S\subseteq L:|S|\le r-2\}.
\]

This isomorphism is equivariant: leaf permutations act on the oriented leaf sequence, whereas interchanging the hubs reverses the oriented pair \([u,v]\) and contributes \(-1\). This includes \(r=2\), when \(K=\{\emptyset\}\) and its reduced augmented chain group in degree \(-1\) is \(\mathbb Z\).

**3. Passage to absolute homology.** The reduced long exact sequence for \((X,A)\), together with the contractibility and nonemptiness of \(A\), gives a natural \(\operatorname{Aut}(T_{a,b})\)-equivariant isomorphism \(\widetilde H_j(X;\mathbb Z)\cong H_j(X,A;\mathbb Z)\) in every degree \(j\). Thus \(\widetilde H_j(X)\cong \chi_{uv}\otimes\widetilde H_{j-2}(K)\). If \(r\ge m+2\), the whole graph fits in a single allowed induced component and \(X\) is a simplex.

**4. Identification with an exterior power over \(\mathbb Z\).** Let \(V_L=\mathbb Z^L\). The augmented simplicial chains of the full leaf simplex identify with the exterior algebra: \(\widetilde C_{s-1}(\Delta^L;\mathbb Z)=\bigwedge^s V_L\). The augmented boundary is contraction by \(\epsilon\). The skeleton \(K\) retains these groups for \(0\le s\le r-2\) and has no higher nonempty faces. Choose any leaf \(\ell_0\); then \(V_L=W_L\oplus\mathbb Z e_{\ell_0}\) integrally, and contraction is zero on \(\bigwedge^s W_L\) and an isomorphism from \(e_{\ell_0}\wedge\bigwedge^{s-1}W_L\) onto \(\bigwedge^{s-1}W_L\). It follows that the only nonzero augmented reduced homology of \(K\) is

\[
\widetilde H_{r-3}(K;\mathbb Z)=\ker\left(\iota_\epsilon:\bigwedge^{r-2}V_L\to\bigwedge^{r-3}V_L\right)=\bigwedge^{r-2}W_L.
\]

The kernel description, unlike the auxiliary choice of \(\ell_0\), is canonical under **all** leaf permutations. This proves the asserted integral module identification, including all boundary cases and the absence of torsion. The exterior rank equals \(\binom{m-1}{r-2}\), consistently with the already-known ordinary homotopy theorem.

**5. Character formula.** For a permutation \(P_g\) on the leaves, \(V_L\otimes\mathbb Q\cong(W_L\otimes\mathbb Q)\oplus\mathbf 1\). The generating function for traces on exterior powers of \(W_L\) is therefore \(\det(I+zP_g)/(1+z)\). A permutation cycle of length \(\ell\) contributes \(1-(-z)^\ell\). Multiplying by the hub-pair sign yields the stated coefficient formula.

The known wedge-of-spheres homotopy type can alternatively be recovered from this relative decomposition as the double suspension of the truncated leaf simplex, but this is a new proof of an **existing** nonequivariant classification, not an additional originality claim.

## Verification

Run `python3 checker.py` with Python 3.9 or later from this package directory. It independently constructs induced components from graph adjacency, enumerates all admissible faces and computes mod-two simplicial boundary ranks for \(128\) parameter triples with \(1\le a,b\le4\). It separately computes signed Lefschetz traces of graph automorphisms and compares them with the exterior-power character formula for \(1{,}050\) cases (complete representative small actions and generators of the larger action). Its successful output is:

`PASS: 128 homology cases, 1050 signed-character cases, 27412 total faces enumerated`

The finite checks test signs, boundary cases, and ranks; they do not prove the infinite theorem or integral torsion-freeness. Those follow from the chain-isomorphism and integral exterior-algebra calculation in the proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Deshpande and Singh, *Higher Independence Complexes of Graphs and Their Homotopy Types*, arXiv:2001.05448 (first posted January 15, 2020), Theorem 3.10 and Corollary 3.13, explicitly cover the **entire ordinary double-star homotopy type** as a two-hub caterpillar. The present theorem does **not** claim that homotopy type or its binomial rank as original. Their full-text argument uses leaf-selecting discrete Morse matchings; it does not state the \(\mathbb Z[\operatorname{Aut}]\)-module, the hub-swap twist or character formula. The proof here uses the canonical relative chain complex to supply precisely that finer information.

Abdelmalek–Deshpande–Goyal–Roy–Singh (2021), arXiv:2106.10863, studied shellability and vertex decomposability, including caterpillars, without identifying the integral symmetry module. Ghosh–Selvaraja (2025), arXiv:2505.06614, treated shellability and chordal constructions. Sawant (May 24, 2026), arXiv:2605.25150, proved vertex decomposability for all trees, motivating the renewed structural question, but likewise did not compute automorphism homology modules. The specific module formula is not a formal implication of a wedge decomposition: two group actions can have identical ordinary homotopy types and different integral character lattices.

Targeted searches of published mathematical finding summaries and ordinary literature, including aliases `higher independence`, `r-independence`, `3-path-free`, `caterpillar`, `bistar`, `double star`, `exterior-power homology`, and `automorphism representation`, did not locate an equivalent module calculation. Unindexed or unpublished equivalents remain possible.

## Limitations

No \(\operatorname{Aut}(T_{a,b})\)-equivariant wedge decomposition is asserted. The representation is of ordinary reduced integral homology, not Borel-equivariant homology, fixed-point spaces, quotient-space homology, or a chain-level natural equivalence to a wedge. The explicit module theorem is restricted to the two-hub double-star family; the general tree family has more complicated relative-chain strata. The known nonequivariant theorem subsumes the sphere-count statement. Independent mathematical validation and exhaustive exclusion of unindexed equivalents have not been performed.
