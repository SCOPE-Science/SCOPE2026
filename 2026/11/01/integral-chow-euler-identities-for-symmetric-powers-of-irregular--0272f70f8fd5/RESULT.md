# Integral Chow–Euler identities for symmetric powers of irregularity-zero surfaces

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(S\) be a smooth connected projective surface over \(\mathbb C\). Write \(S^{[n]}\) for its length-\(n\) Hilbert scheme and \(\rho_n:S^{[n]}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sym}^n S\) for Hilbert–Chow. Then for every integer \(n\ge0\),
\[
\rho_{n*}:\operatorname{CH}_0(S^{[n]})\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{CH}_0(\operatorname{Sym}^nS)
\]
is an isomorphism **with integral coefficients**.

If \(q(S):=h^1(S,\mathcal O_S)=0\), these Chow groups have no nonzero torsion. In particular, writing \(\mathbf e(Z):=c_{SM}(Z)_0\in\operatorname{CH}_0(Z)\) for the degree-zero Chern–Schwartz–MacPherson class, the following identities hold **integrally** for all \(n\), with coefficientwise Chow-group interpretation:
\[
\sum_{n\ge0}\mathbf e(\operatorname{Sym}^n S)t^n=\operatorname{Exp}(\mathbf e(S)),\qquad \mathbf e(S)=c_2(T_S)\cap[S],
\]
\[
\sum_{n\ge0}\rho_{n*}\mathbf e(S^{[n]})t^n
=\prod_{m\ge1}(1-t^m)^{-\mathbf e(S)}.
\]
Here \(\operatorname{Exp}\) and the powers use the zero-cycle Chow power structure of Fantechi–Göttsche, not a naive scalar exponential. For instance if \(\mathbf e(S)=a[o]\) with an integer \(a\), the first series has coefficient \(\binom{a+n-1}{n}[n o]\), with the usual generalized binomial interpretation.

## Assumptions and scope

The field is \(\mathbb C\); the surface is smooth, connected and projective. No minimality or geometric-genus restriction is needed. The isomorphism of zero-cycle Chow groups applies even when \(q(S)>0\); torsion-freeness and the integral Macdonald/Hilbert identities are asserted only under \(q(S)=0\). The Chow groups of possibly singular symmetric powers are Fulton's groups of zero-dimensional cycles modulo rational equivalence, not the Chow rings of their smooth resolutions. All formal series are equal coefficientwise in \(\prod_{n\ge0}\operatorname{CH}_0(\operatorname{Sym}^nS)t^n\), with multiplication induced by addition of effective cycles.

## Proof

**Step 1: fibers of Hilbert–Chow.** For a complex cycle \(y=\sum_{j=1}^{r}m_jp_j\) with distinct support points, the reduced fiber \(\rho_n^{-1}(y)\) has the same zero-cycle theory as a product \(\prod_j\operatorname{Hilb}^{m_j}(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{S,p_j})\) of planar punctual Hilbert schemes. The cell decomposition of a length-\(m\) punctual planar Hilbert scheme consists of affine cells indexed by partitions \(\lambda\vdash m\), with dimension \(m-\ell(\lambda)\) (Cherednik, Theorem 2.7, following Ellingsrud–Strømme). There is exactly one zero-dimensional cell, for \(\lambda=(1,\ldots,1)\). The localization filtration for this cellular proper variety gives \(\operatorname{CH}_0\cong\mathbb Z\), generated by the class of any closed point. Products of these cellular fibers have the same property. Nilpotent scheme structure on a fiber does not affect its Chow zero-cycles. Thus any two complex points lying above the same \(y\) are rationally equivalent **inside the fiber**.

**Step 2: lifting relations integrally.** Every complex point \(y\in\operatorname{Sym}^nS\) has a complex point above it. Choose a lift \(x_y\) and send \([y]\mapsto[x_y]\); Step 1 shows the resulting class is independent of the lift. To check rational equivalences, take any integral curve \(C\subset\operatorname{Sym}^nS\) and rational function \(g\in\mathbb C(C)^*\). Over its function field \(K=\mathbb C(C)\), the generic cycle is a finite sum of closed points of \(S_K\) with multiplicities. At each such support point, smoothness supplies a regular parameter pair over its residue field; the curvilinear ideal generated by one parameter and the appropriate power of the other gives a length-\(m\) subscheme defined over that residue field. Taking the union over the generic cycle produces a \(K\)-rational point of the Hilbert–Chow fiber (this construction respects the residue-field descent). It extends to a rational section over a dense open of the normalization of \(C\). Its closure is a curve \(\widetilde C\subset S^{[n]}\) mapping birationally onto \(C\). The pushforward of the principal divisor of \(g\) on \(\widetilde C\) is the principal divisor of \(g\) on \(C\). By Step 1, each point of this lifted divisor is rationally equivalent to the chosen \(x_y\) in its Hilbert–Chow fiber. Therefore the lift map kills every generating rational-equivalence relation on \(\operatorname{Sym}^n S\). It gives a two-sided inverse to \(\rho_{n*}\), proving the claimed **integral** isomorphism. For \(n=0\), both sides are \(\mathbb Z\) directly.

**Step 3: torsion vanishing.** The Hilbert scheme \(S^{[n]}\) is smooth and projective. Its first irregularity equals that of \(S\), by the standard cohomology formula for Hilbert schemes of points (equivalently, the Hilbert–Chow resolution of the quotient \(S^n/\mathfrak S_n\) and its rational singularities). Thus when \(q(S)=0\), its Albanese variety is trivial. Roitman's torsion theorem identifies the torsion subgroup of \(\operatorname{CH}_0(S^{[n]})^0\) with the torsion subgroup of this Albanese. Since the degree map targets \(\mathbb Z\), all torsion is degree zero, and \(\operatorname{CH}_0(S^{[n]})\) is torsion-free. By Step 2 the same holds for \(\operatorname{Sym}^nS\).

**Step 4: remove the exact factorial ambiguity.** Fantechi–Göttsche, Theorem 4.25, gives \(\mathbf e(\operatorname{Sym}^nS)=\operatorname{Exp}_n(\mathbf e(S))\) modulo \(n!\)-torsion. Both classes are integral zero-cycle classes. Their difference is annihilated by \(n!\); Step 3 makes that difference zero. This proves Conjecture 4.26 of that source for this natural class of surfaces. Their Theorem 6.6 and its Hilbert-scheme specialization, whose integral zero-cycle statement follows when Conjecture 4.26 holds, then give the second displayed generating identity. These steps hold for all \(n\ge0\), not just for tested degrees.

## Verification

The argument checks the three potentially delicate points separately: each closed Hilbert–Chow fiber has a cellular stratification with exactly one zero-cell; for any curve in the symmetric product, the generic Hilbert–Chow fiber admits a rational point given by explicit curvilinear local ideals; and Roitman's theorem applies to the **smooth** Hilbert scheme rather than directly to the singular symmetric power. The published CSM identity is only modulo factorial torsion, which is precisely what the torsion-freeness argument removes.

As a numerical cross-check, for a K3 surface with canonical degree-one zero-cycle \(o\), the known identity \(c_2(S)=24[o]\) gives \(\mathbf e(\operatorname{Sym}^2S)=300[2o]\) and \(\rho_{2*}\mathbf e(S^{[2]})=324[2o]\). The degrees agree with Macdonald's and Göttsche's classical Euler-characteristic series. This check is not substituted for the all-degree proof; the special K3 formula is not asserted to be historically new.

## Relationship to prior work

Fantechi–Göttsche, arXiv:2610.31826v1, Theorem 4.25 and Conjecture 4.26, respectively establish the formula modulo \(n!\)-torsion and propose its integral extension for general varieties. They prove the conjecture for complete curves (Theorem 5.13), not for arbitrary smooth surfaces with \(q=0\). Their Lemma 6.10 establishes **only a rational-coefficient** Hilbert–Chow isomorphism; the present fiber-and-curve argument makes the zero-cycle isomorphism integral. Their Example 6.9 and Remark 6.13 give K3 expressions rationally, while explicitly referring to Voisin's earlier K3 Hilbert-scheme Chow result; the K3 specialization is illustrative rather than a novelty claim. Cherednik's affine-cell theorem, Roitman's torsion theorem, and Göttsche's first-cohomology calculation are essential existing inputs, not new claims. Earlier integral results for projective curves do not imply the general irregularity-zero surface case.

## Limitations

This is a self-review mathematical proof, not an independent audit. The claim is restricted to the ordinary complex projective surface Hilbert–Chow morphism and to integral zero-cycle Chow groups, **not** all-dimensional Chow groups or arbitrary Quot-to-Chow morphisms. For \(q(S)>0\), torsion-freeness is generally false and the integral formula is not asserted. An implicit treatment of the integral Hilbert–Chow zero-cycle isomorphism in earlier birational Chow literature remains possible; search incompleteness is not proof of historical novelty. The full arbitrary-variety Conjecture 4.26 remains open outside the stated scope.
