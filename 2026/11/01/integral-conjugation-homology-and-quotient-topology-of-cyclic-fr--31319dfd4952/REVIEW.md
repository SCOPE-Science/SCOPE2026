# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS

The group-law calculation is explicit. Invertibility of every \(1-h\) for \(h\ne1\) both uniquely assigns outside-kernel elements to conjugate complements and forces commuting outside-kernel elements into the same complement. Since the kernel is a single-prime group, all its nonidentity elements are isolated in the prime-power clique complex. The join decomposition then gives the integral homotopy and tensor-product homology. Affine conjugation on complement indices and free complement action on nonzero kernel points establish the exact integral module and quotient-space formulas. Four full finite graph checks and an independent higher-join check passed. Universal claims follow from proof, not extrapolation.

## Originality: PASS

The July 2026 source introduces this specific group clique complex and determines nilpotent join factors, dihedral cases, and selected non-mono-connected groups. Its stated results do not subsume the fixed-point-free Frobenius decomposition or its integral conjugation representation and quotient topology. The 2024 Cameron paper constructs different independence/generation complexes, while the closest indexed Frobenius-partition result concerns Engel digraphs, whose adjacency is different. Specific database and literature searches did not find the same theorem. Residual risk: its underlying group partition is a classical fact and an equivalent formulation may exist in unindexed work.

## Value: PASS

The motivating source explicitly describes its soluble-group understanding as incomplete beyond dihedral examples and exhibits non-mono-connected groups with only two non-isolated components. This theorem gives arbitrarily many nontrivial components carrying arbitrarily large positive-dimensional integral homology, and determines the full inner-action homology lattice and orbit space for a natural infinite class of solvable groups. In particular, the three-valued character is not determined by ordinary Betti numbers or prime graphs. The explicit semidirect-product restriction is mathematically motivated by the invertible \(1-h\) mechanism, rather than a one-off numerical slice.
