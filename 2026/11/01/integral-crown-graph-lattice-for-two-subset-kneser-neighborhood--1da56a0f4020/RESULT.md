# Integral crown-graph lattice for two-subset Kneser neighborhood homology

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge4\), let \(G_n=\operatorname{KG}(n,2)\) be the graph on the two-element subsets of \([n]\), adjacent when disjoint, and let \(K_n=\mathcal N(G_n)\) be the ordinary Lovász open-neighborhood simplicial complex. Define the crown graph \(C_n\) with vertices \(L_i,R_j\) for \(1\le i,j\le n\) and edges \(L_iR_j\) exactly when \(i\ne j\). The symmetric group \(S_n\) simultaneously relabels both vertex classes.

Write \(E_n=\bigoplus_{i\ne j}\mathbb Z e_{ij}\), where \(S_n\) permutes \(e_{ij}\) by simultaneous relabeling. Let \(R_n\subset E_n\) be the sublattice generated by all row sums \(r_i=\sum_{j\ne i}e_{ij}\) and column sums \(c_j=\sum_{i\ne j}e_{ij}\). Let \(\operatorname{sgn}_n\) denote the integral sign representation. Then there is an isomorphism of integral \(S_n\)-modules
\[
\boxed{\widetilde H_{n-4}(K_n;\mathbb Z)\cong_{\mathbb Z[S_n]}\operatorname{sgn}_n\otimes_{\mathbb Z}(E_n/R_n).}
\]
All other reduced integral homology groups vanish; the displayed group is free of rank \(n^2-3n+1\). The only integral relation among the \(2n\) displayed row and column generators is \(\sum_i r_i=\sum_j c_j\). Thus the module is an explicit signed permutation-cochain quotient, not merely an abstract character.

Consequently, for every prime \(p\), reduction of the same presentation modulo \(p\) gives the complete action on \(\widetilde H_{n-4}(K_n;\mathbb F_p)\). Over \(\mathbb Q\) its character is
\[
\chi_n(\sigma)=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\bigl(f(\sigma)^2-3f(\sigma)+1\bigr),
\]
where \(f(\sigma)\) is the number of fixed ground-set letters. This rational character, and its familiar Specht consequences, are included for consistency; the claimed additional information is the **integral lattice presentation**.

## Assumptions and scope
The integer \(n\) ranges over all \(n\ge4\). The neighborhood complex is the abstract simplicial complex whose simplices are sets of graph vertices with a common graph neighbor. The \(S_n\)-action on its integral simplicial homology is the natural action from relabeling \([n]\). No choice of orientation is needed to define the module; the sign twist arises from the orientation of the boundary of the standard simplex. For the smallest case \(n=4\), \(K_n\) is a six-point discrete space and the formula concerns reduced degree-zero homology.

## Proof
**1. Canonical equivariant model.** A nonempty family \(F\) of graph vertices, each a two-subset of \([n]\), has a common neighbor precisely when its support union \(U(F)\) has at most \(n-2\) elements. Let \(Q\) be the face poset of nonempty simplices of \(K_n\), and let \(P\) be the poset of subsets \(S\subset[n]\) with \(2\le|S|\le n-2\), ordered by inclusion. The maps \(u:Q\to P\), \(F\mapsto U(F)\), and \(v:P\to Q\), \(S\mapsto\binom S2\), are order preserving and \(S_n\)-equivariant. They satisfy \(u\circ v=\mathrm{id}_P\) and \(F\subseteq v(u(F))\). The order-homotopy theorem applied to the pointwise inequality gives an \(S_n\)-equivariant homotopy equivalence \(\mathrm{Sd}(K_n)\simeq\Delta(P)\).

**2. Equivariant Alexander-dual complement.** Let \(B\) be the barycentric subdivision of the boundary of the standard \((n-1)\)-simplex. This is a triangulated sphere of dimension \(n-2\), whose vertices are the nonempty proper subsets of \([n]\). The complex \(\Delta(P)\) is the induced subcomplex on vertices of cardinalities between \(2\) and \(n-2\). The deleted vertices have cardinality \(1\) or \(n-1\); their induced subcomplex is exactly the crown graph \(C_n\): singleton \(\{i\}\) and co-singleton \([n]\setminus\{j\}\) span an edge just when \(i\ne j\).

For any point of \(|B|\setminus|\Delta(P)|\), the total barycentric weight on these deleted vertices is positive. Set the other weights to zero and renormalize the deleted weights. Linear interpolation between the original and renormalized weights remains in the complement, proving an explicit \(S_n\)-equivariant deformation retraction \(|B|\setminus|\Delta(P)|\to|C_n|\).

The action of \(\sigma\in S_n\) on the oriented sphere \(|B|\) has degree \(\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\). Equivariant integral Alexander duality therefore gives
\[
\widetilde H_i(K_n;\mathbb Z)\cong \operatorname{sgn}_n\otimes\widetilde H^{n-3-i}(C_n;\mathbb Z).
\]
This is a natural isomorphism of \(\mathbb Z[S_n]\)-modules, not only an equality of ranks.

**3. Integral cochain lattice.** The graph \(C_n\) is connected for every \(n\ge3\). Orient all its \(n(n-1)\) edges from the \(L\)-class to the \(R\)-class. Its cochain complex has \(C^0=\mathbb Z^{2n}\), \(C^1=E_n\), and \(d^0(\phi)_{ij}=\phi(R_j)-\phi(L_i)\). Hence \(\mathrm{im}(d^0)\) is exactly the lattice generated by row and column sums, and \(H^1(C_n;\mathbb Z)=E_n/R_n\) equivariantly. The connectedness identifies \(\ker(d^0)\) with the constant functions; thus the \(2n\) coboundary generators have precisely one relation. An oriented incidence matrix of a connected graph is totally unimodular; equivalently, a spanning-tree incidence submatrix has determinant \(\pm1\), so its cokernel is torsion-free. Its rank is \(n(n-1)-2n+1=n^2-3n+1\). Since a connected graph has reduced cohomology only in degree one, Alexander duality proves the stated concentration and integral module.

**4. Field reductions and rational check.** The integral homology is free, so the universal coefficient theorem identifies its degree-\(n-4\) homology over any field with the reduction of this integral lattice. On rational cochains, the edge-permutation trace is \(f(f-1)\); the vertex-permutation trace is \(2f\); the connected-graph constant-cochain trace is \(1\). The exact cochain sequence therefore gives trace \(f^2-3f+1\) on \(H^1(C_n;\mathbb Q)\). Twisting by the sphere-orientation sign gives \(\chi_n\) as displayed. Standard rational symmetric-group decomposition gives \(\operatorname{sgn}_n\otimes(S^{(n-2,2)}\oplus S^{(n-2,1,1)})\); this last semisimple decomposition is not the asserted novelty.

## Verification
The standalone script `verify_kneser.py` uses only Python's standard library. It independently enumerates the faces of the original open-neighborhood complex and computes signed fixed-simplex Lefschetz traces. Separately, it computes the trace from fixed chains of the Boolean-rank poset, and compares both with the crown-cohomology formula. Every permutation is checked for \(n=4,5,6\); four nontrivial or representative permutations are checked for \(n=7\). It also verifies connectedness and edge-minus-vertex rank of the crown graph. The recorded results are \(6,50,710,17724\) nonempty faces and integral ranks \(5,11,19,29\), respectively. These finite checks are corroboration; the proof is valid for every \(n\ge4\) independently of them.

## Relationship to prior work
Björner's *Topological Methods*, §4 (1995), already relates Kneser neighborhood complexes to rank-selected Boolean posets via the crosscut theorem; that equivariant model is used here, not claimed as new. Nilakantan and Singh (2018, Theorem 1.3) already prove the non-equivariant wedge-of-spheres homotopy classification for every \(n\); it gives the rank but does not identify an integral automorphism lattice. The general Boolean-rank homology result, including the rational ribbon Specht representation (Stanley and subsequent treatments, e.g. Hersh and Sundaram 2026, Proposition 4.1), implies the characteristic-zero representation and is stronger than any claim that the rational Specht decomposition alone is new. A published six-point calculation likewise already supplies the rational module for \(n=6\).

The distinct assertion made here is the uniform **integral** identification with the sign-twisted first-cohomology *lattice of a crown graph*, including explicit row/column relations and natural modular reductions for all primes. The preceding characteristic-zero results do not, by themselves, determine the integral lattice or its modular reductions. Other graph complexes on two-subsets, including the **independence** complex, have different face conditions and do not imply this identification.

## Limitations
No new claim is made about the nonequivariant homotopy type, the rational Specht decomposition considered in isolation, or an equivariant homotopy type. The isomorphism is a homology-module classification and does not identify individual geometric sphere generators. Although the published comparison is substantive, an equivalent integral lattice calculation in unindexed literature cannot be ruled out. The broad rank-selected Boolean results could offer an alternative derivation after additional integral analysis; this is a residual originality risk. The finite verification is not a proof of the result for arbitrarily large \(n\), and no independent human audit has been performed.
