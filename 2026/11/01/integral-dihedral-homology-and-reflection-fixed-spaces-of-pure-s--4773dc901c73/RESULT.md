# Integral dihedral homology and reflection fixed spaces of pure strong cycle matching complexes

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge7\), let \(X_n=\mathcal{SM}_{\mathrm{pure}}(C_n)\) be the pure strong discrete Morse matching complex of the simple cycle, with its natural dihedral action \(D_n=\langle r,s\rangle\). Put \(d=2k-2\) when \(n=3k\), and \(d=2k-1\) when \(n\in\{3k+1,3k+2\}\). Its first nonzero reduced integral group \(\widetilde H_2(X_n;\mathbb Z)\) is \(\mathbb Z\) with **trivial** \(D_n\)-action. Its only other nonzero reduced integral homology is \(\widetilde H_d\), a free lattice with a natural description
\[
\widetilde H_d(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong_{\mathbb Z[D_n]}
\operatorname{Ind}_{\langle r\rangle}^{D_n}
\left(\det(\mathbb Z^n)\otimes
\widetilde H^{n-d-3}(\operatorname{Ind}(C_n);\mathbb Z)\right).
\]
The cyclic generator acts on the determinant by \((-1)^{n-1}\) and on the cohomology by cyclic vertex relabeling. In particular, for \(3\nmid n\), the degree-\(d\) **rational** representation is \(\mathbf1\oplus\varepsilon\), where \(\varepsilon(r)=1\), \(\varepsilon(s)=-1\); for \(3\mid n\) it is the induced module \(\operatorname{Ind}_{C_n}^{D_n}W\), where \(W\) is the two-dimensional rational representation on which \(r\) acts with characteristic polynomial \(z^2+z+1\).

For any reflection \(u\in D_n\), its **actual geometric fixed-point space** is two disjoint simplices: if \(n=2m+1\), then \(|X_n|^u\cong|\Delta^{m-2}\sqcup\Delta^{m-2}|\); if \(n=2m\), vertex-axis reflections give two \((m-2)\)-simplices and edge-axis reflections give two \((m-3)\)-simplices. Consequently, the dihedral quotient has \(\widetilde H_2(|X_n|/D_n;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q\), and in degree \(d\) has \(\mathbb Q\) exactly when \(3\nmid n\), zero otherwise. All other reduced rational quotient homology vanishes.

## Assumptions and scope
The cycle has vertices \(v_i\) and edges \(e_i=\{v_i,v_{i+1}\}\), indices modulo \(n\). The \(2n\) potential matching vertices are \(a_i=(v_i,e_i)\) and \(b_i=(v_{i+1},e_i)\). Strong matchings arise from vertex filtrations as defined by Knudson–Owens–White; \(X_n\) is generated by maximal-cardinality strong matchings, **not** the full strong matching complex or ordinary graph matching complex. The action is \(r(v_i)=v_{i+1}\), \(s(v_i)=v_{-i}\). Hence \(s(a_i)=b_{-i-1}\) and \(s(b_i)=a_{-i-1}\). The induced module is on integral lattices; the character and quotient statements use \(\mathbb Q\).

## Proof
**1. Facets and invariant cover.** Proposition 8.3 of Knudson–Owens–White gives precisely \(n(n-1)\) facets: choose two consecutive omitted cycle edges and orient the remaining path toward one root, so its incidence list is one consecutive block of \(a\)-vertices followed by one of \(b\)-vertices. Explicitly, with cyclic edge start \(t\) and \(0\le j\le n-2\), the facet is \(\{a_t,\ldots,a_{t+j-1}\}\cup\{b_{t+j},\ldots,b_{t+n-3}\}\). The even-only subcomplex \(E\) and odd-only subcomplex \(O\) each have facets given by complements of adjacent vertex pairs; thus both are the combinatorial Alexander dual \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_n)^*\) of the cycle independence complex on \(n\) positions. Rotation preserves \(E,O\) and reflection interchanges them. The proof of Theorem 8.6 in the primary paper constructs a cover \(A\cup B=X_n\) by the two specified paired-star pieces, with \(A\simeq E\), \(B\simeq O\) naturally under cyclic relabeling, and \(A\cap B\simeq S^1\). Moreover the inclusions of \(E,O\) into the corresponding pieces are homotopy equivalences. For \(d>2\), the inclusions \(E,O\hookrightarrow X_n\) give the canonical direct sum decomposition of top homology by Mayer–Vietoris. Reflection exchanges its summands; therefore the integral \(D_n\)-module is induced from the integral rotation module on \(E\). Equivariant simplicial Alexander duality, with its orientation determinant on the ordered ground set, gives \(\widetilde H_d(E;\mathbb Z)\cong\det(\mathbb Z^n)\otimes\widetilde H^{n-d-3}(\operatorname{Ind}(C_n);\mathbb Z)\). The standard cycle-independence homotopy theorem makes this torsion-free of rank one when \(3\nmid n\), and rank two when \(3\mid n\).

**2. Rotation character.** Every one-step-rotation-invariant independent subset of cycle vertices is empty. Its reduced chain Lefschetz trace is therefore \(-1\). Cycle independence homology is concentrated in degree \(h=n-d-3\), giving trace \((-1)^{h+1}\) on its integral homology and cohomology. The \(n\)-cycle determinant contributes \((-1)^{n-1}\); hence rotation has trace \((-1)^{n+h}\) on \(\widetilde H_d(E;\mathbb Q)\), equal to \(+1\) in rank one and \(-1\) in rank two. Rank-one rotation is therefore trivial; the rank-two rational finite-order module with generator trace \(-1\) is the cubic cyclotomic module \(W\). The published all-rotation reduced Lefschetz formula for these **pure** complexes is \(5\) if \(3\mid\gcd(n,a)\), and \(-1\) otherwise. Subtracting the just-determined high-degree rotation character (with its degree parity) leaves trace \(+1\) on \(\widetilde H_2\) for every rotation. This establishes the full rotation action on the lower integral rank-one lattice.

**3. Geometric reflection fixed points.** For \(u=r^j s\), every incidence vertex lies in a two-element orbit \(\{a_i,b_{j-i-1}\}\). A face is fixed setwise exactly when it is a union of these orbits. In the rooted-path facet indexed by \((t,h)\), orbit \(i\) is included exactly when both cyclic conditions \(i\in[t,t+h-1]\) and \(j-i-1\in[t+h,t+n-3]\) hold. Applying the two cyclic interval conditions, the maximal admissible orbit-index subsets, for the representative \(j=0\), are precisely \(\{1,\ldots,m-1\}\) and \(\{m+2,\ldots,2m\}\) if \(n=2m+1\), or \(\{1,\ldots,m-1\}\) and \(\{m+1,\ldots,2m-1\}\) if \(n=2m\). For even \(n=2m\), the second class \(j=1\) gives \(\{2,\ldots,m-1\}\) and \(\{m+2,\ldots,2m-1\}\). To check exhaustiveness algebraically, intersect the two cyclic intervals for each \(t,h\); each nonempty intersection is a consecutive subinterval of exactly one of the two displayed blocks. Their full blocks occur for the all-one-sign endpoint facets, so these are all maximal faces. For odd \(n\) all reflections are conjugate; for even \(n\), each parity of \(j\) is conjugate. Replacing each orbit by its barycenter identifies the geometric fixed set with the realization of its orbit-face complex, which is the stated disjoint union of two simplices.

**4. The remaining integral character and quotients.** A reflection exchanges the two summands \(\widetilde H_d(E)\) and \(\widetilde H_d(O)\), so its trace on top rational homology is zero. Its fixed space is two contractible components; the reduced Lefschetz number is \(1\). Since the only other reduced homology is degree two, the reflection trace on \(\widetilde H_2\) is \(1\), proving the entire dihedral action there is trivial integrally. Over \(\mathbb Q\), the two rational high modules now have the decompositions stated in the finding. Subdivide barycentrically to eliminate simplex inversions: quotient rational homology equals invariant rational homology. By Frobenius reciprocity, \(\dim(\operatorname{Ind}_{C_n}^{D_n}U)^{D_n}=\dim U^{C_n}\), equal to one for a trivial rank-one \(U\) and zero for the cubic module \(W\). Together with invariant \(\widetilde H_2\), this proves the quotient result. The primary paper's nonequivariant theorem supplies the stated vanishing and integral torsion-freeness in all other degrees.

## Verification
A standalone stdlib checker `verify.py` constructs facets from rooted paths and reconstructs the orbit-face complexes directly for a finite range of cycle sizes. It checks the geometric fixed-set maximal simplices in both reflection classes and independently computes the reduced signed Lefschetz character through fixed incidence orbits. These bounded checks support, but do not substitute for, the all-parameter proofs via the primary cover, equivariant Alexander duality, cyclic Lefschetz and interval classification. The verifier uses no external files.

## Relationship to prior work
Knudson–Owens–White, arXiv:2609.06844v1 (public September 6, 2026), establish the nonequivariant sphere-wedge homotopy classification and the facet and cover structure, but do not classify dihedral representations, geometric reflection fixed sets or quotient homology. The published published-finding corpus finding *The threefold rotation character of pure strong Morse matching complexes of cycles* already gives the cyclic virtual homology character and certain graded rotational constituents; it explicitly leaves the even, non-three-divisible graded rotation action unresolved and makes no reflection claim. The present theorem uses that cyclic character and adds the integral induced-lattice classification, all missing graded rotations and reflections, actual fixed spaces and quotient Betti numbers. Published results for the full strong complex and for ordinary cycle matching complexes concern different simplicial complexes and do not imply this theorem.

## Limitations
This is a classification of ordinary integral homology representations and geometric reflection fixed loci, not an integral decomposition of quotient homology, an equivariant homotopy splitting, or the full equivariant homotopy type. The fixed-space assertion is for reflections, not arbitrary subgroup fixed spaces. Related unpublished or unindexed arguments may exist.
