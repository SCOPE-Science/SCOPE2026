# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The graph-to-complex identification follows by exhaustively classifying directed-spoke faces. The first cycle lattice is an integer row-and-column kernel of rank \(m^2-3m+1\), and integral four-cycle generation follows from chord splitting and the explicit six-cycle identity. For \(m\ge5\), a five-label telescoping identity kills the unique orbit generator; for \(m=4\), a unit minor, an explicit primitive relation, and an invariant parity functional prove exactly \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\); for \(m=3\), the cycle is the sign representation. A distinct Smith-normal-form implementation passes on six consecutive parameters; it corroborates but does not replace the all-parameter proof. Risks: graph conventions must use vertex-disjoint directed paths, and the result applies only for \(m\ge3\).

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The natural object is the multipath complex of a bidirected star and, equivalently, the integral cycle lattice of a crown graph with diagonal symmetric-group action. Searches of indexed published mathematical claims and external literature for the object, coinvariants, group homology, integral cycle representations, and stronger symmetric graph-complex classifications identified no covering result. The 2026 Deshpande–Sawant study addresses vertex decomposability and conflict graphs; the 2022 Caputi et al. paper addresses homotopy types, not the claimed integral coinvariants. The crown graph's ordinary graph-theoretic properties are known and expressly excluded from the originality assertion. Risk: there could be a differently indexed representation-theoretic computation of the same module.

An intrinsic integral equivariant invariant of a natural directed-forest complex is completely determined for all star sizes. The first two exceptional cases carry genuine order-two coinvariants despite vanishing rational coinvariants, and the obstruction disappears exactly at five leaves. The explicit integral presentation isolates the mechanism of the threshold and is more informative than a nonequivariant graph homology rank or isolated small computation. It does not assert a quotient-space theorem or downstream spectral-sequence collapse.
