# Integral mixed-dimensional homotopy of the Pappus graph two-independence complex

## Finding

Let \(P\) be the Pappus graph, the 18-vertex cubic Levi graph of the Pappus \((9_3)\) configuration. Define its two-independence complex by
\[
X=\operatorname{Ind}_{2}(P)=\{\sigma\subseteq V(P):\text{every component of }P[\sigma]\text{ has at most two vertices}\}.
\]
Then the **integral homotopy type**, rather than just the homology, is
\[
\boxed{X\simeq\left(\bigvee^{9}S^{5}\right)\vee S^{6}.}
\]
In particular the only nonzero reduced integral homology groups are \(\widetilde H_{5}(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{9}\) and \(\widetilde H_{6}(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\). This is a mixed-dimensional sphere wedge, with one sphere in the higher degree.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph is specified without requiring a drawing. Set \(V(P)=\{0,\ldots,17\}\), include the 18 cycle edges \(\{i,i+1\bmod 18\}\), and add the distinct unordered edges \(\{i,i+s_i\bmod18\}\) for the length-18 LCF sequence
\[
(s_0,\ldots,s_{17})=(5,7,-7,7,-7,-5,5,7,-7,7,-7,-5,5,7,-7,7,-7,-5).
\]
This gives 27 distinct edges, cubic degree three, girth six, and a connected bipartite graph. Every vertex subset \(\sigma\) lies in \(X\) precisely when the induced degree of every vertex of \(P[\sigma]\) is at most one. Equivalently, \(X\) is the ordinary hypergraph independence complex of the 3-uniform hypergraph whose hyperedges are vertex sets of length-two paths in \(P\). Thus a face may contain isolated edges, but no induced connected triple. The empty set is included in the abstract simplicial complex, but is not a cell in the ordinary nonempty face poset used below. Bit \(i\) in an integer mask represents graph vertex \(i\).

The theorem concerns only this canonical graph and the parameter two. The proof uses ordinary finite simplicial and CW homotopy, with integral cell orientations given by increasing vertex labels. It makes no claim about other higher-independence parameters or equivariant homotopy.

## Proof

**1. Exhaust all simplices.** Enumerate vertex masks by starting with the empty mask. For vertices \(i=0,\ldots,17\) in order, retain each existing mask and append its union with \(\{i\}\) if the new vertex has at most one selected neighbor and none of its selected neighbors already has a selected neighbor. These are necessary and sufficient conditions for keeping induced maximum degree at most one. Because vertices are considered once and in increasing order, every permitted subset is generated exactly once. An independent loop over all \(2^{18}\) masks checking the defining induced-degree condition verifies exact agreement with this recursion. There are exactly 20,651 nonempty simplices, whose face numbers from dimensions zero through nine are
\[
(18,153,762,2394,4806,6027,4428,1728,308,27).
\]

**2. Construct and certify an acyclic matching.** Use the vertex-toggle procedure on the nonempty face poset. At stage \(i\), pair every still-unmatched face \(\sigma\) not containing vertex \(i\) to \(\sigma\cup\{i\}\) whenever both faces remain available, then remove all pairs before stage \(i+1\). Faces are visited in increasing numerical mask order. The construction gives exactly 10,320 matched face pairs and leaves eleven critical faces: one vertex, nine faces of dimension five, and one face of dimension six. The critical five-face masks, in ascending order, are

```text
[13104, 26146, 39186, 52230, 74340, 100486, 135576, 148618, 196812]
```

The sole critical six-face mask is `183554`. Reverse every matched cover in the Hasse diagram, leaving all other face-cover arrows directed from a simplex to its codimension-one faces. An exhaustive topological sort of this **entire** directed graph visits all 20,651 face nodes and all 120,222 covering incidences. Hence the graph is acyclic and the procedure is a genuine discrete Morse matching. Forman's theorem supplies a CW model \(Y\simeq X\) with exactly one zero-cell, nine five-cells and one six-cell, and no other cells.

**3. Compute the integral attaching degrees.** The five-skeleton of \(Y\) is a wedge of nine five-spheres. The cellular differential of its single six-cell is computed by exact signed gradient-path elimination in the original simplicial chains, not by mod-two linear algebra. Orient each simplex by its increasing vertex order. For every six-vertex (dimension-five) face \(\tau\), define a projection \(T(\tau)\) to the free integral group generated by the nine critical five-faces. If \(\tau\) is critical, this is its own basis vector. If \(\tau\) is matched downward to a four-dimensional cell, put \(T(\tau)=0\). Otherwise \(\tau\) is the lower partner of a six-dimensional face \(\eta\). Write the signed simplicial boundary as
\[
\partial\eta=a\tau+\sum_{\tau'\ne\tau}b_{\tau'}\tau',\qquad a\in\{+1,-1\},
\]
and recursively set
\[
T(\tau)=-a\sum_{\tau'\ne\tau}b_{\tau'}T(\tau').
\]
Here \(a^{-1}=a\), so every operation is exact over \(\mathbb Z\); the acyclic matching guarantees termination of the gradient-path expansion. The resulting Morse boundary of the sole critical six-cell \(c\) is
\[
\partial^{\mathrm{Morse}}c=\sum_{\tau\text{ a five-face of }c}[c:\tau]T(\tau).
\]
The independent executable certificate fully expands 933 distinct intermediate projected faces and obtains the complete **integer** nine-component boundary vector
\[
\boxed{(0,0,0,0,0,0,0,0,0).}
\]
This is the cellular degree vector of the six-cell attaching map in the Morse CW model. Nothing is inferred from a finite-field rank alone.

**4. Deduce the homotopy type.** The five-skeleton of \(Y\) has one zero-cell and nine five-cells; it is \(\bigvee^{9}S^{5}\), which is 4-connected. Its integral Hurewicz map identifies
\[
\pi_5\left(\bigvee^{9}S^{5}\right)\cong H_5\left(\bigvee^{9}S^{5};\mathbb Z\right)\cong\mathbb Z^{9}.
\]
Thus the homotopy class of the attaching map \(S^5\to\bigvee^{9}S^5\) is determined by its integer cellular degree vector. Step 3 shows that vector is zero, so the attaching map is nullhomotopic and the six-cell wedges on an \(S^6\). The resulting homotopy equivalence is precisely the stated wedge of nine five-spheres and one six-sphere.

## Verification

The dependency-free `verify_pappus_ind2.py` reproduces the full calculation with Python 3. It constructs the LCF graph, verifies that it is cubic, connected, bipartite and girth six; exhaustively compares two independently implemented face enumerators (including all 262,144 vertex masks); constructs the 10,320 matching pairs and their critical masks; and checks acyclicity by topologically sorting the complete reversed-matching Hasse graph. It then evaluates **signed integer** Morse differential coefficients using a complete memoized gradient-path recursion and requires the nine-component vector to vanish exactly. Finally, it independently forms all original simplicial chain boundaries over \(\mathbb F_2\) and computes the ranks for boundary degrees one through nine:

```text
[17, 136, 626, 1768, 3038, 2980, 1447, 281, 27]
```

These give unreduced mod-two Betti numbers in degrees zero through nine \((1,0,0,0,0,9,1,0,0,0)\), agreeing with the integral proof. This mod-two calculation is corroborative only; the homotopy classification follows from the acyclic matching **and the exact integral degree computation**. From a directory containing the two embedded verification files, run:

```text
python3 verify_pappus_ind2.py
```

The archived `verify_pappus_ind2.txt` contains the expected standard output; success ends with `ALL_CHECKS_PASS`. The code exhausts this finite graph, not an infinite family.

## Relationship to prior work

Conant and Thistlethwaite develop the independence and Alexander-dual theta complexes of finite hypergraphs; their archive-era preprint supplies the hypergraph viewpoint, but its cubical and other illustrated families do not give the present Pappus path-hypergraph calculation. Deshpande and Singh define and study higher independence complexes and establish homotopy types for complete multipartite, fully whiskered, path, cycle and perfect rooted-tree graph families; this cubic nonchordal Levi graph is none of them. The latter paper's statements do not imply the present mixed-dimensional classification.

An exact preceding calculation of the **ordinary matching complex** of the same Pappus graph gives a wedge of forty five-spheres, but its complex is defined on graph **edges**, whereas the present two-independence complex has graph **vertices** as simplicial vertices and forbids every induced three-vertex path. Even the preceding calculation of the **ordinary** Pappus independence complex uses a different face restriction and does not imply this result. Higher-independence computations for the Heawood, Desargues and Möbius–Kantor graphs concern different underlying graphs. No cited theorem supplies either this exact 20,651-face model or its nonzero degree-six homotopy summand. Full-text checks and targeted indexed-record comparisons have found no covering statement, which is evidence of a gap rather than proof of publication priority.

## Limitations

This is an exact finite calculation on a fixed 18-vertex graph, not an all-graphs theorem. Its integer Morse differential is reproducible through a complete executable certificate; the proof depends on the stated finite computations and cannot be replaced by sampled experiments or field homology. It does not determine any automorphism-group representation, integral symmetry quotient, all higher-independence parameters, or all graph families. An equivalent unindexed or otherwise inaccessible computation may exist; independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
