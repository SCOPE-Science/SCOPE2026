# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The exact rank decomposition follows directly from the absence of directed cycles and from the independence of tail/head constraints between rank layers. The two outside rank factors are discrete four-point spaces. Each middle factor is a 162-nonempty-face matching complex for the incidence graph of the tetrahedron, with a reproducible complete acyclic Morse matching leaving one critical vertex and five critical triangles. Each has exactly 78 matched pairs and 432 checked Hasse incidences. The resulting integral homotopy type follows from the join theorem. The rational representation follows from a signed fixed-face Lefschetz computation and the equivariant reduced join Künneth isomorphism. A separate full-graph dynamic program independently reproduces all 664224 nonempty faces, and the full 24-element symmetry computation gives the claimed character.

## Originality — PASS against inspected evidence

The primary 2021 path-poset paper gives definitions and topological interpretation, while the 2022 multipath-homotopy paper develops join decompositions and computes particular one- and two-dimensional graph families. Their inspected sections do not give the exact four-dimensional rank-oriented cube classification or its homology representation. The general decomposition alone does not determine the topology of the middle subdivided-tetrahedron matching factor; the new finite Morse computation supplies it. Checks for alternate terms included the tetrahedron incidence graph, once-subdivided complete graph, and Boolean-lattice Hasse digraph. Relevant public mathematical-result searches found other cube complexes and matchings but no claim implying the present homotopy or representation statement. Risks: an unindexed calculation of the same subdivided-graph matching complex, or an overlooked stronger general matching-complex theorem, could reduce novelty.

## Value — PASS

The four-cube is the first Boolean-lattice Hasse orientation whose internal rank factors contain the subdivided tetrahedral incidence graph rather than a path/cycle-type factor. Its exact integral topology and the full natural coordinate-symmetry representation determine a canonical nontrivial finite case of multipath topology. The character distinguishes the richer equivariant topology from the mere wedge multiplicity and yields ten invariant classes. This is not a routine isolated face count; the proof identifies the reusable rank-layer mechanism and a complete small structural Morse calculation. It makes no unsupported extrapolation to larger cubes.

## Closest literature and limitations

Caputi–Collari–Di Trani, arXiv:2110.11206, Sections 2 and 6, and Caputi–Collari–Di Trani–Smith, arXiv:2208.04656, Sections 4.1 and 4.3, are the primary relevant sources. The first establishes the simplicial setting; the second develops general module factorization and two-row grid calculations. The result is for the upward Hasse orientation of the four-cube, not all cube orientations, and includes no equivariant wedge splitting. An equivalent previously unpublished or unindexed result is still possible.
