# Integral sphere-wedge classification of the borderline squared-cycle cut complexes through \(k=30\)

## Finding
For each integer \(3\le k\le30\), the ordinary \(k\)-cut complex of the squared cycle \(W_{k+5}\) has **integral homotopy equivalence**
\[
\Delta_k(W_{k+5})\simeq S^3\vee\bigvee^{\beta(k)}S^4,\qquad
\beta(k)=\frac{(k-3)(k-2)(k+5)}6.
\]
For \(k=3\), the zero-multiplicity wedge is absent. These are 28 separately checked finite cases, not a theorem for all positive integers.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph \(W_n=C_n^2\) has vertices \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\), with distinct vertices adjacent exactly when their cyclic distance equals \(1\) or \(2\). Its ordinary \(k\)-cut complex is the simplicial complex generated by the complements of all disconnected induced \(k\)-vertex subgraphs. Only the borderline \(n=k+5\), hence \(8\le n\le35\), is considered. All homology and homotopy statements are integral unless otherwise specified. A four-dimensional complex can have homotopy equivalent CW models of smaller effective dimension.

## Proof
**1. Exact facets.** Each complement of a candidate \(k\)-set consists of exactly five cyclic vertices. Deleting five vertices from \(W_n\) disconnects the survivor-induced graph if and only if the deletion word has at least two maximal cyclic runs of length at least two. Indeed, each surviving cyclic run is internally connected. Runs separated by one deleted vertex are connected across the distance-two edge; separated by two or more deleted vertices they are not. Since the deletion set has five vertices, disconnectivity is therefore equivalent to two deleted runs of length at least two. The checker generates all \(\binom n5\) possible deletion sets, retains precisely those satisfying this criterion and closes them downward under taking nonempty subsets. For \(n\le18\), a separate breadth-first-search connectedness test of every induced survivor graph verifies the characterization exhaustively. For all \(8\le n\le35\), the facet count is checked to equal \(n\binom{n-5}{2}\).

**2. Constructed acyclic matching.** The included `verify.py` implements a deterministic sequence of vertex-pivot matchings on nonempty simplices, pivoting in the vertex order \(0,1,\ldots,n-1\). At each pivot, it matches each still-unmatched simplex not containing the pivot to its pivot extension when both remain available. For every \(8\le n\le35\), the program *independently* constructs the complete directed Hasse diagram, reverses precisely the matched incidences and performs a full topological sort. Every one of the \(1,393,910\) tested Hasse incidences across 28 complexes belongs to the verified acyclic directed graph. The matching leaves exactly one critical vertex, \(n-7\) critical three-simplices, and \(\beta(k)+n-8\) critical four-simplices; there are no critical simplices of any other dimension.

**3. Exact integral Morse differential.** The checker evaluates the integral boundary \(\partial_4\) by recursively eliminating matched codimension-one pairs, using standard orientation signs on ordered simplices. In a prescribed sorted critical-cell basis, it checks that precisely \(n-8\) columns are nonzero and are exactly
\[
e_1-e_{n-7},\quad e_2-e_{n-7},\quad\ldots,\quad e_{n-8}-e_{n-7}.
\]
All remaining columns vanish identically. Thus the Morse boundary has a primitive rank-\(n-8\) integer image. In particular the reduced cellular homology is \(\widetilde H_3\cong\mathbb Z\) and \(\widetilde H_4\cong\mathbb Z^{\beta(k)}\), with all other reduced groups zero and no torsion.

**4. Integral homotopy, not just homology.** The Morse CW model has one zero-cell and cells only in dimensions three and four. Its three-skeleton is a wedge of three-spheres. By the Hurewicz theorem for this simply connected skeleton, the attaching classes of four-cells are determined by their integral degree vectors. The explicitly primitive degree vectors above permit homotopy basis changes and successive degree-one cell cancellations, leaving exactly one three-cell and \(\beta(k)\) trivially attached four-cells. This proves the displayed homotopy equivalence, without inferring a wedge merely from Betti numbers.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with Python 3.9 or later; the script uses only the standard library and has no external input dependencies. It checks every integer \(8\le n\le35\) individually. Summed over the range, it enumerates 125,853 maximal simplices and 335,867 nonempty simplices, constructs 150,171 Morse pairs, checks all 1,393,910 directed Hasse incidences, and recomputes every critical-cell count and integral Morse differential column. It independently checks the mod-two simplicial boundary ranks for nine representative cases, including both endpoints and intermediate values (see source for the exact indices). The recorded output `verify.out` ends with the exact marker `BORDERLINE_SQUARED_CYCLE_FINITE_INTEGRAL_HOMOTOPY_VERIFIED`. Its last case \(n=35\), \(k=30\) has 39,200 nonempty faces, 17,367 matched pairs, 28 critical three-cells, 4,437 critical four-cells, and a 27-dimensional primitive boundary image, leaving 4,410 four-spheres. The executable is the proof certificate for the finite matching and differential assertions; the mathematical run-gap and homotopy-cancellation arguments above supply their interpretation.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer, Denker, Jelić Milutinović, Rowlands, Sundaram and Xue proposed the exact borderline sphere-wedge shape in **Conjecture 7.23** and the multiplicity formula over \(3\le k\le15\) in **Conjecture 7.24**. They did not prove the borderline homotopy assertion. A previously published exact integral calculation for \(k=16\) is acknowledged and is not a separate novelty claim here. A published all-parameter face-vector/Euler-characteristic formula for this same borderline family does **not** imply the homotopy equivalence: Euler characteristic alone cannot determine the integral attaching maps or even the individual Betti numbers. Work on the \(n=k+6\) diagonal likewise does not imply this result. The sequel on squared *paths* and grids does not classify squared cycles in this regime; results about **total** cut complexes concern a different definition. The newly certified range \(17\le k\le30\) extends the published numerical range while providing homotopy, not just matching Euler characteristics.

## Limitations
No assertion is made for \(k\ge31\), and the computations do not establish a uniform all-parameter theorem. Acyclicity and signed differential are certified by explicit finite enumeration rather than a closed-form symbolic analysis of the matching. The existing standalone \(k=16\) result remains prior work. Equivalent unindexed literature may exist; the bibliographic assessment is not an independent audit. No group-action or equivariant classification is claimed.
