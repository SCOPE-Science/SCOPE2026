# Integral two-degree homology of ordinary three-cut complexes of all binary hypercubes
## Finding
For every integer \(d\ge3\), let \(Q_d\) be the graph on the binary vectors \(\{0,1\}^d\), with edges at Hamming distance one; put \(N=2^d\). Let \(\Delta_3(Q_d)\) be the **ordinary** three-cut complex: its facets are the complements of triples inducing disconnected subgraphs. Define
\[
\mu_d=2^{d-3}(d^2-5d+8)-1,\qquad h_d=2^{d-1}\sum_{j=3}^{d}\binom dj.
\]
Then the complete reduced **integral** homology is
\[
\widetilde H_i(\Delta_3(Q_d);\mathbb Z)\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z^{\mu_d},&i=N-5,\\
\mathbb Z^{h_d},&i=N-4,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The more structural conclusion is that the combinatorial Alexander dual \(K_d=\Delta_3(Q_d)^*\) collapses simplicially onto a triangulation of the cubical two-skeleton of the \(d\)-cube, together with exactly \(h_d\) edges joining vertices at Hamming distance at least three. In particular
\[
K_d\simeq\bigvee^{\mu_d} S^2\vee\bigvee^{h_d}S^1.
\]
The cut complex is pure of dimension \(N-4\), while \(\mu_d>0\), so it is not shellable. For \(d=3,4,5\), the two nonzero reduced Betti numbers are respectively \((1,4)\), \((7,40)\), and \((31,256)\) in degrees \((3,4)\), \((11,12)\), and \((27,28)\).

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, simple, and undirected. A triple is disconnected precisely when its induced graph is not connected. The ordinary three-cut complex is generated by the complements of disconnected triples, **not** only by complements of independent triples (the latter defines a total cut complex). All homology groups use integral coefficients unless stated otherwise. For a simplicial complex \(L\) on \(V\), \(L^*=\{S\subseteq V:V\setminus S\notin L\}\) denotes its combinatorial Alexander dual. The claim applies to each integer \(d\ge3\), including \(d=3\); the previously known \(d=3\) case is background, not the claimed family advance. No homotopy type is claimed for \(\Delta_3(Q_d)\) itself.

## Proof
**1. Characterize the dual faces.** By definition, \(S\subseteq V\) belongs to \(K_d\) if and only if it contains no disconnected triple, equivalently every triple in \(S\) induces a connected subgraph. Thus all vertices and edges of the complete graph on \(V\) belong to \(K_d\). The binary cube is triangle-free: a connected triple is a length-two path with a unique center and two neighbors obtained by changing two different coordinates. It lies in a unique coordinate square, whose fourth vertex is obtained by changing both coordinates. A four-set whose every triple is connected must induce a four-cycle: in a triangle-free graph, each vertex is adjacent to at least two of the other three vertices; at least four edges occur, and no more than four can occur in a triangle-free graph on four vertices. Every four-cycle of the cube is a coordinate square. No five-set lies in \(K_d\), because among five vertices at least three have the same bipartition parity and hence form a disconnected triple. Therefore the maximal dual faces above dimension one are exactly one tetrahedron on each coordinate square, together with its four triangular faces. There are \(2^{d-2}\binom d2\) squares.

**2. Elementary collapses.** Orient the vertices of any coordinate square cyclically as \(a,b,c,z\). Its tetrahedron \(\{a,b,c,z\}\) is the only tetrahedron containing its triangle \(\{a,b,z\}\), because every connected triple belongs to precisely one square. Delete this free triangle together with the tetrahedron. Now the triangle \(\{b,c,z\}\) has the diagonal edge \(\{b,z\}\) as a free face: two cube vertices at Hamming distance two have exactly two common neighbors, namely \(a,c\), so this diagonal occurs in the two triangles of this square only, and the first has been removed. Delete the second pair. All these chosen faces belong to unique squares, so the collapses over distinct squares do not interfere. Each square retains the two triangles \(\{a,b,c\}\) and \(\{a,c,z\}\), which triangulate its coordinate square along diagonal \(\{a,c\}\). Besides the edges in these triangulated squares, precisely the edges joining vertices at Hamming distance at least three remain. Hence \(K_d\) collapses onto \(T_d\cup E_{\mathrm{long}}\), where \(T_d\) is a triangulation of the cubical two-skeleton and \(E_{\mathrm{long}}\) consists of the long edges with endpoints in \(T_d\).

**3. Identify the dual homotopy type.** The cubical two-skeleton \(T_d\) is simply connected: the full cubical cell structure of the contractible \(d\)-cube is obtained by adding cells of dimension at least three, which do not change its fundamental group. Let \(V=2^d\), \(E=d2^{d-1}\), and \(F=\binom d2 2^{d-2}\) count its vertices, cube-edges, and square two-cells. It has integral homology only in degree two, freely of rank
\[
F-E+V-1=2^{d-3}(d^2-5d+8)-1=\mu_d.
\]
A simply connected finite two-dimensional CW complex with free second homology of this rank is homotopy equivalent to the indicated wedge of \(\mu_d\) two-spheres, by Hurewicz and homology Whitehead. Each long edge with endpoints in the connected \(T_d\) adds one independent loop to the homotopy type; choosing paths in a spanning tree produces a wedge splitting. There are \(2^{d-1}\binom dj\) unordered vertex pairs at Hamming distance \(j\), giving exactly \(h_d\) long edges for \(j\ge3\). Therefore \(K_d\simeq\bigvee^{\mu_d}S^2\vee\bigvee^{h_d}S^1\).

**4. Integral Alexander duality.** Since the homology of \(K_d\) in degrees one and two is free, the universal coefficient theorem identifies its nonzero reduced integral cohomology as \(\widetilde H^1(K_d;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^{h_d}\) and \(\widetilde H^2(K_d;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^{\mu_d}\). Combinatorial Alexander duality gives
\[
\widetilde H_i(\Delta_3(Q_d);\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H^{N-i-3}(K_d;\mathbb Z).
\]
This gives precisely the two groups in the Finding and excludes torsion and all other reduced homology. Disconnected triples exist for every \(d\ge3\), and their complements all have size \(N-3\), making the cut complex pure of dimension \(N-4\). Its nonzero degree-\(N-5\) homology contradicts the homological consequence of shellability for pure complexes; therefore it is non-shellable.

## Verification
The bundled standalone Python 3 checker `verify.py` reconstructs the dual independently from the induced-subgraph definition (enumerating connected triples and four-sets), checks the unique-square incidence conditions and the surviving square triangulation, and calculates the full mod-two simplicial boundary ranks for \(d=3,4,5\). For those dimensions it confirms respectively \((H_1(K_d),H_2(K_d))=(4,1),(40,7),(256,31)\) and exact face counts. Its success marker is `HYPERCUBE_ORDINARY_THREE_CUT_DUAL_FINITE_CHECKS_OK`. These finite computations corroborate but do not replace the above all-dimensional integral proof. The standalone checker computes over \(\mathbb F_2\); integral torsion-freeness follows from the explicit simplicial collapse and duality, not from a modular computation.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer, Denker, Jelić Milutinović, Rowlands, Sundaram and Xue introduced ordinary cut complexes and proved structural results under graph-girth and skeleton hypotheses (arXiv:2304.13675, first posted 2023-04-26); those hypotheses do not imply this family classification, since hypercubes have four-cycles. The earlier published exact \(d=3\) cube computation gives \(\Delta_3(Q_3)\simeq S^3\vee\bigvee^4S^4\) and explicitly does not extend to higher-dimensional cubes. A separate high-girth cut-complex theorem applies when girth is at least five for three-cuts, so cannot be applied to hypercubes. The previously classified Lovász *open-neighborhood* complexes of hypercubes have a parity-component sphere multiplicity numerically equal to \(\mu_d\), but they are different simplicial complexes and that classification does not imply either of the present cut-homology groups. A prior total three-cut computation for \(Q_4\) likewise uses independent-triple complements instead of the present disconnected-triple complements.

## Limitations
The claim classifies integral homology and the **Alexander-dual** homotopy type, not the homotopy type, cup products, or symmetry representation of the cut complex itself. The existence of the lower-degree homology establishes non-shellability but says nothing about nonpure shellings or every induced subgraph. The computer checker covers \(d=3,4,5\) only; the proof is uniform. Literature comparisons used the relevant primary text and indexed mathematical findings; substantially differently stated or unindexed equivalent results remain a priority risk, and no independent audit has been performed.
