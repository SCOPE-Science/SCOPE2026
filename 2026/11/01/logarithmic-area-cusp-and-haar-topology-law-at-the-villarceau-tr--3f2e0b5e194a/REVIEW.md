# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The radial quadratic normal form gives positive endpoints, the annulus/two-disk criterion, a full angular area integral, and the Haar tilt density without simulation. The only singular angles are two quadratic zeros of the angular discriminant. The explicitly integrated two-sided model \(\int\sqrt{(r^2t^2+\delta)_+}\,dt\) supplies the exact logarithmic coefficient, and the remainder is controlled by a cutoff and smooth Taylor estimates. The independent checker verified three radius pairs, the critical area and endpoints, the coefficient, and twelve planar raster comparisons. Universal assertions rest on the analytic proof, not the finite checks.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Classical Villarceau circles and tangency angles are covered by García Monera–Monterde (2011) and the wider history of toric sections. Their plane/torus intersection and moon-shaped construction are correctly attributed. The present claim instead quantifies the **planar area of the solid** near the double-tangent direction and derives an interior logarithmic *zero* in the density of random section areas, with exact constants and a topology-probability law. Compared with projected silhouettes of tori, central intersections have different geometry and thresholds. Semantic record searches and relevant full-text inspection did not reveal the exact area/density law. Older stereological or elliptic-integral sources could still encode an equivalent asymptotic, so independent priority verification is necessary. Absence in searched records is not claimed as proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS
The natural random-section area statistic of a classical nonconvex solid has a topological transition at which ordinary differentiability fails. The calculation gives a sharp measurable singular coefficient and an induced probability-density signature, not merely the known Villarceau angle or a change in contour drawing. It supplies a tractable benchmark for stereological inversion and saddle-transition asymptotics with both exact geometry and Haar normalization.

## Limitations and closest literature
The theorem is only for centered planes of ring tori with \(0<r<R\). No uniform degeneration limit is proved. García Monera–Monterde (2011) is the closest exact Villarceau construction; Moroni (2017) discusses general toric section curves; Breda–Trocado–Dos Santos (2024) treats a different intersection family. The 2011-11-26 date is the earliest directly verified **day-precise** public supporting-source date used here. A cited 2010 Bibnum article and the 2011 journal article might have appeared earlier; their original first public days were not established by the inspected record.
