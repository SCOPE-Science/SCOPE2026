# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For each fixed odd \(n\ge3\), the covariogram identity converts the target pair-distance probability to polar integration over the near-unit radial shell. Every direction belongs to one of \(2n\) vertex-to-arc or reverse sectors. In the interior of a sector, tangent-cone coordinates give a wedge-simplex overlap coefficient \(c_n(\phi)=\tfrac12(\cot\phi+\cot(\pi/n-\phi))\). On sectors trimmed by \(\sqrt{s}\log(1/s)\), disk curvature errors are uniformly lower order. On the excluded endpoints, \(g\le C_n\min\{s^{3/2},s^2/\Delta\}\) yields only \(O_n(s^2\log\log(1/s))\) in the angular integral. The primitive of \(c_n\) then proves \(G_n(s)\sim ns^2\log(1/s)\) and the sharp probability coefficient. The bundled numerical checks cover local cases only; they are not substituted for this proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result gives one sharp asymptotic classification for an infinite natural family singled out by a recent symmetry-minimization theorem. The logarithmic endpoint singularity arises from a geometric transition between corner and curved-arc contacts. The normalized coefficient \(3A_n^2\) times the observed leading tail coefficient reconstructs the corner count \(n\). This is neither an arbitrary numerical slice nor an automatic consequence of the known area formula.

## Scientific limitations and risks

The result concerns fixed odd \(n\), independent area-uniform sampling, and width-one regular Reuleaux polygons. It makes no uniform-in-\(n\) claim or full-distribution claim. Historical priority remains provisional, particularly for unindexed covariogram endpoint-asymptotic literature. Numerical calculations supplement, but never certify, the analytic proof.
