# Low-degree fixed-point persistence and integral symmetry for binary-translation Rips filtrations

## Finding
Let \(Q_n=\{0,1\}^n\) have the Hamming metric, \(n\ge2\), and let \(H\le\mathbb F_2^n\) be nontrivial and act by translation. Assume every nonzero vector of \(H\) has Hamming weight at most two. These are precisely the nontrivial translation subgroups with nonempty geometric fixed points at Rips scale two. Define
\[
F_H(r)=\big|\operatorname{VR}_{\le r}(Q_n)\big|^H,
\qquad r\ge0.
\]
The following assertions hold at **every real filtration scale**, with natural PL identifications on each nonempty range.

1. If \(H\) is generated by a weight-one vector, \(F_H(r)=\varnothing\) for \(r<1\), while
\[
F_H(r)\cong_{\mathrm{PL}}\big|\operatorname{VR}_{\le r-1}(Q_{n-1})\big|\qquad (r\ge1).
\]
2. If \(\dim H=2\), \(F_H(r)=\varnothing\) for \(r<2\), while
\[
F_H(r)\cong_{\mathrm{PL}}\big|\operatorname{VR}_{\le r-2}(Q_{n-2})\big|\qquad (r\ge2).
\]
Here \(Q_0\) is the one-point metric space. There are exactly two support types of such two-dimensional subgroups: the full translation group on two coordinates, and the even-parity subgroup on three coordinates.
3. If \(H\) is generated by a weight-two vector, \(F_H(r)=\varnothing\) for \(r<2\). For \(r\ge2\), its canonical orbit-vertex triangulation is the clique complex of the graph on \(\{0,1\}\times Q_m\), \(m=n-2\), in which distinct vertices \((a,y),(b,z)\) are adjacent exactly when
\[
d_H(y,z)\le \begin{cases}r-2,&a=b,\\r-1,&a\ne b.\end{cases}
\]
At \(r=2\) this graph is isomorphic to the ordinary \((m+1)\)-cube graph. **For every \(r\ge3\), the geometric fixed-point space is simply connected.** In particular, the inclusion \(F_H(2)\hookrightarrow F_H(3)\) is nullhomotopic.

Set \(\beta(0)=0\) and \(\beta(d)=1+2^{d-1}(d-2)\) for integers \(d\ge1\). The **complete reduced integral persistence in homological degrees zero and one**, or equivalently its barcode over any field, is:

| Subgroup type | Reduced degree-zero bars | Degree-one bars |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One generator of weight one | \(2^{n-1}-1\) copies of \([1,2)\) | \(\beta(n-1)\) copies of \([2,3)\) |
| One generator of weight two | None | \(\beta(n-1)\) copies of \([2,3)\) |
| Two-dimensional subgroup | \(2^{n-2}-1\) copies of \([2,3)\) | \(\beta(n-2)\) copies of \([3,4)\) |

Zero multiplicities mean no intervals. There are no other reduced bars in these two degrees. All the relevant first-homology groups are torsion-free. No claim is made about higher-degree persistence for the exceptional weight-two cyclic case.

There is also an **integral residual-symmetry refinement** in the exceptional weight-two case. Write the scale-two fixed graph with two color classes \(A_y,B_y\) indexed by \(y\in Q_m\), and oriented edges \(e_{y,z}:A_y\to B_z\) whenever \(d_H(y,z)\le1\). The image of the ambient centralizer of \(H\) on this graph is \(C_2\times\operatorname{Aut}(Q_m)\). Its integral first-homology module is exactly
\[
L_m=\ker\!\left(\partial:\mathbb Z\langle e_{y,z}:d_H(y,z)\le1\rangle\longrightarrow
\mathbb Z\langle A_y,B_y:y\in Q_m\rangle\right),
\qquad\partial e_{y,z}=B_z-A_y.
\]
The \(C_2\)-factor exchanges the two color classes, acting on oriented edges with a sign reversal. Explicitly, \((0,h)e_{y,z}=e_{h(y),h(z)}\) and \((1,h)e_{y,z}=-e_{h(z),h(y)}\). For \(h\in\operatorname{Aut}(Q_m)\), let \(f(h)\) count fixed cube vertices, \(e(h)\) count cube edges whose **two endpoints are both fixed**, and let
\[
q(h)=\#\{y\in Q_m:h^2(y)=y,\ d_H(y,h(y))\le1\}.
\]
The complete rational character of this integral lattice is
\[
\operatorname{tr}_{L_m}(\epsilon,h)=
\begin{cases}
1-f(h)+2e(h),&\epsilon=0,\\
1-q(h),&\epsilon=1.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, for \(m\ge1\), the pure color-swap eigenspaces have respective \((+1,-1)\) ranks \(\big(1+2^{m-1}(m-2),\,m2^{m-1}\big)\). This lattice and character describe the symmetry carried by the sole nonzero one-dimensional persistent layer.

## Assumptions and scope
A finite group of coordinate translations acts simplicially and isometrically on each inclusive Vietoris--Rips complex. By a *geometric fixed-point space* we mean the fixed points of the action on its geometric realization, not a complex on individually fixed vertices. The signed character refers to the image of the **centralizer** of the two-coordinate translation in the hypercube isometry group; the stabilizer of the moved coordinate pair and the remaining-coordinate signed permutations generate this image. All homotopy and homology claims concern the fixed spaces, rather than the fixed subgroups of the ambient homology. The underlying radius-two orbit classifications had already been determined; the new content is the all-scale comparison, the sharp later death of exceptional first homology, and the integral residual symmetry.

## Proof
**Orbit-simplex lemma.** If a finite group acts by permuting vertices of a finite simplicial complex, its geometric fixed space is PL homeomorphic to the simplicial complex with one vertex at each invariant vertex-orbit that is itself a face; a collection of orbit-vertices is a simplex precisely when the union of its orbits is a face. Indeed, within every invariant simplex fixed barycentric coordinates are constant on each vertex-orbit. Its fixed slice is the convex hull of the orbit centroids, and the slices glue compatibly on overlaps.

For translations, every orbit is a coset of \(H\). For cosets \(U,V\), define the **maximum cross-distance** \(\Delta(U,V)=\max_{u\in U,v\in V}d_H(u,v)\). Every coset is a face at scale \(r\) exactly when its diameter is at most \(r\), and compatible cosets are joined exactly when their maximum cross-distance is at most \(r\). Because the original Rips complex is flag, these pairwise conditions characterize all fixed simplices.

**Classification and the shifted models.** Up to permuting coordinates, the nonzero subgroups with all nonzero vectors of weight at most two have the three forms stated above. The two-dimensional alternatives are generated either by two unit vectors, or by two weight-two vectors sharing one coordinate; the latter has the three nonzero vectors supported on the three edges of a coordinate triangle. A third independent vector cannot preserve the weight bound: in the absence of unit vectors its support would have to meet each of the three triangle edges in exactly one endpoint, which no new edge can do.

For a weight-one generator, label each coset by the \((n-1)\) untouched bits. Within a coset the maximum distance is one, and between cosets it is \(1+d_H(y,z)\). This proves the shift by one. For the full two-coordinate translation subgroup, label cosets by the \((n-2)\) untouched bits; the diameter is two and cross-distance is \(2+d_H(y,z)\). For the triangle-even subgroup, label cosets by the parity of the first three bits and the remaining \((n-3)\) bits. Each coset has diameter two; two cosets of the same first-three-bit parity have maximum distance two in those bits, while opposite parities have maximum distance three. Their cross-distance is therefore \(2+d_H(y,z)\) for the resulting \((n-2)\)-bit labels. Both cases give the shift by two.

**Exceptional two-coordinate translation.** After moving the two flipped positions to the front, label the two possible cosets in their first two coordinates by \(a=0\) (the pair \(00,11\)) and \(a=1\) (the pair \(01,10\)); write the untouched label as \(y\in Q_m\). Two distinct cosets have maximum cross-distance \(2+d_H(y,z)\) if they have the same \(a\), and \(1+d_H(y,z)\) if their \(a\)'s differ. This gives the exact graph at every scale. At scale two it is bipartite, with edges \(A_yB_z\) for \(d_H(y,z)\le1\). It is the graph \(Q_{m+1}\): map \(A_y\) to \((y,p(y))\) and \(B_y\) to \((y,1-p(y))\), where \(p(y)\) is the parity of \(y\). This map is a graph isomorphism.

**The sharp scale-three connectivity transition.** For \(m\ge1\), inside the scale-three fixed complex let \(U\) be the union of tetrahedra with vertices \(A_y,B_y,A_z,B_z\) for the edges \(yz\) of \(Q_m\); when \(m=0\), use the single edge \(A_0B_0\). Sending both \(A_y\) and \(B_y\) to \(y\), and sectioning by \(y\mapsto A_y\), are contiguous simplicial maps on \(U\). Thus \(U\simeq Q_m\). Every additional scale-three edge is \(A_yB_z\) with \(d_H(y,z)=2\); choosing a common cube neighbor \(w\) gives the triangle \(A_yA_wB_z\), homotoping that edge into \(U\). Additional triangles cannot introduce generators of the fundamental group.

Every elementary square \(y_0y_1y_2y_3y_0\) of \(Q_m\) is already nullhomotopic in the scale-three complex: the four section vertices \(A_{y_i}\) are all adjacent to \(B_{y_2}\), and the four resulting triangles form a cone on the square. Squares normally generate \(\pi_1(Q_m)\), because the cubical complex of \(Q_m\) with its square two-cells is the two-skeleton of a contractible cube. Hence the scale-three fixed complex is simply connected. At any larger scale, each newly admitted same-color edge of cube-label distance at least two, and each newly admitted cross-color edge of label distance at least three, lies in a triangle with an edge of cube-label distance one and a strictly shorter edge of the same type. Induction homotopes every such new edge into the scale-three one-skeleton. Attaching higher-dimensional simplices cannot create fundamental-group generators, so \(F_H(r)\) remains simply connected for all \(r\ge3\). The scale-two complex is a connected graph, so its inclusion into this simply connected target is nullhomotopic.

**Low-degree barcodes.** The ordinary cube graph \(Q_d\) is connected and has \(2^d\) vertices and \(d2^{d-1}\) edges when \(d\ge1\). Thus its integral cycle rank is \(\beta(d)\) as stated. The Vietoris--Rips complex \(\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(Q_d)\) is simply connected: the extra length-two edges are homotopic through triangles to length-two cube paths, and each cubical square is filled. At every larger scale all additional edges are homotopic through triangles to shorter ones, so this complex remains simply connected. Applying the two shift identifications, or the exceptional scale-three argument, shows precisely the births and deaths in the displayed barcode table. The explicit graph models give free integral first homology and make the barcode statements valid over arbitrary fields.

**Residual integral action.** The centralizer of the two-coordinate flip consists of all bit translations and coordinate permutations preserving the flipped coordinate pair. Its action on cosets factors through a color-swap \(C_2\) and the full isometry group of the remaining cube. The oriented graph chain complex yields the displayed integral kernel, and connectivity gives the exact equivariant sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow L_m\longrightarrow C_1\xrightarrow{\partial}C_0\xrightarrow{\sum}\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0.
\]
For a color-preserving isometry \(h\), the vertex-chain trace is \(2f(h)\) and the oriented edge-chain trace is \(f(h)+2e(h)\). For a color swap composed with \(h\), the vertex-chain trace is zero, while every fixed oriented edge is reversed; the number of such edges is \(q(h)\). Taking alternating traces in the exact sequence proves the character formula. The eigenspace ranks for the pure swap follow from its trace and the cycle rank.

## Relationship to prior work
The previously established binary-translation **radius-two** fixed-locus classification already identifies the cube graph for weight-one and weight-two cyclic subgroups, and the discrete orbit sets for two-dimensional subgroups. Its homotopy ranks are used here as prior information rather than asserted as new. The present result determines all-scale PL reductions for the remaining two subgroup types, proves the exceptional sharp vanishing of fundamental groups at scale three, identifies the complete low-degree fixed-point barcodes, and gives the exceptional integral residual-symmetry module.

Adamaszek and Adams established the ambient radius-two cube Rips homotopy classification; Adams and Virk studied ambient persistence; Bendersky, Elia and Grbić studied ambient cohomology and connectivity; Galetto, Montaño and Wellner studied ambient hyperoctahedral homology representations. None of these results identifies the geometric fixed-point filtration and residual first-homology lattice asserted here. The distinction between the homology of a fixed locus and the fixed part of ambient homology is essential.

## Limitations
The full all-scale classification is given only for translation subgroups whose nonzero codewords all have weight at most two. Other translation subgroups may develop nonempty fixed loci at higher thresholds. In the exceptional weight-two cyclic case, the result establishes a complete orbit-graph model at every scale, exact topology at scale two, simple connectivity at scales three and above, and degree-zero/one persistence, but not the higher-dimensional homotopy types or persistence modules. The character formula determines the rational character of an explicitly realized integral lattice, not its decomposition into integral irreducibles. Full text of the recent ambient equivariant-representation preprint could not be inspected through the available access routes; this remains an originality-check limitation. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
