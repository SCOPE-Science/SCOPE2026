For every Euclidean ellipsoid, the smallest ball that contains it while touching a prescribed boundary point along its inward normal has radius \(a^2/h(\nu)\), where \(a\) is its longest semiaxis and \(h\) its centered support function. All additional sharp contacts are reflections along maximal-axis eigendirections, and this exact global pointwise radius can exceed the local curvature radius by an arbitrarily large factor.
