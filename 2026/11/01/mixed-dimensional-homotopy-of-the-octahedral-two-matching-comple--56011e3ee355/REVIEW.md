# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For the complete tripartite octahedral graph, membership was checked in two independent ways for every one of the 4096 edge subsets. The complete augmented face poset has 772 faces and 2940 Hasse incidences. The supplied algorithm pairs 378 faces in each direction and verifies acyclicity by exhausting the entire directed Hasse graph; six critical three-cells and ten critical four-cells remain. Exact integral unit-pivot cancellations construct the signed six-by-ten Morse differential. Its last two rows vanish, its fourth row is the negative sum of the first three, and a selected three-by-three minor is +1. Hence the attaching matrix is equivalent over the integers to three primitive unit attachments and zeros. Since the critical three-skeleton is a wedge of three-spheres and its fourth-cell maps are classified by integral degree vectors, these cancellations give the asserted sphere wedge integrally. Independent mod-two simplicial boundary ranks corroborate the Betti numbers. The finite claim has been reconstructed and checked using the actual attached verifier and output.

Closest source [1] develops two-matching homotopy for leafed/clawed graphs, wheels, and caterpillars; [2] classifies specified higher matching complexes of complete and complete bipartite graphs. The octahedral graph is neither a wheel nor complete bipartite, and neither theorem covers its two-matching complex. Searches of equivalent names (octahedral graph, \(K_{2,2,2}\), bounded-degree complex) and comparison of returned graph-complex records distinguished the ordinary matching complex, Morse-vector-field complex, and complete-bipartite two-matching cases: none implies this classification. ## Value — PASS

The octahedral graph is a canonical regular nonbipartite complete tripartite graph; the cutoff-two condition is the natural first relaxation of matchings. Its 5-dimensional nonpure two-matching complex has unexpectedly mixed 3- and 4-dimensional homotopy, distinct from the nearby single-sphere results for complete-bipartite examples. The precise integral homotopy type is a complete, motivated finite invariant and a useful comparison point for bounded-degree complexes; no universal graph-family claim is made.
