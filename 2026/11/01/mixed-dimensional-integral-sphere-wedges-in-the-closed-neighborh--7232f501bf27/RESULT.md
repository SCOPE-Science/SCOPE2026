# Mixed-dimensional integral sphere wedges in the closed-neighborhood complex of \(J(6,3)\)

## Finding

Let \(J(6,3)\) be the Johnson graph on the \(20\) three-element subsets of \([6]\), with two distinct triples adjacent when they have exactly two common elements. Its closed-neighborhood complex \(K=\mathcal N[J(6,3)]\), equivalently the intrinsic vertex-centered graph Čech nerve of radius \(1\), is generated by the closed balls of radius one. The precise integral homotopy classification is

\[
|K|\simeq\left(\bigvee^{11}S^{3}\right)\vee\left(\bigvee^{30}S^{4}\right).
\]

The complex has \(20\) pairwise distinct facets of cardinality \(10\) and is therefore pure of dimension \(9\), notwithstanding its homotopy concentration in dimensions three and four. In particular, \(\widetilde H_3(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{11}\), \(\widetilde H_4(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{30}\), and all other reduced integral homology groups vanish. As a pure complex with homology below its facet dimension, \(K\) is not shellable.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite, undirected, simple. Set \(V=\binom{[6]}{3}\), ordered lexicographically, and define \(N[v]=\{w\in V:|v\cap w|\ge 2\}\). Each neighborhood has \(1+3\cdot3=10\) vertices. Define \(K\) to contain exactly the subsets \(\sigma\subseteq V\) for which \(\sigma\subseteq N[v]\) for some \(v\in V\). The empty set is included for chain bookkeeping. All spheres and homotopy equivalences here are ordinary, nonequivariant and integral; no all-parameter Johnson-graph assertion is made.

## Proof

**Face enumeration.** Encode the \(20\) triples by bit positions \(0,\ldots,19\). Generate all \(2^{10}\) subfaces of each of the \(20\) closed neighborhoods, deduplicating by the resulting bitset. The complete face vector, indexed by dimension \(-1\) through \(9\), is

\[
(f_{-1},f_0,\ldots,f_9)=(1,20,180,960,2850,4500,4110,2400,900,200,20).
\]

In particular there are \(16,141\) total faces including the empty face. Every facet is one of the \(20\) distinct neighborhoods, which are all size \(10\); the asserted purity follows.

**Explicit valid matching.** The included executable `verify.py` first builds, for every nonempty face, its current codimension-one coface set. It repeatedly removes a face \(\sigma\) together with its unique codimension-one coface \(\tau\) whenever that coface set has size one. Since any proper higher coface would force at least two distinct codimension-one cofaces, \(\tau\) is maximal; each such step is an ordinary elementary simplicial collapse. Exactly \(6,814\) pairs are removed. The remaining simplicial core has dimensional face counts \( (f_{-1},f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4)=(1,20,180,684,1066,562)\).

In this core, shuffle bit positions \(0,\ldots,19\) using the fixed Python `random.Random(220)` permutation. For each position in that order, and in descending face size then ascending face-bitset order, match any still-unmatched nonempty \(\sigma\) not containing the position with \(\sigma\cup\{i\}\) if both remain. This contributes \(1,233\) further pairs, for a total of \(8,047\). The resulting critical nonempty faces comprise precisely one vertex, \(13\) three-simplices and \(32\) four-simplices. This face algorithm specifies a completely explicit matching, rather than a claimed but unavailable existence certificate.

**Full acyclicity.** Orient each codimension-one incidence in the complete original nonempty face poset downward except along matching pairs, whose arrows are reversed. All \(87,800\) incidences among \(16,140\) nonempty faces are inserted. A topological sort visits every vertex and hence proves that the directed Hasse graph is acyclic. Forman's discrete Morse theorem produces a CW model with one \(0\)-cell, \(13\) cells of dimension \(3\), and \(32\) cells of dimension \(4\), and no other positive-dimensional cells.

**Integral Morse boundary and homotopy cancellation.** Orient each original simplex by increasing vertex index. For a matched pair \(\sigma^3\subset\tau^4\) with signed boundary coefficient \(a=\pm1\), the algebraic Morse reduction substitutes the identity

\[
[\sigma]\longmapsto -a^{-1}\sum_{\rho\in\partial\tau,\ \rho\ne\sigma}[\tau:\rho]\,[\rho].
\]

Apply these substitutions recursively in the acyclic gradient order, discarding degree-three cells that are upper members of pairs and retaining critical degree-three cells. This computes the exact integer Morse boundary from all \(32\) critical four-cells to the \(13\) critical three-cells. The resulting \(13\times32\) matrix has precisely \(14\) nonzero entries, and exact Smith normal form over \(\mathbb Z\) has nonzero diagonal \(\operatorname{diag}(1,1)\), with all other entries zero. Both pivotal invariant factors are units: no finite torsion is hidden in the calculation.

The three-skeleton of this CW complex is a wedge of \(13\) three-spheres. Since \(\pi_3(\bigvee^{13}S^3)\cong\mathbb Z^{13}\) by Hurewicz, the integer attaching-degree matrix completely determines the attaching maps of the \(32\) four-cells up to homotopy. Integral elementary row/column basis changes, corresponding to homotopy equivalences and cell slides, bring the matrix to the two unit diagonal blocks and zero otherwise. Two three/four-cell pairs cancel; the remaining CW complex is the wedge of \(11\) three-spheres and \(30\) four-spheres. This establishes the claimed actual homotopy type, not just homology ranks.

## Relationship to prior work

Matsushita [1] introduces and systematically studies this *closed* neighborhood complex (including its relation to graph Čech complexes, graph fundamental groups, and independence complexes of canonical double covers). The primary paper does not calculate the Johnson graph \(J(6,3)\). The indexed theorem *Sphere-wedge collapse of scale-one Johnson-graph Rips complexes* [2] proves that the radius-one **Vietoris–Rips / clique complex** of \(J(6,3)\) is a wedge of \(19\) two-spheres. This is a different simplicial complex: a Rips face requires all vertices pairwise adjacent, whereas a closed-neighborhood face requires one center within distance one of every vertex and need not be a clique. Thus [2] neither asserts nor implies the present mixed-dimensional Čech classification. The Johnson clique descriptions of Shuldiner–Oldford [3] likewise govern cliques, not the closed-neighborhood nerve. No equivalent result for this precise object was identified among the inspected sources; an unindexed equivalent computation remains possible.

## Limitations

This is a single, naturally selected finite self-complementary Johnson graph, not a formula for arbitrary Johnson parameters, Čech radii or group actions. The enumerative and Morse calculations are exact and exhaustive for this finite graph, but the program has not been independently validated by a separate mathematical audit. The originality assessment depends on available indexed literature and inspected primary texts and is not proof of absolute priority.
