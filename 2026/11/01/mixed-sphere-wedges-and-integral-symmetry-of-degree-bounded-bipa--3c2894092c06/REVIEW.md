# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For each mixed face of the degree-bounded complex on a complete bipartite graph, the two positive part sizes must both be at most the induced degree threshold. This gives the exact union of two full simplices and a join of simplex skeleta. The skeleta have integral homology identified naturally with exterior powers of augmentation lattices, and their join has one nonzero top degree. Nullhomotopic attaching maps give the claimed mixed sphere wedge; the relative long exact sequence supplies both **integral equivariant** homology modules. The Koszul sign for interchanging two join cycles has exponent the square of the threshold. Independent finite simplicial-chain calculations for four cases and all indicated automorphism samples agree in dimensions, low-degree vanishing, and signed Lefschetz traces. Risk: the proof, not the finite samples, bears the uniform and torsion-free conclusions.

## Originality — PASS, with documented residual risk
The primary source Guo–Zhao–Lai–Mao, arXiv:1505.04867, defines induced-degree-bounded vertex sets and studies numerical independence numbers, including complete multipartite cases; it does not supply topology or integral homology actions. Barmak, arXiv:1007.0418, treats ordinary (degree-zero) independence complexes and a distinct Kneser graph. Targeted searches on induced degree, complete bipartite graphs, join/skeleton topology and symmetric-group homology returned no identical statement; the closest published entries found concern edge-set two-matching or Morse complexes, which use different vertices and face conditions. An uncatalogued equivalent consequence of a broader simplicial-complex theorem remains possible, especially because the present decomposition is elementary once recognized.

## Value — PASS
Degree-bounded independent sets are a natural established extension of independent vertex sets. Their full feasible-set complex, rather than just its extremal cardinality, has a uniform two-degree homotopy classification, exact integral homology lattices and a parity-sensitive action under exchanging equal parts. The result distinguishes vertex-feasible and edge-matching complexes, determines all Betti numbers and provides a reusable structured example of equivariant join/attachment calculations. The matched degrees and signed tensor interchange are information not supplied by published cardinality results.

## Closest literature and limits

The r-independence terminology used elsewhere may instead bound connected-component size, so scope is fixed explicitly by maximum *induced degree*. No equivariant wedge decomposition, unbounded-degree generalization, or independent certification is claimed.
