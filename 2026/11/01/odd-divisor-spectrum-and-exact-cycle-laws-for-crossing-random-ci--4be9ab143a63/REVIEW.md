# Scientific review: crossing-chord spectral and cycle law

## Correctness — PASS

The sample measure is derived from the independent endpoint angles, with half-angle uniform on the half-interval and rotation midpoint independent uniform. The chord crossing criterion is tested against cyclic alternation of four direct endpoint coordinates. Fourier integration yields a signed Green kernel normalized by the density \(2/\pi\). The boundary conditions at the maximal half-angle split into Dirichlet and Neumann cases according to Fourier parity; the complete sine basis gives nonzero eigenvalues \(4/[\pi^2(k^2-m^2)]\). Difference-of-two-squares factorization gives the exact odd-divisor multiplicities, not just a numerical finite spectrum. Hilbert--Schmidt control legitimizes the traces of all powers used for the cycle laws. The independent finite chord-diagram counts and mode residual tests pass, but the exact infinite conclusions rely on the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS, with provisional historical priority

Actual literature comparison examined Bortolotto–Ramos, arXiv:2406.06771v1 (two-chord intersection probability and variational laws); Bortolotto–Souza, arXiv:2504.12866v1 (distribution of the spatial intersection point); Diaconis–Holmes–Janson, arXiv:1102.2841v1 section 10.2 (general chord/circle graphon representation); Acan, arXiv:1501.01489v1 (random chord diagrams, degree distributions and components); and John Gates's 1982 finite-chord moment paper via available metadata. General circle-graph kernel representation and the probability \(1/3\) for two chords are already known and expressly excluded from novelty. No inspected primary result states the signed odd-divisor spectral multiplicities or implies the complete zeta-formula family without a new Fourier--Green analysis. The Gates article's full text was inaccessible, and a previous diagonalization might exist under alternate terminology; these are material priority risks rather than claims of certified uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The exact spectrum of a natural geometric crossing operator provides an unbounded family of exact incidence probabilities for independent random chords. Positive and negative eigenvalue multiplicities encode arithmetic factorization; the resulting distinction between odd and even cycle lengths and rationality of every cycle event is a structural consequence, not a mere low-order numerical integral. This is motivated by the known crossing-incidence and circle-graph model. No downstream theorem or global significance beyond these precise statements is claimed.
