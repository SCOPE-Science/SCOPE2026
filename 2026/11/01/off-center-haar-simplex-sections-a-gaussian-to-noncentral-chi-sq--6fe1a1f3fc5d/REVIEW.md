# Scientific review: off-center random simplex section face vectors

## Correctness
**PASS.** The theorem reduces every generic affine section face count to the single number of vertex heights above the offset. The random unit normal is represented **exactly** by centered and studentized independent Gaussians, not by independent threshold indicators. A moving-threshold empirical-cdf expansion gives the exact two-correction influence function; Gaussian Hermite orthogonality proves its stated strictly positive variance. The independent central limit theorem followed by first-order or second-order polynomial expansion yields respectively the nonzero-offset Gaussian law and the near-centroid shifted chi-square law. Uniform-in-dimension convergence is asserted only for fixed face ranks. The standalone verifier passed 3,551 finite geometric, exact combinatorial and numerical diagnostics; analytic proof establishes all-dimensional claims.

## Originality
**PASS under documented best-knowledge historical uncertainty.** The closest publicly available central-section face-vector theorem already contains the zero-offset chi-square case, so the present statement explicitly does not claim it. The noncentral result requires a distinct exact studentization normalization and produces a nonzero-offset Gaussian variance and a shifted chi-square local limit absent from the earlier statements. A different axial-through-point random-section model has a random offset depending on the normal and a centered, rescaled chi-square critical law; it is not the deterministic signed-offset model and its statement does not cover the shifted law here. Ambrus–Gárgyán's central-volume theorem and König's noncentral-volume theorem, examined through their accessible HTML texts, concern volume extrema rather than Haar-distributed face vectors. Equivalent formulations under standardized-residual exceedance statistics remain a possible earlier source, not excluded by mathematical-title searches. No assertion of unique worldwide priority or independent verification is made.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The object is a natural metric perturbation of random hyperplane slicing, with two different fluctuation scales. The theorem identifies the sharp order \(m^{-1}\) physical displacement that changes the central face-vector deficit law, and the finite-fixed-offset regime in which the combinatorial limit is instead Gaussian. Its variance removes exactly the first two Gaussian-score directions, exposing a geometric effect of the Haar normalization. The result gives a reusable probabilistic benchmark for section-combinatorics studies.

## Limitations and literature
Only regular simplices, Haar random hyperplane directions, and fixed face ranks are treated. Source comparison covers full accessible HTML statements of Ambrus–Gárgyán (arXiv:2609.12714) and König (arXiv:2312.01325), the 2017 Dirksen paper's summary, and full scientific statements of the published central section studies listed in RESULT.md. An equivalent older probability or geometric formulation may exist. Finite diagnostics do not certify weak convergence. The review is not independent.
