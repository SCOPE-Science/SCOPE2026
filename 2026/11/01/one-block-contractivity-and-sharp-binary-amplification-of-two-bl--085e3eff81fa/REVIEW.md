# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For the stated power-normalized Lorentz gauge with \(1<p<\infty\), \(0<q<1\), the decreasing fixed-sum simplex decomposition and strict concavity prove the one-block inequality and its exact equality witness. The two-block binary input formula is obtained by telescoping rank weights; the dense integer-grid limit is exact. The logarithmic derivative has at most two critical roots and its first is the unique global maximizer. Both endpoint regimes follow analytically from exact formulas. `CHECK.py` supplements these steps with 42,704 finite tests; no inference from finite enumeration to infinite dimensionality is used.

## Originality — PASS, best-knowledge
Albiac, Ansorena, and Berasategui (arXiv:2605.11295v1, 2026) prove uniform boundedness for averaging projections on the same quasi-Banach Lorentz class. Their bounds neither identify coefficient-one one-block contractivity for the chosen gauge nor provide the exact binary two-block phase and endpoint scaling. Public research-record and primary-literature comparisons also considered projection norms in weighted Cantor Lipschitz-free spaces, mixing norms of conditional expectations on Banach \(L^p\), and contractive projections on classical \(L^p\). Different objects and/or local convexity hypotheses prevent logical coverage. A prior equivalent majorization or quasi-Banach projection argument might still exist under unsearched terminology. Independently verified priority is not claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The one-/two-block threshold is a natural sharp quantitative refinement of bounded averaging in a nonlocally convex Lorentz class. The closed restricted amplification profile distinguishes coefficient one from strict expansion, and its exponentially small defect as \(q\uparrow1\) gives a precise singular bridge to the Banach regime. A full sharp unrestricted two-block norm and other equivalent gauges are not determined.

## Scientific limitations
The optimum is restricted to binary vectors, not all input vectors. Contractivity is normalization-sensitive, even among equivalent gauges. Evidence from finite tests cannot replace the provided proof; source access and publication priority remain subject to independent review.
