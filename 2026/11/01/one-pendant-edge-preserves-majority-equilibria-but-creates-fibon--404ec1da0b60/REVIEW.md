# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The symmetric-matrix integer potential proves period at most two under the exact tie convention, with a strict increment whenever two-step stationarity fails. Flipping a degree-two rim vertex forces its neighbors to flip, propagating to a unique alternating even-cycle orbit. Otherwise only the attachment vertex and its leaf flip, and the fixed rim word must have opposite endpoints and constant runs of length at least two. The run-composition generating function gives the Fibonacci-minus-six-periodic count. A four-state transfer trace establishes unchanged fixed-point counts. Direct simulation and independent counting checks reproduce all formulas. Risk: finite tests never prove the infinite theorem; the proof supplies that step.

## Originality
PASS with historical residual risk. General finite-graph period-at-most-two behavior is credited to Goles and Olivos, and full-text Benjamini et al. discusses that theorem and the standard majority rule, not this one-leaf exact census. Nearby recurrent censuses for chorded cycles, sunlets (a leaf at *every* cycle vertex), and unperturbed cycles concern different graphs or richer symmetries; none logically implies the one-pendant-edge count without new local flip analysis. An inaccessible full 1980 note and potentially unindexed threshold-automata literature leave nonzero uncertainty. Lack of a search match is not asserted to prove novelty.

## Mathematical value
PASS. A single pendant leaf is the smallest vertex attachment to a cycle. The exact theorem shows an invariant equilibrium population coexisting with a newly created exponentially growing two-cycle population. The structural flip-set dichotomy explains the effect, and the all-length census resolves a natural discrete-dynamical invariant, including the striking activation of oscillations on every odd cycle with at least five vertices. The result is not presented as a solution to transient-basins or broader graph families.
