# Scientific review: open neighborhoods of high-girth line graphs

## Correctness — PASS
The claim concerns all finite connected simple graphs with no triangles or quadrilaterals and minimum degree at least three. The proof reconstructs every simplex as part of the open-neighborhood simplex attached to an original edge. It identifies the embedded vertex-star boundaries, proves their point-intersection incidence graph is the original graph, proves the two-component attachment for every edge, and uses the exclusion of three- and four-cycles to show that distinct attachments intersect only in the established sphere subcomplex. Each attachment contributes precisely one circle through a CW homotopy pushout. This proves the integral wedge decomposition and the canonical higher-degree orientation generators, rather than merely computing Betti numbers. The projective-plane specialization uses exact vertex and incidence counts. The independent finite checker reconstructs the complexes and boundary operators for four distinct examples, including a mixed-degree graph with a bridge; those tests corroborate but do not establish the all-graph theorem. Residual correctness risk is restricted to undetected subtleties in the stated graph-of-spaces and gluing arguments, which have been reconstructed directly.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
Primary sources inspected include Kozlov's 2005 simple-homotopy equivalence of neighborhood and Hom complexes; Kamibeppu's 2008 four-cycle-free box-complex theorem; Matsushita's 2012 fundamental-group description; and Goyal–Shukla–Singh's 2020 clique-complex results for line graphs. None states the open-neighborhood wedge formula. The closest published research-record statements are: block-graph neighborhood sphere bouquets, graph-like neighborhoods with edge-disjoint quadrilaterals, and a closed-neighborhood Čech collapse for line graphs. Their material was read, and the implications compared. The block-graph theorem applies to tree line graphs but not the cyclic high-girth minimum-degree-three domain. The quadrilateral theorem can recover the all-cubic circle-only special case, but fails on the overlapping quadrilaterals of line graphs with degree-four vertices. The closed-neighborhood result cannot imply the open-neighborhood theorem: for the order-three projective-plane incidence graph, the open complex has 26 independent integral two-sphere classes, whereas the closed Čech complex collapses to a graph. The current theorem's full domain, higher spheres, and canonical integral higher-homology modules were not covered by the inspected results. Unindexed or differently named equivalent work remains possible; no absolute publication-priority certificate is asserted.

## Value — PASS
This is a uniform structural classification of a natural topological graph invariant on an unrestricted infinite class containing cubic and higher-degree graphs, graphs with bridges, and all finite projective-plane incidence graphs. The local degree sequence determines every higher sphere dimension and multiplicity while the graph cycle rank controls global circles. For a projective plane of order three the result yields 79 first-homology classes and 26 second-homology classes in a canonical line-graph neighborhood complex; the full integral and symmetry-localized statements are not consequences of existing closed-neighborhood or clique-complex results. Exact topology, the local-to-global decomposition, and natural signed orientation modules give a substantive invariant beyond a small isolated computation.
