# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

Writing a state as a support A, the map Φ(A)=V∖N(A) reverses order, and A⊆Φ²(A) by symmetry of adjacency. The two inclusions for Φ³(A)=Φ(A) follow by reversing that inclusion and applying it at Φ(A); this gives recurrence after one step even with isolates. On a join, any support meeting both factors goes to ∅, and ∅ goes to the whole join. All other recurrent orbits lie on one side and are exactly the factor orbits avoiding the empty support. In a nonempty factor Φ(∅)=V and Φ(V) is the isolate set, so a primitive periodic orbit involving ∅ must be the empty/full cycle, present exactly without isolates. This gives precisely the two subtractions in Q, and fixed supports are the usual maximal independent sets confined to one side. The global two-cycle basin is the empty set of ones and all total-dominating supports, since no nonempty support maps to the whole isolate-free join. The three disjoint types of total-dominating sets give the asserted count. For K_m, E=m and Q=δ, including m=1, and T=2^m−m−1, so the clique formulas follow. For C₅ the recurrent images are ∅, the whole graph, five singleton/triple pairs and five nonadjacent pairs fixed, giving E=5 and Q=6; five triples, five quadruples and the whole graph are total dominating, giving the listed basin of 233 for the join with K₃.

## originality

PASS

Credit is for the arbitrary two-factor dynamical decomposition, especially the exact primitive-orbit correction for isolates and its relation to basin capture, not for the polarity identity, fixed-point interpretation or total-domination count in isolation. Subramanian–Selvakumar's 2025 graph theorem gives the latter count exactly; with the basin-to-domination identification it supplies the numerical basin formula. It does not treat periodic-orbit inheritance. None of the inspected prior dynamics or catalogue sources gives the corrected orbit law for arbitrary open-NOR factors. A later indexed clique-coning record matches a substantial specialization but not joins of arbitrary factors or an isolate-bearing base, and does not establish earlier priority.

## value

PASS

A join is a natural way to couple such networks. The result resolves both which oscillations survive and whether the coupling concentrates attraction: a bounded, isolate-sensitive change in the number of cycles can coexist with almost all initial supports feeding one distinguished attractor under clique growth. The exact deletion rule and concentration link are useful beyond a recomputed total-domination polynomial or a small examples table.
