# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The same graph is obtained from the exact moment-curve supporting-polynomial sign criterion and from Gale evenness. The proof establishes that all its vertices are pairs, each cyclic-polytope facet makes three distinct doubled graph edges, and the only obstruction to a path into the cycle-edge component is equal endpoint parity when the ground cycle is even. It then constructs explicit paths within that second component and counts vertices and edges separately. The graph-Euler formula gives integral circle ranks; it does not extrapolate from the finite checker. The checker independently verifies facet and graph formulas for 25 parameters from 5 to 32, including both parities.

## Originality — PASS (subject to residual uncertainty)
Lesh–Schreiner–Wahl (arXiv:2506.10436) define doubling and give a general connectivity lower bound, while their full-simplex case recovers ordinary matching complexes. For \(n=5\), the double is the already-known matching complex of \(K_5\); no novelty is claimed for that boundary case. For \(n\ge6\), the paper does not supply the facet-restricted cyclic-four-polytope classification. Gale evenness is an essential known input rather than the claimed contribution. Searches for pairing-graph aliases, exact ranks and stronger general coverage returned no matching statement. One cannot rule out equivalent unindexed or differently named work; the accessible cyclic-polytope exposition was read only in its relevant portion, not treated as a complete negative-document search.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a natural complete topological classification for an infinite family of doubles of triangulated three-spheres, a geometrically motivated test family beyond the original full-simplex examples. The parity obstruction shows that the general wCM doubling connectivity estimate cannot uniformly be improved to connectedness in dimension three, and determines the two different free integral first-homology ranks rather than just a component count. The main value is the structural transition, not a routine face-count formula.

## Scope and limitations
The theorem does not classify arbitrary polytopal spheres, higher doubles or the degree-one symmetry representation. The literature comparison is bounded and cannot establish absolute uniqueness. The finite verification serves only as corroboration; the all-parameter conclusion rests on the displayed proof. The homotopy equivalences are ordinary, not equivariant.
