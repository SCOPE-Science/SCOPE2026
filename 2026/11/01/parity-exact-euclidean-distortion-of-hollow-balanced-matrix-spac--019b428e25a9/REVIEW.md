# Scientific review of the exact Euclidean distortion theorem

## Correctness
**PASS.** The dimension and coordinate projection leverage follow from a directly inverted unsigned-incidence Gram matrix. The inner contact radius follows from Cauchy--Schwarz with equality. The outer contact radius is proved for even orders by a sharp odd-row quadratic bound and for odd orders by integral extreme-point analysis of the unsigned-incidence cube section. Alternating Eulerian tours supply equality in all three congruence classes. A permutation-averaging argument establishes optimality among all Euclidean norms, not only Frobenius-preserving coordinate maps. The exact-rational and linear-program checks are consistent with the complete proof.

## Originality
**PASS, qualified by remaining historical-search risk.** The closest earlier general Banach--Mazur cube comparison (Youssef, arXiv:1206.0654) does not give this explicit kernel's exact distortion. The complete 2016 source by Kurata and Bapat is about hollow/centered matrix correspondences and generalized inverses, not this normed intersection. Existing complete-bipartite zero-margin cube sections and cographic oriented-incidence spaces differ in their constraint matrices and dimensions. The particular residue-class formula and all contact configurations were not located in the searched records. A specialist unindexed paper on fractional degree-constrained polytopes could contain components of the extremal-combinatorics lemma; that would not automatically supply the Euclidean Banach--Mazur conclusion, but it remains a priority risk.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The problem is the exact Euclidean distortion of a canonical \(\binom n2-n\)-dimensional cube section arising in symmetric, degree-balanced matrices. The result classifies all dimensions, identifies precise contact configurations, and reveals a genuine residue-modulo-four split: the maximum allowed \(\sqrt{d_n}\) occurs on one infinite residue class, while other classes have qualitatively different deficits. This is a structural invariant rather than an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Limitations
Real, unweighted, complete-graph unsigned incidence only. No arbitrary-graph, complex, weighted or nonzero-diagonal generalization is claimed. Independent scientific audit has not been carried out.
