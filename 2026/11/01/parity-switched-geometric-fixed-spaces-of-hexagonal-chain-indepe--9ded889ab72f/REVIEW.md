# Scientific review of the geometric fixed-space theorem

**Correctness — PASS.** The orbit-barycenter lemma reconstructs the geometric, rather than merely simplicial-vertexwise, fixed points of a graph independence complex. Exact action-orbit inspection gives two-row half-chain quotient graphs. Even half-chains simplicially fold to a cone. For the odd combined-reflection case, the apex link is precisely such an even half-chain, allowing relative collapse. The sole non-elementary input is the published ordinary hex-chain homotopy theorem of Chandrakar and Singh, arXiv:2512.21318v1, Theorem 3.1. The corner case of one hexagon is handled separately and correctly gives three discrete points for one reflection. Independent finite graph reconstruction and mod-two boundary-rank checks passed for all stated subgroup types over the recorded ranges. No assertion is based on finite enumeration as an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, subject to indexing limits.** The closest mathematical source proves only the ordinary wedge-of-two-spheres homotopy type. The closest published symmetry result computes the full integral homology representation of the Klein-four action (and previously notes the row-swap simplex); it does not, by that claim, determine the geometric fixed-point homotopy types. Their characters imply fixed Euler characteristics but not contractibility versus higher fixed homotopy. The present theorem provides exact PL orbit-graph models, an all-width parity-switched classification of the two other involutions, and the subgroup fixed-space diagram. Direct published-result and primary-literature searches disclosed no covering statement. The published symmetry result was available as an indexed summary and recoverable claim, not as full text; this and unindexed equivalent work remain genuine originality risks.

**Value — PASS.** These are natural full symmetry fixed-point invariants of a hexagonal-chain family with known ordinary sphere-wedge topology. A complete classification for every subgroup reveals which reflection retains two geometric homology spheres of half the ambient dimension, which fixed space collapses, and which full-group fixed spaces are PL balls. This goes beyond Betti numbers and rational or integral character data: equivariant Lefschetz traces do not recover the fixed-point fundamental or higher homotopy type. A uniform quotient reduction and explicit collapse mechanisms make the distinction structural, not a list of small computations. The row-swap simplex and the ordinary nonequivariant homotopy theorem are explicitly credited as known inputs.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Chandrakar–Singh (2025), arXiv:2512.21318v1, Section 2.2 and Theorem 3.1; *Reflection characters of linear hexagonal-chain independence complexes* (published 2026 result, comparison at claim level); related circular-ladder symmetry results concern a different graph family. Full inspection of the published character-result document was not available. This is a self-review only; no independent audit or general equivariant stable-homotopy classification is claimed.
