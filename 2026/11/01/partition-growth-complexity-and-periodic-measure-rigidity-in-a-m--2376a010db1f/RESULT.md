# Partition-growth complexity and periodic-measure rigidity in a monotone-gap subshift

## Finding

Define a two-sided binary shift by requiring consecutive distances between markers to be nondecreasing. Its word complexity grows at the exact stretched-exponential partition rate, yet every invariant probability measure is a convex mixture of periodic-orbit measures. Remarkably, the coefficients of its periodic-point zeta function are exactly the first differences of its word complexity.

More precisely, let \(X\subset\{0,1\}^{\mathbb Z}\) consist of all configurations for which, whenever \(i<j<k\) are three consecutive coordinates occupied by \(1\), the distances satisfy \(j-i\le k-j\). Let \(\sigma:X\to X\) denote the left shift, \(c_n\) the number of admissible words of length \(n\), and \(p(s)\) the number of unordered positive-integer partitions of \(s\), with \(p(0)=1\). Then for every integer \(n\ge1\),
\[
c_n=1+\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}(n-s)p(s),\qquad c_0=1.
\]
Writing \(P(z)=\sum_{s\ge0}p(s)z^s=\prod_{m\ge1}(1-z^m)^{-1}\), the exact generating series is
\[
C(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}c_nz^n=\frac{1-z+zP(z)}{(1-z)^2}.
\]
As \(n\to\infty\),
\[
c_n\sim\frac{\sqrt3}{2\pi^2}\exp\left(\pi\sqrt{\frac{2n}{3}}\right).
\]
Consequently \(h_{\mathrm{top}}(\sigma)=0\), while the word-growth entropy dimension (defined by \(\lim_{n\to\infty}\log\log c_n/\log n\)) is \(1/2\) and the exact \(\sqrt n\)-normalization is \(\pi\sqrt{2/3}\).

Let \(F_n=\#\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^n)\). For every integer \(n\ge1\),
\[
F_n=1+\sum_{q\mid n}q,\qquad
\zeta_\sigma(z):=\exp\left(\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{F_n}{n}z^n\right)
=\frac{P(z)}{1-z}.
\]
In particular, the following coefficient-level duality holds for every \(n\ge0\):
\[
\boxed{\ c_{n+1}-c_n=[z^n]\zeta_\sigma(z)=\sum_{s=0}^n p(s).\ }
\]
Every recurrent configuration is periodic. Each \(\sigma\)-invariant Borel probability measure is uniquely of the form
\[
\mu=\alpha_0\delta_{\mathbf0}+\sum_{q\ge1}\alpha_q\mu_q,
\qquad \alpha_q\ge0,\quad\sum_{q\ge0}\alpha_q=1,
\]
where \(\mathbf0\) is the all-zero sequence and \(\mu_q\) is the uniform measure on the \(q\) shifts of the periodic configuration \(\ldots100\cdots0100\cdots\) with exactly one \(1\) in every block of \(q\) sites. Thus all ergodic invariant measures are periodic; positive intermediate word complexity is generated by nonrecurrent configurations.

## Assumptions and scope

All sequences are indexed by \(\mathbb Z\), with the product topology; \(\sigma(x)_i=x_{i+1}\). A triple of \(1\)'s is consecutive if no further \(1\) occurs strictly between successive members. The defining inequalities are imposed only on such triples, and all configurations with fewer than three \(1\)'s satisfy them. Equivalently, \(X\) avoids all finite blocks \(10^{a-1}10^{b-1}1\) with integers \(a>b\ge1\). This family of forbidden blocks defines a compact, shift-invariant subspace. The period of the all-zero point is one. All generating-series identities are identities of formal power series and also hold analytically for \(|z|<1\).

## Proof

**Exact finite-language census.** Every finite word satisfying the monotone-gap requirement extends to a member of \(X\) by placing zeroes at all other coordinates: no new pair or triple of \(1\)'s is created. A length-\(n\) word has either zero \(1\)'s (one possibility), exactly one \(1\) (\(n\) possibilities), or at least two. In the latter case let \(s\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\) be the distance from the first to the last \(1\). There are exactly \(n-s\) placements of its first \(1\). The positive consecutive gap lengths must sum to \(s\) in weakly increasing order, hence correspond bijectively to an integer partition of \(s\). There are exactly \(p(s)\) choices, including the one-part partition. Thus \(c_n=1+n+\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}(n-s)p(s)\). Since \(p(0)=1\), this is the displayed formula. Summing geometric derivatives gives \(C(z)=1/(1-z)+zP(z)/(1-z)^2\), which is the claimed expression.

**Sharp asymptotic.** Put \(a=\pi\sqrt{2/3}\). The classical Hardy--Ramanujan partition estimate is \(p(k)\sim e^{a\sqrt{k}}/(4\sqrt3\,k)\). Write \(c_n-1=\sum_{r=1}^n r p(n-r)\). For each fixed \(u\ge0\), if \(r=\lfloor u\sqrt n\rfloor\), the partition estimate yields \(p(n-r)/p(n)\to e^{-au/2}\). Uniformly over \(1\le r\le n/2\), it also implies an upper bound \(p(n-r)/p(n)\le K e^{-a r/(2\sqrt n)}\) for some fixed \(K\) and all large \(n\): the prefactor ratio is at most two up to a bounded error, and \(\sqrt n-\sqrt{n-r}\ge r/(2\sqrt n)\). For \(r>n/2\), monotonicity of \(p\) bounds the total contribution by \(n^2p(\lfloor n/2\rfloor)=o(np(n))\). Dominated Riemann-sum convergence therefore gives
\[
\frac{c_n}{np(n)}\longrightarrow\int_0^\infty u e^{-au/2}\,du
=\frac4{a^2}.
\]
Inserting the Hardy--Ramanujan prefactor yields \(c_n\sim\sqrt3\,e^{a\sqrt n}/(2\pi^2)\). The entropy and entropy-dimension assertions follow immediately. For a standard two-sided shift metric, Bowen covering counts at any sufficiently small fixed radius are sandwiched between language counts at length \(n\) and \(n+O(1)\), so this also determines the normalized orbit-segment rate.

**Periodic census.** Suppose a periodic configuration contains at least one \(1\). Its cyclically ordered successive positive gaps form a periodic sequence that is nondecreasing at every index. All gaps therefore coincide with a unique integer \(q\ge1\). The configuration is a translate of the period-\(q\) comb with exactly one \(1\) every \(q\) coordinates. Conversely each such comb is allowed, has primitive period exactly \(q\), and contributes \(q\) distinct shifts. Along with \(\mathbf0\), these exhaust all periodic configurations. Thus \(F_n=1+\sum_{q\mid n}q\). In the ring of formal power series,
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{F_n}{n}z^n=-\log(1-z)
+\sum_{q\ge1}\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{z^{qk}}{k}.
\]
Exponentiating gives \(\zeta_\sigma(z)=(1-z)^{-1}\prod_{q\ge1}(1-z^q)^{-1}\). Comparing with \(C(z)=\bigl(1+z\zeta_\sigma(z)\bigr)/(1-z)\) gives the boxed first-difference identity.

**Recurrent points and all invariant measures.** Suppose a configuration exhibits two strictly unequal successive gaps \(a<b\). The finite block \(10^{a-1}10^{b-1}1\) centered at that location can occur at most once: a second occurrence farther to the right would require a gap \(a<b\) after a gap of length \(b\), contradicting nondecreasing gaps. A configuration containing this block cannot be topologically recurrent, since a cylinder neighborhood recording that occurrence is never revisited at arbitrarily large positive times. If no such unequal pair exists, all available gaps are equal to a single \(q\) wherever consecutive gaps are defined. The configuration is then a full bi-infinite comb, a one-sided comb, a finite set of \(1\)'s (including one or two), or \(\mathbf0\). Except for the full comb and \(\mathbf0\), a finite cylinder describing an endpoint of the support occurs only once, so the point is again not recurrent. Hence the recurrent points are exactly the periodic points above.

For any invariant Borel probability measure, Poincaré recurrence applied to a countable basis of cylinder neighborhoods places full measure on the recurrent set. The recurrent set is the countable disjoint union of the zero point and finite comb orbits. The mass on each orbit is distributed uniformly by shift invariance. Summing these orbit masses produces the asserted unique probability decomposition. Conversely every such mixture is shift invariant. The extreme points are exactly the period-orbit measures, proving the ergodic-measure classification.

## Verification

The companion `verify.py` uses only the Python standard library. It exhaustively enumerates all \(2^n\) binary words for \(1\le n\le14\), all binary cyclic configurations for \(1\le n\le12\), and compares their counts with the exact partition and divisor-sum formulas. It checks the generating-series coefficient relation through the listed finite range and evaluates the leading asymptotic ratio at \(n=200,400,800\). All tests passed. This finite validation is not used in place of the general combinatorial, ergodic, or asymptotic proofs above.

## Relationship to prior work

Xie, Chen and Zhou introduce fine entropy scales and exhibit sparse-symbolic subshifts with intermediate growth. Their Section 6 construction uses subsets of the support of one sparse sequence and establishes growth orders; it does not impose monotonicity of *successive* marker gaps or give the exact partition/zeta/ergodic-measure identities above. Greenfeld, Moreira and Zelmanov establish broad realizability of subshift complexity functions up to asymptotic equivalence, not the specific exact counting and periodic-measure rigidity here. Kamarudin, Baloush and Che Dzul-Kifli's **increasing gap shift** is an \(S\)-gap shift in which each successive gap is drawn from a prescribed increasing set; it is not the present rule that each successive gap is at least its predecessor. That earlier system is shown to be topologically mixing, whereas the present system has only periodic recurrent points.

The partition generating function and the Hardy--Ramanujan partition asymptotic are classical and are not claimed as new. The assessed result is their exact simultaneous realization as word count, dynamical zeta, and invariant-measure classification for the single monotone-gap shift.

## Limitations

This statement is specific to a two-sided binary shift with globally nondecreasing **successive** distances between consecutive \(1\)'s. It does not describe unrestricted \(S\)-gap shifts or classify arbitrary intermediate-entropy systems. Historical novelty is evaluated against the primary texts and searchable literature identified below; unindexed prior work on monotone-block constrained languages remains a possible priority risk.
