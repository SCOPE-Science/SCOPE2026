# Scientific review: Möbius-ladder geodesic Fourier spectra

## Correctness

**PASS.** The central claim is fully quantified for every integer \(n\ge3\). The proof reconstructs the geodesic distance with zero-or-one opposite chords, enumerates all distance shells, and evaluates their cyclic Fourier transforms. The resulting trigonometric formulas yield the stated eigenvalues without omitted frequencies. Every nonconstant eigenvalue is nonzero, and the signs give the inertia. Odd determinant factors collapse through the classical odd-sine product; even determinant factors collapse through the roots of \(T_m(x)+1\) and the half-Pell–Lucas recurrence. The untwisted-prism comparison is proved independently with a cut-metric representation. The replayable checker corroborates 437 groups of assertions over many finite sizes, but the infinite case rests on the analytic argument. No externally certified proof is claimed.

## Originality

**PASS under bounded literature and database comparison.** The closest reviewed primary sources were Ilić (2011), on integral circulant distance spectra; Aalipour et al. (2015), on several distance-matrix determinant and inertia families; and Azimi–Farrokhi (2023), on resistance matrices and Laplacian pseudoinverses of Möbius ladders. Their inspected results do not entail the claimed ordinary shortest-path Fourier formulas and parity-sensitive determinant/inertia law. The 2011 integral-circulant theorem applies to the smallest complete-bipartite example but not the general Möbius family; the 2023 result concerns a different metric. Targeted published-finding searches returned the closest ladder result on weighted spanning-tree kernels and a Möbius-ladder independence-complex study, neither covering geodesic distance spectra. Search failure is not a proof of historical priority; an older treatment using a variant name or a generic circulant determinant method remains possible.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** This is a natural, fully exact, all-orders invariant of a canonical vertex-transitive graph family. It is not only a circulant diagonalization: the complete determinant exhibits a non-obvious parity split between powers of two and a Pell–Lucas square, and the inertia identifies a linearly growing family of directions obstructing universal Gaussian positivity. The comparison with the Cartesian circular ladder isolates the geometric effect of a Möbius twist rather than merely another finite numerical example. The proof offers an explicit spectral certificate at every order.

## Limitations and residual risk

The result is restricted to unit-edge shortest-path distance. Neither the precise subunit negative-type exponent nor bandwidth-dependent islands are determined. The closest earlier works do not eliminate every possible equivalent older formulation, and no independent external audit has occurred.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
