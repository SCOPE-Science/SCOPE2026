# Perfect-matching homotopy and cube symmetry of the cuboctahedral graph

## Finding
Let \(Q_3\) be the 3-dimensional cube graph and \(C=L(Q_3)\) its line graph, the cuboctahedral graph. Its **perfect-matching complex** \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm p}(C)\) has one vertex for each edge of \(C\), and a set of such vertices spans a simplex precisely when the corresponding edges extend to a perfect matching of \(C\). Then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal M_{\mathrm p}(C)\simeq\bigvee^{43}S^2.}
\]
In particular, its only nonzero reduced integral homology group is \(\widetilde H_2\cong\mathbb Z^{43}\).

There is also a complete rational homology-character determination under the natural cube symmetry group \(B_3=(\mathbb Z/2)^3\rtimes\mathfrak S_3\), of order 48. Write an element as a coordinate permutation \(p\) followed by binary translation \(t\), so it sends the cube vertex \(v\in\{0,1\}^3\) to \(p(v)\oplus t\). Classify its signed coordinate cycles as *positive* if the sum of the translation bits on the cycle is even, and *negative* if it is odd. With \((\lambda^+,\lambda^-)\) the partitions of the positive and negative cycle lengths, the character \(\chi_2\) of \(\widetilde H_2(\mathcal M_{\mathrm p}(C);\mathbb Q)\) is:

| Positive lengths | Negative lengths | Class size | \(\chi_2\) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| \((1,1,1)\) | none | 1 | 43 |
| \((1,2)\) | none | 6 | 1 |
| \((3)\) | none | 8 | 1 |
| \((1,1)\) | \((1)\) | 3 | -1 |
| \((2)\) | \((1)\) | 6 | 5 |
| \((1)\) | \((1,1)\) | 3 | -1 |
| \((1)\) | \((2)\) | 6 | 3 |
| none | \((1,1,1)\) | 1 | -5 |
| none | \((1,2)\) | 6 | -1 |
| none | \((3)\) | 8 | 1 |

Averaging gives \(\dim_{\mathbb Q}\widetilde H_2(\mathcal M_{\mathrm p}(C);\mathbb Q)^{B_3}=2\). The sphere-wedge equivalence is not asserted to be equivariant.

## Assumptions and scope
Construct the cube graph on \(\{0,1\}^3\) by joining bit strings of Hamming distance one. Enumerate its 12 edges in lexicographic order and use these as the 12 vertices of \(C\). Two vertices of \(C\) are adjacent when the corresponding cube edges share an endpoint; enumerating these pairs lexicographically produces 24 edges of \(C\). A perfect matching of \(C\) contains six disjoint edges covering every vertex. The perfect-matching complex is the subcomplex generated by all its six-edge perfect matchings, rather than the larger ordinary matching complex. All homotopy and homology assertions are integral unless a rational field is explicitly specified. The character is for this natural 48-element cube automorphism action.

## Proof
**Complete enumeration.** Take the least uncovered vertex of \(C\), pair it with every uncovered neighbor in turn, and recurse. Every perfect matching has exactly one forced choice at each least-uncovered step, so the recursion generates all perfect matchings exactly once. There are 32 facets of six edges each. Their complete downward closure has 1,429 faces including the empty face, with nonempty face vector by dimension \((24,192,520,468,192,32)\). The standalone checker also independently verifies that every enumerated face is a matching and that a residual perfect matching exists on its unmatched vertices.

**Integral homotopy.** Enumerate the 24 graph edges \(e_0,\ldots,e_{23}\). Begin with all faces, including the empty face. For edge indices \(i=0,\ldots,23\) successively, pair every remaining face \(\sigma\) not containing \(i\) to \(\sigma\cup\{i\}\) when both are currently unpaired. The 693 pairs are disjoint. Reverse those 693 directed cover relations in the Hasse diagram and direct all others from larger to smaller face. Explicit topological sorting checks all 1,429 face-poset vertices and 4,992 cover incidences and proves that the orientation is acyclic. Precisely 43 two-dimensional faces remain unmatched, no other nonempty faces remain unmatched, and the empty face is paired. Forman's discrete Morse theorem therefore provides a CW model with one zero-cell and precisely 43 two-cells, with no one-cells or higher cells. Every two-cell is attached to the single zero-cell, proving the exact homotopy equivalence with 43 two-spheres. In particular, the result is integral and does not depend on field computations.

**Full rational symmetry character.** Each of the 48 cube automorphisms permutes the twelve edges of the cube and hence the 24 edges of \(C\), preserving perfect matchings and the complex. If an oriented simplex with ascending edge indices is mapped to itself as a set, its contribution to the simplicial chain trace is the sign of the induced permutation of its vertices; other simplices contribute zero. Thus the signed fixed-face formula for the reduced Lefschetz number is
\[
\widetilde L(g)=-1+\sum_{\varnothing\ne\sigma\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm p}(C),\ g\sigma=\sigma}(-1)^{\dim\sigma}\operatorname{sgn}(g|_\sigma).
\]
All reduced rational homology is in dimension two, so \(\widetilde L(g)=\chi_2(g)\). The checker computes the signed fixed-face sum for every automorphism, groups equal values by the ten signed coordinate-cycle types, and verifies their counts and the displayed character table. The weighted character sum is 96, giving invariant dimension \(96/48=2\).

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer, Jelić Milutinović, and Vega established the perfect-matching-complex framework and computed homotopy types of specified hexagonal honeycomb graph families (arXiv:2209.02803, first public 2022-09-06). Their region graphs are not the cuboctahedral line graph of a cube. Later work on perfect-matching complexes of polygonal-line tilings and of Möbius/circular ladders likewise treats different families. The existing ordinary matching-complex calculation for the same cuboctahedral graph concerns all matchings and yields a different space; it does not determine the extendability subcomplex or its symmetry character. Targeted literature and published-record searches did not locate an earlier classification of this exact complex or its ten-class character; this is evidence within the checked record, not proof of global uniqueness.

## Limitations
The classification applies to this graph only; no theorem for arbitrary line graphs, cubelike graphs, or perfect-matching complexes follows. No equivariant sphere splitting or integral group-module decomposition is claimed; the symmetry result is a rational character calculation. An equivalent computation under an unindexed or alternative graph name remains a bibliographic risk. Independent audit has not been performed.
