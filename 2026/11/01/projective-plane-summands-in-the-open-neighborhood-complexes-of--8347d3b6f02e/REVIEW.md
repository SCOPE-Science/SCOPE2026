## Correctness — PASS
The claimed objects use open neighborhoods, with exactly sixteen distinct six-vertex facets. An explicit center permutation and vertex permutation identify the two facet families. An exhaustive program checks all sixteen-bit masks and all 2,736 nonempty face-poset incidences. The staged Morse pairing is checked acyclic, leaving one critical cell each in degrees zero and one and fifty-six in degree two. An exact oriented integral Morse boundary has greatest common divisor two; by Nielsen transformations of the two-cell attaching maps this is precisely the CW homotopy type of \(\mathbb{RP}^2\vee\bigvee^{55}S^2\). Direct independent mod-two and mod-three boundary-rank checks corroborate the integral computation. The result makes no assertion for other graphs.

The published all-size rook result [4] explicitly does not determine first/second homology or full homotopy type. The Čech and independence results [5,6] apply to different complexes; in particular their spheres do not contradict this open-neighborhood two-torsion. The exact integral degree-two attaching map and projective-plane wedge were not found in indexed comparisons. ## Value — PASS
The two nonisomorphic strongly regular graphs with parameters \((16,6,2,2)\) form the canonical test pair for graph-invariant discrimination. Their independence and closed-neighborhood complexes distinguish them, while the present open-neighborhood complexes are identical and, unexpectedly, contain first-integral-homology two-torsion. The result resolves the low-dimensional topology left open by the closest rook-graph computation and provides a natural example where a pure five-dimensional graph complex has the homotopy type of a projective plane with a large sphere wedge. These are precise structural and torsion facts, not arbitrary numeric tabulations.
