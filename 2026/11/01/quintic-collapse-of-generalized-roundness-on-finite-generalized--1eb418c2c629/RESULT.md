# Quintic collapse of generalized roundness on finite generalized-hexagon incidence graphs

## Finding

Let \(\mathcal H\) be **any existing finite generalized hexagon of order \((q,q)\)** with integer \(q\ge2\), and let \(G\) be its point–line incidence graph with the unit-edge vertex shortest-path metric \(d\). Put
\[
N=2(q+1)(q^4+q^2+1).
\]
Its supremal generalized-roundness (equivalently, supremal \(p\)-negative-type) exponent \(\wp_q\) is the **unique** root in \((0,1)\) of
\[
F_q(p)=-(q+1)+q(q+1)2^p-q^2(q+1)3^p
+q^3(q+1)4^p-q^4(q+1)5^p+q^5 6^p=0.
\]
For every \(0<p<\wp_q\), the metric has strict \(p\)-negative type; at \(p=\wp_q\) it has non-strict \(p\)-negative type and the complete equality-coefficient space is the one-dimensional span of the vector taking \(+1\) at every point and \(-1\) at every line. For every \(p>\wp_q\), it fails \(p\)-negative type.

The least Euclidean embedding dimension of the snowflake metric \(d^{p/2}\) is \(N-1\) for \(0<p<\wp_q\), and is exactly \(N-2\) for \(p=\wp_q\). As \(q\to\infty\) through orders for which a finite generalized hexagon of order \((q,q)\) exists,
\[
\wp_q=\frac{1}{q^5\log(6/5)}
+\frac{\log(5/4)}{q^6\log^2(6/5)}+O(q^{-7}).
\]
This is a quintic decay law, in contrast with the lower-degree rates of several other finite-incidence metric families. The result depends only on the generalized-hexagon axioms, not on Desarguesian or group-theoretic realizations.

## Assumptions and scope

A finite generalized hexagon of order \((q,q)\) is a point–line incidence geometry whose incidence graph is connected, bipartite, has diameter six and girth twelve, and every vertex has degree \(q+1\). The two color classes (points and lines) have equal cardinality. The theorem applies only to orders for which such a geometry exists; no existence assertion is made for arbitrary integers.

For \(p\ge0\), write \(D_p=(d(u,v)^p)_{u,v\in V(G)}\), with diagonal entries zero, and interpret the off-diagonal entries at \(p=0\) as one. The metric has \(p\)-negative type when \(a^{\mathsf T}D_pa\le0\) for every real vector \(a\) satisfying \(\sum_v a_v=0\). Strict negative type means strict inequality for every nonzero such \(a\). The real Euclidean embedding dimension counts the affine span dimension of a distance-preserving embedding of \(d^{p/2}\).

## Proof

**1. Incidence distances and the full spectral decomposition.** Fix a vertex. Unique geodesics of lengths below six and regularity \(q+1\) yield the intersection array
\[
\{b_0,\ldots,b_5;c_1,\ldots,c_6\}
=\{q+1,q,q,q,q,q;1,1,1,1,1,q+1\}.
\]
In particular the sizes of distance shells \(0,1,\ldots,6\) are
\[
1,\quad q+1,\quad q(q+1),\quad q^2(q+1),\quad
q^3(q+1),\quad q^4(q+1),\quad q^5.
\]
Let \(A_i\) be the distance-\(i\) adjacency matrix, and put \(A=A_1\). The distance-regular recurrence is
\[
AA_i=b_{i-1}A_{i-1}+c_{i+1}A_{i+1}\quad(1\le i\le5),
\qquad A_0=I,\quad A_1=A.
\]
Consequently \(A_i=P_i(A)\) with \(P_0(z)=1\), \(P_1(z)=z\), and
\[
P_{i+1}(z)=\frac{zP_i(z)-b_{i-1}P_{i-1}(z)}{c_{i+1}}.
\]
The seven distinct eigenvalues of \(A\) are
\[
q+1,\quad -(q+1),\quad\pm\sqrt{3q},\quad\pm\sqrt q,\quad0.
\]
This follows by factoring the characteristic polynomial of the shell quotient matrix:
\[
z(z^2-3q)(z^2-q)(z-q-1)(z+q+1).
\]
For each adjacency eigenvalue \(\theta\), the corresponding eigenvalue of the powered-distance matrix is exactly
\[
\lambda_\theta(p)=\sum_{i=1}^6i^pP_i(\theta).
\]
The \(q+1\) eigenspace consists of constants and is excluded from the zero-sum condition. Connectedness and bipartiteness make the \(-(q+1)\) eigenspace one-dimensional, generated by the point-minus-line vector \(\varepsilon\). The shell coefficients in that sector are \(P_i(-(q+1))=(-1)^i|\Gamma_i(v)|\), yielding \(\lambda_{-(q+1)}(p)=F_q(p)\).

**2. The parity sector strictly dominates all other zero-sum sectors for \(0<p\le1\).** Fix one of the five remaining eigenvalues \(\theta\). Define
\[
\delta_i=P_i(-(q+1))-P_i(\theta),\qquad
H_j=\sum_{i=j}^6\delta_i\quad(2\le j\le6),\qquad
\Delta_j=j^p-(j-1)^p.
\]
Since \(\sum_{i=1}^6\delta_i=0\), summation by parts gives
\[
F_q(p)-\lambda_\theta(p)=\sum_{j=2}^6H_j\Delta_j.
\]
The \(H_j\) are explicit. For \(\theta=\pm\sqrt{3q}\), their ordered list \((H_2,\ldots,H_6)\) is
\[
(q+1+\theta,\ 2q-q^2+\theta,\ q^3+2q+q\theta,\ q^2-q^4+q\theta,\ q^5+q^2).
\]
For \(\theta=\pm\sqrt q\), it is
\[
(q+1+\theta,\ -q^2+\theta,\ q^3-q\theta,\ -q^4-q^2-q\theta,\ q^5-q^2).
\]
For \(\theta=0\), it is
\[
(q+1,\ -q(q+1),\ q(q^2-1),\ -q^2(q^2-1),\ q^5+q^2).
\]
For \(0<p\le1\), the increments \(\Delta_j\) are positive and nonincreasing. More precisely,
\[
\Delta_3<\tfrac32\Delta_4,\qquad
\Delta_5<\tfrac54\Delta_6.
\]
To justify these inequalities, the ratio \(\Delta_j/\Delta_{j+1}\) decreases with \(p\) (write each increment as \(p\int_{j-1}^j x^{p-1}\,dx\) and differentiate the logarithm of the ratio). At \(p\downarrow0\), the respective ratios tend to \(\log(3/2)/\log(4/3)<3/2\) and \(\log(5/4)/\log(6/5)<5/4\); the last inequalities reduce to comparisons of integer powers.

For every \(q\ge3\) and every such \(\theta\), the explicit formulas above give
\[
H_2>0,\qquad H_4+\tfrac32H_3>0,\qquad H_6+\tfrac54H_5>0.
\]
Here are directly checkable bounds: when \(\theta^2=3q\), use \(|\theta|\le q\) to obtain
\[
H_4+\tfrac32H_3\ge q(q^2-\tfrac52q+\tfrac72)>0,
\quad H_6+\tfrac54H_5\ge q^4(q-\tfrac54)+q^2>0.
\]
When \(\theta^2=q\), the first expression is \((q-\tfrac32)(q^2-\theta)>0\), while the second is at least \(q^2(q^3-\tfrac54q^2-\tfrac72)>0\). When \(\theta=0\), the first expression equals \(q(q+1)(q-\tfrac52)>0\), and the second is \(q^2(q+1)(q^2-\tfrac94q+\tfrac94)>0\). Thus each of the three groups \(H_2\Delta_2\), \(H_3\Delta_3+H_4\Delta_4\), and \(H_5\Delta_5+H_6\Delta_6\) is nonnegative, with a strict positive contribution.

At \(q=2\), the same three inequalities hold for \(\theta=\pm\sqrt6\) and \(\theta=-\sqrt2\). For \(\theta=+\sqrt2\), the two group lower bounds add to
\[
(H_4+\tfrac32H_3)\Delta_4+(H_6+\tfrac54H_5)\Delta_6
\ge(5-3\sqrt2)\Delta_6>0;
\]
this uses \(\Delta_4\ge\Delta_6\). For \(\theta=0\), the five tails are \((3,-6,6,-12,36)\), so
\[
3\Delta_2-6\Delta_3+6\Delta_4
=3(\Delta_2-2\Delta_3+\Delta_4)+3\Delta_4>0,
\]
using convexity of consecutive increments of \(x^p\) for \(0<p\le1\); also \(36\Delta_6-12\Delta_5>0\). Therefore in **every** case
\[
\lambda_\theta(p)<F_q(p)\qquad(0<p\le1,\ \theta\ne\pm(q+1)).
\]

**3. The unique sharp exponent and its equality space.** At zero, \(F_q(0)=-1\). At one,
\[
F_q(1)=(q-1)(q^2-q+1)(q^2+q+1)>0.
\]
Hence \(F_q\) has a root in \((0,1)\). Choose its smallest positive root \(\rho\). At \(p=\rho\) the strict spectral domination just proved shows that the zero-sum quadratic form of \(D_\rho\) is negative semidefinite, with precisely the one-dimensional equality space \(\mathbb R\varepsilon\). Thus \(\rho\) is an exponent of non-strict negative type. The strictness theorem of Li and Weston says a metric of \(r\)-negative type has strict \(p\)-negative type for every \(p<r\). It follows that no exponent larger than \(\rho\) has negative type (otherwise strictness at \(\rho\) would be contradicted), and no second root in \((\rho,1)\) can occur (as that would again give non-strict negative type). Hence \(\rho=\wp_q\), it is the unique root in \((0,1)\), and all stated sharpness and strictness assertions follow.

**4. Critical Hilbert rank.** Let \(J=I-N^{-1}\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\). Schoenberg's centered Gram matrix for the distances \(d^{p/2}\) is \(-\tfrac12 JD_pJ\). The strictly negative zero-sum spectrum for \(p<\wp_q\) makes its rank \(N-1\). At the endpoint its sole additional zero mode is \(\varepsilon\), so its rank is exactly \(N-2\). These ranks equal the minimum Euclidean embedding dimensions.

**5. Quintic asymptotics.** Direct differentiation at zero gives
\[
F_q'(0)=q^5\log(6/5)+q^4\log(4/5)
+q^3\log(4/3)+q^2\log(2/3)+q\log2.
\]
Uniformly when \(p=O(q^{-5})\), Taylor's formula yields \(F_q(p)=-1+pF_q'(0)+O(q^5p^2)\). Substitution of \(p=Cq^{-5}\) with any fixed \(C>1/\log(6/5)\) proves \(F_q(p)>0\) for all sufficiently large \(q\), and hence \(\wp_q=O(q^{-5})\). Solving \(F_q(\wp_q)=0\) using the displayed derivative gives the two-term asymptotic and \(O(q^{-7})\) error.

## Relationship to prior work

Sánchez (arXiv:1108.0451, Sections 2–3) gives a general finite-distance-matrix characterization of supremal negative type and evaluates complete bipartite graph metrics; its general criterion does not itself evaluate the generalized-hexagon family or identify the dominant parity sector. Li–Weston (arXiv:0901.0695, Theorem 5.4) establishes strict negative type below an admissible exponent; the present proof invokes that theorem after deriving a new exact spectral threshold. Van Dam–Koolen–Tanaka (arXiv:1410.6294) surveys distance-regular graphs and their intersection arrays; it does not state the claimed negative-type threshold. DeVille (arXiv:1708.02294, Theorem 2.1) gives a general powered-distance spectral mechanism for distance-regular graphs; applying that mechanism to generalized-hexagon incidence graphs still requires the six-distance coefficient calculation and the all-order dominance proof displayed here. No claim of novelty is made for the general recurrence, generalized-roundness definitions, or Schoenberg's embedding criterion.

## Limitations

The graph must be an unweighted point–line incidence graph of an existing finite generalized hexagon of equal order \((q,q)\), with integer \(q\ge2\). This does not cover unequal-order hexagons, generalized octagons, arbitrary graph weights, continuum metrics, or all distance-regular graphs. The stated asymptotic is along existing orders only. Independently indexed literature may contain an equivalent calculation that was not found during the documented source comparison.
