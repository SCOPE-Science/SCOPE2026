# Reflection-fixed spheres in strong Morse complexes of paths

## Finding

Let \(P_n\) be the simple path with vertices \(0,1,\ldots,n\) and \(n\geq1\) edges, and let \(X_n=\mathcal{SM}(P_n)\) be its **full** strong discrete Morse matching complex (not the ordinary or pure Morse complex). The endpoint-reversal automorphism \(\rho(i)=n-i\) induces a simplicial involution on \(X_n\).

**Theorem.** For \(n\geq2\), set \(m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). Define a graph \(G_m\) with vertices \(0,\ldots,2m-2\), consecutive edges \(\{j,j+1\}\) for \(0\leq j<2m-2\), and additional edges \(\{2i+1,2i+4\}\) for \(0\leq i\leq m-3\). Then there is a canonical piecewise-linear homeomorphism

\[
|X_n|^{\langle\rho\rangle}\ \cong_{\mathrm{PL}}\ |\operatorname{Ind}(G_m)|.
\]

Consequently,

\[
|X_n|^{\langle\rho\rangle}\simeq
\begin{cases}
S^{k-1},&n=4k\text{ or }4k+1,\quad k\geq1,\\
\ast,&n\geq2\text{ and }n\notin\{4k,4k+1:k\geq1\}.
\end{cases}
\]

For \(n=1\) the geometric fixed-point set is empty. In particular, the fixed sphere dimensions are unbounded even though the original strong path complexes have nonequivariant homotopy spheres of approximately twice the dimension.

## Assumptions and scope

An incidence vertex of \(X_n\) is one choice of an endpoint of an edge of \(P_n\). Number the right-directed incidence on edge \(\{i,i+1\}\) as \(2i\), and the left-directed incidence as \(2i+1\), for \(0\leq i<n\). A face selects compatible incidences. These are precisely the subsets of strong matchings generated by all strict total orders of the path vertices. The involution sends \(2i\mapsto 2(n-1-i)+1\) and \(2i+1\mapsto 2(n-1-i)\). All spaces are finite polyhedra; \(\operatorname{Ind}(G)\) denotes the ordinary independence complex of the graph \(G\).

The stronger PL statement identifies the geometric fixed locus itself, rather than the invariant part of the ambient homology or the quotient space. Homology coefficients are not needed for the homotopy classification.

## Proof

**1. Exact face criterion from vertex orders.** Let \(c_i\in\{>,<\}\) record which endpoint of edge \(\{i,i+1\}\) is later in a fixed total order of path vertices: \(c_i=>\) if vertex \(i\) is later than \(i+1\), and \(c_i=<\) otherwise. Every prescribed binary comparison string is realizable by a strict total order, since inequalities between successive vertices of a path cannot form a cycle.

When a vertex is added in the order, the strong Morse construction pairs it with its incident edge precisely when it has **one** earlier neighbor; with zero or two earlier neighbors it is unpaired. Thus the incidence \(2i\) is in the resulting gradient exactly if \(c_i=>\) and additionally \(c_{i-1}=>\) whenever \(i>0\). The incidence \(2i+1\) is present exactly if \(c_i=<\) and \(c_{i+1}=<\) whenever \(i<n-1\). Therefore a set of incidences is a face of \(X_n\) if and only if all the comparison constraints imposed by its chosen incidences are consistent. As these are unary constraints on the variables \(c_i\), consistency is equivalent to pairwise consistency. The complete list of forbidden two-element sets consists of: both orientations of the same edge; a left incidence at position \(i\) with a right incidence at \(i+1\); and a left incidence at \(i\) with a right incidence at \(i+2\). The last two restrictions are imposed only when all mentioned indices lie in \(\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\). No higher-order obstruction exists.

**2. Geometry of reflection-fixed faces.** The involution on incidence vertices has only two-element orbits. In a simplex fixed setwise under this involution, a fixed barycentric point assigns equal weights to the two vertices in each orbit. Hence the geometric fixed locus is canonically the realization of the simplicial complex whose vertices are admissible two-element orbits and whose faces are collections of orbits whose union is a face of \(X_n\). The affine map sends each orbit vertex to the midpoint of its two incidence vertices. This map and its inverse, given by barycentric weights, are linear on each simplex; it is a PL homeomorphism onto the entire geometric fixed locus.

Put \(m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). For \(n\geq2\), the incidence orbits represented by the left \(m\) edge positions are labeled \(0,\ldots,2m-1\), with \(2i\) representing a right arrow and \(2i+1\) a left arrow at position \(i\). For odd \(n\), the middle edge's two opposite incidences form an inadmissible orbit, because one matching cannot use both orientations of that edge. At either parity, the orbit labeled \(2m-1\) is inadmissible: for even \(n\) it places a forbidden neighboring \(<>\) across the midpoint; for odd \(n\) it places a forbidden \(<->\) across the middle edge. Every other orbit is admissible. The local forbidden pairs within the left half are exactly consecutive labels \(j,j+1\) and the extra pairs \(2i+1,2i+4\). Pair conflicts on the right half are their reflections. Any pair meeting both halves has no further conflict beyond the excluded terminal left-arrow orbit and those just listed, as follows directly from the one-neighbor comparison constraints above. Thus the orbit-face complex is exactly \(\operatorname{Ind}(G_m)\). When \(n=1\), the only orbit contains both orientations of the sole edge, so the fixed locus is empty.

**3. The two-step suspension recurrence.** For \(m\geq3\), in \(G_m\) vertex \(0\) is a leaf with unique neighbor \(1\), and the closed neighborhood of vertex \(1\) is \(N[1]=\{0,1,2,4\}\). Consider the standard decomposition

\[
\operatorname{Ind}(G_m)=\operatorname{Ind}(G_m-1)\ \cup\ \bigl(1*\operatorname{Ind}(G_m-N[1])\bigr).
\]

The first subcomplex is a cone with apex the now-isolated vertex \(0\), the second is a cone with apex vertex \(1\), and their intersection is \(\operatorname{Ind}(G_m-N[1])\). As both inclusion maps of the intersection into these cones are nullhomotopic, the union has homotopy type \(\Sigma\operatorname{Ind}(G_m-N[1])\).

The graph \(G_m-N[1]\) has vertices \(3,5,6,7,\ldots,2m-2\). It is isomorphic to \(G_{m-2}\) through the explicit vertex bijection

\[
\phi(0)=3,\qquad
\phi(j)=\begin{cases}j+5,&j\geq1\text{ odd},\\j+3,&j\geq2\text{ even},\end{cases}
\quad 0\leq j\leq2m-6.
\]

Consecutive edges of \(G_{m-2}\) map alternately to the extra edges and ordinary edges of the remaining graph, while its extra edges map to the remaining ordinary edges; checking the displayed formulas exhausts both types. Consequently,

\[
\operatorname{Ind}(G_m)\simeq\Sigma\operatorname{Ind}(G_{m-2})\qquad(m\geq3).
\]

**4. Bases and induction.** The graph \(G_1\) has a single vertex, so its independence complex is a point. The graph \(G_2\) is the three-vertex path, whose independence complex is an edge together with an isolated vertex and hence has homotopy type \(S^0\). Iterating the suspension recurrence yields contractibility for odd \(m\) and \(S^{m/2-1}\) for even \(m\). The congruence classes in the theorem follow from \(m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\).

## Verification

The standalone Python 3 file `verify.py` uses two independent finite constructions: it exhausts vertex orderings of the original strong Morse algorithm for all paths with \(1\leq n\leq8\), and it independently enumerates comparator-consistent incidence words for \(2\leq n\leq12\). In both constructions, all reflection-invariant faces agree exactly with the independent sets of the asserted graph. The program also tests the explicit graph recurrence through \(m=30\), and reduced mod-two homology for \(1\leq m\leq7\). The executable prints `VERIFY_OK` if all checks pass. These finite checks corroborate the all-parameter proof; they are not substituted for it.

## Relationship to prior work

Knudson and Owens–White (arXiv:2609.06844v1, publicly posted September 6, 2026) define the full strong matching complex, analyze its incidence constraints, and classify its **nonequivariant** path homotopy types. A published result titled *Path reversal has a mod-eight orientation character on strong Morse matching complexes* (published-finding corpus, October 11, 2026) determines the one-dimensional rational reversal action on the ambient reduced homology. That result also counts reversal-invariant faces by admissible half-words, but does not classify their geometric fixed-point space or identify the orbit complex with an independence graph. Its character and quotient-homology formulas do not imply the fixed locus has a sphere rather than another complex with the same Euler characteristic. The present theorem is precisely that geometric classification, including an explicit PL model and an all-parameter homotopy recurrence. The ordinary Morse complex studied by Chari and Joswig is a different object.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns a single endpoint reversal of the **full strong** matching complex of a simple path. It does not classify arbitrary group actions, inclusion-induced persistence, equivariant homotopy splittings, or the ordinary Morse matching complex. The asserted PL homeomorphism is to an explicitly defined finite independence complex; only the subsequent equivalence to a sphere or a point is up to homotopy. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed. Any unindexed equivalent treatment remains a residual literature risk.
