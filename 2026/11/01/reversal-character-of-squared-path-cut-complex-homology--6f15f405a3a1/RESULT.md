# Reversal character of squared-path cut-complex homology

## Finding

For all integers \(k\ge 2\) and \(n\ge k+3\), put \(r=n-k\), \(t=\lfloor r/2\rfloor\), and \(m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). Let \(\rho(i)=n+1-i\) reverse the labels of the squared path \(P_n^2\). Write
\[
K_{n,k}=\Delta_k(P_n^2),\qquad
S(h,t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(h,t)}\binom hj.
\]
The unique possibly nonzero reduced rational homology group of \(K_{n,k}\) is in degree \(r-1\). Its reversal trace \(T(n,k)\) is
\[
\boxed{T(n,k)=\begin{cases}
(-1)^t\left[\binom{m-1}{t}-S(k/2-1,t)\right], &n\text{ even},\ r\text{ even},\\
(-1)^{t+1}\left[\binom{m-1}{t}-1\right], &n\text{ even},\ r\text{ odd},\\
(-1)^t\left[\binom{m}{t}-S((k-1)/2,t)\right], &n\text{ odd},\ r\text{ even},\\
(-1)^{t+1}\left[S(k/2-1,t)-1\right], &n\text{ odd},\ r\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}}
\]
(The arguments of \(S\) in these parity-compatible cases are nonnegative integers.) The top Betti number, determined previously by Zhang and Yang, is
\[
B(n,k)=\binom{n-1}{k-1}-\sum_{j=0}^{\min(k-1,r)}\binom{k-1}{j}(r-j+1)+r.
\]
Consequently the **complete rational representation** of the path-reversal group \(C_2\) on \(\widetilde H_{r-1}(K_{n,k};\mathbb Q)\) has trivial and sign multiplicities \((B(n,k)+T(n,k))/2\) and \((B(n,k)-T(n,k))/2\), respectively. For instance, \(n=10,k=5\) gives \(B=67\), \(T=-5\), hence multiplicities \(31\) and \(36\). The trace is not a bounded parity correction: along \(k=2h+1,n=2h+4\), \(h\ge1\), it equals \(h\).

## Assumptions and scope

The ordinary \(k\)-cut complex \(\Delta_k(G)\) is generated by complements \(V(G)\setminus C\) of disconnected induced \(k\)-vertex subsets \(C\). Here \(P_n^2\) has vertices \(1,\dots,n\), with an edge when the absolute label difference is \(1\) or \(2\). The involution \(\rho\) acts simplicially on the complex. The asserted representation is over \(\mathbb Q\), for all \(k\ge2\), \(r=n-k\ge3\), including the contractible \(k=2\) cases. Homotopy concentration and torsion-freeness follow from the previously proved shellability theorem; they are cited, not claimed anew.

## Proof

**1. Topological concentration and the missing-face classification.** Bayer–Denker–Jelić Milutinović–Sundaram–Xue prove that \(K_{n,k}\) is pure shellable of dimension \(r-1\), hence its reduced rational homology vanishes outside degree \(r-1\). Zhang–Yang prove that it has every subset of cardinality at most \(r-2\) as a face; the nonfaces of cardinality \(r-1\) are precisely the complements of intervals of \(k+1\) consecutive labels, and nonfaces of cardinality \(r\) precisely the complements of connected induced \(k\)-subsets. The latter are exactly the increasing sequences whose successive gaps are \(1\) or \(2\).

**2. The signed fixed-face trace.** For a \(\rho\)-invariant face \(F\), write \(F\) as \(a\) reflected vertex pairs and \(b\in\{0,1\}\) central fixed vertices. Then its cardinality is \(2a+b\), and the sign of the induced permutation on its oriented simplex is \((-1)^a\). Include the empty face in the reduced complex in degree \(-1\). The reduced Hopf trace identity, together with homology concentration, gives exactly
\[
T(n,k)=(-1)^r\sum_{\substack{F\in K_{n,k}\\\rho F=F}}(-1)^{a(F)+b(F)}.
\]
Thus the calculation requires **signed orbit-face counts**, not merely the number of setwise-fixed simplices.

**3. The unrestricted fixed-face sum.** Before removing the exceptional nonfaces, all invariant subsets of cardinality at most \(r\) contribute
\[
U_{n,r}=\sum_{a=0}^{m}\sum_{b=0}^{n-2m}
\mathbf1_{\{2a+b\le r\}}\binom ma(-1)^{a+b}.
\]
The alternating-binomial identity \(\sum_{a=0}^t(-1)^a\binom ma=(-1)^t\binom{m-1}{t}\) implies: when \(n\) is even, \(U_{n,r}=(-1)^t\binom{m-1}{t}\); when \(n\) is odd, \(U_{n,r}=(-1)^t\binom mt\) for even \(r\) and \(U_{n,r}=0\) for odd \(r\). Since \(r\le n-2\), the binomial boundary cases used here are valid.

**4. The interval correction.** A length-\(k+1\) interval is invariant under reversal if and only if its endpoints sum to \(n+1\). Exactly one such interval exists when \(r\) is odd, and none exists when \(r\) is even. Its complement has \(r-1=2t\) vertices forming \(t\) reversed pairs, so the sum in Step 2 must subtract \((-1)^t\) precisely when \(r\) is odd.

**5. The connected-set correction.** A connected \(k\)-set is determined by \(k-1\) gaps, each \(1\) or \(2\). Such a set is centered and reversal-invariant precisely when the gap word is palindromic, its span is at most \(n-1\), and its span has the parity required for centered integer endpoints. If \(k=2h+1\) is odd, the gaps occur in \(h\) mirrored pairs. Symmetry is possible only for odd \(n\), hence even \(r\), and its count is \(S(h,t)\). If \(k=2h\) is even, there are \(h-1\) paired gaps and one central gap, forced to be \(1\) when \(r\) is even and \(2\) when \(r\) is odd. The number of centered connected subsets is \(S(h-1,t)\). For even \(n\), odd \(k\) is impossible under reversal. A fixed complement of a connected \(k\)-set has orbit count \(t\) when \(r\) is even and \(t+1\) when \(n\) and \(r\) are both odd.

**6. Combining signs.** Subtract the interval and connected-set corrections of Steps 4–5 from \(U_{n,r}\), then multiply by \((-1)^r\) as in Step 2. Evaluating the four possible parities of \((n,r)\) gives exactly the displayed formula for \(T(n,k)\). The dimension formula for \(B(n,k)\) follows from Zhang–Yang. Since \(\rho^2=1\) and \(2\) is invertible in \(\mathbb Q\), its \(\pm1\)-eigenspaces have dimensions \((B\pm T)/2\), proving the full representation assertion. Setting \(k=2h+1\) and \(r=3\) in the even-\(n\), odd-\(r\) case yields \(T=h\), proving the unbounded-asymmetry example.

## Verification

The self-contained \(\texttt{verify.py}\) reconstructs cut-complex faces from the disconnected-complement definition, sums oriented fixed-face traces, independently enumerates reflection-symmetric connected sets, and computes the chain homology split into \(\pm1\) eigenspaces over \(\mathbb F_{101}\) for selected finite cases. This finite testing supplements, but does not replace, the all-parameter proof. It yields, among others, \((n,k,B,T)=(8,4,11,1)\) and \((10,5,67,-5)\).

## Relationship to prior work

Bayer and collaborators, *Topology of Cut Complexes II*, arXiv:2407.08158, Theorem 4.4, prove shellability for the squared-path family. Their §6.2 computes reversal representations for ordinary paths \(P_n\) (and dihedral representations for cycles), **not** for their square \(P_n^2\). Their ordinary-path face-lattice argument omits the additional lower-codimension missing faces that occur here. Zhang and Yang, *Betti Numbers of Cut Complexes of Squared Paths and a Recurrence Conjecture*, arXiv:2605.22808, Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 2.7, give the nonequivariant Betti number and missing-face profile. The present theorem adds the all-parameter symmetry trace and the complete rational \(C_2\)-module multiplicities. Analogs for other graph families do not determine these parameters.

## Limitations

No integral \(\mathbb Z[C_2]\)-lattice decomposition, ring structure, fixed-point homotopy type, or persistence map is asserted. The proof relies on two established topological/combinatorial results and establishes only the new equivariant refinement. Absence of an exact equivalent in the sources searched is not proof that none exists; independent external validation and exhaustive literature coverage have not been performed.
