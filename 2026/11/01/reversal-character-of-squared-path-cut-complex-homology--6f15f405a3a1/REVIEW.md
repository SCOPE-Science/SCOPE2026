# Scientific review: reversal character of squared-path cut-complex homology

## Correctness — PASS

The stated domain is \(k\ge2\), \(n-k=r\ge3\). A previously established pure shelling concentrates reduced homology in degree \(r-1\). The proof reconstructs the reversal signs of oriented fixed faces, includes the reduced empty-face term, and uses the exact two-level nonface classification. It explicitly counts invariant interval complements and palindromic connected-complement gap words, giving all four parity cases. The rational \(C_2\) splitting then follows from diagonalizability of an involution. The accompanying program directly checks 66 eligible parameter pairs for signed fixed-face traces and symmetric connected sets, and eight selected cases for actual eigenspace homology via modular chain reduction. The modular computations are finite corroboration, not a proof of the infinite statement.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The exact nonequivariant Betti formula and missing-face profile are due to Zhang–Yang, arXiv:2605.22808, and the shelling is due to Bayer et al., arXiv:2407.08158. The latter paper's equivariant §6.2 explicitly treats \(P_n\) and \(C_n\), not the squared path \(P_n^2\). The squared path has a second exceptional nonface cardinality, so the ordinary-path character formulas cannot be substituted to obtain the asserted closed trace. Published findings inspected for square-grid, cycle-power, and other cut-complex characters concern distinct graph families and do not imply the four-parity squared-path formula. An equivalent unpublished/unindexed computation may exist, and both source articles provide ingredients that simplify the present derivation; this residual derivation risk is acknowledged.

## Value — PASS

The involution action is a natural missing invariant after the 2026 all-parameter nonequivariant Betti calculation. The result determines the *entire* rational reversal representation, rather than only its rank, for an infinite two-parameter family and gives explicit inequivalent eigenspace dimensions. For example, \(n=10,k=5\) produces \(31\) fixed and \(36\) anti-fixed dimensions; along \(n=2h+4,k=2h+1\) the imbalance grows as \(h\). This is a concrete equivariant classification tied to the structure of squared paths and is not an arbitrary finite sample. It does not claim an integral lattice classification or equivariant homotopy equivalence.
