# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS
The exact assertion is for invertible \(2\times2\) matrices \(A_1,A_2\) over any finite field and invertible linear mates \(B\). The reduction by \(A_1^{-1}\) is bijective. Inclusion-exclusion uses all three singular translates; the pairwise overlap distinguishes ranks \(0,1,2\), and the triple intersection follows from two independent linear constraints on each rank-one image line. The resulting seven similarity classes and conjugacy-class multiplicities are exhaustive. Direct enumeration over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_4,\mathbb F_5\) confirms all formulas and class frequencies. The symbolic proof, not enumeration, establishes arbitrary prime powers.

## Originality: PASS, qualified
The closest work is Loukaki, arXiv:2610.21600v2: it defines simultaneous complete mappings, applies a matrix-singular-translate reduction for abelian groups, and proves existence; it does not present a rank-two exact mate-count classification. The present triple-overlap/eigenline formula and seven rational-canonical multiplicities are stronger enumerative statements not implied by the quoted existence bound alone. Exact-matrix and alternative-terminology searches, including published mathematical records, did not reveal a covering enumeration. The primary paper and its matrix and Latin-square sections were materially read. Similar counts could exist elsewhere under matrix-completion or finite-geometry terminology; priority is qualified.

## Mathematical value: PASS
Simultaneous complete mappings are a newly posed common-transversal and Latin-square problem. The first nontrivial rank-two linear case has a natural exact invariant: the number of automorphism mates for a prescribed pair. This classification resolves it uniformly over all finite fields, displays sharp seven-type arithmetic stratification, gives exact pair frequencies, and detects the characteristic-two obstruction while furnishing a sharp positive minimum for larger fields. It refines known existence assertions rather than varying an arbitrary parameter.

## Limits
Only two prescribed linear maps, their linear mates, and dimension two are covered. The formula does not enumerate arbitrary bijections or higher dimensions; possible equivalent older presentations remain an originality risk. Computational checks do not constitute an infinite-field proof, which is supplied separately.
