For the Gaussian-coefficient Duffing Melnikov function, the probability of exactly two simple phase zeros is asymptotic to \((3\pi/\sqrt2)\omega\exp(-\pi\omega/2)\) as the forcing frequency grows, uniformly over every finite number of forcing harmonics. The probability of four or more zeros is \(O(\exp(-\pi\omega))\); thus nearly every rare high-frequency first-order homoclinic crossing event has exactly two transverse Melnikov zeros.
