# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The one-disk eigenvalues follow from exact radial integrals, yielding the gap \(\delta=se^{-s}\). Factoring the two-set restriction through \(BB^*\) constructs two orthogonal leading restriction modes even though the ambient coherent states overlap. Their exact cross-matrix element has magnitude \(\lambda_0e^{-d^2/2}\). The off-diagonal integral kernel has Hilbert–Schmidt norm at most \(s\exp(-(d-2\sqrt{s})^2/2)\). The complementary spectrum is separated by \(\delta-2\eta_d\), making the positive Schur correction at most \(\eta_d^2/(\delta-2\eta_d)\). Min–max and inertia comparisons prove the two-sided error bound, after which the sharp asymptotics follow. The kernel estimate, multiplicity, disjointness and asymptotic scales have been checked. Sixteen numerical assertions corroborate the analysis but are not substituted for the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

Full-text relevant sections of Nicola–Tilli (arXiv:2106.03423), Marceca–Romero (arXiv:2104.06150) and Ramos (arXiv:2607.21590) were compared with the exact two-disk spectral splitting, not merely title strings. The first establishes disk optimality, the second general-domain spectral deviations and the third general structural localization and decoupling facts. None of those inspected claims gives a \(\lambda_0e^{-d^2/2}\) two-eigenvalue splitting with an exponentially smaller quantified remainder. Searches for coherent-state tunnel coupling, Gaussian STFT disjoint-disk spectra, and phase-space-separated localization did not identify a dominating equivalent. A specialized older result may still exist, and the conclusion is not a formal uniqueness certificate.

## Value — PASS

A disconnected pair of identical optimal concentration regions is the simplest natural multiwell localization geometry. In it, both eigenvalues tend to the same one-disk concentration, so a sharp rate and coefficient are needed to distinguish the leading eigenmodes. The exact coefficient identifies the geometric coherent-state overlap while the independent bound on all excited modes proves it controls the true infinite-dimensional spectral pair. This quantifies a mathematically motivated exponential spectral interaction rather than merely recomputing a radial eigenvalue.

## Limitations

Fixed equal positive radii, only the first two eigenvalues and large-distance behavior; no independent review or general theorem for many components. The computed tests are finite-dimensional.
