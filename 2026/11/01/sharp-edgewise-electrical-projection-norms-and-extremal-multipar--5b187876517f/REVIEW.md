# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** For a unit edge dipole, the complete-multipartite Laplacian decomposes into within-part contrasts and partwise constants, giving the stated explicit potential values. Every edge difference and its multiplicity is enumerated, with the two possibly delicate signs checked by degree bounds. The exact induced operator norm is the largest absolute row sum; signs of one maximizing row attain it. Strict discrete concavity of the part-size function reduces the global graph optimization to balanced pairs; explicit final increments isolate the exceptional vertex counts. The packaged rational checker independently obtains projection matrices by a reduced-Laplacian inverse for all partitions through nine vertices.

## Originality
**PASS at the level of the inspected claims, subject to residual priority risk.** Kelner–Maymounkov 2009 studies electrical routing and a general transfer projection upper bound; Christiano et al. 2010 studies general electrical flow computation. No statement inspected in either supplies the explicit multipartite absolute row sums or the extremal graph classification. The preexisting parallel-path edge projection result covers the \(K_{2,q}\) specialization only. Directed and differently weighted flow matrices are not equivalent without further hypotheses. Literature comparisons did not return a dominating result. Older circuit-theoretic literature remains a priority uncertainty.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The classification gives exact worst-case amplification of an intrinsically defined electrical orthoprojector on every unweighted complete multipartite network. The fixed-vertex extremum exhibits nontrivial small-size exceptions: a uniquely tripartite optimizer at five vertices, a bipartite/tripartite tie at seven, and unique balanced bipartite optimality thereafter. These facts quantify edgewise instability under heterogeneous input flows and provide exact finite-network benchmarks; they are not merely a bound on one arbitrarily chosen example.

## Scope and remaining risks
Positive conductances unequal to one and arbitrary graphs are outside the theorem. An equivalent circuit-theory transfer-current calculation may exist, and no independent priority determination is claimed.
