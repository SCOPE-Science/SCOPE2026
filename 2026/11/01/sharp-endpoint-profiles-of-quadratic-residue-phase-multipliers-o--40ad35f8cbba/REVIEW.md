# Sharp endpoint profiles of quadratic-residue phase multipliers on prime cycles

## Correctness
**PASS.** With the zero mode explicitly killed, inverse discrete Fourier transformation and the quadratic Gauss evaluation yield two exact kernel magnitudes when the prime is congruent to one modulo four, and a real pair of opposite-signed kernel values when it is congruent to three modulo four. Both statements are checked algebraically. The induced complex endpoint norm is the complete kernel absolute sum, attained by an explicit phase-matching input. Cauchy–Schwarz then proves the sharp universal bound and the complete congruence-one equality condition. In the other congruence class, direct maximization on the two branches \(c\ge\sqrt p\,s\) and \(c\le\sqrt p\,s\) gives the exact norm and all phase solutions, including the exceptional tie at \(p=3\). Every maximizing kernel coefficient is nonzero, yielding the claimed input-equality classification. The five-cycle formula follows from the exact Laplacian eigenvalues, and its minimum is verified by an elementary one-variable derivative comparison. The standalone finite direct-DFT checks corroborate but do not establish the all-prime theorem.

## Originality
**PASS relative to inspected literature and search evidence.** The closest contemporary sharp-imaginary-power work establishes asymptotic operator-norm growth on the infinite lattice, not finite prime-cycle endpoint values or Paley phase optimization. Earlier Björck-sequence constructions have a unit DC coordinate and study CAZAC/ambiguity behavior rather than the zero-DC two-phase convolution endpoint norm. The congruence-three maximizing relative angle is in fact a classical Björck angle, and priority for that *phase parameter alone* is not claimed. Power-residue-sequence literature establishes autocorrelation properties of fixed root-of-unity alphabets; the present work optimizes the induced complex \(\ell^\infty\) norm over all continuous phase choices and classifies all cases. Adjacent Paley spectral-concentration and fourth-moment results address different operator norms and objective functions. A prior equivalent calculation in the extensive Paley/difference-set literature remains possible; the absence of a matching indexed hit is not presented as a proof of uniqueness.

## Value
**PASS.** The two-band quadratic-residue decomposition is canonical for prime cyclic groups and has direct meaning for phase-only Fourier filters with the constant frequency suppressed. The exact endpoint norm is not determined by the multiplier's \(\ell^2\) isometry alone. The complete classification exhibits a structural, infinite arithmetic dichotomy: the universal mean-zero multiplier bound is attainable with a flat kernel precisely for primes congruent to one modulo four; the other prime class has a quantified positive sharp gap and different optimizer phase geometry. The exact five-cycle imaginary-power profile provides a concrete application of the same mechanism. The result is a complete natural-family classification, not an arbitrary numerical cutoff or a claim about broader classes of multipliers.

## Closest literature and limitations
Bui–Bui–Cao, arXiv:2610.27385 (2026), Sections 1 and 3, provides the motivating sharp discrete-multiplier context but treats the infinite lattice and \(1<p<\infty\) operator norms. Benedetto–Benedetto–Woodworth, arXiv:1107.1887 (2011), Sections 1–3, discusses classical Björck phase constructions, whose endpoint and zero-frequency conventions differ. Green–Green, DOI:10.1098/rspa.2002.1065 (2003), Sections 2–4 and 7, gives related power-residue sequences. Sahasranand, arXiv:2111.11389 (2021), treats a restrictive real constant-offdiagonal circulant matrix family. Full mathematical claims of the three closest indexed Paley records were compared: one concerns fourth moments, one multiplicative-character concentration spectra, and one cubic-residue projector norms, rather than this zero-DC continuous-phase norm. The theorem does not assert optimality among all spectral masks, real-input sharpness, or general finite-cycle imaginary-power formulas. Earlier obscure equivalent results remain an originality risk.
