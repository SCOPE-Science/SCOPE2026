# Scientific review: Sharp equal-sensitivity two-kernel approximation of a quadratic Bergman monomial

## Correctness — PASS

The object is the squared Hilbert-space projection of \(f(z)=z^2\) onto the span of two distinct analytic Bergman reproducing kernels with equal evaluation norm. The normalized area measure and kernel conventions are explicit. The two-by-two Gram inverse produces the rational objective \(F(t,c)\). Positivity of its denominator and its continuous compact-rectangle extension justify the existence and boundary analysis. Exact symbolic differentiation gives the resultant; polynomial-ideal reductions, Sturm counts and rational brackets leave precisely one admissible interior stationary point. Both angular boundaries have maxima strictly smaller than \(1/8\); an interior rational evaluation exceeds \(1/8\), so the sole stationary point is globally and uniquely optimal modulo the stated geometric symmetries. The stand-alone checker reproduces the exact rational identities and root counts. It provides finite, replayable certificates of the algebraic steps; the proof's global inference is analytical, not a consequence of approximate numerical optimization.

## Originality — PASS (subject to historical priority)

Qu, Qian, Li and Zhu, *Best kernel approximation in Bergman spaces* (2022, available online 2021-11-10, DOI 10.1016/j.amc.2021.126749), establish existence of best finite kernel approximations in standard weighted Bergman spaces. The publisher's online metadata and searchable article text were accessible, but the complete original article could not be read, so a hidden exact example remains a risk. Qian's related full-text preprint, *n-Best Kernel Approximation in Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces* (arXiv:2201.07228), Theorem 2.1 and Remark 5.2, provides general existence and defers finding optimal parameters. Neither accessible result implies the algebraic location of the unique equal-norm two-kernel optimum for the quadratic monomial. The exact optimization is also not one of the closely related published-finding corpus results inspected, including equal-radius Gaussian-Fock frame determinants or equispaced Bergman pole Hessians. The equivalence of this objective to a special two-node kernel dictionary is explicitly acknowledged, and no uniqueness beyond searched historical evidence is claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Equal evaluation norm defines a natural balanced measurement/dictionary design, not an arbitrary fixed-radius numerical slice. Optimizing both the angular separation and the common radius yields a surprising nonorthogonal, nonantipodal pair and an exact algebraic performance benchmark. The proof supplies a reusable elimination-and-global-boundary method and sharply distinguishes optimal distinct nodes from the confluent collision limit. The contribution is limited to this natural two-kernel target; it is not represented as a general multikernel theorem.

## Closest literature and residual limitations

The strongest existing direct framework is Qu–Qian–Li–Zhu (DOI 10.1016/j.amc.2021.126749), whose full article could not be independently examined; Qian (arXiv:2201.07228) is the closest accessible full-text theorem. Potential equivalent calculations from kernel quadrature, rational approximation, or unpublished notes remain possible. Other target monomials, weighted spaces, unequal kernel norms and larger dictionaries are beyond scope. All figures in the result are mathematical approximations to explicitly characterized algebraic numbers. Independent historical-priority review and independent mathematical audit remain outstanding.
