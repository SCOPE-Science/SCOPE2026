# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The published Mellin-diagonalization formula fixes the frequency normalization and converts the sharp analytic norm inequality into a one-dimensional tilted sine-density probability. The two disjoint supports of pairwise angular sums make the derivative-crossing ratio strictly monotone, proving a unique nondegenerate maximum for every real weight above \(-1\). Uniform saddle-point and endpoint Laplace estimates, together with the exact crossing equation, establish the order-one frequency shift and the exponential leakage constant. The standalone finite numerical diagnostic supplies 89 checks over symmetric and asymmetric intervals; finite testing is not a substitute for those arguments. Boundaries, quantifiers and nonattainment are stated in the result.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority risk

Grudsky–Karapetyants–Vasilevski (2004) already give the exact multiplier and the qualitative spectral convergence for piecewise continuous symbols (Theorem 5.5). Rozenblum–Vasilevski (2022) restate and structurally interpret the Mellin diagonalization, and Quiroga-Barranco–Sanchez-Nungaray (2024) give generalized quasi-hyperbolic formulas. The present claim is distinguished by the **unique optimizing frequency**, its explicit bounded correction, and the exact nonzero coefficient of the exponentially small sector leakage. These were not located in the relevant inspected passages. A monographic treatment of the same angular operators was not inspected in full; stronger-equivalent coverage remains possible and independent historical verification is needed.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Sharp concentration of analytic Bergman \(L^2\) mass in a scale-invariant angular window is a natural quantitative Carleson-type norm problem. Previously known qualitative spectral filling says only that the optimal fraction approaches one as weight increases. The new law determines **how fast** it does so, the leading prefactor, and the exact balancing of both exponentially small boundary tails. It is neither a parameter renaming nor a recomputation of a known spectral multiplier.

## Limits

The result concerns one connected fixed sector, one complex variable, and \(\lambda\to\infty\), with no endpoint-uniform or broader-symbol assertions. The supremum is not attained by any nonzero analytic function. Numerical checks only cover finite parameters. Independent historical-priority and mathematical audits have not been performed.
