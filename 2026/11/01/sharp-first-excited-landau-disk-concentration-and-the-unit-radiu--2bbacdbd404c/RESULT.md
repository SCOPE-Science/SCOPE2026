# Sharp first-excited Landau disk concentration and the unit-radius maximizer transition

## Finding
Consider concentration of the first excited Landau level (the true order-two Gaussian polyanalytic Fock space) on a centered disk. For every \(R>0\), writing \(t=R^2\), the exact operator norm is
\[
\|T_R\|=
\begin{cases}
1-e^{-t}(1+t^2),&0<t\le 1,\\
1-e^{-t}(1+t),&t\ge1.
\end{cases}
\]
For \(R<1\), the entire maximizing eigenspace is generated by \(1-|z|^2\); for \(R>1\), by \(\overline z\). At \(R=1\), precisely three basis directions maximize simultaneously: \(\overline z\), \(1-|z|^2\), and \(z(2-|z|^2)/\sqrt2\). Thus the optimizer changes sharply at one Gaussian length unit. As a function of \(t\), the sharp norm has left derivative \(0\) and right derivative \(e^{-1}\) at \(t=1\); as a function of \(R\), the one-sided derivatives are \(0\) and \(2e^{-1}\).

Furthermore, the complete neighboring-eigenvalue difference is explicitly factored:
\[
\lambda_{n+1}(t)-\lambda_n(t)
=-\frac{e^{-t}t^n}{(n+1)!}(t-n)(t-n-1)
\qquad(n\ge1).
\]
For each integer \(m\ge1\), this produces a three-fold *consecutive* eigenvalue coincidence, \(\lambda_{m-1}(m)=\lambda_m(m)=\lambda_{m+1}(m)\). Only the \(m=1\) coincidence occurs at the top of the spectrum.

## Assumptions and scope
Work in \(L^2(\mathbb C,\mathrm d\gamma)\), where \(\mathrm d\gamma(z)=\pi^{-1}e^{-|z|^2}\mathrm dA(z)\). Let \(\mathcal F_1\) be the closed holomorphic Fock space, \(\mathcal F_2\) the closed space of polyanalytic functions of order at most two, and \(\mathcal F_{(2)}=\mathcal F_2\ominus\mathcal F_1\). The concentration operator is the bounded positive self-adjoint compression
\[
T_R=P_{(2)}M_{\mathbf1_{\{|z|<R\}}}\big|_{\mathcal F_{(2)}},
\]
where \(P_{(2)}\) is the orthogonal projection. Equivalently \(\langle T_Rf,f\rangle=\int_{|z|<R}|f(z)|^2\mathrm d\gamma(z)\). The stated norm is the sharp energy fraction among nonzero functions in \(\mathcal F_{(2)}\). The fixed Gaussian normalization matters; no arbitrary physical-radius convention is implicit.

## Proof
**1. Exact basis and spectrum.** A complete orthonormal basis of \(\mathcal F_{(2)}\) is
\[
b_0(z)=\overline z,\qquad b_n(z)=\frac{z^{n-1}(n-|z|^2)}{\sqrt{n!}},\quad n\ge1.
\]
The signs of these complex Hermite functions are immaterial. The radial symbol implies that angular modes are orthogonal on every centered disk; hence \(T_R\) is diagonal. With \(t=R^2\), polar coordinates show
\[
\lambda_0(t)=\int_0^t xe^{-x}\,\mathrm dx=1-e^{-t}(1+t),
\qquad
\lambda_n(t)=\frac1{n!}\int_0^t x^{n-1}(n-x)^2e^{-x}\,\mathrm dx
\quad(n\ge1).
\]
Each eigenvalue lies strictly between zero and one. For fixed finite \(t\), the eigenvalues tend to zero as \(n\to\infty\), because the displayed integrands admit a bound by a polynomial in \(n\) times \(t^n/n!\). Thus the positive operator is compact and its norm equals the largest eigenvalue.

**2. Adjacent-eigenvalue identity.** Set \(G_n(t)=1-e^{-t}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}t^j/j!\) for \(n\ge1\). Integration by parts gives
\[
\lambda_n(t)=G_n(t)+\frac{e^{-t}t^n(n-1-t)}{n!}.
\]
Subtracting consecutive expressions, using \(G_{n+1}-G_n=-e^{-t}t^n/n!\), gives
\[
\lambda_{n+1}(t)-\lambda_n(t)
=-\frac{e^{-t}t^n(t-n)(t-n-1)}{(n+1)!}\quad(n\ge1).
\]
The initial difference is separate:
\[
\lambda_1(t)-\lambda_0(t)=e^{-t}t(1-t).
\]
These formulas supply the complete neighboring-mode sign diagram. In particular the two differences incident to mode \(m\) both vanish at \(t=m\), proving the consecutive triple coincidence.

**3. A strict gamma-median lemma.** For every integer \(k\ge1\),
\[
\lambda_k(k)=\int_0^k\frac{x^k e^{-x}}{k!}\,\mathrm dx<\frac12.
\]
Indeed, the equality follows from step 2 and \(G_{k+1}(k)=\int_0^k x^k e^{-x}/k!\,\mathrm dx\). For \(0<u<k\), the ratio of the density at \(k+u\) to the density at \(k-u\) is strictly greater than one, since
\[
\log\frac{(k+u)^k e^{-(k+u)}}{(k-u)^k e^{-(k-u)}}
=k\log\frac{k+u}{k-u}-2u>0,
\]
by \(\log\frac{1+v}{1-v}>2v\) for \(0<v<1\). Pairing the mass below \(k\) with the mass between \(k\) and \(2k\), and observing the strictly positive remaining upper tail, gives the strict half-mass inequality.

**4. Global maximizer for small disks.** If \(0<t<1\), both factors \(t-n\) and \(t-n-1\) are negative for every \(n\ge1\). Therefore
\(\lambda_1(t)>\lambda_2(t)>\lambda_3(t)>\cdots\). Moreover \(\lambda_1(t)>\lambda_0(t)\), by the initial difference. Hence \(\lambda_1(t)=1-e^{-t}(1+t^2)\) is the unique largest eigenvalue.

**5. Global maximizer for large disks.** For \(1<t<2\), the only increasing step among the positive-index eigenvalues is from \(\lambda_1\) to \(\lambda_2\); all later steps decrease. But
\[
\lambda_0(t)-\lambda_1(t)=e^{-t}t(t-1)>0,
\qquad
\lambda_0(t)-\lambda_2(t)=\tfrac12e^{-t}t^2(t-1)>0.
\]
Thus \(\lambda_0\) is strictly largest.

For \(t\ge2\), choose the unique integer \(m\ge2\) with \(m<t<m+1\), unless \(t\) is integral. The difference formula says that among \(n\ge1\), all steps are decreasing except possibly the step from \(\lambda_m\) to \(\lambda_{m+1}\). Consequently the largest positive-index eigenvalue is either \(\lambda_1(t)\) or \(\lambda_{m+1}(t)\). The first is below \(\lambda_0(t)\), and the second obeys
\[
\lambda_{m+1}(t)<\lambda_{m+1}(m+1)<\frac12,
\]
by monotonicity in \(t\) and step 3. On the other hand
\[
\lambda_0(t)\ge\lambda_0(2)=1-3e^{-2}>\frac12,
\]
since \(e^2>6\). At integral \(t=m\ge2\), all positive-index neighboring differences are nonpositive, so \(\lambda_1\) is their maximum and is strictly below \(\lambda_0\). This proves the large-disk formula and uniqueness.

At \(t=1\), the adjacent identities give \(\lambda_0=\lambda_1=\lambda_2\). Every following positive-index gap is strictly negative, so the top eigenspace is exactly their span. Finally, differentiating \(1-e^{-t}(1+t^2)\) gives \(e^{-t}(t-1)^2\) on the left; differentiating \(1-e^{-t}(1+t)\) gives \(te^{-t}\) on the right. Their values at one yield the stated sharp cusp; the chain rule gives the \(R\)-derivatives.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify_landau_disk.py` uses 60-digit `mpmath` arithmetic to compare the closed-form eigenvalues to direct integral quadrature, the difference formula to subtraction, integer triple crossings, the strict gamma-median estimates, and the claimed maximizing modes across a fixed list of radii and deterministic pseudorandom radii. The completed checker reported **1648 passed assertions**. These are finite corroborations only; the conclusions for all positive radii and all modes are proved analytically in the preceding steps.

For reproducibility, run `python verify_landau_disk.py` with a Python installation containing `mpmath`. No datasets, unpublished tables, or remote services are required for this verification.

## Relationship to prior work
Maximenko and Tellería-Romero, *Radial operators on polyanalytic Bargmann–Segal–Fock spaces* (2019), Proposition 6.3 and Section 6, already prove diagonalization and the general radial-symbol eigenvalue integral. Those formulas and the complex Hermite basis are **prior art**, not new results. The contribution here is the global exact maximum over the infinite eigenvalue sequence, its mode-switch/equality classification, explicit one-sided norm derivatives, and the precise adjacent-crossing identity. Abreu (2022), Section 5, gives the first-excited Landau/Gabor-Hermite interpretation and motivation; the paper addresses Gaussian spectrogram statistics, not this sharp disk concentration norm. Knutsen (2019/2020) obtains eigenvalue formulas and norm estimates for radially symmetric sets with the *Gaussian* Gabor window. Fulsche and Hagger (2023 preprint) treat quantum harmonic analysis on polyanalytic Fock spaces more generally. None of the inspected relevant sections states or dominates this explicit first-excited-level disk norm and maximizer transition.

## Limitations
Only the true order-two (first excited) space, isotropic normalized Gaussian measure, centered disks, and the corresponding positive compression are covered. The result makes no claim for the full two-polyanalytic direct-sum space, higher excited Landau levels, displaced disks without the appropriate magnetic translation, arbitrary domains, or noisy recovery. The general eigenvalue integrals were known. Priority beyond the inspected primary full texts and accessible search records remains uncertain; this analysis has not undergone an independent mathematical audit.
