# Scientific review: bridged complete-graph negative type

## Correctness — PASS
The zero-sum powered-distance quadratic form is expanded from the four exact distance classes and reduced to two within-clique fluctuation norms, one completed square, and a symmetric two-by-two Schur complement. The strict sign of \(2\,3^p-4^p-2\) on \((1,2)\) and the derivative inequality \(3^p\log3>2\,2^p\log2\) establish a unique first crossing. Negative-type propagation completes the range below exponent \(1\); the quadratic form also explicitly excludes all exponents above \(2\). The endpoint kernel, Euclidean rank, strict size monotonicity and asymptotic expansion follow from that same exact reduction. Exact rational quadratic-form checks and independent centered-Gram computations corroborate the proof, but do not replace it.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The closest primary full text is Aalipour et al. (arXiv:1509.01196), Section 5: it studies the same two-clique bridge graph family but only the unpowered distance matrix and its determinant and inertia. Sánchez (arXiv:1108.0451) supplies a general matrix method for any finite metric and treats complete bipartite graphs; the present evaluated sharp two-size threshold and equality geometry are not stated there. Li--Weston (arXiv:0901.0695) proves strictness propagation, not a graph-family classification. The closest located published research record on bridge barbells concerns zero forcing, a different invariant. Distinct titles alone were not used as evidence. Residual risk: an equivalent formula could exist in an unindexed or differently named treatment of clique sums, path metrics, or negative-type exponents; no universal uniqueness is claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Two cliques connected by a single edge are a standard graph family already studied spectrally. The exact negative-type threshold answers a different intrinsic Hilbert-embedding question for every two clique orders, with a sharp critical rank, a strict change under adding a vertex to either clique, and a quantitative large-clique law. These are exact natural invariants and structural consequences, not a re-labeling of the known unpowered distance determinant or a finite numerical census.

## Scope and limitations
The result requires unit shortest-path edges and exactly one bridge. It is not asserted for long connecting paths, arbitrary positive lengths, or full powered-distance inertia above the critical exponent. Numeric tests are finite and independent scientific validation is not performed.
