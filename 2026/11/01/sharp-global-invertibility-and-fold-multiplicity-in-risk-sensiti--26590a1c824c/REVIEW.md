# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The complete inverse-fiber theorem is reduced, without approximation, to \(H(x)=L\) by solving the affine belief coordinate and taking log odds of the multiplicative-weights coordinate. The global monotonicity and fold counts follow from \(H'(x)=1/(x(1-x))-a\beta\). The determinant identity independently identifies the singular set and its orientation. The cubic degeneracy and \(3/2\)-power opening law are exact Taylor calculations. The displayed probability-valid three-preimage example is checked symbolically using the three rational costs and log odds; the executable high-precision calculations are confirmatory rather than substitutes for a proof. All parameter endpoints \(k=1\), \(a\beta=4\), and \(k=k_c\) are addressed separately.

## Originality — PASS under inspected evidence

The source paper [1] defines this risk-feedback dynamical map, derives equilibrium/ergodic properties, and numerically explores complex attractors, but its inspected sections do not establish an all-parameter inverse-fiber theorem or physical triple-preimage witness. The related prior game-learning papers [2,3] concern different maps or scalar reductions, and their results do not imply the formula for this adaptive-belief shear. The closest retrieved published inverse-fiber classifications cover absolute-value piecewise-linear maps rather than this smooth risk-learning map; ordinary fold/cusp theory supplies concepts but not the model-specific exact threshold. Search results cannot exclude every historical equivalent, and the proof is not independent verification.

## Value — PASS

Invertibility is a structural dividing line in a two-dimensional map that was recently investigated through detailed (learning-intensity, belief-adjustment-rate) bifurcation diagrams and complicated basins. The result identifies precisely which parameter regions support one-step folding, proves the distinct one/two/three inverse branches globally, and gives an exact three-to-one example within the physical probability domain. It also explains why a numerically studied complicated-attractor regime can lie entirely inside the globally injective domain. The threshold and its \(3/2\) fold-opening exponent make this a useful analytic benchmark for distinguishing noninvertible mechanisms from other sources of complex learning dynamics.
