# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The generator derivative for a cut is derived from the heat equation, all cut contributions are grouped by pairs of multipartite classes, and the essential integer inequality is proved in cases including zero counts and the equality boundary. Coarea and the semigroup property extend the cut derivative sign to all real signals and times. A singleton in an oversized class gives strict amplification. The packaged standard-library exact checker passed 16,566 pair-inequality comparisons, 32,017 classified cut comparisons and 2,641 direct graph comparisons. Finite enumeration supports but does not replace the unrestricted proof.

**Originality — PASS, subject to residual literature uncertainty.** Inspected primary material includes Ollivier's transport/Markov curvature definition, Bauer et al.'s Li–Yau graph gradient theory, and available bibliographic and abstract material for graph total-variation denoising. These address different norms, hypotheses or nonlinear flows. Actual semantic mathematical-record searches and recoverable prior claims show related graph heat-variation results on trees and cycles but no covering multipartite all-time criterion. A hidden equivalent in unindexed older work is possible; the full text of the denoising reference was unavailable.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The result decides an all-time infinite family of stability inequalities by one exact integer part-size threshold. The boundary is sharp, and the explicit amplification witness distinguishes, for example, the contractive \(K_{2,4}\) from the expansive \(K_{2,5}\). This is a structural classification, not merely a restatement of general semigroup dissipativity.

**Scope and risk.** Only real-valued finite, unweighted complete multipartite graphs with the counting-measure Laplacian and unweighted edge variation are covered. The criterion is not claimed for weighted graphs or general graph families. A broad unindexed literature cannot be ruled out by searching.
