# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Exact beta-integral weighted-shift coefficients give a product formula and the adjacent-ratio inequality \(k(n+k+2)\ge(n+1)\tau^2\). For the \(k\)-step shift, the diagonal self-commutator is nonnegative precisely at and below \(\tau^2=k\). Above the threshold it is eventually strictly negative. Compact equivalence to a constant-modulus shift proves the essential norm formula. A disk-automorphism change of variables, dominated convergence, and Gauss summation prove the uniform boundary Berezin limit. The gamma product proves strictly subunit attenuation and its exponential decay at the hyponormal boundary. The supplementary checker passed 472 finite assertions; it is not treated as proof of the limits.

**Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, not certified).** Looi's 2026 full text (arXiv:2603.10879, Sections 3.1–3.3) already supplies gamma attenuation of oscillatory *radial* Bergman Toeplitz transforms; the current theorem does not claim that mechanism itself as new. Le–Simanek (arXiv:2003.07330) characterize shift-plus-diagonal hyponormality for other symbol families, while Kim–Lee (2022) analyze polynomial symbols. These sources do not state or imply the present sharp imaginary-phase-versus-angular-degree hyponormal threshold and vanishing boundary normalized Berezin signal *among hyponormal Toeplitz operators*. Direct statement/implication comparisons of nearby research results, parameter aliases, and reductions did not identify dominating coverage. Residual risk remains from unindexed quasihomogeneous-symbol results and unavailable theses.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Hyponormality is a structural positivity constraint on the self-commutator; showing that the boundary Berezin signal loses every fixed fraction of essential norm even under this constraint is a substantive boundary for kernel diagnostics. The exact \(\tau^2=k\) transition is intrinsic to a natural family of bounded nonradial oscillatory symbols and simultaneously characterizes norm-versus-essential-norm separation. This is stronger than computing one new beta integral or renaming the radial attenuation effect.

**Limitations.** The statement uses boundary Berezin modulus, not the global Berezin number. The standard unweighted one-variable Bergman space and nonradial phase-twisted monomials are the only class considered. Prior coverage cannot be disproved by database searches alone; independent external review remains unperformed.
