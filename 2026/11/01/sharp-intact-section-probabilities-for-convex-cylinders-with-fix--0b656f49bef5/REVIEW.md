# Scientific review: intact sections of convex cylinders

## Correctness — PASS
The asserted object is the exact probability of a whole untruncated infinite-cylinder section under Euclidean-invariant affine-plane measure conditioned on intersection. The normalization uses the **hit-offset interval length**, so it does not silently replace invariant plane measure with uniform normals. Every normal direction contributes a hit interval of length \(H\cos\theta+w_B(v)\sin\theta\) and an intact interval of length \([H\cos\theta-w_B(v)\sin\theta]_+\). Cauchy's perimeter identity fixes the denominator. The angular integral gives the stated convex kernel. Strict Jensen characterizes all constant-width minimizers, while the classical planar zonoid representation and a second Jensen inequality yield the sharp segment supremum. The full-dimensional strictness argument, continuous interpolation, variance-gap bound, and disk conditional-ellipse calculation were reconstructed directly from the stated definitions and formulas. The embedded checker confirms the curvature identity, endpoint expansions, independent angular quadrature on both symmetric and asymmetric polygonal bases, and the full stated disk CDF. It is not an infinite proof, and it is not used as one.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk
The closest inspected primary mathematical papers are van der Jagt–Jongbloed–Vittorietti, arXiv:2305.02864, which establishes the IUR section sampling model and studies section-area distributions; Dirksen, arXiv:1602.07854, which gives central cylinder section areas and maximal-area classifications; and Jongbloed–McGarrity–Sietsma, DOI:10.1007/s11749-020-00729-9, which treats vertical-orientation cylinder cuts for stereological estimation. None of their inspected main statements establishes this invariant-affine-plane, *untruncated-section* probability across all convex bases at fixed perimeter, with the complete constant-width equality family and strict segment supremum. Existing geometric inequalities for zonoid support functions, Cauchy perimeter, and Jensen are acknowledged as decisive classical **proof ingredients**, not separate new theorems. Search results and accessible related records did not reveal direct prior coverage. A yet-unlocated general convex-transform inequality or specialized stereological source might imply the resulting extremal profile; historical priority is therefore provisional rather than certified. The probability statement is not inferred from the absence of matching titles.

## Mathematical value — PASS
IUR sections are an established stereological observable. Determining when a section avoids the top and bottom caps is a natural identifiable geometric question for finite cylindrical specimens, not an arbitrary numeric slice. The theorem gives a complete all-convex-base, fixed-perimeter range, including a counterintuitive *nonunique* minimizing shape family (all constant-width bases) and an unattained degenerate maximizer. The quantitative gap controls width fluctuations, and for circular bases the section-event calculation yields the entire conditional eccentricity distribution and its two limiting laws. These results characterize sampling bias in an explicit geometric family rather than merely reproducing a generic Jensen calculation.

## Scope, closest comparisons and limitations
The class is exactly Cartesian products of arbitrary full-dimensional planar convex bodies and finite positive intervals. The probability uses the invariant joint plane-offset measure, rather than uniform directions plus independently normalized offsets. Results on *central* section size, on *projected* random shadows, on *vertical fixed-orientation* cut profiles, and on random **width variance** do not dominate the target statement. The sharp upper endpoint is only a supremum of nondegenerate bases. The width-rigidity coefficient is not shown best possible. No inverse tomography, nonproduct body classification, or global priority guarantee is asserted. The conditional eccentricity law is restricted to circular bases.
