# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The proof reduces every potentially better lattice frame to three half-diagonal vectors using the two-vertex diameter bound on **every subset sum**. A complete two-case sign analysis excludes unit vectors, and pairwise Hamming distance two forces the parity-class frame. An exact centroid second-moment identity proves the minimum enclosing radius of every surviving tetrahedron is \(\sqrt5/4\). A six-ordering center certificate attains the second threshold. The exact-rational verifier independently checks 232 short-vector frames, but those finite checks are explicitly not substituted for the infinite argument.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk

Lian–Xue (arXiv:2606.14584, Sections 1–2) supplies the foundational six-Kuhn-simplex lattice-covering criterion. Choudhary–Kachanovich–Wintraecken (2020, Section 1.2) and Labelle (2007, Chapter 3) supply known BCC/Sommerville tetrahedral geometry. The specific sharp second-bound and classification **over all lattice triples** are not consequences of the statements inspected: an optimal Coxeter tetrahedron identifies one minimizer but does not by itself exclude every competing frame below \(1/\sqrt2\). Exact novelty relative to every historical lattice-meshing source remains provisional; the study does not assert certified priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The six-prefix-simplex radius is the natural computable ball-certificate quantity associated with the recent Kuhn-covering construction. The theorem determines its complete isolated minimum layer, classifies all 48 ordered minimizers even among arbitrary independent sublattice frames, and obtains the first attainable certificate deterioration, a sharp \(\sqrt{8/5}\) multiplicative gap. Such a gap gives a precise rigidity/stability benchmark for discrete frame searches and lattice-meshing certificates; it is not a restatement of the classical BCC covering radius.

## Scope and risks

Only one fixed Euclidean lattice and one specific common-frame certificate are treated. The result does not classify all second-layer frames, other lattices, arbitrary triangulations, or perturbations of the lattice metric. Older work on BCC lattice bases and Delaunay meshing may employ equivalent language not captured by the inspected sources. The proof requires only elementary lattice coordinates and exact finite-dimensional ball geometry; it does not need the independent correctness of any prior numerical result.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
