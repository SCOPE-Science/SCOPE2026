The longest ternary \(9/10\)-synchronization strings have length exactly \(32\): a displayed length-\(32\) word passes every adjacent-interval test, while every length-\(33\) word fails one. An exact canonical-state exhaustion, reproduced with two independent longest-common-subsequence algorithms, proves this sharp finite obstruction and implies \(\varepsilon>9/10\) is necessary for unbounded ternary synchronization strings.
