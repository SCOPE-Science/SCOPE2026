# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
For any rank-one operator, the calculation \(T^2=(v^*u)T\) makes the \(2\)-partial-isometry axiom equivalent to \(\|u\|^2(2-|v^*u|^2)=1\). The derived inverse \(S=vw^*\) satisfies \(w^*u=1\), so the first Penrose equations hold. In a single positive Hilbert metric, the last two are exactly two metric-selfadjoint idempotent equations. The forced eigenvalues on orthogonal \(v\) and \(b\) yield necessity and the sharp condition-number lower bound; an explicit bounded metric attains it. The exceptional cases have \(S=T^\dagger\) already. The two-dimensional endpoint contradiction is exact. A finite checker supplements but does not replace this proof.

## Originality — PASS, best-knowledge
Aouichaoui–Buchała–Garcia's Lemma 2.8 supplies the generalized inverse and their Question 6 explicitly asks whether a change of inner product makes it Moore–Penrose. Their reported example addresses only the original metric. The present answer imposes one common equivalent Hilbert metric, gives a sharp iff condition for all rank-one operators and determines the optimal metric condition number. Searches for simultaneous orthogonalization of the two idempotents, equivalent-metric Moore–Penrose inversion, and rank-one \(2\)-partial isometries found no implication-equivalent treatment. General projection theory is background, not independently novel; a broad existing simultaneous-symmetrization theorem could imply the rank-one calculation and remains a publication-priority risk.

## Value — PASS
The work resolves the motivating question negatively via a sharp natural boundary, not merely a cheap failure of the ordinary adjoint condition. The dimension-free positive cases and exact optimal renorming distortion quantify the cost of repairing the generalized inverse; the divergent distortion at \(\|T\|^2=2\) and exceptional no-cost cases provide a meaningful classification useful for stability and operator geometry.
