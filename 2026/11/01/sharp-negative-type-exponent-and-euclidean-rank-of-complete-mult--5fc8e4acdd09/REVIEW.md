# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed all-parameter threshold is proved by an exact orthogonal decomposition of the distance quadratic form into within-part deviations and a diagonal form on the zero-sum vector of part totals. In the repeated-largest regime this diagonal form develops exactly the displayed \(k-1\)-dimensional kernel; in the unique-largest regime a rank-one Schur complement, a strictly increasing reciprocal-sum function, and a fixed witness above threshold prove the exact strict/non-strict/failure trichotomy. Schoenberg's rank criterion gives the critical Euclidean embedding dimension. The independent diagnostic program passed 1,730 finite assertions, including exact-rational identities; finite tests do not constitute the general proof. A direct specialization agrees with the known complete-bipartite formula.

## Originality — PASS, with priority limits

Gomi (arXiv:2610.18132v1) studies possible excess inertias up to nine metric points and does not provide an all-order complete multipartite negative-type exponent theorem. Sánchez (arXiv:1108.0451v1), Theorem 3.1, determines the bipartite subfamily and provides a universal finite-space determinant test. The proposed claim does **not** reassert either contribution: it determines the entire arbitrary-part profile, distinguishes repeated and unique largest parts, supplies explicit null vectors and their multiplicities, and establishes exact strictness throughout the exponent range. General determinant criteria and Deza–Maehara's 1990 graph transforms are powerful broader methods, but no directly covering explicit multipartite singularity classification was found in inspected accessible primary material. Material risk remains because Deza–Maehara (1990) and Deza–Grishukhin (2000) could not be inspected in full; the latter's abstract explicitly discusses negative-type thresholds for two-distance graph metrics. Priority therefore remains provisional, not independently certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Generalized roundness and snowflake Euclidean embeddability are natural metric invariants. The known \(K_{m,n}\) answer does not determine the unrestricted multipartite case by a scalar reparameterization. The exact sharp threshold for every part-size profile, a genuine dichotomy between simple and higher-dimensional boundary kernels, and the resulting minimal Euclidean embedding dimensions resolve a coherent natural family. The formula is not an arbitrary one-off numerical table or finite enumeration: it applies to all part numbers, all sizes, and simultaneous largest-part ties.
