# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The energy matrix is the five-diagonal second-difference Gram matrix. Removing an internal coefficient leaves two positive-definite fourth-difference blocks joined by one matrix entry, with the edge coupling becoming negative after a sign flip. A Cauchy--Binet cofactor proof establishes strict positivity of every Green entry, the quartic/cubic responses give exact bulk and edge data, and an explicit block inverse yields the proposed constant. Positivity also forces one relative phase reversal and uniqueness up to complex scalar. The standalone exact-rational checker reproduces finite instances; the infinite family rests on the analytical proof.

## Originality
PASS under presently inspected evidence. The closest 2026 source treats the full, unconstrained coefficient interval and proves a different exact constant and Landau extremizers. Its general left-inverse sign lemma is not a computed sharp constant for a missing internal coefficient. A 2022 discrete Sobolev Green-function paper treats pointwise maxima and second-order boundary problems, not this Fourier \(\ell^1\)-norm under a second-difference energy. Searches of indexed results and related finite-dimensional extremal problems did not find the one-hole formula or compulsory phase-reversal classification. An implicit prior derivation remains a residual risk.

## Value
PASS. A missing coefficient is a canonical sparse-spectrum restriction of the new sharp Fourier inequality.
