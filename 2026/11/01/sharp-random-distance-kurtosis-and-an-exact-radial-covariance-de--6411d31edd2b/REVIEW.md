# Scientific review: sharp random-distance kurtosis and radial–covariance rigidity

## Correctness — PASS

For each integer \(d\ge2\), independence, centering, and the trace identity give the exact second and fourth interpoint distance moments. Bounded convex bodies are star-shaped about their interior centroid; their volume polar coordinates give the exact radial second/fourth moments. Cauchy--Schwarz in radial coordinates and the covariance Frobenius identity split the normalized fourth-moment excess **exactly** into two squares/variances, with coefficient \((d+2)^2/(2d(d+4))\). Equality entails constant radial function, hence a ball. Cubes give sharp radial coefficient because covariance is scalar but radial function is not constant. Exact rational box/ball tests and independent quadrature pass; they supplement, not replace, the proof. The non-star-shaped annulus calculation verifies why the structural hypothesis matters.

## Originality — PASS, historical priority provisional

Bonnet--Gusakova--Thäle--Zaporozhets (arXiv:2010.03351v1, first posted 2020-10-07) obtain sharp bounds for the **first** mean Euclidean distance divided by mean width, not a fourth/second distance moment ratio. Bäsel (arXiv:2101.03815v1, 2021-01-11) computes the relevant moments for regular polygons and quotes Voss's general planar fourth-moment expansion. The algebraic pairwise expansion is accordingly acknowledged as existing knowledge, while the all-convex-body, all-dimensional sharp ratio, ball rigidity and exact variance-plus-anisotropy decomposition were not found in the inspected sections. A full published record on parallelogram boundary/interior **fourth-to-fourth** amplification compares different sampling laws, while the full difference-body-gauge Beta stochastic-order record involves another metric and simplex equality. The older Voss (1982) full primary text remained inaccessible beyond an abstract, and a historical equivalent remains plausible; the comparison does not imply independent certification or uniqueness by failed search.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The sharp ratio gives a dimension-dependent, scale-free benchmark for a natural random geometric observable using just two raw moments on arbitrary full-dimensional convex bodies. The nonnegative decomposition cleanly separates anisotropy of the covariance from variation in the radial body shape and yields an explicit quantitative rigidity statement; the best possible radial coefficient is certified by nonround isotropic cubes. The annulus shows an exact structural failure beyond the geometric hypotheses. This is a complete natural geometric inequality, not just isolated numerical parameter values. It offers no statistical sample-size theorem, Hausdorff stability modulus, or general inversion of shapes.
