# Sharp Schatten repair tails for essentially scalar moduli

## Finding
Let \(H\) be an infinite-dimensional separable complex Hilbert space, \(c>0\), and \(T\in\mathcal B(H)\) an invertible operator such that
\[
|T|=cI+E,\qquad E=E^*\in\mathcal K(H).
\]
Write the positive and negative parts of \(E\) as \(E_+\) and \(E_-\), and enumerate the positive eigenvalues of \(E_-\), with multiplicities and appended zeros if necessary, as \(d_1\ge d_2\ge\cdots\ge0\). For an integer \(m\ge0\), call a compact perturbation \(K\) an **\(m\)-repair** if
\[
\dim E_{|T+K|}([0,c))H\le m.
\]
Then **every** \(m\)-repair, including nonnormal and noncommuting perturbations, satisfies the simultaneous, pointwise singular-value inequalities
\[
\boxed{\ s_j(K)\ge d_{m+j}\quad(j=1,2,\ldots).\ }
\]
There exists one \(m\)-repair \(K_m\) attaining equality for **every** \(j\) simultaneously. Consequently, for each \(0<p<\infty\), with \(\|K\|_{\mathcal S_p}=(\sum_{j\ge1}s_j(K)^p)^{1/p}\) (a quasi-norm when \(p<1\)),
\[
\boxed{\ \inf_{K\in\mathcal S_p\text{ an }m\text{-repair}}\|K\|_{\mathcal S_p}
=\left(\sum_{j>m}d_j^p\right)^{1/p}.\ }
\]
The infimum over an empty class is \(+\infty\); whenever the displayed sum is finite it is **attained** by \(K_m\).

Let \(\beta_0(H)\) denote operators whose modulus attains its norm upon restriction to every nonzero reducing subspace of that modulus. Within the above essentially-scalar-modulus class, the following are equivalent: (i) some \(\mathcal S_p\)-perturbation belongs to \(\beta_0(H)\); (ii) \(E_-\in\mathcal S_p\). When they hold,
\[
\inf\{\|K\|_{\mathcal S_p}:K\in\mathcal S_p,\ T+K\in\beta_0(H)\}=0.
\]
If \(E_-\) has infinite rank, **no finite-rank** perturbation of \(T\) belongs to \(\beta_0(H)\), even when arbitrarily small \(\mathcal S_p\)-repairs exist. These conclusions concern the stronger modulus-defined class \(\beta_0(H)\), not an asserted characterization of all operators in the larger class \(\beta(H)\).

## Assumptions and scope
The polar decomposition of the invertible \(T\) is \(T=U|T|\) with \(U\) unitary. Compactness of \(E\) implies that its nonzero positive and negative eigenvalues have finite multiplicity and can accumulate only at zero; \(|T|>0\) implies \(0\le E_-<cI\) in operator order. The sequence \((d_j)\) is understood to have zeros if \(E_-\) has finite rank. All singular values \(s_j(K)\) are those of compact \(K\), in nonincreasing order, counted with multiplicity. The critical level \(c\) is the unique essential spectral value of \(|T|\) and of every \(|T+K|\) for compact \(K\).

For the \(\mathcal S_p\) infimum, the domain is compact operators belonging to the indicated ideal, not arbitrary bounded perturbations. No uniqueness of an optimal correction is claimed. The smallest possible singular values are established one rank at a time and attained by a common correction.

## Proof
**1. The attainment criterion at a scalar essential modulus.** If \(K\) is compact and \(X=T+K\), then
\[
X^*X-c^2I=(T^*T-c^2I)+T^*K+K^*T+K^*K
\]
is compact and self-adjoint. As \(c>0\), functional calculus gives
\[
|X|-cI=(X^*X-c^2I)(|X|+cI)^{-1}\in\mathcal K(H).
\]
Hence \(|X|=cI+F\) for compact self-adjoint \(F\). If \(E_{|X|}([0,c))\) has finite rank, all eigenvalues below \(c\) have finite total multiplicity, while the eigenvalues above \(c\) form a descending list with the sole possible accumulation point \(c\). The compact spectral theorem gives an orthonormal eigenbasis, and every nonempty subset of these eigenvalues has a greatest element. Thus on any nonzero reducing subspace of \(|X|\), the top eigenvalue occurs and is norm attaining. Therefore \(X\in\beta_0(H)\). Conversely, if the below-\(c\) spectral projection has infinite rank, there is a sequence of distinct below-\(c\) eigenvalues increasing to \(c\). The reducing subspace generated by corresponding eigenvectors has restriction norm \(c\) without a norming vector, so \(X\notin\beta_0(H)\). Therefore
\[
X\in\beta_0(H)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\dim E_{|X|}([0,c))H<\infty
\]
for compact perturbations of \(T\). This qualitative equivalence is also a specialization of the published reverse-well-ordered point-spectrum criterion of Su--Zhang.

**2. Sharp singular-value obstruction.** Fix an \(m\)-repair \(K\), and set \(X=T+K\). Let \(P=E_{|X|}([0,c))\), so \(\dim(PH)\le m\). For any \(j\ge1\) with \(d_{m+j}>0\), let \(V_{m+j}\) be the span of eigenvectors corresponding to the \(m+j\) largest eigenvalues of \(E_-\). Since the positive and negative parts of \(E\) have orthogonal supports, for all \(x\in V_{m+j}\),
\[
\|Tx\|=\||T|x\|\le(c-d_{m+j})\|x\|.
\]
The subspace \(V_{m+j}\cap(PH)^\perp\) has dimension at least \(j\). On it the spectral theorem gives \(\|Xx\|\ge c\|x\|\). The reverse triangle inequality therefore yields
\[
\|Kx\|\ge\|Xx\|-\|Tx\|\ge d_{m+j}\|x\|.
\]
The Hilbert-space min--max characterization of compact singular values now gives \(s_j(K)\ge d_{m+j}\). If \(d_{m+j}=0\), the inequality is automatic. This proves every rank inequality without assuming \(K\) is normal, self-adjoint, or commutes with \(T\).

**3. Simultaneous extremizer.** For fixed \(m\), let \(Q_m\) be an orthogonal projection onto \(m\) eigenvectors corresponding to the largest eigenvalues of \(E_-\) (onto the entire support when its rank is less than \(m\)). It commutes with \(E_-\). Define
\[
K_m=U E_-(I-Q_m).
\]
Then \(K_m\) is compact, \(s_j(K_m)=d_{m+j}\) for all \(j\), and
\[
T+K_m=U(cI+E_+-E_-Q_m).
\]
The parenthesized operator is strictly positive since it is bounded below by \(|T|\). Consequently its modulus is exactly itself and its below-\(c\) spectral projection is \(Q_m\) restricted to the positive support of \(E_-\). This has rank at most \(m\). Thus \(K_m\) is an \(m\)-repair attaining all lower bounds simultaneously. Summing their \(p\)-th powers proves the exact Schatten formula, including the case of an infinite sum and quasi-norms \(0<p<1\).

**4. Ideal threshold and finite-rank barrier.** By Step 1, a Schatten correction to \(\beta_0(H)\) is an \(m\)-repair for some finite \(m\). Step 2 forces \((d_{m+j})_{j\ge1}\in\ell_p\), equivalently \(E_-\in\mathcal S_p\). Conversely, if \(E_-\in\mathcal S_p\), Step 3 gives repairs \(K_m\in\mathcal S_p\) with \(\|K_m\|_{\mathcal S_p}\to0\). If \(E_-\) has infinite rank and \(K\) were finite rank, Step 2 with \(m=\dim E_{|T+K|}([0,c))H\) and sufficiently large \(j\) would force \(0=s_j(K)\ge d_{m+j}>0\), a contradiction.

## Verification
A standalone numerical script checks singular-value tail equality for the commuting spectral-clipping correction and tests the lower bound against randomly mixed, noncommuting finite-dimensional admissible perturbations. It reports 3,000 finite assertions passed. Those checks illustrate the min--max mechanism; Steps 1--4 are the infinite-dimensional argument. The code requires Python and NumPy, executes without downloads, and uses a fixed random seed. Run `python checker.py`.

As a sharp threshold example on \(H=\ell_2\oplus\ell_2\), take \(c=1\), \(U=I\), \(E_+\) on the first summand with eigenvalues \(\frac14 n^{-1/4}\), and \(E_-\) on the second with eigenvalues \(\frac14 n^{-2}\). Then an arbitrarily small trace-class correction reaches \(\beta_0(H)\), while \(|T|-I\notin\mathcal S_1\): restoring reducing-subspace norm attainment is strictly weaker than making the entire modulus a trace-class perturbation of a scalar. More generally \(d_n=\epsilon n^{-\alpha}(\log(n+1))^{-\gamma}\), with \(\epsilon>0\) small, has a \(\mathcal S_p\)-repair exactly when \(\alpha p>1\), or \(\alpha p=1\) and \(\gamma p>1\).

## Relationship to prior work
Su and Zhang (arXiv:2609.37621, first public 2026-09-29) establish the reverse-well-ordered spectral criterion for the modulus-defined class \(\beta_0(H)\), as well as qualitative finite-rank and compact perturbation principles. They do not state the pointwise sharp singular-value lower bounds for arbitrary corrections, the unique optimal Schatten tail **value** for a prescribed spectral rank budget, or the exact Schatten-ideal repairability threshold. The qualitative target criterion in Step 1 is explicitly credited to that work.

Bala and Golla (arXiv:2607.15222) characterize when an entire essentially scalar modulus differs from a scalar partial isometry by a Schatten operator; that criterion controls both positive and negative spectral tails. The present correction theorem requires only the **negative** tail to be Schatten, and allows the positive tail to lie outside that ideal, as the example in Verification demonstrates. A related published result entitled *Fiberwise density of \(\beta_0(H)\) along fixed polar factors* establishes operator-norm density preserving the polar factor, but explicitly does **not** assert compact or Schatten perturbations; it does not yield these ideal-specific obstructions or sharp costs. Another published result, *Exact distance and nearest-point criterion for positive absolutely norm-attaining operators*, treats the operator norm, not Schatten costs or pointwise singular-value optimality.

## Limitations
The theorem assumes invertibility and a singleton essential spectral value for the modulus; it does not classify arbitrary operators or arbitrary essential spectra. The \(m\)-repair target counts singular values strictly below \(c\), not the number of singular values away from \(c\) in either direction. At each fixed \(m\), the optimal Schatten correction need not be unique. The general value of the novelty claim is a best-knowledge literature comparison; unpublished or unindexed implications may exist. No independent audit has been performed.
