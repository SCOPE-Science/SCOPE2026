Deleting a perfect matching from \(K_{n,n}\) raises its sharp Schoenberg exponent: the crown graph has endpoint \(\beta_3=\beta_4=1\) and, for \(n\ge5\), the unique solution of \((n-1)(2^{\beta_n}-1)=3^{\beta_n}\). The gain over the complete-bipartite metric is asymptotically \(\log3/((\log2)^2(n-1)^2)\), with precisely determined endpoint Euclidean embedding ranks.
