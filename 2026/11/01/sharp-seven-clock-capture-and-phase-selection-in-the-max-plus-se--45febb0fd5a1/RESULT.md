# Sharp seven-clock capture and phase selection in the max-plus Selkov oscillator

## Finding
Consider the original-time planar max-plus map \(F_B(X,Y)=(Y+\max(0,2X),B-\max(0,2X))\), where \(B>0\). Choose an initial point uniformly in arclength on the natural segment \(S_B=\{(B+2Br,B-2Br):0<r\le1\}\). Let \(\mathcal C_B\) be its attracting seven-cycle, let \(\tau\) be the first nonnegative integer time the orbit actually meets \(\mathcal C_B\), and disregard the null set of exceptional endpoint and repelling-cycle initial states. Then, for all integers \(k\ge0\),
\[
\Pr\{\tau=7k+6\}=\frac{14}{16^{k+1}},\qquad
\Pr\{\tau=7k+7\}=\frac{1}{16^{k+1}},
\]
with zero probability at other times. Equivalently, \(\mathbb E[z^\tau]=(14z^6+z^7)/(16-z^7)\) for \(|z|<16^{1/7}\), and \(\mathbb E\tau=98/15\).

Put \(c_j=F_B^j(B,B)\) for \(j\in\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z\), let \(\lambda_{B}\) be the normalized arclength law on \(S_B\), and define \(\eta_m=(14/15)\delta_{c_{m-6}}+(1/15)\delta_{c_m}\), with subscripts modulo seven. For every integer \(q\ge0\) and \(m\in\{0,1,\ldots,6\}\), the **total-variation distance** is exactly
\[
\left\|(F_B^{7q+m})_*\lambda_B-\eta_m\right\|_{\rm TV}=
\begin{cases}16^{-q},&0\le m\le5,\\2\cdot16^{-(q+1)},&m=6.\end{cases}
\]
Here \(\|\mu-\nu\|_{\rm TV}=\sup_E|\mu(E)-\nu(E)|\). Thus the physical-time ensemble does **not** converge to a single probability: it has seven distinct subsequential limits, each supported on two adjacent but unequally weighted phases of the same attracting cycle.

## Assumptions and scope
Use the dynamical system \(F_B\) on the full plane, but sample initial points from the canonical one-dimensional segment \(S_B\), not from area measure. The segment is the affine image of the heptagon section in Ohmori--Yamazaki's Poincare formulation, and is **not invariant** under one step. The orbit visits the attracting cycle in finite time for arclength-almost all starting points. Its measure-zero repelling-cycle point and finitely many partition-boundary preimages do not alter any probability. All times in this finding count actual applications of \(F_B\), not compressed return-map iterations. The vectors \(c_j\) are the seven distinct points of the attracting cycle, with \(c_0=(B,B)\).

## Proof
**Exact branch clocks.** Scaling the state by \(B\) reduces to \(B=1\); the normalized arclength coordinate \(r\) is uniform on \((0,1]\). The originating full-text calculation (arXiv:2204.00745v1, Section 5, equations (28)--(33)) partitions this section into \(R=(0,1/16]\), \(U=(1/16,1/8)\), and \(G=[1/8,1]\). In original discrete time, a starting point in \(G\) meets the attracting seven-cycle after precisely six steps; in \(R\) it meets it after precisely seven steps. Every point of \(U\) returns to the section after precisely seven steps, with new coordinate \(r'=16r-1\). These statements follow also from direct substitution in the seven affine branch transitions; the journal version gives the same three-segment itinerary in its equation (6). At almost every point all pre-capture segments have nonzero length and do not coincide with any cycle point.

**Renewal and exact first hit.** The survivor interval \(U\) has length \(1/16\) and is mapped bijectively onto \((0,1)\) by its seven-step return, with constant derivative \(16\). Requiring \(k\) successive passages through \(U\) therefore contributes the factor \(16^{-k}\) to normalized initial length. A subsequent exit into \(G\), of length \(14/16\), yields capture time \(7k+6\); an exit into \(R\), of length \(1/16\), yields \(7k+7\). This gives the two claimed masses. They sum to \(1\) because \((14+1)\sum_{k\ge0}16^{-k-1}=1\). Summing their power series proves the generating function. The geometric number \(K\) of full section returns has mean \(1/15\), and the final clock is \(6\) with probability \(14/15\) or \(7\) with probability \(1/15\), independently of \(K\). Thus \(\mathbb E\tau=7/15+6+1/15=98/15\).

**Exact phase law and total variation.** Both exit types first enter the same reference point \(c_0=(B,B)\), at time \(7k+6\) or \(7k+7\), respectively, as follows from the explicit segment itinerary; after capture the orbit continues cyclically through \(c_j=F_B^j(c_0)\). Hence, at time \(n=7q+m\), every captured \(G\)-trajectory is at \(c_{m-6}\), and every captured \(R\)-trajectory is at \(c_m\), independent of its number \(k\) of section returns. Their limiting masses are respectively \(14/15\) and \(1/15\). For \(0\le m\le5\), only exits \(k<q\) have occurred, giving total uncaptured mass \(16^{-q}\). For \(m=6\), all \(G\) exits through \(k=q\) and \(R\) exits through \(k=q-1\) have occurred, leaving uncaptured mass \(2\cdot16^{-(q+1)}\). At any fixed finite time, the remaining unabsorbed segment images are non-atomic away from finitely many boundary points; they are disjoint from the seven atomic limit points up to measure zero. Moreover each captured atomic weight is bounded above by its corresponding limiting weight. Thus total variation is exactly the residual uncaptured mass, yielding both formulas. The seven \(\eta_m\) are distinct, since their dominant atoms occupy different phases.

## Verification
The standard-library script `verify.py` checks the original max-plus dynamics using exact rational arithmetic, verifies both branch exit clocks and all seven-cycle points on representative strictly interior rational inputs, reconstructs the affine seven-step return, and independently counts the rational cylinder lengths for renewal depth up to six. These are finite consistency checks, not a replacement for the all-depth interval-similarity argument. Running `python3 verify.py` prints `CAPTURE_CLOCK_VERIFIED`.

## Relationship to prior work
Ohmori and Yamazaki introduced the map and proved the attracting and repelling cycles, the exact three-branch Poincare return map, and the six-/seven-step section itineraries. Those source equations are **premises**, not novel claims here. Their paper does not supply a probability law for the exact capture time or the nonstationary seven-phase distribution of a uniform section ensemble. The published *Exact geometric first-exit law for the ultradiscrete Sel'kov map* concerns **two-dimensional planar area conditioned on staying in the expanding branch**, and explicitly stops at first branch exit; it neither describes first entry into the attracting cycle nor the canonical section ensemble and physical-time phase selection. The present result instead quantifies ultimate cycle capture on the natural one-dimensional transversal, retains the original seven-step clock, and proves an exact total-variation limit. Its algebra is elementary but the derived invariant is the full phase-resolved, finite-time ensemble law, rather than the previously known setwise basin geometry.

## Limitations
The result concerns the specified normalized-arclength initial distribution, not arbitrary planar initial distributions, other ultradiscrete maps, or random perturbations. Zero-mass boundary itineraries can have atypical capture times. The phase limits are periodic, not invariant one-step probabilities; their average over seven phases is the uniform probability on the attracting cycle. The sharp finite-time laws are derived from previously published branch itineraries, so the computational component is short; originality is limited to the ensemble-statistical conclusion. No independent audit is claimed.
