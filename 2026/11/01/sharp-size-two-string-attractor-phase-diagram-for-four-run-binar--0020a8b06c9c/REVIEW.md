# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim has a complete unbounded proof using the factor trichotomy, the unique central \(10\) occurrence, and the forced one-zero/one-one structure of every two-position attractor. Explicit three-position witnesses cover all possibilities. Necessity and sufficiency are proved separately for the cases where the chosen one is the last symbol of the second run and where the chosen zero is the first symbol of the third run (the latter by reverse-complement symmetry). The union of four exact families yields the two obstruction regions, while inclusion-exclusion handles the only overlapping pair. All positive endpoint cases are included, including singleton runs. The independent literal-factor verifier matched all pair sets for \(1296\) parameter quadruples and supplied additional direct occurrence checks; this corroboration is not substituted for the proof. Scientific risk: an unnoticed logical gap in one of the boundary family implications, mitigated by the written if-and-only-if analysis and independent tests.

## Originality — PASS, with qualified residual risk

The 2019 paper by Mantaci et al. and its 2021 expanded open-access treatment study size-two attractors of Sturmian and other binary word families; the latter expressly identifies the general binary size-two classification as open. Neither inspected treatment states the two explicit four-run obstruction regions or the enumeration of all optimal pairs. The 2022 paper by Restivo et al. studies complexities of infinite-word prefixes, rather than a four-run finite-word exact phase diagram. The known binary two-run power result implies only the diagonal subfamily with matching first/third and second/fourth run lengths, and the special alternating example; no novelty is asserted for those subcases. Relevant semantic research-record searches did not return a dominating four-run theorem. This is a best-knowledge originality judgment, not a proof of uniqueness: an unindexed or more general equivalent statement remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The four-run family is the first alternating binary run-length family that can require a three-position attractor while still using only two symbols. The result precisely locates two disjoint sharp failure regions for the alphabet lower bound and classifies every two-position solution through four forced central-boundary families, rather than presenting isolated witnesses or a routine small numerical gain. This answers a natural tractable subfamily of the larger binary size-two classification question, with explicit exact multiplicities for arbitrary run lengths. It does not claim the classification of all optimal three-position sets.

## Relationship and limitations

Primary comparison sources: Kempa–Prezza, arXiv:1710.10964; Mantaci et al., arXiv:1907.04660 and the 2021 Theoretical Computer Science expansion DOI:10.1016/j.tcs.2020.11.006; Restivo–Romana–Sciortino, arXiv:2206.00376. A previously established periodic two-run-power classification yields the equal-parameter special case. No computational finite sweep proves the general theorem; all unbounded cases rely on the written proof. A broader, unlocated characterization could still subsume the result.
