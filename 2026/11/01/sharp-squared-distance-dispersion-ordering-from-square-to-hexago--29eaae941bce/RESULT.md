# Sharp squared-distance dispersion ordering from square to hexagonal flat tori

## Finding
For \(0\le c\le1/2\), let \(T_c=\mathbb R^2/\Lambda_c\) be the unit-area Euclidean flat torus generated by \((1,0)\) and \((c,\sqrt{1-c^2})\), with both generators scaled by \((1-c^2)^{-1/4}\). For two independent uniform points \(X,Y\), write \(W_c=d_{T_c}(X,Y)^2\), where distance is intrinsic. Then
\[
\operatorname{Var}(W_c)=\frac{3c^4+2c^3-7c^2+4c+2}{180(1-c)(1+c)^3}.
\]
The variance is *strictly decreasing* on \((0,1/2)\). Consequently the sharp inequality in this entire rhombic family is
\[
\boxed{\frac{43}{4860}\le \operatorname{Var}(W_c)\le\frac1{90}},
\]
with lower equality only for the equilateral/hexagonal lattice and upper equality only for the square lattice. Both endpoints are stationary and the inequalities are quadratically strict:
\[
\operatorname{Var}(W_0)-\operatorname{Var}(W_c)=\frac7{180}c^2+O(c^3),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(W_c)-\operatorname{Var}(W_{1/2})=\frac{92}{3645}(1/2-c)^2+O((1/2-c)^3).
\]
The two raw moments also have the exact expressions
\[
\mathbb E W_c=\frac{1+c-c^2}{6(1+c)\sqrt{1-c^2}},\qquad
\mathbb E W_c^2=\frac{8c^4-8c^3-12c^2+14c+7}{180(1+c)^2(1-c^2)}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The parameter \(c\) is the cosine of the acute angle of an *equal-length reduced basis*; this is the continuous family interpolating between the square and equilateral flat tori. Each torus has unit total area, and its distance is the quotient shortest-path distance. The result is not a global extremal statement among all lattices: unequal basis lengths are excluded.

## Proof
Temporarily use unscaled unit generators \(a,b\), satisfying \(a\cdot b=c\). The difference of two independent uniform quotient points is itself Haar uniform, and its closest lattice representative is uniform in the Dirichlet cell. Set \(u=x\cdot a\), \(v=x\cdot b\); the Gram matrix is \(G=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&c\\c&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), so \(\|x\|^2=(u^2+v^2-2cuv)/(1-c^2)\). For \(0<c\le1/2\), the three pairs of Voronoi-relevant vectors are \(\pm a,\pm b,\pm(b-a)\). Consequently the oblique-coordinate domain is exactly
\[
H_c=\{(u,v):|u|\le1/2,\ |v|\le1/2,\ |u-v|\le1-c\}.
\]
At \(c=0\) the third constraint is redundant and the same formulas continue by continuity. The domain has area \(1-c^2\): remove the two right triangles of legs \(c\) from the unit square. The jacobian from Euclidean coordinates to oblique coordinates is \(\sqrt{1-c^2}\), so uniform averaging in the Euclidean Voronoi cell is exactly uniform averaging over \(H_c\).

For each integer \(k=1,2\), put \(P_k=(u^2+v^2-2cuv)^k\). Integration over the square minus the two corners gives the finite exact formula
\[
\mathbb E[\|x\|^{2k}]=\frac{1}{(1-c^2)^{k+1}}\left[\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\!\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}P_k\,du\,dv-2\int_{-1/2}^{c-1/2}\!\int_{u+1-c}^{1/2}P_k\,dv\,du\right].
\]
Elementary polynomial integration yields
\[
\mathbb E\|x\|^2=\frac{1+c-c^2}{6(1+c)},\quad
\mathbb E\|x\|^4=\frac{8c^4-8c^3-12c^2+14c+7}{180(1+c)^2}.
\]
Subtracting the squared mean gives \(\operatorname{Var}(\|x\|^2)=(3c^4+2c^3-7c^2+4c+2)/(180(1+c)^2)\). Rescaling the lattice vectors to area one multiplies squared distances by \((1-c^2)^{-1/2}\) and their variance by \((1-c^2)^{-1}\), proving the first formula and the raw moments.

The decisive derivative factorization is
\[
\frac{d}{dc}\operatorname{Var}(W_c)=-\frac{c(2c-1)(c^2+2c-7)}{90(1-c)^2(1+c)^4}.
\]
For \(0<c<1/2\), \(c>0\), \(2c-1<0\), and \(c^2+2c-7<0\), so the derivative is strictly negative. Substitution at the endpoints gives \(1/90\) and \(43/4860\). Differentiating again at \(c=0\) and \(c=1/2\) gives \(-7/90\) and \(184/3645\), respectively; Taylor's theorem establishes the asserted sharp quadratic endpoint behavior. This proves all statements.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify.py` reproduces the two symbolic polynomial integrals using exact rational arithmetic at several parameter points, checks the derivative-factorization identity symbolically, and confirms the endpoint constants and quadratic coefficients. Rational checks corroborate but do not replace the universal analytic computation shown above.

## Relationship to prior work
The planar Dirichlet-cell normal form and exact radial distance CDF for general flat tori are known, including an existing 2026 result that the scalar distance histogram determines the flat two-torus. That stronger *forward distribution* can be integrated to recover the displayed raw moments; novelty is **not** claimed for the ability to integrate an already-known CDF. The claim here is the **sharp, strictly monotone area-normalized variance comparison over the continuous rhombic deformation, its exact endpoint extremizers, and quadratic endpoint stability**. Allen and Agrell (2021) develop Voronoi-cell moment integration in a different, nine-dimensional quantization setting, while the classical planar optimum concerns the mean squared quantization error rather than the variance of that error. An older higher-moment lattice-optimization theorem could imply the ordering; historical priority remains provisional.

## Limitations
Only equal-generator rhombic tori are considered; no global optimality among general two-dimensional lattices, higher dimensions, other powers of distance, or finite-sample inference is asserted. The general radial CDF was previously known; the new result concerns its nontrivial shape ordering rather than independent invention of the distribution. Independent validation has not been carried out.
