# Sharp surface-area extremizers among tree graphical zonotopes

## Finding

Let \(n\ge4\) be an integer and \(T\) any finite simple tree on \(\{1,\dots,n\}\). In the Euclidean subspace \(H=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\sum_i x_i=0\}\), form the graphical zonotope
\[
Z_T=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(T)}[0,e_i-e_j].
\]
For an edge \(e\), let \(s_e\) be the number of vertices in one of the two components of \(T-e\); the product \(s_e(n-s_e)\) does not depend on which component is chosen. Its intrinsic \((n-2)\)-dimensional Hausdorff surface area satisfies the exact cut formula
\[
\mathcal S(Z_T)=2\sum_{e\in E(T)}\sqrt{s_e(n-s_e)}.
\tag{1}
\]
Among all \(n\)-vertex trees, the following inequalities are sharp:
\[
2(n-1)\sqrt{n-1}\ \le\ \mathcal S(Z_T)\ \le\
2\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sqrt{k(n-k)}.
\tag{2}
\]
Equality on the left occurs exactly for the star \(K_{1,n-1}\), and equality on the right exactly for the path \(P_n\), up to graph isomorphism. All the compared zonotopes are combinatorial \((n-1)\)-cubes, and all have the same intrinsic Euclidean volume \(\sqrt n\) and the same first intrinsic volume \((n-1)\sqrt2\). Nevertheless their surface areas differ by an unbounded ratio:
\[
\frac{\mathcal S(Z_{P_n})}{\mathcal S(Z_{K_{1,n-1}})}
\sim \frac\pi8\sqrt n\qquad(n\to\infty).
\tag{3}
\]
For \(n=3\) the two extremal tree types coincide and equation (1) remains valid, with equal bounds.

## Assumptions and scope

All graph edges are unweighted, with the fixed standard-root generators \(e_i-e_j\) of Euclidean length \(\sqrt2\). The Euclidean metric is the restriction of the standard metric on \(\mathbb R^n\) to \(H\), not the graph metric or the coordinate volume measure of a nonorthonormal basis. The segment convention is translation-equivalent to the standard graphical zonotope \(\sum_{\{i,j\}}[e_i,e_j]\). Surface area means \((n-2)\)-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the polytope boundary, counted once per facet; it is twice the penultimate intrinsic volume.

## Proof

**The intrinsic-volume reduction.** Choose an arbitrary orientation on each edge and let \(B\) be the \(n\times(n-1)\) oriented incidence matrix of \(T\), with columns \(b_e=e_i-e_j\). A tree has rank \(n-1\), and its image is exactly \(H\); thus the Minkowski sum of its \(n-1\) segments is a full-dimensional parallelotope in \(H\). The intrinsic-volume formula for a zonotope generated by columns \(b_e\), valid in every Euclidean dimension, gives
\[
V_{n-2}(Z_T)=\sum_{e\in E(T)}
\sqrt{\det(B_{-e}^{\mathsf T}B_{-e})},
\tag{4}
\]
where \(B_{-e}\) omits column \(e\). This formula follows as well from pairing the two opposite facets parallel to the other \(n-2\) generators, and using that \(\mathcal S=2V_{n-2}\).

Removing edge \(e\) makes a forest with two components of orders \(s_e\) and \(n-s_e\). On each component, a column incidence matrix has Gram determinant equal to the number of its vertices, by Cauchy--Binet: each maximal square minor obtained by deleting one row is \(\pm1\), by repeated leaf elimination, and there is one such minor for each vertex. Up to a simultaneous row and column permutation, the two forest incidence Gram matrices form the two blocks of \(B_{-e}^{\mathsf T}B_{-e}\). Hence
\[
\det(B_{-e}^{\mathsf T}B_{-e})=s_e(n-s_e).
\tag{5}
\]
Combining equations (4) and (5) proves (1). Applying the same incidence-minor argument to the whole tree gives \(\det(B^{\mathsf T}B)=n\), so \(\operatorname{Vol}_{n-1}(Z_T)=\sqrt n\). The first intrinsic volume of a zonotope is the sum of its generator lengths, giving \(V_1(Z_T)=(n-1)\sqrt2\).

**The minimum.** For every integer \(1\le s\le n-1\), one has \(s(n-s)\ge n-1\), with equality exactly at \(s\in\{1,n-1\}\). Since \(T\) has \(n-1\) edges, (1) yields the left bound of (2). Equality means every edge is incident to a leaf; a connected tree on at least four vertices with this property is a star. The star realizes equality.

**Strict branch transfer and the maximum.** Suppose \(T\) is not a path. Choose a vertex \(v\) of degree at least three. Let \(A\), \(B\), and \(C\) denote components of \(T-v\) ordered among the three smallest by size \(a\le b\le c\); additional components, if any, only strengthen the inequalities below. Choose a leaf \(w\) in the component \(B\). Detach \(A\) from \(v\) by deleting its sole edge to \(v\), and reconnect the same root of \(A\) to \(w\). The result is again a tree on the same vertices.

Every edge-cut size is unchanged except on the nonempty path from \(v\) to \(w\) in the old component \(B\). For each edge on that path, its cut component away from \(v\) had \(x\) vertices with \(1\le x\le b\), and now has \(x+a\) vertices. Because
\[
n\ge1+a+b+c\ge1+a+2b>2x+a,
\]
we have the strict inequality
\[
(x+a)(n-x-a)-x(n-x)=a(n-2x-a)>0.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus every affected term \(\sqrt{s(n-s)}\) strictly increases, none decreases, and \(\mathcal S\) strictly increases. A maximal-surface-area tree can consequently have no vertex of degree at least three. Every connected tree with maximum degree at most two is a path. For the path, the cuts have sizes \(1,2,\dots,n-1\), yielding the right-hand expression in (2) and its unique equality case.

**Asymptotic contrast.** A Riemann sum gives
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sqrt{k(n-k)}
=n^2\int_0^1\sqrt{x(1-x)}\,dx+o(n^2)
=\frac{\pi}{8}n^2+o(n^2).
\]
Hence the path surface area is \(\pi n^2/4+o(n^2)\), while the star surface area is \(2n^{3/2}(1+o(1))\). This proves (3). Both inequalities have equality exactly as stated for \(n\ge4\).

## Relationship to prior work

Padrol--Pilaud--Poullot provide the canonical graphical-zonotope model and deformation/fan theory. Joós--Lángi give the general intrinsic-volume formula for zonotopes, which explicitly supplies the main geometric input (4). Their general isoperimetric theorem for parallelotopes with fixed volume is a comparison with the Euclidean cube, not an extremal classification inside the constrained class of tree-root parallelotopes. The 2026 paper of Atia evaluates intrinsic volumes of a complete-graph permutohedron by the same forest-Gram principle; it does not furnish the tree-dependent extremal classification (2).

A separate published result determines exact directional-width variance for tree graphical zonotopes and finds that the path minimizes that variance while the star maximizes it. Those variance values depend only on the degree sequence, whereas (1) depends on the global cut sizes. For example, on seven vertices, trees with branch lengths \((1,1,4)\) and \((1,2,3)\) emanating from a unique degree-three vertex have the same degree sequence (and hence equal directional-width variance), but their surface areas are respectively
\[
2(3\sqrt6+\sqrt{10}+2\sqrt{12})
\quad\text{and}\quad
2(3\sqrt6+2\sqrt{10}+\sqrt{12}),
\]
which are unequal. Wang and collaborators' 2019 *Split sizes and extremal tree shapes* proves broad extremal theorems for sums of increasing functions of **leaf-set** split sizes, over nontrivial internal-edge splits under degree-sequence conditions. Its leaf-count statistic is not the vertex-count cut product in (1), and it does not state either the exact root-zonotope surface formula or the fixed-vertex-count sharp path/star inequalities. The strict tree transfer in (6) provides a direct proof without importing a leaf-split extremality result. Neither the volume identity, the width-variance theorem, nor the general intrinsic-volume formula alone asserts the sharp star/path surface-area order; that order is supplied by the strict transfer.

## Limitations

No statement is made for edge-weighted graphical zonotopes, graphs with cycles, non-Euclidean metrics, or other intrinsic-volume indices. The formula for a tree is a specialization of a previously published general zonotope identity; only its explicit cut interpretation and the rigid sharp tree extremum and asymptotic contrast are assessed as the contribution. Historic priority is provisional: a differently indexed tree-split topological index or earlier graphic-zonotope calculation might contain equivalent extrema. No independent external mathematical audit is claimed.
