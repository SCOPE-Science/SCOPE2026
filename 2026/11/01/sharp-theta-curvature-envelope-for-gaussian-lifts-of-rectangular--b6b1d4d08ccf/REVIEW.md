The new proof rigorously establishes one zero of the derivative using Jacobi's transformation for \(t\le2\), a pairwise third-cumulant estimate on \([2,3]\), and the moment bound for \(t\ge3\). Product Hessians and equality conditions then follow by diagonal differentiation. The numerical root is only illustrative. The curvature available from a normalized Gaussian quotient is an important quantitative datum in the recent lattice-lift framework. Even on the canonical orthogonal lattice, the exact best width is not the generally used Gaussian width: the sharp curvature is about 3.55028 rather than merely the universal 2.31145 guarantee. This gives a precise analytic benchmark for how much Gaussian-width tuning alone can achieve; it is not a claim about all possible lattice lifts.
