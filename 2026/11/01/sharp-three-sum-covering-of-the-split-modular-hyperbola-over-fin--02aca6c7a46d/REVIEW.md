# Scientific review: sharp three-sum covering of the split modular hyperbola

## Correctness (PASS)
The universal statement is supported by a complete case split, not by extrapolation from finite enumeration. For targets with one zero coordinate, explicit nonzero triples are constructed by avoiding at most four parameters. For two nonzero coordinates, multiplicative scaling reduces to a single product parameter. The associated projective plane cubic has precisely three smooth rational points at infinity and three forbidden affine points. Differences of its gradient components prove smoothness for all nonzero product parameters except \(1\) and \(9\), which admit explicit nonzero representations. For all other parameters, the Hasse lower bound \(q+1-2\sqrt q>6\) guarantees a seventh rational point, hence a valid representation. The zero-vector criterion is the separability and splitting of \(T^3-a\), with characteristic three treated separately. The sharpness witness in \(\mathbb F_{11}\) is verified by complete enumeration and by the finite quadratic criterion described in the proof. Rechecking the hypotheses includes characteristic two, characteristic three, and coinciding singular parameters.

## Originality (PASS, with residual risk)
The closest archival source of Bower–Evans–Luo–Miller examines **scalar** sums of coordinates with a prescribed product, even in dimension three (arXiv:1212.2930, Theorem 4.1). It does not require the reciprocal sum simultaneously to equal an independently prescribed target. Khan–Magner–Senger–Winterhof (arXiv:1304.6943, Sections 1–2) instead count ordinary lines and distance-value sets. A published published-finding corpus finding on the **nonsplit** norm-one torus has a related three-sum theorem, but over \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\) with \(q+1\) torus elements: its intersection/pigeonhole argument relies on that cardinality and does not apply to the split torus with \(q-1\) elements. The nonsplit theorem also has the opposite congruence condition at the origin. Distinct model, quantifiers, and elliptic-curve argument were compared. Searches did not uncover an equivalent split-torus threshold theorem; this is not definitive historical priority evidence.

## Mathematical value (PASS)
The theorem settles a complete natural threefold-sum support problem for all sufficiently large finite fields, including the **sharp** universal threshold and the unique origin obstruction. The split norm-one torus is a basic modular hyperbola, and its three-term additive basis question is independent of the previously studied scalar coordinate-sum problem. The threshold captures a genuine failure at the largest smaller field, and the smooth/singular cubic decomposition explains why high-field coverage begins. It gives non-asymptotic exact information, not merely a routine reformulation of a known scalar bound.

## Limitations and follow-up risk
The historical overlap check could miss older work under the phrases "split norm-one torus", "reciprocal graph" or "additive basis of a hyperbola". No full multiplicity distribution or results over residue rings are claimed. The verifier tests finite fields; the mathematical universality rests on Hasse and the explicit singular cases, not the enumeration. Independent mathematical review has not been performed.
