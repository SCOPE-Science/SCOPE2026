# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The radial gamma-moment eigenvalues follow from orthogonality, the rational-exponential generating function follows from a convergent Laplace sum, and the Fourier--Gamma expansion is justified by bounded Fejér approximation and absolute summation. The first nonconstant harmonic supplies a sharp oscillatory term; summability of the remaining harmonics provides a strictly smaller geometric error. Geometric decay yields trace class and permits the generating-function limit giving exact trace \(1/4\). Transcendence of \(\pi\) implies irrationality of the phase divided by \(\pi\), establishing both signed envelopes. The standalone checker passes 71 deterministic finite assertions; this is not an infinite-parameter proof by testing.

## Originality — PASS with historical risk
Esmeral--Maximenko (arXiv:1505.07906) characterizes radial eigenvalue sequences and the generated C*-algebra. Dewage--Ólafsson (arXiv:2107.01465) extends quasi-radial spectral diagonalization; Galbis (arXiv:2102.02031) treats integrable radial symbols. Their inspected relevant results do not state the exact trace-class homogenization, scalar trace \(1/4\), and irrationally sign-changing sharp asymptotic for an infinite alternating-annulus indicator. Generic diagonalization and the gamma integral are not claimed as new. Specialization may have been obtained independently elsewhere; no global novelty certificate is implied.

## Value — PASS
The binary aperture has infinitely many annular components and infinite planar area; its radial 0--1 pattern remains macroscopically nonconvergent. Nonetheless analytic Gaussian localization averages it to a scalar with a summable residual, whose nonzero total trace and exact two-sided oscillatory spectral profile are determined. This gives an explicit, sharp example connecting Fock smoothing, trace ideals and periodic phase-space apertures, beyond a numerical bound on an isolated eigenvalue.

## Limits
One complex dimension and one fixed square-wave radial mask; no general periodic-class theorem, no independent historical-priority determination, and no independent mathematical validation. The high-degree sign-change conclusion uses transcendence of \(\pi\), not finite numerical observations.
