# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The classical unique regular-polygon area maximizer localizes the random event. In ordered polar coordinates, the convex-hull area has a positive linear penalty for each radial displacement and a positive definite quadratic penalty for the independent angular gap perturbations. The Gram determinant is exactly \(n\); radial simplex integration and the beta-ball integral give the claimed coefficient and exponent. Compact localization, a uniformly controlled Taylor remainder, and smooth sampling densities give a relative \(O_n(\sqrt\varepsilon)\) error. The computational verifier checks exact Taylor identities and finite stochastic consistency; the analytic proof supplies the universal quantifiers.

## Originality

PASS under available-source comparison, with residual historical risk. An earlier disk-triangle result already covers the \(n=3\) coefficient, which is not claimed as new; that result does not cover \(n\ge4\). The strongest close published paper is Koroleva–Nikitin (arXiv:1301.1360, Section 2.2), which analyzes maxima among samples on a circumference. It supplies the angular-only \((n-1)/2\) law, not the present mixed radial/angular \((3n-1)/2\) law with the explicit interior coefficient. Marckert (arXiv:1402.3512) treats convex-position probability in a disk without an area-tail theorem. Paouris–Pivovarov (2013) treat small-volume bounds for different random-convex-set models. Accessible research-record comparisons did not reveal a stronger covering interior-disk result. Exhaustive historical priority is not claimed.

## Mathematical value

PASS. For fixed sample size at least four, the area upper tail of interior points is a natural extreme geometric-probability invariant, and its complete dependence on polygon order is nontrivial. The exact leading coefficient and exponent distinguish interior from boundary sampling, complement the classical area extremal theorem, and provide a quantitative input for any future analysis of maximal-area subset statistics. No theorem about those dependent maxima is asserted here.

## Limits

The result applies only to fixed \(n\) and independent planar area-uniform disk points. It neither supplies the entire area CDF nor controls a varying sample size. The originality conclusion remains provisional pending independent literature review.
