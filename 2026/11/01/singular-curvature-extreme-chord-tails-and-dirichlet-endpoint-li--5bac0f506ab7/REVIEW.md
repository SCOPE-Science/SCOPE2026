# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The extremizing Euclidean diameter directions are exactly the coordinate axes for fixed \(1<p<2\). A uniform \(\ell_p\)-power endpoint deficit, the exact local endpoint-to-line Jacobian, the Cauchy hitting-measure normalization and the \(2(d-1)\)-dimensional \(\ell_p\)-ball volume prove the scalar asymptotic and conditional limit. The finite checker independently evaluates actual planar chords rather than assuming the local expansion.

**Originality — PASS, with explicit historical-priority risk.** Full accessible regular-polygon chord papers (Bäsel 2012 and García-Pelayo 2015) and Schneider's integral-geometry notes do not give this nonlinear singular-boundary tail and conditional mark law. Known quadratic positive-curvature and polyhedral tangent-cone regimes require incompatible local hypotheses, so their coefficients do not dominate the present statement. Ren's 2014 general chord-distribution article and the 2006 non-spherical particle-chord work remain plausible sources whose full texts could not be inspected; this prevents a definitive priority claim.

**Value — PASS.** Exact dependence on the exponent \(p\) and dimension \(d\), plus the endpoint Dirichlet limit, give a structural geometric characterization for a classical full continuum of convex bodies. The theorem also demonstrates a sharp regime distinction at the Euclidean ball: its continuum of diameter maximizers gives a different exponent. This contributes more than a numerical example or a routine special-case evaluation.

**Limits.** Only fixed \(d\ge2\) and \(1<p<2\); the formula is asymptotic, not an exact finite-deficit CDF. The conditional law is weak convergence. Independent historical and mathematical review remains outstanding.
