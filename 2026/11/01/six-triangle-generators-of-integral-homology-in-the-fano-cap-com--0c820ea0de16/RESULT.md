# Six-triangle generators of integral homology in the Fano cap complex

## Finding
Let \(K\) be the simplicial complex of point sets in the Fano plane containing no complete line. Every integral two-cycle with nonzero class in \(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\) contains at least six triangular faces. Exactly **42 triangular supports** attain this minimum. Each is the six-face boundary of a triangular bipyramid: a five-point subset, a Fano line serving as the equatorial triple, and the other two points as poles. These 42 carriers form one orbit under \(\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\), and each carries a primitive, nonzero integral homology class.

More strongly, the 42 oriented bipyramid boundaries **generate the entire integral two-cycle lattice** \(Z_2(K;\mathbb Z)=\ker\partial_2\), not merely its rational span. Consequently their homology classes generate \(\widetilde H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^6\); all other reduced integral homology vanishes.

## Assumptions and scope
The Fano points are the seven nonzero vectors \(1,\ldots,7\) in \(\mathbb F_2^3\), encoded as binary integers. A line is \(\{a,b,a\oplus b\}\) for distinct nonzero vectors \(a,b\), with \(\oplus\) denoting bitwise addition modulo two. Faces are nonempty subsets not containing any of these seven three-point lines, along with the empty face. Integer simplicial chains use the increasing vertex order and alternating omission boundary. The support of an integer chain is the set of triangular basis faces having nonzero coefficients. “Minimum” refers to number of triangular faces, not number of vertices or \(\ell^1\)-norm. Homologically essential means a cycle outside \(\operatorname{im}\partial_3\). This is a result for this single natural complex, not a uniform statement about larger finite projective planes.

## Proof
**Face and chain structure.** The simplicial face counts in cardinalities one through four are \((7,21,28,7)\), and no larger faces exist. The seven tetrahedra are complements of the seven lines. Their boundaries each comprise four triangles; these seven four-triangle blocks partition the 28 triangles. Consequently their oriented boundaries are linearly independent, have mutually disjoint supports, and generate a saturated subgroup of \(C_2(K;\mathbb Z)\). The edge-to-vertex, triangle-to-edge and tetrahedron-to-triangle matrices have rational ranks \(6,15,7\), respectively. The rank of the integer two-cycle lattice is therefore \(28-15=13\), its boundary subgroup has rank seven, and the quotient is free of rank six. Every excluded Fano-line triangle boundary can be filled by three permitted triangles using any fourth point outside that line. Since the complete seven-vertex simplex has vanishing first homology, replacing its seven missing triangle fillings in this way proves \(\widetilde H_1(K;\mathbb Z)=0\) integrally (also confirmed by fifteen unit Smith factors for the triangle-to-edge matrix). No other nonzero reduced homology occurs.

**Minimum carrier classification.** Enumerate the subsets of the 28 triangles of sizes one through six, and test the mod-two edge boundary by exclusive-or of their 21-bit edge-incidence masks. The resulting complete counts of mod-two two-cycles at respective support sizes \(1,2,3,4,5,6\) are \((0,0,0,7,0,42)\). The seven weight-four supports are exactly the tetrahedron-boundary blocks. Every weight-six support is exactly a triangular bipyramid described above: each of the 21 five-point sets contains precisely two Fano lines, yielding \(21\cdot2=42\) distinct supports. These assertions are checked by complete finite enumeration in the accompanying executable verifier, which constructs all faces from the stated line rule.

To pass from the mod-two enumeration to integral chains, divide any nonzero integer cycle by the highest common power of two in its coefficients, so that its reduction modulo two is a nonzero mod-two cycle. An integral cycle on at most five triangles reduces to a weight-four tetrahedron boundary, possibly with at most one extra triangle carrying an even coefficient. A triangle outside the tetrahedron contains at least two edges outside that tetrahedron; these cannot be canceled by the four interior triangles. Therefore the extra triangle cannot occur and the cycle is a multiple of the tetrahedron boundary, hence nullhomologous. At support size six, the only alternative to a weight-six mod-two support is a tetrahedron boundary with at most two even-coefficient extra triangles. Each extra triangle has at least two edges not in the tetrahedron, whereas two distinct triangles share at most one edge; external boundary cancellation is impossible. Thus every essential six-face integral cycle has one of the 42 bipyramid supports.

For a selected five-point set \(S\), a contained Fano line \(L=(a,b,c)\), and complementary poles \(p,q\), orient the corresponding six-face sphere as \(z_{S,L}=\partial[p,a,b,c]-\partial[q,a,b,c]\). The equatorial triangle \([a,b,c]\) is forbidden, so this expression is a cycle made of exactly six allowed triangular faces. The complex formed by those six faces is an orientable triangulated two-sphere; its integral two-cycle group has rank one generated by \(z_{S,L}\). It is nonboundary in \(K\), because any tetrahedral boundary combination has support a union of disjoint four-triangle blocks, while this sphere's support has six faces. Its coefficients are \(\pm1\), so the cycle is primitive in \(Z_2(K;\mathbb Z)\). For each of the 42 spheres, the integer matrix consisting of the seven tetrahedron boundaries plus that sphere has rank eight with eight unit Smith factors. This stronger saturation check proves the sphere class itself is primitive in \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\).

**Integral generation and symmetry.** Place the 42 oriented \(z_{S,L}\) into a \(28\times42\) integer matrix. Exact Smith normal form gives precisely thirteen nonzero diagonal invariant factors, **all equal to one**. Since this column lattice is saturated in \(\mathbb Z^{28}\) and has the same rank thirteen as \(\ker\partial_2\), the two integer lattices coincide. Independently, all \(168\) invertible linear transformations of \(\mathbb F_2^3\) preserve the Fano lines and act on the 42 minimal supports with a single orbit of size 42 (stabilizer of order four). These finite assertions are reconstructed from definitions in the verifier.

## Verification
Run `python verify.py` with Python 3 and SymPy installed in the same directory. The program constructs all seven lines, 63 nonempty subsets up to cardinality four and all actual faces; rebuilds integer boundary matrices; tests every triangle subset of cardinality at most six over \(\mathbb F_2\); checks all 42 geometric sphere cycles against the entire enumeration; computes the integral Smith invariants; and enumerates the complete 168-element point collineation group and its orbit. The expected last output line is `VERIFY_OK`, and `verify_output.txt` records one actual execution. Finite enumeration is exhaustive only for the specified 28 triangles; no claim about larger projective planes or arbitrary finite complexes is made.

## Relationship to prior work
Conant and Thistlethwaite develop the hypergraph independence-complex/theta-complex framework and Alexander duality for hypergraphs (arXiv:0808.0739, initially public August 5, 2008). This framework accommodates the seven-line Fano hypergraph but does not state the 42 shortest six-triangle integral carriers, their single symmetry orbit, or saturation of their generated two-cycle lattice. Published homology-representation results on the related Heawood incidence-graph independence complex determine rational symmetry information on a *different complex*; those results do not provide an integral support-minimum or saturated-cycle-lattice theorem for \(K\). The familiar Fano incidence and 168-element collineation symmetry are background facts, not claimed as new.

## Limitations
Novelty is assessed against inspected open literature and mathematical result records, not established as an absolute priority claim; equivalent treatments may exist under terminology such as Fano caps, Steiner triple-system avoidance, or hypergraph independence complexes.
