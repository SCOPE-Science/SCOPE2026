# Scientific review: open-neighborhood complexes of high-girth line graphs

## Correctness: PASS
The domain (finite connected simple graphs, minimum degree at least three, no three- or four-cycles) is explicit. For each edge \(e=uv\), the exact open-neighborhood facet is \(\Delta((S_u\setminus\{e\})\cup(S_v\setminus\{e\}))\). If a mixed pair of other edges at \(u\) and \(v\) belonged to a second such facet, the second connecting edge would form a triangle or square, excluded by the hypotheses. Consequently all mixed cofaces have unique generating facets. The two-stage matching in the proof is an explicit, size-ordered sequence of free-face collapses leaving the unmixed star boundaries plus one bridging edge per original edge. The star boundaries form a graph-of-spaces over the original graph; attaching the additional intervals gives the exact sphere-and-circle wedge. The proof works integrally and for unbounded degrees. In six finite examples, actual face-by-face collapse freeness, equality of the retained complex, and independent binary boundary ranks all passed. Risks: none identified within the stated graph domain; the examples alone cannot certify the all-graph theorem, which rests on the separate proof.

## Originality: PASS against the specified inspected corpus
Shukla's low-max-degree theorem does not cover the high-degree line graphs. The previous block-graph neighborhood classification addresses tree line graphs and clique blocks, whereas a base graph of minimum degree at least three and girth at least five contains an induced long cycle, so its line graph is not a block graph. The known edge-disjoint-square neighborhood classification cannot cover the degree-four stars: each \(K_4\) line-graph clique has overlapping four-cycles and the new complexes have nonzero integral second homology. The clique-complex line-graph theorem and the scale-two **closed** neighborhood Čech line-graph theorem concern distinct complexes and do not imply an open-neighborhood formula. This proof's unique mixed-edge collapse is not supplied by those results. Searches of publicly indexed mathematical claims, full archived prior-result text, and primary literature were compared by implication rather than title. Residual risk: equivalent unindexed or indirect full-text results outside the inspected corpus; no absolute priority claim is made. The cubic-only special case is acknowledged as an existing domain, not offered as separate novelty.

## Value: PASS
The arbitrary-degree, long-cycle family is naturally motivated by understanding topological invariants of line graphs beyond block graphs and graphs with bounded degree. The exact closed-form dimensions and integral ranks provide a structural answer for infinitely many graphs and distinguish the open-neighborhood topology from the closed-neighborhood Čech and clique topologies. The first natural four-regular example produces \(26\) independent two-dimensional sphere summands, and controlled edge removal varies that rank without losing the general theorem. This is a nontrivial infinite-family structural classification with an explicit collapse, not a routine single-graph calculation or a renamed prior theorem.
