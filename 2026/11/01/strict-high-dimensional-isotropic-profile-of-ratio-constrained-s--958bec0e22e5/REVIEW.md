The chamber inequality and vertices follow from a direct invertible difference-coordinate substitution; relative volumes are a product of simplex intercepts. A Dirichlet-moment identity gives the exact covariance contraction, and permutation invariance identifies the entire covariance on the zero-sum subspace. A Dirichlet simplex-sampling representation and variance bound give the uniform-in-rank typical-point shape limit in probability, with a monotone-grid argument. Uniform Riemann-sum convergence at fixed positive ratio and Stirling asymptotics establish the isotropic limit. A positive hyperbolic-function power series proves strict monotonicity; endpoint series give the sharp range. Exact-rational and independent planar integrations in the executable verifier corroborate the finite identities. Risks: asymptotic uniformity for dimension-dependent ratios is not established and is not claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The invariant is central in high-dimensional affine convex geometry, and the family is a directly motivated extension of the central-simplex construction with an open large-dimensional shape direction. The theorem gives a deterministic typical-point shape curve and a nontrivial balance between exponentially shrinking volume and covariance contraction, yielding a sharp closed profile with a strictly monotone parameter law and two exact endpoints.
