# Strong Morse complexes of friendship graphs: an exponential sphere-wedge formula

## Finding
Let \(F_m\) be the *friendship graph* (Dutch windmill of triangles): \(m\geq1\) triangles meeting at exactly one common vertex. Regard \(F_m\) as a **one-dimensional** simplicial complex; its graph triangles are not filled two-simplices. Let \(\mathcal{SM}(F_m)\) denote the **full** strong discrete-Morse matching complex, defined as the downward closure of the matchings produced by injective vertex filtrations. Then, for every integer \(m\geq1\),

\[
\boxed{\;|\mathcal{SM}(F_m)|\simeq\bigvee^{3^m+2m}S^{m-1}.\;}
\]

In particular, the first four cases have respectively \(5\) components beyond the base component, \(13\) circles, \(33\) two-spheres, and \(89\) three-spheres. The integral reduced homology is free of rank \(3^m+2m\) in degree \(m-1\) and zero in all other degrees. The exact face vector, with \(f_{k-1}\) counting nonempty faces of cardinality \(k\), is

\[
f_{k-1}=\binom{m}{k}4^k+2m\binom{m-1}{k-1}2^{k-1},\qquad 1\leq k\leq m.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
Write the graph vertices as a hub \(c\) and pairs \(a_i,b_i\), indexed by \(1\leq i\leq m\). The three edges in its \(i\)-th triangle are \(a_ib_i\), \(a_ic\), and \(b_ic\); there are no edges between different pairs. A vertex of \(\mathcal{SM}(F_m)\) is an ordered adjacent pair \((u,v)\), representing the pairing of the graph vertex \(u\) with the edge \(uv\). For an injective ordering of graph vertices, a graph vertex \(u\) is descending dominated if and only if it has **exactly one** earlier neighbor \(v\). In that case the strong matching contains \((u,v)\). The full strong Morse complex is generated by these sets over all orderings, including all their subsets. This criterion uses the fact that the link in a one-dimensional graph is discrete: two distinct earlier neighbors do not form a simplicial cone, even when they are adjacent in the graph.

Define four matching vertices per triangle

\[
D_i=\{(a_i,b_i),(b_i,a_i),(a_i,c),(b_i,c)\},
\]

considered as a discrete zero-dimensional complex, and two inward vertices \(E_i=\{(a_i,c),(b_i,c)\}\subset D_i\). Let

\[
A=D_1*D_2*\cdots*D_m,\quad
B_i=*_{j\ne i}E_j,
\]

with the empty join understood to contain only the empty simplex. For each \(i\), let \(h_i^a=(c,a_i)\) and \(h_i^b=(c,b_i)\).

## Proof
**1. Classification of generating matchings.** We prove the exact simplicial identity

\[
\mathcal{SM}(F_m)=A\ \cup\ \bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\bigl((h_i^a*B_i)\cup(h_i^b*B_i)\bigr).
\]

Here \(h*B\) denotes the simplicial cone with new apex \(h\). Consider any vertex ordering and the position of \(c\). If \(c\) has **not** exactly one earlier neighbor, it is unpaired. Within triangle \(i\), there are three possibilities: both leaves precede \(c\), exactly one does, or neither does. In the first case only the later leaf pairs, toward the earlier leaf; in the second neither leaf pairs; in the third only the first leaf after \(c\) pairs, toward \(c\). Hence at most one of the four vertices in \(D_i\) is used, so the matching is a face of \(A\).

If \(c\) *does* have exactly one earlier neighbor, call it \(a_i\) or \(b_i\). The matching contains precisely the corresponding \(h_i^a\) or \(h_i^b\) at its hub pair. The second leaf of triangle \(i\) has two earlier neighbors when it arrives and cannot pair. Every other triangle lies entirely after \(c\), so it contributes at most one of the two vertices in \(E_j\). Thus the matching belongs to the specified cone. Conversely, every facet of \(A\) can be realized by ordering both leaves of each triangle labeled with an internal pairing before \(c\), then \(c\), then both leaves of every triangle labeled with an inward pairing; for each internal pair, place the intended target first, and for each inward pair, place the intended source first. Every cone facet \(h_i^a*B_i\) is realized by ordering \(a_i\), then \(c\), then the remaining leaves, putting the chosen source in each other triangle before its mate; similarly for \(h_i^b*B_i\). Taking downward closures establishes the identity.

**2. Homotopy type of the main piece.** Each \(D_i\) consists of four isolated vertices, so it is based-homotopy equivalent to a wedge of three zero-spheres. The join formula, applied inductively, gives

\[
|A|\simeq\bigvee^{3^m}S^{m-1}.
\]

For \(m\geq2\), each \(B_i\) is the join of \(m-1\) discrete two-point complexes, hence is a triangulated sphere \(S^{m-2}\). Its inclusion in \(A\) is explicitly nullhomotopic: choosing any vertex \(d\in D_i\) gives the simplicial cone \(d*B_i\subseteq A\). Every new apex appears in only one of the \(2m\) cone summands; any two such summands intersect within \(A\) and their intersections with the union of earlier pieces are precisely their respective \(B_i\). Attaching one cone on \(B_i\) therefore contributes the homotopy cofiber of a nullhomotopic map \(S^{m-2}\to A\), namely one additional \(S^{m-1}\) wedge summand. Performing this operation for all \(2m\) cone apices yields \(\bigvee^{3^m+2m}S^{m-1}\). For \(m=2\), the same argument uses the nullhomotopic inclusion of each two-point \(S^0\) into the connected \(A\), already explicitly coned in \(A\).

For \(m=1\), the source graph is a triangle and the full strong Morse complex consists of its six possible directed vertex–edge pairings as isolated vertices. It is therefore homotopy equivalent to a wedge of five zero-spheres, agreeing with \(3^1+2\). This completes the integral homotopy proof for all \(m\geq1\).

**3. Exact face counts.** The faces of \(A\) of cardinality \(k\) choose \(k\) distinct triangles and one of four vertices in each, yielding \(\binom{m}{k}4^k\). A face outside \(A\) contains exactly one of the \(2m\) hub-apex vertices; choosing \(k-1\) other triangles and one of two inward vertices in each gives \(2m\binom{m-1}{k-1}2^{k-1}\). The cases are disjoint and exhaustive, proving the face-vector formula. \(\square\)

## Verification
The accompanying standalone Python 3 script `verify.py` independently enumerates every ordering of the graph vertices for \(m=1,2,3,4\), constructs each strong matching from its earlier-neighbor condition, and forms the entire downward-closed complex. Separately it generates every face of the proposed join-plus-cones decomposition and compares the two **complete finite face sets**. It also builds the original mod-two boundary matrices and verifies reduced Betti ranks. The four exhaustive cases use \(6,120,5040,362880\) vertex orderings, respectively, and the verifier terminates with `ALL_VERIFY_OK`. The all-\(m\) theorem follows from the symbolic proof above, not from finite sampling. Run `python3 verify.py` from a directory containing that file (Python 3.9 or newer; no dependencies).

## Relationship to prior work
Knudson and Owens–White introduced the full strong discrete-Morse matching complex and studied paths, cycles, stars, simplices, and boundaries. Their Proposition 8.5 supplies the \(m=1\) triangle case (a wedge of five zero-spheres); their Proposition 10.2 treats the complete graph as a one-dimensional complex, but their published theorems do not give the multi-triangle one-point union for \(m\geq2\). Donovan and Scoville computed the **ordinary edge-matching complex** of Dutch windmill graphs (Theorem 30): for the triangle windmill it is contractible. That is a different construction and does not imply the present full strong-Morse homotopy type. The distinction is structural: a graph can have a contractible ordinary matching complex while its strong discrete-Morse matching complex has exponentially many top-dimensional spheres. A known full-strong-Morse classification of complete bipartite graphs likewise does not cover this family, because each friendship graph with \(m\geq2\) has triangles and is not bipartite. No published covering theorem was identified under the friendship, windmill, triangle-bouquet, and strong-gradient formulations; this does not establish absolute novelty.

## Limitations
This theorem concerns the one-dimensional friendship graph and the **full strong** matching complex. It is not a statement about the ordinary Morse complex, the ordinary matching complex, the pure strong matching subcomplex, or the graph's filled two-dimensional clique complex. It establishes ordinary integral homotopy type and the face vector, not an equivariant decomposition, canonical sphere basis, or comparison maps to other Morse constructions. The finite checks cover \(m\leq4\); the general proof is combinatorial and homotopy-theoretic. Equivalent previously unindexed work may exist.
