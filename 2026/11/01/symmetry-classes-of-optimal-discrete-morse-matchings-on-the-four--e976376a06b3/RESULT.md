# Symmetry classes of optimal discrete Morse matchings on the four-simplex

## Finding
Let \(\Delta^4\) denote the simplex on five vertices \(\{0,1,2,3,4\}\). Let \(\mathcal O\) be the set of discrete Morse matchings of its nonempty face-poset Hasse diagram with exactly fifteen matched cover relations. Its full vertex-relabeling group is \(S_5\). There are exactly
\[
\boxed{|\mathcal O/S_5|=3234}
\]
equivalence classes. This is a classification by full automorphisms of the Morse complex, because its automorphism group is \(S_5\) by Lin and Scoville's theorem [2].

Fix the unique unmatched (critical) vertex to be \(0\), and put \(\Gamma=\operatorname{Stab}_{S_5}(0)\cong S_4\). The counts of rooted matchings fixed by elements of \(\Gamma\) are:

| Cycle type in \(S_4\) | Number of group elements | Fixed rooted matchings |
|---|---:|---:|
| \((1,1,1,1)\) | 1 | 76025 |
| \((2,1,1)\) | 6 | 243 |
| \((2,2)\) | 3 | 21 |
| \((3,1)\) | 8 | 8 |
| \((4)\) | 6 | 1 |

The exact stabilizer profile is finer than the orbit total:

| Stabilizer within \(S_4\) | Rooted matchings | Relabeling orbits |
|---|---:|---:|
| Trivial | 74568 | 3107 |
| Generated by a transposition | 1368 | 114 |
| Generated by a double transposition | 60 | 5 |
| A point-stabilizing \(S_3\) | 28 | 7 |
| All of \(S_4\) | 1 | 1 |

In particular, the unique rooted matching invariant under all permutations of the other four vertices pairs each nonempty \(A\subseteq\{1,2,3,4\}\) with \(A\cup\{0\}\). All other rooted optimal matchings have a proper stabilizer.

## Assumptions and scope
A Hasse vertex is a **nonempty** face of \(\Delta^4\). A matching is a set of cover pairs \((a,b)\), where \(a\subset b\) and \(|b|=|a|+1\), with no face in two pairs. It is a discrete Morse matching if reversing all matched cover arrows in the Hasse digraph leaves a directed acyclic graph. A matching is optimal if it minimizes the number of unmatched faces. The complex \(\Delta^4\) has \(31\) nonempty faces; hence fifteen is the maximum possible number of pairs. An acyclic matching with fifteen pairs exists (the explicitly stated cone pairing), so such matchings are optimal. An acyclic matching with exactly one critical face has that face a vertex, by the Morse inequalities and connectedness of the simplex [1,3]. Therefore every optimal matching has exactly one critical vertex. The relabeling group \(S_5\) acts on each matching through its action on subsets.

## Proof
**Reduction to a rooted finite census.** The group \(S_5\) is transitive on the five possible critical vertices. Every symmetry preserving a matching must preserve its unique critical vertex. Thus the orbit set \(\mathcal O/S_5\) is naturally in bijection with the orbit set of matchings critical at \(0\) under \(S_4\). To enumerate these matchings represent a nonempty face by its bitmask in \(\{1,\ldots,31\}\), with bit zero representing vertex \(0\). Exclude the singleton mask \(1\). The remaining thirty masks must form fifteen cover pairs.

**Exhaustive recurrence.** For each subset \(R\) of as-yet-unmatched masks, choose the numerically least \(a\in R\). For every \(b\in R\setminus\{a\}\) satisfying \(a\subset b,\ |b|=|a|+1\), or the same conditions with \(a,b\) exchanged, pair \(a\) with \(b\) and recurse on \(R\setminus\{a,b\}\). A leaf occurs only for \(R=\varnothing\). By induction on \(|R|\), this visits **every** perfect matching of the induced Hasse graph exactly once: every perfect matching pairs the least uncovered mask with exactly one admissible partner, and the residual pairing is a perfect matching of the smaller induced graph. The independent implementations enumerate exactly \(117837\) leaves.

**Exact acyclicity test.** For a candidate pairing, orient every Hasse cover from the smaller face to the larger face and reverse the fifteen matched edges. Kahn's topological-sort algorithm accepts exactly the directed acyclic graphs. An independent test constructs the Forman \(V\)-path digraph: each matched pair \((a,b)\) supplies arrows \(a\to c\) to every other codimension-one face \(c\ne a\) of \(b\). A directed cycle in this latter graph is exactly a nontrivial closed \(V\)-path, and hence exactly a forbidden cycle in the reversed Hasse diagram. Both implementations independently return precisely \(76025\) acyclic leaves. No Monte Carlo sampling, rank approximation, or extrapolation is involved: this is a finite exhaustive certificate for the full claimed domain.

**Fixed-point and stabilizer counts.** Run through all \(24\) permutations of vertices \(1,2,3,4\) for each accepted matching. Apply a permutation to the endpoints of all fifteen matched pairs and test exact equality of the resulting set of pairs. Grouping these exhaustive fixed counts by permutation cycle type gives the first table. Recording the subgroup of permutations fixing each matching gives precisely the second table; in particular, no stabilizer types other than the five listed occur. The scripts include exact assertions for the numeric totals and for every cycle type and stabilizer type. Burnside's lemma now yields
\[
|\mathcal O/S_5|=|\mathcal O_0/S_4|
=\frac{76025+6(243)+3(21)+8(8)+6(1)}{24}
=3234.
\]
For a rooted matching with stabilizer \(J\le S_4\), its orbit has size \(24/|J|\). This converts the rooted stabilizer counts into the orbit counts in the second table, including the distinction between the two conjugacy classes of involutions. The cone matching stated above is visibly \(S_4\)-fixed; its uniqueness follows from the corresponding fixed count of one.

## Verification
The included source files `verify_vpaths.py` and `verify_hasse.cpp` are standalone, self-contained exact enumerators. The first uses rankwise closed-\(V\)-path detection and the second uses a topological sort of the entire reversed Hasse digraph. Execute, from the directory containing these files, `python3 verify_vpaths.py` and `c++ -O2 -std=c++17 verify_hasse.cpp -o verify_hasse && ./verify_hasse`. The first prints both class tables and the stabilizer spectrum, and contains explicit assertions for every claimed value; the second prints the count for every individual element of the rooted \(S_4\). Both were executed from the packaged source paths, and their independent totals agree. The \(76025\) rooted total also reproduces the known labelled enumeration \(5(76025)=380125\) in [1], serving as a cross-check rather than as the novelty claim.

## Relationship to prior work
Scoville [1] gave the labelled number \(380125\) for all optimal matchings of \(\Delta^4\), and a structural identity that reduces the counting to the two-skeleton. Neither result specifies the fixed-point counts of nonidentity relabelings or the classification of their stabilizers, so the orbit total \(3234\) cannot be deduced from the labelled count alone. Lin and Scoville [2] determined the abstract automorphism group of the Morse complex but not its action-orbit distribution on optimal matchings. Chari and Joswig [3] introduced the complexes and obtained smaller-simplex counts. The present assertion is the exact automorphism-orbit and stabilizer refinement of the first computationally substantial simplex case.

## Limitations
This is a finite and complete classification for optimal discrete Morse matchings of the four-simplex, not a formula for higher-dimensional simplices or a determination of the homotopy type of the Morse complex. The mathematical content is an exact exhaustive finite proof with two independently implemented acyclicity tests; independent expert validation is not claimed. Searches may miss unindexed or unpublished equivalent enumerations. The public date of [1] pertains to the recent motivating paper, not to the older foundations [2,3].
