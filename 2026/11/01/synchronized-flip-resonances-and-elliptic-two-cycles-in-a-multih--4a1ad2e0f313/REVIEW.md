# Mathematical assessment: synchronized multiharmonic flip resonances

## Correctness

**PASS, self-review.** The claimed theorem is restricted to the finite-sine torus map with integer \(W\ge2\), positive \(K\), its explicit zero-kick fixed points, and local period-two bifurcations at two thresholds. The finite-sum sine factorization gives exactly \(2W\) distinct zero-kick roots; the bound \(|S_W|\le W\) establishes the stated global fixed-point census only when \(KW<2\pi\). The determinant-one Jacobian gives the spectral classes. Exact Taylor derivatives yield a strictly negative coefficient \(A\) at each positive-slope root. The analytic implicit-function theorem then proves local uniqueness and one-sided existence. The expanded return trace is \(2-8a\Delta+O(\Delta^2)\), yielding strict ellipticity. The standalone checker reconstructs the cycles from the original map and independently checks the expansions at several values of \(W\); its finite tests are not used to infer universal conclusions.

## Originality

**PASS within the inspected evidence, not a claim of exhaustive publication priority.** The full accessible HTML of Cetin–Tirnakli–Boghosian (2022), particularly its defining Methods equations and Results/Discussion, specifies the multiharmonic map and numerical statistics but does not give the two exact flip thresholds, all \(W-1\) simultaneous primitive-two births, the position-dependent opening constants, or their common first-order return-trace coefficient. Tirnakli–Tsallis–Cetin (2019) analyzes a different one-harmonic \(z\)-generalized kick. General one-dimensional period-doubling normal forms, including Glendinning–Simpson (2023), explain square-root branching in appropriate settings but do not calculate the present map-specific all-\(W\) classification. A relevant indexed result on the classical Chirikov map gives stationary covariance and two-cycle rigidity, not this multiharmonic local spectrum. Search results and inspected scientific claims were compared by implication rather than title alone. Unindexed analyses of this exact family remain a genuine originality risk.

## Value

**PASS.** A natural \(W\)-well Hamiltonian kick generates a family with a large number of coexisting elliptic and hyperbolic fixed points. The result gives a sharp, exactly computable pair of thresholds, detects simultaneous loss of ellipticity at \(W-1\) distinct points, determines the local replacement two-cycles and their distinct amplitudes, and finds a surprisingly identical first-order return-trace slope despite their different centers. These facts furnish spectral benchmark parameters independent of numerical phase-portrait resolution. The example \(W=50\), \(K=1/10\) demonstrates a concrete nontrivial exact fixed-point census. This contributes more than a single local normal-form calculation, while remaining deliberately narrower than a global chaos or KAM theorem.

## Closest literature and limitations

The defining 2022 *Scientific Reports* paper was available in full HTML and inspected through its mathematical and results sections. The 2019 arXiv preprint was available in HTML and its distinct \(z\)-generalized kick was inspected. General normal-form literature was examined at the public abstract/metadata level; the general theory does not provide the specialized formulas here. A possible unpublished or unindexed prior calculation is not excluded. The result does not prove nonlinear stability or global periodic-orbit completeness, and its asymptotics are not uniform in \(W\).
