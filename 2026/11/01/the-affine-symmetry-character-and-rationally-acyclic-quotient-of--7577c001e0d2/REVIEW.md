# Scientific assessment of the affine cap-complex character

## Correctness — PASS
An exact finite calculation reconstructs the full nine-point cap complex, the 79-pair acyclic discrete-Morse matching, and the integral witness \(\partial z=0\), \(\delta c=0\), \(\langle c,z\rangle=-1\). These data prove the supporting integral wedge type without relying on numerical homology. Enumeration of all 432 affine transformations verifies the determinant action on the primitive integral second-homology generator and obtains each degree-three trace by the signed Lefschetz formula. The ten profile multiplicities sum to 432, and their character averages vanish. The rational quotient conclusion follows by equivariant barycentric subdivision and exact averaging. The executable code runs with standard-library Python only and produced the recorded `VERIFY_OK` result.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The nonequivariant homotopy classification is already known and is explicitly excluded from the novelty claim. Lew (2017) computes nontrivial real \(H^2\) for the very same complex; Lew (2020) names it \(X_{2,9}\) in a question about convex representability, not its affine homology character. Merino et al. (2012) compute a Tutte polynomial of an associated rank-three matroid; that matroid's independent-set complex is not this four-element-cap complex. Searches of indexed published records and comparisons with the accessible preexisting calculation found no statement determining the full 432-element character or the rational quotient. An unindexed duplicate or stronger uninspected equivariant study remains a risk.

## Value — PASS
The Hesse configuration is the unique Steiner triple system of order nine and is a standard benchmark in topological combinatorics and nerve representability. The calculation supplies an exact group representation and a symmetry-quotient homology consequence that neither a Betti table nor a nonequivariant wedge type determines. It distinguishes the determinant action in degree two from the eleven-dimensional degree-three action, and identifies the disappearance of *all* rational reduced homology in the full quotient. This does not answer the separate convex-representability problem or classify integral quotient torsion.
