# The Chvátal two-independence complex is a three-sphere with a new dihedral orientation character

## Finding

Let \(C\) denote the twelve-vertex, four-regular, triangle-free Chvátal graph specified by the edge list below. Let \(X=\operatorname{Ind}_2(C)\) be its first higher-independence complex: a vertex subset is a simplex exactly when every connected component of its induced subgraph has at most two vertices. The **integral homotopy type** is

\[
\boxed{X\simeq S^3.}
\]

The full automorphism group of \(C\) is \(D_4\) with the generators \(r,s\) below. Its action on the top integral homology is the orientation character

\[
\boxed{(r^as^b)_*=(-1)^a\ \text{on }\widetilde H_3(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,\quad 0\le a<4,\quad b\in\{0,1\}.}
\]

In particular, a quarter turn acts by \(-1\) while the specified reflection acts by \(+1\). Consequently all reduced rational homology of the finite symmetry quotient \(X/D_4\) vanishes. The analogous already-known two-sphere arising from *ordinary* independence of \(C\) has a **different** reflection character.

## Assumptions and scope

Throughout, graphs are finite, simple and undirected; the graph labels are the integers `0` through `11`. The complete edge list, with each pair in increasing order, is:

```text
(0,1) (0,4) (0,6) (0,9) (1,2) (1,5) (1,7) (2,3)
(2,6) (2,8) (3,4) (3,7) (3,9) (4,5) (4,8)
(5,10) (5,11) (6,10) (6,11) (7,8) (7,11) (8,10) (9,10) (9,11)
```

For a subset \(U\) of the vertices, the following are equivalent: (i) all connected components of \(C[U]\) have at most two vertices; (ii) each vertex of \(C[U]\) has degree at most one; (iii) \(C[U]\) has no (not necessarily induced) three-vertex path. This is the \(P_3\)-devoid or *dissociation complex*. All subsets, including the empty simplex, are admitted under these conditions.

The full dihedral action is generated by the following graph automorphisms, written as the images of vertices `0` through `11` in order:

```text
r=(1,5,11,6,0,4,7,10,9,2,3,8)
s=(0,4,3,2,1,5,9,8,7,6,11,10)
```

They satisfy \(r^4=s^2=1\), \(srs=r^{-1}\). No appeal to an unlabeled or unspecified realization of the Chvátal graph is needed.

## Proof

**Complete finite face set.** Generate masks in the range from `1` through `4095`, retaining exactly those for which no selected vertex has two selected neighbors. Independently enumerate the forbidden triple masks formed by pairs of neighbors of each vertex and reject any mask containing one. The two predicates agree on every mask. There are exactly \(72\) distinct forbidden triples, and the resulting simplicial complex has \(424\) nonempty faces, with face vector

\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4,f_5)=(12,66,148,142,52,4).
\]

**A complete acyclic discrete-Morse matching.** Take all nonempty face masks as initially unmatched. For pivots in the precise order `11,10,...,0`, examine currently unmatched lower faces in increasing order of `(number of set bits, mask integer)`. Whenever a lower face omits the pivot and adding it is still an unmatched face, pair the two and remove both from the unmatched set. This deterministic algorithm produces exactly \(211\) matched face/coface pairs, listed as integers in `MORSE_PAIRS.json`. Precisely two critical faces remain: mask `2048` (the vertex `11`) and mask `232` (the tetrahedral face `3,5,6,7`). They are critical cells of dimensions zero and three, respectively.

A complete Hasse diagram of the \(424\) nonempty faces has \(1428\) codimension-one incidences. Direct each unmatched cover downward and each matched cover upward. A topological sort visits all \(424\) vertices; in particular this **proves acyclicity**, rather than presuming it from a greedy pairing algorithm. By Forman's acyclic-matching theorem, \(X\) is homotopy equivalent to a CW complex with exactly one zero-cell and one three-cell, and no cells of dimension one or two. The unique three-cell must attach by the constant map to the only zero-cell. This CW complex is \(S^3\), proving the integral homotopy claim. There is no unresolved attaching-map computation.

**Exact automorphism group.** Exhaustively backtrack over all injective vertex images, requiring at each step preservation of both adjacency and nonadjacency. Exactly eight bijections survive. The two explicit permutations \(r,s\) each preserve the edge list, satisfy the dihedral relations, and generate eight distinct automorphisms, hence exhaust \(\operatorname{Aut}(C)\cong D_4\).

**Integral orientation action.** For a graph automorphism \(g\), compute the unreduced simplicial Lefschetz number by summing \((-1)^{|\sigma|-1}\operatorname{sgn}(g|_\sigma)\) over all **setwise fixed**, nonempty simplices \(\sigma\). Because \(X\simeq S^3\), the Hopf trace formula gives

\[
L(g)=1-\operatorname{tr}\left(g_*\mid\widetilde H_3(X;\mathbb Q)\right).
\]

Exhaustive signed fixed-face counts give \(r_*=-1\) and \(s_*=+1\). As \(\widetilde H_3(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), the rational signs determine the full integral action. Multiplicativity yields \((r^as^b)_*=(-1)^a\) for all eight group elements. Since the average of this nontrivial degree-one character over \(D_4\) vanishes, its invariant subspace is zero; finite-group transfer identifies rational homology of \(X/D_4\) with invariant rational homology, giving the stated quotient conclusion. This says nothing about integral torsion in the quotient.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_chvatal_twoind.py` in a directory containing the exact bundled `MORSE_PAIRS.json` and compare its output with `CHECKS.txt`. The standalone Python 3 program uses only the standard library. It checks both independent face predicates on all \(4096\) masks, exact face vector, all \(211\) certificate pairs, the directed Hasse acyclicity census, complete group enumeration, and each dihedral Lefschetz character. It separately computes simplicial boundary ranks modulo \(2\), \(3\), and \(5\), each giving \((11,55,93,48,4)\) in successive positive dimensions and reduced Betti numbers concentrated in dimension three. These rank computations corroborate the theorem; the **acyclic matching and critical CW structure**, not finite-field homology, prove the integral homotopy type.

## Relationship to prior work

Taylan developed the general matching-tree and graph-devoid-complex framework and calculated specific families such as paths and cycles. Deshpande and Singh formalized higher-independence complexes and classified other graph families, including cycles, complete multipartite graphs, fully whiskered graphs and perfect rooted trees. The Chvátal graph is not an instance of the families in their stated classifications.

Separate prior exact calculations on the *same graph* have already established that its **ordinary independence complex** and its **open-neighborhood complex** are both homotopy equivalent to \(S^2\). They also computed the induced dihedral orientation character on that ordinary two-dimensional homology: \((r^as^b)_*=(-1)^{a+b}\). Those results are **not** claimed here. The present theorem concerns the distinct, larger \(P_3\)-devoid complex \(\operatorname{Ind}_2(C)\), proves its different topological dimension, and computes a contrasting *reflection-even* top-homology character. An ordinary-independence or neighborhood-complex homotopy equivalence neither gives this higher-independence homotopy type nor determines its dihedral homology action.

## Limitations

The result is for the explicit twelve-vertex Chvátal graph and parameter two, not a general classification of four-regular or four-chromatic graphs. The displayed orientation character does not classify equivariant homotopy sphere actions or the quotient's integral torsion. This is a finite exact computation with a complete replayable discrete-Morse certificate; definitive publication priority remains subject to unindexed literature, and independent mathematical review has not yet occurred.
