# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The graph is specified by the five XOR generators `1,2,4,8,15`, and the simplices are exactly the 2-independent vertex subsets. The complete finite checker reconstructs every 16-bit mask, all 2,143 augmented faces and 9,280 ordinary incidences; it proves the 1,065-pair matching acyclic by full topological sorting. The five-by-six integral critical boundary has a unit-rank-five minor, and low-dimensional CW attaching maps are classified by their integer degrees, giving the claimed homotopy equivalence to \(S^4\). The independent full-complex mod-two boundary ranks support this conclusion. Signed Lefschetz traces on verified generating automorphisms establish the sign representation on the unique integral fourth homology class. The computation is exhaustive for this graph, with no unsupported infinite extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected published evidence
Deshpande and Singh (arxiv:2001.05448) establish the higher-independence framework and homotopy results on other graph families. Abdelmalek et al. (arxiv:2106.10863) establish results for chordal and other restricted graphs; the Clebsch graph is nonchordal. The published Clebsch neighborhood-complex result and Clebsch matching-complex result were read in full and have different simplex definitions, so neither is an implication of the present claim. Related published Petersen two-independence and ordinary four-cube independence results apply to different graphs/parameters. published-finding corpus was queried for graph aliases, dissociation complexes, induced-three-path hypergraphs, potential more general theorems, and degree/sign representations. No covering result was identified; this is not proof of universal novelty. Differently indexed or unobserved equivalences remain a bibliographic risk.

## Value — PASS
This is a natural complete integral homotopy computation for the canonical five-regular Clebsch graph at the first higher-independence cutoff. It identifies a four-sphere hidden in a seven-dimensional nonpure complex, and the group-theoretic degree of every automorphism, strengthening a mere Betti calculation. The exact finite classification is motivated by the role of folded cubes among symmetric nonchordal graphs. It is not an arbitrary parameter slice or a routine restatement of a known sphere result.

## Scope and limitations
The result is restricted to the five-regular Clebsch graph, cutoff two. It determines the action on integral homology but not an equivariant homotopy type.
