# Review of the Petersen cut-complex filtration

## Correctness — PASS
Both direct-complement and facet-downward-closure enumerations agree on every simplex for all integer cut sizes from two through ten. Full directed-Hasse topological sorts verify all forward- and reverse-order discrete Morse pairings. The key five-cut complex has 495 nonempty faces, 237 forward pairs, and critical cells in degrees zero (one), three (eight) and four (twelve). Its exact integer Morse boundary has seven unit invariant factors and no nonunit factors; the reverse-order differential has nine unit factors. The absence of low-dimensional cells and the Hurewicz theorem promote this integer result to the precise wedge \(S^3\vee\bigvee^5 S^4\). Direct critical-cell patterns and integral boundary reductions settle all other cut sizes.

## Originality — PASS against inspected literature
The foundational 2023 cut-complex paper explicitly calculates only the Petersen two-cut complex and covers several graph families not including this five-cut computation. The 2024 sequel treats disjoint unions, squared paths and grids. Other known computations cover cut sizes three and four, not the five-cut mixed-dimensional stage. The published high-girth cut-complex theorem requires girth at least \(k+2\), which fails for the Petersen five-cut complex. Semantic searches for equivalent definitions, graphs, Betti signatures, five-cut and total-cut formulations found no covering statement. An unindexed equivalent earlier computation remains possible.

## Value — PASS
A complete integer homotopy classification of every possible ordinary cut size for the canonical Petersen graph is a natural closed finite classification. The unexpected transition from the previously known pure sphere bouquets to the unique pure, nonshellable mixed-dimensional five-cut complex gives a structural boundary that cannot be read off from the original graph's girth or its earlier two-/four-cut classifications. The lower previously known sizes are background, not repackaged novelty.

## Scientific limitations
This result is graph-specific. A mathematical or bibliographic generalization is not claimed; no independent external audit has occurred. An equivalent computation could exist in unindexed literature.
