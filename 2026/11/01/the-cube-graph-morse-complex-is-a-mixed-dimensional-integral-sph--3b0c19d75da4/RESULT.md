# The cube-graph Morse complex is a mixed-dimensional integral sphere wedge

## Finding
Let \(Q_3\) denote the one-skeleton of a three-dimensional cube, with vertex set \(\{0,1\}^3\) and edges joining binary triples differing in one coordinate. Let \(\mathfrak M(Q_3)\) be its **ordinary** simplicial discrete-Morse complex: vertices correspond to vertex-edge gradient pairings, and faces are acyclic pairwise compatible sets of such pairings. Then
\[
|\mathfrak M(Q_3)|\simeq \bigvee^{4}S^{5}\ \vee\ \bigvee^{139}S^{6}.
\]
In particular, its integral reduced homology is \(\mathbb Z^4\) in degree five, \(\mathbb Z^{139}\) in degree six and zero otherwise. No integral torsion occurs. This is a homotopy-equivalence claim about the full Morse complex, not only its mod-two homology and not about the distinct matching complex of the cube graph.

## Assumptions and scope
Number the cube vertices by integers zero through seven, with binary expansions as coordinates. Form the 24 directed arcs \((u,v)\) for pairs differing in exactly one bit, sorted lexicographically. An arc means the gradient pairing between graph vertex \(u\) and its incident undirected edge \(\{u,v\}\). A finite set of arcs forms a face exactly when (i) each graph vertex has at most one outgoing arc, (ii) no undirected graph edge is used twice, and (iii) the resulting directed graph has no directed circuit. Condition (iii) together with (i) ensures the selected underlying undirected graph is a rooted forest: an undirected cycle under outdegree at most one would force an oriented directed cycle. We include the empty face when constructing the augmented face poset, then pass to an ordinary CW model with one critical vertex. The result concerns this particular eight-vertex graph, without extension to higher-dimensional cube graphs.

## Proof
**Finite face census.** Enumerate the three possible outgoing arcs, or absence of an outgoing arc, at each of the eight cube vertices. Reject an assignment if tracing pointers produces any directed circuit. This exhausts every compatible gradient field exactly once: different selected tails encode disjoint graph edges unless two opposite arcs form a directed two-cycle, which is rejected. Sorted faces have size one through seven. The complete face vector is
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4,f_5,f_6)=(24,240,1296,4080,7488,7424,3072).
\]
An independent recursion using three states (absent or either orientation) for each of the twelve undirected edges produces the same vector. Hence the augmented face poset has 23,625 elements, including the empty face.

**An exhaustive acyclic matching.** Start with all augmented faces unmatched. For each of the 24 directed arcs in lexicographic order, pair every still-unmatched face not containing that arc with its still-unmatched coface obtained by adding the arc; delete both from the unmatched pool. This is a completely deterministic algorithm. It produces 11,695 face pairs, leaving precisely 50 six-vertex faces (dimension five) and 185 seven-vertex faces (dimension six), and no others. To certify Forman acyclicity, orient every cover relation \(a\subset b\) of the augmented face poset from \(a\) to \(b\) when matched, and from \(b\) to \(a\) when unmatched. Kahn's topological-sort algorithm exhausts all 23,625 nodes and all 124,200 cover relations. Thus the directed graph is acyclic. The empty-face matched pair can be released, making its singleton a critical zero-cell; reversing that one matched edge gives the empty face only incoming arrows, so acyclicity persists for the ordinary complex. The associated CW model consequently has exactly one zero-cell, 50 five-cells and 185 six-cells, with no other cells.

**Integral attaching degrees.** Orient a simplex by increasing arc indices. Let \(\partial\) be the usual alternating-face integral boundary. For each noncritical five-simplex \(x\), define its projection \(\psi(x)\) to the free abelian group on the 50 critical five-simplices recursively as follows. If \(x\) is critical, project to itself. If \(x\) is the upper member of a four-to-five matched pair, project to zero. Otherwise \(x\) is the lower member of a five-to-six pair \(x\subset y\), and the coefficient \(p\) of \(x\) in \(\partial y\) is \(\pm1\): set
\[
\psi(x)=-p^{-1}\!\sum_{\substack{z\subset y,\ \dim z=5\\z\ne x}}[y:z] \,\psi(z),
\]
where \([y:z]\in\{+1,-1\}\) is its incidence sign. The already certified acyclic matching guarantees that the recursion terminates. Project the boundary of each critical six-simplex through \(\psi\). This gives the exact signed Morse cellular differential \(D:\mathbb Z^{185}\to\mathbb Z^{50}\). The reproducible program computes an integer matrix of size \(50\times185\) with 366 nonzero entries, each \(\pm1\). Its rank over \(\mathbb Q\) is exactly 46, and its integral Smith form has exactly 46 nonzero diagonal entries, all equal to one. Independently, extracting the lexicographically first rational pivot-row/pivot-column minor gives a \(46\times46\) integer determinant of \(+1\); together with rank 46, this establishes that the nonzero Smith factors are units.

**From the integral matrix to homotopy.** The Morse CW complex consists of one vertex, 50 five-cells and 185 six-cells. Since its five-skeleton is \(\bigvee^{50}S^5\), every six-cell attaching map lies in \(\pi_5(\bigvee^{50}S^5)\cong\mathbb Z^{50}\), identified by its cellular degrees. Integral unimodular changes of the five-cell and six-cell bases are realized by cellular homotopy equivalences (elementary slides and their inverses). The Smith form of \(D\) therefore gives 46 cancelling degree-one five/six cell pairs, four remaining five-spheres, and 139 remaining six-spheres, with zero attaching degrees. Thus the displayed wedge homotopy type follows. The reduced Betti numbers agree with the independently computed mod-two ranks, and the Euler characteristic agrees with the alternating face count.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` reconstructs every face, all matched pairs, the **full** oriented Hasse graph, the signed Morse differential, its exact rational rank and integer Smith normal form, and a determinant-one full-rank minor. It terminates with `VERIFY_OK` only on success. Independent `crosscheck.py` processes the twelve undirected edges instead of the eight outgoing-tail choices; it recreates all 23,624 nonempty faces and obtains mod-two boundary ranks 4,487 and 2,933 in the top two dimensions, producing Betti numbers 4 and 139. It terminates with `CROSSCHECK_OK` only on success. The complete code and captured outputs are included. Run `python verify.py` and `python crosscheck.py` from the extracted package directory with Python 3.11+ and SymPy installed. There is no reliance on a black-box timeout, floating-point rank, or a partially enumerated search.

## Relationship to prior work
Chari and Joswig [1] define discrete Morse complexes and explain their rooted-forest interpretation; their discussed cycle/simplex constructions do not provide this cube-graph homotopy type. Kozlov [2] supplies the integral algebraic Morse reduction used for the signed boundary recurrence. Scoville and Zaremsky [3] establish general connectivity bounds, not this classification. Donovan, Lin, and Scoville [6] treat strong collapses, paths, cycles with leaves, trees and centipede graphs; their primary discussion does not compute the cubical graph case. The earlier full accessible same-object finding [4] determines the reduced **mod-two** homology dimensions 4 and 139 in degrees five and six and explicitly leaves integral homology and homotopy type unresolved. Those dimensions alone cannot determine torsion or the attaching maps. The present result adds a constructive acyclic matching and a unit Smith-form certificate that imply the full integral sphere-wedge homotopy type. The distinct graph matching complex, and Morse complexes of other graphs, are not equivalent statements. Courtney's thesis [5] studies directed forest complexes of Cayley graphs, making it a plausible same-object source because \(Q_3\) is a Cayley graph; only its public abstract was accessible, so exact full-text coverage could not be ruled out.

## Limitations
The computation exhausts this one fixed finite complex and establishes its complete homotopy type, but does not imply a general formula for \(Q_n\) when \(n>3\). The result does not determine automorphism-group representations or canonical geometric representatives of generators. The uninspected full text of [5] is a specific originality risk. This result has a self-review only; independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
