# The cube's two-particle configuration space has a collapsible full-symmetry quotient

## Finding
Let \(Q_3\) be the one-skeleton of a three-dimensional cube, considered as a geometric graph with vertices \(\{0,1\}^3\), and let
\[
X=\operatorname{UConf}_2(|Q_3|)=\{\{x,y\}:x,y\in|Q_3|,\ x\ne y\}.
\]
The full graph automorphism group \(G=\operatorname{Aut}(Q_3)\cong(C_2)^3\rtimes S_3\), of order \(48\), acts on \(X\) by moving both particles. **The topological quotient \(X/G\) is contractible.** More precisely, \(X/G\) strongly deformation retracts onto a finite, two-dimensional, collapsible \(\Delta\)-complex with \(8\) vertices, \(15\) edges, and \(8\) triangles. The latter admits an explicit sequence of exactly \(8\) triangle–edge and \(7\) edge–vertex elementary collapses.

This is a distinction between the configuration space and its full-symmetry moduli space: the equivariantly retracted configuration cube complex itself has \(28\) vertices, \(72\) edges, and \(42\) squares, hence Euler characteristic \(-2\), whereas its symmetry quotient is contractible.

## Assumptions and scope
Edges of \(Q_3\) are ordinary unit intervals, with distinct endpoints whose binary labels differ in exactly one coordinate. Two particles are *unordered* but are not permitted to coincide, including at graph vertices. The acting group consists of all coordinate permutations and independent bit flips; no spatial rotations-only restriction is imposed. The quotient is the ordinary orbit space, not a Borel or orbifold classifying space. An elementary collapse below is an operation on a finite \(\Delta\)-complex; the noncompact space \(X/G\) is not claimed to be a finite simplicial complex itself.

Write \(D\) for the unordered cubical deleted product: its \(0\)-cells are pairs of distinct vertices, its \(1\)-cells are pairs consisting of one vertex and one nonincident closed edge, and its \(2\)-cells are pairs of closed edges with disjoint vertex sets. Its cells represent configurations in disjoint closed simplices. An explicit strong deformation retraction \(X\to D\) respecting all cube automorphisms is proved below, so its quotient gives \(X/G\simeq D/G\).

## Proof
**Step 1: an equivariant retraction.** For ordered distinct points \(x,y\) on the graph, use their barycentric vertex-coordinate vectors \(\alpha=(\alpha_i)\) and \(\beta=(\beta_i)\). Each vector is nonnegative, sums to one, and is supported in a single graph vertex or edge. Set
\[
s=\sum_i(\alpha_i-\beta_i)_+=\sum_i(\beta_i-\alpha_i)_+>0,
\qquad t_+=\max(t,0),
\]
and
\[
\alpha'_i=\frac{(\alpha_i-\beta_i)_+}{s},\qquad
\beta'_i=\frac{(\beta_i-\alpha_i)_+}{s}.
\]
These are barycentric vectors of points \(x'\) and \(y'\) whose *closed simplicial supports are disjoint*, and they lie in the original simplices containing \(x\) and \(y\). The homotopy
\[
\alpha(u)=(1-u)\alpha+u\alpha',\qquad
\beta(u)=(1-u)\beta+u\beta',\qquad0\le u\le1
\]
stays in the configuration space, because
\[
\alpha(u)-\beta(u)=\left(1-u+\frac{u}{s}\right)(\alpha-\beta)\ne0.
\]
It fixes every configuration already having disjoint supports, for then \(s=1\). The construction is continuous and invariant under renaming graph vertices and exchanging the two particles. Thus it descends to a \(G\)-equivariant strong deformation retraction \(X\searrow D\), then to a strong deformation retraction \(X/G\searrow D/G\). In particular, no unjustified assumption that an arbitrary nonequivariant retraction descends to an orbit quotient is used.

**Step 2: exact finite quotient.** The graph has \(8\) vertices and \(12\) edges. The three types of cubical cells of \(D\) number \((28,72,42)\). Barycentric subdivision \(\operatorname{sd}D\) consists of flags of its cells, with counts \((142,480,336)\) in simplicial dimensions \(0,1,2\). The full group has \(3!2^3=48\) elements: the image of any vertex has \(8\) choices, and the ordered images of its three neighbors have at most \(3!\) choices, after which a connected cube automorphism is uniquely determined; all \(48\) choices are realized by bit flips and coordinate permutations. As each simplex of the barycentric subdivision is a strictly dimension-increasing flag of cells, no group element can permute its vertices nontrivially while preserving that simplex. Thus the orbit space \(\operatorname{sd}D/G\) is an honest finite \(\Delta\)-complex whose vertices, edges and triangles are the corresponding orbits of flags. Exact enumeration gives \((8,15,8)\). The vertex/edge/triangle orbit representatives and the complete quotient incidence structure are given in `certificate.json` and exhaustively regenerated by `verify.py`.

**Step 3: collapse.** Number the quotient vertices \(V0,\ldots,V7\), edges \(E0,\ldots,E14\), and triangles \(T0,\ldots,T7\) as in the certificate. First remove these free triangle–edge pairs, in precisely this order:
\[
(T0,E1),(T1,E0),(T2,E5),(T3,E3),
(T4,E6),(T5,E4),(T6,E9),(T7,E8).
\]
Next remove the free edge–vertex pairs
\[
(E2,V0),(E7,V1),(E10,V2),(E11,V5),
(E12,V3),(E13,V6),(E14,V4).
\]
Each lower-dimensional face lies in exactly one currently remaining higher-dimensional cell, as directly checked against the complete incidence table. The remaining complex is the single vertex \(V7\). Therefore \(\operatorname{sd}D/G\) collapses to a point and
\[
X/G\simeq D/G\cong |\operatorname{sd}D/G|\simeq\ast.
\]
The displayed homotopy equivalence is in fact induced by the strong retraction from Step 1 and elementary collapses in Step 3.

## Verification
From a directory containing `verify.py` and `certificate.json`, run `python3 verify.py` (Python standard library only). The program independently reconstructs the \(8\) graph vertices, \(12\) graph edges, \(28+72+42\) cubical cells, all \(142+480+336\) barycentric flags, all \(48\) automorphisms, their face-preserving actions, and all quotient face orbits. It then tests every proposed collapse for incidence and unique-free-face conditions *at the instant the collapse occurs*, finishing with a single vertex. Expected terminal marker: `CUBE_CONFIG_QUOTIENT_VERIFY_OK`. The textual output is included in `verification_output.txt`. The continuous retraction in Step 1 is proved algebraically, not inferred from finite enumerations.

## Relationship to prior work
Farley and Sabalka study unordered graph configuration spaces via discrete Morse theory, and Prue and Scrimshaw give stable-equivalence criteria for the standard cubical discrete configuration model. Their results explain why cubical models are natural but do not state the present full cube-automorphism quotient and its explicit collapse. Barnett and Farber study ordered two-point configuration spaces on planar graphs and their homological invariants; those are a different quotient and do not imply the claimed contractibility. A published calculation for the two-particle space of the *cuboctahedral graph* \(L(Q_3)\) concerns a distinct \(12\)-vertex graph rather than the \(8\)-vertex graph \(Q_3\), and computes representation modules, not this orbit-space collapse. An indexed cube three-cut-complex result also concerns a different simplicial object. The claimed result is the **topological full-symmetry quotient**, not the ordinary nonequivariant Betti numbers nor the general discretization theorem.

## Limitations
This proves a single precise case: unordered two-point configurations on the unsubdivided simple cube graph, modulo all \(48\) graph automorphisms. It does not compute Borel-equivariant homology, isotropy-group data, larger particle numbers, or analogous quotients for other graphs. The check of indexed mathematical records and the inspected primary literature cannot rule out an unindexed equivalent result. The finite certificate establishes the quotient collapse; it does not by itself certify the continuous part, which is separately proved in Step 1. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
