# Mathematical review

**Correctness — PASS.** The normalized body is exactly the split spheroid obtained by moving chords of the ball. The proposed ellipsoid satisfies a global upper-ellipsoid inclusion identity and a convexity-based lower-ellipsoid inclusion argument, including the flat-base endpoint. Its normalized contacts form an exact regular simplex with positive isotropic weights, so John's uniqueness theorem proves maximality. Direct expansion yields both endpoint constants and the transition function. The included high-precision checks passed. The proof remains conditional only on the classical, cited John contact theorem, which was inspected in Ball's full text.

**Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk.** The Kipp–Sagmeister paper introduces the operation and studies its iterates; its inspected introduction, John-position usage, and concluding sections do not state the one-step ellipsoid profile. Ball supplies the general contact criterion, not a formula for two joined semiaxial ellipsoid halves. Searches for half-ellipsoid inscribed ellipsoids, glued two-half ellipsoids, shaking-ball John ratios, and high-dimensional transition scalings found no full family result. Güler–Gürtuna analyze John ellipsoids for slabs cut from a single ellipsoid, a different defining geometry from two axially stitched half ellipsoids. Earlier explicit John ellipsoids of homothetic frusta have linear cross-sectional radius and do not cover the two curved half-spheroidal caps. The half-ellipsoid endpoint is classical and is not claimed to be new in isolation. Older literature under different terminology may contain an equivalent family computation.

**Value — PASS.** John normalization is directly used in the motivating symmetrization-rigidity program, making its one-step change a natural geometric diagnostic. A global exact family formula in every dimension and the singular boundary-layer limit sharply distinguish shaking from intermediate symmetrization; the transition is a structural phenomenon rather than a routine numerical instance. No claim is made about repeated random processes.
