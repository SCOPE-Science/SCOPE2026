# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The complete graph and simplex specifications are unambiguous, and two independent full enumerators agree for all \(3\le n\le6\). The explicit deterministic matching is checked against the full Hasse diagram; all four directed diagrams are acyclic. Critical-cell counts give minimal CW models. Integral oriented Morse differentials have rank three at the pentagonal prism with an explicit determinant \(-1\) maximal minor, and rank one at the hexagonal prism with an explicit coefficient \(+1\). Those primitive attachments cancel over integer CW bases because \(\pi_2(\bigvee S^2)\) and \(\pi_3(\bigvee S^3)\) have the required free generators. Independent exact \(\mathbb F_2\) simplicial boundary calculations and Euler checks agree. The certificate proves a finite homotopy classification, not any infinite extrapolation.

**Originality — PASS relative to checked evidence.** The closed circular ladders differ from the open polygonal-line and grid families. The full 2022-11-22 polygonal-tiling paper's Theorem 13 covers strings, not cyclicly glued prisms; its appendix uses open graphs. The full Agarwal–Basak paper, arXiv:2608.30601v2, Main Theorem 2 settles vertex independence and perfect matching complexes, not the ordinary matching complex, and Problem 26 explicitly identifies the latter as further work. Closest exact published results include the cube ordinary matching complex (our \(n=4\) baseline, not new), the hexagonal prism total three-cut complex (a different simplicial complex), and the open six-rung matching complex (a different graph). Search of related result records under graph, invariant, alias, exact ranks and line-graph formulation found no coverage of the new \(n=5,6\) homotopy types. Negative search cannot prove uniqueness; unindexed computations remain a residual risk.

**Value — PASS.** This is a natural first finite classification for a family whose ordinary matching complexes are explicitly singled out as a research direction in Agarwal–Basak, Problem 26. It reveals an unexpected mixed-dimensional integral wedge at the pentagonal prism, absent in the triangular prism and cube baselines, and its disappearance at the hexagonal prism. The pentagonal perfect matching subcomplex is known contractible, so the contrast concretely distinguishes the ordinary and perfect matching constructions. Neither novelty nor code efficiency alone is relied upon as motivation; the object and transition have intrinsic topological meaning. No general-family theorem or further application is claimed.
