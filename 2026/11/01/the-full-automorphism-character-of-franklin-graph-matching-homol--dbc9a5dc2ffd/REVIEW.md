# Scientific review of the Franklin matching-homology character

## Correctness — PASS
The definition is the ordinary matching simplicial complex of the explicitly specified 12-vertex, 18-edge Franklin graph. A complete list of 1,091 nonempty faces supports a 539-pair acyclic discrete-Morse matching. A topological sort checks all 3,942 oriented face incidences, leaving only a zero-cell and twelve three-cells. This proves integral homotopy equivalence with a wedge of twelve three-spheres. Complete enumeration of all 48 graph automorphisms and their induced action on four Hamiltonian cycles proves the asserted group splitting. Signed fixed-simplex traces and an independent edge-orbit inclusion–exclusion count agree for each automorphism. Exact inner products with the ten irreducible characters of the resulting \(S_4\times C_2\) group yield the claimed multiplicities. A separately implemented binary chain complex gives the compatible Betti sequence. These are finite exhaustive computations with reproducible source code, not extrapolations.

## Originality — PASS, subject to residual bibliographic risk
The directly relevant, previously published Franklin-graph matching-complex calculation determines its twelve-sphere homotopy type and the two edge-deletion/link profiles. Its full mathematical statement, proof and limitations were examined; no equivariant decomposition was asserted. The new result explicitly excludes that already-known homotopy type from its novelty claim. A related published octahedral-graph character computation concerns a different matching complex on six graph vertices, and does not imply the Franklin character merely because the automorphism groups are abstractly isomorphic. Engström's 2005 claw-free independence-complex methods are general connectivity tools, not a 48-element Franklin character table. Targeted mathematical-record, graph-alias and primary-literature searches found no identical or broader coverage. This is a best-knowledge priority conclusion, not proof that no unindexed source exists.

## Value — PASS
The Franklin graph is a canonical symmetric cubic graph, and its ordinary matching complex is already topologically known as a twelve-sphere wedge. This makes its **previously undetermined symmetry module** a natural missing invariant, not a gratuitous numerical slice. The complete \(S_4\times C_2\) decomposition reveals the eight/four splitting under central half-turn, a vanishing full-symmetry invariant space, and a one-dimensional index-two-subgroup invariant space. Thus the ordinary quotients have different rational homology although nonequivariant sphere counts alone cannot distinguish them. The exact ten-class calculation, its two independent trace mechanisms and its structural quotient consequence are materially beyond a routine restatement of Betti numbers.

## Closest literature and limitations
Engström (2005, arXiv:math/0512420) furnishes an early matching-complex-related topological framework. The published *Edge-orbit matching-complex splitting for the Franklin graph* (identifier `be4888fadcca`) covers the ordinary homotopy type but not this automorphism module; *The full octahedral symmetry representation of the matching complex* (identifier `c700eceb52bb`) treats a different graph. The Knödel survey supplies graph-family background only. The result is over rational coefficients and for one fixed graph, not an integral representation-lattice decomposition or a classification of all crossed prisms. Unindexed equivalents remain a residual originality risk, and an independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
