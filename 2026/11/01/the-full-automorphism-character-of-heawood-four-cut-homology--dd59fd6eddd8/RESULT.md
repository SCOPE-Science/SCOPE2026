# The full automorphism character of Heawood four-cut homology

## Finding
Let \(H\) be the incidence graph of the Fano projective plane, with fourteen vertices. Its ordinary four-cut complex \(\Delta_4(H)\) is the simplicial complex generated by complements of four-vertex sets that induce disconnected subgraphs. The natural automorphism group \(A=\operatorname{Aut}(H)\) has order \(336\); its index-two collineation subgroup \(A_0\) preserves the two classes of the bipartition. The rational character on \(\widetilde H_9(\Delta_4(H);\mathbb Q)\) is exactly:

| Bipartition | Order | Number of elements | Character trace |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Preserves | 1 | 1 | 188 |
| Preserves | 2 | 21 | -12 |
| Preserves | 3 | 56 | 2 |
| Preserves | 4 | 42 | 0 |
| Preserves | 7 | 48 | -1 |
| Exchanges | 2 | 28 | 0 |
| Exchanges | 6 | 56 | 0 |
| Exchanges | 8 | 84 | 0 |

The order-seven row is the union of two conjugacy classes; they have the same trace. Both group-average character values vanish. Therefore the rational homology has no invariant vectors under \(A_0\) or \(A\), and
\[
\widetilde H_i(|\Delta_4(H)|/A;\mathbb Q)=0\quad\text{for all integers }i.
\]
This is a complete character classification, not an integral representation or equivariant homotopy classification. The nonequivariant homotopy type \(\Delta_4(H)\simeq\bigvee^{188}S^9\) was established previously and is not new here.

## Assumptions and scope
Realize the seven points as nonzero vectors \(p\in\mathbb F_2^3\), and the seven lines as nonzero covectors \(\ell\in\mathbb F_2^3\), with adjacency precisely when \(p\cdot\ell=0\). The complex is **ordinary**, not total: a complementary four-set need only be disconnected, not independent. All homology in the character statement is reduced rational singular (equivalently simplicial) homology. The automorphisms are the full abstract graph automorphisms, including point–line dualities. The order of an automorphism is its order as a permutation of the fourteen graph vertices.

## Proof
**1. The known nonequivariant homology is verified in a compact integral model.** The graph is cubic, bipartite, and has girth six. The Alexander dual \(L=\Delta_4(H)^*\) contains the complete two-skeleton of the thirteen-simplex and has exactly one tetrahedron for each connected induced four-set of \(H\). The connected four-sets are induced paths and three-leaf stars: there are \(21(3-1)^2=84\) paths and \(14\binom33=14\) stars, altogether \(98\). The complete two-skeleton has reduced integral second homology of rank \(\binom{13}{3}=286\). For each connected four-set, remove an internal tree vertex; the remaining three-set is disconnected and, by girth six, cannot be extended to any other connected four-set. The associated triangle is thus a private face of that tetrahedron. The ninety-eight boundary columns contain an integral unit minor and are a split injection. Hence \(\widetilde H_2(L;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^{188}\), with all other reduced homology zero. Every five-set induces a forest and contains a disconnected four-set, so \(\Delta_4(H)\) contains a complete eight-skeleton. Integral Alexander duality and Hurewicz–Whitehead theory yield the known wedge \(\bigvee^{188}S^9\). This paragraph reconstructs the needed premises, rather than claiming novelty for this result.

**2. Enumerate all graph automorphisms.** Every invertible linear map \(M\in GL(3,2)\) acts by \(p\mapsto Mp\) and \(\ell\mapsto (M^{-1})^{\mathsf T}\ell\), yielding \(168\) distinct point–line-preserving graph automorphisms. Polarity \(p\leftrightarrow\ell_p\) doubles this to \(336\). The group is complete: a preserving automorphism induces a permutation of the seven points taking Fano lines to Fano lines, and an independent enumeration of all \(7!\) point permutations finds exactly those \(168\). A class-exchanging automorphism becomes preserving after polarity.

**3. Compute the signed homological traces.** Orient each face by increasing vertex labels (points first, lines second). For any automorphism \(g\), define
\[
T_j(g)=\sum_{\substack{F\in\Delta_4(H),\ |F|=j+1,\ gF=F}}\operatorname{sgn}(g|_F),
\]
where the sign is the parity of the permutation induced on the ordered vertices of the fixed face. Since the only nonzero reduced rational homology is in degree nine, the signed simplicial Lefschetz formula gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}(g\mid\widetilde H_9)=1-\sum_{j=0}^{9}(-1)^j T_j(g).
\]
The supplied checker constructs all \(903\) disconnected four-sets, the \(15{,}815\) nonempty cut faces and all \(336\) automorphisms, and evaluates this *exact integer formula* for every automorphism. Grouping by bipartition action and order gives the table.

**4. Independent dual character calculation.** From the complete dual two-skeleton plus ninety-eight connected-four-set tetrahedra, write \(U_j(g)\) for its signed chain traces. Equivariant combinatorial Alexander duality twists by the sign of the permutation of all fourteen vertices, giving
\[
\operatorname{tr}(g\mid\widetilde H_9)=\operatorname{sgn}_{14}(g)\left(-1+U_0(g)-U_1(g)+U_2(g)-U_3(g)\right).
\]
The checker compares this independent four-level dual calculation with the full cut-face calculation for **each** of the \(336\) automorphisms and finds exact agreement. The collineation contributions sum to \(188-21\cdot12+56\cdot2-48=0\); every bipartition-exchanging contribution vanishes. Taking the character average over either group gives invariant dimension zero. Rational homology of a finite simplicial-group orbit space equals the invariant homology after barycentric subdivision, yielding the quotient assertion.

## Relationship to prior work
Bayer et al. introduced cut complexes and in Proposition 6.2 proved the complete-skeleton fact under the no-short-cycle condition; their equivariant examples concern different graph families. The follow-up focuses on disjoint unions, squared paths, grids, paths and cycles. The published high-girth cut-complex result already implies the \(188\)-sphere homotopy classification for the Heawood graph and gives the private-face argument. **None of these inspected results supplies the full \(336\)-element homology character, the vanishing duality traces, or the resulting rational quotient calculation.** Other records on the Heawood total three-cut, matching, and independence complexes concern different simplicial constructions and cannot imply this character without an additional comparison theorem. Searches for Fano incidence, Levi graph, \(4\)-cut, connected four-subsets, character and invariant formulations found no covering statement; absence of search hits alone is not a novelty proof.

## Limitations
The character determines the rational representation but does not identify an integral lattice or equivariant homotopy type. Rational acyclicity of the quotient does not imply contractibility or integral acyclicity. The no-short-cycle argument is stated for the specific Fano incidence graph, not as a new universal high-girth theorem. Published databases can omit equivalent or unindexed results, and no independent audit has been performed.
