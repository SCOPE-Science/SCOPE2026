# The full dihedral first-homology character of polygonal real moment-angle surfaces

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge5\), let \(C_n\) be the cycle with vertices \(0,\dots,n-1\), let \(X_n=(D^1,S^0)^{C_n}\), and let \(D_n=\langle r,s\rangle\) act by cyclic rotation and reflection of the coordinate positions. The complete rational homology character in degree one is
\[
\operatorname{tr}(1\mid H_1(X_n;\mathbb Q))=2+(n-4)2^{n-2},
\]
\[
\operatorname{tr}(r^k\mid H_1(X_n;\mathbb Q))=2-2^{\gcd(n,k)}\quad(1\le k<n),\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(sr^k\mid H_1(X_n;\mathbb Q))=0\quad(0\le k<n).
\]
All first-homology character values are thus determined for every dihedral symmetry, including both reflection types when \(n\) is even. As a consequence, for every prime \(p\ge5\), \(H_1(X_p;\mathbb Q)\) consists of \((2+(p-4)2^{p-2})/(2p)\) copies of the regular rational \(D_p\)-module. For example, \(n=7\) gives seven copies and \(n=11\) gives 163 copies. The first homology rank, known independently from the surface genus, is not asserted to be new.

## Assumptions and scope
Use the standard cubical cell structure of \(D^1=[-1,1]\), with two vertices \(v^-=-1\), \(v^+=1\), and one oriented edge \(e\) directed from negative to positive. A cell of \(X_n\) selects a set of active edge coordinates that is a face of \(C_n\); hence an active set is empty, a singleton, or one of the \(n\) adjacent pairs \(\{i,i+1\}\), with indices modulo \(n\). All other coordinates are endpoint signs. The action of \(D_n\) only permutes the coordinates, without sign flips of their values. There are \(2^n\), \(n2^{n-1}\), and \(n2^{n-2}\) cells in dimensions zero, one, and two. These form a connected closed orientable cubical surface; connectivity follows from its full hypercube one-skeleton, and each one-cell has exactly two incident squares.

## Proof
**Orientation and top homology.** Orient every square whose active coordinates are the cyclically ordered pair \((i,i+1)\) by \(e_i\wedge e_{i+1}\). For such a square with inactive signs \(\epsilon_j\in\{-1,+1\}\), give its coefficient in a two-chain \(z\) the value \(\prod_{j\notin\{i,i+1\}}\epsilon_j\). For a one-cell active at coordinate \(j\), the two incident squares have active pairs \((j-1,j)\) and \((j,j+1)\). If the fixed neighboring signs are \(a=\epsilon_{j-1}\) and \(b=\epsilon_{j+1}\), their contributions to the oriented boundary are, respectively, \(a\prod_{k\ne j-1,j}\epsilon_k\) and \(-b\prod_{k\ne j,j+1}\epsilon_k\). They cancel because each equals \(ab\prod_{k\ne j-1,j,j+1}\epsilon_k\) up to sign. Thus \(\partial z=0\). Every square appears with a nonzero coefficient, so \(z\) is a nonzero top-dimensional integral cycle. Its coefficients agree across all shared one-cells; since \(X_n\) is a connected closed surface, its top integral homology is generated by \(z\). A cyclic rotation preserves both the ordered edge direction and the product of inactive signs, while a reflection reverses each ordered edge direction. Therefore the action on \(H_2(X_n;\mathbb Q)\) is \(+1\) for rotations and \(-1\) for reflections. The zeroth homology action is always trivial.

**Cellular Lefschetz formula.** For any coordinate permutation \(g\in D_n\), the alternating cellular chain trace \(L(g)=\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_0)-\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_1)+\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_2)\) equals \(1-\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H_1)+\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H_2)\). An invariant zero-cell is an endpoint assignment constant on each cycle of \(g\), giving \(\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_0)=2^{q(g)}\), where \(q(g)\) is the number of coordinate cycles. An invariant one-cell must have its active coordinate fixed by \(g\), with positive orientation contribution. An invariant two-cell must have its active adjacent pair fixed as a set, and contributes the sign of the induced permutation of its two active coordinates. For either dimension, invariant endpoint assignments are counted by two raised to the number of remaining coordinate cycles.

**Identity.** The cellular Euler characteristic is \(2^n-n2^{n-1}+n2^{n-2}=(4-n)2^{n-2}\). Using the orientation trace \(+1\) gives \(\operatorname{tr}(1\mid H_1)=2+(n-4)2^{n-2}\).

**Nonidentity rotations.** If \(g=r^k\), \(1\le k<n\), its permutation has exactly \(d=\gcd(n,k)\) cycles. It fixes no coordinate vertex and no polygon edge as a set for \(n\ge5\). Thus \(\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_0)=2^d\) and the degree-one and degree-two cellular traces are zero. Since rotations preserve orientation, the homology trace is \(2-2^d\).

**Reflections.** Every reflection of an odd \(n\)-gon fixes one vertex and reverses one opposite edge. If \(q=(n+1)/2\), its three cellular traces are \((2^q,2^{q-1},-2^{q-1})\), hence \(L(g)=0\). A reflection of an even polygon fixing two opposite vertices has \(q=(n+2)/2\) coordinate cycles and traces \((2^q,2^q,0)\). An even-polygon reflection reversing two opposite edges has \(q=n/2\) cycles and traces \((2^q,0,-2^q)\). Again \(L(g)=0\). Since reflections reverse orientation, \(\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H_1)=1-1-0=0\).

**Prime-polygon corollary.** If \(n=p\ge5\) is prime, each nonidentity rotation has \(d=1\), so its first-homology trace vanishes; the same holds for every reflection. Therefore its rational character is supported at the identity. Character orthogonality implies it is exactly the indicated positive integral multiple of the rational regular representation. This proves the asserted entire family; no extrapolation from finite examples is used.

## Verification
The attached standalone program `verify_polygon.py` independently generates every dihedral coordinate permutation for \(5\le n\le14\), computes the signed cellular Lefschetz traces by invariant face and endpoint-orbit counts, and checks all \(190\) resulting group-element cases against the closed character formula. For \(5\le n\le10\), it also independently checks zero boundary coefficients of the explicit fundamental two-chain at every one-cell, using all endpoint sign assignments. Its exact printed output is embedded as `verification_output_polygon.txt` and ends with `VERIFY_OK`. These finite tests support, but do not replace, the general proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Bahri–Bendersky–Cohen–Gitler (first publicly available in 2007) supply the broader polyhedral-product framework. Das computes polygonal moment-angle surface genera and the genus of the full cyclic quotient via branched covering and necklaces. Vidaurre analyzes the cohomology ring and combinatorial generators of these real moment-angle surfaces. The genus \(1+(n-4)2^{n-3}\), the additive Betti numbers, and the cyclic-quotient genus are known and are not claimed here. The new assertion is the explicit complete dihedral degree-one character for every polygon size, and the resulting full rational regular-module classification for prime polygons. Knowledge of the Betti numbers or the quotient genus averages does not determine the individual nontrivial rotational traces or reflection traces.

## Limitations
The theorem determines the complete rational representation character, not a classification of the integral \(\mathbb Z[D_n]\)-lattice, cup-product representation, or the quotient-space homotopy type. For \(n=5\), the rational regular-module case is already covered by a stronger integral calculation and is included only as the uniform formula's endpoint. No claim of independent audit or uniqueness against inaccessible literature is made.
