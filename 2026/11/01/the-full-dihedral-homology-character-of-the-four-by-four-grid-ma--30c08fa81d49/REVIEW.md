# Scientific review: the dihedral action on square-grid matching homology

## Correctness — PASS
The domain, orientation, and group generators are fixed explicitly. The complete 10,012-face enumeration agrees with an independent vertex-deletion recurrence. The augmented Morse matching has 5,002 legitimate pairs and exactly the required critical cells, and a full directed-Hasse-graph topological sort verifies acyclicity on all 47,968 incidences. Integral cochain and cycle elimination uses only unit pivots; every relevant cocycle condition, all cycle boundaries, and the identity pairings are checked. The eight group matrices satisfy the group relations. Two separate full-chain and homology trace computations agree for all eight symmetries, and exact character inner products give the stated irreducibles. The proof applies to the single stated finite grid, not an unproved infinite family.

## Originality — PASS, with residual uncertainty
The closest prior result computes precisely the same nonequivariant four-by-four matching-complex homotopy type. That result was read in full, is credited explicitly, and is *not* repeated as a novel theorem. Its statement and proof do not calculate the dihedral action on integral cycles or the rational homology character. Published work on width-three grid matching complexes and square-grid three-cut complexes studies mathematically different statements; a general outerplanar wedge theorem does not cover this nonouterplanar grid. Searches compared alternate group characters, square-grid formulations, and those stronger-looking claims. The 2026 three-row preprint's full text was inaccessible, and unindexed equivariant computations remain possible; they are originality risks, not evidence of coverage.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This determines the full dihedral representation in *each* of the two homology degrees of a natural symmetric graph complex already known to be a mixed-dimensional wedge. The character distinguishes axial and diagonal reflections, determines invariant subspaces in different degrees, and supplies the stronger integral top-degree lattice action. None of these can be inferred from previously known Betti numbers, so the finding is a motivated structural symmetry refinement rather than a routine recounting of spheres.

## Independent validation and scope
The only claimed structural novelty is the square-symmetry representation, not the prior homotopy classification. No general grid formula or equivariant homotopy equivalence follows.
