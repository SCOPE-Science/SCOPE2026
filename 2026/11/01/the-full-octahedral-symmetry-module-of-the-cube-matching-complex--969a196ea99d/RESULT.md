# The full octahedral symmetry module of the cube matching complex

## Finding
Let \(Q_3\) be the three-dimensional cube graph on \(\{0,1\}^3\), and let \(X=\mathcal M(Q_3)=\operatorname{Ind}(L(Q_3))\) be its ordinary matching complex. The full graph automorphism group \(G=\operatorname{Aut}(Q_3)\) is isomorphic to \(S_4\times C_2\), with \(S_4\) the orientation-preserving cube symmetries and \(C_2\) generated by central inversion \(x\mapsto (1,1,1)-x\). The complete rational reduced homology representation is
\[
\widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Q)\cong
\big(S^{(2,1,1)}\boxtimes\mathbf1\big)
\oplus\big(S^{(1,1,1,1)}\boxtimes\varepsilon\big).
\]
Here \(S^\lambda\) is the rational Specht representation of \(S_4\) labeled by the partition \(\lambda\), \(\mathbf1\) is the trivial representation of \(C_2\), and \(\varepsilon\) is its nontrivial sign. Every other reduced rational homology group vanishes. As integral groups, \(\widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^4\) and all other reduced integral homology groups vanish.

The character is specified without ambiguity by the following table. The label \(\mu\) is the cycle type of the orientation-preserving \(S_4\) factor acting on the four body diagonals. The two traces are for \((s,1)\) and \((s,a)\), where \(a\) is central inversion:

| \(S_4\) cycle type \(\mu\) | Number of elements of each central parity | \(\operatorname{tr}(s,1)\) | \(\operatorname{tr}(s,a)\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \((1,1,1,1)\) | 1 | 4 | 2 |
| \((2,1,1)\) | 6 | -2 | 0 |
| \((2,2)\) | 3 | 0 | -2 |
| \((3,1)\) | 8 | 1 | -1 |
| \((4)\) | 6 | 0 | 2 |

In particular, the \(C_2\)-invariant degree-two rational homology has dimension three, whereas the rotational and full octahedral invariants vanish. Therefore
\[
\widetilde H_*(|X|/C_2;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q^3[-2],\qquad
\widetilde H_*(|X|/S_4;\mathbb Q)=
\widetilde H_*(|X|/G;\mathbb Q)=0.
\]
The established nonequivariant homotopy equivalence \(X\simeq\bigvee^4S^2\) is prior work, **not** the new result. The new statement is the complete \(48\)-element symmetry character and its quotient consequences.

## Assumptions and scope
Cube vertices are the triples \((b_1,b_2,b_3)\in\{0,1\}^3\), with an edge between two triples iff they differ in exactly one coordinate. The twelve edges, in binary-vertex-number order, are \((0,1),(0,2),(0,4),(1,3),(1,5),(2,3),(2,6),(3,7),(4,5),(4,6),(5,7),(6,7)\). A simplex of \(X\) is an unordered matching, i.e. a set of mutually vertex-disjoint cube edges. Oriented simplicial chains order those edge indices increasingly; automorphisms can reverse this orientation, and their signs are essential to the character calculation.

Every cube automorphism has the form \(g_{\sigma,b}:x\mapsto (x_{\sigma(1)}+b_1,x_{\sigma(2)}+b_2,x_{\sigma(3)}+b_3)\) over \(\mathbb F_2\), for \(\sigma\in S_3\), \(b\in\mathbb F_2^3\). There are \(48\) of these; a graph automorphism is determined by the image of a cube vertex and the permutation of its three neighbors, so these exhaust \(\operatorname{Aut}(Q_3)\). Its geometric determinant is \(\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)(-1)^{b_1+b_2+b_3}\). The determinant-positive subgroup acts faithfully on the four antipodal vertex pairs as \(S_4\); the central inversion has determinant negative, commutes with every automorphism and yields the asserted direct product.

The quotient notation denotes the ordinary quotient of the geometric realization, not a homotopy quotient or Borel construction. Results about quotient rational homology follow by finite-group averaging; they do not assert an integral quotient theorem.

## Proof
**Integral homotopy input, checked from scratch.** Enumerate all nonempty matchings among the twelve cube edges; the exact face vector is \((12,42,44,9)\), for \(107\) faces total. Process the cube-edge indices \(0,1,\ldots,11\). At each pivot, pair an unmatched face \(\sigma\) not containing the pivot with \(\sigma\cup\{\mathrm{pivot}\}\) when both are unmatched matchings, then delete the pair from the unmatched set. This produces exactly \(51\) Hasse pairs. There are \(252\) original codimension-one Hasse relations. Orient each unmatched Hasse cover from coface to face and each matched cover from face to coface. A complete topological sort visits all \(107\) vertices of the directed Hasse graph, verifying acyclicity. The unmatched faces are precisely the vertex \(\{0\}\) and the four triangles \(\{2,5,11\},\{2,6,10\},\{3,6,8\},\{4,5,9\}\), with no critical 1-cells or 3-cells. Forman's theorem produces a CW model with one 0-cell and four 2-cells. The attaching maps go to the point, so \(X\simeq\bigvee^4S^2\), giving the stated **integral** homology concentration. This rederivation supports the equivariant calculation and is not claimed as new.

**The full character from signed fixed simplices.** For each \(g\in G\), its effect on a cube edge induces a permutation of the twelve simplex vertices. For each degree \(q=0,1,2,3\), the rational chain trace \(\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_q(X;\mathbb Q))\) is the sum over setwise-fixed \(q\)-simplices of the sign of the induced permutation of their \(q+1\) edges. The Hopf trace identity and the already established homology concentration give the exact formula
\[
\operatorname{tr}(g\mid\widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Q))
=\sum_{q=0}^3(-1)^q\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_q(X;\mathbb Q))-1.
\]
The verifier enumerates the full group \(G\) from all six coordinate permutations and eight coordinate flips and independently reconstructs the oriented fixed-simplex counts for every element. Grouping the computed traces by the \(S_4\) cycle structure of the induced action on the four body diagonals and by determinant gives exactly the five paired rows in the table, with the specified multiplicities; all \(48\) individual elements are checked.

**Representation identification.** In the same cycle-type order, the irreducible \(S_4\) character of \(S^{(2,1,1)}\) is \((3,-1,-1,0,1)\), and that of the sign representation \(S^{(1,1,1,1)}\) is \((1,-1,1,1,-1)\). Adding these gives the rotation traces \((4,-2,0,1,0)\); subtracting gives the traces after central inversion \((2,0,-2,-1,2)\). Since \(\mathbb Q[G]\) is semisimple and rational characters determine representations of this finite group, the stated direct-sum decomposition follows. In particular, the central inversion even and odd eigenspaces have dimensions three and one.

**Ordinary quotient homology.** For a finite group acting simplicially, the rational chains of the barycentric-subdivided orbit complex identify with invariant rational chains, because orientations can be chosen compatibly with the action on vertex chains. Exactness of averaging over a finite group gives \(H_*(|X|/H;\mathbb Q)\cong H_*(X;\mathbb Q)^H\) for every subgroup \(H\le G\). Neither irreducible summand in the displayed decomposition is trivial for rotational \(S_4\), so the rotation and full-group quotient invariants vanish; central inversion fixes the three-dimensional first summand and negates the one-dimensional second. This proves all quotient statements.

## Relationship to prior work
Braun and Hough (2016) provide foundational combinatorial and discrete-Morse methods for matching complexes and prove results for planar two-row grid families. Their graphs are not the three-dimensional cube graph and their treatment does not give the cube's full automorphism homology representation. An earlier exact calculation of \(\mathcal M(Q_3)\simeq\bigvee^4S^2\) already covers the nonequivariant homotopy type and Betti numbers; these are used here as background, not presented as new. A full octahedral representation calculation for the **octahedral graph's** matching complex concerns \(\mathcal M(K_{2,2,2})\), with twelve simplicial vertices but first homology dimension eleven; the graph and complex differ from \(\mathcal M(Q_3)\). The published **cuboctahedral matching complex** is \(\mathcal M(L(Q_3))\), not \(\operatorname{Ind}(L(Q_3))=\mathcal M(Q_3)\); it has third homology dimension 57. The cube's ordinary three-cut complex also carries a different automorphism representation in degrees three and four and is not an equivalent simplicial complex. These stronger-looking neighboring results do not imply the present complete degree-two character. An equivalent unpublished or unindexed derivation remains possible.

## Limitations
The result is for the ordinary edge matching complex of the three-dimensional cube only, with a rational representation statement and ordinary rational quotient homology. It makes no claims about higher-dimensional cubes, integral representation lattices, Borel-equivariant invariants, ordinary quotient homotopy types, or an explicit integral equivariant cellular basis. The scientific assessments have not been independently audited, and absence from inspected research records is not a general priority theorem.
