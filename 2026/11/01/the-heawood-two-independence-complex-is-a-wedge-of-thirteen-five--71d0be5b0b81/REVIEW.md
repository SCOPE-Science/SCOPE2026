# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The vertex set, seven Fano lines, face predicate, and scope are completely specified. A standalone exhaustive checker enumerates the 16,384 vertex masks, obtains 2,256 nonempty simplices, verifies that 1,121 codimension-one pairs form a matching, and independently checks that the entire reversed Hasse diagram is acyclic. Its unpaired cells are exactly one vertex and thirteen dimension-five faces. Forman's finite discrete Morse theorem therefore gives a CW complex with only these cells, hence \(\bigvee^{13}S^5\) integrally, with no torsion ambiguity. An independent mod-two boundary reduction agrees. Risk: a reproducibility check relies on executing the included finite algorithm, rather than a short hand-enumerated list of each Hasse edge; the code is complete and all checks are deterministic.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge assessment)
The original higher-independence definition is known from the Salvetti and Paolini–Salvetti literature, and general homotopy classifications for several structured graph families are known from Deshpande–Singh. The full HTML of their article was inspected: its families are complete multipartite, fully whiskered, cycles and perfect rooted trees; none contains the Heawood incidence graph as a stated case. The original 2017 Paolini–Salvetti text studies path graph cases. Indexed-result searches and accessible prior claim comparisons included the ordinary Heawood independence complex, Heawood edge-matching variants, and the Petersen two-independence complex. Those are different face predicates or different graphs, and their statements do not imply the present finite classification. The 2016 foundational chapter was abstract-accessible only (full-text access unavailable), and equivalent unindexed research cannot be excluded. This is not a claim to independent priority certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The Heawood graph is the smallest cubic incidence graph of a finite projective plane and is a standard nonchordal test object in topological graph theory. Parameter two is the first nontrivial higher-independence extension of ordinary independent vertex subsets. The exact integral wedge decomposition and its reproducible acyclic matching give a concrete higher-independence benchmark outside currently inspected closed-form graph families; the degree-five homotopy and rank thirteen are not recoverable from the ordinary independence or matching-complex calculations. It is a natural complete classification, not an arbitrary finite subslice. It does not purport to be an all-projective-planes theorem.
