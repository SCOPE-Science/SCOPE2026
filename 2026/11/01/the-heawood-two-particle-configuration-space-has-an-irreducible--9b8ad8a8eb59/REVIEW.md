# Scientific review: Heawood two-particle rational homology modules

## Correctness — PASS
The complex being computed is the collision-free **unordered** two-point configuration space of the geometric Heawood graph, modeled by Abrams's two-particle disjoint-cell cube complex. The 91/252/168 cell enumeration is exhaustive. Its signed integral boundary operators satisfy the chain condition, with exact characteristic-three ranks 90 and 154. The lower rank bound is matched by a geometrically independent family of fourteen disjoint-hexagon torus cycles among the 42 products, providing an upper bound 154 over the rationals. This proves rational Betti ranks 1, 8, and 14 without extrapolation or relying solely on a failure to find more cycles. All 336 automorphisms are reconstructed from the Fano collineation group and a polarity, with their oriented cell Lefschetz traces evaluated individually. The first homology maps isomorphically to the graph cycle module because the 28 six-cycles span the eight-dimensional graph cycle space; class-function orthogonality proves the 8-plus-6 irreducible decomposition. The standalone executable and an actual successful execution are included. Independent expert verification has not occurred.

## Originality — PASS, with explicit limitations
Abrams (2000) supplies the configuration-space model, while Ko–Park (2011/2012) already determines first-homology ranks and torsion from graph invariants. Farber–Hanbury (2010) treats ordered deleted products and intersection/linking forms. An–Knudsen (2020) proves toric/theta generation for planar graph braid spaces and identifies the nonplanar case as different; the Heawood graph is nonplanar. Earlier indexed calculations for two **ordered** Heawood particles concern mod-two Betti numbers, not the rational full-automorphism representation of this **unordered** space. Previous Heawood matching-complex results use a different object. Targeted research-record and primary literature searches did not identify the same complete character or the specific 42-torus spanning statement. The remaining risk is an unindexed or differently phrased equivalent representation or finite computation; the literature search does not establish global uniqueness.

## Value — PASS
The Heawood graph is the canonical Fano-plane incidence graph and a highly symmetric nonplanar test object. Its entire rational second homology has an unexpectedly economical geometric generating system: disjoint six-cycle product tori span all fourteen dimensions despite the absence of a general planar toric-generation theorem. The full symmetry module splits as the geometric eight-dimensional cycle representation plus a distinct irreducible six-dimensional constituent; those symmetries are invisible in rank-only calculations. Both consequences are structural and usable in comparing graph-braid representations and toric generator phenomena beyond planar graphs. The result makes no assertion of an infinite-family theorem or an integral torus basis.
