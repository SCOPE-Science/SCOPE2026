# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The graph is defined by six explicit edge families. The stated permutation \(\phi\) carries all thirty distance-two edges onto the ordinary icosahedral edges; every antipodal edge is in no triangle. The twenty distance-two triangles form an icosahedral two-sphere with twelve five-cycle vertex links, hence six remaining one-cells attach to a simply connected sphere. The relative integral long exact sequence is equivariant and identifies the exact signed six-axis lattice; unique homotopy classes of paths in the sphere give the signed outer action on the free fundamental group. A fully enumerated 120-element automorphism group confirms both top degree and full signed trace values. Finite-field ranks are checks, not the proof of integral torsion-freeness. The rational representation is carefully described as one six-dimensional rational irreducible, not falsely as a rational direct sum of conjugate three-dimensional modules.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Barmak's 2010 full article treats general independence complexes but nowhere identifies the icosahedral complex or its action. MathWorld documents the already-known distance-two self-isomorphism of the icosahedral graph; the new claim cannot claim that input as novel. The Bristol character table records the irreducibles but not their occurrence in this topological complex. Nearby published computations concern the same graph's neighborhood complex, which is mathematically different. Specific searches for the six-chord sphere decomposition, the free-group outer action, and homology characters found no stronger coverage. This is not a proof of priority. The main risk is an older graph-topology calculation not indexed under the searched vocabulary, or a previously observed immediate consequence of the distance-two graph fact.

## Value — PASS
The ordinary independence complex of the icosahedron is a natural Platonic-graph topological object. Its exact equivariant homotopy model determines not merely ranks, but the full action on its nonabelian fundamental group, its integral homology lattice, its unusual rationally irreducible six-dimensional symmetry module, and the contrast between rotational and full-symmetry quotient homology. The signed-axis identification makes the result reusable for equivariant invariants, rather than an arbitrary finite table entry. Its simplicity is a strength of the geometric explanation, not a claim of a general theory.

## Limitations
The homotopy equivalence is nonequivariant; the group action is described canonically on \(\pi_1\) up to inner automorphism and on integral homology, not by an asserted equivariant wedge decomposition. Additional unindexed literature could cover the exact result. Full integral quotient homology is not asserted.
