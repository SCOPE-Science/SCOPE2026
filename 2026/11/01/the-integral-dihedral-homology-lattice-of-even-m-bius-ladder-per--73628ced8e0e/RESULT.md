# The integral dihedral homology lattice of even Möbius-ladder perfect-matching complexes

## Finding
For every even integer \(n=2k\ge4\), form the Möbius ladder \(M_{2n}\) on the vertices \(\mathbb Z/(2n)\), with consecutive perimeter edges and opposite-vertex spokes. Let \(X_n=\mathcal M_p(M_{2n})\) be its **perfect-matching complex**: graph edges are simplicial vertices, and the faces are exactly those edge sets that extend to a perfect matching. Let the natural dihedral symmetries be \(r(v)=v+1\) and \(s(v)=-v\). There is a canonical integral basis \((c_0,c_1)\) of the only nonzero reduced integral homology group, in degree \(k-1\), such that
\[
\widetilde H_{k-1}(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2,\qquad
[r_*]_{(c_0,c_1)}=\begin{pmatrix}0&(-1)^{k-1}\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
[s_*]_{(c_0,c_1)}=(-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
All other reduced integral homology groups vanish, and \(X_n\simeq S^{k-1}\vee S^{k-1}\) nonequivariantly. The matrices describe the **actual integral lattice**, not merely its rational character. When \(k\) is odd (equivalently \(n\equiv2\pmod4\)), the involution \(r_*\) has integral \(\pm1\)-eigensublattices \(L^\pm\), with
\[
L/(L^+\oplus L^-)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,\qquad
[L:L^+\oplus L^-]=2.
\]
When \(k\) is even, \(r_*^2=-I\), and the integral rotation lattice is free of rank one over \(\mathbb Z[i]\).

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite and simple. The even integer \(n\ge4\) is the number of antipodal spokes, so \(M_{2n}\) has \(2n\) graph vertices, \(2n\) perimeter edges, and \(n\) spokes. Set \(k=n/2\). The dihedral action here is generated by the specific graph automorphisms \(r\) and \(s\), not an assertion about any accidental additional symmetries for small graphs. Homology is reduced simplicial homology with integer coefficients. The displayed homotopy equivalence need not be dihedrally equivariant; the integral homology representation is equivariant.

For each \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\), let the spoke \(u_i=\{i,i+n\}\), and define the **rim pair**
\[
H_i=\big\{\{i,i+1\},\{i+n,i+n+1\}\big\},
\]
with graph-vertex subscripts reduced modulo \(2n\). The two elements of \(H_i\) are distinct and disjoint graph edges. A tile set \(I\subseteq\mathbb Z/n\) is **independent** if it contains no two cyclically consecutive indices. Write \(I+1=\{i+1:i\in I\}\). The two alternating tile sets are \(E_0=\{0,2,\ldots,n-2\}\) and \(E_1=\{1,3,\ldots,n-1\}\).

## Proof
**1. Exact perfect-matching parametrization.** Every perfect matching of \(M_{2n}\) is uniquely
\[
F(I)=\bigcup_{i\in I}H_i\ \cup\ \{u_j:j\notin I\cup(I+1)\},
\]
where \(I\) is an independent set of indices in the cycle \(C_n\). Conversely, every such \(F(I)\) is a perfect matching because the selected disjoint rim pairs cover precisely the columns in \(I\cup(I+1)\), and the remaining columns are covered by spokes. For uniqueness in the other direction, first suppose a perfect matching uses a spoke. That spoke cuts the perimeter into paired arcs with corresponding columns; after removing the chosen spoke columns, each remaining path segment must be matched by consecutive perimeter edges and has a unique perfect matching whenever its vertex count is even. Such perimeter edges necessarily occur in complete opposite rim pairs \(H_i\), and the chosen pairs are disjoint in columns. If no spoke is used, the matching must be one of the two alternating perfect matchings of the perimeter cycle \(C_{2n}\). Since \(n\) is even, these are exactly \(F(E_0)\) and \(F(E_1)\), respectively. No other perfect matchings exist.

**2. Equivariant paired-rim reduction.** Define a simplicial complex \(Y_n\) with vertices \(t_i\) and \(u_i\), \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\), whose facets are
\[
\widehat F(I)=\{t_i:i\in I\}\cup\{u_j:j\notin I\cup(I+1)\}
\]
for the independent tile sets \(I\). Define \(\phi:X_n\to Y_n\) by mapping both rim edges in \(H_i\) to \(t_i\) and each spoke to \(u_i\). The parametrization shows that \(\phi\) is surjective on faces and simplicial. Choose one rim edge from each \(H_i\) to define a simplicial section \(\psi:Y_n\to X_n\). Then \(\phi\psi=\operatorname{id}\), while \(\psi\phi\) and \(\operatorname{id}_{X_n}\) are contiguous: for any face of \(X_n\), adding its omitted opposite rim-edge partners still lies in the same perfect-matching facet. Therefore \(\phi\) is a homotopy equivalence. Crucially, \(\phi\) is *strictly equivariant* under \(r\) and \(s\), since they map whole rim pairs to whole rim pairs. Thus \(\phi_*\) identifies the **integral** homology representations.

**3. Contractible core with precisely two exceptional facets.** Let \(B_n\subset Y_n\) be obtained by deleting the two full simplices on the tile-only facets \(E_0,E_1\), retaining all their proper faces. The two facets have dimension \(k-1\); these are the only independent tile sets covering all \(n\) columns because any tile covers two columns and a covering by disjoint tiles must consist of exactly \(k\) alternating indices. Every other face extends by some spoke. Consequently \(B_n\) is covered by the closed simplicial stars \(\operatorname{St}_{B_n}(u_j)\) over all spokes \(u_j\). Each such star is a cone. Every intersection of a nonempty collection of these stars is also a cone (a face compatible with each chosen spoke is compatible with all chosen spokes, which are mutually compatible); all such intersections are nonempty because they contain the full spoke simplex. Their nerve is a full simplex, so the nerve theorem proves that \(B_n\) is contractible.

Since both exceptional tile simplices attach to \(B_n\) along their entire boundaries and have disjoint relative interiors,
\[
Y_n/B_n\cong S^{k-1}\vee S^{k-1}.
\]
The quotient map is a homotopy equivalence because \(B_n\) is a contractible CW subcomplex. Moreover, the long exact sequence of the pair identifies the absolute integral homology with \(H_{k-1}(Y_n,B_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2\), freely generated by the **relative oriented cells** \([E_0]\) and \([E_1]\). Their unique inverse images in absolute homology define the claimed integral basis \((c_0,c_1)\). This proves the homotopy and all-field homology classification without inferring an infinite statement from finite data.

**4. Exact integral symmetry signs.** Index each \(E_j\) in increasing order. The symmetry \(r\) sends \(t_i\mapsto t_{i+1}\); it carries the ordered \(E_0\) to ordered \(E_1\) without a sign, and the ordered \(E_1\) to the cyclically shifted ordered \(E_0\), with orientation sign \((-1)^{k-1}\). The symmetry \(s\) sends \(t_i\mapsto t_{-i-1}\). It interchanges \(E_0,E_1\) and reverses the order of the \(k\) tiles in either simplex, yielding the sign \((-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\). These are precisely the two matrices stated above. The contractible core and the pair long exact sequence are dihedrally natural, so the action on relative generators is the actual action on absolute homology. As a consistency check, \(s_*r_*s_*=r_*^{-1}\) and \(r_*^{2n}=I\).

**5. Integral obstruction.** When \(k\) is odd, \(r_*\) simply swaps \(c_0,c_1\). Its invariant and anti-invariant saturated rank-one sublattices are generated by \(c_0+c_1\) and \(c_0-c_1\); the determinant of their coordinate columns is \(-2\). Thus the quotient and index formula follow. The corresponding rational eigenspace decomposition cannot detect this index. For even \(k\), the matrix satisfies \(r_*^2=-I\) and gives the standard rank-one \(\mathbb Z[i]\)-lattice.

## Verification
The standalone Python 3 program `verify_pm_dihedral.py` enumerates **all** perfect matchings of the original Möbius ladder directly from its graph adjacency, independently enumerates all independent tile sets, and checks exact equality of the two facet families for \(n=4,6,8,10,12,14\), respectively \(7,18,47,123,322,843\) facets. It checks that the stated rotation/reflection preserve the paired-rim model, verifies the oriented permutations of both exceptional facets, and checks for every nonexceptional independent tile set that a spoke remains admissible. It also independently computes the complete reduced mod-two homology of the original complexes for \(n=4,6,8,10\), finding rank two in degree \(n/2-1\) and zero in all other degrees. Its successful output is `VERIFY_OK`. These finite computations only corroborate the all-parameter arguments in the proof.

Reproduce with `python3 verify_pm_dihedral.py` using Python 3's standard library. The proof does not rely on any external numerical package or an inaccessible computation certificate. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.

## Relationship to prior work
Agarwal and Basak's August 31, 2026 preprint (expanded September 14, 2026) already determines the **nonequivariant** wedge of two spheres for even Möbius-ladder perfect-matching complexes. That statement is not new here. A published rotation calculation determines the rational trace of \(r_*\) and \(r_*^2\), hence its rational order two or four, but explicitly leaves the integral representation unclassified. Another published calculation establishes that the central half-turn acts trivially for even widths, which also cannot distinguish the split integer involution lattice from the nonsplit swap lattice. The established full rational dihedral calculation for **odd** widths does not cover the even case. The present result gives an explicit **integral** basis and both dihedral generators, proving the index-two obstruction for every width \(n\equiv2\pmod4\). The earlier rational eigenvalue data alone does not imply this lattice or the full reflection matrix.

## Limitations
The theorem covers even \(n\ge4\) and the dihedral subgroup generated by the specified vertex rotation and reflection, not odd widths or accidental extra graph automorphisms. It proves a nonequivariant wedge decomposition and an exact equivariant homology lattice, **not** a dihedrally equivariant wedge decomposition. It does not classify all group actions on the underlying CW complex or establish absolute priority against unindexed literature.
