# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim concerns the full ordinary matching complex of the explicitly defined three-by-eight Cartesian grid. Exhaustive face enumeration agrees with an independent column-transfer calculation. A complete 611,209-pair face-poset matching is checked against all 8,827,379 nonempty codimension-one incidences; the resulting directed Hasse graph is acyclic. Signed integral gradient-path reduction yields a 112-by-18 Morse differential with eighteen unimodular pivots. These premises justify cancellation to the wedge of 94 seven-spheres. The finite verification is exhaustive for this finite object, not an extrapolation to larger grids.

## Originality — PASS, subject to stated source-access risk
The direct comparison is Chauhan–Sharma–Shukla, arXiv:2609.27366 (first public 2026-09-23). Its accessible abstract establishes general connectivity and selected homological vanishing, neither of which implies a full integral homotopy equivalence at width eight. The older Goyal–Shukla–Singh claimed all-width wedge theorem (DOI:10.37236/10496) has been explicitly retracted, as stated in its published corrigendum. Searches under ordinary grid matching, three-row ladder, eight-column width, and equivalent graph formulations found no independently valid covering claim. The 2026 primary full text was inaccessible despite open-access and authorized institutional retrieval attempts: a previously uninspected section might contain an overlapping width-eight classification. Novelty is assessed on the available mathematical evidence, not merely on unmatched wording.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A recently studied, specifically motivated three-row family has an open homotopy-classification problem after retraction of an earlier purported general solution. This exact width-eight calculation establishes the complete integral homotopy type rather than a Betti-number experiment, and supplies a full acyclic-Morse and primitive-attaching-map witness. It is a natural finite test case near the range of recent homology calculations, without claiming to resolve the all-width conjecture. The stand-alone structural certificate may be useful for testing future recurrence and homotopy constructions.
