# The negative-adjacency orientation character of forest dominance complexes

## Finding
Let \(F\) be any finite simple forest, possibly disconnected or with isolated vertices, whose matching number \(m\) is at least one. Let \(D(F)\) be its **dominance complex**: a set \(\sigma\subseteq V(F)\) is a simplex exactly when its complement dominates \(F\). Let \(A_F\) be the real symmetric adjacency matrix, and define \(E_-(F)\) to be the direct sum of the strictly negative eigenspaces of \(A_F\). Every graph automorphism \(g\) commutes with \(A_F\), hence preserves \(E_-(F)\).

**Theorem (integral equivariant orientation).** The vector space \(E_-(F)\) has dimension \(m\). The previously known homotopy equivalence \(D(F)\simeq S^{m-1}\) implies that \(\widetilde H_{m-1}(D(F);\mathbb Z)\) is free of rank one. Its complete integral automorphism character is
\[
\boxed{\quad \chi_F(g)=g_*\bigm|_{\widetilde H_{m-1}(D(F);\mathbb Z)}=\det\bigl(g\bigm|_{E_-(F)}\bigr)\in\{+1,-1\}.\quad}
\]
This gives an explicit representation-theoretic bridge between a topological orientation line and an adjacency-matrix spectral orientation line. All other reduced homology groups vanish by the cited nonequivariant theorem.

There is also an exact, wholly integral combinatorial algorithm. Fix **any** maximum matching \(M=(e_1,\ldots,e_m)\), orient each edge as \(e_i=(u_i,v_i)\), and fix **any** minimum vertex cover \(C\). Every minimum cover selects exactly one endpoint \(c_i(C)\) of each \(e_i\). Define \(s_M(C)\in\{\pm1\}\) to be the sign of the permutation sorting \((c_1(C),\ldots,c_m(C))\) into increasing vertex order, multiplied by \((-1)\) for each selected \(v_i\). For an automorphism \(g\), define \(s_g(C)\) as the sorting sign of \((g(c):c\in C\text{ in increasing order})\). Then
\[
\boxed{\chi_F(g)=s_g(C)\,s_M(C)\,s_M(gC).}
\]
The expression is independent of both choices. An omitted extra ordering sign would generally make a formula based only on \(|C\setminus gC|\) incorrect; the matching-orientation coefficients are essential.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, undirected, without loops or repeated edges. Isolated vertices are allowed; they are absent from every nonempty face of the dominance complex. All chain groups are simplicial, with oriented vertices in a fixed total order and integral coefficients. \(m\ge1\) avoids the conventional augmented-homology ambiguity of the empty graph. The action on real eigenspaces is obtained by permuting coordinate axes according to the vertex automorphism. The topological conclusion concerns ordinary geometric realization; it makes no claim about a canonical equivariant homotopy equivalence to a sphere.

## Proof
**1. Spectral dimension.** Give each bipartite component a bipartition \(L\sqcup R\). In that order the adjacency matrix has block form \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&B\\B^\mathsf T&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). Every nonzero minor of \(B\) of order \(r\) requires an \(r\)-edge matching. Conversely, any maximum matching induces a square submatrix of order \(m\) with determinant \(\pm1\): its induced bipartite subgraph is a forest and has exactly one perfect matching, because two distinct perfect matchings would yield an alternating cycle. Thus \(\operatorname{rank}B=m\), and the spectrum has exactly \(m\) negative and \(m\) positive eigenvalues, counted with multiplicities. This also works componentwise, including isolated vertices.

**2. Primitive homology orientations from minimum covers.** The forest is bipartite. König's theorem yields minimum vertex covers of cardinality \(m\). Fix a maximum matching \(M\). A cover of \(m\) vertices must select exactly one endpoint from every matched edge and none of the unmatched vertices. The simplicial join of the \(m\) endpoint pairs, with one vertex selected from each, is the boundary of an \(m\)-dimensional crosspolytope and embeds in \(D(F)\): every forbidden closed neighborhood contains either a matched pair, which no face selects in full, or an unmatched vertex, which no face selects at all. Its oriented fundamental cycle is \(z_M=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(u_i-v_i)\), with product meaning the oriented simplicial join of zero-cycles.

Every minimum cover \(C\) is a facet of \(D(F)\). Indeed, its complement dominates: each member of an inclusion-minimal vertex cover has an edge to a vertex outside the cover, while all outside vertices dominate themselves. But adjoining any vertex \(w\) outside \(C\) creates a nonface, since \(N[w]\subseteq C\cup\{w\}\) (the outside is independent). Therefore coefficient extraction at the oriented facet \([C]\) descends to a homomorphism \(\phi_C:\widetilde H_{m-1}(D(F);\mathbb Z)\to\mathbb Z\). It takes \([z_M]\) to the sign \(s_M(C)\), so \([z_M]\) is a primitive generator by the known rank-one homology theorem. Naturality under \(g\) gives \(\chi_F(g)=s_g(C)s_M(C)s_M(gC)\).

**3. Determinantal orientations.** For any minimum cover \(C\), coordinate restriction \(p_C:E_-(F)\to\mathbb R^C\) is an isomorphism. If a vector \(x\in E_-(F)\) vanishes on \(C\), its support is in the independent set \(V\setminus C\), giving \(x^\mathsf T A_F x=0\). Since the quadratic form is negative definite on \(E_-(F)\), this implies \(x=0\). Both spaces have dimension \(m\), establishing the isomorphism. For a fixed ordered basis of \(E_-(F)\), write \(d_C\) for the nonzero determinant of its coordinate-restriction matrix with rows in increasing cover order.

**4. Local comparison of the two orientations.** Suppose minimum covers \(C,C'\) differ by one replacement \(C'=(C\setminus\{a\})\cup\{b\}\). The two differing vertices must be the endpoints of one edge of the fixed matching \(M\). Choose the unique \(x\in E_-(F)\) with coordinate \(x_a=1\) and all coordinates on \(C\setminus\{a\}\) zero. Every neighbor of \(a\) other than \(b\) lies in \(C\setminus\{a\}\), since \(C'\) still covers all edges. The complement of \(C\) is independent, hence
\[
x^\mathsf T A_Fx=2x_b<0.\]
Thus \(x_b<0\). Replacing the row for \(a\) by the row for \(b\) in the coordinate determinant changes its sign; reordering the rows to the sorted cover order contributes the corresponding sorting-permutation sign. Exactly the same pair of signs changes the coefficient \(s_M(C)\) to \(s_M(C')\), because the two covers exchange one endpoint in the crosspolytope generator. Consequently \(\operatorname{sgn}(d_C)/s_M(C)\) is unchanged by a single permitted cover swap.

**5. Connectivity of the minimum-cover flip graph.** Fix a bipartition and maximum matching, and index its edges. Write \(t_i=0\) when the left endpoint of matching edge \(i\) lies in the cover and \(t_i=1\) when its right endpoint does. Edges between matched vertices impose implications \(t_i\le t_j\), according to their endpoint orientation. Edges incident with unmatched vertices impose fixed values on some coordinates; two unmatched vertices cannot be adjacent. Contracting the matching edges in a forest yields a forest, so the implications have no directed cycles. The admissible assignments form an order-ideal (equivalently, order-filter) lattice subject to forced coordinates. Any pair of assignments can be connected through their coordinatewise meet by changing one unforced coordinate at a time while retaining the implications. Thus all minimum covers are joined by single endpoint swaps.

The sign ratio \(\operatorname{sgn}(d_C)/s_M(C)\) is therefore constant for all minimum covers. Under a graph automorphism, the sorted coordinate orientations of \(E_-(F)\) and the oriented facet coefficient functionals on homology acquire the same permutation sign. Their common cover-to-cover transition rule forces the two one-dimensional characters to agree. This proves the theorem for every finite forest, not merely the examples below.

## Verification
The included `verify.py` independently constructs minimum covers, checks connectivity of their single-swap graph, computes the exact integral matching-coefficient character on **every** minimum cover in each tested case, computes the reduced simplicial Lefschetz number directly from invariant faces, and compares the character to the determinant of the negative adjacency eigenspace using numerical symmetric diagonalization. It checks 344 automorphism actions on 96 forests, including nonisomorphic trees through nine vertices, disjoint edges and an isolated-vertex case. The finite checks reproduce `verification_output.txt`. Numerical eigenvalue computations are verification examples only; the proof of the all-forest theorem uses exact arguments, not floating-point certification.

## Relationship to prior work
Ehrenborg and Hetyei established that the dominance complex of a forest has a sphere homotopy type. Marietti and Testa, *Cores of simplicial complexes*, arXiv:math/0703351, Theorem 8.1, identified the dimension from the matching number and proved an explicit crosspolytope collapse; Corollary 8.3 gives an integral generator associated to a maximal resolution. Neither assertion of nonequivariant homotopy or of the existence of a cycle generator is new here. Their related *A simple uniform approach to complexes arising from forests*, arXiv:0705.1461, treats these nonequivariant complexes. Matsushita and Wakatsuki, arXiv:2312.02639, study dominance, neighborhood and Alexander dual complexes and give more homology computations. The present theorem distinguishes the full automorphism sign character for every forest and identifies it canonically with a negative spectral determinant; these assertions are not implied by knowing only sphere dimension or an individually chosen generator.

## Limitations
The result assumes acyclicity of the graph. It does not establish an analogous formula for graphs with cycles, equivariant homotopy sphere models, integral higher representations (there is only a rank-one reduced group in this family), or a canonical isomorphism of **lattices** between top homology and an integral version of the negative real eigenspace. A complete external publication-priority assessment is not claimed; an equivalent unindexed character theorem remains possible.
