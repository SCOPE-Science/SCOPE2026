# The nonsplit integral rotation lattice of the twelve-vertex Möbius-ladder independence complex

## Finding
Let \(M_{12}\) have vertex set \(\mathbb Z/12\mathbb Z\), cycle edges \(\{i,i+1\}\), and antipodal edges \(\{i,i+6\}\). Put \(X=\operatorname{Ind}(M_{12})\), and let \(\rho(i)=i+1\) act simplicially on \(X\). The free integral group \(L=\widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\) has rank three; its rotation action factors through \(C_4\). There exists an **integral** basis in which
\[
[\rho]_L=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1&1\\1&0&0\\0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its canonical saturated rotation-invariant plane \(W=\ker_L(\rho^2+1)\) is isomorphic to \(\mathbb Z[i]\) with \(\rho\) acting by multiplication by \(i\), and the quotient \(L/W\) is the sign lattice \(\mathbb Z_-\) on which \(\rho=-1\). The extension
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z[i]\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_-\longrightarrow0
\]
is the unique **nonzero**, order-two element of \(\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathbb Z[C_4]}(\mathbb Z_-,\mathbb Z[i])\cong\mathbb Z/2\). Thus the rational decomposition \(L\otimes\mathbb Q\cong\mathbb Q(i)\oplus\mathbb Q_-\) does not lift to an integral direct sum. In particular, the integral rotation coinvariants satisfy
\[
L/(\rho-1)L\cong\mathbb Z/4,
\]
whereas the split lattice would have coinvariants \((\mathbb Z/2)^2\).

## Assumptions and scope
All complexes are finite abstract simplicial complexes, realized geometrically when using homotopy equivalence. The graph is the simple, undirected, twelve-vertex Möbius ladder described above. All integral homology groups use ordinary simplicial orientation conventions and reduced homology. The representation is for the cyclic subgroup generated by the one-step graph rotation; no complete integral dihedral-module classification is asserted. The result concerns precisely this graph, the smallest nondegenerate positive-dimensional rank-three case in the periodic Möbius-ladder family; no assertion is extrapolated to longer ladders.

## Proof
**1. Integral rank and rational constituents.** Agarwal and Basak's independence-complex theorem gives \(X\simeq S^2\vee S^2\vee S^2\); in particular \(L\cong\mathbb Z^3\) with no torsion. To recover the rational rotation character directly, exhaustively enumerate the independent simplices and calculate the signed fixed-face Lefschetz number of \(\rho^j\): a fixed simplex on \(s\) vertices contributes the sign of the permutation of those vertices times \((-1)^{s-1}\), and the augmented empty face contributes \(-1\). The reproducible, finite enumeration in `verify_mobius12.py` obtains trace \(3\) for \(4\mid j\) and \(-1\) otherwise, for all \(0\le j<12\). Since only degree-two reduced rational homology survives, these are the traces of \(\rho^j\) on \(L\otimes\mathbb Q\). Hence the three complex eigenvalues of \(\rho\) are \(-1,i,-i\), so \(\rho^4=1\) rationally and, because \(L\) is torsion-free, integrally. The rational module is \(\mathbb Q_-\oplus\mathbb Q(i)\), agreeing with the existing rational character calculation.

**2. Three geometric mod-two generators.** Let \(O_A,O_B,O_C\) be the induced independence subcomplexes on the respective vertex sets
\[
A=\{0,1,4,5,8,9\},\quad B=\{0,2,4,6,8,10\},\quad C=\{0,3,4,7,8,11\}.
\]
In each induced graph, the six vertices form exactly three disjoint edges. The corresponding independence subcomplex is the join of three copies of \(S^0\), an octahedral two-sphere. Write \(a,b,c\) for their mod-two fundamental two-cycles, each the sum of eight triangles. Exhaustive finite chain enumeration gives the simplex counts in dimensions zero through four as \((12,48,76,48,12)\), and the ranks of the simplicial boundaries \(\partial_1,\partial_2,\partial_3,\partial_4\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) as \((11,37,36,12)\). In particular, \(\dim H_2(X;\mathbb F_2)=76-37-36=3\). Appending \(a,b,c\) to the 36-dimensional image of \(\partial_3\) increases its rank to 39, proving these three classes form an \(\mathbb F_2\)-basis. Rotation of these explicit eight-triangle cycles, followed by exact reduction modulo simplicial three-boundaries, gives
\[
\rho_*a=a+b,\qquad\rho_*b=a+c,\qquad\rho_*c=a.
\]
Thus in the basis \((a,b,c)\) its complete mod-two homology matrix is
\[
A_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\operatorname{rank}(A_2-I)=2,\quad
\operatorname{rank}(A_2-I)^2=1,\quad
(A_2-I)^3=0.
\]
This verifies a single length-three unipotent Jordan block, not merely the rational character reduced modulo two. All ranks, supports, quotient coordinates, and identities are checked by the included standalone exact program. The finite calculation is exhaustive for the stated twelve-vertex graph, rather than a sample used to infer an infinite statement.

**3. Nonsplitting.** The rational two-dimensional constituent has integral characteristic polynomial \(t^2+1\); any invariant rank-two lattice inside it therefore has mod-two characteristic polynomial \((t+1)^2\). By Cayley–Hamilton, \((\rho-I)^2=0\) modulo two on that plane. The integral rank-one sign constituent also has \(\rho\equiv I\) modulo two. An integral decomposition of \(L\) into these two constituent lattices would therefore imply \((\rho-I)^2=0\) on \(L/2L\), contradicting the rank-one square found in step 2.

**4. Classification of the extension.** The integer kernel \(W=\ker_L(\rho^2+1)\) is saturated because the quotient embeds into the torsion-free image of the integer endomorphism \(\rho^2+1\). Its rationalization is the two-dimensional \(\mathbb Q(i)\)-constituent, and \(W\) is a rank-one torsion-free module over the principal ideal domain \(\mathbb Z[i]\). Hence \(W\cong\mathbb Z[i]\), with integral basis \((u,\rho u)\). The quotient is a free rank-one lattice on which \(\rho=-1\). Choosing a lift \(e\) of its positive generator gives \(\rho e=-e+w\), with \(w\in W\). Replacing \(e\) by \(e+v\) replaces \(w\) by \(w+(\rho+1)v\). Conversely, every \(w\in W\) defines an extension compatible with \(\rho^4=1\), because \(\rho^2=-1\) on \(W\). Consequently the extension classes form
\[
W/(\rho+1)W\cong\mathbb Z[i]/(1+i)\cong\mathbb Z/2.
\]
Step 3 excludes the zero class. Using a lift adjustment, choose \(w=u\), the nonzero class, producing the claimed integral matrix. Finally, the integer Smith normal form of \([\rho]_L-I\) is \(\operatorname{diag}(1,1,4)\), establishing the coinvariant assertion and completing the integral classification.

## Verification
The self-contained `verify_mobius12.py` uses Python's standard library only. Run `python3 verify_mobius12.py` from the extracted package. It exhaustively examines all \(2^{12}\) vertex subsets; constructs all independent simplices; forms the four boundary matrices by oriented-face deletion modulo two; computes their exact binary ranks by integer-bitset Gaussian elimination; checks that the three displayed six-vertex supports induce disjoint triples of edges; verifies their eight-triangle cycles; checks their images form a full homology basis; computes rotation on that quotient; verifies its powers and nilpotent ranks; and independently computes characteristic-zero signed Lefschetz traces. The observed result is `VERIFY_OK`, followed by face vector \((12,48,76,48,12)\), boundary ranks \((11,37,36,12)\), rotation columns \((3,5,1)\), nilpotent ranks \((2,1,0)\), and rotation Lefschetz traces \((3,-1,-1,-1)\) repeating with period four. These computations are complete finite checks for the specified graph; the module-theoretic classification is justified by steps 3 and 4, not by numerical extrapolation.

## Relationship to prior work
Agarwal and Basak, arXiv:2608.30601 (first posted August 31, 2026), establish the nonequivariant wedge-of-three-two-spheres homotopy type in this case. Their discussion of Möbius-ladder independence complexes does not classify integral rotation modules. The published result *Dihedral rational homology representations of Möbius-ladder independence complexes* (record `2026/10/11/SCOPE-dihedral-rational-homology-representations-of-m-bius-ladder-inde--baaa00f0cf91`) identifies the rational splitting \(\mathbb Q(i)\oplus\mathbb Q_-\) throughout the periodic family and explicitly leaves integral module splitting unresolved. Neither statement determines the nonzero extension class, the integral conjugacy representative, or the cyclic group of coinvariants found here. Related Möbius-ladder matching-complex and bounded-independence-complex results concern different simplicial complexes. Searches for mod-two Jordan blocks, integral lattices, and Möbius-ladder independence homology found no stronger covering result; this does not prove uniqueness against unindexed literature.

## Limitations
This is an integral \(C_4\)-module theorem for the specified twelve-vertex Möbius ladder, not a full dihedral integral classification or a theorem for arbitrary ladder width. The rational homology concentration uses Agarwal and Basak's nonequivariant theorem, while the characteristic-two matrix is established independently by exhaustive finite computation. The asserted equivariant extension classification is exact, but independent mathematical review has not been performed. A logically equivalent construction could exist under different terminology.
