# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is a statement about the ordinary edge matching complex of the ten-vertex Petersen graph, not its two-matching complex and not the matching complex of the complete graph on five vertices. The graph construction, 15-edge ordering, face admission condition, 160-pair discrete-Morse matching, exhaustive 975-incidence acyclicity test, and all-zero integral 5-by-5 Morse boundary are specified and regenerated by `verify.py`. A CW model with one vertex, five 2-cells and five 3-cells with zero attaching degrees is a wedge of the asserted spheres because degree-two Hurewicz identifies all 3-cell attaching maps. For the symmetry result, the image of the simplicial boundary has rational rank 79; modular action traces recover exact integer traces by finite-order trace bounds, and all 120 group elements satisfy the two explicit character formulas. Scientific risk: finite checking is subject to implementation error, although the matching polynomial uses an independent enumeration and the proof uses a complete Hasse check and exact integer gradient coefficients.

## Originality — PASS
The closest primary general theorem concerns matching complexes of outerplanar graphs (arXiv:2411.04601); its hypothesis excludes the nonplanar Petersen graph. A recent three-row grid paper (arXiv:2609.27366) has another graph family. The complete-graph result of Mondal–Mukherjee–Saha uses \(M_5\) for a complex *equal to* the Petersen graph, not for the present 4-dimensional matching complex *of* the Petersen graph. Index searches for the ordinary matching complex, its line-graph independence alias, its exact face vector, its Betti vector and the full symmetric-group character identified no theorem implying the combined topological and character result. Closest related indexed findings instead address higher two-matchings, the Morse matching complex, or different topological constructions on the Petersen graph. An unindexed source remains a residual novelty risk; the 2026 grid preprint was available at abstract level, but its full primary text could not be obtained.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The Petersen graph is a natural, smallest familiar nonplanar bridgeless cubic graph, making it a motivated test beyond the outerplanar matching-complex wedge theorem. The result determines an exact mixed-dimensional **integral homotopy** type, rather than Betti numbers alone, and resolves the entire natural \(S_5\)-character in both nonzero degrees. The distinction between a reducible signed permutation module in dimension two and an irreducible Specht module in dimension three supplies structural information of use in understanding graph symmetry and matching-complex topology. It is a specific finite benchmark, not an asserted general solution.
