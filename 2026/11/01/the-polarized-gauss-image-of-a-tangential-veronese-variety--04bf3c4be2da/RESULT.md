# The polarized Gauss image of a tangential Veronese variety

## Finding
Let \(V\) be a complex vector space of dimension \(n+1\), where \(n\geq2\), and let \(d\geq3\). Put \(T_{n,d}=\tau(\nu_d(\mathbb P(V)))\subset\mathbb P(\operatorname{Sym}^d V)\). The closure \(G_{n,d}\) of the ordinary Gauss image of the smooth locus of \(T_{n,d}\) in \(\operatorname{Gr}(2n+1,\operatorname{Sym}^d V)\) is isomorphic to the smooth flag variety \(F(1,2;V)\), *with its actual Plücker polarization*.

Write \(\mathcal L_1\subset\mathcal L_2\subset V\otimes\mathcal O_F\) for the tautological subbundles of ranks one and two. Define \(a=(2n+1)(d-2)\), \(b=n\), and \(A=a+b\). The polarization is
\[
\mathcal O_{G_{n,d}}(1)\cong (\mathcal L_1^\vee)^{\otimes a}\otimes(\det\mathcal L_2^\vee)^{\otimes b}.
\]
The Plücker image is projectively normal. Its *complete Hilbert function* in its linear span, for every integer \(m\geq0\), is
\[
H_{n,d}(m)=(ma+1)\prod_{j=3}^{n+1}
\frac{mA+j-1}{j-1}\frac{mb+j-2}{j-2}.
\]
In particular its dimension is \(2n-1\) and its exact Plücker degree is
\[
\boxed{\deg G_{n,d}=\binom{2n-1}{n}\,a\,A^{n-1}b^{n-1}.}
\]
For \((n,d)=(2,3),(2,4),(3,3)\), these degrees are respectively \(210,720,63000\).

## Assumptions and scope
All schemes are over \(\mathbb C\). The Veronese map is defined by the complete system \(\operatorname{Sym}^d V\). Tangential points \([\ell^{d-1}m]\) with \(\ell,m\in V\) linearly independent are smooth for \(d\geq3\); their embedded *affine* tangent spaces have dimension \(2n+1\). The Grassmannian parametrizes these affine vector subspaces, so the Plücker hyperplane class is the dual determinant of the universal rank-\((2n+1)\) subbundle. The Hilbert function is that of the Gauss-image variety in its Plücker linear span (not that of the singular tangential variety).

## Proof
**1. Identify the tangent-space map.** Differentiating the parametrization \((\ell,m)\mapsto\ell^{d-1}m\) gives, at linearly independent \(\ell,m\),
\[
E_{\ell,m}=\ell^{d-2}(\ell V+mV)\subset\operatorname{Sym}^d V.
\]
Inside \(\operatorname{Sym}^2V\), the multiplication map \(\langle\ell,m\rangle\otimes V\to\ell V+mV\) has one-dimensional kernel \(\bigwedge^2\langle\ell,m\rangle\), so \(\dim E_{\ell,m}=2(n+1)-1=2n+1\). The resulting subspace depends only on the flag \(\langle\ell\rangle\subset\langle\ell,m\rangle\). It therefore defines a morphism
\[
\Phi:F(1,2;V)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gr}(2n+1,\operatorname{Sym}^d V).
\]
Its image is exactly the closure of the Gauss image, since independent pairs form a dense locus.

**2. Recover the flag from the subspace.** The greatest common homogeneous polynomial factor of all nonzero elements of \(E_{\ell,m}\) is precisely \(\ell^{d-2}\): after dividing by that power the vector space \(\ell V+mV\) has no nonconstant common factor (\(\dim V\geq3\)). Thus the Gauss tangent subspace recovers the line \(\langle\ell\rangle\). Dividing out this factor recovers the degree-two vector space \(\mathcal L_2 V\). The homogeneous ideal generated by \(\mathcal L_2 V\) is \((\mathcal L_2)\mathfrak m\), where \(\mathfrak m\) is the irrelevant ideal of \(\operatorname{Sym}V\); saturation recovers the linear ideal \((\mathcal L_2)\), and hence the two-plane \(\mathcal L_2\). Consequently distinct flags give distinct subspaces. Since \(\Phi\) is equivariant for \(\operatorname{GL}(V)\), the stabilizer of its image point is exactly the parabolic stabilizer of the flag. In characteristic zero its closed orbit is therefore isomorphic, as a scheme, to \(F(1,2;V)\). This also proves smoothness and irreducibility of \(G_{n,d}\).

**3. Determine the actual polarization.** On the flag variety the rank-\((2n+1)\) tangent-space bundle is
\[
\mathcal E=\mathcal L_1^{\otimes(d-2)}\otimes
\bigl((\mathcal L_2\otimes V)/\bigwedge^2\mathcal L_2\bigr).
\]
Taking determinants and ignoring the constant one-dimensional factor \((\det V)^{\otimes2}\), one obtains
\[
\det\mathcal E\cong\mathcal L_1^{\otimes(2n+1)(d-2)}\otimes
(\det\mathcal L_2)^{\otimes n}.
\]
Since the pullback of the Plücker line is \(\det\mathcal E^\vee\), the asserted ample polarization follows.

**4. Hilbert function and projective normality.** The polarization is the homogeneous line bundle of dominant \(\operatorname{GL}(V)\) weight \((A,b,0,\ldots,0)\), up to a determinant character. By Borel–Weil and the Weyl dimension formula, for each \(m\geq0\) its global sections form the irreducible highest-weight representation of weight \((mA,mb,0,\ldots,0)\), whose dimension is exactly \(H_{n,d}(m)\) displayed above. The Plücker map is nonconstant, equivariant, and its projective orbit is the complete embedding by this line bundle: its linear span dualizes to a nonzero invariant submodule of the irreducible section space, hence is the whole section space. For each \(m\), multiplication of sections onto the highest-weight summand of weight \(m(A,b)\) is surjective (Cartan multiplication); the section algebra is normal by the standard normality theorem for the homogeneous highest-weight orbit. Thus the Plücker image is projectively normal and its graded coordinate ring has exactly this Hilbert function.

**5. Exact degree by two independent calculations.** The degree is \((2n-1)!\) times the coefficient of \(m^{2n-1}\) in the Weyl product above, giving \(\binom{2n-1}{n}aA^{n-1}b^{n-1}\). Independently, let \(p:F\to\mathbb P(V)\), \(Q=(V\otimes\mathcal O)/\mathcal L_1\), \(h=c_1(\mathcal L_1^\vee)\), \(\xi=c_1((\mathcal L_2/\mathcal L_1)^\vee)\). Then \(F=\mathbb P_{\rm lines}(Q)\), \(c(Q)=(1-h)^{-1}\), and the Plücker class is \(Ah+b\xi\). The projective-bundle pushforwards satisfy \(p_*(\xi^{n-1})=1\), \(p_*(\xi^n)=-h\), and \(p_*(\xi^{n-1+i})=0\) for \(i\geq2\). Therefore
\[
\int_F(Ah+b\xi)^{2n-1}=
\binom{2n-1}{n-1}A^n b^{n-1}-
\binom{2n-1}{n}A^{n-1}b^n
=\binom{2n-1}{n}aA^{n-1}b^{n-1},
\]
which independently confirms the coefficient calculation.

## Verification
Run the standalone Python 3 checker `verify_degree.py` with no external dependencies. It evaluates the Weyl Hilbert product exactly as fractions, takes a finite difference of order \(2n-1\), compares with the closed degree formula, and independently evaluates the two-term projective-bundle pushforward, over all \(2\leq n\leq7\), \(3\leq d\leq9\). These finite tests verify arithmetic and normalization; they do not replace the scheme-theoretic proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Torrance, *Terracini loci of tangential varieties of Veroneseans* (2026), Lemmas 2.5 and 2.6, gives the explicit tangent-space span and characterizes when two of these tangent spaces coincide. Its focus is Terracini loci and secant identifiability; it does not state the polarized homogeneous model, Hilbert function, or Gauss-image Plücker degree above. Kaji, *Higher Gauss maps of Veronese varieties* (2017 version, published 2018), computes higher Gauss-image degrees of the smooth Veronese itself, not the ordinary Gauss image of its singular tangential variety. Ran, *Gaussian scrolls, Gaussian flags and duality* (2024 version), develops a structural theory of Gauss-deficient tangential scrolls, including Veronese examples, but does not provide this Plücker embedding or its Hilbert polynomial. The new computation makes the Plücker polarization and all graded dimensions explicit for the tangential Gauss image.

## Limitations
The theorem is over \(\mathbb C\), for \(n\geq2\), \(d\geq3\), and concerns the *Gauss-image* variety rather than the tangential variety itself. It does not give a minimal system of generators for the Plücker ideal or assert independent historical priority. The proof uses characteristic-zero highest-weight theory; no corresponding positive-characteristic projective-normality assertion is made.
