# The scale-two Rips transition and dihedral homology of Möbius ladders

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge3\), let \(M_{2n}\) be the Möbius ladder on vertices \(\mathbb Z/(2n)\), with edges joining \(i\) to \(i\pm1\) and to \(i+n\). Put \(X_n=\operatorname{VR}_{\le2}(V(M_{2n}),d_{M_{2n}})=\operatorname{Cl}(M_{2n}^{\,2})\). Then, integrally,
\[
 X_n\simeq
 \begin{cases}
 *,&n=3,4,\\
 S^2,&n=5,\\
 S^1,&n\ge6.
 \end{cases}
\]
For the dihedral symmetries \(r(i)=i+1\) and \(s(i)=-i\), their actions on the unique nonzero reduced integral homology group are, respectively, \((-1,-1)\) in degree two when \(n=5\), and \((+1,-1)\) in degree one when \(n\ge6\). All other reduced integral homology groups vanish. Thus in the exceptional case \(n=5\), \(r^as^b\) acts as \((-1)^{a+b}\); in the stable regime, it acts as \((-1)^b\).

At the graph scale one, \(\widetilde H_1\cong\mathbb Z^{n+1}\) because the graph has \(2n\) vertices and \(3n\) edges. The scale-one-to-two map is zero when \(n\le5\), and is surjective of rank one when \(n\ge6\). Surjectivity follows because every new scale-two edge can be replaced by a length-two original graph path across a filled triangle.

## Assumptions and scope
All graph edges have length one. The metric is shortest-path distance **only on the finite graph vertex set**; geometric edge interiors are not included. An inclusive Rips simplex is a nonempty subset whose every pair of vertices has distance at most two. The dihedral group in this statement is the subgroup generated by the indicated rotation and reflection, irrespective of any accidental additional automorphisms at small orders. The homology representations are over \(\mathbb Z\), not just over \(\mathbb Q\).

## Proof
**1. Local distance rule.** Write \(q:\mathbb Z/(2n)\to\mathbb Z/n\), \(q(v)=v\pmod n\), and call the two vertices with the same image a rung. In the square graph, the two vertices of any given rung are adjacent, and each is adjacent to **both** vertices of each neighboring rung. A distance-two pair whose rung indices are two apart must come from two successive original cycle edges: using any rung edge and one cycle edge changes the rung index by only one. For \(n\ge6\), this also shows that the projection of any square-graph clique is a clique of the squared \(n\)-cycle.

**2. A cyclic good cover for \(n\ge6\).** For every index \(j\in\mathbb Z/n\), let \(I_j=\{j-1,j,j+1\}\) and let \(Y_j\) be the induced subcomplex of \(X_n\) on the six vertices in the rungs labeled by \(I_j\). Both vertices of the central rung \(j\) are adjacent to every vertex of this induced square graph, so \(Y_j\) is a cone (indeed, has a central edge as join factor) and is contractible.

Every face of \(X_n\) lies in some \(Y_j\). For \(n\ge7\), any clique of the squared \(n\)-cycle lies within three consecutive indices, by the elementary circular-distance-two clique classification. When \(n=6\), there are exactly two additional projected maximal triples, the even and odd alternating triples. Neither can lift to a three-vertex clique in the Möbius ladder square: successive distance-two edges along the original cycle preserve the chosen lift along two steps, whereas traversing all three such edges would reverse it (the lifted walk advances six vertices, the antipodal rung displacement). Equivalently, choose a lift over index zero: its distance-two compatible lift over index two determines a lift over index four; that last lift is at graph distance greater than two from the initial lift. Hence these projected triples cannot be supports of faces, proving the cover in all cases.

For two or more distinct indices \(j_1,\ldots,j_t\), an intersection \(Y_{j_1}\cap\cdots\cap Y_{j_t}\), when nonempty, is the full simplex on one rung or two adjacent rungs. Indeed, the common rung-index set is the intersection of cyclic length-three windows, and for \(n\ge6\) any such nonempty intersection of at least two distinct windows has one or two consecutive indices. Any one rung is a clique and any two adjacent rungs together form a four-vertex clique. Thus all nonempty finite intersections are contractible. The simplicial nerve lemma identifies \(X_n\) up to homotopy with the nerve of this cover.

The nerve has vertices \(j\pmod n\), edges of circular separation one or two, and exactly the \(n\) triangles \(\{j-1,j,j+1\}\); no higher faces occur. For each \(j\), the distance-two edge \(\{j-1,j+1\}\) is a free face of the **unique** triangle \(\{j-1,j,j+1\}\). Removing those \(n\) pairs leaves exactly the ordinary \(n\)-cycle. Hence
\[
 X_n\simeq\operatorname{Nerve}(Y_j)\searrow C_n\simeq S^1\qquad(n\ge6).
\]
The construction is dihedrally natural: \(r\) sends \(Y_j\) to \(Y_{j+1}\) and \(s\) to \(Y_{-j}\). The natural nerve equivalence (or its canonical barycentric partition-of-unity realization) is equivariant up to homotopy. Rotation preserves orientation of the resulting circle, while reflection reverses it. This gives the asserted integral degree-one action.

**3. The small ladders.** For \(n=3\) and \(n=4\), the graph has diameter two, and the Rips complex is the full simplex on respectively six and eight vertices. For \(n=5\), exhaustive enumeration from the explicit distance-two rule yields the face vector
\[
 (f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4)=(10,35,50,25,2).
\]
The standalone file `collapse_n5.json` lists, in order, **48 elementary simplicial collapses**. Each listed lower face has exactly one remaining proper coface, the listed upper face, and the upper face has one additional vertex. After all collapses the remaining complex has six vertices \(\{4,5,6,7,8,9\}\) and is the clique complex of the complete graph on those vertices with exactly the three disjoint opposite edges \(\{4,7\},\{5,8\},\{6,9\}\) deleted. This is the boundary of the three-dimensional cross-polytope, hence \(S^2\). The exact certificate is replayed independently by `verify.py`, which reconstructs the Rips complex rather than trusting the recorded face list.

**4. The exceptional orientation character.** For \(n=5\), a direct signed simplex calculation gives the Lefschetz number \(L(r^as^b)=1+(-1)^{a+b}\), for every \(0\le a<10\) and \(b\in\{0,1\}\). Each fixed nonempty simplex contributes \((-1)^{\dim\sigma}\) times the sign of the vertex permutation induced by \(r^as^b\) on its oriented simplex. The exhaustive exact calculation is included in `verify.py` and applies to the original complex before collapse. Since this connected complex is homotopy equivalent to \(S^2\), Lefschetz gives \(L(g)=1+\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H_2(X_5;\mathbb Z))\). Its second homology has rank one, so the trace completely determines the integral action: both \(r\) and \(s\) have degree \(-1\).

**5. The first persistent map.** The scale-one complex is the connected cubic graph \(M_{2n}\), with first Betti number \(3n-2n+1=n+1\). Every scale-two edge is homotopic in the scale-two complex to a path of at most two original graph edges, because the relevant triangle fills this detour. Thus inclusion at scale one surjects onto the scale-two fundamental group and hence onto first homology. For \(n\le5\), the target first homology is zero; for \(n\ge6\) it is \(\mathbb Z\). Surjectivity follows from the preceding edge-detour argument: each target edge is carried by a graph edge-path, so every loop in the connected target is represented by an original-graph loop.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` alongside `collapse_n5.json` in a standard Python installation. The script exhaustively builds the square-graph cliques and checks the precise free-face condition for each of the 48 supplied collapse pairs, the full surviving octahedral boundary, and all 20 dihedral signed Lefschetz numbers at \(n=5\). It also checks diameter-two contractibility at \(n=3,4\) and reconstructs the cyclic good-cover nerve and its collapses for every \(6\le n\le13\). Its exact recorded stdout is `verify_output.txt`. Finite checks corroborate the all-parameter proof but are not offered as a substitute for the good-cover argument.

## Relationship to prior work
Adamaszek's `arXiv:1104.0433` investigates graph-power clique complexes and completely treats cycle powers. The general high-girth square-graph collapse does not apply here: Möbius ladders have four-cycles. Its closed-neighborhood clique-carrier criterion also fails for this graph's square four-cliques on adjacent rungs. This note instead determines the twisted-rung family using a cyclic cover and a separate exceptional collapse.

A closely motivated result on ordinary circular ladders establishes a different scale-two classification for \(C_n\square K_2\): unlike Möbius ladders, those prisms have a threefold wedge of two-spheres at \(n=6\) and settle to a circle only for \(n\ge7\). The published clique-fiber cyclic product results require a genuine Cartesian product and cannot be transferred to the twisted ladder, which is not that product. Their statements therefore do not imply the present theorem. Related classifications of Möbius-ladder neighborhood, independence, total cut, and matching complexes have different face conditions and do not determine this Rips complex.

Adamaszek et al., `arXiv:1712.06224`, Section 5, investigate metric-graph gluings and discuss ordinary circular ladders; their Theorem 5 uses a supremum metric, and the partial graph-distance discussion concerns a continuous circular ladder, not this finite twisted vertex set. Their result neither contains this graph family nor yields the integral dihedral character.

## Limitations
The classification concerns the single inclusive graph-metric scale two, together with the scale-one inclusion map. It does not claim the entire higher-scale persistence barcode, or the Vietoris–Rips complexes of the continuum of points on the edges. It does not identify integral homology of orbit spaces or equivariant homotopy types. Exact priority requires checking possibly unindexed literature; a negative indexed search alone does not prove uniqueness.
