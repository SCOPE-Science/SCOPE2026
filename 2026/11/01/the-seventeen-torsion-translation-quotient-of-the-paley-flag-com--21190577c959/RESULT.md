# The seventeen-torsion translation quotient of the Paley flag complex

## Finding
Let \(P_{17}\) be the Paley graph on \(\mathbb Z/17\mathbb Z\), with an edge between distinct vertices exactly when their difference is a nonzero square. Let \(X=\operatorname{Cl}(P_{17})\) be its ordinary flag (clique) complex, and let \(C_{17}\) act by vertex translation. Then the **ordinary topological orbit space** (not the Borel quotient) has homotopy type
\[
X/C_{17}\simeq M(\mathbb Z/17,1)=S^1\cup_{17}e^2.
\]
If \(I=\ker(\varepsilon:\mathbb Z[C_{17}]\to\mathbb Z)\) is the integral augmentation ideal and the generator acts by translation through one vertex, then the complete integral deck-action on positive-degree homology is
\[
\widetilde H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong_{\mathbb Z[C_{17}]}I,
\qquad \widetilde H_i(X;\mathbb Z)=0\quad(i\ne2).
\]
In particular, the quotient has \(\pi_1\cong H_1(-;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z/17\) and no higher reduced integral homology. The non-equivariant wedge-of-spheres classification of the original complex is already implied by prior vertex-decomposability work and is **not** claimed as new.

## Assumptions and scope
Write \(Q=\{1,2,4,8,9,13,15,16\}\subset\mathbb Z/17\mathbb Z\), the nonzero squares. For distinct \(a,b\), \(\{a,b\}\) is a graph edge precisely when \(b-a\in Q\). A face of \(X\) is a nonempty subset whose every two distinct vertices are adjacent (together with the empty face). Translation is the order-seventeen action \(t(a)=a+1\pmod{17}\). The orbit space is taken from the geometric realization of this finite simplicial complex. All cellular chains and module identifications are over \(\mathbb Z\), with the evident left translation action. The specific graph has 17 vertices, 68 edges, 68 triangles and no tetrahedra.

## Proof
**Step 1: enumerate free cell orbits.** The nonzero square differences are precisely \(\pm1,\pm2,\pm4,\pm8\). Exhaustively comparing the 17 vertex translates of every graph clique, or alternatively checking the four edge increments by hand, yields the face vector \((17,68,68)\) and no four-vertex clique. No nonidentity translation can preserve a face of size 1, 2 or 3: an orbit under such a translation has size 17, which cannot be contained in a smaller face. Consequently, the translation action is free on the geometric realization, and the orbit space has a CW structure with one vertex, four one-cells and four two-cells. Convenient edge-orbit orientations use increments \(1,2,4,8\); call the corresponding based loops \(x_1,x_2,x_4,x_8\). Representatives for all four triangular orbits are \((0,1,2)\), \((0,2,4)\), \((0,4,8)\), and \((0,1,9)\). Their translation orbits are pairwise disjoint and together exhaust the 68 triangles.

**Step 2: compute attaching words, not merely the abelianized boundary.** Reading the three directed edges of each oriented triangle around its boundary gives the four relators
\[
 x_1x_1x_2^{-1},\qquad x_2x_2x_4^{-1},\qquad
 x_4x_4x_8^{-1},\qquad x_1x_8x_8.
\]
For the final triangle, the increments are \(0\to1\) (step 1), \(1\to9\) (step 8), and \(9\to0\) (step 8 modulo 17). In the first three relators, respectively, the generators \(x_2,x_4,x_8\) occur once with inverse exponent. Successively changing the one-cell basis and canceling these corresponding one-/two-cell pairs is a homotopy-preserving CW elimination. Equivalently, substitute \(x_2=x_1^2\), \(x_4=x_1^4\), \(x_8=x_1^8\) into the remaining attaching map. The resulting two-cell is attached to the remaining circle by the degree-seventeen word \(x_1^{17}\). Thus the quotient is the stated Moore space, not merely a complex having the same abelianized presentation.

**Step 3: lift to integral equivariant homology.** The order-seventeen action on \(X\) is free, and \(X\to X/C_{17}\) is a regular covering. The cellular quotient calculation gives \(\pi_1(X/C_{17})\cong C_{17}\), generated by the first edge loop \(x_1\); a lift of that loop advances by one vertex, so the covering corresponds to the universal cover. Lift the homotopy equivalence above to universal covers, preserving the induced \(C_{17}\)-action. Writing \(R=\mathbb Z[t]/(t^{17}-1)=\mathbb Z[C_{17}]\), the universal-cover cellular chain complex for the standard Moore model has
\[
0\longrightarrow R\xrightarrow{\,1+t+\cdots+t^{16}\,}R
\xrightarrow{\,t-1\,}R\longrightarrow0.
\]
The kernel of the norm multiplication map is precisely \(I=(t-1)R\), because multiplying by the norm sums all coefficients and vanishes exactly for coefficient sum zero. The image of the norm is the kernel of \(t-1\), and the image of \(t-1\) is the augmentation ideal, giving the claimed integral homology and action. No rational or finite-field approximation is used here.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_quotient.py`. This standalone standard-library verifier reconstructs all 17 graph vertices, all 68 edges, all 68 triangles, checks that no four-clique exists, partitions faces into free 17-element translation orbits, verifies the four orbit representatives and their oriented attaching words, and checks the exact four-by-four integral cellular boundary determinant \(17\). It explicitly checks that substitution into the final relator gives degree \(17\). Its actual captured output is `verify_quotient_output.txt` and ends with `VERIFY_OK`. Additionally `verify_paley17.py` independently checks acyclic face-poset collapse and the finite-field ranks of the original complex (recorded in `verification_output.txt`); those finite computations corroborate rather than replace the deck-module proof. The topological CW elimination and covering argument above are the infinite/general theoretical steps.

## Relationship to prior work
Kahle studies topological properties of random clique complexes and establishes the broader clique-complex framework; that random-graph result does not compute the deterministic Paley-17 orbit space or its integral deck module. Elsawy develops Paley-graph arithmetic, symmetry and regularity, but not the orbit-space homotopy type. Rinaldo and Romeo explicitly discuss the Paley-17 circulant example and its vertex-decomposable independence complex; by self-complementarity this already covers the nonequivariant sphere-wedge topology of the Paley clique complex. Their result does **not** determine the translation quotient or its integral action. A distinct calculation in the literature treats the Lovász **open-neighborhood** complex of Paley-17, not its clique complex. Other cyclic quotient examples with Moore-space homotopy use different graph complexes and do not imply the four explicit attaching words found here.

## Limitations
The theorem is specific to the Paley graph of order 17 and its full additive translation subgroup; no theorem is claimed for other Paley orders, for nontranslation subgroups or for its neighborhood complex. The action identified is that of \(C_{17}\), not the full automorphism group. A previously unnoticed equivalent treatment of this quotient or deck module could exist in unindexed work. There has been no independent mathematical audit.
