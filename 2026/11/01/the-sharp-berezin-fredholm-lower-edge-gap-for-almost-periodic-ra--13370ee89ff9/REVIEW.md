# Self-review: sharp radial Fock lower-edge discrepancy

## Correctness — PASS
The final claim is the exact supremum of the Berezin lower-edge minus the Fredholm essential spectral lower-edge over all real continuous Bohr almost-periodic radial profiles with supremum norm at most one, in every finite complex dimension. The normalization and direction of the discrepancy are explicit. The monomial/gamma identity and the coherent-state Gaussian identity give the two heat filters with variances respectively \(1/4\) and \(1/2\); the proof establishes the limiting spectral and Berezin infima using almost-periodic recurrence and vanishing square-root mesh size. The universal bound is the \(L^1\) distance of two precisely normalized Gaussians; their unique crossing radius and the error-function expression were independently recomputed. The lower bound is realized asymptotically by wide-spaced periodic negative intervals; the tail bound is uniform in spatial phase. A smooth approximate identity preserves normalization, periodicity and the limit. Compactness of the Bohr translation hull plus equality rigidity in \(L^1\)--\(L^\infty\) duality gives genuine nonattainment, not a finite-sampling inference. The positivity assertion for nonconstant profiles uses strict Gaussian averaging and injectivity on almost-periodic Fourier modes. The standalone numerical checker is diagnostic only.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, not priority certification)
The sharp universal constant, the fact that it is independent of finite complex dimension, strict nonattainment over all continuous almost-periodic profiles, and a smooth periodic near-extremizing construction were compared at the level of mathematical implication against the relevant published sources and mathematical-record queries. Esmeral and Maximenko (2015), Proposition 6.5, **already prove the one-dimensional time-\(1/8\) eigenvalue Gaussian approximation for all bounded radial symbols**, but not the sharp difference-of-lower-edges optimization or its nonattainment. Looi (2026) gives explicit single-frequency positive-Berezin/negative-essential-spectrum examples and the two attenuation scales, not the universal normalized extremum or smooth near-extremizers. Bdarneh (2026) concerns radial-weight C*-algebras, spectral representations, and a non-Gaussian-weight counterexample; its full 21-page text has no matching variational formula. The nearest accessible published mathematical-record result on a logarithmic Fock spectral filter concerns critical Shubin/Dixmier traces rather than this operator-theoretic Gaussian lower-edge gap. The elementary signed-kernel \(L^1\) maximization is established classical analysis; it is not claimed as new in isolation. **Residual risk:** an earlier theorem phrased as total-variation contraction or semigroup minimum discrepancy might imply this sharp specialization. Search nonappearance alone does not establish historical uniqueness; the originality assessment is best-knowledge only.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Looi's counterexamples show that positivity of the limiting Berezin transform can misclassify essential positivity. The exact worst possible **additive** misclassification margin under the natural uniform symbol normalization is the missing quantitative benchmark. Its dimension independence and strict lack of an extremizer within a compact-translation class are structural properties, and explicit smooth periodic near-extremizers show both the obstruction's sharpness and that the phenomenon does not need pathological symbols or incommensurate frequencies. These properties are not obtained merely by renaming a cosine example or improving a decimal value; the output completely resolves a natural maximization problem in a large nontrivial profile class. This is not a claim about arbitrary radial measurable symbols or the full spectrum.
