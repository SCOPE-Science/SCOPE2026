# The sharp spatial injectivity-radius variance of every flat Klein bottle

## Finding

Let \(a,b>0\), and let \(K_{a,b}\) be the complete flat Klein bottle obtained from the Euclidean plane by the isometries
\[
A(x,y)=(x+a,-y),\qquad B(x,y)=(x,y+b).
\]
Let \(P\) be uniform for Riemannian area on \(K_{a,b}\); let \(i(P)\) be the pointwise geodesic injectivity radius. Put \(s=a/b\), and let \(D\) be uniform on \([0,1/2]\). The **full distribution** of injectivity radius is
\[
\boxed{\quad\frac{2i(P)}b\ \overset{\rm law}=\ \min\{1,2s,\sqrt{s^2+D^2}\}.\quad}
\]
For \(0<s<1\), with \(c=\min\{1,2s\}\) and \(u=\min\{c,\sqrt{s^2+1/4}\}\), the cumulative distribution function of \(L=2i(P)/b\) is \(0\) below \(s\), \(2\sqrt{\ell^2-s^2}\) for \(s\le\ell<u\), and \(1\) at and above \(u\). Its continuous density is \(2\ell/\sqrt{\ell^2-s^2}\) on \((s,u)\). There is additionally an atom at \(u\) precisely when \(s<1/(2\sqrt3)\) or \(\sqrt3/2<s<1\); those atom weights are respectively \(1-2\sqrt3s\) and \(1-2\sqrt{1-s^2}\). At the transition values the weights vanish. For \(s\ge1\), \(L=1\) almost surely.

The natural scale-invariant spatial fluctuation functional is
\[
W(s)=\frac{\operatorname{Var}(i(P))}{\operatorname{area}(K_{a,b})}.
\]
Write \(\lambda=\log(2+\sqrt3)\), \(H=2\sqrt3-\lambda\), and \(Q=\sqrt3-\lambda\). For **all** \(s>0\),
\[
\boxed{\quad 0\le W(s)\le\frac{64Q^3}{27H^4}=0.007976414224378\ldots .\quad}
\]
The maximum is attained at **exactly one** shape parameter,
\[
\boxed{\quad s_*=\frac{8Q}{3H^2}=0.240099811412893\ldots .\quad}
\]
The minimum is attained exactly for \(s\ge1\). For \(0<s<1\), \(W(s)>0\).

## Assumptions and scope

The quotient metric is induced by the ordinary Euclidean metric; the group is generated by the stated free, properly discontinuous isometries. Its fundamental rectangle may be taken as \([0,a)\times[0,b)\), and its Riemannian area is \(ab\). All injectivity radii are pointwise intrinsic radii, not the global minimum injectivity radius or a local geodesic-loop length defined with extra homology restrictions. The random point is sampled by normalized Riemannian area. The aspect parameter \(s\) is positive with no additional shape restriction. The inequality is dimensionless and invariant under uniform rescaling.

## Proof

**1. Deck orbit and injectivity radius.** Every deck transformation is \(A^mB^k\) for integers \(m,k\). At \(p=(x,y)\), a nonidentity deck translate has horizontal displacement \(ma\) and vertical displacement \(kb\) if \(m\) is even, or \(kb-2y\) (up to an irrelevant sign or reindexing of \(k\)) if \(m\) is odd. For \(m=0\), the least nonzero length is \(b\). For nonzero even \(m\), the shortest length is \(2a\). For odd \(m\), the shortest length is
\[
\sqrt{a^2+\operatorname{dist}(2y,b\mathbb Z)^2}.
\]
These three possibilities exhaust the orbit: increasing the absolute horizontal index cannot help, and the vertical index is minimized independently. Because the covering Euclidean plane has no conjugate points, the injectivity radius at the quotient point is exactly half the least nontrivial deck displacement. Uniform area makes \(y\) uniform modulo \(b\); hence \(D=\operatorname{dist}(2y,b\mathbb Z)/b\) has density \(2\) on \([0,1/2]\). This proves the distribution identity, including the case \(s\ge1\).

**2. Exact law and moments.** When \(0<s<1\), set \(c=\min\{1,2s\}\) and
\[
v=\min\left\{\frac12,\sqrt{c^2-s^2}\right\}.
\]
Since \(L=\sqrt{s^2+D^2}\) for \(D<v\) and \(L=c\) for \(D\ge v\), integrating the uniform density gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_1(s)&=\mathbb E L=v\sqrt{s^2+v^2}+s^2\operatorname{arsinh}(v/s)+(1-2v)c,\\
M_2(s)&=\mathbb E L^2=2s^2v+\frac23v^3+(1-2v)c^2,\\
W(s)&=\frac{M_2(s)-M_1(s)^2}{4s}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These formulas, with \(M_1=M_2=1\) for \(s\ge1\), give a closed all-aspect profile. The cap is active on positive measure exactly when \(c<\sqrt{s^2+1/4}\). For \(s<1/2\), substituting \(c=2s\) gives \(s<1/(2\sqrt3)\). For \(s\ge1/2\), substituting \(c=1\) gives \(s>\sqrt3/2\) while \(s<1\). Applying the monotone change of variables \(\ell=\sqrt{s^2+D^2}\) proves the stated density, cumulative distribution, and atom masses.

**3. Exact optimization in the first regime.** For \(0<s\le1/(2\sqrt3)\), the integration cutoff is \(v=\sqrt3s\), and \(\operatorname{arsinh}\sqrt3=\log(2+\sqrt3)=\lambda\). Therefore
\[
M_1=2s-Hs^2,\qquad M_2=4s^2-4\sqrt3s^3,
\]
so
\[
\boxed{W(s)=Qs^2-\frac{H^2}{4}s^3.}
\]
Here \(Q>0\) because \(\lambda=\int_0^{\sqrt3}(1+t^2)^{-1/2}\,dt<\sqrt3\). Differentiation gives exactly one positive critical point, \(s_*=8Q/(3H^2)\), with positive derivative below it and negative derivative above it. We verify that \(s_*<1/(2\sqrt3)\): put \(q=\lambda/\sqrt3\). By the strictly convex function \(z\mapsto(1+z)^{-1/2}\), Jensen's inequality yields
\[
q=\int_0^1\frac{dt}{\sqrt{1+3t^2}}>\frac1{\sqrt2}>\frac23.
\]
Direct algebra shows \(s_*<1/(2\sqrt3)\) is equivalent to \(3q^2+4q-4>0\), which follows from \(q>2/3\). Evaluation of the cubic at \(s_*\) gives \(64Q^3/(27H^4)\).

**4. Global, strict comparison in the remaining regimes.** Let \(s_0=1/(2\sqrt3)\). For all \(s\ge s_0\) the cap \(2s\) never binds, so with \(D\in[0,1/2]\),
\[
L_s(D)=\min\{1,\sqrt{s^2+D^2}\}.
\]
For every fixed \(s\) and almost every \(D\), its derivative in \(s\) equals \(s/\sqrt{s^2+D^2}\) in the uncapped region and \(0\) in the capped region. This derivative is nonincreasing as a function of \(D\). Hence for \(D_2>D_1\), the nonnegative difference \(L_s(D_2)-L_s(D_1)\) is nonincreasing in \(s\). Its decrease is strict on a set of pairs of positive measure for any nontrivial interval of \(s\) inside \([s_0,1)\): take distinct small \(D_1,D_2\) for which neither value is capped. The identity
\[
\operatorname{Var}(L_s(D))=\frac12\mathbb E\left[(L_s(D)-L_s(D'))^2\right],
\]
with \(D'\) independent, proves the variance is strictly decreasing on \([s_0,1)\). Since \(s\) increases and the variance is nonnegative, \(W(s)=\operatorname{Var}(L_s(D))/(4s)\) is also strictly decreasing there. At \(s\ge1\) the law is degenerate and \(W=0\). Thus the single maximum from step 3 is global, unique, and sharp.

## Relationship to prior work

Davis and Recio-Mitter (arXiv:1912.07411v1, first public 2019-12-16; published in New York Journal of Mathematics in 2021) describe the flat Klein-bottle deck metric, closest translates, cut loci, and geodesic-complexity structure. Their pointwise geodesic framework is prior work, not the present novelty. Eyll (2025) studies systolic inequalities and stability over flat/conformal Klein-bottle metrics; its variance is a distinct metric-dependent quantity, not the spatial distribution of pointwise injectivity radii under a fixed metric. Standard injectivity-radius/deck-displacement facts are likewise prior. The contribution asserted here is the complete spatial probability law for every aspect ratio, both atom transitions, and the globally sharp area-normalized fluctuation extremum. Relevant accessible literature and published mathematical-research records were searched using injectivity-radius, shortest-loop, pointwise-cut-locus, and Klein-bottle aliases without identifying a result whose statement implies the sharp variance bound. A negative search does not certify historical priority.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns rectangular flat Klein-bottle quotients with the standard orientation-reversing glide and Riemannian area sampling. It does not apply without additional work to curved or conformally deformed metrics, nonuniform sampling, or higher-dimensional flat manifolds. The vanishing variance for \(s\ge1\) does not characterize homogeneous geometry, only spatial constancy of this particular invariant. No finite-sample statistical confidence statements or independent mathematical audit are claimed. Historical priority is provisional, especially for earlier specialized local-systole calculations.
