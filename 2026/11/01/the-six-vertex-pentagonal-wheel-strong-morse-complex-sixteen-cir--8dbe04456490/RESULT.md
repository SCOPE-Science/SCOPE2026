# The six-vertex pentagonal wheel strong Morse complex: sixteen circles and its full dihedral character

## Finding
Let \(W\) be the **one-dimensional, unfilled** wheel graph with hub \(0\), five rim vertices \(1,2,3,4,5\), five spokes \(\{0,i\}\), and the five cyclic rim edges. Let \(X=\mathcal{SM}(W)\) be the **full**, not pure, strong discrete-Morse matching complex in the sense of Knudson and Owens–White [1]. Then
\[
\boxed{|X|\simeq\bigvee^{16}S^1.}
\]
Every reduced integral homology group other than \(\widetilde H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{16}\) vanishes.

Let \(D_5\) act by rotations and reflections of the rim while fixing the hub. The complete character of its first rational homology representation is
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H_1(X;\mathbb Q))=
\begin{cases}
16,&g=1,\\
1,&g\text{ is a nonidentity rotation},\\
-4,&g\text{ is a reflection}.
\end{cases}}
\]
Let \(\varepsilon\) denote the one-dimensional rational sign representation, trivial on rotations and negative on reflections. Let \(U\) denote the four-dimensional rational augmentation representation of the permutation action of \(D_5\) on the five rim vertices. Then
\[
\boxed{H_1(X;\mathbb Q)\cong\varepsilon^{\oplus4}\oplus U^{\oplus3}.}
\]
In particular, \(H_1(X;\mathbb Q)^{D_5}=0\): the wheel has sixteen independent integral loop classes but no nonzero globally dihedral-invariant rational loop class.

## Assumptions and scope
The input complex is the graph \(W\), with its triangular circuits **not** filled by two-simplices. The graph has six vertices and ten undirected edges. A vertex of \(X\) is an oriented incident pair \((v,u)\) on an input graph edge \(\{v,u\}\); here the first coordinate is the vertex paired with the edge, and the second is its lower-filtration neighbor. Thus there are twenty possible vertices of \(X\).

For a total ordering \(\pi\) of the six input vertices, define
\[
M_\pi=\{(v,u):u\text{ is the unique neighbor of }v\text{ preceding }v\text{ in }\pi\}.
\]
The defining complex \(X\) is the downward-closed simplicial complex generated by the sets \(M_\pi\), for all six-vertex orderings \(\pi\). This description is exact in dimension one: the descending link of \(v\) is a discrete collection of preceding neighbors, and it is a nonempty simplicial cone precisely when it consists of a single vertex [1, §§3–4]. There are no additional choices of a dominating vertex in this graph case.

The dihedral action permutes vertex labels and directed incidence pairs. The homotopy statement uses integral homotopy equivalence. The representation splitting is over \(\mathbb Q\), not asserted as a splitting of integral lattices.

## Proof
**Exhaustive generator classification.** Fix the ordered list of all twenty directed incidences, lexicographically by \((v,u)\). Every total ordering of \(\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\) is one of \(6!=720\) permutations. Applying the displayed rule to each one yields precisely \(81\) distinct generated sets (one is empty and contributes no nonempty simplex). Remove nonmaximal sets and take downward closure. Direct finite enumeration gives exactly \(45\) facets: \(25\) edges and \(20\) triangles, with all nonempty faces counted by
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(20,55,20).
\]
The standalone `verify.py` reconstructs the sets by two separate implementations of the ordering rule and checks agreement for every permutation. The enumeration is complete, not sampled.

**Elementary collapse.** The file `collapse_pairs.json` supplies the lexicographic arc labels and a list of twenty triangle/edge pairs in the exact order in which they are removed. For every pair \((e,t)\), the code checks that \(e\) is a codimension-one face of \(t\), and that \(t\) is the **unique** remaining triangle containing \(e\). Because there are no faces of dimension greater than two, this proves that deleting \(e\) and \(t\) is a valid elementary simplicial collapse. All twenty triangles disappear. The remaining one-dimensional complex has exactly \(20\) vertices and \(35\) edges. Its graph is connected, verified by a full adjacency search. It therefore has rank
\[
35-20+1=16
\]
and is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of sixteen circles. The explicit sequence proves the stronger simple-homotopy reduction, and proves the integral statement without relying on homology computations.

**Dihedral traces.** Orient each simplex by increasing directed-incidence index. For a graph automorphism \(g\), its signed cellular trace in degree \(j\) is the sum of the permutation signs on those \(j\)-simplices whose vertex sets are fixed. A direct check of all ten dihedral elements gives:

| Element type | \(\operatorname{tr}(C_0)\) | \(\operatorname{tr}(C_1)\) | \(\operatorname{tr}(C_2)\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| identity | 20 | 55 | 20 |
| any of four nonidentity rotations | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| any of five reflections | 2 | -3 | 0 |

The nonidentity rotations have five-element orbits on the directed incidences; no nonempty face, of cardinality at most three, is invariant. For a representative reflection fixing hub \(0\) and rim vertex \(1\) and interchanging \(2\leftrightarrow5\) and \(3\leftrightarrow4\), the fixed zero-simplices are \((0,1)\) and \((1,0)\). The only setwise-fixed edges are
\[
\{(2,0),(5,0)\},\quad\{(2,3),(5,4)\},\quad\{(2,1),(5,1)\};
\]
each has reversed orientation. No triangle is setwise fixed. The full trace table is independently recomputed by `verify.py` from the enumerated faces.

Since \(X\) is connected and homotopy equivalent to a graph, its only nonzero ordinary rational homology is \(H_0\cong\mathbb Q\) and \(H_1\cong\mathbb Q^{16}\). The simplicial Lefschetz trace identity therefore yields
\[
\operatorname{tr}(g\mid H_1)=1-\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_0)
+\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_1)-\operatorname{tr}(g\mid C_2),
\]
which gives the displayed character \((16,1,-4)\).

Finally, \(\varepsilon\) has character \((1,1,-1)\) on the three element types, while \(U\), the kernel of the summation map from the rational five-point permutation module to \(\mathbb Q\), has character \((4,-1,0)\). The sum of four copies of \(\varepsilon\) and three copies of \(U\) has character \((16,1,-4)\). Rational representations of finite groups are semisimple and determined by their characters, proving the claimed isomorphism. The multiplicity of the trivial character is zero.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` from a directory containing `verify.py` and `collapse_pairs.json`. The program requires only the standard Python library; it reconstructs the exact graph, its twenty directed incidences, all 720 vertex orderings, and every simplex, using two independently coded ordering-rule evaluations. It checks the full face vector, every elementary free-face deletion in the supplied certificate, the connected graph spine, the action of all ten dihedral symmetries, each signed chain trace, and the rational character identity. Its successful output begins `VERIFY_OK` and reports the complete counts. The certificate's arc labels are a sorted list of pairs \((v,u)\) with input vertices indexed \(0\) through \(5\); each certificate entry gives its edge as two arc indices and its triangle as three. All arithmetic is exact, with no probabilistic sampling.

## Relationship to prior work
Knudson and Owens–White [1] introduce the strong matching complex, establish its construction from vertex filtrations, and calculate paths, stars, simplices, simplex boundaries, and cycle cases. Their primary manuscript does not provide this pentagonal-wheel classification. Their Proposition 10.2 covers complete graphs, but the present wheel is **not** complete. Their disjoint-union join theorem does not apply to this connected wheel.

Results on the **ordinary** Chari–Joswig discrete-Morse complex of wheel graphs, including a complete dihedral top-homology character, concern a strictly different complex and higher-dimensional homology; they do not imply the strong-complex homotopy or rational representation stated here [2]. The full ordinary matching complex of a wheel is likewise a different object. Searches across alternate formulations (wheel with five rim vertices, six-vertex wheel, unfilled cone over a pentagon, strong vertex-filtration matchings) and indexed literature found no logically covering statement. This is a literature-limited originality claim, not a proof of universal novelty.

## Limitations
The classification is for the single natural graph with five rim vertices, not for the full family of wheels or their higher-dimensional filled cones. No integral \(\mathbb Z[D_5]\)-lattice decomposition is claimed, and the simplicial collapses are not asserted to be equivariant. Existing indexed results could omit an equivalent unpublished or unindexed calculation. Independent mathematical validation remains to be performed.
