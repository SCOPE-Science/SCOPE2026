# The strong Morse complex of the house graph: nine circles and reflection symmetry

## Finding
Let \(H\) be the **unfilled** one-dimensional house graph on vertices \(\{0,1,2,3,4\}\) with edges \(01,12,23,30,14,24\). Its graph-theoretic four-cycle is \(0-1-2-3-0\), and its triangle is \(1-2-4-1\). Let \(\mathcal{SM}(H)\) be the full (not pure) strong discrete-Morse matching complex generated by total vertex orders as defined below. Then
\[
\mathcal{SM}(H)\searrow G_{12,20},\qquad
|\mathcal{SM}(H)|\simeq\bigvee^9 S^1,
\]
where \(G_{12,20}\) is an explicitly certified connected graph with 12 vertices and 20 edges. In particular \(H_1(\mathcal{SM}(H);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^9\), and its other reduced integral homology groups vanish.

The reflection \(\rho=(0\ 3)(1\ 2)\), which fixes roof vertex \(4\), acts on \(H_1(\mathcal{SM}(H);\mathbb Q)\) with trace \(1\). Its \(+1\) and \(-1\) eigenspaces have dimensions \(5\) and \(4\), respectively. The underlying integral homology is free; no claim of a canonical integral eigenspace splitting is made.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is treated as a one-dimensional simplicial complex: even though it has a triangle in its one-skeleton, that triangular face is **not** filled. For each total order \(v_1<\cdots<v_5\) on its five vertices, pair a vertex \(v\) to the edge \(\{v,w\}\) precisely when \(w\) is the **unique earlier neighbor** of \(v\). Such a pair is denoted by the ordered incidence \((v,w)\). These pairs form a matching in the graph face poset, and are the strong matching associated with the order. The full strong Morse complex contains every submatching of any one of these order-induced matchings. Its simplicial vertices are the 12 directed incidences of the six undirected edges.

This full-complex convention is essential: the ordinary Chari–Joswig Morse complex, the pure strong subcomplex, and a usual graph-edge matching complex are different objects.

## Proof
The 120 vertex orders are a finite, exhaustive set. For each order, the defining unique-earlier-neighbor rule yields a face; taking all subfaces gives precisely 48 nonempty simplices, with face numbers
\[
(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(12,28,8).
\]
There are 37 distinct order-induced matchings, of which 18 are inclusion-maximal. These counts are verified directly from the defining rule in the self-contained `verify.py`, rather than postulated from a separately indexed table.

For clarity, the following list specifies eight **elementary simplicial collapses** in succession. In each row, the left two incidences form a free edge, and the right three incidences form its unique containing triangle at the moment of removal. The tuples are the ordered incidences defined above.

```
free edge                    unique triangle
(3,0) (4,2)                (2,3) (3,0) (4,2)
(1,0) (3,0)                (1,0) (3,0) (4,1)
(3,2) (4,2)                (0,3) (3,2) (4,2)
(2,4) (3,2)                (0,3) (2,4) (3,2)
(2,3) (4,2)                (0,3) (2,3) (4,2)
(1,0) (4,1)                (0,3) (1,0) (4,1)
(3,0) (4,1)                (0,1) (3,0) (4,1)
(1,4) (3,0)                (0,1) (1,4) (3,0)
```

The checker explicitly tests after each deletion that the free edge has exactly the indicated strict coface; hence these are actual elementary collapses, preserving simple homotopy type. Every triangle is thereby removed, together with one distinct edge. The remaining complex has 12 vertices and 20 edges, with no higher-dimensional faces. Its connectivity is established by an explicit graph traversal in the certificate. A connected graph of \(V\) vertices and \(E\) edges is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of \(E-V+1\) circles. Therefore the remaining graph, and the original strong Morse complex, have homotopy type \(\bigvee^9 S^1\).

To compute the reflection action without choosing generators, the checker applies \(\rho\) to the 12 oriented-incidence vertices and then to every oriented simplex. The signed traces on the simplicial chain groups \(C_0,C_1,C_2\) are each zero. Hence its Lefschetz number is zero. As the complex is connected and has homology only in degrees zero and one, the Lefschetz formula gives \(0=1-\operatorname{tr}(\rho_*|H_1)\); thus that trace equals one. Since \(\rho_*^2=1\) over \(\mathbb Q\), the dimensions of the positive and negative eigenspaces solve \(a+b=9\), \(a-b=1\), and are \(a=5,b=4\).

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` from the extracted package. The script independently enumerates all 120 vertex orders and their subfaces, confirms the exact face vector and 18 maximal order-induced matchings, verifies each of the eight free-face cancellations and connected graph spine, and computes the signed reflection chain traces. Its expected terminal line starts with `VERIFY_OK` and ends with `reflection_eigenspaces=(5,4)`. All assertions are exact combinatorial checks using only Python's standard library. The proof is finite and exhaustive for the specified five-vertex graph; it is not an empirical extrapolation to arbitrary graphs.

## Relationship to prior work
Knudson and Owens-White introduced the full strong matching complex and studied paths, cycles, stars, and simplex-related examples (arXiv:2609.06844v1). Their 2026 study supplies the definition, but does not state the present house-graph classification. The ordinary discrete Morse complex of the same house graph has been reported as a wedge of five three-spheres in an indexed October 11, 2026 result; this is the **larger ordinary complex**, not \(\mathcal{SM}(H)\), so its homotopy statement does not imply this result. The exact contrast on the *same graph*—a degree-one nine-circle wedge versus an ordinary degree-three five-sphere wedge—makes the strong-versus-ordinary distinction concrete. The 2025 strong Morse theory of Fernández supplies background for the vertex-order formulation, not this calculation. Relevant earlier complete-bipartite, complete-multipartite, and star calculations do not include \(H\): its triangle and induced four-cycle prevent it from being complete multipartite, and it has neither the graph nor the object of those classifications.

## Limitations
This is an exact finite classification for the unfilled five-vertex house graph, not a theorem about all roofed cycles, all chordal graphs, arbitrary graph orientations, or the pure strong complex. The rational reflection eigenspace multiplicities do not establish a splitting into integral sign modules. The novelty comparison cannot exclude mathematically equivalent unindexed treatments. No independent mathematical audit is claimed.
