# Review of the three-dimensional barycentric Schur–Horn criterion

## Correctness — PASS

The complete geometric classification follows from the independently identified equality reduction in Kania, Theorem 4.2 and Proposition 5.2, followed by a direct parametrization of all barycentric orthonormal frames in dimension three. The frame is a rotating equilateral triangle in the plane perpendicular to the prescribed normal. Its three diagonal equalities reduce, without approximation, to a single complex phase equation. Equal modulus of the two coefficients is both necessary and sufficient, including the exceptional zero-amplitude case. Traces of the tangent compression eliminate coordinates and establish the quartic. The uniqueness statement accounts for all permutations and frame orientations and specifies the rotational degeneracy. An exact arithmetic witness has \(C=\operatorname{diag}(1,2,3)\) and \(y=(1,2,1)/\sqrt6\). The standalone verification program passed 607 checks. No sampled computation substitutes for the exact universal proof.

## Originality — PASS

Kania's complete open-access article, Sections 4–6 and Problem 6.1, directly identifies the restricted barycentric Schur–Horn gap. Kania proves the unrestricted maximal-surface-area value and the eigenvector obstruction (Theorem 5.5) but does not give the all-direction dimension-three criterion or the complete uniqueness classification. The new quartic does not follow merely from the unrestricted Schur–Horn theorem: that theorem lacks the prescribed-barycentre constraint. The planar Connes–Zagier result concerns perimeter in dimension two. Mathematical record searches for the original problem, for an equivalent tangent-spectrum equation, and for quartic locus formulas did not retrieve equivalent coverage. The strongest residual risk is an equivalent representation elsewhere under different terminology or a contemporaneous result not indexed in the checked sources; search absence is not a proof of historical novelty.

## Value — PASS

The question is explicitly posed in a recent primary article as an unsolved restricted Schur–Horn problem. The result gives a necessary-and-sufficient *intrinsic* test for all ellipsoids and all prescribed boundary points in the first dimension where global facet-area maximization can fail. Its quartic condition can be evaluated without searching over bases, and the proof identifies the number of geometric maximizers: one generically on the solution locus and a rotational family only under exact symmetry. This is a complete natural classification, not a numeric instance or a reparametrization of a known theorem.
