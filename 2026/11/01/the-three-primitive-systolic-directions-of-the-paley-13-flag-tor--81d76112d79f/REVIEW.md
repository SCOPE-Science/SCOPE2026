# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The quotient of the planar triangular tessellation by the rank-two index-13 deck lattice is identified explicitly with the flag Paley-13 complex. Each elementary edge step changes each of the three hexagonal norm coordinates by at most one; constructive edge paths realize the bound for every lattice vector. Exact inequalities classify the six nonzero norm-four lattice vectors. Saturating a coordinate bounds the four-step words to three explicit cyclic families, each with 13 simple carriers. The order-six affine multiplier links their three primitive homology directions. Independently, the included exhaustive verifier counts 78 simple quadrilaterals, exactly 39 essential, and tests all affine actions and the homology rank. The proof does not infer the infinite norm claim from finite tests.

## Originality
**PASS, with residual risk.** The foundational published cyclic-torus description and the general equivelar triangular-lattice framework are prior work. The previously published flag criterion identifies this complex but not its systolic shell; the previously published one-direction systolic theorem excludes 13 vertices by assuming at least 17. The 2023 general torus-spectrum algorithm proves existence and computability of such norms, not the three primitive classes and exact 39-carrier census for this complex. Actual semantic and alias searches identified no same-object statement proving the present classification. An older implicit or unindexed specialization of equivelar torus maps is the main unresolved risk; no absence of literature is treated as proof of priority.

## Value
**PASS.** The thirteen-vertex example is the first flag member of a canonical cyclic torus triangulation family, and, unusually, all three primitive homology directions occur at the shortest combinatorial length. Their exact 13+13+13 distribution and transitive symmetry distinguish this exceptional flag member from the one-direction behavior proved for the family's stable tail. The complete marked length norm further gives exact nonprimitive as well as primitive closed-walk minima. These are natural topology and combinatorial-length invariants for a canonical regular torus map, not arbitrary parameter slices.

## Closest literature and limits
The description of the torus, its universal cover and general norm techniques are acknowledged as known, rather than presented as independent inventions. The assertion of complete carrier classification concerns nonzero four-cycles only, and the all-class length formula concerns closed walks. The finite verifier is exhaustive only for the finite graph and is not a substitute for the all-class proof. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
