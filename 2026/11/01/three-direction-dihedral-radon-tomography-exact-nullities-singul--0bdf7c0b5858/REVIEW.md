# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The normal operator is derived from distinct cosets, fixing the potentially important weighting by subgroup size. Unit-circle Fourier diagonalization supplies exactly \(2n\) eigenvalues. Their zero multiplicities count solutions of \(ka\equiv0\pmod n\), giving \(\gcd(a,n)-1\) with no hidden exceptional parity case. A two-step scalar minimax argument proves uniqueness of the optimal direction weights. Independently built coset matrices and exact modular ranks verify representative orders and noncoprime gaps. These checks are finite and are not used as a substitute for proof.

## Originality

PASS under bounded source review. The closest primary work is Ilmavirta (arXiv:1411.3829, initially submitted 2014-11-14): Proposition 5.1 reconstructs dihedral functions with all reflection directions and Section 7.3 studies maximal cyclic subgroup lines. Neither inspected section supplies the three-direction arithmetic defect classification, weighted normal spectrum, or unique sharp family-weight optimum. Fill's earlier cyclic Radon work provides a related Fourier-convolution approach in a different abelian setting. Published records on cyclic projective tomography and three-tap generalized-dihedral convolution have different objects and sampling operators and do not directly imply this result. An older equivalent treatment of matching-cycle tomography remains a residual risk.

## Value

PASS. Determining the least number of full maximal-cyclic directions needed for injective dihedral tomography is a natural finite inverse problem; the exact gap-dependent nullity locates every arithmetic failure. The complete singular spectrum quantifies how close minimally directional recovery is to singularity, and the uniquely sharp positive weighting improves the asymptotic stability exponent. The statement addresses a natural complete family rather than an arbitrary finite census or a renamed textbook identity.

## Limitations

Scalar weights are applied uniformly to each full direction family. The theorem does not optimize independent weights of individual cosets or establish uniqueness among non-coset measurement designs. The comparison with previous work cannot rule out every unpublished or less readily searchable treatment. Independent validation and audit are not yet performed.
