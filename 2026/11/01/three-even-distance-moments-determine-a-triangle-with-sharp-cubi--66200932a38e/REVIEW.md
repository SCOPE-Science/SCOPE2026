# Review: three even distance moments determine a triangle

## Correctness
**PASS.** Independent area-uniform points have independent Dirichlet barycentric coordinates. The exact mixed Dirichlet factorial moments give coefficient-by-coefficient identities for the second, fourth and sixth Euclidean distance moments. Solving the three triangular polynomial relations produces the elementary symmetric functions of squared side lengths; the monic cubic then recovers the full side-length multiset by side-side-side congruence. Two explicit noncongruent strict triangles share the first two moments, proving minimality. A root-disk/Rouché argument establishes uniform \(1/3\)-Hölder continuity on bounded squared-side domains. The two comparison triangles differ in their squared sides by order \(r\) and in their three moments by order \(r^3\), proving optimality. The packaged checker verifies the finite factorial expansions as rational polynomial identities, not numerical fits.

## Originality
**PASS, with non-exhaustive historical-priority risk.** Bonnet–Gusakova–Thäle–Zaporozhets (2020, arXiv:2010.03351) prove sharp first-moment inequalities. Bäsel (2021, arXiv:2101.03815) computes moments for *regular* polygons; his exact 22-page PDF was inspected, including the moment-formula sections and examples. Tong–Ahmadi–Pan (2013, arXiv:1312.2498) and Pure–Durrani–Tong–Pan (2021/2022, DOI:10.1002/mma.7951) develop *forward* distance distributions for arbitrary triangles/polygons. They do not establish, in accessible material, a finite three-moment inverse reconstruction theorem and its sharp stability. Gates (1982, DOI:10.2307/3213843) proves triangle recognition from odd *line-chord* moments, not explicitly from second, fourth and sixth *interior pair-distance* moments. His complete paper was inaccessible; exact finite-moment implications remain a risk. Relevant indexed results on area-uniform versus boundary-uniform second moments and on unlabeled projection width distributions treat different observables. No stronger directly dominating implication was identified, but this is not a proof of historical uniqueness.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** This solves a well-defined finite-data shape-identification problem for every nondegenerate triangle, rather than giving only forward moments. It specifies an explicit three-number reconstruction algorithm, proves three necessary within the natural hierarchy of even moments, and determines the exact worst-case inverse conditioning exponent near a canonical symmetric extremizer. The result can support geometric moment tomography and distinguish information content of random point distances from chord and projection data. Value does not rest on code correctness or a novelty-by-search claim.

## Limits and closest precedents
The claim assumes two independent area-uniform points in a fixed triangle, exact population moments, and Euclidean distance. Squared-side stability constants require an upper size bound; direct side-length stability additionally requires lower size control. Existing all-polygon forward distribution formulas are stronger as *forward* descriptions but do not state the minimal finite-moment inverse. The 1982 chord-recognition theorem is the principal unresolved historical comparison. Independent validation has not been undertaken.
