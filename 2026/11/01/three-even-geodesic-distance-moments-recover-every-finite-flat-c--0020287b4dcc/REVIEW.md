# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The product distance is exactly the sum of independent squared angular-shortest-arc and axial-difference distances. The exact first three even moments follow by elementary integration. The twin-root identity has strictly positive separating factor; the complete two-root interval is checked including boundary cases. The analytic proof establishes uniqueness for every positive pair and local smooth inverse behavior. Reproducibility is independently tested by the embedded standalone verifier; numerical quadrature is supplemental rather than an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS under bounded literature comparison.** Full accessible web texts of Venet's cylinder paper (particularly Sections 1–3 and its stated results), Mémoli–Needham (definitions, Section 2.3, manifold rigidity in Section 3), and Balch–Peterson–Shonkwiler (initial main theorems/moment material) were considered by exact object and implication. Venet's identical product cylinder metric is used for a non-definiteness result, not a distance-distribution inverse; Mémoli–Needham addresses general distance histograms and sphere/closed-manifold questions, not this finite-statistic boundary-cylinder theorem; Balch et al. concern homogeneous lens spaces. Closely related mathematical-result records concern solid-cylinder Euclidean statistics, flat-torus full distributions, or external shadows, none of which implies this finite-moment result. Unindexed equivalent computations remain a genuine originality risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** A natural metric-measure inverse problem is completely solved by a sharp *finite* number of scalar observables, with a precisely characterized failure set for one fewer statistic and a smooth exact inverse. The two-to-one fold is a nontrivial geometric identifiability boundary for a flat manifold with boundary; it is not merely a single-parameter numerical calculation.
