# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The graph is explicitly the categorical product, with all vertices and edge predicates specified. The executable checker enumerates all 5,281, 18,721 and 216,257 faces independently cross-checked by cyclic layer transfer counts; it verifies each selected matching pair and every directed face incidence, finding no directed cycle. The critical cells and full signed integral Morse matrices are reproduced and every asserted rank has a unit determinant maximal minor, so no nonunit Smith factor or Moore attachment survives. The homotopy argument uses the Hurewicz description of attachments to a simply connected wedge of spheres, with all lower cells absent. Direct mod-two simplicial boundary calculations agree in every dimension. The limits are fixed finite cycle lengths, not an infinite conjecture.

## Originality — PASS, subject to bibliographic limits

Antolín Camarena and Carnero Bravo (2023/2024), Theorems 22–23 and Question 24, already identify the possible homological degrees and note nonzero free homology for these exact cases, but do not determine the integral differentials or exclude Moore spaces; the 2025 path-product theorem has different graphs. A separately published four-color seven-cycle calculation has a different factor size. Comparison against public mathematical research records and searches for categorical/tensor/Kronecker product aliases identified no stronger theorem that implies these three homotopy classifications. This is not a claim that earlier authors' unpublished computations lacked the answer. Equivalent unindexed work remains possible.

## Value — PASS

These are exactly the first three lengths after the established small-cycle and divisible-by-three regimes, for the first complete-graph factor beyond an edge. They cover both open cycle congruence classes and extend a specifically published torsion-freeness problem with complete integral homotopy classifications, not merely repeated finite-field ranks. The three canonical cases form a motivated finite cutoff, not arbitrary parameter perturbations. An infinite-family theorem, equivariant enhancement, and all-color statement remain open here.
