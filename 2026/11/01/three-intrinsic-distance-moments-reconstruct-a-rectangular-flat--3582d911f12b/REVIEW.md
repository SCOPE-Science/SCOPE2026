# Scientific review of finite-moment tomography for rectangular flat three-tori

## Correctness — PASS
The distributional reduction uses the unique Haar difference of two independent points and the shortest representatives in each product circle. The raw moments and three cumulants of a squared uniform coordinate are exact. Newton's identities reconstruct the positive period-square multiset, and the cubic-discriminant condition is necessary and sufficient, including repeated roots. The plane–sphere intersection proves two-moment nonidentifiability away from cubes. Rouché root counting gives a uniform cubic-root inverse bound, and explicit near-cubic families show its exponent is optimal. The standalone exact-rational and independent direct-geodesic-sampling checks passed. Risk: computational samples alone would not prove the universal statements; all such statements are supported instead by analytic arguments in RESULT.md.

## Originality — PASS, historical priority provisional
Mémoli–Needham (arXiv:1810.09646, Example 3.18, full proof material inspected) discuss equal global distance histograms using nonorthogonal flat two-dimensional lattices. Their argument does not imply or cover exact reconstruction in the special class of orthogonal three-tori, independently of the claimed metric inequivalence of those particular examples. Glorieux–Yarmola (arXiv:2003.08190, complete research text inspected) concern random triangle homotopy and a different probability. Holmin–Kurlberg–Månsson (arXiv:1702.08096) concern linear-motion free paths in boxes, a distinct observable. Indexed mathematical findings on rectangular-torus systoles, orthotope projection-volume moments, and random geodesic distances on other quotient geometries were compared and none contains this conclusion. A formula under other older terminology could still be found; no exhaustive priority claim is warranted.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Against the broader inverse-metric-measure problem, the theorem gives a complete positive classification for a familiar, nontrivial three-dimensional orthogonal subfamily with only three scalar observations. It also identifies the sharp exact data region, proves two moments cannot generically suffice, and gives a worst-case optimally sharp stability exponent. These are meaningful structural inverse problems, not merely evaluation of a known forward integral.

## Scope and remaining risks
The argument relies on product-lattice geometry and exact population moments; general flat tori and finite-sample noisy inversion are not covered. Older unindexed equivalent moment inversion could alter the historical-priority assessment. No independent mathematical validation has been performed.
