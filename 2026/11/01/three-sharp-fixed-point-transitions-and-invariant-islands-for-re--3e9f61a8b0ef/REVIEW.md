# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

For a reduced Euclidean planar rotation, a fixed point satisfies a linear integer-displacement equation. Direct inversion and sharp coordinate bounds leave only four possible nonzero integer displacement labels. One is excluded by the half-open-square convention, and the other three give exact, strict threshold inequalities. Reflection treats opposite rotation orientation; the half-turn is evaluated separately. For every interior center, rotation of its largest inscribed Euclidean disk stays within the same modulo branch, proving invariance; exact center separation proves disjointness and hence the invariant-area identity. All boundary cases in the stated angular domain are addressed. The bundled exact-rational checker reproduces 23 representative classifications, including all thresholds, but the continuous-parameter conclusion rests on the proof rather than the finite computations.

## Originality — PASS within checked evidence

The nearest same-system source, M. Blank, *Invariant measures of torus piecewise isometries*, arXiv:2106.16021, Section 7, discusses the identical reduced planar rotation. Proposition 7.1 gives a sufficient range for a nontrivial fixed point, and Propositions 7.2–7.3 prove local invariant-neighborhood statements. Neither the exhaustive three-threshold count, complete center table, nor the explicit area of all the listed disjoint fixed-center disks occurs in the inspected material. The distinct model of J. H. Lowenstein and F. Vivaldi, arXiv:0909.3458, has a different rotation matrix, reduction mechanism, and induced-map theory; its periodic island-area results do not imply this fixed-center angular classification. Searches of indexed research claims and public literature did not identify an equivalent or stronger covering assertion. Because indexing and inspection are not exhaustive, a previously available elementary lattice computation remains a genuine priority risk.

## Value — PASS

The source's only nontrivial-fixed-point existence range leaves open the full angular parameter diagram of the simplest nonextendable planar rotation modulo the square. The result gives an exhaustive natural classification, precisely locates three separate births and quantifies the invariant-area contribution of the resulting rotation islands. Invariant area is dynamically meaningful because it immediately supplies explicit absolutely continuous stationary probabilities with disjoint supports, showing angular dependence of their constructible multiplicity without relying on weak-limit existence methods. The claim does not artificially choose exceptional parameter values; every nontrivial angle is treated, with sharp transition boundaries.

## Limits and remaining risks

The fixed-point census is not a periodic-orbit census and the disks need not exhaust invariant area. Results for other piecewise-isometric families must not be transplanted without proving a conjugacy, and a hidden equivalent elementary fixed-point table is not ruled out by negative searches. Numerical checks are illustrative exact-rational spot checks, not an exhaustion over all angles. The conclusion should receive independent priority and mathematical review before publication.
