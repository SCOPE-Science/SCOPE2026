# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact zero-sum powered-distance quadratic form splits into two strictly negative within-class fluctuation terms and one scalar group-contrast term. The scalar term gives the necessary and sufficient all-parameter test, and strict convexity in exponent proves its unique first crossing. The endpoint kernel is precisely the one-dimensional class contrast. A convex quadratic in the length-imbalance parameter determines the exact Euclidean region; a compactness argument on the rescaled root gives both one-sided singular constants. Exact rational checks (480) and 20 independent Gram-matrix profiles corroborate the derivation. The analytical proof, not those finite tests, handles all integer class sizes and real positive lengths.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The closest primary predecessor is Faver et al. (2014), Proposition 4.4 (arXiv:1201.6669), which calculates a leaf metric with one exceptional arm, and establishes the ultrametric/infinite-roundness correspondence. Its result is not the present two non-singleton class classification. Sánchez (arXiv:1108.0451) provides general matrix criteria and an unweighted complete-bipartite graph example; Robertson (arXiv:2004.10923) gives general determinant criteria. These results can be used as tools, but their inspected statements do not evaluate the present full formula, exact Euclidean window and asymmetric singular limits. Earlier accessible findings involving a weighted star **with its hub included** do not imply the leaf-only threshold: at equal arm lengths, the leaf space is equilateral and has infinite roundness, while the entire star has finite roundness. Relevant research-index and phrase searches revealed no equivalent evaluated theorem. Publication-priority risk remains for unindexed or differently formulated work.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Leaf distances on weighted trees are standard natural objects in additive metric geometry. The distinction between one exceptional arm and two populated classes matters: the present classification reveals an interval of exactly Euclidean metrics, a one-dimensional boundary collapse, and direction-dependent inverse-logarithmic divergence as the metric approaches an ultrametric. These properties determine sharp geometric embeddings and cannot be read from the earlier one-exceptional formula or from results for the full star. The theorem is a complete family classification, not a single ad hoc numerical instance.

## Scope and limitations
Only leaf-only stars with exactly two positive arm-length classes of multiplicity at least two are classified. Existing general finite-metric determinant methods may reproduce the calculation once the particular symmetry is supplied. An unindexed prior derivation could affect novelty; the searches do not establish exhaustive worldwide priority. Finite tests do not certify the proof for infinite parameter ranges.
