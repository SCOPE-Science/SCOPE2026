# Scientific review: exact prime-two hyperbolic spectrum

## Correctness — PASS
The phase identity reduces the antipodal period-two condition to a reciprocal quartic, then a quadratic in \(2\cos(2\pi s)\). The two real cosine roots yield exactly four nonfixed points. A separate global exhaustion argument shows that every two-step derivative is positive and determinant one, hence has trace strictly greater than two: the fixed-point count is constant by compactness and local implicit continuation, and its cat-map value is five. The odd symmetry and strict positivity of \(1+f'_\mu\) establish that one of those five is the unique ordinary fixed point. The remaining four are the two explicit orbits. Direct Jacobian calculation gives their exponents; exact Taylor analysis gives the two endpoint coefficients and the \(1+\sqrt2\) ratio. The verification script checks the critical polynomial, derivative identities and representative finite orbits, but does not substitute for the infinitary argument.

Orbit-resolved Lyapunov spectra distinguish spatial nonuniformity of hyperbolic expansion from the invariant-measure mixing spectrum. Exact classification of **all** prime-two cycles is a natural low-period dynamical invariant of the full torus, not a chosen numerical sample. The strict difference of their multipliers for every nonzero deformation is a concrete finite-orbit manifestation of the nonlinear spatial expansion studied in the 2024 work, and the two distinct \(\sqrt{1-\mu}\) edge coefficients yield a nontrivial universal limiting ratio. These are exact structural data unavailable from the fixed-point trace and numerical splitting plots alone.
