# Scientific review: two-even-moment spherical-cap inversion

## Correctness — PASS

The surface-area coordinate \(U_3\) is uniform on \([z,1]\) and azimuth is independent and uniform. The identity \(D^2=2R^2(1-U\cdot V)\) yields both moment polynomials from first and second axial moments. Their normalized quotient factors into \(4(1+t^2)/3\) where \(t=(z+1)/(z+3)\), so feasibility and uniqueness are exact. The stability proof establishes a rational comparison for every \(0\le s<t<1/2\), and limiting families establish optimality of the global cut-height constant and both exponents. The supplied executable repeats 2,206 exact rational comparisons and direct four-dimensional integrations. Finite checks support, but do not replace, the universal proof. The endpoints \(z=-1\) and \(z\uparrow1\) are distinguished correctly.

## Originality — PASS, historical priority provisional

The source inspected in full at arXiv:1411.5639v1 provides whole-sphere *forward* chord distributions and even moments; it covers our full-sphere endpoint, but not the proper-cap inverse, feasible region, or optimal stability. The inspected introduction, main theorem, spherical-cap corollary and proof of arXiv:2007.07297v1 provide a *forward* geodesic-distance distribution on prescribed convex spherical caps. This is stronger forward data for that restricted cap class, and may be used to derive some moment identities, but it does not state the two-moment inverse theorem, the entire large-cap regime, sharp global reconstruction constant, or quarter-power degeneracy. Actual semantic searches of the research record and primary literature did not reveal direct identical coverage. A result embedded in an unindexed appendix, or an elementary inversion previously published without the word “tomography”, remains a real priority risk. This is not an independently certified novelty verdict.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The natural inverse problem asks whether two scale-sensitive observations of random distances can recover both cap shape and absolute sphere size. The exact sharp feasible region and explicit inverse provide a complete answer in a standard two-parameter geometric family. A second, not merely numerical, contribution classifies two different optimal conditioning exponents, including a quarter-power singularity at the complete sphere. The latter determines a genuine boundary for stable cap inference, rather than being an arbitrary parameter restriction or table recomputation. The model deliberately restricts to surface-area-uniform sampling and Euclidean chords; higher-dimensional and noisy-data problems remain open here.
