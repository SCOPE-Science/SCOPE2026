# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact Haar difference is uniform on the Euclidean Voronoi cell, giving the radial area CDF and recovering covolume at small radii. The angular exclusion profile is determined by the derivative. The Voronoi-relevant-vector parity criterion shows that every facet foot lies strictly inside its facet, so each newly activated excluded arc has a disjoint angular neighborhood and contributes a positive \(2\sqrt{2/h}\)-scaled square-root cusp; existing arc endpoints, including those meeting at vertices, change at most linearly. The full multiset of facet supports follows, and the established planar obtuse-superbase theorem reconstructs the isometry class, also when lengths coincide. These arguments are quantified for all full-rank rank-two Euclidean lattices; finite numerical tests are corroboration, not proof.

Primary full-text comparisons were made with the distinct focal-decomposition rigidity theorem of Kwakkel–Martens–Peixoto (arXiv:1104.5541), Bonifas–Dadush's Voronoi algorithms (arXiv:1412.6168), Leipold–Lazar–Brakke–Srolovitz's perturbed Voronoi topology (arXiv:1509.03280v1), and Kurlin's 2022 complete lattice invariants (doi:10.1007/s10208-022-09601-8). Kurlin's nearest-lattice-neighbor distance sequence is not the normalized Haar two-point quotient-distance law. The complete Voronoi length invariant is an acknowledged prior result, while its extraction from this new probabilistic observable is the result under review. Exact-claim searches through the published mathematical findings corpus and earlier mathematical statements found no direct cover. The classification partition alone cannot establish novelty. ## Value — PASS

The natural one-variable two-point distance distribution turns out to be a complete Euclidean isometry invariant for the entire two-parameter planar-flat-torus moduli space, not merely a moment or a generic-case classifier. The theorem gives a computable-in-principle inversion of geometric features from analytically detected cusps, including square, rectangular, and hexagonal degeneracies. The lack of finite-sample stability and absence of higher-dimensional claims are explicit limitations.
