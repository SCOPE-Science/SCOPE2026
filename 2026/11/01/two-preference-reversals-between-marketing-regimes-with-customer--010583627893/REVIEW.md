# Scientific review: marketing-regime order under customer attrition

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed domain is adopter fractions in \([0,1]\), strictly positive innovation, imitation, attrition and durations. The scalar vector fields point inward at both endpoints. The matrix lift is checked explicitly: \(L_i^2=(\sigma_i^2/4)I\), all four entries of its exponential are positive, and the projective composition difference has a quadratic numerator over a positive denominator. Thus three strict reversals are impossible for every admissible pair. The two exact positive matrices are correctly obtained from the displayed differential equations at the stated logarithmic durations. Their product difference factors as \(128(3697x^2-3962x+493)\), positive/negative/positive at \(0,1/2,1\), yielding exactly two simple interior crossovers. Continuity yields robustness within common attrition. For a common equilibrium, both compositions fix that point and have equal derivatives there; the numerator has a repeated root, preventing preference reversals. The ordinary Bass case is the zero-attrition shared-equilibrium boundary. The verification program reproduces the exact algebra and tests independent numerical flow integration. No extrapolation from finite numerical checks to the universal theorem is made.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical uncertainty

The Bass innovation–imitation equation dates to 1969, and its standard attrition-free form and the Riccati/Möbius representation are prior mathematical knowledge. The polynomial-degree upper bound itself is not presented as a new standalone linear-fractional fact. Fruchter–Van den Bulte (2011), whose relevant marketing-control sections and mathematical appendices were inspected in full-text open access, studies generalized Bass advertising allocation, not the present fixed two-stage positive-abandonment order comparison. Shi–Li–Bigdeli (2016), whose churn modeling and application sections were inspected in the accessible full text, examines multiple new-product diffusion models with churn but does not establish the equal-attrition interior two-crossover witness or the shared-equilibrium rigidity. The Bass publisher abstract/source information does not provide full original text, so no whole-paper exclusion is claimed. Search comparisons of published mathematical research summaries concern different positive Riccati or finite switching models and do not dominate the constrained example. The material contribution is an exact application-specific phenomenon with strict interior reversals despite equal attrition, its openness, and the boundary obstruction. Historical priority is provisional: an unlocated more general composition theorem may already imply this special case.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Campaign sequencing in an innovation–imitation model with customer exits is naturally a state-dependent decision: two prescribed interventions may swap in effectiveness depending on the current adopted fraction. Proving that there can be **two** distinct reversals even with equal departure rates is a counterexample to the tempting expectation of a single switching threshold. The upper bound proves exact maximality, openness rules out tuning artifacts, and the shared-equilibrium case identifies the structural reason classic no-attrition diffusion cannot exhibit it. These results produce a complete sharp ceiling plus a motivated attainable configuration, without claiming a broad optimal-control theorem or empirical predictive relevance. The model is idealized, and the finding does not imply observed marketing practices exhibit either reversal.

## Comparison risks and limitations

The closest mathematical prior work concerns general Riccati flows and positive linear-fractional maps: their degree-two difference already yields the elementary ceiling, and broader order-comparison results might subsume the exact witness. Fruchter–Van den Bulte 2011 concerns advertising optimization without this abandonment-order question. Shi–Li–Bigdeli 2016 concerns churn models but not the compared discrete regime compositions in the inspected sections. Bass 1969 full text was not available in the inspected source, so no assertion about its full body is made. The theorem depends on continuous deterministic dynamics, homogeneous adopters, fixed durations, and instantaneous switching. Its certification remains internal to this scientific review; external independent review has not occurred.
