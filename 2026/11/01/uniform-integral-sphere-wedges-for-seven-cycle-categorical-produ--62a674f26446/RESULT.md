# Uniform integral sphere wedges for seven-cycle categorical products

## Finding
For every integer \(n\geq 2\), let \(G_n=C_7\times K_n\) be the **categorical graph product**, with vertices \((r,c)\in\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z\times\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\) and an edge \((r,c)(s,d)\) if and only if \(r-s\equiv\pm1\pmod 7\) and \(c\ne d\). Its ordinary independence complex has the **integral homotopy classification**
\[
\operatorname{Ind}(G_n)\simeq
\bigvee^{\binom{n-1}{2}} S^3\ \vee\
\bigvee^{\binom n2} S^4.
\]
An empty wedge is understood as a point. Thus reduced integral homology is free of ranks \(\binom{n-1}{2}\) and \(\binom n2\) in degrees three and four, and is zero in all other degrees. In particular the seven-cycle branch, for all color counts, is torsion-free; the stronger homotopy classification is part of the claim.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is finite, simple, and unweighted; the product is categorical, **not** Cartesian or strong. Color labels are distinct, and the seven cycle positions are indexed \(0,\ldots,6\). A face is a set of mutually nonadjacent graph vertices, including the empty face during augmented matching. The statement is for every finite integer \(n\ge2\), not just the tested values; no assertion is made for other cycle lengths. We use the standard discrete-Morse correspondence between an acyclic matching and a CW model, with signed integral Morse incidence numbers.

## Proof
**1. A first-color matching and an upper filter.** Fix color zero. For every independent face \(\sigma\), let \(T\) denote its vertices whose colors are nonzero and let \(R\subseteq\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z\) be the set of cycle positions occupied by \(T\). The zero-colored vertices that can be added independently are exactly those at positions outside \(N_{C_7}(R)\). All zero-colored vertices are mutually nonadjacent. For each fixed \(T\), the faces containing that tail therefore form the full Boolean cube on those available zero-colored positions. If the cube has positive rank, pair its cells by toggling its least available zero-colored vertex; if its rank is zero, leave its sole cell unmatched. The pairing is exactly the first block of the global color-first, row-second greedy matching. It is acyclic: project any directed alternating Hasse cycle onto its fixed nonzero-color tail. Matched steps preserve the tail, whereas downward unmatched steps can only delete tail vertices, so any cycle has constant tail. Within one Boolean cube the least-vertex toggle matching has no directed cycle.

The unmatched faces after this block are precisely the zero-color-free faces for which \(N_{C_7}(R)=\mathbb Z/7\mathbb Z\), i.e. \(R\) is a **total dominating set**. Denote this surviving family by \(P\). It is an **upper filter** in the face poset: every coface of a face of \(P\) must still omit color zero, since an occupied neighbor of every row obstructs adding color zero. The coface still has total-dominating support. Every oriented Hasse edge connecting \(P\) to the removed part points outward from \(P\); since no first-block pair crosses this boundary, a directed gradient cycle cannot cross it and return.

**2. At most two colors survive, and sectors do not interact.** In a face of \(P\), each occupied row must have a neighboring occupied row. If two neighboring rows are occupied, the independence condition forces **each** to contain exactly one vertex and forces their colors to agree. Thus the occupied rows break into cyclic runs of length at least two, each monochromatic, with a vacant row between distinct runs. Three runs would require at least nine cycle positions. Since there are only seven, at most two runs and hence at most two nonzero colors occur. A new previously unused nonzero color cannot be added to a face of \(P\): at each row, an already occupied neighboring row has a different color. Therefore each cover incidence inside \(P\) preserves the exact **set of occurring colors** (and a deletion between two faces in \(P\) is the reverse of such an addition). The face poset \(P\) is a disjoint union of independent sectors, one for each one-element or two-element subset of the \(n-1\) nonzero colors.

**3. Fixed finite sector enumeration and acyclicity.** Within each sector, first normalize the available colors increasingly as \(1\) or \(1,2\). A sector face is specified by a word \((a_0,\ldots,a_6)\) in \(\{0,1\}\) or \(\{0,1,2\}\), where zero denotes a vacant row. Enumerate all \(2^7\), respectively \(3^7\), words, retaining exactly those with (i) every listed positive color occurring, (ii) adjacent positive entries equal, and (iii) for every \(i\), at least one of \(a_{i-1},a_{i+1}\) positive (indices modulo seven). There are exactly **29** one-color and **28** two-color faces. Greedily toggle the vertices in increasing color, then increasing row, in each surviving sector. Full oriented-Hasse acyclicity checks on these finite posets leave:

| Sector | Faces | Critical cells, specified by row word | Degrees |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| One nonzero color | 29 | \((1,1,0,0,1,1,1)\) | \(4\) |
| Two nonzero colors \(1<2\) | 28 | \((1,1,0,2,2,0,0)\), \((0,2,2,2,0,1,1)\) | \(3\), \(4\) |

These finite facts and the edge orientations are checked by the dependency-free, exhaustive `verify.py`, including a topological-sort proof of acyclicity. The words are a **complete finite enumeration**, not a numerical extrapolation in \(n\): every sector for any \(n\) is order-isomorphic to one of these two fixed finite posets, preserving vertex-toggle order and incidence signs. Combining their acyclic matchings with the first-color block is acyclic by the upper-filter argument in step 1. The empty simplex is paired at the first toggle; dropping that pair from the ordinary nonempty matching creates a single critical vertex and cannot create a cycle.

**4. Exact integral attaching degrees.** The only critical cells above the base vertex are one degree-four cell per nonzero color, one degree-three cell per unordered pair of nonzero colors, and one degree-four cell per unordered pair. To compute a degree-four-to-degree-three Morse coefficient, a gradient path departing a critical four-cell into the removed part cannot return to the upper filter \(P\). Every gradient path remaining in \(P\) preserves its occurring color set. Consequently the Morse boundary is block-diagonal across the two-color sectors; a one-color critical four-cell has no possible degree-three target. There is one possible coefficient per two-color sector. In its **entire 28-face poset**, calculate the integral signed Morse reduction. For the critical degree-four word \((0,2,2,2,0,1,1)\), deleting the entries in rows \(1,2,3,5,6\) has, in that order, projected contributions \((-1,0,+1,0,0)\) to the critical three-cell \((1,1,0,2,2,0,0)\); the sum is **zero over \(\mathbb Z\)**. The fully specified finite computation and a full-face independent cross-check appear in `verify.py`. The standard row-major vertex orientation is used. Each sector with two colors has the same relative vertex order, so all its coefficients vanish. Hence the complete integral Morse boundary \(\partial_4\) is zero **for all \(n\)**.

**5. From the Morse complex to homotopy.** The resulting CW model has one zero-cell, \(\binom{n-1}{2}\) three-cells and \((n-1)+\binom{n-1}{2}=\binom n2\) four-cells, with no other cells. Its three-skeleton is a wedge of three-spheres. The Hurewicz isomorphism for that two-connected three-skeleton identifies each four-cell attaching homotopy class with its integral cellular boundary column. Because every column is zero, all four-cell attaching maps are nullhomotopic. This proves the displayed integral homotopy equivalence (including the \(n=2\) case with no three-cells).

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with Python 3.9 or later; there are no third-party dependencies. The checker exhaustively enumerates the 29/28 finite sector faces, checks all cover incidences and acyclic directed graphs, and computes their exact signed integer Morse boundary \([0]\). It separately enumerates **all** augmented faces of the full independence complex and all face-poset incidences for \(n=2,3,4,5\), checks the full greedy matching is acyclic and has exactly the predicted critical cells, and verifies exact zero integer top Morse matrices of sizes \(0\times1,1\times3,3\times6,6\times10\). All assertions must hold before the output concludes `C7_KN_UNIFORM_MORSE_CERTIFICATE_OK`. The checked output is included in `verification_output.txt`. These full-complex checks are finite corroborations; the proof for every \(n\) is the upper-filter reduction to the exhaustively checked **parameter-independent** sectors.

## Relationship to prior work
Antolín Camarena and Carnero Bravo (arXiv:2307.12401, first posted July 23, 2023), Theorem 23, prove concentration in two adjacent degrees and admit possible Moore-space torsion for the length-seven cycle; their Question 24 asks whether such product independence complexes are always torsion-free. The same source reports calculations for three, four, and five colors but not a uniform seven-cycle theorem. The present theorem establishes precisely that uniform seven-cycle subfamily, resolving the corresponding torsion question there, **not** the all-cycle question.

A separately indexed seven-cycle, four-color result already establishes \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_7\times K_4)\simeq \bigvee^3 S^3\vee\bigvee^6S^4\); that special case and previously obtained three-/four-color symmetry refinements are **not** claimed as new. The new content is the all-color formula and the parameter-independent two-color-sector proof. The 2025 work on graph products studies categorical products with a path factor; the 2026 generalized-Mycielskian paper studies a different construction. No inspected source supplies the theorem for arbitrary \(n\), although bibliographic completeness and independent mathematical audit are not claimed.

## Limitations
This classification says nothing about \(C_k\times K_n\) for \(k\ne7\), the behavior of equivariant automorphism modules, or other graph-product conventions. The computational checker certifies the fixed finite sector lemmas and sample full complexes; its correctness as software, the mathematical upper-filter argument, and all literature comparisons require independent examination before treating the result as independently validated. A possibly unindexed or implicit earlier general theorem remains a residual priority risk.
