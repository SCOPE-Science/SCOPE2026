# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The rational Zak-fiber representation uses the standard convention with \(\lambda=2/5\) and \(1/\sqrt2\) normalization, giving a net factor \(1/\sqrt5\). Exact Laurent determinants were rederived for \(q=3\), \(q=5\), and odd \(q\ge7\), and the two unit-circle cubic moduli coincide in a strictly positive polynomial. Uniform trace and Cauchy–Binet inequalities deliver the stated non-optimal frame bounds. The symbolic checker confirms 17 selected identities; the infinite claims rely on the analytical argument.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources
Stangl–Frederick (2026) explicitly leaves the numerator-\(p>1\) converse open. Atindehou–Frederick–Kouagou–Okoudjou (2018 preprint, 2022 journal) give conditions that do not cover \(b=5/2\), \(a\le4/15\). Ghosh–Selvan (2023 preprint, later journal) restrict their explicit fixed-spline region to \(b<2\); their all-density assertion changes the window by a dilation. A separate previously established numerator-two result on \(q=5\) excludes the endpoint \(b=5/2\). An earlier unindexed sufficient result could still imply part of this family; this is a residual originality risk, not a claim of proven world-first uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The complete infinite odd-denominator family with one fixed window and modulation gives an explicit, motivated piece of the unsettled higher-numerator Gabor frame set. Elementary unit-circle certificates convert rank arguments into quantitative bounds, and the exceptional \(q=3\) polynomial is handled analytically rather than omitted. This is more than a numerical frame example.

## Limitations
The bounds are not claimed optimal, the theorem does not classify other modulations or even denominators, and no independent mathematical review has been performed. Comparison rests on the accessible primary theorems and published claim records, not an exhaustive literature census.
