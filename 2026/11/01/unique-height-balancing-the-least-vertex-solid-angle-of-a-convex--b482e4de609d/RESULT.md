# Unique height balancing the least vertex solid angle of a convex pyramid

## Finding

**Theorem (universal height balance).** Let \(d\ge 2\), let \(P\subset\mathbb R^{d-1}\) be a full-dimensional compact convex polytope, and let \(q\in\operatorname{int} P\). For every \(h>0\) form the \(d\)-polytope
\[
K_h=\operatorname{conv}\big((P\times\{0\})\cup\{(q,h)\}\big).
\]
For a vertex \(x\) of a full-dimensional polytope \(K\), set
\[
\omega_K(x)=\gamma_d\big(\operatorname{cone}(K-x)\big),
\]
where \(\gamma_d\) is standard Gaussian probability in \(\mathbb R^d\). Equivalently, this is the vertex solid angle divided by the surface measure of the unit \((d-1)\)-sphere. Put \(a(h)=\omega_{K_h}((q,h))\), \(b_v(h)=\omega_{K_h}((v,0))\) for \(v\in V(P)\), and \(b(h)=\min_{v\in V(P)}b_v(h)\).

Then \(a\) is strictly decreasing and continuous from \(1/2\) to \(0\) as \(h\) runs from zero to infinity. Every \(b_v\) is strictly increasing and continuous from \(0\) to \(\gamma_{d-1}(\operatorname{cone}(P-v))/2\). Consequently there exists exactly one \(h_*=h_*(P,q)>0\) with
\[
a(h_*)=b(h_*).
\]
The function \(h\mapsto\min_{x\in V(K_h)}\omega_{K_h}(x)\) is **strictly increasing** on \((0,h_*)\) and **strictly decreasing** on \((h_*,\infty)\). Hence \(h_*\) is the unique global maximizer of the minimum vertex solid angle. For a fixed \(P\), the optimum \(h_*(P,q)\) depends continuously on \(q\in\operatorname{int}P\).

If \(P\) is a regular \((d-1)\)-simplex of circumradius \(R\) and \(q\) is its circumcenter, then
\[
h_*=R\sqrt{\frac{d+1}{d-1}},
\]
and \(K_{h_*}\) is a regular \(d\)-simplex.

## Assumptions and scope

The base is a fixed full-dimensional convex **polytope**, not a general smooth body; its vertex set is finite. The vertical projection \(q\) of the apex is fixed strictly inside the base. The Euclidean metric and Gaussian (equivalently spherical) solid-angle normalization are used throughout. The theorem concerns optimization over the single height parameter \(h>0\), not arbitrary changes of base or apex projection. The regular-simplex statement is a special case within that constrained family and is not a solution of the unrestricted minimum-solid-angle conjecture.

## Proof

**1. Cross-sections of vertex cones.** Let \(Z\sim N(0,I_{d-1})\) and \(S\sim N(0,1)\) be independent, with density \(\varphi\) for \(S\). At the apex \((q,h)\), a tangent-cone vector with final coordinate \(-s<0\) has horizontal part in \((s/h)(P-q)\). Thus, by conditioning on \(S\),
\[
 a(h)=\int_0^\infty\varphi(s)\,\mathbb P\!\left\{Z\in\frac{s}{h}(P-q)\right\}\,ds.\tag{1}
\]
For a base vertex \(v\), write \(C_v=\operatorname{cone}(P-v)\) and \(w_v=q-v\). The tangent cone at \((v,0)\) is the sum of the horizontal cone \(C_v\times\{0\}\) and the ray generated by \((w_v,h)\). A vector whose vertical coordinate is \(s>0\) therefore has horizontal part in \(C_v+(s/h)w_v\). Consequently
\[
 b_v(h)=\int_0^\infty\varphi(s)\,\mathbb P\!\left\{Z\in C_v+\frac{s}{h}w_v\right\}\,ds.\tag{2}
\]
The integral representations also show continuity in \(h>0\): their integrands converge off polyhedral or convex-body boundaries, which are Gaussian-null, and are uniformly bounded by \(\varphi(s)\). They remain continuous under a change of \(q\) in the interior of \(P\).

**2. Strict apex decrease.** Because \(0\in\operatorname{int}(P-q)\), for each fixed \(s>0\) and \(0<h_1<h_2\) the compact convex sets \((s/h_2)(P-q)\subsetneq(s/h_1)(P-q)\) have a difference of positive \((d-1)\)-dimensional measure. Gaussian density is strictly positive, hence their Gaussian probabilities satisfy a strict inequality. Equation (1) makes \(a(h_1)>a(h_2)\). As \(h\downarrow0\), these dilates exhaust \(\mathbb R^{d-1}\); as \(h\to\infty\), they shrink to the origin. Dominated convergence in (1) gives \(a(0+)=\int_0^\infty\varphi(s)\,ds=1/2\) and \(a(\infty)=0\).

**3. Strict increase of every base angle.** The assumption \(q\in\operatorname{int}P\) implies \(w_v\in\operatorname{int}C_v\) for every base vertex \(v\). The polyhedral cone \(C_v\) is closed, full-dimensional and pointed. For \(t_1>t_2>0\),
\[
 C_v+t_1w_v\ \subsetneq\ C_v+t_2w_v.\tag{3}
\]
Containment holds because \((t_1-t_2)w_v\in C_v\) and the cone is additive. To see that the containment is **strict in Gaussian measure**, choose a nonzero supporting linear functional \(\ell\) nonnegative on \(C_v\). Since \(w_v\) is interior, \(\ell(w_v)>0\). There is a small Euclidean ball around \(t_2w_v+\epsilon w_v\) contained in \(C_v+t_2w_v\) with \(0<\epsilon<t_1-t_2\), and on a sufficiently small such ball we have \(\ell(x)<t_1\ell(w_v)\). In contrast, \(\ell(x)\ge t_1\ell(w_v)\) on \(C_v+t_1w_v\). The difference contains an open ball, so it has positive Gaussian measure. Thus for \(h_1<h_2\), the parameter \(s/h\) strictly decreases and (2) proves \(b_v(h_1)<b_v(h_2)\).

As \(h\downarrow0\), \(t=s/h\to\infty\) for each \(s>0\). The supporting halfspace just used escapes to infinity; the Gaussian probability of \(C_v+t w_v\) tends to zero. As \(h\to\infty\), \(t\downarrow0\), and the indicator of \(C_v+t w_v\) converges almost everywhere to that of \(C_v\), since the cone boundary is Gaussian-null. Dominated convergence in (2) therefore yields
\[
 b_v(0+)=0,\qquad b_v(\infty)=\tfrac12\gamma_{d-1}(C_v)>0.\tag{4}
\]
(The cone has positive Gaussian measure because it is full-dimensional.)

**4. Unique crossing and global maximality.** The minimum of finitely many continuous strictly increasing functions is continuous and strictly increasing: if \(h_2>h_1\) and \(v_2\) minimizes at \(h_2\), then \(b(h_2)=b_{v_2}(h_2)>b_{v_2}(h_1)\ge b(h_1)\). In particular, \(b(0+)=0\) and \(b(\infty)>0\). Therefore \(a-b\) is continuous, strictly decreasing, and goes from \(1/2\) to a negative number. It has exactly one zero \(h_*\). For \(h<h_*\) the minimum of \(a(h)\) and \(b(h)\) equals strictly increasing \(b(h)\); for \(h>h_*\) it equals strictly decreasing \(a(h)\). This proves the strict global optimum. If \(q_j\to q\in\operatorname{int}P\), joint continuity of (1)--(2) and the strictly signed values of \(a-b\) on either side of \(h_*(P,q)\) squeeze their unique zeros, proving continuous dependence on \(q\).

**5. Regular-base equality.** For a regular \((d-1)\)-simplex with circumradius \(R\), the squared length of every base edge is \(2dR^2/(d-1)\), while every lateral edge has squared length \(R^2+h^2\). They coincide exactly at \(h=R\sqrt{(d+1)/(d-1)}\). At that height the \(d\)-simplex is regular; its vertex solid angles are equal by symmetry, so it satisfies the unique balancing equation and must be the unique optimum. \(\square\)

## Verification

Equations (1)--(2) are exact Gaussian integral formulas in every dimension. They give a reproducible one-dimensional monotone root condition \(a(h)-b(h)=0\), permitting bisection after bracketing a sign change. The accompanying `checks/verify.py` independently evaluates Euclidean trihedral solid angles via the determinant--arctangent formula for three-dimensional pyramids with triangular, rectangular and hexagonal bases. Across 75 heights per base, it checks strict apex decrease, strict increase at every base vertex, the unique sign transition, and equality of all four angles of a regular tetrahedron. It reports 1,200 angle evaluations passing. These finite numerical checks are **not** a proof of the all-dimensional statements; the proof above supplies that justification.

## Relationship to prior work

Licht, *On solid angles and simplex shape measures* (2026), Theorem 1.1, proves dimension-dependent inequalities comparing the **minimum** vertex solid angle and fullness of a simplex, motivating the present optimization but not deriving its response to continuously varying an apex height. Akopyan and Karasev, *Bounding minimal solid angles of polytopes* (2015), Conjecture 2.1 and its three-dimensional theorem, concern the **unrestricted** upper bound by the regular-simplex angle. The regular-base specialization here agrees with their dimension-three bound, while the new argument yields a unique height for all dimensions and for arbitrary fixed polytopal bases, without proving the unrestricted conjecture. O'Hara, *The incenter of a triangle as a cone isoperimetric center* (2010), optimizes a cone's boundary-area-to-volume ratio, a different functional. Related convex-pyramid minimum-width formulas and random-shadow extremizers concern different invariants and do not imply the present solid-angle balance.

## Limitations

The apex projection must be interior; boundary projections need not make every base-angle function strictly increasing to the same positive limit. The theorem only varies height at fixed \(P\) and \(q\). The Gaussian integral equations are exact but do not generally simplify to elementary formulas. No claim is made about the full multi-parameter optimization over base shape or apex projection, or the unresolved unrestricted higher-dimensional regular-simplex solid-angle conjecture. The closest-paper comparison cannot exclude undiscovered equivalent work; no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
