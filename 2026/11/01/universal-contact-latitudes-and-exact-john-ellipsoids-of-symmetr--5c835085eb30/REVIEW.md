# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For every integer \(d\ge2\) and \(0<c<1\), the proposed positive axial semiaxis solves a quadratic whose two identities reduce both lens-ball containment inequalities to a manifest nonnegative square. The common contact set in normalized coordinates is exactly the pair of latitudes \(\lvert u_1\rvert=1/\sqrt d\). A set of \(4(d-1)\) contact points has strictly positive weights with zero first moment and identity second moment, and their supporting normals agree with the sphere normals. By the sufficient direction of John's theorem this proves optimality and uniqueness against every affine ellipsoid, not only centered or symmetric candidates. A standalone numerical checker reproduces algebraic identities, contact moments and an axisymmetric optimization benchmark; finite experiments are not substitutes for the proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)
Ivanov and Naszódi (arXiv:2212.11781v1, first public 2022-12-22) provide general John-type contact conditions but no closed symmetric two-ball-lens semiaxis/contact-latitude evaluation. Bezdek (arXiv:1912.05118v1) and Bezdek–Lángi–Naszódi (arXiv:2411.10302v1) study lens intrinsic-volume and inradius extremal inequalities but do not evaluate John ellipsoids. Indexed same-body mean-width and exact-projector work concern different invariants. Indexed exact John ellipsoids of a half-cone and several polytopes concern different bodies, with no evident lens reduction. The 1948 contact theorem and elementary quadratic maximization are explicitly credited as methods. A possible older unpublished or unindexed special-case evaluation remains the principal priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The two-ball lens is a canonical extremal family in convex geometry, while the maximum-volume inscribed ellipsoid is an affine-geometric invariant that supplies an exact ellipsoidal approximation benchmark. The result classifies it in every dimension and every nondegenerate overlap, gives the full contact geometry through a finite isotropic certificate, and shows an unexpectedly universal contact latitude together with an exact dimension-anisotropy law. These facts are not mere numerical table entries or formal substitutions into the generic contact criterion; they require selecting and verifying the exact axial and transverse semiaxes and all equality contacts.

## Scope and risks
The statement does not address unequal balls, intersections of more than two balls, or uniform limits as the overlap parameter approaches its endpoints. Historical search is not exhaustive, and no independent mathematical audit has taken place.
