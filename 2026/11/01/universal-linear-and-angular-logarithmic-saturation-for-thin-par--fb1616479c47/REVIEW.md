# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The mathematical claim is an explicit asymptotic for fixed-angle, unit-long-side Euclidean parallelograms, not a general inequality. Uniform area and arclength probabilities are normalized exactly by the parallelogram parametrization and its four edge lengths. The triangular difference density and its moments are checked directly; the distance kernel is given by an exact primitive and a rigorous second-derivative estimate. The conditional long/short/cross edge decomposition supplies the complete boundary expectation. The remainder from the near-diagonal integral is bounded by a logarithm, and subtraction yields each displayed coefficient. The verification program runs the actual formulas and independent samples. Numeric tests are corroboration, not infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with historical priority risk

Shen (arXiv:2608.06470, full accessible HTML) and Kukushkin (arXiv:2608.14833, accessible full text) establish the underlying planar distance inequality and exhibit the thin-rectangle saturation limit; neither states the sharp fixed-angle linear/logarithmic expansion. Lotnikov (arXiv:2608.14848, accessible thesis text and results) treats the distance inequality and centrally symmetric bodies, with no identified expansion of the present kind. Classical parallelogram line-picking formulas for interior distances are not new. A close fourth-moment parallelogram comparison concerns a different moment and does not imply the present first-moment asymptotics. The remaining risk is unindexed classical boundary line-picking literature; definitive historical priority requires further bibliographic validation.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The general planar inequality is strictly positive but admits no shape-independent positive multiplicative gap, with thin rectangles as the canonical limiting obstruction. The present law resolves the previously qualitative degeneration rate across every fixed-angle parallelogram: a universal first-order constant, an angle-sensitive second-order logarithm, and the full finite second-order coefficient. The ratio-version shows exactly how quickly the sharp comparison approaches equality and demonstrates that angular information first appears at logarithmic order. This is a natural quantitative endpoint classification, not an arbitrary slice or a mere reparameterization of the known qualitative inequality.

## Limitations

Only fixed-angle degenerating parallelograms are covered; no uniform angular estimate, higher moments, arbitrary convex-body stability, or independent audit is asserted. A broad historical-priority search cannot exclude every older treatment of the same asymptotics.
