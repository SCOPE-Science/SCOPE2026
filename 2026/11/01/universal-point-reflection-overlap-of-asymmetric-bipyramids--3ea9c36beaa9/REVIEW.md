# Scientific review of the asymmetric-double-cone central-overlap theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The claim quantifies over every integer \(m\ge1\), every nonempty-interior centrally symmetric base, positive height, and the closed apex-ratio interval \(0\le t\le1\). The proof reduces all horizontal translations using a reflected-pair Brunn–Minkowski argument, then computes the axial overlap exactly on three intervals and proves a unique interior stationary point. Both limiting cases are treated separately. Dividing the exact maximal overlap by the exact total volume yields the stated formula. Logarithmic differentiation, elementary inversion, the binomial bound and the dimension limit establish the ancillary conclusions. Exact rational integrations (570 examples) and independent cubical-section quadratures corroborate the formulas. The finite checks are not used to infer the universal quantifiers.

## Originality — PASS, provisional priority

Stein (1956) already proves unique maximum for every convex body's reflection-overlap functional and computes the symmetry ratio of a simplex; neither theorem supplies the parameter-dependent maximizing point and ratio for arbitrary symmetric-base double cones. Chakerian and Stein (1965) establish general dimensional bounds and symmetric-overlap methods rather than an exact asymmetric-double-cone expression. Huang and coauthors (first public 2018) study maximal translation overlap in their covering arguments, but their analyzed statements do not give an explicit shape-independent two-apex formula. Goenka and coauthors (2023) focus on axial symmetry. A general planar maximum-overlap algorithm from Chan and Hair (2025) computes individual polygons but neither implies the closed all-dimensional identity without additional derivation nor covers nonpolytopal bases. The published material read did not exhibit this formula. Historical priority remains provisional, especially for older literature on bipyramids and Macbeath-type symmetric cores; a failed search is not a uniqueness proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The invariant is the natural maximal centrally symmetric volume fraction used in convex symmetry and translative covering theory. A new exact formula holding in all ambient dimensions and for an arbitrary symmetric equator, rather than one specially shaped example, is structural: it isolates a single observable apex-asymmetry parameter, identifies the optimal point, supplies sharp full-family bounds and inverse reconstruction, and exhibits a nontrivial fixed-parameter high-dimensional limit. The centroid is usually not the maximizing symmetry center, clarifying a geometric distinction relevant to choosing centers in symmetry estimates. The results require no unproved external lemma beyond Brunn–Minkowski and elementary integration. They make no universal minimum claim outside the specified family.

## Closest sources and limits

Primary sources checked: Sherman K. Stein (1956), *The Symmetry Function in a Convex Body*, pp. 145–148; Chakerian and Stein (1965), *On Measures of Symmetry of Convex Bodies*, pp. 497–504; Huang, Slomka, Tkocz and Vritsiou, arXiv:1811.12548 (2018), principal theorems on reflected-overlap covering bounds; Goenka, Moore, Sun and White, arXiv:2309.12597 (2023), central-measure background and main axiality results; Chan and Hair (2025), convex-polygon translative overlap algorithm. The principal unresolved issue is historical completeness of specialized high-dimensional bipyramid cases. The inverse theorem concerns exact population volume, not sampling noise. No assertion is made for misaligned apices, nonsymmetric equators, or a dimension-uniform Lipschitz constant.
