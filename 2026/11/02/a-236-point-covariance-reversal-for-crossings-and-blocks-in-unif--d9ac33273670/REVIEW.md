# Self-review of the 236-point covariance reversal

**Correctness — PASS.** The set-partition crossing convention is explicit, and the fixed-block crossing sum in Kasraoui's Theorem 2.3 yields the displayed generating polynomial. Differentiation under an exponential block-count tilt gives the covariance formula, including all normalization factors. Exact Bell derivatives reproduce the integer numerator. The infinite tail follows from a self-contained real-rootedness induction for Touchard polynomials, the Bernoulli-cumulant inequality, and the published log-concavity theorem for normalized Bell numbers. The last infinite-range hypothesis is reduced to the integer inequality \(5B_{801}<801B_{800}\). The standalone exact verifier checks both intervals \(4\le n\le235\) and \(236\le n\le799\), the threshold inequality, the Stirling formula and independent enumeration. Finite computations are used only for the explicitly finite certificate, not as evidence that the tail continues indefinitely.

**Originality — PASS, with residual historical-priority risk.** The claimed finding is the *global, sharp sign reversal at \(n=236\)*, rather than the source's conditional expectation formula, the basic Touchard recurrence, Bernoulli factorization, or the general shifted-Bell framework. Kasraoui's fixed-block crossing enumeration [1] implies the existence of the mixed moments after summation, and Chern–Diaconis–Kane–Rhoades [3] describe broader machinery for shifted-Bell statistics. Neither inspected full text gives the one-sided sign classification, the exact threshold, or the finite-certificate plus positive-tail proof. Chern et al.'s marginal CLT [4] and Kasraoui's crossing limit theorem [5] do not settle this covariance sign. No equivalent published research record was retrieved by the specific claim-and-alias searches; the closest examined published finding concerns the number of blocks of a *given size* against total block count, with a different parameter and no crossing statistic. Search nonappearance is not proof of absolute historical novelty, and implicit derivability from broad exact-moment machinery remains a residual risk.

**Value — PASS.** Both statistics, total block count and crossing-arc count, are canonical measures of a random set partition's complexity. Their covariance switching from negative to positive at a surprisingly large *exact* finite size is a meaningful qualitative phase boundary, explaining why evidence from small samples alone is misleading. The result covers every size, comes with an explicit exact formula, and provides a reusable rigorous combination of crossing enumerators, real-rooted moment control and normalized-Bell log-concavity. The threshold's location is not an arbitrarily selected finite slice; it is the unique global sign change of a naturally defined dependence statistic.
