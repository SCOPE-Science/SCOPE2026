# Scientific review — macroscopic energy under global resetting

**Correctness (PASS).** Orthogonal diagonalization yields one mean mode and \(N-1\) relative modes, all conditionally independent with a single exponentially distributed reset age. The finite-size second moment follows from chi-square second moments. Its large-population beta limit follows by the strong law. The generator of the limiting age-driven energy observable has an exact affine eigenfunction; a bounded-jump martingale establishes the central limit with the stated coefficient. The sharp local/global gap follows from two independently evaluated chi-square/beta moment ratios. The included program independently tests polynomial-generator balances and sample estimates. The mathematical proof, not these samples, supports the infinite limits.

**Originality (PASS, residual priority risk).** Bressloff (arXiv:2401.03501, §§III–IV) proves correlated macroscopic densities for global reset clocks and derives general moment equations, but does not evaluate the interacting finite-\(N\) mean-square law, the exact local/global excess coefficient, or the interacting population-energy temporal correlation. De Mauro et al. (arXiv:2509.06658, §§I–III) show the shared-age mixture for a *free* Brownian gas and evaluate order statistics/counting, not the harmonic interacting energy. General age mixtures, OU spectral decompositions, the beta variable transform and standard martingale theory are expressly acknowledged. Research-record and exact-title searches found no evaluated theorem subsuming the combined physical statement. A more general unlocated linear-resetting result remains a priority risk; nonappearance in search is not a uniqueness certificate.

**Value (PASS).** Global resetting has a well-recognized macroscopic correlation effect, but its magnitude for a natural mean-square observable is not resolved by a qualitative density-correlation statement. The result gives an exact finite-size law, a strictly positive and optimal global/local relative-noise gap at every \(N>1\), and a nondegenerate thermodynamic energy distribution. It distinguishes population averaging, which fails to remove noise, from long-time averaging, which obeys a quantitative Gaussian limit. This supplies a reproducible benchmark for nonequilibrium particle-population thermodynamics, not an arbitrary moment or a trivial relabeling of a single reset process.

**Literature and limitations.** The mathematically closest source is P. C. Bressloff, arXiv:2401.03501 (first public 2024-01-07); de Mauro et al., arXiv:2509.06658 provide later relevant conditional-independence methods. The result assumes harmonic mean-field interaction, independent thermal noise, full simultaneous origin resets, and fixed parameters at infinite-population limit. The local clock comparison concerns noninteracting particles only. Publication priority remains non-independently assessed.
