For the Proth numerical semigroup generated by \(k2^{n+i}+1\), the isolated gaps number either zero or exactly two, and the distinction depends solely on the binary Hamming weight \(s_2(k)\): the two-gap case occurs exactly when \(s_2(k)=2j+1-s_2(j)\) for some \(j\ge0\). In that case the two isolated gaps are exactly the Frobenius number and the Frobenius number minus the multiplicity, and a Mersenne-coin argument proves the stronger zero-or-two dichotomy for every semigroup generated by \(2^i(m-1)+1\), \(m\ge3\).
