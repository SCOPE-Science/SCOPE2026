A vanishingly small third class of vertices causes a persistent binomial optimality obstruction in random greedy matching on \(K_{n,n,k}\): its terminal unmatched count approaches \(|k-2B_k|\), where \(B_k\sim\operatorname{Bin}(k,1/2)\). The approximation has an explicit total-variation rate and remains valid for \(k=o(n^{1/3})\), yielding a folded-normal limit when the rare class grows.
