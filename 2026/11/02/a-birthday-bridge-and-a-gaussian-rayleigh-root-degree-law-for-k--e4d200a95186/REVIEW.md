# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)
The boundary count is exactly \(n\) until the second hub enters, giving the first-collision product law. After its arrival, paired boundary edges give independent Bernoulli parent choices; conditional characteristic functions establish independent Gaussian noise in the joint Rayleigh scaling. The classical uniform-tree bijection makes the stochastic-dominance coupling exact. All assumptions and boundary cases, including \(n=1\), are covered. The standard-library checker passed 2,541 assertions, including independently enumerated finite trees; finite enumeration is a diagnostic and not a proof of the infinite limit.

## Originality (PASS, qualified)
The root-degree characterization is not the birthday collision law alone, nor the existing uniform-tree count. Devroye et al. define the frontier-growth process but focus on heights; Arratia et al. already establish the classical Rayleigh birthday limit; Dong and Ge count uniform biclique spanning trees; Camarri and Pitman study birthday-derived trees under a different measure. Compared statements do not include the bridge-conditioned binomial degree law, its Gaussian–Rayleigh limit or the stochastic comparison on \(K_{2,n}\). Search and full-source inspection do not certify historical priority; a more specialized earlier treatment remains a residual possibility.

## Value (PASS)
A rooted tree generated by first-passage exploration need not be uniform, even on an elementary graph. Here the root receives an extra \(\Theta(\sqrt n)\) connections with a nonnormal fluctuation law, while the uniform tree has centered Gaussian root degree; the coupling is an all-size bias certificate, not a trivial reformulation of a moment calculation. This provides a tractable benchmark for infection-tree structure and the difference between growth and uniform spanning-tree sampling.

## Limits of review
No proof has been given for broader bicliques, arbitrary graphs or other boundary sampling rules. Classical birthday and uniform-tree components are credited. External independent validation is not available in this report.
