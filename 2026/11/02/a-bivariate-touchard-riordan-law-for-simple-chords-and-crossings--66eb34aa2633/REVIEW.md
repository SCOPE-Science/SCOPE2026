# Scientific review: bivariate short-chord and crossing law

## Correctness — PASS

For every \(n\ge2\) the objects are uniformly distributed perfect matchings of \(2n\) fixed cyclic positions. Marking cyclicly adjacent paired endpoints and deleting any disjoint marked set gives a bijection preserving the crossing count. Selecting \(k\) such boundary edges is counted exactly by matchings in the \(2n\)-cycle. The resulting bivariate generating identity proves all joint coefficients without numerical extrapolation. A first and second factorial moment calculation for simple chords, exact four-endpoint counting for crossing expectation, and the published all-size crossing variance yield the exact covariance and correlation. The classical variance identity was checked in accessible primary literature; the entire combined proof has been reconstructed. Exact enumeration corroborates the finite boundaries but is not claimed as a substitute for an unrestricted argument.

An accessible earlier single-prescribed-chord crossing-variance identity is less informative than the complete bivariate polynomial and cannot determine every coefficient. The combination is plausible but historical priority is not globally certified; older enumerative combinatorics or short-chord refinements might contain an unindexed equivalent expression.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Simple chords are an established local statistic with an asymptotically Poisson law, while total crossings measure global geometric interaction and exhibit an order-cubed variance. An exact bivariate polynomial, valid for every sample size, is more informative than independent marginals and makes mixed moments obtainable by differentiation. The surprisingly simple size-universal negative correlation \(-\sqrt{5/(n+3)}\), the exact deletion/conditional-law mechanism, and the circular-boundary distinction justify studying this joint statistic without an external application claim.
