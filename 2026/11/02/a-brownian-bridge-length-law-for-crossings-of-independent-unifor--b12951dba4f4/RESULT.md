# A Brownian-bridge length law for crossings of independent uniform chords

## Finding

Draw \(n\ge2\) straight chords of the unit circle independently by choosing both endpoint angles independently and uniformly. Define \(X_n\) as the number of intersecting pairs of chords. Write \(W_i\in[0,1]\) for twice the fraction of the circumference in the shorter circular arc between the endpoints of chord \(i\). The lengths \(W_i\) are independent uniform random variables. Let \(N_n(t)=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1\{W_i\le t\}\), \(F_n(t)=N_n(t)/n\), and
\[
\mu_n=\frac{n(n-1)}6,\qquad g(w)=w-\frac{w^2}2-\frac13,\qquad P_n=\mu_n+(n-1)\sum_{i=1}^ng(W_i).
\]

**Exact additive projection and remainder.** For all \(n\ge2\),
\[
\operatorname{Var}(X_n)=\frac{n(n-1)(n+3)}{45},\quad
\operatorname{Var}(P_n)=\frac{n(n-1)^2}{45},\quad
\mathbb E(X_n-P_n)^2=\frac{4n(n-1)}{45}.
\]
Consequently the proportion of crossing variance captured by the *orthogonal additive projection onto single-chord-length functions* is exactly \((n-1)/(n+3)\), tending to one. This does **not** identify \(P_n\) with the full conditional expectation given the entire length vector.

**Exact short-arc dependence and optimal threshold.** For all \(0<t<1\) and all \(n\ge2\),
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(X_n,N_n(t))=-\frac{n(n-1)}6t(1-t)(2-t),
\]
\[
\operatorname{Corr}(X_n,N_n(t))=-\frac{\sqrt5}{2}\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n+3}}(2-t)\sqrt{t(1-t)}.
\]
The absolute correlation has the unique maximizer \(t_*=(7-\sqrt{17})/8\) for **every** \(n\ge2\). The limiting optimal correlation is
\[
-\sqrt{\frac{535+255\sqrt{17}}{2048}}\approx-0.8801167177.
\]

**Joint functional limit.** Let \(B\) be a standard Brownian bridge on \([0,1]\). As \(n\to\infty\),
\[
\left(\frac{X_n-\mu_n}{n^{3/2}},\ \bigl(\sqrt n(F_n(t)-t)\bigr)_{0\le t\le1}\right)
\Longrightarrow\left(-\int_0^1(1-t)B(t)\,dt,\ (B(t))_{0\le t\le1}\right).
\]
This is joint convergence in the product of \(\mathbb R\) and the càdlàg empirical-process space with the usual weak topology; since the limit is continuous, the empirical-process coordinate also converges in the uniform topology in distribution. In particular, the crossing-count fluctuation on the \(n^{3/2}\) scale is asymptotically an explicit linear functional of the chord-length empirical bridge, with variance \(1/45\) and covariance with \(B(t)\) equal to \(-t(1-t)(2-t)/6\).

## Assumptions and scope

Each chord is the straight segment joining two independent uniform points on the same unit circle; different chords are mutually independent. Endpoint coincidences occur with probability zero and are ignored. For circular positions \(u,v\in[0,1)\), let \(\delta=\min\{|u-v|,1-|u-v|\}\) and \(W=2\delta\). Here \(W\) is a dimensionless **minor-arc fraction**, not Euclidean chord length, and is not the integer rank-gap statistic for a model with fixed equally spaced endpoints. Sorting the \(2n\) random endpoints induces a uniformly random matching, so marginal crossing statistics coincide with those of uniform chord diagrams. The geometric length empirical process is additional data unavailable to a bare unlabeled matching with no sampled spacings.

## Proof

**Step 1: conditional crossing kernel.** The short circular arc of chord \(i\) has circumference fraction \(W_i/2\). Another independently drawn chord crosses it exactly when one endpoint lies in this short arc and the other outside it. Therefore the conditional crossing probability is \(p(W_i)=2(W_i/2)(1-W_i/2)=W_i-W_i^2/2\). Rotation invariance implies \(W_i\) is uniform on \([0,1]\). Integrating gives \(\mathbb E p(W)=1/3\), \(g=p-1/3\), and
\[
\mathbb Eg(W)=0,\qquad \mathbb E g(W)^2=\int_0^1(w-w^2/2-1/3)^2dw=1/45.
\]

**Step 2: orthogonal kernel decomposition.** Write \(Z_i=(U_i,V_i)\) for the two endpoints, and \(h(Z_i,Z_j)\) for their crossing indicator. With \(\mathbb Eh=1/3\), define \(h_2(Z_i,Z_j)=h(Z_i,Z_j)-1/3-g(W_i)-g(W_j)\). Then \(\mathbb E[h_2(Z_i,Z_j)\mid Z_i]=0\), and likewise conditioning on \(Z_j\). The Hoeffding identity is
\[
X_n-\mu_n=(n-1)\sum_i g(W_i)+\sum_{i<j}h_2(Z_i,Z_j).
\]
The degenerate summands are pairwise uncorrelated, including pairs of summands sharing one endpoint index, and are orthogonal to all square-integrable additive single-length functions. Since \(h\) is Bernoulli with mean \(1/3\),
\[
\operatorname{Var}h_2=\frac29-2\cdot\frac1{45}=\frac8{45}.
\]
Thus the remainder has variance \(\binom n2(8/45)=4n(n-1)/45\), while the projection variance is \(n(n-1)^2/45\). Their sum yields the asserted exact variance of \(X_n\). This also proves that \(P_n\) is the orthogonal projection of \(X_n\) onto the closed linear subspace generated by constants and individual length functions: all first-order projections depend on each chord solely via \(W_i\).

**Step 3: covariance and sharp maximizer.** The residual is orthogonal to each length indicator, and length variables are independent. Hence
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(X_n,N_n(t))=n(n-1)\int_0^tg(w)dw
=-\frac{n(n-1)}6t(1-t)(2-t).
\]
Dividing by the square root of \(\operatorname{Var}(X_n)\operatorname{Var}(N_n(t))\), with \(\operatorname{Var}(N_n(t))=nt(1-t)\), yields the correlation formula. Its squared absolute value is a positive constant times \(t(1-t)(2-t)^2\). The derivative is proportional, on \((0,1)\), to \((2-t)(4t^2-7t+2)\), so the sole critical point in \((0,1)\) is \(t_*=(7-\sqrt{17})/8\); both endpoint values are zero, proving the global optimum. Direct substitution gives the stated limiting correlation.

**Step 4: functional convergence.** Set \(\alpha_n(t)=\sqrt n(F_n(t)-t)\). The empirical-process Donsker theorem for iid uniforms gives \(\alpha_n\Rightarrow B\) as a process on \([0,1]\). Integration by parts for \(g(0)\), \(g(1)\), and \(g'(t)=1-t\) gives the *exact identity*
\[
\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^ng(W_i)=-\int_0^1(1-t)\alpha_n(t)dt.
\]
By the continuous mapping theorem, \((n-1)\sum_i g(W_i)/n^{3/2}\Rightarrow-\int(1-t)B(t)dt\), jointly with \(\alpha_n\). Step 2 shows \(\mathbb E[(X_n-P_n)^2]/n^3=4(n-1)/(45n^2)\to0\); Slutsky's theorem proves the joint functional limit. The normal variance is \(1/45\) from the same iid projection calculation, and \(\operatorname{Cov}(Z,B(t))=\int_0^tg(w)dw\) by the joint Gaussian covariance computation.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library Python program `verify.py` checks the variance and covariance identities in exact rational arithmetic, exhaustively enumerates all perfect matchings through twelve endpoints, verifies the conditional separated-endpoint probability, confirms the unique optimum, and performs reproducible seeded finite iid-chord simulations. Executing `python verify.py` on this package passed **716 checks**, including **24,000** random six-chord simulations. Finite checks are corroboration, not the proof of the functional limit. The infinite results follow from the independently proved kernel decomposition and the classical iid empirical-process theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Flajolet and Noy (2000), Theorem 3, previously computed the **same marginal crossing-count mean and variance** and established its Gaussian limit for uniformly matched \(2n\) points. Paguyo (first posted 2021) strengthened the rate of Gaussian approximation and treated simple-chord counts. Acan (2015) studied uniform chord diagrams, their intersection graphs, and combinatorial chord positions. These works are not claimed to be new here. The contribution is the specific **geometric minor-arc empirical-process coupling**, exact finite additive projection and variance-explained ratio, joint Brownian-bridge representation, and sharp fixed-\(n\) short-arc threshold. The route through Hoeffding projection and Donsker's theorem is classical; novelty, insofar as it survives further priority checking, lies in their explicit joint application to the geometric observable and resulting sharp formulas.

## Limitations

The result assumes independent chords with independently sampled endpoints uniform in angle. It does not assert that the additive predictor is the full conditional expectation given all lengths, does not extend automatically to fixed equally spaced endpoints where geometric arc spacings are absent, and does not establish a distributional rate for the joint functional limit. The weak limit is not pathwise convergence of the original processes. Existing general U-statistic theory implies the projection method; independently published equivalents for the particular chord-length coupling cannot be ruled out without further human review. This analysis is a self-review, not an independent audit.
