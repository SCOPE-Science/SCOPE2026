# A certified high-frequency Gabor non-frame point at density \(200/201\)

## Finding
For the even rational window
\[
g(x)=\frac{x^2-1}{(x^2+1)(x^2+4)(x^2+9)},
\]
there exists a sampling parameter \(\beta_* >1\) satisfying
\[
\frac{100}{\pi}\log\frac{1000}{969}
<\beta_*<
\frac{100}{\pi}\log\frac{32}{31}
\]
such that the rectangular Gabor system generated by \(g\) with \(\alpha_*=200/(201\beta_*)\) and \(\beta_*\) is not a frame for \(L^2(\mathbb R)\). In particular \(\alpha_*\beta_*=200/201\). The assertion is an existence result, not an identification of a unique root, a classification of the entire frame set, or proof about other density indices.

This rigorously certifies the high-frequency \(\beta>1\) obstruction in the natural density \(200/201\) test case reported numerically in the motivating source.

## Assumptions and scope
We use the Fourier-side fiber criterion and its symmetry reduction from Ghosh, *Gabor Frame Regions and Non-Frame Obstructions for an Even Rational Window*, Section 4. Put \(p=200\), \(m=p/2=100\), \(a=p/(p+1)\), and
\[
h_{\lambda}(t)=e^{-\lambda|t|}(1-e^{-\lambda|t|})^2, \quad \lambda=2\pi\beta/a.
\]
The fiber criterion states that a singular even symmetry block in the Zibulski–Zeevi matrix at the symmetric fiber point suffices to preclude a frame. Its use requires no assertion that other fibers are singular.

For \(u=e^{-\lambda/(p+1)}=e^{-2\pi\beta/p}\), set \(P=p(p+1)=40200\) and \(c_1=1,c_2=-2,c_3=1\). Define the explicit rational function
\[
R_u(L)=\sum_{j=1}^{3}c_j\frac{u^{jL}-u^{j(P-L)}}{1+u^{jP}},\quad 0\le L\le P,\quad 0<u<1.
\]
For integers \(1\le s,k\le100\), define a real \(100\times100\) matrix
\[
C_{s,k}(u)=\begin{cases}
R_u(ps+(p+1)k)-R_u(|ps-(p+1)k|),&k<100,\
R_u(ps+(p+1)100),&k=100.
\end{cases}
\]
All exponents in this formula are nonnegative integers not exceeding \(P\). The \(k=100\) column is a positive rescaling of the natural unpaired middle column; the zero set of the determinant is unchanged.

## Proof
Write the alternating periodicization of the even profile as
\[
H_{\lambda,p}(y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}(-1)^n h_{\lambda}(y+np).
\]
The series is absolutely and locally uniformly convergent; it is even and satisfies \(H_{\lambda,p}(y+p)=-H_{\lambda,p}(y)\). For \(0\le y\le p\), summing the geometric series separately on either side of \(y\) yields
\[
H_{\lambda,p}(y)=\sum_{j=1}^{3}c_j\frac{e^{-j\lambda y}-e^{-j\lambda(p-y)}}{1+e^{-j\lambda p}}.
\]
Hence \(H_{\lambda,p}(L/(p+1))=R_u(L)\) on the displayed range.

The relevant source fiber has entries proportional to \(H_{\lambda,p}(as+k)\). Pairing columns \(k\) and \(p-k\) in the even sector replaces their entries, up to a fixed positive factor, by
\[
H_{\lambda,p}(as+k)-H_{\lambda,p}(as-k).
\]
Pairing the compatible rows \(s\) and \(p+1-s\) doubles those entries, by evenness and antiperiodicity. The unpaired column \(k=p/2\) contributes \(H_{\lambda,p}(as+p/2)\), up to another positive factor. These identities produce precisely \(C(u)\), multiplied by fixed invertible diagonal factors. Therefore \(\det C(u)=0\) implies a singular source fiber and hence failure of the frame property.

It remains to prove a change of determinant sign. The included executable `verify_high_branch.py` is a **finite exact-integer certificate**, not a floating-point experiment. It uses fixed \(192\)-bit dyadic intervals; input fractions \(969/1000\) and \(31/32\) are enclosed by integer floor/ceiling. All additions, subtractions, powers, multiplications, and divisions use outward interval bounds calculated entirely with integer operations. The interval versions of Gaussian elimination encounter no interval pivot containing zero; consequently the signed product of the \(100\) certified pivot signs is the true determinant sign. Its reproducible output is
\[
\operatorname{sgn}\det C(969/1000)=-1,
\qquad \operatorname{sgn}\det C(31/32)=+1.
\]
Specifically, it certifies \(99\) negative pivots at \(969/1000\), and \(100\) at \(31/32\). Since each entry of \(C(u)\) is continuous for \(0<u<1\), the intermediate-value theorem yields \(u_*\) strictly between these rational endpoints with \(\det C(u_*)=0\).

Finally \(\beta(u)=-(p/(2\pi))\log u\) decreases in \(u\). Both endpoint heights exceed one: writing \(t=31/1000\), we have
\[
-\log(969/1000)>t+t^2/2>\frac{22}{700}>\frac{\pi}{100},
\]
where the last inequality follows from \(\pi<22/7\). Thus \(\beta(969/1000)>1\), and so also \(\beta(31/32)>1\). The corresponding \(\alpha=a/\beta(u_*)\) gives the claimed lattice. \(\square\)

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_high_branch.py` using Python 3 and the standard library only. The checker computes exact outward bounds on both full \(100\times100\) matrices and fails if any interval pivot contains zero or the determinant signs differ from the claimed values. In a witnessed replay it printed `SIGN_CERTIFIED` for both inputs and `CHECK_OK`. The displayed small decimal pivot separations and widths are informational only: all certified sign decisions precede any floating conversion and use exact integers. The argument that an intermediate real root exists is analytical, via continuity; no enumeration or root approximation is used as an exhaustive proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Ghosh, arXiv:2609.19232, Section 4 derives the even-window fiber obstruction and gives a *numerical* high-frequency second branch for the density \(200/201\), near \(\beta\approx1.00996\). The certified sign change above establishes rigorously that a second \(\beta>1\) obstruction indeed occurs; its hypothesis is the same window and density rather than a generic window or a different finite index. The separately available published four-fifths obstruction concerns \(p=4\) and does not imply this \(p=200\) result. Earlier rational-window frame results do not, in their inspected statements, certify this specific high-frequency root.

## Limitations
The theorem gives existence and a verified bracket, not uniqueness, exact location, maximal interval of non-frames, or an all-index theorem. It relies on the Fourier/fiber frame-equivalence theorem in the cited source; the integer checker establishes only the finite determinant signs, not that general analytic equivalence. The original full text of the related earlier symmetric-pole preprint was not fully accessible, so a previously implicit covering result cannot be categorically ruled out without further literature review. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
