# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The gap bijection, local move support and cyclic-pointing argument give a fully quantified proof of the degree generating function; connectivity and the Fibonacci edge count follow formally. Exact independent finite verifiers test the formula and the swap graph. The theorem does not infer an infinite statement from finite runs.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** The wheel spanning-tree number is classical (Myers, Hilton, Sedláček). A full accessible wheel-forest article by Tamura and Tanaka computes rooted-forest counts via cyclic intervals, but not exchange adjacency degrees. Published geometric spanning-tree transition work is related without yielding the precise restricted-degree polynomial in available material. Searches for matroid basis-exchange equivalences and exact coefficient sequences revealed no covering result; inaccessible material and generic-matroid deductions remain risks. Neither generic tree enumeration nor graph connectedness by itself is claimed novel.

**Value — PASS.** The result gives an all-size, complete degree classification for a graph of constrained local moves, including the exact number of allowable moves and extremal mobility. This differs substantively from simply counting spanning trees. The gap-statistic factorization can be used to study random local exploration and sampling, although no mixing-time theorem is claimed.

The 2020 Nichols--Pilz--Tóth--Zehmakan full text was unavailable, and classical wheel papers were identified through a fully read modern reference rather than separately examined. Independently locating all equivalent formulations is impossible; these are specific originality uncertainties, not logical proof gaps.
