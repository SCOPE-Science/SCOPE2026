# Review of the radial Weyl criterion

## Correctness — PASS

The statement uses a radial \(C^2\) weight with uniform Hessian bounds, a positive decreasing symbol \((1+|z|^2)^{-\beta/2}\), all fixed finite dimensions, and eigenvalues counted with their homogeneous multiplicities. The proof identifies the exact degree-\(n\) eigenvalue quotient, proves strict monotonicity and compactness, and uses the globally strongly concave radial log-density to obtain a uniform Gaussian localization bound around the unique saddle \(r_n\phi'(r_n)=n+d-1/2\). This gives relative error \(O(n^{-1/2})\) in the eigenvalues. A fixed-width saddle bracketing produces the stronger threshold-by-threshold counting profile with error \(O(t^{1/\beta})\). The binomial multiplicity and continuity of the radial slope establish the necessary-and-sufficient Weyl criterion and complete cluster intervals. The two explicit nonquadratic examples satisfy the curvature bounds by differentiation. The 162 numerical checks are supplementary rather than part of the proof.

## Originality — PASS, best-knowledge and access-qualified

The closest antecedent is Xu and Dong (2026), Proposition 5.3, which uses one-variable plateaux to show *two* distinct spectral subsequential limits for an oscillating Hessian. Their Theorems 5.1–5.2 give Weyl constants for quadratic weights. The present claim instead gives a sharp pointwise counting profile for every radial uniformly bounded-Hessian weight in every fixed complex dimension, a necessary *and* sufficient asymptotic radial slope condition, the complete cluster intervals, and examples separating Hessian convergence from spectral convergence. Grudsky–Vasilevski treat a Gaussian weight and varying radial symbols. The closest published research record about logarithmic radial Fock spectra concerns oscillating symbols for a fixed weight, not varying Hessians. Searches by theorem implication, radial slope, Weyl constant, and weighted Fock Toeplitz aliases did not locate equivalent coverage. Huang–Wang–Zeng (2026) is a plausible broader spectral-rearrangement source, but only its abstract was accessible, so possible implicit coverage remains a publication-priority risk. No inaccessible source is treated as proven not to cover the result.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The newly studied 2026 Weyl limitation naturally asks what weaker radial geometric hypothesis restores a coefficient. The finding supplies an exact characterization, with a quantitative error and all subsequential constants rather than merely a counterexample. It captures the nontrivial degree multiplicities in arbitrary fixed complex dimension, explains why an oscillating Hessian can still give a stable Weyl law, and determines exactly what replaces that law for log-oscillating radial slopes. These are structural spectral statements about a natural class, rather than a table entry or small numerical improvement.
