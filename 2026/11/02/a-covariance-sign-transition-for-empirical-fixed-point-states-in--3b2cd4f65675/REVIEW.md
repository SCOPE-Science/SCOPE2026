# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The singleton count is a time-inhomogeneous birth/death/stay chain with rates theta/(j+theta), c/(j+theta), and (j-c)/(j+theta). On logarithmic time it converges over compact windows to the stationary M/M/infinity immigration–death process, consistent with the full proof of Theorem 6 in Gnedin and Stark (arXiv:2209.12781v1). The initial finite relative-time interval contributes at most epsilon to any occupation fraction. The stationary chain is reversible under Poisson(theta), and conditioning on initial zero gives a Poisson(theta*(1-exp(-t))) marginal; integrated two-time correlations yield the displayed formula. Strict integral monotonicity yields a unique root; upper/lower Gaussian-window estimates prove the large-state root asymptotic. Finite rational state recurrences and independent numerical integration check the formulas without being treated as infinite proofs. Risk: no quantitative convergence rate for the empirical occupation fractions is derived.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The principal source, Gnedin and Stark (2022), already defines these empirical fractions, proves their almost-sure liminf/limsup, computes marginal expectation limits, and proves the functional logarithmic-time limit. It also studies covariances of cycle-lifetime counts, which are different from covariances of empirical fixed-point-state fractions. That source underlies the weak limit and transition law, but no statement establishing a unique covariance sign reversal for zero-versus-d occupation, or its displacement by sqrt(d log d), was found in the inspected full text. The 2024 Stark Markov-chain work considers alternative permutation-generation transitions, including derangements; it does not supply these covariance sign thresholds in its accessible manuscript. Published-record searches for synonymous queue occupation and Ewens restaurant formulations found no directly dominating result. This does not prove absolute priority: a queueing occupation identity could imply the threshold after additional analysis.

## Mathematical value — PASS
In the exact stochastic evolution studied in prior work, the occupation fraction of each singleton-count state fluctuates substantially; it does not converge almost surely. The sign and scale of dependence between distinct empirically occupied states are therefore natural unaddressed characteristics. Although the indicators for zero and d are mutually exclusive at each instant, their empirical fractions become positively correlated above a unique parameter-dependent boundary. The theorem identifies a full sequence of ordered boundaries and their nontrivial displacement scale, not merely a finite numerical covariance.

## Sources, checks, limitations

Stark, *Markov chains generating random permutations and set partitions*, 2024 accepted manuscript, inspected for the process definitions and objectives. The root-asymptotic is not a joint limit of n and d.
