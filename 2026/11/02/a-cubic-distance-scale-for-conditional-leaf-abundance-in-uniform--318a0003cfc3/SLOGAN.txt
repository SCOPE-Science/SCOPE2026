In a uniform labeled tree, the expected number of leaves conditioned on the distance between two marked vertices rises and then falls, with an exact cubic cutoff and a maximizing distance asymptotic to \((2n)^{1/3}\). Its full conditional occupancy law also shows that for every \(n\ge11\), increasing the marked distance from two to three raises the mean leaf count while destroying a positive extreme upper tail, so neither conditional law stochastically dominates the other.
