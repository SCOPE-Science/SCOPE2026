# A cubic-trace weight enumerator and eventual \(3m\) low-weight law for wheel graph codes

## Finding
Let \(m\ge3\) be an integer and \(G_m=K_1\vee C_m\) the simple wheel on \(m+1\) vertices. Let \(\Gamma_m\) be its binary adjacency matrix, and let
\[
 C_m=\{\omega s+\Gamma_m s:s\in\mathbb F_2^{m+1}\}\subseteq\mathbb F_4^{m+1},
 \qquad \omega^2+\omega+1=0.
\]
Here addition and matrix multiplication use the natural inclusion of \(\mathbb F_2\) in \(\mathbb F_4\). The code is an additive trace-Hermitian self-dual code of cardinality \(2^{m+1}\). Its ordinary Hamming weight enumerator is \(W_m(t)=\sum_{c\in C_m}t^{\operatorname{wt}(c)}\).

Define cubic power sums \(P_j(a,c)\) by
\[
P_0=3,\quad P_1=a,\quad P_2=a^2,\quad
P_j=aP_{j-1}-cP_{j-3}\ (j\ge3).
\]
Then for every integer \(m\ge3\),
\[
\boxed{W_m(t)=\frac{1+t}{2}P_m(1+t,t-t^3)
+\frac{1-t}{2}P_m(1-t,-t+t^3)
+tP_m(2t,-t+t^3).}
\]
In particular, writing \(A_j(m)=[t^j]W_m(t)\), the complete low-weight sector laws are
\[
 A_1(m)=0,\qquad
 A_2(m)=6\mathbf1_{m=3}+2\mathbf1_{m=4},\qquad
 A_3(m)=8\mathbf1_{m=4}+3\mathbf1_{m=6},
\]
\[
\boxed{A_4(m)=3m+\mathbf1_{m=4}+30\mathbf1_{m=5}
+11\mathbf1_{m=6}+21\mathbf1_{m=7}
+2\mathbf1_{m=8}+3\mathbf1_{m=9}.}
\]
Thus the count of weight-four words is exactly \(3m\) for every \(m\ge10\), with all smaller exceptions specified. The minimum nonzero Hamming weight is \(2\) for \(m=3,4\), is \(3\) for \(m=6\), and is \(4\) for \(m=5\) and every \(m\ge7\). Equivalently, the pure wheel graph stabilizer states are three-uniform for \(m=5\) and all \(m\ge7\), although the individual six-qubit case was previously known.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is an undirected simple wheel with one hub and a cycle of \(m\ge3\) distinct rim vertices. Addition of the rim index is modulo \(m\). The field \(\mathbb F_4\) has characteristic two and is generated by \(\omega\); the trace-Hermitian form is \(\langle u,v\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb F_4/\mathbb F_2}(\sum_i u_i v_i^2)\). A codeword's Hamming weight counts nonzero field coordinates, not individual nonzero binary components. These conventions fix the normalization \(W_m(1)=2^{m+1}\), corresponding to unnormalized Shor–Laflamme sector counts of the associated pure graph state.

## Proof
**Additive self-duality and local support.** The map \(s\mapsto\omega s+\Gamma_m s\) is injective because the \(\omega\)-coefficient of each field coordinate is \(s_i\). Its image has binary dimension \(m+1\). Since \(\Gamma_m\) is symmetric, the trace-Hermitian pairing of two image vectors is \(s^\top\Gamma_m u+u^\top\Gamma_m s=0\). Thus it is self-orthogonal and, by cardinality, self-dual. The \(i\)-th code coordinate is nonzero precisely when \(s_i=1\) or \((\Gamma_m s)_i=1\).

Write \(h=s_0\in\mathbb F_2\) for the hub bit and \(b_i=s_i\in\mathbb F_2\) for the cycle bits. A rim coordinate is nonzero precisely when
\[
 b_i=1\ \text{or}\ (b_{i-1}+b_{i+1}+h)\equiv1\pmod2,
\]
while the hub coordinate is nonzero precisely when \(h=1\) or \(\sum_i b_i\equiv1\pmod2\).

**Trace transfer.** Use four ordered pair states \((0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\). For \(h\in\{0,1\}\) and a sign \(\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}\), the transfer from state \((a,b)\) to \((b,c)\) has weight
\[
 \varepsilon^b t^{\mathbf1\{b=1\ \mathrm{or}\ a+c+h\equiv1\pmod2\}}.
\]
For the required three combinations these matrices are
\[
T_{0,+}=\begin{pmatrix}1&t&0&0\\0&0&t&t\\t&1&0&0\\0&0&t&t\end{pmatrix},\quad
T_{0,-}=\begin{pmatrix}1&t&0&0\\0&0&-t&-t\\t&1&0&0\\0&0&-t&-t\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
T_{1,+}=\begin{pmatrix}t&1&0&0\\0&0&t&t\\1&t&0&0\\0&0&t&t\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For every \(m\ge3\), closed transfer walks are in bijection with the ordered \(m\)-bit rim configurations. The minus matrix tracks \((-1)^{\sum_i b_i}\). If \(h=0\), the hub weight is \(1\) for even rim parity and \(t\) for odd parity; if \(h=1\), it is always \(t\). The elementary parity projector therefore gives the **exact** identity
\[
W_m(t)=\frac{1+t}{2}\mathrm{tr}(T_{0,+}^{m})
+\frac{1-t}{2}\mathrm{tr}(T_{0,-}^{m})
+t\mathrm{tr}(T_{1,+}^{m}).
\]
Direct determinant expansion yields the three characteristic polynomials, respectively,
\[
 \lambda[\lambda^3-(1+t)\lambda^2+t-t^3],\qquad
 \lambda[\lambda^3-(1-t)\lambda^2-t+t^3],\qquad
 \lambda[\lambda^3-2t\lambda^2-t+t^3].
\]
For every positive power, the zero eigenvalue contributes zero and Newton's identities show that the cubic power sum satisfies \(P_j(a,c)\). Substitution gives the displayed formula for every allowed \(m\). No finite-size extrapolation is used.

**All-length low-weight law.** Put \(q=t-t^3\) and
\[
 D_+=1-(1+t)z+qz^3,\quad
 D_-=1-(1-t)z-qz^3,\quad
 D_*=1-2tz-qz^3.
\]
By the logarithmic derivative identity for cubic root power sums, the formal extension \(F(z,t)=\sum_{m\ge1}W_m(t)z^m\) is
\[
F(z,t)=-\frac z2\left(
 (1+t)\frac{\partial_zD_+}{D_+}
 +(1-t)\frac{\partial_zD_-}{D_-}
 +2t\frac{\partial_zD_*}{D_*}\right).
\]
The extension at \(m=1,2\) is only a formal sequence, not a graph claim. Exact Taylor expansion in \(t\) gives
\[
 [t]F=0,\qquad [t^2]F=3z+3z^2+6z^3+2z^4,
\]
\[
 [t^3]F=4z^2+8z^4+3z^6,
\]
\[
 [t^4]F=\frac{3z}{(1-z)^2}-3z-6z^2+z^4+30z^5+11z^6+21z^7+2z^8+3z^9.
\]
In the last expression the coefficient of \(z^m\) in \(3z/(1-z)^2\) is \(3m\), proving the exact four-weight law including each exceptional \(m\). Because \(A_0(m)=1\), the three preceding sector identities and positivity of the displayed \(A_4(m)\) also establish the asserted minimum nonzero weight for every \(m\ge3\). The usual stabilizer reduced-density criterion then gives the three-uniform statement.

## Verification
The standalone dependency-free `verify.py` reconstructs the integer polynomial independently by enumerating the actual \(2^{m+1}\) graph-code generators and computing the binary support \(s\vee\Gamma_m s\). It compares **all coefficients** with the cubic recurrence for every rim size \(3\le m\le14\), i.e. up to fifteen vertices. It separately checks the exact low-sector formulas and \(W_m(1)=2^{m+1}\) for every \(3\le m\le110\); output: `VERIFY_OK`. These computational ranges are consistency checks, not a substitute for the infinite proof above. For example, \(W_5(t)=1+45t^4+18t^6\) and \(W_6(t)=1+3t^3+29t^4+42t^5+34t^6+19t^7\).

Run: `python3 verify.py` with a standard Python 3 installation. No external data or packages are needed.

## Relationship to prior work
Vallée, Goodenough, Gunnells, Coopmans, and Tura (2026, arXiv:2604.09766, first public 2026-04-10) establish rational families of Shor–Laflamme enumerators using analytic combinatorics and treat several other graph families. Goodenough and Gunnells (2026, arXiv:2604.10719) develop black-white polynomial transfer methods with general rationality results; graph-state weight enumerators are equivalent to their invariants after an explicit change of variables. These methodological results motivate this calculation but do not state the wheel's three cubic traces, the full coefficient law, or the eventual \(3m\) stabilization. The earlier graph-state SLD theory (arXiv:2207.07665) provides the general interpretation. A six-qubit wheel state as an absolutely maximally entangled graph state was already reported in arXiv:2408.16914, Appendix K, so that finite case is **not** claimed as new. Saito (2019, DOI:10.3934/amc.2019014) studies bordered circulant additive \(\mathbb F_4\) codes; its publicly visible abstract and tables give finite code-classification and selected weight distributions, rather than the present all-rim-size formula. Its full text was not available for a complete priority comparison.

## Limitations
This theorem treats only the simple wheel graph and its specified standard additive graph-code realization. No optimality within general stabilizer codes is asserted. No noise threshold, complete higher-order asymptotic, or error-correction rate is inferred. The known six-qubit extremal entanglement phenomenon is explicitly excluded from novelty claims. The inaccessible full text of one older bordered-circulant code paper and the possibility of unindexed equivalent calculations leave a nonzero publication-priority risk. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
