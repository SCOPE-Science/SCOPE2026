# A cycle-rank obstruction to efficient envy-and-equitability fairness with two approved goods per agent

## Finding
Let \(N\) be a nonempty finite agent set, and \(M\) a finite set of indivisible goods. Each agent approves **exactly two distinct goods**, has additive \(0/1\) valuations, and every good is approved by somebody. Define the **approval multigraph** \(H\) on vertex set \(M\): each agent becomes one undirected edge joining the two goods she approves. Distinct agents can give parallel edges, but there are no loops. An allocation is complete and Pareto optimal (PO), envy-free up to any positively valued good (EFX), and equitable up to any positively valued good (EQX) simultaneously **if and only if** the following obstruction is absent:

> One connected component of \(H\) is a tree, and a *different* connected component contains at least two independent cycles (equivalently, has more edges than vertices).

Equivalently, writing \(e(C)\) and \(v(C)\) for component edge and vertex counts, an allocation exists exactly when either \(e(C)\le v(C)\) for **all** components or \(e(C)\ge v(C)\) for **all** components. Equivalently, if \(G\) is the bipartite agent-good incidence graph, its maximum matching size is \(\min(|N|,|M|)\). The criterion gives a linear-time decision and construction algorithm after the input graph is built.

The four-agent boundary is sharp: every such instance with at most three agents admits all three properties, but four agents need not. When no two agents approve the same unordered pair of goods, the corresponding sharp boundary rises to six agents.

## Assumptions and scope
A complete allocation partitions every good into exactly one agent bundle; empty bundles are permitted. For an agent \(i\), \(u_i(S)=|S\cap L_i|\), where the approval set \(L_i\subseteq M\) has size exactly two. PO excludes another allocation that weakly increases every utility and strictly increases at least one. EFX requires \(u_i(A_i)\ge u_i(A_j\setminus\{g\})\) for every \(i,j\) and every \(g\in A_j\) with \(u_i(\{g\})>0\). EQX requires \(u_i(A_i)\ge u_j(A_j\setminus\{g\})\) for each ordered pair \(i,j\) and every \(g\in A_j\) with \(u_j(\{g\})>0\). Since valuations are binary and the allocation is PO, the usual EQ1 and EQX definitions coincide here. Goods valued by no one are excluded to make PO allocations exactly the supported assignments; such goods could alternatively be deleted before applying the result.

## Proof
**1. Pareto efficiency and equitable utility ranges.** An allocation is PO if and only if each good is assigned to an agent approving it. Indeed, a misassigned good can be moved to an approver, strictly benefiting the latter while not hurting its present holder. Conversely a supported assignment attains the maximum possible sum of utilities, \(|M|\), and is even fractionally PO. Every supported bundle has cardinality equal to its owner's utility \(u_i=u_i(A_i)\), necessarily in \(\{0,1,2\}\). Removing any good from a supported bundle decreases its owner's utility by exactly one, so EQX (equivalently EQ1) is precisely \(\max_i u_i-\min_i u_i\le1\). When this inequality holds, EFX follows: for \(g\in A_j\) that \(i\) approves,
\[
 u_i(A_j\setminus\{g\})\le |A_j|-1=u_j-1\le u_i.
\]
Consequently an allocation with PO and EQX already satisfies EFX.

**2. Matching reduction.** Because every \(u_i\) is \(0,1\), or \(2\), a supported allocation with EQX has either no agents with utility zero or no agents with utility two. In the first case, choosing one distinct good from every agent's supported bundle yields a matching saturating the agent side of the incidence graph \(G\). In the second case, every supported good has an owner of utility one, so the owner assignments yield a matching saturating the good side. Conversely, if \(G\) has a matching saturating all agents, first give each agent her matched good and assign every unmatched good to any approving agent. All agent utilities then lie in \(\{1,2\}\) because each approves only two goods, and hence the supported allocation satisfies EQX and EFX. If a matching saturates all goods, give each good to its matched agent, obtaining utilities in \(\{0,1\}\) and the same guarantees. This proves the matching criterion \(\nu(G)=\min(|N|,|M|)\).

**3. From matching to component cycle ranks.** Regard an agent (an edge of \(H\)) matched to an approved good (an endpoint) as assigning that edge to one of its endpoints, never reusing an endpoint. Within a connected loopless multigraph component \(C\), such an injection of **all edges** into vertices exists if and only if \(e(C)\le v(C)\). Necessity follows by counting. Sufficiency follows since a connected component has at least \(v(C)-1\) edges: when \(e=v-1\) the component is a tree, and when \(e=v\) it is unicyclic. Root a tree and assign each edge to its child endpoint; for a unicyclic component, orient cycle edges consistently and all pendant tree edges away from the cycle, assigning each edge to its head. Likewise, an injection of **all vertices** into edges incident to them exists if and only if \(e(C)\ge v(C)\). Necessity again follows by counting; sufficiency chooses a spanning unicyclic subgraph (a spanning tree plus one additional edge) and uses the unicyclic orientation just constructed, producing a different incident edge for every vertex. Both constructions also apply to two-edge parallel cycles. A matching saturating all agents exists precisely when \(e(C)\le v(C)\) for every component; one saturating all goods exists precisely when \(e(C)\ge v(C)\) for every component. In a connected component the only possibility \(e<v\) is a tree, and \(e>v\) signifies cycle rank at least two. Their coexistence in different components is therefore the sole obstruction.

**4. Sharp minimal examples.** For at most three agent edges, a multicyclic connected component needs at least three edges: the smallest example has two goods connected by three parallel edges. A separate tree component needs at least one additional edge, giving the lower bound four. It is attained by four agents with approval pairs \(\{a,b\},\{a,b\},\{a,b\},\{c,d\}\). The latter must get both of her goods in any PO allocation, while one of the first three gets none, violating EQX. With distinct approval pairs, the graph is simple: a multicyclic component needs at least four vertices and five edges, for example \(K_4\) minus one edge. A separate tree requires at least one more edge, so six is minimal. Equality is attained by five agents approving the five distinct edges \(\{a,b\},\{a,c\},\{a,d\},\{b,c\},\{b,d\}\) plus a sixth approving \(\{e,f\}\). These both have identical agent total valuations \(2\).

The algorithm first identifies connected components of \(H\), then checks whether any has \(e<v\) and any has \(e>v\). When there is no mixed obstruction, orient each tree or unicyclic component edge-injectively if every component has \(e\le v\), or choose a spanning unicyclic subgraph in every component if all have \(e\ge v\), to obtain the appropriate matching. Complete the allocation as in Step 2. DFS and adjacency operations take \(O(|N|+|M|)\) time.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` constructs canonical incidence profiles from restricted-growth strings, computes the proposed cycle-rank obstruction, independently computes maximum bipartite matchings, and explicitly enumerates all Pareto-efficient allocations for each profile. It checks PO, EFX, and EQX on the actual bundles. Exhaustive checks cover all \(7\), \(87\), and \(1{,}657\) isomorphism-representative profiles for respectively two, three, and four agents with two distinct approved goods each; an additional large family of five-agent instances is checked for matching versus component criterion. These are finite cross-checks, not the proof for arbitrary sizes. The theoretical argument in the preceding section covers every finite agent and good count in scope.

## Relationship to prior work
Freeman, Sikdar, Vaish and Xia, *Equitable Allocations of Indivisible Goods* (arXiv:1905.10656), Theorem 4, establish polynomial-time existence testing and construction for **general binary** EQ1+EF1+PO instances, and note that PO+EQ1 entails the up-to-any-item variants. Those facts are not claimed as new here; the new assertion is the exact graph-theoretic iff criterion, constructive cycle-rank explanation, and sharp agent thresholds under exactly two approved goods per agent. Hosseini, Pathak, Xia and Zhang, *Simultaneous Envy and Equitability Guarantees* (arXiv:2608.26410v1, 2026-08-26), motivate combining envy/equitability in normalized binary settings; the available abstract and external technical summary discuss a general bounded-agent positive result, a laminar-set regime, and different impossibility instances, rather than the efficiency-constrained degree-two cycle-rank boundary. The complete 2026 paper could not be inspected and remains a material priority risk.

## Limitations
The result requires exactly two approved goods per agent, positive value exactly one on approvals, and no unapproved goods. It does **not** assert that EFX+EQX without PO fails in the four-agent example; the efficiency constraint is essential. The cycle-rank characterization is a specialization of an existing broader polynomial-time algorithm, not an improvement to general-case complexity. The motivating 2026 paper was compared using its public metadata, abstract, and a secondary detailed summary rather than its inaccessible full primary text. Publication priority and stronger differently formulated results remain subject to independent review.
