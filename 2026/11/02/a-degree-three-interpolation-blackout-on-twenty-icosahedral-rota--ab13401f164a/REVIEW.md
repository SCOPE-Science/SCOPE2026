# Scientific review: twenty threefold icosahedral rotations

**Correctness — PASS.** The domain is the twenty-element order-three conjugacy class of the icosahedral subgroup of \(SO(3)\). The Wigner coefficient Gram identity reduces the rank to a finite character kernel. The character values are determined exactly for classes of orders two, three, and five. The checker reconstructs the classes from permutations, verifies annihilating matrix polynomials and a complete set of power traces over \(\mathbb Z[\varphi]\) for degrees zero through nine. These two checks force the stated exact eigenvalue multiplicities because the Gram matrix is symmetric. An independent analytic Gershgorin estimate treats **all** degrees from ten onward. The finite computations are fully finite exhaustive matrix identities, not empirical evidence extrapolated to infinitely many degrees.

**Originality — PASS under bounded primary-literature review.** The closest related results are Fan–Chen–Draayer (1999), which construct icosahedral symmetry-adapted spherical-harmonic functions via \(I\supset C_5\), and McEwen et al. (2015), which provide a band-limited equiangular \(SO(3)\) sampling theorem. The former has class tables and projection operators but no statement of this exact-degree Wigner sampling Gram spectrum on the class of order-three elements; the latter samples different nodes and a different cumulative space. A published complete-standard-Fourier-block theorem for the three-cycle class of \(S_n\), at \(n=5\), gives a rank-sixteen sample range. This is a meaningful previously covered subcomponent of the degree-three analysis after icosahedral restriction, but it does not establish full-rank evaluation at degree two or the permanent degree-four recovery for full Wigner blocks. The known twelve-node order-five icosahedral class is a different restriction operator and does not imply these twenty-node transitions. Comparisons are statement/implication comparisons, not just title comparisons. Residual risk: an older representation-theoretic table may implicitly contain the special Gram spectrum.

**Value — PASS.** The twenty-element threefold conjugacy class is a canonical, symmetry-defined rotation sampling configuration. The sharp and nonmonotone interpolation transition at degrees two, three, and four is a meaningful obstruction to designing monochromatic Wigner sampling; the exact Gram spectra provide all low-degree stability bounds and a uniform high-degree lower bound. This is neither a mere numerical rank observation nor a claimed large-bandwidth sampling theorem.

Scientific limitations: the theorem concerns only the specified nodes, complex sample interpolation, and single exact degrees; it does not recover arbitrary coefficient vectors from fewer sample values. The high-degree stability bound is sufficient rather than sharp. Earlier equivalent formulations may exist.
