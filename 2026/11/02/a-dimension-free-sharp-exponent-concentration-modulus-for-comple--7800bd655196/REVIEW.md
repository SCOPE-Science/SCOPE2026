# Scientific scope and prior-work comparison

The upper square-root rate follows directly by specializing the pre-limit tangential argument in Dantas–Rodríguez, Theorem 3.4, to the supremum norm. It is included as background, not as a novelty claim. Their discussion of optimality concerns the assumptions of the concentration theorem, not this rate's sharpness. The specific contribution is the three-coordinate example ruling out every faster power and the linear estimate when only one coordinate is retained. The example uses an exact tridisk norm and hence does not rely on a numerical extremum or an extrapolation from a grid. The contrast gives the smallest coordinate configuration in which this loss of exponent can arise. The optimal prefactor and broader scalar, degree or target settings are not addressed; priority is limited to the literature inspected.
