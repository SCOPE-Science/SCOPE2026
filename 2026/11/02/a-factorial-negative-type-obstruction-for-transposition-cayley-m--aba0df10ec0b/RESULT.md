# A factorial negative-type obstruction for transposition Cayley metrics

## Finding

Let \(n\ge6\), and equip the symmetric group \(S_n\) with the shortest-path metric of its Cayley graph generated by **all** transpositions:
\[
 d_n(\sigma,\tau)=n-c(\sigma^{-1}\tau),
\]
where \(c(\pi)\) is the number of cycles, including fixed points. Write \(\wp_n\) for the supremal exponent \(p\ge0\) for which \(d_n^p\) has conditional negative type, with \(d_n^0\) interpreted as zero on the diagonal and one off it.

For \(m=n-3\), define the integer polynomial and its fractional-power evaluation by
\[
 P_m(z)=(1-z)(1-2z)\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+jz)
       =\sum_{r=0}^{m+2}a_{m,r}z^r,
 \qquad F_m(p)=\sum_{r=1}^{m+2}a_{m,r}r^p.
\]
There is exactly one \(\beta_n\in(0,1)\) satisfying \(F_{n-3}(\beta_n)=0\). These uniquely specified obstructions obey
\[
 \boxed{\wp_n\le\beta_n,\qquad \beta_{n+1}<\beta_n,\qquad
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\beta_n=0.}
\]
A quantitative factorial bound holds for every integer \(n\ge53\):
\[
 \boxed{\wp_n\le\beta_n<\frac{100}{(n-3)!}.}
\]

**The six-letter case is sharp.** For \(n=6\), let \(\beta_6\) be the unique root in \((0,1)\) of
\[
 \boxed{3-5\,2^p-15\,3^p+4\,4^p+12\,5^p=0.}
\]
Then \(d_6^p\) has conditional negative type **if and only if** \(0\le p\le\beta_6\), with
\[
 0.1541377737<\beta_6<0.1541377739.
\]
The negative type is strict for \(0\le p<\beta_6\). At \(p=\beta_6\), the zero-sum nullspace has dimension exactly \(100\), all from the irreducible \(S_6\)-module of Young shape \((4,1,1)\) under the regular representation. The least Euclidean dimension for realizing \(d_6^{\beta_6}\) as *squared Euclidean distances* is \(619\) (versus \(719\) at smaller nonnegative exponents).

No equality \(\wp_n=\beta_n\) is claimed for \(n\ge7\); the all-order conclusion is an explicit, strictly decreasing obstruction and a factorial upper bound.

## Assumptions and scope

The metric is the **full-transposition** Cayley metric, not the adjacent-transposition (Kendall) metric or Hamming distance on permutations. Every asserted infinite-family inequality concerns the complete finite group \(S_n\), with all \(n!\) vertices. The finite critical classification is for \(S_6\) only. A real symmetric matrix \([d_n(\sigma,\tau)^p]\) has conditional negative type when its quadratic form is nonpositive on all real vectors summing to zero. The exponent \(p=0\) has the standard discrete-metric convention.

## Proof

**1. Harmonic diagonalization.** In \(\mathbb C[S_n]\), let \(J_i=\sum_{j<i}(ji)\), with \(J_1=0\). The classical Jucys identity and the Young-content eigenvalue rule give
\[
 \prod_{i=1}^n(1+zJ_i)=\sum_{\pi\in S_n}z^{\,n-c(\pi)}\pi,
 \qquad
 \prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}(1+(j-i)z)
\]
for the scalar by which this central element acts on the irreducible representation labelled by the partition \(\lambda\vdash n\). Consequently, if the last polynomial is \(\sum_r a_{\lambda,r}z^r\), the convolution matrix \([d_n(\sigma,\tau)^p]\) has scalar eigenvalue
\[
 E_\lambda(p)=\sum_{r\ge1}a_{\lambda,r}r^p
\]
in that representation, with multiplicity \((f^\lambda)^2\), where \(f^\lambda\) is its irreducible dimension. The one-dimensional trivial representation is excluded by the zero-sum condition.

**2. A universal obstruction.** Choose \(\lambda=(n-2,1,1)=(m+1,1,1)\). Its Young contents are \(0,-1,-2,1,\ldots,m\), hence its content polynomial is exactly \(P_m\). Therefore a positive value \(F_m(p)>0\) disproves conditional negative type.

For \(0<p<1\), the positive integral identity
\[
 r^p=\frac{p}{\Gamma(1-p)}\int_0^\infty(1-e^{-rt})t^{-1-p}\,dt
\]
and \(P_m(1)=0\) yield
\[
 F_m(p)=-\frac{p}{\Gamma(1-p)}\int_0^\infty
 P_m(e^{-t})t^{-1-p}\,dt.
\]
Set \(a=\log2\). For \(0<t<a\), \(P_m(e^{-t})<0\); for \(t>a\), it is positive. Write the integrals of its negative and positive parts, respectively, as \(A_m(p)\) and \(B_m(p)\). For \(p_2>p_1\), the factor \(t^{-(p_2-p_1)}\) weights the interval \((0,a)\) strictly more than \((a,\infty)\). Thus \(A_m(p)/B_m(p)\) is strictly increasing in \(p\). Since \(F_m(0)=P_m(1)-1=-1\), whereas \(F_m(1)=P_m'(1)=(m+1)!>0\) for \(m\ge3\), continuity gives exactly one crossing \(\beta_n\in(0,1)\), with \(F_m(p)>0\) whenever \(\beta_n<p<1\). The interval property of negative type then proves \(\wp_n\le\beta_n\) also against exponents at least one.

**3. Strictly descending obstruction.** The recursion \(P_{m+1}(z)=(1+(m+1)z)P_m(z)\) multiplies the two signed integrands by \(1+(m+1)e^{-t}\), a strictly decreasing function of \(t\). At the root of \(F_m\) the two integrals have equal mass; this multiplier increases the negative-part mass strictly more. Hence \(F_{m+1}(\beta_{m+3})>0\), which forces \(\beta_{m+4}<\beta_{m+3}\).

**4. Factorial bound.** Fix \(m\ge50\), put \(p=100/m!\in(0,1)\), and keep \(a=\log2\). For \(0<t<1/m<a\), the inequalities
\[
 1-e^{-t}\ge t/2,\quad 2e^{-t}-1\ge1/2,\quad
 \prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+j e^{-t})\ge(m+1)!e^{-mt}
\]
give
\[
 A_m(p)\ge\frac{(m+1)!}{4e(1-p)}m^{p-1}\ge\frac{m!}{12}.
\]
For \(t\ge a\), put \(R_m(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1+jz)\) and \(M=m(m+1)/2\). Since \(R_m(z)-1\le zMR_m(1/2)\) for \(0\le z\le1/2\), and \(R_m(1/2)=(m+2)!/2^{m+1}\),
\[
 B_m(p)\le\frac{a^{-p}}p+
 \frac{a^{-1-p}M(m+2)!}{2^{m+2}}.
\]
Using \(a>2/3\), \(e<3\), and
\[
 \frac{9m(m+1)^2(m+2)}{2^{m+5}}<\frac1{100}
 \quad(m\ge50),
\]
whose left side decreases beyond \(m=50\), gives
\[
 B_m(p)<\Big(\frac3{200}+\frac1{100}\Big)m!<\frac{m!}{12}\le A_m(p).
\]
Therefore \(F_m(100/m!)>0\) and \(\beta_{m+3}<100/m!\). This also proves \(\beta_n\to0\) and the asserted factorial-scale collapse.

**5. Sharpness for six letters.** For \(n=6\) the eleven Young shapes give eleven scalar eigenbranches from Step 1. The \((4,1,1)\) branch is
\[
 F_3(p)=3-5\,2^p-15\,3^p+4\,4^p+12\,5^p.
\]
The attached exact rational interval certificate isolates its unique root in the displayed rational interval and verifies that the other nine *nontrivial* scalar branches are all strictly negative throughout that interval (the eleventh branch is trivial). Thus all nontrivial branches are nonpositive at \(\beta_6\), with equality only at \((4,1,1)\). Fourier diagonalization proves conditional negative type at this endpoint; the general interval property and its strict lower-exponent form establish the complete interval \([0,\beta_6]\). The hook-length formula gives \(f^{(4,1,1)}=10\), so the regular-representation nullity is \(10^2=100\), and the centered squared-distance Gram rank is \(720-1-100=619\).

It generates every partition of six, reconstructs the corresponding content polynomial using **integer arithmetic**, and uses exact rational upper/lower bounds for logarithms and exponentials to certify all signs, the root bracket, and the Young-hook dimensions. Optionally run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python full_check.py` with NumPy and SciPy. This floating-point test is supplementary; the interval certificate and analytic proof are decisive.

## Relationship to prior work

The group-algebra and content-polynomial identities are classical Jucys–Murphy theory, not new. Lassalle's accessible 2010 treatment provides the representation-theoretic framework. Sánchez (2011) establishes general finite-metric negative-type and interval principles, while Ganesan (2014) studies the automorphism group of the same complete-transposition Cayley graph. Konstantinova–Kravchuk (2022) analyze its ordinary **adjacency** spectrum. None of those inspected treatments states this powered-distance obstruction, its factorial collapse, or the sharp six-letter critical eigenspace. Related prior results on exponential reflection-length kernels for the alternating groups concern a different group and a different kernel family and do not imply the present claims.

## Limitations

For \(n\ge7\), the theorem supplies **only an upper bound** for the unknown optimal exponent \(\wp_n\): other irreducible sectors could cross earlier. No asymptotically matching lower bound or general endpoint-nullity formula is claimed. The constant \(100\) and cutoff \(n\ge53\) are explicit sufficient bounds, not claimed optimal. Searches of accessible literature cannot rule out equivalent older formulas indexed by alternative names such as permutation reflection length or Cayley distance. The six-letter critical claim has a same mathematical model proof and reproducible certificate but no independent external validation.
