# A finite prime-index criterion for globally maximal residue-subgroup GD sets

## Finding
For a prime \(p\) and a multiplicative subgroup \(H\leq\mathbb F_p^\times\), define the set of positive integers
\[S_H=\{n\geq1:n\bmod p\in H\}.\]
Call a set of positive integers GD if it is closed under multiplication and contains no triple \(x,y,z\) with \(x+y=z\), where repeated summands are allowed. If \(H\) is *additively sum-free modulo* \(p\), meaning there are no \(u,v,w\in H\) with \(u+v=w\), then \(S_H\) is GD.

**Theorem.** Assume \(p\) is prime and \(H\leq\mathbb F_p^\times\) is sum-free. Then \(S_H\) is maximal among **all** GD subsets of the positive integers, including nonperiodic supersets, if and only if every minimal proper multiplicative supergroup \(K\supsetneq H\) contains a modular additive triple. Equivalently, for **every prime divisor** \(\ell\) of \((p-1)/|H|\), the unique subgroup of order \(\ell|H|\) contains elements \(a,b\) satisfying \(a+b=1\) in \(\mathbb F_p\). When \(-1\in H\), this is equivalent to \(a-b=1\).

For each prime \(p\geq5\), put \(E_p=S_{\{1,-1\}}\), the two progressions \(n\equiv\pm1\pmod p\). For every prime \(\ell\mid(p-1)/2\) let
\[H_\ell=\{u\in\mathbb F_p^\times:u^{2\ell}=1\}.\]
The special case is an explicit **complete finite test**:
\[
\boxed{E_p\text{ is globally maximal GD}
\iff
\text{for every prime }\ell\mid (p-1)/2\text{, some }a,b\in H_\ell\text{ satisfy }a-b=1.}
\]
If the test fails at \(\ell\), the strictly larger periodic set \(S_{H_\ell}\) is an explicit GD extension. If it passes, no extension of any kind is possible.

In particular, \(E_p\) is maximal for every safe prime \(p=2q+1\) with \(q\) prime; and for every prime \(p=2\cdot3^k+1\) with integer \(k\geq1\). Conversely, \(E_p\) is not maximal for any prime \(p>5\) with \(p\equiv1\pmod4\). In that case the group of fourth roots of unity yields an explicit proper GD extension. For example, \(E_{13}\) is properly contained in the GD set whose allowed residues are \(\{1,5,8,12\}\), while \(E_{19}\) and \(E_{31}\) are maximal.

## Assumptions and scope
The prime \(p\) is any rational prime. The group \(H\) is an arbitrary multiplicative subgroup of the nonzero residue field, **assumed to be sum-free** for the theorem; this is necessary for \(S_H\) itself to be GD. All positive integers in a permitted residue class, rather than a thin selection of them, lie in \(S_H\). Maximality means inclusion-maximality among all sets of positive integers satisfying both GD axioms, without periodicity, finite generation or density assumptions. For \(E_p\), the restriction \(p\geq5\) guarantees that \(\{\pm1\}\) is sum-free modulo \(p\).

## Proof
**Step 1 (lifting modular triples).** Suppose \(T\) is a GD set containing \(S_H\), and \(t\in T\setminus S_H\). If \(p\mid t\), then \(1\in S_H\), \(t+1\equiv1\pmod p\), hence \(1,t,t+1\in T\), impossible. Therefore \(p\nmid t\), and the residue \(r=t\bmod p\) belongs to \(\mathbb F_p^\times\setminus H\).

Write \(K=\langle H,r\rangle\) for the generated multiplicative subgroup. Every \(u\in K\) can be written \(u=r^e h\) for an integer \(e\geq0\) and \(h\in H\), because \(r\) has finite order. By closure under multiplication and because \(S_H\subset T\), we have \(t^e s\in T\) for **every positive** \(s\equiv h\pmod p\).

Assume for contradiction that \(u_1+u_2=u_3\) in \(\mathbb F_p\) for \(u_i\in K\). Write \(u_i=r^{e_i}h_i\) and choose integer representatives \(h_i\in\{1,\ldots,p-1\}\) of their residues. Put \(A=t^{e_1}\), \(B=t^{e_2}\), \(C=t^{e_3}\). The integer \(N=(Ch_3-Ah_1-Bh_2)/p\) exists by the modular triple. The integer \(g=\gcd(A,B,C)=t^{\min(e_1,e_2,e_3)}\) divides the numerator and is coprime to \(p\); thus \(g\mid N\). Bézout's theorem for the coefficients \((A,B,-C)\) supplies integer \(z_1,z_2,z_3\) with
\[Az_1+Bz_2-Cz_3=N.\]
Consequently \(s_i=h_i+pz_i\) satisfy \(As_1+Bs_2=Cs_3\). All \(s_i\) can be made positive simultaneously: increase \((z_1,z_2,z_3)\) by \((LC,LC,L(A+B))\) for sufficiently large integer \(L\geq0\). Then \(As_1,Bs_2,Cs_3\in T\), a prohibited additive triple. This proves that **the group \(K\) generated by \(H\) and any added term must be sum-free modulo \(p\)**.

**Step 2 (equivalence of maximalities).** If some larger sum-free multiplicative subgroup \(K\supsetneq H\) exists, the full residue-class set \(S_K\) is GD and properly contains \(S_H\). Conversely, if a GD superset \(T\supsetneq S_H\) exists, choose \(t\in T\setminus S_H\). Step 1 shows that \(K=\langle H,t\bmod p\rangle\) is a strictly larger sum-free subgroup. Therefore \(S_H\) is globally maximal GD if and only if \(H\) is inclusion-maximal among sum-free multiplicative subgroups of \(\mathbb F_p^\times\).

**Step 3 (prime-index reduction).** The group \(\mathbb F_p^\times\) is cyclic. Its subgroups containing \(H\) are ordered by divisors of \((p-1)/|H|\), and their minimal proper members have orders \(\ell|H|\) for the prime divisors \(\ell\) of that index. If a larger sum-free supergroup exists, at least one of these minimal supergroups is sum-free. So maximality is equivalent to each minimal supergroup being non-sum-free. For any multiplicative subgroup \(K\), the relation \(u+v=w\) with \(u,v,w\in K\) is equivalent, after division by \(w\), to \(a+b=1\) for \(a,b\in K\). When \(-1\in K\), this is also equivalent to \(a-b=1\), because multiplying one summand by \(-1\) stays inside \(K\). The difference formulation is thus valid for all groups \(H_\ell\) in the special case \(E_p\), but the general test uses \(a+b=1\). This proves the general theorem and its explicit \(E_p\) criterion.

**Step 4 (the stated prime families).** If \((p-1)/2=q\) is prime, the only minimal proper supergroup of \(\{\pm1\}\) is the entire group, which contains \(1,2\) and therefore the adjacent pair \(2-1=1\). If \((p-1)/2=3^k\), the only minimal proper supergroup has order \(6\); every such subgroup contains a primitive sixth root \(\zeta\) satisfying \(\zeta^2-\zeta+1=0\), hence \(\zeta-\zeta^2=1\). Finally, if \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), the subgroup \(\{1,-1,i,-i\}\), with \(i^2=-1\), has no difference \(1\) between two of its members unless \(p=5\). Indeed, differences between distinct fourth roots lie among \(\pm2,\pm2i,\pm1\pm i\); equality to \(1\) forces characteristic \(3\) or \(5\), and characteristic \(3\) is incompatible with \(p\equiv1\pmod4\). For \(p>5\), this subgroup is sum-free and properly extends \(\{\pm1\}\). For \(p=19\), the only minimal order is six; for \(p=31\), the order-six and order-ten subgroups both contain adjacent residues (the latter contains \(1,2\)).

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify.py` uses exact integer arithmetic without third-party dependencies. It checks the minimal-supergroup criterion against a separate scan of all larger multiplicative subgroups, for all sum-free multiplicative subgroups of primes below \(501\). It also checks the safe-prime and fourth-root families, named examples, and the divisibility condition used by the lifting argument on enumerated modular triples. In the performed run, 421 sum-free subgroups and 1,020 modular lifting divisibility cases passed. These finite checks are supplementary; the argument in Steps 1–4 supplies the proof for all primes.

## Relationship to prior work
Demirov and Trifonov (arXiv:2607.09937v1, made public 2026-07-10) defined these multiplication-closed, addition-avoiding sequences, proved nonmaximality of the residue-one progression for every modulus at least four by adjoining its negative residue class, and asked for maximal extensions, noting the absence of a formula valid for all primes. Their statement does **not** classify when that two-class extension is itself globally maximal. The present theorem supplies a finite exact criterion for that question, even against arbitrary nonperiodic supersets. It does **not** solve their full extension problem for primes where the two-class extension fails maximality.

Alon and Bourgain, *Additive Patterns in Multiplicative Subgroups*, 2014, studied the existence and absence of additive triples inside finite-field multiplicative subgroups, including large sum-free examples. Those theorems establish the relevance of subgroup sum-freeness but do not assert the lifting equivalence for arbitrary GD integer supersets or this precise prime-index maximality criterion.

## Limitations
The reduction uses the inclusion of **every** positive integer in each allowed residue class. It does not apply directly to sparse multiplicatively closed sets, composite-modulus unit groups, or extensions of \(E_p\) when \(E_p\) is nonmaximal. For primes failing the test, the constructed larger GD set need not itself be maximal. The finite criterion is elementary to compute but does not yield a simple congruence-only classification of primes for which \(E_p\) is maximal. An earlier unindexed or implicit occurrence of the reduction remains possible; no claim of absolute priority or independent review is made.
