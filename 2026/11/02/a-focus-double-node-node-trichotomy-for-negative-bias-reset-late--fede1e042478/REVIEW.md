# Scientific review: negative-bias reset latency

## Correctness — PASS
The statement fixes \(0<D<1\), \(0<T<1+D\), a bias-independent seed \(u>0,v\ge0\), and the convention of contact at \(x\le0\). The proof computes a second-order affine recurrence before first contact, proves that a negative input forces eventual contact for all permitted parameters, and derives the exact two-more-step absorption. For distinct positive roots, the closed-form forcing response gives a positive decreasing-tail threshold. At the repeated root, the unforced term is \(\bigl(u+n(u+v/r)\bigr)r^n\); its polynomial prefactor is responsible for the logarithm-of-logarithm term. In the focus case a nontrivial rotated unforced trajectory must have a negative coordinate in bounded time. The limiting statements concern fixed parameters and fixed seeds; no infinite claim is inferred from numerical experiments. The embedded exact-rational verifier checks 36 finite trajectories and the claimed algebraic recurrences.

## Originality — PASS, subject to residual priority risk
The generalized family itself is credited to Yamazaki and Ohmori (arXiv:2312.13292, Eq. 11) and its subsequent analysis to Ohmori and Yamazaki (arXiv:2402.06230). The latter covers immediate two-step reset after entering the negative quadrant and qualitative finite absorption for the oscillatory negative-input case; these facts are not asserted to be novel. The later fixed-seed limit \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) across all three stable positive-branch spectral types, exact bias-threshold sequence, and critical \(\log\log\) term are not implied by the claims inspected there. The existing homogeneous-border classification has \(B=0\) rather than a bias approaching zero; its persistent orbits do not supply quantitative escape thresholds at \(B<0\). Related phase-bottleneck logarithms concern a different parameter and orbit mechanism. Simpson's zero-determinant border-collision analysis focuses on other parameter regions and bifurcation attractors, not the stated first-contact law. No equivalent result was identified in the searches performed; this is evidence, not proof of global novelty. General prior results on first crossing of affine recurrences remain a residual possibility.

## Mathematical value — PASS
At the switching border, a nonzero positive seed may remain on the active half-plane indefinitely, but any negative input destroys that persistence. The theorem quantifies how fast this happens, distinguishing stable spiral, simple stable node, and defective stable node. The repeated-root \(\log\log\) correction separates the third type even at fixed exponential contraction scale. Exact thresholds provide finite, computable cutoffs rather than merely asymptotic eigenvalue statements. This gives a reproducible, model-specific first-passage classification relevant to border-collision latency and transient excitability; it does not rely on arbitrary slices, numerical-only novelty, or improvement of a routine constant.

## Scope and remaining uncertainty
All conclusions apply to the indicated stable spectral range with fixed nonnegative-quadrant seed. They do not cover parameters on the unit-stability boundary, biased initial seeds, or perturbed/smoothed systems. A previously unindexed derivation or a more general first-passage theorem could partially subsume these formulas. A separate independent mathematical validation has not been undertaken.
