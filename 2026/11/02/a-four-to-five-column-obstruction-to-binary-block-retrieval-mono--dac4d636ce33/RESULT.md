# A four-to-five-column obstruction to binary block-retrieval monotonicity

## Finding

Let \(F_1=\langle e_1\rangle\) and \(F_2=\langle e_2,e_3\rangle\) be complementary files in \(\mathbb F_2^3\). For a rank-three binary generator matrix \(G\) with \(n\) labeled columns, sample a column independently and uniformly at every round, with replacement. Define \(E_i(G)\) to be the expected first time the span of sampled columns contains all of \(F_i\).

The four-column matrix
\[
G_4=[\,e_1\ \ e_1\ \ e_2\ \ e_3\,]
\]
has
\[
(E_1(G_4),E_2(G_4))=(2,6).
\]
Yet every rank-three five-column binary generator matrix \(H\) satisfies the *sharp dichotomy*
\[
\boxed{E_1(H)>2\quad\text{or}\quad
(E_1(H)=5/3\ \text{and}\ E_2(H)\ge15/2).}
\]
In particular, no five-column code weakly improves both expected file-retrieval times of \(G_4\). Thus the one-step inclusion, interpreted under Pareto domination, from the four-column to five-column achievability region fails even though one file has dimension two. This is a counterexample to Conjecture 2 in the cited 2026 block-retrieval paper, in its fixed-field reading.

The boundary of the second alternative is attained: three copies of \(e_1\) together with \(e_2,e_3\) give \((E_1,E_2)=(5/3,15/2)\).

## Assumptions and scope

The field is exactly \(\mathbb F_2\). Codes are length-\(n\), rank-three linear codes, represented by multiset-valued generator columns. Coordinates are labeled, so repeated columns remain separate, equiprobable sampling locations. Zero columns are allowed for the universal five-column assertion. Recovery is noiseless and occurs only when the complete file subspace is contained in the sampled-column span. Sampling is independent with replacement. Dominating \((E_1,E_2)\) means achieving both expected times no larger.

Write \(\tau_1\) for the first recovery time of \(F_1\). Since \(\tau_1\) is a positive integer with finite mean, \(E_1=\sum_{t\ge0}\mathbb P(\tau_1>t)\). In \(\mathbb F_2\), a sampled column generates \(F_1\) on its own if and only if it equals \(e_1\).

## Proof

**The four-column point.** Each draw from \(G_4\) selects \(e_1\) with probability \(1/2\), so \(E_1(G_4)=2\). Recovery of \(F_2\) requires seeing both of its separate columns \(e_2,e_3\). Each has probability \(1/4\); the usual two-coupon calculation gives \(E_2(G_4)=4(1+1/2)=6\). The matrix has rank three.

**All five-column matrices.** Let \(H\) be any rank-three binary matrix of length five, and let \(m\) be its number of columns exactly equal to \(e_1\). Rank three forces \(0\le m\le3\). We consider every possible \(m\), without assuming that other columns are nonzero or distinct.

If \(m=0\), no single draw recovers \(F_1\), and two draws of the same labeled column cannot recover it. Those two draws are identical with probability \(1/5\). Thus
\[
E_1(H)\ge 1+1+1/5=11/5>2.
\]

If \(m=1\), then \(\mathbb P(\tau_1>1)=4/5\). Among the four other labeled columns, the number of unordered index pairs whose vector sum is \(e_1\) is at most four: the non-\(e_1\) vectors split into disjoint complementary type-pairs \(\{u,u+e_1\}\), and four positions can create at most \(\lfloor4^2/4\rfloor=4\) cross-pairs. Two draws without \(e_1\) have probability \(16/25\); at most eight of the twenty-five ordered index pairs can already recover \(e_1\) through a sum. Hence \(\mathbb P(\tau_1>2)\ge8/25\) and
\[
E_1(H)\ge1+4/5+8/25=53/25>2.
\]

Suppose \(m=2\), and call the three remaining labeled vectors \(u,v,w\). If some pair, say \(u,v\), sums to \(e_1\), it is the *only* such pair: otherwise all three vectors would lie in the two-dimensional span of \(e_1\) and one of them, contradicting rank three. Then recovery of \(F_1\) occurs precisely when an \(e_1\)-column has appeared or both \(u\) and \(v\) have appeared. For every integer \(t\ge0\),
\[
\mathbb P(\tau_1>t)=2(2/5)^t-(1/5)^t,
\qquad
E_1(H)=\frac{2}{1-2/5}-\frac{1}{1-1/5}=\frac{25}{12}>2.
\]
If no pair among \(u,v,w\) sums to \(e_1\), then those three vectors can recover \(e_1\) only if all three are present. If they span \(e_1\), they must be three distinct nonzero vectors satisfying \(u+v+w=e_1\). Inclusion-exclusion gives
\[
E_1(H)=\frac{3}{1-2/5}-\frac{3}{1-1/5}+1=\frac94>2.
\]
Otherwise only a direct draw of \(e_1\) recovers the file, and \(E_1(H)=5/2>2\). Thus every matrix with \(m\le2\) satisfies the strict alternative.

Finally, suppose \(m=3\). Let \(u,v\) be the two other columns. Since \(H\) has rank three, \(e_1,u,v\) are linearly independent. In particular, \(e_1\) cannot be generated by \(u,v\), and \(E_1(H)=5/3\). The two-dimensional file \(F_2\) does not contain \(e_1\); it therefore cannot be contained in the span of \(e_1\) and only one of \(u,v\). Recovering \(F_2\) necessarily requires observing *both* of the two individually sampled positions \(u,v\). Each is selected with probability \(1/5\), so
\[
E_2(H)\ge5(1+1/2)=15/2>6.
\]
Equality is attained by \(H=[e_1,e_1,e_1,e_2,e_3]\). All cases are exhausted, proving the dichotomy and the failure of five-column Pareto domination. \(\square\)

## Verification

`verify.py` is a standalone Python standard-library checker. It independently computes each retrieval expectation in two ways: exact rational subset counts using the published formula \(E_i=nH_n-\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}\alpha_i(s)/\binom{n-1}{s}\), and the exact absorbing subspace Markov recursion. It enumerates all \(476\) rank-three multisets of five binary columns, **including zero columns**, comparing both methods for every matrix. Exactly twelve have \(E_1\le2\); all have \(E_1=5/3\), and their smallest \(E_2\) is \(15/2\). The source file and its actual output are included for replication; execute `python3 verify.py`.

The complete mathematical argument above is valid independently of enumeration. Finite computations verify this finite instance exactly, not an assertion over other block lengths, fields, or file dimensions.

## Relationship to prior work

Bar-Lev's 2026 block-retrieval paper introduces the two-file achievability regions and explicitly conjectures that enlarging the code length by one admits weak coordinatewise improvement when at least one file has dimension at least two (Conjecture 2, Section VII-A, revised version). Its Remark 10 already gives a failure when **both** files have dimension one, but suggests that this mechanism has no analog when a file is larger. The example here has dimensions \((1,2)\) and invalidates that broader monotonicity assertion. The earlier two-dimensional witness does not by itself imply failure at dimension three, because mixed columns could conceivably dominate an enlarged-file layout; the five-column structural dichotomy closes that logical gap.

The distinct universal *hyperbolic* conjecture \(\dim(F_1)/E_1+\dim(F_2)/E_2\le1\) is **not** refuted. The related 2026 paper by Vlachos and Bar-Lev, and the published dimension-three one-mixed-column result, establish hyperbolic inequalities, not inclusion of code-length achievability regions. Neither implies the present non-domination statement.

## Limitations

The non-monotonicity is established for the fixed binary field, information dimension three, complementary file dimensions one and two, and the single step from four to five sampled coordinates. Nothing here proves failure for other field orders, larger lengths, or more than two files. The sharp five-column dichotomy is valid for all rank-three binary generator matrices, but the claim is not about nonlinear encoders, noisy recovery, nonuniform sampling, or deletion. Independent external validation and publication-priority certainty are not claimed.
