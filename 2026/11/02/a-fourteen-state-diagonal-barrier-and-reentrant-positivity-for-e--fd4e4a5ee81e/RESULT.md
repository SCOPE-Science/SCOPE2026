# A fourteen-state diagonal barrier and reentrant positivity for extrapolated backward Euler on directed cycles

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge 2\), let \(S_n\) be the cyclic permutation matrix with \((S_n)_{i,i+1\pmod n}=1\), and let \(Q_n=S_n-I_n\). The unit-rate continuous-time Markov chain generated by \(Q_n\) moves one site forward around a directed cycle. The unmodified, second-order, two-level Richardson-extrapolated backward Euler endpoint matrix is
\[
E_n(h)=2(I_n-hQ_n/2)^{-2}-(I_n-hQ_n)^{-1},\qquad h\ge0.
\]
All these matrices have row sums equal to one, but their entries need not be nonnegative. Their entrywise positivity has the following sharp features.

1. For \(2\le n\le4\), \(E_n(h)\) is entrywise strictly positive for every \(h>0\), and \(E_n(0)=I_n\).
2. For every \(n\ge5\), the entry \((E_n(h))_{0,4}\) is negative for all sufficiently small positive \(h\), with the exact expansion
\[
(E_n(h))_{0,4}=-\frac38h^4+O(h^5)\qquad(h\downarrow0).
\]
3. The diagonal of \(E_n(h)\) is positive for every \(h\ge0\) and every \(2\le n\le13\). For every \(n\ge14\), all its diagonal entries are strictly negative at the single, common timestep \(h=6\). Thus fourteen is the least dimension permitting a negative diagonal.
4. At the first diagonal-failure dimension \(n=14\), the **complete** set of timesteps producing an entrywise nonnegative endpoint matrix is disconnected:
\[
\boxed{\{h\ge0:E_{14}(h)\ge0\text{ entrywise}\}=\{0\}\cup[\alpha,\beta]\cup[\gamma,\infty),}
\]
where \(\alpha\) is the unique positive root of the thirteenth-hop coefficient numerator (in \((3,4)\)), and \(\beta<\gamma\) are the two positive roots of the diagonal coefficient numerator (in \((5,6)\), \((6,9)\), respectively). Numerically,
\[
\alpha=3.066370751752789344\ldots,\quad
\beta=5.565679616802894299\ldots,\quad
\gamma=7.994442145531642024\ldots.
\]
In particular, the same method and chain are entrywise positive at \(h=4\), have a negative diagonal at \(h=6\), and become entrywise positive again at \(h=10\). No state-limiting or clipping is applied.

## Assumptions and scope
The dimension is an integer \(n\ge2\), the forward transition rate is exactly one, and all computations are in exact arithmetic. The initial state can be any nonnegative row vector; entrywise nonnegativity of \(E_n(h)\) is precisely the condition for preserving all such initial states. Because \(Q_n\mathbf1=0\), row sums of \(E_n(h)\) are exactly one. A matrix with a negative entry is not a Markov transition matrix, irrespective of its row sums or spectral stability. The orientation of the cycle changes only the placement of the coefficients, not their signs.

The result concerns the **unmodified two-level extrapolate** \(2B(h/2)^2-B(h)\), where \(B(t)=(I-tQ_n)^{-1}\). It does not assert an analogous classification for other extrapolation orders, variable rates, symmetric random walks, or nonlinear limiters. The numbers \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) are defined by exact polynomial conditions below, not by the printed decimal approximations.

## Proof
**Step 1: exact cyclic-resolvent coefficients.** Put
\[
s=\frac{h}{h+2},\qquad r=\frac{h}{h+1},\qquad 0<s,r<1\quad(h>0).
\]
The cyclic identity \(S_n^n=I\) and geometric sums give
\[
(I-hQ_n)^{-1}=\frac{1-r}{1-r^n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}r^jS_n^j,
\]
\[
(I-hQ_n/2)^{-2}=(1-s)^2\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}s^j\frac{(j+1)+(n-j-1)s^n}{(1-s^n)^2}S_n^j.
\]
For the second identity, collect powers \(j+kn\) in \(\sum_{k\ge0}(k+1)s^k\), obtaining \(\sum_{k\ge0}(j+kn+1)x^k=((j+1)+(n-j-1)x)/(1-x)^2\). Consequently \(E_n(h)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}c_{n,j}(h)S_n^j\), where
\[
\boxed{c_{n,j}(h)=2(1-s)^2s^j\frac{j+1+(n-j-1)s^n}{(1-s^n)^2}-\frac{(1-r)r^j}{1-r^n}.}
\]
These are the complete matrix coefficients. The displayed formula and \(E_n(0)=I_n\) together determine the endpoint exactly.

**Step 2: positive-denominator polynomial reduction.** Define positive functions for \(h>0\),
\[
A_n(h)=(h+2)^n-h^n,\quad B_n(h)=(h+1)^n-h^n,\quad d_{n,j}=\max\{0,j+2-n\},
\]
and the integer polynomial
\[
\begin{split}
P_{n,j}(h)={}&8(h+2)^{\max\{0,n-j-2\}}\bigl[(j+1)(h+2)^n+(n-j-1)h^n\bigr]B_n(h)\\
&-(h+1)^{n-j-1}A_n(h)^2(h+2)^{d_{n,j}}.
\end{split}
\]
Directly clearing the positive denominators of Step 1 yields
\[
\boxed{c_{n,j}(h)=\frac{h^jP_{n,j}(h)}{A_n(h)^2B_n(h)(h+2)^{d_{n,j}}}\qquad(h>0).}
\]
Therefore the sign of every coefficient for \(h>0\) equals the sign of its explicit integer polynomial \(P_{n,j}\). These polynomials are generated exactly by the accompanying standalone `verify.py` and require no external data.

**Step 3: the small-cycle and small-step boundaries.** For every \(n=2,3,4\) and \(0\le j<n\), every coefficient of the polynomial \(P_{n,j}\) is nonnegative and at least one is positive. This is a finite exact coefficient check (reproduced in `verify.py`); therefore \(c_{n,j}(h)>0\) for all \(h>0\). For \(n\ge5\), expand the rational matrix function at the origin:
\[
E_n(h)=I_n+hQ_n+\tfrac12h^2Q_n^2+0\cdot h^3Q_n^3-\tfrac38h^4Q_n^4+O(h^5).
\]
Since \(Q_n=S_n-I_n\), and the four-hop displacement is distinct from the displacements \(0,1,2,3\) when \(n\ge5\), the coefficient of \(S_n^4\) is exactly \(-3h^4/8+O(h^5)\). This proves items 1 and 2.

**Step 4: no diagonal zero before fourteen states.** Apply the signed Sturm-sequence algorithm to \(P_{n,0}\), using the variations at \(h=0\) and positive infinity, ignoring zero entries in the sign lists. The integer-arithmetic certificate is

| \(n\) | \(V(0)\) | \(V(+\infty)\) | Positive roots |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|2|1|1|0|
|3|2|2|0|
|4|3|3|0|
|5|5|5|0|
|6|6|6|0|
|7|8|8|0|
|8|9|9|0|
|9|11|11|0|
|10|12|12|0|
|11|14|14|0|
|12|15|15|0|
|13|17|17|0|
|14|19|17|2|

For \(2\le n\le13\), \(P_{n,0}(0)>0\) and it has no positive real root, so \(c_{n,0}(h)>0\) for all \(h>0\). At \(h=0\), the diagonal is one. The exact Sturm calculations (Euclidean remainders over rational coefficients) are replayed by the verifier, not inferred from floating-point experiments.

**Step 5: a uniform witness for all \(n\ge14\).** At \(h=6\), the diagonal coefficient from Step 1 is
\[
c_{n,0}(6)=\frac18\frac{1+(n-1)z_n}{(1-z_n)^2}-\frac17\frac1{1-w_n},\qquad z_n=(3/4)^n,\quad w_n=(6/7)^n.
\]
For \(n=14,15,16\), direct exact rational evaluation gives \(c_{n,0}(6)<0\). For \(n\ge17\), use \(1/(1-w_n)>1\) and
\[
\frac{1+(n-1)z_n}{(1-z_n)^2}=1+\frac{(n+1)z_n-z_n^2}{(1-z_n)^2}
\]
to obtain
\[
c_{n,0}(6)<-\frac1{56}+\frac{(n+1)z_n}{8(1-z_n)^2}<0.
\]
The last inequality is the exact rational condition \(7(n+1)z_n<(1-z_n)^2\), true at \(n=17\) and preserved for all larger \(n\): \((n+1)(3/4)^n\) strictly decreases and \((1-(3/4)^n)^2\) strictly increases. This proves the sharp fourteen-state diagonal onset.

**Step 6: exact complete positivity set in fourteen states.** Apply the same exact Sturm method separately to every \(P_{14,j}\), \(0\le j\le13\). For \(j=1,2,3\) there is no positive root and the coefficients are positive for \(h>0\) (for \(j=3\), its numerator has a zero at \(h=0\), but all its polynomial coefficients are nonnegative and its linear coefficient is positive). For each \(4\le j\le12\) there is exactly one positive root, with \(P_{14,j}(0)<0<P_{14,j}(3)\). The numerator \(P_{14,13}\) likewise has exactly one positive root, but it lies in \((3,4)\), with \(P_{14,13}(3)<0<P_{14,13}(4)\). Therefore all off-diagonal entries become nonnegative simultaneously at \(\alpha\), the root of \(P_{14,13}\), and stay nonnegative thereafter.

The diagonal numerator \(P_{14,0}\) has exactly two positive roots: its signs at \(h=5,6,9\) are respectively \(+,-,+\). Hence the roots \(\beta\in(5,6)\) and \(\gamma\in(6,9)\) are unique and its sign is positive outside \((\beta,\gamma)\). Since \(\alpha<4<5<\beta<\gamma\), intersecting the diagonal and off-diagonal sign constraints, and adding the exact identity at \(h=0\), proves the full three-component positivity set. All endpoints are included since nonnegativity allows a zero entry.

## Verification
The supplied `verify.py` is standalone Python using SymPy for rational polynomial algebra, Sturm chains and exact matrix inversion. Executing `python verify.py` completes **234 exact assertions** and prints all diagonal Sturm variations, the complete fourteen-state root-count classifications and explicit rational sign witnesses at \(h=4,6,10\). The code directly compares the rational cyclic-kernel formula against full \(n\times n\) inverses for \(n\in\{2,3,4,5,14\}\) and several rational timesteps, and independently checks every row sum. All conclusions for arbitrary \(n\) follow from the analytic derivation or the integer/rational finite certificates described above; a finite numerical grid or a timeout is not used as a proof of any continuum statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Constantinescu and Sandu's 2008 public technical report, subsequently developed into the 2010 article, presents the Aitken–Neville extrapolation construction and its linear stability theory. The present method is its standard two-level backward Euler specialization; its construction and second-order consistency are not claimed as new. Prior analysis of the same scalar stability function establishes a sign-loss threshold \(2+2\sqrt2\) for scalar decay, which does not determine the finite cyclic convolution coefficients, the sharp diagonal dimension threshold, or disconnected fourteen-state positivity windows. Quezada de Luna and Ketcheson later use implicit Euler extrapolation stages together with nonlinear bound-preserving limiting; their limited method is different from the unmodified matrix classified here. Related positivity analyses for periodic advection using \(\theta\)-methods investigate different time integrators and semi-discretizations.

The contribution is precisely the finite-cycle entrywise classification proved above, particularly its minimal fourteen-state negative diagonal and exact reentrant positivity set. It is not an assertion of novelty for the extrapolation method itself, the general existence of nonpositive extrapolates, or the general theory of monotonicity.

## Limitations
The exact safe set is fully classified only for \(n=14\); the theorem gives sharp low-dimensional unconditional positivity, universal small-step failure for \(n\ge5\), and a uniform diagonal-negative witness for \(n\ge14\), but does not classify all safe step intervals for every dimension \(n\ge15\). Time steps and rates are normalized to the unit-rate directed cycle. Root approximations in the finding are descriptive only; exact root isolations follow from the polynomial/Sturm certificate. The conclusion is subject to possible overlap with specialized earlier literature under alternative matrix-function or stochastic-positivity terminology. No independent mathematical validation is asserted.
