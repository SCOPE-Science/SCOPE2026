# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof reconstructs the quotient norm through the antisymmetric Jacobian lift and checks the defining total-degree filtration. Angular integration diagonalizes the product-radial compression exactly and reduces the eigenvalues to probabilities for independent beta variables. On the \(N^{-1}\) collar scale, their logarithms converge to scaled gamma variables; ordered-partition Riemann sums then establish the full continuous-test spectral law. Threshold sets have zero measure by strict radial monotonicity. Both extreme-collar coefficients follow from explicit Dirichlet integration and a gamma-tail dominated-convergence argument. In the latter, the domination by a gamma tail with shape \(d(\alpha+1)\) is essential for the unbounded integration range. The standalone checker reproduces finite cases and integral identities without replacing the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)

Misra–Shyam Roy–Zhang (2011, arXiv:1106.3868, §2.1, Theorem 2.3) supplies the orthogonal Schur basis and weighted quotient-space model. Ghosh–Narayanan (2022, arXiv:2202.03184, §5.1, §6.2) supplies the Toeplitz quotient-cover framework and primarily studies algebraic product/commutation identities. Neither inspected work states the product-coordinate shrinking-collar diagonal spectrum, the sharp \(N^{-1}\) critical window, the gamma-convolution spectral measure or the two gamma-function trace-defect endpoints. The closest broad analogues — Pérez-Esteva–Uribe (2017, arXiv:1801.00366) on singular Fock-space operators and Miheisi–O'Loughlin (2024, arXiv:2404.03087) on Hardy model-space truncations — differ in both Hilbert-space construction and symbol/degree scaling. Indexed mathematical comparisons show related Bergman spectral-filling and symmetric-Fock calculations, but not statements covering this theorem. An earlier hidden general weighted-quotient Szegő principle remains possible; the claim does not assert a certified literature priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The quotient geometry changes the degree lattice from a cube to an ordered simplex. The theorem determines *every* limiting spectral statistic of a canonical shrinking product-coordinate concentration operator, identifies the sole nontrivial scaling exponent and gives sharp, calculable small- and large-collar projection-defect constants for all fixed complex dimensions and power weights. These constants are natural spectral uncertainty invariants, not arbitrary computed slices. The large-collar coefficient detects a codimension-one face and has a gamma absolute-difference interpretation; that structural explanation enables use as a benchmark for more general quotient-domain localization problems.
