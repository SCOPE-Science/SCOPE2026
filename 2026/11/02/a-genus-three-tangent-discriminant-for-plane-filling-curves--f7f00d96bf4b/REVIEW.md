# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)
The displayed restriction identities factor the degree-\(q+3\) curve on each rational line into the simple-root polynomial of its \(q+1\) rational points and a nonzero quadratic. Their discriminants agree on two dual charts with a single homogeneous quartic. The exceptional line is checked separately. The quartic's singularity calculation treats characteristic three and seven explicitly, and establishes equivalence with multiple roots of the septic; for defining-field coefficients any multiple root would be rational. The two incidence double counts and Hasse–Weil bound then prove the tangent-line and flex formulas. The independent script verifies symbolic identities and finite-field cases, without being used as an infinite proof.

## Originality (PASS, with residual risk)
The full eight-page original 2023 Asgarli–Ghioca paper was inspected, including its rational smoothness criterion, line-component analysis, and final conjectures. It does not state the quartic tangent discriminant or the rational contact counts. Homma's different degree-\(q+2\) classification is background and does not imply the specific quartic; the earlier recorded two-contact theorem concerns a distinct degree-\(q-1\) curve. Searches for the explicit quartic, rational flexes, tangent two-contact counts, equivalences and larger covering results identified no stronger result. Standard binary-quadratic discriminant algebra and the Weil bound are acknowledged. The principal residual risk is an unindexed or generalized foliation/discriminant treatment from which the full reduction may follow.

## Mathematical value (PASS)
These are natural incidence invariants of the exact plane-filling pencil introduced to study extremal rational-point geometry, not an arbitrary slice. A degree growing with \(q\) has its rational flex and double-contact tangent counts controlled exactly by a fixed-degree smooth genus-three curve. The quartic smoothness criterion matches squarefreeness of the source's septic polynomial, and the Weil error for approximately half the rational lines is only \(O(\sqrt q)\). This is a structural reduction applicable to every root-free parameter in every odd finite field, while avoiding the unresolved global smoothness conjecture.

## Limits
The result neither resolves global smoothness of the plane-filling curves nor counts nonrational geometric contacts. Its exact numerical quartic point count remains parameter-dependent. Independent external mathematical validation has not been carried out, and literature-priority uncertainty remains.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
