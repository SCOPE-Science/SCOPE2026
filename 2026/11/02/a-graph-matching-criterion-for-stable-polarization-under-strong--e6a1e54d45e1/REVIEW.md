# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The source equations and positive normalized weights are specified exactly. The necessity argument uses the sign of a convex combination. The converse uses a complete quantitative contraction construction, including a bound on the small cross-sign weights, the derivative of normalized weights, the scalar stability inequality \(K\operatorname{sech}^2 r<1\), and a Dini-derivative exponential-attraction estimate. The disjoint-edge criterion is proved both ways by a rooted graph-coloring construction. The standalone finite-graph and four-path computations passed; finite enumeration is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to residual bibliographic risk

The primary 2023 paper provides the model, scalar saturation and numerical network polarization and analyzes complete-network two-group examples, not the arbitrary-graph sign-pattern/matching equivalence. A 2022 sigmoidal bounded-confidence study contains graph-sensitive stability results for a different influence and saturation law; those do not imply this theorem. Searches for equivalent sign arrangements, two-edge matching criteria and strong-homophily fixed points found no equivalent published statement. This is best-knowledge originality, not proof that no unknown source contains it.

## Value — PASS

The new topological criterion answers which connected networks can support genuine persistent binary disagreement under strong homophily without deleting any cross-cutting tie. It identifies a sharp four-person threshold and classifies **every** sign pattern for arbitrary finite graphs at sufficiently large homophily, replacing a qualitative or simulated phenomenon with exact structural necessary-and-sufficient conditions. The contraction estimate also controls finite-homophily deviation from the limiting polarized state. This is not merely an arbitrary small-graph numerical experiment.

## Boundaries

No global attraction, a universal explicit exponent threshold, an all-equilibria classification at moderate homophily, or stochastic statement is claimed. Zero-coordinate equilibria are outside the sign-obstruction statement. The numerical example is not an independent formal verification. The related literature is nonexhaustive.
