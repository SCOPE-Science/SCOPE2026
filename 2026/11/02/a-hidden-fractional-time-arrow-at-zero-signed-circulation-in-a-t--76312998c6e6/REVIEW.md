# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The stationary fractional-Gaussian convolution exists for every specified exponent and stable symmetric confinement. Its cross spectrum follows by multiplying the explicit transfer matrix with the diagonal input spectrum; the input normalization is checked against the variance of fractional-Brownian increments. Absolute convergence gives the derivative of the signed lag covariance. A beta integral yields the unique zero-circulation ratio. Subtracting the vanishing linear term, changing variables and applying an explicit integrable majorant produces the fractional cusp with exact coefficient. The coefficient never vanishes at distinct exponents and positive bath strengths. Independent real-frequency quadratures confirm the algebra for four representative parameter combinations. The proof is analytical; the numerical checks do not establish the full theorem.

## Originality — PASS with residual risk

The founding fractional Brownian gyrator paper (arXiv:2204.12145) already derives stationary currents and angular velocity, and discusses a small-coupling cancellation. Those statements are not novel here. The new item compares a **signed two-time** cross-covariance at its exactly calibrated zero *first derivative*, and proves the first surviving small-lag power and amplitude. The white-noise gyrator spectral paper arXiv:2201.04903, finite-memory thermal-bath paper arXiv:2002.07254, and white-noise finite-increment angular-momentum paper arXiv:2307.05248 do not exhibit this fractional-memory cusp in the inspected relevant parts. The closest prior research records results involve white-noise or Markov gyrators and a different three-dimensional rotational sensor problem, rather than independent fractional-Gaussian baths. Semantic mathematical-record queries reported no stronger same-object evaluated statement. A generic known Abelian result may yield the cusp after specialization, so external priority remains an open risk rather than a claim of absolute uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The practical interpretive problem is real: a vanishing mean signed area is commonly read as a lack of rotation, even though memory can retain an arrow of time. The theorem proves, for a natural two-bath family, precisely how that hidden lag signal survives, including a unique physical calibration and an exponent and coefficient determined by the rougher bath. It is a structural separation of an instantaneous statistic from stationary path reversibility, not a numerical parameter tweak or a textbook zero-temperature limit. It does not assert sampling precision, full entropy production or global lag-sign classification.
