# Review of the exact FPFM2 prime-two classification

## Correctness — PASS

The two-step memory equation forces opposite first coordinates. Addition of the two cosine-force equations gives \(|v|+|v+u|=2(1-1/\beta)\); for each positive half-amplitude \(u\), the identity \(|t-u/2|+|t+u/2|=2\max(|t|,u/2)\) yields precisely the positive-sign, negative-sign and crossing branches. The scalar cosine equations have one root at most on the asserted intervals by strict derivative signs, and their two endpoints coincide only at the enumerated nonsmooth collisions. This proves the all-plane exact orbit census including the singular forcing-independent case. Direct matrix multiplication gives the Jacobian polynomials and exact real Schur inequalities; they establish the sink threshold and expanding multiplier. Taylor expansion of \(\ell_\beta\) proves the common pole. The packaged checker replays 882 finite high-precision conditions, but the infinitary proof is analytic. No unstated boundedness, finite search completeness, or genericity hypotheses enter the theorem.

## Originality — PASS within the inspected evidence

The principal full primary paper (Almatroud–Pham, *Mathematics* 11 (2023), 1319, pp. 1–11) was read, including Equation (8) and Figure 7. It gives the exact FPFM2 model and numerical dynamics but not a classification of **all** prime-two orbits, the sharp all-forcing persistence transition, the locked orbit or the smooth sink criterion. Its FPFM1 model replaces the modulus by a square, so a period-two statement about FPFM1 cannot imply these modulus-geometry results. The Ramadoss et al. 2022 primary paper, pp. 1–4 and Table 1, was inspected and distinguishes a similar \(|y|-1\) NFP2 model with additive constant forcing rather than the present cosine forcing. Published searchable records for exact FPFM2 cycle classifications and several independent semantic research-index queries identified no same-object stronger theorem. Relevant exact two-cycle results for Duffing, Bogdanov and rational Rulkov maps have different equations and do not imply the result. The limits of this assessment are possible unindexed earlier derivations and incomplete scrutiny of every reference or unpublished calculation.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The short-period structure is a natural invariant for a fixed-point-free chaotic-map model. The theorem distinguishes a finite-forcing loss of **every** prime-two orbit from unconditional existence at higher memory gain, identifies an exceptional orbit whose coordinates ignore all forcing magnitudes, and describes a sharp asymptotic divergence at the transition. The independent sink criterion separates existence from attraction and identifies an exact interval of genuine locally attracting two-cycles. These are structural parameter-space results over the entire plane, not isolated numerical examples or a parameter substitution into a known classification.

## Scope and remaining risks

Only least-period-two orbits are classified. Results about chaotic attractors, practical device dynamics, and nonlinear stability at a nonsmooth collision are unproved. The related NFP2 paper is a different forcing mechanism, not evidence of a priority proof. External and unindexed literature may change the novelty assessment. The finite checker is not an infinite proof; independent audit is outstanding.
