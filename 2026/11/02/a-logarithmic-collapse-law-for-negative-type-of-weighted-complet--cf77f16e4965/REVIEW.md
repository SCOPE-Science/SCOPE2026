# Scientific review of a weighted corona negative-type classification

## Correctness
PASS. The zero-sum quadratic form has been expanded exactly into an \(m-1\)-fold two-dimensional fluctuation sector and a radial sector. Subadditivity of the power \(p/2\), for \(0<p<2\), proves the fluctuation determinant positive. The radial coefficient changes sign between \(p=1\) and \(p=2\); the strict-downward-propagation theorem excludes two nonstrict endpoints. The nullspace, minimal Euclidean dimensions, monotonicity and three asymptotic laws follow from explicit formulas. The accompanying standard-library checker reconstructs distances independently and passes exact rational and numerical corroborations. No assertion beyond the stated all-parameter analytic proof is based on numerical extrapolation.

## Originality
PASS on the inspected evidence, subject to residual publication-priority risk. Sánchez gives a universal finite-metric procedure and evaluates complete bipartite graph metrics; Li--Weston provides interval and strictness theorems. Ferdi--Baskoro--Santika evaluates corona quadratic embedding constants with the *first* distance power, whereas this result concerns a variable-power threshold and a continuously weighted family. An accessible analysis of complete-corona general-position polynomials studies a different combinatorial invariant. Related previous negative-type studies of two-pendant cliques, metric stars, and bridge-barbells have different distance matrices and do not imply this weighted private-leaf classification. No equal or stronger statement was established by the targeted source and research-record comparisons. The possibility of a differently indexed equivalent computation remains.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The family is a standard graph operation with a geometrically natural length parameter, rather than an arbitrary finite slice. The exact threshold and one-dimensional critical obstruction quantify how shortest-path metric embedding changes as pendant edges vary, and the logarithmic small-edge limit gives a nontrivial singular degeneration: the limiting quotient has infinite negative type while the family approaches exponent \(2\). The full conductance-independent sector calculation and sharp scaling regimes are reusable for other rooted graph products. No general theorem for arbitrary coronas is claimed.
