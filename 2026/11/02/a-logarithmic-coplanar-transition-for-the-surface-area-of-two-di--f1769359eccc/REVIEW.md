# Review of the two-disc surface-area transition

## Correctness

PASS. The centered-segment normalization reproduces each circular disk support function; the standard intrinsic-volume minor formula has the correct full-generator factors and is continuous under the stated limits. Integrating the mixed determinant yields the exact complete-elliptic formula, whose strict angular monotonicity gives both optimal configurations. The complementary-modulus expansion plus two exact weighted logarithm integrals gives the full sharp \(\pi RS\,\theta^2[\log(8/\theta)-1]\) correction; the remainder is uniformly integrable. Triple determinants and spherical support averaging independently provide volume and mean width. The numerical checker reproduces separate cross-product and convex-hull approximations; simulations are only corroboration. The degenerate planar set's actual ambient boundary measure is excluded from the area-continuation equality.

## Originality

PASS, with historical-priority limitations. The full available paper of Mathis and Meroni, *Fiber Convex Bodies*, arXiv:2105.12406, studies the same discotope faces and fiber geometry; Gesmundo and Meroni, *The Geometry of Discotopes*, arXiv:2111.01241, studies their algebraic boundary. Neither inspected text evaluates the area-versus-dihedral-angle law or its logarithmic endpoint. The general zonoid mixed-volume formula of Breiding, Bürgisser, Lerario and Mathis, arXiv:2109.14996, **does imply the unevaluated cross-product integral**, which is prior work and not claimed as original. The distinctive assessed result is the explicit logarithmic nonanalytic transition coupled with strict all-angle extrema. Indexed nearby claims concern algebraic singularities of the orthogonal two-disc sum or **convex hulls** rather than Minkowski sums. Differently named classical mixed-area evaluations and the not-full-text-inspected indexed singularity result are residual historical risks; a negative search is not proof of originality.

## Mathematical value

PASS. Two round disks in distinct planes are a natural minimal model of nonsmooth three-dimensional zonoid geometry. Their surface area has a sharp angular maximum and a non-\(C^2\) limiting law while their mean width remains unchanged and volume has a regular sine profile. The exact positive coefficient of \(\theta^2\log(1/\theta)\) identifies the order of degeneracy in a geometrically motivated family; neither a routine normalization nor finite parameter tabulation is the contribution.

## Limitations and validation

All analytical statements concern positive radii and \(0<\theta\le\pi/2\). The exactly coplanar set has a different ambient surface-area convention. The computational checks use finite resolution and do not prove infinite claims. External mathematical validation remains outstanding. Historical priority is provisional.
