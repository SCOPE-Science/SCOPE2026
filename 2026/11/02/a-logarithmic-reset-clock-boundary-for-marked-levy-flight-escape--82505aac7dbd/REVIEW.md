# Scientific review: logarithmic reset-clock boundary for marked stable escape

## Correctness — PASS

For a symmetric strictly stable Lévy flight, one-large-jump small-time first-exit/overshoot asymptotics yield separate right/left intensities \(k_\pm\). The exact geometric-cycle Laplace transform correctly retains the successful cycle's duration and exit mark. The finite-mean conclusion uses domination by \(CR\), without silently assuming a finite reset-time variance. The Lomax subcritical limit uses dominated convergence under the integrable kernel \(\min(su,1)u^{-1-\delta}\). The critical case correctly requires two logarithmic estimates, not an unjustified extension of the finite-mean dominated convergence argument. The exact renewal-reward mean formula proves the mean limits separately. A bounded interval has an exponential upper tail for an unreset stable first exit, since a jump of magnitude greater than twice the interval half-width necessarily exits; the Lévy measure gives such jumps positive finite intensity. No numerical sampling is offered as an infinite theorem proof.

## Originality — PASS, best-knowledge with explicit risk

Żbik–Palmowski (arXiv:2610.20410, Sections II–IV) treat Poisson-reset mean escape, and the finite-mean branch agrees with their prefactor for that special clock. The available main text does not give renewal-tail-exponent phase classification, directional marked transforms, or critical Lomax cancellation. Żbik–Dybiec (arXiv:2311.16014) treat finite-domain stable flights/walks under resetting with optimization emphasis, not the three-regime joint large-interval law. The general one-big-jump premise is due to established Lévy exit theory (Figueroa-López–Tankov 2014), and geometric restart is classical; neither premise is represented as new. Imkeller–Pavlyukevich (arXiv:math/0409246) prove exponential escape in a small-noise *potential-confining* model, not renewal Pareto resetting. Zweimüller's general rare-event Poisson theorem addresses ergodic invariant-map hitting-time statistics and does not state the infinitely long resetting-clock branch. The decisive extra comparison is that the subcritical Lomax branch retains the full unreset marked law and yields a genuinely different limiting mean in the centered Cauchy example, which a generic exponential approximation cannot capture.

Targeted semantic research-record searches and accessible earlier findings were checked for finite-mean restart universality, stable-flight exit directions, exact Pareto renewal transforms, and the critical tail-one boundary. No equivalent full theorem was located. This is **not** independent priority certification. A general, not fully inspected, rare-event or regenerative theorem could subsume the finite-mean exponential branch; related non-Poissonian stable-flight literature could also contain a reformulation of the subcritical transform. These are residual risks rather than evidence of coverage.

## Value — PASS

The motivating large-interval analysis identifies Poisson-rate invariance of the mean but leaves open whether the exit *distribution*, its boundary orientation, and the clock-tail dependence are invariant. The theorem supplies a complete marked weak-limit classification for any integrable reset clock and the natural Lomax family, including the critical **infinite-mean** clock. The logarithmic cancellation at exponent one is a physically meaningful boundary: infinite-mean reset intervals do not automatically destroy exponential exit kinetics, whereas lighter tail exponents retain the unreset excursion law. The explicit subcritical mean coefficient and the asymmetric side law can inform search and escape modeling with empirical heavy-tailed renewal waiting times. This is more than a parameter rename of the Poisson mean law.

## Limitations and priority comparison

Only symmetric strictly stable flights, proportional interior starting points, i.i.d. independent complete resets, and fixed clock laws are covered. The subcritical limiting law is specified by a Laplace transform involving the unreset exit law, not an elementary density. No uniformity in the stable index, starting-point boundary limit, or rescaling of the clock distribution is asserted. The result does not extend to Gaussian noise or to state-dependent resetting. The source paper's published Poisson mean asymptotic and established one-big-jump/renewal methods receive explicit priority.
