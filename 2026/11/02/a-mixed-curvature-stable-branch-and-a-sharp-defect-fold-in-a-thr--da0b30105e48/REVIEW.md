# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is formulated for all integer ring lengths at least three and all positive coupling strengths. The cyclic Laplacian has a one-dimensional constant-vector kernel; the nonzero spectrum is real and equivalent by congruence to the signed diagonal form restricted to mean-zero vectors. Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz proves the exact reciprocal-cosine threshold including the zero-cosine boundary. The explicit one-impurity branch is realized by solving a mean-zero discrete Poisson equation, not assumed from a scalar relation; strict concavity establishes the unique fold, stability orientation, and two-root interval. Taylor expansion yields the leading width. Independent numerical finite tests cover the local eigenvalues, force-balance equations, and zero eigenvalues at the fold. These tests are not represented as proofs of all dimensions.

## Originality — PASS, subject to stated residual priority risk

Liang et al. (arXiv:2512.18983v1) already introduce the exact governing model, both inverse-sine formal branches, and the principal-branch locking equation; these are prior work. Their accessible full main text does not establish the mixed-negative-cosine exponential-stability criterion or a sharp one-impurity bifurcation window. Song–Hill–Liu's general signed graph definiteness literature covers some underlying algebraic machinery, so the diagonal quadratic-form lemma alone is not claimed as novel. Bick–Böhle–Kuehn study a different nonlocal and infinite-particle higher-order oscillator setting. Searches of indexed mathematical findings for the actual one-impurity branch, mixed cosine sign, and fold did not return an equivalent result. The motivating source explicitly mentions supplementary material that could not be fully inspected; it remains a residual priority risk rather than an asserted noncoverage.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The original paper's natural principal-sine treatment leaves the stability of mixed sine-inverse branches unresolved in accessible text. The theorem shows that a local loss of cosine positivity does not necessarily destroy collective locking; it gives the exact permitted size of a single negative curvature, proves an explicit stable mixed branch, and locates its sharp saddle-node. The window width \(K/[2(N-1)^2]\) quantifies a finite-size effect that would be missed by simply declaring all negative-cosine branches unstable. The results concern a natural frequency-impurity configuration, not an arbitrary numerical slice. Global basins, nonlinear dynamics at the fold, and completeness across all winding branches are not claimed.
