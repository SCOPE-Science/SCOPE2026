# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is reduced to an explicit compact-perturbation model with two mutually orthogonal input residue classes, or a common residue class with two distinct output shifts. The weight limits are calculated from the gamma-moment asymptotics independently for each slant normalization. In the nonresonant case the positive product is a scalar modulo compacts; in the resonant case its Calkin symbol is the nonconstant trigonometric polynomial \(c_\nu^2(2-2\cos(d\theta))\). The spectral ranges and exact essential norms follow. The kernel and boundary cases \(m=n\), \(m=0\), \(n=0\), \(s=2\), and every \(\alpha>-1\) are covered. The 184,712 finite checks in `check_slant.py` support coefficient algebra only, not the infinite assertion.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, not publication-priority certification)

The closest paper is Cui–Li–Lu, arXiv:2610.09728v1, which proves commuting and finite-rank equivalences for the unweighted second-order slant and explicitly uses congruence classes in coefficient proofs. Those statements supply qualitative noncommutativity but no exact Calkin norm or positive-product essential spectrum. Lu–Liu–Yang (2010) already establish qualitative equivalence of commutativity and essential commutativity, so that qualitative consequence is prior art. Liu–Lu (2013) concerns product and commutativity criteria. Singh–Singh–Singh, arXiv:2507.06546v1, treats weighted Bergman slants with a distinct orthonormal normalization and includes finite monomial commutator simulations but no sharp spectral transition; Zhang, arXiv:2504.01042v1, studies commutativity. Searches of published mathematical records with the aliases Calkin spectrum, essential norm, monomial slant, congruence modulo square of slant order and weighted Bergman found no equivalent quantified statement. The 2010 primary full text was unavailable; this is a specifically recorded residual risk, not a novelty proof by failed search.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Monomials are canonical symbols and span the analytic polynomial symbols whose commutators motivate the recent research. The theorem classifies the full essential singular geometry for all degrees, all integer slant orders and all power weights, and it reconciles two genuinely different standard normalization conventions. The abrupt change from a scalar positive Calkin element to one with interval spectrum at divisibility by \(s^2\) is a structural invariant not implied by knowing merely that commutation fails or that finite rank is impossible. The exact essential norm is the optimal compact-perturbation bound in each arithmetic regime.

## Limits and closest sources

The theorem does not resolve general analytic symbols, arbitrary smooth weights, or ordinary commutator spectra. Line-by-line verification against the inaccessible 2010 full text and independent publication-priority review remain outstanding.
