# Scientific review: a modulo-four cycle law for minimum Walsh projection constants

## Correctness — PASS
The proof identifies the norm-minimal Fourier projection by translation averaging; the equality between relative and absolute constants is also supplied by the explicit general theorem of Defant et al. The edge-sign map of a unicyclic graph is two-to-one onto the single parity constraint. Conditional Fourier expansion reduces the constant to one Walsh coefficient of the absolute Rademacher sum. Direct flip-generator identities and elementary binomial generating functions evaluate that coefficient exactly, including both parity cases and all endpoints. A standalone exact-rational checker confirms 45 finite pairs; the proof does not rely on this enumeration. Risk: the proof uses the published absolute projection-constant theorem, whose standard assumptions match the finite real or complex sign cube.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Theorem 3.1 of Defant et al. covers the *general kernel-expectation reduction*, so that is explicitly excluded from originality. The new statement evaluates a natural sparsely supported family indexed by every unicyclic graph, shows a strict cycle-length modulo-four trichotomy, and obtains the exact attachment-size decay. The closest graph-supported quadratic-chaos record optimizes fourth moments rather than norm-minimal projections; the closest degree-two projection result treats complete Walsh layers instead of graph edges. Full texts of those closely related records and the primary Defant article were examined. Searches did not surface the complete unicyclic formula, but bibliographic incompleteness remains a real risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a natural connected-graph family of degree-two Walsh Banach spaces where adding exactly one cycle imposes a global edge-sign relation. The closed formula measures the exact minimal complementability effect, not merely the behavior of a convenient projection. Its sign reverses with cycle length modulo four, and the sharp decay quantifies how the topological obstruction weakens when further tree edges are attached. Contractive four-cycle and expanding six-cycle examples show genuine nonuniform structural behavior.

## Scope and limitations
Only simple unicyclic graphs and unit edge characters are covered; no multicycle enumeration or arbitrary weighted-coefficient analogue is claimed. General Walsh projection formulas and the independent-sign first moment are established background. The published-source comparison is not a proof of absolute novelty.
