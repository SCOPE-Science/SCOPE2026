# A noncyclic base-13 fixed-divisor spectrum for Delannoy polynomials

## Finding

Define the Delannoy polynomial, for each integer \(n\ge 0\), by
\[
D_n(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom nj\binom{n+j}jX^j.
\]
For any odd prime \(p\), let \(\mathcal E_p\) be the set of nonnegative indices \(n\) for which \(D_n(z)\equiv1\pmod p\) for **every** integer \(z\). For \(n\ge1\), equivalently \(p\) divides the integer fixed divisor \(g_n=\gcd_{z\in\mathbb Z}(D_n(z)-1)\). The zero polynomial at \(n=0\) is included by the universal-congruence definition, without defining a positive fixed divisor there.

Write the base-\(p\) digits of \(n\) as \(n=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}d_i p^i\), allowing leading zero digits. Define the finite-field value vector \(v_{p,d}=(D_d(a)\bmod p)_{a\in\mathbb F_p}\), and let \(U_p\subset\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\) consist of the digits whose vectors have no zero coordinates. Let \(H_p\) be the subgroup of \((\mathbb F_p^\times)^{\mathbb F_p}\) generated by \(v_{p,d}\) for \(d\in U_p\). Then the exact general criterion is
\[
n\in\mathcal E_p\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\bigl(d_i\in U_p\text{ for all }i\bigr)
\ \text{and}\ \prod_i v_{p,d_i}=\mathbf1.
\]
In particular \(\mathcal E_p\) has a finite-state base-\(p\) recognition procedure. If \(C_p(k)=|\mathcal E_p\cap[0,p^k)|\), then
\[
C_p(k)=\frac{1}{|H_p|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat H_p}
\left(\sum_{d\in U_p}\chi(v_{p,d})\right)^k
=\frac{|U_p|^k}{|H_p|}+O_p(\rho_p^k)
\]
for some \(\rho_p<|U_p|\). Here \(2\le|U_p|\le p-1\); consequently the universal-congruence indices have natural density zero. Their closure in \(\mathbb Z_p\) is exactly the digit-restricted set with every digit in \(U_p\), of Hausdorff dimension \(\log_p|U_p|\) for the standard \(p\)-adic metric.

The first noncyclic local group occurs at \(p=13\): among the odd primes through \(13\), the groups \(H_3,H_5,H_7,H_{11}\) are cyclic of orders \(1,4,6,10\), whereas
\[
U_{13}=\{0,4,6,8,12\},\qquad H_{13}\simeq(\mathbb Z/12\mathbb Z)^2.
\]
The vectors of digits \(0\) and \(12\) are \(\mathbf1\); digits \(4\) and \(8\) have the same vector, and that vector is independent of the vector of digit \(6\), each having order \(12\). Thus the **complete base-13 criterion** is: every digit belongs to \(\{0,4,6,8,12\}\), the total count of digits \(4\) or \(8\) is divisible by \(12\), and the count of digits \(6\) is divisible by \(12\).

Precisely, for each \(k\ge0\),
\[
C_{13}(k)=\!\!\sum_{\substack{a,b\ge0,\ a+b\le k\\12\mid a,\ 12\mid b}}
\binom{k}{a,b,k-a-b}2^{k-b}
=\frac{5^k}{144}+O(\rho^{k})\quad(\rho<5).
\]
For example \(C_{13}(12)=8193\), and the \(13\)-adic closure has Hausdorff dimension \(\log_{13}5\).

## Assumptions and scope

All congruences are modulo rational primes and apply to **all** integer arguments \(z\), not just \(z=1\) or a single residue. Prime-specific group vectors are pointwise functions on the entire finite field. The count \(C_p(k)\) includes \(n=0\); for every positive \(n\), membership is exactly divisibility of the fixed divisor \(g_n\) by \(p\). The result makes no claim about higher \(p\)-adic valuations of \(g_n\).

## Proof

**1. A polynomial Lucas congruence.** Vandermonde's identity yields the integral polynomial identity
\[
D_n(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom nj^{\!2}X^j(1+X)^{n-j}.
\]
Indeed, the coefficient of \(X^k\) on the right is
\[
\sum_{j=0}^k\binom nj^2\binom{n-j}{k-j}
=\binom nk\sum_{j=0}^k\binom nj\binom kj
=\binom nk\binom{n+k}k.
\]
For \(n=ap+b\), with \(0\le b<p\), apply the ordinary binomial Lucas congruence to the two factors \(\binom nj\). In characteristic \(p\), \((1+X)^p=1+X^p\); separating the digits of \(j\) gives
\[
D_{ap+b}(X)\equiv D_a(X^p)D_b(X)\pmod p.
\]
Iterating, and then evaluating on \(\mathbb F_p\), where \(a^{p^i}=a\), proves \(D_n(a)\equiv\prod_i D_{d_i}(a)\pmod p\) simultaneously for all \(a\in\mathbb F_p\).

**2. The finite group test.** If one digit vector has a zero coordinate, its pointwise product cannot equal \(\mathbf1\). Otherwise all digit vectors are units and their product equals \(\mathbf1\) exactly when the original polynomial evaluates to \(1\) at all field elements. This proves the criterion. The digit \(0\) gives \(v_{p,0}=\mathbf1\), and the digit \(p-1\) gives \(v_{p,p-1}=\mathbf1\), since each nonconstant coefficient \(\binom{p-1}j\binom{p-1+j}j\) is divisible by \(p\) for \(1\le j<p\). Conversely \(v_{p,1}(a)=1+2a\) has a zero over every odd prime field. Hence \(2\le|U_p|\le p-1\).

**3. Exact counts and topology.** The group-character orthogonality identity \(|H_p|^{-1}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat H_p}\chi(g)=1\) for \(g=\mathbf1\), and \(0\) otherwise, gives the formula for \(C_p(k)\). For a nontrivial character, \(\left|\sum_{d\in U_p}\chi(v_{p,d})\right|<|U_p|\): equality in the triangle inequality would force all generator character values to equal \(1\), because \(v_{p,0}=\mathbf1\), contradicting nontriviality. This gives the strict spectral gap and asymptotic. Since \(|U_p|<p\), the upper densities at all \(p\)-power cutoffs vanish; the same growth bound controls arbitrary cutoffs, so natural density is zero. Every admissible finite digit prefix can be extended to a word with group product \(\mathbf1\): the finite semigroup generated by the unit vectors is their group, hence contains the inverse of the prefix product. Therefore the \(p\)-adic closure consists of all infinite words over \(U_p\). Its Hausdorff dimension is the standard equal-ratio digit-set dimension \(\log_p|U_p|\).

**4. The first noncyclic example.** Exhaustive arithmetic of the \(p\) one-digit polynomials on \(\mathbb F_p\), for the four primes less than \(13\), gives the following respective pairs \((U_p,|H_p|)\):
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
p&U_p&|H_p|\\\hline
3&\{0,2\}&1\\
5&\{0,2,4\}&4\\
7&\{0,2,4,6\}&6\\
11&\{0,4,6,10\}&10
\end{array}
\]
Each of these groups is cyclic: its one nonidentity digit-vector type generates the entire group (with identical vectors for the paired active digits at \(7\) and \(11\)). At \(p=13\), the six odd digits have zero at argument \(6\); the even digits \(2,10\) have zero at argument \(1\). The remaining nontrivial digit vectors, in argument order \(0,1,\dots,12\), are
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_{13,4}=v_{13,8}&=(1,9,2,12,9,5,2,5,9,12,2,9,1),\\
v_{13,6}&=(1,6,11,11,11,2,7,2,11,11,11,6,1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Every coordinate is nonzero, and digits \(0,12\) both give \(\mathbf1\). The residue \(2\) is primitive modulo \(13\). Evaluation at \(1\) and \(5\) sends the powers of these vectors to the exponent matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}8&5\\9&1\end{pmatrix}\pmod{12};
\qquad\det=-37\equiv11\pmod{12}.
\]
This determinant is a unit modulo \(12\). Thus the map from the two independent exponent coordinates is injective. Each vector has exponent dividing \(12\) by Fermat's theorem, so these generators give precisely \((\mathbb Z/12\mathbb Z)^2\). The digit-count congruences and the multinomial counting formula follow. The leading term \(5^k/144\) follows from the general character asymptotic.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` uses only Python's standard library. Its successful replay checked \(373\) one-digit finite-field cells, \(4095\) direct coefficient-sum versus digit-product evaluations, \(33\) independent exact counting instances through length \(32\), and \(600\) individual base-13 universal-congruence decisions by testing all thirteen arguments. It separately enumerated the finite digit-vector groups of orders \(1,4,6,10,144\) and checked the determinant certificate \(-37\). These computations verify the finite local tables and exemplify the theorem; the proof of the general statements is symbolic and does not depend on testing finitely many indices.

Run the standalone verifier by executing `python3 verify.py` in the directory containing this document and the code file. No third-party packages or external data are required.

## Relationship to prior work

The underlying binomial Lucas congruence and the classical Delannoy-polynomial identities are prior results, not claimed as new. Guo's 2014 paper examines weighted congruence sums of Delannoy polynomials, while Pan's 2015 paper establishes Lucas-type congruences for (\(q\)-)Delannoy numbers. The modulo-three Cantor-set description of central Delannoy numbers is explicitly already recorded as OEIS A088917; its occurrence here is a baseline check, not a novelty claim. A 2025 paper by Alekseyev, Amdeberhan, Shallit, and Vukusic gives valuations of Legendre polynomials at prime arguments, a different question. The claimed new content is the universal-in-argument fixed-divisor digit-spectrum criterion and counting theory, with the exact first noncyclic base-13 classification and its two independent digit-count obstructions. Searches described in `AUDIT.json` did not locate an equivalent theorem, but this does not establish an exhaustive literature absence.

## Limitations

The statement concerns only divisibility by the first power of an odd prime, not the exact \(p\)-adic valuation of every fixed divisor or the full fixed divisor itself. The finite-field subgroup \(H_p\) is defined algorithmically for general \(p\); a uniform closed-form structure for all primes is not asserted. The \(p=13\) local table is exhaustively verified, whereas the general asymptotic is proved mathematically. Independent external validation and exhaustive coverage of all specialist literature have not occurred.
