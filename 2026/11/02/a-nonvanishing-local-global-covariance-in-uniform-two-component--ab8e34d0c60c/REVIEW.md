# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The conditional joint distribution follows from independent Cayley-tree counts and the Prüfer degree law. An explicit adjacent-binomial likelihood-ratio identity establishes strict ordering. Edge deletion and vertex exchangeability give the covariance, and the published two-component two-point inclusion formula supplies the component variance for the limiting correlation. The standalone exact enumerator passes 240 assertions for forest orders two through eight.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The central joint degree–component law and its strict likelihood-ratio consequence are not contained explicitly in the inspected general two-component forest paper of Kassel–Kenyon–Wu. Existing rooted/massive forest distributions apply to a different product-size-weighted measure, and prior forest counting alone does not provide the covariance. A historical equivalence obtainable by nontrivial extraction from broader shape enumerations remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** This is a natural local-versus-global dependence problem in the well-studied unrooted two-component forest model. An exact global covariance stays of order one while the correlation decays at the sharp square-root rate; the result also orders the complete conditional degree family, not just its mean.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Kassel–Kenyon–Wu (2012) is the closest direct model, supplying size marginals and two-vertex inclusion; Myrvold (1992) supplies forest enumeration background; D'Achille–Enriquez–Melotti (2026) addresses size-weighted rooted massive forests. Complete source comparison is not a claim of exhaustive historical priority. The theorem is specific to unrooted two-component forests of complete graphs.
