# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The cycle gradient subspace is precisely the intersection of the four-dimensional cube with the zero-sum hyperplane. Its six norming vertices are explicitly enumerated. The gradient generator and its two-dimensional hyperbolic exponential are derived, yielding exact row-norm formulas for all four edges. A coefficientwise exponential comparison proves the strict edge selection; the maximizing faces then give *all* extremizers. A two-exponential derivative argument proves the phase threshold, and a controlled singular limit proves the sharp envelope. Exact rational intertwining checks and independent full-state uniformization tests corroborate the formulas, but finite checks are not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The closest general sources are Ollivier's transport-curvature construction (arXiv:math/0701886), Chafaï–Joulin's birth-death intertwinings (arXiv:1011.2331), and Münch–Wojciechowski's curvature/heat-gradient equivalence (arXiv:1712.00875). Their inspected definitions, semigroup theorems and examples do not give the exact one-defect four-cycle profile. The homogeneous four-cycle case is already covered by the complete-bipartite heat problem and is not individually claimed as new. Accessible previous treatments of longer unweighted cycles and a weighted path have different conductance matrices and do not imply this one-defect cyclic calculation. Residual risk: an unindexed equivalent weighted-square computation could exist.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The four-cycle is the smallest simple cycle on which symmetric heat can temporarily enlarge the graph-Lipschitz norm when its **metric remains fixed**. The exact conductance threshold, change from a continuum of norming gradients to two discrete ramps, and unattained sharp \(3/2\) limit give qualitative boundary information, not merely a single numerical evaluation. The phenomenon separates heat conductance from metric length and distinguishes cyclic from complete-graph oscillation contraction.

## Scope and status
Only one exceptional heat conductance on a four-cycle with unit metric edges and counting measure is treated. Larger graphs, different metric edge lengths and arbitrary conductance profiles are not asserted. Priority remains qualified by the stated residual risk.
