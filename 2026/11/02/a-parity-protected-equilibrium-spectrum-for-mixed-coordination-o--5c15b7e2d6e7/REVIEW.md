# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claimed count follows from four mutually exclusive cases for the two part magnetizations. In a strict opposite-part majority, each local action is uniquely forced by its role; in a tie, all players on the other side are indifferent. Counting the both-strict, one-tied (two possibilities), and both-tied cases proves the formula for every positive part size, including the singleton boundary. The parity, sharp extrema, and random-role probability are logical consequences with explicit extremizers and a signed-symmetry calculation. The independent payoff-based enumerator passed 400 role-composition comparisons and checked 102400 action profiles, as well as 25 extrema/existence cases and 25 probability cases. No finite experiment is substituted for the all-size proof.

## Originality — PASS (priority remains provisional)
The 2021 full-text source defines this mixed binary network game and offers cohesive sufficient existence and convergence results, not an exact all-role count on unit-weight complete bipartite networks. The 2019/2020 all-to-all analysis does not specialize to bipartite neighborhoods. The accessible 80-page 2019 thesis was inspected in the relevant introductory, mixed-game, noncohesive examples, and concluding sections: the examples and general sufficient conditions do not imply the complete-bipartite four-stratum formula. The later signed-edge generalization, arXiv:2505.09799, was checked at its Theorems 1 and 2 and supplies sufficient cohesive-core conditions and stability rather than a bipartite role-by-role count. Published results on purely conforming/nonconforming populations and unrelated balanced multipartite colorings cover only different games or the already-trivial balanced profile contribution. Semantic-index comparisons found no stronger source with this same fixed graph, type pattern, and full parameter-count coverage. This does not certify universal publication priority; specialist sources may remain unidentified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Arbitrary mixtures of complementary and substitute strategic responses can lose the existence guaranteed in the homogeneous games. Complete bipartite networks capture two fully cross-interacting communities and permit a sharp answer for **every** role assignment. The theorem identifies a topology-size parity protection mechanism independent of the number or placement of antagonists, gives exact labeled multiplicities (including tie-induced exponential families), and quantifies how likely equilibrium existence is under random role heterogeneity. These are structural game-theoretic questions rather than arbitrary numerical slices or textbook identities.
