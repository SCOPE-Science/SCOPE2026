For a finite Gaussian Markov tree, the covariance of an edge sign-crossing indicator with any squared vertex value is exactly \(-r_e\sqrt{1-r_e^2}\eta^2/\pi\), where \(\eta\) is the correlation along the remaining path. Hence sign-crossing counts and squared amplitude have strictly opposite covariance sign to a common nonzero edge-correlation sign, with an explicit finite-sample formula for stationary Gaussian autoregressive chains. The uniform sign conclusion can fail for a general positively correlated Gaussian triple, demonstrating the role of the tree assumption.
