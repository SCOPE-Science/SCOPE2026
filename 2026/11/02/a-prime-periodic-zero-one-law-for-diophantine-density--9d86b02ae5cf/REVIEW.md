# Review of A prime-periodic zero--one law for Diophantine density

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof explicitly handles the empty set and the prime-two boundary, reconstructs reduced approximants with \(\ell p-aq=1\), and checks the interval for every admissible \(v\). The two full-density cases follow from restricted Dirichlet and modular inverses. The finite verifier passed 99,798 full-density pairs and 2,946 obstruction pairs. These checks corroborate but do not replace the general argument.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Sanford's full-text [1] and [2] introduce the density, full-density multiples and zero-density translated examples, but not the simultaneous prime-periodic classification. An earlier single-progression result is stronger for each separate class and gives a non-subadditivity example, but its claims do not imply arbitrary unions. The missing-residue obstruction is the new structural step. General literature could contain an implicit elementary equivalent, so independent priority review remains warranted.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The theorem computes the invariant for every periodic residue union modulo any prime. Its exact threshold and count of full-density classes explain the discontinuity between ordinary and Diophantine density, yielding ordinary-density \((\ell-2)/\ell\) examples with Diophantine density zero even as the former tends to one.

**Scientific limitations.** Only full residue unions at prime periods are covered; no classification is claimed for composite periods or arbitrary subsets of positive integers. A finite verifier is not an infinite proof, and no expert or independent audit has been conducted.

**Closest literature.** [1] C. Sanford, arXiv:2606.02620v1; [2] C. Sanford, arXiv:2610.27497v1; plus earlier single-progression and broad covering-set results compared for mathematical implication.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
