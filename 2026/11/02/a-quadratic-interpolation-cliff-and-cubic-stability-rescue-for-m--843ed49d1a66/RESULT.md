# A quadratic interpolation cliff and cubic stability rescue for mutually unbiased bases

## Finding
Let \(d\ge3\) be an integer, and let \(\mathcal B_1,\dots,\mathcal B_m\) be \(m\) pairwise mutually unbiased orthonormal bases of \(\mathbb C^d\), where \(1\le m\le d+1\). Regard their \(N=md\) unit vectors as distinct points \(X\) of complex projective space \(\mathbb {CP}^{d-1}\). Let \(E_k\) be evaluation on \(X\) of the complex vector space \(\mathcal H_k\) of bihomogeneous harmonic polynomials of bidegree \((k,k)\), restricted to the unit sphere and regarded as phase-invariant projective functions. Then

\[
\operatorname{rank}E_0=1,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}E_1=m(d-1),\qquad
\operatorname{rank}E_2=md-\mathbf1_{\{m=d+1\}},\qquad
\operatorname{rank}E_3=md.
\]

Consequently the **first individual projective-harmonic degree that interpolates arbitrary data** on these points is exactly \(2\) when \(m\le d\), but exactly \(3\) when \(m=d+1\). This holds for every such system of bases, not only for a particular prime-power construction; existence is asserted only for the systems actually available. Prime-power dimensions supply infinite families of complete examples.

Use the normalized degree-\(k\) reproducing-kernel Gram matrix \(G_k\), whose diagonal entries are one. For \(k=2\), its eigenvalues, with their multiplicities, are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{eigenvalue}&\text{multiplicity}\\\hline
\displaystyle\frac{(d-2)(d+1)}{d(d-1)}&m(d-1)\\[5pt]
\displaystyle\frac{(d+1)(d+2)}{d^2}&m-1\\[5pt]
\displaystyle\frac{(d+2)(d+1-m)}{d^2}&1.
\end{array}
\]

The middle line has multiplicity zero when \(m=1\). In particular, deleting precisely one basis from a complete collection yields \(m=d\) and a positive-definite quadratic Gram matrix with **exact spectral condition number**

\[
\boxed{\kappa_2(G_2)=d+1.}
\]

Adding the last basis produces an exactly one-dimensional quadratic sampling deficiency, generated by the all-ones sample vector. Cubic sampling restores full interpolation. When \(m=d+1\), the cubic Gram eigenvalues are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{eigenvalue}&\text{multiplicity}\\\hline
\displaystyle W_d=\frac{(d+2)(d^2-2d+3)}{d(d-1)(d+1)}&d^2-1\\[6pt]
\displaystyle B_d=\frac{(d-2)^2(d+2)(d+3)}{d^3(d+1)}&d\\[6pt]
\displaystyle C_d=\frac{(d-2)(d+3)(d+4)}{d^2(d+1)}&1.
\end{array}
\]

Their sharp condition-number law is

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_2(G_3)=
\begin{cases}
9/2,&d=3,\\
\displaystyle\frac{d(d+4)}{(d-2)(d+2)},&d\ge4.
\end{cases}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\sup_{d\ge3}\kappa_2(G_3)=9/2,
}
\]

with the global supremum attained only at \(d=3\). The comparison is striking: the quadratic condition number of the one-basis-deleted configuration grows linearly with \(d\), while the completed configuration has uniformly conditioned cubic interpolation.

## Assumptions and scope
A pair of orthonormal bases is *mutually unbiased* when the squared absolute inner product between vectors from different bases equals \(1/d\). Within any basis, distinct vectors have squared inner product zero. No hypothesis about phases, finite fields, or transitivity is required. The \(md\) associated projective points are distinct.

A bihomogeneous polynomial \(p(z,\bar z)\) is in \(\mathcal H_k\) if its degree in \(z\) and degree in \(\bar z\) are both \(k\) and \(\sum_{j=1}^d\partial_{z_j}\partial_{\bar z_j}p=0\). Its restriction to \(\|z\|=1\) is invariant under multiplication of \(z\) by a unit complex phase. Evaluation rank is over \(\mathbb C\). The normalized Gram matrix has one row and one column for each projective point and is not divided by \(N\); its spectral condition number is the ratio of the largest to the smallest positive eigenvalue, applicable when the matrix is invertible. Scaling the Gram uniformly would leave its condition number unchanged.

The theorem assumes \(d\ge3\). For \(d=2\), the quadratic within-basis eigenvalue vanishes and the asserted quadratic/cubic full-rank dichotomy does **not** hold. We do not claim that any maximal collection exists in a specified non-prime-power dimension. We classify degrees \(0,1,2,3\) and their displayed spectra only; no claim is made about exact-degree sampling at \(k\ge4\).

## Proof
The standard complex-projective addition theorem identifies the normalized reproducing kernel on \(\mathcal H_k\) as

\[
R_{d,k}(t)={}_2F_1(-k,k+d-1;d-1;1-t)
=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^j\binom{k}{j}
\frac{(k+d-1)_j}{(d-1)_j}(1-t)^j,
\qquad t=|\langle x,y\rangle|^2.
\]

Here \((a)_j\) denotes the rising factorial, \(R_{d,k}(1)=1\), and the unnormalized reproducing kernel differs from \(R_{d,k}\) only by a positive constant. Thus \(G_k=(R_{d,k}(|\langle x_i,x_j\rangle|^2))_{i,j}\) is positive semidefinite and has rank equal to \(\operatorname{rank} E_k\), by Gram factorization of the evaluation functionals.

Put \(u_k=R_{d,k}(0)\) and \(v_k=R_{d,k}(1/d)\). Group points by their basis, and denote by \(J_r\) the \(r\)-by-\(r\) all-ones matrix. The exact three-distance structure gives

\[
G_k=(1-u_k)I_{md}+(u_k-v_k)(I_m\otimes J_d)+v_kJ_{md}.
\]

Decompose \(\mathbb C^{md}\) orthogonally into the within-basis zero-sum subspace \(W\) of dimension \(m(d-1)\), the vectors constant on each basis but with zero total sum, \(B\), of dimension \(m-1\), and the global constant line \(C\). Direct application of the displayed matrix yields

\[
\lambda_W=1-u_k,\qquad
\lambda_B=1+(d-1)u_k-dv_k,\qquad
\lambda_C=1+(d-1)u_k+d(m-1)v_k.
\]

At degree zero, \(u_0=v_0=1\), so the Gram is \(J_{md}\) and has rank one. At degree one, \(u_1=-1/(d-1)\) and \(v_1=0\). Consequently \(\lambda_W=d/(d-1)>0\) and \(\lambda_B=\lambda_C=0\), establishing the asserted first-degree rank.

At degree two, direct evaluation of the terminating hypergeometric sum gives

\[
u_2=\frac{2}{d(d-1)},\qquad
v_2=-\frac{d+2}{d^3}.
\]

Substitution produces the quadratic table in the Finding. For \(d\ge3\), the within-basis and basis-contrast eigenvalues are strictly positive. The constant eigenvalue \((d+2)(d+1-m)/d^2\) is positive for \(m\le d\) and zero exactly for \(m=d+1\). This proves the quadratic rank formula and identifies its complete-collection annihilating sample vector.

At degree three, the exact zonal values are

\[
u_3=-\frac6{d(d-1)(d+1)},\qquad
v_3=\frac{4(d-2)(d+3)}{d^4(d+1)}.
\]

The three eigenvalues, for arbitrary \(1\le m\le d+1\), therefore factor as

\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_W&=\frac{(d+2)(d^2-2d+3)}{d(d-1)(d+1)},\\
\lambda_B&=\frac{(d-2)^2(d+2)(d+3)}{d^3(d+1)},\\
\lambda_C&=\frac{(d-2)(d+3)((d-2)(d+2)+4m)}{d^3(d+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Every numerator and denominator is strictly positive for the claimed integer range. Thus \(G_3\) is positive definite and the cubic evaluation is onto \(\mathbb C^{md}\). Combined with the degree-zero, -one and -two ranks, this establishes both sharp first-surjective degrees.

For the quadratic condition number when \(m=d\), the eigenvalues obey \(\lambda_C<\lambda_W<\lambda_B\). Indeed,

\[
\lambda_B-\lambda_W=\frac{(d+1)(3d-2)}{d^2(d-1)}>0,
\quad
\lambda_W-\lambda_C=\frac{d^3-2d^2-3d+2}{d^2(d-1)}>0;
\]

for the second inequality, its numerator equals \(2\) at \(d=3\) and is strictly increasing for real \(d\ge3\). Their ratio is \(\lambda_B/\lambda_C=d+1\).

For completed cubic sampling \(m=d+1\), substitution yields \(W_d,B_d,C_d\) as displayed. Direct subtraction gives

\[
W_d-B_d=\frac{2(d+2)(5d^2-10d+6)}{d^3(d-1)(d+1)}>0,
\quad
C_d-B_d=\frac{4(d-2)(d+3)}{d^3}>0.
\]

The sign of \(C_d-W_d\) is the sign of \(2d^3-3d^2-14d+12\), which is negative at \(d=3\), positive at \(d=4\), and strictly increasing thereafter. Hence \(B_d\) is always the least eigenvalue, and the greatest is \(W_3\) at \(d=3\) but \(C_d\) at \(d\ge4\). The ratios are exactly \(9/2\) and \(d(d+4)/((d-2)(d+2))\), respectively. The latter function is strictly decreasing on \([4,\infty)\) and begins at \(8/3<9/2\), proving the global sharp bound.

## Relationship to prior work
Klappenecker and Rötteler (2005) establish the complex-projective two-design property of complete mutually unbiased bases. Roy and Scott (2007) develop weighted complex-projective designs built from bases and their information-theoretic properties. The earlier Jacobi/zonal harmonic addition theory supplies the normalized kernel used here. These results provide the starting objects and tools. The theorem here instead quantifies **exact-degree projective harmonic interpolation**, the sharp change upon including the final unbiased basis, and the opposite dimension dependences of quadratic and cubic spectral conditioning. The two-design identity itself explains why the complete quadratic Gram has a constant null vector, but neither that fact nor the general reproducing-kernel identity alone yields the cubic positivity and two sharp conditioning formulas without the displayed analysis.

## Limitations
The result is conditional on the existence of the indicated mutually unbiased bases; it does not resolve their maximal existence problem in non-prime-power dimensions. It does not assert any rank or stability result for harmonic degrees beyond three, nor does it address numerical perturbations of the bases or noisy measurements. The combinatorial three-level calculation is elementary once the correct Jacobi normalization is chosen. Equivalent spectral calculations might occur in older association-scheme, projective cubature, or quantum-tomographic literature; no exhaustive historical uniqueness claim is made. No independent mathematical validation is claimed.
