# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The four-state load current equals the time derivative of the empty-state population exactly. All finite-detuning load transitions are positive, so the unique grand-canonical equilibrium and its negative net-work limit follow from detailed balance. The Hamiltonian's low-state endpoint weights are each asymptotic to \(\Omega^2/(2\Delta^2)\), and the high-state contact approaches unity. These facts make the fast \(0\leftrightarrow d\) two-state block and its slow leakage mathematically explicit. The projected finite-generator semigroup gives the stated exponential profile uniformly on positive compact scaled-time intervals; strict monotonicity of that profile yields the unique limiting zero. A standalone fresh run gave 265 successful matrix-level checks, including detailed balance, exact exponentiation and root convergence.

## Originality — PASS (qualified best-knowledge)

Singh, Sánchez and Jordan, arXiv:2610.33408v1, already give the load Lindbladian and positive work over their chosen finite endpoint; both are explicitly prior. The standard grand-canonical stationary law is likewise prior. The assessed difference is the exact noncommutation of the infinite-time and infinite-detuning outputs, the quadratic slow-load crossover, and the first explicit coefficient of the accumulated-work zero. Targeted claim searches and comparison with accessible prior scientific results found no statement that dominates the complete conjunction. Residual risk: another analysis may regard the crossover as an implicit consequence of the published star generator; related quantum-battery sources available only at abstract level cannot be ruled out at full-text depth.

## Value — PASS

The result supplies a quantitative maximum-connectivity window for a physically motivated work-extraction protocol: a load can yield positive output on the fast scale yet, after enough time, consume more net charge than it received. The sharp \(\Delta^2\) scale links dot detuning and weak couplings to the onset of loss; the coefficient depends on initial charged-state occupation and the load bias. The boundary is useful even though finite reservoirs or neglected charge channels can change laboratory timescales.

## Scope and limitations

This is a theorem for the secular four-state weak-coupling population generator with a single thermal load. It does not assert finite-detuning global uniqueness of the zero, absence of charge-sector leakage, or independence of mathematical and publication-priority review. The available exact calculation supports the stated large-detuning asymptotic rather than a protocol guarantee at every physical parameter.
