A symmetric run-and-tumble particle launched far outside a harmonic trap's stationary support has a centered first-entry time converging to the negative logarithm of a \(\operatorname{Beta}(q/\mu,q/\mu)\) random variable divided by \(\mu\). For every dimensionless launch distance \(C\ge1\), the complete first-entry law is stochastically sandwiched between this log-beta limit and its translate by \(\mu^{-1}\log(1+C^{-1})\), giving explicit finite-distance quantile and moment errors.
