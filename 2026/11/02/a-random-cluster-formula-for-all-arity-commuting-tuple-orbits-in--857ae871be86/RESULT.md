# A random-cluster formula for all-arity commuting-tuple orbits in radical-square-zero quiver algebras

## Finding
Let \(Q\) be any finite quiver, with loops and parallel arrows permitted, and let \(G\) be its underlying undirected multigraph. Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\), \(q\) a prime power, and \(A=kQ/J^2\), where \(J\) is the ideal generated by all arrows. For every integer \(m\geq 0\), write \(K_Q(m;q)\) for the number of simultaneous-conjugacy orbits of ordered pairwise-commuting \(m\)-tuples in \(A\), under the unit group \(A^\times\). Write \(n=|V(G)|\), \(E=E(G)\), and
\[
Z_G(X,Y)=\sum_{F\subseteq E}X^{k(F)}Y^{|F|},
\]
where \(k(F)\) is the number of connected components of the spanning multigraph \((V,F)\). Then
\[
\boxed{K_Q(m;q)=\frac{Z_G((q-1)q^m,q^m-1)}{(q-1)^n}.}
\]
This is an exact integer for every field, every finite quiver, and every tuple length, including \(m=0\). In particular it does not depend on the orientation of the arrows.

For \(m\geq1\), the stronger orbit classification uses a vertex-labeling \(c:V\to k^m\). Each arrow whose endpoints have distinct labels has a unique allowable orbit-coordinate; on arrows joining equal labels, the orbit is described by a zero/nonzero support \(F\), a projective direction in \(\mathbb P^{m-1}(k)\) on each supported arrow, and a multiplicative cycle class in \((k^\times)^{|F|-n+k(F)}\). Thus nonzero cycles, including loops and parallel-edge cycles, produce independent holonomy parameters.

If \(b(G)\) is the number of bridges and \(\beta(G)=|E|-n+k(E)\) the cycle rank, then for each fixed \(q\),
\[
\boxed{K_Q(m;q)\sim \frac{q^{b(G)}}{(q-1)^{n-k(E)}}q^{m(n+\beta(G))}\quad(m\to\infty).}
\]
In particular the large-arity orbit growth exponent detects the cycle rank when \(n\) is known. For any fixed vertex count, the values for all prime powers \(q\) and all \(m\) determine the full bivariate random-cluster polynomial \(Z_G\).

## Assumptions and scope
A quiver is finite, may be disconnected, and may have loops and multiple arrows. In the quotient \(A=kQ/J^2\), all products of two arrows are zero, while vertex idempotents are pairwise orthogonal. Empty tuple counts as one. The unit group acts by simultaneous conjugation, not by arbitrary change of bases of \(A\) or by quiver automorphisms. \(G\) retains each arrow as an individually labeled undirected edge; loops count as cycles, and spanning components include isolated vertices. \(k\) is any finite field, with no restriction on characteristic.

## Proof
Write each \(a_i\in A\) as \(a_i=\sum_v c_{v,i}e_v+\sum_{a:u\to v}x_{a,i}a\). Collect the diagonal entries at vertex \(v\) into \(c_v\in k^m\), and the arrow entries into \(x_a\in k^m\). Since \(J^2=0\), all \(a_i\) commute if and only if for each arrow \(a:u\to v\) the vectors \(c_u-c_v\) and \(x_a\) are linearly dependent (their exterior product vanishes). When \(c_u\ne c_v\), this forces \(x_a\) into the one-dimensional span of \(c_u-c_v\). When \(c_u=c_v\), any \(x_a\in k^m\) is allowed. This equivalence also covers loops.

Every unit uniquely has the form \(g=\sum_v t_ve_v+s\), with \(t_v\in k^\times\) and \(s\in J\). Conjugation fixes every \(c_v\); at an arrow \(a:u\to v\), it sends \(x_a\) to \((t_u/t_v)x_a+(s_a/t_v)(c_v-c_u)\). Thus the independent additive parameters \(s_a\) eliminate all arrows of unequal endpoint color. The surviving equal-color arrows are acted upon by the torus \((k^\times)^V\) through \(x_a\mapsto(t_u/t_v)x_a\). No other parameters act on them.

Fix \(c\). Choose the set \(F\) of equal-color arrows for which \(x_a\ne0\). Each supported arrow has \(P_m=(q^m-1)/(q-1)\) possible projective directions. For fixed directions, the remaining scalar amplitudes form \((k^\times)^F\). The vertex-torus incidence homomorphism has image order \((q-1)^{n-k(F)}\): choose scalars along a spanning forest independently; exactly one global scalar survives per connected component. Hence the number of amplitude orbits is \((q-1)^{|F|-n+k(F)}\). The formula remains valid with loops, parallel edges, and isolated vertices.

Interchange the sums over \(c\) and \(F\). A fixed edge set \(F\) can occur only if \(c\) is constant on each component of \((V,F)\); there are \((q^m)^{k(F)}\) such colorings. Therefore
\[
K_Q(m;q)=\sum_{F\subseteq E}(q^m)^{k(F)}P_m^{|F|}(q-1)^{|F|-n+k(F)}
=\frac{Z_G((q-1)q^m,q^m-1)}{(q-1)^n}.
\]
For \(m=0\), both sides equal one directly.

For the asymptotic, regard the right-hand side as a polynomial in \(T=q^m\). Its degree is \(n+\beta(G)\), since the term indexed by \(F\) has top degree \(k(F)+|F|=n+\beta(F)\) with positive leading coefficient. Equality \(\beta(F)=\beta(G)\) holds precisely for subsets containing every nonbridge edge and any choice of bridges. Summing their leading coefficients gives \((q-1)^{-n}(q-1)^{k(E)}q^{b(G)}\), proving the displayed asymptotic. To recover \(Z_G\) from the full family of counts for fixed known \(n\), any difference of candidate polynomials vanishes, for each fixed prime power \(q\), at infinitely many \(X=(q-1)q^m, Y=q^m-1\), hence on the entire line \(X=(q-1)(Y+1)\). Distinct prime powers give infinitely many distinct lines, so the difference polynomial is identically zero.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify.py` directly constructs finite-dimensional algebras by vertex idempotent and arrow coordinates, enumerates commuting tuples, applies every algebra unit by the explicit conjugation formula, and counts actual orbits. It compares these with a separately computed edge-subset sum. Ten completed cases include characteristic two and odd characteristic, paths, a square, parallel arrows, a directed three-cycle, and a loop. Invocation: `python3 verify.py`. The completion marker is `RADICAL_SQUARE_ZERO_TUPLE_ORBITS_OK 10`. These are finite tests; the preceding argument proves the formula for all finite quivers and all tuple lengths.

For a connected tree on \(n\) vertices the formula specializes to \(K_Q(m;q)=T(T+(T-1)/(q-1))^{n-1}\), where \(T=q^m\). For the four-cycle it gives \(K_Q(m;q)=(T+(T-1)/(q-1))^4+(T(q-1)-1)((T-1)/(q-1))^4\), yielding \(82\) orbits at \((q,m)=(2,1)\) and \(2644\) at \((2,2)\). These cases were checked by direct orbit enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Sokal defines the random-cluster/Tutte polynomial; that polynomial is not a new object here. Helleloid and Rodriguez-Villegas study point counts of absolutely indecomposable quiver representations and graph-enumeration consequences; the current formula counts simultaneous-conjugacy orbits of unrestricted commuting tuples in an explicitly specified radical-square-zero algebra, not absolutely indecomposable representations. Sharma establishes general rationality and computes commuting-matrix similarity classes for full matrix algebras of small sizes; it does not supply this graph-uniform evaluation. Kaur, Sharma and Singh give finite-field branching rules for invertible triangular matrix groups of small sizes; unit-group commuting tuples are a different object from unrestricted algebra tuples on arbitrary quivers. The single-color torus action is a standard quiver-representation mechanism; the new assertion is its exact coupling to commuting diagonal colorings and a single random-cluster specialization, including graph-cycle asymptotics and the polynomial-reconstruction consequence.

## Limitations
This concerns associative path algebras with all length-two paths killed; it does not apply to general path algebras, nonzero radical-square products, or arbitrary representation dimensions. The formula counts orbits under \(A^\times\), not algebra-isomorphism classes or automorphism-group orbits. It is not a classification of scheme structures. Literature priority for a potentially equivalent graph-weighted quiver orbit formula remains uncertain; the result has not received independent verification.
