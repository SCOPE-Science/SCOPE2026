# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The complete claim follows from the exact all-pairs energy identity, alignment of pair midpoint vectors, weighted Cauchy–Schwarz, and constrained stationarity of a finite box quadratic. The reciprocal-sum subcritical and equality regimes follow from positivity and the equality case of weighted Cauchy–Schwarz. The supercritical global minimizer exists by compactness and has negative reduced energy. Its first-order box conditions force a single scalar fixed-point equation, whose unique positive root follows by strict monotonicity after first saturation. The energy, degeneracy, equal-coupling examples and one-sided jump are algebraic consequences. A separately constructed deterministic checker passed 198 independent algebra/numerical tests including direct Hamiltonian evaluation and global numerical optimizers; finite optimizations are corroborative, not infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, provisional

Searches compared exact physical claims and implied stronger coverage rather than titles. The nearest fully inspected earlier frustrated-spin sources are Hayden–Kaplan–Mahanti (arXiv:1003.5622, 2010) on finite-range 2D classical Heisenberg–XY plus biquadratic models, and Andreanov–Fistul (arXiv:1912.06397, 2019) on periodic kagome sign frustration. Björnberg–Fröhlich–Ueltschi (DOI 10.1007/s00220-019-03634-x, 2019) treat homogeneous ferromagnetic quantum complete graphs, not this classical heterogeneous negative matching. A Granato–Kosterlitz 1986 article addresses unequal positive/negative XY bonds but only its abstract was accessible. No checked source supplied the complete finite matching graph reciprocal-coupling threshold, critical ground-state set, or jump. A generic graph Hamiltonian or minimization theorem could still subsume this elementary-structure result; independent historical verification is pending.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The object is a natural solvable testbed for sparse frustration embedded in all-to-all XY order, of interest for finite spin/Josephson networks. The reciprocal-sum criterion decides collective destruction of magnetization from arbitrary unequal defect strengths; the theorem does more than give a stationary-point equation by classifying all global minima, an exact critical continuum, independent-pair degeneracies, and a nonzero one-sided order-parameter jump. The unequal-coupling formula distinguishes partially canted ordered pairs from the homogeneous all-or-none transition. It is restricted to zero-temperature graph ground states, so no finite-temperature phase transition is inferred.

## Limitations and closest overlap

Older frustration work can exhibit order-disorder switches and high degeneracy but not necessarily on this complete-graph matched-defect family. The reduction is low-dimensional and uses standard tools, so broader coverage in unlocated literature remains a real originality risk. The 1986 unequal-bond text was not inspected beyond an abstract; that limitation is not represented as a full-text exclusion. Independent scientific audit has not been performed.
