# A reduced 23-point superspecial Hasse intersection for Ciani quartics in characteristic seven

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraic closure of \(\mathbb F_7\). In the three-dimensional normalized Ciani parameter space, consider
\[
C_{a,b,c}:\quad X^4+Y^4+Z^4+aX^2Y^2+bX^2Z^2+cY^2Z^2=0.
\]
Write
\[
\Delta=(a^2-4)(b^2-4)(c^2-4)(a^2+b^2+c^2-abc-4),
\]
and restrict to the smooth open set \(U=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_7[a,b,c,\Delta^{-1}]\). Define
\[
H(u,v,w)=(2uv-4w)\bigl((2uv-4w)^2-(u^2-4)(v^2-4)\bigr).
\]
The three elliptic-quotient Hasse equations cut out the closed subscheme
\[
Z=V\bigl(H(a,b,c),H(a,c,b),H(b,c,a)\bigr)\subset U.
\]
**The scheme \(Z\) is finite étale over \(\mathbb F_7\) of degree exactly \(23\)**. Its \(23\) geometric points are all \(\mathbb F_{49}\)-rational, and every corresponding smooth Ciani quartic is superspecial. Conversely, every superspecial smooth member of this normalized family corresponds to one of these points. In particular, the three quotient-Hasse hypersurfaces meet transversely at all their common points in \(U\).

For \(\iota\in\mathbb F_{49}\) with \(\iota^2=-1\), the \(23\) normalized parameter triples break into exactly four orbits under coordinate permutations and even-parity coefficient sign changes. The orbit representatives and sizes are
\[
(0,0,0):1,\qquad (0,0,1):6,\qquad (3,3,4):4,\qquad (3,\iota,\iota):12.
\]
These are orbits under the **stated order-24 parameter-symmetry subgroup**, not a claim of four distinct geometric isomorphism classes of curves. Distinct orbits here can describe isomorphic curves under larger projective transformations.

## Assumptions and scope
The base field has characteristic exactly \(7\). The normalized plane equation has unit coefficients on \(X^4,Y^4,Z^4\), and \(\Delta\ne0\). The smoothness criterion \(\Delta\ne0\) is standard for this form (see Ohashi, Lemma 2.1). For smooth members, the genus is \(3\). The claim concerns the **marked coefficient scheme** \(U\), not the coarse moduli scheme of unmarked quartics, and in particular no geometric-isomorphism-class enumeration is asserted.

Three elliptic quotients arise from the commuting involutions changing the signs of the three projective coordinates. Their degree-two quotient maps induce a \(2\)-power isogeny from the curve's Jacobian to the product of the three elliptic Jacobians. Since \(2\) is prime to \(7\), the induced map on \(7\)-divisible groups is an isomorphism. Thus the quartic is superspecial exactly when all three elliptic quotients are supersingular; this uses the equality of the \(a\)-number with genus for a superspecial Jacobian. No assertion about reductions in other characteristics is made.

## Proof
**1. Quotient Hasse equations.** Quotienting the curve by \(X\mapsto-X\), then completing the square in \(X^2\), yields a genus-one quartic double cover with polynomial
\[
 f(T)=(a^2-4)T^4+(2ab-4c)T^2+(b^2-4).
\]
The other quotients correspond to the permutations \((a,c,b)\) and \((b,c,a)\). For an elliptic curve \(V^2=AT^4+BT^2+C\) in characteristic \(7\), the Hasse invariant is, up to a nonzero normalization factor, the coefficient of \(T^6\) in \((AT^4+BT^2+C)^3\), namely
\[
B^3+6ABC=B(B^2-AC)\quad \text{in }\mathbb F_7.
\]
Consequently the three Hasse equations are precisely those defining \(Z\). The smoothness hypothesis ensures the elliptic quotients have no singular branch divisor.

**2. Saturation and the exact scheme length.** Work in \(R=\mathbb F_7[a,b,c]\). Let \(I\) be the ideal generated by the three displayed Hasse polynomials. Its restriction to \(U\) is computed by the saturation \(I:\Delta^\infty\). A lexicographic Gröbner calculation of the Rabinowitsch ideal
\[
(I,t\Delta-1)\subset\mathbb F_7[t,a,b,c]
\]
with variable order \(t>a>b>c\) eliminates \(t\) and gives a seven-element monic Gröbner basis \(G\) of the saturated ideal. Its leading monomials, in that order, are
\[
a^3,\quad ab,\quad ac,\quad b^4,\quad b^3c^4,\quad bc^5,\quad c^7.
\]
The full seven polynomial expressions, not only the leading terms, are hard-coded as an independently checked certificate in `verify_groebner.py`. The standard monomial basis of \(R/(I:\Delta^\infty)\) is
\[
\{1,c,\ldots,c^6\}\ \cup\ \{b,bc,\ldots,bc^4\}\ \cup\ \{b^2,b^2c,\ldots,b^2c^4\}\ \cup\ \{b^3,b^3c,\ldots,b^3c^3\}\ \cup\ \{a,a^2\}.
\]
This has \(7+5+5+4+2=23\) elements, proving the scheme-theoretic length of \(Z\) is exactly \(23\).

**3. All solutions and reducedness.** In the field \(\mathbb F_{49}=\mathbb F_7[\iota]/(\iota^2+1)\), direct exhaustive field arithmetic verifies that the four stated parameter-symmetry orbits, of sizes \(1,6,4,12\), consist of \(23\) distinct points, each with \(\Delta\ne0\) and all three Hasse equations zero. The length calculation shows that no other geometric solutions, including solutions over extensions of \(\mathbb F_{49}\), can occur. Because all \(23\) geometric points are distinct and the total scheme length is \(23\), each local ring has length one: \(Z\) is geometrically reduced and therefore finite étale over the perfect field \(\mathbb F_7\).

As an independent local check, `verify_f49.py` differentiates the three Hasse equations and computes their \(3\times3\) Jacobian determinant over \(\mathbb F_{49}\) at every one of the \(23\) points. Every determinant is nonzero. The Jacobian criterion independently establishes that the three hypersurfaces are transverse at all \(23\) points.

**4. Superspecial equivalence.** The quotient maps and their \(2\)-power isogeny identify the characteristic-seven \(7\)-divisible groups. The three elliptic Hasse invariants vanish exactly when the three elliptic factors are supersingular. Their product has \(a\)-number three; hence the genus-three Jacobian does as well, and the curve is superspecial. Conversely a superspecial Jacobian forces all three factors to have vanishing Hasse invariant. Therefore \(Z(k)\) is exactly the smooth normalized superspecial parameter locus.

## Verification
Run `python verify_groebner.py` after installing the declared SymPy dependency. It recomputes the saturation from the three Hasse equations, checks the exact seven-polynomial elimination basis, verifies the leading monomials and the \(23\) standard monomials, and prints `VERIFY_GROEBNER_OK`.

Run `python verify_f49.py` using only the Python standard library. It performs exact arithmetic in \(\mathbb F_7[\iota]/(\iota^2+1)\), checks the smooth Hasse-zero points, enumerates and verifies all four symmetry orbits, and checks the Jacobian nonvanishing at every solution. It prints `VERIFY_F49_OK`.

The two verifiers test different critical links: a globally exhaustive elimination ideal, and independent field arithmetic plus local transversality. The algebraic elimination, not a bounded field enumeration, proves completeness over the algebraic closure. All nonstandard computational steps are reproduced from source in the package.

## Relationship to prior work
The normalized Ciani family and its parameter symmetries are classical. Lercier–Ritzenthaler–Rovetta–Sijsling develop representative families and explicitly describe the even-parity sign action on Ciani parameters (arXiv:1403.0562, Section 3). Ohashi's arXiv:2201.12996, Sections 2–4, explicitly establishes the smoothness condition, the three elliptic quotients, the \(2\)-power isogeny, and the **already-known** general result that superspecial Ciani coefficients lie in \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\). These are premises, not claimed discoveries.

Ohashi also cites Brock's 1993 doctoral thesis as studying superspecial Ciani curves **and their enumerations**. That thesis was not available for complete material comparison. Even an older enumeration of geometric curves or rational coefficient triples would not, by itself, prove the scheme-theoretic reducedness and transverse intersection of these three Hasse divisors; nonetheless an exact older scheme calculation could subsume the present statement. The claimed additional assertion is specifically the **reduced degree-23 characteristic-seven Hasse intersection**, together with its reproducible normalized coefficient-orbit certificate. Historical priority requires independent checking of Brock and other specialized material.

## Limitations
The four orbit sizes are **not** the number of distinct unmarked geometric isomorphism classes. The result excludes \(\Delta=0\), which includes singular plane quartics, and is restricted to characteristic seven. The proof relies on reproducible symbolic Gröbner arithmetic with a declared dependency and on finite arithmetic over the field of \(49\) elements; no independent human examination or external formal proof has been performed. Brock's thesis was a plausible source but could not be fully inspected, so an originality risk remains. Known rationality of superspecial Ciani parameters over \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\) is not attributed to this calculation.
