# A seven-element parity-switching semigroup on friendship graphs

## Finding

Let \(F_m\), for any integer \(m\ge2\), be the friendship graph consisting of \(m\) triangles sharing exactly one vertex. It has \(2m+1\) vertices. On binary configurations \(\mathbb F_2^{2m+1}\), let \(A\) be the self-exclusive neighborhood-XOR update, and let \(B=I+A\) be the self-inclusive update, both synchronous and without an affine constant. The transformations generated by the two updates form **exactly seven distinct maps**:
\[
\{I,A,A^2,B,AB,J,0\},\qquad J=\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}.
\]

More precisely, a finite switching word with \(p\) occurrences of \(A\) and \(q\) of \(B\) induces exactly the following operator, regardless of their order:
\[
W_{p,q}=\begin{cases}
 I,&p=q=0,\\
 A,&q=0,\ p\ge1\text{ odd},\\
 A^2,&q=0,\ p\ge2\text{ even},\\
 B,&p=0,\ q=1,\\
 AB,&p\ge1,\ q=1,\\
 J,&p=0,\ q\ge2,\\
 0,&p\ge1,\ q\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]
Thus **a switching word annihilates every initial configuration if and only if it contains both at least one self-exclusive update and at least two self-inclusive updates**. The shortest such words are \(ABB\), \(BAB\), and \(BBA\), all of length three.

The seven maps have exact ranks in their displayed order:
\[
(2m+1,\,2m,\,2m,\,m+\mathbf1_{\{m\text{ even}\}},\,m-\mathbf1_{\{m\text{ odd}\}},\,1,\,0).
\]

For the fixed self-exclusive rule, put \(f_m=2^{m+\mathbf1_{\{m\text{ odd}\}}}\). There are exactly \(f_m\) fixed configurations and exactly \((2^{2m}-f_m)/2\) primitive two-cycles; there are no other primitive cycles, and every state becomes periodic after at most one update. Its Artin--Mazur zeta function is
\[
\zeta_A(z)=(1-z)^{-f_m}(1-z^2)^{-(2^{2m}-f_m)/2}.
\]
Under the fixed self-inclusive rule every configuration reaches one of the two uniform fixed configurations in at most two updates, and exactly \(2^{m+1+\mathbf1_{\{m\text{ odd}\}}}\) configurations are already uniform after one update; its zeta function is \(\zeta_B(z)=(1-z)^{-2}\).

For independent random choices of self-inclusive updates with probability \(\theta\in(0,1)\), let \(T\) be the first switching-word length whose operator is zero **on every input**. For each integer \(t\ge2\),
\[
\Pr(T>t)=(1-\theta)^t+t\theta(1-\theta)^{t-1}+\theta^t,
\qquad
\mathbb E T=\frac2\theta+\frac{\theta^2}{1-\theta}.
\]
These statements concern the first uniform annihilating prefix, not the input-dependent hitting time of the zero configuration.

## Assumptions and scope

Take a vertex \(c\) (hub) and leaves \(a_i,b_i\), indexed by \(1\le i\le m\), with precisely edges \(ca_i\), \(cb_i\), and \(a_ib_i\). All state arithmetic, matrices, vector ranks and update products are over the field \(\mathbb F_2\). The graph has no loops; inclusion of the site's own previous state is the distinction between the maps \(A\) and \(B\). A switching word is applied from left to right in time; because \(B=A+I\), the two maps commute and product order is irrelevant. The parameter bound \(m\ge2\) is essential for exactly seven **distinct** semigroup elements (at \(m=1\), two listed maps coincide).

## Proof

**1. Graph identity.** Each vertex has even degree: the hub has degree \(2m\), and each leaf has degree two. Every pair of distinct vertices has exactly one common neighbor: another leaf of the same triangle for a hub--leaf pair, the hub for two distinct leaves, including two from the same triangle. Consequently, the diagonal entries of the adjacency square are zero modulo two and all its off-diagonal entries are one. Writing \(J=\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\), this proves
\[
A^2=I+J,\qquad A\mathbf1=0,\qquad AJ=JA=0,
\qquad J^2=J.
\]
The last equality uses that \(2m+1\) is odd. Hence \(A^3=A\). Since \(B=I+A\), characteristic two gives
\[
B^2=I+A^2=J,\qquad AB=A+A^2,
\qquad AB^2=0.
\]

**2. Semigroup and reset.** Since \(A\) and \(B\) commute, the operator of any word is \(A^pB^q\). For positive \(p\), the identity \(A^3=A\) reduces each power to \(A\) or \(A^2\). For \(q\ge2\), the factor \(B^q=J\) follows because \(BJ=J\); multiplying by any positive power of \(A\) gives zero. If \(q=1\) and \(p\ge1\), the identity \(A^2B=AB\) yields \(AB\). These reductions prove the seven-case normal form and all sufficiency statements. Necessity of the reset count follows because the other six maps are nonzero, as established by their ranks below.

**3. Exact ranks.** The identity \(A^2=I+J\) projects onto the even-parity hyperplane, of dimension \(2m\). Since \(A^3=A\), both \(A\) and \(A^2\) have this image and rank \(2m\). To obtain the other ranks, let \(x=(c,a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_m,b_m)\), define \(s_i=a_i+b_i\), and \(S=\sum_{i=1}^m s_i\). Directly from the neighbor rules,
\[
Ax=(S,\ b_1+c,a_1+c,\ldots,b_m+c,a_m+c),
\]
\[
Bx=(c+S,\ c+s_1,c+s_1,\ldots,c+s_m,c+s_m),
\]
\[
ABx=(0,\ s_1+S,s_1+S,\ldots,s_m+S,s_m+S).
\]
The independent variables \(c,s_1,\ldots,s_m\) yield \(\operatorname{rank}(B)=m+1\) for even \(m\) and \(m\) for odd \(m\): to be in the kernel one needs \(s_i=c\) and \((m+1)c=0\). Similarly, the map \((s_i)\mapsto(s_i+S)\) has kernel of dimension zero for even \(m\), one for odd \(m\). This gives the claimed rank of \(AB\). Since \(J\) has rank one and zero has rank zero, all seven maps are pairwise distinct except possibly \(A\) and \(A^2\). For \(m\ge2\), applying them to a vector supported only on \(a_1\) distinguishes them: \(A^2x=x+\mathbf1\) has nonzero values at other triangles, while \(Ax\) vanishes at all leaves of those triangles. Thus the semigroup has precisely seven elements.

**4. Deterministic cycles and basins.** If \(Ax=x\), the displayed local formula requires \(s_i=c\) for every \(i\), and \(c=\sum s_i=mc\). There is one free bit per leaf-pair and an additional free hub bit exactly when \(m\) is odd, yielding \(f_m\) fixed points. The equality \(A^2=I+J\) implies \(\operatorname{Fix}(A^2)\) is precisely the even-parity hyperplane of size \(2^{2m}\). Since \(A^3=A\), every trajectory enters this hyperplane after one step and there follows a cycle of length one or two. Subtracting the fixed configurations and dividing by two counts primitive two-cycles. The displayed zeta function is the standard product over primitive periodic orbits. For \(B\), the equality \(B^2=J\) yields either \(\mathbf0\) or \(\mathbf1\) according to input parity, and \(B\mathbf1=\mathbf1\), so both are fixed and there are no other periodic points. The number of states sent to \(\{\mathbf0,\mathbf1\}\) in one step is twice the size of \(\ker B\), namely \(2^{2m+2-\operatorname{rank}B}\), which simplifies to the asserted expression. This also proves the zeta function for \(B\).

**5. Random switching law.** The semigroup criterion makes \(T\) the first time the count of \(A\)-choices is at least one and the count of \(B\)-choices at least two. After \(t\) updates, failure occurs precisely if there are at most one \(B\), or all \(t\) updates were \(B\). These disjoint cases have probabilities \((1-\theta)^t+t\theta(1-\theta)^{t-1}\) and \(\theta^t\), respectively. For expectation, the waiting time for the second \(B\) has mean \(2/\theta\); only if the first two steps are both \(B\), with probability \(\theta^2\), is an additional geometric waiting time of mean \(1/(1-\theta)\) required for the first \(A\). This proves the claimed expectation.

## Verification

The self-contained standard-library program `artifacts/verify.py` independently enumerates every binary configuration for \(m=1,\ldots,6\), using the local neighbor update rather than the algebraic identities. For \(m=2,\ldots,6\), it verifies the seven maps are distinct and their ranks match the formulas. For each tested \(m\), it checks the fixed-point counts for \(A\) and \(A^2\), first-step convergence counts for \(B\), the exact power identities, and all three length-three universal reset words. It reports `ALL CHECKS PASS`. These are finite tests, **not** an exhaustive proof of the unbounded-parameter theorem; the proof is in Steps 1--5.

## Relationship to prior work

Rollier and Baetens [1], in their full-text Sections 2--4, derive graph-parity Lyapunov spectra from real adjacency spectra and distinguish that real tangent-space observable from configurations reduced modulo two. They do not classify switching products, the friendship-graph semigroup, or its orbit counts. Pasqualetti, Borra and Bullo [2], especially Sections 3--5, establish general criteria for finite-field consensus, including finite-time convergence; those results explain the conceptual context of the single-rule identity \(B^2=J\) but do not imply the exact arbitrary-switching classification or the seven-element monoid. Fran, Saputri and Kiftiah [4, Theorems 6--7] previously proved that the mod-two square of the friendship-graph adjacency matrix is graphical, using the even degrees of all vertices; graphicality alone does not give the exact matrix, seven-element switching monoid or any orbit counts. In the present proof the stronger common-neighbor count identifies the square as \(I+J\). The related exact cycle classification for complete multipartite parity automata [3] concerns a different graph class: a friendship graph with at least two triangles is **not** complete multipartite, since the nonadjacency relation on its leaves is not transitive. Hence neither its reported parameter table nor a corollary thereof settles this theorem. General linear-algebraic semigroup methods exist; the contribution here is this explicit natural family and the sharp reset-count law.

## Limitations

The theorem is specific to friendship graphs with at least two triangles, arithmetic modulo two, synchronous updates, and zero affine offset. It does not cover graphs with even numbers of vertices, arbitrary sparse graphs, asynchronous protocols, or nonlinear local rules. Real-valued Lyapunov growth can coexist with the binary finite-time convergence here because the two quantities describe different state spaces. The random law is for the first prefix acting as the zero **linear transformation** for every input; particular initial configurations may reach zero earlier. Historical priority remains subject to unindexed work on switching linear systems over finite fields.
