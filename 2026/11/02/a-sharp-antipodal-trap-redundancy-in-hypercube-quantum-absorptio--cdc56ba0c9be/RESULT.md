# A sharp antipodal trap redundancy in hypercube quantum absorption

## Finding
Let \(Q_d\) be the \(d\)-dimensional binary hypercube with \(d\ge2\) and \(2^d\) vertices. Consider continuous-time single-excitation transport with real symmetric adjacency Hamiltonian \(H=J A(Q_d)\), \(J\ne0\), and two distinct, irreversible local sinks of arbitrary strictly positive strengths \(\kappa_a,\kappa_b\). Each vertex is equally probable as the initially occupied site (an incoherent uniform mixture). Let \(\eta_d(a,b)\) be the eventual total absorption probability averaged over initial vertices. Then
\[
\eta_d(a,b)=\begin{cases}(d+1)/2^d,&\operatorname{dist}(a,b)=d,\\ 2d/2^d,&1\le\operatorname{dist}(a,b)<d.\end{cases}
\]
The formula is independent of both sink strengths. Therefore, adding a sink at the vertex antipodal to an existing sink does not improve average quantum absorption **at all**. Every non-antipodal second vertex is equally optimal, and gives the strictly larger value \(2d/2^d\). The worst-case to best-case gain is \((d-1)/2^d\) in absolute average efficiency, or the sharp multiplicative factor \(2d/(d+1)\) relative to the single-sink baseline.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph has vertices \(x\in\{0,1\}^d\), with edges between strings differing in one bit. Dynamics in the single-excitation space is generated by the effective non-Hermitian operator \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}=J A-iK/2\), where \(K=\kappa_a|a\rangle\langle a|+\kappa_b|b\rangle\langle b|\) and \(\kappa_a,\kappa_b>0\). The loss probability from initial density \(\rho_0=2^{-d}I\) is \(\eta=1-\lim_{t\to\infty}\operatorname{tr}(e^{-itH_{\mathrm{eff}}}\rho_0e^{itH_{\mathrm{eff}}^*})\). No pure dephasing or incoherent hopping is included; the rate parameters are finite and positive. The claim is valid for every integer \(d\ge2\), every nonzero real \(J\), and every distinct pair of sink vertices.

## Proof
**1. Bright-subspace reduction.** Define \(\mathcal B=\operatorname{span}\{H^r|a\rangle,H^r|b\rangle:r\ge0\}\). The orthogonal complement \(\mathcal B^\perp\) is invariant under the Hermitian \(H\) and lies in \(\ker K\). Consequently it is dark: loss vanishes on it. Conversely, every eigenvector of \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}\) whose eigenvalue has zero imaginary part obeys \(\langle v,Kv\rangle=0\), hence belongs to the dark subspace. Since the finite-dimensional evolution on \(\mathcal B\) has all eigenvalues in the strict lower half-plane, its norm tends to zero even at Jordan blocks. For the maximally mixed initial state, eventual absorption equals \(\dim(\mathcal B)/2^d\), irrespective of the positive sink rates.

**2. Eigenspace decomposition.** By translating the bit strings, take \(a=0^d\); put \(k=\operatorname{dist}(a,b)\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\). The Walsh vectors \(|\chi_S\rangle=2^{-d/2}\sum_x(-1)^{|S\cap x|}|x\rangle\), indexed by subsets \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}\), are orthonormal eigenvectors of \(A\) with eigenvalue \(d-2|S|\). Let \(P_\ell\) project onto the distinct eigenspace \(\mathcal E_\ell\), \(0\le\ell\le d\). Spectral interpolation in \(H\) gives
\[
\mathcal B=\bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{d}\operatorname{span}\{P_\ell|a\rangle,P_\ell|b\rangle\}.
\]
For every \(\ell\), the coordinates of these two projections in the Walsh basis \(|S|=\ell\) are proportional to \(1\) and \((-1)^{|S\cap b|}\), respectively.

**3. Exact rank per eigenspace.** At \(\ell=0,d\) the eigenspaces are one-dimensional and each contributes rank one. If \(k=d\), then \(b=1^d\), so \((-1)^{|S\cap b|}=(-1)^\ell\) is constant over all \(S\) of cardinality \(\ell\); each spectral eigenspace contributes rank one, yielding \(\dim\mathcal B=d+1\).

If \(1\le k<d\), then for every \(1\le\ell\le d-1\) the Boolean function \((-1)^{|S\cap b|}\), restricted to subsets \(S\) of cardinality \(\ell\), takes both signs: among the \(\ell\)-subsets one can exchange a coordinate lying in \(b\) with a coordinate outside \(b\), changing the parity. Existence of such a pair also follows from connectivity of the Johnson graph on \(\ell\)-subsets and from the fact that its vertices are not all the same intersection parity when both \(b\) and its complement are nonempty. Thus the two projections are independent in each interior eigenspace, contributing rank two. It follows that \(\dim\mathcal B=1+2(d-1)+1=2d\). Combining with Step 1 proves both formulas, optimality, and the exact gain.

## Verification
A standalone checker constructs the adjacency matrix for every \(2\le d\le6\) and every sink separation \(1\le k\le d\), then directly computes the rank of the two-source Krylov matrix from exact graph combinatorics (using floating-point rank only for this diagnostic). All 20 comparisons agree with the predicted \(2d\) versus \(d+1\) formula. The preceding eigenprojector argument, not finite enumeration, proves the theorem for every \(d\).

## Relationship to prior work
Novo, Chakraborty, Mohseni, Neven and Omar (2015) explicitly derive a single-sink hypercube efficiency from a \((d+1)\)-dimensional symmetric subspace and discuss symmetry-related transport suppression. Their published treatment does **not**, in the material checked, provide the two-sink separation phase diagram above. Razzoli, Paris and Bordone (2021) give the general one-trap spectral-subspace principle and examples of quantum transport efficiencies. The bright-subspace principle here is standard; the new claim is solely the exact all-dimension **two-trap geometry classification** and its sharp antipodal redundancy, obtained by a two-source eigenspace rank calculation. In particular, its proof does not claim novelty for the Walsh diagonalization, single-sink efficiency, or the general dark-space principle.

## Limitations
This exact result is for uniform initial incoherent mixtures, two distinct fixed vertex sinks, and pure coherent nearest-neighbor evolution. Pointwise capture probabilities for prescribed nonuniform initial states can depend on the actual geometry beyond the dichotomy in the theorem. It does not describe dephasing, sink saturation, finite-time capture, or classical random walks. Although targeted sources and semantic research records were compared, historical priority cannot be guaranteed; a differently phrased two-trap projection result may have escaped the search.
