# A sharp characteristic-divisibility law for Nash fans of normal toric threefolds

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge3\), put
\[
 e=(1,0,0),\qquad f=(0,1,0),\qquad h_i=(1,1,i)\ (1\le i\le n),
 \qquad K_n=\mathbb R_{\ge0}\langle e,f,h_n\rangle,
\]
and let \(X_n=\operatorname{Spec}k[K_n\cap\mathbb Z^3]\) over an algebraically closed field \(k\) of characteristic \(p\ge0\). Here \(p=0\) means characteristic zero. Let \(\mathcal J_{n,p}\) be the characteristic-\(p\) logarithmic Jacobian monomial ideal (for \(p=0\), its ordinary characteristic-zero counterpart), and let \(\mathcal N_{n,p}\) be its Newton polyhedron in \(\mathbb R^3\).

**Theorem.** Write
\[
 t(n,p)=\begin{cases}n,&p=0\text{ or }p\nmid n,\\n-1,&p>0\text{ and }p\mid n.\end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal N_{n,p}=
 \operatorname{Conv}\{(2,2,1),(2,2,t(n,p)),(3,2,2n-1),(2,3,2n-1)\}+K_n.}
\]
All four displayed points are distinct exposed vertices. In particular, the fan of the **normalized** Nash blowup of \(X_n\) coincides with the characteristic-zero fan **if and only if** \(p=0\) or \(p\nmid n\).

When \(p\mid n\), the interior ray normal to the compact triangle through the three vertices other than \((2,2,1)\) changes from
\[
 \boxed{\mathbb R_{\ge0}(n-1,n-1,-1)\quad\text{to}\quad\mathbb R_{\ge0}(n,n,-1).}
\]
Therefore **every prime characteristic** occurs as the exceptional characteristic of some normal affine toric threefold (take \(n=p\) for \(p\ge3\), and \(n=4\) for \(p=2\)). The statement concerns equality of toric fans relative to the fixed lattice and base cone, not abstract isomorphism after changing the lattice.

## Assumptions and scope

The semigroup \(\Gamma_n=K_n\cap\mathbb Z^3\) is saturated and has the minimal Hilbert basis
\[
 H_n=\{e,f,h_1,\ldots,h_n\}.
\]
To verify this, write any integral point \((x,y,z)\in K_n\) with \(z=qn+r\), \(0\le r<n\). If \(r=0\), subtract \(qh_n\); otherwise subtract \(qh_n+h_r\). The remaining coordinates are nonnegative integral multiples of \(e,f\) since \(x,y\ge\lceil z/n\rceil\). The displayed generators are indecomposable: every nonzero element with positive third coordinate has first and second coordinates at least one; hence an \(h_i\) cannot split into two nonzero semigroup elements. The two coordinate generators are also indecomposable. Thus \(X_n\) is normal, affine, toric, and three-dimensional.

For \(p=0\), the monomial ideal is generated by \(\chi^{u+v+w}\) over distinct \(u,v,w\in H_n\) with nonzero determinant; for prime \(p\), the determinant must be nonzero modulo \(p\). The cited toric Nash theorem identifies the Nash blowup with the blowup of this ideal, and normalization replaces its monomial Rees algebra by the normal toric modification determined by \(\mathcal N_{n,p}\).

No statement is made about the number of further Nash iterations needed to resolve \(X_n\), nor about characteristic-dependence for arbitrary other threefolds.

## Proof

**1. Determinants and possible monomials.** For \(1\le i<j\le n\),
\[
 \det(e,f,h_i)=i,\qquad
 \det(e,h_i,h_j)=j-i,\qquad
 \det(f,h_i,h_j)=i-j.
\]
All determinants formed from three of the \(h_i\)'s vanish, since they lie in a common two-dimensional vector subspace. Hence the exponent set defining \(\mathcal J_{n,p}\) consists exactly of the permitted members of
\[
 (2,2,i),\qquad (3,2,i+j),\qquad (2,3,i+j),
\]
where \(1\le i\le n\) in the first class and \(1\le i<j\le n\) in the last two. The first-class exponent is permitted precisely when \(p=0\) or \(p\nmid i\); in the other classes the condition is \(p=0\) or \(p\nmid(j-i)\).

**2. Exact lower envelope.** A real linear form \(\ell_{a,b,c}(x,y,z)=ax+by+cz\) is bounded below on \(\mathcal N_{n,p}\) precisely when it lies in the dual cone of \(K_n\):
\[
 a\ge0,\qquad b\ge0,\qquad a+b+nc\ge0.
\]
If \(c\ge0\), the smallest value on every possible exponent is attained at \(A=(2,2,1)\), whose determinant is one and which is thus always permitted. Indeed all exponents have first and second coordinates at least two and third coordinate at least one.

If \(c<0\), among first-class exponents the minimum occurs at the largest admissible \(i\), namely \(i=t(n,p)\). Among the other two classes, the largest possible sum of distinct indices is \((n-1)+n=2n-1\). This pair is always admissible, because its determinant has absolute value one. It follows **for every dual-cone linear form**, not just for sampled forms, that
\[
 \min_{v\in\mathcal N_{n,p}}\ell_{a,b,c}(v)
 =\min\{\ell(A),\ell(B),\ell(C),\ell(D)\},
\]
with \(B=(2,2,t(n,p))\), \(C=(3,2,2n-1)\), and \(D=(2,3,2n-1)\). Equality of the lower-support functions and recession cones proves the asserted equality of closed convex polyhedra.

**3. All four points are vertices.** The dual-cone weights \((1,1,1)\), \((n+1,n+1,-1)\), \((\tfrac12,2n,-1)\), and \((2n,\tfrac12,-1)\) expose \(A,B,C,D\), respectively. The inequalities are strict against the other three candidates because \(n\ge3\) and \(t(n,p)\in\{n-1,n\}\). Since each candidate is an actual ideal exponent, these are exactly four distinct exposed vertices.

**4. The sharp characteristic wall.** If \(p\nmid n\), all four vertices agree with characteristic zero, so the entire polyhedron and its normal fan agree. If \(p\mid n\), the \(B\)-vertex shifts from \(B_0=(2,2,n)\) to \(B_p=(2,2,n-1)\).

For characteristic zero, \(w_0=(n-1,n-1,-1)\) is in the interior of the dual cone, and \(\ell_{w_0}\) is minimized exactly on the triangle \(\operatorname{Conv}\{B_0,C,D\}\). For characteristic \(p\mid n\), the analogous exposed compact triangle is \(\operatorname{Conv}\{B_p,C,D\}\), with primitive inward normal \(w_p=(n,n,-1)\). The triangles are genuine two-dimensional facets, since their edge vectors have independent first and second coordinates. Their normals are distinct interior rays for \(n\ge3\).

To rule out the possibility that the *fans* nonetheless coincide, observe that \(w_0\) is a one-dimensional normal cone of the characteristic-zero fan, but, in characteristic \(p\mid n\), it lies in the relative interior of the two-dimensional normal cone of the edge \(\operatorname{Conv}\{C,D\}\): both \(C,D\) have the same strictly minimal weight, while \(A,B_p\) have strictly greater weight. Thus \(w_0\) is not a ray of the characteristic-\(p\) normal fan. This proves the if-and-only-if assertion and the claimed ray displacement.

Run `python3 verify_toric.py` in a directory containing the accompanying standalone program. It uses integer arithmetic for all determinants and support comparisons, tests all \(n\) from \(3\) through \(24\) for characteristic zero and nine different prime characteristics, and checks exact exposing weights and the changed triple-facet rays. Its success marker is `TORIC_NASH_ALL_PRIMES_NEWTON_SUPPORT_AND_FAN_TESTS_OK`. This finite check is a reproducibility aid; the proof above establishes the theorem for every integer \(n\ge3\) and every characteristic.

## Relationship to prior work

Duarte, Jeffries, and Núñez-Betancourt (first circulated August 11, 2022, arXiv:2208.05599) prove the characteristic-\(p\) toric logarithmic-Jacobian theorem (Theorem 1.9) and exhibit a **single** dimension-three characteristic-two example with extremal ray \(h_2=(1,1,2)\) (Section 3). Their theorem underlies the translation from ideals to Nash modifications here; their worked example is the \(n=2\) predecessor, not the new all-\(n\) assertion.

Atanasov, Lopez, Perry, Proudfoot, and Thaddeus (arXiv:0910.5028, 2009) establish polyhedral Nash methods in characteristic zero and tabulate the cones \(\langle e,f,(1,1,3)\rangle\) and \(\langle e,f,(1,1,4)\rangle\) in their characteristic-zero resolution study. Those calculations do not compare positive-characteristic Newton polyhedra. Duarte and Núñez-Betancourt (arXiv:2002.07081, 2020) treat higher Nash blowups and an \(A_3\) surface singularity, not the ordinary Nash fans of this three-dimensional infinite family.

The refinement here is an explicit **if-and-only-if divisibility classification for every \(n\ge3\) and every prime**, with all Newton vertices and the precise moving interior fan ray. The cited general theory provides an algorithmic description, not this closed classification; deducing it requires the determinant and support-function argument above.

## Limitations

The criterion compares toric subdivisions of the same ambient cone, not geometric invariants of arbitrary non-toric deformations. It does not say that the first blowup is smooth, nor that different fans are necessarily nonisomorphic as abstract varieties without their maps to \(X_n\). The \(n=2,p=2\) predecessor is already present in the 2022 source and is deliberately excluded from the new theorem. Earlier unindexed computations could contain an equivalent parameter-family classification; independent external validation and priority review have not been performed.
