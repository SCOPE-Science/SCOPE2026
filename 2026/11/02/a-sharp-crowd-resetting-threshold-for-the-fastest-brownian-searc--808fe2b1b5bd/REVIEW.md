# Scientific assessment

**Correctness: PASS.** The exact Brownian hitting density and its discounted Laplace transform provide the normalized one-attempt probability. The constrained Laplace principle identifies the two branches of its rate function. A finite-time upper bound follows from expected marked reset starts; the lower bound follows from a Poisson two-point factorial-moment estimate. Together these estimates prove convergence in probability below and at the coefficient \(1/4\) and prove a full unit-exponential distributional limit above it, without treating overlapping attempts as independent. The standard-library numerical checker passes 209 consistency tests. Risks: the theorem is restricted to independent exponential resetting and fixed noncritical coefficient outside the critical point; no moment-convergence or uniform critical-window assertion is made.

**Originality: PASS (qualified, best knowledge).** Bao (2026), arXiv:2607.22528, provides a timely comparison for the \(1/\log N\) extreme-arrival benchmark but treats interaction-driven modifications without resetting. Biroli, Majumdar and Schehr (2023), arXiv:2303.18012, already give the *identical independently resetting Brownian search model*, full survival renewal representation and finite-\(N\) mean optimization. Linn and Lawley (2023), arXiv:2302.05409, give the fast-reset single-searcher exponential limit. The specific simultaneous large-\(N\), rate-\((\log N)^2\) three-regime result with the sharp threshold and its critical time delay was not located in those works or queried published research records; each existing formula or asymptotic requires new nonuniform estimates to obtain it. Residual risk: prior full finite-parameter formulas may have been asymptotically analyzed elsewhere, and some comparison sources were inspected only in the relevant material, not by exhaustive literature audit.

**Value: PASS.** The result answers a physical design question about crowd size and per-searcher restart frequency: an intervention that grows only as a squared logarithm can turn the familiar inverse-logarithmic fastest-hit law into a polynomially *slower* exponential clock. The sharp \(1/4\) threshold separates an early rare-excursion regime, a distinct critical latency, and repeated exponentially unlikely attempts. It does not merely reproduce a fixed-\(N\) formula or compute an arbitrary numerical coefficient. The result is a restricted family classification rather than an unrestricted optimization theorem.

Closest literature: Bao (2026), Biroli–Majumdar–Schehr (2023), Linn–Lawley (2023), and the many-particle resetting extrema analysis of Hartmann–Majumdar–Schehr. The underlying renewal transform and the single-searcher exponential law remain previous work. A complete independent publication-priority audit is still required.
