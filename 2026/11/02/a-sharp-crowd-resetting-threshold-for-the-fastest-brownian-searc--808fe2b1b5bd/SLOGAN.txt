When \(N\) independent Brownian searchers reset at rate proportional to \((\log N)^2\), the fastest arrival undergoes a sharp transition at dimensionless coefficient \(1/4\): below and at it the search time is inverse-logarithmic, but above it the search time grows polynomially in \(N\) and has a rescaled exponential law. The theorem identifies a precise collective penalty for excessively aggressive resetting without asserting a new finite-population optimal rate.
