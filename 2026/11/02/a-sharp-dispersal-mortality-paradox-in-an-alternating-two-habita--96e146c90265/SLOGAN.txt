In a two-patch population with precisely alternating good and bad years, movement mortality creates a sharp paradox: full habitat swapping can be the growth-optimal strategy even though every sufficiently small positive migration fraction makes growth worse than staying put. The optimal strategy switches exactly at survival \(s=\sqrt{b/a}\), while the sign of the local migration effect switches later at \(s=2\sqrt{ab}/(a+b)\); no strictly intermediate migration fraction ever maximizes annual growth.
