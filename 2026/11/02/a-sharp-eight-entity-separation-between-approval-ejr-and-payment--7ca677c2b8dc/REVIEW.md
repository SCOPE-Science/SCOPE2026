# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The assertion uses the exact strict payment expression of Skowron's strong EJR+ up-to-one specialized to unit costs and approval utilities. The analytical proof handles all electorate sizes for one or two elected seats, all two-voter populations regardless of committee size (requiring nonempty support of selected candidates), and the three-voter three-seat boundary; these force the total size lower bound of eight. Explicit cases attain equality. The finite census is exhaustive over 1,470 and 10,200 canonical selected-multiset/unelected-mask signatures at the two possible minimal parameter triples, with exact-integer coalition comparisons. All five permutation orbits and both Pareto-efficient classes are reproduced by the standalone checker. The finite cross-check of 19,448 smaller configurations is corroboration, not the proof of the general bound.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

Skowron's Definition 4 and Example 4 already establish the two axioms and their non-equivalence, using 1,000 voters; Brill–Peters establish the original approval EJR+ formulation. The result here is restricted to a sharp, structurally motivated boundary and complete minimal orbit census, including Pareto-efficiency. Relevant accessible primary documents and research-record searches did not identify that minimality and classification. Scientific priority outside inspected and indexed material remains unresolved.

## Value — PASS

The motivating payment-based axiom changes which committees count as proportional, but the published separation has a large electorate and gives no sharp small-domain boundary. The new minimum and full structural census show the exact first domain in which the distinction is operational, and exhibit that it survives Pareto efficiency. This is a natural finite classification supported by an infinite obstruction theorem, not a renamed corollary of the published separation.

## Limitations

Results concern equal-cost approval committees of full prescribed size, with every selected candidate approved by at least one voter and duplicate approval types allowed. The classification is up to relabeling voters and selected candidates. No weighted-cost, arbitrary-additive-utility, or different-axiom extrapolation is claimed. Publication priority could be affected by unindexed or unpublished equivalents.
