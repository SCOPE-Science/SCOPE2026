For symmetric eigenproblems, the sketched Rayleigh--Ritz matrix differs from its ordinary Ritz matrix by at most \(\varepsilon\|R\|_2/\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^2}\), and a Ritz gap exceeding twice this bound forces every sketched eigenvalue to remain real and simple. A four-dimensional positive-definite example attains the bound and exhibits the exact real-to-complex transition at \(\varepsilon=\delta/\sqrt{\delta^2+\gamma^2}\).
