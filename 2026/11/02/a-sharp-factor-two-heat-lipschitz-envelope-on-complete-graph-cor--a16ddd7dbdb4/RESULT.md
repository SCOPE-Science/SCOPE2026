# A sharp factor-two heat-Lipschitz envelope on complete-graph coronas

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge 2\), let \(G_n=K_n\circ K_1\) consist of a complete graph on core vertices \(c_1,\ldots,c_n\), with one pendant vertex \(\ell_i\) joined only to \(c_i\). All edges have unit length and unit heat conductance. Let \(L_n\) be the unnormalized combinatorial Laplacian, \(H_t=\exp(-tL_n)\), and
\[
\Lambda_n(t)=\sup_{\operatorname{Lip}(f)>0}\frac{\operatorname{Lip}(H_tf)}{\operatorname{Lip}(f)},\qquad
\operatorname{Lip}(f)=\max_{uv\in E(G_n)}|f(u)-f(v)|.
\]
Write
\[
\Delta_n=\sqrt{n^2+4},\quad
\alpha_n=\frac{n+2-\Delta_n}{2},\quad
\beta_n=\frac{n+2+\Delta_n}{2}
\]
and, for \(t\ge0\),
\[
\begin{split}
p_n(t)&=\frac{(\beta_n-n-1)e^{-\alpha_nt}+(n+1-\alpha_n)e^{-\beta_nt}}{\Delta_n},\\
q_n(t)&=\frac{e^{-\alpha_nt}-e^{-\beta_nt}}{\Delta_n},\\
r_n(t)&=\frac{(\beta_n-1)e^{-\alpha_nt}+(1-\alpha_n)e^{-\beta_nt}}{\Delta_n}.
\end{split}
\]
The following formula is exact, sharp, and attained for every \(t\ge0\):
\[
\boxed{\Lambda_n(t)=
\begin{cases}
r_n(t)+q_n(t),&n=2,\\
\dfrac{n-1}{n}\bigl(2r_n(t)+(n-2)q_n(t)\bigr)
-\dfrac{n-2}{n}e^{-2t},&n\ge3.
\end{cases}}
\]
At every positive time, the sole maximizing edge type is the core-to-core edge if \(n=2\), and the pendant edge if \(n\ge3\). For \(n=2\), the unique extremizing profile up to nonzero scaling and additive constants is a four-vertex ramp, with successive values \((2,1,0,-1)\) along the path \(\ell_1,c_1,c_2,\ell_2\). For \(n\ge3\), there are exactly \(n\) extremizing affine rays up to a global sign: choose one index \(i\), and put
\[
f(\ell_i)=2,\quad f(c_i)=1,\quad f(c_j)=0,\quad f(\ell_j)=-1\quad(j\ne i).
\]
There are no other positive-time real extremizers, except nonzero scaling and addition of a constant. At \(t=0\), every nonconstant function is an extremizer.

The norm decreases strictly for all positive times when \(n=2\) or \(n=3\). For every \(n\ge4\), it increases from \(1\), has precisely one strict positive-time maximum, and then decreases strictly to zero. In fact, for \(n\ge3\),
\[
\Lambda_n'(0)=n-3.
\]
Finally, the **sharp universal amplification envelope** over this family is
\[
\boxed{\sup_{n\ge2}\sup_{t\ge0}\Lambda_n(t)=2.}
\]
Each finite \(n\) and finite time has \(\Lambda_n(t)<2\). The supremum is approached by taking \(n\to\infty\) and \(t=(\log n)/n\).

## Assumptions and scope
The underlying space is the real vector space of functions on \(V(G_n)\), modulo constants for the graph-Lipschitz norm. Heat flow is generated by the symmetric, unnormalized, counting-measure Laplacian \((L_nf)(v)=\sum_{w\sim v}(f(v)-f(w))\). The metric is the shortest-path distance for unit edge lengths; it is not a conductance-weighted or resistance distance. Indices range over all integers \(n\ge2\), and times over \(t\ge0\). The classification of proper extremizing profiles is for \(t>0\).

## Proof
**1. A transport identity.** For a zero-total-mass signed measure \(\mu\) on \(G_n\), the graph-distance Kantorovich--Rubinstein norm is
\[
\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{KR}}=
\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\mu(\ell_i)|
+\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\mu(c_i)+\mu(\ell_i)|.
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, every pendant edge must carry exactly the signed leaf mass; after moving that mass to the core, the residual core masses have sum zero. On the complete core, the least possible unit-edge transport cost is half their total variation. These two lower bounds are simultaneously attainable by moving along the corresponding edges, so (1) follows. The dual formula, or decomposition into edge geodesics, shows that
\[
\Lambda_n(t)=\max_{uv\in E(G_n)}\|H_t\delta_u-H_t\delta_v\|_{\mathrm{KR}},
\tag{2}
\]
because the Lipschitz seminorm is the largest adjacent difference.

**2. Exact heat evolution.** Write vectors in core/leaf pairs. On the one-dimensional trivial representation of core-index permutations, the heat generator on each pair is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},
\]
whereas each core-index mean-zero component evolves under
\[
A_n=\begin{pmatrix}-(n+1)&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The exponential of \(A_n\) is the positive symmetric matrix
\[
\exp(tA_n)=\begin{pmatrix}p_n(t)&q_n(t)\\q_n(t)&r_n(t)\end{pmatrix};
\]
its characteristic equation has roots \(-\alpha_n,-\beta_n\). In particular,
\[
q_n(t)>0\ (t>0),\qquad r_n(t)-p_n(t)=nq_n(t).
\tag{3}
\]
For brevity suppress the subscript \(n\) and set \(e=e^{-2t}\). For the initial signed measure \(\delta_{\ell_1}-\delta_{c_1}\), the evolved mass at index \(1\) is
\[
\begin{split}
\mu(c_1)&=-e/n+(1-1/n)(q-p),\\
\mu(\ell_1)&=e/n+(1-1/n)(r-q),
\end{split}
\]
and for \(j\ne1\),
\[
\mu(c_j)=-e/n-(q-p)/n,\qquad
\mu(\ell_j)=e/n-(r-q)/n.
\tag{4}
\]
The core-collapsed masses are \(\mu(c_i)+\mu(\ell_i)=(r-p)(\mathbf1_{i=1}-1/n)\). Put \(s=r-q\). Since \(r'=q-r\) and \(q'=r-(n+1)q\),
\[
s'=-2s+nq,\quad s(0)=1,
\]
which proves \(s>e^{-2t}\) for \(t>0\). Thus the distinguished leaf mass is positive, every other leaf mass is negative, and the collapsed core mass has the sign of \(\mathbf1_{i=1}-1/n\). Substitution in (1) gives the pendant-edge transport norm
\[
P_n(t)=\frac{n-1}{n}\bigl(2r+(n-2)q\bigr)-\frac{n-2}{n}e^{-2t}.
\tag{5}
\]
For the core edge \(c_1c_2\), the signed initial condition \(\delta_{c_1}-\delta_{c_2}\) remains in the mean-zero sector; at indices \(1,2\) its core/leaf masses are \(\pm(p,q)\). Formula (1) gives
\[
E_n(t)=p+3q=r+(3-n)q.
\tag{6}
\]
Every edge is of one of these two types by permutation symmetry.

**3. Which edges maximize, and equality cases.** For \(t>0\), one has \(r(t)>e^{-t}>e^{-2t}\), since \(r'=q-r\), \(r(0)=1\), and \(q>0\). A direct subtraction gives
\[
P_n(t)-E_n(t)=\frac{n-2}{n}\bigl(r-e^{-2t}\bigr)
+\frac{2n^2-6n+2}{n}\,q.
\tag{7}
\]
For \(n=2\), this equals \(-q<0\). For every \(n\ge3\), both coefficients on the right are positive, so it is strictly positive. This proves the displayed norm formula and strict maximizing-edge classification. The signed leaf/core flows in (4) are nonzero at \(t>0\) and use the pendant edges together with all edges \(c_1c_j\); equality in transport duality consequently requires equality on a spanning tree of \(G_n\). It uniquely forces, modulo constants, the pendant-edge ramp in the finding. For a core-edge difference, the two nonzero leaf flows and the direct core flow form a spanning tree only when \(n=2\), and force the four-point ramp. Equality in (2) permits no other edge type. This proves the complete positive-time extremizer classification.

**4. The phase transition.** The positive matrix exponential gives \(E_2(t)=r_2(t)+q_2(t)\), with \(E_2'(t)=-2q_2(t)<0\). For \(n\ge3\), define
\[
\begin{split}
A_n&=\frac{n-1}{n}\left(1+\frac{2(n-1)}{\Delta_n}\right)>0,\\
B_n&=\frac{(n-1)(\Delta_n+2-2n)}{n\Delta_n}<0,\\
C_n&=\frac{n-2}{n}>0.
\end{split}
\]
The inequality \(B_n<0\) follows from \(\Delta_n<2n-2\) for \(n\ge3\). Equation (5) becomes
\[
P_n(t)=A_ne^{-\alpha_nt}+B_ne^{-\beta_nt}-C_ne^{-2t}.
\tag{8}
\]
Because \(0<\alpha_n<2<\beta_n\), the sign of its derivative equals the sign of
\[
F_n(t)=-\alpha_n A_n
-\beta_n B_n e^{-(\beta_n-\alpha_n)t}
+2C_n e^{-(2-\alpha_n)t}.
\tag{9}
\]
This is strictly decreasing for \(t\ge0\), tends to \(-\alpha_n A_n<0\), and has \(F_n(0)=P_n'(0)=n-3\). Hence for \(n=3\) the norm decreases strictly at every positive time, whereas for \(n\ge4\) there is exactly one positive derivative zero and it is a strict global maximum. The derivative at zero is the claimed \(n-3\).

**5. A strict factor-two bound and its sharpness.** For \(n\ge3\), \(B_n<0\), so
\[
P_n(t)<A_ne^{-\alpha_nt}-C_ne^{-2t}\quad(t\ge0).
\]
The right-hand side decreases strictly in time: indeed \(\alpha_nA_n>2C_n\). To verify the last inequality without approximation, use \(\Delta_n<n+1\) and \(\Delta_n<n+2/n\) to obtain
\[
\alpha_n A_n>\frac{(n-1)^2(3n-1)}{n^2(n+1)},
\]
whose difference from \(2C_n\) is
\[
\frac{(n-3)(n^2-2n+3)+8}{n^2(n+1)}>0.
\]
Therefore \(P_n(t)<A_n-C_n\). Since \(\Delta_n>n\),
\[
A_n-C_n=\frac{1+2(n-1)^2/\Delta_n}{n}
<2-\frac3n+\frac{2}{n^2}<2.
\]
For \(n=2\), \(E_2(t)\le1\). Thus every finite member and time has norm strictly less than two. Conversely, when \(t_n=(\log n)/n\), one has \(r_n(t_n)\to1\), \((n-2)q_n(t_n)\to1\), and \(e^{-2t_n}\to1\) as \(n\to\infty\), giving \(P_n(t_n)\to2\). This proves the sharp unattained family envelope.

## Verification
The self-contained script `verify.py` independently constructs the full \(2n\)-state Markov heat kernel via stochastic uniformization, then computes every edge transport cost by the graph's flow formula. It compares all edge classes with (5)--(6), checks their strict ranking, verifies the onset derivative numerically, and checks the limiting sequence. In the supplied run it tested \(n=2,\ldots,12\) at five positive times each, including all \(1{,}815\) individual edge evaluations. The infinite-family proof is analytic; finite numerical checks do not establish any universal assertion by themselves.

Run with `python3 verify.py` in the directory containing this document and the script. No third-party Python packages are required.

## Relationship to prior work
Ollivier's transport-curvature framework (2007) supplies the Wasserstein duality context. Barik--Pati--Sarma (2007), especially their Theorem 3.2, determine Laplacian spectral information for general corona graphs. That spectral theorem accounts for the two temporal eigenmodes, but it neither evaluates the graph-metric transport optimization nor identifies the maximizing edge and extremizing potentials. Chafaï--Joulin (2010) obtain gradient intertwinings for birth-death chains, and Münch--Wojciechowski (2017) establish general graph curvature/heat-gradient characterizations; neither inspected result states the sharp all-time norm on the complete-graph corona. In particular, the transport-flow identity, edge-type strict inequalities, \(n=4\) onset threshold, and unattained factor-two envelope are additional assertions not supplied by the cited spectral theorem alone. Related small-clique graphs with two distinct pendant leaves do not have the same family or all-edge norm problem.

## Limitations
The result assumes unit conductances, one pendant leaf at every core vertex, the unnormalized symmetric combinatorial Laplacian and the unit graph metric. It does not determine the corresponding norms for general base graphs in \(G\circ K_1\), weighted variants, normalized heat generators, or function-space norms other than the graph-Lipschitz seminorm. Source searches cannot rule out unindexed equivalent earlier derivations; no absolute priority or independent audit is claimed.
