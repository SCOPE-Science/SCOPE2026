# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The matrix-valued rational function is defined for every nonnegative step because its scalar Padé denominator has right-half-plane zeros, while the Markov generator has nonpositive-real-part spectrum. The quotient-ring reduction uniquely gives eight cyclic displacement coefficients. Their exact numerator factorizations and the positive common denominator reduce nonnegativity to four sign-sensitive factors. Each has exactly one positive root by Descartes' rule and the sign at zero/infinity. Rational bracketing separates the two active roots from the other two; hence positivity is equivalent to the one closed interval and the isolated zero step. The exact symbolic script verifies the polynomial identities and checks ten rational instances independently. Those instances are not substituted for the analytic global proof.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, with risk

The 2012 Zappavigna–Colaneri–Kirkland–Shorten manuscript proves positivity failure for diagonal Padé approximants using a nilpotent eight-state shift and Metzler variants; it contains a general cyclic-feedback template but no all-step classification for a conservative, uniformly cycling Markov generator. A published eight-state Markov theorem treats an absorbing pure-birth chain and its distinct window, rather than the cyclic closure. The 2013 absolute-monotonicity and 2015 monotonicity-perturbation treatments concern general method classes and modified schemes, not the exact unmodified eight-cycle endpoint. The common eighth-state small-step obstruction is acknowledged as prior art; the added result is the algebraic *entire* stochasticity window on an irreducible cycle. Unlocated equivalent cyclic formulas remain a risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Endpoint nonnegativity is a necessary condition for interpreting a rational step as a Markov transition. Closing the birth path into a cycle changes every rational endpoint coefficient and the two sharp admissible timesteps: this exact bound tests what happens when a recurrent rather than absorbing boundary condition is used. It provides a concrete nontrivial irreducible counterexample to the intuition that reducing the step always restores positivity, while also proving when positivity is recovered and later lost. The degree-six and degree-twelve thresholds come from a natural numerical-preservation question, not an arbitrary parameter slice.

## Limitations and related results

This is an exact eight-state endpoint theorem only; the result does not establish stage positivity, computational accuracy, unequal rates or general cycles. Although the principal source's feedback template already admits a cyclic architecture, the particular conservative specialization and complete admissible-step interval were not found in the inspected argument. Specialists could have derived an equivalent bound elsewhere.
