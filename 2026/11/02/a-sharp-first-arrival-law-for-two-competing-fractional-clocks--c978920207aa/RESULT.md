# A sharp first-arrival law for two competing fractional clocks

## Finding

Consider a nearest-neighbour symmetric random walk on the integers, started at zero, with resetting to zero and independent competing fractional waiting clocks as defined below. For all \(\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)\), with the two Mittag-Leffler clocks at the same time scale, the first-arrival time \(T\) to site \(+1\) satisfies

\[
\boxed{\mathbb P(T>t)\sim
\frac{2(1+\sqrt3)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\beta)}t^{-(\alpha+\beta)}}\qquad(t\to\infty).
\]

In particular \(\mathbb ET<\infty\) **if and only if** \(\alpha+\beta>1\). When it is finite,

\[
\boxed{\mathbb ET=2(1+\sqrt3)\int_0^\infty
E_\alpha(-t^\alpha)E_\beta(-t^\beta)\,dt.}
\]

Let \(\tau_i\) be the successive event holding times up to arrival, and let \(B_t\) be the event that at least one of them exceeds \(t\). Then \(\mathbb P(B_t\mid T>t)\to1\). Moreover, if the first such long event is a jump rather than a reset, its conditional probability given \(T>t\) tends to \(\alpha/(\alpha+\beta)\), and for a reset it tends to \(\beta/(\alpha+\beta)\). This identifies the type of the rare delay responsible for the long first-arrival tail.

The exact first-arrival Laplace transform is also given below. The decisive clock-race identity is \(\mathbb P(D_i<R_i)=1/2\) for *all*, possibly unequal, fractional indices \(\alpha,\beta\) at equal scales.

## Assumptions and scope

For \(0<\theta<1\), set \(E_\theta(z)=\sum_{k=0}^\infty z^k/\Gamma(1+\theta k)\). At each event, draw independent positive random variables \(D_i,R_i\) with respective survival functions

\[
\mathbb P(D_i>t)=E_\alpha(-t^\alpha),\qquad
\mathbb P(R_i>t)=E_\beta(-t^\beta),\quad t\ge0.
\]

All pairs and all jump signs are mutually independent. The event occurs after \(\tau_i=\min(D_i,R_i)\). If \(D_i<R_i\), the position changes by an independent fair \(\pm1\) step; otherwise it resets to zero. Both clocks are completely resampled after **every** event. The starting position is zero, and \(T\) is the physical time of the first event whose resulting position is \(+1\). Define \(N\) as the number of events up to and including that event; then \(T=\sum_{i=1}^N\tau_i\). These results concern a discrete site target and an exactly renewed *competing-clock* mechanism, not a continuous-position point-hitting convention or a global independent-reset process.

For \(u\ge0\), let

\[
a(u)=\mathbb E[e^{-u\tau_1}\mathbf1_{\{D_1<R_1\}}],\quad
b(u)=\mathbb E[e^{-u\tau_1}\mathbf1_{\{R_1<D_1\}}],\quad
h(u)=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-a(u)^2}}{a(u)}.
\]

The denominators are nonzero in the stated parameter range. With \(G(u)=\mathbb E[e^{-uT}]\), the exact transform is

\[
\boxed{G(u)=\frac{h(u)[1-a(u)]}{1-a(u)-b(u)[1-h(u)]}}.
\]

At \(u=0\), \(a(0)=b(0)=1/2\), \(h(0)=2-\sqrt3\), and \(G(0)=1\).

## Proof

**1. The race is fair despite different indices.** The classical exponential-mixture representation of the Mittag-Leffler survival function is

\[
E_\theta(-t^\theta)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-t e^y}
\frac{\sin(\pi\theta)}{2\pi[\cosh(\theta y)+\cos(\pi\theta)]}\,dy.
\]

This follows, for example, by Stieltjes inversion from the Laplace transform \(u^{\theta-1}/(1+u^\theta)\). The displayed mixing density \(g_\theta(y)\) is an even probability density. Hence each clock can be generated by drawing a random log-rate \(Y_\theta\sim g_\theta\), then an independent exponential waiting time of rate \(e^{Y_\theta}\). For independent indices, \(Y_\alpha-Y_\beta\) has a symmetric law. The conditional probability that the first exponential beats the second is \(\ell(Y_\alpha-Y_\beta)\), where \(\ell(z)=1/(1+e^{-z})\). Since \(\ell(z)+\ell(-z)=1\), its expectation is exactly \(1/2\). Thus each event is a jump with probability \(1/2\), or a reset with probability \(1/2\). The holding time and event type need **not** be independent; subsequent arguments respect their dependence.

**2. The embedded first-arrival count.** Between consecutive resets, let \(r\) be the probability of hitting \(+1\) from zero before the next reset. The killed fair walk has harmonic function \(r_x=r^{1-x}\) on \(x\le0\), with boundary \(r_1=1\); its recurrence is \(r_x=\tfrac14r_{x+1}+\tfrac14r_{x-1}\). The bounded root is \(r=2-\sqrt3\), satisfying \(r=\tfrac14(1+r^2)\). Fresh excursions are independent, so the expected number of resets before hitting \(+1\) is \((1-r)/r\). Since the number of resets before arrival has expectation \(\tfrac12\mathbb EN\) by optional counting of the independent event marks,

\[
\mathbb EN=2\frac{1-r}{r}=2(1+\sqrt3).
\]

For any prearrival state, the next two events produce a reset followed by a right jump with probability \(1/8\). Therefore \(\mathbb P(N>2m)\le(7/8)^m\). In particular, \(N\) has moments of every order. The counting identities \(\mathbb E N_{\rm jump}=\mathbb E N_{\rm reset}=\mathbb EN/2\) follow from the same predictable counting argument.

**3. Exact transform.** For \(u\ge0\), let \(F_x(u)\) denote the transform of the remaining first-arrival time, started at \(x\le0\), and set \(F_1(u)=1\). Conditioning on the next event gives

\[
F_x(u)=\frac{a(u)}2[F_{x+1}(u)+F_{x-1}(u)]+b(u)F_0(u).
\]

The bounded solution on the half-line is \(F_x=c+(1-c)h(u)^{1-x}\), where \(c=b(u)F_0(u)/(1-a(u))\). Substituting \(x=0\) gives the stated exact expression for \(G(u)=F_0(u)\), including the \(u=0\) limit.

**4. A one-big-event first-arrival tail.** From the standard Mittag-Leffler survival asymptotic,

\[
\mathbb P(\tau_i>t)=E_\alpha(-t^\alpha)E_\beta(-t^\beta)
\sim C_{\alpha,\beta}t^{-\delta},\quad
\delta=\alpha+\beta,\quad
C_{\alpha,\beta}=\frac1{\Gamma(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\beta)}.
\]

Differentiating the survival asymptotics (or using the corresponding Mittag-Leffler density asymptotic) and integrating the two competing-risk densities yields

\[
\mathbb P(\tau_i>t,D_i<R_i)\sim\frac{\alpha}{\delta}C_{\alpha,\beta}t^{-\delta},\qquad
\mathbb P(\tau_i>t,R_i<D_i)\sim\frac{\beta}{\delta}C_{\alpha,\beta}t^{-\delta}.
\]

Conditional on a finite sequence of event types and jump signs determining \(N=n\), its \(n\) durations are independent and each has the corresponding conditional regularly varying tail. The elementary fixed-\(n\) subexponential convolution principle gives the sum tail as the sum of those \(n\) tail constants. It applies for every \(\delta>0\). To interchange the path sum and the limit, use the elementary union estimate

\[
\mathbb P\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_i>t\mid\text{event types}\right)
\le n\max_i\mathbb P(\tau_i>t/n\mid\text{type}_i)
\le K n^{\delta+1}t^{-\delta},
\]

where \(K\) depends only on the two clock laws. The exponential tail of \(N\) supplies a summable dominating function. Taking expected conditional tail coefficients, using \(\mathbb EN_{\rm jump}=\mathbb EN_{\rm reset}=\mathbb EN/2\), and accounting for the conditional type probabilities \(1/2\), yields \(C_{\alpha,\beta}\mathbb EN\) as the coefficient. This proves the boxed tail formula. The same single-large-event argument shows that all duration contributions of order \(t\), except the one largest, are negligible in this tail sense, and gives the stated limiting jump and reset attribution fractions.

**5. The mean boundary.** Because \(\{N\ge i\}\) depends only on earlier marks and signs, it is independent of \(\tau_i\). Tonelli's theorem, also when expectations diverge, gives \(\mathbb ET=\mathbb EN\,\mathbb E\tau_1\). The integral for \(\mathbb E\tau_1\) is the product-survival integral in the finding. Its tail is comparable to \(t^{-\delta}\), so it converges precisely when \(\delta>1\). More precisely, at the other two regimes, integration of the first-arrival tail gives

\[
\mathbb E(T\wedge t)\sim
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{2(1+\sqrt3)C_{\alpha,\beta}}{1-\delta}\,t^{1-\delta},&\delta<1,\\[5pt]
2(1+\sqrt3)C_{\alpha,\beta}\log t,&\delta=1.
\end{cases}
\]

## Relationship to prior work

Li, Zhang and Liu (2026, arXiv:2609.09738v1) formulate renewal resetting through competing jump and reset clocks, deriving propagators and first-arrival expressions for continuous-space jump distributions. The present result treats a discrete nearest-neighbour target, resolves the dependence between clock winner and holding time, and determines a full first-arrival tail with its constant, marked delay attribution and a sharp finite-mean phase transition. Ascione and Caputo (2026, arXiv:2602.16521v1) analyze related Mittag-Leffler renewal queues and characterize their regimes through an unspecified race probability; they do not give the present reset-to-origin lattice first-arrival law.

Butt, Georgiou and Scalas (2023, Section 3.3) already establish that the minimum of two Mittag-Leffler waits has tail index \(\alpha+\beta\), and their Conjecture 5.1 asks whether equal clock scales imply a fair race for unequal indices. The spectral symmetry in Step 1 proves that identity, but its original placement in a 2023 queue context is acknowledged. Mainardi (2014) supplies the classical exponential-mixture representation; that representation itself is not claimed as new. Denisov, Foss and Korshunov (2010) supply general subexponential stopped-sum theory; the present proof instead explicitly accounts for correlations between event types and durations and identifies the large-delay mark. None of these known tools by itself states the complete target-specific result above.

## Limitations

The target must be the first discrete site \(+1\) reached by a nearest-neighbour jump from zero. The clocks have independent Mittag-Leffler laws with **equal time scales**, though their indices may differ. The theorem does not claim a universal first-passage law for continuous jump distributions, other reset rules, correlated clocks or nonrenewed waiting times. The finite verifier is not an infinite proof; independent review and definitive historical priority remain outstanding. Older general subexponential and spectral-mixture theorems might yield alternative, shorter derivations of parts of the result.
