# A sharp five-state visibility spectrum for antipodal boundary simplices in four dimensions

## Finding

Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^4\) be a centrally symmetric full-dimensional convex body, with \(0\) in its interior. Independently sample five points \(X_1,\ldots,X_5\in\partial K\), each from an antipodally symmetric probability measure charging no affine hyperplane. Let \(V\) be the number of facets of the almost surely nondegenerate simplex \(\operatorname{conv}\{X_1,\ldots,X_5\}\) whose supporting hyperplanes separate the origin strictly from the simplex.

There is a random integer \(D\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}\), determined by the five **unoriented** sample lines and not their independently random antipodal signs, for which the complete conditional distribution is
\[
\Pr(V=0\mid\mathcal L)=\frac1{16},\quad
\Pr(V=1\mid\mathcal L)=\frac5{16},\quad
\Pr(V=2\mid\mathcal L)=\frac{10-D}{16},\quad
\Pr(V=3\mid\mathcal L)=\frac D{16}.
\]
Every other value has probability zero. Consequently the unconditional distribution has the same form with \(D\) replaced by \(\mathbb E[D]\), and
\[
0\le\Pr(V=3)\le\tfrac14,\qquad
\tfrac{25}{16}\le\mathbb E[V]\le\tfrac{29}{16}.
\]
Both bounds are sharp. In fact each of the five integer values of \(D\) can be made almost surely constant by a suitable centrally symmetric convex body and five independent antipodally symmetric, hyperplane-null boundary laws.

## Assumptions and scope

An antipodally symmetric law satisfies \(\mu_i(A)=\mu_i(-A)\) for each Borel set. The five laws can differ. Each gives zero mass to every affine hyperplane, ensuring that every signed choice of the five sample points is affinely independent almost surely, and that any four sample position vectors are linearly independent. The conditional sigma-field \(\mathcal L\) is generated by the five unoriented lines \(\mathbb R X_i\). A facet is visible precisely when the origin lies in the strict opposite halfspace from the remaining simplex vertex. Statements concern the geometry in ambient dimension exactly four and do not assume uniform surface-area sampling.

For any representatives \(Y_i\) of those lines, the unique linear dependence, up to a nonzero scalar, is \(\sum_{i=1}^5 a_iY_i=0\). Define \(w_i=|a_i|\) and \(W=\sum_iw_i\), and set
\[
D=\#\big\{\{i,j\}:1\le i<j\le5,\quad 2(w_i+w_j)>W\big\}.
\]
The definition is invariant under representative signs and a common rescaling. The weights are strictly positive. Almost surely \(2w_i<W\) for every index and \(2(w_i+w_j)\ne W\) for every pair.

## Proof

**General position and weight restriction.** Since each of the independent laws charges no affine hyperplane, all \(2^5\) signed configurations are affine-independent almost surely. The same assumption shows that any four position vectors are linearly independent, so the dependence above exists uniquely with all coefficients nonzero. Let \(\|\cdot\|_K\) denote the Minkowski gauge of the centrally symmetric convex body \(K\); for every sampled boundary point \(\|Y_i\|_K=1\). The dependence and the triangle inequality imply
\[
w_i\le\sum_{j\ne i}w_j,
\]
hence \(2w_i\le W\). Equality would give an affine dependence of the signed configuration obtained by reversing the sign of the \(i\)-th dependence term, contradicting the simultaneous affine general position. Likewise equality \(2(w_i+w_j)=W\) would yield an affine dependence of another signed configuration. Therefore all inequalities stated in the scope are strict almost surely.

**Sign enumeration.** Antipodal symmetry and independence imply that, conditional on \(\mathcal L\), the five choices of antipodal signs are independent fair bits. Absorb the fixed signs of the coefficients \(a_i\) into these bits. If the signed vertices are \(Z_i=\varepsilon_iY_i\), their origin-barycentric coordinates are
\[
\lambda_i=\frac{\varepsilon_i a_i}{\sum_{j=1}^5\varepsilon_j a_j}.
\]
The denominator cannot vanish by signed affine general position. The facet opposite vertex \(i\) is visible if and only if \(\lambda_i<0\). The two configurations with all signed dependence terms of one sign therefore give \(V=0\). There are ten configurations with exactly one minority term, and the strict singleton weight inequality forces \(V=1\). For a specified two-element minority subset there are two complementary sign configurations: they both give \(V=3\) if the pair's total weight exceeds \(W/2\), and both give \(V=2\) otherwise. There are ten unordered pairs. Thus the counts across all thirty-two signs are respectively \(2,10,20-2D,2D\) for \(V=0,1,2,3\), yielding the conditional law.

**The sharp bound \(D\le4\).** No two disjoint pairs can both have total weight strictly greater than \(W/2\). Consequently the graph on five indices whose edges are the dominant pairs is an intersecting family of two-element subsets. Every such family has at most four edges: if all edges share one vertex this is immediate; otherwise the family consists of the three edges of a triangle. Therefore \(0\le D\le4\). Summation over \(V\) gives the stated mean and probability bounds.

**All five regimes are attainable.** Use, respectively, the integer dependence-weight vectors
\[
(2,2,2,2,2),\quad(1,1,1,4,4),\quad(1,1,2,3,4),\quad
(1,1,3,3,3),\quad(1,1,1,3,5).
\]
Their strict dominant-pair counts are exactly \(D=0,1,2,3,4\), respectively; each satisfies \(2\max_iw_i<W\), and none has a pair totaling \(W/2\). For any such vector put \(v_i=e_i\in\mathbb R^4\) for \(i=1,2,3,4\), and \(v_5=-\sum_{i=1}^4(w_i/w_5)e_i\). Then \(\sum_iw_iv_i=0\) is the unique linear dependence. Let \(K_0=\operatorname{conv}\{\pm v_1,\ldots,\pm v_5\}\). Every \(\pm v_i\) is an exposed vertex: the unique dependence forces every representation of \(v_i\) by signed other vertices to have total absolute coefficient \((W-w_i)/w_i>1\), ruling out a convex representation (also allowing \(-v_i\) does not change the conclusion). This polytope is centrally symmetric and full-dimensional.

For \(\varepsilon>0\), form \(K_\varepsilon=K_0+\varepsilon B^4\). At each \(v_i\), the interior of its normal cone supplies an open three-dimensional spherical boundary patch of points \(v_i+\varepsilon u\) in \(\partial K_\varepsilon\). Take for the \(i\)-th law a smooth surface-area probability density on a sufficiently small such patch and its antipode, with masses \(1/2\) each. These independent laws are antipodally symmetric and give every affine hyperplane probability zero. For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\) and patches, the unique dependence weights of every choice of one point from each patch pair remain arbitrarily close to the prescribed weights. The strict inequalities determining \(D\) persist. Each desired \(D\) is therefore almost surely constant, establishing sharpness of all five regimes and the endpoint probability and mean bounds.

## Verification

The self-contained `verify.py` enumerates all \(32\) sign patterns for each of the five integer witnesses. It solves the full five-by-five affine-barycentric system in exact rational arithmetic, independently checks the closed barycentric formula and the count of negative coordinates, confirms every dominant-pair comparison, and tests the rational certificate of vertex extremality. Running `python verify.py` prints `ALL_EXACT_VISIBILITY_CHECKS_PASSED`. This finite calculation certifies the five explicit base configurations; the general arbitrary-body statement and curved-patch realization rely on the analytic arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work

Wendel's origin-containment theorem supplies the universal probability \(\Pr(V=0)=1/16\) but does not give the other visibility counts. Edelsbrunner and Nikitenko (2017) analyze random inscribed spherical polytopes and explicitly use visible facets relative to a Euclidean center and sign/reflection mechanisms. Such mechanisms, including barycentric visibility, are established ingredients and are not asserted as new here. Bonnet and O'Reilly (2019 preprint) study signed facet heights of uniformly spherical random polytopes, and Kabluchko (2019 preprint) studies expected angles and face numbers for related random simplices. The assessed contribution here is the **sharp four-dimensional, arbitrarily anisotropic and nonidentically sampled boundary-law classification** by a dominant-pair invariant, its five possible exact conditional spectra, and constructions attaining each one. The literature checks do not establish definitive historical priority.

## Limitations

The unconditional law is a one-parameter family depending on \(\mathbb E[D]\); no formula for that expectation is asserted for uniform sampling on a specified smooth body. Higher dimensions, interior sampling, and non-antipodal laws are not covered. The extremal constructions use specially chosen smooth-patch-supported laws, not uniform area on the whole boundary. No independent mathematical audit has been performed; historical-priority uncertainty remains.
