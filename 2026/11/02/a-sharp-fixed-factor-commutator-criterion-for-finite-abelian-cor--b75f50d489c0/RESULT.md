# A sharp fixed-factor commutator criterion for finite abelian core-quandle algebras

## Finding

**Theorem (universal fixed-factor commutators and their obstruction).** Let \(A\) be any finite abelian group, written additively, and let \(K\) be any nonzero commutative unital ring. Form the core quandle \(Q=\operatorname{Core}(A)\), with \(x*y=2y-x\), and its \(K\)-algebra \(B=K[Q]\), with basis \(e_x\) indexed by \(x\in A\). Write \([u,v]=u*v-v*u\), and denote by \(B'\) the nonunital \(K\)-subalgebra generated by the commutators. Let \(D:B\longrightarrow B\) be the \(K\)-linear map \(D(u)=[u,e_0]\).

Decompose \(A=A_2\oplus O\) into its two-primary component and odd-order component. Let \(c(O)\) be the number of orbits of the permutation \(o\mapsto-2o\) of \(O\), and set \(g=|A/3A|\). Then there are \(K\)-module isomorphisms
\[
B'\cong K^{\,|A|-g},\qquad \operatorname{im}D\cong K^{\,|A|-c(O)},\qquad B'/\operatorname{im}D\cong K^{\,c(O)-g}.
\]
In fact, \(B'\) is precisely the kernel of the quotient-coefficient-sum map \(K[A]\to K[A/3A]\), and \(\operatorname{im}D\) is precisely the module of vectors whose coefficient sum vanishes on every weak component of the directed graph \(x\to-2x\) on \(A\).

**Sharp criterion.** Every element of \(B'\) is a *single commutator with the same fixed second argument* \(e_0\) if and only if the odd component \(O\) has exponent dividing \(3\), equivalently
\[
A\cong P\oplus(C_3)^r\quad\text{for a finite abelian two-group }P\text{ and }r\ge0.
\]
When \(P\ne0\), this gives commutator width exactly \(1\) over **every** nonzero commutative unital coefficient ring. When \(P=0\), the core quandle is commutative and its commutator width is \(0\).

There is an explicit arithmetic formula for the obstruction. If \(O\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s} C_{m_i}\) with odd \(m_i\), let \(T\) be the multiplicative order of \(-2\) modulo the exponent of \(O\), taking \(T=1\) if \(O=0\). Burnside's formula gives
\[
c(O)=\frac1T\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\prod_{i=1}^{s}\gcd\bigl(m_i,(-2)^t-1\bigr).
\]
For a cyclic group \(A=C_n\) with \(n=2^a m\) and odd \(m\), the equivalent divisor formula is
\[
c(O)=\sum_{d\mid m}\frac{\varphi(d)}{\operatorname{ord}_d(-2)},
\]
where the denominator is defined to equal \(1\) at \(d=1\). Thus \(\operatorname{im}D=B'\) for \(C_n\) exactly when \(m\in\{1,3\}\).

## Assumptions and scope

The coefficient ring \(K\) is arbitrary except for commutativity, a unit, and nonzeroness. No assumption on its characteristic, reducedness, integrality, or finiteness is made. The group \(A\) is finite abelian, possibly trivial. The quandle algebra has no imposed associative multiplication; a commutator means \(u*v-v*u\), where \(*\) is its bilinear quandle multiplication. The commutator width is the maximum minimal number of such elementary commutators whose sum gives an element of the commutator-generated subalgebra, with width zero for that subalgebra \(\{0\}\).

The equivalence concerns a **fixed** commutator factor \(e_0\); failure of that stronger property does not imply that the unrestricted commutator width exceeds one. The rank and quotient assertions hold as free-module decompositions even over rings with zero divisors.

## Proof

**1. Determine the full commutator subalgebra.** For basis elements,
\[
[e_x,e_y]=e_{2y-x}-e_{2x-y}.
\]
The difference of their indices is \(3(y-x)\), so each basis commutator is supported in one coset of \(3A\) with coefficient sum zero. Conversely, if \(a-b=3d\), choose \(x=a-2d\), \(y=a-d\); the displayed commutator is \(e_a-e_b\). Consequently the span of all commutators is exactly
\[
W=\left\{\sum_{x\in A}k_xe_x:\ \sum_{x\in C}k_x=0\ \text{for each coset }C\in A/3A\right\}.
\]
This module is closed under left and right quandle multiplication: multiplying a difference \(e_a-e_b\) with \(a-b\in3A\) on either side by a basis element yields another difference of indices lying in the same coset of \(3A\). Hence \(B'=W\). Choosing one representative of each coset proves \(B'\cong K^{|A|-g}\). The subalgebra identification agrees with the general ideal theorem of Zhang; its use here is as a recovered structural premise, not a novelty claim.

**2. The fixed-factor map is a graph incidence map.** Put \(\sigma(x)=-2x\). Reindexing gives
\[
D(e_{-x})=e_x-e_{\sigma(x)}.
\]
For any finite undirected graph, the edge differences generate precisely the vectors of total coefficient zero on each connected component, over any commutative ring: a spanning tree joins each vertex to a chosen root, yielding all vertex-root differences by telescoping. Applied to the functional graph \(x\to\sigma(x)\), this identifies \(\operatorname{im}D\) and proves \(\operatorname{coker}D\cong K^{c}\), where \(c\) is its number of components.

**3. Count the functional components.** On the two-primary summand \(A_2\), repeated multiplication by \(-2\) reaches zero; on the odd-order summand \(O\), multiplication by \(-2\) is a permutation. Every functional component in \(A_2\oplus O\) therefore has exactly one eventual cycle, determined by a permutation orbit on \(O\). Thus \(c=c(O)\). The displayed formulas follow by Burnside's lemma and the cyclic decomposition of \(O\); in the cyclic case, elements of order \(d\) form \(\varphi(d)/\operatorname{ord}_d(-2)\) cycles.

**4. Compare the two partitions.** Since \(\sigma(x)-x=-3x\in3A\), each functional component lies in one coset of \(3A\). Let \(s:K^{c(O)}\to K^g\) sum the component coordinates over each coset. The coefficient-sum maps form a commuting composition: the coset-sum map on \(B\) equals \(s\) applied to the component-sum map. Its first kernel is \(\operatorname{im}D\), and its composite kernel is \(B'\), so
\[
B'/\operatorname{im}D\cong\ker(s)\cong K^{c(O)-g}.
\]
Each coset contains at least one functional component, which makes the final free decomposition valid over arbitrary \(K\).

**5. Prove the sharp criterion and construct a primitive.** Because \(3\) acts invertibly on \(A_2\), the cosets of \(3A\) correspond to cosets of \(3O\) in \(O\). If \(3O=0\), then \(\sigma\) is the identity on \(O\). Its orbits are the singleton points of \(O\), so \(c(O)=|O|=g\), and \(D\) is onto \(B'\).

For an explicit solution, let \(2^a\) annihilate \(A_2\) (take \(a=0\) for \(A_2=0\)). If \(3O=0\), then \(r_x=\sigma^a(x)\) lies in \(O\) and is fixed by \(\sigma\). For arbitrary \(w=\sum_x k_xe_x\in B'\), the element
\[
v=\sum_{x\in A}k_x\sum_{t=0}^{a-1} e_{-\sigma^t(x)}
\]
satisfies \(D(v)=w\). Indeed, the inner telescoping commutator equals \(e_x-e_{r_x}\), and the sum of \(k_x\) over each fiber \(\{x:r_x=r\}\), which is a coset of \(3A\), is zero.

Conversely, if \(3O\ne0\), the zero coset of \(O/3O\) contains both zero and a nonzero element. The zero element is a fixed orbit of \(\sigma\), disjoint from every nonzero orbit, so this coset contains at least two components. Consequently \(c(O)>g\), and \(B'/\operatorname{im}D\cong K^{c(O)-g}\ne0\) because \(K\ne0\). Thus \(D\) is not surjective. This proves necessity as well as sufficiency.

Finally, \(B'=0\) precisely when \(3A=0\), which is equivalent to \(A_2=0\) and \(O\) having exponent at most \(3\). Otherwise, in the surjective family, nonzero commutators exist and the commutator width is exactly one.

## Verification

Two standalone Python 3 programs included in the accompanying package use exact integer arithmetic and no external dependencies. `verify_cyclic.py` independently constructs basis commutators and the \(x\mapsto-2x\) incidence graph for all orders \(1\le n\le144\), compares the orbit-count formula, checks the sharp criterion, and constructs explicit integral telescoping primitives. It also checks coefficient reductions modulo \(2,3,5,7\). `verify_abelian.py` performs the same structural checks on 29 cyclic and noncyclic finite abelian groups and directly verifies the commutator identity for the proposed primitives over the integers. Both recorded outputs end in `VERIFY_OK`.

The tests include \(A=C_9\), where \(g=3\), \(c(O)=5\), and the obstruction module has free rank \(2\); and \(A=C_2\oplus C_3\oplus C_3\), where \(g=c(O)=9\) and every commutator-generated element has the displayed fixed-factor primitive. The code checks finite instances only; the universal conclusions are proved algebraically above.

## Relationship to prior work

Sangare and Ta (arXiv:2608.01550, Section 5) formulate the commutator-width problem for dihedral quandle algebras and compute numerous finite-field cases, including characteristic two for dihedral orders through \(21\), excluding the commutative order \(3\). They also cite the previously known unrestricted width-one result for order \(4\). The current theorem supplies a coefficient-ring-independent infinite family, including every finite abelian two-group and every product with an elementary abelian three-group, and provides a universal **fixed second factor** rather than merely an unrestricted width-one statement.

Zhang (arXiv:2610.08204, Theorem 2.3) already identifies the commutator-generated subalgebra of all Alexander quandle rings as a subgroup ideal. The formula \(B'=\ker(K[A]\to K[A/3A])\) is recovered here, not claimed as new. The novel comparison is the image of the *single* linear map \(D=[-,e_0]\), its orbit-counted free obstruction, and the sharp classification of when that stronger map is surjective.

Bardakov and Sangare (arXiv:2610.18676, Theorem 5.6) identify the commutativization of the core quandle \(\operatorname{Core}(G)\); this governs the coset quotient but not the fixed-factor image, its exact module rank, or the commutator-width conclusion. These implications were compared rather than relying on title searches alone.

## Limitations

The theorem determines fixed-basis-factor surjectivity, not the unrestricted commutator width for groups outside the listed family. For example, computations over small finite fields can have unrestricted width one even when the fixed-factor map is not onto. It concerns finite abelian core quandles, not arbitrary nonabelian core quandles or arbitrary Alexander parameters. Historical priority beyond the inspected primary literature and the available mathematical-record search remains unconfirmed. A relevant recent monograph on Yang–Baxter algebra and quandles could not be inspected in full; its possible implicit coverage is a residual risk. No external mathematical certification is claimed.
