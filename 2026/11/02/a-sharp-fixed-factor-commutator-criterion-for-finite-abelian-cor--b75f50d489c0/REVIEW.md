# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is proved without field or characteristic restrictions. Basis commutators are exactly differences of vectors in a common coset of \(3A\), giving the commutator-generated subalgebra. The fixed-factor map is an integer graph incidence map, so its image and free cokernel can be proved with a spanning forest even over a coefficient ring with zero divisors. The only functional cycles arise from multiplication by \(-2\) on the odd-primary component. Comparing the component partition with the cosets of \(3A\) gives the exact free obstruction rank. If \(3O=0\), telescoping supplies an explicit right inverse on the commutator module; if \(3O\ne0\), the zero coset necessarily has multiple components, proving non-surjectivity. The separate programs checked all cyclic orders through \(144\) and 29 finite abelian groups with direct integer arithmetic. Those checks are finite corroboration, not the general proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Sangare and Ta, arXiv:2608.01550, Section 5, compute selected finite-field widths and state an open commutator-width problem. Their Theorem 5.4 and the earlier order-four theorem cover only finite cases of the present unlimited family. Zhang, arXiv:2610.08204, Theorem 2.3, already identifies the entire commutator subalgebra for Alexander quandles; the present result does not re-label this as novel, but determines a strictly finer fixed-factor image, its exact free quotient, and the sharp coefficient-independent classification. Bardakov and Sangare, arXiv:2610.18676, Theorem 5.6, identify core-quandle commutativizations, not commutator length or fixed-factor surjectivity. Searches of accessible mathematical records and prior related findings did not recover the stronger statement. Remaining risk: older or book-length quandle-ring results could imply the graph-image criterion without naming it; search noncoverage is not priority proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A currently studied finite-field commutator-width problem receives an explicit, constructive result for an infinite and structurally natural class over *all* commutative coefficient rings. Beyond existence of a commutator representation, the theorem identifies a universal factor and a sharp exact obstruction for every finite abelian core quandle. It explains why the fixed-factor construction works precisely on the two-primary-by-exponent-three class, including noncyclic groups, and quantifies its failure outside that class. These are meaningful module and width invariants, not arbitrary small-instance computations. The distinction between the sharp fixed-factor criterion and unrestricted width is stated throughout.

## Closest sources and limitations

The commutator module itself and the core-quandle commutativization are prior results. The new work concerns the incidence-map image and full obstruction classification. This is not a full classification of unrestricted commutator widths outside the surjective family. The preprint literature was materially inspected in the cited relevant sections; no exhaustive historical priority claim is made. A recent broader monograph remains a possible source of implicit coverage.
