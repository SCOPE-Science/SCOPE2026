For an exactly orthogonal \(2\times2\) linear system, squared-entry-sampled Gauss–Seidel with relaxation \(1/2\) converges almost surely and has squared error decaying by \(3/4\) each step, yet its fourth error moment grows exponentially below the sharp entry-ratio threshold \(r_* = \sqrt{(47-2\sqrt{546})/5}\). This separates mean-square reliability from higher-moment reliability without any matrix conditioning pathology.
