# A sharp function-power boundary for unoversampled Gaussian funNyström on spectral spikes

## Finding

For every fixed integer \(m\ge 1\), integer \(q\ge 1\), and real \(0<\beta\le 1\), consider the positive-definite spectral spike \(A_L=\operatorname{diag}(L,I_m)\) as \(L\to\infty\). Generate the rank-one, zero-oversampling Gaussian funNyström approximation specified below. Its expected **nuclear-norm matrix-function error**, above the optimal rank-one error \(m\), obeys the sharp equivalent

\[
\mathbb E\left\|A_L^\beta-\widehat A_{L,q}^\beta\right\|_*
-m\sim C_{m,\beta}L^{\beta-q+1/2},\qquad
C_{m,\beta}=\frac{2\Gamma((m+1)/2)\Gamma(\beta+1/2)}{\Gamma(m/2)\Gamma(\beta)}>0.
\]

For the unoversampled **single-pass** choice \(q=1\), the expected excess tends to zero when \(\beta<1/2\), tends to the positive constant \(C_{m,1/2}\) when \(\beta=1/2\), and diverges when \(\beta>1/2\). For \(q\ge2\) it tends to zero for every \(0<\beta\le1\). At the single-pass square-root boundary, the error converges in probability to the optimal \(m\), while its expectation converges to \(m+C_{m,1/2}\). In two matrix dimensions \(m=1\), this persistent excess equals \(2/\pi\).

## Assumptions and scope

All matrices and Gaussian random variables are **real**, all computations are in exact arithmetic, and \(m,q,\beta\) are fixed independently of \(L\). The spectral aspect ratio \(L>1\) tends to infinity. Draw one standard Gaussian sketch \(g=(G,g_1,\ldots,g_m)^\mathsf T\in\mathbb R^{m+1}\); the entries are independent \(N(0,1)\). Use the subspace-iteration power parameter \(q\) and precisely one sketch column, without oversampling:

\[
\widehat A_{L,q}=
\frac{A_L^qgg^\mathsf T A_L^q}{g^\mathsf T A_L^{2q-1}g}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\widehat F_{L,q,\beta}=\widehat A_{L,q}^\beta.
\]

The matrix \(\widehat A_{L,q}\) is well-defined almost surely. The target is \(A_L^\beta\), with function \(f(t)=t^\beta\); \(f(0)=0\). We measure the actual nuclear norm \(\|A_L^\beta-\widehat F_{L,q,\beta}\|_*\), not the Frobenius error or trace of \(\widehat A_{L,q}\) itself. Its best possible rank-one nuclear-norm error is \(m\). This theorem concerns the distribution generated by a single independent Gaussian sketch, not screened, orthogonalized-across-multiple-sketch, or oversampled variants.

## Proof

**Positive-semidefinite ordering and scalar reduction.** Put \(B=A_L^{q-1/2}g\). Its one-dimensional orthogonal projector is \(P_B=B(B^\mathsf TB)^{-1}B^\mathsf T\). Then \(\widehat A_{L,q}=A_L^{1/2}P_BA_L^{1/2}\), so \(0\preceq\widehat A_{L,q}\preceq A_L\). As \(0<\beta\le1\), the Löwner--Heinz inequality gives \(0\preceq\widehat A_{L,q}^\beta\preceq A_L^\beta\). Thus the nuclear norm of their difference equals its trace. Let \(R=\sum_{i=1}^m g_i^2\), independent of \(G^2\). The sole nonzero eigenvalue of \(\widehat A_{L,q}\) is

\[
\mu_L=\frac{L^{2q}G^2+R}{L^{2q-1}G^2+R}.
\]

Therefore the exact error is \(E_{L,q,\beta}=m+L^\beta-\mu_L^\beta\); in particular the excess is nonnegative. The best rank-one approximation to \(A_L^\beta\) retains its top eigenvalue \(L^\beta\), leaving \(m\) unit eigenvalues, hence the benchmark error is precisely \(m\).

**Gaussian ratio density.** Because \(G^2\sim\chi_1^2\) and \(R\sim\chi_m^2\) are independent, the positive ratio \(r=G^2/R\) has beta-prime density

\[
p_m(r)=\frac{r^{-1/2}(1+r)^{-(m+1)/2}}{B(1/2,m/2)},\qquad r>0.
\]

Set \(a=L^{q-1/2}\), \(y=a\sqrt r\). Then \(\mu_L/L=(y^2+L^{-1})/(1+y^2)\) and \(y\) has density \(2a^{-1}B(1/2,m/2)^{-1}(1+y^2/a^2)^{-(m+1)/2}\). Substitution yields the **exact finite-parameter integral**

\[
\frac{\mathbb E(E_{L,q,\beta}-m)}{L^{\beta-q+1/2}}=
\frac{2}{B(1/2,m/2)}\int_0^\infty
\left[1-\left(\frac{y^2+L^{-1}}{1+y^2}\right)^\beta\right]
\left(1+\frac{y^2}{L^{2q-1}}\right)^{-(m+1)/2}\,dy.
\]

**Dominated-convergence asymptotics.** For \(L>1\), each bracket lies between zero and \(h_\beta(y)=1-(y^2/(1+y^2))^\beta\), and the final factor is at most one. The function \(h_\beta\) is integrable: it is bounded by one near zero and \(O_\beta(y^{-2})\) as \(y\to\infty\). For each fixed \(y>0\), the integrand tends to \(h_\beta(y)\), since \(q\ge1\). Dominated convergence gives the limit. Integration by parts, with vanishing boundary terms, gives

\[
\int_0^\infty h_\beta(y)\,dy
=2\beta\int_0^\infty\frac{y^{2\beta}}{(1+y^2)^{\beta+1}}\,dy
=\beta B(\beta+1/2,1/2)
=\frac{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(\beta+1/2)}{\Gamma(\beta)}.
\]

Using \(B(1/2,m/2)=\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(m/2)/\Gamma((m+1)/2)\) proves the stated positive leading constant. The exponent \(\beta-q+1/2\) yields exactly the three single-pass regimes and the two-or-more-pass decay assertion.

**A typical-versus-mean boundary.** At \(q=1\), fix a sample with \(G\ne0\) and \(R>0\), an almost-sure event. The eigenvalue loss satisfies \(L-\mu_L=(L-1)R/(LG^2+R)\to R/G^2\). For \(0<\beta<1\), the mean-value inequality

\[
0\le L^\beta-\mu_L^\beta\le
\beta\mu_L^{\beta-1}(L-\mu_L)
\]

is valid by concavity, but as written its right-hand side does not immediately yield decay unless \(\mu_L\to\infty\). Here \(\mu_L\sim L\), so the right-hand side tends to zero. In particular at \(\beta=1/2\) the error converges almost surely (under a fixed-sketch coupling) to \(m\) although its expectation tends to \(m+C_{m,1/2}\). This is failure of uniform integrability, not a claim that individual error samples diverge.

## Verification

The accompanying executable `verify.py` and `requirements.txt` independently assemble real rank-one Gaussian Nyström matrices and compare their sole nonzero eigenvalue to the scalar formula. They also evaluate the exact positive integral by adaptive numerical quadrature, test special exact \(\beta=1\) expectation identities for \(m=1\) and \(m=3\), and check normalized finite-\(L\) values against the analytical limit \(C_{m,\beta}\). The program prints `VERIFY_OK 462 independent matrix, exact-integral and asymptotic-constant checks` when successful. These computations are corroborations; the claimed infinite-\(L\) limits follow from the integrable domination and beta-integral proof above, not from numerical extrapolation.

## Relationship to prior work

Persson and Kressner, arXiv:2209.11023, introduce and analyze funNyström; the present construction uses their subspace-iteration/Nyström algorithm without modification. Their expected nuclear-error estimates require at least two columns of Gaussian oversampling and therefore do **not directly address** a single sketch column with zero oversampling. Persson, Meyer and Musco, arXiv:2311.14023, prove that good Nyström approximation of \(A\) transfers to good approximation of operator-monotone functions \(f(A)\), and their Gaussian subspace-iteration expectation corollary again assumes oversampling \(p\ge2\). That transfer theorem is consistent with, but does not state, the **sharp no-oversampling constant and power-function transition** above. Frangella, Tropp and Udell, arXiv:2110.02820, study Nyström sketches in preconditioning and related effective-dimension regimes; these are not an exact function-power one-column nuclear-error limit. The rank-one eigenvalue identity, beta-prime distribution, and dominated convergence are classical tools; no novelty is claimed for those ingredients alone.

## Limitations

The sharp asymptotic is for fixed \(m,q,\beta\), real Gaussian one-column sketches, a diagonal single-spike spectrum, and exact arithmetic. It does not establish a comparable boundary when dimension grows with \(L\), for arbitrary spectral distributions, oversampled sketches, non-Gaussian probes, finite precision, or non-power matrix functions. Lack of a covering result in searched sources does not prove priority; unindexed or earlier equivalent analyses may exist. The result has undergone only self-review and no independent audit.
