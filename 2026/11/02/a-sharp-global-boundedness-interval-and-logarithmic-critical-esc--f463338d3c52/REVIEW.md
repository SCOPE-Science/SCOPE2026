# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The proof treats every \(a>0\), every real \(b\), every finite initial condition and both nonhyperbolic endpoints. Boundedness of \(f_a\) controls the mean coordinate and supplies sharp linear comparison outside the critical endpoints. The sign of its Gaussian-cubic tail closes the \(b=-1/2\) absorbing boundary and forces strict escape at \(b=1/2\). For every escaping critical orbit, the mean coordinate contracts exponentially and the half-difference has a scalar asymptotic. The reciprocal clock \(e^r/(2ar^2)\) has consecutive differences tending to one, yielding the coefficient \(\log(2a)\) without numeric extrapolation. The included script passed finite consistency tests; no finite sampling is treated as proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence
The defining 2023 full text and related source records were compared by the *actual map and implication*. The source studies critical curves and numerical basin/synchronization regimes, not an all-orbit sharp absorbing interval or the critical two-log escape law. Its earlier nonlinear-output-coupled source and the separate coupled sine-map work do not directly imply the stated theorem. Standard bounded-forcing arguments cover the strict interior/exterior only. The 2022 precursor and the 1996 predecessor were not inspected in full; a general earlier theorem or unindexed analysis remains a publication-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a global phase boundary for a named noninvertible chaotic map, not just another local fixed-point test: it distinguishes an inclusive absorbing endpoint from an excluding escaping endpoint, and quantifies critical escape on every escaping orbit, including nonsymmetric initial conditions. The result supplies an analytic boundary for deciding when numerical basin plots omit distant trajectories, and its \(\log\log n\) correction describes a subtle neutral growth mechanism. It does not classify bounded attractors or full basin partitions.
