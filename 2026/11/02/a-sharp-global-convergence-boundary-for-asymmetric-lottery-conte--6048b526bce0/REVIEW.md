# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** For the precise positive-effort two-player model, the best-response map preserves the unit interval when \(4/25<c\leq1\). The difference of the four best-response square equations forces \(2cS^2-(1+c)S+1=0\) for each primitive two-cycle. Its discriminant and the strict size inequality rule out every primitive period-two point at \(c\geq3-2\sqrt2\); Coppel's published convergence theorem then yields global convergence to the unique positive fixed point. The zero fixed point cannot attract positive trajectories. Below the threshold a sign argument for the second iterate ensures existence, and one admissible quadratic root plus the difference identities ensure uniqueness. Exact symbolic checks of all key algebraic identities and a rational-parameter cycle passed. Coppel's theorem is invoked in its standard compact-interval form rather than re-proved; numerical checks are not used as infinite proofs.

**Originality — PASS, with specified bibliographic risk.** Ghosh and Goldberg's accessible full text explicitly leaves heterogeneity-dependent convergence open and supplies nonconvergent examples only through \(c\leq4/25\). An accessible preceding calculation does prove positive-cycle existence in part of the present range and reports the local multiplier; those statements do *not* imply the asserted global convergence from every interior seed above the critical ratio or the complete count of positive primitive two-cycles below it. The novel implication is the algebraic absence/uniqueness of all period-two points combined with the global interval theorem. A later convex-cost follow-up was accessible only at abstract level; it remains a genuine residual overlap risk, not evidence of noncoverage.

**Value — PASS.** The originating study explicitly asks for a precise homogeneity threshold. The theorem gives an if-and-only-if answer for all positive smooth alternating trajectories, including the neutral multiplier boundary, and eliminates the possibility of hidden additional two-cycles in the instability regime. It distinguishes a *global* convergence guarantee from a merely locally stable equilibrium, and is not a parameter substitution or reproduction of the known cycling example.

**Closest literature and limits.** Ghosh--Goldberg (2023) provides the two-player response rules, zero-opponent convention, previously known cycling regime and open question. Coppel (1955) is a general convergence theorem but does not provide the contest-specific no-cycle criterion. Ewerhart (2017) is homogeneous; Ghosh (2023) on convex costs was not available in full. Limitations: only \(4/25<c\leq1\), interior positive starts, two agents and strict alternating exact responses; no higher-period classification below threshold and no rates at the critical boundary.
