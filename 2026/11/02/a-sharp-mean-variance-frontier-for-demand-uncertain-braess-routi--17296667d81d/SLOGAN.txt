For the classical four-node Braess network with demand-revealed Wardrop routing, a strictly positive expected slowdown from opening the shortcut is forced by the mean \(m\in(2/3,2)\) and variance \(v\) alone exactly when \(v<(m-2/3)(2-m)\). At the sharp boundary a unique two-demand law has zero expected effect, while above it two demand distributions can have identical mean and variance yet give opposite infrastructure decisions.
