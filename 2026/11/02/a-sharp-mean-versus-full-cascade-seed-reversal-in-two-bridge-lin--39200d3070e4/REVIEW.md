# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The domain is the specified six-vertex, seven-edge graph, every one of its fifteen two-seed sets, independently drawn fixed thresholds one/two at the four nonseed vertices, and every real parameter \(0<p<1\). The exact integer coefficients in the proof are obtained by exhausting four representative classes of sixteen threshold assignments. Graph symmetries exhaust all fifteen seed pairs, with multiplicities six, four, four, one. The independent closure characterization and symbolic equalities were replayed successfully from the actual packaged checker. Factorizations strictly eliminate the other types and give the unique positive interior mean crossover; three other factorizations prove a strict global optimum for full adoption. Neither statistical sampling nor a nonexhaustive finite search is used to assert a universal statement. Endpoint cases \(p=0\) and \(p=1\) are deliberately excluded from uniqueness.

## Originality — PASS within examined evidence; residual historical risk

The relevant literature searches covered the two-triangle bridge and barbell synonyms, random one/two thresholds, both seed-selection objectives, the explicit crossover expression, and potential equivalence under general threshold/influence-maximization models. The publicly available expanded Kempe–Kleinberg–Tardos article was read in the general threshold, equivalence, submodularity, and influence maximization sections; it provides a model and approximation theory, not the exact finite optimizer. The full O'Sullivan et al. clustered-network article was examined through the clique model, approximation and results sections, identifying a different large-network approximation problem. Watts (2002) was checked through its publication metadata and abstract, but full article inspection was obstructed; no conclusion about its entire text is drawn. Related published zero-forcing results on the same graph family and threshold-conversion results on complete multipartite graphs have materially different activation rules. No checked claim implies the exact four-orbit inequalities and their two-objective disagreement. A prior equivalent finite calculation may exist under another name: this is a limitation, not an assertion of absolute uniqueness in the literature.

Closest sources: D. J. Watts (2002), doi:10.1073/pnas.082090499; D. Kempe, J. Kleinberg and É. Tardos (2003/2015), doi:10.1145/956750.956769 and doi:10.4086/toc.2015.v011a004; D. J. P. O'Sullivan et al. (2015), doi:10.3389/fphy.2015.00071.

## Value — PASS

The two linked triangles are a natural smallest motif for two cohesive three-person communities connected by a single social bridge, and their four symmetry-distinct placements create a motivated, exact finite influence-allocation problem. The result identifies a non-obvious qualitative conflict between expected network reach and complete community adoption, with a nontrivial algebraic transition that both sharpens and limits intuition about bridge targeting. Exact finite benchmarks can test influence-optimization heuristics and indicate when choosing an objective function matters. No application beyond this mathematical model is claimed.

## Scope and outstanding limitations

The independent threshold variables are assigned once at inception, only two sites can be seeded, and no uncertainty in topology or behavioral heterogeneity beyond this distribution is included. The article by Watts could not be inspected beyond public metadata/abstract; an earlier differently described exact formula remains possible. No independent external examination has been carried out.
