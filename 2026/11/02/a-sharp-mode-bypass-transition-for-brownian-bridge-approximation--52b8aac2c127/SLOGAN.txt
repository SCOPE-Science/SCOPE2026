For smooth Brownian-bridge approximations of an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck step, keeping Fourier frequencies \(\{1,3\}\) beats the usual \(\{1,2\}\) whenever \(0<\lambda h<0.9700737596\ldots\), with a sharp switch at that boundary. In the weak-reversion limit, this nonconsecutive choice reduces the remaining strong endpoint mean-square error by about 84.11% at the same Gaussian-mode budget.
