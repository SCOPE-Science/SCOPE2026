# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The exact continuant characteristic polynomial reduces the four-mode problem to a quadratic for squared eigenvalues. The three positivity requirements for its roots are necessary and sufficient for real-simple spectra; their simultaneous satisfaction gives the exact gain boundaries. The strict budget bound is a monotone rationalized expression in the normalized coupling ratio. The endpoint exceptional-point and asymptotic statements follow from exact characteristic identities and a first-order square-root expansion. The standalone checker recomputes symbolic identities and directly samples matrix eigenvalues. The paper-level claim does not rely on sampled testing.

## Originality

PASS on compared accessible claims, not a claim of exhaustive historical priority. The full accessible text of Liang–Scott–Joglekar (2014) treats two oscillatory gain/loss potentials, rather than the present end-only gain/loss and coupling-budget optimization. The accessible first-page text and diagrams of Rivolta–Maes (2015) feature a different four-mode transverse two-gain/two-loss configuration; its full text could not be inspected, creating a specific priority risk. Phang and coauthors (2016) study modulated finite resonator chains, not the stated sharp fixed-budget recovery-width supremum. Searches for the exact bound, coupling ratios, and equivalent normalized gain metrics identified no direct prior statement; this is limited evidence, not uniqueness certification.

## Value

PASS. A recovered real-spectrum window is physically meaningful only if it tolerates uncertainty in the gain setting. With a fixed total coupling budget, the theorem provides a complete sharp no-go bound of about 7% of that budget, plus nonattainment at a singular exceptional-point limit. It identifies an unavoidable tradeoff between achieving an intervening broken-symmetry phase and maximizing the recovered gain margin. This improves design interpretation of reentry beyond merely locating a gain threshold. The model and budget are carefully delimited; applications to actual fabricated structures require additional modeling.

## Residual scientific limitations

The exact four-mode linear model ignores dispersion, parasitic links, nonlinear saturation and practical coupling-versus-geometry constraints. A closely relevant 2015 publication was not fully accessible, so historical priority remains uncertain. Near exceptional gains eigenvector coalescence may produce algebraic amplification despite real eigenvalues.
