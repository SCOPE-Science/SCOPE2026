A one-vector Gaussian range finder on every real two-column full-rank matrix has expected squared Frobenius residual exactly \(\sigma_1\sigma_2\), while a Rademacher range finder with the same singular spectrum ranges sharply from the harmonic to the arithmetic mean of the squared singular values as the right-singular axes rotate. The exact rotation frontier separates orientations where sign probes beat Gaussian probes from orientations where they can be quadratically worse than the optimal rank-one approximation.
