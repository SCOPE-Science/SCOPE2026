# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The metric-cone angular separation is uniform on \([0,\alpha]\); the independent radial and angular moments give the exact quadratic pair-distance mean. The derivative structure \(g''(\alpha)=8\alpha\cos\alpha\) and explicit endpoint signs establish exactly two critical points and a unique global minimum. The bound is attained by an actual cone. Monte Carlo and bisection are supplementary only.

## Originality

**PASS, with historical-priority risk.** The closest primary source, Bacher–Sturm arXiv:1003.2114v1, covers the metric-cone cosine rule, but its optimization claims concern curvature-dimension bounds, not random-pair dispersion over cone shapes. Tu–Fischbach arXiv:math-ph/0201046v1 covers ambient Euclidean random distances in balls. Kimberling DOI:10.4171/EM/172 studies spherical mean distances; Mayrand arXiv:1407.7919v1 cone geodesic dynamics. published-finding corpus searches and accessible prior-claim comparisons found no matching or dominating fixed-area cone theorem; this does not establish absolute historical priority. The 2008 Kokkendorff paper was checked only at abstract level, with a distinct manifold/Ricci setting.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** This is a sharp geometric optimization of a natural metric-measure functional over the entire nondegenerate cone family. The unique interior minimizing shape, second stationary-point classification, and strict opposite fixed-slant stochastic behavior distinguish the result from a routine computation of one moment. The optimal coefficient is a quantitative benchmark for intrinsic geometric dispersion.

## Limits

Only the lateral intrinsic metric is involved. The closed base disk, ambient chords, frusta, oblique cones and alternative sampling laws are not covered. Numerical checks do not establish infinite-dimensional or continuum statements. No independent expert or historical-priority audit has been performed.
