# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact invariant alternating subspace gives the prime two-cycle equation \(a=2h\sin a\). The Laplacian spectrum reconstructs every nontrivial two-step Floquet multiplier, not merely the alternating eigenvalue, and proves the exact \(h=\pi/4\) transverse transition. The spectral minimax has a unique optimizer. An implicit-function argument on the complement of the simple \(-1\) eigenspace proves that the symmetric two-cycle is locally the only new prime two-cycle at its birth. The numerical replay is supporting evidence, not an infinite-dimensional or all-parameter proof.

**Originality — PASS under inspected literature.** Buescu and Oliveira's 2026 discrete-ring theorem stops at \(h<1/2\) on even rings, noting that larger-strength bifurcations are not treated. The related 2026 Morse–Smale network paper concerns a still smaller diffeomorphism regime. Zhai, Xiao and Zheng's 2023 study already explains qualitative period doubling in networks of sine circle maps; that mechanism is prior art. None of those inspected statements implies the combined sharp all-even-ring cycle stability law, local exclusivity, and exact size-dependent transverse damping optimum.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The claim resolves the first explicitly identified supercritical transition of an established phase-network model. It quantifies the new coherent two-cycle for every even network size and optimizes its *entire transverse* convergence spectrum, with a sharp \(n^{-2}\) finite-size penalty. It is not a claim about the global basin or the dynamics at the later neutral endpoint.

**Risks and limits.** Generic scalar sine-map period doubling is well known and not itself the proposed contribution. A broader historical coupled-map-lattice theorem may subsume some exact formulas. Only the specified relevant portions of full primary texts were inspected; comprehensive priority assessment and independent mathematical validation are outstanding. The conclusions are for identical unweighted even rings, common-phase quotient, local hyperbolicity, and a fixed two-cycle branch.
