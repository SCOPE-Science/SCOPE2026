# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The normalized kernel is reconstructed from its orthonormal basis, the complete circulant Gram matrix is diagonalized by root-of-unity Poisson residues, and injectivity on the finite sample range follows from an analytic Vandermonde minor. The signs for the two- and three-vertex cases are derived globally, not inferred from plots; the four-vertex sequence comparisons are exact; the higher-vertex switching is proved uniformly in the finite Fourier-mode index with exponentially separated root contributions. The package checker confirms 456 finite assertions, but is not used as a proof of infinity. Limitations include fixed polygon size in the asymptotic and the absence of a finite-area irregular-polygon classification.

## Originality — PASS, with priority qualification
The full 2024 paper by Sun, Du and Qi supplies the harmonic Fock kernel and radial Toeplitz spectral context, not a finite regular-polygon sampling stability phase diagram. The inspected main sections of Gou and collaborators' positive-measure Toeplitz analysis concern operator boundedness and compactness. Database searches for polygon Gram spectra, cyclic eigenvalue crossing, and mode switching did not identify an equivalent or stronger result. A related published Bergman boundary-pole Hessian spectrum and a Szegő-kernel orbit-conditioning theorem use different spaces, operators and stability quantities; neither implies the present claim. Finite search is not a priority certificate, and unlocated literature may still overlap.

## Value — PASS
For the natural finite sampling map associated with a canonical harmonic reproducing kernel, the identity of its weakest-observed cyclic frequency determines the direction of worst reconstruction error. This theorem gives a complete, all-polygon-size dichotomy: a unique transition and exact tight design at sizes two and three, versus infinitely recurring worst-frequency changes at every larger size. It distinguishes stable and recurrent conditioning geometry by a sharp discrete threshold, beyond the mechanical kernel or circulant-eigenvalue formula alone.
