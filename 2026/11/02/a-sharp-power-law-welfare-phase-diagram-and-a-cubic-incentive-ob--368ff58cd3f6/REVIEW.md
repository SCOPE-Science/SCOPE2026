# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed domain is \(n\ge3\), distinct points in arbitrary metric spaces, with exactly \(n-1\) facilities; the optimal social cost equals the closest-pair distance by the triangle inequality and a matching construction. The weighted-nearest-distance identity and two forced minimal values yield the all-exponent upper bounds. For \(q>1\), maximizing \(y^{q-1}-Ry^q\) supplies an exact root; a real-line nearest-neighbor vector realizes equality. The truthful cost and small outward deviation are calculated exactly; the derivative is strictly negative only when \(q>3\). Finite verification is corroborative, not proof of the infinite statement. Risks: none detected in the domain-specific mathematical steps; behavior at collocated reports and strategic behavior at smaller powers are outside scope.

## Originality — PASS with residual risk

A complete accessible preprint, Chan–Lin–Wang (2026), Section 4, explicitly states and proves the inverse-square (\(q=2\)) optimal ratio and strategyproofness; Escoffier et al. (2011) already established the inverse-distance case \(q=1\). Both are acknowledged. The new result is the real-exponent sharp welfare phase diagram (including its \(q=1\) finite-versus-infinite transition) and a rigorously witnessed incentive failure for all \(q>3\); these statements are neither asserted nor implied by the two isolated earlier powers. Targeted textual searches and mathematical-record comparisons did not find an equivalent general statement. Risks: unindexed older power-weight facility mechanisms might subsume the result; the 2011 full text was not independently inspected here, so its stated coverage is based on the accessible 2026 source's explicit treatment and bibliography.

## Value — PASS

The inverse-square mechanism improves worst-case welfare over the inverse-distance mechanism while retaining strategyproofness. Varying the naturally adjustable distance-power exponent answers two design questions together: how much welfare improvement occurs for every exponent, and whether simply sharpening the weights can preserve incentives indefinitely. The phase transition at one and the exact sharp curve show a nontrivial structural phenomenon, while the three-agent obstruction above three rules out the naive unrestricted extrapolation of strategyproofness. This is a parameterized efficiency–incentive boundary, not a renaming of the preexisting square case. A full characterization of the strategyproof exponents remains open in this analysis.
