# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The cumulative-infiltration equation has a unique globally continued forward solution for every block; its scalar flow preserves strict state order. Transforming the lower-rate field to translation makes the higher-rate flow nonexpansive because its velocity ratio is nonincreasing, proving the swap inequality and exact strictness integral. Bubble sorting then proves the global extremizers. Direct fraction arithmetic verifies the strict witness; independent finite integrations support but do not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS, within inspected evidence
Chu's unsteady-rain formulation and Green–Ampt capacity laws are established background. Tan and collaborators (2018) already found the favorable decreasing-rainfall effect in a three-pattern numerical comparison. That observation is not claimed as new. The assessed extension is a theorem comparing all permutations of arbitrary finite storm blocks for every positive nonincreasing capacity, together with the exact adjacent-swap equality test and a closed rational runoff difference. The inspected Triadis–Broadbridge 2012 full text addresses capacity derivation, not rainfall-block extremal ordering. Chu 1978 and Philip 1993 could not be reviewed in full, leaving a material priority risk. A generic scalar-flow rearrangement lemma might imply the mathematical statement; no proof of absolute novelty is asserted.

## Value — PASS
Temporal placement of a fixed total amount of rain is central to infiltration-excess calculations. The result supplies global sharp best/worst arrangements under transparent physical assumptions, identifies when temporal ordering matters and when it provably cannot, and gives a benchmark for rain-model implementations and experimental design. Its limitation to instantaneous runoff with no ponding or soil recovery is scientifically essential.
