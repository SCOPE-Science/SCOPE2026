# A sharp reducibility threshold for nilpotent two-block centralizers

## Finding
Let \(n\ge5\), \(r=n-2\), and let \(N=J_r\oplus J_2\) be the direct sum of nilpotent Jordan blocks over a field \(k\). For every integer \(m\ge0\), let \(\mathcal V_{n,m}\) denote the reduced algebraic variety of ordered \(m\)-tuples \((X_1,\ldots,X_m)\) of nilpotent \(n\)-square matrices such that \(X_iN=NX_i\) and \(X_iX_j=X_jX_i\) for all indices.

**Geometric classification.** Over any algebraically closed field, \(\mathcal V_{n,m}\) is irreducible for \(m=0,1,2\), and has exactly two irreducible components for each \(m\ge3\). For \(m\ge3\) the component dimensions are
\[
 nm\quad\text{and}\quad(n-1)m+3.
\]
They coincide at \(m=3\); the first is larger for \(m\ge4\). These statements hold in every characteristic.

**Arithmetic census.** For every prime power \(q\), including even characteristic, and every \(m\ge0\),
\[
 |\mathcal V_{n,m}(\mathbb F_q)|
 =q^{nm}+q^2(q+1)\bigl(q^{(n-1)m}-q^{(n-2)m}\bigr).
\]
The formula includes the unique empty tuple when \(m=0\). The geometric and arithmetic assertions are a single result, arising from the same explicit defining equations.

## Assumptions and scope
The symbol \(J_d\) denotes the nilpotent Jordan matrix with one superdiagonal of ones. All fields and all characteristics are allowed. Matrices are *required* to be individually nilpotent; arbitrary elements of the centralizer are not counted. Tuples are ordered; no quotient by conjugation is taken. For geometric language, use the reduced variety over an algebraically closed field, rather than a scheme-theoretic statement about possible embedded or nonreduced structures. The restriction \(r\ge3\) is essential for the uniform coordinate reduction below.

## Proof
### 1. Coordinates in the centralizer and nilpotence
View \(k^n\) as the \(k[t]\)-module \(M_r\oplus M_2\), where \(M_d=k[t]/(t^d)\) and \(t\) acts as \(N\). Every element commuting with \(N\) has a unique block presentation
\[
X=\begin{pmatrix}
 a(t)&t^{r-2}(b_0+b_1t)\\
 c_0+c_1t&d_0+d_1t
\end{pmatrix},\qquad a(t)\bmod t^r,
\]
where each off-diagonal block is interpreted as the corresponding \(k[t]\)-module map. Here the maps \(M_2\to M_r\) and \(M_r\to M_2\) are generated by their values on \(1\). The induced map on \((M_r\oplus M_2)/t(M_r\oplus M_2)\) is triangular with diagonal entries \(a_0,d_0\). Thus \(X\) is nilpotent if and only if \(a_0=d_0=0\). Indeed, necessity follows from nilpotence on the quotient, while sufficiency follows because \(X^2\) maps the full module into its \(t\)-multiple and \(X\) commutes with multiplication by \(t\), so \(X^{2r}=0\).

Consequently the nilpotent centralizer has coordinates \(a_1,\ldots,a_{r-1},d_1,b_0,b_1,c_0,c_1\). In a tuple write \(x_i=a_{1i}-d_{1i}\); the \(r-2\) coordinates \(a_{2i},\ldots,a_{r-1,i}\) and coordinate \(d_{1i}\) are free, giving \((r-1)m=(n-3)m\) free parameters.

### 2. The four commutator equations
Direct multiplication of the module-homomorphism blocks, including the boundary \(r=3\), yields the following four necessary and sufficient equations, for every \(i,j\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_i b_{0j}-x_jb_{0i}&=0,\\
x_i c_{0j}-x_jc_{0i}&=0,\\
b_{0i}c_{0j}-b_{0j}c_{0i}&=0,\\
b_{0i}c_{1j}+b_{1i}c_{0j}-b_{0j}c_{1i}-b_{1j}c_{0i}&=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Let \(x,b,c,B,C\in k^m\) be the rows of coefficients \(x_i,b_{0i},c_{0i},b_{1i},c_{1i}\). Then the conditions are
\[
\operatorname{rank}\begin{pmatrix}x\\b\\c\end{pmatrix}\le1,
\qquad b\wedge C+B\wedge c=0.
\]
Denote this five-row variety by \(Y_m\). The original variety is \(Y_m\times\mathbb A^{(n-3)m}\).

### 3. Components and sharp threshold
The closed subset \(L=\{b=c=0\}\) is affine space of dimension \(3m\). Let \(U=Y_m\setminus L\). On \(U\), the rank-one equations imply that for a unique line \(\ell=kz\in\mathbb P^{m-1}\), one may write \(x=\nu z\), \(b=\lambda z\), \(c=\mu z\), where \((\lambda,\mu)\ne(0,0)\). The final equation says \(\lambda C-\mu B\in kz\). For fixed \((\ell,\nu,\lambda,\mu)\), this is a surjective linear system of \(m-1\) independent equations in the \(2m\) entries of \((B,C)\). Consequently \(U\) is irreducible, of dimension
\[
(m-1)+3+(m+1)=2m+3.
\]
Thus \(Y_m=L\cup\overline U\), with at most these two irreducible components.

On \(U\) the three rows \(x,B,C\) span a space of dimension at most two: if \(x\ne0\), the relation \(\lambda C-\mu B\in kx\) implies it; if \(x=0\), it holds trivially. The same rank bound holds on \(\overline U\), being determinantal and closed. For \(m\ge3\), \(L\) contains points with \(x,B,C\) independent, hence \(L\not\subset\overline U\). Since \(U\) is irreducible and nonempty, both \(L\) and \(\overline U\) are distinct irreducible components, with dimensions \(3m\) and \(2m+3\).

For \(m\le2\), instead \(L\subset\overline U\). The assertion is immediate for \(m=0\). For \(m=1\), every point is obtained by degeneration from a choice of nonzero \((b,c)\). For \(m=2\), given \((x,0,0,B,C)\in L\) with \(x\ne0\), choose a nonzero \((\lambda,\mu)\) with \(\lambda C-\mu B\in kx\), possible because \(k^2/kx\) has dimension one. Then take \(b=\varepsilon\lambda x\), \(c=\varepsilon\mu x\) for nonzero \(\varepsilon\) and let \(\varepsilon\to0\). If \(x=0\), choose a nonzero row \(z\) and nonzero \((\lambda,\mu)\) such that \(\lambda C-\mu B\in kz\): take \(z=B,\lambda=0,\mu=1\) if \(B\ne0\); otherwise take \(z=C,\lambda=1,\mu=0\) if \(C\ne0\); otherwise any nonzero \(z\) works. Taking \(x=\varepsilon z,b=\varepsilon\lambda z,c=\varepsilon\mu z\) gives the degeneration. Hence \(Y_m=\overline U\) is irreducible for \(m\le2\).

After adjoining \((n-3)m\) free coordinates, the two component dimensions become \(nm\) and \((n-1)m+3\), as claimed.

### 4. Exact rational-point count
When \(b=c=0\), there are \(q^{3m}\) choices of \((x,B,C)\). Otherwise, the pairs \((b,c)\) of proportional vectors, not both zero, number \((q+1)(q^m-1)\): choose their common line in \(k^2\) and one nonzero \(m\)-vector. For each such pair there are \(q\) choices of \(x\) along its common row line, and the equation \(b\wedge C+B\wedge c=0\) imposes exactly \(m-1\) independent linear constraints on \((B,C)\), leaving \(q^{m+1}\) choices. Thus
\[
|Y_m(\mathbb F_q)|=q^{3m}+q^{m+2}(q+1)(q^m-1).
\]
Multiplying by the free-coordinate factor \(q^{(n-3)m}\) and collecting exponents proves the stated formula. The empty tuple case is immediate separately and agrees with the displayed expression.

## Verification
The accompanying standard-library Python script `verify_nilpotent_centralizer.py` constructs explicit dense matrices with the Jordan centralizer block form. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), it directly checks every constructed matrix commutes with \(N\), is nilpotent, and then independently counts commuting pairs and triples using dense multiplication for \(n=5,6\). It also enumerates the five-row defining equations for pairs over \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\).

The direct matrix counts are \((128,3328,75776)\) for \(n=5,q=2,m=1,2,3\), and \((256,13312,606208)\) for \(n=6,q=2,m=1,2,3\). The five-row pair counts are \(208\) at \(q=2\) and \(3321\) at \(q=3\). The script terminates with `NILPOTENT_TWO_BLOCK_CENTRALIZER_OK`. These finite computations are cross-checks, not proofs of the all-field and all-arity statements.

## Relationship to prior work
Britnell and Wildon (2010, arXiv:1001.0811v1) analyze pairwise commuting *similarity types* and classes, not the present all-arity nilpotent subvariety inside the fixed centralizer of type \((n-2,2)\). Basili, Iarrobino and Khatami (2010) treat commuting nilpotent matrices and the nilpotent centralizer at one additional matrix; the present formula and sharp new three-tuple reducibility threshold are stronger in a different direction, and are not implied by the one-matrix irreducibility result. Guralnick and Ngo (2013, arXiv:1308.2420v1) prove reducibility phenomena for unrestricted nilpotent commuting varieties; their ambient variety does not impose commutation with a fixed two-block Jordan matrix. Sharma (2014, arXiv:1409.2698) studies similarity orbits of commuting tuples, as opposed to these raw finite-field point counts and component dimensions for fixed \(N\).

A literature and mathematical-record comparison found no equivalent all-arity formula or two-component classification for this exact fixed-centralizer family, but historical priority cannot be certified from a bounded search. In particular, a complete full-text comparison with every earlier centralizer-geometry article is not claimed.

## Limitations
The theorem assumes \(n\ge5\) and the fixed Jordan partition \((n-2,2)\); no result is asserted for other partitions, general nilpotent commuting varieties, orbit counts or nonreduced scheme structures. Irreducibility is for the reduced variety. The calculation does not establish global normalization, singularity types or connectedness of the scheme. Experimental checks cover only stated small finite fields. Independent mathematical verification and exhaustive historical priority checks have not been performed.
