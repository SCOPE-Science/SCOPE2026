# A sharp regularity boundary for total weak endomorphism monoids of complete multipartite graphs

## Finding
For every finite simple complete multipartite graph \(G=K_{n_1,\ldots,n_r}\), with \(r\ge1\) and all \(n_i\ge1\), the monoid of **total weak graph endomorphisms** is regular if and only if the graph is complete bipartite, edgeless, or complete. Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{\mathrm{wEnd}(G)\text{ is regular}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r\le2\ \text{or}\ n_1=\cdots=n_r=1.}
\]
Among complete multipartite graphs the smallest nonregular instance is \(K_{1,1,2}\), of order four.

## Assumptions and scope
A complete multipartite graph has vertex partition \(V=P_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup P_r\), all parts nonempty, with two vertices adjacent exactly when they belong to different parts. Graphs are finite, simple, and loopless. A **total weak endomorphism** is a map \(f:V\to V\) such that whenever \(u,v\) are adjacent, \(f(u)=f(v)\) or \(f(u),f(v)\) are adjacent. The set \(\mathrm{wEnd}(G)\) of these maps is a monoid under composition. An element is regular if some weak endomorphism \(g\) obeys \(f\circ g\circ f=f\); a monoid is regular if every element is regular. This use of weak means edge-contraction is permitted. Partial weak endomorphisms are NOT included.

## Proof
**Elementary inner-inverse criterion.** A total map \(f\) has \(f\circ g\circ f=f\) if and only if \(f(g(t))=t\) for every \(t\in I=f(V)\). Thus any valid \(g\) must select, on the image, a representative of each fiber of \(f\); the representatives must satisfy the weak graph condition whenever corresponding image points are adjacent. Conversely, if the representatives can be chosen so that this partial section extends to a total weak endomorphism, they yield a valid inner inverse.

**Complete and edgeless cases.** If \(r=1\), the graph has no edges, so every self-map is weak. If every \(n_i=1\), the graph is complete, so again every self-map is weak. The full finite transformation monoid is regular: choose one representative in each nonempty fiber, extend arbitrarily to all other points, and use the preceding criterion.

**Complete bipartite case.** Write \(V=A\sqcup B\), where \(A,B\) are nonempty independent parts, and let \(f\) be weak. If \(|I|=1\), a constant \(g\) selecting any preimage of the unique image value supplies \(fgf=f\). Suppose \(|I|\ge2\).

First, \(I\) meets both \(A\) and \(B\). Otherwise, say \(I\subseteq A\), and choose distinct image values \(a_1,a_2\in A\) and preimages \(x_1,x_2\). Since distinct vertices of \(A\) are nonadjacent, the weak condition forces \(x_1,x_2\) to lie in the same source part, say \(A\) (renaming the source parts for this argument). Every vertex of the opposite source part \(B\), adjacent to both \(x_1,x_2\), must map into the opposite target part \(B\) (its image cannot equal both \(a_1,a_2\)). This contradicts \(I\subseteq A\). The same argument applies if \(I\subseteq B\).

We prove there is a bijective matching between the two source-part labels and the two target-part labels with the following property: every value \(t\in I\) has a preimage in the assigned source part for its target part. If \(I\cap A\) contains two or more values, all their preimages lie in one source part \(X\): any two distinct target values in \(A\) cannot have preimages in opposite source parts. The opposite source part \(Y\) must map entirely into target \(B\), by the two-value argument above. If \(I\cap B\) has at least two values, their preimages all lie in \(Y\), because otherwise the nonempty source part \(Y\) would be forced to map entirely into target \(A\), a contradiction. If \(I\cap B\) has just one value, it occurs as the image of every vertex of \(Y\), so has a preimage there. Thus target \(A\) can be assigned source \(X\) and target \(B\) source \(Y\). The same argument with target labels exchanged handles \(|I\cap B|\ge2\).

The remaining case is \(I=\{a,b\}\) with \(a\in A\) and \(b\in B\). Because both source parts are nonempty and their image union contains both values, one can select a preimage of \(a\) in one source part and a preimage of \(b\) in the other: if a source part has only one of the two values, use that part for the corresponding value and the opposite part for the other; if each source part has both values, either orientation works.

Choose for each \(t\in I\cap A\) a representative \(s_t\) in its assigned source part \(X\), and for each \(t\in I\cap B\) a representative in the other source part \(Y\). Define \(g(t)=s_t\) on \(I\); extend \(g\) to all of target \(A\) by mapping the remaining points arbitrarily into \(X\), and to all of target \(B\) by mapping the remaining points arbitrarily into \(Y\). Because each cross-edge of target \(G\) maps to a cross-edge of source \(G\), \(g\) is a (strict, hence weak) graph endomorphism. Since \(f(g(t))=t\) for every \(t\in I\), the inner-inverse criterion proves \(fgf=f\).

**At least three parts, with a nonsingleton part.** Let \(P,Q,R\) be three distinct parts, with distinct \(z_1,z_2\in R\). Choose \(a\in P\) and \(b\in Q\), and define \(f(z_1)=b\), \(f(z_2)=z_1\), and \(f(v)=a\) for every other vertex \(v\). This is weak: each cross-part input edge either is sent to a single value or has one image \(a\), while the other is \(b\) or \(z_1\); these latter two values both lie outside \(P\), hence are adjacent to \(a\). The image is \(I=\{a,b,z_1\}\), with \(b,z_1\) adjacent because \(Q\ne R\). The fibers of \(b,z_1\) are uniquely \(\{z_1\}\) and \(\{z_2\}\), respectively. Any \(g\) with \(fgf=f\) must therefore have \(g(b)=z_1\) and \(g(z_1)=z_2\). But \(z_1,z_2\) are distinct nonadjacent vertices of the independent part \(R\). The adjacent input pair \(b,z_1\) is mapped to distinct nonadjacent vertices, contrary to weakness. Hence \(f\) is nonregular.

Finally, on at most three vertices every complete multipartite graph has at most two parts or has only singleton parts; \(K_{1,1,2}\) has three parts and a nonsingleton part. This proves the sharp order-four boundary. \(\square\)

## Verification
The auxiliary program `verify.py` exhaustively enumerates finite weak endomorphisms for eight labeled bipartite parameter pairs and searches for the two-color transversal demanded by the proof. It independently enumerates full weak transformation monoids on the three-part graphs with part sizes \((1,1,1)\), \((1,1,2)\), and \((1,2,2)\), checking regularity against *every* admissible inner inverse. It also constructs and validates an explicit obstruction on \((2,2,2)\). Execution prints `VERIFY_OK`. These finite checks do not constitute a proof for unbounded part sizes; the preceding argument does.

## Relationship to prior work
Dimitrova, Fernandes and Koppitz (arXiv:2401.11741, initially public January 22, 2024), principally Theorem 2.3 and the surrounding definitions in Section 2, characterize regular **partial** weak endomorphisms of star graphs and show that their *partial* monoid is nonregular when the star has at least three vertices. A partial weak endomorphism can omit vertices; their nonregular witness omits the star center. This does **not** imply nonregularity of the corresponding **total** monoid, and the present theorem proves that the total weak endomorphism monoid of every star, and indeed every complete bipartite graph, is regular. The same article explicitly distinguishes total from partial graph maps. Gu and Hou (2016) study the regularity of **strict** graph endomorphism monoids of generalized lexicographic products. A strict map forbids contraction of an input edge; regularity of that proper submonoid neither gives nor rules out regularity of all weak maps. Yingtaweesittikul and collaborators (2023) examine **counts** of weak homomorphisms of paths into ladder and prism graphs, not regularity on complete multipartite graphs. Searches for equivalent results in published algebraic-graph/semigroup literature and mathematical records did not find a statement implying the complete-multipartite criterion; that negative search is not a proof of first publication.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns finite simple complete multipartite graphs only, with arbitrary positive part sizes. It does not characterize regular weak monoids of general graphs, nor partial weak endomorphism semigroups. Possible older equivalences in reflexive-relation endomorphism or transformation-semigroup terminology warrant independent literature review. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
