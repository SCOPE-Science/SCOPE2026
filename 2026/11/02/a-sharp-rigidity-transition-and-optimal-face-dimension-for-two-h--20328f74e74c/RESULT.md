# A sharp rigidity transition and optimal-face dimension for two-hop hypercube walks

## Finding

**Theorem.** For every integer \(d\ge2\), let \(X_d=\{0,1\}^d\), and let \(\mathcal P_d\) consist of all real symmetric, entrywise nonnegative, row-stochastic matrices \(P\), indexed by \(X_d\), satisfying \(P_{xy}=0\) whenever the Hamming distance \(h(x,y)>2\). Diagonal entries are permitted. Write \(J\) for the all-ones matrix of order \(2^d\), and set
\[
\mu(P)=\left\|P-2^{-d}J\right\|_2.
\]
Then the unrestricted optimum is
\[
\boxed{\min_{P\in\mathcal P_d}\mu(P)=
\begin{cases}
0,&d=2,\\
1/7,&d=3,\\
(d-3)/(d+1),&d\ge4.
\end{cases}}
\]
An optimal matrix \(P_d^*\) has the following entries:
\[
(P_d^*)_{xy}=\begin{cases}
1/4,&d=2\quad(\text{every pair }x,y),\\
1/7,&d=3\text{ and }h(x,y)\le2,\\
2/[d(d+1)],&d\ge4\text{ and }h(x,y)\in\{1,2\},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, in every dimension \(d\ge4\), the *ordinary simple random walk on the square of the hypercube*, with equal probability on each distance-one or distance-two neighbor and no holding, is globally fastest mixing even against independently assigned, nonsymmetric-in-coordinate edge probabilities (subject to matrix symmetry).

For \(2\le d\le4\), this is the **unique** optimizer in \(\mathcal P_d\). For every \(d\ge5\), non-coordinate-symmetric optimizers occur in continuous families: the optimizer set within translation-invariant chains has local affine dimension exactly \(\binom{d}{2}-1\) near the uniform optimum. Nevertheless every optimizer for \(d\ge4\) is loop-free. More precisely, for each vertex \(x\), every optimizer assigns total mass \(2/(d+1)\) to one-bit moves, total mass \((d-1)/(d+1)\) to two-bit moves, and for each individual coordinate \(i\) assigns aggregate flipping mass \(2/(d+1)\).

Relative to the nearest-neighbor-only optimum, whose second-largest eigenvalue modulus is \((d-1)/(d+1)\), allowing two-bit moves **doubles the optimal spectral gap** for all \(d\ge4\).

## Assumptions and scope

The matrices are **symmetric** and **stochastic**; no reversibility with nonuniform stationary measure, directional transition probabilities, or moves of Hamming distance greater than two are allowed. No coordinate-permutation or translation invariance is assumed for a competitor. The objective is the asymptotic second-largest eigenvalue modulus (SLEM), equivalently the spectral norm on the mean-zero subspace, not a finite-time total-variation mixing criterion.

A shell-invariant matrix chooses, with probabilities \(\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2\ge0\) summing to one, to remain fixed, flip a uniformly chosen single bit, or flip a uniformly chosen unordered pair of bits. A general symmetric feasible matrix need not have this form.

## Proof

**1. Unrestricted problem reduces to a shell invariant optimum.** The group generated by coordinate permutations and independent bit flips acts transitively on ordered vertex pairs of each Hamming distance. Averaging any feasible \(P\) under this group preserves symmetry, nonnegativity, stochasticity and support. Since \(P\mapsto\|P-2^{-d}J\|_2\) is convex and invariant under conjugation by permutation matrices, the average cannot increase the objective. Thus the unrestricted minimum equals the minimum over shell-invariant matrices; this does **not** by itself imply uniqueness of unrestricted optimizers.

**2. Exact eigenvalues.** The Walsh character indexed by \(S\subseteq\{1,\dots,d\}\) is \(\chi_S(x)=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in S}x_i}\). Its shell-invariant eigenvalue depends only on \(k=|S|\):
\[
q_k=\alpha_0+\alpha_1\frac{d-2k}{d}
 +\alpha_2\frac{(d-2k)^2-d}{d(d-1)},\qquad 0\le k\le d.
\]
This follows by counting single-bit flips and unordered pairs meeting \(S\) in exactly one coordinate. The stationary eigenvalue is \(q_0=1\), and \(\mu=\max_{1\le k\le d}|q_k|\).

**3. Lower bound, construction and invariant uniqueness for \(d\ge4\).** The extremal Walsh modes obey
\[
q_1=1-\frac{2\alpha_1+4\alpha_2}{d}
\ge1-\frac4d+\frac{2\alpha_1}{d},
\qquad q_d=1-2\alpha_1.
\]
The maximum of the two right-hand affine functions is at least \((d-3)/(d+1)\), with equality possible only at \(\alpha_1=2/(d+1)\). Equality in the first bound also forces \(\alpha_2=1-\alpha_1\), so \(\alpha_0=0\); hence the unique shell-invariant candidate is
\[
\alpha_0=0,\qquad \alpha_1=\frac2{d+1},\qquad
\alpha_2=\frac{d-1}{d+1}.
\]
Its spectrum is
\[
q_k^*=\frac{(d+1-2k)^2-(d+1)}{d(d+1)}.
\]
The maximum is \(q_1^*=q_d^*=(d-3)/(d+1)\). The minimum is \(-1/(d+1)\) for even \(d\), and \(-1/d\) for odd \(d\). These negative magnitudes do not exceed \((d-3)/(d+1)\) when \(d\ge4\). Hence the lower bound is attained, and the shell-invariant optimum is unique.

**4. Small dimensions.** For \(d=2\), all four vertices are mutually accessible; \(J/4\) is feasible and is the only symmetric stochastic matrix with zero SLEM. For \(d=3\), a shell-invariant transition has a quadratic polynomial \(q(s)\) in the cube adjacency eigenvalue \(s\in\{3,1,-1,-3\}\), normalized by \(q(3)=1\). Quadratic interpolation gives the exact identity
\[
1=3q(1)-3q(-1)+q(-3),
\]
so the SLEM is at least \(1/7\), with equality forcing the three values \((q(1),q(-1),q(-3))=(1,-1,1)/7\). They yield uniquely \((\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2)=(1,3,3)/7\), which has precisely those eigenvalues. For \(d=4\), the formula in step 3 gives \((q_1^*,q_2^*,q_3^*,q_4^*)=(1,-1,-1,1)/5\).

**5. Rigidity through dimension four.** Let \(P\) be any unrestricted optimizer and let \(P_d^*\) be its group average (necessarily the unique shell-invariant optimum). For each centered Walsh character \(v\), the Rayleigh quotient of every conjugate of \(P\) lies between \(-\mu_d^*\) and \(+\mu_d^*\). Whenever \(P_d^*v=\pm\mu_d^*v\), averaging these Rayleigh quotients forces equality for each conjugate. Since the matrices are symmetric and their centered spectral norms are bounded by \(\mu_d^*\), equality forces \(Pv=\pm\mu_d^*v\). At \(d=3,4\), **every** nonconstant Walsh mode is at one of these extrema. The constant mode is fixed by stochasticity, so \(P=P_d^*\). Uniqueness for \(d=2\) was established in step 4.

**6. Every optimizer has zero holding for \(d\ge4\).** At every \(d\ge4\), all degree-one Walsh characters \(\chi_{\{i\}}\) and the parity character \(\chi_{\{1,\ldots,d\}}\) have eigenvalue \(t=(d-3)/(d+1)\) at \(P_d^*\). The extremal-Rayleigh argument in step 5 shows that they have eigenvalue \(t\) for *every* unrestricted optimizer \(P\). At each vertex \(x\), this says that the total probability of flipping any specified bit and the total probability of an odd (here, one-bit) move both equal
\[
c=\frac{1-t}{2}=\frac2{d+1}.
\]
Summing the specified-bit identities counts one-bit transitions once and two-bit transitions twice. Thus the total two-bit mass is \((d-1)c/2=(d-1)/(d+1)\), and row stochasticity makes the holding probability exactly zero. This proves the asserted rowwise rigidity without assuming group invariance.

**7. Sharp failure of unrestricted uniqueness and dimension of the optimal face for \(d\ge5\).** Start from \(P_d^*\) with common nonzero edge probability \(w=2/[d(d+1)]\). For each vertex, add \(\varepsilon\) to the weight of the move flipping bits \(\{1,2\}\), subtract \(\varepsilon\) from the move flipping \(\{1,3\}\), subtract \(\varepsilon\) from the single-bit flip \(\{2\}\), and add \(\varepsilon\) to the single-bit flip \(\{3\}\); other weights are unchanged. Because each move is its own inverse, the resulting matrix is symmetric and stochastic, with positive allowed edge weights whenever \(0<|\varepsilon|<w\).

On Walsh subset \(S\), the eigenvalue changes by
\[
\Delta_S=\varepsilon\big(1-(-1)^{\mathbf1_{1\in S}}\big)
\big((-1)^{\mathbf1_{3\in S}}-(-1)^{\mathbf1_{2\in S}}\big),
\qquad |\Delta_S|\le4|\varepsilon|.
\]
This is zero on all degree-one and degree-\(d\) characters. In \(d\ge5\), the remaining spectral values of \(P_d^*\) satisfy \(|q_k^*|<t\) for \(2\le k\le d-1\): their largest possible positive value lies strictly below \(t\), and their negative values have magnitude at most \(1/d<t\). Therefore a sufficiently small nonzero \(\varepsilon\) retains SLEM exactly \(t\). The resulting non-coordinate-symmetric, unequal-edge kernel is a different unrestricted optimizer. Varying \(\varepsilon\) gives infinitely many such kernels.

More precisely, consider the full set of **translation-invariant** feasible kernels, with probability \(p_i\) on the bit-flip vector \(\{i\}\), probability \(q_{ij}\) on the bit-flip vector \(\{i,j\}\), and holding probability \(\rho\). From step 6, every optimal such kernel satisfies \(\rho=0\), \(p_i+\sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij}=c\) for each coordinate and \(\sum_i p_i=c\), where \(c=2/(d+1)\). Equivalently,
\[
\sum_{i<j}q_{ij}=\frac{d-1}{d+1},\qquad
p_i=\frac2{d+1}-\sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij},\qquad\rho=0.
\]
There are \(\binom{d}{2}-1\) free pair-weight coordinates. At the uniform optimum every allowed edge weight is strictly positive, while all Walsh modes of degrees \(2\) through \(d-1\) are strictly inside the spectral bounds. Consequently a relative open neighborhood of the uniform pair weights within this affine hyperplane still consists of feasible optimizers. Conversely the displayed linear constraints are necessary, so the translation-invariant optimal set has local affine dimension **exactly** \(\binom{d}{2}-1\). The unrestricted optimal set is at least this large.

## Verification

The standalone Python 3 script `VERIFY.py` uses exact rational arithmetic and no external packages. It verifies the shell eigenvalue formulas and optimizing candidate over dimensions \(2\) through \(120\), checks all four matrix requirements and the complete Walsh spectrum from explicit transition matrices through dimension \(7\), checks the dimension-three interpolation certificate, and constructs and validates distinct optimal perturbed chains for dimensions \(5,6,7\), including independent pair-weight directions through dimension \(10\). Its replay produced `VERIFY_OK 228683 exact assertions`. Finite tests support the algebra and implementation but are **not** substituted for the infinite-dimensional proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Boyd, Diaconis, Parrilo and Xiao derive the convex fastest-mixing framework and an **edge-transitive graph theorem** that already implies the existence of a uniform-edge optimum on the present graph once it is recognized as the halved \( (d+1)\)-cube. Thus the existence of a uniform optimum and its numerical SLEM are **not independently asserted to be new**: the edge-transitive theorem plus the standard Walsh spectrum yield them. The new contribution claimed here is the exact **rigidity transition** (global uniqueness only through dimension four), the forced zero-holding and coordinate-marginal laws for every unrestricted optimizer, and the \(\binom{d}{2}-1\)-dimensional local optimal face starting at dimension five. Their existing theorem does not classify the unrestricted optimizer set or this sharp dimension threshold. Apers and Sarlette study degree-two polynomial acceleration and discuss the possibility of negative entries, motivating a positivity-constrained two-hop design; their stated results do not optimize the unrestricted symmetric stochastic kernel on the square of the hypercube or classify its full optimizer rigidity. The 1993 spectral and majorization study by Karlin, Lindqvist and Yao concerns hypercube walks with bounded Hamming range and is especially relevant as a potential historical overlap; its accessible abstract concerns fixed-walk spectra and majorization, while its full text was unavailable for comparison.

The current theorem includes the previously implied optimal value as context and supplies the optimizer-face and all-optimizer rigidity conclusions. It does not claim novelty for the value as a standalone consequence, Walsh diagonalization, symmetry averaging, Krawtchouk formulas, or the standard fastest-mixing SDP framework.

## Limitations

The analysis is restricted to reversible symmetric chains with transitions of Hamming distance at most two. For \(d\ge5\), it does not classify all vertex-dependent optimal edge allocations; it gives the exact local affine dimension only inside the translation-invariant subset, plus a universal rowwise law. The first-public-date evidence for the archival focal work is the September 6, 2007 arXiv v1 record. The inaccessible full text of Karlin, Lindqvist and Yao (1993) leaves a residual possibility of stronger prior coverage, notwithstanding the specifically checked abstract and later full-text comparisons. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
