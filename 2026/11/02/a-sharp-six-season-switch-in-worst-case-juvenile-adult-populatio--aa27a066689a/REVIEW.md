# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The trace matching polynomial follows by expanding the closed two-state matrix paths. All six-calendar permutations share determinant \(s^6\prod_j(a+jd)>0\), hence Perron-growth ordering agrees with trace ordering. The finite orbit-certificate file and the standalone Python checker enumerate **all** 720 six-season permutations, grouped into 60 size-12 dihedral classes, and prove the integer-statistic extrema, uniqueness of extremizers and the relevant second-smallest value without approximate optimization. These inequalities and the explicit difference \(T_A-T_B=s^2p^2d^2(-1+2sd/p^2)\) establish the theorem for every allowed real parameter. The matching-polynomial degree bound proves the claimed minimal season count. The script confirms symbolic identities by exact rational matrix products but is not used as a substitute for a proof over infinitely many real parameters.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence, provisional

The closest inspected materials are Mertens et al. (2006), DOI 10.1890/0012-9658(2006)87[2338:TPDIPM]2.0.CO;2, on periodic matrix transients and **cyclic permutations**; Cushing and Ackleh (2011), DOI 10.1080/17513758.2010.544410, on periodic reproduction numbers; Key and Volkmer (2014), DOI 10.13001/1081-3810.2862, on spectral-radius bounds for companion products; Caswell (2019), DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-10534-1_8, on periodic sensitivity; and De Luca and Zamboni (2021), arXiv:2108.11313, on related ordinary cyclic continuants. The inspected results do not establish an exact six-level fertility-permutation optimizer whose minimizing orbit switches at a survival-ratio threshold. Published claim comparisons found related age-structured and periodic systems but no equal or stronger result. The 2006 original technical article and potentially relevant broader generalized-cyclic-continuant results were not available in complete accessible text; their possible coverage remains a genuine historical-priority risk.

## Value — PASS

This is a complete, motivated classification of an ecologically natural six-period scheduling problem (the earliest period supporting a parameter-induced permutation ranking reversal). It distinguishes a robust worst-case fertility calendar from a parameter-dependent worst case and gives an exact decision boundary independent of baseline fecundity. The minimum-length argument explains the mechanism and why smaller calendar examples cannot exhibit it. Applications to actual population management require independently estimated vital rates and additional biological assumptions; no empirical impact is claimed.

## Remaining scientific limits

The theorem does not address general non-arithmetic fertility inventories, density dependence, random environments, or finite-horizon transients. Historical novelty is provisional pending a wider full-text review. The proof and finite certificate have not undergone independent validation.
