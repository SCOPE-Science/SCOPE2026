# Scientific assessment: Nash welfare versus minimum regret in small stable-marriage markets

## Correctness — PASS

The claim uses strict complete preference orders, full stable-matching optimizer sets, positive rank-induced utilities, and zero-based rank consistently. The finite lemma is exhaustive rather than statistical: `CHECK.py` enumerates all \((n!)^{2n}\) preference profiles for \(n=2,3\), all \(n!\) perfect matchings per profile, and checks every off-matching blocking pair with both strict inequalities. It compares exact-integer products and rank maxima. The results are \(16\) equal-set profiles for two pairs; for three pairs, \(39{,}672\) equal sets and \(6{,}984\) proper Nash-set subsets among exactly \(46{,}656\) inputs, and strict inverse Nash-product ordering in all \(3{,}504\) cross-regret stable-matching comparisons. For four pairs, exhaustive matching checks identify exactly three stable matchings and verify the uniquely different optima from the explicit preferences. The proof of arbitrary-size counterexamples is not a finite extrapolation: new mutual-top-choice pairs are forced into every stable matching, their addition preserves original utilities and scales the product by a matching-independent factor, while uniformly shifting the original worst rank. The verifier passed independently after the same source file was inspected. Residual limitation: this is an executable finite certificate, not a separate formal proof-assistant derivation.

## Originality — PASS, with residual publication-priority risk

The full September 2026 motivating preprint by Desai et al. was examined for model, rank utility, minimum-regret objective, Nash optimization, examples, theory and appendices. It develops a Nash-optimal algorithm and empirical comparisons, but not the exact two-/three-pair Nash-versus-regret inclusion or the \(46{,}656\)-profile optimizer-set classification. Classical minimum-regret algorithms and related refinements likewise do not assert this particular Nash-product comparison. The closest indexed small-market results concern Nash versus *total-rank* egalitarian welfare, or regret versus other non-Nash objectives. Those are logically distinct: for three pairs the egalitarian optimizer set can strictly contain the minimum-regret set, so Nash-optimality plus egalitarian-optimality alone leaves Nash-versus-regret status unresolved. All accessible historical claims for this research category were searched for matching outcomes, including Nash/egalitarian and Nash/serial-dictatorship comparisons; none states this new exact relation. Semantic research-record searches used the named objects, synonyms, small-market parameters and optimal-set implications, and did not locate a dominating theorem. This establishes a documented best-knowledge originality assessment, not a proof of worldwide uniqueness. Residual risks include unindexed equivalent enumerations and overlapping conclusions implicit in deeper independent literature not found in the search.

## Value — PASS

The minimum-regret stable-marriage criterion safeguards the worst-off partner, while the Nash product optimizes a global multiplicative measure. Whether maximizing Nash welfare *automatically* attains the best possible worst-off rank is a substantive fairness compatibility question, not an arbitrary numerical statistic. This result gives a universal guarantee at the two smallest nontrivial market sizes, determines the exact incidence of all optimizer-set relationships at three pairs, and produces a sharp four-pair obstruction that persists for arbitrarily large markets. Its boundary clarifies when pursuing Nash welfare needs no additional minimum-regret constraint, and when these fairness objectives can force incompatible decisions. It does not claim a universal guarantee for larger markets, a new efficient algorithm, or extension to ties/incomplete preference lists.
