# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The unit-spindle origin-containment event is equivalent to two explicit spherical caps of possible unit-ball centers failing to overlap. Their sharp angular separation criterion is proved directly. Polar-coordinate rescaling turns the small-body event into a bounded spherical normal-coordinate integral; dominated convergence yields the \((3d-1)\)-st radial moment for every centrally symmetric convex body, without boundary smoothness. The constant is checked against the published planar formula and the dimension-three radial integral. Jensen, an elementary convex tangent inequality, and a concrete degenerating-box family prove all extremal assertions. Independent numerical tests corroborate examples but are not used as proofs.

## Originality
PASS under currently inspected literature, with nonzero historical-priority risk. The closest primary source, Fodor–Montenegro Pinzón–Vígh (2022), calculates the spindle-origin probability for uniform balls and only asserts vanishing under shrinkage, not the general symmetric-body leading moment or the equal-volume extremal theorem. Lángi–Naszódi–Talata (2011/2012) concern deterministic spindle geometry and do not study these stochastic asymptotics. Fodor–Papvári–Vígh (2020) study many-sample approximation asymptotics, not the fixed-two-point small-body absorption limit. Searched mathematical findings concerning short pair distances, circumradius tails and thin-shell convexity use different events and limits. No older equivalent result was identified, but complete historical uniqueness has not been independently established.

## Mathematical value
PASS. Two-point spindle absorption is a motivated analogue of Wendel's probability. Previously the vanishing limit was known in the ball model; the exact exponent and shape-dependent coefficient quantify the rare event for every centrally symmetric convex body, identify a new radial-moment functional, and produce a strict isoperimetric law with a quantitative deficit. The lack of any volume-only upper bound separates the genuine lower extremal geometry from high-aspect-ratio degeneracy. The proof is self-contained and the coefficient reproducible.

## Closest literature and limitations
The main comparison is the 2022 Wendel-spindle paper, DOI 10.1007/s00010-022-00912-3. Other inspected full texts are arXiv:1110.4823 and DOI 10.1112/mtk.12027. The theorem is a leading-order limit, not a finite-size comparison; it requires central symmetry, a Euclidean spindle and exactly two independent uniform points. Historical priority remains provisional; independent validation has not been performed.
