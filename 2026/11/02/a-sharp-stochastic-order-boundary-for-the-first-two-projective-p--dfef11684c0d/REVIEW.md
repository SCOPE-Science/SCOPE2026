# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact survival formulas follow from inclusion–exclusion for independent group draws. The seven-point and thirteen-point line-avoidance coefficient tables were exhaustively computed from explicit projective coordinates with exact integer arithmetic, and finite-time probabilities were separately checked by a direct coverage-state recurrence. The Fano inequality follows from a closed elementary sequence identity. The thirteen-point inequality for all future draw counts follows from the explicit positive monotone lower bound; computations through a finite horizon are supplementary, not a substitute for that proof. The packaged verifier passes 250 exact assertions. No assertion concerns projective-plane orders above three.

## Originality — PASS, with remaining historical-priority risk

Barak-Pelleg and Berend's Theorem 2.1 tabulates expected coverage times for orders two, three, and four, and Section 3 obtains the Fano mean via a Markov-chain argument. Their statements do not give the full pointwise tail comparison or the single sign crossing at order three. Grunbaum and Yaakobi's recovery-set identities concern expectations and related monotonicity questions, and Berend and Sher's group-drawing extremality theorem is expectation-based. The statements do not logically imply the exact survival differences proved here. Targeted scholarly and mathematical-record searches did not identify a directly covering statement. An older or unindexed exact distributional treatment remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Expected coverage time does not determine a stochastic order, so the original geometric counterexample raises a natural stronger question: is the Fano mechanism uniformly slower for every completion-time tail? The answer is yes, but the very next projective plane disproves the extension. Pinpointing the exact four-to-five-draw reversal distinguishes global average slowdown from transient accelerated completion. This resolves a structural boundary for the two smallest geometric counterexamples, rather than recomputing a published expectation.

## Limitations and relationship to literature

This is a complete result only for the first two finite projective planes. The stochastic-order question remains open for higher orders in this work. The proof uses exhaustive, finite exact enumeration for a coefficient table and then an analytical infinite-time inequality. Independent validation and historical-priority adjudication are not represented as completed.
