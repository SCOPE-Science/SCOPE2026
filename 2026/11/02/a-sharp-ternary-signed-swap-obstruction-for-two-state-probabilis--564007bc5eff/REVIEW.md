# Scientific assessment: signed-swap obstruction

**Correctness: PASS.** For two states, acceptance evolves by a scalar affine map. The exact commutator identity and the nonzero-suffix-slope argument force a strict order on letter fixed points for every adjacent swap. Six inequalities for the explicit seven-letter suffix are inconsistent, independently of any earlier prefix. The eight canonical cases and the first failure at seven letters are determined by a complete finite enumeration, verified through two logically distinct implementations that agree on all \(2,538\) surjective inputs. The published uniform three-state upper bound then gives exact maximal state complexity. Remaining risk: transcription or implementation mistakes in the small finite census; independent verification is desirable.

**Originality: PASS, with residual priority uncertainty.** Gill (2024) proves a binary two-state classification and discusses fixed points/commutators for a *pair* of affine maps. Kjos-Hanssen (2026) proves that three states always suffice, not a multi-letter obstruction. The earlier length-four all-alphabet result determines a different cutoff and does not imply this seven-letter signed-order certificate. Targeted research-record and literature queries did not identify the eight-class ternary census or its forbidden-suffix consequence. Non-indexed literature remains a priority risk. The pairwise commutation identity is explicitly credited as prior background, not called new.

**Value: PASS.** The previously described binary classification leaves the multiletter two-state problem structurally incomplete. This result provides a finitely checkable obstruction valid on arbitrary alphabets, proves its exact first surjective-ternary threshold, and upgrades \(48\) explicit suffix patterns into a positive-density family of maximal-complexity words without nonlinear parameter optimization. This is useful as a lower-bound certificate for probabilistic automatic complexity; it is not a full classification and the density bound may be far from optimal.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Gill, arXiv:2402.13376, especially Lemma 4.3 and the binary classification; Kjos-Hanssen, arXiv:2607.26275v1, especially Theorem 1.2 and Proposition 4.1. The signed-order condition is necessary, not sufficient. The length-seven threshold is for this specific obstruction, not for three-state complexity overall. No independent audit or general publication-priority certification has been completed.
