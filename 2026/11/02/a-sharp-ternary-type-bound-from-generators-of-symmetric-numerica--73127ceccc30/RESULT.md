# A sharp ternary type bound from generators of symmetric numerical semigroups

## Finding
Let \(S\subseteq\mathbb N_0\) be a symmetric numerical semigroup with multiplicity \(m(S)\ge4\), Frobenius number \(F\), and its unique minimal generating set \(G\). Define its ternary quotient by \(H=S/3=\{n\in\mathbb N_0:3n\in S\}\), the residue \(r\equiv F\pmod3\), and the generator classes \(G_j=\{g\in G:g\equiv j\pmod3\}\).

If \(r=0\), then \(H\) is symmetric and \(\operatorname{PF}(H)=\{F/3\}\). If \(r=1\) or \(2\), define the finite candidate set
\[
C=G_r\cup\{g+h:g,h\in G_{3-r},\ g\le h\},\qquad C_{<F}=\{c\in C:c<F\}.
\]
Define \(C_{\min}\) to consist exactly of the \(c\in C_{<F}\) for which there is no \(c'\in C_{<F}\) with \(c'<c\) and \(c-c'\in S\cap3\mathbb Z\). Then
\[
\operatorname{PF}(H)=\left\{\frac{F-c}{3}:c\in C_{\min}\right\},\qquad
 t(H)\le |G_r|+\binom{|G_{3-r}|+1}{2}.
\]
This is an exact determination of the full pseudo-Frobenius set, not merely a bound. The bound is attained for arbitrarily large values. For every \(m\ge4\) with \(3\nmid m\) and every \(d\ge3\) with \(3\mid d\) and \(\gcd(m,d)=1\), put
\[
S_{m,d}=\langle m,m+d,\ldots,m+(m-2)d\rangle.
\]
Then \(S_{m,d}\) is symmetric, and
\[
\operatorname{PF}(S_{m,d}/3)=\left\{\frac d3,\frac{2d}3,\ldots,\frac{(m-1)d}3\right\},
\qquad t(S_{m,d}/3)=m-1.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
A numerical semigroup is a cofinite additive submonoid of \(\mathbb N_0\). For a proper numerical semigroup \(T\), a positive gap \(f\notin T\) is pseudo-Frobenius if \(f+t\in T\) for every \(t\in T\setminus\{0\}\). Its type is \(t(T)=|\operatorname{PF}(T)|\). The standard symmetry condition is \(x\in S\iff F-x\notin S\), for every integer \(x\). Multiplicity at least four ensures \(3\notin S\), so \(H\ne\mathbb N_0\), avoiding the exceptional conventional value \(-1\) for the pseudo-Frobenius number of \(\mathbb N_0\). Repeated candidate sums are identified as one integer. All congruences of generators are modulo three.

## Proof
**1. Duality with a residue module.** Let \(S_0=S\cap3\mathbb Z\). For each \(f\ge1\) with \(3f\notin S\), symmetry gives \(x=F-3f\in S\), with \(x\equiv r\pmod3\). For every \(s\in H\setminus\{0\}\), the same symmetry equivalence gives
\[
f+s\in H\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F-3(f+s)=x-3s\notin S.
\]
Therefore \(f\in\operatorname{PF}(H)\) if and only if \(x=F-3f\) belongs to \(S\), has residue \(r\), lies below \(F\), and has no subtraction by a positive element of \(S_0\) that remains in \(S\). Equivalently, \(x\) is minimal among elements of \(S\) with residue \(r\) for the order \(u\preceq v\iff v-u\in S_0\).

**2. Every nonzero residue is generated by at most two atoms over \(S_0\).** Assume \(r\in\{1,2\}\). In a factorization of \(x\in S\) of residue \(r\), either some minimal generator already has residue \(r\), or every generator has residue zero or \(3-r\). In the latter case the number of nonzero-residue factors is congruent to two modulo three, so at least two such factors occur. Thus, in either case, a nonempty submultiset of at most two factors has sum \(c\in C\); its complementary factors sum to an element of \(S_0\). Conversely \(C+S_0\) lies in \(S\) with residue \(r\). Hence the exact module decomposition holds:
\[
\{x\in S:x\equiv r\pmod3\}=C+S_0.
\]
A member \(x<F\) of this module is minimal precisely when \(x\in C_{\min}\). Indeed, a representation \(x=c+z\) with \(z\in S_0\setminus\{0\}\) precludes minimality. If \(x=c\in C\) were nonminimal, then \(x=y+z\) for a smaller same-residue element \(y\in S\) and positive \(z\in S_0\). Writing \(y=c'+z'\), where \(c'\in C\) and \(z'\in S_0\), gives \(c-c'=z+z'\in S_0\setminus\{0\}\); necessarily \(c'<c<F\). This proves the finite-candidate equivalence. Step 1 proves the claimed exact formula and the binomial bound.

**3. The divisible Frobenius residue.** If \(r=0\), take any gap \(f\notin H\). Symmetry of \(S\) gives \(F-3f\in S\), divisible by three. Hence \(F/3-f\in H\). Thus \(H\) is symmetric, has Frobenius number \(F/3\), and has the claimed singleton pseudo-Frobenius set.

**4. Sharpness.** Write \(G=\{m+jd:0\le j\le m-2\}\), where \(\gcd(m,d)=1\). Modulo \(m\), the Apéry set of \(S_{m,d}\) comprises \(0\), the \(m-2\) singleton values \(m+jd\) for \(1\le j\le m-2\), and the final residue representative \(2m+(m-1)d\). To see minimality, any factorization with total generator-index \(J\) in the residue class of \(jd\) has \(J\ge j\); one factor is optimal when \(1\le j\le m-2\), and two factors are necessary and sufficient when \(j=m-1\). The standard Apéry identities therefore yield
\[
F(S_{m,d})=m+(m-1)d,\qquad
 g(S_{m,d})=\frac{m+1+(m-1)d}{2}=\frac{F(S_{m,d})+1}{2}.
\]
Hence \(S_{m,d}\) is symmetric. The displayed generators are all minimal by the same Apéry argument. Because \(3\mid d\) and \(3\nmid m\), every generator has residue \(r\equiv F\equiv m\pmod3\), and no generator lies in residue \(3-r\). The candidate set is just \(G\), every candidate is minimal under subtraction by \(S_0\), and all are below \(F\). Substituting into the exact formula gives the displayed arithmetic progression and type \(m-1\). The number \(m-1\) is unbounded, proving sharpness of the uniform generator-residue bound. \(\square\)

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify.py` computes Apéry sets by residue-class shortest paths and independently constructs semigroup membership by exact integer dynamic programming. It compares all pseudo-Frobenius numbers obtained by direct gap testing with the finite-candidate formula, and checks the sharp family. An exact run reported `VERIFY_OK symmetric_inputs=1122 residue_classes=0:182,1:383,2:557 sharp_families=22 independent_apery_dp=yes`. The finite census covers the stated generator ranges only; the proof above is unrestricted.

## Relationship to prior work
Feihu Liu, *The Frobenius problem for a class of quotients of numerical semigroups* (arXiv:2604.26415, first posted 29 April 2026), derives Apéry, Frobenius and genus formulas for particular quotient families; it does not state this ternary pseudo-Frobenius candidate classification for arbitrary symmetric input. Francesco Strazzanti, *One half of almost symmetric numerical semigroups* (arXiv:1404.4959), studies half-quotients and type; the quotient divisor here is three. Emmanuel Cabanillas, *Quotients of numerical semigroups generated by two numbers* (arXiv:1904.08240), gives complete pseudo-Frobenius formulas when the source is two-generated, so every two-generator specialization here is already within that earlier coverage and is **not** claimed separately as a novelty. Rosales and García-Sánchez's symmetric-half theorem (2008) likewise covers a different divisor. Kou Takahashi's *Defining ideals of some numerical semigroup rings with arithmetic pseudo-Frobenius numbers* (arXiv:2512.14025) treats arithmetic pseudo-Frobenius lists and ring presentations, without the all-symmetric ternary quotient criterion. The new claim is the uniform residue-filter formula and sharp generator-count bound for arbitrary embedding dimension.

## Limitations
The result covers symmetric source semigroups with multiplicity at least four, and the quotient divisor three only. It is not a formula for arbitrary nonsymmetric sources or arbitrary divisors. The sharp construction does not assert that every combination of generator-residue counts achieves equality. General quotient-generator algorithms in earlier literature may supply an indirect route to the same evaluations; historical priority has not been independently established. The finite verifier corroborates, but cannot establish, the universal proof.
