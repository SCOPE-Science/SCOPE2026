# A sharp three-premise obstruction theorem for the unary fragment of minimal evidence logic

## Finding

Let \(\mathrm{LET}^{-}_{F}\) be the minimal logic of evidence and truth with propositional variables and connectives \(\{\wedge,\vee,\neg,\circ\}\). Restrict **premises** to the unary language generated by variables using only \(\neg\) and the classicality connective \(\circ\); satisfiability means extendibility to a genuine bivaluation of the **entire** language, not merely to an assignment on the unary fragment.

**Theorem (exact forbidden triples and sharp three-Helly property).** For any set \(\Gamma\) of unary formulas, finite or infinite, the following are equivalent:

1. No \(\mathrm{LET}^{-}_{F}\) bivaluation makes every member of \(\Gamma\) true.
2. There are a syntactic unary formula \(B\) which is either a propositional variable or begins with \(\circ\), and nonnegative integers \(r,s,t,k\), such that all three formulas

   \[
   \neg^{2r}B,\qquad \neg^{2s+1}B,\qquad
   \neg^{2t}\circ(\neg^k B)
   \]

   belong to \(\Gamma\).

Every such triple is inclusion-minimal inconsistent. Therefore every inclusion-minimal inconsistent theory in this fragment has exactly three members, and the Helly number for satisfiability of unary theories is exactly three (the family \(\{p,\neg p,\circ p\}\) witnesses sharpness). In fact every inconsistent **infinite** unary theory already contains a three-formula inconsistent subset.

For finite input, this criterion supplies both a witness and a deterministic \(O(L)\) satisfiability algorithm, where \(L\) is the aggregate syntactic length, assuming a standard tree representation or linear-time interning of formula nodes. No search through truth assignments or tableau branches is needed.

## Assumptions and scope

The semantics are the bivaluations of Grandinetti and Rodrigues, Definition 3.2. A valuation \(v\) maps each formula to either zero or one and satisfies:

\[
\begin{aligned}
v(A\wedge D)&=v(A)\land v(D),\\
v(A\vee D)&=v(A)\lor v(D),\\
v(\neg(A\wedge D))&=v(\neg A)\lor v(\neg D),\\
v(\neg(A\vee D))&=v(\neg A)\land v(\neg D),\\
v(\neg\neg A)&=v(A),\\
v(\circ A)=1&\ \Longrightarrow\ v(A)+v(\neg A)=1.
\end{aligned}
\]

The last equality is an ordinary integer equality between zero-one values. Crucially, it is **only one-way**: a classical value of \(A\) does not force \(\circ A\). The truth of \(\neg\circ A\) is not the Boolean complement of \(\circ A\) in general.

A **core** is a unary formula whose first symbol is not \(\neg\). Every unary formula \(F\) has a unique syntactic decomposition \(F=\neg^m B\) into an outer negation count \(m\ge0\) and core \(B\). The corresponding **signed core** is \((B,m\bmod2)\). If a core \(C\) begins with \(\circ\), write uniquely \(C=\circ(\neg^k B)\), where \(B\) is the core of its argument; call \(B\) its parent. For a variable core there is no parent. This parent relation strictly reduces formula length and forms a syntactic forest.

**No congruence simplification is applied beneath \(\circ\)**. The formulas \(\circ p\) and \(\circ(\neg\neg p)\) are distinct cores with potentially independently assigned truth values; nevertheless their parent core is the same \(p\). This distinction is essential for the precise statement.

## Proof

**1. Convert semantic requirements into signed core bits.** For each core \(B\), put \(t_B=v(B)\) and \(f_B=v(\neg B)\). The double-negation clause implies by induction on \(m\) that

\[
v(\neg^mB)=
\begin{cases}t_B,&m\text{ even},\\ f_B,&m\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]

There is no general requirement that \(t_B\) and \(f_B\) be complements. All four binary pairs are legal for a variable. For a circle core \(C=\circ(\neg^kB)\), however, the classicality clause implies

\[
t_C=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (t_B,f_B)\in\{(1,0),(0,1)\},
\]

because negation parity merely swaps the two bits. Conversely these are **all** restrictions in the unary reduct.

**2. Every displayed triple is impossible.** If the first two formulas hold, then \(t_B=f_B=1\). If the third holds, then the associated circle-core \(C\) has \(t_C=1\), and its parent \(B\) must have opposite truth and falsity bits. Contradiction.

**3. Construct a bivaluation when no triple is present.** For each core \(B\), let \(T(B)\) mean that \(\Gamma\) contains an even-negation formula with core \(B\), and let \(F(B)\) mean that \(\Gamma\) contains an odd-negation formula with core \(B\). These are semantic requirements, not actual truth values. Choose explicitly

\[
(t_B,f_B)=
\begin{cases}
(1,1),&T(B)\text{ and }F(B),\\
(1,0),&T(B)\text{ and not }F(B),\\
(0,1),&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

Every requirement in \(\Gamma\) is met. If \(t_C=1\) for a circle core \(C=\circ(\neg^kB)\), then the assignment forces \(T(C)\), hence \(\Gamma\) contains a positive (even outer negation) occurrence of this circle core. Absence of the forbidden triple means that \(T(B)\) and \(F(B)\) cannot both hold. Our construction therefore assigns the parent \(B\) either \((1,0)\) or \((0,1)\). Every unary classicality restriction is satisfied, including restrictions along nested circles.

**4. Extend to the full signature, rather than merely postulating a unary model.** Set each atom's positive and negative evidence bits to its prescribed core pair. Inductively interpret negation by swapping the two bits, conjunction by taking Boolean conjunction of the positive bits and Boolean disjunction of the negative bits, and disjunction by Boolean disjunction of positive bits and Boolean conjunction of negative bits. Whenever a \(\circ\)-formula has a unary operand, use its already prescribed core pair. Whenever its operand is *not* unary, assign that circle formula the pair \((0,1)\), making the classicality implication vacuous. Recursion is well-founded by formula length; the unary assignments agree with all outer-negation parities. The resulting positive-coordinate map \(v\) satisfies all six defining bivaluation clauses on every formula and makes every premise in \(\Gamma\) true. Hence the triple criterion is sufficient even for infinite \(\Gamma\).

**5. Minimality and exact complexity.** Since every inconsistent theory contains a displayed triple, a minimal one consists exactly of that triple. Every proper subset of a displayed triple has size at most two and cannot itself contain any displayed triple: the circle formula has strictly larger syntactic length than the two signed copies of its parent, and the signed copies have opposite parity. They are therefore satisfiable by Step 4. The exact Helly number is three.

For finite \(\Gamma\), scan formula nodes to record, for each syntactic core, which of the two outer-negation parities occur. For each core \(C\) of a positively required circle formula, compute its parent's core and test whether both parity flags were recorded there. Report the three corresponding input formulas if so; otherwise report satisfiable. A shared-syntax tree or interned directed acyclic syntax graph gives \(O(L)\) aggregate scanning, parent extraction and dictionary construction with deterministic trie indexing or standard linear-time formula-node interning. Outputting the three premise strings costs at most their aggregate input length. This is a **satisfiability** criterion, not a consequence algorithm for arbitrary target formulas.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone standard-library program `checker.py` independently enumerates all local zero-one semantic assignments on the cores of every rooted parent forest with up to five vertices, and checks **every set of signed positive premises** against the forbidden-triple test. It covers 153 distinct ordered parent forests and 129,444 premise subsets. It also checks all 2,048 subsets of an explicit list of eleven actual unary strings, including negations of circles, nested circles, and two syntactically distinct circles with the same normalized operand. Its output is `ALL_CHECKS_OK`. These finite tests only corroborate the unrestricted constructive proof above; they are not used to infer the theorem for infinite syntax.

To reproduce, run `python3 checker.py` from the package directory. No third-party packages, external data, access credentials or random sampling are used.

## Relationship to prior work

Grandinetti and Rodrigues (2026, arXiv:2610.24203v1), especially Sections 2, 3 and 4, provide precisely the underlying \(\mathrm{LET}^{-}_{F}\) semantics, the rule \(\circ A,A,\neg A\vdash B\), and a general terminating sequent decision method. In particular, inconsistency of an individual classicality triple is **already known**; it is not claimed as novel. The additional statement here is the **converse for arbitrary nested unary theories**: there are no other minimal incompatible collections, even with arbitrarily nested classicality operators, and a linear local scanner suffices.

Carnielli, Frade and Rodrigues (2024, arXiv:2412.10588v1, Sections 2–4) give the related truth/falsity semantics and a general analytic tableau for \(\mathrm{LET}_{F}\), the richer logic containing a non-classicality connective. The present theorem concerns its common \(\{\neg,\circ\}\)-fragment and classifies all minimally inconsistent premise sets in that language, rather than offering another general decision procedure. No inspected theorem in the cited papers states that precise global forbidden-triple characterization.

## Limitations

The claim is specific to **unary premise formulas** under the **minimal** evidence-logic bivaluation semantics. It does not claim a three-Helly theorem once binary connectives are permitted. It says nothing about formulas with \(\bullet\) in their input, general consequence sets, intuitionistic implication, or logics that add congruence or propagation axioms for \(\circ\). In particular, internal double negations in the scope of \(\circ\) cannot generally be normalized by substitution. The precise priority of a potentially equivalent result under earlier logics-of-formal-inconsistency terminology has not been conclusively established.
