# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The inscribed-angle/gap relation and the exact joint density of the two consecutive angular gaps reduce the CDF to an explicit integral over the intersection of three gap inequalities. A complete polynomial integration yields the claimed positive-part expression for all supported angles. The lower support is sharp by explicit equal-gap or endpoint-span configurations. Separate local evaluations give the quadratic/cubic transitions; no numerical limit is used to prove a universal assertion. The standalone code is supplementary and completed successfully.

## Originality

PASS, with provisional historical priority. Khan (arXiv:1704.05913, 2017) treats area-uniform random triangles in convex planar regions, not arclength-uniform samples on fixed circular arcs. Kabluchko (arXiv:1909.13335, 2019) studies expected angles and face numbers for full-sphere/full-ball random simplices, not proper-arc maxima. Greenwell (arXiv:2608.06591, 2026) analyzes full concentric circles and explicitly explains the classical full-circle uniform-angle-simplex model. The full-circle acute probability \(1/4\) is therefore not new and is credited. No retrieved exact table or primary statement covers the complete fixed-arc CDF and its critical onset. An older result under conditioned-spacings language remains a priority risk.

## Mathematical value

PASS. A fixed angular aperture is a natural one-parameter model for sampling visible sections of a circular boundary. The result is the complete maximum-angle probability law, not merely a low-order moment. Its exact support, the acute threshold \(L=\pi\), and the unique cubic-onset aperture \(L=4\pi/3\) classify two distinct geometric transitions and explain their local scaling.

## Scope and limitations

The result applies to exactly three independent arclength-uniform samples on a Euclidean circular arc. Nonuniform densities, general curves, and random triangles sampled from planar area are not addressed. Numerical checks are not infinite proofs. Historical priority is provisional.
