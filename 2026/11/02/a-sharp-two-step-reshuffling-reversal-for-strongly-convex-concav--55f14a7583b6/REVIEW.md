# Scientific review: two-step reshuffling reversal

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is an exact statement about a single two-call epoch on two homogeneous strongly convex–concave component games. The component saddle operators have the form \(H\pm K\), where \(H\) is symmetric positive definite and \(K\) is skew-symmetric. Thus their explicit simultaneous gradient descent–ascent maps are adjoints. The trace computation comparing four IID products with the two permutation products gives \(\frac14\|MM^\top-M^\top M\|_F^2\) **without** an asymptotic approximation. The block commutator identity gives precisely \(2h^4\|AC-CB\|_F^2\), with the strictness condition \(AC\ne CB\). Every hypothesis and dimension is explicit. For deterministic starts the planar sign formula is derived separately rather than inferred from an isotropic average. The exact contractivity certificate establishes a nontrivial stable-step witness. The standard-library verifier passed 1011 exact-rational assertions on the extracted scientific package. Risk: finite checks cannot certify an infinite theorem; the quantified conclusion rests on the fully displayed algebraic proof, and an independent proof audit has not been performed.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The strongest close source is Das, Schölkopf and Muehlebach, *Sampling without Replacement Leads to Faster Rates in Finite-Sum Minimax Optimization* (arXiv:2206.02953, 2022), particularly Algorithm 1, Section 3.2 and Appendix C, plus its multi-epoch quadratic experiments in Section 5. It gives general convergence bounds and longer-run comparisons, not the exact same-step, one-epoch isotropic comparison, the dimension-free nonnormality equality, or the starting-direction cone. Recht and Ré (2012) and Israel, Krahmer and Ward (2014) establish closely related product comparisons **for positive-semidefinite factors**, whereas simultaneous game gradient maps with nonzero skew coupling are nonsymmetric and fall outside that condition. Koloskova and colleagues (arXiv:2305.19259v4, Sections 3–5) study ordinary minimization; their descent analysis does not imply the saddle-point trace reversal. These differences are comparisons of actual hypotheses, implications, and objectives, not title differences. Risk: the general real-matrix trace identity is elementary and could have appeared elsewhere; a specialized earlier finite-epoch game/product computation may subsume this application. Failure to locate one is not proof of absolute novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The criterion \(AC-CB\) gives a structural, directly checkable obstruction to the attractive but overbroad inference that a random reshuffling pass must improve finite-budget error. It holds in every primal/dual dimension and remains strict even when both component maps are contractive; the exact planar cone simultaneously exhibits starting states where reshuffling helps and hurts. This is useful for interpreting evaluation protocols that average over initial directions or compare fixed early-epoch budgets in strongly convex–concave games. The value lies in the sharp dimension-free sign law and its game-theoretic instability boundary, not in a new general random-matrix trace inequality. Risk: it does not predict the ordering after multiple epochs, for optimized step sizes, or for arbitrary non-adjoint component pairs; those limitations are expressly excluded.
