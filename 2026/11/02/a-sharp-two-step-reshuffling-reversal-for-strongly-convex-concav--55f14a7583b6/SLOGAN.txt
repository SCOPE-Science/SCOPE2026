For a two-component strongly convex–concave quadratic game with opposite bilinear couplings, two-step random reshuffling has exactly \(2h^4\|AC-CB\|_F^2\) more isotropic mean-squared saddle error than sampling with replacement at the same step, even when both individual updates contract. In the planar case, a sharp starting-direction cone determines whether reshuffling helps or harms, so finite-epoch sampling order has a curvature–coupling obstruction invisible to generic asymptotic rate comparisons.
