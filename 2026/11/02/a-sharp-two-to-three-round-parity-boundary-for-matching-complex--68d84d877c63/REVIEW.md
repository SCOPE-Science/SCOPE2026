# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness
PASS. A two-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé strategy follows from a complete enumeration of possible order relations between an initial face and one subsequent face. The explicit rank-three sentence holds precisely when the number of unmatched vertices is two. Its optimality follows because all structures with at least four underlying vertices are rank-two equivalent. Finite independent enumeration agrees through order nine.

## Originality
PASS, qualified. The mathematical statement differs in logical resource, objects, and conclusion from the topological connectivity, homology, and torsion results of Shareshian–Wachs (arXiv:math/0409054). Searches for matching-complex face posets with small-rank first-order parity and logically equivalent formulations yielded no dominating result, but unindexed prior work remains a risk. Relevant work on other matching-complex constructions and on general finite posets does not imply this exact boundary without additional hypotheses.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The exact parity-definability threshold is motivated by the known expressibility tension between counting parity and bounded-rank first-order logic. Unlike a mere graph parity restatement, it establishes that the canonical matching-complex face representation loses *all* differences between sufficiently large ground-set sizes at rank two, yet exposes ground-set parity by a sharp, quantified order-extension property at rank three. The result is a concise structural boundary, not a classification of all rank-three theories.

## Limitations
Complete graphs, full inclusion face posets including the empty face, rank at most three for the stated boundary. No complete rank-three classification, no independent audit, and no exhaustive global priority certification.
