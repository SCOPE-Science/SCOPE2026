# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The reduction to \(x'=Px\pm cD^{-1}(\mathbf e_v-\mathbf e_u)\) is derived directly from the original synchronous model, including the strict threshold and self-inclusion. The connected Laplacian Poisson equation gives the only possible fixed states in each strict antagonistic order chamber. The unit edge is a bridge precisely when its electrical resistance equals one, excluding full receptivity in that case; otherwise the maximum principle gives strict interiority. Since the self-loop random walk is reversible and primitive, its mean-zero spectrum is strictly within the unit disk, giving local exponential attraction. Exact-rational graph checks and exact orbit checks passed; none are substituted for the proof. The neutral translation direction is explicitly factored out.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Kann–Feng [1] defines the update and proves several convergence and width properties, not this resistance/bridge criterion. A prior complete-graph hostile-degree condition concerns universal preservation of full receptivity, rather than existence of two locally stable, fully receptive polarized profiles on arbitrary graphs. Altafini–Ceragioli [2] and He–Liu–Hu–Fang [3] use different signed confidence force rules. Homs-Dones–Devriendt–Lambiotte [4] supplies analogous continuous-time resistance methods but does not imply discrete-time strict-confidence receptivity and attraction for this model. No searched mathematical finding or reviewed source demonstrated dominating same mathematical model coverage. Residual risk: possible specialized equivalents elsewhere, and the inaccessible full text of [3] was not used to assert full-document absence.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A single antagonistic edge is the minimal natural perturbation of an attractive social network. The theorem converts the qualitative question whether such a disagreement can be sustained without losing communication into a complete all-graph bridge-versus-cycle classification, with exact polarization amplitude, strict receptivity margin, and exponential local attraction. It shows that redundant contact paths—not simply the existence of hostility—permit persistent full communication, and directly quantifies how network density buffers disagreement. The result is not a generic restatement of convergence of a linear averaging map because the nonlinear model's switching cutoffs and hostile orientation must first be resolved, and the bridge threshold is sharp.

## Closest literature and limits

The strongest directly relevant source is [1], whose complete model statement and analytical results were inspected. The related continuous-time electrical treatment [4] was read for both its hypotheses and analytical scope; [2] and [3] were checked for force-rule equivalence. The paper [3] was not available in full in this review and is recorded as residual risk. The theorem is local, one-negative-edge, fixed full-receptivity only. General switching and global convergence remain unproved.
