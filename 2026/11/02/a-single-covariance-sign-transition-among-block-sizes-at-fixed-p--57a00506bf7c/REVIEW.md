# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The main identity follows by marking one block and deleting it, after which the remaining partition has exactly the needed uniform law. Strict monotonicity of the singleton mean with fixed positive block count is proved from the coefficient representation of Stirling numbers and a self-contained strict log-concavity/convolution argument. The deterministic excess-count identity forces both signs and rules out more than one zero. The two-block boundary and uniform-occupancy transfer are covered explicitly. The exact program performs 499,367 successful tests, including independent restricted-growth-word enumeration and an independent uniform-occupancy enumeration. The finite tests are corroboration only; the algebraic/analytic proof supplies the universal quantifiers.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

Bóna–Mező (arXiv:0705.2734) derive classical nonsingleton Stirling counting and real-rootedness, not a fixed-block-count covariance ordering across sizes. Barbour–Gnedin (arXiv:0809.4387) analyze covariance and limit laws for infinite occupancy without the present exact conditioning on the occupied-box count. Johnson–Goldschmidt (2006) give general log-concavity closure tools, but the strict conditional-covariance sign classification and excess-balance argument are additional deductions. Closely related results about unconditional richness–frequency covariance cannot imply this conditional theorem: conditioning changes the relevant covariance and its sign. Results for noncrossing set partitions concern a different law, without an implication to uniform unrestricted partitions. Search and source inspection found no exact covering theorem. Residual historical-priority risk remains from older multivariate occupancy factorial-moment literature and possible implicit specializations.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Conditional richness is a natural sampling design in occupancy, species-frequency and set-partition models. The theorem gives a complete all-size sign classification for singleton interaction with every feasible nonsingleton size, including the exceptional two-block plateau, exactly one possible vanishing covariance, and invariance under changing the total number of equally likely boxes. Its positive large-size covariances are not implied by ordinary negative dependence heuristics or unconditional covariance statements. It is a finite-sample structural result rather than a table or a single moment identity.

## Scope and limitations

The theorem does not assert a simple asymptotic or elementary formula for the integer transition location, does not order unnormalized covariance magnitudes, and does not extend to arbitrary nonuniform boxes. Full historical priority has not been established. The scientific assessment is not a claim of external certification.
