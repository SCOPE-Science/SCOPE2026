# A square-class dichotomy for Mordell–Weil groups of the octahedral elliptic pencil

## Finding
Let \(k\) be any field of characteristic zero and let \(r\in k^\times\). Consider the elliptic curve over \(k(t)\)
\[
E_r:\quad S^2=-27R^3+r(18t-4r^2)R^2+t^2(r^2-4t)R.
\]
Fix \(\alpha\in\overline{k}\) with \(\alpha^2=-r\). Then its entire geometric Mordell–Weil group is
\[
E_r(\overline{k}(t))=\mathbb Z\,Q_r\oplus (\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)\,T,
\qquad Q_r=\left(\frac{rt}{9},\frac{2\alpha}{9}t(3t-r^2)\right),\quad T=(0,0).
\]
The generator has Shioda height \(\langle Q_r,Q_r\rangle=1/12\). More sharply, its descent to the ground field has the exact square-class dichotomy
\[
\boxed{E_r(k(t))\cong
\begin{cases}
\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,&-r\in(k^\times)^2,\\
\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,&-r\notin(k^\times)^2.
\end{cases}}
\]
The first case is generated by \(Q_r,T\), and the second consists exactly of the zero section and \(T\). Thus for \(k=\mathbb R\), the rank is zero when \(r>0\) and one when \(r<0\); for rational nonzero \(r\), the rank over \(\mathbb Q(t)\) is one exactly when \(-r\) is a rational square.

## Assumptions and scope
Every assertion is for \(\operatorname{char}(k)=0\), \(r\ne0\), and the function field \(k(t)\) with the displayed zero section at infinity. The field \(\overline{k}\) is an algebraic closure; the statement concerns rational sections of the relatively minimal Jacobian elliptic surface, not arbitrary rational points in individual specialized fibers. The height normalization is Shioda's intersection pairing on sections of a rational elliptic surface. No assertion is made for \(r=0\), positive characteristic, or specialized constant fibers.

## Proof
**Weierstrass model and fibers.** The invertible change \(X=-27R\), \(Y=27S\) gives
\[
Y^2=X^3+A X^2+B X,\qquad A=r(18t-4r^2),\quad B=27t^2(4t-r^2).
\]
Its Weierstrass discriminant and the quadratic-factor discriminant are, by direct expansion,
\[
\Delta=16B^2(A^2-4B)
=186624\,t^4(r^2-4t)^2(r^2-3t)^3,
\qquad A^2-4B=16(r^2-3t)^3.
\]
The finite minimal models therefore have fibers \(I_4\) at \(t=0\), \(I_2\) at \(t=r^2/4\), and \(I_3\) at \(t=r^2/3\). For \(u=1/t\), set \(X'=u^2X\), \(Y'=u^3Y\); then
\[
A'=18ru-4r^3u^2,\qquad B'=108u-27r^2u^2.
\]
The local discriminant has order three and \(c_4'=16((A')^2-3B')\) has order one, yielding type \(III\) at infinity. These are the four fibers of a rational elliptic surface with \(\chi(\mathcal O)=1\), as also recorded for the octahedral surface in Gegelia–van Straten. The nonidentity root lattices are \(A_3,A_1,A_2,A_1\), respectively. Shioda–Tate and \(\rho=10\) give geometric rank \(10-2-(3+1+2+1)=1\).

**Exhibited section and its local heights.** In the \((X,Y)\)-model the proposed free section is
\[
\mathcal Q=(-3rt,\ 6\alpha t(3t-r^2)),\quad \alpha^2=-r.
\]
Substitution verifies the equation identically. It never meets the zero section (including at infinity in the minimal model), so \(\mathcal Q\cdot O=0\). We can compute component contributions after extending constants and setting \(r=1\), since all nonzero parameters become geometrically isomorphic by scaling \(t=r^2v\), \(R=r^3R_1\), \(S^2=r^9S_1^2\).

At the \(I_4\) fiber \(t=0\), \(\mathcal Q\) reduces to the Weierstrass node \((0,0)\), hence maps to a nonidentity component. Its double has exact coordinates
\[
2\mathcal Q=(-9t^2,\,-9\mathrm i\,t^2(3t-1))
\]
when \(r=1\), and also reduces to the node. Thus the component index of \(\mathcal Q\) in \(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\) must be odd, contributing \(3/4\). At \(t=1/3\), the specialized cubic is \(Y^2=X(X+1)^2\), and \(\mathcal Q\) specializes to its node \((-1,0)\); the \(I_3\) contribution is \(2/3\). At \(t=1/4\), \(\mathcal Q\) has \(X=-3/4\) and is smooth, contributing zero from \(I_2\). At infinity, its minimal coordinates vanish at the singular point of the type-\(III\) Weierstrass fiber, hence contribute \(1/2\). Shioda's formula gives
\[
\langle\mathcal Q,\mathcal Q\rangle
=2+2(\mathcal Q\cdot O)-\left(\frac34+\frac23+0+\frac12\right)
=\frac1{12}>0.
\]

**Primitivity and full geometric group.** The section \(T=(0,0)\) has order two. The trivial lattice \(U\oplus A_3\oplus A_1\oplus A_2\oplus A_1\) has absolute determinant \(4\cdot2\cdot3\cdot2=48\). The Néron–Severi lattice of a rational elliptic surface is unimodular. Shioda's discriminant formula therefore gives \(\det(\mathrm{MW}_{\mathrm{free}})=m^2/48\), where \(m\) is the order of the full geometric torsion subgroup. Since the rank is one, if \(\mathcal Q=nG+T'\) for a primitive generator \(G\) and a torsion point \(T'\), the exact height gives \(1/12=n^2m^2/48\), hence \(|n|m=2\). As \(m\ge2\) from the exhibited \(T\), necessarily \(m=2\) and \(|n|=1\). Thus \(\mathcal Q\) generates the free part and \(T\) the entire torsion part. This establishes the complete geometric group independently of assuming the classification's Mordell–Weil group entry.

**Descent.** The full group is generated by sections defined over \(k(\alpha)(t)\). If \(-r\) is a square in \(k\), both generators descend. If not, an element of \(\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{k}/k)\) sends \(\alpha\) to \(-\alpha\), hence \(Q_r\) to \(-Q_r\), while fixing \(T\). For a geometric section \(nQ_r+\epsilon T\), invariance therefore forces \(2nQ_r=0\), so \(n=0\); conversely \(O,T\) are defined over \(k(t)\). This proves the dichotomy.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` uses exact SymPy polynomial and rational arithmetic. Executing `python verify.py` returns `VERIFY_OK` and `UNIFORM_PARAMETER_VERIFY_OK`. It checks the defining equation and discriminant for a symbolic nonzero parameter, the explicit duplication and tripling identities at the normalized parameter, local specializations at all finite singular fibers, the transformed coefficients and valuations at infinity, and the height arithmetic. The infinite-field conclusion is deduced mathematically by Shioda–Tate, the unimodular-lattice discriminant formula, and Galois descent; finite example checks do not replace these arguments.

## Relationship to prior work
Gegelia and van Straten, *The platonic elliptic surfaces* (arXiv:2608.25644, first version 2026-08-26), construct the octahedral invariant equation and state its complex geometric fiber configuration and abstract Mordell–Weil lattice, including the two-torsion. Those results supply essential context, but their fiber tables do not specify this parameter-dependent ground-field Mordell–Weil classification. Oguiso–Shioda classify the geometric rank-one lattice of rational elliptic surfaces. Jain (2010, Theorem 1) characterizes minimal-height sections of complex elliptic surfaces with two-torsion up to isomorphism. The underlying height bound, lattice and integrality patterns are therefore **not** new claims. The claim here is the explicit square-class descent for the real/arithmetical platonic reflection-invariant model, including the complete section group over every characteristic-zero ground field. Generic complex classifications alone do not identify the Galois action on their generators. An older equivalent descent computation is a residual historical-priority risk.

## Limitations
The parameter must be nonzero and the characteristic zero. The statement concerns the generic fiber over \(k(t)\), not fibers at individual \(t\) values. Nothing is asserted about the tetrahedral or icosahedral surfaces, height behavior under nonconstant base changes, or the real topology of individual fibers. Historical priority is not independently certified; the review accompanying this result is not an independent audit.
