For Brownian search with a phase-preserving, randomly switching resetting intensity, the mean time to hit a distant target diverges as \(a/(2\sqrt{D\nu})\) when the environmental switching rate \(\nu\) vanishes, yet approaches constant-rate resetting with a computable first correction as \(\nu\to\infty\). When \(a\sqrt{\rho/(2D)}>1.593624260\ldots\), switching at some **finite** rate strictly outperforms arbitrarily rapid switching, and a global finite-speed optimum is guaranteed.
