Subdividing every edge of an *unequal* complete bipartite graph strictly lowers its sharp negative-type exponent below the known unsubdivided value, even though balanced subdivisions retain that value. For fixed thin side \(m\), the critical Gaussian-kernel exponent decays as \(\tau_{m,n}\sim (m+1)/\{n[(4m-1)\log2-2m\log3]\}\), while the original biclique cutoff stays bounded away from zero.
