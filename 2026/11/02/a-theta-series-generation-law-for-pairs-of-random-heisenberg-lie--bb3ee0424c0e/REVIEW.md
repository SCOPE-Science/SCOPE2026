# Assessment of the random-subalgebra generation law

**Correctness: PASS.** Generation of the Heisenberg Lie algebra is equivalent, without a characteristic restriction, to the projections spanning the nondegenerate symplectic quotient. The established graph/central decomposition shows that a sampled subalgebra avoiding the central derived line has probability \(O_q((n+1)q^{-n^2/3+n+3/8})\). The Gaussian-binomial calculation gives the exact spanning kernel conditional on two projection dimensions, and uniform tail bounds rigorously justify the theta-series limit and the stated \(O_q(q^{-n/2})\) rate. The finite verifier exhaustively checks five independent small-field configurations and separately tests twelve finite approximations; none is substituted for an infinite proof.

**Originality: PASS relative to inspected sources.** Eick–Moede compute finite-field subalgebras for bounded-dimensional simple Lie algebras. Glasby–Niemeyer–Praeger give an exact fixed-complementary-dimension ordinary-subspace probability (arXiv:2109.10015, Lemma 2.1) and bounds for complementary nondegenerate subspaces at fixed dimensions; the present distribution samples *all* dimensions and does not impose nondegeneracy. The published higher-Heisenberg note already gives the exact individual subalgebra census, which is **not** claimed as new here; its pair invariant is permutability, not generation. The published Grassmann harmonic analysis note gives a fixed-rank disjointness count and infinite-product limit, not the unrestricted theta mixture. No inspected theorem states or directly evaluates the present Lie-generation probability. The structural link to known counts means an equivalent, terminology-hidden consequence remains possible.

**Value: PASS.** Generating the full Lie algebra by two sampled subalgebras is a natural global invariant not determined by their individual dimensions alone. The exact reduction, strictly between-zero-and-one theta-series constant, and quantified universality explain why the rare exceptional subalgebra type does not alter pairwise generation, despite the nontrivial symplectic bracket. The explicit limiting values are useful for finite-field sampling and for comparing generation with the separately studied permutability degree. This is not a claim of independent publication priority.

**Limits and closest literature:** The results apply only at fixed field size for the nondegenerate Heisenberg family, not to arbitrary two-step Lie algebras or fixed-rank sampling. A broader random Grassmannian or extraspecial-group theorem may implicitly contain the statement; independent literature and mathematical review remain necessary.
