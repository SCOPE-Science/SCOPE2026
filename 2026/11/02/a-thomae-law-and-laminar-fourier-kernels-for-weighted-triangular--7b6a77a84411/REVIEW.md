# Research assessment: weighted triangular Fourier-zero density

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem explicitly restricts to odd-prime scaling, the probability weights \(1-2t,t,t\), \(0<t<1/2\), and integer frequencies. Each Fourier transform factors into a normally convergent product of probability mask values. The summable tail deviation proves that a zero needs an exactly vanishing factor; neither a small infinite-product limit nor finite numerical sampling is mistaken for such a factor. For odd-order roots, a nonzero real sum of two unimodular numbers requires conjugacy; the cosine equation identifies the unique primitive cyclotomic pair and establishes its exact conductor. The valuation fixes exactly one cancelling level. Counting the two disjoint residue cylinders at each level and bounding the tail proves the centered-square density; the density \(2/[p^{2(k-1)}(p^2-1)]\) is the convergent series, not an estimate. Modulo reduction proves the laminar inclusion/disjointness alternatives, and conductor growth proves the Thomae continuity criterion.

The standalone checker tests all residues for specific prime powers and the finite-level counts. The golden-ratio instance has a separate exact symbolic identity. Those experiments are not substituted for the infinite analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS (bounded literature comparison)
Lam–Leung (arXiv:math/9511209) concerns vanishing sums of roots of unity with integral coefficients; it does not imply a continuous-weight Fourier-null density law. Dutkay–Jorgensen (arXiv:math/0604547) develops the affine-IFS Fourier framework, including products and Fourier spectra; its main questions concern bases and equal-weight Hadamard constructions, not the entire exceptional-weight stratification, precise lattice density, and nesting of weighted convolution nullspaces. Dutkay–Ranasinghe (DOI:10.1016/j.jmaa.2017.12.055) explicitly defines its fractal IFS measures with equal digit weights and uses *weighted* to qualify exponential frame functions, a distinct parameter. Recent studies of the planar Sierpiński measure focus on equal-weight spectrality. The closest identified prior finite-group results classify weighted three-atom zeros on dyadic cyclic groups and three-sparse support over elementary-abelian groups; neither implies the all-conductor odd-prime triangular IFS theorem. The known equal-weight ternary simplex density is a special case and is not advanced as new.

Strichartz's *Self-similar measures and their Fourier transforms. II* (1993) is particularly relevant general prior work. Its abstract/metadata were inspected, but full text could not be obtained, so precise comparison with an unstated theorem in that paper remains a residual originality risk. Broad published work on weighted self-similar distributions could contain an equivalent specialization. No inspected accessible theorem decisively covers this specific complete density/laminar claim.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The object is the natural family of nonuniform triangular affine-IFS probability measures, and the invariant is the annihilating integer-frequency density of the associated torus convolution operator. The theorem classifies *every* symmetric positive digit weight at *every* odd-prime scale, giving both the exact p-adic conductor dependence and finite-level counts. It also identifies a dense countable set of exceptional weights, a sharply discontinuous spectral-density law, and an inclusion/orthogonality hierarchy between distinct convolution nullspaces. These are structural phenomena beyond checking a single cyclotomic cancellation or re-expressing the known uniform-weight case; the parameter-to-spectrum and nested-kernel behavior are relevant to Fourier analysis of fractal measures.

The theorem does not cover arbitrary three independent digit weights, even bases, noninteger frequencies, or existence of spectral orthonormal bases. The finite checker is a corroborating device, not a certification of the infinite argument.
