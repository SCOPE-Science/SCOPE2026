# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The record and peak indicators are precisely defined on a uniform one-line permutation. Independence outside the three adjacent positions follows respectively from disjoint iid coordinates or independence of the next insertion rank from the internal ordering of the previous prefix. Each of the three nonzero entries is obtained by an exact conditional integral. Summation telescopes to the stated finite covariance. Standard two-dependent peak-moment counts and independent record indicators yield the correlation. The accompanying exact rational verifier passed 150 local and aggregate comparisons for 46,230 enumerated permutations through size eight; the proof covers all finite sizes without reliance on those checks.

## Originality

PASS on the inspected evidence, subject to historical priority limitations. Gnedin (2006) describes record ranks and coherent permutation laws; Kasraoui (2012) solves an extremal peak-set problem; Baril and Petrossian (2015) count equivalence classes from separate record and descent structures. Warren and Seneta (1996) study peaks marginally, although their complete text could not be examined. None of the inspected material states or logically implies without additional new mixed-moment calculations the precise three-diagonal local record–peak covariance and its finite-size and limiting totals. The complete historical corpus has not been exhausted, so an equivalent older computation may exist. This is not a claim of independently certified novelty.

## Mathematical value

PASS. Records quantify the emergence of global maxima in rank sequences, whereas peaks are local changes of order and form a classical distribution-free randomness diagnostic. The exact kernel shows rigorously that only three record positions interact with each peak, including a negative neighboring term and a positive central term, which cancel almost completely. The residual positive contribution decays cubically in the peak location and sums to a strictly positive nonzero limit, even as correlation decays. Both the local support theorem and this finite-versus-asymptotic dependence distinction supply motivated information unavailable from the marginals alone.

## Risks and boundaries

The model requires uniform permutation ranks, not tied or heterogeneously distributed observations. Published sources with abstract-only access present historical-priority uncertainty; no equivalence was established from those abstracts. The verification program is an exact finite check, not an external or infinite-domain certification.
