# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The four-state count process includes separate independent deaths and direct common resets; its explicit stationary balance equations normalize to the claimed rational expression. Its derivative reduces to a linear function of catastrophe synchronization, and the two analytically defined rescue thresholds order strictly, including the boundary cases. The universal conclusion follows from positivity of the stationary denominator and the derivative signs, not finite experiments. The independent exact-rational `CHECKER.py` stationary solution passed 18,755 assertions.

## Originality — PASS within inspected literature, with residual risks

The claim is not a statement that spatial synchrony or rescue effects are new. The closest available primary work concerns empirical two-to-four patch persistence (Holyoak 2000), stochastic moment-closure persistence (Keeling 2000), and stochastic conservation interventions (Etienne--Heesterbeek 2001). The latter was accessible only through its abstract, so its full mathematical coverage cannot be excluded. A previous two-patch common-catastrophe occupancy result gives only monotone synchronization effects; the three-patch nonmonotone *interior minimum* is neither a restatement nor a parameter substitution of that result. The exact formula and thresholds were not found in the available comparisons, but a prior equivalent common-shock Markov-chain theorem remains possible.

## Value — PASS

This is a complete, natural classification of the probability of simultaneous occupancy in a minimal spatial model with rescue and matched individual disaster hazards. The three-patch interior minimum demonstrates a genuine qualitative change from the two-patch case. It identifies when neither fully independent nor fully common catastrophes give the lowest three-patch joint occupancy. It does not prove trends for extinction times, heterogeneous patch networks, or field ecology.

## Limits of scientific verification

The full manuscript of the closely relevant Etienne--Heesterbeek stochastic conservation analysis could not be inspected; available material does not decide any possible indirect implication from a more general model. Holyoak (1996, 2000) was assessed through accessible article information/abstracts; Keeling (2000) was read in full on the publisher's openly accessible page. The finite checker supports, but does not replace, the self-contained proof.
