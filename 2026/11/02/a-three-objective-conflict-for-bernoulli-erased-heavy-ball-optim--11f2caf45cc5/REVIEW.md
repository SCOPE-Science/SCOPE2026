# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The single claim is the joint classification in the finding. Independent Bernoulli increments give the three-moment matrix and the stated exact quadratic-energy identity. The energy matrix is positive definite throughout the stability region, and two-step observability makes dissipation geometric. Conversely, the impossible stationary covariance at nonpositive defect establishes necessity, with the critical equality separately covered by conserved positive energy. Derivatives of the exact stability ceiling and of the stationary variance denominator establish the unique optimizers. A positive-probability all-erasure event proves a universal lower bound at every finite horizon, and two explicitly conditioned observation patterns prove uniqueness. The standalone verifier checked the formulas using exact symbolic algebra and 696 exact rational test groups. As usual, finite checks alone do not establish any quantified theorem.

**Originality — PASS with a specialized-scope qualification.** The 2017 stochastic two-step criterion (Stamatiou, arXiv:1704.08515) is mathematically broader than the isolated stability test. Loizou–Richtárik (arXiv:1710.10737) already prove stochastic-heavy-ball convergence and establish that their sufficient L2 rate bound is not improved by momentum. Thus neither generic two-step stability nor qualitative stochastic momentum nonacceleration is claimed as new. The compared inspected treatments do not give the **combined sharp Bernoulli-erasure phase classification** consisting of (a) the uniquely optimized nonzero momentum stability-capacity window for success probabilities exceeding one half, (b) a different uniquely optimal momentum for stationary additive update noise, and (c) a unique, all-horizon, zero-initial-velocity optimum with a one-hit annihilation proof. Exact models, not title matches, were compared. A specialized earlier theorem covering the same joint classification remains a genuine residual possibility. The conclusion is a self-review priority assessment, not independent publication certification.

**Value — PASS.** A gradient-availability probability is a natural controllable numerical resource in randomized iteration and lost-gradient systems. The theorem exposes a practical objective mismatch: increasing the largest stable learning rate need not improve any finite-budget mean-square error, whereas reducing persistent update noise can favor a third parameter choice. The probability-one-half phase boundary and strict illustrative rational values answer an exact optimization question and prevent misleading interpretation of a stability-ceiling gain as statistical acceleration. The result is intentionally restricted to scalar interpolation, constant parameters, unbiased Bernoulli erasures, independent additive **update** noise and zero-velocity initial data for its finite-horizon part.

**Nearest published literature and scientific limits.** Bollapragada–Chen–Ward, arXiv:2206.07553, provide the stochastic heavy-ball and minibatch setting. Loizou–Richtárik, arXiv:1710.10737, provide a close stochastic heavy-ball quadratic framework with general convergence bounds. Stamatiou, arXiv:1704.08515, gives stronger generic two-step mean-square stability theory. Gitman–Lang–Zhang–Xiao, arXiv:1910.13962, treat stationary noise for a different, state-independent additive-noise stochastic-gradient model. Kidambi et al., arXiv:1803.05591, discuss momentum nonacceleration with different constructions. The present proof is limited to its precise Bernoulli oracle and says nothing about general strongly convex problems, changing step schedules, arbitrary matrix spectra or finite-precision implementation. It is also not a claim to have invented the generic second-moment matrix.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
