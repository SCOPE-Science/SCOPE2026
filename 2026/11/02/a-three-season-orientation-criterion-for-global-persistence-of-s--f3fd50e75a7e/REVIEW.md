# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The identities for calendar trace difference, common determinant, and denominator under composition have been derived directly, checked symbolically, and replayed with exact-rational tests. The crucial global argument is the *closed form* \(x_k=M^kx_0/[1+C^{\mathsf T}\sum_{j<k}M^jx_0]\), not a simulation. Strict positivity of the three-step matrix supplies a simple Perron eigenvalue and a positive leading projection of every nonzero cone state, covering extinction, critical \(1/k\) asymptotics, persistence and uniqueness. Exact rational parameters give a strict viability reversal. Risk: these conclusions rely on a single common density denominator in each phase; they do not extend automatically to independently saturated matrix entries.

## Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority

The closest inspected full research paper, Mertens et al. (2006), treats general linear periodic matrix sensitivities, cyclic permutations and transient sizes, but not an explicit three-season two-stage chiral determinant paired with nonlinear globally attracting saturation. Caswell and Shyu (2012; openly adapted in Caswell 2019, chapter 8) covers nonlinear periodic matrix sensitivity and illustrates other stage models, not the precise determinant/closed-form classification. Cushing and Ackleh (2011) provides broad net reproduction thresholds and period-two applications; access to its full text was limited. published-finding corpus and accessible previous research were compared using source, domain, aliases, calendar reversal, common-denominator vector saturation, and implication searches. General noncyclic order dependence is already established and **is not** itself original. Residual risk: an unidentified general matrix-fractional or determinant rearrangement result could subsume the combined theorem, so independent archival priority remains open.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A natural and consequential ecological decision is whether changing the order of available environments affects ultimate population survival. This theorem completely resolves that decision in a biologically interpretable two-stage, three-season saturating model: a single determinant sets direction, a trace-plus-loop expression gives the sharp extinction boundary, and all initial nonzero populations share a global fate, even exactly at criticality. The rational example shows a qualitative persistence switch that cannot be read off from independent season averages. It is a genuine complete regime classification, not a reparametrization or numerically observed ranking.
