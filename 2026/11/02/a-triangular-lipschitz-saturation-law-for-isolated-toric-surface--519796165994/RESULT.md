# A triangular Lipschitz-saturation law for isolated toric surfaces

## Finding
For each integer \(n\geq3\), consider the complex analytic semigroup algebra
\[
A_n=\mathbb C\{u,v^n,v^{n+1},\ldots,v^{2n-1},u^nv,u^nv^2,\ldots,u^nv^{n-1}\}
\subset B=\mathbb C\{u,v\}.
\]
Its Lipschitz saturation within its normalization \(B\) is exactly
\[
\boxed{A_n^{\mathrm{Lip}}=
\mathbb C\{u,v^n,v^{n+1},\ldots,v^{2n-1},
 u^nv,u^{n-1}v^2,u^{n-2}v^3,\ldots,u^2v^{n-1}\}.}
\]
Equivalently, with exponent semigroups \(\Gamma_n\subseteq\Gamma_n^{\mathrm{Lip}}\subset\mathbb N^2\),
\[
\Gamma_n^{\mathrm{Lip}}\setminus\Gamma_n
=\{(a,b):1\le a,b\le n-1,\ a+b\ge n+1\}.
\]
The finite-length correction is
\[
\boxed{\dim_{\mathbb C}(A_n^{\mathrm{Lip}}/A_n)=
\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}2,}
\]
while both algebras have embedding dimension \(2n\). The correction length is therefore unbounded despite unchanged embedding dimension. The smallest case \(n=3\), involving the single added monomial \(u^2v^2\), appears in the motivating paper; the complete law for all \(n\) is the finding here.

## Assumptions and scope
All germs are over \(\mathbb C\), at the origin, with convergent complex analytic power series. The normalization map is the monomial map with coordinate functions listed in the definition of \(A_n\); its image carries the induced Euclidean metric (any norm on the finite coordinate space gives the same local Lipschitz property). The normalization is \(B\): the exponent lattice is all of \(\mathbb Z^2\), since \((1,0),(0,n),(0,n+1)\) generate it, and the cone is the first quadrant. The map is injective because \(u\) is a coordinate and \(v\) is recovered from the pair \((v^n,v^{n+1})\) away from \(v=0\). It is an isomorphism off the origin: when \(u\ne0\), recover \(v=(u^nv)/u^n\); when \(v\ne0\), recover \(v=v^{n+1}/v^n\). Hence these are isolated nonnormal toric surface germs.

Here \(A_n^{\mathrm{Lip}}\) denotes the intermediate analytic algebra of normalization functions descending to locally Lipschitz functions on the image germ, equivalently the Lipschitz saturation defined using the integral closure of the diagonal ideal. This equivalence is standard in the cited primary literature. It is not the ordinary integral closure \(B\), and no positive-characteristic statement is asserted.

## Proof
**1. Original exponent region.** The numerical semigroup generated by \(n,n+1,\ldots,2n-1\) is \(\{0\}\cup\{m\ge n\}\). Because \(u\) is a generator and each \(u^nv^j\), \(1\le j<n\), is a generator, the exponents of \(A_n\) are exactly
\[
\Gamma_n=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb N^2:b=0\text{ or }b\ge n\text{ or }a\ge n\}.
\]
Thus the only gaps in the normalization have \(0\le a<n\) and \(1\le b<n\).

**2. Uniform cusp inequality.** Put \(\rho=\max(|v|,|v'|)\le1\). For every \(n\ge2\) there is an explicit constant \(K_n\), independent of \(v,v'\), such that
\[
\rho^n|v-v'|\le K_n\bigl(|v^{n+1}-v'^{n+1}|+
\rho|v^n-v'^n|\bigr).\tag{1}
\]
Assume \(|v|=\rho\), exchanging the points if necessary. If \(|v'|\ge\rho/2\), use
\[
v^{n+1}-v'^{n+1}=v(v^n-v'^n)+(v')^n(v-v')
\]
to obtain (1) with \(K_n=2^n\). If \(|v'|<\rho/2\), then \(|v^n-v'^n|\ge (1-2^{-n})\rho^n\) and \(|v-v'|\le3\rho/2\), which gives (1) with \(K_n=3/[2(1-2^{-n})]\). Take the larger constant. Also, for any integer \(b\ge1\),
\[
|v^b-v'^b|\le b\rho^{b-1}|v-v'|.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\rho^{n+1-b}|v^b-v'^b|
\le bK_n\bigl(|v^{n+1}-v'^{n+1}|+
\rho|v^n-v'^n|\bigr).\tag{2}
\]

**3. Every upper-triangular gap is Lipschitz.** Fix \(1\le a,b<n\) with \(a+b\ge n+1\). For normalized pairs \((u,v),(u',v')\) near the origin, let \(D\) be the maximum of the four coordinate differences
\[
|u-u'|,\quad |v^n-v'^n|,\quad|v^{n+1}-v'^{n+1}|,\quad|u^nv-(u')^nv'|.
\]
Each is a difference of coordinates of the original monomial embedding. Since
\[
|u^av^b-(u')^a(v')^b|
\le |u|^a|v^b-(v')^b|+|v'|^b|u^a-(u')^a|,
\]
the second term is at most \(aD\) on the unit bidisk. Write \(s=|u|\) and \(\rho=\max(|v|,|v'|)\). If \(s\le\rho\), then \(s^a\le\rho^{n+1-b}\) and (2) bounds the first term by \(2bK_nD\). If \(s\ge\rho\), then
\[
s^a\rho^{b-1}\le s^{a+b-1}\le s^n.
\]
Moreover,
\[
s^n|v-v'|\le |u^nv-(u')^nv'|+
|u^n-(u')^n|\,|v'|\le(n+1)D.
\]
The first term is therefore at most \(b(n+1)D\). Thus \(u^av^b\) is locally Lipschitz on the image germ.

**4. Every lower-triangular gap is excluded, even in a nonmonomial analytic combination.** Fix a primitive \(n\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\). For every fixed \(c\in\mathbb C\), compare normalization points \((ct,t)\) and \((ct,\zeta t)\), with \(t\to0\). All coordinate differences of the original monomial map are \(O(|t|^{n+1})\): the \(u\) and \(v^n\) differences vanish, while all remaining differences have total degree at least \(n+1\). Let
\[
f(u,v)=\sum_{a,b\ge0}c_{ab}u^av^b\in B
\]
be locally Lipschitz on the image. Necessarily,
\[
\sum_{a+b=k}c_{ab}c^a(1-\zeta^b)=0
\quad\text{for every }c\in\mathbb C\text{ and }k\le n.
\]
As a polynomial identity in \(c\), each coefficient vanishes separately. For \(1\le b<n\) one has \(1-\zeta^b\ne0\). Hence \(c_{ab}=0\) whenever \(1\le b<n\) and \(a+b\le n\). These are precisely the forbidden lower-triangular gaps. In particular, no cancellation between distinct monomials can conceal one of them.

**5. Algebra equality and finite invariants.** Every convergent series supported on the allowed exponents has a convergent decomposition
\[
f(u,v)=g_0(u)+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}
(u^{n+1-j}v^j)g_j(u)+
\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}v^{n+r}h_r(u,v^n),
\]
for convergent series \(g_j\) and \(h_r\). This proves that the allowed support gives exactly the displayed finitely generated analytic algebra. Each displayed generator is locally Lipschitz by the previous steps, so every convergent analytic expression in these generators is locally Lipschitz. Conversely, Step 4 excludes every other exponent.

For each \(2\le b<n\), precisely the exponents \(n+1-b\le a\le n-1\) are newly admitted, giving \(b-1\) new monomials. Their residue classes form a \(\mathbb C\)-basis of the finite quotient, hence its length is \(\sum_{b=2}^{n-1}(b-1)=(n-1)(n-2)/2\). The displayed generators of \(A_n\) and of \(A_n^{\mathrm{Lip}}\) are semigroup-minimal: the former have \(u\), the \(n\) pure-\(v\) exponents, and the \(n-1\) lowest positive-\(v\) exponents with first coordinate \(n\); the latter replace those positive-\(v\) generators by the boundary exponents \((n+1-j,j)\). Each has \(2n\) generators, proving the embedding-dimension assertion.

## Relationship to prior work
Bernard, Chávez-Martínez and Giles Flores (arXiv:2604.03164, first submitted April 3, 2026) characterize arbitrary toric Lipschitz-saturation exponents via Newton polyhedra and lattice subgroups. Their Example 3.10 uses exactly the \(n=3\) member and identifies the extra monomial \(u^2v^2\). That individual case is not new. The all-parameter triangular rule, exact correction length, minimal generating sets, and constant embedding-dimension comparison are not stated in that example. The present proof gives a direct uniform metric argument rather than merely reporting outputs of the general membership algorithm.

Duarte and Giles Flores (arXiv:2310.03216) prove toricity of Lipschitz saturation under smooth normalization and compute other families, notably three-generator hypersurface semigroups and products of monomial curves. Their formulas do not present this mixed high-embedding-dimension family. Dalbelo, Hernandes and Ruas (2026) study normal forms for isolated nonnormal toric varieties with smooth normalization but not this Lipschitz correction. A general algorithm can in principle compute individual cases, so unpublished or implicit equivalents remain possible.

## Limitations
The statement concerns complex analytic surface germs with the specified monomial normalization; it does not classify arbitrary isolated toric surface singularities or positive-characteristic variants. The quotient length is the length of the Lipschitz correction, not the delta-invariant \(\dim_{\mathbb C}(B/A_n)\). The embedding-dimension claim is for each pair \(A_n\) and its own saturation, not uniform in \(n\). Mathematical priority remains subject to independent expert/literature scrutiny.
