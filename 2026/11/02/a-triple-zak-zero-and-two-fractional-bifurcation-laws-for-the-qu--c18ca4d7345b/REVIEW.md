# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

A direct eight-term evaluation of the centered quadratic B-spline gives an exact cubic factor for the symmetry-line Zak transform. Its discriminant is strictly negative throughout the open interval, as established by a five-term positive Bernstein-basis certificate. Three exact endpoint evaluations determine the location of its unique real root. Frequency multiplicities then follow from the explicitly factored cosine term. Two separately verified implicit-function calculations produce the square-root and two-thirds-power laws; the latter uses a rescaled cubic with a simple limiting root. The package's exact-arithmetic checker independently recomputes all decisive polynomial identities and performs direct finite-sum cross-checks. Finite tests are support, not a proof of the full parameter classification.

## Originality — PASS under inspected sources

Hasannasab–Lemvig (arXiv:2609.13116, §§1, 4–7) establishes the palindromic approach and gives a special order-three calculation, but does not state the present \(R=3\) odd-index scale window \(8/3<b<3\), its algebraic discriminant threshold, or the two Puiseux exponents. Lemvig (arXiv:2601.07642) and Lemvig–Nielsen (arXiv:1507.03982) address Gabor frame-set hyperbolic obstructions rather than this full vertical-slice zero count. Stangl–Frederick (arXiv:2609.00481) treats the order-two hat spline. Closest indexed related claims concern a different spatial slice of the order-three spline or optimal frame bounds for the order-two hat spline. Statement-level comparison found neither implication nor an exact table containing this one-to-three-zero transition. Residual risk: an older or unindexed spline-zero calculation might encode the same classification.

## Value — PASS

The recent order-three treatment explicitly leaves richer subunit-scale root geometry unresolved. The first natural odd nearest-integer window near the support-change scale \(b=3\) has an unexpectedly sharp interior transition where the compulsory symmetry zero becomes triple. The exact threshold and two distinct fractional exponents reveal both the interior birth and the boundary coalescence of zeros. This is a motivated, exhaustive classification for a natural parametric cross-section, not a routine evaluation of one numerical point; it provides a precise benchmark for future complete zero-set descriptions.

## Limitations

Only the vertical slice \(x=1/2\) and the open interval \(8/3<b<3\) are classified. No conclusion about full Gabor frame bounds, all spatial zeros, or the excluded endpoint is claimed. Independence has not been established by an external reviewer.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
