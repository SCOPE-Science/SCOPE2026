# Review: A two-branch phase diagram for real Blaschke-quotient circle maps

## Correctness

PASS. Multiplying the angular derivative \(1+P_a-P_b\) by the two strictly positive Poisson denominators yields the explicit quadratic \(N_{a,b}(x)\). Both endpoint values factor into sharp linear constraints. When \(ab\leq0\), concavity reduces global nonnegativity to endpoint signs. When \(ab>0\), the vertex location and the discriminant of \(Q_a(b)\) give the exact forbidden band, including its contact at \(|a|=\sqrt3/2\). The topological criterion follows from degree one and nonnegative analytic angular derivative. The two-versus-four turning-point count follows from the number of interior simple roots of the quadratic. A rational witness and exact-algebraic checks independently test critical identities. ## Originality

PASS relative to the material inspected, subject to the following residual risk. Wang and Zhong, *A counterexample to an open problem of Dorff* (arXiv:2609.11556v1), give a single admissible rational Blaschke-quotient instance, its harmonic Poisson extension, and a counterexample concerning its harmonic self-convolution; the relevant full-text sections do not give a global phase diagram for the boundary map. The closest publicly indexed mathematical results address a self-convolution Jacobian sign threshold on a fixed ray and a fold of the self-convolution, not circle-boundary orientation. Related articles inspected only through summaries cannot establish comprehensive absence; hidden or classical equivalent parametrizations remain possible.

## Value

PASS. The boundary map is the exact mechanism used to produce recent convex harmonic ellipse constructions. Its admissible parameter region is natural to determine because boundary orientation is the hypothesis permitting harmonic Poisson extension by the classical Radó–Kneser–Choquet theorem. A complete two-parameter result uncovers disconnected admissible sections and a four-fold angular obstruction invisible to both endpoint tests, not just another special parameter or a routine perturbation.

## Scope and evidence limits

Only real parameters strictly within the unit disk and the unit-circle boundary quotient are classified. The harmonic extension consequence is classical. The theorem neither determines self-convolution univalence nor covers arbitrary or complex Blaschke parameters. The exact algebraic proof covers all stated parameters; numerical sampling supplies supplementary tests only. No human or independent validation is claimed.
