# A two-denomination greedy frontier for cumulative temporal envy

## Finding
Fix any finite arrival sequence of indivisible goods whose **strictly positive**, identical additive value to each of two agents belongs to \(\{a,2a\}\), where \(a>0\). Allocating each good irrevocably to an agent with the smaller current bundle value minimizes the sum of maximum pairwise envy after every prefix, simultaneously for all stopping horizons. This holds without advance knowledge of future goods. The restriction is sharp in three natural, separately quantified ways: with three agents and values \((a,2a,a,2a)\), the greedy and optimal totals are \(6a\) and \(5a\); with two agents and values \((a,a,3a,a)\), they are \(6a\) and \(4a\); with two agents and values \((a,a,2a,0)\), they are \(5a\) and \(3a\). The three failures first occur at four arrivals in their respective restricted settings.

## Assumptions and scope
There are \(n\) agents with the same nonnegative additive valuations for each arriving good. Goods arrive in a specified order, one at each round, and each is allocated immediately and irrevocably to exactly one agent. Let \(L_i(t)\) be agent \(i\)'s total received value after round \(t\). Maximum pairwise envy at round \(t\) is \(c_t=\max_{i,j}\max\{0,L_j(t)-L_i(t)\}=\max_i L_i(t)-\min_i L_i(t)\). The total temporal envy at horizon \(T\) is \(C_T=\sum_{t=1}^{T}c_t\). Greedy assigns the next good to an agent with minimum current \(L_i\); ties are arbitrary. The positive result requires precisely two agents and every arriving value in \(\{a,2a\}\), with no zero-valued arrivals counted as rounds. The negative examples retain identical values and immediate assignment.

## Proof
Normalize \(a=1\), restoring \(a\) at the end by homogeneity. With two agents, choose signed load differences and let \(g_t\) be the absolute difference under greedy after round \(t\), with \(g_0=0\). For value \(w_t\in\{1,2\}\), greedy obeys \(g_t=|g_{t-1}-w_t|\). Inspection of the six possible pairs \((g_{t-1},w_t)\) proves inductively that \(g_t\in\{0,1,2\}\) for all \(t\).

Consider any other complete allocation sequence, with absolute difference \(h_t\) after round \(t\). Since every signed difference is congruent modulo \(2\) to the total value of the goods received up through round \(t\), \(h_t\) and \(g_t\) have the same integer parity. Thus \(h_t\ge g_t\) whenever \(g_t=0\) or \(g_t=1\). When \(g_t=2\), the only possibility for \(h_t<g_t\) is \(h_t=0\), and the difference is exactly \(-2\). Such a negative difference can occur only if \(w_t=2\) and \(g_{t-1}=0\), because those are the only predecessors of greedy state \(2\). For the competitor to reach \(h_t=0\) after adding value \(2\), it must have \(h_{t-1}=2\). Therefore the immediately preceding difference \(h_{t-1}-g_{t-1}\) is \(+2\). Pair each negative contribution with its unique immediately preceding positive contribution. These pairs are disjoint: a predecessor used in a pair has greedy difference \(0\), whereas a negative event has greedy difference \(2\). All unpaired differences are nonnegative. Consequently \(\sum_{t=1}^{T}(h_t-g_t)\ge0\) for every horizon \(T\), proving global and prefixwise optimality of greedy.

For the three-agent example, after values \((1,2)\) the greedy loads are, up to permutation, \((1,2,0)\). The third value \(1\) goes to the empty agent, and the last value \(2\) to a current minimum, giving gaps \((1,2,1,2)\) and cost \(6\). Assigning the third item to the agent holding the first and the fourth to the empty agent gives load vectors \((1,0,0),(1,2,0),(2,2,0),(2,2,2)\), gaps \((1,2,2,0)\), and cost \(5\). To see this is optimal, the second item cannot go to the first agent in an optimum of cost at most \(5\): such a choice gives a gap of \(3\) at round two and at least \(3\) at round three, already exceeding \(5\) together with the first-round gap. Otherwise the loads after round two are \((1,2,0)\). The three possible recipients for the third value yield minimal achievable totals of \(5\), \(6\), or at least \(8\) after the fourth, as can be checked directly from the three resulting load vectors. Thus the optimum is \(5\).

For two agents, the sequence \((1,1,3,1)\) gives greedy discrepancies \((1,0,3,2)\) and total \(6\); putting the first two values together, the value \(3\) on the other side, and the last value with the first two gives discrepancies \((1,2,1,0)\) and total \(4\). Every assignment satisfies \(|D_2|+|D_3|\ge3\), since the third value is \(3\), while \(|D_1|=1\), proving the lower bound \(4\). For \((1,1,2,0)\), greedy discrepancies are \((1,0,2,2)\) of total \(5\), while the analogous anticipatory allocation has discrepancies \((1,2,0,0)\) of total \(3\). Here \(|D_2|+|D_3|\ge2\) and \(|D_1|=1\), proving the lower bound \(3\).

No two-agent sequence of at most three nonnegative goods can defeat greedy. Let the first three values be \(x,y,z\ge0\). Relative to assigning the second good against the first, assigning it to the same agent raises the second-round gap by \(2\min\{x,y\}\). The best third-round gap from a preceding gap \(d\) is \(|d-z|\), a \(1\)-Lipschitz function of \(d\), so any future reduction is at most that increase. Hence greedy is optimal through three rounds for arbitrary nonnegative values. With three agents and values in \(\{1,2\}\), all eight three-item sequences have greedy costs equal to exhaustive minima (see the exact table below), so the three-agent counterexample is also shortest. These observations establish the stated four-arrival sharpness; they do not classify every larger-population or larger-alphabet sequence.

## Verification
The standalone Python checker `verification/check.py` enumerates all assignments for all two-agent sequences with values \(1\) or \(2\) through ten goods, and all three-agent sequences with those values through three goods. It separately checks exact minima for the three displayed four-good witnesses. The exact three-agent three-item table, listing greedy and optimum costs in order, is \(111:2,112:3,121:4,122:4,211:5,212:5,221:5,222:4\). Running `python verification/check.py` returned `VERIFY_OK cases=2063 assignments=1398471`. The finite enumeration corroborates the general two-agent proof and is an exhaustive certificate only for the explicitly bounded checks.

## Relationship to prior work
Goldberg, Robinson, and Teh (2026) introduce the cumulative-maximum-envy objective for fixed-order temporal fair division, prove hardness in general, and show that minimum-current-load greedy is optimal when identical valuations are binary \(\{0,1\}\). Their algorithm for the sequencing variant also chooses an arrival order; its approximation guarantees concern a different optimization problem. The present theorem treats the previously uncharacterized positive two-denomination class \(\{a,2a\}\) under a fixed order, and precisely identifies which added features immediately invalidate the same greedy rule. The binary case does not cover it by rescaling, since a two-unit good remains an indivisible decision, and subtracting a common unit from a good's valuation changes load discrepancies when the two agents hold unequal item counts.

Elkind, Lam, Latifian, Neoh, and Teh (2024/2025) show existence of temporal EF1 allocations for two item types and generalized binary valuation settings. Temporal EF1 is a pointwise fairness feasibility condition, not optimality for the sum of numerical envy across rounds, and their two-type round-robin construction does not imply this specific greedy optimum.

## Limitations
The guarantee is about maximum pairwise envy and its **undiscounted sum**; it does not claim minimum envy separately at every round. All arriving goods must have strictly positive value \(a\) or \(2a\), and there must be precisely two agents. A zero-valued arrival still adds a term to cumulative envy and can invalidate optimality, as the witness demonstrates. The proof does not extend to heterogeneous valuations, arbitrary real-valued two-level alphabets, three agents, or the freedom to reorder arrivals. Published mathematical and scheduling literature could contain an equivalent result expressed as optimal signed-walk control; the cited search and source comparisons do not prove global uniqueness.
