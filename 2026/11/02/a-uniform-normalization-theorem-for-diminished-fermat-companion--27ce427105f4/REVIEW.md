# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The six source forms exactly match the arbitrary-parameter formulas in Kabat--Strycharz-Szemberg's Lemma 9. The base-point argument handles \(a=0\), \(b=0\), and \(c^m=b^m\); positivity makes the image map finite. Over the generic boundary curve \(c=0\), the output ratio \(T=-u_6/u_5\) retrieves the parameter, while a hypothetical second preimage with \(c\ne0\) would force \(T^m=1\). The two actual image functions \(G,H\) have valuations \(m-1,m\); their gcd is one and the residue degree is one. The characteristic-zero fundamental equality then gives function-field degree one. Intersection theory and the generic differential rank give the asserted surface degree, boundary degree, and nonnormality. The symbolic verifier and an independent finite-field enumerator completed successfully; finite tests do not replace the all-\(m\) proof. No char-positive assertion is made.

## Originality — PASS, with explicit residual risk

The motivating 2020 paper proves the base-point-free input and studies unexpected curves but does not establish normalization or image degree of its entire arbitrary-\(m\) companion family. An indexed result already handles the first \(m=3\) quintic map with degree \(25\); a further existing case-specific calculation describes that map's differential rank-drop and generic cusp singularities. Neither \(m=3\) result is treated as a new discovery. The scientific advance assessed here is the general proof and exact degree and nonnormality for all \(m\ge4\). Searches for the uniform \((2m-1)^2\) degree formula, codimension-one nonnormality, and valuation criterion did not return an equivalent general statement. A 2019 paper by Szpond concerns a different degree-seven rational surface associated with unexpected quartics. Residual risk: full text of the indexed special-case note was unavailable through the consulted interface, and unindexed independent generalizations could exist.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The original work explicitly singles out the geometry of the companion image as worth investigating. The result answers a natural structural question for the entire infinite family: it identifies its normalization, exact degree, and a codimension-one locus where the image fails to be normal. The valuation method extracts a uniform geometric mechanism not provided by the source's base-point-free argument. It is not a parameter relabeling or a single-case degree computation. No claim is made that it classifies all singularities.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest direct source is Kabat--Strycharz-Szemberg, arXiv:2003.02596 (2020), Sections 2.1 and 3.1; earlier special cases in \(m=3\) already establish normalization and degree and separately the generic cusp behavior. Szpond (2019/2020), DOI:10.1002/mana.201800455, is related but concerns different defining equations. The calculation is confined to characteristic zero; analytic local type and the full singular locus remain open.
