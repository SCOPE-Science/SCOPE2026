# A uniform splitting-subspace count for two nilpotent Jordan blocks

## Finding
Let \(q\) be any prime power, \(d\ge 3\), and \(0\le r<d\). For a nilpotent linear operator \(T\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) with precisely two Jordan blocks of lengths \(d+r\) and \(d-r\), the number of two-dimensional subspaces \(W\) satisfying
\[
V=W\oplus TW\oplus\cdots\oplus T^{d-1}W
\]
is exactly
\[
\sigma(2,d;T)=\begin{cases}q^{4d-4},&r=0,\\(q-1)q^{4d-5},&1\le r<d.\end{cases}
\]
In particular, the value is independent of the positive imbalance \(r\). Among two-dimensional subspaces complementary to \(TV\), every such subspace splits when \(r=0\), while exactly the fraction \(1-q^{-1}\) splits when \(r\ge1\). The new closed-count assertion concerns \(r\ge2\): the balanced and imbalance-one cases fall within existing explicit formulas.

## Assumptions and scope
A splitting subspace means precisely a two-dimensional \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear subspace \(W\) for which the displayed sum is direct and equals the entire \(2d\)-dimensional space. The field may have any characteristic. Put \(L=d+r\) and \(S=d-r\), with \(S\ge1\). Identify the underlying \(\mathbb F_q[t]\)-module with
\[
V=\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^L)\oplus\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^S),
\]
so that \(T\) acts as multiplication by \(t\). The block lengths and \(d\) are fixed; only subspaces \(W\), not bases for them, are counted. The result is an exact finite-field theorem, not an extrapolation from experiments.

## Proof
**1. Every splitting subspace complements \(TV\).** If the displayed direct sum holds, its reduction modulo \(TV\) is generated by the image of \(W\). The quotient \(V/TV\) has dimension two. Since \(\dim W=2\), reduction restricts to an isomorphism \(W\to V/TV\). Conversely, every two-dimensional complement has a unique basis \(u,v\) whose images modulo \(TV\) are the two standard generators. Thus
\[
u=(U,tC),\qquad v=(tB,V_0),
\]
where \(U\in\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^L)\) and \(V_0\in\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^S)\) have constant coefficient one, while \(tB\) and \(tC\) are arbitrary elements divisible by \(t\) in their respective quotient rings. Consequently there are exactly
\[
q^{(L-1)+(S-1)+(L-1)+(S-1)}=q^{4d-4}
\]
complements \(W\).

**2. Normalize by an invertible module transformation.** Since \(U\) is a unit, let \(P=U^{-1}(tB)\) in \(\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^L)\), and put \(V'=V_0-PtC\), interpreted modulo \(t^S\). Both \(U\) and \(V'\) are units. The \(\mathbb F_q[t]\)-module automorphism defined by
\[
\phi(e_1)=u,\qquad \phi(e_2)=(0,V')
\]
sends \(e_1\) to \(u\) and \(Pe_1+e_2\) to \(v\). Therefore \(W\) splits if and only if the normalized subspace
\[
W_P=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_q}\{e_1,Pe_1+e_2\}
\]
splits. Since multiplying by a fixed unit \(U^{-1}\) permutes the possible \(tB\), the coefficients \(b_1,\ldots,b_{L-1}\) of \(P=\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}b_jt^j\) are uniform and independent.

**3. The balanced case.** When \(r=0\), \(V\) is free of rank two over \(\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^d)\). The two lifts \(u,v\) of a basis modulo \(t\) form a module basis by Nakayama's lemma; equivalently, their coefficient matrix has unit determinant. Their powers \(u,Tu,\ldots,T^{d-1}u,v,Tv,\ldots,T^{d-1}v\) therefore form an \(\mathbb F_q\)-basis, proving the first count.

**4. The unequal case reduces to one Hankel determinant.** Suppose \(r\ge1\). For \(W_P\), the \(d\) columns \(e_1,te_1,\ldots,t^{d-1}e_1\) occupy the first \(d\) levels of the long block. The \(S\) columns \(Pe_1+e_2,t(Pe_1+e_2),\ldots,t^{S-1}(Pe_1+e_2)\) project to a basis of the short block. The remaining \(r=d-S\) columns have second coordinate zero. After eliminating the previous columns, they are independent precisely when the matrix
\[
M_{k,\ell}=b_{r+k-\ell}\quad(0\le k,\ell<r)
\]
is invertible, where \(b_j=0\) for \(j\le0\). Reversing the column order turns \(M\) into the \(r\times r\) Hankel matrix
\[
H=(b_{1+i+j})_{0\le i,j<r}.
\]
This depends on exactly \(2r-1\) independent coefficients, all available because \(2r-1\le L-1\) is equivalent to \(S\ge1\).

**5. Count nonsingular Hankel matrices.** The finite-field Hankel rank theorem (Dwivedi--Grinberg, Theorem 1.1) says that among the \(q^{2r-1}\) Hankel matrices of the displayed shape, exactly \(q^{2r-2}\) are singular. Therefore exactly \((q-1)q^{2r-2}\) are invertible. Hence the fraction \(1-q^{-1}\) of all complements split for every \(r\ge1\). Multiplying by the complement count from Step 1 yields \((q-1)q^{4d-5}\), as claimed. \(\square\)

## Verification
The standalone, dependency-free `verify.py` checks the quotient-normalized splitting condition directly for eight parameter choices through \(d=4\), including \(q=2,3\). It separately enumerates all short Hankel matrices for \(r\le4\) over the prime fields of orders two and three. An independent check enumerates **all** two-dimensional subspaces of \(\mathbb F_2^6\) rather than restricting to complements and verifies the totals for \(r=0,1,2\). Execute `python verify.py` beside this document; successful execution prints `VERIFY_OK`. These finite checks corroborate but do not replace the proof for arbitrary prime powers and dimensions.

## Relationship to prior work
Aggarwal--Ram (2020, arXiv:2012.08411) develop the general finite-field splitting-subspace problem. Aggarwal--Ram (2021, arXiv:2105.15155), Theorems 1.2--1.3 and Corollary 4.3, give explicit counts when the least nonconstant invariant factor has degree \(d\) or \(d-1\), covering respectively the equal-block and imbalance-one nilpotent cases. For \(r\ge2\), the smaller block has degree \(d-r\le d-2\), so their displayed explicit formulas do not apply. Prasad--Ram (2022, arXiv:2205.11076) provide arbitrary-operator counts in the two-step case, which does not imply the all-\(d\ge3\) formula. Dwivedi--Grinberg (2021, arXiv:2109.05415) establish the Hankel rank theorem used here; that theorem is not claimed as new. The 2022 Prasad--Ram public Sage worksheet implements symbolic splitting counts for \(V=W\oplus TW\) on even-dimensional spaces, i.e. splitting length two; its general-looking routine therefore does not compute the fixed two-dimensional, \(d\)-step invariant when \(d\ge3\). The assertion assessed here is the coupling of the Hankel rank count with all unequal two-block nilpotent splitting spaces and its uniform independence from \(r\).

## Limitations
The theorem concerns only two-block nilpotent operators and splitting dimension two; it makes no assertion for general rational canonical forms, more than two Jordan blocks, or arbitrary splitting dimensions. The balanced and imbalance-one cases are already consequences of prior formulas. Publication priority for the unequal case remains subject to equivalent formulations in broader finite-field controllability or polynomial-matrix literature. No independently performed mathematical audit is claimed.
