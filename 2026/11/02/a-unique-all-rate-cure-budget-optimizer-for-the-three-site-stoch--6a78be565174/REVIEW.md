# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact absorption-time equations arise by conditioning on the next transition of the continuous-time seven-state Markov process. A five-state quotient suffices after (and only after) endpoint rates have been proved equal: the full seven-state algebra confirms that averaging endpoints decreases expected extinction time strictly, because a difference formula factors into the endpoint-rate squared difference times a ratio of polynomials with positive integer coefficients. The two positivity certificates contain 55 and 16 terms and are recomputed, not asserted on samples, in `VERIFY.py`. After normalization, the displayed quintic derivative has one positive zero by Descartes' rule, which must lie strictly between one half and one. The zero-infectorate and high-rate asymptotics follow from explicit coefficient expansions, independently checked symbolically. Boundary allocations cannot produce extinction from full infection. Limit: this is a finite-state exact model, not a statement about infinite-volume persistence or non-Markovian disease dynamics.

## Originality — PASS under inspected evidence, with historical limitations

The nearest paper is McFadden–Daly–Shneer (2025), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spl.2024.110284, particularly its sections 2.2–3: it supplies weak/strong infection results and a numerical three-node-star illustration but explicitly chooses one uniformly randomly infected starting vertex in the illustration. The current theorem concerns all three initially infected, unrestricted endpoint allocations, an exact global strict symmetrization proof and an all-rate unique quintic optimality classification; these are not consequences of the inspected theorems. Van de Bovenkamp–Van Mieghem (2015), https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.92.032806, compute star extinction times with a common curing rate but not an optimized heterogeneous budget. Borgs–Chayes–Ganesh–Saberi (2010), https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.20315, address more general but qualitatively distinct epidemic control and asymptotic threshold bounds. Wagner–Anantharam (2005), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spa.2004.08.003, optimize reproduction-rate profiles for large-network longevity rather than curing small-network extinction. Dynamic policies in https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.2241 do not imply this static optimum. A February 2026 doctoral thesis discussing heterogeneous SIS allocation could not be read in full and remains a genuine priority risk. This assessment is not an assertion that every publication has been screened.

## Value — PASS

The three-node path is the smallest connected nonregular simple graph and the smallest model distinguishing a hub from endpoints. Its exact finite-horizon mean lifetime is a mathematically distinct objective from a linearized epidemic threshold; the theorem establishes a strict, universal allocation interval, unique optimizer for all positive transmission intensities, and different weak/strong infection corrections. Proving that equal endpoint curing is globally optimal rather than imposing it removes a meaningful symmetry assumption. The result is a finite benchmark for testing epidemic-budget heuristics, not a population-level field recommendation.
