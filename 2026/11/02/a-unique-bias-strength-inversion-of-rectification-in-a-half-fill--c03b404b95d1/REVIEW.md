# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is a finite-state algebraic identity: six Markov configurations have explicit positive transition rates, stationary cofactor weights, and a directly computed signed-hop observable. The two determining polynomial factorizations were independently checked from the exact symbolic generator in `verify.py`, then 32 separate exact-rational stationary linear solves checked the current. The unique threshold and the reflection criterion follow analytically from affine dependence on squared bias and AM-GM. Finite tests alone do not establish the all-parameter claim; the symbolic identities and their reconstructed matrix derivation do.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The full accessible Sakai–Akimoto manuscript provides general symmetry mechanisms and simulations, not an exact two-particle four-site identity or a finite-field polarity inversion. Its cited Goldstein–Speer theorem resolves only the completely asymmetric endpoint; Juhász–Santen–Iglói study large-system scaling. Published mathematical-record searches for exact half-filled trap-ring crossover variants returned no implicated result, and the accessible preceding one-hole four-site classification concerns a different particle sector whose rectification sign is fixed. The main residual risk is an unlocated general six-configuration current theorem implying the present factorization; the older Juhász–Santen–Iglói full text was not inspected.

## Value — PASS

An exact classification of when a single fixed, quenched landscape reverses rectification polarity as the external field is varied is a physically interpretable nonlinear-response boundary, rather than a routine recalculation of linear mobility. It also separates symmetry-protected global oddness from accidental finite-field cancellation and contrasts sharply with fixed-sign rectification in the one-hole sector. This directly addresses the interaction/disorder transport mechanism motivating the primary paper; no thermodynamic-limit conclusion is asserted.
