# A unique one-leaf roundness resonance on unbalanced complete bipartite metrics

## Finding

For integers \(m,n\geq 2\), start with the unit-edge shortest-path metric of the complete bipartite graph \(K_{m,n}\), with vertex classes \(A\) and \(B\) of respective sizes \(m\) and \(n\). Choose \(a_0\in A\), attach exactly one new vertex \(z\) by a pendant edge of length \(t>0\), and retain all \(m+n+1\) vertices in the shortest-path metric \(d_t\). Write \(\wp_{m,n}(t)\) for its supremal exponent of negative type and put

\[
p_0=\log_2\!\left(\frac{2mn}{2mn-m-n}\right).
\]

**Theorem (one-leaf resonance).** If \(m=n=2\), then \(\wp_{2,2}(t)=p_0=1\) for every \(t>0\). For all other \((m,n)\), \(0<p_0<1\), and the following exhaustive comparison holds.

1. If \(m\leq n\), then \(\wp_{m,n}(t)<p_0\) for every \(t>0\).
2. If \(m>n\), there is exactly one \(t_{m,n}>0\) such that \(\wp_{m,n}(t_{m,n})=p_0\). At all other positive lengths, \(\wp_{m,n}(t)<p_0\). The resonant length is the unique **positive** solution of
   \[
   F_{m,n}(t):=\frac{t^{p_0}+(m-1)((t+2)^{p_0}-2^{p_0})}{m}-((t+1)^{p_0}-1)=0.
   \]

At every resonance, including \(m=n=2\), the \(p_0\)-negative-type equality space on zero-sum coefficient vectors is one-dimensional. It is generated by a coefficient vector constant \(1/m\) on \(A\), constant \(-1/n\) on \(B\), and zero at \(z\). The minimum Euclidean embedding dimension of \((V,d_t^{p_0/2})\) is consequently \(m+n-1\); every smaller nonnegative exponent has strict negative type.

The resonance lengths obey two distinct, sharp branching laws. For every fixed positive integer \(k\),
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{t_{n+k,n}}{n}=\frac{2}{k\log 2}.
\]
For every fixed \(n\geq2\), let \(p_\infty=\log_2(2n/(2n-1))\). Then
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}m^{1/(1-p_\infty)}t_{m,n}
=\bigl[p_\infty(1-2^{p_\infty-1})\bigr]^{-1/(1-p_\infty)}.
\]

For instance, adding one leaf to a vertex in the five-vertex class of \(K_{5,2}\) preserves the core's supremal exponent \(p_0\approx0.621488376746\) only at \(t\approx1.583616485202\).

## Assumptions and scope

For a finite metric space \((X,d)\), \(p\)-negative type means
\[
Q_p(\alpha):=\sum_{x,y\in X}\alpha_x\alpha_y d(x,y)^p\leq0\quad\text{whenever }\sum_{x\in X}\alpha_x=0.
\]
Strict negative type means equality only for \(\alpha=0\). The exponent supremum is \(\wp(X,d)=\sup\{p\geq0:(X,d)\text{ has }p\text{-negative type}\}\). We use the standard finite-metric equivalence between \(p\)-negative type and Hilbert embeddability of the snowflake \(d^{p/2}\), and the centered Gram-matrix rank formula for minimal Euclidean dimension.

Distances within each core vertex class are \(2\), distances between classes are \(1\), and the leaf has distances \(t\), \(t+2\), \(t+1\) respectively to \(a_0\), \(A\setminus\{a_0\}\), \(B\). All conclusions concern the metric on every vertex, including the distinguished leaf. The external exponent \(p\) is not the count of leaves.

## Proof

**1. Core inequality and the null vector.** Put \(u=2^p\). For a zero-sum coefficient vector \(c\) on the core, set \(\sigma=\sum_{i\in A}c_i=-\sum_{j\in B}c_j\), and decompose \(c_i=\sigma/m+a_i\) for \(i\in A\), and \(c_j=-\sigma/n+b_j\) for \(j\in B\), where each fluctuation family sums to zero. Direct summation gives
\[
Q_p^{\rm core}(c)=\left[2(u-1)-u\left(\frac1m+\frac1n\right)\right]\sigma^2-u(\|a\|_2^2+\|b\|_2^2).
\]
The radial coefficient vanishes precisely at \(p=p_0\), is negative below it and positive above it. Thus the core has supremal exponent \(p_0\), with a one-dimensional null space at the endpoint, spanned by the claimed radial vector. Since it is an isometric subspace of the one-leaf metric, \(\wp_{m,n}(t)\leq p_0\).

**2. Exact one-leaf quadratic form at the endpoint.** At \(p=p_0\), write \(u=2^{p_0}\), \(T=t^{p_0}\), \(R=(t+2)^{p_0}-u\), and \(U=(t+1)^{p_0}-1\). Given any zero-sum vector \(\alpha\) on the extended metric, let \(h=\alpha_z\), and form the zero-sum core vector \(c\) by adding \(h\) to the coefficient at \(a_0\). Decompose \(c\) as in step 1 and put \(\Delta=T-R=t^{p_0}+2^{p_0}-(t+2)^{p_0}\). Comparing the leaf distances with those from \(a_0\) yields the **exact identity**
\[
Q_{p_0}^{\rm ext}(\alpha)=-u(\|a\|_2^2+\|b\|_2^2)
+2h\bigl[\sigma F_{m,n}(t)+\Delta a_0\bigr]-2Th^2.
\]
If \(F_{m,n}(t)\neq0\), choose \(h\neq0\) and zero fluctuations, and then choose \(\sigma\) with sufficiently large, appropriate sign. The quadratic form becomes positive, so the extended metric fails \(p_0\)-negative type. Hence resonance \(F_{m,n}(t)=0\) is **necessary**.

If \(F_{m,n}(t)=0\), then \(0<p_0\leq1\) and subadditivity of the concave power give \(0\leq\Delta\leq\min\{T,u\}\). Completing the square over zero-sum \(A\)-fluctuations gives
\[
2h\Delta a_0-u\|a\|_2^2
\leq\frac{m-1}{m}\frac{\Delta^2}{u}h^2
\leq\frac{m-1}{m}Th^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
Q_{p_0}^{\rm ext}(\alpha)
\leq-u\|b\|_2^2-\left(2-\frac{m-1}{m}\right)Th^2\leq0.
\]
Equality forces \(h=0\), all fluctuations zero, and leaves \(\sigma\) unrestricted: the null space is exactly the original radial line. Thus resonance is also **sufficient**, and its centered Gram matrix has rank \((m+n+1)-2=m+n-1\). Standard strictness inheritance for negative type gives strict \(p\)-negative type for all \(0\leq p<p_0\).

**3. Number of positive resonance lengths.** For \(m=n=2\), \(p_0=1\) and the formula gives \(F_{2,2}(t)=0\) for every \(t>0\). In every other case \(0<p_0<1\). Then \(F_{m,n}(0)=0\), \(F_{m,n}(t)>0\) for sufficiently small \(t>0\), and
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}F_{m,n}(t)=1-\frac{m-1}{m}2^{p_0}
=\frac{n-m}{2mn-m-n}.
\]
Its derivative has the sign of
\[
g(t)-(m-1)g(t+1),\qquad g(t)=t^{p_0-1}-(t+1)^{p_0-1}>0.
\]
The function \(g\) is a strictly log-convex positive Laplace transform: for \(0<p_0<1\), up to a positive constant,
\[
g(t)=\int_0^\infty e^{-tr}(1-e^{-r})r^{-p_0}\,dr.
\]
Consequently \(g(t)/g(t+1)\) decreases strictly from infinity to one. For \(m=2\), \(F\) is strictly increasing; for \(m>2\), it strictly rises to a single maximum and then strictly decreases. Combining this unimodality with its endpoint limit proves: one positive zero exactly when \(m>n\), and no positive zero when \(m\leq n\) apart from the \((2,2)\) identity. Since the full finite distance form depends continuously on \(p\), failure at \(p_0\) together with the core upper bound implies strict inequality of supremal exponents: a finite metric's negative-type exponent set is closed.

**4. Asymptotic scales.** Let \(m=n+k\), \(k\geq1\) fixed, and \(t=cn\) for \(c>0\) fixed. Expanding the exact resonance equation uniformly for \(c\) in compact subsets of \((0,\infty)\), using \(p_0=1/(n\log2)+O(n^{-2})\), gives
\[
n^2F_{n+k,n}(cn)=-\frac{k}{2}+\frac1{c\log2}+o(1).
\]
Its unique positive zero is therefore asymptotic to \(2n/(k\log2)\).

For fixed \(n\), put \(m\to\infty\) and \(p_m=p_0(m,n)\to p_\infty<1\). At every fixed \(t>0\), \(F_{m,n}(t)\) tends to \((t+2)^{p_\infty}-(t+1)^{p_\infty}+1-2^{p_\infty}<0\); uniqueness forces \(t_{m,n}\to0\). The small-length expansion, uniform for these \(p_m\), is
\[
F_{m,n}(t)=\frac{t^{p_m}}{m}
-p_m\left(1-\frac{m-1}{m}2^{p_m-1}\right)t+O(t^2).
\]
At the unique root, \(t_{m,n}^{p_m-1}/m\to p_\infty(1-2^{p_\infty-1})\). Since \(p_m-p_\infty=O(m^{-1})\), this yields the second asserted limit.

## Relationship to prior work

Sánchez (2011) calculated the supremal negative-type exponent of the **unmodified** complete bipartite graph, providing the core benchmark \(p_0\). Li and Weston (2009) proved the general theorem that lower exponents inherit strict negative type, used in step 2. Robertson (2023) studied negative type and Hilbert distortion of complete bipartite metrics, again without the asymmetric one-leaf attachment. Ferdi, Baskoro, Obata, and Santika (2025) studied quadratic embedding constants of **uniform corona** graphs, which add an attachment at every core vertex and investigate the fixed distance exponent one. Those hypotheses do not imply the present one-leaf, variable-length, variable-exponent resonance criterion or its two scaling laws. These comparisons do not exclude equivalent work in unindexed sources.

## Limitations

Only exactly one new pendant leaf and a unit-edge complete bipartite core are treated. No claim is made to calculate the exact exponent \(\wp_{m,n}(t)\) for nonresonant lengths, classify multiple leaves, provide arbitrary weighted-core formulas, or settle literature priority without an independent search. The embedding-dimension conclusion holds at the preserving endpoint; lower exponents are covered only by strictness, not by a new exact dimension formula.
