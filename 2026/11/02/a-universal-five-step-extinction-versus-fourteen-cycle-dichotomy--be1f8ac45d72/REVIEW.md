# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

A deterministic all-orders algebraic proof reduces both incidence-toggle cases to the identity \(BB^{\mathsf T}=B^{\mathsf T}B=J\). Rank-one editing raises the binary incidence rank by exactly one. The squared update has a three-dimensional image in each bipartition. Deleting an existing incidence yields the exact nonzero power ranks \(2\rho+2,6,4,2\) followed by zero at step five; inserting a missing incidence yields a primitive order-seven action on each three-dimensional image and an order-fourteen exchange on their sum. The explicit kernel dimensions prove the entrance and basin formulas, without treating finite sampling as a proof. Two standalone implementations check characteristic cases \(q=2,4,8\) and all 49 one-edge Fano edits; the Fano exhaustive calculation enumerates every 14-bit initial condition. The proof applies to any finite plane meeting the hypotheses, not just fields.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The closest inspected primary XOR paper, Noual–Regnault–Sené (arXiv:1208.2767, Section 4), proves circulant-network convergence statements rather than an incidence-graph one-edge dichotomy. A projective-plane extremal-cycle paper addresses the unperturbed incidence graph under unrelated graph invariants. General finite-map Fitting theory explains why eventual periodic states form an invertible summand but does not furnish its six-dimensional form, primitive periods, or exact one-edge edit ranks here. Searches for Levi-graph XOR, projective-plane incidence parity dynamics, edge perturbations, and equivalent nilpotent/period-fourteen formulas found no result implying this joint classification; closely ranked research findings on Boolean cubes, Kneser graphs, and complete multipartite graphs do not cover projective-plane Levi graphs. Database incompleteness and historical algebraic equivalences remain genuine priority risks, so this is a qualified finding rather than external certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This single natural graph family exhibits opposite global dynamical behavior after the smallest possible graph edit, uniformly across all existing even-order projective planes, including those without field coordinates. The universal nilpotency exponent five and the appearance of seven- and fourteen-cycles, together with exact basin populations that detect incidence binary rank, make the result a structural classification rather than a routine finite enumeration. The one-edge perturbation locates a sharp stability boundary in sparse, high-girth incidence graphs and supplies explicit test cases for robustness of linear Boolean networks.
