# A universal nodal Fourier spectrum over finite chain rings

## Finding
Let \(R\) be **any** finite commutative chain ring with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m=(\pi)\), nilpotency length \(k\ge1\), residue-field order \(q=|R/\mathfrak m|\ge2\), and \(|R|=q^k\). The canonical split nodal cone is
\[
C=\{(x,y)\in R^2:xy=0\}.
\]
Let \(T_C\) be **unnormalized** convolution by \(\mathbf1_C\) on \(\ell^2(R^2)\). Write \(\nu(0)=k\), and \(\nu(z)=j\) for \(z\in\mathfrak m^j\setminus\mathfrak m^{j+1}\). Choose any generating additive character \(\psi\) of \(R\); every character of \(R^2\) is \(\chi_{u,v}(x,y)=\psi(ux+vy)\).

**The complete Fourier multiplier** \(\Lambda(u,v)=\sum_{(x,y)\in C}\psi(ux+vy)\), with \(a=\nu(u)\) and \(b=\nu(v)\), is
\[
\Lambda(u,v)=q^{k-1}\begin{cases}
q+k(q-1),&a=b=k,\\
(q-1)(b+1),&a=k,\ b<k,\\
(q-1)(a+1),&b=k,\ a<k,\\
0,&a,b<k,\ a+b\le k-2,\\
(q-1)(a+b-k+1)-1,&a,b<k,\ a+b\ge k-1.
\end{cases}
\]
Consequently **the full integer spectrum, including every multiplicity**, is obtained by adding multiplicities for coincident eigenvalues from these disjoint classes:

1. The eigenvalue \(q^{k-1}[q+k(q-1)]\) occurs once.
2. For each \(b=0,\ldots,k-1\), the eigenvalue \((q-1)(b+1)q^{k-1}\) occurs \(2(q-1)q^{k-b-1}\) times.
3. For each \(j=0,\ldots,k-1\), the eigenvalue \(q^{k-1}[(q-1)j-1]\) occurs \((k-j)(q-1)^2q^{k-1-j}\) times.
4. The remaining \(q^{2k}-q^k[1+k(q-1)]\) frequencies have eigenvalue zero **before** any additional collisions with the zero eigenvalue listed in class 3.

The only negative eigenvalue is \(-q^{k-1}\), with multiplicity \(k(q-1)^2q^{k-1}\). The exact convolution rank is
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}T_C=\begin{cases}
q^k[1+k(q-1)],&q>2,\\
2^k(k+1),&q=2,\ k=1,\\
2^k(k+1)-(k-1)2^{k-2},&q=2,\ k\ge2.
\end{cases}}
\]
For \(q>2\), Fourier vanishing occurs exactly when \(a,b<k\) and \(a+b\le k-2\). For \(q=2\), **one additional antidiagonal** \(a+b=k\), with \(a,b<k\), also vanishes. Thus the exceptional cancellation depends on the residue-field *size*, not the characteristic of \(R\). All finite commutative chain rings with the same \((q,k)\) have the same complete convolution spectrum, even when their additive groups are nonisomorphic.

## Assumptions and scope
A finite commutative chain ring means a unital finite commutative ring whose ideals are linearly ordered by inclusion. The maximal ideal is generated by one element \(\pi\), and \(\mathfrak m^k=0\ne\mathfrak m^{k-1}\); the field \(R/\mathfrak m\) has \(q\) elements. The convention \(\nu(0)=k\) is essential. The Fourier transform and convolution are unnormalized; all eigenvalue formulas and ranks refer to the full \(q^{2k}\)-dimensional complex space of functions on the **additive** group \(R^2\). No assumption of odd characteristic or a particular ring presentation is made.

## Proof
**Generating characters.** Since \(\mathfrak m^{k-1}\) is a nonzero simple ideal, select a nontrivial additive character on this ideal and extend it to \(R^+\). Extension is possible for every character of a subgroup of a finite abelian group (extend across successive cyclic generators by choosing suitable complex roots). Call the extension \(\psi\). For any nonzero \(u\in R\), the ideal \(uR\) contains \(\mathfrak m^{k-1}\), so \(x\mapsto\psi(ux)\) is nontrivial. Hence the map \(u\mapsto\psi(u\cdot)\) identifies the \(q^k\) ring elements with all \(q^k\) additive characters. Product characters diagonalize \(T_C\); \(C=-C\), so the eigenvalues coincide with the displayed real Fourier sums.

**Valuation shells.** Every \(\mathfrak m^r\) has size \(q^{k-r}\). Character orthogonality gives
\[
\sum_{z\in\mathfrak m^r}\psi(uz)=q^{k-r}\mathbf1_{\{\nu(u)+r\ge k\}}.
\]
Subtract the next ideal to obtain, for \(0\le r<k\),
\[
S_r(u):=\sum_{\nu(x)=r}\psi(ux)=
q^{k-r}\mathbf1_{\{a+r\ge k\}}-q^{k-r-1}\mathbf1_{\{a+r+1\ge k\}}.
\]
In particular, when \(a<k\), this is zero for \(r<k-a-1\), equals \(-q^a\) at \(r=k-a-1\), and equals \((q-1)q^{k-r-1}\) for larger \(r\). When \(a=k\), it is \((q-1)q^{k-r-1}\) for every \(r\).

For \(\nu(x)=r\), the equation \(xy=0\) is equivalent to \(y\in\mathfrak m^{k-r}\), whose character sum is \(q^r\mathbf1_{\{b\ge r\}}\). Separating \(x=0\) therefore yields the exact finite identity
\[
\Lambda(u,v)=q^k\mathbf1_{\{v=0\}}+
\sum_{r=0}^{k-1}q^r\mathbf1_{\{b\ge r\}}S_r(u).
\]
If \(a,b<k\), the negative boundary term appears only when \(b\ge k-a-1\), and there are \(a+b-k+1\) subsequent positive terms, each \((q-1)q^{k-1}\). This proves the last two cases. If either frequency vanishes, direct summation of the equal terms proves the other three cases, including the \(u=v=0\) cardinality \(|C|=q^{k-1}[q+k(q-1)]\).

**Multiplicity and rank.** Each finite valuation shell contains \((q-1)q^{k-a-1}\) elements. Frequencies with exactly one zero coordinate supply class 2, while frequencies with \(a,b<k\) and \(a+b=k-1+j\) comprise \(k-j\) valuation pairs, each of size \((q-1)^2q^{k-1-j}\). The remaining pairs satisfy \(a+b\le k-2\) and give class 4. The zero-frequency class is unique. The displayed spectrum and all multiplicities follow.

For \(q>2\), class 3 never has a zero eigenvalue: its \(j=0\) entry is negative and every \(j\ge1\) entry is positive. For \(q=2\), its \(j=1\) entry vanishes and contributes exactly \((k-1)2^{k-2}\) additional zero eigenvalues when \(k\ge2\). The initial nonzero count is
\[
2q^k-1+(q-1)^2\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}(t+1)q^t
=q^k[1+k(q-1)],
\]
where the finite geometric derivative identity supplies the last equality. Subtract the extra binary-residue cancellation to obtain the rank formula. The \(j=0\) stratum gives the unique negative level and its multiplicity.

This proof holds simultaneously for all chain rings and all lengths; no finite computational sample is used to infer the general conclusion.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` using Python 3.9 or newer. The standalone stdlib checker forms every zero-product point and every frequency in \(\mathbb Z/p^k\mathbb Z\) for the explicitly listed small cases, and reduces all Fourier values **exactly** modulo the prime-power cyclotomic polynomial, avoiding floating-point zero tests. It also directly checks \(\mathbb F_p[t]/(t^k)\) for several cases and \(\mathbb F_4[t]/(t^2)\), distinguishing the residue-field-size-two cancellation from characteristic two itself. These cases verify the multipliers, rank, negative inertia, and trace in the listed finite instances; they do not replace the universal shell argument.

For example, the ranks at \((q,k)=(2,2),(2,3),(3,2),(4,2),(5,2)\) are respectively \(11,28,45,112,225\). The ring cardinality and nilpotency length, rather than its multiplication-table presentation, control the result.

## Relationship to prior work
Iosevich and Koh (arXiv:0804.4505v1, 28 April 2008) treat Fourier restriction/extension for nondegenerate quadratic surfaces over finite fields, principally nonzero level sets; their hypotheses do not establish the prime-power zero-product-cone spectrum here. Cai, Chen, Lipton and Lu (arXiv:1005.2632v1, 14 May 2010) establish an algorithm to evaluate general quadratic exponential sums over arbitrary cyclic moduli; it is an algorithmic method rather than the explicit all-finite-chain-ring zero-product spectral/inertia classification. Suntornpoch and Meemark (DOI 10.1017/S0004972715001380, first online 22 January 2016), particularly Sections 2–3, determine spectra of single-coordinate valuation-shell Cayley graphs on finite chain rings and tensor-product gcd graph families. Those results motivate, and supply related techniques for, the present analysis. Our connection set is the **nonseparable triangular condition** \(\nu(x)+\nu(y)\ge k\), not a single valuation-shell union in one chain ring or one rectangular tensor product. Its individual tensor terms interfere, and the exact extra \(q=2\) null stratum must be resolved before claiming the rank. No assertion is made that general Ramanujan-sum methods are new.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns exactly the indicator of \(xy=0\) over finite **commutative chain rings** and its unnormalized additive convolution. It does not cover arbitrary nonchain local rings, higher-product singularities, nonzero hyperbolas, nonabelian convolution or bounds for arbitrary Fourier multipliers. The universality of the final spectrum is not a claim of algebraic isomorphism of the underlying rings. The overlap check is bounded; an earlier exact formulation or an implicitly stronger general association-scheme computation could cover this specialization. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
