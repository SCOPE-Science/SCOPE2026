# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
Under a uniform-simplex slice, conditioning on one coordinate exceeding the threshold gives an exactly affine image of an independent uniform simplex. This yields the central covariance identity without approximation. Strict convexity of the conditional expected additive cost, negative derivative at zero (strictly positive covariance of a nondegenerate beta variable with an increasing derivative), and strict vertex-endpoint dominance prove a unique interior sign switch for every permitted cost. Beta and Dirichlet moment calculations give the explicit Greenwood sign threshold, variances and limiting correlation. Independent rational-integral calculations in the standalone verifier corroborate every algebraic component on a finite grid; they are not used as infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority limitations
The full 1988 Jammalamadaka–Wells article contains the marginal distribution of an extreme-spacing count, the spacing empirical-process Gaussian kernel, and efficiency comparisons with Greenwood. Those findings do not directly imply the finite-dimensional all-convex-cost covariance's strict one-switch property. Ghosh–Jammalamadaka (1998) contains moments and marginal approximations for additive spacing statistics, not this mixed law. Holst (1980) treats stick-breaking extremes and L'Ecuyer (1997) treats sum functions without this mixed finite-sample theorem. The prior max-versus-convex-additive law concerns a maximum (a Schur-convex statistic), whereas the threshold count need not be Schur-convex and has opposite signs in different regimes; neither result implies the other. No equivalent theorem was found in searched research records. Historical completeness cannot be certified, and the limiting covariance may be indirectly obtainable from older empirical-process theory.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The large-spacing exceedance count and convex sum-of-spacings tests are standard but distinct goodness-of-fit procedures. An exact universal single-sign-change relationship is information about their joint null calibration absent from marginal tables. The explicit crossover at twice the typical spacing and the nonzero, sign-changing correlation limit are substantive across sample sizes. The all-\(f\) theorem predicts the qualitative form for whole classes of concentration statistics and is not an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Boundaries and independent status
Only the uniform-simplex, strictly convex continuously differentiable, additive-cost domain is proven. The general root is not evaluated in closed form. Vanishing covariance does not assert independence. No independent validation has been performed.
