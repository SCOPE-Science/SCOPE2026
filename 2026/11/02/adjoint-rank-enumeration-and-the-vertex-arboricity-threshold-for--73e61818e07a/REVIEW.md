# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For every finite loopless quiver, the path-algebra quotient by the square of the arrow ideal has the vertex and arrow basis explicitly described in RESULT.md. Multiplication gives the commutator coordinate equation (5) without characteristic restrictions. Its nonmonochromatic arrow coordinates are independent pivots; the remaining linear map is a rescaled incidence matrix of a spanning multigraph and has rank equal to the number of vertices minus the number of connected components. This proves the rank formula (6), with exact exhaustive parameter counts for (1). Rank-nullity yields the centralizer cardinalities. Maximizing over arrow supports proves (3), including the forest and vertex-arboricity criterion. Summing centralizer sizes and changing the edge-subset/labeling order proves (4). No computational data replace these steps. The included direct field and matrix checker exhaustively verifies eight configurations, including parallel edges, two nonisomorphic orientations, a nonprime field, and the non-full-rank case of the five-vertex clique over the binary field. Edge-free quivers and isolated vertices satisfy the same counting formulas. Remaining scope risks: loops and nonzero paths of length two are excluded.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected literature

The exact all-quiver adjoint-rank polynomial, its orientation-blind cardinality distribution, and the maximal-rank criterion through induced-forest colorings are not asserted by the inspected primary papers. Dutta, Basnet and Nath (arXiv:1803.04111, §§2–3) compute commuting probabilities for finite rings possessing few distinct centralizers; this cannot imply all-multigraph rank strata. The full public RESULT.md of *Commutator fibers and commuting probabilities of finite Kronecker path algebras* (2026) explicitly treats the two-vertex, parallel-arrow special case, including its commuting probability and centralizer sizes. That result subsumes this special case but not the general all-quiver claim or its vertex-arboricity threshold. Green and Schroll (arXiv:2302.08169) instead study a quotient generated by differences of parallel paths, not element commutator ranks. Abdelgadir, Mellit and Rodriguez-Villegas (arXiv:1910.01633) study quiver varieties and a different Tutte-polynomial specialization. Shumyatsky and Vannacci (arXiv:2307.12633) give general structural implications of commuting probabilities, not all-quiver exact counts. The Fortuin–Kasteleyn identity is established prior mathematics and is not offered as a new theorem. Residual risk: an equivalent statement could be encoded in earlier general bilinear-incidence or graph-polynomial theory. Inaccessible records and nonmatching search results cannot prove global uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The classification supplies a natural invariant of an infinite family of finite associative algebras, with the exact multiplicity of every possible adjoint rank. Orientation independence is a nontrivial isomorphism-insensitive counting phenomenon. The maximal-rank condition translates precisely into an established graph-theoretic invariant (vertex arboricity), yielding an elementary obstruction in the five-vertex clique at field order two and removal of that obstruction at field order three. The commuting-pair count recovers a precise graph partition function; the result unifies centralizer enumeration and coloring obstructions across arbitrary finite quivers rather than recomputing a known numerical table. Its narrow scope and the previously known two-vertex case are explicit.

## Limitations

The findings concern centralizer **cardinalities**, not equality or isomorphism types of centralizer subalgebras. The graph-polynomial specialization and elementary incidence rank fact are prior results. Prior research on arbitrary bilinear commutator maps may cover the theorem implicitly; independent review and publication-priority verification remain outstanding. Exhaustive finite tests validate the implementation on tested cases but are not a proof in all characteristics.
