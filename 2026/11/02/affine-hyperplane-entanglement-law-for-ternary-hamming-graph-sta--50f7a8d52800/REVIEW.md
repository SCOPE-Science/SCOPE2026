# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The specified object is one qubit per vertex of \(H(d,3)\), with an unweighted pure controlled-phase graph state. The exact binary cut-matrix reduction, field-extension argument and orthogonality count yield the rank for every dimension and every affine hyperplane. The flat spectrum follows from an explicit computational-basis partial-trace identity, independent of a generic citation. A checker separately builds actual graph edges and cut matrices for \(1113\) affine normal-and-offset instances, independently verifies the base family through dimension eight, and diagonalizes two small reduced states. The finite computations are corroboration; they are not substituted for the character argument. Risk: no independent proof audit, and all-dimensional correctness rests on the stated convolution identification and additive-character count.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence

The 2003 and 2006 full-text graph-state sources contain the general rank-to-entanglement framework, but do not evaluate the characteristic-two cross matrices of ternary affine-hyperplane cuts. The 2022 full-text Hamming-graph source treats a different physical state and different subsystems; its full-graph adjacency diagonalization does not directly supply the required binary cut rank. Mathematical record searches under hyperplane, parity-class, graph-state, Hamming and finite-field convolution formulations found no equivalent statement among examined records. The closest previous exact claims concern binary hypercubes, periodic grids or classical spin configurations on ternary Hamming graphs, not the present object. This supports a best-evidence originality assessment only; a specialized coding-theoretic rank result or unpublished derivation could predate it. One broader graph-quantum-state paper was accessible in snippets but full-text examination was rate-limited, so no whole-document negative claim is made for it.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Affine hyperplanes are the canonical codimension-one additive partitions of a Hamming network. The theorem classifies the **entire spectrum**, not just an isolated rank, for every such partition. Its exact support-dependent deficit, sharp global minimum at support two, maximal mixing at support one, and alternating approach to a one-quarter deficit provide a natural structural entanglement phenomenon on an exponentially growing qubit network. These are not merely a renamed general cut-rank rule or a finite table: the theorem evaluates the previously unspecified cross rank and all hyperplane extrema by a finite-field convolution argument. The result supplies reproducible symmetry-adapted many-body benchmarks without assuming any unproved physical downstream use.
