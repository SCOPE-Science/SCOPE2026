# Affine-plane quantum transport has an exact parallel-line two-sink penalty

## Finding

Let \(\mathcal A\) be **any finite affine plane of order \(q\ge3\)**, including a non-Desarguesian one if it exists at that order. On its point–line incidence (Levi) graph, consider a single coherent excitation with uniform nonzero real adjacency hopping and two distinct absorbing vertices with arbitrary strictly positive absorption rates. Write \(N=q(2q+1)\) for the number of vertices. The dimension \(D\) of the bright subspace, and hence the absorption probability \(D/N\) of the maximally mixed single-excitation state, are exactly:

| Absorber locations | \(D\) |
|---|---:|
| Two points | \(6\) |
| One point and one line, incident or not | \(7\) |
| Two parallel lines | \(7\) |
| Two intersecting lines | \(8\) |

Thus a pair of intersecting line absorbers is exactly optimal among all two-vertex placements for the maximally mixed initial state. The gain over a parallel pair is \(1/N\), and the gain over two point absorbers is \(2/N\). These are exact infinite-time statements, not estimates of finite-time speed.

The theorem additionally supplies an **exact formula for capture from each individual starting vertex** for every absorber placement: the five explicit spectral projectors below, restricted to the two sinks, yield the probability by a sum of five at-most-two-dimensional Gram pseudoinverses.

In the particularly natural mixed placement where the absorbing point \(u\) lies on the absorbing line \(\ell\), that formula simplifies to a complete four-valued site distribution:

| Initial vertices | Count | Eventual capture probability |
|---|---:|---:|
| The two absorber vertices | \(2\) | \(1\) |
| Other points of \(\ell\), and other lines parallel to \(\ell\) | \(2(q-1)\) | \(1/(q-1)\) |
| Other lines through \(u\) | \(q\) | \(1/q\) |
| All remaining points and lines | \(2q(q-1)\) | \(1/[q(q-1)]\) |

These populations sum to \(N\), and their probability-weighted sum is \(7\), as required by the bright-subspace trace.

## Assumptions and scope

An affine plane of order \(q\) has \(Q=q^2\) points and \(L=q(q+1)\) lines, with \(q+1\) parallel classes, each consisting of \(q\) mutually disjoint lines. Every line contains \(q\) points, every point lies on \(q+1\) lines, and every pair of distinct points lies on exactly one line. Let \(B\) be its \(Q\)-by-\(L\) zero–one incidence matrix, and let
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&B\\ B^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The no-jump evolution on the network is generated by
\[
H_{\mathrm{eff}}=J A-\frac{i}{2}\bigl(\gamma_1|s_1\rangle\langle s_1|+\gamma_2|s_2\rangle\langle s_2|\bigr),
\]
where \(J\) is any nonzero real hopping amplitude, \(s_1\ne s_2\) are vertices, and both \(\gamma_j>0\). The loss is irreversible into external absorbing states; there is no dephasing, independent radiative loss, disorder, or drive. For a normalized initial site state \(|x\rangle\), the eventual capture probability is \(\eta(x)=1-\lim_{t\to\infty}\|e^{-iH_{\mathrm{eff}}t}|x\rangle\|^2\). The uniform mixture means \(I_N/N\), not a coherent uniform superposition. The theorem uses adjacency hopping rather than the graph Laplacian; those are different models on this biregular graph.

## Proof

**1. Incidence identities and the full spectral resolution.** Since every two points share one line,
\[
BB^{\mathsf T}=qI_Q+\mathbf 1_Q\mathbf 1_Q^{\mathsf T}.
\]
On the line side, \((B^{\mathsf T}B)_{tt}=q\), while off-diagonal entries equal \(1\) for intersecting lines and \(0\) for parallel lines. Consequently \(A\) has exactly five distinct eigenvalues \(\pm\sqrt{q(q+1)},\ \pm\sqrt q,\ 0\), with multiplicities \(1,1,Q-1,Q-1,q\), respectively.

For clarity define \(U=\mathbf1_Q\mathbf1_Q^{\mathsf T}/Q\), \(V=\mathbf1_L\mathbf1_L^{\mathsf T}/L\), \(C=\mathbf1_Q\mathbf1_L^{\mathsf T}/\sqrt{QL}\), \(X=I_Q-U\), \(Y=(B^{\mathsf T}B-\mathbf1_L\mathbf1_L^{\mathsf T})/q\), and \(Z=(B-\mathbf1_Q\mathbf1_L^{\mathsf T}/q)/\sqrt q\). Direct multiplication using the incidence identities shows that the orthogonal spectral projectors are
\[
E_{\pm\sqrt{q(q+1)}}=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}U&\pm C\\\pm C^{\mathsf T}&V\end{pmatrix},\quad
E_{\pm\sqrt q}=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}X&\pm Z\\\pm Z^{\mathsf T}&Y\end{pmatrix},\quad
E_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&I_L-V-Y\end{pmatrix}.
\]
They are pairwise orthogonal, sum to the identity, and obey \(AE_\lambda=\lambda E_\lambda\).

**2. Absorbing subspace and rates.** Let \(S\) be the two-vertex set and let \(\mathcal B=\sum_\lambda E_\lambda\operatorname{span}\{|s_1\rangle,|s_2\rangle\}\). Its orthogonal complement is invariant under \(A\), is annihilated by the sink projector, and evolves unitarily. On \(\mathcal B\), every eigenvalue of \(-iH_{\mathrm{eff}}\) has strictly negative real part: taking its inner product with an eigenvector shows that any eigenvector with zero real part must vanish at both sinks, and its \(A\)-eigenvector condition would then place it in \(\mathcal B^\perp\), a contradiction. All finite-dimensional Jordan blocks on \(\mathcal B\) consequently decay. Hence \(\eta(x)=\langle x|P_\mathcal B|x\rangle\), and \(\operatorname{tr}P_\mathcal B=D\); both are independent of the positive rates and the nonzero overall hopping scale.

In full detail let \(G_\lambda=(E_\lambda)_{S,S}\) and \(c_\lambda(x)=(E_\lambda)_{S,x}\). Then the promised exact sitewise formula is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle\eta(x)=\sum_{\lambda\in\{0,\pm\sqrt q,\pm\sqrt{q(q+1)}\}}c_\lambda(x)^{\mathsf T}G_\lambda^{+}c_\lambda(x),}
\]
where \(G_\lambda^{+}\) denotes the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse. This needs only the explicit incidence matrix and two-by-two arithmetic, not time integration.

**3. Complete bright-dimension classification.** Each principal eigenspace contributes rank one, regardless of the nonempty sink choice, giving two dimensions. For two point sinks, their restricted \(X\)-Gram matrix is \(I_2-\mathbf1_2\mathbf1_2^{\mathsf T}/q^2\), with eigenvalues \(1\) and \(1-2/q^2\); each nonprincipal sign contributes two more, and \(E_0\) contributes zero. This yields \(D=6\).

For one point and one line, the restricted Gram matrix of either nonprincipal projector is one-half of
\[
M_\delta=\begin{pmatrix}1-q^{-2}&(\delta-q^{-1})/\sqrt q\\(\delta-q^{-1})/\sqrt q&(q-1)/q\end{pmatrix},
\]
where \(\delta=1\) for incidence and \(0\) otherwise. Its determinant is \((q-1)^2/q^2\) if \(\delta=1\), and \((q^2-q-1)/q^2\) if \(\delta=0\); both are positive for \(q\ge3\). Therefore each nonprincipal sign has rank two, and \(E_0\) contributes one dimension from the line sink. The total is \(D=7\).

For two distinct line sinks, the restricted \(Y\)-matrix has diagonal \((q-1)/q\) and off-diagonal \(0\) for intersecting lines or \(-1/q\) for parallel lines. It is positive definite for \(q\ge3\), so the two nonprincipal signs contribute four dimensions. The restricted \(I_L-V-Y\) has diagonal \(1/(q+1)\) and off-diagonal \(-1/[q(q+1)]\) for intersecting lines or \(1/(q+1)\) for parallel lines. Its rank is two for intersecting lines and one for parallel lines. Adding the two principal contributions gives respectively \(D=8\) and \(D=7\). This proves every table entry and the optimal-placement statement.

**4. Explicit mixed-incident site law.** When \(u\in\ell\), the preceding \(M_\delta\) becomes \(M_1\), with positive determinant \((q-1)^2/q^2\). For a point \(p\), the paired nonprincipal contributions equal \(z^{\mathsf T}M_1^{-1}z\), with \(z=(\mathbf1_{p=u}-q^{-2},(\mathbf1_{p\in\ell}-q^{-1})/\sqrt q)^{\mathsf T}\); the two principal contributions total \(q^{-2}\). For a line \(t\), use \(z=((\mathbf1_{u\in t}-q^{-1})/\sqrt q,(|t\cap\ell|-1)/q)^{\mathsf T}\), where \(|t\cap\ell|=q\) if \(t=\ell\). Add the two principal contributions \(1/[q(q+1)]\) and the zero-mode contribution \((q+1)(E_0)_{t\ell}^2\). Substituting the three possible point types and four possible line types gives exactly the four values and multiplicities in the stated site-distribution table. The symbolic algebraic certificate checks all seven substitutions.

## Verification

The standalone programs `affine_check.py` and `symbolic_check.py` require Python, NumPy, and SymPy. Run `python affine_check.py` and `python symbolic_check.py` from the directory containing them. The first independently diagonalizes the full adjacency matrix of the prime-coordinate affine planes of orders \(3,5,7\), instead of using the projected matrices as its comparison oracle. It checks \(475\) two-sink placements and all site probabilities in those placements, including an exhaustive census of all \(210\) unordered sink pairs at order \(3\), and checks \(56\) full mixed-incident site histograms. Its largest numerical projector discrepancy is approximately \(5.33\times10^{-15}\). The second independently simplifies all critical Gram determinants, the seven mixed-incident site classes, the site-count identity, and the trace identity exactly with symbolic arithmetic. The embedded output files report the actual executions. Finite checks are corroboration, not the proof for arbitrary affine planes or non-Desarguesian cases.

## Relationship to prior work

Varbanov, Krovi and Brun (2008) establish the general quantum-hitting framework. Novo and collaborators (first public preprint 2014) develop invariant-subspace reductions for quantum transport on graphs and discuss concrete complete, bipartite, tree, and hypercube systems. Le and collaborators (2017) analyze dark-state suppression and graph-theoretic quantum networks. Razzoli, Paris and Bordone (2020/2021) classify single-trap efficiencies for several graph families, including strongly regular graphs. The general spectral/Krylov principles and the affine-plane incidence identities are not claimed as new. The claim here is the explicit *two-sink finite-affine-plane classification*, including the exact parallel-line penalty and incident mixed-placement site distribution. General single-sink or strongly-regular results do not give these figures directly: the affine incidence graph is biregular, has five eigenvalues and a line-supported zero eigenspace. A previously unknown specialized multi-trap incidence-geometry calculation could still affect historical priority.

## Limitations

The theorem applies to ideal coherent single-excitation adjacency transport with precisely two distinct, positive-rate irreversible absorbers and exact infinite-time limits. It does not cover Laplacian hopping on this biregular graph, noisy transport, varying edge weights, three or more absorbers, finite-time capture rates, or vertex-specific dissipation outside the sinks. Numerical checks are on Desarguesian prime-order planes only; coverage of all possible affine planes follows from the incidence axioms in the proof. Independent scientific audit is not asserted.
