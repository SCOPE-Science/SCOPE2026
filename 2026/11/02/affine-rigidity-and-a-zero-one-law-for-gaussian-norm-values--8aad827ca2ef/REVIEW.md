# Scientific review: affine Gaussian norm values

## Correctness — PASS
The proof counts every local valuation-one obstruction at each prime \(q\equiv3\pmod4\), \(q\nmid ab\), using a surjective non-split finite-field norm and nonzero derivative for each lift. It has exactly \(q(q^2-1)\) forbidden pairs modulo \(q^2\), including \(q=3\). Finite-prime CRT frequencies and divergence of reciprocal primes in that residue class give the zero-density conclusion. The zero-intercept dichotomy follows from Fermat–Euler's valuation criterion and the two-square product identity. The standalone exact verifier passed fourteen prime-local parameter tests and 194481 CRT-pair checks; these finite checks are not infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS, subject to priority risk
Radomskii's 2026 study concerns long intervals and primitive norms, not exact affine preservers on the unrestricted norm set or lattice-box frequencies. Harrington, Jones, and Lamarche characterize modular two-square solvability and record the classical closure identity, not the joint local density and global 0–1 theorem. Published literature and mathematical-record searches located no directly covering theorem. An earlier general sieve theorem might imply the zero-density conclusion, and priority remains unverified independently.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The theorem completely determines which integer affine maps preserve a classical quadratic norm set and exposes a universal, exactly counted local obstruction at every admissible inert prime. Its box-density zero–one law is more informative than a single constructed counterexample; the natural motivation is affine shifts of norm-representable integers studied in contemporary sieve work. Its limits exclude quantitative decay rates, primitive representations, arbitrary quadratic norms and densities among distinct norm values.
