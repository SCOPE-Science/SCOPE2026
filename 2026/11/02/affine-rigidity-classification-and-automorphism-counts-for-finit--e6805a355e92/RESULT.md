# Affine-rigidity classification and automorphism counts for finite abelian core quandles

## Finding
For a finite abelian group \(A\), write \(Q(A)\) for the quandle with operation \(x*y=2y-x\). Call an automorphism *affine* if it is \(x\mapsto a+\varphi(x)\) for \(a\in A\) and \(\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}(A)\). Then
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(Q(A))=A\rtimes\operatorname{Aut}(A)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
A_2\text{ is cyclic or }A_2\cong C_2\times C_2,
\]
where the trivial group is deemed cyclic and \(A_2\) is the Sylow-two subgroup.

There is also an exact full-group order formula without the affine restriction. Set \(H=2A\), \(\Omega=A/H\), \(d=|H[2]|=|H/2H|\), and let
\[
\delta:\Omega\longrightarrow H/2H,\qquad [x]\longmapsto[2x],
\qquad s=|\ker\delta|.
\]
Then \(\delta\) is surjective, \(|\Omega|=sd\), and
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}(Q(A))|
=|A|\,|\operatorname{Aut}(H)|\,(s-1)!\,(s!)^{d-1}d^{sd-1}.
\]
These two conclusions are parts of one classification theorem: they quantify and characterize exactly when the quandle has symmetries that do not arise from the affine group of its underlying abelian group.

In particular, for even \(n,m\), put \(A=C_n\oplus C_m\). The above count simplifies to
\[
|\operatorname{Aut}(R_n\times R_m)|=nm\,|\operatorname{Aut}(C_{n/2}\oplus C_{m/2})|\,\kappa(n,m),
\]
where \(\kappa=6\) if both moduli are \(2\pmod4\), \(\kappa=16\) if exactly one is \(2\pmod4\), and \(\kappa=64\) if both are divisible by four. For example \(|\operatorname{Aut}(R_4\times R_4)|=6144\), with affine subgroup of order \(1536\), whereas \(R_2\times R_2\) has \(24\) automorphisms, all affine.

## Assumptions and scope
All groups are finite, written additively. The operation is \(x*y=2y-x\) (right translations); an automorphism is a bijection respecting this operation. The map \(\delta\) is independent of coset representatives because changing \(x\) by \(h\in H\) changes \(2x\) by \(2h\). Its surjectivity follows from \(H=2A\). The symbol \(H[2]\) denotes the elements killed by multiplication by two, including zero. The formula applies to arbitrary finite abelian groups, with no parity or rank restriction. The simplified \(R_n\times R_m\) formula assumes both \(n\) and \(m\) are positive even integers.

## Proof
**1. The intrinsic translation subgroup.** For every \(y\in A\), the right translation \(R_y(x)=2y-x\) is an involution and
\[
R_yR_z^{-1}(x)=x+2(y-z).
\]
Thus the subgroup generated by products of right translations consists exactly of translations by \(H=2A\). A quandle automorphism \(F\) normalizes this intrinsic subgroup. Since the translation action of \(H\) is faithful, there is a unique \(\lambda\in\operatorname{Aut}(H)\) satisfying
\[
F(x+h)=F(x)+\lambda(h)\quad(x\in A,\ h\in H).
\]
Consequently \(F\) permutes the cosets \(\Omega\).

**2. Exact normal form.** Choose representatives \(r_c\in A\) for \(c\in\Omega\) with \(r_0=0\). Put \(a=F(0)\), and write \(F(r_c)=a+b_c\) so \(b_0=0\). Necessarily
\[
2b_c=\lambda(2r_c)\qquad(c\in\Omega).
\]
Indeed \(r_0*r_c=2r_c\), whose image is \(a+\lambda(2r_c)\), while \(F(r_0)*F(r_c)=a+2b_c\). Conversely, for any \(a\in A\), \(\lambda\in\operatorname{Aut}(H)\), and coset values \(b_c\) satisfying these equations and inducing a permutation via \(c\mapsto[a+b_c]\), define
\[
F(r_c+h)=a+b_c+\lambda(h).
\]
This is a bijection. Using \(x=r_c+h\), \(y=r_e+k\), we have
\[
x*y=r_c+[2(r_e-r_c)+2k-h],
\]
and the two sides of \(F(x*y)=F(x)*F(y)\) differ by \(\lambda(2r_e-2r_c)-(2b_e-2b_c)=0\). Hence the normal form is necessary and sufficient.

**3. Exact enumeration.** Fix \(a,\lambda\). If \(\sigma(c)=[a+b_c]\), the displayed doubling equation is solvable within the indicated coset exactly when
\[
\delta(\sigma(c))=[a]+\bar\lambda\delta(c)
\quad\text{in }H/2H,
\]
where \(\bar\lambda\) is induced by \(\lambda\). The map \(\delta\) has \(d\) fibers, each of size \(s\); the prescribed fiber bijections therefore allow exactly \((s-1)!(s!)^{d-1}\) choices of \(\sigma\), after imposing \(\sigma(0)=[a]\). Each \(c\ne0\) has exactly \(|H[2]|=d\) choices of \(b_c\), since the solution set to doubling on one \(H\)-coset is either empty or an \(H[2]\)-torsor. The zero coset forces \(b_0=0\). Multiplying by the independent \(|A|\) and \(|\operatorname{Aut}(H)|\) choices proves the count.

**4. Affine rigidity.** Split canonically \(A=A_2\oplus A_{\rm odd}\). If \(A_2=0\), then \(H=A\), so the normal form already gives \(F(x)=a+\lambda(x)\). If \(A_2\) is cyclic of order \(2^k\), the right-translation subgroup and the two cosets of \(2A\) show that \(F-a\) restricts on \(2A\) to \(\lambda\), and is completely determined by the image of a generator of \(A_2\). The doubling equation says that this image is an odd generator, and hence it determines a unique automorphism of the cyclic two-primary factor. The odd factor is forced by \(\lambda\); the result is an additive automorphism of \(A\). For \(k=1\), \(H\) has odd order and the same argument is immediate.

If \(A_2\cong C_2^2\), then \(H=A_{\rm odd}\) and \(H[2]=0\). The normal form shows that on the four two-primary cosets \(F\) induces an arbitrary permutation, while on the odd factor it acts affinely and independently. Every permutation of \(C_2^2\) is affine, since \(\operatorname{AGL}(2,2)\cong S_4\). Hence every \(F\) is affine also in this case.

Conversely, if the two-primary rank of \(A\) is at least two and \(A_2\) contains an element of order at least four, take nonzero \(t\in (2A)[2]\) and one nonzero coset \(c\in A/2A\). Define \(F(x)=x+t\) for \([x]=c\), and \(F(x)=x\) otherwise. This preserves the quandle operation: it is a special case of the normal form with \(a=0\), \(\lambda=\operatorname{id}\), and coset shifts killed by doubling. But \(F(0)=0\) while \(F\) is not additive: pick another independent nonzero coset \(e\), so the shifts at \(c,e,c+e\) are respectively \(t,0,0\). Thus it is nonaffine. Finally, if \(A_2\cong C_2^r\) with \(r\ge3\), its core quandle is trivial and supports every permutation. A permutation fixing zero but not preserving vector addition gives a nonaffine automorphism, extended by the identity on the odd factor. These cases exhaust finite abelian groups and prove the iff criterion.

**5. Two-factor parity specialization.** For even \(n,m\), the quotient \(\Omega\) has four elements. Each cyclic factor of order congruent to \(2\pmod4\) contributes one factor two to \(s\), whereas each factor divisible by four contributes one factor two to \(d\). Thus \((s,d)=(4,1),(2,2),(1,4)\); the factor \((s-1)!(s!)^{d-1}d^{sd-1}\) equals respectively \(6,16,64\), proving the displayed specialization.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify.py` performs direct exhaustive permutation tests on core quandles \(C_2^2\), \(C_2\oplus C_3\), \(C_2\oplus C_4\), \(C_4\oplus C_2\), \(C_2^3\), \(C_4\), \(C_6\), and \(C_8\) (each of size at most eight). It checks the exact count formula against all permutations for those samples, together with further count checks for \(C_2\oplus C_6\) and \(C_3^2\). Independently, it generates all \(6144\) automorphisms of \(C_4^2\) by the displacement normal form, tests all \(256\) ordered quandle-products for each, and independently enumerates the \(1536\) affine maps. Run `python verify.py`, which prints `VERIFY_OK` on success. Finite tests corroborate; the infinite family theorem follows from the displayed proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Bardakov and Sangare (2026, arXiv:2610.18676) establish the automorphism group for Latin Alexander quandles, compute odd-odd dihedral products and explicitly ask about even-factor dihedral products (Question 3.5). The formulas here address that non-Latin case and give a general finite-abelian affine-rigidity criterion. Sangare (2026, arXiv:2602.03080) establishes affine equality for abelian groups with no two-torsion and examples of non-group-induced automorphisms; the present theorem distinguishes all two-primary types. Elhamdadi, Macquarrie and Restrepo (2012) prove that automorphisms of each single dihedral quandle are affine, consistent with the cyclic case.

Hou (2011, DOI 10.1016/j.jalgebra.2011.06.027) announces a determination of automorphism groups of general Alexander quandles. This potentially broader work is a serious antecedent. The specific affine-rigidity iff and three-branch even-even enumeration were not located in accessible source statements; Hou's full text could not be obtained here, so the result's priority relative to that general classification is unresolved. The theorem should not be described as an independently certified first general description of Alexander-quandle automorphisms.

## Limitations
The theorem applies to the standard *core* operation on finite abelian groups and does not classify general Alexander quandles with other defining automorphisms, nonabelian cores, or automorphisms of quandle algebras. It gives cardinalities, not an abstract presentation for every automorphism group. Historical originality is qualified by the inaccessible full text of Hou (2011); finite exhaustive tests alone do not establish the all-group theorem. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
