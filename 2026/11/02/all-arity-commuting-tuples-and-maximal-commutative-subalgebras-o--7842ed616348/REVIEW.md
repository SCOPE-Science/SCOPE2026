# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Writing diagonal coefficient vectors and three classes of arrow/path coefficients converts the simultaneous commutator equations to three exterior-product constraints. The five disjoint cases for the two diagonal differences cover all possibilities and have exact multiplicities, including zero-dimensional tuple space, all characteristics, and both arrow multiplicities equal to one. Leading-term extraction is valid for fixed finite-field order, and the finite-cover argument correctly converts leading coefficients into counts of largest-dimensional unital commutative subalgebras. A standalone direct-multiplication verifier checked five complete commuting-pair censuses, an independent triple census, four bilinear core censuses, and three full binary maximal-subalgebra censuses. Exhaustive finite tests do not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected literature and available records

The closest full primary text, Kaur–Sharma–Singh (arXiv:2006.01493v1, 2020), is about invertible upper triangular and unitriangular groups, assumes odd field order and computes group branching matrices. It neither treats unrestricted nonunits nor independent parallel-arrow bundles, so it does not state or immediately imply the present all-arity full-algebra formula. Green–Schroll (arXiv:2302.08169v3, 2023) studies a different quotient called a *commuting algebra*, identifying parallel paths, and contains no element-commuting tuple counts. Earlier full-matrix similarity enumeration counts orbit types, not the present labelled tuples. The closest available published quiver algebra examples address radical-square-zero Kronecker algebras and commuting pairs of different four-vertex radicals; their underlying commutator spaces and quantifiers differ. There remains a risk that a broader unlocated result covers this family, so priority is provisional rather than certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a complete natural enumerative invariant for a nontrivial infinite family of hereditary finite-dimensional algebras with independent composable parallel arrows and nonzero radical square. It shows a structural dichotomy in the **number of maximum-dimensional commutative subalgebras**, uniquely determining a canonical arrow-layer subalgebra when arrow multiplicities differ and yielding exactly two when both multiplicities are at least two and equal. The smallest single-arrow case has a qualitatively different exact count. This goes beyond a routine one-off finite census; the full simultaneous-commuting sequence controls maximal commutative dimension and multiplicity.
