# All-arity finite-field commuting counts for the one-variable Witt Lie algebra

## Finding

Let \(p\) be a prime, \(q=p^e\) with \(e\ge1\), and
\(W_p(q)=\operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb F_q}(\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^p))\), with its ordinary Lie bracket. Let \(C_{p,q}(m)\) count ordered, pairwise commuting \(m\)-tuples in \(W_p(q)\), including the empty tuple for \(m=0\). Put \(s=\lfloor(p-1)/2\rfloor\). For every \(m\ge0\),
\[
\boxed{C_{p,q}(m)=1+(q^m-1)\left[q^{p-1}+\sum_{r=1}^{s}q^{p-2r+(r-1)m}+\sum_{r=s+1}^{p-1}q^{(p-r-1)m}\right].}
\]
For \(H_a=t^a\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^p)\,\partial_t\) with \(1\le a\le p\), the count of such tuples entirely in \(H_a\) is
\[
\boxed{C^{(a)}_{p,q}(m)=1+(q^m-1)\left[\sum_{r=a}^{s}q^{p-2r+(r-1)m}+\sum_{r=\max(a,s+1)}^{p-1}q^{(p-r-1)m}\right].}
\]
Empty sums vanish. These are exact finite-field point counts, including characteristic two, not just component dimensions. For odd \(p\), \(C_{p,q}(2)=q^{p+1}+\frac{p-1}{2}(q^2-1)q^{p-2}\); for \(p=2\), \(C_{2,q}(2)=q^3+q^2-q\). For instance \(C_{5,5}(2)=21625\) and \(C_{5,5}(3)=186125\).

## Assumptions and definitions

Write \(k=\mathbb F_q\), \(R=k[t]/(t^p)\) and \(\partial_t=d/dt\). It is a derivation on \(R\) because \(p=0\) in \(k\). Every derivation is uniquely \(f\partial_t\), \(f\in R\), and
\([f\partial_t,g\partial_t]=(fg'-gf')\partial_t\). For nonzero \(f\), let \(\nu(f)\) be the least exponent with nonzero coefficient; put \(\nu(0)=p\). The tuples are labeled and ordered, with no restriction on the individual entries and no quotient by automorphisms or conjugacy. The filtered algebra \(H_p\) is zero.

## Proof

**1. Leading terms and centralizers.** If \(r=\nu(f)=0\), \(f\) is a unit and \(fg'-gf'=f^2(g/f)'\). As the kernel of differentiation in \(R\) is exactly \(k\), its centralizer is \(kf\partial_t\).

Suppose \(1\le r<p\), and \(g\ne0\) has valuation \(j\). Whenever \(r+j-1<p\), the coefficient of \(t^{r+j-1}\) in the bracket is \((j-r)f_rg_j\), where \(f_r,g_j\ne0\). For \(j\ne r\), this coefficient is nonzero: \(j-r\not\equiv0\pmod p\) since \(0\le j,r<p\). Thus no commuting \(g\) can start in a degree \(j\ne r\) with \(r+j-1<p\).

If \(1\le r\le s\), every centralizing \(g\) is uniquely \(cf+h\), where \(c\in k\) and \(h\in H_{p-r+1}\). Indeed a commuting \(g\) must start in degree \(r\) (or at least \(p-r+1\)); subtract \(cf\) to remove that leading term and iterate the leading-term obstruction. Conversely any \(h\) of degree at least \(p-r+1\) commutes with \(f\), because both terms of its bracket have degree at least \(p\). Therefore
\[
Z(f\partial_t)=kf\partial_t\oplus H_{p-r+1}\quad(1\le r\le s).
\]
In particular this centralizer is abelian: its tail is abelian because the bracket of two elements of \(H_{p-r+1}\) is supported beyond degree \(p-1\), and \(f\) centralizes the tail.

If \(r\ge s+1\), the filtered algebra \(H_r\) is abelian: the bracket of two degree-at-least-\(r\) terms has degree at least \(2r-1\), and at the borderline \(p=2,r=1\) the only possibly surviving leading bracket is zero because its coefficient is \(r-r=0\). For \(p\) odd the displayed lower degree already satisfies \(2r-1\ge p\). We do not need the entire centralizer in the high-valuation case. **In characteristic two, \(Z(t\partial_t)=H_1\), not \(H_2=0\); this boundary case is explicitly retained and does not change the tuple count.**

**2. Partition by minimal valuation.** For a nonzero commuting ordered tuple \((f_i\partial_t)_{i=1}^m\), let \(r=\min_i\nu(f_i)\). These strata are disjoint. If \(r=0\), the entries span a line generated by a unique unit \(f\) normalized to constant coefficient one. There are \(q^{p-1}\) lines and \(q^m-1\) choices of a nonzero scalar \(m\)-tuple; the contribution is \(q^{p-1}(q^m-1)\).

For \(1\le r\le s\), a tuple with an entry of valuation \(r\) lies in its abelian centralizer. Its images in \(H_r/H_{p-r+1}\) lie in a uniquely determined one-dimensional subspace whose generator may be normalized to coefficient one at degree \(r\). This quotient has dimension \(p-2r+1\), giving \(q^{p-2r}\) such lines. Fixing a normalized lift \(f\), every tuple entry is \(c_i f+h_i\), \(h_i\in H_{p-r+1}\), and at least one \(c_i\ne0\). The tail has dimension \(r-1\), so the contribution is
\[
q^{p-2r}(q^m-1)q^{(r-1)m}.
\]
The quotient normalization is essential: a full representative \(f\) would otherwise overcount lifts differing by tail vectors.

For \(s+1\le r\le p-1\), the entire \(H_r\) is abelian. Every tuple in \(H_r^m\setminus H_{r+1}^m\) is therefore commuting and has minimum valuation exactly \(r\). This stratum has size
\[
q^{(p-r)m}-q^{(p-r-1)m}=(q^m-1)q^{(p-r-1)m}.
\]
This argument also covers \(p=2,r=1\) directly. Summation over all strata and addition of the zero tuple prove the first formula for \(m\ge1\); for \(m=0\) both sides equal one.

**3. Filtered subalgebras and pairs.** A tuple belongs to \(H_a^m\) exactly when its minimum valuation is at least \(a\), except for the zero tuple, so restricting the same disjoint strata proves the second formula. When \(p=2s+1\), setting \(m=2\) produces \(s\) copies of \(q^{p-2}\) in the middle sum and the geometric sum \(1+q^2+\cdots+q^{2(s-1)}\) in the final sum. Cancelling the latter against the factor \(q^2-1\) gives the pair formula. At \(p=2\), direct substitution yields \(q^3+q^2-q\). This completes the proof.

## Verification and reproducibility

The unchanged standalone `verify.py` constructs the matrices of \(\operatorname{ad}(f\partial_t)\) from \([t^i\partial_t,t^j\partial_t]=(j-i)t^{i+j-1}\partial_t\) in the truncated ring. It uses finite-field elimination to enumerate actual centralizer vectors and counts all commuting ordered pairs and triples; it does not build the centralizers from the target formula. Its verification includes \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_4,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_9,\mathbb F_5\). Direct finite-field enumeration reproduces \(C_{2,2}(2)=10\), \(C_{2,2}(3)=22\), \(C_{3,3}(2)=105\) and \(C_{3,3}(3)=339\). These finite checks corroborate the theorem but do not substitute for its all-arity proof.

## Relationship to previous results and limitations

Yao and Chang's *Nilpotent commuting varieties of the Witt algebra*, arXiv:1301.5667 (2013), gives relevant centralizer descriptions and irreducible components over algebraically closed fields. Yao and Chang's *Commuting variety of Witt algebra*, DOI:10.1007/s11464-018-0725-9 (2018), treats the pair variety, and Yao and Zhang's *Commuting variety of r-tuples over the Witt algebra*, DOI:10.3969/j.issn.1000-5641.2021.03.001 (2021), treats tuple-variety geometry. Centralizer descriptions and geometric component results are not claimed new. The asserted addition here is the exact disjoint finite-field enumeration for every arity, including standard filtered subalgebras, with normalization preventing overcounting.

The result counts \(\mathbb F_q\)-points of ordinary commuting tuples only; it does not classify components or scheme structures, orbit classes, restricted nilpotent tuples, or different truncation exponents. It makes no historical-priority certification. The relevant later articles could contain an equivalent arithmetic enumeration not established by accessible material; their full texts have not been conclusively compared.
