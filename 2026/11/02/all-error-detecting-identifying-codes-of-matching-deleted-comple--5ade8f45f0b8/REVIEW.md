# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof uses the published characterization of error-detecting identifying codes by two-domination and asymmetric \(2^{\#}\)-distinguishing of all distinct vertex pairs. Two identical open-neighborhood unmatched vertices make codes impossible. Exact exclusive-neighborhood formulas force a single possible omission from each matched shore, and unmatched vertices force entire opposite shores into the code. The one remaining class of adjacent matched pairs gives sharp thresholds at matching sizes three, four and five. The proof treats all smaller orders and every pair type. An independent direct-neighborhood enumeration covered 155 bounded triples and a separate construction checker verified 32 larger instances. Finite checks are supporting evidence, not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual publication-priority uncertainty

Jean and Seo, *Optimal Error-detection system for Identifying Codes*, arXiv:2208.06052 (2022), Theorem 2.1, supply the underlying general code criterion; its complete accessible manuscript covers hypercubes, cubic graphs and infinite grids, but not the matching-deleted complete bipartite graph family or its all-code polynomial. Their later published version, first online October 2024, was accessible through metadata/abstract but not full text, and could contain changes not checked here. Seo and Slater's 2018 generalized systems give a framework rather than a family census. Related ordinary identifying-code, redundant identifying-code, and locating-dominating results use different conditions; no exact stronger result implying the present coefficients was found. Searches of research records, published literature, equivalent terminology, and the related published results did not reveal a duplicate, but absence of a search hit is not a uniqueness proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem is a complete classification, existence criterion, and labelled set enumerator for every bipartite graph obtained by deleting a matching from a complete bipartite graph. It identifies a sharp transition in extremal multiplicity: quadratic for a full deleted matching, linear with one unmatched vertex, unique/full with an unmatched vertex on each shore, and impossibility when there are at least two unmatched vertices on either shore. It establishes explicit high detector-density examples, and the counts resolve information not given by the general criterion or an optimal cardinality alone.

## Limits of assessment

No independent mathematical audit has been performed. The published journal text and the 2018 generalized-system article were not fully inspected; their possible unexamined extensions are residual priority risks. The theorem does not address stronger error correction, other omitted-edge patterns, or code orbits under graph automorphisms.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
