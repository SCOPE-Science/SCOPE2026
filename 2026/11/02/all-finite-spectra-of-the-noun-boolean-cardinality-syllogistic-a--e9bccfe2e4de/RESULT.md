# All finite spectra of the noun-Boolean cardinality syllogistic are additive semigroups

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal L_{\vee}\) extend the finite cardinality syllogistic by Boolean operations **on nouns** only: complement and binary union. Its sentences remain inclusion, nonempty intersection, non-strict cardinality comparison and strict cardinality comparison. Then a subset of the positive integers is the finite spectrum of a possibly infinite theory if and only if it is either empty or closed under addition. Moreover every such spectrum is already the spectrum of a finite theory using only inclusion and non-strict comparisons. Thus all positive additive semigroups, including those with nontrivial finite gaps such as \(\langle4,7\rangle\), occur.

## Assumptions and scope
The domain \(U\) is any **nonempty finite set**. A raw noun is interpreted as a subset of \(U\); \(\overline A\) means \(U\setminus A\) and \(A\vee B\) means \(A\cup B\). There are no Boolean sentence connectives; a theory \(\Gamma\) is an arbitrary set of sentences interpreted conjunctively through satisfaction of every member. The four sentence forms are \(\forall(A,B)\) meaning \(A\subseteq B\), \(\exists(A,B)\) meaning \(A\cap B\neq\varnothing\), \(\exists^{\ge}(A,B)\) meaning \(|A|\ge|B|\), and \(\exists^{>}(A,B)\) meaning \(|A|>|B|\). Set \(\operatorname{Spec}(\Gamma)=\{|U|>0:U\text{ admits a model of }\Gamma\}\). An *additive positive semigroup* is a nonempty subset of \(\mathbb N_{>0}\) closed under addition; no multiplicative or identity condition is imposed.

## Proof
**1. Every spectrum is additive.** If \(M_1,M_2\) are finite models of the same theory, replace their universes by disjoint copies and interpret each raw noun as the disjoint union of its two interpretations. Induction on noun terms, including complement relative to the disjoint-union universe, shows each term evaluates to the disjoint union of its two previous values. Inclusion persists, a nonempty intersection persists, and the two inequalities persist after adding their cardinalities (the sum of two strictly positive differences is strictly positive). Thus \(|M_1|+|M_2|\in\operatorname{Spec}(\Gamma)\). This applies to infinite theories without compactness assumptions.

**2. Every nonempty additive positive set is finitely generated.** Let \(S\) be nonempty and additive, and put \(m=\min S\). For each residue \(r\) modulo \(m\) represented in \(S\), let \(b_r\) be its least representative. If \(s\in S\) has residue \(r\), then \(s=b_r+tm\) with \(t\in\mathbb N_0\). Conversely \(b_r+tm\in S\) by additivity (take \(t=0\) separately). Therefore \(S\) is generated by \(m\) and the finitely many \(b_r\). The residue-zero representative is \(m\), so at most \(m\) distinct generators are needed.

**3. Finite theory for a prescribed finitely generated spectrum.** Fix integers \(a_1,\ldots,a_q\ge1\). Introduce mutually distinct raw nouns \(P_{i,j}\), one for each \(1\le i\le q\), \(1\le j\le a_i\). Let \(W=\bigvee_{i=1}^{q}\bigvee_{j=1}^{a_i}P_{i,j}\), a syntactically parenthesized binary-union noun. Impose the following finite list of permitted sentences:

* For any distinct labels \((i,j)\ne(k,\ell)\), assert \(\forall(P_{i,j},\overline{P_{k,\ell}})\).
* Assert \(\forall(\overline W,W)\).
* For each \(i\) and \(j\), assert both \(\exists^{\ge}(P_{i,1},P_{i,j})\) and \(\exists^{\ge}(P_{i,j},P_{i,1})\).

The first family makes all labelled sets pairwise disjoint. The second, because \(W\cap\overline W=\varnothing\), forces \(W=U\). The third forces the \(a_i\) sets in group \(i\) to have equal cardinality \(t_i\in\mathbb N_0\). Thus every nonempty model has \(|U|=\sum_{i=1}^q a_i t_i>0\). Conversely, for every such sum partition any \(U\) of that cardinality into \(a_i\) sets of size \(t_i\) for each group and interpret the nouns by those parts. This satisfies every sentence. Hence the spectrum is exactly the generated positive semigroup.

**4. Empty spectrum.** The two inclusion sentences \(\forall(P,\overline P)\) and \(\forall(\overline P,P)\) force both \(P\) and its complement to be empty, so no nonempty universe supports them. No existence or strict-cardinality form is required.

The preceding steps prove both directions and finite realization. For instance the generators \(4,7\) yield exactly \(\{4a+7b>0:a,b\in\mathbb N_0\}\), which includes \(4,7,8,11,12,14,15,16,\ldots\), with gaps \(1,2,3,5,6,9,10,13,17\).

## Relationship to prior work
Ruiting Jiang's 2026 paper (arXiv:2609.24902), Theorem 3.16, classifies the **bare-noun** system by eventual positive tails or eventual even tails. Section 4 adds Boolean operations **to sentences**, yielding finite unions of those tails. Section 5.1 instead introduces union and complement **to nouns** and constructs the special spectrum of positive multiples of three, but does not state or prove the complete additive-semigroup characterization. The present finite-generator partition construction is a classification of this latter syntactic extension. Earlier work by Ding, Harrison-Trainor and Holliday studies completeness of a richer Boolean set-term/cardinality language, while Kopczyński and Tan classify spectra of a richer counting logic as all semilinear sets. Neither of those reported results identifies the exact proper subclass of spectra belonging to this restricted noun-Boolean syllogistic.

## Limitations
This is a theorem about arbitrary theories over finite nonempty universes and about noun-level Boolean terms, not the different sentence-level Boolean extension. The result does not classify spectra when arbitrary sentence disjunctions are added to this stronger noun language. The proof requires no decision-procedure complexity assertion. Literature priority outside the inspected sources remains uncertain, particularly because older comparative-cardinality systems contain richer term constructions.
