# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof classifies ranks zero, one, and two without assuming diagonality or algebraic closure. Simplicity, absence of nonzero square-zero elements, trivial endomorphisms/derivations, and the ordered-root projection correspondence are all proved from the multiplication table. Weight-zero rank-one maps are possible only at the unique repeated root in characteristic \(31\). The independent finite-field checker exhaustively compares the entire operator sets over nine finite fields including \(\mathbb F_{31}\), and returns `VERIFY_OK`; generality rests on symbolic proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The closest primary source, Kaygorodov–Martín González–Viruel (arXiv:2610.22248v1), explicitly poses Rota–Baxter classification on perfect evolution algebras as Question 39. Qaralleh–Mukhamedov–Khakimov (arXiv:2601.08669v1) classify operators on nilpotent maximal-nilindex algebras, a disjoint domain. Related published calculations for exterior algebras and diagonal operators on evolution cycles do not imply the exact all-operator classification here. An equivalent historical table under a different low-dimensional classification label cannot be excluded; absolute priority is not claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The problem is the complete first two-dimensional simple perfect case of a recent operator-classification question. It reveals an exact arithmetic classification: for each nonzero weight, all solutions are splitting projections and their number depends on rational roots of a cubic; at weight zero a nontrivial operator family appears if and only if the field has characteristic \(31\), exactly the cubic discriminant prime. That exceptional characteristic is a structural boundary, not a chosen arbitrary finite slice.

## Scope and limits
The theorem does not classify arbitrary perfect evolution algebras or the full nonreduced operator scheme. Exhaustive tests are finite supporting checks; the proof addresses arbitrary fields. Historical-priority uncertainty remains until broader bibliographic audit.
