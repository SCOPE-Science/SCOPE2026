# An 80-bit binary counterexample to the factor-two greedy superstring bound

## Finding

For a finite set \(S\) of strings, let \(\operatorname{OPT}(S)\) be the minimum length of a word containing every member of \(S\) as a contiguous factor. The maximum-overlap greedy algorithm repeatedly replaces an ordered pair of distinct live words with their overlap merge, choosing a pair whose suffix-prefix overlap is maximal among all live ordered pairs. Ties may be resolved arbitrarily.

**Theorem.** There exists a set \(S\subset\{0,1\}^{80}\) of exactly \(252\) distinct binary words such that \(\operatorname{OPT}(S)=2590\), and a legal \(251\)-merge greedy execution on \(S\) outputs a word of length \(5190\). Consequently the worst-case greedy approximation ratio over binary words of common length \(80\) satisfies

\[
\rho_{80}^{(2)}\ge \frac{5190}{2590}=\frac{519}{259}=2+\frac1{259}>2.
\]

The instance, all its merging choices, and an optimal common superstring are generated by the accompanying standalone `verify_binary80.py`; no external packages or optimization solver are needed.

## Assumptions and scope

A positive suffix-prefix overlap of words \(u,v\) is the largest positive integer \(r\) for which the last \(r\) characters of \(u\) equal the first \(r\) characters of \(v\); it is zero when no such integer exists. For any ordered distinct live pair, its merge is \(u\) followed by \(v\) with the common prefix of length \(r\) deleted. The theorem concerns the standard *worst legal tie-breaking* version of greedy, not every possible execution on this instance.

Start with the \(17\)-symbol alphabet \(\Gamma=\{x,y,a,b_1,\ldots,b_{14}\}\). For every \(1\le i\le14\), define three cyclic words

\[
U_i=xaxb_ixaxy,\qquad V_i=xaxb_i,\qquad W_i=xaxyxb_i.
\]

For a cyclic word \(w\), let \(\mathcal C_8(w)\) be the set of all length-\(8\) factors of its infinite periodic continuation, one starting at each position of \(w\). Define

\[
T=\bigcup_{i=1}^{14}\bigl(\mathcal C_8(U_i)\cup\mathcal C_8(V_i)\cup\mathcal C_8(W_i)\bigr).
\]

The generated \(8+4+6=18\) words for each \(i\) are all distinct, giving \(|T|=252\). This is the length-eight obstruction of *The greedy superstring ratio at small string lengths*, not a newly discovered large-alphabet example.

Let \(C\) be the first \(17\) words, in lexicographic order, of

\[
\{c\in\{0,1\}^{10}:c\text{ begins with }000,\ c\text{ ends with }1,\text{ and }000\text{ occurs nowhere else in }c\}.
\]

This defining set has \(24\) members. Choose an arbitrary fixed bijection \(E:\Gamma\to C\) (the verifier uses \(x,y,a,b_1,\ldots,b_{14}\) in that order and lexicographic order on \(C\)). Extend \(E\) by concatenation to finite words. The binary instance is \(S=E(T)\). Every member has length \(80\).

## Proof

**1. Exact preservation of overlaps.** Any two words \(c,d\in C\), including \(c=d\), have no nonempty proper suffix-prefix overlap. For lengths one or two, the suffix ends in \(1\) and the prefix begins in \(0\). For lengths three to nine, a prefix begins with \(000\), whereas no proper suffix begins with \(000\). Thus \(C\) is cross-bifix-free.

For all \(u,v\in\Gamma^*\), an overlap of \(E(u)\) with \(E(v)\) whose length is not divisible by ten would force a nonempty proper suffix of a codeword to equal a prefix of another. This contradicts cross-bifix-freeness. In an overlap divisible by ten, aligned codewords are equal exactly when their source symbols are equal, since \(E\) is injective. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{ov}(E(u),E(v))=10\operatorname{ov}(u,v).
\]

Consequently \(E(u\odot v)=E(u)\odot E(v)\), and a greedy merge of a pair of source words is maximal if and only if the encoded pair is maximal among the encoded live words. This remains true for merged intermediate words of arbitrary lengths.

**2. A finite source certificate.** The standalone verifier generates all \(252\) source words from the cyclic definitions. Within each \(U_i,V_i,W_i\) group it merges the cyclic windows at overlap seven in the order chosen by a specified border, obtaining \(42\) live words. It then merges the \(V_i\) and \(U_i\) aggregates at overlap one, chains the resulting \(14\) aggregates at overlap one, and appends the remaining \(14\) \(W_i\) aggregates at overlap zero. At every one of the \(251\) steps, it independently recomputes the maximum overlap across *all* ordered distinct live pairs and checks equality with the chosen overlap. It also checks that no live word is a substring of any other live word. The final source word has length \(519\). This finite exhaustive computation certifies a valid greedy history; it is not an inference from a sampled trace.

**3. Binary greedy length.** By Step 1, the complete source greedy history lifts to \(S\); the resulting binary word has length \(10\cdot519=5190\).

**4. An optimal binary superstring.** The verifier constructs an Euler tour in the directed order-seven de Bruijn graph of \(T\), checking that every one of its \(252\) edges is traversed and that the circuit is closed. This yields an explicit source common superstring of length \(252+7=259\). Its image under \(E\) contains every binary input word and has length \(2590\).

For the matching lower bound, the binary inputs are pairwise distinct, and their pairwise proper suffix-prefix overlaps have length at most \(70\) by Step 1. In any common superstring, choose one occurrence of each of the \(252\) inputs and order their distinct starting positions. Successive start positions must differ by at least ten: a smaller gap would create a forbidden overlap longer than seventy. Hence every common superstring has length at least \(80+251\cdot10=2590\). The constructed word attains this bound.

Steps 1–4 yield the claimed exact optimum and strict factor-two failure.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_binary80.py` from the directory containing this document. Its checks use exact strings and integers only: the \(17\) binary codewords and their cross-bifix conditions, all \(252\) distinct binary inputs, all \(252\cdot251=63252\) ordered distinct source/binary input overlaps, all \(251\) maximum-overlap greedy choices, all source and binary superstring containment relations, and the exact arithmetic \(5190>2\cdot2590\). The script also independently constructs the \(259\)-character source Eulerian superstring and checks its \(252\) contained words. The recorded output is `verify_binary80.out`.

## Relationship to prior work

Shibata (2026) disproved the factor-two greedy conjecture on alphabets growing with the construction, including a length-ten source example. The September 2026 *Greedy superstring ratio at small string lengths* gave the length-eight, \(252\)-word, \(519/259\) source obstruction used here. These source results, their ratios and their source merging histories are credited as established, not new.

Vassilevska (2005) already showed that a suitable alphabet-to-binary substitution preserves superstring approximation ratios, using code length \(2(q+1)\) for a \(q\)-letter alphabet. Applied to the \(17\)-letter source obstruction, that particular construction gives \(8\cdot36=288\)-bit inputs. The present result uses a shorter explicitly verified cross-bifix-free code, proving the **quantitative binary length-eighty bound** and giving its complete finite witness. The fact that binary alphabet reduction is possible, or that greedy can exceed factor two somewhere over binary words, is not presented as novel.

## Limitations

This is an existence theorem for a particular finite input set and a legal adversarial tie-breaking. It neither proves \(80\) is the smallest binary input length allowing a factor-two failure nor claims that every greedy execution on this instance is suboptimal. The shorter code and length-eighty cutoff may also be independently obtainable from standard non-overlapping-code constructions; unindexed prior constructions remain a publication-priority risk. No independent audit has been performed.
