# Research assessment: affine-design negative-type threshold

## Correctness — PASS

The argument is an all-parameter proof for existing affine resolvable designs \(\mathrm{AD}(n,\mu)\) with \(n\ge3\). The asserted distance matrix follows from point/block incidence and disjoint parallel classes. The two incidence Gram identities split the entire zero-sum space into \(r-1\) parallel-class modes, \(v-1\) coupled two-dimensional modes, and one point-versus-block constant contrast. The latter is strictly negative up to the parallel bound by an explicit convex quadratic and two strictly negative endpoint evaluations. A monotonic scalar function gives one unique transversal boundary; integrality of the design parameter separates the two branches strictly. Dimension counts and the small-exponent asymptotic are independently reconstructed. The standalone crosscheck compares whole compressed spectra, and breadth-first-search shortest-path matrices for four finite geometries, against independently enumerated affine hyperplanes. No infinite claim relies on this finite crosscheck.

## Originality — PASS, with bounded historical risk

Sánchez's 2011 arXiv full text supplies general finite-metric negative-type criteria, not the incidence-design classification. Ma's 2016 arXiv full text contains the incidence-design algebra and complete ordinary-distance characteristic polynomial, not the sharp powered-distance boundary, the forbidden simultaneous contact, or the high-index endpoint embedding dimension. The affine-plane case is acknowledged as covered previously; in particular an accessible research claim already establishes its critical exponent and is not the new contribution. The nontrivial new part treats **every existing higher-index affine resolvable design** and proves that its obstruction necessarily moves to the \(v-1\) transversal sector. Searches using source identities, aliases, kernel positivity, generalized roundness, and exact incidence parameters found no broader covering statement. Residual risk: an equivalent analysis may occur in earlier distance-spectrum, negative-type, or combinatorial-design literature without using these terms.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem determines a natural embedding invariant and its equality structure for a whole combinatorial family, including potentially nonisomorphic designs with identical parameters. The result provides a strict structural separation between affine planes and higher-index designs and changes the critical nullity from \(r-1\) to \(v-1\), not merely a recomputation of a known ordinary-distance eigenvalue. Its sharp threshold and explicit least Euclidean embedding dimension inform the geometry of incidence metrics; the asymptotic exhibits strong decay along higher-dimensional finite affine spaces.

## Limits

Only existing affine resolvable designs with \(n\ge3\) are covered. This gives no classification of design existence or isomorphism types. The proof determines universal-in-scale Gaussian positive definiteness, not positivity of each individually chosen scale beyond the threshold. The numerical checks do not establish any case not proved analytically. Equivalent historical coverage has not been ruled out completely, and independent mathematical validation has not been performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
