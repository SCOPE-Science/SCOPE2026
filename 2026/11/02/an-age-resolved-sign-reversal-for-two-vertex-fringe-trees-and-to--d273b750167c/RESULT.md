# An age-resolved sign reversal for two-vertex fringe trees and total path length

## Finding
For a uniform random recursive tree on \(n\ge 3\) vertices, let \(P_n\) be the sum of all root-to-vertex depths and let \(Z_{n,i}\) indicate that the fringe subtree of the vertex born at time \(i\) has exactly two vertices. For every \(2\le i\le n-1\),
\[
\Pr(Z_{n,i}=1)=\frac{(i-1)(n-i)}{(n-1)(n-2)},
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(Z_{n,i},P_n)=
\frac{(i-1)(n-i)}{(n-1)(n-2)}
\left[2(H_{i-1}-H_{n-2})+\frac{n-2}{n-1}\right],
\]
where \(H_m=\sum_{r=1}^m r^{-1}\). For every \(n\ge4\), the expression in brackets increases strictly with \(i\), is negative at \(i=2\), and is positive at \(i=n-1\). Hence the covariance changes sign exactly once in insertion-age order, allowing a zero at the boundary between the two signs.

If \(i=i_n\) and \(i_n/n\to\alpha\in(0,1)\), then
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(Z_{n,i_n},P_n)\longrightarrow
\alpha(1-\alpha)(1+2\log\alpha).
\]
The limiting sign switches sharply at \(\alpha=e^{-1/2}\). This concerns the *birth-indexed* statistic, not the previously studied unmarked number of two-vertex fringe subtrees.

A stronger conditional distribution identity underlies the result. For any \(i<j\le n\), let \(E_{i,j}\) be the event that vertex \(i\) has exactly one descendant, namely a child \(j\). Then
\[
\Pr(E_{i,j})=\frac{i-1}{(n-1)(n-2)},
\qquad
(P_n\mid E_{i,j})\;\overset{d}{=}\;P'_{n-2}+2D'_A+3,
\]
where the primed quantities refer to a uniform random recursive tree on the \(n-2\) remaining vertices, relabeled in increasing order; \(A\) is uniform among its first \(i-1\) vertices independently of that tree; and \(D'_A\) is the depth of vertex \(A\). In particular, the entire conditional distribution is independent of the child birth time \(j\).

## Assumptions and scope
A random recursive tree on \([n]\) is generated by choosing the parent of each vertex \(t\ge2\) uniformly and independently from \([t-1]\), with vertex \(1\) as root. Its fringe subtree at \(i\) contains \(i\) and all descendants of \(i\). The depth of the root is zero. The index restrictions \(n\ge3\) and \(2\le i\le n-1\) are necessary for a nonzero two-vertex-fringe event. No assumptions of planarity or unlabeled uniformity are made.

## Proof
Fix \(n\ge3\), \(2\le i\le n-1\), and \(i<j\le n\). On \(E_{i,j}\), vertex \(i\) chooses a parent \(a<i\), vertex \(j\) chooses parent \(i\), and every other vertex chooses a parent outside \(\{i,j\}\). Erase \(i\) and \(j\) and preserve the remaining birth order. Every retained parent choice is then independently uniform among earlier retained vertices: for times preceding \(j\), attachment to \(i\) is forbidden; for times following \(j\), attachment to either \(i\) or \(j\) is forbidden. Thus the retained tree is an ordinary independent-parent recursive tree on \(n-2\) vertices. The parent \(a\) of \(i\) is uniform among the first \(i-1\) retained vertices, independently of the retained tree. There are \((i-1)(n-3)!\) valid parent sequences among the \((n-1)!\) equiprobable recursive trees. This proves the exact probability of \(E_{i,j}\) and the asserted independence of its law from \(j\).

The deleted vertices have depths \(D'_a+1\) and \(D'_a+2\). All retained depths are unchanged, so the distributional identity follows by adding these depths to \(P'_{n-2}\). In a recursive tree on \(m\) vertices, \(\mathbb E D_t=H_{t-1}\) and \(\mathbb E P_m=m(H_m-1)\); consequently, averaging \(D'_a\) over \(a=1,\ldots,i-1\) gives \(H_{i-1}-1\). Therefore
\[
\mathbb E(P_n\mid E_{i,j})=(n-2)(H_{n-2}-1)+2H_{i-1}+1.
\]
For fixed \(i\), the events \(E_{i,j}\) over \(j=i+1,\ldots,n\) are disjoint and exhaust \(\{Z_{n,i}=1\}\). Their equal probabilities imply
\[
\Pr(Z_{n,i}=1)=\frac{(i-1)(n-i)}{(n-1)(n-2)},
\quad
\mathbb E(P_n\mid Z_{n,i}=1)=(n-2)(H_{n-2}-1)+2H_{i-1}+1.
\]
Since \(\mathbb E P_n=n(H_n-1)\), subtracting the unconditional mean and simplifying with \(H_n=H_{n-2}+(n-1)^{-1}+n^{-1}\) yields
\[
\mathbb E(P_n\mid Z_{n,i}=1)-\mathbb E P_n
=2(H_{i-1}-H_{n-2})+\frac{n-2}{n-1}.
\]
Multiplying by \(\Pr(Z_{n,i}=1)\) proves the covariance formula.

Write \(g_n(i)=2(H_{i-1}-H_{n-2})+(n-2)/(n-1)\). Then \(g_n(i+1)-g_n(i)=2/i>0\). At the last permitted index, \(g_n(n-1)=(n-2)/(n-1)>0\). At the first, when \(n\ge4\), \(H_{n-2}\ge3/2\) and hence \(g_n(2)\le-1+(n-2)/(n-1)<0\). The positive probability factor proves the finite sign transition. Finally, for \(i_n/n\to\alpha\in(0,1)\), harmonic differences converge to \(\log\alpha\), while the probability factor converges to \(\alpha(1-\alpha)\). The limit and unique limiting root \(e^{-1/2}\) follow.

## Relationship to prior work
Holmgren and Janson (2014, arXiv:1406.6883, especially Theorems 1.4 and 1.15) establish expectations and joint covariances for *aggregate*, unlabeled-by-birth fringe-subtree size counts. Feng, Mahmoud, and Panholzer (2008) likewise analyze aggregate size-count phase changes. Those results do not specify the covariance of a *fixed birth-index* fringe indicator with total path length; in particular, summing their aggregate covariances cannot recover the opposing individual signs proved here. Fuchs, Hwang, and Neininger (2006) treat tree depth profiles, not this conditional marked-fringe dependence. The present conditional deletion coupling identifies the additional birth-index information.

## Limitations
The sign transition is for linear covariance, not stochastic monotonicity or independence. An exact zero can occur at an integer index. The limit assumes a nonvanishing birth-time fraction; it does not describe regimes with \(i/n\to0\) or \(i/n\to1\). Historical priority cannot be guaranteed: a more specialized source about labeled fringe trees or mixed depth moments may contain an equivalent formula. The result is not a claim that aggregate fringe-count covariances are new.
