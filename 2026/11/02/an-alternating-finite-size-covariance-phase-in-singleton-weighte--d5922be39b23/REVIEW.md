# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is confined to four orders and to nonnegative singleton fugacity. The noncrossing block-profile coefficient formula is a published exact count; direct finite integer-profile sums give the stated partition-function and covariance numerator coefficients. For orders six and eight, strict negativity follows from explicit polynomials with nonnegative coefficients and positive constant term. At orders five and seven, the strictly increasing numerator polynomials each have a unique positive root, with signs proved on both sides. A separately structured enumeration program reproduces all coefficients using a direct crossing test and independently verifies the profile-count formula. It reports 127 integer and rational assertions passed. No sampling limit or computational extrapolation is used as proof.

## Originality — PASS (historical-priority caveat)
The closest uniform-model finite negative-covariance and asymptotic block-count results do not imply positive covariance under singleton fugacity \(q<1\), where the distribution differs. General simply generated noncrossing partition theorems cover the weighted model and asymptotic covariance, but no inspected theorem gives this complete finite four-order sign diagram. Classical exact profile counts do algebraically imply the finite formulas by explicit substitution; novelty is narrowly assigned to the sign thresholds and the alternating dependence phenomenon, not their enumerative inputs. Searches of published mathematical-result records and accessible previous findings produced no exact covering claim. A hidden tabulation or a generic exact bivariate Gibbs generating function may still cover the computation.

## Value — PASS
This is the complete finite covariance sign classification at the first four orders for which both block sizes can appear. It demonstrates, at one strictly positive fugacity, repeated \(+,-,+,-\) alternation despite the negative correlation known for unweighted noncrossing partitions. Exact algebraic thresholds quantify when singleton suppression reverses the dependence, providing a useful boundary case for general weighted NC block-count correlation conjectures. The work is expressly a finite boundary rather than a claim to a new general limit theorem.
