# An arithmetic slope switch for coherent capture on prime square tori

## Finding
Let \(p\ge5\) be an odd prime. Consider the ordinary nearest-neighbor toroidal grid \(G=C_p\square C_p\) on \(\mathbb F_p^2\), with a strictly positive uniform hopping strength \(J\). Install irreversible local absorbers at all \(p\) vertices of one affine line \(L\subset\mathbb F_p^2\), with arbitrary site-dependent positive absorption rates. An initially maximally mixed single excitation is absorbed eventually with probability \(K(L)/p^2\), where the exact accessible dimension \(K(L)\) is

\[
K(L)=\begin{cases}
 p(p+1)/2, & L\text{ has slope }0,\infty,1,\text{ or }-1,\\
 p^2-3(p-1)/4, & L\text{ has slope }r\in\mathbb F_p\text{ with }r^2=-1,\\
 p^2-3(p-1)/2, & L\text{ has any other slope}.
\end{cases}
\]

The middle case exists exactly when \(p\equiv1\pmod4\). All affine translates of each direction have the same capture probability. Among affine-line absorber placements, the middle case is optimal when available, and otherwise every slope outside \(\{0,\infty,1,-1\}\) is optimal. This is not a claim of optimality among arbitrary sets of \(p\) sites.

For example, on \(C_5\square C_5\), an axial or diagonal line captures \(15/25\) of the maximally mixed excitation, whereas a line of slope \(2\) or \(3\) captures \(22/25\). On \(C_{13}\square C_{13}\), the three dimensions are \(91\), \(151\), and \(160\), respectively (the special slopes are \(5\) and \(8\)). Thus an exact finite-field arithmetic property of the trap-line orientation changes the size of the protected quantum dark subspace.

## Assumptions and scope
The vertex Hilbert space is \(\mathcal H=\mathbb C^{\mathbb F_p^2}\). With \(A\) the real adjacency matrix of the nearest-neighbor Cartesian product \(C_p\square C_p\), the coherent Hamiltonian is \(H=JA\), with \(J>0\). Each trapped vertex \(s\in L\) carries an arbitrary constant loss rate \(\kappa_s>0\). The unnormalized one-excitation density matrix evolves under

\[
\rho(t)=e^{Bt}\rho(0)e^{B^\dagger t},\qquad
B=-iH-\tfrac12\sum_{s\in L}\kappa_s|s\rangle\langle s|.
\]

The loss probability up to time \(t\) is \(1-\operatorname{tr}\rho(t)\). The result concerns its \(t\to\infty\) limit, and initially \(\rho(0)=I/p^2\). No assertion is made about rates of decay, mean trapping time, dephasing, disorder, interacting excitations, or infinite lattices.

We call \(\mathcal K=\operatorname{span}\{A^j|s\rangle:s\in L,\ j\ge0\}\) the accessible subspace, and its orthogonal complement the dark subspace. An affine line includes vertical lines \(\{(a,t):t\in\mathbb F_p\}\) and nonvertical lines \(\{(t,rt+b):t\in\mathbb F_p\}\). Translation of \(L\) is a graph automorphism, so the intercept \(b\) is immaterial.

## Proof
**1. Absorption and the accessible subspace.** For any eigenvector \(Bv=\mu v\), taking the real part of its inner product with \(v\) gives

\[
\operatorname{Re}\mu\,\|v\|^2=-\tfrac12\sum_{s\in L}\kappa_s|\langle s|v\rangle|^2\le0.
\]

Equality implies that \(v\) vanishes at every absorber and is an eigenvector of the Hermitian \(H\). Conversely, each Hamiltonian eigenvector vanishing at all absorbers remains unaffected by loss. Such eigenvectors span exactly \(\mathcal K^\perp\), because a Hermitian spectral decomposition splits \(\mathcal H\) into the invariant cyclic space generated by the sink vectors and its orthogonal complement. Restricted to \(\mathcal K\), every eigenvalue of \(B\) has strictly negative real part; the corresponding finite-dimensional semigroup decays even if Jordan blocks occur. On \(\mathcal K^\perp\), evolution is unitary. Therefore the surviving trace for the maximally mixed initial state is \((p^2-\dim\mathcal K)/p^2\), and the eventual capture probability is \(\dim\mathcal K/p^2\). This also proves independence from the positive numerical values of the \(\kappa_s\).

**2. Prime-cycle spectral separation.** Let \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i/p}\). The Fourier eigenvectors are \(\psi_{k,l}(x,y)=p^{-1}\zeta^{kx+ly}\), with energy

\[
E_{k,l}=J\big(\zeta^k+\zeta^{-k}+\zeta^l+\zeta^{-l}\big),
\qquad (k,l)\in\mathbb F_p^2.
\]

We show that two energies are equal exactly when the unordered multisets \(\{\{\pm k\},\{\pm l\}\}\) coincide. Write \(m=(p-1)/2\), \(c_0=2\), and \(c_j=\zeta^j+\zeta^{-j}\) for \(1\le j\le m\). If two sums of two \(c_j\)'s agree, their difference has integer coefficients \(d_0,\ldots,d_m\) with \(\sum_jd_j=0\). The polynomial

\[
P(X)=2d_0+\sum_{j=1}^m d_j(X^j+X^{p-j})
\]

vanishes at \(\zeta\). Since the minimal polynomial of \(\zeta\) over \(\mathbb Q\) is \(\Phi_p(X)=1+X+\cdots+X^{p-1}\), all coefficients of \(P\) equal a common rational value \(c\): \(2d_0=c\) and \(d_j=c\) for \(j\ge1\). The additional identity \(\sum_jd_j=0\) gives \(pc/2=0\); hence all \(d_j=0\). Thus the eigenspaces are exactly the momentum orbits under independent sign changes and coordinate interchange, the eight-element signed-permutation group \(D_4\).

**3. Each eigenspace's observable rank.** By translation, a nonvertical trap line can be taken to be \(L_r=\{(t,rt):t\in\mathbb F_p\}\). The restriction of \(\psi_{k,l}\) to its \(p\) sink vertices is proportional to the character \(t\mapsto\zeta^{(k+rl)t}\). Characters with distinct frequencies are linearly independent. Consequently, for each momentum orbit \(O\), the rank of the projection of the sink vectors onto its eigenspace is exactly \(|\{k+rl:(k,l)\in O\}|\). Summing over disjoint eigenspaces gives

\[
K(L_r)=\sum_{O\in\mathbb F_p^2/D_4}|\ell_r(O)|,
\qquad\ell_r(k,l)=k+rl.
\]

Equivalently, for each \(u\in\mathbb F_p\), count the number of \(D_4\)-orbits represented on the momentum line \(M_u=\{(k,l):k+rl=u\}\), and sum these counts over \(u\). Each \(M_u\) has \(p\) points.

**4. Axis and diagonal directions.** For \(r=0\), fixing \(k=u\) and varying \(l\) produces one orbit for each unsigned frequency \(\{\pm l\}\), a total of \((p+1)/2\) distinct orbits per \(u\). For \(r=1\), the coordinate swap \((k,l)\mapsto(l,k)\) acts on \(M_u\) as an involution with exactly one fixed point, giving \((p+1)/2\) orbits. No additional distinct identifications occur: the single-coordinate sign flips and reflected swaps can preserve \(k+l\) only at fixed points; a quarter-turn can preserve it only for a pair already identified by swapping; the half-turn can preserve it only when \(u=0\), where it agrees with the swap. The slope \(-1\) case is obtained by reversing one coordinate, and vertical lines by interchanging coordinates. Hence \(K=p(p+1)/2\) in these four directions.

**5. All other slopes.** Fix \(r\notin\{0,1,-1\}\). Under the eight signed permutations, an identification of two *distinct* momentum points in the same \(M_u\) cannot be caused by a reflection: equality of the forms \(\ell_r(k,l)\) and \(\ell_r(g(k,l))\) for any of the four reflections forces \(g(k,l)=(k,l)\). A half-turn identifies \((k,l)\) and \((-k,-l)\) only when \(u=0\). The remaining transformations are the two quarter-turns \(R(k,l)=(-l,k)\) and \(R^{-1}(k,l)=(l,-k)\).

When \(r^2+1\ne0\), the equations

\[
 k+rl=u,\qquad -l+rk=u
\]

have exactly one solution \((k,l)\) for each \(u\), because their determinant is \(-1-r^2\). For \(u\ne0\), this solution and its quarter-turn are a pair of distinct points in \(M_u\). The inverse quarter-turn gives the same unordered pair. There are no other identifications, so each of the \(p-1\) nonzero fibers contains exactly \(p-1\) spectral labels. In the zero fiber, half-turns pair all nonzero points; no extra quarter-turn pairs lie in that fiber, so it contains exactly \((p+1)/2\) labels. Therefore

\[
 K=(p-1)^2+(p+1)/2=p^2-\frac{3(p-1)}2.
\]

When \(r^2=-1\), the quarter-turn instead satisfies \(\ell_r(R(k,l))=r\ell_r(k,l)\). Since \(r\ne1\), it cannot preserve any nonzero value of \(\ell_r\). Thus each of the \(p-1\) nonzero fibers contributes all \(p\) distinct labels. In the zero fiber, the quarter-turn has order four on every nonzero momentum vector; reflections do not add identifications. Hence the zero fiber contains one zero-vector label plus \((p-1)/4\) nonzero labels, and

\[
 K=p(p-1)+1+(p-1)/4=p^2-\frac{3(p-1)}4.
\]

Finally, \(-1\) has a square root modulo the odd prime \(p\) exactly when \(p\equiv1\pmod4\). Comparing the three explicit expressions proves the optimal line-direction statements. All arguments hold for every odd prime \(p\ge5\), with no continuum limit or finite-search extrapolation.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify_torus.py` implements two distinct checks without external packages: (i) it enumerates Fourier momentum orbits and projected-frequency counts for **all** slopes of each tested prime \(p=3,5,7,11,13,17,19,23,29\), and checks the closed formula; (ii) for selected directions at \(p=5,7,11,13\), it independently constructs the integer adjacency operator and the block-Krylov span from all trapped vertices, then performs exact modular elimination over two distinct large primes. The latter tests verify the ranks in physical site coordinates without using the Fourier orbit reduction. Run `python3 verify_torus.py`; the recorded output is `verification_output.txt`. A finite number of tests does not prove the theorem; the preceding cyclotomic and orbit argument does.

## Relationship to prior work
Krapivsky, Luck and Mallick (2013, arXiv:1311.6298) analyze quantum survival under traps on chains, rings and disordered higher-dimensional lattices, with explicit one-dimensional trap configurations. Varbanov, Krovi and Brun (2008, arXiv:0803.3446) discuss continuous-time quantum hitting times, dark invariant subspaces and graph symmetry, under a Poisson measurement model. Novo and coauthors (2014 preprint arXiv:1412.7209, updated 2015) give the general Krylov-subspace reduction and a local-loss formulation of capture. Le, Donati, Severini and Caruso (2017 preprint arXiv:1707.07482) study how dark subspaces can be identified or suppressed on quantum networks. Those conceptual methods and the general loss-to-dark-subspace identity are not claimed as new.

Torney (1988, report LA-UR-88-915) studies eigenmodes of the **classical** random walk on a two-dimensional torus with **one** trap and is directly relevant to toroidal spectral degeneracy. The exact all-prime quantum line-trap placement law above, including its square-root-of-minus-one exception and arbitrary positive sink-rate invariance, was not found in the material actually examined; priority is not guaranteed. In particular, Torney's complete report was not examined in full, and its potential implicit spectral implications remain a stated risk. Different geometries or known general dark-space methods do not alone entail the three explicit line-slope dimensions.

## Limitations
This is an exact eventual-capture theorem for homogeneous, finite, *odd-prime* square tori with absorbers occupying a full affine line. It neither covers composite side lengths nor describes arbitrary, noncollinear sets of \(p\) sinks. The design optimality is only within the affine-line class. A vanishingly small positive rate may make absorption arbitrarily slow even though the final capture probability is unchanged. The results do not establish time-to-capture bounds, decoherence tolerance, random disorder behavior, or independent historical priority.
