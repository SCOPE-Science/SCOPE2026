# Scientific review of the asymmetric rational six-direction family

## Correctness — PASS
Every finite multiple point is included by exhaustive pairwise incidence enumeration at the base width. For every one of the 57 direction subsets the exact weighted polygon area and its lattice normalization are computed with rational arithmetic. Each sixfold-dilated polygon is a lattice polygon; Ehrhart theory therefore gives a quadratic formula on every admissible parameter, and its values at zero and six determine it without extrapolation. Binomial inversion then proves the full multiplicity census, and the projective infinity contributions yield the displayed Chern formulas. The independent checker verifies both the full projective pair count and the formulas at two admissible widths. The endpoint and density inference match the hypotheses of [1, Lemma 4.2].

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The closest established rational Chern-geography construction is [1, Example 4.5], with endpoint \(19/8\); the accessible equal-width six-direction census gives \(43/18\). The current asymmetric-width point census is not implied by either, and its endpoint \(197/81\) strictly improves \(43/18\) by \(7/162\). Searches of published mathematical records for the exact ratio, aliases involving weighted six-direction affine arrangements, and the source's geography problem produced no equivalent statement. Characteristic-polynomial results for deformations of root arrangements may cover part of the arrangement structure, but a characteristic polynomial alone does not give the exhibited complete projective multiplicities or the cited interval without further work. The priority of an earlier unindexed full multiplicity calculation is not ruled out.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a nontrivial extension of a documented rational logarithmic-Chern geography question: a complete natural infinite-family census improves an actual rational accumulation bound. All details are exact and reproducible; the incidence structure also explains why the asymmetric widths yield a higher endpoint. Neither source formulas nor the already established equal-width case alone makes this particular improvement automatic.
