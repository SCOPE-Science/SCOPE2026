# An equisingular plane-branch family with a Frobenius jump invisible to the Milnor and Tjurina numbers

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of prime characteristic \(p\ge5\). For each \(\lambda\in k\), define the completed plane curve germ
\[
f_{p,\lambda}=x^{p+1}+y^{p+2}+\lambda x^{p-1}y^{p-1}\in k[[x,y]].
\]
Every such germ is an irreducible plane branch with the same numerical semigroup \(\langle p+1,p+2\rangle\), delta invariant \(\delta=p(p+1)/2\), and Milnor number \(\mu=p(p+1)\). Its Tjurina number and \(F\)-pure threshold are exactly
\[
\tau(f_{p,\lambda})=\begin{cases}
p(p+1),&\lambda=0\text{ or }p=5,\\
p(p+1)-2,&\lambda\ne0\text{ and }p\ge7,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\operatorname{fpt}(f_{p,\lambda})=
\begin{cases}1/p,&\lambda=0,\\1/(p-1),&\lambda\ne0.\end{cases}
\]
Thus, in characteristic five, the two parameter strata have *identical semigroup, delta, Milnor number and Tjurina number* (\(\mu=\tau=30\)), but different \(F\)-pure thresholds (\(1/5\) and \(1/4\)), so the germs from the two strata are not formally contact equivalent.

## Assumptions and scope
All invariants are at the origin of the formal local ring \(R=k[[x,y]]\). Write \(J(f)=(f_x,f_y)\), \(\mu=\operatorname{length}_k(R/J(f))\), \(\tau=\operatorname{length}_k(R/(f,J(f)))\). These lengths are finite in the stated family. For \(q=p^e\), write \(I_q=(x^q,y^q)\) and \(\nu_e(f)=\max\{N\ge0:f^N\notin I_q\}\); then \(\operatorname{fpt}(f)=\lim_{e\to\infty}\nu_e(f)/p^e\). The deformation parameter \(\lambda\) varies over the given field; no distinctness is claimed among nonzero-parameter germs. Assertions about formal equivalence concern multiplication by a unit and formal coordinate changes.

## Proof
**1. Equisingularity.** Assign weights \(w(x)=p+2\) and \(w(y)=p+1\). The two binomial terms have weight \((p+1)(p+2)\). The mixed term has strictly larger weight, because
\[
(p-1)(2p+3)-(p+1)(p+2)=p^2-2p-5>0\quad(p\ge5).
\]
The initial Newton edge has coprime intercepts \(p+1\) and \(p+2\), both prime to the characteristic. A characteristic-independent Newton–Puiseux/Weierstrass argument for this one-edge tame binomial gives one branch with initial parametrization of valuations \(v(x)=p+2\), \(v(y)=p+1\), and a single characteristic pair. In detail, taking an \((p+2)\)-nd root after setting \(y=t^{p+1}\) constructs \(x=t^{p+2}u(t)\) with \(u(0)\ne0\) through Hensel's lemma, because \(p+1\) and \(p+2\) are units in \(k\); the nontrivial unit powers are absorbed. The distinct valuation classes modulo \(p+1\) of the powers of \(x\) establish that the value semigroup is generated by \(p+1,p+2\). Hence \(\delta=p(p+1)/2\).

**2. Milnor and Tjurina numbers.** In characteristic \(p\), differentiation gives
\[
f_x=x^p-\lambda x^{p-2}y^{p-1},\qquad
f_y=2y^{p+1}-\lambda x^{p-1}y^{p-2}.
\]
Their lowest weighted terms are the coprime monomials \(x^p\) and \(2y^{p+1}\). The weighted initial ideal of the Jacobian ideal is therefore \((x^p,y^{p+1})\), because the two leading monomials are relatively prime (Buchberger's product criterion, or the regular-sequence deformation argument). The Jacobian quotient has a vector-space basis represented by
\[
\{x^iy^j:0\le i<p,\ 0\le j\le p\},
\]
so \(\mu=p(p+1)\). Put \(M=x^{p-1}y^{p-1}\). Multiplying the two displayed derivative relations by \(x\) and \(y\) respectively shows that in the Jacobian quotient \(Q=R/J(f)\),
\[
x^{p+1}=\lambda M,\qquad y^{p+2}=\frac\lambda2 M,
\quad\text{hence}\quad [f]=\frac{5\lambda}{2}[M].
\]
The largest weight among standard monomials is \(w(x^{p-1}y^p)=2p^2+2p-2\). The monomial \(M\) has weight \(2p^2+p-3\), and \(yM\) has exactly the largest standard-monomial weight. Both are nonzero independent elements of \(Q\); all their products by \(x\), and the product of \(yM\) by \(y\), have higher weight than any standard monomial and vanish. Consequently the principal ideal \(([M])\subset Q\) has length exactly two, with basis \([M],[yM]\). If \(p=5\), the scalar \(5/2\) vanishes and \([f]=0\), giving \(\tau=\mu\) for every parameter. For \(p\ge7\), it vanishes only at \(\lambda=0\); otherwise quotienting by \(([f])=([M])\) reduces the length by two.

**3. Frobenius thresholds for nonzero coefficients.** Suppose \(\lambda\ne0\). At level \(e=1\), in \(R/I_p\) the two binomial monomials vanish, leaving \(f\equiv\lambda x^{p-1}y^{p-1}\), whose square is zero. Put \(N_e=(p^e-1)/(p-1)\). Induction using \(N_{e+1}=pN_e+1\) and the Frobenius homomorphism yields the *exact congruence*
\[
f^{N_e}\equiv\lambda^{N_e}x^{p^e-1}y^{p^e-1}\pmod{I_{p^e}}.
\]
Indeed, at the next level \(f^{pN_e}\) is the Frobenius power of the previous top monomial; multiplying by \(f\) retains only the mixed term, because either binomial monomial pushes one exponent above \(p^{e+1}-1\). Moreover \(f^{N_e+1}\equiv0\pmod{I_{p^e}}\): every monomial of \(f\) increases some exponent of the top monomial. Thus \(\nu_e=N_e\) for every \(e\), and taking the limit yields \(1/(p-1)\).

**4. Frobenius thresholds at the central parameter.** If \(\lambda=0\), then \(f=x^{p+1}+y^{p+2}\in I_p\), hence \(f^{p^{e-1}}\in I_{p^e}\), giving \(\nu_e\le p^{e-1}-1\). Let \(N=p^{e-1}-1\) and \(j=N/2\). Since \(p\) is odd, every base-\(p\) digit of \(N\) equals \(p-1\), and every digit of \(j\) equals \((p-1)/2\). By Lucas's theorem \(\binom Nj\) is nonzero modulo \(p\). The term \(x^{(p+1)j}y^{(p+2)j}\) in \(f^N\) has both exponents strictly below \(p^e\); it cannot cancel because binomial terms have distinct exponent pairs. Hence \(\nu_e=N\) and \(\operatorname{fpt}=1/p\).

**5. Characteristic-five separation.** The \(F\)-pure threshold is invariant under completed local coordinate automorphisms and multiplication of the defining equation by a unit. The values \(1/5\) and \(1/4\) are distinct. The preceding computations show that neither the fixed Puiseux semigroup nor the equal values of \(\mu\) and \(\tau\) distinguish these two characteristic-five formal contact classes, while \(\operatorname{fpt}\) does.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify.py` uses exact modular polynomial arithmetic and Gröbner bases in finite local truncations to check the displayed Milnor/Tjurina lengths for characteristics \(5,7,11,13\) with parameters \(0,1,2\). It also computes all Frobenius powers needed for the threshold counts at \(e=1,2\) and verifies the stronger top-monomial congruence when \(\lambda\ne0\). Executing it with SymPy produces `VERIFY_OK`. These are illustrative finite calculations; the unbounded assertions rely on the induction and local-algebra arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work
Ma and Zuo (2026) establish general bounds relating Milnor and Tjurina numbers in arbitrary characteristic, but not the parameter-by-parameter classification of these plane branches. Svoray (2026), particularly the higher-weight plane-branch example in Section 3, already demonstrates that higher weighted-order terms can change an \(F\)-pure threshold. That qualitative mechanism, and the use of Frobenius powers, are **prior art**, not claimed as novel. The statement here isolates an explicit prime-uniform equisingular family, determines two independent stratifications exactly, and exhibits the characteristic-five case in which \(\mu=\tau\) fails to register the changed \(F\)-pure threshold. Hernández's diagonal and binomial formulas provide important special-case context but do not explicitly supply this whole trinomial/Tjurina-family statement.

## Limitations
This result is restricted to algebraically closed fields of characteristic \(p\ge5\), completed *plane* branches, and this specific one-parameter family. It does not assert any classification of all branches of semigroup \(\langle p+1,p+2\rangle\) or classify nonzero parameters up to analytic equivalence. Although the calculation and its prime-uniform formulation appear absent from the inspected works, historical priority of the exact example remains provisional; specialized treatments of trinomial \(F\)-thresholds might imply the threshold half of the claim. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
