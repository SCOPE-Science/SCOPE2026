# An equivariant graph spine for the Petersen perfect-matching complex

## Finding

Let \(P=\mathrm{KG}(5,2)\) be the Petersen graph and let \(X=\mathcal M_p(P)\) be the simplicial complex generated by its perfect matchings: a vertex of \(X\) is an edge of \(P\), and a face is a subset of some perfect matching. The natural group \(G=S_5=\mathrm{Aut}(P)\) acts on \(X\).

**Theorem.** There is an explicit \(G\)-equivariant strong deformation retraction of \(X\) onto the barycentric subdivision of the complete graph \(K_6\), where the six nodes of \(K_6\) are the six perfect matchings of \(P\). Thus \(X\simeq_G K_6\), and
\[
H_i(X;\mathbb Z)\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb Z,&i=0,\\ \bigwedge^2 U_{\mathbb Z}\cong\mathbb Z^{10},&i=1,\\0,&i\ge2,\end{cases}
\]
where \(U_{\mathbb Z}=\ker(\mathbb Z[\mathscr F]\xrightarrow{\epsilon}\mathbb Z)\) and \(\mathscr F\) is the natural six-element \(G\)-set of perfect matchings. The degree-one isomorphism is an isomorphism of **integral** \(G\)-modules.

Let \(V_{\mathbb Q}=\ker(\mathbb Q^{5}\xrightarrow{\sum}\mathbb Q)\), the standard four-dimensional \(S_5\)-module. Over the rationals, the complete nonconstant homology representation is
\[
H_1(X;\mathbb Q)\cong\bigwedge^2 V_{\mathbb Q}\oplus(V_{\mathbb Q}\otimes\mathrm{sgn})\cong S^{(3,1,1)}\oplus S^{(2,1,1,1)}.
\]
For any subgroup \(H\le S_5\), the quotient \(X/H\) deformation-retracts onto the finite quotient graph \(K_6/H\) (with barycentric subdivision to account for edge inversions). In particular \(X/S_5\) and \(X/A_5\) are contractible; for a subgroup \(C_5\) induced by a five-cycle, \(X/C_5\simeq S^1\vee S^1\).

## Assumptions and scope

The graph \(P\) has vertices the two-element subsets of \(\{1,2,3,4,5\}\), with adjacency of disjoint subsets. A perfect matching is a set of five pairwise disjoint graph edges covering all ten graph vertices. The simplicial complex consists of **subsets of perfect matchings**, not of all matchings (the latter is a different simplicial complex). The group acts by relabeling the five ground-set elements. A quotient of a simplicial complex is understood as a topological orbit space; when passing to graph quotients, the barycentric subdivision prevents inversion of unoriented edges.

## Proof

**1. Intersections and incidence graph.** Every Petersen graph edge lies in exactly two perfect matchings, there are exactly six perfect matchings, and two distinct perfect matchings share exactly one Petersen graph edge [1, Proposition 2.1]. The standalone checker independently enumerates them and confirms that triple intersections are empty. Hence the six maximal simplices \(F_M\cong\Delta^4\), one per perfect matching \(M\in\mathscr F\), have pairwise intersections each consisting of a **single vertex**, and no triple intersections. The fifteen pairwise intersection vertices correspond bijectively to the fifteen Petersen graph edges. The face numbers in dimensions zero to four are \((15,60,60,30,6)\): the dimension-zero vertices are shared twice, while every nonvertex face belongs to exactly one facet.

**2. A canonical strong deformation retraction.** For each facet \(F_M\), let \(c_M\) be its barycenter and let \(T_M\) be the union of the five straight segments joining \(c_M\) to the facet's vertices \(p_e\), for \(e\in M\). Define a retraction of the standard four-simplex onto this star as follows. In nonnegative barycentric coordinates \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_5)\) with \(\sum_i x_i=1\), write \(b(x)\) for the second-largest coordinate, counting multiplicity, and put \(w_i(x)=\max\{x_i-b(x),0\}\). At most one \(w_i(x)\) is positive. The formula
\[
r_M(x)=c_M+\sum_{i=1}^{5}w_i(x)(p_i-c_M),\qquad H_{M,t}(x)=(1-t)x+t\,r_M(x),\quad 0\le t\le1
\]
defines a continuous retraction and homotopy: \(r_M(x)\in T_M\), and whenever \(x\in T_M\), its unique noncentral arm parameter is exactly \(w_i(x)\), so \(r_M(x)=x\). In particular all facet vertices are fixed. Because different facets intersect only at such vertices, the maps glue to a continuous strong deformation retraction \(X\to Y=\bigcup_{M}T_M\). The construction depends only on unordered barycentric coordinates, so it commutes with all permutations of ground-set labels, making it \(S_5\)-equivariant. The graph \(Y\) has six central nodes \(c_M\), fifteen intersection nodes \(p_e\), and thirty incidence edges. Each intersection vertex lies on exactly two such arms, so \(Y\) is canonically the edge-barycentric subdivision of \(K_6\), with \(S_5\) acting on its six perfect matchings. This proves the first assertion, including the **strong** and **equivariant** properties.

**3. Integral homology and the natural module.** Orient every edge of \(K_6\) by \([M,N]=-[N,M]\). Let \(L=\mathbb Z[\mathscr F]\), with augmentation \(\epsilon:L\to\mathbb Z\). The cellular chain group in degree one is \(\bigwedge^2 L\), and the boundary is \(\partial(M\wedge N)=N-M\). Equivalently for arbitrary \(a,b\in L\), \(\partial(a\wedge b)=\epsilon(a)b-\epsilon(b)a\). Fixing any one matching \(M_0\) gives an abelian-group splitting \(L=\mathbb Z M_0\oplus U_{\mathbb Z}\), where \(U_{\mathbb Z}=\ker\epsilon\). The restriction of \(\partial\) to \(M_0\wedge U_{\mathbb Z}\) is an isomorphism onto \(U_{\mathbb Z}\), while \(\partial\) vanishes on \(\bigwedge^2U_{\mathbb Z}\). Therefore \(\ker\partial=\bigwedge^2U_{\mathbb Z}\), canonically and \(S_5\)-equivariantly (the splitting only proves exactness, not equivariance). As \(\mathrm{rank}(U_{\mathbb Z})=5\), \(\mathrm{rank}(H_1)=\binom52=10\); there are no chain groups above degree one. This proves the complete integral claim.

**4. Rational character.** For any ground-set permutation \(g\in S_5\), let \(F(g)\) count its fixed perfect matchings and let \(\chi_U(g)=F(g)-1\). The elementary exterior-square character formula gives
\[
\chi_{H_1}(g)=\frac{(F(g)-1)^2-(F(g^2)-1)}2.
\]
In the seven cycle types \((1^5),(2,1^3),(2^2,1),(3,1^2),(3,2),(4,1),(5)\), the trace sequence is respectively \(10,-2,-2,1,1,0,0\). The six-matching action has fixed-point counts respectively \(6,0,2,0,0,2,1\). For the standard \(S_5\)-module \(V_{\mathbb Q}\), with \(\chi_V(g)=\#\operatorname{Fix}_{\{1,\ldots,5\}}(g)-1\), direct application of the same formula plus multiplication by the sign character shows, on every conjugacy class,
\[
\chi_{H_1}(g)=\chi_{\bigwedge^2 V_{\mathbb Q}}(g)+\mathrm{sgn}(g)\chi_{V_{\mathbb Q}}(g).
\]
The two pieces are the irreducible Specht modules of shapes \((3,1,1)\) (dimension six) and \((2,1,1,1)\) (dimension four). Since characteristic-zero representations of a finite group are determined by characters, this proves the stated decomposition.

**5. Symmetry quotients.** Equivariant deformation retractions descend to orbit spaces for every subgroup. For \(S_5\) and \(A_5\), the action on the six perfect matchings is transitive, and on unordered pairs of distinct matchings is transitive; the barycentrically subdivided graph quotient has two nodes joined by one edge, hence is contractible. For the subgroup generated by a five-cycle, the action on the six matchings has one fixed point and one orbit of size five, giving five vertex orbits for the subdivided graph and six edge orbits; its connected quotient graph has first Betti number \(6-5+1=2\), so is a wedge of two circles. No assertion of a free group action on the original space is needed.

## Verification

The standalone file `verify_petersen_perfect_matching.py` uses only the Python standard library and exactly enumerates the ten vertices, fifteen graph edges, six perfect matchings, all pair/triple facet intersections, all simplicial faces, all \(120\) group elements, classwise traces and the three subgroup quotient graphs. Run `python3 verify_petersen_perfect_matching.py` and require `PETERSEN_PERFECT_MATCHING_EQUIVARIANT_CERTIFICATE_OK`. A successful finite checker is not a substitute for the general, explicit deformation-retraction argument; it validates the finite incidences and character arithmetic used in that proof.

## Relationship to prior work

The Petersen perfect-matching intersection facts are established in [1]; the six-matchings count and pairwise-single-edge intersection are foundational inputs, **not new claims**. Perfect-matching simplicial complexes and nerve methods are developed in [2], and the later family study [3] concerns polygonal-line graphs. The elementary nonequivariant homotopy type \(\bigvee^{10}S^1\) is already implied by these intersection facts together with standard nerve reasoning; **novelty is not asserted for that bare conclusion**. The contribution here is the canonical explicit **equivariant strong deformation retract**, its **integral \(S_5\)-module**, the full **rational Specht decomposition**, and the **homotopy types of symmetry quotients**. Existing work on the ordinary Petersen matching complex (all matchings), higher matching complexes and cut complexes concerns different spaces and does not, by itself, imply this theorem.

## Limitations

The statement concerns the Petersen graph and its indicated graph automorphism group; it does not establish analogous equivariant retractions for arbitrary bridgeless cubic graphs. Novelty is established only to the extent of the literature and indexed record inspections summarized in the accompanying review; independently indexed or implicit equivalent arguments remain possible. This is a mathematical proof with exact reproducibility checks and **no independent external audit**. The rational splitting does not assert a direct integral splitting into the named rational irreducibles; the integral result is the exterior-square lattice module.
