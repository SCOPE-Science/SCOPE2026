# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The grid transition support is exactly the integer floor/ceiling of \(4j(N-j)/N\), with both outgoing edges assigned positive probability. All 65,535 denominators in the natural 16-bit range were exhaustively checked by a complete exact 64-bit reverse-edge enumeration, not finite trajectory sampling. The observed zero-unreachable set is exactly empty unless four divides the denominator, in which case it is exactly the two indices representing \(1/4\) and \(3/4\). A direct finite-Markov-chain closed-class proof derives almost-sure absorption and the entire stationary simplex. The sharp Wasserstein value comes from a separate exact one-dimensional optimal-transport integral. Code-dependent claims remain strictly finite, with the source and execution output included.

**Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, with explicit risk.** The defining preprint and its complete 2023 open-access article study stochastic rounding of logistic and generalized Bernoulli maps and report numerical improvements; they do not give the specific uniform-rational-grid all-denominator stationary-class theorem. The earlier recorded binary16 logistic map finding uses deterministic ties-to-even and staged rounding, and cannot imply this random-chain classification. Search comparisons also considered random coprime expanding grids and deterministic quantized affine maps; neither dominates the proposition. The 2011 IEICE integer logistic-map paper was accessible only as an abstract (institutional retrieval unavailable); it is a meaningful unresolved priority risk. No absence from all previous literature is claimed.

**Value — PASS.** A complete stationary-state classification for a natural discretization of the canonical chaotic logistic map establishes an exact arithmetic phase transition between adjacent high-resolution grids, and the optimal-transport computation quantifies a persistent, resolution-independent long-time bias despite unbiased per-step rounding. The result distinguishes stationary limits from finite-trajectory behavior and directly motivates checking whether unbiased stochastic rounding preserves invariant measures, while being carefully confined to its grid semantics.

**Limits.** The conclusion stops at the 16-bit denominator cutoff and does not cover IEEE floating point, intermediate arithmetic rounding, or all possible grid sizes. Even with stochastic rounding, transient trajectories can appear chaotic over experimentally long finite times. The predecessor article could not be read fully.
