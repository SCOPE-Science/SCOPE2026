# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The discriminant substitution reduces all quadratic tuples to a single four-character counting problem, with exactly \(q\) monic quadratics per admissible discriminant. The six pair sums contribute \(-6\), odd cubic sums cancel in opposite pairs, and the projective involution with nonsquare multiplier makes the quartic sum exactly \(-1\). The boundary correction is necessarily either zero or eight and integrality forces its value in each congruence class modulo sixteen. The homogeneous identity and the nonsquare determinant have been verified algebraically, with 26 finite-field configurations independently enumerated by `verify.py`. No finite experiment is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to residual priority risk
The 2012 Bary-Soroker theorem is a uniform asymptotic with error \(O(q^{3/2})\) in the quadratic/four-shift case, not an exact count. The 2014 Bank–Bary-Soroker short-interval theorem likewise concerns general asymptotics. Related exact arithmetic-progression tuples already cover the characteristic-seven subfamily, where the harmonic quartet is affinely equivalent to a four-term progression. In every other admissible characteristic, the harmonic quartet cannot be affinely transformed into an arithmetic progression, so that earlier result does not imply the present infinite family. Searches for harmonic, elliptic, quadratic-character and exact four-translate formulations found no matching earlier statement. The relevant two primary papers were read beyond their abstracts; this does not exclude an undiscovered classical character-sum specialization. That residual risk remains explicit.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Exact four-tuple prime-polynomial counts are naturally motivated by the function-field Hardy–Littlewood problem. The harmonic cross-ratio is a canonical four-point configuration: its nonsquare projective symmetry removes the usual elliptic fluctuation and produces a clean exact floor law for infinitely many prime powers (including those outside characteristic seven, where the arithmetic-progression formula does not apply), together with a sharp existence boundary. This is not a numeric isolated case or a restatement of the general asymptotic.
