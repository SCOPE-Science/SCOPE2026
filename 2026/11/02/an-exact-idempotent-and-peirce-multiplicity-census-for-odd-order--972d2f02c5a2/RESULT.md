# An exact idempotent and Peirce-multiplicity census for odd-order core quandle algebras

## Finding
Let \(G\) be any finite abelian group of odd order \(n\ge1\), and put \(A=\mathbb C[\operatorname{Core}(G)]\), with \(e_xe_y=e_{2y-x}\). Let \(\widehat G\) be its character group. Partition \(\widehat G\setminus\{1\}\) into orbits \(\mathcal O\) under the two commuting permutations \(\chi\mapsto\chi^2\) and \(\chi\mapsto\chi^{-1}\). For an orbit choose \(\chi\) of order \(h>1\), put \(m=\operatorname{ord}_h(2)\), and define
\[
J_{\mathcal O}=\begin{cases}2^{m/2}+1,&-1\in\langle 2\rangle\subseteq(\mathbb Z/h\mathbb Z)^\times,\\2^m-1,&-1\notin\langle2\rangle.\end{cases}
\]
In the first case \(m\) is even and \(|\mathcal O|=m\); in the second \(|\mathcal O|=2m\). The bivariate idempotent census is the explicit product
\[
\boxed{\mathcal F_G(X,Y)=(1+Y)\prod_{\mathcal O}\left(1+J_{\mathcal O}X^{|\mathcal O|/2}\right).}
\]
Precisely, for integers \(j\ge0\) and \(a\in\{0,1\}\), the coefficient \([X^jY^a]\mathcal F_G\) counts idempotents \(u\in A\), including zero, for which right multiplication \(\rho_u(v)=vu\) has respective eigenvalue multiplicities
\[
\dim E_{+1}=j+a,\qquad \dim E_{-1}=j,\qquad \dim E_0=n-2j-a.
\]
Every such operator is diagonalizable. In particular, the total number of complex idempotents is finite and is exactly
\[
\boxed{|\{u\in A:u^2=u\}|=2\prod_{\mathcal O}(1+J_{\mathcal O}).}
\]
The same-order groups \(G=\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z\) and \(H=(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z)^2\) have respectively \(80\) and \(512\) complex idempotents. Their complete census polynomials are \((1+Y)(1+3X)(1+9X^3)\) and \((1+Y)(1+3X)^4\). Thus the full multiplicity census distinguishes these two nine-dimensional core-quandle algebras, although their *sets* of possible Peirce eigenvalues are both \(\{0,1,-1\}\).

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is \(\mathbb C\), and \(G\) is finite abelian with odd order; the identity group is allowed. The algebra is generally nonassociative and is defined on the group-element basis by \(e_xe_y=e_{2y-x}\), extended bilinearly. A zero idempotent is included for counting; remove its contribution \(1\) to count nonzero idempotents. Right multiplication, not left multiplication, defines the Peirce data. \(\chi^2\) means the character \(g\mapsto\chi(2g)\); the trivial character is \(1\). The integer \(h\) is the *order of a character*, not necessarily the exponent or order of \(G\). Every orbit \(\mathcal O\) has even cardinality. Coefficients in the product are ordinary nonnegative integers; no assertion is made for coefficient fields of positive characteristic.

## Proof
Fix the Fourier convention \(c_\chi=\sum_{g\in G}a_g\chi(g)^{-1}\) for \(u=\sum_ga_ge_g\). Character orthogonality makes \(u\leftrightarrow(c_\chi)\) a complex-linear bijection, with inverse \(a_g=n^{-1}\sum_\chi c_\chi\chi(g)\). Directly from \(x*y=2y-x\), the transform of a product satisfies \(\widehat{uv}(\chi)=\widehat u(\chi^{-1})\widehat v(\chi^2)\). Hence idempotence is equivalent to
\[
c_\chi=c_{\chi^{-1}}c_{\chi^2}\qquad\text{for every }\chi\in\widehat G.\tag{1}
\]
Let \(S=\{\chi:c_\chi\ne0\}\). Equation (1) forces \(S\) to be closed under inversion and doubling; finiteness implies it is a union of full \(\langle-1,2\rangle\)-orbits. Multiplying (1) for \(\chi\) and \(\chi^{-1}\), and writing \(P_\chi=c_\chi c_{\chi^{-1}}\), gives \(P_\chi=P_\chi P_{\chi^2}\). On \(S\), \(P_\chi\ne0\), so \(P_{\chi^2}=1\). Since doubling permutes \(S\), this means \(P_\chi=1\) there. Consequently
\[
c_{\chi^{-1}}=c_\chi^{-1},\qquad c_{\chi^2}=c_\chi^2 \quad(\chi\in S).\tag{2}
\]
The trivial character has \(c_1=c_1^2\), thus precisely two choices \(0,1\), independent of every nontrivial orbit.

Fix a nontrivial orbit with a character \(\chi\) of order \(h\), let \(m=\operatorname{ord}_h(2)\), and set \(z=c_\chi\). One option is that the entire orbit is zero. Otherwise (2) forces \(c_{\chi^{2^r}}=z^{2^r}\) and \(c_{\chi^{-2^r}}=z^{-2^r}\). If \(-1\) is not a power of \(2\) modulo \(h\), the two length-\(m\) doubling cycles are disjoint. The unique consistency restriction is \(z^{2^m-1}=1\), giving exactly \(2^m-1\) nonzero choices. If \(-1\) is a power of \(2\), its location in the cyclic group generated by \(2\) is necessarily \(2^{m/2}\); the doubling cycle already contains the inverses. Thus the consistency restriction is \(z^{2^{m/2}+1}=1\), which also implies \(z^{2^m-1}=1\), giving exactly \(2^{m/2}+1\) choices. Every listed assignment really satisfies (1), including at zero coordinates because the support is a union of full orbits. Orbit choices are independent. Counting an occupied orbit by \(X^{|\mathcal O|/2}\) and the optional occupied trivial character by \(Y\) proves the product.

For the Peirce statement, the Fourier transform of \(\rho_u(v)\) is \(\widehat v(\chi^{-1})c_{\chi^2}\). Thus each nontrivial inverse pair \(\{\chi,\chi^{-1}\}\) yields a \(2\times2\) off-diagonal block. When its orbit is occupied the product of off-diagonal entries equals \(c_{\chi^2}c_{\chi^{-2}}=1\), so its eigenvalues are \(+1,-1\); when unoccupied the block is zero. The trivial-character block has eigenvalue \(a=c_1\in\{0,1\}\). An orbit of size \(|\mathcal O|\) contributes \(|\mathcal O|/2\) inverse pairs. Summing all blocks yields exactly \((j+a,j,n-2j-a)\). Every block is diagonalizable over \(\mathbb C\), proving the full multiplicity statement and completing the proof.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone script `verify_core_idempotents.py` computes exact integer doubling/inversion orbit weights, enumerates all corresponding complex Fourier solutions for the groups \(\mathbb Z/3\), \(\mathbb Z/5\), \(\mathbb Z/7\), \(\mathbb Z/9\), \(\mathbb Z/11\), \(\mathbb Z/15\), and \((\mathbb Z/3)^2\), reconstructs coefficient vectors using a discrete Fourier inverse, independently multiplies vectors using \(e_xe_y=e_{2y-x}\), and checks \(\rho_u^3=\rho_u\), \(\operatorname{tr}\rho_u\), and \(\operatorname{tr}\rho_u^2\). It returns `VERIFY_OK`. Representative total counts in that order are \(8,12,16,80,68,768,512\). Numerical reconstruction is illustrative, not the infinite-family proof; the latter is the algebraic argument above.

## Relationship to prior work
Elhamdadi–Nunez–Singh (2022) initiated the explicit Peirce-spectrum question for complex dihedral quandle algebras and calculated low-order examples. Elhamdadi–Ta–Virgin (2026), Proposition 3.4 and Section 4, introduced the Fourier idempotence framework for finite latin Alexander quandles, focusing on integral idempotents. Zhang (2026), Proposition 5.1 and Theorem 5.5, explicitly established equation (1) and the \(\{0,1,-1\}\) right Peirce eigenvalue *set* for every odd-order finite abelian core quandle. These equations and eigenvalue-set statements are prior results and are not claimed as new. Kaur–Singh (2023) gave a decomposition of Takasaki quandle representations into right-simple modules, which is also distinct prior structure. The narrower contribution here is the **closed arithmetic orbit weight \(J_{\mathcal O}\)** for every nontrivial dual orbit, the independent-orbit product determining **all complex idempotents**, and the resulting **complete multiplicity distribution**. The formula refines, rather than reproves, the previously established Peirce eigenvalue set. It requires solving the orbit consistency equations and accounting for all root-of-unity choices, not merely noting the three possible eigenvalues.

## Limitations
These statements apply only to finite odd-order abelian cores over \(\mathbb C\), not to arbitrary Alexander quandles, nonabelian groups, or modular coefficient fields. The formula concerns *right* multiplication. The direct finite checks do not independently certify the all-group proof. The main originality uncertainty is that the arithmetic orbit count may already have been stated under a different counting terminology; the literature supplied the underlying Fourier equations, so the result should not be described as a new general Fourier principle. No external independent audit is asserted.
