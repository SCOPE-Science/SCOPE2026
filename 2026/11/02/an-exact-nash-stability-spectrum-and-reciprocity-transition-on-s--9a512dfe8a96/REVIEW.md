# Review of the signed-star stability theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The equilibrium characterization follows by forcing each leaf's membership relative to the hub and checking every strict unilateral hub deviation, including singleton formation. The existence condition follows from the sign of the hub payoff and the sum of outsider-block payoffs. The exponential generating function follows from block decomposition; the sharp stochastic transition follows from independent bounded sums and a triangular-array central limit theorem; the parity formula follows from sign-reversal symmetry and Vandermonde's identity. Direct finite verification of 1,365 profiles against 222,233 game-partition cases and 30 additional exact-rational probability checks passed. These checks corroborate rather than substitute for the proof; no infinite computation is claimed.

## Originality — PASS, subject to priority uncertainty

The closest structural treatment, Hanaka–Lampis, establishes strong NP-hardness of arbitrary-weight star instances, which has no implication giving exact Nash partitions in the binary signed subclass. Brandt–Bullinger–Tappe's restricted-weight results concern broader topologies and related stability concepts; no exact signed-star spectrum was identified in its inspected results. The 2016 graph-restricted communication model additionally requires connected feasible coalitions, unlike the unrestricted signed-star partitions. Bullinger (2022) concentrates on consent-variant complexity, not this full equilibrium count. The random complete-interaction model in Bullinger–Kraiczy has different asymptotic behavior and assumptions, and does not imply this sparse-star reciprocity threshold. The closest accessible prior outcomes concerned fractional hedonic friendship networks and additive complete-bipartite greedy welfare, neither of which subsumes the theorem. Publicly available mathematical searches and inspected primary sources identified no equivalent theorem; an incomplete specialist-literature census remains a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This result resolves a natural tractable restriction of a known hardness boundary, characterizes every equilibrium rather than exhibiting isolated instances, and exposes a genuine reciprocity-driven random phase transition with an exact finite parity correction. The critical window and exact stability count provide quantitative information not supplied by mere existence or hardness statements. Standard probabilistic and enumerative ingredients are acknowledged and are not themselves claimed as novel.
