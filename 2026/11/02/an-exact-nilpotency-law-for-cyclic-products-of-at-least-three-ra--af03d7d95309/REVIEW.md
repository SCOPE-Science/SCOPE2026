# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact transfer identity for a fixed linear map of rank \(r\) gives conditional nilpotency probability \(q^{-r}\). Its proof uses the known Fine–Herstein nilpotent count and a uniform pushforward on the image. The prefix kernel expectation then counts survival of every nonzero vector through successive independent random maps. These two steps recover the formula with no asymptotic approximations. The standalone verifier exhausts several small square and rectangular cycles, including all triples of binary \(2\times2\) matrices and a binary rectangular \((1,2,3)\) cycle.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The one-factor matrix count is the theorem of Fine–Herstein (and Hall); Wästlund's 2020 Proposition 14 is **exactly** the two-factor rectangular formula. Neither is claimed as original. The 2026 Sarma–Subedi paper supplies the finite-ring nilpotent-product-probability setting. The claim assessed here is the uniform arbitrary-length cyclic formula for \(t\ge3\), not merely another presentation of \(t=2\). Relevant searchable records and the nearest primary texts did not show that statement. A multipartite extension of Wästlund's martingale could make the formula implicit elsewhere; this remains a real historical-priority risk and is not represented as resolved by a failed search.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The length and dimensions of random finite-field matrix products are natural structural parameters of nilpotent-product probability. The theorem replaces a complicated convolution and rank-distribution problem by a dimension-symmetric closed form for every number of factors, including rectangular cycles. It exhibits an exact law unavailable from the known two-factor statement alone without an additional argument, while giving a transparent independent proof. No priority or value is claimed for the previously established one- and two-factor cases.

## Limitations and sources
Only independent uniform finite-field maps are treated, and the theorem does not give Jordan types or nilpotency-index distributions. Closest work: Sarma–Subedi, arXiv:2610.07892v1; Wästlund, arXiv:2008.13017v1, Proposition 14; Leinster, arXiv:1912.12562. Potential earlier implicit multipartite treatment remains a limitation.
