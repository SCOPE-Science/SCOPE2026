## correctness

PASS

For 0<e<1, 0<η<1−e, uniform independent periapsis angle ω and u=|cos i| uniform, the two eclipse conditions are nested intervals with thresholds W(1±e sinω), W=η/(1−e²), both strictly <1. Their joint event is the *minimum* W(1−e|sinω|), not a product. Averaging arbitrary joint (e,η) mixtures gives M0−M1|sinω|, M0=E[W], M1=E[eW], so posterior (1−ε|sinω|)/(2π−4ε) and P(occultation|transit)=1−2ε/π, ε=M1/M0. Symmetry kills all sine and odd cosine coefficients; ∫_0^{2π}|sinω|cos(2kω)dω=−4/(4k²−1) gives the exact even spectrum and invertible m1→ε. With η=1/10, one population e≡1/2 and another e∈{1/4,3/4} probabilities {15/22,7/22} have ε=1/2 exactly but different M0, giving a bona fide nonidentifiability pair for *conditioned angle data*, not all possible observables. Independently verified both rational moments and four even Fourier quadratures. Boundary e=0 excluded because periapsis undefined; unsaturated geometry essential.

## originality

PASS

Qualified PASS, narrowed: 2009 Kane–von Braun already derive individual primary and secondary geometric eclipse probabilities; 2014 Kipping derives transit-only eccentricity/ω selection. Stronger direct precedent Shabram et al. 2015/2016 explicitly recognize that a *double-eclipse* sample biases the periapsis-angle distribution and discuss a simulated <4% bias. Thus the phenomenon and qualitative direction are prior, not new. Their complete paper does not present the arbitrary-mixture one-weighted-moment closed posterior, full harmonic signature, exact inverse nor rational nonidentifiability witnesses. These restricted evaluated statements remain a best-knowledge distinct result, though the derivation is short and general selection-algebra priority is a serious risk.
