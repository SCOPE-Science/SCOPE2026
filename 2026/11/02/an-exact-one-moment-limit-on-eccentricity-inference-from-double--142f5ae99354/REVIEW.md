# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** With isotropic \(u=|\cos i|\), the joint event is the intersection of *nested intervals* and has probability \(W(1-e|\sin\omega|)\), not a product of marginal eclipse probabilities. The unsaturated bound validates the interval lengths; linear expectation reduces the unknown population law to \(M_0,M_1\); direct Fourier integration gives the complete angular spectrum and invertibility. Exact rational calculation supplies two distinct measures with one identical weighted eccentricity. The independent executable reproduces five discrete-mixture cases and the Fourier integrals. All claims are about the stated ideal geometry, not exact full-orbit photometry.

**Originality — PASS, provisional.** Primary source arXiv:0908.0519v1 provides the separate primary/secondary geometric formulas and an inclination-conditioned comparison; arXiv:1408.1393v1 provides transit-only periapsis selection/Bayesian priors. Those known ingredients were not rebranded as discoveries. Compared to these sources, the claim is the *entire distribution* of the doubly selected periapsis angle for arbitrary heterogeneous eccentricities and geometric scales, one-moment closure, all Fourier coefficients, and a concrete nonidentifiability pair. Relevant research-record and primary-literature queries found no identical theorem. The identities follow from simple integration of known geometry, so an unlocated general selection-model theorem could imply them; priority is qualified and requires independent checking.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** An exact selection law and sharp identifiability limit answer what information survives if only both-eclipse indicators and periapsis angles are available. The theorem covers arbitrary mixtures (not a hand-selected finite sample) and establishes both the recoverable weighted moment and the irrecoverability of the shape of the underlying law. It is a potentially useful calibration/null model for population studies, with explicit well-defined failure modes. It does not imply photometric survey completeness or infer any actual empirical eccentricity population.

**Closest prior work and residual risks.** Kane–von Braun (2009) supplies the underlying orbital probability equations; Kipping (2014) supplies a closely related but *transit-only* posterior-prior construction. A broader selection kernel result may subsume the computation, and purely geometric idealization can fail observationally. The original papers' relevant sections were read; broader community bibliographic coverage and independent audit are incomplete.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
