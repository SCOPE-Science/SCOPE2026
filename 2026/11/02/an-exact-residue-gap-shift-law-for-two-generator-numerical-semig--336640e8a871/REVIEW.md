# Self-review

## Correctness: PASS

The finite gap polynomial identity is derived from the unique semigroup representation modulo either coprime generator. Its values at every \(m\)-th root of unity are evaluated separately at generic points and each removable singular point, and Sylvester's genus identity fixes the value at one. The two elementary finite Fourier sums follow by a direct difference argument and hold when the relevant gcd equals one. CRT then proves the multiset law, sharp spread, variance, and quotient-genus corollary. Exact integer verification independently checked 57,323 residue comparisons across 5,005 admissible parameter triples. The proof is not an inference from finite testing.

## Originality: PASS, with priority uncertainty

Direct comparison with Liu (arXiv:2607.23076), Adeniran et al. (arXiv:1809.09360), Shor (arXiv:2101.01760), and Cabanillas (arXiv:1904.08240) identified no stated residuewise generator-translation theorem or sharp CRT histogram. Adeniran et al. provide a general Fourier method that is closely related and could imply a substantial portion after nontrivial specialization. Shor's static gap-equidistribution condition is not the condition for uniform increments, and Liu concerns Frobenius numbers. Searches of mathematical literature and related research records identified no exact predecessor, but absence of a search result is not proof of priority. Independent historical review remains outstanding.

## Mathematical value: PASS

The result supplies a motivated exact description of the full change in numerical-semigroup gap distribution as both generators are translated, including singular cases excluded by pairwise coprimality with the modulus. Its finite sharp CRT spectrum and variance refine the one-coordinate quotient-genus calculation, and the constant-profile criterion identifies a structural boundary. It is not merely the classical Sylvester genus formula or a recomputation of an established table.

## Scientific limits

Both initial and translated generator pairs must be coprime. No result for largest gaps of quotients or for noncoprime semigroup generators is claimed. The Fourier method is classical, and originality can be undermined by an unlocated stronger published theorem. Finite verification corroborates only the ranges tested. Independent scientific validation has not been completed.
