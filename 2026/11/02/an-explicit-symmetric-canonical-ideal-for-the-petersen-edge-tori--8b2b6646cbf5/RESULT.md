# An explicit symmetric canonical ideal for the Petersen edge toric variety

## Finding
Let \(P\) have vertex set \(V=\binom{\{1,2,3,4,5\}}2\), with two vertices adjacent precisely when the represented two-subsets are disjoint. Fix any field \(k\), and put
\[
R=k[t_ut_v:\{u,v\}\in E(P)],\qquad \deg(t_ut_v)=1.
\]
Define \(p=\prod_{v\in V}t_v\) and, for each \(v\in V\),
\[
q_v=p\,t_v\prod_{u\sim v}t_u.
\]
Inside \(R\), the graded canonical module admits the exact monomial-ideal realization
\[
\boxed{\omega_R\cong (p,q_v\mid v\in V).}
\]
These eleven monomials form a minimal generating set: \(\deg p=5\), and \(\deg q_v=7\) for all ten vertices. On this explicitly specified monomial ideal, the natural permutation action of \(S_5\) fixes \(p\) and permutes the ten \(q_v\) transitively. In characteristic zero its degree-seven generator quotient is the permutation representation \(S^{(5)}\oplus S^{(4,1)}\oplus S^{(3,2)}\). The associated projective edge toric variety has dimension nine.

## Assumptions and scope
All fields and characteristics are allowed. The canonical module is considered as a graded module for the standard grading of the edge algebra, not the ambient degree of the variables \(t_v\). The stated \(S_5\)-action concerns the displayed monomial-ideal realization (which fixes a particular equivariant structure); it does not impose an unexamined canonical linearization on the dualizing sheaf. The claim is about the ordinary edge polytope, not the different *symmetric* edge polytope.

## Proof
Write \(e_v\) for the coordinate vectors of \(\mathbb Z^{10}\). The semigroup of \(R\) is generated by \(e_u+e_v\) for the fifteen edges. The graph is connected, has girth five, and satisfies the odd-cycle condition: any two disjoint odd cycles must both have length five and exhaust the vertex set, hence some graph edge joins them. By the normality criterion of Ohsugi--Hibi, \(R\) is a normal semigroup ring; Hochster's theorem makes it Cohen--Macaulay of Krull dimension ten. The group of exponent differences is \(L=\{x\in\mathbb Z^{10}:\sum_v x_v\equiv0\pmod2\}\).

By the edge-polytope facet theorem, the full cone has ten coordinate facets \(x_v\ge0\) and fifteen further facets indexed by fundamental independent sets \(T\): ten triples and five quadruples. In every case \(T\cup N(T)=V\). Their integral primitive support forms on \(L\) are
\[
\ell_T(x)=\frac12\left(\sum_{v\notin T}x_v-\sum_{v\in T}x_v\right).
\]
For the Petersen graph these are all the facets: enumerate every independent subset of the ten vertices, test connectedness of its induced bipartite graph with its neighbor set, and test odd cycles in every remaining connected component (the complete finite procedure is in `verify.py`). The ten triples are the neighbor sets of individual vertices; the five quadruples are its maximum independent sets.

By the Danilov--Stanley theorem, the canonical module is the ideal generated by all monomials with exponents in the *strict* interior of this cone. Such an exponent vector satisfies \(x_v\ge1\) for all vertices. Therefore write uniquely \(x=\mathbf1+b\), where \(b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{10}\) and \(\sum_v b_v=2r\); then the degree is \(5+r\), and the remaining facet inequalities are exactly
\[
5-|T|+r-\sum_{v\in T}b_v\ge1\qquad\text{for each fundamental }T.
\]
A strict interior monomial is *minimal* over \(R\) if and only if no edge monomial can be divided out while staying in the interior. For an edge \(\{a,c\}\), this is decided exactly by \(b_a,b_c\ge1\) and the displayed fifteen inequalities after replacing \(b\) by \(b-e_a-e_c\). This tests all possible nontrivial semigroup divisors because every nonzero semigroup element is a sum of graph edges and the interior is stable under addition by the cone.

The standard graded Cohen--Macaulay canonical module has regularity ten: graded local duality gives \(a(\omega_R)=-\operatorname{indeg}R=0\), and \(\operatorname{reg}(\omega_R)=a(\omega_R)+\dim R=10\). Every minimal generator has degree at most ten. Thus enumerating \(r=0,1,2,3,4,5\) is an *exhaustive infinite-proof certificate*, not just a sample. The respective counts of interior monomials and minimal generators in degrees five through ten are
\[
(1,1),\ (15,0),\ (130,10),\ (760,0),\ (3345,0),\ (11953,0).
\]
The unique degree-five generator is \(p\); the ten degree-seven minimal exponents are precisely \(\mathbf1+\sum_{u\in N[v]}e_u\), where \(N[v]\) is the closed neighborhood of \(v\). These are exactly the \(q_v\), and there are no other degrees to inspect. Permuting the underlying five labels acts transitively on the ten closed neighborhoods, establishing the explicit equivariant statement. The characteristic-zero decomposition of the ten-point permutation module is the standard two-subset permutation-character decomposition.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify.py` with the standard Python 3 library. The program enumerates all fundamental independent sets, checks graph normality conditions, exhaustively enumerates integer exponent compositions through degree ten, tests strict interior inequalities and edge-removability, and separately counts Hilbert functions through degree five. It confirms ten fundamental triples, five quadruples, exactly eleven minimal generators in the indicated degrees, and the independently published Hilbert numerator \(1+5t+15t^2+25t^3+5t^4+t^5\). Exact integer arithmetic is used. The termination of the infinite statement follows from the regularity argument, not from an unsupported finite search.

## Relationship to prior work
Ohsugi--Hibi (1998) establish the edge-polytope facet and normality criteria used here. Danilov--Stanley identify interior lattice monomials with the canonical module. Valencia--Villarreal (2003) study canonical modules for *bipartite* edge subrings and do not specialize to the nonbipartite Petersen graph. Hatasa--Kowaki--Matsushita (arXiv:2409.03176, Example 4.6) explicitly compute the Petersen edge ring's \(h\)-polynomial and establish almost-Gorensteinness. Together with their general formula, this already implies Cohen--Macaulay type eleven and nonlevelness. **Neither those numerical facts nor the Hilbert numerator are claimed as new.** The additional statement here is the explicit full monomial generating ideal, its precise generator degrees, and its natural ten-vertex symmetry orbit.

## Limitations
This is a determination of the canonical *module*, not a complete minimal free resolution or a calculation of all higher syzygies. The chosen monomial representation gives a convenient equivariant structure; a natural linearization of the geometric dualizing sheaf could differ by a character. The assertion is not an independent historical-priority certificate: equivalent explicit canonical ideals might exist in literature not recovered by the comparisons described in `AUDIT.json`. It has not undergone independent scientific validation.
