# An infinite family disproving weighted higher-Milnor rigidity

## Finding
For every pair of integers \(a\geq3\), \(b>2a\), define germs over \(\mathbb C\)
\[
f_0=x^a+y^b+z^b,\qquad f=x^a+y^b+z^b+xz^{b-2}.
\]
With positive weights \((b,a,a)\), the weighted principal part of \(f\) is \(f_0\). Define \(R=\mathbb C[[x,y,z]]\), \(\mathfrak m=(x,y,z)\), \(J(g)=(g_x,g_y,g_z)\), and \(\mu_k(g)=\dim_{\mathbb C}R/(\mathfrak m^kJ(g))\) for every nonnegative integer \(k\).

Both germs have an isolated critical point and their ordinary Milnor numbers agree:
\[
\mu_0(f)=\mu_0(f_0)=(a-1)(b-1)^2.
\]
Nevertheless, for \(k=b-1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_k(f_0)&=(a-1)(b-1)^2+3\binom{b+1}{3}-2\binom{b-a+2}{3},\\
\mu_k(f)&=\mu_k(f_0)-1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently the proposed higher-Milnor invariance under arbitrary semi-quasihomogeneous perturbations is false, even for a family of isolated hypersurface singularities with constant Milnor number. The smallest example \((a,b)=(3,7)\) has \(\mu_6(f_0)=200\) and \(\mu_6(f)=199\).

## Assumptions and scope
All power series and dimensions are over \(\mathbb C\). The defining germs are polynomial and are understood in the completed three-variable regular local ring. The weighted degree of each principal monomial is \(ab\). The weighted degree of the perturbation is \(b+a(b-2)=ab+b-2a>ab\), and \(f_0\) has an isolated critical point. The assertion concerns ordinary maximal-ideal powers \(\mathfrak m^k\), not weighted filtrations. The parameters range over all integers \(a\ge3\) and \(b>2a\), with no other genericity assumptions.

## Proof
**1. Jacobian complete intersections and Milnor length.** The three partial derivatives of \(f\) are
\[
A=ax^{a-1}+z^{b-2},\quad B=by^{b-1},\quad C=bz^{b-1}+(b-2)xz^{b-3}=z^{b-3}\bigl(bz^2+(b-2)x\bigr).
\]
Put \(h=a-1\), \(T=\mathbb C[[x,z]]/(A)\), and \(L=bz^2+(b-2)x\). Since \(A\) is a monic degree-\(h\) polynomial in \(x\) up to a nonzero constant, Weierstrass division shows that \(T\) is free of rank \(h\) over \(\mathbb C[[z]]\). In particular, \(z\) is a nonzerodivisor and \(\operatorname{length}(T/z^{b-3}T)=h(b-3)\). Substituting \(x=-bz^2/(b-2)\) in \(A\) gives a unit times \(z^{2h}\): indeed \(b-2>2h\). Therefore \(\operatorname{length}(T/LT)=2h\). As \(T\) is a one-dimensional Cohen--Macaulay local ring and \(T/LT\) is Artinian, \(L\) is a nonzerodivisor. Multiplication by \(L\) yields an exact sequence comparing \(T/(z^{b-3}L)\), \(T/(L)\), and \(T/(z^{b-3})\), hence
\[
\operatorname{length}\bigl(\mathbb C[[x,z]]/(A,C)\bigr)=h(b-1).
\]
The separate regular parameter \(y\) contributes a factor \(b-1\) via \(B=by^{b-1}\), proving \(\mu_0(f)=h(b-1)^2\); the same formula for \(f_0\) follows immediately from its monomial Jacobian. Thus each Jacobian ideal is generated by a regular sequence of length three in the three-dimensional regular local ring, so its full syzygy module is generated by the Koszul relations.

**2. A length identity.** For either \(g=f\) or \(f_0\), the short exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow J(g)/\mathfrak m^kJ(g)\longrightarrow R/\mathfrak m^kJ(g)\longrightarrow R/J(g)\longrightarrow0
\]
implies
\[
\mu_k(g)=\mu_0(g)+3\ell_k-\rho_k(g),\qquad \ell_k=\operatorname{length}(R/\mathfrak m^k)=\binom{k+2}{3},
\]
where \(\rho_k(g)\) is the dimension of the image of the Koszul syzygy module inside \((R/\mathfrak m^k)^3\).

**3. The principal part.** Set \(k=b-1\). In \(\overline R=R/\mathfrak m^k\), only the \(x\)-derivative of \(f_0\) survives, namely \(ax^h\); its other two derivatives have degree \(k\). Thus the Koszul image has dimension twice the dimension of the ideal \((x^h)\overline R\), which is
\[
r=\binom{k-h+2}{3}=\binom{b-a+2}{3},\qquad\rho_k(f_0)=2r.
\]
Here the binomial coefficient counts all monomials of total degree less than \(k-h\) in three variables.

**4. The perturbation creates exactly one new syzygy direction.** Modulo \(\mathfrak m^k\), its Jacobian generators become
\[
\overline A=ax^h+z^{k-1},\qquad \overline B=0,\qquad \overline C=(b-2)xz^{k-2}.
\]
The lowest homogeneous term of \(A\) is \(ax^h\), so the multiplication map \(\overline R\to\overline R\), \(u\mapsto\overline Au\), has image dimension \(r\) and kernel \(\mathfrak m^{k-h}/\mathfrak m^k\): its lowest degree cannot cancel because \(\mathbb C[x,y,z]\) is a domain. Moreover \(\overline C\notin \overline A\overline R\), since its degree-\((k-1)\) monomial \(xz^{k-2}\) is not divisible by \(x^h\), using \(h\ge2\). Therefore the two Koszul relations involving \(B\) produce the ideal \((\overline A,\overline C)\) in the middle free-module coordinate, of dimension \(r+1\).

The remaining Koszul relation produces vectors \((\overline C u,0,-\overline A u)\). These form a space of dimension exactly \(r\): vanishing of the \(\overline A\) component forces \(u\in\mathfrak m^{k-h}/\mathfrak m^k\), and then \(\overline Cu=0\), because \(\operatorname{ord}(C)=k-1\). The spaces from these disjoint coordinate supports form a direct sum. Hence \(\rho_k(f)=2r+1\), and the length identity gives
\[
\mu_k(f)-\mu_k(f_0)=-(\rho_k(f)-\rho_k(f_0))=-1.
\]
Substituting \(k=b-1\) and \(r=\binom{b-a+2}{3}\) yields the claimed exact formulas. All statements hold for the entire indicated infinite parameter range. \(\square\)

## Verification
The companion standalone `verify.py` performs exact finite-field row reduction on the truncated Koszul-syzygy spaces; it independently recomputes the predicted values for \((a,b)=(3,7),(3,8),(4,9),(4,10),(5,11)\) and prints `VERIFY_OK`. Coefficients are reduced modulo the prime \(32003\), which does not divide any of the Jacobian derivative coefficients in those tests. These finite checks verify the implementations and particular instances, not the infinite theorem; the preceding commutative-algebra argument proves the general statement. Run with `python3 verify.py` (standard library only).

## Relationship to prior work
Shi, Yau, and Zuo, *On \(T\)-maps and ideals of antiderivatives of hypersurface singularities* (2024), Theorem 4.63, proved the equality of higher Milnor numbers for a restricted initial range and Conjecture 4.65 proposed equality for all orders. Their Corollary 4.59 already gives the inequality \(\mu_k(f)\le\mu_k(f_0)\) for semi-quasihomogeneous germs, so the present strict defect is consistent with that known inequality but disproves the proposed global equality. Liu, Yau, and Zhang, *Jacobian layers and higher Milnor sequences: rigidity, stabilization, and log-concavity* (2026, arXiv:2610.32523v1), prove the conjectured equality in the equal-weight ordinary-semi-homogeneous case. The unequal weights \((b,a,a)\) in this construction are essential, and their theorem does not apply. The one-dimensional syzygy defect is the additional structural conclusion obtained here.

## Limitations
The family is over characteristic zero, with exactly three variables and integer parameters in the stated range. It does not determine the higher-Milnor difference at every index nor classify all weighted semi-quasihomogeneous germs. Computational checks at finitely many parameters are supplementary. Historical literature priority is subject to independent assessment; the cited 2024 conjecture and 2026 equal-weight theorem are not claimed as new. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
