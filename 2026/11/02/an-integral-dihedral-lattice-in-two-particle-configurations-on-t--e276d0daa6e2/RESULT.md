# An integral dihedral lattice in two-particle configurations on the Wagner graph

## Finding
For the eight-vertex Wagner (Möbius ladder) graph \(W\), consider the space \(X=\operatorname{UConf}_2(|W|)\) of unordered configurations of two distinct points. Its integral second homology is free of rank two. For the dihedral automorphisms \(r(i)=i+1\) and \(s(i)=-i\) there is an integral basis \(z_0,z_1\) such that
\[
r_*(z_0)=-z_1,\quad r_*(z_1)=-z_0,\quad s_*(z_0)=z_1,\quad s_*(z_1)=z_0.
\]
The full integral \(\mathbb Z[D_8]\)-module is **not** a direct sum of two rank-one character lattices: its rational eigenspaces meet the lattice in the primitive lines generated by \(z_0+z_1\) and \(z_0-z_1\), and these lines span an index-two sublattice.

Its rational Betti numbers in degrees zero through two are \((1,5,2)\). The corresponding quotient rational Betti numbers are \((1,0,0)\) for \(D_8\), \((1,1,1)\) for the rotation subgroup \(C_8\), and \((1,2,1)\) for the reflection subgroup generated by \(s\). In particular, the full quotient is rationally acyclic; no claim of contractibility is made.

## Assumptions and scope
Let vertices be \(\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z\). The twelve undirected edges are the eight cycle edges \(\{i,i+1\}\) and four opposite chords \(\{i,i+4\}\). Every vertex has degree three and the girth is four. The action is the natural graph-automorphism action of \(D_8\), with order sixteen, on unordered configurations. All integral claims refer to ordinary integral singular homology, while quotient Betti numbers use rational homology. The first-public 2020 source addresses second homology of graph braid groups, motivating the nonplanar instance; its planar result is not used to assert this calculation.

## Proof
The cubical discrete unordered two-particle configuration space \(Y\) has a cell for each unordered pair of disjoint closed cells of \(W\): 28 zero-cells, 72 oriented one-cells \((v,(a,b))\) and 42 oriented two-cells \(((a,b),(c,d))\), where each edge is oriented from smaller to larger endpoint and edge pairs are lexicographically ordered. The signed cubical boundary is
\[
\partial_1(v,(a,b))=\{v,b\}-\{v,a\},
\]
\[
\partial_2((a,b),(c,d))=(b,(c,d))-(a,(c,d))-(d,(a,b))+(c,(a,b)).
\]
Each incidence is interpreted as the corresponding oriented cell. The accompanying standard-library checker builds these matrices from these rules and verifies \(\partial_1\partial_2=0\).

An exact rational elimination gives \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_1=27\) and \(\operatorname{rank}\partial_2=40\), hence the rational Betti numbers \((1,5,2)\). Independently, modular reduction at prime 101 gives lower bounds 27 and 40; connectivity bounds the first rank by 27, and two independent integer cycles in degree two bound the second by 40, so the ranks do not depend on an unverified modular-to-rational inference.

The accompanying checker contains two explicit integer length-42 two-cycles, \(z_0\) and \(z_1\), each with 16 nonzero coefficients, satisfying \(\partial_2z_i=0\). Their entries at the last two indexed two-cells are respectively \((1,0)\) and \((0,1)\). Since the rational kernel of \(\partial_2\) has dimension two, any integral two-cycle is an integral linear combination of these cycles: its two final coordinates are precisely the two integral coefficients. Thus \(H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Zz_0\oplus\mathbb Zz_1\); there are no three-cells or higher cells.

Signed transport of each oriented cubical cell by all sixteen dihedral automorphisms commutes with both integral boundaries. In particular, the checker verifies the displayed action on the two explicit integral generators. Over \(\mathbb Q\), the two lines spanned by \(z_0+z_1\) and \(z_0-z_1\) are the character summands. These two vectors have determinant of absolute value two relative to \(z_0,z_1\), so no integral splitting into one-dimensional character lattices exists.

For every subgroup \(K\le D_8\), the cubical invariant rational chain complex computes \(H_*(Y;\mathbb Q)^K\): averaging is exact in characteristic zero. The checker enumerates signed cell orbits, forms their invariant chain boundary matrices, uses exact rational elimination, and obtains the three asserted quotient rank triples. The finite-group rational transfer theorem identifies these invariant groups with rational homology of the respective quotients.

For the continuous space \(X\), the stable-equivalence theorem of Prue and Scrimshaw applies to two strands because distinct essential vertices have graph distance at least one and every essential cycle has at least three edges (here, at least four). The natural inclusion \(Y\hookrightarrow X\) is a homotopy equivalence. Crucially, the inclusion itself is graph-automorphism equivariant, so it induces an isomorphism of integral dihedral homology modules whether or not a chosen retraction is equivariant. The quotient rational homology follows from invariance under a finite group; no equivariant deformation-retraction claim is necessary.

## Verification
Execute `python3 verify_wagner.py` using Python's standard library. It reconstructs all 142 cubical cells, both boundary maps and their exact rational ranks; checks all sixteen group actions on every cell; checks the two actual integer top cycles and their unimodular coordinate projection; and recomputes exact rational ranks of every invariant quotient chain complex. The successful marker is `WAGNER_TWO_POINT_INTEGRAL_DIHEDRAL_LATTICE_OK`. This is an exhaustive finite computation for the named graph, not evidence of a claimed infinite-family theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Prue and Scrimshaw supply the graph-configuration stable-equivalence theorem, not this homology module. An and Knudsen study second homology for planar graph braid groups and explicitly distinguish the nonplanar setting; their theorem does not cover this nonplanar graph or its integral dihedral representation. The graph braid presentations of An and Maciazek do not present the displayed Wagner-graph homology lattice. Indexed Wagner-graph matching, cut and discrete-Morse complexes have different defining cells, while an indexed two-particle Petersen calculation concerns a different underlying graph. The claimed novelty is strictly the computed integral lattice, its rational characters, and the named quotient Betti numbers, not the general cubical model or established graph braid theorems.

## Limitations
The calculation is specific to the eight-vertex Wagner graph. It gives no general classification for all Möbius ladders or all two-particle graph braid groups. The quotient assertions are about rational homology, not homotopy type or integral quotient torsion. Absence of an equivalent calculation in the inspected literature and mathematical index is not proof of global uniqueness; an unindexed equivalent result remains possible.
