# Mathematical review

## Correctness

The argument is conditional only on established ingredients: CSM proper functoriality, the integral zero-cycle exponential and its functoriality, the smooth projective curve identity of Fantechi–Göttsche, and existence of finite pushouts identifying finitely many distinct closed points. The new case requiring care is gluing points on different connected or irreducible components. The geometric fiber over an effective cycle with multiplicity \(m\) at the glued point has \(m+1\) points, while the correction fiber has \(m\), which proves the needed constructible-function identity. Finite bijections are compatible with symmetric powers over \(\mathbb C\). The branch product and the precise polynomial degree follow without division. Potential risks are accidental use of scheme-length counts in place of Euler counts, or extending characteristic-zero finite bijectivity arguments to positive characteristic; neither is done.

## Originality

The directly related paper arXiv:2610.31826v1 explicitly defines a curve to be complete, reduced and irreducible in §5.2, and proves that case in Theorem 5.13. The present theorem removes all three restrictions and gives an explicit normalization-branch product for reducible curves and an integral sharp vanishing phenomenon. Proposition 5.11 already contains the essential gluing mechanism for irreducible curves, so the novelty is a structural extension to disconnected normalizations and arbitrary reduced one-dimensional schemes, not a new CSM formalism. Public mathematical-record searches found no matching statement. Residual priority risk: the extension could be regarded as implicit in the original proof or elsewhere in the literature.

## Value

This completes the dimension-at-most-one case of an explicitly stated integral conjecture for projective complex schemes, which is stronger than the source's general equality modulo \(n!\)-torsion. The branch formula is sensitive to the normalization's component genera and branch multiplicities and proves exact, sharp integral vanishing for arbitrarily large symmetric powers of reducible curves with no rational normalization components. For two elliptic curves glued at \(r\) nodes, the degree-\(n\) zero-cycle CSM class vanishes integrally for all \(n>r\), while its top coefficient has degree \((-1)^r\).
