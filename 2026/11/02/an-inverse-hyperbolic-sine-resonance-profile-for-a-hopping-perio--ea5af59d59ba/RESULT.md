# An inverse-hyperbolic-sine resonance profile for a hopping period-three EOS attractor

## Finding

Let \(F_{a,b}(x)=x+b-g_a(x)\), where \(g_a(x)=(1+e^{-ax})^{-1}\), \(a>0\), and \(0<b<1\). Put \(q_a=e^{-a/6}\). Fix any finite \(S>0\). For every sufficiently large \(a\), uniformly for all real \(\sigma\) with \(|\sigma|\le S\), set
\[
b=b_a(\sigma)=\frac13+\sigma q_a.
\]
There exists an attracting, primitive period-three orbit \(x_0\mapsto x_1\mapsto x_2\mapsto x_0\), uniquely specified among periodic orbits satisfying \(|a(x_0+1/2)|\le L(S)\) and with the indicated itinerary \(x_0<x_1<0<x_2\). Here \(L(S)>0\) is a sufficiently large constant independent of \(a\). Define the universal response function
\[
z_*(\sigma)=\operatorname{arsinh}\left(\frac{3\sigma}{2}\right),\qquad
R(\sigma)=\sqrt{1+\frac{9\sigma^2}{4}}.
\]
Then the three orbit positions have the **uniform nonlinear crossover**
\[
\boxed{x_j=c_j+\frac{z_*(\sigma)}a+O_S(q_a),\qquad
(c_0,c_1,c_2)=\left(-\frac12,-\frac16,\frac16\right).}
\]
In particular, \(a(x_0+1/2)\to z_*(\sigma)\) uniformly on every compact \(\sigma\)-interval. If \(\mu(a,\sigma)=(F_{a,b}^3)'(x_0)\) is the full-return multiplier, then
\[
\boxed{\mu(a,\sigma)=1-2a q_a R(\sigma)+O_S(a^2q_a^2).}
\]
The smallest orbit point, considered as a smooth function of \(b\) at fixed \(a\), obeys
\[
\boxed{\frac{\partial x_0}{\partial b}=
\frac{1}{a q_a}\left[\frac{3}{2R(\sigma)}+O_S(aq_a)\right].}
\]
Consequently its exponentially amplified parameter slope and its vanishing attraction gap satisfy
\[
\boxed{\left(1-\mu(a,\sigma)\right)\frac{\partial x_0}{\partial b}
=3+O_S(aq_a).}
\]
At exact resonance \(b=1/3\), the slope is asymptotic to \(3e^{a/6}/(2a)\); neither a mere linear approximation nor the fixed-parameter half-grid law gives the nonlinear dependence on \(\sigma\).

## Assumptions and scope

The asymptotic limit is \(a\to+\infty\) with the bound \(S\) fixed and real \(|\sigma|\le S\). Thus \(0<b_a(\sigma)<1\) eventually. \(O_S(\cdot)\) means an error bounded uniformly over that \(\sigma\)-set, with constants allowed to depend on \(S\). The orbit is local to the indicated half-grid itinerary; no claim is made about other periodic orbits or global attraction. Each of the three orbit points lies in the invariant interval \([b-1,b]\) for all sufficiently large \(a\).

The phase coordinate is \(z=a(x_0+1/2)\); the specific half-grid representative \(-1/2\) is selected so that \(x_1\) lies near \(-1/6\) and \(x_2\) near \(+1/6\). Multipliers refer to the three-step derivative, not a one-step derivative. The sensitivity derivative holds with \(a\) fixed while \(b\) varies.

## Proof

**Step 1: an explicit three-step displacement and its differentiated expansion.** Choose fixed \(L,S<\infty\) and write \(q=e^{-a/6}\), \(b=1/3+\sigma q\), \(x=-1/2+z/a\) for \(|z|\le L\), \(|\sigma|\le S\). For \(a\to\infty\), standard expansions of \(g_a\) uniformly with first derivatives in \(z,\sigma\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 g_a(x)&=q^3e^z+O_{L,S}(q^6),\\
 F_{a,b}(x)&=-\frac16+\frac za+\sigma q+O_{L,S}(q^3),\\
 g_a(F_{a,b}(x))&=q e^z+O_{L,S}(a q^2),\\
 F_{a,b}^2(x)&=\frac16+\frac za+(2\sigma-e^z)q+O_{L,S}(a q^2),\\
 1-g_a(F_{a,b}^2(x))&=q e^{-z}+O_{L,S}(a q^2).
\end{aligned}
\]
These estimates are uniform also after a single derivative with respect to either \(z\) or \(\sigma\). For example the sole potentially large factor generated by differentiating an exponential perturbation is \(a\), while \(a q\to0\). Define the smooth scaled return displacement
\[
H_a(z,\sigma)=q^{-1}\left[F_{a,b}^3\left(-\frac12+\frac za\right)-\left(-\frac12+\frac za\right)\right].
\]
Since three occurrences of the translation by \(b\) total \(1+3\sigma q\) and exactly the last sigmoid value approaches one, the previous expansions yield the uniform \(C^1\) bounds
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
H_a(z,\sigma)&=3\sigma-2\sinh z+O_{L,S}(a q),\\
\partial_z H_a(z,\sigma)&=-2\cosh z+O_{L,S}(a q),\\
\partial_\sigma H_a(z,\sigma)&=3+O_{L,S}(a q).
\end{aligned}}
\]
All estimates follow by composition of elementary, uniformly differentiable logistic expansions; no formal asymptotic interchange is needed.

**Step 2: existence, exact period and uniqueness on the stated branch.** For a prescribed \(S\), choose \(L>\operatorname{arsinh}(3S/2)+1\). Then \(3\sigma-2\sinh(-L)>0\) and \(3\sigma-2\sinh L<0\) for every \(|\sigma|\le S\). By uniform approximation these inequalities hold for \(H_a(-L,\sigma)\) and \(H_a(L,\sigma)\) for large \(a\). Moreover \(\partial_z H_a<-1\) throughout \([-L,L]\). The intermediate value theorem and strict monotonicity give exactly one zero \(z_a(\sigma)\in(-L,L)\) for each \(\sigma\), smoothly depending on \(\sigma\). This zero defines the claimed three-cycle. Its three points are near three distinct sites \(-1/2,-1/6,+1/6\); hence its least period is three. Uniform comparison with the strictly monotone limiting function yields
\[
z_a(\sigma)=\operatorname{arsinh}(3\sigma/2)+O_S(aq).
\]
Since the successive one-step displacements from their target half-grid sites are \(O_S(q)\), this also proves the stated orbit-position expansions.

**Step 3: attracting multiplier.** Let \(x_0=-1/2+z_a/a\). Differentiating the *definition* of \(H_a\) gives the exact identity
\[
\partial_z H_a(z_a,\sigma)=\frac{(F_{a,b}^3)'(x_0)-1}{aq}=\frac{\mu-1}{a q}.
\]
Therefore the second expansion in Step 1 and \(z_a=z_*+O_S(aq)\) give
\[
\mu=1-2aq\cosh z_*(\sigma)+O_S(a^2q^2)
=1-2aqR(\sigma)+O_S(a^2q^2).
\]
In particular \(0<\mu<1\) for all sufficiently large \(a\), uniformly for \(|\sigma|\le S\). The period-three orbit is hyperbolic and attracting.

**Step 4: parameter response.** The strict inequality \(\partial_z H_a<0\) permits implicit differentiation of \(H_a(z_a(\sigma),\sigma)=0\):
\[
\frac{d z_a}{d\sigma}=-\frac{\partial_\sigma H_a}{\partial_z H_a}
=\frac{3}{2R(\sigma)}+O_S(aq).
\]
Because \(db/d\sigma=q\), \(dx_0/d\sigma=(1/a)dz_a/d\sigma\), and \(a\) is fixed, the claimed \(\partial_b x_0\) formula follows. Finally multiplication by \(1-\mu=2aqR(\sigma)+O_S(a^2q^2)\) gives the universal phase-slope identity. This proves every asserted infinite-parameter statement.

## Verification

The standalone `verify_eos.py` uses 95-digit `mpmath` arithmetic. It locates the three-cycle by solving the exact return displacement, verifies the three-step residual and ordering, differentiates the return map, and compares the multiplier gap and parameter sensitivity against their predicted nonlinear \(\sigma\)-profiles. It tests three steepness parameters \(a\in\{72,96,120\}\) and five shifts \(\sigma\in\{-2,-1/2,0,1/2,2\}\), for 15 configurations. All assertions passed on execution. The maximum relative slope discrepancy at \(a=72\) was below \(0.002\), improving rapidly as \(a\) increases. The symbolic identity between the return displacement derivative and the Floquet multiplier is also checked numerically. The proof above, rather than finite testing, supplies the conclusions for every sufficiently large real \(a\) and every \(\sigma\) in a fixed compact interval.

Run with `python verify_eos.py` in a Python environment containing `mpmath`.

## Relationship to prior work

Bielawski, Chotibut, Falniowski, Misiurewicz and Piliouras [1], Section 3, describe an exceptionally steep slope of the attracting period-three point as \(b\) passes near \(1/3\), using numerical bifurcation diagrams and near-identity graphs of the third iterate. Their full accessible text does not give the exponentially rescaled parameter width, the inverse-hyperbolic-sine crossover, or its joint Floquet and derivative asymptotics. Their earlier theorem [2], Theorem 3.3 and Sections 4–5, establishes almost-everywhere attraction at **fixed rational** \(b=k/n\) for steep maps, without a drifting-parameter profile.

The published result [3] supplies a fixed-rational-parameter half-grid expansion and \(1-2ae^{-a/(2n)}\) multiplier asymptotic, again with \(b\) fixed. At \(\sigma=0\) its multiplier formula agrees with the present limit, but it gives no uniform formula when \(b\) is detuned by \(\sigma e^{-a/6}\), nor the nonlinear parameter derivative. The 2018 game-theory source [4] covers qualitative complex dynamics and periodic behavior for another conjugate parametrization, not this resonance response law. These are comparisons of mathematical implications, not assertions of priority based on title differences.

## Limitations

The statements are uniform only for bounded \(\sigma\), not for parameter deviations of fixed size or a growing rescaled window. They neither describe the complete macroscopic hopping transition nor classify all three-cycles, their basins, or global dynamics. No explicit minimum steepness \(a\) is asserted. The calculations do not prove historical uniqueness against every possible specialized treatment of smoothed rational rotations; the recorded literature search and close-text comparisons support originality within inspected evidence only. The verification script is a numerical diagnostic, not a proof by exhaustive enumeration or an independent audit.
