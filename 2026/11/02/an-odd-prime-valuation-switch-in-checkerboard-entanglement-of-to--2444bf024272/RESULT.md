# An odd-prime valuation switch in checkerboard entanglement of toroidal qudit graph states

## Finding

Let \(p\ge3\) be any odd prime and let \(m,n\ge4\) be even integers. Prepare the standard unweighted \(p\)-dimensional qudit graph state on the periodic square grid \(G=C_m\square C_n\), and bipartition its \(mn\) sites into even and odd checkerboard parity. Put \(g=\gcd(m,n)\), and let \(v_p\) denote the ordinary \(p\)-adic valuation on positive integers. Define

\[
\delta_p(m,n)=\begin{cases}1,&v_p(m)=v_p(n),\\0,&v_p(m)\ne v_p(n).\end{cases}
\]

**The exact Schmidt rank** of this cut is

\[
\operatorname{SR}_p(G)=p^{r_p(m,n)},\qquad
r_p(m,n)=\frac{mn}{2}-g+\delta_p(m,n).
\]

All nonzero squared Schmidt coefficients are equal to \(p^{-r_p(m,n)}\), each occurring exactly \(p^{r_p(m,n)}\) times. Therefore the von Neumann entropy and every positive-order Rényi entropy equal \(r_p(m,n)\) in base-\(p\) dits, or \(r_p(m,n)\log_2 p\) in bits. In particular, equal \(p\)-adic valuations increase the entanglement by *exactly one dit* relative to unequal valuations at fixed \(m,n,g\); the statement is a comparison of the two arithmetic branches, not an assertion that the valuations can be changed while holding both dimensions fixed.

For example, a \(4\times6\) grid has \(r_3=10\) and \(r_5=11\), whereas a \(6\times18\) grid has \(r_3=48\) and \(r_5=49\).

## Assumptions and scope

The graph \(C_m\square C_n\) is the simple undirected Cartesian product of two cycles, with each of its four nearest-neighbor edges carrying unit phase weight in \(\mathbb F_p\). The dimensions \(m,n\) are even and at least four, ensuring a proper checkerboard bipartition with \(mn/2\) sites on each side and no duplicate neighbor edges. Each local Hilbert space has prime dimension \(p\); \(\omega=\exp(2\pi i/p)\). Start with \(|+\rangle=p^{-1/2}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}|j\rangle\) on each site and apply the commuting two-qudit controlled-phase operator \(\mathrm{CZ}_p|a,b\rangle=\omega^{ab}|a,b\rangle\) along each edge. No measurement, noise or missing bonds are assumed. The theorem does not include characteristic two or nonprime local dimensions.

## Proof

**1. Quantum reduction to a finite-field cut rank.** Index the two checkerboard classes by \(A,B\), each of size \(h=mn/2\), and let \(D\in\mathbb F_p^{h\times h}\) be the binary-incidence matrix of grid edges between them, interpreted in \(\mathbb F_p\). In computational bases the graph state is

\[
|G\rangle=p^{-h}\sum_{u\in\mathbb F_p^h}\sum_{v\in\mathbb F_p^h}
\omega^{u^{\mathsf T}Dv}|u\rangle_A|v\rangle_B.
\]

For fixed \(u\), the normalized \(B\)-side vector is the Fourier character indexed by \(D^{\mathsf T}u\). Characters are mutually orthogonal, and each frequency in the image of \(D^{\mathsf T}\) occurs for exactly \(p^{h-r}\) values of \(u\), where \(r=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_p}D\). Equivalently the reduced density matrix has precisely \(p^r\) equal nonzero eigenvalues \(p^{-r}\). Hence all specified entropies equal \(r\) dits.

**2. Laurent polynomial representation.** Let \(T\) be the full \(mn\times mn\) adjacency matrix, over \(\mathbb F_p\). In the ordered checkerboard decomposition,

\[
T=\begin{pmatrix}0&D\\D^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\operatorname{nullity}T=2\operatorname{nullity}D.
\]

Identify periodic functions on the grid with

\[
R=\mathbb F_p[x^{\pm1},y^{\pm1}]/(x^m-1,y^n-1).
\]

The operator \(T\) is multiplication by

\[
\Phi=x+x^{-1}+y+y^{-1}=\frac{(x+y)(1+xy)}{xy}.
\]

Since \(xy\) is a unit and \(R\) is finite-dimensional, the nullity of multiplication by \(\Phi\) equals the vector-space dimension of \(R/(\Phi)\). The factorization will count that quotient exactly, including all inseparable root multiplicities.

**3. Separate the prime-power and prime-to-prime parts.** Write

\[
m=PM,\quad n=QN,\quad P=p^a,\quad Q=p^b,\quad p\nmid MN,
\quad d_0=\gcd(M,N),\quad d=\gcd(m,n)=d_0\min(P,Q).
\]

Because \(p\) is odd and \(m,n\) are even, \(M,N\) are even. Extend scalars to an algebraic closure \(K\) of \(\mathbb F_p\), which preserves all finite matrix ranks. Frobenius gives \(x^m-1=(x^M-1)^P\) and \(y^n-1=(y^N-1)^Q\). Thus \(R\otimes K\) is a direct sum of truncated local rings

\[
K[s,t]/(s^P,t^Q)
\]

one for each root pair \((\zeta,\eta)\) satisfying \(\zeta^M=\eta^N=1\), using \(x=\zeta(1+s)\), \(y=\eta(1+t)\). A summand can contribute to the quotient by \(\Phi\) only if either \(\zeta+\eta=0\) or \(\zeta\eta+1=0\). Each of these two root loci has exactly \(d_0\) pairs: substitute \(\eta=-\zeta\) or \(\eta=-\zeta^{-1}\) and use that \(M,N\) are even. Their intersection consists of exactly the two pairs \((1,-1)\) and \((-1,1)\), since \(p\ne2\).

**4. Nonintersection local lengths.** At any root pair lying on exactly one factor, the other factor is a unit. The quotient is generated by a single local coordinate: for \(x+y\), the equation has \(s\) equal to a unit multiple of \(t\); for \(1+xy\), solve \(s=-t/(1+t)\). In either case the quotient is isomorphic to \(K[t]/(t^P,t^Q)\), of dimension

\[
L_1(P,Q)=\min(P,Q).
\]

There are precisely \(2d_0-4\) such local summands.

**5. Exact crossing multiplicity.** At either common root, up to invertible factors and harmless sign changes, the local equation is

\[
h(s,t)=(s-t)(s+t+st)=s^2(1+t)-(1+s)t^2.
\]

We claim that

\[
I(P,Q):=\dim_K K[s,t]/(s^P,t^Q,h)
=\begin{cases}2\min(P,Q)-1,&P=Q,\\2\min(P,Q),&P\ne Q.\end{cases}
\]

To prove it, first work with \(A_P=K[s]/(s^P)\). Because \(1+s\) is a unit, the equation \(h=0\) makes \(t\) satisfy a *monic quadratic* over \(A_P\):

\[
t^2=s^2\frac{1+t}{1+s}.
\]

Thus \(L=A_P[t]/(h)\) is free over \(A_P\) on \(1,t\), of dimension \(2P\). Frobenius applied to the displayed identity gives \(t^{2P}=0\). If \(P<Q\), both are powers of \(p\), hence \(Q\ge pP\ge3P>2P\); therefore \(t^Q=0\) already in \(L\), and \(I(P,Q)=2P\). By symmetry in \(s,t\), \(I(P,Q)=2Q\) when \(Q<P\).

For \(P=Q=R\), the exponent \(R\) is odd. Inside \(L\),

\[
t^R=s^{R-1}t\left(\frac{1+t}{1+s}\right)^{(R-1)/2}.
\]

The parenthesized factor is a unit. Since \(L\) has the free \(A_R\)-basis \(1,t\), the element \(s^{R-1}t\) is nonzero; it is annihilated by multiplication by both \(s\) and \(t\) (use \(t^2=s^2(1+t)/(1+s)\)). Therefore the ideal generated by \(t^R\) is exactly one-dimensional, and imposing \(t^R=0\) reduces the dimension from \(2R\) to \(2R-1\). This establishes the crossing formula, including \(R=1\).

**6. Count and return to entanglement.** Summing the disjoint local pieces gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{nullity}T
&=(2d_0-4)\min(P,Q)+2I(P,Q)\\
&=\begin{cases}2d-2,&a=b,\\2d,&a\ne b.\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(\operatorname{nullity}D=d-1\) for equal valuations and \(d\) otherwise, and

\[
\operatorname{rank}D=h-\operatorname{nullity}D
=\frac{mn}{2}-\gcd(m,n)+\mathbf1_{\{v_p(m)=v_p(n)\}}.
\]

The first step now yields the claimed exact Schmidt spectrum and entropy. All local-algebra identities were proved for arbitrary allowable parameters; finite calculations are not used to extrapolate the theorem. \(\square\)

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `qudit_torus_check.py` uses only Python's standard library. It independently builds the checkerboard edge matrix, computes its rank by exact modular Gaussian elimination, and compares the rank against the theorem for 420 combinations of \(p,m,n\) with \(p\in\{3,5,7,11\}\), \(m,n\) even in a specified finite range. It also independently constructs multiplication-by-\(h\) matrices in the truncated crossing algebra and checks the local quotient length for 15 prime-power parameter pairs. All 435 comparisons passed. Run `python3 qudit_torus_check.py` and compare to `qudit_torus_verification.log`. These are finite checks, not a replacement for Steps 1–6.

## Relationship to prior work

Graph-state Schmidt spectra reduce to graph cut ranks by standard stabilizer and character arguments. In characteristic two this appears, for example, in Hein, Eisert and Briegel and Hein and collaborators. The qudit controlled-phase formalism and associated finite-field adjacency conventions are standard, as in Looi and collaborators and Chen–Zhou. Zaidenberg develops the much broader periodic finite-characteristic convolution viewpoint; that foundational machinery is acknowledged rather than claimed here. Hunziker, Machiavelo and Park analyze finite-field square-grid automata with open rectangular boundary and different local update polynomials.

The present statement concerns **the explicit all-odd-prime toroidal adjacency nullity**, in particular the difference between *equal and unequal prime-adic valuations* arising from the common-zero local intersection. These exact coefficients and the ensuing qudit checkerboard entropy dichotomy were not located in the primary material inspected. General cut-rank and convolution frameworks do not supply this closed formula without resolving the inseparable two-branch crossing. Priority against unlocated specialized modular-grid rank tables remains provisional.

## Limitations

Only odd-prime local dimension, unit-weight simple toroidal square-grid bonds, even side lengths at least four, ideal pure graph states and the checkerboard cut are covered. Local edge weights, boundary twists, other cuts, noise and composite local dimensions may alter the answer. The formula computes entanglement, not preparation depth or robustness. Literature search and self-review cannot establish independent priority or external audit; those have not been performed.
