## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
The foundational quantum-complete-intersection paper of Bergh–Erdmann (arXiv:0709.3029) studies Hochschild (co)homology, not the algebra anti-involution locus. The closely related Jin–Zhang papers (arXiv:2208.05687 and arXiv:2309.01485) study bi-Frobenius coalgebra compatibility and special permutation antipodes; a restricted antipode construction is not a complete classification of arbitrary algebra anti-involutions. Earlier finite-field automorphism and free cube-zero anti-involution calculations concern different operator conditions or different algebras. No inspected published statement implies the joint four-regime census, fixed-space spectrum, and geometric component classification. A broad unlocated classification of involutions on quantum complete intersections remains a priority risk; searches alone cannot establish publication novelty.

## Value — PASS
The family is a standard two-generator quantum complete intersection. Complete anti-involution classification is a natural \(\ast\)-algebra invariant, and the four regimes produce nontrivial shifts in both finite-field statistics and reduced moduli geometry. In particular, the exterior case has three components in odd characteristic but only one in characteristic two, while the commutative odd-characteristic specialization has five. The fixed-space histogram distinguishes structures that a raw automorphism-group cardinality does not capture. This is a structural classification across all parameters and fields, not a single arbitrary enumeration.
