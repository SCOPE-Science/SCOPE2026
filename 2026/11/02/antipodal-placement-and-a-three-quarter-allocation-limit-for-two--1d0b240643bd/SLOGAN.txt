On a homogeneous cycle with two imperfect traps and a fixed total killing-rate budget, a particle launched on one trap has the shortest mean lifetime when the other trap is antipodal and receives exactly \(K/[2+\sqrt{4+K\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor/(Dn)}]\) of the rate. The distant trap always receives less than one quarter of the budget, yet the optimized arrangement asymptotically halves the mean lifetime compared with concentrating all killing at the starting site.
