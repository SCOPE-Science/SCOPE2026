# Antipodal rigidity and two-replacement mobility of total perfect codes on \(Q_4\)

## Finding
Let \(Q_4\) be the graph with vertex set \(\mathbb F_2^4\), in which vertices are adjacent if they differ in one coordinate. A *total perfect code*, also called an *efficient open dominating set*, is a subset \(C\) for which every vertex of \(Q_4\), including vertices of \(C\), has exactly one neighbour in \(C\). Put \(u=(1,1,1,1)\) and let \(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4\) denote the coordinate vectors. Every total perfect code is of the form
\[
 C_{x,i}=\{x,x+e_i,x+u,x+u+e_i\},\qquad x\in\mathbb F_2^4,\quad 1\le i\le4.
\]
There are exactly \(16\) distinct such sets. Let \(\mathcal R\) have these codes as vertices, adjacent if one is obtained from the other by removing exactly two occupied cube vertices and inserting exactly two previously unoccupied vertices. Then
\[
 \mathcal R\cong L(K_{4,4})\cong K_4\mathbin{\square}K_4.
\]
In particular \(\mathcal R\) is connected and strongly regular with parameters \((16,6,2,2)\), has \(48\) edges, and has radius and diameter \(2\). Every vertex has distance profile \((1,6,9)\). For any two codes \(C,D\), their distance is exactly \(\lvert C\setminus D\rvert/2\), and each distance-two pair has precisely two two-step shortest transformations. The graph allowing only a single occupied-vertex replacement has \(16\) isolated vertices, so two simultaneous replacements are the sharp minimum batch size enabling connectivity. Finally, precisely \(24\) unordered partitions of the \(16\) cube vertices into four total perfect codes exist, and every code belongs to exactly six such partitions.

## Assumptions and scope
All additions are in the binary vector space \(\mathbb F_2^4\). The four coordinates and all sixteen cube vertices are labeled, but partitions and codes are regarded as unordered sets. Each permitted reconfiguration step exchanges exactly two selected vertices for two unselected vertices while retaining the total-perfect-code condition. Distance is ordinary shortest-path length in this fixed exchange graph. The assertions concern the four-dimensional cube only, not arbitrary hypercube dimensions.

## Proof
**Forced size and matching.** The open neighbourhoods of elements of a total perfect code partition the sixteen cube vertices. Since every open neighbourhood has four vertices, the code has exactly four vertices. Every code vertex has exactly one code neighbour, hence \(Q_4[C]\) consists of two disjoint edges.

**Exhaustive structural classification.** Select one code edge \(\{a,a+e_i\}\) and translate it to \(\{0,e_i\}\). For distinct cube vertices \(v,w\), their open neighbourhoods intersect if and only if their Hamming distance is two; moreover, each open neighbourhood intersects itself. Thus a third code vertex \(c\), different from \(0,e_i\), must avoid distances zero and two from *both* \(0\) and \(e_i\). Of the fourteen other binary words, only \(u\) and \(u+e_i\) meet both exclusions. To see this without enumeration, if \(c\) has Hamming weight one then it either is \(e_i\) or lies at distance two from \(e_i\); if it has weight two it is excluded by \(0\); if it has weight three containing coordinate \(i\), it is at distance two from \(e_i\), while the unique weight-three word omitting coordinate \(i\) is \(u+e_i\); the unique weight-four word is \(u\). Accordingly both remaining code vertices are forced to be \(u,u+e_i\). Conversely the four open neighbourhoods of \(0,e_i,u,u+e_i\) are disjoint and cover all sixteen vertices (pairwise distinct centers are at distances one, three or four). Translation gives exactly the displayed codes. For each fixed direction \(i\), the affine plane \(\langle u,e_i\rangle\) has four cosets. Its induced matching edges have direction \(i\), which is intrinsic to the code, so the four directions yield \(4\cdot4=16\) distinct codes.

**Antipodal quotient.** Identify each \(x\) with \(x+u\). These are eight antipodal pairs, partitioned into four even-parity and four odd-parity pairs. Each antipodal pair has one neighbour pair in each coordinate direction, and the four neighbours are distinct. Consequently the simple quotient is exactly \(K_{4,4}\). The code \(C_{x,i}\) is the full inverse image of the quotient edge connecting \(\{x,x+u\}\) to \(\{x+e_i,x+e_i+u\}\). Conversely each of the sixteen quotient edges gives one of the sixteen codes, a bijection.

**Complete exchange metric.** For distinct quotient edges \(e,f\), their inverse-image codes intersect in exactly two cube vertices if the quotient edges share one endpoint, and in zero vertices otherwise. Since codes have size four, a two-vertex replacement is legal precisely for incident quotient edges. The reconfiguration graph is therefore \(L(K_{4,4})\). Label quotient edges by pairs \((a,b)\), with \(a\) and \(b\) ranging over four-element parts. Two pairs are adjacent precisely if they agree in one coordinate. This is \(K_4\square K_4\): each state has three neighbours by changing either coordinate, total degree six; there are \(16\cdot6/2=48\) edges. From any state, six pairs differ in exactly one coordinate and nine differ in both. The latter can be reached in two steps in exactly two ways, by changing coordinates in either order. These facts give every distance and the strongly regular parameters \((16,6,2,2)\). The intersection sizes also give \(d(C,D)=\lvert C\setminus D\rvert/2\). No distinct codes differ by only one occupied vertex, so one-vertex exchange permits no move.

**Optimal partitions.** A collection of four codes partitions the sixteen cube vertices exactly when their four quotient edges partition the eight antipodal quotient vertices. Such collections are the perfect matchings of \(K_{4,4}\), of which there are \(4!=24\). A given edge lies in \(3!=6\) such matchings, proving the final multiplicity.

## Verification
Two standard-library Python programs provide independent finite checks. Running `python direct_certificate.py` enumerates every four-subset of the sixteen cube vertices and tests the exact open-neighbourhood condition; it builds the two-replacement adjacency, computes breadth-first-search distance profiles from every code, checks every distance and common-neighbour count, excludes single swaps, and counts all partitions. Running `python quotient_certificate.py` independently constructs the antipodal quotient, checks that it is \(K_{4,4}\), verifies the full line-graph adjacency equivalence, the affine classification, the cross-edge geodesics and the perfect-matching count. Both were executed and passed; the reports are `DIRECT_Q4_OPEN_NEIGHBORHOOD_PASS 16 48 Counter({4: 72, 2: 48})` and `ANTIPODAL_QUOTIENT_K44_PASS 8 16 Counter({'distance_two': 72, 'adjacent': 48})`. The proof above, not just the finite census, establishes the claimed classification and metric.

## Relationship to prior work
Kwon and Lee (2014) had already characterized hypercubes admitting perfect total dominating sets using graph-bundle and covering projections; in particular their existence theorem includes a covering of \(K_{n,n}\) at dimensions that are powers of two. Zhou (2016, arXiv first publicly posted 2016-01-14) established the corresponding cubelike-graph criterion and displayed one partition of \(Q_4\) into four explicit total perfect codes (Example 4.3). That example and the general existence criterion are not claimed as new. Zhou's Lemma 3.1 already gives a partition by translates from any total perfect code, but not the exact number of partitions. The new, specifically scoped assertion is a proof that *all* total perfect codes on \(Q_4\) are antipodal-edge preimages, together with the complete two-replacement graph and partition multiplicity. The quotient \(K_{4,4}\) itself is not claimed to be a new graph observation. The accessible primary texts and targeted searches did not locate this full reconfiguration classification.

## Limitations
This is a theorem only for \(Q_4\), and does not classify total perfect codes in larger cubes. The structural formula should not be mistaken for a characterization of all codes in cubes of dimensions eight or more, where nonlinear examples are known. The full text of Kwon and Lee (2014) was not accessible for a detailed local-count comparison; their published abstract confirms the covering result, so unindexed or inaccessible equivalent computations are a residual originality risk. Neither the literature search nor the finite computations independently certifies historical priority.
