The theory of coherent dark states and their removal by dephasing is established in Caruso et al. (2009). Kassal and Aspuru-Guzik (2012) treat the same ordered path Hamiltonian, but use uniform dephasing and emphasize capture versus bulk recombination. Le et al. (2017), especially section II.1, explicitly provide the path sine eigenvectors and nodal sites, and section III.3 discusses noise-induced removal of dark states. Their displayed consequences do not specify the joint gcd of sink and *arbitrary subset* of noisy sites nor establish the full all-initial-state asymptotic capture formula with a positive-rate exhaustion proof. Searches for equivalent formulations, broad coverage, exact tables, and prior implication produced no established dominating classification in examined material. This absence is not proof of novelty. ## Mathematical value — PASS

The question is motivated by minimal spatial placement of dephasing for complete excitation capture in a coherent path. It supplies an all-path-length, all-site, exact decision criterion and the retained fraction, not merely an example or a normal-form rewrite. It distinguishes a single noise site that removes all trapping from one that leaves an exact rational nonabsorbing fraction, at arbitrary positive dephasing strengths. This makes a concrete structural bridge between eigenvector nodes, arithmetic, and asymptotic quantum transport design.
