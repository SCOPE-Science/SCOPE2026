# Scientific review: Arithmetic duality of ground and bond damping in a fixed–free harmonic chain

## Correctness — PASS

The complete proof derives the fixed–free normal modes, the exact divisibility condition for ground and intermass bond dashpots, the Bézout classification of protected oscillations, exponential decay of the complementary stable subspace, and an exact projector sum for a single-mass velocity kick. The formula depends on the strictly positive damping coefficients only through which damper vectors are present. Boundary conditions, possible zero modes, and coefficient positivity are addressed. A standalone finite diagnostic program corroborates the calculation (6,478 trigonometric node checks; 238 single-damper and 390 mixed-damper state-space spectra; 79 placement-count checks; 31,950 energy-projector checks), without being used to infer the all-size theorem.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence

The closest text is Giurdanella and Sparavigna, *Self frequency-locking of a chain of oscillators* (2010), arXiv:1006.1722, whose single-site damping and frequency-locking mechanism precede this result. That special case is not novel. The complete accessible 2014 Lecomte paper, DOI 10.1098/rspa.2013.0751, treats related disturbance and transfer-pole problems but no equivalent exact arbitrary mixed ground-and-bond damping invariant, nor a localized retained-energy formula. The new claim is the integrated all-positive joint-damper spectral classification and kick-energy response. Targeted semantic-index and literature searches found no stronger directly covering statement. Historical-priority uncertainty remains for alternate controllability/observation formulations: absence from searches is not a novelty certificate.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The complete criterion answers which mixed damping configurations eliminate *all* resonant undamped vibrations, and quantifies how much an impulse at every site remains in the protected sector. In particular, two physically distinct damper types obey the same divisor law, and a bond dashpot at the free endpoint is unconditionally stabilizing. These are concrete structural design properties for finite vibration isolation, rather than routine restatements of the known frequency spectrum.

## Limits and nearest literature

The result assumes finite, homogeneous, one-fixed-one-free linear mass–spring chains with positive viscous dashpots. It does not give decay-rate optimization or cover nonuniform, nonlinear, infinite or differently constrained networks. Relevant complete texts are arXiv:1006.1722 (2010) and DOI:10.1098/rspa.2013.0751 (2014). A published broader path-controllability theorem could conceivably imply the result; independent priority verification is outstanding.
