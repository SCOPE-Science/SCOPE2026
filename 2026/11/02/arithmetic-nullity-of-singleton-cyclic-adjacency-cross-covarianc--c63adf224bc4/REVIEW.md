# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed covariance entry follows by two finite bijections: remove a singleton and merge a disjoint pair. Incident events are incompatible. This proves the full matrix for every integer \(n\ge3\) and \(1\le d<n\). The circulant Fourier diagonalization establishes every eigenvalue, singular value, and the exact arithmetic nullity; no numerical spectral approximation is used. The constant eigenvalue is nonzero and negative by the published all-order Bell-ratio bound, while strict log-convexity independently follows from Dobinski's Poisson-moment formula and strict Cauchy–Schwarz. All ranges and the special duplication at \(d=n/2\) have been included. The standalone checker performed 4358 exact assertions over all partitions through nine sites, all admissible displacements, and Bell ratios through index 99. These finite assertions are supporting evidence, not an infinite proof. An external source is used for the all-order normalized Bell-ratio bound; the exact inequality and index shift were checked against its displayed published statement.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Callan (arXiv:math/0508052) establishes a bijection equidistributing *total* singletons and cyclic nearest-neighbor adjacency counts. Munagi (doi:10.1216/RMJ-2016-46-1-301) establishes all-displacement *marginal counts* for uniform set partitions. The relevant full-text statements and proofs were inspected. Neither result gives the mixed local two-family covariance matrix, its circulant singular spectrum, the exact \(\gcd(n,d)\) rank defect, or the positive-local/negative-global contrast. Asai–Kubo–Kuo (arXiv:math/0104137) supplies a Bell-number inequality only, not an indicator covariance theorem. No direct equivalent or dominating claim was identified by searches of set-partition adjacency covariance, Fourier rank, cyclic displacement statistics, and related Bell-number results. The residual risk is an implicit consequence of broader published exchangeability or association-scheme theory and incomplete coverage of all potentially relevant literature. Search absence is not proof of first discovery; the assessment is conditional on inspected sources.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The question is motivated by a recognized combinatorial coincidence: singleton counts and each displacement-adjacency count have equal expectations, and nearest-neighbor totals are even equidistributed. The finding shows that their *spatial dependence* can be sharply different: for the same random partition and the same marginal means, displacements \(1,2,3\) at \(n=6\) produce ranks \(5,6,3\). This is a complete finite-parameter structural classification, with a concrete nullspace basis and a strict aggregate sign opposite to most local covariances. It distinguishes first-order identities from second-order geometry without introducing an artificial parameter or arbitrary slice.
