# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The proof is an exact rational-function argument. Its two involutions come respectively from the degree-two parametrization of the hyperbola and its complex-conjugate parametrization; their product is rotation by \(r^2\). Irrational rotation forces a constant quotient by finiteness of the generic fibers of a nonconstant rational function. For finite rotation order \(m\), the invariant field under the dihedral action is generated by the degree-\(m\) Dickson polynomial, proving the complete quotient characterization, sharp first ambiguous degree, and explicit equality witnesses. The finite-sample guarantee follows by clearing the Laurent powers in the squared-modulus difference. The all-parameter proof is independent of symbolic examples.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)

The original theorem for two crossing lines of irrational angle in Chalendar–Partington [1, Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.3] does not cover a single hyperbola branch, and its finite-degree failure threshold is not the claimed dihedral invariant-field classification. The two-circle and parallel-line results of the same paper have different domain geometry. Liehr [2, Theorem 1.1] covers unions of parallel lines for a function class containing exponentials; it does not imply phase rigidity on a hyperbola. Orevkov–Pakovich [3, Theorems 1.1 and 2.5] treat intersections of two rational or polynomial lemniscates; they do not state the single-prescribed-hyperbola complete rational quotient classification. These are specific scope comparisons, not an assertion that related general reflection methods are unknown.

Comparisons to results on two circles, parallel lines, noncircular ellipses and contracting spirals show different geometries and do not themselves provide the finite dihedral group generated by the hyperbola parametrization. An ellipse has a strict-contraction deck product rather than a unit-modulus rotation, so it does not imply the arithmetic exceptional-degree threshold. An equivalent invariant-field result indexed under reflection groups or real algebraic lemniscates remains a historical priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result identifies a precise and unexpected arithmetic boundary: arbitrarily close hyperbola shapes can exhibit universal polynomial phase rigidity or a finite-degree obstruction, with the exact first failing degree and an explicit classification of every possible rational phase quotient. This answers a natural question about minimal single-curve sampling geometry and yields deterministic arbitrary-location finite sampling below a sharp degree threshold. The result goes beyond a routine special case of a general circle or ellipse statement because it requires a different, finite-or-infinite dihedral invariant-field analysis.

## Limits and independent review

The theorem is exact and noiseless. No sample-size optimality or stability constant is established. An inaccessible or unindexed general theorem about invariant fields of conic reflection groups remains a residual historical priority risk.
