# Arithmetic spectral switch and corner-capture geometry on prime-sided quantum grids

## Finding
Let \(p,q\ge5\) be odd primes, and let \(a,b>0\) have rational ratio \(a/b\). Consider the single-excitation Hamiltonian on the open rectangular grid \(P_{p-1}\square P_{q-1}\),
\[
H=aA_{p-1}\otimes I_{q-1}+bI_{p-1}\otimes A_{q-1},
\]
where \(A_m\) is the unweighted path adjacency matrix. An irreversible absorber of any positive rate is placed at the corner \((1,1)\). For a maximally mixed initial excitation, its eventual absorption probability obeys the **complete rational-coupling dichotomy**
\[
P_{\mathrm{abs}}=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac12+\frac{1}{(p-1)^2},&p=q\ \text{and}\ a=b,\\[3pt]
1,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
In the exceptional square case, set \(n=p-1=2d\) and retain \(a=b\). A second positive-rate absorber at the diagonally opposite corner \((n,n)\) changes nothing; the accessible dimension remains \(2d^2+1\). Instead placing the second absorber at the adjacent corner \((n,1)\) raises that dimension to exactly \(3d^2-d+2\). Thus the three eventual capture probabilities from the uniform mixture are, respectively,
\[
\frac{2d^2+1}{4d^2},\qquad
\frac{2d^2+1}{4d^2},\qquad
\frac{3d^2-d+2}{4d^2}.
\]
These formulas hold for all indicated primes and for arbitrary strictly positive absorber rates; the two rates need not agree.

## Assumptions and scope
The vertices are ordered pairs \((x,y)\) with \(1\le x\le p-1\), \(1\le y\le q-1\). Nearest-neighbor hopping is \(a\) along the first coordinate and \(b\) along the second, with no onsite potentials or extra edges. The Hilbert space is \(\mathbb C^{(p-1)(q-1)}\). The absorber dynamics is the trace-decreasing evolution generated by
\[
H_{\mathrm{eff}}=H-\frac{i}{2}\sum_{s\in S}\kappa_s|s\rangle\langle s|,\qquad \kappa_s>0,
\]
where \(S\) contains either the single corner, both diagonally opposite corners, or the stated adjacent-corner pair. The initial density operator is \(\rho_0=I/N\), with \(N=(p-1)(q-1)\). The captured probability means \(1-\lim_{t\to\infty}\operatorname{tr}(e^{-iH_{\mathrm{eff}}t}\rho_0e^{iH_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\dagger}t})\). All assertions are exact finite-dimensional statements. In the single-corner dichotomy the condition is \(a/b\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\); no claim is made for all irrational ratios.

## Proof
**1. Reduction to the spectral cyclic subspace.** Let
\[
\mathcal K_S=\operatorname{span}\{H^k|s\rangle:s\in S,\ 0\le k<N\}.
\]
As \(H\) is Hermitian, \(\mathcal K_S\) and its orthogonal complement are \(H\)-invariant. Every absorber vector belongs to \(\mathcal K_S\); therefore the absorber term annihilates \(\mathcal K_S^\perp\), so the latter is exactly dark. To see that there are no additional nondecaying states inside \(\mathcal K_S\), suppose an eigenvector \(v\) of \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}\) restricted to \(\mathcal K_S\) had a real eigenvalue. Taking the imaginary part of its quadratic form yields \(\sum_s\kappa_s|\langle s|v\rangle|^2=0\), hence \(\langle s|v\rangle=0\) for every absorber. It follows that \(Hv\) is a scalar multiple of \(v\) and \(v\perp\mathcal K_S\), a contradiction. Thus all eigenvalues of that restriction have negative imaginary part and its evolution decays exponentially (up to polynomial Jordan factors). Consequently
\[
P_{\mathrm{abs}}(\rho_0)=\operatorname{tr}(\Pi_{\mathcal K_S}\rho_0)
=\frac{\dim\mathcal K_S}{N}.
\]
For any Hermitian \(H\), the spectral theorem also gives \(\mathcal K_S=\bigoplus_E\operatorname{span}\{\Pi_E|s\rangle:s\in S\}\), where \(\Pi_E\) projects onto the eigenspace at energy \(E\).

**2. Exact path eigenstructure.** For \(m=p-1\), the eigenvalues of \(A_m\) are \(\lambda_j^{(p)}=2\cos(\pi j/p)\), for \(1\le j<p\), with eigenvectors proportional to \(\sin(\pi jx/p)\). Every path eigenvector is nonzero at either endpoint. Hence every tensor eigenvector of \(H\) is nonzero at \((1,1)\). The cyclic rank for one corner is therefore precisely the number of distinct values of
\[
a\lambda_i^{(p)}+b\lambda_j^{(q)},\qquad 1\le i<p,\quad 1\le j<q.
\]

**3. Prime cyclotomic independence.** For any odd prime \(p\), put \(d=(p-1)/2\), \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i/p}\), and \(u_r=\zeta^r+\zeta^{-r}\) for \(1\le r\le d\). The complete one-dimensional path spectrum is exactly \(\{\pm u_r:1\le r\le d\}\). In the real cyclotomic field \(K_p=\mathbb Q(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})\), one has
\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{K_p/\mathbb Q}(u_ru_s)
=\begin{cases}p-2,&r=s,\\ -2,&r\ne s.\end{cases}
\]
Indeed \(\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb Q(\zeta)/\mathbb Q}(\zeta^j)=-1\) for \(j\not\equiv0\pmod p\), and \([\mathbb Q(\zeta):K_p]=2\). Thus the Gram matrix is \(pI_d-2\mathbf1\mathbf1^T\), with eigenvalues \(p\) of multiplicity \(d-1\) and \(p-2d=1\) of multiplicity one. It is nonsingular, proving that the \(u_r\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\).

If \(p\ge7\), no difference between distinct path eigenvalues is rational. To prove this, note that every path eigenvalue is an algebraic integer in \(K_p\), whose field trace is \(+1\) or \(-1\). If \(\lambda_i^{(p)}-\lambda_j^{(p)}\in\mathbb Q\), the difference is a rational algebraic integer, hence an integer \(c\). Taking traces gives \(dc\in\{-2,0,2\}\). Since \(d\ge3\), necessarily \(c=0\), and the distinctness of cosine eigenvalues forces \(i=j\).

For two distinct odd primes \(p,q\), the cyclotomic fields \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\) and \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)\) have intersection \(\mathbb Q\): their compositum is \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{pq})\) and has degree \((p-1)(q-1)\), the product of their individual degrees. Thus \(K_p\cap K_q=\mathbb Q\). If two weighted grid energies agree and \(a/b\) is rational, the two path-eigenvalue differences belong to this intersection and are rational. Since distinct primes \(p,q\ge5\) include at least one prime of size at least seven, the difference in that direction vanishes. Then the other difference vanishes too. Hence all \((p-1)(q-1)\) grid energies are distinct when \(p\ne q\).

**4. Equal prime sides.** When \(p=q\), write the path eigenvalues as signed basis elements \(\pm u_r\). Rational independence from Step 3 turns equality between weighted grid energies into equality between formal vectors \(a\varepsilon_i+b\varepsilon_j\in\mathbb Q^d\), where \(\varepsilon_i,\varepsilon_j\in\{\pm e_1,\ldots,\pm e_d\}\). Suppose \(a\varepsilon_i+b\varepsilon_j=a\varepsilon_k+b\varepsilon_l\) for distinct ordered index pairs. Then
\[
a(\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_k)=b(\varepsilon_l-\varepsilon_j)\ne0.
\]
Any nonzero difference of two signed coordinate vectors has either one nonzero component equal to \(\pm2\), or two nonzero components each equal to \(\pm1\). Two such differences on the same positive rational ray must be identical, so \(a=b\). Therefore, when \(a/b\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\) is unequal to one, all \(4d^2\) grid energies are distinct.

At \(a=b\), the possible coefficient vectors are the zero vector, the \(2d\) vectors \(\pm2e_r\), and the \(4\binom d2\) vectors \(\pm e_r\pm e_s\) with \(r<s\). Independence proves these energies distinct, giving exactly
\[
1+2d+4\binom d2=2d^2+1
\]
distinct energies. This proves the single-corner dichotomy.

**5. Two absorbing corners in the isotropic prime square.** For a normalized product eigenvector labeled \((i,j)\), the amplitude at \((n,n)\) is \((-1)^{i+j}\) times its amplitude at \((1,1)\), and at \((n,1)\) it is \((-1)^{i+1}\) times that amplitude. Every nonzero-energy eigenspace has either one ordered pair or exactly the swapped pair \((i,j),(j,i)\); the zero eigenspace consists of all \((i,p-i)\) and has dimension \(2d\). At the opposite diagonal corner, the sign is constant within each eigenspace, including zero since \(i+(p-i)=p\) is odd. Hence the two diagonal-corner projections are collinear in every eigenspace, so the second absorber adds no dimension.

At \((n,1)\), a nonzero-energy swapped eigenspace gains one additional direction precisely when \(i,j\) have opposite parity. There are \(d^2\) such unordered pairs, of which exactly \(d\) are the zero-energy pairs \(\{i,p-i\}\). The zero eigenspace itself contributes two independent projected vectors, rather than the single one from the first corner, so it adds exactly one direction. The total increase is \(d^2-d+1\); therefore the two-adjacent-corner rank is \(2d^2+1+d^2-d+1=3d^2-d+2\). Step 1 converts all ranks to absorption probabilities.

It tests isotropic one-, diagonal-two- and adjacent-two-corner ranks, plus rational anisotropy, for square side lengths \(4,6,10\); it tests full single-corner rank on rectangles \(4\times6\), \(4\times10\), and \(6\times10\). It also checks the trace-Gram identity for five prime sizes and the exact quadratic-field resonance below. Reproduce with `python3 CHECKER.py`; the expected output is recorded in `CHECKS.txt`. Full modular rank, coupled with the proved ambient-dimension upper bound, certifies full rational rank in the simple-spectrum cases. The general all-prime result rests on the preceding analytic argument, not on finite enumeration.

For an explicit boundary showing that rationality matters, take \(p=q=5\). The path eigenvalues \(\lambda_1=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), \(\lambda_2=(\sqrt5-1)/2\), \(\lambda_3=(1-\sqrt5)/2\) satisfy \(\lambda_1-\lambda_2=1\) and \(\lambda_1-\lambda_3=\sqrt5\). At irrational ratio \(a/b=\sqrt5\), grid energies labeled \((1,3)\) and \((2,1)\) coincide; thus there can be exceptional nonisotropic irrational resonances.

## Relationship to prior work
Novo et al. (first publicly available 2014-12-22) identify the trap-accessible Krylov subspace as the correct transport-efficiency object, but do not state this prime-sided rectangle dichotomy or the two-corner formulas. Notarstefano and Parlangeli (first publicly available 2012-03-01) investigate grid controllability using **graph Laplacians** and arithmetic conditions; their system has boundary degree terms absent from the **adjacency** Hamiltonian here. Varbanov, Krovi, and Brun (first publicly available 2008-03-24) analyze measured continuous-time hitting and dark states via a different Poisson-measurement/Laplacian model. A prior weighted Cartesian-power spectral calculation already covers the homogeneous \(4\times4\) single-corner rank \(9\), so that instance is not claimed as new. The contribution assessed here is the exact all-prime rational-anisotropy dichotomy together with geometric two-corner ranks and the irrational boundary witness.

## Limitations
The theorem concerns finite open-boundary grids, strictly positive local absorption, homogeneous hopping along each coordinate, no onsite disorder, and an initially maximally mixed single excitation. It proves eventual absorption, not finite-time speed, and it does not prove robustness as the hopping ratio approaches one. Rationality of the anisotropy is essential to the stated full-capture dichotomy; irrational resonances can occur. Extensions to non-prime side lengths, generic interior absorbers, decoherence and interacting excitations are not established. Historical novelty has not undergone independent validation.
