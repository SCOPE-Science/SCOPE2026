# Scientific review of the finite-section monomial composition theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The nonzero monomial arrows have source degrees divisible by the inner exponent and destination degrees obtained by the corresponding outer exponent. Coprimality after reducing the greatest common divisor forces the inputs of the \(j\)-th power to be exactly the degrees \(g b^j s\), and the degree cutoff retains exactly \(\lfloor N/(gM^j)\rfloor\) such paths. Acyclicity and one incoming/outgoing arrow imply that the blocks are orthogonal **unweighted** Jordan shifts, permitting unitary rather than merely algebraic classification. Rank second differences determine each block count; the two earliest nonzero-rank cutoff thresholds recover the gcd and larger exponent and prove necessity in the all-cutoff equivalence statement. Exact sine diagonalization of symmetrized shifts yields all eigenvalues. A geometric tail bound independent of cutoff gives convergence of the empirical measures and shows the limit is a probability measure; the longest block gives the numerical-radius edge. No limiting assertion is inferred solely from tests.

The standalone standard-library checker passed 61661 exact integer/rational assertions covering both composition directions, noncoprime pairs, unitary equivalence fingerprints, and the finite path/trace identities. The proof remains the definitive source for all \(N\) and weak convergence.

## Originality — PASS, best-knowledge
Clifford, Dabkowski and Wiggins (2021), Section 2.3, provide the basis action and their Theorem 6 gives global point-spectrum information for the same products; both are credited. A prior qualitative arithmetic Jordan-chain decomposition likewise does not state the exact **degree-cutoff** ranks, all-cutoff unitary fingerprint, and complete limiting Hermitian measure. Leng and Zhao (2026), arXiv:2610.15472, address Schatten membership rather than the finite-section problem. Classical numerical-radius formulas for individual Jordan blocks are specifically excluded from the novelty assertion. Targeted searches for aliases, all-cutoff conjugacy, Chebyshev finite-section spectra and broader weighted-composition results identified no equivalent or dominating statement.

Residual risk: general results on finite compressions of partial permutations may subsume the arithmetic formulas, despite the inspected primary sources not presenting them. This is an explicit priority limitation, not a certificate of novelty. The 1992 nilpotent numerical-radius paper was checked bibliographically rather than read in full, and is only invoked for a classical ingredient reproduced directly in the proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The polynomial-degree cutoff, rather than a tailored chain truncation, is the canonical computational approximation of Hardy-space operators. The exact rank sequence gives an algorithm-free natural classification for every cutoff and distinguishes which parameters a finite compression can recover. The dense-support yet purely atomic limiting Hermitian eigenvalue distribution is structurally different from a continuous spectral law; its explicit weights and independence of the smaller coprime exponent are natural exact invariants. This is more than a restatement of elementary Jordan-block eigenvalues, which by themselves do not determine their multiplicities in these arithmetic finite sections.

## Boundaries and sources
The theorem applies only to unweighted Hardy \(H^2_0\) and two unequal integer power maps; it makes no statement about all analytic symbols, other cutoffs, strong convergence of eigenvalue lists for the infinite operator, or standalone novelty of the classical cosine formula.
