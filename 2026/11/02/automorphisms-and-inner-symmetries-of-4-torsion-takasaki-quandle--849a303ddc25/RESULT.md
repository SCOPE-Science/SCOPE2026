# Automorphisms and inner symmetries of \(4\)-torsion Takasaki quandles

## Finding

Let \(d\ge 1\), let \(V=\mathbb F_2^d\), and let \(Q_d\) be the Takasaki (core) quandle on \(A=(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^d\), with operation \(x*y=2y-x\). Then every quandle automorphism has one and only one expression
\[
F_{L,v,t}(a,b)=(La+v,Lb+t_a),
\qquad L\in\operatorname{GL}_d(2),\ v\in V,\ t\in V^V,
\]
where \((a,b)\in V\times V\) denotes the residue-class coordinate representation \(a+2b\in A\), and \(V^V\) denotes all set maps from \(V\) to \(V\), under pointwise addition. Consequently,
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(Q_d)\cong V^V\rtimes\operatorname{AGL}_d(2),
\qquad
|\operatorname{Aut}(Q_d)|=2^{d2^d+d}\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(2^d-2^j).
\]
For the displayed semidirect product, the affine group element \((L,v)\) acts on a fiber-translation function \(t\) by \(((L,v)\cdot t)_a=L t_{L^{-1}(a-v)}\).

Moreover, its inner automorphism and displacement groups satisfy
\[
\operatorname{Inn}(Q_d)\cong (C_2)^{d+1},\qquad
\operatorname{Dis}(Q_d)\cong(C_2)^d.
\]
The ordinary affine automorphism subgroup induced by \(A\rtimes\operatorname{Aut}(A)\) has index \(2^{d(2^d-d-1)}\). Hence for \(d\ge2\) the full group contains quandle automorphisms that are not affine transformations of the underlying abelian group.

In particular \(|\operatorname{Aut}(Q_1)|=8\), \(|\operatorname{Aut}(Q_2)|=6144\), and \(|\operatorname{Aut}(Q_3)|=22{,}548{,}578{,}304\). For \(d=2\), \(|\operatorname{Inn}(Q_2)|=8\). This last value contradicts the order \(16\) asserted for this case by Corollary 5.5 of Bardakov, Nasybullov, and Singh (arXiv:1705.10607v3); the general formula there is \((C_2)^{2^d}\), whereas the correct rank is \(d+1\).

## Assumptions and scope

The integer \(d\) is arbitrary with \(d\ge1\). Quandle automorphisms are bijections preserving the indicated binary operation; no linearity over any field is assumed. The quandle is a finite, generally disconnected, involutory medial quandle of order \(4^d\). By \(C_2\) we mean the cyclic group of order two; by \(\operatorname{Dis}(Q_d)\) we mean the group generated by products of two right translations.

The two coordinates \(a,b\in V\) are bit vectors with entries \(0,1\), identified with the unique residue representation \(a+2b\) modulo four. They are not treated as a direct-sum decomposition of abelian groups: carrying under ordinary addition modulo four matters, but the quandle operation admits the simple expression proved below.

## Proof

**Step 1: operation and characteristic fibers.** Directly in each residue coordinate modulo four, \(-a\equiv a+2a\pmod4\) and \(2(c+2e)\equiv2c\pmod4\). Therefore
\[
(a,b)*(c,e)=(a,b+a+c),
\]
where the additions on the right take place in \(V\). Thus the right translation indexed by \((c,e)\) depends only on \(c\) and acts by
\[
R_c(a,b)=(a,b+a+c).
\]
For each fixed \(a\), choosing \(c\) freely makes \(R_c\) act transitively on the fiber \(Q_a=\{(a,b):b\in V\}\); none of the right translations changes \(a\). Consequently these fibers are exactly the orbits of the intrinsically defined group of inner automorphisms. Every quandle automorphism must therefore induce some permutation \(\pi\) of \(V\), and can be written \(F(a,b)=(\pi(a),f_a(b))\) for bijections \(f_a:V\to V\).

**Step 2: the induced permutation is affine.** Preservation of \(*\) is equivalent, for all \(a,b,c,e\in V\), to
\[
f_a(b+a+c)=f_a(b)+\pi(a)+\pi(c).
\]
Let \(t_a=f_a(0)\). Setting \(b=0\) and replacing \(c\) by \(a+u\) gives
\[
f_a(u)=t_a+\pi(a)+\pi(a+u).
\]
Comparing the original identity at increments \(u,w\) shows that \(u\mapsto\pi(a)+\pi(a+u)\) is additive. In particular, at \(a=0\), the map \(L(u)=\pi(u)+\pi(0)\) is additive. A bijective additive map on \(V\) is an element of \(\operatorname{GL}_d(2)\). With \(v=\pi(0)\) one has \(\pi(a)=La+v\) for every \(a\), and the displayed expression becomes \(f_a(b)=Lb+t_a\), where \(t_a\) is otherwise unrestricted.

**Step 3: sufficiency, uniqueness, and group structure.** For any such \(L,v,t\), the map \(F_{L,v,t}\) is a bijection because \(L\) and the induced permutation \(a\mapsto La+v\) are bijective. Substituting in \((a,b)*(c,e)=(a,b+a+c)\) gives
\[
F_{L,v,t}((a,b)*(c,e))=(La+v,Lb+La+Lc+t_a)
=F_{L,v,t}(a,b)*F_{L,v,t}(c,e),
\]
so every displayed map is an automorphism. Its induced action on the intrinsic fibers uniquely recovers \(L,v\), and evaluation at \((a,0)\) uniquely recovers \(t_a\). The kernel of the action on the fibers is the group of arbitrary translations \(\tau_t(a,b)=(a,b+t_a)\), canonically \(V^V\). The homomorphic section \((L,v)\mapsto((a,b)\mapsto(La+v,Lb))\) identifies the quotient with \(\operatorname{AGL}_d(2)\), yielding the stated split extension. Conjugation by the section gives \(((L,v)\cdot t)_a=L t_{L^{-1}(a-v)}\).

There are \(|V^V|=2^{d2^d}\) fiber translations, \(2^d\) choices of \(v\), and \(\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(2^d-2^j)\) invertible linear maps \(L\). Their product is the claimed automorphism-group order.

**Step 4: exact inner and displacement groups.** Each \(R_c\) is the fiber translation associated with the function \(r_c(a)=a+c\). Since fiber translations commute and have order at most two, \(\operatorname{Inn}(Q_d)\) is the \(\mathbb F_2\)-span of these functions. The differences \(r_c+r_0\) are all constant functions with values \(c\), a subspace of dimension \(d\). The function \(r_0(a)=a\) is not constant for \(d\ge1\), so adjoining it yields dimension \(d+1\) and no further independent generators. Thus \(\operatorname{Inn}(Q_d)\cong(C_2)^{d+1}\). The products \(R_cR_0\) are precisely translations by the constant functions \(c\), proving \(\operatorname{Dis}(Q_d)\cong(C_2)^d\). In particular, the independence of all \(2^d\) distinct right translations is false when \(d\ge2\).

**Step 5: compare affine symmetries.** Every translation of \(A\), and every element of \(\operatorname{GL}_d(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)\), preserves \(x*y=2y-x\), and these form an embedded affine subgroup \(A\rtimes\operatorname{GL}_d(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)\). Reduction modulo two has kernel of size \(2^{d^2}\) in \(\operatorname{GL}_d(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)\), since every \(I+2M\) is invertible and every invertible matrix modulo two has a lift. Hence the affine subgroup has order \(2^{2d+d^2}|\operatorname{GL}_d(2)|\). Dividing into the exact full group order gives the claimed index \(2^{d(2^d-d-1)}\). This is greater than one exactly when \(d\ge2\). \(\square\)

## Verification

Run the accompanying standalone Python 3 script with `python3 check.py`. It constructs the quandle operations independently in binary coordinates and checks their homomorphism equations for every candidate induced fiber permutation and fiber-origin image for \(d=1,2\), rather than merely multiplying closed-form orders. For \(d=2\) it examines every one of the \(4!\cdot4^4=6144\) such image assignments, and confirms all \(6144\) are quandle automorphisms, with precisely \(24\) permitted fiber permutations. In dimension three it checks all \(8!\) possible fiber permutations for the affine condition and samples genuine preservation equations; it also computes the independent right-translation rank for \(1\le d\le4\). It prints `VERIFY_OK` on successful completion. The computer checks are finite corroboration, not the proof of the all-\(d\) statements.

## Relationship to prior work

Bardakov and Sangare, *Constructions and automorphisms of quandles* (arXiv:2610.18676; first publicly submitted 2026-10-13), prove an automorphism-group formula for Latin Alexander quandles and explicitly ask in Question 3.5 about products of even dihedral quandles. The case \(Q_d=R_4^d\) is a uniform, non-Latin subfamily of that question, with a new fiber-translation phenomenon beyond the Latin affine formula.

Bardakov, Nasybullov, and Singh, *Automorphism groups of quandles and related groups* (arXiv:1705.10607v3, §5), establish a general core-quandle affine subgroup and treat odd torsion, but their Corollary 5.5 states \(\operatorname{Inn}(T((\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^d))\cong(C_2)^{2^d}\). Directly calculating the dependence among the right translations gives \((C_2)^{d+1}\) instead; their expression agrees only for \(d=1\). The exact full automorphism group calculated here also describes the nonaffine symmetries that the ordinary group-holomorph subgroup misses.

Hou, *Automorphism groups of Alexander quandles* (Journal of Algebra, 2011), states in its abstract that the automorphism group of general Alexander quandles is determined. Its primary full text was inaccessible; it could specialize to parts of the formula above, so the claim of historical novelty is qualified. The 2026 paper cites Hou while still posing the even-product question.

Jedlička, Pilitowska, Stanovský, and Zamojska-Dzienio, *The structure of medial quandles* (arXiv:1409.8396), provide a broad affine-mesh representation and isomorphism theory for medial quandles; the present calculation is a concrete evaluated family and is compatible with that general framework. Salix and Wood, *More Automorphism Groups of Quandles* (arXiv:2510.26673), give exact small-quandle enumerations through order ten, while \(|Q_d|=4^d\) exceeds ten as soon as \(d\ge2\).

## Limitations

The theorem applies only to \((\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^d\), not to all even-modulus dihedral products or general finite abelian \(2\)-groups. It concerns ordinary groups of automorphisms of a quandle, not automorphisms of its linearized quandle algebra. The distinct evaluation of this family may be derivable from Hou’s general Alexander-quandle theorem or from medial-quandle affine-mesh methods; historical priority beyond the examined sources is not proved. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
