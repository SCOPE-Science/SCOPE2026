# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof fixes the unnormalized two-tap window and Euclidean squared-analysis normalization, including the antipodal exclusion. A local rank-two calculation gives an exact weighted-path decomposition of every surviving frame operator. Positivity, explicit hyperbolic lowest-eigenvector and Perron–Frobenius comparison establish strictly increasing lower and upper path eigenvalues for all lengths. Integer gap averaging proves the sharp condition-number bound, rigidity and exact labeled optimizer census. A supplementary checker rebuilt the original complex frame matrices and performed 4,126 numerical spectral comparisons in 190 parameter families, with no mismatches. The checker cannot establish the all-size statements by itself; the analytic argument does.

## Originality — PASS relative to examined sources
The closest motivating preprint (Asipchuk–De Carli–Rodriguez, arXiv:2605.16139) establishes finite Gabor block equivalences, not optimal erasure-spacing and associated exact path eigenvalue bounds. Salanevich–Strachan (arXiv:2509.01325) establish broad erasure-robustness results for other Gabor constructions rather than the specified sparse, paired-erasure deterministic design. The complete accessible mathematical statements of the two closest published findings concern, respectively, two-dimensional harmonic frames with separately deleted atoms and a nonlinear two-tap spectrogram Jacobian; neither implies this result. Semantic searches also considered aliases involving cyclic graph paths, sparse Gabor windows, consecutive erasures, spectral bounds, and balanced gaps. Residual risk: an unindexed earlier graph or Gabor-design theorem could subsume the specialized conclusion.

## Value — PASS
The result answers a natural sensor-loss design problem that cannot be inferred from merely knowing whether the sparse system spans. It determines exact finite-dimensional sharp stability constants for every admissible dimension, modulation separation and loss count, proves the full rigidity of optimal erasure arrangements, and counts every labeled optimum. It converts a general spectral design task into explicit one-variable hyperbolic equations and path characteristic polynomials. This is a complete, reproducible classification for a motivated sparse Gabor family, not an arbitrary small instance.

## Limits
Two-tap equal coefficients and exactly two fixed modulations are essential; the result does not optimize over different windows or arbitrary lost individual coefficients. No independent external mathematical audit was performed.
