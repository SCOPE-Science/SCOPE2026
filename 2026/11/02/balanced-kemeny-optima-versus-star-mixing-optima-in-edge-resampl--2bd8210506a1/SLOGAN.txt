For independently edge-resampled random walks on \(K_{a,b}\), balancing the parts uniquely minimizes the stationary-target mean hitting time, while a star uniquely minimizes the absolute spectral mixing radius at availability \(p=1/2\) for every order at least five. On even orders, the balanced chain consequently mixes in exponential time, whereas the star mixes in at most three steps, despite the balanced chain having asymptotically half its Kemeny constant.
