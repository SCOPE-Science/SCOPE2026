For two average-sink habitats exposed to equally long, opposite square-wave seasons, the principal Floquet growth rate strictly increases with the product \(ab\) of the two directed migration rates when their sum \(a+b\) is fixed. Balanced bidirectional migration uniquely maximizes growth, one-way migration gives exactly the isolated-patch growth rate, and a single sharp product cutoff separates decay from growth whenever persistence is attainable.
