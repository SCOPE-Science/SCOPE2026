# Review of the cycle minimal-Roman census

## Correctness — PASS
The definition of pointwise minimality is checked against immediate decreases, using monotonicity of the Roman condition. The unique labeling induced by any set of vertices carrying label \(2\) has an elementary necessary-and-sufficient private-neighbor criterion. On cycles, every selected vertex must touch a gap containing at least two unselected vertices. Thus cyclic gap words contain no two consecutive short gaps. The weight of a gap is exact, including length-zero and length-one gaps. The weighted closed-walk identity and the factor \(n/t\) for rooted gap descriptions yield a formal determinant calculation, from which the stated two-variable coefficient formula and the all-size recurrence follow. The positive dominant pole and its residue give the exact leading asymptotic constant. Direct exhaustive definition checks for \(3\le n\le12\) and independent gap checks through \(n=17\) agreed with every bivariate coefficient. The verification is finite support for, not a substitute for, the proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The relevant general bijection between pointwise-minimal Roman functions and their sets of label-\(2\) vertices was already proved by Abu-Khzam, Fernau, and Mann (arXiv:2204.04765); their Proposition 10 fully counts \(C_5\), and is expressly credited. The same authors' *Roman Census* (arXiv:2208.05261) counts minimal Roman functions on paths but does not enumerate the cycle family by weight or the number of label-\(2\) vertices. A historical Fielder sequence has exactly the same univariate numerical recurrence; no novelty is asserted for the abstract univariate sequence. The assessed new result is the labeled cycle interpretation, exact bivariate polynomial for every \(n\ge3\), and its gap classification. Targeted searches of exact terminology, equivalent private-neighbor/strong-irredundance language, and mathematical records identified no prior matching bivariate cycle theorem. Publication priority is not certain.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Counting all pointwise-minimal Roman functions was the explicit motivation of the 2022 enumeration work, which supplied a cycle of order five as a sharp general-example construction but developed exact recurrences only for paths. The five-term polynomial recurrence gives a compact, all-order answer for the neighboring natural family of cycles, including the full weight and defender statistics rather than a single extremal number. The cyclic gap criterion is a usable structural characterization, and the leading asymptotic constant is exact. These supply a natural complete classification, not an arbitrary finite table or mere recoding of an already tabulated number.

## Closest literature and limits
Abu-Khzam, Fernau, and Mann (arXiv:2204.04765; first public 2022-04-10), Sections 3 and 5, prove the general structural characterization and the \(C_5\) count. Their *Roman Census* (arXiv:2208.05261; first public 2022-08-10), Sections 4 and 9, examines paths and explicitly points to other simple graph classes. Plouffe (1992, table A1639) records the same univariate Fielder sequence, but not the bivariate Roman interpretation. Residual risks include an unindexed equivalent enumeration and priority of the unweighted sequence. No independent mathematical assessment has been undertaken.
