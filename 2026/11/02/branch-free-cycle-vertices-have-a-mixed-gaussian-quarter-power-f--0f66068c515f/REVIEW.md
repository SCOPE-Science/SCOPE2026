# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** A prescribed-root forest deletion count gives an explicit joint-avoidance formula, which agrees with a mandatory-root occupancy experiment by inclusion-exclusion. The binomial approximation follows from the binomial-to-Poisson count comparison and its multinomial conditional kernel. Classical triangular-array binomial CLT and the Rayleigh cyclic-point law yield the claimed joint weak limit. Exact finite mapping enumeration and factorial moment checks passed 1,682 assertions; this corroboration is not an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The 2010 conditioned-root forest model, the 1982 origin/indegree tables and the 2023 deepest-cycle theorem were compared to the exact branch-free cyclic-root object. Neither inspected account states the compulsory-root binomial approximation or the two-scale Rayleigh-mixed fluctuation law. The earlier noncyclic-source occupancy formula is a different statistic and does not itself establish this root-mandatory law or its quarter-power fluctuations. An equivalent result in older enumerative literature remains a possibility; database searches alone do not establish priority.

**Value — PASS.** The result measures how often a cyclic root has no tree attached in a typical functional graph. The cyclic-root population has square-root size and fluctuates on a Rayleigh scale, whereas branch-free status produces an additional square-root-of-root-count Gaussian scale. Distinguishing these two scales and proving a finite total-variation approximation clarifies random-mapping fringe geometry and supplies a practical conditional approximation.

**Limitations.** Assumes uniform self-maps; a separate deterministic rule applies when all vertices are cyclic. No quantitative convergence rate for the mixed limit is claimed. The finding has not undergone independent mathematical audit.
