# Cartesian tensorization of nonlinear graph-heat variation contraction

## Finding
Let \(G_1,\ldots,G_r\) be finite, undirected graphs, with at least two vertices each, positive symmetric edge conductances \(c_{j,e}\), and unit vertex masses. Fix \(p>1\). Denote by \(S^j_t\) the nonlinear \(p\)-heat flow on \(G_j\), and by \(S_t\) the nonlinear \(p\)-heat flow on their Cartesian product, with the same factor conductances repeated identically on every coordinate fiber. Let \(J_H(v)=\sum_{ab\in E(H)}|v_a-v_b|\), with each undirected edge counted once, and let the product variation \(J_G\) use its Cartesian edges similarly.

**Tensorization and reflection theorem.** For every \(r\ge1\), \(J_G(S_tu)\le J_G(u)\) for all real data \(u\) and all \(t\ge0\) if and only if, for every factor \(j\), \(J_{G_j}(S^j_tv)\le J_{G_j}(v)\) for all real \(v\) and \(t\ge0\). The conclusion remains true with any strictly positive coefficient multiplying the variation of each coordinate direction and any strictly positive coefficient multiplying that coordinate's diffusion generator. Different exponents \(p_j>1\) may be used for different factors.

**Explicit family and sharp branching boundary.** Paths and cycles (with arbitrary positive edge conductances), complete graphs (with constant conductance within each complete-graph factor), and stars \(K_{1,m}\) with \(1\le m\le3\) (with arbitrary positive conductances) all have the factor property, for every \(p>1\). Consequently all Cartesian products of these factors are variation-contracting. For Cartesian products consisting entirely of stars \(K_{1,m_j}\), with arbitrary positive conductances, variation contracts for all signals and times if and only if \(m_j\le3\) for every factor. If some \(m_j\ge4\), a lifted single-leaf impulse has strictly positive initial variation derivative \((m_j-3)c_{j,e}\prod_{k\ne j}|V(G_k)|\) in the unweighted product variation, where \(e\) is the excited leaf edge.

## Assumptions and scope
On a graph \(H\), the real vector field is \(F_H(v)_a=\sum_{b\sim a}c_{ab}\phi_p(v_b-v_a)\), where \(\phi_p(s)=|s|^{p-2}s\) for nonzero \(s\), and \(\phi_p(0)=0\). The equation is \(\dot v=F_H(v)\). All vertices have mass one; the weights in \(J_H\) are one, apart from the optional whole-coordinate factors. For each coordinate \(j\), the product generator \(F^{[j]}\) acts only along \(G_j\)-edges, with its coefficients repeated unchanged over every other coordinate choice. Thus \(F_G=\sum_jF^{[j]}\). Every factor is finite; the theorem allows arbitrary factor sizes and any number of factors. No assumption that nonlinear coordinate flows commute is made.

## Proof
**1. Existence and uniqueness.** The vector fields are continuous. Each is minus the gradient of the convex function \(\sum_{ab} c_{ab}|v_a-v_b|^p/p\); hence for two trajectories the squared Euclidean distance has nonpositive derivative, by monotonicity of \(\phi_p\). Standard finite-dimensional existence and this one-sided contraction give unique global solutions. Their sup norms do not increase by the maximum principle.

**2. The cross-fiber inequality.** For any finite graph \(H\), its \(p\)-heat flow is two-input nonexpansive in unweighted vertex \(\ell^1\). In fact, for real arrays \(v,w\) and any choice \(s_a\in\partial|v_a-w_a|\), the edge contribution to \(\sum_a s_a(F_H(v)_a-F_H(w)_a)\) is
\[
-c_{ab}(s_a-s_b)\{\phi_p(v_a-v_b)-\phi_p(w_a-w_b)\}\le0.
\]
Indeed \(a\mapsto\partial|a|\) and \(\phi_p\) are monotone, while the difference of the two arguments of \(\phi_p\) is \((v_a-w_a)-(v_b-w_b)\). This holds for every subgradient selection, including zero differences, and proves the instantaneous upper right \(\ell^1\)-dissipativity inequality.

For distinct coordinates \(i\ne j\), pair the two \(j\)-fibers lying above each \(i\)-edge and each fixed choice of the remaining coordinates. Their \(j\)-velocities are generated by the identical factor field \(F_{G_j}\). The displayed two-input inequality, summed over those pairs, implies
\[
J_i'(u;F^{[j]}(u))\le0\quad(i\ne j),
\]
where \(J_i\) is the sum of absolute differences along direction \(i\), and the prime denotes the convex one-sided directional derivative.

**3. Own-fiber inequality and tensorization.** By the hypothesis that \(G_j\) is variation-contracting, the right directional derivative of its variation along its heat vector field is nonpositive at every factor signal. Applying that fact separately to every \(j\)-fiber yields \(J_j'(u;F^{[j]}(u))\le0\). Convex directional derivatives are subadditive in their direction argument, so
\[
J_G'(u;F_G(u))=J_G'(u;\sum_jF^{[j]}(u))
\le\sum_i\sum_jJ_i'(u;F^{[j]}(u))\le0.
\]
This proves global monotonicity along the unique solution. Positive coordinate multipliers do not affect any sign. For the converse, lift any factor datum \(v(x_j)\) to \(u(x_1,\ldots,x_r)=v(x_j)\). Every other directional field vanishes identically, and the lifted flow agrees with the factor flow. Moreover \(J_G(u)=\prod_{k\ne j}|V(G_k)|J_{G_j}(v)\), times the appropriate positive variation multiplier. Contractivity of the product therefore forces contractivity of each factor. The argument is unchanged when factors use different exponents.

**4. The factor classes.** For paths orient the edges consecutively and write \(g_i=v_{i+1}-v_i\), \(h_i=c_i\phi_p(g_i)\), setting \(h_0=h_n=0\). Then \(\dot g_i=h_{i-1}-2h_i+h_{i+1}\). With any subgradient \(s_i\in\partial|g_i|\), the coefficient of each nonzero \(h_i\) in the variation derivative is \(s_{i-1}+s_{i+1}-2s_i\), which has sign opposite to \(h_i\) or is zero; the same periodic computation holds on a cycle. Thus their edge variation is nonincreasing, including at ties.

For a complete graph with common conductance \(c>0\), order the vertex values \(v_{(1)}\le\cdots\le v_{(q)}\). Equal values have equal velocities and do not split, so the ordered representation of variation gives
\[
\frac{d}{dt}J(v)=-2c\sum_{1\le a<b\le q}(b-a)\phi_p(v_{(b)}-v_{(a)})\le0.
\]
For a star with \(m\) leaves, let \(g_i=v_i-v_0\) and \(h_i=c_i\phi_p(g_i)\). The gradient system is \(\dot g_i=-h_i-\sum_kh_k\). For any \(s_i\in\partial|g_i|\), the coefficient of nonzero \(h_j\) in its variation derivative is \(-(s_j+\sum_i s_i)\). Since \(s_j(s_j+\sum_i s_i)=2+\sum_{i\ne j}s_js_i\ge3-m\), the variation is nonincreasing for \(m\le3\). Conversely, for \(m\ge4\), take center value zero, one leaf value one, all other leaf values zero. Its initial right variation derivative is \((m-3)c_j>0\). Lifting this witness into any Cartesian product yields exactly the stated multiplied derivative. This establishes the sharp star threshold.

## Verification
The standalone \(\texttt{verify.py}\) uses integer-exact derivatives for \(p=2,3,4\) and enumerates all \(3^9\) three-valued signals on \(P_3\square K_3\), and all \(3^8\) signals on each of \(C_4\square P_2\) and \(K_{1,3}\square P_2\). It checks that the upper directional variation derivative from each individual coordinate field is nonpositive for every edge direction, for a total of 393,660 exact comparisons. It also checks the explicit strictly positive derivative 2 for a lifted one-leaf impulse on \(K_{1,4}\square K_2\). These finite checks are supporting diagnostics, not substitutes for the universal proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Mugnolo's 2012 preprint (arXiv:1207.1996, first public 2012-07-09) establishes general well-posedness, positivity, order preservation, and vertex-space \(\ell^q\)-contractivity of discrete nonlinear graph heat. Hua--Mugnolo (arXiv:1410.1584) studies its generator, temporal regularity, and long-time behavior. Neither of the inspected relevant theorem statements gives the product-graph **edge-variation** equivalence above. The present implication uses vertex \(\ell^1\)-contraction only across distinct coordinate fibers; it still requires and reflects the stronger one-factor edge-variation hypothesis. The generator is genuinely nonlinear for \(p\ne2\), so the assertion is not obtained by identifying the product flow with a tensor product of linear heat semigroups.

## Limitations
All graphs are finite, with unit vertex masses and factor-repeated conductances. The result concerns one-input decay of **unweighted within-factor** edge variation and its coordinate-rescaled sum, not independently weighted edges in distinct fibers, two-input edge-variation Lipschitz constants, non-Cartesian graph products, or general infinite graphs. Potential implicit equivalents in abstract nonlinear tensorization literature cannot be entirely excluded. The finite checker does not prove infinite-parameter statements.
