# Cartier and Frobenius strata of smooth plane-filling quintics over \(\mathbf F_2\)

## Finding

Let \(C_F\subset\mathbf P^2_{\mathbf F_2}\) be the zero locus of a homogeneous quintic \(F\) that contains all seven points of \(\mathbf P^2(\mathbf F_2)\) and is smooth over an algebraic closure. Let \(f_2(C_F)\) be the \(2\)-rank of its Jacobian and \(a(C_F)\) its \(a\)-number. **The joint spectrum is exactly**

| \(f_2(C_F)\) | \(a(C_F)\) | Equations in fixed projective coordinates | Projective orbits |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 3 | 168 | 1 |
| 2 | 1 | 252 | 2 |
| 5 | 1 | 336 | 2 |
| 6 | 0 | 756 | 5 |
| **Total** | | **1512** | **10** |

In particular, the only possible \(2\)-ranks are \(2,5,6\): no such smooth genus-six curve has \(2\)-rank \(0,1,3,4\). The maximum \(a\)-number is \(3\), attained by exactly one plane-projective orbit of size \(168\). These are counts of equations and plane-projective equivalence classes, **not** a claim about the number of distinct abstract curve isomorphism classes.

## Assumptions and scope

Work over the prime field \(k=\mathbf F_2\), and order the \(21\) quintic monomials as \(X^{5-a-b}Y^aZ^b\), with exponent pairs ordered as follows: for \(i=5,4,\ldots,0\), list \(X^iY^jZ^{5-i-j}\) in decreasing \(j\). More precisely, the executable coefficient convention is

\[
F_m(X,Y,Z)=\sum_{i=0}^{5}\sum_{j=0}^{5-i} \epsilon_{\nu(i,j)} X^iY^jZ^{5-i-j},
\quad\nu(i,j)=\sum_{h=i+1}^{5}(6-h)+(5-i-j),
\quad m=\sum_{r=0}^{20}\epsilon_r 2^r.
\]

This is equivalent to the simpler programmatic list `[(a,b,5-a-b) for a in range(5,-1,-1) for b in range(5-a,-1,-1)]`. The decimal mask is an identifier for an equation, not a degree or exponent. All smooth plane quintics have geometric genus \((5-1)(5-2)/2=6\). For this family, requiring the seven rational points means precisely that the coefficient vector lies in the kernel of the seven-point evaluation matrix.

## Proof

**1. Exhaustion and geometric smoothness.** The \(21\)-dimensional space of homogeneous degree-five forms has a seven-point evaluation map of rank \(7\). Its kernel therefore has dimension \(14\), hence exactly \(2^{14}\) plane-filling sections including zero. The zero polynomial does not define a smooth curve. For a form \(F\), its geometric singular locus is empty exactly when the ideal generated by the dehomogenized equation and its two partial derivatives is the unit ideal over \(\mathbf F_2\) on each of the three coordinate affine charts. This criterion follows from the algebraic-closure Nullstellensatz and checks geometric, not merely rational, smoothness.

A complete, deterministic enumeration is implemented in `verify_census.py`. It enumerates the \(2^{14}\) sections via an explicit binary nullspace; filters out those singular at any of the seven rational points; generates all orbits from the coordinate swaps and elementary transvection generating \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbf F_2)\), which has order \(168\); and performs exact Gröbner unit-ideal tests over \(\mathbf F_2\) for one representative of every rational-point-smooth orbit. There are exactly \(2187\) sections smooth at rational points, falling into \(18\) orbits. Exactly the ten representatives in the table are geometrically smooth. Their orbit sizes sum to \(1512\). Thus the list is exhaustive. Smoothness also implies geometric irreducibility of these plane quintics: two positive-degree plane components would intersect by Bézout, contradicting smoothness.

**2. Cartier matrix.** Dehomogenize on \(Z=1\), put \(f(x,y)=F(x,y,1)\), and index the six regular differentials by \((i,j)\) with \(i,j\ge0\) and \(i+j\le2\), represented by \(x^iy^j\,dx/f_y\). In characteristic two, the Cartier coefficient rule for a plane quintic makes its matrix, with source index in the row and target index in the column,

\[
H_{(i,j),(k,l)}=[x^{2k+1-i}y^{2l+1-j}]\,f(x,y),
\]

where a coefficient with a negative exponent is zero. This follows by extracting monomials of odd exponent in each variable from \(x^iy^j f(x,y)\). The entries lie in \(\mathbf F_2\), so the Frobenius twist of \(H\) equals \(H\). Consequently

\[
a(C_F)=6-\operatorname{rank}(H),\qquad
f_2(C_F)=\operatorname{rank}(H^6).
\]

Substituting the ten masks and using exact binary row reduction yields, in table order, the pairs

\[
(2,3),(6,0),(6,0),(6,0),(6,0),
(5,1),(2,1),(6,0),(2,1),(5,1).
\]

The orbit sizes then give the four frequencies in the finding. The computation includes no approximations and is replayable from the exact equation coefficients.

**3. Independent Frobenius check.** The separate program `check_points.py` enumerates every projective point over \(\mathbf F_{2^n}\) for \(1\le n\le6\) using explicitly defined irreducible binary polynomials. For each representative it recovers the initial Hasse–Weil numerator coefficients by the integral recurrence

\[
 c_0=1,\qquad
 kc_k=-\sum_{i=1}^k c_{k-i}(2^i+1-\#C_F(\mathbf F_{2^i})).
\]

In genus six, the functional equation implies the coefficients after degree six are even, so \(f_2(C_F)\) equals the degree of this numerator modulo \(2\). All ten independently recovered \(2\)-ranks agree with the Cartier computation. The \(a\)-numbers require the separate Cartier calculation; they are not inferred from point counts.

## Verification

Execute `python verify_census.py` (requires SymPy) and `python check_points.py` (Python standard library only) in a directory containing both files. The programs run from their embedded coefficient tables without external data files or network access. The first asserts the complete exact orbit and Cartier-rank table and prints `CENSUS_CARTIER_VERIFY_OK`; the second independently verifies all ten \(2\)-ranks via extension-field point counts and prints `POINT_COUNT_ZETA_VERIFY_OK`. Both checks were executed for this finding. These finite enumerations prove the claimed exhaustive finite-field classification because the section space and group are finite and completely traversed; they do not assert a theorem about all fields.

## Relationship to prior work

Asgarli and Ghioca, *Plane-filling curves of small degree over finite fields* (first public version July 6, 2023, arXiv:2307.03072), investigate degree \(q+3\) plane-filling curves and give a family-level rational-smoothness test and a no-quadratic-singularity statement. Their main questions concern existence and smoothness of such curves. The complete geometry of smooth plane-filling binary quintics and their projective orbits is not claimed here as an independent new contribution; it is used as the finite base for the **additional Jacobian \(2\)-rank and \(a\)-number stratification**. Their inspected paper does not contain this Hasse–Witt or Cartier spectrum. General Cartier theory supplies the matrix method but does not imply the numerical spectrum without evaluation on the entire constrained finite family.

## Limitations

The statement is restricted to the prime field \(\mathbf F_2\), smooth degree-five plane curves containing all seven rational plane points, and counts of equations in fixed coordinates and their projective orbits. No claim is made for singular curves, for other degrees or field sizes, for abstract-isomorphism class counts, for complete Newton polygons, or for independent verification by external experts. Earlier unpublished computations or unindexed arithmetic databases could conceivably contain the same numerical rank data.
