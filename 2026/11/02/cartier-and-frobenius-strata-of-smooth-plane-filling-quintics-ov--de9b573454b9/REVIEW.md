# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The \(14\)-dimensional binary linear subspace is exhausted. Its \(18\) rational-point-smooth projective orbits are all subjected to exact geometric-smoothness tests, leaving ten orbits. The Cartier coefficient formula for smooth plane quintics determines their \(a\)-numbers and the stable Frobenius ranks. Independent exact extension-field point counts and zeta-numerator reconstruction match all ten \(2\)-ranks. The principal limit is that independently computed point counts do not independently determine the \(a\)-number.

## Originality

PASS, with residual risk. The motivating 2023 article by Asgarli and Ghioca addresses degree-\(q+3\) plane-filling smoothness and does not supply the complete \(2\)-rank/\(a\)-number distribution. Earlier projective-orbit enumerations already account for the smooth binary quintics; the ten classes and their sizes are not claimed as a new classification. The assessed new statement is exclusively the additional Jacobian arithmetic spectrum. Prior work on the same equations could contain unindexed Cartier calculations, which remains the principal risk.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The complete natural rank stratification identifies unexpectedly restricted \(2\)-ranks (only \(2\), \(5\), and \(6\)) among extremal point-filling genus-six curves and isolates a single orbit of \(a\)-number three. These are meaningful moduli/Jacobian invariants not recoverable from tangent-incidence and smoothness data alone; the numbers give explicit test cases for arithmetic stratification of genus-six curves in characteristic two.
