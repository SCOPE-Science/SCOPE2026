# Cartier rank and an embedded Hasse degeneracy along the characteristic-five genus-ten Artin–Schreier family

## Finding
Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(5\). Write
\[
X_{u,a,b,c}:\quad y^5-y=f(x)=x^6+u x^4+a x^3+b x^2+c x,
\qquad (u,a,b,c)\in k^4.
\]
Every smooth projective normalization is connected of genus \(10\), with \(5\)-rank \(0\). Define
\[
P=a^2-u b+2u^3,\qquad \mathfrak m=(u,a,b)\subset R=k[u,a,b,c].
\]
The exact rank of the Cartier operator on \(H^0(X_{u,a,b,c},\Omega^1)\) is
\[
\operatorname{rank}\mathcal C=\begin{cases}
4,&P\ne0,\\
3,&P=0\text{ and }(u,a,b)\ne(0,0,0),\\
0,&u=a=b=0.
\end{cases}
\]
Consequently the Jacobian \(a\)-numbers are \(6,7,10\), respectively; in particular, values \(8\) and \(9\) do not occur in this normalized monic degree-six family. The locus with \(a\)-number at least \(7\) has reduced support the irreducible cubic hypersurface \(P=0\). More precisely, the ideal of four-by-four minors of the **entrywise fifth-power Cartier coefficient matrix** is
\[
I_4=P\mathfrak m^2.
\]
Thus the polynomial determinantal scheme is nonreduced along the superspecial parameter line. Its maximal submodule supported on this line is \((P)/(P\mathfrak m^2)\cong R/\mathfrak m^2\), whose transverse length is \(4\).

## Assumptions and scope
The characteristic is exactly \(5\), and parameters range over the entire algebraic closure. The equation is taken as a separable Artin–Schreier cover; no smoothness exception is imposed on the coefficients because its smooth projective model is always connected and has one pole of order six, prime to five. The monic normalization eliminates a possible degree-five term by an Artin–Schreier change of the \(y\) coordinate, and removes the constant similarly; thus this is the full monic degree-six one-pole coefficient family, rather than a hand-picked lower-dimensional slice. The invariants refer to the Jacobian of the normalization, not the singularity of a particular plane projective closure.

The determinantal ideal is the ideal generated by minors of a matrix with polynomial entries \(B_{rj}=(\mathcal C_{rj})^5\), where \(\mathcal C_{rj}\) denotes the \(5^{-1}\)-linear Cartier matrix coefficient in the stated basis. This entrywise Frobenius power is **not** a fifth iterate of the operator. Since \(k\) is perfect, it has the same rank at each geometric point. Scheme statements refer to this explicit polynomial matrix over \(R\); changing to a different scheme-theoretic Frobenius-twist convention can change the scheme structure, though not the geometric rank strata.

## Proof
**Geometry and basis.** The Artin–Schreier extension has a unique branched place, above infinity, with jump six. The genus formula gives \((5-1)(6-1)/2=10\), and the Deuring–Shafarevich formula gives \(5\)-rank zero. A basis of holomorphic differentials is
\[
\mathcal B=(dx,x\,dx,x^2dx,x^3dx,\ y\,dx,xy\,dx,x^2y\,dx,\ y^2dx,xy^2dx,y^3dx).
\]
Indeed the basis condition is \(5i+6j\le18\) for \(x^i y^jdx\) with \(j=0,1,2,3\), and these ten pairs exhaust the condition.

**Cartier coefficients.** In characteristic \(5\), \(y=y^5-f(x)\); expand \(y^j=(y^5-f)^j\) and use the defining rule for affine Cartier on \(x^{5m+4}dx\). For a basis element the computation is
\[
\mathcal C(x^iy^jdx)=\sum_{t=0}^j(-1)^{j-t}\binom jt\,y^t\mathcal C_{\mathbb A^1}\!\bigl(x^if(x)^{j-t}dx\bigr),
\quad \mathcal C_{\mathbb A^1}(x^{5m+4}dx)=x^m dx.
\]
Taking fifth powers of all coefficients gives the polynomial matrix \(B\). Indexing rows and columns from \(0\) through \(9\) in order \(\mathcal B\), its only nonzero entries are:
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
(r,j)&B_{rj}\\\hline
(0,4), (0,5), (0,6)&-u,\ -a,\ -b\\
(0,7), (0,8), (0,9)&2ac+b^2,\ 2bc,\ 2bc^2\\
(1,7), (1,8), (1,9)&2a,\ 2b+u^2,\ -a^3-abu-bc+2cu^2\\
(2,9)&2b+2u^2\\
(4,7), (4,8), (4,9)&-2u,\ -2a,\ ac-2b^2\\
(5,9)&a\\
(7,9)&2u
\end{array}
\]
All other entries vanish. This formula is an identity in \(R\), not a calculation at selected parameter values.

**Rank calculation.** The three columns indexed \(4,5,6\) lie entirely in row \(0\) and contribute one rank precisely when \(\mathfrak m\ne0\). After deleting row \(0\), the columns \(7,8\) have support only in rows \(1,4\), with block
\[
T=\begin{pmatrix}2a&2b+u^2\\-2u&-2a\end{pmatrix},
\qquad\det T=a^2-ub+2u^3=P
\]
(the equality uses characteristic five). Column \(9\) has, in rows \(2,5,7\), entries \((2b+2u^2,a,2u)\), which generate the same ideal \(\mathfrak m\). Thus when \(\mathfrak m\ne0\), its component away from rows \(1,4\) contributes exactly one more rank, giving \(2+\operatorname{rank} T\). If \(P=0\) but \(\mathfrak m\ne0\), the block \(T\) is nonzero, so it has rank one. If \(\mathfrak m=0\), every entry of \(B\) vanishes. These observations prove the three cases. The \(a\)-number equals \(10-\operatorname{rank}\mathcal C\); maximal \(a=10\) makes those Jacobians superspecial over \(k\).

**The determinantal scheme.** A nonzero size-four minor must choose exactly one of columns \(4,5,6\), together with columns \(7,8,9\), and it must use row \(0\), rows \(1,4\), and one of rows \(2,5,7\). Every such minor is, up to sign, a product of an element of \((-u,-a,-b)\), the determinant \(P\), and an element of \((2b+2u^2,a,2u)\). Therefore
\[
I_4(B)=P\,(u,a,b)\,(b+u^2,a,u)=P\mathfrak m^2.
\]
The polynomial \(P\) is irreducible (it is primitive and linear in \(b\) over \(k[u,a]\)). Its initial quadratic form along \(\mathfrak m\) is \(a^2-ub\), a nondegenerate quadratic form in the three transverse directions, so the reduced hypersurface has a transverse ordinary double point along the superspecial \(c\)-line. As \(\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak m}(P)=2\), one has \((P)\cap\mathfrak m^4=P\mathfrak m^2\). The natural embedded torsion quotient \((P)/(P\mathfrak m^2)\cong R/\mathfrak m^2\) has length \(1+3=4\) at the generic point of the \(c\)-line.

## Verification
Run `python VERIFY.py` after installing `sympy` (see `requirements.txt`). The verifier independently expands \(f(x)^j\) in the polynomial ring, constructs all \(100\) potential Cartier matrix entries, compares the \(15\) nonzero entries with the displayed formulas, checks \(\det T=P\), and computes the nine decisive minors. It also exhaustively checks all \(125\) triples in \(\mathbb F_5^3\), with a fixed auxiliary \(c\), finding one rank-zero triple, \(24\) rank-three triples, and \(100\) rank-four triples. The coefficient-ring identities, rather than this finite enumeration, prove the classification over the algebraic closure.

## Relationship to prior work
Farnell–Pries, *Families of Artin-Schreier curves with Cartier-Manin matrix of constant rank* (arXiv:1202.4183v1, 2012), supplies established genus, differential-basis and Cartier framework. Its principal coefficient-independent \(a\)-number theorem assumes \(p\equiv1\pmod L\) for pole-order lcm \(L\); here \(p=5,L=6\), so it does not imply the changing ranks or their determinantal ideal. Bouw–Ho–Malmskog–Scheidler–Srinivasan–Vincent (arXiv:1410.7031v1, 2014) study \(y^p-y=xR(x)\) with \(R\) additive. In this characteristic and degree that covers the much smaller subfamily \(x^6+bx^2\), not arbitrary \(u,a,b\), and principally determines zeta functions and supersingular features. Zhou, *On the existence of curves with prescribed a-number* (arXiv:1901.08375), studies related \(a\)-number problems, including special monomial cases, but not the present complete coefficientwise rank-drop scheme. One-parameter calculations in the \(x^6+bx^2\) subfamily do not imply a four-parameter cubic rank locus. No claim is made to the classical Cartier formula, superspecial Hermitian example, or a general solution for Artin–Schreier \(a\)-number strata.

## Limitations
The result concerns the rank of the Cartier operator and the \(a\)-number, not the full Ekedahl–Oort type, Newton polygon or point counts. The scheme structure is tied to the explicitly defined entrywise fifth-power coefficient matrix; the underlying rank strata are intrinsic. The normalization does not quotient coordinate equivalence, so the parameter scheme should not be read as a moduli stack or an isomorphism-class count. Exhaustive historical priority is not established; results phrased through a more general algorithm for Artin–Schreier Cartier matrices or characteristic-five special families could conceivably subsume part of the conclusion.
