# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The curve is smooth exactly off \(a^2=4\); the Hasse--Witt coefficient matrix is diagonal because the support of the fifteenth power occupies one residue class modulo three. Explicit multinomial coefficients give its two diagonal polynomials. Independently checkable factorizations, gcd and squarefreeness establish the exact rank strata and all counts. Frobenius-stable rank agrees with rank because the matrix is diagonal. A zero Cartier matrix is equivalent to a superspecial genus-three Jacobian. The smallest-prime comparison checks all relevant prime characteristics and all nine matrix entries, not sample parameter values.

## Originality

PASS with residual historical uncertainty. The 2012 Gutierrez--Sevilla--Shaska paper explains extra involutions, dihedral invariants and quotient Jacobians but has no characteristic-31 Hasse--Witt stratification. The 2025 Ohashi et al. paper gives a global superspecial enumeration, including known examples, without stating the complete rank stratification of this marked family. The 2023 Kudo et al. paper treats genus four, not the present genus-three object. Semantic mathematical-record and keyword searches found no equivalent rank-polynomial stratification. Existing general coefficient algorithms are not claimed as new. Unindexed parameter tables are a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value

PASS. This is a complete arithmetic stratification of a natural bielliptic genus-three family with cyclic-six automorphisms, not an arbitrary isolated numerical example. It identifies precisely the simultaneous vanishing of the elliptic and complementary genus-two Hasse blocks, two additional superspecial marked fibers, and the first characteristic at least five when nonzero parameters can be superspecial. The rank-one and rank-two strata distinguish different failures of ordinarity invisible in a superspecial-only census.

## Scope and risks

The result counts marked geometric parameter values rather than isomorphism classes. It does not determine non-superspecial Newton polygons. The earliest date used concerns an identified public version of the motivating original classification; it is not a claimed first appearance of the present result. Further historical comparison with unindexed explicit computations could alter the priority assessment.
