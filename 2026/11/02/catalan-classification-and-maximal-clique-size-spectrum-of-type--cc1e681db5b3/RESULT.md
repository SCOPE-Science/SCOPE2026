# Catalan classification and maximal-clique size spectrum of type-A 0-Hecke idempotents

## Finding

**Theorem.** Fix an integer \(m\ge1\). Let \(H_m=H_0(S_{m+1})\) be the 0-Hecke monoid with generators \(\pi_1,\dots,\pi_m\), and let \(\Gamma_m\) be its graph whose vertices are **monoid** idempotents, with two different vertices adjacent if their product commutes. Every inclusion-maximal clique of \(\Gamma_m\) is uniquely of the form
\[
\mathcal C(T)=\{e_I:I\in J(T)\},
\]
where \(T\) is a binary search tree on the ordered keys \(1,\dots,m\), the partial order puts each descendant below its ancestors, \(J(T)\) is its lattice of order ideals, and \(e_I\) is the longest parabolic element in the 0-Hecke monoid on generators indexed by \(I\). Each \(\mathcal C(T)\) is a subsemilattice under multiplication, isomorphic to \(J(T)\) under ideal union. In particular, the number of inclusion-maximal cliques is the Catalan number
\[
C_m=\frac{1}{m+1}\binom{2m}{m}.
\]

Define \(f_m(s)\) to be the number of these cliques with precisely \(s\) elements and set \(f_0(1)=1\), with all other \(f_0(s)=0\). Then the **complete size spectrum** is computed by
\[
f_m(s)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\ \sum_{\substack{a,b\ge1\\ab=s-1}}f_i(a)f_{m-1-i}(b),\qquad m\ge1.
\]
The smallest maximal clique has \(m+1\) elements; exactly \(2^{m-1}\) such cliques attain this size. If \(M_m\) denotes the largest maximal clique size, then \(M_0=1\) and
\[
M_m=1+\max_{0\le i<m} M_i M_{m-1-i}.
\]
For example \(f_4(5)=8\), \(f_4(6)=2\), \(f_4(7)=4\), and all other \(f_4(s)\) vanish.

## Assumptions and scope

The monoid has relations \(\pi_i^2=\pi_i\), \(\pi_i\pi_j=\pi_j\pi_i\) for \(|i-j|>1\), and \(\pi_i\pi_{i+1}\pi_i=\pi_{i+1}\pi_i\pi_{i+1}\). The 0-Hecke product is the Demazure product \(\ast\): if \(s_i\) is an adjacent transposition then \(w\ast s_i=ws_i\) when Coxeter length rises, otherwise \(w\ast s_i=w\). Every idempotent is exactly one \(e_I=w_I\), where \(I\subseteq[m]\) and \(w_I\) is the longest element of the subgroup \(W_I\) generated by \(\{s_i:i\in I\}\), as in Elias–Ko, Lemma 2.9. The empty support gives the identity. The full support gives the absorbing longest element. A clique is inclusion-maximal in this **idempotent commutation graph**; it need not be maximum-cardinality, and we make no claim about maximal commutative submonoids containing nonidempotents.

## Proof

**1. Pairwise commuting criterion.** For any \(I,J\subseteq[m]\),
\[
e_I\ast e_J=e_J\ast e_I\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{each connected component of }I\cup J\text{ (in the path on }[m]\text{) is contained in }I\text{ or }J.
\]
When this holds, \(e_I\ast e_J=e_{I\cup J}\). For the forward direction, a product of commuting idempotents is idempotent. Its Coxeter support is \(I\cup J\), so the idempotent classification forces it to be \(e_{I\cup J}\). Restrict to one connected interval \(K\) of \(I\cup J\). If neither \(I\cap K\) nor \(J\cap K\) is all of \(K\), the equality \(w_{I\cap K}\ast w_{J\cap K}=w_K\) would imply \(w_K\in W_{I\cap K}W_{J\cap K}\), because Demazure multiplication on the right by \(W_{J\cap K}\) stays in that ordinary group-product set (Elias–Ko, Lemma 2.2).

We show this membership impossible. The set \(W_AW_B\) is a lower ideal for Bruhat order: every element below \(xy\), with \(x\in W_A\) and \(y\in W_B\), has a reduced expression that is a subword of a concatenation of reduced expressions for \(x\) and \(y\), and therefore lies in \(W_AW_B\). (Choose a reduced word for \(xy\) as a subword of this concatenation, then apply the Bruhat subword criterion.) As \(w_K\) is the Bruhat maximum of \(W_K\), membership would imply \(W_K=W_AW_B\). Identify \(W_K\) with the full symmetric group on \(|K|+1\) consecutive positions. The proper parabolic \(W_B\) preserves a nonempty proper prefix of size \(p\); hence, if \(W_K=W_AW_B\), the proper parabolic \(W_A\) must act transitively on all \(p\)-subsets. But \(W_A\) preserves a nontrivial consecutive block of positions, and \(p\)-subsets have varying intersection size with that block. Contradiction. Conversely, if each component \(K\) is contained entirely in \(I\) or \(J\), its longest element absorbs the other factor on both sides; different components commute. Thus the products commute and equal \(e_{I\cup J}\).

**2. The unique missing root.** Let \(\mathcal F\) be an inclusion-maximal clique. It contains \(e_\varnothing\) and \(e_{[m]}\), which commute with every idempotent. The product of mutually commuting members is their union idempotent by Step 1, so \(\mathcal F\) is union-closed by maximality. Let \(U\) be the union of the supports of its members other than \([m]\). If there were a sequence of proper members whose accumulated union first reached \([m]\), at that step two proper, commuting supports would have connected union \([m]\), forbidden by Step 1. Therefore \(U\ne[m]\). Choose \(r\in[m]\setminus U\) and put \(K=[m]\setminus\{r\}\). Since every proper member's support is contained in \(K\), \(e_K\) commutes with all of \(\mathcal F\), so maximality places \(e_K\) in \(\mathcal F\). Any other proper member must be contained in \(K\), since a proper support containing \(r\) would have connected union \([m]\) with \(K\) and would not commute. Thus \(K\) is the unique maximal proper support, and \(r\) is uniquely determined by \(\mathcal F\).

**3. Recursive factorization.** Remove the root \(r\), leaving intervals \(L=[1,r-1]\) and \(R=[r+1,m]\). Every proper support lies in \(L\cup R\) and has components there. By Step 1, compatibility factors independently in the two intervals. Let \(\mathcal F_L\) and \(\mathcal F_R\) be the projections of the proper supports. If a projected family were not maximal compatible within its interval, an extra compatible support there would extend \(\mathcal F\), impossible. Likewise, if a pair of projected supports were absent from the proper supports, their union would be a compatible additional member, impossible. Hence the proper supports are exactly \(\mathcal F_L\times\mathcal F_R\), and the full support is the sole additional member. Inductively, \(\mathcal F_L\) and \(\mathcal F_R\) correspond to unique binary search trees; adjoining \(r\) as root yields a unique tree \(T\). Descendant-first ideals of this tree are exactly independent ideals of the two subtrees, or the single full ideal containing the root. The product is ideal union, so every such family is indeed maximal and the claimed bijection holds.

**4. Enumeration.** With \(i\) left keys, the right subtree has \(m-1-i\) keys. A tree with \(a\) left ideals and \(b\) right ideals has exactly \(1+ab\) ideals. This gives the size recurrence; forgetting size gives the Catalan recurrence. As \(1+ab\ge(a+1)+(b+1)-1\) for positive integers \(a,b\), induction gives size at least \(m+1\), with equality exactly when one subtree is empty and the other is extremal. There are two choices of the endpoint root at every positive rank after the first, producing \(2^{m-1}\) minimizing trees. Maximizing the same recursion proves the recurrence for \(M_m\).

## Verification

The standalone `verify.py` uses ordinary symmetric-group permutations to implement 0-Hecke multiplication, independently of the commuting-component criterion. It checks every pair of parabolic idempotents for all ranks \(1\le m\le7\), independently enumerates maximal graph cliques, and compares their complete support families with the ideals of every binary search tree. It also tests all permutations for idempotency for \(1\le m\le5\) and checks the entire size-spectrum recurrence through \(m=10\). Its final line is `VERIFY_OK`. These finite tests corroborate, but do not replace, the general proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Elias and Ko establish the Demazure product and the classification of monoid idempotents as parabolic longest elements; both are used and explicitly credited rather than claimed as new. He studies a different commutative submonoid built from certain 0-Hecke products. Denton constructs idempotents and commuting phenomena in the **monoid algebra**, whose linear idempotents need not be monoid elements. Ganyushkin–Mazorchuk obtain Catalan counts for **Kiselman quotient monoids**, rather than maximal commuting families inside the original 0-Hecke monoid. The claim here is the classification of maximal **pairwise commuting monoid idempotents** by ordered binary trees and the full cardinality spectrum. Related general Coxeter-parabolic or graph-associahedron results might imply a reformulation; publication priority is not independently certified.

## Limitations

The result addresses type-A path Coxeter graphs only, the monoid itself rather than its monoid algebra, and maximal pairwise-commuting **idempotent** sets rather than arbitrary commutative subsemigroups containing nonidempotents. The Catalan count and spectrum refer to labeled keys, not tree shapes up to unlabeled isomorphism. The result is proved for all \(m\ge1\); finite verification covers only its specified ranks. The self-review does not constitute independent validation or a full literature priority determination.
