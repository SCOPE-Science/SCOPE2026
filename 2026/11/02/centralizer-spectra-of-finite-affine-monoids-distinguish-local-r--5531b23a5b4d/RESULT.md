# Centralizer spectra of finite affine monoids distinguish local-ring geometry

## Finding

For a finite commutative unital ring \(R\) of order \(s\), let \(\operatorname{Aff}(R)\) consist of every map \(f_{a,b}:x\mapsto ax+b\), where \(a,b\in R\), under composition. Write \(C(f)\) for the centralizer of \(f\) **in this entire monoid**, not just in its group of units. The following formulas completely identify its centralizer-size statistics for two natural infinite classes of local rings.

**Ideal-size correspondence.** For every \(a,b\in R\), let \(J=(a-1,b)\) be their generated ideal. Then
\[
|C(f_{a,b})|=\frac{s^2}{|J|}.
\]
Thus the multiset of centralizer sizes is precisely the image of the two-generated-ideal size distribution under \(j\mapsto s^2/j\). If \(R\) is local with residue field of order \(q\), exactly \(s^2(1-q^{-2})\) of its \(s^2\) affine maps have the smallest possible centralizer size \(s\).

**Chain rings.** If \(R\) is a finite commutative chain ring of length \(k\ge1\) with residue field \(\mathbb F_q\), then \(s=q^k\). For each \(0\le j<k\), precisely
\[
q^{2(k-j)}-q^{2(k-j-1)}
\]
affine maps have centralizer size \(q^{k+j}\), and exactly one (the identity) has centralizer size \(q^{2k}\). No other sizes occur. Consequently the probability that two uniform independent affine maps commute is
\[
\operatorname{cp}(\operatorname{Aff}(R))
=q^{-k}\bigl(1+q^{-1}-q^{-k-1}\bigr).
\]
These formulas depend on the residue-field size and length, not the characteristic or the choice of chain ring.

**Square-zero local rings.** Suppose \(R\) is finite and local, its maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) satisfies \(\mathfrak m^2=0\), and \(d=\dim_{R/\mathfrak m}\mathfrak m\ge2\). Put \(q=|R/\mathfrak m|\) and \(s=q^{d+1}\). Its complete centralizer spectrum is:

| Centralizer size | Number of affine maps |
|---|---|
| \(q^{d+1}\) | \(q^{2d+2}-q^{2d}\) |
| \(q^{2d}\) | \((q^d-1)(q^d-q)\) |
| \(q^{2d+1}\) | \((q^d-1)(q+1)\) |
| \(q^{2d+2}\) | \(1\) |

The four centralizer sizes are distinct for \(d\ge2\), and none other occurs. Their commuting probability is
\[
\operatorname{cp}(\operatorname{Aff}(R))
=\frac{q^{d+1}+q^d-q^{d-1}+q^{2d-2}-q^{d-2}}{q^{2d+2}}.
\]

**Sharp first comparison.** For any prime power \(q\), consider \(R_{\rm chain}=\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^3)\) and \(R_{\rm sq}=\mathbb F_q[u,v]/(u,v)^2\). Both rings have order \(q^3\) and residue field \(\mathbb F_q\), but
\[
\operatorname{cp}(\operatorname{Aff}(R_{\rm sq}))-
\operatorname{cp}(\operatorname{Aff}(R_{\rm chain}))
=\frac{(q-1)^2(q+1)}{q^7}>0.
\]
For \(q=2\), the centralizer-size multiplicities at sizes \(8,16,32,64\) are, respectively, \((48,12,3,1)\) for the chain ring and \((48,6,9,1)\) for the square-zero ring. The commuting probabilities are \(23/128\) and \(13/64\).

## Assumptions and scope

All rings are finite, associative, commutative, and unital, with \(1\ne0\). In particular, the coefficient \(a\) of an affine map is not assumed invertible. Ring ideals and their cardinalities are taken in the underlying finite ring. A chain ring is a finite local principal ideal ring; length means the smallest positive \(k\) for which its maximal ideal has \(k\)-th power zero. For the square-zero assertion the residue field may have any prime-power order, and the ring need not be a split \(\mathbb F_q\)-algebra. The theorem includes mixed-characteristic chain rings. The probability uses ordered pairs with replacement, including equal elements and identity.

## Proof

**1. Centralizers and ideals.** Uniqueness of the affine presentation follows by evaluating at zero and one. Composition satisfies
\[
f_{a,b}\circ f_{c,e}=f_{ac,ae+b}.
\]
Since \(R\) is commutative, two affine maps commute exactly when
\[
(a-1)e=(c-1)b.
\]
Fixing \(a,b\), put \(u=a-1\), \(v=c-1\). Translation \(c\mapsto v\) is a bijection of \(R\). The additive homomorphism
\[
\Phi_{u,b}:R\oplus R\longrightarrow R,\qquad (v,e)\longmapsto ue-vb
\]
has image exactly the ideal \((u,b)\). Therefore the number of pairs \((v,e)\) in its kernel is \(s^2/|(u,b)|\), proving the centralizer formula without any Frobenius, principal-ideal, or local hypothesis.

**2. Universal local atom.** In a local ring, \((u,b)=R\) if and only if at least one of \(u,b\) is a unit. The maximal ideal has \(s/q\) elements. Thus exactly \(s^2-(s/q)^2=s^2(1-q^{-2})\) pairs generate the unit ideal, and hence exactly that many affine maps have centralizer size \(s\). Every other map has a strictly larger centralizer.

**3. Chain rings.** Choose a generator \(\pi\) of the maximal ideal. Each ideal is \(\pi^jR\) for a unique \(0\le j\le k\), with size \(q^{k-j}\). Define the valuation of zero as \(k\). The ideal \((u,b)\) is generated by \(\pi^j\), where \(j\) is the minimum of the valuations of \(u,b\). For \(0\le j<k\), the number of pairs of elements whose valuations are both at least \(j\), but not both at least \(j+1\), is \(q^{2(k-j)}-q^{2(k-j-1)}\). For \(j=k\), only \((u,b)=(0,0)\) occurs. Applying Part 1 gives the exact spectrum. Summing centralizer sizes over the \(s^2\) affine maps and dividing by \(s^4\) yields
\[
q^{-4k}\left[\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}
\bigl(q^{2(k-j)}-q^{2(k-j-1)}\bigr)q^{k+j}
+q^{2k}\right]
=q^{-k}(1+q^{-1}-q^{-k-1}).
\]

**4. Square-zero rings.** Since \(\mathfrak m^2=0\), multiplication by \(R\) on \(\mathfrak m\) factors through \(R/\mathfrak m=\mathbb F_q\), so \(\mathfrak m\) is an \(\mathbb F_q\)-vector space of dimension \(d\) and \(s=q^{d+1}\). If \(u\) or \(b\) is a unit, their ideal is \(R\), giving the first spectrum row. Otherwise \(u,b\in\mathfrak m\), and the ideal \((u,b)\) equals their \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear span. If this span has rank \(r\in\{0,1,2\}\), its size is \(q^r\), hence the centralizer has size \(s^2/q^r\). Rank zero occurs once. Rank one occurs \((q^d-1)(q+1)\) times: choose its one-dimensional subspace and a nonzero ordered pair of coefficients. Rank two occurs \((q^d-1)(q^d-q)\) times, by choosing two independent vectors. Substitution into Part 1 proves all four spectrum rows. Summing their contributions and dividing by \(s^4\) simplifies to the stated probability.

**5. Equal-size rings separate.** In the chain case at \(k=3\), the formula is \((q^3+q^2-q^{-1})/q^6\). In the square-zero case at \(d=2\), it is \((q^3+2q^2-q-1)/q^6\). Subtraction gives the strictly positive difference \((q-1)^2(q+1)/q^7\). The two rings are not isomorphic because their maximal ideals have different squares.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone \(\texttt{verify.py}\) enumerates the affine monoid directly, computes each element's centralizer by comparing both orders of composition, and independently computes the ideal generated by the two relevant coefficients. It checks the theorem's exact centralizer spectrum and commuting probability for truncated-polynomial chain rings, residue rings \(\mathbb Z/p^k\mathbb Z\), and split radical-square-zero local rings over the prime fields of orders two and three. The tested total ring orders range from \(2\) to \(27\). Run with \(\texttt{python verify.py}\); successful completion prints \(\texttt{VERIFY_OK}\). These finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the proofs for arbitrary \(q,k,d\).

## Relationship to prior work

Shahzamanian and Steinberg [1] introduce and analyze exactly the finite affine monoid considered here, establishing the Gabriel quiver of its complex monoid algebra. Their results about regular classes and the quiver are not asserted as new; their stated theorems do not provide the centralizer-size distributions above. Kasai [2, Lemma 2.1] states the affine commutation equation for the **group of invertible affine transformations**, in connection with common-centralizer obstructions. The commutation equation itself is not claimed as new here. The group-level common-centralizer theorem neither counts centralizers in the full monoid nor implies the stated nonunit-coefficient spectra. Juráš and Ursul [3] investigate commuting probabilities of finite rings and groups; their invariants concern multiplication in those algebraic structures, not the affine transformation monoid. The contribution isolated here is the exact ideal-size correspondence together with two complete infinite-family spectra and a strict equal-order ring comparison.

## Limitations

No general closed formula is asserted for the two-generated ideal-size distribution of arbitrary finite commutative rings. The square-zero four-row formula requires embedding dimension at least two (the dimension-one case merges rows and is covered by the chain-ring formula). The formulas concern centralizer **cardinalities**, not a classification of centralizer submonoid isomorphism types. Published-paper priority is assessed only against the inspected literature; an equivalent result under two-generated-ideal statistics or affine-semigroup terminology could still exist. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
