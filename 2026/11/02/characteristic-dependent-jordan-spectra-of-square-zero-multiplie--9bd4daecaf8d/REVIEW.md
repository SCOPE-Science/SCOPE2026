# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS
The square-zero equations are exactly \(a^2=0\) and \(2ab=0\) in \(k[y]/(y^n)\); there is no assumption that truncation and squaring commute with differentiation. The multiplication matrix has invariant-factor valuations \(\min(u,v)\) and \(2u-\min(u,v)\), with the necessary zero-factor truncation, proving the rank identity even at the boundary. The rank inequalities combine with the characteristic-dependent valuation restrictions to give the displayed linear subspaces. Counting successive intersections proves the finite-field census. The square-zero Jordan partition follows because every Jordan block has length at most two. All quantifiers and boundaries were checked against the proof.

## Originality: PASS, conditional on historical coverage
The strongest inspected background is Cook (arXiv:1111.4979) on Lefschetz properties of linear forms, Iarrobino–Macias Marques–McDaniel (arXiv:1802.07383) on Jordan types and their loci, and Altafi–Iarrobino–Khatami (arXiv:1810.00716) on height-two complete-intersection Jordan types. Their results do not directly supply this exact square-zero-element finite-field rank distribution or the characteristic-dependent **reduced** union-of-linear-subspaces geometry. A published dual-number Jordan-lift result treats the index of \(J_n+\varepsilon B\), not the rank of a square-zero multiplication operator. Other general square-zero matrix counts do not specialize by themselves to the constrained equal-diagonal subset. Residual risk: an unindexed specialized formula or consequence of a more general representation-theoretic stratification may exist.

## Value: PASS
The rank of multiplication by an element is a canonical Artinian-algebra Jordan-type invariant, and the square-zero locus is a natural nilpotence subvariety. The theorem classifies the **entire** rank spectrum for every truncation length and prime-power field, gives its exact finite-field multiplicities and reduced components, and isolates the first rank where the arithmetic and geometry diverge by characteristic. The linear-component chain gives a structural explanation beyond a finite recomputation. It neither presupposes a broader theorem nor claims classification of the nonreduced scheme.

## Verification and limits
A standalone enumerator counted and row-reduced 6,244 square-zero multiplication matrices in 18 completed configurations over four finite fields, including a nonprime field; all matched the formulas. The infinite-family result rests on the explicit algebraic proof, not the test count.
