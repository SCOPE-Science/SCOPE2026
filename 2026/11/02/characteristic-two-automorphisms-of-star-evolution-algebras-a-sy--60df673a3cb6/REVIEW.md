# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

For a general block map G(αa+u)=(cα+ℓ(u))a+αv+Tu, the leaf products imply B(Tu,Tw)=cB(u,w) and ℓ(u)ℓ(w)s=B(u,w)v. Taking a pair with B(u,w)=1 makes v a multiple of s. The rank of that multiple of B would be m≥2 if nonzero, whereas ℓ⊗ℓ has rank at most one. Hence v=ℓ=0. Invertibility gives c≠0, and the central square forces Ts=c²s. Since B(u,u)=B(u,s)², pairing Tu with Ts shows c⁻²B(u,u)=cB(u,u); taking a coordinate vector gives c³=1. Thus T=c²U with UᵗU=I. Conversely an isometry fixes s by injectivity of squaring and nondegeneracy, so these and only these block maps are automorphisms, with componentwise composition. When m is odd, ks is nondegenerate and its complement is alternating; the restriction and extension by identity are mutual inverses. When m is even, the span of s,t has Gram matrix with rows (0,1),(1,1), hence nondegenerate, and its orthogonal complement H is alternating. Fixing s and preserving B(s,t) and B(s,H) forces the t-coordinate in U(t) to be 1 and in U(H) to vanish. Pairing U(t) with U(h) then forces the s-coordinate of U(h) to be B(u,Vh); the resulting formulas preserve all pairings, including B(t,t). The kernel of restriction to the quotient has product (u+u′,β+β′+B(u,u′)), which is symmetric and has square neutral; it has q^{2r+1} elements and the section acts by (u,β)↦(Vu,β). This also works when H=0. Counting symplectic bases contributes a factor (q^{2j}−1)q^{2j−1} in dimension 2j, giving the stated order, and the independent central factor has size gcd(3,q−1). The odd case retains this central factor; its symplectic part has no normal nontrivial 2-subgroup, since such a group on the natural space would have a nonzero fixed vector and normality plus symplectic transitivity forces it to fix the whole space.

## originality

PASS

The supported distinction is the exact identification of the full ordinary automorphism group of the nonperfect star algebra in characteristic two. The structure and order of the corresponding classical bilinear groups are known; the originality assessment is not a claim about those groups in isolation. The strongest inspected automorphism results for graph algebras either require perfectness, work in characteristic zero or odd characteristic, or give only the diagonal subgroup for a star.

## value

PASS

The full automorphism group of this natural nonperfect graph-derived algebra is not determined by the graph automorphism group or by the standard perfect-algebra theorem. Identifying its matrix normal form makes the classical parity distinction applicable and describes the normal subgroup and its action, information not visible in the diagonal subgroup or in a small-field order check.
