# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The homomorphism identities reduce to preservation of the defining nondegenerate symplectic bracket and to centrality of the image of its central generator. In characteristic two, the target is itself a Heisenberg Lie algebra with central scalar matrix, and the wedge equation \(b\wedge c=\lambda\omega\) gives the decisive rank obstruction for every \(n\ge2\). The prime determinantal ideal supplies the reduced geometric structure. Exact rank-one matrix counts and independent row-line choices supply all finite-field rank strata. The four stated exhaustive tests independently check matrix commutators and image ranks, but no finite test is used in lieu of proof.

## Originality — PASS with specified residual risk

Ngo (2012), Section 4.2, already covers the commuting \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) determinantal locus when \(p\ne2\); that portion is expressly a comparator rather than original. The faithful characteristic-two \(H_3\) representation is likewise known. Rojas (2012) studies minimal degree in characteristic zero, not the higher-rank characteristic-two Hom-variety. Result-record searches, closest relevant records, and full primary-paper inspection identified no stronger statement implying the new higher-rank characteristic-two decomposition and image-rank census. Historical completeness is not guaranteed; in particular, abstract determinantal reductions may have appeared independently under different terminology.

## Value — PASS

The invariant is the natural space of Lie algebra representations with a fixed small target, refined by image dimension. The higher-rank characteristic-two locus is an explicit full geometric classification with exact point-count polynomials, singular-stratum geometry and a rank-dependent obstruction to faithful representations. A positive-dimensional singular locus appears in a family of arbitrary source rank, while odd characteristic is governed by a different cone. The classification is more than a fixed finite computation or a routine rephrasing of the characteristic-zero commuting-variety theorem.
