# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The fixed-point argument works on the full nonnegative spectral interval, including rank-deficient matrices. The rank identity excludes eigenvalue one. Positive congruence preserves the support of the fixed state, and uniqueness of the positive square root on that support proves commuting rigidity. Conversely every spectral projection in an eigenspace with gain above one is a fixed point. The Hermitian derivative has exactly \(N^2\) real eigendirections, with two modes for each off-diagonal pair. A direct multiplier sign analysis yields the inversion-count unstable index and the exact sink threshold. Exact-rational arithmetic checks independently reproduce 8 fixed points and 72 real mode slots in a non-diagonal three-dimensional example. No finite computation is used as proof of an all-dimensions claim. The proof does not require unproved dynamical regularity lemmas.

## Originality — PASS within inspected literature

The source model is Pawela–Życzkowski, arXiv:2303.06176 (first posted 2023-03-10). Its relevant full-text sections treat scalar-gain spectral dynamics, matrix gain through graph coupling, and invariant distributions for \(N=2\), but do not state the general positive-Hermitian equilibrium rigidity, repeated-gain Grassmannian stratification, or the simple-spectrum inversion-count unstable index. The 2011 Navickas–Smidtaite–Vainoras–Ragulskis matrix logistic model uses one scalar gain and is a covered special case, not the claimed anisotropic theorem. Cánovas studies a different coordinate-coupled logistic map; the abstract does not imply the matrix congruence result. No equivalent or stronger record was identified among the actual semantic database comparisons. Priority cannot be guaranteed by incomplete literature indexing; an abstract-only potentially related source was not treated as a full-text exclusion.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The model's noncommuting trajectories make its fixed-point structure and transverse stability genuinely different questions from scalar logistic iteration. The complete classification distinguishes the case of repeated gains (Grassmannian fixed-point families) from simple gains (an exact count of isolated hyperbolic equilibria). The integer unstable dimension can vary among equilibria of equal rank; its gain-order inversion correction is a natural spectral invariant relevant to local recurrence and coherence perturbations. The gain-three threshold gives an exact criterion for a unique hyperbolic sink in every dimension, not merely a numerical stability window. This is a structural classification, not an arbitrary parameter slice or a numerical recomputation.

## Remaining scope and risk

The result does not apply to general non-Hermitian coupling matrices, indefinite fixed points, higher-period cycles, or global attraction. The comparison with published sources is evidence for originality, not proof of worldwide priority.
