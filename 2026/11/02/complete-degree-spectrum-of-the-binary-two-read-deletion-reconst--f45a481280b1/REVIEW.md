# Scientific review: binary two-read deletion graph degree spectrum

## Correctness
PASS. The published Type-A intersection theorem identifies the graph edges with complementary alternating intervals of length at least two. Their unique first and last mismatching coordinates give a bijection with alternating intervals in a fixed word. The difference-word run decomposition gives exactly the triangular run contributions. A unique zero-block/one-run factorization proves the bivariate generating function. Both derivatives at unit degree weight and the extracted coefficient identities were reconstructed. The bounds include the \(n=1\) boundary. Independent brute-force deletion-ball intersection checks cover every binary word through length nine, while independent exact coefficient and rational moment checks cover all words through length fourteen.

## Originality
PASS, with a remaining priority risk. The 2020 primary pairwise Type-A theorem supplies only the adjacency characterization, while the 2022 primary research establishes the coding relevance and clique-cover analysis. The closest public all-alphabet graph-edge enumeration determines the **mean**, but explicitly does not determine the **degree distribution**. An edge count cannot imply the complete spectrum or its variance. Targeted record searches against degree distribution, run-length/triangular equivalents, and neighboring reconstruction statistics found no equivalent exact all-length bivariate law. No claim of exhaustive priority certification is made; older unindexed enumeration could remain.

## Value
PASS. The complete degree histogram is a natural local graph invariant for two-read deletion ambiguity, relevant beyond the global edge count. The result classifies the ambiguity of each word, exact distribution at every blocklength, all finite moments in principle, and a sharp linear variance, establishing quantitative concentration for uniformly random centers. It is not merely a recomputation of the already known edge total.

## Boundaries
Binary alphabets, exact-one-deletion descendant **sets**, at least two common descendants. No graph independence-number determination, higher deletion counts, or independently certified publication priority. The proof cites the published structural Type-A theorem; finite computations only corroborate it.
