# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The unrestricted support criterion is proved by reducing each requested labeling to a Hamiltonian cycle in a complete graph minus forbidden block adjacencies. Dirac's theorem settles large residual block counts; explicit small cycles and a detailed orientation argument settle the two-component boundary. The all-order coefficient formulas count unoriented labeled path components without duplication. The independent finite enumerator agrees through seven vertices, with boundary representatives at eight and nine. The proof, not the finite enumeration, is the basis for the unrestricted claim.

## Originality — PASS under accessible comparisons
Existing numerical VC statements for Hamiltonian-cycle concepts do not give a complete support characterization or shattering polynomial. The literature on arbitrary graph cycles permits shorter cycles and yields different shattering behavior. Work on graph learning by vertex-subset edge detection and on Hamiltonian symmetric-difference codes uses other predicates. A 1997 article on graph-defined set systems is a stated residual historical-priority risk because the full text was not available; noncoverage of that article is not asserted.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The theorem solves the natural all-support problem, not just its largest-cardinality case: it classifies every support and explicitly enumerates them at every size. The sharp exclusion of path components of orders two and three at the maximum boundary is a structural constraint arising from the spanning-cycle requirement, and the exact polynomial provides reusable combinatorial data.

## Limitations
Only simple undirected complete graphs and individual edge-incidence queries are covered. The argument does not determine an adaptive online dimension or classify noncomplete host graphs. Independent validation and complete historical-priority review have not been performed.
