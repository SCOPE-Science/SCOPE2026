# Scientific assessment: equality rigidity for radial weighted Fock domination

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is quantified over every nonzero measurable nonnegative radial density with finite moments, all entire norm-finite pairs, and exterior domination at all points beyond the sharp radius. Strict moment log-convexity gives an explicit unique norm-attaining line for the scaled backward shift. Reversing a rational exterior Schur quotient gives an exact bounded-operator identity without unbounded polynomial multiplication. The Schwarz–Pick/Schur decomposition yields an exact positive operator defect. Equality forces the Schur tail to be unimodular constant, because the adjoint shift on positive-degree vectors has norm strictly less than one. This makes both functions affine, and the converse follows by a direct squared-modulus identity. The point-mass boundary is explicitly excluded; it would defeat strict log-convexity. The exact checker is supplementary only.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence

Wikström (arXiv:2609.39622v1, 30 September 2026) classifies the Gaussian equality cases. The publicly available *Universal extremal rigidity for monotone weighted Fock Korenblum radii* proves the affine equality family for non-increasing weights only. *Sharp Korenblum radius for every radial entire-function \(L^2\) norm* proves the radius for arbitrary weights but explicitly excludes classification of equality pairs. Inspection of the latter two full theorem/proof files and comparisons with the original paper's scope show that neither includes the present all-weights equality rigidity. The strict Schur-tail argument supplies the needed missing implication. Searches cannot eliminate unindexed historical equivalences; the full text of the older Wee–Le paper was not obtained.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is the natural complete equality classification attached to a recently established sharp inequality, extended from the monotone class to *every* radial density. Nonmonotone gamma and annular weights cannot be handled by merely applying the monotone equality theorem, and sharpness of the underlying inequality alone does not determine equality pairs. The classification gives the precise obstruction to nonlinear extremizers and a starting point for future quantitative stability work.

## Limitations

The result does not cover arbitrary radial probability measures with atoms, nonradial weights, non-Hilbert norms, or bounded annular domination. No near-extremizer stability rate is proved. Equivalent historical coverage could still be found in an unindexed source. Verification remains non-independent.
