# Scientific assessment

## Correctness: PASS

The domain is every prime-power field size and every linearized coefficient pair, including characteristic two and singular linear parts. The norm-translation case follows by completing the norm. Otherwise the target quotient partitions into exactly \(q\) disjoint lines; restricted maps are ordinary quadratics for odd \(q\), while the nondegenerate trace pairing produces exactly one exceptional bijective line and \(q-1\) two-to-one lines for even \(q\). The explicit fiber totals sum to \(q^2\) targets and have weighted sum \(q^2\) inputs. Every algebraic step is included in the proof. A standalone exhaustive checker for all \(\mathbb F_p\)-linear maps at \(p=2,3,5,7\) confirms the counts.

## Originality: PASS with literature-search limitations

Mullen–Wan–Wang (arXiv:1210.8119, full PDF inspected) prove a general upper bound, not a closed fiber distribution. Gao–Wang (arXiv:1407.5884, introduction and main topic inspected) treat random and cyclotomic distributions. Zheng et al. (arXiv:2503.06640, Theorem 3.3 and its hypotheses inspected) require a rank-one trace-type inner linear expression, and do not derive the full norm-plus-arbitrary-linear dichotomy. Direct formula searches and mathematical research-record comparisons did not reveal covering results. A previously studied special polynomial image does not imply the classification of all \(q^4\) pairs. Residual risk: classical quadratic-map normal forms or finite-geometry results may subsume this statement without using these polynomial coordinates. Negative searches do not prove novelty.

The exact answer classifies every coefficient pair of a natural norm perturbation over every quadratic extension, giving all possible image sizes, all fiber multiplicities, and the characteristic-two structural difference that nevertheless yields identical counts. It extends beyond bounds or isolated examples to a natural complete family, with relevance to finite-field value-set theory and design of few-to-one polynomial maps. No claim of general downstream applications is needed.
