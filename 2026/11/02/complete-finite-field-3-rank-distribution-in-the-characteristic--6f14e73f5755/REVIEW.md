# Review of the characteristic-three Ciani rank distribution

## Correctness

PASS. The displayed normalized quartic is geometrically smooth exactly when its four discriminant factors are nonzero; the gradient and kernel calculation handles both zero-coordinate and nonzero-coordinate singularities. Its three genus-one involution quotients have Hasse invariants proportional to \(ab+c\), \(ac+b\), and \(bc+a\). The group-algebra decomposition has two-primary kernel, so it preserves the characteristic-three \(p\)-divisible group and therefore all three asserted Jacobian invariants. The three hypersurfaces meet in coordinate axes with one transverse triple point. The exact finite-field counts use a closed quadratic-character sum with a separately checked exclusion of \(c=\pm1\). A standalone verifier independently confirms all counts for \(q=3,9,27\); finite enumerations are not used as a proof of the all-extension identity.

## Originality

PASS, qualified. Lercier–Ritzenthaler–Rovetta–Sijsling discuss the Ciani coefficient family and moduli parametrization in their 2014 work, but their representative-family arguments assume characteristic exceeding the genus-dependent small-characteristic bound; they do not provide the asserted characteristic-three rank counts. Ohashi (2022) proves the Ciani smoothness criterion, constructs elliptic quotients and studies superspecial maximality, without stating a complete four-stratum \(3\)-rank distribution or the closed arithmetic counts for every \(q=3^m\). Brock's prior work on superspeciality is acknowledged rather than treated as novel. The distinctive claim is the simultaneous full geometric rank stratification and its exact polynomial enumeration in this normalized coefficient space. Searches of indexed mathematical records and accessible earlier claims found no equivalent assertion; unindexed historical tables remain a priority risk.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The coefficient family is a natural three-dimensional genus-three family, and the stratification simultaneously resolves four intrinsic Jacobian states at the smallest odd characteristic. The exact polynomial frequencies over every finite extension distinguish marked-family statistics from moduli isomorphism classes. The calculation requires both an elliptic-quotient analysis and a genuinely global discriminant character sum; it is not a finite-field census extrapolation or an arbitrary coefficient slice. No larger theorem or claim about all genus-three curves is implied.

## Limitations and nearest literature

The counts are for smooth normalized coefficient triples, not geometric moduli points, and may include many isomorphic curves. The source literature supplies the genus-three family, the discriminant criterion, and elliptic quotient background; the present counts are specialized to characteristic three. Historical priority is provisional, and no external scientific reviewer has checked the proof.
