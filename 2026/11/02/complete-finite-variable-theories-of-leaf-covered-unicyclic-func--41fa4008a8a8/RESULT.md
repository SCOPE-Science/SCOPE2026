# Complete finite-variable theories of leaf-covered unicyclic functional digraphs

## Finding
For every finite nonempty cyclic word \(\mathbf b=(b_0,\ldots,b_{\ell-1})\) of positive integers, define the finite directed graph \(D(\mathbf b)\) with core vertices \(c_i\) (indices modulo \(\ell\)), arcs \(c_i\to c_{i+1}\), and exactly \(b_i\) additional leaf vertices, each with its sole outgoing arc to \(c_i\). There are no other arcs. For \(\ell=1\), the core arc is a loop; for \(\ell=2\), there are two opposite core arcs. The signature has only equality and this binary arc relation, without counting quantifiers or named vertices.

**Theorem.** For the nonempty graphs \(D(\mathbf b)\), the unrestricted-depth counting-free finite-variable first-order equivalence relation is completely classified as follows.

1. With one variable, there are exactly two theories: \(\ell=1\) and \(\ell\ge2\).
2. With two variables, there are exactly five theories, according as (i) \(\ell=1,b_0=1\); (ii) \(\ell=1,b_0\ge2\); (iii) \(\ell=2\); (iv) \(\ell=3\); or (v) \(\ell\ge4\). For cases (iii)--(v), all positive leaf-multiplicity words are equivalent, regardless of their entries.
3. At every width \(k\ge3\), \(D(\mathbf b)\equiv_{\mathrm{FO}^k}D(\mathbf b')\) if and only if the cycle lengths agree and the two clipped words \((\min(b_i,k))_i\) and \((\min(b'_i,k))_i\) agree up to cyclic rotation (not reflection). This is equivalence at **every quantifier depth**.

For each \(k\ge3\) and fixed core length \(\ell\ge1\), the number of theories is the positive-integer necklace number
\[
N_k(\ell)=\frac1\ell\sum_{d\mid\ell}\varphi(d)\,k^{\ell/d}.
\]
Every equivalence class has a unique least-order representative within this graph family: replace each leaf count by its truncation at \(k\), retaining the cyclic order. The natural graph size is \(\ell+\sum_i b_i\). For \(\mathrm{FO}^2\), the least representatives of the five classes have respectively \(2,3,4,6,8\) vertices; for \(\mathrm{FO}^1\), they have \(2,4\) vertices.

## Assumptions and scope
The domain comprises all core and leaf vertices. Every core has at least one pendant leaf and every leaf has indegree zero and precisely one outgoing arc. The core is one directed cycle, possibly a loop or a two-cycle. All statements are relative to this precisely defined class of finite digraphs, not to all unary-function structures. Variable width refers to **reusable variable names**, not quantifier rank; quantifier depth is unrestricted. Neither counting quantifiers nor a distinguished cyclic root nor a unary-function symbol belongs to the signature.

## Proof
**Core and leaf predicates.** Write \(E(u,v)\) for a directed arc. Within this family, \(\operatorname{Core}(x):=\exists y\,E(y,x)\) defines precisely the cycle vertices; every leaf has indegree zero. Let \(\operatorname{Leaf}(x):=\neg\operatorname{Core}(x)\). Both use at most two variables. Every leaf has a unique parent, the core vertex it points to.

**One variable.** The only nontrivial atomic relation on a single variable is \(E(x,x)\), which holds for one core vertex exactly if \(\ell=1\). Each graph has at least one vertex with \(\neg E(x,x)\). In one-variable logic with equality, Boolean combinations and nested one-variable quantifiers can see no distinction beyond which of these unary atomic types are realized. Thus the claimed two phases are exact.

**Two variables.** Root/leaf types and the atomic types of ordered pairs determine a permanent two-pebble back-and-forth invariant. For a fixed core vertex the available other-core patterns are: the vertex itself; its successor/predecessor, which coincide as a loop if \(\ell=1\), are one mutual-neighbor core if \(\ell=2\), or are two distinct one-way neighbors if \(\ell\ge3\); and a core adjacent in neither direction exactly if \(\ell\ge4\). For a fixed core, an incoming pendant leaf always exists; a nonincoming pendant leaf exists exactly when \(\ell\ge2\). A fixed leaf has its unique parent, a different core if \(\ell\ge2\), itself, and a distinct leaf exactly when the total number of leaves is at least two. As every \(b_i\ge1\), these availability tables depend only on the five stated cases. In each case, if one pebble stays fixed, any desired one-point atomic pair pattern and target unary type exists in the other graph; Duplicator responds using it. This maintains the invariant indefinitely.

The five phases are separated by two-variable sentences. A loop detects \(\ell=1\). Among looped graphs, existence of two distinct loopless vertices detects \(b_0\ge2\). Existence of distinct vertices with arcs in both directions detects \(\ell=2\). On loopless, non-mutual-arc graphs, existence of distinct core vertices with no arc in either direction distinguishes \(\ell\ge4\) from \(\ell=3\). All unary core tests may shadow the other variable locally, so no third variable is used.

**Three-variable paths.** For each prescribed integer \(s\ge0\), the existence of an \(E\)-walk of length \(s\) from free endpoints \(u,v\) is expressible with three reusable variables: start with equality for \(s=0\) and iteratively compose with one arc, binding the third variable and alpha-renaming bound occurrences. Since core vertices have only their next-core outgoing arc, the exact directed core-cycle length \(\ell\) is recovered from the least positive \(s\) for which a core vertex has a closed walk of length \(s\).

**Local child counts using exactly \(k\) variables.** Fix \(k\ge3\). For each \(1\le r<k\), the predicate that a given core vertex \(x\) has at least \(r\) distinct leaf children uses the free root variable and \(r\) bound child variables, at most \(k\) names. The threshold \(r=k\) also uses only \(k\) names, by *recycling the root variable*. First choose \(k-1\) distinct leaf children \(u_1,\ldots,u_{k-1}\) of the free root \(x\). In a subformula, rebind \(x\) to a further leaf \(z\), distinct from all the \(u_i\), and for every \(i\) require \(z,u_i\) to have a common parent. A common-parent test
\[
\operatorname{Sib}(u,v):=\exists w\,(E(u,w)\land E(v,w))
\]
uses a third variable, which can be selected from the other \(k-1\) child names and shadowed only within that conjunct; \(k-1\ge2\). Hence the whole predicate still has at most \(k\) variable names. Every leaf has a unique parent, so the last leaf must be attached to the original root. Conversely, any \(k\) siblings provide the witnesses. Call the resulting predicates \(A_r(x)\). They are true exactly when \(b_i\ge r\) at core vertex \(c_i\).

**Separation of clipped necklaces.** The predicates \(A_1,\ldots,A_k\) define, with at most \(k\) names, the clipped value \(\min(b_i,k)\) at each core vertex. By the three-variable walk formulas, for any finite word \((s_0,\ldots,s_{\ell-1})\in\{1,\ldots,k\}^{\ell}\), a \(k\)-variable sentence says that there is a starting core vertex with precisely this clipped word in its successive core positions, and that its core-cycle length is exactly \(\ell\). The root variable may be shadowed inside each local-count predicate since that predicate is a separate conjunct. This sentence identifies one cyclic rotation orbit, not a reflection. Therefore different clipped necklace words or different cycle lengths have different \(\mathrm{FO}^k\) theories.

**Indefinite matching strategy.** Conversely, suppose two clipped words are cyclic rotations. Fix the corresponding rotation of their core cycles. In the \(k\)-pebble game, permanently use this rotation for any occupied core vertices. For occupied leaves, maintain a partial injective correspondence inside the two fibers over matched core vertices, preserving equality and the fixed-parent relation. At a new leaf move, at most \(k-1\) distinct other leaves are currently occupied. If the fiber size is below \(k\), its two exact sizes agree. If its size is at least \(k\), both fibers contain at least \(k\) leaves, so an unused matching leaf exists. The response preserves all directed arcs and equalities; thus the strategy persists through arbitrarily many rounds and proves equivalence at every finite quantifier depth.

**Enumeration and minimality.** The clipped positive words form the alphabet \(\{1,\ldots,k\}\), modulo the rotational action of the cycle group. Burnside's lemma yields the displayed necklace formula. Each clipped word gives the lower bound of \(\ell+\sum_i\min(b_i,k)\) vertices in its logical class; the clipped-word graph attains it. The directed core rotation is the only isomorphism freedom, making this representative unique up to isomorphism. The five two-variable minima follow by choosing the shortest admissible core and minimum leaf numbers in each phase.

## Verification
The included executable files `verify_necklace_profiles.py` and `verify_necklace_pebbles.py` use only Python's standard library. Run them with `python verify_necklace_profiles.py` and `python verify_necklace_pebbles.py` from the extracted package directory; their stdout is recorded in the corresponding `.out` files. The first checks 1,092 finite directed coronas against the five two-variable atomic extension profiles, 30 Burnside necklace enumerations, and 112 local recycled-anchor child-count witnesses. The second performs exact unrestricted-depth fixed-point type refinement for 135 test words at two variables, 52 test words at three variables, and eight selected four-variable comparisons. The observed five and 26 type classes in these respective finite samples agree with the formulas. These finite checks do **not** prove statements for unbounded cycle lengths or branching numbers; those follow only from the preceding arguments.

## Relationship to prior work
Brown and Hoshino (2007) solve ordinary finite-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé comparison for undirected paths and cycles. Their complete cycle results concern quantifier-rank bounds, not a fixed number of variable names, directed arcs, or attached leaf multiplicity words. Dawar and Holm (2012) establish the general pebble-game framework for finite-variable logic; the present proof specializes that general technique to an exact cyclic-necklace invariant. General results about unary-function structures with counting quantifiers distinguish many more cases and do not yield the present counting-free equivalence directly. No equivalent or stronger published classification of the precise directed leaf-covered family was identified in the checked literature or research records. This is a qualified priority assessment, not proof of historical uniqueness.

## Limitations
The theorem does not cover disconnected functional digraphs, core vertices with zero pendant children, trees of depth greater than one, undirected sunlet graphs, a unary-function signature, counting logic, or bounded-quantifier-rank spectra. The model-theoretic proof is self-contained but has not been independently audited. It remains possible that a more general theorem in the literature implies the statement under different terminology.
