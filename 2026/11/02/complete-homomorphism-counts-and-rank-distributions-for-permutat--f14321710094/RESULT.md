# Complete homomorphism counts and rank distributions for permutation evolution algebras

## Finding
Let \(q\) be an arbitrary prime power and let \(\mathbf m=(m_1,\ldots,m_r)\) and \(\mathbf n=(n_1,\ldots,n_s)\) be nonempty finite lists of positive integers. Define the evolution algebras \(E_{\mathbf m}\) and \(E_{\mathbf n}\) in the next section. Put
\[
\gamma_q(n)=\gcd(q-1,2^n-1),\qquad A_j=\sum_{i:\,m_i\mid n_j}m_i.
\]
Then the complete number of \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear (not necessarily unital, injective, or surjective) algebra homomorphisms is
\[
\boxed{\big|\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb F_q\text{-alg}}(E_{\mathbf m},E_{\mathbf n})\big|=\prod_{j=1}^{s}\big(1+\gamma_q(n_j)A_j\big).}
\]
Moreover, the complete rank-generating polynomial is obtained in the ring
\(B=\mathbb Z[z_1,\ldots,z_r]/(z_1^2-z_1,\ldots,z_r^2-z_r)\) from
\[
Q(\mathbf z)=\prod_{j=1}^{s}\left(1+\gamma_q(n_j)\sum_{i:\,m_i\mid n_j}m_i z_i\right).
\]
Write its unique multilinear expansion as \(\sum_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}}c_S\prod_{i\in S}z_i\). Then
\[
\boxed{\sum_{\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom}(E_{\mathbf m},E_{\mathbf n})}t^{\operatorname{rank}\varphi}=\sum_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}}c_S\,t^{\sum_{i\in S}m_i}.}
\]
In particular, a nonzero homomorphism exists if and only if at least one source cycle length divides a target cycle length. These conclusions hold in every characteristic, including two.

## Assumptions and scope
The source \(E_{\mathbf m}\) is the vector space over \(\mathbb F_q\) with natural basis \(e_{i,a}\), \(1\le i\le r\), \(a\in\mathbb Z/m_i\mathbb Z\), and multiplication \(e_{i,a}e_{i,a}=e_{i,a+1}\), while all products of distinct natural basis elements vanish. The target \(E_{\mathbf n}\) has basis \(f_{j,b}\), \(1\le j\le s\), \(b\in\mathbb Z/n_j\mathbb Z\), subject to the analogous equations. These are finite-dimensional commutative, usually nonassociative, perfect evolution algebras with permutation structure matrices; repeated cycle lengths denote distinct labeled summands. Rank is ordinary \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear map rank. Homomorphisms preserve the indicated product, without a unit condition.

## Proof
**Single target-cycle support lemma.** Consider first a homomorphism \(\varphi:E_{\mathbf m}\to E_{(n)}\), and write \(v_{i,a}=\varphi(e_{i,a})\). Because the target's squaring map sends a coefficient vector \((c_b)\) to \((c_{b-1}^{2})\), its support is translated by one, and the map has trivial kernel: a vector whose square is zero must be zero, as Frobenius is injective in a field. Thus \(v_{i,a+1}=v_{i,a}^{2}\) implies that all images along a given source cycle are either zero or nonzero. For distinct source basis elements, the homomorphism property requires \(v_{i,a}v_{k,b}=0\), so their supports are disjoint: in this target, distinct coordinate products vanish and the target structure matrix is invertible, forcing all coordinatewise products to vanish.

Fix a source cycle of length \(m\) whose image is nonzero, and let \(S\subseteq\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) be the support of \(v_{i,0}\). Its subsequent supports are \(S+a\) for \(0\le a<m\), pairwise disjoint, and \(v_{i,m}=v_{i,0}\) implies \(S+m=S\). Every orbit of translation by \(m\) in \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) has size \(n/g\), where \(g=\gcd(m,n)\), and \(S\) is a union of such orbits. If \(g<m\), then \(S+g=S\) because the subgroups generated by \(g\) and \(m\) agree in \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\); this contradicts pairwise disjointness at shift \(g\). Hence \(g=m\), or \(m\mid n\). In this case the orbits are precisely the residue classes modulo \(m\). Since the \(m\) translated supports are pairwise disjoint, \(S\) consists of exactly one residue class modulo \(m\): it cannot contain two classes, as their translates would overlap. Consequently the images of that source cycle collectively have full support in the target cycle.

Choose the residue class \(h\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\) of \(S\) and the nonzero coefficient \(\lambda\) of \(f_{h}\) in \(v_{i,0}\). Repeated squaring determines every other coefficient uniquely. The target cycle closes after \(n\) squarings, so closure requires \(\lambda^{2^n}=\lambda\), i.e. \(\lambda^{2^n-1}=1\). Conversely, each such \(\lambda\) together with \(h\) yields the explicit map
\[
\varphi(e_{i,a})=\sum_{k=0}^{n/m-1}\lambda^{2^{a+km}} f_{h+a+km\pmod n},\qquad 0\le a<m,
\]
and all other source cycles mapping to zero. The closure and zero cross-products are immediate from \(\lambda^{2^n-1}=1\) and residue-class disjointness. The number of nonzero \(\lambda\) is \(\gamma_q(n)\), since \(\mathbb F_q^\times\) is cyclic of order \(q-1\). Thus a fixed source cycle of length \(m\mid n\) contributes exactly \(m\gamma_q(n)\) possible nonzero components.

No two different source cycles can both map nontrivially into this target cycle: a nonzero first cycle collectively occupies every target coordinate, whereas all images of distinct source basis vectors must have disjoint supports. Therefore the component into each target cycle is uniquely either zero or one of the nonzero maps just classified.

**Arbitrary target.** Projection to each of the \(s\) target summands is an algebra homomorphism, and independently chosen target components reassemble uniquely into a homomorphism because distinct target summands annihilate each other. Multiplying the \(1+\gamma_q(n_j)A_j\) independent choices gives the first displayed formula.

**Rank refinement.** If some target component selects source cycle \(i\), the images of that cycle's \(m_i\) basis elements in the selected target have pairwise disjoint nonempty supports; hence they are linearly independent. Different source cycles have images supported in disjoint target summands, since each target summand selects at most one source cycle. Therefore the full image rank is exactly the sum of \(m_i\) over the source cycles selected by at least one target. Recording selection of cycle \(i\) by \(z_i\), and reducing \(z_i^k\) to \(z_i\) for \(k\ge1\), proves the rank-generating formula. The nonzero criterion follows from the product formula. No step assumes any restriction on \(\operatorname{char}\mathbb F_q\).

## Verification
The standalone standard-library program `verify.py` independently enumerates every \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear matrix in 24 small configurations (including \(\mathbb F_4\)), directly checks the defining products in the algebras and computes matrix ranks by finite-field Gaussian elimination. It compares the resulting rank histogram with a separate target-choice dynamic program implementing the closed formula. Invocation: `python3 verify.py`. All 24 independent exhaustive finite cases passed with output `VERIFY_OK 24 independent exhaustive cases`. For example, over \(\mathbb F_4\), the homomorphisms \(E_{(1)}\to E_{(2)}\) have rank histogram \(\{0:1,1:3\}\), while the reverse direction over \(\mathbb F_2\) has only the zero map. The unrestricted claim relies on the preceding proof, not finite exhaustion.

## Relationship to prior work
Narkuziyev's 2018 paper, `arXiv:1803.11415v1`, studies absolute nilpotents, idempotents and cycle-summand decompositions of evolution algebras associated with two permutations; Section 4 addresses isomorphisms of those algebras. Its 2020 related preprint `arXiv:2005.05793v1` likewise examines idempotents and cycle decompositions. These works motivate examining permutation-cycle algebras but their inspected theorems do not give all noninvertible algebra homomorphisms between arbitrary pairs or an exact rank distribution. Wei and Zou's `arXiv:2310.12368v1` / `doi:10.1016/j.laa.2023.06.006` counts **isomorphism classes** of idempotent evolution algebras over finite fields, based on invertible monomial changes of basis. The present classification deliberately includes noninvertible, nonmonomial homomorphisms: for example, a one-cycle mapping diagonally into a two-cycle. Costoya, Mayorga and Viruel's `arXiv:2207.01335v1` studies automorphism groups of Cayley evolution algebras, not the complete Hom sets in this statement. Searches under the alternatives “permutation evolution algebra”, “cycle evolution algebra homomorphism”, “endomorphism monoid”, and “finite-field rank enumerator” did not locate the same complete formula; lack of a search hit is not proof of publication priority.

## Limitations
The theorem covers only permutation structure matrices with every nonzero structure coefficient normalized to one. It does not classify homomorphisms for general invertible structure matrices, weighted permutation matrices, or evolution algebras with branching support. An earlier general theorem on all homomorphisms of monomial or cycle evolution algebras might imply this formula after specialization; publication priority has not been independently established. The self-review is not independent mathematical certification.
