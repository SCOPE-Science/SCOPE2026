# Complete intrinsic eccentricity and a Voronoi transition on rectangular flat Klein bottles

## Finding

Let \(K_{L,w}\) be the rectangular flat Klein bottle defined below. For every \(L,w>0\), the intrinsic farthest-point distance from a source at transverse coordinate \(u\in[0,w/2]\) is
\[
 e(u)=\begin{cases}
 \sqrt{w^2+\left(\dfrac{L^2-4u(w-u)}{2L}\right)^2},&4u(w-u)\le L^2,\\[5pt]
 (w-u)+\dfrac{L^2}{4(w-u)},&4u(w-u)\ge L^2.
 \end{cases}
\]
The two branches agree when \(4u(w-u)=L^2\). Eccentricity is strictly decreasing from \(u=0\) to \(u=w/2\), with the known diameter and newly classified intrinsic Chebyshev radius
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(K_{L,w})=\sqrt{w^2+L^2/4},\qquad
 \operatorname{rad}(K_{L,w})=\frac{L^2+w^2}{2\max\{L,w\}}.
\]
Exactly two one-sided closed-geodesic circles consist of sources of maximal eccentricity, while the intrinsic Chebyshev centers form the single two-sided central closed-geodesic circle.

For \(w>L\), set
\[
 u_* =\frac{w-\sqrt{w^2-L^2}}{2},\quad b_*=2u_*,\quad c_*=2w-b_*.
\]
The source Voronoi cell is a hexagon for \(0<u<u_*\), a quadrilateral at \(u=u_*\), and a hexagon for \(u_*<u\le w/2\), away from endpoint-specific identifications. The eccentricity has an actual corner at this transition:
\[
 e'_-(u_*)=0,\qquad e'_+(u_*)=-\frac{c_*-b_*}{c_*}<0.
\]
When \(w\le L\), this interior transition is absent. The two-parameter eccentricity phase diagram and its optimizing center loci are the result; the diameter formula itself is prior work.

## Assumptions and scope

For \(L,w>0\), consider the complete Euclidean plane modulo the free group generated by the glide reflection \(g(x,y)=(x+L,-y)\) and translation \(h(x,y)=(x,y+2w)\). The resulting compact smooth flat Klein bottle has area \(2Lw\). Distances are intrinsic quotient lengths, equivalently the minimum Euclidean distances to deck images; no ambient three-dimensional embedding is assumed.

Horizontal translations, reflection in a horizontal axis, and deck transformations allow the source to be represented by \(p=(0,u)\), uniquely up to isometry with \(0\le u\le w/2\). Eccentricity is \(\max_{q\in K_{L,w}}d(p,q)\). The intrinsic Chebyshev radius is \(\min_p\max_q d(p,q)\), not the radius of an extrinsic embedding or a minimal enclosing ball in \(\mathbb R^3\).

## Proof

**1. Orbit and exact Voronoi cell.** Translate the lift of \(p=(0,u)\) to the Euclidean origin. Write \(b=2u\in[0,w]\), \(c=2w-b\), and \(T=bc\). The images of this lift under the deck group, relative to it, form the orbit
\[
 \mathcal O=\{(2jL,2kw),\ ((2j+1)L,2kw-b):j,k\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]
The source Voronoi cell \(C\) is the set of Euclidean points closest to \((0,0)\). Comparison with neighboring even sites gives \(|x|\le L\), \(|y|\le w\). Within this rectangle, the closest odd-column sites are at horizontal coordinates \(\pm L\) and vertical levels \(-b\) or \(c\); all farther sites have no stronger inequality. Thus \(C\) is exactly the compact convex polygon
\[
 \begin{split}
 |x|&\le L,\qquad |y|\le w,\\
 L|x|-by&\le (L^2+b^2)/2,\\
 L|x|+cy&\le (L^2+c^2)/2.
 \end{split}
\]
All quotient points have representatives in \(C\), and for \((x,y)\in C\) their distance from \(p\) is \(\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\). Therefore the eccentricity is the maximum Euclidean norm over \(C\). Convexity of the squared norm reduces this maximum to polygon vertices.

**2. Vertex classification for \(T\le L^2\).** Put \(x_0=(L^2-T)/(2L)\) and \(x_*=(L^2+T)/(2L)\). The polygon vertices, with duplicates ignored at parameter boundaries, are
\[
 (\pm x_0,\pm w),\qquad (\pm x_*,w-b).
\]
Indeed, they arise from the top and bottom horizontal edges and the intersection of the two oblique edges, while the inequalities \(0\le x_0\le x_*\le L\) follow from \(T\le L^2\). Each vertex has squared norm
\[
 w^2+x_0^2=(w-b)^2+x_*^2.
\]
Hence \(e(u)=\sqrt{w^2+x_0^2}\). At \(T=L^2\), the vertices coalesce to \((0,\pm w)\) and \((\pm L,w-b)\), making a quadrilateral.

**3. Vertex classification for \(T\ge L^2\).** Necessarily \(b,c>0\). Define
\[
 R_b=\frac{L^2+b^2}{2b},\qquad R_c=\frac{L^2+c^2}{2c}.
\]
The vertices are
\[
 (0,-R_b),\ (0,R_c),\quad
 (\pm L,R_b-b),\ (\pm L,c-R_c),
\]
again merging coincidences at equality. The squared norms at these vertices are respectively \(R_b^2\) and \(R_c^2\). Moreover
\[
 R_c-R_b=\frac{(c-b)(bc-L^2)}{2bc}\ge0,
\]
so the farthest distance is \(R_c=(L^2+c^2)/(2c)\). This establishes the formula for all parameters and all source points, including the transition and degeneracies.

**4. Monotonicity, centers, and transition.** On \([0,w/2]\), \(T=4u(w-u)\) increases strictly for \(u<w/2\). In the first regime \(L^2-T\ge0\), hence eccentricity strictly decreases until its branch endpoint. In the second regime \(e=(L^2+c^2)/(2c)\) and \(c=2(w-u)\); since \(c^2\ge bc\ge L^2\), its derivative is
\[
 \frac{de}{du}=-1+\frac{L^2}{c^2}\le0,
\]
strictly for \(u<w/2\). Thus the maximal source eccentricity is at \(u=0\), and the minimum precisely at \(u=w/2\). Inserting these endpoints gives the displayed diameter and Chebyshev radius. In the quotient the sources \(u=0\) are the two separate one-sided geodesic circles \(y=0\) and \(y=w\), while the minimizing \(u=w/2\) is a single two-sided geodesic circle (the representatives \(y=\pm w/2\) are identified by the glide). When \(w>L\), the interior equation \(4u(w-u)=L^2\) has the unique root \(u_*\) above in \((0,w/2)\). The left derivative vanishes because \(x_0\to0\); the right derivative equals \(-1+L^2/c_*^2=-(c_*-b_*)/c_*\), which is negative because \(b_*<c_*\). At this point the six vertices merge to four and re-split as six.

## Verification

The included `verify_flat_klein_eccentricity.py` uses only Python's standard library and reconstructs every polygon vertex by exact rational intersections of all eight halfplanes, independently of the analytical vertex lists. It passed 704 exact rational dimension/source combinations (including a nonendpoint exact four-vertex transition), checking each maximal squared norm against the piecewise formula. It also passed 25,000 seeded direct orbit-nearest-distance checks. Run `python3 verify_flat_klein_eccentricity.py`. These are finite corroborations; the preceding proof establishes the universal quantifiers.

## Relationship to prior work

Conway–Rossetti (2003, Section 9) explicitly determine the diameter of the Klein bottle using an orbit-lattice rectangle. Their translatable rectangular tiling for arbitrary sources need not be the true Voronoi tiling and therefore does not give the complete source eccentricity, minimizing center circle, or aspect-ratio transition established here. The known diameter is not asserted new. Davis–Recio-Mitter (2019, Section 3 and Theorem 4.8) give explicit source Voronoi polygon vertices for a fixed unit-square Klein metric and higher-dimensional analogues, which covers a restricted fixed-shape portion of the method and can imply its restricted farthest-point radius. A Euclidean anisotropic scaling does not preserve intrinsic metric distances; their unit-square formulas cannot be transported to all ratios \(w/L\) as isometries. In particular their fixed unit-square model does not exhibit the \(w>L\) Voronoi four-vertex bifurcation. El Mir–Yassine study systolic and conformal inequalities, and Hubard–Kaluža–de Mesmay–Tancer analyze shortest-path embeddings; neither supplies this full rectangular source-eccentricity classification. The distinct previously obtained Möbius-band diameter calculation is also not a theorem about Klein-bottle source eccentricities.

## Limitations

Only orthogonally rectangular flat Klein metrics with the specified deck-group generators are covered; tilted or nonflat metrics are not treated. A two-parameter explicit formula is not a general flat-quotient covering-radius theorem. This is a mathematical self-review, not independent verification or historical-priority certification. Fixed-square Voronoi vertices and the global diameter are prior knowledge; unindexed older derivations of arbitrary-aspect eccentricity remain possible.
