# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

A monic polynomial of degree at least \(3p\) gives independent uniform quadratic Hasse jets at each prime-field residue because the respective cubic local factors are comaximal. Exact Taylor expansion modulo \(p^3\) gives only the outcomes \(0,1,p,2p,p^2\), with an explicit, exhaustive finite-field quadratic census. The all-prime, including \(p=2\), polynomial law follows by multiplication of independent local generating functions. Its moments and asymptotics are proved by direct algebra and rare-event bounds. A standalone independent full enumeration corroborated the dyadic boundary. No finite check is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge; residual risk)

The most directly comparable general published result, Monagan--Tuncer (2016), states first and second moments for monic polynomials modulo arbitrary integers, but notes that it does not obtain the full probabilities. Simhi (2026, arXiv:2610.29911) studies local statistics modulo prime squares. Related findings on monic prime-square and nonzero quadratic prime-square laws do not cover cubed moduli. Caruso (2022) treats roots in p-adic fields rather than residue roots, though that full article was not exhaustively inspected. Searches for prime-cube pgfs, quadratic local jets, monic degree stabilization, and moment aliases returned no covering research record. Unindexed implicit priority remains possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The complete finite root-count distribution is a natural arithmetic invariant beyond previously known first and second moments. Prime-cube lifting introduces a new quadratic singular branch and a \(p^2\)-root event. This explains why a Poisson(1) weak limit coexists with an exact third moment asymptotic \(5p\) and higher moments of order \(p^{2k-5}\). The claim covers every prime, including characteristic two, in a uniform stable-degree regime, while making no unsupported optimal-threshold claim.
