# Complete quartic harmonic interpolation defects on signed constant-weight spherical orbits

## Finding

For integers \(n\ge 8\) and \(3\le k\le n-3\), let
\[
X_{n,k}=\{x\in S^{n-1}:x_i\in\{0,\pm k^{-1/2}\},\ \#\{i:x_i\ne0\}=k\}.
\]
It is the normalized orbit of a \(k\)-sparse vector under signed coordinate permutations; it contains \(2^k\binom nk\) points. Let \(\mathcal H_4(\mathbb R^n)\) denote the real homogeneous polynomials of degree four annihilated by the ordinary Euclidean Laplacian. Write \(E_{n,k}f=(f(x))_{x\in X_{n,k}}\) for their evaluation map. The exact nullity is
\[
\boxed{\dim\ker E_{n,k}=\binom n2+
\mathbf1_{\{k=3\}}\binom n4+
\mathbf1_{\{n=3k-4\}}\binom n2+
\mathbf1_{\{n=3k-2\}}+
\mathbf1_{\{n=6k-4\}}(n-1).}
\]
In particular, since \(\dim\mathcal H_4(\mathbb R^n)=\binom n4+n\binom n2\), the rank is this dimension minus the displayed nullity. The three arithmetic resonance conditions are mutually exclusive in the stated domain. Thus the theorem classifies all quartic spherical harmonics invisible to these samples, including the generic \(\binom n2\)-dimensional obstruction and every exceptional rank loss.

Explicit components of the kernel are as follows. For each \(i<j\), the harmonic quartic \(x_ix_j(x_i^2-x_j^2)\) always vanishes. If \(k=3\), each squarefree quartic \(x_ix_jx_ax_b\) in four distinct indices also vanishes. If \(n=3k-4\), an additional vanishing harmonic quartic exists for each pair:
\[
 x_ix_j\left[(n-2)(x_i^2+x_j^2)-6\sum_{\ell\notin\{i,j\}}x_\ell^2\right].
\]
If \(n=3k-2\), the additional one-dimensional kernel is generated by
\[
\sum_i x_i^4-\frac{3}{n+2}\left(\sum_i x_i^2\right)^2.
\]
If \(n=6k-4\), an additional \((n-1)\)-dimensional kernel is spanned by all differences \(P_i-P_j\), where
\[
P_i=x_i^4-\frac{6}{n+4}x_i^2\sum_\ell x_\ell^2.
\]
The proof below shows that no other invisible quartic harmonic exists.

## Assumptions and scope

All scalar spaces and ranks are over \(\mathbb R\). The sphere has radius one, so every point in the displayed orbit has exactly \(k\) nonzero coordinates of squared magnitude \(1/k\). Both choices of each nonzero coordinate's sign are present independently. The evaluation map uses *exact homogeneous degree four*, not the band of all degrees at most four, and the Laplacian is \(\Delta=\sum_i\partial_i^2\), not the Boolean-slice lowering operator. The restricted range \(3\le k\le n-3\) guarantees that the fixed-size subset-sum arguments below are nondegenerate. Arbitrary positive weights on these same sampling points do not change evaluation rank.

## Proof

**1. A fixed-size subset lemma.** Suppose real numbers \(u_1,\ldots,u_m\) have the same sum on all \(r\)-element subsets, with \(1\le r\le m-1\). Fix two indices and swap them in a set containing exactly one: their numbers must agree. Thus all \(u_i\) are equal. A related incidence assertion will be used: for \(2\le r\le m-2\), the functions \(\mathbf1_{\{i,j\}\subset S}\), indexed by unordered pairs \(\{i,j\}\), are linearly independent on all \(r\)-subsets \(S\subset[m]\). Indeed, if \(\sum_{\{i,j\}\subset S}b_{ij}=0\), compare \(S=T\cup\{a\}\) and \(S'=T\cup\{b\}\) with \(|T|=r-1\) disjoint from \(\{a,b\}\). Then \(\sum_{t\in T}(b_{at}-b_{bt})=0\) for every such \(T\); the first lemma implies \(b_{at}=b_{bt}\) for all \(t\notin\{a,b\}\). Symmetry forces all \(b_{ij}\) to be equal, and the original equation forces that common value to be zero.

**2. Decompose by coordinate-sign parity.** Each degree-four monomial has an even set of variables occurring with odd exponent, of size zero, two, or four. The Laplacian preserves each parity type. On every fixed support of \(k\) coordinates, independent sign averaging orthogonally distinguishes these types and their index sets; therefore vanishing of a sum of harmonic components is equivalent to vanishing within each parity-index sector. The harmonic dimension can be counted within these sectors: \(\binom n4\) for parity four, \(n-1\) for every two-index parity sector, and \(\binom n2\) for the all-even sector. Their total is \(\binom n4+n\binom n2\).

**3. Four-odd-coordinate sectors.** The only degree-four polynomial with odd variables exactly \(i,j,a,b\) is \(x_ix_jx_ax_b\), which is harmonic. It vanishes on every orbit point precisely when \(k=3\); if \(k\ge4\), a support containing the four indices makes it nonzero. There is no cancellation across the distinct sign-parity characters. Thus this sector contributes exactly \(\mathbf1_{\{k=3\}}\binom n4\).

**4. Two-odd-coordinate sectors.** Fix \(i<j\). Every degree-four polynomial in their sign-parity sector is uniquely
\[
 x_ix_j\left(a x_i^2+b x_j^2+\sum_{\ell\notin\{i,j\}}c_\ell x_\ell^2\right).
\]
The Laplacian vanishes if and only if \(3(a+b)+\sum_{\ell\notin\{i,j\}}c_\ell=0\): directly, the Laplacian of \(x_i^3x_j\) is \(6x_ix_j\), and that of \(x_ix_jx_\ell^2\) is \(2x_ix_j\). For orbit points supported on \(\{i,j\}\cup T\), where \(|T|=k-2\), evaluation vanishes if and only if
\[
a+b+\sum_{\ell\in T}c_\ell=0\quad\text{for every }T\subset[n]\setminus\{i,j\},\ |T|=k-2.
\]
The subset-sum lemma applies because \(1\le k-2\le n-4\). Hence \(c_\ell=c\) for all external indices, and \(a+b=-(k-2)c\). Substituting into harmonicity yields \((n-3k+4)c=0\). The free difference \(a-b\) always gives exactly one null direction; at \(n=3k-4\), the scalar \(c\) is also free and gives precisely one additional direction. These are exactly the two displayed pair-dependent harmonic polynomials (up to scalar), and there are \(\binom n2\) independent sign sectors.

**5. All-even sector.** Write \(R=\sum_jx_j^2\) and \(y_i=kx_i^2\in\{0,1\}\) on the orbit. Then \(\sum_i y_i=k\), and the evaluations of homogeneous even quartic monomials \(x_i^4\) and \(x_i^2x_j^2\), after multiplying by \(k^2\), are respectively \(y_i\) and \(y_iy_j\). Since \((k-1)y_i=\sum_{j\ne i}y_iy_j\) on the slice, their evaluation space is the span of the pair functions \(y_iy_j\). By the incidence assertion in step 1, this space has dimension exactly \(\binom n2\). The original all-even quartic polynomial space has dimension \(n+\binom n2\), so its evaluation kernel has dimension \(n\), with basis
\[
Q_i=x_i^4-\frac1k x_i^2R,\quad i=1,\ldots,n.
\]
Indeed, each \(Q_i\) vanishes because \(x_i^4=k^{-1}x_i^2\) and \(R=1\) on the orbit, and the \(Q_i\) are linearly independent: the squarefree pair coefficients together with \(k\ge3\) force this, or equivalently compare the coefficient of each \(x_i^4\).

It remains to find which linear combinations of these \(Q_i\) are *harmonic*. Direct differentiation gives
\[
\Delta Q_i=\left(12-\frac{2(n+4)}k\right)x_i^2-\frac2kR.
\]
On coefficients \(c=(c_1,\ldots,c_n)\), the Laplacian map from the span of \(Q_i\) into the span of \(x_j^2\) acts as \(\left(12-2(n+4)/k\right)I-(2/k){\bf1}{\bf1}^{\mathsf T}\). On the constants line, its eigenvalue is \(4(3k-n-2)/k\); on the sum-zero hyperplane, the eigenvalue is \(2(6k-n-4)/k\). Thus the even harmonic kernel contributes exactly one dimension if \(n=3k-2\), exactly \(n-1\) dimensions if \(n=6k-4\), and zero otherwise. At the first resonance the constant combination of the \(Q_i\) is proportional to \(\sum_i x_i^4-3R^2/(n+2)\). At the second resonance the differences \(Q_i-Q_j\) equal the displayed \(P_i-P_j\) because \(1/k=6/(n+4)\).

**6. Sum and exhaust.** The parity sectors are independent by step 2 and exhaustive by monomial parity. Adding the nullities from steps 3–5 proves the boxed formula and the rank identity. For \(k\ge3\), the equalities \(n=3k-4\), \(n=3k-2\), and \(n=6k-4\) cannot hold simultaneously. The domain and equality boundary cases have been included in every preceding step.

## Verification

The standalone Python 3 standard-library checker `artifacts/check_rank.py` performs independent exact modular row reduction on sampled integer-scaled harmonic bases separated by sign parity. It checks the all-even evaluation block using the \(\binom n2\) basis quartics \(6x_i^2x_j^2-x_i^4-x_j^4\); the two-odd-coordinate block using the \(n-2\) basis quartics \(x_ix_j(x_i^2+x_j^2-6x_\ell^2)\) together with its forced zero direction; and the four-odd-coordinate block by direct monomial nonvanishing. The reductions use two different prime fields and require no floating-point tolerance. Eleven parameter choices test both generic cases and all exceptional mechanisms, giving 22 successful exact modular rank comparisons. This is finite corroboration; the proof of the universal theorem is the algebraic argument in steps 1–6. Execute with `python3 artifacts/check_rank.py` after extracting that file.

Selected examples are \((n,k)=(8,3)\) with nullity \(98\), \((8,4)\) with nullity \(56\), \((10,4)\) with nullity \(46\), and \((14,3)\) with nullity \(1105\). No extrapolation from these examples is required or claimed.

## Relationship to prior work

Bajnok's investigation of generalized hyperoctahedra and their Euclidean-design strengths [1] establishes the classical signed-orbit setting and uses harmonic-polynomial moment conditions. In contrast, a single orbit's **evaluation nullspace for every quartic harmonic** is not determined by an invariant-moment condition: the latter sees only the group-invariant subspace and cannot detect the permanent pairwise modes or the additional nontrivial parity-sector resonance. Sawa and Xu [2] study invariant positive cubature nodes on spheres, a different moment-exactness problem. Filmus [3] develops Boolean-slice harmonic bases and associated incidence identities, which support the combinatorial part of this proof but use a different notion of harmonicity from the Euclidean Laplacian. A published spectral classification of entire nonbinary Hamming Fourier bands [4] is broader at the level of *all low-interaction coordinate functions*, yet does not directly give the intersection of a fixed degree-four Euclidean harmonic subspace with its shell kernel or the three stated arithmetic resonance dimensions. Thus this claim is a refined restriction-rank classification, not a claim that all of its ingredients are novel.

## Limitations

The theorem treats the exact fourth spherical-harmonic degree only, with the indicated interior values of \(n,k\). It does not classify other harmonic degrees, interpolation condition numbers, or general weighted and nonsymmetric point sets. It gives exact rank and nullity rather than the nonzero singular-value spectrum. The prior-literature comparison is bounded: an equivalent rank classification could exist under the language of hyperoctahedral representation theory, harmonic-polynomial ideals, or interpolation on ternary constant-weight slices. Its presence cannot be ruled out by unsuccessful searches. Independent mathematical assessment has not been performed.
