# Complete rational dihedral and layer-exchange homology of two-point prism configurations

## Finding
For each integer \(n\ge5\), let \(P_n=C_n\square K_2\) be the circular-prism graph and \(X_n=\operatorname{UConf}_2(|P_n|)\) the space of **unordered**, pairwise distinct points on its geometric realization. The graph automorphisms generated by cyclic column rotation \(r\), column reflection \(s\), and layer exchange \(t\) form \(D_n\times C_2\), with \(r^n=s^2=t^2=1\), \(srs=r^{-1}\), and \(t\) central.

Write \(\varepsilon\) for the one-dimensional representation of \(D_n\) on which \(r\) acts as \(+1\) and \(s\) acts as \(-1\); write \(\tau\) for the nontrivial character of the layer-exchange \(C_2\). Let \(L_n\cong\mathbb Q^n\) be the permutation representation of \(D_n\) on the \(n\) rim edges \(\{i,i+1\}\) of a single rim cycle. Let \(U_n=\ker(L_n\xrightarrow{\sum}\mathbb Q)\). Then the **complete rational homology representation** is
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_0(X_n;\mathbb Q)&\cong\mathbf1,\\
H_1(X_n;\mathbb Q)&\cong\bigl(\varepsilon\boxtimes(\mathbf1\oplus\tau)\bigr)\oplus\bigl((\varepsilon\otimes L_n)\boxtimes\tau\bigr),\\
H_2(X_n;\mathbb Q)&\cong\bigl((\bigwedge^2 U_n\ominus\varepsilon)\boxtimes\mathbf1\bigr)\oplus(\mathbf1\boxtimes\tau),\\
H_i(X_n;\mathbb Q)&=0\quad(i\ge3).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\boxtimes\) denotes an external tensor product; \(\ominus\varepsilon\) means **deleting one actual sign summand**, not asserting negative multiplicity. Indeed \(\bigwedge^2U_n\) contains \(\varepsilon\) with multiplicity \(\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\ge2\). The decomposition is of rational group representations, not a claimed canonical choice of complementary subspace.

The integral homology is torsion-free with Betti numbers
\[
(b_0,b_1,b_2)=(1,n+2,\tbinom{n-1}{2}).
\]
For rational homology of symmetry quotients, \(X_n/D_n\) has \(b_0=1,b_1=0,b_2=1\), whereas \(X_n/(D_n\times C_2)\) has \(b_0=1\) and \(b_i=0\) for all \(i>0\). The rotation quotient \(X_n/C_n\) has \((b_0,b_1,b_2)=(1,3,\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor)\). These assertions hold for all integers \(n\ge5\), not just the tested small cases.

## Assumptions and scope
Vertices of \(P_n\) are \((i,a)\), \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\), \(a\in\{0,1\}\); edges are horizontal rim edges between consecutive \(i\)'s and vertical rung edges. On vertices, \(r(i,a)=(i+1,a)\), \(s(i,a)=(-i,a)\), \(t(i,a)=(i,1-a)\). The graph is embedded as a convex \(n\)-prism in the two-sphere, with two \(n\)-gonal cap faces and \(n\) quadrilateral lateral faces. All singular homology uses integral or rational coefficients as displayed, and the ordinary quotient spaces use the indicated graph automorphism groups (no freeness of these actions is assumed).

Abrams' unordered cubical two-particle model consists of unordered pairs of disjoint closed graph cells, and deformation retracts \(X_n\), since the graph meets the two-particle subdivision conditions: essential vertices are separated by at least one edge and each nontrivial cycle uses at least three edges. Its cellular chain groups have oriented bases \(C_0\) (unordered vertex pairs), \(C_1\) (oriented edge and nonincident vertex), and \(C_2\) (unordered pairs of disjoint oriented edges, oriented by their ordered square coordinates).

## Proof
**1. First homology and its orientation character.** The prism is three-connected and planar, with \(2n\) vertices, \(3n\) edges and \(n+2\) faces; its first graph Betti number is \(n+1\). The established first-homology theorem for braid groups of three-connected planar graphs (Ko--Park, Theorem 3.16) gives \(H_1(X_n;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^{n+2}\). The total-motion homomorphism \(H_1(X_n;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(P_n;\mathbb Z)\), formed by adding the paths traversed by the two particles, is equivariant and surjective: every facial cycle can be traversed by one particle while the second sits at a vertex outside that face. Thus its kernel has rank one. The extra generator is the oriented local \(Y\)-exchange class, whose equality up to orientation in a three-connected planar graph is part of the planar first-homology description: its sign changes on reversing the cyclic ordering at a trivalent vertex. The prism symmetries preserve this order under \(r\) and reverse it under either \(s\) or \(t\). Hence the kernel is the orientation character \(\varepsilon\boxtimes\tau\).

For the spherical embedding, the oriented boundaries of the \(n+2\) faces generate \(H_1(P_n;\mathbb Q)\) with the sole relation that their sum is zero. An orientation-reversing spherical symmetry changes all boundary signs. Thus \(H_1(P_n;\mathbb Q)\cong(\varepsilon\boxtimes\tau)\otimes\widetilde{\mathbb Q[F]}\), where \(F\) is the face set and tilde indicates its augmentation submodule. Semisimplicity of rational finite-group representations splits the total-motion exact sequence. Together with the exchange line, this gives \(H_1(X_n;\mathbb Q)\cong(\varepsilon\boxtimes\tau)\otimes\mathbb Q[F]\). On the two caps, \(D_n\) is trivial and \(t\) interchanges them, giving \(\mathbf1\oplus\tau\); on the lateral faces, \(D_n\) permutes the rim edges and \(t\) fixes each face. This proves the displayed first-homology decomposition.

**2. Exact equivariant cellular Euler character.** In the cubical model, direct cell counting gives
\[
(\dim C_0,\dim C_1,\dim C_2)=\left(n(2n-1),\;6n(n-1),\;\frac{9n^2-15n}{2}\right).
\]
For a graph automorphism \(g\), let \(v(g)\) be its number of fixed vertices. Fix arbitrary consistent orientations for edges \(e\) and write \(g(e)=\sigma_g(e)e'\), where \(\sigma_g(e)\in\{+1,-1\}\) and \(e'\) is the corresponding oriented basis edge. The signed traces on cubical chains are explicit finite sums:
\[
\begin{aligned}
T_0(g)&=\#\{\{u,v\}:u\ne v,\;\{gu,gv\}=\{u,v\}\},\\
T_1(g)&=\sum_{e:\,g(e)=\pm e}\sigma_g(e)\bigl(v(g)-\#\{u\in e:gu=u\}\bigr),\\
T_2(g)&=\sum_{\substack{\{e,f\}\text{ disjoint}\\g e=\pm e,\;g f=\pm f}}\sigma_g(e)\sigma_g(f)
-\sum_{\substack{\{e,f\}\text{ disjoint}\\g e=\pm f,\;g f=\pm e}}\sigma_g(e)\sigma_g(f).
\end{aligned}
\]
The second sum in \(T_2\) has a minus sign because interchanging a square's two coordinates reverses its orientation. The sums range over unordered edge pairs and use oriented endpoint images to calculate \(\sigma_g\). They determine all traces without choice of homology generators. In particular the cycle-type enumeration of prism vertices, rim edges and rungs gives the following exhaustive table; a reflection is represented by \(r^a s\), and an omitted power of \(t\) means \(t^0\):

| Group-element case | \(T_0\) | \(T_1\) | \(T_2\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| identity | \(n(2n-1)\) | \(6n(n-1)\) | \((9n^2-15n)/2\) |
| nonidentity \(r^a\) other than half-turn | \(0\) | \(0\) | \(0\) |
| half-turn \(r^{n/2}\), even \(n\) | \(n\) | \(0\) | \(-3n/2\) |
| \(r^as\), odd \(n\) | \(n\) | \(-4\) | \((5-3n)/2\) |
| \(r^as\), even \(n\), even \(a\) | \(n+4\) | \(4\) | \(6-3n/2\) |
| \(r^as\), even \(n\), odd \(a\) | \(n\) | \(0\) | \(8-3n/2\) |
| \(t\) | \(n\) | \(0\) | \(n(n-3)/2\) |
| \(r^at\), \(a\ne0\), not half-turn | \(0\) | \(0\) | \(0\) |
| \(r^{n/2}t\), even \(n\) | \(n\) | \(0\) | \(-3n/2\) |
| \(r^ast\), odd \(n\) | \(n\) | \(0\) | \((1-3n)/2\) |
| \(r^ast\), even \(n\), even \(a\) | \(n\) | \(0\) | \(2-3n/2\) |
| \(r^ast\), even \(n\), odd \(a\) | \(n\) | \(0\) | \(-3n/2\) |

For transparency, every table entry is a direct evaluation of the three stated fixed-cell formulas; for example \(t\) fixes no vertices but exchanges \(n\) pairs of vertices and fixes no oriented one-cells, hence \(T_0(t)=n\) and \(T_1(t)=0\). Among disjoint edge pairs, all \(n(n-1)/2\) pairs of distinct rungs are fixed with positive orientation, but the \(n\) top/bottom rim-edge pairs are interchanged with negative square orientation. Their net trace is \(n(n-1)/2-n=n(n-3)/2=T_2(t)\). The independent program uses the formulas rather than the table.

**3. Second-homology character.** Since this model has no cells above dimension two, and \(H_0\) is trivial one-dimensional, equivariant Euler--Poincaré gives
\[
\chi_{H_2}(g)=T_0(g)-T_1(g)+T_2(g)-1+\chi_{H_1}(g).
\]
Put \(u(g)=\operatorname{tr}(g|U_n)\) and \(w(g)=\operatorname{tr}(g^2|U_n)\). The exterior-square character is \(\chi_{\wedge^2U_n}(g)=(u(g)^2-w(g))/2\). Substitution of the finite cell table and the fixed-face formula for \(H_1\), considering all rotations, both reflection classes and layer exchange, yields the uniform identity, for \(h\in D_n\) and \(c\in\{0,1\}\):
\[
\boxed{\chi_{H_2}(ht^c)=\frac{u(h)^2-u(h^2)}2-\varepsilon(h)+(-1)^c.}
\]
Character orthogonality gives the multiplicity of \(\varepsilon\) in \(\wedge^2 U_n\) as \(\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\). Since finite-group representations over \(\mathbb Q\) are semisimple and their complex characters distinguish rational representations here, the resulting character is realized by the displayed actual rational direct sum \((\wedge^2U_n\ominus\varepsilon)\boxtimes\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf1\boxtimes\tau\).

**4. Integral ranks and quotient consequences.** The integral first homology is torsion-free by the planar Ko--Park theorem. The integral second homology, as a kernel of a homomorphism between free abelian cellular chain groups, is free. Euler characteristic and the first Betti number give \(b_2=\binom{n-1}{2}\). Rational homology of a finite-group quotient agrees with invariant rational homology after equivariant subdivision. Averaging the displayed characters gives \(\dim H_1(X_n/D_n;\mathbb Q)=0\), \(\dim H_2(X_n/D_n;\mathbb Q)=1\), and no positive-degree invariants for \(D_n\times C_2\). Averaging only over rotations gives the remaining formula. This completes the proof.

## Verification
The included code reconstructs every cubical cell and its integral boundary. Run `python3 code/prism_chain.py` for exact integer cell counts and modular rank comparisons for each \(5\le n\le12\) over characteristics \(2,3,5\); run `python3 code/prism_h1_character.py` to compute the actual chain-level homology characters for all \(D_n\times C_2\) elements at \(5\le n\le10\), using an independent quotient-of-chains method modulo \(1009\). The script `code/prism_core.py` implements the signed fixed-cell formulas, and `code/prism_character.py` checks their exterior-square interpretation and subgroup averages at every size from five through eighteen. Finite checks corroborate the general combinatorial trace count but do not replace the universal argument or prove the absence of integral torsion. All scripts use only Python's standard library and are self-contained within this package.

## Relationship to prior work
Ko and Park give the first-homology rank and planar graph-braid relations, not the module under prism symmetries. An and Knudsen prove a general planar second-homology generation theorem; it does not state or evaluate the homology character for cyclic prisms. An and Maciążek's geometric graph-braid presentations likewise concern presentations, not this representation. Barnett and Farber treat **ordered** two-point planar graph configurations and hence do not supply this unordered action. Maciążek and Sawicki study particle-exchange homology, including wheels, but do not state the prism-family modules and quotient contrast.

Other identified related calculations concern the *independence complex* of circular ladders, intrinsic *Čech nerves* of high-girth prisms, *Borel--Moore homology with a sign local system*, and unordered configurations on individual dodecahedral or cuboctahedral graphs. Their conclusions are not the same statements and neither imply the displayed full prism-family homology characters without new group-action calculations. Comparison of these actual claims does not establish uniqueness against unindexed work.

## Limitations
Only **two unordered particles** and \(n\ge5\) are covered. The rational representation decomposition is not an integral-lattice decomposition. No higher-particle statement or integral torsion calculation for symmetry **quotients** is made; quotient statements concern rational homology. The finite checker is corroborative, while the universal proof uses the planar first-homology theorem and explicit symbolic cellular traces. Unindexed equivalent calculations and different parametrizations are remaining literature risks. No independent audit has been performed.
