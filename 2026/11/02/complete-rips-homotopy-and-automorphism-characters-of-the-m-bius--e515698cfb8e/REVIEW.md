The graph is defined by explicit modular adjacencies, and all 65,535 nonempty vertex subsets are screened for the inclusive distance condition. At scale two the face vector is \((16,72,112,48)\). The deterministic 118-pair matching has precisely one critical zero-cell, nine critical two-cells and two critical three-cells. The directed cover graph is verified acyclic; integral elementary cancellations use unit pivots and give the zero Morse differential. The resulting low-dimensional CW space is necessarily the stated sphere wedge because the two-cell complex is simply connected and \(\pi_2\) is freely generated by its nine spheres. At scale three the eight forbidden disjoint pairs produce a cross-polytope boundary. Explicit integral third cycles are checked and span the degree-three homology. All 96 automorphism permutations preserve adjacency; signed simplicial Lefschetz traces and cross-polytope orientations verify every character and quotient invariant. All-scale bars follow because successive positive-degree groups have disjoint dimensions.

## Originality — PASS against inspected sources
The closest theoretical paper, Adams–Frick–Virk (arXiv:2206.08812), establishes a general comparison of homotopy groups of thickenings and complexes, not graph-specific homotopy types. Shukla (arXiv:2202.02756) and Feng (arXiv:2305.07084) calculate hypercube Rips complexes with different graph metrics and do not include \(\operatorname{GP}(8,3)\). Knill (arXiv:2605.30799) directly studies the named graph, but his Lefschetz and geodesic calculations refer to the original graph and are not the scale-two or scale-three Rips characters. The known Čech ball nerve of the same metric graph uses a different simplex condition; no comparison identified a theorem implying these Rips homotopy types or the character. Indexed mathematical searches for the alternate graph names, graph-square clique complex, mixed sphere wedge and automorphism characters likewise revealed no dominating result. Unindexed or unpublished equivalent calculations remain a risk; this review is not a proof of absolute novelty.

## Value — PASS
The Möbius–Kantor graph is a canonical sixteen-vertex cubic symmetric configuration-incidence graph. Its mixed-dimensional Rips wedge, the sharp seven-dimensional jump, and full symmetry quotient barcode are natural topological invariants; neither graph Betti numbers nor the known Čech nerve, nor rational Rips homology ranks alone determine this package. The two simultaneous nonzero degrees at scale two and their very different symmetry-invariant multiplicities make the exact computation useful as a reproducible test case for integral and equivariant persistent topology. No unmotivated parameter slice or claimed general theorem is necessary for this geometric contrast to be substantive.
