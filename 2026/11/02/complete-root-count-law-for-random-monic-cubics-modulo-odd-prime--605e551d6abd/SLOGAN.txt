For a uniformly random monic cubic modulo \(p^3\), every odd prime \(p\) has an exact seven-level law for its number of modular roots, including rare configurations with \(p^2\) distinct roots of probability exactly \(p^{-5}\). The evaluated generating function yields the fourth moment \(p^3+8p^2+2p+4-42/p+28/p^2\).
