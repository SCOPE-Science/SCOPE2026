# Review: complete two-marker shuffle-root multiplicity spectrum

**Correctness — PASS.** A shuffle root has precisely one one, and the proof's feasible root indices form the exact integer interval between the stated endpoints. Exchanging identical copies permits assigning the first and second ones consistently. The bounds derived separately from each of the three zero gaps justify the domain of the stripping bijection. The contraction changes the two endpoints by exactly one and two units, giving the full root-count decrement. An independent interleaving dynamic program agrees for 1,222 two-marker words with half-length at most 12; exhaustive interval enumeration of 671,650 further inputs through half-length 100 matches every tail and extremal formula. The algebraic proof is not dependent on these finite tests.

**Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk.** Grytczuk–Pawlik–Pleszczyński (arXiv:2308.13882), Theorem 3 and Corollary 1, already prove the membership criterion and total count of two-one shuffle squares, exactly the one-root-or-more tail. Datko–Pawlik (2025) study output counts for each root and finite root multiplicities, not this complete inverse degree distribution for every two-marker output. Grytczuk–Pawlik–Ruciński (arXiv:2503.22043), Section 3.1, concerns membership under four/five runs. The new all-\(r\) tail identity, contraction bijection, and residue-dependent counts of maximizers were not found in the inspected relevant text or semantic database comparisons. An unindexed equivalent, or one in less accessible supplementary material, remains possible.

**Value — PASS.** The two-marker slice is the first nonconstant-composition layer of the difficult shuffle-square enumeration problem, and its total count was previously established. The new answer gives the complete inverse multiplicity profile, addressing the separate root-count question directly. Every multiplicity threshold is obtained by a nontrivial three-gap bijection to the known base layer, giving a sharp maximum and exact counts of maximizing words. This identifies structural redundancy invisible from either membership alone or outgoing counts for individual roots.

**Boundaries.** The result neither generalizes to unrestricted binary compositions nor counts different shuffling colorings. Independent scientific validation has not been performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
