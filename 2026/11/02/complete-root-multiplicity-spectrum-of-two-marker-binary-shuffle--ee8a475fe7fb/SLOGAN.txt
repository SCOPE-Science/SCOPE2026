For binary words with exactly two ones, the number of length-\(2n\) words having at least \(r\) distinct shuffle roots is exactly \(H(n-3r+3)\), where \(H(m)=1+3m(m-1)/2\) for \(m\ge1\) and zero otherwise. Deleting two zeros from each of the three gaps decreases the root count by one, giving a sharp maximum of \(\lfloor(n+2)/3\rfloor\) roots and an exact multiplicity distribution.
