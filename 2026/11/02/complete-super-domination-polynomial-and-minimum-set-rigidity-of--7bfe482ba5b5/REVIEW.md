# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The full omission polynomial is proved by an if-and-only-if local private-neighbor test, a disjoint three-case classification and an unambiguous labeled star-component EGF. The stable-range optimizer theorem follows from the number of occupied vertices and components in any star forest. A standalone verifier using direct rook graph adjacency matches all coefficients for seven small boards and checks all \(177100\) relevant six-cell subsets on the five-by-five board. These finite checks support but do not replace the universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

Klein, Rodríguez-Velázquez and Yi's Theorem 30 (arXiv:1705.00928) **already gives the scalar minimum number**, credited here without novelty claim. It does not supply the complete cardinality polynomial or count all minimum configurations. Ghanbari, Jäger and Lehtilä (arXiv:2209.01795) count minima in other graph families. Mertens's 2024 polynomial covers ordinary, not super, domination of rook graphs. A closely relevant Kaidash (2024) chapter on super-domination polynomials was available only by abstract and summarized examples: its full content could not be checked, so definitive priority remains uncertain. The accessible prior claims on other rook domination/visibility variants do not imply this theorem; no equivalent full enumeration was found in targeted mathematical-record queries. An inaccessible plausible paper does not count as a negative full-text inspection.

## Value — PASS

The formula determines every feasible selected-set cardinality for all rectangular rook graphs, while the exact \(m(m-1)n(n-1)\) minimum-set count and two-star classification in the stable range explain which previously known minimum sizes can occur and why their configurations are rigid. The result refines a natural two-parameter extremal graph invariant, rather than recomputing its known scalar value.

## Scope, sources and limitations

For \(m,n\ge2\) the polynomial is exact; the optimizer classification is for \(m,n\ge5\) only. References: arXiv:1705.00928, Theorem 30; arXiv:2209.01795, Section 6; Mertens, JIS 2024, article 24.3.7; Kaidash, DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-73365-9_6, accessible abstract only.
