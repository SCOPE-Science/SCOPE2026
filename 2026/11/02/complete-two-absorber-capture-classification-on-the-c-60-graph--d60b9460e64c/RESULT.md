# Complete two-absorber capture classification on the \(C_{60}\) graph

## Finding
Let \(G\) be the ordinary 60-vertex truncated-icosahedral graph, \(A\) its unweighted adjacency matrix, and \(J>0\). Place irreversible local absorbers at **distinct** vertices \(s,t\), with arbitrary strengths \(\gamma_s,\gamma_t>0\), and evolve a single excitation using
\[
H_{s,t}=JA-i\gamma_s|s\rangle\langle s|-i\gamma_t|t\rangle\langle t|.
\]
Define the bright subspace
\[
\mathcal K_{s,t}=\operatorname{span}\{A^ke_s,A^ke_t:0\le k\le14\},
\qquad r(s,t)=\dim\mathcal K_{s,t}.
\]
Its dimension takes **exactly four values** over all \(\binom{60}{2}=1770\) unordered pairs:

| Bright dimension \(r(s,t)\) | Number of unordered pairs | Eventually absorbed fraction from the maximally mixed state |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 15 | 30 | \(1/4\) |
| 27 | 60 | \(9/20\) |
| 28 | 120 | \(7/15\) |
| 29 | 1560 | \(29/60\) |

This classification has the following **geometric if-and-only-if rule**. Every vertex has a unique antipode at graph distance nine. Rank 15 occurs precisely for antipodal pairs. Let a *spoke edge* be an edge joining the two flags at opposite ends of the same edge in the underlying icosahedron. Rank 27 occurs precisely for a spoke edge or the pair obtained from it by replacing either endpoint with its antipode (30 pairs of each distance one and eight). Rank 28 occurs precisely for distance-five pairs joined by more than one shortest path (two symmetry classes of 60 pairs each, with two and three shortest paths). All other distinct pairs have rank 29. In particular, graph distance alone does not determine absorption efficiency.

For every positive pair of rates and every initial density matrix \(\rho_0\) on the 60-dimensional one-excitation space, the exact long-time total absorption is
\[
P_{\mathrm{abs}}(\rho_0)=\operatorname{tr}(\Pi_{\mathcal K_{s,t}}\rho_0).
\]
Thus a **single** absorber captures \(15/60\) of a maximally mixed excitation. An antipodal second absorber captures no additional fraction for *any* initial state, whereas the optimal two-absorber fraction is \(29/60\), attained by 1560 of the 1770 unordered placements.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is finite, simple, and unweighted. For an explicit combinatorial model, take an icosahedral graph with poles \(p,q\), two rings \(a_i,b_i\), indices modulo five, and edges \(pa_i,qb_i,a_ia_{i+1},b_ib_{i+1},a_ib_i,a_ib_{i-1}\) for \(0\le i<5\). The truncated graph has one vertex \((u,v)\) for each oriented icosahedral edge \(uv\). Connect \((u,v)\) with \((v,u)\), and connect \((u,v)\) with \((u,w)\) whenever \(v,w\) are adjacent icosahedral neighbors of \(u\). This produces 60 vertices, 90 edges, twelve pentagons, and twenty hexagons.

The initial state may be any trace-one positive semidefinite density matrix. The uniform mixture is \(I_{60}/60\). Hopping is homogeneous and Hermitian; the only non-Hermitian terms are the two specified strictly positive local losses. All assertions concern eventual absorption, not its finite-time rate.

## Proof
**1. Finite spectral cutoff.** For the explicitly constructed integer adjacency matrix \(A\), set
\[
\begin{aligned}
P(x)={}&(x-3)(x-1)(x+2)(x^2-x-3)(x^2+x-4)\\
 &\cdot(x^2+x-1)(x^2+3x+1)\\
 &\cdot(x^4-3x^3-2x^2+7x+1).
\end{aligned}
\]
This monic polynomial has degree 15. Direct exact-integer evaluation on **all 60 standard basis vectors** proves \(P(A)=0\). Consequently, every higher adjacency power of either absorber vector is a combination of its first fifteen powers. The one-vertex Krylov rank is exactly 15 for a representative vertex, as verified by rational elimination. The explicit 120-element symmetry group acts transitively on vertices; hence the one-vertex rank is 15 everywhere.

**2. Exhaustive pair classification.** Each of the 120 verified automorphisms of the underlying icosahedron induces a permutation of the 60 flags and preserves the truncated adjacency relation. The automorphisms are exhaustively generated by selecting the image of one vertex (12 choices), selecting one of its five neighboring images, and choosing a direction on the induced five-cycle (two choices). Each extends uniquely to the remaining vertices. These 120 permutations partition all 1770 unordered pairs into **23 disjoint orbits**: twelve orbits of size 60, eight of size 120, and three of size 30. Their sizes sum to 1770.

For each orbit representative \((s,t)\), exact rational Gaussian elimination on the 60-by-30 integer matrix with columns \(A^ke_s,A^ke_t\), \(0\le k\le14\), gives its rank. The 23 individual certificates, including representative labels, orbit sizes, shortest-path counts, and exact ranks, are supplied in `CERTIFICATE.tsv`. The resulting totals are 30, 60, 120, and 1560 pairs with ranks 15, 27, 28, and 29. Shortest-path breadth-first counting and the unique-antipode involution verify the stated intrinsic geometric rule at each representative. Every condition in the rule is symmetry-invariant, so the check covers every pair in each orbit. The numerical ranks are over the rationals, not floating-point estimates or ranks inferred from modular lower bounds.

An independent structural check bounds every two-site rank by 29: the two one-site spaces each have dimension 15, and both contain the constant eigenvector of \(A\), since this graph is connected and 3-regular. Thus no two-site bright subspace can have dimension 30. The rank-15 antipodal cases demonstrate complete absorption redundancy, whereas 1560 pairs attain the sharp 29-dimensional maximum.

**3. Exact capture law.** Let \(\mathcal K\) denote the computed bright subspace and \(\Gamma=\gamma_s|s\rangle\langle s|+\gamma_t|t\rangle\langle t|\). Both \(\mathcal K\) and its orthogonal complement are invariant under \(A\), and \(\Gamma\) is zero on \(\mathcal K^\perp\). Thus the latter subspace evolves unitarily and never decays. Conversely, an eigenvector in \(\mathcal K\) of \(JA-i\Gamma\) with eigenvalue having zero imaginary part would satisfy \(\Gamma x=0\) by taking the imaginary part of its inner product. Then \(Ax=\lambda x/J\), and its orthogonality to \(e_s,e_t\) makes it orthogonal to every \(A^ke_s,A^ke_t\), contradicting \(x\in\mathcal K\) unless \(x=0\). Every eigenvalue of the restricted dissipative generator therefore has strictly negative imaginary part, and finite-dimensional evolution on \(\mathcal K\) decays to zero even in the presence of Jordan blocks. Taking the limit of the surviving trace gives \(P_{\mathrm{abs}}(\rho_0)=\operatorname{tr}(\Pi_{\mathcal K}\rho_0)\). Substitution \(\rho_0=I_{60}/60\) proves the fractions.

## Relationship to prior work
Varbanov, Krovi and Brun (arXiv:0803.3446, 2008) analyze quantum-walk hitting and infinite hitting times, including symmetry obstructions, but not the truncated-icosahedral two-trap classification. Coutinho (2016) supplies general strong-cospectrality and walk-module tools; these explain the *type* of antipodal redundancy but not its occurrence and exact rank partition here. Bougroura and collaborators (2016) investigate transport on \(C_{60}\) via a **coined discrete-time** walk and numerical arrival curves, not this uncoined continuous-time lossy model. Le and collaborators (arXiv:1707.07482, 2017) analyze dark-subspace suppression on quantum networks, without the complete \(C_{60}\) pair geometry. Dhamapurkar and Dahlsten (arXiv:2304.01572, 2023) analyze closed fullerene quantum walks and equilibration rather than two local sinks. The general dark-space/cyclic-space mechanism and the previously known antipodal symmetry are **not** claimed as new; the contribution assessed here is the complete explicit four-level, 1770-pair classification, the intrinsic placement rule, and its physical capture consequence.

## Limitations
This is a theorem for the idealized finite, unweighted \(C_{60}\) graph. It makes no claim for distinct bond strengths, energetic disorder, dephasing, interactions among excitations, imperfect detector models other than constant positive local loss, or a continuum/thermodynamic limit. It does not optimize waiting times or absorption-rate magnitudes. Historical priority is provisional pending independent source review; a relevant unpublished or differently phrased prior computation cannot be ruled out merely by unsuccessful searches.
