# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The asserted domain is every \(4\mid n\), \(n\ge4\), with two omitted cross-cut gates whose even endpoints differ. Binary graph-state entropy equals the rank of the cut matrix by Hein--Eisert--Briegel. The exact kernel of the four-ones circulant is proved from \((I+P)^4=I+P^4\). The independent-cokernel cases are handled by a rank-update kernel identity; the dependent case uses a concrete vector \(x\) and two exact scalar constraints to distinguish zero and one rank gain. The proof is infinite and does not extrapolate from finite data. The standalone checker independently recomputed \(8{,}448\) exact finite rank instances, all agreeing.

**Originality — PASS on checked evidence, not externally certified.** The full relevant material of Hein--Eisert--Briegel supplies generic graph-state cut rank and a single-edge Schmidt-measure bound (Proposition 5), not an exact two-edge checkerboard entropy formula; Williams's Section 3.1 and Corollary 3.3 cover the pristine four-ones circulant and therefore the baseline rank, not the placement-dependent two-entry deletion law. Campbell and collaborators analyze adaptive weighted graph-state construction under monitored errors; their accessible full text does not contain this sharp modulo-four two-omission classification. Targeted published research-record searches for missing controlled-\(Z\) gates, two-entry binary circulant rank changes and cyclic cluster-state double defects found no equivalent theorem; negative searches alone do not establish novelty. Closest adjacent records concerned unrelated cyclic ranks, hypercubic or toroidal entanglement or single fault patterns. A stronger yet unlocated arbitrary-two-entry binary circulant theorem remains the principal priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** Third-power cycles are a natural minimal-range model that simultaneously allows nearest and third-neighbor checkerboard gates and has a nontrivial three-dimensional undamaged cut kernel. The complete double-fault classification shows sharply different entanglement responses—including exact cancellation and maximal two-bit growth—for geometrically close, controlled local changes. This is more than the generic two-rank bound or the previously covered undamaged circulant rank, and it is an exact infinite-family sensitivity benchmark useful when assessing graph-state resource loss.

Limitations: only the specified pure standard graph states, this bipartition, and omitted ideal cross edges; other edge arrangements, interactions and noise remain untreated. Novelty remains provisional against specialized unexamined literature. The finite checker is corroboration, not a substitute for proof.
