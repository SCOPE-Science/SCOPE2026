# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is for every integer \(k\ge2\) and every integer \(q\ge2\). Each message has a unique codeword because the generator contains an identity block. Partition by whether the coordinate sum is zero. Zero-sum messages yield \(B_{k,q}\) via the additive-character projection; nonzero-sum messages yield \(H_{k,q}\), using \(\sum_{s\ne0,a\ne0}\chi_a((j-1)s)=q(\gcd(q,j-1)-1)-(q-1)\). Appending the coordinate sum multiplies the latter term by \(z\). This is a proof for composite as well as prime moduli. The standalone `verify.py` corroborates it through direct exhaustive codeword enumeration for 41 small parameter pairs and 1,421 exact arithmetic tests of structural properties. The exact full-support coefficients agree with the previously proved result. No finite test is used to infer the all-parameter theorem.

## Originality — PASS, subject to publication-priority limitations

Imamura, Nakashima, and Saito, *Periodicity of weight enumerators for codes generated by an integral matrix*, arXiv:2601.21121, Section 5, examine the identical matrices, prove minimum-distance statements and obtain full-support counts in Theorem 5.3. They specifically distinguish these from a complete description of the weight enumerators. The new claim determines *every* coefficient for both families and all integer moduli. Kuroda, Nakashima, and Tsujie's coboundary-quasi-polynomial work provides a general related framework, not this evaluated all-modulus pair of formulas in the inspected abstract, introductory statements and examples. Searches for the matrices, spike-matroid Tutte evaluations, equivalent root-of-unity sums and complete spectra did not identify a stronger covering theorem. This is evidence for originality, not a guarantee: an unindexed arithmetic Tutte evaluation, or a treatment of binary spikes in another representation, could imply a specialization or the full theorem.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The previously available distance and full-support facts for two canonical integral spike-code families did not specify intermediate Hamming-weight multiplicities. The theorem closes that natural gap by an explicit simultaneous coefficient formula for every dimension and every modulus, including composite rings where field-based enumerator identities alone do not suffice. It supplies a sharp minimum-weight multiplicity for every \(k\ge5\), gives a direct means to compute the distribution without enumerating \(q^k\) words, and exposes the arithmetic gcd dependence of the spectrum. It is a complete classification of a mathematically motivated invariant, not an arbitrary finite slice.

## Closest literature and residual risks

The main source is arXiv:2601.21121v2, Sections 4–5 and especially Theorem 5.3; it establishes only the high-weight part being refined. The general arithmetic coboundary literature includes arXiv:2512.10838v3 and Brändén–Moci (2014). A previously known Tutte or arithmetic Tutte polynomial for these particular integral spike representations might, on specialization, cover the claimed expressions. No definitive publication-priority or independent correctness assessment is asserted.
