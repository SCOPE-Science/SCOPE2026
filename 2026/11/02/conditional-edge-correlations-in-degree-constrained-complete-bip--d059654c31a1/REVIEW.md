# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For every pair of positive side sizes at least two and every feasible full labeled degree vector, the weighted complete-bipartite spanning-tree polynomial factors as the product of vertex weights times powers of the side sums. Explicit effective resistances and the transfer-current determinant give weighted inclusion formulas for shared and disjoint edge pairs. Coefficient extraction converts one- and two-point powers into multinomial first and second factorial moments, including the one-position boundary cases. The sign inequalities follow from sampling without replacement. Direct exact enumeration verifies 231,113 assertions for all degree classes and edge pairs in seven finite host sizes; these computations supplement the algebraic proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
The unconditioned negative-association theorem and transfer-current determinant in Lyons (2002) and Grimmett–Winkler (2003) are acknowledged premises, not claimed as new. Martin–Reiner (2003) concern other weighted tree enumerators. Dong–Ge (2021) provide an exact fixed-forest inclusion enumeration but do not simultaneously constrain the complete vertex degree vector. The assessed statement is the *degree-conditioned complete pair law*, the full incident/nonincident sign classification, and sharp positive examples. Comparisons to the closest searched records on random-tree degree dependence, other complete-bipartite spanning-tree enumerations, and random spanning-tree determinants found no implication establishing the same conditional law. These searches cannot exclude an older equivalent specialization encoded differently; external historical confirmation remains appropriate.

## Value — PASS
A major structural question about a canonical determinantal probability measure is whether its negative dependence survives natural conditioning on all degrees. The exact answer is unexpectedly mixed: incident edge covariances cannot become positive, but disjoint ones can achieve the maximal Bernoulli covariance \(1/4\) already on a minimal host, and positivity survives with no leaf endpoints. The theorem gives an all-parameter classification, not just a single curiosity or routine numerical increment.
