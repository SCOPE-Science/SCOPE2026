# Conditional geodesic dispersion and three-point covariance on flat Klein bottles

## Finding
Let \(K_{a,b}\) be the flat Klein bottle defined below, where \(a\ge b/2>0\). For three independent points \(P,Y,Z\), sampled uniformly by Riemannian area, put \(D_{PY}=d_K(P,Y)\) and \(D_{PZ}=d_K(P,Z)\). If a lift of \(P\) has vertical coordinate \(v\), set \(h=\{2v/b\}\in[0,1)\), the fractional part. Its **conditional geodesic squared-distance profile** is exactly
\[
 m(v):=\mathbb E[D_{PY}^{2}\mid P]
 =\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}}{12}
 -\frac{b^{4}}{12a^{2}}h^{2}(1-h)^{2}.
\]
Thus \(m\) is nonconstant: its maximum occurs precisely on the two glide-axis circles \(v=0,b/2\pmod b\), its minimum precisely at the horizontal-coordinate levels \(v=b/4,3b/4\pmod b\) (which together form one closed curve on the quotient), and
\[
 \max m-\min m=\frac{b^{4}}{192a^{2}}.
\]
The complete **shared-endpoint three-point covariance** is
\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{Cov}(D_{PY}^{2},D_{PZ}^{2})
 =\frac{b^{8}}{302400a^{4}}>0.}
\]
With \(A=\operatorname{area}(K_{a,b})=ab\), the scale-free three-point statistic obeys
\[
 \boxed{\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(D_{PY}^{2},D_{PZ}^{2})}{A^{2}}
 =\frac1{302400}\left(\frac ba\right)^{6}.}
\]
It therefore identifies the aspect ratio \(a/b\) uniquely throughout \(a\ge b/2\), with the explicit inverse \(a/b=(302400C/A^2)^{-1/6}\), where \(C\) denotes the covariance. Its sharp range is \(0<C/A^{2}\le1/4725\), the upper endpoint occurring only at \(a=b/2\).

There is also a sampling consequence. For independent area-uniform \(P_1,\ldots,P_n\) with \(n\ge2\), let
\[
 U_n=\binom n2^{-1}\sum_{i<j}d_K(P_i,P_j)^2,
 \qquad \theta=\mathbb E D_{PY}^2.
\]
Writing \(\sigma^2=\operatorname{Var}(D_{PY}^2)\), which is finite, the exact variance identity is
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(U_n)
 =\frac{2\sigma^2}{n(n-1)}
 +\frac{4(n-2)}{n(n-1)}\frac{b^{8}}{302400a^{4}}.
\]
In particular,
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}n\operatorname{Var}(U_n)
 =\frac{b^{8}}{75600a^{4}},
 \qquad
 \sqrt n(U_n-\theta)\ \Longrightarrow\
 N\!\left(0,\frac{b^{8}}{75600a^{4}}\right).
\]
This nonzero first-order fluctuation is absent for an intrinsically homogeneous flat torus, where the fixed-anchor conditional mean is constant.

## Assumptions and scope
The metric surface is the quotient of the Euclidean plane by the freely acting isometry group generated by
\[
 (x,y)\mapsto(x,y+b),\qquad (x,y)\mapsto(x+a,-y).
\]
A fundamental rectangle has coordinates \(0\le x<a\), \(0\le y<b\) and area \(ab\). Geodesic distance is the intrinsic quotient distance, **not** ambient distance in any embedding or immersion. Uniform points are sampled with respect to the normalized flat area measure. The deck-separation condition \(a\ge b/2\) includes its equality boundary and guarantees that squared vertical distances can never differ by more than \(a^2\). The same formulas are not asserted for \(a<b/2\).

## Proof
**1. Nearest deck lifts.** Horizontal translation descends to an isometry, so take the anchor \(P=(0,v)\) and write a second uniform point as \(Y=(x,y)\) in the rectangle. Set
\[
 A_y=\operatorname{dist}(y-v,b\mathbb Z)^2,\quad
 B_y=\operatorname{dist}(y+v,b\mathbb Z)^2.
\]
The nearest even and odd deck translates respectively have horizontal displacements \(x\) and \(a-x\), hence
\[
 d_K(P,Y)^2=\min\{x^2+A_y,(a-x)^2+B_y\}.
\]
This is an exact minimum over the entire deck group because every alternative even or odd horizontal translate is at least as far in horizontal absolute value, and the vertical integer shift is optimized independently.

**2. Integrating the horizontal coordinate.** Put \(s=A_y-B_y\) and \(\Delta=a^2-2ax\). For uniform \(x\in[0,a]\), \(\Delta\) is uniform on \([-a^2,a^2]\). Therefore
\[
 d_K(P,Y)^2=x^2+A_y-(s-\Delta)_+,
 \qquad (z)_+=\max(z,0).
\]
Both \(A_y\) and \(B_y\) belong to \([0,b^2/4]\); since \(a^2\ge b^2/4\), we have \(|s|\le a^2\). Direct integration gives
\[
 \mathbb E_x(s-\Delta)_+=\frac{(s+a^2)^2}{4a^2},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_x d_K(P,Y)^2
 =\frac{a^2}{12}+\frac{A_y+B_y}{2}
 -\frac{(A_y-B_y)^2}{4a^2}.
\]
A uniform \(y\) has \(\mathbb E_y A_y=\mathbb E_y B_y=b^2/12\). It remains to evaluate the squared difference of two shifted circular squared-distance functions.

**3. A complete circular correlation.** Let \(g(t)=\operatorname{dist}(t,\mathbb Z)^2\), a period-one function, and put \(h=\{2v/b\}\). Then
\[
 \frac1b\int_0^b(A_y-B_y)^2\,dy
 =b^4\int_0^1[g(t)-g(t+h)]^2\,dt.
\]
For \(0\le h\le1/2\), represent one period as \([-1/2,1/2]\). On \([-1/2,1/2-h]\) the difference is \(t^2-(t+h)^2\), and on \([1/2-h,1/2]\) it is \(t^2-(t+h-1)^2\). Consequently exact polynomial integration yields
\[
 \int_{-1/2}^{1/2-h}\![t^2-(t+h)^2]^2dt
 +\int_{1/2-h}^{1/2}\![t^2-(t+h-1)^2]^2dt
 =\frac{h^2(1-h)^2}{3}.
\]
For \(1/2<h<1\), replace \(h\) by \(1-h\): the correlation is unchanged, and the same formula results. The quartic is also invariant under the glide identification \(v\mapsto-v\), which sends \(h\) to \(1-h\) modulo one, so the profile is intrinsically defined. Substitution into Step 2 proves the full pointwise profile. The quartic has its sole interior maximum at \(h=1/2\), proving the extremal anchor locations and gap.

**4. Three-point covariance and sampling.** Conditional on \(P\), the two squared distances to \(Y\) and \(Z\) are independent and have the same mean \(m(P)\). Thus total covariance equals \(\operatorname{Var}(m(P))\). Since \(v\) is uniform modulo \(b\), the fractional part \(h=\{2v/b\}\) is uniform on \([0,1]\). Direct beta integrals give
\[
 \int_0^1h^2(1-h)^2dh=\frac1{30},\qquad
 \int_0^1h^4(1-h)^4dh=\frac1{630},
\]
so the quartic's variance equals \(1/630-1/900=1/2100\). Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(m(P))=
 \left(\frac{b^4}{12a^2}\right)^2\frac1{2100}
 =\frac{b^8}{302400a^4}.
\]
The area normalization and sharp range follow algebraically. For \(U_n\), disjoint edges \(\{i,j\},\{k,l\}\) are independent, whereas two edges sharing one vertex have covariance \(\operatorname{Var}(m(P))\). Counting \(3\binom n3\) unordered overlapping edge pairs proves the exact finite-sample variance formula. The Hoeffding decomposition of this bounded symmetric order-two kernel has linear term \(2n^{-1}\sum_i(m(P_i)-\theta)\), and a degenerate remainder with variance \(O(n^{-2})\); the ordinary independent-sum central limit theorem yields the stated Gaussian limit. No independence is assumed between distances sharing a common endpoint.

## Verification
The accompanying executable \`checks/verify.py\` (dependencies in \`requirements.txt\`) checks the circle-correlation polynomial identity by exact symbolic integration and the two beta moments exactly. Independently, it computes the quotient distance by the even/odd deck-lift minimum on transverse midpoint grids at 28 geometric parameter/anchor combinations, and independently integrates across 192 anchors to reproduce the three-point covariance. The run reported \`ALL_CHECKS_PASSED\`. Finite grids are corroboration only: the universal proof is Steps 1–4.

For a square fundamental rectangle \(a=b=1\), the conditional mean ranges from \(31/192\) to \(1/6\), and the three-point covariance is \(1/302400\). The area-normalized statistic is positive, in contrast to the zero shared-anchor covariance on any homogeneous flat torus.

## Relationship to prior work
Davis and Recio-Mitter (2019) study geodesic complexity and nearest-deck representations for flat Klein bottles. Bérard, Helffer, and Kiwan (2020) explicitly describe rectangular flat Klein-bottle quotients and analyze their Laplace spectral and nodal properties. These establish the underlying geometrical setting but not the conditional mean-square profile or a shared-endpoint three-point covariance. Existing one-pair distance moment identities and stochastic comparisons concern the marginal law of a single uniformly sampled pair; **such a marginal does not determine the joint dependence of two distances sharing an endpoint**. The present contribution is the pointwise anchor-dependence formula and its consequences, not a rederivation of the earlier unconditional mean.

## Limitations
The exact quartic conditional profile and covariance require \(a\ge b/2\) in the specified flat Klein-bottle quotient. Different metric deformations, nonuniform sampling, and intrinsic distances on immersed nonflat models are outside the theorem. The reconstruction formula concerns exact population covariance and known area; no confidence interval or noise-stability theorem is given. The Gaussian statement is asymptotic and relies on independent area-uniform samples. Historical priority remains provisional because searching the inspected literature and records cannot exclude every earlier specialized computation; no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
