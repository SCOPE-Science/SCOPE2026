# Self-review

## Correctness (PASS)
The quotient is defined by a reflection glide of length \(a\) and a perpendicular period \(b\). Every candidate shortest lift belongs to one of two parity classes. The exact uniform-horizontal integral yields the pointwise conditional mean for \(a\ge b/2\). Splitting the circular correlation at its wrap point proves the quartic identity for every anchor, rather than merely checking examples. Exact beta integrals then prove the shared-endpoint covariance, and exact overlapping-pair counting plus a nondegenerate U-statistic projection proves the sampling formulas. The companion checker reproduced the polynomial proof identities and independent quotient-minimum quadratures.

## Originality (PASS, priority provisional)
Davis–Recio-Mitter (arXiv:1912.07411v1) treats shortest-geodesic complexity; Bérard–Helffer–Kiwan (arXiv:2007.09219v1) treats the spectrum of the same flat quotient. Earlier marginal distance laws determine single-pair expectations or distributions but not the covariance of two distances **sharing an anchor**, which depends on a new nonconstant conditional geodesic-energy profile. Searches under conditional Fréchet energy, three-point correlation, quotient-metric moments and shared-endpoint covariance found no directly covering result. Some specialist sources were not fully accessible, so historical priority is not independently verified.

## Mathematical value (PASS)
The theorem is a complete sharp anchor-dependence classification on a naturally defined family of nonhomogeneous closed flat surfaces. Its covariance detects lack of transitivity, gives an exact area-normalized aspect statistic, and determines the leading variance and central-limit scaling of an all-pairs metric estimator. The glide-separation condition is mathematically motivated by an exact deck-distance threshold, not an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Limits and closest literature
The result assumes intrinsic flat quotient geometry, independent normalized-area sampling and \(a\ge b/2\). It neither supplies a formula below this threshold nor treats nonflat deformations or noisy measurements. The two named primary papers establish geometric and spectral background, not the claimed three-point statistic. A relevant general compact-set review could be inspected only through its abstract.
