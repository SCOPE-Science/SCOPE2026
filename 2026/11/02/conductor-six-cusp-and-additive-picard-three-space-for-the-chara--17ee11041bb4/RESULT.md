# The characteristic-seven Klein plane model has a single semigroup-\(\langle2,7\rangle\) cusp and additive three-space Picard group

## Finding
Let \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_7\) and let \(C\subset\mathbb P_k^2\) be the *naive reduction* of the classical Klein plane quartic, \(X^3Y+Y^3Z+Z^3X=0\). Then \(C\) is a geometrically integral rational quartic, its only singularity is the rational point \(P=[2:4:1]\), and that singularity is geometrically unibranch with value semigroup \(\langle2,7\rangle\), delta invariant \(3\), and conductor exponent \(6\). Its connected generalized Jacobian is the split three-dimensional vector group
\[\operatorname{Pic}^0(C)\simeq\mathbb G_a^3.\]
The normalization induces a bijection on geometric points and therefore \(\#C(\mathbb F_{7^n})=7^n+1\) for every integer \(n\ge1\). This point-count identity is included for completeness but is *not* claimed as new; an earlier counting argument is acknowledged below.

## Assumptions and scope
All curves and local rings are over characteristic seven. This is the special fiber of the **displayed integral plane equation**, not the potentially good smooth genus-three reduction of the Klein quartic over a ramified extension. In particular, its generalized Jacobian must not be confused with the abelian Jacobian of a smooth stable model. For the rational point count use the model defined over \(\mathbb F_7\).

## Proof
Work first on \(Z=1\) with \(x=X/Z\), \(y=Y/Z\). The polynomial is \(f=x^3y+y^3+x\). The Jacobian equations are \(3x^2y+1=0\), \(x^3+3y^2=0\), and \(y^3+3x=0\) (the last is the homogenized \(Z\)-derivative). Eliminating \(x\) gives \(x=2y^3\) and \(y^7=4\), which over an algebraically closed field of characteristic seven singles out \((x,y)=(2,4)\). At \(Z=0\) the two points \([1:0:0]\) and \([0:1:0]\) have nonzero gradient. Thus \(P\) is the unique geometric singularity.

Set \(u=x-2\), \(w=y-4-3u\), and \(r=w/u\) away from \(u=0\). Direct substitution yields
\[f=u^2\{(r+3)u^2+r(r^2+2r-2)u-2r^2\}.\]
The quadratic discriminant in \(u\) is \(r^5(r-3)\), a nonsquare in \(k(r)\). Hence the polynomial defines a geometrically integral quartic. Its function field is a quadratic extension of \(k(r)\) with square class \(r(r-3)\), the rational conic with a rational point at \(r=0\). Thus \(k(C)\simeq k(a)\) and the normalization is \(\mathbb P^1_k\).

An explicit normalization map is obtained using the homogeneous degree-four forms
\[D=(s+2t)(s+t)^3,\qquad U=2s^2(s^2-t^2),\qquad W=6s^4\]
by setting
\[\nu([s:t])=[2D+U:\,4D+3U+W:\,D].\]
The displayed forms have no common geometric zero: \(W=0\) forces \(s=0\), where \(D\ne0\); the point \([1:0]\) is also covered. Direct expansion shows \(F(2D+U,4D+3U+W,D)=0\). Since the image is the integral quartic and the forms have degree four with no base point, \(\nu\) has generic degree one. The fiber over \(P\) is just \([s:t]=[0:1]\), proving the singularity is geometrically unibranch.

Take \(a=s/t\) near zero. Then \(D=(a+2)(a+1)^3\), \(U=2a^2(a^2-1)\), \(W=6a^4\), and \(u=U/D\), \(w=W/D\). One checks
\[\operatorname{ord}_a(u)=2,\qquad \operatorname{ord}_a(w-3u^2-5u^3)=7.\]
Write \(\eta=w-3u^2-5u^3\). The complete subring generated by \(u,\eta\) in \(k[[a]]\) has numerical semigroup \(\langle2,7\rangle\), with exactly three gaps \(1,3,5\); its normalization colength is three. As \(C\) is a plane quartic of arithmetic genus three whose normalization has genus zero and whose only singularity is \(P\), the full completed local ring also has normalization colength three. It therefore equals that subring. Its conductor is \(a^6k[[a]]\) (up to a unit and permissible change of uniformizer).

For an integral projective rational curve with one unibranch singularity, the generalized Jacobian is the quotient of local normalized units by the units of the local ring. Here, modulo the conductor, the quotient has dimension three. Since the conductor exponent \(6\) is smaller than characteristic \(7\), the truncated logarithm \(\log(1+h)=\sum_{m=1}^5(-1)^{m+1}h^m/m\) gives an additive group isomorphism on principal units modulo \(a^6\). The quotient is the vector space represented by the semigroup gaps \(a,a^3,a^5\), hence \(\operatorname{Pic}^0(C)\simeq\mathbb G_a^3\) as algebraic groups.

Finally, the explicit normalization is defined over \(\mathbb F_7\), and its only exceptional geometric fiber is one rational point mapping to the singular rational point. It is bijective on \(\mathbb F_{7^n}\)-points for every \(n\ge1\). Thus \(\#C(\mathbb F_{7^n})=\#\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_{7^n})=7^n+1\).

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` reproduces the parametrization identity over \(\mathbb F_7\), the discriminant, uniqueness of the rational singular parameter, valuations two and seven, the semigroup gaps and conductor, and the eight \(\mathbb F_7\)-points. The remaining algebraic steps are given above; symbolic checks are not substituted for the arguments about irreducibility, all geometric singularities, or the generalized Picard group.

## Relationship to prior work
Elkies' treatment of characteristic seven proves potentially good **smooth hyperelliptic** reduction after extension, and therefore does not identify the bad plane fiber with that smooth special fiber. Poonen–Schaefer–Stoll use precisely the classical quartic equation and discuss its modular/arithmetic context. A 2015 answer by David E. Speyer derives \(q+1\) point counts when \(q\not\equiv1\pmod7\), including the powers of seven; those counts are therefore not asserted as new. The specific complete local semigroup, explicit normalization and split connected generalized-Picard group are the additional result here. They are compatible with, and do not supersede, the established potential-good-reduction theory.

## Limitations
Historical priority for the local singularity and generalized-Picard calculation remains uncertain: these can conceivably be implicit in an earlier explicit model of the characteristic-seven special fiber. No assertion about the stable reduction, Néron model of the smooth generic Jacobian, or a new point-counting formula is made. The generalized Picard group here is *not* the Jacobian of the good hyperelliptic special fiber.
