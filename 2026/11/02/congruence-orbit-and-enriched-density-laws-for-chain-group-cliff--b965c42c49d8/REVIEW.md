# Scientific review: chain–group congruence-density laws

**Correctness — PASS.** The normal form follows from the restriction of a congruence to the chain idempotents and to each group level; the proof checks that mixed-level relations force identical normal subgroups, and verifies the converse under multiplication. The maximum-element condition forces constant subgroup data. The automorphism calculation, flag-orbit enumeration and discrete-Gaussian limit are derived with explicit quantifiers. The directly enumerated cases \((p,d,m)=(2,1,2),(2,1,3),(2,2,2),(3,1,2),(3,1,3),(5,1,2)\) reproduce both ordinary and enriched counts; exact orbit comparisons are also supplied in the cases with at most nine elements. There is no numerical extrapolation in the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with historical-priority qualification.** The recent F-congruence paper gives general kernel and congruence-pair criteria and uses a two-level semilattice times a group as an illustrative example, not an explicit complete orbit/density classification for finite chains times groups. General Clifford-semigroup congruence descriptions and classical flag-counting literature already cover major ingredients. In particular, Kousidis (arXiv:1109.2546, Theorem 3.2) already proves theta asymptotics for generalized Galois numbers, and no separate novelty is claimed for those theta limits. The proposed contribution is the exact algebra-specific normal form identifying maximum-compatible congruences, both automorphism-orbit counts, and their contrasting polynomial versus quadratic-exponential prevalence laws. Targeted mathematical-literature and research-record searches found no direct statement of this combined classification. A historical general congruence theorem could make the structural part a short specialization; this remains the principal originality risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Preserving the natural maximum operation is the central distinction introduced in the 2026 research program on F-inverse monoids. The result answers a natural quantitative question for a coherent infinite family: how many usual congruences survive the richer signature, and how does the answer change after quotienting by symmetries? It supplies exact formulas for every finite group and sharp asymptotic density and orbit-density laws for an increasing-rank family. The mismatch of the two decay scales is an informative algebraic phenomenon rather than an arbitrary finite enumeration.

**Closest literature and limits.** Furlani (2026) supplies the definition and abstract criteria, not these enumerations. Spitznagel (1973) belongs to broad band-of-groups congruence theory. Bliem–Kousidis (2011), Vinroot (2012) and Kousidis (2011/2013) treat the flag enumerators and limits independently of enriched congruences. The theorem does not treat non-chain semilattice actions, general nonabelian congruence-orbit enumeration, or uniform asymptotics in multiple parameters.
