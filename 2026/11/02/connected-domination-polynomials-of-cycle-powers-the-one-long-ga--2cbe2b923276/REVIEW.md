# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The cyclic-gap equivalence is proved in both directions by tracking domination inside each omitted interval and connectivity across consecutive selected vertices. Counting anchored cyclic compositions gives the exact polynomial, with the factor \(n/k\) justified by double counting. Minimum size and multiplicity follow from a sharp gap-length bound and a bounded weak-composition enumeration; the sharp saturation threshold follows by counting omitted vertices. These steps prove the result for all integer parameters, including complete-power and singleton-set boundary cases. Independent graph-level exhaustive checks found exact agreement for 26 noncomplete instances (74,390 nonempty subsets) and additional complete cases. No infinite claim rests solely on those computations.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Galvin–Zhang (arXiv:2408.12731) treats ordinary domination of powers of paths and cycles; their size distribution permits disconnected selections, whereas the present theorem imposes a new connectivity constraint and enumerates its consequences. Mojdeh–Emadi (DOI:10.1515/fascmath-2018-0007) treats the connected-domination polynomial and includes ordinary cycles, so the \(r=1\) specialization is prior knowledge. Caputi–Celoria–Collari (arXiv:2201.00721) connects this polynomial to homology without the present power-of-cycle evaluation. Circulant domination-number bounds concern scalar minima, not the full coefficients. Query comparisons included alternative names, circulant generators, connected dominating sets, gap-composition models, and general connected-domination enumerators. No equivalent all-\(n,r\) connected-polynomial formula was identified in the inspected literature or indexed research records. Unindexed/older enumerations remain a publication-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The object is a standard two-parameter graph family already studied for ordinary domination, and the invariant records every connected dominating set by size. The one-long-gap theorem explains the structural distinction between ordinary and connected domination. The resulting exact generating function and minimum-set multiplicities refine scalar connected-domination numbers; the sharp universal threshold provides an additional graph-structural consequence. These are not arbitrary small numerical instances or merely a renaming of a known special case. They do not solve analogous questions for general circulant graphs.

## Boundaries and residual risks

The \(r=1\) ordinary-cycle polynomial is credited as known. Related general theories could imply the result through an unexamined specialization; a comprehensive independent priority search is outstanding. The finite verifier is corroboration, not a replacement for the general combinatorial proof. Independent audit has not been performed.
