# Constant-rank idempotent factorization in full-domain partition monoids

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge2\), every full-domain partition diagram \(\alpha\) on two copies of \([n]\) with rank \(1\le r<n\) is a product of idempotent full-domain diagrams, **all of rank exactly \(r\)**. Consequently every proper two-sided rank ideal of the finite full-domain partition monoid is idempotent-generated:
\[
I_r=\langle\{e\in I_r:e^2=e\}\rangle,
\qquad 1\le r<n.
\]
In fact the rank-preserving factorization is a uniform constructive strengthening of ideal generation. The statement excludes full-rank nonidentity permutation diagrams, which cannot be products of idempotents in this finite monoid.

## Assumptions and scope
The partition monoid \(\mathcal P_n\) comprises all set partitions of a top copy of \([n]\) and a disjoint primed bottom copy. Its product is the usual three-row diagram concatenation, with middle-only components discarded. The submonoid \(\mathcal P_n^{\mathrm{fd}}\) consists of those partitions with every top point in a transversal block (one intersecting the bottom). Thus each \(\alpha\in\mathcal P_n^{\mathrm{fd}}\) is uniquely describable by transversal blocks
\[
A_1\cup B_1',\ldots,A_r\cup B_r'
\]
and bottom-only blocks \(C_1',\ldots,C_s'\), where the nonempty \(A_i\) partition the top row and the nonempty \(B_i,C_j\) partition the bottom row. The rank \(r\) counts transversal blocks, and \(1\le r\le n\). Multiplication is written left to right, consistent with diagram stacking. The proper rank ideals are \(I_r=\{\alpha:\operatorname{rank}(\alpha)\le r\}\), with \(1\le r<n\).

## Proof
**Step 1: A rank-preserving transformation–idempotent factorization.** Fix a singular diagram \(\alpha\) of rank \(r\), displayed above, and choose \(b_i\in B_i\). Let \(t\in\mathcal T_n\subseteq\mathcal P_n^{\mathrm{fd}}\) be the full transformation sending every \(a\in A_i\) to \(b_i\). Thus \(t\) has rank \(r\). Construct another full-domain partition \(e\) by taking transversal blocks
\[
D_i\cup B_i'\quad(1\le i\le r),
\]
and the same bottom-only blocks \(C_j'\) as \(\alpha\), where \(D_1=B_1\cup C_1\cup\cdots\cup C_s\) and \(D_i=B_i\) for \(i\ge2\). These \(D_i\) form a partition of the top row and each satisfies \(B_i\subseteq D_i\). The rank of \(e\) is exactly \(r\). In the product \(e^2\), the first copy's through-block lower part \(B_i\) reaches exactly the second copy's top block \(D_i\), and cannot reach another \(D_h\). Its first-copy bottom-only \(C_j\) enters \(D_1\) of the second copy and introduces no new connections between distinct transversal blocks. The product's outer through and lower-only blocks are therefore exactly those of \(e\). Thus \(e^2=e\). In \(te\), the image point \(b_i\) of \(A_i\) enters \(D_i\), producing exactly the block \(A_i\cup B_i'\). Bottom-only singleton blocks of \(t\) cannot connect different \(A_i\), and the bottom-only blocks of \(e\) persist. Hence \(\alpha=te\), with \(e\) idempotent and both factors of rank \(r\).

**Step 2: Rank-preserving idempotent factorization of full transformations.** Let \(t\colon[n]\to[n]\) have rank \(1\le r<n\), with kernel blocks \(A_i\) and distinct image points \(b_i=t(A_i)\). Choose distinct representatives \(a_i\in A_i\). The map \(p\) sending every \(A_i\) to \(a_i\) is an idempotent full transformation of rank \(r\). Regard the representatives \((a_1,\ldots,a_r)\) as positions of \(r\) labeled tokens and the \((b_1,\ldots,b_r)\) as their target positions. Since \(r<n\), there is always an unoccupied position. Every configuration of these tokens can be changed into any other using moves of one token into an unoccupied position: vacancy moves allow relocation to desired sites, and any remaining permutation cycle is broken and restored using a temporary vacancy (alternatively, swaps with a vacancy generate the symmetric group on token/hole placements).

For a move of one occupied point \(u\) to an unoccupied point \(v\), with current occupied set \(S\), define a full transformation \(f\) to fix every point of \((S\setminus\{u\})\cup\{v\}\), to send \(u\) to \(v\), and to send all other points arbitrarily into that new occupied set. Its image is exactly that set, and it fixes its own image, so \(f^2=f\) and \(\operatorname{rank}f=r\). Following a finite sequence of these moves gives \(t=pf_1\cdots f_m\). Hence every singular full transformation factors into idempotent transformations *of its own rank*.

**Step 3: Combine and conclude.** Substituting the factorization of \(t\) into \(\alpha=te\) gives \(\alpha=pf_1\cdots f_m e\), with every factor idempotent in \(\mathcal P_n^{\mathrm{fd}}\) and of rank precisely \(r=\operatorname{rank}\alpha\). Applying this to each element of rank at most \(r<n\) proves that every \(I_r\) is generated by its own idempotents; all factors lie in that ideal because their ranks do not exceed the element's rank. Finally, rank-\(n\) diagrams are units (permutations), while the only full-rank idempotent is the identity. This proves the asserted sharp boundary.

## Verification
The included standalone `verify.py` independently represents diagram products by three-layer connectivity and constructs the rank-preserving idempotent factors using a labeled-token breadth-first search, not the topological proof of token connectivity. It verifies all singular elements for \(n=2,3,4,5\), respectively \(3,46,831,19801\) elements, including the equality of product, idempotence of every factor, and exact preservation of rank. It separately verifies full idempotent-generated rank-ideal closures for every proper rank ideal at \(n=2,3,4\). The checker terminates with `VERIFY_OK`. These finite tests corroborate, but do not replace, the proof for all \(n\).

## Relationship to prior work
East and Gray's *Ehresmann theory and partition monoids*, arXiv:2011.00663 (first public November 2, 2020), introduces the full-domain partition monoid and explicitly asks whether all its finite proper rank ideals are idempotent-generated (end of the full-domain discussion). Carroll, East and Fresacher, *Presentations for semigroups of full-domain partitions*, arXiv:2507.05497 (July 7, 2025), Theorem 3.16, proves idempotent generation of the **largest singular ideal**. Its Proposition 6.6 gives a general transformation–semilattice decomposition, but without the rank-preserving idempotence property required here. Idempotent generation of the largest singular ideal by unrestricted singular factors does not logically imply generation of smaller ideals by their own idempotents. East and Gray's earlier arXiv:1404.2359 proves related statements for ideals in the **unrestricted** partition monoid, which is a different subsemigroup problem. The standard idempotent generation of singular full transformations is used here with an explicit constant-rank construction, rather than claimed as novel.

## Limitations
This is an elementary constructive semigroup result, not a determination of the *minimal* numbers of idempotent generators for the ideals. The proof does not claim a sharp shortest-factor length. Full-rank nonidentity units are excluded, necessarily. Prior work may encode the same factorization implicitly in presentations or regularity arguments; the literature comparison is a best-knowledge originality assessment, not independent publication-priority certification. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
