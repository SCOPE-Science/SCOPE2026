The exact forest/permutation counting law is reconstructed from known ingredients, with all endpoints covered. A cycle-indicator mean calculation, strictly increasing positive factorial sums, two explicit opposite-sign tails, and the one-vertex telescoping calculation provide independent algebraic checks of each all-size statement. The Gaussian gap uses a controlled product-to-Gaussian sum. The standalone exact rational checker confirms 10,059 assertions, including exhaustive maps through seven vertices; no finite verification is mistaken for a proof of all sizes.

Pitman's forest-volume identity and Hansen–Jaworski's cyclic-point theorems are prior foundations, and the joint count is not claimed original. The assessed contribution is the strict convexity and unique minimizer of the conditional regression, the complete adjacent stochastic-incomparability phenomenon, and the zero unconditional covariance that masks the conditional dependence. The full text of some earlier self-loop-biased mapping studies and an exhaustive text search of the 2010 PDF were unavailable. ## Value — PASS
The number of fixed points is the simplest observable statistic of a random mapping, while cyclic points govern recurrent structure. Strict convexity reveals a sharp minimum at one fixed point; opposite tails show why stochastic-order inference fails throughout the positive interior, even as means rise; exactly zero covariance demonstrates why linear dependence is insufficient. Together these give a natural full finite-sample dependence classification not supplied by marginal enumeration alone.

The priority assessment is qualified by inaccessible historical material, and no independent audit has taken place.
