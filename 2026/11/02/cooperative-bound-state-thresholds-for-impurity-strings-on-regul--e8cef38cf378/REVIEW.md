# Scientific review: cooperative binding for geodesic impurity strings

## Correctness

PASS. The free resolvent follows from the quadratic cavity equation and unique-path factorization; its band-edge matrix is exactly a geometric Toeplitz kernel. Strict Birman–Schwinger monotonicity turns its positive eigenvalues into all above-band eigenvalue crossings and handles equality without spurious modes. The tridiagonal inverse gives simple finite thresholds, and the monotone angular quantization equation establishes their ordering. Toeplitz Fourier bounds and endpoint phase expansions prove the two inverse-square shifts; angular equidistribution gives the mode-density law. The embedded reproducibility program independently compares matrix eigenvalues, thresholds, and truncated-tree resolvents. Finite tests are supplementary, not a replacement for the proof.

## Originality

PASS on examined evidence, with nontrivial residual priority risk. The Bethe-lattice free Green function (Kollar et al.), a general multi-impurity Green determinant (Glasser), and Toeplitz eigenvalue asymptotics (Bourget et al. surveying classical work) supply the main ingredients. Their statements were compared by implications: the cited articles do not state the complete regular-tree string threshold classification, its cooperative-binding interval, or the impurity-mode fraction with the two critical finite-length shifts. This is a specialized, explicitly worked out consequence of powerful known tools, **not** a claim to those tools themselves. Literature priority may be affected by less discoverable impurity-chain studies and existing equivalent calculations.

## Value

PASS. The question is physically motivated: can individually subcritical impurities cooperate to generate defect states outside a Bethe-lattice band, and how does the number of such states scale with the defect-string length? The result supplies a natural complete parameter classification, sharp finite-size onset and saturation scales, and an exact large-array population fraction. This provides design constraints not conveyed by the free resolvent or a bare determinant equation alone. The result does not claim a general new spectral method.

## Closest literature and scientific limits

The 2005 Bethe-lattice Green-function article covers host spectral machinery, not geodesic strings of added defects. The multi-impurity determinant identity covers arbitrary hosts formally, but does not evaluate these thresholds or derive the large-string law. Kac–Murdock–Szegő asymptotic theory already covers the standalone matrix eigenvalue distribution and extreme eigenvalue scales, so those are acknowledged as prior tools. The scope is finite positive equal on-site defects at equal graph spacing on a regular tree, with only above-band point spectrum counted. Historical priority remains provisional; no independent validation has been performed.
