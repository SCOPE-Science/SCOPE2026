# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** Every column is an incidence vector in a signed bipartite multigraph. Connected-component left-kernel propagation yields an exact finite nullity identity. Standard independent-edge sparse-graph factorial moments produce the independent Poisson counts of cycles, including parallel pairs, while bicyclic graphs and long cycles vanish in the subcritical regime. Correlation changes exactly the cycle-balance probability, and monotonicity in the number of columns proves the critical and supercritical divergence. The 5,018 exhaustive small-matrix comparisons and 40,000 larger simulations support the algebra and constants; they are not infinite proofs.

**Originality (PASS under the accessible primary-literature comparison).** The source establishes the stacked randomized sketch, and a later SparseStack preprint establishes embedding bounds, but the searched formulations did not state the joint Poisson nullity law with correlated signs. Classical signed-graphic ranks and Poisson unicyclic component limits are prior ingredients, not new propositions. A close older random signed-incidence-matrix theorem or the inaccessible source PDF could still subsume the formula; historical priority is therefore provisional.

**Value (PASS).** This gives an exact probability of losing injectivity for the minimally sparse two-block sketch and a strict quantification of the effect of sharing sign randomness. For a coordinate subspace, rank failure is a catastrophic subspace-embedding failure. The distinction between the two-sign constructions persists as dimension increases at fixed subcritical density, and the exact Poisson law resolves more than the average rank deficit.

**Limits.** Results concern coordinate subspaces, exact arithmetic, two stacked single-hash blocks, independent uniform hashes, and fixed sign correlation. They do not establish condition-number bounds, norm preservation, or arbitrary-subspace embedding guarantees. Published full-text access and historical completeness remain limitations.
