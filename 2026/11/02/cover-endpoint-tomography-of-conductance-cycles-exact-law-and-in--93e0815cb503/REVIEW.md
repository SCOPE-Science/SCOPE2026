# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The stopping event that target \(j\) is last is equivalent to reaching both neighbors of \(j\) before \(j\). The complement of \(j\) is an ordinary path, and its two hitting-stage harmonic probabilities are linear in the sum of edge resistances. This proves the full law without an asymptotic assumption. Its neighboring-target specialization yields a cyclic recurrence with a unique solution because the product of probabilities lies strictly below one. The alternating-product inversion, feasible-data test, stability estimate, and all-start uniformity converse follow algebraically. All positive-conductance, finite-cycle boundary cases, including the triangle, are covered. An independent exact first-step calculation matched the result in 340 parameter/start/target cases; these finite checks supplement rather than establish the proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The closest motivating paper is Idnani–Salem, arXiv:2610.15115v1 (2026). Its available abstract covers constant symmetric and biased walks and temporally varying bias, but does not state a spatial conductance inverse theorem. Its complete manuscript was inaccessible, so overlap in unseen sections remains a real priority risk, not an asserted negative inspection result. Lovász–Winkler (1993) and Buchanan–Horn–Rombach (2022) treat the last-vertex uniformity of unweighted graph walks; the full 2022 paper does not establish edge-parameter recovery. The complete Blåsten–Isozaki–Lassas–Lu preprint (2021) concerns first-passage distributions and heat observations under a different inverse-data model; neither its theorems nor its proof imply the claimed inversion from compressed cover-endpoint statistics without the new event/hitting reduction. Mathematical-record and targeted literature searches identified no covering exact law or inverse. These negative searches are not proof of absolute novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The cover tour's final newly seen vertex is a natural observable in exploration of finite weighted networks. Recovering \(n-1\) degrees of freedom from \(n\) simple endpoint probabilities, rather than full paths or time-indexed transitions, is a natural complete inverse classification. The explicit feasible-data condition and quantitative stability bound support estimation and distinguish true conductance-induced data from arbitrary observations. Rigidity explains precisely when the classical uniform last-vertex phenomenon persists under spatial heterogeneity. The weighted gambler's-ruin lemma itself is standard; the substantive result is the identification mechanism for the cover observable. Scale, topology, positivity, and model-class restrictions remain essential.
