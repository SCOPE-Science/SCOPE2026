# Scientific review

## Correctness
The finite-depth eigenvalue recurrence is independently reconstructed from the half-line Jacobi equation, including the Dirichlet condition and all two-site matching conditions at the exceptional edge. Monotonicity of the secular equation gives both the sharp threshold and uniqueness; the essential spectral interval accounts for the non-eigenvalue case. The scaling limit follows by monotone-root convergence; normalized-eigenfunction strong convergence follows from a left Riemann sum and an exact right geometric series. The limiting mass ratio follows by integrating the squared sinh profile. The supplied numerical checker tests the recurrence, finite sections, and scaling at nontrivial parameters, but is not substituted for the proof.

## Originality
The 2015 Undrakh–Nakazato–Vandanjav–Chien theorem gives the general eventual-unit-weight characteristic equation and eigenfunction recurrence, and hence subsumes the fixed-depth algebraic part. The 2022 Chakraborty–Ojha–Birbonshi paper extends related spectral equations. Sharif (2023) develops the numerical-radius norm perspective and boundary perturbations. The claimed new mathematical point is confined to the joint critical window with a moving edge and the explicit *strongly normalized continuum eigenfunction and mass-partition limit*. A cross-domain equivalence with weak-coupling discrete Jacobi models remains possible; literature and database searches did not reveal one.

## Mathematical value
The critical window is forced by the finite-depth resonance threshold rather than chosen arbitrarily. It resolves how the isolated numerical-radius eigenmode nucleates from the essential spectral edge, and describes its spatial mass distribution and its limiting localization at long range. This gives a natural structural invariant invisible to fixed-depth determinantal formulas and potentially useful for analyzing nearly resonant weighted-shift norm attainment. The result is an exact nontrivial continuum-limit classification for all fixed supercritical strengths, not a finite computational census.

## Limitations and audit status
The theorem is specialized to a Hilbert shift with one positive edge defect, and the eigenmode convergence requires fixed supercritical scaling. General prior spectral recurrences cover the finite-depth identity. Related Jacobi weak-coupling papers might contain the same normalized profile under another notation. Independent scientific examination has not been conducted.
