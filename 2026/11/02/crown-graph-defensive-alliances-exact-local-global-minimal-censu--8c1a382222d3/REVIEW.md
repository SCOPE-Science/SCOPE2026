# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

Every crown-graph vertex has degree \(n-1\). The exact alliance criterion is \(x,y\ge t\) for zero shore-index overlap and \(x,y\ge s=t+1\) for positive overlap. The local-minimal classifications follow by deleting one vertex and checking the overlap transition. The global-minimal classification follows by bounding the two possible deletions needed to reach a disjoint smaller alliance: exactly two common indices permit such deletions, three or more do not. Ordered pairs of index sets yield the full two-term polynomials. Explicit binomial identities and Stirling's formula establish both parity cases and exponential rarity. Graph-level testing of 1,398,072 nonempty subsets over eight graph orders independently corroborates the formulas; it does not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS, provisional

The closest works address upper alliance numbers and complexity (Bazgan–Fernau–Tuza, 2018 online), global minimality and neighborhood-diversity algorithms (Gaikwad–Maity, 2022), local minimality and parameterization (Gaikwad–Maity–Saurabh, 2022), and minimal-alliance enumeration, including trees (Feng–Fernau–Mann, 2023). The accessible texts and relevant result statements were examined. Ibrahim's defensive-alliance polynomial (2018) counts connected induced vertex sets by order and a minimum-degree-balance statistic rather than the inclusion-minimal local/global sets counted here; its complete-bipartite formula is not the crown-graph result. Available indexed research findings were queried for all principal formulations and the closest differently defined alliance records compared. No inspected claim covers these two crown-graph cardinality polynomials, their local-only overlap-two class, or their odd central-binomial-square count. The failure to locate an equivalent is not a guarantee of absolute priority; an unindexed equivalent or alternative terminology remains a risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The difference between locally minimal and globally minimal defensive alliances is a substantive issue in existing complexity and enumeration research. A canonical connected regular bipartite family exposes the exact exceptional obstruction, with a fully labeled polynomial census and parity-dependent closed totals. The odd-case square law and exponentially small local/global discrepancy give information not implied by minimum or upper defensive-alliance numbers. This is a complete, naturally motivated classification rather than a numerical slice or a general-algorithm restatement.
