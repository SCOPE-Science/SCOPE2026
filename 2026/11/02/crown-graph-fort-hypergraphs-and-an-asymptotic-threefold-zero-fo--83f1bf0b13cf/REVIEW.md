# Scientific review: Crown-graph forts and forcing parameters

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is an infinite-family classification, not a computational extrapolation. Every exterior neighborhood count follows directly from the labelled definition of the crown graph: an exterior vertex on one side sees all selected vertices of the other side except its indexed nonneighbor. The proof first establishes the two one-sided triple families. It then uses them as proper-fort obstructions to eliminate every larger mixed candidate and checks the four possible small side cardinalities, leaving precisely the aligned \(2+2\) sets. The minimality argument considers *all* proper forts, not merely one-vertex deletions.

The standard fort-transversal characterization is proved within the result. It yields a complete characterization of all successful initial blue sets by their white complements and therefore the full polynomial, minimum cardinality, and minimum-set count. The fractional optimum follows from summing all triple inequalities on each side and checking a uniform feasible assignment. Equality establishes the claimed uniqueness for \(n\ge4\); the nonunique \(n=3\) endpoint is exhibited explicitly. Disjoint-fort packings attain the fractional upper bound after rounding down in each residue class modulo three. All quantifiers and boundaries are \(n\ge3\). A separate checker exhaustively tests the original graph, every proper fort subset, complete forcing dynamics, packing, and small linear programs.

## Originality — PASS, with residual source-access and priority risks

Cameron, Hogben, Kenter, Mojallal, and Schuerger, *Forts, (fractional) zero forcing, and Cartesian products of graphs* (arXiv:2310.17904), introduce the obstruction framework and fractional invariant. Their inspected 25-page manuscript determines the complete-bipartite case and gives a table of its fractional zero-forcing number; it does not state the classification after deleting a perfect matching, its forcing polynomial, or the asymptotic gap of three. The present result explicitly acknowledges and uses their general definitions and fort-transversal theorem as prior foundations.

Becker, Cameron, Hanely, Ong, and Previte, *On the number of minimal forts of a graph* (arXiv:2404.05963), inspectably treats complete bipartite graphs in Proposition 9, other individual families, and join/corona/vertex-sum constructions. That work does not classify the crown-graph minimal forts; its product results do not concern the categorical product realization of a crown graph. Two accessible published descriptions of minimal forts for complete multipartite graphs were also read in full. Their minimal forts have cardinalities two or three and yield a different fractional formula. A crown graph with at least six vertices is not complete multipartite, and it has essential four-vertex aligned-pair minimal forts, so those classifications neither contain nor imply the present one.

Abiad and Ghasemi Nezhad, *Fort Abundance in Zero Forcing* (arXiv:2609.11754v1, first public date 2026-09-10), is the motivating current study. Its accessible abstract describes tree and block-graph counting, general fort abundance, and dense graph lower bounds; it does not announce a crown formula. Its complete manuscript could not be retrieved for direct inspection, so no whole-document noncoverage claim is made. Semantic-record searches and targeted searches for crown graphs, matching-deleted bicliques, bipartite double covers, minimal forts, fractional zero forcing, and the precise claimed ratio found no covering result. Inaccessibility and imperfect indexing remain real historical-priority risks and do not constitute proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The object and invariants are motivated by the fort formulation of standard zero forcing and its fractional relaxation. The result provides the entire obstruction hypergraph, not merely an isolated small count: all coefficients of the zero-forcing polynomial, every minimum forcing placement, the packing optimum, the fractional optimum, and uniqueness of the fractional optimizer for all but the smallest case. Deleting one perfect matching from \(K_{n,n}\) changes its fractional zero-forcing number from \(n\) to \(2n/3\) while changing its integer zero-forcing number only from \(2n-2\) to \(2n-4\). This creates an exact asymptotic integrality-gap transition from two to three, a structural phenomenon not entailed by the complete-bipartite formulas. The three- and four-vertex obstructions also explain the complete forcing enumerator and admit future study of transversal polytopes and other forcing models. No global sharpness claim across all graphs is made.

## Limitations

Only ordinary zero forcing on labelled crown graphs with \(n\ge3\) is addressed. No positive-semidefinite or skew forcing claim is made. Finite checks are corroboration only; the infinite classification rests on the proof. The accessible motivating paper's full text and some possible differently indexed historical formulations remain unresolved. Self-review does not replace independent mathematical assessment.
