# Curvilinear singular schemes of elementary-symmetric cubics in characteristic two

## Finding

Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(2\), and let \(n=4m\) for an arbitrary integer \(m\ge1\). Put \(R=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\), and let \(F=e_3(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\sum_{i<j<\ell}x_ix_jx_\ell\). Write \(J=(\partial F/\partial x_1,\ldots,\partial F/\partial x_n)\). For every subset \(S\subset[n]\) with \(|S|\equiv1\pmod4\), choose \(a\in S\) and \(b\notin S\), and define the homogeneous primary ideal

\[
Q_S=\bigl(x_i-x_a:i\in S\setminus\{a\},\quad x_j-x_b:j\in[n]\setminus(S\cup\{b\}),\quad x_b^2\bigr).
\]

All these ideals are independent of the choices of \(a\) and \(b\). The exact primary decomposition is

\[
\boxed{\ J=\bigcap_{\substack{S\subset[n]\\ |S|\equiv1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)}} Q_S.\ }
\]

Consequently the singular scheme of the cubic hypersurface \(V(F)\subset\mathbb P^{n-1}_k\) is a disjoint union of \(2^{n-2}\) curvilinear length-two points, located at the indicator vectors \([\mathbf1_S]\) with \(|S|\equiv1\pmod4\). It has total length \(2^{n-1}\), has no embedded associated points, and is a projective complete intersection of \(n-1\) quadrics. Its homogeneous coordinate ring has Hilbert series and Hilbert function

\[
\boxed{\ H_{R/J}(t)=\frac{(1+t)^{n-1}}{1-t},\qquad
\dim_k(R/J)_d=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(d,n-1)}\binom{n-1}{j}\quad(d\ge0).\ }
\]

The ambient cubic equation belongs to \(J\), so no additional equation is needed to define the singular scheme.

## Assumptions and scope

All scheme statements are over an algebraically closed field of characteristic exactly \(2\). The integer \(n\) is any positive multiple of \(4\), including \(n=4\). Each \(S\) in the intersection is nonempty and proper automatically. The vector \(\mathbf1_S\) has coordinate \(1\) on \(S\) and \(0\) outside. The ideals are homogeneous in the standard grading. The phrase *curvilinear length-two point* means that its local Artin ring is \(k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\); it does not assert that the underlying hypersurface singularity is an ordinary node.

## Proof

**Step 1: the Jacobian ideal has only \(n-1\) necessary generators.** Write \(g_i=\partial F/\partial x_i=e_2(x_1,\ldots,\widehat{x_i},\ldots,x_n)\). Every unordered pair contributes to exactly \(n-2\) of the derivatives. Since \(n\equiv0\pmod4\),

\[
\sum_{i=1}^n g_i=(n-2)e_2=0.
\]

Thus \(J=(g_1,\ldots,g_{n-1})\). Euler's homogeneous identity gives \(\sum_i x_i g_i=3F=F\) in characteristic two. Hence \(F\in J\).

**Step 2: classify every geometric zero.** For distinct indices \(i,j\), direct cancellation yields

\[
g_i+g_j=(x_i+x_j)\sum_{\ell\ne i,j}x_\ell
=(x_i+x_j)(T+x_i+x_j),\qquad T=\sum_\ell x_\ell.
\]

At a common zero in \(k^n\), if coordinates \(a\ne b\) occur then \(T=a+b\), and comparison with either of them forces every coordinate to equal \(a\) or \(b\). If \(a\) occurs \(r\) times, then \(T=ra+(n-r)b\), so \(r\) must be odd. Evaluating \(g_i\) at an index with value \(a\) gives

\[
\binom{r-1}{2}a^2+(r-1)(n-r)ab+\binom{n-r}{2}b^2.
\]

The middle coefficient is even. When \(r\equiv1\pmod4\), the other two coefficients reduce to \(0,1\), so the expression equals \(b^2\) and forces \(b=0\). When \(r\equiv3\pmod4\), they reduce to \(1,0\), so it equals \(a^2\) and forces \(a=0\). Thus every nonzero common zero is a scalar multiple of an indicator vector of cardinality congruent to \(1\) modulo \(4\). Conversely, substitution at any such indicator vector makes every \(g_i\) zero. If all coordinates coincide with \(a\), then \(g_i=\binom{n-1}{2}a^2=a^2\), so the only such zero is the origin. The projective zero locus is therefore finite and has exactly the displayed supports.

**Step 3: complete intersection and degree.** The homogeneous affine zero set of \(J\) is the union of the lines through those projective points. Therefore \(\operatorname{ht}(J)=n-1\). Since \(J\) is generated by \(n-1\) quadrics in the regular Cohen–Macaulay ring \(R\), these form a regular sequence. The quotient \(R/J\) is one-dimensional Cohen–Macaulay, hence \(J\) is saturated with respect to the homogeneous maximal ideal. The projective scheme has degree \(2^{n-1}\) by the complete-intersection degree formula and the displayed Hilbert series.

**Step 4: explicit primary components and equality.** Fix \(S\) of permitted cardinality \(s\), with \(a\in S\) and \(b\notin S\). Modulo \(Q_S\), all variables in \(S\) become \(u=x_a\), all others become \(v=x_b\), and \(v^2=0\). For \(i\in S\), the coefficients of \(u^2\) and \(uv\) in \(g_i\) are \(\binom{s-1}{2}\) and \((s-1)(n-s)\); for \(i\notin S\), they are \(\binom{s}{2}\) and \(s(n-s-1)\). All four integers are even because \(s\equiv1\pmod4\) and \(n\equiv0\pmod4\). Terms involving \(v^2\) vanish. Thus \(g_i\in Q_S\) for every \(i\), giving \(J\subseteq\bigcap_S Q_S\).

Each \(R/Q_S\cong k[u,v]/(v^2)\) is a one-dimensional Cohen–Macaulay graded ring. Hence \(Q_S\) is saturated and primary to the line ideal of \(\mathbf1_S\), and its projectivization is a length-two curvilinear point. The supports of distinct \(Q_S\)'s are disjoint. The roots-of-unity filter gives

\[
\#\{S:|S|\equiv1\pmod4\}=\sum_{s\equiv1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)}\binom ns=2^{n-2}
\quad\text{when }4\mid n.
\]

Thus the closed subscheme defined by \(\bigcap_S Q_S\) has length \(2\cdot2^{n-2}=2^{n-1}\), exactly the degree of \(\operatorname{Proj}(R/J)\). Since it is a closed subscheme of the latter and both are finite, equal length forces equality of projective schemes. Both homogeneous ideals are saturated, so they are equal. This proves the primary decomposition and all stated consequences.

## Relationship to prior work

Ying Xie's 2026 study of derivative and higher-multiplicity loci of elementary symmetric polynomials proves general dimension formulas and generic local multiplicities along coordinate subspaces (Theorems 1.1 and 1.4). Its Section 7 explicitly leaves full global primary decompositions and embedded components unclassified. Those dimension and local statements do not give the present complete decomposition, the indicator-support census, or the absence of embedded components for all \(n\equiv0\pmod4\). Ian Orzel's 2025–26 work concerns dimensions of order-two zero loci, not full primary decompositions. The characteristic-zero singular-locus discussion of Meckler and Zaimi is likewise not a characteristic-two scheme classification. The new input here is the explicit characteristic-two two-value analysis combined with the global complete-intersection degree comparison.

## Limitations

This is a theorem about the Jacobian/singular scheme for the degree-three elementary symmetric hypersurface when \(4\mid n\) in characteristic \(2\). No analogous complete decomposition is asserted for other degrees, other characteristics, or \(n\equiv2\pmod4\). The proof does not classify analytic types of the hypersurface singularities or derive circuit-complexity consequences. Historical priority beyond the inspected references is not independently certified.
