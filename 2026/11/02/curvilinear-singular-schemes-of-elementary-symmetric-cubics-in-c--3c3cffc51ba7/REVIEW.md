# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The derivative-difference identity reduces every field-valued critical point to at most two coordinate values; the parity analysis shows the only projective supports are indicator vectors of cardinality congruent to \(1\) modulo \(4\). Exactly \(n-1\) gradient quadrics generate an ideal of codimension \(n-1\), hence a complete intersection of degree \(2^{n-1}\). The explicit primary ideals contain the Jacobian ideal and contribute the same total projective degree, forcing equality because both are saturated. Finite exact checks corroborate the first two nontrivial variable counts. The argument uses algebraic closure and characteristic two exactly as stated.

## Originality

PASS, subject to historical-priority limitations. Xie's arXiv:2610.00259, Theorems 1.1 and 1.4, gives dimension and certain generic local rings, but not global primary decompositions and explicitly leaves these unresolved in Section 7. Orzel's arXiv:2509.05009 gives dimension bounds, and the Meckler–Zaimi discussion considers primarily characteristic zero. Targeted searches for the indicator-support scheme, complete intersection, and Hilbert series found no equivalent stated result. Closely related Jacobian-scheme constructions in other examples do not imply the present all-degree-two-generator family. A differently phrased published algebraic-combinatorial classification remains a residual risk.

## Value

PASS. An exact primary decomposition answers a specifically identified gap in the scheme structure of a symmetric polynomial family. The result simultaneously gives all components, their nonreduced local rings, the absence of embedded components, a complete-intersection presentation, and the Hilbert function for every \(n\equiv0\pmod4\). The exponential count of support points also demonstrates why restricting attention to coordinate axes misses most components already at \(n=8\).

## Scope and outstanding limitations

The theorem is restricted to characteristic \(2\), cubic elementary symmetric forms, and positive multiples of four variables. It is not a complete treatment of other characteristic strata, analytic singularity types, or arithmetic circuit lower bounds. No independent validation has been performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
