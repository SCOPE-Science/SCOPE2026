# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof uses the standard active-cell structure of positive-mass isotonic regression, a cyclic-interval representative-sampling contraction lemma, and an edgewise convex minimum principle. Each actual cover edge admits a locally realized two-vertex pooling cell; this fact is essential to the necessity direction. The exact supplementary `verify.py` tests 59,460 cyclic-interval averaging cases, 66 local rigidity profiles and the diamond ratio \(5/4\). Finite checks are not substituted for the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual uncertainty.** The closest inspected sources are Németh and Németh (arXiv:1201.4677 and arXiv:1503.08458), Guyader and collaborators (arXiv:1211.3930), and Yang and Barber (arXiv:1706.01852). In particular, the Yang–Barber norm-contraction theorem is for a chain, whose cover graph is a path. None of these inspected statements contains the cyclic-cover all-positive-mass weighted-edge rigidity. A possible implicit equivalent in older active-set sensitivity literature remains unresolved, and negative searches cannot prove novelty.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** This is a complete stability boundary for a natural non-chain family of order cones with two independent weighting systems. It identifies an abrupt loss of contraction for every nonconstant edge weighting and supplies a sharp four-vertex obstruction, without relying on a fabricated broad application.

**Scope and risks.** The theorem does not determine the exact Lipschitz modulus for nonuniform edge weights or apply to general graph-shaped orders. The proof and tests are self-contained; publication priority remains subject to additional expert and literature scrutiny.
