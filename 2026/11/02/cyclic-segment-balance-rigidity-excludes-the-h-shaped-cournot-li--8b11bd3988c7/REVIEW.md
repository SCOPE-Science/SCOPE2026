# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

A finite rectilinear skeleton has finitely many maximal axis segments; a cyclic coordinate map sends each such segment into one segment of the next orientation or collapses it. Strong invariance requires every target segment to receive a nondegenerate same-orientation image, as all other images meet that target in finitely many points. Thus the cyclic inequalities \(N_{i+1}\leq N_i\) force equality, bijection, and exact segment images; orientation rotation gives cycle lengths divisible by \(n\). The separate direct calculation of the image of the H-shape confirms the special case. No numerical experiment is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS under inspected evidence

García Guirao (arXiv:2610.27229v1), Corollary D, establishes balanced segment counts **conditional on a finite coordinate omega-limit**; its Section 11 explicitly asks whether the H-shape can arise without that condition. The new statement is a general strong-invariance obstruction for any finite rectilinear skeleton and extends to all cyclic coordinate dimensions. The 2021 Linero-Bas–Muñoz-Guillermo source treats nonempty interiors and individual examples, not this universal condition. Retrieved mathematical-record results and accessible prior claims were compared by hypotheses and conclusions, not merely titles. A broader unnamed geometric invariance result remains a possible historical-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The H-shape is singled out as an open realizability question in the source; the unconditional impossibility settles that precise question. The underlying orientation-permutation theorem is a reusable structural obstruction: any proposed finite segment geometry with unequal directional counts is impossible even as a strongly invariant set, without dynamics-specific symbolic or smoothness assumptions. The higher-dimensional cycle-divisibility condition adds a natural geometric restriction. The theorem does not address general infinite or curved omega-limit sets.
