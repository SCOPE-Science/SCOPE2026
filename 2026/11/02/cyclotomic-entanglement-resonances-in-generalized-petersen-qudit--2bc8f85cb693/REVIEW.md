# Scientific review

## Correctness (C): PASS

The asserted graph-state spectrum follows from a finite-field additive-character computation, not only a rank assertion. Direct edge accounting gives the balanced cut block and its odd-skip Schur complement. The cyclic module argument proves nullity as a polynomial gcd degree even in inseparable characteristic, which is crucial for the valuation-dependent special cases. The polynomial factorizations in characteristics two and three have been multiplied out independently. The supplied program reconstructs graphs from edges rather than from the claimed circulant block and passes 1,740 finite-field rank comparisons and five separate coefficient-spectrum cases. The mathematical proof, not these tests, covers all parameters.

## Originality (O): PASS on examined evidence, provisional

The established graph-state cut-rank theorem [Hein–Eisert–Briegel, arXiv:quant-ph/0307130] and prime-dimensional controlled-phase formalism [Looi et al., arXiv:0712.1979] are explicitly treated as known. Gera–Stănică's complete *complex adjacency* spectrum of generalized Petersen graphs (Australasian J. Combin. 49, 39–45) does not give the modular cut-block nullity or repeated-root valuation law. Wu–Kampermann–Bruß (arXiv:1504.03302) give a general X-chain Schmidt-decomposition approach but not this graph-family classification. Related periodic-grid and line-graph qudit results treat different underlying graphs, cut matrices and parameter reductions and do not imply this statement. No examined external statement or accessible comparison directly establishes the cyclotomic-gcd theorem and the two \(k=5\) staircases. Residual risk: an earlier specialized modular-circulant or graph-state computation could cover the same cut.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS

The entire nonzero spectrum of an infinite, sparse, cubic quantum-network family is decided by a polynomial of degree at most \(k+1\), independently of total network size. The prime-dependent transition between always-deficient and intermittently maximally mixed cuts, together with exact two-adic and three-adic defect staircases at fixed network geometry, is a structural and physically interpretable finite-size effect. It is not an isolated numeric rank or a restatement of the universal graph-state recipe. The result can benchmark stabilizer-network entanglement resource structure; no unsupported practical quantum advantage is claimed.

## Closest literature and limitations

The general cut-rank correspondence and generalized Petersen circulant spectrum are the closest methodological predecessors. This theorem is restricted to the specified balanced bipartition, pure unweighted prime-dimensional graph states, and does not decide arbitrary cuts. Numerical tests do not establish priority, and independent review could still discover prior coverage.
