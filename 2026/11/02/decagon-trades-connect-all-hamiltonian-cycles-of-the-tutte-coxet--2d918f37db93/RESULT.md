# Decagon trades connect all Hamiltonian cycles of the Tutte–Coxeter graph

## Finding
Let \(T\) be the Tutte–Coxeter graph on the duads and synthemes of a six-element set. A *duad* is an unordered two-element subset, and a *syntheme* partitions the six symbols into three duads. There is an edge for each containment of a duad in a syntheme, giving 30 vertices and 45 edges. Let \(\mathcal H_{10}(T)\) have undirected Hamiltonian 30-cycles of \(T\) as vertices; distinct cycles are adjacent when their symmetric difference as edge sets is exactly one alternating ten-cycle. Both intermediate configurations must be Hamiltonian.

The complete result is
\[
|V(\mathcal H_{10}(T))|=144,\quad |E(\mathcal H_{10}(T))|=504,\quad \deg=7,\quad \operatorname{rad}=\operatorname{diam}=4.
\]
The graph is connected and, from **each** vertex, its numbers of vertices at distances zero through four are precisely \((1,7,36,85,15)\). No smaller single alternating-cycle trade connects distinct Hamiltonian cycles. Therefore an alternating ten-cycle is the minimum Hamiltonicity-preserving local trade, and at most four such trades transform any Hamiltonian cycle into any other.

## Assumptions and scope
The graph is the **simple**, **undirected** incidence graph: vertices are the 15 pairs of \(\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\) and the 15 partitions of these symbols into three disjoint pairs; a pair is incident with a partition exactly when the pair belongs to that partition. Each graph edge occurs once. An undirected Hamiltonian cycle is a 30-edge subset forming a connected degree-two spanning subgraph; traversal orientations and starting points do not distinguish configurations. An alternating ten-cycle trade replaces its five present edges by its five absent edges and requires the resulting 30-edge set to be a Hamiltonian cycle. The theorem concerns this specific graph and move, not arbitrary cubic graphs or trades of length greater than ten.

## Proof
**Complement reduction.** In any cubic graph, the edge complement of a perfect matching is a spanning two-factor, and conversely. It is a Hamiltonian cycle if and only if the two-factor is connected. The complements of two perfect matchings have the same edge-set symmetric difference as the original matchings. Their symmetric difference is a disjoint union of even alternating cycles. The incidence graph has girth eight: two distinct duads belong together to at most one syntheme, ruling out 4-cycles; a 6-cycle would need three mutually disjoint duads and three different synthemes containing their respective pairs, although the unique partition of those six symbols is the only syntheme containing any two of them. Thus a nonempty symmetric difference with ten edges must be one alternating ten-cycle. Conversely, every ten-edge symmetric difference of two Hamiltonian complements is a legal ten-cycle trade.

**Exhaustive enumeration with an explicit finite algorithm.** Order the 45 incidence edges lexicographically. Represent a perfect matching by a 45-bit integer with its selected edges set. Starting from all 30 unmatched vertices, choose the least unmatched vertex and recurse over its (at most three) unmatched neighbors, deleting each chosen pair and recording the corresponding edge bit. This enumerates each perfect matching exactly once, since its partner at the least unmatched vertex is forced in that matching. Exhaustion returns precisely 288 distinct matchings. For each, retain the 30 edges complementary to its 15 matching edges and find connected components of their degree-two spanning subgraph. The resulting counts are exactly 144 with a single 30-cycle and 144 with components of sizes 10 and 20. Only the former 144 matchings are retained.

Compare all \(\binom{144}{2}=10{,}296\) unordered retained pairs. The symmetric differences have at least ten edges. Exactly 504 have symmetric difference consisting of ten edges; by the preceding girth argument these are precisely the legal trades. Each of 144 configurations is incident with exactly seven such pairs, consistent with \(144\cdot7/2=504\). Breadth-first search from each of the 144 configurations covers all 144 and gives the same distance histogram \((1,7,36,85,15)\). Since this profile has nonzero entries at distance four and zero beyond, every vertex has eccentricity four. Hence the trade graph is connected and self-centered with radius and diameter four.

**Independent generation.** A separate procedure enumerates Hamiltonian cycles directly as simple spanning paths anchored at the smallest vertex, retaining a path only if its last vertex is adjacent to its first. The path is normalized against reversal. Its 144 unique edge sets, their ten-edge differences, 504 trades and all 144 distance profiles agree with the matching-complement computation. This traversal does not rely on perfect-matching enumeration.

**Minimality.** A nontrivial symmetric difference of two perfect matchings is a union of even alternating cycles, all of length at least eight; the complete comparison above finds no two Hamiltonian complements at difference eight. Thus every Hamiltonicity-preserving one-cycle exchange needs at least ten edges, and the verified 504 ten-cycle trades attain this lower bound.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_matchings.py` and `python3 verify_cycles.py` in the package directory, using Python 3 and only the standard library. Both scripts reconstruct the 30-vertex incidence graph, enumerate their entire respective configuration spaces, and assert all graph and distance invariants. The matching script checks all 10,296 pairs and all 144 BFS roots; the cycle script enumerates directly by DFS and independently checks all 144 BFS roots. These exhaustive finite algorithms are part of the proof certificate, not samples or a claim about infinitely many graph orders.

## Relationship to prior work
Eppstein (first public 20 February 2003) studies algorithms for finding and listing Hamiltonian cycles in cubic graphs; this motivates efficient exact enumeration but does not give the Tutte–Coxeter ten-cycle trade graph. The classical duad–syntheme description is documented by the 2015 AMS Visual Insight exposition. Takaoka (2018) studies reconfiguration of Hamiltonian cycles by **two-edge switches**, a different move from the five-edge exchange here. Ito and collaborators (2019) study perfect-matching reconfiguration by alternating cycles in general graphs, without a claim about the 144 Hamiltonian-complement configurations and their ten-cycle distances. Previously established computations for this same graph count 288 perfect matchings in two complementary two-factor classes, and describe the graph of **eight-cycle flips on all matchings**. Such eight-cycle flips always move between the two factor classes; connectivity of that 288-state graph does not imply connectivity of the present 144-state graph under ten-cycle moves. Neither the established counts nor that eight-cycle graph is claimed as new.

## Limitations
This is an exact finite classification, not an asymptotic theorem or a proof of uniqueness in all historical literature. The originality comparison is limited to recoverable research and inspected sources, and another account under decagon, \(k\)-opt, or Hamiltonian switching terminology remains possible. Reconfiguration is restricted to one alternating ten-cycle per step and requires every intermediate state to be Hamiltonian. Independently conducted scholarly verification has not been performed.
