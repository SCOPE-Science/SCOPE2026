# Scientific review

**Correctness: PASS.** For a nonzero pure tensor, direct expansion of the original associative Yang–Baxter equation yields precisely three tensor monomials. Projection onto quotient tensor factors forces the two quadratic factor relations, and associativity forces the product of their scalar multipliers to vanish. Conversely, these relations imply the original equation; factor normalization gives three exhaustive types. The matrix-unit tensor-to-bilinear-map isomorphism proves both directions of the Rota–Baxter correspondence. Square-zero image/kernel flags and complementary idempotent subspaces give the Gaussian-binomial rank enumerator with the necessary factor-rescaling quotient. Every proof step works in arbitrary characteristic; all stated finite checks are corroborative rather than proofs.

**Originality: PASS (provisional historical priority).** The original Aguiar paper (arXiv:math/0211074) supplies the AYBE convention and the AYBE-to-Rota–Baxter correspondence, including a special square-zero example, which are explicitly credited. Bai–Guo–Ni (arXiv:0910.3261) provides operator correspondences; Schedler (arXiv:math/0212258) studies trigonometric spectral-parameter solutions; Sokolov (arXiv:1212.6421) and Zobnin (arXiv:1310.1193) primarily study skew-symmetric solutions. Their inspected material did not state a full decomposable, all-size, all-finite-field rank enumerator. The earlier exact rank-one *linear-operator* census in full matrix algebras overlaps the rank-one coefficient, but does not imply the higher-rank coefficients of elementary multipliers. A broader unlocated theorem may subsume the result, so independent priority certification is unavailable.

**Value: PASS.** The Segre/decomposable stratum is a natural first geometric locus of the associative Yang–Baxter equation; elementary left-right multipliers are a natural and independently recognizable class of Rota–Baxter operators. The three-type classification and the sharp rank ceiling expose structural rigidity in all characteristics, while the explicit all-size Gaussian-binomial polynomial supplies previously unaccounted rank-by-rank data. This is not a reformulation of a single rank-one count.
