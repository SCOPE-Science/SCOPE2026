# Scientific review: Degeneracy-only splitting of Schottky peaks

## Correctness — PASS
The claimed domain is an exactly three-level canonical spectrum with energies \(0,\Delta,2\Delta\), positive \(\Delta\), and integer multiplicities \(1,M^2,M^3\) for \(M\ge256\). Differentiation of the canonical variance yields a factored third-cumulant numerator. The four ordered derivative checkpoints have signs \(+,-,+,-\); the two negative signs follow from explicit positive rational lower bounds \(294424/789173\) and \(1341440000/2975509823\), both larger than \(1/3\). This establishes **at least two** strict maxima, not an unproved exact peak count. A two-level logistic reduction in each of two separated inverse-temperature windows proves the stated locations and heights; the midpoint heat capacity is an exact finite expression. The included independent moment computation and rational-form calculator passed 256 checks. Risks: finite tests cannot establish the uniform parameter theorem, so acceptance relies on the detailed analytic proof; further stationary points outside the proved branches are not ruled out.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence, with priority risk
The closest primary source is de Souza et al., arXiv:1504.07525, which derives a general three-level partition-function formula and documents qualitative double peaks and degeneracy dependence. Its fully inspected sections II–V do not state the uniform equal-gap cutoff \(M\ge256\), the two separated logarithmic inverse-temperature branches, or the vanishing intermediate heat-capacity test value. The source's general canonical formula does not by itself prove the quantified derivative-sign or asymptotic statements; those require new inequalities and estimates. The ferrimagnet preprint arXiv:cond-mat/0206555 explains physical double peaks by ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic excitations, not the displayed equal-gap degeneracy scaling, but only its abstract was available in this review. The older three-level study attributed to Meijer, Colwell and Shah in the 2015 bibliography was not inspected in full; a previously established equivalent construction is a genuine residual risk. Public web searches and the available published-claims index did not return an exact or stronger statement, which is evidence of scope only, not proof of priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result addresses the concrete thermodynamic interpretation of multiple Schottky maxima. It shows with a uniform proved finite threshold that peak separation can be produced by entropy/degeneracy hierarchy even when all adjacent excitation gaps coincide; hence two heat-capacity peaks need not indicate distinct bare excitation-gap magnitudes. The logarithmic peak positions, growth rates and explicit inter-peak suppression explain the mechanism quantitatively. The sufficient threshold is not claimed to be optimal, and realistic microscopic mechanisms yielding very large prescribed multiplet degeneracies are not established.
