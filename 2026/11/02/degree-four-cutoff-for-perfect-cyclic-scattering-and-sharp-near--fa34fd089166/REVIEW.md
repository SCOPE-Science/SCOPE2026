# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The classification uses the exact established unitary-circulant eigenvalue data and on-shell Möbius scattering map, not a numerical approximation. Pairwise Fourier conjugacy rules out global phases, upper-half-plane rigidity excludes alternative cyclic destinations, and strict monotonicity of the modal matching function proves the all-degree cutoff. The optimized leakage is derived by a positive quadratic projection, with the global optimizing branch justified by uniform endpoint control and a strict Hessian. The standalone finite verification program gives independent algebraic/numerical consistency checks, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (within the inspected literature)

The 2018 Exner–Turek–Tater paper explicitly gives the family and full S-matrix formula, including degree-four expressions. The 2017 Exner–Tater paper explicitly gives perfect cyclic scattering at the pure rotation endpoint, and the 2024 Exner–Pekař paper studies related periodic chain spectra. None of those inspected texts states the exhaustive sub-endpoint degree cutoff together with the strict high-degree globally optimized leakage coefficient. Searches for cyclic quantum-star routing, interpolation, finite-degree thresholds, and optimized scattering loss found no exact overlap. These conclusions remain subject to broader publication-priority uncertainty; the prior formulas substantially enable the new derivation.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The degree boundary is a structural constraint on deterministic coherent routing: one real momentum tunes the single nontrivial Fourier-pair condition for degrees three/four, whereas degrees five and above require incompatible modal phase matches. The positive sharp leakage coefficient quantifies this obstruction under optimal operating-frequency adjustment and supplies explicit design-scale constants, not merely a qualitative nonexistence claim.

## Limitations and priority risk

The precise family of self-adjoint vertex couplings is fixed; other network elements could achieve circulation. The loss coefficient is for \(\alpha=0\) as \(t\uparrow1\), not an all-parameter optimal control theorem. Related phase-matching calculations might appear in uninspected literature or be inferred from the published scattering formulas.
