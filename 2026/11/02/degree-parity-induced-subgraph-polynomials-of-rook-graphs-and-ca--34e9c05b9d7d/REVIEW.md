# Scientific review: degree-parity induced rook-subgraph polynomials

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is an exact coefficient identity for all integers \(m,n\ge1\), followed by an exact two-row specialization and support classification. An occupied rook cell \((i,j)\) has induced degree \(d_i+e_j-2\) where \(d_i,e_j\) count selected cells in its row and column. This proves the equal/opposite parity criterion. Every matrix has a unique partition of rows and columns by sum parity; these classes force exactly two independent allowed rectangles, with margins modulo two prescribed. A direct \(\{\pm1\}\)-Fourier projection proves the finite binary-matrix polynomial. For two rows, presence of any doubly selected column forbids singly selected columns; the remaining case is two disjoint cliques. These disjoint cases give the elementary closed forms and exact supports. Critical boundary cases include empty induced graphs, zero-dimensional parity blocks, \(m=1\), and \(n=1\). All are included explicitly. The standard-library verifier reconstructed direct graph adjacency and tested 1,412,734 vertex subsets across 19 general rook graphs and ten prisms, as well as 64 Fourier blocks and 60 further symbolic supports. Finite tests corroborate but do not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with bounded priority uncertainty
Belmonte–Sau (arXiv:2002.06078, first posted 2020-02-14), the closest directly relevant work inspected in accessible article text, defines maximum odd-degree induced subgraphs and studies graph-class bounds, parameterized complexity, and odd colorings; it does not enumerate all induced even/odd sets of rook graphs or give the complete-prism cardinality residue law. Belmonte et al. (arXiv:2303.03225) address odd coloring, not this coefficient distribution. Dörfler–Roth–Schmitt–Wellnitz (DOI:10.1007/s00453-021-00894-9) address general induced-subgraph counting hardness, not a closed all-parameter enumerator for this graph family. Searches for rook/complete-prism degree-parity enumerators, Cartesian-product formulations, bipartite incidence-matrix parity margins, and induced odd subgraph polynomials found no covering formula. Standard line-graph and parity-projection techniques are not claimed as new. Residual risk: an unindexed combinatorial enumeration or alternative formulation may exist, so independent publication priority is not certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The polynomial gives exact labeled cardinality counts for both uniform degree parities in every rectangular rook graph, a natural line-graph family, and specializes to striking sharp arithmetic holes for complete prisms. The fact that, above order \(n\), only one residue class modulo four occurs in each polynomial is a nontrivial structural boundary, not a small numerical observation. Every positive coefficient in that upper range is explicitly \(\binom n{k/2}\); all other upper sizes vanish. This refines ordinary maximum-order odd/even induced-subgraph questions to complete spectra and provides an infinite family with increasing numbers of missing even sizes. Both the general correspondence and the sharp two-row classification are necessary parts of this single theorem.
