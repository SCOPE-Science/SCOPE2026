For two intersecting circles meeting at rational angle \(p\pi/q\), every nontrivial polynomial phase ambiguity has reduced rational-quotient degree divisible by \(q\), and all such quotients admit an explicit cyclic-reflection normal form. Thus the first ambiguity occurs at polynomial degree exactly \(q\), while any \(2d+1\) distinct magnitude samples per circle recover degree-\(d\) polynomials up to global phase precisely below this threshold; irrational angles and nontransverse circle pairs remain unambiguous.
