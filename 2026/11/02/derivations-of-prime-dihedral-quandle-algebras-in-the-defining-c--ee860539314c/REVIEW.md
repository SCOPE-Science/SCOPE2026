# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The transported multiplication follows from the invertible Vandermonde change of basis between the dihedral basis and truncated exponentials. Equations for a derivation applied to \(1\star1\), \(1\star u\), and \(u\star1\) force both parameters into the stated character-eigenspace. The operators satisfy the Leibniz rule directly, and the nonassociative generator identity \(u\star u^j=-2^j u^{j+1}\) establishes uniqueness. The full Lie bracket is a direct operator commutator. An independent original-basis linear-system check agrees for primes \(3,5,7,11,17\); all predicted generators and pairwise Lie brackets were checked for these and for \(31\). Risks: the finite computations are not universal proofs; the written proof uses invertibility of factorials and powers of two, hence exactly the stated characteristic-and-prime hypotheses.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The closest published derivation analysis is Elhamdadi–Makhlouf–Silvestrov–Zappala (arXiv:2106.08289). Its Theorem 3.9 assumes characteristic zero, while its Section 4.2 explicitly tabulates only quandle orders \(3\) to \(6\) in positive characteristic. Those known \(p=3,5\) cases are expressly not new. The reviewed statement treats **every odd prime** in matching characteristic, provides the multiplicative-order dimension formula and identifies the **complete Lie brackets**, including higher-dimensional examples. Sangare–Ta (arXiv:2608.01550) gives integral odd-order automorphisms and a reduction for even orders, rather than the modular derivation Lie algebra; Zhang (arXiv:2610.08204) treats augmentation ideals. Prior related work on the same algebra's idempotent locus does not imply the derivation classification. A possible unresolved priority risk is an unindexed paper or a derived corollary not found in the examined sources. This is a best-knowledge scientific comparison, not a uniqueness certification.

## Value — PASS

The derivation Lie algebra is the intrinsic linear infinitesimal-symmetry invariant for this natural family of nonassociative algebras. The uniform characterization and bracket explain a nontrivial modular contrast to the earlier characteristic-zero rigidity, show arithmetic growth in dimensions for primes such as \(17\) and \(31\), and give exact derivation counts over every characteristic-matched finite field. The known low-order examples alone do not determine this unrestricted arithmetic behavior.

## Limitations

No claims concerning other coefficient characteristics, composite quandle orders, automorphism schemes, or higher deformation cohomology. The stand-alone verification program is finite corroboration, not exhaustive for infinitely many primes. Priority comparison cannot eliminate every unindexed source, and independent validation remains outstanding.
