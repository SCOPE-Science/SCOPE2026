# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For a stationary two-state continuous-time chain with occupancy probability \(q\), total turnover rate \(\gamma\), known independent detection efficiency \(p\), and two observation visits at lag \(\Delta\), each site's four binary-pair probabilities are explicitly derived. Differentiation of all four cells and a nuisance Schur complement at \(q=1/2\) give the displayed efficient Fisher information with denominator \((2-p)^2+4(1-p)^2r-p^2r^2\), where \(r=e^{-\gamma\Delta}\). This formula is independently reconstructed using exact rational arithmetic at 460 pairs, rather than presuming the denominator. For all detector efficiencies the logarithmic-derivative sign reduces to a scalar function negative up to \(\log2\) and strictly increasing beyond, proving a unique global maximizer. A separate parameter derivative establishes that the maximizer decreases strictly as detector efficiency increases. The unique crossing of one switching time is derived algebraically. The checker runs 2,300 rational and 863 numerical assertions. No finite test is used as a substitute for these universal arguments. Main qualification: local information only and the occupancy truth is specified at one half.

## Originality — PASS, with priority limitations

The primary archival starting point is the 2003 occupancy-transition framework of MacKenzie et al. (doi:10.1890/02-3090). Guillera-Arroita et al. (doi:10.1111/j.2041-210X.2010.00017.x) work on single-season closed occupancy study design, optimizing repeated observations and accounting for imperfect detection but not changing-state two-visit lag. Fukaya and Royle (doi:10.1890/12-1540.1) and Hines et al. (doi:10.1111/2041-210X.12186) examine dynamic state estimation or correlated surveys, but in their inspected articles no exact all-detector-efficiency lag-optimization classification appears. Michel's 2018 publicly accessible full preprint proves the limiting perfect-detection value \(0.796812130020\ldots\); that endpoint is explicitly excluded from novelty. Mehtälä et al. (doi:10.1111/rssc.12084) consider optimal observations for multistate Markov models; their full manuscript was unavailable, and a general result could conceivably subsume this specific imperfect-detection design. Published mathematical records and accessible prior claims were compared for equivalent formulations and stronger implications. The narrower novelty claim is the detector-dependent nuisance-adjusted exact information law, global monotone transition and exact detector threshold, not Markov-chain inference or perfect-detection sampling in general. Bibliographic searches cannot certify historical priority; this is self-review, not an independent originality assessment.

## Value — PASS

The optimum changes systematically with known detection quality, shifting by about \(43\%\) between the perfect-detection and vanishing-detection limits, and the exact cutoff \(\sqrt{2e}/(1+\sqrt{2e})\) identifies when the best survey separation crosses one mean switching time. This is a direct operational consequence of a core ecological measurement problem: distinguishing occupancy turnover from imperfect detection in repeated surveys, even when baseline occupancy is an unknown nuisance. The exact cutoff is more useful than substituting a perfect-detector rule for poorly detectable species. The result is deliberately a local benchmark, not a claim about field optimality, heterogeneous habitats, detector calibration, or all sampling budgets.

## Closest literature and residual risks

The perfect-observation endpoint is old and included only to delimit the new parameter range. A general hidden-Markov observation-design theorem may imply this specialization without reproducing its formula. The full Mehtälä et al. 2015 manuscript could not be examined after open-access attempts and an unsuccessful authorized institutional-access attempt.
