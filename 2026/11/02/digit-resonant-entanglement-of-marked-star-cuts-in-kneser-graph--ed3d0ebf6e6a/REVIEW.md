# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The object is the unit-weight prime-qudit Kneser graph state, cut into vertices meeting an \(s\)-element marked ground-set subset versus its complement, with \(k\ge2\), \(1\le s\le k\), and \(n\ge2k+s-1\). The graph-state character sum gives flat eigenvalues indexed by cross-matrix rank. Complementing columns turns disjointness blocks into a stack of ordinary subset-incidence matrices; the published integral chain basis diagonalizes every level simultaneously, including characteristics dividing the binomial factors. Lucas' digit criterion yields the exact first missing block and the remaining-block bound. The exact matrix checker independently compared 135 cut ranks and directly checked four small pure-state spectra; it additionally checked 4,284 resonance instances. Finite checks corroborate, not establish, infinite statements.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical priority risk

The general graph-state cut-rank identity, quantum qudit graph-state construction, Wilson's inclusion-matrix diagonalization, and Lucas' theorem are prior results, not new claims. Hein--Eisert--Briegel (2003), Looi et al. (2007), and Ghorbani et al. (2007) were examined materially. Claudet--Perdrix (2024) study a different incidence-graph equivalence problem, not the marked-star Kneser cut spectrum. The reviewed contribution is an explicit all-parameter spectral law plus a sharp, prime-power congruence condition identifying the first missing incidence block and a growth-order jump of the deficiency. No inspected source states or directly specializes this marked-star entanglement resonance. Historical priority is not certified: unlocated specialized modular-incidence applications could overlap.

## Value — PASS

Kneser graphs are symmetric sparse-network test beds, and a marked-point-star partition is a canonical geometric cut. The result distinguishes genuinely different local prime dimensions using an exact, observable entanglement spectrum. The periodic loss of a full incidence block of order \(n^{k-s-1}\), versus at most \(n^{k-s-2}\) off resonance, is a structural boundary rather than a restatement of a single small rank computation. The formula also produces a full-rank interval when \(s=k\).
