# Scientific assessment

## Correctness (PASS)
The fully quantified claim concerns planes avoiding all vertices of either centered quasiregular polyhedron, and their invertible affine images. The geometric proof reduces orientation changes to crossing a vertex-orthogonality great circle. For each rectified vertex, an exact axial antipodal pairing of its four neighbors leaves exactly two neighbor signs positive and two negative at a transverse crossing, so the crossed-edge count cannot change. A connected chamber argument extends a single exact base computation to all generic orientations. Triangle/other-face alternation follows from the face incidence of each edge. The packaged exact quadratic-integer checker verifies all 42 edge-diamond identities and both base counts, and tests additional normal directions. It does not substitute sampling for the global argument.

## Originality (PASS; priority risk remains)
The closest inspected substantive treatment is Matteo's 2014 classification (arXiv:1403.2125, introduction and classification of quasiregular three-polytopes). It establishes the two underlying solids and their symmetry, not the fixed numbers of sides intersected by every generic central plane. Freire's geometric exposition discusses rectangular vertex figures and particular equatorial hexagonal/decagonal sections; the latter are vertex-containing exceptional cuts, not the generic laws. The 2011 cuboctahedral construction literature likewise concerns facet configurations, not the asserted all-orientation incidence theorem. Searches for the section-count formulation and synonym variants found no covering statement. This supports a self-review originality assessment but cannot exclude older uncatalogued stereological descriptions or unpublished derivations.

## Mathematical value (PASS)
This is an exact natural central-section invariant for the complete class of nonregular convex quasiregular three-polyhedra, not an arbitrary slice or a recomputation of named equators. The invariant contrasts with changing central section types of familiar Platonic solids and is explained by a reusable local edge-diamond balance lemma. It also distinguishes the universal generic configuration from the historically illustrated exceptional equatorial cuts. The inherited facet-type counts and affine stability make the result directly applicable to section-complexity questions for distorted variants.

## Known limits and comparison risks
The theorem is not about off-center planes, vertex-containing exceptional planes, or unrelated semi-regular solids. Existence of the exceptional equatorial cuts is prior knowledge. The greatest residual scientific risk is an equivalent older claim in specialist descriptive geometry or stereological cross-section catalogs; no independent priority examination was performed.
