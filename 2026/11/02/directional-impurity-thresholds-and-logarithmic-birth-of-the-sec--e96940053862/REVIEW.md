# Self-review

**Correctness — PASS.** The two-site Birman–Schwinger matrix is diagonalized into symmetric and antisymmetric channels, each strictly monotone in energy. The antisymmetric edge integral is finite and positive, whereas the symmetric integral diverges. Its critical Fourier vector behaves as an inverse radial momentum, so it is bounded in position space but not square-summable. The anisotropic quadratic dispersion and angular integration give the exact coefficient \((m^2/t_x+n^2/t_y)/(16\pi\sqrt{t_xt_y})\) for the \(\delta\log(1/\delta)\) singularity, and inversion yields the energy law. The published numerical checker independently tests the anisotropic quadrature, finite-volume eigenvalues, and a slowly convergent edge-slope diagnostic. The analytic limit, not the finite checks, proves the arbitrary-separation result.

**Originality — PASS on examined evidence; historical-priority risk remains.** The general finite-rank determinant, resistor-network Green functions, and previously known isotropic point-defect mode counting are antecedents and excluded as separate novelty. The closest inspected primary materials are Cserti (arXiv:cond-mat/9909120, resistor-network integrals), Bak (DOI:10.1007/s10773-011-0993-6, rectangular two-particle Green functions), and Glasser (arXiv:1702.05111, general impurity determinants). A separately published two-dimensional lower-threshold theorem for a different finite-support potential (arXiv:1804.05339) does not imply this antisymmetric, anisotropic, fixed-two-site edge coefficient. No equivalent coefficient-and-placement statement was located in targeted published-record searches, but a more specialized historical source could cover it.

**Value — PASS.** The result quantifies how the relative direction of two fabricated defects changes the exact onset of the second localized excitation, and replaces a qualitative threshold/resistance test with an explicit, geometry-dependent, nonanalytic law for the emerging mode. The infinite lattice, exact coefficient, and non-\(\ell^2\) boundary distinguish it from elementary finite-size diagonalization.

**Limits.** Equal positive defects, finite nonzero fixed displacement, uniform positive anisotropic nearest-neighbor hoppings, and infinite-volume bound-state energies. No assertion for disorder, interactions, unequal defect strengths, or uniform scaling in displacement.
