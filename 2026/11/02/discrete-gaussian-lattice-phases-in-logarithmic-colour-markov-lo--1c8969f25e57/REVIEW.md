# Review of the discrete-Gaussian lattice-phase theorem

## Correctness — PASS
The exact colour-summed law follows by multiplying the binary-edge factors for each ordered pair, including loops. On either stated critical surface, the colour-count distribution reduces without approximation to the binomial-quadratic weights. The Lambert saddle solves the stated logarithmic stationarity equation; fixed-window Stirling expansion yields the discrete-Gaussian kernel. Exact adjacent-mass ratios decrease geometrically away from the saddle and imply uniformly summable quadratic-exponential bounds in both directions. Those bounds justify exchanging local asymptotics with the complete partition sum and establish total-variation convergence to the phase-indexed discrete Gaussian. The lattice phase coverage follows from the saddle's vanishing increment. The symmetry by complement is algebraic. Independent finite checks support the formula but are not offered as a proof of the limit.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The full random colour-count distribution is the natural order-one question after a logarithmic minority regime has been identified. A generic central limit theorem would give the wrong picture: the distribution has bounded variance and a phase-dependent integer lattice profile. The theorem provides total-variation control, the explicit nonconstant partition normalization needed for probability calculation, and every subsequential limiting law. It applies to both symmetry-related exact critical boundaries of the network, without requiring a new free parameter or an arbitrary finite slice. The result has no implication for unrelated first-order sentences or off-boundary couplings.
