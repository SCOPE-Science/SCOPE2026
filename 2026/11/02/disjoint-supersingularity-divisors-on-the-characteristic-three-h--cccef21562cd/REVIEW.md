# Review: disjoint supersingularity divisors on the characteristic-three Humbert locus

## Correctness

**PASS.** The smooth \((2,2,2)\) genus-five model has an explicit tame \((\mathbb Z/2)^4\)-action. Its five four-branch-point elliptic quotients give, by mutually orthogonal characters, a prime-to-three isogeny of the Jacobian with their product. The Legendre Hasse coefficient in characteristic \(3\) is \(-(1+\lambda)\), yielding the five displayed equations. Two distinct harmonic four-subsets of five distinct points cannot occur: they share three points, whose normal form is \((\infty,0,1)\), forcing two distinct fourth points to be \(-1\). This verifies the zero-or-one supersingular factor classification. Counting each of the disjoint rational divisors as a thrice-punctured affine line gives the stated all-extension formulas. Exact saturation and small-field checks independently corroborate the algebra. The finite tests are not used as a proof for infinitely many extensions.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to historical coverage limitations.** The precise contribution is the characteristic-three incidence geometry, its sharp geometric \(3\)-rank floor and the complete ordered-parameter frequency formula. Carvacho–Hidalgo–Quispe, arXiv:1507.02903v1, Theorem 5.1, established the five elliptic factors over \(\mathbb C\); Hidalgo–Reyes-Carocca, arXiv:1609.04895v1, describes the ordered classical Humbert moduli in characteristic zero. The inspected relevant sections do not cover the claim here. Direct literature and mathematical-record searches did not identify an equivalent or dominating statement. These searches cannot rule out uncatalogued small-characteristic results, and a separate priority check remains appropriate.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** A two-dimensional genus-five Jacobian locus with five elliptic factors might a priori admit several simultaneous supersingular factors; the theorem shows that the smallest odd characteristic forces their supersingular divisors to be completely disjoint. This gives the sharp structural lower bound \(3\)-rank \(\ge4\), an explicit decomposition of the nonordinary locus into five rational curves, and exact frequencies over every finite extension. The objects and invariants are natural in the geometry of tame maximal abelian covers and Newton strata, rather than an arbitrary selected small-field computation.

## Limits and closest references

The statement applies to the ordered parameter surface and does not count unmarked geometric isomorphism classes. The sources used to establish context are Carvacho–Hidalgo–Quispe (10 July 2015, arXiv:1507.02903v1, Sections 2.1.3 and 5.1) and Hidalgo–Reyes-Carocca (16 September 2016, arXiv:1609.04895v1, Section 2). The geometric claim is algebraically proved in characteristic three independently of the complex geometric decomposition. Possible unindexed earlier results remain a novelty risk.
