Four independent, potentially nonidentically distributed, antipodally symmetric boundary points on any centrally symmetric convex body in \(\mathbb R^3\) form a tetrahedron with exactly zero, one, or two facets externally visible from the center with probabilities \(1/8,1/2,3/8\), regardless of the shape or sampling densities. A sixteen-sign dependence argument proves the complete law, and allowing interior points can produce three visible facets.
