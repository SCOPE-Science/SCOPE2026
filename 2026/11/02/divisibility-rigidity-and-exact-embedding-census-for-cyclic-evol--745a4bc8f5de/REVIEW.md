# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof starts from the exact multiplication \(e_i^2=e_{i+1}\) and verifies that squaring shifts nonzero support in every characteristic. Periodicity forces support to be whole residue classes modulo \(\gcd(m,n)\), while pairwise zero products force disjoint support shifts. Their combination proves \(n\mid m\) and a unique support class, after which coefficient recursion gives precisely \(c^{2^m-1}=1\). Conversely the explicitly constructed maps satisfy all products and have independent images. The number of distinct image subalgebras follows from a free source-automorphism action, and the target action is transitive by rotations and diagonal roots. Seventeen exhaustive small-field tests and five independent subspace censuses corroborate the formulas; they are not used as the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS, with priority risk.** Elduque–Labra (2013), Lemma 4.3 and §4 establish disjoint-support and automorphism results; Camacho–Khudoyberdiyev–Omirov (2014), §§2–3 distinguish subalgebra notions, including nonextendable examples; Cadavid–Rodiño Montoya–Rodríguez (2017), Theorem 2.4 studies same-graph companion algebras and the more restrictive notion of evolution homomorphism. The 2026 Kaygorodov–Martín González–Viruel survey explicitly records the cyclic root-of-unity automorphism boundary. None of the inspected material states or implies an exact proper **cross-dimensional** embedding and subalgebra census. The \(n=m\) automorphism case is prior work and the \(n=1\) idempotent boundary is elementary, neither being a separate novelty claim; the new claim concerns strict embeddings with \(2\le n<m\). Literature and mathematical-record discovery cannot certify absence of an unindexed explicit prior formula.

**Value — PASS.** The question classifies a natural complete family of ordinary algebra embeddings between strongly connected directed-cycle evolution algebras and gives a sharp divisibility obstruction, a single target-automorphism orbit, and exact finite-field subalgebra counts for every divisor. This is not an arbitrary small-case enumeration: the distinction between strict subalgebra embeddings and natural-basis-extendable subalgebras is central in the cited literature, and the finite-field root arithmetic distinguishes embedded-copy counts that automorphism counts alone do not determine. The result does not extend to general weighted evolution algebras or classify every subalgebra.
