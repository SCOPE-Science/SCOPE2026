# Eight projective orbit classes of conic-free points over \(\mathbb F_{64}\)

## Finding
Let \(K=\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^6+t+1)\cong\mathbb F_{64}\). A point of \(\mathbb P^2(K)\) is *conic-free* if it lies on no nonzero homogeneous quadratic curve defined over \(\mathbb F_2\); reducible and nonreduced conics are included. There are exactly **1344** conic-free points. The group \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\), of order \(168\), acts freely with exactly eight orbits. Frobenius \((a:b:c)\mapsto(a^2:b^2:c^2)\) permutes those eight orbits in cycles of lengths **2 and 6**. Thus the joint projective-and-Galois action has exactly two orbits, of sizes **336** and **1008**, and the 1344 points comprise precisely **224** geometric Frobenius sextuples.

In particular, \(P=[1:t:t^4]\) is conic-free. Its six Frobenius conjugates impose six independent conditions on plane quadrics over \(\mathbb F_2\). This realizes the smallest planar quadratic case omitted by the condition \(|\mathbb F_q|>2\) in Asgarli--Ghioca--Reichstein's general Galois-orbit theorem.

## Assumptions and scope
All counts concern actual projective points over the unique field \(K\) of 64 elements, not points over algebraic closure or moduli of arbitrary degree-six extensions. The base field of the conics and the projective transformation group is precisely \(\mathbb F_2\). The condition excludes **all** nonzero binary quadratic forms, including singular conics and double lines. Arithmetic projective equivalence is generated by \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\) and the order-six Frobenius group of \(K/\mathbb F_2\).

## Proof
**Field and first witness.** The polynomial \(u^6+u+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb F_2\): modular exponentiation gives \(u^{64}\equiv u\) and both \(\gcd(u^6+u+1,u^4-u)=1\) and \(\gcd(u^6+u+1,u^8-u)=1\). Therefore the residues \(1,t,t^2,t^3,t^4,t^5\) form a binary basis of \(K\). At \(P=[1:t:t^4]\), the six quadratic monomials take values
\[
1,\quad t,\quad t^4,\quad t^2,\quad t^5,\quad t^8=t^3+t^2.
\]
They form a binary basis. Hence no nonzero binary quadratic vanishes at \(P\).

**Exact exhaustive census.** For \(Q=[1:a:b]\), the six evaluation values in the preceding monomial order are \(1,a,b,a^2,ab,b^2\). A quadratic over \(\mathbb F_2\) vanishes at \(Q\) precisely when these six values are binary-linearly dependent. No point with first coordinate zero is eligible, because the nonzero quadratic \(X_0X_1\) vanishes there. The attached standalone verifier represents each element of \(K\) by a six-bit integer; it checks the field polynomial's irreducibility, then loops over **all 4096 pairs** \((a,b)\). It evaluates the six monomials using the defining multiplication and checks independence in two distinct ways: exact binary Gaussian elimination and evaluation of each of the **63** nonzero binary quadratics. These independent tests agree for every pair. The complete affine rank spectrum is
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrr}\text{rank}&1&2&3&5&6\\\hline
\text{pairs}&4&12&384&2352&1344.
\end{array}
\]
The entries sum to 4096; exactly 1344 pairs have rank six. This is an exhaustive finite certificate, not an extrapolation from samples.

**Projective group orbits.** Every invertible binary \(3\times3\) matrix represents a distinct element of \(\operatorname{PGL}_3(\mathbb F_2)\), since the only nonzero binary scalar is 1. The verifier enumerates the 512 possible binary matrices and retains exactly 168 invertible ones, and applies all of them to each as-yet-unclassified conic-free point. It obtains eight disjoint orbits, each containing 168 points, with lexicographically least affine pairs (using the field's six-bit encoding)
\[
(2,16),\ (2,20),\ (2,24),\ (2,40),\ (2,44),\ (2,48),\ (4,24),\ (4,50).
\]
The orbit enumeration is exhaustive because each orbit is formed by **all** 168 matrices and removed from the finite 1344-point set. The sizes prove that all projective stabilizers are trivial.

**Frobenius action.** Index the eight orbits by the displayed order, starting from zero. Applying the square map to each representative and locating the image's unique orbit gives the permutation
\[
(0\ 5)(1\ 6\ 2\ 3\ 7\ 4).
\]
Frobenius commutes with the binary projective group, so this determines its full action on orbit classes. Consequently the two combined orbit sizes are \(2\cdot168=336\) and \(6\cdot168=1008\). Finally, a point defined over a proper subfield of \(K\) cannot have six binary-linearly independent monomial values: the largest proper subfield has binary dimension three. Every conic-free point therefore has a six-element Frobenius orbit and the sextuple count is \(1344/6=224\).

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_f64_conics.py` from the directory containing that file. The program uses only the Python standard library and ends with `F64_QUADRATIC_ORBIT_CENSUS_PASS`. It checks the field algebra, all 4096 affine pairs, every binary conic at those pairs, all 168 projective transformations, all eight disjoint orbits, and the Frobenius permutation. The displayed numeric representatives are bit-encoded residues of \(t\), not ordinary integers embedded in a prime field.

## Relationship to prior work
Asgarli--Ghioca--Reichstein, *Linear system of hypersurfaces passing through a Galois orbit* (arXiv:2310.10361, first submitted October 16, 2023), proves general existence for base fields of more than two elements and explicitly observes that the excluded binary case was only supported by experiments. Its Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 2.2 provide the motivating monomial-rank formulation. Its explicit exceptional examples in Section 6 concern fields of size at least three. The result proved here is a complete finite-point and arithmetic-orbit census in the first planar quadratic binary case, not a proof of the unresolved assertion for arbitrary degrees or dimensions. The projective group size and the linear-algebra criteria themselves are standard.

## Limitations
No uniform assertion is made for general \((n,d)\) or for larger extension fields. The exact finite counts and orbit types depend on the exhaustive certificate; the witness's conic-freeness is also proved symbolically without code. A prior calculation of this particular finite configuration under another terminology remains a publication-priority risk. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
