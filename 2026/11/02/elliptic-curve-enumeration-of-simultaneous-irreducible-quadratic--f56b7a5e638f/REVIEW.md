# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The irreducibility reduction is proved via two field extensions of degree two. Quadratic character of the discriminant over the quadratic extension equals character of its norm, giving the exact indicator \((1-\chi(x^2+16c))/2\). The quadratic-character sum is \(-1\); in the simultaneous case the quartic character sum is reduced, by root multiplicity of the squaring map, to the point count of the nonsingular elliptic curve. The three rational nonzero two-torsion points explain divisibility by four. Hasse's bound yields unconditional existence. Direct polynomial-gcd verification for 74 parameter cases corroborates all branches without replacing the proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Bender–Pollack's 2009 twin-prime results and Bary-Soroker's 2012 tuple theorem concern general monic polynomials and asymptotic counts as field size grows, not the exact sparse quartic family. Heath-Brown–Micheli (2017) treat dynamically irreducible quadratic compositions, not the simultaneous fixed-shift quartic enumeration. Searches of equivalent quartic-composition, Capelli/character-sum, elliptic curve and exact-table formulations found no decisive prior coverage. The underlying character-sum steps are classical, and an unlocated specialized enumeration or general formula could imply this identity; novelty is not independently certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

A natural degree-four, constant-shift twin-irreducibility question is completely classified over every odd finite field. The genus-one exact formula links arithmetic fluctuations to the Frobenius trace and, using Hasse and the rational two-torsion, proves unconditional simultaneous existence for every locally admissible pair, including arbitrarily small fields. This goes beyond a list of examples, a raw finite enumeration, or a large-field estimate.

## Scope and limitations

Only two constant perturbations of squares of monic quadratics are counted. Characteristic two and higher-degree inner polynomials are outside the proof. The most significant originality risk is implicit coverage by classical composition/character-sum results; the explicit comparison was made against full accessible main results rather than claimed exhaustive literature coverage.
