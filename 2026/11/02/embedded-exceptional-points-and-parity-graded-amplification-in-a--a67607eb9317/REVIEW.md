# Scientific assessment: antipodal gain–loss ring Jordan law

## Correctness — PASS

The model has exactly \(2m\) sites, \(m\ge2\), with hopping one, opposite imaginary onsite potentials \(\pm i\gamma\), and no flux. Reflection yields a tridiagonal even sector with all nonzero off-diagonal entries and a real symmetric odd path sector. The determinant identity
\[
\det(zI-H)=(z^2+\gamma^2-4)U_{m-1}(z/2)^2
\]
is established by a full recurrence, not inferred solely from a plotted spectrum. The even sector has one-dimensional eigenspaces; exact root multiplicities therefore give the claimed exceptional Jordan blocks. The nilpotent exponential supplies matching norm upper and lower bounds. Exact calculations cover boundary \(\gamma=0\), embedded strengths, \(\gamma=2\), both parities, and \(\gamma>2\). The 23 finite checker assertions corroborate but do not justify the universal assertion.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence; residual priority risk

Scott and Joglekar (arXiv:1203.1345, 2012) study a more general two-hopping ring with gain/loss and its phase boundary. Jin and Song (arXiv:1605.08953, 2016) already derive the zero-flux full spectrum and the four-site third-order exceptional point; those are prior art, not new claims. Ortega, Benet and Larralde (arXiv:2107.00286, 2021) describe singular/opacities and the six-site \(\gamma=2\) point. The assessed new statement is the explicit all-size classification of embedded size-two Jordan blocks at \(\gamma=2\sin(\pi k/m)\), the parity transition in the largest Jordan block at \(\gamma=2\), and the corresponding exact propagator growth orders. A known characteristic polynomial does not determine Jordan forms, although a general eigenvector analysis elsewhere could imply this classification. No claim of exhaustive historical uniqueness is made.

## Value — PASS

The theorem separates spectral reality from bounded dynamical amplification: for an exactly real spectrum, isolated internal gain strengths still force linearly growing propagators; at the terminal threshold the growth can be quadratic and depends only on ring-size parity. This is a complete, natural structural classification with direct bearing on stability and observability of gain/loss systems, rather than a routine fixed-size spectrum computation.

## Limits and independent status

The result assumes equal hoppings, symmetric opposite-site gain and loss, no flux and linear evolution. No robustness claim is made under parameter errors, disorder, other topologies or nonlinearities. The most substantial originality risks are derivations from previously published all-size eigenvector formulas and as-yet-uninspected neighboring literature. Symbolic checks are corroboration only. Independent scientific audit has not been performed.
