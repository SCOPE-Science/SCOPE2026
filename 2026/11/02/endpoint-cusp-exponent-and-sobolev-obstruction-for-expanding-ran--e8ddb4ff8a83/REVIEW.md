# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The branch inverse law follows from a strictly monotone scalar equation, with uniform slope exceeding one. The jump seminorm of the transfer operator contracts strictly for every stationary BV density, giving interior continuity; a dense inverse orbit establishes strict positivity. The exact positive density coefficient follows from its two inverse branches, and the Sobolev obstruction follows from a little-o endpoint Hölder inequality. These arguments establish the stated universal quantifiers; the numerical checks support only finite test cases. Risk: the conditional BV assumption is substantive; arbitrary mixtures' existence is not established here.

## Originality — PASS under available comparisons

The 2026 random cusp and critical-cusp papers study respectively stronger cusps and the critical parameter, rather than the weaker-cusp parameter range of this theorem. The 2025 single-map cusp response results assume a different parameter regime, and the classical 2001 exact cusp family has different geometry. None of the inspected full texts or accessible mathematical record summaries implies the positive coefficient and pointwise optimal exponent in this family. The 2026-08-27 random-cusp preprint full text was inaccessible despite open-access retrieval attempts, so a remaining priority risk is stated rather than dismissed. The public abstract and the related full-text critical-cusp paper support the comparison, not a claim that the inaccessible entire text was read.

## Mathematical value — PASS

An existing smooth-response regime at the critical square-root cusp could suggest that weaker cusps retain strong stationary-density regularity. The theorem identifies an opposite-side discontinuity in regularity, gives the exact exponent and coefficient, and makes the obstruction independent of finite randomization weights. This is a structural boundary for regularity-based dynamical response methods, not just a numerical example or a parameter substitution. The proof does not assert an exact positive Sobolev membership range.
