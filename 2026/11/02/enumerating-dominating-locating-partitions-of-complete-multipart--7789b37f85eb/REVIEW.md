# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The two coded polynomial formulas follow from explicit necessary-and-sufficient constraints on the number of selected vertices in each false-twin class and among the singleton true-twin vertices. Every class-profile boundary is treated, including the one-nonsingleton case and \(K_2\). Reconstructing the neighbourhood signatures yields the same enumeration in both directions. The standalone direct enumeration matched all coefficients for 147 part profiles (209812 candidate subsets); this corroborates, but does not substitute for, the proof for all orders.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
Chakraborty–Foucaud–Henning–Laihonen (2026, DOI `10.37236/14049`) prove universal existence of a dominating/LD bipartition, not its full spectrum. Foucaud–Maniya–Paul–Pradhan (arXiv:`2506.12933`, 2025) prove two-LD *existence* for several twin-free classes, not a multipartite characterization admitting twins. Targeted research-record and primary-literature searches found no matching all-part-profile polynomial. General older enumerations or implicit consequences remain a historical-priority risk, not a claim of exhaustive uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a natural complete classification of both the recent universal partition type and its stronger, twin-sensitive two-LD version, including all cardinality multiplicities. The double-LD criterion sharply separates admissible twin profiles from inadmissible ones, and the enumerator shows every possible double-LD partition is balanced. The quantitative result does not follow from existence alone and gives exact search-space sizes for enumerating or uniformly sampling such graph partitions.

## Closest sources and limits
The first source is the 2026 general existence result; the second is the 2025/2026 two-LD result under a twin-free restriction. Scope is labelled complete multipartite graphs with at least two parts, not arbitrary graphs or equivalence modulo automorphisms. No independent assessment or historically exhaustive priority claim is asserted.
