# Equivariant first-order deformation characters of higher-leg spider algebras

## Finding

Let \(r\ge4\), let \(a_1,\ldots,a_r\ge2\) be integers, and let \(S=\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_r]\). Put
\[
I=(x_i^{a_i}:1\le i\le r)+(x_ix_j:1\le i<j\le r),\qquad A=S/I,
\quad d=\dim_{\mathbb C}A=1+\sum_{i=1}^r(a_i-1).
\]
For the coordinate-scaling torus with \(x_i\) of character \(t_i\), define the following Laurent characters:
\[
\begin{aligned}
H(\mathbf t)={}&\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{s=1}^{a_i-1}t_i^{s-a_i}
+\sum_{j\ne i}t_j^{a_j-1}t_i^{-a_i}\right)\\
&+\sum_{1\le i<j\le r}\left(\sum_{s=1}^{a_i-1}t_i^{s-1}t_j^{-1}
+\sum_{s=1}^{a_j-1}t_j^{s-1}t_i^{-1}
+\sum_{k\notin\{i,j\}}t_k^{a_k-1}t_i^{-1}t_j^{-1}\right),\\
B(\mathbf t)={}&1+\sum_{j=1}^r\sum_{s=1}^{a_j-1}t_j^s,\\
D(\mathbf t)={}&\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{s=1}^{a_i-1}t_i^{s-1}
+\sum_{j\ne i}t_j^{a_j-1}t_i^{-1}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Then the **complete coordinate-torus character** of the embedded Hilbert-scheme tangent space, and the character of the abstract first-order deformation space, are exactly
\[
\boxed{\chi_T\bigl(T_{[I]}\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(\mathbb A^r)\bigr)=H(\mathbf t),\qquad
\chi_T\bigl(\mathbb T^1(A)\bigr)=H(\mathbf t)-B(\mathbf t)\sum_{i=1}^r t_i^{-1}+D(\mathbf t).}
\]
Here \(\mathbb T^1(A)=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{A}(L_{A/\mathbb C},A)\), with the cotangent complex \(L_{A/\mathbb C}\). In particular,
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\dim T_{[I]}\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(\mathbb A^r)
&=rd+\frac{r(r-2)(r+1)}2,\\
\dim\mathbb T^1(A)&=d-1+\frac{r(r^2+r-4)}2.
\end{aligned}}
\]
The embedded tangent excess depends **only** on the number of legs, not on their lengths. For the four-leg algebra with exponents \((2,3,4,5)\), the length, tangent dimension, and abstract deformation dimension are \((11,64,42)\); for the five-leg algebra with exponents \((2,3,2,4,2)\), they are \((9,90,73)\).

## Assumptions and scope

All schemes and deformation functors are over \(\mathbb C\); the integers \(r,a_i\) range as specified above, without a genericity assumption. The torus acts by \(x_i\mapsto t_i x_i\) on the polynomial coordinate algebra, and a map sending a homogeneous ideal generator \(g\) to a monomial \(m\) has weight \(\operatorname{wt}(m)/\operatorname{wt}(g)\). The character equations are identities in the integral Laurent-character ring \(\mathbb Z[t_1^{\pm1},\ldots,t_r^{\pm1}]\).

## Proof

The quotient \(A\) has basis \(1\) and the monomials \(x_i^s\) for \(1\le i\le r\), \(1\le s<a_i\). Write \(P_i=x_i^{a_i}\) and \(Q_{ij}=x_ix_j\) for the minimal generators of \(I\). Any \(S\)-linear homomorphism \(\varphi:I\to A\) is determined by their images, subject to the relations among generators. For a monomial ideal, these relations are generated by the pairwise least-common-multiple syzygies.

First, for every \(i\ne j\), the syzygy between \(P_i\) and \(Q_{ij}\) imposes
\[
x_j\varphi(P_i)=x_i^{a_i-1}\varphi(Q_{ij}).
\]
Multiplication by \(x_j\) has image on the \(x_j\)-axis, whereas multiplication by \(x_i^{a_i-1}\) has image on the different \(x_i\)-axis. Both sides must therefore vanish. Since \(a_i\ge2\), this forces the constant coefficient of \(\varphi(Q_{ij})\) to vanish and restricts \(\varphi(P_i)\) to the span
\[
V_i=\operatorname{span}\left(\{x_i^s:1\le s<a_i\}\cup
\{x_j^{a_j-1}:j\ne i\}\right).
\]
The equation also forces the constant coefficient of \(\varphi(P_i)\) to vanish.

Next, whenever \(i,j,k\) are distinct, the syzygy between \(Q_{ij}\) and \(Q_{ik}\) reads
\[
x_k\varphi(Q_{ij})=x_j\varphi(Q_{ik}).
\]
The already-vanishing constants place these two expressions on different coordinate axes, so both are zero. Varying \(k\) shows that \(\varphi(Q_{ij})\) lies in
\[
W_{ij}=\operatorname{span}\left(
\{x_i^s:1\le s<a_i\}\cup\{x_j^s:1\le s<a_j\}\cup
\{x_k^{a_k-1}:k\notin\{i,j\}\}\right).
\]
Conversely, arbitrary choices \(\varphi(P_i)\in V_i\) and \(\varphi(Q_{ij})\in W_{ij}\) satisfy the two displayed types of relations. The remaining least-common-multiple syzygies have multipliers annihilating \(A\), hence impose no further conditions. Consequently there is an equality of torus representations
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_S(I,A)\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^r V_i\oplus\bigoplus_{i<j}W_{ij},
\]
where each summand's weights are shifted by the inverse character of its respective generator. This gives \(H(\mathbf t)\) term by term. Counting bases yields
\[
\dim\operatorname{Hom}_S(I,A)
=\sum_i(a_i+r-2)+\sum_{i<j}(a_i+a_j+r-4)
=rd+\frac{r(r-2)(r+1)}2.
\]

For the abstract deformation space, the cotangent-complex transitivity sequence for \(S\twoheadrightarrow A\) supplies the exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb C}(A,A)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb C}(S,A)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_S(I,A)
\longrightarrow\mathbb T^1(A)\longrightarrow0.
\]
An algebra derivation is specified by images \(\delta(x_i)\). The relations \(x_ix_j=0\) imply that these images lie independently in the same spans \(V_i\); conversely, such images annihilate all the relations \(x_ix_j\) and \(x_i^{a_i}\). The resulting derivation character is \(D(\mathbf t)\). Meanwhile \(\operatorname{Der}_{\mathbb C}(S,A)\) has character \(B(\mathbf t)\sum_i t_i^{-1}\). Additivity of torus characters gives the asserted \(\mathbb T^1(A)\) formula, and evaluation at \(\mathbf t=(1,\ldots,1)\) gives its dimension.

## Verification

The accompanying dependency-free \(\texttt{spider_character_verify.py}\) constructs monomial quotient bases and all pair-lcm syzygies from the input exponent tuple. It independently row-reduces the resulting linear systems weight by weight over \(\mathbb F_{1000003}\), and computes the rank of the infinitesimal coordinate-change map \(\operatorname{Der}(S,A)\to\operatorname{Hom}_S(I,A)\). It compared the resulting full characters with the formulas, not merely their total dimensions. It returned \(\texttt{VERIFY_OK}\) for the four- and five-leg samples specified above and five further inputs; a three-leg example was used only as a calibration against established literature. These checks are finite examples and are not the proof of the universal theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Turturean, *Explicit deformation of a spider algebra to a curvilinear scheme via Möbius generators* (arXiv:2603.00886v1, publicly posted March 1, 2026), constructs an explicit flat deformation for the three-leg length-22 spider, discusses higher-leg extensions, and specifically identifies understanding deformation-theoretic \(T^1\) and \(T^2\) as a direction for further study (Section 9.4). The formulas here address \(T^1\) for all higher-leg spiders, rather than constructing a smoothing.

The three-variable tangent *dimension* is already known: Hu, *On singular Hilbert schemes of points: Local structures and tautological sheaves* (arXiv:2101.05236, Section 4.5.2), and Jelisiejew–Ramkumar–Sammartano, *The Hilbert scheme of points on a threefold, I* (arXiv:2409.17009, Examples 5.9–5.10), establish the tripod tangent excess \(6\). **That three-variable dimension statement is not claimed as new.** The latter paper also recalls a general bounded-components method to count weights of arbitrary monomial ideals, which supplies a computational framework but does not state this closed multigraded character for all \(r\ge4\), nor the derived abstract \(T^1\) character. Doležálek et al., *Geometric and homological interpretations of tensor flattenings* (arXiv:2610.17875v1), connect Hilbert tangent spaces with tensor invariants but do not evaluate these higher-leg spiders in the inspected relevant sections.

## Limitations

The result calculates tangent representations and first-order abstract deformations, not obstruction spaces \(\mathbb T^2(A)\), versal deformation equations, irreducible components, or actual smoothing dimensions. It does not assert that tangent excess alone determines local singularity type. The formulas are derived in characteristic zero; the modular code tests are supplementary checks and do not independently certify the infinite family. The prior literature provides general combinatorial tangent-counting machinery, so absolute priority for this particular closed specialization remains subject to broader bibliographic review.
