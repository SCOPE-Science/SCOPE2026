# Scientific review: two-valued unary pentagon size spectrum

## Correctness — PASS
The constructed carriers \(A=B\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{x,y\}\) have arbitrary sizes \(|B|+2\ge4\). The prescribed extensions \(\widehat g\) and maps \(h_x,h_y\) have images of exactly two points. Each of the five proposed equivalence relations is preserved. For an arbitrary congruence, the restriction to \(B\) is either equality or universal, because all two-valued maps on \(B\) occur in the construction. These two cases force respectively \(0_A\) or \(\tau\), and \(\rho\), \(\sigma\), or \(1_A\). The partition-lattice height bound excludes carriers of size below four. These arguments cover all integers \(n\ge4\); exhaustive checking for sizes four through eight corroborates the proof, but is not used to extrapolate it.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The relevant primary paper, Dormán–Kátai-Urbán–Horváth–Waldhauser, arXiv:2610.33305v1 (23 October 2026), explicitly asks for carrier-size spectra in Problem 40, establishes the spectrum of partition lattices in Proposition 41 and of distributive lattices in Proposition 42, and supplies a four-point pentagon example in Appendix B. The four-point example alone does not imply existence on all larger carriers. The new contribution is a direct, uniformly defined two-valued operation family for every \(n\ge4\) and a sharp complete classification of the pentagon's size spectrum. Searches for the pentagon/diamond aliases and two-valued unary cardinality spectra found no identical statement. Bandelt's 1977 general characterization of two-valued congruence lattices is a real priority risk: its full chapter was unavailable for inspection and could encode an implicit construction. Priority is therefore best-knowledge rather than independently established.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The exact carrier-size spectrum is a natural invariant singled out in the motivating paper, not an arbitrary finite computation. The theorem closes the remaining nondistributive size-spectrum case among five-element lattices: the five-element diamond \(M_3\) is locked to carrier size three, whereas the pentagon \(N_5\) occurs on every size at least four. An explicit arbitrary-size realization and a complete obstruction are more informative than a small isolated representation. The result does not resolve spectra for larger nondistributive lattices or minimize the operation count.

## Prior comparisons and limits
The motivating paper's Proposition 41 gives \(\operatorname{spec}_2(M_3)=\{3\}\) and Proposition 42 gives all nontrivial distributive spectra; neither implies the new pentagon spectrum. Its Appendix B establishes only one four-point realization. Bandelt's 1977 article underlies a general criterion but was not inspected in full; any older implicit treatment remains a residual risk. Independent mathematical audit has not been undertaken.
