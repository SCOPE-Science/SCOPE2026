# Exact Abelian multiplicities and a dense atomic limit for Cantor factors

## Finding

For the infinite binary Cantor word \(\mathbf c=\sigma^\omega(1)\) generated by \(\sigma(1)=101\) and \(\sigma(0)=000\), let \(\mathcal F_k\) be the set of **distinct**, not occurrence-weighted, contiguous factors of length \(N=3^k\), with \(k\ge1\). Put \(M=2^k\). Define \(A_k(j)=|\{u\in\mathcal F_k:|u|_1=j\}|\). Then the exact Abelian-class multiplicities are

\[
A_k(j)=\begin{cases}
1,&j=0\text{ or }j=M,\\
2\cdot3^{k-1}+1,&j=M/2,\\
2\cdot3^{\nu_2(j)}+2,&1\le j<M,\ j\ne M/2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

In particular, \(|\mathcal F_k|=2\cdot3^k-1\), and the Abelian complexity is \(M+1\). If a factor is sampled uniformly from \(\mathcal F_k\) and its number of ones is divided by \(2^k\), the resulting probability measures converge weakly to the dense pure-point probability law

\[
\mu=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\;\sum_{\substack{1\le m<2^r\\m\text{ odd}}}3^{-r}\,\delta_{m/2^r}.
\]

The limiting mean is \(1/2\) and its variance is \(1/20\). Its atoms are precisely the dyadic rationals strictly between zero and one, with mass \(3^{-r}\) at a reduced dyadic rational of denominator \(2^r\). Thus the limit is **purely atomic but has dense support**.

## Assumptions and scope

A factor is an ordinary contiguous binary substring of the one-sided infinite word; identical strings at different locations count once. Abelian equivalence at a fixed length means equality of the number of ones (equivalently of the Parikh vector). We use zero-based coordinates for finite words and let \(\nu_2(j)\) be the exponent of two dividing a positive integer \(j\). The Dirac point mass at \(x\) is \(\delta_x\). The weak limit concerns uniformly sampled distinct factors, **not** a random starting position in the infinite word. The result holds for each integer \(k\ge1\), including the smallest substitution scale.

## Proof

**1. Exhaustive factor representation.** Let \(B=\sigma^k(1)\), so \(|B|=N\). The recursion
\[
B=H\,0^L\,H,\quad H=\sigma^{k-1}(1),\quad L=3^{k-1},\quad N=3L
\]
shows that \(B\) is a palindrome beginning and ending in one, with \(|B|_1=M\). Moreover, every zero run inside \(H\) has length strictly less than \(L\). At scale \(N\), the infinite word is a concatenation of blocks \(B\) and \(0^N\), with macroscopic adjacent pairs \(00,01,10\), but never \(11\). Consequently its length-\(N\) factors are precisely the words

\[
Z=0^N,\qquad P_t=\operatorname{suff}_t(B)0^{N-t},\qquad
S_t=0^{N-t}\operatorname{pref}_t(B)\quad(1\le t\le N).
\]

All three macroscopic adjacent pairs actually occur in the Cantor word, so this list is exact, not merely an upper bound. The \(P_t\) are pairwise distinct because their final one is at coordinate \(t-1\), and the \(S_t\) are pairwise distinct because their first one is at coordinate \(N-t\).

**2. Exactly two coincidences between the lists.** The identities
\[
P_{2L}=S_{2L}=0^L H0^L,\qquad P_N=S_N=B
\]
are immediate. They are the only coincidences, as follows. The word \(P_t\) ends in exactly \(N-t\) zeros, whereas every \(S_s\) ends in at most \(L\) zeros. Hence a coincidence requires \(t\ge2L\). Dually, \(S_s\) starts in exactly \(N-s\) zeros, whereas every \(P_t\) starts in at most \(L\) zeros, so \(s\ge2L\). At \(t=2L\), the leading zero-run has length \(L\), forcing \(s=2L\), and symmetrically for \(s=2L\). If \(t,s>2L\), write
\[
P_t=\operatorname{suff}_{t-2L}(H)\,0^L H0^{N-t},\qquad
S_s=0^{N-s}H0^L\operatorname{pref}_{s-2L}(H).
\]
In both words the displayed internal \(0^L\) is the unique run of \(L\) consecutive zeros: all other zero runs are shorter than \(L\), and the block is separated from its neighbors by ones. Equality therefore forces their starting coordinates to agree, yielding \(t-2L=N-s+L\), or \(t+s=2N\). Since \(t,s\le N\), necessarily \(t=s=N\). Thus the factor inventory has exactly \(1+2N-2=2N-1\) members.

**3. Exact Abelian classes.** Since \(B\) is a palindrome, \(P_t\) and \(S_t\) each contain precisely \(|\operatorname{pref}_t(B)|_1\) ones. List the positions of ones in \(B\), in increasing one-based order, as \(p_1,\ldots,p_M\). For \(1\le j<M\), precisely \(p_{j+1}-p_j\) prefix lengths \(1\le t\le N\) contain exactly \(j\) ones, while only \(t=N\) contains all \(M\). The zero word \(Z\) contributes one factor at weight zero. The two list coincidences identified above subtract one at weights \(M/2\) and \(M\).

The positions of ones are exactly \(1+2\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}b_i3^i\), where \((b_{k-1}\cdots b_0)_2\) runs over all binary strings of length \(k\), in increasing order. If \(1\le j<M\) and \(r=\nu_2(j)\), passing from the \(j\)-th to the next one flips \(r\) terminal binary ones to zero and the preceding zero to one. Thus
\[
p_{j+1}-p_j=2\cdot3^r-2\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}3^i=3^r+1.
\]
Twice these prefix-length counts, corrected at the two coincidences, give the claimed values of \(A_k(j)\). Every \(j\in\{0,\ldots,M\}\) appears.

**4. The limiting law and moments.** For a fixed reduced dyadic \(x=m/2^r\), with \(m\) odd and \(0<m<2^r\), its counterpart in the \(k\)-th distribution, for \(k\ge r\), has weight index \(j=m2^{k-r}\). The formula above gives
\[
\frac{A_k(j)}{2\cdot3^k-1}\longrightarrow3^{-r}.
\]
The total proposed mass is \(\sum_{r\ge1}2^{r-1}3^{-r}=1\). For each fixed \(R\), all dyadic atoms with reduced denominator at most \(2^R\) have convergent masses. The remaining mass is bounded, uniformly for sufficiently large \(k\), by \(O((2/3)^R)+(2/3)^k\), using the explicit multiplicity formula and summing its \(2^{r-1}\) indices at each valuation level \(r>R\). Thus convergence on the finite collections extends to weak convergence of probability measures on \([0,1]\).

Reflection \(j\mapsto M-j\) preserves every finite multiplicity, including the exceptional values, so every finite normalized mean is exactly \(1/2\). At reduced dyadic denominator \(2^r\), total limiting mass is \((1/3)(2/3)^{r-1}\), and conditional on this level the support is uniform on the \(2^{r-1}\) interval midpoints \((2i+1)/2^r\). Its conditional variance is \((1-4^{-(r-1)})/12\). Hence the total variance is
\[
\frac1{12}\left(1-\frac{1/3}{1-(2/3)/4}\right)=\frac1{20}.
\]
This completes the proof.

## Verification

Run \(\texttt{python3 checker.py}\) with Python 3 and no third-party dependencies. The checker directly enumerates all overlapping length-\(3^k\) windows in a sufficiently long Cantor iterate for \(1\le k\le7\), deduplicates the actual factor strings, counts ones, and compares the complete observed histogram with the theorem. Independently it compares with the two boundary-crossing families, checks their exact two intersections, and verifies every successive one-position gap. The observed factor totals are \(5,17,53,161,485,1457,4373\). It checks the mean exactly as a rational number and evaluates the rational limiting-moment identity. Finite tests corroborate, but do not prove, the all-depth claim or weak convergence; those follow from the arguments above.

## Relationship to prior work

Lü, Chen, Guo, and Wen (2016) gave the factor-complexity formula \(p_{\mathbf c}(n)=2n-1\) for \(n\ge2\) as part of their study of Cantor-like permutation complexity. Chen, Lü, and Wu (arXiv:1703.04063, 2017) established the regularity of the number of \(k\)-Abelian signatures and gave a recurrence for the gaps between successive ones (their Lemma 5). Those results concern the number of factors or equivalence classes, not the complete **cardinality of each Abelian equivalence class of distinct factors** at substitution scales or the resulting probability law under uniform sampling of distinct factors. The current proof is independent of the published factor-complexity count, explicitly classifies the two family overlaps, and derives the full multiplicity spectrum and weak limit. The 2017 paper provides supporting comparison literature; the earlier 2016 paper establishes the foundational publication context.

## Limitations

The formulas are proved only for factor lengths that are powers of three under the specified morphism. They do not count occurrence frequencies of those factors and do not apply to arbitrary uniform morphisms. The limiting law is an asymptotic of the distribution across *distinct strings*; it is not a shift-invariant frequency measure. Historical priority may be affected by an earlier equivalent statement under other terminology. The complete original 2016 publisher PDF was not available through the attempted access route; the accessible indexed sections and the entire 2017 open preprint were examined.
