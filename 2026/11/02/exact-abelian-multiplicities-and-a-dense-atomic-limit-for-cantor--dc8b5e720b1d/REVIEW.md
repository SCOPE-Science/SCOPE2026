# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)
The claim fixes the exact population (distinct length-\(3^k\) factors) and states integer multiplicities for every substitution depth. The proof enumerates all length-matched macro-block factors; distinguishes the two families using their first and last one; proves the sole two coincidences using the unique separating zero run of length \(3^{k-1}\); and obtains every weight count from the ternary positions of ones. Fixed-denominator atoms and a uniformly bounded high-denominator tail establish weak convergence. The variance follows by an explicit convergent geometric sum. The standalone checker explicitly enumerated factors at all seven levels 1 through 7 and passed; the mathematical proof, not these tests, establishes unrestricted quantifiers.

## Originality (PASS, qualified priority)
The 2016 Cantor-like sequence paper gives factor complexity but not the individual Abelian-class multiplicities in the accessible public sections. Chen–Lü–Wu (2017) studies the number of k-Abelian classes and the successive-one gaps, rather than counts of distinct factors in each class or their weak-limit law. Actual targeted semantic searches of mathematical research records and web-indexed literature did not identify the exact class-size and dense atomic limit result. This is a reasoned comparison, not a proof of worldwide novelty. Full inspection of the 2016 publisher article could not be completed, so equivalent earlier coverage remains a recorded priority risk.

## Value (PASS)
Abelian complexity counts how many distinct Parikh vectors are observed but conceals how ambiguously each vector determines a factor. This result supplies the entire class-size profile for a canonical sparse automatic word at every substitution scale, and exposes a dense pure-point limit with explicitly determined masses and variance. The weak-law phenomenon and exact inverse-multiplicity classification are not mere restatements of the known total complexity.

## Closest literature and limits
Lü, Chen, Guo and Wen, *On the permutation complexity of the Cantor-like sequences* (2016), DOI 10.1016/j.tcs.2015.12.022, is a related factor-complexity source; the complete publisher text was inaccessible. Chen, Lü and Wu, *On the k-Abelian complexity of the Cantor sequence* (2017), arXiv:1703.04063v1, supplies relevant one-gap recurrences and equivalence-class complexity but not the exact distribution over distinct factors in the inspected material. The conclusion is restricted to the stated morphism, factor lengths, and uniform weighting over distinct factor strings. Independent historical-priority assessment remains open.
