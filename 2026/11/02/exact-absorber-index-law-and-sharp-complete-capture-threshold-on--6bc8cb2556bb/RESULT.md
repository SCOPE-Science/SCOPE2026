# Exact absorber-index law and sharp complete-capture threshold on crown quantum networks

## Finding

Let \(n\ge 3\) be an integer. The crown graph \(\operatorname{Cr}_n=K_{n,n}\setminus M\) has paired vertices \(u_i,v_i\), \(1\le i\le n\), and edges \(u_iv_j\) precisely when \(i\ne j\). Consider a single coherent excitation with homogeneous nonzero hopping \(J>0\) and positive local absorption at every site of a nonempty set \(T\). Write \(I(T)=\{i:u_i\in T\text{ or }v_i\in T\}\) and \(t=|I(T)|\). The two vertices with a common index need not both be absorbers.

The entire nondecaying Hilbert subspace, in the ordered \(u\)- and \(v\)-coordinate representation, is
\[
\mathcal D_T=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb C^n\oplus\mathbb C^n:\ x_i=y_i=0\ \text{for }i\in I(T),\ \sum_{i=1}^n x_i=\sum_{i=1}^n y_i=0\right\}.
\]
Its dimension, the complementary bright dimension, and the eventual absorption probability from the maximally mixed one-excitation state are exactly
\[
\boxed{\dim\mathcal D_T=2\max\{n-t-1,0\},\quad B_T=2n-2\max\{n-t-1,0\},\quad P_{\rm mix}=\frac{\min\{t+1,n\}}n.}
\]
More strongly, for every initial site-localized pure state, the eventual capture probability is
\[
\boxed{\eta(u_j)=\eta(v_j)=\begin{cases}1,&j\in I(T),\\ (n-t)^{-1},&j\notin I(T).\end{cases}}
\]
The second branch occurs only if \(t<n\). All these formulas hold for arbitrary strictly positive, mutually unequal site absorption strengths. The minimum number of absorbing vertices guaranteeing absorption of **every initial pure or mixed state** is exactly \(n-1\). Precisely \(n2^{n-1}\) labelled absorber sets of minimum cardinality do so: choose one omitted index, and one member of each of the other \(n-1\) pairs.

In particular, adding \(v_i\) when \(u_i\) is already absorbing does not change the eventual absorption probability of any initial state, although it can alter transient decay times. Conversely, a site at the unmarked mate of an absorber is eventually captured with probability one. As examples, \(\operatorname{Cr}_3=C_6\) requires two suitably indexed absorbers and \(\operatorname{Cr}_4=Q_3\) requires three; a pair with both sites of one omitted matching edge is completely redundant for eventual capture.

## Assumptions and scope

The system is finite, undirected, and has exactly one excitation. Every vertex has the same coherent hopping amplitude \(J>0\) along each graph edge. Let \(A\) denote the ordinary adjacency matrix and let \(\kappa_z>0\) for each \(z\in T\). The no-jump evolution is generated by
\[
K=-iJA-\sum_{z\in T}\kappa_z|z\rangle\langle z|,
\]
so a normalized state \(\psi\) has survival norm \(\|e^{Kt}\psi\|^2\). Eventual capture is \(1-\lim_{s\to\infty}\|e^{Ks}\psi\|^2\). For a mixed state \(\rho\), use the corresponding trace evolution. This convention gives a loss rate \(2\kappa_z\) for population initially at \(z\). The absorption set must be nonempty; \(n=2\) is excluded because the crown graph is disconnected and its spectral levels coalesce.

## Proof

**1. Complete adjacency eigenspaces.** With vertices ordered as \(u_1,\ldots,u_n,v_1,\ldots,v_n\), set \(C=\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}-I_n\). Then
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&C\\C&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Since \(C\mathbf1=(n-1)\mathbf1\) and \(Cq=-q\) whenever \(\sum_iq_i=0\), the four distinct eigenvalues for \(n\ge3\) are \(n-1,-(n-1),1,-1\). Their eigenspaces, respectively, consist of \((\mathbf1,\mathbf1)\), \((\mathbf1,-\mathbf1)\), \((q,-q)\) and \((q,q)\), with \(\sum_iq_i=0\). The two principal spaces have dimension one, and each other eigenspace has dimension \(n-1\).

**2. The exact dark space.** In finite dimension, every nondecaying mode must lie in an eigenspace of the Hermitian hopping matrix and vanish at all absorbing coordinates. Neither principal eigenspace contains a nonzero such vector when \(T\ne\varnothing\). In either nonprincipal eigenspace, vanishing at \(u_i\) *or* at \(v_i\) imposes the same condition \(q_i=0\). Thus the remaining \(q\) are supported on the \(n-t\) unmarked indices and have coordinate sum zero, a space of dimension \(\max(n-t-1,0)\). Taking both signs gives exactly the stated subspace and dimension. Conversely this subspace is \(A\)-invariant and lies in the loss kernel, so no additional dissipative mechanism removes it.

**3. Why every complementary mode decays.** Split the Hilbert space orthogonally into the derived dark subspace and its orthogonal complement; both are invariant for \(A\), while the loss operator vanishes on the former and preserves the latter. If an eigenvector \(w\) of the generator restricted to the complement had eigenvalue \(\lambda\) with \(\operatorname{Re}\lambda=0\), then
\[
0=\operatorname{Re}\langle w,Kw\rangle=-\sum_{z\in T}\kappa_z|\langle z,w\rangle|^2.
\]
Every strictly positive rate forces \(w\) to vanish at every absorber; the eigenvector equation then makes \(w\) an \(A\)-eigenvector in the dark subspace, a contradiction. All eigenvalues on the complement therefore have strictly negative real parts; any finite Jordan blocks still decay. The long-time survival norm equals the squared orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal D_T\), independently of the positive loss coefficients.

**4. Sitewise projection.** Let \(W\subset\mathbb C^{n-t}\) denote the zero-sum hyperplane of unmarked indices. If \(n-t\ge1\), its orthogonal projector is \(P_W=I_{n-t}-\mathbf1\mathbf1^*/(n-t)\). For \(j\in I(T)\), the dark projector annihilates either site basis vector of index \(j\); its capture is one. For any \(j\notin I(T)\), the squared dark projection norm is \(1-1/(n-t)\), giving capture exactly \(1/(n-t)\). The dark projector vanishes when \(t\ge n-1\), resolving the complete-capture boundary.

**5. Threshold and multiplicity.** If fewer than \(n-1\) sites absorb, at most that many distinct indices are represented and \(\dim\mathcal D_T\ge2\). Any \(n-1\) absorbers at distinct indices have \(\dim\mathcal D_T=0\), and every minimum-size successful set must have that form. There are \(n\) choices for the omitted index and two choices of side for each of the remaining \(n-1\) indices. The maximally mixed result follows by taking the trace of the bright projector and dividing by \(2n\).

## Verification

The portable integer checker `crown_check.cpp` constructs each crown adjacency matrix directly, computes the span of all site-source adjacency Krylov vectors using modular Gaussian elimination over the prime field of order \(1009\), and compares the independently obtained rank to the stated bright-space dimension. It passed all nonempty absorber subsets for \(n=3,4,5,6\), plus \(80\) deterministic pseudorandom subsets at each \(7\le n\le12\): \(5916\) cases, with output `VERIFY_OK exact_krylov_cases=5916`. A separate independent floating-point spectral-projector calculation checked \(1357\) placements over \(3\le n\le9\) against both the bright dimension and sitewise probabilities. These are finite checks corroborating, not substituting for, the all-order proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Agliari, Mülken and Blumen (2009) formulate continuous-time coherent trapping and study trap arrangements on rings; Mülken and Blumen (2011) review the general local non-Hermitian trap formalism. Their results supply the physical framework, not a crown-graph all-trap-set classification. Klee and Stamps (2020) give the known Laplacian spectrum of a matching-deleted complete bipartite graph, and its eigenspaces motivate the exact calculation above; the eigenvalues themselves are not claimed as new. General graph controllability and strong-cospectrality principles explain why cyclic subspaces and pairs of equivalent sites matter, but a graph's spectrum or a generic rank test does not by itself state the full index-dependent capture formula, its sharp sitewise probabilities, and its complete minimum-absorber classification. The special imprimitive crown graph is outside the hypotheses of primitive distance-regular two-sink results.

## Limitations

The exact statements concern infinite-time capture, not finite-time relaxation rates, spectral gaps of the dissipative generator, capture-time optimizers, or efficiency under inhomogeneous hopping, dephasing, correlated loss, incoherent reinjection or multiple excitations. The rates must all be strictly positive. The known general spectral and graph-controllability methods make the proof elementary once the appropriate geometry is recognized; possible historical priority in earlier specialized crown controllability or quantum trapping publications remains an open literature risk. No independent mathematical audit is claimed.
