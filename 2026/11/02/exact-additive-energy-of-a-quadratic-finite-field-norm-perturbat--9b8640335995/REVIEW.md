# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The norm-coordinate image is exactly a translated quadratic-residue epigraph in \(\mathbb F_q^2\). The indicator decomposes into constant, quadratic-character and zero-delta contributions. The displayed Fourier transform follows from two Gauss sums; Parseval's fourth-moment identity proves the energy. Direct enumeration over nine finite fields, including extensions, agrees. The Fourier proof covers every odd prime power, while enumerations are only finite checks.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
Kelley–Owen (arXiv:1510.01758) bound roots of trinomials. Bluher (2004) and Kim–Choe–Mesnager (arXiv:1912.12648, sections 1, 3 and 5) address fibers and root criteria. These are not equivalent to determining the additive fourth moment of a polynomial's image: even knowing all fiber sizes separately does not determine cross-target additive relations. Gao–Wang (arXiv:1407.5884) analyze random value-set sizes, not this fixed polynomial's additive energy. Targeted searches for the norm-perturbation image's additive energy and related published research yielded no exact-equivalent source. Stronger unlocated results and inaccessible primary coverage remain risks; failure to find a match is not proof of uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Additive energy is a natural invariant of polynomial value sets: it measures additive collisions, not merely the number of values. The exact formula for an infinite family of fields and its arithmetic \(q\bmod4\) split are determined by a nonconstant full Fourier spectrum. It supplies more information than the classical image-size or fiber-count data.

## Limitations
Only odd characteristic and the specified norm-perturbation are addressed. The complete 2004 Bluher article was inaccessible, so related coverage could not be ruled out exhaustively. The finite replay checks nine fields; it does not prove the universal result.
