# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof uses four independent Hermite conditions on two distinct rational repeated roots, counts the previously invisible square-of-irreducible-quadratic strata, and corrects multiplicities of split quadratic squares. The resulting character sum is evaluated by cubic-character orthogonality and the standard Jacobi-sum norm identity. All denominators are invertible because the characteristic is odd and the slope is nonzero. Exceptional characteristic three is explicitly covered by cube-map bijectivity. A standalone Euclidean-gcd enumeration passed over eight test fields. Finite enumeration is not substituted for the universal proof.

## Originality — PASS (self-review, residual risk)
Carmon–Rudnick, arXiv:1205.1599, Theorem 1.1 requires at least one odd Möbius exponent, so it does not evaluate the even-exponent squarefree-pair count. Keating–Rudnick, arXiv:1504.03444, Appendix A.1, Theorem A.2 gives an asymptotic with an error term for general shifts, not the exact quartic affine-linear correction or its slope variance. Earlier exact cubic affine-shift and quartic constant-shift families are different and do not imply this quartic nonconstant-slope result. Searches of mathematical records did not identify the same claim, but missing or unindexed historical exact formulas remain possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The count is a natural low-degree specialization of a prominent polynomial-squarefreeness correlation, and gives a complete infinite-field stratum. Its sharp slope-moment dichotomy \(0\) versus \(8q\) arises from cubic Jacobi sums and exposes arithmetic behavior obscured by general asymptotics. It is not simply a renamed constant-shift or cubic result.
