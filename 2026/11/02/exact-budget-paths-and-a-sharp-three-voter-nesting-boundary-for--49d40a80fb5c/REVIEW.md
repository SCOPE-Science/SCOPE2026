# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The formal MES definition is the unit-cost, approval-utility version with supporter contributions capped by their remaining budgets and one fixed global candidate priority for ties. The proof establishes equal residual balances while core projects are purchased, strict common-core price precedence, and the absence of further purchases when aggregate money is below one. Thereafter, private petals have disjoint supporter pools and acquire exactly the floor of their proportional remaining funds, clipped at available candidates. Those steps prove the formula for every nonnegative real budget and any finite populations. Two arbitrary distinct ballots always decompose into a sunflower. The three-voter counterexample is reconstructed with exact payment prices. A standalone exact-rational verifier checked 156,245 predicted outcomes and reproduced both exact witness traces. Finite comparisons supplement, rather than replace, the all-size proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority uncertainty

The closest published prior is Peters–Skowron's laminar-proportionality result, which covers fixed-budget integral party-list and common-core examples but expressly omits nonintegral party-list quotas; it does not imply the real-budget exact nested path. Lackner–Skowron already demonstrate nonmonotonicity for MES with four voters; only the exact three-voter boundary, on the uncompleted method, is claimed here. Kraiczy–Elkind's AMES is a different update algorithm and does not state this exact result; their paper directly motivates the virtual-budget question. A 2020 variable-number-of-winners paper concerns different rules and objectives. Available semantic mathematical research records and accessible previous claims showed no stronger or equivalent sunflower-path result; this is a best-knowledge scientific priority assessment, not an assertion of worldwide uniqueness. An equivalent observation in specialist notes remains possible.

## Value — PASS

The structural result identifies an unlimited-size, naturally motivated approval domain where a budget-instability concern disappears, and gives every project entry threshold exactly. The all-budget path can be obtained by sorting those thresholds once, rather than recomputing MES at every virtual-budget increase; this directly addresses the repeated-budget question motivating the 2023 literature. Its three-voter witness proves the two-ballot guarantee is the largest uniform promise by number of distinct voter ballots, and improves the known counterexample voter count for this exact uncompleted procedure. This is a complete parametric classification of a mathematically natural special domain, not a generic correctness or exercise claim.

## Closest literature and limits

The principal comparisons are Peters and Skowron, arXiv:1911.11747, Section 3; Lackner and Skowron, *Multi-Winner Voting with Approval Preferences*, Appendix A, Proposition A.2; and Kraiczy and Elkind, arXiv:2310.10215v1, Sections 2 and 4. No result is claimed for completed MES, non-unit costs, general crossed approval supports, arbitrary tie priority changes, or tie-independent nonmonotonicity. Exact program checks do not verify infinite universality without the written proof. Independent specialist priority checking remains outstanding.
