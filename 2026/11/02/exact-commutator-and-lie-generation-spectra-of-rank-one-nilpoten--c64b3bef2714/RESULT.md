# Exact commutator and Lie-generation spectra of rank-one nilpotent matrices

## Finding

Let \(q\) be any prime power and \(n\ge 2\). Write \(\mathcal N_{1,n}(q)\) for the nonzero rank-one square-zero matrices in \(M_n(\mathbb F_q)\), and let
\[
M=\frac{(q^n-1)(q^{n-1}-1)}{q-1}.
\]
For any fixed \(X\in\mathcal N_{1,n}(q)\), the number of matrices \(Y\in\mathcal N_{1,n}(q)\) with commutator \(C=XY-YX\) of rank \(0\), \(1\), and \(2\), respectively, is
\[
\begin{aligned}
N_0&=M-2q^{n-1}(q^{n-2}-1)-(q-1)q^{2n-3},\\
N_1&=2q^{n-1}(q^{n-2}-1),\\
N_2&=(q-1)q^{2n-3}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For every \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_q^\times\), precisely \(q^{2n-3}\) choices of \(Y\) have \(\operatorname{tr}(XY)=\lambda\). Their commutator is diagonalizable, of rank two, with eigenvalues \(\lambda,-\lambda\), and \(n-2\) further zero eigenvalues (counted with multiplicity). The statement includes characteristic two, where the two nonzero eigenvalues coincide. Every rank-one commutator is square-zero.

The Lie algebra generated by \(X,Y\) is one-dimensional precisely for the \(q-1\) nonzero scalar multiples \(Y\) of \(X\). For the remaining rank-zero commutators, it is two-dimensional abelian. For rank-one commutators, it is the three-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra. For rank-two commutators, it is \(\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb F_q)\) when \(q\) is odd and the three-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra when \(q\) is even. Consequently, over odd-characteristic fields, the exact number of ordered pairs generating \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) is \(M(q-1)q^{2n-3}\).

The ordered-pair counts across the entire orbit follow by multiplying each fixed-\(X\) count by \(M\). In particular, at fixed \(q\) and growing \(n\), the rank-zero, rank-one, and rank-two proportions tend to \(q^{-2}\), \(2(q-1)q^{-2}\), and \((q-1)^2q^{-2}\), respectively. There is a sharp dimensional boundary: \(N_1=0\) for \(n=2\), but \(N_1>0\) for \(n\ge3\).

## Assumptions and scope

The matrix rank and trace are taken over the specified finite field, of any characteristic. The term square-zero means \(X^2=0\); the zero matrix itself is excluded from \(\mathcal N_{1,n}(q)\). Pairs are ordered and include equal matrices. The Lie bracket on \(M_n(\mathbb F_q)\) is \([A,B]=AB-BA\), also in characteristic two. A three-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra means a nonabelian Lie algebra with central one-dimensional derived algebra.

## Proof

**Orbit and total count.** Every rank-one matrix has a factorization \(X=uv^{\mathsf T}\), where \(u,v\ne0\). It satisfies \(X^2=0\) exactly when \(v^{\mathsf T}u=0\). There are \(q^n-1\) choices for \(u\) and \(q^{n-1}-1\) nonzero annihilators \(v\), with precisely \(q-1\) equivalent factorizations of each matrix. This gives \(M\). All such matrices have Jordan type \((2,1,\ldots,1)\); hence a change of basis sends any fixed \(X\) to \(e_1e_2^{\mathsf T}\) and preserves the sought quantities.

**Commutator ranks.** Fix \(X=e_1e_2^{\mathsf T}\), and write \(Y=st^{\mathsf T}\) with \(s,t\ne0\), \(t^{\mathsf T}s=0\), modulo simultaneous rescaling \((s,t)\mapsto(cs,c^{-1}t)\). Put \(a=s_2\), \(b=t_1\), and \(\lambda=ab\). Direct multiplication gives
\[
C=a e_1t^{\mathsf T}-b s e_2^{\mathsf T},\qquad \operatorname{tr}(XY)=ab.
\]
If \(a=b=0\), then \(C=0\). If exactly one of \(a,b\) is nonzero, then \(C\) is nonzero rank one, and its square vanishes because \(t_1=0\) or \(s_2=0\), respectively. If \(a,b\ne0\), then \(e_1,s\) are linearly independent and \(t,e_2\) are linearly independent; consequently \(C\) has rank two. Moreover \(C(e_1)=\lambda e_1\), \(C(s)=-\lambda s\), and \(C\) vanishes on the \((n-2)\)-dimensional simultaneous kernel of \(t^{\mathsf T}\) and \(e_2^{\mathsf T}\). Because \(\lambda\ne0\), this kernel complements \(\operatorname{span}\{e_1,s\}\), so \(C\) is diagonalizable as claimed, including in characteristic two.

**Exact counts.** There are \((q-1)q^{n-1}\) choices of \(s\) with \(a=s_2\ne0\). For fixed \(\lambda\ne0\), impose \(t_1=\lambda/a\) and \(t^{\mathsf T}s=0\). Since \(s\) is not a multiple of \(e_1\), these are independent linear conditions on \(t\), giving \(q^{n-2}\) solutions, all nonzero. Divide by the \(q-1\) factorization multiplicity to obtain exactly \(q^{2n-3}\) matrices \(Y\) for each \(\lambda\ne0\). For \(a\ne0\), \(b=0\), the two independent homogeneous conditions on \(t\) admit \(q^{n-2}-1\) nonzero solutions, giving \(q^{n-1}(q^{n-2}-1)\) matrices \(Y\). Exchanging the two factors gives the same number when \(a=0\), \(b\ne0\). These disjoint cases produce \(N_1\) and \(N_2\); subtraction from \(M\) yields \(N_0\).

**Lie algebras.** The rank-one identities \(XYX=\lambda X\) and \(YXY=\lambda Y\), together with \(X^2=Y^2=0\), give
\[
[C,X]=2\lambda X,\qquad[C,Y]=-2\lambda Y.
\]
Whenever \(C\ne0\), the matrices \(X,Y,C\) are linearly independent: for rank-two \(C\), the two rank-one summands have distinct image and functional lines; for rank-one \(C\), either \(C=e_1t^{\mathsf T}a\) with \(t\) not proportional to \(e_2\), or the transposed analogous case, and the image of \(Y\) separates it from the span of \(X,C\). The displayed bracket relations show that these three matrices form a Lie subalgebra. If \(\lambda=0\) it is Heisenberg. If \(\lambda\ne0\) and \(2\ne0\), set \(E=X\), \(F=Y/\lambda\), and \(H=C/\lambda\); these obey \([E,F]=H\), \([H,E]=2E\), \([H,F]=-2F\), the standard \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) relations. When the characteristic is two, \(C\) is central and the algebra is Heisenberg instead. If \(C=0\), the span of \(X,Y\) is abelian; it has dimension one just when \(Y\) is a nonzero scalar multiple of \(X\), of which there are exactly \(q-1\). This completes the classifications.

**Asymptotics.** Divide the three formulas by \(M\) and take \(n\to\infty\) with \(q\) fixed, yielding the stated limits.

## Verification

The independent enumeration routine `check_rankone_commutators.py` in this package constructs every rank-one square-zero matrix over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_4\) in selected dimensions, computes commutators and matrix ranks directly, and checks the rank and trace spectra, square-zero cases, Lie identities, and generated-space dimensions. It exhaustively tests all ordered pairs for six configurations and tests every partner of one fixed matrix in three more. It reports `RANKONE_NILPOTENT_COMMUTATOR_SPECTRUM_OK` upon success. These finite tests corroborate but do not replace the proof for all \(q,n\).

## Relationship to prior work

Britnell and Wildon (2010) study commuting pairs of similarity classes and their types; their results do not state the complete rank-stratified and trace-stratified pair counts inside this fixed minimal nilpotent conjugacy class. Chistopolskaya (2018) studies existence of Lie algebra generators involving nilpotents, rather than this finite-field census when both factors are rank-one nilpotent. Cheng and Liang (2020) treat spectral constraints of commutators of complex rank-one matrices, not this finite-field enumeration or the characteristic-dependent Lie-generation classification. Older rank-one commutator geometry (Guralnick, 1979; Neubauer, 1989) supplies relevant context, but the accessible abstracts did not resolve the precise enumeration statements. The elementary parametrization and Lie relations are ingredients; the asserted new contribution is the uniform complete finite-field joint spectrum and Lie-generation distribution, not the classical Jordan normal form or an established existence theorem.

## Limitations

The statement concerns the single minimal nonzero nilpotent conjugacy class (rank one and square zero), not arbitrary nilpotent matrices or matrix-pair conjugacy orbits. It describes commutator ranks and eigenvalues, not full commutator fibers for each matrix \(C\). Publication-priority comparison is limited by historical and possibly unindexed literature, including older sources inspected only in abstract.
