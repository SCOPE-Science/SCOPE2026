# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof enumerates complete mappings of the full-support zero-adjoined rectangular band via independent row and column permutation families, and identifies the one-defect obstruction as exactly one forbidden image relation for each first coordinate. Inclusion-exclusion uses the probability \((n)_r/(mn)^r\) for any fixed \(r\) simultaneous obstructions, yielding the all-integer exact count. The asymptotic limit is justified by termwise convergence dominated by \(1/r!\). Boundary assumptions \(m,n\ge2\) are explicit. Direct brute-force checking in four small dimensions agrees with both the defect and full-support formulas.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The closest inspected source, Araújo et al., arXiv:2608.25092v1, §6, treats **existence** under general zero-one sandwich supports and separately enumerates Brandt (identity-support) semigroups via Latin squares. Neither result states this one-defect enumeration or its \(e^{-1}\) sensitivity law; existence by itself cannot determine a number of mappings, and Brandt support is a different object. Targeted mathematical-record and general-literature searches using complete mapping / Cayley-table transversal / zero-one sandwich / near-rectangular-band aliases did not produce the same count. Standard rook-style inclusion-exclusion is a priority risk, and the searches cannot certify absolute historical novelty. The proof does not use absence of hits as a mathematical argument.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A one-entry deletion is the canonical smallest perturbation of the fully supported combinatorial Rees zero-matrix semigroup, directly motivated by the current balanced-support existence theory. The exact formula gives the number of complete mappings in every rectangular size, including the sharp nonvanishing loss fraction \(1-e^{-1}\) as both dimensions grow. This reveals a sensitivity of transversal enumeration that an existence criterion misses; it is not merely a small numerical table. The exact formula does not extend without further work to arbitrary defects or group-labelled Rees matrices.
