# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The coordinate-product characterization is proved in both directions; a continuous positive-simplex interpolation handles every strictly interior coefficient vector, including one-point support and zero. At the \(\ell_1\)-boundary, equality in each triangle inequality yields the exact real affine-independence criterion for extreme points. Finite-dimensional convexity generates the three-coordinate decomposition, while a rank-three phase witness proves its sharpness. The universal statements rest on these arguments rather than numerical sampling.

## Originality
PASS, best-knowledge rather than a priority guarantee. The motivating continuous-family paper develops joint \(q\)-ranges but its main realization theorem treats unimodular \(q\), and its diagonal coefficient calculation supplies only necessity, not sufficiency for all finite dimensions. The 2024/2026 joint-tuple paper proves commuting-tuple convexity in two dimensions, not the present arbitrary finite simultaneously normal case. Classical scalar normal \(q\)-ranges exhibit three-eigenvalue principles, but their scalar-image statements do not directly imply this multi-function convex-body equality and sharp extreme-point classification. Related convex geometry may have an equivalent formulation; see the structured comparison.

## Mathematical value
PASS. General joint \(q\)-ranges can be nonconvex, while this natural operator subclass admits a full explicit shape independent of the number of evaluation sites and a sharp universal spectral-site bound. The phase characterization determines the extreme coefficient configurations rather than merely bounds on a norm. The continuous family formulation includes every finite commuting normal operator tuple.

## Limits
Simultaneous *fixed-basis* diagonalization and finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space are essential assumptions. The extreme-point description concerns coefficient vectors rather than their possibly noninjective images in function space. No claim is made that any individual range point requires or admits only three sites without convexification. Earlier constrained-\(\ell_1\) geometry and scalar normal results remain equivalence-search risks; there is no independent validation.
