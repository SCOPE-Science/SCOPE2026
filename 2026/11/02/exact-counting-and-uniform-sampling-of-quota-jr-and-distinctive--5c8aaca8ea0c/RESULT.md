# Exact counting and uniform sampling of quota-JR and distinctive committees on candidate intervals

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(m\ge1\) candidates be ordered as \(1,\ldots,m\), and let \(n\ge1\) voters approve candidate intervals \(A_i=[\ell_i,r_i]\) (empty ballots may be omitted). Fix an integer quota \(q\ge1\), a permitted candidate set \(E\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}\), and a desired committee size \(1\le k\le m\). A committee \(W\subseteq E\) has **quota-justified representation** (quota-JR) when for every candidate \(c\) fewer than \(q\) voters both approve \(c\) and approve no candidate in \(W\).

The number of size-\(k\) quota-JR committees is exactly computable in \(O(m^2(n+m)+km^2)\) integer-arithmetic operations, with integers of \(O(m)\) bits. If at least one committee exists, a uniform sample among all valid size-\(k\) committees can be drawn with polynomial expected bit complexity.

In particular, setting \(q=\lceil n/k\rceil\) counts and uniformly samples ordinary justified-representation (JR) committees. Under the Distinctive Representation (DR) definition of Chingoma et al., setting
\[
E=\{c:|N_c|=\lceil n/k\rceil\}
\]
counts and uniformly samples all DR committees. Thus DR feasibility on this domain is decided by whether the count is nonzero.

## Assumptions and scope

All ballots are contiguous in a given candidate order, all voters are individually indexed (repeated ballots count with multiplicity), committees are sets of exactly \(k\) distinct candidates, and quotas are positive integers. This is not an algorithm for unrestricted approval profiles, voter-interval profiles without a candidate-interval order, PJR, EJR, or randomized mechanisms with additional preferences. No uniqueness or output-size assumption is required.

An ordinary JR violation means there exist at least \(n/k\) entirely unrepresented voters approving a common candidate. Since group sizes are integers, this is equivalent to at least \(q=\lceil n/k\rceil\) such voters. DR additionally requires every elected candidate to have exactly that many approving voters; hence the restriction to \(E\).

## Proof

Place sentinels at positions \(0\) and \(m+1\). For integers \(0\le a<b\le m+1\), set
\[
U_{a,b}(c)=\big|\{i:a<\ell_i\le c\le r_i<b\}\big|\quad (a<c<b),
\]
where ballots with empty approval sets contribute zero. Let \(G(a,b)=1\) exactly when \(U_{a,b}(c)<q\) for every \(a<c<b\); otherwise put \(G(a,b)=0\).

**Gap equivalence.** Write a putative committee as \(W=\{w_1<\cdots<w_k\}\) and set \(w_0=0\), \(w_{k+1}=m+1\). Consider an unelected candidate \(c\) between consecutive selected positions \(a=w_j\) and \(b=w_{j+1}\). If a voter approves \(c\) and is unrepresented, that voter's interval cannot reach \(a\) or \(b\), because any such reach would include a selected candidate. Hence the interval lies strictly inside \((a,b)\). Conversely, an interval strictly inside that gap cannot contain any selected position, so every voter counted in \(U_{a,b}(c)\) is unrepresented. The uncovered-support count at \(c\) is therefore **exactly** \(U_{a,b}(c)\). For an elected candidate \(c\), the uncovered-support count is zero. Thus \(W\) satisfies quota-JR if and only if
\[
\prod_{j=0}^{k}G(w_j,w_{j+1})=1.
\]
Every size-\(k\) candidate set has a unique increasing sequence; no committee is double-counted.

**Counting recurrence.** For any starting position \(a\in\{0\}\cup E\) and \(t\ge0\), let \(F_t(a)\) count legal increasing paths from \(a\) to sentinel \(m+1\) that use exactly \(t\) further selected positions in \(E\), with every gap good. Then
\[
F_0(a)=G(a,m+1),\qquad
F_t(a)=\sum_{\substack{b\in E\\b>a}}G(a,b)F_{t-1}(b),\quad 1\le t\le k.
\]
The recurrence follows by splitting each nonempty path at its unique next selected position \(b\). The gap equivalence and the one-to-one encoding of committees imply that \(F_k(0)\) is exactly the desired count, including the zero case.

**Algorithmic complexity.** For each \((a,b)\) scan the \(n\) ballots; for every ballot inside the gap increment a difference array at \(\ell_i\) and decrement at \(r_i+1\). A prefix scan over at most \(m\) positions determines all \(U_{a,b}(c)\). Over \(O(m^2)\) pairs this takes \(O(m^2(n+m))\) arithmetic operations. The suffix recurrence has \(O(km)\) states and at most \(m\) transitions per state, taking \(O(km^2)\) further operations. Every count is bounded by \(\binom{m}{k}\le2^m\), so the integers have \(O(m)\) bits. This proves polynomial bit complexity as well as the stated arithmetic bound.

**Uniform sampling.** Suppose \(F_t(a)>0\). Pick the next candidate \(b>a\) from \(E\) with probability
\[
\frac{G(a,b)F_{t-1}(b)}{F_t(a)}.
\]
All admissible paths can be generated by drawing uniform integers on the relevant exact count ranges; no floating-point arithmetic is needed. Along a complete path, conditional factors telescope to \(1/F_k(0)\), so every valid committee has the same probability. Standard rejection from random bits generates the bounded integers with polynomial expected bit complexity.

## Verification

The included standalone \(\texttt{verify_counting.py}\) implements both the gap dynamic program and an independently expressed direct checker that enumerates each candidate's uncovered approving voters. Run \(\texttt{python3 verify_counting.py}\) with Python 3. The main test exhausts 23,370 cases (all ordered nonempty candidate-interval ballots for \(m\le5\), \(n\le3\) and all eligible DR-derived committee sizes), then tests 12,000 seeded random generalized quota/eligibility instances (including empty ballots) against direct brute force. An additional independent extension exhaustively tested all \(n=4\) ordered nonempty ballot profiles for \(m\le5\), comparing every committee size, another 297,176 cases. The combined exhaustive DR check comprises 320,546 profiles and sizes. No finite test substitutes for the gap-equivalence proof.

For example, with \(m=6\), \(n=8\), \(k=3\), the ordered voter ballots
\[
[1,2],\ [2,5],\ [4,6],\ [1,2],\ [6,6],\ [1,1],\ [5,6],\ [2,4]
\]
give \(q=3\) and eligible set \(E=\{1,4,5,6\}\). Exactly three of the four possible eligible triples satisfy DR: \(\{1,4,5\}\), \(\{1,4,6\}\), and \(\{1,5,6\}\). The triple \(\{4,5,6\}\) leaves three approving voters of candidate \(1\) unrepresented.

## Relationship to prior work

Chingoma, Grossi, Lindeboom and Maly (2026) define DR as JR plus a requirement that each selected candidate has exactly the Hare-ceiling quota of supporters. They also use candidate-interval gap dynamic programming to optimize the **semi-strong JR** score. That is an important methodological precursor, but their inspected results do not count all JR or DR committees or construct an exact uniform sampler. The present gap statistic is specifically the number of **unrepresented approving voters**, which differs from semi-strong JR candidate coverage.

Bredereck, Faliszewski, Kaczmarczyk and Niedermeier (2019, Theorem 1) establish \(\#\mathrm{P}\)-hardness of counting JR committees for general approval profiles; their reduction does not supply a candidate-interval counting algorithm. Becker and Frank (2025, Section 6.3) find optimal quota-JR committees on candidate intervals but do not count every feasible committee. Elkind and collaborators (2022, Theorem 6) find a smallest justifying group on tree-representable profiles (a superclass of the candidate-interval domain), but do not count all JR committees.

This result therefore identifies a tractable exact-counting and sampling regime for the general counting problem, including arbitrary election eligibility restrictions. The counting conclusion is not an automatic consequence of a polynomial-time algorithm for finding one committee.

## Limitations

The algorithm requires a supplied candidate-interval order, and its generalization to arbitrary approval ballots is not claimed. Uniformity is over distinct eligible committees, not over ballots or candidates. The first-public reference date records the date of the motivating DR work, not a claimed publication date for this theorem. The literature comparison covers identified accessible primary sources and database queries; unindexed or inaccessible equivalent work remains a priority risk. Independent audit has not been performed.
