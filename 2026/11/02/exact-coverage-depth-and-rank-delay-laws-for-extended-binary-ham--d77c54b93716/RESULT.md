# Exact coverage-depth and rank-delay laws for extended binary Hamming codes

## Finding

For every integer \(m\ge 3\), set \(n=2^m\) and \(k=n-m-1\). Let \(C_m=\operatorname{RM}_2(m-2,m)\) be the extended binary Hamming code, with its \(n\) coordinate positions indexed by \(\mathbb F_2^m\). Independently draw coordinate indices uniformly with replacement until the corresponding columns of any generator matrix of \(C_m\) span their full \(k\)-dimensional space. Denote the number of draws by \(T_m\). Denote by \(J_m\) the number of distinct sampled indices beyond \(k\) that have appeared when this first happens.

Define the integer counts
\[
 I_0=1,\qquad I_t=\frac{2^m}{t!}\prod_{j=0}^{t-2}(2^m-2^j)
 \quad(1\le t\le m+1),
\]
where an empty product is \(1\). Then, for every integer \(0\le h\le m\),
\[
 \boxed{\Pr(J_m\le h)=\frac{I_{m+1-h}}{\binom{2^m}{m+1-h}}.}
\]
In particular, \(\Pr(J_m\le m)=1\). The number of coordinate subsets of cardinality \(s\) spanning \(C_m\) is exactly \(I_{n-s}\) when \(k\le s\le n\), and is \(0\) when \(s<k\). Hence the complete finite coverage-depth expectation is
\[
 \boxed{\mathbb E T_m=2^m H_{2^m}
 -2^m\sum_{t=1}^{m+1}\frac{I_t}{t\binom{2^m}{t}},\qquad H_v=\sum_{i=1}^{v}\frac1i.}
\]
Moreover the full draw-count distribution is determined explicitly: for each nonnegative integer \(\ell\),
\[
 \Pr(T_m\le\ell)
 =\frac1{n^\ell}\sum_{s=k}^{\min\{n,\ell\}}
 I_{n-s}\,s!\,\left\{\begin{matrix}\ell\\s\end{matrix}\right\},
\]
where the braces are Stirling numbers of the second kind.

Let \(B_m=n(H_n-H_{m+1})\) denote the MDS coupon-collector benchmark with the same formal parameters. For each fixed nonnegative integer \(h\),
\[
 \boxed{\lim_{m\to\infty}\Pr(J_m\le h)
 =Q_h:=\prod_{j=h+1}^{\infty}(1-2^{-j}).}
\]
This is the same excess-rank waiting-time distribution as for independently sampled uniform binary vectors in growing dimension. In addition, the **sharp leading correction to the benchmark** satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{m}{2^m}\left(\mathbb E T_m-B_m\right)
 =\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac1{2^j-1}
 =1.60669515241529\ldots.}
\]
The series is the classical Erdős–Borwein constant; no new identity for that constant is claimed. The assertion is its occurrence as the sharp coverage-delay coefficient for this deterministic extended-Hamming family.

## Assumptions and scope

The alphabet is \(\mathbb F_2\), the code dimension is \(k=2^m-m-1\), and the complete set of \(2^m\) coordinate positions is equally likely on each independent error-free draw. An information set here means any coordinate subset whose corresponding generator columns have full row rank; it need not be a minimal basis. The result is invariant under changing the generator-matrix basis. No assertion is made about codes of other dimensions, arbitrary nonuniform distributions, optimality among all \([n,k]_2\) codes, noisy channels or quantum codes.

For clarity, \(C_m\) is also called the extended binary Hamming code. Its dual is the first-order Reed–Muller code \(D_m=\operatorname{RM}_2(1,m)\). A generator matrix for \(D_m\) has one column \((1,x)^{\mathsf T}\) for each \(x\in\mathbb F_2^m\). Thus its columns form an affine chart of the binary projective geometry. When \(s=n\), the spanning-set formula uses \(I_0=1\).

## Proof

**1. Dual identification.** Express a binary Reed–Muller code as evaluations on \(\mathbb F_2^m\) of squarefree monomials. The dimension of \(C_m\), generated by monomials of degrees at most \(m-2\), equals \(\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\binom mj=n-m-1\). The dimension of the affine-linear evaluation code \(D_m\) is \(m+1\). Products of a monomial of degree at most \(m-2\) with an affine-linear monomial have degree at most \(m-1\). Their sums over all points of \(\mathbb F_2^m\) vanish modulo \(2\), since at least one coordinate remains free and contributes an even factor. The codes are orthogonal and their dimensions add to \(n\), so \(D_m=C_m^\perp\).

**2. Complements of spanning sets.** Let \(E\) be the complete coordinate index set and let \(S\subseteq E\) have complement \(U=E\setminus S\). For generator column matroids of dual codes, elementary rank-nullity gives
\[
 \operatorname{rank}_{C_m}(S)
 =|S|-(m+1)+\operatorname{rank}_{D_m}(U).
\]
Consequently \(S\) spans the dimension \(k=n-m-1\) if and only if \(\operatorname{rank}_{D_m}(U)=|U|\). This is equivalence, not merely a sufficient condition; it covers all subset sizes.

**3. Exact affine independence census.** A collection of columns \((1,x)\) is linearly independent precisely when the underlying points \(x\) are affinely independent. There are \(n\) choices of the first point. Given \(j\ge1\) affinely independent points, their affine span contains exactly \(2^{j-1}\) points; hence there are \(n-2^{j-1}\) choices for the next point. For an ordered affine-independent \(t\)-tuple, the product is
\[
 n\prod_{j=0}^{t-2}(n-2^j),
\]
valid for \(1\le t\le m+1\). Divide by \(t!\) to forget the order, yielding \(I_t\). There are no affinely independent sets of size above \(m+1\). Step 2 now shows that the number of spanning \(s\)-subsets of \(C_m\) is \(I_{n-s}\) for \(k\le s\le n\), and zero otherwise.

**4. Stopping distributions.** In uniform sampling with replacement, the first \(s\) distinct coordinate indices, in their order of discovery, form a uniformly random ordered sample of \(s\) distinct indices. When exactly \(k+h\) distinct indices have been seen, full rank is achieved if and only if the complement of those \(k+h\) coordinates (of cardinality \(m+1-h\)) is affinely independent. Because full rank is monotone under adding columns, this is equivalent to \(J_m\le h\). Step 3 proves the exact finite CDF. At a fixed number \(\ell\) of ordinary draws, each spanning \(s\)-element image set occurs in exactly \(s!\left\{\begin{smallmatrix}\ell\\s\end{smallmatrix}\right\}\) sequences. Summation yields the displayed finite draw-count distribution.

**5. The exact expectation.** Let \(N_s\) be the number of ordinary draws required to see \(s\) distinct indices, with \(N_0=0\). Conditional on seeing \(s\) distinct indices, the waiting time to the next new index is geometric with success probability \((n-s)/n\), hence mean \(n/(n-s)\). The identities of the distinct indices are uniformly ordered and independent of the successive waiting times. Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E T_m
 =\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}\frac{n}{n-s}\Pr(\text{the first }s\text{ distinct columns do not span}).
\]
The spanning probability vanishes for \(s<k\) and equals \(I_{n-s}/\binom ns\) for \(s\ge k\). Subtracting the spanning contributions from \(nH_n\), and putting \(t=n-s\), proves the finite expectation formula. The equivalent benchmark-gap expression, used below, is
\[
 \mathbb E T_m-B_m
 =n\sum_{h=0}^{m}\frac{\Pr(J_m>h)}{m+1-h}.
\]
The term for \(h=m\) is zero. This identity follows by writing \(s=k+h\) in the waiting-time sum.

**6. Limiting rank-delay CDF and a uniform tail.** For any fixed \(h\ge0\), let \(t=m+1-h\). For all sufficiently large \(m\), the exact CDF can be factored as
\[
 \frac{I_t}{\binom nt}
 =\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{r=h+1}^{m}(1-2^{-r})}
 {\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m-h}(1-j/2^m)}.
\]
The denominator tends to \(1\): its logarithmic discrepancy is at most of order \(m^2/2^m\). The numerator converges to the stated convergent infinite product \(Q_h\). Because the denominator is at most \(1\), and because the product of terms in \([0,1]\) is at least one minus the sum of their deficits, for every \(0\le h\le m\) we have
\[
 0\le\Pr(J_m>h)
 \le1-\prod_{r=h+1}^{m}(1-2^{-r})
 \le\sum_{r=h+1}^{m}2^{-r}\le2^{-h}.
\]
Thus the tails are uniformly summable and \(\mathbb E J_m\to\sum_{h\ge0}(1-Q_h)\).

For identification of this series, sample independent uniform vectors in \(\mathbb F_2^m\) until they span the entire space, and call the number of vector draws \(Y_m\). The full-rank probability of the first \(m+h\) vectors is
\[
 \Pr(Y_m-m\le h)=\prod_{r=h+1}^{h+m}(1-2^{-r}),
\]
which converges to exactly \(Q_h\). Further, when the already sampled vectors span a subspace of dimension \(j\), the next sample increases the rank with probability \(1-2^{j-m}\). The expected total number of draws is therefore
\[
 \mathbb E Y_m=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac1{1-2^{j-m}}
 =m+\sum_{r=1}^m\frac1{2^r-1}.
\]
The same elementary product estimate gives \(\Pr(Y_m-m>h)\le2^{-h}\). Thus uniform tail summability permits expectation convergence, proving
\[
 \sum_{h\ge0}(1-Q_h)=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac1{2^r-1}.
\]
In particular \(J_m\) converges in distribution and in its first moment to the excess-rank waiting time of this random-vector experiment.

**7. Sharp benchmark correction.** Multiply the benchmark-gap identity from Step 5 by \(m/n\):
\[
 \frac m n(\mathbb E T_m-B_m)
 =\sum_{h=0}^{m}\frac{m}{m+1-h}\Pr(J_m>h).
\]
For \(0\le h\le\lfloor m/2\rfloor\), the factor is at most \(2\). Step 6 yields the summable envelope \(2^{1-h}\), so dominated convergence applies to that range. For \(h>m/2\), the total contribution is bounded by
\[
 m\sum_{h>m/2}2^{-h}=O(m2^{-m/2}),
\]
which tends to zero. Hence the limit is \(\sum_{h\ge0}(1-Q_h)\), identified in Step 6 as \(\sum_{r\ge1}(2^r-1)^{-1}\). This proves the sharp asymptotic coefficient and completes the theorem.

## Verification

The standalone file `verify_affine_coverage.py` uses only the Python standard library and can be executed using `python3 verify_affine_coverage.py`. It constructs the monomial-evaluation generator and the independent affine parity-check generator, checks their ranks, and exhaustively examines all coordinate subsets for dimensions \(m=3\) and \(m=4\); for \(m=4\), it also checks the dual rank identity for all \(65{,}536\) subsets. Independent absorbing-state recurrences calculate the expected number of draws at both of these dimensions. It tests the rational CDF and expectation identities for each \(3\le m\le12\) and numerically cross-checks the constant's two convergent series expressions. The final success marker is `AFFINE_COVERAGE_VERIFY_OK`.

For reproducibility, the exact expected draw counts at the first two dimensions are
\[
 \mathbb E T_3=\frac{115}{21},\qquad
 \mathbb E T_4=\frac{172043}{9009}.
\]
These finite computations are independent checks, not substitutes for Steps 1–7, which prove the claims for all \(m\ge3\).

## Relationship to prior work

Bertuzzo, Ravagnani and Yaakobi (arXiv:2507.20639, first public 2025-07-28) introduced finite-alphabet coverage-depth formulas and dual-code methods, notably analyzing simplex and Hamming codes. Their expanded arXiv:2603.06489 presents the general information-set duality machinery and first-order Reed–Muller expectations. Its Theorem 5.1 applies to unextended Hamming codes of length \(2^m-1\); the present family is the **extended** binary Hamming code of length \(2^m\). The earlier duality identity is used as a standard ingredient, not claimed as new.

Gross and coauthors (arXiv:2610.07370) investigate optimality for first-order Reed–Muller, simplex and Hamming codes and derive random-code ensemble coverage formulas. Wang and Yaakobi (arXiv:2601.07053) establish general sampling bounds and asymptotic results. The inspected sections do not give the exact extended-Hamming spanning-set census, its complete distinct-index stopping-time law, or the proved matching of its scaled coupon-collector overhead with the binary random-matrix rank-delay constant. The asymptotic distribution itself is classical in random matrix theory; its **identification as the limit for this particular deterministic code family** is the point here.

Published formulas for arbitrary codes can, in principle, be specialized using independent auxiliary geometry. The novel claim is therefore not that a general expectation formula fails to apply, but the concrete exact affine-census specialization together with the deterministic high-rate limit and sharp leading correction. Priority cannot be established solely from indexing and the accessible literature.

## Limitations

The result assumes equiprobable error-free independent draws and full-information recovery. It does not prove optimality of extended Hamming codes among competing binary codes or improve the general lower bound for every code. It is also not an error-correction claim about the sampled data. The limit is for \(m\to\infty\), and the finite-code formulas are not approximations. Search coverage is necessarily incomplete: the result could have an earlier formulation in affine-matroid reliability, independent-set enumeration, or coding-theory sampling work not located in the examined sources. No external independent proof audit has been performed.
