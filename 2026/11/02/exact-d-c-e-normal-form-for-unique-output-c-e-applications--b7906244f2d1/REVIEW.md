# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact-domain assertion follows from selecting the first enumerated output and enumerating the inputs that later acquire a second distinct output. The converse inserts a conflicting output on a c.e. erasure set. Both index constructions are explicit and effective. For composition, the c.e. erasure set is the union of the first function's erasures and inputs whose intermediate output is erased by the second, restricted to the domain of the partial computable composite. The proof also verifies the converse d.c.e. domain realization and distinguishes noncomputable total functions with d.c.e. graphs from represented functions. The finite exhaustive checker confirms the set-theoretic identities in its stated range; no infinite theorem is inferred from the checker.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence
The closest primary source, Wang's arXiv:2610.23365v1, studies the same unique-output scheme and records inclusion of partial computable functions and a non-c.e.-domain phenomenon. Its inspected substantive discussion does not supply this exact normal form, complete difference-of-c.e. domain classification, effective closure under arbitrary extensional composition, or totality obstruction. Searches under unique-output, single-valued part, partial c.e. multifunction and d.c.e. aliases identified no stronger statement that covers all four elements together. General recursion-theoretic facts are treated as background. The primary residual risk is historical coverage under older enumeration-theory terminology, since the proof uses elementary effective operations.

## Value — PASS
The recent construction raises a natural structural question: exactly which extensional partial functions are produced by permitting a c.e. enumeration to have either zero, one or multiple outputs? The theorem completely answers it and locates the precise domain-complexity level. The effective closure theorem is mathematically relevant because weak combinatory structure alone does not by itself state the full extensional composition characterization. The result also exposes a sharp contrast: non-c.e. partial domains occur, but total noncomputable functions cannot; merely checking that a graph is d.c.e. is insufficient. This is a natural complete classification, not a numerical example or unmotivated special parameter.
