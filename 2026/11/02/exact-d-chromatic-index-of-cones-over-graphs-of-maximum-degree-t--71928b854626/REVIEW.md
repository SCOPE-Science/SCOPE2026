# Scientific review: cones with degree-two links

## Correctness

**PASS.** Every diamond in a cone over a graph of maximum degree at most two uses the universal apex. Its rim vertices necessarily contain a two-edge path, which makes the local constraint explicit. Independent lower bounds are the number of spokes, a triangular rim-edge configuration, five edges on a rim path of length two, and six colors forced by an exceptional small rim triangle or four-cycle. The constructive upper coloring assigns consecutive distinct spoke labels within each component, shifts rim-path edge colors by three modulo the palette size, and treats cycles of lengths three, four, and at least five separately. The proof verifies properness and every diamond. Deterministic exhaustive checks for 305 labeled and 295 component-profile instances support—but do not replace—the general proof.

## Originality

**PASS, with priority qualification.** Runze Wang's original paper develops general bounds rather than a cone classification; Hu–Kong–Wang and Verma prove much broader planar upper bounds without deriving the exact formula. Verma explicitly colors the different join with **two** universal vertices and a path link in a minimal-counterexample argument. Existing complete-multipartite and single-wheel conclusions cover some special subfamilies; the present claim is the full mixed-component classification and its sharp small-link transition. Searches of mathematical records, primary full text, published summaries and equivalent formulations did not reveal the complete theorem. Residual risk: alternate prior nomenclature for D-conflict graph colorings, and priority for some fan or wheel specializations.

## Value

**PASS.** This is a complete natural family classification, not a substitution in the general planar bound. For every cone with six or more rim vertices, the exact optimum collapses to the trivial degree lower bound, sharply improving the general planar bound on a structurally motivated class. The three exceptional local obstructions and a uniform cyclic-shift construction explain this collapse and cover disconnected rims; the result can be used as an exact building block in future graph decomposition studies.

## Limits

Only one universal vertex and a degree-at-most-two link are considered. No classification of all optimal colorings or of links with branching is asserted. Earlier wheel and complete-multipartite special cases are acknowledged rather than claimed as independently novel. Priority and correctness have not received independent mathematical audit.
