# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The single-excitation Lindblad generator, dephasing convention, absorber rate, and diagonal initial-state family are defined explicitly. The proof first uses Hilbert–Schmidt dissipation and the connectedness of the graph to establish that all generator eigenvalues have negative real parts. Integration of the eight symmetric density-matrix amplitude equations gives the trace-integral lifetime formula for all integers \(m,n\ge2\). The strict dephasing optimum follows from a positive second derivative and opposite endpoint signs. Positivity of \(A,B,C\), the Hessian, and boundary coercivity give a unique global joint optimum. The case \(p=1\) is expressly excluded from dephasing uniqueness. Exact symbolic identities (11), independently built full-Liouvillian solves (108), and root-monotonicity tests (30) pass, without extrapolating finite tests into infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence

A scientific-record search for the complete-bipartite Markovian dephasing/single-sink lifetime formula and its exact optimizing rate found no identical or dominating implication. Kassal–Aspuru-Guzik, *Environment-assisted quantum transport in ordered systems*, arXiv:1201.5202, treats dephasing-assisted transport principally on finite paths and cycles, with distinct loss-and-efficiency objectives. Novo et al., *Systematic Dimensionality Reduction for Quantum Walks*, arXiv:1412.7209, discusses bipartite coherent spatial search and coherent trapping, but no all-site dephasing/sink lifetime formula. Alterman–Berman–Strauch, *Optimal Conditions for Environment-Assisted Quantum Transport on the Fully Connected Network*, arXiv:2309.00164, calculates a complete-graph dissipative problem, rather than the no-within-part-edges bipartite one. These were inspected in accessible full text for their models, analytical results, and conclusions. Broader Haken–Strobl identities or uncatalogued earlier specializations remain a historical-priority risk; this evidence does not establish absolute novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Noise-assisted trapping is a well-motivated physical problem where a nonzero optimum competes with dark-state persistence at small dephasing and the quantum-Zeno slowdown at high dephasing. The exact law solves a natural two-class family across all sizes, and the strict decrease of the optimum with local absorption provides a parameter-design relation, not merely a finite example. The result distinguishes the initial population on the sink, where dephasing can no longer change the mean capture time.

## Limits and closest literature

The result assumes one sink, homogeneous zero-detuning coupling, equal site-local Markovian pure dephasing, and the explicitly given permutation-symmetric diagonal initial mixture. It does not provide capture-time distributions, a quantum many-particle transport theorem, non-Markovian robustness, or absolute historical priority.
