# Review of boundary-pole derivative-energy asymptotics

## Correctness — PASS
The independently reconstructed derivative formula and central-binomial Stirling normalization yield the exact Laplace representation. Endpoint Laplace gives the circular-cap coefficient, interior Gaussian Laplace gives the straight-edge coefficient, and a one-sided Gaussian gives the junction coefficient. The exact moving-boundary denominator identities produce the compact-uniform \(e^{16(\eta_+)^2}\operatorname{erfc}(4\eta)\) profile by dominated convergence. Power-series summability and polar cone integration prove the separate trace and Bergman thresholds. Finite quadrature checks are supporting evidence only.

## Originality — PASS with limitations
The nearest direct source, Hartmann–Orsoni, arXiv:2602.14801v2, already proves the qualitative one-diagonal failure and exhibits a simple pole at the upper midpoint; these are expressly excluded from a novelty claim. Its inspected Section 3 does not report the all-boundary individual derivative-order leading constants or the coalescing-saddle error-function window. The Aikawa–Hayashi–Saitoh 1991 sector identity has no finite square-related junction. Focused searches for these equivalent formulations and stronger covering statements found none. Implicit coverage in older Laplace/trace literature remains a genuine historical-priority risk, and independent comparison has not occurred.

## Value — PASS
The result determines where and how a geometrically natural derivative norm loses control over boundary poles. The two power laws, all nonvertex boundary strata, exact leading coefficients, and uniform error-function junction window provide a complete, mathematically motivated phase diagram for this model family, not merely a reproof of the source counterexample.

## Scope and caveats
Only powers of single boundary poles and a specified disk-union domain are covered. The source's general two-diagonal theorem remains distinct and is not refined here. Numerical tests are not infinite proofs. No claim of peer review, historical uniqueness, or external certification is made.
