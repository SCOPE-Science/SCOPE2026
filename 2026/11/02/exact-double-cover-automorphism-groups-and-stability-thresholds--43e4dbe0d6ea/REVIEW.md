# Scientific review: exact double-cover automorphisms of clique bouquets

## Correctness — PASS

For at least two peripheral cliques the common hub is the unique vertex of greatest degree, so its two lifts are the unique maximum-degree vertices of the cover. Deleting these lifts decomposes the cover into precisely \(2k\) isolated edge atoms arising from triangular blades and connected crown components corresponding to peripheral clique sizes at least three. An automorphism fixing the two hub lifts permutes the edge atoms freely (\(S_{2k}\)) and acts on each group of larger equal-size crown components by \(S_r\wr S_{t_r}\). A central involution exchanging the lifted hubs splits the full group as a product with \(C_2\). The ordinary automorphism group retains the original pairs of edge atoms, yielding the ratio \((2k)!/(2^k k!)\). Single-blade bouquets are complete graphs and stable. Eleven independently constructed small bouquet covers were checked by exhaustive graph automorphism enumeration. The proof rather than the finite enumeration covers unbounded parameters.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The existence of nontrivial instability in the multi-triangular-blade case is **already covered** by Hujdurović–Mitrović's construction (Proposition 5.2), subsequently used by Bychawski et al. (2026). It is not treated as a discovery here. What was not stated in the highly relevant primary texts or located exact mathematical-record comparisons is the complete automorphism group, explicit symmetry index, and full mixed-bouquet criterion. The nearest exact record on generalized windmills concerns dissociation polynomials; a result about theta graphs concerns homology of Vietoris–Rips complexes, not Kronecker-cover automorphisms. An unlocated general block-graph automorphism reduction remains a historical-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result determines an infinite mixed-size family completely, and exhibits the precise combinatorial mechanism of symmetry enhancement: the cover forgets the original pairing of \(2k\) peripheral edge atoms. The result cleanly separates stable cases from nontrivially unstable cases while giving the full automorphism group and a rapidly growing exact group-index sequence, rather than merely repeating a known instability witness. The first nontrivial index is three, and the index depends only on triangular blades even when larger clique blades are arbitrarily numerous.
