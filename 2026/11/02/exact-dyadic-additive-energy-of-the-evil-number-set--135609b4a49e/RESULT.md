# Exact dyadic additive energy of the evil-number set

## Finding
For an integer \(r\ge0\), let \(s_2(n)\) be the sum of binary digits of \(n\) and define
\[
A_r=\{n\in\mathbb Z:0\le n<2^r,\ s_2(n)\equiv0\pmod2\},\qquad
E_r=\#\{(a,b,c,d)\in A_r^4:a+b=c+d\}.
\]
The ordered additive energy of the evil numbers in a dyadic prefix has the exact ordinary generating function
\[
\boxed{\sum_{r\ge0} E_r z^r=\frac1{16}\left(
\frac{1-4z}{(1-2z)(1-8z)}+4(1-4z^2)+
\frac2{1+2z}+\frac4{1-2z}+
\frac{4(1-4z^2)}{1-8z^2}+
\frac{1+4z}{1-2z-16z^2}\right).}
\]
Here \(E_0=1\), \(E_1=1\), \(E_2=6\). For \(r\ge3\), write \(H_0=1\), \(H_1=6\), and \(H_r=2H_{r-1}+16H_{r-2}\) for \(r\ge2\). Then
\[
\boxed{E_r=\frac{8^r}{24}+\frac{13\cdot2^r}{48}+\frac{(-2)^r}{8}+\frac{H_r}{16}+\mathbf1_{2\mid r}\frac{8^{r/2}}8.}
\]
The sharp secondary growth is
\[
\boxed{E_r=\frac{8^r}{24}+\frac{1+5/\sqrt{17}}{32}(1+\sqrt{17})^r+O\bigl((\sqrt{17}-1)^r\bigr).}
\]
Equivalently, \(E_r=\int_0^1\left|\sum_{a\in A_r}e^{2\pi iat}\right|^4dt\). The exponential \(1+\sqrt{17}\) and its coefficient come from a genuine sign-four correlation component; the exact mixture with lower-order correlations for the zero-one digit class is the point of the statement.

## Assumptions and scope
All statements quantify over **every** integer \(r\ge0\), and quadruples are ordered, with repetition allowed. Zero belongs to \(A_r\). Additive equality is equality of ordinary integers, **not** addition in a Boolean cube and **not** congruence modulo \(2^r\). The generating function is a formal series over the rationals; its asymptotic uses \(r\to\infty\). No unproved hypothesis is used.

## Proof
Write \(t(n)=(-1)^{s_2(n)}\), so \(\mathbf1_{A_r}(n)=\frac12(1+t(n))\) for \(0\le n<2^r\). Expand four such indicators:
\[
16E_r=\sum_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^4}F_\epsilon(r),\qquad
F_\epsilon(r)=\sum_{\substack{0\le a,b,c,d<2^r\\a+b=c+d}}t(a)^{\epsilon_1}t(b)^{\epsilon_2}t(c)^{\epsilon_3}t(d)^{\epsilon_4}.
\]
Represent the four variables by their \(r\) binary digits, read from least to most significant. The addition on each side of the equality has a carry in \(\{0,1\}\); use four carry states \((u,v)\in\{0,1\}^2\), in the order \((0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\). For a fixed sign mask \(\epsilon\), define the explicit integer matrix
\[
M_\epsilon[(u,v),(u',v')]=
\sum_{a,b,c,d\in\{0,1\}}
\mathbf1_{a+b+u\equiv c+d+v\;(\mathrm{mod}\,2)}
\mathbf1_{u'=\lfloor(a+b+u)/2\rfloor}
\mathbf1_{v'=\lfloor(c+d+v)/2\rfloor}
(-1)^{\epsilon_1a+\epsilon_2b+\epsilon_3c+\epsilon_4d}.
\]
The low-order carry starts at \((0,0)\); after \(r\) positions, equality of ordinary integers holds precisely if the final carries agree, namely state \((0,0)\) or \((1,1)\). Consequently, with \(e_0=(1,0,0,0)^T\) and \(w=(1,0,0,1)^T\),
\[
F_\epsilon(r)=e_0^TM_\epsilon^r w,\qquad
\sum_{r\ge0}F_\epsilon(r)z^r=e_0^T(I-zM_\epsilon)^{-1}w.
\]
This is a bijective bitwise enumeration, not a numerical approximation. Each matrix has dimension four and all sixteen matrices are constructed by the displayed sum of at most sixteen signed terms per entry. Symmetry of swapping \(a,b\), swapping \(c,d\), and swapping the two sides gives **six** mask orbits. Their representative masks, orbit multiplicities, and exact rational generating functions \(e_0^T(I-zM_\epsilon)^{-1}w\) are:

| Mask | Multiplicity | Exact rational series |
|---|---:|---|
| `0000` | 1 | \(\frac{1-4z}{(1-2z)(1-8z)}\) |
| `0001` | 4 | \(1-4z^2\) |
| `0011` | 2 | \(\frac1{1+2z}\) |
| `0101` | 4 | \(\frac1{1-2z}\) |
| `0111` | 4 | \(\frac{1-4z^2}{1-8z^2}\) |
| `1111` | 1 | \(\frac{1+4z}{1-2z-16z^2}\) |

For transparency, the first and last representative matrices are
\[
M_{0000}=\begin{pmatrix}5&1&1&1\\2&4&0&2\\2&0&4&2\\1&1&1&5\end{pmatrix},\qquad
M_{1111}=\begin{pmatrix}5&1&1&1\\-2&-4&0&-2\\-2&0&-4&-2\\1&1&1&5\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The remaining representatives and every entry can be regenerated by the standalone verifier. Each rational identity in the table follows by computing the corresponding four-by-four resolvent (or multiplying the claimed series numerator by its denominator and checking the four-by-four recursion). Multiplying each of the six rational functions by its orbit multiplicity and dividing by sixteen proves the boxed generating function.

For coefficient extraction, the first orbit yields \((2\cdot8^r+2^r)/3\), the single-sign orbit vanishes after \(r=2\), the double-sign same-side orbit yields \((-2)^r\), and the cross-side double-sign orbit yields \(2^r\). The three-sign orbit yields zero at odd positive indices and \(8^{r/2}/2\) at positive even indices. The final orbit yields \(H_r\), since its denominator gives \(H_r=2H_{r-1}+16H_{r-2}\), with \(H_0=1\) and \(H_1=6\). Substitution gives the closed formula for all \(r\ge3\).

The recurrence has roots \(1\pm\sqrt{17}\); hence
\[
H_r=\frac12\left(1+\frac5{\sqrt{17}}\right)(1+\sqrt{17})^r
+\frac12\left(1-\frac5{\sqrt{17}}\right)(1-\sqrt{17})^r.
\]
Because \(\sqrt{17}-1>\sqrt8>2\), the remaining terms, apart from the main \(8^r/24\), are bounded by \(O((\sqrt{17}-1)^r)\). This proves the stated sharp secondary term and error bound.

## Verification
The accompanying dependency-free script `artifacts/verify.py` derives all sixteen carry matrices directly from the digit rule, checks sixteen exact coefficients of each of the six matrix–resolvent rational generating functions, and independently counts integer-sum quadruples by convolution for \(0\le r\le10\). It also checks the formula against carry-state enumeration through \(r=16\). These finite checks are supplemental; the proof for all \(r\) is the carry bijection plus the rational matrix identities.

## Relationship to prior work
Shparlinski and Thuswaldner (arXiv:2105.04835v1, 11 May 2021; primary MSC 11A63) introduce the same dyadic sum-of-digits residue classes and obtain polynomial Weyl-sum estimates, not the exact fourth moment of the class indicator. Aistleitner, Hofer and Larcher (arXiv:1502.06738) study exponential sums over evil integers and their metric discrepancy. Aistleitner and Larcher (arXiv:1608.06847v1) note that the additive energy of the evil numbers has maximal cubic order; this is qualitatively weaker than the exact generating function and secondary spectral term. Mauduit, Montgomery and Rivat (DOI:10.1007/s11854-018-0050-y), preceded by Montgomery's 2014 public lecture (DOI:10.24350/CIRM.V.18610603), study **signed** Thue–Morse even moments and already cover the `1111` component \(H_r\). The exact result here concerns the **indicator of even digit sum**, which requires the full six-orbit mixture. Known signed-moment work is not claimed as new.

## Limitations
The result concerns dyadic prefixes only, not arbitrary truncation lengths or digit-sum moduli other than two. It establishes one fourth moment, not higher moments or new discrepancy estimates. Existing signed-polynomial fourth-moment formulas are prior art and overlap with a component of the proof; the 2018 article's full text was not accessible in this review, so an implicit mixed-moment statement there remains a bibliographic risk. Novelty is assessed against identified published statements, not proven by a failed search. This report has no independent scientific audit.
