# Exact Engel-word laws and inverse signatures for finite abelian-by-involution groups

## Finding

Let \(A\) be a finite abelian group, let \(\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(A)\) satisfy \(\theta^2=1\), and put \(G=A\rtimes_\theta C_2\). Define the left-normed Engel words by \(e_1(x,y)=[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy\) and \(e_{n+1}(x,y)=[e_n(x,y),y]\). Write \(H=(\theta-1)A\), let \(U_K\) denote the uniform measure on a subgroup \(K\le A\), and let \(\delta_1\) denote the point mass at the identity. For independent uniformly distributed \(x,y\in G\), the **full distributions**, for every \(n\ge1\), are

\[
\mathcal L(e_1(x,y))=\tfrac14\delta_1+\tfrac34U_H,\qquad
\mathcal L(e_n(x,y))=\tfrac12\delta_1+\tfrac12U_{2^{n-1}H}\quad(n\ge2).
\]

In particular, if \(p_n(G)=\Pr(e_n(x,y)=1)\), then

\[
p_1(G)=\tfrac14+\frac{3}{4|H|},\qquad p_n(G)=\tfrac12+\frac{1}{2|2^{n-1}H|}\quad(n\ge2).
\]

The infinite scalar sequence \((p_n(G))_{n\ge1}\) **uniquely determines the order of the odd-primary part of \(H\) and the isomorphism type of the Sylow \(2\)-subgroup of \(H\)**. Two groups in the stated class have the same sequence if and only if these two invariants agree. Every finite abelian group occurs as \(H\) for some group in this class, so the description is also a realizability classification of the possible sequences. The odd-primary part is not in general determined up to isomorphism.

## Assumptions and scope

All groups are finite; \(A\) is written additively. The action of the distinguished involution can be trivial or nonfaithful. An expression such as \(2^jH\) denotes the image of multiplication by \(2^j\) on the abelian group \(H\), not a subgroup of index \(2^j\). Probabilities are uniform on ordered pairs of elements of the entire group, and the commutator convention is \([x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy\). The normal subgroup \(H=(\theta-1)A\) is the derived group \(G'\).

## Proof

**The first commutator.** Write elements as \((a,\varepsilon)\), where \(a\in A\), \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}\), and

\[
(a,\varepsilon)(b,\eta)=\bigl(a+\theta^\varepsilon(b),\varepsilon+\eta\pmod2\bigr).
\]

Set \(D=\theta-1\). Direct multiplication gives the four cases, with the commutator written in the embedded copy of \(A\):

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
&\eta=0&\eta=1\\\hline
\varepsilon=0&0&Da\\
\varepsilon=1&-Db&D(b-a)
\end{array}.
\]

The pair of cosets is uniform on four choices. In each of the three nonzero-case rows/cells the argument \(a\), \(b\), or \(b-a\) is uniform on \(A\); its image under \(D\) is uniform on \(H=D(A)\). This proves the first distribution. The commutator subgroup is generated by the same image \(H\), proving \(G'=H\).

**Higher Engel words.** For \(h\in A\) and \(y=(b,\eta)\), the commutator \([h,y]\) is \(0\) if \(\eta=0\) and \(Dh\) if \(\eta=1\). If \(\eta=1\), then for either value of \(\varepsilon\) in the first commutator, \(e_1(x,y)\) is uniform on \(H\). Thus at every \(n\ge2\), conditionally on \(\eta=0\), the Engel word is identically zero, while conditionally on \(\eta=1\), its distribution is uniform on \(D^{n-1}H\). Since \(\theta^2=1\),

\[
D^2=(\theta-1)^2=\theta^2-2\theta+1=-2D.
\]

For every \(h=Da\in H\), it follows that \(Dh=-2h\); hence \(D^{n-1}H=2^{n-1}H\). A homomorphism sends a uniform random element of a finite group to the uniform distribution on its image. The higher-word distributions follow, including the case \(H=\{0\}\).

**Inverse recovery.** Put \(h_j=|2^jH|\), for \(j\ge0\). The probabilities give

\[
h_0=\frac{3}{4p_1-1},\qquad h_j=\frac1{2p_{j+1}-1}\quad(j\ge1).
\]

Decompose \(H=H_2\oplus H_{\mathrm{odd}}\) and let \(o=|H_{\mathrm{odd}}|\). The sequence \(h_j\) eventually stabilizes at \(o\), since multiplication by \(2\) is invertible on \(H_{\mathrm{odd}}\). Write \(H_2\cong\bigoplus_{r\ge1}(C_{2^r})^{m_r}\), with only finitely many nonzero \(m_r\). Set \(s_j=\log_2(h_{j-1}/o)\) for \(j\ge1\), and set the trailing \(s_j=0\) after stabilization. Then

\[
s_j=\sum_{r\ge j}(r-j+1)m_r,\quad
m_j=s_j-2s_{j+1}+s_{j+2}\quad(j\ge1).
\]

These integers uniquely determine \(H_2\). Conversely the scalar formulas depend only on \(o\) and the isomorphism type of \(H_2\), so these two invariants are precisely what the sequence retains. Notice that the order \(o\), not the odd-primary decomposition, is retained.

**Realizability.** Given any finite abelian group \(B\), take \(A=B\oplus B\) and let \(\theta(a,b)=(b,a)\). Then \((\theta-1)A=\{(c,-c):c\in B\}\cong B\). This proves that every finite abelian group \(H\) can arise and hence that every sequence described by the formula and a finite abelian \(H\) is attained.

## Verification

The standalone file `check_engel_laws.py` uses exact rational arithmetic, independently implements semidirect multiplication and inverses, computes every pair of group elements, and evaluates successive commutators by direct multiplication. For twelve choices of finite abelian groups and involutions (including trivial action, inversion, non-inversion involutions of \(C_8\), swap, and negative swap), it compares the complete probability mass function for each \(1\le n\le6\) with the predicted mixture, not merely its mass at the identity. The recorded result is `VERIFY_OK`. This is finite corroboration, not the proof of the unrestricted theorem.

To reproduce from the artifact directory, run `python3 check_engel_laws.py` and compare standard output with `checker_output.txt`; no nonstandard Python packages are required.

## Relationship to prior work

The recent paper by Putignano and Vannacci (arXiv:2610.23100, first posted 2026-10-16) compares word probabilities through inequalities and in particular gives a bound involving the \(2\)-Engel word; it does not give the present finite abelian-by-involution distribution and inverse-recovery laws. A known **special case must not be claimed as new**: Erfanian and Farrokhi (2013) calculated the \(2\)-Engel identity probability for the ordinary dihedral group of order \(2m\) as \((m+\gcd(m,4))/(2m)\), which agrees with the displayed formula when \(A=C_m\) and \(\theta=-1\). Other prior work studies Engel-pair existence probabilities and general word maps rather than this entire series of distributions and its inverse classification. The formulas are elementary enough that some portions may be implicit in earlier treatment; historical priority requires independent checking.

A related accessible mathematical finding on Engel digraphs of abelian-kernel Frobenius groups classifies reachability components of a directed graph, a different invariant from the distribution of any fixed Engel word on random ordered pairs. None of these comparisons certifies absolute novelty.

## Limitations

The result does not extend as stated to arbitrary cyclic complements, arbitrary metabelian groups, or general non-involutive actions. The scalar signature does not distinguish odd-primary abelian groups having the same order, nor does it determine the whole semidirect group. The finite checker is exhaustive only for its stated small instances and Engel-word lengths. Independent review has not been conducted.
