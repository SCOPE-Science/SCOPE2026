# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS

The unrestricted lower bound is obtained by combining exact Hilbert-space leaf variance identities, the center–leaf and bridge constraints, and the centroid triangle inequality. The resulting scalar expression strictly decreases in leaf radii, giving a rigorous global lower bound for **every** map and **every** target dimension. A mutually orthogonal pair of regular simplices with a common centroid axis realizes equality; the nontrivial opposite-center distances are checked against both bounds using a separate sharp scalar comparison. Equality conditions prove congruence and affine-dimension rigidity. A high-precision standalone checker numerically verifies 173 parameter pairs and 28,223 explicit vertex pairs. Finite verification is not substituted for the all-parameter proof.

## Originality: PASS (qualified historical priority)

Choudhury–Nandi [1, §3.3, Theorem 3.6] study the same double stars but compute their quadratic embedding constant, a different negative-type invariant, not the minimum Hilbert distortion of the shortest-path metric. Robertson [2, Theorem 4.3] computes optimal Hilbert distortion for complete bipartite metrics, not double stars. Babilon–Matoušek–Maxová–Valtr [3] give fixed-dimensional general-tree/fan bounds rather than the exact unrestricted-dimensional constant. Lee–Naor–Peres [4] derive universal-factor distortion estimates for metric trees, not exact bistar formulas. Comparable accessible research records for bistar symmetric edge polytopes, zero forcing, and p-negative type address different invariants. A 1998 paper of Linial–Magen–Saks was bibliographically relevant but inaccessible in full text; equivalent older results cannot be ruled out with certainty. Absence of a search hit is not asserted to prove novelty.

## Mathematical value: PASS

Hilbert distortion is a fundamental invariant of finite tree metrics, and diameter-three trees are a natural small-depth test family. The theorem resolves the entire two-parameter family by a dimension-independent scalar equation, supplies sharp strictly monotone dependence on both branch sizes, derives a nontrivial bounded asymptotic extremum, and classifies **all** optimizers and their forced affine dimension. These are structural, quantitative statements beyond a routine normalization or a single finite example. A more general weighted-tree theory is not claimed.

## Sources and residual risks

The strongest direct same-object comparison is [1, Theorem 3.6]; its QEC is not the claimed bi-Lipschitz invariant. The closest exact distortion method is [2, Theorem 4.3], but its graphs and admissible distance matrices differ. The fixed-plane and asymptotic results [3] and [4] cannot imply sharp all-dimension formulas without additional arguments. Full text of the 1998 Linial–Magen–Saks source was unavailable despite legitimate access attempts, leaving a limited historical-priority risk. Independent mathematical audit has not been conducted.
