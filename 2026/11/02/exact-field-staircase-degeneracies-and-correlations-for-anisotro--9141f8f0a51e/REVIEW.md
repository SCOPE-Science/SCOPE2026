# Scientific review: anisotropic rectangular rook-graph Ising ground states

## Correctness — PASS
The exact pair-sum identity reduces the Hamiltonian to a positive combination of row- and column-degree square sums. Strict convex balancing gives a lower bound for any fixed number of positive spins. A constant fractional matrix with the desired total proves feasibility of the capacitated bipartite flow, and total unimodularity supplies an integral matrix attaining both row and column balance simultaneously. Because both exchange strengths are strictly positive, these are precisely *all* minimizers. Subtracting adjacent fixed-magnetization energies yields the complete nondecreasing threshold sequence and the gcd count of plateau sectors. The generating coefficient counts all labelled balanced-margin matrices without double-counting. Row/column permutation invariance, exact line-sum variances and fixed total magnetization determine all three pair-covariance classes. Critical degeneracies, saturation matching specialization and the rectangular worked example follow directly. Exact and exhaustive finite tests agree; the proof does not rely on their finite ranges.

## Originality — PASS on evidence inspected, with residual risks
The closest historical problem of magnetization plateaux is in Honecker–Schulenburg–Richter (2003), arXiv:cond-mat/0309425, whose full accessible text treats *quantum* spin models on chain, square, triangular and kagome lattices, not classical rook graphs. Yu (2016), arXiv:1606.08191, examines Archimedean lattice Ising systems, not clique-product geometry. Dyer–Heinrich–Jerrum–Müller (2020), arXiv:2005.07944, analyze the antiferromagnetic Ising partition function on arbitrary line graphs, including magnetic fields and general maximum-cut tractability, but their inspected theorems establish approximate sampling/counting and hardness; they do not imply the exact coupled-margin staircase and covariance. Canfield–Greenhill–McKay (2006), arXiv:math/0606496, establish general binary-margin matrix counting and asymptotics, a preexisting constituent which is explicitly credited. Guruswami (1999) treats general line-graph maximum cut; full primary text was not inspected and could in principle contain related optimizing reductions. Earlier related finite complete-clique line-graph studies can also be a priority risk. Published research-record queries, general-literature searches, and accessible prior-item claim comparisons revealed no verified dominating claim, but searches do not prove absolute novelty. The mathematical contribution here is a complete all-field classification *with anisotropy and all three conditional spatial correlations*, not the known counting subproblem alone.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The rectangular rook family is a natural complete-bipartite line-graph spin system and its two distinct exchange channels are physically motivated, not a fabricated numerical slice. The gcd formula measures a genuine commensurability-controlled number of magnetization sectors, while exact labelled critical coexistence counts and three-orbit correlations are useful zero-temperature benchmarks for frustrated-spin sampling and inverse inference. The combined classification is substantially stronger than an isolated min-energy computation or routine matrix enumeration; no thermodynamic phase transition or broad algorithmic breakthrough is implied.

## Limitations
Positivity of both couplings, uniform couplings within each row and column, classical binary spins and a uniform longitudinal field are essential. Critical-field covariance requires sector conditioning. The full text of Guruswami's original optimization paper was not examined; the remaining historical-priority risk is explicitly retained. Exhaustive numerical tests cover only finite graphs, whereas the analytic proof establishes all sizes. No independent audit has occurred.
