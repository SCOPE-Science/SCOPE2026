# Exact finite-depth \(p\)-kernel ranks for valuations of totally split irreducible polynomials

## Finding
For a polynomial \(Q\) of degree \(d\ge2\) that is monic and irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) and has \(d\) distinct roots modulo a prime \(p\), the valuation sequence \(a(n)=\nu_p(Q(n))\) has exact cumulative finite-depth \(p\)-kernel ranks
\[
\dim_{\mathbb Q}V_e=\begin{cases}1,&e=0,\\ d+1,&e=1,\\de+2,&e\ge2.\end{cases}
\]
Here \(V_e\) is the rational span of all sequences \(n\mapsto a(p^j n+r)\) with \(0\le j\le e\), \(0\le r<p^j\). Thus the rank increases by exactly the algebraic degree at every depth beyond the second. This gives a quantitative, level-by-level refinement of the established qualitative non-\(p\)-regularity criterion.

## Assumptions and scope
All indices \(n\) are nonnegative integers. Write \(\nu_p\) for the usual normalized \(p\)-adic valuation. The polynomial has integral coefficients, is monic, is irreducible over the rationals and has degree \(d\ge2\). The reduction of \(Q\) modulo \(p\) splits into \(d\) *different* linear factors. In particular \(d\le p\), and Hensel's lemma gives distinct roots \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_d\in\mathbb Z_p\) with pairwise distinct residues modulo \(p\). Irreducibility ensures \(Q(n)\ne0\) for every integer \(n\), so all terms of \(a\) are finite.

For \(j\ge1\), let \(\rho_{i,j}\in\{0,\ldots,p^j-1\}\) represent \(\alpha_i\pmod{p^j}\), and put \(\beta_{i,j}=(\alpha_i-\rho_{i,j})/p^j\in\mathbb Z_p\). Define \(g_{i,j}(n)=j+\nu_p(n-\beta_{i,j})\). These are integer-valued sequences and the roots \(\beta_{i,j}\) are irrational, so no term is infinite.

## Proof
**1. Precise kernel decomposition.** Since \(Q\) is monic and splits into simple linear factors over \(\mathbb Z_p\),
\[
Q(X)=\prod_{i=1}^d(X-\alpha_i),\qquad a(n)=\sum_{i=1}^d\nu_p(n-\alpha_i).
\]
Fix \(j\ge1\) and \(0\le r<p^j\). If \(r=\rho_{i,j}\) for some unique \(i\), then \(p^j n+r-\alpha_i=p^j(n-\beta_{i,j})\), while all other root factors are \(p\)-adic units. Therefore
\[
a(p^j n+\rho_{i,j})=j+\nu_p(n-\beta_{i,j})=g_{i,j}(n).
\]
If \(r\) matches no root modulo \(p^j\), each factor valuation is strictly below \(j\), and hence \(a(p^j n+r)=a(r)\) is a constant sequence. For \(j=1\), nonmatching residues yield the zero constant. At every \(j\ge2\) a residue matching some root modulo \(p\) but not modulo \(p^2\) produces constant value \(1\). Thus \(V_1\) is generated by \(a,g_{1,1},\ldots,g_{d,1}\). For \(e\ge2\), \(V_e\) is generated by \(a\), the constant \(1\), and all \(g_{i,j}\) with \(1\le i\le d\) and \(1\le j\le e\).

**2. Affine rigidity of irrational conjugate roots.** Suppose \(\alpha_i=u\alpha_h+v\) for rational \(u>0\) and \(v\in\mathbb Q\), where \(u\) is an integral power of \(p\). Because \(Q\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\), rational affine map \(T(x)=ux+v\) carries every conjugate of \(\alpha_h\) to the corresponding conjugate of \(\alpha_i\). It therefore permutes the finite set of roots. Some positive power \(T^m\) fixes \(\alpha_h\). If \(u\ne1\), then \(u^m\ne1\), so solving \(\alpha_h=u^m\alpha_h+v(1+\cdots+u^{m-1})\) makes \(\alpha_h\) rational, contradicting irreducibility. If \(u=1\), a finite-order translation has \(v=0\) and hence \(i=h\). In particular, all \(\beta_{i,j}\) for \(j\ge1\) are pairwise distinct and none equals any \(\alpha_h\): an equality between two tails, or between a tail and an original root, would produce exactly a forbidden rational affine relation with multiplier \(p^{j-k}\) or \(p^j\). Each original root and tail is irrational because it is rational-affine-equivalent to an irrational \(\alpha_i\).

**3. Exact independence.** For any finite set of distinct \(p\)-adic irrational points \(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_s\), the valuation sequences \(n\mapsto\nu_p(n-\gamma_l)\), together with the constant \(1\), are rationally linearly independent. Indeed, to eliminate the coefficient of the function belonging to \(\gamma_l\), take arbitrarily large nonnegative integers \(n\) approaching \(\gamma_l\) in \(\mathbb Z_p\); its valuation tends to infinity while every other valuation is eventually constant, as all points are distinct. This forces that coefficient to be zero. Repeating for each point and then evaluating the surviving constant proves independence. In our setting \(a\) is the sum of valuations at the original \(d\) points, while each \(g_{i,j}\) is a constant shift of the valuation at a different tail point. The same limit argument isolates the coefficient of each \(g_{i,j}\) by approaching its tail point; approaching any original root isolates the coefficient of \(a\); finally the constant coefficient vanishes. Thus the displayed generators are independent.

For \(e=0\), only nonzero \(a\) occurs, giving rank \(1\). For \(e=1\), there are \(1+d\) independent generators, giving rank \(d+1\). For every \(e\ge2\), there are exactly \(1+1+de\) independent generators, giving rank \(de+2\). This completes the proof.

## Verification
The standalone program `verify_kernel_growth.py` computes exact integer valuations for three concrete split/irreducible polynomial-prime pairs and all residue subsequences through selected depths, and calculates sample-row ranks over the finite field of order \(1{,}000{,}003\). Observed ranks are \(3,6,8,10\) for \(X^2+1\) at \(p=5\) and depths \(1,2,3,4\); \(3,6\) for the same polynomial at \(p=13\) and depths \(1,2\); and \(4,8\) for \(X^3-2\) at \(p=31\) and depths \(1,2\). The program also checks that roots remain simple at the sampled depths. The code's finite sample ranks corroborate but do not certify the unrestricted infinite-sequence rank theorem; the proof above does.

## Relationship to prior work
Bell (2007) proved the qualitative criterion for \(p\)-regularity of polynomial valuation sequences, including nonregularity when a polynomial has irrational \(p\)-adic roots. His Proposition 3.2 constructs irrational-digit tails and uses a failure-of-finite-generation argument. In particular, nonregularity of \(\nu_5(n^2+1)\) is already explicit in that paper. The present finding **does not** claim that qualitative fact. It instead gives the exact rank of the *finite-depth* cumulative kernel at every depth and proves that its growth slope is the degree \(d\), using a separate rational-affine rigidity argument across all conjugate Hensel roots.

Medina, Moll and Rowland (2015 preprint) classified boundedness/periodicity versus unboundedness of polynomial valuation sequences and gave a root-based approach, but not this kernel-rank formula. Shu and Yao (2011) and Sobolewski (2023) provide broader qualitative regularity criteria, not an explicit degree-sensitive depthwise rank formula in the portions inspected. A full priority search cannot exclude an unlocated equivalent result, especially a specialized computation following Bell's argument.

## Limitations
The assumption that \(Q\) be irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) and have distinct simple roots at \(p\) is essential to this proof: rational-affine coincidences of roots can collapse the tail count. No claim is made for ramified primes, repeated modular roots, reducible polynomials, or multiple different local valuation scales. The result determines linear *rational* ranks of spans of entire sequences, not their finite-precision truncation ranks over finite fields. Historical originality is qualified; independent mathematical review is not performed.
