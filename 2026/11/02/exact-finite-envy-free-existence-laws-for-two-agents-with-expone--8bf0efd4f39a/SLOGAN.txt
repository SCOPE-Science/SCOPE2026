For two agents with independent exponential additive chore costs, the probability that an envy-free allocation exists is exactly \(1/2\), \(3/4\), or \(29/32\) for respectively two, three, or four chores. At four chores the normalized simplex has four singleton-dominance chambers of mass \(1/8\) and eight other comparison chambers of mass \(1/16\), so failure is an exact same-chamber collision of probability \(3/32\).
