# Scientific review: finite-extension graphical evolution-algebra orbits

**Correctness — PASS.** The identity \(T=A\sigma\), with \(A\) defined over \(\mathbb F_2\), makes the iterates \(T^m=A^m\sigma^m\). The finite-field normal-basis theorem decomposes Frobenius into cyclic coordinate shifts, producing the fixed-point rank expression with \(\operatorname{lcm}(m,f)\). The Fitting decomposition gives an exact, disjoint partition by first entry into the periodic image. For complete multipartite graphs, explicit factorization of the binary adjacency matrix yields its ranks at steps one, two and three and the stable Frobenius or involution-twisted Frobenius actions; the period and depth bounds follow. Independent direct state-map iteration in 21 small exhaustive configurations over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_4,\mathbb F_8,\mathbb F_{16}\) agreed with all formulas. This is a finite proof for arbitrary graph size and extension degree, not an inference from experiments.

**Originality — PASS (qualified).** Graph evolution algebras are introduced in Elduque–Labra (2013), whose primary full text was inspected; this construction is not claimed as new. Classical normal-basis and finite-linear-dynamics tools subsume the abstract linearization method. A published complete-multipartite Boolean parity census (full RESULT.md inspected) already yields all binary graph-power ranks and the field-two case; both are explicitly excluded from the new-priority claim. The new mathematical content assessed is the simultaneous all-extension-field fixed-point and transient census for the quadratic graph-evolution map, with the closed multipartite period law and the nonmonotone \(\mathbb F_4\)-versus-\(\mathbb F_8\) fixed-point count for \(K_{1,1}\). Searches for graph-evolution Frobenius-semilinear extensions and equivalent matroid/matrix orbit parametrizations did not locate the same formulas. Inaccessible possible covering text remains a material publication-priority risk, particularly Varro's characteristic-two study; a negative abstract search does not certify noncoverage.

**Value — PASS.** Evolution squaring is the canonical intrinsic quadratic operator of the graph-defined algebra and its finite-state periodic and transient behavior is a natural invariant. Noninvertible adjacency and Frobenius twists make counts differ from naïve alphabet-size substitution. The closed complete-multipartite formulas yield each primitive period and every transient depth for an unbounded graph and extension-field family; the parity-sensitive jump in fixed-point counts is a concrete motivated boundary between Boolean dynamics and characteristic-two extension-field dynamics. The general graph reduction is acknowledged as an application of standard tools, rather than claimed as a freestanding structural discovery.
