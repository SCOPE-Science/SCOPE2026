# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed domain is every odd prime-power field and nondegenerate bilinear form of dimension at least two; no characteristic-two assertion is made. Derivations of a spin factor identify with skew-adjoint operators, and Jordan-invertibility of pure vectors is equivalent to anisotropy. Alternating-matrix rank parity and the finite-field quadratic isotropy bound force rank two. Orthogonal stabilizer ratios, checked separately at dimension two, yield the two closed plane counts. On each anisotropic plane the one-dimensional skew Lie algebra supplies exactly \(q-1\) nonzero maps; its squared scalar is nonsquare. Witt extension and a determinant-minus-one reflection give exactly two maps per nonsquare scalar on each plane and one global orbit. The accompanying checker verifies the raw operator conditions in twelve configurations including a genuine extension field; general formulas rest on the proof, not finite extrapolation.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

A close primary full-text comparison was made against Kaygorodov–Lopatin–Popov, arXiv:1511.00742v2, especially Section 4.3. Lemmas 6–9 already imply that image subspaces are anisotropic even-dimensional spaces and construct suitable operators; that structural result is expressly not new. The examined text does not state the all-dimension odd-finite-field enumeration in both discriminant classes, the number of maps, or the \(\operatorname{Aut}(J)\)-orbit profile indexed by \(\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(D^2)\). The accessible version's Corollary 8 says that perfect fields admit no such derivation on simple spin factors, contradicted by the explicit example over \(\mathbb F_3\). This is a claim about that version alone. published-finding corpus searches using spin-factor, anisotropic-plane, rank-two derivation, finite-field counts, orbit and perfect-field wording returned no statement that implies the new count-and-orbit theorem. An unindexed equivalent formula and an inaccessible changed journal version remain specific risks; negative search is not a uniqueness proof.
