# Exact finite-field strata for stable rank-two squares of three-dimensional evolution algebras

## Finding
For every prime power \(q\), consider the \(q^9\) structure matrices \(A\in M_3(\mathbb F_q)\) of evolution algebras in a fixed ordered natural basis, with \(e_i e_j=0\) for \(i\ne j\) and \(e_i^2=\sum_j A_{ij}e_j\). Restrict to matrices for which \(S=E^2\) has dimension two and \(S^2=S\). The latter ensures that \(S\) is the stable nonzero derived-series term.

Let \(G_q=|\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_q)|=(q^2-1)(q^2-q)\). The numbers of these matrices, partitioned by the factorization type of the unique projective kernel of the multiplication \(\mathrm{Sym}^2(S)\to S\), are
\[
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{split}}&=G_q\left((q-1)^2\frac{q(q+1)}2+3q^3\right),\\
N_{\mathrm{double}}&=G_q(q-1)^2(q+1),\\
N_{\mathrm{irreducible}}&=G_q(q-1)^2\frac{q(q-1)}2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here ``split'' means two distinct rational linear factors, ``double'' a square of a rational linear factor, and ``irreducible'' an irreducible binary quadratic over \(\mathbb F_q\). Precisely in the split case, the stable square \(S\) itself admits a natural basis and is an evolution algebra. Thus, without excluding characteristic two,
\[
\Pr(S\text{ is evolution}\mid \dim S=2,\ S^2=S)
=\frac{q^4+5q^3-q^2+q}{2(q^4+2q^3-q+1)}\longrightarrow\frac12.
\]
The total number of matrices in the conditioned class is \(G_q(q^4+2q^3-q+1)\). All frequencies concern labeled structure matrices, not isomorphism classes.

## Assumptions and scope
Let \(k=\mathbb F_q\) be any finite field, including fields of characteristic two. For each matrix the vector space \(E=k^3\) has fixed natural basis \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) and bilinear multiplication \(e_i e_j=0\) for distinct indices and \(e_i^2\) given by the \(i\)-th row of \(A\). For subspaces \(U,V\subseteq E\), \(UV\) is the span of all products \(uv\). The derived sequence starts with \(E\) and iterates squaring of subspaces. \(S=E^2\) is the row space of \(A\); we restrict to \(\dim_k S=2\) and \(S^2=S\), with no further assumption about the annihilator, nonzero row count, or algebra isomorphisms.

The multiplication map \(\mu_S:\mathrm{Sym}^2(S)\to S\) is surjective by \(S^2=S\). As \(\mathrm{Sym}^2(S)\) has dimension three, \(\ker\mu_S\) is a line. Its factorization type as a homogeneous degree-two element of the symmetric algebra on \(S\) is invariant under base change; in particular the classification uses factorization, not a characteristic-dependent discriminant convention. Here \(u\odot v\) denotes the class of \(u\otimes v\) in the symmetric square, valid in all characteristics.

## Proof
**1. A geometric parameterization.** Fix a two-dimensional plane \(S\le k^3\). Let \(T_S:\mathrm{Sym}^2(S)\to k^3\) be the coordinatewise product map: for \(u,v\in S\),
\[
T_S(u\odot v)=(u_1v_1,u_2v_2,u_3v_3).
\]
A structure matrix with row space exactly \(S\) is equivalent to a surjection \(V:k^3\to S\), sending the \(i\)-th coordinate basis vector to the \(i\)-th row of \(A\). In these terms \(\mu_S=V\circ T_S\). A surjection \(V\) is determined by its kernel line \(K\le k^3\), together with an isomorphism \(k^3/K\cong S\). Therefore, for every fixed kernel line \(K\), there are exactly \(G_q\) such matrices.

**2. Generic planes.** Write \(S=\ker(w_1x_1+w_2x_2+w_3x_3)\), where \([w_1:w_2:w_3]\in\mathbb P^2(k)\). Exactly \((q-1)^2\) planes have all three \(w_i\) nonzero. On any such plane the three restricted coordinate functionals \(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\in S^*\) are pairwise nonproportional. Choose coordinates on \(S\) so that these forms, up to nonzero scalars, are \(x,y,ax+by\) with \(ab\ne0\). The three functionals \((u,v)\mapsto\ell_i(u)\ell_i(v)\) on \(\mathrm{Sym}^2(S)\) have coefficient triples \((1,0,0)\), \((0,0,1)\), and \((a^2,ab,b^2)\), hence are independent in *every* characteristic. Thus \(T_S\) is an isomorphism.

Consequently each kernel line \(K=\ker V\) gives \(\ker\mu_S=T_S^{-1}(K)\), uniformly over all \(q^2+q+1\) projective quadratic lines. By unique factorization, the number of projective binary quadratics that are products of two distinct rational lines is \(\binom{q+1}2=q(q+1)/2\); there are \(q+1\) squares of rational lines and \(q(q-1)/2\) irreducibles. Multiplication by the number \((q-1)^2G_q\) proves the generic contributions to all three formulas.

**3. Coordinate-degenerate planes.** The remaining \(q^2+q+1-(q-1)^2=3q\) planes have at least one zero entry among \(w_1,w_2,w_3\). On each, two restricted coordinate functionals are proportional, possibly with one vanishing; the third is independent. Therefore \(T_S\) has rank two, with one-dimensional kernel generated by the product of two independent vectors of \(S\), and its image \(W\) is a plane in \(k^3\). The composition \(V T_S\) is onto \(S\) exactly when \(\ker V\not\subseteq W\). There are \((q^2+q+1)-(q+1)=q^2\) possible kernel lines outside \(W\); for each, \(V T_S\) has the same kernel as \(T_S\), which is split with two distinct rational factors. Thus these planes supply \(3q\cdot q^2\cdot G_q=3q^3G_q\) additional split instances and none of the other two types.

**4. Equivalence with an evolution square and the probability.** Since \(\ker\mu_S\) is one-dimensional, an independent pair \(u,v\in S\) with \(uv=0\) exists exactly when its generator is a product of two distinct rational linear factors: one implication follows because \(u\odot v\in\ker\mu_S\), and conversely such a factorization gives independent orthogonal vectors \(u,v\) forming a natural basis. A repeated factor gives only a square-zero line rather than an independent pair, and an irreducible factor gives neither. Adding the three counts yields
\[N_{\rm all}=G_q\bigl((q-1)^2(q^2+q+1)+3q^3\bigr)=G_q(q^4+2q^3-q+1).\]
Dividing the split count by this total and simplifying proves the probability and the limit.

## Verification
A standalone enumerator (`verify.cpp`) constructs every \(3\times3\) structure matrix for \(q=2,3,4,5\), directly tests rank two and surjectivity of the induced square multiplication, and independently checks for two orthogonal independent vectors and for a square-zero projective line. It uses prime fields for \(q=2,3,5\) and the representation \(\mathbb F_4=\mathbb F_2[z]/(z^2+z+1)\). No formula-derived shortcut is used to classify a matrix. The outputs are:

| \(q\) | Stable, rank-two matrices | Split | Double | Irreducible |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 186 | 162 | 18 | 6 |
| 3 | 6384 | 5040 | 768 | 576 |
| 4 | 68580 | 50760 | 8100 | 9720 |
| 5 | 418080 | 295200 | 46080 | 76800 |

Each case returned `VERIFY_OK`. Compile with `g++ -O3 -std=c++17 verify.cpp -o verify` and run `./verify 2`, `./verify 3`, `./verify 4`, `./verify 5`. These checks are finite evidence; the all-prime-power statement follows from the foregoing symbolic proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Hu and Wen [1] demonstrated a non-solvable three-dimensional evolution algebra whose nonzero stable derived term does not admit a natural basis; their Question 2.11 asks when the stable term of an evolution algebra is itself an evolution algebra. The present theorem counts the answer exactly for the natural rank-two stable-square slice over *all finite fields*. It neither claims a solution of that question for all evolution algebras nor classifies isomorphism classes. Kaygorodov–Martín González–Viruel [2] survey low-dimensional structure theory, finite-field counts for *perfect* algebras, and general recognition methods, but do not provide the labeled rank-two stable-derived-square distribution above. Existing general simultaneous-diagonalization recognition criteria [3] are acknowledged: the new content is the exact finite-field three-stratum distribution and conditional probability, not a novel recognition theorem in general.

## Limitations
The counts are for a specified ordered basis, not orbits under isomorphism, and they are conditional on \(\dim E^2=2\) and \((E^2)^2=E^2\). They do not settle unrestricted derived-series recognition in larger dimension or enumerate nonstable rank-two cases. Historical priority is not absolute: an older unindexed enumeration or a combination of known classification results might imply the formulas. Independent mathematical audit has not been conducted.
