# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The exact co-rotating equations, noncollinear closure rank, complexified real Jacobian, rank-two determinant reduction, and explicit fourth-order Routh–Hurwitz inequalities reconstruct the complete claim. The eigenspaces at \(0\) and \(-2\beta\) match their stated algebraic multiplicities. The slow branch follows from a nonzero derivative of the characteristic polynomial at the collinear limit; even-population antipodal pairs provide admissible approaching configurations. Ordinary local stable-foliation theory yields convergence to a nearby member of the equilibrium manifold, not to a fixed preselected point. Exact rational eight-dimensional symbolic and multiple finite-dimensional numerical checks replay the certificate; computation is not treated as proof for all ensemble sizes.

## Originality — PASS under inspected evidence
Zhao and Uchida (arXiv:2610.08239v1, Section 5) give the invariant polygon family and perform numerical transverse-stability sampling but not its exact finite-population characteristic polynomial. León and Pazó (arXiv:2006.02159, Sections IV.3.2 and V.1.3) already establish second-moment-dependent stability boundaries for incoherent states and must receive priority for that qualitative insight. Their continuum-limit/quasi-phase analysis does not itself assert the precise \(2M\)-dimensional finite-system spectrum, semisimple \(M-2\) multiplicities, nonlinear local stable foliation, or universal coupling-independent slow eigenvalue. Ku, Girvan and Ott (arXiv:1412.3803) study a different parameter regime of identical mean-field oscillators, principally clumps and chaotic transitions. The most significant residual priority risk is earlier theory of quadrupolar-order stability, including Chabanol, Hakim and Rappel (1997), whose full text was not accessible for a decisive comparison. No uniqueness claim follows from searches.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The full finite-ensemble spectral identity certifies all noncollinear closed polygons in the purely reactive regime at once, without numerical sampling and with the exact number of neutral and stable modes. More specifically, the sharp \(q\uparrow1\) law identifies a singular loss of transverse spectral gap and shows that its first-order rate is independent of the coupling and population size. This directly addresses the shape-sensitive stability object in recent polygonal-oscillator work while refining a historically studied incoherent-state stability phenomenon. No global basin-size, heterogeneity, or singular-collinear nonlinear statement is made.

## Limits and closest work
The principal technical restrictions are identical positive growth rates, zero dissipative coupling, zero nonlinear frequency shift, and a noncollinear polygon. Earlier literature already relates stability to the second Fourier harmonic; the present finite-size exact spectrum and slow-mode asymptotic, rather than generic harmonic dependence, are the proposed additional results. Historical priority and the singular collinear dynamics remain uncertain. An independent review has not taken place.
