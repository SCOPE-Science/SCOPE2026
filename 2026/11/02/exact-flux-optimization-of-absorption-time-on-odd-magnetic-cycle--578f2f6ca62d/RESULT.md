# Exact flux optimization of absorption time on odd magnetic cycles

## Finding

Consider any odd integer \(N=2m+1\ge3\), any source vertex \(j\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}\), a hopping strength \(J>0\), a local sink strength \(\kappa>0\), and a real total magnetic flux \(\phi\notin\pi\mathbb Z\). Set \(d=\min(j,N-j)\), \(L=\max(j,N-j)\), and \(\sigma_j=j\) for \(j\le m\), or \(\sigma_j=j-N\) for \(j\ge m+1\). The exactly integrated survival time of a single excitation initially at vertex \(j\) is

\[
T_j(\phi,\kappa,J)=\frac{N}{\kappa}+\frac{\sigma_j}{2J\sin\phi}+\frac{\kappa j(N-j)}{8J^2\sin^2\phi}.
\]

Its global minimum over all permitted fluxes is reached with \(\operatorname{sgn}(\sin\phi)=-\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma_j)\), and has a sharp sink-strength transition at \(\kappa_c=2J/L\). If \(0<\kappa<\kappa_c\), the minimizing fluxes have \(\lvert\sin\phi\rvert=\kappa L/(2J)\), with precisely two minimizers modulo \(2\pi\), and

\[
T_{j,\min}=\frac{N-d/(2L)}{\kappa}.
\]

If \(\kappa\ge\kappa_c\), the unique minimizing flux modulo \(2\pi\) is the quarter-flux with the stated sign, and

\[
T_{j,\min}=\frac{N}{\kappa}-\frac{d}{2J}+\frac{\kappa dL}{8J^2}.
\]

At equality the two branches agree. Starting at the sink site \(j=0\) instead gives \(T_0=N/\kappa\) for all allowed fluxes.

## Assumptions and scope

The vertex set is \(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\). With one-particle basis \(\lvert r\rangle\), take the Hermitian nearest-neighbor Hamiltonian \(H\) defined by \(H_{r,r+1}=J e^{i\phi/N}\), \(H_{r+1,r}=J e^{-i\phi/N}\) (indices modulo \(N\)), and all other entries zero. Let \(P_0=\lvert0\rangle\langle0\rvert\). The trace-decreasing no-jump evolution is generated by \(A=-iH-(\kappa/2)P_0\). The survival probability and mean absorption time are, respectively, \(S_j(t)=\lVert e^{At}\lvert j\rangle\rVert^2\) and \(T_j=\int_0^\infty S_j(t)\,dt\). The local sink is Markovian and perfectly absorbing when a jump occurs; there is no bulk dephasing. The flux interval excludes \(\sin\phi=0\), where spectral degeneracy and dark states can obstruct complete absorption.

## Proof

**1. Strict decay.** Write \(w=e^{i\phi}\). The eigenvalues of \(H\) are \(E_k=2J\cos((2\pi k+\phi)/N)\). For odd \(N\), these are all simple whenever \(w^2\ne1\). Every eigenvector is a plane wave with nonzero component at vertex \(0\). If \(A\) had an eigenvalue with zero real part, its eigenvector would have vanishing coordinate at \(0\) from the dissipative identity \(2\operatorname{Re}\langle u,Au\rangle=-\kappa\lvert u_0\rvert^2\); it would then be a nonzero eigenvector of \(H\) vanishing at \(0\), an impossibility. Hence \(A\) is strictly stable and \(T_j\) is finite.

**2. Integrated Lyapunov equation.** Set \(X=\int_0^\infty e^{At}\lvert j\rangle\langle j\rvert e^{A^\dagger t}\,dt\); then

\[
i[H,X]+\frac\kappa2\{P_0,X\}=\lvert j\rangle\langle j\rvert,\qquad T_j=\operatorname{tr}X.\]

Temporarily set \(J=1\), which amounts to rescaling time and the dimensionless strength \(\kappa/J\). In the plane-wave eigenbasis define \(s_k=\sum_l X_{kl}\). The diagonal equations give \(\operatorname{Re}s_k=1/\kappa\); write \(s_k=1/\kappa+i y_k\). The off-diagonal equations, together with \(\sum_l X_{kl}=s_k\), yield

\[
y_k=\frac1N\sum_{l\ne k}\frac{1-\cos(2\pi(l-k)j/N)}{E_k-E_l},\]

and the trace identity

\[
T_j=\frac N\kappa+C_j+\kappa B_j,\quad C_j=-\frac1N\sum_{k\ne l}\frac{\sin(2\pi(l-k)j/N)}{E_k-E_l},\quad B_j=\frac1N\sum_k y_k g_k,\quad g_k=\sum_{l\ne k}\frac1{E_k-E_l}.
\]

These are finite identities, not asymptotic expansions.

**3. The oriented term.** Put \(z_k=e^{i(2\pi k+\phi)/N}\), so \(z_k^N=w\), \(E_k=z_k+z_k^{-1}\), and define

\[F_j(z)=\sum_{t^N=w,\,t\ne z}\frac{(t/z)^j}{(z+z^{-1})-(t+t^{-1})}.\]

The partial-fraction formula for the denominator and the root sum \(\sum_{t^N=w}t^j/(a-t)=Nwa^{j-1}/(a^N-w)\), valid for \(1\le j<N\), imply

\[
F_j(z)-F_0(z)=\frac z{z^2-1}\left[j+\frac{Nw^2}{1-w^2}(1-z^{-2j})\right].
\]

Let \(S_r=\sum_{z^N=w}z^r/(z^2-1)\). Because \(N\) is odd, \(S_1=N/(2i\sin\phi)\), while \(S_{r+2}-S_r=\sum_{z^N=w}z^r\) gives \(S_{1-2j}=S_1\) if \(j\le m\), and \(S_{1-2j}=S_1-Nw^{-1}\) if \(j>m\). Also \(\sum_k F_0(z_k)=0\) by pair cancellation. Consequently \(\sum_k F_j(z_k)=\sigma_jS_1\). Rewriting the sine sum as the imaginary part of these expressions gives

\[C_j=\frac{\sigma_j}{2\sin\phi}.\]

**4. The even term by a residue identity.** Write \(P(E)=2[T_N(E/2)-\cos\phi]\), where \(T_N\) is the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind. It is the monic characteristic polynomial of \(H\) at \(J=1\). Its roots are the \(E_k\); \(P'(E)=N U_{N-1}(E/2)\). Let \(Q(E)=U_{j-1}(E/2)U_{N-j-1}(E/2)\). Reduction of the finite trigonometric sums in step 2 gives

\[y_k=\frac N2\frac{Q(E_k)}{P'(E_k)},\qquad g_k=\frac{P''(E_k)}{2P'(E_k)}.\]

Therefore \(B_j=\tfrac14\sum_k Q(E_k)P''(E_k)/P'(E_k)^2\). Apply the residue theorem to \(Q(E)P''(E)/[P'(E)P(E)]\). Since this rational function is \(O(E^{-3})\) at infinity, its total finite residue is zero. Its extra poles are the simple roots \(\xi_q=2\cos(q\pi/N)\) of \(P'\), with \(1\le q<N\). Hence

\[
B_j=-\frac14\sum_{q=1}^{N-1}\frac{Q(\xi_q)}{P(\xi_q)}
=\frac18\sum_{q=1}^{N-1}\frac{\sin^2(j\pi q/N)}{\sin^2(\pi q/N)[1-(-1)^q\cos\phi]}.
\]

For odd \(N\), pairing \(q\) with \(N-q\) gives \(1/\sin^2\phi\) times the same-weight sum. The finite discrete Fourier identity

\[
\sum_{q=1}^{N-1}\frac{\sin^2(j\pi q/N)}{\sin^2(\pi q/N)}=j(N-j)
\]

follows by Parseval for a length-\(j\) interval in the cyclic group. Thus \(B_j=j(N-j)/(8\sin^2\phi)\). Restore \(J\) through \(T(J,\kappa)=J^{-1}T(1,\kappa/J)\), obtaining the claimed three-term expression. For \(j=0\), all \(y_k\), \(C_0\), and \(B_0\) vanish, giving \(T_0=N/\kappa\).

**5. Global flux minimum.** The odd-cycle orientation coefficient has magnitude \(d\) and sign \(\sigma_j\). The optimal sign of \(\sin\phi\) is opposite \(\sigma_j\). With \(x=1/\lvert\sin\phi\rvert\ge1\), minimize the strictly convex quadratic

\[\frac N\kappa-\frac d{2J}x+\frac{\kappa dL}{8J^2}x^2.\]

Its free minimizer is \(x_*=2J/(\kappa L)\). It is feasible if and only if \(\kappa\le2J/L\); otherwise the constrained minimizer is \(x=1\). Substitution proves both minimum formulas and the number of optimizing fluxes.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` computes the Lyapunov integral directly in finite-dimensional complex arithmetic, independently of the root/residue derivation. It checks multiple odd cycle sizes, signs and strengths, source locations, and the flux-optimization threshold, and verifies the Fourier interval identity numerically. Run with Python 3, NumPy and SciPy: `python verify.py`. These tests are diagnostics; the proof of the arbitrary-odd-order theorem is steps 1–5 above.

## Relationship to prior work

Bose, Jin and Korepin (2004) study twisted-boundary spin-ring transmission and provide a precursor magnetic-ring dispersion relation, but optimize unitary finite-time transfer rather than the integrated survival of a ring with a Markovian site sink. Zimborás and collaborators (2012) study time-reversal-breaking enhancement of transport in finite quantum networks and dissipative settings; no general odd-ring exact Lyapunov sum or all-strength optimal-flux transition was located in the inspected model/results sections. Finocchiaro and collaborators (2025) study *stroboscopic projective local detection* on a chiral ring, a closely related but different detection protocol; their probability-versus-step optimization does not imply this continuous-absorption, integrated-mean, all-strength classification. These distinctions do not certify historical priority against the entire literature.

## Limitations

Only odd cycles, a single Markovian site absorber, single-particle coherent nearest-neighbor hopping and initial basis states are addressed. At fluxes in \(\pi\mathbb Z\), degeneracies can create dark components; the formula does not apply there. No statement is made about even cycles, fixed-time success probability, repeated-measurement first detection, bulk dephasing, many-particle systems, or physical realizability of arbitrarily tunable flux. The self-review is not an independent audit; historical novelty remains subject to expert verification.
