The six gaps between successive indices where the binary-partition number \(b(2n)\) fails the three-square test occur with exact limiting probabilities \((1/4,1/4,1/16,3/16,3/16,1/16)\) in order of gap sizes \((6,8,10,16,18,24)\), each with logarithmic counting discrepancy. The known six-value gap restriction therefore admits a complete quantitative frequency law, with mean gap \(12\) and variance \(32\).
