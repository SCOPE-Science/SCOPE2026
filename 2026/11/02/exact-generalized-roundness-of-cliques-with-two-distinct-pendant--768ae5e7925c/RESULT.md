# Exact generalized roundness of cliques with two distinct pendant edges

## Finding
Let \(m\ge3\) be an integer. Take the unweighted complete graph \(K_m\), choose two distinct core vertices \(A,B\), and attach leaf \(x\) only to \(A\) and leaf \(y\) only to \(B\). Equip all \(m+2\) vertices with their shortest-path metric \(d\). Put \(k=m-2\) and
\[
U(p)=4^p-6\,2^p+3^p+3,\qquad V(p)=2\,3^p-4\,2^p-2.
\]
Then the supremal \(p\)-negative-type exponent \(\wp_m\) is the **unique** solution in \((1,2)\) of
\[
\boxed{kU(\wp_m)+V(\wp_m)=0.}
\]
The metric has strict \(p\)-negative type for every \(0\le p<\wp_m\), has non-strict \(\wp_m\)-negative type, and fails \(p\)-negative type for every \(p>\wp_m\).

At \(p=\wp_m\), set \(q=2^{\wp_m}\). The whole zero-sum equality space is the line generated by
\[
\alpha_A=\alpha_B=k(q-1)+2,\quad
\alpha_x=\alpha_y=-(k+2),\quad
\alpha_{C_j}=-2(q-2)\quad(1\le j\le k),
\]
where \(C_1,\ldots,C_k\) are the other clique vertices. In particular the minimum isometric Euclidean dimension of the snowflaked metric \(d^{p/2}\) is \(m+1\) for \(0<p<\wp_m\), and exactly \(m\) for \(p=\wp_m\).

The exponents decrease strictly with \(m\), and
\[
\wp_m=p_\infty+\frac{c_\infty}{m-2}+O((m-2)^{-2}),\qquad
p_\infty=1.629581635586\ldots,\quad c_\infty=0.342777710028\ldots.
\]
Here \(p_\infty\) is the unique solution in \((1,2)\) of \(U(p_\infty)=0\), and \(c_\infty=-V(p_\infty)/U'(p_\infty)\). For example \(\wp_3=1.801349035082\ldots\) and \(\wp_4=1.743145057500\ldots\).

## Assumptions and scope
The graph has exactly one unit-length pendant edge at each of **two different** vertices of a unit-edge clique, and no other added edges. There are \(m\ge3\) clique vertices and exactly two pendant vertices. All coefficients in the negative-type inequality are real. Define \(D_p=(d(v,w)^p)_{v,w}\), with zero diagonal, for \(p\ge0\), interpreting the off-diagonal distance to the zeroth power as one. Strict \(p\)-negative type means \(\alpha^\top D_p\alpha<0\) for every nonzero real \(\alpha\) with \(\sum_v\alpha_v=0\).

## Proof
For \(p>0\), write \(q=2^p\) and \(r=3^p\). The only distinct positive graph distances are \(1,2,3\): every two distinct core vertices have distance one; \(d(A,x)=d(B,y)=1\); \(d(A,y)=d(B,x)=d(C_j,x)=d(C_j,y)=2\); and \(d(x,y)=3\). This list defines the whole powered-distance matrix. For \(m=3\), the underlying graph is the classical bull graph; for larger \(m\), it is the bull graph with its third triangle vertex expanded into a clique.

Decompose an arbitrary real zero-sum coefficient vector uniquely as
\[
\alpha_A=a+u,\quad \alpha_B=a-u,\quad
\alpha_x=b+v,\quad \alpha_y=b-v,\quad
\alpha_{C_j}=c+z_j,\qquad
\sum_{j=1}^k z_j=0,\quad 2a+2b+kc=0.
\]
A direct expansion of the full quadratic form, including every ordered pair, gives the **exact orthogonal-sector identity**
\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha^\top D_p\alpha
={}&-\sum_{j=1}^k z_j^2
-2\bigl(u^2+2(q-1)uv+rv^2\bigr)\\
&-\frac2k\bigl[(k+2)a^2+2(kq-k+2)ab
+(4kq-kr-2k+2)b^2\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
The fluctuation sector is strictly negative whenever nonzero. The antisymmetric sector is strictly negative for \(0<p<2\), because its positive quadratic matrix has determinant \(r-(q-1)^2>0\). Indeed
\[
\frac{2^p-1}{3^{p/2}}
\]
is strictly increasing for \(p>0\): the logarithmic derivative is
\[
\frac{2^p\log2}{2^p-1}-\frac12\log3
>\log(2/\sqrt3)>0,
\]
and the ratio equals one at \(p=2\).

The remaining real symmetric two-by-two matrix is
\[
M_{k,p}=
\begin{pmatrix}
k+2&kq-k+2\\
kq-k+2&4kq-kr-2k+2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
A polynomial identity yields
\[
\det M_{k,p}=-kF_k(p),\qquad
F_k(p)=kU(p)+V(p).
\tag{2}
\]
As its top-left entry is positive, this matrix is positive definite exactly when \(F_k(p)<0\), and positive semidefinite with a one-dimensional kernel exactly when \(F_k(p)=0\).

For every \(k\ge1\), \(F_k(1)=-2k-4<0\) and \(F_k(2)=4k>0\). Furthermore \(U'(1)=3\log3-4\log2>0\), while for \(p\ge1\),
\[
U''(p)=(\log2)^2\bigl(4\,4^p-6\,2^p\bigr)+(\log3)^2 3^p>0.
\]
Also \(V'(p)=2\,3^p\log3-4\,2^p\log2>0\) on \([1,2]\), since \((3/2)^p\log3\ge(3/2)\log3>2\log2\). Thus \(F_k\) is strictly increasing on \([1,2]\) and has exactly one root \(\wp_m\in(1,2)\).

Equations (1) and (2) prove strict negative type on \([1,\wp_m)\) and non-strict negative type at \(\wp_m\). The kernel of \(M_{k,\wp_m}\) is generated by \((a,b)=(kq-k+2,-k-2)\). The zero-sum relation gives \(c=-2(q-2)\), while \(u=v=0\) and every \(z_j=0\), proving the claimed, complete one-dimensional equality space. Strict negative type below \(1\) follows from the general exponent-reduction theorem: a metric of \(1\)-negative type has strict \(p\)-negative type for \(0\le p<1\) [Li--Weston, Theorem 5.4]. If the metric had \(p\)-negative type for some \(p>\wp_m\), the same theorem would force strict \(\wp_m\)-negative type, contradicting the nonzero equality vector. This proves the complete exponent classification.

For Euclidean dimension, the standard centered Gram matrix
\[
B_p=-\tfrac12 JD_pJ,\qquad
J=I-\tfrac1{m+2}\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top,
\]
is positive definite on the \((m+1)\)-dimensional zero-sum space below the endpoint, and at the endpoint has exactly the one-dimensional kernel described above within that space. The finite-dimensional Schoenberg criterion therefore gives minimum dimensions \(m+1\) and \(m\), respectively.

For the asymptotic and monotonicity, the function \(V(p)\) is strictly negative on \([1,2)\): \(3^p/(1+2^{p+1})\) is strictly increasing, and equals one only at \(p=2\). Hence at a root of \(F_k\), \(U(\wp_m)=-V(\wp_m)/k>0\). Increasing \(k\) therefore makes \(F_{k+1}(\wp_m)>0\), and its unique root must be smaller. The function \(U\) is strictly increasing from \(-2\) to \(4\) on \([1,2]\), so it has a unique zero \(p_\infty\in(1,2)\) and the decreasing roots tend to it. Taylor expansion of \(kU(p)+V(p)=0\) around that zero gives
\[
\wp_m=p_\infty-\frac{V(p_\infty)}{(m-2)U'(p_\infty)}+O((m-2)^{-2}).
\]
All coefficient and sign assertions in this proof are for every indicated integer \(m\) and every real exponent in the stated range.

## Verification
The standalone file `verify_two_pendant_clique.py` builds shortest-path matrices separately from the analytic formula by breadth-first search. It verifies (1) with exact rational arithmetic in 528 nontrivial zero-sum tests across \(3\le m\le13\) and integral exponents \(1,2,3\); it checks centered Gram signatures for \(3\le m\le24\), strict monotonicity in a larger finite range, and numerical asymptotic coefficients. Execution terminates with `ALL_TWO_PENDANT_CLIQUE_CHECKS_OK`. These tests corroborate but do not replace the universal algebraic proof.

## Relationship to prior work
The five-vertex case is the bull graph, and this family is its one-clique expansion. Sánchez, *On the supremal \(p\)-negative type of a finite metric space* (first arXiv version August 2, 2011), provides a general distance-matrix criterion and evaluates complete bipartite graphs and specific five-vertex examples. Its pictured five-vertex Example 2.6 has a central attachment hub and is not the graph with leaves on two **distinct** clique vertices here. The general matrix criterion makes finite evaluations possible but does not itself provide the evaluated all-orders equation (2), equality space, monotonicity, or inverse-order asymptotic. Li and Weston, *Strict \(p\)-negative type of a metric space* (first arXiv version January 6, 2009), prove the strict exponent-reduction principle used in this proof. These inspected works do not state the present family classification. Other complete-clique graph-attachment studies may provide formulas for different invariants; no equivalence with the present negative-type theorem has been established.

## Limitations
The result concerns *vertex shortest-path* distances and unit-length edges; it is not a claim about electrical effective resistance or heat-kernel transport. It does not classify attachment of multiple leaves at each of the two vertices, nonunit lengths, or attachment vertices inside different blocks. The computation and source searches do not prove publication priority: equivalent calculations may exist in literature not indexed or inspected. No independent audit or external formal certification is claimed.
