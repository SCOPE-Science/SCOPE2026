# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact characterization of prefix normality by consecutive one-gap sums includes every factor length and both boundary cases. Reducing the six positive gaps to five nonnegative offsets gives precisely four inequalities. A partition on whether the fourth gap offset exceeds the second yields two explicit geometric sums, and their cross-product identity proves the rational generating function in all degrees. A standalone integer-polynomial checker independently confirms that full identity, not only finitely many coefficients. Direct tests cover every seven-one binary word through length eighteen; a separate cone enumeration confirms counts through length twenty-nine. The asymptotic follows from the order-seven pole, and primitive fourth/sixth root poles establish the least quasipolynomial period.

## Originality — PASS, conditional on incomplete global indexing

Burcsi et al., Proposition 16, present density-specific formulas only for zero through six ones and explicitly report failure to extend the list beyond six. Their general fixed-density rationality theorem does *not imply* this specific reduced numerator, pole structure, or explicit coefficients. The 2025 paper by Adamson et al. develops word-chain generation, not a fixed-seven-ones rational formula. Published related studies of weighted prefix normality concern different classifications. Checks for aliases, prior stronger statements, tabulations and direct implication found no identical prior formula. Unindexed or subsequently published equivalent work remains a residual priority risk; search absence is not a proof of first discovery.

## Value — PASS

An explicit exact enumerator at density seven resolves the first case beyond a source paper's stated calculational limit. Its palindromic numerator, degree-six quasipolynomial, minimal period twelve and sharp leading fraction are nontrivial structural information for a natural fixed-content language. The result gives a reproducible infinite-parameter benchmark for enumerating prefix-normal word classes without asserting a general all-density advance.
