# Scientific assessment: flat Möbius-band geodesic dispersion

## Correctness — PASS
The quotient is precisely specified and its Euclidean covering strip is convex, so shortest intrinsic paths reduce rigorously to a minimum over image points. The two parity classes give exactly two candidate squared lengths for all endpoints, not merely sampled paths. Integrating the triangular longitudinal difference and logarithmic product density gives the displayed piecewise formula; the derivative-sign calculation proves global uniqueness of the fixed-area minimizer. The transition differentiability is verified by an exact fourth-order Taylor coefficient. The standalone code independently checks the formulas, but the mathematical proof does not rely on finite sampling.

## Originality — PASS with residual priority risk
Tanaka–Hiscock (arXiv:gr-qc/9504021, §III.B, Eq. 18–19) already provide the underlying glide-reflection image distances, which is explicitly credited. They do not average the minimum over two uniform points in a finite-width band, and their quantum-field setting does not imply the displayed area-constrained optimizer by specialization. Gálvez–Mira (arXiv:math/0301075) concern flat immersion geometry. Schwartz (arXiv:2308.12641) proves a sharp embedding aspect-ratio bound, optimizing a different property. Related existing rectangular Klein-bottle and cylindrical distance-law results concern other quotients. Searches for the Möbius-band squared-distance formula, the fixed-area minimizer, and glide-reflection metric dispersion did not uncover an exact precursor. Search absence is not uniqueness proof; older statistical-geometry literature may remain unindexed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Two-point squared intrinsic distance is a natural metric-measure dispersion invariant. Its all-aspect closed formula identifies where a new geodesic homotopy candidate becomes active, yields a genuine fourth-derivative phase transition, and solves a natural one-parameter fixed-area optimization problem with a unique nontrivial transcendental optimum. The result distinguishes a compact nonorientable quotient from an equally sized orientable cylinder. It does not assert a physical embedding optimum: the intrinsic minimizer cannot be smoothly embedded as an ordinary paper Möbius band.
