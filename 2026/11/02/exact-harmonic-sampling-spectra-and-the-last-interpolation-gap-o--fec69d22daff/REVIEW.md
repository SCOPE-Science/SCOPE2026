# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact object is the twenty unit dodecahedral directions, and \(E_k\) evaluates every real homogeneous spherical harmonic of precisely one degree \(k\ge0\). The addition theorem identifies the evaluation rank with the Legendre Gram rank, while antipodal parity reduces the problem from twenty vertices to ten representatives. Exact coordinate identities produce the Petersen adjacency algebra for even degrees and a signed matrix with minimal polynomial dividing \(z(z^2-5)\) for odd degrees. The resulting scalar eigenvalues, including multiplicities, are derived in RESULT.md. The standalone checker reconstructs the vertices, verifies those identities, and checks every rational eigenvalue numerator through degree \(185\), with zero degrees exactly \(0,1,2,3,4,5,8,14\). The analytic Laplace-integral estimate gives strict positivity at every degree at least \(186\). Thus the proof does not extrapolate finite experiments. The narrow limits concern only this exact-degree, unweighted sampling problem.

## Originality — PASS under bounded source access
The closest relevant primary sources examined were Hitchin (arXiv:0706.0088v1, readable first sections of the full primary PDF), Ye et al. (PMCID:PMC3343185, full relevant sampling/reconstruction sections), Cohan (DOI:10.1017/S0305004100033156, publisher extract but full text inaccessible), and Zheng–Doerschuk (DOI:10.1137/S0036141098341770, accessible abstract only). Hitchin studies degree-three icosahedral nodal sets, and Ye uses dodecahedral sample directions without these ranks. Cohan's explicit harmonic expansions stop at degree fourteen, so the source cannot by that stated range alone cover an all-degree permanent-surjectivity theorem; the exact low-degree comparison nevertheless remains a risk until the full text is inspected. Zheng–Doerschuk computes symmetrized harmonics for the identity representation, not a complete vertex-sampling evaluation spectrum. An existing thirty-two-point icosahedral-union result implies surjectivity of its dodecahedral subset in some degrees, including all degrees from \(45\), but it does not imply the sharp threshold at \(15\), the entire lower-degree sequence, or the exact three-sector eigenvalues. Relevant research-record searches found no equivalent all-degree dodecahedral statement. This is bounded originality assessment, not a mathematical proof that no prior version exists.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The regular dodecahedron is a standard twenty-direction spherical sampling constellation and an icosahedrally symmetric five-design. Exact-degree sampling is not monotone in degree: the image drops from rank nine to rank six between degrees four and five, returns to full rank at degree six, loses one dimension at degrees eight and fourteen, and remains full forever starting exactly at degree fifteen. A closed all-degree Gram spectrum and a sharp permanent interpolation threshold are natural structural invariants of this canonical configuration. This is a complete classification of a motivated finite-configuration/infinite-degree problem, rather than a renamed exercise or an arbitrary table cell.
