# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The incidence identity \(NN^{\mathsf T}=qI+J\) gives the four signed heat-kernel blocks and three positive signed masses \(a,b,c\) on each incidence-edge difference. Positivity is established for all real times by an integral identity for \(K\), not by sample numerics. A fully specified 1-Lipschitz potential yields the lower Wasserstein-one bound \(a+3qb+q^2c\), and a constructive optimal transport uses two elementary pairings plus a perfect matching in a regular bipartite residual incidence graph. A shortest-path triangle inequality lifts this edge certificate to the unrestricted operator norm without symmetry assumptions. A one-variable exponential-polynomial sign analysis proves the unique peak and crossing, and locally uniform rescaling gives the sharp large-order peak constant. The attached checker independently reconstructs small prime-field examples and compares primal transport linear programs with the expression; these are checks, not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Münch–Wojciechowski (arxiv:1712.00875, Theorem 3.8) gives **uniform exponential curvature bounds**, not the exact nonexponential finite-time coefficient. Fathi–Lakzian (arxiv:2203.16837, Section 6) gives operator-theoretic curvature formulations, not an explicit projective-incidence heat semigroup optimum. Cushing et al. (doi:10.1002/jgt.22630, Sections 4–6 and Example 6.3) investigate different curvature phenomena, including a non-projective symmetric-design incidence graph, rather than all-time graph-metric heat transport. Isoperimetry of projective incidence graphs (doi:10.37236/6980) concerns a different invariant. Published records and related mathematical records searched under projective-plane, Levi/incidence graph, W1, heat semigroup, curvature, distance-regular, and Bose–Mesner terminology did not reveal a stronger theorem implying the displayed formula or its peak asymptotic. This does not establish historical uniqueness. Equivalent earlier association-scheme or transport work remains a priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The same exact nonexponential norm holds across every finite projective plane of a specified order, including possible non-Desarguesian planes, despite lack of graph symmetry. It separates sharp *transient* W1 amplification from the instantaneous curvature estimate and determines both a unique amplification peak and its order-uniform limiting magnitude \(1.64046408905\ldots\). The matching primal/dual transport is a structural certificate usable beyond this numerical family. This supplies a new exact semigroup invariant, not merely a restatement of an adjacency eigenvalue or a numerical sample.

## Closest literature and limitations

The most relevant general antecedent is the heat-Wasserstein gradient equivalence of Münch–Wojciechowski. Its bound may be exponentially growing for negative curvature and does not identify the exact spectral-transport crossover here. No result is claimed for weighted or normalized semigroups, arbitrary incidence designs, or \(W_2\). Projective plane existence is a necessary hypothesis, and large-order limits run along existing orders. The novelty search does not certify that alternative earlier formulations are absent. Independent validation is still outstanding.
