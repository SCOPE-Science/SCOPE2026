# Review of the hierarchical Dudley-norm theorem

## Correctness

PASS. The normalization is \(\|f\|_\infty+\operatorname{Lip}_d(f)\le1\) and \(\sigma(X)=0\); centering the range is allowed only because total mass vanishes. The oscillation-constrained problem is exactly the truncated-metric Lipschitz dual. A direct tree-cut inequality and an edge-by-edge attaining potential establish the numerator, without relying on a numerical inference or an unproved transport identity. The quotient of affine functions has no interior strict maximum between cluster heights, proving the finite height reduction. All endpoint cases, signs, ties, and the two-cluster threshold are treated. The separately packaged checker passed 281 independent finite comparisons. Residual risk: independent expert proof review has not occurred.

## Originality

PASS, qualified. The strongest relevant primary result is the ultrametric \(W_1\) cut formula of Kloeckner (arXiv:1304.5219), which is an explicitly acknowledged ingredient; it does not state the dual **sum-type** bounded-Lipschitz formula or scale optimization. Hille--Theewis (arXiv:2305.08500v1, May 15, 2023) characterize general norming extreme functions and their finite-space setting; their earlier arXiv:2206.12234 Section 6 supplies a general Dudley linear program, not a closed ultrametric profile. Leeb (DOI:10.1016/j.acha.2016.06.008) gives a tree formula for a distinct mean-based max norm. Database searches were compared by mathematical implication, not title. Residual priority risk: another source may have derived the truncation-to-transport envelope using flat/transport duality, perhaps yielding this formula implicitly or explicitly. No independent novelty certification is asserted.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The finite ultrametric family is a standard multiscale setting for probability comparison; Dudley norms are used to metrize weak convergence. The statement replaces a general-purpose coupled linear program with an exact \(O(|X|\log|X|)\) hierarchical formula and a constructive norming function. The necessity of each possible cluster height and the sharp between-cluster mass-imbalance threshold are structural, parameter-complete consequences, not arbitrary isolated computations.

## Scientific scope

Only finite ultrametrics and zero-total-mass real signed measures are covered. The published equal-arm star identity covers the one-height equilateral leaf-only specialization, but not the multilevel formula. The classical ultrametric \(W_1\) formula and linear-programming methods are credited. The finite checker is supplementary, and independent validation is outstanding.
