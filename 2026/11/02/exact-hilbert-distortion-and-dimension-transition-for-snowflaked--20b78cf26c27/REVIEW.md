# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For every integer \(k\ge2\), every tuple \(m_i\ge1\), and every \(0\le\alpha\le1\), the proof gives a weighted-variance lower bound for every noncontracting real Hilbert embedding and an explicit regular-simplex realization attaining it. The positive weights are defined everywhere, including singleton blocks and the two-block path boundary. Rank, congruence, the strict snowflake-threshold monotonicity, and the dimension transition follow from independently written equality and quadratic-form arguments. The package's exact-rational checker passed 585 finite cases and 72,300 assertions. Finite tests supplement rather than replace the proof; arbitrary weighted graphs and exponents outside the claimed interval are not covered.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The nearest directly inspected embedding theorem is Robertson's 2023 complete-bipartite and Hamming-cube theorem. It does not imply the formula for a common hub joining multiple cliques or the variable-size phase transition. Lou–Obata–Huang calculate the quadratic embedding constant of friendship graphs; their invariant uses the unsquared distance matrix and does not settle least Euclidean distortion of the original metric or the continuous snowflake range. Choudhury–Nandi and Baskoro–Obata focus on the related quadratic embedding constant rather than the new least-distortion invariant. The accessible prior claim on two equilateral clusters has no common hub and does not imply the new hub-radius obstruction. Targeted searches of the public mathematical record and research index did not identify a dominating theorem or exact matching table. This is evidence of noncoverage, not proof of global novelty; alternative Euclidean-distance-matrix terminology remains a historical-priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The theorem determines a natural nonlinear Banach-space embedding invariant for every clique bouquet with arbitrary block sizes, not an arbitrary numerical slice. The exact formula yields a complete snowflake-isometry threshold, a harmonic block-size law, uniqueness of all normalized optimizers, and a sharp drop of one in the least affine target dimension. Friendship graphs, stars, and nonuniform clique bouquets are included. The simultaneous distortion, rigidity and dimension classification makes the result a reusable example for distorted negative-type geometry. No extrapolated general theorem for all block graphs is claimed.
