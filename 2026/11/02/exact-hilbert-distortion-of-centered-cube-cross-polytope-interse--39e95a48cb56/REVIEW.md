# Scientific review: centered cube–cross-polytope sections

## Correctness — PASS
Permutation averaging preserves any Hilbertian upper/lower norm sandwich, and the only permutation-invariant quadratic form on the coordinate-sum-zero hyperplane is Euclidean up to scale. Exact dual calculations yield both coordinate-norm maxima on its Euclidean sphere. The positive/negative mass split gives an exact Euclidean circumradius, with explicit signed-coordinate extremizers; the derivative argument verifies the unique optimal parameter for all dimensions at least four, including boundaries and the saturated regime. Pure rational checker tests reproduce the component identities and grid bounds. Those finite tests do not replace the unrestricted proof.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority qualification
The full-text primary inspections cover the introductions, definitions and main results of Vasil'eva (2023, arXiv:2303.13147) and Kobos–Varivoda (2023, arXiv:2305.06427). The first treats Kolmogorov widths of ball intersections, not the sharp Hilbert Banach–Mazur distance of the zero-sum section; the second treats selected small-dimensional cube–cross-polytope Banach–Mazur comparisons. Additional published mathematical claims concerning quadratic \(\ell_1\)–\(\ell_\infty\) mixtures, pure \(\ell_4\) zero-sum sections, and Mahler products of cube–cross-polytope intersections have different conclusions and do not logically determine the present formula. Results in earlier terminology may have been missed; independent novelty assessment has not been undertaken.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A parameter-complete exact Banach–Mazur distortion law for the natural codimension-one, mass-conserving section of an intersection of norm balls goes beyond the usual finite-dimensional extremal comparison estimates. It gives all sharp radius transitions, a unique optimally Hilbertian interpolation scale in every dimension at least four, and the dimension-growth law \(n^{1/4}\). The zero-sum constraint makes the optimal signed sparsity and parity behavior genuinely different from full-coordinate-space intersections. This is a finite-dimensional quantitative theorem, not a result about arbitrary ball intersections.

## Residual limitations
No assertion is made about weighted coordinate bounds, other hyperplanes, complex scalar fields, or classification of all minimizing linear maps. Published sources were examined for their actual proved invariants, but an earlier equivalent identity outside the inspected literature remains possible. Computational checks are not independent or exhaustive infinite-dimensional certification.
