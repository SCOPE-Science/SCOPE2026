# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For the unit cube with source at a face center and uniform normalized surface area, the distance-squared formulas on top, side and opposite faces are proven by an exhaustive set of unfolded simple face sequences. Every possible route from the source face to a side or opposite face is represented; the square-corner checks involve exact fractions and imply an inequality on the entire continuum because the difference of squared distances is affine. The canonical candidates are real surface paths. The circular-cap integrations yield the full stated CDF; rational-polynomial integration gives all even moments, and a polar expansion gives the two leading tail terms. The checker was executed successfully with exact face-route counts 28 and 26, moments through order eight, and numerical CDF corroboration. Standard convex-polyhedron path straightening/non-repetition is the only structural background lemma.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
The closest primary paper is Goldstone–Roca–Suzzi Valli (2020), whose Section 4.3 **already gives** the four three-face opposite-face center routes; no originality is assigned to those routes or to the squared-distance expression. Its probability calculation instead measures four-face shortest-path occurrence with *variable* source points on opposite faces. Davis–Dods–Traub–Yang (2015) handle geodesic trajectories involving vertices; Davis–Guo (2023) and Davis (2023) describe cut loci and motion-planning complexity. Inspected primary full texts do not state the fixed-center all-surface CDF, exact moment sequence or two-term upper-tail law. Mathai et al.'s 1999 distance distributions concern ambient Euclidean distances, not intrinsic surface paths. Yang's 2023 paper has only been checked at the abstract level; its stated subject is geodesic-flow equidistribution, not a shortest-distance random variable. Targeted public and research-record searches returned unrelated cube distributions. Historical priority remains unconfirmed, with the risk that a prior integration of the known unfolding formula was published under another heading.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The cumulative area of intrinsic balls centered at a highly symmetric point is a natural metric-measure invariant of a canonical convex polyhedral surface. A full evaluated law (not a numerical plot), all rational even moments, the exact worst-case radius and its second-order high-distance tail provide precise tests for stochastic polyhedral-geodesic computations. The distribution requires combining three geometrically different face-area regimes, including the bottom-face Voronoi switch, and is not a mere renaming of a known source-target distance formula. No claim is made about general source points, or novelty of the individual shortest paths.
