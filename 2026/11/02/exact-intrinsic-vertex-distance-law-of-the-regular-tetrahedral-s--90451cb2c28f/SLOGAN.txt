For a fixed vertex and a uniformly random point on the surface of a unit regular tetrahedron, the exact intrinsic shortest-distance law is quadratic up to distance \(1\) and then follows an elementary inverse-cosine branch until the sharp radius \(2/\sqrt3\). Its mean is \(2\sqrt3/9+\sqrt3\log3/6\), its second moment is \(5/9\), and its two transitions have sharp \(3/2\)-power and quadratic area deficits.
