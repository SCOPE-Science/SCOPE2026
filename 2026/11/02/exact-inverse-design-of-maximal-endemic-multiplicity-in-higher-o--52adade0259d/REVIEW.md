# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The hypothesis uses fixed finite interaction order \(M\ge2\), strictly positive rates \(s_j\), unit recovery, and an entire ordered list of \(M\) distinct endemic fractions in \((0,1)\). Evaluation at prevalence one makes the root polynomial's normalization unique; coefficient division gives the explicit rates. The penultimate coefficient enforces the necessary sum bound, and the alternating elementary-symmetric estimate under the sum bound proves every rate is strictly positive. Root derivatives give alternating stability; the supplied exact-rational program checks finite samples, not the universal quantifiers.

## Originality

PASS with residual priority uncertainty. The September 2023 primary source develops the arbitrary-order polynomial and analyzes low orders, and its discussion anticipates increasing multiplicity. A 2025 full-text paper explicitly notes the possible maximal *number* of positive roots. Neither inspected source provides the full inverse theorem for **every specified ordered tuple** of maximal endemic fractions, the exact necessary-and-sufficient inequality on their sum, or unique recovery of each positive infection rate. This is strictly stronger than existence of some maximal-root instance. Literature and research-index queries found no equivalent prescribed-root theorem; absence of search hits does not establish global novelty.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The natural modeling gap is whether multiple desired endemic levels can be implemented using physically admissible positive pairwise and group-contagion rates. The criterion provides an exact attainable region in prevalence space, an explicit design, and a sharp obstruction for impossible targets. This is more informative than a count of roots or a selected example. It does not purport to solve parameter estimation, arbitrary graph dynamics, or finite stochastic extinction.
