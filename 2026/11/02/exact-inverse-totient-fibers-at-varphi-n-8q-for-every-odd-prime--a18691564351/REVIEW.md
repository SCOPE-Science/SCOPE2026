# Scientific review of the inverse-totient \(8q\) classification

## Correctness

PASS. For primes \(q\ge7\), the factorization of \(8q\) restricts all prime divisors of an inverse-totient preimage, while the prohibition of repeated odd factors follows from divisibility of prime-power totients. Comparing exact \(q\)-adic and two-adic valuations yields precisely one mixed prime and the three complete multiplier sets. The mod-three dichotomy eliminates the appropriate candidates. The separately bounded exceptional cases \(q=3,5\) are finite and match the previously published table. An independent totient sieve enumerates every preimage for 302 primes through \(q=1999\), with a proved enumeration bound. No experimental inference is used to establish the unrestricted theorem.

## Originality

PASS on the inspected record, with historical priority risk. The 2025 paper of Shannon, Shiue, He and Saito classifies a related \(4q\) family under a residue restriction and tabulates the exceptional values \(24,40\); it does not state this all-prime \(8q\) formula. He's 2026 follow-up gives \(4q\) fully, explicitly leaves \(2^sq\) at \(s\ge3\) open, and lists selected \(8q\) cardinalities without a family formula. The OEIS inverse-totient table supplies examples rather than a symbolic classification. Searches under inverse-totient, Euler-phi preimage, cardinality, and Carmichael terminology identified no covering all-prime formula. Existing results already rule out a singleton when the totient value is \(8q\); no novelty is asserted for that implication alone. Generic inversion algorithms could imply individual cases after further analysis, and an earlier complete parameterized formula may remain unindexed.

## Value

PASS. The initial exponent-three slice of the structured family \(\varphi^{-1}(2^sq)\) is an explicit motivated boundary: prior recent treatments stop at exponent two and observe nonuniform behavior at exponent three. The result determines the entire infinite family of fibers, detects the simultaneous-primality branch responsible for six preimages, and gives the precise multiplicity spectrum. This is a complete natural inverse-image classification, not merely a table extension or the already-known absence of singleton fibers.

## Scope and risk

The result concerns exactly exponent three of the power of two, with all odd primes including the two previously tabulated small exceptions. It makes no claim about higher exponents or prime-density asymptotics. Priority is based on available full-text comparisons and targeted record searches, not an exhaustive historical survey. Independent verification has not been performed.
