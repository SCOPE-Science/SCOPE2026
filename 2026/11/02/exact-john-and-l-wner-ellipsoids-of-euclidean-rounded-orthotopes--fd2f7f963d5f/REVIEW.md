# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof specifies all parameters \(d\ge3\), \(r>0\), and \(a_i>0\). The inner problem follows from an explicit Minkowski decomposition, coordinate projection bounds and the equality case of Hadamard's inequality. The outer parameter is uniquely normalized by strict concavity and a monotone scalar equation. Its enclosure identity has positive quadratic remainder coefficients \(a_i z_i>0\); the exact set of contacts is established rather than inferred from sampling. The all-orientation, all-center optimality certificate is an averaged inequality over every signed corner followed by matrix arithmetic–geometric mean. All asymptotic limits hold with fixed positive side lengths. The standalone numerical checker passed in five dimensions across 150 independent configurations; finite tests do not establish the theorem on their own.

**Originality — PASS (provisional historical comparison).** Todd (2007) and Henk (2012) establish the general enclosing-ellipsoid/contact framework. The known capsule case has only one segment direction and continuously many outer contact points, not \(d\) independent positive half-lengths with isolated signed corners. The directly relevant Courteaux–Ramlot–Lambert–Van Wallendael (2025) full text, particularly §§2–4, treats Minkowski ellipsoid–box sums but constructs two bounding boxes and compares numerical families of outer ellipsoids, rather than deriving the unrestricted exact minimum-volume enclosing ellipsoid of a ball plus an orthotope. The general Seeger (1993) ellipsoid calculus is related; its abstract was available, but its full text was not established accessible for inspection. The completed database and prior-record comparisons identified no statement implying this exact all-parameter result. This is a self-review and not independent historical priority certification.

**Value — PASS.** Rounded rectangular solids are canonical parallel bodies and arise as exact ball–box Minkowski sums in geometric modeling. The result determines *both* natural extremal ellipsoids (including arbitrary competing centers and orientations), reduces the outer problem to one strictly monotone scalar root instead of high-dimensional optimization, and identifies every contact point and the exact volume ratio. This structural finite-design classification for arbitrary positive anisotropic half-lengths differs from a routine single-parameter specialization.

**Residual limitations.** Exact global formula requires Euclidean ball and mutually perpendicular box axes. The paper does not extend complete contact-set assertions to degenerate boxes, and unrelated historical priority may remain unknown.
