The regular icosahedron has a fully elementary joint law for the number of sides in a Haar-random central section and its **same-normal** width, obtained by subtracting two concentric spherical-pentagon cap areas. Small normalized widths force twelve-sided sections, large widths force ten-sided sections, and the two types coexist only between the sharp golden-ratio thresholds \(\sqrt{(1+1/\sqrt5)/2}\) and \(2/\sqrt5\).
