# Exact leaf sensitivity and positivity loss for quadratic least-squares splines on a metric star

## Finding
For each integer \(k\geq1\), form a metric star from \(k\) copies of \([0,1]\), joined only at their \(0\)-endpoints, with unit arclength measure. Let \(P_k\) be real \(L^2\)-orthogonal projection onto edgewise polynomials of degree at most two whose values agree at the common hub. Fix any outer endpoint \(v\) and define its sharp boundary amplification
\[
E_k=\sup_{\|f\|_{L^\infty}\leq1}|(P_k f)(v)|.
\]
Then, with \(t=1/k\),
\[
\boxed{E_k=1+\frac{4\sqrt6}{25}\left(1-t+\frac{(t^2+2t+6)^{3/2}}{(t+2)^2}\right).}
\]
The supremum is attained by a bounded measurable sign function. The hub evaluation norm is independent of \(k\), equal to \(1+12\sqrt6/25=E_1\). The endpoint amplification grows strictly with branching: \(E_1<E_2<E_3<\cdots\), and
\[
\boxed{\lim_{k\to\infty}E_k=\frac{61+4\sqrt6}{25},\qquad
\lim_{k\to\infty}k\left(\frac{61+4\sqrt6}{25}-E_k\right)=\frac{18+4\sqrt6}{25}.}
\]
There is a sharp branching threshold \(E_k>5/2\) if and only if \(k\geq3\). For inputs \(0\leq f\leq1\) almost everywhere, the exact maximal *negative* outer-endpoint excursion is
\[
\boxed{\sup_{0\leq f\leq1}[-(P_k f)(v)]=\frac{E_k-1}{2}.}
\]
In particular, the limiting negative excursion is \((18+2\sqrt6)/25\). These are **point-evaluation** constants, not a determination of the global operator norm \(\|P_k\|_{L^\infty\to L^\infty}\).

## Assumptions and scope
The domain is the disjoint union of the interiors of \(k\) unit edges with the left endpoint identified, endowed with the sum of Lebesgue edge measures. Identifying endpoints does not change the measure. An input is a real essentially bounded measurable function on the edges, with no continuity requirement. Every projected output has a unique continuous representative. The projection space has dimension \(2k+1\) and requires **value continuity only** at the hub, with no derivative matching or Kirchhoff condition. The endpoint \(v\) is any one of the \(k\) free leaf vertices. All assertions quantify over every integer \(k\geq1\).

## Proof
Let \(W_0=\operatorname{span}\{x,x^2\}\) on one edge. Its \(L^2(0,1)\) Gram matrix and inverse are
\[
G=\begin{pmatrix}1/3&1/4\\1/4&1/5\end{pmatrix},\qquad
G^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}48&-60\\-60&80\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its reproducing kernel is therefore
\[
K_0(x,y)=48xy-60(xy^2+x^2y)+80x^2y^2.
\]
The unique quadratic with value one at the hub and orthogonal to \(W_0\) is
\[
q(x)=1-4x+\tfrac{10}{3}x^2,\qquad
\int_0^1q(x)^2\,dx=\tfrac19.
\]
Indeed, both first and second moments of \(q\) vanish. The global projection space decomposes **orthogonally** into the \(k\) edge-supported copies of \(W_0\) and the one-dimensional space generated by the function equal to \(q\) on every edge. The latter has squared norm \(k/9\). Thus, for edge indices \(i,j\), the exact reproducing kernel is
\[
K_k((i,x),(j,y))=\mathbf1_{i=j}K_0(x,y)+\frac9kq(x)q(y).
\]
At the tip \(x=1\) of edge \(i\), use \(q(1)=1/3\) and \(t=1/k\). The kernel on edge \(i\) becomes the quadratic
\[
p_t(y)=10(2+t)y^2-12(1+t)y+3t,
\]
and on each of the other \(k-1\) edges it equals \(3tq(y)\).

Direct integration between the two roots \((6\pm\sqrt6)/10\) of \(q\) gives
\[
Q=\int_0^1|q(y)|\,dy=\frac19+\frac{4\sqrt6}{75}.
\]
The polynomial \(p_t\) is positive at \(0\) and \(1\) and negative at \(1/2\) for every \(0<t\leq1\). It thus has exactly two roots in \((0,1)\). Its leading coefficient, discriminant, and ordinary integral are
\[
A=10(2+t),\quad \Delta=24(t^2+2t+6),\quad
\int_0^1p_t(y)\,dy=\frac23+\frac t3.
\]
For an upward quadratic with both roots in \((0,1)\), twice the magnitude of its negative signed area is \(\Delta^{3/2}/(3A^2)\). Consequently,
\[
\int_0^1|p_t(y)|\,dy=\frac23+\frac t3+\frac{\Delta^{3/2}}{3A^2}.
\]
The norm of the leaf evaluation functional on \(L^\infty\) is its kernel's \(L^1\) norm,
\[
E_k=\int_0^1|p_t(y)|\,dy+3(1-t)Q,
\]
which simplifies to the stated formula. Equality is realized by \(f=\operatorname{sgn}K_k(v,\cdot)\), with arbitrary values at the finite zero set. Hub evaluation has kernel \((9/k)q(y)\) on every edge, so its norm is \(9Q=1+12\sqrt6/25\).

Set \(h(t)=(t^2+2t+6)^{3/2}/(t+2)^2\). Differentiating exactly,
\[
h'(t)=\frac{(t-1)(t+6)\sqrt{t^2+2t+6}}{(t+2)^3}\leq0\qquad(0<t\leq1).
\]
Thus \(1-t+h(t)\) strictly decreases in \(t\), proving strict increase of \(E_k\) with \(k\). Substituting \(t=0\) and taking the first derivative there yield both limits. Direct algebraic comparison gives \(E_2<5/2<E_3\), so monotonicity proves the exact threshold.

Finally, \(P_k\) reproduces the constant function one. Hence the leaf kernel has signed integral one and total absolute integral \(E_k\). Its negative part has integral \((E_k-1)/2\). For \(0\leq f\leq1\), the smallest possible value of \((P_kf)(v)\) is obtained by taking \(f=1\) precisely where the leaf kernel is negative and \(f=0\) elsewhere. This proves the sharp positivity-loss formula.

## Relationship to prior work
Classical interval spline work by Shadrin proves uniform \(L^\infty\)-boundedness of \(L^2\) projectors for polynomial splines of fixed order, including reduced continuity at knots. In the two-edge case, the present space can be identified with a single interval carrying a value-continuity quadratic knot, but the qualitative boundedness theorem does not state the exact leaf evaluation constant, its branching law, or its positivity defect. Kayumov studies orthogonal projectors for **second-order differential-operator** splines (analogous to linear polynomial splines), not the degree-two graph space defined here. Arioli and Benzi develop **linear** finite elements and associated mass matrices for metric-graph differential equations rather than sharp quadratic least-squares endpoint sensitivities. Closely related previously recorded results on **affine** metric-graph projection do not imply this quadratic kernel, since the polynomial degree and Gram matrix differ.

## Limitations
The result is about leaf and hub evaluations on an equilateral star with constant arclength density and one degree-two polynomial element per edge. It does not assert that a leaf is a global norming point, nor that the displayed \(E_k\) is the full \(L^\infty\)-operator norm. Unequal lengths, variable weights, subdivided edges, higher polynomial degrees, and graph topologies beyond a star are not covered. The extremizing sign functions may be discontinuous; the same suprema are approached by continuous inputs using approximation in the kernel-weighted \(L^1\) norm. Earlier equivalent calculations in unindexed specialized graph-spline literature remain a publication-priority uncertainty.
