# Mathematical review

## Correctness
PASS. The quadratic Gram inverse is explicitly reconstructed, and a common-hub polynomial orthogonal to both edgewise monomials gives the exact kernel. The leaf kernel reduces to a quadratic on the evaluated arm and a rescaled quadratic on every other arm. Its sign pattern and discriminant give an exact absolute integral. Differentiation proves monotonicity, the branch threshold follows from two exact algebraic comparisons, and reproduction of constants gives the sharp positivity defect. The standalone checker passes symbolic identities plus 291 independent finite Gram/kernel/root tests. The claim carefully excludes the unproved identification of this evaluation norm with the full projection norm.

## Originality
PASS with residual priority risk. Shadrin's interval theorem provides a broad boundedness result but not the exact branching-indexed endpoint law; its linear-interval case is not identical to a star of at least three edges. Kayumov's second-order L-splines are generated by order-two differential equations, not arbitrary edgewise quadratics; this difference in spline order and continuity is substantive. Arioli–Benzi consider linear finite elements for graph PDEs and do not supply the present quadratic reproducing kernel or sharp leaf positivity defect. Published nearby calculations on affine least-squares projection and other finite graph evaluation problems have different spaces and invariants. Unindexed older graph-spline computations have not been ruled out categorically.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The boundary evaluation functional controls worst-case amplification in a branched least-squares reconstruction, and its negative kernel mass controls how badly nonnegative observations can be turned negative at a leaf. Both quantities are natural and sharp, not arbitrarily chosen finite parameters. The formula settles an infinite family, proves strict and quantified branching sensitivity, identifies the first branch count crossing a meaningful amplification threshold, and gives an exact nonzero limiting positivity defect. It does not rely on speculative downstream theorems.
