# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** For the explicitly defined semi-infinite discrete Hamiltonian, the compact mode and its normalization follow from the three-periodic recurrence at the band energy. Exact elimination of the background and pendant yields a secular determinant without division by a vanishing reference-state factor. Its simple zero supplies a unique nearby outgoing resonance for fixed background hopping strictly above threshold, and direct second-order coefficient extraction proves the linewidth. At threshold, the same determinant in a square-root local coordinate yields a directional fifth-halves imaginary term and, for the opposite sign, a physical below-band eigenvalue. The companion standalone script checks all nonstandard recurrence and coefficient identities, plus independent complex-root diagnostics. Its finite numerical checks are not presented as proofs of infinite-parameter statements.

**Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, with residual priority risk.** The focal 2013 paper constructs the exact compact modes, but its inspected original presentation does not give the new local resonance-pole expansion and its attachment-distance prefactor. Longhi 2006 studies single-site Fano–Anderson continuum bound states; Molina et al. 2011 treat engineered surface states; Flach et al. 2013 discuss flat-band detangling and perturbations. These works share interference and continuum vocabulary but do not, in the material inspected, imply the exact coefficient or the direction-dependent threshold law for this triangular pendant geometry. Semantic database checks of aliases and rates, and the accessible local research history, found no dominating statement. Older scattering calculations using other parameterizations could still be equivalent; this remains a nonzero priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** Coupling mismatch is the most direct imperfection of the interference-produced compact mode. The exact distance-dependent loss coefficient quantitatively ranks how strongly different compact surface states leak, rather than merely indicating that they become resonances. At the band threshold it changes the power law and distinguishes leaky and genuinely bound perturbations, a motivated physical boundary classification. The coefficients and the edge exponent are exact, nontrivial and reusable when evaluating finite-array trapping robustness.

The theorem is local in perturbation, uses a semi-infinite lossless nearest-neighbor array, and does not claim uniform-in-distance asymptotics or independent external review. Primary literature priority is provisional.
