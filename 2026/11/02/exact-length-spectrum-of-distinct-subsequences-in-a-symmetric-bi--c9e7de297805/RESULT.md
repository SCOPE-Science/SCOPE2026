# Exact length spectrum of distinct subsequences in a symmetric binary Markov source

## Finding

Let \(X_1,X_2,\ldots\) be a binary stationary Markov chain, with uniform initial bit and probability \(r\in[0,1]\) that any consecutive bits differ. For each \(n\geq0\) and \(k\geq1\), let \(D_{n,k}\) be the number of **distinct** binary length-\(k\) scattered subsequences of \(X_1\cdots X_n\), counting a string once regardless of its number of occurrences. Put \(Q_n(t)=\sum_{k\geq1}\mathbb E[D_{n,k}]t^k\) and \(Q_0(t)=0\). The full two-variable generating function is

\[
\boxed{\sum_{n\geq0}Q_n(t)z^n=
\frac{tz\,[1-(1-2r)z]}{(1-z)\,[1-(1-r+t)z+t(1-2r)z^2]}.}
\]

This determines the complete expectation profile by both host length \(n\) and scattered-subsequence length \(k\) for every \(r\in[0,1]\). The corresponding expected *total* number of nonempty distinct subsequences is \(Q_n(1)\). Its exponential growth rate is

\[
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}(Q_n(1))^{1/n}=\lambda(r)=1-\frac r2+\frac12\sqrt{r(r+4)}.}
\]

The rate is strictly increasing for \(0<r\leq1\), from \(\lambda(0)=1\), through \(\lambda(1/2)=3/2\), to \(\lambda(1)=(1+\sqrt5)/2\). For \(r=0\), the deterministic constant-string case has \(Q_n(t)=t+\cdots+t^n\) for \(n\geq1\).

## Assumptions and scope

A scattered subsequence of a finite binary word is obtained by deleting arbitrary positions while retaining order; it is not a contiguous substring. The random word is generated by choosing \(X_1=0\) or \(1\) with probability \(1/2\), then independently at each step switching the current bit with probability \(r\), or staying with probability \(1-r\). Thus the transition matrix is \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1-r&r\\r&1-r\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\). Each possible subsequence *word*, not embedding, is counted once. Polynomial \(Q_n\) has degree at most \(n\). For \(n=0\) there are no nonempty scattered subsequences.

## Proof

For a deterministic prefix \(w\), let \(F_0(w;t)\) and \(F_1(w;t)\) count distinct nonempty scattered subsequences ending in the indicated letter, weighted by \(t^{\text{length}}\). Appending bit \(0\) replaces \(F_0\) by \(t(1+F_0+F_1)\) and leaves \(F_1\) unchanged; appending bit \(1\) does the symmetric operation. Indeed, appending the new last symbol to each distinct old subsequence (including the empty word) is an injective correspondence to *all* subsequences ending in that bit in the extended host: every such subsequence either was already present before, in which case it can be formed this way using its prefix, or uses the new symbol. The correspondence is on resulting subsequence strings, not occurrence positions.

Define \(U_n(t)=\mathbb E[F_0(X_1\cdots X_n;t)\,\mathbf1_{\{X_n=0\}}]\) and \(V_n(t)=\mathbb E[F_1(X_1\cdots X_n;t)\,\mathbf1_{\{X_n=0\}}]\). Invariance of the stationary source under complementing both symbols gives \(\mathbb E[F_0\mathbf1_{\{X_n=1\}}]=V_n\) and \(\mathbb E[F_1\mathbf1_{\{X_n=1\}}]=U_n\). Conditional on the last bit, the preceding deterministic update therefore yields

\[
U_{n+1}=t(\tfrac12+U_n+V_n),\qquad
V_{n+1}=rU_n+(1-r)V_n,\qquad (U_1,V_1)=(t/2,0).
\]

In particular, \(Q_n(t)=2(U_n+V_n)\). With \(B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}t&t\\r&1-r\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and \(b=(t/2,0)^T\), we have \((U_n,V_n)^T=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}B^jb\) for \(n\ge1\). Formal matrix summation (valid coefficientwise in \(z\) for every fixed \(r\)) gives

\[
\sum_{n\ge0}Q_n(t)z^n=\frac{2z}{1-z}(1,1)(I-zB)^{-1}b.
\]

Since \(\det(I-zB)=1-(1-r+t)z+t(1-2r)z^2\), direct multiplication gives the displayed rational expression. Equivalently, \(Q_0=0\), \(Q_1=t\), \(Q_2=(1+r)t+t^2\), and for all \(n\ge3\),

\[
Q_n=(2-r+t)Q_{n-1}-[1-r+2(1-r)t]Q_{n-2}+t(1-2r)Q_{n-3}.
\]

For \(r>0\) the matrix \(B\) at \(t=1\) is nonnegative, irreducible, and primitive (including \(r=1\)), and its positive Perron root is \(1-r/2+\sqrt{r(r+4)}/2\). Because \(b\) and the observation vector are nonzero and nonnegative, Perron–Frobenius theory gives the asserted total-count exponential growth. At \(r=0\), the sequence is a constant word and has exactly \(n\) distinct nonempty subsequences, giving growth rate one. Differentiation yields \(\lambda'(r)=[(r+2)/\sqrt{r(r+4)}-1]/2>0\) for \(r>0\), using \((r+2)^2-r(r+4)=4\).

## Verification

The included `verify_markov_length.py` uses exact rational arithmetic. Independently of the proof and of the two-state expectation recurrence, it explicitly enumerates all binary hosts through length ten, weights each by \(\frac12r^{\text{switches}}(1-r)^{\text{stays}}\), constructs the **set** of distinct scattered subsequences, groups them by length, and compares every coefficient with a direct expansion of the rational function. It tests \(r\in\{0,1/4,1/2,3/4,1\}\); 10,230 host strings and 275 expected length coefficients are covered. The fair-i.i.d. total \(2(3/2)^n-2\) and the constant-string boundary are also checked. These finite tests corroborate, but do not replace, the all-parameter algebraic proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Biers-Ariel, Godbole, and Kelley (arXiv:1704.08661, first circulated 2017) studied expected distinct-subsequence counts in binary random words and gave a four-variable matrix recurrence for the **unrefined total** under a two-state Markov chain (Section 3.2). Their paper does not give the present length-marked generating function or the explicit rate under the symmetric Markov interpolation. The length marking and complement-symmetric two-state closure here are different from computing just the overall expectation. Collins (arXiv:1310.7288) treats the independent fair binary source, which is recovered at \(r=1/2\). Flaxman, Harrow, and Sorkin (2004) determine the deterministic alternating word's extremal distinct-subsequence count, corresponding to the limit \(r=1\) of the present family. These are related benchmarks, not independent certifications of the claimed result.

## Limitations

The rational expression concerns **expected** counts, not the distribution or concentration of the random counts. It is limited to a stationary **symmetric binary** Markov chain; it does not assert an equivalent two-variable formula for general asymmetric or higher-order chains. The growth-rate assertion concerns the total expected count, not an almost-sure sample-path growth rate. Prior-work searches may have missed an equivalent length-refined Markov calculation; independent audit is not yet performed.
