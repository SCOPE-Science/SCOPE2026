# Exact liquidity thresholds for two-size envy-free transfers

## Finding

Fix an integer \(n\ge 1\), an integer \(r\ge 2\), and nonnegative integers \(m,k\). There are \(m\) indivisible goods of value \(1\) and \(k\) indivisible goods of value \(r\). All \(n\) agents have the **same** additive valuation, and agent \(i\) can make a net payment of at most \(B_i\ge0\) monetary units. Monetary transfers are internal and budget-balanced; allocations are complete.

Write \(W=m+rk\), \(\mu=W/n\), and
\[
C_i=\lfloor\mu+B_i\rfloor.
\]
A complete envy-free allocation with budget-balanced payments respecting every individual budget exists **if and only if**
\[
\boxed{\sum_{i=1}^n C_i\ge W\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^n\left\lfloor\frac{C_i}{r}\right\rfloor\ge k.}
\]
Whenever it exists, the allocation and its necessarily unique net-payment vector can be constructed by two greedy passes. The feasibility decision takes \(O(n)\) arithmetic operations when the two item multiplicities are supplied in binary (plus the bit complexity of the input numbers).

For a **common** individual budget \(B_i=B\), the sharp minimum budget is
\[
\boxed{B_{\min}=\max\!\left\{\left\lceil\frac Wn\right\rceil,
\;r\left\lceil\frac kn\right\rceil\right\}-\frac Wn.}
\]
The formula is valid when \(m=0\), \(k=0\), or \(n=1\). Scaling all goods to values \(a\) and \(ra\) with \(a>0\) replaces the capacity by \(\lfloor W/n+B_i/a\rfloor\), where \(W=m+rk\) is measured in units of \(a\), and multiplies the uniform threshold by \(a\).

## Assumptions and scope

There is no outside subsidy. A signed payment \(p_i\) is the net monetary amount **received** by agent \(i\); a negative amount is paid. Thus \(\sum_i p_i=0\), \(p_i\ge -B_i\), and a bundle worth \(w_i\) gives effective utility \(w_i+p_i\). Full envy-freeness compares every agent's adjusted utility against every other agent's bundle and payment. All goods are allocated, and goods are indivisible but distinguishable even when equal-valued. There is no individual rationality or exogenous entitlement restriction beyond the stated budgets. The budget input may be any exact nonnegative real vector; computational assertions presume exact representations supporting comparison and integer-floor operations.

## Proof

**1. Transfers are forced.** Because all agents value every bundle identically, envy-freeness in both directions between \(i\) and \(j\) gives \(w_i+p_i=w_j+p_j\). Budget balance and \(\sum_i w_i=W\) force \(w_i+p_i=\mu\) for every \(i\); hence \(p_i=\mu-w_i\), uniquely. The individual budget constraint is exactly \(w_i\le\mu+B_i\). As each \(w_i\) is an integer, this is equivalent to \(w_i\le C_i\).

**2. Necessary inequalities.** Let \(x_i\) and \(y_i\) be agent \(i\)'s counts of unit and \(r\)-valued goods. Then \(x_i+r y_i\le C_i\). Summing these inequalities gives \(W\le\sum_i C_i\). Since \(y_i\le\lfloor C_i/r\rfloor\), summation also gives \(k\le\sum_i\lfloor C_i/r\rfloor\).

**3. Sufficiency and construction.** Under both displayed inequalities, distribute the \(k\) large goods sequentially, giving agent \(i\) up to \(\lfloor C_i/r\rfloor\) of them, until all are allocated. Let these counts be \(y_i\). Each residual integer capacity is \(R_i=C_i-r y_i\ge0\), and
\[
\sum_i R_i=\sum_i C_i-rk\ge m.
\]
Distribute the \(m\) unit goods greedily into these residual capacities, yielding counts \(x_i\). All \(m+k\) goods are allocated and each load \(w_i=x_i+r y_i\le C_i\). Define \(p_i=\mu-w_i\). Then \(\sum_i p_i=0\), every \(p_i\ge-B_i\), and every adjusted utility \(w_i+p_i=\mu\), proving envy-freeness. The two passes need only count arithmetic, rather than enumerating individual goods.

**4. Exact common-budget frontier.** If all budgets equal \(B\), all capacities equal \(C=\lfloor\mu+B\rfloor\). The theorem's conditions become \(nC\ge W\) and \(n\lfloor C/r\rfloor\ge k\). Since \(C\) is an integer, these are equivalent to \(C\ge\lceil W/n\rceil\) and \(C\ge r\lceil k/n\rceil\). The smallest admissible \(C\) is their maximum. Consequently the smallest admissible \(B\) is precisely the asserted \(B_{\min}\).

**5. Why the unit denomination matters.** The two-capacity test does not extend by simply treating the smaller item as a filler of value \(2\): three agents of budget zero with goods worth \(2,2,2,3\) have total value \(9\), equal average value \(3\), and integer capacities \((3,3,3)\). Total capacity is adequate and one value-\(3\) good individually fits, yet all four goods require distinct bins because every pair of goods has value above \(3\). Thus no envy-free allocation with zero payments exists. The unit-value filler is the exact ingredient used in Step 3.

## Verification

The standalone `verify.py` runs an independent, exact-rational exhaustive check of the stated feasibility inequalities against all load vectors generated by allocating the complete finite test set of goods. It covers \(40{,}800\) rational-budget parameter profiles, with \(5{,}069{,}616\) load-vector comparisons and \(26{,}145\) successful constructive allocations, and also checks the unique balanced payment vector. Its initial run returned `VERIFY_OK`, along with the explicit size-\(2,3\) boundary check. These computations are finite corroboration; the proof above covers every finite \(n,m,k\), every integer \(r\ge2\), and every nonnegative budget vector.

## Relationship to prior work

Bredereck, Deltl, Inamdar, Jain, and Pandey formulate envy-free allocation with internal payments and individual payment caps (EFA-IP). Their general work develops an LP for payment feasibility with a fixed allocation, type-based algorithms for identical valuations, and a polynomial algorithm for **one good type with identical budgets**. Those results supply the model and related algorithmic coverage, not the displayed two-scalar necessary-and-sufficient conditions for arbitrary budgets in the two-denomination indivisible-goods domain, nor the exact uniform-budget frontier. The distinguishing conclusion here is a closed-form **liquidity region**, not merely decidability or fixed-parameter tractability. The constructive step is a divisible-size packing lemma; its possible appearance in differently worded packing literature is an originality risk.

## Limitations

This characterization assumes identical additive valuations and exactly two positive denominations \(\{1,r\}\) with integral ratio, with no assignment restrictions and no restrictions on the form of bilateral transfers beyond balanced net amounts. It does not characterize arbitrary values, heterogeneous valuations, nonintegral denomination ratios, or nonlinear utilities. The main scientific comparison uses the original authors' abstract and publicly indexed excerpts of their workshop manuscript. Direct full-text retrieval was unavailable during the check, so an unlocated equivalent statement in that manuscript or other packing literature remains a publication-priority risk. The statement is analytically proved but has not undergone independent review.
