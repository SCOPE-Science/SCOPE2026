# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The forbidden-swap count is a direct count of directed permutation-cycle arcs with exactly one duplication per two-cycle. The labelled exponential formula marks two-cycles and gives the complete degree polynomial. A cycle-splitting and -merging reduction converts every configuration to two- and three-cycle normal form. Connectivity then follows from the exhaustively checked four- and five-letter local switch graphs. The n=4 isometry statement is a nine-state bipartite-neighborhood argument; the n>=5 nonisometry has two explicit four-switch paths and a three-cycle fixed-point obstruction to two switches. Both supplied programs regenerate n=3,...,8 and validate the critical local lemmas and witnesses; neither program is used as proof of an unbounded case.

## Originality
PASS on the accessible evidence, with unresolved search risk. Dyer, Jerrum and Müller formalize the perfect-matching switch chain and give results about hereditary graph classes and mixing, without the crown-family degree polynomial or the precise isometric threshold. Baril and Vajnovszki's Gray code for derangements and Korsh and LaFollette's algorithm permit 3-rotations and/or multiple transpositions, and do not specify constrained geodesic distances. Mütze's survey confirms the distinction between elementary moves. A published current result for perfect-matching flip graphs on complete bipartite host graphs does not directly specialize to the crown graph with a missing diagonal matching. Previously recorded ménage-restricted permutations forbid two diagonals rather than one, so their parity obstruction and finite Gray-code construction do not imply the present degree distribution or metric boundary. An equivalent geometric theorem in an unindexed source remains possible.

## Value
PASS. This specifies the exact local mobility distribution of the canonical diagonal-forbidden perfect-matching switch chain, not just its state count, and locates the first order at which every ambient-shortest transposition realization of a pair can be blocked. The connected/non-geodesic contrast holds in every order at least five and is useful when comparing shortest unrestricted sorting sequences with admissible matching-switch paths. The standard parity obstruction is provided only as context, not claimed as the motivating new result.
