# Scientific review: exact maximal period \(106\) for quaternary order-four orientable sequences

## Correctness (C): PASS

The theorem is a universal maximum, with matching independently replayable finite witness. Every selected four-tuple is nonpalindromic and no reversal pair can contribute twice. For each uniform triple, conservation enforces an even count among three reversal pairs, at most two. The explicit triple potential and reversal weights obey a five-case inequality over *all* 240 nonpalindromic four-tuples. The five cases partition the domain and sum to the sharp universal upper bound \(54+36+16=106\); flow cancels the potential. The witness check enumerates each of 106 cyclic windows and certifies all required distinctness conditions. The checker also exhausts the five local cases, but the general theorem follows from the explicit proof, not from a finite search over possible cycles.

## Originality (O): PASS with documented priority risk

Mitchell and Wild (arXiv:2507.02526, first 2025-07-03), Table 7 and Section 7, list the specific quaternary order-four maximum as open, with known construction period 88 and general upper bound 112. The present complete numerical optimum is neither a specialization nor a consequence of these bounds, because both endpoints leave a 24-symbol gap; it provides a separate 106-symbol construction and an additional local potential inequality. Relevant prior general constructions and special-orientable variants do not imply this exact ordinary-orientability maximum. Research-record searches for reverse-free Eulerian cycles, equivalent indexing, alphabet/order parameters and period 106 found no matching result. The residual risk is unpublished/unindexed work or a source not found in the available searches; no independently verified publication-priority claim is made.

## Value (V): PASS

The precise four-letter order-four optimum is an explicitly documented open cell of a natural orientable-sequence table, relevant to bidirectional position sensing and reverse-unique de Bruijn cycles. Resolving it replaces a strict known interval \([88,112]\) by a certified exact invariant, with both an elementary universal bound and a concrete usable sequence. This is a natural complete parameter case, not an arbitrary computed slice or routine reformulation.
