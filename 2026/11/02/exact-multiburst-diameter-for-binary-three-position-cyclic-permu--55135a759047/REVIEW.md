# Scientific review: exact multiburst diameter for binary three-position cyclic permutations

## Correctness — PASS

The precise objects are binary length-\(n\) words, \(n\ge3\), weight \(k\), \(1\le k<n\), under repeated rotations of contiguous length-three blocks. The all-parameter proof establishes (i) exact equality of the channel adjacency graph with \(F_k(P_n^2)\); (ii) equality of token-slide distances and integral minimum-cost transshipment by a constructive occupied-to-unoccupied arc schedule; (iii) the integer Lipschitz-potential duality of that integral network-flow problem; (iv) the restriction that sorted potential-value increments cannot be adjacent ones, using removal-of-one-vertex connectivity of \(P_n^2\); and (v) matching upper and lower bounds, the latter witnessed by \(1^k0^{n-k}\) and \(0^{n-k}1^k\). Complement symmetry covers \(k>n/2\), and the exceptional small admissible length \(n=3\) is included. The all-pairs C++ checker independently reproduces actual rotation adjacency and diameters on lengths \(3\)–\(15\), including 110,998,233 ordered pairs. A separate Python implementation confirms distance equals minimum matching cost on 66,174 ordered pairs. Neither finite check is misrepresented as an infinite proof. Main correctness risks are potential unnoticed flaws in the network-flow scheduling argument and application of sorted potential restrictions; both were explicitly reconstructed above and tested on finite cases.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

The most directly relevant 2026 burst-channel paper (Chen–Milenkovic, arXiv:2610.15701v1, §V Proposition 3) supplies the length-three local move model and one-burst error correction but not an all-pairs shortest multiburst metric or diameter. The foundational token graph paper (Fabila-Monroy et al., arXiv:0910.4774v1, §3 Theorem 1) establishes general bounds in terms of the base graph diameter, not the present sharp all-parameter formula. The 2021 Adame–Rivera–Trujillo-Negrete arXiv preprint (arXiv:2101.01855) explicitly identifies this same \(F_k(P_n^2)\) graph in its Gray-code appendix, but proves Hamiltonian/Gray-code properties rather than pair distances or diameter. This important equivalence has been compared by implication, not disregarded. The 2025 supertoken paper concerns allowing multiple tokens per vertex. Targeted mathematical-record searches for the equivalent token-graph, binary-move and Lipschitz transport formulations, and review of available previous scientific claims, returned no covering exact diameter. However, a long-established graph model makes an unindexed or unrecognized prior diameter calculation plausible. The originality assessment is best-knowledge, not proof of global priority.

## Value — PASS

The new theorem gives one sharp formula for **every** nontrivial binary composition and every length \(n\ge3\) in a recently motivated constrained permutation-burst channel. Beyond verifying reachability, it determines the exact worst-case number of sequential local errors, identifies a canonical diametral pair, and supplies an extremal transport-potential proof. It closes a natural global metric question arising from the one-burst error model rather than re-running known finite code-size tables, and its quadratic growth is relevant to multiburst reachability budgets and to any coding-theoretic distance analysis built on this channel. The result does not itself yield a new codebook or a multierror decoder, and its value is stated at that level only.

## Closest published literature and limitations

The primary model and source date are Chen and Milenkovic, *Correcting Burst Errors with Applications to DNA-Based Data Storage*, arXiv:2610.15701v1 (2026-10-11). The structurally closest graph-theoretic sources are Fabila-Monroy et al., *Token Graphs*, arXiv:0910.4774v1 (2009), and Adame et al., *Hamiltonicity of Token Graphs of some Join Graphs*, arXiv:2101.01855 (2021). The primary claim concerns binary, exactly three-position contiguous cyclic rotations, sequential application, and nontrivial constant-weight classes.
