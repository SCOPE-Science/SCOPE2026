# Exact natural-basis graph spectra and distribution for binary rank-one evolution algebras

## Finding
For every nondegenerate finite-dimensional evolution algebra \(E\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) satisfying \(\dim E^2=1\), the entire collection of directed graphs attached to its natural bases has a simple exact classification. There is an intrinsic nonzero \(u\in E^2\), an intrinsic nonsingular symmetric form \(B\) with \(xy=B(x,y)u\), and an intrinsic characteristic vector \(c\) defined by \(B(c,x)=B(x,x)\). If \(u=c\), every natural-basis graph is \(G_{n,n}\). Otherwise the possible graph types are precisely
\[
\{G_{n,k}:1\le k<n,\quad k\equiv B(u,u)\pmod 2\}.
\]
Here \(G_{n,k}\) is the directed graph on \(n\) vertices with an arrow from every vertex to each vertex of a fixed \(k\)-element subset (loops included). The graph isomorphism type depends only on \(k\), since each vertex has outdegree \(k\). Every listed value is attained by a natural basis of the *same* algebra.

The spectrum admits a uniform *probability law*. Sample an ordered natural basis uniformly from all natural bases of \(E\), and let \(K\) be its graph's common outdegree. If \(u=c\), then \(\Pr(K=n)=1\). Otherwise let \(s=B(u,u)\in\{0,1\}\) and
\[
d_{n,s}=2^{n-1}-\mathbf 1_{s=0}-\mathbf 1_{s\equiv n\pmod2}.
\]
Then \(d_{n,s}>0\) and, for every \(1\le k<n\) with \(k\equiv s\pmod2\),
\[
\Pr(K=k)=\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{d_{n,s}},
\]
with zero probability at all other values. Equivalently, \(K\) is the Hamming weight of a uniformly chosen nonzero, noncharacteristic binary vector of norm parity \(s\). For instance, in dimension four a noncharacteristic odd-norm square vector produces \(G_{4,1}\) and \(G_{4,3}\) with probability \(1/2\) each.

## Assumptions and scope
The ground field is exactly \(\mathbb F_2\). An evolution algebra is a commutative, not necessarily associative algebra admitting a natural basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\) with \(e_ie_j=0\) whenever \(i\ne j\). Nondegenerate means \(\operatorname{ann}(E)=0\); the ordinary square \(E^2\) is the span of products. Assume \(n\ge1\) and \(\dim E^2=1\). A directed edge \(i\to j\) occurs iff the coefficient of \(e_j\) in \(e_i^2\) is nonzero. No loop deletion or undirected symmetrization is imposed.

## Proof
**1. Intrinsic bilinear form.** The one-dimensional \(\mathbb F_2\)-space \(E^2\) has a unique nonzero vector \(u\). Every natural-basis square \(e_i^2\) is nonzero: otherwise \(e_i\) annihilates the whole algebra, contradicting nondegeneracy. Therefore \(e_i^2=u\) for every \(i\). Every product has a unique coefficient in \(\mathbb F_2u\), defining a symmetric bilinear form \(B\) with \(xy=B(x,y)u\). In a natural basis its Gram matrix is the identity. In particular \(B\) is nonsingular, and the natural bases of \(E\) are precisely its orthonormal bases: pairwise orthogonality is necessary, and an orthogonal basis for a nonsingular form has no zero diagonal entries, which over \(\mathbb F_2\) must be one.

**2. The characteristic vector and graph.** In characteristic two, \(x\mapsto B(x,x)\) is a linear functional. Nondegeneracy supplies a unique \(c\) satisfying \(B(c,x)=B(x,x)\) for every \(x\). If \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\) is an orthonormal basis, then \(B(c,v_i)=1\) for all \(i\), so \(c=\sum_i v_i\). Write \(u=\sum_i\epsilon_i v_i\), with \(\epsilon_i\in\mathbb F_2\). Every basis square is \(u\), giving the graph \(G_{n,k}\) with \(k=\sum_i\epsilon_i\) (ordinary integer sum). As \(u\ne0\), \(k\ge1\). Also \(B(u,u)=\sum_i\epsilon_i\) in \(\mathbb F_2\), hence the parity restriction. If \(u=c\), all \(\epsilon_i=1\), so \(k=n\). Conversely, if \(k=n\) in any orthonormal basis, then \(u=\sum_i v_i=c\). Therefore \(u\ne c\) forces \(k\le n-1\).

**3. All permitted support sizes occur.** Fix one natural basis and identify \((E,B)\) with the standard binary dot-product space. Permutations of coordinates preserve \(B\). For any four distinct coordinates form their indicator vector \(w\) of Hamming weight four. The linear transformation
\[
T_w(x)=x+B(x,w)w
\]
is an isometry because \(B(w,w)=0\), and expanding \(B(T_w(x),T_w(y))\) makes the extra term \((2+B(w,w))B(x,w)B(y,w)=0\). If \(x\) has weight \(k\), choose a four-set meeting its support in exactly one coordinate when \(1\le k\le n-3\); then \(T_w(x)\) has weight \(k+2\). Choose a four-set meeting the support in exactly three coordinates when \(3\le k\le n-1\); then \(T_w(x)\) has weight \(k-2\). These conditions are exactly the availability of one occupied plus three vacant, or three occupied plus one vacant, coordinates. Starting from \(u\) of weight \(k_0<n\), successive weight-four transvections therefore attain every integer \(k\) with \(1\le k<n\) and \(k\equiv k_0\pmod2\). Each isometry transports an orthonormal basis to another one, thereby attaining the corresponding natural-basis graph. The argument also covers \(n\le3\): there are no distinct attainable weights of the same parity below \(n\), and no transvection is needed.

**4. Uniform natural-basis distribution.** The coordinate-permutation isometries act transitively on all vectors of any given Hamming weight. The weight-four transvections in the preceding step join every permissible weight class in a fixed parity. Thus the orthogonal group acts transitively on the *entire* set of nonzero, noncharacteristic vectors of norm parity \(s\). The number of such vectors is \(2^{n-1}\) (one parity half of all binary vectors), minus \(1\) when the zero vector has that parity and minus \(1\) when the characteristic vector \(c\) has that parity. Since \(B(c,c)=n\pmod2\), this is precisely \(d_{n,s}\). For a uniformly chosen orthonormal frame, the coordinate vector of \(u\) is uniformly distributed over its orthogonal-group orbit: all fibers of the action map have the same stabilizer cardinality. Exactly \(\binom{n}{k}\) vectors have weight \(k\), proving the stated probability formula. When \(u=c\), its orbit is a singleton. This argument also shows directly that the uniform distribution is unchanged if natural bases are considered modulo permutations.

**5. Sharp first dimension and realization.** For \(n=1,2,3\), each parity class of \(\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\) has at most one member, and \(u=c\) likewise gives one class. For \(n=4\), let \(B\) be the dot product, \(u=e_1\), and define multiplication by \(xy=B(x,y)u\). Its standard natural basis yields \(G_{4,1}\). With \(w=e_1+e_2+e_3+e_4\), the orthonormal basis \(v_i=T_w(e_i)=e_i+w\) has \(u\)-coordinate vector \((0,1,1,1)\), giving \(G_{4,3}\). Conversely, any nonzero \(u\in\mathbb F_2^n\) with the standard dot product gives a nondegenerate evolution algebra with one-dimensional square, so the classification covers every algebra in the stated class.

## Verification
The included Python 3 script `verify_graphs.py` independently enumerates every orthonormal basis of the standard binary dot product in dimensions \(1\) through \(5\), then computes the actual graph-support sizes and their multiplicities for every nonzero square vector; it checks the uniform-basis probability formula by exact integer cross-products. It also independently computes the orbits of coordinate permutations and weight-four transvections on all nonzero vectors in dimensions \(1\) through \(9\). Each observed spectrum agrees with the stated classification and each generated orbit contains exactly the vectors of the predicted support weights. Run `python3 verify_graphs.py`; the embedded output ends with `VERIFY_OK`. The finite verification is supplementary: the proof above covers all finite dimensions.

## Relationship to prior work
The recent survey by Kaygorodov, Martín González, and Viruel (arXiv:2610.22248, first public 2026-10-15) explicitly asks for natural-basis graph invariants, possible changes of associated graphs, and simultaneous graphical realizations (Questions 14, 27 and 30). Its Remark 22 exhibits graph variation in characteristic different from two but does not contain the binary parity-spectrum classification. Boudi, Cabrera Casado, and Siles Molina (arXiv:2006.14460, Proposition 2.16 and Corollary 2.13) establish structural facts on natural basis changes, without determining this graph spectrum. Martín Barquero, Martín González, and Sánchez-Ortega (2025, DOI below) classify algebras with one-dimensional square in terms of bilinear forms, but do not determine the directed graphs realized by every natural basis. Kramer and Stroppel (arXiv:2208.11326) study general nonalternating symmetric bilinear isometry groups in characteristic two, with particular attention to dimension four. Their inspected sections cover form theory and isometry groups, but do not state the associated evolution-algebra graph spectrum or binomial law. The result here adds the complete finite-field graph-spectrum invariant and its exact random-natural-basis distribution to that structural framework.

## Limitations
The classification covers *only* nondegenerate evolution algebras with one-dimensional ordinary square over \(\mathbb F_2\). It does not assert an analogous parity rule over larger finite fields or for higher-dimensional square spaces, nor does it classify arbitrary simultaneous digraph realizations. The elementary binary-orthogonal orbit argument is closely related to classical isometry theory and could be implicit in Kramer--Stroppel or earlier coding-theoretic literature; historical priority is not independently certified. The check of published records does not prove historical uniqueness.
