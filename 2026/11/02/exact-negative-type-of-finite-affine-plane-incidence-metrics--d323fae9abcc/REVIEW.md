# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The affine-plane axioms yield the two exact incidence-matrix identities in RESULT.md. Those identities exhaust the zero-sum powered-distance quadratic form into a parallel-class scalar block, paired point/line zero-sum blocks, and a remaining constant-population direction. At the proposed endpoint the scalar is zero; elementary convexity bounds make the paired and constant blocks nonpositive. The order-two boundary is checked separately. A parallel-class witness disproves every larger exponent; Schoenberg's Gaussian-kernel representation establishes strictness below the endpoint and the stated Hilbert affine dimensions. The attached prime-field graph checker corroborates, but does not prove, the all-plane assertion.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Bartoli, Héger, Kiss, and Takáts (2017), arXiv:1706.06583, analyze resolving sets for these incidence graphs, not powered-distance conditional negative type. Robertson (2020), arXiv:2004.10923, proves a universal determinant characterization of generalized roundness but does not evaluate this family. Related symmetric-design computations assume different distance blocks: an affine plane has parallel lines at graph distance four. The exact logarithmic threshold and endpoint rank are thus not covered by the checked statements. Historical equivalence under another design or kernel formulation remains a priority risk; the absence of a search hit is not uniqueness evidence.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result exactly solves a natural Hilbert-snowflake threshold for every finite affine plane, including non-Desarguesian ones, with a sharp equality-space dimension and a concrete obstruction. The order-only formula \(\log_2(q/(q-1))\), the exceptional order-two rank, and the scaling in \(q\) clarify a nontrivial structural invariant of incidence metrics. The result does not determine exponents for general resolvable block designs, weighted incidence graphs, or nonexisting plane orders.
