# Mathematical review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The claim concerns a finite adjacency-Dirac Connes metric for every integer \(m\ge2\). Restriction to the one- or two-petal principal commutator produces a rigorous norm lower bound; extending every omitted petal constantly achieves equality. One-petal operator norms are computed from a cubic characteristic polynomial and completed squares. The automorphism-antisymmetrization in the two-petal case preserves the endpoint difference and decreases the norm; the resulting quintic characteristic polynomial and completed square give the sharp \(\sqrt{3/2}\) value. These steps establish the infinite family directly; finite tests merely check formulas. The package includes source code with exact symbolic identities and additional finite norm checks.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to residual bibliography risk.** Besnard, arXiv:2105.09056v1, proves pruning and gives the known unit-triangle distance. Clare--Li--Poon--Swartz, arXiv:2409.04146v1, develop the noncommutative-distance framework and explicit path solutions. Those results do not supply the precise between-petals five-vertex optimum. Swartz, arXiv:2610.07718, concerns graph edge deletion and cycles. Searches for the same graph under friendship/windmill/butterfly/bouquet terminology and the equivalent adjacency-Dirac commutator calculation found no earlier exact cross-petal formula. This is positive evidence for a new explicit instance, not proof of absolute uniqueness. Earlier treatments of more general spectral graph metrics remain a risk.

## Value

**PASS.** The graph family is canonical, its metric has distinct within- and across-block classes, and the result completes its pair-distance classification. The nonadditivity is quantitative: the interpetal distance is \(3/2\) times the local distance, rather than the shortest-path factor two. The exact optimizer demonstrates interaction between two triangles through the shared vertex and clarifies what graph-pruning alone does not compute.

## Scientific limits

No result is claimed for nonuniform edge weights, arbitrary cactus graphs, or non-adjacency Dirac choices. No general independence or external certification is asserted. There remains the possibility of an equivalent derivation hidden in a broader matrix-optimization source.
