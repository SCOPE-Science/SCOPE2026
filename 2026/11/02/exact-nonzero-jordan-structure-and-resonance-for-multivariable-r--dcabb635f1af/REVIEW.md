# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. Trace-norm summability of homogeneous output rows controls the infinite operator and determinant. The transverse monomial decomposition reduces each isolated nonzero eigenvalue to finite row-constant triangular matrices. A cumulative-sum recurrence shows one eigenvector and therefore one Jordan chain per transverse block, even when the coefficient sequence contains zeros elsewhere. The compact tail has no matching eigenvalue and is invertible after subtracting the target eigenvalue. The full nonzero Jordan structure follows. The finite standalone checker passed 16 exact rational nullity identities but is not a substitute for the proof.

## Originality

PASS, best-knowledge comparison. The closest relevant primary paper is Pilla (arXiv:2606.25885): its Section 4.4 supplies a point-spectrum inclusion and compact spectral inclusion, while Section 2.2 already supplies the transverse decomposition. Earlier one-variable articles by Mursaleen, Yildirim and Durna and by Galanopoulos and Girela concern spectra, boundedness or compactness but do not state the dimensional Jordan diagrams inspected here. The one-dimensional recurrence is elementary and not claimed as an original ingredient. Searches of spectral classifications and factorable-matrix aliases did not reveal an equivalent multivariable block-count theorem.

## Mathematical value

PASS. Resolvent pole order and nonsemisimplicity are intrinsic spectral invariants invisible in the known set-theoretic spectra. The theorem classifies every isolated nonzero eigenvalue under a natural trace-class hypothesis, and yields an explicit countable resonance locus for the polynomial-geometric family with sharp dimension-dependent counts of length-two and length-one blocks.

## Literature and limitations

The full 2026 weighted-Hardy factorable-matrix paper could not be inspected; it is a priority risk, along with potentially broader earlier triangular-matrix spectral theorems. The theorem does not characterize the zero root space, all Schatten singular values, or global eigenvector bases. Independent mathematical review remains outstanding.
