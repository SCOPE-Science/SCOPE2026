# Exact normalized matching for Delannoy lattices through size eighteen

## Finding

**Theorem (finite exact range).** For every integer \(0\le n\le18\), the Delannoy lattice \(\mathcal D_n\) of Chen and Dong has the normalized matching property. Writing \(P_k\) for its rank-\(k\) elements and \(\Gamma(X)\) for the upper cover neighborhood of \(X\subseteq P_k\), this states, **simultaneously for every** \(0\le k<n\) and every subset \(X\subseteq P_k\), that

\[
|\Gamma(X)|\,|P_k|\ \ge\ |X|\,|P_{k+1}|.
\]

The cases \(n=6,\ldots,18\) extend the previously established range \(0\le n\le5\). The largest checked lattice has \(6{,}625{,}109\) elements. In its hardest tested lower-middle comparison, the two rank sizes are \(1{,}256{,}465\) and \(1{,}462{,}563\), and an exact integral maximum flow saturates all \(1{,}837{,}659{,}219{,}795\) demand units.

## Assumptions and scope

Words over \(\{H,V,D\}\) have size \(h+v+2d=n\), where \(h,v,d\) count the respective letters. The poset order is generated by the cover moves

\[
H\longrightarrow V,\qquad HH\longrightarrow D,\qquad D\longrightarrow VV,
\]

applied to consecutive factors in a word. The rank is \(r(w)=v(w)+d(w)\), and the rank sets are \(P_k=\{w:r(w)=k\}\). These are precisely the size-\(n\) Delannoy lattices introduced in the cited paper, not the fixed-endpoint Delannoy-path lattices of earlier literature. For \(n=0\), the poset has one element and the normalized matching condition is vacuous.

## Proof

**1. Exact reduction from exponentially many shadow inequalities to one flow per rank pair.** Fix \(n\), \(k\), and let \(a=|P_k|\), \(b=|P_{k+1}|\). Construct a directed integral-capacity network with source \(s\), sink \(t\), and one vertex for every word in the two ranks. Put arcs \(s\to x\) of capacity \(b\) for \(x\in P_k\), arcs \(y\to t\) of capacity \(a\) for \(y\in P_{k+1}\), and arcs \(x\to y\) of capacity \(b\) exactly when \(x\lessdot y\). Every unit entering a lower vertex can traverse any of its outgoing arcs, since its total input is bounded by \(b\); thus the capacity \(b\) on a cover arc is just as effective as infinite capacity. By the integral max-flow/min-cut theorem, a flow of value \(ab\) exists exactly when

\[
\forall X\subseteq P_k:\quad b|X|\le a|\Gamma(X)|.
\]

Indeed, cuts of the standard infinite-cover-arc network are exactly the Hall cuts \(b|X|\le a|\Gamma(X)|\), and replacing infinity by \(b\) does not alter feasibility of a flow saturating the source arcs. Conversely, any such saturating flow gives the inequality for each \(X\) by summing its outgoing capacity into \(\Gamma(X)\).

**2. Lower-half finite exhaustive certificate.** The included standalone `verify_normality.cpp` generates *all* words of size \(n\), partitions them by exact rank, and independently compares the rank counts with the Delannoy recurrence

\[
D(a,b)=D(a-1,b)+D(a,b-1)+D(a-1,b-1),\qquad D(0,b)=D(a,0)=1.
\]

It generates every cover using the three displayed moves, constructs the flow network from those actual words and covers, and runs exact integer Dinic maximum flow. For every \(0\le n\le18\) and every \(0\le k<\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), it checks the equality \(\operatorname{maxflow}=|P_k||P_{k+1}|\). The accompanying output prints both integers for every checked pair. No sampled subsets or approximate optimization are used. The largest flow value is below \(2^{63}\), so signed 64-bit capacities and totals are exact in the stated range.

**3. Central comparison for odd \(n\).** Let \(n\) be odd and \(k=(n-1)/2\). Since \(h-v=n-2r(w)=1\) for \(w\in P_k\), parenthesis matching of \(H\) as an opening parenthesis and \(V\) as a closing parenthesis (ignoring fixed \(D\)'s) leaves at least one unmatched \(H\). Change the *first* unmatched \(H\) into \(V\). The resulting word covers the original. The usual cancellation invariant ensures that the matched pairs remain matched, and the reverse map changes the *last* unmatched \(V\) into \(H\). Thus this map is a bijection \(P_k\to P_{k+1}\), so the normalized matching inequality holds at the equal-size central pair. The standalone verifier also directly constructs this bijection and checks all images and their uniqueness for every odd \(n\le17\).

**4. Upper-half comparisons.** The involution interchanging \(H\) and \(V\), and fixing \(D\), is order-reversing and sends rank \(k\) to rank \(n-k\). Normalized matching in any finite bipartite relation is preserved by reversing its sides: for \(Y\) in the upper part, apply the forward inequality to the complement of its lower neighborhood to obtain \(|N^{-1}(Y)|/a\ge|Y|/b\). Duality therefore transfers every verified lower-half comparison to its upper-half counterpart.

Steps 1–4 jointly establish the theorem for **all** ranks and **all** subsets, not merely the tested ranks or a collection of examples.

## Verification

Compile the standalone deterministic certificate in this package using

```sh
g++ -O3 -std=c++17 -o verify_normality verify_normality.cpp
./verify_normality 0 18
```

Successful execution prints one line for each tested rank-pair with its two equal exact flow totals, central-bijection confirmations for odd sizes, and `VERIFY_OK 0_to_18`. Any mismatched rank count, missing cover word, nonsaturating flow, or nonbijective central map causes a nonzero exit. A separately implemented Python Dinic verifier in `independent_check.py` reproduces the flow checks, including odd central comparisons, for \(0\le n\le13\); run `python3 independent_check.py` to obtain `INDEPENDENT_VERIFY_OK_0_TO_13`. The Python program is an independent cross-check of the algorithm rather than the logical basis for the full bound.

The largest computation is a finite exact certificate: the source and verified execution are needed, and the printed totals alone are not a substitute for either. There is no inference about \(n\ge19\).

## Relationship to prior work

Chen and Dong, *Combinatorial aspects of the Delannoy Lattice*, arXiv:2609.30350v1 (first public September 24, 2026), introduce this size-\(n\) lattice, give its symmetric chain decomposition (Proposition 3.7), and explicitly ask in Problem 6.1 whether it is normal for every size. Their Theorem 6.2 establishes only the sizes \(0\le n\le5\), while their Lemma A.1 treats the rank-one to rank-two comparison for every size. Neither assertion supplies the interior normalized matching inequalities for sizes \(6\) through \(18\).

Autebert, Latapy and Schwer's 2002 work concerns a fixed-endpoint Delannoy-path lattice and is not automatically equivalent to the graded union of endpoint positions used here. Its accessible abstract concerns lattice structure, not this finite normalized matching cutoff; its uninspected full text leaves a residual historical-priority risk. Searches of the available relevant research records and exact scientific phrases uncovered no stronger covering statement. Absence from a search index alone is not a novelty proof.

## Limitations

The conclusion is confined to \(n\le18\). It neither proves the conjectured normality of every Delannoy lattice nor constructs a general symbolic family of normalized matching flows. The proof uses complete deterministic finite enumeration with exact integer max flow; independent formal checking and an exhaustive historical-priority audit have not been performed. The conclusion depends on the specified Chen–Dong word order, not on all posets called Delannoy lattices in the literature.
