# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The observation classes are exactly the pairs (atomic profile, support of their S5 class): pointwise induction proves sufficiency and exact-value tests plus derived existential possibility prove necessity. A reversible labelled-set decomposition gives the bivariate EGF; support families give the quotient-type census. The probability estimate follows from the ratio of two coefficient saddles; the first perturbative quadratic correction is indispensable for two atomic colours, and larger-support exclusions plus joint singleton exclusions are negligible by Cauchy bounds. The standalone checker reproduces the marked coefficients, the logical closure equivalence and the Bell-number totals. The asymptotic argument relies on standard saddle-point Gaussian estimates and is not claimed to be proved merely by the checks.

## Originality — PASS within checked evidence

Das--Ray--Mali (arXiv:2608.05550, Sections 2 and 4) already establish the general exact-observation quotient and must be credited. Das (arXiv:2608.08755, Section 2.1) provides the Boolean exact tests and derived possibility. Ciardelli--Otto (arXiv:2312.14573, Section 3.3.1) identify propositional supports, not multiplicities, as the relevant one-agent S5 bisimulation feature. The *support criterion itself* is therefore not claimed as novel. The new claims are the bivariate exact distribution of quotient sizes over every labelled S5 model, the exact available quotient-type count by carrier size, and a sharp asymptotic with the two-colour correction. No inspected primary text or matching research record states or implies this quantitative package. Older species/coupon-collector treatments with equivalent formulas may exist, so historical priority is not certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The general quotient theorem motivates asking how large its canonical reduction typically is on the natural finite S5 class, and which quotient types can occur on a carrier of given size. The exact bivariate EGF simultaneously answers every finite size question. The asymptotic proves typical saturation and quantifies rare non-saturation sharply; its \(m=2\) correction is a mathematically informative boundary of first-order saddle substitution. These statements go substantially beyond reproducing a known bisimulation characterization.
