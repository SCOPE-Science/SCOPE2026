With three pairs, the probability that a uniformly selected adjacent one-agent swap destroys every common stable marriage is exactly \(245/1944\), and swapping the top two positions is more than twice as disruptive as swapping the bottom two. A complete \(559{,}872\)-case census also determines the joint distribution of original and jointly stable matching counts and shows that a three-stable original profile always retains at least one jointly stable matching.
