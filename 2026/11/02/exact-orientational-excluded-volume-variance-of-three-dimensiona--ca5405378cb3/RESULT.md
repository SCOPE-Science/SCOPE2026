# Exact orientational excluded-volume variance of three-dimensional rectangular boxes

## Finding

Let \(K=\prod_{i=1}^3[-a_i/2,a_i/2]\subset\mathbb R^3\), where all \(a_i>0\), and put \(v=a_1a_2a_3\). Choose \(R\) independently from normalized Haar measure on \(SO(3)\), and define the excluded volume \(E(R)=\operatorname{Vol}_3(K-RK)\). Because \(K=-K\), this is \(\operatorname{Vol}_3(K+RK)\). Define the symmetric weights and three invariants
\[
w_{ij}=\frac{a_i}{a_j}+\frac{a_j}{a_i},\quad
T_0=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}w_{ij},\quad
T_1=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}w_{ij}^{2},\quad
T_2=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{3}w_{ij}\right)^2.
\]
For every rotation \(R\) the following exact identity holds:
\[
\frac{E(R)}v=2+\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}w_{ij}|R_{ij}|.
\]
Consequently the (known) mean and the **exact orientational variance** are
\[
\mathbb E[E(R)]=v\left(2+\left(\sum_i a_i\right)\left(\sum_i\frac1{a_i}\right)\right),
\]
\[
\boxed{\frac{\operatorname{Var}(E(R))}{v^2}
=\gamma T_0^2+(\alpha-2\beta+\gamma)T_1+2(\beta-\gamma)T_2,}
\]
where
\[
\alpha=\frac1{12},\qquad \beta=\frac2{3\pi}-\frac14,\qquad
\gamma=\frac8{3\pi^2}-\frac14.
\]
Among all three-dimensional rectangular boxes of a fixed positive volume, the variance is uniquely minimized by cubes. Precisely,
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(E(R))\ge
v^2\left(-69+\frac{96}{\pi}+\frac{384}{\pi^2}\right),}
\]
with equality if and only if \(a_1=a_2=a_3\). The universal normalized minimum is about \(0.4650835923\); the unit-volume cube has mean excluded volume \(11\) and standard deviation about \(0.682\).

## Assumptions and scope

The box sides are strictly positive, the two boxes are congruent, their relative orientation is Haar-uniform in the proper rotation group \(SO(3)\), and no translations are randomized. The excluded volume is the Lebesgue volume of the set of displacements of their centers for which their interiors intersect (boundary conventions have zero volume). The theorem describes the exact variance over relative orientations, not the variance of a Monte Carlo estimator or the randomness of a whole particle fluid. The orthogonal reflection group would yield the same law because the formula uses absolute matrix entries.

## Proof

**1. Exact rotation identity.** Write \(K\) as the zonotope generated by the three vectors \(a_i e_i\). Then \(K+RK\) is a translate of the zonotope generated by these vectors and \(a_jRe_j\), \(1\le j\le3\). The standard zonotope volume identity in three dimensions is the sum of the absolute determinants of all triples of generating vectors. The triples entirely from either box contribute \(2v\). A triple containing two unrotated generators (with missing index \(i\)) and one rotated generator (indexed \(j\)) contributes \(v(a_j/a_i)|R_{ij}|\). A triple containing one unrotated generator (indexed \(i\)) and two rotated generators (with missing index \(j\)) contributes \(v(a_i/a_j)|R_{ij}|\), since the cofactor matrix of \(R\in SO(3)\) equals \(R\). Summing proves the displayed rotation identity without any generic-position assumption; vanishing determinants cause no problem.

**2. Absolute Haar correlations.** Put \(X_{ij}=|R_{ij}|\). Every row and column is uniformly distributed on the unit two-sphere, whence \(\mathbb E X_{ij}=1/2\), \(\mathbb E X_{ij}^2=1/3\), and \(\mathbb E(X_{ij}X_{i\ell})=\mathbb E(X_{ij}X_{k j})=2/(3\pi)\) for distinct indices. To evaluate disjoint row/column indices, take the first row \(u\) uniform on the sphere. Conditional on \(u\), the second row is uniform on the great circle \(u^\perp\), giving \(\mathbb E(|R_{22}|\mid u)=(2/\pi)\sqrt{1-u_2^2}\). On conditioning further on \(u_2=t\), which is uniform on \([-1,1]\), obtain \(\mathbb E(|u_1|\mid t)=(2/\pi)\sqrt{1-t^2}\). Therefore
\[
\mathbb E|R_{11}R_{22}|=\frac4{\pi^2}\mathbb E(1-t^2)=\frac8{3\pi^2}.
\]
Haar row and column symmetries make this valid for every pair in distinct rows and distinct columns. Subtracting the product of means \(1/4\) yields respectively covariance \(\alpha\) for a repeated entry, \(\beta\) for a distinct same-row or same-column pair, and \(\gamma\) for a pair in different rows and columns.

**3. Variance polynomial.** Since \(w_{ij}=w_{ji}\), the ordered double sum over same-row or same-column distinct matrix entries is \(2(T_2-T_1)\). The ordered double sum over disjoint row/column entries is \(T_0^2+T_1-2T_2\). Expanding \(\operatorname{Var}(\sum w_{ij}X_{ij})\) by the three covariance types gives
\[
\alpha T_1+2\beta(T_2-T_1)+\gamma(T_0^2+T_1-2T_2),
\]
which is the claimed formula. The mean follows from \(\mathbb E X_{ij}=1/2\) and summation of the symmetric ratios.

**4. Sharp cube minimization.** Set \(S=\sum_{i,j}X_{ij}\) and \(\delta_{ij}=w_{ij}-2=(a_i-a_j)^2/(a_ia_j)\ge0\). The exact excluded volume identity rewrites as
\[
E(R)/v=2+2S+\sum_{i,j}\delta_{ij}X_{ij}.
\]
Every entry has the same covariance with \(S\), namely
\[
c_*=\operatorname{Cov}(S,X_{11})=\alpha+4\beta+4\gamma
=\frac{128+32\pi-23\pi^2}{12\pi^2}>0.
\]
For a rigorous elementary sign certificate, the numerator \(128+32t-23t^2\) is decreasing for all \(t\ge3\), while its value at \(t=22/7\) is \(68/49>0\); use \(3<\pi<22/7\). Thus
\[
\operatorname{Var}(E/v)-\operatorname{Var}(2S)
=4c_*\sum_{i,j}\delta_{ij}
+\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\sum_{i,j}\delta_{ij}X_{ij}\right)\ge0.
\]
Strict positivity holds unless all \(\delta_{ij}=0\), equivalently all three side lengths coincide. With every \(w_{ij}=2\), the three Haar covariance classes give
\[
\operatorname{Var}(2S)=4(9\alpha+36\beta+36\gamma)
=-69+\frac{96}{\pi}+\frac{384}{\pi^2}.
\]
This proves the bound and its equality condition for any prescribed positive volume. \(\square\)

## Verification

Run `python CHECK_EXCLUSION.py` after installing the NumPy/SciPy packages in `requirements.txt`. The program draws 240,000 reproducible Haar rotations and compares empirical covariances and excluded-volume means/variances against their analytical values for three boxes. It also constructs the actual Minkowski sum of 64 corner pairs using independent convex-hull software for several rotations, checking its 3D volume against the determinant formula. The reproducible output concludes `ALL_EXCLUDED_VOLUME_CHECKS_PASSED`. These finite numerical tests corroborate—but do not establish—the all-positive-side-length theorem. The rigorous argument is the four-part proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Torquato and Jiao (initial public arXiv version March 31, 2022) develop excluded volume using mixed-volume geometry and give orientationally averaged formulas; this work deliberately starts from their model but determines a second moment and a sharp fixed-volume variance minimizer instead of re-establishing their mean. Sreenivasan, Baker, Paul and Stanley (2002) studied mean excluded volumes of randomly oriented rectangular prisms, including the cube mean \(11\). Neither inspected paper states the present variance tensor or the minimization theorem. Other exact moment formulas for random projections of *one* orthotope concern a different random object and do not imply the variance of the sum of two independently oriented congruent boxes. The reported novelty is conditional on the accessible literature, not a declaration of exhaustive historical priority.

## Limitations

The result does not determine the full orientation distribution or higher moments, nor does it apply directly to unequal bodies, nonuniform relative orientations, or more than two bodies. The variance of a finite-sample collision estimator is a distinct quantity. No independent specialist audit has been performed, and exact historical priority remains subject to further bibliographic verification.
