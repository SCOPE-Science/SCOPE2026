# Scientific review

## Correctness (C): PASS

The theorem and all corollaries are derived from a bijective description of greedy binary arches under the hypothesis of exactly \(k\) occurrences of one letter. The proof counts all baseline and excess scattered \(ab\) pairs, including the unfinished suffix. Substitution at \(z=1\) gives an exact binomial sum, from which a fixed-slack asymptotic follows. Independent brute-force enumeration of words agrees with the formal product on 35 exhaustive cases; a further 160 arithmetic cases verify totals, reversal symmetry, and endpoint support. Finite checks are corroboration rather than a proof for all parameters.

## Originality (O): PASS, subject to ordinary priority risk

The closest full-text primary source, Fazekas–Huang–Mercaş (arXiv:2610.06717, October 5, 2026), gives the arithmetic criterion for the existence of a universal word in a Parikh-matrix fiber, not the exact number in each fiber. Its fixed-pattern probability approximation does not count a conjunction of all \(2^k\) required patterns with variable \(k\). Standard arch factorization and the \(s=0\) pair-count identity are established prior art and acknowledged. An accessible finite-length tally records universality without simultaneous fixed-composition and scattered-pair refinement. A published equivalent weighted-arch formula could have escaped the searches; there is no independent priority certification.

## Value (V): PASS

The new formula addresses a quantitative invariant that is not settled by either existential or universal endpoints: precisely what fraction of words in each Parikh-matrix class is maximally subsequence universal. It determines all refined coefficients across an infinite natural boundary family and yields a sharp exponential rarity law with an explicit fixed-slack leading constant. Both the distribution and the limiting scale can guide work on broader fiber-density questions. The result is narrower than a complete count for arbitrary compositions and makes no claim beyond this boundary regime.

## Limitations and prior literature

The result is confined to binary alphabets with the minimum letter multiplicity equal to the desired universality index. The asymptotic holds only at fixed slack. There has been no independent mathematical audit. Closest references are *Subsequence Analysis Problems for Binary Parikh Matrices* (2026), *Scattered factor-universality of words* (2020), and older arch-factorization work.
