# Exact phase diagram and basin cylinders for leader–feedforward threshold networks

## Finding

In a logistic threshold-influence network whose only feedback components are \(s\) autonomous self-influencing leaders, the sharp logistic feedback threshold is \(\beta=4\). Below and at that threshold all agents converge to the common half-active state. Above it, for arbitrary nonnegative feedforward weights, the network has exactly \(3^s\) equilibria, with \(\binom{s}{k}2^{s-k}\) of unstable dimension \(k\). Exactly \(2^s\) are attractors. Every initial state converges, and the basin of an attractor is determined solely by which side of \(1/2\) each leader initially occupies. Every attractor basin occupies exactly \(2^{-s}\) of the state cube. The full equilibrium census, unlike the literal basin cylinders, persists after sufficiently small directed-network perturbations that create feedback edges.

## Assumptions and scope

Fix integers \(n\ge1\) and \(1\le s\le n\), feedback strength \(\beta>0\), and the logistic response \(L(z)=(1+e^{-z})^{-1}\). Label vertices so leaders are \(1,\ldots,s\). Each leader is autonomous, with matrix row \(W_{rr}=1\) and all other entries in that row zero. Every other node \(i>s\) is a follower with a row of nonnegative weights adding to one, supported entirely on predecessors \(j<i\). Thus the directed graph with leader self-loops removed is feedforward. We study the continuous-time network ODE

\[
\dot x_i=-x_i+L\!\left(\beta\left(\sum_{j=1}^nW_{ij}x_j-\frac12\right)\right),\qquad x(0)\in[0,1]^n.
\]

The cube is forward invariant because at coordinate zero the derivative is positive and at coordinate one it is negative. A stable set means *all initial conditions converging to a specified equilibrium*, including measure-zero sets of saddles. The unstable dimension counts Jacobian eigenvalues with strictly positive real part. There is no upper bound on follower weights apart from the stated stochasticity and acyclicity.

## Proof

**Scalar leader bifurcation.** Every leader obeys \(\dot z=-z+L(\beta(z-1/2))\). Write \(u=2z-1\). Its equilibria are the solutions of \(u=\tanh(\beta u/4)\). For \(\beta\le4\), \(\tanh(y)<y\) for positive \(y\), so the sole root is \(u=0\), corresponding to \(z=1/2\). The scalar vector field points toward it from both sides, including at the degenerate equality \(\beta=4\). For \(\beta>4\), strict concavity of \(\tanh\) on positive arguments, its derivative \(\beta/4>1\) at zero, and the bound \(\tanh(\beta/4)<1\) establish one positive root \(u_\beta\in(0,1)\), one negative root, and zero. Put \(\ell=(1-u_\beta)/2\) and \(h=(1+u_\beta)/2\). The low and high equilibria are attracting because their scalar derivative \(-1+\beta z(1-z)\) is negative; at \(z=1/2\) it is positive. These equilibria are therefore hyperbolic whenever \(\beta>4\).

**Exact equilibrium and index census.** Fixing each leader at any one of its scalar equilibria determines every follower equilibrium uniquely, in increasing vertex order, by the equation

\[
x_i^*=L\!\left(\beta\left(\sum_{j<i}W_{ij}x_j^*-\frac12\right)\right).
\]

Conversely, an equilibrium must obey exactly this recursion. Hence there is one full equilibrium for each of the \(3^s\) leader choices when \(\beta>4\); for \(\beta\le4\) the sole equilibrium is the constant half-active vector, by row-stochasticity. The Jacobian is lower triangular. Its first \(s\) diagonal entries are \(-1+\beta x_r^*(1-x_r^*)\), and its remaining \(n-s\) diagonal entries are exactly \(-1\). An equilibrium with exactly \(k\) leaders at the middle root has exactly \(k\) positive eigenvalues and no eigenvalues on the imaginary axis. Choosing the middle-root positions and then the low/high assignments on all other leaders gives \(\binom{s}{k}2^{s-k}\). In particular, there are \(2^s\) hyperbolic sinks.

**All trajectories and every stable set.** Scalar phase-line dynamics imply that each leader converges to \(\ell\) when its initial value is below \(1/2\), to \(1/2\) when exactly equal, and to \(h\) when above, for \(\beta>4\). For \(\beta\le4\) each leader converges to \(1/2\). Inductively, for a follower the incoming response \(g_i(t)=L(\beta(\sum_{j<i}W_{ij}x_j(t)-1/2))\) converges to a finite value \(g_i^*\). Variation of constants gives \(x_i(t)=e^{-t}x_i(0)+\int_0^t e^{-(t-v)}g_i(v)\,dv\to g_i^*\). Thus every trajectory converges to precisely the equilibrium generated by its leaders' limiting roots. It follows *without ignoring saddle stable sets* that for each equilibrium the complete set of initial states converging to it is the product of the specified root-side or root-equality conditions with the entire follower cube. For the sinks there are no middle roots. Consequently their basin boundaries inside the cube are precisely the union of the \(s\) root hyperplanes \(x_r=1/2\); each basin has \(s\)-dimensional root volume \(2^{-s}\), with unconstrained follower coordinates. Uniformly independently distributed leader initial values give independent fair binary final leader states, hence the claimed binomial count. If leaders have independent continuous nonuniform laws, each basin probability instead factors using the individual masses below and above \(1/2\).

**Persistence under small feedback perturbations.** Fix \(\beta>4\) and a feedforward \(W_0\). All \(3^s\) equilibria are hyperbolic, so their fixed-point Jacobians are invertible. The implicit function theorem continues each of these equilibria uniquely to every sufficiently nearby matrix. The equilibria remain in the open cube and retain unstable index by continuity of eigenvalues. There cannot be another equilibrium for matrices approaching \(W_0\): otherwise a sequence of extra equilibria in the compact cube would have a convergent subsequence, whose limit is an equilibrium at \(W_0\), contradicting local uniqueness in that equilibrium's implicit-function neighborhood. Restricting the perturbations to nonnegative row-stochastic matrices preserves cube invariance. Thus small new directed cycles do not alter the equilibrium and index counts. This argument does not claim that basin hyperplanes survive perturbation.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone \(\texttt{verify\_feedforward.py}\) checks scalar roots, recursive equilibrium residuals, triangular Jacobian indices, and numerical convergence from varied initial conditions for randomly generated nonnegative row-stochastic feedforward matrices. In a deterministic trial set with \(\beta\in\{4.5,5,8\}\), \(s\in\{1,2,3\}\), and zero, one, or three followers, it checked 162 matrix profiles, 2,106 constructed equilibria and 486 trajectories; every assertion passed. These finite numerical checks corroborate but do not replace the all-dimension proof. The perturbation persistence conclusion follows analytically from compactness and the implicit function theorem, not finite sampling.

## Relationship to prior work

Boudourides, *Collective Hysteresis and Multistability in Threshold Networks* (arXiv:2609.10580v1, first public 2026-09-05), studies the row-stochastic threshold-feedback network, equitable-partition reductions, stable equilibria for uncoupled blocks, and their persistence under weak interblock coupling. The present result treats arbitrary-strength one-way follower coupling on non-equitable networks and exactly classifies *all* equilibria, saddle indices, complete basin cells, and normalized basin volumes. A separate two-community model studies interaction-dependent activation contrast rather than this root-determined basin partition. Qi and coauthors analyze three-node coupled feedforward/feedback motifs with Hill-gate dynamics and computational multistability sampling; their article does not give the stated logistic arbitrary-size exact basin law. Earlier steep-sigmoid regulatory-network and cellular-network basin studies discuss related stability and attraction mechanisms. The full text of the immediately motivating arXiv manuscript could not be inspected, and an equivalent unindexed or implicit result remains a genuine priority risk.

## Limitations

The exact basin cylinders depend on having strictly autonomous leaders and no directed follower-to-ancestor feedback. For perturbed networks, only the local robustness of the *equilibrium counts and indices* is proved; global convergence and unchanged basin geometry are not asserted. The exact arithmetic of the counts does not automatically extend to networks with nonlinear follower self-feedback, heterogeneous signs, or stochastic temporal switching. At \(\beta=4\), the unique equilibrium is nonhyperbolic; the persistence conclusion applies only to \(\beta>4\). Literature access limits leave a nonzero risk of prior exact coverage.
