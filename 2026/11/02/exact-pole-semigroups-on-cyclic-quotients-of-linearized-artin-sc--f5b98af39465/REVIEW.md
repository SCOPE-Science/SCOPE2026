# Review of the pole-semigroup result

**Correctness — PASS.** The quotient invariants \(u,v\) give a degree-\(p\) map and an exact equation; tame Riemann–Hurwitz gives genus \((p^N-1)/2\). Explicit local branches over \(u=0\) and a unique place above \(u=\infty\) establish the poles of the constructed global functions. Their numerical semigroup has the same genus as the curve, forcing equality by the Weierstrass gap theorem. Minimality, conductor, type, and Riemann–Roch dimensions follow from its Apéry set. The independent residue-shortest-path arithmetic in `semigroup_checker.py` checks twelve parameter pairs, but does not replace the proof. Risk: the proof uses standard normalization and tame ramification facts, rather than a formal geometric computer certificate.

**Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional).** The relevant 2026 paper by Mendoza (arXiv:2606.01369v1, Theorem 4.2, Examples 4.4–4.5) supplies multipoint maximum-element formulas for constant-coefficient linearized extensions. This quotient has nonconstant \(u^2\) coefficient and its infinity place is not simply a place on the original linearized function field; the semigroup formula does not directly follow as a stated corollary. The 2024 work of Navarro concerns bases on the original linearized field, and Castellanos–Mendoza–Tizziotti 2025 concerns Kummer extensions. Targeted algebraic-geometry searches and research-record comparisons did not locate an equivalent coefficient-uniform formula or an earlier encompassing theorem. Risk: an equivalent result could be latent in a broader invariant-field theorem or a differently presented Artin–Schreier quotient; absence from search results is not proof of novelty.

**Value — PASS.** Explicit Weierstrass semigroups govern all one-point Riemann–Roch spaces and are a principal input to algebraic-geometric code constructions. Here a whole positive-dimensional coefficient family of quotient curves has a uniform semigroup with a high embedding dimension, a precise conductor and type, and explicit Riemann–Roch dimensions, despite the quotient equation having a variable coefficient in its additive part. This is a structural classification, not an isolated finite computation. Risk: no claims are made about improved code distances or the number of distinct moduli points.
