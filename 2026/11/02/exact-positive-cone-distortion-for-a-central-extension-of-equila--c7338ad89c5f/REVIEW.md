# Scientific assessment

## Correctness (PASS)
The claim applies to the nonnegative Dirac cone for an equilateral pointed metric with an added uniformly distant center. Cúth–Raunig's finite spanning-tree theorem gives an exact formula for the norm. Positive subtree flows justify moving the center directly to the root, and moving leaf-to-leaf child subtrees toward the root without increasing edge length or transported mass. These operations reduce every candidate to a two-level star, proving the minimum over all subsets. The power-mean bound gives a universal lower cost, and the singleton/all-equal witnesses match both branches. The result holds at \(p=1\), \(r=1/2\), \(r=1\) and equality thresholds. The included exhaustive finite tree diagnostics support, but do not establish, the general proof.

## Originality (PASS; residual bibliographic risk)
The inspected primary 2023 paper gives the spanning-tree algorithm and general inclusion theory, not an exact positive-cone constant for this star family. The 2018 foundational non-isometric-inclusion paper establishes a phenomenon but not its present exact parameter profile. The closely related published 2026 equilateral-to-star result determines a universal Lipschitz extension trace; its quantified target-center optimization is not the restricted inverse operator norm, and the former result cannot be substituted for the latter. The 2026 general canonical-inclusion result provides qualitative injectivity and general bounds, not this exact cone formula. Semantic research searches additionally covered inclusion distortion, barycentric formulas, and positive coefficients; a differently named earlier equivalent result remains possible.

## Value (PASS)
A finite free \(p\)-space inclusion can lose norm, but even determining an exact subcone modulus is nontrivial because the norm minimizes over all spanning trees. The closed charge-vector formula identifies the full positive-cone geometry, a sharp metric-radius/leaf-count boundary for when inclusion loses norm, and an asymptotic distortion of \(1/r\) for fixed nonlocally convex exponent. This provides a controlled family of rigorous lower-bound witnesses for the general canonical-inclusion problem, rather than a single isolated example.

## Scope and risks
The result is not the full inverse operator norm on the signed linear span. Possible equivalent prior cone calculations are not excluded; independent scientific and bibliographic audit remain outstanding.
