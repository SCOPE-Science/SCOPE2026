For uniform complete multipartite graph diffusion, degree-two and classical fourth-order Taylor–Runge–Kutta steps have exact nonnegative end-step horizons \(2/N\) and \(2.7852935634\ldots/N\), independent of all part sizes. Third-order steps instead have a sharp singleton/within-part phase law and, from five vertices onward, generally preserve positivity for *less* time than second-order steps; balanced complete bipartite graphs uniquely maximize that third-order horizon for every vertex count \(N\ge6\).
