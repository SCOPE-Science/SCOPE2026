# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. The claim is proved for all primes at least five using a complete decomposition of the graph of the two forbidden binary relations. The proof treats repeated-operand loops, the exceptional unipotent target, and the split/nonsplit projective eigenvalue cases. The exact count of smaller-extremum targets follows from the square subgroup of the unique order-four-divisible eigenvalue-ratio torus. An independent graph-component program checked 4,178 cases and a separate subset enumerator checked the 48 smallest cases; neither finite check is used as an infinite proof.

## Originality
PASS under available evidence, with residual priority risk. Tang's arXiv:2603.29992 treats the target one, where the composition of involutions has order three. Its main theorem and proof do not determine the maxima for arbitrary targets or count how often the smaller value occurs. Relevant searches for fixed-residue sum/product avoidance, generalized involution graphs and projective orders returned no theorem implying the displayed target spectrum. The fact that the graph method is motivated by Tang is disclosed; older implicit dihedral-graph coverage remains possible. The distinct source on real-field sum-product cardinality does not imply membership-level finite-field results.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The exact targetwise spectrum answers a natural quantitative refinement of the recent finite-field sum-product avoidance problem: it proves that not every nonzero target behaves like the previously studied target one, completely characterizes which targets differ, and counts them for every prime. It is an infinite classification with a structural projective-group explanation, rather than an arbitrary small-modulus calculation.

## Scope and risks
The theorem requires nonzero targets and prime fields in odd characteristic at least five. Extremizing sets are not classified, and extension fields are untreated. Independent literature-priority and mathematical validation have not been performed.
