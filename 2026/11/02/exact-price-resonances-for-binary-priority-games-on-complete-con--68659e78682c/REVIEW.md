# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Starting from the full service-order payoff, the one-step comparison is \(A_i-c\). Its integrality combined with adjacent transitions yields the necessity of integer prices whenever a bidding switch occurs. The adjacent recurrence forces alternating suffixes and leads to exact prefix-length formulas. Constant words and the boundary \(c=n-1\) are handled explicitly; the candidate-count trichotomy follows algebraically. An independent direct-payoff enumerator confirms all predicted equilibria on 150 finite game instances and all gain identities on 8,192 cases. This verification complements rather than replaces the proof.

## Originality — PASS with residual risk

The primary complete manuscript, Lin–Lan–Nguyen–Kawahara (arXiv:2610.05832v1, 2026-10-05), establishes the price interval \(0<c<1\) with no equilibrium for general conflict graphs and identifies exactly when the all-zero word is an equilibrium. Those claims are prior results. It does not assert the exact full price spectrum or exact two-equilibrium count for the two-level complete graph. Brandt–Fischer–Harrenstein–Shoham (2008) study ranking-only utilities, not a separately priced priority announcement. Hassin–Haviv (1997) address stochastic priority queues with different payoff and interaction assumptions. The novelty assessment is bounded by accessible prior work; differently phrased publications or nonindexed results remain a risk.

## Value — PASS

The manuscript's nonexistence interval suggests a natural design question: which positive prices restore pure equilibria, and how many alternatives arise? The theorem answers that question on the entire simplest complete-conflict two-level family, not just at sampled parameters. Its exact resonance and alternating-profile classification show that generic prices below the maximum threshold have no pure equilibrium despite isolated integer-price recoveries. This informs pricing robustness in decentralised scheduling; claims about noncomplete graphs and welfare are not made.

## Limitations

This is a self-contained two-level complete-graph classification. There is no independent validation, no claim about mixed strategies, and publication priority is not certified.
