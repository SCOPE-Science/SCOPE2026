On a priced double-star tree with independently adjustable conductances, the unique allocation minimizing the largest pairwise effective resistance has an exact three-regime solution governed by whether \(\sqrt{pa}\), \(\sqrt{qb}\), and \(\sqrt c\) satisfy the triangle inequality. The boundary marks the appearance of within-community worst pairs alongside cross-bridge worst pairs; for unit prices and an increasingly unbalanced tree, the optimum beats equal-conductance allocation by a factor approaching \(3/2\).
