# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact state space has \(Nq^{n-1}\) idempotents, parametrized uniquely by a projective image line and a normalized linear form. Internal zero contractions give the zero-atom probability, and the cyclic projective-line transfer matrix has the two explicitly verified eigenvalues. Multiplicative-character orthogonality yields the nonzero trace masses, while conjugacy yields per-matrix masses; the excess of the trace-one orbit proves the sharp total-variation formula. The proof covers every prime-power field and the edge cases \(k=1\), \(q=2\), \(n=2\). Both exact finite checkers pass, including nonprime \(q=4\).

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Sarma–Subedi (arXiv:2610.07892v1) study nilpotence for two random unrestricted elements; Ferraz–Polcino Milies–Taufer (arXiv:1711.09289v1) classify and enumerate rank-one idempotent generators; Holmes–Im (arXiv:2608.16636) count nilpotent unrestricted cyclic quiver representations. Erdős studies factorability of singular matrices by idempotents; Dolžan (arXiv:2608.20934) studies mixed nilpotent/idempotent factors. None of the inspected relevant material states the all-target law for independent **rank-one idempotent** factors or the sharp conditional uniformization/total-variation law. Result-record, previous-claim, and sequence-database searches found no matching statement. Residual risk: full texts of some older factorization works and one 2026 adjacent work were unavailable, so absolute historical priority is not certified.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Rank-one idempotents are the primitive nonzero idempotents in full finite-field matrix rings and form a canonical conjugacy class. The theorem completely resolves their random-product law for all dimensions, finite fields, word lengths, and target matrices. It detects a precise trace-one bias and proves quantitative mixing onto all nonzero rank-one matrices after conditioning on nonzero products. This is a natural infinite-family classification, not a small-table or a specialization of unrestricted-factor counts.
