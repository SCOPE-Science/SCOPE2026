# Scientific review: Möbius-ladder quadratic embedding constant

## Correctness
**PASS.** The object is the ordinary unit-edge shortest-path metric on the circulant \(C_{2n}(1,n)\), not the effective resistance metric. The Cayley displacement argument establishes the first distance row exactly. Its circular second difference has explicit support and coefficients, giving the Fourier spectrum without an unproved spectral lemma. Every even nonconstant frequency is nonpositive, every odd frequency has the signed trigonometric form displayed in the proof, and monotonicity on the half circle makes the frequency-three mode the unique maximum up to complex conjugation. The \(n=3\) degeneracy is handled separately. Taylor expansions give the stated parity-dependent lower-order terms. The standard-library checker independently constructs shortest paths and passes 298,986 supplementary finite assertions; these are not a substitute for the general proof.

## Originality
**PASS, with historical-priority qualification.** The 2018 foundational result of Obata and Zakiyyah already identifies the QEC with the second distance eigenvalue for transmission-regular graphs; that method is explicitly credited. Choudhury and Nandi's 2023 primary work treats QEC and graph distance spectra but focuses on trees, other specific families, and clusters. Azimi and Farrokhi's 2023 Möbius-ladder calculations concern electrical effective resistance and pseudoinverses, which do not coincide with shortest-path distance. The earlier distance-spectrum works of Indulal and Gutman (2008) and Aalipour et al. (2015) study different constructed graph families, not the exact Möbius geodesic formula. The general cubic-circulant adjacency spectrum also cannot by itself supply the claimed maximum of the nonlinear shortest-path distance matrix. No directly dominating claim was found in the inspected literature or targeted searches. An older, differently titled geodesic spectrum calculation may still exist, so uniqueness is not certified. The complete family formula, precise top eigenspace, and parity-sensitive asymptotics are the claimed contribution, not the general circulant method or known six-vertex special case.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** Möbius ladders are a basic vertex-transitive cubic family, so their graph-metric obstruction to squared-Hilbert embedding is a natural quantitative invariant. The sharp closed form is a complete all-order calculation, not a single numerical example, and the maximizing third Fourier mode gives an exact extremal certificate. Its two parity classes have distinct order-\(1/n\) corrections to linear growth, distinguishing graph metric geometry that the leading asymptotic suppresses. Such exact spectra provide benchmarks for quadratic embeddings and comparisons with the distinct electrical resistance metric.

## Remaining limitations
The result does not classify other generalized Möbius ladders, weighted or resistance metrics, or powered-distance snowflake exponents. The complete small case \(M_6=K_{3,3}\) is prior knowledge. While several close primary sources were inspected, searching cannot rule out all historical formulations. The finite checker is supplementary; no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
