# Exact random-generation law and competing thresholds for finite three-valued MV algebras

## Finding
Let \(L_3=\{0,\tfrac12,1\}\) be the three-element MV chain and \(L_2=\{0,1\}\) its Boolean subalgebra, equipped with \(x\oplus y=\min(1,x+y)\), \(\neg x=1-x\), and constant \(0\). For integers \(k,\ell\ge0\) with \(k+\ell\ge1\), set \(A_{k,\ell}=L_3^k\times L_2^\ell\). Put \((a)_{\underline b}=a(a-1)\cdots(a-b+1)\) for \(0\le b\le a\), \((a)_{\underline0}=1\), and set the expression to zero when \(b>a\).

**Exact random-generation theorem.** The number of ordered \(d\)-tuples generating all of \(A_{k,\ell}\) is
\[
G_d(k,\ell)=(3^d-2^d)_{\underline k}(2^d)_{\underline\ell}\qquad(d\ge0).
\]
Under independent uniformly sampled generators the exact success probability is
\[
P_d(k,\ell)=\frac{(3^d-2^d)_{\underline k}}{3^{dk}}\cdot\frac{(2^d)_{\underline\ell}}{2^{d\ell}}.
\]
Consequently the deterministic generating rank is the least integer \(d\ge0\) satisfying simultaneously \(k\le3^d-2^d\) and \(\ell\le2^d\).

**Two-mechanism Poisson law.** Consider any sequence \(d\to\infty\), \(k=k_d\), \(\ell=\ell_d\), with \(k_d(2/3)^d\to\lambda\in[0,\infty)\) and \(\ell_d(\ell_d-1)/2^{d+1}\to\mu\in[0,\infty)\). Then
\[
\lim_{d\to\infty}P_d(k_d,\ell_d)=e^{-\lambda-\mu}.
\]
More precisely, the number of three-valued factor profiles entirely contained in the Boolean cube converges in law to \(\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)\), and the number of pairs of equal Boolean-factor profiles converges to an **independent** \(\operatorname{Poisson}(\mu)\); collisions among three-valued-factor profiles vanish in probability. Hence random generation is governed by two distinct obstructions with different exponential scales.

These results also apply to finite algebras of disjoint alternatives in the **signature without a designated indeterminate constant**, through the established term equivalence with three-valued MV algebras. Term equivalence preserves generating sets and the uniform distribution on underlying tuples.

## Assumptions and scope
All algebras here are nontrivial finite direct products of the specified standard two- and three-element MV chains, in the variety generated by \(L_3\). Coordinates are distinguished but the elements are sampled uniformly from the entire algebra. An ordered \(d\)-tuple generates the full algebra if its closure under the MV operations and constant \(0\) is the entire direct product. The theorem includes \(d=0\): the only nontrivial algebra generated by no variables is \(L_2\). Under the established finite Boolean-algebra-plus-filter representation, every finite algebra in the three-valued MV variety has such a product decomposition; thus the formula covers all finite algebras in this variety, up to isomorphism.

The Poisson theorem requires only the two displayed finite limits and allows either component to be absent. It does not address infinitely many truth values, arbitrary finite MV chains beyond \(L_3\), algebras with a designated constant naming \(\tfrac12\), or sampling from a nonuniform distribution.

## Proof
**1. Coordinate profiles.** An ordered tuple \(g_1,\ldots,g_d\) determines a column \(v_j=(g_1(j),\ldots,g_d(j))\) at each factor. Every MV term evaluated at a Boolean column has Boolean output because \(L_2\) is a subalgebra. Therefore a three-valued factor whose profile lies in \(L_2^d\) cannot attain its middle value. If two factors of the same type have equal profiles, every term gives equal outputs in those factors, so they cannot be controlled independently. These are necessary obstructions.

**2. Exact interpolation.** Define the MV terms
\[
\Delta x=x\odot x=\max(0,2x-1),\qquad \nabla x=\neg\Delta(\neg x),\qquad x\odot y=\neg(\neg x\oplus\neg y).
\]
There are Boolean-valued one-variable terms \(\delta_0(x)=\Delta(\neg x)\), \(\delta_{1/2}(x)=\nabla x\wedge\nabla(\neg x)\), and \(\delta_1(x)=\Delta x\), taking value \(1\) at the indicated truth value and \(0\) at the other two. For any profile \(v\in L_3^d\), their meet \(e_v=\bigwedge_{i=1}^d\delta_{v_i}(x_i)\) is its Boolean-valued point indicator. (For \(d=0\), the only indicator is the constant \(1\).)

The terms \(e_v\) independently assign Boolean outputs to each distinct profile. If \(v\notin L_2^d\), choose an index \(i\) with \(v_i=\tfrac12\); the term \(e_v\wedge x_i\) is \(\tfrac12\) at \(v\) and zero at every other profile. The join of these one-profile terms (using a suitable subset of them and Boolean indicators) realizes every desired output in \(L_3\) at each of the distinct non-Boolean profiles, and in \(L_2\) at distinct Boolean profiles. Hence the stated necessary conditions are sufficient. A Boolean profile need not differ from a three-valued one because the latter contains a middle coordinate.

**3. Exact census.** There are \(3^d-2^d\) eligible profiles for three-valued factors, whose ordered selection must be injective; and there are \(2^d\) profiles for Boolean factors, whose ordered selection must also be injective. The two selections are independent. This proves \(G_d\), and division by the total \(3^{dk}2^{d\ell}\) gives \(P_d\). The rank condition follows immediately. The point-separating proof also verifies that there is no additional cross-type obstruction.

**4. Poisson limits and residual failures.** Write \(N=3^d\), \(B=2^d\). For each of the \(k\) three-valued factor profiles, the event of being Boolean has probability \(B/N=(2/3)^d\), independently. Thus the count \(H_d\) has a binomial law and converges to \(\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)\). The probability of any collision among all \(k\) three-valued profiles is bounded by \(\binom k2/N\). Since \(k(2/3)^d\) is bounded, this is \(O((3/4)^d)\) and tends to zero.

The \(\ell\) Boolean factor profiles are uniform independent draws from \(B\) profiles. Let \(J_d\) count unordered equal pairs. Its factorial moments converge to those of \(\operatorname{Poisson}(\mu)\): disjoint collections of \(r\) equalities contribute \((\ell)_{\underline{2r}}/(2^rB^r)\to\mu^r\), while connected overlapping collections are \(o(1)\), since \(\ell=O(B^{1/2})\) and a connected graph on \(v\ge3\) vertices costs \(O(\ell^v/B^{v-1})=o(1)\). If \(\mu=0\), Markov's inequality gives the same assertion. The profile families for the two factor types were sampled independently, so \(H_d\) and \(J_d\) are independent for each \(d\). Generation succeeds exactly when \(H_d=0\), \(J_d=0\), and there is no three-valued collision. Joint Poisson convergence and the vanishing collision error prove the asserted limit.

For the all-three-valued family \(L_3^k\), the least deterministic generator count grows as \(\log_3 k\), whereas random generation requires the larger logarithmic scale \(\log_{3/2}k\). For the all-Boolean family \(L_2^\ell\), the corresponding deterministic and random scales are \(\log_2\ell\) and \(2\log_2\ell\). These are threshold-scale comparisons, not assertions of an integer-valued sharp window for arbitrary sequences \(k,\ell\).

## Verification
The included `checker.py` exhaustively constructs MV subalgebra closures in all nontrivial products with at most three simple factors, using every ordered generator tuple of lengths \(0,1,2\), and compares generation against the profile criterion. It also checks the exact formula by enumeration and tests numerical convergence of the two-mechanism probability formula. Run `python checker.py` using Python 3 standard libraries; the expected terminal line is `ALL_CHECKS_OK`. These finite checks are corroborations and are not substituted for the all-size proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Vergottini, Ledda, and Graziani prove the 2026 representation of algebras of disjoint alternatives by Boolean algebras with a distinguished filter and their term equivalence with regular double Stone, three-valued Łukasiewicz, and three-valued MV algebras. These equivalences and the general finite-product background are assumed, not claimed here. Older work on finite and freely generated MV and three-valued algebras addresses their algebraic structure. The contribution assessed here is the **exact labelled ordered generating-tuple count and the independent two-obstruction Poisson transition for uniform random generators**. Classical occupancy and Poisson birthday estimates enter the proof, but do not alone identify the relevant logical profile restrictions.

The papers of Kravchenko, Mazur, and Petrenko (2010) and Sercombe (2023) treat probabilities of generators for associative algebras over arithmetic orders or finite fields, not this finite many-valued variety. Aguzzoli, Cabrer, and Marra analyze MV algebras freely generated by finite Kleene algebras in a different adjunction problem. The older three-valued closure-algebra paper of Abad and Díaz Varela is concerned with closure/interior expansions rather than the plain three-valued MV variety. None of the inspected statements supplies the two-parameter probability law proved here.

## Limitations
The underlying representation and term equivalence are known. Deterministic rank and the falling-factorial count are close consequences of the general theory of finite free three-valued algebras; novelty is not claimed for the existence of those free algebras. No historical uniqueness claim is made: an equivalent generator-count formula or two-mechanism threshold may appear in uninspected universal-algebra or random-generation literature. The 2026 motivating paper was inspected via its indexed mathematical text, not a complete downloaded PDF. The asymptotic describes uniform independent sampling, not biased, dependent, or adaptive generation. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
