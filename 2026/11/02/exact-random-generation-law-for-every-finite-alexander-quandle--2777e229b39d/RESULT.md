# Exact random-generation law for every finite Alexander quandle

## Finding

Let \(A\) be a finite abelian group, \(T\in\operatorname{Aut}(A)\), and let \(Q=\operatorname{Alex}(A,T)\) be the quandle with \(x*y=Tx+(1-T)y\). All groups are written additively. Fix any integer \(k\ge1\), and choose \(k\) elements independently and uniformly from \(Q\). The probability that these elements generate the whole quandle is an explicit product of (a) a coupon-collector probability for the nonconnected component quotient and (b) finite-field spanning probabilities for the connected summand. The law is valid for **all** finite Alexander quandles, including non-Latin ones; it determines their minimum generator number exactly.

Write \(U=1-T\). Choose an integer \(N\) large enough that \(\ker U^N=\ker U^{2N}\) and \(\operatorname{im}U^N=\operatorname{im}U^{2N}\), and set
\[
K=\ker U^N,\qquad C=\operatorname{im}U^N,\qquad A=K\oplus C.
\]
The endomorphism \(U\) is nilpotent on \(K\) and invertible on \(C\). Put \(c=|K/UK|=|A/UA|\).

For each prime \(p\mid |C|\), let \(C_p\) be the \(p\)-primary subgroup. Form the \(\mathbb F_p[t]\)-module \(M_p=C_p/pC_p\), with \(t\) acting as \(T\). For each monic irreducible \(f\) for which \(M_p/f(T)M_p\ne0\), set
\[
q_f=p^{\deg f},\qquad r_f=\dim_{\mathbb F_{q_f}}\big(M_p/f(T)M_p\big).
\]
The coefficient field on the right is canonically \(\mathbb F_p[t]/(f)\); no choice of root is required. Every such polynomial satisfies \(f(0)f(1)\ne0\). Let \(S(k,c)\) be the Stirling number of the second kind, with \(S(k,c)=0\) when \(k<c\).

**Exact probability theorem.** For all \(k\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{\Pr(\langle X_1,\ldots,X_k\rangle_*=Q)=
\frac{c!S(k,c)}{c^k}\prod_{p,f}\prod_{j=0}^{r_f-1}\big(1-q_f^{j-(k-1)}\big).}
\]
Empty products are one. The exact number of generating ordered tuples equals \(|A|^k\) times the displayed rational number. The least cardinality of a generating set is
\[
\boxed{d(Q)=\max\big(c,\,1+\max_{p,f}r_f\big),}
\]
where \(\max_{p,f}r_f=0\) if \(C=0\). The trivial one-element quandle has \(d(Q)=1\).

**The component graph is not enough.** Over \(\mathbb F_3^2\), both \(T=-I\) and \(T=\begin{psmallmatrix}2&1\\0&2\end{psmallmatrix}\) yield connected Alexander quandles and hence identical complete Cayley graphs on nine vertices. Nonetheless, the first requires three quandle generators (and has zero generating pairs), whereas the second needs only two (and a random pair generates with probability \(2/3\)). The module-head multiplicity, invisible to the Cayley graph, explains this sharp difference.

## Assumptions and scope

The underlying abelian group is finite and may be trivial, may have arbitrary primary exponents, and may have nontrivial \(p\)-torsion for any primes. The map \(T\) is invertible but \(1-T\) is not assumed invertible or semisimple. Generation means closure under the binary quandle operation; because the quandle is finite, this closure is automatically closed under inverse right translations. The probability is for ordered sampling **with replacement**, independently and uniformly. An empty generating family is excluded.

## Proof

**1. Generated-subquandle formula.** For a nonempty finite set \(S\subseteq A\), write \(H=\langle T^j(s-s'):s,s'\in S,\ j\in\mathbb Z\rangle_{\mathbb Z}\), its \(T\)-invariant difference subgroup. Let \(B=\langle S\rangle_*\). Since \(x*y=x+U(y-x)\), the union \(V=\bigcup_{s\in S}(s+UH)\) contains \(S\) and is closed under the quandle operation: if \(x=s+Uh\), \(y=s'+Uh'\), then \(y-x\in H\) and \(x*y\in s+UH\). Thus \(B\subseteq V\).

Let \(D\) be the additive subgroup generated by all differences \(x-y\) with \(x,y\in B\). Because \(B\subseteq s_0+H\) for a fixed \(s_0\in S\), one has \(D\subseteq H\). Conversely \(D\) contains all differences in \(S\), and is invariant under \(T\): for \(z\in B\), the difference \((x*z)-(y*z)=T(x-y)\) lies in \(D\); finiteness and invertibility of \(T\) imply \(TD=D\). Therefore \(H\subseteq D\), proving \(D=H\).

For each \(z\in B\), the right translation \(R_z(w)=w*z=Tw+Uz\) is injective on the finite set \(B\) and maps \(B\) into itself, hence permutes \(B\). For \(y,z\in B\), \(R_yR_z^{-1}\) is precisely translation by \(U(y-z)\). Such translations therefore preserve \(B\). Since the differences \(y-z\) generate \(H\), every translation by \(UH\) preserves \(B\), giving \(s+UH\subseteq B\) for each \(s\in S\). Consequently,
\[
\boxed{\langle S\rangle_*=\bigcup_{s\in S}(s+UH).}
\]
This identity also shows why hitting one representative from each full-quandle orbit is necessary but not in general sufficient.

**2. Finite Fitting decomposition.** The kernel and image chains of the endomorphism \(U\) stabilize on the finite group \(A\). For a sufficiently large exponent \(N\), \(A=K\oplus C\), where \(K=\ker U^N\), \(C=\operatorname{im}U^N\). These summands are stable under \(U\) and \(T=1-U\), and \(U|_C\) is a permutation. The decomposition is canonical. Every \(T\)-stable additive subgroup \(H\le A\) itself splits as \(H=(H\cap K)\oplus(H\cap C)\). To see this, the Fitting decomposition of \(U|_H\) has kernel \(H\cap K\) and image \(U^N H\). Since \(U|_C\) has finite order, every \(x\in H\cap C\) has a positive \(U\)-power preimage \(U^{m}(x)\in H\cap C\), so \(U^N H=H\cap C\).

The cosets of \(UA\) are exactly those of \(UK\) in the \(K\)-coordinate because \(UA=UK\oplus C\). Hence their number is \(c\).

**3. Exact generation criterion.** Let \(S\) be the sampled tuple's underlying set. If it generates \(Q\), it meets all \(c\) cosets of \(A/UA\), because the operation preserves the coset of its left operand. Moreover the full difference subgroup is \(A\), so the \(C\)-projection of \(H\) is \(C\).

Conversely assume \(S\) meets every class of \(K/UK\) and \(H\cap C=C\). Fix \(s_0\in S\). Differences between the \(K\)-projections of the sampled elements span \(K/UK\) as an additive group: they include a representative of each coset after translation by the fixed class of \(s_0\). Thus \((H\cap K)+UK=K\). The quotient \(K/(H\cap K)\) is a finite group on which \(U\) is both surjective and nilpotent, so this quotient is zero. Therefore \(H\cap K=K\) and \(H=A\). By part 1, \(\langle S\rangle_*=\bigcup_{s\in S}(s+UA)=A\). Hence generation is equivalent to the independent-looking pair of conditions:

(i) every class of \(K/UK\) is sampled; (ii) the differences of the sampled \(C\)-coordinates generate \(C\) as a \(\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]\)-module, with \(t\) acting as \(T\).

**4. Independence and exact probabilities.** Uniform sampling from \(A=K\oplus C\) makes the two coordinates independent and uniform. The classes of the \(K\)-coordinates are independent uniform samples from a set of \(c\) elements. Their chance of covering all classes in \(k\) draws is \(c!S(k,c)/c^k\).

If the independent uniform \(C\)-coordinates are \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_k\), then \(Y_2-Y_1,\ldots,Y_k-Y_1\) are \(k-1\) independent uniform elements of \(C\). Their generated \(\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]\)-submodule is \(H\cap C\). A family generates the finite module \(C\) exactly when it generates every simple quotient: otherwise its proper generated submodule has a nonzero simple quotient. Such quotients have coefficient fields \(\mathbb F_p[t]/(f)\) and are detected by the natural surjections onto \(M_p/f(T)M_p\). Chinese remainder decomposition of \(M_p\) into primary polynomial factors shows that the joint map from \(C\) onto the direct sum of these quotients is surjective. Therefore samples induce independent uniform vectors in all \(\mathbb F_{q_f}^{r_f}\). The probability that \(k-1\) such vectors span \(\mathbb F_{q_f}^{r_f}\) is
\[
\prod_{j=0}^{r_f-1}\bigl(1-q_f^{j-(k-1)}\bigr).
\]
Multiplying the independent coverage and spanning factors proves the formula.

**5. Minimum generators.** The coverage factor is positive if and only if \(k\ge c\). Every finite-field spanning factor is positive if and only if \(k-1\ge r_f\). Thus a generating tuple of length \(k\) exists precisely for \(k\ge\max(c,1+\max r_f)\). The displayed rank formula follows.

## Verification

An independent exhaustive checker constructs each finite quandle's multiplication table, starts from every ordered tuple at the listed lengths, iterates the binary operation until the generated subset stabilizes, and counts those whose closure is all of \(A\). The checker does **not** use the Fitting decomposition or module-head criterion internally. It verified all tested tuples for the following families:

| Quandle | \(k\) | Exact generating probability |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Trivial quandle on \(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\) | 4 | \(3/32\) |
| Core quandle on \(\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z\) | 2, 3 | \(1/2,\ 3/4\) |
| Multiplier \(2\) on \(\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z\) | 2, 3 | \(2/3,\ 8/9\) |
| Unipotent Jordan action on \(\mathbb F_3^2\) | 2, 3 | \(0,\ 2/9\) |
| Diagonal action \(\mathrm{diag}(1,2)\) on \(\mathbb F_3^2\) | 2, 3 | \(0,\ 16/81\) |
| Scalar action \(-I\) on \(\mathbb F_3^2\) | 2, 3 | \(0,\ 16/27\) |
| Jordan action with eigenvalue \(-1\) on \(\mathbb F_3^2\) | 2, 3 | \(2/3,\ 8/9\) |
| Irreducible quadratic action on \(\mathbb F_2^2\) | 2, 3 | \(3/4,\ 15/16\) |
| Mixed action on \(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\) | 2, 3 | \(1/3,\ 2/3\) |

For the trivial four-element quandle, the checker also verified zero generating tuples at lengths 1, 2, and 3. The source checker is `check_alexander.py`, runnable with the standard Python library, and its recorded output is `verification_output.txt`. It finishes with `VERIFY_OK`. These finite tests corroborate but do not replace the all-finite-module proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Dolžan and Oliynyk (arXiv:2604.17011v1, April 18, 2026), Theorem 3.5, prove that the right Cayley graph of a finite Alexander quandle has components given by cosets of \(UA\). Their statement concerns the graph with **all possible right operands**, not whether a **random sampled set** generates as a quandle; the graph component count alone cannot establish the module-spanning factor. The present result uses that component picture but adds the exact simultaneous coverage-and-spanning criterion.

Jedlička, Pilitowska, Stanovský, and Zamojska-Dzienio (arXiv:1708.02362v2, 2018), study quasi-affine subquandles and their displacement groups; their Section 2 identifies \(UA\) as the displacement group and its cosets as orbits. This is an important structural antecedent, not an exact random tuple probability law. Jedlička, Pilitowska, and Zamojska-Dzienio's *Free medial quandles* (2017), Proposition 2.5, describes the displacement group of a generated medial quandle in terms of generating elements. It supplies a related general mechanism, but the conditional finite-field rank factors and disconnected coupon-collector decomposition were not found in the material inspected, and implicit coverage elsewhere in the article remains a priority risk.

Ta's January 20, 2026 note *Structure theory of commutative quandles and medial Latin quandles*, especially Sections 5–6, describes connected/Latin medial quandles and free affine modules; its free-object statement explains the \(k-1\) phenomenon for Latin module generators. The present theorem also allows arbitrary non-Latin Alexander quandles and computes an exact law and minimal generator count across their component quotient.

Ferman, Nowik, and Teicher (arXiv:0811.4211) establish a prime-order two-generator assertion, consistent with the case where the unique module-head dimension is one. Saki and Kiani (DOI:10.1080/00927872.2020.1797065) study lattices of subquandles of Takasaki quandles, a specialized \(T=-1\) family; a complete text comparison was not available. This is a residual historical-coverage risk, especially for the core-quandle examples, not a verified broader formula. No claim is made that failure to locate an explicit exact generating-probability theorem proves absolute novelty.

## Limitations

The result applies only to finite abelian-group Alexander quandles with an automorphism \(T\), not generalized Alexander quandles over nonabelian groups, arbitrary racks, or infinite modules. It assumes independent uniform sampling with replacement. The formula gives the full-generation probability and minimum generator number, not the distribution of all proper generated subquandles. The proof is fully mathematical; exhaustive finite computations alone would not establish the universal statement. A potentially relevant inaccessible subquandle-lattice paper and the possibility that older generation theory implicitly yields the product remain historical-priority risks. Independent review has not been performed.
