# Review of exact rank-two probability spectra for independent random successor orders

## Correctness

PASS. The mixed factorial-moment proof tracks all order-specific directed edge constraints, treats overlapping geometric edges as lower-order contributions, and yields *joint*, not merely marginal, rate-one Poisson limits. The local-profile argument explicitly includes both path endpoints and the two orientations of each rare shared edge and bounds all unwanted coincidences. The quantifier-rank-two restriction justifies reducing closed formulas to Boolean functions of realized rooted pair-type sets. The Bernstein basis and transcendence of \(e^{-1}\) establish the exact spectrum cardinality. The checker validates exhaustive small-domain collision distributions and clean-profile instances but is not substituted for proof.

## Originality

PASS relative to inspected sources and accessible deduplication history. The 2026 Jeż–Michaliszyn–Witkowski paper concerns finite satisfiability; Michaliszyn–Witkowski concerns general satisfiability; Tóth et al. concerns weighted counts. Diaconis–Evans–Graham prove the classical one-pair marginal Poisson law, and Bender–Canfield prove a general unmarked overlap theorem. Those older results are acknowledged explicitly, not renamed. None of their inspected statements supplies the full orientation-resolved multiorder joint law *and* the complete quantifier-rank-two Boolean event algebra with its exact number of distinct limits. Relevant historical priority risk remains, particularly from older stochastic finite-model theory and multivariate permutation adjacency studies.

## Value

PASS. This is a complete spectrum theorem for a natural syntactically bounded fragment over exactly the path semantics motivating current two-variable successor research. It identifies a sharp logical zero-one transition between one and two independent successors, and the rapidly growing but exactly enumerable rank-two limit spectrum. The rank limitation is motivated by the precise locality boundary at which rare collisions are visible but multiplicities are not; no arbitrary finite numerical slice is used.
