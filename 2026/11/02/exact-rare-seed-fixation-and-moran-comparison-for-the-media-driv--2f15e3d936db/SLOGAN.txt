In a complete-graph q-voter model where nonunanimous panels can trigger media adoption, any fixed number \(m\) of initial media supporters reaches complete consensus with limiting probability \(1-(pq)^{-m}\) when \(pq>1\), and with probability tending to zero otherwise. The finite-population fixation probability also dominates the Moran formula with effective advantage \(pq\) when \(pq>1\); even perfect media influence leaves a single supporter with a limiting failure probability \(1/q\).
