# Exact rational first-order probability spectrum of one-descent permutations

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal G_n\) be the set of permutations of \([n]\) with at most one descent. Equip a permutation with its two strict linear orders: position order \(<_{p}\) and value order \(<_{v}\). Sample uniformly from \(\mathcal G_n\). For every first-order sentence \(\varphi\) in these two relations, the limit \(\lim_{n\to\infty}\Pr(\mathcal G_n\models\varphi)\) exists and belongs to \(\mathbb Q\). Conversely, for every pair of integers \(b\ge1\) and \(0\le a\le b\), a constructible first-order sentence has limiting probability exactly \(a/b\). Thus the full limit-probability spectrum is \(\mathbb Q\cap[0,1]\).

## Assumptions and scope
A descent is an adjacent position pair \(i,i+1\) for which the entry at \(i\) exceeds that at \(i+1\). The two orders are on the positions/entries as a common domain, and a first-order sentence is fixed independently of \(n\). No claim is made for permutations having more than one descent, for a different sampling distribution, or for monadic second-order logic. The empty word can be ignored; \(n\ge1\).

## Proof
**1. Encoding and probability comparison.** For a bit word \(u\in\{0,1\}^n\), form a permutation \(\pi(u)\) by listing in increasing order first the values whose bit is \(1\), then those whose bit is \(0\). There is at most one descent. A nonidentity permutation with at most one descent has a unique descent, and its two increasing blocks uniquely recover \(u\) in *value order*: bit \(1\) means membership in its first block. The only noninjectivity is that the \(n+1\) initial-segment subsets yield the identity permutation. Therefore \(|\mathcal G_n|=2^n-n\). The push-forward of uniform fair bit words to permutations differs in total variation from uniform measure on \(\mathcal G_n\) by at most \((n+1)2^{-n}\).

On a nonidentity permutation, the unique descent is first-order definable as the unique pair adjacent in \(<_p\) and decreasing in \(<_v\). The unary membership predicate for its first block is first-order definable from the earlier member of that pair. The order of bit positions is exactly \(<_v\). Conversely, on any binary word the position order induced by \(\pi(u)\) is first-order definable from \(<_v\) and the bit predicate: first all \(1\) positions in value order, then all \(0\) positions in value order. Thus every first-order property of the permutation pulls back to a first-order property of binary words, and every first-order bit-word property lifts to a first-order permutation property away from the exceptional identity. This logical correspondence and the vanishing total-variation error reduce the probability spectrum to that of independent fair binary words with their usual order and a unary letter predicate.

**2. Rational limits for first-order binary-word properties.** By the classical finite-automata characterization of first-order definable languages, a sentence has a complete deterministic finite automaton with aperiodic transition monoid: for some common \(N\), each word transformation \(T_w\) satisfies \(T_w^N=T_w^{N+1}\). Let \(T_0,T_1\) denote its two letter-transition matrices and \(P=(T_0+T_1)/2\). This is a finite rational stochastic matrix, and the acceptance probability for length \(n\) is \(e_{q_0}P^n\mathbf1_F\). Each closed communicating class of \(P\) is aperiodic. Indeed, if such a class had period \(d>1\), its cyclic partition would be advanced by every outgoing edge, hence by the deterministic transition for the letter \(0\) alone; repeated \(0\)-transitions could not become stationary, contradicting \(T_0^N=T_0^{N+1}\). Consequently \(P^n\) converges. Its limit has rational entries, as transient absorption probabilities and invariant probabilities of finite irreducible chains solve finite linear systems with rational coefficients. Therefore every first-order word-sentence limit exists and is rational. The transfer in Step 1 proves the upper bound for \(\mathcal G_n\).

**3. Explicit realization of every rational.** Fix integers \(b\ge1\) and \(0\le a\le b\). Put \(d=\lceil\log_2 b\rceil\), \(K=d+1\), and choose distinct binary words \(s_1,\ldots,s_b\) of common length \(d\). (For \(b=1\), take the empty word.) Define the codewords \(w_i=0^K1s_i1^K\), each of length \(L=2K+d+1\). No nonempty proper suffix of any \(w_i\) is a prefix of any \(w_j\): suffixes of length at most \(K\) are all ones, whereas longer proper suffixes cannot begin with \(K\) zeros because the variable middle block has length \(d<K\). Hence this is a cross-bifix-free family.

For a uniformly random bit word of length \(n\), consider the earliest occurrence of any of the \(b\) codewords. Fix any endpoint \(t\ge L\) of that first occurrence. Replacing its terminal length-\(L\) codeword \(w_i\) by \(w_j\) is a bijection between histories first terminating with types \(i\) and \(j\): no earlier occurrence can be introduced across the replacement boundary, by cross-bifix-freeness. Thus all \(b\) types have *equal unconditional probability* of being the first hit. The probability that no hit appears is at most \((1-b2^{-L})^{\lfloor n/L\rfloor}\), by independent nonoverlapping blocks. Hence the event that the first type belongs to \(\{1,\ldots,a\}\) has probability tending to \(a/b\). This event is first-order: a fixed-length pattern is definable using adjacent elements in the word order, and one can quantify its earliest starting position and the finite disjunction of accepted types. Translate it to a first-order sentence of the two permutation orders using Step 1.

The event on words has probability \(\frac ab(1-\Pr(\text{no hit}))\); its associated permutation sentence differs in probability by at most the total-variation error. This also proves the explicit error bound in the claim.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone Python checker tests all proper prefix/suffix overlaps for \(1\le b\le8\), tests exact first-hit type symmetry on finite binary strings in bounded cases, enumerates all binary encodings for \(1\le n\le7\), and checks their at-most-one-descent property, the exact count \(2^n-n\), and recovery of the encoded word from every nonidentity permutation. It prints `ALL_CHECKS_OK`. The infinite statement follows from the preceding proof, not from finite enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work
Markström and Räty (2026) established first-order convergence for every small permutation class via regular-language techniques, which already covers the **existence** of limits here. Their primary text was inspected at its introduction, main theorem, automata lemma, and proof of the convergence theorem; no exact rational-probability spectrum for the one-descent class is stated there. The present result strengthens that existence claim for this concrete class by determining *all* attainable limit values and providing explicit formulas for witnessing sentences. Earlier work on logical limit laws for layered permutations (Braunfeld and Kukla, 2022) and related complete-spectrum arguments with cross-bifix-free words supplies an important methodological precedent; the proof here uses a different natural class, whose definable descent identifies the binary word along value order. Cross-bifix-free codes themselves are standard and are not claimed as a new invention. Smith's 2016 work on permutations with fixed descent number concerns their containment posets, not logical probability spectra.

## Limitations
This is a complete limiting spectrum, not a finite-\(n\) classification of all first-order sentence probabilities. The construction of each realizing sentence may be long as its denominator increases. No claim is made about novelty of the individual automata or cross-bifix-free code lemmas, and an equivalent one-descent formulation elsewhere remains a historical-priority risk. No independent audit has been performed.
