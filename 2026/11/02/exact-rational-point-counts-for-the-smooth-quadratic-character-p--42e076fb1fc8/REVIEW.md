## Correctness — PASS
For every prime power \(q=p^r\), \(p\ge5\), the quadratic character identifies the polynomial equation over the defining field with an integer zero-sum condition, because four terms lie between \(-4\) and \(4\). Orthogonality reduces the affine count to an exact Laurent coefficient of Fourier factors. Splitting into \(\chi(-1)=\pm1\), the quadratic Gauss-sum identity gives two symbolic polynomials, and exact coefficient extraction proves the point-count formulas. The coordinate-axis argument is a complete three-term sign classification. The derivative argument excludes geometric singularities. The accompanying program independently reproduces the identities over \(\mathbb Q[q,T,T^{-1}]\) and samples prime and extension fields. The symbolic proof, not the finite checks, covers all \(q\).

## Mathematical value — PASS
The cardinality of the rational point set is the basic quantitative invariant of a blocking curve. The sharp, closed formulas distinguish two characteristic-parity classes across an unbounded family and locate the construction at asymptotic density \(3/8\) of the projective plane. This quantifies the efficiency and size of a distinguished published smooth-blocking construction beyond binary blocking/nonblocking existence, and the axis intersection counts provide an independent arithmetic boundary. The precise invariant is natural in finite geometry; no downstream theorem is assumed or claimed.
