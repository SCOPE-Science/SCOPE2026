# Scientific review: exact cusp and edge phases

## Correctness — PASS
The stochastic model and all parameter quantifiers appear in `RESULT.md`. Its proof derives the distributional forward equations with the correct reset source of \(r/2\) in each orientation. Zero outgoing edge flux and reflection symmetry give \(J(0+)=r/2\). The exact origin slope then follows from the orientation-imbalance equation, without an undetermined normalization constant. At the edge, a positive-current comparison bounds the integrating factor and proves a **positive finite** leading coefficient; integration of the inward-current ODE gives the independent ratio \(\lambda/(r+\lambda)\). The peak coexistence criterion and its \(1/\sqrt2\) shear-thinning threshold follow by solving two simultaneous strict inequalities. The no-reset comparison follows by differentiating the standard zero-current identity. Seven numerical parameter sets reproduce the origin slope, endpoint power, and flux ratio (21 finite comparisons). These checks are supportive rather than a proof of asymptotic statements.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, not independently certified)
The closest primary paper, Howlader–Mondal–Das (2026), arXiv:2606.00560, gives the model and numerical cusp/edge plots but no exact reset-origin derivative or shear-thinning coexistence-amplitude boundary. Dhar et al. (2018), arXiv:1811.03808, determine **zero-reset** telegraph steady states and boundary exponents; their zero-current formula cannot give the nonzero reset current \(J(0+)=r/2\). The 2025 tangent-drag paper also treats the reset-free velocity distribution. Olsen–Löwen (2024), arXiv:2401.12685, develops general velocity-reset transport and diffusivity formulas, but the accessible abstract does not address this stationary two-orientation singularity phase diagram. The result is not a claim that the kinetic master equation or basic Frobenius method is new. Potential broader general piecewise-deterministic Markov process boundary theorems and the incompletely inspected 2024 source remain priority risks.

## Value — PASS
The 2026 motivating article reports numerical transitions in stationary velocity profiles without analytically determining the transition rates. The exact origin slope quantifies the observed cusp and distinguishes maxima from minima. The edge exponent predicts a different transition; combining these identifies a physically distinguishable coexistence regime and the unexpected sharp shear-thinning force-amplitude boundary \(\Sigma=1/\sqrt2\). The theorem also proves that an arbitrarily weak reset protocol reverses the *local* central morphology when \(1/2<\lambda<1\), while not asserting a discontinuity of the global probability distribution. These are nontrivial shape effects relevant to active-particle velocity statistics rather than arbitrary parameter slices.

## Limitations and literature relationship
The proof treats monotone smooth odd drag, independent Poisson velocity resetting without orientation resetting, and finite simple velocity endpoints. It does not establish an exact global count of interior modes, general asymmetric forcing, stochastic diffusion in velocity, or finite-time convergence rates. The 2024 general transport source was only available in summary form during this review, so that source's detailed contents were not excluded by a whole-document claim. A broader unpublished or unlocated theorem may imply some formulas; independent mathematical priority certification remains outstanding.
