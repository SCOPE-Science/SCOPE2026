# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The proof explicitly uses the exact plateau \((-1/4,1/4)\), normalized uniform starting distribution and *first genuine intervention*, avoiding any unsupported inference about subsequent orbits. Two independent fair sign symbols identify an intervention, and an exact run factorization follows because distinct runs use disjoint bits. The strict merging identity and balancing argument establish sharp bounds and all equality cases. The exact-rational script recomputes direct first-event fractions for every schedule through horizon eight; the unlimited quantifiers rely on the proof rather than on finite tests.

## Originality

PASS within inspected evidence. Silva (2023, DOI: 10.3390/math11122677) introduced precisely the time-dependent flat-top tent model and analyzed attractors; the present finding neither reclaims that definition nor the well-known full-shift coding. Silva (2019, DOI: 10.3934/dcdsb.2018243) gives periodic-attractor existence, while Silva and Silva (2025, DOI: 10.12775/TMNA.2024.023) treat kneading and period incrementing. The observed statements do not supply the run-factorized probability and budget-optimal control placements. A generic word-avoidance/scheduling theorem, the inaccessible portions of the later paper, and parts of older optimal-limiter literature remain genuine originality risks; search absence is not a proof of priority.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The central control-design variable is *when* to spend a fixed number of limiter applications. The theorem settles the finite-horizon measure objective for every binary pattern, gives equality cases and an exponentially growing contrast between clustered and widely separated use, and proves eventual intervention for every infinitely repeated control pattern even with zero limiting frequency. These are quantitative conclusions not implied by existence of a periodic attractor; the dyadic plateau is motivated by exact symbolic tractability.

## Scope and limitations

Only deterministic schedules, the \(u=1/2\) limiter, Lebesgue-uniform starting points and the first actual truncation are classified. No claim about how post-truncation trajectories stabilize is made. Primary-material access was partial for a later paper and an older relevant generic control source; the completed comparison records those limits and does not infer noncoverage merely from inaccessible text.
