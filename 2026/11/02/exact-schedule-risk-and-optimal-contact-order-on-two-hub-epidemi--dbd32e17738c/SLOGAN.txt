For a one-contact two-hub epidemic tree with \(a\) and \(b\) leaves on its two hubs, moving the hub contact from first/last to between the two opposing leaf-contact blocks raises the expected outbreak by exactly \(abq^3/(a+b+2)\), the largest possible timing effect. Under a uniformly random contact order, the entire schedule-dependent mean-outbreak law and its variance are explicit, distinguishing calendar uncertainty from infection randomness.
