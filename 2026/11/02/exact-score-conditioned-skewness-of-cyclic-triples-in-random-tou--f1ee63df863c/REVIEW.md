# Review of exact score-conditioned skewness of cyclic triples

## Correctness
PASS. The edge-sign expansion is a finite identity for every fair tournament and each fixed outneighborhood. Distinct two-edge wedge monomials are orthogonal. For the third moment, three wedges survive only on a three-star or triangle of the original complete graph, with signs checked by antisymmetry; the other mixed contribution comes exactly from two marked-cut edges sharing an endpoint. The resulting formulas agree with direct orientation enumeration over all conditioning degrees through seven vertices. The standardized-skewness derivative reduces to a strictly positive explicit factor for the entire claimed domain. No finite computation is used as a substitute for the general proof.

## Originality
PASS, with residual historical-priority risk. Komarov and Mackey, *On the number of 5-cycles in a tournament* (arXiv:1410.6828), address a deterministic five-cycle edge-score identity and historical extremal results for cyclic triples, not finite conditional skewness. Coregliano and Razborov, *On the Density of Transitive Tournaments* (arXiv:1501.04074), study large-order extremal density and quasirandomness, not conditional third centered moments. Andersson (1998) investigates general tournament-subgraph count asymptotics; its abstract is accessible, but its full article was not verified, leaving implicit coverage possible. A known unconditional fourth-cumulant identity and unconditional cycle-count covariance formulas do not determine the score-conditioned third cumulant. The central new statement is the all-degree third-cumulant formula and strict normalized-skewness balance law, not classical score enumeration or basic variance counting.

## Value
PASS. The third centered moment measures the finite-sample asymmetry of a standard nontransitivity count in complete paired comparisons. Its sign classification is uniform across all conditioned scores, and the standardized magnitude has a sharp extremal rule rather than merely a moment expression. Pairwise independence of individual cyclic-triple indicators does not expose these higher-order interactions. The result is a mathematically motivated finite-sample distributional characterization, without an unsupported claim of improved inference.

## Limitations
Only independent fair orientations and a predetermined marked vertex are covered. No whole-law stochastic order, biased-tournament generalization or independent scientific validation is asserted. The earlier broad asymptotic literature may contain an implicit derivation not found in accessible material.
