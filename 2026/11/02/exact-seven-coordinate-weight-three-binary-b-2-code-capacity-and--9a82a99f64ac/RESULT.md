# Exact seven-coordinate weight-three binary \(B_2\) code capacity and double-Fano rigidity

## Finding

Consider a set \(\mathcal C\subseteq\{0,1\}^7\) in which every word has Hamming weight exactly \(3\). Call it a binary \(B_2\) code if the integer coordinatewise sums \(u+v\), for all unordered pairs of **different** words \(\{u,v\}\subseteq\mathcal C\), are pairwise different. Then

\[
\max_{\mathcal C\;B_2}|\mathcal C|=14.
\]

There are exactly \(120\) distinct optimal labeled codebooks, all equivalent under coordinate permutations. Their supports are precisely the blocks of the simple \(2\)-\((7,3,2)\) designs (double Fano planes). In particular, each unordered pair of coordinate positions occurs in exactly two weight-three codewords, and every optimizer is a coordinate permutation of the fourteen cyclic triples obtained from \(\{0,1,3\}\) and \(\{0,2,3\}\) by addition modulo \(7\).

## Assumptions and scope

The seven coordinates are labeled \(0,1,\ldots,6\). Each codeword is the characteristic vector of a three-element subset of these labels. Sums are over the integers, not over the binary field. Pairwise distinctness is required for two different *unordered* input pairs, and repeated input words are excluded. Codebooks themselves contain no repeated words. The assertion is at exactly seven coordinates and weight three, and does not assert analogous optima at other lengths or weights.

## Proof

**1. Equivalent finite constraint system.** There are exactly \(\binom73=35\) possible codewords, indexed by three-element sets. Bucket each of the \(\binom{35}{2}=595\) unordered *distinct-word* pairs according to their seven-component integer sum. Two different pairs with equal sum cannot share a codeword: cancelling their common word would force the remaining words equal. Thus a collision uses four different candidate words. Form all four-subset constraints from distinct equal-sum pairs, deduplicate, and obtain precisely \(630\) forbidden quadruples. A codebook is \(B_2\) if and only if its candidate-index set contains none of these quadruples. The accompanying \(\texttt{verify.cpp}\) regenerates the constraints directly from all \(595\) pairs and verifies their count; no forbidden constraints are supplied as an external table.

**2. A valid pruning bound.** Fix coordinate \(x\). Associate with every selected triple containing \(x\) an edge between its two other coordinates, making a simple graph on six vertices. A four-cycle with successive edges \(ab,bc,cd,da\) would yield equal sums for the two distinct unordered pairs \(\{xab,xcd\}\) and \(\{xbc,xda\}\). Hence the link graph is four-cycle-free. The maximum size of a \(C_4\)-free graph on six labeled vertices is \(7\): this is verified by examining all \(2^{15}\) undirected simple graphs on six vertices and rejecting any pair of vertices with two common neighbors. Consequently each coordinate lies in at most seven selected triples. This restriction is necessary, not an assumed characterization.

**3. Complete finite upper bound and classification.** Coordinate permutations act transitively on the \(35\) possible triples. Any codebook of size at least \(14\) therefore can be relabeled to include \(\{0,1,2\}\). The standalone branch-and-bound checker \(\texttt{verify.cpp}\) exhausts such codebooks. Its state comprises disjoint included and excluded candidate-triple masks. It branches on a not-yet-decided candidate, so every valid completion is in exactly one descendant branch. The only automatic exclusions are the complement of three already included members of one forbidden quadruple, and additional triples incident with any coordinate already at its rigorous seven-triple cap. A branch is pruned if fewer candidates remain than the desired size. At the target of \(15\), it visits \(242909\) states and finds no valid code. At the target of \(14\), it visits \(432937\) states and finds exactly \(48\) valid codes containing the fixed triple; *every one* has exactly two selected triples on each of the \(21\) coordinate pairs. These are exhaustive finite proofs because all candidate triples, all integer-sum collisions and all search branches are generated by the provided executable algorithms, without a heuristic cutoff, external integer-programming solver or probabilistic acceptance condition.

**4. Existence and total number.** The fourteen triples formed by the seven translates of \(\{0,1,3\}\) and seven translates of \(\{0,2,3\}\) constitute a valid \(B_2\) codebook, checked directly by a separately implemented Python integer-sum comparator. Thus the maximum is \(14\). The labeled optimizer count follows by double-counting the pairs consisting of an optimizer and one of its contained triples: symmetry gives \(35\cdot48/14=120\). The \(7!\) coordinate relabelings of the explicit cyclic codebook form an orbit of exactly \(120\) distinct codebooks. Therefore this orbit exhausts all optimizers and each is a double Fano plane. Conversely, the well-known single isomorphism class of simple double Fano planes coincides with this orbit, so every such design meets the \(B_2\) condition. The coordinate automorphism group of the cyclic witness has size \(5040/120=42\).

## Verification

From the directory containing both packaged source files, compile and run:

```sh
c++ -O3 -std=c++17 verify.cpp -o verify
./verify
python3 verify_orbit.py
```

The deterministic expected terminal lines are:

```text
VERIFY_OK triples=35 forbidden_quadruples=630 C4free_six_max=7 size15_nodes=242909 size15_solutions=0 size14_nodes=432937 size14_containing_012=48 all_48_are_double_Fano=1 labeled_optima=120 witness_orbit=120 max_size=14
VERIFY_ORBIT_OK labeled=120 all_sum_unique=1 all_pair_incidence_two=1
```

The two programs are standalone and use only C++17 and Python standard libraries. The first is an exhaustive finite certificate for the upper bound, classification and count; the second independently computes all \(120\) codebooks in the explicit orbit and independently checks all their \(91\) pair sums. A failing assertion or incorrect enumerated count terminates with a nonzero exit status. The classification is restricted to this finite parameter set: no claim about all seven-coordinate binary codes of unrestricted weights is made.

## Relationship to prior work

Sima, Li, Shomorony and Milenkovic (2023) study constant-weight binary \(B_2\) sequences and give information-theoretic bounds and constructions for larger parameters. The present fixed seven-coordinate, weight-three exact optimum and exhaustive characterization are not stated in the inspected version of that paper. Meszka and Rosa (2018) previously established that simple double Fano planes form one isomorphism class, with \(120\) labeled designs; **the pre-existing design enumeration is not claimed as new**. The result here links that design class *exactly* to the finite optimum of the binary \(B_2\) uniqueness-of-pair-sums problem, with an independent exhaustive upper-bound certificate. Other combinatorial code classifications may conceivably imply this correspondence under different terminology.

## Limitations

The upper bound is computer-assisted, albeit exhaustive and fully reproducible from source. The proof relies on the verifiable finite search and is not a short pen-and-paper extremal argument. The result concerns length \(7\), constant weight \(3\), and distinct input pairs; extension to other lengths, weights, or different error models is not established. Originality comparisons did not exhaust every potentially relevant older code or hypergraph article; an unindexed equivalent characterization remains possible. The pre-existing double Fano plane classification and its count are explicitly attributed.
