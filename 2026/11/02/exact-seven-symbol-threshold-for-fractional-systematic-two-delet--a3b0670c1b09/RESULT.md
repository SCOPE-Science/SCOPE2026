# Exact seven-symbol threshold for fractional systematic two-deletion packing

## Finding

Let \(\Sigma\) be an alphabet of integer cardinality \(q\ge2\). Write \(\mathsf D_2(w)\) for the **set of distinct ordered length-three subsequences** of a length-five word \(w\in\Sigma^5\). Consider the rational polytope in variables \(x_w\ge0\):

\[
\sum_{w\in\Sigma^5:\,w_1w_2=ab}x_w=1
\qquad\text{for every }(a,b)\in\Sigma^2,
\]
\[
\sum_{w\in\Sigma^5:\,y\in\mathsf D_2(w)}x_w\le1
\qquad\text{for every }y\in\Sigma^3.
\]

**Theorem.** This polytope is nonempty **if and only if** \(q\ge7\). For every such \(q\), it has an explicit symbol-permutation-invariant rational point whose load on an output \(y\) depends only on its equality pattern and equals

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
\text{pattern}&AAA&AAB&ABA&ABB&ABC\\\hline
\text{load}&1&1&4/5&1&5/(q-2).
\end{array}
\]

Here distinct letters in a pattern denote distinct actual symbols. In particular, at \(q=7\) all output families except \(ABA\) are saturated. **This is a fractional relaxation, not the existence of a literal-systematic deletion-correcting code with seven symbols.**

## Assumptions and scope

Coordinates of codeword candidates are numbered \(1,\ldots,5\); the first two are the unaltered information symbols, and the last three are exposed to deletion along with the first two. The output constraint treats each distinct length-three descendant only once, irrespective of the number of deletion patterns producing it. Every genuine systematic code correcting two arbitrary deletions produces a feasible integral \(0\)-\(1\) point, but the converse is not asserted. The polytope imposes capacities only for outputs after **exactly two** deletions. Adding fractional capacity constraints at other output lengths can define a stronger relaxation, although for genuine equal-length integral codes disjoint exact-two-deletion shadows already imply correction of fewer deletions.

## Proof

**Necessity.** Give each output word \(y\) a nonnegative weight depending on its equality pattern:

\[
\omega(AAA)=14,\quad\omega(AAB)=3,\quad
\omega(ABA)=2,\quad\omega(ABB)=5,\quad\omega(ABC)=2.
\]

For every length-five \(w\), the weight \(\sum_{y\in\mathsf D_2(w)}\omega(y)\) is at least \(14\) when \(w_1=w_2\), and at least \(19\) when \(w_1\ne w_2\). These are **all-parameter** finite inequalities: relabeling the symbols does not change any term. There are precisely \(52\) equality patterns on five positions, generated by restricted-growth words. Their complete classification by the first-pair relation and number of distinct letters is:

| First pair | Distinct letters | Pattern count | Minimum weighted shadow |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Equal | 1 | 1 | 14 |
| Equal | 2 | 7 | 17 |
| Equal | 3 | 6 | 16 |
| Equal | 4 | 1 | 17 |
| Different | 2 | 8 | 19 |
| Different | 3 | 19 | 19 |
| Different | 4 | 9 | 19 |
| Different | 5 | 1 | 20 |

The attached finite verifier enumerates all \(52\) patterns, all ten three-index selections, and exactly calculates these minima; this is a complete finite certificate of the lemma, not a sample of alphabet sizes.

Weighting the output-capacity inequalities gives

\[
\sum_{w\in\Sigma^5}x_w
\sum_{y\in\mathsf D_2(w)}\omega(y)
\le14q+10q(q-1)+2q(q-1)(q-2)
=14q+q(q-1)(2q+6).
\]

The prefix-equality constraints and the lemma give a lower bound of \(14q+19q(q-1)\) on the same left-hand side. Therefore \(19\le2q+6\), so the integer \(q\) must be at least \(7\).

**Sufficiency.** Suppose \(q\ge7\). For every repeated prefix \(aa\), put weight \(1\) on \(aaaaa\). For each ordered prefix \(ab\), \(a\ne b\), distribute its total weight as follows (all auxiliary symbols are distinct from the prefix letters):

- Weight \(2/5\) over the words \(abbca\), with \(c\) uniform among the \(q-2\) possible symbols.
- Weight \(1/5\) over the words \(abbcc\), with \(c\) uniform among the same possibilities.
- Weight \(2/5\) over the words \(abcde\), with \(c,d,e\) an ordered triple of pairwise distinct symbols from \(\Sigma\setminus\{a,b\}\), uniformly among \((q-2)(q-3)(q-4)\) possibilities.

These choices assign nonnegative rational weights, and each prefix has total weight exactly one. For any representative with five letters labeled by restricted-growth symbols, direct enumeration of distinct length-three subsequences gives:

| Source family | \(AAA\) | \(AAB\) | \(ABA\) | \(ABB\) | \(ABC\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(abbca\) | 0 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 2 |
| \(abbcc\) | 0 | 1 | 0 | 3 | 1 |
| \(abcde\) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 10 |

Hence a different-letter prefix produces expected distinct-output counts \((0,1,4/5,1,5)\) in the five pattern families. The construction is invariant under every permutation of the alphabet. There are \(q\) outputs of type \(AAA\), \(q(q-1)\) of each two-letter equality type, and \(q(q-1)(q-2)\) of type \(ABC\). Since there are \(q(q-1)\) different-letter prefixes, total weighted incidence divided by the number of outputs of each type gives their individual loads \((1,1,4/5,1,5/(q-2))\); the repeated prefixes supply exactly the type-\(AAA\) loads. The maximum is at most one precisely when \(q\ge7\). All output inequalities hold, completing the proof.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone file `verification/check_shadow_lp.py` uses only the Python standard library and exact `Fraction` arithmetic. It independently enumerates the \(52\) canonical five-position equality patterns, checks the complete weighted-shadow table and the three family profiles, and constructs the rational feasible point directly for alphabets \(q=7,8,9\), checking **every** prefix sum and all \(q^3\) output constraints. The asserted all-alphabet sufficiency follows from the symbolic orbit-load calculation in the proof, not extrapolation from those three tests. ## Relationship to prior work

Grishin and Zabokritskiy's 2026 study of literal-systematic two-deletion codes establishes three transmitted checks over sufficiently large alphabets for every information length at least two, and distinguishes the full-symbol problem from fixed-alphabet bit redundancy. Its adjacent-burst fractional-cover bound concerns a different deletion pattern and does not supply the present exact arbitrary-two-deletion prefix-constrained fractional threshold. Earlier weighted-shadow converses already establish the **necessary seven-letter barrier** for length-five integral systematic codes; necessity alone is not the new contribution here. The substantive additional result is an explicit rational feasible packing for **every** \(q\ge7\), showing that the entire basic single-output-capacity relaxation cannot improve that barrier even though integral existence at \(q=7\) remains unresolved.

## Limitations

The fractional point is not a genuine systematic encoder. The theorem makes no claim about the existence or nonexistence of integral feasible points when \(q=7\), nor about higher-order collision, clique or integer-programming constraints. It is restricted to five transmitted symbols, two fixed prefix symbols and capacity constraints at length-three outputs; it does not classify longer blocks or the LP obtained by additionally imposing length-four output capacities. Unindexed equivalent literature remains a priority risk; no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
