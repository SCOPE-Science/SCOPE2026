# Exact shattering profile and online dimension of multipartite geodesic convexity

## Finding
Let \(G=K_{n_1,\ldots,n_r}\) be a finite complete multipartite graph with \(r\ge2\) nonempty parts \(V_1,\ldots,V_r\) of sizes \(n_i\ge2\). Let \(\mathcal C(G)\) contain **all** subsets \(C\subseteq V(G)\) that are geodesically convex: every shortest path between any two vertices of \(C\) is wholly contained in \(C\). Include the empty set and full vertex set as hypotheses, and query membership of individual vertices. A *partial transversal* is a set meeting every part in at most one vertex.

**Theorem.** The proper convex sets are exactly the partial transversals. A query support \(S\subseteq V(G)\) is shattered by \(\mathcal C(G)\) if and only if it is a partial transversal or it consists of precisely two vertices in one part. Therefore, with \(\Phi_G(z)=\sum_{S\text{ shattered}}z^{|S|}\),
\[
|\mathcal C(G)|=1+\prod_{i=1}^r(n_i+1),\qquad
\Phi_G(z)=\prod_{i=1}^r(1+n_i z)+z^2\sum_{i=1}^r\binom{n_i}{2}.
\]
In particular,
\[
\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal C(G))=r,
\qquad
\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal C(G))=r+1.
\]
For \(r=2\), all two-element supports are shattered; for \(r\ge3\), the top-degree coefficient is \(\prod_i n_i\). On every balanced, vertex-transitive graph \(K_{m,\ldots,m}\), \(m\ge2\), the static–adaptive difference is exactly one independently of \(m\).

## Assumptions and scope
Graphs are finite, simple, unweighted, undirected, and have at least two parts, with every part having at least two vertices. This excludes complete graphs and stars with singleton parts; no formula is asserted for those excluded cases. Geodesic convexity uses **all** shortest paths, not a chosen path per pair. The Littlestone dimension is the maximum depth of a complete binary mistake tree with each internal node labeled by a vertex query and every root-to-leaf answer path realized by some concept. The VC dimension counts supports realizing all subsets by ordinary simultaneous membership traces. A geodetic *covering* set is a different object: its shortest-path hull covers the graph, whereas a convex set is closed under those paths.

## Proof
**1. Exact convex sets.** Vertices in different parts are adjacent; their unique geodesic has no internal vertex. Two distinct vertices \(u,v\) in the same part \(V_i\) have distance two, and **every** vertex outside \(V_i\) lies on a length-two shortest path between them. If a convex set \(C\) contains such \(u,v\), it contains all vertices outside \(V_i\). Because \(r\ge2\) and all other part sizes are at least two, another part \(V_j\) then contributes two vertices to \(C\). Their shortest paths force every vertex outside \(V_j\), including the entire part \(V_i\), into \(C\). Thus \(C=V(G)\). Conversely, any partial transversal induces a clique and hence is convex, as are the full and empty sets. The two kinds are disjoint because \(|V(G)|>r\). This also gives the hypothesis count.

**2. All shattered supports.** If \(S\) is a partial transversal, then each trace \(T\subseteq S\) is realized by the convex hypothesis \(T\) itself. If \(S=\{u,v\}\subset V_i\), its four traces are realized by \(\varnothing\), \(\{u\}\), \(\{v\}\), and \(V(G)\). Conversely, suppose \(S\) contains distinct \(u,v\) from one part and a third vertex \(w\). Only the full hypothesis contains both \(u\) and \(v\); it also contains \(w\). Hence the trace \(\{u,v\}\) on \(S\) is impossible. Every non-partial-transversal support of size other than two contains such a triple. This proves the classification. The partial-transversal size enumerator is \(\prod_i(1+n_i z)\); the extra shattered supports are exactly the \(\binom{n_i}{2}\) two-subsets inside each part. Since \(r\ge2\), the maximal size of a shattered support is \(r\).

**3. Online upper bound.** In a complete shattered binary tree of positive depth, no vertex can be queried twice along one root-to-leaf path: the opposite answer at the second occurrence would contradict the fixed first answer and leave an unrealizable leaf. Consider an all-positive branch. Every proper hypothesis has at most one selected vertex per part, hence at most \(r\) positives in total. If a complete mistake tree had depth \(r+2\), its first \(r+1\) all-positive queries would be on distinct vertices and could be answered positively only by the single full hypothesis. The next query must have both answers realizable, which is impossible. Thus \(\operatorname{Ldim}\le r+1\).

**4. Matching online lower bound.** Define an abstract concept family \(F_s(m_1,\ldots,m_s)\) on disjoint blocks of sizes \(m_i\ge2\) to consist of all partial transversals and the full block union. For one block, the family comprises the empty set, every singleton, and the whole block; it shatters a complete depth-two tree. Specifically query one vertex \(x\); the positive branch distinguishes \(\{x\}\) from the full block by querying another vertex \(y\), and the negative branch distinguishes \(\varnothing\) from \(\{y\}\) by querying \(y\). A depth-three tree is impossible because the sole full hypothesis is the only concept admitting two distinct positive answers. So \(\operatorname{Ldim}(F_1)=2\).

Inductively suppose \(\operatorname{Ldim}(F_{s-1})=s\) for \(s\ge2\). Query a vertex \(x\) in the first block of \(F_s\). In the positive branch, restrict subsequent queries to the remaining \(s-1\) blocks. The surviving hypotheses induce exactly \(F_{s-1}\): proper positive concepts choose \(x\) and at most one vertex from each remaining block, and the full concept projects to the full remaining union. Hence this branch has a depth-\(s\) complete shattered tree. In the negative branch, pick a second vertex \(y\ne x\) from the first block and one vertex from every other block. Their \(s\)-vertex partial transversal is VC-shattered by proper concepts avoiding \(x\), so the negative branch also has a complete depth-\(s\) tree (an arbitrary fixed ordering of these \(s\) vertex queries suffices). Joining these at the root gives depth \(s+1\). Thus \(\operatorname{Ldim}(F_r)\ge r+1\). Part 1 identifies \(F_r\) with \(\mathcal C(G)\), and part 3 supplies equality.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `CHECK.py` directly enumerates all subsets of several multipartite graphs. It independently tests geodesic convexity using pairwise shortest-path intervals, compares the resulting concept class with the claimed transversals-plus-full description, enumerates every query support and its Boolean traces, evaluates the polynomial coefficients, and computes the Littlestone rank by an exhaustive version-space recurrence. Cases through five parts of size two are checked, including the ten-vertex graph with \(244\) concepts. Run `python3 CHECK.py`; the final line must be `ALL_GEODESIC_CONVEXITY_CENSUS_CHECKS_PASS`. These are finite checks only; the all-parameter theorem follows from the proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Dourado, Gutierrez, Protti, Sampaio and Tondato survey graph-convexity geometry (arXiv:2203.15878, initially public 2022-03-29). Thiessen and Gärtner's *Online Learning of Convex Sets on Graphs* (2022 proceedings; DOI `10.1007/978-3-031-26412-2_22`) gives general VC-dimension and online mistake bounds (notably Proposition 11, identifying VC dimension with a maximum hull-set invariant), and explicitly observes that in \(K_{2,3}\) individual vertices and edges are convex while nontrivial halfspaces do not exist. **Those observations are prior art, not claimed as new.** Its Sections 3–4 do not state the complete support enumerator or a sharp arbitrary-part-size Littlestone formula. Sokolov, Thiessen, Akhmejanova, Vitale and Orabona (arXiv:2409.01428, submitted 2024-09-02) study **self-directed** convex-labeling learning, a different query protocol; their stated results do not imply the classical adversarial online dimension here.

The published *Geodetic polynomial of complete multipartite graphs* (2026) derives a complete characterization of **geodetic covering** sets via how many selected vertices lie in each part. The underlying same-part shortest-path observation overlaps with Part 1 of the proof, but covering-set enumerators are not convex-set shattering enumerators; its statement does not entail the displayed exact VC supports or Littlestone dimension without separate analysis.

## Limitations
All part sizes are assumed at least two, and the theorem concerns the entire convex-set concept family, not geodesic halfspaces, convex bipartitions, or geodetic covering sets. The static–adaptive gap is one, not unbounded as the size of a part increases. No result is claimed for singleton parts or graph families outside complete multipartite graphs. Although relevant primary literature and published records were compared by implication, exhaustive priority over all other terminology and unpublished results is not certified. Independent scientific audit has not been performed.
