# Joint edge differences and signed-filter Lipschitz norms on two-subset Kneser graphs

## Finding

Let \(G=KG(n,2)\), \(n\ge5\), with its unit-edge shortest-path metric, and let \(A\) be its adjacency operator. A vertex is a two-element set, and two vertices are adjacent exactly when disjoint. For any fixed oriented edge \((u,v)\), the full set of feasible pairs
\[
K_n=\{(f(u)-f(v),(Af)(u)-(Af)(v)):\operatorname{Lip}(f)\le1\}
\]
is
\[
K_5=\{(x,y):|x|\le1,\ |x|+|y|\le4\},\qquad
K_n=\{(x,y):|x|\le1,\ |x+y|\le2(n-4)\}\quad(n\ge6).
\]
In particular, for \(s>0\) the exact Lipschitz norm of the non-Markov filter \(A-sI\) is \(\max\{4,s+3\}\) on the Petersen graph, and \(s+1+2(n-4)\) for \(n\ge6\). More generally, the support functions of these regions give, for all real \(\alpha,\beta\),
\[
\|\alpha I+\beta A\|_{\mathrm{Lip}\to\mathrm{Lip}}
=\begin{cases}
\max\{4|\beta|,|\alpha|+3|\beta|\},&n=5,\\
|\alpha-\beta|+2(n-4)|\beta|,&n\ge6.
\end{cases}
\]
These describe signed filters, not just positive mixtures of a walk and the identity. The full signed region is the claim; nonnegative-coefficient consequences are included for context, not claimed as new transport results.

## Assumptions and scope

Functions are real; \(\operatorname{Lip}(f)=\max_{x\sim y}|f(x)-f(y)|\) is a norm modulo constants on this connected graph. Adding a constant does not alter either edge difference since the adjacency degree is constant. Graph distance is two for any two distinct intersecting vertices: the complement of their union contains at least two elements. No assertion is made about higher-subset Kneser graphs, complex functions or nonunit edge metrics.

## Proof

**Edge identity.** A ground-set permutation takes any oriented edge to \(u=\{1,2\},v=\{3,4\}\). Put \(R=\{5,\ldots,n\}\), let \(U=\{\{i,j\}:i\in\{1,2\},j\in R\}\) and \(V=\{\{i,j\}:i\in\{3,4\},j\in R\}\). Cancellation of common neighbors gives
\[
x=f(u)-f(v),\qquad y=-x+\sum_{w\in V}f(w)-\sum_{w\in U}f(w).
\]
The feasible pair set is convex and centrally symmetric as the linear image of the Lipschitz unit ball.

**At least two external elements.** When \(n\ge6\), choose a derangement \(\sigma\) of \(R\). Match \(\{i,j\}\in U\) with \(\{i+2,\sigma(j)\}\in V\); their disjointness implies that each paired difference has absolute value at most one. Hence \(|x+y|\le 2(n-4)\) and \(|x|\le1\). All four corners in the coordinates \((x,x+y)\) are feasible. For prescribed \(x=\pm1\) and sum difference \(\pm2(n-4)\), use \(f(u)=x/2\), \(f(v)=-x/2\), one common value \(+1/2\) on \(V\) and \(-1/2\) on \(U\) for the positive sign, or exchange the group values for the negative sign; assign zero to the remaining vertices. These functions take values in an interval of length one. Convexity now proves equality with the stated parallelogram.

**One external element.** When \(n=5\), averaging on the group generated by \(1\leftrightarrow2\) and \(3\leftrightarrow4\) preserves \((x,y)\) and the Lipschitz bound. Take \(p\) to be the common value on \(U\) and \(q\) the common value on \(V\). Writing \(z=q-p\) gives \(y=2z-x\). Cross-group pairs have distance two, so \(|z|\le2\). Each member of \(V\) is adjacent to \(u\), and each member of \(U\) is adjacent to \(v\); hence \(|z-x|\le2\). This yields \(|y+x|\le4\) and \(|y-x|\le4\), equivalent to \(|y|+|x|\le4\); the edge also gives \(|x|\le1\).

The assignments \((f(u),f(v),p,q)=(0,0,-1,1),(0,-1,-1,1),(0,-1,0,-1)\) give the vertices \((0,4),(1,3),(1,-3)\) of this hexagon, and negating gives the other vertices. On the six assigned vertices the distance-one pairs are \(u,v\), the pairs from \(u\) to \(V\), and those from \(v\) to \(U\). On these pairs the listed differences are at most one; all other pairs have distance two and differences at most two. The McShane extension \(f(w)=\min_i\{f(i)+d(i,w)\}\), with \(i\) over the assigned vertices, preserves the assigned values and remains 1-Lipschitz. Thus every corner is realized and convexity proves the full hexagon.

**Operator norms.** By edge transitivity and central symmetry, the norm of \(\alpha I+\beta A\) is the maximum of \(\alpha x+\beta y\) on \(K_n\). On the parallelogram write this as \((\alpha-\beta)x+\beta(x+y)\) and maximize independently. On the hexagon the six listed corners give the two competing expressions. Counting common neighbors also gives \(A^2=(n-3)I-(n-4)A+\binom{n-3}{2}J\), where \(J\) sends functions to constants. Thus polynomial filters in \(A\) reduce to affine filters in this quotient; the support-function formulas apply to them irrespective of the signs of the reduced coefficients.

## Relationship to prior work

For nonnegative coefficients with positive row sum \(s_0=\alpha+k\beta\), where \(k=\binom{n-2}{2}\), normalization turns the operator into the usual idleness walk with parameter \(p=\alpha/s_0\). Bourne, Cushing, Liu, Münch and Peyerimhoff (arXiv:1704.04398, Theorem 1.1) give the two linear parts of the idleness function in the regular case, and Bonini et al. (arXiv:1907.06733, Theorem 1.2) compute the late-idleness slope by maximum matching in strongly regular graphs. The finite-time heat coefficient of this family is an application of these results: the quotient heat filter has \(\alpha_t\ge\beta_t\ge0\), so its normalized representative samples only the high-idleness branch. No new heat contraction result is claimed here.

Even the entire nonnegative support function on the Petersen graph does not determine its joint signed region. For instance the centrally symmetric convex hull of \((0,\pm4),(1,3),(-1,-3)\) gives the same support function for \(\alpha,\beta\ge0\) as the hexagon, but misses the two corners \((1,-3),(-1,3)\). Those corners govern sufficiently large opposite-sign coefficients. The explicit attainable region certifies these directions; in particular it gives the exact amplification of the shifted adjacency filter \(A-sI\), where idleness would not be a probability.

## Reproducibility and limitations

The corner assignments and edge-distance upper bounds are explicit certificates for all \(n\) and all real coefficients. A finite LP check can use one value anchored at zero, two inequalities per graph edge, and the linear objective for one oriented edge. Such spot checks test normalization and mixed-sign examples, not the all-\(n\) statement. Historical priority is limited to the sources compared.
