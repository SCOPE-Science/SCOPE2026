# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The two majority decisions differ only on two boundary events: the donor is wrong and proxy right with exactly \(h-1\) correct background voters, or donor right and proxy wrong with exactly \(h\). Their binomial coefficients agree, giving an exact factored gain identity for all positive integers \(h\). A two-term positive-base exponential expression is monotone after dividing by the larger base, proving the one-crossing result, including equality and zero-coefficient cases. Explicit rational witnesses were recomputed. The standalone program independently convolves votes using fractions, checking 4,800 reproducible rational profiles and 2,916 small-grid profiles. Computation supplements, rather than replaces, the universal proof.

Its full available text was inspected, including finite-size illustrations, mechanisms, and discussion. Search-based comparison of a claim cannot establish absolute novelty: an unindexed equivalent local pivotality argument remains a real priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Whether an apparently superior expert's single delegation improves collective correctness is a natural elementary decision in epistemic liquid democracy. The exact sign criterion includes the background electorate's odds, and the two-state result proves a non-obvious global constraint: the edge's risk can flip once but never oscillate with odd electorate size. Both reversal directions are realized with every voter more likely than not to be correct and the recipient strictly more competent in every signal state. This gives an exact finite-size phase classification, not a numerical example or a restatement of a generic concentration bound.

The assumptions are material: homogeneity of background voters within each state, exactly two signal states, a single edge, and odd total weight. Unindexed earlier formulations cannot be ruled out, and the result has not been independently audited.
