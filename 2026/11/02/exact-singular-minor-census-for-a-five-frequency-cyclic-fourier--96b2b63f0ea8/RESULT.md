# Exact singular-minor census for a five-frequency cyclic Fourier pattern

## Finding
For every integer \(N\ge6\), put \(\omega=\exp(2\pi i/N)\) and select the five Fourier rows \(R=\{0,1,2,3,5\}\) in the order-\(N\) discrete Fourier matrix. For each five-element column set \(C\subset\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\), let \(F_N[R,C]=(\omega^{rc})_{r\in R,c\in C}\). Its rank is either four or five, and the **exact number** of rank-four selections is
\[
\boxed{S_N=\mathbf1_{5\mid N}\frac N5+\mathbf1_{6\mid N}\frac{N(N-6)}6.}
\]
Every other maximal minor is nonzero. Precisely, a rank-four column set is either a rotated regular pentagon (possible only when \(5\mid N\)) or the disjoint union of an antipodal pair and a rotated equilateral triple (possible only when \(6\mid N\), and nonexistent at \(N=6\)). These two types do not overlap. The five-row harmonic frame is full spark exactly when \(5\nmid N\) and either \(6\nmid N\) or \(N=6\). The fraction of all five-column selections that fail recovery is exactly \(S_N/\binom N5\).

## Assumptions and scope
The order \(N\) is an arbitrary integer at least six, so all five row indices are distinct modulo \(N\). Columns are **unordered distinct indices**; each is counted exactly once. Full spark means all square minors using all five selected rows are nonsingular. The roots lie in the complex numbers and algebraic equalities are exact, not floating-point approximations. The rank assertion concerns only five-by-five maximal minors; no conclusion is claimed for other row patterns or smaller column subsets.

## Proof
**1. Determinantal reduction.** For any five distinct column roots \(z_j=\omega^{c_j}\), the alternant/Schur identity (or expansion of the last row against the Vandermonde determinant) gives
\[
\det(z_j^r)_{r\in\{0,1,2,3,5\},\,1\le j\le5}
=\pm\prod_{i<j}(z_j-z_i)\,(z_1+z_2+z_3+z_4+z_5).
\]
The Vandermonde factor is never zero. Thus a maximal minor vanishes if and only if its five **distinct** roots of unity sum to zero. The first four rows are an ordinary Vandermonde system of row rank four on these five distinct roots, so every singular five-by-five minor has rank **exactly** four.

**2. Classification of a five-term zero sum.** A vanishing sum is *minimal* if no nonempty proper subcollection vanishes. Two distinct unit complex numbers sum to zero precisely when they are antipodal; a minimal triple of unit complex numbers sums to zero precisely when it forms a rotated regular triangle. There are no minimal vanishing four-term sums: this is verified together with the five-term case by the following finite reduction, for which the executable exact checker is included.

Here is an elementary proof of the conductor bound behind that reduction. For any minimal vanishing sum of \(k\le5\) roots, divide by its first term and let \(m\) be the least common multiple of the orders of all ratios. If \(p^a\) exactly divides \(m\), write each root uniquely as a product of a \(p^a\)-th root and a root of order prime to \(p\). The cyclotomic polynomial \(\Phi_{p^a}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}X^{j p^{a-1}}\) remains irreducible over the cyclotomic field of coprime conductor. Therefore coefficients at exponents \(t+jp^{a-1}\), for each fixed \(0\le t<p^{a-1}\), are all equal in that field. The terms in each such residue block separately sum to zero; minimality forces one block, whence \(a=1\) (otherwise every ratio has \(p\)-part of order at most \(p\)). For \(a=1\), the same coefficient equality and minimality force all \(p\) residues to appear, since a block with a zero coefficient would already contain a forbidden zero subsum. Thus \(p\le k\). Hence the ratio conductor is squarefree with prime factors drawn from \(\{2,3,5\}\): every normalized minimal vanishing sum of at most five roots is entirely within the 30th roots of unity.

The precise finite conclusion is checkable without a classification theorem: reduce sums \(1+X^{a_2}+\cdots+X^{a_k}\) with distinct exponents modulo \(\Phi_{30}(X)=X^8+X^7-X^5-X^4-X^3+X+1\), testing all subsets of the 30 exponent classes of sizes two through five and removing those with a proper vanishing subsum. The attached reproducible integer-arithmetic checker exhausts the \(\binom{30}{k}\) subsets at each \(k\). It finds exactly 15 minimal antipodal pairs, 10 minimal equilateral triples, no minimal four-sets, and six minimal five-sets, all six being the cosets of the unique five-element subgroup. This is a finite exhaustive **proof certificate for the reduced universe**—the conductor argument proves it suffices for arbitrary roots of unity. Consequently any five distinct roots with zero sum are either a minimal regular pentagon or, after extracting a proper vanishing subsum, an antipodal pair plus an equilateral triple. No other partition of five into allowable vanishing summands is possible.

**3. Exact incidence count.** If \(5\mid N\), the order-\(N\) roots contain exactly \(N/5\) different five-element cosets of their unique subgroup of order five. If \(6\mid N\), there are \(N/2\) unordered antipodal pairs and \(N/3\) unordered equilateral triples. For each antipodal pair, its two endpoints lie in **different** equilateral-triple cosets, because an equilateral triple contains no antipodal pair. Thus exactly \(N/3-2\) triples are disjoint from that pair, and their unions contribute \((N/2)(N/3-2)=N(N-6)/6\) distinct five-sets: an opposite pair in such a set is unique, because the triple has no opposite pair and an opposite of one of its elements cannot lie inside the same triple. A pentagon also contains no opposite pair, so the two types are disjoint. Adding the counts proves the formula, the rank statement, and the full-spark criterion.

## Verification
Run `python3 check.py` with standard Python 3 from the directory containing the accompanying file. No third-party packages or network resources are needed. The checker performs exact reduction in integer polynomial quotient rings \(\mathbb Z[X]/(\Phi_N(X))\). It tests **every** five-column set at orders \(6,7,8,9,10,11,12,15,18,20,24,30\), including all \(142506\) possibilities at order thirty, against both the zero-sum test and the two constructive types. It also exhausts all size-two-through-five subsets of 30th roots as described in the proof, with exact cyclotomic arithmetic, obtaining minimal counts \(15,10,0,6\). The final output is `VERIFY_OK exact_cyclotomic_all_five_subsets_and_exhaustive_Mann_reduction`. Since the conductor bound is proved algebraically, the small finite census **at order thirty** is a complete check of the one nontrivial reduced case needed for the infinite theorem; the other sampled orders merely corroborate the resulting formula.

## Relationship to prior work
Lam and Leung's weight classification of vanishing root sums supplies broad background, but their weight existence criterion alone does not distinguish the **distinct-root** configurations or count maximal Fourier minors. Classical short-minimal-sum analysis by Conway and Jones and related work motivates the conductor reduction; the present proof supplies its own finite exhaustive short-length certificate. Alexeev, Cahill and Mixon formulate the full-spark problem for harmonic frames. Existing exact classifications for Fourier orders ten and twelve encompass the particular instances of this fixed row set; in particular the order-twelve literature already records twelve singular five-column minors. Neither those finite-order classifications nor the inspected general Fourier-frame statements give this **all-order exact singular-minor count**, its partition into pentagon and pair-plus-triangle types, and the order-six boundary. The result does not claim that the Schur alternant factorization or short vanishing-root classification is itself new.

## Limitations
This is a fixed five-row pattern, not a complete classification of five-row harmonic frames or of arbitrary Fourier minors. The central root-of-unity classification is elementary but depends on a conductor argument and an exhaustive exact finite check; checking only a few Fourier orders would not establish the infinite result. Full historical priority cannot be guaranteed, especially because equivalent descriptions in older sparse-array or harmonic-frame literature may be unindexed. No independent external mathematical validation has been performed.
