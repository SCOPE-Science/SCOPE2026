# Exact six-term zero-sum threshold and extremal profiles modulo \(36\)

## Finding

Let \(s_1(6)\) be the least positive integer \(N\) such that every list of \(N\) integers not divisible by \(6\) contains six distinct positions whose sum is divisible by \(6\) but not by \(36\). Then

\[
\boxed{s_1(6)=13.}
\]

There are exactly \(23{,}970\) unordered twelve-element multisets of admissible residue classes modulo \(36\) with no such six-term sum. Under the affine transformations

\[
x\longmapsto ux+6t\pmod {36},\qquad \gcd(u,36)=1,\quad 0\le t<6,
\]

they form exactly \(361\) orbits: \(314\) of size \(72\), \(33\) of size \(36\), \(13\) of size \(12\), and one of size \(18\). Their vectors of multiplicities in the nonzero residue classes \(1,2,3,4,5\pmod6\) have exactly the following eleven possibilities, with exact counts:

| Residue-class multiplicities \((c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4,c_5)\) | Number of multisets |
| --- | ---: |
| \((0,6,6,0,0)\) | 5928 |
| \((0,0,6,6,0)\) | 5928 |
| \((6,6,0,0,0)\) | 5850 |
| \((0,0,0,6,6)\) | 5850 |
| \((5,1,0,1,5)\) | 108 |
| \((1,5,0,5,1)\) | 108 |
| \((0,5,2,5,0)\) | 54 |
| \((5,5,0,1,1)\) | 36 |
| \((1,5,5,1,0)\) | 36 |
| \((1,1,0,5,5)\) | 36 |
| \((0,1,5,5,1)\) | 36 |

In particular, \(23{,}556\) of the \(23{,}970\) extremal multisets use two adjacent nonzero residue classes modulo \(6\), each exactly six times. The remaining \(414\) have the other seven multiplicity profiles.

## Assumptions and scope

All lists may repeat integers; subsequences select **distinct indices**. The property depends only on the residue of each integer modulo \(36\). Thus an admissible entry is one of the thirty residues
\[
A=\{x\in\{0,1,\dots,35\}:6\nmid x\}.
\]
A six-term sum is qualifying precisely when its residue belongs to
\[
T=\{6,12,18,24,30\}\subset\mathbb Z/36\mathbb Z.
\]
A multiset is called **bad** if no six distinct positions have a sum in \(T\). The counts above concern multisets (not ordered twelve-tuples); orbit sizes refer to distinct residue multisets, not the number of affine transformations.

## Proof

**A bad twelve-term witness.** Take five copies each of \(1\) and \(2\), together with \(31\) and \(26\). Exactly six terms have residue \(1\pmod6\) and exactly six have residue \(2\pmod6\). A six-element selection containing \(k\) of the former has sum congruent to \(12-k\pmod6\). It can be divisible by \(6\) only when \(k=0\) or \(k=6\). The sum of all six terms in either class is \(36\), so every divisible six-sum is \(0\pmod{36}\). Hence \(s_1(6)\ge13\).

**Exhaustive upper bound with an exact finite recurrence.** For a finite multiset \(B\) of residues modulo \(36\), let \(S_j(B)\subset\mathbb Z/36\mathbb Z\) be the residues of sums formed from exactly \(j\) different positions, for \(0\le j\le6\), with \(S_0(B)=\{0\}\). On appending an entry \(x\), the exact recurrence is
\[
S_j(B\cup\{x\})=S_j(B)\cup\bigl(x+S_{j-1}(B)\bigr),
\qquad 1\le j\le6.
\]
Represent each set by a thirty-six-bit mask. Rotation of a mask by \(x\) positions implements addition by \(x\) modulo \(36\). Updating \(j=6,5,\ldots,1\) ensures the newly appended element is used at most once. The predicate for a bad sequence is exactly \(S_6(B)\cap T=\varnothing\). If a prefix fails it, every extension fails it; hence pruning such prefixes is logically exhaustive.

The attached deterministic generator recursively appends admissible residues in nondecreasing order. Every unordered multiset has one and only one such ordered representation, and the preceding pruning criterion removes only branches unable to produce a bad multiset. The numbers of visited bad prefixes at depths \(0\) through \(12\) are respectively
\[
1,30,465,4960,40920,278256,1397210,3565140,3172686,1271740,500586,156528,23970.
\]
The full list of \(23{,}970\) bad twelve-term multisets is the attached `artifacts/sun6_extremals.txt`, generated by the attached `artifacts/sun6_enumerate.cpp`. This finite output is a complete enumeration, not a sampling argument.

**No extension.** For each bad twelve-term multiset \(B\) in that list, and **every** \(x\in A\), the generator checks
\[
\bigl(x+S_5(B)\bigr)\cap T\ne\varnothing.
\]
The independent direct-subset checker reconstructs the sums of all five-element subsets and checks the same statement without using the bitmask recurrence. Thus no bad twelve-term multiset can be extended by one admissible term. Every thirteen-term multiset contains a twelve-term submultiset; if the thirteen-term multiset were bad, that submultiset would be bad and extendible, a contradiction. Therefore \(s_1(6)\le13\), proving the equality.

**Exact extremal statistics.** The checker verifies that every listed multiset is bad by directly enumerating its six-position sums. It then counts the multiplicity vectors modulo \(6\), giving the table above. The affine transformations form a group of order \(72\), and each preserves admissibility and the bad property because adding \(6t\) to six selected entries changes the sum by \(36t\), and multiplication by a unit permutes \(T\). The checker constructs the complete orbit of each remaining multiset, removes it, and repeats until none remain. The resulting orbit counts are \(314,33,13,1\) at orbit sizes \(72,36,12,18\). Their weighted sum is \(23{,}970\), so they exhaust the listed objects.

## Verification

The package contains two standalone C++17 programs and the complete certificate. From its root, run:

```sh
c++ -std=c++17 -O2 artifacts/sun6_enumerate.cpp -o sun6_enumerate
c++ -std=c++17 -O2 artifacts/sun6_crosscheck.cpp -o sun6_crosscheck
./sun6_enumerate > regenerated.txt

./sun6_crosscheck < artifacts/sun6_extremals.txt
```

The generator reports `GENERATOR_OK width12=23970 nonextendible=23970` on stderr, and the checker reports `CROSSCHECK_OK rows=23970 orbits=361 patterns=11` on stdout. The certificate `artifacts/sun6_extremals.txt` has \(23{,}970\) newline-terminated rows and SHA-256 digest `c1e36c249abab1240f35d029f52526e4f24555c0850529d5a765eec4245542f1`. The rows are strictly increasing in lexicographic order; each row is nondecreasing, may repeat entries, and uses only residues not divisible by six. The checker computes subset sums directly by index combinations, not by trusting the generator's masks; the generator separately verifies branch exhaustiveness through the explicit recurrence.

## Relationship to prior work

Zhi-Wei Sun introduced \(s_1(n)\), proved \(s_1(n)\ge2n+1\) for \(n>2\), and conjectured equality for all such \(n\) [1, Definition 1.3, Theorem 1.3, Conjecture 1.2]. His general upper bounds imply only \(s_1(6)\le21\) through the two-dimensional EGZ constant. This result supplies the missing sharp upper bound at the first squarefree composite modulus and determines all its maximal bad multisets.

Li, Ling, Sun, and Zhu [2] subsequently proved results about the unrestricted-length constants \(w_r(n)\) and disproved the rank-two fixed-length conjecture; their rank-two \(w_2(6)\) equality does not imply a fixed-length rank-one result for \(s_1(6)\). Gao and collaborators [3] investigate the related fixed-length property for **prime** moduli, not the composite modulus six. A published exact computation at modulus four [4] concerns twelve admissible residue classes modulo sixteen, not the present thirty classes modulo thirty-six. The overlap search did not identify an earlier proof or census of the present claim. This is a priority assessment, not a claim that search failure establishes uniqueness.

## Limitations

The general conjecture \(s_1(n)=2n+1\) remains unproved here for every modulus other than \(6\); no result for \(s_2(n)\) or unrestricted-length \(w_r(n)\) is asserted. The extremal classification is exact only at \(n=6\). The proofs of the numerical counts are finite exhaustive algorithms whose full source and data are supplied; they do not yield a closed symbolic classification at general \(n\). No independent mathematical audit or external priority certification has been performed. Older or unindexed calculations could contain overlapping numerical counts.
