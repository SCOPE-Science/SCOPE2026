# Exact size and symmetries of the free two-generator multiplicatively idempotent semiring

## Finding

Let \(\mathsf{MI}\) be the variety in the binary signature \(\{+,\cdot\}\) in which addition is associative and commutative, multiplication is associative and distributes over addition, and every element obeys \(x^2=x\). Neither an additive zero nor a multiplicative identity is included or required. The free algebra \(F_{\mathsf{MI}}(a,b)\) on two named generators has precisely
\[
\boxed{\lvert F_{\mathsf{MI}}(a,b)\rvert=94,\qquad
\operatorname{Aut}(F_{\mathsf{MI}}(a,b))\cong C_2.}
\]
Its only automorphisms are the identity and the interchange of the two free generators. Exactly two ordered pairs of elements generate the entire algebra.

## Assumptions and scope

The operations are genuinely binary, addition is commutative, multiplication need not be commutative, and there are no nullary operations. A polynomial always denotes a **nonempty** sum of nonempty multiplicative words. Thus this theorem does not concern a variety with named \(0\) or \(1\), nor the subvarieties satisfying commutativity of multiplication or idempotence of addition.

## Proof

**Step 1: the two-letter free band.** A multiplicative word belongs to a band, since every element is multiplicatively idempotent. Adjacent repeated letters reduce by \(aa=a\) and \(bb=b\), and the band law applied to the words \(ab\) and \(ba\) gives \(abab=ab\) and \(baba=ba\). Repeated application reduces every word to one of six words
\[
W=\{a,b,ab,ba,aba,bab\}.
\]
These six words are distinct in the six-element band with multiplication obtained by concatenation followed by these reductions: its multiplication is associative and idempotent, as verified exhaustively on all ordered triples and elements in the accompanying checker. Evaluating the free words in this band separates them. Thus \(W\) is exactly the free band on \(a,b\).

**Step 2: the finite polynomial semiring.** Applying multiplicative idempotence to \(x+x\) yields \((x+x)^2=x+x\), whence \(4x=2x\). Consequently every positive multiplicity reduces to exactly one of \(1,2,3\), with positive even multiplicities at least two represented by \(2\) and positive odd multiplicities at least three by \(3\). Let \(C=\{0,1,2,3\}\), with \(0\) denoting absence of a monomial, addition and multiplication given by ordinary nonnegative-integer arithmetic followed by this reduction. Define \(P\) to be the \(4^6-1=4095\) nonzero coefficient vectors \(C^W\setminus\{0\}\), with coefficientwise addition and distributive multiplication obtained from the six-element free band. The absent vector is not an algebra element. The coefficient reduction is a semiring congruence, so \(P\) is associative and distributive and every term over \(a,b\) has a representative in \(P\).

**Step 3: identifying precisely the free quotient.** Let \(\theta\) be the smallest semiring congruence on \(P\) containing \((p,p^2)\) for every \(p\in P\). The quotient \(P/\theta\) satisfies all axioms of \(\mathsf{MI}\) and is generated by \(a,b\). Conversely, every evaluation of \(a,b\) into any member of \(\mathsf{MI}\) induces a homomorphism from \(P\), since both the band reductions and coefficient reductions are identities there, and its kernel contains all \((p,p^2)\). Hence the homomorphism factors uniquely through \(P/\theta\). This proves \(P/\theta\cong F_{\mathsf{MI}}(a,b)\); the computation below is not an arbitrary finite approximation.

**Step 4: an exhaustive finite certificate.** Initialize disjoint singleton classes for the integers \(1\) through \(4095\), interpreting each integer in base four as a vector in \(C^6\). Enqueue the \(4095\) pairs \((p,p^2)\). Whenever a pair joins distinct classes, also enqueue its translates under addition by each of the six monomial basis vectors and under left and right multiplication by \(a\) and \(b\). There are six additive and four multiplicative unary translation contexts. These contexts suffice: addition by any polynomial is composition of additions by its monomial summands, while multiplication by any polynomial is built from multiplication by \(a,b\), addition and distributivity. Upon exhaustion, the resulting relation is both a congruence and contains every defining pair, and induction over the processing steps shows that every identified pair lies in \(\theta\). It is therefore exactly \(\theta\).

The deterministic implementation in `verify.py` processes \(44105\) queued pairs, makes \(4001\) successful identifications, and leaves \(94\) equivalence classes. Their sizes have the following exact frequency distribution:
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrrr}
\text{class size}&1&2&4&5&6&103&104&114&128\\\hline
\text{number of classes}&24&6&4&4&24&1&1&12&18
\end{array}
\]
The column frequencies sum to \(94\), and their size-weighted sum is \(4095\). The checker explicitly tests the resulting congruence under all generating contexts and all semiring axioms on every triple of quotient elements, rather than relying solely on the union-find count.

**Step 5: automorphisms.** A homomorphism out of the free algebra is uniquely specified by the ordered images of \(a,b\). It is surjective exactly when those images generate the algebra under the two operations. Because this algebra is finite, such a surjection is an automorphism. The checker enumerates all \(94^2=8836\) ordered candidate pairs and repeatedly closes each pair under addition and both product orders. Exactly two pairs generate all \(94\) elements: the original pair and its reversal. Swapping \(a,b\) is an involutive automorphism by the universal property. Hence the full group is \(C_2\).

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` using the Python standard library. The exact checks output `CONGRUENCE_CLASSES= 94`, `GENERATING_ORDERED_PAIRS= 2`, `QUOTIENT_SEMIRING_AXIOMS=PASS for all 94^3 triples`, and `VERIFY_OK`. The code is included in full and constructs its data anew. The checks are exhaustive because \(P\) has exactly \(4095\) members, not because computations at a few sample sizes extrapolate.

## Relationship to prior work

Wang and Wang's October 2026 paper establishes local finiteness and hereditary finite basability of \(\mathsf{MI}\), including the general \(4^{|B_X|}-1\) polynomial bound. For two generators that bound is \(4095\); the paper does not give the sharp order or automorphism group of the free rank-two object. Vechtomov and Petrov's earlier work gives general semiring structure and studies the **commutative** subvariety. An accessible indexed extract of a 2014 conference proceeding reports \(39\) elements for the corresponding free *commutative* rank-two object, and direct computation also gives \(39\) in that different variety. Since commutativity of multiplication is not assumed here, that count neither states nor implies the number \(94\). Work on semirings with *both* operations idempotent, or with named constants, concerns different signatures or stronger identities. Exact historical priority remains subject to fuller comparison with older Russian-language monographs.

## Limitations

This is an exact two-generator result, not a rank-\(n\) formula, a classification of all finite multiplicatively idempotent semirings, or a finding for varieties with named constants. The numerical order and generator-pair census are proved by transparent exhaustive finite congruence computations whose complete algorithm is supplied; no shorter symbolic classification of the 94 representatives is claimed. Older semiring monographs not obtained in full could contain equivalent rank-two counts. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
