# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Every two-deletion descendant has a unique left-greedy embedding. Counting the four disjoint canonical deletion-pair cases establishes the per-word identity, including both endpoints. Conditional on the adjacent-change pattern, consecutive-change rainbow indicators have independent choices of \(q-2\) versus one, yielding the polynomial. Stars-and-bars gives the compressed sum; centered independent Bernoulli changes and the law of total variance give the rational moment formula. Exact full enumerations and a separate greedy-embedding implementation corroborate boundary cases, without substituting for the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS (with risks).** The most relevant published precedents derive fixed-word double-deletion counts, binary run-extremal bounds, and general nonbinary run-extremal bounds. The checked material does not state a histogram over *all* \(q^n\) inputs or an all-\(q\) exact variance. The earlier binary variance is explicitly excluded as a novel consequence. A nonbinary fixed-word formula could in principle be summed, so further archival literature and unsurfaced precomputed tables remain residual risks; no independent priority determination is claimed.

**Value — PASS.** The complete natural distribution of deletion-ball degrees quantifies the nonuniformity of a classical synchronization-error channel at every finite blocklength and alphabet size. In contrast to an arbitrary instance table, the formula gives all tail probabilities and moments with an explicit finite binomial sum, and its nonbinary three-letter correction explains behavior invisible in binary runs alone. It is useful for finite-block structural and counting bounds, without promising a new capacity or packing theorem.
