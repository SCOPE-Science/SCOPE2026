# Scientific review: quadratic spherical quarter-wedge concentration

## Correctness

**PASS.** The domain is the round sphere in each integer dimension \(d\ge3\), the harmonic band is exactly degrees zero through two, and the concentration operator is a positive self-adjoint compression of a characteristic function. The proof uses exact normalized spherical absolute moments and a complete \(O(d-2)\)-symmetry/swap decomposition into scalar, transverse-vector and transverse-traceless sectors. Its dimensions add to \(d(d+3)/2\). The supplied Gram matrices follow from the moment recurrences, and their generalized eigenvalues give the entire spectrum. The signed determinants at \(\lambda=1/4\) yield an exact inertia classification; an explicit trial polynomial gives an all-dimensional Rayleigh quotient above \(3/4\); gamma log-convexity places the vector root above \(1/2\). This proves all four interval counts and the simple sharp top eigenline, without relying on finite experiments.

Independent implementations compare full unreduced monomial spectra with the reduced spectra for each \(3\le d\le12\); symbolic algebra checks nine exact block identities. Both programs executed successfully from the supplied standalone sources. Their finite outputs are checks, not the basis for the infinite-dimensional quantifiers. No machine-assisted formal proof or independent verification is claimed.

## Originality

**PASS, best-knowledge priority assessment.** Simons–Dahlen–Wieczorek (arXiv:math/0408424, 2004), Sections 3–5, establish arbitrary-region spherical Slepian matrix concentration and special polar-cap methods, but the inspected theorem and application sections do not state this quarter-wedge quadratic all-dimensional spectrum or its four spectral interval counts. Michel–Plattner–Seibert (arXiv:2103.14650, 2021), Introduction, Sections 3–4, develop spin-weighted scalar/vector/tensor Slepian constructions for caps; their matrix decoupling acts on tensor components, rather than the coordinate-reflection representations here. Roddy–McEwen (arXiv:2106.02023, 2021) applies Slepian wavelets to incomplete spherical domains; accessible metadata/abstract do not report the present precise invariant, but its full PDF was too large for direct full-text inspection and remains a residual risk. Available earlier concentration results on a **single** hemisphere do not imply the two-cut spectral staircase: no half-energy pairing determines the off-diagonal \(uv\) sector or the new \(1/4\) plateau.

Relevant scientific searches included exact phrases for quadratic spherical quadrants and wedges, plus published-finding corpus semantic comparisons for quarter-sphere spectra, orthogonal hemispherical intersections, spectral multiplicities, and dominating general theorems. No indexed exact duplicate was identified. General algorithms for arbitrary-region Slepian matrices are not claimed as new; a sufficiently general symbolic theorem not located by the search could indirectly encompass this calculation. That priority uncertainty remains explicit.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The intersection of two perpendicular observation hemispheres is a canonical non-axisymmetric region with corners. At quadratic bandwidth, it is the first spherical harmonic band where the two sign cuts couple to a mixed \(uv\) mode. The result determines an entire infinite-dimensional family of spectra by matrices of maximum size four and, more importantly, gives a universal **exact count of modes exceeding half concentration**, a sharply pinned \(1/4\) eigenspace, strict spectral gaps around both thresholds, and a unique sharp maximizing line. These quantities quantify effective information concentration and provide closed spectral benchmarks for partial spherical observation; they are not a tautological restatement of the general concentration matrix construction. No claim is made for higher bandwidth, arbitrary wedge angles, sampling stability, or priority certification.
