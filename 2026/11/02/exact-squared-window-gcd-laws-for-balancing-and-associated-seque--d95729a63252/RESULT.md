# Exact squared-window gcd laws for balancing and associated sequences

## Finding
For integer \(k\ge1\), let \(B_0=0,B_1=1\), \(C_0=1,C_1=3\), and \(c_0=-1,c_1=1\); each sequence satisfies \(X_{n+2}=6X_{n+1}-X_n\). Define the positive integer
\[
G_X(k)=\gcd_{n\ge0}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}X_{n+i}^{2}\right),\qquad d_k=\gcd(k,B_k).
\]
Then for every \(k\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{G_C(k)=d_k,\quad G_B(k)=\begin{cases}d_k,&k\text{ odd},\\d_k/2,&k\text{ even},\end{cases}\quad G_c(k)=\begin{cases}3d_k,&k\equiv3\pmod6,\\d_k,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}}
\]
The division by two is integral. This settles the balancing, Lucas-balancing and Lucas-cobalancing square-sum branches of a published four-sequence question. The cobalancing-number branch is not included.

## Assumptions and scope
Indices in each square sum begin at \(n+1\), with \(n\ge0\). A gcd over an infinite sequence means its positive gcd, equivalently the generator of the ideal of \(\mathbb Z\) generated by the terms. The three recurrences and all starting values are as written. Let \(P_0=0,P_1=1\) and \(Q_0=Q_1=1\) satisfy the Pell recurrence \(Y_{j+2}=2Y_{j+1}+Y_j\). Standard doubling identities give \(B_m=P_{2m}/2\), \(C_m=Q_{2m}\), and \(c_m=Q_{2m-1}\) for \(m\ge1\).

## Proof
**Step 1 (exact window sums).** The Pell addition identities, or geometric summation of the two algebraic-conjugate roots \(1\pm\sqrt2\), yield for every \(k\ge1\) and \(n\ge0\)
\[
\sum_{i=1}^k C_{n+i}^2=\frac{B_kQ_{4n+2k+2}+k}{2},\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^k B_{n+i}^2=\frac{B_kQ_{4n+2k+2}-k}{16},
\]
\[
\sum_{i=1}^k c_{n+i}^2=\frac{B_kQ_{4n+2k}-k}{2}.
\]
For transparency, the termwise identities are \(C_j^2=(Q_{4j}+1)/2\), \(B_j^2=(Q_{4j}-1)/16\), and \(c_j^2=(Q_{4j-2}-1)/2\). The geometric Pell sums satisfy \(\sum_{i=1}^k Q_{4(n+i)}=B_kQ_{4n+2k+2}\) and \(\sum_{i=1}^k Q_{4(n+i)-2}=B_kQ_{4n+2k}\). Summing the termwise identities yields the three displayed window formulas.

**Step 2 (gcd of the differences).** The Pell identity \(Q_{j+4}-Q_j=8P_{j+2}\) and the strong-divisibility identity \(\gcd(P_u,P_v)=P_{\gcd(u,v)}\) show for any even \(s\ge2\) that
\[
H_s:=\gcd_{n\ge0}(Q_{s+4n}-Q_s)=8P_{\gcd(s+2,4)}=\begin{cases}96,&s\equiv2\pmod4,\\16,&s\equiv0\pmod4.\end{cases}
\]
Indeed adjacent differences reduce this gcd to \(8\gcd_{n\ge0}P_{s+2+4n}\), which equals the stated value; the latter identity follows by repeated application of Pell addition and the Euclidean algorithm, using coprimality of consecutive Pell numbers. Consequently, for any fixed integer \(v\),
\[
\gcd_{n\ge0}(B_kQ_{s+4n}+v)=\gcd(B_kQ_s+v,B_kH_s).
\]
Apply this with \(v=k\) for \(C\), and \(v=-k\) for \(B,c\), and subsequently divide by the exact denominators \(2,16,2\), respectively. Thus each infinite gcd is reduced to an explicit two-integer gcd, with no appeal to truncation.

**Step 3 (odd primes).** For every prime \(p\ge5\), the multiplier \(H_s\) is prime to \(p\), and
\[
\nu_p\bigl(\gcd(B_kQ_s\pm k,B_kH_s)\bigr)=\min\{\nu_p(k),\nu_p(B_k)\}.
\]
The only additional odd prime possible is three. Modulo three, \(B_{2j}\equiv0\) and \(3\nmid B_{2j+1}\). For even \(k\), one has \(\nu_3(B_k)>\nu_3(k)\): at even indices not divisible by three the left valuation is already positive, and each subsequent tripling multiplies \(B_m\) by \(4C_m^2-1\), divisible by three for even \(m\). When \(k\) is even the value \(s=2k+2\) is \(2\pmod4\), and \(Q_s\) is divisible by three. It follows that the \(3\)-part of the relevant \(C\)- and \(B\)-numerators is exactly that of \(k\), even though \(H_s=96\) has one extra factor of three. When \(k\) is odd, \(B_k\) is prime to three, so the \(B,C\) gcds gain no factor three; for the \(c\)-case \(s=2k\equiv2\pmod4\), \(Q_s\equiv0\pmod3\), and \(H_s=96\), yielding precisely one extra factor of three when \(3\mid k\). In the remaining cases \(H_s=16\), with no extra three.

**Step 4 (the prime two).** Duplication \(B_{2m}=2B_mC_m\) and oddness of \(C_m\) imply \(\nu_2(B_k)=\nu_2(k)=a\). Straight recurrence induction modulo sixteen gives
\[
Q_{4j}\equiv1\pmod{16},\quad Q_{4j+2}\equiv3\pmod{16},\quad C_{2j}\equiv1\pmod{16},\quad C_{2j+1}\equiv3\pmod{16}.
\]
For odd \(k\), recurrence reduction gives \(B_k\equiv k\pmod{16}\). For even \(k\), repeated doubling gives \(B_k/k\equiv3\pmod{16}\) in \(\mathbb Z_2\), i.e. after dividing out their common power of two; this also follows from the odd-index congruence and the above \(C_m\) congruences. If \(k\) is odd, the numerators for \(C,c\) have valuation exactly one, whereas the \(B\)-numerator is divisible by sixteen. If \(k\) is even, the respective numerator valuations of \(C,c,B\) are \(a+1,a+1,a+3\). Comparing with \(\nu_2(B_kH_s)\), which equals \(a+4\) or \(a+5\), and dividing the relevant gcd by \(2,2,16\), gives precisely \(\nu_2(d_k)\) for \(C,c\), and \(\nu_2(d_k)-1\) for \(B\) when \(k\) is even; at odd \(k\) all three have zero 2-adic valuation. Together with Step 3 this proves every displayed identity.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone Python file `verification/verify.py` independently builds each recurrence, computes direct finite sums and their gcds for all \(1\le k\le128\) and all starting positions \(0\le n\le40\), checks the closed formulas, and separately checks Pell representations and differences. It reports 384 finite window-gcd comparisons, 15,744 exact window-expression comparisons, 128 tests each of doubling, tripling and Pell identities, and 520 Pell difference checks. The infinity-wide gcd and all-index theorem are justified by Steps 1–4, not by the finite computation.

## Relationship to prior work
Mbirika, Schrader and Spilker (2023, arXiv:2301.05758, Question 30) explicitly ask for square-sum gcd formulas for balancing, Lucas-balancing, cobalancing and Lucas-cobalancing sequences. Their numerical remarks include the factor-two and factor-three patterns, so those observations are not new; the contribution claimed here is proof for three named families and a uniform closed index-gcd formula \(d_k\). Mbirika and Spilker (2022, arXiv:2204.12626) study squares of generalized Fibonacci recurrences with a different characteristic polynomial, so those results do not directly imply the present coefficient-six identities. Other Lucas periods, prime-local realizability, and reciprocal-power balancing-tail results do not state the squared-window formulas. A broad bibliographic search cannot rule out every implicit older derivation.

## Limitations
The cobalancing-number squared-window case of the published four-sequence question is not solved here. The formula \(\gcd(k,B_k)\) is exact but does not independently classify all primes and powers dividing that gcd; further such classification may remain interesting. The recurrence proof uses standard Pell strong-divisibility properties, summarized rather than presented as an independent novel theorem. Scientific overlap was checked using accessible published texts and mathematical records, but earlier implicit coverage remains possible. Independent validation has not been performed.
