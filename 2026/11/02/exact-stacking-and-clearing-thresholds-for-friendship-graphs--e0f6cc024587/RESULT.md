# Exact stacking and clearing thresholds for friendship graphs

## Finding
For every integer \(m\ge1\), let \(F_m=K_1\vee(mK_2)\) be the graph of \(m\) edge-disjoint triangles sharing a universal center. Under ordinary undirected pebbling, a move removes two pebbles from a vertex and places one at an adjacent vertex. Write \(\operatorname{stack}(G)\) for the least integer \(t\ge2\) for which every configuration of \(t\) pebbles can reach a positive pile on one vertex, and \(\operatorname{clear}(G)\) for the least such \(t\) for which every configuration can reach a single pebble. Then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{stack}(F_m)=\operatorname{clear}(F_m)=4m.}
\]

For \(m\ge2\), a sharp nonstackable configuration has \(2m+1\) pebbles on one peripheral vertex, no pebbles on its mate or the center, and one pebble on each of the other \(2m-2\) peripheral vertices. Its mass is \(4m-1\). For \(m=1\), one pebble at each of the three vertices obstructs mass three.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, undirected and simple; vertices and configurations are labelled. An arbitrary configuration is a function \(c:V(F_m)\to\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) with total mass \(\sum_v c(v)\). A legal move \(u\to v\), along an edge, replaces \(c(u)\) by \(c(u)-2\) and \(c(v)\) by \(c(v)+1\). It decreases the mass by one. The center is \(h\), and each peripheral edge has its two endpoints \(a_i,b_i\). The theorem concerns all configurations of the specified mass, not merely uniformly distributed ones, and does not require a prescribed final vertex.

## Proof
**1. Clearing all configurations of mass \(4m\).** We give an explicit center-gathering construction. First, a petal with \(s\ge4\) pebbles on its two endpoints (and no reliance on center pebbles) can be emptied into the center, depositing at least \(\lfloor(s-2)/2\rfloor\) pebbles there. For \(s=4\), the three configurations up to exchanging endpoints are \((4,0),(3,1),(2,2)\). The first and third send at least one pebble directly to the center. For \((3,1)\), the legal moves \(a\to b,b\to a,a\to h\) leave one at the center and zero on both endpoints. For \(s=5\), the possibilities \((5,0),(4,1),(3,2)\) are cleared without borrowing from the center: respectively, \(a\to b,a\to b,b\to a,a\to h\); \(a\to b,a\to h,b\to h\); and \(b\to a,a\to h,a\to h\). (Relabel the endpoints if necessary.) For \(s\ge6\), move once from an endpoint containing at least two pebbles directly into the center and induct on the remaining \(s-2\) peripheral pebbles. The total deposit is at least \(1+\lfloor(s-4)/2\rfloor=\lfloor(s-2)/2\rfloor\).

Each petal with fewer than four pebbles can now be prepared independently as follows. An empty petal contributes nothing. A petal with a single pebble is left as one residual unit. A petal of type \((2,0)\) sends one to the center without residue. A petal of type \((1,1)\) leaves both peripheral singles. A petal of type \((2,1)\) uses \(a\to b,b\to h\) (starting with two at \(a\)) to empty itself and contribute one center pebble. A petal of type \((3,0)\) sends one center pebble and retains one peripheral pebble. Let \(p_i\) be the guaranteed number deposited in the center by preparation of petal \(i\), and let \(r_i\in\{0,1\}\) indicate whether that petal has a nonempty residue. For initial petal mass \(s_i\), the case analysis yields

\[
p_i-r_i\ge (s_i-4)/2,
\]

with strict inequality except possibly for a petal initially of type \((1,1)\), for which equality holds. Denote the original center pile by \(h_0\), the center mass after preparation by \(H=h_0+\sum_i p_i\), and the number of residual petals by \(R=\sum_i r_i\). Since \(h_0+\sum_i s_i=4m\), summation gives \(H-R\ge h_0/2\). In fact \(H-R>0\): if equality held, then \(h_0=0\) and every petal would have to be \((1,1)\), giving total mass \(2m\), not \(4m\). Hence \(H\ge R+1\).

Every residual petal can be cleared using two pebbles from the center and returning one pebble, thus losing one center pebble in net. For a single peripheral pebble, use \(h\to a,a\to h\). For \((1,1)\), use \(h\to a,a\to b,b\to h\). Process the \(R\) residual petals in any order. Before each operation the center has at least two pebbles, since \(H\ge R+1\). The final configuration has only \(H-R\ge1\) pebbles at the center. A center pile of size one or two clears immediately, and a center pile of at least four pebbles is clearable inside any incident triangle by the elementary complete-graph clearing lemma: every configuration on \(K_3\) of total mass at least four with a vertex containing at least two pebbles is clearable (proof of Theorem 8.1 in the cited source). This already handles all cases except \(H-R=3\).

**2. The three-pebble center exception.** If \(H-R=3\) and \(R\ge1\), clear only \(R-1\) residual petals. The center then contains exactly four pebbles, and one residual petal remains with one or two pebbles. Restrict to the triangle on the center and that petal: it has at least five pebbles and is clearable by the same complete-graph lemma. If \(R=0\), then \(H=3\). At least one original petal has a positive preparation deposit \(p_i\): otherwise the initial configuration would have mass at most three (the center contains three and all other petals are empty). Choose such a petal and postpone its preparation, performing all other preparations first. The center then contains \(3-p_i\ge0\) pebbles; the selected petal still contains its initial \(s_i\ge2\) pebbles. For every positive-deposit case of the preparation table, \(p_i\le s_i-1\), so the selected triangle has at least \(3-p_i+s_i\ge4\) pebbles. At least one vertex on this triangle has two pebbles (the positive-deposit patterns are \((2,0),(2,1)\), or have petal mass at least four). The complete-graph lemma clears this triangle. Thus every configuration of total mass \(4m\) can be cleared, giving \(\operatorname{clear}(F_m)\le4m\); clearing implies stacking.

**3. Sharp obstruction to stacking at mass \(4m-1\).** Suppose \(m\ge2\), and let \(c_*\) have \(2m+1\) at \(a_1\), zero at \(b_1,h\), and \((1,1)\) on every other petal. Consider any pebbling sequence, and let \(o_i,u_i,s_i\) count moves from center to petal \(i\), from petal \(i\) to center, and inside petal \(i\), respectively. If a petal is empty at the end and initially contained \(w_i\) pebbles, summing its two vertex-balance equations gives \(2u_i+s_i=w_i+o_i\).

For an initially \((1,1)\) petal cleared at the end, at least one center-to-petal move must occur, and \(u_i\le o_i\), so it consumes a net center budget \(2o_i-u_i\ge1\). For the heavy petal initially \((2m+1,0)\), if emptied, its net center export is \(u_1-2o_1=(2m+1-3o_1-s_1)/2\le m-1\). Indeed this is immediate if \(o_1\ge1\); if \(o_1=0\), at least three internal moves are necessary, because to remove the odd original pile, a move from the originally empty mate back into its endpoint is necessary, preceded by at least two moves into that mate. Therefore no positive center stack can be reached: all the other \(m-1\) petals would consume at least \(m-1\) center pebbles, while the heavy petal supplies at most \(m-1\), and the center was initially empty.

If a stack were reached at a peripheral vertex with one or two pebbles, the configuration would be clearable; the same holds for a peripheral stack with at least four pebbles by the complete-graph clearing lemma on its triangle. Any clearing sequence has, immediately before its last move, exactly two pebbles on the source vertex; if the source is peripheral, that move could instead end at the universal center, and if the source is the center, a center stack was already reached. Therefore every clearable configuration can reach a positive center stack. The displayed impossibility rules out all these peripheral stack sizes.

For a peripheral stack of size three on one of the originally \((1,1)\) petals, the heavy petal must be emptied (export at most \(m-1\)); each of the other \(m-2\) initially \((1,1)\) petals consumes at least one center pebble; and the target petal, which grows from two pebbles to three, consumes at least two center pebbles (its balance is \(o-2u-s=1\), giving \(2o-u\ge2\)). This demands at least \(m\) center pebbles, a contradiction.

For a peripheral stack of size three on the heavy petal, the net heavy export is \(m-1-(3o_1+s_1)/2\). The \(m-1\) other petals need at least \(m-1\) net center pebbles, so \(o_1=s_1=0\). In particular the center cannot subsequently transfer pebbles to the heavy petal. But the last move involving any of the other petals, all of which end empty, must be a move into the center, leaving at least one center pebble permanently. This contradicts the alleged peripheral-only final stack.

Thus \(c_*\) is nonstackable. For \(m=1\), the all-ones configuration on \(K_3\) is nonstackable at mass three. To pass from one nonstackable configuration at mass \(4m-1\) to the numerical lower bound, note that no mass \(t\) with \(2\le t\le|V(F_m)|\) universally guarantees stacking (place one pebble on each of \(t\) vertices). If universal stacking held at any mass above the vertex count, it would hold at every greater mass: every larger configuration has a legal move, giving a configuration of one less pebble, and induction applies. Hence \(\operatorname{stack}(F_m)\ge4m\) for all \(m\ge1\).

## Verification
The standalone `VERIFY.py` evaluates reachability by dynamic programming on configurations, identifying only actual automorphisms generated by exchanging the two vertices in each petal and permuting petals. It exhausts the canonical configurations for masses up to \(4m\) at \(m=1,2,3,4,5\). For each such \(m\), all configurations of mass \(4m\) are stackable and clearable and the mass \(4m-1\) obstruction is nonstackable. These finite checks are corroborative; they do not replace the infinite proof. The two threshold parameters need separate proof conclusions.

## Relationship to prior work
Csernák and Soukup, *Stacking and clearing in graph pebbling*, arXiv:2604.22341 (first public 2026-04-24), introduce these parameters and prove exact formulas for complete graphs, complete bipartite and complete multipartite graphs, and paths, with conditional cycle formulas. Their Sections 1, 2, 8 and 11 do not give this result for the triangle bouquet \(K_1\vee(mK_2)\); it is neither a tree nor a complete multipartite graph when \(m\ge2\). Fairfax-Ball, *The stacking number of a tree*, arXiv:2609.31811, addresses trees, not these triangle-containing graphs. A directed variant of the model (arXiv:2606.04659) has distinct orientation requirements. A targeted search did not locate an equivalent equality for friendship graphs. Absence from search results alone is not a proof of priority.

## Limitations
The theorem addresses friendship graphs and the two ordinary undirected pebbling parameters, not arbitrary generalized windmills, directed pebbling, or complete extremal-obstruction classifications. The finite replay supports rather than proves the universal statement; the proof depends on the complete-graph triangle clearing lemma, which is available in the cited primary full text. Historical-priority uncertainty is retained.
