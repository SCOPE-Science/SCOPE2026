# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The uniform path, one terminal Lindblad sink, arbitrary density matrix and positive-rate domain are precisely specified. Backward recurrence excludes eigenvectors with zero terminal amplitude, implying a Hurwitz no-jump generator and uniqueness of the survival Gramian. Four elementary parity-and-boundary matrix identities produce the claimed exact Lyapunov solution. The Brownian-bridge covariance representation proves \(F\) positive definite and \(G\) positive semidefinite with one-dimensional kernel, yielding the optimizer and its sole boundary exception. The standalone checker independently reconstructs 180 Lyapunov systems, 900 coherent-state expectations and all structural identities for 29 dimensions. No infinite-dimensional inference rests on the finite checks.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence
The strongest closely related sources inspected in full are the trapped-line survival study by Mülken et al. (arXiv:0705.3700), the projective-detection study by Varbanov et al. (arXiv:0803.3446), the several-geometry absorber study by Krapivsky et al. (arXiv:1311.6298), and the semi-infinite absorbing-boundary solution by Riberi (arXiv:2605.08056). They do not state or logically furnish the all-initial-state finite-path integrated operator and the parity-selective optimal sink. A known three-site rate-dependence is a special case and is not asserted as novel. Full historical priority remains uncertain because some older sources, especially Parris (1989), were unavailable in full.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A single formula resolves the exact lifetime and global sink-rate design for every finite path length and initial coherent density matrix. It identifies the non-obvious structural separation of same-parity coherence (rate optimization) from opposite-parity coherence (rate-independent lifetime contribution). These are physically natural figures of merit for irreversible excitation capture and are not mere reparameterizations of a single-site calculation.
