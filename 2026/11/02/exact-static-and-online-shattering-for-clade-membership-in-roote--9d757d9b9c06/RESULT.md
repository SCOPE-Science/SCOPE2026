# Exact static and online shattering for clade membership in rooted and unrooted trees

## Finding
Let \(X\) be a set of \(n\ge2\) labeled leaves, and let \(Q(X)=\{A\subset X:2\le |A|\le n-1\}\). On each rooted phylogenetic tree \(T\), the Boolean answer to query \(A\in Q(X)\) is one if and only if \(A\) is the exact leaf set below an internal, non-root vertex (a nontrivial proper clade). Consider both the class \(\mathcal H_n\) of all possibly nonbinary rooted phylogenetic trees and its fully binary subclass \(\mathcal B_n\). The exact dimensions are, for every \(n\ge2\),
\[
\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal H_n)=\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal H_n)
=\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal B_n)=\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal B_n)=n-2.
\]
For each \(n\ge3\), the analogous Boolean split-membership concept classes of **unrooted**, either fully binary or arbitrary phylogenetic trees on \(n\) labeled leaves have both dimensions \(n-3\). A split query asks whether an unordered bipartition \(\{A,X\setminus A\}\) with both sides of cardinality at least two is induced by an internal edge. These statements concern Boolean membership of a whole prescribed subset/split, **not** rooted-triplet labels, pairwise similarity, or query-identification complexity.

## Assumptions and scope
A *hierarchy* on \(X\) is a family \(\mathscr F\subseteq Q(X)\) such that any two members are disjoint or one contains the other (laminarity). Singletons and \(X\) are implicit, not queried. Every hierarchy has a rooted phylogenetic-tree representation, permitting multifurcations; a fully binary tree corresponds exactly to a maximal hierarchy of size \(n-2\). Thus each concept is the indicator of its hierarchy in \(\{0,1\}^{Q(X)}\). Standard VC shattering uses a single fixed set of queries; Littlestone shattering uses a complete binary query tree, with a distinct consistent concept available at every answer leaf. For \(n=2\), the query domain is empty and dimensions are zero. In the unrooted case, trees have no degree-two internal vertices and may have multifurcations; queries are internal splits only.

## Proof
**1. Basic hierarchy bound.** Every rooted hierarchy has at most \(n-2\) nontrivial proper clades. Indeed a rooted tree with \(n\) leaves and every internal outdegree at least two has at most \(n-1\) internal vertices (sum the outdegree minus one over internal vertices), with the root not counted as a proper clade. Therefore, if a set \(S\) of \(m\) clade queries is VC-shattered by any hierarchy class, the all-positive response vector forces \(S\) to be laminar and \(m\le n-2\). This establishes both VC upper bounds.

**2. Exact static lower bound for binary trees.** Label the leaves \(1,\dots,n\), and consider the \(n-2\) prefix queries \(P_i=\{1,\dots,i\}\), \(2\le i\le n-1\). For any selected set \(I\) of these indices, start with the singleton tree on leaf \(1\), and successively graft the existing tree to a binary rooted tree whose leaves are the next consecutive block, stopping precisely at each index in \(I\) and finally at \(n\). At each stopping index \(i<n\), the new root has exact descendant clade \(P_i\); every intermediate prefix between stopping indices crosses the two children and is not a clade. Hence the trace on \(\{P_2,\dots,P_{n-1}\}\) is exactly the prescribed arbitrary labeling \(I\). This proves \(\operatorname{VCdim}(\mathcal B_n)\ge n-2\); inclusion gives the same for all hierarchies. The displayed construction works also for an empty selected set.

**3. Positive-branch factorization.** Fix \(A\in Q(X)\), and write \(k=|A|\), where \(2\le k\le n-1\). Conditioning on the positive answer \(A\in\mathscr F\), every other clade belongs to one of three types: a proper subset of \(A\), a subset disjoint from \(A\), or a proper superset of \(A\). A set that crosses \(A\) must answer zero. Let \(a_*\) replace the contracted block \(A\), and put \(Y=(X\setminus A)\cup\{a_*\}\), so \(|Y|=n-k+1\). The proper subclades of \(A\) constitute an arbitrary hierarchy on \(k\) labels; the clades disjoint from or properly containing \(A\), after replacing \(A\) with \(a_*\), constitute an arbitrary hierarchy on \(Y\). Conversely, any pair of such hierarchies lifts uniquely to a hierarchy containing \(A\). This is a genuine bijection of concepts: the positive-answer version space is the Cartesian product \(\mathcal H_k\times\mathcal H_{n-k+1}\) on disjoint nonconstant query coordinates, with constant answers to all other queries.

**4. Product online-dimension identity and induction.** If finite Boolean concept classes \(\mathcal F\) and \(\mathcal G\) have disjoint query domains, then \(\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal F\times\mathcal G)=\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal F)+\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal G)\). The lower bound concatenates complete mistake trees. For the upper bound, use induction on total dimension: every root query addresses only one factor, and in at least one answer branch the dimension of that queried factor drops strictly (otherwise its original Littlestone dimension would be larger), while the other factor remains unchanged. Thus a complete tree cannot have depth exceeding the sum. Constants do not increase dimension.

By strong induction on \(n\), suppose the equality \(\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal H_j)=j-2\) holds at all smaller sizes \(j\ge2\). For any first query \(A\) of cardinality \(k\), the positive branch has dimension
\[
(k-2)+(n-k+1-2)=n-3,
\]
by step 3 and induction. Hence a complete depth-\(n-1\) query tree cannot exist: its positive child would need depth \(n-2\). This proves \(\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal H_n)\le n-2\). The case \(n=2\) has no nonconstant queries. Since \(\operatorname{VCdim}\le\operatorname{Ldim}\) and \(\mathcal B_n\subseteq\mathcal H_n\), the four rooted equalities follow.

**5. Unrooted transfer.** Fix a distinguished outgroup leaf \(o\). Removing \(o\) and rooting at the vertex formerly adjacent to it is a bijection between unrooted phylogenetic trees on \(n\) leaves and rooted phylogenetic trees on \(X\setminus\{o\}\), preserving binary versus nonbinary status. Every nontrivial unrooted split has precisely one side that excludes \(o\), of size between two and \(n-2\). This side is exactly a proper nontrivial rooted clade, and the split answer equals the clade answer. The two Boolean classes are therefore isomorphic, with query sets in bijection. Replacing \(n\) by \(n-1\) gives \(n-3\).

## Verification
The standalone Python 3 standard-library verifier `verify_hierarchy.py` exhaustively builds the canonical binary tree representations, derives clade-incidence vectors, and applies the exact depth recursion \(D(S)\ge d\) if and only if some query has both Boolean branches of dimension at least \(d-1\). It checks the all-size proof against exact cases \(2\le n\le7\) for fully binary trees (up to 10,395 hypotheses), \(2\le n\le6\) for arbitrary hierarchies (up to 2,752 hypotheses), and every labeling of the prefix witness sets in this finite range. It also checks the proposed Cartesian-product positive-branch bijections for \(n=3,4,5\). A successful run terminates with `CLUSTER_QUERY_ALL_CHECKS_PASS`. These are finite cross-checks of an independently stated proof, not a substitute for the all-size induction.

## Relationship to prior work
Hendriksen and Francis (2019) study rooted phylogenetic hierarchies, their refinement ordering, and cluster-similarity distances. Their tree/hierarchy formalism supplies the natural objects, but the inspected manuscript does not give this clade-query VC/Littlestone theorem. Avdiukhin et al. (2023) establish online-learning bounds for hierarchical trees from **labeled triplets**, which are not single-clade Boolean membership evaluations; neither the labels nor the query domains coincide, and their asymptotic multiclass results do not imply the equality above. Chase, Freitag and Reyzin (2024) analyze general Littlestone-based query-learning relations but do not supply this exact hierarchy factorization in the inspected text. Earlier work on adaptive hierarchical clustering using ordinal queries similarly poses a different query semantics. The cardinality of the fully binary class is \((2n-3)!!\), so its logarithmic size grows on the \(\Theta(n\log n)\) scale; the exact clade-membership online dimension grows only as \(n-2\). This underscores how the query language, not merely the number of trees, determines adaptive shattering capacity.

## Limitations
No claims are made about sample complexity or Littlestone dimension for triplet-valued, quartet-valued, pairwise-ordinal, or partial/noisy clade queries; allowing membership queries of *arbitrary* whole subsets is essential. This does not compute the number of adaptive questions required for exact identification. Historical priority is qualified by the accessible literature comparisons; obscure equivalent formulations may exist. The structural proof is independent of the finite enumeration, and no external independent audit has been performed.
