# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

The finite asynchronous rate-one noisy-voter model refreshes each vertex to a fair ±1 spin with probability p and otherwise copies a uniform-neighbor spin. For each unordered pair, the stationary generator yields 2Cij=s sum P(i,k)Ckj+s sum P(j,k)Cik, Cii=1. In K1∨nKm the three orbit equations solve to a=s/[2+(m−2)p], b=s/[1+(m−1)p], U=sF/(2−sG), V=a(1+U), W=bU. G<2 gives a positive denominator and W<U. Direct independent sympy simplification confirms b(2−sG)−sF=p(n−1)(1−p)(m+1−p)/[n(1+(m−1)p)^2]>0, so V−U=(1−a)(b−U)>0. For n/m→rho>0, asymptotic expansion gives the stated p=lambda/m², lambda/m, and m^-2<<p<<m^-1 limits, with conditions on fixed lambda/rho. Independent exact-rational solutions for (n,m,p)=(2,2,1/4),(3,4,1/3),(5,6,3/5) agree; these are corroborative, not asymptotic proof.

## originality

PASS

Best-of-knowledge PASS for the complete explicitly solved three-orbit finite-graph stationary correlations and two distinct noise-scale regimes, not for the voter process, generator/duality relation or generic stationary existence. Pymar–Rivera study macroscopic density laws and examples complete/cycle/torus/hypercube, not K1∨nKm orbit pairs. Benjamini et al use parallel updates and temporal repetition statistics. Peralta et al present pair-approximation treatments, not an accessible exact windmill formula. Resultary query hits concern unrelated covariance invariants and windmill geometric features. A stochastic duality framework may permit computation but does not itself publish these evaluated formulas.

## value

PASS

Exact spatial-correlation ordering V>U>W is counterintuitive because hub–rim are adjacent while same-petal rims can be more correlated, and two distinct p scales m^-2 and m^-1 separate global alignment from within-petal persistence. The combination is a natural parameter-uniform classification of a widely used stochastic network model on a symmetric but heterogeneous graph; the crossovers are not a cheap restatement of generic noisy-voter drift identities.
