# Exact strong \(\ell^2\) norm and all extremizers of the centered maximal operator on the four-cycle

## Finding

Let \(C_4\) denote the simple four-vertex cycle with graph distance and counting measure. Its centered Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator \(M\) has the **exact optimal strong-type constant**
\[
\boxed{\|M\|_{\ell^2(C_4)\to\ell^2(C_4)}=\frac{\sqrt{7+2\sqrt{10}}}{3}.}
\]
Every nonzero extremizer is characterized exactly: after cyclic rotation and multiplication by a positive scalar, its coordinatewise absolute values are
\[
\boxed{(1,\,3+\sqrt{10},\,1,\,3+\sqrt{10}).}
\]
Conversely, every vector with those moduli after rotation, positive scaling, and independently chosen coordinate signs attains equality. Thus the sharp strong \(\ell^2\) bound is attained and has a rigid alternating two-level modulus pattern.

## Assumptions and scope

Label vertices \(V=\{0,1,2,3\}\), cyclically, and let \(d\) be their shortest-path graph distance. For each real-valued \(f:V\to\mathbb R\), define
\[
Mf(v)=\max_{r\in\{0,1,2\}}\frac{1}{|B(v,r)|}\sum_{w\in B(v,r)}|f(w)|,
\qquad B(v,r)=\{w:d(v,w)\le r\}.
\]
Use the ordinary counting-measure \(\ell^2\) norm, \(\|f\|_2=(\sum_v|f(v)|^2)^{1/2}\). The asserted norm is the supremum of \(\|Mf\|_2/\|f\|_2\) over nonzero real inputs. Radii \(0,1,2\) enumerate all distinct centered balls. The theorem is limited to this graph, this maximal-operator normalization, and this exponent.

## Proof

**1. Exact linearization.** For each choice vector \(\alpha=(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_3)\in\{0,1,2\}^4\), let \(A_\alpha\) be the nonnegative stochastic matrix whose \(v\)-th row is the normalized indicator of \(B(v,\alpha_v)\). Its rows are, respectively, \(e_v\), \((e_{v-1}+e_v+e_{v+1})/3\), and \((e_0+e_1+e_2+e_3)/4\). For any nonnegative \(g\), simultaneous maximization of the four independently chosen row averages gives
\[
\|Mg\|_2^2=\max_{\alpha\in\{0,1,2\}^4}\|A_\alpha g\|_2^2.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\|Mf\|_2^2\le\bigl(\max_\alpha\lambda_{\max}(A_\alpha^{\mathsf T}A_\alpha)\bigr)\|f\|_2^2.
\]
There are exactly \(3^4=81\) configurations; no continuum of radii or inputs is being discretized in this reduction.

**2. Exhaustive upper certificate.** A complete exact-rational enumeration, reproduced by the standalone program `verify_c4.py`, proves that for every choice except \((1,0,1,0)\) and \((0,1,0,1)\),
\[
\frac{29}{20}I-A_\alpha^{\mathsf T}A_\alpha\succ0.
\]
The program constructs the matrices from their ball definitions as rational fractions; it checks all four leading principal minors of each of the 79 resulting symmetric matrices, with positive results, so Sylvester's criterion supplies a *complete* positive-definiteness certificate. The two exceptional choices are cyclic conjugates.

**3. Exceptional spectrum.** For \(\alpha_*=(1,0,1,0)\) the Gram matrix is
\[
B_* = A_{\alpha_*}^{\mathsf T}A_{\alpha_*}
=\frac19\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0&1\\1&11&1&2\\0&1&1&1\\1&2&1&11\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its characteristic polynomial is
\[
\det(tI-B_*)=\frac{(t-1)(9t-1)(9t^2-14t+1)}{81}.
\]
Thus the largest eigenvalue is \(\Lambda=(7+2\sqrt{10})/9\), strictly greater than \(29/20\) (for example \(40\sqrt{10}>121\)). The simple top eigenspace is generated by the strictly positive vector
\[
g_*=(1,\,3+\sqrt{10},\,1,\,3+\sqrt{10})^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The displayed matrix and eigenvector identity are independently checked with exact coefficients in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{10})\) by the verifier. Therefore every other radius choice has strictly smaller spectral norm, and the linearization yields \(\|M\|_2^2\le\Lambda\).

**4. Sharpness and classification.** Put \(b=3+\sqrt{10}>1\). At each low entry of \(g_*=(1,b,1,b)\), the radius-one average \((1+2b)/3\) strictly dominates both the singleton value \(1\) and the global average \((1+b)/2\). At each high entry, the singleton \(b\) strictly dominates its radius-one and global averages. Hence \(Mg_*=A_{\alpha_*}g_*\), so its Rayleigh quotient is exactly \(\Lambda\) and equality holds. Conversely, equality for \(f\ne0\) forces \(|f|\) into a top eigenspace for one of the two exceptional radius choices, since all other configurations lie strictly below \(29/20<\Lambda\). The two top eigenspaces are rotations of the single positive vector above, and all absolute coordinate signs are immaterial to \(M\). This proves both the optimal constant and every equality case.

It verifies \(79\) rational strict positive-definiteness certificates and precisely the two alternating exceptions, the exceptional characteristic polynomial, the \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{10})\) eigenvector equation, the strict maximizing-ball choices, and the rational comparison with \(29/20\). Because the domain has four vertices and each has exactly three possible centered balls, this is a finite exhaustive proof component rather than numerical evidence extrapolated to an infinite setting.

## Relationship to prior work

Soria and Tradacete (2014), arXiv:1410.6204, define the centered graph maximal operator and in Example 2.6 evaluate the four-cycle's \(\ell^1\) norm as \(23/12\). Their strong \(\ell^p\) results in that article do not supply the present \(\ell^2(C_4)\) constant or the equality classification. González-Riquelme and Madrid (2020), arXiv:2005.03146, obtain sharp \(\ell^2\) norms for complete graphs and stars, which are different graph families; their follow-up arXiv:2011.02630 develops those models and variation estimates. The present statement distinguishes the noncomplete, nonstar four-cycle from these benchmark cases. No publication-priority claim is made beyond the compared sources and the recorded overlap search.

## Limitations

The result is specific to the centered (not uncentered) graph-ball maximal operator, the ordinary unweighted four-cycle, and the \(\ell^2\) exponent. It is not an exact formula for all cycles, all bipartite graphs, weighted measures, or variation seminorms. Exhaustive finite certification covers the four-vertex domain only. The literature comparison is subject to possible earlier unindexed equivalent calculations; it is not an independent scholarly certification.
