# Review of the four-cycle centered maximal operator result

## Correctness

**PASS.** For the four-cycle every centered ball is one of three explicit sets, and the squared maximal-output norm is exactly the maximum over \(3^4=81\) rational averaging matrices. The accompanying exact-rational verifier tests all \(79\) nonalternating matrices against a \(29/20\) spectral ceiling via four strict Sylvester minors each. The other two matrices are cyclic conjugates; one has the displayed factored characteristic polynomial and simple largest eigenvalue \((7+2\sqrt{10})/9>29/20\). A positive eigenvector activates exactly its claimed radius choices, giving sharpness. The simplicity/strict-gap argument yields all extremizers. The proof covers all nonzero real inputs, not sampled inputs.

## Originality

**PASS, with publication-priority qualifications.** Soria and Tradacete (arXiv:1410.6204, Example 2.6) give the four-cycle's exact \(\ell^1\) constant \(23/12\), not its strong \(\ell^2\) norm. González-Riquelme and Madrid (arXiv:2005.03146 and arXiv:2011.02630v1) establish sharp \(\ell^2\) norms/extremizers for cliques and stars and other variation results, not the square graph's two-level \(\ell^2\) extremizers. A semantic-record comparison found similarly named finite cyclic operator results for other norms or operators; those statements do not imply this claim. Earlier equivalent unindexed calculations may nevertheless exist; search absence is not definitive novelty proof.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The first noncomplete finite cycle is a natural test case left outside existing complete/star sharp \(\ell^2\) classifications. This exact value fills a motivated norm gap while distinguishing a genuinely two-level sharp input from singleton indicator extremizers. The complete spectral selection argument provides a natural benchmark for finite-graph maximal-operator stability and equality patterns.
