# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness — PASS
For degree three, the open-neighborhood parity condition is exactly monochromatic or rainbow triples. Properness and the four named colors force a unique successor of every legal ordered column state, with a direct algebraic case split ensuring the successor column is proper. The 84 states are partitioned into 12 reversed, 48 partially reversed, and 24 complementary states; the explicit representative cycles and color-name symmetry prove minimal periods 2, 6, and 4 respectively. Thus odd periods have no four-palette colorings. The independently checked length-4, length-5 and length-7 blocks have matching first/last column seams, and every larger odd length is a 4-block concatenation; even lengths have valid 2-colorings. The triangular prism equals the Cartesian product of two complete graphs and its exact value six follows from the published exact theorem. Computations corroborate all steps but are not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)
The strongest nearby published full-text source is Caro et al. (arXiv:2410.02336v1): its Theorem 4.2 supplies a general product upper bound and its Theorem 4.4(i) already covers the triangular prism. Neither theorem yields the all-odd-prism five-color optimum or the exact four-palette cycle count. Pradhan et al. (arXiv:2610.01441v1) studies complexity and algorithms; the accessible abstract/excerpt does not present the present formulas, but its entire full text was not available. The earlier Kwon–Park work treats strong odd colorings of sparse graphs under general hypotheses and gives no specific prism classification in the material inspected. Searches of mathematical literature and similar research records using prism, ladder, Cartesian-product, periodic-census, and strong-odd aliases found no covering statement. An unindexed or inaccessible result remains possible.

## Value — PASS
Cycle prisms are a canonical bounded-treewidth cubic family already appearing naturally in Cartesian-product coloring theorems. The exact gap between odd and even circumferences is not implied by generic complexity or product bounds, while the 84-state permutation exhibits a rigid, complete 2/4/6-period classification and an exact arithmetic coloring census. The infinite sharp parameter and full finite-palette enumeration are meaningful structural answers for this recently studied coloring invariant, not just small sample computations.

## Limitations and closest sources
This concerns strong odd coloring, not the weaker odd coloring; four named palette colors are available even when unused. The \(n=3\) value is explicitly prior published work (Caro et al., Theorem 4.4(i)). Full text for the motivating 2026 preprint was not inspected; therefore historical priority is qualified. No classification for other cubic product families is claimed, and no independent review was performed.
