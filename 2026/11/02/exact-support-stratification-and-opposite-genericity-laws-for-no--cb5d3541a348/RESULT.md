# Exact-support stratification and opposite genericity laws for nominal binary relations

## Finding

Let \(\mathbb A\) be any infinite set of equality atoms, let \(G\) be its finite-permutation group, and fix a named context \(S\subseteq\mathbb A\) of size \(k\geq0\). An \(S\)-supported relation is invariant under every permutation fixing \(S\) pointwise. Its **least support** is the smallest finite set with this property.

Write \(B_n\) for the Bell numbers (\(B_0=1\)) and \(a_n\) for the number of partitions of an \(n\)-element set without singleton blocks, with \(a_0=1\). Then
\[
a_n=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^j\binom nj B_{n-j}.
\]

**Exact-support census for equivalences.** Among the \(S\)-supported equivalence relations on \(\mathbb A\), precisely
\[
\boxed{H_k(d)=\binom{k}{d}(B_{k-d}+a_{k-d})}\qquad(0\leq d\leq k)
\]
have least support of cardinality \(k-d\). In particular, their total number is \(B_{k+1}+B_k\), and precisely \(B_k+a_k\) have least support *equal to* \(S\).

**Comparison with arbitrary binary relations.** There are precisely
\[
T_k=2^{k^2+2k+2}
\]
\(S\)-supported binary relations on \(\mathbb A\), of which precisely
\[
F_k=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom kj2^{j^2+2j+2}
\]
have least support equal to \(S\).

Uniformly and independently sample an \(S\)-supported equivalence relation or an unrestricted \(S\)-supported binary relation from its respective finite class. As \(k\to\infty\), with \(W\) the principal Lambert function defined by \(W(k)e^{W(k)}=k\),
\[
\boxed{\frac{F_k}{T_k}=1-k2^{-(2k+1)}+O(k^2 2^{-4k})},
\qquad
\boxed{\frac{B_k+a_k}{B_{k+1}+B_k}\sim\frac{W(k)}{k}\longrightarrow0}.
\]
Furthermore, for a uniformly sampled supported equivalence relation \(E_k\),
\[
\boxed{\frac{k-|\operatorname{supp}(E_k)|}{W(k)}\ \xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{prob.}\ }\ 1}.
\]
Thus almost every unrestricted supported binary relation requires every named atom, but almost every supported equivalence relation omits a logarithmic number of named atoms, despite retaining a \(1-o(1)\) fraction of its named context.

## Assumptions and scope

All statements hold for every infinite pure equality-atom set, including countably infinite ones, under the group of finite permutations. The binary relations are ordered relations \(R\subseteq\mathbb A^2\), not necessarily symmetric. The equivalence subfamily satisfies reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity on the whole infinite carrier. A finite context is fixed pointwise rather than merely setwise. Sampling is uniform on the *finite set* of supported relations, not on the uncountable unrestricted relation space. Statements about probabilities are asymptotic in context size \(k\); no randomness on the atom set itself is required.

## Proof

**1. Least support exists.** For finite \(S,T\), the pointwise stabilizer of \(S\cap T\) is generated by the pointwise stabilizers of \(S\) and \(T\). To see this, consider a transposition \((a\ b)\) fixing \(S\cap T\). It fixes either \(S\) or \(T\), unless (after relabelling) \(a\in S\setminus T\) and \(b\in T\setminus S\). In the exceptional case choose \(c\notin S\cup T\), and write \((a\ b)=(a\ c)(b\ c)(a\ c)\); the three factors alternately fix \(T,S,T\). Since finitary symmetric groups are generated by transpositions, invariance under both stabilizers implies invariance under their intersection stabilizer. All subsets of a given finite supporting context form a finite lattice, so intersecting those that support a relation yields its unique least support, contained in \(S\).

**2. Arbitrary binary relations.** The \(G_{(S)}\)-orbits on ordered pairs consist of \(k^2\) named-named cells, \(2k\) named-fresh/fresh-named cells, and two fresh-fresh cells (equal and unequal). Consequently an \(S\)-supported binary relation is any Boolean choice on \(k^2+2k+2\) independent cells, yielding \(T_k\). For any \(J\subseteq S\), the relations supported by \(J\) number \(2^{|J|^2+2|J|+2}\). By step 1, a relation supported by \(S\) has least support \(S\) exactly when none of the \(k\) named atoms can be deleted. Inclusion-exclusion over deletion events proves the formula for \(F_k\).

Let \(A_s\) be the event that \(S\setminus\{s\}\) supports the uniformly chosen binary relation. Then
\[
\Pr(A_s)=2^{-(2k+1)},\qquad
\Pr(A_s\cap A_t)=2^{-4k}\ (s\ne t).
\]
Bonferroni's inequalities show that \(1-F_k/T_k\) is between \(k2^{-(2k+1)}-\binom{k}{2}2^{-4k}\) and \(k2^{-(2k+1)}\), proving its sharp asymptotic.

**3. Complete classification of supported equivalences.** Put \(U_0=\mathbb A\setminus S\), an infinite transitive fresh-atom orbit. By invariance, either all distinct fresh atoms are equivalent or none are. In the latter **discrete-fresh** case, no fresh atom can be equivalent to a named atom, as this would force all fresh atoms into the same class. The equivalence is thus an arbitrary partition of \(S\), together with singleton classes for every fresh atom. A named atom is absent from its least support exactly when it is a singleton block of the partition.

In the former **giant-fresh** case, all fresh atoms form one infinite equivalence class. That class may contain an arbitrary subset \(U\subseteq S\); the remaining named atoms have an arbitrary partition. The least support is precisely \(S\setminus U\): membership in the infinite class does not distinguish its named elements from the fresh ones, whereas moving any element of \(S\setminus U\) into that class changes the relation.

For precisely \(d\) omitted names, the discrete-fresh case contributes \(\binom{k}{d}a_{k-d}\) partitions with exactly \(d\) singleton blocks. The giant-fresh case contributes \(\binom{k}{d}B_{k-d}\), by choosing the \(d\) named elements in the infinite class and partitioning the rest. Their sum is \(H_k(d)\). Summing over \(d\) gives \(B_k+B_{k+1}\): the discrete-fresh cases are indexed by partitions of \(S\), while the giant-fresh cases are indexed by partitions of \(S\cup\{*\}\), with \(*\) marking the fresh class.

**4. Equivalence asymptotics.** The classical Bell-ratio estimate is
\[
\frac{B_{k+1}}{B_k}\sim\frac{k}{W(k)}.
\]
The probability of the giant-fresh case is \(B_{k+1}/(B_{k+1}+B_k)\to1\). Conditional on it, let \(D_k\) be the number of named elements joining the infinite fresh class. Its exact distribution is
\[
\Pr(D_k=d\mid\text{giant-fresh})=\frac{\binom{k}{d}B_{k-d}}{B_{k+1}}.
\]
By exchangeability of the \(k+1\) elements in the corresponding uniform set partition,
\[
\mathbb E D_k=k\frac{B_k}{B_{k+1}}\sim W(k),\qquad
\mathbb E[D_k(D_k-1)]=k(k-1)\frac{B_{k-1}}{B_{k+1}}\sim W(k)^2.
\]
It follows that \(\operatorname{Var}(D_k)/W(k)^2\to0\), so \(D_k/W(k)\to1\) in probability. The discarded discrete-fresh case has probability tending to zero; hence the unconditioned support-defect law follows.

Finally, \(a_k/B_k\to0\). Indeed, in a uniform partition of \(k\) labels the number \(X_k\) of singleton blocks has \(\mathbb E X_k=kB_{k-1}/B_k\sim W(k)\) and \(\mathbb E[X_k(X_k-1)]=k(k-1)B_{k-2}/B_k\sim W(k)^2\). Thus \(X_k/W(k)\to1\) in probability, so \(\Pr(X_k=0)=a_k/B_k\to0\). Therefore \((B_k+a_k)/(B_{k+1}+B_k)\sim B_k/B_{k+1}\sim W(k)/k\).

The formulas for \(k=0\) follow directly from the two globally invariant equivalence relations (identity and universal) and the four globally invariant ordered binary relations; asymptotics require positive \(k\to\infty\).

## Verification

An independent finite-domain checker is included as `checker.py`, executable with `python checker.py` using only the Python standard library. It enumerates partitions on \(k+3\) atoms for \(0\le k\le5\), checks invariance under all generators of the fresh-atom permutation group, tests essentiality of every named atom, and compares *all* exact-support strata against \(H_k(d)\). It independently constructs tuple orbit signatures for arities \(1,2,3\) at \(0\le k\le3\). For \(0\le k\le3\), it exhaustively checks every Boolean choice on the \(k^2+2k+2\) binary orbit cells and whether each parameter is essential. On successful execution it prints `ALL_CHECKS_OK`.

These finite calculations test definitions and small instances only. The proof of the all-size count and limit laws is given above, using the cited classical Bell-ratio asymptotic. The checker does not certify the infinite theorems.

## Relationship to prior work

Classical nominal-set theory establishes least supports for pure equality atoms and identifies higher-arity stabilizer orbits with equality patterns. Ciobanu's 2026 finite-context semantic framework makes the size and indispensability of a declared context a natural issue for semantic compilation and supported quotienting, but the inspected passages do not state the present exact-support distribution or the contrast between arbitrary and equivalence-constrained binary relations. These standard components are credited, not claimed new. The Bell numbers, singleton-free partition numbers, and the Bell-ratio asymptotic are also classical; the assessed result is their exact integration into least-support strata, with the contrasting limiting laws on the same binary carrier.

A 2026 companion paper on finite-dependence hierarchies and a 2021 study of finitely supported binary relations were identifiable but their complete texts could not be inspected through available access routes. They remain material historical-coverage risks; no assertion that they do not cover the theorem is made on the basis of abstracts alone.

## Limitations

The theorem assumes pure equality atoms and finitary symmetric-group actions. More structured atom symmetries need not have the least-support intersection law, the same orbit counts, or this equivalence classification. The binary-relation probability is over all ordered relations, and equivalence sampling is uniform on the finite admissible class at each fixed context. The result does not imply similar typical-support laws for arbitrary constrained relations, higher-order semantic objects or probability measures on all relations on an infinite set. Independence from every possible earlier formulation is not asserted.
