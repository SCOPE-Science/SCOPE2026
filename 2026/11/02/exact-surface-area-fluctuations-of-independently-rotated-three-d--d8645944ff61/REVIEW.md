# Scientific assessment: Exact surface-area fluctuations of independently rotated three-dimensional Minkowski sums

## Correctness (PASS)

PASS: The surface-area integral equals 4π times the square of mean support minus a positive spectral deficit; the identity extends to every convex body by rotational smoothing. Haar Schur orthogonality gives the exact pair covariance δ_l²/(2l+1), yielding the stated finite-n mean and variance. A finite-degree multivariate CLT gives χ²_{2l+1}, while the tail is uniformly controlled in L1 by twice the summable spectral mass. The support and sharp 2/5 bound follow by positivity; independent Gauss-map area quadrature checked l=2 and mixed l=2,4 perturbations and Monte Carlo checked finite and limiting coefficients. Scientific limits: no explicit finite-n CDF or quantitative CLT rate. The support-function normalization uses total spherical area \(4\pi\); the derivation explicitly treats \(n=1\), arbitrary nonsmooth bodies, and degree-one translation modes. Analytical proofs, not experiments, cover the full infinite-dimensional limit.

Two published planar Fourier fluctuation records were read and are restricted to planar reflection or a single planar probe. Multiple actual semantic and keyword searches found no claimed stronger stochastic spatial result. Unindexed literature and inverse-problem methods remain risks, so priority is provisional. The closest literature is Ruan–Chirikjian (2020 deterministic spatial Minkowski boundary sums), Schneider–Schuster (2012 rotational harmonic classes), Coupier–Davydov (2012 iterative reflection symmetrization), and the two published planar Fourier fluctuation results identified in AUDIT.json. ## Mathematical value (PASS)

PASS: The complete fluctuation spectrum is a natural second-intrinsic-volume statistic for Minkowski averaging in ordinary three-dimensional space; it gives exact errors at every n, a non-Gaussian infinite-dimensional limit, and a sharp, attained isoperimetric-deficit variance inequality with structural equality cases. Its χ² multiplicities reveal geometric shape information invisible to mean width and are not a mere parameter substitution of planar SO(2) Fourier formulas. The result concerns a complete natural class of spatial convex bodies, not a single arbitrary low-complexity slice. The degree-two equality class is geometrically realizable by smooth nonballs.

## Scope and limits

The analytical claim requires independent Haar \(SO(3)\) rotations, concerns surface area rather than volume, and does not provide a finite-sample CDF or convergence speed. The standalone checker covers only finite harmonic families and simulated finite samples. Historical priority has not been independently certified.
