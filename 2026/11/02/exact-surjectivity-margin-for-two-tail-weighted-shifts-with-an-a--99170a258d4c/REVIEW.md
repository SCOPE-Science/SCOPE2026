# Mathematical assessment

## Correctness
**PASS (self-review).** The adjoint decomposition is an exact sum of two constant-tail gradients plus a nonnegative interface term. Strict boundary coefficients give a global quantitative lower bound; long constant blocks separately realize both tail edges, so the sharp constant is independent of the interface. The adjoint is bounded below but has no minimizing unit vector; closed range proves surjectivity, the recurrence proves a one-dimensional kernel, and the Hilbert-space Moore--Penrose identities prove the inverse norm and its nonattainment. All-operator perturbations of norm below the bound preserve surjectivity, and a whole-tail perturbation at exactly the bound destroys it. Exact finite checks support, but do not replace, these steps.

## Originality
**PASS (best-knowledge, not a priority certificate).** The qualitative spectral criteria and classical step-weight examples of Pituk and Eisenberg--Hedlund do not state this universal sharp distance-to-non-surjectivity or its interface independence. The 2026 generalized hyperbolicity result develops qualitative stability and right-spectrum criteria. Earlier weighted-shift dynamics literature concerns spectra and shadowing rather than the strict lower-edge factorization. Searches for equivalent and stronger quantitative statements, related operator models, and the published mathematical record did not reveal the exact formula. A general older Toeplitz/Jacobi inequality could nevertheless subsume it; that risk remains.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The exact perturbation margin is a natural conditioning invariant for robust surjectivity, especially where the ordinary spectrum contains a whole annulus and invertibility fails. Its complete independence from an arbitrarily strong or weak interface link is a structural rigidity phenomenon, not a generic consequence of the qualitative shadowing criterion. The distinction between an unattained optimal adjoint bound and an attained critical perturbation also specifies a meaningful spectral boundary.
