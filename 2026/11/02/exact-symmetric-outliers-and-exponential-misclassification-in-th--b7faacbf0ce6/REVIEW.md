# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The final claim is the exact class-symmetric misclassification count and associated unrestricted exponential bounds for a precisely defined three-class simple game. The proof identifies the only four potentially conflicting profiles, obtains the sharp lower bound from their convex score relation, and supplies two integer-weight classifiers attaining the two candidate orbit counts. A finite, complete enumeration of profiles with original weight at most \(61\), together with global score-ratio inequalities beyond that boundary, verifies the classifications for every class size. Averaging an imagined perfect unrestricted classifier over class permutations proves nonweightedness without assuming the 2026 article's nonweightedness theorem. Thus every unrestricted surrogate makes at least one error. The verifier checks all \(32768\) coalitions for \(m=5\) and exact profile calculations on a bounded range. The general proof, not those experiments, establishes the all-size statement. No correctness blocker identified.

## Originality — PASS, subject to publication-priority uncertainty
The closest source is Einy and Haimanko (arXiv:2610.08968, version 2), which introduces exactly this flip family and computes its excess-weight threshold. Its inspected sections discuss generic coalition-misclassification bounds, not an exact class-symmetric outlier count or exponential error rate for the concrete flip family. A 2025 paper by Albareda-Sambola, Molinero and Roura studies outlier distances in general and reports a small-player census; it cannot subsume the all-size three-class result by its cited census. The closest published research-record search hits concern power-index rank reversals and enumeration in other voting games, not the same four profiles or any misclassification implication. Explicit tests against aliases (outlier count, threshold classification, class-homogeneous weighted approximation), stronger coverages, and known tables did not identify an equivalent statement. Search failure alone does not establish uniqueness. Residual risks: the 2025 paper's full text was not accessible in the inspected public routes, and an unindexed exact enumeration or independent classification could exist.

## Value — PASS
The 2026 source demonstrates a sharp \(\Theta(1/m)\) excess-weight separation and separately discusses how far coalition-misclassification can depart from weightedness. For the very same published examples, the new theorem finds the exact equal-treatment outlier optimum, a switch in which *kind* of losing coalition must be sacrificed at \(m=8\), and a much faster exponential scale for the unrestricted count loss. This comparison distinguishes margin error from frequency error without adding an arbitrary parameter or requiring a general theorem about every voting game. The model's three classes represent genuinely equal voter types, making class-symmetric surrogates natural. The exact single-error finding at \(m=5\) is an interpretable extremal benchmark. The unrestricted prefactor above \(m=5\) remains unknown.

## Closest literature and limitations
Einy–Haimanko (2026), arXiv:2610.08968, Sections 4 and 6, is the principal comparison. Albareda-Sambola–Molinero–Roura (2025), doi:10.1016/j.ijar.2025.109418, is related outlier-method background. The proof determines the symmetric optimum but does not establish symmetric optimality among all individually weighted surrogates for \(m\geq6\). The result is special to the three-class flip family; it is not a global upgrade of general asymptotic estimates.
