# Review of the ternary onto-word universal-cycle classification

## Correctness — PASS

The edge family is the 36 surjective length-four ternary words. The overlap graph has 24 active triple vertices, including 18 indegree-one/outdegree-one vertices. Its forced-path suppression is a bidirected triangular prism with six degree-three vertices. The prism Laplacian spectrum gives 75 arborescences, and the fixed-edge BEST theorem gives 4,800. The transposition quotient has Laplacian cofactor 5 and odd cover voltage, giving 40 fixed cycles per transposition. The three-cycle quotient has zero net voltage and cannot lift to a 36-factor threefold-symmetric cycle. Four invariant factors or six distinct palindromic triples obstruct the remaining reversal symmetries. Burnside then yields 820 and 410. The included independent exhaustive graph walk agrees on all fixed-point counts. Risk: conventions for rooting and direction require care; they are fixed explicitly by the unique 0120 window and the statement that reversals are distinct until the last quotient.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Burstein–Kitaev (2003 preprint, Theorem 3.8) proves Hamiltonian existence for a broader word-pattern graph, and its full public text contains no special-case Euler-tour or symmetry-class count. The highly relevant 2010 onto-word existence theorem of Blanca and Godbole and the 2017 discussion of Gardner and Godbole already imply existence, so that fact is not presented as novel. The inspected relevant sections contain no numerical Euler-tour or orbit count for the 36 surjective factors. Chen and Kitaev's 2020 full text concerns universal partial words for surjective patterns, rather than literal labeled four-factor universal cycles. Descriptive literature and mathematical-record searches did not reveal the same exact enumeration under equivalent names. Residual risk: an inaccessible or unindexed source might already state the specialized 4,800/820/410 classification. Search absence is not a proof of priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is the first nonbinary onto-word case with window span one longer than the alphabet, making it a natural base example for enumeration beyond existence. Its complete count splits labeled, relabeling, and bracelet-like symmetry conventions, and the triangular-prism quotient provides a compact structural explanation rather than an arbitrary machine census. The 40 symmetric cycles per transposition and the absence of 3-cycle or reflection stabilizers are additional nontrivial structural facts. The case is finite and does not predict a general formula; that scope is explicitly limited.
