# Review of the exact ternary derangement residue-image spectrum

## Correctness

PASS. The claimed value-set count follows from a convergent three-adic falling-factorial interpolation, not from experimental extrapolation. Modulo nine, 27 and 81 coefficient certificates establish a bijective unit disc and an integral quadratic critical disc. A contraction argument gives the unique critical point, and the integral three-adic Morse coordinate reduces the singular disc exactly to a shifted square image. The critical value satisfies \(c\equiv18\pmod{27}\) by evaluation at \(D_{10}\). Counting unit squares and squares with positive valuation gives the explicit formulas for \(Q_h\), \(I_m\) and \(R_a\); a convergent geometric sum yields the limiting proportion. The recurrence and polynomial certificates were also checked independently by the accompanying standard-library program for nine moduli. Reproducibility does not substitute for the unrestricted proof.

## Originality

PASS under available evidence, with the specified uncertainty. Miska's 2015 full article already gives exact modular periodicity for derangements, the p-adic interpolation and prime-adic valuation conditions; these are not claimed. O'Desky and Richman develop more general interpolation and local analyticity; Sun and Zagier establish relations to Bell numbers modulo primes. The new statement is the complete **value-image cardinality** at every ternary prime power and its exact limiting occupancy, including a shifted-square critical-value calculation. Failed keyword retrieval by itself has not been treated as proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value

PASS. The number of distinct values attained by a classical integer sequence modulo successive prime powers is a natural global invariant beyond known periodicity and zero divisibility. The exact rational limiting occupancy \(725/972\) indicates a persistent arithmetic obstruction and the proof explains it through a nondegenerate three-adic critical point. This is a structural infinite-level classification, not a small numerical improvement or a routine restatement of the period.
