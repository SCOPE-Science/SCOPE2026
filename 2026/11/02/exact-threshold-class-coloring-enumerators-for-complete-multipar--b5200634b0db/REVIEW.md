# Review: threshold-class coloring enumerators of complete multipartite graphs

## Correctness — PASS
The object is the ordinary threshold-induced coloring of the labelled complete multipartite graph \(K_{n_1,\ldots,n_r}\), with \(r\ge1\) and all \(n_i\ge1\). The proof is a complete two-way characterization: an induced class is threshold exactly when at most one original part contributes two or more vertices. Two heavy parts force an induced \(C_4\); at most one heavy part leaves universal singleton-part vertices and then an edgeless graph. These disjoint possibilities give the displayed labeled-set exponential series without double counting. Formal coefficient extraction produces named-color assignments; ordinary inclusion-exclusion and color-label quotienting give the onto and unordered counts. For the minimum palette, the number of original parts of size greater than the palette is a necessary lower bound on heavy colors, and a matching explicit coloring supplies sufficiency. These arguments apply to all finite positive part sizes. The included checker also compares literal induced-graph tests with exact coefficient arithmetic on 166 instances and 345279 assignments, but finite tests are not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The closest motivating source, Bang-Jensen, Bessy and Reinald (arXiv:2610.32954v1, 2026), proves NP-completeness for general two-class alternating coloring and supplies the threshold graph correspondence. It does not provide the all-palette enumerator on complete multipartite graphs. The earlier Demange–Ekim–de Werra paper (2006, DOI 10.7155/jgaa.00129) defines split-coloring and analyzes approximation, not these multipartite enumerators. Their 2005 cograph-coloring work (DOI 10.1016/j.disopt.2005.03.003) establishes an efficient *decision* algorithm for a superclass, so computing the minimum in this family is not itself claimed as a new algorithmic result. The new, narrower symbolic claim is the exact multivariate enumeration for arbitrary part-size vectors and arbitrary palettes, with the sharp palette rule as its structural consequence. Searches for threshold/split/chromatic aliases, complete multipartite exact counts, and equivalent clique/stable partitions did not identify a covering formula. The 2005 cograph paper was accessible only through its abstract/metadata rather than its full text; this remains a genuine priority risk and is not treated as evidence of noncoverage.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The invariant is motivated by the graph-partition problem identified in a 2026 hardness paper, and by the older minimum split-coloring problem. The theorem resolves the full distribution of admissible ordered palettes and unordered class partitions on an unrestricted natural graph family, rather than just one numerical threshold or a hand-picked finite example. The exact structural constraint of one repeated original part per color class makes every coefficient computable as a finite symbolic expression; it also yields the optimal class number for arbitrary uneven part sizes. Counting all feasible allocations is strictly more informative than the earlier algorithms for finding an optimum. The outcome is not a result on general graphs or a complexity-theoretic improvement over previously known algorithms.

## Closest literature and limitations
The 2026 paper treats the general hard decision problem and establishes the threshold equivalence. Demange–Ekim–de Werra (2006) explains split-coloring and approximation; Demange–Ekim–de Werra (2005) treats clique/stable partitions of all cographs. The latter yields an algorithm for finding the minimum on the current family, but neither inspected text nor available abstract establishes the claimed exact all-palette multivariate enumerator. The 2005 full text was not accessible for full comparison; an explicit earlier specialization, or a later unindexed counting formula, remains possible. No independent validation has been performed.
