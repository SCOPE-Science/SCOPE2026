# Exact Toeplitz-algebra distances for periodically modulated Cesàro operators

## Finding

Let \(C\) be the discrete Cesàro operator on \(\ell^2(\mathbb N_0)\), identified with the Hardy space \(H^2(\mathbb D)\). Let \(\mathfrak T\) be the \(C^*\)-algebra generated by all Toeplitz operators with bounded symbols, and let \(\mathfrak E=\{B:[B,S]\text{ is compact}\}\) be the essential commutant of the unilateral shift \(S\). For an arbitrary complex sequence \(a\) of period \(m\ge1\), set \(T_a=D_aC\) and
\[
\mu=\frac1m\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}a_r,\qquad
v=\frac1m\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}|a_r-\mu|^2,\qquad
w=\frac1m\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}|a_r|^2,\qquad
\delta=\frac1m\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}|a_{r+1}-a_r|^2,
\]
where subscripts on \(a\) are cyclic. Then
\[
\boxed{\ \|T_a\|_{\mathrm{ess}}=2\sqrt w,\qquad
\operatorname{dist}(T_a,\mathfrak T)
=\operatorname{dist}(T_a,\mathfrak E)=2\sqrt v,\qquad
\|[T_a,S]\|_{\mathrm{ess}}=2\sqrt\delta.\ }
\]
In particular, a periodic row modulation of \(C\) belongs to \(\mathfrak T\) (or to \(\mathfrak E\)) **if and only if** the modulation is constant. A concrete asymptotically best sequence of approximants in \(\mathfrak T\) is
\[
B_N=\mu C+P_ND_{a-\mu}C,\qquad N\to\infty,
\]
where \(P_N\) projects onto the first \(N+1\) coordinate vectors. The distance infimum need not be attained by \(\mu C\) itself; the finite-rank correction is essential to the asserted approximation.

For the alternating modulation \(a=(1,-1)\), the distance is \(2\), equal to the essential norm of \(T_a\). For \(a=(1,0,0)\), the exact distance is \(2\sqrt2/3\), whereas the essential norm is \(2/\sqrt3\). Thus the distance measures periodic standard deviation, not the magnitude of the mean or the largest row weight.

## Assumptions and scope

The Hilbert space is complex \(\ell^2(\mathbb N_0)\), with \(e_0,e_1,\ldots\) its canonical basis. The Cesàro matrix is
\[
(Cx)_n=\frac1{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^n x_k,\qquad
C_{nk}=\frac{\mathbf1_{k\le n}}{n+1}.
\]
The diagonal operator \(D_a\) acts by \((D_ax)_n=a_nx_n\). The period \(m\) need not be minimal, and the coefficients may be zero or nonreal. The essential norm is the distance to the compact operators \(\mathcal K\), and the operator-algebra distances use the usual operator norm on \(\mathcal B(\ell^2)\). The commutator is \([T_a,S]=T_aS-ST_a\); reversing its sign does not change its norm. A previously established theorem of Sang supplies the nontrivial inclusion \(C\in\mathfrak T\). The established sharp \(\ell^2\) Hardy inequality gives \(\|C\|=2\).

## Proof

**1. Periodic cancellation gives a compact sandwich.** If \(b\) is bounded, periodic and has mean zero, its partial sums are uniformly bounded, say by \(M_b\). The matrix coefficients of \(C^*D_bC\) are
\[
(C^*D_bC)_{jk}=\sum_{n\ge\max(j,k)}\frac{b_n}{(n+1)^2}.
\]
For every \(L\ge0\), summation by parts, using the bounded partial sums of \(b\) and the decreasing weights \((n+1)^{-2}\), gives
\[
\left|\sum_{n\ge L}\frac{b_n}{(n+1)^2}\right|
\le\frac{2M_b}{(L+1)^2}.
\]
The squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm is bounded by a constant times
\(\sum_{j,k\ge0}(1+\max(j,k))^{-4}<\infty\).
Consequently \(C^*D_bC\in\mathcal S_2\subset\mathcal K\). Applying this to \(b_n=|a_n|^2-w\), and also to \(b_n=|a_n-\mu|^2-v\), yields
\[
C^*D_{|a|^2}C-wC^*C\in\mathcal K,\qquad
C^*D_{|a-\mu|^2}C-vC^*C\in\mathcal K.
\]

**2. The Cesàro essential norm and all periodic row-weight essential norms.** The classical sharp Hardy inequality gives \(\|C\|\le2\). To obtain the opposite bound modulo compacts, take the unit vectors obtained by normalizing \(f^{(N)}\), where
\[
f^{(N)}_k=\frac{\mathbf1_{N\le k\le N^2}}{\sqrt{k+1}}.
\]
They are weakly null. Their squared norms are \(\log N+O(1)\). Uniformly on \(N(\log N)^4\le n\le N^2\), the integral comparison for \(\sum_{k=N}^n(k+1)^{-1/2}\) gives
\[
(Cf^{(N)})_n=\frac{2+o(1)}{\sqrt{n+1}}.
\]
The squared norm on those output indices is \((4-o(1))\log N\). Hence \(\|C\|_{\mathrm{ess}}=2\). Let \(\pi\) denote the quotient map to the Calkin algebra \(\mathcal Q=\mathcal B(\ell^2)/\mathcal K\). From Step 1,
\[
\pi(T_a)^*\pi(T_a)=w\,\pi(C)^*\pi(C).
\]
The \(C^*\)-identity gives \(\|T_a\|_{\mathrm{ess}}=2\sqrt w\), including the zero-weight case. More generally, applying the same identity to \(a-\mu\) gives \(\|D_{a-\mu}C\|_{\mathrm{ess}}=2\sqrt v\).

**3. The shift action and its finite orbit average.** The matrix \(S^*CS-C\) has entries \(-\mathbf1_{k\le n}/[(n+1)(n+2)]\), a Hilbert–Schmidt matrix. Thus \(c=\pi(C)\) is fixed by the isometric \(*\)-automorphism
\[
\alpha(X)=u^*Xu,\qquad u=\pi(S),
\]
where \(u\) is unitary in the Calkin algebra. Every element of \(\pi(\mathfrak E)\) is fixed by \(\alpha\), and conversely a representative \(B\) with \(\alpha(\pi(B))=\pi(B)\) belongs to \(\mathfrak E\). In particular \(\pi(\mathfrak T)\) lies in this fixed-point algebra. For a period-\(m\) sequence \(a\),
\[
\alpha^j(\pi(D_aC))=\pi(D_{a^{(j)}}C),\qquad
 a^{(j)}_n=a_{n+j},
\]
and therefore
\[
\frac1m\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\alpha^j(\pi(D_{a-\mu}C))=0.
\]
This finite average of automorphisms, denoted \(E_m\), is unital, positive, and contractive, even though no periodicity assumption is imposed on the entire Calkin algebra.

**4. Sharp lower distance bound.** Fix any \(B\in\mathfrak E\). Put \(y=\pi(D_{a-\mu}C)\) and \(z=\mu c-\pi(B)\). Since \(z\) is fixed by \(\alpha\) and \(E_m(y)=0\), Step 1 and positivity imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|T_a-B\|^2
&\ge\|y+z\|^2
 =\|(y+z)^*(y+z)\|\\
&\ge\|E_m((y+z)^*(y+z))\|\\
&=\|v c^*c+z^*z\|
\ge v\|c\|^2=4v.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is valid for every element of \(\mathfrak E\), hence also every element of \(\mathfrak T\).

**5. Sharp upper distance bound.** Sang's factorization theorem shows \(C\in\mathfrak T\), and \(\mathcal K\subset\mathfrak T\). For each \(N\), the operator \(B_N=\mu C+P_ND_{a-\mu}C\) belongs to \(\mathfrak T\). For an arbitrary bounded operator \(A\) on \(\ell^2\),
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\|(I-P_N)A\|=\|A\|_{\mathrm{ess}},
\]
because \(P_NA\) is finite rank and \(\|(I-P_N)K\|\to0\) for every compact \(K\). Taking \(A=D_{a-\mu}C\) gives
\(\lim_N\|T_a-B_N\|=2\sqrt v\), matching Step 4.

**6. Exact essential commutator.** From \(\alpha(\pi(T_a))-\pi(T_a)=\pi(D_{a^{(1)}-a}C)\) and unitarity of \(u\),
\[
\|[T_a,S]\|_{\mathrm{ess}}
=\|\alpha(\pi(T_a))-\pi(T_a)\|
=2\left(\frac1m\sum_{r=0}^{m-1}|a_{r+1}-a_r|^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
The membership classification follows from \(v=0\) exactly for constant \(a\): then \(T_a=\mu C\in\mathfrak T\); otherwise its positive distance excludes membership in either algebra. This completes the proof.

## Relationship to prior work

Sang (2026, arXiv:2608.25740, Theorem 1.1 and equation (5.3)) proved the fundamental inclusion \(C\in\mathfrak T\), resolving the original Barría–Halmos membership question. The present theorem uses that inclusion, but asks how far **periodic diagonal row modulations** of \(C\) lie from \(\mathfrak T\) and from the larger essential shift commutant. The result supplies an exact, all-period, complex-coefficient distance profile, not just a qualitative nonmembership statement.

Bellavita and Stylogiannis (2024, arXiv:2409.10014) describe essential shift commutation for analytic integral operators, not periodically row-weighted discrete Cesàro matrices. Nikpour (2019, DOI:10.21136/CMJ.2018.0391-17, Theorem 2.6) computes distances **to the vanishing strongly asymptotic Toeplitz class**; that target class is different from the Toeplitz operator algebra or the essential shift commutant considered here. Indeed, every bounded diagonal modulation of \(C\) is strongly asymptotically Toeplitz with zero symbol, so Nikpour's distance is zero in this example. The positive distance proved here therefore neither duplicates nor contradicts that theorem.

The finite-orbit averaging argument is a standard operator-algebra technique, and the sharp Hardy norm is classical. Originality is asserted only for their joint application producing the exact periodic-modulation distance and essential commutator formulas; equivalent earlier formulations, if found, would supersede that priority claim.

## Limitations

The theorem treats only periodic row multipliers on the discrete Cesàro operator acting on complex \(\ell^2\), not arbitrary almost-periodic or slowly varying rows, other \(\ell^p\) exponents, or all weighted Hardy matrices. It gives the distance infimum with an explicit approximating sequence; it does not classify all nearest Toeplitz-algebra operators or assert that a nearest operator always exists. A finite program cannot independently validate compactness or a Calkin-algebra statement. Publication priority remains subject to further specialist literature review, and no independent audit has been performed.
