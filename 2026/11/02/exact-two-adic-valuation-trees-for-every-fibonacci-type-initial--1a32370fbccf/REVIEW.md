# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The state-cycle calculation modulo two produces one even index in each three-step block, and the exact identity \(M^3=I+2M\) makes one of the two even positions uniquely divisible by four. The key additional identity \(M^6=5I+8M\) yields an interpolation \(G_r\) whose first difference coefficient is exactly \(8U_{r+1}/5\) with \(U_{r+1}\) odd. The difference bound for higher binomial terms is at least two powers of two stronger. This proves the exact isometry without asymptotic assumptions, and bijectivity modulo all powers gives a unique two-adic zero precisely in the root branch. The exceptional zero convention and the normalization of nonprimitive pairs are explicit. Independent integer enumeration of all 48 modulo-eight primitive input pairs and 528 modular censuses corroborates the proof. Risk: arithmetic or proof-review mistakes are possible; no external verification has been performed.

## Originality — PASS, subject to literature limitations
Medina and Rowland give valuations and regularity of the normalized Fibonacci sequence, not the complete arbitrary-initial-pair dichotomy. The August 2014 study of Chavoya, Lucero, and Reynolds is especially close: it studies the exact same family \(aF_n+bL_n\), proves some special cases, and labels other coefficient patterns unresolved. The present all-pair classification, unique two-adic parameter, and complete prime-power zero-class tree are not established in the inspected pages. Shu and Yao address \(p\)-regularity rather than this exact classification. Rowland and Yassawi provide a general interpolation framework that does not state these exact valuations. Searches for synonymous forms, integer initial conditions, modular periods, and unique Hensel roots revealed no equivalent statement. Residual risk: the full text of Shu and Yao was not available for direct inspection; an unlocated specialization or classical source may imply the claim.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This closes the explicit-coefficient gap emphasized by the 2014 generalized-Fibonacci study, replacing its partial cases by a uniform classification with a sharp mod-eight decision test and a unique compatible solution branch for every power of two. It determines exact divisibility-index densities rather than merely proving regularity or periodicity. The family is natural and infinite; the result makes no claim to solve arbitrary Lucas recurrences or higher-order cases.
