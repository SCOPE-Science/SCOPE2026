# Exact two-generator subalgebra spectra in free class-three nilpotent Lie algebras

## Finding

Fix an integer \(n\ge2\) and a finite field \(k=\mathbb F_q\), with \(q\) any prime power. Let \(L\) be the free nilpotent Lie algebra of class three on \(n\) generators. Set
\[
m=\binom n2,\qquad w=\frac{n^3-n}{3},\qquad N=n+m+w,
\qquad A=q^n,\ B=q^m,\ C=q^w.
\]
Every subalgebra generated by an ordered pair \((x,y)\in L^2\) has exactly one of the following five isomorphism types: the zero algebra; the one- or two-dimensional abelian Lie algebra; the three-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra; or the five-dimensional free class-three nilpotent Lie algebra on two generators. No other dimension occurs, in any characteristic.

Let \(P_d\) count ordered pairs generating a subalgebra of dimension \(d\), where \(d\in\{0,1,2,3,5\}\). The complete spectrum is
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_0&=1,\\
P_1&=(q+1)(ABC-1),\\
P_2&=BC^2\bigl(B+(q+1)(A-1)\bigr)-1-(q+1)(ABC-1),\\
P_3&=(A-1)(q+1)B(B-1)C^2,\\
P_5&=(A-1)(A-q)B^2C^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular \(\sum P_d=q^{2N}\). The number \(S_d\) of **distinct** two-generated subalgebras of each type is exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_0&=1,\\
S_1&=\frac{q^N-1}{q-1},\\
S_2&=\frac{P_2}{(q^2-1)(q^2-q)},\\
S_3&=\frac{q^n-1}{q-1}\frac{q^m-1}{q-1}\,q^{m+2w-3},\\
S_5&={n\brack2}_q\,q^{2(m+w-3)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \({n\brack2}_q=\frac{(q^n-1)(q^n-q)}{(q^2-1)(q^2-q)}\) is a Gaussian binomial coefficient. The formula for \(S_2\) is an integer by the ordered-basis argument below.

As a structural complement, all noncentral element-centralizers are abelian. Their dimensions are \(m+w\) when the degree-one component vanishes but the degree-two component does not, and \(w+1\) when the degree-one component is nonzero. Thus, for \(n\ge3\), this free class-three family has exactly three distinct element-centralizer dimensions (including \(N\) for the center).

## Assumptions and scope

The free Lie algebra is the ordinary alternating free Lie algebra over \(k\), and \(L=\mathcal L(V)/\gamma_4(\mathcal L(V))\), with \(V=k^n\). This definition includes characteristics two and three; no restricted-Lie structure is imposed. It has the natural degree decomposition
\[
L=V\oplus W_2\oplus W_3,\quad W_2\cong\bigwedge^2V,\quad
\dim W_3=w,
\]
where \(W_3\) is central, \([W_2,W_2]=0\), and \(\dim W_2=m\). The dimension of \(W_3\) follows from the integral Hall basis/Witt formula. Counts refer to \(k\)-linear Lie subalgebras (not just additive subgroups). A pair generates the smallest \(k\)-Lie subalgebra containing both entries.

## Proof

**1. Injectivity of the degree-three bracket.** For any nonzero \(v\in V\), the map \(W_2\to W_3\) sending \(t\mapsto[v,t]\) is injective over every field. To see this, embed the free Lie algebra in the tensor algebra \(T(V)\) via commutators, and change basis to \(v=e_1\). If a homogeneous quadratic tensor \(t\) commutes with \(e_1\), compare coefficients in \(e_1t=te_1\). Words not starting with \(e_1\) first force \(t=e_1s\) with \(s\in V\), and comparison of \(e_1e_je_1\) for \(j\ne1\) forces \(s\in ke_1\). Thus \(t\in ke_1^2\). But the degree-two free Lie space is spanned by alternating commutators \(e_ie_j-e_je_i\), and meets \(ke_1^2\) only in zero, including in characteristic two. Therefore \(t=0\). This argument occurs in tensor degree three and is unaffected by the class-three quotient.

**2. Classification of pair-generated algebras.** Write \(x=v+s+z\), \(y=v'+s'+z'\), with \(v,v'\in V\), \(s,s'\in W_2\), and \(z,z'\in W_3\). When \(v,v'\) are linearly independent, they extend to a basis of \(V\). The endomorphism of the free class-three algebra sending the first two free generators to \(x,y\) and the others to lifts of a completing basis is invertible, since it induces an isomorphism on \(L/[L,L]\); invertibility follows iteratively through the lower-central filtration. It follows that the Lie subalgebra generated by \(x,y\) is isomorphic to the free class-three nilpotent Lie algebra on two generators, of dimension five.

If \(\dim\langle v,v'\rangle=1\), replace \(x,y\) by an invertible linear combination \(a=v_0+s_0+z_0\), \(b=t_0+u_0\), with \(0\ne v_0\in V\) and \(t_0\in W_2\). Their commutator is \([v_0,t_0]\in W_3\). By Step 1 it vanishes precisely when \(t_0=0\). Otherwise it is nonzero and central, and independent of \(a,b\) by degree, hence these elements generate exactly the three-dimensional Heisenberg algebra. When it vanishes they generate an abelian algebra of dimension at most two. If \(v=v'=0\), the pair commutes because \([W_2\oplus W_3,W_2\oplus W_3]=0\). The remaining abelian dimensions are simply the vector-space ranks of \((x,y)\), namely zero, one or two. This proves the classification without a restriction on the characteristic.

**3. Ordered-pair enumeration.** The two degree-one components are independent in exactly \((q^n-1)(q^n-q)\) ordered ways. Each has \(q^{m+w}=BC\) lifts, yielding \(P_5\). For degree-one span of rank one, choose a line in \(V\) in \((q^n-1)/(q-1)\) ways and a nonzero ordered scalar pair in \(q^2-1\) ways. If the degree-one parts are \(av\) and \(bv\), their bracket's degree-three component is \([v,as'-bs]\). Injectivity from Step 1 shows that exactly \(q^m\) of the \(q^{2m}\) choices of \((s,s')\) commute, and the other \(q^{2m}-q^m\) choices generate the Heisenberg algebra; the two central components are arbitrary. Therefore
\[
P_3=\frac{q^n-1}{q-1}(q^2-1)(q^{2m}-q^m)q^{2w},
\]
which simplifies to the stated expression. The number of commuting ordered pairs is
\[
q^{2(m+w)}+\frac{q^n-1}{q-1}(q^2-1)q^{m+2w}
=BC^2\bigl(B+(q+1)(A-1)\bigr).
\]
The zero pair is unique. There are \((q^N-1)/(q-1)\) nonzero one-dimensional subspaces, each containing \(q^2-1\) nonzero dependent ordered pairs, giving \(P_1=(q+1)(q^N-1)\). Removing \(P_0\) and \(P_1\) from the commuting count gives \(P_2\). The partition exhausts \(L^2\), proving the displayed sum.

**4. Distinct-subalgebra enumeration.** In any two-dimensional abelian algebra the number of ordered generating pairs is \(|\mathrm{GL}_2(q)|=(q^2-1)(q^2-q)\). A three-dimensional Heisenberg algebra has a two-dimensional abelianization and a one-dimensional derived ideal; each ordered basis of the abelianization has \(q^2\) lifts that generate. Similarly, the five-dimensional free class-three algebra on two generators has a three-dimensional derived ideal, so it has \(|\mathrm{GL}_2(q)|q^6\) ordered generating pairs. Dividing \(P_2,P_3,P_5\) by these uniform fiber sizes gives \(S_2,S_3,S_5\), respectively; the stated simplifications use the Gaussian coefficient. Zero- and one-dimensional subalgebras give \(S_0,S_1\).

**5. Centralizers.** If \(v=0\ne s\), then \([s,\cdot]\) is injective on \(V\) by Step 1, while it vanishes on \(W_2\oplus W_3\); hence \(C_L(x)=W_2\oplus W_3\), of dimension \(m+w\). If \(v\ne0\), a centralizing element \(u+t+r\) satisfies \(v\wedge u=0\), hence \(u=av\), and then \([v,t-as]=0\), giving \(t=as\) by Step 1. Thus \(C_L(x)=kx\oplus W_3\), of dimension \(w+1\). Both are abelian. The central elements are precisely \(W_3\). For \(n\ge3\), the three dimensions \(N,m+w,w+1\) are pairwise different.

## Verification

The standalone `verify.py` constructs free Lie bases as independent commutators inside the truncated tensor-word algebra, then computes actual Lie brackets and the rank of \(x,y,[x,y],[x,[x,y]],[y,[x,y]]\). It exhaustively checks every ordered pair and the count of distinct generated subalgebras for \((n,q)=(2,2)\) and \((2,3)\). It also tests seeded finite samples at \((3,2),(3,3),(4,2)\). Its output ends with `FREE_CLASS3_TWO_GENERATOR_CENSUS_OK`. The scripted finite checks are supplementary to the field-independent proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Research on two-generator subalgebras and free nilpotent Lie algebras long predates this calculation. Boral's 1977 thesis specifically considers two-generator free nilpotent subalgebras and necessary-and-sufficient criteria; the elementary rank-two free-subalgebra criterion is not claimed as new. Bowman–Towers–Varea (2007) investigate qualitative global conditions from two-generated subalgebras, especially solvability and triangulability. Kundu–Naik–Singh (2022) classify class-three Lie algebras having only two element-centralizer dimensions, a different classification problem; when \(n\ge3\), the present family has three centralizer dimensions. The present assertion concerns uniform exact finite-field distributions of **all** pair-generated isomorphism types and of distinct two-generated subalgebras. No such complete enumerative result was identified in the material actually inspected, but the unavailable full text of Boral's thesis is a meaningful priority uncertainty.

## Limitations

The formulas classify only subalgebras generated by at most two elements, not the entire subalgebra lattice. They concern the ordinary free class-three nilpotent Lie algebra and not arbitrary class-three quotients, higher nilpotency class, or group-theoretic analogues. The literature comparison is a best-knowledge search, not proof of publication priority; in particular, the 1977 thesis could not be inspected beyond its repository description and table-of-contents evidence. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
