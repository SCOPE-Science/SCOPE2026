# Scientific review: circular-ladder two-packing profile

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem concerns every integer \(n\ge3\) and distinguishes indexed graph vertices from automorphism classes. The local three-symbol characterization follows from the exact Cartesian-product distance. Its five-state transfer matrix counts every admissible cyclic word exactly once, and the determinant expansion yields the claimed rational identity and recurrence. The independent gap argument proves the coefficient formula and residue-dependent maxima, including the small-order boundary \(n=3\). The cubic-regularity argument transports these counts to unique-response Roman weights using the *known* bijection. The complete standalone verifier matches direct graph-distance packings through \(n=12\), ternary Roman conditions through \(n=6\), and the symbolic determinant. Its finite scope is not used to infer the infinite theorem.

## Originality — PASS, subject to documented risk
The nearest primary work is Fernau–Mann (arXiv:2309.07018v1), which explicitly establishes the general two-packing/unique-response-Roman bijection, and Brešar–Klavžar–Rall (arXiv:2309.04963v1), which examines open packing of prisms over bipartite graphs. These results are acknowledged rather than rediscovered. General local-state matrix methods and the known bijection make derivation possible in principle, but neither cited theorem specifies the closed five-state determinant, the full coefficients, nor the optimum census for \(C_n\square K_2\). Searches were also made under 'circular ladder', 'prism', '2-packing', 'Roman', 'distance-two packing', and polynomial aliases, and accessible previous scientific claims were compared semantically. No matching complete theorem was located. Absence from these indexes is not proof of worldwide novelty; earlier unpublished, unindexed, or inaccessible work remains a risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is a natural full cardinality distribution of a standard distance-two packing invariant on an infinite cubic graph family, not merely an isolated graph order. Its five-state denominator supplies all exact coefficients, while the \(n\bmod4\) extremal transition identifies a genuine cyclic parity obstruction, with especially rigid perfect packings when \(4\mid n\). The same results exactly enumerate all unique-response Roman functions by weight and all optimizers. The general transfer method is standard, but a complete evaluated natural-family census and its sharp boundary behavior are not merely a normalization change.

## Residual limitations and source comparison
The cited broad prism theorem is about open packings, which differ from two-packings. The cited Roman paper supplies the universal bijection rather than the circular-ladder enumeration. A related 2017 circular-strip paper studies identifying and locating codes, not closed-neighborhood-disjoint two-packings. A 2024 paper whose title mentions complementary prisms concerns a different graph operation; its entire text was not inspected. Priority therefore remains conditional on future literature checks. No result is claimed for Möbius ladders, higher-width cylinders, or non-unique-response Roman variants.
