# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The finite-state claim concerns the minimum iteration at which each modulo-four cyclic-sum vector is periodic. The proof constructs the explicit CRT decomposition into invertible and nilpotent cyclic modules, verifies the characteristic-two factor separation over the full residue ring, computes exact ideals of every power of \(y=1+x\) and then takes differences of cumulative kernel sizes. All quantifiers cover even \(n\ge2\) and nonnegative \(t\), with sharp \(3q/2\) maximum. Symbolic identities prove the theorem; the 69,904-vector and 165-matrix checks are corroboration, not an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence
Dular (2019) studies the same operator and its periods and cycle sets; Lewis–Tefft (2024, two works) determine maximal preperiods or bounds, not the full counts of every transient-depth stratum. Comparison included the papers' full accessible HTML texts and relevant proof sections. Related count-of-cycles-by-period literature measures a different statistic. Semantic research-record and targeted textual searches found no exact or stronger covering distribution. The main residual risk is an implicit theorem in an unavailable older general treatment of Ducci sequences over abelian groups or in broader cyclic-code/SNF theory. Search absence is not proof of priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result is a complete, naturally parameterized distribution in a classical arithmetic recurrence over a finite residue ring. It sharpens a maximum-depth question into exact two-phase counts, a surprising fifty-percent maximal-depth law and the invariance of normalized probabilities under odd changes of tuple length. The theorem requires a structural ring decomposition and ideal filtration, not just a finite enumeration.

## Limitations
Only modulus four and even lengths; does not determine cycle periods, odd lengths or higher moduli. The older Breuer full text could not be inspected in full, leaving a bibliographic risk. Independent validation has not been performed.
