# Scientific review: dyadic Pascal-row three-square counts

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof independently derives the exact \(2\)-adic valuation \(r-\nu_2(k)\) and its odd unit modulo \(8\) by a three-factor reduction. Legendre's three-square criterion then yields a necessary and sufficient index classification. A valuation-stratified count, including both short boundary strata, gives the full count and two distinct limiting densities. An exact-integer checker tests all entries through exponent \(13\), plus all classification positions through exponent \(20\). Finite checks support rather than replace the symbolic argument.

**Originality — PASS within inspected evidence.** Granville–Zhu's original complete paper gives broad existence and central-term information, not the present exact count for all dyadic rows. Burns's full text concerns factorials; the public abstracts of Moshe and Mincu–Panaitopol concern respectively global Pascal-triangle frequency and run lengths. Queries for equivalent dyadic three-square counts, odd-part residues, and stronger statements revealed no direct coverage. The complete text of Moshe and Mincu–Panaitopol could not be inspected; implicit coverage is an identified residual risk, and absence from searches is not uniqueness proof.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Exact obstruction counts in arithmetically distinguished Pascal rows and the failure of a common limiting density are natural, informative refinements of classical three-square representability questions. The result offers a complete formula and classifies every obstructed index, not merely an isolated finite example. Its domain is deliberately limited to powers-of-two row indices; it neither addresses general rows nor resolves global density questions.

Primary comparisons: Granville–Zhu, *American Mathematical Monthly* (1990), full nine-page scanned paper; R. Burns, arXiv:2203.16469v1 (2022), full text; Y. Moshe, DOI: 10.1112/S0025579300015515, abstract only; G. Mincu and L. Panaitopol, DOI: 10.1007/s00013-010-0182-5, abstract only.
