# Exact two-resample calibration and its sharp boundary for weighted involutive permutation tests

## Finding
Let \(G\cong(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^d\) be a group of \(K=2^d\ge2\) commuting involutive coordinate permutations. Under an exchangeable null distribution for the data \(X\), let \(T\) be a measurable real statistic whose values \(T(X_g)\), \(g\in G\), are almost surely pairwise distinct. Let \(q\) be an arbitrary probability distribution on \(G\), and draw \(\sigma_0,\sigma_1,\sigma_2\) independently from \(q\), independently of \(X\). Consider the valid generalized permutation \(p\)-value
\[
P_2=\frac{1+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mathbf 1\{T(X_{\sigma_i\sigma_0^{-1}})\ge T(X)\}}3.
\]
Write \(s_j=\sum_{g\in G}q(g)^j\) for \(j=2,3\). The exact unconditional null distribution, independent of the particular exchangeable data distribution and statistic satisfying the no-ties assumption, is
\[
\begin{split}
\Pr(P_2=1/3)&=\frac13-\frac{s_2}{2}+\frac{s_3}{6},\\
\Pr(P_2=2/3)&=\frac13-\frac{s_3}{3},\\
\Pr(P_2=1)&=\frac13+\frac{s_2}{2}+\frac{s_3}{6}.
\end{split}
\]
For fixed \(K\), the uniform probability measure on all \(K\) transformations is the **unique** distribution \(q\) that stochastically minimizes \(P_2\): it maximizes the null rejection probability simultaneously at every possible nontrivial support threshold. The sharp cumulative probabilities at the uniform distribution are
\[
\Pr(P_2\le1/3)=\frac13-\frac1{2K}+\frac1{6K^2},\qquad
\Pr(P_2\le2/3)=\frac23-\frac1{2K}-\frac1{6K^2}.
\]
This distribution-free moment law is specific to the small-resample regime. With three auxiliary transformations rather than two, even the four-element involutive group admits two statistics satisfying the same no-ties condition, the same exchangeable null and the same nonuniform \(q\), but different \(p\)-value laws; an exact witness is provided below.

## Assumptions and scope
The action of \(G\) on the data consists of commuting permutations that square to the identity. One canonical embedding is generated by disjoint swaps of coordinate pairs in a vector of length \(2d\). Exchangeability under the full symmetric group implies invariance under \(G\), which is all the proof needs. The transformations act on the data consistently with the notation \(X_g\); in particular \((X_g)_h=X_{gh}\) in the commuting group.

The **no-ties** assumption is \(\Pr(T(X_g)=T(X_h))=0\) for all distinct \(g,h\in G\). Zero weights in \(q\) are allowed. Ties created because the same transformation is drawn repeatedly are retained and counted with the non-strict comparison \(\ge\). The law concerns the *unconditional null* over both the data and the random transformations, not a conditional law given a particular observed data vector. The minimization refers only to null calibration, not to alternative power. All identities are finite-sample, with no asymptotic limit.

## Proof
**1. All-distinct draws.** Conditional on a fixed unordered triple of three distinct transformations, its ordering across \(\sigma_0,\sigma_1,\sigma_2\) is uniform. By invariance of \(X\), shifting \(X\) by \(\sigma_0^{-1}\) before evaluating all three scores does not change the distribution. Thus the observed rank among the three different scores is uniform, giving probability \(1/3\) to each \(P_2\in\{1/3,2/3,1\}\). The probability of three distinct draws is
\[
D=1-3s_2+2s_3.
\]

**2. Exactly two equal draws.** Each of the three possible index patterns, namely \(\sigma_0=\sigma_1\ne\sigma_2\), \(\sigma_0=\sigma_2\ne\sigma_1\), or \(\sigma_1=\sigma_2\ne\sigma_0\), has probability \(E=s_2-s_3\). Given distinct \(a,b\in G\), their quotient \(h=ba^{-1}\) is a nonidentity involution. The pair of scores \((T(X),T(X_h))\) is exchangeable in law, because applying \(h\) to \(X\) leaves its law unchanged and swaps the two scores. By the no-ties hypothesis, either strict ordering occurs with probability \(1/2\). Consequently, in the pattern with equal auxiliary draws \(\sigma_1=\sigma_2\ne\sigma_0\), the values \(1/3\) and \(1\) each have probability \(1/2\); in each pattern in which \(\sigma_0\) is duplicated, the values \(2/3\) and \(1\) each have probability \(1/2\).

**3. All-equal draws.** This occurs with probability \(s_3\), in which case \(P_2=1\). Adding contributions gives
\[
\Pr(P_2=1/3)=D/3+E/2,\quad
\Pr(P_2=2/3)=D/3+E,\quad
\Pr(P_2=1)=D/3+3E/2+s_3,
\]
which simplify to the stated three masses. The analogous one-auxiliary-draw law, useful for identifying the first nontrivial case, is \(\Pr(P_1=1/2)=(1-s_2)/2\) and \(\Pr(P_1=1)=(1+s_2)/2\).

**4. Strict stochastic extremality.** The only nontrivial CDF support levels have values
\[
F_q(1/3)=\frac13-\frac{s_2}{2}+\frac{s_3}{6},\qquad
F_q(2/3)=\frac23-\frac{s_2}{2}-\frac{s_3}{6}.
\]
Take two unequal probabilities \(x>y\). Transferring infinitesimal mass \(dt\) from \(x\) to \(y\) changes the two CDFs, respectively, at the positive rates
\[
(x-y)\left(1-\frac{x+y}{2}\right),\qquad
(x-y)\left(1+\frac{x+y}{2}\right).
\]
Here \(x+y\le1\). Every nonuniform probability vector admits a finite succession of strict equalizing transfers toward the uniform vector, and each increases both CDFs. Thus uniform \(q\) is the unique maximizer of both CDFs, hence the unique stochastic minimizer of \(P_2\). Substitution \(s_2=1/K\), \(s_3=1/K^2\) gives the sharp values.

**5. First failure with three auxiliary transformations.** Let \(G=(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^2=\{0,1,2,3\}\), written with bitwise exclusive-or for group multiplication, and take the fixed masses
\[
(q(0),q(1),q(2),q(3))=(1/2,1/4,1/8,1/8).
\]
Let the data be uniformly distributed over all permutations of four distinct real numbers. This null is fully exchangeable. The four-element group generated by the disjoint coordinate swaps \((12)\) and \((34)\) acts freely on the \(24\) data permutations, partitioning them into six orbits. Choose one representative of each orbit; assign scores \(4,3,2,1\) to its four group images in descending group-label order \((0,1,2,3)\) for statistic \(T_A\), or order \((0,2,1,3)\) for \(T_B\). Extend these assignments over all six orbits. Both scores are distinct within every orbit. Defining \(P_3\) by replacing two with three auxiliary draws and denominator three with four, exhaustive rational enumeration, or the finite sum displayed in the verification script, gives in the support order \((1/4,1/2,3/4,1)\):
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
&\Pr(P_3=1/4)&\Pr(P_3=1/2)&\Pr(P_3=3/4)&\Pr(P_3=1)\\ \hline
T_A&63/512&3/16&123/512&115/256\\
T_B&981/8192&1617/8192&1887/8192&3707/8192
\end{array}
\]
In particular, \(\Pr(P_3=1/4)\) differs by \(27/8192\). Both constructions have the same \(G\), \(q\), fully exchangeable null, and no-ties condition. Therefore a law determined solely by \(q\)'s power sums cannot generally extend from two to three auxiliary draws, even for involutive groups.

## Verification
The included standalone \(\texttt{check.py}\) evaluates the exact distribution with integer-weight enumeration on the four-element group. It compares the formula against direct full-orbit enumeration for all \(165\) integer weight compositions of total mass eight and all \(24\) strict group-score orders (\(3960\) exact rational distribution comparisons). It checks both stated three-auxiliary-draw witness distributions and the pairwise-balancing CDF inequalities at rational grid points, totaling \(5163\) successful exact assertions when run with the Python standard library. These finite checks corroborate the algebra; the proof covers every \(d\) and every probability vector \(q\).

## Relationship to prior work
Ramdas, Barber, Candès and Tibshirani, *Permutation Tests Using Arbitrary Permutation Distributions* (first public preprint 2022-04-28), Theorems 2.2 and 2.3, establish validity of the randomized-center \(p\)-value for arbitrary \(q\) and give its exchangeable-rank interpretation. Their paper explicitly identifies null conservativeness of such procedures as a question worth understanding. Those foundational validity and exchangeability results are **prior work**, not claims here. The present result determines the exact two-draw null law in an involutive subgroup, proves sharp stochastic extremality within this model, and proves a concrete three-draw limitation.

Hemerik and Goeman, *Exact testing with random permutations* (2018), analyze exactness and ties for uniform sampling from permutation groups. Phipson and Smyth (2010) describe exact random-permutation \(p\)-values sampled with replacement. Neither of these frameworks' stated uniform-sampling formulas directly furnish the nonuniform arbitrary-weight moment identities and extremality above. The three-draw counterexample distinguishes an actual breakdown of statistic independence from mere differences in wording. The 2023 generalized validity theorem is logically compatible with all results here but does not assert this distribution, strict extremal comparison or boundary example.

## Limitations
The unconditional exact law requires pairwise-distinct orbit scores and an involutive group; it does not apply without modification to ties in the statistic, to arbitrary non-involutive groups, or to sampling auxiliary transformations with dependence. The extremality is a null-calibration result and is **not** an assertion that uniform sampling gives optimal statistical power under alternatives. The counterexample establishes failure already at three auxiliary samples, not a classification of all larger sampling budgets or all permissible null distributions. Earlier equivalent derivations outside the inspected sources cannot be excluded, and independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
