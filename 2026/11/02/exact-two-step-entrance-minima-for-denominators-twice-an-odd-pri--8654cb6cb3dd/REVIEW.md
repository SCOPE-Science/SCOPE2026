# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The exact-order-two criterion follows from reducing the first iterate to denominator \(d=M/\gcd(q,M)\) and requiring its integer part to be divisible by \(d\). Every smaller quotient is excluded by \(Q\le2q-1\), and the first quotient gives a precisely bounded remainder interval. The proof solves that interval for both prime congruence classes and checks all five small-prime exceptions. Exact-rational experiments reproduced the minimizers but are not the infinite proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The family \(2p\) is the first natural infinite denominator family with two distinct prime factors and is directly motivated by a stated minimum-entrance question. Its closed form provides a sharp mod-four phase boundary and a finite exceptional set that are absent from prime-power results. This is a complete arithmetic classification, not an arbitrary numerical slice. Other mixed denominators and higher orders remain open.
