For an isotropically oriented circular exoplanet detectable solely outside an inner working angle, two direct-imaging visits have an exact spherical-cap completeness law and are optimally separated by a quarter orbit; a whole interval of delays is equally optimal when the normalized obscuration radius is below \(1/\sqrt2\). Above that sharp threshold, complete detection becomes impossible and the optimal miss probability turns on as \((\tau-1/\sqrt2)^{3/2}\).
