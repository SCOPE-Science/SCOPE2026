# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The claim is an exact formula for the maximum equidistance-bisector size on every \(C_m\square C_n\) with \(m,n\ge3\), and hence for the universal metric-dimension threshold. The general identity between nonresolving sets and bisector subsets is proved directly. Explicit distance-difference histograms are derived for odd and even cycles, including antipodal shifts. The majorization/rearrangement argument gives a sharp diagonal maximum for both-coordinate displacements. Zero-coordinate displacements are enumerated separately. The remaining comparison accounts for every parity and the sharp four-cycle exception. The supplied checker independently computes graph shortest paths for small parameters; all tested cases agree. Finite tests are not substituted for the general proof.

**Originality — PASS, subject to the stated literature-access risk.** The 2022 metric-dimension-threshold primary paper provides the definition and an unevaluated bisector bound, not this graph-family formula. The 2005/2007 Cartesian-product study determines smallest resolving sets, which do not imply the largest bisector or universal threshold. The 2026 paper on equidistant dimension studies distance-*equalizer* sets with opposite quantifiers and only cycle-prism products, not this invariant on rectangular tori. Separate published-result searches for analogous largest-bisector, toroidal and resolving-number statements identified no exact equivalent or stronger all-parameter coverage. Extant research records involving cylindrical multiset metric dimension or unrelated graph-product thresholds do not imply the claimed formula. Priority of unindexed results remains uncertain.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The universal threshold is a natural robustness invariant: it is the least number of arbitrarily placed landmarks guaranteed to distinguish every vertex. The formula completely determines this invariant for a canonical two-parameter family of Cartesian-product graphs. Its parity dependence and unique four-cycle-factor obstruction explain the transition from diagonal to axial worst-case ambiguities; neither is supplied by the known minimum metric dimension of a torus.

Closest work and limitations are detailed in `RESULT.md` and `AUDIT.json`; the mathematical claim is not extended to twisted, weighted or higher-dimensional tori. The complete proof applies to all specified orders; finite verification is explicitly restricted to its actual ranges.
