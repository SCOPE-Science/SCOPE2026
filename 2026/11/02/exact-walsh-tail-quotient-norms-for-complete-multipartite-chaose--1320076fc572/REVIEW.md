# Scientific review: exact multipartite Walsh-tail quotient norms

## Correctness

PASS. The complete claim is quantified for all integers \(r\ge2\) and positive block sizes, with real uniform Boolean-cube normalization. The exact lower certificate is \(\mathbb E[QF]=\prod_j n_j\), and the matching upper certificate is the scaled product of block sign functions. Odd-parity Fourier support proves that no unwanted degree-\(\le r\) modes appear. Equality gives uniqueness on the nonzero set; weighted stability uses the dual equality and a bounded positive-part identity. Even/odd binomial evaluations and Stirling yield the stated exact and asymptotic constants. The 269 exact finite tests supplement, not replace, the all-dimensional proof.

## Originality

PASS, qualified. Defant et al., arXiv:2302.00233, Theorem 3.1, compute a Fourier-subspace **projection constant**, whereas this result fixes one complete multipartite polynomial's low-degree coefficients and optimizes its full high-degree correction. The associated universal lower bound can be strict for other hypergraphs (triangle), so the new sign-spectrum attainment argument is essential. Eskenazis–Zhang, arXiv:2311.09021, treat tail approximation through general inequalities and \(L_1\) estimates, without the present exact \(L_\infty\) multipartite result. Actual published-finding corpus aliases and prior-claim checks did not locate exact coverage. An older equivalent via standard tensorization remains a concrete priority risk; an unsuccessful search is not novelty proof.

## Mathematical value

PASS. Complete \(r\)-partite homogeneous Walsh polynomials are canonical low-degree objects in Boolean harmonic analysis and finite-dimensional Banach quotient geometry. The theorem computes a natural exact all-parameter quotient norm with an explicit optimizer, equality rigidity and a stability theorem, plus a parity-corrected growth law. This distinguishes genuinely sharp Fourier-tail completion from the related projection constant and from the original uncorrected polynomial norm.

## Closest literature and limitations

The closest primary full texts inspected are arXiv:2302.00233 and arXiv:2311.09021. The result is confined to real uniform cubes, complete unweighted multipartite supports and fixed-block-count asymptotics. It does not classify arbitrary hypergraph coefficients or \(L_p\) tail approximation, and it does not claim uniqueness on zero sets when block sizes are even.
