Among binary words of length \(n\ge8\) with exactly three ones, the number of distinct full nonadjacent ordered-pair decks is \(14n-66\) for odd \(n\) and \((27n-130)/2\) for even \(n\). A complete eight-run-type classification explains the parity effect and shows that the attainable zero-error information grows as only \(\log_2 n+O(1)\) bits despite \(\binom n3\) possible source words.
