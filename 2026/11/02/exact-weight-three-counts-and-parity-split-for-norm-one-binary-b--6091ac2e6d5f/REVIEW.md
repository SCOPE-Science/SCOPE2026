# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is an infinite-family formula for \(A_3\), plus its one-coordinate support degree, for the binary BCH code with one prescribed root. Every weight-three word corresponds to a zero-sum triple in the norm-one subgroup, counted six times by ordered normalization. The nonstandard step counts rational-root parameters of \(T^3+aT^2+(a+1)T+1\) using the order-three map \(t\mapsto1/(1+t)\), whose only fixed points are the two primitive cube roots of unity when \(s\) is even. This gives the exact root count \(K_s=2q+1-(-1)^s\). The norm and trace identities, fixed-point exceptions, and absence of weight-one/two words are explicitly proved. Independent integer-field enumeration and an independent cubic-map census agree for \(s=1\) through \(5\), but finite checks are not used as the universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risks
The closest full-text publications establish, respectively, a minimum-distance conjecture and then its resolution by **existence** of a suitable cubic (Chen–Xie–Ding 2025, Section 5; Wang–He–Yi–Zheng 2026, Section III-B). Neither supplies the exact all-parameter number of irreducible members, the parity-dependent minimum-weight coefficient, or the incidence degree. The 2026 proof's mere upper bound on the image size of the rational-root map is strictly weaker than determining every fiber. Searches for alternate finite-field, norm-one, and BCH multiplicity formulations found no exact dominating theorem in the inspected materials or mathematical research records. Residual risks include older Singer-cycle incidence tables, unindexed cyclic-code enumerators, and implicit coefficient identities in more general formulas. The 2019 related BCH work was seen at abstract level, not fully inspected, and is not used as whole-document negative evidence.

## Value — PASS
The coefficient \(A_3\) is the complete minimum-weight multiplicity for an infinite BCH family, naturally refining the recently solved existence question; the per-coordinate incidence gives the local geometry of the first nonzero weight layer. The periodic parity split is an intrinsic finite-field phenomenon, and the values can inform low-weight enumerator estimates without claiming coding-rate or decoding-complexity improvements. The theorem is substantially different from merely restating known distance three or tabulating isolated small cases.

## Boundaries
The proof does not recover higher-weight coefficients. Numerical examples through \(s=5\) corroborate but do not establish the universal statement. Equivalent prior coverage remains an uncertainty requiring independent expert review.
