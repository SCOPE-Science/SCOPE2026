# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The case split on the hub action is exhaustive. For thresholds \(r\ge3\), a hub-inactive equilibrium is exactly a rim maximal independent set whose size avoids \(r\); the cyclic distances between selected rim vertices are exactly two or three. For a hub-active equilibrium the rim is forced to be inactive, except at \(r=3\) when all-active is additionally possible only for the triangular rim. At \(r=2\), the rim response reduces to one of two exact three-periodic equations over \(\mathbb F_2\). All conditions on the hub are checked. The uniqueness and asymptotic consequences follow from the permitted size interval and the Perrin recurrence. The independent profile enumerator matched all \(99\) small parameter cases and \(425{,}952\) action-profile evaluations. This finite evidence is supplementary to the fully quantified proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority uncertainty

The source papers of Klimm--Stahlberg (arXiv:2301.11849), Gilboa--Nisan (arXiv:2207.04238) and Gilboa (arXiv:2301.11580) were compared at the level of their actual equilibrium problems, proofs and graph families; they establish complexity results for broad response patterns, not the exact wheel count or its one-equilibrium boundary. The classical Bisdorff--Marichal cyclic maximal-independent-set paper is openly acknowledged as covering the Perrin component. Its result alone does not incorporate the wheel hub's strategic compatibility or the distinct \(r=2\) modular spectrum. Relevant mathematical index searches did not identify a stronger wheel theorem subsuming the claim. This remains a best-knowledge judgment rather than an absolute priority certificate.

## Value — PASS

The all-size wheel family provides a natural structural probe of general NP-hard nonmonotone public-goods response patterns. The exact count reveals a phase distinction between bounded and exponentially many stable configurations, plus the only unique-equilibrium threshold/size pair. Such a complete classification offers a sharp benchmark for network design, equilibrium enumeration and the effect of adding a universal hub; it is not merely a recomputation of a known sequence because the hub constraints change the answer in a threshold-dependent way.

## Limitations and closest literature

The theorem concerns only strict, homogeneous, unweighted wheel games. It makes no claim about convergence or welfare. A classical count of maximal independent sets on cycles and general nonmonotone equilibrium complexity classifications are prior results and are separated from the new hub-dependent assertions. Specialist verification and independent audit remain outstanding.
