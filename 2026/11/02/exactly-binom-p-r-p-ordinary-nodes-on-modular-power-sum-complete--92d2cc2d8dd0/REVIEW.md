# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The three defining power sums are equivalent to the first three elementary symmetric equations in characteristic at least five, hence a regular sequence. The Jacobian is a Vandermonde matrix of column degree two, so any singular projective point has at most two coordinate values. The two leading power-sum equations force one block to be zero and the other to have exactly \(p\) coordinates, as the ambient count lies strictly between \(p\) and \(2p\). Formal elimination of two independent equations at each supported point produces a nondegenerate quadratic form: the determinants of its two blocks are \(-1\) and \(r\) modulo \(p\), both units. This proves exact ordinary-node local types, reduced Jacobian singular scheme, and, through \(S_2+R_1\) and connectedness, geometrical integrality and normality. A separate exact-arithmetic checker ran successfully; the proof is uniform.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The directly motivating arXiv:2610.32056v1 proves regularity and radicality and gives singular-dimension bounds, but Theorem 3.7 explicitly assumes \(p>n-1\), excluding every member of the present \(n=p+r\) family. Its materially inspected text does not give this exact nodal singular scheme. Conca–Singh–Soundararajan's inspected treatment does not give the prime-field node count, and the five-coordinate modular Bring degeneration has \(n=p\), not \(p<n<2p\). Actual source, alias, finite-database and broader-coverage comparisons found no implication of the exact formula. Residual risks include terminology outside the searched power-sum and elementary-symmetric descriptions and incomplete coverage of earlier mathematical literature; absence of a hit is not proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result addresses a stated gap in finite-field power-sum geometry: it describes what happens precisely when the large-characteristic assumption in the motivating normality theorem fails. It produces an infinite family with an explicit complete singular scheme, all local analytic types and their exact number, not merely another upper bound. The first case exhibits a natural degree-six nodal K3 complete intersection in characteristic five. These facts can inform point-counting with singular corrections, deformation and resolution geometry, without those applications being claimed as theorems.

## Scientific limitations
The argument assumes \(p\ge5\), exactly the first three consecutive power sums, and \(p<n<2p\). No statement is made for higher moments, smaller characteristic, projective dimensions outside this range, or resolution invariants beyond the stated surface consequence. Bibliographic completeness and independent validation remain open.
