# Exceptional-characteristic arithmetic of the one-base-point cubic pencil

## Finding

The integral plane-cubic pencil
\[
 C_t:\quad xy^2+yz^2+zx^2+t(x^3+xyz-y^3)=0,\qquad C_\infty:\ x^3+xyz-y^3=0
\]
has a single curvilinear base point of length nine in every characteristic. Its plane singular-fiber geometry changes sharply in characteristics two and three, as follows.

**Characteristic two.** Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic two the singular members are exactly the three finite fibers with \(t^3=1\) and the member at infinity. Each finite singular member is an irreducible nodal cubic with its unique node at \([0:t:1]\); the infinity member is an irreducible nodal cubic with its unique node at \([0:0:1]\). If \(q=2^r\), the number of smooth finite members defined over \(\mathbb F_q\) is
\[
q-\gcd(3,q-1).
\]
For every singular finite parameter in \(\mathbb F_q\), the rational point count is \(q+2\) when \(r\) is odd (nonsplit node) and \(q\) when \(r\) is even (split node). The infinity member has exactly \(q\) rational points. Among the smooth finite fibers, \(C_0\) is supersingular and every \(C_t\) with \(t\ne0\) is ordinary.

**Characteristic three.** Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic three the singular members are exactly \(C_0\) and \(C_\infty\). The former is an irreducible cubic with a single \(A_2\) cusp at \([1:1:1]\); the latter is an irreducible cubic with a split ordinary node at \([0:0:1]\). All finite \(C_t\) with \(t\ne0\) are smooth ordinary genus-one curves, with
\[
 j(C_t)=-t^9.
\]
Thus the nonconstant modular map on the finite smooth locus is purely inseparable of degree nine. Over \(\mathbb F_{3^r}\) there are exactly \(3^r-1\) smooth finite parameter fibers, while the cusp and infinity members have respectively \(3^r+1\) and \(3^r\) rational points.

All singularities, splitting claims and counts concern these **plane cubics**, not the Kodaira fibers of a subsequently blown-up surface. No assertion of uniqueness among all characteristic-two or characteristic-three pencils is made.

## Assumptions and scope

Work with the specific two cubic forms displayed above, with their coefficients reduced from \(\mathbb Z\). When discussing geometric types the ground field is algebraically closed; when counting points it is \(\mathbb F_q\) with exactly the indicated characteristic. A parameter at infinity means the generator \(x^3+xyz-y^3\), not a limit in the finite affine parameter chart. A node is split over \(\mathbb F_q\) if both normalization preimages of its rational singular support are rational.

The primary literature assigns this pencil to a study over \(\mathbb C\). The fact that its first generator becomes cuspidal in characteristic three had also been explicitly observed before this computation; it is *not* claimed as new on its own.

## Proof

**1. The base point.** In the chart \(z=1\), write
\[
 F=xy^2+y+x^2,\quad G=x^3+xy-y^3.
\]
Exact elimination gives \(\operatorname{Res}_y(F,G)=x^9\). Thus every affine geometric base point has \(x=0\), whence \(F=0\) forces \(y=0\). At \(z=0\) the equations reduce to \(xy^2=x^3-y^3=0\), with no projective solution. The gradient \(F_y(0,0,1)=1\) makes the local base scheme curvilinear, and Bézout, with no shared component, gives its length nine.

**2. Exhaustive finite singularity calculation.** For a finite member let \(P_t=F+tG\). The exact affine Jacobian ideals have reduced zero sets obtained by the following lexicographic Gröbner bases (the displayed ideals are in fact the full ideals generated by the equation and its two affine partial derivatives):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{char}2:\quad &(x,\ y+t,\ t^3+1),\\
\operatorname{char}3:\quad &(x-y^2,\ y^3-1,\ t).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the parameter \(t\) is treated as an additional indeterminate. These identities are recomputed by the supplied exact checker. There are no singular points on \(z=0\): in characteristic two the partial derivatives there include \(t y^2\), \(t x^2+y^2\), and \(x^2+txy\), whose joint vanishing has no nonzero projective solution; in characteristic three they include \(y^2\) and \(x^2+txy\), again excluding nonzero solutions. It follows that the listed affine sets exhaust all geometric singular members.

**3. Local analytic type in characteristic two.** At a finite singular parameter \(t^3=1\), put \(z=1\), \(y=t+v\) and retain terms of degree two in \((x,v)\). The quadratic initial form is
\[
x^2+txv+t^2v^2.
\]
It has two distinct tangent lines over an algebraic closure because \(t\ne0\). The tangent directions over the residue field are determined by \(u^2+u+1\), where \(u=x/(tv)\), so they are defined over \(\mathbb F_4\) and over \(\mathbb F_{2^r}\) precisely when \(r\) is even. At infinity, \(G\) has its only singularity at \([0:0:1]\), with reduced tangent cone \(xy\), hence a split ordinary node.

**4. The characteristic-three cusp.** The affine singular equations force \(t=0\), \(x=y^2\), \(y^3=1\). In characteristic three the latter has only one geometric root, yielding \((x,y)=(1,1)\). Put \(x=1+w-v\), \(y=1+v\), \(z=1\). The defining equation becomes exactly
\[
w^2+w v^2-v^3=0.
\]
Completing the square in \(w\), then absorbing the remaining unit into a formal coordinate, gives \(w'^2-v'^3=0\), an \(A_2\) cusp with normalization defect one. The infinity member has only the singularity \([0:0:1]\) and split tangent cone \(xy\), also in characteristic three. Every other finite member is nonsingular.

**5. Irreducibility and rational-point counts.** Any smooth projective plane cubic is geometrically integral. A reduced reducible cubic cannot have just one singularity that is an ordinary node or unibranch cusp: a line and conic must meet with total intersection multiplicity two, and the alternatives produce at least two distinct intersection supports or a higher contact singularity; three concurrent lines produce a triple point. Therefore all listed singular cubics are geometrically integral. Their normalizations are \(\mathbb P^1\); a rational cusp identifies one rational normalization point with itself, a split node identifies two rational points, and a nonsplit node identifies a Galois-conjugate quadratic pair into a rational singular point. Their counts are respectively \(q+1\), \(q\), and \(q+2\). The finite singular parameters are the \(\mathbb F_q\)-solutions of \(t^3=1\) in characteristic two and \(t=0\) in characteristic three, giving the asserted parameter counts.

**6. Ordinarity and the modular map.** For a smooth plane cubic in characteristic \(p\), its Hasse invariant up to a nonzero scalar is the coefficient of \((xyz)^{p-1}\) in the \((p-1)\)-st power of its defining cubic. In characteristic two this coefficient is \(t\); in characteristic three the coefficient of \(x^2y^2z^2\) in \(P_t^2\) is \(t^2\). Since a smooth genus-one curve is ordinary exactly when the coefficient is nonzero, the ordinarity statements follow.

To obtain the characteristic-three modular map, project from the base point via \(y=ux,z=1\); removing the factor \(x\) gives the generic quadratic
\[
\bigl(u^2+t(1-u^3)\bigr)x^2+(1+tu)x+u=0.
\]
In characteristic different from two its discriminant is the binary quartic
\[
Q_t(u)=4tu^4-4u^3+t^2u^2-2tu+1.
\]
Its classical binary-quartic invariant and discriminant are
\[
I=t(t^3+24),\qquad \operatorname{disc}(Q_t)=-256(t^3+27).
\]
For a smooth genus-one double cover of \(\mathbb P^1\) branched at a squarefree quartic, \(j=256I^3/\operatorname{disc}(Q_t)\). Consequently
\[
j=-\frac{t^3(t^3+24)^3}{t^3+27}.
\]
This formula is valid under the stated smoothness condition in characteristic three, since two remains invertible; reduction gives exactly \(j=-t^9\). Its formal derivative vanishes and it is a nonconstant ninth power, giving inseparability degree nine.

## Relationship to prior work

Moschetti–Pirola–Stoppino (2024 preprint; expanded 2025 edition) give exactly the integral generators used here and prove a uniqueness theorem and four nodal plane members **over the complex numbers** (Sections 5–7). They relate the resolved complex surface to Beauville's \(I_9+3I_1\) fibration. These antecedents establish the motivating geometry but do not, in the inspected text, provide this specific reduction-by-two-or-three classification, rational split-versus-nonsplit node counts, or characteristic-three inseparable modular function. The characteristic-three cusp of \(F=0\) itself was already pointed out publicly in 2020 and is treated as known.

Lang's positive-characteristic classification of extremal rational elliptic surfaces and the Jarvis–Lang–Rimmasch–Rogers–Summers–Petrosyan characteristic-three classification constrain Kodaira-fiber possibilities after resolution, but an abstract classification of possible singular-fiber configurations does not determine the explicitly marked singular points, rational branch splitting, or rational point counts in these **unresolved plane models**. No claim is made about new Kodaira types.

## Limitations

Only the specified integral pencil is considered; the results are not a classification of all one-base-point pencils in small characteristic. The characteristic-two rational count is stated for finite **singular** members only; no closed formula is asserted for the rational points of arbitrary smooth members. The characteristic-three modular function concerns the smooth finite locus. The precise resolution graphs, wild discriminant conductors, and Mordell–Weil groups of the associated elliptic surfaces are outside the claim. A covering calculation could exist in unindexed classical treatments of modular elliptic surfaces; the originality assessment is not an independent priority certification.
