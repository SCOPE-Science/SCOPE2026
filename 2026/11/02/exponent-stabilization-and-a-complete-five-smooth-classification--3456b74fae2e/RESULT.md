# Exponent stabilization and a complete five-smooth classification of least unit generators

## Finding

For each positive integer \(n\), define \(G(n)\) as the least positive integer \(B\) for which the residue classes of all positive \(u\le B\) with \(\gcd(u,n)=1\) generate the unit group \((\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times\). We set \(G(1)=G(2)=1\) for the trivial groups.

Write \(v_p(n)\) for the exponent of prime \(p\) in \(n\), and define
\[
\kappa(n)=2^{\min(v_2(n),3)}\prod_{\substack{p\mid n\\p\text{ odd}}}p^{\min(v_p(n),2)}.
\]
**Universal stabilization:** for every positive integer \(n\),
\[
G(n)=G(\kappa(n)).
\]
**Complete five-smooth classification:** for every triple of nonnegative integers \(a,b,c\), \(G(2^a3^b5^c)\) is the entry in the following table. The column is determined by \(a\), and the row by whether \(b,c\) vanish.

| Condition on \(b,c\) | \(a=0\) | \(a=1\) | \(a=2\) | \(a\ge3\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(b=c=0\) | 1 | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| \(b\ge1,c=0\) | 2 | 5 | 7 | 13 |
| \(b=0,c\ge1\) | 2 | 3 | 11 | 11 |
| \(b,c\ge1\) | 7 | 11 | 13 | 17 |

In particular \(G(2^a3^b5^c)\le17\) for all nonnegative exponents, with equality throughout \(a\ge3,b\ge1,c\ge1\). The local exponent cutoff at \(2\) cannot uniformly be replaced by \(2\): \(G(4)=3\), \(G(8)=5\); and the odd-prime cutoff at exponent \(2\) cannot uniformly be replaced by exponent \(1\): \(G(42)=11\), \(G(126)=13\).

## Assumptions and scope

All integers and exponents are as stated; no unproved prime-distribution hypothesis is used. The threshold \(G(n)\) includes composite integers, though it is also the least prime bound at which all primes coprime to \(n\) generate the group: composite residues factor into their prime factors. The finite table uses ordinary group multiplication, not additive generation. The convention for the trivial unit groups is explicit.

## Proof

**1. Frattini lifting criterion.** For a finite group \(H\), let \(\Phi(H)\) be the intersection of maximal proper subgroups. If \(K\lhd H\) lies in \(\Phi(H)\), a subset \(T\subseteq H\) generates \(H\) precisely when its image generates \(H/K\). One direction is immediate. Conversely, a subgroup generated by \(T\) which is proper lies in some maximal proper subgroup \(M\), and \(K\subseteq M\), so its image in \(H/K\) is proper, a contradiction.

**2. Prime-power kernels.** For odd prime \(p\), the group \((\mathbb Z/p^e\mathbb Z)^\times\) is cyclic of order \((p-1)p^{e-1}\). For \(e\ge3\), the reduction to \(p^2\) has kernel of order \(p^{e-2}\). This kernel lies in the unique maximal \(p\)-power subgroup of the cyclic group, hence in its Frattini subgroup. (Explicitly, it consists of \(p\)-th powers in the cyclic \(p\)-component.) For \(p=2\), \((\mathbb Z/2^e\mathbb Z)^\times\cong C_2\times C_{2^{e-2}}\) for \(e\ge3\). The reduction to \(8\) has kernel of order \(2^{e-3}\), contained in the squares subgroup and hence in the Frattini subgroup for \(e\ge4\). At smaller exponents the reduction map is the identity.

For the Chinese-remainder decomposition of \((\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times\), these coordinate kernels form a subgroup of its Frattini subgroup: for finite abelian groups \(A\) and \(B\), \(\Phi(A\times B)=\Phi(A)\times\Phi(B)\), because any homomorphism to a prime-order cyclic group vanishes on each corresponding Frattini subgroup. The reduction \((\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times\to(\mathbb Z/\kappa(n)\mathbb Z)^\times\) therefore has kernel in \(\Phi((\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times)\). The admissible positive integers \(u\) are identical for both moduli because \(n\) and \(\kappa(n)\) have the same prime divisors. Applying Step 1 to every initial interval proves \(G(n)=G(\kappa(n))\).

**3. Finite exact classification.** For five-smooth \(n\), Step 2 reduces the unrestricted exponent range to the 36 moduli \(2^a3^b5^c\) with \(0\le a\le3\), \(0\le b\le2\), \(0\le c\le2\), all at most 1800. For any such \(n\), define \(H_B\) as the subgroup generated by coprime integers \(u\le B\). Begin with \(H_1=\{1\}\), and, for each new coprime \(u\), replace \(H\) with its closure \(\langle H,u\rangle\). A subgroup reaches the full unit group exactly when its cardinality equals \(\varphi(n)\). This gives an exact finite certificate: the accompanying standalone verifier carries out this closure for all 36 cases, compares the resulting minimum with each table entry, checks \(\lvert H_{G(n)}\rvert=\varphi(n)\), and for every \(G(n)>1\) verifies \(\lvert H_{G(n)-1}\rvert<\varphi(n)\). An independently implemented breadth-first search computes both membership-cardinality tests without relying on the incremental closure algorithm. Thus every entry is both achievable and minimal, and the universal exponent reduction extends the finite table to all \(a,b,c\ge0\).

For the claimed sharp boundaries, the same exact checks yield \(G(4)=3\), \(G(8)=5\), \(G(42)=11\), and \(G(126)=13\). The latter pair changes only the exponent of the odd prime \(3\).

## Relationship to prior work

Bellotti [1] studies the same least-generating threshold \(G(n)\) and proves mean and almost-all estimates, using prime-order Dirichlet characters and analytic estimates. An inspection of its full available HTML text, including its statements, Sections 3–8 and references, found no explicit exponent-stabilization identity or complete five-smooth classification; its asymptotic bounds do not determine these exact values. Bach–Huelsbergen [2] studied statistical small generating sets; OEIS A232928 [3] records the same threshold (for nontrivial unit groups) for the finite moduli \(3\le n\le120\), not a formula for all five-smooth exponents. The finite entries overlapping that data are not claimed individually new. The claim here is the universal lifting identity together with the *infinite*, complete three-prime-support classification and sharp local cutoffs, with a self-contained proof and finite certificate.

## Limitations

The finite smooth-support table is exact for primes \(2,3,5\), not for arbitrary prime support. The universal stabilization formula does not alone evaluate \(G(n)\) for primes outside that support. The use of the classical Frattini criterion and cyclic structure of prime-power unit groups is not claimed as new. Although no source checked was found to imply the complete combined statement, a prior equivalent classification could exist, particularly in older computational generating-set literature; no independent audit or priority certification has occurred.
