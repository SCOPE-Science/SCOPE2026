# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The kernel of the map on unit groups to the exponent-clipped modulus is contained in the Frattini subgroup, so generation of the quotient is equivalent to generation upstairs for every fixed collection of coprime input integers. The required prime-power unit-group decompositions, exceptional small powers of two and all nonnegative exponent boundaries are explicit in the proof. The 36 representatives exhaust the reduced \(\{2,3,5\}\) support; their exact subgroup closures and strict previous-bound failures were checked with two independent algorithms. The finite checks are not asserted to prove the unrestricted group-theoretic step.

## Originality — PASS, with limitations

Bellotti's full accessible HTML text concerns analytic mean and almost-all bounds on the same invariant; its stated results and arguments do not evaluate the finite-smooth family or imply the exact exponent-clipping identity. OEIS A232928 lists the same sequence only for finite moduli through \(120\), so it covers individual small entries but not the infinite classification. Bach and Huelsbergen's older generating-set study is closely relevant, and an equivalent classification hidden there or in other older literature remains a priority risk because its complete text was not available for comparison. Mathematical-record and literature searches found no stronger covering statement. This is a best-knowledge originality assessment, not a novelty guarantee.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The least unit-generating threshold is a recognized arithmetic invariant, not a contrived slice. The general structural reduction eliminates arbitrary powers from *every* exact evaluation on fixed prime support. The first nontrivial three-prime smooth support receives a full closed classification, gives infinitely many moduli with the sharp value \(17\), and demonstrates both local exponent thresholds really matter. Exact classification of this natural invariant contributes information not implied by average or almost-all estimates; the arguments deliberately use elementary finite group theory rather than claiming an analytic breakthrough.

The earlier data for small integers remain prior art, and classification outside the specified support is unproved.
