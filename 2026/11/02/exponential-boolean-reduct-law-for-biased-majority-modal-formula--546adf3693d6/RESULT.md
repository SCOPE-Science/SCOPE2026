# Exponential Boolean-reduct law for biased majority modal formulas on dense directed graphs

## Finding
Fix \(k\ge1\) propositional colours with independent marginal probabilities \(0<\pi_i<1\), and a fixed edge probability \(0<\rho\le1\). Assume **full Boolean bias**: for all Boolean colour formulas \(A,B\), equality of their probabilities under the product distribution implies their logical equivalence. Consider the majority modal language generated by colours, negation, conjunction, and the modal counting comparison \(\#\varphi\ge\#\psi\), interpreted as the number of outgoing successors satisfying each argument. Arbitrary finite nesting is permitted.

**Theorem (simultaneous Boolean reduction).** Every formula \(\varphi\) has an effectively computable propositional Boolean reduct \(b(\varphi)\) such that there are constants \(C_\varphi,c_\varphi>0\), independent of the number \(n\) of vertices, for which
\[
\Pr\bigl(\forall v\in[n]:\ \mathcal M_n,v\models\varphi\ \Longleftrightarrow\ b(\varphi)(\operatorname{col}(v))\bigr)\ge1-C_\varphi e^{-c_\varphi n}.
\]
Here \(\mathcal M_n\) has all \(n^2\) directed edges, including loops, independently present with probability \(\rho\), independently of the i.i.d. coloured vertices. In particular, for every fixed vertex \(v\),
\[
\Pr(\mathcal M_n,v\models\varphi)=\Pr_\pi(b(\varphi))+O_\varphi(e^{-c_\varphi n}).
\]
The reduct of any counting comparison is a Boolean constant. Consequently every Boolean combination solely of counting comparisons has a zero-one limit, whereas the unrestricted language has the exact collection of limits given by its propositional Boolean probabilities.

**Explicit full-bias spectrum.** For every \(k\ge1\), choose the rational biases \(\pi_i=(1+2^{2^i})^{-1}\) for \(0\le i<k\). Put \(D_k=2^{2^k}-1\). All \(2^{2^k}\) Boolean truth tables have different probabilities, and the attainable limits, over all fixed majority formulas with these \(k\) colours, are exactly
\[
\left\{\frac j{D_k}:\ 0\le j\le D_k\right\}.
\]
This is a full convergence classification, not a blanket zero-one assertion for formulas with free colours at the root.

## Assumptions and scope
The number of colours, each bias, edge probability, and formula are **fixed as \(n\to\infty\)**. Vertices are labelled; self-loops are admitted; each directed edge is an independent Bernoulli variable. Colours are independent across vertices and independent between colours at one vertex. The finite language contains \(p_i\), Boolean connectives and counting comparisons \(\#\varphi\ge\#\psi\); each successor is counted once. The formula may contain comparisons under further comparisons and Boolean connectives. Full Boolean bias is equality-injectivity on equivalence classes of Boolean truth tables, not merely pairwise distinct atomic biases. It is essential: without it two inequivalent Boolean formulas may have equal measures and random tie signs need not concentrate on a constant.

## Proof
Define \(\beta(A)=\Pr_\pi(A)\) for each Boolean formula \(A\). Define \(b\) recursively: \(b(p_i)=p_i\); \(b(\neg\varphi)=\neg b(\varphi)\); \(b(\varphi\wedge\psi)=b(\varphi)\wedge b(\psi)\), and similarly for other Boolean connectives. For \(\xi=(\#\varphi\ge\#\psi)\), first compute \(A=b(\varphi)\), \(B=b(\psi)\), then set \(b(\xi)=\top\) if \(\beta(A)\ge\beta(B)\), and \(\bot\) otherwise. All Boolean expectations are finite exact sums over \(2^k\) assignments, so this recursion is effective for rational biases; for general prescribed real biases, it is an exact mathematical recursion assuming their comparisons are supplied. No claim of algorithmic decidability of arbitrary real-number comparisons is made.

We prove the simultaneous assertion by structural induction. It is exact for atomic colours and preserved by Boolean operations by taking the union of exceptional events. For a comparison \(\xi\), assume inductively that with probability at least \(1-Ce^{-cn}\), **every vertex** satisfies the two arguments exactly when its Boolean reduct holds. On that event, the comparison at any vertex \(v\) agrees with the sign of
\[
S_v=\sum_{u=1}^n E_{vu}\bigl(\mathbf1_{A(\operatorname{col}(u))}-\mathbf1_{B(\operatorname{col}(u))}\bigr).
\]
For each fixed \(v\), these \(n\) summands are independent, lie in \([-1,1]\), and have common expectation \(\rho\,\delta\), where \(\delta=\beta(A)-\beta(B)\). They are independent even when \(u=v\), since edges are independent of colours. If \(\delta>0\), Hoeffding's inequality yields \(\Pr(S_v<0)\le\exp(-n\rho^2\delta^2/2)\); if \(\delta<0\), it gives \(\Pr(S_v\ge0)\le\exp(-n\rho^2\delta^2/2)\) after an innocuous adjustment for equality (or bound \(S_v\ge0\) by the corresponding non-strict tail). A union bound over all \(n\) vertices preserves an exponential estimate. If \(\delta=0\), full Boolean bias says \(A\equiv B\) as truth tables; therefore all summands vanish **identically**, including on arbitrary graphs, so the comparison is true and its reduct \(\top\). This resolves the critical tie case without a central-limit approximation. Induction proves the first display. Taking the expectation of the indicator at one vertex gives the second display.

For the explicit biases, if \(a=(a_0,\ldots,a_{k-1})\in\{0,1\}^k\) is a complete colour assignment, its probability is
\[
\Pr(a)=\frac{2^{\sum_{i:a_i=0}2^i}}{\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(1+2^{2^i})}=\frac{2^{j(a)}}{2^{2^k}-1},
\]
where \(j(a)\) runs bijectively through \(0,\ldots,2^k-1\). Every Boolean formula selects a subset of these atom assignments, so its probability is the corresponding distinct subset sum of powers of two divided by \(D_k\). Every integer \(0,\ldots,D_k\) occurs once as such a sum. Thus the bias is full, and the spectrum follows. Finally a formula formed only by Boolean combinations of comparisons has constant reduct by induction, while an atomic colour has a nontrivial limit \(\pi_i\); this proves the stated boundary of the zero-one subfragment.

## Verification
The complete result is analytical. The accompanying standalone \(checker.py\) implements exact fractional probabilities for \(k=2\) with \((\pi_0,\pi_1)=(1/3,1/5)\), verifies all \(16\) Boolean truth tables have pairwise distinct probabilities, computes the reduct on four nested or Boolean-combined examples, and exhaustively enumerates all coloured directed graphs (loops included) on \(1\), \(2\), and \(3\) vertices for those examples. It uses exact rational arithmetic and prints `ALL_CHECKS_OK`. These finite checks are not used to infer the infinite theorem. Execute `python3 checker.py` from the extracted artifact directory.

## Relationship to prior work
Ghosh and Schwarzentruber, *On Counting Graphs for Modal Logic with Counting* (arXiv:2610.26187v1, first posted 2026-10-19), introduce the model-counting setting, give finite formulas and discuss nonconvergence and a full-Boolean-bias zero-one conjecture (Appendix B, Conjecture 43). The present result supplies an exact **uniform nodewise Boolean-reduct theorem**, exponentially small error, and the complete nondegenerate limit spectrum, rather than identifying all atomic-root formulas with zero-one limits. For formulae whose outer layer consists only of majority comparisons, it establishes the natural zero-one subcase.

Adam-Day, Iliant and Ceylan (arXiv:2301.13060) prove asymptotic constancy for GNN graph classifiers, with non-splitting and synchronously-saturating assumptions, in a different graph model and architecture class; their Theorem 4.10 does not assert this exact all-vertex Boolean reduct for the counting-formula language with equality ties. Adam-Day et al. (arXiv:2403.03880) address weighted-mean aggregation, not hard sign comparisons of unnormalised sums. The reduction here is directly proved and no priority over those general concentration techniques is claimed.

## Limitations
The theorem requires independent dense directed edges and i.i.d. vertex colours with strictly positive full bias; it makes no claim for sparse \(\rho=\rho_n\to0\), undirected-edge dependence, growing formula size, arbitrary real bias decision procedures, or bias collisions. Atom-root formulas may have limits strictly between zero and one, so an unrestricted zero-one law is false. The result has not undergone independent review; potential earlier equivalent specialised convergence theorems remain a priority risk.
