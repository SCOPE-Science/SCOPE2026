# Review of antipodal Hamming-cube negative type

**Correctness — PASS.** The graph metric is the minimum of Hamming distance and its antipodal complement. Fourier diagonalization on the binary quotient produces exactly the half-weight-four Krawtchouk sum, with the correct factor of two and the zero-sum condition. The universal perturbation lower bound uses only the vertex count and the actual distance range. The polynomial Krawtchouk identity, binomial central limit argument (including endpoint and uniform-integrability control), and second-order Taylor error yield the displayed spectral constant and upper exponent bound. The claimed logarithmic decay follows from the resulting two-sided estimates. The checker tests the character normalization and derivatives; it is not substituted for asymptotic proof.

**Originality — PASS, with priority qualification.** Robertson's complete discussion of Hamming cubes treats the unfurled Hamming metric and does not produce the antipodal-quotient negative-type exponent. Chen–Naserasr–Sarti determine the folded/projective graph's equivalent constructions and metric but not this spectral sign transition. Weston (2008) already supplies stronger universal finite-metric lower bounds than the elementary one presented here, which is used only for the logarithmic-rate sandwich. Murugan and Sánchez supply broad finite-metric determinant principles; these permit finite-dimensional computations but do not establish the uniform exponential asymptotic or the fourth-Walsh-mode constant. Relevant searchable published results on folded-cube Rips complexes and Walsh projection norms address other quantities and do not imply this statement. Searches for the quotient, its aliases, Krawtchouk coefficients, the rate and equivalent Hilbert-snowflake thresholds found no directly covering theorem. The source priority search is not exhaustive; an unindexed result or different terminology remains possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Quotienting a canonical family with fixed supremal negative type \(1\) has the surprising effect of driving its negative-type and Hilbert-snowflake thresholds exponentially to zero. The leading constant of a structurally identified Fourier obstruction is determined analytically, and the lower bound proves this is the actual *exponential rate* rather than an overly weak obstruction. This is a natural infinite graph family of interest in metric embedding theory; no arbitrary graph parameters were introduced solely to produce a number.

**Scientific boundaries.** The upper constant is not a claimed asymptotic equivalent for the global critical exponent, and other characters may dominate. No exact individual finite-dimensional critical exponent, higher-order expansion, or weighted extension is established. Numerical tests are finite; historical novelty has not been independently certified.
