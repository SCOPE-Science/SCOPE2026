# Scientific comparison and assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The all-positive feasibility criterion follows by matching the even number of unused vertices separately within each convex face. The necessity of the active-face condition follows by fixing all other selected chords, leaving a forced two-vertex completion. For sufficiency, cyclically rotate an existing local noncrossing matching within any face containing at least four free vertices; this avoids every original edge in the face. The special two-vertex faces contain no forbidden selected chord by the same local forcing condition. The VC bound follows from perfect matching cardinality, the Littlestone bound from an explicit online mistake algorithm, and the \(n^2\) extremal-support count from the star-shaped face dual. The verification program cross-checks 4,786 complete all-positive-feasible supports through twelve vertices by exhaustive label projections, not merely random tests.

**Originality — PASS under available evidence.** The closest geometric primary literature studies flip graphs, optimization or disjoint compatibility rather than the face-by-face all-labeling condition. The work *New variants of Perfect Non-crossing Matchings* gives an applicable single-chord parity test, which does not decide shattering of multiple chords. The flip-graph and disjoint-compatibility analyses do not state the capacity and maximum-support count in their identified theorems. Existing matroid-basis and Hamiltonian-path shattering results are incomparable: matching incidences do not form a matroid base class, while Hamiltonian paths impose a different global structure. Residual risk: a reformulation in older Catalan matching literature might cover a component of the theorem, so historical novelty is not independently certified.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** This is a full, checkable characterization of every feasible shattered query support in an established Catalan family, together with a sharp all-parameter complexity invariant and an exact count of maximal supports. The face-activity condition exposes a local obstruction not captured by parity and noncrossing alone, and yields a geometric test for arbitrary prescribed query sets. Examples with two noncrossing extendible selected chords nevertheless failing shattering show this is not a tautological property of partial matchings.

The proofs cover all positive integers \(n\). Finite enumeration supports but does not replace those proofs. The result pertains only to convex-position noncrossing perfect matchings under edge-incidence queries.
