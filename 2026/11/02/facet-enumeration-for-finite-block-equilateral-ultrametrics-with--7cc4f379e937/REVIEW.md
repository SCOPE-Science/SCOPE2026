# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS (self-review).** The dual ball is the polytope of real normalized Lipschitz potentials. The known active-incidence rank criterion equates extremality with connectedness of its tight-edge graph; the proof also establishes this directly. A connected tight graph for at least two clusters must contain a unit cross-cluster edge, forcing the full range to be exactly one. Hence the cross-cluster tight edges can only connect zero and one, and every cluster must have one of these anchors. Internal distances strictly below one prevent both types in the same cluster. An unanchored interior point has no incident tight edge, so each lower cluster uses exactly the possible values zero and its own internal length, while an upper cluster uses one and one minus its length. This exhausts possible potentials; every listed choice yields a connected tight graph. Independent choices are uniquely recoverable and give the stated count. Exact rational exhaustive grid tests pass for ten profiles. No numerical evidence is substituted for the arbitrary-size analytic argument.

## Originality

**PASS (self-review, with bibliographic risk).** A published count covers independently shortened *disjoint pairs* only. The present result classifies clusters of any cardinality (short edges may overlap at vertices) and independently chosen within-cluster lengths. The published complete-multipartite interior facet laws concern within-part distances above the cross-part distance, not the short-distance ultrametric regime. Classical general connected-tight-graph and generic-metric theorems supply tools but do not state this exact cluster product law. Targeted semantic searches and material reading of these closest published claims and mathematical papers revealed no dominating theorem giving the result. This is a best-knowledge novelty judgement, not an external certification.

## Mathematical value

**PASS (self-review).** The family comprises a natural full classification of finite ultrametrics with one common upper distance and equilateral (possibly differently scaled) lower clusters, a persistent metric model of hierarchical grouping. The exact number of facets is a nontrivial polyhedral invariant not determined merely by knowing the metric or its primal extreme molecules. Arbitrarily large clusters yield exponentially many potential states and a closed factorized facet count independent of all positive internal scales. The example of two three-point clusters gives 98 versus 62 facets at the equilateral endpoint, and the theorem both recovers and genuinely extends the published matching-pair case to overlapping short-edge cliques. This is a mathematically motivated complete family classification, not a selected small computation.

## Closest literature and limits

Montrucchio–Pistone (arXiv:1905.07547) prove the general tight-path characterization and count the equilateral case; Gordon–Petrov (arXiv:1608.06848) count faces in the generic metric setting; Aliaga (arXiv:2102.01219) treats primal extreme molecules. Published disjoint-pair and long-distance multipartite facet formulas are explicitly distinguished from the new class. The classification is limited to positive internally equilateral blocks with common unit intercluster length, and the complete face vector and general multilevel ultrametrics remain outside scope. Inaccessible or unindexed equivalent statements are a residual originality risk.
