# Factual hedged contractions generate left-regular bands with sharp ranks

## Finding

Let \(W\) be a finite nonempty set of \(n\) worlds. For each \(A\subseteq W\) define the factual-contraction row transformation
\[
C_A(S)=\begin{cases}S\cup A,&S\cap A=\varnothing,\\S,&S\cap A\ne\varnothing,\end{cases}\qquad S\subseteq W.
\]
The set \(A\) is the set of worlds falsifying the contracted factual proposition. Products of transformations are written in **chronological order**: \((FG)(S)=G(F(S))\). Write \(H_n\) for the transformation monoid on \(\mathcal P(W)\) generated by the \(C_A\), and \(H_n^{\mathrm{ser}}\) for its analogue on the nonempty subsets \(\mathcal P(W)\setminus\{\varnothing\}\). The latter models serial successor rows.

**Theorem.** Both \(H_n\) and \(H_n^{\mathrm{ser}}\) are left-regular bands: for all transformations \(F,G\) in either monoid,
\[
F^2=F,\qquad FGF=FG.
\]
Their minimum numbers of nonidentity semigroup generators, where an arbitrary element may be selected as a generator, are exactly
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(H_n)=2^n-1,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}(H_n^{\mathrm{ser}})=2^n-2.}
\]
Indeed every \(C_A\) with \(A\ne\varnothing\) is indispensable in every generating set of \(H_n\); in \(H_n^{\mathrm{ser}}\), the indispensable elements are exactly \(C_A\) with \(\varnothing\ne A\subsetneq W\).

More strongly, for every \(k\ge1\), the monoid \(H_{k+1}\) contains a copy of the free left-regular band on \(k\) generators; so does \(H_{k+2}^{\mathrm{ser}}\). Consequently, an identity in finitely many variables holds in **all** the unrestricted monoids, or in **all** the serial monoids, exactly if it follows from the two left-regular-band identities above. In particular, the absence of any further uniform word identities is sharp. The free submonoids contain exactly \(\sum_{j=0}^{k}k!/(k-j)!\) distinct first-occurrence words.

## Assumptions and scope

The mechanism is the factual (propositional) hedged belief contraction of Belardinelli and Zhang [1], not arbitrary modal or epistemic formulas. A Kripke model has a fixed nonempty world set, fixed propositional valuation, and either unconstrained accessibility rows or serial (nonempty) rows. For a factual formula with truth set \(P\), put \(A=W\setminus P\). Its update replaces each selected accessibility row \(S\) by \(C_A(S)\); factual truth sets remain unchanged under later updates. Thus the rowwise transformation calculations govern arbitrary finite sequences of these updates. If the propositional vocabulary distinguishes all worlds, every set \(A\subseteq W\) is definable as a falsity set. Alternatively, the theorem is intrinsically about all set-indexed row transformations. With a restricted valuation or vocabulary, only a submonoid may be realized.

The chronological convention is essential for naming the handedness of the band. If transformations are multiplied instead by standard right-to-left functional composition, the same family satisfies the reversed regular-band law, as expected.

## Proof

**1. Stabilization and band identities.** Each map \(C_A\) is extensive: \(S\subseteq C_A(S)\). At the moment it is applied to a row, either it makes no change because the row already meets \(A\), or it adds all of \(A\). In either case the resulting row meets \(A\), except for the vacuous \(A=\varnothing\), whose map is the identity. Since subsequent maps only add elements, \(C_A\) fixes every future row after its first occurrence. Therefore deleting any subsequent occurrence of the same generator from a chronological update word preserves that word's action on every starting row. In particular every word of generators is idempotent, and appending a repeat of an entire preceding word after an intervening word changes nothing. Hence \(F^2=F\) and \(FGF=FG\) for arbitrary \(F,G\) in either monoid. This reasoning remains valid after excluding the empty row.

**2. The unrestricted generating rank.** The empty content \(A=\varnothing\) induces the identity. Fix any nonempty \(A\subseteq W\) and suppose a word in the canonical maps represents \(C_A\):
\[
C_{B_1}C_{B_2}\cdots C_{B_t}=C_A.
\]
Remove occurrences of \(C_{\varnothing}\). For each \(a\in A\), the desired map fixes the singleton \(\{a\}\). As every factor is extensive, each nontrivial factor must itself fix \(\{a\}\), forcing \(a\in B_i\) for every \(i\). Thus \(B_i\supseteq A\). On the empty input, the first factor produces \(B_1\), and later factors never remove an element. Since \(C_A(\varnothing)=A\), we have \(B_1\subseteq A\), hence \(B_1=A\). Every subsequent \(B_i\) contains \(A\), and therefore the corresponding map is inactive after \(C_A\) operates: the word is already \(C_A\).

Now let \(\mathcal G\) be **any** generating set of \(H_n\), with generators themselves possibly complicated words. Express \(C_A\) as a product of elements of \(\mathcal G\), replace each factor by a canonical-word representative, and discard identity factors. The preceding paragraph shows that the first nonidentity canonical letter is \(C_A\) and all others have content containing \(A\). Therefore the first nonidentity \(\mathcal G\)-factor in this flattened expression already equals \(C_A\). Hence \(C_A\in\mathcal G\). All \(2^n-1\) nonempty \(A\) are indispensable, and they generate the monoid, proving the first rank formula. This also proves that different nonempty contents give different transformations.

**3. The serial generating rank.** On nonempty rows, \(C_{\varnothing}\) and \(C_W\) are identities. Fix a nonempty proper \(A\subsetneq W\). Suppose a canonical word on nonempty rows equals \(C_A\), after deleting these two identity letters. As before, its action on each \(\{a\}\), for \(a\in A\), shows that every remaining \(B_i\) contains \(A\). The first remaining \(B_1\) is proper. If \(B_1\supsetneq A\), select \(x\in W\setminus B_1\). On input \(\{x\}\), this factor adds \(B_1\), including a world of \(B_1\setminus A\) that is absent from \(C_A(\{x\})=\{x\}\cup A\). Later updates cannot delete it. Thus \(B_1=A\). The rest are inactive after \(C_A\). Flattening an expression over an arbitrary generating family as in part 2 shows that every nonempty proper \(C_A\) must itself belong to that family. Conversely these maps generate all nonidentity updates. Their number is \(2^n-2\), proving the serial rank formula, including \(n=1\), when the serial monoid is trivial.

**4. Free submonoids and exact equational theory.** Fix \(k\ge1\). For the unrestricted case take \(W=\{c,p_1,\ldots,p_k\}\); for the serial case add one extra world \(d\). For each index \(i\), set \(A_i=\{c,p_i\}\), and denote the corresponding map by \(E_i\). Any repeated \(E_i\) in a word is redundant by part 1. To distinguish two different repetition-free index words \(u,v\), let \(P\) be their longest common prefix and start from
\[
S_P=\{p_i:i\text{ occurs in }P\}
\]
in the unrestricted case, or \(S_P=\{d\}\cup\{p_i:i\text{ occurs in }P\}\) in the serial case. All prefix updates are inert. If one word ends at the prefix, its final row is \(S_P\). Otherwise its next letter \(i\) adds exactly \(\{c,p_i\}\), and all later letters are inert because every \(A_j\) contains \(c\). If both words continue with different letters, their final rows differ at their respective private markers. Hence different repetition-free words induce different transformations, so the submonoid is precisely the free left-regular band on the \(k\) generators.

In any monoid with \(F^2=F\) and \(FGF=FG\), word identities reduce each word to its ordered list of first occurrences: the relation \(XYX=XY\) removes a later occurrence when \(X\) is a single previously occurring letter and \(Y\) is the intervening word. Thus two words with the same first-occurrence list are universally equal. If their lists differ, the corresponding free band on their finite common alphabet separates them. Part 4 embeds that free band into one of the \(H_n\) and into one of the serial monoids. The two left-regular-band laws therefore form the exact common equational theory, without further uniform identities.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `checker.py` uses bitmask subsets and exhaustive closure of the generated transformation monoids, not symbolic assumptions about the identities. On universes of sizes \(1,2,3\), it checks \(F^2=F\) and \(FGF=FG\) for every pair of monoid elements, and checks that omitting each canonical nonidentity update destroys its representation. It obtains unrestricted sizes \(2,7,97\) and serial sizes \(1,4,45\). It also verifies faithful free-band embeddings for each \(1\le k\le5\), and tests the row update against the stated factual contraction rule on all three-world rows. Execution command: `python3 checker.py`; expected terminal marker: `ALL_CHECKS_OK`. These finite tests corroborate but do not replace the arbitrary-rank proofs above.

## Relationship to prior work

Belardinelli and Zhang [1] define the factual hedged-announcement contraction, its row-expansion semantics, and a dynamic logic for it. Two prior studies [3,4] already establish idempotence of individual factual contractions and their exact **pairwise commutation criterion**, including the counting of commuting pairs. Neither of those studies establishes the complete monoid identities, either exact semigroup generating rank among arbitrary generators, or the free-band embedding. Those results are the present contribution; the earlier one- and two-update laws are credited rather than claimed as new. Brauner, Commins and Reiner [2] study the already-known free left-regular band and its injective-word normal form; the new assertion here is its faithful occurrence inside the factual-contraction transformations. General transformation representations of regular bands may provide alternative derivations of some structural implications, but do not by themselves identify these particular epistemic transformations and exact ranks.

## Limitations

The proof is specific to factual formulas, so it does not cover modal inputs whose truth sets can change after contraction. The serial rank concerns preservation of nonempty rows only; no transitivity, Euclideanness, or introspection assumptions are imposed. The full rank needs the family of all semantically distinct falsity sets \(A\), which may require sufficiently many atoms to distinguish worlds. The findings are equalities of model transformations, not a decision procedure for arbitrary syntactic programs. The exact individual monoid cardinalities for general \(n\) are not determined here. While relevant literature was compared, equivalence with an earlier general transformation-semigroup construction under different vocabulary remains a priority risk.
