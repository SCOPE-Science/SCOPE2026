# Scientific review

## Correctness (C): PASS
For arbitrary integer \(n\ge3\), the proof establishes a forcing lemma for adjacent complete triangle columns, a three-case local obstruction for successive occupied columns at gap smaller than the sum of occupancies, and a cyclic sum yielding \(n\ge2|S|\). The isolated-column case at \(n=3\) is treated separately. An explicit uniform alternating-column stalled configuration attains \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\). Exhaustive independently implemented monitoring tests for \(3\le n\le7\) and further construction/local-obstruction tests corroborate the proof. The finite tests alone cannot certify all \(n\).

## Originality (O): PASS, with residual uncertainty
The relevant full primary-source passages were compared by implication. The 2019 clique-path theorem gives a related but different answer for \(K_3\square P_n\); it cannot be transferred unchanged to cyclic boundaries. The 2023 square-torus theorem covers \(C_n\square C_n\) for \(n\ge4\), not the present constant-width three-row family. The 2024 generalized-Petersen monitoring result concerns cubic graphs rather than this four-regular product. Searches for equivalent cyclic-clique, triangular torus, and failed-power-domination formulations did not reveal an already-published exact formula for this full family, and accessible related mathematical statements did not duplicate it. Negative searches are not proof of priority, and unindexed results remain possible.

## Mathematical value (V): PASS
The family is the natural first thin rectangular torus left outside previously established square-torus results. The theorem gives an exact placement-independent monitoring guarantee across all lengths, and the proof identifies a weighted cyclic sensor-spacing obstruction. The comparison with the path case exhibits an even-length boundary effect. No arbitrary-width rectangular-torus theorem is claimed.
