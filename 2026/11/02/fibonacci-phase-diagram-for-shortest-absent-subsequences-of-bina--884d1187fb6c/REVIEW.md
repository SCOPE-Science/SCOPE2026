# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The lemma that any absent word of length one more than the arch count must greedily select one position in each arch is proved from leftmost matching and the one-letter-per-arch embedding bound. Exact arch factorizations for all run-length regimes are written out. The local early/late transition restrictions and the terminal-tail restrictions prove an explicit bijection of legal type paths to shortest absent words. The Fibonacci recurrence and free-choice regimes then count all such paths. Independent literal enumeration agrees with the full classification for 216 parameter instances, 139,272 candidate targets, and 985 absent words. Risks: the finite calculation is not an infinite proof; no such reliance is made.

**Originality — PASS with residual risk.** Relevant source statements were compared by implication, not just by titles. Kosche et al. (arXiv:2108.13968) supply the general SAS/MAS and arch theorems but not the five-branch symbolic run-power profile in their inspected definitions, theorem, extremal discussion and examples. Schnoebelen–Veron (arXiv:2304.11932) address arch-jump and circular/compressed universality; the inspected introduction and definitions do not state this count. Manea et al. (arXiv:2504.21471) give general-purpose enumeration algorithms, which do not imply a symbolic all-parameter run-block formula without further derivation. published-finding corpus returned a Fibonacci extremal over unrestricted binary hosts, which does not imply the exact count for this specified host family. Risk: an unindexed specialization or an appendix theorem not located here might still cover it.

**Value — PASS.** The invariant is the exact cardinality and complete structure of shortest absent scattered subsequences, a standard object in stringology and subsequence universality. The run-thickness thresholds \(1\), \(2\), and \(3\) induce mathematically distinct growth laws (linear, two, Fibonacci, power-of-two, singleton) for every exponent, and the arch automaton explains each transition. This is more than a finite census, but does not claim a new general enumeration algorithm or a result for arbitrary host words.
