# Review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact \(q\)-adic valuation follows from Legendre’s digit-sum formula. A specific shared one-bit at position \(\nu_2(q-1)\) proves that \(\binom{q^2}{q}\) is even for every odd prime \(q\), ruling out exponent \(t=1\). The two-digit evaluation for \(q>2^e\) forces the uniform bound. An exhaustive exact enumeration of the finite candidate set proves the four stated small-layer classifications. The attached verifier corroborates, but does not replace, the unrestricted argument.

## Originality — PASS, subject to bibliographic risk
Guedes–Machado's June 29, 2026 source treats \(n=q^t p\) and prime squares, but does not provide this fixed-dyadic-layer finiteness for all \(e\). The closest published prime-power-tower summary treats a cofactor prime \(p\ge5\), not the dyadic prime \(2\). OEIS already lists the values \(36\) and \(400\); their numerical occurrence is not a novelty claim. Searches of equivalent and stronger statements did not identify complete coverage; unindexed equivalents cannot be excluded.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The binomial-congruence literature seeks infinite structured solutions when a prime cofactor varies. This theorem shows that the exceptional cofactor \(2\) exhibits a quantitative opposite behavior: if its exponent \(e\) is fixed, the admissible prime base \(q\) and its exponent \(t\) are both bounded, producing an effective finite search over an otherwise infinite two-parameter family. The exact low-layer classification exhibits both empty and nonempty fibers and is supported by a short general carry-and-digit structural proof, not an arbitrary cutoff.

## Limits
No global enumeration is provided for \(e\ge5\), nor for unrelated prime-power cofactors. Source inspection cannot establish absolute bibliographic uniqueness.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
