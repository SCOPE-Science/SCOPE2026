# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof independently classifies the nilpotent cone over each finite field, partitions every nonzero nilpotent first entry into explicit centralizer types, counts the two-dimensional abelian and three-dimensional Heisenberg cases, and solves a valid recurrence with the empty-tuple boundary condition. The standalone checker directly tests \(3125\) derivations in characteristic five and the predicted centralizer sizes and two- and three-tuple counts. The proof is for all extension degrees and tuple lengths; finite testing alone would not establish that scope.

**Originality — PASS, with residual risk.** Yao–Chang (2013), Lemma 2.3, already proves the algebraically closed centralizer stratification and Sections 2–3 establish nilpotent pair-variety geometry: that material is prior, not a new claim. Yao (2015) treats the geometry of higher nilpotent commuting varieties. The reviewed accessible 2015 abstract states component reducibility, their number and dimensions, but not the finite-field point-count series. The complete 2015 text could not be examined, so an equivalent calculation or arithmetically sufficient explicit decomposition remains a residual historical-priority risk. Yao–Zhang (2021) treats the full, not nilpotent-restricted, commuting varieties and similarly advertises geometric component results. An all-arity cardinality law, including the rational generating series and precise first-entry finite-field centralizers, is not a formal consequence of these dimension-only statements.

**Value — PASS.** Restricted-nilpotent commuting tuples are natural in representation theory and the geometry of restricted Lie algebras and Frobenius kernels. Rather than a small arbitrary numerical slice, this gives the complete point-count invariant simultaneously at every tuple length and every finite extension of the prime field for the first simple Witt algebra \(W_1(1)\) beyond the special small-characteristic case. The nonabelian Heisenberg centralizer is essential to the new all-arity formula; merely using the pair-count geometry would not determine it.

The result does not address other characteristics, all commuting (non-nilpotent) tuples, or the geometric structure of the corresponding schemes. No independent validation has been performed.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
