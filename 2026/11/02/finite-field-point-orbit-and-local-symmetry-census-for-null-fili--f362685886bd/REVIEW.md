# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

For the stated right-Leibniz algebra, multiplication is \([x,y]=y_1S(x)\). The generator-image recurrence \(f(e_i)=b_1^{i-1}S^{i-1}(f(e_1))\) is necessary and sufficient for multiplicativity; it gives all automorphisms and a nonzero leading scalar. At any first-nonzero index \(i\), the next output coefficient depends linearly on one new free automorphism parameter, with nonzero coefficient. Consequently the power-coset orbit condition and the full triangular local-automorphism criterion follow over arbitrary fields. Finite-field orbit sizes, stabilizers and group orders follow from the cyclic multiplicative group. The finite checker independently tests the original multiplication, all linear maps and all vectors for six small prime-power cases. Infinite statements are proved by the recurrence rather than inferred from examples.

## Originality — PASS, subject to residual risks

The 2013 arXiv paper concerns deformation and cohomology of the null-filiform algebra, not its vector-orbit and local-automorphism census. Normurodovna (2026), §§4 and 6, proves the complex automorphism formula and the complex lower-triangular local criterion; its real-field remark already signals a power obstruction, so neither these antecedents nor the algebraically closed case are claimed as new. The specific arbitrary-field power-coset equivalence, complete finite-field vector-orbit sizes and stabilizers, arithmetic divisor sum for orbit numbers, and exact finite-field local-automorphism group counts were not established in the full relevant material examined or mathematical-record comparisons. Adashev–Yusupov (2022/2023) is a relevant unresolved source because full text was unavailable; see limitations. Search non-detection alone is not treated as proof of uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

The linear pointwise closure of an algebra automorphism group is a natural orbit-reflexivity invariant. This gives its complete description simultaneously with the orbit partition and stabilizers for a full infinite-dimensional-index family, over every finite field; the distinction from the algebraically closed-field result is arithmetic and varies periodically with dimension. For instance \(\mathbb F_2\) in dimension four has eight actual automorphisms but 64 linear local automorphisms, while \(\mathbb F_3\) in dimension two has no such excess. The formulas address an invariant not determined by the ordinary automorphism-group order or the previously known complex-field classification.
