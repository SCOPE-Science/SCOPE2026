# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS

The microscopic transition odds are recomputed with distinct sampling, match the product measure by exact local detailed balance, and yield the labeled count law after multinomial aggregation. The finite one-valley ratio argument reduces *all* global modes to at least one balanced-remainder leader; convexity of the derivative of the log weights and majorization prove monotonicity of the largest leading candidate. The six-voter candidate polynomials give exactly two positive crossings, with strict domination of the intervening profiles. The independent full-state checker executed 16,615 exact deterministic assertions; it supplements rather than constitutes a proof for arbitrary population size. Boundary \(p=0\), orbit aggregation and other panel sizes are excluded.

## Originality: PASS, with historical risk

Nowak–Stoń–Sznajd-Weron, arXiv:2012.11725 (2020), define the identical multi-opinion annealed model but analyze the infinite-population mean-field equation and its deterministic phase portrait rather than the finite stationary measure and global discrete count modes. Herrerías-Azcué–Galla, arXiv:1903.09198 (2019), give exact *one-opinion marginals* for linear imitation with mutation, not the nonlinear unanimity-two joint count law. Peralta et al., arXiv:1803.06861 (2018), derive exact all-to-all results for a binary nonlinear noisy-voter process; Mobilia, arXiv:1506.04911 (2015), has a binary zealot mechanism. The two-opinion birth-death and continuum fixed-point results do not entail the finite three-opinion, no-reentrance classification. Product-form detailed balance is a standard technique, and no methodological novelty is attributed to it. Searches of related mathematical results and accessible prior scientific results disclosed no stronger covering assertion; this is not a guarantee of complete historical priority. A source with the same finite three-opinion stationary maximum law could change this assessment.

## Value: PASS

The distinction between a mode of the *actual finite invariant distribution* and a mean-field fixed point is mathematically substantial near the smallest finite populations. The all-population leader reduction and no-reentrance result turn a high-dimensional search over count compositions into a one-parameter optimization and prohibit reentrant modal leader sizes. The six-voter three-opinion model exhibits an exact, natural nontrivial two-jump staircase with skipped intermediate shapes and radical critical values. This supplies a useful finite-state counterpart to the known discontinuous large-population behavior, without claiming a large-population theorem or a general panel-size classification.
