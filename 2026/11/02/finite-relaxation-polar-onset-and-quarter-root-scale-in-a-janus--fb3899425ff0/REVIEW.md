# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is for one explicitly defined linear surface–bulk block with positive feedback and zero translational mobility, not the full nonlinear active-colloid system. The positive self-adjoint weighted realization follows from the matching polar-to-bulk and bulk-to-polar boundary couplings; the boundary form has its coefficients independently checked against the motivating article's linearized first-harmonic and solute equations. The Robin–Dirichlet resolvent independently supplies the sign and threshold, and monotonicity of the explicitly derived stationary-point map proves uniqueness for *every* positive parameter choice. The small-rotation singular asymptotic follows from the exact stationary equation, not a finite grid. A standalone numerical cross-check compares finite-volume bulk spectra and direct characteristic roots; finite calculations are not claimed as infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified best-knowledge)

The closest primary source is Mabillard, van Wijland and Fournier (arXiv:2610.26657v1), which derives the underlying physical model and *quasistatic* solute transfer. Its quasistatic polar neutral curve is already implicit in its displayed matrix and is explicitly credited, so only the finite-relaxation spectral theorem, growth-rate comparison, exact single-band classification and singular scale characterization are assessed. Tucci, Golestanian and Saha (arXiv:2402.09279v1) already develop single-species polarization instabilities and mixed-species oscillatory regimes in a different screened-chemical model; these are also credited. Liebchen and collaborators (arXiv:1508.04673v1) already model a fully dynamic chemical field with Janus anisotropy and delayed-orientation oscillations, but use 2D volumetric self-propulsion and chemical degradation rather than a consuming 3D boundary slab; this is explicitly credited as well. Relevant published research records were queried for aliases for the same mathematical model, finite diffusion, Robin slab response, Hopf and wavenumber coverage, with no direct dominating result identified. This is a best-knowledge literature comparison, not proof of global priority. Remaining risks include an equivalent general rank-one parabolic boundary-feedback theorem and older unpublished/less searchable chemical-mode analyses.

## Value — PASS

The distinction between the quasistatic solute approximation and a dynamically diffusing 3D solute is central to assessing whether chemical memory can destabilize oscillatory modes. The selfadjoint structure excludes that mechanism *throughout the stated sector*, and the exact unique finite-wave band, strict dynamic growth-rate suppression, and a quarter-root low-rotation law with slab-dependent coefficient provide sharp measurable scales and a control boundary, not a generic linearization exercise. The zero-drift restriction is explicit and the result is not generalized to the active self-propelled case.

## Scientific limits and independent checking

The proof treats only a first-order dipolar source, fixed positive diffusion and slab height, and the zero-translational-mobility sector. The stability of finite-amplitude nonlinear patterns and global publication priority have not been established. Published quasistatic results and the existing single-species polar mechanism are not attributed as new.
