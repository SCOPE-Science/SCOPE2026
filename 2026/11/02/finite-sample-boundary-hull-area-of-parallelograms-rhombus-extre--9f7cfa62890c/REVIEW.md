# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** A general arc-cap identity integrates consecutive sample-point gaps exactly, and a four-edge determinant evaluation yields the closed finite-sample formula without neglecting configurations where edges receive no sampled points. The affine reduction preserves Euclidean arclength on each edge and normalized area. Strict convexity of the explicit two-uniform kernel proves the shape ordering and its equality cases for every integer \(n\ge3\). Dominated convergence gives the thin-shape scaling, while direct exponential comparison gives the fixed-shape scaling. Twenty-eight rational exact-integral comparisons, convex-hull simulations with nonorthogonal shears, and numerical boundary-layer tests independently corroborate the proof. Finite checks are not substituted for universal proof.

**Originality — PASS, provisionally.** The closest inspected direct primary reference is Reitzner–Schütt–Werner, arXiv:1911.05917 (2019), especially Theorem 1.2 and its asymptotic volume-deficit treatment: it addresses the same sampling model but proves asymptotic orders rather than this exact finite sample formula and sharp side-ratio ordering. Prochno–Schütt–Sonnleitner–Werner, arXiv:2404.02870 (2024), concerns polygonal boundary-sampling in Hausdorff distance rather than area. A prior polygonal edge-allocation result already implies the fixed positive-side-ratio leading coefficient \(8/[u(1-u)]\), and includes a finite-sample *approximation*, so that leading term is not presented as new. The nonuniform thin-side crossover and exact finite-sample strict extremality are not implied by results whose assumptions fix all positive edge weights. Mathematical-record and literature searches found no direct exact equivalent, but this is not proof of historical priority. Broad older convex-hull integral identities are a residual priority risk.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The shape-independent angle reduction and all-\(n\) exact formula solve a natural finite-sampling comparison within the fundamental family of parallelograms. Strict rhombus extremality and exact aspect-ratio identifiability are meaningful finite-sample consequences. The collapsing-side \(n^{-1}\) limit explains a genuine change in sampling approximation rate missed by fixed-shape asymptotics, not an arbitrary parameter substitution.
