# Finite-support filters are exactly the universal Fubini commuters

## Finding

Let \(X\) be any nonempty set and \(\mathcal F\) any proper filter on \(X\). For any nonempty set \(Y\), proper filter \(\mathcal G\) on \(Y\), and relation \(R\subseteq X\times Y\), define the vertical and horizontal sections
\[
R_x=\{y\in Y:(x,y)\in R\},\qquad R^y=\{x\in X:(x,y)\in R\}.
\]
Write \(\mathsf L_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G}(R)\) for the statement
\[
\{x\in X:R_x\in\mathcal G\}\in\mathcal F,
\]
and \(\mathsf R_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G}(R)\) for
\[
\{y\in Y:R^y\in\mathcal F\}\in\mathcal G.
\]
In ZFC, the following conditions on \(\mathcal F\) are equivalent.

1. For **every** nonempty \(Y\), **every** proper filter \(\mathcal G\) on \(Y\), and **every** \(R\subseteq X\times Y\), the statements \(\mathsf L_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G}(R)\) and \(\mathsf R_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G}(R)\) are equivalent.
2. The same equivalence holds when the partner \(\mathcal G\) is required merely to range over **ultrafilters** on nonempty carrier sets \(Y\).
3. The filter \(\mathcal F\) contains a nonempty finite set.
4. There is a nonempty finite \(H\subseteq X\) such that
   \[
   \mathcal F=\{A\subseteq X:H\subseteq A\}.
   \]

Moreover, whenever condition 3 fails, one can select an ultrafilter \(\mathcal G\) and construct \(R\) explicitly (apart from the ultrafilter extension) so that **exactly one** of the two Fubini orders accepts \(R\). Thus testing only ultrafilter partners detects every failure of universal commutation, even when \(\mathcal F\) itself is not an ultrafilter.

In the semantics of filter-valued generalized quantifiers, the theorem characterizes precisely those quantifiers whose two iterated scope readings commute with **every** other filter quantifier, allowing the two quantifier domains to differ. It equally characterizes local normal-neighborhood filters whose two coordinate box modalities commute with every possible filter-valued partner on all binary valuations.

## Assumptions and scope

A **proper filter** is a family of subsets of a nonempty carrier that contains the carrier, omits the empty set, and is closed under finite intersections and supersets. An **ultrafilter** is a maximal proper filter. The quantified partner \(Y\) may be of any cardinality; no uniform bound on its size is imposed. The underlying set theory is ZFC. In particular, the converse uses the ultrafilter lemma, a consequence of choice, and is not asserted to hold in ZF alone.

A filter is **principal at** \(H\) when its members are exactly the supersets of \(H\). The finite-support condition is not equivalent to being an ultrafilter: a finite kernel of size greater than one defines a proper, non-ultra principal filter that still universally commutes. The theorem is deliberately stronger than a statement about self-commutation or a fixed second filter.

## Proof

**Step 1: finite membership and finite principality.** Suppose a proper filter \(\mathcal F\) contains a finite set \(A\). Consider its trace on \(A\): the family \(\{A\cap B:B\in\mathcal F\}\). This is a finite family of nonempty sets, closed under intersections. Its intersection \(H\) is therefore a nonempty member of \(\mathcal F\), and \(H\subseteq B\) for every \(B\in\mathcal F\). Upward closure then gives
\[
\mathcal F=\{B\subseteq X:H\subseteq B\}.
\]
Conversely any filter generated by nonempty finite \(H\) contains \(H\) itself. This proves equivalence of 3 and 4.

**Step 2: finite kernel implies every-partner commutation.** Fix a finite nonempty generator \(H\). The left Fubini assertion is true precisely when
\[
R_h\in\mathcal G\qquad\text{for every }h\in H.
\]
By closure under finite intersections, this is equivalent to
\[
\bigcap_{h\in H}R_h\in\mathcal G.
\]
For every \(y\in Y\), we have \(R^y\in\mathcal F\) exactly when \(H\subseteq R^y\), or equivalently \(y\in\bigcap_{h\in H}R_h\). Thus the intersection is precisely \(\{y:R^y\in\mathcal F\}\). This is the right Fubini assertion. Hence 4 implies 1, and 1 trivially implies 2.

**Step 3: filters with no least member fail against an ultrafilter.** Suppose \(\mathcal F\) is not principal, and define its (possibly empty) kernel
\[
K=\bigcap_{A\in\mathcal F}A.
\]
Because \(\mathcal F\) is not principal, \(K\notin\mathcal F\). Use the set \(Y=\mathcal F\) itself as the partner carrier. For every \(x\in X\setminus K\), let
\[
D_x=\{A\in\mathcal F:x\notin A\}\subseteq Y.
\]
The family \(\{D_x:x\in X\setminus K\}\) has the finite-intersection property: for finitely many such points \(x_1,\ldots,x_t\), choose \(A_i\in\mathcal F\) omitting \(x_i\), and observe that \(\bigcap_i A_i\in\mathcal F\) belongs to every \(D_{x_i}\). The empty finite family is also harmless because \(Y\ne\varnothing\). By the ultrafilter lemma, this family is included in an ultrafilter \(\mathcal G\) on \(Y\).

Define the membership-incidence relation
\[
R=\{(x,A)\in X\times\mathcal F:x\in A\}.
\]
Its column at each \(A\in Y\) is \(R^A=A\in\mathcal F\). Therefore \(\{A:R^A\in\mathcal F\}=Y\in\mathcal G\), making the **right** assertion true. For \(x\in K\) its row is all of \(Y\) and belongs to \(\mathcal G\). For \(x\notin K\) the complement of its row is \(D_x\in\mathcal G\), so its row does not belong to the proper ultrafilter \(\mathcal G\). Hence
\[
\{x:R_x\in\mathcal G\}=K\notin\mathcal F,
\]
which makes the **left** assertion false. Universal ultrafilter-partner commutation is impossible.

**Step 4: infinitely generated principal filters also fail against an ultrafilter.** Suppose \(\mathcal F\) is principal at an **infinite** nonempty set \(H\). Let \(Y=H\), and extend the cofinite filter on \(H\) to a nonprincipal ultrafilter \(\mathcal G\) on \(Y\). Put
\[
R=\{(x,y)\in X\times H:x\in H\text{ and }x\ne y\}.
\]
For each \(x\in H\), its row is \(H\setminus\{x\}\in\mathcal G\); for \(x\notin H\), its row is empty. Thus \(\{x:R_x\in\mathcal G\}=H\in\mathcal F\), making the **left** assertion true. For each \(y\in H\), the column \(R^y=H\setminus\{y\}\) fails to contain all of \(H\) and so is not in \(\mathcal F\). The set of \(\mathcal F\)-large columns is empty, hence not in \(\mathcal G\). The **right** assertion is false.

**Conclusion.** A proper filter either is not principal (Step 3), or is principal at a finite or infinite generator (Steps 2 and 4). Condition 2 excludes both failure cases, leaving precisely a nonempty finite generator, as claimed.

## Verification

A standalone standard-library checker enumerates every proper filter on carriers of cardinalities one through three and every binary relation for each ordered pair of such carriers. It evaluates both iterated Fubini predicates directly, covering 121 pairs of filters and 28,058 relations; all comparisons agreed. This corroborates the finite-support direction. It cannot test the infinite obstructions, for which the set-theoretic constructions in Steps 3 and 4 are the proof. No finite calculation is used to infer an infinite universal statement.

## Relationship to prior work

Engström's study of generalized-quantifier scope dominance develops branching-product criteria and shows, in Proposition 4.5, that a principal outer quantifier with generator \(A\) dominates a fixed partner precisely when the partner is closed under intersections indexed by \(A\). Thus finite-generator sufficiency and the infinite principal-generator obstruction have substantial antecedents. The present claim is the **complete universal-partner equivalence for arbitrary proper filters**, including those with no least member, together with the assertion that *ultrafilter partners alone* detect all non-universal filters. It uses partner domains of arbitrary cardinality, rather than requiring a shared fixed domain.

Goldberg investigates commutation and Cartesian products of pairs of **ultrafilters**; his theory does not itself classify all proper filters against every ultrafilter partner. Aghamov, Kudinov, Nguyen, and Piribauer develop neighborhood full-product modalities and their interaction. A separate published mathematical analysis of those semantics gives pointwise rectangularization and finite-factor collapse; that result concerns a **specified pair** and the rectangular product modality, not the present all-partner, two-iteration classification. Altman, Peterzil, and Winter establish a countable fixed-domain classification, including Corollary 8 on scope independence. Their criterion already covers part of the finite-generator phenomenon in the countable same-domain setting; the claim here is limited to arbitrary cardinalities and arbitrary (possibly different) partner carriers, with a direct ultrafilter witness construction for every nonprincipal filter. No novelty is claimed for the countable special cases already implied by their classification.

## Limitations

The universal quantification over partner carriers and partner filters is essential. Nonprincipal or infinite principal filters may commute with particular partners, including principal-ultrafilter partners. The theorem does not classify all commuting pairs or settle other cardinal-sensitive cases in scope dominance. The ultrafilter witnesses use a choice principle, so no claim is made about models of ZF without the ultrafilter lemma. The finite checker is only a corroboration of the finite special case.

Historical priority remains uncertain: terminology from older work on product filters, commuting generalized quantifiers, or Fubini products may encode an equivalent all-partner characterization. The 2005 countable-domain independence classification supplies substantive partial antecedents; extension to arbitrary carrier cardinalities and the universal ultrafilter-witness criterion are the proposed new structural aspects. Independence from all older all-cardinality formulations is not certified.
