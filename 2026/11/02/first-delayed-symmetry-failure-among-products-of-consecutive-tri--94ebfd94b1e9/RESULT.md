# First delayed symmetry failure among products of consecutive-triple numerical semigroups

## Finding

Let \(m\geq2\) be an integer, and let \(S_m=\langle m,m+1,m+2\rangle\) be the additive numerical semigroup generated by three consecutive positive integers. Write \(S_m^k\) for its **multiplicative ideal power** in the semiring of nonnegative integers, not for a Minkowski sum. Let \(t(T)\) denote the number of pseudo-Frobenius numbers of a numerical semigroup \(T\). Then

\[
\min\{m\geq2:t(S_m)=t(S_m^2)=1,\ t(S_m^3)>1\}=8.
\]

More precisely, for \(S=\langle8,9,10\rangle\), the types of its first three multiplicative powers are
\[
(t(S),t(S^2),t(S^3))=(1,1,7).
\]
Their respective Frobenius numbers and genera are
\[
(F(S),F(S^2),F(S^3))=(31,519,5791),
\qquad
(g(S),g(S^2),g(S^3))=(16,260,3072).
\]
The exact pseudo-Frobenius sets are
\[
\mathrm{PF}(S)=\{31\},\quad
\mathrm{PF}(S^2)=\{519\},\quad
\mathrm{PF}(S^3)=\{4023,4095,5223,5431,5503,5567,5791\}.
\]
In particular, both \(S\) and \(S^2\) are symmetric, but \(S^3\) is not. Thus symmetry at the first two powers does not certify symmetry at all subsequent multiplicative ideal powers, even in the simplest consecutive-triple family.

## Assumptions and scope

An additive numerical semigroup is a cofinite subset of \(\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\) that contains zero and is closed under addition. For two such semigroups \(A,B\), their ideal product \(AB\) is the additive semigroup generated by all products \(ab\) with \(a\in A\) and \(b\in B\). This is not the ordinary sumset operation. The product of ideals generated by sets of integers is generated by all pairwise generator products.

For a numerical semigroup \(T\), its Frobenius number \(F(T)\) is the largest positive integer absent from \(T\), and its genus \(g(T)\) is the number of positive integers absent from \(T\). A pseudo-Frobenius number is a gap \(x\notin T\) for which \(x+s\in T\) for every \(s\in T\setminus\{0\}\). A nontrivial numerical semigroup is symmetric exactly when \(t(T)=1\), equivalently \(2g(T)=F(T)+1\). All powers in this finding are \(k\in\{1,2,3\}\), and the minimality statement ranges over all integers \(m\geq2\).

## Proof

**1. Finite product presentation.** By distributivity, if \(S_m\) is generated by \(m,m+1,m+2\), then for every positive integer \(k\), its multiplicative ideal power is additively generated by
\[
H_{m,k}=\{m^i(m+1)^j(m+2)^{k-i-j}:i,j\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0},\ i+j\leq k\}.
\]
In particular, its smallest positive generator is \(q=m^k\). For \(m=8\), the generator lists are
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_{8,1}&=\{8,9,10\},\\
H_{8,2}&=\{64,72,80,81,90,100\},\\
H_{8,3}&=\{512,576,640,648,720,729,800,810,900,1000\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

**2. Exact finite membership certificate.** For each finite list \(H=H_{m,k}\), put \(R_H(0)=1\) and for each positive integer \(n\) set
\[
R_H(n)=\max\{R_H(n-h):h\in H,\ h\leq n\},
\]
where the maximum over an empty set is zero. By strong induction on \(n\), \(R_H(n)=1\) if and only if \(n\) is an additive nonnegative-integer combination of elements of \(H\). The supplied exact-arithmetic verifier and its complete Apéry certificates check each finite case with no approximate arithmetic.

For each tested \((m,k)\), the certificate gives a proposed largest gap \(f\), verifies \(R_H(f)=0\), and checks \(R_H(f+j)=1\) for **every** \(1\leq j\leq q\). Because \(q\in H\), adding \(q\) inductively shows that every integer greater than \(f\) belongs to the semigroup. Thus \(f\) is the true Frobenius number, not an unproved enumeration cutoff. The entire check requires only integer indices at most \(f+\max(H)+q\).

Finally, the same finite recurrence checks every missing positive integer \(x\leq f\) against every \(h\in H\): the necessary and sufficient condition for \(x\) to be pseudo-Frobenius is \(R_H(x+h)=1\) for all generators \(h\). Necessity is immediate; sufficiency follows because every nonzero element of the semigroup is a sum of positive generators. Therefore the complete pseudo-Frobenius set is found by this **exhaustive** finite test, with the infinite tail already ruled out.

For \(m=8\), the membership test yields the three Frobenius numbers and the seven pseudo-Frobenius numbers displayed in the finding. Counting all missing integers up to each proved Frobenius bound gives the genera \(16,260,3072\). In particular, \(2g(S^2)=520=F(S^2)+1\), while \(2g(S^3)=6144>5792=F(S^3)+1\). This independently distinguishes the symmetric square from the nonsymmetric cube.

**3. Minimality within the consecutive-triple family.** The same exhaustive certificate gives the following decisive smaller cases:

| \(m\) | Complete relevant pseudo-Frobenius sets | Reason no delayed failure occurs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | \(\mathrm{PF}(S_2)=\{1\}\), \(\mathrm{PF}(S_2^2)=\{11\}\), \(\mathrm{PF}(S_2^3)=\{49\}\) | All three powers symmetric |
| 3 | \(\mathrm{PF}(S_3)=\{1,2\}\) | First power nonsymmetric |
| 4 | \(\mathrm{PF}(S_4)=\{7\}\), \(\mathrm{PF}(S_4^2)=\{83\}\), \(\mathrm{PF}(S_4^3)=\{607\}\) | Third power still symmetric |
| 5 | \(\mathrm{PF}(S_5)=\{8,9\}\) | First power nonsymmetric |
| 6 | \(\mathrm{PF}(S_6)=\{17\}\), \(\mathrm{PF}(S_6^2)=\{207,215\}\) | Second power nonsymmetric |
| 7 | \(\mathrm{PF}(S_7)=\{19,20\}\) | First power nonsymmetric |

No smaller \(m\geq2\) satisfies the required symmetry profile. Since \(m=8\) does, the minimum is exactly eight.

## Verification

The artifact `verify.py` is a self-contained Python 3 standard-library program using the accompanying `certificates.json`. It recomputes every finite case by **two separately implemented algorithms**: (i) the exact bounded membership recurrence just proved and (ii) Dijkstra shortest paths for least semigroup representatives in each residue class modulo \(q=m^k\). For the second method it verifies the standard facts \(F=\max(\mathrm{Ap})-q\), \(g=\sum\mathrm{Ap}/q-(q-1)/2\), and that the maximal Apéry representatives under the semigroup order give the pseudo-Frobenius set. It compares every supplied Apéry representative, every genus and Frobenius number, and every pseudo-Frobenius set with its independently recomputed counterpart.

Execute both files in the same directory with `python3 verify.py`. The checked output is:

`VERIFY_OK independent_dp=yes independent_dijkstra=yes complete_cases=14 first_delayed_m=8 type_profile=1,1,7 F_profile=31,519,5791 genus_profile=16,260,3072`

The finiteness of the computation is proved in Step 2; a mere scan to an arbitrary numerical cutoff would not suffice. No infinite-parametric classification of all consecutive-triple powers is claimed.

## Relationship to prior work

Chen, Hyde, Laurens, Piermarini and Simons, *The Arithmetic of Semirings Part I: Ideals* (arXiv:2607.10778v1, first public July 12, 2026), Section 4.1, introduces symmetry/type questions for multiplicative powers of numerical semigroups. It proves symmetry at every power for two-generated numerical semigroups (Corollary 4.3), gives a symmetric three-generated example with a nonsymmetric **square**, and provides a type-growth formula under a specialized decomposition hypothesis (Proposition 4.7). None of those statements asserts that the first **two** powers can remain symmetric while the third loses symmetry, nor gives this smallest consecutive-triple example or its exact pseudo-Frobenius sets. Its two-generator results do not apply because \(\langle8,9,10\rangle\) has three minimal generators. The special hypothesis of Proposition 4.7 is not verified here and its formula does not yield this type profile.

Earlier numerical-semigroup literature gives the standard Apéry method and symmetry criteria. Fel's 2016 study of *separately powered generators* \(\langle(m-1)^k,m^k,(m+1)^k\rangle\) investigates a different construction; it does not include the mixed monomials in a multiplicative ideal power. Tringali and Wen's 2026 *power semigroup* studies subsets under additive sumset, also a different operation. Relevant mathematical-record searches found no precise matching prior statement; this is a best-knowledge priority assessment rather than independent certification.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns the natural one-parameter consecutive-triple family and only its first three ideal powers, with a first-occurrence result in that family. It does not classify arbitrary generating triples, determine all later type values, prove the existence of arbitrarily long symmetry-preserving prefixes, or quantify growth as the power tends to infinity. The finite arithmetic proof is exhaustive for its specified finite cases, but independent historical-priority review has not been conducted; a broadly phrased older theorem might imply the observation.
