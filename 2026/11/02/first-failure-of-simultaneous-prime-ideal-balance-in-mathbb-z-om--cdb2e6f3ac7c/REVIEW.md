The local criterion of almost-uniform distribution modulo every prime-ideal power is correctly used for all n+1 points. For a hypothetical six-point n=5 optimal set, all pair differences must avoid primes with norm>=6 and squares of the two primes of norms 3 and 4, forcing norm in {1,3,4,12} by unique factorization. Translation fixes zero and confines five nonzero points to the exact 24 Eisenstein neighbors; 4N=(2a-b)^2+3b^2 bounds coordinates, so enumerating all C(24,5)=42504 subsets is complete. Independent recomputation found 54 pairwise norm-compatible rooted choices with three profile types: 12 have 3-residue (2,2,2) but 4-residue (1,1,1,3); 36 have (1,2,3) and (1,1,2,2); six have (0,3,3) and (0,2,2,2). None balances both. Four explicit witnesses have all necessary small-prime profiles and pairwise norm restriction, hence are balanced for every larger prime-power modulus. A six-point 5-universal set would be 5-optimal; published Byszewski–Frączyk–Szumowicz n+2 upper bound gives seven. The latter external theorem is credited, not independently proved.
