# First-radical-layer isomorphism classes in a finite-field matrix order

## Finding

Fix a prime power \(q\) and an integer \(d\geq2\). Put \(k=\mathbb F_q\), \(L=\mathbb F_{q^d}\), \(O=k[[\pi]]\), and \(F=k((\pi))\). Embed \(L\) in \(M_d(k)\) through a regular representation, chosen so that \(k^{1\times d}\) is a one-dimensional right \(L\)-space. Let
\[
 R=L+\pi M_d(O)\subseteq M_d(O),\qquad S=O^{1\times d}
\]
with the ordinary right matrix action, and consider the **first radical layer**
\[
 \mathscr L=\{N\leq S\oplus S:\ \pi(S\oplus S)\subseteq N\subseteq S\oplus S\}.
\]
Submodules are counted as distinct embedded submodules, while isomorphisms are right \(R\)-module isomorphisms, not necessarily induced by a prescribed automorphism of \(S\oplus S\).

Set \(\varepsilon=1\) when \(d\) is even and \(\varepsilon=0\) otherwise, and set
\[
 H=q(q^2-1),\qquad T=\frac{q^d-q-\varepsilon(q^2-q)}{H}.
\]
Then \(T\) is a nonnegative integer, and \(\mathscr L\) has exactly \(q^d+3\) members forming **exactly**
\[
 \boxed{\ 1+\varepsilon+T\ }
\]
right \(R\)-module isomorphism classes. Their complete multiplicity profile is:

1. Precisely \(q+3\) members are isomorphic to \(S\oplus S\).
2. If \(d\) is even, exactly one further class consists of \(q^2-q\) members, all associated with elements of exact degree two over \(k\).
3. There are precisely \(T\) further classes, each containing exactly \(H=q(q^2-1)\) members. Their representatives are indexed by orbits of \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(k)\) on nonrational elements of \(L\) whose degree over \(k\) is at least three.

These classes are pairwise distinct, and the three descriptions exhaust the layer. In particular, exactly two isomorphism types occur for \(d=2\) or \(3\); more than two occur for every \(d\geq4\). For fixed \(q\), the class count grows on the order of \(q^{d-3}\) as \(d\to\infty\).

## Assumptions and scope

All rings are unital and modules are unital right modules. The finite field \(L\) is embedded as a constant matrix subalgebra, and \(\pi\) is central. The notation \(\pi S\) means coordinatewise multiplication by \(\pi\). There is **no** identification of a point of \(\mathbb P^1(L)\) with its Frobenius conjugates: these are distinct embedded submodules, though some can become isomorphic by a fractional linear transformation over \(k\). The result classifies only the indicated first layer, not every submodule of \(S^2\).

## Proof

**1. Every intermediate module comes from an \(L\)-subspace.** The ideal \(\pi M_d(O)\) annihilates \(S/\pi S\), and the image of \(R\) on that quotient is \(L\). Since \(S/\pi S\) is one-dimensional over \(L\), the reduction map identifies \(\mathscr L\), without repetitions, with all \(L\)-linear subspaces of \(L^2\). These comprise zero, \(L^2\), and the \(q^d+1\) one-dimensional subspaces. Hence \(|\mathscr L|=q^d+3\).

Write \(N_t\) for the inverse image of \(W_t=L(1,t)\), for \(t\in L\); put \(W_\infty=L(0,1)\) and define \(N_\infty\) analogously. The endpoints are \(\pi S^2\) and \(S^2\). Every \(N_t\) has index \(q^d\) in \(S^2\) as an additive subgroup.

**2. The rational type is the free type.** For \(t\in k\cup\{\infty\}\), an element of \(\mathrm{GL}_2(k)\) sends \(W_t\) to \(L(1,0)\), and its constant lift induces an \(R\)-automorphism of \(S^2\). The inverse image of \(L(1,0)\) is \(S\oplus\pi S\cong S\oplus S\); also \(\pi S^2\cong S^2\). Thus exactly these \(q+3\) displayed modules have the free type, provided that no nonrational line module is free, as shown next.

For \(t\notin k\), an \(R\)-map \(S\to S^2\) must have the form \(s\mapsto(as,bs)\) with \(a,b\in O\). Indeed, \(\operatorname{End}_R(S)=O\): any endomorphism commutes with \(\pi M_d(O)\) and therefore with every matrix unit after cancellation of \(\pi\), hence is scalar. Its image lies in \(N_t\) precisely when \((\bar a,\bar b)\in W_t\cap k^2\). But \(W_t\cap k^2=0\) since \(t\notin k\). Therefore the sum of the images of all maps \(S\to N_t\), the \(S\)-trace of \(N_t\), is exactly \(\pi S^2\), strictly smaller than \(N_t\). By contrast \(S^2\) is generated by the images of its coordinate copies of \(S\). Hence \(N_t\not\cong S^2\) for every \(t\notin k\).

**3. Isomorphism of nonrational modules is projective equivalence.** Suppose \(t,u\in L\setminus k\) and \(f:N_t\to N_u\) is an \(R\)-isomorphism. Tensor with \(F\): since \(R\otimes_O F=M_d(F)\), the centralizer of this full matrix algebra on \((F^{1\times d})^2\) is \(M_2(F)\). Therefore \(f\) is restriction of a unique invertible scalar \(2\)-by-\(2\) matrix \(C\in\mathrm{GL}_2(F)\), acting on the two summands.

We claim \(C\in M_2(O)\). If an output-coordinate linear form \(a x+b y\) has a coefficient of negative \(\pi\)-valuation, multiply it by the smallest power \(\pi^h\) clearing the poles, with \(h>0\). Its leading coefficient pair \((\alpha,\beta)\in k^2\) is nonzero. For \((x,y)\in N_t\) with any prescribed reduction \((z,zt)\in W_t\), the requirement \(a x+b y\in S\) forces \((\alpha+\beta t)z=0\) in \(L\). Thus \(\alpha+\beta t=0\), impossible for a nonzero \((\alpha,\beta)\) when \(t\notin k\). This contradiction proves integrality.

As \(C(N_t)=N_u\), compare the equal indices \([S^2:N_t]=[S^2:N_u]=q^d\). Since \(C\) is integral, its ambient index is \([S^2:C(S^2)]=q^{d v_\pi(\det C)}\). The equality
\[
 [S^2:N_u]=[S^2:C(S^2)]\,[C(S^2):C(N_t)]
 =q^{d v_\pi(\det C)}[S^2:N_t]
\]
forces \(v_\pi(\det C)=0\), so \(C\in\mathrm{GL}_2(O)\). Reduction gives an element of \(\mathrm{GL}_2(k)\) carrying \(W_t\) onto \(W_u\). Conversely every such element lifts as a constant invertible matrix and induces an isomorphism of the corresponding modules. Consequently
\[
 \boxed{\ N_t\cong_R N_u\ \Longleftrightarrow\ W_t,W_u\text{ lie in the same }\mathrm{PGL}_2(k)\text{-orbit}\ }
 \quad(t,u\notin k).
\]

**4. All projective orbit sizes.** The group \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(k)\) has order \(H=q(q^2-1)\). It acts transitively on the \(q+1\) rational points. Every point of exact degree two belongs to \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\setminus k\), which lies in \(L\) exactly when \(2\mid d\). A degree-two point has projective stabilizer of order \(q+1\): writing its irreducible quadratic relation, the projective matrices fixing it correspond to \(k\)-scalar classes of nonzero elements of \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\), so the orbit has size \(H/(q+1)=q^2-q\). This accounts for all such points.

A projective matrix \(\begin{psmallmatrix}a&b\\c&e\end{psmallmatrix}\) fixing any nonrational affine point \(t\) satisfies \(ct^2+(a-e)t-b=0\), up to the choice of row/column convention. If \([k(t):k]\geq3\), this degree-at-most-two polynomial can vanish only when the matrix is scalar. Thus its stabilizer in \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(k)\) is trivial and its orbit has size \(H\). There are exactly \(q^d-q-\varepsilon(q^2-q)\) such points, yielding exactly \(T\) regular orbits. Including the rational class and (when relevant) the quadratic class gives the claimed formula and multiplicity profile.

Finally \(d=2,3\) yield \(T=0,1\), respectively, while for \(d\geq4\) the regular-orbit count is greater than one. Its leading term is \(q^d/H\), proving the stated growth and completing the proof.

## Verification

The companion `verify.py` explicitly constructs finite extensions of the prime fields \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\), enumerates every fractional-linear orbit on \(\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_{p^d})\), and checks both orbit sizes and the total number of classes. Run with `python3 verify.py` using standard Python 3 only.

Checked pairs \((p,d)\) are \((2,2),(2,3),(2,4),(2,5),(3,2),(3,3),(3,4)\). The respective class counts are \(2,2,4,6,2,2,5\), with all predicted orbit multiplicities. The script prints `VERIFY_OK` if all assertions pass. These finite tests validate the projective orbit arithmetic in seven examples; the module-isomorphism classification and the arbitrary-prime-power theorem follow from the proof, not from exhaustive tests.

## Relationship to prior work

Yan and Zhang, *On the square of an isosimple module* (arXiv:2610.26905v1, 2026-10-17), Theorem 3.1, construct exactly this matrix order and isosimple module and prove that its square is not isoartinian. Their proof exhibits one nonfree intermediate module and an alternating descending chain. The present result takes that existing obstruction as the motivation and classifies **all** isomorphism types in its first radical layer, with exact multiplicities. The construction, the endomorphism ring, and the existence of the obstruction are not claimed as new.

The finite-field fractional-linear action and its classical small-degree orbit behavior are standard. Howe, *Enumerating places of \(\mathbb P^1\) up to automorphisms of \(\mathbb P^1\) in quasilinear time* (arXiv:2407.05534, 2024), studies \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(k)\)-orbits on closed places and divisors, identifying the unique degree-two place orbit. Closed places identify Frobenius conjugates, whereas the modules here are indexed by **individual points**, and module-isomorphism equivalence first requires a separate integral-lifting argument. The projective orbit counts alone are not claimed as new; the ring-module classification is the contribution.

## Limitations

The theorem treats the finite-field residue extension and the first radical layer only. It does not classify deeper levels \(\pi^m S^2\subset N\subseteq S^2\) for \(m\geq2\), general noncommutative orders, or all submodules of \(S^2\). No independent mathematical review is claimed. Equivalent module-lattice classifications under older terminology for orders and integral representations may exist; the historical-priority question is not fully closed.
