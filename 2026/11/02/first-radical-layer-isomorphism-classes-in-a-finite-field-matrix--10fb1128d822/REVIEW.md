# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The inverse-image correspondence with \(L\)-subspaces is exact because \(\pi M_d(O)\) acts trivially on the reduction and \(S/\pi S\) has \(L\)-dimension one. Nonrational line modules are nonfree: their \(S\)-trace is \(\pi S^2\), while the free module is generated by images of \(S\). For isomorphisms between nonrational modules, scalar centralizer theory over \(k((\pi))\) gives a \(\mathrm{GL}_2\) matrix, the absence of a nonzero \(k\)-linear relation in \(L(1,t)\) forces integral entries, and equal lattice indices force unit determinant. Reduction therefore classifies isomorphism via \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(k)\). The orbit sizes follow from stabilizers: \(q+1\) rational points, \(q^2-q\) degree-two points when present, and free orbits of size \(q(q^2-1)\) at degrees at least three. A standalone finite-field computation checks all orbit multiplicities for seven small extensions. The symbolic proof, rather than these computations, covers all prime powers and all extension degrees at least two.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
The matrix order and the existence of a nonfree intermediate submodule already appear in Yan–Zhang, *On the square of an isosimple module*, arXiv:2610.26905v1, Theorem 3.1; neither is claimed new. Standard projective-linear orbit facts and degree-two transitivity are likewise not claimed new. The additional mathematical statement is the complete isomorphism classification of the whole first radical layer, with the precise number and sizes of types, obtained by identifying **module** isomorphisms with integral fractional-linear equivalence. Howe, arXiv:2407.05534, studies projective orbits of closed places and is not, by itself, an isomorphism classification of these order modules; its Frobenius-orbit quotient also differs from the individual-point parameter space here. No directly covering full-layer module statement was located in the compared sources. Older lattice theory or an equivalent integral representation classification may nonetheless imply the result; historical priority is not certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The first radical layer is the smallest natural finite stratum containing the obstruction in the existing iso-Artinian example. Its complete isomorphism distribution measures that obstruction and makes its rapid growth with residue extension degree precise, while distinguishing a free type, a quadratic type, and increasingly many generic types. The information cannot be obtained merely by counting submodules: it depends on the integral nature of their isomorphisms.

## Limitations and relationship to literature
The result is specific to the finite-field residue extension and to the first layer. It does not describe deeper lattices or arbitrary orders. Direct enumeration verifies projective orbit counts, not the infinite algebraic proof. Relevant primary sources are Yan–Zhang, arXiv:2610.26905v1, and Howe, arXiv:2407.05534. A broader classification of orders that makes the module classification a corollary remains a residual originality risk.
