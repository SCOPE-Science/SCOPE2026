# Review of first spherical-cap width transitions

## Correctness — PASS

The orthant decomposition is exact and the cap tangent threshold is the distance to the nearest coordinate face. Finite-face inclusion–exclusion is exact near the largest facet diagonal, and the rescaling \(s=\varepsilon z\), \(y=\sqrt\varepsilon\,\xi\) gives an explicit bounded limit region. Integrating its volume and dividing by \(|S^{d-1}|\) gives the stated coefficient without an unstated normalization. In dimension four, the conditional \(S^2\)-cap integral evaluates independently to the displayed elementary correction. The independent numerical checks cover unequal side lengths and ties, but the proof is the universal evidence. Risk: the elementary formula is local only; it is not asserted beyond the next cap crossing.

## Originality — PASS, with historical priority limitations

The published equal-sided cube first-transition theorem contains the \(a_i=1\) instance and is expressly excluded from novelty. The prior 2026 moment-tomography theorem already recovers all side lengths from the first \(d\) width moments; recovery per se is expressly excluded. The retained new statement is the *local anisotropic first-face asymptotic*, including the exact factor \(k/m\), the exact all-dimensional local one-facet correction integral, its noncongruent-tail-germ collision, and its dimension-four elementary finite-width correction. Searches for weighted-orthotope first-facet cap leakage, phase transition, and corner multiplicity did not locate this claim. Historical risk is real: the full arXiv:2608.03305 companion paper was inaccessible, although the related 2026 all-dimensional paper was reviewed in its relevant sections from an accessible full-text rendering.

## Value — PASS

The first nontrivial departure of a width law from its unrestricted diagonal spherical-cap model is governed by an explicit geometric face-incidence invariant rather than its global mean or variance. The sharp fractional/integer exponent and coefficient quantify the onset of facet clipping in any dimension, the exact all-dimensional local law shows sharp partial identifiability and a constructive nonidentifiability boundary, while the four-dimensional exact formula is an analytical benchmark beyond the regular cube. This is a motivated singularity classification, not a reproof of general recoverability or a mere tabulation of moments.

## Closest literature and limitations

Finch (arXiv:1110.0671) covers three-dimensional regular polyhedra and is inspected in full. Kabluchko–Litvak–Zaporozhets (arXiv:1511.08479) and Paouris–Pivovarov–Zinn (arXiv:1210.7012) provide cube widths and asymptotics. A published cube-only crossing calculation is a strict special case. Abas (arXiv:2608.06348) has stronger full moment identifiability, which is not claimed new. The inaccessible complete companion text and other obscure sources remain priority risks. Theorem applies for fixed \(d\ge4\), positive edge lengths, and uniform spherical directions; no statistical robustness is proved.
