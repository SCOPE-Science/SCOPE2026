# Five arithmetic regimes for coupled cat-map orders on complete bipartite graphs

## Finding

Let \(a,b,s\ge1\) be integers, \(z=ab\), and let \(\nu_2(u)\) denote the exponent of two in the positive integer \(u\). For the complete bipartite graph \(K_{a,b}\), take its integer adjacency matrix \(A\). Consider the linear map
\[
T=\begin{pmatrix}I&A\\A&I+A^2\end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{on }(\mathbb Z/2^s\mathbb Z)^{2(a+b)}.
\]
Its **exact order**, namely the smallest positive \(L\) for which \(T^L=I\) modulo \(2^s\), is
\[
L_s(a,b)=\begin{cases}
2^s,& a,b\text{ both even};\\
2^{s+1},& \text{exactly one of }a,b\text{ even, }z\equiv0\pmod4;\\
4\,2^{\max(0,s-\nu_2(z+2))},&\text{exactly one even, }z\equiv2\pmod4;\\
3\,2^{\max(0,s-\nu_2(z+3))},& a,b\text{ odd, }z\equiv1\pmod4;\\
3,&a,b\text{ odd, }z\equiv3\pmod4,\ s=1;\\
6\,2^{\max(0,s-1-\nu_2(z+1))},& a,b\text{ odd, }z\equiv3\pmod4,\ s\ge2.
\end{cases}
\]
Every state is periodic immediately, and at least one state has primitive period exactly \(L_s(a,b)\). Thus this is both the order of the permutation of states and the maximal attained primitive orbit length.

The product \(z\) alone does *not* determine the order: \(K_{1,4}\) gives \(2^{s+1}\), while \(K_{2,2}\) gives \(2^s\), even though both have \(z=4\).

## Assumptions and scope

All vectors and matrices are over the finite commutative ring \(R_s=\mathbb Z/2^s\mathbb Z\). The vertices are labelled, with two nonempty shores of sizes \(a,b\), and the graph has no self-loops. No invertibility assumption on \(A\) is made. The matrix \(T\) equals
\[
\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\A&I\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}I&A\\0&I\end{pmatrix};
\]
both factors are integral symplectic shears because \(A\) is symmetric. In particular \(T\) is invertible over every \(R_s\). It is therefore a permutation of a finite set, so there are no strictly transient states.

## Proof

**Step 1 (the exact scalar reduction).** The integer adjacency matrix has
\[
A^2=\begin{pmatrix}bJ_a&0\\0&aJ_b\end{pmatrix},\qquad A^3=zA,\qquad A^4=zA^2,
\]
where \(J_h\) is the all-ones matrix. Define polynomials \(q_0(w)=0\), \(q_1(w)=1\), and
\[
q_{t+1}(w)=(w+2)q_t(w)-q_{t-1}(w).
\]
For \(t\ge1\), define \(p_t(w)=(q_t(w)-q_{t-1}(w)-1)/w\). The numerator vanishes at \(w=0\) (where \(q_t(0)=t\)), so \(p_t\in\mathbb Z[w]\).

By Cayley--Hamilton over the commutative subring generated by \(A\),
\[
T^2-(2I_{2(a+b)}+\operatorname{diag}(A^2,A^2))T+I_{2(a+b)}=0.
\]
Multiplying the recurrence therefore gives
\[
T^t-I=\begin{pmatrix}
p_t(z)A^2&q_t(z)A\\
q_t(z)A&(p_t(z)+q_t(z))A^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Since \(A\) has unit entries on its two off-diagonal blocks and \(A^2\) has the nonzero entries \(a\) and \(b\), this yields the **necessary and sufficient** congruence criterion
\[
T^t\equiv I\pmod{2^s}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
2^s\mid q_t(z)\ \text{and}\ 2^s\mid\gcd(a,b)\,p_t(z).
\tag{1}
\]
In particular the full graph dynamics is not interchangeable with the two-dimensional cat map indexed only by \(z\), because the second divisibility test remembers \(\gcd(a,b)\).

**Step 2 (the first congruence layer).** Directly from (1), the least exponent modulo two is \(t_0=2\) if both shores are even, \(t_0=4\) if exactly one shore is even, and \(t_0=3\) if both are odd. The polynomial values that determine the next layers include
\[
\begin{aligned}
&q_2=z+2,\quad p_2=1;\\
&q_3=(z+1)(z+3),\quad p_3=z+3;\\
&q_4=(z+2)(z^2+4z+2),\quad p_4=(z+2)(z+3);\\
&q_6=(z+1)(z+2)(z+3)(z^2+4z+1),\\
&p_6=(z+1)(z+3)(z^2+5z+5);\\
&q_8=(z+2)(z^2+4z+2)(z^4+8z^3+20z^2+16z+2),\\
&p_8=(z+2)(z^2+4z+2)(z^3+7z^2+14z+7).
\end{aligned}
\]
For a positive integer \(t\), set \(h_t=\min\{\nu_2(q_t(z)),\nu_2(\gcd(a,b)p_t(z))\}\), with \(\nu_2(0)=\infty\). This is exactly the minimum two-adic valuation of an entry of \(T^t-I\). Straight parity and the displayed factorizations give the following *exact* anchors:

| Regime | Base order mod 2 | Initial exact valuations |
|---|---:|---|
| \(2\mid a,\ 2\mid b\) | 2 | \(h_2=1,\ h_4=2\) |
| exactly one even, \(z\equiv0\pmod4\) | 4 | \(h_4=1,\ h_8=2\) |
| exactly one even, \(z\equiv2\pmod4\) | 4 | \(h_4=\nu_2(z+2)\ge2\) |
| both odd, \(z\equiv1\pmod4\) | 3 | \(h_3=\nu_2(z+3)\ge2\) |
| both odd, \(z\equiv3\pmod4\) | 3 | \(h_3=1,\ h_6=1+\nu_2(z+1)\ge3\) |

For clarity, when \(z\) is even, \(\nu_2(z^2+4z+2)=1\); when \(z\equiv3\pmod4\), \(\nu_2(z^2+4z+1)=1\) and \(z^2+5z+5\) is odd. These identities control the only delicate rows.

**Step 3 (sharp lifting).** If an integer matrix satisfies \(B^t=I+2^h E\), where \(h\ge2\) and some entry of \(E\) is odd, then
\[
B^{2t}-I=2^{h+1}\bigl(E+2^{h-1}E^2\bigr),
\]
and the parenthesized matrix still has an odd entry. Inductively the exact entrywise valuation is \(h+r\) at exponent \(2^rt\). The kernel of reduction from \(\mathrm{GL}_{2(a+b)}(\mathbb Z/2^s\mathbb Z)\) to \(\mathrm{GL}_{2(a+b)}(\mathbb F_2)\) is a finite two-group, so every order is a power-of-two multiple of its base order \(t_0\). Applying the exact lifting rule to each valuation anchor in Step 2 yields precisely the six displayed cases for \(L_s(a,b)\).

**Step 4 (a full-length orbit exists).** Let \(L=L_s(a,b)\). For each prime \(p\mid L\), the subgroup \(\ker(T^{L/p}-I)\) is proper; otherwise \(L\) would not be the matrix order. Only the primes two and three can divide \(L\). A finite group cannot be the union of two proper subgroups, so there is a state outside all these kernels. Its primitive orbit length is divisible by the full prime-power factors of \(L\) and divides \(L\), hence equals \(L\). This also handles a single prime divisor (take a state outside the one proper kernel). The theorem follows.

## Relationship to prior work

Rivas--Saraceno--Ozorio de Almeida (1999) studied general multidimensional integral symplectic cat maps and periodic points. Nance (2011) considered a different higher-dimensional construction. Chen's two-dimensional period-distribution program, including the 2014 paper, and Li--Tan--Feng--Lü (2017) provide exact arithmetic structure for generalized **two-dimensional** maps. They do not imply the present complete-bipartite graph order: notably the graph pairs \(K_{1,4}\), \(K_{2,2}\) share \(ab=4\) while their actual orders differ.

Axenides--Floratos--Nicolis (2022) introduced the relevant general matrix-shear form and matrix-Fibonacci criterion for coupled cat-map lattices, but analyze particular couplings without the complete-bipartite two-adic order classification. Manolas--Floratos (2026) analyze period spectra on circulant couplings, whereas general \(K_{a,b}\) is not a circulant graph. An accessible published research summary proves an exact order formula for complete-graph couplings, a different adjacency family. The new contribution assessed here is the *exact five-arithmetic-regime order law and full-length realization* for the complete-bipartite graph family, not the known general shear construction or Fibonacci criterion.

## Limitations

The result concerns moduli which are powers of two, full synchronous time steps of this particular symmetric two-shear map, and complete bipartite adjacency only. It does not enumerate all shorter primitive cycles or classify arbitrary graph couplings. The modular computation does not establish the infinite-family result without the accompanying proof. The literature comparison includes relevant full primary texts, but an unindexed or differently formulated equivalent theorem remains a historical risk; the complete-graph research result was available as a title and summary rather than an inspected full paper.
