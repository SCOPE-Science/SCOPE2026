# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The chord distribution is proved directly from the affine-square strip representation and uniform transverse offset, including the pure-atom and no-atom boundary cases. Its two population moments algebraically invert both directional absolute strip evaluations. The five-projective-root argument forces two competing homogeneous quadratics to agree up to sign, and recovering their rank-one sums recovers the actual strips. Four exact rational directions and two explicit noncongruent parallelograms have identical complete chord laws; all evaluations and nondegeneracy conditions have been checked. A standalone rational/numerical checker provides independent spot-checks but does not replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority provisional)
The strongest direct antecedent is Gasparyan–Ohanyan (2014), whose author-uploaded full text explicitly computes the directional parallelogram law and the full covariogram, but whose stated objectives and results do not claim universal *five prescribed directions* recovery or a four-direction obstruction. Martirosyan (2023) handles general quadrilateral forward laws but does not establish the present finite-probe inverse theorem. Nagel (1993) and Averkov–Bianchi (2009) use a continuum of directions. Gasparyan–Ohanyan (2013) shows a contrasting finite-view impossibility for triangles. Database searches for moment tomography, directional chord identifiability, exact finite-view bounds, equivalence to phase retrieval, and existing related records found no same statement. Absence from the inspected literature does not prove priority; obscure inverse/tomography references may remain.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The finite-view count is a natural sample-complexity invariant in geometric tomography: a fixed small number of physically measured projection directions must work regardless of the body's orientation. Exact recovery from two moments per view and a matching whole-law four-view ambiguity provide a complete meaningful threshold for all parallelograms, separating them sharply from the finite-view behavior previously reported for triangles. The boundary examples are nondegenerate and use explicit rational data.

## Limits
The theorem does not determine general quadrilaterals, does not give noisy-estimation guarantees, and does not assert all four-view direction sets fail. Historical novelty is subject to discovery of equivalent finite-view reconstruction statements. No independent review is claimed.
