# Five-periodic rigidity of minimum 1-limited domination on generalized Petersen graphs

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge5\), let \(G_n=GP(n,2)\). The number of **labelled** minimum \(1\)-limited dominating sets in \(G_n\) is
\[
N_n=\begin{cases}12,&5\mid n,\\ 2,&5\nmid n.\end{cases}
\]
Every minimum set has exactly \(n\) vertices. All minimum sets have a period-five characterization, including the ten nonlayer sets that exist precisely when \(5\mid n\). The cardinality \(n\), and the existence of layer sets, follow from known general structural principles; the contribution is the complete equality classification and its enumeration.

## Assumptions and scope
The vertices of \(G_n\) are \(a_i,b_i\) for \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\). Its edges are \(a_i a_{i+1}\), \(a_i b_i\), and \(b_i b_{i+2}\). Since \(n\ge5\), the graph is simple and cubic. A \(1\)-limited dominating set \(D\) dominates every vertex outside \(D\) and has at most one outside neighbour per selected vertex.

Every minimum set is generated by a cyclic binary word \(u_i\in\{-1,+1\}\) and its derived word \(v_i=u_i-u_{i-1}-u_{i+1}\): take exactly those \(a_i\) with \(u_i=+1\) and \(b_i\) with \(v_i=+1\). When \(5\nmid n\), the only allowed words are the two constant words. When \(5\mid n\), the allowed words are repetitions of the twelve length-five cyclic words consisting of (i) the two constant words and (ii) the ten words having one run of two identical signs and one run of three opposite signs. Indices of every word are taken cyclically; no identification under graph automorphisms is made in the count.

## Proof
**Lower bound and equality constraints.** Let \(D\) be \(1\)-limited and write \(x_w=1\) when \(w\in D\) and \(0\) otherwise. A selected vertex of the cubic graph has at least two selected neighbours, while an unselected vertex has at least one selected neighbour. Thus each vertex satisfies \(\sum_{z\sim w}x_z\ge1+x_w\). Summing over all \(2n\) vertices gives \(3|D|\ge2n+|D|\), hence \(|D|\ge n\). Either complete layer \(\{a_i\}\) or \(\{b_i\}\) is a \(1\)-limited dominating set of size \(n\). Therefore the lower bound is sharp. For a set of size exactly \(n\), each of the \(2n\) local inequalities must be an equality. Equivalently, each selected vertex has exactly one unselected neighbour and each unselected vertex has exactly one selected neighbour.

**Spin equations.** Put \(s_w=2x_w-1\) and write \(u_i=s_{a_i}\), \(v_i=s_{b_i}\). The exact one-opposite-neighbour rule at a cubic vertex means that the sum of its three neighbour spins equals its own spin. For every \(i\) this is exactly
\[
u_{i-1}+u_{i+1}+v_i=u_i,\qquad v_{i-2}+v_{i+2}+u_i=v_i.\]
The first relation gives \(v_i=u_i-u_{i-1}-u_{i+1}\). On substitution in the second, all terms except four cancel, leaving
\[
u_{i+3}-u_{i+2}=u_{i-2}-u_{i-3}.\]
For \(d_i=u_{i+1}-u_i\), this is \(d_{i+2}=d_{i-3}\), so \(d_{i+5}=d_i\) for every cyclic index. It follows that \(u_{i+5}-u_i=d_i+\cdots+d_{i+4}\) is independent of \(i\). Summing the latter equality over all \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) shows its common value is zero. Therefore \(u_{i+5}=u_i\) for all \(i\), and the derived \(v_i\) has the same period.

**Classification and converses.** If \(\gcd(n,5)=1\), the permutation \(i\mapsto i+5\) traverses every index, so \(u_i\) is constant. The formula for \(v_i\) then gives \(v_i=-u_i\), producing precisely the two layer sets. If \(5\mid n\), let \((u_0,\ldots,u_4)\) be the five-periodic block. As \(u_i\in\{\pm1\}\), the derived value \(v_i=u_i-u_{i-1}-u_{i+1}\) belongs to \(\{\pm1\}\) if and only if the cyclic word has no index at which both neighbours bear the sign opposite to its own: exactly the exclusion of a singleton cyclic sign run. Among binary cyclic words of length five, these are precisely the two constants and the ten words with opposite runs of lengths two and three. For any one of these twelve blocks the derived \(v\)-word is \(\pm1\)-valued; its five-periodicity makes the second spin equation hold (its difference equation is identically satisfied). Hence each block supplies a valid one-opposite-neighbour two-coloring. The opposite-spin edges form a perfect matching between the color classes, so the two classes have equal cardinality \(n\). Thus each block supplies an optimal set. The twelve words are distinct on labelled vertices, giving \(N_n=12\). Conversely every equality set was forced into this list, completing the proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Alaeiyan and Karami (2016) enumerate **which quotient matrices occur** for perfect two-colorings of generalized Petersen graphs and construct the layer coloring with quotient matrix \(\begin{psmallmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{psmallmatrix}\). Karami (2026) extends the quotient-matrix existence classification across generalized Petersen parameters. Neither examined paper counts or classifies all actual colorings with this fixed quotient matrix for every \(GP(n,2)\). In fact, minimum \(1\)-limited sets are exactly the first color classes of the two-colorings having this quotient matrix. The works by Radić and collaborators (2026) give general structural bounds for \(1\)-limited domination in cubic graphs and applications to several snark families, but not this labelled count. Existence, the general half-order bound, and the known quotient-matrix classification are not presented as new.

## Limitations
The enumeration uses labelled vertices and distinguishes a set from its complement; it does not count orbits modulo automorphisms. It covers only \(GP(n,2)\), not all generalized Petersen graphs \(GP(n,k)\). Searches and primary-source comparisons did not establish independent historical priority; an equivalent count under another terminology remains a residual risk. Independent mathematical audit has not been conducted.
