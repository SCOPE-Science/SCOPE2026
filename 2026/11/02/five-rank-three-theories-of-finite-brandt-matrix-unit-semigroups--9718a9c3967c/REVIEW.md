# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The proof is valid specifically in the language with a named zero and ternary relational multiplication. For every nonzero matrix unit, the nonzero idempotents acting as one-sided identities are exactly its one or two endpoint idempotents. Explicit rank-at-most-three sentences therefore test upper index bounds one through four. After two moves the game can preserve all coordinate-index equalities with at most four indices. The only six-index triple arising in round three consists of three pairwise product-annihilating off-diagonal units; replacing its final unit by one with a repeated *row index* preserves the complete atomic diagram, not merely selected tests. This gives a full strategy for every pair of parameters at least five. The full atomic-diagram enumerator and an independent orbit-based enumerator verify the finite characterization; the infinite result is proved in the text.

## Originality
**PASS, with residual priority risk.** Search queries using Brandt semigroup, matrix units, exact three-round equivalence, Rees matrices, and logical depth found no published theorem implying the five-class classification. Jackson–Volkov (2009) treat varieties and universal Horn behavior of related Rees matrix constructions; Maysami Sadr (2012) studies Banach-algebra Morita equivalence; Gould–Quinn-Gregson (2018/2019) establish categoricity and coordinate automorphism-orbit results. None of the inspected full texts gives a finite rank-three cutoff or entails the sharp five-index bound as a stated quantitative corollary. A priori unindexed sources could contain an equivalent result; this is a documented priority risk, not a proof of absolute novelty.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** This is a complete sharp fixed-depth classification of a standard natural family of inverse semigroups. A picked matrix unit can name two indices, suggesting a generic six-index three-round upper bound, yet the exact cutoff is five because row–row coincidence can be made atomically invisible in the last round. The result isolates a structural expressivity limit of ternary relational multiplication, while the endpoint-cover formulas prove that the threshold is optimal. These are motivated parameters of the full Brandt matrix-unit family, not an arbitrary finite slice.

## Prior-work relationship and limitations
The result is in model theory of finite relational semigroup presentations (primary MSC 03C13). Compared with the cited primary sources, it is neither a conclusion about semigroup identities/universal Horn classes, nor a Morita-equivalence result, nor a categoricity theorem. It does not extend automatically to multiplication as a function symbol, to other signatures, or to nontrivial coefficient groups. The available full-text comparisons and recorded searches do not amount to exhaustive archival priority verification.
