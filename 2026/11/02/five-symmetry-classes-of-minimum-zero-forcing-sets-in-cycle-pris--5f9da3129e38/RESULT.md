# Five symmetry classes of minimum zero-forcing sets in cycle prisms

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge5\), the prism graph \(G_n=C_n\square K_2\) has ordinary zero-forcing number \(Z(G_n)=4\). The exact number of its four-vertex zero-forcing sets is
\[
z(G_n;4)=13n.
\]
The full collection splits into exactly five orbits of the group generated by rotations and reflections of the cycle columns and the interchange of its two rows. Orbit sizes are \(2n,4n,4n,2n,n\) in the order A–E below.

## Assumptions and scope
Work with finite, simple, labelled, undirected graphs. The columns are indexed by \(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\); in each column the two vertices are \(u_i\) and \(v_i\). Edges are \(u_i u_{i+1}\), \(v_i v_{i+1}\), and \(u_i v_i\) for all column indices. Every vertex has exactly three neighbours. Ordinary zero forcing means: initially a subset is black and all other vertices are white; at any step a **black** vertex with exactly one white neighbour changes that neighbour to black. A set is zero forcing if repeated such steps colour all vertices black. Let \(z(G;j)\) count labelled zero-forcing sets of size \(j\), with no quotient by symmetry.

For a row \(r\in\{u,v\}\), write \(\bar r\) for the other row. Every subscript below is modulo \(n\). The five families are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm A&:\quad\{r_i,r_{i+1},r_{i+2},r_{i+3}\}, &&r\in\{u,v\},\ i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z;\\
\mathrm B&:\quad\{r_i,r_{i+\epsilon},r_{i+2\epsilon},\bar r_i\}, &&r\in\{u,v\},\ \epsilon\in\{-1,1\},\ i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z;\\
\mathrm C&:\quad\{r_i,r_{i+\epsilon},r_{i+3\epsilon},\bar r_{i+\epsilon}\}, &&r\in\{u,v\},\ \epsilon\in\{-1,1\},\ i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z;\\
\mathrm D&:\quad\{u_i,u_{i+\epsilon},v_i,v_{i-\epsilon}\}, &&\epsilon\in\{-1,1\},\ i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z;\\
\mathrm E&:\quad\{u_i,u_{i+1},v_i,v_{i+1}\}, &&i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z.
\end{aligned}
\]
All indicated parameters vary independently. For \(n\ge5\), the displayed sets are distinct within each family, and the family sizes are \(2n,4n,4n,2n,n\). They are pairwise disjoint. The theorem asserts these families are **exactly** the minimum zero-forcing sets.

## Proof
**First-force constraint and lower bound.** A cubic black vertex can force at an initial stage only if it and two of its neighbours are black. Thus an initially black set of size at most two cannot force. Up to rotation, reflection, and row swap, the three black vertices required for a first force are of precisely two kinds:
\[
T=\{u_{-1},u_0,u_1\},\quad u_0\to v_0;
\qquad
R=\{u_0,u_1,v_0\},\quad u_0\to u_{-1}.
\]
In either case the set immediately after the indicated force is
\[
H=\{u_{-1},u_0,u_1,v_0\}.
\]
For \(n\ge5\), each black vertex of \(H\) has zero or two white neighbours, never exactly one. Therefore no three-vertex seed forces the prism, and \(Z(G_n)\ge4\).

**Exhaustive first-force obstruction for four seeds.** Fix a hypothetical forcing four-vertex seed and one valid first force. Its black set contains a triple congruent to \(T\) or \(R\), together with a fourth vertex \(x\) not in that triple and not equal to the forced target. After the first force, its black set is \(H\cup\{x\}\), with \(x\notin H\). From the explicitly displayed graph neighbours, the only white neighbours of \(H\) whose addition can decrease a vertex's two-white-neighbour count to one are
\[
X=\{u_{-2},u_2,v_{-1},v_1\}.
\]
Moreover, each vertex outside \(H\cup X\) has at most one neighbour in \(H\); hence the additional black vertex itself cannot force if it is outside \(X\). Consequently, if \(x\notin X\), the process is stuck just after its first force. This is a complete local case distinction valid for every \(n\ge5\), with no unstated restriction on the location of the fourth seed. Therefore the fourth vertex must lie in \(X\).

The eight possibilities, four for each initial triple, reduce exactly to the five families above, with this explicit correspondence:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
\text{initial triple}&x=u_{-2}&x=u_2&x=v_{-1}&x=v_1\\\hline
T&A&A&B&B\\
R&C&B&D&E
\end{array}
\]
Here family B from \(R\) with \(x=u_2\) agrees with one already generated by \(T\); symmetry images of these eight prototypes produce all the parameter choices in the displayed families.

**Sufficiency.** For each of the eight prototypes the indicated first force and a further force create two consecutive fully black rungs. For example, with triple \(T\) and \(x=u_2\), first \(u_0\to v_0\), then \(u_1\to v_1\). With \(T\) and \(x=v_1\), first \(u_0\to v_0\), then \(v_0\to v_{-1}\). For triple \(R\), the four choices \(u_{-2},u_2,v_{-1},v_1\) similarly produce full adjacent columns (when \(x=v_1\), columns zero and one are full immediately). Reflections handle the remaining cases. Whenever two adjacent rungs are fully black, the vertices in the last full rung each have a unique white neighbour in the next rung, until the next rung is fully black; repeatedly extending the full interval in the circular direction forces all vertices. Hence all A–E sets are zero forcing and \(Z(G_n)=4\).

**Distinctness, symmetry, and exact count.** Family A has all four vertices in one row; B and C have row multiplicities three and one, while B's three-row induced graph is a three-vertex path and C's consists of one edge and one isolated vertex (for all \(n\ge5\)). Families D and E have two vertices in each row: D induces a four-vertex path; E induces a four-cycle. These intrinsic characteristics distinguish the families. Within each family, the indexing of the unique isolated/special vertex or full rungs (and the direction of the asymmetry) gives the claimed number of distinct labelled sets; in particular D's orientation is recovered from its path's degree-two rung vertex, and E's full rung pair is unique. Each family is a single orbit under column dihedral symmetry together with row swap, because its parameters are obtained from any fixed representative by these symmetries. Summing gives
\[
2n+4n+4n+2n+n=13n.
\]
The first-force obstruction proves that no further four-vertex forcing sets exist. This completes the proof.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `verify.py` independently implements both parallel-wave and sequential ordinary forcing. It exhaustively checks all three-vertex seeds and all four-vertex seeds for \(5\le n\le13\); the total number of four-subset tests is 44,322. It directly constructs A–E for \(5\le n\le16\), checks pairwise disjointness, exact orbit cardinalities, every member's forcing success, and the local first-force obstruction. The replay output is:

`VERIFY_OK complete_n=5..13 constructive_n=5..16 exhaustive_three_sizes=9 exhaustive_four_sizes=9 four_subsets_checked=44322 families_per_n=5 exact_count=13n`

Reproduce with `python verify.py` using Python's standard library. The assertion for unbounded \(n\) follows from the structural proof, not the finite tests.

## Relationship to prior work
The zero-forcing number \(Z(G_n)=4\) for prisms of length at least four is an established result in Alameda et al. (2018, Section 2). That numerical equality is **not claimed as new**. Chang's 2026 paper gives skew zero-forcing numbers for Cartesian products of paths and cycles; skew forcing permits forces by white vertices, unlike the ordinary black-only forcing treated here. Neither the previously known optimal cardinality alone nor a skew-forcing parameter determines the **number and complete five-orbit structure of ordinary minimum initial sets**. The present contribution is exactly that classification and enumeration. Existing zero-forcing polynomial results for fans, friendship graphs, and chain graphs refer to different graph families. Published-index searches for 'prism', 'generalized Petersen \(P(n,1)\)', and 'circular ladder' with ordinary minimum-set counts did not identify this expression, but non-identification is not a uniqueness certificate.

## Limitations
The theorem assumes labelled simple prisms \(C_n\square K_2\) with \(n\ge5\), ordinary zero forcing, and sets of **minimum cardinality only**. It does not determine the entire zero-forcing polynomial, the exceptional small prism counts, or generalized Petersen graphs with other step parameters. Historical-priority review remains incomplete, in particular for older complete enumeration or general composition theorems potentially implying this special case. Independent assessment has not been performed.
