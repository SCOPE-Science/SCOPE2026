# Scientific review of the prism minimum-set classification

**Correctness — PASS.** Every prism vertex has degree three, making the first force require a black triple of one of two explicit types. Both reach the same stalled four-black configuration; in a four-seed set, a fifth black vertex produced at the first force permits continuation only when its extra original vertex lies in one of four specified neighbouring locations. These yield exactly the five proposed set families, and each family constructs two adjacent complete rungs, which propagate around the prism. Index distinctions prove the exact orbit sizes. Exhaustive 3-/4-seed experiments across nine prism orders and a separate forcing implementation corroborate but do not replace the all-length proof.

**Originality — PASS, qualified by historical-priority risk.** Alameda et al. 2018 explicitly already give the ordinary zero-forcing NUMBER four for prism graphs; Chang 2026 determines SKEW zero-forcing numbers on cylinders, not a count of optimal ordinary seeds. The new exact five-orbit classification and \(13n\) labelled minimum-set count follow from neither cited parameter value. Searches of mathematical records, published primary sources and related zero-forcing polynomial classes did not identify the identical classification. Indexed Menon–Singh 2025 material could not be fully compared; a general unlocated minimum-set enumeration remains a residual priority risk. No lack-of-hit is treated as proof of global novelty.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The minimal-seed count and all five group orbits refine a previously known optimization value for a canonical infinite family of connected cubic graphs. The five configurations describe all extremal propagation geometries; the exact \(13n\) multiplicity is a natural invariant not determined from \(Z=4\).

**Scientific limits.** Only ordinary black-only zero forcing of labelled \(C_n\square K_2\) for \(n\ge5\) is proved, not all seed-set sizes or other Petersen parameters. Some broader-source full text is inaccessible; historical priority and independent verification are open.
