# Scientific review: five three-round theories of planar rectangular grid graphs

## Correctness

**PASS.** The logical recursion is exact: a pair's one-round continuation consists of all possible adjacency/equality diagrams of a third vertex. Taking the sets of pair types at a first vertex, then over first vertices, characterizes ordinary first-order quantifier rank three. The proof enumerates every possible pair extension by Manhattan distance and common-neighbour multiplicity; it isolates the only small cases in which two selected vertices dominate all other vertices. The all-parameter coordinate argument shows that every grid other than the four named exceptions has precisely three one-point types. The five class signatures are distinct. Two scripts using distinct representations reproduce all boundary cases and representative equivalences; neither is used as a substitute for the infinite argument.

## Originality

**PASS, qualified.** The nearest checked primary literature treats general graph quantifier-rank bounds (Pikhurko–Veith–Verbitsky 2003), graph separator definability (Verbitsky 2004), and exact single-path/cycle games (Brown–Hoshino 2007). Those results do not explicitly calculate the five classes or the sharp ten-vertex bound for pure-adjacency Cartesian products of two paths in the material examined. A related three-round theorem for products of at least three path factors excludes the present two-dimensional family; its hypercube exception and extension profiles differ. A different result for products of chains uses the order relation, not adjacency. Published work on grid topology, matching complexes, and multiset metric dimension concerns different invariants. The possibility of an earlier implicit consequence of a general finite-model theorem remains a historical priority risk.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The exact classification concerns all two-dimensional rectangular grid graphs, canonical planar and graph-product objects. It exhibits a sharp increase from one common rank-two theory to five rank-three theories, a structural distinction between the \(3\)-by-\(3\) grid and all sufficiently large grids, and the non-obvious rank-three equivalence of the thin \(2\)-by-\(5\) ladder with the thicker \(3\)-by-\(4\) grid. Ten vertices is a justified optimal uniform representative bound, not an arbitrary finite census point.

## Closest literature and limitations

The game and quantifier-rank framework is standard; the exact family-specific extension-signature table is the new content under review. The theorem is restricted to the pure adjacency language, both path factors of order at least two, and quantifier rank three. It does not classify rank-four theories, labelled boundaries, counting logic, or higher-dimensional grids. Historical priority is not certified, and no independent mathematical audit has been performed.
