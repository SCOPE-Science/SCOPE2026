## correctness

PASS

In the pure strict-prefix order language, depth r is definable with only two reusable names using r iterated proper-ancestor quantifiers. For two pebbles a surviving vertex of depth a admits a new vertex at depth d in each equality/ancestor/descendant/incomparable relation exactly according to height and β, the first branching level; hence FO² equivalence iff heights and β agree. With k≥3, sibling sets of t≤k children of a depth-i node are definable by at most k variables by rebinding a third name in pairwise shared-ancestor subformulas. Thus different min(b_i,k) are distinguished. Conversely a permanent k-pebble strategy preserving depths and all pairwise longest common prefix depths extends paths through occupied matching directions or unused directions, available because ≤k−1 old branches are occupied. This proves iff across all finite heights; representatives and class counts follow by truncation. Independently implemented exact k-pebble color refinements for eight illustrative pairs, including distinction between k2 and k3; bounded tests corroborate but do not prove all-height equivalence.
