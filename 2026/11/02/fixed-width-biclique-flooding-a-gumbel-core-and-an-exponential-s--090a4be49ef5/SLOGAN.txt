On a complete bipartite network with a fixed-size hub class, any initial hub-side seed produces standard Gumbel flooding fluctuations, whereas a fixed number \(k\) of low-degree seeds alone leaves an independent \(\operatorname{Exp}(k)\) delay. For a single seed at any finite size, choosing the higher-degree part is optimal for every deadline, and the exact mean-time advantage is \((a-b)/(a+b)\).
