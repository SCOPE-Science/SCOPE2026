# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The cyclic eigenmodes and energy-degeneracy pairing yield the exact two-site orthogonality condition \(N\mid d(2k+q)\) at flux \(q\pi\). A complete gcd count, fixed-point subtraction and finite character sum prove the dimension and all localized weights in every parity case. Contractive finite-dimensional spectral arguments prove the equality of dark projection and the two stated absorption/measurement limits. The coprime criterion follows by exhaustion of the odd, even-large-gcd and even-minimal-gcd cases. Standalone extracted checker: 32,975 assertions, including all sizes through 36 and independent propagation. Numerical tests are explicitly supportive, not the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Wang et al., arXiv:2402.15843v1, work on the triangle with one-site local measurement or whole-position tracking, not simultaneous detection at an arbitrary pair of sites. Thiel et al., arXiv:1906.08112, give the abstract spectral dark-projection framework and single-target magnetic-ring examples, so this general principle is credited. Their published single-target formula does not directly give the two-sensor character sums, the parity-sensitive half-flux weights, or the coprime detector-spacing criterion. Thiel et al., arXiv:1909.02114, develop one-target symmetry bounds. A related published finite-cycle two-sensor frame-bounds study concerns graph Fourier conditioning under a strict low-pass mask, not a two-detector quantum dark-space survival law. Mathematical-record searches on the exact objects, parameters and equivalent observability interpretation found no directly covering statement. The main residual risk is an unexamined general multi-target observability or controllability theorem whose specialization yields the same arithmetic formula.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Detector placement is an elementary control variable in monitored quantum search. The theorem supplies a complete exact criterion for all-flux absence of dark states, all localized nondetection probabilities, and two opposite half-flux switching effects. This quantifies detector geometry rather than recasting a single-target result and gives a directly usable search-design rule, independently of detection strength. The results are exact for every ring size and not arbitrary finite numerical slices.
