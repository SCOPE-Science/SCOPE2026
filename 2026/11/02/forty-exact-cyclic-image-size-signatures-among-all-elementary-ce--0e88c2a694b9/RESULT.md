# Forty exact cyclic image-size signatures among all elementary cellular automata

## Finding

For each elementary binary radius-one cellular automaton rule \(r\in\{0,\ldots,255\}\) and each integer \(n\ge1\), let \(F_{r,n}:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}^n\) be the synchronous local update with indices interpreted modulo \(n\). Let
\[
A_r(n)=|F_{r,n}(\{0,1\}^n)|.
\]
Two rules are *cyclic image-count equivalent* if \(A_r(n)=A_s(n)\) for **every** \(n\ge1\). There are **exactly 40 equivalence classes** among all 256 rules. Under the usual output-color conjugation and left/right reflection, the 256 rules form 88 conventional classes; the new 40-way partition consists of 8 singleton groups of these conventional classes, 26 groups of two, 4 of four and 2 of six. Every pair of nonequivalent rules is distinguished by the image count of some ring length \(1\le n\le6\). This separation bound is attained.

For example, Rules 30 and 106, Rules 22 and 104, and Rules 29 and 184 are pairwise cyclic image-count equivalent, notwithstanding their membership in different conventional rule classes. The six conventional representatives `3`, `11`, `19`, `136`, `138`, `200` share one sequence, and `14`, `28`, `42`, `50`, `56`, `76` share another.

## Assumptions and scope

For a three-bit neighborhood \((a,b,c)\in\{0,1\}^3\), the Wolfram-numbered local map is
\[
f_r(a,b,c)=\left\lfloor\frac{r}{2^{4a+2b+c}}\right\rfloor\bmod2.
\]
On a ring \(x=(x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1})\), the updated symbol is \((F_{r,n}(x))_i=f_r(x_{i-1},x_i,x_{i+1})\), using cyclic subscripts even for \(n=1,2\). A ring is indexed, so distinct rotations are counted separately. The result concerns one step, not multistep dynamics or the number of distinct precursors of an individual output. For the algebraic recurrence only, use the formal convention \(A_r(0)=1\).

## Proof

**1. Exact circular-recognition automaton.** Take the four de Bruijn states \(ab\in\{0,1\}^2\). For each output bit \(e\in\{0,1\}\), form the Boolean \(4\times4\) matrix \(M^{(r)}_e\) whose entry from state \(ab\) to state \(bc\) is one if and only if \(f_r(a,b,c)=e\); all other entries are zero. Multiplication of these matrices is over the Boolean semiring. A length-\(n\) output \(y_0\ldots y_{n-1}\) is realizable by a same-length periodic input exactly when the Boolean product \(M^{(r)}_{y_0}\cdots M^{(r)}_{y_{n-1}}\) has at least one true diagonal entry. Indeed, a diagonal entry witnesses a closed path of triples with consistent successive overlaps and output labels; conversely every input ring supplies such a closed path. This argument includes the repeated-neighbor cases \(n=1,2\).

**2. Finite integer recurrence.** Begin with the \(4\times4\) Boolean identity relation \(I\). Close the set \(S_r\) of relations reachable from \(I\) under right multiplication by the two label matrices; write \(d_r=|S_r|\). This is a finite set of at most \(2^{16}\) relations. Define the integer-valued column vector
\[
v_r^{(0)}(R)=\mathbf1\{\operatorname{diag}(R)\text{ contains a true entry}\},\qquad R\in S_r,
\]
\[
v_r^{(k+1)}(R)=v_r^{(k)}(RM^{(r)}_0)+v_r^{(k)}(RM^{(r)}_1).
\]
Every output word is generated by a unique sequence of deterministic relation states, so \(A_r(k)=v_r^{(k)}(I)\) for every \(k\ge0\). Hence the whole cyclic image-size sequence is a fixed coordinate of the powers of an explicit \(d_r\times d_r\) integer transition matrix \(T_r\).

**3. Finite criterion for infinite equality.** For two rules \(r,s\), their sequence difference is a fixed integer linear functional of the powers of \(\operatorname{diag}(T_r,T_s)\), a block-diagonal matrix with \(d_r+d_s\) rows. By Cayley–Hamilton, that difference satisfies a monic linear recurrence of order at most \(d_r+d_s\). Consequently, agreement for all \(0\le k<d_r+d_s\) implies agreement for every integer \(k\ge0\). This establishes an exact finite, not approximate, all-length equivalence test; no spectral or floating-point assumptions are involved.

**4. Complete certificate.** The standalone pure-Python verifier constructs all 88 conventional rule classes by exact reflection and color conjugation, the relation closures and the first seven integer terms for each class representative. Those fingerprints separate the 40 groups given above, and the largest first separation length is exactly six. In each group the verifier compares the smallest representative against each other member at every degree below the rigorous Cayley–Hamilton bound. There are exactly 48 such comparisons; the largest bound is 4,750, and the largest individual reachable-relation set has 2,375 states. Every comparison succeeds. The group partition therefore holds at **all** positive ring lengths; between-group inequality is witnessed by an explicitly computed length no greater than six. Every one of the 256 rules belongs to exactly one of the 88 symmetry orbits, completing the classification.

## Relationship to prior work

Krawczyk (2013) classifies elementary cellular automata by the number \(\#(N)\) of compressed local-symmetry classes in a full state-transition network, discusses standard 88 conventional symmetry classes and notes some further coincidences. That network-compression count is a **different invariant** from the one-step image size \(A_r(N)\). Her observed finite-size coincidences do not state the 40-class all-length one-step image-count theorem here. Fukś (2013) studies counts of fixed block preimages through iterations of the cellular automaton, not this all-rule partition of distinct same-period one-step outputs. Martínez (2013) surveys dynamical classification schemes, including surjectivity and symmetry, rather than equality of finite-ring image cardinalities. Salo (2018) studies regularity and split epimorphisms of elementary cellular automata, not equality of their finite-ring image-size sequences. Existing finite Garden-of-Eden tabulations can furnish finitely many values of \(2^n-A_r(n)\), but their tables alone neither prove all-length equality nor certify the sharp six-length separation theorem.

## Limitations

This theorem classifies **cardinalities** of one-step images, not equality of image languages or conjugacy of the cellular automata. Distinct automata grouped here need not have the same individual reachable configurations, dynamics or predecessor multiplicities. The bound six is a separation bound for this precisely defined set of 256 rules, not for arbitrary finite automata. Exact computations and the Cayley–Hamilton implication are internally checked, but independent validation and a definitive historical-priority assessment have not been performed. Additional unindexed earlier work may contain overlapping results.
