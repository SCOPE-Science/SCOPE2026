# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The domain is the exact length-\(504\) tenth Tribonacci morphism iterate with zero-based positions. Reconstructing its \(127260\) substring occurrences yields \(80559\) distinct factors and \(12\) inclusion-minimal factor-position supports. The exact count of three-position hitting sets is \(416\) both for all twelve supports and for the ten-factor basis. Four explicit Cartesian boxes each satisfy the ten tests and total \(416\) distinct triples. Ten exclusive invalid-witness triples certify no fewer than ten factor tests can suffice. The independent factor-ID replay examines all \(\binom{504}{3}\) position triples and agrees. A separate one-letter argument proves that three positions are minimal.

## Originality — PASS with residual priority risk

Schaeffer–Shallit's Theorem 6 already gives the universal size-three bound and one optimal Tribonacci triple. Gheeraert–Romana–Stipulanti give families of attractor constructions and optimality results but do not enumerate this iterate. Banbara and collaborators classify all optimal attractors of three *other* well-known morphic families, not Tribonacci. Related published exact attractor counts for Thue–Morse and other binary words are different objects. No exact \(416\)-attractor four-box structure or optimal ten-factor testing basis was identified in the compared primary sources or mathematical records. This does not rule out unpublished computations or an implicit finite consequence of a general-purpose enumeration algorithm.

## Value — PASS

The minimal number of factors needed to *test* three-position attractors is a natural covering/hitting-set invariant, unlike merely selecting an arbitrary substring window. Here it is exactly ten, with a concrete witness for the necessity of every test. The four-box formula compresses a complete \(416\)-member classification into six short coordinate sets. This is a reproducible, non-binary benchmark for exact string-attractor enumeration in a morphic class previously treated mainly through attractor-size existence theorems. It also records the breakdown of naive stage-to-stage doubling between the ninth and tenth morphism iterates, motivating an as-yet-unproved general recurrence.
