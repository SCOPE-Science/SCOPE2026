# Scientific assessment

## Correctness

The factor support antichain has twelve members. Testing the specified ten instead leaves exactly the same 416 triples, by finite signature-class counting and inclusion of the solution sets. Each obstruction is rejected by at most one minimal support. As every factor support contains a minimal one, a factor cannot reject two of the specified obstructions. This proves that fewer than ten factor predicates cannot suffice on triples.

## Relation to existing results

Resultary record `1c390833d969` already lists all 416 minimum triples and reports twelve inclusion-minimal masks. Their set is not newly enumerated here. The prior antichain reduction does not determine how many of those masks can be dropped when the domain consists only of triples, and it does not give the obstruction argument against arbitrary original factors. Published Tribonacci and episturmian size bounds likewise answer a different question.

## Scientific value and limitations

The minimum test size is a finite invariant at a canonical substitution stage. It quantifies a difference between inclusion-minimal supports and predicates indispensable on the triple domain. The exact bound gives a short usable test and establishes that the compression cannot be improved within this testing model. No claim is made for other stages, larger candidate sets, or general decision procedures.
