# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The defining equations split into one residue commutator and independent first-order commutators. Nonscalar residue matrices are cyclic in dimension two, yielding exactly \(q^{2d+2}\) centralizing matrices. Scalar residue matrices reduce to the common commutant of a \(d\)-tuple in \(M_2(\mathbb F_q)\), whose possible dimensions are precisely \(1\), \(2\), and \(4\). Uniqueness of the two-dimensional commutative subalgebra generated by any nonscalar matrix yields the exact counts without overlaps. The four counts sum to the total matrix-ring size; independently implemented coefficientwise finite-ring commutators verify three complete small cases and the direct pair census. The asymptotic follows by comparing powers of \(q\).

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence
Xi–Zhang (arXiv:2012.11089v1) treat cellularity and Frobenius extensions of matrix centralizers, not these counting fibers. Prasad–Singla–Spallone (arXiv:1212.6157, Section 10) explicitly introduce the nonprincipal ring used here but stop at a general orbit framework and do not give its element-centralizer-size distribution. Shumyatsky–Vannacci (arXiv:2307.12633v1) provide a general structural commuting-probability bound without exact values. The one-generator principal case is outside the claimed novel \(d\ge2\) scope. A possible unknown closed formula for common commutants of finite-field matrix tuples remains a genuine priority risk; search absence is not proof of absolute novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The embedding dimension of a square-zero radical is a basic structural parameter; the complete four-level centralizer-size spectrum is a natural intrinsic invariant of an infinite family of nonprincipal matrix rings and refines their commuting probability. The positive fixed-field limit \(q^{-6}\) shows how a growing nilpotent layer can leave persistent commutation via scalar residues, unlike naïve large-ring-size heuristics. The result identifies the new generic one-dimensional joint-commutant stratum absent in the principal case, without relying on an artificial finite cutoff.

## Scientific limitations
The result does not classify conjugacy orbits, and it does not cover arbitrary nonsplit or higher-nilpotence rings. Independent expert audit and global priority certification are absent.
