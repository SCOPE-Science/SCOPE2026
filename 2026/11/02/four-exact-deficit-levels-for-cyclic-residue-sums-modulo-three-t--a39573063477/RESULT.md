# Four exact deficit levels for cyclic residue sums modulo three times a power of two

## Finding
For an integer \(a\ge4\), set \(m=3\cdot2^a\), \(P=2^{a-1}\). For any subgroup \(H\) of the unit group modulo \(m\), let \(\langle h\rangle_m\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}\) be the least positive representative and define the centered-residue deficit
\[
D(H)=\frac{m|H|}{2}-\sum_{h\in H}\langle h\rangle_m.
\]
For every \(j\) satisfying \(2\le j\le a-2\), write \(k=2^j\). There are **exactly four cyclic subgroups of order \(k\)**. Their four distinct deficits are
\[
\boxed{\{D(H): H\text{ cyclic},\ |H|=k\}=\begin{cases}
\{P,\,3P-k,\,3P,\,5P-k\},& a+j\text{ even},\\
\{P-k,\,3P-k,\,3P,\,5P\},& a+j\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}}
\]
Every listed deficit is strictly positive. At order two there are exactly seven cyclic subgroups, each of the form \(\{1,h\}\) with \(h^2\equiv1\pmod m\) and \(h\ne1\), and its deficit equals \(m-1-h\). There is one cyclic subgroup of order one; its deficit equals \(m/2-1\). Consequently **the only cyclic subgroup with centered sum is \(\{1,m-1\}\)**.

## Assumptions and scope
The theorem concerns all integers \(a\ge4\), all admissible powers-of-two orders \(k\), and every cyclic subgroup of the indicated unit group; no primality condition is imposed on subgroup generators. The sum uses each group element **once**, represented by an integer between \(1\) and \(m-1\). The central term is \(m|H|/2\). The statement is not about the distinct *cosets* of a given subgroup, nor about all composite moduli.

## Proof
Write \(Q=2^a=2P\), \(M=Q/k=2^{a-j}\) (so \(4\mid M\)), and \(h=k/2\). The Chinese remainder theorem gives
\[
(\mathbb Z/3Q\mathbb Z)^\times\cong (\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z)^\times\times\{\pm1\},\qquad
(\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z)^\times\cong C_2\times C_{2^{a-2}}.
\]
For \(k\ge4\), the projection of any order-\(k\) cyclic subgroup to the first factor still has order \(k\): the second factor has exponent two. Exactly two order-\(k\) cyclic subgroups occur in the first factor. Indeed, the elementary cyclic-factor structure yields exactly twice \(\varphi(k)\) elements of order \(k\), hence two subgroups. Explicitly these are
\[
\begin{aligned}
K_0&=\{1+Mt:0\le t<k\},\\
K_1&=\{1+2Mt:0\le t<h\}\ \sqcup\ \{M(2t+1)-1:0\le t<h\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
All displayed integers lie in \([1,Q)\). The subgroup \(K_0\) is generated by \(1+M\), since \(v_2((1+M)^{2^s}-1)=v_2(M)+s\). The subgroup \(K_1\) is generated by \(M-1\): its square is a principal unit with \(v_2((M-1)^2-1)=v_2(M)+1\), giving the stated order and two disjoint cosets. Their congruence classes modulo four distinguish the two groups.

Each projection \(K_i\) has precisely two subgroup lifts to \((\mathbb Z/3Q\mathbb Z)^\times\), the graphs of the trivial homomorphism and the unique nontrivial homomorphism \(K_i\to\{\pm1\}\). On \(K_0\), the nontrivial homomorphism assigns \((-1)^t\) to \(1+Mt\); on \(K_1\), it assigns \(+1\) to the first displayed block and \(-1\) to the second. This proves the total of four subgroups.

For a representative \(x\in[1,Q)\) and a prescribed sign \(\chi(x)\in\{\pm1\}\) modulo three, its least positive lift to modulus \(3Q\) is \(x+Qr(x)\), where \(r(x)\in\{0,1,2\}\) is the unique solution of
\[
r(x)\equiv (-1)^a(\chi(x)-x)\pmod3.
\]
Put \(\varepsilon=(-1)^a\), \(\delta=(-1)^j\) and \([z]_3\in\{0,1,2\}\) for the least residue of an integer \(z\) modulo three. On \(K_0\), the lift indices in the trivial and nontrivial graphs are, respectively,
\[
r_{0,\mathrm{tr}}(t)=[-\delta t]_3,\qquad
r_{0,\mathrm{sgn}}(t)=[-\delta t+\varepsilon((-1)^t-1)]_3,
\quad 0\le t<k.
\]
On \(K_1\), use \(t=0,\ldots,h-1\) separately for its two blocks. On the first block both graphs have indices \([\delta t]_3\). On the second block, the trivial and nontrivial graphs have indices
\[
r_{1,\mathrm{tr},\mathrm{odd}}(t)=[-\varepsilon-\delta(2t+1)]_3,\qquad
r_{1,\mathrm{sgn},\mathrm{odd}}(t)=[-\delta(2t+1)]_3.
\]
The first two sequences are periodic in \(t\) with periods dividing three and six, respectively; the block sequences for \(K_1\) have period three. Since \(k=2^j\equiv(-1)^j\pmod3\) and \(h=2^{j-1}\equiv-(-1)^j\pmod3\), summing complete residue periods and their final one- or two-term tails gives this **exact** table for \(T=\sum r(x)\):

| projection | trivial graph | nontrivial graph |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(K_0\) | \(k-1\) | \(k-2\) if \(a+j\) even; otherwise \(k\) |
| \(K_1\) | \(k\) if \(a+j\) even; otherwise \(k-2\) | \(k-1\) |

For transparency, this table is also directly reconstructible using the four formulas for \(r\) above without a group-theoretic counting assumption. The sums of the underlying modulo-\(Q\) representatives are
\[
X_0=(k-1)P+k,\qquad X_1=(k-1)P.
\]
For each graph, its sum modulo \(3Q\) as ordinary integers is \(X_i+QT\). The central value is \(mk/2=3Pk\), so substitution yields, in the order \(K_0\) trivial, \(K_0\) nontrivial, \(K_1\) trivial, \(K_1\) nontrivial,
\[
\begin{cases}
(3P-k,\,5P-k,\,P,\,3P),&a+j\text{ even},\\
(3P-k,\,P-k,\,5P,\,3P),&a+j\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
These values are strictly positive and pairwise distinct because \(k\le P/2\).

Every unit modulo \(Q\) satisfying \(x^2\equiv1\pmod Q\), for \(a\ge4\), belongs to \(\{1,Q-1,1+Q/2,Q/2-1\}\). Combining with both signs modulo three gives eight square roots of one modulo \(m\), including the identity. The seven other roots generate all the order-two cyclic subgroups; for \(H=\{1,h\}\),
\[
D(H)=m-(1+h)=m-1-h\ge0,
\]
with equality only at \(h=m-1\). The singleton subgroup has deficit \(m/2-1>0\). This completes the proof for every possible cyclic order.

## Verification
The companion standalone integer-arithmetic script `verify.py` constructs the four predicted graphs for all \(a=4,\ldots,12\) and every \(j\), checks their closure, exact sizes, and residue-sum formulas, and **independently** enumerates all cyclic subgroups of the full unit group for \(a=4,\ldots,9\), comparing every resulting deficit and the uniqueness of the centered subgroup. It reports `VERIFY_OK`; finite verification is supplementary to the unrestricted proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Ma, Merca and Zhang (2026, arXiv:2610.29608v1) study centered sums of cyclic subgroups specifically modulo products of two **distinct odd primes**, with general character criteria and classifications at small indices. Their definition of centered residue sums and the pairing by \(-1\) are adopted here; neither is claimed to be new. Their published domain excludes the conductor \(3\cdot2^a\), and the manuscript does not give this four-valued spectrum for arbitrary powers-of-two cyclic orders.

Pomerance and Ulmer (2012/2013, arXiv:1204.6705) instead require *every coset* to have equal cardinalities in the two half-intervals; their character criterion and results for order-two balanced subgroups do not give a numerical sum of least representatives for these four subgroup lifts. Girstmair (2026) treats deviation vectors for power residues modulo a **prime**, especially quadratic and sixth-power residues. Girstmair (1997) provides the broad framework of residue-distribution vectors for arbitrary moduli, but the accessible abstract alone cannot exclude an unlocated specialization or implicit consequence; that priority risk remains acknowledged. The result here is the explicit infinite-tower arithmetic classification rather than a general character expansion or an elementary sufficient condition for balancing.

## Limitations
The theorem does not give sums over all *cosets*, nor a formula for arbitrary composite moduli. A survey of indexed and accessible sources cannot establish global publication priority; one pertinent 1997 article was not available in full text. An exact class-number characterization is not claimed. Independent mathematical audit has not occurred.
