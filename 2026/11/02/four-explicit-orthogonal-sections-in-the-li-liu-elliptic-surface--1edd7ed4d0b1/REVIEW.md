# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The asserted sections satisfy the Weierstrass equation identically since \(a_i^3+a_i+1=0\). The cubic's discriminant \(-31\) makes \(0,a_1,a_2,a_3\) distinct, excluding all finite-base pairwise intersections. At the unique ramified infinity point, the minimal model has smooth fiber \(Y^2=X^3+X\); the sections' \(Y\)-slopes \(0,b_1,b_2,b_3\) are different. Hence each distinct pair meets with multiplicity one exactly there and all four avoid the zero section. Li–Liu's verified twelve irreducible \(I_1\) fibers and \(\chi=1\), together with Shioda's standard height formula, give Gram matrix \(2I_4\). The rational antecedent has \(I_0^*+6I_1\); the Oguiso–Shioda table and degree-two height scaling give the index-two claim. The accompanying symbolic script passed its exact polynomial checks. The script is not substituted for the geometric proof or the cited classification.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The Li–Liu construction and its single non-torsion section are explicit prior results, not contributions here. Their relevant proof sections in arXiv:2605.27926v1 and v2 were read and do not give the other three coordinates or the four-section incidence/height calculation. Oguiso–Shioda's 1991 Main Theorem (case \(D_4\), row 9) already implies abstract rank four on the rational antecedent, and consequently a rank-four lower bound after quadratic base change; these rank-existence facts cannot be claimed as new. The surviving specific contribution is the concrete formula for three further sections with a complete transverse incidence proof valid over every prescribed hyperelliptic base, identifying a natural orthogonal frame and its exact rational index. Directed literature and research-record searches found no equivalent coordinate-level statement. An unindexed older example or reparametrization remains a priority risk, not excluded by absence of search hits.

## Value — PASS

The 2026 construction answers the existence of a non-torsion section on surfaces with genus-\(g\) \((-1)\)-curves; here the explicit, pairwise orthogonal rank-four frame provides constructive generators of a large subgroup without computing the entire Mordell–Weil group. The common smooth-fiber intersection and the exact index-two relation to the classical rational \(D_4^*\) lattice explain how a ramified base change creates this geometry. Both the coordinate formulas and their incidence can be reused in studying specializations, sections, and negative curves across all genera; the significance is not a mere restatement of an abstract rank classification.
