# Four Frobenius types in a two-parameter genus-four Jacobian-square family

## Finding

Let \(K=\mathbb F_q\) have odd cardinality. Consider the two-parameter hyperelliptic genus-four family displayed in Bruin--Kulkarni, Example 7.3:
\[
X_{u,v}:\quad y^2=(x^2+1)G_{u,v}(x),\qquad
G_{u,v}(x)=vx^8+4(u+v)x^6+(8u+6v+16)x^4+4(u+v)x^2+v.
\]
The precise smooth coefficient domain is
\[
\mathcal U_q=\{(u,v)\in K^2:v(u^2-4v)(1+u+v)\ne0\},
\qquad |\mathcal U_q|=q^2-3q+3.
\]
For \((u,v)\in\mathcal U_q\), all ten geometric Weierstrass points are distinct. Put \(\chi=\chi_q\) for the quadratic character, \(\epsilon=\chi(-1)\), and
\[
g=\frac{q-4-\epsilon}{4}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\qquad
B=\begin{cases}1^2,&\epsilon=1,\\2,&\epsilon=-1.\end{cases}
\]
Cycle notation such as \(1^4 2^2\) records lengths and multiplicities of the \(q\)-power Frobenius orbits; juxtaposition with \(B\) means adjoining the independent two-point block. Every member has **exactly one of the following four types**, with the listed numbers of distinct *coefficient pairs*, not isomorphism classes:

| Type | Frobenius cycles on ten points | Number of pairs \((u,v)\) | Number of rational Weierstrass points | Exact splitting-field degree | \(|\operatorname{Jac}(X_{u,v})[2](K)|\) |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| I | \(B\,1^8\) | \(\binom g2\) | \(8+(1+\epsilon)\) | \(1\) if \(\epsilon=1\), else \(2\) | \(256\) if \(\epsilon=1\), else \(128\) |
| II | \(B\,1^4 2^2\) | \(g(q-2-g)\) | \(4+(1+\epsilon)\) | \(2\) | \(64\) if \(\epsilon=1\), else \(32\) |
| III | \(B\,2^4\) | \(\binom{q-2-g}{2}+\frac{(q-1)(q-3)}8\) | \(1+\epsilon\) | \(2\) | \(16\) |
| IV | \(B\,4^2\) | \(\frac{3(q^2-1)}8\) | \(1+\epsilon\) | \(4\) | \(4\) |

Zero-frequency types in small fields are, of course, absent. Every displayed frequency is a nonnegative integer and the four frequencies add to \(q^2-3q+3\). In particular, a common splitting extension of degree at most \(4\) always exists, and exact degree \(4\) occurs for precisely \(3(q^2-1)/8\) admissible coefficient pairs. For \(q=13\), \((u,v)=(12,1)\) has all ten Weierstrass points rational, with \(|\operatorname{Jac}(X_{u,v})[2](K)|=256\).

More explicitly, let \(t_1,t_2\) be the geometric roots of \(t^2+ut+v\). The *exact* field generated by the Weierstrass coordinates is
\[
K\bigl(i,\sqrt{t_1},\sqrt{1-t_1},\sqrt{t_2},\sqrt{1-t_2}\bigr),\qquad i^2=-1.
\]
The assertion is about the displayed coefficient-marked family; it does not count geometric isomorphism classes.

## Assumptions and scope

Work over any finite field \(K\) of odd characteristic, including nonprime fields. The smoothness condition \(v(u^2-4v)(1+u+v)\ne0\) is necessary and sufficient for this displayed degree-ten model to have ten simple branch roots. The underlying hyperelliptic Jacobian is \((2,2)\)-isogenous to a square of a genus-two Jacobian by Bruin--Kulkarni's original result; the isogeny statement itself is not new here. The new claim classifies the arithmetic of the ten branch points, its Frobenius frequency distribution over the entire marked parameter surface, and the consequent rational two-torsion groups.

## Proof

**1. The branch polynomial and smoothness.** Introduce
\[
P_t(x)=t(x^2+1)^2-4x^2,\qquad H(t)=t^2+ut+v.
\]
If \(t_1,t_2\) are the roots of \(H\), a direct multiplication gives
\[
G_{u,v}(x)=P_{t_1}(x)P_{t_2}(x)
=v(x^2+1)^4+4ux^2(x^2+1)^2+16x^4.
\]
If \(t\ne0,1\), the four roots of \(P_t\) are distinct and have the form
\[
x=\frac{1+\delta\sqrt{1-t}}{\eta\sqrt t},\qquad
\delta,\eta\in\{+1,-1\}.
\]
Conversely, for these roots \(x\), the value \(t\) is recovered by \(t=4x^2/(x^2+1)^2\). Hence roots belonging to different \(t\)'s cannot overlap. The remaining roots \(x=\pm i\), with \(i^2=-1\), are distinct from them because \(G_{u,v}(\pm i)=16\ne0\) after division by \((\pm i)^4=1\). Thus the degree-ten polynomial is separable exactly when \(t_1,t_2\) are distinct, nonzero, and not equal to \(1\). These conditions are respectively \(u^2-4v\ne0\), \(v\ne0\), and \(1+u+v\ne0\). The resulting smooth hyperelliptic curve has genus \(4\), and its Weierstrass locus is precisely the ten geometric branch points.

**2. The exact splitting field.** Every displayed root \(x\) determines \(z=2x/(x^2+1)\), satisfying \(z^2=t\), and conversely \(xz-1=\pm\sqrt{1-t}\). Therefore the field generated by all roots of \(P_t\) is exactly \(K(t,\sqrt t,\sqrt{1-t})\); adjoining \(i\) accounts for the other two roots. This proves the splitting-field assertion, not merely an upper bound.

**3. Frobenius over a rational quadratic root.** If \(t\in K\setminus\{0,1\}\), then \(\operatorname{Frob}_q\) acts on the four roots of \(P_t\) by its signs on \(\sqrt t\) and \(\sqrt{1-t}\). If both are in \(K\), it fixes all four roots. Otherwise at least one sign reverses; the nontrivial order-two translation of the four sign pairs is fixed-point-free and has cycle type \(2^2\). The roots \(\pm i\) contribute \(B\).

**4. Frobenius over an irreducible quadratic.** If \(H\) is irreducible over \(K\), Frobenius swaps its roots \(t,t^q\) in \(K_2=\mathbb F_{q^2}\). Over \(K_2\), the two character tests for the four roots of \(P_t\) are the characters of \(t\) and \(1-t\), whose norms to \(K\) are, respectively,
\[
N_{K_2/K}(t)=v,\qquad N_{K_2/K}(1-t)=1+u+v.
\]
Since \(\chi_{q^2}(z)=\chi_q(N_{K_2/K}z)\), if both \(\chi_q(v)\) and \(\chi_q(1+u+v)\) equal \(1\), then Frobenius squared fixes the eight roots, while Frobenius itself exchanges their two four-element blocks. The type is \(2^4\). Otherwise Frobenius squared acts on each four-element block as a nontrivial fixed-point-free involution, and Frobenius has type \(4^2\) on the eight roots. This exhausts the four types in the statement.

**5. The exact frequencies for split roots.** Let
\[
\mathcal G_q=\{t\in K\setminus\{0,1\}:\chi(t)=\chi(1-t)=1\}.
\]
Using the elementary quadratic character identities
\[
\sum_{t\ne0,1}\chi(t)=\sum_{t\ne0,1}\chi(1-t)=-1,
\qquad \sum_{t\in K}\chi(t(1-t))=-\chi(-1),
\]
one obtains \(|\mathcal G_q|=g=(q-4-\epsilon)/4\). The map sending an unordered pair \(\{t_1,t_2\}\subset K\setminus\{0,1\}\) of distinct elements to the coefficients of \((T-t_1)(T-t_2)\) is bijective onto the admissible parameters with split \(H\). Pairs with two, one, or zero members of \(\mathcal G_q\) occur, respectively, \(\binom g2\), \(g(q-2-g)\), or \(\binom{q-2-g}{2}\) times. These contribute types I, II, or III.

**6. The exact frequencies for irreducible roots.** There are \(q(q-1)/2\) monic irreducible quadratics over \(K\). Each is automatically admissible: it has no root \(0\) or \(1\). By the same character identity over \(K_2\),
\[
|\mathcal G_{q^2}|=\frac{q^2-5}{4}.
\]
Every \(t\in K\setminus\{0,1\}\) belongs to \(\mathcal G_{q^2}\), since every nonzero element of \(K\) is a square in \(K_2\). Thus the number of conjugate pairs \(\{t,t^q\}\) in \(\mathcal G_{q^2}\setminus K\) is
\[
\frac12\left(\frac{q^2-5}{4}-(q-2)\right)=\frac{(q-1)(q-3)}8.
\]
These give type III. The other \(q(q-1)/2-(q-1)(q-3)/8=3(q^2-1)/8\) irreducible quadratics give type IV. Together with Step 5 this proves all four frequencies. The total is \(\binom{q-2}{2}+q(q-1)/2=q^2-3q+3\).

**7. Rational Weierstrass points and two-torsion.** Rational Weierstrass points are exactly the Frobenius-fixed branch points, so their counts follow from the four cycle types. For a genus-four hyperelliptic curve in odd characteristic, the geometric \(2\)-torsion module is the space of even subsets of the ten branch points, modulo the full ten-point subset. A class fixed by Frobenius has an even-subset representative \(E\) satisfying either \(F(E)=E\) or \(F(E)=E^c\). The latter requires all Frobenius cycles to be even and, on each even cycle, alternating membership. Since there are ten points, every such alternating subset would have five members, contradicting evenness. Hence all fixed classes have Frobenius-invariant even representatives. If there are \(c\) Frobenius cycles and at least one odd-length cycle, these invariant classes have dimension \(c-2\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). If all cycles are even, their dimension is \(c-1\). Applying these counts to the four types and both possibilities for \(B\) gives the stated orders of \(\operatorname{Jac}(X_{u,v})[2](K)\). Finally, the least common multiple of the orbit lengths is exactly the extension degree needed to split all branch points, establishing the last column about splitting fields.

## Verification

The standalone standard-library checker `verify_bruin_kulkarni.py` evaluates the **original degree-ten model**, tests squarefreeness, computes the genuine Frobenius cycle structures by polynomial gcds with \(x^{q^d}-x\) for \(d=1,2,4\), compares the counts with all four formulae, and enumerates invariant even subsets modulo complementation for the two-torsion calculation. Exhaustive prime-field checks were completed for \(q=3,5,7,11,13,17,19,23\), comprising \(1{,}282\) admissible coefficient pairs. Independent direct rational-root and parameter-frequency checks over \(\mathbb F_9\) and \(\mathbb F_{25}\) covered another \(610\) admissible pairs. All checks passed. The proof above, rather than the finite tests, establishes the assertion for every odd prime power.

## Relationship to prior work

Bruin and Kulkarni, *On (2,2)-decomposable genus 4 Jacobians* (first public arXiv version September 5, 2023), Section 7.1, Example 7.3, give the entire two-parameter model and prove the associated Jacobian-square isogeny. Those two facts are prior work. The present finding concerns the additional complete arithmetic Frobenius classification, exact frequency formulas across the smooth coefficient surface, exact Weierstrass splitting fields, and rational two-torsion. The accessible September 2023 preprint and September 2025 open-access journal presentation of Example 7.3 were examined for those claims; neither states this four-type frequency classification. The work uses classical finite-field quadratic-character sums and the standard even-subset model for hyperelliptic two-torsion, so the result should not be interpreted as novelty of those individual tools.

## Limitations

The frequencies are for literal coefficient pairs \((u,v)\), not isomorphism classes or moduli-stack points. The result is limited to finite fields of odd characteristic, the smooth locus of the displayed hyperelliptic family, and Frobenius on the branch divisor; it does not determine the full zeta functions or Newton polygons of these curves. The Jacobian-square isogeny is cited, not reproved here. A prior calculation of these exact frequencies in unindexed literature cannot be excluded.
