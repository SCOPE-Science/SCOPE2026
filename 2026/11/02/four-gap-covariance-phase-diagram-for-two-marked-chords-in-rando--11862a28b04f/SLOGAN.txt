Conditioning on two marked chords in a uniform random diagram produces an exact four-arc covariance sign law for their crossing degrees: noncrossing chords have nonnegative covariance, while crossing chords can have either sign. The sign is determined by the squared imbalance \((ad-bc)^2\) against an explicit finite-size correction; with all four intervening gaps nonempty, the first positive crossing case uses six chords.
