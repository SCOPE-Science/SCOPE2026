# Four independent local choices in minimum one-limited dominating sets of ternary Sierpiński graphs

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge 3\), let \(M_n\) be the number of minimum one-limited dominating subsets of the ternary Sierpiński graph \(S(n,3)\). There is an explicit injective construction of \(4^{3^{n-3}}\) such subsets. In particular,
\[
 M_n\ge 4^{3^{n-3}},\qquad \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log M_n}{3^n}\ge\frac{\log4}{27}.
\]
Each construction chooses independently one of four optimal configurations on each induced 27-vertex copy \(S(3,3)\) corresponding to a fixed prefix of length \(n-3\). Two choices arise from the two perfect matchings of a six-cycle; two arise from the two orientations of a directed three-cycle and have a different local pattern of occupied triangles. Thus the construction includes genuinely non-balanced minimizers, not only configurations with exactly two selected vertices per terminal triangle.

## Assumptions and scope
Vertices of \(S(n,3)\) are words \(u=(u_1,\ldots,u_n)\in\{0,1,2\}^n\). Distinct words \(u,v\) are adjacent if there is an index \(h\) such that their prefixes through \(h-1\) coincide, \(u_h\ne v_h\), and for all \(j>h\), \(u_j=v_h\) and \(v_j=u_h\). A set \(D\) is one-limited dominating if every vertex outside \(D\) has a neighbor in \(D\), and each vertex in \(D\) has at most one neighbor outside \(D\). The last-coordinate triples with a common prefix of length \(n-1\) induce disjoint triangles. Vertex labels and prefixes are fixed; the count is of labelled subsets, without quotienting by automorphisms.

## Proof
**Optimal cardinality, independently.** Partition \(S(n,3)\) into the \(3^{n-2}\) nine-vertex blocks with common prefix of length \(n-2\). In each block, three disjoint last-coordinate triangles form an \(S(2,3)\). In a triangle exactly one selected vertex is impossible: that vertex would have two neighbors outside the set. Hence each triangle contains either zero or at least two selected vertices. If all three are nonempty, the block contains at least six selected vertices. If a triangle \(T\) is empty, each of the two vertices of \(T\) on an intertriangle edge has no selected neighbor in \(T\), and must be dominated across its intertriangle edge; the corresponding endpoints in the other two triangles are therefore selected. A selected endpoint adjacent to \(T\) already has an omitted neighbor and consequently cannot have an omitted neighbor within its own triangle, making each of those other triangles fully selected. Thus every block has at least six selected vertices. Summing disjoint blocks gives \(|D|\ge 6\cdot3^{n-2}=2\cdot3^{n-1}\).

**Two balanced local states.** In \(S(3,3)\), index the nine terminal triangles by their length-two prefixes \((a,b)\). Pick exactly two vertices in every triangle, with the missing vertex of each of the three diagonal triangles \((a,a)\) being the extremal vertex \((a,a,a)\). An intertriangle edge is compatible with one-limited domination exactly when both its endpoints are selected or both are omitted. Every non-diagonal terminal triangle has one vertex that is incident with an intertriangle edge corresponding to each neighbor of \((a,b)\) in \(S(2,3)\). The three diagonal triangles may omit their extremal vertices (which have no such edge). The six non-diagonal vertices of \(S(2,3)\) induce a six-cycle. Selecting one of its two perfect matchings identifies the omitted endpoints of its matched intertriangle edges, yielding exactly two configurations: all three extremal vertices omitted, and exactly 18 selected vertices. In each, omitted vertices are dominated by their two selected partners within a terminal triangle, and selected vertices have exactly one omitted neighbor within their terminal triangle and none across an intertriangle edge.

**Two unbalanced local states.** For each \(s\in\{1,2\}\), take all three vertices in each terminal triangle indexed \((a,b)\) except when \(b\equiv a+s\pmod3\), in which case take none. The three empty triangles each have two full neighboring triangles within their first-coordinate group and a full neighboring triangle reached by their intergroup swap edge: \((a,a+s)\) swaps to \((a+s,a)\), which is not empty. Every omitted vertex is therefore dominated. Each selected vertex is in a full terminal triangle and has at most one neighbor outside that triangle, hence at most one outside the selected set. The three extremal vertices are all selected; each state contains 18 vertices. The two states are distinct from each other and from the two balanced states.

**Independent gluing.** Fix the first \(n-3\) coordinates. The resulting \(3^{n-3}\) vertex-disjoint induced blocks are copies of \(S(3,3)\). An edge joining two such blocks can meet a block only at one of its three extremal vertices. In a balanced local state each extremal vertex is omitted, but it has two selected neighbors inside its terminal triangle and therefore remains dominated regardless of the other block. In an unbalanced local state each extremal vertex is selected and lies in a full terminal triangle, so it has no omitted neighbor inside its block and can tolerate its single cross-block neighbor being omitted. Other vertices have no cross-block edges. Therefore any independent choice of four local states is a one-limited dominating set. Its size is \(18\cdot3^{n-3}=2\cdot3^{n-1}\), meeting the lower bound. Distinct local choices yield distinct labelled global subsets. Taking logarithms proves the asserted entropy lower bound.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` constructs graph edges directly from the word definition and checks the original domination and one-limited constraints. It independently identifies the two balanced boundary states by literal enumeration of terminal-triangle omissions, verifies both unbalanced orientations, exhausts all four local choices for \(n=3\) and all 64 products for \(n=4\), and verifies seeded collections for \(n=5,6\). It also cross-checks related finite perfect-matchings using a separate matching recurrence. Invoke `python3 verify.py` with standard Python 3. All checks return `FOUR_STATE_VERIFY_OK`. These finite tests are checks, not a proof of the infinite construction.

## Relationship to prior work
Božović, *On k-limited domination: complexity and Sierpiński graphs*, arXiv:2610.01584v1 (first public 2026-10-01), determines the minimum one-limited domination cardinality for Sierpiński graphs and supplies a construction. The present result supplies a separate multiplicity statement and a new four-state block construction with nonbalanced optimal choices; the optimal-cardinality conclusion itself is not claimed as new. The 2019 dimer-covering literature on Tower of Hanoi graphs studies matchings and may cover the balanced portion; the nonbalanced directed-cycle states and their free inter-block products are separate phenomena. Its full text was not obtained, leaving historical-priority uncertainty. Teufl and Wagner's perfect-matching enumeration concerns a different, vertex-amalgamated Sierpiński gasket graph, not this word-labelled Sierpiński graph.

## Limitations
This is a lower bound on the number of minimum solutions, not an exact count or classification of every optimal set. Only ternary graphs and integer \(n\ge3\) are treated. The historical-priority search cannot exclude an equivalent result hidden in inaccessible or differently indexed literature. No independent review has been performed.
