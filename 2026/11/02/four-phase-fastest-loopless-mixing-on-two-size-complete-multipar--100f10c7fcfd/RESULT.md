# Four-phase fastest loopless mixing on two-size complete multipartite graphs

## Finding

Let \(p,q\ge2\) and \(a,b\ge1\) be integers. Consider the complete multipartite graph with \(p\) independent parts of size \(a\) and \(q\) independent parts of size \(b\). Among **all** real symmetric, entrywise nonnegative, row-stochastic transition matrices supported on its edges, with every diagonal entry exactly zero, the minimum second-largest eigenvalue modulus is given by the following four-phase formula. Put \(r=pa/(qb)>0\). Then

\[
\mu_*(r)=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{1-r}{q-1}, &0<r\le 1/q,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{1}{q-1+qr}, &1/q\le r\le p/q,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{r}{p+(p-1)r}, &p/q\le r\le p,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{r-1}{r(p-1)}, &r\ge p.
\end{cases}
\]

All expressions agree at their shared boundaries. Define

\[
x_*=\begin{cases}
1, &0<r\le 1/q,\\[3pt]
\dfrac{q}{q-1+qr}, &1/q\le r\le p/q,\\[5pt]
\dfrac{p}{p+(p-1)r}, &p/q\le r\le p,\\[5pt]
\dfrac1r, &r\ge p.
\end{cases}
\]

An attaining symmetric stochastic matrix assigns the probability

\[
w_{AA}=\frac{1-x_*}{(p-1)a},\qquad
w_{AB}=\frac{x_*}{qb},\qquad
w_{BB}=\frac{1-rx_*}{(q-1)b}
\]

to each edge between distinct size-\(a\) parts, between size-\(a\) and size-\(b\) parts, and between distinct size-\(b\) parts, respectively. Every other entry is zero. This optimizer is **unique among matrices invariant under permutations within individual parts and permutations of parts of each size**, and the optimal value is global without imposing such invariance.

There are sharp support transitions: \(w_{AA}=0\) exactly when \(pa\le b\), and \(w_{BB}=0\) exactly when \(a\ge qb\). For fixed \(p,q\), the optimal modulus depends on \(a,b\) only through their ratio and is strictly minimized at \(a=b\), where \(\mu_*=1/(p+q-1)\) and all allowed edges have common weight \(1/((p+q-1)a)\).

## Assumptions and scope

The underlying graph has \(N=pa+qb\) vertices. Distinct vertices are adjacent if and only if they lie in distinct multipartite classes. All probabilities are nonnegative; an allowed edge may receive probability zero. No holding probability is allowed: every diagonal entry equals zero. A transition matrix is real and symmetric, and therefore the uniform distribution is stationary. Write \(J_N\) for the all-ones matrix. Its second-largest eigenvalue modulus is \(\mu(P)=\|P-J_N/N\|_2\), the operator norm on the subspace perpendicular to constants. This definition counts repeated unit eigenvalues correctly.

Only the symmetric, zero-holding fastest-mixing criterion is optimized. No claim is made for nonreversible chains, relaxation-time objectives with different weights, optimal finite-time total-variation mixing, or strict positivity of every allowed edge. Global uniqueness of the full transition matrix is not asserted.

## Proof

**1. A global symmetry reduction.** Let \(\Gamma\) be the group generated by arbitrary permutations inside each multipartite class and permutations of the \(p\) size-\(a\) classes and the \(q\) size-\(b\) classes. For every feasible \(P\), average \(UPU^T\) over all permutation matrices \(U\) from \(\Gamma\). The average \(\overline P\) remains feasible and has three edge weights. Since every \(U\) fixes \(J_N\), convexity and unitary invariance of the operator norm give

\[
\mu(\overline P)=\left\|\overline P-J_N/N\right\|_2
\le\frac1{|\Gamma|}\sum_{U\in\Gamma}
\left\|UPU^T-J_N/N\right\|_2=\mu(P).
\]

Thus optimizing within this invariant family proves the unrestricted optimal value.

**2. Exact one-parameter spectrum.** Write the three edge weights as \(w_{AA},w_{AB},w_{BB}\). Stochasticity at the two vertex types is equivalent to

\[
(p-1)a w_{AA}+qb w_{AB}=1,\qquad
(q-1)b w_{BB}+pa w_{AB}=1.
\]

Set \(x=qb w_{AB}\) and \(r=pa/(qb)\). Nonnegativity is equivalent to \(0\le x\le c=\min\{1,1/r\}\), and the three weights are precisely the displayed functions of \(x\).

The invariant matrix splits into the constant eigenspace (eigenvalue \(1\)); the \(p-1\) contrasts among size-\(a\) classes (eigenvalue \(-(1-x)/(p-1)\)); the \(q-1\) contrasts among size-\(b\) classes (eigenvalue \(-(1-rx)/(q-1)\)); one aggregate contrast between the two types (eigenvalue \(1-(1+r)x\)); and \(p(a-1)+q(b-1)\) within-class contrasts (eigenvalue zero). These multiplicities add to \(N\) and give a full eigenbasis, including when some class size equals one. Consequently

\[
\mu(x)=\max\left\{\frac{1-x}{p-1},\frac{1-rx}{q-1},\left|1-(1+r)x\right|\right\}.
\]

**3. Solve the minimax problem.** On \(0\le x\le1/(1+r)\), all three displayed nonnegative affine branches decrease, so the objective decreases. On \(1/(1+r)\le x\le c\), put

\[
A(x)=\frac{1-x}{p-1},\quad B(x)=\frac{1-rx}{q-1},\quad D(x)=(1+r)x-1.
\]

The first two functions decrease strictly and the third increases strictly. The intersections of \(D\) with \(A\) and \(B\) occur at

\[
x_A=\frac{p}{p+(p-1)r},\qquad
x_B=\frac{q}{q-1+qr},
\]

respectively. Both are strictly greater than \(1/(1+r)\). The unique minimizer over the full feasible interval is therefore

\[
x_*=\min\left\{c,\max\{x_A,x_B\}\right\}.
\]

Cross-multiplication gives \(x_A\ge x_B\) if and only if \(r\ge p/q\), equivalently \(a\ge b\). Moreover \(x_B\ge1\) exactly when \(r\le1/q\), while \(x_A\ge1/r\) exactly when \(r\ge p\). These observations yield the four stated phases without omitted parameter ranges. Substituting \(x_*\) into the exact spectrum gives the four formulas for \(\mu_*\). Strictly decreasing and increasing branches establish uniqueness within the invariant family.

**4. Boundary, support, and balance.** Both weight formulas have nonnegative numerators throughout the feasible domain. The equality conditions \(x_*=1\) and \(rx_*=1\) are respectively \(pa\le b\) and \(a\ge qb\), establishing the two support-deletion thresholds. Within each of the first two phases \(\mu_*\) decreases strictly with \(r\); within each of the last two it increases strictly. The unique minimum occurs at \(r=p/q\), exactly \(a=b\), and substitution gives \(1/(p+q-1)\). Every formula is a function of \(p,q,r\), proving invariance under common integer scaling of \(a,b\).

## Verification

The accompanying `verify_two_size_multipartite.py` is executable with Python 3 and the standard library only:

```sh
python3 verify_two_size_multipartite.py
```

It constructs full exact-rational transition matrices, tests the claimed eigenspaces directly, verifies all row sums and support boundaries, and compares the claimed minimum to a rational grid of feasible competitors. Its completed run passed **576,512 exact-rational assertions** across 4,900 parameter combinations, including 144 full-matrix eigenbasis cases. This finite computational evidence is supplementary; the preceding symmetry reduction and minimax proof establish the theorem for all stated integers.

## Relationship to prior work

Boyd, Diaconis and Xiao (2004) formulated fastest symmetric mixing as a convex semidefinite program with self-loops allowed. Boyd, Diaconis, Parrilo and Xiao (2007), Section 5.3.2, provided a general block-diagonal reduction for complete multipartite graphs and left the resulting semidefinite optimization to be solved; their displayed matrices retain diagonal holding probabilities. The present result imposes the qualitatively different zero-diagonal constraint and solves a two-size family completely, including explicit support-deletion thresholds and four phase boundaries.

Jafarizadeh (2010) studied several layered \(K\)-partite networks, including chains in which only neighboring multipartite classes communicate and each layer has the same size. Those topologies and feasible transition sets differ from the complete multipartite, two-size, zero-holding family here. No claim of first discovery is made beyond the available checked literature.

## Limitations

The classification requires at least two parts of each size type. With one type having only one part, the contrast multiplicities and optimization change. Allowed edges may carry zero probability in the outer phases, so an additional strict-positive-edges constraint changes attainment. Symmetric stationary-uniform chains only are considered. A literature search cannot exclude an equivalent specialized result in material not inspected; the review is not independent mathematical validation.
