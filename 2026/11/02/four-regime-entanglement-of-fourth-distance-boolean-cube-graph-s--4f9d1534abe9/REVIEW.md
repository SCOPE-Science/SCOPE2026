# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim has unambiguous domain: all integer dimensions \(d\ge4\), pure qubit graph states of the entire fourth-distance Boolean Hamming relation, and exactly the coordinate-half cuts. The spectrum follows directly from the character sum over the graph-state coefficient matrix, reducing the claim to a binary cut-matrix rank. The full cut matrix is a distance-three convolution on \(\mathbb F_2^{d-1}\), converted without approximation to multiplication in a truncated square-zero polynomial algebra. Exact expansion and characteristic-two symplectic normal forms give all four modulo-four cases. The key rank lemma is independently established using the square-zero multiplication differential and elementary tensor homology; the degree-three mixed case is explicitly reduced to a tensor product. Computations are consistent with, but not replacements for, the proof.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence, subject to explicit risk

The general graph-state rank rule (Hein–Eisert–Briegel, 2003) is not new. Ordinary hypercube graph-state calculations (Hashizume et al., 2021) address Hamming-distance-one edges; Hamming-graph free-fermion results (Parez et al., 2022) address a different quantum state and correlation-matrix spectrum. The algebraic setting of Yoshikawa (2004) is broader and potentially relevant, but only its abstract was available; it does not itself display an exact distance-three binary adjacency rank formula. A published distance-two Boolean-cube parity-automaton result is also adjacent but concerns a different operator. The exact all-dimension fourth-shell coordinate-cut classification and its flat spectrum were not identified in the examined claims or sources, but historical priority is provisional. The uninspected modular-algebra source and further modular rank literature are residual risks.

## Value — PASS

A coordinate cut is canonical under the full hypercube coordinate symmetry, and fourth-distance interactions are a natural relation in the Hamming association scheme. The computation identifies four distinct arithmetic entanglement regimes, including a maximally mixed half-system class and large systematic deficiencies across the remaining classes. It gives the exact full reduced spectrum for a family of \(2^d\)-qubit network states without diagonalizing their exponentially larger density matrices. The nontrivial structure lies in the modular convolution rank classification, not in restating a known generic graph-state fact.
