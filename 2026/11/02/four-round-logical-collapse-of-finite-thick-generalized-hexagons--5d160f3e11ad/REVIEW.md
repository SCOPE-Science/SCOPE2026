# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is confined to finite thick generalized hexagons and octagons in the named point–line incidence language, with an explicit comparison to generalized quadrangles. The proof reconstructs the rank-one triple extension diagrams from short distances and absence of short cycles, the fourteen rank-two pair profiles from unique geodesics and off-path branching, and the shared rank-four empty type from their common rank-three singleton spectra. The separating sentence has quantifier rank exactly four and detects point–line pairs farther than three steps, which occur in hexagons and octagons but not quadrangles. The standalone verifier independently checks exact finite extension diagrams on two large covers and the rank-three boundary against a finite symplectic quadrangle. Finite experiments are not substituted for the general graph argument.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

The 2008 Exoo–Jajcay survey supplies graph-theoretic background rather than first-order type classification. Bamberg et al. (arXiv:1410.3423v1) classify consequences of point-primitivity in generalized hexagons and octagons, not low-rank logical equivalence. Existing logical results about finite generalized quadrangles do not yield equivalence between hexagons and octagons and cannot imply the rank-four merger across these two different gonalities. Searches for equivalent formulations, named two-sorted incidence theories, high-girth graph Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé equivalence, and broader logical coverage identified no theorem that directly subsumes the present claim. It remains possible that an unindexed general locality theorem gives the statement as a corollary; this residual risk does not amount to found strong coverage.

## Value — PASS

The exact fourteen-profile classification and the sharp three-to-four round boundary measure the expressive visibility of gonality in a natural and historically central geometric family. The result is uniform in both unequal incidence parameters, requires no symmetry or classicality hypotheses, and identifies a complete logical collapse between hexagons and octagons that is not inherited from the existing quadrangle theorem. The finite-rank invariant is useful when interpreting bounded-depth formulas across finite incidence geometries.

## Limits

No rank-five completeness is claimed; no extension to all high-girth graphs without additional hypotheses is asserted. Exact coverage of older or unindexed literature is not guaranteed, and finite software tests provide non-independent corroboration only.
