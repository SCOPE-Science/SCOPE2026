# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The proof exhausts all equality and ternary-collinearity patterns obtainable by moving one of four pebbles while at most three remain. For three distinct collinear stationary points, the only exceptional support is an unused same-line point, whose count is \(q-2\). For a noncollinear stationary triple, each side has \(q-1\) fresh points and the ambient plane has \((q-1)^2\) points off all three sides. These counts hold for arbitrary finite projective planes, including non-Desarguesian ones, and extend to all higher dimensions by taking the generated plane. The four-pebble induction, the four class separators, and the sharp smallest-representative sizes are explicitly proved. The finite checker independently confirms the complete support patterns on ten small examples; its finite scope is not substituted for the proof.

## Originality

**PASS, qualified.** Grohe and Otto's 2012/2015 paper treats the general equivalence between variable-bounded logic and pebble games, not this exact collinearity classification; a direct comparison of its finite-variable foundations found no projective-collinearity claim. Bartoli and collaborators' 2015/2016 paper concerns saturating subsets of arbitrary projective planes and projective spaces rather than first-order logical equivalence. Searches for equivalences of finite projective collinearity geometries in four-variable logic and their formulation in pebble-game language did not locate a stronger statement. General results on interpretations or small-variable matroid theories might contain an implicit implication, so historical priority is not asserted as complete.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** The finding is a complete classification for a natural geometric signature at an unrestricted logical depth. It demonstrates loss of both field-order information and projective dimension, uniformly including non-Desarguesian planes, and proves the precise escalation from one to two to four logical classes at variable widths two through four. Four-variable equivalence survives even when the two geometries have different line sizes or dimensions, while the five-variable skew-line sentence separates dimensions; the representative bound of thirteen points is sharp. The result is more than an isolated finite computation or a restatement of a general pebble-game criterion because the geometric extension-support table and optimal representatives are established for the entire family.

## Literature and limitations

Sources: M. Grohe and M. Otto, *Pebble Games and Linear Equations*, arXiv:1204.1990, §2; D. Bartoli et al., *On upper bounds on the smallest size of a saturating set in a projective plane*, arXiv:1505.01426, §§1–2, 5. The theorem does not apply to arbitrary linear spaces, richer signatures, counting logic, or general unrestricted first-order equivalence. The finite-field checker does not enumerate non-Desarguesian planes; the proof instead uses axioms shared by them. An implicit stronger prior theorem remains a residual priority risk.
