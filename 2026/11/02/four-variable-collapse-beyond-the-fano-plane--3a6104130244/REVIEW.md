# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** For any finite projective plane of order \(q\ge3\), the exact counts of extensions of each enhanced three-object diagram depend on \(q\) but have support independent of \(q\). The proof checks three collinear/noncollinear points and the three two-point/one-line configurations, then invokes point-line duality and handles repeated pebbles separately. The resulting permanent four-pebble strategy preserves point/line sorts, equality, incidence, collinearity and concurrency at every round; four-variable equivalence therefore holds at unbounded depth. The three-line covering sentence distinguishes the Fano order. Two programs independently checked the critical support and symbolic counts for coordinate-plane examples; these experiments do not replace the proof.

## Originality
**PASS with residual literature risk.** The substantive unrestricted-depth \(\mathrm{FO}^{4}\) equivalence for all finite projective planes of orders at least three is not implied by earlier rank-four/rank-five claims or by the three-variable equivalence of symmetric-design incidence structures. Venema's two-sorted modal axiomatization uses modal validity and valuations rather than the ordinary four-variable first-order signature. Grohe–Otto give the general pebble-game theorem, not this specialized extension-support calculation. De Winter–Lazebnik–Verstraëte's full paper treats extremal eight-cycle counts, not logical indistinguishability. A currently unidentified broader statement might imply the result, so historical priority is qualified rather than certified.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** This is a complete unrestricted-depth four-variable spectrum for a natural infinite class that includes different orders and distinct, possibly non-Desarguesian incidence geometries. The sharp transition between the Fano plane and all other orders, and the minimum variable widths needed to separate orders two versus three and three versus four, identify a substantive expressive-power boundary. The content is stronger than a routine fixed-rank cutoff because one back-and-forth invariant works at every quantifier depth.

## Limitations
Only the pure named-sort incidence signature and ordinary counting-free first-order logic are covered. The theorem does not classify five-variable logic, higher-dimensional spaces, arbitrary partial planes or richer signatures. Computational checks examine classical planes only; the proof supplies the arbitrary-plane argument. Relevant literature has been inspected, but an independent review and comprehensive priority determination remain outstanding.
