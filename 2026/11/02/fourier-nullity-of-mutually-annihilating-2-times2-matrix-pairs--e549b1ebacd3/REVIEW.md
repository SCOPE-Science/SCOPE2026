# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem applies to the eight-dimensional additive matrix-pair space over every finite field, with unnormalized Fourier transform and trace pairing. The proof partitions \(XY=YX=0\) into coordinate subspaces and mixed rank-one pairs, evaluates the rank-one transform by double character orthogonality, and reduces Fourier zeros to a count of rank-one points on projective matrix pencils. The Segre quadric has precisely \(2(q+1)\) generator lines, \((q+1)^2q^2/2\) proper secants, and \((q+1)^2(q-1)\) nongenerator tangents. Counting ordered line endpoints yields the stated polynomial and the extra 36 null frequencies at \(q=2\). The checker independently computes raw Fourier coefficients at all frequencies for \(q=2,3\) and at 213 frequencies for \(q=4\), and exhaustively tallies the polynomial criterion for \(q=2,3,4,5\). The infinite-field-order statement is proved algebraically, not extrapolated from code.

## Originality — PASS under bounded comparison
Bondarenko–Gerasimova–Sergeichuk (arXiv:0812.2155, inspected introduction and canonical-form theorem) classify the same pairs up to simultaneous similarity rather than additive characters on the entire solution variety. Huang (arXiv:2110.15566, inspected the main theorem and §3) counts all mutually annihilating pairs but determines only the Fourier coefficient at zero. Britnell–Wildon (arXiv:1001.0811, inspected abstract, introduction, and relevant commuting-type results) classify commuting matrix types, a broader algebraic question that does not itself state the claimed Fourier-zero law. A related zero-composition condition \(XY=0\) is not equivalent to \(XY=YX=0\); the additional reverse equation changes the Fourier transform, so invertibility for the former does not imply the present singular-convolution formula. The determinant-quadric line geometry and the rank-one character sum are pre-existing techniques, explicitly separated from the assessed new conclusion. Targeted searches did not locate a theorem covering the complete zero set and its nullity polynomial; possible implicit coverage in matrix-association or old finite-field character-sum literature remains a residual risk.

## Value — PASS
The bidirectional zero-product variety parametrizes representations of a nodal algebra and is a canonical object in matrix geometry. Its finite-field point counts and simultaneous-similarity classification are known, but neither determines its convolution kernel. The exact Fourier-null criterion, a closed rank polynomial for every finite field, and the geometrically explained extra binary contribution give a natural complete invariant; these are not merely a change of notation or a point-count recomputation. The finding does not require a higher-dimensional extension to be mathematically useful.

## Boundaries
No claim is made about higher matrix sizes, local rings, or independent validation. The exact small-field checker is supplementary; the general proof depends on character orthogonality and the projective quadric count. The most serious residual originality risk is a pre-existing association-scheme theorem implying the same determinant-pencil zero law without using the present wording.
