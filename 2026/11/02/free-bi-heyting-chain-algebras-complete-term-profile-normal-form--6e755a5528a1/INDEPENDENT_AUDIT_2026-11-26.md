## correctness

On a bounded chain, all basic operations select from their arguments and the two endpoints, and each selection is governed by comparisons. Thus the output rank of a term in the finite subchain generated by a tuple depends only on its ordered equality type with endpoints marked. The map to the product of such chains is a homomorphism with kernel the identities of the generating class, hence injective on the free algebra. For a>b we have a→b=b<1, so D(a,b)=1∖(a→b)=1; for a≤b the implication is 1 and D=0. On the Boolean endpoint pair negation by implication into 0 switches the two values. The meet of correct crisp comparisons and their negations recognizes exactly one type, including endpoint ties. Joining these disjoint indicators each met with a representative of the desired output realizes any profile; thus the product map is onto. To count the factors C_{r+2}, choose m interior variables, give each of the k−m remaining labels either endpoint, and partition the interior variables into r nonempty ordered blocks. This gives the stated binomial/Stirling sum, including r=0 when only endpoints occur. For k=1 and k=2 the multiplicities are (2,1) and (4,5,2), hence orders 12 and 62,208. All types occur in C_{k+2}, and the term detecting 0<x_1<⋯<x_k<1 is identically zero on smaller chains but not there. This checks sharpness within single finite-chain tests, not within arbitrary finite algebras or for branching duals.
