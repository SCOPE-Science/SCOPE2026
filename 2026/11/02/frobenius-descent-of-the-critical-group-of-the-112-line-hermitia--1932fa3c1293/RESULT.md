# Frobenius descent of the critical group of the 112-line Hermitian quartic

## Finding

Let \(X\) be the Fermat quartic over \(\mathbb F_3\),
\[
X:\quad X_0^4+X_1^4+X_2^4+X_3^4=0,
\]
and let \(G\) be the graph of its 112 geometric lines, joining two vertices exactly when the lines meet. Its graph critical group is \(K(G)=\operatorname{Div}^0(G)/\operatorname{im}(L)\), where \(L=30I-A\) is the integral graph Laplacian. Let \(\sigma\) be arithmetic Frobenius on these lines (coefficients raised to the third power). Then
\[
\boxed{K(G)/(\sigma-1)K(G)\ \cong\
(\mathbb Z/28\mathbb Z)^{36}\oplus
\mathbb Z/280\mathbb Z\oplus
(\mathbb Z/1120\mathbb Z)^{10}.}
\]
The fixed subgroup \(K(G)^\sigma\) has the *same abstract invariant factors* (the isomorphism between fixed and coinvariant groups is not asserted canonical). Thus both have order \(2^{125}5^{11}7^{47}\) and exponent \(1120\).

The 112 lines consist of eight lines defined over \(\mathbb F_3\) and 52 conjugate pairs. The eight rational lines have intersection graph \(K_{4,4}\). The result is an arithmetic refinement of the abstract critical group; it is not a claim about the Picard group, Brauer group, or Néron–Severi discriminant of \(X\).

## Assumptions and scope

The ground field in the quartic equation is exactly \(\mathbb F_3\). Geometric lines are considered after passage to an algebraic closure and, by the classical 112-line theorem, are all defined over \(\mathbb F_9\). They form a finite configuration, and arithmetic Frobenius induces an involution on its vertex set. Graph divisors and Laplacians are over the **integers**, even though the surface is in characteristic three. No claim is made about a characteristic-zero Fermat quartic, whose line set differs.

Write \(\mathbb F_9=\mathbb F_3[t]/(t^2+1)\) with conjugation \(a+bt\mapsto a-bt\). The quartic is the Hermitian surface associated to
\[
h(v,w)=\sum_{i=0}^3v_i w_i^3.
\]
Its lines correspond to maximal totally isotropic two-dimensional \(\mathbb F_9\)-subspaces. The incidence structure is the generalized quadrangle \(H(3,9)\), of order \((9,3)\); it has 280 rational points and 112 lines. The pairwise-intersection graph \(G\) is the line graph of this incidence structure, or equivalently the point graph of its dual generalized quadrangle.

## Proof

**1. Full geometric line configuration.** Every totally isotropic two-plane for \(h\) is contained in \(X\), because the Hermitian quartic vanishes identically there. Conversely, if a projective line lies on the quartic, its restriction to an \(\mathbb F_9\)-basis \(v,w\) of the corresponding two-space has four coefficients: \(h(v,v)\), \(h(w,w)\), \(h(v,w)\), and its conjugate; hence all vanish. By the known completeness of the 112-line configuration over \(\mathbb F_9\), this is exhaustive. Explicit enumeration produces 280 isotropic projective points, 112 distinct ten-point lines, each meeting exactly 30 other lines. Adjacent line pairs have two common neighbors; disjoint line pairs have ten. Therefore \(G\) is strongly regular with parameters \((112,30,2,10)\). Its adjacency spectrum is
\[
30^1,\quad 2^{90},\quad (-10)^{21},
\]
and its Laplacian spectrum is \(0^1,28^{90},40^{21}\).

**2. Frobenius action.** Cubing the coordinates defines \(\sigma\) on the 112 ten-point subsets. The exact enumeration yields eight fixed lines and 52 two-element orbits. The induced graph on the eight fixed lines is \(K_{4,4}\). Moreover
\[
\operatorname{tr}(\sigma)=8,\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(A\sigma)=32.
\]
If \(u\) and \(v\) are the traces of \(\sigma\) on the adjacency eigenspaces of eigenvalues \(2\) and \(-10\), respectively, then \(u+v=7\) and \(2u-10v=2\). Thus \(u=6\), \(v=1\). The \(\sigma\)-invariant rational vector space has eigenvalue multiplicities \(1,48,11\) for adjacency eigenvalues \(30,2,-10\). In particular, the rational quotient Laplacian has rank 59.

**3. Integral quotient Laplacian.** Let \(O_1,\ldots,O_{60}\) be the eight singleton orbits and the 52 pair orbits. Choose \(r_j\in O_j\). Form the integer matrix
\[
B_{ij}=\sum_{k\in O_i}\bigl(30\mathbf 1_{k=r_j}-A_{k,r_j}\bigr).
\]
This is the matrix of the induced Laplacian on the permutation-module coinvariants \(\mathbb Z^{112}/(\sigma-1)\mathbb Z^{112}\), in the orbit basis. Its left kernel is generated by the all-one row and its right kernel by the vector of orbit sizes \((|O_i|)_{i=1}^{60}\). Those vectors are primitive and their scalar product is 112. The nonzero eigenvalues of \(B\) are \(28\) with multiplicity 48 and \(40\) with multiplicity 11. Consequently the order of the torsion cokernel of \(B\) is
\[
\frac{28^{48}40^{11}}{112}=2^{125}5^{11}7^{47}.
\]
(The formula follows by comparing the product of nonzero eigenvalues with the primitive left/right kernel generators and the gcd of the rank-59 minors.)

**4. Complete integral elementary divisors.** The attached deterministic verifier reconstructs \(B\) using arithmetic in the nine-element field, then performs invertible integral-local row and column operations modulo sufficiently high prime powers. Its valuations of the 59 nonzero Smith entries are
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
p&\text{valuation : multiplicity}\\ \hline
2&0:12,\ 2:36,\ 3:1,\ 5:10\\
5&0:48,\ 1:11\\
7&0:12,\ 1:47.
\end{array}
\]
The row/column algorithm at each step selects an entry of minimum \(p\)-valuation, isolates it by invertible operations over \(\mathbb Z/p^e\), and continues on the remaining block. Since 59 nonzero pivots are found at exponents strictly below \(e\), and rank 59 is already proved over \(\mathbb Q\), these are exhaustive local Smith valuations. Their weighted sums exactly match the above torsion order, excluding additional prime-power factors. Aligning the valuations in nondecreasing order gives the Smith form
\[
\operatorname{diag}\bigl(1^{12},28^{36},280,1120^{10},0\bigr).
\]

**5. Coinvariants versus invariants.** The graph divisor sequence gives an equivariant exact sequence \(0\to K(G)\to\operatorname{coker}(L)\to\mathbb Z\to0\), with trivial Frobenius action on \(\mathbb Z\). For an involution, \(H_1(C_2,\mathbb Z)=0\); coinvariants therefore preserve injectivity of \(K(G)\) here, and \(\operatorname{coker}(B)\cong\mathbb Z\oplus K(G)_\sigma\) as abelian groups. Hence the above torsion Smith factors yield the claimed coinvariants. Finally the canonical perfect graph-monodromy pairing \(K(G)\times K(G)\to\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z\) is invariant under graph automorphisms. It identifies \(K(G)^\sigma\) with the Pontryagin dual of \(K(G)_\sigma\), yielding the same abstract invariant factors.

## Verification

Execute the two standalone Python 3 scripts in their containing directory:

`python hermitian_lines.py`

`python frobenius_coinvariants.py`

The former reconstructs every Hermitian point and line, all adjacencies, the strongly regular parameters, and the untwisted integral Laplacian elementary divisors. The latter reconstructs the Frobenius orbits, checks the \(K_{4,4}\) subgraph and the quotient-kernel vectors, derives the invariant eigenvalue multiplicities, and performs the entire quotient Smith computation. Neither script requires third-party packages. Two machine-readable certificates are included, but are **not** substitutes for executing and inspecting the algorithms. All computations use exhaustive finite arithmetic; the only infinite mathematical statement is the standard graph-group identification, proved above.

## Relationship to prior work

Rams and Schütt, *112 lines on smooth quartic surfaces (characteristic 3)*, arXiv:1409.7485 (first version dated 2014-09-26), establish completeness and rigidity of the 112-line Fermat configuration. The 2019 work of Pantangi and Sin, *Smith and critical groups of polar graphs* (arXiv:1706.08175), computes untwisted Smith/critical groups of families of orthogonal polar graphs, an important source of broader coverage because the **dual** of \(H(3,9)\) is a polar generalized quadrangle. Thus the ordinary (non-equivariant) critical group is expressly **not** claimed new or used as novelty evidence here. The present statement additionally concerns the **specific arithmetic Frobenius automorphism** and the integral quotient by \(\sigma-1\). The untwisted invariant factors alone do not determine that action or its fixed and coinvariant groups.

Search comparisons included aliases for the Hermitian surface, the dual polar graph, the generalized quadrangle, Frobenius quotient Laplacians, and arithmetic chip-firing groups. The inspected accessible sources did not state this Frobenius-equivariant decomposition. See the structured review for exact comparisons and residual historical risks.

## Limitations

This is a finite integral graph-Jacobian calculation attached to the geometric line configuration, not a claim about the arithmetic Picard scheme of the quartic, and not a new general formula for all unitary quadrangles. The 2019 primary paper's full text could not be accessed in this examination; the comparison to it uses its abstract and bibliographic scope, so a concealed equivariant calculation there remains a material historical-priority risk. The fixed/coinvariant isomorphism is noncanonical; only the duality is canonical. All code certificates were run locally, and no independent audit was performed.
