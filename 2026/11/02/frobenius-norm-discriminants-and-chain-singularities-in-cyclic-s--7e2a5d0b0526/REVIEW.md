# Scientific review: Frobenius-norm discriminants and chain singularities

## Correctness
**PASS.** The geometric smoothness criterion is reconstructed from the defining polynomial, including the converse omitted from the motivating positive-coefficient lemma: the relation \(Y_iF_{Y_i}=t_{i-1}-t_i\), the nonvanishing of \(m\) in the field, and the backward-propagation argument prove that exactly the coefficients with at least one zero produce singular hypersurfaces. Frobenius descent identifies the parameter vector space with \(\mathbb F_{q^m}\). The finite-étale tensor-product decomposition yields the exact extension-stratum counts, including the boundary where \(\gcd(m,s)=1\). For a single missing coefficient, the critical-point equations prove uniqueness of the singular point; normalization of the local coefficients produces the chain germ. A positive weighted-regular-sequence computation gives its Jacobian length, and the invertible-degree Euler identity gives equality with the Tjurina length. Gröbner computations in three distinct characteristic/dimension cases corroborate the colengths, and a complete small-field support census corroborates the arithmetic count. The proof does not rely on a finite experiment as an infinite theorem.

## Originality
**PASS, subject to the stated residual priority risk.** Asgarli--Ghioca--Reichstein (arXiv:2207.00918, Section 3) prove existence of maximal-dimensional systems whose base-field members are smooth and provide the normal-basis cyclic equations. They do not state a reduced norm discriminant, a count for all finite extensions, the earliest singular extension, or the generic discriminant fiber's analytic type. This theorem makes those additional conclusions with proofs. Aramaki--Takahashi (arXiv:1903.02732, Introduction) already treat abstract invertible chain polynomials and give the familiar alternating characteristic-zero Milnor-number formula; neither the chain normal form nor that general formula is asserted as an independent innovation. The additional content is identifying and classifying the boundary singularity in the specific arithmetic linear system, including its positive-characteristic Jacobian/Tjurina algebra. Earlier published general facts about norm tori account for the field-theoretic counting method, not a previously located theorem about this family.

No equivalence or stronger published statement was identified in the inspected motivating primary text, the relevant chain-singularity text, the targeted mathematical-record comparisons, or the 100 accessible preceding claims used for overlap exclusion. The lack of a located result is not proof of world-wide novelty; unindexed equivalent work remains possible.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The basic motivation of the underlying family is unusually strong base-field smoothness in a maximal-dimensional linear system. The first extension degree where that property fails, the number of smooth members in every extension, and the geometry of the discriminant are intrinsic natural refinements of this phenomenon. The analysis also identifies what singularity replaces a smooth member along the generic geometric boundary and computes its exact deformation-theoretic local length, which grows with dimension and degree. These are structural facts about the entire family rather than an arbitrary single example or a routine parameter increment. The proof deliberately does not claim a classification of higher-codimension singular fibers or the scheme-theoretic discriminant multiplicity.

## Closest literature and limitations
The closest motivating work is Asgarli, Ghioca, and Reichstein, *Linear families of smooth hypersurfaces over finitely generated fields* (arXiv:2207.00918, first available July 2, 2022), especially Lemma 4 and the finite-field construction in Section 3. The strongest relevant prior local-singularity statement is Aramaki and Takahashi, *Maximally-graded matrix factorizations for an invertible polynomial of chain type* (arXiv:1903.02732, 2019), which covers general chain forms and their established Milnor-number formula in characteristic zero. The field-extension and geometric-boundary combination for the cyclic finite-field family is not in the inspected passages of either source.

The analysis only counts missing coefficient blocks for intersections of two or more geometric discriminant hyperplanes; it does not describe all singularities there. The Jacobian-colength definition of Milnor number is algebraic in positive characteristic and is not equated with topological vanishing cycles. Independent external review is not available, and equivalent unindexed results cannot be ruled out.
