# Scientific review: Pascal–Kirkman incidence flags

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is conditional on incidence-generic six-point geometry over finite fields of characteristic at least seven. The classical Pascal and Kirkman constructions identify sixty unoriented six-cycles and their sixty corresponding points. Incidence is exactly edge-disjointness; the supplied verifier independently confirms this on an explicit rational conic sextuple and again modulo \(10007\). Its exhaustive action on 720 permutations calculates every orbit in each of the eleven conjugacy types of \(S_6\), while the fixed-point checks verify the same orbit counts by a second method. Descent identifies Frobenius with the corresponding permutation, proving the eleven spectra, the rational-incidence obstruction, and the exact finite étale zeta formula. The computation is exhaustive over the finite action, not a finite-field sample claimed as a universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, with explicit antecedent and priority limits

Classical concurrence and the three-regular \(60\)-by-\(60\) incidence configuration are established; no novelty is claimed for these premises. Closest inspected primary work: Chipalkatti, *On the coincidence of Pascal lines* (arXiv:1407.1447, 2014), Chipalkatti, *When are the Cayley-Salmon lines conjugate?* (arXiv:1505.07144, 2015), and Chipalkatti–Ryba, *Absolute Projectivities in Pascal's Multimysticum* (arXiv:2007.04315, 2020), especially §§2.1–2.9. These works analyze the general classical configuration and explicitly furnish its \(S_6\)-equivariant input but do not state the eleven-case arithmetic flag table or the two rational-incidence obstructions. Targeted mathematical-record searches and deduplication comparisons found no equivalent calculation. The full Conway–Ryba 2012 and 2013 papers could not be inspected; because the arithmetic data follow by finite computation from known geometry, an implicit or unindexed earlier classification remains a meaningful risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The complete classification gives natural zeta functions and extension-field counts for a canonical incidence scheme, rather than an arbitrary combinatorial subcase. More importantly, it identifies precisely two arithmetic partitions where a rational line and a rational point both exist but no rational incidence connects them. This distinguishes individual descent of objects from descent of geometric compatibility. The complete eleven-type calculation is a useful reference invariant for arithmetic forms of the hexagrammum mysticum. It does not classify nongeneric or nonreduced configurations, low characteristics, or universal realization of each splitting pattern over every small base field.
