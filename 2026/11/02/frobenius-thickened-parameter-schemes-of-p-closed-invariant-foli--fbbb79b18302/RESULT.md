# Frobenius-thickened parameter schemes of \(p\)-closed invariant foliations

## Finding

**Theorem.** Let \(k\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic \(p>0\), let \(A/k\) be an abelian variety of positive dimension \(g=r+s\), and suppose the kernel of relative Frobenius admits an isomorphism of group schemes
\[
A[F_A]\simeq \mu_p^{\,r}\oplus\alpha_p^{\,s},\qquad r,s\ge0.
\]
Let \(Z_A\subset\mathbf P(\operatorname{Lie}A)\simeq\mathbf P^{g-1}\) be the **scheme-theoretic zero locus of the universal restricted-\(p\) operation modulo the tautological line**. Its geometric points are the translation-invariant rank-one \(p\)-closed foliations of \(A\).

Choose coordinates \([X_1:\cdots:X_r:Y_1:\cdots:Y_s]\) adapted to the displayed group-scheme decomposition. Then \(Z_A\) has homogeneous ideal sheaf generated by
\[
X_i^pX_j-X_j^pX_i\quad (1\le i<j\le r),\qquad
X_i^pY_a\quad (1\le i\le r,\ 1\le a\le s).
\]
For \(r,s>0\), this scheme is the **disjoint union** of (i) the scheme-theoretic inverse image \(T_{r,s}=F_{\mathbf P}^{-1}(\{X_1=\cdots=X_r=0\})\), with ideal \((X_1^p,\ldots,X_r^p)\), and (ii) the reduced finite set \(\mathbf P^{r-1}(\mathbf F_p)\) on \(\{Y_1=\cdots=Y_s=0\}\). Thus \(T_{r,s}\) is a Frobenius-thickened copy of \(\mathbf P^{s-1}\), with scheme-theoretic generic multiplicity \(p^r\), and the finite reduced component has
\[
N_r=\frac{p^r-1}{p-1}
\]
points. For \(r=0\), all of \(\mathbf P^{s-1}\) is the reduced zero scheme; for \(s=0\), it is the reduced set of \(N_r\) points.

When \(s\ge1\), the exact Hilbert polynomial of \(Z_A\), for sufficiently large integers \(m\), is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
P_{Z_A}(m)=\sum_{e_1=0}^{p-1}\cdots\sum_{e_r=0}^{p-1}
\binom{m-\sum_{i=1}^{r}e_i+s-1}{s-1}
+\frac{p^r-1}{p-1}.}
\]
The empty summation for \(r=0\) is one and the last summand is zero. In particular, when \(r,s>0\), the scheme \(Z_A\) is nonreduced; its Zariski tangent space at every geometric point on the thick component has dimension \(r+s-1\), although that component has reduced dimension \(s-1\). In the smallest mixed case \(r=s=1\), \(Z_A\subset\mathbf P^1\) is the divisor \(X^pY=0\): it has two geometric points but length \(p+1\). For \(s=1\) the entire zero-dimensional scheme has length \(p^r+N_r\); for \(s\ge2\) its degree is \(p^r\).

## Assumptions and scope

All group schemes and projective spaces are over \(k\), and \(F_A:A\to A^{(p)}\) is relative Frobenius. The hypothesis describes a **split height-one Frobenius kernel**, not an arbitrary abelian variety of given \(p\)-rank. It holds, for example, for products of \(r\) ordinary elliptic curves and \(s\) supersingular elliptic curves. The scheme structure is the intrinsic vanishing scheme of the \(p\)-power closure condition, not merely the reduced locus of its geometric solutions.

Concretely let \(V=\operatorname{Lie}A\), \(\mathcal U=\mathcal O_{\mathbf PV}(-1)\subset V\otimes\mathcal O_{\mathbf PV}\), and let \(F_{\mathbf PV}\) be absolute Frobenius. Since the restricted Lie algebra \(V\) is abelian, its restricted \(p\)-map induces an \(\mathcal O\)-linear map \(F_{\mathbf PV}^*\mathcal U\to V\otimes\mathcal O_{\mathbf PV}\). The composite into \((V\otimes\mathcal O)/\mathcal U\) defines \(Z_A\) as its zero scheme. This definition fixes the potentially nonreduced scheme structure.

## Proof

**1. Normal form.** The restricted-Lie correspondence for finite height-one group schemes identifies \(\operatorname{Lie}\mu_p\) with a one-dimensional abelian restricted Lie algebra generated by \(e\) with \(e^{[p]}=e\), and \(\operatorname{Lie}\alpha_p\) with one generated by \(f\) with \(f^{[p]}=0\). Under the stated splitting, the restricted operation on \(V\simeq k^r\oplus k^s\) is therefore
\[
(X_1,\ldots,X_r,Y_1,\ldots,Y_s)^{[p]}
=(X_1^p,\ldots,X_r^p,0,\ldots,0).
\]
The universal image belongs to the tautological line exactly when its wedge with the tautological coordinate vector vanishes. The displayed homogeneous generators are exactly the coordinates of this wedge, so they give the **scheme** \(Z_A\), not just its support.

**2. All affine charts.** Suppose \(s>0\). On \(D_+(Y_a)\), rescale so \(Y_a=1\). The equations \(X_i^pY_a=0\) become \(X_i^p=0\) for all \(i\). Every other defining equation is then automatic. Hence these charts form the Frobenius inverse image \(T_{r,s}\) of the linear subspace \(\mathbf P^{s-1}\), with local ring
\[
k[y_b\ (b\ne a),x_1,\ldots,x_r]/(x_1^p,\ldots,x_r^p).
\]
On \(D_+(X_i)\), rescale so \(X_i=1\). The mixed equations force every \(Y_a=0\); the ordinary equations involving \(X_i\) force \(X_j^p-X_j=0\) for every \(j\ne i\). The remaining equations follow from these. Since \(t^p-t=\prod_{c\in\mathbf F_p}(t-c)\) has derivative \(-1\), this affine scheme is a reduced set of \(p^{r-1}\) points with coordinates in \(\mathbf F_p\). Gluing the ordinary charts gives precisely \(\mathbf P^{r-1}(\mathbf F_p)\), containing \((p^r-1)/(p-1)\) geometric points. The two types have disjoint support and together exhaust all charts, establishing the global scheme-theoretic disjoint union. The cases \(r=0\) and \(s=0\) follow from the same charts with absent variables.

**3. Hilbert polynomial and multiplicities.** For \(s\ge1\), projection away from \(\{Y_1=\cdots=Y_s=0\}\) defines a finite morphism \(\pi:T_{r,s}\to\mathbf P^{s-1}\). On the standard opens the monomials \(\prod_i x_i^{e_i}\), \(0\le e_i<p\), form a free basis; their transition degrees yield the global decomposition of coherent sheaves
\[
\pi_*\mathcal O_{T_{r,s}}\cong
\bigoplus_{0\le e_1,\ldots,e_r<p}
\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^{s-1}}\left(-\sum_i e_i\right).
\]
Also \(\mathcal O_{T_{r,s}}(1)=\pi^*\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^{s-1}}(1)\). Tensor with \(\mathcal O(m)\), take Euler characteristics (or global sections for \(m\ge r(p-1)\)), and add the \(N_r\) disjoint reduced points. This is the announced polynomial. The free module rank is \(p^r\), so the thick component has generic multiplicity \(p^r\). When \(s\ge2\), its reduced support has degree one and the isolated points do not contribute to the leading term, giving degree \(p^r\). When \(s=1\), it is a fat point of length \(p^r\); adding the ordinary points gives length \(p^r+N_r\).

**4. Tangent spaces.** At a point of \(T_{r,s}\), the affine local ideal is generated by the \(p\)-th powers of \(r\) regular parameters. Every such generator has zero linear term, so the tangent space is all of the ambient \(\mathbf P^{r+s-1}\), while the reduced support has dimension \(s-1\). At ordinary points, the affine equations \(X_j^p-X_j\) and \(Y_a\) have independent linear parts; those points are reduced and have zero tangent dimension. This proves the final assertions.

## Verification

The standalone file `verify.py` performs exact finite-field projective-point enumeration and checks the thickening Hilbert function in two independent ways: direct monomial counting and coefficient extraction from \((1-t^p)^r/(1-t)^{r+s}\). It tests the \(27\) combinations \(p\in\{2,3,5\}\) and \(1\le r,s\le3\), across degrees through \(rp+7\), and checks the smallest mixed length. Run `python3 verify.py`; the expected final prefix is `VERIFY_OK`. These computations check examples only; the chart and sheaf arguments above prove the unrestricted theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

Andreatta–Goren (2026), §1.10, characterize \(p\)-closed invariant foliations using restricted-Lie subspaces and explicitly count them in abelian-surface \(p\)-kernel types. Their discussion gives the **geometric-point classification**, not the embedded nonreduced zero scheme, its Frobenius-thickened component or Hilbert polynomial. Kondō–Schröer (2019/2020), §1, explain \(p\)-closed lines and subgroup schemes of order \(p\) for restricted Lie algebras, including examples whose geometric loci are projective linear spaces; they do not compute the present split abelian scheme's nonreduced local rings. Tziolas (2024), Theorem 3.38, provides the general height-one group-scheme/restricted-Lie correspondence but not this specific parameter scheme and its Hilbert polynomial. The result builds on these standard principles and isolates scheme-theoretic deformation information invisible to the already known point counts.

## Limitations

The formula assumes the entire relative-Frobenius kernel splits as \(\mu_p^r\oplus\alpha_p^s\). It does not describe non-split local-local group schemes, arbitrary rank foliations, turbulent foliations, or full moduli of inseparable quotients. The scheme is defined as the universal \(p\)-closure zero scheme on the invariant-line projective space; no equivalence with every conceivable alternative moduli functor is asserted. Historical priority beyond the inspected sources remains subject to independent verification.
