# Scientific review: Clebsch neighborhood homology symmetry

## Correctness — PASS
The domain is the exactly specified 16-vertex, degree-five Cayley graph and the ordinary open-neighborhood complex. Its face vector is \((16,80,160,80,16)\); signed boundaries compose to zero. Modular boundary rank lower bounds saturate chain-complex rank upper bounds and therefore prove rational Betti number \(31\) in degree two, with all other reduced rational groups zero. The entire \(1920\)-element group is exhibited and bounded via its action on the five neighbors of a fixed vertex. The reduced Lefschetz trace for every element and all three stabilizer Fourier characters are reproduced by `verify.py`. The three rational little-group modules are irreducible, of dimensions \(6,15,10\), and exhaust homology. Standard finite-group rational descent proves quotient acyclicity, not quotient contractibility. No infinite parameter extrapolation is claimed.

## Originality — PASS against inspected evidence
A previous public calculation on the *same graph and same complex* proves the nonequivariant \(31\)-sphere wedge. That result was inspected in full and is not claimed again. It contains no complete action of the \(1920\)-element group and its statement does not imply the present homology character. The primary Robinson, Shukla, and Matsushita–Wakatsuki texts were compared by objects, assumptions and outcomes; none gives a Clebsch equivariant homology decomposition. Indexed searches by Clebsch/folded-five-cube/Dowker/character/decomposition and comparisons with other neighborhood-complex symmetry calculations found no covering result, but cannot exclude unindexed equivalents.

## Value — PASS
A 31-sphere wedge homotopy type alone leaves the natural group action undetermined. On this canonical highly symmetric four-chromatic graph, the exact three-summand rational representation and absence of full-symmetry invariants provide substantive equivariant structure and a computable topological invariant of its quotient. The group-specific little-group decomposition is not a parameter renaming or an arbitrary slice; it is the complete representation of the graph's full automorphism group.
