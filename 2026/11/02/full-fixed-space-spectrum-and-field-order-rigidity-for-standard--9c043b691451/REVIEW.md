# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The proof first characterizes the codimension-one abelian ideal intrinsically, so the automorphism form is derived instead of assumed. It parameterizes all maps by a nonzero multiplier, a Jordan-block intertwiner, and an arbitrary translation. The crucial cyclic-unit lemma constructs an invariant factor by the finite-field norm and unique roots in a characteristic-power group; its explicit coboundary gives an actual conjugacy, including even characteristic. Residue-modulo-order decomposition and valuation probabilities yield every coefficient, while a separate direct enumeration confirms six small cases including the nonprime field of order four. All quantifiers are finite-field prime powers and Jordan-block lengths at least three. There is no proof assertion for excluded three-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebras. Exhaustive small cases are corroboration, not proof of the infinite theorem.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected material
Avetisyan's *Jordanable almost Abelian Lie algebras* (arXiv:1811.01252, Proposition 11) establishes the general Lie-automorphism-group structural starting point and must be credited. Its inspected proposition does not state a fixed-space counting polynomial or the finite-field order criterion. Burde and Dekimpe (arXiv:2604.27916, Theorems 5.7 and 6.5) work on complex fixed-point-free automorphism existence, not exact finite-field multiplicities. The publicly indexed three-dimensional Heisenberg automorphism count concerns an excluded dimension, and the related filiform adjoint-rank result counts different operators. The result asserted original is only the full fixed-space spectrum and its cyclic-unit proof and arithmetic consequence. Semantic searches by eigenvalue-one multiplicity, cocycle, semilinear normal form, and fixed-space distributions produced no matching stronger theorem. Originality remains conditional on what could be inspected, not certified global priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A complete eigenvalue-one nullity enumerator is a natural invariant of the full Lie automorphism group and determines each fixed-space probability and all its moments. The new phase boundary for existence of fixed-point-free automorphisms depends on whether the multiplicative field group has a proper nontrivial subgroup or whether its order exceeds the Jordan-chain length. This turns a broad qualitative structural classification into a concrete field-arithmetic trichotomy for every dimension at least four; the contrast with complex-field existence criteria is mathematically motivated.
