# Full symmetries of odd elliptic matroids and the unlabelled modular curve

## Finding
For every odd integer \(n\ge 11\), let \(T_n\) be the rank-three matroid on \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) whose three-element nonbases are exactly the sets of *distinct* residues \(\{i,j,k\}\) satisfying \(i+j+k\equiv0\pmod n\). Its **entire** automorphism group is
\[
\operatorname{Aut}(T_n)=\{i\mapsto ai+b:\ a\in(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times,\ 3b=0\}
\cong C_{\gcd(n,3)}\rtimes(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times.
\]
In particular, its order is \(\gcd(n,3)\varphi(n)\); the translation factor is nontrivial exactly when \(3\mid n\).

Over \(\mathbb C\), the coarse moduli curve of *unlabelled* projective point arrangements realizing \(T_n\) **and lying on a smooth plane cubic** is the open modular curve \(Y_0(n)\), parametrizing elliptic curves with cyclic subgroups of order \(n\). The forgetful map from the corresponding labelled smooth realization curve \(Y_1(n)\) has generic degree \(\varphi(n)/2\). For an elliptic curve of generic \(j\)-invariant, the full projective stabilizer of the resulting unlabelled set of points has order \(2\gcd(n,3)\), generated by inversion and the translations by the subgroup's three-torsion.

The bound on \(n\) is substantive: for \(n=9\), the full matroid automorphism group has order \(36\), whereas the displayed affine subgroup has order \(18\). An explicit nonaffine automorphism is provided in the verification artifact.

## Assumptions and scope
The combinatorial classification is over the residue group \(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\) and makes no characteristic assumption. All geometric assertions are for smooth elliptic curves and their degree-three plane embeddings over \(\mathbb C\); singular degenerations and compactified modular curves are not classified. The smooth labelled moduli identification \(Y_1(n)\) is the existing Borisov–Roulleau/Baker correspondence, not a new claim.

## Proof
**Step 1: the translation obstruction.** Let \(f\in\operatorname{Aut}(T_n)\) and put \(b=f(0)\). As \(n\) is odd, for each nonzero residue \(t\) the triple \(\{0,t,-t\}\) consists of distinct elements, hence
\[
f(t)+f(-t)=-b.
\]
The two triples \(\{1,2,-3\}\) and \(\{-1,-2,3\}\) are distinct-element nonbases for \(n\ge11\). Summing their image equations and applying the preceding three opposite-pair identities gives \(-3b=0\). Consequently \(3b=0\), and translation by \(-b\) itself preserves zero-sum triples. The permutation \(g=f-b\) is therefore a matroid automorphism with \(g(0)=0\) and \(g(-t)=-g(t)\).

**Step 2: forced doubling.** Fix any nonzero \(x\) and choose a residue \(t\) outside
\[
\{0,\pm x,\pm2x,\pm x/2,\pm3x\}.
\]
Division by two is well-defined because \(n\) is odd. This excludes at most nine residues, so the choice exists for every \(n\ge11\). Each of
\[
\{x,t,-x-t\},\qquad\{x,-t,-x+t\},\qquad\{x+t,x-t,-2x\}
\]
then consists of distinct residues summing to zero. Applying \(g\) to these three nonbases, using oddness, yields
\[
g(x+t)=g(x)+g(t),\quad g(x-t)=g(x)-g(t),\quad
 g(2x)=g(x+t)+g(x-t)=2g(x).
\]
The same doubling identity holds for \(x=0\).

**Step 3: forced additivity.** For arbitrary \(x,y\), if \(\{x,y,-x-y\}\) is a distinct-element triple, its image relation and oddness imply \(g(x+y)=g(x)+g(y)\). The only failures of distinctness are \(x=y\), \(y=-2x\), or \(x=-2y\); these are covered by the doubling identity and oddness. The case \(x+y=0\) is immediate. Thus \(g\) is an automorphism of the cyclic additive group, so \(g(i)=ai\) for a unit \(a\). Conversely every affine map \(i\mapsto ai+b\) with unit \(a\) and \(3b=0\) preserves all distinct zero-sum triples and their complement. This proves the exact automorphism formula and semidirect product.

**Step 4: the unlabelled modular quotient.** For a smooth complex elliptic curve \((E,O)\) embedded by the complete linear system \(|3O|\), let \(P\) have order \(n\) and let \(C=\langle P\rangle\). The set \(C\subset\mathbb P^2\) realizes \(T_n\). Borisov–Roulleau and Baker identify the smooth labelled realization space with \(Y_1(n)\). A relabelling \(i\mapsto ai+b\) changes \(iP\) to \(aiP+bP\). Since \(3bP=O\), the translation \(\tau_{bP}\) preserves the isomorphism class of \(\mathcal O_E(3O)\), hence extends to a projective automorphism of the plane cubic. Thus the translation factor acts trivially on the labelled realization classes, while \(a\) acts by the standard diamond relabelling \((E,P)\mapsto(E,aP)\). Quotienting by all units forgets the generator and retains the cyclic subgroup \(C\); this is precisely \(Y_0(n)\). Because \((E,P)\cong(E,-P)\) and a generic elliptic curve has no other origin-preserving automorphisms, the generic degree is \(\varphi(n)/2\).

**Step 5: the projective stabilizer.** By Bézout's theorem, two distinct plane cubics cannot pass through the same \(n\ge11\) distinct points, so any projective symmetry of \(C\) preserves its unique smooth cubic \(E\). When \(j(E)\notin\{0,1728\}\), every automorphism of \(E\) is \(\tau_Q\circ u\), with \(u\in\{+1,-1\}\). It preserves \(C\) as a set precisely when \(Q\in C\). It preserves the plane embedding \(|3O|\) precisely when \(3Q=O\). Hence the projective stabilizer is \(C[3]\rtimes\{\pm1\}\), of order \(2\gcd(n,3)\). These conditions are also sufficient because preservation of the hyperplane line bundle induces a projective linear transformation.

## Relationship to prior work
Borisov and Roulleau (2024), Section 2.3, explicitly establish only that \((\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times\) *is contained in* the automorphism group of \(T_n\), and their Theorem 2 establishes the labelled complex realization correspondence. Baker (2026), Theorem 1.2, upgrades the labelled correspondence to a scheme-level statement over \(\mathbb Z[1/n]\). Neither cited statement classifies the full matroid automorphism group, its exceptional three-torsion translations, or the full unlabelled smooth realization quotient. The familiar diamond-operator quotient \(Y_1(n)\to Y_0(n)\) is classical and is used, not claimed as new. The full-group theorem supplies the missing assertion that **no additional incidence relabellings** collapse the unlabelled moduli curve further.

## Limitations
The full symmetry classification is proved here only for odd \(n\ge11\). Even levels and small odd levels are not classified by the theorem; the exact \(n=9\) discrepancy shows that a universal extension to all smaller odd levels is false. The geometry is over \(\mathbb C\), restricted to smooth cubic configurations. At elliptic curves with exceptional complex multiplication, projective stabilizers may exceed the generic size; no detailed classification of special fibers or integral-stack structure is asserted. Exhaustive finite checks are not a substitute for the general argument. Historical priority for equivalent formulations under other terminology is not conclusively settled.
