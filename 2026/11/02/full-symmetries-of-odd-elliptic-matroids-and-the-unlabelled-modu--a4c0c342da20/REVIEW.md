# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The theorem has a self-contained combinatorial proof for all odd \(n\ge11\): opposite pairs force \(3f(0)=0\); three distinct-element circuits force doubling after an explicit exclusion of at most nine residues; and doubling extends incidence preservation to all additive pairs. The projective and modular conclusions use standard elliptic line-bundle and automorphism facts together with the established smooth labelled-realization correspondence. The genus-one cubic is unique through at least eleven distinct configuration points by Bézout. A standalone incidence backtracker independently checks exact full automorphism orders for every odd \(n\) through \(33\), while the symbolic proof handles all larger odd levels. The word "generic" in the stabilizer statement excludes exceptional \(j\)-invariants.

## Originality
**PASS, with priority qualification.** The 2024 point-arrangement paper states only containment of the unit group in the elliptic-matroid automorphism group. The 2026 paper develops the labelled realization scheme and its modular identification. Neither inspected theorem proves the full affine classification, the exceptional \(3\)-torsion kernel or the absence of further quotients of the unlabelled moduli curve. Focused published-record and literature searches returned no equivalent statement. Older work on zero-sum ternary hypergraphs or matroid automorphisms could conceivably express the combinatorial part differently and remains a priority risk. The classical diamond quotient is not itself claimed as novel.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The full combinatorial automorphism group determines the actual quotient of a geometrically natural labelled realization curve, not merely one convenient symmetry subgroup. Its order changes structurally according to divisibility by three, with visible projective stabilizers, and provides a uniform identification of unlabelled configuration moduli in infinitely many levels. The small-level nonaffine example marks a genuine boundary of the uniform theorem.

## Limits and prior comparison
The theorem covers the smooth complex elliptic locus and odd levels at least eleven; it does not settle singular degenerations, special complex-multiplication stabilizers, even levels, or integrality of the unlabelled quotient. Principal literature: Baker, arXiv:2608.05299v1, and Borisov–Roulleau, arXiv:2404.04364v1. Historical priority is necessarily provisional.
