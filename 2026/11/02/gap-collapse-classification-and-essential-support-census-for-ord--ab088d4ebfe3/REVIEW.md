# Mathematical review

## Correctness

PASS. The gap-collapse dichotomy follows because the pointwise stabilizer of a finite parameter set acts independently and transitively inside every rational gap, and has only two oriented off-diagonal pair orbits there. Symmetry and transitivity force each gap to be either wholly discrete and disconnected or one collapsed block; partitions of named points plus collapsed gaps are in bijection with admissible equivalence relations. This proves the Bell convolution for all nonnegative parameter counts. The least-support property of total-order nominal symmetry, documented by Bojańczyk (2013), is explicitly used to justify inclusion–exclusion over deletable parameters. Dobinski's moment formula proves Bell log-convexity and the published Bell-ratio asymptotic supplies the rate. The finite proxy checker reproduced the first four support counts and essential-support counts independently. Residual mathematical reliance: the cited least-support and Bell-ratio facts, neither of which is presented as newly proved.

## Originality

PASS relative to inspected evidence, with explicit risks. Masters (2025) develops rational-order atom symmetries and finite support but not the complete equivalence-relation classification. Bojańczyk (2013) proves the nominal framework and cites least supports, without the present particular census. OEIS A208782, recorded by Hardin in 2012, already contains the first numerical values in a distinct array-coloring context; the numerical sequence itself is explicitly credited and is **not** a novelty claim. Searches of other mathematical records for the partition convolution, aliases in total-order symmetries, exact numeric values and essential supports returned no stronger statement covering the full claim. The main residual risk is an older uninspected oligomorphic-group or labelled-species result proving an equivalent structural interpretation and support asymptotic.

## Value

PASS. For a canonical infinite-atom data symmetry, the statement gives a complete finite representation of every finitely supported equivalence relation, an exact enumeration, and a new quantitative statement: nearly all relations supported by \(k\) names genuinely require all those names as their least support. This is a natural resource/identifiability invariant in nominal mathematics, not an arbitrary finite subslice. It is applicable to data symmetry representations and finite descriptions of equivariant quotients, without claiming any unproved algorithmic complexity improvement.

## Closest literature and limitations

The motivating first-public source is Masters, *Logic of Sets with Atoms* (November 25, 2025, arXiv:2512.02041). Related primary sources are Bojańczyk, *Nominal Monoids* (2013), Grunwald–Serafin on Bell ratios (2024), and OEIS A208782 (Hardin 2012). Exact numeric values are previously tabulated; historical structural priority has not received independent confirmation. The theorem is restricted to order-preserving automorphisms of \((\mathbb Q,<)\) and finite rational supports. Finite proxy checks are evidence, not an infinite certificate.
