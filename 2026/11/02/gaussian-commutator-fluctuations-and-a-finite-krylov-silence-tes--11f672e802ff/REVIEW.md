# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact independent-sign representation separates stable deterministic propagation from the order-\(h^2\) commutator perturbation. Martingale orthogonality bounds the nonlinear replacement in squared \(L^2\) by \(O(h^6)\), and a triangular-array Lindeberg argument establishes the Gaussian covariance with the factor \(1/2\) from the paired time grid. Analyticity and Cayley–Hamilton establish the complete finite silence test. An exact-arithmetic stage reconstruction and the finite-step covariance recursion were executed from the included verifier. Only the stated finite-dimensional fixed-horizon claim is proved.

**Originality — PASS, qualified best-knowledge assessment.** The motivating independent-suboperator randomized splitting paper supplies the implicit resolvent framework but not the paired-order covariance or null-Krylov classification. Berger (1984) is the closest general predecessor: its stochastic product limit has diffusive \(n^{-1/2}\) random perturbations, whereas the present problem has \(n^{-2}\) random commutator perturbations and concerns their smaller centered fluctuations. That general mathematical technique makes some of the Gaussian argument potentially derivable, but no examined theorem gives the explicit paired-resolvent covariance and finite initial-vector silence criterion. The 2025 random splitting paper already knows a \(3/2\) error rate, so rate novelty is specifically disclaimed. Mathematical-record searches and accessible prior-claim comparisons did not return a covering statement. A hidden more specialized earlier formula remains a genuine priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** This is a concrete diagnostic for a natural design decision: using both suboperators once per pair removes order-\(h^{1/2}\) selection variability but leaves an exactly computable commutator-noise fluctuation. Unlike a rate upper bound, the covariance quantifies its size and the finite Krylov criterion characterizes precisely when that cost vanishes for the requested initial data. The two-state positive-definite witness demonstrates sharp nonzero fluctuation and the three-state verifier demonstrates a noncommuting silent initial state.

**Closest literature and limitations.** Eisenmann–Stillfjord, arXiv:2210.05375; Berger, doi:10.1090/S0002-9947-1984-0752503-3; Li–Wang, doi:10.1002/num.70040. The result treats constant symmetric positive-semidefinite finite matrices, a fixed horizon, fair independent pair orders and exact arithmetic. It proves the centered noise scale, not a global \(3/2\)-order approximation to the differential equation, and independent priority verification has not been conducted.
