# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact random Volterra operator is reconstructed from the independently sampled dissipative coefficients. The normal-equation sensitivity gives a continuous Hilbert-space kernel, a triangular-array central limit theorem gives its Gaussian covariance, and a fourth-moment maximal estimate controls the second-order remainder. The optimal state solves a positive Dirichlet–Robin boundary-value problem, proving that the leading variance and cost-regret coefficients are strictly positive for every horizon. The standalone numerical checks replay the finite-grid derivative and covariance formulas, without being mistaken for an infinite-dimensional proof.

**Originality — PASS (qualified best-knowledge assessment).** The source paper [1] is credited for the split dynamics and convergence-rate estimates. Its full accessible text was compared to the covariance and sharp asymptotic statement. Two plausible related sources [2,3] concern sensitivity decay and stochastic-process-noise LQ control, respectively; neither covers this independent random-coefficient offline control fluctuation theorem. Mathematical research records were queried for randomized splitting, Gaussian control fluctuations, covariance, regret, and equivalent formulations. No covering theorem was identified, but the broad generality of classical functional central limit theorems and sensitivity analysis leaves an explicit residual priority risk; this assessment is not independent originality certification.

**Value — PASS.** A known mean-square upper rate becomes a sharp two-sided asymptotic with a positive, computable covariance and corresponding expected true-objective regret, for a natural dissipative instance. This supplies direct uncertainty and error-budget diagnostics not delivered by a rate-only upper estimate. It concerns a fully specified pathwise offline control method; neither general matrices nor causal controls are covered.

**Limitations and closest prior literature.** Veldman–Zuazua [1] is the nearest source and owns the algorithm and convergence upper bound. Na–Anitescu [2] studies dynamic-programming sensitivity but not randomized time-splitting, while Ou et al. [3] concern a stochastic state-noise LQ regime. Any undiscovered generic random-evolution-operator CLT with a sufficiently strong differentiability theorem could imply some or all of this special case; independent priority review remains necessary.
