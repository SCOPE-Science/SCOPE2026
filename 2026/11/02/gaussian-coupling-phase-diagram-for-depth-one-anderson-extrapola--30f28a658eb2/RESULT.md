# Gaussian coupling phase diagram for depth-one Anderson extrapolation

## Finding

**Theorem (exact noise-coupling law, moment boundary, and worst correlation).** Let \(a\in(-1,1)\), \(c=1-a>0\), \(\theta\in\mathbb R\), and choose two distinct, fixed real trial points \(x_0,x_1\). Set \(d=x_1-x_0\ne0\) and \(m=(x_0+x_1)/2-\theta\). Observe the affine contraction \(F(x)=\theta+a(x-\theta)\) with noisy images \(Y_i=F(x_i)+\varepsilon_i\), where \((\varepsilon_0,\varepsilon_1)\) is a centered jointly Gaussian vector with equal positive marginal variance \(\sigma^2\) and correlation \(\rho\in[-1,1]\). Endpoint correlations permit degenerate jointly Gaussian laws. Define \(R_i=Y_i-x_i\), and use the unregularized depth-one Anderson step with mixing parameter one,
\[
 \Gamma=\frac{R_1}{R_1-R_0},\qquad Z=Y_1-(Y_1-Y_0)\Gamma .
\]
The denominator vanishes with probability zero, including the endpoint correlations. The following statements hold.

1. There is an **exact independent Gaussian-ratio representation**, simultaneously valid for all \(\rho\in[-1,1]\):
\[
 Z-\theta=m+\frac{dN}{D},\qquad
 N\sim N\!\left(cm,\frac{\sigma^2(1+\rho)}2\right),\quad
 D\sim N\!\left(-cd,2\sigma^2(1-\rho)\right),\quad N\perp D.
\]
A zero variance denotes a deterministic normal variable.

2. For \(\rho<1\), except when \(\rho=-1\) and \(m=0\), the error has the **sharp positive reciprocal tail**
\[
 \lim_{t\to\infty}t\Pr\{|Z-\theta|>t\}=K_{\rho,m}
 =\frac{|d|\,A_{\rho,m}}{\sigma\sqrt{\pi(1-\rho)}}
 \exp\!\left(-\frac{c^2d^2}{4\sigma^2(1-\rho)}\right)>0,
\]
where \(A_{\rho,m}=\mathbb E|N|\). Equivalently, if \(\tau=\sigma\sqrt{(1+\rho)/2}\),
\[
 A_{\rho,m}=
 \begin{cases}
 \tau\sqrt{2/\pi}\exp(-c^2m^2/(2\tau^2))
 +|cm|\operatorname{erf}(|cm|/(\sqrt2\tau)),&\tau>0,\\
 |cm|,&\tau=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Consequently, for every \(q>0\), \(\mathbb E|Z-\theta|^q<\infty\) if and only if \(q<1\).

3. When \(\rho=1\), the error is exactly \(N(0,\sigma^2/c^2)\), so every positive moment is finite. When \(\rho=-1\) and \(m=0\), \(Z=\theta\) almost surely. Those are the **only** parameter cases with finite first absolute error moment.

4. If the two trials are centered on the true fixed point (\(m=0\)), set \(b=c^2d^2/(4\sigma^2)>0\). The tail constant for \(-1<\rho<1\) is
\[
 K_{\rho,0}=\frac{|d|}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}}
 \exp\!\left(-\frac b{1-\rho}\right).
\]
It tends to zero at either correlation endpoint, yet has the **unique global maximizer**
\[
 \rho_*=\frac{1-b}{1+b},\qquad
 \max_{-1\le\rho\le1}K_{\rho,0}
 =\frac{2\sigma}{\pi c}\exp\!\left(-\frac{1+b}{2}\right).
\]
At both exceptional endpoint couplings the reciprocal-tail coefficient is zero, even though the resulting error laws are different: Gaussian for shared noise and identically zero for perfectly antithetic noise with centered trials.

## Assumptions and scope

The trial points are prescribed and distinct, not necessarily generated by a previous random iterate. The affine map is strictly contractive, the two noises have identical positive marginal variance, and their entire two-dimensional law is Gaussian with the stated correlation. The Anderson step is the *unsafeguarded, unregularized* residual-minimizing depth-one step with mixing parameter one. All probability and moment assertions concern the single extrapolated output \(Z\), not an infinite iterative trajectory. The centered-trial optimum is a conditional mathematical benchmark: choosing trials symmetrically around the unknown \(\theta\) is not a generic implementable rule.

## Proof

**Algebraic reduction.** Since \(R_i=-c(x_i-\theta)+\varepsilon_i\), put \(D=R_1-R_0=-cd+\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_0\). The least-squares coefficient in one real dimension is \(\Gamma=R_1/D\) whenever \(D\ne0\). Since \(Y_1-Y_0=d+D\),
\[
 Z=Y_1-\frac{(d+D)R_1}{D}=x_1-\frac{dR_1}{D}
 =x_0-\frac{dR_0}{D}.
\]
Define \(N=cm-(\varepsilon_0+\varepsilon_1)/2\). Direct substitution gives \(R_0=-D/2-N\), hence \(Z-\theta=m+dN/D\). (The sign of \(N\) is immaterial to the symmetric zero-mean case, but its mean is \(cm\); the correct identity is \(R_0=-D/2-N\).) The Gaussian sum and difference are uncorrelated:
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_0+\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_0)=0,
\]
so they are independent, including degenerate endpoints. Their respective variances are \(2\sigma^2(1+\rho)\) and \(2\sigma^2(1-\rho)\), giving the claimed \(N,D\) laws. The variable \(D\) is nonzero almost surely for \(\rho<1\) since it has positive variance; for \(\rho=1\), it equals the nonzero constant \(-cd\).

**Sharp reciprocal tail.** Suppose \(\rho<1\). The normal density \(f_D\) is bounded and continuous, and \(f_D(0)>0\). Because \(N\) is independent of \(D\), conditional on \(N=n\),
\[
 \Pr\{|dN/D|>t\mid N=n\}
 =\int_{-|dn|/t}^{|dn|/t}f_D(u)\,du.
\]
After multiplication by \(t\), this tends to \(2|dn|f_D(0)\) and is dominated by \(2|dn|\|f_D\|_\infty\). Gaussian integrability and dominated convergence yield \(t\Pr\{|dN/D|>t\}\to2|d|f_D(0)\mathbb E|N|\). The finite translation by \(m\) does not alter the leading constant: sandwich the error-tail event between the corresponding unshifted events with thresholds \(t+|m|\) and \(t-|m|\). Evaluating \(f_D(0)\) gives
\[
 f_D(0)=\frac{1}{2\sigma\sqrt{\pi(1-\rho)}}
 \exp\!\left(-\frac{c^2d^2}{4\sigma^2(1-\rho)}\right),
\]
which establishes \(K_{\rho,m}\). The displayed formula for \(A_{\rho,m}\) follows by integrating the absolute normal density (and by continuity for its degenerate endpoint). The constant vanishes precisely when \(N=0\) almost surely, namely \(\rho=-1\) and \(m=0\). Otherwise \(K_{\rho,m}>0\).

**Moment threshold and exceptional endpoints.** In every positive-tail case, \(\Pr\{|Z-\theta|>t\}\sim K_{\rho,m}/t\). Using the exact tail-integral identity \(\mathbb E|Z-\theta|^q=q\int_0^\infty t^{q-1}\Pr\{|Z-\theta|>t\}\,dt\), the integral converges exactly for \(0<q<1\). If \(\rho=1\), both errors coincide almost surely, \(D=-cd\), and \(Z-\theta=\varepsilon_0/c\); this is Gaussian. If \(\rho=-1\) and \(m=0\), the noise is perfectly antithetic and \(N\equiv0\), whence \(Z=\theta\) almost surely. These exhaust the cases \(\rho\in[-1,1]\).

**Optimal coupling for centered trials.** When \(m=0\), the normal absolute-moment formula reduces to \(A_{\rho,0}=\sigma\sqrt{(1+\rho)/\pi}\). Substitution yields the stated \(K_{\rho,0}\). Its logarithmic derivative for \(-1<\rho<1\) is
\[
 \frac{d}{d\rho}\log K_{\rho,0}=
 \frac{1}{1-\rho^2}-\frac{b}{(1-\rho)^2}.
\]
Its sign is the sign of \((1-\rho)-b(1+\rho)\), so there is precisely one sign change, from positive to negative, at \(\rho_*=(1-b)/(1+b)\). The coefficient tends to zero at both endpoints, and substituting \(\rho_*\) gives the stated global maximum. This proves all claims.

## Verification

The standalone `verify.py` is executable with ordinary Python standard-library modules. It checks the Anderson-to-ratio identity using exact fractions, verifies the two degenerate-correlation cases directly, checks the Gaussian sum/difference identity by sampled substitutions, tests the unique correlation maximum, and independently numerically integrates Gaussian denominator-window probabilities to check the leading tail constants for several nondegenerate and degenerate numerator configurations. At packaging, the checker returned `VERIFY_OK 7987 finite checks`. These numerical checks support the algebra and leading constants; the limit, global maximum, and infinite moment assertions follow from the proof above, not finite sampling.

## Relationship to prior work

Wei, Bao and Liu (2021) give the classical Anderson residual-minimization formula and explain why stochastic methods require damping and adaptive regularization. Their analyzed stochastic algorithm is regularized; the present result classifies the exact *unregularized* two-evaluation scalar output as a function of Gaussian cross-evaluation correlation. Scieur, d'Aspremont and Bach (2017) establish stability bounds for regularized nonlinear stochastic acceleration; those bounds do not state this exact unregularized correlation/cancellation phase diagram. Classical ratios of Gaussian variables, including Cauchy-type tails, predate this note and are not claimed as new. The distinction here is the exact Anderson sum/difference reduction, the exceptional common-noise and antithetic regimes, and the sharp nonmonotone worst correlation. Toth and coauthors (2017) study inaccurate Anderson function values and provide a particularly relevant possible precursor; only their abstract and publisher's opening-page preview could be inspected, so possible additional prior overlap in the inaccessible body remains a publication-priority risk. This note claims no priority over any such unidentified theorem.

## Limitations

Only a single scalar affine map and a single depth-one extrapolated output are treated. The two trial points are fixed, not adapted to past noise; the errors are equal-variance jointly Gaussian with possibly perfect correlation. The theorem does not cover nonlinear maps, unequal marginal noise variances, finite-precision cancellation, regularized or safeguarded Anderson schemes, or long-run stochastic convergence. The rare-event coefficient describes the asymptotic tail and not every finite quantile. Symmetry about the true fixed point is used only for the explicit coupling maximization and antithetic cancellation and should not be represented as a practical fixed-point oracle. Prior-publication comparison remains qualified by inaccessible literature, and no independent scientific audit has occurred.
