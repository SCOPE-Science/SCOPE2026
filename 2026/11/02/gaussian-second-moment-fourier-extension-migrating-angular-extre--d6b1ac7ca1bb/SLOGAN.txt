The sharp Gaussian second-moment Fourier-extension inequality on the circle has a migrating angular extremizer: its optimal angular degree is asymptotic to \(\sqrt z\), and its squared sharp constant is asymptotic to \(2e^{-1/2}z^{3/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}\). A uniform all-frequency spectral law forces every asymptotically extremizing input to concentrate its angular energy near that migrating degree, unlike the hard-ball Fourier-extension problem.
