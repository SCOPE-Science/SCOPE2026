# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

Cartesian-product distance separates path displacement from a single clique-coordinate change. That identity gives an exhaustive two-form characterization of general-position subsets and excludes all alternative three-vertex and doubled-column patterns. Multiplication over columns gives the all-set polynomial. For inclusion-maximal sets, the proof distinguishes consecutive doubled heights from nonconsecutive doubled heights and counts extendible one-per-column tuples by inclusion–exclusion, including their exact overlap. Both formulas were independently reproduced by a shortest-path-based exhaustive enumeration over 1,450,496 subsets of eight small graph instances. The finite checks alone are not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (with priority risk)

The full text of Iršič–Klavžar–Rus–Tuite, *General Position Polynomials* (arXiv:2401.05696; DOI 10.1007/s00025-024-02133-3), was compared at its definition, inclusion–exclusion proposition, and complete Cartesian-product subsection. That paper gives a general counting framework and path-by-path grid enumerators, but not this clique-by-path all-set or inclusion-maximal polynomial. Klavžar–Patkós–Rus–Yero (DOI 10.1007/s00025-021-01438-x) concentrates on extremal general-position sets of other Cartesian products. Klavžar–Neethu–Chandran (arXiv:2111.07425, Theorem 3.5) already implies the achievement-game outcome on all products with a path factor: that consequence, including the existence of terminal pairs, is specifically credited and not treated as new. Tian–Klavžar (DOI 10.1007/s40840-024-01788-z) develops other general-position variants rather than the ordinary all-set enumerator.

Targeted semantic research-record searches for clique-path, Cartesian-product, terminal-set, and polynomial aliases found no equivalent statement or stronger statement implying the two explicit enumerators. This does not prove priority; differently indexed literature may contain the same result.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Counting all feasible placements and all inclusion-maximal placements answers two natural, distinct enumerative questions in a standard geodesic-avoidance invariant. The second polynomial reveals a striking terminal cardinality spectrum consisting of exactly three sizes although the ambient graphs have arbitrarily large order. The classification supplies structural reasons for the coefficients, rather than a limited numerical table. The pre-existing second-player strategy explains why small terminal pairs exist but does not determine the number or sizes of all terminal configurations.

## Limits

The proof is restricted to \(n,L\ge3\) and ordinary shortest-path general position. The exhaustive program verifies eight finite instances, not every graph in the family. No claim of comprehensive publication priority or independent validation is made.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
