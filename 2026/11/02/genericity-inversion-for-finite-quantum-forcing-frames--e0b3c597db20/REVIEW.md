# Review of genericity inversion for finite quantum forcing frames

## Correctness
PASS. The component-row normal form and the equivalence-accessibility condition \(Q\subseteq M\) are proved directly from the forcing axiom. The unrestricted expansion follows from an explicit rank-two classification and a uniform spanning-forest tail estimate. The equivalence expansion follows from partition cut weights, with a separate bound on every non-leading partition. A standalone checker independently tests the original forcing equations for every labelled pair through three worlds, and the two exact census formulas through six worlds. The infinite statements rely on the displayed asymptotic bounds, not on finite tests.

## Originality
PASS within the available evidence. The component invariance theorem in Tokuo (2026), the random-cluster partition-function identity, and standard random-graph connectivity estimates are prior results and are credited. The final contribution is their quantitative comparison in two intrinsically natural quantum-modal frame ensembles, with two distinct sharp first-defect probabilities and conditional classifications. Targeted primary-literature and scholarly-index queries did not expose an equivalent paired result. Strong general coverage by a more specialized large-cluster-weight or partition-graph asymptotic theorem remains a risk; complete text inspection of the motivating article could not be completed.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The formulas identify how a single natural semantic restriction (equivalence accessibility) reverses the typical compatibility geometry, from an entirely Boolean orthoframe to a connected compatibility frame with globally universal modality. Sharp rare-event coefficients and canonical first-defect structures make the two classes quantitatively comparable. This is a finite-model invariant of a recently characterized semantic class rather than a free-standing random-graph reformulation.

## Limits and sources
The claim is for labelled, valuation-free, nonempty finite frames and one specified uniform measure in each of two classes. No universality is claimed for physically induced measures or isomorphism-weighted measures. Relevant sources: Tokuo, arXiv:2607.14757 (2026); Tokuo, DOI:10.1093/jigpal/jzae123 (2024 online); Bollobás–Grimmett–Janson, DOI:10.1007/BF01213683 (1996); Mayes, arXiv:2201.05485 (2022).
