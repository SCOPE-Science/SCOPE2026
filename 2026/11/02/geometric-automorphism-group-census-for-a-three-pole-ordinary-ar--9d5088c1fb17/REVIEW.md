# Self-review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The full claim is a finite orbit classification for all primes \(p\ge5\). Three simple poles give genus and \(p\)-rank \(2(p-1)\). The relevant exceptional Artin--Schreier cases are excluded by distinct genus or \(p\)-rank (or by characteristic), so the unique degree-\(p\) rational quotient applies. A coboundary with only simple poles has no nonconstant rational primitive; hence geometric isomorphisms coincide with projectivized coefficient-permutation orbits. Two explicit rational substitution identities give the \(S_3\)-action. Fixed-point counts and orbit-stabilizer give all four frequencies. A splitting theorem identifies the corresponding full groups, including the different deck actions. The standalone finite verifier independently checks the combinatorial formulas for many primes, but the infinite result is mathematical rather than empirical.

## Originality
**PASS, qualified.** The October 2026 existence theorem (arXiv:2610.13279) does not enumerate isomorphism classes of its three-pole stratum. The February 2026 invariant-ring analysis (arXiv:2602.22435, Section 6.3) establishes the finite standard-form equivalence action but does not calculate the prime-field torus stabilizer distribution. Ho--Pries (arXiv:2203.10187, Section 5) supplies weighted mass formulas for finite-field covers, not this unweighted geometric automorphism census. Searches for the same coefficient presentation, geometric classes and automorphism groups found no equivalent theorem. The group action was already known, so the claim is the exact new class distribution, not discovery of the action; historical priority under alternative coordinate presentations remains uncertain.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The exact spectrum quantifies the minimal-automorphism phenomenon in a natural ordinary, three-branch-point family, for every prime at least five. It distinguishes the generic \(C_p\) geometric classes from each jump locus and identifies how the residue class of \(p\pmod3\) creates a further exceptional orbit. Exact isomorphism-class and full-group frequencies are useful for arithmetic moduli enumeration and automorphism-weighted versus unweighted comparisons; this is a natural complete finite slice, not an arbitrary small sample.

## Scope and residual risks
The counts concern geometric isomorphism classes represented by prime-field nonzero coefficients, not all geometric members of the moduli stratum and not arithmetic twists. An older classification for the same three-pole coefficient slice or a consequence of a more complete invariant-ring calculation could overlap despite the inspected texts. Independent mathematical validation has not occurred.
