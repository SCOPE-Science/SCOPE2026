# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim's domain is explicit: integers \(N\ge m\ge2\), positive integer block lengths summing to \(N\), uniform hopping \(J>0\), arbitrary real uniform energy shift, and positive (possibly unequal) loss strengths at every block-code vertex. In energy shell \(w\), restricted Walsh characters are indexed by \(m\) independent binary block parities; every parity syndrome contributes one rank precisely at the feasible total weights. The finite geometric-series sum and the identity that the two possible block top weights are \(b_i-1,b_i\) prove the stated generating polynomial without assuming an asymptotic approximation. Its value at one yields the bright dimension. Positivity of the loss operator plus a dissipative-eigenvalue argument proves strictly negative imaginary parts on the bright complement and gives the limiting maximally mixed capture probability. All boundaries, including \(m=N\), give correct ranks. The supplemental checker uses 134 exact Walsh restriction comparisons and 30 independently constructed Krylov spans.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence, with residual risk
Compared against Moore–Russell's complete quantum-hypercube dynamics, Novo et al.'s single-target transport reductions, Le et al.'s general dark-state graph theory, Razzoli et al.'s transport-efficiency analysis, related published mathematical summaries, and accessible prior findings. Prior one-block antipodal-trap and Hamming-ball trap results cover different special cases, not this rank polynomial for all nonisometric product-repetition-code trap geometries. Standard Fourier diagonalization and general observability principles are credited. An unlocated earlier coding-theory or equitable-partition theorem might imply the calculation, so priority is provisional; a failed search alone is not evidence of novelty.

## Value — PASS
The precise full energy-shell rank profile, not merely the total dark dimension, gives an exact invariant across many nonisometric multitrapping configurations and a loss-rate-independent capture law. Product repetition codes are natural structured trap configurations, so the size-versus-geometry comparison answers a motivated transport-design question rather than a free parameter substitution. Examples with different minimum trap-code distance nevertheless have the identical quantum dark spectrum. The result does not claim a finite-time equivalence.

Limits: complete nonoverlapping product-repetition-code traps, positive diagonal losses, single-particle uniform hypercube; prior exact product-code absorber tables could narrow historical priority.
