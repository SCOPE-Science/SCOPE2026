# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Each element of the rank-\(r\) class is determined by an interval kernel and increasing image; the two semigroup inverse equations are exactly two independent crossed-transversal conditions. Their incidence number is the composition generating-function coefficient \(\binom{n+r-1}{2r-1}\). The mode ratio is exact and Pell's equation gives all ties. Stirling's uniform interior approximation and discrete Laplace's method yield the global constant, local normal law, and exponentially small chance of mutual inversehood for independent maps. Direct semigroup multiplication checks every pair for \(n\le6\); an independent incidence enumerator checks \(n\le11\) and the arithmetic and asymptotic calculations.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** The 2026 Higgins paper describes the entire crossed-square inverse relation and proves existence of permutation matchings; this important structural prior information is acknowledged. The rank-\(r\) idempotent incidence numbers are also classical (OEIS A078812; Lavers). This result does not claim those components as original. Its specific additional claim is the rank law *for uniformly sampled inverse pairs*, the exact Pell characterization of tied modes and the Gaussian local limit with its constant and growth rate. These quantitative consequences are not stated or worked out in the two inspected Higgins articles, and targeted mathematical-record and literature searches located no equal or stronger result. The combinatorial antecedents mean the priority risk is real; the earlier Lavers chapter was not fully accessible and the 1993 Higgins copy was partly garbled. This is a qualified originality self-review judgment, not a historical uniqueness certificate.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The new rank law quantifies the density and structure of the inverse relation in a monoid whose permutation-matchability was resolved recently; it separates mere existence of a matching from the number and typical rank of available inverse edges. The exact arithmetic mode classification reveals infinitely many exceptional chain sizes rather than a single continuous approximation, while the Gaussian law and golden-ratio growth quantify the entire large-chain regime. This is a natural invariant with a complete exact-and-asymptotic answer, not an arbitrary finite slice.

**Limitations and overlap risk.** Any inference that an explicit crossed-square relation automatically contains its edge count must distinguish logical definability from having derived the asymptotic and Pell analysis. The idempotent array and its Fibonacci properties are prior work. Higgins (1993) and Lavers (1998) are especially relevant possible antecedents, not proved not to cover the new refinements because full material inspection was incomplete. No claim is made about an involution matching or other transformation monoids.
