# Gonality and tetragonal-pencil rigidity of the maximal Kummer curves \(y^{q+1}=x^2(x^2+1)\)

## Finding

Let \(q\) be any odd prime power, let \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_q\), and let \(C_q\) be the smooth projective curve whose function field is
\[
k(C_q)=k(x,y),\qquad y^{q+1}=x^2(x^2+1).
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{gon}_k(C_q)=\min\left\{4,\frac{q+1}{2}\right\}=
\begin{cases}2,&q=3,\\3,&q=5,\\4,&q\ge7.\end{cases}
\]
For all \(q\ge13\), the rational function \(y\) induces the **unique geometric degree-four pencil** up to automorphisms of its target \(\mathbb P^1\). For \(q=7\), two distinct degree-four pencils are exhibited by \(y\) and the function \(t=x+y^{(q+1)/2}/x\). No uniqueness assertion is made for \(q=9,11\).

## Assumptions and scope

All curves are taken over the algebraically closed field \(k\) in odd characteristic \(p\), with \(q=p^e\), \(e\ge1\). Gonality means the minimum degree of a nonconstant morphism \(C_q\to\mathbb P^1_k\); equivalently, the minimum of \([k(C_q):k(f)]\) over nonconstant rational functions \(f\). A pencil is identified with its rational subfield; two maps are equivalent exactly when their rational subfields coincide, equivalently when they differ by a fractional linear transformation. Write \(m=q+1\), \(d=m/2\). Both are prime to \(p\).

## Proof

**1. Function-field degree, genus and two pencils.** The rational function \(x^2(x^2+1)\) has valuations \(2,1,1,-4\) at \(0,\sqrt{-1},-\sqrt{-1},\infty\). In particular, the valuation \(1\) ensures the Kummer extension \(k(C_q)/k(x)\) has degree \(m\). The tame Kummer Riemann--Hurwitz formula gives
\[
2g-2=-2m+\sum_{v\in\{0,i,-i,\infty\}}(m-\gcd(m,\operatorname{ord}_v(x^2(x^2+1))))=2m-4-\gcd(m,4),
\]
so \(g=m-1-\gcd(m,4)/2\). In particular \(g=1,4,5\) for \(q=3,5,7\), and \(g\ge8\) for \(q\ge9\).

Let \(z=y^d/x\). Then \(z^2=x^2+1\). With \(t=x+z\), we have \(t^{-1}=z-x\), and thus
\[
x=\frac{t-t^{-1}}2,\qquad z=\frac{t+t^{-1}}2,\qquad y^d=\frac{t^4-1}{4t^2}.
\]
This last Kummer equation is degree \(d\) over \(k(t)\), because it has a simple zero at \(t=1\). Hence \([k(C_q):k(t)]=d\). The relation \(x^4+x^2=y^m\) gives \([k(C_q):k(y)]\le4\). Equality follows from four distinct transformations over \(k(y)\), namely the Klein four generated by \(a(x)=-x\) and \(b(x)=iy^d/x\), each fixing \(y\), where \(i^2=-1\). Consequently \(y\) has degree \(4\). These yield the upper bound \(\operatorname{gon}\le\min(4,d)\).

**2. Quadratic intermediates of the degree-four pencil.** Set \(w=2x^2+1\), \(u=x+iy^d/x\), \(v=x-iy^d/x\). The three distinct quadratic subfields between \(k(y)\) and \(k(C_q)\) are
\[
k(y,w):w^2=1+4y^m,\qquad
k(y,u):u^2=-1+2iy^d,\qquad
k(y,v):v^2=-1-2iy^d.
\]
Since \(p\ne2\) and \(p\nmid md\), the polynomials have simple roots. The three quotient genera are
\[
\frac{m-2}{2},\qquad \left\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\right\rfloor,\qquad
\left\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]
They are all at least \(2\) for \(q\ge9\). In particular, no quadratic intermediate of \(k(C_q)/k(y)\) is rational for \(q\ge9\).

**3. Gonality at the two smallest parameters.** At \(q=3\), the genus is \(1\); the displayed map \(t\) has degree \(2\), and a positive-genus curve cannot admit a degree-one map to \(\mathbb P^1\). At \(q=5\), \(t\) has degree \(3\), whereas \(g=4\). If an independent degree-two map existed, the Castelnuovo--Severi inequality for a degree-two and a degree-three map to \(\mathbb P^1\) would give \(g\le(2-1)(3-1)=2\). The degrees are coprime, so the two maps necessarily generate the full function field; contradiction. Thus the gonality is respectively \(2\) and \(3\).

**4. The exceptional tetragonal parameter \(q=7\).** Here \(g=5\) and both \(t\) and \(y\) have degree \(4\). No degree-two map can exist: if it were independent of \(y\), Castelnuovo--Severi would give \(g\le3\); otherwise it would exhibit a rational quadratic intermediate of \(k(C_q)/k(y)\), but each such intermediate has genus \(1\) or \(3\). A degree-three map \(f\), if it existed, would be unique up to target automorphisms: two distinct degree-three maps would generate the full function field, since degree \(3\) is prime, and give \(g\le4\). All automorphisms would therefore preserve the unique trigonal rational subfield. However, the transformations
\[
(x,y)\longmapsto(\epsilon x,\zeta y),\qquad \epsilon\in\{+1,-1\},\quad\zeta^8=1,
\]
form a subgroup \(C_2\times C_8\) of order \(16\). Its action on any degree-three rational subfield is faithful: the kernel has order dividing the separable extension degree \(3\), hence has trivial intersection with a \(2\)-group. The group would embed in \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(k)\) in characteristic \(7\). An element of order \(8\) in this group is semisimple, and its centralizer is the diagonal torus; any finite subgroup centralizing it is cyclic. This contradicts the noncyclic abelian group \(C_2\times C_8\). Thus no trigonal map exists, and the gonality equals \(4\). Moreover, \(a\) fixes \(y\) but sends \(t\) to \(-t\), so the two degree-four rational subfields differ.

**5. All remaining parameters.** Let \(q\ge9\), so \(g\ge8\). A degree-three map \(f\) cannot exist: if separable, \(f\) and the degree-four \(y\) generate the entire field because their degrees are coprime, which would give \(g\le(3-1)(4-1)=6\). A degree-three map can be inseparable only in characteristic \(3\); then it is purely inseparable of degree \(3\), forcing \(k(C_q)\) to be a purely inseparable degree-three extension of a rational function field over perfect \(k\), hence rational, again impossible. A degree-two map, when paired with \(y\), either generates the whole field (forcing \(g\le3\)) or yields a rational quadratic intermediate, excluded in Step 2. Degree one is impossible. Thus \(\operatorname{gon}(C_q)=4\) for every \(q\ge9\).

**6. Uniqueness for \(q\ge13\).** Here \(g>9\). Let \(f\) be any function of degree \(4\), set \(K=k(C_q)\), \(L=k(y,f)\), and \(e=[K:L]\). Necessarily \(e\mid4\). If \(e=1\), the two degree-four maps are independent and Castelnuovo--Severi gives \(g\le9\), impossible. If \(e=2\), then \(L\) is one of the three quadratic intermediates in Step 2, all of genus at least \(2\). Both \(y\) and \(f\) induce degree-two maps from this intermediate curve to \(\mathbb P^1\). A curve of genus at least two has a unique degree-two rational subfield, so \(k(y)=k(f)\). If \(e=4\), likewise \(L=k(y)=k(f)\). Hence every degree-four map differs from \(y\) by a fractional linear change of parameter, as claimed.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` checks the rational substitutions, Klein-four involution identities, the three quotient equations, and genus and degree inequalities on explicit prime-power test values. Run with `python3 verify.py` in an environment with SymPy installed; successful execution terminates in `VERIFY_OK`. The claims for infinitely many prime powers, the Castelnuovo--Severi arguments, the characteristic-seven stabilizer obstruction and uniqueness all rest on the proofs above, not on finite testing.

## Relationship to prior work

Guardieiro, da Silva and Tafazolian (arXiv:2605.25257v1, 2026) introduce and study the ambient family \(y^{q+1}=x^n(x^n+1)\), including its maximality, Weierstrass semigroups and automorphisms. The present curve is its \(n=2\) member; the formula for its genus and its visible symmetries are not asserted as new. Their inspected full HTML manuscript does not state geometric gonality or degree-four pencil uniqueness. Beelen, Montanucci and Vicino (2023) study the exponent \(n=(q+1)/3\), which coincides with \(n=2\) when \(q=5\). Their displayed model at that parameter already gives a degree-three map and is treated as prior knowledge; their work does not classify gonality for all \(n=2\) parameters or establish high-genus tetragonal rigidity. The Castelnuovo--Severi inequality and uniqueness of degree-two maps on a hyperelliptic curve are standard tools, not new theorems. The specifically assessed contribution is the uniform sharp gonality classification together with its characteristic-seven transition and degree-four-pencil rigidity for \(q\ge13\).

## Limitations

No assertion is made for characteristic two or for the parameters \(n\ne2\). The result concerns geometric, not ground-field-specific, gonality. The degree-four pencils at \(q=9,11\) are not classified. The classification does not determine higher-dimensional special linear systems or Brill--Noether strata. Although close primary sources and mathematical research records were compared, priority with respect to all possible older general superelliptic gonality theorems is not established, and the 2023 source comparison was based on its accessible full text rather than a systematic search of all its citations.
