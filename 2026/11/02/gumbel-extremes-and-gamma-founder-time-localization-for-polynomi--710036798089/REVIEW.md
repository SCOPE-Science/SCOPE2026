# Scientific review: Gumbel extremes and Gamma founder-time localization

## Correctness — PASS
The exact critical geometric-clan law is derived from the iterated probability-generating function, not estimated from simulation. Compact immigration-time windows contribute a Gamma-profiled exceedance intensity, and both early and late founding times are controlled by uniform envelopes. Independent rare indicators then yield a marked Poisson process, Gumbel leader size, and independent Gamma founder time. The exact clan second moment yields total-population concentration and the claimed fraction. All quantifiers are fixed real \(c>0\), \(\alpha>0\), with integer time \(n\to\infty\); the endpoint \(\alpha=0\) is expressly excluded. The arithmetic checker replayed 3,214 finite tests, which do not stand in for the proof.

## Originality — PASS (bounded priority comparison)
The closest published result is Mitov–Pakes–Yanev (2003), which identifies maxima of one-generation offspring across a branching generation and row maxima of same-law geometric variables, not descendants of differently aged immigrant founders. Barczy–Bezdány (arXiv:2609.18503 and arXiv:2610.39771) study aggregate immigration-population functional limits rather than ancestor-resolved extreme marks. Smadi–Vatutin (2021) condition on a sole surviving clan in a shared random environment; Fontes–Schinazi (2026) use nonextinguishing, fitness-dependent families. These claims do not imply the present \(n\log n\) leader scale and independent Gamma founder-time law. The full earlier Yanev–Tsokos paper was not accessible, and more general geometric-triangular-array results may imply parts; definitive historical priority requires independent comparison.

## Mathematical value — PASS
It is mathematically natural to ask whether the oldest, most numerous or youngest immigrants create the dominant lineage, and whether the leader controls population mass. The theorem resolves the full joint extreme and founder-time law in a critical, precisely solvable model, including the population-share scale. It reveals that the winning founder arrives at time order \(n/\log n\) even while the largest family contributes only order \(\log n/n^\alpha\) of total population. This is a structural localization law, not a single finite calculation.
