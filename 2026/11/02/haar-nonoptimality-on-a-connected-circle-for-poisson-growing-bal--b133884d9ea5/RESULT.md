# Haar nonoptimality on a connected circle for Poisson growing-ball coverage

## Finding
Let \(S=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\) be the circle of circumference one with shortest-arc metric. In the unit-rate Poisson growing-ball model with unit radial speed, write \(C_\mu\) for the time when balls generated by seed centers with law \(\mu\) first cover \(S\). Let \(h\) denote Haar (normalized arclength) measure and put
\[
\nu=\tfrac12\delta_0+\tfrac12\delta_{1/2},\qquad
\mu_* = \tfrac{1023}{1024}\nu+\tfrac1{1024}h.
\]
Then the connected compact group \(S\) has the strict, rationally certified comparisons
\[
\mathbb E C_h-\mathbb E C_\nu
\ge \frac{61807}{62914560}>0,
\qquad
\mathbb E C_h-\mathbb E C_{\mu_*}
\ge \frac{15727}{62914560}>0.
\]
Thus Haar measure does not minimize mean cover time even on a connected compact group, and the counterexample can be chosen to have full topological support. The exact antipodal expectation is
\[
\mathbb E C_\nu=\frac94-e^{-1/4}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
A rate-one homogeneous Poisson process has arrival times \(0<\tau_1<\tau_2<\cdots\). Conditional on these times, seed positions \(\sigma_i\in S\) are independent with common law \(\mu\), independent of the arrival process. At time \(t\), seed \(i\) has grown a closed metric ball of radius \(t-\tau_i\) when \(\tau_i\le t\); the covered set is their union. The cover time is the first time the entire circle is covered. Closed-ball boundary conventions do not affect the expectations. No density or atomlessness assumption is imposed on seed positions, although \(\mu_*\) has full support. Only this particular circumference and unit time/space normalization are asserted.

## Proof
**First arrival and reduction.** The first arrival \(\tau_1\) has expectation one. For every law, its seed alone covers the entire circle by time \(\tau_1+1/2\). Hence writing \(H_\mu=C_\mu-\tau_1\), we have \(0\le H_\mu\le1/2\), and \(\mathbb E C_\mu=1+\mathbb E H_\mu\). Under Haar law, rotation invariance permits conditioning the first center to be \(0\); subsequent centers remain Haar distributed and their shifted arrivals are a fresh rate-one Poisson process.

**Antipodal law.** After the initial center at one support point of \(\nu\), seeds at its opposite point appear at rate \(1/2\). Let \(W\) be the delay to the first such opposite seed, so \(W\sim\operatorname{Exp}(1/2)\). Subsequent seeds at either of these two positions cannot outrun the first seed at that position. Two antipodal balls of radii \(u\) and \(u-W\), with \(u\ge W\), cover the circle exactly when their radii sum to at least \(1/2\). It follows that
\[
H_\nu=\min\left\{\tfrac12,\tfrac14+\tfrac W2\right\},
\qquad
\mathbb E H_\nu=\tfrac14+\int_{1/4}^{1/2}e^{1/4-u}\,du
=\tfrac54-e^{-1/4}.
\]

**Haar lower bound using three target locations.** Work after the first Haar seed, placed by rotation at \(0\). For \(0\le u<1/2\), that first ball has not reached the antipode \(1/2\). A later seed born at shifted time \(s\in[0,u]\) covers this point by time \(u\) exactly when its Haar-uniform center belongs to an arc of length \(2(u-s)\). The Poisson void formula therefore yields
\[
\Pr(H_h>u)\ge e^{-u^2}.
\]
To sharpen this, use the two additional target points \(z_1=3/8\) and \(z_2=5/8\). The first seed does not reach either of them for \(u<3/8\). Each has uncovered probability \(e^{-u^2}\) against subsequent seeds. Their joint uncovered probability is \(e^{-J(u)}\), where \(J(u)\) is the integrated Haar length of the union of two radius-\(r\) arcs around the targets:
\[
J(u)=\int_0^u |B(z_1,r)\cup B(z_2,r)|\,dr
=\begin{cases}
2u^2,&0\le u\le1/8,\\
u^2+u/4-1/64,&1/8\le u\le3/8.
\end{cases}
\]
Indeed the target separation is \(1/4\); the union length is \(4r\) for \(r\le1/8\) and \(2r+1/4\) for \(1/8\le r\le3/8\). By inclusion–exclusion, the probability at least one of the two targets is uncovered is \(2e^{-u^2}-e^{-J(u)}\). Since complete coverage requires covering both, combining this event on \([0,3/8)\) with the antipodal event on \([3/8,1/2)\) gives
\[
\mathbb E H_h
\ge \int_0^{1/2}e^{-u^2}\,du
+\int_0^{3/8}\left(e^{-u^2}-e^{-J(u)}\right)\,du.
\tag{1}
\]

**Exact rational separation.** For every real \(x\ge0\), Taylor's theorem with signed remainder gives
\[
P_3(x):=1-x+\frac{x^2}{2}-\frac{x^3}{6}
\le e^{-x}\le
Q_2(x):=1-x+\frac{x^2}{2}.
\]
Apply the lower polynomial to each positive \(e^{-u^2}\) term in (1), and the upper polynomial to its subtracted \(e^{-J(u)}\) term. Elementary integration on the two rational subintervals gives the **exact** lower certificate
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E H_h &\ge
\int_0^{1/2}P_3(u^2)\,du
+\int_0^{1/8}\left(P_3(u^2)-Q_2(2u^2)\right)\,du\\
&\quad+\int_{1/8}^{3/8}\left(P_3(u^2)-Q_2(u^2+u/4-1/64)\right)\,du
=\frac{29716847}{62914560}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Using \(e^{-1/4}\ge P_3(1/4)=299/384\) gives
\[
\mathbb E H_\nu=\tfrac54-e^{-1/4}
\le\tfrac{181}{384}.
\]
Subtracting yields
\[
\mathbb E C_h-\mathbb E C_\nu
\ge\frac{29716847}{62914560}-\frac{181}{384}
=\frac{61807}{62914560}>0.
\]

**Explicit full-support law.** Couple \(\mu_*\) and \(\nu\) with identical Poisson arrival times, and independently replace each \(\nu\)-distributed seed by a Haar seed with probability \(\varepsilon=1/1024\). Let \(A\) be the event of at least one replacement among arrivals through \(\tau_1+1/2\). Their cover times coincide off \(A\), while always differing by at most \(1/2\). The expected number of arrivals in that time interval is \(1+1/2=3/2\), so a union bound gives \(\Pr(A)\le(3/2)\varepsilon\) and
\[
\left|\mathbb E C_{\mu_*}-\mathbb E C_\nu\right|
\le\tfrac12\Pr(A)\le\frac3{4096}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbb E C_h-\mathbb E C_{\mu_*}
\ge\frac{61807}{62914560}-\frac3{4096}
=\frac{15727}{62914560}>0.
\]
Because \(\mu_*\) gives positive mass to every nonempty open arc, it has full topological support. This completes the claim.

## Verification
The standard-library-only `check.py` evaluates every displayed rational polynomial integral with exact fractions, verifies continuity of the integrated arc-union formula at \(1/8\), checks its geometric slope transitions, and verifies the two strictly positive rational gaps. The checker executed successfully from the newly produced package. This is a finite **algebraic** verification of exact coefficients; the Poisson-void, coupling, and Taylor-remainder steps are analytic proofs stated above, not conclusions inferred from simulation. The proof uses no stochastic simulation, external numerical integrator, unpublished dataset, or nonstandard probabilistic lemma.

## Relationship to prior work
Aldous introduced this Poisson growing-ball model and explicitly asked whether Haar seed placement minimizes mean cover time for a compact group with invariant metric. His January 2021 preprint raises the question in Section 4.2; the published 2022 version retains it in Section 7.1 and discusses fixed-radius examples separately. The present claim establishes a counterexample **on the connected circle**, whereas conclusions confined to finite cyclic groups cannot imply it. Falgas-Ravry, Larsson, and Markström analyze i.i.d. fixed random covering sets or coupons and fixed-radius coverage; their stopping time and coverage mechanism differ from continually expanding balls from timed seeds. Their fixed-radius observations do not entail the present inequality.

## Limitations
No globally minimizing seed law is identified, and there is no assertion about optimality or uniqueness of the antipodal law. The rational bounds prove strict inequalities without giving the exact Haar cover-time mean. The result is for one connected circle geometry and rate/speed normalization, not every circumference or other connected compact group. The explicit full-support mixture may not minimize the mean. Historical-priority comparisons are limited to accessible texts and indexed research records; independent audit has not been performed.
