# Self-review: half-mass colored-hypercube stars

**Correctness — PASS.** The critical Poisson process estimate uses exact independent marginal probabilities, a bounded-radius dependency graph and independent Poisson-point-process approximation. For different critical palettes at the same center, the overlap correction is exponentially small because every color has strictly positive probability. For neighboring centers, all intersecting restrictions occupy at least \(2d-2\) input sites. Explicit bounds are \(b_1\le m^2D_d2^{-d}\) and \(b_2\le m^2\rho^d+4m^2D_d2^{-d}\). The quantitative theorem follows from the published point-process bound and the data-processing inequality. Markov and Chebyshev prove the other two regimes. The first-order result is only for Boolean combinations of fixed local-star threshold sentences. The independent checker passes 82,096 exact rational indicator-pair checks plus full small-model enumerations; no finite experiment is used in place of an infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS within available sources.** Weber (1987) has a genuinely overlapping Poisson site-percolation marginal for the binary cube, and Arratia, Goldstein and Gordon (1989) provide both the general process theorem and an oriented-edge hypercube example; neither claim is presented as new. The full Weber article could not be inspected, making it a substantial residual priority risk. Search nonmatches alone are not novelty evidence.

**Value — PASS.** The mathematically natural threshold \(p(S)=1/2\) splits every local-star test into extinction, concentration, or rare-event phases. Despite the heavily overlapping closed neighborhoods and cross-palette events, *all* balanced tests and center marks become jointly independent Poisson counts. The complete star-sentence evaluation gives a nontrivial concrete first-order limit-law classification, notably a palette-size parity phase transition. No wider first-order zero–one law, priority certification, or independent proof check is claimed.

**Closest literature and limits.** Arratia–Goldstein–Gordon (1989), Theorem 2, is the explicitly used general lemma. Weber (1987) is a close marginal predecessor, but its complete text could not be assessed; this is the principal literature limitation. The count theorem is for fixed strictly positive palette weights and independent vertex colors only. The star-fragment logical consequence does not address arbitrary first-order sentences.
