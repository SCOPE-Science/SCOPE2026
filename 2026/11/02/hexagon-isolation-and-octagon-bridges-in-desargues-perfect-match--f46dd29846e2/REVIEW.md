# Scientific review: Desargues perfect-matching flips

## Correctness

**PASS.** Every perfect matching of the precisely specified bipartite double cover corresponds bijectively to a Petersen-supported permutation. Its cycle-type classification follows from the Petersen cycle and complementary-vertex analysis. The symmetric-difference/relative-permutation correspondence gives the exact local flip rule. Two separately implemented exhaustive checkers enumerate all matchings and all \(1770\) unordered pairs, one using permutations and the other labelled generalized-Petersen edge masks. Direct all-source breadth-first searches agree on components, degrees, distance profiles and the doubled-\(K_6\) property. A further exact Ryser inclusion–exclusion permanent calculation confirms the count \(60\) independently of both enumeration recursions. The finite proofs do not imply any untested infinite-family claim.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to stated residual risks.** Targeted comparisons with Csikvári's vertex-transitive-bipartite matching bounds (arXiv:1407.5409), Tratnik–Ye's *facial* surface resonance graphs (arXiv:1710.00761), Ito and coauthors' general unrestricted alternating-cycle reconfiguration (arXiv:1907.01700), a Desargues matching-polynomial article (Symmetry 17 (2025), 133) and mathematical research-record searches did not identify a theorem implying the exact non-facial bounded-cycle graph, its component metrics or its six-factor incidence geometry. The closest other records concern the Desargues matching complex and the Desargues three-colouring Kempe graph, which are mathematically different constructions. Neither limited textual search nor distinct nomenclature proves priority. The 2025 article's stated count of \(120\) is at odds with the exact count of \(60\) for the explicitly presented graph and is recorded as a discrepancy requiring reconciliation; its full publisher text was not accessible for a complete comparison.

## Value

**PASS.** A canonical distance-transitive cubic bipartite graph supplies a natural reconfiguration question about its optimal one-factor configurations. The sharp local flip threshold, the complete shortest-path metric and the unusual doubled-\(K_6\) interpretation using all six Petersen one-factors give structural information not supplied by knowing the number of matchings. The result is a complete, reproducible finite classification rather than an arbitrary small subcase or merely a recomputation of a known table.

## Scope and limitations

Only the labelled Desargues graph and the stated non-facial six/eight alternating-cycle rules are proved. An equivalent historical formulation could exist under one-factor, resonance or dimer terminology. A published matching-count discrepancy remains unresolved; it is not silently represented as a correction to the publication. Independent scholarly review has not occurred.
