# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The domain, characteristic-independent bracket, ideal definition, and Gaussian normalization are explicit. Projection onto the vertex space and intersection with the center classify ideals by triples \((S,T,\phi)\), with \(\phi:S\to Z/T\). The star bracket splits the count exhaustively into \(S\subseteq U\) and \(S\not\subseteq U\), producing exactly the two summands. The \(q\)-degree comparison proves the fixed-dimension large-field limit. Direct RREF-subspace enumeration checks the polynomial for six small field/graph configurations, but is not substituted for an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected evidence

The star graph and its Lie algebra are established constructions [1,2]. DeCoste et al. [2], Section 3, treat abelian factors, not ideal counts. Barrionuevo and Tirao [1] classify rigidity of graph Lie algebras in a different setting. Benito and Roldán-López [3] classify certain Lie algebras with finite ideal lattices over characteristic zero, not finite-field counts. Rossmann [4] treats centralizers and conjugacy classes, which do not imply the specified ideal enumeration. Searches for finite-field ideal enumeration, Gaussian ideal polynomials, ideal zeta, and generalized-Heisenberg formulations found no covering theorem. Nevertheless, equivalent older results under alternative terminology cannot be ruled out; the novelty verdict is relative to the specific material inspected, not a universal priority certificate.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Lie ideals determine quotient Lie algebras and invariant subspace structure. Exact dimension-refined ideal counts are natural invariants for the central-extension family attached to all distinctly labeled star graphs. The result gives every stratum for every prime power, and explains an even–odd large-field transition in the dimension of a uniformly chosen ideal. Unlike a single small computation, this is a closed invariant for an infinite two-parameter family, with a proof retaining tilted as well as graded ideals. The low-dimensional cases and extension-field validation are clearly delimited.
