# Idempotent-centralizer order reconstructs semilattice graph products

## Finding

For every finite simple graph \(\Gamma=(V,E)\), including the empty graph, let
\[
M_\Gamma=\langle v\ (v\in V)\mid v^2=v,\quad uv=vu\ \text{for}\ \{u,v\}\in E\rangle
\]
be the graph product of two-element semilattice monoids. For \(x\in M_\Gamma\), denote its multiplicative centralizer by \(C(x)\). Form the **unlabelled poset of distinct idempotent centralizers**
\[
\mathcal C(\Gamma)=\{C(e):e\in M_\Gamma,\ e^2=e\},
\]
ordered by set inclusion. Let \(U\subseteq V\) be the set of universal vertices, each adjacent to every other vertex, and let \(H=\Gamma[V\setminus U]\) be the induced graph after removing them. Write \(\mathcal F(H)\) for the poset of all cliques of \(H\), including the empty clique, ordered by inclusion.

Then
\[
\boxed{\mathcal C(\Gamma)\cong\mathcal F(H)^{\mathrm{op}},\qquad Z(M_\Gamma)=\{e_T:T\subseteq U\},\qquad |Z(M_\Gamma)|=2^{|U|}.}
\]
Here \(e_T\) denotes the product of vertex generators indexed by clique \(T\), with \(e_\varnothing=1\). In particular, the pair consisting of the unlabelled centralizer-poset order and the size of the center **recovers the entire defining graph up to isomorphism**. Equivalently, for finite simple graphs \(\Gamma,\Lambda\),
\[
\boxed{M_\Gamma\cong M_\Lambda\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathcal C(\Gamma)\cong\mathcal C(\Lambda)\ \text{as posets and}\ |Z(M_\Gamma)|=|Z(M_\Lambda)|.}
\]
The reconstruction statement is the scientific claim assessed here. The elementary centralizer identities below give a self-contained proof; determining centralizers in general graph products of semigroups is prior literature, not claimed as new.

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are finite, undirected, loopless and without multiple edges. Their vertex sets may be empty. The monoid is specified by the indicated generator-idempotence and edge-commutation relations only; it is generally infinite and is **not** the quotient in which every monoid element is idempotent. The center comprises elements that commute with all monoid elements. The poset \(\mathcal C(\Gamma)\) remembers inclusion among **distinct subsets of the monoid**, but forgets the names of their idempotent generators; the integer \(|Z(M_\Gamma)|\) is an additional invariant. The theorem does not reconstruct a graph from the poset alone when universal vertices are present.

For a clique \(S\subseteq V\), set \(e_S=\prod_{s\in S}s\), and for a subset \(W\subseteq V\), let \(M_W\) be the full subgraph-product submonoid generated by \(W\). Define \(\mathrm{link}(v)=\{w\ne v:\{v,w\}\in E\}\).

## Proof

**1. Supports and two-letter retractions.** All defining relations preserve the set of letters occurring in a word, so the support \(\mathrm{supp}(w)\subseteq V\) of a monoid element is well-defined. For nonadjacent distinct vertices \(a,b\), killing all other generators gives a homomorphism to
\[
B_{a,b}=\langle a,b\mid a^2=a,\ b^2=b\rangle.
\]
Every nonidentity element of this free product has a unique alternating reduced word. A nonidentity alternating word commutes with \(a\) precisely when its reduced word starts and ends in \(a\). The center of \(B_{a,b}\) is \(\{1\}\): no nonidentity word can commute with both \(a\) and \(b\).

**2. Idempotents.** If \(\mathrm{supp}(w)\) contains a nonedge \(\{a,b\}\), its image in \(B_{a,b}\) is a nonidentity alternating word containing both generators. Its square has strictly greater reduced length, hence \(w\) cannot be idempotent. If the support is a clique \(S\), all its generators commute, so repeated generators collapse and \(w=e_S\). Different cliques have different supports. Thus the idempotents of \(M_\Gamma\) are exactly the \(e_S\) for cliques \(S\).

**3. Centralizers of clique idempotents.** For a vertex \(s\), consider a word representing \(w\). If \(s\notin\mathrm{supp}(w)\) and \(sw=ws\), projection to every nonedge \(\{s,b\}\) shows that no nonneighbor \(b\) can occur in \(w\). Consequently \(w\in M_{\mathrm{link}(s)}\). If \(s\in\mathrm{supp}(w)\) and \(sw=ws\), projection to each nonedge \(\{s,b\}\) forces the first and last occurrences among the two letters to be \(s\). Therefore every letter preceding the first occurrence of \(s\), and every letter following its last occurrence, commutes with \(s\). Move those occurrences to the ends of the word; the relation \(s^2=s\) gives \(sw=w=ws\). In summary, for any element commuting with \(s\), either \(s\) is absent from its support, in which case all its letters commute with \(s\), or \(s\) is present and \(sw=w=ws\).

If \(e_Sw=we_S\), projection to every two-letter nonedge \(\{s,b\}\) for \(s\in S\) gives \(s\pi(w)=\pi(w)s\), because \(b\notin S\) and \(\pi(e_S)=s\). Applying the preceding word argument separately for each \(s\) proves \(sw=ws\) for every \(s\in S\). The converse is immediate, so
\[
\boxed{C(e_S)=\bigcap_{s\in S}C(s).}
\]
For completeness, this also yields an exact disjoint decomposition. For \(T\subseteq S\), put
\[
W_T=T\cup\bigcap_{s\in S\setminus T}\mathrm{link}(s),
\]
with the intersection over an empty set understood as \(V\). Then
\[
\boxed{C(e_S)=\bigsqcup_{T\subseteq S}e_T M_{W_T}e_T.}
\]
Indeed, for a commuting element take \(T=S\cap\mathrm{supp}(w)\). Present vertices \(t\in T\) absorb the word on both sides, while all absent \(s\in S\setminus T\) commute with every letter. Conversely any word of the displayed sandwich type commutes with each generator in \(S\); its intersection of support with \(S\) is exactly \(T\), proving disjointness. This formula is used here as a proof device rather than asserted as publication-priority novelty.

**4. Exact centralizer containment.** For cliques \(S,T\),
\[
\boxed{C(e_S)\subseteq C(e_T)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T\setminus S\subseteq U.}
\]
If \(T\setminus S\subseteq U\), every generator of \(T\setminus S\) commutes with the entire monoid, and the intersection formula proves inclusion. Conversely choose \(t\in T\setminus S\) not universal, and a nonneighbor \(b\) of \(t\). The element \(w=e_S b e_S\) commutes with \(e_S\), because \(e_S\) is idempotent. But the retraction onto \(B_{t,b}\) sends \(e_T\) to \(t\) and \(w\) to \(b\), so \(w\) cannot commute with \(e_T\). Hence inclusion fails.

**5. Center and recovery.** If \(w\) is central and \(v\) is nonuniversal, choose its nonneighbor \(b\). The image of \(w\) in \(B_{v,b}\) is central, hence is the identity. In particular \(v\) does not occur in \(w\). This holds for each nonuniversal \(v\), so \(\mathrm{supp}(w)\subseteq U\). The universal generators commute with each other; therefore \(w=e_T\) for some \(T\subseteq U\). Conversely every such element is central. This gives the stated Boolean center and its size.

By Step 4, \(C(e_S)\) depends only on \(S\setminus U\), and every clique of \(H\) occurs in this way. The inclusion relation is precisely reverse clique inclusion. The unlabelled face poset reconstructs \(H\): its minimal nonempty faces are the vertices, and a set of vertices spans a face exactly when the corresponding elements have a common upper face (equivalently, when a face with that vertex set occurs). The size of the center recovers \(|U|=\log_2|Z(M_\Gamma)|\); adjoining that number of universal vertices uniquely restores \(\Gamma\). In the empty and complete-graph cases the clique poset is a singleton and the same reconstruction works. Finally, a graph isomorphism relabels the presentation and gives a monoid isomorphism; a monoid isomorphism preserves the centralizer poset and center, proving the two-way classification.

## Verification

A standalone standard-library Python script, `verify.py`, checks **all 75 labelled graph presentations** on one through four vertices (all \(2^{\binom n2}\) graphs for each \(1\le n\le4\)). For each graph it generates all words through length six when \(n\le3\) and length five when \(n=4\), reduces them by commuting shuffles and idempotent cancellations, and checks the idempotent, center, idempotent-centralizer intersection, sandwich-membership and centralizer-inclusion conditions. It tests explicit witnesses for every predicted strict noncontainment. In total it checks 10,971 normalized monoid elements across these graphs. Executing `python3 verify.py` returned `VERIFY_OK: 75 graphs; 10971 normal forms inspected`. This finite test is not an exhaustive verification of the infinite-monoid theorem; the preceding proof is the unrestricted evidence.

## Relationship to prior work

Yang and Gould, *On graph products of monoids* (initial public preprint 12 February 2021; journal publication 2023), establish Foata normal forms and structural tools for general graph products and explicitly raise the centralizer problem in Question 7.10. Alqahtani, Yang and Gould, *Graph products of semigroups* (2021), develops a related semigroup construction and word-reduction theory. Importantly, Yang and Li, *Centralizers in graph products of semigroups* (first publicly available December 2022), **already calculate centralizers for arbitrary elements of general semigroup graph products**. The explicit centralizer descriptions in Steps 3–4 are not claimed to be new relative to that broad work. The distinct result here is the inverse determination of the defining graph from the **unlabelled inclusion poset of idempotent centralizers and the center cardinality**. The obtainable abstract and publisher preview of Yang–Li do not state that reconstruction theorem; its complete text could not be inspected, so a coverage risk remains. General group graph-product centralizer results (Barkauskas, 2007) concern vertex groups, not these idempotent monoids.

## Limitations

The reconstruction applies only to graph products of two-element semilattice monoids, not arbitrary graph products of semigroups or groups. The centralizer poset alone forgets the number of universal vertices; the center size is indispensable. The displayed sandwich identity is an exact specialization of a topic covered by stronger prior centralizer theory and is not claimed as a separate novel theorem. Priority relative to the inaccessible full Yang–Li paper and to less-indexed literature cannot be conclusively established, so independent literature review is needed. No external or independent mathematical audit has been performed.
