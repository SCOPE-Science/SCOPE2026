# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The asserted theorem has a finite, explicitly labeled twelve-vertex icosahedral domain with classical ternary spin values and uniform antiferromagnetic exchange. The key exact identity writes the edge interaction as twice the sum of induced-edge counts within the positive and negative occupied sets, minus the induced-edge count of the full occupied set. The separate integer enumeration of all \(3^{12}=531441\) configurations gives the complete thirteen-row occupancy-minimum certificate and its multiplicities; the independent direct product enumeration gives exactly the same table. In particular, the two sharp inequalities \(b+m/2\ge-4\) and \(b+m\ge0\) yield the entire piecewise ground-energy law for every real \(D/J\), including both boundary equality cases. The intermediate \(75\) induced-\(2K_2\) vacancy sets, their two sign patterns each, and the magnetization-resolved critical counts are fully enumerated, not inferred from samples. The finite-temperature susceptibility identity followed by the finite-volume zero-temperature limit yields the exact Curie coefficient \(40/117\) at the upper transition and zero elsewhere. Re-execution of `check_icosahedron.py` returned all four PASS lines.

Risk: A computer-assisted finite certificate is used for the thirteen occupancy minima and the geometric classifications, rather than a short hand classification of all states. The certificate is complete, integer exact, self-contained, and small enough to rerun; no infinite-volume or continuum assertion is made.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence, with residual priority risk
The closest papers were examined by their actual Hamiltonians and domains. Konstantinidis (2005, arXiv:cond-mat/0503658) concerns two-component and three-component **quantum Heisenberg** spins and magnetic spectra on the icosahedron; a full accessible PDF was inspected. Žukovič–Bobák (2013, arXiv:1309.3593) treats the spin-one Blume–Capel model on a periodically repeated triangular **lattice** using Monte Carlo; its full four-page PDF was inspected. Strečka–Karľová–Madaras (2015, arXiv:1502.04449) includes the icosahedron, but only with non-vacant two-valued Ising spins; its accessible abstract explicitly states that setting. Mohylna–Žukovič (2020, arXiv:2010.16287) includes spin-one vacancies and exact enumeration but explicitly studies a single triangle and a seven-spin pair of corner-sharing tetrahedra, not a twelve-site icosahedron. The latter source was inspected through its indexed model and results sections. The accessible full 2005 and 2013 texts do not imply the four-vacancy induced-matching classification or the isolated Curie coefficient. Search comparisons also considered the misleadingly titled generalized icosahedral spin-alphabet model on a cubic lattice: its sites are not vertices of an icosahedron.

In previously documented work, the pure \(\pm1\) icosahedron with distance-one and distance-two interactions has no vacant-spin sector; the broad Blume–Capel classification for **line graphs of cubic graphs** does not cover the five-regular icosahedral graph (a line graph of a cubic graph is four-regular). General source and semantic-record searches returned no equivalent or logically stronger statement. These are implication-based distinctions, not inferences from titles alone.

Risk: The full text of the 2015 two-valued Ising-polyhedra article was unavailable despite arXiv and open-text attempts; historical priority is not independently certified. A broader unlocated spin-one finite-cluster treatment could affect priority, though none of the examined close sources entails the present theorem. Search absence alone is not treated as a uniqueness proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Geometric frustration on the five-regular icosahedron forbids a proper three-coloring, while a vacancy penalty naturally competes with the frustrated edge energy. The result identifies two precise occupancy jumps and, more strongly, a complete **induced-two-edge combinatorial rule** for every intermediate ground state: the active-spin configurations are not characterized merely by a computed energy or a tiny numerical improvement. Exact degeneracy enhancements at two thresholds and the isolated nonzero low-temperature Curie response supply distinct observable signatures. The geometry is a standard finite molecular-spin test case; the particular vacancy templates and magnetic response are motivated by frustration and cannot be read off from the ordinary fully occupied Ising limit or the prior three-edge-colorable line-graph result.

Risk and boundary: It is a rigorous but finite-cluster result; no claim is made about bulk critical exponents, realistic material parameters, quantum-spin behavior, nonuniform exchange, or finite-temperature phase transitions.
