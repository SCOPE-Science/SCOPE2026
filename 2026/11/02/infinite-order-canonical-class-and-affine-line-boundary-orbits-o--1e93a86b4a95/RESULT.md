# Infinite-order canonical class and affine-line boundary orbits on smooth generically flexible quotients

## Finding

For every integer \(n\geq 1\), over \(\mathbb C\), let
\[
 X_n=\{x^2y+z^3w^2u_1^2\cdots u_n^2=1\}\subset\mathbb A^{n+4}
\]
with the \(\mathbb G_m\)-weights \((1,-2,2,-2,-1,0,\ldots,0)\) on \((x,y,z,w,u_1,u_2,\ldots,u_n)\). Let \(Y_n=X_n/\!/\mathbb G_m\) and let \(\mathcal D_n\) be the image of \(\{x=0\}\). These are the smooth generically flexible quotients and invariant divisors of Nikitina [1]. Then:

1. The complement \(Y_n\setminus\mathcal D_n\) is \(\mathbb A^{n+2}\), whereas \(\mathcal D_n\cong\mathbb A^1\times(\mathbb G_m)^n\). Every orbit of \(\operatorname{SAut}(Y_n)\) **contained in** \(\mathcal D_n\) is exactly an affine-line fiber of the natural projection \(\mathcal D_n\to(\mathbb G_m)^n\). In particular, the invariant divisor has infinitely many distinct such orbits, parameterized by \((\mathbb C^\times)^n\).
2. The full Picard group and canonical bundle are
\[
 \operatorname{Pic}(Y_n)=\mathbb Z[\mathcal D_n],\qquad
 \omega_{Y_n}\cong\mathcal O_{Y_n}(2\mathcal D_n).
\]
Consequently the canonical class has infinite order. The assertion concerns the divisor and canonical class in this specific family, not all smooth generically flexible affine varieties.

## Assumptions and scope

Work over \(\mathbb C\) in characteristic zero. Fix \(n\geq1\) and put \(P=\prod_{i=2}^n u_i^2\), with \(P=1\) for \(n=1\). The weights give a regular \(\mathbb G_m\)-action preserving the equation. The quotient is geometric: the invariant opens \(\{x\neq0\}\) and \(\{u_1\neq0\}\) cover \(X_n\), and a weight-\(1\) or weight-\((-1)\) invertible coordinate trivializes the action on each. Hence \(X_n\to Y_n\) is a principal \(\mathbb G_m\)-torsor. The divisor \(\mathcal D_n\) is reduced and irreducible. Here \(\operatorname{SAut}\) denotes the subgroup generated by algebraic \(\mathbb G_a\)-actions; its preservation of \(\mathcal D_n\) is Nikitina's Proposition 8 [1]. The orbit description is only for points of \(\mathcal D_n\).

## Proof

**Charts and the boundary.** On \(x\neq0\), the weight-zero coordinates
\[
 Z=z/x^2,\quad W=x^2w,\quad V=xu_1,\quad U_i=u_i\ (2\leq i\leq n)
\]
are free: the hypersurface equation determines \(x^2y=1-Z^3W^2V^2P\). Setting the gauge \(x=1\) proves
\[
 Y_n\setminus\mathcal D_n\cong\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C[Z,W,V,U_2,\ldots,U_n].
\]
On \(u_1\neq0\), take the independent invariant coordinates
\[
 s=xu_1,\quad q=zu_1^2,\quad \rho=w/u_1^2,\quad \eta=y/u_1^2,\quad U_i=u_i.
\]
The quotient chart is the hypersurface
\[
 s^2\eta+q^3\rho^2P=1.
\]
The boundary has \(s=0\), hence \(q^3\rho^2P=1\), so \(q,\rho,U_i\) are units there. Set \(B=q\rho=zw\) and \(C=q\eta=yz\). Its inverse is
\[
 q=(B^2P)^{-1},\qquad \rho=B^3P,\qquad \eta=CB^2P.
\]
Thus \(\mathcal D_n\cong\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C[B^{\pm1},U_2^{\pm1},\ldots,U_n^{\pm1},C]\). This also proves that the boundary is smooth and irreducible. The two quotient charts are smooth: on \(s\neq0\), the equation has \(\partial/\partial\eta=s^2\neq0\), and on \(s=0\), it has \(\partial/\partial q=3q^2\rho^2P\neq0\).

**Boundary orbits.** By [1, Proposition 8], every algebraic \(\mathbb G_a\)-action on \(Y_n\) preserves \(\mathcal D_n\). Every such action on the boundary fixes the units \(B,U_2,\ldots,U_n\): indeed, the units of \(\mathbb C[B^{\pm1},U_i^{\pm1},C,t]\) come from \(\mathbb C[B^{\pm1},U_i^{\pm1},C]\), and setting \(t=0\) fixes them. Therefore every \(\operatorname{SAut}\)-orbit in the boundary is contained in a fiber of \((B,U_2,\ldots,U_n)\).

Conversely, the homogeneous degree-zero locally nilpotent derivation of \(\mathbb C[X_n]\)
\[
 \delta(z)=x^2,\qquad \delta(y)=-3z^2w^2u_1^2P,\qquad
 \delta(x)=\delta(w)=\delta(u_i)=0
\]
annihilates the defining equation and descends to \(Y_n\). On the boundary,
\[
 \delta(B)=x^2w=0,\qquad
 \delta(C)=x^2y-3z^3w^2u_1^2P=-3,
 \qquad\delta(U_i)=0.
\]
Thus the descended additive action translates \(C\) by \(-3t\) and is transitive on every fiber. The orbits contained in the boundary are precisely these fibers.

**Picard group.** The smooth variety \(X_n\) has factorial localization \((X_n)_x\cong\mathbb G_m\times\mathbb A^{n+2}\). Its complement is the single irreducible prime divisor \(\{x=0\}\), since \(z^3w^2\prod u_i^2=1\) defines a connected torus (the exponents have greatest common divisor \(1\)), times the \(y\)-line. The localization sequence for divisor class groups implies \(\operatorname{Cl}(X_n)=0\): its only possible generator is already \(\operatorname{div}(x)\). Moreover \(\mathbb C[X_n]^\times=\mathbb C^\times\), since the units of its \(x\)-localization are precisely \(c x^m\), and both this function and its inverse can be regular at \(x=0\) only for \(m=0\).

Since \(Y_n\setminus\mathcal D_n\) is affine space, the localization sequence shows that \(\operatorname{Cl}(Y_n)\) is generated by \([\mathcal D_n]\). If \(m[\mathcal D_n]=0\), some rational invariant function \(f\) on \(Y_n\) has \(\operatorname{div}(f)=m\mathcal D_n\). Pulling back along the principal torus bundle gives \(\operatorname{div}_{X_n}(f)=m\operatorname{div}_{X_n}(x)\), hence \(f/x^m\) is a unit on \(X_n\), so \(f=cx^m\). Invariance under \(\mathbb G_m\) forces \(m=0\). Thus \(\operatorname{Cl}(Y_n)\cong\mathbb Z\), and smoothness gives \(\operatorname{Pic}(Y_n)=\operatorname{Cl}(Y_n)\).

**Canonical class.** On \(Y_n\setminus\mathcal D_n\), take the nowhere-zero rational top form
\[
 \Omega=dZ\wedge dW\wedge dV\wedge dU_2\wedge\cdots\wedge dU_n.
\]
In the boundary chart, \(Z=q/s^2\), \(W=s^2\rho\), \(V=s\). Near the generic point of \(\mathcal D_n\), \((s,\eta,\rho,U_2,\ldots,U_n)\) form étale local coordinates because \(3q^2\rho^2P\) is invertible. Differentiating \(s^2\eta+q^3\rho^2P=1\) gives
\[
 dq\equiv-\frac{s^2}{3q^2\rho^2P}\,d\eta\pmod{ds,d\rho,dU_i}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \Omega=-\frac{s^2}{3q^2\rho^2P}\,
 d\eta\wedge d\rho\wedge ds\wedge dU_2\wedge\cdots\wedge dU_n.
\]
The coefficient has order exactly \(2\) along \(\mathcal D_n\), and \(\Omega\) has no zero or pole off that divisor. Therefore \(\operatorname{div}_{Y_n}(\Omega)=2\mathcal D_n\), proving \(\omega_{Y_n}\cong\mathcal O_{Y_n}(2\mathcal D_n)\). Since \([\mathcal D_n]\) has infinite order, so does the canonical class.

## Verification

The proof is uniform in \(n\) and is algebraic. The standalone program `artifacts/verify_boundary.py` independently checks the invariant-coordinate formulas, the boundary inverse, the descended locally nilpotent derivation's defining-equation identity, the \(-3\) boundary translation, and the differential coefficient. The file explicitly distinguishes these checks from the divisor-class and orbit arguments, which are proved above. All checks were executed from the embedded candidate path before packaging. The finite substitutions are sanity checks only and are not used to infer universal quantifiers.

## Relationship to prior work

Nikitina [1] constructs these quotients, proves smooth generic flexibility and identifies \(\mathcal D_n\) as an invariant divisor. The current result builds upon that theorem, but computes an explicit boundary orbit partition and determines the full Picard group and non-torsion canonical class. The existence and invariance of the divisor are not new. Gaifullin [2] constructs different normal, singular examples of generically flexible quotients and discusses finite boundary orbit strata; that classification concerns another hypersurface and does not imply the present affine-line orbit partition. Standard localization and Cox-ring tools [3] motivate the class-group approach but do not state the canonical calculation for these varieties.

## Limitations

The statement does not classify all \(\operatorname{SAut}(Y_n)\)-orbits in the complement \(Y_n\setminus\mathcal D_n\), nor all automorphisms of \(Y_n\). It uses Nikitina's published invariance theorem for the boundary, while giving independent proofs of the additional geometric and canonical conclusions. Equivalent implicit descriptions in other work remain a literature-priority risk. No independent validation is claimed.
