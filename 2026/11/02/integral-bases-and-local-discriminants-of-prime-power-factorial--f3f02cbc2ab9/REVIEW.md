# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
For \(n=p^a\), the coefficient valuation difference above the line from \((0,rh)\) to \((n,0)\) is \(r(s_p(k)-k/n)/(p-1)>0\). Since \(p\nmid rh\), the primitive Newton slope forces local irreducibility and root valuation \(rh/n\). Scaling powers by \(p^{\lfloor jrh/n\rfloor}\) produces pairwise distinct fractional valuations; the minimum-valuation argument proves an entire integral basis, not merely an integral spanning set. In the derivative, the \(k=1\) term has strictly least valuation: for \(s_p(k)\ge2\) the digit-sum term is positive, and for \(k=p^j\ge p\), the \(j\)-term dominates a correction smaller than one. The norm of the derivative and discriminant-index identity then give the exact exponents. Independent exact resultant checks were run for eight finite instances; finiteness of these checks is not the proof.

## Originality — PASS (residual specialist-literature risk)
Sun–Wu–Zhuang (2010), arXiv:1009.2929v1, studies derangement and Bell congruences and supplies an archival source for the factorial-ratio polynomial setting, not these powered-coefficient field invariants. Jindal–Khanduja (2024), DOI 10.1017/S0013091524000105, explicitly studies discriminants/integral bases of ordinary truncated exponentials, covering a close unpowered \(r=1\) direction, which is excluded from the claim. Jakhar (2025), DOI 10.1017/S0004972724000819, imposes local-unit conditions on the coefficient multipliers, unlike factorial powers for \(r\ge2\). No inspected material proves or dominates the stated universal \(r\ge2\) classification. **Risk:** full text of the Jindal–Khanduja article was unavailable, so stronger implicit coverage cannot be excluded. Search noncoverage is not novelty proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The polynomial has a natural factorial-ratio combinatorial interpretation: each coefficient counts tuples of independent ordered injective lists. The result provides an explicit infinite collection of generally wild totally ramified number fields with a complete integral basis and *exact* discriminant exponent, and differentiates all \(p-2\) permitted exponents by a local isomorphism invariant. The normalizations and valuation computations lead to a global-in-parameters classification rather than a single coefficient identity or small finite table. This does not assert a general Galois-group classification or global arithmetic consequences.

## Limits
The exact results concern \(p\)-local invariants, the specific exponent range, and prime-power degrees. A closely related article was compared only at abstract level. No independent review is claimed.
