# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim is for a smooth complex Hirzebruch surface and the very affine Cox locus of a specified positive chart, not its entire projective surface. The Cox degrees and two primitive nef classes were reconstructed; normalizing the two sections identifies the locus with the stated affine plane minus four divisors. The graph divisor \(D\) is smooth even at repeated roots of \(P\), because its points have \(P(s)\ne0\). Its principal class makes the first Gysin pushforward zero. The integral Gysin sequences give the ranks without torsion. The residue of the fourth logarithmic form under cup product with a pulled-back degree-one class is its restriction to \(D\). The restriction matrix has two unit basis columns plus the multiplicity vector, so Smith reduction gives the stated cokernel. The family \(P_{a,m,h}\) has all coefficients positive and produces one repeated root of multiplicity \(m\); the resulting cyclic groups distinguish the integral cohomology rings. Checks in `verify.py` confirm exact polynomial and restriction arithmetic in finite cases but are not used as an infinite proof.

General integral cohomology-ring formulas for toric arrangements have a different premise, since \(1-tP(s)=0\) with roots of \(P\) is generally not a torus-character level set. Semantic record searches did not identify the precise statement; they do not prove historical uniqueness. Residual risk: the formula could be implicit in older complements-of-graphs or fiber-type-arrangement work.

## Mathematical value — PASS
Positive charts are natural noncanonical compactification charts of smooth projective toric surfaces. A basic question is which features of their non-boundary topology are determined by the ambient fan. This result shows that even on **one fixed** Hirzebruch surface, the very affine loci of charts can have identical additive integral cohomology and Euler characteristic yet pairwise different full integral cohomology rings, with arbitrarily large cyclic orders across the surface family. The exact gcd-of-multiplicities formula explains the mechanism and gives a computable structural invariant beyond previously understood likelihood degree. No arbitrary parameter slice is needed: the full class of allowed degree-\(a\) polynomials is treated, and the displayed subfamily proves sharp variability.
