# Integral Fourier identifiability from noncrossing perfect matchings

## Finding
Let \(n\ge2\). Place \(2n\) labelled vertices \(0,1,\ldots,2n-1\), in that cyclic order, in strictly convex position. Let \(\mathcal N_n\) be the set of noncrossing perfect straight-segment matchings and give it uniform probability. Every edge in a noncrossing perfect matching joins an even-labelled vertex \(2i\) to an odd-labelled vertex \(2j+1\); identify its incidence variable with \(X_{ij}(M)=\mathbf 1_{\{\{2i,2j+1\}\in M\}}\), where \(0\le i,j<n\). Put \(Z_{ij}=X_{ij}-\mathbb E X_{ij}\) and \(\Gamma=(\mathbb E[Z_{ij}Z_{k\ell}])_{(i,j),(k,\ell)}\).

**Theorem.** The covariance matrix has exact nullspace
\[
\ker\Gamma=\{(u_i+v_j)_{i,j}:u,v\in\mathbb R^n\},
\qquad \operatorname{rank}\Gamma=(n-1)^2.
\]
Consequently the constant function together with the \(n^2\) degree-one edge Walsh characters \(\chi_{ij}=1-2X_{ij}\) spans a space of dimension \((n-1)^2+1\) on \(\mathcal N_n\), and there are no linear constraints beyond the \(2n\) vertex-matching equations (one of which is redundant after adjoining the constant).

More strongly, if \(x(M)=(X_{ij}(M))\), then the **integer lattice** generated by differences of noncrossing matching incidence matrices is
\[
\left\langle x(M)-x(M'):M,M'\in\mathcal N_n\right\rangle_{\mathbb Z}
=\left\{A\in\mathbb Z^{n\times n}:\sum_jA_{ij}=0\ \forall i,\ \sum_iA_{ij}=0\ \forall j\right\}.
\]
In particular, for any fixed \(M_0\in\mathcal N_n\), the matrix with rows \(x(M)-x(M_0)\), indexed by all \(M\in\mathcal N_n\), has rank \((n-1)^2\) over **every field**, including every positive characteristic. The kernel and real-rank assertion remains true for every probability law giving strictly positive mass to every noncrossing matching.

The entire covariance matrix is also explicitly evaluable by a two-edge Catalan product formula given below. No claim of closed-form individual nonzero covariance eigenvalues is made.

## Assumptions and scope
A chord joins two vertices of a strictly convex polygon; two chords cross if their interiors intersect. All labels, edges, and matchings are distinct and the sample space is finite. The edge family contains exactly \(n^2\) even-to-odd chords. Let \(C_k=(k+1)^{-1}\binom{2k}{k}\) for integers \(k\ge0\), with \(C_0=1\). The number of noncrossing perfect matchings is \(C_n\). The rank statements concern centered indicator covariance and, equivalently, centered Walsh covariance \(4\Gamma\), rather than a claim of orthogonality or quantitative lower eigenvalue bounds.

For an edge \(e=\{a,b\}\), write \(a<b\), and let \(p_e=\mathbb P(e\in M)\). If \(a,b\) have opposite parity, put \(u=(b-a-1)/2\); then
\[
p_e=\frac{C_u C_{n-u-1}}{C_n}.
\]
For two distinct eligible edges \(e,f\), write their four distinct endpoints as \(a<b<c<d\) and set \(g_0=b-a-1\), \(g_1=c-b-1\), \(g_2=d-c-1\), \(g_3=2n-d+a-1\). If endpoints overlap, or if \(e,f\) are the crossing pair \(\{a,c\},\{b,d\}\), set \(q_{ef}=0\). In the two noncrossing cases,
\[
q_{ef}=\mathbb P(e,f\in M)=
\begin{cases}
 C_{g_0/2}C_{g_2/2}C_{(g_1+g_3)/2}/C_n,&\{e,f\}=\{\{a,b\},\{c,d\}\},\\[2mm]
 C_{g_1/2}C_{g_3/2}C_{(g_0+g_2)/2}/C_n,&\{e,f\}=\{\{a,d\},\{b,c\}\}.
\end{cases}
\]
Every Catalan subscript here is a nonnegative integer because the prescribed chords connect opposite parities. For \(e=f\), use \(q_{ee}=p_e\). In all cases, \(\Gamma_{ef}=q_{ef}-p_ep_f\), and the centered Walsh Gram matrix is \(4\Gamma\).

## Proof
**1. Parity and Catalan moments.** A chord of a noncrossing perfect matching separates the remaining polygon vertices into two regions, each matched internally; hence each side must contain an even number of vertices. The endpoints have opposite parity. Conversely every opposite-parity chord can be completed, by pairing consecutive vertices on each of its two sides. The usual recursion on the partner of vertex zero gives \(|\mathcal N_n|=C_n\), and fixing one chord splits the choices into \(C_uC_{n-u-1}\) completions. Fixing two disjoint noncrossing chords divides the remaining vertices into three polygonal faces. Within each face the matching is independent and contributes the Catalan number of half its remaining vertex count. Reading the gaps between their four endpoints yields precisely the two displayed product formulas. Crossing or incident chords cannot coexist in a perfect noncrossing matching.

**2. Realizable alternating-quadrilateral flips.** Let four distinct polygon vertices appear in circular order \(a,b,c,d\) with alternating parities. Match all vertices strictly between successive marked vertices in consecutive pairs along the boundary. This yields one common outside collection \(T\). Both
\[
T\cup\{\{a,b\},\{c,d\}\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
T\cup\{\{a,d\},\{b,c\}\}
\]
are noncrossing perfect matchings. Their incidence difference, in the even-row/odd-column representation, is a signed elementary checkerboard on two rows and two columns.

**3. All checkerboards are generated integrally.** Fix two distinct even rows \(i<k\). Partition odd column labels into the nonempty sets \(I=\{i,i+1,\ldots,k-1\}\) and \(J=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\setminus I\). For each \(j\in I\) and \(\ell\in J\), the cyclically ordered vertices \(2i,2j+1,2k,2\ell+1\) alternate in parity. Step 2 therefore realizes, up to sign, the integer matrix
\[
H_{ik;j\ell}=E_{ij}+E_{k\ell}-E_{i\ell}-E_{kj}
\]
as a difference of two noncrossing matching incidences. The complete bipartite graph joining columns \(I\) to \(J\) is connected. Hence, by telescoping along paths of this graph, every checkerboard \(H_{ik;j\ell}\) with arbitrary distinct columns \(j,\ell\) is an integer sum of realizable ones. This holds for every pair of distinct rows.

The integer zero-row/zero-column lattice has the explicit basis
\[
\left\{E_{ij}-E_{i,n-1}-E_{n-1,j}+E_{n-1,n-1}:0\le i,j<n-1\right\}.
\]
These are checkerboards and so belong to the difference lattice by the preceding argument. Conversely, every difference of incidence matrices has zero row and column sums, since each matching uses each polygon vertex once. This proves equality of integer lattices, including integral saturation. Reducing the displayed basis identities in any field gives the asserted field-independent rank.

**4. Fourier nullspace and rank.** For a real array \(A=(a_{ij})\), the quadratic form \(A^{\mathsf T}\Gamma A\) is the variance of \(\sum_{ij}a_{ij}X_{ij}\). Because every matching has positive probability, this variance vanishes exactly when the linear statistic is constant on all matchings, i.e. when \(A\) is orthogonal to every incidence difference. By step 3, this is equivalent to orthogonality to the zero-row/zero-column real subspace, whose orthogonal complement consists exactly of arrays \(a_{ij}=u_i+v_j\). This space has dimension \(2n-1\), and so \(\operatorname{rank}\Gamma=n^2-(2n-1)=(n-1)^2\). The identities \(\sum_jX_{ij}=\sum_iX_{ij}=1\) account for precisely the stated affine relations. Since \(\chi_{ij}=1-2X_{ij}\), adjoining the constant gives the corresponding degree-one Walsh claim. Changing to any strictly positive probability weights does not change which finite-support statistics have variance zero.

## Verification
Run `python3 verify_noncrossing.py` from the same directory. The standard-library-only program enumerates all noncrossing matchings for \(2\le n\le8\), verifies \(8{,}771\) exact integer one-/two-edge occurrence counts against the Catalan formulas, checks \(714\) explicit alternating-quadrilateral flip witnesses, and verifies the predicted incidence ranks modulo the prime \(1{,}000{,}003\) in all seven sizes. Since the predicted rank is also an unconditional upper bound from row/column equations, each finite modular test is an exact rank certificate for its tested size. These finite tests do **not** establish the statements for all \(n\); the general proof is steps 2–4.

## Relationship to prior work
Noncrossing perfect matchings of convex polygon vertices are classically counted by Catalan numbers. Hernando, Hurtado, and Noy studied their flip graph, including connectivity and Hamiltonicity; Houle, Hurtado, Noy, and Rivera-Campo studied more general flip-graph connectivity. Paffenholz described Birkhoff polytopes and the affine dimension of the convex hull of **all** permutation matrices. These are distinct from the present claim that the much smaller Catalan family generates the **entire integer zero-marginal lattice**, as well as the exact first-degree Fourier covariance nullspace. The flip and Catalan facts are ingredients, not independently claimed innovations.

## Limitations
There is no quantitative lower bound here for the smallest positive eigenvalue of \(\Gamma\), nor a closed formula for the full nonzero spectrum. The integer-lattice and rank conclusions concern labelled polygon matchings in strictly convex position; arbitrary point configurations may behave differently. The complete original Hernando–Hurtado–Noy flip-graph article was not accessible for full-text comparison during this review, so equivalent older consequences in geometric matching or Temperley–Lieb literature remain possible. Independent verification of mathematical priority and correctness has not been performed.
