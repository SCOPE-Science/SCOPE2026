# Integral Smith forms and cyclic actions for graded Cartan specializations of truncated Nakayama algebras

## Finding

Let \(Q_n\) be the oriented \(n\)-cycle (a single loop when \(n=1\)), \(J\) its arrow ideal, and \(A_{n,L}=kQ_n/J^L\) for integers \(n\geq1\), \(L\geq3\) and any field \(k\). Define the graded Cartan polynomial \(C_{n,L}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}t^jP_n^j\), with \(P_n\) the integral cyclic permutation matrix. For every integer \(a\geq1\) put
\[
 g=\gcd(n,L),\quad h=\operatorname{lcm}(n,L)=nL/g,\quad D=a^h-1,\quad B=\sum_{j=0}^{L/g-1}a^{nj}.
\]
Then the **full integral Smith form** of the specialized graded Cartan matrix is
\[
\operatorname{SNF}_{\mathbb Z}C_{n,L}(a)=\operatorname{diag}(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{n-g},B,\underbrace{D,\dots,D}_{g-1}),
\]
where \(D=0\) when \(a=1\), and the zero invariant factors appear last. In particular,
\[
\operatorname{coker}_{\mathbb Z}C_{n,L}(a)\cong\mathbb Z/B\mathbb Z\oplus(\mathbb Z/D\mathbb Z)^{g-1}.
\]
At \(a=1\), the ordinary Cartan group is \(\mathbb Z/(L/g)\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z^{g-1}\). At \(a>1\) the finite group has order \(B D^{g-1}\). For every prime \(p\), including \(p\mid a\), the rank of the matrix reduced modulo \(p\) is
\[
n-(g-1)\mathbf1_{p\mid D}-\mathbf1_{p\mid B}.
\]
The divisibility convention \(p\mid0\) is used for \(a=1\).

There is a refinement recording the canonical cyclic action on the cokernel. Put \(S=\mathbb Z/(D)\) (with \(S=\mathbb Z\) for \(D=0\)), choose integers \(u,v\) with \(u(n/g)+v(L/g)=1\), and define \(c=a^{nu}\in S^\times\). Then, as a module with the action of cyclic permutation \(P_n\),
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{coker}C_{n,L}(a)\cong S[w]/\bigl(w^g-c,\ B(1+w+\cdots+w^{g-1})\bigr),\quad P_n\cdot x=a^{-1}wx.}
\]
For \(a=1\), inverses are ordinary units \(1\); for \(a>1\), the inverse of \(a\) exists in \(S\) because \(a^h=1\). The ring quotient above is also a convenient description of the underlying abelian module and its cyclic symmetry. The claim is a classification of integer specializations and their cyclic actions, **not** a graded-derived invariance assertion for \(a>1\).

## Assumptions and scope

The cyclic quiver has one directed arrow from vertex \(i\) to \(i+1\) modulo \(n\). Every nonzero path in \(A_{n,L}\) has length less than \(L\). Its graded Cartan matrix counts paths by length and is \(\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}t^jP_n^j\), up to harmless transpose depending on Cartan-matrix convention. All statements are over the integer coefficient ring after evaluating the grading indeterminate at a **positive integer**, not over \(\mathbb Q[t]\). The restriction \(L\ge3\) excludes the quadratic gentle case already covered by known weighted-Cartan diagonalization. The algebra itself is defined over an arbitrary field; its path-count Cartan polynomial has integral coefficients independent of that field.

## Proof

**1. Ring presentation.** Identify \(\mathbb Z^n\) with the free cyclic module \(R=\mathbb Z[z]/(z^n-1)\), where multiplication by \(z\) represents \(P_n\). Thus the cokernel is
\[
M=\mathbb Z[z]/\bigl(z^n-1,\ 1+az+\cdots+(az)^{L-1}\bigr).
\]
Write \(w=az\) in \(M\). Multiplying the second defining relation by \(1-w\) gives \(w^L=1\). Since \(z^n=1\), we also have \(w^n=a^n\), hence \(a^h=1\) because \(h\) is a common multiple of \(n\) and \(L\). Thus \(D=a^h-1\) acts by zero on \(M\). When \(a\ge2\), \(a\) is a unit of \(S=\mathbb Z/(D)\); when \(a=1\), \(S=\mathbb Z\) and \(a=1\) is already a unit. Over \(S\), the substitution \(z=a^{-1}w\) is invertible.

**2. Two cyclic relations.** From \(w^n=a^n\), \(w^L=1\), and the Bézout identity \(u(n/g)+v(L/g)=1\), raise the first to \(u\) and the second to \(v\), giving \(w^g=a^{nu}=c\). Negative exponents are valid because \(w\) and \(a\) are units. Set \(N_g(w)=1+w+\cdots+w^{g-1}\). As \(L/g\) complete residue blocks of length \(g\) occur in the geometric sum,
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}w^j=N_g(w)\sum_{r=0}^{L/g-1}c^r.
\]
The element \(a^n\) has \((L/g)\)-th power \(1\) in \(S\); since \(u\) is coprime to \(L/g\), multiplication by \(u\) permutes the residues \(0,\ldots,L/g-1\). Therefore \(\sum c^r=\sum a^{nr}=B\), and we obtain \(B N_g(w)=0\).

Conversely, in \(T=S[w]/(w^g-c,B N_g(w))\), \(w\) is invertible since \(c\) is; \(w^L=c^{L/g}=a^{nuL/g}=a^{hu}=1\), and \(w^n=c^{n/g}=a^n\), using \(nu/g-1=-vL/g\) and \(a^h=1\). Hence \(z=a^{-1}w\) satisfies \(z^n=1\) and its length-\(L\) Cartan sum is \(B N_g(w)=0\). This gives mutually inverse maps between \(M\) and \(T\), preserving the named action \(z=a^{-1}w\).

**3. Abelian Smith invariants.** The polynomial quotient \(S[w]/(w^g-c)\) is free over \(S\) on \(1,w,\ldots,w^{g-1}\). We have \((a^n-1)B=a^{h}-1=D\), and \(c\) is a power of \(a^n\). Thus \(B(c-1)=0\) in \(S\), so
\[
w\,B N_g(w)=B\bigl(N_g(w)+c-1\bigr)=B N_g(w).
\]
The entire ideal generated by \(B N_g(w)\) is therefore the \(S\)-submodule generated by its coefficient vector \(B(1,\ldots,1)\). Since \((1,\ldots,1)\) is a primitive unimodular vector in \(S^g\), an elementary integral change of basis splits the quotient as \(S^{g-1}\oplus S/(B)\). Because \(B\mid D\) for \(a>1\), and because \(S/(B)=\mathbb Z/(B)\) also for \(a=1\), the claimed invariant factors follow, including the \(n-g\) initial units. The displayed diagonal entries are in divisibility order since \(B\mid D\) (with every positive integer dividing zero).

**4. Ranks and boundaries.** Reducing the Smith diagonal modulo \(p\) gives the rank formula, with each zero \(D\) reducing to zero. At \(a=1\), \(B=L/g\). For \(g=1\) the \(D\)-summands and extra ring generators are empty, so the same argument applies. For \(n=1\), the Cartan matrix is the one-by-one sum \(1+a+\cdots+a^{L-1}\), again agreeing with \(B\).

## Verification

The independent finite checker `check_smith.py` constructs every matrix entry directly from the cyclic path counts and computes integer Smith form using SymPy's exact integer-domain Smith routine. It passed 406 full Smith-form comparisons (396 grid configurations with \(1\le n\le9\), \(2\le L\le12\), \(1\le a\le4\), plus 10 larger cases) and 1458 independent Gaussian-elimination rank checks modulo six small primes. It tests the original integer matrices, not merely the closed formulas. Reproduce with `python check_smith.py` (SymPy required); `check_output.txt` is the complete captured output. The included checks cover \(L=2\) as an additional known-case boundary, though no originality is asserted there.

For example, \(n=6,L=4,a=2\) gives Smith entries \((1,1,1,1,65,4095)\); for \(a=1\) they are \((1,1,1,1,2,0)\). These finite computations check examples; the general theorem is established by the quotient-ring argument, not by enumeration.

## Relationship to prior work

Bessenrodt and Holm (2005, arXiv:math/0511610) establish weighted Cartan determinants and, for gentle quadratic cases, unimodular normal forms. The \(L=2\) part here is therefore not claimed as new. Zhao and Zhang (2020) state in their abstract the determinant formula for graded Cartan matrices of self-injective Nakayama algebras, matching the absolute determinant here; **the determinant formula is not claimed as new**. Telloni and Williams (2014) establish Smith forms for circulant matrices over polynomial fields \(\mathbb Q[\lambda]\), where unimodularity has a different meaning and does not itself determine the integer torsion after evaluation at a fixed positive integer. Marcos, Mendoza and Sáenz (2018) study integral Cartan cokernels in a different (stratified-algebra) setting and motivate the group invariant. The assessed distinguishing contribution is the **all-parameter integral invariant-factor classification of the evaluated graded Nakayama Cartan matrix, together with its explicit cyclic action** for \(L\ge3\).

## Limitations

No result is asserted for arbitrary Kupisch series, unequal arrow weights, noncyclic quivers, noninteger or negative grading evaluations, or graded-derived invariance of the integer specialization at \(a>1\). The 2020 Zhao–Zhang full text and 2017 Xie–Zhang–Zan article were not accessible for complete verification, so overlap with an uninspected integral diagonalization theorem remains a meaningful priority risk. The literature search does not certify absolute novelty. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
