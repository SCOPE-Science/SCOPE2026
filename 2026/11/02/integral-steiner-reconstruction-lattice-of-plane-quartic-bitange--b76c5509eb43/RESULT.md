# Integral Steiner reconstruction lattice of plane-quartic bitangents

## Finding

Let \(C\) be any smooth, geometrically connected nonhyperelliptic genus-three curve over a field \(k\) of characteristic different from \(2\), and let \(\overline k\) be an algebraic closure. There are \(28\) geometric bitangent lines, canonically identified with the odd theta characteristics \(\Theta^-\) of \(C_{\overline k}\), and \(63\) Steiner complexes \(\Sigma(v)\), indexed by the nonzero classes \(v\in\operatorname{Jac}(C_{\overline k})[2]\). A Steiner complex consists of its twelve bitangents. Define the integral incidence homomorphism

\[
I_C:\mathbb Z^{\{\Sigma(v)\}}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{\Theta^-},\qquad [\Sigma(v)]\longmapsto\sum_{\theta\in\Sigma(v)}[\theta].
\]

**Theorem.** The integral Smith invariant factors of \(I_C\) are

\[
\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{7},\quad\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{14},\quad\underbrace{4,\ldots,4}_{6},\quad 12.
\]

In particular, the finite incidence quotient is

\[
Q_C:=\operatorname{coker}(I_C)\cong (\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^{14}\oplus(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^6\oplus\mathbb Z/12\mathbb Z,
\qquad |Q_C|=805306368.
\]

For **each** of the \(28\) bitangents \(\theta\), the class of its integral basis vector \([\theta]\) has exact order \(12\) in \(Q_C\). Thus the smallest positive integer \(N\) for which \(N[\theta]\) is an integral linear combination of Steiner-complex sums is \(N=12\), for every \(\theta\). The construction is invariant under geometric Galois action; the asserted abstract abelian-group isomorphism is not a specification of the Galois module structure.

## Assumptions and scope

Smoothness and characteristic different from \(2\) ensure the identification of all \(28\) geometric bitangents with odd theta characteristics and the finite étale rank-\(64\) two-torsion group scheme after base change. The theorem is geometric, independent of the field and of any particular plane-quartic equation. It does not assert that the bitangents or Steiner complexes are individually defined over \(k\).

A choice of symplectic basis of the six-dimensional \(\mathbb F_2\)-space \(\operatorname{Jac}(C_{\overline k})[2]\) labels quadratic refinements by

\[
q_{a,b}(x,y)=x\cdot y+a\cdot x+b\cdot y\pmod 2,
\qquad a,b,x,y\in\mathbb F_2^3.
\]

The \(28\) odd refinements are precisely the pairs \((a,b)\) with \(a\cdot b=1\). Columns are indexed by the \(63\) nonzero vectors \(v=(x,y)\). In this ordering the explicit integer matrix is

\[
M_{(a,b),v}=\begin{cases}1,&q_{a,b}(v)=0,\\0,&q_{a,b}(v)=1.\end{cases}
\]

This agrees with the classical criterion \(\Sigma(v)=\{\theta\in\Theta^-:\theta(v)=0\}\), and identifies \(I_C\) with \(M\) up to row and column permutations.

## Proof

**Geometric identification.** The set of theta characteristics is an affine space for two-torsion. Every characteristic determines a quadratic refinement of the Weil pairing; parity equals the Arf invariant. For a smooth plane quartic in characteristic different from two, the odd characteristics correspond to the \(28\) bitangents. The \(63\) Steiner complexes are naturally indexed by nonzero two-torsion, each selecting odd refinements that vanish on its index. This gives the matrix \(M\) uniformly.

**Combinatorial Gram identity.** Each of the \(63\) columns contains \(12\) ones, and each of the \(28\) rows contains \(27\) ones. For two distinct odd quadratic refinements \(q\) and \(q'\), their difference is the symplectic linear functional associated with a nonzero \(d\) satisfying \(q(d)=0\). Their common zero locus is the zero locus of \(q\) on \(d^\perp\). Modulo the isotropic line generated by \(d\), this becomes an odd quadratic refinement on a four-dimensional symplectic space, having six zero vectors. The preimage has \(12\) zeros, one of which is the zero vector excluded from our columns. Thus distinct rows meet in exactly \(11\) columns, proving

\[
MM^{\mathsf T}=16I_{28}+11J_{28}.
\]

This matrix has positive eigenvalues \(324\) (once) and \(16\) (with multiplicity \(27\)). Hence \(M\) has full row rank and its integral cokernel is finite.

**Exact integral reduction.** The accompanying standalone program `verify_smith.py` constructs the \(28\times63\) matrix from the six binary coordinates and executes elementary unimodular row and column operations. At the end it verifies the full equality

\[
U M V=\operatorname{diag}(1^{\times7},2^{\times14},4^{\times6},12)
\]

with \(U\) and \(V\) products of integer elementary operations, with the remaining \(35\) columns zero. The program checks every matrix entry of this equality and the divisibility of successive diagonal entries. The row and column operation records are generated by exact integer arithmetic, with no floating-point steps. Since all applied operations are unimodular, these exact checks constitute an integral Smith certificate, establishing the asserted invariant factors and group structure. The independent program `verify_hnf.py` computes a Hermite basis of the same image lattice and confirms both its index and the \(28\) sharp individual reconstruction denominators.

**Sharpness for each bitangent.** The \(28\) basis-vector classes generate \(Q_C\), whose exponent is \(12\) by the Smith result. The symplectic group \(\operatorname{Sp}(6,2)\) permutes both rows and columns of the incidence matrix and acts transitively on the odd refinements. Therefore all \(28\) basis-vector classes have a common order \(t\). As they generate \(Q_C\), the exponent of \(Q_C\) equals \(t\), and hence \(t=12\).

## Verification

Reproduce the exact certificate with Python 3:

```text
python3 verify_smith.py
```

The final line must read:

```text
VERIFY_OK: all 28 diagonal entries and unimodular row/column certificate, incidence counts, Gram identity
```

Independently, with SymPy installed, run:

```text
python3 verify_hnf.py
```

The result must include:

```text
HNF_VERIFY_OK: index 805306368; independent Smith factors 1^7,2^14,4^6,12; all 28 basis-vector orders 12
```

The former program requires only the Python standard library and reconstructs the original incidence matrix before the reduction; the latter independently reconstructs it, computes Hermite and Smith forms through SymPy, and checks the denominators of all columns of the inverse Hermite basis. Both scripts are supplied in full.

## Relationship to prior work

The \(28\) bitangents, \(63\) Steiner complexes, the relation with odd theta characteristics, and the incidence parameters of the classical \(2\)-\((28,12,11)\) design are established in earlier literature, notably Plaumann–Sturmfels–Vinzant, Bergvall, and Crnković–Mikulić Crnković and collaborators. Those are premises, not new results. Earlier symplectic-design papers study these designs and associated finite-field codes. The additional assertion here concerns the **integral** image lattice, its full Smith invariant factors, and the sharp integral bitangent-reconstruction denominator, information not determined by the \(2\)-design parameters or binary code dimension alone.

## Limitations

The result is about the abstract integral geometric incidence cokernel, not the refined action of an individual arithmetic Frobenius or the classification of all twisted Galois modules. The computation is small and exact, but no proof of complete historical priority is asserted. General literature on Smith forms of quasi-symmetric designs could contain an equivalent older calculation; that possibility has not been excluded by a full inspection of all such literature.
