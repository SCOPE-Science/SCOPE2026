# Integral tritangent presentation of the \(E_6\) weight lattice

## Finding

Let \(S\) be any smooth cubic surface over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, let \(\mathcal L\) be its \(27\) lines, and let \(\mathcal T\) be its \(45\) tritangent planes. For each tritangent plane, let \(\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\}\) be the three lines it cuts out. Consider the integral incidence map
\[
\iota:\mathbb Z^{\mathcal T}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{\mathcal L},\qquad
[t]\longmapsto [\ell_1]+[\ell_2]+[\ell_3].
\]
**The complete integer relation module** of the line classes, viewed in the Picard group modulo the anticanonical class, is generated by the tritangent triples:
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{im}(\iota)=\ker\bigl(\mathbb Z^{\mathcal L}\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Pic}(S)/\mathbb Z[-K_S]\bigr).}
\]
Consequently, there is a natural \(W(E_6)\)-equivariant exact sequence
\[
\boxed{\mathbb Z^{45}\overset{\iota}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb Z^{27}
\longrightarrow P(E_6)\longrightarrow0,}
\]
where \(P(E_6)\) is the integral weight lattice, canonically identified with \(\operatorname{Pic}(S)/\mathbb Z[-K_S]\) through the restriction of the intersection pairing to the \(E_6\) root lattice. Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{SNF}(\iota)=\operatorname{diag}(1^{21},0^6),\qquad
\operatorname{coker}(\iota)\cong\mathbb Z^6.}
\]
Moreover, an **explicit set of only \(21\) of the \(45\) tritangent triples** is an integral basis of \(\operatorname{im}(\iota)\); this number is minimal. Thus rational relations hide no additional finite-index obstruction, and every integral weight relation has an integer, not merely rational, expression in tritangent triples.

## Assumptions and scope

Work geometrically over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Choose a blowdown of six mutually skew lines to six points in general position in the plane, so that \(\operatorname{Pic}(S)=\mathbb ZH\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^6\mathbb ZE_i\). The line classes are
\[
E_i,\qquad L_{ij}=H-E_i-E_j\ (1\le i<j\le6),\qquad
Q_i=2H-\sum_{j\ne i}E_j\ (1\le i\le6),
\]
and \(-K_S=3H-\sum_i E_i\). Each tritangent section has class \(-K_S\). We use **unoriented** integer sums of three line generators; no signs or weights are inserted into the incidence matrix.

The \(45\) triples, with indices \(1,\ldots,6\), are exactly the \(30\) mixed triples
\[
T_{ij}=E_i+L_{\min(i,j),\max(i,j)}+Q_j\qquad (i\ne j),
\]
and the \(15\) pure triples
\[
U_{ij\mid kl\mid mn}=L_{ij}+L_{kl}+L_{mn}
\]
for partitions of \(\{1,\ldots,6\}\) into three unordered pairs. Here expressions such as \(T_{ij}\) denote **formal vectors in the free lattice**, not equalities of classes unless explicitly stated.

## Proof

**1. Every tritangent vector belongs to the claimed kernel.** The divisor-class equalities in the preceding section give
\[
[E_i]+[L_{ij}]+[Q_j]=-K_S,\qquad
[L_{ij}]+[L_{kl}]+[L_{mn}]=-K_S.
\]
Both statements are checked directly using \(-K_S=3H-\sum E_i\). Hence \(\operatorname{im}(\iota)\) is contained in the kernel of the natural quotient-class map. The \(27\) line classes generate \(\operatorname{Pic}(S)\), because the six exceptional classes and \(H=L_{12}+E_1+E_2\) lie in their span; therefore this map is surjective.

**2. Select \(21\) relations and eliminate integrally.** Let \(F\) be the free abelian group on the \(27\) formal line symbols. Let \(R_0\) be generated by the following \(21\) formal tritangent vectors:
\[
\{T_{ij}:1\le i<j\le6\}\quad(15),\qquad
\{T_{j1}:2\le j\le6\}\quad(5),\qquad
\{U_{12\mid34\mid56}\}\quad(1).
\]
For clarity the same symbols \(E_i,L_{ij},Q_i\) now denote the generators of \(F\). First, the fifteen \(T_{ij}\) for \(i<j\) allow integer elimination of every \(L_{ij}\), giving \(L_{ij}=-E_i-Q_j\). Next, from the five reverse triples \(T_{j1}\), we obtain
\[
Q_j-E_j=Q_1-E_1\qquad(2\le j\le6).
\]
Introduce the integral generator \(H=E_1-Q_1\). The resulting formulas, **within** \(F/R_0\), are
\[
Q_i=E_i-H,\qquad L_{ij}=H-E_i-E_j.
\]
The last selected matching triple becomes \(3H-\sum_{i=1}^6 E_i=0\). Thus elementary \(\mathbb Z\)-linear Tietze transformations yield the explicit presentation
\[
\boxed{F/R_0\simeq
\bigl(\mathbb ZH\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^6\mathbb ZE_i\bigr)
\big/\mathbb Z(3H-\sum_iE_i).}
\]
Because the seven-entry relation vector has a unit coordinate, the quotient is torsion-free of rank \(6\).

**3. All remaining \(24\) tritangent vectors already vanish.** For any ordered \(i\ne j\), substituting \(Q_j=E_j-H\) and \(L_{ij}=H-E_i-E_j\) gives \(T_{ij}=0\) in \(F/R_0\), including the ten mixed triples not among the chosen relations. For *every* perfect matching, the three paired \(L\)-terms sum to \(3H-\sum_iE_i=0\), so all fourteen other pure triples also vanish. Hence \(R_0=\operatorname{im}(\iota)\). The resulting quotient is exactly the displayed presentation of \(\operatorname{Pic}(S)/\mathbb Z[-K_S]\), proving equality of kernels rather than only equal rational ranks. The \(21\) chosen vectors generate a free group of rank \(21\), hence form its \(\mathbb Z\)-basis; a smaller spanning set cannot exist.

**4. Identify the quotient as an integral weight lattice.** The Picard intersection pairing of the six-point blowup is unimodular. The primitive orthogonal sublattice \(K_S^\perp\subset\operatorname{Pic}(S)\) is the negative \(E_6\) root lattice. Restriction of the unimodular pairing gives a surjection \(\operatorname{Pic}(S)\to (K_S^\perp)^*\), whose kernel is \(\mathbb ZK_S\): surjectivity follows by dualizing the short exact sequence \(0\to K_S^\perp\to\operatorname{Pic}(S)\to\mathbb Z\to0\). Therefore \(\operatorname{Pic}(S)/\mathbb Z[-K_S]\cong P(E_6)\). The construction uses only intrinsic line, tritangent and intersection data, so it is compatible with the incidence-preserving Weyl-group action. In particular, the Smith form has \(21\) units followed by six zero rows.

## Verification

The included standalone script `verify_tritangent.py` is executable with Python 3 and only its standard library. Run `python verify_tritangent.py`. It constructs the \(27\) line labels and all \(45\) tritangent triples from the displayed formulas, then checks in exact rational arithmetic that the chosen \(21\) columns have a \(21\times21\) minor equal to \(+1\). For that minor take the \(21\) rows indexed by all fifteen \(L_{ij}\), the five \(Q_j\) for \(2\le j\le6\), and \(E_6\), in that order. Using the inverse of this unimodular submatrix, the verifier expresses **all \(45\) columns** integrally in the selected basis and checks each reconstructed \(27\)-entry vector. It also independently checks all \(45\) Picard-class sums. Thus the program supplies an exact finite certificate for the lattice statement; the proof above provides its conceptual explanation and geometric identification.

## Relationship to prior work

The twenty-seven lines, the two types of tritangent triples, the six-point blowup model, and the \(E_6\) weight identification are classical. Dolgachev–Kapranov (1993) discuss the blowup and double-six realization. Van Geemen (2001, §§1.6–1.8) identifies the root/weight lattices and writes the \(45\) triples explicitly, while Manivel (2005, §§1–2) treats their weight-zero property and representation-theoretic meaning. These facts are not new.

The distinguishing statement is **integral completeness**: the full kernel over \(\mathbb Z\) is generated by the \(45\) triple sums, and a specified set of \(21\) of those sums is already a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis. A known rational six-dimensional span alone does not imply that the generated sublattice is saturated. Boxer and collaborators' published \(E_6\) weight-triple discussion and Ahmed's 2026 signed-circuit treatment also give close geometric/combinatorial context; the inspected portions do not state this particular unimodular \(21\)-generator presentation. An implicit treatment within a broader account of the minuscule weight configuration remains a genuine priority risk.

## Limitations

This theorem concerns the **integral abelian-group presentation** of a classical line-weight configuration, not a new classification of cubic surfaces, a new incidence configuration, or a proof of a separate arithmetic application. Its application to Galois descent or integral representation calculations requires further work. The theorem is proved for smooth cubic surfaces over algebraically closed characteristic-zero fields; positive-characteristic extension is not asserted here. Historical priority cannot be certified from finite literature searches; in particular, abstract weight-lattice presentations may imply the result without using cubic-surface terminology. Independent scientific validation has not been performed.
