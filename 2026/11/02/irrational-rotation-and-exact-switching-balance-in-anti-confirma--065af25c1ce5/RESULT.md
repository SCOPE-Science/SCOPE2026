# Irrational rotation and exact switching balance in anti-confirmation Bayesian beliefs

## Finding
Consider the two-likelihood anti-confirmation Bayesian opinion update in [1, Eq. (15)], without in-group influence. Whenever the lower-belief likelihood ratio satisfies \(0<g_-<1\) and the upper-belief ratio satisfies \(g_+>1\), **every interior initial belief eventually enters one common invariant interval and is conjugate there to a rigid circle rotation.** The rotation number, periodicity, invariant belief distribution, and switching frequencies are all explicit. In the oscillatory parameter choice \((g_-,g_+)=(0.176,1.86)=(22/125,93/50)\) used in [1, Fig. 2], the rotation number is irrational; consequently every interior orbit is nonperiodic and dense in the same belief interval, with exactly zero branchwise asymptotic Lyapunov exponent. The sequence of high-belief updates obeys a strict discrepancy bound of one for **every** finite number of local updates.

More precisely, let \(a=\log(1/g_-)\), \(b=\log g_+\), \(L=a+b\), and \(\rho=a/L\). Define \(c_-=1/(1+g_+)\) and \(c_+=1/(1+g_-)\). Once an orbit enters \([c_-,c_+)\), let \(t_0=(\log(x_0/(1-x_0))+b)/L\). If \(H_n\) is the number of the next \(n\) local updates made from beliefs \(x\ge 1/2\), then

\[
H_n=\lfloor t_0+n\rho\rfloor,\qquad |H_n-n\rho|<1.
\]

If \(\rho=p/q\) in lowest terms, all points of the invariant interval have exact primitive period \(q\). If \(\rho\) is irrational, each orbit is dense in the closed interval \([c_-,c_+]\), and the unique invariant probability measure has density

\[
f(x)=\frac{1}{Lx(1-x)}\,\mathbf 1_{(c_-,c_+)}(x).
\]

For irrational \(\rho\), the time fraction spent at beliefs at least one half is \(\rho\) and the long-run mean belief is

\[
\overline x=\frac{\log(1+e^a)-\log(1+e^{-b})}{L}.
\]

For the tabulated numerical ratios of [1], \(\rho\approx0.736803836456\), the common orbit closure is approximately \([0.349650349650,0.850340136054]\), and \(\overline x\approx0.623087297648\). The exact fractions of likelihoods behind the rounded table entries, \((g_-,g_+)=(3/17,13/7)\), also yield irrational rotation.

## Assumptions and scope
A belief is a real number \(x\in(0,1)\). At each **local update of that same agent** the rule is

\[
F(x)=\begin{cases}
\frac{x}{x+g_-(1-x)},&0<x<1/2,\\
\frac{x}{x+g_+(1-x)},&1/2\le x<1.
\end{cases}
\]

The assignment of the tie \(x=1/2\) to the upper branch is part of the definition; it matches [1, Eq. (15)]. Both ratios are constant over time and do not depend on neighbors. The conclusions describe each agent indexed by its own updates, including any multi-agent schedule that activates each agent infinitely often while retaining this same independent per-agent rule. They do **not** assert the same time fractions in wall-clock time under arbitrary unequal update schedules.

Only the oscillatory regime \(0<g_-<1<g_+\) is claimed. The boundary states \(x=0,1\) are fixed by the original update and excluded. In-group-dependent switching, noise, changing data, delayed responses and other coupling mechanisms are excluded.

## Proof
**1. Log-odds conjugacy and finite capture.** Put \(u=\log(x/(1-x))\). Bayes' rule gives the exact identity \(\log(F(x)/(1-F(x)))=u-\log g\) for any fixed positive ratio \(g\). Consequently

\[
T(u)=\begin{cases}u+a,&u<0,\\u-b,&u\ge0.\end{cases}
\]

The half-open interval \(I=[-b,a)\) is invariant: for \(u\in[-b,0)\), \(u+a\in[a-b,a)\subset[-b,a)\), whereas for \(u\in[0,a)\), \(u-b\in[-b,a-b)\subset[-b,a)\). If \(u<-b\), the update adds the positive quantity \(a\) repeatedly until the first entrance into \(I\); the first overshoot is strictly below \(a\). Similarly, if \(u\ge a\), the update subtracts the positive quantity \(b\) repeatedly, and its first value below \(a\) is greater than \(-b\). Thus each finite \(u\) enters \(I\) in finitely many iterations, and conversion by the logistic map gives the asserted belief interval.

**2. Rigid rotation and an exact finite-time count.** On \(I\), write \(t=(u+b)/L\in[0,1)\). The threshold \(u=0\) corresponds to \(t=b/L=1-\rho\). If \(t<1-\rho\), the next iterate is \(t+\rho\); otherwise the next iterate is \(t+\rho-1\). Hence \(t_{k+1}=\{t_k+\rho\}\). An upper-branch update at step \(k\) occurs precisely when \(\lfloor t_k+\rho\rfloor=1\). Telescoping the wrap indicators yields

\[
H_n=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(\lfloor t_0+(k+1)\rho\rfloor-\lfloor t_0+k\rho\rfloor)=\lfloor t_0+n\rho\rfloor,
\]

because \(0\le t_0<1\). The discrepancy estimate follows directly from \(0\le\{t_0+n\rho\}<1\), with strict bound \(|H_n-n\rho|<1\).

**3. Periodic versus quasiperiodic behavior.** If \(\rho=p/q\in\mathbb Q\) in lowest terms, every point of the circle is returned after exactly \(q\) applications of the rotation, and no smaller positive return is possible. If \(\rho\notin\mathbb Q\), the subgroup generated by \(\rho\) is dense in the circle, giving a dense orbit and no periodic point. In the latter case, invariance forces the nonzero Fourier coefficients of any invariant circle probability measure to satisfy \(\hat\mu(k)=e^{2\pi ik\rho}\hat\mu(k)\) for every nonzero integer \(k\), so each coefficient vanishes. Trigonometric polynomial density implies the unique invariant measure is normalized arc length. Pushing this measure through \(u=-b+Lt\) and \(x=e^u/(1+e^u)\) gives \(f(x)\); integrating \(x\) against that density yields the formula for \(\overline x\).

**4. Zero asymptotic exponent and arithmetic nonresonance.** Along any finite smooth itinerary which avoids the branch discontinuity, the chain rule and the affine map in \(u\) give

\[
(F^n)'(x_0)=\frac{x_n(1-x_n)}{x_0(1-x_0)}.
\]

The captured orbit is confined to a compact subinterval of \((0,1)\), so this derivative and its reciprocal are bounded independently of \(n\), yielding branchwise Lyapunov exponent zero. For the displayed decimal values, suppose \(\rho\) were rational. Then \(a/b=r/s\) for positive integers \(r,s\), implying \((125/22)^s=(93/50)^r\). Taking the exponent of prime \(11\) gives \(-s=0\), contradiction. The unrounded ratios \((3/17,13/7)\) likewise yield a contradiction by comparing prime \(17\). The arguments do not depend on floating-point evidence.

Run \(\texttt{python verify_rotation.py}\) with Python 3.10 or newer. The checker performs 3,620 exact rational Bayes/odds and finite-itinerary comparisons, verifies six exact rational-resonance cycles, tests the wrap-count identity along 200,000 irrational-rotation steps, and compares a 200,000-step mean against the exact integral. The predicted mean and rotation number are independently recomputed from the two likelihood ratios. Finite checks support but do not replace the arbitrary-parameter proof above.

## Relationship to prior work
Dong and Marzen [1] give the two-branch update and identify belief cycling for this regime, including the displayed parameter pair. The result here completes the local-update dynamical classification: it distinguishes true rational periodic cycles from irrational dense quasiperiodicity, gives a universal trapping interval, a unique belief-distribution law, and an exact all-horizon threshold-word count. In particular, for their displayed decimal ratios, no interior local-update orbit becomes periodic, even though it continues to cross the belief threshold forever. This does not dispute their separate observations of complicated finite-time dynamics when in-group coupling is present.

Log-odds linearization, circle rotations, equidistribution and Fourier uniqueness are classical mathematical tools; none are asserted to be new. Nishi and Masuda [2] study a different peer-signal stochastic Bayesian model with confirmation-dependent signal misperception; its transition rule and its dependence on other agents do not give the specific deterministic two-gain classification proved here. The Bayesian source-reliability model of Pilgrim and collaborators [3] is also distinct from [1, Eq. (15)].

## Limitations
The assumption that each update uses one of two fixed likelihood ratios is essential. In-group bias makes the ratio state-dependent across agents and can invalidate the rotation structure. The threshold rule is discontinuous in belief; derivative claims exclude the countable set of initial states whose iterates meet the switch exactly, although the orbit classification and discrepancy remain valid there. The source parameters are presented both as rounded ratios and as ratios reconstructed from the tabulated likelihood values; each choice yields an irrational rotation, but their numerical frequencies differ slightly. No empirical cognitive realism, convergence at global-clock sampling times, or results on coupled transient chaos are claimed.
