# Scientific review: anti-confirmation Bayesian rotation theorem

## Correctness (PASS)
The claim is an exact statement about the stated two-branch Bayesian update with constant likelihood ratios, not about the model with in-group coupling. The change of variables \(u=\log(x/(1-x))\) gives the exact translations \(u\mapsto u+a\) below zero and \(u\mapsto u-b\) at or above zero. The interval \([-b,a)\) traps every finite initial \(u\) in finitely many steps. Scaling that interval by its length gives precisely the rotation \(t\mapsto\{t+\rho\}\), including the specified tie rule. Both the rational-period conclusion and the irrational minimality/unique invariant distribution follow from this conjugacy. The wrap indicators telescope to the exact finite-horizon formula \(H_n=\lfloor t_0+n\rho\rfloor\). Distinct prime supports of \(125/22\) and \(93/50\) rule out all possible rational logarithm ratios. The same argument with prime \(17\) covers the ratios reconstructed from the paper's tabulated unrounded likelihoods.

The chain-rule derivative formula is valid for itineraries avoiding the discontinuity; the theorem explicitly excludes those exceptional points from its Lyapunov claim. Independent finite checks exercise the original fractional Bayesian map and the odds map with exact arithmetic. The irrational infinite-orbit and equidistribution conclusions are proved mathematically, not inferred from those computations.

## Originality (PASS under stated inspection limits)
The target paper [1] defines and numerically explores the two-branch rule, and correctly reports threshold-crossing belief oscillations. It does not derive the log-odds rotation, arithmetic nonresonance of the tabulated likelihood ratios, unique absolutely continuous distribution, all-horizon branch-count identity, or rational/irrational periodicity dichotomy. These constitute the single combined mathematical classification here. The source's accounts of finite-time coupled behavior and dynamically changing credibility are not challenged by the independent-agent result.

Broader known results about irrational circle rotations and log-odds transformations supply mathematical tools, but do not explicitly identify the parameter-arithmetic obstruction and the exact belief-statistics of [1, Eq. (15)]. The prior stochastic peer-signal rule [2] and the bounded-rational source-reliability rule [3] involve different update dependencies and cannot imply a deterministic fixed-two-ratio orbit classification without an additional model reduction that is not present in their inspected material. The 2024 multiactor continuous-bias model of Alvim et al. (arXiv:2402.17615) also has different bias functions; only its abstract was accessible in this round, so it is noted as a residual risk rather than certified as not covering the claim.

A mathematical-record search, searches for the target title and relevant aliases, and claim-comparison checks against accessible prior mathematical records did not reveal a covering theorem. These negative searches are not proofs of global novelty. The most material residual risk is an unindexed independent derivation of the rotation conjugacy for this particular model; a second risk is a more general previously published result whose specialization has not been located.

## Mathematical value (PASS)
The originating study explicitly treats whether its anti-confirmation belief oscillations represent cycles or more complicated long-term behavior. Distinguishing finite periodic cycles from an irrational orbit dense on a whole interval is an important qualitative boundary, not a numerical remeasurement. The theorem also yields a population-independent local-update observable: after capture, high-belief update counts have error less than one at every horizon, while the long-run mean belief is exactly computable. For the specific displayed parameters the orbit cannot settle to a periodic cycle, despite persistent and seemingly irregular threshold crossings. The result supplies a clean nonchaotic baseline for interpreting more complicated behavior with in-group coupling. This is a natural complete classification of a publicly studied regime, rather than an arbitrary parameter slice.
