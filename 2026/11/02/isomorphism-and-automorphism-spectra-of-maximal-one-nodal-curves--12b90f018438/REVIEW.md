# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS. For maximal arithmetic-genus-two curves with maximal elliptic normalization, the Aubry--Perret equality case forces precisely one rational nonsplit node obtained by pinching one degree-two point. The source of the moduli count is therefore the action of the full unpointed elliptic automorphism group on degree-two closed points. Maximal Frobenius is multiplication by \(-p\), giving a surjective difference map onto the nonzero \((p-1)\)-torsion and identifying translation orbits. Burnside's lemma, the kernels of the elliptic endomorphisms \(i-1\) and \(\zeta_3-1\), and a direct two-orientation stabilizer count give all class and automorphism-order formulas. The proof specifies hypotheses and all exception cases. The included checker independently enumerates sample torsion orbits and rational elliptic point totals; samples are not the basis of the infinite-prime theorem.

## Originality
PASS on the inspected published literature, with historical-priority risk. Hilario--Salomão--Martins, arXiv:2609.22557, Theorem 3.4, explicitly constructs maximal genus-two singular curves by nodal projection, not a complete unpointed isomorphism and automorphism-order census. Aubry--Iezzi, arXiv:1501.03676, Proposition 5.2, characterizes the equality case and pinching by degree-two points, while arXiv:1510.01853 studies genera and existence. Neither inspected primary work counts quotient orbits or their stabilizer orders. Searches using the phrases “maximal singular elliptic curves isomorphism classes degree two”, “maximal nodal curves elliptic number of isomorphism classes”, and “maximal singular curves automorphism groups elliptic” found no equivalent formula. General moduli-theoretic descriptions of nodal curves may subsume the orbit reduction; an unlocated specialized isomorphism census remains a priority risk.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The geometric construction of maximal singular elliptic-normalization curves raises a natural finite-field moduli question: how many genuinely different curves result, and how often do exceptional symmetries occur? The complete answer is uniform over all maximal elliptic normalizations, separates the generic and \(j=0,1728\) symmetry types, and gives exact automorphism-order frequencies. It covers all admissible degree-two pinches, including points not handled by the source's explicit interpolation models. This is a natural complete classification of an arithmetic-geometric family, rather than an arbitrary subcase or mere re-evaluation of point counts.

## Residual limitations
No independent mathematical audit was performed. Published material examined does not establish an absolute historical-priority claim. The result concerns fixed normalization, \(p\ge5\), square base fields, and curves with exactly one nonsplit node; it does not sum over distinct normalizations or count embedded models.
