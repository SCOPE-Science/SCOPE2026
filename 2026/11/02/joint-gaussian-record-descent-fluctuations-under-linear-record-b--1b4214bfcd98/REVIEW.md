# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The independent relative-rank model follows by an exact bijection and factorized weights. It makes record indicators independent and descent indicators one-dependent. Conditional sums give all finite two-point descent probabilities; the cross-covariance is independently derived by exponential-tilt differentiation of the known exact descent mean. Direct Riemann-sum computations give all entries of the limiting covariance; a conditional three-rank argument gives strict positive definiteness. Independent blocking of a bounded, one-dependent triangular array proves the joint CLT. The bundled checker gives exact-rational small-size corroboration without treating finite data as proof.

## Originality — PASS, qualified
The closest paper, Bouvel–Nicaud–Pivoteau (arXiv:2409.01692v1), Section 4.3, proves marginal CLTs for fixed bias and Section 5 a permuton limit for linearly growing bias; it does not present the explicit joint Gaussian law in the latter regime. Their Section 3.2 independent-rank construction and Section 4.1 expectation identities are explicit precursors. Auger et al. (2016) established the earlier model and expectation. Féray (2013) handles Ewens statistics in the fixed-parameter setting; Borodin–Diaconis–Fulman (2010) one-dependent stationary descents for uniform/Mallows models; Butler (2023) Stirling–Euler–Mahonian joint finite laws. None of the inspected relevant sections states or directly dominates the displayed \(\Sigma_\lambda\) joint limit. An implicit earlier asymptotic deduction from general finite generating functions or rank independence remains possible, so priority is not independently certified. The derivative-derived exact covariance is not advanced as independently novel.

## Value — PASS
The linearly record-biased regime is structurally different from the classical fixed-bias regime, changing the number of records from logarithmic to linear and changing the asymptotic descent mean. A full joint Gaussian law reveals whether the competing statistics decouple at order \(\sqrt n\); the explicit strictly negative correlation, including approximately \(-0.84024\) at \(\lambda=1\), answers that natural dependence question and supplies the covariance needed for combined record/descent observables. This goes beyond a routine restatement of a mean, although its proof uses standard bounded-dependence limit theory.
