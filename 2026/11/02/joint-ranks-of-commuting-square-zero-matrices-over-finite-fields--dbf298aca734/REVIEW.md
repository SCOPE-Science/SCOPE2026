# Scientific review: joint ranks of commuting square-zero matrices

## Correctness — PASS

The unique centralizer block form, square-zero equations, rank decomposition through the quotient \(\ker P/\operatorname{im}P\), count of rank-\(k\) square-zero blocks, and Gaussian rank counts directly establish the theorem for every prime power and all positive \(r\). The identity \(\operatorname{rank}B=2k+\operatorname{rank}Q\) has been justified from the image of the complete block map, not assumed from numerical patterns. The finite arithmetic checker exhaustively confirmed all joint strata in five stated fields/dimensions, including characteristic two and the nonprime field of order four. The proof does not rely on those finite samples.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge, residual risk)

The closest existence theorem is Kokol Bukovšek–Košir–Novak–Oblak (2007, Proposition 2.2), which proves the sharp product-rank threshold but does not count each commuting square-zero factor by both its rank and the product's rank. Košir's 2026 survey addresses Jordan-type realizability, rather than the finite-field joint enumeration; Bogdanić et al. (2024) study attainable Jordan structures for two equal Jordan blocks; Mohammadian (2012) proves rank bounds. Searches covering dual-number representations, square-zero centralizers, fixed Jordan type, finite-field rank matrices, and joint-product rank did not find an exact formula or the limiting uniform law. The 2024 full text was not accessible during this review, and unindexed or representation-theoretic reformulations remain a real priority risk; absence of a returned record does not prove novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The rank of a product of commuting nilpotents detects information not contained in the separate Jordan types. The theorem quantitatively refines a sharp classic factorization boundary over every finite field and every even dimension, giving a complete natural two-invariant census and an unexpected uniform limiting law. It does not purport to solve arbitrary nilpotent commuting pairs or classify simultaneous similarity orbits.
