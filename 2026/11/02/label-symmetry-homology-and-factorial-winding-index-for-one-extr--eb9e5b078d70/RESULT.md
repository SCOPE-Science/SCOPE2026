# Label-symmetry homology and factorial winding index for one-extra-particle anchored graph spaces

## Finding
Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite connected simple graph with \(v=|V|\ge3\) vertices, \(e=|E|\ge v\) edges, and at least one cycle. Set \(n=v+1\). Define the all-vertex anchored configuration space
\[
X_G=\{(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in|G|^n:V\subseteq\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}\}.
\]
Coordinates may coincide. The particle-label permutation group \(S_n\) acts on \(X_G\). Let \(H\le S_n\) denote a subgroup generated by one transposition; \(\mathbf1\) and \(\mathrm{sgn}_H\) denote its two one-dimensional rational representations. Then \(X_G\) is a connected graph, with \(H_1(X_G;\mathbb Z)\) free of rank
\[
1+n!\bigl(e-v/2\bigr),
\]
and all higher homology zero. Its **complete rational \(S_n\)-homology module** is
\[
\boxed{H_1(X_G;\mathbb Q)\cong \mathbf1\oplus(e-v)\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{S_n}\mathbf1\oplus e\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{S_n}\mathrm{sgn}_H.}
\]
Equivalently, its character equals \(1+n!(e-v/2)\) at the identity, \(1-v(n-2)!\) at a transposition, and \(1\) at every remaining permutation. For any partition \(\lambda\vdash n\), writing \(f^\lambda\) for the degree of its rational Specht module and \(\chi_\lambda(t)\) for its character at a transposition, the multiplicity of \(S^\lambda\) is
\[
\mathbf1_{\lambda=(n)}+e f^\lambda-\frac v2\bigl(f^\lambda+\chi_\lambda(t)\bigr).
\]
There is also an **integral invariant-lattice statement**. The quotient map \(p:X_G\to X_G/S_n\cong |G|\) induces a canonical identification
\[
H_1(X_G;\mathbb Z)^{S_n}\cong H_1(|G|;\mathbb Z)
\]
by summing all oriented lifts of each graph edge. Under this identification the map on invariant homology is exactly multiplication by \(n!\):
\[
p_*\big|_{H_1(X_G;\mathbb Z)^{S_n}}=n!\,\mathrm{id}.
\]
The integral image therefore has index \((n!)^{e-v+1}\) in \(H_1(|G|;\mathbb Z)\), although \(p_*:H_1(X_G;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(|G|;\mathbb Z)\) itself is surjective. In particular, for each unicyclic graph the label-invariant primitive cycle winds exactly \(n!\) times around the graph quotient.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs in the theorem are finite, connected, simple, with at least one cycle, and their edges are regarded as closed intervals in the geometric realization. Every graph vertex is an anchor required to be occupied throughout the configuration. Coinciding particle coordinates are allowed; these are *not* collision-free graph braid configuration spaces. The action is by permutation of the \(n=v+1\) coordinate labels, not by graph automorphisms. The group \(H\) may be any transposition subgroup of \(S_n\); different choices are conjugate. The statement concerns the first nontrivial excess-particle case, where the constrained space has dimension one. The assumption \(e\ge v\) makes the displayed decomposition a genuine nonnegative direct sum of induced modules; the chain calculation can also be applied to trees, but tree results are not claimed here.

## Proof
**1. Exact cell structure and connectedness.** Give \(|G|^n\) its product cubical cell structure. An allowable cell must contain at least \(v\) coordinates at distinct graph vertices, hence can have no more than one positive-dimensional coordinate. Every zero-cell is an onto assignment of \(v+1\) labeled particles to \(v\) anchor vertices: one anchor contains exactly two, the other anchors one each. For each anchor there are \(n!/2\) assignments, giving \(v n!/2\) zero-cells. In each one-cell precisely one label travels along an underlying edge while the remaining \(v\) labels occupy each anchor exactly once. Fixing the oriented underlying edge, the moving label, and the bijection of stationary labels to vertices gives exactly \(n!\) one-cells per edge. Thus the cell counts are \(v n!/2\) and \(e n!\), respectively.

The graph is connected. To see this constructively, choose a spanning tree and a target assignment, rooting the tree at the target doubled vertex. Process nonroot leaves in succession. To place the desired singly occupying label at a leaf, move the doubled location along the remaining tree to the location of that label (moving a duplicate particle into each next occupied vertex), then transport the desired particle along the path to the leaf. At arrival the leaf contains the desired label and another label; move the other label back toward the tree, leaving the leaf correctly occupied. If the desired label is already at the leaf, either retain it or move its unwanted double occupant out. Previously fixed leaves are not used again. At the root the two remaining labels must be the desired pair. Each move is one allowable one-cell. Hence any two vertices are joined by an edge path. Thus \(H_0(X_G;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\), \(H_j=0\) for \(j\ge2\), and \(H_1\) is free with rank \(en!-vn!/2+1\).

**2. Exact rational representation.** Orient each cubical one-cell by the orientation of its moving coordinate along the underlying graph edge. A permutation of particle labels maps such oriented edges to oriented edges *without a sign*, because the other coordinate factors have degree zero. For each graph vertex, the corresponding \(S_n\)-orbit of zero-cells has stabilizer \(H\cong S_2\), interchanging the two coincident labels. For each graph edge, the corresponding orbit of oriented one-cells is free. Consequently, as integral permutation lattices, \(C_0\cong v\mathbb Z[S_n/H]\) and \(C_1\cong e\mathbb Z[S_n]\). The connected cellular exact sequence gives \([H_1]=[C_1]-[C_0]+[\mathbf1]\) in the rational representation ring. Since \(\mathbb Q[S_n]=\operatorname{Ind}_H^{S_n}(\mathbf1\oplus\mathrm{sgn}_H)\), semisimplicity yields the displayed direct-sum isomorphism for \(e\ge v\). The character values follow from the facts that a nonidentity permutation can fix a zero-cell only when it is a transposition of the two labels at the doubled anchor, and no nonidentity permutation fixes an oriented one-cell. Frobenius reciprocity gives the explicit Specht multiplicities.

**3. Integral fixed lattice and quotient index.** The quotient has one zero-cell for each graph vertex and one one-cell for each graph edge, with their usual incidences; therefore \(X_G/S_n\) is canonically homeomorphic to \(|G|\). Choose orientations for the graph edges. For each edge \(a\), let \(\widehat a\) be the sum of all its \(n!\) oriented one-cell lifts. For each vertex \(u\), let \(\widehat u\) be the sum of all \(n!/2\) zero-cells with the double at \(u\). The two end labels at a doubled vertex can each be chosen as the moving one, hence the cellular boundary is
\[
\partial\widehat a=2(\widehat{\mathrm{head}(a)}-\widehat{\mathrm{tail}(a)}).
\]
The \(\widehat a\)'s, respectively \(\widehat u\)'s, form integer bases of the \(S_n\)-fixed chain groups. Their cycle kernel is therefore exactly the ordinary integral graph cycle lattice, because multiplication by two does not alter an integer kernel. As there are no two-cells, this is the fixed subgroup \(H_1(X_G;\mathbb Z)^{S_n}\). The quotient cellular map sends \(\widehat a\) to \(n!a\), proving the multiplication formula and its lattice index, since the graph cycle lattice has rank \(e-v+1\). Finally, fix \(v\) stationary labels at distinct anchors and let the extra label traverse any loop in \(|G|\); this is a closed path in \(X_G\) projecting to that loop. Hence \(p_*\) is surjective before taking invariants.

## Verification
The standalone `anchored_graph_checker.py` constructs all allowable zero-cells and one-cells independently from the surjection and active-coordinate definitions. It checks connectedness by breadth-first search, exact cell counts, all label-permutation Lefschetz traces, and the boundary identity \(\partial\widehat a=2(\widehat v-\widehat u)\). Tests cover the triangle, square, square with a diagonal, complete four-vertex graph, five-cycle, and a triangle with two pendant edges. They enumerate all \(24\), \(120\), or \(720\) label permutations, as applicable. The final marker is `ONE_EXTRA_ANCHORED_GRAPH_EQUIVARIANT_CHECKS_OK`. These finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the all-graphs proof.

## Relationship to prior work
Kozlov's 2023 preprint (published in 2024) determines Euler characteristics of generalized anchored configurations on connected graphs and the nonequivariant homology of the circle case, explicitly including the one-extra-particle regime. The paper and its published duality appendix establish topological and rank results, not the stated label-permutation module or the integral invariant-to-quotient index. Earlier Stirling-complex literature treats anchored configurations on trees; the theorem here is deliberately restricted to graphs containing cycles. An additional indexed 2026 result on integral anchored-configuration homology for all connected graphs provides broader nonequivariant rank coverage but, on the accessible summary, not the complete label-symmetry character or the invariant-lattice index. This work does not present already-known Betti ranks as the original contribution.

## Limitations
The theorem determines rational \(S_n\)-isomorphism types and the \(S_n\)-fixed integral sublattice, not the full isomorphism class of the integral \(\mathbb Z[S_n]\)-module. It does not address collision-free configurations or higher excess-particle numbers, and does not give the full joint action of graph automorphisms. The originality comparison includes an indexed 2026 result for which full text was not accessible; an unindexed prior equivariant computation could alter the novelty assessment. No independent external validation is claimed.
