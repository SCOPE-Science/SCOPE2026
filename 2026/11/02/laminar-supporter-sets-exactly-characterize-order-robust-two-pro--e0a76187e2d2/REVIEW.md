# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim quantifies over every pair of positive mandatory-project costs for which both projects are fully funded in either ordering, not all costs. Nested and disjoint support pairs yield identical payments by explicit uniform-charge inequalities. For every crossing pair the proof selects an open interval of \(\theta\) values, establishes both projects' complete funding, computes unequal budgets, and inserts one single-support optional project whose affordability differs. It explicitly proves that maximum-minimum rebalancing fixes the residual vector when all costs are paid. For all \(n\le6\), the exact-rational checker tested 383,958 cost profiles with full funding in both orders and constructed 1,278 crossing-support witnesses. Limits: finite verification does not establish the infinite theorem and no partial-funding claim is made.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

The primary manuscript of Baychkov, Brill and Utke (https://markus-utke.github.io/files/mixed_PB.pdf) was examined in the definition of MES-Style and Example 2 in §4.2, the theory in §5 and Appendices A–B. Example 2 already establishes order dependence, using four voters; its result is strictly weaker than the iff laminar-vs-crossing classification for all electorate sizes and positive cost pairs under full financing. The manuscript does not state this classification or the universal construction yielding downstream selection sensitivity at three voters. Relevant mathematical research-record searches for laminar/nested supporter sets, sequential equal-share charging and preselected-project order sensitivity returned no equivalent or broader covering claim; high-ranked hits involved different apportionment, school-assignment, or resource-allocation models. General water-filling commutativity principles in unindexed literature are a residual risk. Failed searches are not proof of novelty; the conclusion is best-knowledge, not independently certified.

## Value — PASS

For mixed budgeting rules, prior selected projects must be charged before remaining choices. The iff theorem identifies a natural combinatorial domain where that mandatory charging is unambiguous, and proves that crossing supporter sets are exactly the obstruction; unlike a one-off example it applies to arbitrary electorates, supporter multiplicities and admissible costs. The added project exhibits a real change in subsequent funded outcomes, not merely bookkeeping. These are motivated stability and implementation results for a recently specified voting procedure. The full-funding restriction is a material and explicitly acknowledged boundary.

## Closest literature and limitations
