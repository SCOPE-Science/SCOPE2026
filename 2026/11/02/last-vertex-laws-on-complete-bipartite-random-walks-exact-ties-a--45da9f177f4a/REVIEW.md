# Scientific review

## Correctness (C): PASS
The complete bipartite walk has independent uniform visit streams for its two sides. Starting from a known vertex deletes precisely the first compulsory new-coupon draw, so the last-side event is the non-strict comparison of two independent coupon completion times. A separate visited-count recursion matches the resulting finite rational formula for all part sizes through eight. At balance, the strict positive correction is an exact completion-time tie mass; the local identity \(m p_m(k+1)=\Pr(U_{m,k}=1)\), the Poisson limit for unseen coupons and the Gumbel limit give the integral \(1/4\), hence the coefficient \(1/8\). Independent coupon Gumbels and continuity give the logistic window. Mathematical limits have proofs; finite computations are not asserted to certify limits.

## Originality (O): PASS, qualified
The 2022 Buchanan–Horn–Rombach preprint (all four pages inspected) and the 1993 Lovász–Winkler characterization address when the last new vertex is uniform, not a complete part-wise law and asymptotically sharp finite-balance correction. Standard coupon-collector Gumbel asymptotics underlie the proof and are not new. Ilienko's 2026 extended coupon-collector theorem has an explicit logistic difference, but its dependence and repeated-level objects are different; it does not formulate the complete bipartite cover-tour probability or the balanced discrete-tie coefficient. Targeted searches of mathematical records and literature revealed no directly covering result, although failure to retrieve one is not a uniqueness proof. The full 1993 article was unavailable; a hidden special example remains a priority risk.

## Value (V): PASS
The last discovered vertex is a natural location-specific cover statistic, and complete bipartite graphs form an elementary yet asymmetric graph family. The theorem gives an exact all-size law, the critical \(n/\log n\) imbalance scale and the exact subtle \(1/(8n)\) finite-balance bias. These answer a concrete distributional refinement of the cover-tour classification, beyond a standalone textbook Gumbel limit or a single numerical example. The independent-completion-time identity makes this family reproducibly analyzable.
