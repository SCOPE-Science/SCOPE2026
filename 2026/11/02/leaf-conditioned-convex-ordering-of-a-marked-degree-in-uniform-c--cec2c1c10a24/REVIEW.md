# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The Prüfer occupancy bijection identifies exactly the relevant finite population. Formula (1) counts the tagged-label multiplicity at every admissible boundary. A real-rootedness induction proves the required strict Stirling ratio monotonicity, which establishes a single crossing; constant first moments turn that into strict convex ordering. The factorial-moment calculation supplies the conditional variance and covariance claims. The attached verifier independently checks finite cases exactly; no finite test is used to assert an infinite theorem.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Remmel and Williamson (arXiv:1009.2060, Introduction, Sections 2 and 5) prove the tree/ordered-partition encoding and cardinalities for fixed leaf counts; these contain the counting foundation and can imply the conditional mass formula after summation. They do not state or prove the all-strata convex order or unique tail sign crossing in the inspected complete online preprint. Wagner's 2012 open proceedings treatment and 2014 journal abstract concern asymptotic additive-functionals, not finite conditional tagged-degree order. Aldous's accessible abstract concerns random-tree distributions, not the exact order. Direct implication comparisons distinguish these claims from known counting identities and marginal limit laws. A published one-switch occupancy richness–frequency covariance result was inspected in full: its unconditional covariance across frequency classes does not imply fixed-richness conditional tagged-cell convex order. A residual priority risk remains from older occupancy and total-positivity literature and unavailable full texts.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The leaf stratum is a canonical global random-tree statistic and the marked degree is the simplest local statistic. Constancy of the conditional mean hides a strict increase of every nonaffine convex degree functional and a sharp unique change in the sign of conditional degree-tail differences. This provides an all-threshold dependence classification that is not merely a recomputation of a marginal count and is useful when comparing heterogeneous degree variability at fixed leaf totals.

The exact theorem is restricted to uniform labeled Cayley trees and one fixed vertex; it does not establish joint degree-vector ordering, other tree ensembles, or historical priority in inaccessible texts.
