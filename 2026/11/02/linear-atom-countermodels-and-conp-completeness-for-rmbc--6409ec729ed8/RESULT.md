# Linear-atom countermodels and coNP-completeness for RmbC

## Finding

For every formula \(\varphi\) in the propositional language of the logic \(\mathrm{RmbC}\), let \(t\ge 1\) be the number of its **distinct syntactic subformulas** (including \(\varphi\)). If \(\varphi\) fails in any Boolean algebra with LFI operators (BALFI), it fails in a BALFI whose Boolean reduct is \(\mathcal P([m])\) for some \(1\le m\le t\). Moreover, the interpretation of every subformula is representable by an \(m\)-bit vector, and the Boolean operations and the two LFI operators can be checked at the occurring arguments in polynomial time.

Consequently, using BALFI completeness for \(\mathrm{RmbC}\) and the established lower bound, its formula-validity problem is **coNP-complete**. The finite BALFI has at most \(2^t\) Boolean elements, but is given succinctly by at most \(t^2\) bits of designated subformula values and local consistency checks. The theorem concerns validity of single finite formulas, not arbitrary-premise consequence, other LFI extensions, or minimum model size.

## Assumptions and scope

A BALFI is a Boolean algebra \((B,\wedge,\vee,\to,0,1)\), where \(x\to y=\neg_{\!B}x\vee y\), equipped with two total unary functions \(N,C:B\to B\) satisfying, for every \(x\in B\),
\[
x\vee N(x)=1,\qquad x\wedge N(x)\wedge C(x)=0.
\]
The object-language operations \(\neg\) and \(\circ\) denote \(N\) and \(C\), respectively; Boolean complementation \(\neg_{\!B}\) is not the object-language negation. Formula validity means that every BALFI valuation evaluates the formula to \(1\), as in the established algebraic semantics of \(\mathrm{RmbC}\). Equality of arguments to \(N\) or \(C\) is equality of their **whole** Boolean-algebra values, not agreement at a selected coordinate.

## Proof

**1. Select a distinguishing set of Boolean coordinates.** Assume a BALFI \(B\) and a valuation \(v\) satisfy \(v(\varphi)\ne1\). Set
\[
S=\{1\}\cup\{v(\psi):\psi\in\operatorname{Sub}(\varphi)\}.
\]
Thus \(2\le |S|\le t+1\). Let \(B_0\) be the Boolean subalgebra generated by \(S\). It is finite, even if \(B\) is infinite, so it has a finite atom set \(A\). Distinct elements of \(S\) differ on at least one atom of \(B_0\). Starting with no atoms, repeatedly select an atom of \(B_0\) that separates two currently indistinguishable elements of \(S\). Each selection strictly increases the number of distinct coordinate profiles among elements of \(S\), from one to \(|S|\); thus no more than \(|S|-1\le t\) atoms are selected. Call their set \(T\), with \(m=|T|\). Because \(1\) and \(v(\varphi)\) are different, \(1\le m\le t\).

The map \(h:B_0\to\mathcal P(T)\) sending a Boolean element to the selected atoms beneath it is a Boolean-algebra homomorphism and, crucially, its restriction to \(S\) is injective. It sends \(1\) to \(T\), but \(h(v(\varphi))\ne T\).

**2. Extend the partial operator tables.** When \(\neg\alpha\) is a subformula, prescribe
\[
N'(h(v(\alpha)))=h(v(\neg\alpha)).
\]
Similarly, whenever \(\circ\alpha\) is a subformula, prescribe
\[
C'(h(v(\alpha)))=h(v(\circ\alpha)).
\]
Every argument and output belongs to \(S\). Injectivity of \(h\) on \(S\), together with functionality of the original operations, guarantees that each prescription is well-defined. All simultaneously prescribed values at an argument obey the two BALFI identities because \(h\) preserves Boolean operations. Complete the tables at every other argument \(x\subseteq T\) by assigning \(N'(x)=T\setminus x\) when its \(N'\)-entry is missing, and \(C'(x)=\varnothing\) when its \(C'\)-entry is missing. At arguments where exactly one function was prescribed, the same defaults guarantee both identities; at arguments where both were prescribed the original identities apply. Hence \((\mathcal P(T),N',C')\) is a BALFI.

**3. Preserve the counterexample.** Give each propositional variable \(p\) its projected value \(h(v(p))\). A structural induction on subformulas proves that their interpretations in the new BALFI are exactly \(h(v(\psi))\). The unary steps use the prescribed table values; the Boolean steps use the homomorphism property. In particular the new value of \(\varphi\) is not top. This proves the linear-atom countermodel statement.

**4. Succinct verifier and complexity.** Guess an integer \(1\le m\le t\) and one \(m\)-bit vector \(b_\psi\) for each distinct subformula. Check constants (if present) and every binary Boolean connective by bitwise arithmetic. For every occurrence \(\neg\alpha\), check \(b_\alpha\vee b_{\neg\alpha}=\mathbf1\); for each pair of unary occurrences of the *same* operator with equal whole input vectors, require equal output vectors; and for every occurrence \(\neg\alpha\) and \(\circ\beta\) with \(b_\alpha=b_\beta\), check \(b_\alpha\wedge b_{\neg\alpha}\wedge b_{\circ\beta}=\mathbf0\). Finally require \(b_\varphi\ne\mathbf1\). Every check is polynomial in \(t\); the obvious pairwise implementation takes \(O(t^3)\) bit operations.

Soundness follows by defining missing unary table entries as in step 2; the checks guarantee total BALFI operations and a falsifying valuation. Completeness follows from steps 1–3. Thus BALFI nonvalidity is in NP and validity is in coNP. The previously established coNP-hardness of \(\mathrm{RmbC}\) validity yields coNP-completeness.

**5. Why full vectors are necessary.** A one-atom projection need not preserve unary-function congruence. Indeed, the formula \((p\leftrightarrow q)\to(\neg p\leftrightarrow\neg q)\) is valid in every BALFI on the two-element Boolean algebra, but is refuted on \(\mathcal P(\{a,b\})\): take \(v(p)=\{a\}\), \(v(q)=\{a,b\}\), and, listing values for \(\varnothing,\{a\},\{b\},\{a,b\}\), set \(N=(\{a,b\},\{a,b\},\{a\},\varnothing)\) and \(C=(\varnothing,\varnothing,\varnothing,\{a,b\})\). Every BALFI identity holds; the formula evaluates to \(\{b\}\), rather than the top element.

Run `python3 checker.py` with Python 3 and its standard library. The checker exhaustively tests \(41{,}568\) evaluation/compression instances for one- and two-atom BALFIs, adds \(2{,}000\) seeded tests of randomly generated BALFIs on two to six atoms, checks the structural preservation and local-certificate conditions, and verifies the explicit one-atom insufficiency witness. Successful execution prints `ALL_CHECKS_OK`. These finite tests verify implementation and examples; the unbounded theorem is supported by the argument above, not by exhaustive computation.

## Relationship to prior work

Coniglio and Mallea (2026, arXiv:2608.28443, first publicly posted 2026-08-28) prove BALFI filtration, decidability, a doubly-exponential-time validity upper bound, and coNP-hardness for \(\mathrm{RmbC}\). Their reported bound does not supply the present linear-atom separating-coordinate construction or the tight upper bound. Carnielli, Coniglio and Fuenmayor (2020/2022, arXiv:2003.09522) give the BALFI semantics and an embedding into a fusion of two minimal non-normal modalities. Dalmonte and Mazzullo (2023, DOI below) establish coNP complexity for such modal fusions. This is an important possible route to an alternative complexity argument, but the recursively expanded embedding can duplicate subformulas, and the inspected prior sources did not establish a size-preserving reduction for native \(\mathrm{RmbC}\) formulas or the precise linear-atom BALFI lemma.

## Limitations

The bound is an **upper** bound, not a claim that \(t\) atoms are always necessary. Countermodels on one Boolean atom are not sufficient for all formulas, as step 5 demonstrates. Validity is for formulas over arbitrary BALFIs; this does not classify complexity for extensions with additional identities, proof lengths, or premise-relative consequence. Older results on non-normal modal fusions create a substantial remaining priority risk for the coNP corollary: their combination with an efficient, sharing-aware translation may independently imply it. The direct linear-atom BALFI statement and polynomial checker are the primary structural contribution. No independent review has been performed.
