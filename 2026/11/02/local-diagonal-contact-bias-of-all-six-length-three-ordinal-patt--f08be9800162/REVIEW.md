A published two-position fixed-point–descent localization law is a closer analogy, but does not imply the triple-rank formula. Priority remains subject to uninspected historic exact-pattern refinements.

This is a complete finite-sample dependence classification at the first nontrivial ordinal-pattern length, where peaks and valleys arise and local peak shapes are distinguishable. The sparse exact fixed-site kernel explains how diagonal contacts in permutation matrices bias local order patterns; the six-case profile finds position-dependent sign changes that disappear when ordinary peaks and valleys are pooled. The strongest aggregate conclusions are classically accessible and are included only as consistency consequences, not as independent novelty. No independent audit has been undertaken.
