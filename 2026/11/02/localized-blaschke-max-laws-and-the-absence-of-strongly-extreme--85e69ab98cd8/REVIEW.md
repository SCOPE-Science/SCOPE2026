# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is a precise norm-limit theorem for fixed \(f\in\mathrm{BMO}\), fixed complex scalar, and almost every boundary approach direction. Poisson Fatou limits of \(f\) and \(|f|^2\) force vanishing variance on every fixed Stolz cone at such a boundary point. For a disk automorphism, centered Poisson variance equals \(1-|b_a|^2\); the norm is exactly one and the peak lies at its zero. An explicit inversion identity proves that the pseudohyperbolic peak region approaches the chosen boundary point non-tangentially. The exterior variance is uniformly small when the pseudohyperbolic cutoff is near one, giving a sharp upper estimate. A fixed interior evaluation and evaluation at the automorphism zero give independent matching lower estimates. The resulting plus/minus perturbations have norm tending to one while retaining unit norm, conclusively excluding strong extremality. The proof applies without an unproved compactness assertion or extrapolation from finite experiments.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge)

The first-public September 2026 article of Dyakonov expressly poses the Garsia-norm strongly extreme problem, and the previously published 2024/2025 Garsia-ball paper establishes ordinary extremality rather than the strong midpoint property. The earlier \(H^\infty\) strongly extreme theorem instead uses the supremum norm. Searches of related research records for strongly extreme Garsia balls, Poisson-variance localization, Blaschke peaking, and implication-equivalent max-laws did not locate an equivalent classification. The exact max-law and simultaneous four-space negative classification therefore survive the actual comparisons reviewed. Remaining risk: a standard earlier localization lemma or unindexed text could imply the full result without using the same formulation; a lack of search hits is not a proof of publication priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The finding addresses an explicit contemporary Banach-space geometry problem, provides a complete answer (rather than an arbitrary test family), and shows a sharp separation between ordinary and strongly extreme points even in \(\mathrm{VMO}\). The stronger max-law specifies how norm-one perturbations can escape to the boundary without increasing the host function's oscillation norm, making the obstruction reusable. It is an infinite-dimensional structural phenomenon for natural classical spaces, not a reparametrized basic inequality.

## Scope and remaining uncertainty

The norm is the Garsia norm on constant quotients, not all equivalent \(\mathrm{BMO}\) norms. The a.e. boundary set is allowed to depend on the function. The statement does not settle ordinary extremality in full \(\mathrm{BMO}\), nor claim unindexed literature exhaustiveness.
