# Scientific review: Logarithmic anisotropy drift in resistance-optimal square tori

## Correctness: PASS
The claim fixes the domain \(n\ge3\), \(P,Q,B>0\), the unrestricted edgewise budget, and the effective-resistance normalization. Strict trace-inverse convexity and injectivity of the conductance-to-Laplacian map give global uniqueness; class symmetries are then consequences rather than restrictions. The two-class Fourier spectrum gives the exact scalar objective. The finite-cycle resolvent identity and endpoint decomposition establish a uniform twice-differentiable asymptotic with an explicit exponentially convergent constant. Its derivative yields the displayed finite-size correction, and the zero-frequency modes keep minimizers away from singular parameter boundaries. The independent-weights direct-matrix rational tests in `verify.py` agree with the spectrum; the exact formula, not those finite tests, proves the infinite statement. The proof does not cover varying prices or rectangular tori.

## Originality: PASS, with residual priority risk
The full arXiv manuscript of Rossi, Frasca and Fagnani (arXiv:1309.2172, first public 2013-09-09), including its torus spectra and dimension-two asymptotic proofs, treats unit-conductance grids, not price-constrained allocation. The Ghosh--Boyd--Saberi full paper (DOI 10.1137/050645452, especially Sections 4--5) provides general convex design theory and examples for other graph families, not this anisotropic square-torus logarithmic drift. Cserti's accessible HTML manuscript (arXiv:cond-mat/9909120, introduction and fixed-network Fourier Green formulas, with square/rectangular sections located but not exhaustively inspected) addresses fixed-network resistances; specialized earlier coverage remains a residual risk. Published mathematical research-record searches under directional cost, toroidal resistance, inverse-price anisotropy, Green-function eta constant and logarithmic drift did not identify an implication-equivalent published claim. Accessible earlier findings on optimal prism allocation and anisotropic hypercube allocation concern different asymptotic regimes, neither implying this two-dimensional correction. The fixed-weight lattice Green constant can be classical in another representation; the originality claim is the optimized design and the explicit first logarithmic correction, not priority over that fixed-weight identity. Hidden specialized literature remains possible.

## Mathematical value: PASS
A two-dimensional periodic network is a natural benchmark for noise-robust consensus and relative-position estimation; pricing its two directional edge classes is an unavoidable design extension. The result gives a complete global allocation rule for each size, the inverse-price limiting law, a nontrivial \(1/\log n\) bias with a computable rapidly convergent coefficient, and the sharp optimal-resistance constant term. The logarithmically slow convergence is operationally relevant: even large finite grids can differ visibly from their continuum allocation. This is not a renamed finite graph spectrum, arbitrary parameter choice, or a routine fixed-weight table entry.

## Scope and residual limitations
The uniform price vector is fixed with \(n\); no claim concerns variable price scaling, boundary effects, arbitrary edge price disorders, or non-square lattices. Search incompleteness and possible alternative formulations in the lattice-Green/theta-function literature remain originality risks. Direct numerical checks have finite reach; independent audit remains outstanding.
