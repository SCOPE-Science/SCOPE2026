# Scientific review

## Correctness (C): PASS

The event and the killed two-center transitions are defined on the original labeled seed edge. The star boundary probability, entrance mass, positive lower survival product, and upper harmonic supersolution are derived from the exact transition. The upper-bound function is a convergent product, is at least one, and satisfies the displayed strict one-step inequality for every pair of positive integer leaf counts. Both inequalities are uniform in the tree size. The independent checker passes 720 assertions, with the infinite theorem proved algebraically rather than inferred from tests.

## Originality (O): PASS (qualified)

Redner's 2017 complete-redirection paper already develops star and arbitrary double-hub recurrences; the 2024 random friend-tree paper develops the stochastic model and its broader structural theory. Those prior contributions are credited. The present event fixes the original labeled seed edge as central, and its entry flux is distinct from the unmarked hub creation mechanism. Searches for marked seed-edge survival, labeled double stars, and equivalent two-original-branch-vertex events did not find an implication-equivalent result. Absolute priority cannot be certified by finite literature searches; an unlocated more general labeled recursive-tree identity remains possible.

## Value (V): PASS

Tracking whether the original seed edge persists as the sole hub bridge is a natural measure of seed-memory in local attachment. The theorem sharpens its probability from the vacuous upper bound of unrestricted two-hub rarity to the different \(\Theta((\log n)/n^2)\) scale, with all-size explicit bounds. The exact logarithmic leading coefficient is not resolved. This is a natural rare-event distinction between chronological and unmarked graph observables, not a recomputation of known star frequencies.

## Closest literature and limitations

* S. Redner, *Emergent Network Modularity*, arXiv:1706.01514, Sections 2 and 4: exact star and unmarked two-hub recurrences.
* L. Addario-Berry and collaborators, *Random friend trees*, arXiv:2403.20185, March 29, 2024: full neighbor attachment model and global structure.
* C. Cannings and J. Jordan, *Random walk attachment graphs*, arXiv:1303.1052: earlier attachment model and leaf law.

The result addresses only the seed-marked event with one-step neighbor redirection. It does not establish an exact normalized logarithmic limit, general initial configurations, or independent historical priority.
