# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The statement fixes process normalization, boundary convention, reset-state dynamics, stable index and quantifiers. The short-time marked stable exit rates follow by integrating the Lévy measure and invoking the published interval first-exit theorem. The exact Markov renewal vector equation separates absorption from resetting. A simple-eigenvalue/left-stationary projection gives the marked exponential limit. An explicit uniform exponential-moment bound proves all integer moments; deterministic finite-state experiments (30 assertions) verify the matrix arithmetic but do not serve as a substitute for the analytical argument.

## Originality — PASS (qualified best-knowledge)
The 2026 Żbik–Palmowski paper studies one fixed reset destination and the mean, not Markov-correlated multisite destinations, side-resolved limiting law or all moments. The earlier González–Riascos–Boyer network results address multiple destinations using state-independent location draws and finite-network spectral FPT, not the present finite-memory destination process in an expanding continuous stable interval. Figueroa-López–Tankov provide the required jump exit asymptotic, and Żbik–Dybiec treat resetting with fixed initial points. No exactly matching implication was located in claim-level database searches. General Markov-renewal rare-event theory may subsume the mechanism and remains the principal publication-priority risk; database search alone is not a uniqueness proof.

## Value — PASS
A nonreversible Markov destination protocol has extensive memory and no scalar renewal equation. The theorem nonetheless identifies a simple quantitative escape and directional-target law valid at all fixed positive reset rates, including exact convergence of all moments. This permits comparison of distinct correlated resetting strategies sharing stationary location weights, and distinguishes averaging escape hazards from averaging mean passage times. These are physically motivated, directly measurable transport observables, not arbitrary numerical slices.
