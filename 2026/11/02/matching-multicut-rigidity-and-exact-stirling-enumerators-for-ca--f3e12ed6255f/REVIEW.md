# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The Cartesian-square argument forces a crossing edge to propagate in every perpendicular direction, then makes every orthogonal fiber monochromatic; connectedness and induction give uniqueness. Crossing-edge multiplicity is exactly the order of the complementary factor. On paths and cycles, matching crossings select disjoint boundary edges, while proper partitions of monochromatic runs supply the Stirling/chromatic factors; the two-edge lower boundary for cycles and the two-run case are explicitly handled. The standalone verifier independently enumerates each unlabeled partition on small examples. Finite computations are corroboration, not the infinite proof.

**Originality — PASS (best-knowledge literature assessment).** The original complete arXiv version and conference version of Gomes–Juliano–Martins–dos Santos on matching multicuts were inspected for Cartesian products, grids and these two-variable enumerators. Centeno et al. study a distinct (for three or more blocks) path-convex partition invariant. Other relevant matching-cut work studies complexity rather than this universal factor-rigidity identity. Searches of alternate phrases, corresponding exact formulas and mathematical research records did not identify a prior implication of the result. A later abstract on convex partitions of Cartesian products was accessible only as an abstract, presenting a residual unassessed-full-text priority risk; unindexed earlier equivalences remain possible. The absence of a hit is not a novelty proof.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The structural theorem converts an apparently multidimensional partition problem to an exact disjoint union of one-factor problems for all connected Cartesian products. Its joint distribution of the number of blocks and cut edges is stronger than existence or optimum-block counts and yields explicit nontrivial Stirling and chromatic formulas for every path–cycle grid. The scope is natural because matching-multicut decisions/enumeration are studied on difficult general graph classes. This is not a mere special-case numerical calculation.

**Closest literature and limits.** Gomes et al., arXiv:2407.02898v1 (2024-07-03), defines the matching-multicut problem and addresses algorithms and enumeration; Centeno et al., DMTCS (2010), analyzes path-of-order-three convexity; Bonsma, JGT (2009), treats ordinary matching cuts. An abstract on \(P_3\)-convex graph-product partitions (2026) cannot establish a full-text negative coverage judgment. No conclusion is made for disconnected factors or non-Cartesian products; scientific priority and correctness await independent validation.
