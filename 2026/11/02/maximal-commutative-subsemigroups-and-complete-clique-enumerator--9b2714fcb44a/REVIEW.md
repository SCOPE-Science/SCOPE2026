# Review of the finite Brandt-semigroup clique classification

## Correctness (C) — PASS

The multiplication law in the theorem gives all three kinds of pairwise commutation explicitly. The disjointness of occupied source and target index sets is forced by these products, not conjectured from small cases. The maximal-clique proof handles both the offdiagonal-empty case and every possible occupied source–sink partition; omitted group labels and diagonal elements can be added unless the displayed forms are complete. Every maximal pairwise-commuting subset in a finite group is a maximal abelian subgroup. Counting the unique partitions gives the maximal-subsemigroup size polynomial, and inclusion–exclusion over isolated endpoints gives the full clique polynomial. The clique-number bound uses the discrete convexity of the complete-bipartite product bound. The finite verifier independently reconstructs Brandt multiplication and compares complete clique profiles and maximal-clique profiles in ten configurations, including nonabelian \(S_3\). It also checks a full pairwise commuting-graph isomorphism for the cyclic and Klein groups of order four. The general proof does not rely on these finite checks. The claim is restricted to finite Brandt semigroups with at least two indices.

## Originality (O) — PASS relative to inspected sources

Kumar–Dalal–Pandey, arXiv:2010.05000 (10 October 2020), study the same aperiodic Brandt commuting graph. Their Lemma 3.10 classifies a source–sink block that attains the **maximum clique size**, and their Theorem 3.14 gives the clique number. The disjoint-source/target observation and the maximum-size aperiodic result are therefore existing mathematics, expressly acknowledged. Their statements do not determine all inclusion-maximal cliques, sizes of all maximal commutative subsemigroups, all clique coefficients, or arbitrary nonabelian structure groups. Margolis–Rhodes–Silva, arXiv:1705.04956, describe subsemigroups of the aperiodic Brandt semigroup through transitive relations and study the associated subsemigroup complex, a different simplicial complex; that broad description is acknowledged but does not explicitly furnish the two formulas. The 2025 paper on commuting graphs of Rees matrix semigroups addresses a different matrix product and graph invariants. Searches of relevant published records and previous indexed mathematical claims found no equivalent formula. This negative search result is not proof of global novelty.

## Mathematical value (V) — PASS

Maximal commutative subsemigroups are natural algebraic objects in finite inverse semigroups. The classification describes their actual algebraic structure, not just a small parameter adjustment to a known clique number: it identifies two complete types for every finite structure group and gives the exact maximal-clique size distribution in terms of maximal abelian subgroup data. Separately, the complete clique polynomial determines the number of commuting subsets at every size and specializes to an explicit closed count \(3^n-2^{n+1}+2\) for all abelian structure groups. The nonisomorphic same-order abelian-group example shows why this richer graph invariant still does not reconstruct the underlying semigroup. A previously unlocated general classification might subsume portions of the result; this residual publication-priority risk remains.

## Closest literature and limitations

The comparison does not claim as new the underlying Brandt multiplication, clique-number theorem, source–sink observation, or transitive-relation description of subsemigroups. It leaves open formulas for the group input polynomials in arbitrary large finite groups, and does not extend the semigroup classification to general sandwich matrices or infinite groups. Computational examples do not establish the universal result, which is supplied by the separate structural proof.
