# Maximal quadratic-extension reduction of Barth's sextic at the ramified prime five

## Finding
Let \(R=\mathbb Z[\varphi,1/2]\), where \(\varphi^2-\varphi-1=0\). Consider Barth's integral homogeneous sextic
\[
4(\varphi^2X^2-Y^2)(\varphi^2Y^2-Z^2)(\varphi^2Z^2-X^2)
 -(1+2\varphi)(X^2+Y^2+Z^2-W^2)^2W^2=0.
\]
Specialize to \(\mathbb F_5\) by \(\varphi\mapsto3\), and denote the resulting projective surface by \(S\). Let \(\pi:Y\to S\) be its minimal resolution. Then:

1. \(S\) is geometrically integral and has precisely \(65\) geometric singular points. Every one is an \(\mathbb F_5\)-rational ordinary double point, and the entire singular scheme is reduced.
2. \(Y\) is a smooth projective surface of general type with \(K_Y^2=24\), \(\chi(\mathcal O_Y)=11\), and \(b_2(Y)=106\). It is maximal over \(\mathbb F_{25}\):
\[
\#Y(\mathbb F_{25})=3276=1+25^2+106\cdot25.
\]
3. Geometric Frobenius on \(H^2_{\mathrm{et}}(Y_{\overline{\mathbb F}_5},\mathbb Q_\ell)\), for every \(\ell\ne5\), has eigenvalues \(+5\) with multiplicity \(92\) and \(-5\) with multiplicity \(14\). For every integer \(m\ge1\), writing \(q=5^m\),
\[
\#Y(\mathbb F_q)=1+q^2+(92+14(-1)^m)q,
\qquad
\#S(\mathbb F_q)=1+q^2+(27+14(-1)^m)q.
\]
In particular,
\[
Z(S/\mathbb F_5,T)=\frac1{(1-T)(1-25T)(1-5T)^{27}(1+5T)^{14}},
\]
and
\[
Z(Y/\mathbb F_5,T)=\frac1{(1-T)(1-25T)(1-5T)^{92}(1+5T)^{14}}.
\]

## Assumptions and scope
The coefficient specialization takes place in the residue field of the ramified prime \((5,\varphi-3)\) of \(R\); it does **not** assert that the whole scheme-theoretic fiber of \(R/5R\) is reduced. All point counts use the actual projective hypersurface defined by the displayed integral model. The resolution is the blowup at the \(65\) distinct ordinary nodes (equivalently the minimal resolution); no claim is made about other integral models or a universal positive-characteristic upper bound for sextic nodes.

Dividing the specialized equation by the nonzero scalar \(4\), and putting \(Q=X^2+Y^2+Z^2-W^2\), one obtains the explicit polynomial
\[
F=-(X^2+Y^2)(Y^2+Z^2)(Z^2+X^2)-3Q^2W^2\in\mathbb F_5[X,Y,Z,W].
\]
All proof statements are over \(\mathbb F_5\) unless a geometric closure or finite extension is explicitly indicated.

## Proof
**Exact singular-scheme exhaustion.** On each of the four disjoint projective charts where the first nonzero coordinate (in order \(X,Y,Z,W\)) is normalized to \(1\), form the three-variable affine equation \(f\). Its singular scheme is generated by \(f\) and its three partial derivatives. Exact Gröbner reduction over \(\mathbb F_5\) gives, on **every** chart, zero remainder for each polynomial \(t_i^5-t_i\) modulo that Jacobian ideal; `verify.py` constructs the four ideals directly and performs all twelve polynomial ideal-membership tests. Consequently, all geometric singular points lie in \(\mathbb F_5^3\) on their respective charts. Moreover, the quotient of \(\mathbb F_5[t_1,t_2,t_3]/(t_1^5-t_1,t_2^5-t_2,t_3^5-t_3)\) by the singular ideal is a quotient of a product of fields; hence the singular scheme is reduced. This algebraic identity proves completeness over the algebraic closure, not merely a search over rational points.

Exhaustively evaluating the four singular ideals on their \(125,25,5,1\) respective rational chart candidates gives exactly \((55,7,3,0)\) solutions, totaling \(65\). At every solution, the determinant of the corresponding three-variable quadratic Hessian is nonzero. The \(65\) Hessian determinants are \(1\) at \(27\) points and \(4\) at \(38\) points, modulo \(5\). Because \(2\) is invertible, these are geometrically ordinary quadratic double points. Thus \(S\) has exactly the stated singularities, all reduced and rational.

An algebraic hypersurface in \(\mathbb P^3_{\overline{\mathbb F}_5}\) with finite nonempty singular locus cannot be nonreduced or have two positive-degree components: either condition would give a positive-dimensional singular locus. Therefore \(S\) is geometrically integral. Its isolated nodes are rational double points. Blowing them up gives its smooth minimal resolution \(Y\). For each node, the exceptional curve is a nonsingular projective conic over \(\mathbb F_5\), hence isomorphic to \(\mathbb P^1\) over this finite field. It has \(q+1\) rational points over every \(\mathbb F_q\), replacing one rational point of \(S\). Thus
\[
\#Y(\mathbb F_q)=\#S(\mathbb F_q)+65q
\]
for every \(q=5^m\).

**Point counts.** Exhaustive projective enumeration in the four disjoint charts gives
\[
(75,13,3,0)\quad\text{over }\mathbb F_5,\qquad
(1635,13,3,0)\quad\text{over }\mathbb F_{25}.
\]
Thus \(\#S(\mathbb F_5)=91\) and \(\#S(\mathbb F_{25})=1651\). The field of \(25\) elements is explicitly \(\mathbb F_5[\theta]/(\theta^2-2)\); this is a field because \(2\) is nonsquare in \(\mathbb F_5\). `verify.py` computes the \(\mathbb F_{25}\)-counts twice, once by all projective coordinate vectors and independently by enumerating square values with their exact square-root fiber weights. Both return the same four integers. Hence
\[
\#Y(\mathbb F_5)=91+65\cdot5=416,
\qquad
\#Y(\mathbb F_{25})=1651+65\cdot25=3276.
\]

**Cohomology and rigidity.** A degree-six hypersurface has dualizing sheaf \(\mathcal O_S(2)\), with \(K_S^2=4\cdot6=24\). Ordinary nodes are rational double points and their resolutions are crepant, so \(K_Y^2=24\). The hypersurface exact sequence on \(\mathbb P^3\) gives \(h^1(S,\mathcal O_S)=0\) and \(h^2(S,\mathcal O_S)=10\). Rationality of the node singularities gives \(h^i(Y,\mathcal O_Y)=h^i(S,\mathcal O_S)\), hence \(\chi(\mathcal O_Y)=11\). As \(K_Y\) is the pullback of the ample \(\mathcal O_S(2)\), \(Y\) is of general type. Noether's formula yields \(c_2(Y)=12\cdot11-24=108\). Since \(h^1(\mathcal O_Y)=0\), the Albanese has dimension zero and \(b_1=b_3=0\); étale Gauss--Bonnet then gives \(b_2=108-2=106\).

For every \(\ell\ne5\), let \(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_{106}\) be Frobenius eigenvalues on \(H^2_{\mathrm{et}}\). Smooth properness and the Weil conjectures give \(|\iota(\alpha_i)|=5\) for each complex embedding \(\iota\). The trace formula and the two counts show
\[
\sum_i\alpha_i=416-1-25=390,
\qquad
\sum_i\alpha_i^2=3276-1-625=2650=106\cdot25.
\]
The latter equality attains the triangle-inequality bound for the \(106\) complex numbers of modulus \(25\), forcing \(\alpha_i^2=25\) for all \(i\). The first equality then determines exactly \(92\) positive and \(14\) negative eigenvalues. The cohomological trace formula and the exceptional-conic relation prove the stated formulas over **every** extension and both zeta functions.

## Relationship to prior work
Barth's classical sextic and its \(65\) complex nodes are established, not newly claimed. Endraß's mathematical account supplies the sextic equation and its square-coordinate covering of a Cayley cubic; Jaffe and Ruberman study the \(65\)-node bound over \(\mathbb C\). The arithmetic contribution here is the **ramified-characteristic-five persistence and complete splitting of all \(65\) nodes**, together with the quadratic-extension maximality, full Frobenius spectrum and all-extension zeta functions. The complex node count alone neither controls flat specialization at \(5\) nor implies those Frobenius eigenvalues. The relevant inspected accounts do not state the above characteristic-five arithmetic identities.

## Limitations
No claim is made that this is the unique or maximally nodal sextic in arbitrary positive characteristic. The cohomological Frobenius spectrum does not, by itself, establish algebraicity of every divisor-class eigenvector or a Picard-rank equality. Original publication by Barth (1996) could not be examined in full; historical priority of an unindexed explicit arithmetic treatment remains possible. The result refers only to the specified integral equation and its residue-field reduction at \((5,\varphi-3)\).
