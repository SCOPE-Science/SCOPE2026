# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The proof's two independent iff statements (domination by all gaps at most \(2r+1\), and inclusion-minimality by a private witness for each selected vertex) are proved using periodic integer lifts. The transfer-matrix trace counts rooted gap words, with each labeled configuration counted \(k\) times; the \(n/k\) coefficient factor is exact, including the one-element boundary. Summed adjacent inequalities and a constructive choice of gaps prove the entire support interval, and the forced two-periodicity at \(n=k(r+1)\) proves the parity counts. The standalone graph-level checks independently corroborate 32 graph instances and 35 parity cases; no infinite proof is inferred from them.

## Originality — PASS, subject to priority uncertainty
Galvin and Zhang's 2024 paper gives all-domination polynomials of cycle powers, not inclusion-minimal-domination distributions. The known \(r=1\) univariate sequence OEIS A253413 is explicitly credited. General minimal-domination algorithms do not directly give this finite matrix, spectrum and parity-rigid enumeration. An accessible preceding result on *connected* dominating sets of cycle powers is not an equivalent or stronger assertion. Dedicated semantic-index, literature and ledger searches found no equivalent all-parameter theorem; unindexed sources or unavailable complete texts remain genuine publication-priority risks, and no independent assessment has been performed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The exact all-cardinality classification, rational transfer-matrix enumeration and sharp no-gap spectrum concern a natural family with well-established domination-polynomial literature. The parity-dependent number of largest inclusion-minimal sets is a nontrivial structural invariant beyond minimum or upper domination numbers alone.

## Limits
Only simple cycle powers \(C_n^r\) for \(r\ge1,n\ge2r+1\) are treated. The original cycle case is known at univariate level. The finite checker does not establish the infinite result. Claims of independent validation or publication priority are not made.
