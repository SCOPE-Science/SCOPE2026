# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof derives the exact three-condition domination criterion from token adjacency, bounds the internal/cross-pair type counts, classifies the equality cases, and treats the exceptional \(n=4\) configuration separately. Every choice is reversible and determines a feasible minimum set. Independent direct bitmask enumeration of \(47,432,132\) labelled candidate subsets across \(n=3,\ldots,7\) agrees with the complete classification. The finite test is corroborative, not the reason for the infinite conclusion.

**Originality — PASS, with qualified historical priority.** Both 2026 primary preprints determine the *minimum cardinality* and display the ordinary construction; the more detailed work supplies a general domination criterion and remarks on an equality structure. Neither inspected paper states the complete minimum-set count, the exceptional second orbit, or its \(36\)-fold multiplicity. Targeted published-record and literature searches found no stronger enumerator that entails the classification. The same criterion provides a starting point, so the contribution is the global equality analysis, not a new formulation of domination. General unlocated earlier enumerations remain a priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** The number and orbit structure of minimum solutions refine the recently established optimization problem. The complete classification exhibits rigidity for all large \(n\) and a small-order exception that more than triples the naive count at \(n=4\); it is information unavailable from the domination number alone. In particular, it permits exact uniform sampling of optimal solutions and distinguishes two qualitatively different optimal architectures.

Closest literature: Chinman et al., arXiv:2610.28936v1; Acosta Troncoso et al., arXiv:2610.31557v1. Limits: the claim is confined to ordinary domination of \(F_2(K_{3,n})\) for \(n\ge3\); no full domination polynomial or general \(m\)-side classification is asserted. Independent validation and exhaustive global priority determination have not occurred.
