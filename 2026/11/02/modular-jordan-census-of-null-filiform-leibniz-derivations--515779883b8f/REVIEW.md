# Scientific review: Modular Jordan census of null-filiform Leibniz derivations

## Correctness — PASS

For the explicitly defined right Leibniz algebra, induction from the multiplication table gives every ordinary derivation uniquely as \(D=uH+f(N)\). The nilpotent branch \(u=0\) is controlled by the first nonzero coefficient of \(f\). For each fixed \(u\ne0\), conjugation by a unit Toeplitz polynomial removes exactly the nonresonant degrees; division is performed only by \(uj\) for \(p\nmid j\). Triangularity proves a bijection of input coefficients with normalization and retained resonance coefficients, not merely surjectivity. On each mod-\(p\) residue class the remaining nilpotent operator has the same power kernels as a shift power, determining its Jordan partition over the ground field. Explicitly counting first nonzero resonant coefficients produces the displayed Jordan multiplicities and the separate nullity/rank count, which sums to \(q^n\). The polynomial argument is valid for all primes and extension degrees. The standalone program independently extracts Jordan types and nullities from the original matrices for twenty exhaustively enumerated test configurations.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical priority risk

The existing characteristic-zero derivation-matrix description and general Jordan–Chevalley results are identified as prior work, not new contributions. The claimed new invariant is the complete positive-characteristic, finite-field enumeration of the actual Jordan types (labelled by eigenvalues) and multiplicities, including the modular resonance and nilpotent branches. The 2011 general derivation paper discusses complex Jordan–Chevalley structure, the 2013 paper displays the null-filiform derivation matrix for deformation questions, and the 2015 paper classifies central extensions; none of the inspected relevant sections states or implies the finite-field census without additional arithmetic-normalization arguments. Searches for modular derivation rank/Jordan distributions and comparable results did not identify a matching statement. A potentially equivalent modular Toeplitz normal form in historical matrix literature remains a genuine priority risk; an unsuccessful search does not prove novelty.

## Value — PASS

Jordan type is a natural conjugacy invariant of the actual multiplication-preserving linear operators. The closed finite-field distribution classifies this invariant in every dimension, every characteristic, and every extension field for a canonical family of nonassociative algebras. Characteristic resonance produces nontrivial Jordan blocks and the sharp invertibility boundary \(p>n\); the example in dimension three over characteristic two has distinct Jordan types of equal rank. The result therefore determines strictly more than the standard derivation-matrix presentation or a routine dimension count, without claiming broader deformation or automorphism-orbit results.

No complete historical search can exclude an equivalent result expressed as a normal form for triangular Euler-plus-shift matrices. The algebra is one specified family only; the proof is internal, and there has been no independent mathematical validation.
