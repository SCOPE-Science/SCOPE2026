# Modular local derivations and two-local rigidity of null-filiform Leibniz algebras

## Finding
For every field \(k\) and \(n\ge 2\), let \(L_n(k)\) be the right Leibniz algebra with basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\) and products \([e_i,e_1]=e_{i+1}\) for \(1\le i<n\), all other basis products zero. A **local derivation** means a *linear* map \(T:L_n(k)\to L_n(k)\) such that, for every \(v\), some ordinary derivation \(D_v\) satisfies \(T(v)=D_v(v)\). A **two-local derivation** is a map \(F:L_n(k)\to L_n(k)\), not assumed linear, for which every pair \(v,w\) admits one derivation \(D_{v,w}\) with \(F(v)=D_{v,w}(v)\) and \(F(w)=D_{v,w}(w)\).

**Theorem.** An endomorphism \(T\) is a linear local derivation if and only if (i) \(T(e_i)\in\operatorname{span}(e_i,\ldots,e_n)\) for every \(i\), and (ii) if \(\operatorname{char}k=p>0\), its coefficient of \(e_i\) in \(T(e_i)\) is zero whenever \(p\mid i\). There are no conditions (ii) in characteristic zero. In particular,
\[
\dim_k\operatorname{LocDer}(L_n(k))=
\begin{cases}
\frac{n(n+1)}2,&\operatorname{char}k=0,\\
\frac{n(n+1)}2-\lfloor n/p\rfloor,&\operatorname{char}k=p>0.
\end{cases}
\]
Every two-local derivation, **even without linearity**, is an ordinary derivation. Moreover \(\dim_k\operatorname{Der}(L_n(k))=n\) in every characteristic.

For \(k=\mathbb F_q\) of characteristic \(p\), the counts of labelled linear local derivations, linear two-local derivations, and ordinary derivations are respectively
\[
q^{\frac{n(n+1)}2-\lfloor n/p\rfloor},\qquad q^n,\qquad q^n.
\]
Thus the modular drop from the characteristic-zero local-derivation dimension is exactly \(\lfloor n/p\rfloor\). For instance, for \(n=4\) and \(k=\mathbb F_2\) there are \(256\) linear local derivations but only \(16\) two-local derivations.

## Assumptions and scope
The multiplication is right Leibniz, not associative and not a Lie bracket; the convention is \([u,[v,w]]=[[u,v],w]-[[u,w],v]\). The theorem is valid for every field, including imperfect fields and characteristic two. Linear local derivations are required to be \(k\)-linear; two-local derivations are not. Dimensions refer to spaces of \(k\)-linear operators, not to spaces of all set maps. For \(n\ge2\), the algebra is generated by \(e_1\).

## Proof
Put \(S(e_i)=e_{i+1}\) for \(i<n\), \(S(e_n)=0\), and \(N(e_i)=i e_i\), interpreted in \(k\). Then \([u,v]=v_1S(u)\), where \(v_1\) is the first coordinate of \(v\). Every ordinary derivation \(D\) is uniquely determined by its value \(D(e_1)=\sum_{j=1}^n a_j e_j\). Indeed \(e_{i+1}=[e_i,e_1]\), so the derivation identity gives
\[
D(e_{i+1})=S(D(e_i))+a_1e_{i+1}.
\]
Induction yields
\[
D(e_i)=i a_1e_i+\sum_{j=2}^{n-i+1}a_j e_{i+j-1},\qquad
D=a_1N+\sum_{j=2}^n a_j S^{j-1}.
\]
Conversely these maps satisfy the derivation identity on every basis pair: for a right input other than \(e_1\), the product and both Leibniz terms vanish; for right input \(e_1\), the recurrence verifies it. The parameterization by \(D(e_1)\) is bijective, so ordinary derivations have dimension \(n\).

Let \(V_t=\operatorname{span}(e_t,\ldots,e_n)\) with \(V_{n+1}=0\), and take a nonzero vector \(v=c_t e_t+\cdots+c_n e_n\), \(c_t\ne0\). The vectors \(Sv,S^2v,\ldots,S^{n-t}v\) are a triangular basis of \(V_{t+1}\). Hence
\[
\{D(v):D\in\operatorname{Der}(L_n(k))\}=kNv+V_{t+1}.
\]
The coefficient of \(e_t\) in \(Nv\) equals \(t c_t\). Therefore the evaluation space is exactly \(V_t\) if \(t\) is nonzero in \(k\), and exactly \(V_{t+1}\) if \(t=0\) in \(k\). This is the critical modular evaluation lemma; it makes no divisibility assumptions about other coefficients.

If \(T\) is a linear local derivation, evaluating at \(v=e_t\) gives \(T(e_t)\in V_t\), and also gives the vanishing of the \(e_t\) coefficient when \(\operatorname{char}k\) divides \(t\). Conversely suppose \(T\) has the stated triangular form and vanishing diagonal entries. For any nonzero \(v\) with leading index \(t\), triangularity gives \(T(v)\in V_t\), and its \(e_t\) coefficient is \(c_t T_{tt}\). If \(t\ne0\) in \(k\) this lies in the full evaluation space \(V_t\); if \(t=0\), the diagonal restriction forces \(T(v)\in V_{t+1}\), again exactly the evaluation space. A suitable \(D_v\) thus exists. The zero vector causes no obstruction. Counting the free entries of a lower triangular matrix gives \(n(n+1)/2\) minus the number \(\lfloor n/p\rfloor\) of forbidden diagonal positions in positive characteristic.

Finally let \(F\) be any two-local derivation. Pairing \(e_1\) with itself supplies a derivation \(D\) satisfying \(F(e_1)=D(e_1)\). For each arbitrary \(v\), two-locality of the pair \((e_1,v)\) supplies \(D_v\) with \(D_v(e_1)=F(e_1)=D(e_1)\). The proved uniqueness of the derivation determined by its value at \(e_1\) implies \(D_v=D\), so \(F(v)=D(v)\) for all \(v\). This argument requires neither linearity of \(F\) nor any cardinality or characteristic assumption.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library script `verify.py` constructs all \(p^n\) ordinary derivations over each tested prime field, checks the derivation identity on basis and vector pairs, computes each evaluation image, and compares pointwise locality to the predicted triangular condition for **every** linear endomorphism in six small cases: \((p,n)=(2,2),(2,3),(2,4),(3,2),(3,3),(5,2)\). It checks that exactly \(p^n\) of these local maps equal the unique derivation prescribed by their first basis-vector image, thereby independently testing the two-local rigidity criterion in the exhaustive cases. Three larger pairs receive sampled identity tests. All tests print `MODULAR_LEIBNIZ_LOCAL_DERIVATIONS_OK` upon success. Exhaustive finite tests are not the proof for all \(n,p\), which is the argument above.

## Relationship to prior work
Khudoyberdiyev and Omirov's 2013 work on null-filiform Leibniz superalgebras supplies the established characteristic-zero null-filiform setting and the standard explicit ordinary-derivation formula. The formula itself is **not** claimed as new. Normurodovna (2026) proves the full lower-triangular description of linear local derivations over the complex numbers, with dimension \(n(n+1)/2\). That case is also **not** claimed as new. Its argument uses the nonzero scalar corresponding to the vector's leading index; that invertibility fails precisely at multiples of the positive characteristic. The new contribution here is the uniform modular correction at those indices, the resulting exact local-derivation dimensions/counts in every positive characteristic, and the accompanying arbitrary-field two-local rigidity for this nonassociative family. Results on local derivations of associative null-filiform algebras concern a different multiplication and do not imply this classification.

## Limitations
Only the indicated null-filiform **right** Leibniz algebras are considered, not arbitrary cyclic, filiform, or left-convention variants. The classification concerns linear local derivations; arbitrary nonlinear one-local maps may behave differently. The evaluation-space calculation proves the formula for all fields, while computational verification uses only prime fields and low dimensions. The characteristic-zero local-derivation classification and standard derivation formula are established antecedents. Publication priority of the positive-characteristic correction has not received independent certification; relevant specialized literature may exist beyond inspected sources.
