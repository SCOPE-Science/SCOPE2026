# Multicritical Poisson–binomial laws for finite non-contingency algebras

## Finding
Fix an integer \(k\geq1\), and write \(q=2^k\). Consider \(m\) disjoint, equally sized \(r\)-element equivalence classes, with independently and uniformly assigned \(k\)-bit propositional colours. Let \(\Delta\varphi\) mean that \(\varphi\) is constant on the equivalence class of the current world, and let \(Z_{m,r}\) be the number of atoms in the Boolean algebra generated by the \(k\) propositional variables and closed under \(\Delta\).

**Exact law.** If \(S\) ranges through nonempty subsets of the \(q\) colours and \(I_S\) indicates that some class has precisely the colour support \(S\), then
\[
Z_{m,r}=\sum_{\varnothing\neq S\subseteq[q]}|S|I_S.
\]
For \(s=|S|\), one class has support exactly \(S\) with probability
\[
p_{r,s}=\frac{1}{q^r}\sum_{j=0}^{s}(-1)^{s-j}\binom{s}{j}j^r.
\]
These expressions give an exact finite distribution by inclusion–exclusion over the support types, as well as
\[
\mathbb E Z_{m,r}=\sum_{s=1}^q s\binom qs\bigl(1-(1-p_{r,s})^m\bigr).
\]

**Complete limiting laws.** Assume that \(m=m_n\to\infty\) and \(r=r_n\to\infty\). Put
\[
a_{n,s}=m_n(s/q)^{r_n}\quad(1\leq s<q),\qquad B_s=\sum_{t=s}^q t\binom qt.
\]
Then the following statements are equivalent:

1. \(Z_{m_n,r_n}\) converges in distribution.
2. \(\mathbb E Z_{m_n,r_n}\) converges to a finite limit.
3. Every \(a_{n,s}\) converges in the extended interval \([0,\infty]\).

When these conditions hold, at most one index \(s<q\) has a limit \(\lambda\in(0,\infty)\). At that critical index,
\[
Z_{m_n,r_n}\ \Longrightarrow\ B_{s+1}+s\,\mathrm{Binomial}\left(\binom qs,1-e^{-\lambda}\right).
\]
If there is no such index, the limiting distribution is a point mass at one of \(B_q,B_{q-1},\ldots,B_1\). In particular, for \(q=4\) at the support-size-two scale \(m_n/2^{r_n}\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)\),
\[
Z_{m_n,r_n}\ \Longrightarrow\ 16+2\,\mathrm{Binomial}(6,1-e^{-\lambda}).
\]
This identifies every limit law and every subsequential phase of this finite-algebra atom statistic.

## Assumptions and scope
The frame is a **finite disjoint union of \(m\geq1\) equivalence classes**, each having exactly \(r\geq1\) worlds. Accessibility is the full reflexive equivalence relation. Exactly \(k\) independent fair Boolean variables are sampled at every world; equivalently, the \(q=2^k\) joint propositional colours are IID uniform on \([q]\). The syntax allows propositional Boolean combinations and the non-contingency operator \(\Delta\), with arbitrary nesting; it has no counting quantifiers, extra accessibility relations, or public-announcement operators. The generated algebra has ground set the \(mr\) worlds and consists of truth sets definable in this syntax. The asymptotic criterion assumes \(q\) fixed and \(m_n,r_n\to\infty\), with **no** condition on their relative rate.

A support is the set of different propositional colours seen in a class, without recording their multiplicities. All empty-support terms are excluded since the classes are nonempty. Here \(\mathrm{Binomial}(d,p)\) is the number of successes among \(d\) independent Bernoulli \(p\) variables; \(\lambda=+\infty\) is interpreted only for extended intensity limits, not in the binomial expression.

## Proof
**1. Exact identification of atoms.** Let \(\chi_u\) be the ordinary propositional conjunction defining precisely colour \(u\in[q]\). At a world whose class has support \(S\), the truth of \(\Delta\chi_u\) is false exactly when \(|S|\geq2\) and \(u\in S\). The vector of all \(\chi_u\) and \(\Delta\chi_u\) therefore determines the ordered pair \((S,t)\), where \(t\in S\) is the world's own colour. Indeed, for \(|S|\geq2\) the false \(\Delta\chi_u\) coordinates recover \(S\); for \(|S|=1\) the local colour recovers \(S\). Conversely, induct on formulas: every Boolean connective preserves dependence only on \((S,t)\); applying \(\Delta\) to a truth set depending on these pairs produces a truth value constant on each class and determined only by \(S\), since it checks whether all its \((S,t)\), for \(t\in S\), have the same truth value. Thus all formulas are unions of the \((S,t)\) cells, and each such cell is distinguished by Boolean combinations of \(\chi_u\) and \(\Delta\chi_u\). Every realized pair yields one Boolean atom. There are \(|S|\) realized pairs for each occupied support \(S\), proving the exact atom formula. This structural reduction is used as a premise for the new distributional classification; a modal-depth-one support normal form alone is not asserted as new.

**2. Finite occupancy distribution.** For a fixed \(S\) of size \(s\), each world must choose one of its \(s\) colours and every colour must occur at least once. Inclusion–exclusion gives \(q^rp_{r,s}=\sum_{j=0}^s(-1)^{s-j}\binom{s}{j}j^r\). Different equivalence classes are independent. If \(\mathcal S\) is the set of all nonempty colour supports and \(\mathcal F\subseteq\mathcal S\), the probability that the *exact family of observed supports* is \(\mathcal F\) equals
\[
\Pr(\{S:I_S=1\}=\mathcal F)
=\sum_{\mathcal T\subseteq\mathcal F}(-1)^{|\mathcal F|-|\mathcal T|}\left(\sum_{S\in\mathcal T}p_{r,|S|}\right)^m.
\]
A sum over the empty \(\mathcal T\) is zero, and \(m\geq1\). Grouping by \(\sum_{S\in\mathcal F}|S|\) yields the full exact distribution; summing \(\Pr(I_S=1)=1-(1-p_{r,|S|})^m\) gives the stated expectation.

**3. Separated critical intensities.** For fixed \(s\), the inclusion–exclusion formula yields
\[
p_{r,s}=(s/q)^r\left(1+O(((s-1)/s)^r)\right),
\]
with the obvious exact reading \(p_{r,1}=q^{-r}\). In particular \(p_{r,q}\to1\). If \(s<t\), then
\[
\frac{a_{n,s}}{a_{n,t}}=(s/t)^{r_n}\to0.
\]
It follows that in any convergent extended intensity vector, all coordinates below a finite positive index are zero, and all coordinates above it are infinite. Two different coordinates cannot have finite positive limits.

**4. Joint Poisson counts and binomial atom law.** Fix \(s<q\) for which \(a_{n,s}\to\lambda\in(0,\infty)\). For each \(s\)-element support \(S\), let \(N_S\) count the classes with that exact support. These variables have a multinomial joint law. For every collection of nonnegative fixed integers \(b_S\), their mixed falling-factorial moment is
\[
\mathbb E\prod_{|S|=s}(N_S)_{b_S}
=(m_n)_{\sum b_S}\prod_{|S|=s}p_{r_n,s}^{b_S}
\longrightarrow\lambda^{\sum b_S}.
\]
Hence the \(\binom qs\) counts converge jointly to independent \(\mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)\) counts, so the indicators \(1_{N_S>0}\) converge jointly to independent Bernoulli variables with success probability \(1-e^{-\lambda}\). Meanwhile, for supports of size \(t<s\), the expected number of observed such supports is at most \(\binom qt m_np_{r_n,t}\to0\); for supports \(t>s\), \(m_np_{r_n,t}\to\infty\) and the probability any named type is absent is at most \(e^{-m_np_{r_n,t}}\to0\). All larger supports are present and all smaller absent with probability tending to one. The exact atom formula therefore gives the claimed shifted-scaled binomial law. The same elementary bounds give a constant \(B_j\) in each noncritical intensity regime.

**5. Necessary and sufficient convergence criterion.** Sufficiency follows from the preceding argument by partitioning into the cases of one finite positive critical intensity or a zero/infinite staircase. Conversely suppose \(\mathbb E Z_n\to L\). Each sequence \(a_{n,s}\) has subsequences converging in \([0,\infty]\) (compactification by \(x\mapsto x/(1+x)\)), and finitely many simultaneous coordinates admit convergent subsubsequences. A staircase sublimit produces exactly the expected value
\[
F_s(\lambda)=B_{s+1}+s\binom qs(1-e^{-\lambda}),
\]
for \(0\leq\lambda\leq\infty\), or the corresponding plateau \(B_j\). The open intervals \((B_{s+1},B_s)\) are pairwise disjoint; their endpoints describe precisely the same zero/infinite staircase vector from adjacent critical labels. Hence two distinct extended intensity vectors cannot produce the same limiting expectation. Since every simultaneous subsequential intensity limit must produce \(L\), the intensity vector has one unique subsequential limit and therefore converges. (This use of the expectation is legitimate because \(Z_n\) is uniformly bounded by \(q2^{q-1}\), the total number of possible \((S,t)\) pairs.) If instead the distribution converges, this boundedness already forces convergence of its expectation. Thus all three conditions are equivalent.

## Verification
A standalone standard-library Python checker enumerates every coloured valuation for tested small \((q,r,m)\), computes all modal signatures from propositional singletons and their non-contingency images, exhaustively confirms that every union of signature cells remains a union after applying \(\Delta\), and compares the resulting exact atom-count law with an independently computed rational occupancy dynamic program. It additionally checks the expectation identity. Its output was `ALL_CHECKS_OK exhaustive_assignments= 9502` for \(q\in\{2,4\}\) and the specified small sizes. These finite checks do **not** establish the infinite limiting theorem, which is proved by the preceding moment and subsequence arguments. Run `python3 checker.py`; only Python standard-library modules are required.

## Relationship to prior work
Sierra Miranda, Wang, and Zenger develop proof theory for non-contingency logic over generalized path conditions, including S5; this supplies motivation but does not give finite random-model atom-count distributions. Fan, Wang, and van Ditmarsch establish the expressive relationship between knowing-whether logic and normal modal logic on reflexive frames; therefore the support-based normal form, the modal expressive translation, and the finite maximum atom count are **not** claimed as novel in themselves. Zantedeschi studies modal exchangeability and de Finetti representations on S4 and S5 frames, addressing distributions of valuations under modal symmetries but not the finite uniform independent colouring model's support-occupancy threshold law. Classical independent-box occupancy and Poisson approximation are ingredients, not novel assertions. The asserted contribution is the combined **exact algebra atom-count distribution, complete multi-threshold limiting laws, and expectation-equivalent convergence criterion for the specified model**.

## Limitations
The theorem assumes exactly equal class sizes, independent uniform colour vectors, fixed \(k\), and a single S5 relation. Unequal classes, nonuniform or correlated colours, multiple agents, and \(q\) growing with the model size are not covered. Poisson limits do not give error rates in the general statement. The proof establishes convergence in distribution, not almost-sure convergence of differently sampled models. An equivalent statement may already be implicit in older S5 support normal forms combined with occupancy literature; the precise priority has not been independently certified.
