# Near-information-theoretic online shattering of quadratic-residue translates

## Finding
For every odd prime power \(q\ge 3\), let \(Q\subset\mathbb F_q\) denote the set of **nonzero squares** and let
\[
\mathcal H_q=\bigl\{h_c:\mathbb F_q\to\{0,1\}:h_c(x)=\mathbf1[x-c\in Q],\ c\in\mathbb F_q\bigr\}.
\]
The zero difference is declared negative. For every integer \(d\ge0\), put
\[
T_d=8\cdot 2^d-7\cdot 2^{d/2}.
\]
Then the following **explicit finite-size sufficient condition** holds:
\[
\boxed{q\ge T_d\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal H_q)\ge d.}
\]
In particular, for all odd prime powers \(q\),
\[
\boxed{\max\{0,\lfloor\log_2q\rfloor-3\}
\le \operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal H_q)
\le\lfloor\log_2q\rfloor.}
\]
Thus these finite-field translation classes have online shattering depth \(\log_2 q+O(1)\), uniformly even when \(q\) runs through nonprime finite-field orders. The statement is about *adaptive* shattering, not the ordinary VC dimension; no near-maximal VC-dimension theorem is asserted.

## Assumptions and scope
The field order \(q\) is an odd prime power, the domain and center set are both the entire field, and \(h_c(x)\) is one precisely when \(x-c\) is a nonzero square. The Littlestone dimension is the maximum depth of a complete binary tree whose internal nodes are points of \(\mathbb F_q\), with every root-to-leaf sequence of point labels and Boolean answers realizable by some \(h_c\). All logarithms in the result have base two. The bound involves all \(q\) hypotheses, including every center, but requires no distinctness assumption beyond their explicit definition.

## Proof
**1. An exact quadratic-character moment identity.** Extend the quadratic multiplicative character \(\chi\) by \(\chi(0)=0\). For a current nonempty version space of centers \(A\subseteq\mathbb F_q\) of cardinality \(m\), define
\[
X_A(x)=\sum_{c\in A}\chi(x-c).
\]
For equal centers \(a=b\), the sum of \(\chi(x-a)^2\) over \(x\) is \(q-1\). For distinct centers \(a\ne b\),
\[
\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_q}\chi((x-a)(x-b))=-1.
\]
To verify the second equality directly, for \(x\ne b\) set \(t=(x-a)/(x-b)\). This substitution is a bijection from \(\mathbb F_q\setminus\{b\}\) onto \(\mathbb F_q\setminus\{1\}\); since \(x-b\ne0\), the summand equals \(\chi(t)\). Its sum is \(-\chi(1)=-1\), while the omitted \(x=b\) contributes zero. Expanding the square gives the exact identity
\[
\boxed{\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_q}X_A(x)^2=m(q-m).}
\]
In particular, there exists \(x\) with \(|X_A(x)|\le\sqrt{m(q-m)/q}\le\sqrt m\).

**2. One almost-balanced adaptive query.** Among the \(m\) centers in \(A\), let \(N_A(x)\) be the number answering one to query \(x\). The convention that zero is not a square gives the *exact* identity
\[
2N_A(x)=m+X_A(x)-\mathbf1[x\in A].
\]
For the point \(x\) selected above, both successor version spaces have cardinality at least
\[
\boxed{\min\{N_A(x),m-N_A(x)\}\ge (m-\sqrt m-1)/2.}
\]
This statement applies to **every** nonempty subset \(A\), including arbitrary version spaces generated by previous adaptive answers, not only to translates or affine subsets.

**3. A uniform depth threshold.** The sequence \(T_d\) is increasing, with \(T_0=1\). We establish for each integer \(d\ge1\) that
\[
(T_d-\sqrt{T_d}-1)/2\ge T_{d-1}.
\]
Set \(z=2^{d/2}\), so that \(T_d=8z^2-7z\) and
\[
T_d-2T_{d-1}=7(\sqrt2-1)z.
\]
Concavity of the square root supplies
\[
\sqrt{8z^2-7z}\le2\sqrt2\,z-\frac7{4\sqrt2}.
\]
Since \(5\sqrt2-7>0\) and \(7/(4\sqrt2)>1\),
\[
7(\sqrt2-1)z-\sqrt{T_d}-1
\ge(5\sqrt2-7)z+\frac7{4\sqrt2}-1>0.
\]
This proves the threshold inequality. The function \(m\mapsto(m-\sqrt m-1)/2\) increases for real \(m\ge1\). Therefore any version space of size at least \(T_d\) admits a query with both children of size at least \(T_{d-1}\). By induction, every such version space shatters a complete decision tree of depth \(d\). Taking the initial version space of cardinality \(q\) gives the first claim.

**4. Quantitative corollary and upper bound.** Because \(T_d\le8\cdot2^d\), for \(d=\lfloor\log_2 q\rfloor-3\ge0\) we have \(q\ge T_d\). When the right-hand side is negative, the trivial depth-zero lower bound applies. A depth-\(d\) complete tree needs at least \(2^d\) distinct terminal concepts, while the definition has at most \(q\) concepts. Hence \(\operatorname{Ldim}(\mathcal H_q)\le\lfloor\log_2q\rfloor\), proving the stated three-level additive window.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone Python checker constructs nonzero-square translates over every odd prime at most \(131\) and over the quadratic extensions of orders \(9,25,49\). It computes the maximum possible Littlestone depth independently via exact version-space recursion, in these sampled orders obtaining \(\lfloor\log_2q\rfloor\). It separately checks the character second-moment identity by exhaustive subsets for primes through \(11\) and deterministic samples for larger primes, and checks the threshold inequality numerically through depth \(50\). Run `python3 check_paley.py`; `check_output.txt` gives a sample successful execution. The infinite theorem is proved above and does not depend on this finite census.

## Relationship to prior work
McDonald, Sahay and Wyman (first public preprint 2022-10-07) study **ordinary VC dimension** for the same family of nonzero quadratic-residue translates, its Paley graph representation, and its multiplicative-subgroup analogues. Their Theorem 1.4 gives a lower bound approaching one half of \(\log_2q\) for VC dimension and their experiments often see near-maximal VC dimensions at small primes. This work instead establishes a uniform **additive** three-level deficit bound for *adaptive Littlestone* shattering. A bound on VC dimension of roughly half the information bound does not imply the present adaptive near-saturation. Their use of multiplicative character bounds is acknowledged; the specific exact second-moment recursion above is applied to online version spaces rather than to fixed-support traces.

Guingona, Kolesnikov, Nierwinski and Soucy (first public preprint 2021-09-10) determine equal VC and Littlestone dimensions for zero sets of all nontrivial linear combinations of finitely many independent functions. Those hypotheses and their fixed-dimensional linear span are different from the \(q\) translated quadratic-residue indicators here, so their theorem does not imply this quantitative result. General counting bounds are used as the upper bound and are not claimed as new.

## Limitations
The constant three is a proven upper bound on the **additive deficiency**, not claimed optimal. Computations for small field orders suggest exact saturation of the counting bound but give no proof of that conjectural strengthening. The result does not improve published VC-dimension bounds or solve their open conjecture. It covers all odd prime powers, not characteristic-two fields, other multiplicative subgroups, or restrictions to arbitrary query/center subsets. The priority comparison includes relevant accessible literature and published research records, not a proof of global historical uniqueness. Independent external validation has not been performed.
