# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** The argument proves every two-dimensional proper subalgebra in odd characteristic is a rational Borel, verifies the exact eigenline incidence for each nonzero quadratic type, and applies inclusion–exclusion to the one or two Borels. Characteristic two is handled independently through the nondegenerate central commutator pairing on the two-dimensional quotient. Direct exhaustive finite-field generation checks cover the stated small cases, including \(\mathbb F_4\). No inference from finite experiments is used in the universal proof.

**Originality — PASS, with qualification.** The principal theorem is the exact **nilpotent-sampling invariance** across all finite fields, all fixed seeds, and all tuple lengths, together with the zero probability of nilpotent-generated dimension-two subalgebras, rather than the basic Borel counts alone. The conditional formulas are part of the stronger comparison.  The closest accessible contemporary work establishes asymptotic random generation in arbitrary rank, existence of generating pairs, random-bracket mixing, and the total number of three-dimensional Lie-subalgebra planes. The present claim instead calculates the full **conditional** distribution for a prescribed first generator, distinguishing three determinant types in odd characteristic and centrality in characteristic two. A total plane count alone does not imply these incidence-stratified probabilities. Exact literature searches and comparable published-finding corpus records did not reveal the complete same statement. Full historical priority remains unverified: a highly relevant older classification or \(3/2\)-generation theorem could imply some parts, and some full texts were not available to inspect.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Replacing a uniform three-dimensional sampling space by a quadratic two-dimensional nilpotent cone preserves exactly the chance of full generation, while forcing all intermediate two-dimensional outcomes to disappear. This exact universal cancellation is a natural, nonobvious strengthening of the random-generation problem.  Conditional generation is the quantitative refinement of fixed-element \(3/2\)-generation; it measures how many independently sampled auxiliary elements a specified seed needs. Sharp distinctions among nilpotent, split, and nonsplit seeds, together with the characteristic-two central obstruction, yield an all-finite-field classification and exact expected waiting times. This goes beyond repeating the known unconditioned probability or a plane count.
