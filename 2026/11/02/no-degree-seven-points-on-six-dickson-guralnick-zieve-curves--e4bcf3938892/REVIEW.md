# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem has exact quantifiers: q is one of six explicit prime powers, the object is the smooth projective DGZ normalization, and the field extension has degree seven. The geometric premises are sourced to Giulietti–Korchmáros–Timpanella, especially the two short-orbit theorem, N1=0, and genus. Any seventh-extension point must lie in a free PGL3(q)-orbit. If that orbit were Frobenius-stable, Frobenius would act by right translation by an element whose seventh power is the identity; seven is coprime to the group order, so this would give an Fq-point. Hence seven *disjoint* full orbits must exist. Exact integer inequalities show that Hasse–Weil prohibits that for each q. The optional finite-field enumeration in q=3 is a check only; no inference from finite testing is used for the general six-field theorem.

Risk: correctness depends on the cited 2018 nonstandard classification of exactly two short orbits, whose stated hypotheses q≥3 and proof were inspected. It is not an independent reproof of that published theorem.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge assessment)

The 2018 DGZ paper counts extension degrees one through three; the 2022 full-text Section 7 counts four through six. Their methods and exact counts are acknowledged, not claimed anew. Their numeric data do not entail seventh-extension vanishing. Crucially, for every q in the theorem a single long group orbit has size strictly below the Hasse–Weil upper bound; the additional Frobenius-stable-orbit obstruction provides the new factor seven. Focused published-finding corpus, public literature and historical-claim searches did not identify a stronger DGZ statement or the six-field degree-seven table.

Risk: equivalent seventh-degree counts might occur in unindexed follow-up work; the published orbit and Hasse estimates are established techniques, and priority has not been independently certified. A search failure is not a proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

These are exact point-free extension fields for a natural high-automorphism algebraic curve, and degree seven is the first uncomputed extension beyond the published uniform N1–N6 results. The obstruction is structural and explains why an otherwise ineffective automorphism-orbit/Hasse comparison suddenly becomes decisive when the extension degree is coprime to the group order. The six fields comprise every prime power at most 47 with the required order-six residue class modulo seven, a natural finite cutoff tied to the inequality, rather than an arbitrary parameter slice. Such zero-point data constrain the Hasse–Weil zeta function and closed-point spectrum of these curves.

Risk: the argument does not handle other fields, provide a complete zeta function, or imply the absence of closed points of other degrees. No larger theorem is claimed.
