# Non-counting three-variable logic sees exactly the support of distance-intersection numbers

## Finding
Let \(G\) and \(H\) be finite, nonempty, simple, connected distance-regular graphs. Let \(D_G,D_H\) denote their graph diameters. For each graph \(X\) and \(0\le h,i,j\le D_X\), let \(p^{h}_{ij}(X)\) be the number of vertices \(z\) with \(d(x,z)=i\) and \(d(y,z)=j\), for any ordered pair \((x,y)\) satisfying \(d(x,y)=h\). Then the following are equivalent:

1. \(G\) and \(H\) satisfy exactly the same first-order sentences using at most three variable symbols, with equality and undirected adjacency only, with no counting quantifiers and no bound on quantifier nesting.
2. \(D_G=D_H=D\) and, for every \(0\le h,i,j\le D\), \(p^h_{ij}(G)>0\) if and only if \(p^h_{ij}(H)>0\).

Thus the zero/nonzero pattern of the full distance-intersection table is a complete invariant of ordinary \(\mathrm{FO}^{3}\)-equivalence on connected distance-regular graphs; multiplicities of the positive entries are invisible. In particular, for Hamming graphs \(H(d,q)\), where \(d\ge1\) and \(q\ge2\), two such graphs agree in all \(\mathrm{FO}^{3}\) sentences exactly when their dimensions are equal and either both alphabets have size two or both have size at least three.

## Assumptions and scope
The graphs are finite, nonempty, simple, connected and distance-regular, including complete graphs of diameter one and the singleton graph of diameter zero. Distance regularity means that the number \(p^h_{ij}(X)\) is well-defined independently of the particular ordered pair at distance \(h\). The first-order signature contains only adjacency and equality. The logical fragment permits reuse of three variable symbols arbitrarily many times but no counting, colours, constants or named distance predicates. The distance \(d(x,y)\) is ordinary shortest-path distance.

## Proof
**Distance is definable with three variables.** Working with three variable symbols, one may recursively express the existence of a walk of length at most \(r\) between two free vertices. Define \(\delta_0(x,y)\) to mean \(x=y\), and recursively define \(\delta_{r+1}(x,y)\) as \(\delta_r(x,y)\lor\exists z\,[E(x,z)\land\delta_r(z,y)]\). At each recursive expansion rename bound variables so that only the third of the symbols \(x,y,z\) is quantified and the free endpoint symbols are retained. This is legitimate even though the variable symbols reused in inner subformulas may shadow outer bindings. Therefore exact distance \(r\) is expressed by \(\delta_r(x,y)\land\neg\delta_{r-1}(x,y)\) when \(r>0\); equality expresses distance zero. These formulas can be large, but finite variable width, not formula length or depth, is at issue.

**Necessity.** If the diameters differ, the sentence asserting existence of two vertices at exact distance \(r\), for the larger diameter \(r\), distinguishes the graphs. If the diameters agree but some support bit \(p^h_{ij}>0\) differs, the sentence asserting existence of vertices \(x,y,z\) realizing the three distances \(h,i,j\) distinguishes them. Every distance comparison in that sentence is expressed with the same three variable names, reusing a temporarily unneeded name within its subformula. Hence identical \(\mathrm{FO}^{3}\) theories force equality of all support bits.

**Sufficiency.** Play the ordinary, non-counting, three-pebble Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game for an arbitrary finite number of rounds on \(G,H\). Duplicator maintains the invariant that the graph distances between every two currently pebbled vertices agree across the structures, including zero distance for coincident vertices. Initially the invariant is vacuous. When Spoiler moves a pebble, at most two other pebbles remain. If none remains, Duplicator chooses any vertex. If one remains at \(x\), and the selected new vertex lies at distance \(i\) from \(x\), distance-regularity (equivalently uniform distance-sphere sizes) and connectedness give a vertex at distance \(i\) from the corresponding vertex on the other graph: every distance \(0,\ldots,D\) occurs about every vertex. With two retained vertices at distance \(h\), let the new vertex have distances \(i,j\) from them. The number of corresponding choices is \(p^h_{ij}\), positive by the actual move; equal support bits guarantee at least one corresponding choice in the other graph. This preserves all three pairwise distances. Since adjacency is exactly distance one and equality is exactly distance zero, the final pebbled correspondence is a partial isomorphism. Duplicator wins for every finite game length, and the three-pebble game/finite-variable correspondence proves identical \(\mathrm{FO}^{3}\) theories. No cardinality equality is required.

**Hamming specialization.** A vertex of \(H(d,q)\) is a word of length \(d\) over a \(q\)-letter alphabet, with edges between words differing in exactly one coordinate. Choose reference words \(x,y\) at distance \(h\). Among their \(h\) disagreeing coordinates, a third word \(z\) agrees with \(x\) in \(a\) positions, agrees with \(y\) in \(b\), and differs from both in \(c\), with \(a+b+c=h\). Among the \(d-h\) agreeing positions, let \(e\) positions differ from both. The resulting distance pair is \((d(x,z),d(y,z))=(b+c+e,a+c+e)\). All nonnegative choices of \(a,b,c,e\) with \(0\le e\le d-h\) occur if \(q\ge3\); for \(q=2\) precisely those with \(c=0\) occur. Hence the support pattern depends exactly on \(d\) and the predicate \(q=2\). Different dimensions are separated by definable diameter; at equal dimension, binary and nonbinary alphabets are separated by existence of a triangle. This proves the stated Hamming equivalence criterion.

## Verification
The included standalone program `verify.py` exhaustively computed all ordered-pair distance-extension supports for Hamming graphs in dimensions one through three with alphabet sizes two, three and four. It separately enumerated the coordinate-type formula and compared the two support sets for every distance and ordered pair. The two computations agreed throughout; the ternary and quaternary supports coincided in every tested dimension, and differed from the binary supports. The program also checked pair-distance-uniformity on cycles of lengths five through eight, as a second graph family. Run `python verify.py` from a directory containing the embedded program. The exact captured stdout is in `verification_output.txt`; it ends `ALL_CHECKS_PASS`. These finite checks corroborate, but do not substitute for, the general argument.

## Relationship to prior work
The standard definitions and numerical intersection coefficients \(p^h_{ij}\) occur in van Dam, Koolen and Tanaka's survey of distance-regular graphs, especially Sections 2.1 and 2.6. The unrestricted three-pebble game and its relation to three-variable graph logic are explained by Pikhurko and Verbitsky in their survey, Section 3. The present statement connects their two frameworks by identifying precisely which **Boolean support** information, rather than multiplicities, determines the full three-variable theory, and proves both directions. Earlier results on strongly regular graphs (the diameter-two subclass) and on bounded-quantifier-rank Hamming graphs do not by themselves give the arbitrary-diameter and unrestricted-depth equivalence criterion, although their specialized extension arguments are closely related. A broad, possibly already known result for relation algebras or coherent configurations could imply the present characterization; exhaustive priority verification has not been performed.

## Limitations
The criterion is restricted to connected distance-regular graphs; uniformity of extension counts is essential. The logical equivalence is not an isomorphism claim and does not extend to counting logic, four-variable logic or full first-order logic. The Boolean support pattern need not determine the exact intersection array, graph order, spectrum or automorphism group. Computational verification covers only the finite examples listed and supplies no infinite certificate. Independent mathematical review and comprehensive historical-priority verification remain outstanding.
