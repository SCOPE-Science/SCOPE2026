# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS
The stated claim concerns every odd repetition r at least five on the exact three-letter alphabet. The proof first establishes all nine adjacent pairs, extends the three-letter weighted-factor recurrence with the explicit nonnegative transition inequality, obtains exact minimum/maximum factors, and proves the modular parity obstruction from the irrational argument of r+exp(2πi/3). Finite checks independently enumerate all boundary pairs, confirm the recurrence at selected parameters, and verify an exact parity/sign identity. Critical limits: the finite enumeration does not prove the infinite theorem; the symbolic proof does.

## Originality: PASS, with residual risk
The strongest source, Allouche et al. (arXiv:2610.03837v1), proves only the r=3 case and raises the other-r question in Remark 4. Its general three-symbol proof idea is openly credited. The result here supplies all odd r at least five, changing both substitution length and spectral parameter. Chen–Wen's generalized Thue–Morse results treat different substitutions with eventually periodic abelian complexity. Research-record and public literature searches yielded no covering all-odd-repetition classification, but indexing is not exhaustive; earlier uncatalogued generalizations remain a priority risk. The full October source was inspected, whereas the Chen–Wen source was compared through its arXiv abstract and secondary bibliographic account, leaving some literature risk.

## Value: PASS
The 2026 source explicitly asks what happens for other repetition counts. An infinite family of automatic words with nonregular abelian complexity, not only the earlier isolated base-four example, answers a coherent part of that question. The exact subsequence formula and parity obstruction distinguish the repeated-block mechanism from generalized Thue–Morse families with ultimately periodic complexity. This is not a mere numerical extension.
