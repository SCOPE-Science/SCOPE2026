For every odd repetition \(r\ge5\), the cyclic ternary substitution \(a\mapsto a^r(a+1\bmod3)\), followed by marking a single letter, gives an \((r+1)\)-automatic binary word whose abelian complexity is not \((r+1)\)-regular. An exact formula at lengths \((r+1)^m\) and an irrational-rotation parity obstruction explain this infinite family beyond the previously established \(r=3\) example.
