At the Erdős–Rényi connectivity threshold, average all-terminal reliability multiplied by \(\log n\) has a two-atom limit: zero on disconnected networks and the explicit value \(e^{e^{-x}}E_1(e^{-x})\) on connected ones. The entire near-unit-retention reliability curve becomes asymptotically deterministic once the presence of isolated vertices is known, so the unconditioned reliability remains genuinely non-self-averaging.
