# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The claimed row-dependent flip probabilities satisfy \(Np_{N,\lfloor Nt\rfloor}\to c e^{-\beta}/t\) locally uniformly away from the origin. Independent Bernoulli Laplace functionals converge to the Poisson process with this intensity because the sum of squared probabilities vanishes. The fair initial sign makes the sign at the beginning of any positive-time subinterval fair and independent of subsequent flips. Local path convergence, integration, and the uniformly Lipschitz near-origin truncation prove the functional limit. The stationary logarithmic-time generator yields exactly the symmetric Beta moments; boundedness transfers second and fourth moments. The two-time covariance follows by integration of the exact parity-Poisson correlation. The finite checker separately confirms the exact moment recurrence and provides numerical checks; it is not treated as proof of weak convergence.

## Originality — PASS, with priority qualification

Pradhan, Roy and Ray (arXiv:2605.20116, main equations (28)–(32), supplement SVI) establish the transition, logarithmic scaling variable and a small-parameter variance expression, but do not state the nonlinear fixed-window **functional** law or the exact Binder curve. Engländer–Volkov–Wang (arXiv:1904.10953, Theorem 4 and Remark 15) establish Poisson zigzag convergence for a single fixed critical sequence; the classical Beta distribution is not new. The source on critical Markov switching (arXiv:2610.02609, Proposition 3.5) establishes a related Beta law for a different fixed-parameter system. The present triangular-array regime requires a separate limiting argument and evaluates the nonlinear dependence on the finite exponent-window parameter. No identical complete result was found in the compared mathematical records. A more general unpublished or unlocated triangular-array invariance theorem could subsume the method, and independent priority review remains outstanding.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The 2026 persistent-walk study directly motivates finite observation horizons near the exponent-one transition, at scale \((\alpha-1)\log N\). A first-order expansion in \(\alpha-1\) is not uniform when this product has a nonzero limit. The result resolves that precise finite-size window, determines the entire displacement law and path correlations, and supplies an explicit nonperturbative Binder scaling curve. It connects the observed numerical collapse with the previously known critical zigzag laws without claiming the latter as discoveries.

## Limits and closest literature

The proof assumes independent one-dimensional flips and a fair starting direction. It does not cover unbounded window parameters, rate estimates or correlated environments. The closest primary sources are Pradhan–Roy–Ray (2026), Engländer–Volkov (2016/2018), Engländer–Volkov–Wang (2019/2020), and arXiv:2610.02609 (2026). The novelty claim excludes all fixed-critical zigzag and Beta theorems.
