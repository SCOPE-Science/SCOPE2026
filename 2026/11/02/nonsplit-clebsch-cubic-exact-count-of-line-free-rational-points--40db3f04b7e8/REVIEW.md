# Review

**Correctness — PASS.** For \(q\equiv\pm2\pmod5\) in odd characteristic at least seven, the explicit Fourier lines have skew conjugates and therefore no rational points. The fixed rational line classes give Picard trace five: their intersection Gram matrix has rank six, while the action is nontrivial on the remaining one-dimensional quotient. The explicit fifteen-line intersections consist of ten triple and fifteen double points, giving exactly \(15q-20\) line-covered rational points. Subtraction from the Picard-trace point count proves \((q-3)(q-7)\). Exact finite-field enumeration and the 15-line Gram rank were checked with the included program; the general prime-power claim does not depend on enumerating finitely many fields.

**Originality — PASS (historical caveat).** The geometry of Clebsch's lines and general finite-field point-count formula are prior work. Hirschfeld's accessible 1981 summary specifically assumes 27 rational lines, while Knecht's accessible full paper gives general Picard/Frobenius methods without this nonsplit Clebsch line-avoidance polynomial. The 2017 split-cubic classification over thirteen elements also does not treat the nonsplit case of this surface. published-finding corpus searches for the nonsplit polynomial, aliases and Frobenius trace returned no encompassing scientific claim. Older original work of Hirschfeld was not available in full, so the priority risk is explicitly retained.

**Value — PASS.** The exact number of rational points outside the exceptional-line locus is a natural arithmetic invariant in cubic-surface unirationality and rational-point constructions. Unlike the general surface point count, it requires arithmetic descent for six skew Frobenius pairs and the collision structure of the fifteen rational lines. It gives a complete infinite-field formula with the sharp small-field exception \(q=7\), rather than a one-field census or an arbitrary slice. This does not claim broader theorems about unirationality.
