# Nonvanishing critical fluctuation power in the stationary Pelikan random map

## Finding
For every \(1/2<p<1\), let \((X_n)_{n\geq0}\) be the stationary random interval map which independently applies \(x\mapsto2x\pmod1\) with probability \(p\), and \(x\mapsto x/2\) with probability \(q=1-p\). Set \(C_n(p)=\operatorname{Cov}(X_0,X_n)\). The covariance sequence is absolutely summable and its Green–Kubo (partial-sum) variance is exactly
\[
\sigma^2(p):=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}X_n\right)
=C_0(p)+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}C_n(p)
=\frac{-12p^4+50p^3-43p^2+14p-1}{(3p-1)^3(5p-1)}.
\]
In particular, \(\lim_{p\downarrow1/2}\sigma^2(p)=4\), even though the stationary distributions converge weakly to the Dirac mass at zero and
\[
C_0(p)=\frac{(2p-1)(6p^2-3p+1)}{3(3p-1)^2(5p-1)}\sim\frac{16}{9}\left(p-\frac12\right)\to0.
\]
Thus the ratio of long-run variance to one-time variance diverges sharply as
\[
\frac{\sigma^2(p)}{C_0(p)}\sim\frac{9}{4(p-1/2)}.
\]
At the deterministic endpoint \(p=1\), the continuous extension of the variance formula is \(1/4\), agreeing with the standard doubling map.

## Assumptions and scope
At each step the map selection is independent of all earlier states and selections. At dyadic rationals, choose any consistent binary convention; these points have zero probability under the stationary law considered. The stationary law is the absolutely continuous stationary probability for \(p>1/2\). No stationary finite-density law is assumed at \(p=1/2\), and the limiting Dirac law must not be confused with the limit of long-time variances. The coefficient \(\sigma^2(p)\) is proved as a variance limit, without asserting a central limit theorem.

Put \(r=q/p\), \(v_j=2^{-j}\), and \(\pi_j=(1-r)r^j\) on nonnegative integers. Let \(U\) be independent uniform on \([0,1)\) and let \(J\) have distribution \(\pi\). Then \(X=2^{-J}U\) has the stationary law. Indeed, doubling decrements \(J\) when \(J>0\), whereas at \(J=0\) it leaves \(J\) unchanged and shifts the uniform binary digits of \(U\). Halving increments \(J\). The resulting reflected birth–death chain has stationary probabilities \(\pi_j\), and uniform binary tails remain independent of its state.

## Proof
Let \(K_n\) count doubling steps made at level \(J=0\) before time \(n\). Then pathwise
\[
X_n=2^{-J_n}\{2^{K_n}U\},\qquad
X_0=2^{-J_0}U.
\]
The exact dyadic identity
\(\mathbb E_U[U\{2^kU\}]=1/4+2^{-k}/12\) for integers \(k\geq0\) follows by expanding the independent fair binary digits (or by integrating over dyadic subintervals). Consequently
\[
C_n=\frac14\bigl(\mathbb E[v_{J_0}v_{J_n}]-m^2\bigr)
+\frac1{12}\mathbb E[v_{J_0}v_{J_n}2^{-K_n}],\qquad
m=\mathbb E_\pi v_J=\frac{1-r}{1-r/2}.
\]

On functions of \(j\geq0\), define \(P_b\), for \(b=1\) or \(b=1/2\), by
\[
(P_bf)_0=pb f_0+q f_1,\qquad
(P_bf)_j=p f_{j-1}+q f_{j+1}\quad(j\geq1).
\]
Then \(P_1\) is the ordinary reflected-chain operator and \(P_{1/2}\) weights each self-loop at zero by \(1/2\), precisely recording \(2^{-K_n}\). Therefore
\[
C_n=\frac14\langle v-m,P_1^n(v-m)\rangle_\pi
+\frac1{12}\langle v,P_{1/2}^n v\rangle_\pi.
\]
Here \(\langle f,g\rangle_\pi=\sum_j\pi_j f_jg_j\).

Write \(d=2p-1>0\), \(a=-2/(3p-1)\), and \(s=\mathbb E_\pi(v_J^2)=(1-r)/(1-r/4)\). The following explicit Poisson solutions can be checked separately on \(j\geq1\) and at \(j=0\):
\[
(I-P_1)g=v-m,\quad g_j=a2^{-j}-\frac{m}{d}j;
\qquad
(I-P_{1/2})u=v,\quad u_j=a(2^{-j}-3).
\]
The first solution is in \(L^2(\pi)\) because \(r<1\), while the second is bounded. For justification of summing the Poisson series, conjugate the two operators by multiplication by \(\sqrt{\pi_j}\). Both become symmetric half-line Jacobi matrices with off-diagonal entries \(\sqrt{pq}\) and a single boundary diagonal entry \(p\) or \(p/2\). Their essential spectral interval is \([-2\sqrt{pq},2\sqrt{pq}]\). For \(P_1\) the only eigenvalue outside the interval at the upper end is the simple eigenvalue \(1\), generated by constants; \(P_{1/2}\) has no eigenvalue of absolute value \(1\). Since \(2\sqrt{pq}<1\), on the mean-zero subspace for \(P_1\), and on the full space for \(P_{1/2}\), the iterates decay geometrically in \(L^2(\pi)\). This also proves absolute covariance summability.

Thus summing over \(n\geq0\) gives
\[
S:=\sum_{n\geq0}C_n
=\frac14\langle v-m,g\rangle_\pi
+\frac1{12}\langle v,u\rangle_\pi.
\]
The needed geometric moments are
\[
\mathbb E_\pi J=\frac r{1-r},\qquad
\mathbb E_\pi[Jv_J]=\frac{(1-r)r/2}{(1-r/2)^2},\quad
\mathbb E_\pi v_J=m,\quad \mathbb E_\pi(v_J^2)=s.
\]
Substitution, without any time truncation, yields
\[
S=\frac{51p^3-51p^2+17p-1}{3(3p-1)^3(5p-1)},
\qquad
C_0=\frac{s}{3}-\frac{m^2}{4}
=\frac{(2p-1)(6p^2-3p+1)}{3(3p-1)^2(5p-1)}.
\]
The Green–Kubo formula follows from \(\sigma^2=2S-C_0\). Rational substitution at \(p=1/2\) gives the finite limit \(4\), whereas \(r\to1\) makes \(J\) escape to infinity in probability and therefore \(2^{-J}U\to0\) in probability. The stated one-time variance asymptotic and ratio follow by Taylor expansion. Positivity throughout the parameter range follows also from the explicit numerator: in the variable \(t=2p-1\in[0,1]\), it has degree-four Bernstein coefficients \(3/4,17/16,47/24,17/4,8\), all strictly positive.

## Relationship to prior work
Yan, Majumdar, Ruffo, Sato, Beck and Klages (2023/2024) identify the invariant density and study time correlations for this map, obtaining exact expressions at short lags and approximations beyond them. Monthus (first posted 2024, expanded 2025), especially Sections VI.4–VI.5 and Eq. (201), derives an exact all-time transform of the **uncentered correlation from a uniform initial distribution** using a tilted reflected walk. The representation by a birth–death chain and the tilted operator is therefore not new. The present claim concerns the **stationary centered** two-time process, its explicitly summed Green–Kubo variance and its finite, nonzero critical limit despite collapse of its one-time variance; those assertions are not stated by either inspected source. Monthus's general propagator can serve as an alternate starting point, making detailed historical priority comparison especially important.

## Limitations
The conclusions refer only to the stationary probability law for \(p>1/2\), the coordinate observable, and independent switching. The limit \(4\) is not the variance of a process started from the critical Dirac state. The proof does not establish any central limit theorem or quantify finite-time crossover uniformly as \(p\downarrow1/2\). Priority risk persists because the general propagators already available in Monthus may facilitate an unpublished or implicit equivalent stationary calculation. No independent scientific audit has been performed.
