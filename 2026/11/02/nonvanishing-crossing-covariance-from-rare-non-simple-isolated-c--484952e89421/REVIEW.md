# Scientific review: non-simple isolated chords and crossings

**Correctness — PASS.** A specified isolated chord forces two independently uniformly paired complementary arcs. The conditional crossing count is the sum of the two inside-arc crossing counts, yielding the exact deficit \(-[(n-1)+s(n-1-s)]/3\); counting candidate chords with a separate antipodal case proves the full covariance identity. The ratio \(r_{m,s+1}/r_{m,s}=(2s+1)/(2(m-s)-1)\) controls the entire positive-gap tail and establishes the nonvanishing limit. Exhaustive rational enumeration over \(146{,}598\) matchings through seven chords and exact checks through 150 chords support, but do not replace, the proof. No hypotheses or edge cases were omitted.

**Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk.** Paguyo (arXiv:2104.01134), read in full accessible text, treats crossing and simple-chord marginals; its fixed-length remarks do not identify isolated non-simple arcs or the mixed covariance. Acan (arXiv:1501.01489), inspected across its random-intersection-graph degree and component sections, treats isolated vertices and chord lengths without establishing this joint statistic. Flajolet–Noy (2000) was inspected in its full publicly accessible INRIA research-report version: Section 1 treats component moments and Section 2 crossing moments separately. It does not assert a joint covariance for the isolation-conditioned positive-gap classes, although its framework might allow an implicit derivation. Earlier exact simple-chord covariance calculations already cover \(s=0\), which is specifically excluded from the novelty claim. Semantic searches for chord gaps, crossings, isolated degrees, and equivalent covariance formulations returned no directly dominating public theorem. A prior implicit generating-function computation remains a residual historical-priority risk.

**Value — PASS.** Isolated non-simple chords are a geometrically natural rare component class of random circle graphs. Their probability of occurring tends to zero while their *unscaled* crossing covariance converges to a nonzero constant. The explicit complete conditional deficit and asymptotic tail estimate identify and explain a meaningful failure of naive rare-event covariance heuristics, beyond a fixed finite computation or a special case of the simple-chord result. This result does not claim nonlinear conditional dependence or a new general matching technique.
