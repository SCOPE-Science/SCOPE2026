# Odd-degree arithmetic automorphism groups of the characteristic-two Klein cubic

## Finding
Let \(X/\mathbb F_2\) be the smooth cubic threefold in \(\mathbb P^4\) defined by
\[
F(x_0,\ldots,x_4)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z}x_i^2x_{i+1}=0.
\]
For every **positive odd** integer \(m\), the full group of biregular \(\mathbb F_{2^m}\)-automorphisms is
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb F_{2^m}}(X)=
\begin{cases}
\langle [x_0:x_1:x_2:x_3:x_4]\mapsto[x_1:x_2:x_3:x_4:x_0]\rangle\cong C_5,&5\nmid m,\\
\operatorname{Sp}_4(\mathbb F_2)\cong S_6,&5\mid m.
\end{cases}
\]
The two orders are \(5\) and \(720\), respectively. In particular, the first odd-degree extension over which the automorphism group grows is \(\mathbb F_{32}\). An explicit projective change of variables over \(\mathbb F_{32}\) identifies \(X\) with the Fermat cubic \(\sum_{i=0}^4 y_i^3=0\). The five known \(\mathbb F_2\)-lines on \(X\) form one regular orbit under its complete \(\mathbb F_2\)-automorphism group.

## Assumptions and scope
All varieties and automorphisms are defined over the displayed finite field; geometric automorphisms not defined over the field are **not** counted. The integer \(m\) is positive and odd. Indices are taken modulo five. The group is the full automorphism group of the abstract smooth projective threefold, not merely a subgroup selected from visible coordinate permutations. No claim is made for even extension degrees. In binary matrix notation, \(B^{[2]}\) and \(B^{[4]}\) mean entrywise squaring and fourth powers.

## Proof
**1. Smoothness and projective linearity.** The partial derivatives are \(\partial F/\partial x_i=x_{i-1}^2\). They do not vanish simultaneously at any projective point, so \(X\) is smooth. The Grothendieck–Lefschetz theorem gives \(\operatorname{Pic}(X_{\overline{\mathbb F}_2})\cong\mathbb Z\), generated by the hyperplane bundle; this standard fact for smooth cubic threefolds also appears in Furukawa [3, Lemma 1.4]. An automorphism preserves the ample generator and acts on its five global sections; hence every biregular automorphism is induced by a unique projective linear transformation.

Let \(P\) be the five-by-five binary permutation matrix with \(P_{i,i+1}=1\) (cyclic indices), so \(F(x)=x^{[2]T}Px\). Equality of these cubic forms is equivalent to equality of their coefficient matrices: the monomials \(x_i^2x_j\), including those with \(i=j\), are linearly independent. For a projective automorphism represented by \(B\in\operatorname{GL}_5(\mathbb F_{2^m})\), there is \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_{2^m}^{\times}\) with
\[
B^{[2]T}PB=\lambda P.
\]
When \(m\) is odd, \(\gcd(3,2^m-1)=1\). Multiplying \(B\) by the **unique** scalar whose cube is \(\lambda^{-1}\) therefore gives a uniquely normalized matrix satisfying
\[
B^{[2]T}PB=P.\tag{1}
\]

**2. A forced field-of-definition bound.** Transposing (1) and applying entrywise squares gives \(B^{[2]T}P^T B^{[4]}=P^T\). Combining this with (1) yields
\[
B^{[4]}=(P^{-1}P^T)^{-1}B(P^{-1}P^T)=P^2BP^{-2},\tag{2}
\]
because \(P^T=P^{-1}\) and \(P^5=I\). Iterating (2) five times yields \(B^{[2^{10}]}=B\). Every entry of \(B\) therefore belongs to
\[
\mathbb F_{2^m}\cap\mathbb F_{2^{10}}=\mathbb F_{2^{\gcd(m,10)}}.
\]
For odd \(m\), the intersection is \(\mathbb F_2\) when \(5\nmid m\), and \(\mathbb F_{32}\) when \(5\mid m\). Thus it suffices to compute the two normalized isometry groups over those fields.

**3. The group over \(\mathbb F_2\).** Equation (1) becomes \(B^TPB=P\). The asymmetry operator \(A=P^{-1}P^T=P^{-2}\) of this bilinear form commutes with every such isometry. Since \(P=A^2\), every isometry commutes with \(P\), and hence obeys \(B^TB=I\). The cyclic permutation matrix has one cyclic vector, so its full binary matrix centralizer is
\[
\mathbb F_2[P]\cong\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^5-1)\cong\mathbb F_2\times\mathbb F_{16}.
\]
Here \(t^4+t^3+t^2+t+1\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb F_2\). An invertible centralizing matrix \(f(P)\) corresponds to \((1,a)\) with \(a\in\mathbb F_{16}^\times\). Orthogonality \(f(P)^Tf(P)=I\) is precisely \(a^{4}a=a^5=1\), since inversion of a primitive fifth root acts on \(\mathbb F_{16}\) by its fourth-power Frobenius. There are exactly five solutions, realized by \(I,P,P^2,P^3,P^4\). Thus \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb F_2}(X)\cong C_5\).

**4. An explicit Fermat model over \(\mathbb F_{32}\).** Take \(K=\mathbb F_2[\alpha]/(\alpha^5+\alpha^2+1)\), and let \(\beta=1+\alpha\). The five elements \(\beta,\beta^2,\beta^4,\beta^8,\beta^{16}\) form a self-dual normal basis of \(K/\mathbb F_2\). To remove any dependence on a normal-basis existence assertion, the provided exact-arithmetic certificate checks this explicitly. As binary polynomial encodings (least significant bit equals the constant coefficient), the five elements are \(3,5,17,12,26\). Define \(U\in\operatorname{GL}_5(K)\) by \(U_{ij}=\beta^{2^{i+j}}\), reducing the exponent index modulo five. Its first row is \((3,5,17,12,26)\), with subsequent rows obtained by successive cyclic shifts. Direct multiplication in \(K\) gives
\[
U^{[2]}=PU,\qquad U^TU=I.
\]
Therefore \(U^{[2]T}PU=I\) and the substitution \(x=Uy\) transforms \(F\) into \(\sum_i y_i^3\).

For any normalized isometry \(B\in\operatorname{GL}_5(K)\), set \(D=U^{-1}BU\). Equation (2), together with \(U^{[4]}=P^2U\), gives \(D^{[4]}=D\). Because the fourth-power fixed field inside \(K\) is \(\mathbb F_2\), the entries of \(D\) are binary. Under \(x=Uy\), equation (1) becomes \(D^{[2]T}D=I\), hence \(D^TD=I\). Conversely, every binary orthogonal \(D\) gives an isometry \(UDU^{-1}\) over \(K\). Thus the complete group over \(K\) is the nonalternating symmetric-bilinear isometry group \(O_5(\mathbb F_2)\).

Let \(u=(1,1,1,1,1)\). For the standard dot product, \(\langle v,v\rangle=\langle v,u\rangle\), so every orthogonal transformation fixes \(u\). Since \(\langle u,u\rangle=1\), the orthogonal complement \(u^\perp\) is a four-dimensional symplectic space; restriction gives an isomorphism
\[
O_5(\mathbb F_2)\cong\operatorname{Sp}_4(\mathbb F_2)\cong S_6,
\]
whose order is \(2^4(2^2-1)(2^4-1)=720\). Combining this with the field-of-definition bound proves the stated dichotomy for **every** odd \(m\).

**5. Rational lines.** Debarre–Laface–Roulleau [2, §4.5.2] identify exactly five \(\mathbb F_2\)-rational lines: the coordinate line spanned by the first and third coordinate vectors and its four nonidentity cyclic images. The proven full group \(C_5\) permutes them regularly.

## Relationship to prior work
Roulleau [1] studies the Klein threefold's complex automorphisms and Fano surface, including its characteristic-zero symmetry group. Debarre–Laface–Roulleau [2] give the characteristic-two zeta-function data and exactly five rational lines. Furukawa [3] classifies the characteristic-two Fermat cubic phenomenon over algebraically closed fields through Gauss maps of rank zero. Liendo–Vásquez [5] give a systematic 2026 treatment of finite-field forms of Fermat, Delsarte and Klein hypersurfaces, but their finite-field theory assumes odd characteristic and their automorphism-group hypotheses explicitly exclude \(p\mid d-1\); for this cubic \(p=2\) and \(d-1=2\). None of those statements alone determines how the full automorphism group **descends** to odd-degree finite extensions for the particular cyclic integral model. The result here is the two-case arithmetic group classification, with an explicit Fermat descent matrix; it does not claim novelty for the cubic equation, geometric Fermat equivalence in general, the classical geometric automorphism group, the rational-line census, or the standard orders of finite classical groups.

## Limitations
Even-degree extensions are not classified here; projective scalar normalization is different there. The result is about biregular automorphisms, not the birational selfmap group. The complete geometric automorphism group is not separately computed. Exact historical priority remains provisional because older literature on twists of Fermat cubics and finite classical groups may imply this particular descent classification without naming the Klein model. An independent expert audit has not been carried out.
