# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The Hermite basis is explicitly normalized. Exact Gaussian integrations give the symmetric \(2\times2\) Gram matrix, a strictly positive central spectral gap, and the universal positive center curvature \(4Le^{-L^2}/\sqrt\pi\). Its decay at infinity yields genuinely noncentral global maxima. Uniform Taylor expansion of the matrix, an explicit second-order simple-eigenvalue expansion, Gaussian tail control, and local strict concavity prove the exact small-width maximizer location and optimal concentration. The original standalone checker passed 100 high-precision deterministic checks, including independent quadrature. These tests supplement rather than prove the continuum theorem.

## Originality — PASS (self-review, subject to historical priority)
The 2018, 2021 and 2023 polyanalytic Fock literature supplies Hermite-matrix representations but not a statement implying the exact displaced-interval optimizer, positive central curvature at every width, or quartic narrow-window shift. The closely related 2022 coherent Hermite–Gaussian paper treats moments and propagation, not interval spectral optimization. Search comparisons include conceptual aliases (Gaussian-weighted linear polynomials, two-Hermite finite-band Slepian concentration, horizontal-strip spectral fibers), stronger rank-two or all-mode claims, and available research ledgers. No equivalent or dominating theorem was identified; failure to find one is not proof of absolute priority.

## Value — PASS
The leading two-Hermite concentration problem is the smallest band in which coherent mode mixing breaks the symmetry expected from pure Gaussian localization. The result answers whether symmetric windows are optimal for every width (they never are), calculates the exact \(2/\sqrt e\) narrow-window gain, and uncovers a nonobvious cancellation of the quadratic optimizer drift: the first displacement correction occurs at order four. The sharp spectral quantities are meaningful for finite Hermite-band concentration and the associated matrix fibers of polyanalytic localization; the result is not based merely on the fact that the Gram matrix is computable.

## Closest literature and limits
The three polyanalytic Toeplitz/Fock sources are representation results and do not display the optimizing-center law. Floettmann's beam superposition result treats a distinct invariant (beam moments). Residual risks include unlocated special-case spectral-optimization results in optical-engineering or concentration literature, lack of a global uniqueness classification at general widths, and absence of independent validation.
