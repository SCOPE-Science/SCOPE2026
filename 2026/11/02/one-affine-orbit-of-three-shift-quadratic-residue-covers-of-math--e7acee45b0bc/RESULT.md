# One affine orbit of three-shift quadratic-residue covers of \(\mathbb F_{25}\)

## Finding
Let \(K=\mathbb F_5[u]/(u^2-2)\), and let \(Q_0=\{z^2:z\in K\}\), a set of thirteen elements. Exactly **100** three-element subsets \(A\subset K\) satisfy
\[
A+Q_0=K.
\]
There is no cover by one or two translates. More precisely, for every unordered pair of distinct elements \(x,y\in K\), the number of further elements \(z\notin\{x,y\}\) for which \(\{x,y,z\}+Q_0=K\) is **two** if \(x-y\) is a nonsquare, and **zero** if it is a square. Thus all 100 three-element covering sets have three nonsquare pairwise differences.

All 100 covering sets lie in one orbit under the affine-square group \(z\mapsto sz+t\), where \(s\in (K^\times)^2\) and \(t\in K\). A representative is \(\{0,u,1+3u\}\); its stabilizer has order three, generated by \(z\mapsto (2+3u)z+u\).

## Assumptions and scope
The field is the unique field of order 25 up to isomorphism. The polynomial \(T^2-2\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb F_5\). All sums, differences, and products below are operations in \(K\), and covering means equality of sets, not a statement about the number of representations. Only **distinct** centers are counted.

For \(v=a+bu\), define its norm
\[
N(v)=a^2-2b^2\pmod5.
\]
For \(v\ne0\), it is a square in \(K\) exactly when \(N(v)\in\{1,4\}\). Indeed, \(N(v)=v^6\), so \(N(v)^2=v^{12}\) is the quadratic character of \(v\) in the cyclic group \(K^\times\) of order 24. In particular, \(u\) is a nonsquare because \(N(u)=3\), whereas \(-1\) is a square.

## Proof
Write \(D(B)=K\setminus(B+Q_0)\) for the uncovered set. Translation preserves quadratic-difference character, and multiplying all centers by a nonzero square preserves both the covering property and quadratic-difference character. Consequently every ordered pair of distinct centers can be normalized to \((0,1)\) if its difference is square, and to \((0,u)\) if its difference is nonsquare. The second normalization is valid because a quotient of two nonsquares is a square.

For each of these two normalized pairs, the remaining 23 choices of a distinct third center are exhaustively determined by the stated norm criterion. The resulting complete cardinality census is:

| Initial pair | \(|D(\{0,b,c\})|=0\) | \(=2\) | \(=3\) | \(=4\) | Total choices of \(c\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \(b=1\) | 0 | 11 | 10 | 2 | 23 |
| \(b=u\) | 2 | 13 | 8 | 0 | 23 |

In the second row the two and only two successful third centers are \(c=1+3u\) and \(c=4+3u\). This is an exact enumeration of a field with 25 elements, reproduced by the self-contained verifier; it is **not** an extrapolation from selected examples. To make the arithmetic transparent, the two-center uncovered sets are
\[
D(\{0,1\})=\{2u,3u,1+2u,1+3u,3+u,3+4u\},
\]
\[
D(\{0,u\})=\{2u,3u,4u,1+3u,4+3u\}.
\]
For each candidate \(c=a+bu\), checking whether its translate covers each listed missing element requires only determining whether \(N(v-c)\) belongs to \(\{0,1,4\}\). The verifier checks all 46 possible third-center cases and independently checks every one of the \(\binom{25}{3}=2300\) triples using a separately constructed square set. The census proves the pair-extension rule. In particular no covering triple contains a square-difference pair.

Every element has twelve nonsquare differences, so there are \(25\cdot12/2=150\) unordered nonsquare pairs. Each extends to exactly two covering triples, and each covering triple contains three such pairs. Double-counting gives \(150\cdot2/3=100\) covers. Neither type of two-center set covers, as the displayed uncovered sets are nonempty; hence three is the minimum size.

The affine-square group has order \(25\cdot12=300\). Every covering triple can first be normalized on any pair to \(\{0,u,c\}\) with \(c\in\{1+3u,4+3u\}\). The square-affine involution \(z\mapsto u-z\) exchanges these two alternatives. Thus all covers are equivalent to \(T=\{0,u,1+3u\}\) under this group. Its orbit therefore consists of all 100 covers, and its stabilizer has order \(300/100=3\). Direct arithmetic shows \(N(2+3u)=1\) and that \(z\mapsto(2+3u)z+u\) cycles \(0\mapsto u\mapsto1+3u\mapsto0\), generating the stabilizer.

## Relationship to prior work
Zhou and Wu, *Covering finite fields by translates of multiplicative subgroups* (arXiv:2610.25228v1, publicly submitted October 18, 2026), introduce \(A+G_d^0=\mathbb F_q\), prove general bounds, and study counts of covering shift sets asymptotically. Their Section 4.2 does not provide the exact 100-set classification, pair-extension rule, or one-orbit structure at \(q=25,d=2,s=3\).

The established Paley-graph domination table at A. E. Brouwer's Paley graph reference explicitly gives domination number three for \(q=25\), and therefore **already covers the minimum size and existence of such covers**. The new claim is the finer exact multiplicity, independent-set necessity, pair-extension law, and orbit classification. The equivalence is exact: closed neighborhoods in the Paley graph on \(K\) are precisely the additive translates of \(Q_0\).

## Limitations
The classification concerns only the 25-element field and does not assert analogous counts for other field sizes, other subgroup indices, or coverings with more centers. The observation that the minimum size is three is already known. Exhaustive published literature priority has not been independently certified, and a previously unindexed enumeration of the minimum dominating sets of the Paley graph of order 25 could subsume the count or orbit statement. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
