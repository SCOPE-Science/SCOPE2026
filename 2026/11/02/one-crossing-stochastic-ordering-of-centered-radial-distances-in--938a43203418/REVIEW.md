# Scientific review: one-crossing polygon radial ordering

## Correctness
PASS. For all integers \(3\le n<m\), the independently derived polar-sector CDF and monotonicity of \(\rho_k\), \(R_k\), and \(k\rho_k\) make the one-crossing function strictly decreasing with opposing boundary signs. This proves full first-order stochastic ordering, one unique interior density intersection, exact total-variation/Kolmogorov equality, and a one-Wasserstein mean gap. The script passed 75,384 deterministic checks, which corroborate but do not prove the universal statement.

## Originality
PASS with historical-priority risk. Dacey (1965) and Khalid–Durrani (2012) establish individual center-radius distributions; the latter's complete regular-polygon formula is acknowledged as prior. Haimovich–Magnanti (1988, abstract only) proves related asymptotic facility-location hexagonal optimality. A published Fraenkel-asymmetry formula already covers each polygon against the disk; that endpoint and its asymptotic are excluded from the new contribution. The finite-finite all-pair one-crossing comparison and exact transport distances were not identified in the inspected material or published-record searches. Uninspected full papers and unindexed older results remain risks.

## Value
PASS. Equal-area polygonal approximation has a natural radial distribution and facility-service-distance interpretation. The simultaneous all-side-count stochastic ordering and sharp finite-pair distribution-metric discrepancy supply a structural comparison stronger than mean convergence, without relying on arbitrary numerical cutoffs.

## Limits and closest literature
The assertion is strictly about area-uniform radial distance from a common polygon center, not pairwise point distances or arbitrary shapes. Full historical priority is not proven.
