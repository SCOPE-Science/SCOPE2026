# One projective class of minimal space-filling complete-intersection curves over \(\mathbb F_2\)

## Finding
Let \(k=\mathbb F_2\). Among the geometrically smooth complete intersections \(C\subset\mathbb P^3_k\) of a cubic and a quartic containing all \(15\) points of \(\mathbb P^3(k)\), there is **exactly one** equivalence class under \(\operatorname{PGL}_4(k)\). It has \(4032\) distinct embedded members (as homogeneous ideals in fixed projective coordinates), and the full \(k\)-projective stabilizer of each member is cyclic of order \(5\).

A representative is \(C=V(G,F)\), where
\[
G=X^2Y+XY^2+Z^2W+ZW^2
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
F={}&X^3Z+X^3W+X^2Z^2+XY^2Z+XYZ^2+XW^3\\
 &+Y^3Z+Y^3W+Y^2W^2+YZ^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
This curve has degree \(12\), genus \(19\), and precisely the \(15\) rational points of the ambient projective space. Its order-five projective automorphism admits an explicit geometric interpretation: the quotient curve has genus \(3\), and the covering is branched at exactly four geometric points, forming one Frobenius orbit of length \(4\). Thus \(C\) is a tame cyclic degree-five cover of a genus-three curve.

The new statements are **projective transitivity, the exact projective automorphism group, and the quotient/branch geometry**. An earlier exact finite census already recorded the number \(144\) of smooth quartic divisors on the fixed cubic and their arithmetic ordinarity; neither count nor ordinarity is claimed here as a new result.

## Assumptions and scope
All curves, changes of coordinates, automorphisms and divisor classes in the finite classification are defined over \(k=\mathbb F_2\). Smoothness and integrality are geometric (over an algebraic closure). The phrase *projective automorphism* means an element of \(\operatorname{PGL}_4(k)\) stabilizing the embedded curve; the claim does not identify the full abstract automorphism group of the underlying curve. The classification concerns complete intersections of type \((3,4)\), the minimum degree \(12\) possible among complete-intersection space-filling curves in \(\mathbb P^3(k)\), not arbitrary space-filling curves of smaller degree or larger degree.

## Proof
**1. The unique cubic equation has symplectic type.** Let \(J\) be the homogeneous ideal of \(\mathbb P^3(k)\). Its degree-three subspace is spanned by the six Frobenius alternating forms \(X_i^2X_j+X_iX_j^2\), indexed by \(0\le i<j\le3\). Hence any cubic vanishing on all rational projective points is \(G_A=\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}(X_i^2X_j+X_iX_j^2)\), for an alternating \(4\times4\) matrix \(A=(a_{ij})\).

Suppose \(C=V(G_A,F)\) is a geometrically smooth complete intersection and \(F\) is quartic. If \(A\) has rank \(2\), its kernel gives a projective line \(L\subset V(G_A)\) on which all partial derivatives of \(G_A\) vanish. The restriction \(F|_L\) has a geometric zero, so \(C\) has a singular point there. Rank \(0\) is the zero cubic and does not give a complete intersection. Consequently \(A\) must have rank \(4\). Conversely, for rank \(4\), the gradient \(A(X_0^2,\ldots,X_3^2)^t\) has no projective zero, so the cubic surface is smooth. All nondegenerate alternating forms in dimension four over \(k\) are linearly equivalent. We may therefore take \(G=X^2Y+XY^2+Z^2W+ZW^2\).

The number of such nondegenerate cubics in fixed coordinates is
\[
\frac{|\operatorname{GL}_4(\mathbb F_2)|}{|\operatorname{Sp}_4(\mathbb F_2)|}
=\frac{20160}{720}=28.
\]
A complete-intersection ideal of type \((3,4)\) has a unique nonzero cubic equation, since the coefficient field has only one nonzero scalar.

**2. Exhaustive quotient and orbit reduction.** The space \(J_4\) of degree-four forms vanishing on the \(15\) rational points has dimension \(20\). Quartics defining the same divisor on \(V(G)\) differ by \(G\) times a linear form, a four-dimensional subspace. Thus the quotient \(V=J_4/(G\cdot k[X,Y,Z,W]_1)\) is \(16\)-dimensional over \(k\), with exactly \(65536\) elements. The full stabilizer of \(G\) inside \(\operatorname{PGL}_4(k)\) is \(\operatorname{Sp}_4(k)\), of order \(720\).

The supplied `verify_orbits.py` builds \(J_4\) as the exact binary kernel of the evaluation map on the \(15\) points, passes to this \(16\)-dimensional quotient, and generates \(\operatorname{Sp}_4(k)\) through all \(15\) symplectic transvections. A complete orbit traversal of all \(65536\) classes finds \(128\) orbits. Direct, class-by-class gradient evaluation at the \(15\) rational points leaves exactly \(288\) classes nonsingular at every rational point. These are precisely two symplectic orbits, each of size \(144\). In the deterministic quotient basis generated by that verifier, the orbit representatives are the binary integers `13387` and `13394`; the latter represents the displayed polynomial \(F\). No smooth curve can be missed by filtering out forms singular at a rational point.

**3. Geometric smoothness distinguishes the two orbits.** For each representative \(F_*\), consider the ideal generated by \(G,F_*\) and the six \(2\times2\) Jacobian minors \((\partial_iG)(\partial_jF_*)-(\partial_jG)(\partial_iF_*)\). Set each one of \(X,Y,Z,W\) equal to \(1\), in turn, and compute the exact Gröbner basis over \(\mathbb F_2\) in the other three variables. For representative `13394`, all four bases contain \(1\); therefore the projective singular locus is empty, even after extending scalars. The supplied `verify_geometry.py` redoes each calculation.

For representative `13387`, let \(u\) be a root of the irreducible polynomial
\[
h(T)=T^5+T^3+T^2+T+1\in\mathbb F_2[T].
\]
Then the point
\[
[1:u^4+u+1:u^2+u:u]
\]
satisfies both equations and all six Jacobian minors. This is a degree-five geometric singular point, explicitly checked by polynomial remainders modulo \(h\) in `verify_geometry.py`. Thus the first candidate orbit is not geometrically smooth. All \(144\) classes in the other orbit are geometrically smooth. Because these are smooth complete intersections of two surfaces in projective three-space, they are geometrically connected and hence geometrically integral. Their degree is \(3\cdot4=12\), and adjunction gives genus \(1+3\cdot4(3+4-4)/2=19\).

**4. Uniqueness and full projective automorphisms.** The sole geometrically smooth fixed-cubic orbit has size \(144\) under the group of order \(720\). Each curve therefore has exactly \(720/144=5\) projective automorphisms preserving \(G\). Every projective automorphism of the curve must preserve \(G\), because its homogeneous ideal has only one cubic generator. Hence the full \(k\)-projective stabilizer is \(C_5\). Varying the cubic among its \(28\) congruent nonsingular alternating forms gives exactly \(28\cdot144=4032\) distinct smooth complete-intersection ideals. All are equivalent under the full projective group: there is one orbit of size \(20160/5=4032\).

An explicit generator \(A\) of the projective stabilizer sends
\[
(X,Y,Z,W)\longmapsto(Z,X+W,Y+Z,X+Y+Z).
\]
It satisfies \(A^5=1\), \(G\circ A=G\), and
\[
F\circ A=F+YG.
\]
The exact matrix-group and polynomial identities are checked by `verify_group.py`.

**5. The genus-three quotient.** The characteristic polynomial of this \(4\times4\) binary matrix is \(T^4+T^3+T^2+T+1\), irreducible over \(\mathbb F_2\). Over an algebraic closure it has four distinct primitive fifth-root eigenvalues. These yield exactly four projectively fixed points of \(A\), all lying on the curve: for an eigenvector of eigenvalue \(\zeta\ne1\), the identity \(G\circ A=G\) and cubic homogeneity force \((\zeta^3-1)G=0\), hence \(G=0\); the identity for \(F\) then forces \((\zeta^4-1)F=0\), hence \(F=0\). There are no other fixed points. These four fixed points form one length-four Frobenius orbit because the eigenvalue polynomial is irreducible over \(\mathbb F_2\).

Since the order five is coprime to the characteristic two, the quotient map is a tame cyclic cover, totally ramified at these four points and unramified elsewhere. Riemann–Hurwitz gives
\[
2\cdot19-2=5(2g'-2)+4(5-1),
\]
so the smooth quotient has \(g'=3\).

## Verification
Run in the directory containing the three scripts:

```sh
python3 verify_orbits.py
python3 verify_group.py
python3 verify_geometry.py
```

`verify_orbits.py` and `verify_group.py` use only Python's standard library. `verify_geometry.py` uses SymPy for exact Gröbner bases and polynomial arithmetic over \(\mathbb F_2\); no floating-point computations occur. The verified outputs are `ORBIT_VERIFY_OK`, `GROUP_VERIFY_OK`, and `GEOMETRY_VERIFY_OK`. In particular, the computation searches **all** binary quartic section classes, and Gröbner bases over the base field certify smoothness over its algebraic closure. The explicit singular witness for the discarded orbit gives an additional independent distinction.

## Relationship to prior work
Campbell, Dedvukaj, McCormick, Moon and Morales introduced concrete complete-intersection space-filling curves in \(\mathbb P^3\), exhibited examples for small finite fields, and proved the complete-intersection minimal-degree bound \((q+1)(q+2)\). Their construction uses a smooth Frobenius-alternating cubic, but their example-generation argument does not classify the projective isomorphism classes or automorphism groups of all binary minimal-degree examples. An earlier exact fixed-cubic census already established the \(144\)-smooth-class count, the \(288\)-rationally-smooth count, and ordinarity; the present result adds the non-implied statement that those \(144\) form a **single** symplectic orbit, yielding a full projective classification and a cyclic quotient of genus three.

## Limitations
The assertion is specifically about embedded complete-intersection space-filling curves of type \((3,4)\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). It does not classify arbitrary space-filling curves, curves over other fields, nonminimal-degree complete intersections, or abstract isomorphism classes under arbitrary nonprojective maps. The \(C_5\) group is the full group of **\(\mathbb F_2\)-rational projective automorphisms**, not an asserted geometric or abstract full automorphism group. The quotient is shown to have genus three, but no explicit plane quartic equation for it is claimed. The complete-search certificate determines uniqueness within the prescribed finite domain; it is not a global novelty certificate.
