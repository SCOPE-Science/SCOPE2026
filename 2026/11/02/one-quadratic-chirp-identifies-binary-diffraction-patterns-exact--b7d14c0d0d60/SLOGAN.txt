A single fixed quadratic chirp followed by one full Fourier intensity measurement identifies every binary signal of weight at least two precisely when the cyclic length is an odd prime and the chirp coefficient is nonzero modulo that prime; composite lengths always have explicit two-point ambiguities. At prime lengths the only remaining collisions are the unavoidable coordinate singletons, giving the maximum possible \(2^p-p+1\) distinguishable binary patterns and an explicit nonzero intensity separation bound.
