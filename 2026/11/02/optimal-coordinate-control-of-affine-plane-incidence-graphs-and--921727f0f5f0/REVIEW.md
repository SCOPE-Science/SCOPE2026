# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** For a finite affine plane of order \(q\ge2\), the incidence identity \(NN^{\mathsf T}=qI+J\) is immediate from pairwise unique joins and point degree \(q+1\). The displayed block Laplacian decomposes into two radial eigenvalues, a line-kernel eigenvalue of multiplicity \(q\), and two eigenvalues each with multiplicity \(q^2-1\); the multiplicities total all \(2q^2+q\) graph vertices and no coincident eigenvalues occur for \(q\ge2\). The PBH test reduces exactly to line parallel-class coverage and an incidence matrix whose row count equals its column count for minimum-size placements. The pencil construction gives a nonsingular instance, and the exhaustive signature circuit gives the count. There are no unproved genericity or prime-power steps. The extracted finite verifier is a supplementary check, not the justification of the general theorem.

## Originality

**PASS on inspected evidence, with residual specialist priority risk.** The maximum-eigenvalue-multiplicity principle in Yuan et al. (2013) already covers the bare driver-number lower bound and generic minimal control theory; it is **not** represented as new. Zhang–Camlibel–Cao (2011) give general graph-partition bounds and explicit distance-regular graph cases, but the affine incidence graph is biregular, not distance regular. The specific parallel-direction necessity, all-minimum-placement determinant characterization, simultaneous count-optimal construction and exact concurrent-pencil census are not supplied by these inspected papers. Searches for affine incidence graph coordinate controllability and synonyms did not produce a dominating earlier statement. The possible existence of an unlocated specialist note on incidence-graph leader placements remains a real risk; failed keyword searches alone do not establish priority.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** Biregular point–line incidence networks are natural finite-design models, and the requirement that a minimum number of controlled *line* vertices cover nearly every parallel class is an interpretable structural constraint not visible from the raw spectral multiplicity. An if-and-only-if incidence determinant certificate classifies all minimal placements, while the nontrivial exact integer \(2q^3(q+1)(q-1)^q\) enumerates a canonical concurrent-pencil family for every existing plane, including non-Desarguesian cases. This constitutes a complete, mathematically motivated constrained-placement result, not a routine finite census or a renaming of a generic Kalman test.
