# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The statement is quantified for \(\mu>1\), \(0<L<H\), constant \(h\), and exactly two high seasons in six. The proof establishes positive-cycle existence below a boundary, excludes all cycles by an explicit two-low-season bound at \(h_0\), obtains the repeated-positive-eigenvalue equation for each maximal sustainable quota, and derives both trace-difference factorizations. The strict comparison of the *first* roots uses continuity and the positive trace order on the complete feasible range, not root samples. Reconstructed symbolic matrices and exact rational brackets pass in the companion executable. Numerical checks are corroboration, not a substitute for the uniform proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional priority)
The 2010 paper proves dihedral invariance and classifications through five seasons, then explicitly asks for higher-period permutation classifications. The new six-season two-high/four-low classification does not follow by specializing its period-five cases. The 2009 paper compares harvesting regimes in a constant environment; the 2016 paper uses proportional harvesting effort; the 2020 paper permutes quotas and measures a different invariant. Source comparisons, exact-object and equivalence queries, and prior claims were checked. The 2020 full text remained inaccessible and an unknown stronger general theorem could subsume the claim. Novelty beyond inspected sources is not certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This resolves a natural complete three-class placement problem at the first period beyond the 2010 classification, answering a defined binary-capacity subcase of its explicit higher-period question. The two identical favorable seasons are the minimal nontrivial repeated-resource motif; the strict, parameter-free ordering gives actionable mathematical structure about spacing resource-rich years when fishing quotas cannot vary by season. It is not a generic statement for arbitrary season counts or a claim about real-world fishery robustness.
