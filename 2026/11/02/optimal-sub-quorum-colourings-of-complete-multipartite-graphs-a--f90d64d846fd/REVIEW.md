# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The proof begins from the coloured-support graph, proves the necessary upper bound on its colour-class count, and forces an optimal support to contain a complete maximum part. An integer lower bound on each quorum-class size restricts the remaining coloured vertices to at most three when the largest part size is three and at most two otherwise. The remaining cases are eliminated or classified by direct local-neighbourhood checks. The three families are disjoint, and their multiplicities are derived from labelled part/vertex choices. A standalone exhaustive enumerator compared all optimum colourings and each claimed support family on 66 multipartite profiles (orders four to ten). The infinite result follows from the written argument, not this finite check.

**Originality — PASS, with priority risk.** The closest source is Ma, Sahbi, and Zhang (arXiv:2609.25128v4), Theorem 6.4, which gives only the maximum colour count. The present theorem sharpens that bound to *all* optimal supports, provides a three-family classification, and counts the solutions. The source's same-object class-size argument is explicitly credited; the resulting optimiser theorem is not identified in the inspected sections. The foundational full-support quorum-colouring paper differs in the role of uncoloured vertices. Search results for partial quorum colouring, induced alliance partitions, and optimal multipartite colourings did not establish stronger coverage. It remains possible that an equivalent classification appears in less visible literature.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Optimal solution-space enumeration is a natural refinement of the recently established multipartite extremum, not a renamed scalar bound. A sharp rigidity transition at size four replaces a family with linearly many structural alternatives by only choices of full largest parts. The size-three exception reduces an otherwise unrestricted part-size family to three explicit supports of at most six coloured vertices and produces an exact count depending only on the order and number of triple parts. These structural and enumerative statements are useful for distinguishing optimal configurations and sampling them uniformly.

**Closest sources and limitations.** Ma, Sahbi, and Zhang, *Sub-quorum colorings of graphs*, arXiv:2609.25128v4, Definition 2.2 and Theorem 6.4; Hedetniemi, Hedetniemi, Laskar, and Mulder, *Quorum Colorings of Graphs*, EI 2012-20, Proposition 17. The new theorem excludes largest part sizes below three and does not handle suboptimal palettes or symmetry-reduced graph colourings. The previous scalar theorem's equality cases are a genuine implication-comparison risk: they permit the proof strategy, but they do not state the new enumeration.
