# Review: Optimal symmetric realization of co-singleton actual-effectivity frames

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem is proved for all finite carriers. The lower bound counts the \(n\) distinct required neighborhoods of each agent; the excluded orders \(1\) and \(2\) fail respectively nonemptiness and independence of actions. The odd-order construction is the affine midpoint operation with an admissible diagonal replacement. The even-order construction adjoins one outcome to an odd cyclic set and checks each finite row and the new row separately. Both are symmetric and realize the co-singleton row and column images. The standalone checker tests \(3\le n\le200\), and exhaustive small-order symmetric matrix counts provide independent finite sanity checks. None of these finite checks replaces the general proof.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence
Ciardelli's 2026 Theorem 2.2 is a general existence characterization, not a minimum-action or symmetric-construction result. The closest separately identified quantitative bound, *Sharp root-size action realization for finite actual-effectivity frames*, gives a general worst-case action-copy guarantee; it does not assert that the full family of co-singletons admits one action per neighborhood with symmetric agent exchange. Classical modular midpoint operations are known, and their existence in isolation is not asserted as novel. Search and inspection did not reveal a covering statement, but equivalent results under symmetric matrix, graph edge-colouring or quasigroup terminology remain possible.

## Value — PASS
A co-singleton neighborhood models an agent's ability to exclude exactly one outcome, independently of the other agent. The minimum number of deterministic actions needed to reproduce those actual-effectivity choices is a natural quantitative invariant not supplied by representability alone. The theorem gives a complete all-order answer, a sharp impossibility boundary, an exact action lower bound, and a swap-symmetric optimal design. It does not claim results for arbitrary neighborhood families.
