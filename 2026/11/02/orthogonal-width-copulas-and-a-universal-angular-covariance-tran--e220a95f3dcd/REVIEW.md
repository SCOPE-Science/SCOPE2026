# Scientific review: angular width dependence of regular polygons

## Correctness — PASS
The claim specifies regular \(n\)-gons for every integer \(n\ge3\), positive circumradius, a single Haar-uniform angular orientation, and one fixed shift. Central symmetrization produces precisely the stated even-order support profile, including the odd-order radius factor. The exact covariance follows from integrating two cosines on the two fold intervals; its strict decrease uses the strict monotonicity of \(\sin x/x\). The signed endpoint covariance is controlled by reverse monotonicity, not a finite-sample observation. For the orthogonal shift the parity reduction gives the upper or lower Fréchet copula, and monotone inversion yields the entire enclosing-rectangle-area distribution. Uniform Taylor remainder control establishes the quartic profile, its root, and the \(-7/8\) limit. Direct finite geometric checks (11,564) and high-precision asymptotic checks passed, but the general proof is analytical.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical uncertainty
The closest full-text inspection is Akiyama and Kamae, *Width deviation of convex polygons* (arXiv:2201.06736v1): it treats single-direction random widths and their second moments, not the joint law under a fixed rotation or the random enclosing-rectangle area. Kim–Yaskin–Zvavitch (arXiv:1512.07289v1) treats projection-size distributions, not their coupling at two specified directions. Horváth–Lángi (arXiv:1302.6164v1) and the published regular-polygon translative-convex-hull profile concern longest chord times perpendicular width, a different observable. A published minimum enclosing parallelogram result optimizes a containing enclosure over free angles; it does not give its random-orientation distribution. A published marginal-width inverse theorem does not determine these angularly coupled observables from a marginal alone. Targeted exact and equivalent-formulation research-record and literature searches found no matching combined result. Given that the proofs use elementary known width support profiles, an uncatalogued regular-polygon special case remains plausible: this is not a worldwide priority certificate.

## Value — PASS
Dependence between two measurements of one rotated object cannot generally be deduced from either single-direction distribution. The exact upper/lower Fréchet dichotomy, whole area CDF of the natural orientation-dependent minimal bounding rectangle, and unique angular zero with a uniform universal scaling law are structural geometric facts rather than an arbitrary numerical sample. They give a complete family-wide benchmark for projected-width dependence and orientation-sensitive bounding geometry. No wider theorem for arbitrary convex bodies, freely optimized bounding rectangles, or noisy observations is claimed.

## Scope and limitations
The general width formula itself is standard support geometry, and the literature on single-direction widths and enclosure optimization is explicitly acknowledged. The result concerns uniform rotation of a regular polygon in the plane. Its exact correlation does not extend by continuity to the limiting disk, where the width variance vanishes. Published equivalent calculations not indexed by the available searches remain an originality risk.
