# Scientific review of the paired-Lagrangian spectrum

## Correctness — PASS

The bipartite graph-state character sum proves that nonzero spectrum multiplicity is exactly the image size of the cross matrix modulo the local dimension; it remains valid for composite dimensions. The cross matrix is separable in two complementary binary contexts; Boolean zeta factorization gives the *previously known* single-context Smith form. Its image-pair kernel is precisely a constant vector, and the integer identity \(H(2H-J)=2^{m-1}I\) computes exactly when that constant is attainable modulo any local dimension. Rational rank and every modular image size determine the claimed rectangular Smith form. Verification runs include exact invariant factors, direct modular images and a complete small quantum partial trace. No finite check is used to assert the infinite case.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority uncertainty

Hacıoğlu (DOI:10.3906/mat-1112-18) has the *single*-Hadamard-design incidence Smith form, which is an essential credited premise and not the new claim. The paired-context rectangular matrix is not that incidence matrix, its transpose, or a single derived/residual incidence matrix from the examined theorem; its additional constant-overlap quotient removes exactly one highest invariant factor. Planat–Saniga (arXiv:quant-ph/0701211) supply the binary isotropic geometry; Abiad–Haemers (arXiv:1412.2945) supply global symplectic-graph ranks; Looi et al. (arXiv:0712.1979), Hein et al. (arXiv:quant-ph/0307130), and Helwig (arXiv:1306.2879) provide general graph-state formalism. The examined materials do not state the all-dimensional paired-cut Smith form or resulting spectrum. An earlier specialized paired-incidence calculation remains possible. These searches do not constitute a proof of global uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

The two contexts are a canonical geometric cut arising from complementary maximal commuting sets in binary Pauli geometry. The theorem classifies the *entire* entanglement spectrum for an infinite family of growing quantum networks for every integer local dimension, not merely qubits or primes. The exact one-dit odd-dimension deficit versus the exponentially increasing qubit deficit is a structural distinction, and the rectangular Smith form gives a reusable arithmetic invariant for arbitrary composite moduli. The result is not a numerical table or a relabeling of the known single-context formula.

## Limits

Only complementary maximal binary isotropic contexts and pure unit-weight graph states are covered. Historical priority beyond the cited sources is not settled. The numerical checks confirm finite examples, not the general theorem.
