# Pairwise-annihilating tuple varieties of radical-square-zero quiver algebras

## Finding

Let \(Q=(V,E,s,t)\) be any finite directed multiquiver, allowing loops, parallel arrows, oriented cycles, and isolated vertices, with \(n=|V|\ge1\) and \(h=|E|\). Let \(k\) be an arbitrary field and let \(A_Q(k)=kQ/J^2\), where \(J\) is the ideal generated by the arrows. For \(m\ge1\), consider the **reduced** affine variety
\[
\mathcal Z_m(A_Q)=\{(x_1,\ldots,x_m)\in A_Q^m:x_ix_j=0\text{ for all }i\ne j\}_{\mathrm{red}}.
\]
The condition is ordered: both products vanish for every distinct pair, but no square-zero condition is placed on the individual \(x_i\).

For \(S\subseteq V\) write \(e_Q(S)=|\{\alpha\in E:s(\alpha),t(\alpha)\in S\}|\), counting loops and arrow multiplicities. Define the induced-edge polynomial
\[
\Xi_Q(X,Y)=\sum_{S\subseteq V}X^{n-|S|}Y^{e_Q(S)}.
\]
This is a graph invariant of the underlying undirected multigraph with loops; its definition is not asserted to be a new graph polynomial.

**Arithmetic theorem.** For every prime power \(q\ge2\) and every \(m\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{N_m(Q;q):=|\mathcal Z_m(A_Q)(\mathbb F_q)|
=q^h\sum_{S\subseteq V}[m(q-1)]^{n-|S|}q^{(m-1)e_Q(S)}
=q^h\Xi_Q(m(q-1),q^{m-1}).}
\]
The count includes all ordered tuples, and depends on \(Q\) only through the underlying undirected multigraph, with arrow multiplicities and loops retained. It is characteristic-independent.

**Geometric theorem.** For each choice of \(S\subseteq V\) and coloring \(\ell:V\setminus S\to\{1,\ldots,m\}\), the locus where the joint diagonal coordinate at a vertex vanishes exactly for vertices of \(S\), and has unique nonzero tuple-coordinate \(\ell(v)\) at each vertex outside \(S\), is a geometrically irreducible smooth locally closed subvariety isomorphic over \(k\) to
\[
(\mathbb G_m)^{n-|S|}\times\mathbb A^{h+(m-1)e_Q(S)}.
\]
These locally closed subvarieties partition the reduced variety. Consequently, putting
\[
\delta_m(Q)=\max_{S\subseteq V}\big((m-1)e_Q(S)-|S|\big),
\]
one has
\[
\boxed{\dim\mathcal Z_m(A_Q)=n+h+\delta_m(Q).}
\]
The **exact number of geometrically irreducible components of maximum dimension** is
\[
\boxed{T_m(Q)=\sum_{S:\,(m-1)e_Q(S)-|S|=\delta_m(Q)}m^{n-|S|}.}
\]
This is a count of top-dimensional components only; lower-dimensional irreducible components are not classified here. All top-dimensional components arise as closures of the indicated locally closed loci and are defined over \(k\).

For \(\rho(Q)=\max_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq V}e_Q(S)/|S|\) (with \(\rho=0\) if \(E=\varnothing\)), there is a sharp dimension threshold
\[
\boxed{\dim\mathcal Z_m(A_Q)>n+h\ \Longleftrightarrow\ (m-1)\rho(Q)>1.}
\]
The threshold is defined entirely by the densest vertex-induced submultigraph.

**Fixed-field reconstruction theorem.** Fix any one prime power \(q\ge2\). The infinite sequence of integers \(\{N_m(Q;q):m\ge1\}\) uniquely determines \(n\), \(h\), and every coefficient of \(\Xi_Q(X,Y)\). This does **not** assert that the sequence determines the quiver or its underlying multigraph up to isomorphism.

## Assumptions and scope

The ground field is arbitrary for the geometric statements and is exactly \(\mathbb F_q\) for the counts. No algebraic-closure assumption is needed for the explicit strata; geometric irreducibility refers to extension to an algebraic closure. Quiver edges may repeat and have equal source and target. Products of two arrows vanish in \(A_Q\). The variety always concerns pairwise products with **distinct tuple indices**; requiring \(x_i^2=0\) would define a different problem. The characteristic may be two. The assertion about \(\Xi_Q\) concerns an invariant polynomial, not graph-isomorphism reconstruction.

## Proof

**1. Multiplication in the path-algebra quotient.** Let \(e_v\) be the length-zero path at \(v\) and let \(\alpha\) range over arrows. They form a vector-space basis of \(A_Q\), with
\[
 e_ue_v=\mathbf1_{u=v}e_v,\qquad e_u\alpha=\mathbf1_{u=s(\alpha)}\alpha,\qquad
 \alpha e_v=\mathbf1_{v=t(\alpha)}\alpha,\qquad \alpha\beta=0.
\]
Write \(x_i=\sum_{v\in V}a_{vi}e_v+\sum_{\alpha\in E}u_{\alpha i}\alpha\). For \(i\ne j\), \(x_ix_j=0\) is exactly the combined system
\[
 a_{vi}a_{vj}=0\quad(v\in V),\qquad
 a_{s(\alpha),i}u_{\alpha j}+u_{\alpha i}a_{t(\alpha),j}=0\quad(\alpha\in E).
\]
These are the original defining equations; no genericity or characteristic assumptions have been made.

**2. The vertex condition.** Over a field, \(a_{vi}a_{vj}=0\) for all distinct \(i,j\) means the vector \(a_v=(a_{v1},\ldots,a_{vm})\) is either zero, or has a unique nonzero coordinate. Let \(S\) be the set of zero vectors and let \(\ell(v)\) record the nonzero coordinate for each \(v\notin S\). The admissible nonzero scalar coefficients form \((\mathbb G_m)^{n-|S|}\). There are exactly \(m^{n-|S|}\) possible choices of \(\ell\).

**3. Every arrow contributes either one or \(m\) free coordinates.** For an arrow \(\alpha:s\to t\), put \(u=(u_{\alpha1},\ldots,u_{\alpha m})\). If both \(a_s\) and \(a_t\) vanish, every arrow equation is void: \(u\in k^m\), giving \(m\) free coordinates. Otherwise the arrow equations define exactly a one-dimensional linear space, as follows.

If only the source vector is nonzero in coordinate \(r\), the equations with first index \(r\) force \(u_i=0\) for \(i\ne r\), leaving \(u_r\) free. The same holds with source and target interchanged. If both endpoints have nonzero coordinate \(r\), all \(u_i\) for \(i\ne r\) vanish, again leaving \(u_r\) free. Finally, if the source has coordinate \(r\) and the target a different coordinate \(j\), all coordinates outside \(\{r,j\}\) vanish, while the sole remaining relation is
\[
 a_{s(\alpha),r}u_j+a_{t(\alpha),j}u_r=0,
\]
where the source and target vertex subscripts distinguish the two endpoint coefficients. Both scalars are nonzero, so the solutions are \(u_r=a_{s(\alpha),r}z\), \(u_j=-a_{t(\alpha),j}z\) with one free \(z\in k\). The argument also applies to loops (then the two endpoint colors coincide) and to characteristic two. Every arrow system is independent of all other arrows because \(J^2=0\).

The one-dimensional arrow spaces admit regular global bases on each diagonal-support stratum: the nonzero endpoint coordinates are invertible regular functions. Therefore the full stratum is isomorphic to the claimed product of a torus and an affine space. Its dimension is
\[
 (n-|S|)+h+(m-1)e_Q(S)
 =n+h+(m-1)e_Q(S)-|S|.
\]

**4. Count, dimension, and top components.** Over \(\mathbb F_q\), the \(m^{n-|S|}\) color choices and \((q-1)^{n-|S|}\) nonzero scalars multiply the arrow-fiber cardinality \(q^{h+(m-1)e_Q(S)}\). Summing disjoint strata gives the boxed count. Since there are finitely many smooth geometrically irreducible locally closed strata, the dimension of their union is their maximum dimension, yielding \(n+h+\delta_m(Q)\).

Each stratum of maximum dimension has irreducible closure of that dimension, hence is an irreducible component of the reduced variety: any larger irreducible closed subset would have dimension greater than the total dimension. Distinct maximum-dimensional strata cannot have the same closure, because each is a nonempty open subset of its own irreducible closure and two nonempty open subsets of an irreducible space must intersect. Their number is the displayed sum \(T_m(Q)\). This does not imply that no additional lower-dimensional components exist.

Since the empty set makes \((m-1)e_Q(S)-|S|=0\), the maximum \(\delta_m(Q)\) is nonnegative. It is positive precisely when a nonempty \(S\) satisfies \((m-1)e_Q(S)>|S|\), equivalent to \((m-1)\rho(Q)>1\).

**5. Fixed-field recovery.** Let \(c_{r,e}\) count subsets \(S\subseteq V\) with \(n-|S|=r\) and \(e_Q(S)=e\). Grouping the count by \(e\) gives the exponential-polynomial identity
\[
 N_m(Q;q)=\sum_{e=0}^{h}q^{me}P_e(m),\qquad
 P_e(z)=q^{h-e}\sum_{r=0}^{n}c_{r,e}(q-1)^rz^r.
\]
Distinct functions \(m\mapsto q^{me}P_e(m)\) are linearly independent when the \(P_e\) are polynomials: divide a purported nonzero zero sum by the largest exponential \(q^{me_*}\); all lower-exponential terms tend to zero, whereas the nonzero polynomial \(P_{e_*}(m)\) cannot. The same argument recursively gives uniqueness.

The largest exponent \(e\) appearing is exactly \(h\), because \(S=V\) has \(e_Q(V)=h\). The largest polynomial degree among the \(P_e\) is exactly \(n\), because \(S=\varnothing\) uniquely contributes a nonzero term of degree \(n\) to \(P_0\). Thus the fixed-field sequence recovers \(h\), then \(n\), then all \(P_e\) and the coefficients \(c_{r,e}\) by dividing their coefficients by the nonzero numbers \(q^{h-e}(q-1)^r\). These coefficients determine \(\Xi_Q(X,Y)=\sum_{r,e}c_{r,e}X^rY^e\). This completes the proof.

## Verification

The standalone program `checker.py` independently creates the original radical-square-zero path-algebra multiplication, tests all ordered products of distinct tuple elements, and compares exhaustive counts with the subset formula. Its finite-field arithmetic includes a genuine degree-two extension field \(\mathbb F_4\), not integer arithmetic modulo four. Running `python checker.py` prints the output recorded in `checker_output.txt` and ends with the marker `ANNIHILATING_QUIVER_TUPLES_OK 17`. Seventeen exhaustive configurations include loops, parallel and opposing arrows, paths, cycles, tuple lengths one through four, and fields of orders two, three, and four.

For the one-edge quiver on two vertices, the theorem specializes to
\[
 N_m=q\big([m(q-1)]^2+2m(q-1)+q^{m-1}\big).
\]
Over \(\mathbb F_2\), this gives \(N_2=20\), \(N_3=38\), and \(N_4=64\). The dimension equals three at tuple lengths two and three, then four at length four. The numbers of maximum-dimensional components at these three lengths are four, ten, and one, respectively. The proof of these facts is independent of the finite tests.

## Relationship to prior work

Bleher, Chinburg and Huisgen-Zimmermann (arXiv:1407.3045v1, July 11, 2014) analyze module representation varieties for algebras with vanishing radical square, including the structure of their irreducible components. Those varieties parametrize modules of fixed dimension vectors, not pairwise-annihilating tuples of algebra elements; their main theorems do not entail this intrinsic all-arity point count or its density threshold.

Huang (arXiv:2110.15566, initial 2021 preprint, revised 2022) counts mutually annihilating **pairs of unrestricted square matrices**, including nilpotent variants. It motivates zero-product enumeration but neither fixes the radical-square-zero quiver subalgebra nor treats arbitrary tuple lengths and graph-polynomial recovery. Tittmann, Averbouch and Makowsky (arXiv:0812.4147, 2008) study the vertex-induced **component** polynomial, which records numbers of connected components rather than numbers of induced edges; this is a different graph polynomial and cannot be substituted for \(\Xi_Q\) on multigraphs with loops.

Previously recorded results for commuting tuples and simultaneous-conjugacy orbits of radical-square-zero quiver algebras concern \(x_ix_j=x_jx_i\), not the stronger ordered zero-product constraints \(x_ix_j=0\). Their orbit-counting and random-cluster formulas do not imply the new full element counts. An earlier count for one-sided zero products in a diamond incidence algebra similarly does not cover these arbitrary-quiver, all-arity, two-sided equations. These comparisons exclude the underlying quiver algebra construction and the basic graph-polynomial formalism from any novelty claim.

## Limitations

Only the **reduced** zero-product variety and its top-dimensional irreducible components are characterized geometrically; possible lower-dimensional components and nonreduced scheme structures are not classified. The point-count formula concerns the algebra \(kQ/J^2\), not arbitrary higher radical powers or arbitrary split basic algebras. Equality of all tuple counts recovers the induced-edge polynomial, not necessarily graph or algebra isomorphism. Historical priority for the combined general theorem remains subject to further independent review. The standalone finite computations corroborate, but do not replace, the proof.
