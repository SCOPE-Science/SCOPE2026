# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The proof explicitly fixes the finite graph, spin normalization, triangle incidence, and zero-temperature order of limits. Each triangle has at least one frustrated edge; the sharp inequality \(F\ge n+(R_a+R_b)/2\), with each rim's frustration parity, establishes the exact energy lower bound. Equality forces alternating rims when \(n\) is even, or exactly one frustrated edge on each rim when \(n\) is odd. The two defect-triangle constraints are shown equivalent and yield a complete bijection with labelled states; the magnetization counts and full same-/cross-layer two-point functions then follow from finite uniform averaging. Independent brute-force enumeration, a periodic four-state transfer recursion, and constructive rational checks all passed. Remaining risk: none identified in the proof; the computational checks are finite and do not certify infinity.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence, with residual risk
We compared the claim, not merely its title, with the established equivalence of an antiprism to the square of a cycle, with general Ising spectrum transforms, and with previously studied frustrated quantum/zigzag ladders. The exact formula for all finite circumference parities, the full magnetization census, and all cross-layer two-point correlations do not occur in the accessible primary full texts of Moessner–Sondhi or Mosseri, which study different frustrated geometries/quantum effects and general spectrum computation respectively. Published Jurčišinová–Jurčišin results concern an infinite zigzag ladder with multisite interactions, and the accessible abstract does not settle the finite-periodic two-point claim. The Williams article on frustrated Ising chains was also not accessible in full; it remains a priority risk, but its abstract neither identifies the specific antiprism graph nor provides the complete finite cross-layer correlation kernel. The new result is not a special case of earlier prism or generalized-Petersen claims: these have distinct edge sets, and their formulas do not imply this antiprism solution. Neither inaccessible paper was treated as proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The finite zigzag antiferromagnet is a natural frustrated spin model: the octahedron is the first antiprism and antiprisms are precisely the squares of even cycles. A qualitative parity transition changes the complete ground-state set from four nonmagnetic configurations to a quadratic family with a three-sector magnetization distribution. Full exact two-point functions and a parity-selective Curie coefficient reveal an unusual nonuniform large-system correlation limit. This physical content is more than a graph renaming, a routine single-number calculation, or a numerical extrapolation. The result may support finite-size frustration studies and tests of transfer-matrix calculations, without asserting a quantum or finite-temperature extension.
