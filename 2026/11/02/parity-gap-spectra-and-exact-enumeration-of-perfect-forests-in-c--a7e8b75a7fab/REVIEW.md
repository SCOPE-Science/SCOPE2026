# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For a component to be an induced tree in a complete bipartite graph, it must contain exactly one vertex in one of the two shores. Requiring odd degree at every vertex leaves precisely odd-leaf stars, and the one-edge case is corrected for double counting. The labelled exponential formula follows by partitions into unordered connected components. Parity and component bounds are necessary; the explicit one-opposite-shore-center construction realizes every permitted component count. Extremal coefficients follow from odd-surjective allocations and the forced form of two-component forests. Finite independent edge-subset enumeration confirms examples but is not used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (publication-priority uncertainty retained)

The nearest primary result is Gutin and Yeo, arXiv:2105.00254 (2021). Their NP-hardness theorem treats maximizing the edges of a 0-perfect forest on arbitrary graphs, not counting all forests on a complete bipartite graph. Inspection of the arXiv full-text introduction, theorem statements, relevant constructions, and searches for complete-bipartite/enumeration language identified no equivalent generating function or sharp support classification. Egawa–Kano–Kelmans' 1997 star-partition theorem concerns existence of bounded-star partitions; the available abstract does not establish the weighted, odd-star, labelled count. Purwanto–Winanthi (2020) studies a decomposition of *all graph edges* into stars, not a spanning forest whose connected components are induced trees. Multiple mathematical-record searches and available earlier claims yielded no implication-equivalent result. These checks do not constitute a proof of global publication priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The prior complexity literature explicitly highlights the maximum-size 0-perfect forest problem. The theorem solves the full labelled cardinality distribution for a standard infinite family rather than merely a scalar optimum: it gives an exact two-variable exponential generating function, a sharp parity-gapped edge spectrum, and extremal multiplicities. These are natural, reusable quantitative refinements of the 0-perfect-forest existence and optimization questions. The result is limited to complete bipartite graphs and may be known under odd-star-factor terminology.

## Remaining scientific limitations

The counted forests are vertex-labelled, and distinct ambient components may be joined by unused edges. The proof does not cover more general graph families, nor does it address isomorphism classes or the complexity of arbitrary-graph counting. A relevant 1997 paper was inspected from its abstract and metadata rather than its full text, leaving a bounded publication-priority risk.
