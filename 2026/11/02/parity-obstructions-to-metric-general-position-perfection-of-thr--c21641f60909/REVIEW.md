# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The domain \(k\ge4\) even and \(q\ge3\) odd ensures integral arm length and a legal pair of parameters. A diameter path supplies a clique of \(d+1\) vertices; any larger candidate has representatives from all three arms and therefore a forbidden \(k\)-subset. In a hypothetical \(q\)-coloring the three diameter paths have exactly \(q(k-1)\) vertices, so each color must occupy exactly \(k-1\) of each path. A color distinct from the center then satisfies three pairwise sum equations and hence has half the odd number \(k-1\) of occurrences on an arm, a contradiction. This proves a strict chromatic-versus-clique inequality on the graph itself. The stated small upper bound is supplied by an explicit four-coloring and checked on all geodesics. Finite calculations are not substituted for the infinite argument.

## Originality — PASS (provisional historical priority)

Cody and Detore [1, Definition 3.1; Proposition 3.6; Question 6.7] define the invariant, prove the special positive tree case \((k,d)=(3,2)\), and explicitly ask whether all trees are \((k,d)\)-perfect. The claimed three-arm obstruction is not covered by their path results, tree special case, or cycle classification. Cody and Moore [2] establish \(k\)-general \(d\)-position set results without the counterexample to metric perfection. Chandran et al. [3] address general-position colorings for a different geodesic multiplicity parameter. Searches for tree metric perfection, three-arm spider colorings, higher-order geodesic-hypergraph perfection, and equivalent parameter conventions did not reveal an earlier covering statement. Published-record hits on spider general-position polynomials or edge general position do not imply the chromatic parity obstruction. Search failure is not proof of priority; unpublished or unindexed equivalent results remain possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result answers an explicit structural question in a recently introduced graph-invariant framework, separating the infinite family of three-arm trees from the positive tree result known at a special parameter pair. It gives a two-parameter infinite obstruction with a simple exact small witness and an explanatory parity argument, not a routine recomputation or an unsupported numerical pattern.

## Limits and references

The general-family chromatic value is not claimed to be exact, the small witness is not proved smallest, and the landscape of other trees and parameter pairs remains open. The 13-vertex verifier was executed successfully, but no independent mathematical audit has occurred.
