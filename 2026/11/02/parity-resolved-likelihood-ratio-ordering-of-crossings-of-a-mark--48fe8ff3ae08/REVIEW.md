# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Conditioning on the specified chord leaves a uniform matching. Choosing \(x\) endpoints from each arc, matching them across, and matching remaining endpoints internally yields the full exact mass law, including zero and maximal arc lengths. The factorial quotient \(R_a(x)\) strictly increases because \(b>a+2\); the new upper atom provides strictness even for degenerate smaller support. Sign algebra gives the stated cutoff. Same-parity transitivity and the explicit \(a=1\) versus \(a=2\) counterexample cover the boundaries. Independent exhaustive matching enumeration for \(3\le n\le8\) and further exact rational checks through \(n=64\) support the proof without purporting to certify infinitely many values. The standalone script passed 119,211 assertions.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Acan (2015, Section 2) already gives conditional internal-chord moments and the chord-degree asymptotic, and the earlier 2013 thesis treats related chord-diagram statistics. Chen et al. (2005) treat crossing/nesting symmetry and maxima. Those known statements alone do not imply a monotone likelihood-ratio comparison between distinct chord lengths: first two moments or a limit law do not specify all finite upper tails, and maximum crossing parameters are not marked-chord degrees. Bounded checks for alternative language (random pairing, two-color matchings, cross-arc pairs, total positivity, stochastic orders) identified no equivalent or stronger statement; this does not prove global novelty. The principal residual priority risk is an earlier general two-color-pairing total-positivity theorem.

**Value — PASS.** Conditional chord degree versus chord span is an explicitly studied natural random-graph statistic. The new same-parity likelihood-ratio order controls *every* conditional upper-tail probability and provides a sharp observation-to-span testing threshold, whereas previously examined mean/variance and asymptotic laws cannot by themselves supply finite-sample all-threshold dependence. The adjacent opposite-parity counterexample shows an essential lattice obstruction rather than an arbitrary restricted parameter slice.
