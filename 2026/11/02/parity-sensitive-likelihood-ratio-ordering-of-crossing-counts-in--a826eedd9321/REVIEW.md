# Mathematical review

## Correctness (PASS)

The proof enumerates matching cuts by the exact number of inter-arc pairs, including endpoint and parity constraints. An exchangeable nested-subset exposure gives an explicit \(\pm1\) transition kernel; two upper-tail differences of opposite strict sign prove adjacent incomparability. Dividing factorial probabilities at arc lengths two apart gives a strictly increasing ratio and an additional upper support point. The independent exact verifier checks the formula by exhaustive enumeration through twelve residual endpoints and checks all ordering inequalities to forty. Finite checks corroborate, but are not used as infinite proof.

## Originality (PASS, with limitations)

Acan's 2015 paper is the direct source: it covers the conditional internal-pair mean and variance and an asymptotic crossing-degree law, not the finite distributional ordering. Acan's \(J_a\) and the present \(X_a=a-2J_a\) were explicitly compared; a mean identity or asymptotic concentration does not imply likelihood-ratio order, and cannot yield the adjacent upper-tail sign reversal without new calculations. Other known joint-chord covariance and whole-diagram crossing enumerations involve different random variables. The factorial point masses may be implicit in classical matching counts, so no priority is claimed for those masses in isolation. Residual risk is unlocated historical work on matching cuts or conditional chord-degree laws that already proves the order dichotomy.

## Mathematical value (PASS)

Chord length is a basic predictor of crossing degree in a random chord diagram. Strictly increasing conditional mean can misleadingly suggest stochastic growth, but adjacent lengths in fact remain incomparable for every size; the obstruction is exactly parity. Within the even and odd subsequences, the much stronger likelihood-ratio ordering holds with a closed finite ratio. This is a natural, sharp all-size description of how geometric separation affects a random diagram's local crossing structure, beyond a routine moment calculation.

## Limits and closest literature

The complete comparison for arbitrarily separated opposite-parity arc lengths is not claimed. The source directly treats the same local statistic but only its moments and asymptotic law. Independent review remains outstanding.
