# Review: parity-sharp exact-degree harmonic sampling on the 600-cell

## Correctness — PASS
The exact scope is the 120-point unit 600-cell in \(S^3\), one degree \(\ell\ge0\), and the **unnormalized** Chebyshev character kernel \(K_\ell(x,y)=U_\ell(\langle x,y\rangle)\). The proof reconstructs the kernel as a finite-group character Gram matrix, applies regular-representation orthogonality with eigenvalues \(120m_{\ell,i}/d_i\) and multiplicities \(d_i^2\), and obtains all nonnegative multiplicities from the affine \(E_8\) adjacency/Clebsch–Gordan recurrence. Character vanishing at elements of orders 4, 6 and 10 also proves exact tightness if and only if \(\ell\equiv29\pmod{30}\). The antipodal restriction makes the rank ceiling \(60\). A degree-12 invariant binary form yields an **injective** 12-step inclusion; exact recurrence certificates at six odd and six even base degrees prove all subsequent rank-60 assertions, while degree 27 (rank 56) and degree 58 (rank 59) prove sharpness. Normalization, parity, boundary degrees, and finite versus infinite proof obligations are explicit. The supplied checker was replayed successfully (241 assertion groups, including direct 120-by-120 numerical eigenvalue comparisons for degrees 0 through 14). This is corroboration, not a substitute for the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS under bounded source comparison
The 600-cell/2I identification, the affine \(E_8\) McKay graph and general character orthogonality are acknowledged existing background, not claimed as discoveries. The mathematical assertion assessed is the sharp parity-separated eventual full-interpolation thresholds, the complete exact tight-degree congruence, the 60-step spectral-shift identity, the last exceptional ranks, and the explicit all-degree sampling spectrum in this concrete configuration. Dechant's publicly accessible paper presents the group realization and McKay graph but not these sampling cutoffs. Teh's primary paper calculates Dirac spectra on the quotient \(S^3/2I\), a distinct spectral problem. Earlier related exact-degree sampling work on other four-dimensional point configurations does not imply the 600-cell rank thresholds. Published mathematical-record searches for 600-cell exact-degree sampling, binary icosahedral restrictions and the numbers 29/60 found no matching statement; the nearest published findings concern q-ary Hamming spheres and continuous \(SU(2)\) Fourier projection norms, neither of which dominates the discrete 120-site result. A failed search is not a novelty proof: Backhouse–Gard (1974), Cohan (1958) and Choi–Lee (2018) could not all be inspected in complete text; their abstracts/extracts were considered, and historical equivalent formulations remain a real residual risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The exact-degree interpolation question is natural for data on a highly symmetric antipodal spherical configuration: at what single homogeneous degree does one recover *all* data consistent with antipodal parity? The theorem answers it sharply for each parity, explains why the rank can decrease at intermediate degrees, and gives an all-degree exact spectrum useful for identifying missing modes and nonzero sampling eigenvalues. The final even obstruction at degree 58 is a missing invariant mode rather than a numerical anomaly; the last odd obstruction at degree 27 is four-dimensional. The exact tight-degree law \(\ell\equiv29\pmod{30}\) identifies perfect sampling frames, while the 60-degree matrix translation gives asymptotically tight parity-frame conditioning even at other large degrees. This is more than a reparametrization or one isolated finite table. It does **not** give sharp conditioning constants beyond the derived \(1+O(\ell^{-1})\) Gram bound, and it does not establish independent novelty beyond the stated bounded literature review.
