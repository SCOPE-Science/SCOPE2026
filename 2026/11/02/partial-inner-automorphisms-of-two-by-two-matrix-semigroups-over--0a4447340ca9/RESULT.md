# Partial inner automorphisms of two-by-two matrix semigroups over finite fields

## Finding
For every prime power \(q\), put \(S=M_2(\mathbb F_q)\) with ordinary matrix multiplication, and define \(\operatorname{Inn}(S)\) as the inverse monoid generated by the partial maps below. There are exactly four disjoint types of elements:

1. The full-domain conjugations by \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_q)\), forming the group \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(q)\), of order \(q(q^2-1)\).
2. The partial identity on the singleton \(\{0\}\), which is the zero of the inverse monoid.
3. For any ordered pair of rank-one idempotents \(e,f\), the unique map \(c e\mapsto c f\), defined on \(\mathbb F_q e\). These \(q^2(q+1)^2\) maps form a Brandt component \(B_{q(q+1)}(1)\) with the common zero.
4. For any ordered pair of rank-one square-zero subalgebra lines \(\mathbb F_q N\) and \(\mathbb F_q N'\), and any \(\lambda\in\mathbb F_q^\times\), the map \(cN\mapsto c\lambda N'\). After choosing one nonzero representative of each line, these \((q-1)(q+1)^2\) maps form a Brandt component \(B_{q+1}(\mathbb F_q^\times)\) with the common zero.

The two Brandt components multiply to zero across types, and full conjugations act on the components through their natural actions on the matrix lines. Hence
\[
|\operatorname{Inn}(S)|=q(q^2-1)+1+q^2(q+1)^2+(q-1)(q+1)^2.
\]
In particular, the orders at \(q=2\) and \(q=3\) are \(52\) and \(201\), respectively.

## Assumptions and scope
Here \(q\) is any prime power, including powers of primes greater than one. For \(g,h\in S\), set
\[
D_{g,h}=\{A\in S:(gh)A=A=A(gh)\},\qquad
\phi_{g,h}:D_{g,h}\longrightarrow D_{h,g},\quad A\longmapsto hAg.
\]
The inverse monoid consists of all finite compositions of these partial semigroup isomorphisms, including their partial inverses. Its zero is the partial identity of \(\{0\}\), not an empty map. The source and target of every map are concrete subsets of the matrix semigroup.

## Proof
**Domains.** Write \(P=gh\), \(E=\ker(P-I)\), and \(F=\operatorname{im}(P-I)\). The equations defining \(D_{g,h}\) say that \(\operatorname{im}A\subseteq E\) and \(F\subseteq\ker A\). Therefore
\[
D_{g,h}\cong_{\mathbb F_q}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb F_q}(\mathbb F_q^2/F,E).
\]
If \(d=\dim E\in\{0,1,2\}\), then \(\dim D_{g,h}=d^2\); in particular its dimension is exactly \(0\), \(1\), or \(4\), never \(2\) or \(3\).

If \(d=0\), the domain is \(\{0\}\), so the generator is the zero partial identity. If \(d=2\), then \(P=I\). The square matrices \(g,h\) are mutually inverse, and the generator is global conjugation. Its kernel as a function of the conjugating matrix consists exactly of nonzero scalar matrices, giving the projective general linear group.

Suppose \(d=1\). Then \(D_{g,h}\) is a line spanned by a nonzero rank-one matrix \(A\). Any rank-one two-by-two matrix satisfies \(A^2=(\operatorname{tr}A)A\). Thus the line is either of **idempotent type**, uniquely of the form \(\mathbb F_q e\) with \(e^2=e\) and \(\operatorname{rank}e=1\), or of **square-zero type**, of the form \(\mathbb F_q N\) with \(N^2=0\ne N\). Since \(\phi_{g,h}\) is \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear and a semigroup isomorphism, it preserves the two types. Between idempotent lines the sole possible linear semigroup isomorphism sends \(e\) to \(f\). Between square-zero lines every nonzero scalar multiplier is an isomorphism.

**Realization of every arrow.** Any rank-one idempotent \(e\) occurs as a generating domain: take \(P=e\), for then \(D_P=\mathbb F_qe\). Every nonzero rank-one square-zero \(N\) also occurs: take \(P=I+N\), for then \(D_P=\mathbb F_qN\). The group \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_q)\) is transitive by conjugation on rank-one idempotents and on nonzero rank-one square-zero matrices. Consequently, for any required source line and target arrow, choose a suitable invertible \(U\), put \(g=PU^{-1}\) and \(h=U\), and calculate on \(D_P\):
\[
\phi_{g,h}(A)=UA(PU^{-1})=UAU^{-1}.
\]
This realizes every stated arrow, including every nonzero scalar on square-zero lines.

**Closure and counts.** The intersection of two distinct one-dimensional matrix subspaces is \(\{0\}\). Thus composing partial arrows either yields the unique compatible arrow between the prescribed lines, with scalar multipliers multiplied in the square-zero case, or yields the zero map. Global conjugation takes each type of line to its own type, so its composition with an arrow is another arrow. Hence the displayed union is closed under composition, contains every generator, and consists entirely of generators or global products: it equals \(\operatorname{Inn}(S)\).

A rank-one idempotent is determined by its image line \(L\) and a distinct kernel line \(K\). There are \(q+1\) possible \(L\) and \(q\) possible \(K\), giving \(q(q+1)\) idempotent lines. A rank-one square-zero line is determined by its common image and kernel line, giving \(q+1\) square-zero lines. Counting ordered arrows, invertible scalar labels, the common zero, and global conjugations yields the claimed formula. Every component and its multiplication are thereby classified.

## Verification
The standalone program `verify_linear_inner.py` enumerates all matrices, all ordered pairs of generating matrices, all resulting partial maps, and all pairwise partial-map compositions over the prime fields \(\mathbb F_2\) and \(\mathbb F_3\). It returns `VERIFY_OK` with exactly \(52\) and \(201\) maps, and verifies closure under all \(2704\) and \(40401\) ordered pairs of distinct or repeated maps. The symbolic proof above covers every finite field, whereas the computational checks cover only the two prime fields just named.

## Relationship to prior work
Araújo, Bentz, Kinyon, Konieczny, Malheiro and Mercier (2026, arXiv:2605.00041v1) define this inverse monoid, characterize it for several classes of semigroups, and explicitly ask in Problem 7.9 for the endomorphism monoid of finite-dimensional vector spaces. The present classification answers its first nontrivial matrix dimension over all finite fields. Its general definition and several earlier structural results were already publicly available in the longer June 2024 preprint arXiv:2301.04252v2; no novelty is attributed to that framework. The 2024 Problem 8.13 about conjugacy of linear endomorphisms is related but does not determine the inverse monoid classified here. Neither accessible full text states or implies the concrete two-dimensional Brandt-component classification.

## Limitations
The theorem treats dimension exactly two and finite fields; it does not establish the corresponding structure in dimension three or higher, or over arbitrary infinite fields. The 2026 question was preceded by the broader 2024 manuscript, so novelty of the exact formulation is qualified. Previously unidentified general results concerning inverse monoids of matrix-semigroup partial automorphisms may cover the classification; absolute priority and independent auditing have not been established.
