# Perfectness of every positive-index Lucas-shift numerical semigroup

## Finding
Let the Lucas integers satisfy \(L_0=2\), \(L_1=1\), and \(L_{j+2}=L_{j+1}+L_j\). For every integer \(a\geq 0\), set
\[
S_a=\langle L_a,\ L_a+L_0,\ L_a+L_1,\ L_a+L_2,\ldots\rangle\subseteq\mathbb N_0.
\]
A *gap* of a numerical semigroup \(S\) is an integer \(g\geq1\) outside \(S\). It is *isolated* when both \(g-1\) and \(g+1\) belong to \(S\); a semigroup without isolated gaps is *perfect*.

**Theorem.** For every integer \(a\geq1\), \(S_a\) has no isolated gaps. The unique nonperfect member of the family indexed by \(a\geq0\) is \(S_0=\langle2,3\rangle\), whose isolated gap is \(1\). In particular, the family has perfect numerical semigroups of unbounded embedding dimension (the source establishes \(e(S_a)=a+1\) for \(a\geq2\)).

## Assumptions and scope
All indices and semigroups are over nonnegative integers. The result concerns exactly the family above, not the related family generated by the shifted Lucas terms without \(L_a\), nor arbitrary recurrence-generated numerical semigroups.

For \(a\geq2\), put \(m=L_a\). Define the increasing modified Lucas coins \(w_0=1\), \(w_1=2\), and \(w_j=L_j\) for \(j\geq2\). By the Zeckendorf–Lucas representation lemma of Robles-Pérez and Rosales [1, Lemma 4.1], each \(r\geq0\) has a unique expansion
\[
r=\sum_{j\geq0}d_jw_j,
\quad d_j\in\{0,1\},\quad
 d_jd_{j+1}=0\ (j\geq0),\quad d_0d_2=0,
\]
with finitely many nonzero digits, and the minimum number of coins in any coin representation is \(\beta(r)=\sum_jd_j\). Their Apéry formula [1, Theorem 4.6] gives the least element in the residue class \(r\) modulo \(m\) as
\[
A_r=r+m\beta(r)\quad(0\leq r<m).
\]
Thus, with \(q\geq0\) and \(0\leq r<m\), membership is exactly \(qm+r\in S_a\) if and only if \(q\geq\beta(r)\).

## Proof
**Digit lemma.** For every integer \(r\geq1\), the following hold:

(i) If the lowest digit \(d_0(r)=0\), then \(\beta(r-1)\geq\beta(r)\).

(ii) If \(d_0(r)=1\), then \(\beta(r+1)=\beta(r)\).

For (i), choose the least index \(j\geq1\) with \(d_j(r)=1\). Construct an expansion of \(r-1\) by replacing the single coin \(w_j\) in the expansion of \(r\) by the canonical expansion of \(w_j-1\). The replacement uses only indices below \(j\) (because the coin values increase), and the next original nonzero digit, if any, has index at least \(j+2\). Consequently the resulting digits are still nonadjacent and still respect the exceptional prohibition on indices \(0\) and \(2\); they form a canonical expansion of \(r-1\). Its digit count is \(\beta(r)-1+\beta(w_j-1)\), which is at least \(\beta(r)\) because \(j\geq1\) and \(w_j\geq2\). This proves (i).

For (ii), the restrictions \(d_0d_1=d_0d_2=0\) force \(d_1(r)=d_2(r)=0\). Replace the coin \(w_0=1\) by \(w_1=2\). All remaining nonzero indices are at least \(3\), so the resulting digits are canonical for \(r+1\) and have exactly the same count. Uniqueness yields (ii).

Together, these statements show that there is **no strict local maximum** of \(\beta\) at any \(r\geq1\): either \(\beta(r)\leq\beta(r-1)\) or \(\beta(r)=\beta(r+1)\).

**Endpoint lemma.** For every integer \(a\geq2\), the canonical expansion of \(L_a-1\) has no digit at index \(0\). Indeed, the base cases are \(L_2-1=w_1=2\) and \(L_3-1=w_2=3\). For \(a\geq4\), the recurrence
\[
L_a-1=L_{a-1}+(L_{a-2}-1)
\]
adds index \(a-1\) to the canonical expansion of \(L_{a-2}-1\), whose greatest index is \(a-3\), preserving all admissibility restrictions. In particular, the least index is \(1\) or \(2\), never \(0\). By digit lemma (i),
\[
\beta(m-1)\leq\beta(m-2).
\]

**Exclusion of isolated gaps.** Let \(g=qm+r\geq1\), with \(q\geq0\) and \(0\leq r<m\). If \(r=0\), then \(g\) is a multiple of \(m\) and belongs to \(S_a\). If \(1\leq r\leq m-2\), an isolated gap would force
\[
\beta(r)>q\geq\max\{\beta(r-1),\beta(r+1)\},
\]
contradicting the no-strict-maximum property. If \(r=m-1\), then \(g+1=(q+1)m\in S_a\), while requiring \(g-1\in S_a\) and \(g\notin S_a\) forces
\[
\beta(m-1)>q\geq\beta(m-2),
\]
contradicting the endpoint lemma. Therefore \(S_a\) is perfect for all \(a\geq2\).

Finally, \(S_1=\langle1\rangle=\mathbb N_0\) is perfect. Conversely \(S_0=\langle2,3\rangle\) excludes \(1\) but contains both \(0\) and \(2\), so its unique gap is isolated. This completes the classification. \(\square\)

## Relationship to prior work
Robles-Pérez and Rosales [1] establish the Lucas-shift family, its minimal generators, the Zeckendorf–Lucas coin system, exact Apéry sets, Frobenius numbers, genus, and Wilf's inequality. They do not state a classification of its isolated gaps or perfectness. The present claim relies explicitly on [1, Lemma 4.1 and Theorem 4.6], adding the digit-adjacency lemma and the consequent perfectness classification.

Moreno-Frías and Rosales [2] study perfect numerical semigroups and their covarieties at fixed Frobenius number, including general membership algorithms. Their inspected full text does not identify the Lucas-shift family or imply this uniform classification without further family-specific arguments. The property of being perfect and its formulation via isolated gaps are standard, not claimed as new.

## Limitations
This is a self-review, not an independent audit or a certification of bibliographic priority. An unindexed theorem or unexamined journal revision could contain an equivalent statement. The claim does not classify isolated gaps of semigroups generated by the shifted Lucas terms alone or extend to arbitrary Lucas-type recurrences. Independent reproductions of the proof are welcome; finite tests alone would not prove the theorem.
