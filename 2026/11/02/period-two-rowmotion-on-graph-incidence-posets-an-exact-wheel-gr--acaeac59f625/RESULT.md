# Period-two rowmotion on graph incidence posets: an exact wheel-graph census

## Finding

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite connected simple graph with at least one edge. Let \(P(G)\) be its vertex-edge incidence poset, with vertices minimal and each edge immediately above its two endpoints. Let \(c_2(G)\) be the number of orbits of length **exactly two** under ordinary rowmotion on lower order ideals of \(P(G)\). Let \(B(G)\) be the number of edge two-colourings \(E\to\{0,1\}\) in which every vertex is incident to an edge of each colour. The colours are distinguished.

**Structural theorem.** For every such graph,
\[
 c_2(G)=\mathbf{1}_{G\text{ bipartite}}+\frac{B(G)}2.
\]
More precisely, every two-cycle belongs to exactly one of two disjoint types: (i) the two ideals selecting opposite classes of a graph bipartition, with no edge elements selected; or (ii) the two ideals selecting all vertices and complementary spanning edge covers. No other two-cycles exist.

**Wheel theorem.** Let \(W_{r+1}=C_r\vee K_1\) be the simple wheel with \(r\ge3\) rim vertices and one hub. With Lucas numbers specified by \(L_0=2\), \(L_1=1\), and \(L_{j+2}=L_{j+1}+L_j\),
\[
\boxed{c_2(W_{r+1})=\frac{3^r+(-1)^r-2L_r}{2}\qquad(r\ge3).}
\]
The first values for rim sizes \(r=3,4,5,6,7,8\) are \(9,34,110,347,1064,3234\). All these two-cycles are of the spanning-edge-cover type. In particular, their count grows as \(3^r/2+O(((1+\sqrt5)/2)^r)\).

## Assumptions and scope

All graphs are undirected, finite, simple and labelled; there is no quotient by graph automorphisms. A lower order ideal contains an edge element only if it contains both endpoint vertex elements. The rowmotion map \(\rho\) sends an ideal \(I\) to the lower ideal generated by the minimal elements outside \(I\). Orbits are counted without distinguishing their two temporal phases. An edge cover is a set of edges incident to every vertex. A two-colouring contributing to \(B(G)\) need not be a *proper edge-colouring*; multiple incident edges may share a colour, but both colours must occur at each vertex.

The all-graphs statement requires connectivity and \(|E|\ge1\). The wheel formula requires \(r\ge3\), so that the rim is a simple cycle and the wheel has triangles. This is ordinary (Boolean) rowmotion, not birational rowmotion.

## Proof

**1. Exact dynamical encoding.** For \(S\subseteq V\), write \(E(S)=\{uv\in E:u,v\in S\}\), and for \(F\subseteq E\), write \(\partial F\) for all endpoints of edges in \(F\). Every ideal corresponds uniquely to \((S,T)\) with \(S\subseteq V\) and \(T\subseteq E(S)\). The vertex elements minimal outside the ideal are precisely \(V\setminus S\). An edge element outside it is minimal precisely when both endpoints already lie in \(S\), so the minimal excluded edge set is \(E(S)\setminus T\). Consequently
\[
\rho(S,T)=\left((V\setminus S)\cup\partial(E(S)\setminus T),\ E(S)\setminus T\right). \tag{1}
\]
This identity holds for every ideal, including the empty and full ideals.

**2. Dichotomy for a two-cycle.** Suppose \(\rho(S,T)=(S',U)\) and \(\rho(S',U)=(S,T)\). Formula (1) gives \(U=E(S)\setminus T\) and \(T=E(S')\setminus U\), whence
\[
E(S)=T\sqcup U=E(S'). \tag{2}
\]
Also \(S\cup S'=V\) since \(V\setminus S\subseteq S'\). If \(S\cap S'\) were nonempty and proper, connectivity would provide an edge from a vertex in \(S\cap S'\) to a vertex in its complement. Because \(S\cup S'=V\), the other endpoint lies in exactly one of \(S,S'\). The edge would therefore belong to exactly one of \(E(S),E(S')\), contrary to (2). Hence either \(S\cap S'=\varnothing\) or \(S=S'=V\).

If \(S\cap S'=\varnothing\), the sets partition \(V\). Equation (2) now forces \(E(S)=E(S')=\varnothing\), so \(T=U=\varnothing\). Thus \(S,S'\) form the two independent classes of a bipartition of \(G\). Conversely the corresponding two ideals exchange under (1). A connected bipartite graph has exactly one such unordered pair of ideals.

If \(S=S'=V\), (1) implies \(\partial U=V\) and \(\partial T=V\); in view of (2), \(T,U\) partition the entire edge set into two edge covers. Conversely, any edge colouring using both named colours at every vertex yields the exchanging ideals \((V,T)\leftrightarrow(V,E\setminus T)\). Complementing the colours pairs up these colourings without fixed points, so they contribute \(B(G)/2\) two-cycles. There can be no rowmotion fixed ideal in a nonempty poset: any proper ideal has a minimal excluded element outside itself that enters its image, while the full ideal maps to the empty ideal. This establishes that all the pairs identified have primitive period two, and proves the structural theorem.

**3. Count the edge bicolourings of a wheel.** Index rim edges by \(i\pmod r\), and give edge \(i\) colour \(t_i\in\{0,1\}\). Let \(s_i\) denote the colour of the spoke at rim vertex \(i\). That rim vertex must see both colours among \(t_{i-1},t_i,s_i\). If its two rim-edge colours differ, either value of \(s_i\) is allowed; if they agree, precisely the opposite value is allowed. Ignoring the hub condition initially, a fixed rim word \(t\) therefore permits \(2^{q(t)}\) spoke assignments, where \(q(t)\) is the number of rim positions at which successive rim-edge colours differ.

The transfer matrix for equal/different successive rim colours is
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Summing around the labelled rim counts each cyclic edge word exactly once, and yields
\[
\sum_{t\in\{0,1\}^r}2^{q(t)}=\operatorname{tr}(M^r)=3^r+(-1)^r, \tag{3}
\]
since the eigenvalues of \(M\) are \(3\) and \(-1\).

The hub violates the two-colour condition exactly when **all** spokes have one common colour. Fix that colour as \(0\). At a rim vertex the two incident rim edges then cannot both be colour \(0\). Thus the set of rim edges coloured \(0\) is an independent set of the cycle on the \(r\) edge positions. The number of independent subsets of \(C_r\) is the Lucas number \(L_r\): either omit a fixed position and count independent subsets of a path on \(r-1\) positions, or include it and omit its neighbours, giving \(F_{r+1}+F_{r-1}=L_r\) in standard Fibonacci notation. All spokes equal to \(1\) contributes another \(L_r\) forbidden assignments, disjoint from the first case. Therefore
\[
B(W_{r+1})=3^r+(-1)^r-2L_r. \tag{4}
\]
Every wheel with \(r\ge3\) contains a triangle formed by the hub and consecutive rim vertices, hence is nonbipartite. Substituting (4) in the structural theorem proves the boxed wheel formula and the stated asymptotic.

**4. Boundary comparison.** For the simple cycle \(C_n\), \(n\ge3\), a two-colouring whose two colour classes are edge covers must alternate around the cycle, which exists only for even \(n\). There are then exactly two named colourings, giving one edge-cover two-cycle, alongside one graph-bipartition two-cycle. Consequently \(c_2(C_n)=2\) for even \(n\), and \(c_2(C_n)=0\) for odd \(n\). This illustrates the two mechanisms separately from the wheel case.

## Verification

Two standalone programs using only the Python standard library accompany this result.

Run `python3 verify_rowmotion.py` to enumerate **all** order ideals of the wheel incidence posets for each rim size \(3\le r\le8\). It computes rowmotion directly from minimal excluded poset elements and tests primitive period two. The tested numbers of ideals are \(113,480,2098,9331,41938,189664\), and the two-cycle counts match \(9,34,110,347,1064,3234\).

Run `python3 verify_edgecovers.py` to count colourings by a separate edge-incidence representation for all rim sizes \(3\le r\le10\), compare them with equation (4), and independently check the general structural theorem for **every connected labelled simple graph** on two, three or four vertices (respectively \(1,4,38\) graphs). The programs are finite cross-checks, not substitutes for the all-graph and all-wheel proofs above.

## Relationship to prior work

Rowmotion is the established order-ideal permutation of a finite poset. Striker and Williams, *Promotion and Rowmotion* (originally public August 4, 2011), develops promotion/rowmotion equivalences for special poset families, rather than a classification of two-cycles for arbitrary graph incidence posets. Grinberg and Roby, *Iterative Properties of Birational Rowmotion I*, examines broad dynamics and skeletal posets; a theorem asserting finite period on a class of posets does not furnish the present period-two orbit census. Elizalde, Plante, Roby and Sagan, *Rowmotion on fences*, studies fence posets, including incidence-poset structures arising from paths, with orbit tilings and homomesy; this is not a wheel-incidence-poset enumeration.

The new statement is specifically the two-cycle/edge-cover correspondence for all connected graphs and its exact Lucas-number census on wheel incidence posets. Ordinary graph two-colourings, Lucas numbers, cyclic-independent-set counts and the rowmotion definition are background ingredients, not separately claimed as new. A prior equivalent formulation could exist under terminology such as two-cover edge partitions or fixed points of the square of rowmotion; no absolute priority is asserted.

## Limitations

No claim is made about longer rowmotion orbits, their least common multiple or the full orbit structure. The theorem concerns the vertex-edge incidence poset of a graph, not the graph's independent-set or colouring reconfiguration graph. The transfer argument counts named edge bicolourings only as intermediate data; it is not an assertion about proper edge-colourings. Exhaustive verification stops at the finite ranges stated. Equivalence with an unindexed earlier theorem remains an originality risk. Independent scholarly validation has not been performed.
