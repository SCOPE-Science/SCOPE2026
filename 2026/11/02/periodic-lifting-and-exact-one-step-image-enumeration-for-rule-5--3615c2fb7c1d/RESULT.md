# Periodic lifting and exact one-step image enumeration for Rule 54

## Finding

Let \(F_n:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}^n\) be synchronous elementary cellular automaton Rule 54 on an indexed cyclic ring with \(n\ge1\). The local rule is \(f(a,b,c)=b\oplus(a\lor c)\), with indices interpreted modulo \(n\). Let \(\mathcal P=\{\texttt{10110},\texttt{10101},\texttt{01101},\texttt{10111101}\}\). Define a circular output as \(\mathcal P\)-avoiding if none of these four strings appears in its doubly infinite periodic extension.

**Exact periodic lifting.** For every \(n\ge2\), an indexed binary ring state \(y\) has an \(n\)-periodic Rule-54 predecessor if and only if \(y\) circularly avoids \(\mathcal P\). For \(n=1\), both one-symbol states avoid \(\mathcal P\), but only `0` has a one-symbol predecessor. Thus the sole failure of period-preserving lifting among these cyclically avoiding periodic outputs occurs at fundamental period one, at the all-one configuration. The uniform-one bi-infinite output is genuine: the length-two alternating input `01` maps to `11`.

**Exact census.** With \(a_n=|\operatorname{Im}F_n|\) for \(n\ge1\) and
\[
Q(z)=(1+z^2)(1-2z+z^3-z^4)=1-2z+z^2-z^3-z^4+z^5-z^6,
\]
we have the all-length identity
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}a_n z^n=-z\frac{Q'(z)}{Q(z)}-z
=\frac{z(1+2z^2+5z^3-4z^4+5z^5+z^6)}{Q(z)}.
\]
The indexed-state counts for lengths \(1\) through \(16\) are
`1, 2, 5, 14, 22, 41, 79, 150, 275, 512, 959, 1793, 3343, 6239, 11650, 21750`.
If \(\lambda\approx1.8667603991738622\) is the positive root of \(\lambda^4-2\lambda^3+\lambda-1=0\), then \(a_n=\lambda^n+O(1)\); in particular the fraction of one-step reachable states is asymptotic to \((\lambda/2)^n\).

## Assumptions and scope

Outputs are labeled binary rings, not necklaces or bracelets. Ring length \(n=1\) uses the same cell at all three input positions; length \(n=2\) likewise uses the periodic-neighbor convention without treating its two neighbors as independent cells. Each output is counted once, regardless of predecessor multiplicity. Occurrence of a forbidden pattern is tested against infinite periodic continuation, including patterns longer than the ring.

For a letter \(b\in\{0,1\}\), define a Boolean four-by-four matrix \(T_b\) on the overlapping predecessor pair states \(00,01,10,11\). There is an edge from \(uv\) to \(vw\), labelled \(b\), precisely when \(f(u,v,w)=b\). Writing each Boolean row as a four-bit mask, with bit order \(00,01,10,11\), gives
\[
T_0=(1,8,0,12),\qquad T_1=(2,4,3,0).
\]
Boolean matrix multiplication is composition of binary relations.

## Proof

**1. The exclusion direction.** The Boolean products \(T_{p_1}\cdots T_{p_k}\) are zero matrices for each of the four words \(p\in\mathcal P\). Therefore a cyclic output containing one of them cannot label a closed walk in the predecessor-pair graph. Conversely, a word \(y=y_0\cdots y_{n-1}\) has an \(n\)-periodic predecessor if and only if \(T_{y_0}\cdots T_{y_{n-1}}\) has a nonzero diagonal entry: a diagonal pair state closes a length-\(n\) walk whose overlapping edges specify a periodic binary input. This equivalence holds even for \(n=1,2\).

**2. An exact finite-state certificate for the lifting direction.** Build the deterministic forbidden-pattern prefix automaton. Its states are the 13 distinct proper prefixes of members of \(\mathcal P\), including the empty string:

`'', '0', '1', '01', '10', '011', '101', '0110', '1010', '1011', '10111', '101111', '1011110'`.

On reading \(b\), reject if adding \(b\) completes a forbidden word; otherwise move to the longest suffix which is one of these proper prefixes. Every circularly avoiding word corresponds to a unique closed walk of this deterministic automaton: the state after each position is the longest such suffix of the infinite periodic past. Conversely a closed walk can be repeated to give an avoiding periodic output. Uniqueness follows because the state is determined by at most seven previous symbols, regardless of its initial state.

For **each** of the 13 initial automaton states \(s\), enumerate all reachable triples \((q,R,c)\), initialized at \((s,I,0)\). Here \(q\) is the current prefix state, \(R\) is the Boolean product of predecessor matrices along the path, and \(c\in\{0,1,2\}\) is path length capped at two. Each allowed letter \(b\) makes the exact transition
\[
(q,R,c)\longmapsto(\delta(q,b),RT_b,\min\{2,c+1\}).
\]
During this exhaustive finite-state closure, every transition returning to \(q=s\) with \(c=2\) has a nonzero diagonal in its resulting relation. The 13 closed search sizes, in the state order above, are
`144, 131, 132, 119, 103, 112, 84, 67, 67, 65, 119, 112, 67`.
All transitions and all visited states are regenerated by `verify.py`; no arbitrary depth cutoff is used, because duplicate triples are merged and the transition rule is deterministic. Consequently **every** avoiding circular word of length at least two has a periodic predecessor. At length one \(f(0,0,0)=f(1,1,1)=0\), proving the unique exception.

**3. Exact enumeration.** Let \(A\) be the integer adjacency matrix of the 13-state prefix automaton, with each allowed letter contributing one edge. Closed paths of length \(n\) correspond one-to-one to indexed \(\mathcal P\)-avoiding circular words, including length one. Hence their number is \(b_n=\operatorname{tr}(A^n)\). Exact Newton identities, applied to the first 13 traces computed by ordinary integer multiplication of the explicit matrix, give
\[
\det(I-zA)=1-2z+z^2-z^3-z^4+z^5-z^6=Q(z).
\]
This is an exact 13-dimensional determinant certificate, not a recurrence guessed from initial counts. The identity
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\operatorname{tr}(A^n)z^n
=-z\,\frac{d}{dz}\log\det(I-zA)
\]
is formal over the rationals and yields the stated generating function after subtracting \(z\), for the unique excluded length-one output. The nonzero eigenvalues of \(A\) are the four roots of \(t^4-2t^3+t-1\) and \(\pm i\). The positive real root \(\lambda\) is strictly dominant; the other quartic roots have modulus below one, while \(\pm i\) have modulus one. Summing eigenvalue powers proves \(a_n=\lambda^n+O(1)\).

## Relationship to prior work

Jen (1989, Proposition 2) already established the four forbidden words for linear finite-block Rule-54 outputs and proved their finite forbidden-basis property. The forbidden list itself is **not** claimed as new. Martínez, Adamatzky and McIntosh (2014, Section 3) developed de Bruijn, subset and cycle diagrams for Rule 54 and exhibited finite Garden-of-Eden patterns. These results concern allowed linear factors and glider structures; neither inspected argument supplies the exact same-period lifting classification for **all** binary ring lengths or the all-length rational count above. Salo (2022/2023) supplies the general framework of weak periodic-point lifting and finite-state decidability; no specialized Rule-54 formula matching this theorem was found in the inspected material. Earlier results for other elementary rules likewise do not imply these Rule-54 assertions.

## Limitations

This is a one-step binary radius-one periodic-ring result, not a characterization of eventual images, trajectories, attractors, exact predecessor multiplicities, or noise resilience. The explicit proof depends on a small exact finite-state computation; every state update and certificate assertion is independently reproducible from the attached standard-library program. Historical priority is qualified by the possibility of an unindexed or inaccessible equivalent calculation, especially in older finite-state cellular-automata tables. No independent mathematical audit has been performed.
