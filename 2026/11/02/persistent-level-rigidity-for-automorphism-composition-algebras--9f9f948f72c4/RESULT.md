# Persistent-level rigidity for automorphism composition algebras on discrete Hardy spaces

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal T\) be the infinite \((q+1)\)-homogeneous rooted tree, \(q\ge2\). On its discrete Hardy space \(\mathbb T_p\), \(1\le p<\infty\), let \(\mathcal A_p\) be the norm-closed unital algebra generated by composition operators \(C_g f=f\circ g\) with root-preserving graph automorphisms \(g\). Then, for **every** \(A\in\mathcal A_p\) and every integer \(k\ge1\),
\[
 a_k(A)=\inf_{\operatorname{rank}R<k}\|A-R\|=\operatorname{dist}(A,\mathcal K(\mathbb T_p))=\|A\|,
 \qquad \sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(A)=\sigma(A).
\]
Here essential spectrum means Fredholm essential spectrum. In particular, **no nonzero finite linear combination of these automorphism-induced compositions is compact**: the quotient homomorphism into the Calkin algebra is isometric and spectrally faithful on \(\mathcal A_p\).

For \(g\), let \(\Omega(g)\) be the union of the sets of complex roots of unity whose orders are the cycle lengths of \(g\) on some finite level. Then, for all \(1\le p<\infty\),
\[
 \sigma(C_g)=\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(C_g)=\overline{\Omega(g)}.
\]
This is the entire unit circle if and only if \(g\) has infinite order; otherwise it is the finite union of roots given by its cycle lengths. For \(p=2\) and every complex polynomial \(P\),
\[
 \|P(C_g)\|=\|P(C_g)\|_{\mathrm{ess}}=\max_{z\in\overline{\Omega(g)}}|P(z)|.
\]
Consequently, for any root-preserving \(g,h\), setting \(r=h^{-1}\circ g\),
\[
 \|C_g-C_h\|=\|C_g-C_h\|_{\mathrm{ess}}=\sup_{z\in\Omega(r)}|1-z|.
\]
On \(\mathbb T_2\), every pair \(g\ne h\) therefore obeys the **sharp uniform gap**
\[
 \|C_g-C_h\|\ge\sqrt3.
\]
Equality holds precisely when all cycles of \(r\) on all levels have length \(1\) or \(3\), with at least one 3-cycle. If \(r\) has an even-length cycle or infinite order, the norm is \(2\); otherwise, if the largest (necessarily odd) cycle length is \(L\), the norm is \(2\cos(\pi/(2L))\), with \(L=1\) giving zero. Indeed, child-subtree transpositions supported at successively deeper vertices converge pointwise to the identity automorphism, but their induced compositions remain at norm-distance \(2\) from the identity. These conclusions are parts of one persistent-level rigidity theorem.

## Assumptions and scope
There is one root \(o\), its degree is \(q+1\), and every other vertex has exactly \(q\) descendants at the next level. Let \(D_n=\{v:\operatorname{dist}(o,v)=n\}\), and \(c_0=1\), \(c_n=(q+1)q^{n-1}\) for \(n\ge1\). The complex Banach space is
\[
 \mathbb T_p=\left\{f:\mathcal T\to\mathbb C:\|f\|_p=\sup_{n\ge0}\left(\frac1{c_n}\sum_{v\in D_n}|f(v)|^p\right)^{1/p}<\infty\right\}.
\]
Every root-preserving graph automorphism permutes each \(D_n\) and preserves ancestry. The norm-closed algebra \(\mathcal A_p\) is the closure of the finite linear span of these composition operators, since products and inverses remain in this family. No statement is asserted for arbitrary self-maps of the tree, and the sharp \(\sqrt3\) operator-norm gap is asserted only for \(p=2\).

## Proof
**Step 1 (isometric persistence).** Set \(H_n=\mathbb C^{D_n}\), with its normalized \(\ell_p\)-norm, so \(\mathbb T_p\) is isometric to the \(\ell_\infty\)-sum of the finite-dimensional \(H_n\). For \(m\ge n\), define \(J_{n,m}:H_n\to H_m\) by \((J_{n,m}u)(v)=u(\operatorname{anc}_n(v))\). Every vertex at level \(n\) has the same number of descendants at level \(m\), so \(J_{n,m}\) is an isometry. Root-preserving automorphisms commute with taking ancestors, hence their finite-level permutation matrices obey \(U_{g,m}J_{n,m}=J_{n,m}U_{g,n}\). The same identities hold for any \(A\in\mathcal A_p\): it is level diagonal, with finite-level restrictions \(A_n\), \(A_mJ_{n,m}=J_{n,m}A_n\), and \(\|A\|=\sup_n\|A_n\|\).

**Step 2 (flat approximation profile).** Fix \(n\) and a unit \(u\in H_n\). For each \(m\ge n\), place \(J_{n,m}u\) only in the \(m\)-th level of \(\mathbb T_p\). The set \(E_{n,u}\) of **all bounded scalar combinations** of these level vectors is a closed subspace isometric to scalar \(\ell_\infty\), with \(\|Ax\|=\|A_nu\|\|x\|\) for every \(x\in E_{n,u}\). Every finite-rank map \(R\) has nonzero kernel on this infinite-dimensional subspace, so \(\|A-R\|\ge\|A_nu\|\). For every compact \(K\), compactness precludes a uniform lower bound on \(\|Kx\|\) for unit vectors in \(E_{n,u}\), and thus \(\|A-K\|\ge\|A_nu\|\). Taking suprema over \(u,n\) proves all the norm, approximation-number, and essential-norm identities.

**Step 3 (spectral equality without normality).** Suppose \(A-\lambda I\) is Fredholm. A bounded left parametrix \(B\) and compact \(K\) satisfy \(B(A-\lambda I)=I-K\). For any unit \(u\in H_n\), the subspace \(E_{n,u}\) contains unit vectors on which \(K\) is arbitrarily small, while \(\|(A-\lambda I)x\|=\|(A_n-\lambda I)u\|\). The parametrix identity therefore forces \(\|(A_n-\lambda I)u\|\ge\|B\|^{-1}\), uniformly in \(n,u\). Every \(A_n-\lambda I\) is a square finite-dimensional matrix, so it is invertible with inverse norm at most \(\|B\|\). The levelwise inverse defines a bounded inverse for \(A-\lambda I\) on the \(\ell_\infty\)-sum. Thus Fredholmness implies invertibility, and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(A)=\sigma(A)\).

**Step 4 (automorphism cycles and sharp gap).** On \(H_n\), \(C_g\) is a permutation matrix, whose eigenvalues are precisely the roots of unity associated with its permutation cycles. Every finite-level eigenvector extends isometrically by \(J_{n,m}\) to arbitrarily high levels, so its eigenvalue persists with infinite multiplicity. If \(g\) has finite order, all cycle lengths are bounded, so \(\Omega(g)\) is finite. Every level block is annihilated by the product of \(z-\zeta\) over \(\zeta\in\Omega(g)\); hence \(\prod_{\zeta\in\Omega(g)}(C_g-\zeta I)=0\). This excludes other spectral values, while the level eigenvectors supply each \(\zeta\in\Omega(g)\). If its order is infinite, the cycle lengths are unbounded; roots of unity of unbounded orders are dense in the unit circle, while the isometry and invertibility of \(C_g\) put its spectrum inside that circle. The polynomial spectral-mapping theorem yields the asserted polynomial spectral sets. For \(p=2\), every finite-level permutation is unitary, giving the polynomial norm formula by finite-dimensional normality and the levelwise supremum.

The identity \(C_g-C_h=(C_{h^{-1}\circ g}-I)C_h\) and surjective isometry of \(C_h\) reduce pairwise norms to \(I-C_r\). For a cycle of length \(\ell\), the largest chord \(|1-z|\) among its roots is \(2\) when \(\ell\) is even, and \(2\cos(\pi/(2\ell))\) when \(\ell\) is odd. Every nonidentity permutation has a cycle of length at least two, and the smallest nonzero such chord maximum is \(\sqrt3\), attained by a 3-cycle. A permutation of three root children, fixing all other root children and propagating the permutation to descendants, realizes equality for every \(q\ge2\). This also proves the precise equality condition.

## Verification
A standalone deterministic checker, `CHECK.py`, enumerates every permutation of orders \(1\) through \(7\), compares its computed matrix singular norm with the root-of-unity chord formula, and checks four independent finite-level descendant intertwiners for noncommuting automorphisms. It reports 5,928 finite examples passing. The infinite-dimensional approximation, compactness, and Fredholm conclusions follow from the written proof, not from this finite test.

## Relationship to prior work
Muthukumar and Ponnusamy (2016, 2020) prove norms, boundedness, isometries, and compactness criteria for individual discrete-Hardy tree composition operators, including unit norm for root-fixing automorphisms; they do not establish norm/spectral rigidity for their entire closed linear algebra. Liu and Ma (2026) characterize when **arbitrary finite linear combinations** are compact and exhibit cancellations for general symbols; the present theorem supplies a quantitative *absence of nontrivial compact cancellation* specifically inside the root-fixing automorphism algebra, along with flat approximation numbers and exact Fredholm spectral preservation. Allen, Le, and Pons (2022) classify spectra for automorphic composition on analytic disk spaces, not the discrete tree algebra. Generic sequence-space results about translation-invariant flat \(s\)-numbers are related in method but concern different actions and spaces.

## Limitations
The algebra consists exclusively of operators generated by root-preserving tree automorphisms, not general tree self-maps. The sharper chord-norm classification is proved only for the Hilbert-level exponent \(p=2\). The theorem is based on repeating finite-level representations, not on a classification of every bounded operator on \(\mathbb T_p\). An equivalent abstract statement could exist under terminology such as persistent direct-limit representations; no independent external audit or definitive publication-priority certification has been performed.
