# Scientific review

## Correctness
PASS (self-review). A level-preserving automorphism acts by a finite permutation on each normalized level. The explicit ancestor-pullback isometry intertwines every larger level. Repeating any fixed unit level vector indefinitely gives an infinite-dimensional subspace with a constant operator amplification ratio, proving sharp distance from finite-rank and compact maps. A Fredholm left parametrix bounds below every finite-level block uniformly, which forces a globally bounded blockwise inverse. The Hilbert exponent's sharp gap follows from the exact chord maximum for each finite permutation cycle. The proof includes the infinite-order/unbounded-cycle equivalence.

## Originality
PASS (best-knowledge). The 2016 and 2020 Muthukumar–Ponnusamy papers provide the unit norm of a single root-fixing automorphism composition and other basic operator properties. The 2026 Liu–Ma paper gives compactness criteria for general linear combinations but not a quantitative, algebra-wide Calkin isometry, exact full Fredholm spectrum, or sharp Hilbert-level pairwise gap. Allen–Le–Pons classify disk-symbol spectra, not root-tree operators. Published related flat-s-number results for convolution and sequence-space multipliers concern different operators; no direct implication of the complete algebra theorem was located. An equivalent general direct-limit operator-algebra result remains a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The 2026 work emphasizes cancellation among finite composition sums. In the natural symmetry-generated subalgebra, the theorem proves the opposite extremal rigidity: no nonzero combination can be compact, even approximately improved by finite-rank maps. It quantitatively separates distinct root automorphisms despite their arbitrarily deep local perturbations and completely determines the spectrum-versus-essential-spectrum behavior of every element of the closed algebra. This is a structural classification, not a mere finite permutation computation.
