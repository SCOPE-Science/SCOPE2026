# Persistent three-switch richness–frequency dependence with three unequal boxes

## Finding

Allocate \(n\) independent labeled balls to three labeled boxes with probabilities \((9/13,3/13,1/13)\). Let \(K\) be the number of nonempty boxes and \(M_r\) the number containing exactly \(r\) balls, for \(1\le r\le n\).

At \(n=14\), the exact covariance-sign sequence \(\operatorname{sgn}\operatorname{Cov}(K,M_r)\), indexed by \(r=1,\ldots,14\), is
\[
(+,+,+,+,-,+,+,+,+,+,-,-,-,-).
\]
Thus it has exactly three strict sign changes. More importantly, for **every integer \(t\ge15\)**, at \(n=10t\) there are four increasing frequencies \(t,4t,6t,9t\) with
\[
\operatorname{sgn}\big(\operatorname{Cov}(K,M_t),\operatorname{Cov}(K,M_{4t}),\operatorname{Cov}(K,M_{6t}),\operatorname{Cov}(K,M_{9t})\big)=(+,-,+,-).
\]
Hence a single fixed three-box probability vector exhibits at least three sign changes at arbitrarily large sample sizes. Both assertions persist on a sufficiently small open neighborhood of the displayed probability vector (with the same indices and \(t\ge15\)).

For two positive-probability boxes and \(n\ge2\), the sign is instead strictly positive for every \(1\le r<n\) and strictly negative for \(r=n\). For one box all the covariances vanish. Three is therefore the **minimum number of boxes permitting more than one change**, even without equal box probabilities.

## Assumptions and scope

Each ball is allocated independently; sample size is fixed, not Poissonized. All boxes are labeled. The three probabilities are exactly as above; a neighborhood corollary allows perturbations. The frequency \(r\) always denotes the **exact** occupancy count, not at least \(r\). Covariances are ordinary unnormalized covariances. The four frequencies in the infinite statement are valid because \(t\ge15\) and \(n=10t\).

## Proof

**General exact identity.** For \(m\) boxes with probabilities \(p_1,\ldots,p_m>0\) summing to one, put \(M_0=m-K\). For \(1\le r\le n\), individual binomial and joint-multinomial probabilities give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E M_0&=\sum_i(1-p_i)^n,\\
\mathbb E M_r&=\binom nr\sum_jp_j^r(1-p_j)^{n-r},\\
\mathbb E(M_0M_r)&=\binom nr\sum_{i\ne j}p_j^r(1-p_i-p_j)^{n-r}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The diagonal term is absent because an empty box cannot simultaneously contain \(r\ge1\) balls. As \(K=m-M_0\), subtracting products of expectations yields the following exact expression, valid for every positive probability vector:
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(K,M_r)=\binom nr\left[\left(\sum_i(1-p_i)^n\right)\left(\sum_jp_j^r(1-p_j)^{n-r}\right)-\sum_{i\ne j}p_j^r(1-p_i-p_j)^{n-r}\right].\tag{1}
\]

Set \(w=(9,3,1)\) and \(S=13\), and define positive integer quantities
\[
A_n=\sum_i(S-w_i)^n,\quad B_{n,r}=\sum_jw_j^r(S-w_j)^{n-r},\quad C_{n,r}=\sum_{i\ne j}w_j^r(S-w_i-w_j)^{n-r}.
\]
Equation (1) becomes
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(K,M_r)=\frac{\binom nr}{S^{2n}}\,H_{n,r},\qquad H_{n,r}=A_nB_{n,r}-S^nC_{n,r}.\tag{2}
\]

**Finite three-switch certificate.** Direct integer evaluation of (2) at \(n=14\), in order \(r=1,\ldots,14\), yields the sign sequence in the finding. For a succinct independently checkable boundary certificate, the integer \(H_{14,r}\) at the four critical transitions is
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
r&4&5&6&10&11&14\\\hline
H_{14,r}&88897880485138114994496282&-11924002417235160340109502&12223135042522757043498522&108840489915735879792714522&-680471699968930504931637822&-148489494201863815765568820198
\end{array}
\]
The other eight integers are all nonzero with the indicated signs; a complete exact list and an independent multinomial-state enumeration are generated by the accompanying `verify.py`. There is no floating-point inference.

**Infinite persistence by dominant integer powers.** Because there are exactly three boxes, in each summand of \(C_{n,r}\) the complement \(S-w_i-w_j\) is the weight \(w_k\) of the third box. At \(r=an/b\) with \(n/b\) integral, the polynomial \(H_{n,r}\) is a sum of nine positive integer powers minus six positive integer powers, with respective bases
\[
U_{ij}(a,b)=(S-w_i)^b w_j^a(S-w_j)^{b-a},\quad
V_{jk}(a,b)=S^b w_j^a w_k^{b-a}\quad(j\ne k).
\]
Specifically, \(H_{n,r}=\sum_{i,j}U_{ij}(a,b)^{n/b}-\sum_{j\ne k}V_{jk}(a,b)^{n/b}\). Let \(U_*\) and \(V_*\) denote the respective largest bases. Their exact values and strict rational comparisons are:

| \(a/b\) | \(U_*\) | \(V_*\) | Certified comparison |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| \(1/10\) | 319479999370622926848 | 160227612974826282483 | \(U_*/V_*>3/2\) |
| \(2/5\) | 2239488000 | 2436053373 | \(V_*/U_*>13/12\) |
| \(3/5\) | 2902376448 | 2436053373 | \(U_*/V_*>7/6\) |
| \(9/10\) | 95952222101012742144 | 160227612974826282483 | \(V_*/U_*>3/2\) |

These maxima follow by comparing the six and nine explicitly defined integer bases; every inequality is an integer cross multiplication. For \(n=10t\), the relevant power exponent is \(t,2t,2t,t\) in the four rows. For \(t\ge15\),
\[
(3/2)^t>9,\qquad (13/12)^{2t}>9,\qquad (7/6)^{2t}>6.
\]
Therefore in the first and third rows **one positive summand** exceeds all six negative summands combined, and in the second and fourth rows **one negative summand** exceeds all nine positive summands combined. This forces the four strict signs \((+,-,+,-)\), with no appeal to asymptotic approximations.

**Minimal box count.** With two boxes and \(n\ge2\), for \(r<n\) the event \(M_r>0\) implies both boxes are occupied, so \(M_0M_r=0\) identically and \(\operatorname{Cov}(K,M_r)=\mathbb EM_0\,\mathbb EM_r>0\). At \(r=n\), \(M_n=M_0\) and \(\operatorname{Cov}(K,M_n)=-\operatorname{Var}(M_0)<0\). One box has constant \(K\). Thus fewer than three boxes cannot have three switches.

**Open-set persistence.** At \(n=14\) the fourteen covariances are nonzero continuous functions of the two free box probabilities. At the rational witness vector each strict base inequality is also continuous in these probabilities. An open neighborhood preserves all finite signs and all four displayed rational lower bounds, so the identical \(t\ge15\) dominance argument holds uniformly there.

## Relationship to prior work

The March 25, 2024 paper of Iksanov, Kabluchko, Marynych, and Wachtel develops multinomial combinatorial probability models and random occupancy constructions (especially Sections 4.1 and 5.1). O'Neill's 2023 extended-occupancy article develops the uniform-bin occupancy-number distribution, not this heterogeneous cross-frequency sign profile. An accessible published mathematical result titled *A one-switch covariance law for occupancy richness and frequency counts* treats **uniform** occupancy, where only one sign change occurs. The present result shows that its one-switch conclusion does not extend to arbitrary unequal bin probabilities; that prior uniform statement is not contradicted. The general expectation formula in (1) is elementary multinomial algebra and is not claimed as a novel identity.

## Limitations

The concrete three-switch certificate is for \(n=14\); the universal infinite guarantee is for multiples \(n=10t\) with \(t\ge15\), not for every intermediate sample size. It guarantees **at least** three switches for this infinite family; it does not classify all their signs or prove three is the global maximum. The open-neighborhood assertion is existential and supplies no numerical perturbation radius. No earlier-literature uniqueness or independent validation is claimed.
