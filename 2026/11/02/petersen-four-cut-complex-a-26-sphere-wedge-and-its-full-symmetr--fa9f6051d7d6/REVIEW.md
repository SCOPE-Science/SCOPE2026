# Review of the Petersen ordinary four-cut calculation

## Correctness — PASS

The ten graph vertices are all unordered pairs from five labels, with disjointness adjacency. Exhaustive construction finds 140 distinct maximal six-vertex facets and 765 nonempty faces. A fully specified deterministic vertex-toggling matching has 369 pairs and leaves precisely one zero-cell and 26 five-cells. Complete topological sorting of every reversed-matching Hasse edge proves acyclicity, not merely an Euler-characteristic identity. The discrete-Morse CW model is thus a wedge of 26 integral five-spheres. Signed simplicial Lefschetz traces were evaluated for every one of the 120 permutations and decomposed using an orthogonality-checked character table. The presented proof and executable finite certificate agree on all central numbers. No reliance is placed on mod-two rank alone for an integral assertion.

## Originality — PASS with bibliographic residual risk

The published first source (Bayer et al., 2023 preprint, 2024 journal version, arXiv:2304.13675) treats ordinary cut complexes generally and Petersen two-cut specifically; the full accessible preprint's relevant introduction, table, Section 6, and Petersen occurrence were examined. Its listed general specializations do not cover the Petersen four-cut calculation; the face-lattice theorem requiring a complete four-skeleton is inapplicable, since the computed 240 four-simplices are fewer than the 252 possible. Indexed publications for the Petersen three-cut complex give a different threshold and six-sphere result; a separate four-cut computation uses the distinct triangular graph \(J(5,2)\), not its complement. Those results do not imply the present statement. Published record full texts for the two closest index entries could not be inspected through the available link, so an equivalent unpublished or unindexed argument remains a genuine residual risk.

## Value — PASS

The Petersen graph is a canonical highly symmetric graph, and the first higher ordinary cut threshold beyond the already-studied three-cut case supplies a natural finite invariant rather than an arbitrary induced subcomplex. In contrast with the three-cut case's six-dimensional irreducible \(S_5\)-homology, the four-cut case has 26 five-spheres and an actual trivial representation summand; a rational top-dimensional class survives the full symmetry quotient. Its complete multiplicities under the seven \(S_5\) conjugacy classes distinguish these complexes much more sharply than ranks. This is a motivated exact equivariant classification, not an assertion of a general downstream theorem.

## Unresolved limitations

The conclusion is restricted to ordinary \(k=4\) cut topology of the Petersen graph; no integral quotient classification or universal statement is claimed. Full-text inspection of two close published research-note entries was unavailable. Independent mathematical validation remains unperformed.
