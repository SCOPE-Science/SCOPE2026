# Placement-invariant two-sink quantum capture on nonbinary Hamming networks

## Finding

For every integer \(q\ge3\), every dimension \(d\ge3\), and any two distinct vertices \(a,b\) of the \(q\)-ary Hamming graph \(H(d,q)\), a coherent continuous-time excitation with **independent positive-rate irreversible sinks** at \(a\) and \(b\) has an eventual capturing subspace of dimension exactly \(2d+1\). This dimension is independent of the sink separation, sink strengths, and positive uniform hopping magnitude. The orthogonal dark subspace consequently has dimension \(q^d-2d-1\), and a maximally mixed starting excitation is captured with probability

\[
\overline\eta=\frac{2d+1}{q^d}.
\]

There is also an exact **site-resolved** formula. Write \(r=d_H(x,a)\), \(s=d_H(x,b)\), and \(t=d_H(a,b)\), where \(x\) is an arbitrary starting vertex. Define, for \(0\le k\le d\),

\[
K_k(h)=\sum_{j=\max\{0,k-d+h\}}^{\min\{k,h\}}(-1)^j(q-1)^{k-j}\binom{h}{j}\binom{d-h}{k-j},
\qquad m_k=\binom dk(q-1)^k.
\]

Then the total eventual absorption probability is

\[
\boxed{\eta(x)=\frac1{q^d}\left[1+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\frac{m_k(K_k(r)^2+K_k(s)^2)-2K_k(t)K_k(r)K_k(s)}{m_k^2-K_k(t)^2}\right].}
\]

Every denominator is strictly positive in the stated domain. Thus the exact probability depends **only on the three pairwise Hamming distances**, not on any finer coordinate arrangement. Moreover,

\[
\eta(x)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=a\ \text{or}\ x=b.
\]

The theorem includes all composite as well as prime alphabet sizes. As a concrete illustration, in \(H(3,3)\) the uniformly averaged probability is \(7/27\) for every pair of distinct sinks. With \(a=(0,0,0)\) and \(x=(2,0,0)\), taking \(b=(1,0,0)\) gives \(\eta(x)=11/27\), whereas taking \(b=(1,1,1)\) gives \(\eta(x)=71/315\): placement affects individual sites although it cannot affect the average.

## Assumptions and scope

The vertices are \(V=\{0,\ldots,q-1\}^d\), with an edge between two distinct vertices exactly when they differ in one coordinate. Let \(A\) be the real symmetric adjacency matrix, and let \(J,\gamma_a,\gamma_b\) be any strictly positive real numbers. Single-excitation, no-jump evolution is generated by the non-Hermitian operator

\[
H_{\mathrm{eff}}=JA-i\gamma_a|a\rangle\langle a|-i\gamma_b|b\rangle\langle b|.
\]

For a normalized starting basis state \(|x\rangle\), define \(\eta(x)=1-\lim_{u\to\infty}\|\exp(-iuH_{\mathrm{eff}})|x\rangle\|^2\). Equivalently it is the time-integrated probability lost through the two sinks. The result is a zero-disorder, infinite-observation-time statement for ideal coherent dynamics, not a bound on absorption speed or a theorem about finite-temperature transport.

## Proof

**1. Characterization of decaying and dark sectors.** Let \(E_k\), \(0\le k\le d\), be the mutually orthogonal spectral projections of \(A\); their eigenvalues are \((q-1)d-qk\). In the eigenspace \(E_k\mathbb C^V\), all vectors orthogonal to both \(E_k|a\rangle\) and \(E_k|b\rangle\) have zero sink amplitudes and are strictly nondissipative. Their orthogonal sum \(D\) is invariant for \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}\). Its orthogonal complement is

\[
B=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{d}\operatorname{span}\{E_k|a\rangle,E_k|b\rangle\}.
\]

The operator \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}\) leaves \(B\) invariant. Its dissipativity identity is \(\operatorname{Im}\langle v,H_{\mathrm{eff}}v\rangle=-\gamma_a|\langle a,v\rangle|^2-\gamma_b|\langle b,v\rangle|^2\). A hypothetical eigenvector in \(B\) with real eigenvalue would therefore have zero amplitudes at both sinks and hence be an eigenvector of \(A\) belonging to \(D\), which is impossible. All eigenvalues on the finite-dimensional space \(B\) thus have negative imaginary parts; polynomial Jordan factors also decay. Consequently \(\eta(x)=\langle x,P_Bx\rangle\), and no positive sink rate affects this eventual probability.

**2. Exact rank of the two local spectral vectors.** Identify the alphabet with the cyclic group \(\mathbb Z_q\). Tensor-product discrete Fourier characters diagonalize \(A\), and \(E_k\) selects characters with exactly \(k\) nonzero frequencies. At \(k=0\), the two projections of the sinks coincide and span one dimension. For each \(1\le k\le d\), choose a coordinate where \(a\ne b\) and a Fourier support of size \(k\) containing it. Holding the remaining frequencies fixed but using frequency values \(1\) and \(2\) on the differing coordinate produces two distinct relative phases between the sink characters, because \(q\ge3\). Therefore \(E_k|a\rangle\) and \(E_k|b\rangle\) are linearly independent. This gives

\[
\dim B=1+\sum_{k=1}^d2=2d+1.
\]

**3. Explicit spectral Gram matrices.** Character orthogonality, or the Hamming association scheme, gives the exact kernel \(\langle x,E_ky\rangle=q^{-d}K_k(d_H(x,y))\). In particular \(K_k(0)=m_k\). The two-sink Gram matrix in level \(k\ge1\) is

\[
G_k=q^{-d}\begin{pmatrix}m_k&K_k(t)\\K_k(t)&m_k\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Step 2 proves it is positive definite, so \(m_k^2-K_k(t)^2>0\). The orthogonal projector onto the level-\(k\) two-vector span is \(U_kG_k^{-1}U_k^*\), where \(U_k\) has columns \(E_k|a\rangle\), \(E_k|b\rangle\). Evaluate its \((x,x)\) entry using the kernel formula. The \(k=0\) contribution is \(q^{-d}\). Summing yields the boxed expression. Summing its diagonal over vertices gives \(\operatorname{Tr}(P_B)=2d+1\), establishing the average law.

**4. Exactly two perfectly captured sites.** The two sink basis vectors belong to \(B\), and hence each has unit capture. Conversely, if \(\eta(x)=1\), then \(|x\rangle\in B\), which implies \(E_1|x\rangle=\alpha E_1|a\rangle+\beta E_1|b\rangle\) with the *same* complex \(\alpha,\beta\) for every coordinate. Let \(u_z=e_z-q^{-1}{\bf1}\) be the centered local basis vector. Decomposing the first Fourier-degree space by coordinates gives \(u_{x_i}=\alpha u_{a_i}+\beta u_{b_i}\) for every \(i\).

If \(a_i=b_i\), this forces \(\alpha+\beta=1\). If \(a_i\ne b_i\) and \(q\ge4\), the only local centered basis vectors in their span are \(u_{a_i}\) and \(u_{b_i}\), forcing \((\alpha,\beta)=(1,0)\) or \((0,1)\). If \(q=3\), the only additional possibility at a differing coordinate is the third alphabet symbol, with \((\alpha,\beta)=(-1,-1)\). A common coordinate excludes that possibility by \(\alpha+\beta=1\). If all coordinates differ, its only extra first-degree candidate is the vertex taking the third symbol in every coordinate. Such a candidate fails the degree-two condition: on any two coordinates its local tensor \((u_{a_i}+u_{b_i})\otimes(u_{a_j}+u_{b_j})\) contains mixed tensors outside the span of \(u_{a_i}\otimes u_{a_j}\) and \(u_{b_i}\otimes u_{b_j}\); the four tensors are linearly independent because each local pair is. Since \(d\ge3\), degree two is available. Thus no nonsink basis state lies in \(B\), and \(\eta(x)<1\) for all \(x\notin\{a,b\}\).

## Verification

Run \(\texttt{python3 verify.py}\) with Python 3, NumPy, and SymPy. It independently constructs adjacency matrices and their numerical spectral projectors for 13 graph-and-separation cases, compares all 972 site probabilities and projector ranks, and checks 81 site probabilities against exact rational SymPy projector construction. The recorded maximum floating-point discrepancy is \(4.44\times10^{-16}\). The machine-readable command output is in \(\texttt{verification\_output.txt}\). These finite tests substantiate the implementation and examples but are **not** a proof for arbitrary \(d,q\); the general theorem is supplied by the preceding analytic argument.

## Relationship to prior work

Varbanov, Krovi and Brun (2008, arXiv:0803.3446) examine quantum hitting and nonhitting through graph symmetries using a different observation model. Godsil (2010, arXiv:1010.3231) gives the general spectral-support framework for controllable graph subsets. The Hamming Fourier and Krawtchouk toolkit is established mathematics, also treated in Bernard, Crampé and Vinet (2021, arXiv:2103.15742). Razzoli, Paris and Bordone (2021, DOI 10.3390/e23010085) give the spectral transport-efficiency framework for one absorbing site; their analysis explicitly does not classify multiple sinks. Lugão, Portugal, Sabri and Tanaka (2022, arXiv:2203.14384) consider coherent multi-marked spatial search, a different sink mechanism. None of the materially inspected statements directly supplies the nonbinary arbitrary-separation two-sink capture dimension together with the exact sitewise law and complete-capture classification; an uncatalogued specialized result remains a possibility. The two-dimensional strongly regular case and the binary hypercube are **not** claimed as new results.

## Limitations

The conclusions need uniform nonzero hopping, exactly two distinct positive-rate absorbing vertices, a single-excitation Hilbert space, \(q\ge3\), \(d\ge3\), and infinite time. They do not extend without separate proof to binary cubes (where placement affects rank), nonuniform bonds, dephasing, other loss channels, finite-time efficiencies, or arbitrary multiple traps. The result has passed a self-review only; no independent scientific audit is represented.
