# Poisson critical law for one-step tense algebras of coloured random digraphs

## Finding

Let \(k\ge1\) be fixed, put \(q=2^k\), and give each vertex of a directed random graph \(k\) independent fair Boolean colours. At edge probability
\[
p_n=\frac{q(\log n+t)}n\qquad(t\in\mathbb R\text{ fixed}),
\]
include loops and take all ordered edges independently. Write \(C_a\) for the vertex set with complete Boolean colour \(a\in\{1,\ldots,q\}\). For every ordered pair of colours \((a,b)\), let \(X^+_{ab}\) count colour-\(a\) vertices with no outgoing edge to \(C_b\), and let \(X^-_{ab}\) count such vertices with no incoming edge from \(C_b\).

**Theorem (critical-window joint law and algebraic saturation).** As \(n\to\infty\), all \(2q^2\) counts \(X^+_{ab},X^-_{ab}\) converge *jointly* to mutually independent Poisson variables with common mean \(\lambda=e^{-t}/q\). Let \(B_0\) be the Boolean subalgebra of \(\mathcal P([n])\) generated by the \(k\) propositional colours; let \(B_1\) additionally contain the images of every colour class under the two existential tense modalities; and let \(B_\infty\) be the least Boolean subalgebra closed under both modalities. Then
\[
\bigl|\operatorname{At}(B_1)\bigr|-q\ \Longrightarrow\ \operatorname{Binomial}\bigl(2q^2,1-e^{-e^{-t}/q}\bigr)
\]
and
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\Pr(B_\infty=B_0)=\exp(-2q e^{-t}).
\]
The first convergence is in distribution; the second is a limiting probability. Equivalently, the cardinality of the first-step Boolean algebra is \(2^{q+J}\), where \(J\) has the displayed limiting binomial law.

More generally, if \(p_n=q(\log n+t_n)/n\) and \(|t_n|=o(\log n)\), the saturation probability tends to \(1\) when \(t_n\to+\infty\) and to \(0\) when \(t_n\to-\infty\). This is a **high-density saturation window**, not a global monotonicity statement: at edge probability zero both modalities are trivial.

## Assumptions and scope

The vertex set is \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\). Each vertex independently receives one of \(q\) colour vectors with equal probability; each ordered pair \((v,w)\), **including** \(v=w\), independently belongs to \(R\) with probability \(p_n\). Statements involving \(p_n\) apply to sufficiently large \(n\), when \(p_n\in[0,1]\). Put
\[
F(A)=\{v:\exists w\in A\ (vRw)\},\qquad
P(A)=\{v:\exists w\in A\ (wRv)\}.
\]
These are the existential future and past operators on the Boolean powerset algebra, with the adjoint/converse interaction characteristic of relational tense algebras. The Boolean algebra \(B_0\) is generated by the individual propositional colour sets; on the event all \(q\) complete colours occur, its atoms are precisely the \(C_a\). Let \(B_1\) be the Boolean algebra generated by \(B_0\), \(F(C_b)\), and \(P(C_b)\) for all \(b\). This is the full *first modal refinement* of \(B_0\), because both operators preserve finite unions. Define \(B_\infty\) by repeated Boolean and modal closure, with no restriction on depth.

## Proof

**1. Conditional edge-avoidance estimates.** Write \(N_b=|C_b|\). By multinomial concentration, with probability tending to one,
\[
\max_b|N_b-n/q|\le n^{2/3}.
\]
On this event, uniformly in \(b\), for fixed \(t\),
\[
(1-p_n)^{N_b}=n^{-1}e^{-t}(1+o(1));
\]
indeed \(p_nN_b=\log n+t+o(1)\) and \(p_n^2n=o(1)\). This remains valid with \(t=t_n\), \(|t_n|=o(\log n)\), in the form \((1-p_n)^{N_b}=n^{-1}e^{-t_n}(1+o(1))\).

**2. Mixed factorial moments.** Fix a finite collection of nonnegative integers \(r^s_{ab}\) (\(a,b\in[q]\), \(s\in\{+,-\}\)), and let \(r=\sum r^s_{ab}\). Expand the conditional expectation of \(\prod_{a,b,s}(X^s_{ab})_{r^s_{ab}}\) into ordered choices of \(r\) witness vertices, distinct within each factorial factor. In terms with all \(r\) vertices distinct, choices of each prescribed anchor colour are \((n/q)^r(1+o(1))\). Each selected outgoing (respectively incoming) hole forbids all edges from its anchor into \(C_b\) (respectively all edges from \(C_b\) into its anchor). Distinct constraints' forbidden directed-edge sets overlap only on ordered pairs of selected anchors, hence on \(O(r^2)\) edges total. Therefore their joint conditional probability is
\[
(1-p_n)^{\sum_{a,b,s}r^s_{ab}N_b+O(r^2)}
=n^{-r}e^{-tr}(1+o(1)).
\]
Their aggregate factorial-moment contribution is \((e^{-t}/q)^r(1+o(1))\).

Terms with a witness vertex reused in *different* factors vanish: two different constraints at that same vertex must have distinct target colours, distinct directions, or both. They forbid disjoint sets of order \(n/q\) directed edges, except possibly one shared loop. More generally, the union of the edge sets of \(r\) distinct selected constraints forbids \(r n/q+o(n)\) edges even when a bounded number of anchors coincide, because repeated constraints are different types and overlaps not arising from the finitely many anchors are empty. Thus every such term has conditional probability \(O(n^{-r})\) and there are \(O(n^{r-1})\) choices. Its total contribution is \(O(n^{-1})\). The conditional estimates are uniform on the concentration event; boundedness of fixed factorial moments on its complement follows directly from the same exponential estimates after separating unusually small colour classes, whose deviations have exponentially small probability. Therefore the unconditional mixed factorial moments tend to
\[
\prod_{a,b,s}\lambda^{r^s_{ab}},\qquad\lambda=e^{-t}/q.
\]
The multivariate Poisson law is moment-determinate, establishing joint convergence and asymptotic independence. In particular the total number of vertex-hole incidences is tight.

**3. Boolean refinement atoms.** A vertex's membership vector in the generators of \(B_1\) is exactly its complete colour \(a\) together with the \(2q\) bits indicating whether it has an outgoing or incoming neighbour in each colour class. Consequently the atoms of \(B_1\) are the nonempty fibres of these membership vectors. With probability tending to one every colour class contains \(N_a\sim n/q\) vertices; the total number of hole incidences is bounded in probability, and the chance of a vertex having two or more distinct holes is \(O(1/n)\) by the pairwise avoidance estimates above. On this event every colour class contains a vertex with no holes, and each positive variable \(X^s_{ab}\) creates exactly one additional nonempty membership fibre, containing the vertices with precisely that single hole. Hence, with probability tending to one,
\[
|\operatorname{At}(B_1)|=q+\sum_{a,b,s}\mathbf1\{X^s_{ab}>0\}.
\]
The joint Poisson limit makes these indicators independent Bernoulli variables with parameter \(1-e^{-\lambda}\), proving the binomial law. A finite Boolean algebra with \(j\) atoms has exactly \(2^j\) elements.

**4. Full tense saturation.** Every colour class having at least one hole-free vertex, \(F(C_b)\) is a union of entire colour classes precisely when \(X^+_{ab}=0\) for every \(a\); the analogous criterion for \(P(C_b)\) is \(X^-_{ab}=0\) for every \(a\). Because \(F,P\) preserve unions, \(B_0\) is closed under both if and only if all \(2q^2\) variables are zero. Closure of \(B_0\) is equivalent to \(B_\infty=B_0\). Joint Poisson convergence at the all-zero event gives
\[
\exp(-2q^2\lambda)=\exp(-2q e^{-t}).
\]

**5. Window sides.** If \(t_n\to+\infty\) and \(|t_n|=o(\log n)\), the expected total number of holes is \((2q+o(1))e^{-t_n}\to0\); Markov's inequality gives closure with probability tending to one. If \(t_n\to-\infty\), fix \((a,b)\). Conditional on the colours, the outgoing-hole count \(X^+_{ab}\) is exactly binomial with \(N_a\) trials and success probability \((1-p_n)^{N_b}\), because outgoing rows of the edge matrix are independent, including loops. Its conditional mean is \((1+o(1))e^{-t_n}/q\to\infty\), so a hole exists with probability tending to one. The expected total hole incidence count is \(n^{o(1)}=o(n)\); hence every colour class also has hole-free vertices with probability tending to one. Therefore \(B_\infty\ne B_0\) with probability tending to one.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `checker.py` exhaustively enumerates colourings and directed relations (loops included) on up to three vertices. It independently compares the signature partition with the directly generated Boolean algebra, checks the equivalence of one-step and full saturation, and verifies exact finite expected hole counts. These tests check normalization and small boundary cases but are **not** proofs of the multivariate infinite limit. The infinite theorem follows from the preceding factorial-moment and saturation arguments.

Run: `python3 checker.py`. Expected final line: `ALL_CHECKS_OK`.

## Relationship to prior work

Figallo, Sarmiento and Pezzini (arXiv:2610.01547v1, first posted 2026-10-01) provide a contemporary axiomatization and algebraic setting for tense Boolean algebras. Their inspected introduction and foundational definitions concern algebraic completeness of the minimal tense calculus, not sparse random digraph generation or the distribution of finite generated-algebra atoms. Halpern and Kapron (1994, Section 4, Proposition 4.4 and Theorem 4.5) prove propositional reduction and a zero-one law for *dense fixed-probability* random modal structures. Those statements do not cover the logarithmic sparse edge window, its nondegenerate joint Poisson defects, the first-refinement atom-count law, or two-directional tense saturation. The 2003 erratum to their frame-validity argument does not enter the present proof. Existing colour-refinement literature often counts full neighbour-colour multisets rather than the existential, Boolean membership signatures used here.

## Limitations

The full distribution of \(B_\infty\) **conditional on nonsaturation** is not determined; only the event \(B_\infty=B_0\) and the one-step atom law are classified. The colour number \(q=2^k\) and real critical shift \(t\) are fixed; there is no statement for a growing number of predicates, nonuniform colour probabilities, independent-undirected edges, or loop-excluding conventions. This saturation probability is not globally monotone in edge density. Priority against equivalent specialised sparse-occupancy or random-tense-algebra formulations cannot be certified solely by keyword searches. Independent validation has not been performed.
