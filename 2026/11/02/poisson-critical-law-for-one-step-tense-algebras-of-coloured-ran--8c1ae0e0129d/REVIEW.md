# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem fixes the random-structure law (including independent loops), both relational operators, and the two generated Boolean algebras unambiguously. Its mixed factorial-moment proof examines collisions of selected witnesses, explicitly separating edge-overlaps on the finitely many anchored pairs. It derives the joint independent-Poisson law for all \(2q^2\) deficiency counts, rather than assuming independence of dependent counts. The binomial first-refinement law follows from the exact membership-signature partition and the high-probability absence of vertices with two missing flags. The full saturation equivalence rests on preservation of finite unions by both tense modalities. The plus and minus sides use, respectively, a first-moment bound and an exact conditional binomial law. Independent exhaustive finite testing supports the definitions and normalization, without being represented as infinite verification.

## Originality — PASS within inspected sources

The closest directly relevant modal source is Halpern and Kapron, *Zero-One Laws for Modal Logic* (1994), Section 4, which proves almost-sure propositional reduction in the dense fixed-edge-probability random model. Its Proposition 4.4 and Theorem 4.5 do not assert the sparse logarithmic critical law or the joint two-directional Poisson and first-step atom distributions. Figallo, Sarmiento and Pezzini (arXiv:2610.01547v1, 2026-10-01) supply the contemporary algebraic setting and proof-theoretic completeness results, not random generation statistics. Penrose (2018) provides general Poisson-approximation techniques for isolated vertices, and Kiefer–McKay (2020) studies a different counting-based colour-refinement algorithm; neither inspected statement supplies the present *combined* tense Boolean-algebraic classification. Searches of related published research summaries returned no result with the same object, random law and final claim. The principal residual priority risk is an equivalent formulation under sparse coloured digraph occupancy, random modal model, or finite modal-algebra generation terminology; the motivating paper's complete PDF was unavailable during the comparison, although its abstract and introductory definitions were examined.

## Value — PASS

The result identifies an exact, nondegenerate critical window for a natural algebraic property of randomly generated tense Boolean algebras. The limiting first-stage atom count is *binomial*, even though the underlying exceptional vertex counts are Poisson; this distinction matters because one Boolean atom encodes an entire common modal signature, not the number of vertices sharing it. The same analysis determines the probability of stabilization at *all* modal depths, not just the first stage, and finds its limiting threshold profile and two sides. These are motivated structural statistics, not arbitrary finite cases or small numerical gains.
