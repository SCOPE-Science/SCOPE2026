# Scientific review: Poisson fringe defects and a sharp near-complete-reinforcement mixing window

## Correctness — PASS

The random recursive tree representation of the walk identifies each fresh uniform binary step with the independent random label of one forest component. The exact pre-cut fringe expectation is \(N/[k(k+1)]\); independently marking its child edge with probability \(q_N\), where \(Nq_N\to\lambda\), yields independent Poisson counts of intensity \(\lambda/[k(k+1)]\). Uniform second moments of depths control fringe-count fluctuations, and the expected number of nested cut pairs is \(O(q_N^2N\log N)=o(1)\). Marking edges of original fringe size exceeding \(K\) has expectation at most \(Nq_N/(K+1)\), excluding hidden large components. This justifies joint convergence of the countable component-count sequence in \(\ell^1\) and a compound-Poisson root deficit. Character orthogonality proves the walk's parity formula exactly; odd terminal times are uniform and even times have limiting total-variation distance \(2^{-\lambda-1}\). Summing the jump tail gives \(\Pr(W\ge m)\sim\lambda/m\), while the first-moment identity yields \(\mathbb ED_N\sim\lambda\log N\) for fixed positive \(\lambda\) and exact \(q_N=\lambda/N\). A standalone exact-rational script passed 63 assertions, checking original fringe expectations and an independently computed walk/forest parity identity. Finite assertions are supporting evidence, not proof of limits.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The full texts of Peres and Qin, arXiv:2604.07207 and arXiv:2604.07227, were inspected for the forest representation, two-state even/odd behavior and mixing bounds. The companion establishes exact odd uniformity and exponential upper/lower bounds on even bias at fixed reinforcement but does not give the exact \(N(1-\alpha_N)\to\lambda\) crossover, Poisson component spectrum or heavy-tailed root defect. The full accessible mathematical content of Seelbach Benkner and Wagner's 2022 Algorithmica article was inspected at its fringe-count lemma; this establishes the baseline fringe profile and variance bound, not sparse-percolation component counts or reinforced mixing. Baur's arXiv:1407.2508 studies high-retention percolation in a distinct large-cluster window requiring the cut intensity to diverge. Published summary and abstract evidence from Fuchs (2008) concern uncut fringe subtree sizes and are acknowledged as earlier inputs. Targeted mathematical-record searches for reinforced parity, rare recursive-tree cuts, component-size Poisson spectra and \(\log2\) mixing yielded no dominating exact result. Important residual risk: an earlier general theorem on sparse cuts of random recursive trees could imply the Poisson component-spectrum portion, or a Fourier argument could offer an alternative derivation; mathematical priority has not been independently audited. No theorem follows merely from an absence of matching search results.

## Value — PASS

A natural question left quantitative by the motivating binary reinforced-walk bounds is whether the even-time distance is explicitly determined in a sharp near-total-reinforcement window. The limit \(2^{-\lambda-1}\) fixes both its critical scale and the exact coefficient \(\log2\), and identifies the source of the bias as the absence of odd Poisson fringe defects. The complete finite component spectrum also yields a tight, heavy-tailed root deficit with infinite limiting mean despite a diverging finite-size expectation. These are specific probabilistic phenomena, not arbitrary parameter substitutions, and the results explain the behavior of a canonical non-Markovian walk at a previously unresolved boundary.

## Scope and residual risk

The finite-window result is restricted to uniform recursive trees and uniform binary fresh increments. It does not assert an exact convergence rate, arbitrary-group limits, or novel fringe expectation identities. Priority relative to general Poisson-thinning or random-forest frameworks requires further human verification.
