# Scientific review

**Correctness (PASS).** The proof gives an explicit \(q\)-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé invariant (the twice-truncated class-colour census) and a move-by-move duplicator strategy. The binomial means and exact two-category multinomial generating function are derived from independent vertex colouring. Rare categories with bounded means converge jointly to independent Poisson counts; divergent means concentrate above any fixed threshold, and generic classes proliferate. These facts prove the sufficient direction for *every* fixed-rank sentence. Necessity uses first-order definable exact-minority-class events and the injectivity of the extended Poisson void probability. The ratio of consecutive means proves the unique critical index. The script checks 12 exact finite distributions and 417 small EF-games; the general assertion does not rely on enumeration.

**Originality (PASS, qualified).** Source and parameter comparisons were made against Koponen's full accessible HTML around her random-expansion remarks; Braunfeld–Kukla's ordered convex-equivalence limit laws; Demin–Zhukovskii's first-order complexity/isolated-edge Poisson example; and Spencer–St. John's random unary-colour theory with order. None of the inspected statements specifies this family of uniformly sized *unordered* equivalence classes with independently fair-coloured elements, or gives the necessary-and-sufficient simultaneous intensity criterion. Searches for random-coloured equivalence-class logical laws, rare monochromatic components, and the equivalent disjoint-clique/complete-multipartite formulation found no exact prior statement. This is an evidence-bounded originality assessment; the use of standard Poisson approximation and EF games is not itself novel.

**Value (PASS).** Koponen's investigation explicitly makes first-order convergence of random expansions of finite base structures a structural concern. The present sharp, completely specified model separates necessary from sufficient conditions; identifies a countable hierarchy of nontrivial Poisson boundary laws; provides a test for all first-order sentences without imposing a scaling formula; and demonstrates failure of convergence after adding just one independent unary predicate to a deterministic base family already satisfying a zero-one law. These are structural consequences, not a numerical one-off.

**Closest prior literature and residual limits.** The 2026 Koponen paper gives abstract results about bases and expansions, not this distributional phase classification. Braunfeld–Kukla study ordered randomly selected partition structures; Demin–Zhukovskii examine different random component models; Spencer–St. John study ordered unary-colour structures. Potential hidden equivalents in graph-theoretic cluster language remain a priority risk. The theorem assumes independent, unbiased colours and equal-sized equivalence classes, and says nothing about varying logical formula complexity. The computation is supporting evidence only.
