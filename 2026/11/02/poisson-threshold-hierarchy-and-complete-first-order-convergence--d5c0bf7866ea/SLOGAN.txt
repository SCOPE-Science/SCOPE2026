For independently fair-coloured equivalence classes with both the class count and class size growing, first-order convergence holds exactly when every near-monochromatic intensity \(m2^{-r}\binom{r}{j}\) has an extended limit. Each nontrivial phase has exactly two independent Poisson counts, and the natural logarithmic class-size choice can destroy first-order convergence even though the uncoloured structures obey a zero-one law.
