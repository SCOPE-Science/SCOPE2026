# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

The reference sequence \(H_n=\sum_k\binom nk^4\) is integral and satisfies the specific recurrence published by Franel and Cusick. For a general solution with integer initial pair, direct subtraction of two recurrence equations proves the determinant product identity. For every prime \(p\ge5\), exactly two distinct nonzero residue classes contribute one factor of \(p\) to the numerator, while \((p!)^3\) contributes exactly three. Therefore \(\nu_p(W_{p-1})=\nu_p(v-2u)-1\). If the initial discrepancy is nonzero, choose a prime not dividing it; integrality of the two adjacent terms contradicts the rational determinant. The zero-discrepancy case follows by recurrence uniqueness. The verifier checks 672 determinant identities plus 22 local prime valuations; finite tests are not used as infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with stated risk

Cusick's 1989 paper gives the fourth-power Franel recurrence but not a classification of all integer initial pairs. Straub (arXiv:1401.0854, Example 3.6) identifies the fourth-power sequence and its supercongruences. Straub and Zudilin (arXiv:2112.09576, Theorems 1.1 and 1.3) establish minimal-order and rational-companion/Apéry-limit properties, without an exact prime-index denominator formula. Tonien's later determinant formula concerns the **cubic**, not fourth-power, Franel recurrence. The present contribution is the explicit fourth-power two-linear-factor valuation and the complete integral-solution lattice. Equivalent statements expressed as integrality of formal solutions or recurrences for Yang–Zudilin numbers remain a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The integrality of Apéry-like recurrence solutions is a recognized arithmetic phenomenon, not a manufactured numerical slice. This result completely determines integer-valued solutions for a classical rank-two family, and provides a uniform local obstruction at every prime not dividing a simple initial discrepancy. It extends the study from the known canonical integer solution to the entire two-dimensional rational solution space. The prime-local defect is an exact infinite statement rather than an isolated calculation.

## Limits and closest literature

The classical recurrence itself, the binomial identity, general Abel/Casoratian propagation, and the cubic counterpart are prior work. None is claimed as new. The historical search was not exhaustive, and external verification of originality and correctness is still outstanding.
