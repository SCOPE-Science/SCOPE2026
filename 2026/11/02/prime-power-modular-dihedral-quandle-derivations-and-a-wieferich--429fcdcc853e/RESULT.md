# Prime-power modular dihedral-quandle derivations and a Wieferich dimension jump

## Finding

Let \(p\) be any odd prime, \(a\ge 2\), and \(K\) any field of characteristic \(p\). Give the dihedral quandle \(R_{p^a}=\mathbb Z/p^a\mathbb Z\) the operation \(i\triangleright j=2j-i\) and let \(A=K[R_{p^a}]\) be its (generally nonassociative) quandle algebra. This result gives an exact normal form, Lie bracket, and dimension for *every* ordinary Leibniz derivation of \(A\).

Identify the underlying vector space of \(A\) with the commutative associative algebra
\[
B=K[t]/(t^{p^a}-1),\qquad e_i\longleftrightarrow t^i.
\]
The quandle-algebra product is \(f\star g=\sigma(f)\tau(g)\), using ordinary multiplication in \(B\), where \(\sigma(t)=t^{-1}\) and \(\tau(t)=t^2\). Define
\[
W=\{w\in B:\sigma(w)=-w,\ \tau(w)=2w\},\qquad
\delta(t^i)=i\,t^i.
\]
The coefficient \(i\) is its residue in the prime subfield. Since \(p\mid p^a\), the map \(\delta\) is a well-defined ordinary derivation of \(B\). **Every** derivation of the quandle algebra is uniquely
\[
D_{u,v}(f)=u f+v\delta(f),\qquad (u,v)\in W\times W.
\]
Here the products on the right are ordinary products in \(B\). In particular,
\[
[D_{u,v},D_{w,z}]
=D_{\,v\delta(w)-z\delta(u),\,v\delta(z)-z\delta(v)}.
\]

For \(h=\operatorname{ord}_p(2)\), \(L=\operatorname{lcm}(h,2)\), \(s=\nu_p(2^h-1)\), and \(m=(p-1)/L\), the **complete dimension formula** is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\dim_K\operatorname{Der}(A)
=2m\sum_{j=1}^{a}p^{\min(j-1,s-1)}.}
\]
Consequently, for \(K=\mathbb F_q\) of characteristic \(p\), the number of derivations is exactly \(q^{\dim_K\operatorname{Der}(A)}\). If \(p\) is not a base-two Wieferich prime (\(s=1\)), the dimension equals \(2am\). For the Wieferich prime \(p=1093\), one has \(h=L=364\), \(m=3\), \(s=2\), and the dimension at \(a=2\) is \(6564\), compared to \(12\) from the non-Wieferich linear pattern with the same \(m\).

## Assumptions and scope

The theorem is valid over arbitrary fields of **odd positive** characteristic \(p\), for all integers \(a\ge2\); it does not require algebraic closedness or a finite field. The enumeration applies when \(K\) has \(q\) elements. Derivations mean \(K\)-linear operators satisfying \(D(x\star y)=D(x)\star y+x\star D(y)\). The identification with \(B\) is a vector-space identification: ordinary products in the formulas for \(D_{u,v}\) are not the quandle product. The classification deliberately does not include the already-studied prime-modulus case \(a=1\), or the coprime-characteristic case.

## Proof

**1. Reduction of the Leibniz equations.** For any linear operator \(D\), write uniquely \(D(t^i)=t^iF_i\) with \(F_i\in B\), indices modulo \(p^a\). Because \(t^i\star t^j=t^{2j-i}\), the Leibniz identity is equivalent, for every \(i,j\), to
\[
F_{2j-i}=\sigma(F_i)+\tau(F_j).\tag{1}
\]
Putting \(i=0\) gives \(F_{2j}=\sigma(F_0)+\tau(F_j)\); putting \(i=2j\) gives \(F_0=\sigma(F_{2j})+\tau(F_j)\). Since \(\sigma\tau=\tau\sigma\) and \(\sigma^2=1\), the two equalities imply \(\tau(\sigma(F_j)+F_j)=0\). The doubling map is invertible modulo odd \(p^a\), so \(\tau\) is an automorphism; hence \(\sigma(F_j)=-F_j\). Equation (1) with \(i=j\) now yields \(\tau(F_j)=2F_j\). Therefore **every** \(F_j\) lies in \(W\), and (1) simplifies to
\[
F_{2j-i}=2F_j-F_i.\tag{2}
\]

**2. The affine-index rule.** Put \(G_i=F_i-F_0\). Equation (2) with \(j=0\) gives \(G_{-i}=-G_i\); with \(i=0\), it gives \(G_{2j}=2G_j\). Substituting \(i=-z\) into (2), we obtain \(G_{z+2j}=G_z+2G_j=G_z+G_{2j}\). Since multiplication by two permutes the residue classes modulo odd \(p^a\), \(G\) is an additive map \(\mathbb Z/p^a\mathbb Z\to W\). Thus \(G_i=i v\), for a unique \(v=G_1\in W\), and \(F_i=u+i v\) with \(u=F_0\in W\). Conversely these coefficients satisfy (1) because \(\sigma u=-u\), \(\tau u=2u\), and likewise for \(v\). This proves the asserted unique normal form. The operator \(\delta(t^i)=i t^i\) is well-defined on \(K[t]/(t^{p^a}-1)\) because its defining polynomial relation has derivative \(p^a t^{p^a-1}=0\) in characteristic \(p\).

**3. Explicit eigenspace basis.** Index the monomials of \(B\) by \(\mathbb Z/p^a\mathbb Z\). The subgroup generated by exponent doubling and sign reversal acts on nonzero indices. Its orbits partition by the **additive order** \(p^j\), for \(1\le j\le a\). An index of order \(p^j\) has orbit size
\[
H_j=|\langle2,-1\rangle\subseteq(\mathbb Z/p^j\mathbb Z)^\times|
=\operatorname{lcm}(2,\operatorname{ord}_{p^j}(2)).
\]
Each orbit contributes exactly one basis vector to \(W\): assign coefficient \(1\) at a chosen representative \(r\), then assign coefficient \(2^{-1}\) at \(2r\) and coefficient \(-1\) at \(-r\), recursively. These assignments are consistent because the character of \(\langle2,-1\rangle\) sending \(2\mapsto 2\in K\) and \(-1\mapsto-1\in K\) is simply reduction modulo \(p\) of the acting unit; its kernel contains every stabilizer. The zero index has no contribution since \(\sigma(1)=1\ne-1\). There are \(\varphi(p^j)=(p-1)p^{j-1}\) indices of additive order \(p^j\), so
\[
\dim_K W=\sum_{j=1}^{a}\frac{\varphi(p^j)}{H_j}.\tag{3}
\]
This orbit argument uses coefficient relations directly and does not require semisimplicity of the permutation representations in characteristic \(p\).

**4. Number-theoretic evaluation.** By definition \(h=\operatorname{ord}_p(2)\) and \(s=\nu_p(2^h-1)\). The elementary lifting-the-exponent identity for an odd prime gives
\[
\operatorname{ord}_{p^j}(2)=h p^{\max(0,j-s)}.
\]
Because \(p\) is odd, adjoining \(-1\) multiplies this order by two exactly when \(h\) is odd. Therefore \(H_j=L p^{\max(0,j-s)}\), and (3) becomes
\[
\dim_K W=\frac{p-1}{L}\sum_{j=1}^{a}p^{\min(j-1,s-1)}.
\]
The normal form has two independent parameters in \(W\), proving the boxed dimension formula.

**5. Lie bracket.** Write \(M_u\) for ordinary multiplication by \(u\) in \(B\). The derivations are \(M_u+M_v\delta\). Direct commutation in the associative operator algebra gives
\[
[M_u+M_v\delta,M_w+M_z\delta]
=M_{v\delta(w)-z\delta(u)}+M_{v\delta(z)-z\delta(v)}\delta.
\]
The identities \(\sigma\delta=-\delta\sigma\) and \(\delta\tau=2\tau\delta\), together with \(\sigma w=-w\), \(\tau w=2w\) for \(w\in W\), imply that \(\delta(W)\) is fixed by both \(\sigma\) and \(\tau\). Products of those invariant elements with vectors in \(W\) remain in \(W\), so both displayed bracket coefficients belong to \(W\). This verifies closure and the Lie formula.

## Verification

The attached standalone `verify.py` builds the \(n^2\)-variable Leibniz system directly from the quandle multiplication and reduces all \(n^3\) coefficient equations over the prime field; it does not use the normal form in assembling that system. For \((p,a)=(3,2),(3,3),(5,2)\), it obtains nullities \(4,6,4\), respectively, exactly as predicted, from \(729\), \(19683\), and \(15625\) direct coefficient equations. It separately constructs signed-doubling orbit vectors, checks each of their derivations against the full multiplication table, verifies the Lie commutator formula, and checks the \(p=1093\) arithmetic parameters. Explicit normal-form tests also cover \((p,a)=(7,2)\). Output ends in `VERIFY_OK`. These are finite checks only; the proof above establishes all fields and exponents.

## Relationship to prior work

Elhamdadi, Makhlouf, Silvestrov, and Zappala (2021 preprint, later journal publication) prove a general coefficient characterization of derivations of quandle algebras (Proposition 3.2), prove a complete dihedral classification in **characteristic zero** (Theorem 3.9), and tabulate positive-characteristic examples for dihedral orders \(3\) through \(6\) (Section 4.2). The coefficient characterization supplies a general starting point but does not state the prime-power modular normal form, the two-copy eigenspace Lie bracket, the closed dimensions for all \(p^a\), or the Wieferich distinction. Work on ring-theoretic aspects, idempotents, and complex representation decompositions concerns distinct invariants. Earlier prime-modulus derivation formulas and odd-modulus formulas in characteristics coprime to the modulus are excluded from the claimed new scope; neither covers \(a\ge2\) in characteristic \(p\).

## Limitations

The theorem does not classify even-order quandles, general odd composite moduli with several distinct prime factors, derivations in characteristic different from \(p\), or the inner-derivation subspace in the sense of Schafer. It does not assert a new result for \(a=1\). An earlier, unlocated derivation formula for quandle algebras over modular group rings might imply the same arithmetic result; publication priority is consequently provisional. The finite verifier cannot certify the infinite statement independently.
