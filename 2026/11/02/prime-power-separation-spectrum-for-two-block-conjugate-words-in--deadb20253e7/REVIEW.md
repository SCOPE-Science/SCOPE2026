# Scientific review: two-block permutation-automaton separation

## Correctness (C): PASS

The claim is equivalent to noncommutation of \(A^a\) and \(B^b\) in \(S_m\). The subgroup generated by each class of powers is normal, and the two classes commute pairwise exactly when these normal subgroups centralize one another. The classification of normal subgroups of \(S_m\) for \(m\ge5\), explicit power subgroup computations for degrees \(3\) and \(4\), and the group exponent \(L_m\) reconstruct every quantitative condition. Monotonicity and the exact jump locations of \(L_m\) prove the prime-power spectrum and length minima. Exhaustive degree-\(2\) through degree-\(6\) tests and explicit permutation witnesses were replayed. Numerical tests support, but do not replace, the infinite proof. No unproved external computational claim is needed.

## Originality (O): PASS, with priority limitations

The first two-block special case in \(S_3\), \(X^2Y^2=Y^2X^2\), was already known and is not claimed as original. The full 2017 Bulatov–Karpova–Shur–Startsev paper, particularly Section 3 and Proposition 15, was inspected: its general identity \((XY)^a(YX)^b=(YX)^b(XY)^a\) and its explicit low-degree identities are different statements. The 2011 Demaine–Eisenstat–Shallit–Wilson treatment likewise discusses general word separation, but not the full power-commutator classification for the two-block rotations. Semantic mathematical-record searches and accessible prior claim checks found no statement covering the complete all-parameter test, the exact prime-power achievable minima, or the sharp minimum witness lengths. The 2026 Xu–Regan abstract addresses worst-case lower bounds and positive identities, but its entire manuscript could not be inspected; an implicit or unindexed prior treatment remains a priority risk, and absence of search results is not proof of novelty.

## Value (V): PASS

Two-block cyclic conjugates are a canonical family of equal-length distinct positive words; their reversible-automaton separation complexity directly instantiates a current group-identity approach to word separation. The complete characterization reveals an arithmetic *spectrum theorem* (precisely prime-power attainable minimal degrees) and exact sharp extremal word lengths across every prime power. This is an infinite structural classification with a natural extremal interpretation, rather than a renaming of the previously known degree-three identity or a short finite numerical table. The proof explains the exceptional small degrees and their transition to the symmetric-group exponent regime. It does not resolve general worst-case word separation.

The closest inspected primary material is Bulatov et al. (2017), Section 3, which already contains the \(S_3\) identity, the exact short-identity data, and Proposition 15 for different power factors; Demaine et al. (2011) provides general permutation-separation context. Xu and Regan (2026) was checked through publication metadata and abstract only. A less accessible 2010 symmetric-identity conference treatment and possible unindexed group-theoretic exercises could affect full priority. Independent mathematical audit and independent publication-priority assessment have not been performed.
