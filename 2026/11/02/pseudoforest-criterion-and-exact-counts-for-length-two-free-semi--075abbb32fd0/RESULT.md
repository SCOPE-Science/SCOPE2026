# Pseudoforest criterion and exact counts for length-two free-semigroup endomorphisms

## Finding
Let \(\Sigma\) be a labeled alphabet of size \(n\ge1\). Consider an endomorphism \(\varphi:\Sigma^+\to\Sigma^+\) such that every generator image has length exactly two. Let \(W=\{\varphi(a):a\in\Sigma\}\), and regard each distinct word \(uv\in W\) as an undirected edge on the vertex set \(\Sigma\), retaining loops and the distinct opposite directed words \(uv\) and \(vu\) as parallel edges.

The endomorphism is rank-preserving precisely when its images are pairwise distinct and this multigraph is a spanning pseudoforest: each component has exactly one cycle (with loops and parallel-edge two-cycles permitted). Among rank-preserving maps it is irreducible under composition into two nonautomorphisms precisely when the graph is one spanning cycle. In particular, for \(n\ge3\), irreducible image sets are arbitrarily oriented undirected Hamilton cycles; the exceptional sizes \(n=1,2\) correspond to one loop and the opposite-word pair, respectively.

The number \(I_n\) of labeled irreducible length-two endomorphisms is
\[
I_1=1,\qquad I_2=2,\qquad I_n=n!\,2^{n-1}(n-1)!\quad(n\ge3).
\]
The proportion of irreducibles among all length-two endomorphisms with distinct generator images is asymptotic to
\[
\pi\sqrt e\,(2/e^2)^n.
\]
Moreover, the exponential generating function \(F(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}f_nz^n/n!\), where \(f_n\) counts all rank-preserving sets of \(n\) distinct length-two words over a labeled \(n\)-letter alphabet, is
\[
T(z)=\sum_{m\ge1}2^{m-1}m^{m-1}\frac{z^m}{m!},\qquad
F(z)=\frac{\exp(-T(z)^2/2)}{\sqrt{1-2T(z)}}.
\]
The first positive values of \(f_n\) are \(1,6,72,1274,29610\) at \(n=1,2,3,4,5\), respectively. For each \(n\), there are exactly \(n!f_n\) corresponding rank-preserving endomorphisms.

## Assumptions and scope
All words are nonempty; \(\Sigma^+\) is the free semigroup, without an empty word. The rank \(\rho(W)\) of a finite word set is the least cardinality of a set \(V\subseteq\Sigma^+\) satisfying \(W\subseteq V^+\). An endomorphism on \(n\) letters is *rank-preserving* if \(\rho(W)=n\). It is *irreducible* if every composition \(\varphi=\psi_2\circ\psi_1\) of endomorphisms of the same free semigroup contains an automorphism; the automorphisms are precisely alphabet permutations. A pseudoforest is a multigraph with at most one independent cycle per component. In this theorem it has exactly \(n\) edges on \(n\) vertices, so every component is unicyclic. All multiplicities count distinct **words**, not duplicate occurrences of the same generator image.

## Proof
**1. Exact word-set rank.** For \(B\subseteq\Sigma\), write \(e_W(B)=|W\cap B^2|\), counting length-two words whose two letters lie in \(B\). We claim
\[
\rho(W)=\min_{B\subseteq\Sigma}\bigl(|B|+|W|-e_W(B)\bigr).
\]
Indeed, the set \(B\cup(W\setminus B^2)\) generates \(W\), proving the upper bound. Conversely, any generating set \(V\) for \(W\) can be restricted to the words actually used in factorizations of members of \(W\). Because each target has length two, all useful generators of \(V\) have length one or two. Let \(B\) denote its single-letter generators. Every target \(uv\notin B^2\) then must occur as a length-two generator itself. Consequently \(|V|\ge|B|+|W|-e_W(B)\). Removing unused generators can only decrease cardinality, giving equality.

If \(|W|<n\), then \(\rho(W)\le|W|<n\). Suppose henceforth that all \(n\) generator images are distinct, so \(|W|=n\). The displayed identity implies \(\rho(W)=n\) if and only if \(e_W(B)\le|B|\) for every vertex subset \(B\). For a finite undirected multigraph (loops and parallel edges included), these induced-subgraph inequalities characterize pseudoforests: any two cycles in a component have a connected subgraph with more edges than vertices, and a pseudoforest has no such subgraph. Since there are \(n\) edges and vertices in total, all its components are unicyclic.

**2. Proper tight subsets and factorization.** Assume \(\rho(W)=n\). We show that \(\varphi\) is reducible if and only if there exists \(\varnothing\ne B\subsetneq\Sigma\) with \(e_W(B)=|B|\).

Suppose \(\varphi=\psi_2\circ\psi_1\) with both factors nonautomorphisms. Since the rank of the image of a composite does not exceed the rank of either factor's image, both factors preserve rank \(n\). In particular each factor has distinct generator images, and every intermediate letter appears somewhere in the words \(\psi_1(a)\); otherwise the composite could be generated by fewer than \(n\) words. Since each \(\varphi(a)\) has length two, each \(\psi_2(b)\) therefore has length one or two. Let \(B\) be the set of distinct single-letter values among \(\psi_2(b)\); the remaining \(n-|B|\) values have length two. Every length-two word \(\varphi(a)\) must either be one of these longer values or have both letters in \(B\). Thus \(e_W(B)\ge|B|\), whereas rank preservation gives \(e_W(B)\le|B|\). If \(B=\varnothing\), length constraints force \(\psi_1\) to be a permutation; if \(B=\Sigma\), distinctness forces \(\psi_2\) to be a permutation. Both contradict the nonautomorphism condition, so \(B\) is proper nonempty and tight.

Conversely, suppose such a tight \(B\) exists. The set \(V=B\cup(W\setminus B^2)\) has cardinality \(n\), generates \(W\), and has both single-letter and length-two members. Assign these \(n\) distinct words as the images of the intermediate letters under \(\psi_2\). Decode each member of \(W\cap B^2\) as the concatenation of its two letters from \(B\), and each member of \(W\setminus B^2\) as its unique intermediate generator. This defines \(\psi_1\) with \(\varphi=\psi_2\circ\psi_1\). Both factors are nonautomorphisms: \(\psi_2\) has a length-two image, and \(\psi_1\) has a length-two image because \(e_W(B)=|B|>0\).

**3. The single-cycle criterion.** In a unicyclic graph, the vertices on any chosen component's cycle form a tight subset: a cycle with \(r\) vertices has exactly \(r\) induced edges within that subset, because an additional induced edge would create a second cycle. If the graph has more than one component, or its sole cycle does not span all \(n\) vertices, this is a nonempty proper tight subset and the morphism is reducible. Conversely, in a single spanning cycle every nonempty proper induced vertex set spans at most one fewer edge than vertices, so no tight proper subset exists. This includes the loop when \(n=1\) and the opposite-pair double edge when \(n=2\).

**4. Exact enumeration.** When \(n\ge3\), the number of unoriented Hamilton cycles on the labeled vertices is \((n-1)!/2\). Orienting each of their \(n\) edges independently produces \(2^n\) distinct word sets, giving \(2^{n-1}(n-1)!\) sets. For \(n=1\) the loop word is unique, and for \(n=2\) only \(\{ab,ba\}\) qualifies. Assigning the \(n\) distinct image words to the \(n\) generator names produces a factor of \(n!\).

Every distinct-image length-two morphism gives one of \(\binom{n^2}{n}\) possible word sets and each set admits \(n!\) assignments. Thus the irreducible fraction for \(n\ge3\) is \(2^{n-1}(n-1)!/\binom{n^2}{n}\). Writing \(\binom{n^2}{n}=n^{2n}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(1-j/n^2)/n!\), the product tends to \(e^{-1/2}\), and Stirling's formula gives the asserted asymptotic.

**5. Enumeration of all rank-preserving sets.** A connected component has a unique cycle and outward attached rooted trees. A tree rooted at a specified core vertex on \(m\) labeled vertices has \(m^{m-1}\) rooted labeled structures and each of its \(m-1\) edges can independently be represented by either ordered word, yielding the rooted-tree EGF \(T(z)\). A cycle of length one contributes \(T\), a cycle of length two contributes \(T^2/2\) because it must contain both opposite words, and a cycle of length \(\ell\ge3\) contributes \(2^{\ell-1}T^\ell/\ell\) from an unoriented labeled cycle with two orientations per edge. Hence the connected-component EGF is
\[
C(z)=T+\frac12T^2+\sum_{\ell\ge3}\frac{2^{\ell-1}}{\ell}T^\ell
=-\frac12\log(1-2T)-\frac12T^2.
\]
The exponential formula for finite sets of labeled connected components gives \(F=\exp C\). This proves the generating function and the exact coefficient counts.

## Relationship to prior work
Bell, Foster, and Reidenbach introduced the relevant composition irreducibility question and established a general factor-basis characterization for rank-preserving endomorphisms. Their March 2026 manuscript is the earliest publicly identified version; an October 2026 version develops the result further. Their general condition motivates the present exact length-two factor-rank formula, multigraph characterization, and full enumerations, which require additional specific combinatorics not stated in their investigated sections. Classical graph enumeration and Cayley tree counts supply standard counting ingredients; originality is claimed only for the combined specialized algebraic classification and enumeration, not for these established techniques or the general factor-basis theorem.

## Limitations
The theorem classifies **only** constant-length-two endomorphisms and gives counts for word-image sets with distinct words. It does not classify all reducible endomorphisms by irreducible factorization length, nor extend to arbitrary image lengths. The quoted asymptotic denominator includes only distinct-image endomorphisms. Exhaustive computational verification covers \(n\le5\); all higher-rank assertions rely on the supplied proofs. Historical priority may be affected by an equivalent pre-existing graph-theoretic specialization under different terminology.
