# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The algebra is explicitly transported from the defining dihedral-quandle multiplication into the cyclic group ring. In characteristic five, a concrete local parameter diagonalizes the substitution automorphism. The resulting four coordinate expressions for a bracket are verified by coefficient comparison. Normalization of a pair of inputs with a nonzero constant term reduces the only obstructed case to a quadratic equation; the discriminant gives a necessary condition. Constructed arguments cover each sufficient case and give a universal two-bracket expression. Counting nonsquare discriminants proves the exact finite-field formula. The independent direct-product implementation over the prime field reproduced the full image: 585 single-bracket values and 40 obstructions. Exact field arithmetic over 25 elements verified the 7200-value discriminant count. No extrapolation from finite tests is used in the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority risk
Sangare and Ta, *Idempotents, automorphism groups, and commutator widths of quandle algebras* (arXiv:2608.01550v2, Section 5, Theorem 5.4), previously found commutator width two and exactly 40 length-two elements for the five-element dihedral quandle algebra over the five-element field. Those statements are explicitly excluded from claims of novelty. The new result explains those 40 exceptional elements through an exact discriminant in a canonical local coordinate and extends the count and criterion to all fields of characteristic five, including the formula for all finite extensions and the quadratically-closed-field width-one phase.

Zhang, *Group-ring methods for Alexander quandle rings* (arXiv:2610.08204v1, Theorem 2.3), already identifies the full linear span/nonunital algebra of commutators for all Alexander quandles. In the present case it establishes the full augmentation ideal, but it does not classify the image of a **single** commutator or count the elements needing two. The relevant full-text sections of both papers were inspected. Targeted searches for the discriminant, extension-field formula, and equivalent twisted-bracket descriptions found no direct coverage in the accessible mathematical records. This is not a guarantee of absolute historical priority: earlier work on twisted commutators or bilinear-map images could potentially contain an equivalent specialization.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The distinction between a commutator subalgebra and the image of one commutator is a genuine representation-length problem, and a recent exhaustive computation exposed a characteristic-five anomaly at the smallest odd dihedral example. The theorem supplies its exact square-class mechanism, determines **every** element's commutator length over **every** characteristic-five field, and determines complete multiplicities over every finite extension. The field-dependent dichotomy (quadratically closed fields versus fields with nonsquares) is structural, not a numerical increment or an unmotivated finite census. It does not solve the commutator-width problem for other quandle orders or characteristics.

## Closest literature and limitations
The exact forty-element \(\mathbb F_5\) count and the general description of the commutator-generated ideal are prior theorems. The new result is the single-commutator discriminant criterion and its consequences over arbitrary characteristic-five fields. Finite checks are reproducible but do not constitute independent mathematical audit. The source comparison is substantial rather than exhaustive; the historical-priority risk is explicit. The count is of algebra elements, not isomorphism classes.
