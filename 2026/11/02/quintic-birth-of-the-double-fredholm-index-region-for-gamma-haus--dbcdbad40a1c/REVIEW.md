# Mathematical review

## Correctness
The proof reduces the operator's Fredholm index to a published count of Mellin-transform preimages, maps the right half-plane bijectively to a tangent disk, and counts the two possible angular branches for \(2<\alpha<4\). Polar integration gives the exact area and a uniform small-angle expansion gives the fifth-order constant. The supporting checker tests representative phase boundaries and exact evaluations; it is not an infinite-dimensional certificate.

## Mathematical value
The measure of the index-two locus quantifies how much of the complex spectral plane acquires an additional Fredholm range defect when the Gamma order crosses the first multivalency threshold. The sharp fifth-order law is a natural phase-transition invariant, and the entire interval of exponents and its minimal nontrivial integer case are determined. It is not a claim about all Gamma orders or a general spectral area theorem.
