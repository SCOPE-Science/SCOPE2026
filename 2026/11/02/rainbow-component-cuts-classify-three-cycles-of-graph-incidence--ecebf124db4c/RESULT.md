# Rainbow component cuts classify three-cycles of graph-incidence rowmotion

## Finding
Let \(G=(V,E)\) be any finite, connected, undirected simple graph with at least one edge. Let \(P(G)\) be the height-two poset consisting of \(V\sqcup E\), with \(v<e\) exactly when vertex \(v\) is an endpoint of edge \(e\). Rowmotion \(\rho\) sends an order ideal to the ideal generated by the minimal elements of its complement.

For every independent vertex set \(X\subseteq V\), consider the connected components of the induced graph \(G-X\). Let \(R_3(G,X)\) be the number of assignments of the three **named** colours \(0,1,2\) to those components such that every \(x\in X\) has a neighbour in a component of **each** of the three colours. If \(X=\varnothing\), the condition is vacuous; connectivity gives \(R_3(G,\varnothing)=3\). Then the number \(c_3(G)\) of primitive period-three **orbits** of \(\rho\) is exactly

\[
c_3(G)=\frac13\sum_{X\subseteq V:\,X\text{ independent}}R_3(G,X).
\]

More precisely, the period-three orbits are in bijection with admissible pairs \((X,\phi)\), modulo simultaneous cyclic rotation of the three colour names. This includes the universal orbit \(\varnothing\to V\to V\sqcup E\to\varnothing\), arising from \(X=\varnothing\). There are no fixed ideals of \(\rho\).

For all positive integers \(a,b\), the specialization to the complete bipartite graph is

\[
c_3(K_{a,b})=3^{a-1}+3^{b-1}-2^a-2^b+3.
\]

For every connected graph of maximum degree at most two, \(c_3(G)=1\). The complete-bipartite specialization at \(a=2\) agrees with a previously obtained special-family count and is **not** asserted to be new; the contribution is the graph-wide structural classification and counting reduction.

## Assumptions and scope
All graphs are finite, connected, simple, undirected, with at least one edge. Hence no vertex is isolated. Colour names \(0,1,2\) are distinguished for counting, but a cyclic rotation of names is identified when passing to rowmotion orbits. A component colouring does **not** have to be a proper graph colouring: each component of \(G-X\) is assigned a single colour, and different components may receive the same colour. \(X\) may be empty, and the empty case is explicitly included. Rowmotion acts on ordinary lower order ideals, not antichains or birational labels.

## Proof
Represent an ideal as \((S,T)\), with \(S\subseteq V\) and \(T\subseteq E(S)\), where \(E(S)=\{uv\in E:u,v\in S\}\). Direct inspection of minimal omitted elements gives the exact update

\[
\rho(S,T)=\big((V\setminus S)\cup\operatorname{end}(E(S)\setminus T),\;E(S)\setminus T\big),
\]

where \(\operatorname{end}(F)\) is the union of endpoints of edges in \(F\).

Suppose \((S_i,T_i)\), indexed modulo three, form a \(\rho^3\)-fixed triple. At every vertex \(v\), the update implies \(v\in S_i\cup S_{i+1}\) for all \(i\), so the three membership bits have weight two or three. Put \(X=\bigcap_iS_i\) and \(Y_i=V\setminus S_i\). The \(Y_i\) partition \(V\setminus X\).

For an edge \(e\), write \(a_i(e)=\mathbf1_{e\in E(S_i)}\) and \(t_i(e)=\mathbf1_{e\in T_i}\). The update says \(t_{i+1}=a_i-t_i\), so cycling three times yields \(2t_0=a_0+a_2-a_1\). Because all bits are zero or one, the triple \((a_0,a_1,a_2)\) must have **even** Hamming weight: either zero or two. Consequently two vertices of \(X\) cannot be adjacent (their edge would have three ones), and vertices of distinct \(Y_i\) cannot be adjacent (their edge would have exactly one one). Thus \(X\) is independent, and each connected component of \(G-X\) lies inside one \(Y_i\).

An edge with at least one endpoint in \(Y_j\) (and its other endpoint in \(X\cup Y_j\)) belongs to the unique edge state \(T_{j+2}\), subscripts modulo three. Therefore

\[
T_i=E(G[X\cup Y_{i+1}]).
\]

Here the induced edge set contains no edge inside \(X\), since \(X\) is independent. Every \(x\in X\) stays in all three successive vertex states, and each successive appearance must be supplied by an endpoint of the newly selected edges. It follows that \(x\) has at least one neighbour in each \(Y_0,Y_1,Y_2\). This is exactly the rainbow-neighbour condition for the component colouring \(\phi\).

Conversely, start with independent \(X\) and an admissible colouring of the components of \(G-X\), and let \(Y_i\) be the union of components coloured \(i\). Define

\[
S_i=V\setminus Y_i,\qquad T_i=E(G[X\cup Y_{i+1}]),\qquad i\in\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z.
\]

The three induced edge sets are disjoint, every edge of \(G\) lies in exactly one of them, and \(E(S_i)=T_i\sqcup T_{i+1}\). Thus the edge part of \(\rho(S_i,T_i)\) is \(T_{i+1}\). The vertex part is \(Y_i\cup\operatorname{end}(T_{i+1})\). Every vertex in \(Y_{i+2}\) is incident to an edge (since \(G\) has no isolated vertices), and every vertex in \(X\) has a neighbour in \(Y_{i+2}\) by the rainbow condition. Therefore \(\operatorname{end}(T_{i+1})=X\cup Y_{i+2}\) and the new vertex part is \(S_{i+1}\). Hence these three ideals form an orbit whose period divides three.

There are no fixed ideals: if \(I\) is proper, a minimal element of its nonempty complement belongs to \(\rho(I)\) but not to \(I\); and the full ideal maps to the empty ideal. Thus the constructed orbits have primitive period three. Relabelling \(Y_i\mapsto Y_{i+1}\) merely shifts the chosen starting ideal around the same orbit, and no nontrivial cyclic relabelling fixes an admissible colouring (because \(G-X\neq\varnothing\)). Every orbit supplies exactly three labelled colourings, proving the factor \(1/3\) and the bijection.

For \(K_{a,b}\), any admissible nonempty \(X\) lies in one bipartition class. The rainbow condition forces \(X\) to be **that entire class**, and then the opposite class may be coloured by any surjection onto \(\{0,1,2\}\). There are \(3^t-3\cdot2^t+3\) such surjections on a nonempty \(t\)-element set. Adding the empty-set case gives the displayed closed formula. If \(\Delta(G)\le2\), no vertex of nonempty \(X\) can see three colours, leaving only the universal orbit.

## Verification
Two standalone Python 3 programs require only the standard library. Run `python verify_direct.py` and `python verify_structural.py` in the same directory as this document.

The first program independently implements rowmotion by determining minimal omitted poset elements (without using the displayed \((S,T)\) formula), enumerates **all 771 connected labelled simple graphs** with two through five vertices, and compares the actual primitive-three-cycle count with the rainbow-partition count. The observed count frequencies by graph order are: order two \(\{1:1\}\), order three \(\{1:4\}\), order four \(\{1:34,3:4\}\), and order five \(\{1:623,3:100,13:5\}\).

The second program constructs all three explicit ideals from each admissible four-way vertex labelling and checks all three rowmotion transitions. It verifies the closed formula for every \(K_{a,b}\) with \(1\le a,b\le4\), and the unique-orbit assertion for cycles of lengths three through eight. The code checks finitely many examples; the unrestricted-graph theorem relies on the proof above, not an extrapolation from experiments.

## Relationship to prior work
Striker and Williams' *Promotion and Rowmotion* (first arXiv posting 2011-08-04, arXiv:1108.1172) is a foundational source for the ordinary order-ideal action and its toggle-group representation. Striker's *Rowmotion and generalized toggle groups* (arXiv:1601.03710, first posted 2016-01-14) develops broader toggle actions, including independent sets of graphs; the graph-incidence three-cycle statement proved here concerns a different action and is not entailed by those general group statements. Specific earlier incidence-poset computations for complete bipartite graphs contain the \(K_{2,n}\) numerical specialization, and previous classifications of period-two graph-incidence orbits concern a different orbit length. The general independent-cut/rainbow-component correspondence is the claim assessed here.

## Limitations
This result counts only primitive period-three orbits, not all rowmotion orbits or the full permutation order. It requires connectivity and at least one edge; disconnected graphs are not covered by the stated formula without additional component-period bookkeeping. No computational complexity or asymptotic optimality claim is made for arbitrary graph inputs. A historically equivalent formulation could exist outside the literature and databases inspected; searches are not a proof of priority.
