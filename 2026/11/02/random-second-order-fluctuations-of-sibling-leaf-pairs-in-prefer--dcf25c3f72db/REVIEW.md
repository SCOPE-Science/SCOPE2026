# Scientific review: sibling-leaf pairs in preferential attachment trees

## Correctness — PASS
The specified model begins with a single edge and uses attachment probabilities proportional to current *undirected* degrees. The proof independently derives all conditional drifts of the quadratic degree sum, degree-weighted leaf-neighbor sum, sibling-leaf-pair count and cubic degree sum, including the leaf versus nonleaf transition. The harmonic finite-size expectation follows by telescoping. Crucially, the cubic-degree estimate is used to establish summable quadratic variations for the quadratic-degree martingale and the two remaining innovation series. Martingale convergence, Kronecker's lemma, and weighted Cesàro summation then establish the almost-sure and \(L^2\) random limit. Nondegeneracy follows from the equal-probability star/path states at size four. The independent exact-rational enumerator passed 184,947 finite-state assertions through size nine. These computations test the transition formulas; the infinite assertion rests on the analytic proof.

## Originality — PASS, with bounded priority risk
Hartle–Krapivsky, arXiv:2602.01021v1 (2026), determine fixed-class leafdegree statistics and a leafdegree tail asymptotic to \(\ell^{-3}\). The divergent quadratic weighted aggregate motivating this theorem cannot be inferred by interchanging the infinite-degree summation with their fixed-degree limiting fractions. Their examined main results, fixed-degree variance sections and appendices do not give the present exact finite mean or nondegenerate order-\(n\) limit. Magner–Janson–Kollias–Szpankowski (2014), examined in the full paper's symmetry and cherry arguments, use common-parent leaf pairs as structural witnesses of graph automorphisms but do not derive their number's present asymptotic or random limit. Holmgren–Janson–Šileikis, arXiv:1603.08125 (2016), establish normal limits for *fixed finite* fringe-shape counts, not this sum over unbounded degrees with logarithmic mean and order-\(n\) random fluctuations. Focused public literature searches and independent mathematical-record queries were also compared for synonymous leaf sibling pairs, cherries and quadratic leafdegree sums; no covering statement was identified. Results on quadratic degree martingales may supply a part of the proof but do not alone imply the cherry-count transfer. Unidentified general additive-functional results remain a priority risk and prevent any claim of exhaustive novelty certification.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The recently identified \(\ell^{-3}\) leafdegree law leaves the global second factorial moment outside the regime of fixed-degree statistics: its tail suggests logarithmic divergence but does not establish an exact random correction. The theorem identifies both the exact \(n\log n\) coefficient and the source of the surviving order-\(n\) randomness, an inherited quadratic-degree martingale rather than ordinary square-root Gaussian fluctuations. It is a natural graph invariant, counts all leaf siblings without arbitrary cutoffs, and distinguishes an infinite-support aggregate from the familiar fixed-fringe census. The exact finite-size formula and nondegeneracy make the result directly testable and potentially useful in topology/symmetry statistics for growing trees.
