# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

Every Lie endomorphism has three parameters: a quotient linear map, a central functional, and the central scalar. The bracket equation is necessary and sufficient, including characteristic two. Idempotence makes the central scalar zero or one. In the scalar-one branch the symplectic condition and projection property force identity; in the zero branch the projected image must be totally isotropic. The isotropic-subspace, complement, and functional counts are multiplicative and nonoverlapping. Their degree formula has successive differences \(4m-5r+4\), which proves the one-rank versus two-rank transition. The embedded enumerator checks all candidate endomorphisms in finite tested fields, including all \(4\times4\) quotient matrices over \(\mathbb F_2\). No computational extension beyond enumerated ranges is assumed.

## Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources

Balla–Olarte (arXiv:2103.16512v1) establishes the symplectic-subspace context, not Lie-retraction counting. Rossmann (arXiv:2107.05564v1) examines graphical Lie algebras, finite-field group conjugacy and associated zeta functions, not the rank distribution of idempotent endomorphisms. arXiv:2405.09652v1 addresses spaces of commuting Heisenberg-group elements. Closest research records concern Heisenberg subalgebra commutativity, centralizer lattices, or automorphisms, none of which determines the retraction spectrum from its assertion. General isotropic Grassmannian counts and projection-complement counts are recognized as standard. The new component under review is the complete rank census and its asymptotic phase transition in dimension \(2m+1\), \(m\ge2\). Residual risk: an equivalent formula may be present in work on idempotents of symplectic-similitude monoids or on Lie-algebra retractions under a different name. Search coverage is not proof of priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Retracts form a canonical family of subalgebras, and their rank spectrum is a natural invariant of a Lie algebra. The theorem handles an unrestricted positive-dimensional Heisenberg family in every field characteristic, quantifies the imbalance between the abundance of isotropic images and freedom of complementary kernels, and identifies the non-obvious modulo-five transition of typical retract dimension. This goes beyond a low-dimensional census or a relabeling of the standard Grassmannian formula.

## Limits and evidence

The claim is for linear Lie endomorphisms over finite fields and for fixed dimension in the large-field limit. The proof is independent of exhaustive enumeration; tests cover \(m=2,q=2\) and boundary \(m=1\) cases. No peer or external audit has been performed. The literature comparison includes relevant full-text materials and semantic research queries, but not every possible equivalent source.
