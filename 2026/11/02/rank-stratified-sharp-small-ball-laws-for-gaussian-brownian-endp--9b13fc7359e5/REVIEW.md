## Value — PASS

The exact Brownian endpoint classification yields a natural three-stratum covariance geometry that is not quantitatively resolved by simply naming its Gaussian processes. Confinement in a narrow tube is an intrinsic continuous-path small-deviation probability used for rare-event calibration and supremum-based inference. At fixed covariance, the prefactor jumps from inverse tolerance to a constant to direct tolerance at ranks zero, one and two, despite having the same exponential rate. A single **covariance-uniform** formula retains validity through covariance eigenvalues of order \(\varepsilon^2\), where naive fixed-rank formulas cease to be valid; it also identifies the observable endpoint law given rare confinement. This resolves a mathematically motivated boundary/crossover question rather than an arbitrary parameter sample, even though no broad downstream theorem is asserted.
