# Scientific review — rare-corner first-order ANOVA projection

## Correctness
PASS. The projector kernel follows directly from the orthonormal centered-coordinate basis. A two-point convex-order comparison proves that each coordinate of a maximizing evaluation vertex may be selected as the less probable outcome, without a symmetry or equal-bias assumption. The sign of that kernel attains the exact \(L^\infty\) norm, and its negative part determines the sharp positivity defect. Reapplying the same convex order as a rare probability increases proves bias monotonicity. The fair parity formula is an exact binomial identity. Gaussian and Poisson limits use standard distributional convergence with explicit uniform integrability; Poisson truncation uses an exact factorial-moment identity. The finite exact-arithmetic checker corroborates the kernel, extremizer, defect and formulas, but does not prove the infinite statements by enumeration.

## Originality
PASS, subject to the specifically noted possibility of prior implicit equivalent coverage. Searches tested biased Walsh, first-order Hoeffding projection, affine ANOVA, sharp \(L^\infty\) norms, sign extremizers, and convex-order rare-outcome reductions. De Wolf (2008) supplies the standard *uniform* Fourier setup, not the arbitrary-bias norm. Nayar (2014) handles biased Boolean Fourier concentration and \(L^2\) FKN approximation, not this exact \(L^\infty\) operator calculation. Owen's ANOVA exposition defines the same additive projector but studies optimality in \(L^2\), rather than its sharp amplification in \(L^\infty\). Rzeszut–Wojciechowski (2019) treats more general Hoeffding projections on infinite products and Hardy/\(L^p\) endpoint estimates, which do not supply the current finite exact sharp bias formula. The nearest recorded projections on a deleted Boolean cube, simplex products and Johnson slices have different measures or projection ranges. The fair-binomial identity alone is not claimed as original.

## Value
PASS. The first-order Hoeffding/ANOVA truncation is a standard approximation to multivariable data. Its sharp pointwise amplification and the largest possible loss of positivity were not determined by its \(L^2\) best-approximation property. The heterogeneous formula provides an attained exact stability invariant for every product design, shows why a simultaneous rare corner is worst, proves that balancing probabilities never worsens the endpoint norm, and determines both an exact fair-design minimum and the sharp rare-design supremum. A Poisson profile quantifies the physically natural sampling regime in which the expected count of rare coordinates remains bounded. These are object-driven bounds and structural transitions, not selected parameter-table entries.
