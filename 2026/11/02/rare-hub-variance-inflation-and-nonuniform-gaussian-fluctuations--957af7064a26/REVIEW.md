# Scientific review

**Correctness (C): PASS.** The exact event partition by the first-priority hub versus rim vertices gives the full finite wheel law, and its conditional-variance and third-moment identities are algebraic consequences. The path first-acceptance recurrence gives a formal power-series ODE and exact analytic first/two-factorial-moment functions. Their poles establish the required moment coefficients; Theorem 11 of Penrose--Sudbury supplies the established one-dimensional central limit theorem. A decreasing-priority dependency-truncation argument yields a fourth-moment estimate strong enough for both the sharp lower tail and the third-moment asymptotic. Exact rational independent-permutation checks support 62 finite cases, but neither certify infinite proofs nor replace them. Main risk is any hidden normalization discrepancy between the path model and terminal blocking RSA; the papers define arrival priorities uniformly over the unit interval and the exact coefficient calculation independently anchors the moments.

**Originality (O): PASS (qualified).** The claimed new unit is a *wheel* classification: exact law, normal weak limit versus inflated variance, sharp hub-induced rare left tail, and skewness divergence. Penrose--Sudbury already give the path variance and CLT, and those are explicitly credited. Krivelevich et al. treat greedy densities and variance of the normalized density under local-growth assumptions, not the wheel's unnormalized variance and failure of moment convergence. Searched available probability research records for wheel greedy independent sets, blocking adsorption on wheels, rare high-degree hub fluctuation effects and equivalent normal/variance/third-moment statements; no implication-equivalent record was found. Closest records on complete multipartite, threshold and chain graphs do not imply a result on \(K_1\vee C_n\). Risk: a generic rare-vertex conditioning result, or a wheel-specific result outside inspected literature, could already contain this elementary but motivated mechanism; novelty is not independently certified.

**Value (V): PASS.** Wheel graphs form the smallest natural example combining a bounded-degree backbone with a single unbounded-degree vertex. The exact discrepancy between distributional Gaussian fluctuations and finite second moments shows why bounded-degree adsorption central limit theorems cannot be naively extended to uniform moment bounds. A sharp polynomial lower-tail probability and diverging skewness reveal a concrete structural hazard invisible to the limiting occupation density; the result is a motivated quantitative boundary rather than an arbitrary small-parameter computation. It is not a general graph-sequence theorem.
