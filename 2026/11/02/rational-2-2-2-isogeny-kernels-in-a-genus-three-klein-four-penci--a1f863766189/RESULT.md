# Rational \((2,2,2)\)-isogeny kernels in a genus-three Klein-four pencil

## Finding

Let \(q\) be an odd prime power and let \(a,b\in\mathbb F_q\) satisfy
\[
(a-b)(a^2-4)(b^2-4)\ne0.
\]
For the smooth genus-three hyperelliptic curve
\[
C_{a,b}:\quad y^2=(x^4+a x^2+1)(x^4+b x^2+1),
\]
let \(J_{a,b}=\operatorname{Jac}(C_{a,b})\). A *rational maximal isotropic kernel* means an order-eight finite étale subgroup scheme \(H\subset J_{a,b}[2]\), defined over \(\mathbb F_q\), that is maximal totally isotropic for the Weil pairing. Distinct kernels, rather than distinct isogeny targets, are counted.

Put
\[
S_q=\frac{q-1-\gcd(4,q-1)}4,\qquad U_q=q-2-S_q.
\]
Then all admissible **ordered** coefficient pairs \((a,b)\) fall into exactly the following three arithmetic strata:

| Frobenius cycles on eight branch points | Ordered pairs | \(\#J_{a,b}[2](\mathbb F_q)\) | Rational maximal isotropic kernels | Matching / affine-type kernels |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(1^8\) | \(S_q(S_q-1)\) | \(64\) | \(135\) | \(105/30\) |
| \(1^4 2^2\) | \(2S_qU_q\) | \(16\) | \(15\) | \(9/6\) |
| \(2^4\) | \(U_q(U_q-1)\) | \(16\) | \(39\) | \(25/14\) |

Here *matching* indicates a Lagrangian subgroup arising from a partition of the eight geometric Weierstrass points into four unordered pairs. *Affine-type* indicates one of the complementary thirty Lagrangians, naturally parametrized by affine \(\mathbb F_2^3\)-structures on the eight points, up to affine-coordinate change. The counts total \((q-2)(q-3)\) admissible ordered pairs, including the vacuous case \(q=3\).

In particular, the two nonsplit strata have **identical rational two-torsion groups**, each isomorphic to \((\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^4\), but their numbers of rational maximal isotropic kernels differ by \(15\) versus \(39\). Every one of the \(135\) geometric maximal isotropic kernels becomes defined over \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\).

## Assumptions and scope

The characteristic is any odd prime, with no lower bound beyond oddness. The polynomial has eight distinct roots precisely under the displayed conditions. All branch points are finite; the two points above infinity are unramified. The stated frequencies count ordered elements of the given affine coefficient chart. They do not count geometric isomorphism classes, independent isogeny targets, isogeny maps with chosen codomain identifications, or extra automorphisms. No superspecial or ordinary hypothesis is imposed. The complete list of \(135\) kernels is over an algebraic closure; the rational count is the number of Frobenius-stable subgroup schemes.

## Proof

**1. Quartic splitting and coefficient frequencies.** Fix \(t\in\mathbb F_q\setminus\{-2,2\}\) and put \(Q_t(X)=X^4+tX^2+1\). If \(r\) is a geometric root, its four distinct roots are \(r,-r,r^{-1},-r^{-1}\). The Klein four-group generated by \(r\mapsto-r\) and \(r\mapsto r^{-1}\) acts freely and transitively on these roots. Frobenius commutes with both transformations. Therefore its action on the roots is either the identity or one of three fixed-point-free involutions, with cycle type \(2^2\). In particular, **all four roots lie in \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\)**, and a nonsplit quartic has no \(\mathbb F_q\)-root.

The polynomial splits completely over \(\mathbb F_q\) exactly when \(r\in\mathbb F_q^\times\) satisfies \(r^4\ne1\) and
\[
t=-(r^2+r^{-2}).
\]
Every split \(t\) has exactly four preimages, namely \(r,-r,r^{-1},-r^{-1}\). Since \(\#\{r\in\mathbb F_q^\times:r^4=1\}=\gcd(4,q-1)\), there are exactly \(S_q\) split parameters and \(U_q\) nonsplit parameters. The two quartics have disjoint root sets when \(a\ne b\), and their splitting types combine independently. Thus the Frobenius types on eight points and the three ordered-pair frequencies are exactly those displayed. Their sum is \((S_q+U_q)(S_q+U_q-1)=(q-2)(q-3)\).

**2. The symplectic two-division module.** Let \(\Omega\) be the eight geometric branch points and write \(E(\Omega)\) for their even-cardinality subsets, with addition by symmetric difference. The standard hyperelliptic identification is
\[
J_{a,b}[2](\overline{\mathbb F}_q)
\cong E(\Omega)/\langle\Omega\rangle,
\]
a six-dimensional symplectic \(\mathbb F_2\)-space. The Weil pairing corresponds to \(\langle[A],[B]\rangle=|A\cap B|\pmod2\), which is well-defined on classes because both subsets are even. Frobenius permutes the eight branch points and acts on this quotient.

For the identity permutation every one of the \(64\) classes is rational. For type \(1^4 2^2\), a Frobenius-fixed class must have a fixed even-subset representative: the complement alternative would contradict any fixed point. Choosing an even subset of four fixed points and arbitrary unions of the two transposition orbits gives \(2^3 2^2/2=16\) classes modulo complementation. For type \(2^4\), Frobenius fixes \(2^4\) unions of full transposition orbits, giving \(8\) classes modulo complementation; it also sends to their complements the \(2^4\) subsets selecting exactly one point from each orbit (all even), giving another \(8\) classes. Hence both nonsplit types yield \(16\) rational two-torsion points. As the characteristic is odd, these are elementary abelian groups of rank four.

**3. All maximal isotropic kernels.** A maximal isotropic subgroup of this symplectic six-space has dimension three. Its geometric number is
\[
(2+1)(2^2+1)(2^3+1)=135.
\]
A perfect matching of \(\Omega\) into four pairs gives the Lagrangian spanned by their four pair classes (one linear relation), and distinct matchings give distinct Lagrangians. There are \(8!/(2^4 4!)=105\) of them. Conversely any Lagrangian containing a class represented by a two-element subset corresponds, after deleting that pair, to a Lagrangian on the remaining six branch points; the three nonzero elements of that genus-two Lagrangian form a matching of those six points. Thus every Lagrangian with a two-element class comes from a unique matching.

Exactly \(135-105=30\) Lagrangians have no two-element class. To describe them, label \(\Omega\) by the eight points of \(\mathbb F_2^3\) and take the classes of the seven nonconstant affine hyperplane partitions, or equivalently the seven nonzero linear functionals (modulo complementary level sets). These form a three-dimensional isotropic subspace with no weight-two classes. Its permutation stabilizer is \(\operatorname{AGL}(3,2)\), of order \(8\cdot168=1344\), because its seven hyperplane partitions recover the affine geometry. The \(S_8\)-orbit therefore has size \(8!/1344=30\), exhausting all nonmatching Lagrangians.

**4. Counting Frobenius-fixed matching kernels.** For the identity all \(105\) matchings are fixed. For \(1^4 2^2\), the four fixed points must be matched among themselves in \(3\) ways, while the two moving transposition orbits admit \(3\) invariant pairings, giving \(9\). For \(2^4\), pair either zero, two, or four transposition orbits with each other, retaining internal pairings for the unused orbits. The count is
\[
1+\binom42\cdot2+3\cdot2^2=25.
\]

**5. Counting Frobenius-fixed affine kernels.** The thirty affine kernels constitute the coset space \(S_8/H\), where \(H=\operatorname{AGL}(3,2)\). For a fixed permutation \(\sigma\in S_8\), the number of invariant cosets is
\[
\frac{|C_{S_8}(\sigma)|\,|H\cap\sigma^{S_8}|}{|H|}.
\]
There are exactly \(21\) transvections in \(\operatorname{GL}(3,2)\): choose a nonzero functional in seven ways and a nonzero vector in its two-dimensional kernel in three ways. For each, the four translations that yield affine involutions divide into two with four fixed points and two without fixed points. Hence \(H\) has \(42\) elements of cycle type \(1^4 2^2\). Its \(2^4\) elements comprise the other \(42\) affine transvection lifts and the seven nonidentity translations, for a total of \(49\). The corresponding symmetric-group centralizers have orders \(4!\,2^2 2!=192\) and \(2^4 4!=384\), respectively. The fixed affine-kernel counts are therefore
\[
\frac{192\cdot42}{1344}=6,
\qquad
\frac{384\cdot49}{1344}=14.
\]
The identity fixes all \(30\). Adding matching and affine counts yields \(135,15,39\). This completes the enumeration. Because Frobenius squared acts trivially on all eight branch points, it acts trivially on every geometric Lagrangian, so all kernels descend to \(\mathbb F_{q^2}\).

## Relationship to prior work

Borówka and Shatsila (arXiv:2308.07038, Theorem 3.3 and Propositions 4.2–4.3) establish the genus-three hyperelliptic family, its Klein-four symmetries, its quotient curves, and its complex Prym geometry. These foundational facts are not claimed as new here. The general number \(135\) of maximal isotropic kernels for an abelian threefold is also standard (see Ohashi–Onuki–Kudo–Yoshizumi–Nuida, arXiv:2401.10500, Proposition 2.3); likewise, classical descriptions of hyperelliptic two-division and affine geometry are used without novelty claims.

The present statement concerns the **arithmetic descent of all \(135\) kernels in this specific finite-field two-parameter family**, including exact frequencies and the split of rational kernels into matching and affine types. The cited primary literature does not state these exact three Frobenius-dependent counts and their frequencies. Existing finite-field studies of the same family that focus on Hasse–Weil numerators, Cartier rank, or complex Prym quotients do not imply the full Frobenius-stable Lagrangian census: in particular, the two nonsplit strata have the same rational two-torsion rank but different numbers of invariant Lagrangians. An equivalent calculation in other literature remains possible.

## Limitations

The classification is for the indicated coefficient chart, not isomorphism classes of curves or isogeny targets. Rational kernels may have nonrational geometric generators; subgroup descent is the criterion used. No explicit equations for the quotient principally polarized abelian threefolds are supplied and no claim is made that every quotient is a Jacobian. The existence of separately listed matching/affine-type kernels is not asserted to distinguish targets up to isomorphism. No independent mathematical validation has been performed. The historical-priority search cannot rule out unindexed or differently parametrized prior work.
