# Review of the rational idempotent Nijenhuis orbit theorem

## Correctness — PASS

The classification follows from the direct Nijenhuis-projector torsion identity, the full classification of two-dimensional subalgebras of \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) over characteristic different from two, and an explicit calculation of upper-triangular conjugation on the affine plane of complements to a fixed Borel. The coordinate \(u=b+a^2\) transforms by multiplication by a square, leaving three and only three rational orbits. Fibre orbit sizes multiplied by the \(q+1\) Borels give the exact global spectrum. The rank-two version is the involution \(N\mapsto I-N\); ranks zero and three give the two points. Over an algebraically closed field, each nonzero square class merges into one open orbit, and the incidence space is a smooth affine two-space bundle over the smooth Borel conic. The attached checker independently tests original torsion identities, subalgebra closure, the finite-field orbit calculation, and all ambient three-by-three operators over \(\mathbb F_3\); it additionally checks full projective-group conjugation orbits for \(q=3,5\). There is no claim that the finite checks prove the infinite family.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk

The 2001 classical projection criterion is already known and not claimed. The previously published finite-field three-dimensional Lie-subalgebra-plane theorem proves the \(q+1\) Borel count and makes the total operator count a direct corollary; that total is not the novelty. The 2026 complex BiHom-Lie paper, inspected in its untwisted \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) special case, classifies all complex Nijenhuis matrices but does not supply the odd-finite-field rational \(\operatorname{PGL}_2\) orbit spectrum. The contribution assessed is narrowly the eight orbits, their stabilizers/sizes, and the split of one geometric open orbit into two distinct rational square-class orbits at each nontrivial rank. A more general treatment of Lie-algebra factorizations with rational orbit invariants might have appeared under different terminology; publication priority is not independently certified.

## Value — PASS

Conjugacy-orbit classification is a natural stronger invariant than a raw count of Nijenhuis projections: equal-size rational semisimple types are provably distinct under the operator-preserving automorphism action. The theorem gives a complete arithmetic and geometric classification of the idempotent-integrable projector locus of a standard simple Lie algebra for all odd finite fields. The square-class splitting and stabilizer orders supply structural information absent from the earlier unweighted subalgebra census. The underlying parameter space is canonical (complementary Lie-subalgebra factorizations), not an artificial slice.

## Closest sources and limitations

- Cariñena, Grabowski, Marmo (2001), arXiv:math/0103103, Theorem 5: established Nijenhuis projection criterion; no newness claimed for it.
- *Plane counts control subalgebra commutativity in dimension three* (published result, 2026): determines \(q+1\) subalgebra planes for \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) and counts unweighted factorizations, but neither mentions Nijenhuis operators nor provides automorphism-conjugacy orbits.
- Mosbahi (2026), arXiv:2609.09392, especially Theorems 3.1 and 11.1: complex classification, including the untwisted \(\mathfrak{sl}_2\) case; no finite-field rational square-class analysis.
- Liu, Sheng, Zhou, Bai (2016), arXiv:1601.02356: Nijenhuis framework for higher Lie products, not an exact finite-field conjugacy count.

The scope excludes characteristic two, non-idempotent Nijenhuis operators, semilinear conjugation, and claims of scheme-theoretic global reducedness. The originality comparison is substantive but non-exhaustive; independent validation has not been performed.
