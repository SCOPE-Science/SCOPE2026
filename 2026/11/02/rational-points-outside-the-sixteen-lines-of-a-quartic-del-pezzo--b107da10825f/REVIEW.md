# Scientific review — Frobenius-stratified point counts on quartic del Pezzo complements

**Correctness — PASS.** The point-count formula follows by subtracting a rigorously enumerated boundary: the sixteen exceptional curves have no triple intersections; fixed lines contribute \(q+1\) points each; two fixed intersecting lines contribute one duplicate; conjugate intersecting lines contribute one additional rational point. The Weil–Lefschetz formula contributes \(q^2+(1+t)q+1\). The sixteen-line adjacency is the even-subset Clebsch graph; the standalone program exhausts all \(1920\) even signed permutations, proves signed-cycle invariance of its counts, and checks exactly eighteen types and the two zero polynomials. Two explicit blowups establish existence of both zero cases. The calculations rely on standard geometric and cohomological theorems stated in the result, not only on finite experiments. No assertion that all types occur over every field is made.

**Originality — PASS, with historical risk.** Knecht's treatment of points off exceptional curves motivates the question. Rybakov and Banwait–Fité–Loughran give zeta/Frobenius information on degree-four del Pezzo surfaces, but no matching complete open-locus point-count table was found in the inspected material. An independently published computation of the integral homology of the complex complement of the same sixteen curves concerns a different invariant. Hirschfeld's older classification includes split surfaces covered by exceptional lines, so the split \(\mathbb F_5\) case alone is explicitly **not** claimed as new. The claimed distinction is the full eighteen-type Frobenius-equivariant table and exact two-type emptiness criterion. A prior equivalent table, implicit finite-geometry classification, or exhaustive historical treatment remains a genuine risk and independent literature verification is warranted.

**Value — PASS.** Whether a finite-field del Pezzo surface has a rational point off every exceptional curve is a natural geometric input to rational-point constructions and unirationality arguments. The result provides an exact answer uniformly across all arithmetic Frobenius actions rather than for a few examples: it identifies the only two possible empty regimes and proves that the complement is nonempty for every such surface over fields of at least seven elements. The table also isolates the contribution of conjugate intersections, which a naive count of rational exceptional curves would miss. The value is the complete structured classification, not the elementary split specialization.

**Residual limitations.** Weyl actions are formal possibilities, not asserted to be realizable in every small field. The older split-case classification and possible unrecovered equivalent arithmetic tables constrain historical priority. The finite certificate checks the Weyl-group combinatorics and explicit examples but cites the standard classification of exceptional curves and the trace formula as mathematical inputs.
