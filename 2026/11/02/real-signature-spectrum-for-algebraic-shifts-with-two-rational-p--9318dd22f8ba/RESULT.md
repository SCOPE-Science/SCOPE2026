# Real-signature spectrum for algebraic shifts with two rational powers

## Finding

Let \(\theta\) be a real algebraic irrational number, let \(r\in\mathbb R\), and suppose that for **distinct** positive integers \(a<b\), both \(u=r^a+\theta\) and \(v=r^b+\theta\) belong to \(\mathbb Q\). Write \(K=\mathbb Q(\theta)\) and \(d=[K:\mathbb Q]\), and let \(r_1(K)\) be the number of real field embeddings of \(K\).

**Complete signature spectrum.** Necessarily \(r_1(K)\le3\). Thus for even \(d\ge2\) the signature of \(K\) is exactly \((2,(d-2)/2)\); for odd \(d\ge3\) it is either \((1,(d-1)/2)\) or \((3,(d-3)/2)\). Every listed possibility occurs, **for each degree**, even under the simultaneous requirements \(\theta>0\), \(r<0\), and \((a,b)=(1,d)\). Consequently, no totally real field of degree at least four admits two distinct rational power-shifts with a real generator \(\theta\).

**A quantitative obstruction in the three-real-place case.** Put \(g=\gcd(a,b)\), \(A=a/g\), \(B=b/g\) and
\[
M_{A,B}=\frac{B-A}{B}\left(\frac AB\right)^{A/(B-A)}\quad(0<M_{A,B}<1).
\]
If \(r_1(K)=3\), then \(B\) is odd. If \(A\) is odd, \(0<|u-v|<M_{A,B}\); if \(A\) is even, \(0<u-v<M_{A,B}\). In particular **both shifted powers cannot be integers** when \(K\) has three real embeddings.

## Assumptions and scope

The exponents are positive and distinct, and \(r\) and \(\theta\) are real, with \(\theta\) algebraic irrational. The signature refers to the number field generated by \(\theta\), not an arbitrary larger field containing it. The spectrum is existential **by signature and degree**, not a characterization of which individual number fields admit a solution. The rational values \(u,v\) may be nonintegral. The bound does not restrict a single rational shifted power or equal exponents.

## Proof

### Field-generation and a trinomial identity

Set \(s=r^g\), \(A=a/g\), \(B=b/g\). Since \(\theta\notin\mathbb Q\), \(s\ne0\). We have \(s^A=u-\theta\) and \(s^B=v-\theta\), and \(\gcd(A,B)=1\). Bézout integers \(j,k\) with \(jA+kB=1\) give
\[
s=(s^A)^j(s^B)^k\in K.
\]
Conversely \(\theta=u-s^A\in\mathbb Q(s)\); hence \(K=\mathbb Q(s)\). Subtraction yields
\[
s^A-s^B=u-v=:c\in\mathbb Q.
\]
Here \(c\ne0\): otherwise \(s^{B-A}=1\), forcing real \(s\in\{1,-1\}\) and thus rational \(\theta\). The irreducible minimal polynomial of \(s\) over \(\mathbb Q\) divides \(F(X)=X^A-X^B-c\). All real embeddings of \(K\) send \(s\) to *distinct* real zeros of \(F\).

### Counting real roots and the sharp rational-gap restriction

Put \(h(t)=t^A-t^B\) and \(t_0=(A/B)^{1/(B-A)}\in(0,1)\), so that \(h(t_0)=M_{A,B}\). For \(t\ne0\),
\[
h'(t)=t^{A-1}(A-Bt^{B-A}).
\]
Since \(A,B\) are coprime, their parities can only be odd/even, even/odd, or odd/odd. In the odd/even case, \(h\) increases to the single maximum \(M_{A,B}\) at \(t_0\), then decreases; there are at most two real solutions of \(h(t)=c\). In the even/odd case, \(h\) decreases on the negative half-line, increases from zero to \(M_{A,B}\), then decreases to \(-\infty\); exactly three distinct solutions occur for \(0<c<M_{A,B}\), and fewer otherwise. In the odd/odd case, \(h\) has local minimum \(-M_{A,B}\) at \(-t_0\) and maximum \(M_{A,B}\) at \(t_0\), with strict monotonicity on the three intervening intervals (a possible horizontal tangent at \(0\) does not change monotonicity); exactly three distinct solutions occur for \(0<|c|<M_{A,B}\), and fewer otherwise. Thus the number of real zeros of \(F\) never exceeds three. The asserted parity and strict rational-gap conditions follow if \(r_1(K)=3\). As \(M_{A,B}<1\), two integer outputs would have a nonzero integral difference of absolute value at least one, a contradiction.

For a degree-\(d\) number field, \(d=r_1(K)+2r_2(K)\). Since \(K\subset\mathbb R\), \(r_1(K)\ge1\). Combining parity with the universal bound \(r_1(K)\le3\) gives the stated necessary signatures.

### Explicit realization of every allowed signature

For every \(d\ge2\) and \(e\in\{1,-4\}\), consider
\[
P_{d,e}(T)=3T^d-3T-e.
\]
Its negative reciprocal polynomial is \(eT^d+3T^{d-1}-3\), which is Eisenstein at the prime \(3\): the leading coefficient \(e\) is not divisible by \(3\), the remaining nonleading coefficients are divisible by \(3\), and the constant term is not divisible by \(9\). Consequently \(P_{d,e}\) is irreducible of degree \(d\). Every real root \(\alpha\) of \(P_{d,e}\) satisfies \(\alpha^d-\alpha=e/3\). With \(r=\alpha\) and \(\theta=-\alpha\), the two rational outputs are \(r+\theta=0\) and \(r^d+\theta=e/3\).

For even \(d\), use \(e=1\): \(P_{d,1}(-1)=5>0\) and \(P_{d,1}(0)=-1<0\), so there is a negative root; also \(P_{d,1}(1)=-1\) and \(P_{d,1}(2)>0\), yielding a second real root. The preceding real-root bound gives exactly two. For odd \(d\ge3\), use \(e=1\): \(P_{d,1}(-1)=-1<0\), \(P_{d,1}(-1/2)=3(1/2-2^{-d})-1>0\), \(P_{d,1}(0)=-1<0\), and \(P_{d,1}(2)>0\). There are exactly three real roots, including two negative roots. Finally, for odd \(d\ge3\), use \(e=-4\): \(P_{d,-4}(-2)<0<P_{d,-4}(0)\), so a negative root exists; \(T^d-T\) has maximum less than \(1\) on \(( -\infty, 0]\), hence \(P_{d,-4}(T)=3(T^d-T)+4>0\) for \(-1\le T\le0\), while for \(T<-1\) the map \(T\mapsto T^d-T\) increases strictly from \(-\infty\) to \(0\), giving exactly one negative root. On \(T\ge0\), \(T^d-T\ge-M_{1,d}>-1\), so there are no positive roots. This produces exactly one real root. In all three cases, choosing a negative root \(\alpha\) makes \(\theta>0\) and \(r<0\), completing sharpness.

## Verification

The included `verify.py` uses exact symbolic polynomials and a Sturm real-root count to check all constructed degree/signature branches for \(2\le d\le18\), checks the reciprocal Eisenstein coefficients, and compares the predicted three-root patterns for low coprime exponent pairs. Run `python verify.py` with Python 3 and SymPy installed. The argument above proves the unrestricted theorem; finitely many symbolic examples do not establish it.

## Relationship to prior work

Anthony Pisani, *On the Open Problem in the 2023 Simon Marais Mathematics Competition* (arXiv:2610.30658v1, public 22 October 2026), determines all exponent pairs for the specific shift \(\sqrt n\) with positive nonsquare integer \(n\). In its Lemma 7, the useful Bézout reduction already appears for quadratic shifts. The present result generalizes that reduction to arbitrary algebraic irrational shifts and turns the resulting trinomial into a sharp **field-signature spectrum**, plus explicit witnesses for each degree and the three-real-place rational-gap obstruction. It does not strengthen Pisani's exponent-pair classification for square-root shifts. Prior literature on the arithmetic of trinomial number fields (e.g. arXiv:2203.07625) concerns different invariants; only its abstract was inspected, so implicit overlap is a residual risk.

## Limitations

The signature restriction is necessary for an *individual* field, but not sufficient for an arbitrary field with that signature. This proof gives no complete classification of exponent pairs for any fixed algebraic shift outside the quadratic situation. The examples are existence witnesses with specially chosen algebraic shifts; no claim of optimal polynomial height is made. Earlier literature may contain the root-count interpretation or this consequence implicitly; independent priority and proof assessments have not been carried out.
