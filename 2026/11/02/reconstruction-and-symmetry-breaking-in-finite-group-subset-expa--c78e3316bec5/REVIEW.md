# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The complete statement is an all-finite-group isomorphism classification of the full powerset-union semidirect expansion and the identity-and-group-element-containing Szendrei expansion, including the intrinsic stabilizer/orbit relationship. The full inverse semigroup's units and Boolean idempotent semilattice force a group isomorphism and a left-equivariant singleton permutation, hence exactly the right-translation parameter; conversely each such map respects multiplication. In the pointed expansion, the Boolean idempotent atoms and the uniquely characterized non-idempotent element with each atomic range idempotent reconstruct the multiplication table of the group. Factorization by these elements proves exhaustiveness. Distinct pointed copies are distinguished by their identity idempotents, and unit-conjugation factorization follows explicitly. The trivial group boundary is covered.

The algebraic derivations were reconstructed, and a standalone script tested all stated map families for six finite groups, including the nonabelian symmetric group on three points; it also exhaustively enumerated all expansion permutations in the small cases for which feasible. This corroboration does not substitute for the general argument.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The exact objects occur in Gould and Johnson, *Subset expansions of monoids*, arXiv:2511.13435 (first posted 2025-11-17). Its accessible full HTML was examined, especially the construction, inverse-monoid discussion and final comparison with Szendrei; the text focuses on finitary properties and does not state these isomorphism classifications or the pointed-subsemigroup orbit. The full automorphism group is a holomorph, a classical group, so the holomorph's definition is not being claimed as new; the contribution is the intrinsic realization, complete reconstruction across two different inverse semigroups and the symmetry-breaking stabilizer theorem. Rago's 2025 finite-abelian reduced power-monoid theorem is about a different product (setwise multiplication, not union with translation). Lawson–Margolis–Steinberg 2006 and Szendrei's earlier constructions establish prefix expansions, which are prior structural inputs. The 1990 Fountain–Gomes original had incomplete full-text access; an unstated general result in older prefix-expansion literature may subsume the pointed component. Complete historical priority therefore remains unverified.

Searches compared aliases (Szendrei, Birget–Rhodes, prefix and Exel expansions), isomorphism rigidity, full subset semidirect products, Boolean semilattice actions, holomorphs and reduced power monoids. Accessible prior-result searches found no statement dominating the **combined full/pointed isomorphism and orbit/stabilizer classification**. This judgment is not a proof of global uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The new 2025 parent construction explicitly places the pointed Szendrei expansion inside a larger inverse semigroup, yet it is not automatic whether its structural symmetries extend or whether the containing inverse semigroup admits extra automorphisms. The theorem answers these natural reconstruction questions completely for finite groups, not just a finite census: every isomorphism is classified, the full object remembers its group through units, the pointed object reconstructs multiplication despite having only a trivial unit group, and the full automorphism group acts regularly on the \(|G|\) pointed copies modulo \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\). The faithful inner action even detects central elements invisible to ordinary conjugation on \(G\). That contrast supplies a structural mechanism explaining the added symmetry and a precise finite index \(|G|\), not merely a textbook relabeling.

## Closest sources and limitations

Gould–Johnson 2025 is the direct motivating primary source. Szendrei 1989; Fountain–Gomes 1990; Lawson–Margolis–Steinberg 2006 and Hollings 2007 underlie the pointed construction, and Rago 2025 concerns a different power monoid. The classification is limited to finite groups; arbitrary monoids and reduced power monoids are not covered. Primary historical literature was not all available in complete text, and no independent external mathematical review or priority certification has taken place.
