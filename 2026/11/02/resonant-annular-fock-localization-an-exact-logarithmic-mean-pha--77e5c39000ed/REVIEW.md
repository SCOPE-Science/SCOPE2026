# Scientific review

## Correctness
**PASS.** The exact squared-radius integral gives all eigenvalues and a strictly unimodal sequence with at most one adjacent top tie. The logarithmic mean uniquely labels that tie. Differentiation of the coherent-state integral in trace norm is justified by uniform smoothness on a fixed compact radial strip. The isolated two-dimensional top spectral cluster is split by an explicit Hermitian matrix whose off-diagonal is the first angular Fourier coefficient. Standard isolated-cluster perturbation theory produces both one-sided slopes and the quadratic remainder. The supplementary finite-matrix test corroborates the exact coefficient at several distinct resonance indices; it is not used as the proof.

## Originality
**PASS, provisional.** Relevant primary comparison: Svela (arXiv:2410.18769, Sections 5.1–5.2, Proposition 5.5) proves diagonalization for rotational/Reinhardt masks, covering the radial spectrum but not the first variation under masks that immediately lose radiality. Gómez et al. (arXiv:2307.09304, Section 5 and Proposition 5.3) prove perturbative stability around the disk, not the annular repeated-top-eigenvalue transition. Galbis (arXiv:2102.02031) addresses radial symbol norm bounds and provides related context. The exact integer-logarithmic-mean resonance criterion and mode-selective cusp were not found in the directly inspected applicable statements or in the checked mathematical record. Generic spectral perturbation and radial diagonalization are acknowledged inputs, not claimed inventions. Remaining risk: a broader study of shape derivatives for radial localization operators might already derive the same corollary.

## Mathematical value
**PASS.** The result gives a natural *complete* phase diagram of the differentiability of optimized Gaussian time-frequency concentration on annuli under fixed-area boundary corrugations, with exact transition curves and a sharp, geometrically selective first-order coefficient. This identifies a concrete mechanism by which the optimizer switches between two adjacent Hermite levels. It distinguishes genuine resonant shape instability from first-order stationarity elsewhere, rather than reporting an isolated numerical eigenvalue or a generic Kato formula.

## Limitations
The finding does not establish optimality of annuli, uniformity as the annulus parameters vary, or analogous results for arbitrary time-frequency windows. The second-order term at perturbations with vanishing first Fourier harmonic is not evaluated. Historical priority is provisional and independent review has not occurred.
