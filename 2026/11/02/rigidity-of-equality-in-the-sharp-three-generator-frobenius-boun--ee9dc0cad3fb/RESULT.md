# Rigidity of equality in the sharp three-generator Frobenius bound

## Finding
Let \(1\le a<b<c\) be positive integers with \(\gcd(a,b,c)=1\). Write \(F(a,b,c)\) for the largest nonnegative integer outside the numerical semigroup \(\langle a,b,c\rangle\); when \(a=1\), use the standard convention \(F(a,b,c)=-1\). Define \(k=\lceil c/2\rceil\). The bound of Chen–Hu–Mao–Zhang states that \(F(a,b,c)\le kb-c-1\).

**Theorem (complete equality classification).** Equality holds precisely for the following disjoint families:
\[
(a,b,c)=(q,q+1,2q),\quad q\in\mathbb Z,\ q\ge 2;
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
(a,b,c)=(1,2,2q),\quad q\in\mathbb Z,\ q\ge2.
\]
In particular every equality triple has a redundant generator, so no numerical semigroup of embedding dimension three realizes equality.

## Assumptions and scope
The integers are strictly ordered, the total greatest common divisor is one, and representations use nonnegative integer coefficients. No pairwise coprimality is assumed. A *redundant generator* is an element lying in the semigroup generated by the other two. The theorem treats the unit generator separately and includes all boundary moduli.

## Proof
Let \(X=\{0,a,b\}\) in \(G=\mathbb Z/c\mathbb Z\). Since \(a<b<c\), its three elements are distinct modulo \(c\), and \(X\) generates \(G\). We first show
\[
\underbrace{X+\cdots+X}_{k\text{ summands}}=G.
\]
Write \(H\) for the stabilizer of the displayed sumset and \(h=|H|\). For the trivial stabilizer, the multiple-set form of Kneser's theorem gives \(|kX|\ge k(|X|-1)+1=2k+1>c\), a contradiction if \(kX\ne G\). For a nontrivial stabilizer, since \(X\) generates \(G\), its image in \(G/H\) is not a singleton, so \(|X+H|\ge2h\). Applying Kneser to \(k\) copies of \(X+H\), which have sum \(kX\) because \(H\) stabilizes \(kX\), yields \(|kX|\ge k|X+H|-(k-1)h\ge(k+1)h\ge2k+2>c\), again impossible. Hence \(kX=G\).

For each residue \(r\pmod c\), let \(w_r\) be the least nonnegative sum of copies of \(a\) and \(b\) in residue \(r\). Such a sum exists because \(\gcd(a,b,c)=1\), and the usual Apéry argument gives \(F(a,b,c)=\max_r w_r-c\). Suppose equality holds in the stated upper bound. Then some residue \(r\) satisfies \(w_r=kb-1\). The equality \(kX=G\) supplies a representation \(s=ua+vb\equiv r\pmod c\) with \(u,v\ge0\) and \(u+v\le k\). Necessarily \(kb-1=w_r\le s\le kb\). The number \(kb\) is not congruent to \(kb-1\) modulo \(c\), so \(s=kb-1\). Subtracting this representation from \(kb\) gives the exact deficit identity
\[
1=(k-u-v)b+u(b-a).
\]
Both terms are nonnegative integers; moreover \(b\ge2\). Consequently \(k-u-v=0\), \(u=1\), and \(b-a=1\). Thus equality forces \(b=a+1\).

Assume first \(a\ge2\), \(b=a+1\). Since \(\gcd(a,a+1)=1\), Sylvester's two-generator formula gives
\[
F(a,a+1,c)\le F(a,a+1)=a(a+1)-a-(a+1)=a^2-a-1.
\]
If \(a+2\le c\le2a-1\), write \(c=a+t\) with \(2\le t\le a-1\). The integer \(k=\lceil(a+t)/2\rceil\) satisfies \(k\ge t+1\), and the upper-bound value is *itself* representable:
\[
kb-c-1=t a+(k-t-1)(a+1).
\]
It therefore cannot equal the Frobenius number. If \(c=2a\), the third generator is redundant, and \(k=a\); consequently \(kb-c-1=a^2-a-1=F(a,a+1,2a)\).

For \(c\ge2a+1\), define \(B(c)=(a+1)\lceil c/2\rceil-c-1\). At the first two points,
\[
B(2a+1)=a^2-1,\qquad B(2a+2)=a^2-2.
\]
The recursion \(B(c+2)=B(c)+(a-1)\) and \(a\ge2\) imply \(B(c)\ge a^2-2>a^2-a-1\ge F(a,a+1,c)\). Equality is therefore impossible. Together with \(a<b<c\), the preceding cases exhaust \(a\ge2\).

Finally, when \(a=1\), the necessary difference \(b-a=1\) gives \(b=2\). The semigroup is all nonnegative integers and \(F(1,2,c)=-1\); its upper bound is \(2\lceil c/2\rceil-c-1\), which equals \(-1\) if and only if \(c\) is even. Strict ordering forces \(c\ge4\), giving \((1,2,2q)\) with \(q\ge2\). This proves necessity and sufficiency of both families, as well as the redundant-generator consequence. \(\square\)

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` enumerates all coprime triples \(1\le a<b<c\le95\). For each, an Apéry shortest-path computation evaluates the Frobenius number without using the formula being tested. It checks the published bound and the new equality classification. Invocation: `python3 verify.py 95`. It returns `PASS: checked 116920 coprime triples, 92 equality cases, 3<=c<=95`. This diagnostic does not constitute an infinite proof; the proof above establishes the general assertion.

## Relationship to prior work
Chen, Hu, Mao and Zhang prove the sharp upper bound for arbitrary increasing triples and exhibit the equality examples \((q,q+1,2q)\), but their stated theorem and proof do not classify all equality instances. Our inverse analysis adds the necessary condition \(b=a+1\), eliminates every other value of \(c\), treats the unit-generator family, and shows that three essential generators cannot attain their bound. Aguiló and Miralles characterize extremizers of a different optimization problem in which only the largest generator is fixed; their extremal function does not establish the equality set for a bound retaining the middle generator. Classical Kneser and Sylvester theorems supply tools, not this classification.

## Limitations
This is an equality-case classification, not a stronger bound away from equality or a stability estimate for near-extremizers. Its originality is assessed against the inspected cited texts and research records, not certified against all unindexed literature. The general 1998 Frobenius work of Hamidoune was identified but its full text was not read. Earlier implicit derivations of the short rigidity argument remain a priority risk. Computational tests cover only bounded parameters. Independent audit has not been performed.
