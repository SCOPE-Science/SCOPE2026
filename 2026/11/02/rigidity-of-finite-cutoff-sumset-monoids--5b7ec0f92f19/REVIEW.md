# Scientific review: rigidity of finite cutoff sumset monoids

## Correctness — PASS

The result concerns every integer \(n\ge1\), with product defined by truncating ordinary (not modular) sumsets. Lemma 1 detects the top singleton \(\{0,n\}\) intrinsically using the image size of its multiplication map and idempotency at the small cutoffs. The key missed-image witnesses rule out all lower singletons, and a cardinality bound rules out elements with at least two positive entries. Lemma 2 identifies the principal ideal generated by \(\{0,n\}\) with the preceding cutoff monoid. Induction forces an automorphism to fix the ideal, and then each remaining element. This proves all cutoffs without extrapolation. The independent finite-set and bit-mask algorithms, exhaustive base-automorphism checks, and terminal-ideal tests in `verify.py` returned `VERIFY_OK`.

The proof does not address other truncation conventions. The terminal-element claim is false if integer sums are reduced modulo a cycle; that is deliberately outside scope.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

Bhowmik–Tringali, arXiv:2607.23301, Theorem 1.2, classifies untruncated reduced power monoids of numerical monoids and explicitly retains the order-two reversion for \(\mathbb N\). Wen–He–Liu, arXiv:2608.27292, realizes reduced finitary power monoids as Kleisli convolution monoids and transfers untruncated rigidity. Tringali–Yan, arXiv:2312.04439, treats the original untruncated sumset monoid. Rago, arXiv:2510.17533, treats groups rather than nonnegative cutoff sums. The separate papers of Wong–Xu–Zhang–Zhao and Tringali–Wen study full numerical power semigroups. Their inspected main theorems and relevant discussions do not imply the all-cutoff claim by substitution: an automorphism of a quotient need not lift to its source, and modular addition differs from discarding large sums.

Targeted mathematical-record and literature searches for the cutoff set monoid, its Boolean truncated-polynomial realization, and the multiplication-image-rank invariant returned no equivalent claim. Relevant untruncated monoid statements and finite-group statements were compared as implications, not merely titles. Residual risk remains from older terminology for Boolean Toeplitz/convolution monoids and possibly unindexed finite-semigroup work. Search absence is not a priority certificate.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The theorem classifies the symmetry group of an infinite natural family of \(2^n\)-element finite sumset monoids, obtained as the systematic finite cutoff quotients of a well-studied monoid with a nontrivial global symmetry. The proof explains why **every** finite cutoff loses that symmetry: an intrinsic terminal idempotent makes each preceding stage a characteristic principal ideal. This is a structural rigidity mechanism, not a numerical exercise or an arbitrary finite table. It provides a concrete boundary for transferring automorphism results through quotient and inverse-system constructions.

The result does not classify arbitrary finite quotient monoids, full semiring automorphisms, or endomorphism monoids. Historical-priority uncertainty remains separate from mathematical correctness and value.
