# Scientific review

## Correctness (C): PASS

For an arbitrary binary word with exactly two ones, each possible root has one one. The two necessary and sufficient boundary inequalities in the proof give an interval of integer root parameters; the sufficiency step explicitly assigns every zero to one of two increasing copies. Counting runs of \(t\) consecutive parameters leads to a finite binomial convolution, and the second difference selects exact root multiplicities. This establishes the all-length distribution, generating function, positive support, and zero mass. The triangular weak limit follows by Riemann sums, with the known total from [1] used only as a check. Independently constructed shuffle-DP computations exhaust all 9,500 relevant words at semilengths 1–24 and 175,100 word/root incidences; 100 further algebraic length checks pass. The script and its output are part of the same package. Finite checks cannot independently prove the infinite theorem, and the proof is not independently audited.

## Originality (O): PASS, subject to publication-priority uncertainty

The closest primary full texts were compared at the level of mathematical implication. In *Variations on shuffle squares* (2023), Theorem 3 determines which two-one words are shuffle squares and Corollary 1 determines the **same total count** as equation (7) here: those are **covered by prior work**. In *Roots of Binary Shuffle Squares* (2025), Proposition 1 counts target squares generated by a specified root, while Theorem 2 gives some single-root patterns; it does not state or imply without additional overlap analysis the entire reverse incidence distribution by the number of distinct roots. The 2025 structural paper on nest-free graphs concerns eligibility and other families, and the 2025 large-scale binary shuffle-square theorem is not fixed-content root enumeration. No indexed prior record matched the full root-multiplicity law, but index non-matches alone are weak evidence. The exact closed two-parameter inverse histogram and triangular limit are the assessed contributions; the already-known total and elementary density consequence are not. Residual risk: obscure prior discussions, unpublished notes or supplementary computations may contain an equivalent root-multiplicity formula; no independent priority audit has occurred.

## Value (V): PASS

Root multiplicity is a natural ambiguity statistic of the shuffle operation, distinct from merely recognizing a shuffle square and explicitly motivated by the 2025 root-enumeration program. The theorem resolves its full first nontrivial fixed-content spectrum for all semilengths, including exact support, a rational bivariate generating function, sharp count of uniquely rooted words, and an explicit mixed discrete/continuous limit law. In the sparse layer, the positive-root probability approaches three quarters (already implicit in the 2023 total), but the newly determined distribution shows that the positive-root words typically have linearly many distinct roots, with a triangular scaled profile. The statement does not purport to settle the global conjecture about unique roots or solve higher weights.
