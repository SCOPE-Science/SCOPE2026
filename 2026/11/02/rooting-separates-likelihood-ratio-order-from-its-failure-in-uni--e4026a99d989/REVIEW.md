## Originality — PASS (historical-priority qualification)
Pitman's 1996 coalescent forest paper already gives fixed-root enumeration and a weaker monotonic coupling for uniformly rooted forest sampling. Pitman's 2001 Proposition 15 gives a general component-subset law that specializes to the probability formula used here; that formula and its mean are explicitly not new. Sokal's 2021 total-positivity theorem concerns the forest enumeration matrix indexed by vertex count and number of components, not the tagged component-size matrix indexed by root count and component size. The contribution under assessment is the complete likelihood-ratio comparison and the general unrooted boundary: in every order at least five, uniform unrooted two- and three-component forest laws reverse the necessary first likelihood-ratio step. Relevant semantic literature and published-result queries found no direct treatment of the full contrast. Because the rooted comparison is an accessible algebraic consequence of older exact mass formulas, its separate historical priority is not guaranteed; the unrooted infinite obstruction supplies the additional boundary statement.

## Value — PASS
Specifying roots is a natural sampling mechanism in Cayley forests and the additive coalescent, whereas sampling unrooted forests is an equally natural alternative. A full monotone likelihood-ratio order compares every pair of support values, not just means; it also induces all-threshold inequalities and an exact transport distance. The fact that dropping root ownership destroys the stronger order for every \(n\ge5\), with an exact first obstruction and a closed-form sign, gives a structural boundary between these two standard ensembles. The result is not an arbitrary numerical slice or a re-enumeration of known tables.
