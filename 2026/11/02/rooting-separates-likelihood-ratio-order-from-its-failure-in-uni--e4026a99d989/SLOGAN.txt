The size of a tagged root's component in a uniformly sampled fixed-root Cayley forest decreases strictly in likelihood-ratio order as the number of roots increases, yielding exact Wasserstein distance \(n(1/k-1/\ell)\). This strengthening of classical component-size formulas disappears without specified roots: uniform unrooted forests violate the corresponding order between two and three components for every \(n\ge5\), with five vertices the sharp first obstruction.
