# Rowmotion on the ternary projective-plane incidence poset: 6,202 cycles in 22 projective symmetry types

## Finding
Let \(\mathcal P=\operatorname{PG}(2,3)\), the projective plane with thirteen points and thirteen lines. Let \(Q\) be its rank-two point-line incidence poset: each point is below the four lines containing it; there are no further nontrivial comparisons. Let \(J(Q)\) denote its order ideals, and let rowmotion \(\rho(I)\) be the order ideal generated by the minimal elements of \(Q\setminus I\).

**Exact finite theorem.** The lattice \(J(Q)\) has exactly \(38{,}730\) ideals. Its rowmotion permutation has precisely \(6{,}202\) cycles, with complete length spectrum:

| Cycle length \(\ell\) | Number of cycles \(c_\ell\) |
|---:|---:|

There are no cycles of any other length. In particular, \(\rho\) has permutation order \(\operatorname{lcm}(2,3,5,6,7,9,11)=6930\). The natural projective collineation group \(\operatorname{PGL}(3,3)\), of order \(5616\), commutes with rowmotion and has **exactly 22 orbits on the set of 6,202 rowmotion cycles**. All orbit types are specified by the following exhaustive table: the third column is the number of projective-group orbits of cycles of length \(\ell\), each containing \(s\) cycles.

The unique projectively invariant cycle is the length-three cycle of the empty ideal, the ideal of all thirteen points, and the full ideal.

## Assumptions and scope
All sets, permutations, and projective transformations are finite. The points of \(\mathcal P\) are the one-dimensional vector subspaces of \(\mathbb F_3^3\); lines are the two-dimensional subspaces, equivalently kernels of nonzero linear forms up to scalar. Rowmotion is applied to **order ideals**, not to arbitrary point subsets. Both the points and the lines are distinctly labelled objects; group orbit counts identify cycles under projective collineations but do not quotient the ideal states in the first spectrum.

Write an ideal as a pair \((A,B)\), where \(A\) is a subset of thirteen points, and \(B\) a subset of thirteen lines for which every line in \(B\) is entirely contained in \(A\). Let \(E(A)\) be the set of all lines contained in \(A\), and \(U(C)\) the union of points on lines in \(C\).

## Proof
**1. Exact parametrization and count.** Every order ideal uniquely has the form \((A,B)\) with \(B\subseteq E(A)\). Consequently
\[
 |J(Q)|=\sum_{A\subseteq \mathcal P_0}2^{|E(A)|},
\]
where \(\mathcal P_0\) is the point set. For point subsets containing exactly \(k\) entire lines, the complete frequency table \((k,N_k)\) is
\[
(0,4330),(1,2236),(2,1014),(3,286),(4,234),(6,78),(9,13),(13,1).
\]
The sum of the frequencies is \(2^{13}=8192\); the weighted sum \(\sum_k N_k 2^k=38{,}730\). These are exact results of enumeration of all 8192 point subsets using the thirteen explicit line masks.

**2. Rowmotion formula.** The minimal members of the complement of \((A,B)\) are exactly the missing points and the lines belonging to \(E(A)\setminus B\). Thus, setting \(C=E(A)\setminus B\),
\[
\rho(A,B)=\big((\mathcal P_0\setminus A)\cup U(C),\; C\big).
\]
The right-hand pair is again an ideal because all points of every line in \(C\) lie in the first coordinate. This is an explicit finite transition function, not a heuristic update.

**3. Cycle exhaustion.** In the first verifier, take the thirteen normalized nonzero triples over \(\mathbb F_3\) as points, and all thirteen normalized dual triples as line equations. Enumerate every \(A\subseteq\mathcal P_0\), then every \(B\subseteq E(A)\), and repeatedly apply the displayed formula until returning to the start. Mark each visited pair, and assert that every image and predecessor is present, every orbit closes at its starting point, and all \(38{,}730\) states are visited. The complete integer cycle census is precisely the table in the Finding section; its lengths times multiplicities sum to \(38{,}730\), so there are no unexamined states. The least common multiple proves the claimed permutation order.

**4. Projective symmetry refinement.** A collineation permutes points and lines while preserving inclusion, so it commutes with \(\rho\). The verifier constructs the permutations induced on the thirteen points and thirteen lines by an elementary shear, coordinate swap, three-cycle, and a nonscalar diagonal matrix in \(\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbb F_3)\). These generate the projective linear group: conjugated elementary shears give elementary row operations, and swaps and diagonal rescalings complete Gaussian elimination. Explicit closure on the thirteen projective points gives exactly \(5616\) transformations, confirming the whole \(\operatorname{PGL}(3,3)\). Apply the four generators to each of the \(6202\) rowmotion cycles, identifying the target by any one of its member ideals. Breadth-first closure under the generators gives exactly 22 orbit classes, with the table stated above. The table totals \(6202\) cycles and refines the first census for every cycle length. The unique size-one orbit is \(\varnothing\mapsto\mathcal P_0\mapsto Q\mapsto\varnothing\).

## Verification
Two complete standard-library Python programs are embedded: `verification/coordinates.py` and `verification/singer.py`. Execute either with `python3` followed by its relative path in a directory containing the extracted files. The first program computes the complete rowmotion census from homogeneous coordinates, checks the 22 projective symmetry orbit classes and the projective group's size, and prints `LINEAR_COORDINATES_ROW_AND_GROUP_PASS`. The second independently generates the same plane as the thirteen translates of the Singer difference set \(\{0,1,3,9\}\) modulo thirteen, enumerates all 26-element ideal masks, implements the minimal-complement definition directly (not the displayed transition formula), and verifies the full length census, 6202 cycles, and order 6930. It prints `SINGER_INDEPENDENT_38730_IDEALS_6202_ORBITS_ORDER_6930_PASS`.

These are exhaustive computations on the **finite** poset. The proof of completeness follows from the explicit enumeration ranges and the permutation/cycle assertions, rather than extrapolation from a sample or timeout. The computer programs constitute reproducible finite certificates whose implementation must be inspected and executed when independent assurance is required.

## Relationship to prior work
Striker–Williams (2011 preprint; 2012 journal) defines rowmotion and studies its conjugacy with promotion for several families including chain products, classical root posets, and alternating-sign-matrix posets; its inspected full PDF gives no computed cycle spectrum for this 26-element projective point-line incidence poset. Grinberg–Roby (2015, 2016) develop birational rowmotion on rectangles, triangles and skeletal posets, not the present exact projective-plane orbit classification. Previous smaller-plane and affine-plane incidence-poset calculations, where accessible, concern **different posets** and do not imply this exact projective-plane spectrum or 22-class collineation refinement. No direct, stronger, or equivalent coverage was identified in targeted mathematical records or web queries. The absence of a match is not a publication-priority guarantee.

## Limitations
This is a complete finite result for the unique Desarguesian projective plane of order three, not an infinite-family formula for projective planes or a claim about birational rowmotion. It does not classify all projective-plane orders or supply closed symbolic formulas for general field size. The 22-class orbit table is verified by the stated exact finite computation; no independent scholarly audit has been performed. It remains possible that an unindexed calculation or alternate terminology (Panyushev complement or Fon-der-Flaass action) contains the same census.
