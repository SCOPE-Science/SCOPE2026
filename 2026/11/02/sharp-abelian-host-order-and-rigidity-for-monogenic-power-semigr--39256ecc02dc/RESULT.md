# Sharp abelian host order and rigidity for monogenic power-semigroup embeddings

## Finding

Let \(M(m,r)\) denote the finite monogenic semigroup of **index** \(m\geq1\) and **period** \(r\geq1\), generated by an element \(a\) whose least eventual repetition is \(a^m=a^{m+r}\). Let \(\mathcal P^*(G)\) be the semigroup of all nonempty subsets of a finite abelian group \(G\), under setwise addition. Write \(\mu_{\mathrm{ab}}(m,r)\) for the least possible order of a finite abelian group whose power semigroup contains an isomorphic copy of \(M(m,r)\).

**Theorem.** For all positive integers \(m,r\),
\[
\mu_{\mathrm{ab}}(m,r)=
\begin{cases}
r,&m=1,\\
(m+1)r,&m\geq2.
\end{cases}
\]
The minimum is attained by a cyclic group, and for \(m\geq2\) by a two-element generating subset.

**All optimal embeddings.** If \(m\geq2\) and \(|G|=(m+1)r\), a subset \(A\subseteq G\) generates a copy of \(M(m,r)\) exactly when
\[
A=\{g,g+h\},\qquad |\langle h\rangle|=m+1,
\qquad \operatorname{ord}(g+\langle h\rangle)=r
\]
and \(G/\langle h\rangle\) is cyclic of order \(r\) (the last clause is automatic from the preceding conditions and host order). For \(m=1\), an optimal host has order \(r\) and the generator subset is a singleton consisting of a generator of \(G\), hence the host is cyclic.

**Host rigidity.** For \(m\geq2\), every minimum-order abelian host is cyclic if and only if \(\gcd(m+1,r)=1\). For noncoprime parameters, both cyclic and noncyclic minimum-order hosts exist.

## Assumptions and scope

All groups are finite and abelian, written additively. A power \(A^{[j]}\) of a nonempty subset means its \(j\)-fold Minkowski sum, for an integer \(j\geq1\), and not scalar multiplication of every element by \(j\). The ambient power semigroup contains **nonempty subsets only**, with no requirement that the embedding preserve identities. Index and period use positive exponents, so \(|M(m,r)|=m+r-1\). The theorem concerns *direct embeddings* and the minimum order of the ambient abelian group; it makes no assertion about minimum order among nonabelian hosts or the general decision problem for arbitrary semigroups.

## Proof

**1. Stabilization of normalized subset powers.** Take any nonempty \(A\subseteq G\), choose \(g\in A\), put \(D=A-g\), and let \(H=\langle D\rangle\leq G\). Then \(0\in D\) and for every \(j\geq1\),
\[
A^{[j]}=jg+jD,
\]
where \(jD\) is the \(j\)-fold sumset. Since \(0\in D\), the sequence \(D\subseteq 2D\subseteq\cdots\subseteq H\) is increasing. It reaches \(H\) in finitely many steps: every element of the finite subgroup generated by \(D\) is a sum of nonnegative multiples of members of \(D\), and zeros can pad to a common length.

Let \(s\geq1\) be the least \(j\) for which \(jD=H\). Before \(s\), every inclusion is strict. Indeed, if \(jD=(j+1)D=jD+D\), then the finite set \(jD\) is stable under addition by each element of \(D\), hence by their generated subgroup \(H\). Being a nonempty subset of \(H\), it must equal \(H\), contrary to \(j<s\). Thus every size \(|jD|\), for \(1\leq j\leq s\), is distinct unless \(s=1\).

For \(j\geq s\),
\[
A^{[j]}=jg+H.
\]
Its least period is precisely \(\operatorname{ord}(g+H)\) in the quotient \(G/H\). Since each earlier \(A^{[j]}\) has cardinality smaller than \(|H|\), none can coincide with a later term. Therefore the **index** of the monogenic subsemigroup generated by \(A\) is \(s\), and its **period** is \(\operatorname{ord}(g+H)\).

**2. Lower bound.** Suppose the index is \(m\geq2\). Necessarily \(|D|\geq2\), since a singleton containing zero is already stable at the first power. The strict inclusions before stabilization imply
\[
|H|=|mD|\geq |D|+(m-1)\geq m+1.
\]
Since the period is \(r=\operatorname{ord}(g+H)\), Lagrange's theorem gives \(|G/H|\geq r\). Therefore
\[
|G|=|H|\,|G/H|\geq(m+1)r.
\]
For \(m=1\), the quotient still contains an element of order \(r\), so \(|G|\geq r\).

**3. Sharp constructions.** For \(m\geq2\), work in the cyclic group
\[
G=\mathbb Z/((m+1)r)\mathbb Z
\]
and take \(g=1\), \(h=r\), \(A=\{g,g+h\}\). The element \(h\) has order \(m+1\), and the image of \(g\) generates \(G/\langle h\rangle\), of order \(r\). For \(1\leq j\leq m\),
\[
jD=\{0,h,2h,\ldots,jh\},
\]
whose cardinality is \(j+1\); at \(j=m\) it first becomes \(H\). By Step 1 the index and period of \(A\) are exactly \((m,r)\). This realizes \(M(m,r)\) faithfully and attains the lower bound. For \(m=1\), use a singleton generator of \(C_r\).

**4. Rigidity of all minimizers.** Let \(m\geq2\) and suppose \(|G|=(m+1)r\). Equality throughout Step 2 forces \(|H|=m+1\), \(|G/H|=r\), and \(|D|=2\). Thus \(D=\{0,h\}\) for some generator \(h\) of \(H\) (because \(D\) generates \(H\)). Also \(g+H\) has order \(r\) and hence generates the quotient. Conversely any such \((G,g,h)\) has \(jD=\{0,h,\ldots,jh\}\) and the required index, period, and host order. At \(m=1\), optimality forces \(H\) trivial and \(G/H\) cyclic of order \(r\); hence \(A=\{g\}\) with \(g\) a generator.

**5. Cyclic versus noncyclic hosts.** Put \(t=m+1\). If \(\gcd(t,r)=1\), any abelian group \(G\) of order \(tr\) with cyclic \(H\) of order \(t\) and cyclic quotient of order \(r\) is the direct product of its coprime-order primary parts and therefore is cyclic. If \(\gcd(t,r)>1\), both \(C_{tr}\) and \(C_t\times C_r\) admit the required \(H\), but the second is noncyclic. This proves the dichotomy.

Run `python monogenic_host_checker.py` with Python 3, using no third-party libraries. The program enumerates **every nonempty subset** in each cyclic group through order twelve and five additional direct-product abelian groups. It computes the eventual index and period by recording successive subset powers and checks the lower bound and equality's forced two-element shape for each instance. It independently checks the explicit cyclic construction for all \(121\) pairs \((m,r)\) with \(1\leq m,r\leq11\). Its final output is `VERIFY_OK 17 groups`. These finite checks support, but do not establish, the universal theorem; Steps 1–5 do so.

For example, \(M(3,2)\) needs an abelian host of order at least \(8\). Both \(C_8\) and \(C_4\times C_2\) attain this order, while \(M(2,3)\) needs at least \(9\) points and can be realized by \(\{1,4\}\subset C_9\).

## Relationship to prior work

Bershadsky, Kublanovsky, and Mashevitzky's 2026 collection poses the general **finite-group power-semigroup direct-embedding decision problem** (Problem 1.3) and related embedding questions (Problems 5.2–5.5). It reports existing existence results for embeddings of commutative semigroups into power semigroups over abelian groups and discusses finite/periodic constraints. The present theorem is a **quantitative, finite, monogenic, abelian-host result**: it neither decides the general problem nor claims that monogenic embeddability itself is newly discovered.

Bershadsky and Kublanovsky (2025) address general necessary and sufficient conditions for power-semigroup embeddability over periodic groups. Only its abstract and initial publisher-preview page were accessible for comparison; the full article was not inspected, so a hidden equivalent host-size formula remains a priority risk. The 1973 subgroup classification by McCarthy and Hayes concerns the group-like (index-one) part and does not on its stated summary give the index-dependent minimum. Trnková's 1975 general commutative representation theorem, as reported by the 2026 notebook, concerns existence, not a stated sharp finite minimum. The subsequent Kleisli representation work uses another framework, with no claimed size optimum in its abstract. This is a best-knowledge comparison, **not independent historical-priority certification**.

## Limitations

The argument relies on commutativity to express every subset power as a translate of an increasing sequence of normalized sumsets. It does not establish any lower bound for nonabelian host groups. The exact minimum is with respect to the host group's **cardinality**, not the subset's cardinality, computational cost, or the number of distinct ambient subset products. The broad direct-embedding decision problem remains open in the cited 2026 source. Accessible abstracts and the first-page preview cannot exclude all equivalent results in the full historical literature.
