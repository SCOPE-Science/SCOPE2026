# Sharp adjacency–Dirac spectral distances and their large-book asymptotics

## Finding

For each integer \(m\ge2\), the *book graph* \(B_m\) has two adjacent vertices \(a,b\) and \(m\) mutually nonadjacent leaves \(z_1,\dots,z_m\), each joined to both \(a\) and \(b\). With the unweighted adjacency matrix as Dirac operator, its spectral distances consist of three orbits:

\[
d_D(a,b)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{m+2}},\qquad
 d_D(z_i,z_j)=1\quad(i\ne j),\qquad
 d_D(a,z_i)=d_D(b,z_i)=\frac1{r_m}.
\]

Here \(r_m\) is an *attained*, explicit two-real-variable minimum. Put \(t=\sqrt{m-1}\) and
\[
S_m(x,v)=x^2+1+(1-x)^2+t^2\bigl(v^2+(x-v)^2\bigr),\qquad
P_m(x,v)=tx(1-v),
\]
\[
 r_m=\min_{x,v\in\mathbb R}\frac{\sqrt{S_m(x,v)+2|P_m(x,v)|}+\sqrt{S_m(x,v)-2|P_m(x,v)|}}2.
\]
The two radicands are nonnegative for all real parameters. The hub–leaf distance is nonincreasing in \(m\), and \(1/\sqrt2<d_D(a,z_i)\le\sqrt{2/3}\). More sharply, as \(m\to\infty\),
\[
 r_m=\sqrt2-\frac{\sqrt2}{\sqrt{m-1}}+O\bigl((m-1)^{-5/8}\bigr),\qquad
 d_D(a,z_i)=\frac1{\sqrt2}+\frac1{\sqrt{2(m-1)}}+O\bigl((m-1)^{-5/8}\bigr).
\]
Thus the three pair types exhibit incompatible limiting behaviors: the central edge distance vanishes at inverse-square-root scale, different leaves stay distance one apart, while a hub–leaf distance approaches \(1/\sqrt2\) with an explicit nonzero first correction.

## Assumptions and scope

Use the complex Hilbert space \(\mathbb C^{m+2}\) with standard orthonormal vertex basis and adjacency matrix \(D=A(B_m)\). For each real vertex potential \(f\), let \(M_f\) be its diagonal multiplication matrix and set \(L_D(f)=\|[D,M_f]\|_{\mathrm{op}}\). Define \(d_D(u,v)=\sup\{|f(u)-f(v)|:f\text{ real},L_D(f)\le1\}\). All edges have weight one. The asymptotic concerns integer \(m\to\infty\); constants implicit in the displayed big-O are absolute for sufficiently large \(m\).

## Proof

**Symmetry and normalization.** For any graph automorphism fixing the two target vertices individually, averaging \(f\) over its orbit does not change its target difference and does not increase \(L_D(f)\): permutation conjugation preserves the operator norm and the norm is convex. Adding constants leaves the commutator unchanged, and homogeneous scaling reduces nonzero differences to magnitude one. These facts justify the symmetric reductions used below.

**The two hubs.** Normalize the hub potentials as \(f(a)=s\), \(f(b)=-s\), and average all leaves to a common value \(u\). The real skew-adjoint commutator vanishes outside the three-dimensional subspace generated by the two hubs and the normalized all-leaves vector. Its norm squared is
\[
4s^2+m\bigl[(u-s)^2+(u+s)^2\bigr]
=(2m+4)s^2+2mu^2.
\]
At prescribed difference \(2|s|\), this is least at \(u=0\); the sharp ratio is \(d_D(a,b)=2/\sqrt{2m+4}\).

**Two distinct leaves.** Project the commutator from \(\operatorname{span}\{e_{z_i},e_{z_j}\}\) onto the one-dimensional hub subspace spanned by \((e_a+e_b)/\sqrt2\). Writing \(h=(f(a)+f(b))/2\), the resulting row has entries of magnitude \(\sqrt2\,|f(z_i)-h|\) and \(\sqrt2\,|f(z_j)-h|\). Its Euclidean norm is at least \(|f(z_i)-f(z_j)|\); hence \(d_D(z_i,z_j)\le1\). Taking \(f(z_i)=1\), \(f(z_j)=-1\), and all remaining potentials zero yields an operator of norm \(2\) and target difference \(2\), so equality holds.

**One hub and one leaf.** Normalize \(f(a)=1\), \(f(z_1)=0\). Averaging over permutations of the other leaves gives \(f(b)=1-x\), \(f(z_j)=1-v\) for \(j\ge2\). The commutator is zero on the subspace of leaf differences within \(\{z_2,\dots,z_m\}\). In the orthonormal basis \(e_a,e_b,e_{z_1},(m-1)^{-1/2}\sum_{j\ge2}e_{z_j}\), its real skew matrix is
\[
K_4(x,v)=\begin{pmatrix}
0&-x&-1&-tv\\
x&0&x-1&t(x-v)\\
1&1-x&0&0\\
tv&t(v-x)&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Every real skew \(4\times4\) matrix has singular speeds \(\sigma_1\ge\sigma_2\ge0\), each occurring twice, with \(\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2=\tfrac12\|K_4\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\) and \(\sigma_1\sigma_2=|\operatorname{Pf}(K_4)|\). Here the two invariants equal \(S_m(x,v)\) and \(|P_m(x,v)|\), respectively. Thus \(\|K_4\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\bigl(\sqrt{S_m+2|P_m|}+\sqrt{S_m-2|P_m|}\bigr)/2\), establishing the exact minimum. In particular the nonnegativity of both radicands follows from \(S_m\ge2|P_m|\). A minimizer exists: the column indexed by \(z_1\) gives \(\|K_4\|\ge\sqrt{1+(1-x)^2}\), while the aggregate-leaf column gives \(\|K_4\|\ge t\sqrt{v^2+(x-v)^2}\); these bounds make sublevel sets bounded and closed.

**Monotonicity and finite-parameter bounds.** Embed \(B_m\) as the induced graph of \(B_{m+1}\) obtained by discarding one leaf. Compressing any commutator to the old vertex set cannot increase its norm, and its hub–leaf target difference is unchanged. Consequently \(r_{m+1}\ge r_m\). The induced triangle on \(a,b,z_1\) has hub–leaf distance \(\sqrt{2/3}\) by the same three-dimensional skew-norm calculation, giving \(r_m\ge\sqrt{3/2}\). The choice \(x=v=0\) gives \(r_m\le\sqrt2\). In fact it is strict: taking \(x=2b/t\), \(v=b/t\), and differentiating the explicit radical norm at \(b=0\) gives derivative \(-\sqrt2/t<0\), so a sufficiently small positive \(b\) yields \(r_m<\sqrt2\).

**Sharp large-book asymptotic.** Let \(t=\sqrt{m-1}\). Since \(r_m\le\sqrt2\), any minimizing pair obeys \(t\sqrt{v^2+(x-v)^2}\le\sqrt2\), hence \(|x|\le2/t\). For \(t\ge2\), the other column estimate gives
\[
r_m\ge\sqrt{1+(1-x)^2}\ge\sqrt{1+(1-2/t)^2}
=\sqrt2-\frac{\sqrt2}{t}+O(t^{-2}).
\]
For the upper bound take \(b=1-t^{-1/4}\), \(x=2b/t\), \(v=b/t\). The two radicands simplify *exactly* to
\[
S_m\pm2P_m=2\left[\left(1\pm b-\frac bt\right)^2+\frac{3b^2}{t^2}\right].
\]
For sufficiently large \(t\), the numbers \(1\pm b-b/t\) are positive and the smaller is bounded below by a constant times \(t^{-1/4}\). The elementary inequality \(0\le\sqrt{y^2+u}-y\le u/(2y)\), valid for \(y>0,u\ge0\), now gives
\[
 r_m\le\sqrt2\left(1-\frac bt\right)+O(t^{-7/4})
=\sqrt2-\frac{\sqrt2}{t}+O(t^{-5/4}).
\]
Combining the bounds proves the norm asymptotic. Since \(r_m\to\sqrt2>0\), reciprocal expansion gives \(1/r_m=1/\sqrt2+1/(\sqrt2t)+O(t^{-5/4})\), proving the distance asymptotic.

## Verification

The standalone file `check_book.py` compares full adjacency commutators with their explicit four-dimensional compressions and closed radical norms for 850 deterministic random parameter choices, checks nine numerical convex minima with monotonicity diagnostics, and tests four large-book trial bounds. Run with `python check_book.py` with NumPy and SciPy installed. These are finite checks of explicit formulas, **not** a substitute for the all-integer analytical proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Besnard, *Estimating noncommutative distances on graphs* (arXiv:2105.09056v1, publicly posted 2021-05-19), proves general graph-norm bounds and pruning constructions; these do not supply the book-graph three-orbit distance law or its growing-book asymptotics. Clare, Li, Poon and Swartz, *Noncommutative Distances on Graphs: An Explicit Approach via Birkhoff–James Orthogonality* (arXiv:2409.04146v1, publicly posted 2024-09-06), studies the same adjacency-commutator metric and explicitly develops complete formulas for paths, not the book family. Swartz, *The effect of edge deletion on noncommutative distances on graphs* (arXiv:2610.07718v1, 2026-10-06), gives general comparisons under edge deletion rather than the present exact metric; adding the central hub edge is not an invariance principle. Exact Connes distances for complete bipartite and single-hub friendship graphs involve different edge structures and do not imply the present rank-four formula.

## Limitations

The hub–leaf distance is expressed by an attained two-variable minimization, not a closed elementary expression for each fixed \(m\). The sharp leading asymptotic and error are proved as \(m\to\infty\), but no full second-order expansion is claimed. The result applies to unweighted book graphs and the adjacency Dirac operator, not other spectral triples or arbitrary weighted joins. Previously published finite small-graph computations could overlap with a single fixed value; an equivalent general formula under an alternative spectral-norm terminology remains a literature risk. Independent mathematical validation has not been performed.
