# Sharp alternating-cycle threshold for perfect matchings of Möbius ladders

## Finding
Let \(n\ge4\) be an integer. The Möbius ladder \(M_{2n}\) has \(2n\) rim-cycle vertices and \(n\) antipodal rungs. For an even integer \(L\ge4\), consider the graph \(\mathcal F_{\le L}(M_{2n})\): its vertices are perfect matchings, and two vertices are adjacent precisely when their symmetric difference is the edge set of one simple alternating cycle of length at most \(L\).

**Theorem.** The square-flip graph \(\mathcal F_{\le4}(M_{2n})\) is the Lucas cube \(\Lambda_n\) when \(n\) is even, and \(\Lambda_n\sqcup 2K_1\) when \(n\) is odd. Here \(\Lambda_n\) has as vertices all independent subsets of the cycle \(C_n\), with an edge whenever exactly one element is inserted or removed. Consequently, two matchings in the Lucas component indexed by independent sets \(D,E\subseteq E(C_n)\) have exact flip distance \(\lvert D\triangle E\rvert\).

In particular, the **least even cycle-length allowance that makes the perfect-matching reconfiguration graph connected** is
\[
L_{\min}(n)=\begin{cases}4,&n\text{ even},\\ n+1,&n\text{ odd}.\end{cases}
\]
For odd \(n\), both exceptional matchings are isolated for every even \(L<n+1\), whereas flips of length \(n+1\) connect each to the Lucas component. The threshold therefore grows linearly with odd ladder size, despite every graph in the family being cubic.

The number of perfect matchings is \(L_n+2\mathbf1_{\{n\text{ odd}\}}\), where the Lucas number \(L_n=F_{n-1}+F_{n+1}\). The four-cycle flip graph has exactly \(nF_{n-1}\) edges. These enumerative consequences follow from the graph identification and known Lucas-cube counts; the counting sequences themselves are **not asserted to be new**.

## Assumptions and scope
Label the vertices \(u_0,\ldots,u_{n-1}\) and \(w_0,\ldots,w_{n-1}\). The edges are the \(n\) rungs \(u_iw_i\), the \(2(n-1)\) ordinary rails \(u_iu_{i+1}\) and \(w_iw_{i+1}\) for \(0\le i<n-1\), and the two twisted-seam rails \(u_{n-1}w_0\), \(w_{n-1}u_0\). This is the standard \(2n\)-cycle with opposite-vertex chords. All cycles used for moves must be *simple* and alternate with respect to the original matching. Unrestricted edge exchanges, simultaneous multiple-cycle changes and transformations through nonperfect matchings are excluded.

An independent subset \(D\subseteq E(C_n)\) means a set of disjoint edges of the \(n\)-cycle of *columns*, also called a dimer packing. The graph \(\Lambda_n\) here is the independence graph of these \(n\) cyclic positions, with single-position toggles; its standard binary-string realization forbids consecutive ones including across the cyclic boundary.

## Proof
**1. Classification.** Deleting the rungs from a perfect matching leaves vertices covered by rail edges. A rung occupies a column completely. In every other occupied column, the two vertices must be matched to vertices of neighboring columns. On an interval of columns between occupied rungs, the forced rail choices occur in parallel pairs linking adjacent columns. Every such pair occupies precisely two consecutive columns and corresponds to one edge of \(C_n\). The same forced pairing propagates across the twisted seam; two paired rails crossing the seam also occupy two consecutive columns. Hence any perfect matching with at least one rung determines, and is uniquely determined by, a set \(D\) of disjoint cyclic column edges: for each edge of \(D\), choose its two parallel rail edges, and for each uncovered column, choose its rung.

If no rung is used, all rim vertices must be matched using only the rim cycle. This has exactly two perfect matchings, say \(X\) and \(Y\). If \(n\) is even, each consists of parallel rail pairs and equals one of the two alternating perfect matchings of the column cycle \(C_n\), so both have already been indexed by \(D\). If \(n\) is odd, neither can be written as parallel rail pairs (the twisted seam forces a mismatched parity), so both are additional configurations. Every configuration indexed by \(D\) uses exactly \(n-2\lvert D\rvert\) rungs, and for odd \(n\) this number is odd.

**2. Square flips and distances.** For \(n\ge4\), every four-cycle of the cubic Möbius ladder is one of its \(n\) elementary ladder squares joining two consecutive columns. A square flip interchanges its two rungs with its parallel rail pair, and therefore toggles one edge of \(D\) without ever creating overlapping dimers. This gives exactly the edges of \(\Lambda_n\). Conversely, no exceptional rim-only matching at odd \(n\) permits a four-cycle flip: its rail edges on each square are not a parallel matching pair, while it contains no rungs. Thus the two exceptions are isolated.

Since any independent subset \(D\) can be reached from any independent subset \(E\) by deleting all \(D\setminus E\) and then inserting all \(E\setminus D\), this construction produces a path of length \(\lvert D\triangle E\rvert\). No move changes more than one coordinate, so this is a shortest path. In particular the Lucas component is connected.

**3. The odd-cycle lower bound.** Assume \(n\) is odd and let \(X\) be one of the two exceptional rim-only matchings. For any regular matching \(M_D\), the number of rungs is \(n-2\lvert D\rvert\), which is odd. Therefore any symmetric difference \(X\triangle M_D\) that forms a single alternating cycle must contain an odd number of rungs. Any closed simple cycle in the twisted-strip model using an odd number of rungs must traverse the twisted seam an odd number of times (counting seam occurrences mod two): each rung switches the row, as does each seam crossing, whereas ordinary rail edges preserve it. Projecting the cycle onto the column cycle \(C_n\), an odd seam-crossing parity means odd winding. More concretely, its net signed displacement along the unwrapped column line is an odd multiple of \(n\); thus at least \(n\) rail edges occur. With at least one rung, the cycle length is at least \(n+1\). A transition between \(X\) and \(Y\) would require their symmetric difference to be the entire \(2n\)-edge rim. Since \(2n>n+1\), no transition from either exceptional configuration exists when \(L<n+1\).

**4. Sharpness.** Fix any column \(j\). Removing the two rim vertices incident to the rung at column \(j\) leaves two disjoint rim paths, each on \(n-1\) vertices, because diametrically opposite vertices split the \(2n\)-cycle into two arcs. For odd \(n\), each path contains an even number \(n-1\) of vertices and hence has a unique matching by consecutive rim edges. Together with the rung \(u_jw_j\), these define a perfect matching \(R_j\) with exactly one rung. The symmetric difference between \(R_j\) and either rim-only matching \(X\) or \(Y\) is one alternating cycle with \(n\) rail edges and the chosen rung, hence length exactly \(n+1\). This is readily seen by following the alternation from the rung around the rim; each of the two rim-only orientations gives one of the complementary arcs. Therefore allowing cycles of length \(n+1\) attaches both \(X\) and \(Y\) to \(R_j\). Since the Lucas component is already connected by squares, the full reconfiguration graph becomes connected. This proves the threshold.

For even \(n\) the square-flip graph is connected, and a nontrivial symmetric difference of perfect matchings cannot have fewer than four edges. Thus four is minimal also in the even case. \(\square\)

## Verification
The included `verify_primary.py` independently enumerates perfect matchings by pairing uncovered vertices. For every \(4\le n\le11\), it constructs the full exchange graph by testing every pair of matchings for a single alternating simple cycle, verifies all components at threshold four, and checks the sharp odd threshold, with special one-rung witnesses. Independently, `verify_alt.cpp` builds matchings from cyclic column-dimer masks and constructs the two rim-only matchings directly, then performs separate symmetric-difference cycle and connected-component tests for every \(4\le n\le12\). To reproduce, run `python3 verify_primary.py` and `g++ -std=c++17 -O2 verify_alt.cpp -o verify_alt && ./verify_alt` in a directory containing the extracted files. The program outputs end in `PRIMARY_ALL_PASS` and `ALT_ALL_PASS` respectively. These bounded checks corroborate, but do not replace, the proof for every integer \(n\ge4\).

## Relationship to prior work
The Lucas-cube identification implies known Lucas-number configuration counts and known Lucas-cube edge counts; Lucas cubes and their enumerative invariants were studied before (Codara and D'Antona, 2014, and predecessors). Earlier work also enumerates perfect matchings of Möbius ladders and prisms (Lekshmi and West, 2020). These prior results are not newly proved counting discoveries. General perfect-matching reconfiguration by four-cycle switches was studied in complete graphs (Ben-Ari, 2013, subsequently Jennings) and in graph classes/complexity (Bonamy and collaborators, 2019). The present specifically new object of study is the **least permitted length of one alternating-cycle move sufficient for connectivity in the Möbius-ladder family**, including its parity-dependent unbounded obstruction. None of the accessible relevant statements directly implies this threshold; an equivalent older result in unindexed matching-flip literature remains possible.

## Limitations
The theorem treats exactly Möbius ladders with \(n\ge4\), with a move defined by *one* simple alternating symmetric-difference cycle and a bound on its even length. It does not give the complete diameter or distance distribution after large-cycle moves are admitted. An inaccessible full text of the 2020 Möbius-ladder enumeration work could conceivably contain an equivalent reconfiguration result beyond its available abstract; no contrary theorem is known from the material examined. The computational checks cover only the parameter ranges stated, and originality is a best-knowledge determination, not a proof of absence of older equivalent work. Independent scholarly audit is not performed.
