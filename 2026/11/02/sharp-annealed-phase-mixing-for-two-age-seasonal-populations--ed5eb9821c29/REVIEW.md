# Scientific review: sharp annealed phase mixing for two-age seasonal populations

## Correctness — PASS
The exact annual phase matrices are strictly positive and have the same trace and negative determinant. Their mixture has determinant \(\delta-Jp(1-p)\), with \(J=bc[(bf-ce)^2+(ae-bd)(cd-af)]\). The Perron quadratic therefore gives the claimed complete three-way classification, including all boundary and equality cases. Independence of the annual phase selection establishes that this is precisely the expected-population growth factor, not a sample-path Lyapunov exponent. Exact rational arithmetic checks of 16,128 instances passed. The witness gives \(\det(I-M_p)=1/32-(9/64)p(1-p)\), hence the sharp \((1/3,2/3)\) supercritical interval. No numerical approximation is needed for the strict threshold reversal.

## Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority
Caswell and Trevisan (1994) establish periodic-matrix sensitivity analysis. Mertens et al. (2006), examined in full, treat fixed cyclic environmental permutations and transients, not independent selection of alternative cyclic phase orderings each year. Benaïm and Schreiber (2009), examined in full, give stochastic-growth and persistence conditions, not the annealed isospectral phase-mixture criterion. Cáceres and Cáceres-Saez (2010), whose full text was examined, analyze randomness in vital rates and establish that fluctuations can improve expected-population growth; this broader qualitative phenomenon is explicitly treated as known. The new assessed object is the all-parameter two-Leslie sharp commutator sign test and the exact interval-crossing witness with both deterministic annual arrangements below one. A 2012 periodic-matrix sensitivity paper was compared through its available abstract only. Broader nonnegative-matrix mixture results may imply some or all of this specialization and remain a priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The classification identifies the **precise condition** under which randomizing only the annual *phase* of a fixed inventory of environments is helpful, harmful or neutral for expected abundance. Its significance is not merely the matrix identity: biologically admissible rational vital rates yield two individually declining annual schedules but an explicitly characterized interval of increasing expected population. This gives a sharp design boundary and an exact example separating deterministic Floquet equivalence from annual stochastic ensemble responses. Its applicability is limited to the specified linear two-age model; a crossing for expected abundance does not establish almost-sure ecological persistence.
