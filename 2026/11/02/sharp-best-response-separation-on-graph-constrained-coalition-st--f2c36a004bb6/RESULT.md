# Sharp best-response separation on graph-constrained coalition stars

## Finding
Let \(n\ge2\), and let the communication graph be the star \(K_{1,n-1}\), with hub \(c\). Feasible coalitions induce connected subgraphs. Dynamics consist of single-agent **individually stable best responses**: a mover strictly prefers her destination to her current coalition, all receiving members weakly accept her, and the destination maximizes her preference among all admissible destinations; when a move disconnects the former coalition, its components become separate coalitions.

**Theorem (tight linear/quadratic boundary).** If every leaf has individually rational preferences and the hub's preferences are monotone under coalition inclusion, every best-response trajectory from every feasible partition terminates after at most \(2n-3\) moves. This is exactly sharp for every \(n\ge2\), even with nonnegative local additively separable valuations. In contrast, if only individual rationality is assumed for every agent, best-response trajectories of length at least
\[
\frac{n^{2}-5n+8}{2}
\]
exist for every \(n\ge3\). Together with a quadratic upper bound proved below, the worst-case length in the individually rational class is \(\Theta(n^{2})\). Thus improving responses need not become asymptotically faster when upgraded to best responses under individual rationality, whereas monotonicity of the *single hub* gives a sharp linear bound.

## Assumptions and scope
A connected coalition containing a leaf but not the hub is necessarily a singleton. A feasible partition therefore consists of one hub coalition \(C\ni c\) and singleton leaves outside \(C\). Each agent has a complete transitive preference on feasible coalitions containing her, represented where convenient by real utilities. Individual rationality means every feasible coalition containing an agent is weakly preferred by her to being alone. Monotonicity of the hub means \(S\subseteq T\), with \(c\in S\), implies \(T\succeq_c S\). The hub's preferences need not be additive, symmetric or strictly increasing. All moves use strict improvement and weak recipient consent. Best response means maximizing among currently admissible single-agent destinations, not necessarily a uniquely maximizing one. Every sequence and all tie-breaking choices are covered by the upper bound.

The second clause deliberately drops monotonicity at the hub, but retains individual rationality of every agent. Length is counted in individual deviations, not synchronous rounds or evaluations of possible deviations. This theorem concerns the specific reconfiguration convention described above and does not address other topologies, preferences allowing a leaf to prefer being alone, or alternative departure operations.

## Proof
**Basic star transitions.** A leaf in \(C\) cannot strictly improve by departing: her only alternative feasible destination is her singleton, which individual rationality ranks no higher than \(C\). A singleton leaf can only move into \(C\). A hub move can only go to a singleton leaf \(x\notin C\); afterward the new hub coalition is \(\{c,x\}\), and all former hub-coalition leaves become singletons. Leaf arrivals to \(C\) are subject to consent, so none can lower the hub's utility.

**At most one hub move under hub monotonicity.** Suppose the hub best-responds from \(C\) to the singleton \(\{x\}\), so \(\{c,x\}\succ_c C\). Every previously unattached leaf \(y\notin C\) is a permissible recipient for the hub, since its individual rationality gives \(\{c,y\}\succeq_y\{y\}\). Best-response maximality therefore gives \(\{c,x\}\succeq_c\{c,y\}\) for all those \(y\). For every formerly attached leaf \(z\in C\setminus\{c\}\), hub monotonicity gives \(C\succeq_c\{c,z\}\), hence \(\{c,x\}\succ_c\{c,z\}\). Thus the hub prefers or ties \(\{c,x\}\) against *every* possible singleton-leaf destination, including those freed by her own departure. Thereafter \(x\) stays with the hub (a leaf cannot leave), while further leaf arrivals only enlarge the hub coalition; hub monotonicity keeps it at least as preferred as \(\{c,x\}\). No second hub deviation is possible.

Writing \(m=n-1\), if the hub never moves, at most \(m\) leaves join once each. If the hub moves once, its destination leaf has not previously joined and does not move afterward. Each of the other \(m-1\) leaves can join at most once before and once after the hub move. Total moves are at most \(1+2(m-1)=2n-3\).

**Tightness.** Designate one prize leaf \(p\) and \(m-1\) ordinary leaves. Give the hub local additive values \(v_c(p)=1\), \(v_c(q)=0\) for every ordinary leaf \(q\); give every leaf value \(v_q(c)=1\) and all nonedge values zero. Initially all agents are singletons. First let all ordinary leaves join the hub; these \(m-1\) moves are individually stable best responses and do not change its utility zero. The hub then best-responds to the still-singleton prize leaf, strictly gaining one; all ordinary leaves are freed and subsequently rejoin, for another \(m-1\) best responses. The length is \(2m-1=2n-3\). For \(m=1\), only the hub-to-prize move is needed.

**Quadratic best-response lower construction without monotonicity.** Fix any integer \(k\ge1\), so \(n=k+2\), with leaves \(a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_k\). Every leaf values any coalition containing the hub at one, and its singleton at zero. Define the hub utility by
\[
u_c(\{c\}\cup A)=\begin{cases}\min\{j:a_j\in A\},&A\ne\emptyset,\\0,&A=\emptyset.\end{cases}\]
All agents have individually rational preferences, but the hub is nonmonotone when \(k\ge2\). Start with hub coalition \(\{c,a_0,a_2,\ldots,a_k\}\) and singleton \(a_1\). Its hub utility is zero; the hub's best response is to join \(a_1\), obtaining utility one. For each \(j=1,\ldots,k-1\), when the hub coalition contains \(a_j\), let all currently free leaves \(a_{j+2},\ldots,a_k\) join it. All such joins strictly benefit the moving leaf and are weakly accepted: the hub's utility remains \(j\), and the other attached leaves remain at utility one. Leave \(a_{j+1}\) unattached. All free singleton leaves have indices at most \(j+1\); consequently the hub's unique best improving destination is \(a_{j+1}\), of utility \(j+1\). Its departure frees the higher-index leaves for the next stage. There are exactly \(k\) hub moves and \(\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(k-j-1)=(k-1)(k-2)/2\) leaf-joining moves, yielding
\[k+\frac{(k-1)(k-2)}2=\frac{n^2-5n+8}{2}.\]
Each indicated move has been checked under the full best-response and recipient-consent conditions.

**Quadratic upper bound under individual rationality.** Along a sequence, hub utility never decreases: it increases strictly when the hub moves and weakly increases when a leaf is admitted (by recipient consent). At each hub move its new coalition is \(\{c,a_i\}\). Hence the hub's payoff at successive hub moves is a strictly increasing sequence drawn from the \(m\) fixed utilities of singleton-leaf pair coalitions. The hub moves at most \(m\) times. Between consecutive hub moves, each leaf can join at most once; there are at most \(m+1\) such intervals. Therefore the entire trajectory contains no more than \(m(m+1)+m\) deviations, proving \(O(n^2)\) and completing the matching-order classification.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library Python script `verify_star.py` exhaustively explores all feasible partitions and every permitted best-response transition for 8,404 local-additive discrete valuation instances with up to six vertices. It also enumerates 7,578 arbitrary monotone integer-valued hub set functions through five vertices and checks the stated linear bound, without assuming additive hub utility. Finally, it constructs and verifies each move in the nonmonotone individually rational quadratic witness for \(3\le n\le16\). These are finite tests supplementing, not replacing, the proof for arbitrary finite \(n\), arbitrary preference orders and unrestricted real utilities. Run with `python3 verify_star.py`.

## Relationship to prior work
Fanelli, Gourvès, Igarashi and Moscardelli (arXiv:2408.11488v1, 2024), especially Sections 2, 4, 5 and Appendix A, prove quadratic convergence on stars under individually rational preferences and give a quadratic lower trajectory for improving responses. They note that their existing lower constructions do not extend to best responses and conjecture that best response may significantly improve convergence. The present theorem distinguishes hub monotonicity (exact linear best-response limit) from individually rationality alone (new best-response quadratic trajectory). It does not claim priority for their general convergence theorem or their earlier quadratic improving-response example. Brandt, Bullinger and Tappe (2022) treat different consent variants in additively separable hedonic games, and Brandt, Bullinger and Wilczynski (2022/2023) analyze other preference families without the exact star-specific distinction here.

## Limitations
The improvement from quadratic to linear is guaranteed only when the hub has monotone preferences (the leaves need merely be individually rational). The counterexample uses nonmonotone, nonadditive hub preferences. The bounds count arbitrary but fully best-responding sequential steps, not stochastic scheduling or approximate best responses. Complete literature priority beyond the inspected primary works cannot be certified; an unlocated specialized predecessor remains possible. No independent audit has been performed.
