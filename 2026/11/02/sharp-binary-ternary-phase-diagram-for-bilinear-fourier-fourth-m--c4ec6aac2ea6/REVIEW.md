# Scientific review: sharp binary–ternary bilinear Fourier fourth moments

## Correctness

PASS. The domain is the free product torus on three row and \(n\) column characters. Additive-pair enumeration proves the fourth-moment identity \(\mathcal M_4=18n^2-15n+4S\), where \(S\) is the sum of three squared row inner products. A complete four-type Walsh transform reduction proves the binary minimum and every equality case. Root-of-unity count arithmetic, an explicit three-column cycle, and a nine-variable linear-system reduction prove the ternary minimum, equality conditions, and the divisible-by-three optimizer count. The separate exact checker fully enumerates the stated finite test cases and directly computes additive Fourier convolutions. The symbolic proof, not the finite checker, establishes all widths.

## Originality

PASS, provisional. The closest inspected foundational sources concern univariate flat Littlewood polynomials (Balister et al., arXiv:1907.09464), rectangular-exponent multivariable character arrays (Katz, arXiv:1205.1069), and bilinear forms under different norms (Raposo–Serrano-Rodríguez, 2023). Their stated objects or conclusions do not imply the exact three-by-\(n\) *independent-character Segre* binary and ternary minima. Searches of published mathematical records for binary/ternary phase fourth-moment optimization found nearby results for ordinary two-variable rectangular grids, which have a different additive relation structure and do not dominate the current claims. A general quantized tight-frame theorem or a specialist sequence-design table remains a plausible prior-art risk, so historical priority is not independently settled.

## Mathematical value

PASS. This is an all-width exact natural classification rather than one small computed optimum. A complete phase-design problem is solved for two competing restricted alphabets; the minimal defects are arithmetic and display infinitely recurring strict alphabet reversals. The characterization of all equality arrays connects Fourier fourth-moment flatness to discrete Walsh balancing and to ternary cyclic Latin configurations, with exact normalized optimizer counts in two substantial classes. These restrictions matter because complex-phase orthogonality is always possible, while quantized phases can force a sharp positive defect.

## Limits

The result is for three rows and independent column characters, not general grid frequency supports. It proves no classification for fourth-root alphabets, arbitrary support sets, or nonunit coefficient magnitudes. The ternary all-width equality criterion is complete, but a simple count formula is supplied only when the width is divisible by three. Priority is provisional; independent replication of the mathematical argument is outstanding.
