# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For fixed \(n\ge3\), the original radial Bloch profile has a unique nondegenerate maximum on the circle \(|z|=\sqrt{(n-1)/(n+1)}\). Vanishing lower elementary symmetric sums imply the derivative's first nonconstant term is exactly of order \(\varepsilon^k\), uniformly in a closed annulus of the norming circle. The angular first variation is one explicit cosine, whose coefficient has strictly positive sign by monotonicity of \(\operatorname{artanh}(x)/x\). Localization plus nondegenerate radial optimization makes the correction \(O(\varepsilon^{k+1})\), without confusing finite checks with proof. A separately derived hyperbolic-parameter expansion gives the stated \(n^{-3}\) coefficient asymptotic. The standalone checker independently tests finite instances.

## Originality — PASS (subject to historical-priority risk)

The focal Baranov–Kayumov–Nasyrov manuscript treats universal Bloch-seminorm bounds, and its monomial sequence supplies the \(2/e\) limit; the inspected main theorem and proof do not state this centered zero-splitting hierarchy. Dubinin supplies derivative distortion inequalities for finite Blaschke products under critical-value conditions, not the claimed local supremum expansion. Exact one-parameter formulas are already known for unicritical products, which correspond to \(k=n\) and are expressly excluded. The present \(2\le k<n\) directions contain non-unicritical splits and have distinct directional vanishing orders. The inspected literature and indexed comparisons show no implication of the exact coefficients; a prior equivalent formula could nevertheless exist outside these sources.

## Value — PASS

The global Bloch-seminorm lower-bound problem motivates understanding how the central multiple zero of \(z^n\) reacts to an algebraically centered splitting. The result specifies every attainable first-variation flatness order, positive exact coefficient and a fixed-mode large-degree law. It distinguishes structurally different zero constellations that have the same degree and centroid. This is a stability invariant rather than a mere restatement of the monomial norm or the known unicritical case.

## Limits and source relationship

The expansion is directional with fixed degree and zero pattern. It does not assert global minimality, uncentered stability or a uniform two-parameter asymptotic. Sources: A. D. Baranov, I. R.
