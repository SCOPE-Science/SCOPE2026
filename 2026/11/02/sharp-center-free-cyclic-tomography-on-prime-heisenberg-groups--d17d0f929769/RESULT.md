# Sharp center-free cyclic tomography on prime Heisenberg groups

## Finding
For every odd prime \(p\), a function on the nonabelian group \(H_p\) of order \(p^3\) can be reconstructed from complete coset sums along just \(p+1\) cyclic subgroup directions, and no collection of \(p\) or fewer directions suffices. There is an explicit optimal construction that avoids the center and uses only the two central lifts \(0\) and \(1\) on finite slopes. For any \(S\subset\mathbb F_p\) of size \((p+1)/2\) and any vertical lift \(c_\infty\), select \(L_{s,0}\) for \(s\in S\), \(L_{s,1}\) for \(s\notin S\), and \(L_{\infty,c_\infty}\). Every such choice is injective, giving at least \(p\binom{p}{(p+1)/2}\) distinct labeled center-free optimal designs. When \(p\ge5\), selecting central lift zero for all finite slopes and any single vertical lift is not injective.

## Assumptions and scope
Here \(p\ge3\) is an odd prime, \(\mathbb F_p\) is the prime field, and \(H_p=\mathbb F_p^3\) with group law
\[
(a,b,c)(u,v,w)=(a+u,b+v,c+w+av),
\]
where coordinates and products are taken modulo \(p\). Its center is \(Z=\{(0,0,c):c\in\mathbb F_p\}\), and every nonidentity element has order \(p\). All noncentral order-\(p\) cyclic subgroups are uniquely \(L_{s,c}=\langle(1,s,c)\rangle\) with \(s,c\in\mathbb F_p\), or \(L_{\infty,c}=\langle(0,1,c)\rangle\) with \(c\in\mathbb F_p\). The remaining order-\(p\) subgroup is \(Z\). A direction means one such subgroup, not a coset; data for a direction consist of the unnormalized sums \(R_Lf(gL)=\sum_{h\in L}f(gh)\) over **all distinct left cosets** \(gL\). The theorem concerns arbitrary complex-valued functions, not positivity- or sparsity-constrained signals.

## Proof
**1. Fourier criterion and lower bound.** For any finite group, the coset-sum data of a subgroup \(L\) vanish if and only if convolution by \(\mathbf1_L\) vanishes. Under the complete unitary group Fourier transform, the latter acts on each matrix coefficient block through \(pP_{\rho,L}\), where \(P_{\rho,L}=p^{-1}\sum_{h\in L}\rho(h)\) is the orthogonal projector onto the \(L\)-fixed vectors of an irreducible representation \(\rho\). Therefore a collection of directions is injective if and only if, in each irreducible \(\rho\), the sum of its \(L\)-fixed subspaces spans the whole representation space. This follows because a matrix \(A\) satisfying \(AP_{\rho,L}=0\) for all selected \(L\) is zero precisely under that spanning condition; Fourier inversion then applies.

The irreducibles of \(H_p\) are the \(p^2\) one-dimensional characters \(\chi_{\xi,\eta}(a,b,c)=\zeta^{\xi a+\eta b}\), together with \(p-1\) inequivalent representations \(\rho_k\) of dimension \(p\), \(1\le k<p\), described below. They are irreducible: shift and diagonal modulation generate the full matrix algebra; their squared dimensions sum to \(p^2+(p-1)p^2=p^3\), so there are no more.

A nonzero linear character is trivial on a selected noncentral direction precisely when that direction's image in \(H_p/Z\cong\mathbb F_p^2\) is its unique kernel line. Thus any center-free injective collection contains a direction above **every** one of the \(p+1\) quotient slopes. On the other hand, \(\rho_k\) has no \(Z\)-fixed vectors, while every noncentral cyclic direction has exactly one \(\rho_k\)-fixed vector, as verified in step 2. Thus if the central direction is used, at least \(p\) additional noncentral directions are needed. In either case at least \(p+1\) directions are necessary, including collections allowed to use the center.

**2. Schrödinger fixed vectors.** Fix \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i/p}\). On functions \(u:\mathbb F_p\to\mathbb C\), the representation
\[
(\rho_k(a,b,c)u)(t)=\zeta^{k(c+bt)}u(t+a)
\]
respects the displayed group law. For each finite slope its subgroup-average projector has the one-dimensional image generated by
\[
v_{s,c}^{(k)}(t)=\zeta^{-k\{ct+s\,t(t-1)/2\}},\qquad t\in\mathbb F_p,
\]
where \(1/2\) is the inverse of \(2\) in \(\mathbb F_p\). Indeed the invariant equation for the generator is \(u(t+1)=\zeta^{-k(c+st)}u(t)\); periodicity holds because the sum of its \(p\) exponents vanishes modulo \(p\). For the vertical subgroup, the fixed space is spanned by the coordinate vector \(\delta_{-c}\). The center acts by \(\zeta^{kc}\), so has no invariants when \(k\ne0\).

**3. The prime Fourier-minor lemma.** If \(A,B\subset\mathbb F_p\) have the same positive size, then \((\zeta^{ab})_{a\in A,b\in B}\) is nonsingular. For completeness, choose distinct integer representatives \(a_i,b_j\), put \(m=r(r-1)/2\) where \(r=|A|\), and define \(D(X)=\det(X^{a_i b_j})\in\mathbb Z[X]\). Expanding \(X=1+z\) (or writing \(\log X=u\) and expanding the exponential determinant) shows that \(D(X)\) has a zero of order \(m\) at \(1\), with the first nonzero coefficient
\[
\frac{\prod_{i<j}(a_j-a_i)\prod_{i<j}(b_j-b_i)}{\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}j!}.
\]
This is an integer and is nonzero modulo \(p\), since all differences are nonzero in \(\mathbb F_p\) and \(r\le p\). Hence \(E(X)=D(X)/(X-1)^m\) is an integer polynomial with \(p\nmid E(1)\). Were \(D(\zeta)=0\), then \(E(\zeta)=0\) and the minimal polynomial \(1+X+\cdots+X^{p-1}\) would divide \(E(X)\) over \(\mathbb Z[X]\), forcing \(p\mid E(1)\), a contradiction. Replacing \(\zeta\) by any \(\zeta^k\), \(k\not\equiv0\pmod p\), gives the same conclusion. This is the classical Chebotarev prime Fourier-minor theorem, proved here in the needed form.

**4. Explicit two-lift spanning.** Write \(q(t)=t(t-1)/2\in\mathbb F_p\) and consider the involution \(J:t\mapsto1-t\). It has one fixed point \(t=1/2\), and its remaining points form \((p-1)/2\) unordered pairs. The \(J\)-even and \(J\)-odd coordinate spaces have dimensions \((p+1)/2\) and \((p-1)/2\), respectively. Also \(q(t)=q(1-t)\), and the \((p+1)/2\) distinct pair-orbit values \(q(t)\) are pairwise distinct: \(q(t)=q(u)\) implies \((t-u)(t+u-1)=0\).

For \(s\in S\), \(v_{s,0}^{(k)}(t)=\zeta^{-ksq(t)}\) is \(J\)-even. Restrict these \((p+1)/2\) vectors to the distinct \(q\)-values. Their matrix is a square Fourier minor and therefore nonsingular by step 3; hence they form a basis of the entire even space. For \(s\notin S\), the \(J\)-odd projection of \(v_{s,1}^{(k)}\), evaluated at one point \(t\) of each nonfixed pair, is proportional to
\[
\zeta^{-ksq(t)}\big(\zeta^{-kt}-\zeta^{-k(1-t)}\big).
\]
The factor in parentheses is nonzero because \(t\ne1/2\). The remaining \((p-1)/2\)-square matrix, indexed by distinct \(s\notin S\) and distinct nonfixed orbit values of \(q\), is another prime Fourier minor. Those odd projections therefore form a basis of the odd space. The full collection of \(p\) finite-slope vectors is a basis of \(\mathbb C^p\) for **every** \(k=1,\ldots,p-1\). Adding the arbitrarily lifted vertical subgroup completes all quotient slopes, hence recovers every linear character by step 1. The selected \(p+1\) directions are injective. Distinct choices of \(S\) and of the vertical lift give \(p\binom p{(p+1)/2}\) labeled designs.

**5. Boundary example.** When all finite slopes use \(c=0\), their \(p\) invariant vectors lie in the \((p+1)/2\)-dimensional even space for each nonlinear representation. One vertical invariant vector raises the span dimension by at most one. If \(p\ge5\), this is strictly less than \(p\), so the associated transform fails injectivity. Thus covering all projective slopes alone does not guarantee reconstruction.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone `checker.py` computes exact integer coset-incidence matrices and their ranks modulo two. Full rank modulo two implies full column rank over \(\mathbb C\). It verifies all \(\binom p{(p+1)/2}\) two-lift partitions at primes \(p=3,5,7\), and a selected partition at \(p=11\); all yield full column rank. These finite checks corroborate, but do not replace, the proof for every odd prime. Run `python checker.py` using Python 3 standard library only.

## Relationship to prior work
Ilmavirta (arXiv:1411.3829, first posted 2014-11-14), in particular Theorem 6.8 and Section 7, develops the full cyclic-coset Radon transform and the irreducible fixed-space criterion; it does not specify minimal center-free direction selections on \(H_p\). Misaghian (2010) describes the irreducible representations of finite Heisenberg groups but does not study restricted acquisition. The prime Fourier-minor theorem, used in the constructive step, predates this result; see Tao (arXiv:math/0308286, 2003-08-29), Lemma 1.3. Wootters–Fields (1989) and Pittenger–Rubin (arXiv:quant-ph/0308142, 2003-08-26) construct mutually unbiased finite Weyl bases. Those bases support a generic existence argument for selections, but the universal **two-lift partition construction**, explicit count, and matching no-center lower bound are the claims here. No novelty is claimed for the separate irreducible classification, the Fourier-minor theorem, or the general Fourier fixed-space criterion.

## Limitations
The result assumes an odd prime, the three-dimensional Heisenberg group, complete noiseless coset sums, and exactly specified subgroup directions. It gives injectivity but does not bound condition numbers, optimize noisy recovery, classify all optimal designs, or cover higher-rank extraspecial groups. The explicit two-lift class is a certified subfamily rather than an exhaustive classification. Related older quantum-tomographic constructions could conceal an equivalent direction-selection theorem; the Pittenger–Rubin complete preprint was not accessible for full-text comparison. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
