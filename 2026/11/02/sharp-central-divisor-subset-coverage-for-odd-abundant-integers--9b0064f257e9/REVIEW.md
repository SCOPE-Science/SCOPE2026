# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. A monotone exact-rational classification shows that every relevant integer is divisible by one of eight explicitly listed minimal abundant integers. The finite central-interval assertion is exhaustively verified on each base by a proved subset-sum bitset invariant, with an alternative Boolean dynamic program on the two decisive examples. An independent infinite interval-extension lemma bounds every newly introduced divisor via the two-prime cofactor's monotonically increasing deficiency, and the abundance recurrence supplies the required overlap margin. The sharp missing sum is checked against all divisors not exceeding the target.

## Originality

PASS within investigated evidence. Jokar (arXiv:2207.09053v1, 2022) proves an almost-practical-to-Zumkeller implication and gives the odd almost-practical family with third prime \(7\), which does not include third-prime \(11\) and \(13\) families. Fang (arXiv:2207.12906v1, 2022) obtains finite searches for odd weird numbers, not full interior subset-sum coverage on an unbounded family. The historical eight-element primitive-abundant table is explicitly acknowledged and independently rederived; it does not imply the sharp full interval without a separate propagation argument. Relevant searchable mathematical records yielded no stronger covering theorem. An older unindexed equivalent or unstated consequence remains possible.

## Mathematical value

PASS. This is a sharp structural interval theorem valid for infinitely many non-almost-practical odd integers, not a new table of examples. It resolves a natural restricted-support case of both the odd-weird and odd-Zumkeller questions and controls every central subset sum, substantially more than a single equal-partition witness. The endpoint cannot be improved because \(7425\) misses \(22\).

## Boundaries

The theorem does not settle odd weirdness for four or more distinct prime factors, or universal attainability of edge sums. Finite experiments alone do not establish the infinite theorem. Same-author bibliographic inspection does not constitute external validation.
