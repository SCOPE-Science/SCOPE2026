# Sharp circular superstring length for all binary weight-two windows

## Finding
For every integer \(k\ge4\), the minimum length of a nonempty cyclic binary word that contains **every** length-\(k\) binary word with exactly two ones as an oriented cyclic factor is
\[
L(k)=\binom{k}{2}+k-1=\frac{k(k+1)}{2}-1.
\]
This is a literal full-window cover, not a shorthand encoding of fixed-weight strings. The minimum is attained by a constructive Euler circuit. At the exceptional boundary \(k=3\), the exact minimum is \(3\), since the three required words are rotations of \(011\). The formula is asserted only for \(k\ge4\).

## Assumptions and scope
A cyclic word \(C=c_0\cdots c_{L-1}\) of positive length \(L\) has length-\(k\) factors \(c_i c_{i+1}\cdots c_{i+k-1}\), with subscripts taken modulo \(L\). Multiplicity is allowed, but every binary length-\(k\) word of Hamming weight exactly two must occur at least once. Reversal is not identified with rotation. No restrictions are imposed on the weights of *additional* windows. The result applies to every integer \(k\ge4\), with no asymptotic assumption.

## Proof
**1. Required edges form separate rotation circuits.** Consider the binary order-\(k\) de Bruijn digraph with vertices the length-\(k-1\) binary words and edges the length-\(k\) binary words, directed from their prefix to suffix. Let \(T\) be the \(\binom{k}{2}\) edges of weight two. Every vertex incident to \(T\) has weight one or two. At a weight-one vertex, exactly the extension by one is in \(T\), and exactly the incoming extension by one is in \(T\); at a weight-two vertex, exactly the extension by zero and incoming extension by zero are in \(T\). Thus the \(T\)-subgraph is a disjoint union of directed simple circuits, the rotation necklaces of binary weight-two words. For \(k\ge4\), it has at least two components: one containing \(11 0^{k-2}\), and another containing \(1010^{k-3}\). Denote the first component by \(Q\). Its length-\(k-1\) vertices are precisely the words with either two adjacent ones and other entries zero, or a single one at an endpoint.

**2. Construction.** Form an extra closed directed walk of length \(k-1\) from the periodic binary word \((10^{k-2})^\infty\). Its consecutive de Bruijn edges are the length-\(k\) windows of that period; one is \(10^{k-2}1\) (already required), and the other \(k-2\) edges have weight one. The vertices of this walk are precisely the \(k-1\) length-\(k-1\) words with a single one at each possible position. Every rotation necklace in \(T\) has at least one such vertex: rotate either one of its two ones into the omitted final position of a length-\(k\) edge, leaving a weight-one prefix. Hence the union of \(T\) and this extra walk is balanced and connected after ignoring isolated vertices, so it possesses an Euler circuit. Its edge labels constitute a cyclic covering word with exactly \(\binom{k}{2}+k-1\) positions.

**3. A short periodic circuit cannot bridge \(Q\).** Fix \(\ell\le k-2\), and consider a closed de Bruijn walk of length \(\ell\). Its vertex labels are length-\(k-1\) factors of a bi-infinite binary sequence with period \(\ell\). Suppose the walk visits a vertex of \(Q\). A vertex with exactly one one at an endpoint is impossible: since \(\ell\le k-2\), periodicity repeats that one inside the same length-\(k-1\) window. Therefore some window has exactly two *adjacent* ones and zeros elsewhere. As a length-\(k-1\) window contains an entire period, that period has at most two ones. It cannot have a single one: repetitions of a lone marker are separated by the period length, and adjacent repetitions would require period one, producing at least three ones in this window. Thus the period has exactly two ones, forming an adjacent pair cyclically. Every length-\(k-1\) window consequently contains at least two ones. Furthermore, whenever it contains exactly two, they must be adjacent. Indeed, the only alternative consists of the last one of one adjacent pair and the first one of the next pair, spanning \(\ell\) positions; a window of length at least \(\ell+1\) that contains these two also includes a third one immediately before or after them. Hence every visited vertex belonging to *any* required-weight-two necklace lies in \(Q\). Thus this short walk cannot visit vertices from both \(Q\) and another required circuit.

**4. Short extra circuits are disjoint unless identical.** Consider a putative cover with fewer than \(k-1\) extra edges beyond one traversal of each edge of \(T\). Subtract those mandatory traversals from its balanced directed multigraph. The remainder is a nonnegative integral circulation with total size \(e\le k-2\), and thus decomposes into closed directed walks. If two such walks of lengths \(a,b\) share a vertex, their periodic label sequences have a common factor of length \(k-1\) with periods \(a,b\). Since \(a+b\le e\le k-2<k-1\), the Fine–Wilf periodicity lemma forces the common factor to have period \(\gcd(a,b)\). It contains at least one complete period of each sequence; periodic extension shows that the two cyclic walks have the same primitive periodic vertex support. After grouping coincident primitive supports, distinct extra support components are vertex-disjoint. All of them are shorter than \(k-1\).

**5. Connectivity contradiction.** The mandatory subgraph \(T\) consists of vertex-disjoint circuits. To obtain a single cyclic covering walk, the extra circulation must connect \(Q\) to at least one other mandatory circuit. But the short extra support components are vertex-disjoint, and step 3 shows that a component meeting \(Q\) cannot meet another mandatory circuit. Thus no such connection is possible when \(e\le k-2\). Consequently every covering word has at least \(k-1\) excess windows, completing the matching lower bound.

## Verification
The accompanying `verify.py` constructs Euler circuits and counts **literal** length-\(k\) cyclic windows for every \(4\le k\le19\), checking full coverage and the exact length. For \(k=4,5,6\), it also exhaustively tests every shorter binary cyclic word (up to rotation, fixing one marker) and confirms that none covers the target family. The exhaustive small-case checks are independent finite corroboration, not the proof of the all-length theorem. Execute using `python verify.py` with Python 3 standard library; no additional package is required.

## Relationship to prior work
Ruskey, Sawada and Williams (2012) construct exact cycles for *shorthand* fixed-weight binary words (equivalently, length-\(k-1\) factors of weights one or two when the original target weight is two). Their original-paper construction and its length \(\binom{k}{2}\) do **not** assert that all full length-\(k\) weight-two windows occur in an actual cyclic word. Sawada and Williams (2021) generalize shorthand fixed-content universal cycles. The present statement instead minimizes the length of a full-window circular superstring, where additional windows are allowed and unavoidable. Blanca and Godbole (2010) study asymptotically short subset covers; their stated asymptotic guarantee does not supply the sharp finite formula. Nellore and Ward (2021) address arbitrary-length near-balanced de Bruijn analogues, a different all-window frequency requirement. Recent bounded-weight de Bruijn constructions concern different permitted weight ranges. A general Eulerian augmentation is classical; the new work here is the sharp excess \(k-1\) and its periodicity lower bound in this special case.

## Limitations
No uniqueness or classification of all optimal cyclic words is asserted. The formula is for **weight two only** and for the literal oriented full-window covering model, not a general statement for other weights, a bound on a linear superstring, or an exact fixed-weight universal cycle. Although targeted published-literature and mathematical-record searches found no implication of this exact bound, an unlocated equivalent formulation remains possible; independent literature assessment is outstanding.
