# Scientific review

## Correctness (PASS)
The quantified sharp norm is proved for every integer \(v\ge2\), \(1\le k<v\), every width \(n\ge1\), and every complex coefficient pair \((a,b)\). A centered-column factorization gives a uniform **upper** bound with identical row-factor and column-factor Euclidean norms. This is a genuine operator spectral-norm Schur-factorization inequality, not a Frobenius estimate. The independent **lower** bound \(\|\mathcal S_M\|\ge\|M\|_*/\sqrt{vn}\) follows by testing the conjugate polar partial isometry against normalized constant vectors. The complete \(k\)-uniform incidence matrix has two explicitly computed singular values, including the constant direction, which gives equality. Degenerate cases \(a+bk/v=0\), \(b=0\), and \(k=1\) are treated. Real scalar symbols have real factors and a real polar witness. The balanced-column width obstruction is the general Frobenius inequality \(\|\mathcal S_M\|\le\sqrt n\), with an order-\(v\) Hadamard construction attaining \(n=v-1\).

The standalone test checks all \(2\le v\le9\), \(1\le k<v\), five coefficient pairs, integral incidence Gram identities, singular values, and polar witnesses (180 orbit cases), plus 1,155 random column-support cases. The numerical checks are ancillary; the theorem's infinite quantifiers are discharged by algebra.

## Originality (PASS, with priority risk)
The closest primary literature is Davidson–Donsig's 2005 Schur factorization and square-matrix Mathias criterion; Levene's 2013/2014 idempotent multiplier norms; and Holbrook–Johnston–Schoch's 2022 sign-matrix/Hadamard bounds. The classical factorization, polar trace witness, and design intersection counts are not claimed as new. The assessed exact result is the **simultaneous all-width, all-column-support extremum at prescribed column weight**, uniformly in a complex two-level affine pencil, with a sharp finite \(v,k\) constant. In the inspected articles, unrestricted square/rectangular norms, sparse support bounds, and special symmetric families do not state this constrained universal optimum. The inspected indexed mathematical publications did not provide a statement with the same quantified admissible family and constant. However, a stronger-looking abstract factorization theorem supplies important ingredients, and a previously published equivalent application under hypergraph/frame terminology cannot be excluded. This qualification does not affect the self-contained proof.

## Mathematical value (PASS)
The degree restriction is motivated by sparse operator masks and constant-weight hypergraph incidences. The theorem identifies an exact universal operator-norm constant, not a numerical table or individual easy example. It interpolates sharp zero-one idempotent and signed cases, gives a finite-size improvement on the coarse column-sparsity bounds, and identifies extremizers without any transitivity hypothesis on the candidate being bounded. Its sparse-negative limit \(1+2\sqrt k\) shows a persistent norm penalty even when the fraction of negative entries vanishes, while the balanced-sign case has a sharp \(v-1\)-column threshold attained on Hadamard orders. Thus the class-wide extremal statement is a natural complete classification in functional analysis; no downstream paper-scale theorem is needed for its value.

## Closest literature and limitations
Holbrook–Johnston–Schoch (arXiv:2206.02863v1, first public 2022-06-06), Davidson–Donsig (arXiv:math/0506073v1), and Levene (arXiv:1302.4849) were examined at the relevant norms, factorization results, idempotent classifications and reference lists. The present bound follows from a new choice of centered factorization and a complete-design equality argument; the general factorization theorem itself is prior art. A historical-priority search cannot rule out all synonymous descriptions. Equality need not require a block design, and no fixed-width extremal classification is asserted. Verification is analytical with finite numerical spot checks, not independent audit.
