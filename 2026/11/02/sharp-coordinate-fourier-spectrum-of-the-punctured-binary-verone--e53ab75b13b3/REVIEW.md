# Scientific review: binary symmetric rank-one coordinate Fourier frame

## Correctness — PASS
The sample space is exactly the set of nonzero vectors in \(\mathbb F_2^d\), since the diagonal of \(vv^{\mathsf T}\) uniquely determines \(v\). On this space the specified matrix-coordinate characters are the functions \(1\), \((-1)^{v_i}\), and \((-1)^{v_iv_j}\), with no duplicate edge frequencies. The proof gives the full probability-normalized Gram matrix from independent-bit moments, then decomposes the coefficient space through the unsigned incidence matrix of the complete graph into a three-dimensional constant sector, two standard irreducible sectors, and the incidence kernel. The dimensions add to the entire feature count and the exact sector matrices yield all eigenvalues. The punctured degree-two Walsh comparison proves strict independence and the determinant. Separate strict Rayleigh comparisons identify both global extremal eigenvalues in the three-dimensional sector for every \(d\ge3\), including the boundary \(d=3\). Explicit roots of the full-cube limiting three-dimensional block prove the \(d^4/16\) squared-conditioning asymptotic with a negligible exponential perturbation. The standalone exact-rational checker independently evaluates the full character-sampled Gram, checks the determinant, full column rank and invariant-sector formulas for every \(3\le d\le8\). This is finite corroboration, not the proof of the infinite assertion.

## Originality — PASS under bounded literature inspection
The closest published direct comparison is *Sharp Fourier Riesz spectrum and a dimension transition on the finite-field Veronese cone* (public research record, 2026): its stated theorem assumes **odd field order**, samples only **off-diagonal** coordinate characters, and includes the zero matrix. Its lower spectral mode changes with dimension and its condition number grows cubically; none of those hypotheses includes the binary, punctured, diagonal-plus-offdiagonal theorem here. *Complete first-coordinate Fourier spectrum on finite-field rank-one matrices* treats general rank-one rectangular matrices (two independent vectors) rather than the symmetric self-product. Schmidt's 2010 and 2014 primary texts concern rank-distance coding and association schemes; his 2018 article gives general characteristic-two character/eigenvalue tables but does not state this selected coordinate band's sharp Gram spectrum, both extremizer comparisons, or its quartic asymptotic. The 2018 tables may supply raw moments, so they are acknowledged as substantial prior background, not ignored. A previously unlocated specialization in harmonic-frame, Boolean monomial-design or association-scheme literature remains a real residual priority risk; the searches do not prove global uniqueness.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The coordinate-choice issue is intrinsic to reconstructing functions from a constrained set of binary symmetric rank-one matrices. Although the canonical intrinsic degree-two Walsh system becomes asymptotically tight under omission of the origin, the natural ambient-matrix coordinate characters instead suffer a sharp quartic squared-conditioning penalty. The exact full spectrum, all-dimensional extremal-mode rigidity, and determinant give concrete, nontrivial finite-dimensional reconstruction data for a natural infinite family. These are not merely renamings of the classical Walsh orthogonality relation; the explicit unstable coordinate spectrum requires the incidence decomposition and strict extremal inequalities. The claim does not purport to prove optimality among arbitrary frequency bands or address general finite fields.
