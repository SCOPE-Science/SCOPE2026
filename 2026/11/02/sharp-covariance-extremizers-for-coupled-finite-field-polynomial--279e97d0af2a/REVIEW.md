# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The statement quantifies over every prime power \(q\ge2\), the unique uniform degree-less-than-\(q\) polynomial representative of each map, and every fixed perturbation. At each input, two target exclusions coincide exactly when the perturbation value equals their difference. Multiplying the one- or two-forbidden-value probabilities yields the exact covariance formula. Discrete strict convexity of the fiber contribution gives unique extremizers and their next-best gaps. The independent finite-field enumerator reproduced the claimed formulas for complete function grids over \(\mathbb F_2,\mathbb F_3,\mathbb F_4\) and selected \(\mathbb F_5\) profiles. These checks corroborate, but do not replace, the proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The 2012 work of Mullen–Wan–Wang concerns deterministic value-set bounds. Gao–Wang (2014 preprint; 2015 journal) evaluates the marginal size distribution of one random polynomial and considers unions of independently modeled random sets, not the covariance of two value-set sizes sharing the same random function under arbitrary deterministic pointwise shifts. Kyureghyan–Li–Pott (2021) studies geometric intersections for fixed cubics, not all-degree random pairs. Related occupancy covariance and deterministic polynomial-image papers inspected do not imply the strict Schur-convex fiber-profile classification or its two sharp gaps. An older correlated-occupancy identity may imply part of the formula, so no claim of absolute historical priority is made.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Joint range-size dependence is a natural next invariant after the known marginal law. The theorem gives a closed exact function of the perturbation's entire collision profile, a universal negative optimum at permutation maps and positive optimum at constants, and rigid next-best gaps. The positive/negative sign contrast and exact extremal rigidity give structural information that a marginal count or a single example does not supply.

## Limitations and risks

The random polynomial must be uniform over all polynomial functions, not restricted to degree much smaller than the field order. The full bivariate law is not determined. Overlap with unlocated general correlated-occupancy literature remains a priority risk. Independent mathematical audit remains outstanding.
