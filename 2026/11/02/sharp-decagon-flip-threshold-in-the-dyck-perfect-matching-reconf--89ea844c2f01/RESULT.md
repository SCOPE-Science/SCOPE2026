# Sharp decagon-flip threshold in the Dyck perfect-matching reconfiguration graph

## Finding

Let \(D\) denote the Dyck graph, the connected cubic graph on 32 vertices obtained by taking as vertices the 32 triangles of the Shrikhande graph and joining triangles that share an edge. A perfect matching selects 16 disjoint edges covering all 32 vertices. For an even integer \(L\), form the exchange graph \(\mathcal F_{\le L}(D)\): its vertices are the perfect matchings of \(D\), and distinct matchings are adjacent exactly when their symmetric difference is one simple alternating cycle of even length at most \(L\).

**Theorem.** The Dyck graph has exactly 417 perfect matchings. Its exact-length flip-graph edge counts at lengths 6, 8, 10 are, respectively, 864, 96, 2736. The length-six graph has 45 isolated vertices and three components of order 124; the length-eight graph has 372 isolated vertices and one component of order 45; the length-ten graph has 21 isolated vertices and one component of order 396. The union allowing lengths 6 and 8 has four components of orders \(45,124,124,124\). When length ten is also allowed, the exchange graph has 3696 edges, is connected, and has radius 4 and diameter 5. Precisely 360 perfect matchings have eccentricity 4 and 57 have eccentricity 5. In particular, the **minimum maximum allowed flip length for connectivity is ten**, and five allowed flips between arbitrary perfect matchings always suffice and sometimes are required.

The graph of flips using only lengths 8 and 10 is itself connected; hexagon flips are not individually necessary for connectivity.

## Assumptions and scope

Use the Shrikhande Cayley graph on \(\mathbb Z_4^2\) generated by \((\pm1,0),(0,\pm1),(1,1),(-1,-1)\). List its three-element cliques; these form exactly 32 triangles. For each pair of triangles, insert a Dyck edge if they share two Shrikhande vertices; this constructs a 32-vertex cubic graph with 48 edges. An equivalent standard Dyck representation is the LCF graph \([-13,5,-5,13]^8\). Both constructions are explicitly implemented and were also checked to be isomorphic.

A flip of length \(\ell\) means replacing the \(\ell/2\) matching edges along one alternating simple \(\ell\)-cycle by the other \(\ell/2\) edges. It does *not* allow toggling a disconnected union of several alternating cycles in one step. Here \(\mathcal F_{\le L}\) permits all even alternating cycle lengths up to and including \(L\). The graph has girth 6, so lengths below 6 give no flips. All displayed counts concern the full, labeled configuration spaces of this particular finite graph; no assertion is made for other cubic graphs or other values of the length cutoff.

## Proof

**Complete configuration enumeration.** Consider any perfect matching of the 32-vertex graph. The least-index uncovered vertex has exactly one partner in that matching, which must be one of its still-uncovered neighbors. Enumerate all possible partners recursively, then repeat on the least-index uncovered vertex. Every possible matching appears because its least-index vertex has a unique matching partner; no matching is duplicated because recursive decisions uniquely determine the selected edges. The exhaustive recursion gives 417 distinct perfect matchings. Each recursive leaf is checked to consist of 16 disjoint graph edges.

**Characterization of legal transitions.** For any two perfect matchings \(M,N\), each vertex lies on no edge or on exactly two edges of \(M\triangle N\), one from either matching. Consequently the symmetric difference is a vertex-disjoint union of even alternating cycles. Therefore \(M\) and \(N\) are related by exactly one simple \(\ell\)-cycle flip if and only if \(M\triangle N\) is connected and contains exactly \(\ell\) edges. The first implementation checks every unordered pair among the 417 matchings, applies precisely this characterization, and counts 864, 96, and 2736 edges at respective lengths 6, 8, and 10. It computes the asserted single-length component decompositions exhaustively.

**Threshold and metric.** Taking the union of the 6- and 8-flip adjacency lists yields four components with vertex counts \(45,124,124,124\). Since there are no 4-cycles, no smaller maximum cycle length can connect all 417 perfect matchings. Incorporating the 2736 length-ten edges gives one connected 417-vertex graph with 3696 edges. Breadth-first search from **each** of the 417 vertices computes every graph distance. The maximum distance from each source is either 4 (360 sources) or 5 (57 sources), so the radius is 4 and diameter is 5. The connectedness of the 8-and-10-only flip graph is checked separately.

**Independent enumeration.** A second implementation builds the same abstract Dyck graph from its standard 32-vertex LCF notation, enumerates matchings recursively, and enumerates all simple cycles of lengths 6, 8, and 10. For every such cycle and every matching, it checks that one alternating parity class belongs to the matching, toggles the cycle, and confirms the resulting matching exists among the complete configurations. This second cycle-first method exactly reproduces the counts, components, and all 417 eccentricities without using pairwise symmetric-difference detection. An additional exact graph-isomorphism check confirms that the two graph constructors describe the same object. These algorithms have finite loop bounds and the preceding completeness arguments; the claim does not rely on sampling or extrapolation.

## Relationship to prior work

Figueroa, Fresán-Figueroa, and Rivera-Campo, *On the perfect matching graph defined by a set of cycles* (2015), introduce and study perfect-matching graphs defined by allowed alternating cycles, and establish general connectivity conditions for several graph classes. The inspected manuscript develops the general exchange operation but does not tabulate the Dyck graph, its cutoff-8/10 component decompositions, or its sharp five-flip diameter. Existing Dyck graph references establish its classical structure, girth, LCF notation, and short-cycle geometry, not this exact exchange classification. The numerical perfect-matching count is used as a base enumeration, not asserted as historically unprecedented. Different finite matching-complex and perfect-matching results for other graphs do not imply the Dyck exchange graph's claimed component structure. No universal alternating-cycle connectivity theorem with unrestricted cycle lengths by itself implies the specific maximum allowed length ten or the diameter five.

## Limitations

The theorem is a finite exact classification of one canonical graph, not an asymptotic or general reconfiguration theorem. A matching's distance in the exchange graph counts whole allowed alternating-cycle flips, not single-edge substitutions. Although the enumeration is exhaustive, historical novelty remains conditional on accessible literature: equivalent results may appear in unindexed or inaccessible sources. The verification programs and proof provide no independent scholarly audit; publication priority is not certified.
