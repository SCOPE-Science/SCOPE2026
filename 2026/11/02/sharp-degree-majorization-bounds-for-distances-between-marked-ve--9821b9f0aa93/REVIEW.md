# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is a statement about uniform labeled trees conditioned on the *full assigned degree vector*, not just its histogram. The weighted spanning-tree polynomial \(\left(\prod_i x_i\right)\left(\sum_i x_i\right)^{n-2}\) and the rooted-forest Laplacian determinant give the probability of each specified marked path after coefficient extraction. Summing paths yields an exact telescoping law, including the maximal-length boundary. Every nontrivial integer balancing transfer changes \(e_k\) by exactly \((x-y-1)e_{k-2}\), so all upper tails are ordered, and \(e_2\) establishes strictness. The two degree-profile extremizers and their expectations follow by majorization and a finite binomial sum. The extracted exact verifier completed 1,991,963 assertions and exhaustively enumerated Prüfer words through eight vertices; the all-size result depends on the algebra, not those checks.

## Originality — PASS, with priority qualification

The underlying enumeration and matrix-tree facts are classical. Relevant published comparisons include Broutin–Marckert (2011, arXiv:1110.5203), who prove degree-profile asymptotics of plane trees and discuss labeled trees; Addario-Berry–Donderwinkel–Maazoun–Martin (2021, arXiv:2107.09726), who give root-to-random-leaf first-repeat laws and a splitting comparison for tree types; and Addario-Berry–Donderwinkel (2022, arXiv:2201.11773), whose main comparison concerns overall tree height. None of the inspected passages directly supplies the fully conditioned two-fixed-label survival law together with the all-threshold ordering under *all* unmarked balancing transfers and both sharp extremal degree profiles. published-finding corpus semantic searches for aliases, degree majorization, fixed-degree paths and Prüfer collision laws returned no identified dominating statement; this is not proof of global novelty. Meir–Moon's 1970 distance study and Dahl's 2007 majorization work were accessible only as summaries, leaving historical implicit-coverage risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Distance between two designated vertices is a canonical geometric observable of a uniform tree. A prescribed assigned degree sequence is a natural and demanding random-tree ensemble. The theorem gives an exact stochastic envelope simultaneously for every distance threshold, identifies its unique extremal full-degree patterns, and evaluates both extremal means. It compares nontrivial degree transfers between already-positive vertices that are not just leaf-splitting operations. These sharp distributions give useful finite-size benchmarks when only the two endpoint degrees are fixed.

## Scope and limitations

The comparison preserves both designated endpoint degrees. It does not compare majorization-incomparable profiles, condition only on an unordered multiset, or give a statement for nonuniform tree measures. Historical priority remains qualified by uninspected older sources; independent audit has not occurred.
