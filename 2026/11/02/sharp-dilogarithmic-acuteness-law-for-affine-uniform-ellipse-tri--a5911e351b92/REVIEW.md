# Review of the ellipse acute-triangle law

## Correctness

PASS. The three eccentric-anomaly parameters are independent and uniform; a uniform rotation makes the chord-direction midpoint uniform independently of the triangular difference. The Euclidean obtuse-angle sign equals that of \(\cos\Delta+\tau\cos(2m)\), and the exact triangular difference density gives the squared-arccosine average. The arcsine-square series follows from a second-order differential identity, and circular moment integration produces precisely \(\operatorname{Li}_2(\tau^2)/2\). Only one angle can be obtuse. Boundary values, strict monotonicity, logarithmic degeneration and the circular expansion follow from standard dilogarithm identities. Monte Carlo and independent quadrature corroborate the derivation but are not used as universal proof.

## Originality

PASS, with explicit priority risk. The strongest nearby source is the 2018 off-center point-containment law for three independent uniform points on a circle, with exactly the same dilogarithm profile; the function itself is expressly credited. The present event is *Euclidean acuteness of the affine image* of three uniform-circle points, a distinct condition and sample space. The numerical equality is established by a separate chord-direction argument; it does not follow simply by replacing the point-distance variable in the circle-containment theorem. Akopyan–Edelsbrunner–Nikitenko (2020) concern round-sphere acuteness and ellipsoidal intrinsic volumes; Helman–Garcia–Reznik (2020) concern deterministic ellipse-triangle centers. A 2025 ellipse-acute discussion uses Euclidean arclength, not the affine-uniform law. Searches did not locate a prior statement of the exact ellipse probability and its shape law, but this is not proof of historical uniqueness.

## Mathematical value

PASS. Acute-vs-obtuse is a canonical geometric-shape statistic. The affine-uniform measure is the natural pushforward of uniform circular sampling, which makes it well suited to testing shape sensitivity under anisotropy. The theorem gives the complete exact profile for *every* ellipse, a unique circular optimizer, an exact equality with a previously known but geometrically different circle-containment probability, and a nonanalytic logarithmic slender-shape rate. These are specific natural laws, not an arbitrary numerical slice or a textbook symmetry deduction.
