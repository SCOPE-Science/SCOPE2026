# Sharp edge growth and universal reciprocity in Zipf range-renewal graphs

## Finding

Let \(s>1\), \(\gamma=1/s\), and \(c=1/\zeta(s)\). Let \(X_1,X_2,\ldots\) be independent random variables with \(\Pr(X_t=i)=c i^{-s}\), for every integer \(i\ge1\). A distinct observed nonloop ordered adjacent pair is a directed arc; forgetting its orientation gives an undirected edge. Write \(D_n\) for the number of distinct directed arcs generated by \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\), \(U_n\) for the number of distinct undirected edges, \(M_n\) for edges whose two directions both occur, and \(S_n\) for edges with exactly one direction. Define

\[
K_s=\gamma\Gamma(1-\gamma)c^{2\gamma}>0.
\]

**Theorem.** As \(n\to\infty\), almost surely and in \(L^2\),

\[
\frac{(D_n,U_n,M_n,S_n)}{n^\gamma\log n}
\longrightarrow K_s\big(1,2^{\gamma-1},1-2^{\gamma-1},2^\gamma-1\big).
\]

Hence the fraction of *directed arcs* with a reverse arc tends almost surely to \(2-2^\gamma\), whereas the fraction of *undirected edges* with both orientations tends to \(2^{1-\gamma}-1\). These fractions are strictly between zero and one. The first limit measures reciprocal observed transitions, not the probability that two consecutive samples reverse direction.

## Assumptions and scope

Here \(n\ge2\) is the number of symbols, so there are \(n-1\) adjacent-pair opportunities. The alphabet is the positive integers; \(s>1\) is fixed and does not depend on \(n\). Loops \((i,i)\) are discarded from all four counts. Each edge is counted once irrespective of repeated appearances. Both directions of an unordered pair must actually appear for it to contribute to \(M_n\). The law is specifically normalized Zipf, rather than a general slowly varying sequence. No assertion is made for \(s\le1\), changing parameters, or nonindependent underlying symbols.

The elementary identities \(D_n=2M_n+S_n\) and \(U_n=M_n+S_n\) give \(M_n=D_n-U_n\) and \(S_n=2U_n-D_n\). Therefore it suffices to prove the limits for \(D_n\) and \(U_n\).

## Proof

**1. Uniform rare-transition approximation.** Write \(p_i=c i^{-s}\), \(q_{ij}=p_i p_j\), and \(m=n-1\). For \(i\ne j\), let \(W_{ij}\) count windows equal to the *ordered* pair \((i,j)\); similarly let \(W_{\{i,j\}}\) count windows equal to either \((i,j)\) or \((j,i)\). Their respective means are \(m q_{ij}\) and \(2m q_{ij}\). The window indicators are independent whenever their positions differ by at least two.

The local-dependence Poisson inequality of Arratia--Goldstein--Gordon bounds the error in the probability of zero occurrences by \((1\wedge\lambda^{-1})(b_1+b_2)\), where \(b_1\) sums products of the indicator means over pairs of dependent windows, and \(b_2\) sums their joint probabilities for distinct dependent windows. For the ordered event, two successive indicators cannot both be one because \(i\ne j\): therefore \(b_2=0\) and \(b_1\le3mq_{ij}^2\). For the unordered event, a successive double occurrence is either \((i,j,i)\) or \((j,i,j)\), with total probability \(q_{ij}(p_i+p_j)\); thus \(b_1\le12m q_{ij}^2\) and \(b_2\le2m q_{ij}(p_i+p_j)\). Since \(\lambda\) is the appropriate mean, a numerical constant \(C\) independent of all indices and \(n\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\Pr(W_{ij}=0)-e^{-mq_{ij}}\right|
&\le C\min(1,mq_{ij})(q_{ij}+p_i+p_j),\\
\left|\Pr(W_{\{i,j\}}=0)-e^{-2mq_{ij}}\right|
&\le C\min(1,mq_{ij})(q_{ij}+p_i+p_j).
\end{aligned}
\]

For any fixed integer \(B\ge1\), the sum of \(\min(1,mq_{ij})\) over pairs where \(i\le B\) or \(j\le B\) is \(O_B(n^\gamma)\): for each fixed \(i\), compare the sum over \(j\) with the integral of \(\min(1,np_i c x^{-s})\). On the complementary pairs, \(q_{ij}+p_i+p_j\le 2p_{B+1}+p_{B+1}^2\). The total sum of \(\min(1,mq_{ij})\) is \(O(n^\gamma\log n)\), as shown in Step 2 below. Summing the two probability errors and first taking \(n\to\infty\), then \(B\to\infty\), proves

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E D_n&=\sum_{i\ne j}(1-e^{-m p_i p_j})+o(n^\gamma\log n),\\
\mathbb E U_n&=\frac12\sum_{i\ne j}(1-e^{-2m p_i p_j})+o(n^\gamma\log n).
\end{aligned}
\]

The diagonal sums excluded here are \(O(n^{\gamma/2})\), since \(\sum_i\min(1,nc^2i^{-2s})=O(n^{\gamma/2})\).

**2. Weighted divisor asymptotic.** Define \(T=(mc^2)^\gamma\) and let \(d(k)\) count positive ordered factorizations of \(k\). The elementary Dirichlet hyperbola estimate

\[
\sum_{k\le x}d(k)=x\log x+O(x)
\]

implies, for each fixed \(a>0\),

\[
\sum_{i,j\ge1}\left(1-e^{-a m c^2(ij)^{-s}}\right)
=\sum_{k\ge1}d(k)h_a(k/T)
\sim T\log T\int_0^\infty h_a(u)\,du,
\]

where \(h_a(u)=1-e^{-a u^{-s}}\). To justify the passage from the divisor estimate to the infinite weighted sum, first truncate to \(\varepsilon T\le k\le RT\). Partial summation and \(\sum_{k\le x}d(k)/(x\log x)\to1\) give convergence of the normalized sum there to \(\int_\varepsilon^R h_a(u)du\). The lower tail is bounded by \(O(\varepsilon T\log(2T))\); the upper tail is bounded by \(O_a(T^s(RT)^{1-s}\log(2RT))\) using \(h_a(u)\le a u^{-s}\), partial summation and \(s>1\). On normalization by \(T\log T\), the limits are \(O(\varepsilon)\) and \(O_a(R^{1-s})\). This also establishes the summability estimate of Step 1 by comparing \(\min(1,a u^{-s})\) to a fixed multiple of \(h_a(u)\).

The change of variables \(t=a u^{-s}\), followed by integration by parts, gives

\[
\int_0^\infty (1-e^{-a u^{-s}})\,du=a^\gamma\Gamma(1-\gamma).
\]

Because \(T\log T\sim\gamma c^{2\gamma}n^\gamma\log n\), it follows that

\[
\mathbb E D_n\sim K_s n^\gamma\log n,
\qquad
\mathbb E U_n\sim2^{\gamma-1}K_s n^\gamma\log n.
\]

**3. Concentration and the strong law.** Let \(F_n\) be either \(D_n\) or \(U_n\), and let \(F_n^{(t)}\) be the same statistic after independently resampling only \(X_t\). Resampling affects at most two old and two new potential arcs; thus \(|F_n-F_n^{(t)}|\le4\). For all sufficiently large \(n\) there exist at least \(n/3\) pairwise disjoint adjacent windows at positions away from \(t\), and these windows are independent both of each other and of the four candidate changed pairs. If \(F_n\ne F_n^{(t)}\), at least one changed candidate pair is absent in the relevant version of the graph, hence absent from these remote windows. Each candidate has two independent endpoints distributed as \((p_i)\). Its probability of absence from the remote windows is bounded by \(\exp(-n p_i p_j/3)\), for either ordered or unordered edges. Therefore

\[
\Pr(F_n\ne F_n^{(t)})
\le 4\sum_{i,j\ge1}p_i p_j e^{-n p_i p_j/3}.
\]

The Efron--Stein inequality and the bounded-difference estimate imply

\[
\operatorname{Var}(F_n)
\le Cn\sum_{i,j}p_i p_j e^{-np_i p_j/3}
=O(n^\gamma\log n).
\]

For the last equality write \(n p_i p_j=(T_0/(ij))^s\), where \(T_0=(nc^2)^\gamma\), and use the same divisor-summation estimate with the integrable weight \(u^{-s}e^{-u^{-s}/3}\). Chebyshev then yields convergence in \(L^2\) of \(F_n/(n^\gamma\log n)\) to its expected leading constant.

For each fixed \(\delta>0\), along \(n_k=\lfloor(1+\delta)^k\rfloor\), the Chebyshev bounds are summable. By Borel--Cantelli, the same two limits hold almost surely along that subsequence. The counts \(D_n,U_n\) are nondecreasing in \(n\), and the normalizer \(n^\gamma\log n\) is regularly varying; sandwiching between consecutive geometric subsequence terms and letting \(\delta\downarrow0\) along a countable sequence proves almost-sure convergence for all \(n\). The linear identities in the scope section transfer both modes of convergence to \(M_n,S_n\). Finally, \(2M_n/D_n\to2-2^\gamma\) and \(M_n/U_n\to2^{1-\gamma}-1\), as asserted. \(\square\)

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `verify.py` (Python standard library only) calculates exact finite probabilities by an independent complete enumeration of short categorical words and independently via ordered-pattern and symmetric-pattern automata. It tests the identities \(D_n=2M_n+S_n\), \(U_n=M_n+S_n\), and finite exact pattern-avoidance probabilities. It also checks divisor summation identities and an elementary numerical integral identity. Run `python verify.py` from a directory containing that file. The script checks finite identities, **not** the infinite limit or bibliographic originality: the limiting statements follow from the proof above.

## Relationship to prior work

Xie and Zhou, *Universal Exponent-Two Degree Laws in Range-Renewal Networks* (arXiv:2609.05290, first public 2026-09-04), prove limiting degree-frequency tail and local laws with an iterated \(n\)-then-degree limit. Their full article's model and main theorem use the same adjacent-pair construction but do not give the \(n^\gamma\log n\) finite-size edge-count coefficients or the reciprocal-edge fraction. The original range-renewal work of Chen, Xie and Ying (arXiv:1305.1829) establishes other occupancy/degree-frequency limits. The finite distinct-pair counts in Archibald and collaborators (arXiv:1806.04962) use a geometric, rather than Zipf, alphabet. Janson's 2018 edge-exchangeable model samples independent endpoint pairs rather than overlapping consecutive windows; its rank-one edge-growth order is related, but does not by itself establish the dependence comparison and mutual-orientation law proved here. The Poisson approximation, divisor formula, and concentration inequalities are standard tools, not new claims.

## Limitations

The theorem is proved only for a fixed exactly normalized Zipf distribution with \(s>1\). The asymptotic does not give a finite-sample error rate, a central limit theorem, or a general slowly-varying extension. The independence of distant windows is essential to the displayed proof. Results in broader occupancy or exchangeable-edge theory might imply portions of the limit after a separate reduction; exhaustive historical priority is not certified.
