# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The first-move degree bound and endpoint-rigidity are exact. For a shorter deleted edge, every possible initial size-\(2k-1\) seed freezes after the first force, while a cyclic interval of size \(2k\) still succeeds. For a longest edge, each endpoint-centered seed of size \(2k-1\) succeeds, and all minima are exhausted. The proof handles \(k=1\). Exhaustive direct graph simulation confirms 80 cases and 341272 minimum-size candidates, but does not establish the infinite theorem alone.

**Originality — PASS, provisional.** Abiad et al. (arXiv:2610.03892v1, Proposition 8) establish only the original cycle-power scalar value. Edholm et al. (DOI:10.1016/j.laa.2010.10.015, Theorem 2.17) establish the general absolute-one edge-spread bound, not its cycle-distance phase or optimizer count. The two results do not imply the new statement. Searches of mathematical records and accessible earlier claims identified no covering result; an unindexed equivalent remains possible.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The exact sensitivity to an edge's geometric length is a natural robustness question for a dense cycle-power network. The strict long-chord versus short-chord transition and the complete classification of all optimal seeds provide an infinite-family structural result beyond generic spread bounds.

**Limits.** One deleted edge, \(n\ge2k+3\), ordinary zero forcing only. The formula does not address the dense boundary or multiple deletions. The work has no independent validation.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
