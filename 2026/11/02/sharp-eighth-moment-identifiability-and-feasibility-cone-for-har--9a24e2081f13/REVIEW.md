# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The stationary solution is a Gaussian variance mixture with a symmetric beta-distributed conditional variance. Its density follows from the stationary telegraph-input transport equations; beta integrals give the fourth/eighth moment ratios, an explicit inverse and strict necessity/sufficiency. The sixth-moment identity and order-seven obstruction follow from the same conditional-Gaussian expansion. All physical endpoint restrictions were checked: positive switching rate, strict diffusivity positivity/inequality, finite stationary moments and known trap rate. Independently derived stationary Markov-generator recursions exactly matched the beta representation in 144 rational experiments, each through order twelve. Exact finite tests supplement, not replace, the proof.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, provisional priority

The general forward moment recursion of Huang et al., arXiv:1412.7952, is acknowledged and logically contains computation of these low moments on specialization. What it does not explicitly establish in its examined statement and moment section is the constrained inverse uniqueness, optimal minimal degree, or complete attainable strict moment cone. Chubynsky–Slater (arXiv:1404.3573) and Miyaguchi–Akimoto–Yamamoto (arXiv:1605.00106) study diffusing diffusivity primarily outside harmonic confinement. Zhang–Wang (DOI 10.1080/03610918.2015.1132321) addresses stationary densities and transforms, but the full paper was not accessible; Wang–Wang (DOI 10.1016/j.physa.2023.129433) studies harmonic confinement with random diffusivity but the full 2024 paper could not be accessed. These are material priority risks, especially the latter two. Published mathematical-record comparisons turned up OU models and inverse problems on other models, not the particular sharp moment-cone theorem. No statement of universal novelty or independent certification is made.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The natural inverse problem arises when one can sample only the stationary particle position but cannot observe the switching environment or collect reliable trajectories. It is structurally informative that lower moments collapse to only one shape parameter, that the sixth adds exactly no identification information, and that eighth moment information resolves the rate–contrast degeneracy. The sharp realizable cone supplies a falsifiable model-consistency check and exact recovery formulas. This goes beyond a routine forward-moment calculation; it characterizes which empirical population moments the model can possibly generate and proves the optimal moment cutoff. Finite-sample estimation of the eighth moment remains potentially ill-conditioned and is not addressed.
