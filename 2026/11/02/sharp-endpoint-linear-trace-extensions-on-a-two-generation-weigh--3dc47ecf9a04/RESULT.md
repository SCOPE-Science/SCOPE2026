# Sharp endpoint linear trace extensions on a two-generation weighted binary tree

## Finding

For the real homogeneous edge-variation space on a full binary tree of height two, with root-edge weight \(\rho>0\) and leaf-edge weight \(1\), the exact optimal amplification factor of a **linear leaf-to-tree extension** is
\[
\Lambda(\rho)=\begin{cases}
1+\rho/2,&0<\rho\le1,\\
(4-\rho)/(\rho^2-3\rho+4),&1\le\rho\le4/3,\\
3/2,&\rho\ge4/3.
\end{cases}
\]
There is a unique global maximum at \(\rho_*=4-2\sqrt2\), where \(\Lambda(\rho_*)=(5+4\sqrt2)/7\). The sharp extension switches twice: at \(\rho=1\) it begins mixing information between sibling pairs, and at \(\rho=4/3\) its two internal children both become the average of all four leaf values.

## Assumptions and scope

The seven vertices are a root \(o\), its two children \(u,v\), and the four leaves \(x_1,x_2\) below \(u\), \(x_3,x_4\) below \(v\). For each real-valued vertex function \(F\) define
\[
 |F|_\rho=\rho\bigl(|F(u)-F(o)|+|F(v)-F(o)|\bigr)
 +\sum_{i=1}^2|F(x_i)-F(u)|+\sum_{i=3}^4|F(x_i)-F(v)|.
\]
For \(f:\{x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\}\to\mathbb R\), its trace seminorm is
\[
|f|_{\mathrm{tr},\rho}=\min\{|F|_\rho:F(x_i)=f(x_i)\ (1\le i\le4)\}.
\]
Minima exist because this is a finite-dimensional linear-programming problem modulo constants. Define \(\Lambda(\rho)\) as the infimum over all real-linear right inverses \(E\) of the trace of the ratio \(|Ef|_\rho/|f|_{\mathrm{tr},\rho}\), maximized over nonconstant \(f\). Finite-norm extensions necessarily preserve constants, and the homogeneous norms are taken modulo constants.

Write \(a=(f(x_1)+f(x_2))/2\), \(b=(f(x_3)+f(x_4))/2\), and \(\mu=(a+b)/2\). One explicit optimum, for every \(\rho>0\), has
\[
(E_\rho f)(o)=\mu,\qquad
(E_\rho f)(u)=\mu+\frac{t_\rho}{2}(a-b),\qquad
(E_\rho f)(v)=\mu-\frac{t_\rho}{2}(a-b),
\]
with leaf values left unchanged and
\[
t_\rho=\begin{cases}
1,&0<\rho<1,\\
(4-3\rho)/(\rho^2-3\rho+4),&1\le\rho\le4/3,\\
0,&\rho>4/3.
\end{cases}
\]
At \(\rho=1\), every \(t\in[1/2,1]\) is optimal, so the displayed choice \(t_1=1/2\) is not unique.

## Proof

**1. Norm reduction to weighted edge coordinates.** Orient all edges away from the root, in the order \(ou,ov,ux_1,ux_2,vx_3,vx_4\). The gradient map induces a linear isometry from vertex functions modulo constants onto \(\mathbb R^6\) with weighted \(\ell_1\) norm
\[
\|(z_1,\ldots,z_6)\|_{1,\rho}
=\rho(|z_1|+|z_2|)+\sum_{j=3}^6|z_j|.
\]
For a constant-preserving linear extension \(E\), let \(P\) be the matrix obtained by composing its trace with integration of an arbitrary edge gradient and then taking the extended gradient. The operator norm of \(P\) equals the extension amplification factor: for each leaf trace the denominator is precisely the minimum gradient norm among its preimages. Thus the global optimum is the minimum norm of the permitted projection matrices on weighted \(\ell_1^6\). The norm of a matrix in weighted \(\ell_1^6\) is the largest weighted column sum divided by the input-column weight.

**2. Symmetrization.** The group generated by interchanging the two branches and independently interchanging the two leaves in either branch preserves the weighted edge norm and the trace map. Averaging \(E\) over its conjugates by these tree isometries preserves the right-inverse property and does not increase its norm. An equivariant, constant-preserving extension must assign the grand leaf mean to the root and, for some real \(t\), the two values \(\mu\pm t(a-b)/2\) to the children. Indeed, the root's four leaf coefficients are equal and sum to one; each child coefficient is equal within either sibling pair, and branch exchange swaps the two pairs. This proves that optimizing over all linear extensions reduces exactly to one scalar parameter.

**3. Computing the norm.** For \(0\le t\le1\), direct integration of a unit gradient in either root edge gives the weighted column ratio
\[
A_\rho(t)=t+\frac{2(1-t)}\rho
=\frac{2+(\rho-2)t}{\rho}.
\]
A unit gradient in any of the four leaf edges gives
\[
B_\rho(t)=\frac{3+(\rho-1)t}{2}.
\]
All columns are equivalent to one of these two cases under the tree symmetries. Therefore
\[
\|P_t\|=\max\{A_\rho(t),B_\rho(t)\}.
\]
To justify restriction to \(0\le t\le1\), observe that a root-edge column has weighted magnitude \(\rho|t|+2|1-t|\); a leaf-edge column has weighted magnitude \(\rho|t|/2+\max\{1,|1-t|/2\}+|1-t|/2\). Both are nondecreasing in the two nonnegative variables \(|t|\) and \(|1-t|\). Replacing \(t<0\) by \(0\), or \(t>1\) by \(1\), reduces both variables and thus cannot increase the norm.

**4. Sharp minimax and phase changes.** For \(0<\rho\le1\), both affine branches are nonincreasing on \([0,1]\), and \(t=1\) gives \(\Lambda=1+\rho/2\). For \(1<\rho<4/3\), \(A_\rho\) strictly decreases and \(B_\rho\) strictly increases; their unique intersection occurs at \(t=(4-3\rho)/(\rho^2-3\rho+4)\), giving \(\Lambda=(4-\rho)/(\rho^2-3\rho+4)\). For \(\rho\ge4/3\), the value of \(B_\rho\) at zero is \(3/2\), dominates \(A_\rho(0)=2/\rho\), and \(B_\rho\) is strictly increasing, so \(t=0\) is optimal. All lower bounds follow from the exact column formula and symmetrization; the displayed \(E_\rho\) attains them.

Finally the middle branch has derivative
\[
\frac{\rho^2-8\rho+8}{(\rho^2-3\rho+4)^2},
\]
which changes sign exactly once in \((1,4/3)\), at \(4-2\sqrt2\). Direct substitution gives \((5+4\sqrt2)/7\), greater than the values at the two branch endpoints. The remaining branches are increasing then flat, proving global maximality and uniqueness.

## Verification

The included `verify_tv_h2.py` uses Python standard-library exact fractions to check all six gradient-coordinate columns against the closed column formulas and optimum at 2,200 rational cases; all tests pass. The optional `verify_tv_h2_lp.py` uses NumPy and SciPy's HiGHS linear-programming solver to optimize **unrestricted, nonsymmetric** internal vertex coefficients at ten parameter values; all match the theorem within solver tolerance. Reproduce using `python verify_tv_h2.py`, or install NumPy/SciPy and run `python verify_tv_h2_lp.py`. These finite diagnostics do not prove the formula; Steps 1--4 supply the proof for every positive \(\rho\).

## Relationship to prior work

Fefferman and Klartag's *Linear extension operators for Sobolev spaces on radially-symmetric binary trees* (arXiv:2301.13792, first posted January 31, 2023) defines exactly this radial binary-tree trace-extension framework, but its Theorem 1.1 requires \(1<p<\infty\), whereas the present claim treats the total-variation endpoint \(p=1\) and computes the sharp constant for the first nontrivial branching depth. The paper's final remarks explicitly separate endpoint extension questions from its proved results. This finding does **not** solve its unrestricted-depth endpoint question.

Björn, Björn, Gill, and Shanmugalingam study infinite-tree trace/extension theory in *Geometric analysis on Cantor sets and trees* (arXiv:1304.0566); their weighted infinite-tree Besov/Newtonian estimates and averaging extensions do not give the sharp finite seven-vertex norm. Kazaniecki and Wojciechowski analyze \(W^{1,1}\) trace right inverses for von Koch's snowflake (arXiv:1903.01100), a geometrically distinct trace space. More general results on projection constants may imply this small-dimensional case indirectly, which has not been exhaustively excluded.

## Limitations

The proof applies only to **real scalar**, **homogeneous**, **weighted \(\ell_1\) edge variation** on the specified height-two full binary tree, with common root-edge weight and common leaf-edge weight normalized to one. It does not classify arbitrary tree depth, nonradial weights, \(W^{1,p}\) for \(p\ne1\), or nonlinear extensions. The minimum over linear extensions is not the same as asserting that each fixed trace has this worst-case nonlinear extension cost. The finite diagnostic tests are not an independent proof or independent scientific audit. Equivalent prior finite-dimensional projection formulas may exist under different terminology.
