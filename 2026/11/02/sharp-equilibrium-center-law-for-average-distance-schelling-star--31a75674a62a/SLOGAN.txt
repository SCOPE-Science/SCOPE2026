For average-type-distance swap Schelling games on fully occupied stars, a strict hub–leaf inequality exactly identifies all equilibrium centers, including finite noninterval examples. When agent types become uniformly distributed, the fraction of equilibrium placements tends sharply to \(2-\sqrt3\), with a uniform limiting hub-type band and mean per-agent equilibrium cost \(1/4+(2-\sqrt3)^2/12\).
