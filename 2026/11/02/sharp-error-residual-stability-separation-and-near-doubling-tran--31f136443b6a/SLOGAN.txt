Uniform row-and-coordinate sampling on flat orthogonal-column linear systems has two distinct sharp stepsize ceilings: Euclidean solution-error contraction is controlled by the second spectral moment, while physical-residual monotonicity is controlled by the fourth. At the error-optimal step, a two-mode Hadamard system can exhibit an expected squared-residual transient arbitrarily close to twice its initial value, despite monotone expected squared solution error.
