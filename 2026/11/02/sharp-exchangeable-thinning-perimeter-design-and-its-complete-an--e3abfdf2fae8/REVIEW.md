# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The oriented-chord counting identity is exact for every retained cardinality and covers two-vertex degeneracy consistently. Jensen's strictly concave chord-length inequality and the positive nearest-neighbor weight give global uniqueness. Taylor expansion plus Parseval yields every nonzero Hessian eigenvalue with its normalization. The checked standalone enumeration reproduces both conditional subset probabilities and the local Fourier coefficients. Full rigor comes from the finite-sum argument, not numerical checks.

## Originality — PASS (historical priority risk)
Madras and KC (2006) cover regular input polygons and their expected uniform-subset perimeters. They do not state the angular optimization over irregular cocircular sets or the complete arbitrary-exchangeable-law Hessian. Morin (2024) studies interior sample points, and Reitzner–Schütt–Werner (2019) sample continuously from boundary faces. Comparisons with the accessible indexed research records found no equivalence to the full curvature-spectrum claim. Classical concavity of chord sums could already imply the global optimizer, so only the combined law and explicitly resolved angular stability are the proposed contribution; earlier diagonal-chord second variations remain a priority risk.

## Value — PASS
Vertex failures in cyclic sensor and polygon designs naturally call for guarantees uniform over a deletion mechanism whose retained cardinality may vary. The theorem provides exact optimization for all exchangeable mechanisms at once, not a special sample size, and the full Fourier spectrum quantifies which geometric perturbations degrade expectation and by how much. This yields a natural complete local stability classification instead of an isolated numerical gain.

## Limits
The Fourier statement is infinitesimal, not a global optimal modulus. Neither the full perimeter distribution nor nonexchangeable deletion is ordered. No independent validation has taken place.
