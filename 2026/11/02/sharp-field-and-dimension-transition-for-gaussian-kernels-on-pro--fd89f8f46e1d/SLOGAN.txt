For point–hyperplane incidence graphs of \(\operatorname{PG}(d,q)\), universal Gaussian distance-power positivity is controlled by a transversal incidence mode for every \(q\ge3\), but over \(\mathbb F_2\) it switches permanently at \(d=5\) to a one-dimensional point-versus-hyperplane contrast. The sharp binary exponent then decays as \(1/(2^d\log(4/3))\), rather than at the transversal scale \(2^{-d/2}\), and the endpoint Euclidean embedding dimension jumps from \(v\) to \(2v-2\).
