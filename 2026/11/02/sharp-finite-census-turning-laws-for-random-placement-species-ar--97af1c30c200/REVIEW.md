# Scientific assessment of the revised finding

## Correctness

After factoring the fixed-census richness polynomial at zero area, the derivative numerator for the local exponent in the singleton/common family has exactly one sign change for every common-species abundance at least three. The opposite endpoint signs put its only positive zero inside the area range. The change from the unsampled fraction to sampled area reverses the derivative sign. All communities with census at most three are handled by the single-species, all-singleton, or \((1,2)\) cases, and \((1,3)\) proves sharpness. The supplied finite checks corroborate but do not replace these arguments.

## Relationship to prior work

The random-placement richness model and its most uneven abundance curve are given by He and Legendre (2002), Eq. (1) and Table 1. The singleton/doubleton endpoint formula is a direct differentiation of the classical model and is included only for context. The narrower finding is the exact all-integer global classification for the singleton/common family and the sharp minimum census. Qualitative shape changes and the related Poisson turning results are not part of the originality claim. Incomplete historical access, notably to Coleman (1981), leaves priority risk.

## Scientific value and scope

The classification gives an exact condition for a reversal in a natural scale-dependent quantity and settles the smallest population size that can realize it. It concerns expected richness under independent uniform placement, not empirical detectability or turning counts for all larger abundance vectors.
