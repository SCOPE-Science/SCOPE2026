# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The online policy is fully specified and irrevocable. Before the first shared high occurrence, at most one still-unsatisfied agent is occupied; hence a shared high good always has an empty high-valuing eligible recipient. Frozen and forced-fill phases ensure every high value assigned away from its owner belongs to a singleton, and the terminal cardinality invariant makes every bundle nonempty. EFX then follows from a unit good in every own bundle and an upper bound of \(m-3\) low goods after a deletion in any other bundle. The matching adversary is exhaustively divided by recipient of the penultimate and final goods; each branch bounds the factor by \((m-2)/H\), \(1/H\), or \(1/(m-3)\). The separated value range makes the last bound sharp. Finite independent rational calculations check, but do not replace, the all-horizon proof.

## Originality — PASS (self-review)

The most directly related full primary papers were examined at their definitions, statements and pertinent proofs. Neoh–Teh, arXiv:2610.34625v1, Theorem 4.2 and Appendix C prove impossibility of a horizon-independent positive EFX factor on a matching two-value one-spike domain, but do not construct a fixed-horizon matching guarantee or state \(1/(m-3)\) for every horizon. Neoh–Peters–Teh, arXiv:2505.24503v3, Theorem 5.5 and Appendix C likewise prove a general no-uniform-positive-factor statement; that is weaker than this exact finite-horizon minimax conclusion. The related stored five-good one-spike value is a different fixed size and cannot imply all \(m\ge6\). Conceptual similarity to the terminal adversarial construction is explicit; the new substance is the all-horizon matching policy and exact frontier. Search limitations mean equivalent unindexed results cannot be ruled out; independent priority confirmation remains outstanding.

## Value — PASS

The primary work says that no constant EFX approximation survives as the number of goods grows, without quantifying the sharp decay at a fixed planning horizon. Exact-horizon guarantees determine what fairness a resource-allocation planner can still ensure with a prescribed number of arrivals. The result supplies a precise reciprocal rate, a simple constructive implementation, and a matching adaptive lower-bound mechanism over an infinite family, not a one-off numeric example or a routine restatement of impossibility.

## Limits and closest literature

The exact equality requires three agents, one high value per agent, and the sufficient high/low separation. The threshold may not be tight. Related no-uniform-factor theorems and the known five-good special case have different quantifiers. Identifying or excluding future equivalent results requires independent review.
