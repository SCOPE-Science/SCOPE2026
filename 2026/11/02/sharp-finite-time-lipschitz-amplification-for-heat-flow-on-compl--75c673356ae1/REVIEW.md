# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For every positive pair of part sizes and every nonnegative time, the combinatorial Laplacian splits into two within-part mean-zero eigenspaces and the interpart mean-difference eigenspace. Their eigenvalues give the exact edge gradient. The dual graph-Lipschitz constraints give a sharp two-case upper bound, with explicit attaining inputs. Comparing the two coefficients and differentiating the resulting two-exponential formula proves the global parameter/time conclusions, including the unique peak and universal limiting factor two. Exact-rational spectral calculations independently check 50 small-size/time cases; independent numerical matrix-exponential linear programs test 245 more instances. Finite checks do not replace the all-size proof.

## Originality — PASS, with qualification

The sharp all-time graph-Lipschitz norm and its optimal transient amplification are not stated in the inspected primary sources. Ollivier provides the transport-curvature framework; Veysseire provides general continuous-time curvature contraction; Lin–Lu–Yau use a degree-normalized Laplacian; Cheng–Li–Wu study normalized bicliques with modified-metric exponential coupling bounds. An existing exact edge-total-variation criterion for complete multipartite heat flow already contains the same size-imbalance contractivity threshold; that threshold is treated as prior, and the present originality claim is restricted to the full exact time profile and sharp peak/global amplification. Search and full-text inspection do not prove universal uniqueness. An unindexed formula, particularly in work on finite-time transport of bicliques, remains a residual priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Graph-Lipschitz norms measure transport and worst-case gradient stability under heat evolution. A mere curvature sign or contraction threshold cannot tell the size of the temporary amplification, when it occurs, or the optimal extremizing input. A natural two-parameter graph family has an exact all-time formula, a genuine size-imbalance bifurcation, an explicit unique peak, and a dimension-independent sharp factor of two. These quantitative conclusions are potentially useful as benchmarks for transport coupling and graph heat regularization; no larger theorem is claimed.

## Limitations and closest literature

The graph Laplacian is unnormalized and all edges have unit rate. Cheng–Li–Wu's complete-bipartite example concerns a degree-normalized generator and a modified metric, and is therefore a close but not exact covering result. The already-known multipartite edge-total-variation criterion covers the zero-vs-positive amplification threshold but not the numerical time profile. Earlier equivalent computations may exist; independent audit and publication-priority confirmation remain outstanding.
