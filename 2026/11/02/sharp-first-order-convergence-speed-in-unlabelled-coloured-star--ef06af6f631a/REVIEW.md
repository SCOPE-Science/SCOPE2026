# Review: sharp first-order convergence speed in coloured star forests

## Correctness

PASS within the stated scope. Star isomorphism types are specified by root colour and weak compositions of the leaf count, yielding the exact Euler product. The deletion bijection proves the finite-event identity, and the Boltzmann geometric variables give a coefficient representation. The saddle-point mean and variance, central Fourier Gaussian approximation, and off-central exponential bounds establish the sharp fixed-shift ratio at precision \(o(n^{-1/(q+1)})\), not merely a qualitative ratio limit. The rank-\(r\) truncated star profiles supply a finite family of canonical witnesses and an explicit \(r\)-round EF strategy. The rank-dependent finite union bound transfers the coefficient asymptotic to all first-order sentences. A colour-specific singleton witnesses optimality. The finite checker independently confirmed Euler products, deletion identities and scaling diagnostics. Independent verification remains outstanding.

## Originality

PASS for the *quantitative sharp-speed formulation* under the inspected evidence, not for the qualitative zero–one law. Bell (2002), using Compton's earlier theorem, already gives a monadic second-order zero–one law when the connected-component count is polynomial; this directly covers the zero–one assertion here. Burris et al. (1997), Theorem 2.1, also establish a qualitative first-order result through front-loaded counting; the actual PDF pages covering Theorem 2.1 and its asymptotic section were read. Bell and Burris (2003) provide general ratio-test results. Their inspected statements do not give the explicit optimal \(n^{-1/(q+1)}\) all-sentence rate or the specified leading constant for the coloured star family. Koponen's 2026 paper supplies the bounded-height DAG motivation, not an inspected sharp rate. Residual risk: a quantitative generalized-partition theorem may imply the result without using the vocabulary of finite-model logic; Bell (2002) and Koponen (2026) were not both available in complete full-text form for this comparison.

## Value

PASS. The rate \(n^{-1/(q+1)}\) connects the logical convergence of a natural class of one-parent finite hierarchies to the polynomial growth degree of their component-type inventory. The explicit fixed-sentence upper bound and matching isolated-vertex lower bound are a complete and sharp quantitative limit law, stronger than the known qualitative zero–one statement. The constant \((q^2\zeta(q+1))^{1/(q+1)}\) specifies the first-order effect of adding named colours. The claim is for fixed ranks and isomorphism-class sampling and does not promise a result for arbitrary finite DAGs.

## Closest literature and limitations

Bell (2002), DOI 10.1090/S0002-9947-01-02884-7, subsumes the existence of a zero–one limit. Burris et al. (1997), DOI 10.4153/CJM-1997-030-6, give an earlier qualitative criterion. Bell–Burris (2003), DOI 10.1090/S0002-9947-03-03299-9, develops component-ratio transfer methods. Koponen (2026), arXiv:2610.16163, motivates the bounded-height DAG family. No independent comprehensive literature-priority certificate is claimed, and the fixed-shift proof does not apply to growing shifts.
