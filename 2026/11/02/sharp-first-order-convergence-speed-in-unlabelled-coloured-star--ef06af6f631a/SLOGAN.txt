Uniformly unlabelled directed star forests with \(q\) named vertex colours satisfy every fixed first-order sentence with probability within \(O(n^{-1/(q+1)})\) of zero or one, and this is the optimal universal convergence exponent. The probability of finding an isolated vertex of a given colour is \(1-(q^2\zeta(q+1))^{1/(q+1)}n^{-1/(q+1)}+o(n^{-1/(q+1)})\). The qualitative zero–one law was already known from general results; the claim is its sharp quantitative refinement.
