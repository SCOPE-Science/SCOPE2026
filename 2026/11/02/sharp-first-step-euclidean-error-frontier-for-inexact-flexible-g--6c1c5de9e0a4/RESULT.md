# Sharp first-step Euclidean error frontier for inexact flexible GMRES

## Finding

For the first outer step of flexible GMRES (FGMRES) on a real symmetric positive-definite (SPD) system, the conventional inner residual tolerance admits an **exact**, dimension-independent worst-case solution-error amplification profile. Fix \(\kappa\ge1\) and \(0\le\mu<1\). Define
\[
\delta=\kappa-\kappa^{-1},\qquad
G_\kappa(\mu)=g_\kappa\bigl(\min\{\mu,t_*\}\bigr),
\]
where
\[
g_\kappa(t)=\frac t2\left(\sqrt{4+\delta^2(1-t^2)}+\delta\sqrt{1-t^2}\right),\qquad
 t_*=\sqrt{\frac{4+\delta^2}{4+2\delta^2}}.
\]
The supremum of \(\|x_*-x_1\|_2/\|x_*-x_0\|_2\), over all real SPD systems with spectral condition number at most \(\kappa\), all nonzero initial residuals, and every first preconditioning action satisfying \(\|v_1-Az_1\|_2\le\mu\), equals \(G_\kappa(\mu)\). It is attained in two dimensions (and is also attained in the isotropic case \(\kappa=1\)). In particular, for \(\kappa>1\),
\[
\boxed{\quad \|x_*-x_1\|_2\le \|x_*-x_0\|_2\ \text{for all admissible first steps}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mu\le\frac1{\sqrt{1+(\kappa-\kappa^{-1})^2}}.\quad}
\]
For \(\kappa=1\), the exact factor is \(G_1(\mu)=\mu\). For \(\kappa>1\) and \(\mu\ge t_*\), the factor saturates at
\[
\boxed{G_\kappa(\mu)=\frac{\kappa+\kappa^{-1}}2.}
\]
Thus a strictly contracting first residual can coexist with increasing Euclidean solution error. For example, at \(\kappa=10\), \(\mu=1/5\), the exact worst amplification is \(1.9603998767\ldots\), despite a residual contraction factor of \(1/5\).

## Assumptions and scope

Let \(n\ge2\), let \(A\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}\) be SPD, and let \(b\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(x_0\in\mathbb R^n\) satisfy \(r_0=b-Ax_0\ne0\). Write \(x_*=A^{-1}b\), \(v=r_0/\|r_0\|_2\). In exact arithmetic choose any \(z\in\mathbb R^n\) satisfying \(\|v-Az\|_2\le\mu<1\), set \(w=Az\), and perform the first FGMRES least-squares step
\[
\alpha=\frac{v^{\mathsf T}w}{w^{\mathsf T}w},\qquad
x_1=x_0+\|r_0\|_2\alpha z.
\]
The norm condition guarantees \(w\ne0\) and \(v^{\mathsf T}w>0\). The theorem imposes **only** the inner residual criterion; in particular, it does not assume that \(z\) results from a prescribed inner Krylov iteration, a symmetric preconditioner, or a fixed preconditioner on later steps. Every nonzero action constructed below can be represented as the action of some invertible linear preconditioner on the given \(v\). The condition number is the ratio of extreme eigenvalues of \(A\). No finite-precision or later-iterate guarantee is claimed.

## Proof

**1. Reduction to a constrained pair of unit vectors.** Normalize \(\|r_0\|_2=1\) (homogeneity). The least-squares residual is \(q=v-\alpha w\), orthogonal to \(w\), and satisfies
\[
\|q\|_2=\min_{\beta\in\mathbb R}\|v-\beta w\|_2
\le\|v-w\|_2\le\mu.
\]
If \(q=0\) the exact solution is recovered. Otherwise set \(t=\|q\|_2\), \(u=q/t\). Since \(q\perp(v-q)\),
\[
v^{\mathsf T}u=t,\qquad 0<t\le\mu<1.
\]
With \(B=A^{-1}\), the initial and new solution errors are \(Bv\) and \(tBu\), so their norm ratio is \(t\|Bu\|_2/\|Bv\|_2\).

Conversely, **every** pair of unit vectors \(u,v\) with \(v^{\mathsf T}u=t\in(0,1)\) is realizable by taking \(w=v-tu\), \(z=Bw\). Indeed, \(\|v-w\|_2=t\), \(v^{\mathsf T}w=w^{\mathsf T}w=1-t^2\), hence \(\alpha=1\) and \(q=tu\). Taking \(b=v\), \(x_0=0\) supplies a corresponding linear-system instance. Thus no relaxation of the feasible first FGMRES geometry was introduced.

**2. Sharp SPD angular distortion.** Fix \(t\in(0,1)\) and write \(s=\sqrt{1-t^2}\). On the two-dimensional plane generated by \(u,v\), compress \(B^2\) to a symmetric positive-definite matrix \(C\). Its square root has a spectral ratio \(\gamma\le\kappa\) by the Rayleigh bounds for \(B^2\). In an eigenbasis of \(C\), the unit vectors are related by a planar rotation through angle \(\pm\arccos(t)\). Consequently
\[
\frac{\|Bu\|_2}{\|Bv\|_2}
\le \left\|\begin{pmatrix}\gamma&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}t&-s\\s&t\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}\gamma^{-1}&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\right\|_2.
\]
The matrix on the right has determinant one and squared Frobenius norm \(2+(\gamma-\gamma^{-1})^2s^2\). If its largest singular value is \(L\), then \(L-L^{-1}=(\gamma-\gamma^{-1})s\); therefore
\[
L=\frac{\sqrt{4+(\gamma-\gamma^{-1})^2s^2}+(\gamma-\gamma^{-1})s}{2}
\le\frac{\sqrt{4+\delta^2s^2}+\delta s}{2}.
\]
Equality is possible with \(B=\operatorname{diag}(\kappa,1)\) and a suitable choice of \(v\) in two dimensions. This proves the **exact fixed-residual-factor** envelope \(g_\kappa(t)\).

An explicit equality construction is as follows. Let \(R_t=\begin{pmatrix}t&-s\\s&t\end{pmatrix}\), \(B=\operatorname{diag}(\kappa,1)\), and choose a unit top right singular vector \(y\) of \(BR_tB^{-1}\). Put \(v=B^{-1}y/\|B^{-1}y\|_2\), \(u=R_tv\), \(A=B^{-1}\), \(w=v-tu\), \(z=Bw\), \(b=v\), and \(x_0=0\). All first-step identities hold exactly, and \(\|Bu\|_2/\|Bv\|_2=\|BR_tB^{-1}\|_2\). This construction is valid for \(\kappa=1\) as well.

**3. Optimization over the permitted inner tolerance.** For \(\delta>0\), write \(p=\delta/2\), \(s=\sqrt{1-t^2}\). Then
\[
g_\kappa(t)=\sqrt{1-s^2}\left(\sqrt{1+p^2s^2}+ps\right),
\quad
\frac{d}{ds}\log g_\kappa
=-\frac{s}{1-s^2}+\frac{p}{\sqrt{1+p^2s^2}}.
\]
This derivative vanishes exactly when \(s^2=p^2/(1+2p^2)\), giving the unique maximum at \(t=t_*\) with value \(\sqrt{1+p^2}=(\kappa+\kappa^{-1})/2\). Thus maximizing over \(0\le t\le\mu\) gives the stated clipped profile. If \(\delta=0\), then \(g_1(t)=t\).

Finally, for \(\kappa>1\) and \(0<t<1\), solving \(g_\kappa(t)=1\) by isolating and squaring the positive square root is equivalent to \(\delta t=\sqrt{1-t^2}\). Hence its unique interior crossing is \(t=(1+\delta^2)^{-1/2}\); the profile is below one before that crossing and above it thereafter (until the limit at \(t=1\)). The two-dimensional equality construction proves both the universal nonexpansion threshold and strict amplification immediately beyond it.

## Verification

The accompanying `verify.py` is a standard-library-only deterministic test. Running `python3 verify.py` verifies exact-rational first-step identities for 99 cases and checks 27,461 numerical cases including explicit two-mode equality witnesses, critical thresholds, stationary-maximizer identities, and a grid survey against the analytically optimized envelope. A successful run prints `VERIFY_OK`. The general theorem rests on the proof above; finite tests do not prove the universal quantifiers.

## Relationship to prior work

Güttel and Nyman's April 30, 2026 preprint, *Flexible GMRES converges in two phases*, imposes the same bound \(\|v_j-Az_j\|_2\le\mu\) and proves sharp **outer residual** estimates, including the first-step contraction \(\|r_1\|_2/\|r_0\|_2\le\mu\). The first-step **Euclidean solution-error minimax envelope**, its exact condition-number-dependent safe tolerance, and the saturating two-mode witnesses are not stated in its inspected mathematical results. Weiss (1994) describes the classical distinction between residual and solution error, but the accessible abstract does not state the parameterized FGMRES formula. Bauer and Householder (1960) provide classical matrix condition-number inequalities related to the angular-distortion step; this element is not claimed as a new standalone principle. An earlier published sharp error result for **unpreconditioned symmetric GMRES(1)** concerns a different algorithmic action (the current residual itself), and in particular does not impose the variable inner-action budget used here. The contribution is the exact composition of constrained FGMRES projection geometry with sharp SPD distortion and the resulting complete tolerance frontier.

## Limitations

The statement is limited to the **first outer FGMRES step** in exact real arithmetic, SPD matrices, and unrestricted approximate preconditioner actions meeting a norm bound. It does not assert that a particular inner algorithm realizes the worst-case action; nor does it claim multistep amplification, guarantees for indefinite/nonsymmetric matrices, or finite-precision robustness. The singular-vector equality construction is existential and can require an adversarial preconditioner. Classical condition-number geometry may admit older equivalent formulations of parts of the argument, and historical priority beyond the inspected sources remains uncertain. This is a self-review, not independent validation.
