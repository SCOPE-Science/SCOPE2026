In a uniformly sampled labeled forest with \(k\ge2\) prescribed roots and \(m\ge2\) nonroots, conditioning on exactly one versus two root-to-child edges increases the total number of undirected leaves in stochastic order **if and only if** \(m\le2k\). For \(m>2k\), stochastic ordering fails despite a strictly larger conditional leaf mean, and a second saturation obstruction occurs between \(k\) and \(k+1\) root-to-child edges when \(k\le m-2\).
