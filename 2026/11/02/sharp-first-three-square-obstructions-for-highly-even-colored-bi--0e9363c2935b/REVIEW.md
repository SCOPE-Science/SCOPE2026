# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The integer coefficient recurrence follows by logarithmic differentiation of the defining product; the rational coefficient-polynomial recurrence is the same identity in the formal parameter. Exact rational arithmetic through coefficient 14 gives the displayed, individually checkable valuation-margin certificate; the bound extends to every odd multiplier and every valuation at least six. Legendre's three-square theorem reduces the entire first-index assertion to eight residue classes and fourteen initial indices. Exact integer recurrence exhaustively establishes the five small power-of-two exceptions. Computations on forty representative high-valuation colors are cross-checks only, not the infinite proof.

## Originality

**PASS under inspected published and indexed evidence.** Sobolewski–Ulas (arXiv:2211.16622), Sections 2–7, studies \(2^k-1\) colors, and Section 7, Problem 7.2, asks for general colors; its fixed-modulus automaticity proposition does not establish our uniform first-obstruction values. Gawron–Miska–Ulas (arXiv:1703.01955), Sections 1–4, supplies background identities and valuations, without the classified three-square first indices. Residual risk: an unindexed result or an implicit extraction from existing general congruence theory could cover a subcase.

## Value

**PASS.** The named first-obstruction invariant is natural for the specific open three-square representation problem. This theorem completely classifies the first failure simultaneously across infinitely many color counts, yields the sharp universal cutoff 14 and sharp valuation threshold six throughout the highly-even regime, and resolves all powers-of-two counts, including their exceptional low-valuation behavior. The two-adic coefficient-polynomial mechanism explains the eight residue classes. It is not a full solution of the general index-set problem and does not establish a density theorem.
