# Sharp five-alternative acceptant-expansion guarantee for path-independent choice

## Finding

Let \(U\) be a nonempty finite set with \(|U|\le5\). A choice rule is a map \(C:2^U\to2^U\) with \(C(A)\subseteq A\), \(C(\varnothing)=\varnothing\), and \(C(A)\ne\varnothing\) whenever \(A\ne\varnothing\). Path independence means
\[
C(A\cup B)=C(C(A)\cup B)\quad\text{for all }A,B\subseteq U.
\]
A \(q\)-acceptant rule selects exactly \(\min\{q,|A|\}\) alternatives from every menu \(A\). An expansion \(T\) of \(C\) satisfies \(C(A)\subseteq T(A)\) for every menu.

**Theorem.** For all integers \(q\ge1\), every such path-independent rule with \(\max_A|C(A)|\le q\) has a path-independent \(q\)-acceptant expansion. For five labeled alternatives, the complete counts are:

| Capacity \(q\) | Path-independent source rules with \(\max |C|\le q\) | Path-independent \(q\)-acceptant target rules | Unexpandable sources |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 120 | 120 | 0 |
| 2 | 13,710 | 480 | 0 |
| 3 | 56,140 | 80 | 0 |
| 4 | 59,385 | 5 | 0 |
| 5 | 59,386 | 1 | 0 |

For \(q=3\), the exact distribution of the *number* of available expansions, across those \(56,140\) sources, is
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrrrr}
\text{expansions}&1&2&4&6&8&10&12&16&24&36\\
\text{sources}&3340&7320&9540&11160&3780&7020&8460&3240&1840&440
\end{array}.
\]
Of these sources, precisely \(39,860\) violate the law of aggregate demand (monotonicity of chosen-set cardinality). Hence the result is not simply an application of the previously known law-of-aggregate-demand sufficient condition.

Chambers and Yenmez (2026, Example 1) provide a nonempty-valued path-independent six-alternative rule of capacity \(4\) with no acceptant path-independent expansion. Consequently \(6\) is the **sharp minimum number of alternatives permitting failure**, across all capacities. Their six-alternative example is previously published, not an original component of this theorem.

## Assumptions and scope

The ground set is *labeled* and of size at most five; all subsets, including the empty subset, are included in the choice domain. Nonempty-valuedness is essential to the enumerative exhaustiveness as formulated. No assumption of the law of aggregate demand is made. Capacities greater than the number of alternatives are immediate: the identity rule \(T(A)=A\) is an acceptant expansion. The finite-size theorem does not settle whether every path-independent rule with capacity \(q\le3\) has an acceptant expansion for arbitrary ground-set size.

## Proof

The Aizerman–Malishevski maximizer-collecting representation states that every nonempty-valued path-independent choice rule on a finite ground set is the pointwise union of the maximum-choice rules generated by some family of strict linear orders. Conversely, a union of such priority-maximum rules is path independent. To see the converse directly, for each order the highest-ranked member of \(A\) remains highest-ranked in \(C(A)\), because every other selected member also lies in \(A\). Thus both sides of the path-independence identity have the same maximum under every generating order.

This reduces the theorem for each ground-set size \(n\le5\) to an exact finite computation. Enumerate all \(n!\) strict linear orders, form their maximum-choice table on every menu, then close the set of tables under pointwise union. A table can be represented without collisions by concatenating \(n\)-bit masks, one mask for each of the \(2^n\) menus. The closure explores every subset of the generating orders: when the next order is processed, retain each current table and its union with that order's table. Start from the empty union, and delete it when all orders are processed. The cited representation proves **exhaustiveness** of the resulting distinct nonempty-valued path-independent rules; the computation independently checks path independence of every generated table by all \(2^{2n}\) menu pairs.

For each capacity \(q\), retain exactly those rules for which the cardinality of every choice is at most \(q\), and among them mark the \(q\)-acceptant rules. A bitwise inclusion test \(C(A)\subseteq T(A)\) is performed for every source and every acceptant target, on every menu. The resulting zero-uncovered counts for all \(1\le n\le5\) and \(1\le q\le n\) prove existence in the claimed domain. Because this is a finite exhaustive proof, no sampling or probabilistic inference is used.

For \(n=5,q=3\), the checker independently counts the number of acceptable targets of each source to obtain the complete distribution displayed above, and checks each source for a pair of nested menus witnessing failure of the law of aggregate demand. The case \(q>n\) follows from the identity expansion as noted above. Sharpness at six is supplied by the **already published** counterexample, not by a new general no-go argument.

## Relationship to prior work

Chambers and Yenmez, *Acceptant Expansions of Path-Independent Choice Rules* (arXiv:2608.07022v1, publicly posted 2026-08-07), Section 3 and Example 1 provide the six-alternative counterexample; Sections 5–6 give a law-of-aggregate-demand sufficient condition and a general necessary-and-sufficient monotone-rejection characterization. Neither those sections nor the conclusions and appendices inspected state this exhaustive five-alternative guarantee or its exact extension census. Their Section 8 leaves the general capacity-at-most-three expansion problem open. The Aizerman–Malishevski order representation is classical, and is used here for exhaustive generation, not claimed as new.

The contribution is the sharp finite lower boundary and exact capacity-three census, not the known six-item obstruction, the classical priority representation, or a general characterization of acceptant expansion.

## Limitations

No claim is made about all market sizes at capacity \(2\) or \(3\). The result requires nonempty-valued path-independent rules. It does not guarantee preservation of extrinsic institutional feasibility constraints by an expansion. A computation-based finite proof requires the supplied checker and a correct implementation of the published maximizer-collecting representation. Although highly relevant primary literature was inspected in full-text HTML, earlier unindexed computational enumerations remain a potential priority risk. No independent scientific audit has been performed.
