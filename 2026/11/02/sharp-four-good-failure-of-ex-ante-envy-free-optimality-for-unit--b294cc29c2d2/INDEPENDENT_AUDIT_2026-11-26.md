## correctness

PASS

For bundle totals U,V from a single representative agent's independent draws, the joint success probability is P(U≥V)P(V≥U), using different agents in the factors. The general lower bound P(U≥V)≥P(V=0) and its reverse show that an empty bundle never beats a split. At zero total all choices are perfect; for one good the two allocations merely swap agents; for two goods the only nonempty splits are swaps of one another. For a singleton with probability a against b,c, enumerating the eight one-agent realizations gives factors ((1−b)(1−c)+a(b+c−2bc)) and (1−a(1−b)(1−c)). Symbolic expansion from those outcomes gives the stated exchange difference c(a−b)[2−c−(1−c)(a+b−2ab)]; the bracket is at least 1, so a largest-probability singleton always maximizes. For an ex-ante balanced split its probability equals the sum on the other side, hence is largest; this includes endpoint probabilities. ## originality

PASS

The surviving distinction is the full heterogeneous unit-Bernoulli statement: a universal small-item theorem including the maximum-singleton rule, and strict failure already at four goods with interior success probabilities. Same-objective earlier theory either assumes powers of a common distribution or reaches a three-good counterexample with nonunit utilities. A same-day Resultary match has the very close four-good boundary but changes utility magnitudes rather than probabilities. The same date establishes no priority; its assumptions do not supply the frozen result.

## value

PASS

For an existing objective, the result locates exactly when balancing expected utility can first lose optimality under the basic unit-support restriction. The three-item exchange rule is constructive and valid even when ex-ante balance is infeasible; the interior four-item witness shows the boundary does not require extreme probabilities or a change in utility magnitudes. The significance is the sharp item count, not a large quantitative gain.
