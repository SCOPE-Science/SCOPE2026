# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The trace-class differentiability of the analytic Fock Toeplitz family follows by integrating smooth coherent-state projectors over the moving polar endpoint. The disk has a strictly separated two-dimensional leading spectral block, permitting ordinary Kato second-order perturbation theory. The diagonal trace term is \(-te^{-t}\|h\|_2^2\), while the off-block terms are the two explicitly evaluated reciprocal eigenvalue gaps. This yields the claimed Fourier Hessian. Positivity and the uniquely minimizing degree-two harmonic follow from strictly increasing falling-factorial denominator ratios, for all degrees and every \(t>0\). A separate finite-domain matrix calculation and exact rational checks corroborate, but do not replace, the analytic all-degree argument.

## Originality

**PASS under inspected evidence.** The 2025 paper by Nicola, Riccardi and Tilli and its 2026 follow-up treat radial maximization and existence; Abreu (2026) proves global Ky Fan disk optimality but not the numerical local rank-two shape spectrum or its sharp area-dependent quadratic coefficient. Gómez, Guerra, Ramos and Tilli (2024) address rank-one stability. Direct searches for the Hessian, Fourier perturbation, and rank-two shape curvature and examination of these primary sources did not reveal an equivalent quantitative rank-two result. A stronger unrestricted disk inequality supplies only the sign of the variation, not its exact coefficient; its existence is fully acknowledged. Relevant additional older or unindexed work could contain an equivalent formula.

## Value

**PASS.** This determines the entire quadratic spectral stability profile of an exact area-preserving perturbation problem for the first nontrivial Ky Fan rank, identifies translation as the full nullspace, and proves a sharp positive transverse coefficient attained by ellipsoidal (degree-two) infinitesimal deformation. It quantitatively refines the global extremal inequality and provides a finite-area benchmark without claiming a global stability estimate.

## Scope and limitations

The statement covers only fixed \(C^2\) radial-square boundary profiles around disks. The \(O(\varepsilon^3)\) constant is profile dependent; no uniform nonlinear or global inequality follows, and no assertion is made for higher ranks. The coefficient is a second-order asymptotic. Literature novelty has not been independently certified.
