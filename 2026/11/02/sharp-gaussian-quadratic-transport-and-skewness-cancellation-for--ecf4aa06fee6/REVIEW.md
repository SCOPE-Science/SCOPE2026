# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is restricted to strictly positive two-state transitions with nonzero correlation. Stationarity gives the exact geometric two-point and three-point identities, and summing their finite geometric sums fixes the long-run variance and third cumulant. In the finite-dimensional analytic characteristic matrix, the Perron eigenvalue is isolated and the nontrivial Fourier frequencies have a strict spectral gap; the stationary spectral prefactor has zero first derivative. Those facts warrant a first-order local lattice Edgeworth expansion, including the half-lattice continuity correction. Arbitrarily high-order Fourier expansions make its inverse-CDF error negligible on a slowly expanding central interval. The optimal monotone transport cells yield an independent lattice-width contribution and a squared skewness-shift contribution. Chernoff and fourth-moment bounds control the discarded tails. Exact rational finite-size moment calculations and numerical transport values agree, with 97 deterministic checks. Unproved: uniform parameter-boundary rates and higher asymptotic terms.

## Originality — PASS, qualified

The directly motivating 2026 result of Hafouta and Leppänen proves \(W_2\) rates for mixing systems, not the sharp coefficient for this binary Markov chain. Bobkov's sharp Edgeworth transport asymptotics require a nonlattice condition for their stated leading constant. Kolassa--McCullagh supply lattice continuity-corrected Edgeworth asymptotics for independent observations, not correlated Markov occupation sums. The independent finite-lattice transport calculation covers the special case \(\rho=0\), which is deliberately not claimed as new. The new nonzero-dependence coefficient and the feasible cancellation root \(\rho=-2+\sqrt3\) are not implied by these statements without an additional dependence-specific calculation. Mathematical record searches for the exact formula, Markov transport and skewness cancellation returned no covering theorem. Historical risk remains that an older spectral Edgeworth transport formulation contains an implicit equivalent.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a sharp, natural invariant of Gaussian approximation for a canonical correlated binary process, where generic optimal-rate estimates conceal the leading constant. Negative temporal dependence can cancel the first-order third cumulant without imposing symmetry on the one-site marginal. The explicit cancellation locus is physically admissible only in a precise interval of stationary densities, giving a structural boundary not present in independent sampling. The quantization floor also shows the rate is unavoidably of square-root order.
