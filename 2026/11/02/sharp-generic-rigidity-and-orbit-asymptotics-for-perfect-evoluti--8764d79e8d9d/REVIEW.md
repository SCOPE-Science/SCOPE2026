# Review: sharp generic rigidity for finite-field perfect evolution algebras

## Correctness — PASS

The row-basis automorphism equation is independently derived. Monomiality holds because the invertible structure matrix makes the basis-square vectors independent. The proof counts fixed matrices by scalar restrictions and ordered-position permutation orbits, eliminates nonidentity diagonal stabilizers using stationary scalar-square cycles, and isolates reciprocal-scaled transpositions as the sole leading symmetry. Odd- and even-characteristic centralizer orders are reconstructed, not guessed. The asymptotic errors are uniform in the dimension with each field order fixed. The standalone verifier exhaustively reconstructs automorphism groups and independent monomial orbits in ten small cases, including a genuine characteristic-two extension field; finite checks are not a substitute for infinite proofs.

## Originality — PASS, within inspected sources

Sriwongsa–Zou (2022) gives automorphism structure and special-group classifications; Wei–Zou (2023) gives a general exact Burnside orbit formula and low-dimensional class counts; Costoya–Mayorga–Viruel (2024) describes permutation-representation realizations. Their inspected full-text statements are consistent with, but do not establish, the sharp fixed-field, large-dimension nonbinary probability coefficient or the conditional involution theorem. The 2026 Kaygorodov–Martín González–Viruel survey identifies random evolution-algebra automorphism probabilities as a live question, and the present result treats the perfect-algebra subcase. The binary \(q=2\) analogue has prior coverage and is excluded from novelty. Risks: a broader monomial matrix theorem may conceal an equivalent asymptotic; universal publication priority is not certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The new infinite-family theorem establishes typical automorphism rigidity over every fixed nonbinary finite field, quantifies the exceptional probability sharply, identifies its generic group, and provides a first relative correction to the isomorphism-class orbit count. It goes beyond a numerical small-dimension tabulation and addresses a naturally motivated generic classification problem. The theorem does not resolve singular or sparse ensembles or fields growing with dimension.

## Scope and independent verification

The mathematical claim is restricted to uniformly sampled nonsingular structure matrices in \(\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q)\) with fixed prime-power \(q\ge3\), ordinary automorphism groups over that field, and the dimension limit. The proof and source comparisons are within this review; no independent mathematical audit was conducted.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
