# Scientific review: sharp viability thresholds for a predator–prey–parasite model

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is the necessary and sufficient condition for **every** solution initially in the nonnegative unit simplex to remain there. The source equations are reproduced with their full parameter assumptions. Coordinate nonnegativity follows from multiplicative species equations. At full occupancy, the outward-flux test is exactly a quadratic polynomial; for fixed parasite density its second derivative in the prey/predator split is \(-2(1-\varepsilon)\le0\), reducing the triangle minimum to two edges. The nonnegative quadratic on each edge is equivalent to the square-root bound proved using a two-term Cauchy–Schwarz minimization. The tangent-cone argument proves sufficiency and global existence, while strictly negative flux proves immediate exit when any condition fails. Both equality and zero-mortality boundary cases are covered. An exact-rational independent implementation of the boundary formulas ran successfully on 5,000 parameter tuples and 330,000 grid points, with source-specific rational witnesses and the sharp mortality cutoff verified. The finite tests corroborate rather than replace the proof.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

The originating article (Nag Chowdhury et al., *J. Theor. Biol.* 564 (2023) 111446; DOI 10.1016/j.jtbi.2023.111446) was inspected in the publicly accessible complete paper, including its defining equations and numerical/bifurcation results. It analyzes survival and coexistence from selected initial conditions, not the necessary-and-sufficient condition that the **entire** normalized state simplex be invariant. The later Roy et al. (*Proc. R. Soc. A* 480 (2024) 20240127; DOI 10.1098/rspa.2024.0127) is especially close in motivation and authorship; its relevant full-text model section was inspected. Its pairwise interactions are antisymmetric, whereas the originating model permits separate conversion and loss coefficients. The 2024 model's pairwise terms cancel in the total-density derivative and therefore cannot imply the present two nontrivial square-root restrictions on asymmetric interactions. Older May–Leonard carrying/balance-simplex works discuss invariant attracting surfaces rather than global inward-pointing conditions for this fixed unit simplex. Actual academic searches and available prior research records revealed no covering result. A specialized or differently indexed equivalent may remain unidentified; searches do not prove priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The named biological state variables are **fractions of one fixed finite habitat** and their sum must not exceed one. That physical constraint is part of the scientific meaning of the source's free-space model; positivity of each species separately is not enough. The result gives the complete sharp feasible parameter domain for all initial ecological compositions, identifies two boundary edges as sufficient to detect all failures, and proves that free-space benefit coefficients are irrelevant to global feasibility. At the source's concrete parameter set, it identifies an exact parasite-death cutoff and explicit physically admissible initial states that immediately leave the habitat simplex. The full classification, not merely the observed negative derivative at one example, makes the finding useful for interpreting and calibrating this model and structurally related nonconservative food webs.

## Limitations and closest literature

The result neither establishes that a particular trajectory used in the 2023 paper leaves the physical region, nor says anything about persistence or attractor stability. It assumes the source's restriction \(0<\varepsilon\le1\). Only source-specific forward invariance is claimed; the tangent-cone criterion and scalar minimization are standard tools. The origin paper and the 2024 strategic-species paper were materially inspected at their defining equations. A comprehensive literature-wide novelty guarantee and independent external verification are not available.
