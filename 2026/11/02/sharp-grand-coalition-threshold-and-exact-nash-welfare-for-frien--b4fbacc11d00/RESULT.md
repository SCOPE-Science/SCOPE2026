# Sharp grand-coalition threshold and exact Nash welfare for friendship cycles

## Finding

Consider a symmetric appreciation-of-friends hedonic game on a labeled cycle \(C_n\), for integer \(n\ge4\). A friend gives value \(+n\); every other agent sharing a coalition gives \(-1\). For each partition with strictly positive member utilities, maximize Nash welfare, the geometric mean of those utilities. Then:

1. For \(4\le n\le11\), the unique maximizer is the grand coalition, with welfare \(n+3\).
2. For every \(n\ge12\), all maximizers consist solely of proper non-singleton contiguous cycle arcs, and they strictly beat the grand coalition. Define
   \[
   F_n(s)=(n+2-s)^2(2n+3-s)^{s-2}\qquad (2\le s\le n-1).
   \]
   Set \(P_n(0)=1\), \(P_n(t)=0\) for impossible positive states, and
   \[
   P_n(t)=\max_{2\le s\le\min(t,n-1)}P_n(t-s)F_n(s),\qquad 1\le t\le n.
   \]
   The exact optimal Nash welfare is
   \[
   \mathrm{NW}^{\ast}_n=\left(\max\{(n+3)^n,P_n(n)\}\right)^{1/n}.
   \]
   Optimal coalition sizes are recovered by backtracking the maximizing transition. The calculation requires \(O(n^2)\) exact-integer arithmetic operations (not a polynomial bound on bit complexity independent of multiplication).
3. As \(n\to\infty\),
   \[
   \log\!\frac{2n}{\mathrm{NW}^{\ast}_n}=2\sqrt{\frac{\log 2}{n}}+O(1/n).
   \]
   For each fixed \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), the fraction of agents in coalitions of length outside \([(1-\varepsilon)2\sqrt{n\log2},(1+\varepsilon)2\sqrt{n\log2}]\) in any optimum is \(O_\varepsilon(n^{-1/2})\).

## Assumptions and scope

Agents are the vertices of the simple undirected cycle. Utilities are unnormalized additive sums over all *other* members of the agent's own coalition; two cyclic neighbors are friends and all remaining vertices are enemies. A singleton's utility is zero. Nash welfare is zero if some member's utility is zero, and the optimization is over individually rational partitions (nonnegative utilities). The grand coalition has strictly positive welfare for \(n\ge4\), so every optimizer has strictly positive member utilities. No condition of Nash or core stability is imposed. All comparison inequalities are exact; asymptotics are for the number of vertices tending to infinity.

## Proof

**Step 1: connectivity of an optimum.** Suppose a coalition is disconnected in the induced friendship graph. Split it into its nonempty connected components. No friendships cross distinct components, so every member loses at least one enemy and no friend; each utility strictly rises. Utilities of other coalitions are unchanged, and all utilities in an optimum are positive. The Nash product therefore strictly increases, a contradiction. Thus all coalitions are connected. Connected proper subsets of a cycle are consecutive cyclic arcs. An optimal singleton is impossible because the grand coalition has strictly positive product. Hence the only possibilities are one grand coalition or a cyclic tiling by arcs of sizes \(2\le s\le n-1\).

**Step 2: exact contribution and recurrence.** In a proper arc of length \(s\), its two endpoints have one in-coalition friend and \(s-2\) enemies, hence utility \(n+2-s\). Every one of the \(s-2\) interior agents has two friends and \(s-3\) enemies, hence utility \(2n+3-s\). The contribution to the product is \(F_n(s)\). In the grand coalition, each agent has two friends and \(n-3\) enemies, so utility \(n+3\). Every list of legal arc sizes summing to \(n\) is realizable as consecutive blocks around the cycle. Multiplicativity yields the displayed recurrence and exact optimum; backtracking gives a constructive optimal partition.

**Step 3: the sharp finite threshold.** Exact recurrence evaluation for \(4\le n\le12\) gives the following pairs (maximum proper-arc product, grand-coalition product):

| \(n\) | Proper-arc product | Grand product |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 256 | 2401 |
| 5 | 4000 | 32768 |
| 6 | 90000 | 531441 |
| 7 | 2129400 | 10000000 |
| 8 | 65610000 | 214358881 |
| 9 | 2088124416 | 5159780352 |
| 10 | 81663349824 | 137858491849 |
| 11 | 3269493248000 | 4049565169664 |
| 12 | 154922431942656 | 129746337890625 |

Each comparison is finite, exact integer arithmetic generated by the explicit recurrence; a standalone independent exhaustive verifier evaluates the original definition directly for these sizes. Because any proper connected arc partition has smaller product for \(n\le11\), uniqueness follows.

For every even \(n=2k\ge12\), split into two arcs of length \(k\). Its per-agent log-welfare advantage over the grand coalition is
\[
D_e(k)=\log\frac{3k+3}{2k+3}-\frac2k\log\frac{3k+3}{k+2}.
\]
The first term strictly increases in \(k\), whereas \(\log[(3k+3)/(k+2)]/k\) strictly decreases for \(k\ge6\): the numerator logarithm \(B\) satisfies \(B>\log 2\), \(B'=1/((k+1)(k+2))\), and \(kB'<B\). At \(k=6\), \(8^2\cdot21^4>15^6\), so \(D_e(k)>0\) throughout.

For every odd \(n=2k+1\ge13\), split into arcs of lengths \(k\) and \(k+1\). The four endpoints each receive at least \(k+2\), and the other \(2k-3\) agents receive at least \(3k+4\). A lower bound on the per-agent log-welfare advantage is
\[
D_o(k)=\log\frac{3k+4}{2k+4}-\frac4{2k+1}\log\frac{3k+4}{k+2}.
\]
Its first term strictly increases. The second term strictly decreases for \(k\ge6\), since \(B=\log[(3k+4)/(k+2)]>\log2\), \(B'=2/((3k+4)(k+2))\), and \((2k+1)B'<2B\). At \(k=6\), \(8^4\cdot22^9>16^{13}\), proving \(D_o(k)>0\). This establishes the sharp global threshold without extrapolating finite enumeration.

**Step 4: asymptotic optimum and concentration.** Define the per-agent log-deficit of an arc of length \(s\) by
\[
d_n(s)=\log(2n)-\frac1s\log F_n(s).
\]
For every \(2\le s<n\), the endpoint utilities are no greater than \(n\), and interior utilities are \(2n+3-s\). Applying \(-\log(1-u)\ge u\) to the interior terms gives
\[
d_n(s)\ge\frac{2\log2}{s}+\frac{s-5}{2n}
\ge 2\sqrt{\frac{\log2}{n}}-\frac{5}{2n}.
\]
For \(s=2\), the first inequality follows directly from \(d_n(2)=\log2\). By the product formula, the deficit of any proper-arc partition is its agent-weighted mean arc deficit. Conversely, take \(q\) nearly equal arcs, with \(q\) the nearest integer to \(n/(2\sqrt{n\log2})\). Their lengths are \(2\sqrt{n\log2}+O(1)\). Uniform Taylor expansion of endpoint and interior logs for such lengths gives
\[
d_n(s)=\frac{2\log2}{s}+\frac{s}{2n}+O(1/n),
\]
so the constructed partition has overall deficit at most \(2\sqrt{\log2/n}+O(1/n)\). The grand coalition's deficit tends to \(\log2\), and is therefore not asymptotically optimal. The two bounds prove the asymptotic formula.

Finally, the exact identity
\[
\frac{2\log2}{s}+\frac{s}{2n}-2\sqrt{\frac{\log2}{n}}
=\frac{(s-2\sqrt{n\log2})^2}{2ns}
\]
combined with the \(O(1/n)\) optimum gap bounds the agent-weighted average of the right-hand side by \(O(1/n)\). Outside a fixed relative \(\varepsilon\)-neighborhood of the center, it is at least \(c_\varepsilon/\sqrt n\) (for all legal lengths), proving an \(O_\varepsilon(n^{-1/2})\) fraction bound.

## Relationship to prior work

Pagano and Schlenga (2026) introduced Nash-welfare optimization in additively separable hedonic games, including the symmetric appreciation-of-friends subclass, and proved a general \(2n\)-approximation for that subclass. Their Section 4 and related appendices do not provide the exact cycle optimum or the sharp threshold above. Elkind, Flammini and Varricchio (2025) study *egalitarian* (minimum-utility), not multiplicative Nash, welfare in friends-and-enemies games; their positive and hardness results do not imply the present formula. Classical connected-path compositions and product dynamic programming are used as standard mathematical tools, not asserted as new. The asserted contribution is their complete application and sharp transition for this Nash-welfare cycle family.

## Limitations

The theorem concerns the specific cycle friendship graph, with its \(+n/-1\) additive utilities. It does not extend to arbitrary graphs, asymmetrically valued friendships, generic coalition sizes, other hedonic objectives, or approximation guarantees for general games. The dynamic program counts arithmetic operations, not a uniform machine-bit complexity bound. Its originality is assessed relative to the accessible primary literature and mathematical database searches; differently formulated or unindexed earlier work could exist. Independent validation has not been performed.
