# Review

**Correctness — PASS.** Exact stationary covariance and the Gaussian intensity identity give an exact positive harmonic-energy formula for the zero deficit. The gamma-ratio Fourier coefficients and a convergent Fourier shell sum yield the fractional and logarithmic constants, with a separate localization argument controlling the nonlinear denominator. Boundary \(L^2\) convergence proves the remaining linear regime. The independently implemented standard-library checker passed 40 finite tests; these do not constitute the infinite proof. Positivity and all parameter endpoints in the stated open ranges were reviewed.

**Originality — PASS, with risks.** The closest original literature is Noda–Shirai (2021, arXiv:2106.02860) for the Gaussian zero identity and finitely dependent spectral asymptotics, and Shirai (2025, arXiv:2501.02586) for smooth fixed-direction boundary density. Neither inspected theorem provides the finite-deficit sharp convergence law at a positive Hölder cusp. Earlier published comparison results involving spectral jumps, spectral zeros, and a general half-Sobolev energy analogy do not imply the nonlinear local constant. Full text of Mukeru et al. (2020, DOI:10.1215/00192082-8720490) was inaccessible; historical priority is not certified and remains a real risk.

**Value — PASS.** The classification pinpoints a natural regularity threshold separating singularly localized and globally controlled convergence to a finite expected-zero deficit. Its sharp coefficients reveal a measurable effect of spectral roughness even though the spectral density remains strictly positive throughout. The result concerns an explicit cusp family; it does not claim general rough spectra or fluctuations.

**Limitations.** Only stationary proper complex Gaussian coefficients, one explicit positive cusp, \(0<\alpha<1\), and expected zero counts in radial disks are treated. The total finite deficit need not have an elementary closed form. Independent verification has not been conducted.
