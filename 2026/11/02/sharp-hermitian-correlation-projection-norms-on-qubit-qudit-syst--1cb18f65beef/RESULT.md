# Sharp Hermitian correlation-projection norms on qubit–qudit systems

## Finding

For every integer \(n\ge2\), consider the real Banach space \(\mathcal H_{2n}\) of Hermitian operators on \(\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^n\). Define the normalized marginal-preserving conditional expectations
\[
E_A(X)=\operatorname{Tr}_B(X)\otimes\frac{I_n}{n},\qquad
E_B(X)=\frac{I_2}{2}\otimes\operatorname{Tr}_A(X),\qquad
E_0(X)=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(X)}{2n}I_{2n},
\]
and the two-body correlation projection \(P_n=I-E_A-E_B+E_0\). Put \(c_n=(n-2)/n\) and \(b_n=1-c_n^2/4\). Its exact two endpoint norms are
\[
\boxed{\|P_n\|_{1\to1,\mathrm{Herm}}=\|P_n\|_{\infty\to\infty,\mathrm{Herm}}
=\frac12+\sqrt{\frac{4+3c_n^2}{4-c_n^2}}.}
\]
The largest trace norm of \(P_n(\rho)\) over separable density operators is exactly \(1+c_n=2-2/n\), and is attained by all pure product states. In contrast, the unrestricted norm is strictly larger for every \(n\ge2\). Its pure-state maximizers have Schmidt coefficients
\[
\left(\sqrt{\frac{1+u_n}{2}},\sqrt{\frac{1-u_n}{2}}\right),\qquad
u_n=\frac{c_n}{\sqrt{b_n(b_n+c_n^2)}}.
\]
Here \(u_2=0\) gives maximally entangled two-qubit maximizers, while \(0<u_n<1\) for all \(n\ge3\) gives strictly partially entangled maximizers. The Schmidt parameter is unique up to swapping the two coefficients. The norm increases with \(n\) and converges to \(1/2+\sqrt{7/3}\).

## Assumptions and scope

All traces are unnormalized matrix traces. Both induced norms act only on the **real** space of Hermitian matrices. The Schatten trace norm is \(\|X\|_1=\operatorname{Tr}|X|\), and the spectral norm is \(\|X\|_\infty\). The map \(P_n\) is the Hilbert–Schmidt-orthogonal projection onto operators traceless in each local factor, equivalently onto the tensor product of the local traceless matrix spaces. It is generally not a positive map. The statement does not concern the unrestricted complex-matrix induced norm, diamond norm, completely bounded norm, or dimensions larger than two in both local factors.

A state is separable if it is a convex combination of product density operators. The conclusion about maximizing pure states is global because the Hermitian trace-norm unit ball is generated by signed rank-one orthogonal projections.

## Proof

**Reduction to pure states and a single real parameter.** The trace-norm unit ball of \(\mathcal H_{2n}\) is the closed convex hull of the positive and negative rank-one projections. Convexity and linearity therefore give
\[
\|P_n\|_{1\to1,\mathrm{Herm}}=\max_{\|\psi\|=1}\|P_n(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)\|_1.
\]
The map commutes with conjugation by every local unitary. Schmidt decomposition reduces the maximization to
\[
|\psi_u\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{1+u}{2}}|00\rangle+\sqrt{\frac{1-u}{2}}|11\rangle,
\qquad 0\le u\le1,
\]
with the first two basis vectors in the qudit factor; the Schmidt rank is at most two.

**Exact output spectrum.** Put \(\lambda=(1+u)/2\) and \(\mu=(1-u)/2\). On the invariant span of \(|00\rangle\) and \(|11\rangle\), the output has the Hermitian block
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
(1/2-1/n)\lambda+1/(2n)&\sqrt{\lambda\mu}\\
\sqrt{\lambda\mu}&(1/2-1/n)\mu+1/(2n)
\end{pmatrix},
\]
with eigenvalues \(1/4\pm\frac12\sqrt{1-b_nu^2}\). On \(|01\rangle\) and \(|10\rangle\) the output is diagonal with entries \(-\mu/2-u/(2n)\) and \(-\lambda/2+u/(2n)\), both nonpositive, whose absolute sum is \(1/2\). For each \(j=2,\ldots,n-1\), the two basis entries \(|0j\rangle,|1j\rangle\) are \(-u/(2n)\) and \(+u/(2n)\), contributing \(u/n\). Thus, exactly,
\[
F_n(u):=\|P_n(|\psi_u\rangle\langle\psi_u|)\|_1
=\frac12+c_nu+\max\left\{\frac12,\sqrt{1-b_nu^2}\right\}.
\]
The larger of the two expressions arises because the first block has total trace \(1/2\).

**Global maximization including the branch boundary.** On the branch with \(\sqrt{1-b_nu^2}\ge1/2\), the strictly concave function \(\frac12+c_nu+\sqrt{1-b_nu^2}\) has its unique maximum at
\[
u_n=\frac{c_n}{\sqrt{b_n(b_n+c_n^2)}}\qquad\text{with value}\qquad
\frac12+\sqrt{1+\frac{c_n^2}{b_n}}.
\]
This point belongs to \([0,1)\) and the indicated branch: for \(0\le c_n<1\), \(b_n>3/4\), and \(b_n(b_n+c_n^2)-c_n^2=1-c_n^2/2-3c_n^4/16\ge5/16\), hence \(u_n^2<1\); moreover \(1-b_nu_n^2=b_n/(b_n+c_n^2)>1/4\). The complementary branch has the linear value \(1+c_nu\le1+c_n\). To compare its maximum with the concave-branch optimum, set \(c=c_n\), \(b=1-c^2/4\), and note the identity
\[
1+\frac{c^2}{b}-(c+1/2)^2
=\frac34-c+\frac{c^4}{4b}\ge\frac{c^4-4c+3}{4}
=\frac{(1-c)^2(c^2+2c+3)}4>0.
\]
This proves the stated norm and strict inequality over the product-state value. The same strictness and strict concavity prove the uniqueness of the maximizing Schmidt parameter. Self-adjointness \(P_n^*=P_n\) under the real trace pairing and duality between Hermitian spectral and trace norms give the second endpoint norm exactly.

**Separable cutoff and asymptotics.** For a pure product density operator, \(P_n(\rho_A\otimes\rho_B)=(\rho_A-I_2/2)\otimes(\rho_B-I_n/n)\). Its trace norm equals \(1\cdot2(1-1/n)=1+c_n\). Convexity extends the upper bound to every separable state and yields equality for pure products. Substituting \(c_n\uparrow1\) in the closed formula gives \(1/2+\sqrt{7/3}\). Strict monotonicity in \(c\ge0\) follows by differentiating \((4+3c^2)/(4-c^2)\).

## Relationship to prior work

Zhu, Wang, Bao, Li, Shen and Fei (arXiv:2305.00460v1, first publicly posted 2023-04-30) study separability through nuclear norms of **Bloch-correlation coefficient matrices** and augmented coefficient arrays. Those are differently normalized maps between operator-coordinate spaces. Their theorems do not determine the Schatten trace norm of the physical \(2n\times2n\) operator \(P_n(\rho)\) or the induced Hermitian norm of \(P_n\). Classical Schatten duality and the Schmidt decomposition underlie this calculation, but do not provide its two-branch extremization or its dimension-uniform entanglement gap without computation. A separate previously available exact formula for weighted **one-body** marginal projections concerns \(\sum_j t_j(E_j-E_0)\), not the interacting second-order projection \(I-E_A-E_B+E_0\); product-state extremality for that different operator does not imply the present entangled extremality.

## Limitations

Only one tensor factor is two-dimensional. No claim is made about complex-linear input norm maximizers, ancilla-amplified norms, multipartite or higher-local-dimension correlation projections, or whether the trace-norm test detects every entangled state. The originality comparison does not rule out an equivalent formula under different noncommutative projection or norm terminology; independent mathematical and historical verification remains outstanding.
