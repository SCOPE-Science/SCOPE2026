# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The exact norm reduction is an identity: for each input the componentwise maximizing radii produce one of all \(3^{10}\) averaging matrices. The standalone standard-library verifier partitions all \(59049\) cases into \(792\) orbits under \(120\) explicitly verified graph automorphisms and proves an integer Sylvester certificate for the \(791\) nonexceptional orbits. The remaining orbit has \(20\) cases. The displayed positive eigenvector and the cubic are checked by exact polynomial identities, and Perron--Frobenius identifies the simple leading eigenvalue. Rational interval checks establish strict radius activation. The classification follows from the strict spectral gap and positivity of the leading eigenvector. No finite sampling has been used as an infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS, with specified risk

The exact cubic sharp norm, its positive three-level eigenvector and all twenty extremal rays were checked against the graph-maximal literature and research records. Soria--Tradacete arXiv:1410.6204 treats sharp \(p\le1\) bounds and small graph examples; González-Riquelme--Madrid arXiv:2005.03146 and arXiv:2011.02630 treat strong \(\ell^2\) norms on complete graphs and stars. Their stated results neither imply nor tabulate the Petersen invariant. Greenblatt's 2016 thesis studies powers and spherical maximal averages of the Petersen graph; only its abstract was accessible, creating residual risk of an earlier unindexed exact computation. Searches are evidence of comparison, not proof of publication priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The Petersen graph is the canonical cubic graph of girth five and a minimal nonbipartite distance-regular test case. Its previously unlisted exact strong maximal norm and complete symmetry-breaking extremizer classification provide a natural analytic invariant and a reproducible benchmark for geometry-dependent maximal inequalities. The equality pattern is a near-star of three high coordinates with one exceptionally low pair, rather than a point mass or constant input. The result makes no broad extrapolation beyond this canonical graph.
