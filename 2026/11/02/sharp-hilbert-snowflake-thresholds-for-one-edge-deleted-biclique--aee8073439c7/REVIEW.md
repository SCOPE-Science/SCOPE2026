# Scientific review: one-edge-deleted biclique snowflake thresholds

## Correctness — PASS
The two-sided distance profile is correct for all integers \(m,n\ge2\) with \(m+n\ge5\): all cross-part pairs are at distance one except the deleted pair, which is at distance three; same-part pairs are at distance two. The exact powered-distance quadratic identity is obtained by summing these three types. Holding the two distinguished coefficients and one part sum fixed, flattening the remaining within-part coefficients maximizes the quadratic form, so the full sign problem reduces without loss to a three-variable symmetric matrix. Its lower-right block is positive definite throughout \(0\le p\le1\) by \(3^p-1\le2^p\); its scalar Schur complement is the stated function. Endpoint substitution, finite-metric strictness, and the one-dimensional kernel establish the unique global threshold. The balanced-mode determinant, isometric inclusion argument, and mean-value estimate establish the strict monotonicity and sharp asymptotic coefficient. Independent breadth-first-search and Gram-spectrum tests, 5,414 assertions total, replayed successfully. These finite tests do not replace the all-parameter proof.

## Originality — PASS, with historical-priority qualification
The closest inspected paper is Młotkowski, Skrzypczyk and Wojtylak, arXiv:2409.17662, Section 2 (initial public version September 26, 2024): it gives exact quadratic embedding constants for bicliques after deleting matching edges, including one deleted edge, **at exponent one**. It does not determine the powered-distance transition for \(0<p<1\), the critical equality rank throughout the two-parameter family, or the \(r^{-3}\) balanced gain. Robertson, arXiv:2004.10923, supplies a universal determinant criterion, which in principle applies to every finite metric but does not itself provide this symmetry-reduced formula or sharp asymptotic. Li and Weston, arXiv:0901.0695, supply the strict-negative-type interval theorem used in the proof; Doust and Weston, arXiv:2404.06658, treat general polygonal equalities. Scientific search of the available published research index and relevant literature located no direct equivalent. Older or unindexed equivalent calculations remain an originality risk; no independent priority certification is claimed.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The exponent is a geometrically natural invariant governing exact Hilbert embeddings of powered graph metrics. A single deleted edge creates the only distance-three pair in an otherwise diameter-two biclique; its exact effect on the sharp exponent was unresolved by the closest ordinary-distance literature. The theorem gives a complete two-parameter classification, explicitly isolates the single critical equality direction, and proves a strictly positive, quantitatively sharp cubic gain for balanced bicliques. The result is neither merely a restatement of the known exponent-one classification nor a table recomputation. The scope does not include multiple deletions or unequal edge lengths.
