# Review of the finite uncentered interval maximal theorem

## Correctness

**PASS.** The proof establishes contact at every local-maximum plateau using only the admissibility of each maximizing interval and the fact that a plateau's outside neighbor has smaller maximal value. Consecutive peaks and valleys then partition the output variation into disjoint path contributions. For every truncation, the pointwise majorant gives coefficient \(1\); only for the full interval family does a peak-to-valley interval average enforce the strictly improved factor \((n-1)/n\). Endpoint impulses exactly attain both bounds. The arguments include endpoints, plateaus, signed inputs, and the cases \(R=1\) and \(n=2\). The exact-rational standalone checker supplied corroborating finite checks, not a substitute for proof.

## Originality

**PASS, subject to historical literature uncertainty.** The closest inspected primary papers establish variation contraction for continuous uncentered/local maximal functions (Aldaz–Pérez Lázaro, 2006), discrete uncentered maximal functions over \(\mathbb Z\) (Bober–Carneiro–Hughes–Pierce, 2010), and a separate finite **centered** path conjecture (González-Riquelme–Kovač–Madrid, 2026). The new strict factor \((n-1)/n\) for all finite inputs and the precise full-horizon versus truncated-horizon transition were not located there or in searched prior research records. The truncated coefficient-one upper bound has close prior coverage and is not asserted as an independently original inequality. An implicit proof of the strict finite bound in earlier literature remains possible; available searches cannot exclude it.

## Value

**PASS.** The all-horizon constant is a sharp and attained finite-size stability invariant for the natural uncentered discrete maximal operator, whereas existing real-line and infinite-lattice theorems give coefficient \(1\). The exact discontinuity at the last admissible interval distinguishes global averaging from every finite truncation on the same chain. This supplies a rigorous solved benchmark for the substantially different centered finite-path problem, without claiming to solve that conjecture.

## Limitations

Only counting-measure paths, contiguous uncentered interval averages, one-input edge variation, and integer cutoffs are covered. The argument proves no centered graph-ball or nonlinear two-input stability result. The originality conclusion is based on accessible inspected literature and search results, not a universal negative claim.
