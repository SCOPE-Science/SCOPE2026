# Sharp improvement-path lengths on one-vacancy single-peaked Schelling cycles

## Finding
For a two-color single-peaked jump Schelling game on a cycle with \(N\ge3\) vertices, exactly one vacant vertex, both colors represented and common peak \(0<\Lambda<1\), **every strict-improvement trajectory terminates**. Maximizing the number of moves over all color multiplicities, starting arrangements and choices of strictly improving moves gives exactly
\[
L_N(\Lambda)=\begin{cases}N-3,&0<\Lambda<1/2,\\\lfloor(N-2)/2\rfloor,&1/2\le\Lambda<1.\end{cases}
\]
These are maximum lengths, not just upper bounds. The switch at \(\Lambda=1/2\) is genuine.

## Assumptions and scope
A cycle \(C_N\) has \(N-1\) distinguishable agents, colored \(R\) and \(B\), with at least one of each; one vertex is empty. An agent may jump only to the empty vertex. Its same-type fraction is the number of agents of its color in its *occupied closed neighborhood*, divided by the number of occupants of that neighborhood (including itself). It receives \(u(f)\), where \(u(0)=u(1)=0\), \(u\) increases strictly to \(\Lambda\) and is given on the descending side by \(u(f)=u(\Lambda(1-f)/(1-\Lambda))\). Thus \(u(1/3)>u(2/3)\) iff \(\Lambda<1/2\). A move is allowed exactly when the moving agent's utility increases strictly. The proof does not assume a move-selection rule.

## Proof
Let \(M\) be the number of cycle edges with two occupied same-color endpoints and let \(H\) indicate that the two neighbors of the unique vacancy have the same color. Because both colors occur, \(0\le M\le N-3\). An agent adjacent to the vacancy has fraction \(1/2\) or \(1\); a nonadjacent agent has fraction \(1/3\), \(2/3\) or \(1\). After a nonadjacent jump into a mixed-color vacancy, its fraction is \(2/3\); into a homogeneous vacancy it is either \(1\) (never improving) or \(1/3\). An adjacent jump preserves its two occupied-neighbor counts except for repositioning the vacancy, so a strict improvement is possible only from \(1\) to \(1/2\).

For \(\Lambda\le1/2\), a strict move into a mixed vacancy must originate at fraction \(1\), and removes one monochromatic occupied edge. A strict move into a homogeneous vacancy must originate at fraction \(2/3\) or \(1\); it decreases \(M\) by one or two. Every improving adjacent move decreases \(M\) by one. Hence each move decreases the nonnegative integer \(M\), proving termination and at most \(N-3\) moves for \(\Lambda<1/2\).

For \(\Lambda\ge1/2\), a strict move with \(H\) true can only relocate a nonadjacent agent with old fraction \(1\) to the vacancy with new fraction \(1/3\). Its old location becomes the new vacancy, again with same-color neighbors; \(H\) remains true and \(M\) falls by exactly two. Whenever \(H\) is false, every strict improvement creates \(H\) true (an adjacent improving agent has two same-color neighbors on its occupied side; a nonadjacent improving agent must have old fraction \(1/3\) or \(1\), also with equal-color neighbors). If both colors occur and \(H\) is true, the occupied chain between vacancy-neighbors starts and ends in the same color, so it has at least two bichromatic occupied edges; hence \(M\le N-4\). Consequently an initial mixed-vacancy step can be followed by at most \(\lfloor(N-4)/2\rfloor\) steps; a homogeneous-vacancy initial state permits no more. Thus every trajectory has length at most \(\lfloor(N-2)/2\rfloor\). The case \(N=3\) has zero strict improvements.

For sharpness below one half, put \(m=\lfloor N/2\rfloor\). If \(N=2m+1\), begin at the cyclic state \(R^{m-1}\,\square\,B^mR\); if \(N=2m\), begin at \(R^{m-1}\,\square\,B^{m-1}R\). Here the exponents denote literal runs of colors. At the stage \(R^a\square B^c(RB)^jR\), move the penultimate \(B\) of the long \(B\)-run into the vacancy, then the final \(R\) of the initial \(R\)-run into the vacancy newly flanked by \(B\) vertices. The first move improves from utility zero to positive utility; the second improves from fraction \(2/3\) to \(1/3\), which is strict for \(\Lambda<1/2\). Each pair changes the stage to \(R^{a-1}\square B^{c-1}(RB)^{j+1}R\). For odd \(N\) perform \(m-1\) pairs; for even \(N\), perform \(m-2\) pairs and one final adjacent move from fraction \(1\) to \(1/2\). This gives exactly \(N-3\) moves, with empty pair ranges allowed at small sizes.

For sharpness at or above one half, initialize \(\square R^r B^b\), where \(r=\lfloor N/2\rfloor\) and \(b=N-1-r\). Write \(K=\lfloor(N-2)/2\rfloor\). If \(K=0\), nothing is needed. When \(N\not\equiv2\pmod4\), first move the first \(R\) into the vacancy, then for steps \(t=2,\ldots,K\), select the second vertex in the final contiguous \(B\)-run when \(t\) is even, or the second vertex in the unique longest \(R\)-run when \(t\) is odd. The occupied runs have lengths at least three at their selection. When \(N\equiv2\pmod4\), first move the second \(B\) of the initial \(B\)-run into the vacancy; for even later steps move the second vertex in the longest \(R\)-run, and for odd later steps move a \(B\) with two \(B\) neighbors in the remaining long \(B\)-run. The run lengths decrease by two at each repeated selection; the displayed choices exist until step \(K\). In each construction every chosen agent has old same-type fraction \(1\) and new fraction in \(\{1/3,1/2,2/3\}\). Its utility therefore rises strictly for every \(0<\Lambda<1\). The stated maximum is attained.

## Verification
The standalone `verify.py` uses exact rational utility evaluations to enumerate all two-color occupancy states with one vacancy for \(3\le N\le10\), comparing longest strict-improvement paths with the theorem at peaks \(1/4\), \(1/2\), \(3/4\). It checks potential-change claims for every strict move and exercises both constructive sharpness families through \(N=100\) at five rational peaks. These finite checks are supplementary to the proof, not a proof of the general infinite claim. Run `python3 verify.py` with Python 3 standard library.

## Relationship to prior work
Friedrich, Lenzner, Molitor and Seifert prove the one-vacancy ring finite improvement property for the **single peak** \(\Lambda=1/2\) (Theorem 2.4) and exhibit improvement cycles when at least two vacancies are allowed (Theorem 2.1). Their theorem does not state a result for all peaks nor the exact maximum length for either peak regime. The present proof gives a full all-peaks classification on the one-vacancy cycle; it neither extends to multiple vacancies nor conflicts with their cycle examples. Swap-game theorems concern a different move operation.

## Limitations
Only two colors, an undirected cycle, one vacancy, strict improvements, and the specified self-including single-peaked utility are covered. The result is the supremum over arbitrary valid initial color counts and trajectories, not a claim about expected asynchronous convergence. Literature priority remains subject to undiscovered equivalent formulations. No independent audit has been performed.
