# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

For all integers \(m,n\ge2\), the rook-graph fort predicate is exactly the absence of a nonselected cell with one selected neighbor. Incidence cycles yield fort subconfigurations. A minimal fort must be either a single cycle or an incidence forest; cyclic and forest bounds give the stated upper bound, including the square spanning-cycle exception. The cross construction attains \(m+n-2\), and the consecutive-sides cycle attains \(m+n-1\). Inclusion-minimality is proved against every proper subset, not inferred from failures of one-element deletions. The independent finite checker exhaustively evaluates 1,414,992 masks on ten boards; its finite scope is not mistaken for universal proof.

## Originality — PASS, subject to priority risk

The closest inspected full texts are Cameron et al., arXiv:2310.17904 (fort packing, fractional forcing, Cartesian products), and Becker et al., arXiv:2404.05963 (counts of minimal forts, complements of maximal failed forcing sets, other families). Neither inspected statement supplies the largest cardinality of an inclusion-minimal fort for every \(K_m\square K_n\). Alternate line-graph, lattice/Hamming, and failed-zero-forcing formulations and mathematical-record searches yielded no dominating implication. Unindexed or differently formulated literature may exist; novelty is not externally certified.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This determines the sharp rank of the entire minimal-fort obstruction hypergraph for a natural two-parameter infinite family, including a one-vertex exceptional phenomenon for consecutive sides. Both extremal constructions have distinct structural explanations: a double-star cross and a nonspanning bipartite cycle. A full classification or count of all minimal forts is not claimed.
