# Independent mathematical audit

## correctness

PASS

PASS. A half-turn quotient of the flat torus R²/(2Ze1+2Ze2), e1=(1,0),e2=(1/2,sqrt(3)/2), gives four unit equilateral faces with tetrahedral edge gluing and cone angle pi at four vertices; quotient orbital metric is min_{lambda,sign}||u±v-lambda||. Fixing u and translating v gives eccentricity as Euclidean covering radius of Lambda union (Lambda+2u). For any full-rank d>=2 torus, two open geodesic balls of radius <lambda1(Lambda)/2 are individually contractible; their union cannot cover T^d by cup-length-two obstruction (two nontrivial H¹ classes cup to nonzero H²). Thus any such two-coset cover has radius at least lambda1/2, equal to 1 here. At edge midpoint 2u=e1, the union forms rectangular lattice generated by (1,0),(0,sqrt(3)), with covering radius sqrt(1/4+3/4)=1. At a vertex 2u in Lambda, single triangular lattice covering radius 2/sqrt(3). Every source is bounded by single-coset covering and the universal lower bound; continuity and connectivity yield entire range, scaling gives a. Independently checked hexagonal and rectangular Voronoi endpoint distances; numerical generic source samples do not prove the all-source bounds.

## originality

PASS

PASS, qualified. The standard tetrahedral triangular unfolding appears in Davis–Dods–Traub–Yang 2015, and Davis 2023 investigates tetrahedral source cut loci and geodesic complexity; known vertex-source eccentricity determines the upper endpoint. Their inspected results do not imply the lower bound over every source or exhibit edge midpoints as sharp intrinsic Chebyshev centers. The general cup-length bound is classical topology but its sharp application to the triangular torus quotient gives this specific minimax theorem. A prior intrinsic metric-center paper under different terminology remains possible.

PASS. The intrinsic Chebyshev radius is a canonical global geometric invariant that differs from ambient Euclidean radii. The theorem identifies actual minimax sources and complete attainable eccentricity interval of a familiar polyhedral surface; the general two-ball topological obstruction and matching periodic lattice cover offer reusable geometric structure. It does not claim full uniqueness or pointwise eccentricity formulas.
