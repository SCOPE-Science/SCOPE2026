# Sharp inverse-fiber sectors for a four-partition planar map

## Finding
For the continuous planar map
\[
T_{a,c}(x,y)=(|x|+ay-1,\ x+c|y|-1),\qquad a,c\in\mathbb R,
\]
let \(N_{a,c}(u,v)=\#T_{a,c}^{-1}(u,v)\). We classify its fibers for all nondegenerate parameters \(|a|\ne|c|\). The **new contribution** is the exact spatial partition into zero-, two-, and four-sheet target sectors, including the critical-ray multiplicities and the sharp four-sheet aperture. Invertibility in the other parameter regime also follows from the earlier general four-convex-piece theorem and is included only for completeness.

**Theorem.** If \(|a|>|c|\), then \(N_{a,c}(u,v)=1\) for every \((u,v)\in\mathbb R^2\); the map is a piecewise-linear homeomorphism of degree \(-\operatorname{sgn}(a)\).

If \(|c|>|a|\), define \(X=u+1\), \(Y=v+1\), \(b=|a|\), \(t=|c|\), and \(\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(t,b)\in(\pi/4,\pi/2]\). For targets off the four critical rays from \((-1,-1)\), the following conditions are exact:

* For \(c>0\): the **four-sheet** region is \(Y>X\) and \(tX>bY\). The **two-sheet** region is the rest of \(X+Y>0\) and \(tX+bY>0\) after deleting the four-sheet region. Every other regular target has zero preimages.
* For \(c<0\): the **four-sheet** region is \(X+Y<0\) and \(tX+bY>0\). The **two-sheet** region is the rest of \(X-Y>0\) and \(tX-bY>0\) after deleting the four-sheet region. Every other regular target has zero preimages.

The open four-sheet cone has angular width
\[
\Delta=\theta-\frac\pi4\in(0,\pi/4],
\]
and the exact area of four-sheet targets in any radius-\(R>0\) disk centered at \((-1,-1)\) is \(R^2\Delta/2\). Their disk area fraction is \(\Delta/(2\pi)\); hence the fraction is at most \(1/8\), with equality exactly when \(a=0\). The global degree in this regime is zero.

To specify **all boundary targets** as well, use the polar angle \(\phi=\arg(X+iY)\in(-\pi,\pi]\). When \(c>0\) and \(b>0\), the image interior is \(-\pi/4<\phi<\pi-\theta\): its interior-ray counts are \(N=3\) at \(\phi=\pi/4,\theta\), its other interior sectors have counts two or four as above, and its outer rays \(\phi=-\pi/4,\pi-\theta\) each have count one. When \(c<0\) and \(b>0\), the image interior is \(\theta-\pi<\phi<\pi/4\): the interior-ray counts are three at \(\phi=-\theta,-\pi/4\), and the outer rays \(\phi=\theta-\pi,\pi/4\) each have count one. For \(b=0\), the coalesced inner/outer ray \(\phi=\pi/2\) when \(c>0\), or \(\phi=-\pi/2\) when \(c<0\), has count two; the other inner ray has count three and the other outer ray count one. In every case the apex \((u,v)=(-1,-1)\) has exactly one preimage, and targets outside the closed image have none.

## Assumptions and scope
The parameters are arbitrary real numbers subject only to \(|a|\ne|c|\). There is no restriction on the forward orbit, attraction, or parameter magnitude. 'Regular target' means a target outside the image of the coordinate axes. Those images are the four rays emanating from \((-1,-1)\) in the directions \((1,1)\), \((a,c)\), \((1,-1)\), and \((-a,c)\). For the case \(a=0\), the last two vertical rays agree, and the preceding boundary conventions address that coincidence. The inverse images are counted as distinct geometric points, not quadrant listings.

## Proof
Translate the target: put \(F(x,y)=T_{a,c}(x,y)+(1,1)\). Its restrictions to the four source quadrants are nonsingular linear maps whenever \(|a|\ne|c|\). Traverse the source axes counterclockwise; their positively directed images are
\[
r_1=(1,1),\quad r_2=(a,c),\quad r_3=(1,-1),\quad r_4=(-a,c).
\]
The image of quadrant \(j\) is precisely the nonnegative cone generated by \(r_j,r_{j+1}\), taking \(r_5=r_1\). An interior target of one such cone has exactly one preimage in that quadrant, because the associated determinant is nonzero. No two quadrant-interior preimages coincide.

First let \(|a|>|c|\). Then \(a\ne0\), and eliminating the second source coordinate via \(y=(X-|x|)/a\) gives
\[
Y=g_X(x):=x+\frac{c}{|a|}\big|X-|x|\big|.
\]
For fixed \(X\), every linear piece of \(g_X\) has slope in the positive interval \((1-|c|/|a|,1+|c|/|a|)\), and \(g_X(x)\to\pm\infty\) as \(x\to\pm\infty\). Thus \(g_X\) is a strictly increasing bijection. Recovering \(y\) proves a globally continuous piecewise-linear inverse. Every quadrant determinant has sign \(-\operatorname{sgn}(a)\), so the degree is \(-\operatorname{sgn}(a)\).

Now suppose \(t=|c|>b=|a|\). Let \(\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(t,b)\). If \(c>0\), the four critical-ray angles are the set \(\{-\pi/4,\pi/4,\theta,\pi-\theta\}\), with the two last rays interchanged when the sign of \(a\) changes. Each quadrant cone has angular aperture strictly less than \(\pi\). Counting the quadrant cones that cover an angular interval gives exactly:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
\text{angle }\phi & (-\infty,-\pi/4)&(-\pi/4,\pi/4)&(\pi/4,\theta)&(\theta,\pi-\theta)&(\pi-\theta,\infty)\\\hline
N&0&2&4&2&0.
\end{array}
\]
The first/last intervals are interpreted within \((-\pi,\pi]\). For \(c<0\), the ordered critical-ray angles are \(\theta-\pi,-\theta,-\pi/4,\pi/4\); the corresponding counts on successive open angular intervals are \(0,2,4,2,0\). The formulas remain valid with empty middle intervals at \(b=0\) (where the two vertical rays coincide). Translating angular cones into half-plane intersections gives exactly the strict linear inequalities stated in the theorem. On a critical ray, count the interior-quadrant preimages plus each boundary-axis preimage **once** rather than twice. This gives the stated boundary counts; direct cone-coordinate evaluation verifies the coalesced-ray count of two when \(b=0\).

Every nonzero source point maps to a nonzero translated target because each quadrant cone is pointed and both of its ray generators are nonzero. Homogeneity and compactness of the unit circle therefore give \(\inf_{\|p\|=1}\|F(p)\|>0\), so \(T_{a,c}\) is proper. Each regular two-sheet or four-sheet target has equally many preimages with positive and negative quadrant determinant, making the Brouwer degree zero. Finally, a radial wedge of angle \(\Delta\) subtends area \(\Delta R^2/2\) in a centered disk. This establishes every part of the theorem.

## Relationship to prior work
Tikjha (2026, arXiv:2607.13519v1, §2–§4) introduces this exact four-partition map, studies periodic bifurcations and critical curves, and treats degenerate equality cases; the full manuscript does not state the above target-by-target inverse-fiber census or the explicit four-sheet aperture law. Poggiolini and Spadini (2013, arXiv:1110.1329, Theorem 4.5) already prove a general nonsingular four-convex-piece inversion result: **the homeomorphism half of this theorem is not asserted to be new.** Their theorem assumes compatible determinant signs and therefore does not yield the opposite-sign sector census. Their 2018 extension is likewise chiefly about sufficient conditions for inversion; only its abstract and introductory material could be checked, leaving an access-related priority risk. The 2020 two-partition transition family studies a different map, and general noninvertible-map nomenclature \(Z_0,Z_2,Z_4\) does not specify the new model's parameter threshold, critical-ray counts, or angular area.

## Limitations
The exceptional singular parameter lines \(|a|=|c|\), at which quadrant determinants vanish, are deliberately excluded and are not classified here. The result concerns global inverse fibers, not forward orbit stability, entropy, or attractor basins. It is an elementary but complete geometric theorem for this concrete family rather than a new general theorem for all planar piecewise-linear maps. The 2018 piecewise-regular comparison was not inspected in complete full text; historical priority awaits further independent review.
