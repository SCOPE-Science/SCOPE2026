At fixed normalized triangle slack \(\delta=(a+b-c)/b\), the smallest possible maximal embedded-isosceles area ratio has a complete sharp two-branch profile, with a quartic-governed transition between two extremal configurations. Every nondegenerate triangle admits an isosceles subtriangle covering strictly more than \(1/\sqrt2+(\sqrt2-1)\delta/4\) of its area, and the linear coefficient is globally optimal.
