# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact backward tilted-generator equation has a simple principal eigenvalue. The fourth-order Taylor expansion gives the second- and fourth-cumulant growth rates with correct units, sign and factors; the reciprocal-quartic reduction proves one and only one sign threshold on the full physical contrast range, and a factored derivative establishes the unique maximum. The independently assembled finite-state matrix and forward moment system agree with the formulas at six physically distinct points. Critical limits and the equal-speed boundary were explicitly checked. The code is supporting evidence; the proof is the analytic eigenvalue and polynomial argument.

## Originality — PASS on examined evidence
This is the **forced-sign reversal, freshly resampled speed-magnitude** model, not constant-speed telegraph motion, a fresh independently signed velocity at each tumble, or a population of trajectories each assigned a single fixed speed. Stadje and Zacks (2004) give a general random-velocity position distribution and represent the strongest possible broader-coverage risk; their accessible abstract is insufficient to establish equivalence for the present conditional-resampling model. Sim, Liepe and Stumpf (2015) study random-speed distributional mixing; De Bruyne, Majumdar and Schehr (2021) focus on absorbing-boundary survival. Searches of published results and accessible previously recorded mathematical claims did not expose the exact fourth-order sign/maximum theorem. Historical priority is provisional; the complete 2004 and 2015 texts were not accessible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The result establishes when a bounded-speed persistent process approaches its Gaussian limit with positive rather than negative standardized fourth cumulant. Both the unique sign threshold and the different finite contrast maximizing the positive tail correction are quantitatively sharp and informative for displacement-shape diagnostics of active transport. No claim is made that the limit itself is non-Gaussian.

## Closest literature and limitations
The constant-speed subcase is classical and not new. The comparison sources are Stadje–Zacks (2004), Sim–Liepe–Stumpf (2015), Kolesnik (2012), and De Bruyne–Majumdar–Schehr (2021); see RESULT.md and AUDIT.json for exact identifiers, search comparisons, material actually inspected, and residual priority risks. The theorem assumes equal probabilities for exactly two fresh speed magnitudes, forced sign alternation and Poisson waiting times. The coefficient's sign governs sufficiently late times only.
