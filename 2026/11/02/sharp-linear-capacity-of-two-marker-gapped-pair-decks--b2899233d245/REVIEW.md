# Scientific review

## Correctness

PASS. The four-coordinate observation is equivalent to \((X,Y,h)\), where \(X\) counts eligible earlier positions for the two marked sites, \(Y\) counts eligible later positions, and \(h\) records whether the two marked positions form an admissible pair. Partitioning the position curve into left, middle, and right zones partitions the images of all pairs into disjoint excess slices. The proof counts each slice with explicit realizing indices and tracks the binary spacing bit, including the separate \(s=2\) parity case and the first transient boundary. The external-free verifier independently enumerates literal pairs for small lengths and checks the closed formula on hundreds of additional parameter pairs; its finite scope is acknowledged.

## Originality

PASS under accessible evidence, with residual literature risk. The gap-constrained observation is motivated by Golm et al. (arXiv:2201.12671); its accessible abstract and indexed preliminary material concern collisions of full gapped decks, not a weight-two count. The full text of Chrisnata et al. (DOI:10.3934/amc.2021032) concerns ungapped \(k\)-decks and gives different two-deck counts. Searches for gapped pair-deck capacity, fixed-weight gapped-deck enumeration, aliases, and the displayed formulas did not locate prior implication of the theorem. The most relevant gapped-deck primary full text was not fully accessible, so an implicit specialization cannot be excluded.

## Value

PASS. This is an exact two-parameter family of zero-error observation capacities, rather than a single arbitrary finite instance. Weight two is the first sparse layer with a nontrivial interaction between the marked positions; changing the physical separation requirement changes which pairs of marker sites are distinguishable. The sharp \(n=3s-1\) onset identifies a finite-length regime change and gives a baseline for fixed-weight gapped-reconstruction code design. It does not purport to solve the general-weight problem.
