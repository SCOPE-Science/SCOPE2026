# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The claim specifies a real continuous-function Banach space, a total-variation-one signed-power functional, its codimension-one kernel, and the intrinsic coefficient in every linear projection. The reduction of projection norms to \(1+\|h\|_\infty\) is an application of the rank-one Daugavet identity for \(C([0,1])\). Direct pairing of points equidistant from the density's unique sign change gives an integrable sharp majorant, with equality attained by one explicit clipped ramp. The resulting scalar envelope is strictly increasing before saturation, yielding the exact feasibility boundary and unique least-amplitude solution. Saturation in the Lipschitz inequality determines the complete equality case, including the critical affine endpoint. Both asymptotic expansions follow from the exact scalar equation; the square-root coefficient was independently recomputed using the partial derivatives at its fold. The standalone checker passed 4,971 finite assertions, but infinite-parameter correctness rests on the analytic proof rather than sampling.

## Originality — PASS (best-knowledge assessment)
The closest motivating result is Kania–Lewicki, arXiv:2604.10771v1, which establishes the projection constant of every hyperplane in a Daugavet space and gives a norm-attainment criterion for an unconstrained minimal projection. The present statement assumes those results and instead solves a different regularized optimization over projection coefficients with bounded Lipschitz seminorm. Hille–Theewis, arXiv:2305.08500v1, supplies general norming sets for combined bounded-Lipschitz norms of signed measures, not this separately constrained minimax projection problem. Related published records address weak-star projection norm nonattainment in Lipschitz spaces, sharp Lipschitz constants of nonlinear metric projections, or signed-measure norms with different function gauges. Their statements neither imply nor coincide with the exact signed-power feasibility law, unique projection, two asymptotic regimes, or the critical fold. Actual record and literature searches disclosed no stronger statement that subsumes this one. The principal residual publication-priority risk is an equivalent older clipped-potential calculation under optimal-transport, regularization, or bounded-Lipschitz duality terminology.

## Mathematical value — PASS
A hyperplane's projection constant being unattained gives no rate at which implementable regular coefficients can approach the infimum. The Lipschitz-budget problem asks for that rate with an intrinsic coefficient, rather than an arbitrary finite-dimensional slice. The answer simultaneously identifies a sharp minimum regularity, a unique optimal projection, a tunable continuum of polynomial convergence exponents controlled by the density near its sign change, and a universal square-root cusp at feasibility. This is a quantified boundary of a natural nonattainment phenomenon, not merely a finite calculation. The theorem does not extend to all nonattaining measures, asymmetric densities, general Daugavet spaces, or uniformly in the singular density parameter.
