## Correctness: PASS
The statement fixes the probability-normalized weighted Bergman measure, a hyperbolic-area-preserving radial boundary coordinate, and a fixed real bounded zero-mean perturbation. The exact normalized monomial moments give a simple isolated leading eigenvalue and the gap \(\lambda_0-\lambda_n=(1-T)^c S_n(T)>0\). Uniform geometric decay of the moving-domain moment matrix gives an analytic trace-class family, so the Rayleigh–Schrödinger simple-eigenvalue second-derivative formula is applicable. Direct endpoint differentiation and Parseval give the claimed coefficient for every positive Fourier mode. The strict positivity proof for \(P_n(T)\), based on its single sign change and positive sum of coefficients at \(T=1\), proves strict ordering for every \(c>0\) and \(0<T<1\). The independent finite-matrix checker reports 212 supplementary assertions, including the exact unweighted cosine-two coefficient \(1/32\). These finite checks are supplemental, not evidence of an infinite-dimensional proof.

## Originality: PASS, with historical-priority risk
Ramos–Tilli (arXiv:2205.07998, initially public 2022-05-16) prove Faber–Krahn optimality and uniqueness for Cauchy-wavelet concentration, but not the Fourier-resolved shape Hessian of the *optimized largest localization eigenvalue*. Gómez–Kalaj–Melentijević–Ramos (arXiv:2411.16010), notably Section 4.1 and Lemma 4.7, provide global quantitative stability and a different second variation obtained by perturbing an analytic signal and readjusting its optimal superlevel set. This does not explicitly state the present fixed-region operator-norm perturbation spectrum or its strictly least-curved second angular mode. Fock/STFT stability and Hilbert–Schmidt purity results concern different spaces or invariants. A hidden equivalence or previously unrecognized derivation from the global function-side result remains a substantial but not presently decisive priority risk. Searching mathematical record summaries using phrases such as “hyperbolic disk top localization eigenvalue shape perturbation Fourier Hessian” found no exact equivalent claim; search absence is not proof of uniqueness. The preceding results about related stability and second variations are credited.

## Mathematical value: PASS
The maximal concentration eigenvalue is the natural operator-norm objective in the analytic Cauchy-wavelet Faber–Krahn problem. An exact local second-order form determines the mode-by-mode sensitivity of that objective and the optimal quantitative local coefficient once area-preserving translation modes are quotiented out. It is not a routine restatement of the known global maximizing-disk theorem, since that does not determine the full ordered Hessian or its exact second-harmonic coefficient.
