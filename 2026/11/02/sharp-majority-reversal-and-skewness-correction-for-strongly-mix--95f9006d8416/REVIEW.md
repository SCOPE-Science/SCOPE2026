# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For each initial state with an interior mean and for every \(n\ge2\), \(r>0\), \(\mu>r\) and \(0<\theta<1\), the invariant cube and \(r\)-Lipschitz bound imply exponential synchronization of all patches. An exact central-moment identity reduces the mean trajectory to a scalar cubic perturbed by exponentially decaying forcing. An energy argument proves convergence to one of the three uniform equilibria. Cooperative parameter comparison, continuous dependence and the open basins of the two hyperbolic endpoint equilibria yield exactly one threshold and the two outcomes on its sides. The exponentially convergent integral identity for the critical trajectory gives a controlled first-order moment expansion. For binary occupancy data, the explicit boundary drift at \(\theta=p\) and a no-return argument yield the global, strict sign law; its balanced case follows from exact complement symmetry. Exact-rational checks and representative ODE integration supplement these arguments.

Limit: numerical checks do not prove the assertions for all dimensions, rates, or initial states. The analytical derivation is conditional on complete uniform mixing and \(\mu>r\).

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence; historical priority provisional

Kang and Lanchier's publicly available 2010 work (arxiv:1003.0121v1) treats deterministic two-patch Allee growth and discusses high-mixing occupation-fraction heuristics, but does not state the finite-rate, arbitrary-initial-data third-moment correction or the all-\(n\) binary majority-sign theorem. Lanchier (arxiv:1301.0125v1) addresses a different stochastic interacting-patch process. Gyllenberg, Hemminki and Tammaru (1999; doi:10.1006/tpbi.1999.1430) addresses a biparental two-patch model; only its abstract was accessible for comparison. Local two-patch equilibrium-count results do not imply an initial-data-dependent, all-\(n\) finite-mixing transition. The precise moment coefficient, global sign for unequal binary occupation and same-mean/same-variance skewness separation remain distinguishable from the inspected results.

Risk: prior singular-perturbation or center-manifold analyses could contain an equivalent averaged-threshold correction. The incomplete access to some older literature prevents a guarantee of historical novelty.

## Value — PASS

For a classical strong-Allee metapopulation, the result turns a qualitative rapid-mixing heuristic into a sharp establishment/extinction boundary, identifies the direction of finite-rate correction according to the initially colonized fraction, and shows explicitly why mean occupancy and variance alone can be insufficient for predicting establishment. The first nontrivial majority effect requires at least three patches; it distinguishes model outcomes on either side of the balanced initial state. This is useful for assessing idealized rescue and introduction experiments, but is not an empirical conservation claim.
