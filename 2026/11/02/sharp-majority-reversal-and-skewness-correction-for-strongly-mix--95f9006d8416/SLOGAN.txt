For identical Allee-growth patches mixed faster than their intrinsic growth rate, every initial population has one sharp threshold between global establishment and extinction. When a fraction \(p\) of patches starts full and the rest empty, finite mixing raises this threshold if \(p<1/2\), lowers it if \(p>1/2\), and leaves it exactly unchanged at \(p=1/2\); the first correction is \(\frac r\mu\frac{p(1-p)(1-2p)}6\), while for arbitrary initial densities it depends separately on variance and skewness.
