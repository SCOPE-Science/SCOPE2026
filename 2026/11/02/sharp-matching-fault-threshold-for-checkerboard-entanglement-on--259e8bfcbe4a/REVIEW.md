# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The claim is established for all even integers \(d\ge4\), and for every matching of at most \(d-1\) cube edges, by (i) the graph-state cut-rank identity, (ii) a self-contained nilpotent group-algebra computation of the cut rank, (iii) the established full binary hypercube adjacency-code minimum distance \(d\), and (iv) a proved low-rank update lemma. At the threshold, the dependent matching has a fully solved kernel; a modified matching has the two required quotient independences. These steps verify the stated exact ranks, not merely an experimental extrapolation. The supplied independent finite-field checker validates 4031 rank assertions at finite sizes. The 2007 code-distance theorem is explicitly cited as an existing nontrivial premise.

## Originality — PASS (historically provisional)

Hein–Eisert–Briegel (2003) and Hein et al. (2006) give the general graph-state Schmidt/cut-rank relation and change bounds. Key–Seneviratne (2007) gives the minimum distance and dimension of the even-cube adjacency code; Seneviratne (2016) reiterates those code facts. None of these full inspected texts presents the matching-fault additive interval or the two different outcomes at the first threshold. General statements are not treated as automatically non-covering: the rank identity plus the code-distance theorem *enable* the current argument, but they do not directly give the two threshold configurations or the entire graph-state statement without additional reasoning. A previously recorded even-torus single-bond result covers the \(d=4,k=1\) instance; no novelty is claimed for that subcase. Unrecognized specialized fault/graph-state papers may still cover the theorem; no independent priority attestation exists.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Vertex-disjoint missing entangling gates are a natural correlated graph-state preparation fault family. The result provides a complete uniform entropy law *for all placements up to a sharp defect-count threshold*, and exactly exhibits the first two distinct placement classes at that threshold. It connects error-pattern weight to a nontrivial binary-code distance and resolves a concrete design/boundary question rather than an arbitrary finite table or an incremental numerical example.

## Evidence and limitations

Primary sources were inspected in full available text (the 2003 and 2006 arXiv HTML full texts, the author-uploaded 2007 full article, and the 2016 full journal PDF). The code minimum distance is a published theorem, not independently re-proved from scratch here. Computational tests are finite and are not themselves infinite-dimensional proof.
