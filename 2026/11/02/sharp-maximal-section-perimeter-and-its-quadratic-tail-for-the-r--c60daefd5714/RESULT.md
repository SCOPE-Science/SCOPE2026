# Sharp maximal section perimeter and its quadratic tail for the rhombic dodecahedron

## Finding

Let
\[
Q=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb R^3:\ |x|+|y|\le2,\ |x|+|z|\le2,\ |y|+|z|\le2\}.
\]
For a unit vector \(n\in S^2\), write \(P(n)=\operatorname{Perimeter}(Q\cap n^\perp)\). Then
\[
\boxed{P(n)\le8\sqrt2,}
\]
with equality precisely when \(n\) is one of the six signed coordinate-axis vectors. If \(N\) is uniformly distributed on \(S^2\), then, as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\),
\[
\boxed{\Pr\{P(N)>8\sqrt2-\varepsilon\}
 =\frac{3(3+2\sqrt2)}{64\pi}\,\varepsilon^2+o(\varepsilon^2).}
\]
Moreover, in the local chart \(n=(\sqrt{1-u^2-v^2},u,v)\) near the positive first coordinate axis,
\[
\boxed{P(n)=8\sqrt2-8(\sqrt2-1)(|u|+|v|)
 +O(u^2+v^2).}
\]
The leading local deficit is an \(\ell_1\) cusp, not a smooth quadratic maximum. Both the extremum and tail coefficient are exact for the displayed scaling of \(Q\).

## Assumptions and scope

The section is the *solid planar section* of the three-dimensional convex polytope \(Q\), not its planar orthogonal projection. Perimeter is intrinsic Euclidean length in the section plane. The orientation distribution is Haar-uniform on unoriented planes, equivalently uniform on \(S^2\) with antipodal normals identified. All bounds include exceptional section directions. The tail statement concerns the upper endpoint only; no full perimeter density is claimed.

## Proof

**1. Zonotope representation and global maximum.** Define the four regular-tetrahedral generators
\[
g_1=(1,1,1),\quad g_2=(1,-1,-1),\quad
g_3=(-1,1,-1),\quad g_4=(-1,-1,1).
\]
The Minkowski sum \(Z=\sum_{j=1}^4[-g_j/2,g_j/2]\) has vertices among half-sign sums of the four generators; these are the origin, the eight sign triples \((\pm1,\pm1,\pm1)\), and the six axial points \(\pm2e_i\). Its convex hull is exactly \(Q\), as follows directly from the three displayed pair-absolute-value inequalities. Hence \(Z=Q\).

Fix a plane \(H=n^\perp\). Its section \(Q\cap H\) is contained in the orthogonal projection \(\pi_H Q\). By Cauchy's planar perimeter formula (the integral of width), convex inclusion implies
\[
P(n)\le\operatorname{Perimeter}(\pi_H Q).
\]
Planar perimeter is Minkowski additive for convex planar bodies. A projected segment generated by \(g_j\) has perimeter contribution \(2\|\pi_H g_j\|\), so
\[
\operatorname{Perimeter}(\pi_H Q)
 =2\sum_{j=1}^4\sqrt{3-(n\cdot g_j)^2}.
\]
The tetrahedral tight-frame identity \(\sum_{j=1}^4g_jg_j^\mathsf T=4I_3\) gives \(\sum_j[3-(n\cdot g_j)^2]=8\). Cauchy--Schwarz therefore bounds the last sum by \(4\sqrt2\). This proves \(P(n)\le8\sqrt2\).

Equality in Cauchy--Schwarz requires \((n\cdot g_j)^2=1\) for every \(j\). Writing \(n=(a,b,c)\), equality of these four squares yields \(a(b+c)=b(a+c)=c(a+b)=0\), together with the analogous equalities obtained by pairwise subtraction. If two coordinates were nonzero, these equations would force their product to vanish; hence exactly one coordinate is nonzero. Conversely, for \(n=\pm e_i\), the section is a planar diamond with vertices \(\pm2e_j,\pm2e_k\) and perimeter \(8\sqrt2\). These are exactly the six maximizing normals.

**2. An exact local polygon formula.** By signed-coordinate symmetries, near \(e_1\) take \(n=(a,u,v)\), where \(u,v\ge0\), \(a=\sqrt{1-u^2-v^2}\), and \(a>u+v\). On the section plane, \(x=-(uy+vz)/a\). Projection to the \((y,z)\)-coordinates identifies the section with the planar diamond \(|y|+|z|\le2\), whose four axial corners are clipped by the remaining two inequalities. The induced Euclidean line element is
\[
ds^2=dy^2+dz^2+\frac{(u\,dy+v\,dz)^2}{a^2}.
\]
The uncut diamond's perimeter in this metric is \(4(s_-+s_+)\), where
\[
s_- =\sqrt{2+(u-v)^2/a^2},\qquad
s_+=\sqrt{2+(u+v)^2/a^2}.
\]
At the corner \((y,z)=(2,0)\), the two clipping endpoints on the old diamond edges have nonnegative axial distances
\[
p=\frac{2u}{a+u-v},\qquad q=\frac{2u}{a+u+v}.
\]
The two removed boundary pieces have lengths \(p s_-\) and \(q s_+\); the new chord has length
\[
T(u,v)=\sqrt{2(p^2+q^2)+\frac{[(u-v)p-(u+v)q]^2}{a^2}}.
\]
Thus the perimeter decrease at this corner is
\[
D(u,v)=p s_-+q s_+-T(u,v).
\]
There are two opposite \(y\)-axis corners and two opposite \(z\)-axis corners. Exchanging \(u,v\) treats the latter, giving the **exact**, not asymptotic, identity whenever \(a>u+v\):
\[
P(a,u,v)=4(s_-+s_+)-2D(u,v)-2D(v,u).
\]
The clipping interpretation remains valid on \(u=0\) or \(v=0\) by continuity. It follows directly from the inequalities that no other edge of the diamond is removed in this regime: the extra constraints become active only on disjoint neighborhoods of its four corners.

**3. Uniform cusp.** As \((u,v)\to(0,0)\) in the nonnegative quadrant, \(s_\pm=\sqrt2+O(u^2+v^2)\), \(p=q=2u+O(u(u+v))\), and \(T(u,v)=4u+O(u(u+v))\), uniformly including \(u=0\). Consequently
\[
D(u,v)=4(\sqrt2-1)u+O(u(u+v)).
\]
Substitution in the exact perimeter formula gives
\[
P(a,u,v)=8\sqrt2-8(\sqrt2-1)(u+v)+O(u^2+v^2).
\]
Reflection symmetries replace \(u,v\) by \(|u|,|v|\), proving the stated expansion for all local directions.

**4. Haar endpoint probability.** Set \(C=8(\sqrt2-1)\). The preceding expansion shows that in a chart at an axial maximum the strict-tail set \(P(n)>8\sqrt2-\varepsilon\) has, after scaling \((u,v)=(\varepsilon/C)(s,t)\), limiting planar region \(|s|+|t|<1\), of area \(2\). The spherical area element is \((1-u^2-v^2)^{-1/2}\,du\,dv=(1+O(u^2+v^2))du\,dv\). Uniqueness of the six global maximizers and compactness ensure no other directions contribute for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\). Thus the total spherical area is
\[
6\cdot2(\varepsilon/C)^2+o(\varepsilon^2).
\]
Divide by \(4\pi\) and simplify \(C^{-2}=(3+2\sqrt2)/64\) to obtain the claimed probability asymptotic. Passing from oriented sphere to unoriented planes does not change this ratio.

## Verification

The included standalone Python script `check.py` constructs the fourteen vertices and twenty-four edges from first principles, computes the planar intersection polygon by a two-dimensional convex hull, and verifies the sharp upper bound for 12,000 deterministic-seed random directions. It checks the four coordinate-axis cases, fifteen small-displacement cusp comparisons, and five hundred comparisons between the exact local clipping formula and the independent edge-intersection construction. All checks passed. These finite checks are not used as proofs of uniqueness, the global bound, or the limiting probability; those are analytical consequences of the preceding argument.

## Relationship to prior work

Makai and Martini's 2015 investigation concerns maximal quermassintegrals of hyperplane sections and differentiable perturbations, rather than the exact directional maximum or Haar upper tail for this polyhedral section perimeter. König and Koldobsky's 2018 theorem concerns maximal perimeters of *cube* sections in all dimensions; it does not specialize to this rhombic-dodecahedral body. Živaljević's 2013 study identifies simplex-generated zonotopes including the regular rhombic dodecahedron, but proves polyhedral-curtain and illumination statements, not the present section-perimeter laws. The zonotope representation, Cauchy's planar perimeter formula, and Cauchy--Schwarz inequality are classical tools, not newly claimed. The contribution is the sharp equality classification together with the non-smooth local cusp and its exact Haar probability coefficient.

## Limitations

Only the canonical regular rhombic dodecahedron in the stated scale is treated; no result for arbitrary affinely deformed rhombic dodecahedra, noncentral sections, or the full perimeter distribution is claimed. The error terms are local, not a uniform global probability estimate at finite thresholds. The result has undergone internal mathematical checking but no independent audit or historical-priority certification. Unindexed or inaccessible historical stereology may contain an equivalent statement.
