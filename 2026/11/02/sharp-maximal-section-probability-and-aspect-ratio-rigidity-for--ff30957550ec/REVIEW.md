# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The full theorem concerns right regular even-sided prisms with at least six base edges and Haar-uniform central plane normals. Section sides are enumerated by testing all vertical, top, and bottom edge crossings; one maximal section arises precisely between the two largest absolute base-vertex projections. The normalized azimuth integral yields the exact inverse-sine formula; a derivative sign factor \(1-3\cos^2\theta\,t^2\) establishes the unique global optimum. The \(m^{-2}\) asymptotic follows from an order-three inverse-sine cancellation. The independent verifier checks the geometric crossing predicate on random normal vectors, and finite computations are not the proof for infinite parameters.

## Originality — PASS, with priority risk

Brandenburg, De Loera and Meroni, arXiv:2304.14239v1, develops polytope slicing chambers but does not state this all-height prism probability or rigidity. Ambrus and Gárgyán's central-cube section research studies volumes instead of face counts. The published rectangular-box central-section theorem includes the square-base case and is explicitly excluded from the new assertion. The published regular-prism mean-square width theorem treats a distinct observable. Searches for stereological and maximal central prism section probabilities did not identify a dominating formula, but an older or unindexed equivalent result remains possible; priority has not been independently established.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The infinite family supplies an exact combinatorial probability for Haar-random central sections, a unique maximizing geometry, and a sharp asymptotic optimal success rate \(\pi^2/(6\sqrt3\,m^2)\). These identify geometric behavior not supplied by the already-known cube special case and can serve as benchmarks for random section algorithms.

## Scientific limitations

The claim excludes triangular-base and square-base prisms, off-center cutting planes and irregular/odd-sided bases. It does not classify all other section side counts. Numerical checks are diagnostics, not independent certification. Historical priority has residual uncertainty.
