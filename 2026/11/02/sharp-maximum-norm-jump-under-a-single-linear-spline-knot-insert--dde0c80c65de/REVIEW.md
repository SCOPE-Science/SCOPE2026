# Scientific review: one-knot linear-spline projection difference

**Correctness — PASS.** With the orthogonal affine residual \(u_t\) of the positive part \((x-t)_+\), nested orthogonal projection makes the difference a rank-one operator. The explicit two-piece residual is orthogonal to \(1\) and \(x\). Its \(L^1\), \(L^2\) and supremum norms give exactly the stated formula, attained by its sign. Differentiation establishes the unique midpoint minimizer; direct integration for the fixed input \(x^2\) proves the nonzero boundary limit. The embedded independent checker reconstructs the finite-element mass-matrix kernel and passes 905 exact-rational assertions in addition to symbolic moment checks.

**Originality — PASS, qualified.** Material read from Gawlik–Lew (arXiv:1408.4104: introduction, principal theorem, Section 4 and numerical section) concerns function-dependent \(L^2\)/\(H^1\) supercloseness for nearby meshes, not a sharp \(L^\infty\) difference law under a single collapsing knot. Oswald (arXiv:math/0611573: statement and proof of the higher-dimensional counterexample, and the one-dimensional survey) supplies bounds for individual projectors, which do not entail this exact difference profile. Dickopf–Krause (DOI:10.4208/nmtma.2014.1218nm: introduction and descriptions of theory and numerical studies) addresses primarily non-nested transfer operators and other norms. Search comparisons included exact parameter and Franklin-detail aliases; no inspected result asserted this norm formula or its minimax/limit consequences. The largest residual novelty risk is unindexed older research on nonuniform Franklin detail functions and one-knot spline updates. Search absence does not prove publication priority.

**Value — PASS.** The norm is a natural stability measure for a nested one-knot mesh update, not an arbitrary scalar slice. The theorem completely solves the knot-placement minimax for the smallest such update, quantifies a sharp boundary layer in which the difference exceeds the classical upper bound for either individual projector, and exhibits a smooth function witnessing lack of convergence despite collapse of the new interval. This distinguishes robustness for arbitrary bounded observations from smooth-data supercloseness under compatible refinement assumptions. It does not purport to cover general meshes.
