# Self-review

## Correctness

PASS. The precise one-exceptional-good EF1 constraints yield both endpoints, including the necessary \(\lfloor H\rfloor\) for nonintegral special values. The standard balancing exchange proves the product formula; discrete log-concavity gives the endpoint minimum. The approximation proof compares arbitrary optimum special-good counts with an EF1 count and supplies a sharp asymptotic family. A standalone exact-fraction exhaustive checker agrees across 228 finite parameter triples; finite checks are corroboration, not proof of the unbounded claim.

## Originality

PASS on evidence currently accessible, with priority risk. The directly motivating 2026 full manuscript proves uniform item optimality and an epsilon-small-item approximation, neither of which covers the regime of one arbitrarily large exceptional item. The 2017 general constant covers the family but is weaker than the sharp, dimension-dependent factor and supplies no exact phase classification. The 2018 greedy optimal-welfare paper addresses an algorithm, not the welfare of every EF1 outcome. The nearest earlier local mathematical analyses concern online cumulative envy, monetary-transfer envy-freeness, or optimal Lorenz balance, and do not imply this worst-EF1 Nash-welfare statement. Actual semantic research-record searches disclosed no equivalent direct result; coverage of unpublished/unindexed writings is incomplete.

## Value

PASS. The parameterized exact interval and endpoint formula connect a well-motivated uniform-item equality case to the first exceptional-value regime; the welfare loss remains sharply bounded even as the large good tends to infinite value. The result distinguishes inherent EF1 allocation quality from the quality of particular algorithms, and the sharpness example explains the multiplicative price.

## Limitations and closest prior work

The bound is confined to exactly one exceptional good and positive identical additive valuations, not arbitrary heterogeneous valuations. The infimum may require \(H\to\infty\). The accessible literature does not prove absolute novelty, and mathematical validation is self-review only. Sources: Yang et al., arXiv:2609.03846; Barman--Krishnamurthy--Vaish, arXiv:1707.04731 and arXiv:1801.09046.
