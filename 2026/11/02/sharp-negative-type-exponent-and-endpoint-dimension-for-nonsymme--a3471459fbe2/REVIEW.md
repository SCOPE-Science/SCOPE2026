# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The explicit powered-distance matrix follows from all four distance classes and the design identity \(NN^{\mathsf T}=(r-1)I+J\). Singular-space decomposition yields a block-kernel obstruction \(a((k-1)a-k)\) that becomes positive above \(\log_2(k/(k-1))\). At the critical exponent, every coupled two-dimensional mode is strictly negative by the checked determinant estimate, and the remaining constant-population mode is strictly negative by concavity of the power function. This leaves exactly \(b-v\) null directions. Schoenberg's theorem supplies the strict subcritical part and the embedding-dimension interpretation. The attached checker independently reconstructs four sample designs and their graph distances; finite evidence is not the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk.** Robertson's 2020 method for maximal generalized roundness and his 2023 study of Hilbert embeddings provide frameworks but not the Steiner incidence-graph formula. Literature on combinatorial design incidence and neighborhoods has different conclusions. The affine-plane case is already known and not claimed separately; general nonsymmetric Steiner designs include, for example, the binary triple system on \(15\) points with \(35\) blocks, which is not an affine plane. Results on symmetric designs concern \(r=k\) and do not cover the present hypothesis \(r>k\). The arbitrary-design endpoint nullity is also distinct. An unlocated earlier equivalent calculation remains possible, so priority is provisional.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The theorem determines a natural finite-metric Banach/Hilbert embedding invariant across all nonsymmetric Steiner \(2\)-designs, not merely a numerical sample. The critical exponent depends only on block size, whereas the exact endpoint dimension defect depends on the global incidence dependency space; the binary Steiner triple system provides a concrete non-affine instance. These features motivate the exact classification and can guide analysis of other design-incidence metric families.

**Limits.** The theorem concerns real targets, \(k\ge3\), \(r>k\), and unit-edge incidence metrics; it makes no claims about symmetric or weighted cases. Existing general embedding theorems are credited. Numerical tests cannot establish all parameter values.
