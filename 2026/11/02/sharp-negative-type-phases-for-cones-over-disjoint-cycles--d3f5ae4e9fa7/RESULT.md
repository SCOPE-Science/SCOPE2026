# Sharp negative-type phases for cones over disjoint cycles

## Finding
Let \(G=K_1\vee(C_{\ell_1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup C_{\ell_r})\), where \(r\ge1\), every integer \(\ell_i\ge3\), and at least one \(\ell_i\ge4\). The join adds a hub joined to every vertex of the vertex-disjoint cycle union. Set \(n=\sum_i\ell_i\), so \(n\ge4\), and equip \(G\) with its unweighted shortest-path metric. Write \(\wp(G)\) for the supremum of exponents \(p\ge0\) for which the metric has \(p\)-negative type, and define \(q_*=2^{\wp(G)}\).

**Exact threshold and kernel classification.** If at least one \(\ell_i\) is even, then \(q_*=2\). At this endpoint the space of nonzero equality loads (including zero, considered linearly) has dimension equal to the number of even cycles, and is spanned by alternating signed loads on those rims, with zero hub load.

If every \(\ell_i\) is odd, then \(L=\max_i\ell_i\ge5\). Define
\[
q_{\mathrm{local}}=\frac{2\cos(\pi/L)}{2\cos(\pi/L)-1},\qquad
q_{\mathrm{global}}=\frac{2(n-1)}{n-3},\qquad
T_L=\cot^2\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2L}\right).
\]
Then \(q_*=\min\{q_{\mathrm{local}},q_{\mathrm{global}}\}\), and exactly one of the following cases occurs:

* **Localized phase:** \(n<T_L\). Then \(q_*=q_{\mathrm{local}}\), and the endpoint equality-load space has dimension \(2h\), where \(h=\#\{i:\ell_i=L\}\). It is the direct sum of the two-dimensional least-adjacency-eigenvalue Fourier modes on each longest rim; the hub receives load zero.
* **Global phase:** \(n>T_L\). Then \(q_*=q_{\mathrm{global}}\), and the endpoint equality-load space is one-dimensional, generated by the load \((-n,1,\ldots,1)\) with its first coordinate at the hub. This direction involves every vertex.

Equality \(n=T_L\) is impossible for integer \(n\) and odd \(L\ge5\), so the two mechanisms never resonate. Shorter odd cycles affect the answer only through their contribution to the total \(n\) until the global phase takes over.

In all cases, \(G\) has *strict* \(p\)-negative type for \(0\le p<\wp(G)\), has nonstrict \(p\)-negative type at \(p=\wp(G)\), and fails \(p\)-negative type above it. The minimum affine Euclidean dimension of an isometric embedding of the snowflake \((V(G),d^{p/2})\) equals \(n\) for \(p<\wp(G)\) and equals \(n-k\) at its endpoint, where \(k\) is the stated equality-space dimension. Every positive even integer occurs as \(k\) in the localized phase, by taking sufficiently large equal odd rim lengths.

For instance, one seven-cycle with four disjoint triangle rims has \(n=19<T_7\) and retains the local threshold \(q_*=2\cos(\pi/7)/(2\cos(\pi/7)-1)\); adjoining one further triangle gives \(n=22>T_7\), switching to \(q_*=42/19\) with a globally supported equality load. Two seven-cycles give critical dimension drop four; three nine-cycles give drop six. The isolated five-cycle already has the threshold \(q_*=(3+\sqrt5)/2\), as explicitly calculated by Sánchez, and that isolated special value is not a new result.

## Assumptions and scope
All graph distances are unweighted shortest-path distances. The graph has one hub, with disjoint cycles among its other vertices and no other edges. There is no restriction that the cycles have the same length. All measures and quadratic forms are real. A signed load \(\alpha\in\mathbb R^{n+1}\) is zero-sum if \(\sum_v\alpha_v=0\). The metric has \(p\)-negative type when \(\sum_{u,v}\alpha_u\alpha_vd(u,v)^p\le0\) for every zero-sum load, and is strict when the inequality is strict for every nonzero such load. At a nonzero distance, \(d(u,v)^0=1\). By Schoenberg's theorem, the snowflake \(d^{p/2}\) is Hilbert-embeddable exactly when the metric has \(p\)-negative type; the affine dimension is the rank of its anchored Gram matrix.

The case in which all rims are triangles is excluded. Such graphs are clique windmills rather than genuinely cyclic cones, and a single triangle gives the complete graph. The exclusion isolates the structural phase statement from this previously treated family.

## Proof
Write \(q=2^p\) and index the \(n\) non-hub vertices by \(1,\ldots,n\). Let \(A\) be the adjacency matrix of the disjoint union of cycles. Distinct non-hub vertices are at distance \(1\) if adjacent in their cycle and \(2\) otherwise; every hub-to-rim distance equals \(1\). Thus the rim powered-distance matrix is
\[
D_{\mathrm{rim},p}=q(J-I)-(q-1)A.
\]
Every zero-sum load can be written uniquely as \(\alpha=(-\mathbf1^Ty,y)\). Its powered-distance quadratic form is
\[
\alpha^TD_p\alpha
=y^T\left[(q-2)J-qI-(q-1)A\right]y=-2y^TG_p y,
\qquad
G_p=\tfrac12\left[(2-q)J+qI+(q-1)A\right].
\]
Equivalently, \(G_p\) is the Gram matrix anchored at the hub, since \((G_p)_{ij}=(d(o,i)^p+d(o,j)^p-d(i,j)^p)/2\). Therefore negative type is equivalent to \(G_p\succeq0\), and strict negative type is equivalent to \(G_p\succ0\).

Each cycle is \(2\)-regular. Consequently \(\mathbb R^n\) splits orthogonally and invariantly into: (a) the global constant direction \(\mathbf1\); (b) the \((r-1)\)-dimensional space of vectors constant on each cycle but with zero total sum; and (c) the mean-zero Fourier subspaces of each cycle. The corresponding eigenvalues of \(G_p\) are, respectively,
\[
\gamma_0(q)=\frac{2(n-1)-(n-3)q}{2},\quad
\gamma_{\mathrm{between}}(q)=\frac{3q-2}{2},\quad
\gamma_{i,j}(q)=\frac{q+2(q-1)\cos(2\pi j/\ell_i)}2,
\quad1\le j<\ell_i.
\]
These dimensions sum to \(n\). For \(q\ge1\) the between-component eigenvalue is positive. The global eigenvalue first vanishes at \(q_{\mathrm{global}}>2\). If a rim is even, its lowest adjacency eigenvalue is \(-2\), producing \(\gamma_{i,\ell_i/2}=(2-q)/2\); every other eigenvalue stays positive at \(q=2\). This proves the even-rim threshold and kernel.

Suppose all rims are odd and at least one is longer than three. On an odd \(\ell\)-cycle, the smallest adjacency eigenvalue is \(-2\cos(\pi/\ell)\), attained in precisely two real Fourier directions for \(\ell\ge5\). Triangle mean-zero eigenvalues are \(-1\) and do not cross zero. The first local zero therefore occurs at \(q_{\mathrm{local}}\) for the *longest* odd rims, with multiplicity \(2h\). Comparing the two critical values by positive cross-multiplication gives
\[
q_{\mathrm{local}}<q_{\mathrm{global}}
\iff n<\frac{1+\cos(\pi/L)}{1-\cos(\pi/L)}=T_L.
\]
If equality held, then \(2\cos(\pi/L)=2(n-1)/(n+1)\) would be rational. But \(2\cos(\pi/L)=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\), where \(\zeta=e^{i\pi/L}\) is a root of unity, so it is an algebraic integer. Because \(L\ge5\) is odd, it lies strictly between \(1\) and \(2\), whereas a rational algebraic integer must be an integer. Equality is impossible. Hence exactly one critical eigenspace appears. Its explicit Fourier or global-constant eigenvectors give the described signed loads.

Every listed eigenvalue is positive for \(1\le q<q_*\). At \(q_*\) precisely the described eigenspace vanishes, and at least one of its eigenvalues becomes negative for every \(q>q_*\), since its defining affine function of \(q\) has negative slope. This proves the exact interval of negative type. The Gram-rank characterization of minimal affine embedding dimension proves the dimension claims. Finally, for any integer \(h\ge1\), choose \(h\) equal odd cycles of sufficiently large length \(L\); then \(n=hL<T_L\), since \(T_L\sim 4L^2/\pi^2\) as \(L\to\infty\). Their critical nullity is \(2h\), as claimed.

## Verification
The standalone standard-library script `verification/verify.py` independently constructs anchored Gram matrices from shortest-path distances, diagonalizes them numerically using Jacobi rotations, and compares their spectra and zero eigenspace multiplicities with the derived formulas, before/at/after thresholds. It covers cycle lengths from \(3\) to \(9\), with one to three cycles and total rim count at most \(30\), plus longer multicycle examples. Running `python verification/verify.py` produced `VERIFY_OK: 499 independent finite Gram-eigenvalue tests over cone cycles; locality-to-global transition verified`. The numerical tests check the analytical derivation but do not prove the infinitely many graph cases.

## Relationship to prior work
Sánchez (2011) gives a general determinant-and-inverse characterization of the supremal negative-type exponent for any finite metric space, including an exact computation for the isolated five-cycle (Example 2.8). Robertson (2020) gives further general Gram/determinant formulations. Those general mechanisms are acknowledged; the contribution here is the **explicit mixed-cycle phase classification**, exact endpoint equality-load support and multiplicity, the arithmetic exclusion of simultaneous mechanisms, and the arbitrarily large critical embedding-rank drops. Deza and Maehara (1990) give an older graph-truncated-metric treatment via characteristic polynomials and are a substantial potential source of stronger implicit coverage; their primary full text could not be inspected. In particular, novelty is not asserted for the general Gram criterion, for cycle adjacency eigenvalues, for the all-triangle family, or for a single even-rim graph already subsumed by complete-multipartite results.

Ipsen and Mallik (first public preprint January 6, 2022) analyze wheel incidence and Laplacian pseudoinverses using circulant matrices, rather than varying powered graph distance. Balaji, Bapat and Goel (2020 preprint, 2021 journal) establish an ordinary distance-matrix inverse for even-order wheel graphs; this addresses the unpowered metric rather than the exponent-dependent critical phase. The previous results motivate but do not themselves state the joint classification above.

## Limitations
Only disjoint cycle rims and a single universal hub are treated. The statement does not extend to arbitrary graph cones, weighted shortest paths, general diameter-two graphs, or Banach targets other than Hilbert space. A primary older paper on truncated graph metrics was not available in full text; equivalent older coverage remains a residual originality risk. The numerical checker is finite and approximate; the infinite theorem rests on the analytic eigenvalue decomposition and algebraic-integer argument. There is no independent review or certification.
