# Sharp negative-type thresholds of uniformly whiskered cliques

## Finding

For integers \(n\ge3\) and \(r\ge1\), let \(G_{n,r}=K_n\odot\overline K_r\) be the unweighted graph formed by attaching \(r\) distinct degree-one leaves to each vertex of a complete graph on \(n\) vertices. Its metric is the ordinary unit-edge shortest-path distance \(d\). Write \(\alpha=\log_2 3\). There is a unique \(a_{n,r}\in(2,4)\) satisfying
\[
 r\left[(n-3)+(n-1)(a_{n,r}^{\alpha}-2a_{n,r})\right]
 +(r-1)a_{n,r}=0.
\]
The exact supremal negative-type exponent is
\[
 \boxed{\wp(G_{n,r})=\log_2 a_{n,r}.}
\]
In detail, for each real \(p\ge0\), the inequality
\[
\sum_{x,y\in V(G_{n,r})}z_xz_y d(x,y)^p\le0
\qquad\left(\sum_xz_x=0\right)
\]
holds for all real loads precisely for \(0\le p\le\wp(G_{n,r})\); it is strict for nonzero zero-sum loads when \(0\le p<\wp(G_{n,r})\). At the threshold the zero-sum equality space has dimension exactly one, generated by assigning \(-r\) to every original clique vertex and \(+1\) to every pendant vertex. For larger \(p\) that load strictly violates negative type.

Accordingly, the snowflaked distance \(d^{p/2}\) admits an exact real Hilbert embedding precisely for \(0\le p\le\wp(G_{n,r})\). Its minimum affine embedding dimension is \(n(r+1)-1\) below the endpoint and \(n(r+1)-2\) at the endpoint.

The exponent decreases **strictly** in either positive integer parameter (within \(n\ge3\), \(r\ge1\)). Put \(c=3\log3-4\log2>0\), with natural logarithms. The two distinct fixed-parameter limits are
\[
 \wp(G_{n,r})=1+\frac{2}{rcn}+O_r(n^{-2})\quad(n\to\infty),
\qquad
 \wp(G_{n,r})=1+\frac{2}{r[(n-1)c+2\log2]}+O_n(r^{-2})\quad(r\to\infty).
\]

A structural consequence separates the negative-type exponent from the ordinary quadratic embedding constant: for every fixed \(r\), all the graphs \(G_{n,r}\) have the **same already-known unpowered** quadratic embedding constant,
\[
 \operatorname{QEC}(G_{n,r})=\frac{-r-3+\sqrt{r^2+6r+1}}{2},
\]
even though their supremal negative-type exponents strictly decrease with \(n\). In particular, the clique itself admits every negative-type exponent, but attaching private leaves forces a finite exponent between one and two.

## Assumptions and scope

The parameters are integers \(n\ge3\), \(r\ge1\); every original clique edge and every pendant edge has length one. There are \(n(r+1)\) distinct vertices. Leaf pairs at the same parent are distance two, leaves with different parents distance three, and leaves and unrelated parents distance two. We use \(0^p=0\) on the distance-matrix diagonal, including \(p=0\), so that the zero-exponent distance matrix has zero diagonal and ones off diagonal. The result does not assert that \(d^{p/2}\) obeys the metric triangle inequality for every \(p>2\); its Hilbert embedding criterion concerns precisely the finite powered-distance condition in the displayed range.

## Proof

**1. Orthogonal decomposition.** Fix \(p\ge1\), and abbreviate \(a=2^p\), \(b=3^p=a^{\alpha}\). Let \(D_p=[d(x,y)^p]\). In each parent group split the leaf coordinates into their constant direction and the \(r-1\)-dimensional sum-zero subspace. The latter has eigenvalue \(-a\) for \(D_p\), with total multiplicity \(n(r-1)\). On the remaining space use orthonormal coordinates \(x_i\) for the core and \(y_i\) for the normalized leaf-constant direction. Splitting the \(n\) parent indices into constants and mean-zero vectors gives \(n-1\) copies of
\[
 M_{\mathrm{std}}(a)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -1&\sqrt r(1-a)\\
 \sqrt r(1-a)&(r-1)a-rb
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
On the constant parent-index sector, the overall zero-sum constraint leaves only the line \((x_i,y_i)=(-\sqrt r,1)\). Its quadratic form per group is
\[
 G_{n,r}(a)=r\big[(n-3)+(n-1)(b-2a)\big]+(r-1)a,
\]
with squared Euclidean norm \(r+1\). Thus the zero-sum distance form is negative semidefinite if and only if \(G_{n,r}(a)\le0\) and the standard matrix is negative semidefinite; the leaf-contrast sector is automatically negative.

**2. Unique global zero.** Since \(4/3<\alpha=\log_2 3<8/5\), the function \(a\mapsto G_{n,r}(a)\) is strictly convex on \([2,\infty)\). Direct substitution gives \(G_{n,r}(2)=-2\) and \(G_{n,r}(4)=2rn-4>0\). Convexity implies a unique root \(a_{n,r}\in(2,4)\), with negative values before the root and positive values after it.

**3. The standard sectors remain strictly negative.** The determinant of \(M_{\mathrm{std}}(a)\) equals
\[
 F_r(a)=r(a^{\alpha}-a^2+a-1)+a.
\]
It is strictly concave on \([2,4]\), with \(F_r(2)=2\) and \(F_r(4)=4(1-r)\), so it has a unique rightmost zero \(a_s\in(2,4]\), with \(a_s=4\) when \(r=1\), and \(F_r(a)>0\) on \([2,a_s)\). To locate this zero, set \(h(a)=2a^{\alpha}-a^2-2\). The inequalities \(4/3<\alpha<8/5\) imply \(\alpha(\alpha-1)<1\), and therefore \(h''(a)<0\) for \(a\ge2\). Since \(h(2)=h(4)=0\), the function is positive on \((2,4)\). For \(r>1\), at \(a_0=2+2/r\),
\[
 F_r(a_0)=\frac{h(a_0)}{a_0-2}>0,
\]
so \(a_s>a_0\). For \(r=1\) the same non-strict comparison holds, \(a_s=4=a_0\).

At \(F_r(a_s)=0\) one has \(b=(r-1)a_s/r+(a_s-1)^2\), whence
\[
 G_{2,r}(a_s)=a_s[r(a_s-2)-2]\ge0.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 G_{n,r}(a_s)-G_{2,r}(a_s)
 =r(n-2)(1+a_s^{\alpha}-2a_s)>0
\]
for \(n\ge3\), since \(1+2^{\alpha}-4=0\) and \(\alpha a^{\alpha-1}-2>0\) for \(a\ge2\). Consequently \(a_{n,r}<a_s\). At the global root the determinant is strictly positive and the first diagonal entry of \(M_{\mathrm{std}}\) is \(-1\), so every standard block is negative definite. The only zero-sum equality direction is the asserted global load. For all \(a>a_{n,r}\), the global load makes the quadratic form positive.

**4. Smaller powers and affine rank.** The spectra at \(p=1\) are strictly negative on the zero-sum subspace. The classical negative-type nesting theorem (Schoenberg; recalled explicitly in Robertson, Theorem 1.2) makes every lower exponent \(0\le p<1\) strict as well. The same matrix decomposition gives strictness for \(1\le p<\wp\). For a finite powered distance matrix, \(-\tfrac12 J D_pJ\), with \(J=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}/[n(r+1)]\), is the centered Hilbert Gram matrix. Its rank is \(n(r+1)-1\) away from the endpoint, and \(n(r+1)-2\) at it. This proves the embedding statements, including the precise critical affine dimension.

**5. Monotonicity and scaling.** For \(a>2\), the function \(1+a^{\alpha}-2a\) is positive, so increasing \(n\) strictly increases \(G_{n,r}(a)\) and strictly decreases its rightmost root. Writing \(G_{n,r}=rQ_n(a)-a\), at its root \(Q_n(a)=a/r>0\), so increasing \(r\) likewise decreases the root. Taylor expansion of the composite \(p\mapsto G_{n,r}(2^p)\) at \(p=1\) gives \(G_{n,r}(2^1)=-2\) and first derivative
\[
 r(n-1)(3\log3-4\log2)+2(r-1)\log2.
\]
The implicit-function expansion produces the two displayed asymptotics; remainder estimates follow because the second derivative is of the same leading parameter order as the first derivative.

**6. Comparison with the classical unpowered constant.** At \(p=1\), the mean-zero standard block is \(\begin{psmallmatrix}-1&-\sqrt r\\-\sqrt r&-r-2\end{psmallmatrix}\), whose larger eigenvalue is \((-r-3+\sqrt{r^2+6r+1})/2\). The global eigenvalue is \(-2/(r+1)\), and each leaf contrast has eigenvalue \(-2\). The determinant of the standard block shifted by \(+2/(r+1)I\) equals \(-4r/(r+1)^2<0\), proving that the larger standard eigenvalue dominates. It is independent of \(n\), as previously established by ordinary quadratic-embedding methods, whereas the new critical exponent is strictly decreasing in \(n\).

## Verification

A standalone Python checker reconstructs all shortest-path distances using independent breadth-first search, projects the full powered-distance matrices onto the mean-zero subspace, and calculates Gram eigenvalues without using the displayed block formulas. Running `python check_corona.py` with NumPy gave `PASS 1180 independent checks`. The run covers the parameter grid \(2\le n\le8\), \(1\le r\le7\), five subcritical-or-critical exponents for each graph, a supercritical exponent, equality loads, parameter monotonicity, and both asymptotic regimes. The \(n=2\) tree cases are tested only as numerical boundary controls, not claimed as new results. The computer checks are finite and do not constitute a proof for arbitrary parameters; the analytic steps above do.

## Relationship to prior work

Baskoro and Obata (first public version April 17, 2019, §4.5) already treat **bearded complete graphs** for the ordinary quadratic embedding constant; this directly includes the one-leaf case at \(p=1\). Choudhury and Nandi (first public version June 27, 2023, Theorem 1.7 and §4) establish ordinary quadratic embedding constants for star-cluster operations, including the present multileaf corona after identifying the operations. Ferdi, Baskoro, Obata and Santika (first public version December 19, 2025, §5, Examples 5.1–5.3) rederive and extend ordinary corona QEC formulas. The unpowered constant shown above is therefore a comparison **to prior work, not a novelty claim**.

Sánchez (2011, §2–3) gives a general determinant criterion for supremal negative type and an exact complete-bipartite example, but not the asserted uniform-leaf clique corona threshold. Robertson (2023, §1 and §4) treats negative-type embedding foundations and distorted examples for bicliques and Hamming cubes, not these corona metrics. The new claim is the complete two-parameter strict-versus-nonstrict powered-distance classification, its unique global equality load, critical Hilbert dimension, monotonicity, asymptotics, and consequent separation from the known unpowered QEC invariant. The generic finite-matrix criterion by itself does not imply the first crossing or the global-sector dominance proved here.

## Limitations

The asserted new classification is restricted to \(n\ge3\) with uniform integer leaf number \(r\ge1\). The \(n=2\) double-star tree family has been investigated separately and is not included in the novelty claim. The proof does not cover weighted edges, nonuniform numbers of leaves, arbitrary core graphs, or multiple pendant generations. No exhaustive proof of historical novelty is possible; equivalent old formulations under graph-block, hypermetric, or generalized-roundness terminology remain a residual literature risk. Independent mathematical audit has not been performed.
