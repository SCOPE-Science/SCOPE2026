# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The explicit rational fiber follows from integer modulation Parseval, residue decomposition of the rational translation lattice, and absolutely convergent geometric series. Its computed trace and determinant give the correct two eigenvalues; monotonicity in the Fourier fiber modulus and the real-fiber endpoint supplies genuine essential suprema and infima. The unique optimizer follows from a necessary critical-point polynomial, an exact palindromic reduction, and divergence of the condition number at both endpoints. Exact symbolic identities and direct fiber numerical tests are supplied in the executable checker. Risks: numerical sampling is not the global proof; the argument assumes the stated unitary convention and excludes zero decay.

## Originality — PASS, subject to historical priority
The 1995 rational-lattice fiber representation and Janssen's 1996 integer-density calculations are prior art, not new claims. Faulhuber and Shafkulovska's Theorem 2.6 explicitly assumes integer oversampling and does not provide the exact density-\(3/2\) optimizer or sharp bounds. Other accessible general theory addresses existence, decay, or frame criteria rather than this precise extremal quantity. Closest direct line: Faulhuber–Shafkulovska (arXiv:2204.02917); Janssen (1995, 1996). The 2000 Bölcskei–Janssen article was available for comparison in abstract/introduction but not conclusively inspected in all pages; an uncatalogued specialization of general rational-density formulas remains a priority risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The density \(3/2\) is the simplest reduced noninteger rational oversampling factor by denominator and necessarily introduces a coupled matrix fiber, in contrast to the scalar integer-density setting. Exact optimal frame bounds and a uniquely best-conditioned rectangular lattice address the natural quantitative design problem singled out by the foundational integer-density studies. The result provides a rigorously checkable noninteger-density benchmark rather than a numerical optimum or arbitrary parameter slice. It does not assert general rational-density optimality.
