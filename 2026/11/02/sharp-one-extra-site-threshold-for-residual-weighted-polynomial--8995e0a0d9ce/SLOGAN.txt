For residual-based polynomial stencil weights on fully unisolvent sites, \(m+1\) samples force every leave-one-out smoothness indicator to depend on the same scalar defect, so two stencils cannot reverse their relative preference; \(m+2\) samples are the sharp threshold permitting opposite preferences. On equally spaced one-dimensional sites, this obstruction makes the two-endpoint compact Shepard blend exactly independent of the nonlinear indicator correction and leaves an explicit nonzero jump-response value.
