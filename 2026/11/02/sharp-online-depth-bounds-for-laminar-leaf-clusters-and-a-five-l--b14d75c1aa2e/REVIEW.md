# Scientific assessment

The depth-three test follows from the ability to split the positive ancestor chain into two groups of two and the presence of a nonsingleton cluster in the negative branch. The path count and its equality case identify the first threshold and the three attaining shapes; a separate count of all unordered five-leaf shapes gives twelve. The two finite enumerations in the package support these counts but are not needed for the proof.

The twelve total shapes and the three binary shapes are known tree counts. The classification here concerns adaptive rank on fixed-tree clusters, not those counts alone. The older vertex-query height law gives the chain argument and an adaptable regular-tree construction but not the finite irregular-tree onset or its equality case. Static pair shattering is background. The threshold isolates the first divergence of static and adaptive depth on this hierarchy class; no stronger priority or classification is asserted.
