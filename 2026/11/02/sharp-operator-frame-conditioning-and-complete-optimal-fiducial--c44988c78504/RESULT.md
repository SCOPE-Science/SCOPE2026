# Sharp operator-frame conditioning and complete optimal-fiducial classification for the tetrahedral qutrit orbit

## Finding
Let \(G\) be the order-12 subgroup of real orthogonal \(3\times3\) matrices generated by
\[
R=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
D_{\epsilon}=\operatorname{diag}(\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3),
\quad \epsilon_j\in\{\pm1\},\quad\epsilon_1\epsilon_2\epsilon_3=1.
\]
Thus \(G=\{D_{\epsilon}R^k:0\le k<3\}\), a faithful three-dimensional representation of the tetrahedral rotation group. For any unit \(v=(a,b,c)^T\in\mathbb C^3\), set \(P_g=(gv)(gv)^*\) and let
\[
\mathcal A_v:\operatorname{Herm}(3)\longrightarrow\mathbb R^{12},\qquad
\mathcal A_v(X)=\bigl(\operatorname{tr}(XP_g)\bigr)_{g\in G}.
\]
The sharp condition-number bound and equivalent sharp lower frame bound are
\[
\inf_{\|v\|=1}\kappa(\mathcal A_v)=2,
\qquad
\sup_{\|v\|=1}\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal A_v^*\mathcal A_v)=1,
\]
where the condition number is infinite if the map is not injective. **Every** unit optimal generator, and no other, satisfies the exact pair of equations
\[
\boxed{\quad |a|^4+|b|^4+|c|^4=\tfrac12,
\qquad a^2\overline b^{\,2}+b^2\overline c^{\,2}+c^2\overline a^{\,2}=0.\quad}
\]
For exactly these generators the twelve labelled projectors form a uniform complex projective \(2\)-design and the frame operator satisfies
\[
\sum_{g\in G}P_g\otimes P_g=I\otimes I+F,
\qquad
(\mathcal A_v^*\mathcal A_v)(X)=X+\operatorname{tr}(X)I,
\]
with \(F(u\otimes w)=w\otimes u\). Its Hermitian-operator eigenvalues are \(4\) once and \(1\) eight times. Consequently, for every Hermitian \(X\), its twelve real intensities \(y_g=\operatorname{tr}(XP_g)\) give the **exact reconstruction**
\[
\boxed{\quad X=\sum_{g\in G}\left(y_g-\tfrac1{16}\sum_{h\in G}y_h\right)P_g.\quad}
\]
An explicit generator attaining every equality is
\[
v_*=\left(\sqrt{\tfrac23},\ \frac{\sqrt7+3i}{4\sqrt6},\ \frac{\sqrt7+3i}{4\sqrt6}\right)^T.
\]
There are twelve distinct projective orbit points for any optimal generator. No real generator produces an informationally complete complex-Hermitian measurement frame.

## Assumptions and scope
The complex vector norm is Euclidean; \(\operatorname{Herm}(3)\) is a real nine-dimensional Hilbert space with inner product \(\langle X,Y\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(XY)\). The displayed twelve outcomes are labelled and counted with their group multiplicity. The condition number refers to this **unweighted** map on all Hermitian matrices, not to nonlinear vector phase retrieval or a statistical risk functional. The coefficient equations concern all complex unit generators, including those with zeros; global phase and replacing \(v\) by a group translate do not change the conclusion. An orbit with repeated projective points is allowed as an input but cannot be optimal. Every statement is exact; neither noise stability beyond the operator condition number nor minimality among all possible non-group-covariant measurements is asserted.

## Proof
**1. The first moment and the universal spectral obstruction.** The four diagonal sign matrices eliminate the off-diagonal entries of \(vv^*\). Averaging its three cyclic translates gives
\[
\sum_{g\in G}P_g=4I
\]
for every unit vector. Hence the self-adjoint positive operator \(\mathcal S_v=\mathcal A_v^*\mathcal A_v\) has eigenvalue \(4\) on \(I\). Since each \(P_g\) is a rank-one projector, its Hilbert–Schmidt norm squared is one, giving
\[
\operatorname{tr}_{\operatorname{Herm}(3)}\mathcal S_v=\sum_{g\in G}\operatorname{tr}(P_g^2)=12.
\]
The remaining eight eigenvalues therefore sum to \(8\), with minimum at most one. Because the largest eigenvalue is at least four, every injective \(\mathcal A_v\) satisfies \(\kappa(\mathcal A_v)\ge2\). The lower frame bound cannot exceed one. Equality in either sharp bound forces all remaining eight eigenvalues to equal one and hence
\[
\mathcal S_v(X)=X+\operatorname{tr}(X)I
\]
for every Hermitian \(X\). Conversely this identity gives the required spectrum and both equalities. In particular it is enough to characterize its solutions.

**2. A complete calculation on the symmetric tensor square.** Put \(\psi_v=v\otimes v\in\operatorname{Sym}^2(\mathbb C^3)\), and define the orbit second-moment operator
\[
M_v=\frac1{12}\sum_{g\in G}\bigl|(g\otimes g)\psi_v\bigr\rangle\bigl\langle(g\otimes g)\psi_v\bigr|.
\]
An orthonormal basis of the six-dimensional symmetric tensor square consists of the three diagonal vectors \(e_j\otimes e_j\) and the three cross vectors \((e_j\otimes e_k+e_k\otimes e_j)/\sqrt2\), with unordered pairs \(\{j,k\}=\{1,2\},\{2,3\},\{3,1\}\). The four-element diagonal sign subgroup acts trivially on the diagonal sector and by **three distinct nontrivial characters** on the cross sector. Therefore its average eliminates all mixed blocks and all cross-sector off-diagonals. The cyclic permutation acts transitively on the three cross basis vectors. Let
\[
Q=|a|^4+|b|^4+|c|^4,
\qquad
U=a^2\overline b^{\,2}+b^2\overline c^{\,2}+c^2\overline a^{\,2}.
\]
It follows directly that the cross block of \(M_v\) is \((1-Q)I_3/3\). For the diagonal block, the cyclic average of \(zz^*\), where \(z=(a^2,b^2,c^2)^T\), has diagonal entries \(Q/3\) and cyclic off-diagonal entries \(U/3\) and \(\overline U/3\) (orientation merely exchanges these two). There are no other terms.

The uniform projective \(2\)-design equation in dimension three is
\[
M_v=\frac{1}{6}I_{\operatorname{Sym}^2(\mathbb C^3)},
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
\frac1{12}\sum_gP_g\otimes P_g=\frac{I\otimes I+F}{12}.
\]
By the complete block calculation, it holds **if and only if** \(Q=1/2\) and \(U=0\). This proves both necessity and sufficiency of the two polynomial constraints, including all degenerate coordinate cases.

**3. Equivalence of tightness and the design moment.** For any Hermitian \(X,Y\),
\[
\langle X,\mathcal S_v(Y)\rangle
=\sum_g\operatorname{tr}(XP_g)\operatorname{tr}(YP_g)
=\operatorname{tr}\big[(X\otimes Y)\sum_gP_g\otimes P_g\big].
\]
The identity \(\operatorname{tr}[(X\otimes Y)(I+F)]=\operatorname{tr}(X)\operatorname{tr}(Y)+\operatorname{tr}(XY)\) implies that the design equation yields \(\mathcal S_v(Y)=Y+\operatorname{tr}(Y)I\). Conversely, if that superoperator identity holds for all Hermitian \(X,Y\), the same trace pairing forces \(\sum_gP_g\otimes P_g=I+F\), since simple Hermitian tensors span the Hermitian operators on the tensor product. Thus there is no additional equality case outside the displayed quartic equations.

**4. Explicit attainment, distinctness, and inversion.** For \(v_*\), both last coordinates have squared modulus \(1/6\), and the first has squared modulus \(2/3\). Thus \(Q=4/9+2/36=1/2\). Moreover
\[
\left(\frac{\sqrt7+3i}{4\sqrt6}\right)^2
=\frac{-1+3i\sqrt7}{48},
\]
so \(U=2(2/3)(-1/48)+1/36=0\). This exhibits a unit generator meeting the sharp bound. From the design identity, \(\mathcal S_v\) is injective and its spectrum is precisely \(4,1,\ldots,1\). Any projective orbit has cardinality dividing \(12\), while injectivity of a rank-one frame on the nine-dimensional Hermitian space requires at least nine distinct projectors. The only divisor of \(12\) at least nine is \(12\); thus all optimal orbit points are distinct. Finally \(\sum_gy_g=4\operatorname{tr}(X)\) by the first moment, and \(\sum_gy_gP_g=X+\operatorname{tr}(X)I\); substitution and \(\sum_gP_g=4I\) yield the stated reconstruction formula.

If \(v\) is real up to overall phase, all \(P_g\) are real symmetric and fail to span imaginary-skew Hermitian directions. In fact the quartic constraints are already contradictory for real coordinates, because \(U=(1-Q)/2=1/4\) under \(Q=1/2\).

## Verification
The standalone file `check_a4.py` reproduces the twelve group matrices, verifies closure and orthogonality, constructs the symmetric tensor-square representation, and checks the general two-block twirling formula for three unrelated exact complex generators. It then verifies the two quartic equalities and projective \(2\)-design identity for \(v_*\), the nine-dimensional moment identity, the first moment, and exact reconstruction and superoperator identities on a complete nine-element Hermitian basis. The command is `python3 check_a4.py`, with SymPy required; the script outputs `ALL_CHECKS_PASS 34`. This is an algebraic cross-check; the proof above provides the universal quantifiers and equality classification, which no finite computation alone could establish.

The selected orbit is not the union of four mutually unbiased bases: its overlap spectrum includes \(\operatorname{tr}(P_gP_h)=1/9\) for a distinct pair, whereas such a union in dimension three has only distinct-pair overlaps \(0\) and \(1/3\). The result is therefore not merely a relabelling of the familiar four-basis construction.

## Relationship to prior work
Scott's work establishes the general tight informationally complete measurement/projective-design equivalence, optimality theory, and reconstruction principle [1]. The general orbit-invariant-polynomial criterion is given by Mohammadpour and Waldron, especially their Lemma 2.1 [2]. Innocenti et al. also develop the general tight measurement-frame equivalence [3]. These **general principles are prior work**, not claimed here. The specific contribution is the exact **three-dimensional tetrahedral representation**, its complete complex-fiducial quartic locus, a concrete algebraic optimal seed, the sharp scalar frame optimum within this orbit family, and its explicit twelve-outcome reconstruction. The nearest available orbit-design treatment [2] discusses binary tetrahedral representations on \(\mathbb C^2\), distinct from the order-12 rotation-group representation on \(\mathbb C^3\) here. Elzenaar and Waldron's twelve-point projective family in \(\mathbb R^4\) [4] is a real projective design problem, not complex-Hermitian qutrit tomography.

## Limitations
The result is a deterministic exact linear measurement theorem. It does not characterize experimental noise, nonlinear pure-state phase retrieval, optimality among all twelve-projector ensembles regardless of group, or projective-design strength above order two. General projective-design theory could contain an unrecognized specialized corollary; exhaustive historical priority and independent mathematical audit are not claimed. The symbolic checks are finite algebraic tests and are not replacements for the proof.
