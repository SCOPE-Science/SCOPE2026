The continuous vector field has a local C¹ solution even for 1<p<2. For two solutions the derivative of half the squared mass norm of their difference is the negative sum of conductances times products of edge-difference increments and flux increments, hence is nonpositive by monotonicity. This gives uniqueness without local Lipschitz regularity. Maximal coordinates have nonpositive and minimal coordinates nonnegative derivative, so boundedness gives global continuation. At each nonconstant state the right derivative of max−min is the maximum of the velocity difference over active ordered extreme pairs. Normalize the range to [0,1]. For any pair its direct edge contributes the sum of the two outgoing rates to the decrement; exclusive neighbors can each cost zero, and each common neighbor contributes a(1−s)^(p−1)+b s^(p−1). Its minimum is min(a,b) at an endpoint for p≤2; for p>2 strict convexity gives s/(1−s)=(a/b)^(1/(p−2)) and the stated power-mean value. There is no coupling between these choices at different neighbors. ## value

PASS

The diameter of the current state is a natural consensus observable. Evaluating and attaining its worst initial decrement gives a quantitative answer that a general Lyapunov or contractivity criterion does not give. In the superquadratic regime it identifies a change of active vertex pair and an explicit scale loss on noncomplete diameter-two graphs; for arbitrary conductances and masses the asymptotic constant is a weighted common-neighbor overlap. This is a useful sharp invariant of the standard nonlinear flow, without claiming a sharp fixed-time envelope or novelty for the known linear diameter test.
