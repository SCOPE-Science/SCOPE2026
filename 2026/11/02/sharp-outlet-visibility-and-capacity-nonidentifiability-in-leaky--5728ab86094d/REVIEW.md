# Mathematical review

## Correctness

PASS. Positive leakage and spill coefficients make each equilibrium scalar balance strictly monotone; upward elimination gives a unique positive-part recursion. Induction gives the precise convex and piecewise-affine forcing response. Under distinct activation intensities, the local derivative jump is positive and is multiplied at each already-active downstream vertex, while the first inactive vertex blocks it. The capacity realization recursively assigns strictly positive thresholds and creates exactly any desired number of visible activations. An open product set of nonroot capacities coupled to a uniquely chosen root capacity proves that the complete outlet response can be identical across an \((n-1)\)-dimensional family. Exact-rational tests support but do not replace the proof.

## Originality

PASS within the inspected sources, with historical-priority limitations. Tromp-van Meerveld and McDonnell (2006) give physical connectivity evidence; Graham and McDonnell (2010) implement numerical threshold and leakage dynamics; Chu et al. (2015) document threshold-connected runoff in experiments; and McDonnell et al. (2021) synthesize connectivity. None of their inspected material states the exact derivative-jump test, sharp attainable kink counts, or full-curve capacity nonidentifiability for the specified steady tree model. Rolnick and Kording (2019) have general ReLU reconstruction machinery, but its visible-intersecting-boundary assumptions do not cover the scalar hidden-boundary regime. The full 2013 Chu et al. article was not inspected, so older exact coverage remains a possibility; search failure is not a proof of historical priority.

## Value

PASS. Outlet runoff thresholds have physical value as potential signatures of subsurface or surface connectivity. The theorem establishes a sharp, testable limitation on interpreting them: a physically present storage transition can be invisible at the outlet, and even a continuum of different positive internal capacities can yield the exact same full steady response. The all-size visibility rule and realizability classification give structural guidance for what additional internal sensors may be necessary. This is stronger than an isolated numerical example, while remaining a deliberately restricted mechanistic benchmark.

## Closest literature and boundaries

The key distinction from physical runoff studies is the exact steady-state all-tree classification; the key distinction from general ReLU identifiability is that one scalar forcing may hide all but one activation boundary. This does not establish novelty relative to every possible mathematical theorem on positive-part networks. Dynamic stormflow, nonlinear permeability, heterogeneous event timing, and finite noisy measurements are out of scope.
