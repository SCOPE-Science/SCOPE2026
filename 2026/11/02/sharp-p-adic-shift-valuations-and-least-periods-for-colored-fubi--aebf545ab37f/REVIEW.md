# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The coefficient identity follows by formally expanding the colored ordered-partition exponential generating function in \(h=t+1\) and using vanishing of finite differences. Its resulting moment sum converges over \(\mathbb Z_p\). For an even shift, the base-two term has valuation \(s+\nu_p(2^q-1)\); every other prime-unit term has strictly greater valuation by the primitive-root and lifting hypotheses, and every nonunit base vanishes beyond the stated index threshold. Odd shifts are ruled out modulo \(p\). The exact modular least period follows from the multiplicative order of two modulo prime powers. All quantified cases including low modulus powers and shift parity have been reviewed. The verifier gives 228 independent Stirling comparisons, 108 moment-expansion comparisons, 1596 sharp shifts, and 45 period/minimality tests. Finite checks support but do not constitute the proof.

## Originality — PASS (residual risk)

Poonen (1988) and Mező (2013) treat unweighted Fubini numbers and restricted variants; the weighted-polynomial parameter in this theorem is disjoint from unweighted \(t=1\) when the odd prime divides \(t+1\). The closest direct polynomial paper is Mihoubi–Taharbouchet (2019, arXiv preprint 2017), whose inspected congruence section proves modulo-prime polynomial identities, not exact index-shift valuations or least eventual periods modulo all prime powers. Schreyer's later modular-period theorem concerns a distinct \(r\)-horse ordering condition. OEIS A000670 lists ordinary Fubini numbers but not the colored-weight classification. Relevant published research-index records are about Fubini orbit-profile identities rather than these modular claims. The identified Diagana–Maïga (2017) general Fubini congruence paper was not fully accessible; possible hidden equivalent coverage is disclosed as an originality risk, not represented as excluded.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Independent colors on ordered-partition blocks give a standard and natural two-parameter deformation. The theorem supplies a complete exact prime-power period tower and all even-shift valuations in the singular residue class \(t\equiv-1\pmod p\). Its period shortening depends only on the divisibility of \(t+1\), and occurs for infinitely many positive colored weights and every prime-power modulus in a nonempty family (e.g. \(p=3\)). This is structural information beyond modulo-prime identities or a single numerical period.

## Risks and boundaries

Primes without primitive root two, nonpositive color counts, the dyadic prime, and initial rather than eventual periodicity are outside the theorem. The inaccessible plausible 2017 generalized congruence source is the main uneliminated literature risk. The proof is not claimed to be independently validated.
