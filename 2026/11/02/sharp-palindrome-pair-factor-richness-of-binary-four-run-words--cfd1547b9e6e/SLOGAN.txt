For every \(n\ge8\), a binary four-run word has at most \(\lceil(n^2+n+9)/3\rceil\) distinct palindrome-pair factors counting the empty factor, and this bound is sharp. The exact maximum falls \(\lceil(n-7)/3\rceil\) below an explicit three-run construction, resolving the first nontrivial run-class competitor in the palindrome-pair richness problem.
