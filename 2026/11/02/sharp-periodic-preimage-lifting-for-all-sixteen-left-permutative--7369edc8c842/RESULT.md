# Sharp periodic-preimage lifting for all sixteen left-permutative elementary cellular automata

## Finding

For a binary word \(y\) of length \(n\ge1\), write \(y^m\) for its \(m\)-fold concatenation, interpreted as an indexed cyclic configuration of length \(mn\). Given a binary elementary cellular automaton \(F_r\), define its **minimum ring-lift factor**
\[
\ell_r(y)=\min\{m\ge1: y^m\in F_{r,mn}(\{0,1\}^{mn})\}.
\]
Every one of the sixteen rules permutative in its leftmost input has finite \(\ell_r(y)\), and its exact possibilities and exceptional conditions are:

| Rule numbers | Possible minimum factors | Exact condition for a factor above two |
|---|---|---|
| 15, 240 | \(\{1\}\) | Never |
| 45, 60, 75, 105, 150, 180, 195, 210 | \(\{1,2\}\) | Never |
| 30, 120 | \(\{1,2,3\}\) | \(\ell_r(y)=3\) precisely if \(y=1^n\) and \(3\nmid n\) |
| 135, 225 | \(\{1,2,3\}\) | \(\ell_r(y)=3\) precisely if \(y=0^n\) and \(3\nmid n\) |
| 90 | \(\{1,2,4\}\) | \(\ell_{90}(y)=4\) precisely if \(n\) and \(|y|_1\) are both odd |
| 165 | \(\{1,2,4\}\) | \(\ell_{165}(y)=4\) precisely if \(n\) is odd and \(|y|_1\) is even |

Every factor listed actually occurs for a nonempty target word. In all cases not covered by an exceptional condition, the precise choice between one and two is decided by a four-state transformation: it is one exactly when that transformation has a fixed point, and two otherwise. In particular, no left-permutative elementary rule requires a minimum factor greater than four. This statement concerns the **minimum enlargement of the specified ring**, not a claim that the least spatial period of every predecessor equals \(mn\) when \(y\) itself has a smaller least period.

## Assumptions and scope

All cells are binary, the spatial boundary condition is cyclic, and one synchronous update is used. A rule is left-permutative if, for each pair of its central and right inputs, changing its left input changes the output. Precisely these sixteen rules have a unique representation
\[
 f_g(a,b,c)=a\oplus g(b,c),\qquad g:\{0,1\}^2\longrightarrow\{0,1\}.
\]
Here \(\oplus\) is addition modulo two. Encode \(g\) by a four-bit integer \(G\in\{0,\ldots,15\}\), with bit \(2b+c\) equal to \(g(b,c)\). The rule number is \(r_G=\sum_{a,b,c\in\{0,1\}}2^{4a+2b+c}(a\oplus g(b,c))\). The resulting rule numbers are exactly those in the first column of the classification.

## Proof

**Finite inverse recurrence.** Given an output bit \(d\) and the two predecessor bits \((b,c)\) at the central/right positions, the forced left bit is \(d\oplus g(b,c)\). Thus, on the four pair states \(s=2b+c\), define
\[
 T_{G,d}(2b+c)=2(d\oplus g(b,c))+b.
\]
For \(y=y_0\cdots y_{n-1}\), let \(\Phi_{G,y}=T_{G,y_0}\circ\cdots\circ T_{G,y_{n-1}}\). Starting with the last overlapping input pair, applying the inverse recurrences around the ring closes consistently exactly when \(\Phi_{G,y}\) fixes that pair. Because the word \(y^m\) gives \(\Phi_{G,y}^m\), a ring predecessor exists precisely when \(\Phi_{G,y}\) has a periodic point with cycle length dividing \(m\). Consequently,
\[
 \ell_{r_G}(y)=\min\{\text{cycle lengths of }\Phi_{G,y}\}.
\]
Any transformation of four elements has at least one directed cycle of length at most four, proving existence and a universal bound before rule-specific analysis.

**Exhaustive finite-semigroup classification.** Form the submonoid \(M_G\) of the 256 transformations of the four pair states generated by \(T_{G,0}\) and \(T_{G,1}\) under composition. Beginning from the identity, repeatedly append either generator on the right until no new transformation appears. The exact closure sizes and counts of maps by their minimum cycle length are:

These counts concern **transformations in a finite monoid**, not numbers of output words. Each row is verified by `verify.py` through an explicit enumeration of every generator image and a closure assertion; no finite-length extrapolation is used.

**All-length exceptional-language classification.** To distinguish words producing an exceptional transformation, augment a transformation state with the length modulo six, parity of the number of ones, two flags recording whether all letters seen are zero or all are one, and a nonemptiness flag. Starting from \((\mathrm{id},0,0,\mathrm{true},\mathrm{true},\mathrm{false})\), reading a bit composes the appropriate generator and updates the auxiliary fields. This is a finite deterministic automaton, and every nonempty word reaches exactly its own descriptor. Full reachable-state closure has respectively 37, 43, 61, 63, 105, or 428 states depending on \(G\) (the exact row-by-row sizes appear in the checker output). At **every** reachable nonempty descriptor the verifier proves the equivalence between a minimum cycle length of three or four and the exceptional conditions in the finding table. The remaining descriptors have minimum cycle length one or two. Thus these conditions hold for words of arbitrary lengths, not only those enumerated in an initial range.

**Sharpness.** Explicit shortest target witnesses for minimum factors two, three, and four are: rule 30 with `011` has factor two; rule 30 with `1` has factor three; rule 90 with `01` has factor two; and rule 90 with `1` has factor four. Every other displayed factor for each of the sixteen rules likewise has a nonempty witness, verified by finite closure. These examples prove the classwise bounds cannot be decreased.

## Verification

Run `python3 verify.py` with the Python 3 standard library. The verifier constructs the sixteen truth tables independently from \(G\), generates all finite transformation monoids, checks the complete four-state cycle classifications and all-length augmented closure invariants, and checks existence of witnesses for every claimed factor. It also independently enumerates the **forward** images of all input configurations of ring sizes one through twelve, comparing 5,120 target/repetition tests against the inverse-cycle criterion. The expected terminal line is `VERIFY_OK`.

The proof of the infinite theorem is the finite-state closure and recurrence argument. The short-ring checks are a second, independent consistency test, not a substitute for this argument.

## Relationship to prior work

Wolfram's 1983 study introduced and systematically investigated elementary cellular automata. Martin, Odlyzko, and Wolfram (1984) studied the algebraic dynamics of linear finite cellular automata, relevant especially to Rules 90 and 165. Mariot, Leporati, Dennunzio, and Formenti (2017) established a general de Bruijn-based method to compute the least periods of preimages of a **given** spatially periodic output of a surjective cellular automaton, with detailed theory for linear bipermutive rules. The finite inverse-pair construction here is based on this known perspective; its claimed contribution is the **complete simultaneous all-output classification** of the sixteen left-permutative elementary rules, including the exact nonlinear triple-lift exceptions and the sharp partition of all rules into four lift spectra. It does not claim a new general preimage-period algorithm or the novelty of linear Rule-90 dynamics.

## Limitations

The result is specific to the sixteen left-permutative binary radius-one rules and **one** synchronous update. The minimum lift factor is defined relative to a supplied ring size, whether or not the output word is primitive. It does not classify the total number of distinct predecessors, multistep inverse trees, or arbitrary cellular automata. The originality assessment is based on an accessible full postprint of the 2017 preimage-period study, accessible bibliographic and introductory material for classical works, and targeted claim searches; it is not an independent historical-priority certification. In particular, the full classical 1984 algebraic paper was not available in a sufficiently compact inspectable version, and its known linear special cases must not be presented as independently new.
