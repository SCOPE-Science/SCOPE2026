# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS

For the precise infinite one-dimensional tight-binding Hamiltonian with at least four equal positive on-site defects, the exact compressed free Green matrix is inverted by a tridiagonal conditional-correlation factorization. Its band-edge limit is the weighted Neumann path Laplacian, and its first-order correction is an endpoint projector. Positive-definite resolvent monotonicity then proves the exact number and simplicity of above-band modes; a separate half-line recurrence rules out other point spectrum. The bounded piecewise-linear threshold states establish a resonance (not an eigenvalue) at equality. The positive endpoint mass of every path eigenvector fixes the claimed quadratic energy-emergence coefficient. The included verifier independently checks Green inversion, long finite-chain spectra, equal-spacing thresholds and well-conditioned onset comparisons. Numerical checks corroborate but do not establish the all-parameter theorem.

## Originality — PASS on inspected evidence, provisional

Direct full-text comparison of the closest continuum antecedent: Goloschapova–Oridoroga (2009), arXiv:0903.1180v1, Theorem 3.8, relates arbitrary-point-interaction negative-spectrum counts to a finite Jacobi matrix; this already covers the equivalent weighted-path counting criterion. The originality assessment therefore excludes the counting criterion and instead addresses the exact new discrete-lattice quadratic threshold energy coefficient. Other primary comparisons: Hiroshima–Sasaki–Shirai–Suzuki (2012), arXiv:1209.0522v1, handles one lattice impurity; Ito–Jensen (2013), arXiv:1312.1396v1, establishes a broad threshold/resolvent framework without the explicit arbitrary-gap finite-array values; Hiroshima–Muminov–Kuljanov (2018), arXiv:1804.05339v1, develops a three-site, two-coupling one-dimensional classification rather than arbitrary arrays of four or more defects. Related continuum point-interaction eigenvalue-count algorithms of Albeverio–Nizhnik (2003) and Goloschapova–Oridoroga (2009) are structural neighbors and are not claimed as new. The 2003 full text remained inaccessible for a theorem-by-theorem comparison. Specialized literature could still contain the same quantitative coefficient. Relevant published mathematical results concern regular-tree, higher-dimensional and two-dimensional defect problems, not an explicit discrete-lattice band-edge birth coefficient for all arbitrary gaps. Historical priority is not independently verified; specialized prior Jacobi/Green-kernel literature remains the principal risk.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The quantitative band-edge rate at which each localized mode emerges is a natural spectral question in defect engineering, and the endpoint-eigenvector formula reveals its sensitive dependence on all inter-defect distances. The underlying weighted-path count is an antecedent of known continuum point-interaction inertia calculations and is not a novelty claim. The exact resonance boundary and quadratic onset constants add information unavailable from a bare finite-rank bound or an isolated computed eigenvalue. The result is not offered as a general theorem for unequal impurities or interactions.

The claimed theorem is confined to the homogeneous one-dimensional nearest-neighbor single-particle lattice, finite arrays of at least four identical positive onsite defects, and the discrete spectrum at or above the upper free band edge.
