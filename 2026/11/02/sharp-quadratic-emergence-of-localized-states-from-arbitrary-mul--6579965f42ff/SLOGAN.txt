For any finite array of at least four identical positive impurities on a one-dimensional tight-binding lattice, every nonzero binding threshold has a sharp quadratic birth law: the energy excess is asymptotic to the squared coupling excess divided by \(J(|u_1|^2+|u_m|^2)^2\), where \(u\) is the normalized eigenvector of the weighted Neumann path Laplacian at that threshold. The underlying all-array mode-count/threshold classification is credited to the analogous known point-interaction inertia principle; the assessed contribution is the exact discrete-lattice energy-emergence coefficient for arbitrary gaps.
