## Correctness

The exact normalized moments \(\mu_n=n!/(\alpha)_n\), the identity \(\|f(r\cdot)\|_4^4=\|f(r\cdot)^2\|_2^2\), and the decomposition \(f^2=P+R\) yield the displayed quartic normal form. The fact that \(R\) begins in degree three removes potentially dangerous first-order cross terms and yields an all-mode Hilbert-norm remainder. The positive form is comparable to \(|a|^4+\|h\|_2^2\), allowing the sharp liminf and equality characterization for arbitrary sequences approaching the constant. The finite checker verified 550 exact rational statements for complex coefficient polynomials; the analytic argument, not that finite test, proves the theorem for all real \(\alpha>1\) and all functions.

The closest accessible mathematical-results records address other local quartic problems and Bergman geometry, not an equivalent hypercontractive remainder. A general earlier equality-stability expansion or an unindexed thesis may nevertheless cover this exact statement; independent historical-priority verification remains advisable.

## Value

At sharp dilation the usual quadratic stability test vanishes in the first analytic direction, so the first nonzero defect and the interaction with higher coefficients are mathematically necessary to understand near-extremizers. The result determines the full infinite-dimensional degeneracy resolution, identifies the uniquely optimal second-order analytic correction, and quantifies a finite-weight obstruction relative to the Fock exponential model. It is not a generic Taylor expansion of one test polynomial: the asymptotic constant is minimized over an infinite-dimensional transversal space and the equality conditions are complete.

## Boundaries
