# Sharp random-edge matching efficiency and Gaussian losses on complete split graphs

## Finding

Let \(S_{a,b}=K_a\vee\overline K_b\), with a clique of \(a\) vertices joined to every vertex of an independent set of \(b\) vertices. Run **RANDOM-EDGE** maximal matching: repeatedly choose one of the currently admissible edges uniformly, match its endpoints, and remove those endpoints. Let \(Y_{a,b}\) be the number of independent-set vertices unmatched at termination, and let \(M_{a,b}\) be the number of accepted edges.

**Exact law.** For all integers \(a,b\ge0\), the probability distribution \(p_{a,b}(j)=\Pr(Y_{a,b}=j)\) is uniquely determined by the following finite recursion, with any branch having zero coefficient omitted:
\[
 p_{a,b}(j)=\frac{a-1}{a-1+2b}p_{a-2,b}(j)
 +\frac{2b}{a-1+2b}p_{a-1,b-1}(j), \quad a\ge2,
\]
where \(p_{0,b}(b)=1\), \(p_{1,0}(0)=1\), and \(p_{1,b}(b-1)=1\) for \(b\ge1\). All omitted probabilities are zero. The exact relation to matching size is \(M_{a,b}=(a+b-Y_{a,b}-C_{a,b})/2\), where \(C_{a,b}\in\{0,1\}\) is the number of unmatched clique vertices.

**Scaling law and Gaussian loss.** Suppose \(a_n/n\to\alpha>0\) and \(b_n/n\to\beta>0\), with deterministic integers \(a_n,b_n\). Set \(r=\alpha/\beta\), \(r_n=a_n/b_n\), \(z_n=b_n e^{-r_n}\), and \(v=\beta e^{-r}[4-e^{-r}(r+2)^2]\). Then \(v>0\), and
\[
 \frac{Y_{a_n,b_n}}n\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}\beta e^{-r},\qquad
 \frac{Y_{a_n,b_n}-z_n}{\sqrt n}\Longrightarrow N(0,v).
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \frac{M_{a_n,b_n}}n\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}
 \frac{\alpha+\beta-\beta e^{-r}}2,
 \qquad
 \frac{M_{a_n,b_n}-(a_n+b_n-z_n)/2}{\sqrt n}
 \Longrightarrow N(0,v/4).
\]
In particular the asymptotic ratio of the expected random-edge matching size to the maximum matching size is
\[
 \mathcal R(r)=\frac{r+1-e^{-r}}{2\min\{r,(r+1)/2\}},
 \qquad \inf_{r>0}\mathcal R(r)=1-\frac1{2e}.
\]
The infimum is attained **only** at \(r=1\), the balanced clique/independent-set regime. Thus random edge selection loses a fraction \(1/(2e)\) even on a split graph admitting a perfect matching.

## Assumptions and scope

All edges of the *given deterministic complete split graph* are initially present, and the eligible edge is chosen uniformly among edges whose two endpoints are still unmatched. This is also the matching generated by inspecting all edges in one uniform random order and accepting each edge if both endpoints are available. There are no random graph edges and no independent-set internal edges. The exact law includes zero-sized boundary cases; the asymptotic law requires both part sizes to be positive linear proportions of \(n\). The central limit theorem is centered at the *actual finite-size deterministic* quantity \(z_n=b_n\exp(-a_n/b_n)\), not merely its first-order limit.

## Proof

**1. Exact finite recursion.** If \(x\) clique and \(y\) independent-set vertices remain, the eligible graph has \(\binom x2\) internal edges and \(xy\) cross edges. An internal match leaves \((x-2,y)\); a cross match leaves \((x-1,y-1)\). The respective probabilities are \((x-1)/(x-1+2y)\) and \(2y/(x-1+2y)\) when \(x\ge2\). When \(x=1,y>0\), a cross match occurs surely, whereas \(x=0\) and \((x,y)=(1,0)\) are absorbing. Conditioning on the first accepted edge proves the recursion. All matches use two vertices, and termination leaves at most one clique vertex; hence the matching-size identity.

**2. Deterministic path and endpoint.** Give every eligible edge an independent exponential clock of rate \(1/n\); after each accepted edge, discard all incident clocks. Memorylessness gives exactly the discrete transition law above. Write \(x_n(t)=X_n(t)/n\) and \(y_n(t)=Y_n(t)/n\) for unmatched counts. Their transition rates are \(X_nY_n/n\) and \(X_n(X_n-1)/(2n)\). The drift converges uniformly on compact sets to
\[
 \dot x=-x^2-xy,\qquad \dot y=-xy,
 \qquad (x(0),y(0))=(\alpha,\beta).
\]
Uniform martingale-quadratic-variation bounds \(O(1/n)\) on every fixed time interval, together with the Lipschitz drift and Gronwall's inequality, prove convergence in probability uniformly on such intervals. Whenever \(x,y>0\),
\[
 \frac{dx}{dy}=\frac{x+y}{y},\qquad
 \frac{x}{y}-\log(y/\beta)=r.
\]
Therefore \(x=y[r+\log(y/\beta)]\), and the trajectory decreases toward \((0,y_*)\), with \(y_*=\beta e^{-r}>0\). For every \(\varepsilon>0\), the trajectory reaches \(x\le\varepsilon\) at finite time. Once the finite process has \(X_n\le2\varepsilon n\), no more than \(2\varepsilon n\) independent-set vertices can be removed. Taking first \(n\to\infty\), then \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), proves \(Y_{a_n,b_n}/n\to y_*\) in probability. It also shows that with probability tending to one the independent-set population stays at least \(ny_*/2\) during the final stage.

**3. Fluctuation martingale.** Use \(\beta_n=b_n/n\), \(r_n=a_n/b_n\), and
\[
 H_n(x,y)=\frac{x}{y}-\log(y/\beta_n)-r_n.
\]
This is zero at the initial point and constant along the finite-parameter fluid trajectories. On the event \(y_n\ge y_*/2\), Taylor expansion at a cross match (increment \((-1,-1)/n\)) and an internal match (increment \((-2,0)/n\)) gives respectively
\[
 \Delta H_n=\frac{x}{ny^2}+O(n^{-2}),\qquad
 \Delta H_n=-\frac{2}{ny},
\]
uniformly on bounded \(x\) and positive bounded-away-from-zero \(y\). The leading conditional drift cancels exactly because \(\nabla H\cdot(-x^2-xy,-xy)=0\); the finite-size correction and Taylor remainder have total expected absolute magnitude \(O(1/n)\) up to termination. To see the latter without a uniform bound on the (logarithmic) clock time, index by *accepted matches*: at most \(a_n\) steps occur, and their one-step conditional drift is uniformly \(O(n^{-2})\) on the stopped region.

Let \(N_n\) be the martingale made by subtracting these conditional drifts. Its jumps are \(O(1/n)\), and the predictable variation of \(\sqrt nN_n\) converges to
\[
 V=\int_0^\infty\left(\frac{x(t)^3}{y(t)^3}
                      +\frac{2x(t)^2}{y(t)^2}\right)dt.
\]
Indeed, on each bounded interval this follows from the fluid limit and the two jump rates. To justify extending to termination, stop when \(X_n\le\varepsilon n\). Thereafter at most \(\varepsilon n\) matches occur; while \(Y_n\ge ny_*/2\), each cross-edge increment of \(H_n\) is \(O(\varepsilon/n)\), each internal-edge increment is \(O(1/n)\), and the conditional probability of the internal edge is \(O(\varepsilon)\). Hence the remaining predictable variation after multiplying by \(n\) is \(O(\varepsilon^2)\), uniformly in \(n\). The complementary event has probability tending to zero by the endpoint law. The bounded-jump martingale central limit theorem (or its conditional-characteristic-function proof) yields \(\sqrt nH_n(x_n(T),y_n(T))\Rightarrow N(0,V)\).

For explicit evaluation, put \(u=x/y=r+\log(y/\beta)\). Since \(dt=-dy/(xy)\) along the trajectory and \(y=y_*e^u\),
\[
 V=\int_{y_*}^{\beta}\frac{(x/y)^2+2(x/y)}{y^2}\,dy
   =\frac1{y_*}\int_0^r e^{-u}(u^2+2u)\,du
   =\frac{4-e^{-r}(r+2)^2}{y_*}.
\]
The bracket is positive for \(r>0\), because its derivative is \(re^{-r}(r+2)>0\) and its value at zero is zero. At termination \(x_n(T)\in\{0,1/n\}\), so \(H_n(x_n(T),y_n(T))=-\log(Y_{a_n,b_n}/z_n)+O(1/n)\). Taylor expansion at \(z_n/n\to y_*\) gives the survivor central limit theorem with variance \(y_*^2V=v\). The matching-size CLT follows from \(C_{a_n,b_n}\le1\).

**4. Sharp efficiency.** Every edge touches the clique, so a maximum matching has size exactly \(\min\{a,\lfloor(a+b)/2\rfloor\}\). The survivor law, bounded by \(0\le Y_{a_n,b_n}\le b_n\), also gives convergence of expected normalized matching size. For \(0<r\le1\),
\[
 \mathcal R(r)=\frac{r+1-e^{-r}}{2r},\quad
 \mathcal R'(r)=\frac{(r+1)e^{-r}-1}{2r^2}<0.
\]
For \(r\ge1\),
\[
 \mathcal R(r)=1-\frac{e^{-r}}{r+1},\quad
 \mathcal R'(r)=\frac{e^{-r}(r+2)}{(r+1)^2}>0.
\]
Thus the unique minimum is at \(r=1\), and equals \(1-1/(2e)\).

## Relationship to prior work

Arnosti (2021) analyzes random-edge matching against other greedy algorithms on random *bipartite* input graphs; its full open-access article does not contain this fixed dense *nonbipartite* complete-split-graph law. Dyer and Frieze (1991) study general randomized greedy matching, especially worst-case bounds and sparse families. Dyer, Frieze and Pittel (1993) obtain normal limits for sparse random graphs and random trees, a different graph regime. The split-graph calculation is not implied by those source descriptions or by the compared published records, but an inaccessible earlier general-graph full text leaves a genuine priority risk. The result makes no claim of independence or external certification.

## Limitations

The graph is a complete join of one clique and one independent set, not an arbitrary split graph. The edge rule is uniform among admissible edges; selecting a random vertex and then a random neighbor defines a different process. Both part sizes must be asymptotically proportional to the common scaling parameter for the Gaussian limit; extreme aspect-ratio triangular arrays require separate analysis. The tight ratio is asymptotic over fixed positive aspect ratios, not a finite-size universal bound. Independent validation and a complete historical priority audit have not been performed.
