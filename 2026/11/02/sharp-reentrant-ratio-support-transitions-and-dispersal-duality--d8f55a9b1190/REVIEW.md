# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The stationary ratio recursion follows by direct division of the two-population equations in Morita and Yoshimura's model. Both maps contract globally in log-ratio distance by a factor at most \(|(1-\theta)/(1+\theta)|<1\). The extremal image interval is calculated from one positive quadratic root. Its two exact branch images cover that interval precisely at the proposed migration thresholds, or separate with a strict central gap; uniqueness of the compact attractor proves the interval/Cantor dichotomy. The full-law dispersal duality follows from inversion covariance, fair environmental signs, and uniqueness of invariant probability. The included checker executed 2,383 rational and numerical consistency assertions. Finite tests are corroborative only.

**Originality — PASS, subject to historical-priority risk.** Morita and Yoshimura (2012, arXiv:1207.4618v1) supplied the entire stochastic model, ratio recursion and qualitative self-similarity; they are expressly not new. The accessible full article's ratio and numerical sections do not identify a migration-controlled sharp interval/Cantor transition or the global paired stationary-law identity. Barnsley and Vince (2013, doi:10.1007/s13373-013-0041-3) give the generic IFS mechanism, not these application-specific thresholds or distributional symmetry. Morita and Yoshimura (2013, arXiv:1307.7903v1) study a many-patch approximation and growth statistics, not this exact two-patch support classification. Some older general Möbius-IFS results could imply the phase rule after algebraic specialization; that unlocated-coverage risk remains.

**Value — PASS.** Stationary patch-abundance imbalance is a natural diagnostic in dispersal ecology. A complete, two-threshold phase diagram identifies when entire ranges of relative population sizes are unattainable versus when every intermediate ratio is supported. The equality of full stationary laws under two biologically different migration fractions also gives a structural nonidentifiability mechanism for one-time stationary ratio samples. This is not an empirical prediction or an inference guarantee with noisy observations.

**Closest-source and scope limitations.** The result applies only to positive two-habitat linear stochastic growth with iid equally probable antiphase environmental multipliers. It is about the support of the stationary *ratio*, not about extinction probabilities or finite-time growth, and it does not claim density or dimension formulas. Historical priority and independent validation are outstanding.
