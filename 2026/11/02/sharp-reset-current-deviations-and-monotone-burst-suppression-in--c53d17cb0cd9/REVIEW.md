# Scientific assessment: threshold-reset sluggish current deviations

## Correctness — PASS

The claim specifies the nonsticky time-changed diffusion, instantaneous symmetric threshold resets, exact normalization of the current, and the full parameter range. Its cycle transform follows from the unique even solution of the killed backward equation and the Bessel spectral representation. Exponential tilting turns the renewal count equation into a probability-kernel renewal equation, giving the global scaled cumulant generating function. Its strict analyticity and slope range justify the Gärtner--Ellis LDP, including the independently checked empty-count boundary. Modified-Bessel asymptotics give the sharp high-current coefficient, and positive hypergeometric coefficient comparisons prove strict ordering of every upward-deviation rate. Finite checks reproduce exact rational series inequalities, ODE solutions, transform identities and rate inversion. Their finite nature is not used to prove a universal statement.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Frimpong--Bresque--Roldán (2026, arXiv:2610.21962v1, Sections 5--7) gives the threshold-resetting physical model, mean cycles, stationary costs and a different first-passage-to-origin law; it does not state the reset-count LDP or an all-upper-tail cross-sluggishness comparison. Clark (2026, arXiv:2610.19233) and Martin--Behn--Germano (2010/2011, arXiv:1007.4588) already give the Bessel first-exit law and related spectral identities, which directly cover the individual excursion transform and are explicitly **not** claimed new. Del Vecchio Del Vecchio--Majumdar (2024, arXiv:2410.22097) treats unreset arcsine/time-of-maximum distributions. A previous bilateral-extrema analysis of this same diffusion already encodes the single-cycle two-sided first-exit transform; this is treated as prior coverage, not a new result. The assessed addition is the explicitly solved global *count* SCGF/LDP with sharp boundary constants and strict mean-normalized upward-tail comparison. Searches against research records, literature, and accessible earlier claims found no statement dominating that conjunction. A general Bessel/renewal parameter-comparison theorem could still subsume it, and publication-priority verification remains independent and unperformed.

## Value — PASS

Reset-cost and reset-frequency studies motivate fluctuating numbers of costly threshold-triggered returns, not just their stationary mean rate. The exact large-deviation rate identifies both a long no-reset interval (first Bessel eigenvalue) and arbitrarily high reset bursts (quadratic cost). Its strict normalized cross-sluggishness ordering measures noise suppression at matched mean frequency for **every** upward deviation rather than a selected finite-size parameter or merely a variance. The natural observable is precisely the count of the resets already integral to the motivating model; no arbitrary numerical slice is introduced.

## Scope and limitations

The analysis is for the ideal continuum, nonsticky, instant-reset model, and compares normalized count rates; it does not claim a discrete lattice theorem, finite-time tail inequality, or lower-tail monotonicity. Classical first-exit and general renewal results supply ingredients; only their new combination and sharp parameter comparison are assessed. Numerical tests check representative finite cases, while proof is analytical. Potential implicit coverage by broader comparison theory remains a residual risk.
