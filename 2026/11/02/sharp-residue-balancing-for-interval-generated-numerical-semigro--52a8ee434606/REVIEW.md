# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The domain is exactly \(n\ge3\), \(2\le k<n\), and \(m\ge2\) dividing \(k\). Every positive semigroup element with exactly \(t\) generators lies in the complete interval \([tn,t(n+k)]\); the resulting finite gap intervals are nonoverlapping. Modulo \(m\mid k\), the interval comparison subtracts precisely \(tk/m\) occurrences of each residue at level \(t\). The explicit histogram is therefore exact. The near-balance follows from two separately balanced residue counters under multiplication by a unit. The polynomial identity is over integer coefficients modulo \(X^m-1\); the cyclic-difference kernel is exactly the constant vectors. The two cyclic arcs are equal only at matching starts unless their length is a multiple of the cycle order. The exceptional nonunit case has disjoint residue cosets and is excluded. The displayed examples establish optimality and unbounded resonance. The independent finite verifier passed its enumerations; no finite test is treated as proof of the infinite statement.

## Originality — PASS within inspected evidence

Shor's arXiv:2101.01760 full text, especially Proposition 3.9, Theorem 4.5 and Corollary 5.10, provides a more general *implicit* Apéry-set test and handles particular boundary families. Thus there is prior coverage of the underlying criterion and some subfamilies; those are acknowledged, not portrayed as new. Its inspected statements do not supply the explicit width-divisor criterion for all \(k\), the exact residue histogram, or the sharp bounded-discrepancy classification. Robles-Pérez–Rosales arXiv:2310.09287 studies monotone Apéry sets, with no modular gap-distribution theorem identified in the inspected introduction and structural results. Targeted record searches and source-title searches identified no direct equivalent. Risks: a generic Apéry criterion can imply the present specialization after additional argument; related literature may contain implicit versions or older interval-generated-semigroup calculations not indexed by the queries. Bibliographic noncoverage is not claimed as a certainty.

## Value — PASS

The result completes a motivated arithmetic regime within the literature's gap-equidistribution question: all interval-generated numerical semigroups, all widths, and simultaneously all moduli dividing the width. It replaces residue enumeration of a potentially quadratic-size gap set by exact short-interval counts; it identifies every perfectly balanced case and a sharp absolute discrepancy bound of two even when the genus grows unboundedly. The contrast with unbounded discrepancy for nonunit multiplicity explains why coprimality is a structural threshold rather than a technical convenience. The theorem is not merely a fixed-parameter table or a restatement of a single two-generator case.

## Closest literature and residual limitations

Shor's general Apéry-multiset equivalence is the closest broader framework. The theorem is restricted to modulus divisors of width, and the same mathematical model literature comparison cannot exclude all implicit consequences of older generalized-arithmetic semigroup results.
