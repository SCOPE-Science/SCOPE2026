# Self-review

## Correctness
PASS. The optimization domain, Laplacian normalization and cost are explicit. Concavity justifies rim-dihedral averaging; the two invariant eigenbranches are derived from the radial and cycle-Fourier subspaces. Their affine minimax balance gives the price boundary and all claimed allocations. Equality in the variational characterization forces the entire optimal averaged Fiedler space to remain an eigenspace of any competitor, giving independent-weight rigidity: radial vectors fix every spoke, and nonreal Fourier edge fluxes fix every rim weight. The equality-price family and the critical asymptotic follow by direct substitution. The standalone standard-library verifier confirms 827 independent assertion groups, but finite tests are not infinite proofs.

## Originality
PASS, with qualified historical risk. Sun–Boyd–Xiao–Diaconis (2006) supplies the general continuous-time semidefinite optimization model, not this wheel-specific classification. Boyd–Diaconis–Parrilo–Xiao (2007) computes the *discrete-time* fastest-mixing wheel chain using a different objective and stochastic constraints; its known wheel formula does not dominate a priced continuous-time Laplacian optimum. Wan et al. (2008) supplies general eigenspace and edge-utilization theory but no inspected priced-wheel threshold. Mathematical-record searches for alternate wheel, algebraic-connectivity and price terminology produced no equivalent theorem. Fiedler's foundational full text was unavailable in this comparison, and specialized older literature may exist. A failed search is not evidence of absolute uniqueness.

## Mathematical value
PASS. The equal-price result is a sharp natural wheel-size phase boundary: all optimal cycle conductances are positive for 3–5 rim vertices, multiple allocations coexist for six, and all cycle edges are unavoidably deleted for seven or more. Full unrestricted uniqueness on each strict price side is stronger than a symmetry-restricted calculation; the inverse-square price window quantifies when outer-cycle investment remains useful on large networks. These facts directly address which edges a finite budget should retain for fastest continuous-time diffusion.

## Principal sources and limitations
The closest scientific sources are the continuous-time optimization framework of Sun et al. (SIAM Review 2006, DOI 10.1137/S0036144504443821), the discrete-time wheel example of Boyd et al. (arXiv:0709.0955v1, §5.3.3), and the optimal-edge structural theory of Wan et al. (IEEE CDC 2008, DOI 10.1109/CDC.2008.4738734). The full text of Fiedler's 1989 chapter could not be obtained. The claim does not address adaptive or discrete-time design or exhaust all equality-price nonsymmetric optimizers.
