# Sharp rooted-broom Hardy norms, strict branch majorization, and an endpoint-mass limit

## Finding

Consider a compact metric tree with one root-to-junction edge of length \(a>0\) and \(m\ge2\) terminal edges of lengths \(b_1,\ldots,b_m>0\). On real arclength \(L^2\), define the root-to-point integration operator \((Hf)(x)=\int_{[o,x]}f\,ds\), where \([o,x]\) is the unique root-to-point path. Put \(B=\sum_i b_i\). Its **sharp operator norm** is \(\|H\|_{2\to2}=1/k\), where \(k\) is the unique root
\[
\cot(ak)=\sum_{i=1}^m\tan(kb_i),\qquad 0<k<\frac{\pi}{2\max\{a,b_1,\ldots,b_m\}}.
\]
The norm-attaining inputs form a one-dimensional space, generated by the derivative of the positive function specified in the proof.

**Strict length-majorization law.** If two positive terminal-length vectors \(\mathbf b,\mathbf c\in(0,\infty)^m\) have the same sum and \(\mathbf b\) majorizes \(\mathbf c\), then
\[
\|H_{a,\mathbf b}\|_{2\to2}\ge \|H_{a,\mathbf c}\|_{2\to2},
\]
with equality **only** when the vectors differ by a permutation. In particular, for fixed \(a,B,m\), the unique minimizing length profile up to permutation is \(b_i=B/m\); the sharp supremum is \(2(a+B)/\pi\), approached as all but one terminal edges shrink to zero, and is not attained by a positive-length \(m\)-arm profile.

**Exact local stability.** Let \(b=B/m\), let \(k_0\) solve \(\cot(ak_0)=m\tan(bk_0)\), and let \(D=a\csc^2(ak_0)+mb\sec^2(bk_0)\). For \(\varepsilon_i\to0\) with \(\sum_i\varepsilon_i=0\),
\[
\|H_{a,(b+\varepsilon_i)_i}\|_{2\to2}
=\frac1{k_0}+
\frac{\sec^2(bk_0)\tan(bk_0)}{D}
\sum_i\varepsilon_i^2+O(\|\boldsymbol\varepsilon\|^3).
\]
The displayed coefficient is strictly positive. The remainder is for fixed \(a,B,m\) and sufficiently small perturbations preserving all positive lengths.

**Many-short-arm limit.** For the equilateral profiles \(b_i=B/m\) with \(a,B\) fixed, the norms strictly decrease as the integer \(m\ge2\) increases. If \(k_\infty\) is the unique root of \(\cot(ak_\infty)=Bk_\infty\) in \((0,\pi/(2a))\), and \(D_\infty=a\csc^2(ak_\infty)+B\), then
\[
\|H_{a,(B/m,\ldots,B/m)}\|_{2\to2}
=\frac1{k_\infty}+\frac{k_\infty B^3}{3D_\infty m^2}+O(m^{-4}).
\]
Thus a large number of short terminal edges behaves, at leading order, like a positive endpoint mass at the junction, with an explicit finite-branch correction.

## Assumptions and scope

The tree is a continuous metric graph, not a finite-vertex counting-measure graph. All edges carry ordinary arclength Lebesgue measure; vertices have zero mass. Edge lengths \(a,b_i\) are arbitrary strictly positive real numbers, and \(m\) is any integer at least two. All statements concern the real \(L^2\) operator norm of the linear, unweighted path-integration map. The root is the **leaf at the free end of the common stem**; changing the root changes the operator. Majorization refers to the standard decreasing-partial-sum order with identical total sum, not to coordinatewise order.

## Proof

**1. Variational equivalence.** Let \(\mathcal D\) be the space of continuous functions \(u\) on the metric tree that are \(H^1\) on each edge and satisfy \(u(o)=0\). For each \(f\in L^2\) its path integral \(u=Hf\) lies in \(\mathcal D\), has edge derivative \(u'=f\), and \(\int|u'|^2=\int|f|^2\). Conversely any \(u\in\mathcal D\) equals \(H(u')\). Hence
\[
\|H\|_{2\to2}^2=\sup_{u\in\mathcal D\setminus\{0\}}
\frac{\int_\Gamma |u|^2\,ds}{\int_\Gamma|u'|^2\,ds}
=\lambda_1^{-1},
\]
where \(\lambda_1>0\) is the first eigenvalue of the edgewise negative second derivative with Dirichlet condition at the root, Kirchhoff continuity/flux at the junction, and Neumann conditions at each free terminal endpoint. Compactness of the metric graph and the root condition give existence and positivity.

**2. Positive eigenfunction and sharp constant.** Define \(F(k)=\cot(ak)-\sum_i\tan(kb_i)\). On \(0<k<K=\pi/(2\max\{a,b_1,\ldots,b_m\})\), \(F\) decreases strictly from \(+\infty\) to a negative limit (possibly \(-\infty\)); if the maximum length is \(a\), its terminal limit is strictly negative, while if the maximum belongs to a branch it is \(-\infty\). Thus there is exactly one root \(k\). Parameterize the common edge by \(x\in[0,a]\) and branch \(i\) by \(y\in[0,b_i]\) from the junction. Set
\[
\phi_0(x)=\sin(kx),\qquad
\phi_i(y)=\frac{\sin(ka)}{\cos(kb_i)}\cos(k(b_i-y)).
\]
Every branch solution has zero derivative at its terminal endpoint, joins continuously to \(\phi_0(a)\), and is strictly positive away from the root. The junction Kirchhoff condition is exactly \(\cot(ak)=\sum_i\tan(kb_i)\). Therefore \(-\phi''=k^2\phi\) on each edge.

For any \(u\in\mathcal D\), the ground-state identity, first for smooth functions and then by density, is
\[
\int_\Gamma(|u'|^2-k^2|u|^2)\,ds
=\int_\Gamma \phi^2\left|\left(\frac{u}{\phi}\right)'\right|^2\,ds\ge0.
\]
Integration-by-parts boundary terms cancel by Kirchhoff/Neumann and the root Dirichlet condition. The apparent root singularity causes no boundary term: \(u(x)^2/x\le \int_0^x |u'|^2\to0\). Equality requires \(u/\phi\) constant along every edge and continuous at the junction, hence \(u=c\phi\). This proves \(\lambda_1=k^2\), the norm formula, and the one-dimensional norm-attaining input family \(f=c\phi'\).

**3. Strict majorization.** For fixed \(k>0\), the function \(t\mapsto\tan(kt)\) is strictly convex on \((0,\pi/(2k))\). If \(\mathbf b\) strictly majorizes \(\mathbf c\), its maximum coordinate is at least as large, and Karamata's inequality gives \(\sum_i\tan(kb_i)>\sum_i\tan(kc_i)\) whenever both sums are defined. Let \(k_b,k_c\) be their respective unique roots. If \(k_c\) is beyond the first pole for \(\mathbf b\), then \(k_b<k_c\) immediately. Otherwise \(F_b(k_c)<F_c(k_c)=0\), so strict decrease of \(F_b\) implies \(k_b<k_c\). Taking reciprocals proves the law and equality condition. The equal profile is majorized by every other fixed-sum profile. A sequence with one terminal length tending to \(B\) and the others to zero has secular equation tending to \(\cot(ak)=\tan(Bk)\), whose first positive root is \(\pi/(2(a+B))\); continuity of the root and strict majorization give the stated sharp, unattained supremum. This global path bound is also a known general quantum-graph estimate; the new statement is its sharp profile-specific majorization and stability.

**4. Quadratic stability.** Write \(\mathbf b=(b+\varepsilon_i)_i\), \(\sum_i\varepsilon_i=0\), and \(k=k_0+\delta\). Taylor's formula gives
\[
\sum_i\tan(k_0(b+\varepsilon_i))
=m\tan(k_0b)+k_0^2\sec^2(k_0b)\tan(k_0b)\sum_i\varepsilon_i^2
+O(\|\boldsymbol\varepsilon\|^3).
\]
Since the derivative with respect to \(k\) of the secular equation at its equal-arm root is \(-D\), the implicit-function theorem gives
\[
\delta=-\frac{k_0^2\sec^2(k_0b)\tan(k_0b)}{D}\sum_i\varepsilon_i^2+O(\|\boldsymbol\varepsilon\|^3).
\]
Expanding \(1/(k_0+\delta)\) establishes the claimed coefficient and the strict quadratic minimum.

**5. Infinitely many short branches.** For each fixed positive \(k\), \(m\tan(kB/m)\) strictly decreases with \(m\), because \(\tan z-z\sec^2z<0\) for \(0<z<\pi/2\). Taking the pole bounds into account as in Step 3 shows that the equal-arm roots increase strictly with \(m\), hence the norms decrease. As \(m\to\infty\), the secular equation converges to \(\cot(ak)=Bk\). Its unique root is \(k_\infty\). Using \(m\tan(kB/m)=Bk+k^3B^3/(3m^2)+O(m^{-4})\) and the nonzero derivative \(-D_\infty\) yields
\[
 k_m=k_\infty-\frac{k_\infty^3B^3}{3D_\infty m^2}+O(m^{-4}).
\]
Taking reciprocals gives the asserted limit and second-order correction. All expansions are for fixed positive \(a,B\).

## Relationship to prior work

Naimark and Solomyak (2000) study spectral estimates for weighted Laplacians on metric trees; Evans, Harris, and Lang (2001) study Hardy-type operators and their approximation numbers. These establish the relevant framework, not the present strict majorization and finite-fringe expansion in the inspected available material. Ekholm, Frank, and Kovařík (2007) study Hardy inequalities on infinite regular metric trees, with different domain and weight assumptions. Del Pezzo and Rossi (2014) already establish existence, simplicity for a leaf Dirichlet vertex, global total-length bounds, and a general shape-derivative formula for quantum-graph first eigenvalues. In particular, the global upper bound \(\|H\|\le2(a+B)/\pi\) is **not claimed as novel**; the specific sharp profile order, local quadratic coefficient, and many-arm correction are the assessed additions. Rohleder (2016) optimizes the *nonzero standard-Neumann spectral gap* rather than the single-root-Dirichlet first eigenvalue. General shape derivatives may offer another way to derive some local consequences; that relationship is acknowledged.

## Limitations

This concerns only an unweighted \(L^2\) operator on a single-stem metric tree with terminal branches. No assertion is made about \(L^p\) norms for \(p\ne2\), weighted arclength, multiple branching levels, arbitrary root positions, or all higher singular values. The earlier original full texts of Naimark–Solomyak and Evans–Harris–Lang were not accessible for complete comparison, leaving a literature-coverage risk. Independent scientific review has not been performed.
