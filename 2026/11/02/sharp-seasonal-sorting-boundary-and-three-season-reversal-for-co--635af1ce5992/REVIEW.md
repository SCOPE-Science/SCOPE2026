## Correctness — PASS

The stated finite-composition domain and quantifiers are explicit. Substitution gives an exact positive-denominator representation for the two possible orderings. Its derivative-ratio sign switches across the common fixed point but integrates to a globally fixed strict commutator sign because the denominator-difference function vanishes there. Strict increase of each Hassell update at \(p<1\), or of all relevant updates on the invariant interval \([0,K]\) at \(p>1\) with \(a(p-1)K\le1\), validates the adjacent-swap argument for **every** permutation, including repeated strengths. At \(p=1\) the exact commutator is zero. When \(c(p-1)K>1\), the last map has negative derivative at the fixed point, reversing the strict first-two-season ordering on a sufficiently small positive neighborhood. This proves that two seasons cannot display the three-season failure and the universal monotonicity cutoff is sharp. The bundled exact fractions verify a nontrivial numerical witness; finite random tests are not used as proof.

## Originality — PASS (provisional priority)

The historical sources establish the Hassell density-response family and nonautonomous/periodic recruitment analysis. Hassell's source text addresses single-map stability; Jaggi–Joshi addresses spatial density variation; Elaydi–Sacker addresses a rational Beverton–Holt periodic system. No inspected source supplies the exact strict ordering across all positive states, arbitrary finite-season permutations across both exponent regimes, and the sharp three-season obstruction when the strongest season ceases to be monotone below the shared equilibrium. Closest published-record search results concern distinct mosquito/Ricker/harvesting maps rather than this Hassell finite-horizon sorting theorem. The general adjacent-swap argument and the \(p=1\) commuting special case are not claimed as independent discoveries. Broader nonlinear-composition rearrangement theorems might imply the result, so priority is provisional.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Season ordering is an intrinsic finite-horizon question for environmentally varying, density-dependent recruitment. The result gives a parameter-exact phase change from contest-like \(p<1\) sorting through commuting \(p=1\) to scramble-like \(p>1\) sorting; crucially, it demonstrates a sharp *loss of global schedule optimality* exactly at the classical overcompensation/monotonicity boundary. The three-season minimality and an exact rational witness distinguish it from merely restating a two-map sign or a routine symbolic substitution. The limitation to shared carrying capacity and deterministic finite-horizon objectives is substantive and made explicit.
