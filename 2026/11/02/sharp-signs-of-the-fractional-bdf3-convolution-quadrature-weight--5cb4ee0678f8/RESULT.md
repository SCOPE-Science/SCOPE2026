# Sharp signs of the fractional BDF3 convolution-quadrature weights

## Finding

**Theorem.** For every fractional order \(0<\alpha<1\) and time step \(h>0\), define the *uncorrected fractional-derivative BDF3 weights* \((w_n)_{n\ge0}\) by the power series at zero
\[
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}w_nz^n=h^{-\alpha}\left(\frac{11}{6}-3z+\frac32z^2-\frac13z^3\right)^\alpha.
\]
The exponent denotes the real-analytic branch with positive value at \(z=0\). Then \(w_0>0\), \(w_1<0\), \(w_3<0\), and \(w_n<0\) for **every integer** \(n\ge4\). The only variable sign is
\[
\operatorname{sgn}(w_2)=\operatorname{sgn}\left(\alpha-\frac7{18}\right).
\]
In particular, every noninitial coefficient is strictly negative if and only if \(0<\alpha<7/18\), and every noninitial coefficient is nonpositive if and only if \(0<\alpha\le7/18\). At the endpoint \(\alpha=7/18\), precisely \(w_2=0\) among the noninitial weights.

## Assumptions and scope

The statement concerns the scalar, infinite formal Taylor sequence generated by the BDF3 *fractional derivative* symbol, not the reciprocal symbol for a fractional integral, a finite truncated convolution, or any corrected start-up weights. The step size \(h\) merely multiplies every coefficient by the positive factor \(h^{-\alpha}\). The proof is uniform over the whole open order interval and all integer indices; it makes no stability or solution-positivity assertion for a particular differential equation.

## Proof

Divide by the positive factor \(h^{-\alpha}(11/6)^\alpha\), and write
\[
F(z)=\left(1-\frac{18}{11}z+\frac9{11}z^2-\frac2{11}z^3\right)^\alpha=\sum_{n\ge0}c_nz^n.
\]
Put \(\beta=1-\alpha\in(0,1)\) and \(t=1+\alpha=2-\beta\in(1,2)\). Differentiating \(F\), multiplying by the cubic, and equating coefficients gives the exact identity
\[
\frac{11n}{6}c_n=3(n-t)c_{n-1}-\frac32(n-2t)c_{n-2}+\frac13(n-3t)c_{n-3},\qquad n\ge1,
\]
with \(c_0=1\) and \(c_n=0\) for negative indices. The initial coefficients are
\[
c_1=-\frac{18\alpha}{11},\quad
c_2=\frac{9\alpha(18\alpha-7)}{121},\quad
c_3=-\frac{2\alpha}{1331}(486\beta^2-405\beta+121).
\]
The quadratic in the last expression has positive leading coefficient and discriminant \(-71199<0\), so \(c_3<0\).

For every \(n\ge4\), recurrence evaluation shows \(c_n=-\beta(1-\beta)s_n(\beta)\), where \(s_n\) is a polynomial with rational coefficients. The standalone exact-arithmetic checker `verify_bdf3.py` constructs those polynomials for \(4\le n\le21\). It certifies all \(s_n(\beta)>0\) on \([0,1]\) by expanding each in the Bernstein basis and checking that **every** rational Bernstein coefficient is strictly positive. It separately certifies
\[
s_{20}-\frac45s_{19}>0,\qquad \frac{11}{10}s_{19}-s_{20}>0,
\]
\[
s_{21}-\frac45s_{20}>0,\qquad \frac{11}{10}s_{20}-s_{21}>0
\]
on \([0,1]\), again through strictly positive rational Bernstein coefficients. In total these are \(18+4\) polynomial certificates and \(285\) positive rational Bernstein coefficients. The checker independently recomputes \(52\) individual series coefficients using the generalized binomial theorem. These are finite exact polynomial identities and positivity certificates, not floating-point experiments; the script is included as a standalone dependency-free artifact.

To cover *all* subsequent indices analytically, define \(q_n=s_n/s_{n-1}=c_n/c_{n-1}\) for \(n\ge20\). In the recurrence, whenever all three preceding coefficients are negative,
\[
q_n=\Phi_n(q_{n-1},q_{n-2}),\qquad
\Phi_n(x,y)=\frac{3(n-t)-\frac32(n-2t)x^{-1}+\frac13(n-3t)(xy)^{-1}}{11n/6}.
\]
Set \(c=4/5\) and \(C=11/10\). For \(n\ge22\), \(t\in(1,2)\), and \(c\le x,y\le C\), the function \(\Phi_n\) is strictly increasing in \(x\) and decreasing in \(y\), because its partial derivatives have these signs and
\[
\frac32(n-2t)-\frac{n-3t}{3c}
=\frac{13n}{12}-\frac{7t}{4}>0.
\]
Exact simplification at the opposing corners gives
\[
\Phi_n(c,C)-c=\frac{49n-510t}{2420n}>0,
\qquad
C-\Phi_n(C,c)=\frac{n+930t}{1210n}>0.
\]
The first inequality uses \(n\ge22\) and \(t<2\), so \(49n-510t\ge1078-1020>0\). Consequently \(\Phi_n\) maps the closed rectangle \([c,C]^2\) strictly inside \((c,C)\). The exact finite certificates supply \(q_{20},q_{21}\in(c,C)\) and all earlier \(s_n>0\). Induction yields \(q_n\in(c,C)\) and \(s_n>0\) for every \(n\ge22\). Thus \(c_n<0\) for all \(n\ge4\). Combining the first three signs proves the theorem. \(\square\)

## Verification

Run `python3 verify_bdf3.py` from this package's directory; it uses only Python's standard library, with rational arithmetic throughout. It recomputes the coefficient recurrence to index \(21\), performs exact polynomial division by \(\beta(1-\beta)\), calculates the Bernstein representations, checks all \(285\) rational positivity inequalities, independently compares \(52\) rational-order generalized-binomial expansions, and verifies the symbolic corner/derivative formulas underlying the infinite induction. The finite checker does not enumerate an infinite tail; the displayed invariant-region argument proves it for every later index.

## Relationship to prior work

Lubich's *Discretized Fractional Calculus* (1986), especially its third-order backward differentiation generating function, establishes the underlying fractional linear multistep construction, not this complete order-and-index sign classification. Jin, Li and Zhou's correction work (2017) gives the higher-order BDF fractional-derivative symbols and start-up correction/convergence analysis, but the inspected formulation and analysis do not state the present coefficientwise threshold. Karaa (2024) analyzes positivity of convolution quadratic forms generated by certain sequences, including BDF2 integral weights; that property is distinct from the sign of every BDF3 derivative weight. Accordingly, the claimed contribution is only the exact BDF3 derivative coefficient sign frontier and rigorous all-index negativity, not the generating scheme, convergence theory, or positivity of unrelated quadratic forms. The literature comparison does not establish global uniqueness of the finding; an unlocated equivalent coefficient inequality remains possible.

## Limitations

The theorem is strictly for \(0<\alpha<1\), the BDF3 cubic, and the unmodified coefficient sequence. The all-index result is proved analytically using finite exact-arithmetic positivity certificates plus induction, and has not undergone independent expert validation. No claim is made for orders outside this interval, BDF orders other than three, corrected starting weights, maximum principles, unconditional stability, or solution positivity. The computer certificate is an auditable calculation, not a stand-alone proof of an infinite statement without the displayed induction.
