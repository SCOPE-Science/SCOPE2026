# Scientific review — sharp single-joker cyclic binary factor-cover length

**Correctness (C): PASS.** A host of length \(N\) yields exactly \(N+k\) resolved window occurrences; equality at \(N=2^k-k\) is impossible because two joker-resolution walks and a positive-length ordinary walk cannot partition a balanced binary de Bruijn graph. The explicit order-\(k\) edge set for \(k\ge4\) has a single duplicate, and the remaining graph is weakly connected with exactly the Eulerian start/end imbalance necessary for construction. Small orders are handled directly. A standard-library verifier reproduced hosts for all orders through 12, verified all factors/multiplicities, and exhaustively ruled out shorter hosts for orders 2–4. The all-order proof does not depend on finite tests. Remaining scientific boundary: exactly one joker, binary, cyclic, host length at least target length.

**Originality (O): PASS, with residual risk.** The closest primary result, Blanchet-Sadri et al. (TAMC 2010), proves a *linear* one-hole optimum \(2^k-1\), not a cyclic one; transferring its lower bound to a cycle yields only the *unattainable* \(2^k-k\). Its Euler-path construction cannot be used as a cyclic construction without additional closure. Orenstein et al. (2018) work with specified repeating joker templates, ILP/greedy optimization and general bounds, not an accessible stated all-order single-joker-total cyclic formula. The 2016 and 2023 universal partial word/cycle literature imposes exactly-once coverage, which is a different target. Statement-level comparisons, queries for aliases and stronger results, and checks against related reconstruction/covering findings did not find a dominating theorem. The 2018 PMC indexed full text was inspected in substantial sections, but rendered equation images and direct full-page access were incomplete; equivalent hidden or differently indexed results remain a genuine risk.

**Value (V): PASS.** The exact optimum is a natural fixed-joker-budget k-mer coverage problem motivated by compact molecular probe libraries and de Bruijn synthesis. It establishes that the naive optimal packing of \(k\) wildcard gains is impossible on a cycle for *every* order, and that exactly one extra resolved factor always suffices. The constructive family, exact all-order threshold, and precise unavoidable duplicate are structural, rather than a small finite-case gain.

**Closest literature and limits.** TAMC 2010: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-13562-0_13 (linear theorem and graph construction). Orenstein et al. 2018: https://doi.org/10.1089/cmb.2018.0032 (periodic-template k-mer coverage). Chen et al.: https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.06456 and Fillmore et al.: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13067 (exactly-once partial words/cycles). This finding does not address nonbinary alphabets, multiple wildcards, or shortest linear hosts.
