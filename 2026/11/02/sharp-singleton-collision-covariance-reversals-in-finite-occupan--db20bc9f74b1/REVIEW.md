# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The claimed general formula is proved by conditioning on one matching sample pair and requiring that any singleton category occur exactly once among the other observations. Exact boundary treatment for two observations avoids a negative exponent. Uniform substitution gives a factor of \(n-2m\); for a two-category law, the complementary singleton event gives an independent factorization with factor \(np(1-p)-1\). The proof checks all strict inequalities and endpoint zeros. Exact rational sequence enumeration and factor checks pass 2334 assertions. Finite enumeration supplements the argument, not replaces it.

## Originality

**PASS under the inspected literature, with residual historical-priority risk.** Barbour–Gnedin treat infinite-urn small-count moments, Källberg–Leonenko–Seleznjev treat coincidence estimators, and Ben-Hamou–Boucheron–Ohannessian treat occupancy/missing-mass concentration and the Good–Turing estimator. Their relevant accessible full text did not give the present exact singleton/pair-collision covariance phase diagram. Relevant published results about total richness versus Simpson's estimator or a single frequency count concern different first statistics and do not imply the present exact mixed moment or reversal. Classical multinomial factorial moments might yield a reduction under other notation; global priority is not independently established.

## Value

**PASS.** Both \(S_n/n\) and \(Q_n/\binom n2\) are standard estimates of different aspects of categorical populations: sample coverage deficit and Simpson concentration. Understanding their joint fluctuations matters when the same sample is used to report both. The universal equal-weight reversal at exactly twice alphabet size, the genuine zero-covariance locus, and a sharp unequal-binary probability interval characterize a nontrivial boundary that is absent when singleton count is replaced by total richness.

## Limitations

Finite categorical sampling; uniform or binary assumptions are needed for the full sign diagrams. The joint covariance alone does not imply association, independence at zero covariance, or conditional ordering. Historical coverage in older occupancy/moment literature cannot be ruled out.
