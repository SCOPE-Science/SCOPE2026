# Sharp smoothability threshold and nodal boundary for a squarefree hypersurface Gröbner cell

## Finding

**Theorem.** Fix integers \(d\ge4\) and \(r\ge1\). Over \(\mathbb C\), give the polynomial ring in coordinates \(x_1,\ldots,x_d,y_1,\ldots,y_r\) its standard total grading. Consider the affine linear system of homogeneous degree-\(d\) equations
\[
F(x,y)=x_1x_2\cdots x_d+\sum_{j=1}^r y_jG_j(x)+H(x,y),
\]
where each \(G_j\) is an arbitrary homogeneous form of degree \(d-1\) in the \(x\)'s and every monomial of \(H\) contains at least two of the \(y\)'s. Set \(X_F=V(F)\subset\mathbb P^{d+r-1}\).

Every member admits the squarefree Gröbner initial ideal \((x_1\cdots x_d)\). The following trichotomy is sharp:

1. If \(1\le r\le d-4\), **every** \(X_F\) is singular; the singular locus on the distinguished coordinate subspace contains a closed subscheme of dimension at least \(d-3-r\).
2. If \(r=d-3\), a general \(X_F\) is smooth except for exactly
   \[
   N_d=\binom d2(d-1)^{d-3}
   \]
   ordinary double points, all isolated, with one set of \((d-1)^{d-3}\) points for each unordered coordinate pair.
3. If \(r\ge d-2\), a general \(X_F\) is smooth. Therefore the family contains a smooth hypersurface **if and only if** \(r\ge d-2\), equivalently the ambient dimension satisfies \(d+r-1\ge2d-3\).

In particular the critical counts for \(d=4,5,6\) are \(18,160,1875\).

## Assumptions and scope

The base field is \(\mathbb C\), and \(\textit{general}\) means on a nonempty Zariski-open subset of the indicated finite-dimensional coefficient affine space. The defining coefficient of the squarefree monomial is normalized to \(1\). Its initial term is specified using the weighted order of highest \(x\)-degree first (weights \(1\) on each \(x_i\) and \(0\) on each \(y_j\)), refined to a monomial term order. The special fiber is the reduced union of the \(d\) coordinate hyperplanes, rather than an irreducible toric hypersurface.

This is an embedded smoothing criterion for this specific Gröbner cell, not a universal numerical criterion for all squarefree initial ideals. A member at the nodal boundary is singular but is a flat Gröbner deformation of the squarefree union.

## Proof

**Step 1: the initial scheme.** Every monomial of \(F\) other than \(x_1\cdots x_d\) has strictly fewer than \(d\) of the \(x\)'s. Thus the initial term is \(x_1\cdots x_d\). Since the defining ideal is principal, its initial ideal is generated by that term. The monomial is squarefree, and the standard weighted Gröbner family provides the flat degeneration.

**Step 2: the singularity scheme forced on a coordinate subspace.** Put
\[
\Pi=V(y_1,\dots,y_r)\cong\mathbb P^{d-1}.
\]
Along \(\Pi\), \(F=\prod_i x_i\), and its partial derivatives satisfy
\[
\left.\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_i}\right|_\Pi=\prod_{k\ne i}x_k,\qquad
\left.\frac{\partial F}{\partial y_j}\right|_\Pi=G_j(x).
\]
A point of \(X_F\cap\Pi\) at which exactly one \(x_i\) vanishes is smooth. Any other singular point on \(\Pi\) belongs to a coordinate double intersection \(L_{ij}=V(x_i,x_j)\cap\Pi\cong\mathbb P^{d-3}\) and is cut out there by all \(r\) forms \(G_1,\dots,G_r\). Thus, set-theoretically,
\[
\operatorname{Sing}(X_F)\cap\Pi
=\bigcup_{1\le i<j\le d}V_{L_{ij}}(G_1,\dots,G_r).
\]
For any \(r\le d-3\), the common zero set of \(r\) positive-degree homogeneous polynomials on \(\mathbb P^{d-3}\) is nonempty, by the projective dimension theorem; each irreducible component has dimension at least \(d-3-r\) when this bound is nonnegative. This gives the universal obstruction to smoothness. In particular no member is smooth at \(r=d-3\).

**Step 3: generic transversality and the exact boundary count.** Restricting arbitrary degree-\((d-1)\) forms in the \(x\)'s to each \(L_{ij}\) produces arbitrary forms of that degree. If \(r=d-3\), then \(r\) general such forms on \(L_{ij}\cong\mathbb P^r\) intersect transversely in exactly \((d-1)^r\) reduced points, by Bézout. A coordinate triple intersection has dimension \(d-4<r\) and therefore meets no common zero of all \(r\) forms for general coefficients. Finitely many pairs/triples are involved, so one common nonempty Zariski-open set works for all of them. Hence each of the \(\binom d2\) pair strata contributes a disjoint set of \((d-1)^{d-3}\) points.

At such a point, precisely \(x_i\) and \(x_j\) vanish, and \(\prod_{k\notin\{i,j\}}x_k=c\ne0\). Because the restricted \(G_j\)'s vanish transversely, their values give \(r\) independent local coordinates \(u_1,\dots,u_r\) along \(L_{ij}\). Together with normal coordinates \(s=x_i\), \(t=x_j\) and \(y_1,\dots,y_r\), these form a complete local coordinate system: at \(r=d-3\) its size is \(2+2r=d+r-1\). The quadratic part of \(F\) is of the form
\[
q_2=cst+\sum_{a=1}^r y_a u_a+\sum_{a=1}^r y_a(\alpha_a s+\beta_a t)+\sum_{a\le b}\gamma_{ab}y_ay_b.
\]
The Hessian is nondegenerate: the mixed \(u\)-\(y\) block is an identity and the residual \(s\)-\(t\) block has determinant \(-c^2\); in fact the full Hessian determinant is \((-1)^{r+1}c^2\). Consequently each point is an isolated ordinary double point by the complex Morse lemma.

**Step 4: no further singularities.** Consider the open complement \(U=\mathbb P^{d+r-1}\setminus\Pi\). The degree-\(d\) linear system spanned by monomials having at least one \(y\) is basepoint-free on \(U\), since some \(y_j^d\) is nonzero at each point. Bertini applied to the projective linear system generated by this space and \(x_1\cdots x_d\) shows that its general member is smooth on \(U\). The affine coefficient slice with coefficient of \(x_1\cdots x_d\) equal to \(1\) is a nonempty open chart of that linear system, so the same generic smoothness holds here. This completes the nodal assertion.

**Step 5: smoothness above the threshold.** If \(r\ge d-2\), then on each \(L_{ij}\cong\mathbb P^{d-3}\), \(r\) general degree-\((d-1)\) forms have no common projective zero; this is again a finite intersection of Zariski-open conditions. By Step 2 the member is smooth along \(\Pi\), and Step 4 gives smoothness away from \(\Pi\). Together with the obstruction in Step 2, this is the sharp threshold.

## Verification

An explicit quartic witness at the nodal boundary is
\[
F=x_1x_2x_3x_4+y(x_1^3+x_2^3+x_3^3+x_4^3)+y^4\quad\text{in }\mathbb P^4.
\]
On \(y=0\), each pairwise coordinate line contributes the three distinct roots of \(x_k^3+x_l^3=0\); none lies on a triple intersection. The local Hessian determinant at each is \(9\) in the chart with one nonvanishing coordinate normalized to \(1\). For \(y\ne0\), set \(y=1\) and \(q=\prod_i x_i+\sum_i x_i^3\). If a critical point of \(q\) has any zero coordinate then all are zero, so \(q=0\). Otherwise letting \(P=\prod_i x_i\), the gradient equations force \(x_i^3=-P/3\), hence \(P=81\) and \(q=-27\). Thus \(q+1\) has no critical zero. The quartic has exactly \(18\) nodes and no other singularities.

The accompanying executable `verify.py` checks the integer boundary counts through \(d=8\), verifies the general local Hessian determinant for four independent block sizes, and carries out exact symbolic tests for this quartic witness. Its output is in `certificate.json`. Finite checks are auxiliary; Steps 1–5 constitute the proof for all the stated integers.

## Relationship to prior work

Squarefree initial ideals and their cohomological constraints are studied by Ramkumar, *Hodge theory and Gröbner degenerations* (arXiv:2610.34641v1, especially Theorem 1.5 and Example 5.3), including a smooth cubic threefold example. Huang–Tarasova–Varbaro–Witt, *Smooth Herzog projective curves* (arXiv:2512.00584v1), establish genus-zero rigidity and a combinatorial smoothing criterion for curves. Neither treatment supplies the present sharp dimension threshold or exact nodal boundary for the specified higher-dimensional hypersurface cell.

Kloosterman's *Maximal families of nodal varieties with defect* studies general hypersurfaces containing one fixed linear space and counts their nodes using a complete-intersection construction (Notation 4.3). That is a different incidence condition from the simultaneous arrangement of \(d\) coordinate hyperplanes here; the separate single-space node count does not itself determine the sharp smoothability threshold and \(\binom d2\)-weighted node count. The existing theory of irreducible toric initial degenerations, such as Ilten–Lautsch, does not directly address this reducible squarefree special fiber.

The explicit number \(18\) is not offered as an isolated priority claim; classical nodal quartic configurations contain comparable node incidences. The precise claimed new object is the **uniform threshold and generic nodal boundary in this fixed Gröbner cell**.

## Limitations

The classification is for the prescribed monomial initial ideal and for characteristic zero. It does not assert a smoothability criterion for arbitrary squarefree monomial ideals, nor does it classify nongeneral coefficients (which may acquire worse singularities). The singularity counts require generic coefficients. Related results on arrangements and hypersurfaces may yield an equivalent formulation not identified in the inspected literature; historical priority therefore remains provisional. No independent mathematical validation has been performed.
