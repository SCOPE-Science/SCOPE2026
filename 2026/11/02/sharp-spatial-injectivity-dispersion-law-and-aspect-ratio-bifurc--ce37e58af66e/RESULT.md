# Sharp spatial injectivity-dispersion law and aspect-ratio bifurcation for rectangular half-turn flat three-manifolds

## Finding
For a rectangular half-turn flat three-manifold, the full distribution of the *pointwise* injectivity radius is a truncated rectangle-distance distribution. In particular, let \(0<a\le b\) and \(h>0\), and write \(L(P)=2\operatorname{inj}(P)\) for the length of a shortest nontrivial based geodesic loop at a uniformly sampled point. If \(U,V\) are independent uniforms on \([0,a/2]\) and \([0,b/2]\), respectively, then
\[
L(P)^2\ \overset{\mathrm{law}}{=}\ \min\{a^2,\,4h^2,\,h^2+U^2+V^2\}.
\]
Equivalently, with \(Q=U^2+V^2\) and \(q(h)=\min\{3h^2,a^2-h^2\}\), the squared-loop-length law is \(h^2+\min\{Q,q(h)\}\). When \(h\ge a\) this is interpreted literally: it is the constant \(a^2\).

For **every** positive \(a\le b\) and \(h\),
\[
0\le \operatorname{Var}(L(P)^2)\le\frac{a^4+b^4}{180}.
\]
This upper bound is attained by at least one height \(h\) **if and only if** \(b/a\le\sqrt2\). Its complete equality locus is
\[
\frac{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}{2\sqrt3}\ \le h\le\ \frac{\sqrt{3a^2-b^2}}2.
\]
For a fixed transverse rectangle, the heights maximizing spatial variance are exactly this interval when \(b/a\le\sqrt2\); if \(b/a>\sqrt2\), the **unique** maximizing height is \(h=a/2\), and the upper bound is strict. At \(b/a=\sqrt2\) the interval collapses to \(h=a/2\); for smaller ratios it has positive length. The variance is zero precisely when \(h\ge a\).

The full distribution is elementary. Put \(A=a/2\), \(B=b/2\), \(R=A^2+B^2\), and for \(0\le u\le\sqrt t\) define
\[
H(u,t)=\tfrac12\big(u\sqrt{t-u^2}+t\arcsin(u/\sqrt t)\big).
\]
The cumulative distribution of \(Q\) is
\[
F_Q(t)=\begin{cases}
0,&t\le0,\\
\pi t/(4AB),&0<t\le A^2,\\
H(A,t)/(AB),&A^2<t\le B^2,\\
\big(H(A,t)+H(B,t)-\pi t/4\big)/(AB),&B^2<t\le R,\\
1,&t\ge R.
\end{cases}
\]
(The second interior interval is empty when \(a=b\).) For \(0<h<a\), the distribution of \(L(P)\) has CDF \(F_Q(\ell^2-h^2)\) for \(\ell<\min(a,2h)\), followed by value \(1\) at or above the cap. In particular, it has an atom at the cap exactly when \(q(h)<R\); the atom has mass \(1-F_Q(q(h))\). Within the maximizing-height interval the law is atomless, whereas beyond that interval it is truncated.

## Assumptions and scope
The manifold is the orientable, compact, flat quotient \(M_{a,b,h}=\mathbb R^3/\Gamma\), with Euclidean metric and torsion-free group generated by
\[
T_x(x,y,z)=(x+a,y,z),\quad T_y(x,y,z)=(x,y+b,z),\quad S(x,y,z)=(-x,-y,z+h).
\]
The positive parameters satisfy \(0<a\le b\) and \(h>0\). The quotient has volume \(abh\), transverse translation lattice \(a\mathbb Z\times b\mathbb Z\), and order-two holonomy. Sample \(P\) by normalized Riemannian volume. For every point \(p\), \(2\operatorname{inj}(p)\) is the shortest displacement of \(p\) by a nonidentity deck transformation. All statements concern intrinsic Euclidean quotient distances and exact population quantities; the symbol \(L\) is a based-loop length, not a random interpoint distance.

## Proof
Every element of \(\Gamma\) is \(T_x^mT_y^nS^k\), with \(m,n,k\in\mathbb Z\). When \(k\) is even, it is a translation by \((ma,nb,kh)\). The smallest nonzero even-element displacement is \(\min(a,2h)\) because \(a\le b\). When \(k\) is odd, it sends \((x,y,z)\) to \((ma-x,nb-y,z+kh)\); minimizing over odd \(k\) and integers \(m,n\) gives displacement
\[
\sqrt{h^2+\operatorname{dist}(2x,a\mathbb Z)^2+\operatorname{dist}(2y,b\mathbb Z)^2}.
\]
A fundamental region is \([0,a)\times[0,b)\times[0,h)\). For independently uniform \(x,y\) on the first two intervals, \(\operatorname{dist}(2x,a\mathbb Z)\) and \(\operatorname{dist}(2y,b\mathbb Z)\) are independent uniforms on \([0,a/2]\) and \([0,b/2]\). Taking the shorter displacement in the even and odd families proves the stated exact law, with all deck elements accounted for.

The formula for \(F_Q\) is the area of the intersection of the first-quadrant disk of squared radius \(t\) with the rectangle \([0,A]\times[0,B]\), divided by \(AB\). For \(0<t\le A^2\), the intersection is a quarter disk. For \(A^2<t\le B^2\), integrate \(\sqrt{t-u^2}\) from \(0\) to \(A\). For \(B^2<t\le A^2+B^2\), the sum of the two clipped circular segments minus the quarter-disk overlap gives the third branch. These cases also establish continuity at both junctions and at \(t=R\).

For \(U\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,a/2]\), the moments are \(\mathbb E U^2=a^2/12\) and \(\mathbb E U^4=a^4/80\). Thus \(\operatorname{Var}(U^2)=a^4/180\), and independence gives \(\operatorname{Var}(Q)=(a^4+b^4)/180\). To determine exactly when truncation changes this variance, let \(v(q)=\operatorname{Var}(\min\{Q,q\})\). For \(0<q<R\), differentiation under expectation yields
\[
\frac{d}{dq}\mathbb E\min(Q,q)=\mathbb P(Q>q),\qquad
v'(q)=2\mathbb P(Q>q)\big(q-\mathbb E\min(Q,q)\big)>0.
\]
Both factors are strictly positive since \(Q\) has positive density on \((0,R)\). For \(q\le0\), the truncation is constant; for \(q\ge R\), it equals \(Q\) almost surely. Consequently \(v\) is strictly increasing on \((0,R)\) and constant at its maximal value for \(q\ge R\).

Now \(q(h)=3h^2\) for \(h\le a/2\), and \(q(h)=a^2-h^2\) for \(h\ge a/2\). It increases strictly to the maximum \(3a^2/4\) at \(h=a/2\), then decreases strictly, becoming nonpositive at \(h\ge a\). Therefore \(h=a/2\) is the unique maximizing height unless the cap reaches the full support \(R=(a^2+b^2)/4\); if it does, the complete maximizing set is precisely the heights for which \(q(h)\ge R\). This is possible exactly when \(3a^2/4\ge(a^2+b^2)/4\), or \(b/a\le\sqrt2\). Solving the two quadratic inequalities gives the announced closed interval, including the equality endpoint. Since \(q(h)>0\) whenever \(h<a\), strict positivity of \(v\) proves the zero-variance characterization. This completes all parameter boundaries.

For explicit moment reproduction at any height one may use \(v(q)=2\int_0^q(q-t)F_Q(t)\,dt-\big(\int_0^q F_Q(t)\,dt\big)^2\) for \(q>0\) (and \(v(q)=0\) for \(q\le0\)). It follows by writing \(\min(Q,q)=q-(q-Q)_+\) and integrating the positive-part moments.

## Verification
`check.py` is a standalone Python standard-library script. Run `python check.py`. It reconstructs minima independently over the relevant deck transformations for 1,760 points, checks exact rational fourth-moment identities, compares empirical distributions with the piecewise CDF at 40 parameter/threshold combinations, checks 30 planar disk-rectangle areas by independent quadrature, and tests the two height-optimizer regimes, including their boundary. The final diagnostic is `ALL_CHECKS_PASSED`. These finite tests corroborate the proof but are not a proof of its universal quantifiers; the exhaustive deck decomposition, exact integration, and strict derivative argument above supply that proof.

## Relationship to prior work
The group action and shortest-clone geometry are standard in flat manifold theory. Petersen et al., *Cosmic topology. Part I* (public preprint 2022-11-04), Section 4, describe half-turn-space nearest-clone exclusion cylinders and numerical observer-position probabilities. Eskilt et al., *Cosmic topology. Part IIa* (public preprint 2023-06-29), give the underlying half-turn generators and analyze observer-dependent mode correlations. Those starting ingredients are not claimed as new. The present claim is the complete **pointwise squared-injectivity-radius law**, its exact sharp spatial variance, and the transverse-anisotropy threshold separating a nonunique maximizing-height interval from a unique maximizing height. This is not a global systole, a two-point distance distribution, or a combinatorial systole of a triangulation. No historical-priority certification is asserted.

## Limitations
The theorem treats flat half-turn quotients with rectangular transverse lattices and positive screw height; no general oblique transverse lattices, other holonomy types, or nonflat metrics are covered. The probabilistic law uses exact normalized volume sampling, not noisy finite samples, and the variance is for the **square** of the shortest based nontrivial loop length. The original group geometry was already available in the literature; possible overlooked specialized calculations of its complete spatial distribution and variance remain a priority risk. This document makes no claim of independent mathematical validation.
