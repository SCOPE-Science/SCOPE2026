# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

The proof obtains the exact perturbed stationary law from the rank-one stationarity equation on the zero-sum hyperplane, then reduces its entire response span to the incidence space of the known common-target graph. The spanning-tree reconstruction uses measured finite-amplitude responses, not an unjustified small-perturbation approximation. The six-state counterexample is verified rationally to be doubly stochastic, irreducible and aperiodic while its common-target graph has two components. The embedded standalone verifier checks a nonreversible four-state finite-response inversion, all permissible probes in small examples, and recovery of the jump-visit response from time-occupation probabilities. Proof applies to every irreducible finite matrix under the stated known-support and positive-rate hypotheses; finite numerical checks are corroboration only.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk

Calvert's 2026 equiaccessible McLennan calculation supplies the starting stationary law and forward traffic-response formalism; it does not state a common-target graph rank classification or finite-response inverse reconstruction. Hunter's 2002 Theorem 6.3 **does** provide the general exact single-row stationary perturbation formula, which is credited and is not asserted to be new. Berkhout, Heidergott and Van Dooren's 2024 full-text one-row control results solve the opposite inverse task (choose a perturbation given the original matrix and desired stationary law), not identification of an unknown matrix from stationary interventions. Published research-record and accessible earlier-claim comparisons did not identify the exact graph criterion and aperiodic counterexample. Stronger structural observability literature, the newly expanded appendices of the 2026 preprint and unavailable primary full text of some sensitivity studies remain priority risks, not evidence of proof of uniqueness.

## Value — PASS

The baseline equilibrium-like occupation law of equiaccessible jump dynamics hides the embedded kinetic matrix. The theorem gives an exact support-only test for which controlled stationary measurements can reveal that hidden matrix, an experiment count and constructive finite-amplitude recovery algorithm in the observable regime, and a natural aperiodic mixing counterexample showing what support geometry obstructs full response coverage. This is a complete, physically motivated identifiability boundary for a constrained class of perturbations, rather than a parameter substitution or numerical table extension. It does not address sampling noise or unrestricted intervention designs.
