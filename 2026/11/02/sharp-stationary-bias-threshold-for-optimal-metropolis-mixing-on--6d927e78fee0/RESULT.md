# Sharp stationary-bias threshold for optimal Metropolis mixing on alternating cycles

## Finding

For every integer \(m\ge2\), let the vertices of the cycle \(C_{2m}\) be indexed by \(\mathbb Z/(2m)\). Fix \(0<r\le1\) and the strictly positive target distribution
\[
\pi_{2j}=\frac{r}{m(1+r)},\qquad
\pi_{2j+1}=\frac{1}{m(1+r)}\qquad(0\le j<m).
\]
Among **all** reversible row-stochastic matrices \(P\) whose transitions off the diagonal follow only cycle edges and whose stationary distribution is \(\pi\), order their real eigenvalues with multiplicities as \(1=\lambda_1(P)\ge\lambda_2(P)\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_{2m}(P)\ge-1\). Minimize the second-largest eigenvalue modulus
\[
\mu(P)=\max\{\lambda_2(P),-\lambda_{2m}(P)\}.
\]
This excludes **exactly one** stationary eigenvalue, even if the chain is reducible. Write
\[
\sigma_m=\sin^2\!\left(\frac\pi m\right),\qquad
G_m(r)=1+r-\sqrt{(1+r)^2-4r\sigma_m},\qquad
r_m=1-\frac{\sigma_m}{2}.
\]
The globally optimal value is
\[
\boxed{\mu_*(m,r)=
\begin{cases}
1-\frac{G_m(r)}2,&0<r\le r_m,\\[3pt]
\displaystyle\frac{2(1+r)-G_m(r)}{2(1+r)+G_m(r)},&r_m\le r\le1.
\end{cases}}
\]
One global optimizer assigns every even vertex probability \(\alpha_*\) to **each** adjacent odd vertex, every odd vertex probability \(r\alpha_*\) to **each** adjacent even vertex, and the remaining probability to staying in place, with
\[
\boxed{\alpha_*=
\begin{cases}
\frac12,&0<r\le r_m,\\[3pt]
\displaystyle\frac{2}{2(1+r)+G_m(r)},&r_m\le r\le1.
\end{cases}}
\]
Both expressions agree at the threshold. In particular, the usual nearest-neighbor proposal, accept/reject Metropolis--Hastings chain has \(\alpha=1/2\) and is a **global** SLEM minimizer precisely when
\[
\boxed{0<r\le1-\frac12\sin^2\!\left(\frac\pi m\right).}
\]
Above that threshold the Metropolis--Hastings SLEM is strictly larger than the optimal value. The threshold satisfies \(r_m=1-\pi^2/(2m^2)+O(m^{-4})\). Thus for every fixed \(r<1\), this Metropolis--Hastings proposal is SLEM-optimal on all sufficiently long even cycles. The existence and value of the optimum are asserted, **not** uniqueness among every unsymmetrized feasible chain.

## Assumptions and scope

A permitted transition matrix has real nonnegative entries, nonnegative diagonal, each row sum equal to one, detailed balance \(\pi_iP_{ij}=\pi_jP_{ji}\), and \(P_{ij}=0\) when \(i\ne j\) are not adjacent in the simple cycle. Self-loops are permitted. For \(m=2\) the graph is the simple four-cycle: each vertex has two distinct neighbors, not parallel directed edges. An arbitrary feasible matrix may be reducible. Eigenvalues are real by reversibility; excluding just one stationary eigenvalue means an additional unit eigenvalue contributes to \(\mu\). No nonreversible chain is admitted, and no requirement that every allowed edge have positive transition probability is imposed. The normalized alternating vertex masses above are fixed; conductances are free.

## Proof

**1. Global reduction by convexity and weight-preserving symmetry.** For every undirected edge \(\{i,j\}\), put \(w_{ij}=\pi_iP_{ij}=\pi_jP_{ji}\ge0\). Then \(P_{ii}=1-\pi_i^{-1}\sum_{j\sim i}w_{ij}\ge0\). Conversely every nonnegative family \(w\) satisfying these vertex inequalities gives a feasible reversible kernel. Set \(D=\operatorname{diag}(\pi)\), \(u=(\sqrt{\pi_i})_i\), and \(S=D^{1/2}PD^{-1/2}\). This symmetric matrix depends affinely on \(w\), has \(Su=u\), and \(\lVert u\rVert=1\). The objective is exactly
\[
\mu(P)=\bigl\lVert S-uu^{\mathsf T}\bigr\rVert_2.
\]
Indeed this norm is the largest modulus among all nonstationary eigenvalues, including an extra \(1\) if present. Therefore \(\mu\) is convex in the flows \(w\).

The dihedral subgroup generated by a rotation through two vertices and a reflection fixing an even vertex preserves both the cycle and \(\pi\). It acts transitively on all undirected edges. Average any feasible flows over this finite group. Feasibility is preserved, while convexity and invariance of the spectral norm show that the objective cannot increase. All averaged flows equal one common number \(c\). Thus an unrestricted global optimum exists within the one-parameter homogeneous-flow family, regardless of whether an asymmetric optimum may also exist.

At an even vertex, set \(\alpha=c/\pi_{2j}\); at an odd vertex the probability along each adjacent edge is \(c/\pi_{2j+1}=r\alpha\). Both neighboring probabilities are present at each vertex. The even-vertex holding constraint gives \(0\le\alpha\le1/2\), and the odd-vertex holding constraint \(r\alpha\le1/2\) follows automatically because \(r\le1\). Every such \(\alpha\) is feasible.

**2. Exact periodic spectrum.** Group vertices into \(m\) ordered even--odd pairs. For Fourier phase \(\zeta_k=\exp(2\pi i k/m)\), the two-by-two Bloch block of \(P\) is
\[
B_k=\begin{pmatrix}
1-2\alpha &\alpha(1+\overline{\zeta_k})\\
r\alpha(1+\zeta_k)&1-2r\alpha
\end{pmatrix},\qquad 0\le k<m.
\]
It is diagonally similar to a Hermitian matrix. Its eigenvalues are
\[
\lambda_{k,\pm}=1-\alpha\left(1+r\mp
\sqrt{(1-r)^2+4r\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi k}{m}\right)}\right).
\]
The plus branch at \(k=0\) equals the stationary eigenvalue \(1\). For all remaining eigenvalues, write \(\lambda=1-\alpha\nu\). The smallest positive possible \(\nu\) is \(G_m(r)\), realized by \(k=1\) or \(m-1\) on the plus branch; the largest is \(2(1+r)\), realized at \(k=0\) on the minus branch. Since \(r>0\) and \(\sigma_m>0\), \(G_m(r)>0\). The modulus of the affine expression \(1-\alpha\nu\) is a convex function of \(\nu\), so its largest value on this finite spectral set is attained at one of those two extreme \(\nu\)'s. Because \(G_m(r)\le2r\le2\), the first extreme is nonnegative for \(0\le\alpha\le1/2\). Consequently the **exact** SLEM of the homogeneous chain is
\[
\mu(\alpha)=\max\bigl\{1-\alpha G_m(r),\ \bigl|1-2(1+r)\alpha\bigr|\bigr\}.
\]

**3. Constrained minimax and the sharp transition.** Write \(G=G_m(r)\), \(A=1+r\). The first branch \(1-\alpha G\) is strictly decreasing. The negative half of the absolute-value branch increases linearly as \(2A\alpha-1\). These branches intersect at \(\alpha_0=2/(2A+G)\); thus the unique minimizer of the displayed scalar objective on \([0,1/2]\) is \(\alpha_*=\min\{1/2,\alpha_0\}\). This also covers the preliminary decreasing side of the absolute-value branch. To identify the boundary, observe
\[
\alpha_0\le\frac12\iff G\ge2(1-r).
\]
With \(s=\sigma_m\), multiplication by the positive conjugate of the radical yields
\[
\operatorname{sgn}\bigl(G-2(1-r)\bigr)
=\operatorname{sgn}\bigl(3r-1-\sqrt{(1+r)^2-4rs}\bigr)
=\operatorname{sgn}\bigl(s-2(1-r)\bigr).
\]
The conjugate is positive since \(3r-1+\sqrt{(1+r)^2-4rs}\ge2r>0\). Therefore \(\alpha_0\le1/2\) exactly for \(r\ge r_m\). The two stated formulas for \(\alpha_*\) and \(\mu_*\) follow by direct substitution; they agree when \(r=r_m\). Strict monotonicity of the scalar objective on either side of its minimizer proves that \(\alpha=1/2\) is strictly suboptimal for \(r>r_m\).

**4. Metropolis--Hastings and long-cycle limit.** The symmetric nearest-neighbor proposal chooses either neighbor with probability \(1/2\). From an even vertex, the ratio of target masses is \(1/r\ge1\), so both proposals are accepted; from an odd vertex each is accepted with probability \(r\). This is precisely the \(\alpha=1/2\) kernel. The sharp equivalence follows from step 3. Finally, \(\sin^2(\pi/m)=\pi^2/m^2+O(m^{-4})\) gives the asserted threshold expansion.

## Verification

`verify_alternating_cycle.py` constructs the complete weighted reversible kernel and checks its eigenvalues by independent symmetric-matrix diagonalization. It compares the exact derived formula across \(2\le m\le24\), nine positive mass ratios, scalar grids, and 3,960 asymmetric feasible random-flow examples, and confirms exact rational threshold cases. It reports **4,585 assertions/check cases passed**. `optimize_global.py` independently solves the unrestricted full-edge constrained SLEM problem by numerical optimization on 25 parameter combinations; each optimized value matches the theorem within the stated numerical tolerance. Run both from a directory containing `requirements.txt` using `python verify_alternating_cycle.py` and `python optimize_global.py`. These finite checks are supporting tests and do not constitute proof for every \(m\) or \(r\); the all-parameter argument is the four-step derivation above.

For example, with \(m=4\), \(r=1/2\), the threshold is \(3/4\), so Metropolis--Hastings is optimal with \(\mu_*=(1+\sqrt5)/4\). At \(r=1\) it is periodic and has SLEM one; optimal positive holding makes the SLEM strictly smaller.

## Relationship to prior work

The foundational convex reformulation of the fastest-mixing Markov-chain (FMMC) problem, including prescribed reversible stationary distributions, is due to Boyd, Diaconis and Xiao (2004). Boyd, Diaconis, Parrilo and Xiao (2007) supply the full symmetry-reduction framework and analyze the ordinary symmetric/uniform-stationary cycle. That **uniform case \(r=1\) is already known**, and neither convexity of SLEM nor group averaging alone is claimed as new. The present statement gives an explicit, all-length, nonuniform, period-two stationary-mass solution and a necessary-and-sufficient criterion for Metropolis--Hastings global optimality. The 2010 treatment of cyclic \(K\)-partite networks by Jafarizadeh assumes symmetric stochastic transitions with uniform stationary masses, not these prescribed alternating masses. The exact threshold and its full-domain implication were not located in the inspected sources or the searches recorded in `AUDIT.json`; this absence is not a proof of universal novelty.

## Limitations

Only reversible nearest-neighbor plus self-loop chains on a simple even cycle, with alternating stationary masses, are considered. Nonreversible schemes, general target distributions and longer-range edges can outperform this optimum. The objective is SLEM, not total-variation mixing time at every finite time or a cost-weighted implementation objective. The conclusion establishes existence of a homogeneous optimal kernel, not uniqueness of all possibly asymmetric optimizers. Independent mathematical audit has not yet been performed. Relevant literature beyond inspected sources may contain an equivalent statement.
