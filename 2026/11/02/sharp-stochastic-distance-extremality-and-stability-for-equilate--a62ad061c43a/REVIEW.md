# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For every integer \(m\ge3\), the proof decomposes the random geodesic distance into an identical-edge \(|U-V|\) term and distinct-edge \(\delta+U+V\) terms; this follows from the uniqueness of paths in trees. An induction after deleting a leaf establishes the universal cumulative tail bounds on inter-edge separation, yielding stochastic order for all real thresholds. Exact incident-edge counts establish the small-threshold formula and uniqueness. Cut counting proves the exact Wasserstein formulas, and the single-internal-edge characterization establishes the sharp nonstar gap. Proof covers the complete stated domain. The standalone exact-rational checker enumerates all labeled trees through seven vertices, tests CDF inequalities at rational thresholds, and independently checks sampled edge geodesics. Enumeration is corroboration, not a substitute for induction.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical uncertainty

Garijo–Márquez–Silveira, Proposition 4.4, proves only the mean-distance star/path inequality, and the complete source was inspected. Baptista–Kennedy–Mugnolo's full article likewise treats mean distance rather than an entire distribution order. Hosoya-polynomial studies encode edge-pair distances but do not supply the continuous sampled-point stochastic sandwich or the sharp W1 nonstar stability statement. Dahl's majorization is for a rooted vertex-distance vector, a different sample space. The accessible Jelen dissertation concerns discrete tree distance orders. Relevant semantic research-record searches found no dominating exact claim. No claim is made that absence of such a search result proves worldwide priority. The full Doyle–Graver article (1982) was unavailable, and the historical weighted-tree literature may include related edge-distance comparisons; both remain residual priority risks.

## Value — PASS

First-order stochastic comparison controls every nondecreasing cost or penalty of continuous route length, not just a mean. The result classifies the extreme laws over all finite tree topologies with a fixed number of unit edges. Its exact short-distance expansion recovers a natural branching index from a probability law, while its optimal Wasserstein separation gives a quantitative topology-stability threshold with a unique nonstar extremizer. This is a natural complete extremal problem on metric trees rather than an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Limitations and comparisons

The result requires equal edge lengths and uses normalized arclength sampling. It does not assert variance order or general weighted-tree extremality. Source comparisons were limited to accessible primary texts and bibliographic/abstract material for inaccessible papers. Independent mathematical validation remains outstanding.
