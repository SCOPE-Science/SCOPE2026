# Sharp stochastic shortening on generalized flat Klein quotients, with an exact cutoff and a seven-halves transition

## Finding
For every integer \(k\ge1\), every choice of positive horizontal periods \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\), and every positive glide period \(b\), the intrinsic distance between two independent uniform-volume points in the corresponding compact flat generalized Klein quotient \(K\) is **strictly stochastically smaller** than the corresponding distance in the rectangular flat torus \(T\) of identical volume and coordinate periods. The comparison holds simultaneously for every threshold and strictly decreases every positive distance moment.

Writing \(D_K,D_T\) for these distances, set \(Q=\sum_{i=1}^k a_i^2/4\). The improvement in the second distance moment, \(G=\mathbb E[D_T^2]-\mathbb E[D_K^2]\), has an exact threshold:
\[
G=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^k a_i^4}{360b^2}\qquad\text{if and only if }b^2\ge Q.
\]
Below that threshold the same expression is a **strict upper bound** for \(G\). The stronger dimension-independent, globally sharp comparison is
\[
\frac12<\frac{\mathbb E[D_K^2]}{\mathbb E[D_T^2]}<1.
\]
Neither endpoint can be improved uniformly over periods and dimensions.

For the ordinary flat Klein bottle (\(k=1\)), put \(a=a_1\), \(c=2b/a\), and
\[
I(c)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_c^1(u^2-c^2)^{5/2}\,du,&0<c<1,\\0,&c\ge1.\end{cases}
\]
Then for **every** \(a,b>0\),
\[
G(a,b)=\frac{a^4}{360b^2}-\frac{a^4}{60b^2}I(2b/a).
\]
At \(c=1\), the correction satisfies \(I(c)\sim (2^{7/2}/7)(1-c)^{7/2}\) as \(c\uparrow1\). Consequently the gain, as a function of \(c\) with \(a\) fixed, is \(C^3\) but not \(C^4\) across the sharp threshold. It has a fourth derivative unbounded on approach from below.

## Assumptions and scope
Define \(\Lambda=\prod_i a_i\mathbb Z\subset\mathbb R^k\). On \(\mathbb R^{k+1}=\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R\), let the deck group of \(K\) be generated by horizontal translations \((x,y)\mapsto(x+a_i e_i,y)\) and the glide reflection \((x,y)\mapsto(-x,y+b)\). Let \(T\) be the quotient by translations of lengths \(a_i\) horizontally and \(b\) vertically. Both spaces inherit their intrinsic locally Euclidean metrics and have common volume \(b\prod_i a_i\). Sample two independent points from normalized Riemannian volume, not from a fixed base point or from embedded Euclidean space. All periods are finite and strictly positive. The higher-dimensional quotient is called a generalized Klein quotient regardless of its dimension-dependent orientability.

## Proof
**1. Two independent horizontal candidates.** Represent both uniform points by independent uniform \((x,y),(x',y')\) in \(\prod_i[0,a_i)\times[0,b)\), and put \(t=|y-y'|\). An even deck translate gives the squared horizontal residual
\[
S_0=\sum_i\operatorname{dist}(x_i-x_i',a_i\mathbb Z)^2
\]
with vertical cost \(t^2\); an odd translate gives
\[
S_1=\sum_i\operatorname{dist}(x_i+x_i',a_i\mathbb Z)^2
\]
with vertical cost \((b-t)^2\). These are the only relevant vertical candidates: additional even or odd translations change the vertical displacement by multiples of \(2b\), and the nearest is the displayed one. The torus has squared distance \(S_0+\min(t,b-t)^2\) for these original representatives. In constructing a distributional coupling, however, we are free to choose either of the identically distributed horizontal residuals as the torus horizontal coordinate.

Conditional on \(t\), \(S_0,S_1\) are independent and identically distributed. Indeed \((x,x')\mapsto(x-x',x+x')\) is a surjective group homomorphism on the product of two rectangular tori; it takes product Haar measure to product Haar measure. Each reduced coordinate distance is uniform on \([0,a_i/2]\). Moreover the horizontal variables are independent of \(t\). Since the joint distribution of \(S_0,S_1\) is exchangeable, we may relabel the two horizontal costs according to whether \(t\le b/2\). Write their resulting values as independent copies \(S,S'\) of \(\sum_i U_i^2\), where \(U_i\sim\operatorname{Uniform}[0,a_i/2]\) independently.

**2. An exact stochastic coupling.** Let \(z=\min(t,b-t)\). For independent uniform vertical coordinates, \(t\) has density \(2(b-t)/b^2\) on \([0,b]\). Therefore \(J=b(b-2z)=b|b-2t|\) is exactly uniform on \([0,b^2]\) and independent of \(S,S'\). The two squared distances have the exact joint representation
\[
D_T^2=S+\left(\frac{b^2-J}{2b}\right)^2,
\qquad D_K^2=D_T^2-(S-S'-J)_+,
\]
where \(u_+=\max(u,0)\). Thus \(D_K\le D_T\) pointwise. For strictly positive periods, \(S-S'\) is positive on a set of positive probability and \(J\) has a positive chance of lying below it; hence \(\Pr(D_K<D_T)>0\). This proves the claimed stochastic dominance and strictness of every positive power moment.

**3. Sharp cutoff identity.** Integrating the positive-part expression against uniform \(J\) gives
\[
G=\frac1{2b^2}\left(\mathbb E[(S-S')_+^2]
-\mathbb E[(S-S'-b^2)_+^2]\right).
\]
Because the law of \(S-S'\) is symmetric,
\(\mathbb E[(S-S')_+^2]=\operatorname{Var}(S)\). Since \(U_i\) are independent uniform variables, \(\operatorname{Var}(U_i^2)=a_i^4/180\). Thus the first term is \(\sum_i a_i^4/(360b^2)\). Almost surely \(S-S'<Q\), and its essential supremum is \(Q\), with positive mass arbitrarily near \(Q\). The remaining correction is exactly zero if and only if \(b^2\ge Q\), and otherwise strictly positive. Also \(\mathbb E[D_T^2]=(b^2+\sum_i a_i^2)/12\).

**4. Uniform sharp ratio.** Since \(J\ge0\),
\[
G\le\mathbb E[(S-S')_+]
\le\sum_i\mathbb E[(U_i^2-V_i^2)_+]
=\frac16\sum_i(a_i/2)^2=\frac12\mathbb E[S].
\]
Here the independent \(V_i\) have the same laws as \(U_i\), and the last identity follows by a two-dimensional triangular integral. The torus mean-square distance is \(\mathbb E[S]+b^2/12\), proving the strict ratio lower bound \(1/2\); strict upper bound follows from \(G>0\). The limit \(k=1\), \(b/a\downarrow0\), makes the ratio approach \(1/2\), whereas \(b/a\to\infty\) makes it approach \(1\).

**5. Ordinary Klein bottle and fractional transition.** Take \(k=1\) and \(A=a/2\). The correction in step 3 is evaluated by integrating over \(0\le V\le\sqrt{U^2-b^2}\), obtaining
\[
\mathbb E[(U^2-V^2-b^2)_+^2]
=\frac{8}{15A^2}\int_b^A(u^2-b^2)^{5/2}\,du
\]
for \(b<A\), and zero otherwise. Substitution \(u=Aw\) proves the stated formula for \(G\). As \(c\uparrow1\), put \(h=1-c\) and use \(u=c+s\): uniformly for \(0\le s\le h\), \(u^2-c^2=2s+O(hs)\), and hence
\[
I(1-h)=\int_0^h(2s)^{5/2}(1+O(h))\,ds
=\frac{2^{7/2}}7h^{7/2}(1+O(h)).
\]
More explicitly, substituting \(u=c+(1-c)v\) shows that \(I(c)=(1-c)^{7/2}H(c)\), where \(H(c)=\int_0^1[v(2c+(1-c)v)]^{5/2}\,dv\) is smooth and strictly positive near \(c=1\). Thus derivatives through order three extend continuously, while the fourth derivative diverges as \(c\uparrow1\); for \(c\ge1\), \(I=0\). Since its prefactor is nonzero and smooth at the threshold, \(G\) has precisely the asserted smoothness.

All tests returned `ALL_CHECKS_PASSED`. A unit-square comparison gives \(\mathbb E[D_T^2]=1/6\), \(G=1/360\), and \(\mathbb E[D_K^2]=59/360\). Finite experiments corroborate but are not used as proof of the all-dimensional statements. Reproduce with `python3 check.py`.

## Relationship to prior work
Davis and Recio-Mitter describe the geodesic and cut-locus geometry of flat generalized Klein bottles and establish their geodesic complexity; their results do not provide the present random-point distribution coupling, stochastic order, mean-square cutoff, or fractional transition [1]. Mémoli and Needham develop distance distributions as metric-measure invariants and discuss flat-torus comparisons but do not establish the particular same-volume Klein–torus dominance [2]. Balch, Peterson and Shonkwiler derive distance moments for round spherical lens-space quotients, not these flat glide-reflection quotients [3]. The distinction from the separate known Möbius-band-versus-cylinder comparison is essential: those spaces have boundary and different deck identifications; the compact boundaryless quotients here need independent sum/difference Haar coordinates, and the quantitative cutoff and nonanalytic exponent are new statements, not implied by that result. This priority comparison is provisional rather than a proof of novelty.

## Limitations
The result concerns rectangular-period flat quotients with the specified simultaneous inversion action and exact population laws. It does not treat arbitrary Bieberbach manifolds, arbitrary flat-torus lattices, noisy finite-sample reconstruction, point-conditioned distance laws, or nonuniform sampling. The exact finite-dimensional cutoff concerns the *second* moment and does not by itself provide analogous cutoff formulas for all powers. Historical-priority uncertainty remains because an equivalent older integral-geometric formulation may exist.
