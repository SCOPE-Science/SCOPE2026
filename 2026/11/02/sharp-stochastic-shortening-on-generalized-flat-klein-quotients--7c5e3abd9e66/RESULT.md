# Stochastic shortening and a sharp second-moment cutoff on higher flat Klein quotients

## Finding

Fix an integer \(k\ge2\), positive horizontal coordinate periods \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\), and a positive glide period \(b\). Let \(D_K\) and \(D_T\) denote intrinsic distances between two independent volume-uniform points on the flat generalized Klein quotient and the rectangular torus of the same coordinate periods, respectively. Then \(\Pr(D_K>r)\le\Pr(D_T>r)\) at every real threshold, the two laws are not identical, and \(\mathbb E[D_K^p]<\mathbb E[D_T^p]\) for every real \(p>0\).

With \(Q=\sum_i a_i^2/4\), the second-moment gain \(G=\mathbb E[D_T^2]-\mathbb E[D_K^2]\) is exactly \(\sum_i a_i^4/(360b^2)\) if and only if \(b^2\ge Q\); otherwise this expression is a strict upper bound. Also
\[
\frac12<\frac{\mathbb E[D_K^2]}{\mathbb E[D_T^2]}<1.
\]
Both constants are sharp even at each fixed \(k\ge2\) when all positive periods are allowed to vary.

## Assumptions and scope

The horizontal translation lattice is \(\Lambda=\prod_i a_i\mathbb Z\). The group defining \(K\) is generated by the horizontal translations and the glide \((x,y)\mapsto(-x,y+b)\) of \(\mathbb R^k\times\mathbb R\). The group defining \(T\) is generated by the same horizontal translations and the vertical translation of length \(b\). Both quotients carry the induced flat metric and have volume \(b\prod_i a_i\). The asserted comparison is between two-point laws, not distances from a fixed base point. Strict stochastic comparison means ordered tails and nonidentical laws, not strict inequality at every threshold.

## Proof

**The two routes.** Sample each point uniformly in a fundamental rectangle \(\prod_i[0,a_i)\times[0,b)\), independently. Put \(t=|y-y'|\). Even powers of the glide give minimum squared distance \(S_0+t^2\), where \(S_0=\sum_i\operatorname{dist}(x_i-x_i',a_i\mathbb Z)^2\). Odd powers give minimum \(S_1+(b-t)^2\), where \(S_1=\sum_i\operatorname{dist}(x_i+x_i',a_i\mathbb Z)^2\). These are the only candidates: even and odd vertical translates each vary by multiples of \(2b\), and each horizontal minimization is independent of the vertical one.

The torus homomorphism \((x,x')\mapsto(x-x',x+x')\) on \((\mathbb R^k/\Lambda)^2\) is onto, since multiplication by 2 is onto. The pushforward of normalized Haar measure is normalized Haar measure, which is product measure on the two factors. Thus \(S_0,S_1\) are independent and identically distributed with the law of \(S=\sum_i U_i^2\), where the \(U_i\) are independent and uniform on \([0,a_i/2]\). They are independent of \(t\). Write \(S,S'\) for the horizontal costs attached to the shorter and longer vertical routes, respectively: relabeling based only on \(t\) preserves their joint law and independence from \(t\).

**Coupling.** Let \(z=\min(t,b-t)\). The density of \(t\) on \([0,b]\) is \(2(b-t)/b^2\), so \(z\) is uniform on \([0,b/2]\). Consequently \(J=(b-z)^2-z^2=b(b-2z)\) is uniform on \([0,b^2]\), independent of \(S,S'\). The two squared distance laws can be coupled as
\[
D_T^2=S+z^2,\qquad D_K^2=D_T^2-(S-S'-J)_+,
\]
where \(x_+=\max(x,0)\). Indeed the torus horizontal squared distance has the same law as \(S\) and is independent of \(z\). This gives the tail inequalities; the gain is positive on a set of positive measure because \(S>S'\) with positive probability and \(J\) has positive probability in every interval \((0,\eta)\) with \(\eta>0\). Strict decrease for all positive moments follows. For instance the CDFs agree for \(0\le r\le b/2\), since the longer vertical route has length at least \(b/2\) even before adding the horizontal cost, with equality only on a null set.

**Second moment.** Integrating over \(J\) gives
\[
G=\frac{\mathbb E[(S-S')_+^2]-\mathbb E[(S-S'-b^2)_+^2]}{2b^2}.
\]
Symmetry gives \(\mathbb E[(S-S')_+^2]=\operatorname{Var}(S)=\sum_i a_i^4/180\). The essential supremum of \(S-S'\) is \(Q\), as each independent horizontal coordinate can be made arbitrarily close to its corresponding endpoint in each copy, with positive probability. This proves the exact threshold and strict correction below it.

**Sharp uniform ratio.** With independent copies \(V_i\) of the \(U_i\),
\[
G\le\mathbb E[(S-S')_+]\le\sum_i\mathbb E[(U_i^2-V_i^2)_+]=\tfrac12\mathbb E[S].
\]
For the last equality, the integral over \(0\le v\le u\le A\) gives \(A^{-2}\int_0^A\int_0^u(u^2-v^2)\,dv\,du=A^2/6\), whereas \(\mathbb E[U^2]=A^2/3\). Since \(\mathbb E[D_T^2]=\mathbb E[S]+b^2/12\) and \(G>0\), both ratio inequalities are strict. For the upper endpoint, fix the horizontal periods and let \(b\to\infty\); the exact above-threshold formula makes \(G\to0\). For the lower endpoint at fixed \(k\), set \(a_1=2\), \(a_i=2\varepsilon\) for \(i>1\), and \(b=\varepsilon\). By realizing both horizontal costs with fixed independent unit uniforms and \(J=b^2 W\) with an independent unit uniform \(W\), the gain integrand converges boundedly to \((U_1^2-V_1^2)_+\) of expectation \(1/6\), while \(\mathbb E[D_T^2]\to1/3\). The ratio therefore approaches \(1/2\) with all periods strictly positive along the sequence.

## Relationship to prior work and limits

- In the ordinary flat Klein bottle (one horizontal coordinate), the pair-distance stochastic comparison is stated in the Resultary record [d7dcab7002eb](https://github.com/Resultary/2026/tree/main/2026/11/2/SCOPE-strict-stochastic-distance-dominance-and-cubic-diameter-tails-on--d7dcab7002eb). The exact ordinary-bottle second-moment gain, including the \(C^3\) but not \(C^4\) transition, is stated in [8a65b8a5d4b3](https://github.com/Resultary/2026/tree/main/2026/11/2/SCOPE-exact-two-point-squared-geodesic-distance-on-rectangular-flat-kl--8a65b8a5d4b3) using half-horizontal period \(A=a_1/2\). These distinct records contain the ordinary-bottle conclusions, and the common date does not determine priority. The ordinary-bottle formula and its transition are not claimed here.
- Davis and Recio-Mitter, *The geodesic complexity of n-dimensional Klein bottles*, [arXiv:1912.07411](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07411), treat the geometry of geodesics and the cut locus. Mémoli and Needham, *Distance distributions and inverse problems for metric measure spaces*, [arXiv:1810.09646](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09646), provide distance-distribution context. Neither is used as a two-point stochastic comparison for the present higher-dimensional glide quotients.
- The results are for rectangular lattices and simultaneous inversion of all horizontal coordinates. No cutoff for higher moments, classification for other deck actions, or fixed-base-point comparison is implied.
