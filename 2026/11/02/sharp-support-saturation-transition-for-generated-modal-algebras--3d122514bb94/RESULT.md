# Sharp support-saturation transition for generated modal algebras of random clustered chains

## Finding

Fix integers \(k,r\ge1\), and put \(q=2^k\). Give each world of a chain of \(m\) size-\(r\) clusters an independent uniform Boolean \(k\)-color vector. Let \(Z_m\) count the Boolean atoms of the modal subalgebra generated by the \(k\) proposition sets. The right-to-left palette reduction used below is a known normal form [1]. The result here is the probability law and growth of \(Z_m\) for this clustered ensemble.

**Finite law.** For nonempty \(C\subseteq[q]\) of size at most \(r\), let \(F_m(C;z)\) be the joint probability generating polynomial of the atom count and the current retained palette \(C\) in a suffix of length \(m\). Write \(p_j=j!S(r,j)/q^r\), where \(S(r,j)\) is a Stirling number of the second kind. Then
\[
 F_1(C;z)=p_{|C|}z^{|C|},\qquad
 F_{m+1}(C;z)=\left(\frac{|C|}{q}\right)^rF_m(C;z)
  +p_{|C|}z^{|C|}\sum_{A:\, C\not\subseteq A}F_m(A;z),
 \qquad \mathbb E[z^{Z_m}]=\sum_CF_m(C;z).
\]

**Limit and rate.** Couple the sizes by extending one independently colored suffix to the left. If \(r\ge q\), then \(Z_m\) stabilizes almost surely to a finite random integer with an exponential tail. If \(r<q\), then \(Z_m/m\to\mu_{q,r}>0\) almost surely. In the latter regime, write \(\pi_j\) for the stationary probability of each specified \(j\)-element retained palette. It is determined in descending order \(j=r,\ldots,1\) by
\[
 \pi_j=\frac{p_j\left(1-\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=j+1}^r\binom{q-j}{\ell-j}\pi_\ell\right)}{1-(j/q)^r+p_j},
\]
and the rate is
\[
 \mu_{q,r}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\binom qj\pi_j\left[q\left(1-\left(\frac{q-1}{q}\right)^r\right)
 -j\left(\left(\frac jq\right)^r-\left(\frac{j-1}q\right)^r\right)\right].
\]
In particular, for \((q,r)=(4,2)\), \(\pi_1=1/28\), \(\pi_2=1/7\), and \(\mu_{4,2}=159/112\).

## Assumptions and scope

The frame is a finite reflexive transitive total preorder with exactly \(m\) linearly ordered equivalence classes, each with exactly \(r\) worlds. A world accesses all worlds in its own or a later cluster and no others. The modal operator is existential forward accessibility \(\Diamond\), with the usual Boolean operations. The algebra has cardinality \(2^{Z_m}\); \(Z_m\) counts atoms, not generators or worlds. Colors are independent and uniform over \([q]\), and \(k,r\) are held fixed as \(m\) grows. The almost-sure assertions use the specified suffix coupling.

## Proof

**Known palette reduction.** In a fixed valuation, write \(C_i\) for the set of colors of cluster \(i\). Scan from the terminal cluster toward the initial one, retaining the first palette and then retaining a palette only if it is not a subset of the last retained one. The palette-word normal form [1, Proof §§1–3] shows that the colored bisimulation quotient has one state per distinct color in each retained cluster. On a finite frame, modal formulas separate distinct bisimulation classes and define their unions, so the Boolean atoms are precisely those classes. Thus the number of atoms is the sum of the retained palette sizes. This uses the previously known quotient result, not a new assertion of deterministic palette rigidity.

**Probability recursion.** A fixed \(j\)-element palette occurs with probability \(p_j\), since \(j!S(r,j)\) color assignments are onto it. Reveal the terminal cluster first and add fresh clusters to the left. With current retained palette \(A\), a fresh palette \(D\) is wholly within \(A\) with probability \((|A|/q)^r\); then the state is unchanged and no atoms are added. Otherwise the state becomes \(D\) and the atom count increases by \(|D|\). Conditioning on these two cases gives the finite recursion, including the initial reward \(|C|\).

**Full-palette absorption.** If \(r\ge q\), a fresh cluster has full palette \([q]\) with probability \(q!S(r,q)/q^r>0\). After the first full palette is reached, every cluster revealed further left is discarded. Its waiting time \(G\) is geometric, indexed starting at one. Thus the monotone count stabilizes with \(Z_\infty\le qG\), giving the exponential tail bound.

**Stationary reward below saturation.** If \(r<q\), the retained-palette chain is irreducible: from \(A\) it can move directly to any \(D\not\subseteq A\); if \(D\subseteq A\), it can first move to a singleton outside \(A\) and then to \(D\). Every state has positive holding probability. The stationary law is invariant under color permutations. Fix a \(j\)-element palette \(C\); stationarity gives
\[
 \pi_j[1-(j/q)^r]=p_j\left[1-\sum_{A\supseteq C}\pi(A)\right].
\]
Move the \(A=C\) term to the left and group strict supersets by size to obtain the triangular recursion. The expected reward from a state of size \(j\) is the expected number of distinct colors in a fresh cluster minus the expected number counted only on the event that all sampled colors are in the current palette. The first expectation is \(q[1-((q-1)/q)^r]\). For each of the \(j\) colors in the current palette the probability of being sampled while all draws stay in the palette is \((j/q)^r-((j-1)/q)^r\). This proves the reward formula. The reward is a bounded function of two successive chain states (zero on a hold and the size of the new state on a move). The finite-state ergodic theorem for transitions gives the almost-sure average reward in the displayed formula; the initial reward has no effect on the limit. Each state has positive probability of adding a palette, so the stationary mean is positive. Rationality follows from the triangular recursion and strictly positive denominators. For \((q,r)=(4,2)\), the stationary masses on sizes one and two are \(1/7\) and \(6/7\), respectively; their conditional rewards are \(27/16\) and \(22/16\), giving \(159/112\).

## Verification

The companion standard-library `checker.py` compares the known palette count with finite bisimulation refinement, checks finite distributions by word enumeration in three configurations, and tests the stationary recurrence in 21 unsaturated parameter settings, including the rational example above. Such checks corroborate finite examples; the unbounded-size conclusions follow from the geometric bound and finite-state ergodic theorem.

## Relationship to prior work

The deterministic atom count follows from the established canonical palette-word quotient [1]. The adjacent-palette full-generation criterion in [2] is related but asks when every world forms a separate atom, not for the random distribution of the generated size. The singleton-cluster case \(r=1\) is already covered by the colored-run and binomial collapse law in [3]; its limiting slope is \(1-1/q\). Agnew's local-finiteness theorem [4, Thm. 3.20] concerns the full complex algebra of a transitive frame, not the law of random finitely generated subalgebras on fixed-size clustered windows. The support-occupancy law for non-contingency algebras [5] counts distinct palettes in disjoint S5 classes, not an ordered sequence of retained palettes. The comparison here is specifically for the joint finite law, fixed-size saturation boundary and explicit stationary growth-rate calculation in the forward clustered setting. Ordinary finite-state Markov theory is a tool, not a novelty claim.

## Limitations

The result does not address correlated colors, nonuniform valuations, unequal cluster sizes, modalities in both directions, or general transitive frames. The single-color-run law at \(r=1\) and the deterministic reduction are not claimed as new. The literature comparison is limited to the statements inspected.
