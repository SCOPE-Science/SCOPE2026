# Scientific assessment of the revised claim

**Correctness.** The established colored palette-word quotient gives one Boolean atom per color in each retained palette. Independent uniform coloring then determines all transitions and rewards of the retained-palette process. Full-palette absorption bounds the limiting count by a multiple of a geometric waiting time. Below saturation, a singleton outside the current palette connects any otherwise unavailable transition; finite-state stationarity and the ergodic theorem give the positive linear rate. Superset balance yields a triangular recurrence and the rate 159/112 at four colors and two worlds per cluster. The companion finite checks cannot replace the limit proof.

**Originality.** The earlier canonical palette-word result already establishes the deterministic quotient, and the earlier finite reflexive-chain law covers the singleton-cluster case. The revised statement credits both. It is the distribution and fixed-size asymptotic calculation for the random clustered setting, in particular the joint finite probability recursion and the explicit stationary growth rate, that are distinguished from earlier work. Agnew's full-complex-algebra local-finiteness theorem and the support-occupancy law for disjoint non-contingency classes do not supply these ordered-cluster probabilities. This comparison supersedes the earlier novelty discussion of the deterministic reduction; it is not an exhaustive priority claim.

**Value.** The result resolves the distribution and long-window size of a finitely generated modal algebra in a specified natural random ensemble. A structural cutoff distinguishes a finite atom count from linear growth, and the triangular stationary formula determines the coefficient across the nonsaturated parameter range rather than giving only an existence theorem or a single enumerated example.
