# Scientific review of the two-inertia frequency-lag theorem

**Correctness: PASS.** The complex response is the parallel sum of two first-order responses; differentiating its principal phase produces a quadratic in squared frequency. Both endpoint coefficients are positive. The discriminant factors with a strictly positive prefactor into the explicit concave quadratic \(Q_r(m)\), and the companion identity \(B+Q_r(m)=-6rm\) resolves the potentially delicate positive-root condition. The discriminant of \(Q_r\) gives precisely \(7+4\sqrt3\); evaluation at \(1,r,\sqrt r\) fixes all root locations. At equality the derivative has one isolated zero and the function remains strictly increasing. Symbolic and exact-rational checks in the package replay successfully. Finite checking is not used as proof of infinitely many cases.

**Originality: PASS under inspected sources, with residual historical-priority risk.** Cowan, Voigt and Abbot (arXiv:1205.5034, original 2012 version) provide the physically motivated single-pole phase law and state that mixed high-/low-thermal-inertia surfaces mask simple phase interpretation, but not the universal two-pole contrast threshold or the complete stationary-frequency classification. The 2004 Gaidos–Williams work and 2005 Gaidos–Moskovitz–Williams paper concern seasonal/thermal lightcurves; only their abstracts were accessible for comparative inspection, so no whole-document absence is asserted. General passive two-time-constant electronic or dielectric filter theory could contain an equivalent theorem. Searches for two-Debye nonmonotonic phase, two-relaxation phase reversals and mixed thermal phase lag did not identify an immediately dominating exact threshold; negative searches are not proof of global novelty.

**Value: PASS.** A planetary observer may use forcing periods to infer thermal inertia, but the same response can have a lag that decreases over a frequency interval when two surface classes are mixed. The result gives an exact, model-internal obstruction boundary and a complete mixing-fraction and frequency classification, rather than merely exhibiting an example. Its significance is a precise diagnostic of a known inverse-problem ambiguity, not a field-tested exoplanet inference method.

**Limitations.** Independent parallel linear reservoirs with common forcing and constant positive weights; no thermal coupling or nonlinear radiative climate dynamics. Broad filter-theoretic historical priority requires independent human assessment.
