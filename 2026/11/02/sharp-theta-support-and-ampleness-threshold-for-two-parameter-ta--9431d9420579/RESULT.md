# Sharp theta-support and ampleness threshold for two-parameter Tate degenerations

## Finding
Let \(R=\mathbb C[[u,v]]\), \(S=\operatorname{Spec}R\), and \(q=uv\). Let \(G\to S\) denote the smooth semiabelian part of the Tate degeneration associated with the rank-one period \(q\), as in Kallal [1, Section 4.1]. Thus its generic fiber is an elliptic curve and its central fiber is the split torus \(G_0\cong\mathbb G_{m,\mathbb C}\). Fix integers \(d\ge1\) and \(e,f\in\mathbb Z\). The Faltings--Chai--Lan degeneration datum with character and period lattices \(X=Y=\mathbb Z\) has
\[
\tau(y,\chi)=q^{y\chi},\qquad\phi(y)=dy,\qquad
\psi(y)=u^{ey+d y(y-1)/2}v^{fy+d y(y-1)/2}.
\]
It defines a rigidified invertible sheaf \(\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{d,e,f}\) of degree \(d\) on the generic elliptic fiber. The positivity condition on the data follows from \(d>0\) (the valuations of \(\psi(y)\) grow quadratically).

**Theorem.** The invertible sheaf \(\mathcal L\) is ample on the quasi-compact scheme \(G\) exactly when
\[
|e-f|<d.
\]
For every \(N\ge1\), write \(V_N\) for the image of the restriction map
\[
H^0(G,\mathcal L^{\otimes N})\longrightarrow H^0(G_0,\mathcal O_{G_0})=\mathbb C[Z,Z^{-1}],
\]
using the canonical formal-torus trivialization. Put \(a_N=-N\min(e,f)\) and \(b_N=N(d-\max(e,f))\). If \(e\ne f\) and \(a_N\le b_N\), then
\[
V_N=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{Z^j:a_N\le j\le b_N\},\quad
\dim V_N=N(d-|e-f|)+1.
\]
If \(e\ne f\) and \(a_N>b_N\), then \(V_N=0\). If \(e=f\), then
\[
V_N=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\bigl(\{Z^{a_N+1},\ldots,Z^{b_N-1}\}\cup\{Z^{a_N}+Z^{b_N}\}\bigr),
\qquad\dim V_N=Nd.
\]
The bases are literal: no quotient by a generic-fiber section space is implicit.

## Assumptions and scope
All schemes and fibers are over characteristic zero. The base is the two-variable *complete* regular local ring above, not an arbitrary two-dimensional base. The sheaf is defined by the displayed cocycle, including possibly negative linear exponents, and is an actual invertible sheaf on the smooth semiabelian degeneration by the classification recalled in [1]. Here 'ample' means ample on the nonproper scheme \(G\), not merely on its generic elliptic fiber. The relative torus trivialization fixes the exponents of \(Z\). All assertions hold for every integer triple \((d,e,f)\) in the displayed range and every integer \(N\ge1\).

## Proof
**1. Integral theta basis.** Put \(D=Nd\). The Fourier expansion principle for the split degeneration [1, Section 2, formula (2.4); 2, Section 1.5] identifies sections with convergent formal Laurent expansions \(\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\sigma_j Z^j\) whose coefficients belong to \(R\) and obey
\[
\sigma_{j+D}=u^{Ne+j}v^{Nf+j}\sigma_j.
\tag{1}
\]
For each class \(c\in\{0,\ldots,D-1\}\), define integer quadratics
\[
A_c(k)=k(Ne+c)+\tfrac D2 k(k-1),\qquad
B_c(k)=k(Nf+c)+\tfrac D2 k(k-1),\quad k\in\mathbb Z.
\]
Let \(\alpha_c=\min_{k\in\mathbb Z}A_c(k)\) and \(\beta_c=\min_{k\in\mathbb Z}B_c(k)\). The minimum exists since \(D>0\); it is nonpositive since the value at zero is zero. Equation (1) makes each residue class a free rank-one \(R\)-module generated by
\[
\Theta_c=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}
 u^{A_c(k)-\alpha_c}v^{B_c(k)-\beta_c}Z^{c+kD}.
\tag{2}
\]
Both exponents are nonnegative integers, grow quadratically, and ensure convergence in the \((u,v)\)-adic topology. The uniqueness of coefficients and the integrality test in (1) give the complete theta basis \(\Theta_0,\ldots,\Theta_{D-1}\) for the integral global-section module. The use of the Fourier expansion principle, rather than an assertion that every formal series automatically algebraizes, is essential here.

**2. Exact central restriction.** The reduction of \(\Theta_c\) modulo \((u,v)\) has one term \(Z^{c+kD}\) for each integer \(k\) simultaneously minimizing \(A_c\) and \(B_c\). A coefficient at a general exponent \(j\) can have nonzero reduction only if both its forward and backward neighbors in (1) are integral, namely
\[
Ne+j\ge0,\quad Nf+j\ge0,\quad N(d-e)-j\ge0,\quad N(d-f)-j\ge0.
\]
These inequalities are equivalent to \(a_N\le j\le b_N\). Conversely, every integer in that interval minimizes both quadratic exponent functions within its class. If \(e\ne f\), the interval has length strictly less than \(D\), so no two of its integers share a residue class modulo \(D\); each appears as a single Laurent monomial in the reduction of one theta generator. If \(e=f\), the interval has length exactly \(D\), so its two endpoints share one residue class; equation (1) shows they occur in that generator with equal scalar coefficients, and the other \(D-1\) generators restrict to the interior monomials. This proves all the spaces and dimensions claimed.

**3. Failure of ampleness on and beyond the wall.** If \(|e-f|>d\), every positive-power global section vanishes identically on the central torus. At equality, the restriction images of all positive powers have dimension one, spanned by one character; on the central torus all their nonvanishing loci are either empty or the whole torus. Neither behavior can occur for an ample invertible sheaf: positive-power section nonvanishing loci must form a basis of affine open neighborhoods, in particular distinguish two different central-torus points. Hence \(\mathcal L\) is not ample when \(|e-f|\ge d\).

**4. Ampleness inside the wall.** Suppose \(|e-f|<d\). Choose \(N\) such that \(D=Nd\ge3\) and \(N(d-|e-f|)\ge2\). The full set of theta sections \(\Theta_c\) has no common zero on any geometric fiber. Indeed, on the central torus its surviving characters include two consecutive monomials, so their projective ratios recover \(Z\). If \(e=f\), the two consecutive interior monomials do the same. On either generic boundary fiber \(u=0\) or \(v=0\), away from the closed point, the reduction of (2) is the standard rank-\(D\) Tate/nodal-cubic theta linear system: \(D-1\) interior monomials and an endpoint binomial with nonzero coefficient. This separates points and tangent vectors of the torus for \(D\ge3\). On fibers where \(uv\ne0\), the sections constitute the complete degree-\(D\) linear system on a smooth genus-one curve; it is very ample for \(D\ge3\). The theta basis remains a basis on these fibers by the integral Fourier description and inversion of the boundary parameters.

Consequently the theta sections define an \(S\)-morphism \(\Phi:G\to\mathbb P_R^{D-1}\), with \(\Phi^*\mathcal O(1)=\mathcal L^{\otimes N}\), which is injective and unramified on every geometric fiber. Hence it is universally injective and unramified, thus an immersion (an unramified universally injective morphism of finite type is a locally closed immersion). The pullback of \(\mathcal O(1)\) under this immersion is relatively ample. Because \(S\) is affine, \(\mathcal L\) is ample. This proves the if-and-only-if statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Kallal [1, Section 4.1] gives the degree-one counterexample corresponding to \((d,e,f)=(1,1,0)\). It proves that generic ampleness need not imply total-space ampleness, but does not classify the entire integer parameter family or calculate the special-fiber restriction spaces. The general Fourier expansion principle of Faltings--Chai--Lan, recalled in [1, Section 2], and the related Tate cohomology treatment [2] are the key inputs for constructing the explicit theta basis. Their general formalism is not represented here as novel. The sharp wall and all-power restriction calculation are the specific additional statements. No claim of historical uniqueness or independent validation is made.

## Limitations
The criterion is specific to the split one-dimensional Tate degeneration with period \(uv\), a complete regular two-variable base and cocycles with integral monomial linear factors. It does not classify arbitrary semistable abelian schemes, higher-dimensional degeneration cones, nonsplit tori, all rigidified line bundles on other base rings, or other notions of generic positivity. The global-section identification relies on the published Fourier expansion principle; a reader should check its precise formal-model hypotheses when transporting the argument to a different setting. Literature priority remains provisional.
