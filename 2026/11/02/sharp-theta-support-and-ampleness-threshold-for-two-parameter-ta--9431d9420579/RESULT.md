# Sharp theta-support and ampleness threshold for two-parameter Tate degenerations

## Finding
Let \(R=\mathbb C[[u,v]]\), \(S=\operatorname{Spec}R\), and \(q=uv\). Let \(G\to S\) denote the smooth semiabelian part of the Tate degeneration associated with the rank-one period \(q\), as in Kallal [1, Section 4.1]. Thus its generic fiber is an elliptic curve and its central fiber is the split torus \(G_0\cong\mathbb G_{m,\mathbb C}\). Fix integers \(d\ge1\) and \(e,f\in\mathbb Z\). The Faltings--Chai--Lan degeneration datum with character and period lattices \(X=Y=\mathbb Z\) has
\[
\tau(y,\chi)=q^{y\chi},\qquad\phi(y)=dy,\qquad
\psi(y)=u^{ey+d y(y-1)/2}v^{fy+d y(y-1)/2}.
\]
It defines a rigidified invertible sheaf \(\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{d,e,f}\) of degree \(d\) on the generic elliptic fiber. The positivity condition on the data follows from \(d>0\) (the valuations of \(\psi(y)\) grow quadratically).

**Theorem.** The invertible sheaf \(\mathcal L\) is ample on the quasi-compact scheme \(G\) exactly when
\[
|e-f|<d.
\]
For every \(N\ge1\), write \(V_N\) for the image of the restriction map
\[
H^0(G,\mathcal L^{\otimes N})\longrightarrow H^0(G_0,\mathcal O_{G_0})=\mathbb C[Z,Z^{-1}],
\]
using the canonical formal-torus trivialization. Put \(a_N=-N\min(e,f)\) and \(b_N=N(d-\max(e,f))\). If \(e\ne f\) and \(a_N\le b_N\), then
\[
V_N=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{Z^j:a_N\le j\le b_N\},\quad
\dim V_N=N(d-|e-f|)+1.
\]
If \(e\ne f\) and \(a_N>b_N\), then \(V_N=0\). If \(e=f\), then
\[
V_N=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\bigl(\{Z^{a_N+1},\ldots,Z^{b_N-1}\}\cup\{Z^{a_N}+Z^{b_N}\}\bigr),
\qquad\dim V_N=Nd.
\]
The bases are literal: no quotient by a generic-fiber section space is implicit.

## Assumptions and scope
All schemes and fibers are over characteristic zero. The base is the two-variable *complete* regular local ring above, not an arbitrary two-dimensional base. The sheaf is defined by the displayed cocycle, including possibly negative linear exponents, and is an actual invertible sheaf on the smooth semiabelian degeneration by the classification recalled in [1]. Here 'ample' means ample on the nonproper scheme \(G\), not merely on its generic elliptic fiber. The relative torus trivialization fixes the exponents of \(Z\). All assertions hold for every integer triple \((d,e,f)\) in the displayed range and every integer \(N\ge1\).

## Proof
**1. Integral theta basis.** Put \(D=Nd\). By the Fourier expansion principle for the split degeneration [1, Section 2, formulas (2.2) and (2.4); 2, Section 1.5], global sections inject into convergent formal Laurent expansions \(\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\sigma_j Z^j\) whose coefficients belong to \(R\) and obey
\[
\sigma_{j+D}=u^{Ne+j}v^{Nf+j}\sigma_j.
\tag{1}
\]
For each class \(c\in\{0,\ldots,D-1\}\), define integer quadratics
\[
A_c(k)=k(Ne+c)+\tfrac D2 k(k-1),\qquad
B_c(k)=k(Nf+c)+\tfrac D2 k(k-1),\quad k\in\mathbb Z.
\]
Let \(\alpha_c=\min_{k\in\mathbb Z}A_c(k)\) and \(\beta_c=\min_{k\in\mathbb Z}B_c(k)\). The minimum exists since \(D>0\); it is nonpositive since the value at zero is zero. Equation (1) makes each residue class a free rank-one \(R\)-module generated by
\[
\Theta_c=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}
 u^{A_c(k)-\alpha_c}v^{B_c(k)-\beta_c}Z^{c+kD}.
\tag{2}
\]
Both exponents are nonnegative integers, grow quadratically, and ensure convergence in the \((u,v)\)-adic topology. Within each residue class, (1) determines every coefficient from the coefficient at \(c\); integrality forces that coefficient to be divisible by both \(u^{-\alpha_c}\) and \(v^{-\beta_c}\), hence by their product. Thus (2) is a basis of the free rank-\(D\) module of integral formal solutions of (1).

Here is why these particular solutions give actual global sections. Write \(K=\operatorname{Frac}R\). Global sections commute with flat localization, and the degree-\(D\) line bundle on the generic elliptic curve has \(D\) sections by Riemann–Roch. Thus the injection from algebraic sections to the free rank-\(D\) formal solution module is an isomorphism after tensoring with \(K\). For each formal generator \(\Theta_c\), some nonzero \(\rho\in R\) has the property that \(\rho\Theta_c\) is the expansion of an algebraic section \(s\). The rational section \(s/\rho\) is regular on the generic fiber. Let \(x\) be the identity point of the central torus. In the \((u,v)\mathcal O_{G,x}\)-adic completion the section \(s\) is divisible by \(\rho\), because its expansion is \(\rho\Theta_c\) in the formal line bundle trivialization. Faithful flatness of that completion gives divisibility in \(\mathcal O_{G,x}\) after trivializing the line bundle there.

Every vertical codimension-one point of \(G\) lies over a height-one base prime by flatness. Its fiber is irreducible (a torus or an elliptic curve), and a height-one point there must be the generic point of that fiber: locally the height-one base prime is principal and its inverse image cuts out the fiber over the localized DVR. Its closure contains the identity over that prime, and hence contains \(x\). So regularity of \(s/\rho\) at \(x\) rules out all vertical codimension-one poles. There are no horizontal poles, and the smooth scheme \(G\) over the regular base is normal. The rational section is therefore global by the codimension-one regularity criterion for sections of a line bundle on a normal scheme. Its expansion is \(\Theta_c\), establishing the claimed algebraic basis without a general assertion that formal sections algebraize.

**2. Exact central restriction.** The reduction of \(\Theta_c\) modulo \((u,v)\) has one term \(Z^{c+kD}\) for each integer \(k\) simultaneously minimizing \(A_c\) and \(B_c\). A coefficient at a general exponent \(j\) can have nonzero reduction only if both its forward and backward neighbors in (1) are integral, namely
\[
Ne+j\ge0,\quad Nf+j\ge0,\quad N(d-e)-j\ge0,\quad N(d-f)-j\ge0.
\]
These inequalities are equivalent to \(a_N\le j\le b_N\). Conversely, every integer in that interval minimizes both quadratic exponent functions within its class. If \(e\ne f\), the interval has length strictly less than \(D\), so no two of its integers share a residue class modulo \(D\); each appears as a single Laurent monomial in the reduction of one theta generator. If \(e=f\), the interval has length exactly \(D\), so its two endpoints share one residue class; equation (1) shows they occur in that generator with equal scalar coefficients, and the other \(D-1\) generators restrict to the interior monomials. This proves all the spaces and dimensions claimed.

**3. Failure of ampleness on and beyond the wall.** If \(|e-f|>d\), every positive-power global section vanishes identically on the central torus. At equality, the restriction images of all positive powers have dimension one, spanned by one character; on the central torus all their nonvanishing loci are either empty or the whole torus. Neither behavior can occur for an ample invertible sheaf: positive-power section nonvanishing loci must form a basis of affine open neighborhoods, in particular distinguish two different central-torus points. Hence \(\mathcal L\) is not ample when \(|e-f|\ge d\).

**4. Ampleness inside the wall.** Suppose \(|e-f|<d\). Choose \(N\) such that \(D=Nd\ge3\) and \(N(d-|e-f|)\ge2\). On the central torus the restrictions contain two consecutive monomials, including in the case \(e=f\) since then there are \(D-1\) interior monomials. They have no zeros on the torus and their ratio recovers \(Z\).

For the generic point of either boundary divisor, first flatly base change to the completed DVR \(\widehat{R_{(u)}}\) or \(\widehat{R_{(v)}}\), respectively. Global sections commute with that flat base change (apply flatness to the equalizer for a finite affine cover of the separated scheme). In the resulting one-parameter Tate degeneration, apply the same Fourier principle over the DVR. If \(t\) is its uniformizer and \(h=\operatorname{ord}_t(q)>0\), the valuation of the step from exponent \(j\) to \(j+D\) has the form \(m+hj\), with \(m\) the valuation of the power-\(N\) linear cocycle factor; any unit factors do not affect the minima. Normalizing each residue-class theta generator by its minimum valuation gives a nonzero reduction supported on its minimizing exponents. These generators are algebraic by the same rank and divisibility argument as in step 1, now with a single vertical prime and the torus identity specializing to a point on its closure. The minimizing interval is \([-m/h,D-m/h]\). Its strictly interior integers include at least \(D-1\) consecutive integers; none shares a residue class with another minimizing integer. Thus the boundary restrictions contain consecutive monomials, up to scaling by nonzero constants, and again are basepoint-free and recover the torus coordinate by a ratio. Since the sections over the completed DVR come by flat base change, these are properties of the linear system spanned by the original sections over the boundary residue field.

Over \(D(uv)\subset S\) the Tate model is a smooth proper genus-one curve. Localization is flat, and cohomology and base change on this proper family shows that its degree-\(D\) global sections specialize to the complete linear system in each fiber: \(H^1\) vanishes in all such fibers by duality and positive degree. These complete systems are very ample for \(D\ge3\).

The sections therefore define a morphism \(\Phi:G\to\mathbb P_R^{D-1}\) with \(\Phi^*\mathcal O(1)=\mathcal L^{\otimes N}\). On every geometric fiber over \(S\) the stated systems give finite fibers of \(\Phi\); hence \(\Phi\) is quasi-finite. It is separated since \(G\) is separated over \(S\). Zariski's Main Theorem factors it as an open immersion followed by a finite morphism, so it is quasi-affine. The pullback of an ample line bundle by a quasi-affine morphism is ample [3]. In particular \(\mathcal L^{\otimes N}\) is ample, and so is \(\mathcal L\). This proves the if-and-only-if statement.

## Relationship to prior work
Kallal [1, Section 4.1] gives the degree-one counterexample corresponding to \((d,e,f)=(1,1,0)\). It proves that generic ampleness need not imply total-space ampleness, but does not classify the entire integer parameter family or calculate the special-fiber restriction spaces. The general Fourier expansion principle of Faltings--Chai--Lan, recalled in [1, Section 2], and the related Tate cohomology treatment [2] are the key inputs for constructing the explicit theta basis. Their general formalism is not represented here as novel. The sharp wall and all-power restriction calculation are the specific additional statements. No claim of historical uniqueness or independent validation is made.

The Laurent recurrence and nonvanishing-locus obstruction already appear in [1], along with a sufficient ampleness condition via Lan's category \(\mathrm{DD}^{*}\) in the proof of Lemma 3.1. The strict-chamber sufficiency can also be recovered from that condition: for sufficiently large \(N\) a character shift \(j\) can be chosen with \(a_N+1\le j\le b_N-1\). After the shift, the first forward and backward exponents remain nonnegative for each additional character \(-1,0,1\); all larger translations are then nonnegative by the convex quadratics. Sufficiency in isolation is thus an application of a known criterion. The combined exact support classification (including both endpoints, the wall, and the zero spaces beyond it) and the necessity across this family are the claims compared here.

## Limitations
The criterion is specific to the split one-dimensional Tate degeneration with period \(uv\), a complete regular two-variable base and cocycles with integral monomial linear factors. It does not classify arbitrary semistable abelian schemes, higher-dimensional degeneration cones, nonsplit tori, all rigidified line bundles on other base rings, or other notions of generic positivity. The global-section identification relies on the published Fourier expansion principle; a reader should check its precise formal-model hypotheses when transporting the argument to a different setting. Literature priority remains provisional.
