# Scientific review — sharp three-regime seasonal-occupancy budget law

## Correctness — PASS

The object is the two-coordinate continuous-time cooperative colonization–extinction system with independent positive rate parameters and the joint targets \(p_*\ge u\), \(q_*\ge v\). The positive equilibrium exists exactly when the colonization-rate product exceeds the extinction-rate product, and inverting its two balance equations gives every interior target pair a unique realizing rate pair. This turns the original rate-constrained problem into the exact smooth cost \(C(p,q)=Ep/[q(1-p)]+Fq/[p(1-q)]\) on \([u,1)\times[v,1)\). Simultaneous radial reduction proves that an optimum cannot have both target inequalities strict. Each target boundary, after the corresponding odds substitution, has a strictly unimodal \(Az+B/z\) variable part, so its constrained optimum is explicit. The derivative argument at the first edge minimizer proves strict ordering of the two transition thresholds and excludes competing global minimizers. Positive equilibrium attraction follows by cooperative sub-/supersolution comparison. Boundary and positivity hypotheses are explicit. The independently executable, standard-library-only checker tests equilibria, thresholds, target feasibility, optimal costs, and numerous feasible perturbations across all three regimes; finite checks do not certify the theorem for all real parameters.

## Originality — PASS under inspected literature; historical-priority risk retained

The motivating Taylor–Hall paper (2011, DOI 10.1098/rsbl.2011.0916) establishes the seasonal occupancy model, persistence criterion, and equilibrium fractions; these are treated as known here. Its mathematical statements do not include the minimum linear colonization-rate budget for **two simultaneous positive equilibrium occupancy targets**, nor the three distinct target-binding regimes and exact thresholds. Ross–Pollett (2009) give a *single-habitat-type stochastic persistence-time* management optimum involving colonization and extinction; neither its assumptions nor conclusion implies this two-season equilibrium design. Santika et al. (2015) optimize a multi-action 50-year spatial occupancy objective numerically, not this two-coordinate exact equilibrium problem. Zhang–Lu–Pei (2018) solve spectral-decay-driven epidemic resource allocation, not lower bounds on endemic equilibrium occupancies. Conceptual equivalence to a two-node directed SIS mean-field model and the elementary odds transformation mean that an earlier equivalent general resource-allocation result is possible; it was not identified in targeted searches or relevant full-text inspections. The conclusion is a best-knowledge novelty judgment, not independent certification.

## Value — PASS

Simultaneously securing breeding and nonbreeding seasonal habitats is a motivated conservation target, while separately choosing two colonization intensities is a legitimate simplified management lever. The exact answer reveals both a robust central region in which two constraints bind and two outer regions where the cheapest policy must *exceed* one of the targets, with sharp transition values and a unique policy for all positive parameters. It distinguishes qualitative management regimes that the source equilibrium formula alone does not provide, and gives a reproducible benchmark for rate-allocation models. This does not establish empirical cost effectiveness or policies under annual transition probability caps, and does not address finite-horizon stochastic persistence.
