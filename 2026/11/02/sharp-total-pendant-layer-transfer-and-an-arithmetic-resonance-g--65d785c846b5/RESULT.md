# Sharp total pendant-layer transfer and an arithmetic resonance gap

## Finding
Let \(G\) be any finite undirected simple graph with real adjacency matrix \(A\). Attach one pendant site to every vertex, with each pendant edge of coupling strength \(c>0\); the quantum-walk Hamiltonian is
\[
H_c=\begin{pmatrix}A&cI\\cI&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Start the adjacency continuous-time quantum walk at a backbone vertex \(u\), and let \(p_u(t;c)\) be the **total** probability (summed over all pendant sites) after time \(t\ge0\). If \(P_\lambda\) is the orthogonal spectral projector of \(A\) for the distinct eigenvalue \(\lambda\), write \(w_\lambda=(P_\lambda)_{uu}\) and \(\Omega_\lambda=\sqrt{\lambda^2+4c^2}\). Then
\[
p_u(t;c)=\sum_{\lambda\in\operatorname{spec}(A)}w_\lambda\,\frac{4c^2}{\lambda^2+4c^2}\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\Omega_\lambda t}{2}\right).
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently the sharp spectral envelope is
\[
0\le p_u(t;c)\le B_u(c):=\sum_\lambda w_\lambda\frac{4c^2}{\lambda^2+4c^2}\le1.
\tag{2}
\]
For the distinct **absolute** base eigenvalues with positive local spectral weight, let \(\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_s\) be the corresponding distinct positive frequencies. The dynamical supremum equals the envelope,
\[
\sup_{t\ge0}p_u(t;c)=B_u(c),
\tag{3}
\]
**if and only if** every integer relation \(\sum_{j=1}^s q_j\Omega_j=0\) satisfies \(\sum_{j=1}^s q_j\equiv0\pmod2\). In particular, (3) holds for all \(c>0\) except at most a countable set, for every fixed finite graph and starting vertex. For a vertex with a nonzero edge, \(B_u(c)<1\) for all finite \(c\).

For the three-vertex path \(G=P_3\), start at an endpoint and set \(R(c)=\sqrt{1+1/(2c^2)}>1\). Then
\[
B_u(c)=\frac12+\frac{c^2}{1+2c^2}.
\]
If \(R(c)\) is irrational, the supremum equals this bound but is not attained at finite time. If \(R(c)=p/q\) in lowest terms, the bound is attained at finite time exactly when both positive integers \(p,q\) are odd; otherwise the supremum is strictly smaller. At \(c=1/\sqrt6\) the frequency ratio equals \(2\), and the **actual** global maximum is \(1/2\), strictly below the spectral envelope \(5/8\). These resonant obstructions are dense in the coupling range, although their union is countable.

## Assumptions and scope
The Hamiltonian acts on \(\mathbb C^n\oplus\mathbb C^n\), with no on-site energy or dissipation, with real positive identical backbone-to-pendant coupling \(c\), and with \(A\) the adjacency matrix of any finite simple undirected graph. Vertex labels are aligned between layers. The initial state is a single backbone vertex \((e_u,0)\). Total pendant probability is \(\Vert(0,I)e^{-itH_c}(e_u,0)\Vert^2\). The objective is the **supremum over unbounded observation times**, not a fixed-time maximum or a mixing-time estimate. Every statement applies to any number of vertices (including disconnected graphs); the strict \(B_u<1\) clause assumes \(u\) is not isolated. An empty-edge graph allows complete pairwise Rabi transfer.

## Proof
First decompose \(A=\sum_\lambda\lambda P_\lambda\). On each invariant subspace obtained from the \(\lambda\)-eigenspace in both layers, \(H_c\) equals the two-level matrix \(\begin{psmallmatrix}\lambda&c\\c&0\end{psmallmatrix}\). Its off-diagonal transition amplitude is \(-i(2c/\Omega_\lambda)e^{-i\lambda t/2}\sin(\Omega_\lambda t/2)\). Summing these amplitudes against \(P_\lambda e_u\) and using their pairwise orthogonality proves (1) exactly, including eigenvalue multiplicities. Each coefficient is nonnegative, giving (2). Since \(\sum_\lambda w_\lambda=1\) and \(\sum_\lambda\lambda^2w_\lambda=\Vert Ae_u\Vert^2=\deg(u)\), a nonisolated vertex has positive weight at some nonzero eigenvalue, and hence \(B_u(c)<1\).

For the exact supremum, collect equal absolute eigenvalues, so every retained coefficient is strictly positive and the frequencies are distinct. Equality in the sum of sine-squares is approached if and only if all phases \(\Omega_jt\) approach \(\pi\pmod{2\pi}\) simultaneously. The closure of the one-parameter torus subgroup generated by \((\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_s)\) consists exactly of phases annihilated by the integer relations among those frequencies. Evaluating an integer relation at the target \((\pi,\ldots,\pi)\) gives precisely the parity condition in (3). The forward orbit \(t\ge0\) has the same compact closure as the two-sided orbit (by recurrence), so restricting to nonnegative times does not change the supremum.

For genericity, write the distinct squared absolute eigenvalues as \(a_j\ge0\). For any fixed nonzero integer vector \(q\), the real-analytic function \(f_q(c)=\sum_jq_j\sqrt{a_j+4c^2}\) is not identically zero on \(c>0\). Indeed, continue the square roots in the complex \(c\)-plane: for each distinct \(a_j>0\) the square root has a separate branch point at \(i\sqrt{a_j}/2\); continuation around that branch point changes only that term's sign, proving its coefficient zero if the identity held. The \(a_j=0\) term is \(2c\) on the positive real axis and then also has zero coefficient. Thus each zero set is discrete, and the countable union over all nonzero integer \(q\) is at most countable. Off this set the frequencies are rationally independent, so (3) holds.

For \(P_3\) at an endpoint, the weights are \(w_0=1/2\) and \(w_{\sqrt2}+w_{-\sqrt2}=1/2\). Formula (1) reduces, with \(z=ct\), to
\[
p_u(t;c)=\frac12\sin^2 z+\frac{c^2}{1+2c^2}\sin^2(R(c)z).
\tag{4}
\]
The two sine-squares can approach one simultaneously for irrational \(R\) by Kronecker density. If \(R=p/q\) is reduced, both become one exactly when odd multiples of their half-periods coincide, which holds precisely for odd \(p,q\). If they have opposite parity, the periodic orbit in (4) is compact and misses \((1,1)\), giving a strict deficit. At \(c=1/\sqrt6\), put \(x=\sin^2z\in[0,1]\). Formula (4) becomes \(p_u=x-x^2/2\le1/2\), and equality occurs at \(x=1\); while \(B_u=5/8\). Rational ratios with opposite-parity reduced numerator and denominator are dense in \((1,\infty)\); continuity and surjectivity of \(R:(0,\infty)\to(1,\infty)\) give density of exceptional couplings.

## Relationship to prior work
Godsil's 2011 work develops the spectral treatment of continuous-time quantum walks and average mixing on graphs. Ackelsberg, Brehm, Chan, Mundinger and Tamon's 2016 preprint establishes spectral idempotents and state-transfer criteria for corona graphs, which supply closely related spectral ingredients. The present result addresses a different observable: **total population transferred from an initial backbone site into the complete pendant layer**, optimized over arbitrary observation times. The sharp parity criterion and the dense arithmetic resonance deficits are not asserted in the inspected portions of those studies. Those prior formulas can be used to derive the starting spectral identity (1), so novelty is claimed only for the exact global optimization/commensurability classification, not for corona eigenvalues or two-level dynamics. Earlier non-indexed treatments of layer occupation remain a priority risk.

## Limitations
The bound is generally a supremum rather than a time at which the transition is attained; the classification explicitly distinguishes these. Genericity means outside an at-most-countable set for a fixed graph, not a uniform finite-time approximation rate. The physical graph has unit-weight backbone edges; pendant edges have weight \(c\). No assertion is made for inequivalent pendant couplings, decoherence, continuous spectra, or bounds on the waiting time needed to approach the supremum. The work has received no independent audit.
