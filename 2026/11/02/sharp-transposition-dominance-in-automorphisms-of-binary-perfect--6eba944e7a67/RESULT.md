# Sharp transposition dominance in automorphisms of binary perfect evolution algebras

## Finding
For every integer \(n\ge 2\), let \(A\) be uniform on \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb F_2)\). Form the evolution algebra \(E_A\) with natural basis \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\) and multiplication \(e_i e_j=0\) for \(i\ne j\), and \(e_i^2=\sum_j A_{ij}e_j\). Then
\[
\Pr(\operatorname{Aut}(E_A)\ne 1)
= (1+o(1))n(n-1)2^{-2n}.
\]
Furthermore, conditional on having a nonidentity automorphism, the automorphism group is \(C_2\), generated by a single transposition of natural basis elements, with probability tending to one. For any particular transposition \(\tau\in S_n\), the exact probability that it is an automorphism equals
\[
 p_n=\frac{2^{1-2n}}{(1-2^{1-n})(1-2^{-n})}.
\]
Thus a random binary perfect evolution algebra is rigid with high probability, and the exceptional algebras overwhelmingly have one elementary symmetry.

## Assumptions and scope
The base field is exactly \(\mathbb F_2\); the model is the uniform distribution on *labeled*, nonsingular \(n\times n\) structure matrices. The automorphism group is that of the algebra, not a general matrix centralizer. The limit is \(n\to\infty\); there is no claim here for extension fields or uniformly sampled isomorphism classes (these are distinct distributions). The first displayed formula is asymptotic, while the transposition formula is exact for every \(n\ge2\). All matrix ranks, determinants, and commuting relations are taken over \(\mathbb F_2\).

## Proof
**Step 1: reduction to permutation symmetries.** For a perfect evolution algebra, the vectors \(e_i^2\) form a basis. If \(\phi\) is an algebra automorphism, \(\phi(e_i)\phi(e_j)=0\) for \(i\ne j\). Expanding in the independent basis of squares shows \(\phi(e_i)\) and \(\phi(e_j)\) have disjoint supports in the natural basis. The invertibility of \(\phi\) forces precisely one nonzero entry in each row and column of its matrix. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), every nonzero scalar is one, so \(\phi\) is a permutation matrix. Comparing \(\phi(e_i^2)\) with \(\phi(e_i)^2\) shows that it is an automorphism exactly when its permutation matrix commutes with \(A\). In particular, \(\operatorname{Aut}(E_A)=\{\sigma\in S_n:P_\sigma A=AP_\sigma\}\). This reduction is compatible with the established monomial-isomorphism theorem for perfect evolution algebras.

**Step 2: a fixed transposition.** Relabel coordinates so the permutation matrix of a transposition is \(I+N\), where \(N\) has rank one and satisfies \(N^2=0\). In a suitable basis \(N\) has its sole nonzero entry at position \((1,2)\). A general commuting matrix has block shape
\[
 X=\begin{pmatrix}a&b&u\\0&a&0\\0&v&D\end{pmatrix},
\]
where \(a,b\in\mathbb F_2\), \(u\) is a row and \(v\) a column of length \(n-2\), and \(D\in M_{n-2}(\mathbb F_2)\). The matrix is invertible iff \(a=1\) and \(D\) is invertible. Therefore the exact number of invertible matrices commuting with a fixed transposition is \(2^{2n-3}|\mathrm{GL}_{n-2}(\mathbb F_2)|\) (with the order of \(\mathrm{GL}_0\) defined as one). Since
\[
 |\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb F_2)|=2^{n^2}\prod_{j=1}^n(1-2^{-j}),
\]
division gives the exact expression for \(p_n\). In particular \(p_n=2^{1-2n}(1+o(1))\).

**Step 3: exclude larger-support permutations.** Let \(\sigma\) have support \(s\ge3\) and \(k\) nontrivial cycles of lengths \(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_k\), so \(k\le s/2\). There are \(f=n-s\) fixed points. The centralizer algebra of its permutation matrix has dimension
\[
 d(\sigma)=f^2+2fk+\sum_{i,j=1}^k\gcd(\lambda_i,\lambda_j).
\]
Indeed, the permutation module is a sum of \(\mathbb F_2[t]\)-cyclic modules \(\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^{\lambda_i}-1)\); the dimension of an inter-cycle homomorphism space is the degree of the gcd of the two defining polynomials, namely \(\gcd(\lambda_i,\lambda_j)\). Fixed-point cycles are length one. Each inner sum over \(j\) is at most \(s\), hence
\[
 d(\sigma)\le f^2+2fk+ks\le n^2-s(n-s/2).
\]
The infinite product \(c=\prod_{j\ge1}(1-2^{-j})\) is strictly positive, so \(|\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb F_2)|\ge c 2^{n^2}\). Consequently,
\[
 \Pr(AP_\sigma=P_\sigma A)\le c^{-1}2^{-s(n-s/2)}.
\]
There are at most \(n^s\) permutations with support \(s\). For \(3\le s\le n/2\), the total contribution is bounded by \(c^{-1}\sum_{s\ge3}(n2^{-3n/4})^s=O(n^3 2^{-9n/4})\). For \(s>n/2\), each codimension is at least \(3n^2/8\) and the total is at most \(c^{-1}n!2^{-3n^2/8}=2^{-\Omega(n^2)}\). Thus
\[
 \Pr(\exists\ \sigma\ne1:\ |\operatorname{supp}\sigma|\ge3,\ AP_\sigma=P_\sigma A)=o(n^2 2^{-2n}).
\]
The bounds hold for sufficiently large \(n\), which is all the asymptotic theorem requires.

**Step 4: rule out multiple transpositions.** If \(\tau\) and \(\rho\) are distinct transpositions preserving \(A\), then so does \(\tau\rho\). This product is a three-cycle or a product of two disjoint transpositions. Using the preceding exact dimension formula, its matrix-centralizer codimension is respectively \(4n-6\) or \(4n-8\). Hence, uniformly over pairs, the probability of the joint event is at most \(c^{-1}2^{-4n+8}\). There are \(O(n^4)\) ordered pairs; their summed probability is \(O(n^4 2^{-4n+8})=o(n^2 2^{-2n})\).

**Step 5: combine.** There are \(\binom n2\) transpositions, each with probability \(p_n\). Bonferroni's two inequalities and Step 4 imply that the probability of at least one is \(\binom n2 p_n+o(n^2 2^{-2n})\). Step 3 shows that other permutations do not change this leading term. Thus the probability of nontrivial automorphism is \(\binom n2 2^{1-2n}(1+o(1))=n(n-1)2^{-2n}(1+o(1))\). With conditional probability \(1-o(1)\), exactly one nontrivial permutation survives, and it is a transposition, yielding the claimed \(C_2\) structure.

## Verification
The accompanying standalone file `verify.py` enumerates all \(2^{n^2}\) binary matrices in dimensions \(n=2,3,4\), filters by an independent binary Gaussian-elimination test, and computes the centralizer in \(S_n\) by direct row/column index permutation. It checks \(|\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb F_2)|\), the exact transposition-centralizer count, and the Burnside orbit sum. The recorded results are: \(n=2\): \(6\) invertibles, \(2\) with nontrivial symmetry, \(2\) commuting with one specified transposition, \(4\) conjugacy orbits; \(n=3\): \(168,24,8,33\); and \(n=4\): \(20160,1344,192,908\). The checker prints `VERIFY_OK` on successful completion. These finite computations corroborate the finite identities; they are not the proof of the limiting assertion.

## Relationship to prior work
Sriwongsa–Zou (2022, arXiv:2202.01333) establishes the monomial-automorphism framework for perfect evolution algebras and studies prescribed automorphism groups. Wei–Zou (2023, DOI:10.1016/j.laa.2023.06.006) establishes an orbit enumeration framework and a general isomorphism-class counting formula for arbitrary finite fields. Costoya–Mayorga–Viruel (2024 preprint, arXiv:2401.05924) studies realizable permutation representations. Britnell–Wildon (2013, arXiv:1310.5261) provides background on matrix and permutation centralizers. These known reductions and exact counting frameworks are **not claimed as new**. The contribution assessed here is the sharp exceptional-automorphism *probability*, including the coefficient affected by conditioning on invertibility, and the conditional concentration onto a unique transposition. This event-level statement is not a formal consequence of a first-moment orbit-count formula alone; it requires control of all other supports and of intersecting transposition stabilizers.

## Limitations
The theorem is specific to \(\mathbb F_2\) and uniformly labeled invertible matrices. It does not give a classification of exceptional algebras, a finite-\(n\) closed formula for the total nontrivial-stabilizer probability, or a corresponding statement for uniformly sampled unlabeled classes. The Wei–Zou full paper could not be inspected from the available lawful arXiv or open-access pathways; its abstract and publication metadata were inspected, leaving residual uncertainty about an equivalent asymptotic buried in its full text. Wider graph-asymmetry literature could contain an equivalent binary invertible-adjacency statement under alternative terminology. No independent mathematical audit is represented.
