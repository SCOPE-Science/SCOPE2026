# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The stationary backward linear-filter representation gives a sum of independent spherical directions with deterministic weights conditional on the on/off trajectory. Orthogonality of different direction-pair terms proves the uniform \(2/d\) mean-square error bound. Stationary exponential run/rest ages yield a two-beta perpetuity, and contraction yields uniqueness. The upper-tail proof separates the first-run-small-discount event from the strongly constrained near-one feedback event, with a finite recursive estimate. The lower-tail proof bootstraps negative moments until the beta product identity yields its exact exponent and constant. The on/off mixture and first-moment normalization have been checked independently by stationary simulations. Risks: the law's near-zero prefactor is a perpetuity negative moment, not a closed elementary expression; finite stochastic checks do not certify the infinite proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

Sun–Ye–Podgornik (arXiv:2409.00578v1, Sections II.3–II.4 and IV) provide the correct finite-tumble model, an all-moments coordinate recursion, the coordinate-edge exponent and conversion to radial moments. The theorem here does not rebrand these known facts: it identifies a random limit of the squared *radius* as dimension tends to infinity, with a quantitative \(L^2\) bound, and proves two different probability-tail exponents and sharp constants for that limit. Fixed-dimensional moments in principle encode the whole distribution; deriving sharp scaling of a singular dimension limit and its lower edge is additional analysis, not a new stationary-process definition. Guéneau–Majumdar–Schehr (arXiv:2602.08436v1, Sections III–IV and appendices) treat zero rest duration, not the two-rate beta-discount process. Basu et al. (arXiv:1910.10083) use a lower-dimensional three-state model. Available indexed results on generic sphere-radius isotropization and diversity statistics have different hypotheses and conclusions. A broad specialized theorem for beta perpetuities could imply the edges; its possible existence remains a publication-priority risk. Neither indexing partition nor lack of an exact search hit is claimed to establish novelty on its own.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The limit exhibits nontrivial radial fluctuations despite high-dimensional angular orthogonality, and it separates two physically identifiable mechanisms: long runs create the rare near-turning-surface events, while long rest periods create rare near-center events. Exact powers and constants make this a structurally informative classification rather than a moment recomputation or arbitrary finite example. It is particularly motivated because the primary paper had an exact moment scheme but no explicit general-dimensional radial law. The result does not settle simultaneous dimension/edge scaling or a closed density formula.
