# Sharp two-route transit-time modality and a fixed-ratio moment alias

## Finding

A steady tracer impulse is routed independently through two parallel pathways: a one-reservoir route and a route of \(k\) identical reservoirs in series, with positive integer \(k\). For every set of positive rates and every nondegenerate route weight, the resulting residence-time density has an **exact sharp phase diagram**: it has either one or two strict peaks, counting a peak at time zero as a boundary peak. The transition is expressed by one explicitly characterized maximum of an elementary function, with a qualitatively different boundary behavior at \(k=2\) and \(k\ge3\). Moreover, even knowing the first two moments and the fixed ratio of the two reservoir rates does not determine whether two peaks exist: two explicit three-stage models with identical mean, variance, and rate ratio have different modal counts.

Write \(R=a/b\), where \(a,b>0\) are the fast-branch and serial-branch rates. For \(k\ge3\), define
\[
\Phi_{k,R}(s)=s^{k-2}(k-1-s)e^{(R-1)s},\qquad 0<s<k-1,
\]
let \(s_{k,R}\) be its unique maximizer, and put
\[
w_{k,R}=\frac{\Phi_{k,R}(s_{k,R})}{\Phi_{k,R}(s_{k,R})+(k-1)!R^2}.
\]
Then **exactly two strict peaks occur if and only if \(0<w<w_{k,R}\)**; otherwise there is exactly one, at zero. The positive maximizer is the unique root in \((0,k-1)\) of
\[
(R-1)s^2-(k-1)(R-2)s-(k-1)(k-2)=0.
\]
At \(w=w_{k,R}\), an interior horizontal shoulder is not a strict peak.

For \(k=2\), put \(w_0=(1+R^2)^{-1}\). If \(R\le2\), there is always exactly one peak: it is interior for \(0<w<w_0\), and at zero for \(w_0\le w<1\). If \(R>2\), put
\[
H_*=\frac{e^{R-2}}{R-1},\qquad w_*=\frac{H_*}{R^2+H_*}.
\]
Exactly two peaks occur precisely for \(w_0<w<w_*\). For \(0<w\le w_0\), there is one interior peak; for \(w_*\le w<1\), there is one boundary peak. In the special case \(k=1\), there is always only the peak at zero.

An exact moment-aliased pair arises at \(k=3\) and \(a/b=3\):
\[
(w,a,b)=\left(\frac7{16},\frac{11}{2},\frac{11}{6}\right)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(w,a,b)=\left(\frac{1971}{1972},\frac{495}{493},\frac{165}{493}\right).
\]
Both distributions have \(\mathbb ET=1\) and \(\mathbb ET^2=493/242\), hence variance \(251/242\). The first has two strict peaks; the second has one boundary peak.

## Assumptions and scope

Choose an integer \(k\ge1\), positive rates \(a,b\), and a mixing weight \(0<w<1\). On the physical half-line \(t\ge0\), define the normalized transit-time density
\[
f(t)=wae^{-at}+(1-w)\frac{b^k t^{k-1}e^{-bt}}{(k-1)!}.
\]
For the first route the transit time is exponential with rate \(a\). For the second it is the sum of \(k\) independent exponentials with common rate \(b\); an impulse takes one of these routes with probabilities \(w\) and \(1-w\). The resulting density integrates to one. A **strict peak** means a strict local maximum relative to the half-line \([0,\infty)\). In particular, \(t=0\) can be a strict boundary peak, while a stationary inflection or a horizontal shoulder does not count. All classification statements concern this precise time-density model, not a hydrologic response function generated by changing pre-event storage.

## Proof

**1. Dimensionless derivative.** Put \(s=bt\) and \(R=a/b\). For \(k\ge2\), differentiation and factoring a strictly positive quantity give
\[
\frac{f'(t)}{b^2 e^{-at}}=-wR^2+
\frac{1-w}{(k-1)!}
 s^{k-2}(k-1-s)e^{(R-1)s}.
\]
For \(s\ge k-1\), this derivative is strictly negative. For \(0<s<k-1\), its sign is that of \(\Phi_{k,R}(s)-C\), where \(C=(k-1)!wR^2/(1-w)>0\). Thus all positive stationary points lie in \((0,(k-1)/b)\), and crossing the level \(C\) in \(\Phi\) decides all peaks.

**2. Serial routes of length at least three.** If \(k\ge3\), then \(\Phi\) is positive in \((0,k-1)\), vanishes at both endpoints, and
\[
\frac{d}{ds}\log\Phi_{k,R}(s)
=\frac{k-2}{s}-\frac1{k-1-s}+R-1.
\]
Its derivative equals \(-(k-2)/s^2-1/(k-1-s)^2<0\). The logarithmic derivative decreases from positive infinity to negative infinity. It therefore vanishes at exactly one \(s_{k,R}\), making \(\Phi\) increase strictly and then decrease strictly. Clearing denominators yields the stated quadratic, whose root in \((0,k-1)\) is unique, including the case \(R=1\). Because \(f'(0)=-wa^2<0\), the origin is always a strict boundary peak. If \(C<\Phi(s_{k,R})\), there are exactly two positive derivative zeros, first a local minimum and then an interior strict maximum. If \(C\ge\Phi(s_{k,R})\), the derivative is nonpositive and has no sign-changing zero; there is only the boundary peak. Solving \(C<\Phi(s_{k,R})\) for \(w\) gives the claimed sharp inequality. Equality creates only a stationary shoulder.

**3. The two-stage case.** For \(k=2\), \(\Phi_{2,R}(s)=(1-s)e^{(R-1)s}\) on \((0,1)\), and \(\Phi(0)=1\). Its derivative has the sign of \(R-2-(R-1)s\). If \(R\le2\), \(\Phi\) decreases strictly, so the sign of \(f'(0)=b^2[(1-w)-wR^2]\) decides whether the sole peak is interior or at zero. If \(R>2\), \(\Phi\) rises from 1 to the strict maximum \(H_*=e^{R-2}/(R-1)>1\), reached at \(s_*=(R-2)/(R-1)\), and then falls to zero. A horizontal level \(C\) cuts this graph twice precisely when \(1<C<H_*\), equivalently \(w_0<w<w_*\). The first intersection is a local minimum of \(f\), and the second is an interior maximum, together with the strict boundary maximum. When \(C\le1\), the origin is not a strict maximum (including \(C=1\)); there is only one interior maximum. When \(C\ge H_*\), there is only the boundary maximum, with a nonpeak shoulder at equality. If \(k=1\), the density is a strictly decreasing positive mixture of exponential densities, giving the remaining case.

**4. Two moments do not identify the phase.** For all \(k\), independence and standard exponential moments give
\[
\mathbb ET=\frac wa+(1-w)\frac{k}{b},\qquad
\mathbb ET^2=\frac{2w}{a^2}+(1-w)\frac{k(k+1)}{b^2}.
\]
Substituting the two rational triples in the finding yields exactly the same first and second moments, as well as \(a/b=3\). To prove that their modal counts differ without relying on numerical optimization, for \(k=3,R=3\) the maximizing point is \(s_*=(1+\sqrt5)/2\), hence
\[
\Phi_*=(s_*-1)e^{2s_*},\qquad w_{3,3}=\frac{\Phi_*}{\Phi_*+18}.
\]
The rational inequalities \(8/5<s_*<5/3\) imply \(\Phi_*>(3/5)e^{16/5}\). The first seven Taylor terms for \(e^{16/5}\) already exceed \(70/3\), so \(\Phi_*>14\), giving \(w_{3,3}>14/32=7/16\). Conversely \(s_*<2\) and \(s_*-1<1\) imply \(\Phi_*<e^4<81\), hence \(w_{3,3}<81/99<1971/1972\). The preceding classification proves the contrasting modal counts by exact strict inequalities.

## Verification

The accompanying standalone `proof_check.py` can be executed with Python 3 using only its standard library. It computes both moment pairs with exact rational arithmetic, verifies exact rational bounds separating the two weights from the critical value, checks the critical-point formula, and compares the predicted peak counts with direct derivative sign changes at 800 sampled choices of stage number, rate ratio and weight. It prints three `PASS` messages when successful. The finite samples and numerical root searches do not prove the all-parameter result; the preceding derivative and monotonicity argument does.

## Relationship to prior work

A linear-reservoir chain has a gamma/Erlang impulse-transit distribution, while parallel independent routes mix transit densities; such exponential/gamma descriptions are established hydrological idealizations. Roa-García and Weiler (2010) distinguish response and transit-time distributions in watersheds. Heidbüchel and colleagues (2012), in their freely readable Sections 1–3, explicitly use exponential and gamma transfer functions and mass-weighted superpositions of transit-time distributions. Neither inspected hydrology study claims the sharp one-versus-two-peaks classification under the exact two-route steady model or the specified fixed-ratio two-moment alias.

The directly relevant mathematical predecessor is Došlá (2009), whose general mixture-modality criteria apply in principle, and whose Propositions 6–7 give explicit gamma results with, respectively, equal shapes or equal rates with both shapes above one. The discussion following Proposition 7 expressly leaves the different-rate, different-shape gamma mixture untreated as a general explicit classification. This result concerns its narrow exponential-versus-integer-Erlang subclass; it does **not** claim a general solution for all gamma mixtures. It adds an explicit phase boundary, boundary-mode classification and the concrete moment-aliasing construction. Those criteria may admit alternative derivations from earlier general mixture theorems, which remains a historical-priority risk. Johnson and Taaffe (1989, 1990) also study moment matching and density shapes of phase-type laws. Their common-order moment-matching results do not state this mixed-one-stage/\(k\)-stage threshold, but the full 1990 density-shape study was not accessible for a decisive comparison; this is a material priority risk. The result is claimed only within this reviewed scope, not as externally certified novelty.

## Limitations

The theorem holds for a time-invariant, linear, two-route system with positive rates, a fixed discrete serial-stage number and exact continuum transit-time densities. It does not cover variable-rate routing, reactive tracers, more than two parallel routes, nonlinear or transient catchments, parameter recovery from noisy finite samples, or full uniqueness of the mixture from its entire distribution. The time-zero peak is meaningful for an idealized impulse density but can be masked by nonideal temporal sampling. Equality at the critical weight yields a stationary shoulder rather than an additional strict peak. The literature comparison is not an independent exhaustive priority certification; older generic mixture criteria could imply this specialized formula after further work.
