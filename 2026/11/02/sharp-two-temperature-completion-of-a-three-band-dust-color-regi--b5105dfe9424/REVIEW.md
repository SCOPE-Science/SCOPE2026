# Scientific review: Three-band thermal-mixture color region

## Correctness

**PASS.** The normalized one-component color is the rational curve \(f(u)=u^3/(1-2u+2u^2)\). Positive thermal sums are exactly convex combinations of its points after first-band reweighting. Explicitly positive curvature establishes the strict lower bound and equality criterion; a direct rational identity gives the strict upper bound. A continuous family of two-point chords from the curve to its limiting end-to-end chord realizes every interior point, after reconstructing finite positive temperatures and amplitudes. The two-band impossibility and exact rational witness are separately proved. The packaged checker corroborates algebra and boundary cases with 4,008 assertions; its finite trials do not establish the universal statements.

## Originality

**PASS within the inspected literature, provisional.** Reach et al. (1995), Finkbeiner et al. (1999), Shetty et al. (2009), and Meisner and Finkbeiner (2014), and the Planck Collaboration (2011) fixed-β color-color diagram were compared at the level of models and exact implications. They establish multi-temperature dust spectral fitting and, in Shetty et al., three-flux methods; neither those generic techniques nor the classical convex-hull principle are being claimed as novel. The assessed new result is the sharp explicit three-band image including necessary and sufficient inequalities, all equality/nonattainment cases, and two-temperature sufficiency for every interior point. Searches of published mathematical records and relevant astrophysical sources found no directly covering exact classification. Residual risk: overlooked mathematical spectroscopy and color-gamut literature, or a stronger previous result specialized to these frequencies. The search does not prove priority and no independent historical audit was performed.

## Value

**PASS.** Distinguishing line-of-sight temperature mixing from changes in fitted emissivity is a documented astrophysical inference problem. With the emissivity index fixed independently, this complete feasible-domain theorem gives an exact consistency test and a sharp minimum of three monochromatic measurements for noiseless mixture detection. It also proves that two thermal components already realize every physically feasible three-band color, so adding more positive components cannot resolve this particular inverse diagnostic. The two-sided bounds exclude some observed color pairs even from arbitrarily many components under the idealized common-index assumptions. These are exact structural statements, not claims about finite signal-to-noise observations.

The result applies to three monochromatic frequencies in geometric progression, positive finite temperatures, known common spectral index, unrestricted positive coefficients, exact calibration and an optically thin model. General bandpasses, noisy fitting, optical-depth effects and physical component identification remain outside scope.
