With two competing permanent proxies, a proxy's normalized a-priori Penrose–Banzhaf power is at most \(1/(2+m\binom{m-1}{(m-1)/2}/2^{m-1})\), uniformly over every independent delegation probability, and the bound is sharp. Hence even though each proxy may receive linearly many delegated ballots, its relative pivotal power always vanishes at the sharp rate \(\sqrt{\pi/(2m)}\) or faster as the odd number \(m\) of delegators grows.
