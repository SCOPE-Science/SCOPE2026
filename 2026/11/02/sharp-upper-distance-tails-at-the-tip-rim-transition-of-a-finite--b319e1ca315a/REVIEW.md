The exact cone-sector formula reduces the random distance to two independent radii with density \(2r\) and independent uniform short angular separation \(\Theta\in[0,\pi]\). Convexity in the radial coordinates gives precisely two possible extremal strata. Near tip–rim pairs, the polar-area density vanishes linearly with distance from the tip, producing a cubic law and coefficient \(4\tan(\pi c)/(3\pi c)\); near diametrically separated rim pairs, three independent linear constraints produce coefficient \(2/(3\pi c\cos(\pi c/2)\sin^2(\pi c/2))\). At the tie \(c=1/3\), the strata are disjoint and their coefficients sum. For the disk \(c=1\), the angular derivative vanishes and the angular scale is \(\sqrt\varepsilon\), giving the \(5/2\)-power with coefficient \(32/(15\pi)\). All limiting integrals are finite, with uniform localization and dominated-convergence justifications. ## Mathematical value — PASS
For a compact cone, the maximum distance is generated by two geometrically distinct kinds of point pair, which exchange dominance at an exact opening parameter. The near-maximum probability gives a finer and genuinely natural metric-measure invariant than the maximum distance itself. The coexisting critical maximizer families and noncommuting disk limit create four different sharp coefficient/exponent regimes; this is a full classification of a natural probability asymptotic, not a chosen numerical slice.
