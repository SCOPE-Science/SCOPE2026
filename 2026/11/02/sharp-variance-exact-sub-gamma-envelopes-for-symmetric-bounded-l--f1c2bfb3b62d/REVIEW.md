# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For centered symmetric infinitely divisible laws with Lévy support in \([-L,L]\), the bounded symmetric Lévy–Khintchine exponent and the variance-weighted canonical measure yield exactly \(K(t)=Vt^2\int r(tu)\mu(du)/2\). The scale supremum follows algebraically. Since \(r\) is increasing, all variance profiles satisfying a minimum short-jump share are pointwise dominated by the explicit two-atom profile; finite nonzero maximizing arguments and strictness give the precise equality case. An exact positive-coefficient quotient argument proves the unique universal optimizer. The Gaussian dilution rate follows by a two-region large-argument estimate. A standard-library rational checker passed 1,625 assertions, including a certified optimizer bracket; the general mathematical conclusion relies on the analytical arguments, not finite enumeration.

## Originality — PASS, with priority qualification

Chen (arXiv:2610.00032v1) develops variance-fixed optimal scales for **spectrally positive** infinitely divisible laws. Chen and Wang (arXiv:2609.02590v1) provide directional variational formulas for **general** infinitely divisible laws and already discuss individual Skellam optimizers; those results are acknowledged and are not claimed as original. Targeted comparisons of statement implications found no directly covering published class-wide symmetric bounded-Lévy extremal envelope, sharp variance-share refinement, or rigidity and Gaussian-dilution boundary result. Related indexed results concern different invariants. The complete Chen–Wang text was inaccessible, so possible prior stronger coverage and classical priority remain genuine risks; a negative search is not a proof of novelty.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result sharpens a natural concentration inequality from the standard \(L/3\) variance-exact scale to the attained optimal \(0.1742179596517\ldots L\), identifies precisely when equality can occur throughout a large class, and shows how a prescribed amount of short-jump or Gaussian variance forces a strict quantitative reduction. The sharp two-magnitude extremizers and logarithmic Gaussian dilution are structurally meaningful rather than arbitrary numerical slices.
