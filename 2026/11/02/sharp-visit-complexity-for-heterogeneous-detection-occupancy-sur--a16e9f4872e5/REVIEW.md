# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The law of the full binary visit history is Möbius-equivalent to the values of one Laplace transform at all positive duration subset sums. Two mixtures with at most \(k\) positive rates and a fixed zero-rate atom differ by at most \(2k+1\) exponentials. The elementary exponential zero-count theorem forces equality when the number of positive subset sums is at least \(2k\). When there are fewer, the open parameter domain has dimension \(2k\) and factors continuously through a lower-dimensional set of transform evaluations; invariance of domain proves global nonidentification. Binary-weighted durations realize all subset sums, and equal duration yields exactly \(n\) distinct sums. Exact-rational checks in `verify.py` passed for four different class counts and explicit ambiguous/identifiable three-visit designs. The analytic proof does not rely on these finite checks.

## Originality — PASS, provisional historical priority
Classical occupancy and heterogeneous-detection mixture frameworks, exponential-polynomial zero counts, and Prony reconstruction are established. The closest accessible full text, MacKenzie and Royle (2005), studies effort allocation and estimator variance primarily for homogeneous detection, not an if-and-only-if criterion based on the distinct subset sums of **labeled binary exposure durations**. Hwang, Stoklosa and Chen (2022, Section 3) analyzes zero-inflated binomial models and heterogeneous detection likelihoods with count aggregation, not the complete sharp subset-sum schedule classification. Royle (2006) treats heterogeneous detection and nonidentifiability; accessible abstracts do not establish the sharp design theorem, but its complete text could not be inspected and remains a material residual priority risk. Relevant broad finite-mixture/latent-class identification results may contain a dominating specialization and require independent bibliographic review. A failed search is not a novelty proof.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This is an exact field-design boundary, not a numerical gain: a fixed total survey duration can be divided into \(\lceil\log_2(2k+1)\rceil\) unequal visits rather than \(2k\) equal visits without sacrificing population-level structural identification of site occupancy and a finite detection-heterogeneity distribution. The complete if-and-only-if criterion identifies which arbitrary effort schedules do or do not suffice; for two classes even a small departure from three equal visits changes global identification, though potentially with poor conditioning. Per-visit logistical overhead makes this a naturally motivated discrete design parameter. No claim is made about optimized finite-sample precision or total field cost.

## Limits and closest prior work
MacKenzie et al. (2002), MacKenzie and Royle (2005), Royle (2006), and Hwang et al. (2022) provide the main ecological reference frameworks. The mathematical ingredients from exponential-sum uniqueness are classical and no authorship or novelty is attributed to their discovery here. The missing full Royle (2006) text and the possibility of a previously published more general binary-exposure design theorem are priority risks. Unknown nonparametric mixing, false positives, changing occupancy, correlated visits, and finite-sample robustness are outside the theorem.
