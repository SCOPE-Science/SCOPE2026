# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS

For arbitrary integers \(p,q\ge2\), positive row prices and positive budget, strict convexity of the full independent-edge objective makes the minimizer unique and forces right-side permutation symmetry. Three elementary unit-current identities yield the reduced resistance formula. The positive-definite reduced Hessian and KKT equations produce a unique scalar shift and establish the exact global optimum. The large-right-class asymptotics follow by controlled expansion at the unique lowest price. Exact symbolic and independent numerical checks corroborate the critical reduction.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical risk

The 2006 conference and longer 2008 SIAM Review versions of Ghosh–Boyd–Saberi were examined at their optimization statements, convexity proof, analytical families, and extensions. These cover the general graph problem but do not state the heterogeneous-cost complete-bipartite scalar law or its singular large-part limit. Search comparisons for vertex-weighted complete multipartite resistance formulas and budget optimization found no implication-equivalent optimum theorem. Chen–Yan 2021 is a plausible related source for resistance formulas, but only its abstract was accessible, so the possibility of material overlap cannot be ruled out. Published search coverage is not exhaustive.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The result replaces an optimization problem with an independently controlled conductance on every biclique edge by one uniquely solvable scalar equation for arbitrarily many different row prices. The additive price shift quantifies a real cycle-induced deviation from the tree square-root rule; the large-right-class theorem proves asymptotic concentration onto the cheapest channel while retaining finite-size strict positivity. This identifies a useful, natural design law and a sharp structural limiting phenomenon rather than a numerical instance or arbitrary parameter slice.

## Scope and residual risks

The price pattern is constant across the right neighbors of each left vertex and does not cover fully arbitrary edge prices. The asymptotic concentration statement requires one strictly smallest price. General published convex optimization frameworks can numerically solve the problem; specialized exact classifications may exist beyond the inspected material. The available abstract for Chen–Yan concerns multiplicatively vertex-weighted *resistances*, not this allocation problem, but full-text comparison remains incomplete.
