# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem has a canonical alternating-block model, an exact characterization of flippable edges, and a tree characterization of the sharp lower degree. Even and odd block sequences are counted without overlap, including the star boundary. The separate binary-column argument proves the \(K_{2,n}\) polynomial and its asymptotic proportion. Both stand-alone enumerators reconstruct the finite profiles, but the infinite-family conclusions follow from the combinatorial proof rather than finite enumeration. The structural proof and exact routines were checked in this review.

## Originality — PASS, with residual risk
The alternating-block correspondence and characterization of flippable edges already appear in Cameron–Glass–Rekvényi–Schumacher (2014); they are explicitly excluded as novel. The general identification with zonotope edges appears in Cardinal–Hoang–Merino–Mička–Mütze (2022). Carballosa–Khera–Reyes (2023) enumerates orientation encodings but not flip-degree polynomials. Compared at the level of implications, the older fixed-edge marginal and ordinary orientation count do not determine the joint degree distribution or the nontrivial closed enumeration of orientations having a tree transitive reduction. The new claim is restricted to these exact refinements. An equivalent specialized simple-chamber enumeration in other literature cannot be excluded.

## Value — PASS
Simple vertices are an intrinsic measure of local nonsimplicity of a graphical zonotope. Counting them in every complete-bipartite graphical zonotope and identifying their exact alternating-layer geometry answers a natural structural enumeration problem, not an arbitrarily selected statistic. The explicit \(K_{2,n}\) polynomial supplies the full local branching law and exhibits exponential scarcity of simple vertices against a known poly-Bernoulli total. The balanced-family decay gives another intrinsic consequence, without claiming a general polytopal classification.

## Scope and limitations
Covers all positive labelled part sizes for the stated graph and single-edge-flip rule. It neither reproves the historical orientation classification as a new theorem nor claims novelty for the known total number of acyclic orientations or for counts of maximal-degree orientations. There is no independent scholarly audit, and priority over unindexed specializations is not established.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
