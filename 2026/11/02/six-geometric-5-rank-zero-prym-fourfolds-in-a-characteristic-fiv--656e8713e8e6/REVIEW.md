# Scientific review: six rank-zero Prym fourfolds in characteristic five

## Correctness — PASS
The tame Kummer branch count gives genera 7 and 3; the involution's connected double cover has four branch points, so its Prym has dimension 4. The displayed holomorphic basis is complete by local valuations. Fifth-root semilinear Cartier expansion produces the seven-by-seven matrix, in which the invariant subspace has stable rank 2 and the anti-invariant subspace has stable rank 0 exactly at \(1+3a^6=0\), otherwise 2. A prime-to-five Prym/quotient isogeny transfers the computed invariants; exactly six simple exceptional roots avoid the singular parameters. The standalone exact \(\mathbb F_{25}\) test reconstructs the full matrix and checks all 23 smooth parameters, without replacing the infinite-field proof.

## Originality — PASS, with residual priority risk
Obus–Shaska (arXiv:2006.12685, 2020) account for the symmetric central member and its automorphisms, but do not determine this marked characteristic-five pencil's Cartier/Prym jumps. Celik et al. (arXiv:1708.03652, 2017) establish the significance of low-p-rank Pryms primarily for unramified genus-three double covers, unlike this four-branched dimension-four Prym. Clingher–Malmendier–Shaska (arXiv:2005.04839, 2020) study different bielliptic Pryms. Targeted formula, symmetry, stable-rank, and cover-description searches did not reveal equivalent coverage. This is evidence, not proof of historical priority; an unindexed equivalent calculation is possible.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The coefficient-marked pencil deforms an automorphism-rich superelliptic curve and has a canonical genus-three quotient and Prym fourfold. The result classifies exactly where the fourfold's geometric \(5\)-rank drops to zero, while its \(a\)-number remains two; this distinction is invisible from the first Cartier rank. The exceptional six-point locus and quotient/anti-invariant interpretation are natural complete invariants of this family, not an arbitrary numerical slice.

## Scope and limitations
The theorem concerns smooth marked coefficients over algebraically closed characteristic-five fields; it neither counts abstract isomorphism classes nor determines complete Newton polygons, Ekedahl–Oort types or Hasse divisor multiplicities. Mathematical priority remains subject to subsequent independent examination.
