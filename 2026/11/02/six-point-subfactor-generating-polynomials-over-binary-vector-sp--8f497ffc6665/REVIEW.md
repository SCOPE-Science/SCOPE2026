# Scientific review — six-point subfactor generating polynomials

## Correctness: PASS

The proof classifies all affine ranks possible for six points, reduces arbitrary ambient dimension to independent difference-span cosets, and determines the local generating polynomial in every affine shape. The two rank-four forms follow respectively from a two-component cube graph and exhaustive coverage of nonzero differences. The rank-five argument proves that maximal distance-three binary five-codes have sizes two and four, counts each class without quotienting by symmetries, and rules out maximal triples. The labeled five-cube census has both a group-action count and an independent full finite scan. All canonical local counts were independently reproduced by the attached checker. The exact scope is six distinct support points over a characteristic-two vector space.

## Originality: PASS, with historical-priority limitations

The earliest recent motivating paper is Kabenyuk, arXiv:2602.06770 (February 6, 2026), whose fully inspected Sections 1, 2 and 5 contain the Cayley graph formulation and the known six-point simplex instability witness with lower/upper indices two/four. The earlier complete preprint of Hooshmand and Yousefian Arani, arXiv:2310.03438 (October 5, 2023), already exhibits the same witness; neither that example nor its extremal sizes are claimed as original. The new statement classifies **every** six-point support across **all** binary dimensions, gives exact counts of **all** maximal packings by size, and provides a complete labeled census in the five-cube. Generic coset reductions and stability classification do not imply the stated coefficients by themselves. Focused mathematical-index searches and comparisons with available prior findings revealed no equivalent complete polynomial classification. Historical exhaustive coding-table results may imply the 120-count alone and remain a residual priority risk; no absolute uniqueness claim is made.

## Mathematical value: PASS

The result supplies the full local distribution behind a specifically highlighted finite-group instability witness and identifies the exact geometric condition for stability for every six-point support, rather than considering a selected example. Its formulas distinguish an affine four-point circuit, a six-point circuit, and an independent simplex by their subfactor multiplicities, and extend to arbitrary ambient dimension. These exact finite-group invariants provide reproducible benchmarks for Cayley-graph maximal-independent-set and packing problems. The earlier lower/upper index result is not presented as the contribution; the new multiplicities and all-support classification are the substantive finding.

## Closest coverage and limitations

Kabenyuk, arXiv:2602.06770, Lemma 11, proves the known extrema for the simplex support. Hooshmand–Yousefian Arani, arXiv:2310.03438, proof of Theorem 3.1, Step 1, already gives two witness packings. Hooshmand, arXiv:2607.08805, supplies the continuing fixed-support index-stability motivation but studies cyclic groups primarily. No claim is made about arbitrary finite groups, nonbinary fields, supports of another cardinality, or universal historical priority. Independent mathematical review has not been performed.
