# Scientific review: the characteristic-five Bring power-sum fiber

## Correctness — PASS

The three power sums yield the characteristic-five Bring–Jerrard polynomial \(U^5+e_4U-e_5\). Nonzero derivative gives five roots forming an affine copy of \(\mathbb F_5\), producing exactly six projective lines, while zero derivative gives the common point. A Vandermonde Jacobian minor proves reducedness away from that point; the complete-intersection Cohen–Macaulay property proves reducedness everywhere. The conic–cubic tangent cone has six distinct rational points and Hilbert function \((1,3,5,6,\ldots)\); comparison with the six-branch normalization gives delta \(9\) and seminormal defect \(4\). The normalization units sequence, quotient by the five scalar parameters, and a characteristic-five polynomial logarithm truncated at degree three establish \(\operatorname{Pic}^0\cong\mathbb G_a^4\) as a group scheme. Exact finite-field code confirms the six transverse directions, orbit index, evaluation ranks, and point counts. The relative complete-intersection flatness and arithmetic genus are proved algebraically.

## Originality — PASS, with priority reservations

The complex Bring model is explicitly documented by Braden–Northover. Korchmáros–Lia–Timpanella consider symmetric power-sum curves under \(p\ge7\) and do not treat this \(p=5,m=5\) fiber. Braden–Disney-Hogg discuss complex geometry and theta characteristics, not the nonsemistable characteristic-five fiber. Conca–Krattenthaler–Watanabe investigate regular sequences of power sums over \(\mathbb C\), and the corrected general Picard theory of Geisser does not evaluate this specific six-branch local algebra or prove its \(\mathbb G_a^4\) structure. Searches of the available mathematical-record index and accessible earlier research claims found no result that entails the full special-fiber and connected-Picard classification. The key surviving assertion is the evaluated group-scheme and local geometric decomposition, not the elementary Newton identities in isolation. It is possible that classical Bring–Jerrard or positive-characteristic degeneration literature implicitly covers these calculations; priority is provisional.

## Mathematical value — PASS

The characteristic-five fiber is a natural exceptional reduction of the canonical highly symmetric genus-four Bring curve, rather than an arbitrary sliced example. The exact six-line splitting, six-branch delta \(9\) singularity, and additive dimension-four Picard group illuminate how a smooth genus-four complete intersection can degenerate into a reduced, nonseminormal curve with no abelian or toric connected Picard part. This is stronger than point counting and relevant to singular models, generalized Jacobians, and arithmetic degeneration. The exceptional six-point permutation representation connects the branch geometry to the wild \(S_5\)-symmetry. The result does not claim stable reduction or a Néron model.
