# Six roommates first separate blocking-neutrality from Pareto efficiency in an unsolvable perfect market

## Finding

**Theorem (sharp first separation).** Consider a roommate market on an even number of agents. Every agent has a strict complete ranking of the other agents, prefers every possible roommate to being single, and only *perfect pairings* are feasible. Let \(\mathrm{BN}(P)\) denote the feasible blocking-neutral matchings in the sense of Takahashi (2026), and let \(\mathrm{PO}(P)\) denote the Pareto-optimal feasible pairings. Among markets with **no stable feasible matching**, six is the least possible number of agents for which \(\mathrm{BN}(P)\subsetneq\mathrm{PO}(P)\).

A witnessing six-person market has the following decreasing rankings (agents are numbered \(0,1,2,3,4,5\)):

| Agent | Full ranking, best first |
|:--|:--|
| \(0\) | \(2\succ1\succ3\succ4\succ5\) |
| \(1\) | \(2\succ0\succ3\succ4\succ5\) |
| \(2\) | \(0\succ1\succ3\succ4\succ5\) |
| \(3\) | \(4\succ5\succ0\succ1\succ2\) |
| \(4\) | \(5\succ3\succ0\succ1\succ2\) |
| \(5\) | \(3\succ4\succ0\succ1\succ2\) |

For this profile there are no stable perfect pairings, exactly \(9\) Pareto-optimal perfect pairings, and exactly \(6\) blocking-neutral perfect pairings. The three Pareto-optimal pairings excluded by blocking-neutrality are
\[
\{01,23,45\},\qquad\{01,24,35\},\qquad\{01,25,34\},
\]
where \(ij\) denotes the pair \(\{i,j\}\). Every other one-cross-pair matching is blocking-neutral. The six matchings with three cross pairs are neither Pareto optimal nor blocking-neutral.

This separation is generated by **one adjacent swap**: replacing the first ranking by \(1\succ2\succ3\succ4\succ5\), while leaving all others fixed, yields a still-unsolvable market with exactly \(9\) Pareto-optimal and exactly \(9\) blocking-neutral matchings. Restoring \(2\succ1\) reduces the blocking-neutral set from nine to six without changing the Pareto-optimal set.

## Assumptions and scope

Let \(I\) be a finite even-cardinality set. A perfect matching \(\mu\) maps each agent to another agent and satisfies \(\mu(\mu(i))=i\). The feasible set \(K\) comprises **all** perfect matchings on \(I\). Every agent strictly ranks all other agents above the option of remaining unmatched; because \(K\) consists only of perfect matchings, self-matches do not appear in comparisons.

An unordered pair \(\{i,j\}\) *blocks* \(\mu\) if the agents are not paired in \(\mu\) and each prefers the other to their current partner. Let \(B(\mu)\) be the set of blocking pairs and \(A(\mu)\) their endpoints. A matching \(\nu\) **blocking-neutrally dominates** \(\mu\) if some member of \(A(\mu)\) strictly prefers \(\nu\) to \(\mu\), while no member of \(A(\nu)\) strictly prefers \(\mu\) to \(\nu\). A matching is blocking-neutral if no other feasible matching dominates it. A matching is Pareto optimal if no other feasible matching weakly improves every agent and strictly improves at least one. A matching is stable if \(B(\mu)=\varnothing\). These definitions agree with Takahashi's Definition 1 after restricting feasibility to perfect pairings.

The **minimality** compares only even-cardinality markets with perfect-pairing feasibility and complete, strict, acceptable roommate lists. It does not assert a minimum for arbitrary constraints, markets permitting singles, or weak preferences. The two six-agent exact counts are for the displayed profiles, not arbitrary six-person preferences.

## Proof

**A published blocking-set monotonicity fact.** Takahashi (2026, Lemma 1) proves that if \(\nu\) blocking-neutrally dominates \(\mu\), then \(B(\nu)\subseteq B(\mu)\). We apply this to rule out domination when the blocking-pair sets are distinct and have equal cardinality. Takahashi's Theorem 2 also proves \(\mathrm{BN}(P)\subseteq\mathrm{PO}(P)\). These are attributed prior facts, not new lemmas.

**The four-person lower bound.** There is a single perfect pairing with two agents, and it is stable. For four agents, the established complete classification of stable-roommates profiles shows that an unsolvable profile consists, after relabeling, of three agents in a directed preference cycle, each ranking the fourth agent last. The outsider's ranking of the three is arbitrary; the resulting \(48\) labeled profiles are exactly the four-person unsolvable stratum. In each of the three perfect pairings, precisely one cycle member is paired with the outsider and the other two are paired together. The **unique** blocking pair is a cycle edge involving the outsider's roommate, and these three blocking pairs are distinct as the pairing changes. There is no blocking pair involving the outsider because every cycle member ranks the outsider last. Thus each of the three matchings has a distinct singleton \(B(\mu)\). By blocking-set monotonicity none can blocking-neutrally dominate another, and hence all three are blocking-neutral. Takahashi's inclusion then makes all three Pareto optimal. The standalone verifier additionally checks all \((3!)^4=1296\) four-agent profiles, not just a sample, confirming the \(48\) no-stable cases and their three distinct singleton blocking sets.

**Six-person market geometry.** Partition the six agents into two triples \(U=\{0,1,2\}\) and \(V=\{3,4,5\}\). Every agent ranks both members of their own triple above all outsiders. A perfect pairing must have either one or three cross pairs (one endpoint in each triple). The \(V\) preferences form a directed top-choice cycle \(3\to4\to5\to3\). If a matching has one cross pair, its single \(V\)-agent matched outside \(V\) and the other two \(V\)-agents paired internally have one blocking pair inside \(V\). If all three pairs cross the partition, every pair of distinct \(V\)-agents blocks. Thus **no perfect pairing is stable** for either of the two displayed profiles.

**All nine one-cross pairings, and only those, are Pareto optimal.** With exactly one cross pair, two agents in \(U\) and two in \(V\) are paired internally. None can become cross-matched in a Pareto improvement, because each strictly prefers an internal partner to any outsider. Hence all four must keep their existing internal pairs, forcing the same cross pair. This proves Pareto optimality of every one-cross pairing. Conversely, for each of the six all-cross pairings, retain one existing cross pair and pair the other two agents inside \(U\), and the other two inside \(V\). Four agents improve strictly and the remaining two are unchanged, so all-cross pairings are Pareto dominated. Therefore \(\lvert\mathrm{PO}(P)\rvert=9\) in both profiles.

**Baseline: all nine one-cross pairings are blocking-neutral.** Before the swap, both \(U\) and \(V\) are directed top-choice triples: \(0\to1\to2\to0\) and \(3\to4\to5\to3\). For a one-cross pairing, exactly one pair inside each triple blocks. Which pair blocks uniquely identifies the cross-matched person within that triple; the nine possible one-cross pairings therefore have nine *distinct two-element* blocking-pair sets. Takahashi's monotonicity lemma rules out domination between any two of them. The six all-cross matchings cannot be blocking-neutral because they are Pareto dominated. Thus the baseline has exactly nine blocking-neutral pairings.

**One adjacent reversal: precisely six remain blocking-neutral.** After the swap, agents \(0\) and \(2\) rank one another first, agent \(1\) ranks \(2\) first, and the \(V\) cycle is unchanged. Group the nine Pareto-optimal one-cross pairings by which internal edge of \(U\) they contain:

* If the edge is \(02\), there is no \(U\)-blocking pair. The only blocker is the one determined by the cross-matched \(V\) agent. The three such matchings have distinct singleton blocker sets, and no other feasible matching has a blocker set contained in them. Each is blocking-neutral.
* If the edge is \(12\), the unique \(U\)-blocking pair is \(02\). There is also the one \(V\)-blocking pair. Any different feasible matching with blockers contained in this two-element set must retain that same \(V\) blocker and use the \(02\) internal edge instead. However, the cross-matched \(V\) agent would then lose partner \(0\) for partner \(1\), and \(0\succ1\succ2\) is its ranking of outsiders. This agent blocks the proposed replacement matching and strictly prefers the original; therefore the proposed replacement cannot dominate. All three \(12\)-edge matchings are blocking-neutral.
* If the edge is \(01\), there are two \(U\)-blocking pairs, \(02\) and \(12\), and one \(V\)-blocking pair. Replace internal edge \(01\) by \(02\), retaining the same internal \(V\)-edge, and change the cross pair from \(2v\) to \(1v\). Agents \(0\), \(2\), and \(v\) strictly improve; only agent \(1\) worsens. In the new pairing agent \(1\) does **not** block, since \(0\) and \(2\) are mutually first-choice partners. Thus the replacement blocking-neutrally dominates the original. Each of the three \(01\)-edge pairings is Pareto optimal but not blocking-neutral.

Consequently, the final profile has exactly six blocking-neutral and nine Pareto-optimal pairings, proving the strict separation at six. The four-person lower bound makes six the sharp first even market size.

## Verification

The standalone `verify_bn.py` is a complete standard-library Python checker with no hidden data. It enumerates all four-agent strict profiles by six permutations per agent and constructs all three perfect pairings, all blocking sets, all pairwise blocking-neutral dominance tests, and all Pareto dominance tests. It asserts the exact \(48\) unsolvable-profile count, singleton distinctness of the three blocking-pair sets, and complete blocking-neutrality and Pareto optimality of each unsolvable profile.

For each of the two specified six-agent profiles, it generates all \((6-1)!!=15\) feasible perfect pairings, directly tests every potential blocking pair, computes both dominance relations for every ordered pair of perfect matchings, and asserts the claimed before/after sets. Execute from this package directory with `python3 verify_bn.py`. The final output is `ALL ASSERTIONS PASSED`. An exact printout of the six-agent matchings and their blocker sets is emitted, allowing independent comparison without relying only on result totals. All calculations involve finite integer comparisons, with no sampling or floating-point approximation.

## Relationship to prior work

Takahashi (2026, arXiv:2608.11682v1) *introduced* blocking-neutral matching and established existence under arbitrary feasibility constraints, blocking-set monotonicity, inclusion in Pareto-optimal matchings, and coincidence with stability whenever stable matchings exist. The manuscript's Examples 1–3 and Sections 2–4 were inspected; they do not state a minimum even population for strict inclusion within **unsolvable perfect-pairing** markets, the displayed six-person configuration, or the exact nine-to-six one-swap transition. Its three-agent Pareto separation concerns a **solvable** market and allows singles; its eight-agent example concerns blocking-set monotonicity and almost-stable matchings. Those are different conclusions.

The published *Stability-stratified isomorphism census for four-person stable roommates* records the \(48\) unsolvable labeled four-person profiles and their two orientation classes. That known stability classification supplies the first layer of the lower bound; it does not analyze blocking-neutrality. The present finding does **not** claim this prior census as new.

The experimental roommate-markets literature also studies absorbing sets, almost-stability and decentralized dynamics, but those solution concepts are not the blocking-neutral dominance relation. The new conclusion is the sharp first separation of blocking-neutrality from Pareto optimality **conditional on absence of a stable perfect pairing**, and a natural local perturbation producing that separation.

## Limitations

The theorem is specific to perfect-pairing feasibility and complete strict individually acceptable preferences. It is an exact boundary theorem and a single six-agent parametric structure, not a classification of all six-agent unsolvable profiles or a population-wide formula for blocking-neutral set size. For arbitrary feasibility constraints or allowable singles, smaller separations may exist; the claim intentionally makes no statement about them. The original paper's general results are credited. No independent scientific review has been performed, and unindexed prior computations or equivalent formulations may still exist.
