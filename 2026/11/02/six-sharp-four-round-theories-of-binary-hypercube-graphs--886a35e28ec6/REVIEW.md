# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The proof reduces four-round Ehrenfeucht--Fraïssé equivalence to exact nested extension-type sets, accounting for equality, adjacency, repeated moves, and all quantifier patterns. A four-coordinate cap is proved using preservation of zero, one, or at least two mismatches; a pair cap of eight yields an infinite stabilization bound at dimension sixteen. Exhaustive enumeration establishes the sharp lower cutoff at dimension six and five smaller singleton theories. Direct graph enumeration through dimension five cross-checks the coordinate-orbit algorithm. The finite output does not replace the unbounded saturation proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

A full-text reading of Pikhurko--Verbitsky's graph-logic survey supports the general game framework but does not supply the six-class cube result. Goldwasser--Hansen's accessible full article studies exact subcube configurations and inducibility, not first-order rank-four equivalence. An existing rank-three classification of binary Hamming powers is strictly shallower and does not imply the computed four-round separation. Searches for rank-four cube theories, equivalent dimension cutoffs, and stronger all-rank coverage yielded no decisive covering statement. Remaining risk: an equivalent statement could occur in unindexed finite-model-theory literature or follow from a more general unpublished product theorem.

## Value — PASS

The theorem exactly identifies what a fixed four-round observer can and cannot detect in a canonical dimension-growing graph family. The sharp stabilization at dimension six is not the trivial four-coordinate cutoff and differs materially from the rank-three threshold; the proof explains finiteness via two structural saturation lemmas rather than tabulation alone. The resulting optimal 64-vertex representative provides a benchmark for bounded-depth graph logic.
