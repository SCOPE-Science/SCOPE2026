# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

The theorem is about all integer branching numbers \(b\ge2\) and all heights \(h\ge0\) in the unrooted, undirected adjacency signature. The proof reduces the three-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game to the attainable atomic patterns of a third vertex after an ordered pair is selected. In height at least three the graph has more vertices than the two closed one-step neighborhoods can cover, so every pair has an outside nonneighbor; its remaining extension bits are fixed by tree distance one, two or at least three and leaf status. The four attainable pointed rank-two types are the same for every branching number and height in that range. Heights zero, one and two are handled by explicit finite level analyses; five rank-at-most-three separating sentences distinguish the six proposed classes. Direct complete-type recursions and an independent local pair-pattern checker corroborate the proof on finite representatives. The argument for arbitrary heights and branching numbers is mathematical, not experimental.

## Originality — PASS with residual priority risk

The closest relevant general first-order tree-definability study, Bohman et al. (arXiv:math/0506288, first public 2005-06-15), develops game methods and asymptotic or extremal definability depths, not the exact six-class partition in this signature. Benedikt and Segoufin (doi:10.1145/1614431.1614435) characterize definability of regular labeled rooted-tree languages, not sharp rank-three equivalence of uncolored undirected complete branching trees. A previous classification of the same family in a strict ancestry signature has different atomic relations and does not imply the rank-three adjacency claim at unchanged depth. Searches of indexed mathematical work and primary literature for rank-three full trees and signature variants returned no directly covering result. The inspected material and search coverage do not exclude an obscure older equivalent statement.

## Mathematical value — PASS

This is a sharp complete classification of a canonical two-parameter family used to test finite-model-theoretic locality and Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé games. The collapse of both branching and height at height three is not automatic from a generic locality theorem, and the 15-vertex representative bound is exact. Compared with richer rooted or ancestry languages, the result isolates the information lost by forgetting the root, edge direction and ancestor relation; an explicit rank-four neighborhood-twin sentence proves that the branching collapse is depth-sensitive. The classification is a natural exact bounded-depth invariant rather than an arbitrary parameter slice. Its scientific scope is deliberately narrow: no claim about general trees or higher-rank classifications.

## Limitations and sources

The main source texts inspected were Bohman et al., *First Order Definability of Trees and Sparse Random Graphs* (arXiv:math/0506288, relevant introductory and general-tree material), and Benedikt–Segoufin, *Regular Tree Languages Definable in FO and in FOmod* (author-hosted journal full text, sections on languages and rooted successor relations). Exhaustive historical priority and independent audit are outstanding. Finite scripts are corroboration only.
