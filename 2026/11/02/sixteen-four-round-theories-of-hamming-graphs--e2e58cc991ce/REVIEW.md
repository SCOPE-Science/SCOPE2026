# Scientific review: sixteen four-round Hamming-graph theories

**Correctness (PASS).** The complete orbit of an ordered vertex tuple is its coordinate equality-partition histogram. Every possible next vertex corresponds to an exhaustive independent refinement of those patterns. Rank-four equivalence is computed by backward induction on atomic diagrams and sets of extension types. The critical unbounded-dimension step is a finite, exhaustive twenty-seven-state transition identity for each of the five triple patterns; the resulting pair-profile cap sums are fourteen in each alphabet regime. Exact enumerations through that bound distinguish all sixteen alleged types. A separate direct vertex-enumeration program checks pair types on small Hamming graphs without relying on the orbit-extension generator. The result remains confined to pure adjacency and quantifier rank four.

**Originality (PASS with priority qualification).** Pikhurko–Verbitsky supply the general game framework but not this classification; Klavžar–Peterin classify subgraph embeddings, not bounded-depth elementary equivalence. Published-index searches for the specific Hamming-graph logical classification found no covering work; semantic-index searches found no equivalent statement. The binary rank-four component was already recorded elsewhere, as was the rank-three classification of more general Cartesian complete-graph products; neither implies the additional nonbinary rank-four dimension-five threshold or the sixteen-class comparison. Unindexed and implicit older results remain a historical-priority risk.

**Value (PASS).** Hamming graphs are a standard, natural parameterized family of Cartesian products. The theorem separates the two information resources available to four quantifiers—alphabet size and number of factors—by a sharp phase diagram, with distinct binary and nonbinary stabilization thresholds and a sharp uniform within-family representative bound. This is a complete classification of a motivated infinite family, not a computation of an arbitrary small graph invariant.
