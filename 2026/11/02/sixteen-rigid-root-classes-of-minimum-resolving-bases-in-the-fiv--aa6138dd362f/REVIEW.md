# Review of the five-cube metric-basis exchange classification

## Correctness
PASS. The ternary-kernel criterion follows from exact Hamming-distance subtraction. The sign-column pigeonhole bound excludes three landmarks; signed maximal minors exclude any unresolved four-row matrix lacking a Hadamard minor. Orthogonality forces the unique primitive null vector to have coefficient magnitudes \(2,1,1,1,1\). Its sign patterns give sixteen root pairs, five holes and sixteen transversals for each hole. Two different roots have at most two common selected landmarks, so no single exchange crosses roots; the explicit same-root exchanges and overlap-distance proof give all 12-regular components and the shell counts. Two different executable implementations replay the finite cases. No finite experiment is used to imply a general-dimensional theorem.

## Originality
PASS under the recorded accessible-source comparison, with residual bibliographic risk. The classical ternary-kernel criterion (Beardon; Laird–Tillquist–Becker–Lladser) and known antipodal basis replacement (Kelenc et al.) are explicitly prior work, not discoveries here. An earlier signed Johnson graph metric concerns **only** support replacements (degree eight at five coordinates and support four), whereas the present one-exchange graph also permits sign flips (degree twelve), and neither that graph nor the other inspected publications classifies the sixteen metric-basis connected components. Equivalent formulations, stronger coverage, exact tables and implications were separately compared. Full Beardon text and a recent reconfiguration thesis were unavailable; no universal claim of historical uniqueness is made.

## Value
PASS. Minimum metric bases represent optimal landmark configurations for unique localization in a natural hypercube. Here the entire space of optimal configurations has sixteen intrinsic barriers under single-landmark replacement, despite connected movement within each of the sixteen components. The exact metric, degree and shell structure quantify this obstruction; those data are not consequences of merely knowing the metric dimension. The root-class decomposition is a structural description, not an arbitrary isolated numerical census.

## Limitations
The result is for the labeled five-cube, ordinary vertex metric bases, and arbitrary single-vertex exchanges. It does not assert results in other cube dimensions or other resolution/reconfiguration variants. Independent scholarly verification has not occurred.
