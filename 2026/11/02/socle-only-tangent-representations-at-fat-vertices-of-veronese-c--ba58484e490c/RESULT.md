# Socle-only tangent representations at fat vertices of Veronese cones

## Finding

Let \(d,r\ge 1\). At the length-\(N\) point \([\mathfrak m^r]\) of the Hilbert scheme of the cone over the degree-\(d\) rational normal curve, the entire Zariski tangent representation is concentrated in a single negative grading. There is a canonical vector-space identification
\[
T_{[\mathfrak m^r]}\operatorname{Hilb}^{N}(\operatorname{Spec}R_d)
\simeq \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb C}(\mathfrak m^r/\mathfrak m^{r+1},\mathfrak m^{r-1}/\mathfrak m^r),
\]
so every tangent vector sends degree-\(r\) generators to degree-\((r-1)\) socle elements and kills all lower image degrees. The colength and tangent dimension are exactly
\[
N=r+\frac{dr(r-1)}2,\qquad
\dim_{\mathbb C}T=(dr+1)(d(r-1)+1).
\]
Under the torus action scaling \(u\) and \(v\), the complete tangent character is
\[
\operatorname{ch}_{U,V}T=\sum_{i=0}^{dr}\sum_{j=0}^{d(r-1)}
U^{i-j-d}V^{j-i}.
\]
For the quadratic cone \(d=2\), an ordinary \(A_1\) rational double point, \(N=r^2\) and \(\dim T=4r^2-1\). Thus \([\mathfrak m^r]\) is singular in the full Hilbert scheme for every \(r\ge1\), with tangent excess \(2r^2-1\) above its local dimension \(2r^2\).

## Assumptions and scope

Work over \(\mathbb C\). Define the standard-graded, two-dimensional domain
\[
R_d=\mathbb C[u^d,u^{d-1}v,\ldots,uv^{d-1},v^d]
=\bigoplus_{q\ge0}\mathbb C[u,v]_{dq},
\]
where each homogeneous polynomial of ordinary degree \(d\) is assigned standard degree one. Its homogeneous maximal ideal is \(\mathfrak m=(R_d)_{>0}\). Set \(I=\mathfrak m^r\) and \(Q=R_d/I\). These are finite-colength ideals of the affine surface \(\operatorname{Spec}R_d\), not ideals in a chosen smooth ambient affine space. The displayed tangent is the tangent to the **full Hilbert scheme**, not the punctual fiber of the Hilbert–Chow map. The torus \((\mathbb C^*)^2\) acts by rescaling \(u,v\); because a finite diagonal subgroup acts trivially on the cone, the character is understood as a character of its effective quotient torus.

## Proof

**Step 1: Colength and generators.** The vector space \((R_d)_q\) consists of all binary monomials of total ordinary degree \(dq\), hence \(\dim (R_d)_q=dq+1\). Since \(I=\bigoplus_{q\ge r}(R_d)_q\), summing \(dq+1\) for \(0\le q<r\) gives the formula for \(N\). The ideal \(I\) is generated by the degree-\(r\) monomials
\[
g_i=u^{dr-i}v^i\qquad(0\le i\le dr),
\]
which are a basis of \(I/\mathfrak m I\). The socle of \(Q\), annihilated by \(\mathfrak m\), is exactly \(Q_{r-1}=(R_d)_{r-1}\).

**Step 2: Vanishing below the socle.** The standard Hilbert-tangent identification is \(T_{[I]}\operatorname{Hilb}^{N}(\operatorname{Spec}R_d)=\operatorname{Hom}_{R_d}(I,Q)\). Decompose an arbitrary homomorphism by its homogeneous image degree on the generators. Fix \(t\le r-2\) and let \(h_i\in Q_t=(R_d)_t\) be the image of \(g_i\) in that component. The generating syzygies include
\[
u^d g_{i+1}=u^{d-1}v g_i\qquad(0\le i<dr).
\]
Applying the homomorphism gives \(u^d h_{i+1}=u^{d-1}v h_i\) in \(Q_{t+1}\). Since \(t+1<r\), this is an equality of actual binary forms in the integral domain \(\mathbb C[u,v]\). Cancel \(u^{d-1}\) to get \(u h_{i+1}=v h_i\). Iterating gives \(u^{dr}h_{dr}=v^{dr}h_0\). Coprimality of \(u\) and \(v\) forces \(u^{dr}\mid h_0\); but \(h_0\) has ordinary degree \(dt<dr\). Thus \(h_0=0\), and the same recurrence forces every \(h_i=0\). Consequently every \(R_d\)-linear map \(I\to Q\) has image in \(Q_{r-1}\).

**Step 3: All socle maps exist.** Conversely, any \(\mathbb C\)-linear map \(I/\mathfrak m I\to Q_{r-1}\) induces a well-defined \(R_d\)-linear map \(I\to Q\): all positive-degree coefficients annihilate \(Q_{r-1}\), so the relations among the minimal homogeneous generators impose no constraints. This proves the isomorphism in the finding and the dimension \((dr+1)(d(r-1)+1)\). In standard grading, its homogeneous shift is exactly \(-1\).

**Step 4: Full torus character.** The basis map sending \(g_i\) to \(u^{d(r-1)-j}v^j\) has weight \(U^{i-j-d}V^{j-i}\). All such maps form a basis, giving the claimed double sum without cancellation. In particular the weight with exponent \(s=j-i\) on \(V\) has multiplicity
\[
\begin{cases}
dr+s+1,&-dr\le s<-d,\\
d(r-1)+1,&-d\le s\le0,\\
d(r-1)-s+1,&1\le s\le d(r-1),\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The corresponding \(U\)-exponent is \(-d-s\).

**Step 5: The canonical surface case.** For \(d=2\), write \(a=u^2,b=uv,c=v^2\), so \(R_2\cong\mathbb C[a,b,c]/(ac-b^2)\), a type-\(A_1\) surface singularity. The formulas reduce to \(N=r^2\) and \(\dim T=(2r+1)(2r-1)=4r^2-1\). The known theorem that the underlying reduced Hilbert scheme of \(N\) points on a Kleinian surface singularity is a normal variety of dimension \(2N\) implies that the full Hilbert scheme has local dimension \(2N\) at these points. Since \(4r^2-1>2r^2\) for all \(r\ge1\), the tangent dimensions force singularity. No assertion of reducedness at unrestricted \(N\) is needed.

## Verification

The standalone `verify.py` constructs binomial syzygy constraints separately from the proof among the degree-\(r\) monomial generators, including arbitrary monomial multipliers in each lower target degree, and computes exact kernel dimensions using connected-component linear algebra. It checks all \(30\) pairs \(1\le d\le6\), \(1\le r\le5\), finding no lower-degree maps and the expected socle dimensions. Run `python verify.py`; the final line is `VERIFY_OK: 30 Veronese-cone pairs, monomial syzygy constraints checked`. Finite computations are corroboration only; Step 2 proves the arbitrary-parameter assertion.

## Relationship to prior work

Craw and Yamagishi establish normality and dimension \(2N\) for the underlying reduced Hilbert schemes of canonical surfaces; their announced theorem motivates a precise local tangent calculation at natural fat points. Earlier Craw–Gammelgaard–Gyenge–Szendrői work establishes the quiver-variety realization and normality for Kleinian cases. The exact two-parameter socle-only tangent representation here is not claimed as a consequence of their dimension statement alone. Gyenge–Némethi–Szendrői study Euler-characteristic invariants, not this explicit local tangent representation. Hu studies local tangent and singular structures for Hilbert schemes of points in smooth three-space. Pearce's poster on cyclic quotient surfaces mentions a combinatorial mechanism for tangent weights at torus-fixed points; it is a significant potential source of implicit overlap, but its accessible one-page text contains no explicit vertex-power character or dimension formula. These comparisons are about the statements and their implications, not mere title matching.

## Limitations

The formula concerns powers of the vertex maximal ideal on scalar cyclic quotient cones and no other punctual ideals. For \(d>2\), the surfaces need not have canonical singularities, and only the tangent calculation is claimed; the argument does not compute their entire Hilbert-scheme dimension. The result does not determine the completed local Hilbert-scheme equations or obstruction spaces, nor does it imply that tangent vectors integrate. Historical priority is provisional: the complete July 2026 paper could not be inspected and more general pre-existing fixed-point descriptions may implicitly recover the formula. Independent review has not been performed.
