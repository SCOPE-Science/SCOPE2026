# Sorted-rate criterion and sharp first obstruction for clique-heat variation

## Finding

Let \(n\ge2\), and let \(m_1,\ldots,m_n>0\) be arbitrary vertex masses on a unit-conductance complete graph. Set \(r_i=1/m_i\). Under the linear mass-weighted heat equation
\[
 m_i\dot u_i=\sum_{j\ne i}(u_j-u_i),\qquad 1\le i\le n,
\]
consider the *unweighted clique variation*
\[
 J(u)=\sum_{1\le i<j\le n}|u_i-u_j|.
\]
For each \(1\le k<n\), define the integer multiset
\[
 c^{(k)}_i=\begin{cases}
 (n-k)(2i-n-1),&i\le k,\\
 -k(2i-n-1),&i>k,
 \end{cases}\quad 1\le i\le n.
\]
Write \(c^{(k)}_{[1]}\le\cdots\le c^{(k)}_{[n]}\) and \(r_{[1]}\le\cdots\le r_{[n]}\) for increasing rearrangements, and set
\[
 M_k=\sum_{i=1}^n c^{(k)}_{[i]}r_{[i]},\qquad
 \mu=\max_{1\le k<n}\frac{M_k}{k(n-k)}.
\]
Then \(\mu\) is the **sharp infinitesimal exponential variation growth bound**: for every \(u\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(t\ge0\),
\[
 J(u(t))\le e^{\mu t}J(u(0)),\qquad
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{\|H_t\|_{J}-1}{t}=\mu,
\]
where \(H_t\) is the heat semigroup and \(\|H_t\|_J=\sup_{J(u)>0}J(H_tu)/J(u)\). Hence **variation never increases for any initial signal and any time if and only if every \(M_k\le0\)**. This gives an exact \(O(n^2\log n)\) test.

For \(n\le3\), all positive vertex masses give contraction. On four vertices, with increasing reciprocal masses \(r_{[1]}\le r_{[2]}\le r_{[3]}\le r_{[4]}\), the exact criterion becomes
\[
 r_{[4]}\le 9r_{[1]}+3r_{[2]}+r_{[3]}.
\]
The choice \((r_1,r_2,r_3,r_4)=(1,14,1,1)\) and strictly increasing values \(u=(0,1,101/100,102/100)\) gives the **positive exact initial derivative** \(\frac{d}{dt}J(u(t))|_{t=0}=33/100\). With the last reciprocal rate reduced from \(14\) to \(13\), the semigroup is contractive for every initial signal and every time.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph is the complete, undirected graph on \(n\) labeled vertices, all conductances are exactly one, every vertex mass is strictly positive, the state is real-valued, and the edge-variation weights are all one. The semigroup is generated by the operator \((Lu)_i=r_i(\sum_j u_j-nu_i)\). It preserves constants and the mass-weighted average, but is generally not doubly stochastic relative to counting measure. The displayed norm is the operator norm on the quotient of \(\mathbb R^n\) by constant vectors with norm \(J\).

## Proof

1. **Ordered chambers.** Fix any permutation \(\pi\) and initially suppose \(u_{\pi_1}<\cdots<u_{\pi_n}\). Put \(b_i=2i-n-1\) and \(d_k=u_{\pi_{k+1}}-u_{\pi_k}>0\). Directly summing pair differences gives
\[
 J(u)=\sum_{i=1}^n b_i u_{\pi_i}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}k(n-k)d_k.
\]
For the stated heat generator, differentiation gives
\[
 \frac{dJ}{dt}=\sum_{i=1}^n b_i r_{\pi_i}\left(\sum_j u_j-nu_{\pi_i}\right)
 =\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}d_k\left((n-k)\sum_{i\le k}b_ir_{\pi_i}-k\sum_{i>k}b_ir_{\pi_i}\right).
\]
Call the coefficient of \(d_k\) in parentheses \(C_{\pi,k}\). The coefficients multiplying the permuted rates in \(C_{\pi,k}\) are exactly \(c^{(k)}_i\).

2. **Sharp maximization over orders.** Rearrangement inequality gives
\[
 \max_\pi C_{\pi,k}=\sum_i c^{(k)}_{[i]}r_{[i]}=M_k.
\]
Hence \(C_{\pi,k}\le \mu k(n-k)\) in every strict-order chamber. Multiplying by \(d_k\) and summing yields \(\dot J\le\mu J\). At a tie, approximate the state with strictly ordered vectors, or sort the tied coordinates by their instantaneous velocity: the upper right directional derivative of the convex piecewise-linear \(J\) obeys the same inequality by continuity. Thus Gronwall gives \(J(H_tu)\le e^{\mu t}J(u)\) for every state and all \(t\ge0\).

3. **Attainment of the infinitesimal bound.** Choose \(k\) maximizing \(M_k/[k(n-k)]\), and order the rates along a maximizing permutation from the rearrangement step. Let \(d_k=1\) and all other \(d_\ell=\varepsilon>0\). These gaps define a strictly increasing initial state. As \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), the ratio \(\dot J(0)/J(0)\) tends to \(M_k/[k(n-k)]=\mu\). Consequently the upper semigroup bound is first-order optimal and
\[
 \lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{\|H_t\|_J-1}{t}=\mu.
\]
If some \(M_k>0\), choose \(\varepsilon\) small enough that \(\dot J(0)>0\); strict instantaneous increase follows for all sufficiently small positive times. If all \(M_k\le0\), the bound from step 2 proves all-time contraction.

4. **First mass-imbalance obstruction.** For \(n=2\), the sorted coefficient list is \((-1,-1)\). For \(n=3\), both lists equal \((-4,-2,0)\); each has strictly negative dot product with positive increasing rates. For \(n=4\), the lists for \(k=1,3\) equal \((-9,-3,-1,1)\), while the list for \(k=2\) equals \((-6,-6,-2,-2)\). The middle inequality is automatic, and the two endpoint inequalities are equivalent to the claimed sharp threshold. With rates \((1,14,1,1)\) and values \((0,1,101/100,102/100)\), the three ordered-gap coefficients are respectively \(1,-42,-25\), whence \(\dot J(0)=1-42/100-25/100=33/100>0\). For rates \((1,13,1,1)\) the extremal coefficient becomes zero; the contraction criterion holds.

## Verification

The bundled standalone \(\texttt{verify.py}\) uses only Python's standard library and exact integer/Fraction arithmetic. It compares the sorted-rearrangement bounds to every order of numerous finite reciprocal-rate profiles up to six vertices, tests every cutoff \(k\), and exactly checks the strict four-vertex witness. Run it as \(\texttt{python3 verify.py}\). The test is supporting finite evidence, not a proof of the universal rearrangement or semigroup claims; the preceding argument supplies those proofs.

## Relationship to prior work

Mugnolo's discrete \(p\)-Laplace semigroup treatment (2012) establishes general diffusion, conservation, positivity and vertex-space contraction phenomena, but does not give the unweighted complete-graph pairwise-variation criterion for freely varying positive vertex masses. Van Gennip–Guillen–Osting–Bertozzi (2014) develop graph total variation, diffusion and curvature, and their Section 6.1 treats homogeneous complete-graph heat. Its homogeneous-mass formula does not imply the sorted heterogeneous-mass threshold or the first four-vertex obstruction. The present characterization concerns a different operator norm from ordinary vertex-space \(L^p\) contractivity; the latter does not settle the former.

## Limitations

This is a statement about **one-input variation decay** under linear heat flow. It does not classify nonuniform edge conductances, additional variation weights, arbitrary graph topologies, nonlinear \(p\)-heat, or two-input Lipschitz stability. The first-order sharp exponent is not asserted to be an equality for the finite-time norm at every positive time. Literature equivalence cannot be excluded entirely, particularly through a general positive-matrix theorem expressed in different norm language.
