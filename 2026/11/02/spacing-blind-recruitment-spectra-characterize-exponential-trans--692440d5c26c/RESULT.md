# Spacing-blind recruitment spectra characterize exponential transport, while two stages favor opposite sites

## Finding

There is a sharp distinction between a memoryless, one-way dispersal-distance law and its simplest two-stage analogue on a circular habitat. For any number of distinct point sites, any positive site-specific production and capture factors, and any positive transport attenuation rate, the complete spectrum of the recruitment matrix from a wrapped exponential dispersal density is **independent of the locations of the sites**. Its characteristic polynomial depends only on the number of sites and their production–capture products.

Conversely, among positive continuously differentiable directional point-transfer kernels on a circle, independence of the entire spectrum from site placement for *both every two-site configuration and every three-site configuration* characterizes an exponential kernel. Two sites alone do not characterize it.

For the wrapped two-stage Erlang travel-distance density, two sites of arbitrary positive strengths instead have a uniquely largest Perron recruitment eigenvalue when placed opposite one another on the circle. Their other eigenvalue is strictly negative at every distinct-site separation. Thus the first non-memoryless Erlang stage already turns complete spectral placement indifference into a strict geometric design rule.

## Assumptions and scope

Fix a circular length \(L>0\). Place \(n\geq2\) distinct point sites at \(0\leq x_1<\cdots<x_n<L\). For source site \(j\) and receiving site \(i\), let \(d_{ij}\in[0,L)\) be the clockwise displacement \((x_i-x_j)\bmod L\); thus \(d_{ii}=0\). Choose positive receiving factors \(u_i\) and emitting factors \(v_j\); write \(w_i=u_iv_i>0\). The low-density recruitment matrix is
\[
M_{ij}=u_i v_j k(d_{ij}).
\]
The matrix represents effective transfer coefficients evaluated at site centers, not integration over finite-width marine reserves; no row-stochastic or probability normalization of \(M\) is assumed. Its Perron eigenvalue is the linearized invasion multiplier. In a low-density linear recruitment model, the threshold \(\rho(M)>1\) means growth of the linearized population.

For the converse, let \(k:(0,L)\to(0,\infty)\) be continuously differentiable, with any fixed positive self-transfer coefficient \(k(0)=k_0\). For the explicit forward kernels use \(\alpha>0\), \(q=e^{-\alpha L}\in(0,1)\), and the wrapped densities generated by a single exponential distance or by the sum of two independent exponential distances of common rate \(\alpha\). Each forward-travel density is wrapped by summing over every nonnegative whole-circle winding. Self-transfer is the specified wrapped-density value at zero.

## Proof

**1. Complete spectral invariance under one-stage transport.** For the exponential distance density \(g_1(s)=\alpha e^{-\alpha s}\), \(s\geq0\), its wrapped kernel is
\[
k_1(d)=\sum_{m\geq0}g_1(d+mL)=c e^{-\alpha d},\qquad c=\frac{\alpha}{1-q},\qquad 0\leq d<L.
\]
Let \(D=\operatorname{diag}(e^{-\alpha x_1},\ldots,e^{-\alpha x_n})\) and let \(B\) be the matrix \(B_{ij}=1\) if \(i\geq j\), and \(B_{ij}=q\) if \(i<j\). Directly from clockwise distance,
\[
M=cD\operatorname{diag}(u_i)B\operatorname{diag}(v_i)D^{-1}.
\]
Because \(D\) commutes with the other diagonal matrices, \(M\) is similar to \(c\operatorname{diag}(u_i)B\operatorname{diag}(v_i)\). The locations disappear.

For every \(k\)-element index set, its principal submatrix of \(B\) has the same form as the \(k\times k\) matrix with ones on and below the diagonal and \(q\) above. Successively subtracting each preceding row from the row beneath it, from bottom to top, gives determinant \((1-q)^{k-1}\). The \(k\)-th principal-minor sum of \(M\) is therefore \(c^k(1-q)^{k-1} e_k(w_1,\ldots,w_n)\), where \(e_k\) is the elementary symmetric polynomial. Consequently
\[
\boxed{\det(zI-M)=z^n+\sum_{k=1}^n(-1)^k c^k(1-q)^{k-1}e_k(w_1,\ldots,w_n)z^{n-k}.}
\]
This proves invariance even under permuting site qualities. If all \(w_i=w>0\), the equivalent polynomial identity
\[
(1-q)\det(zI-M)=(z-cw(1-q))^n-qz^n
\]
gives the Perron eigenvalue
\[
\boxed{\rho(M)=\frac{\alpha w}{1-e^{-\alpha L/n}}.}
\]

**2. The converse uses only two- and three-site spectra.** Suppose a strictly positive \(C^1\) kernel \(k\) gives the same eigenvalue multiset for all distinct configurations of two sites and likewise for all distinct configurations of three sites (with unit factors). The determinant of a two-site matrix with gap \(d\) is \(k_0^2-k(d)k(L-d)\); hence
\[
k(d)k(L-d)=Q>0\quad\text{for all }0<d<L.
\]
For three sites, let their consecutive clockwise gaps be \(a,b,c>0\) with \(a+b+c=L\), and set \(S=k(a)k(b)k(c)\). Expanding the three-site determinant gives
\[
\det M_3=k_0^3-3k_0Q+S+\frac{Q^3}{S}.
\]
The determinant is placement independent; hence \(S+Q^3/S\) is constant on the connected open triangle of positive gaps. The positive continuous function \(S\) must itself be constant: its values are restricted to the at most two roots of one fixed quadratic equation. Set \(f=\log k\). Therefore \(f(a)+f(b)+f(L-a-b)\) is constant. Differentiating with respect to the first two variables gives \(f'(a)=f'(b)=f'(L-a-b)\) whenever \(a,b>0\), \(a+b<L\). For arbitrary \(x,y\in(0,L)\), choose a small \(t>0\) with \(x+t<L\) and \(y+t<L\); then \(f'(x)=f'(t)=f'(y)\). Thus \(f\) is affine, and \(k(d)=A e^{\beta d}\) with \(A>0\), \(\beta\in\mathbb R\). Conversely such an exponential kernel, with fixed diagonal, admits the diagonal similarity above (the diagonal entry of the location-free matrix is adjusted if \(k_0\neq A\)), proving placement invariance at every \(n\).

**3. Two-stage travel breaks invariance and selects the antipode.** For \(g_2(s)=\alpha^2s e^{-\alpha s}\), geometric series give
\[
k_2(d)=b e^{-\alpha d}(d+a),\qquad b=\frac{\alpha^2}{1-q},\qquad a=\frac{Lq}{1-q}.
\]
For two sites at clockwise separation \(d\in(0,L)\), diagonal entries are \(ba w_1\) and \(ba w_2\), and the off-diagonal product is \(b^2q w_1w_2(d+a)(L-d+a)\). Since \(q a(a+L)=a^2\),
\[
\boxed{\det M_2(d)=-b^2q w_1w_2d(L-d)<0.}
\]
Its Perron eigenvalue is thus
\[
\boxed{\rho_2(d)=\frac b2\left[a(w_1+w_2)+\sqrt{a^2(w_1+w_2)^2+4qw_1w_2d(L-d)}\right].}
\]
All factors outside \(d(L-d)\) are positive; the latter increases strictly to \(L^2/4\) for \(d=L/2\) and decreases strictly thereafter. Hence \(\rho_2\) has exactly one global maximum at antipodal placement, and its second real eigenvalue is negative. The smallest Erlang stage change has therefore destroyed the one-stage spacing invariance.

## Verification

The companion `verify.py` uses only the Python standard library and checks the full characteristic-polynomial coefficient identity from exact-rational principal minors for ten layouts (two layouts at each of \(n=2,3,4,5,6\)), with nonuniform rational site factors. The check uses \(L=1\), \(\alpha=8\log 2\) and rational grid sites, making every normalized exponential kernel entry rational. It also verifies the exact negative-determinant identity for seven two-site Erlang separations and checks the explicit Perron formula and peak ordering. All 54 sampled identities passed. These computations verify the implementation and representative instances; the arguments above prove the stated continuum and arbitrary-size claims.

## Relationship to prior work

Artzy-Randrup and Stone (2010) derive metapopulation persistence criteria involving the dominant connectivity eigenvalue and the role of dispersal cycles. Kleinhans and Jonsson (2010) examine directional connectivity effects, but their full mathematical model is a stochastic occupancy network with binary adjacency. Neither inspected primary text states the wrapped one-way exponential point-kernel isospectral classification, its two-/three-site converse, or the Erlang-2 antipodal reversal. Botsford, Hastings and Gaines (2001) analyze marine reserve configuration through the fraction of natural larval settlement, a distinct finite-reserve quantity; only its abstract was accessible. Cecino and Treml (2021) examine realistic species-specific marine connectivity matrices and eigenanalysis, not the present exact point-kernel comparison. The geometric diagonal similarity, elementary symmetric functions and Perron-Frobenius theorem are established tools, not claimed as new mathematics in isolation.

## Limitations

The ring, strictly one-way travel, memoryless and Erlang travel-distance families, center-sampled transfer, and local linearized growth are assumptions. Real marine currents can reverse, larvae have finite competency windows and finite-width settlement habitat, and site locations can change local quality. In those cases spacing independence need not hold. The theorem does not assert that finite reserves can be placed arbitrarily without ecological consequences, nor does a linear invasion multiplier prove stochastic long-term persistence. The converse requires positive \(C^1\) kernels and equality of complete spectra for all two- and three-site configurations; it says nothing about one fixed layout or merely equal Perron eigenvalues. Exact scientific priority remains subject to broader semiseparable-matrix literature and unavailable historical full texts.
