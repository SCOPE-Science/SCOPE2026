# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The edge commutator has a standard incidence-matrix factorization over the fraction field of the DVR. The oriented incidence matrix is totally unimodular, and its nonzero minors correspond exactly to rooted spanning forests. Thus valuations of all minors are determined by forest costs, and the determinantal-ideal theorem gives actual Smith exponents, including when a valuation coordinate represents zero modulo the level. The conjugation fibers and counts of valuation shells then give the displayed polynomial with nonnegative \(q-1\) coefficients. The argument handles cycles, disconnected graphs, all characteristics, and all levels. The independent verifier exercised small cycles, complete graphs, a diamond, bipartite graphs, and the non-cograph four-vertex path, with `VERIFY_OK 44`. Finite computations support the proof but do not replace it.

**Originality — PASS relative to inspected sources.** Rossmann–Voll (arXiv:1908.09589, Theorem E) prove the positivity for cographs at all levels and explicitly ask in Question 1.7 which graphs enjoy it. Their general uniformity theorem covers existence of class-number polynomials but does not establish coefficientwise positivity beyond cographs. Rossmann (arXiv:2107.05564, Corollary C) proves positivity for every graph at level one, and Section 8 discusses higher levels. These results are accurately retained as prior coverage. The new claim applies to all graphs and all higher levels, refining class numbers by conjugacy-class size. Search of a mathematical findings database returned related character-degree results for trees, cycles, and joins, not the same class-size/congruence-level theorem. Residual risk: general valuation-minor or p-adic enumeration literature may contain an equivalent proof not identified by the bounded search. Priority has not been independently certified.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** The conclusion directly resolves the question posed in Rossmann–Voll (2019, Question 1.7) as stated, and upgrades total counts to each class-size stratum for arbitrary graphs. Unlike a single small-graph computation, the result uses a natural spanning-forest structural invariant that works over arbitrary finite residue fields and arbitrarily high congruence levels, including graph families outside the earlier cograph theorem. It supplies an explicit, independently implementable combinatorial formula. This does not claim a compact rational formula for the full zeta function or new analytic results.
