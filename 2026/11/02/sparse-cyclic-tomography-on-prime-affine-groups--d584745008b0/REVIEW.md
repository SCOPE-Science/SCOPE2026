# Scientific review: sparse cyclic tomography on prime-affine groups

## Correctness — PASS
The exact domain is a prime \(p\), divisor \(q\ge3\) of \(p-1\), and a faithful cyclic affine action. The claimed kernel formula follows from a unitary one-coordinate Fourier reduction. The zero-frequency block is treated separately, including the empty complement family. For every nonzero frequency, Chebotarev nonvanishing gives precisely rank \(\min(m,q)\); the full proof also reconstructs the requisite nonstandard lemma. The normal-block and Cauchy–Binet determinants are derived with counting-measure normalization. The full-affine spectral corollary uses one deleted row and column of a prime DFT. Independent finite-incidence and spectral diagnostics passed. No finite test is presented as a proof of the all-prime theorem.

## Originality — PASS under bounded source review
The closest inspected primary source, Ilmavirta (arXiv:1411.3829, especially Sections 2, 6 and 7.3), treats full finite-group Radon transforms and maximal cyclic variants, but does not state the sharp selection-invariant nullity of prime-affine subfamilies. Tao (arXiv:math/0308286, Lemma 1.3) establishes the crucial prime Fourier-minor theorem; the present nonabelian coset measurement classification is not its stated object. The previously treated dihedral boundary is excluded from the novel scope. Scarabotti (DOI:10.1137/S0895480102416398) is closely titled but treats multiplicity-free permutation representation transforms in its accessible abstract; full text was inaccessible and remains an explicit equivalence risk. Literature searches and mathematical implication comparisons, not absence of matching titles, support the provisional assessment.

## Value — PASS
Determining the least number of complete view directions is an intrinsic sampling-design question. This theorem gives that minimum for an infinite nonabelian affine family, classifies the rank of **every** partial direction set, proves choice-independent exact recovery at the minimum, and provides an exact normal determinant and a sharp three-level full-affine stability law. In particular, only \(q\) extra scalar measurements beyond the \(pq\) unknowns are required at the direction minimum. The contribution is not a mere renaming of the prime Fourier-minor lemma or a finite census.

## Limitations and residual risks
The translation group must have prime order, and direction families must contain every left coset of each chosen maximal cyclic subgroup. General exact eigenvalues are given by explicit Fourier blocks rather than radicals. Equivalent preexisting theorems in specialized finite Radon or group-frame literature remain possible; the Scarabotti full paper was not materially available. Independent mathematical validation is unperformed.
