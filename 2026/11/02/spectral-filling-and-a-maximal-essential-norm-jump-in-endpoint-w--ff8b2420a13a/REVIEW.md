# Scientific review

## Correctness

**PASS.** The critical endpoint kernel is conjugate by the logistic coordinate to convolution on the full real line with Fourier multiplier \(1/(1/2+i\xi)\). The standard Hardy baseline is conjugate to the adjoint of multiplication by \(1-\zeta\) on disk Hardy space. The squared Hilbert–Schmidt kernel integral is evaluated by a digamma difference; compact perturbation preserves its Fredholm circle and index. Explicit integration of the eigenfunction ODE identifies the complete point spectrum, and Fredholm index plus injectivity rules out exterior spectrum. Weakly null endpoint-layer test functions converge to the adjoint Hardy operator, certifying the exact essential norm \(2\) for every gap to the critical operator. The same parameter family converges strongly by dominated convergence on a dense class. A localized near-endpoint test proves the unboundedness phase. The standalone numerical checker passed 54 supplementary checks; these are not substitutes for infinite-dimensional proofs.

## Originality

**PASS with residual risk.** Belli–Gül–Ross–Siskakis (arXiv:2604.19691v1 and arXiv:2606.08600v1) prove the critical operator’s normality and circle spectrum, with the latter paper varying ambient \(L^p\), not the kernel exponent. Abadías–Mahillo–Miana (arXiv:2604.20408) treat fractional kernels built from \((x-t)^{\alpha-1}\), not the \((1-t)^{-\gamma}\) endpoint deformation. Classical Hardy shift theory supplies the subcritical reference operator and is expressly used rather than claimed as new. The comparison of full mathematical statements and searched mathematical records found no complete classification that simultaneously gives the subcritical Fredholm index, critical normality, strong convergence, and an exact maximal essential-norm discontinuity. General weighted Hardy spectral results not found in the inspected search may still imply parts of the classification; no proof of absolute publication priority is asserted.

## Mathematical value

**PASS.** A natural one-parameter deformation between the classical Hardy average and the recently analyzed normal real-variable Cesàro operator has a complete sharp spectral and boundedness phase diagram. The ordinary spectra abruptly fill a disk for every subcritical exponent even though the Fredholm essential spectral circle does not move. The Fredholm index changes, while the operators converge strongly but remain an exact maximal distance \(2\) apart even modulo compacts. This cleanly separates compact perturbation stability from strong-operator topology and supplies a concrete boundary-layer mechanism, rather than merely a routine norm estimate.

## Limitations and relationship to literature

The result is specific to real powers and complex unweighted \(L^2(0,1)\). Established endpoint normality, Hardy baseline results and Fredholm compact invariance are credited. A fully exhaustive priority search, independent peer review, and exact singular-value sequence of the perturbation are not provided.
