# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** The exact forward equations, weighted-density conjugation, Darboux factorization, spatial continuum threshold, and temporal quadratic fully determine the claimed bound spectrum and the sharp spectral abscissa. Integer-parameter threshold states are not square integrable; the nonconserving temporal branch of the zero spatial mode is excluded. Symbolic and finite-difference tests corroborate critical algebraic steps without serving as the proof.

**Originality — PASS, with risk.** Jain and Chatterjee (arXiv:2405.13521) already derive the stationary density and the limiting step-gradient decay scale; neither is new. Their finite-gradient time evolution is not analytically evaluated. The present finite-gradient spectral staircase, mode-onset gradients, parity classification, and exact saturation point were not found in inspected primary sources or record searches. The Pöschl–Teller bound spectrum is standard, and an undiscovered transport mapping could subsume part or all of the application; priority is not independently certified.

**Mathematical value — PASS.** Identifying when relaxation ceases to improve under increased rate-gradient steepness, and when the distinct odd and even modes disappear, resolves a motivated dynamic gap of the original smooth saturating-rate model, with explicit control-parameter thresholds rather than a generic upper bound. It also sharpens the interpretation of the source's step-rate limit.

**Scope and risks.** Only constant speed, hyperbolic-tangent reversal rates and strictly positive limiting rates are covered; the gap is spectral in a weighted density space, not a universal convergence theorem for singular measures. Continuum thresholds can bring algebraic factors and some prepared data suppress the slowest mode.
