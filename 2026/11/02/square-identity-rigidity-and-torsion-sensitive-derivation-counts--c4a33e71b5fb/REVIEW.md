# Scientific review: square-identity derivations of poset incidence algebras

## Correctness — PASS

The ring-level proof derives support restrictions from diagonal idempotents without division by two. Polarization over any commutative coefficient ring identifies a unique strictly offdiagonal inner correction; two Peirce identities restrict every remaining basis value to a scalar multiple of its interval generator. Polarized products on three-element chains impose exactly the simplicial cocycle condition, which suffices for the ordinary Leibniz rule on every pair of basis elements. The count uses the explicit inner-plus-cocycle parametrization and the integral universal coefficient theorem. No finite enumeration is substituted for the proof.

For computational cross-checks, the original multiplication, all square/polarization identities and all Leibniz equations were independently assembled as integer matrices, checked across seven posets and 26 field/ring configurations. An independent integral Smith calculation verifies the exhibited projective-plane torsion and the relevant comparable-pair count.

## Originality — PASS, subject to priority risk

Xiao's 2014 full text defines the square-only identity but proves incidence-algebra rigidity under a \(2\)-torsion-free hypothesis (Theorem 3.3). Khrypchenko's 2015 full text removes characteristic restrictions only while strengthening the **definition** of Jordan derivation to include an independent \(D(aba)\) identity (Section 1, equations (1)–(2)). Neither statement by itself implies the candidate's characteristic-free square-only conclusion. Cheng–Jing's triangular-algebra theorem and Yang's nonlinear incidence result maintain \(2\)-torsion-free hypotheses. Prior work already knows inner-plus-transitive decompositions for ordinary incidence derivations; this is explicitly **not** an independent novelty claim.

The current contribution is the sharp hypothesis relaxation for every finite poset and the general finite-coefficient-ring counting consequence, including the same-cardinality \(\mathbb F_4\) versus \(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\) example. Published published-finding corpus records and previous accessible findings were searched for the same statement, mathematical aliases, cohomological reductions, stronger theorems and tables; none retrieved directly covered the full weak-square statement and torsion-sensitive counts. The projective-plane homology witness itself is classical and also appears in an earlier published six-vertex torsion census; it is not presented as novel. Full-text access to some potentially relevant older idempotent-algebra results was unavailable, so historical priority remains provisional.

## Value — PASS

The square-only versus square-plus-triple distinction is a genuine characteristic-two boundary in the derivation literature. Resolving it without any \(2\)-torsion-free assumption simultaneously answers the original finite-poset incidence-algebra question for all commutative coefficient rings, not merely one small exceptional example. The finite-ring formula shows how the complete first integral homology, including torsion, affects a concrete algebraic invariant. A six-vertex projective-plane triangulation gives a natural demonstration that the invariant distinguishes equal-cardinality finite coefficient rings. These are structural consequences rather than arbitrary parameter substitutions.

## Closest literature and limits

Full primary texts inspected: Xiao (arXiv:1411.6123v1, Sections 1–3 including Theorem 3.3) and Khrypchenko (arXiv:1510.00944v1, Section 1 and Theorem 3/Remark 6). Related triangular-algebra and nonlinear-incidence results were compared using their available theorem statements/abstracts. Benkovič–Širovnik's *Jordan derivations of unital algebras with idempotents* (Linear Algebra Appl. 2012) and Benkovič's 2005 triangular-matrix paper are plausible historical sources not verified in complete full text, and are specific priority risks. No external independent review was performed. The proof requires finite antisymmetric posets and coefficient-linear maps; the formula's numerical part additionally requires a finite commutative ring.
