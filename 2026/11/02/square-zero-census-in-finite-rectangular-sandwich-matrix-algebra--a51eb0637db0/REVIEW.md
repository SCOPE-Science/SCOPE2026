# Scientific review: Square-zero census in finite rectangular sandwich matrix algebras

## Correctness — PASS
The asserted object is the sandwich algebra \(M_{m\times n}(\mathbb F_q)\) with product \(X\star Y=XPY\), for every prime power \(q\), positive integers \(m,n\), and every allowed sandwich rank \(r\). Base changes reduce the sandwich matrix to rank normal form without changing the count. The equation becomes four independent block constraints \(A^2=AB=CA=CB=0\). Rank-\(j\) square-zero middle blocks correspond bijectively to image/kernel flags and an isomorphism; the projected \(B\)-map has rank \(u\), each such map has the claimed number of lifts, and \(C\) factors through a quotient of exactly the stated dimension. The resulting sum is exhaustive and nonoverlapping. The \(r=0\), full-rank, and \(r=1\) boundaries agree with their defining equations. A self-contained program also directly enumerates 19 canonical/noncanonical finite-field examples and confirms four geometric point-count checks, including a nonprime field. No finite test is used as the proof of a quantified statement.

## Originality — PASS (with residual risk)
The main closest primary paper, Dolinka–East, arXiv:1503.03139, addresses these precise sandwich semigroups, including the exact idempotent count in Proposition 7.2. Its enumerated equation \(XPX=X\) does not imply the present square-zero count \(XPX=0\); the full text's subject and central numbered results were inspected rather than relying on the abstract. Classical square-zero matrix enumerations apply to the special case \(m=n=r\) and are not claimed as original. Searches for the rectangular Munn/sandwich square-zero equation and for plausible reductions to known matrix equations did not identify a covering general statement in the accessible literature or published mathematical search results. The absence of a matched source is not conclusive novelty proof; historical specialized semigroup or matrix-equation literature remains a priority risk.

## Value — PASS
Square-zero elements describe the first nontrivial nilpotent stratum of Munn and generalized rectangular matrix algebras. The exact all-parameter count complements the established finite-field idempotent census on the same natural family, distinguishes intermediate sandwich ranks, and provides a reducible rank-one boundary with explicit component dimensions and intersection. This is a family-level semigroup-algebra invariant, rather than an arbitrary low-dimensional table or simple scalar substitution. It leaves higher nilpotency-index distributions and orbit stratification open.

Related finite-field matrix-equation papers may contain implicit specializations not recognized by the searches. The full-rank square-matrix case and rank-zero trivial case are accepted as consistency checks, not novelty claims. The reduced rank-one loci should not be conflated with the possibly nonreduced defining schemes. Independent validation and exhaustive priority verification are outstanding.
