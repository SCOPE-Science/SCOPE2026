# Mathematical review

## Correctness: PASS

For a finite local commutative ring with square-zero maximal ideal, all proper ideals are exactly the subspaces of its maximal ideal over the residue field. Two proper ideals always have zero product, so counting nonzero tuples gives the exact formula \(1-[1+k(S-1)]/(S+1)^k\). Algebraic comparison with \(1-2^{-k}\) proves the full sharp sign classification, with the unique nonfield equality parameters \((q,d,k)=(2,2,2)\). The six explicit ideals of the eight-element ring produce 27 zero pairs out of 36; order-four and order-six classifications establish minimality. Direct independent multiplication-table enumeration corroborates the result and includes a characteristic-four example. A separate exhaustive program checks the four smaller-ring isomorphism classes used in the minimality proof. The proof does not rely on numerical extrapolation.

## Originality: PASS, with residual risks

The inspected Chakraborty–Patra 2026 paper defines this invariant and expressly conjectures that the finite-field value characterizes fields (Conjecture 4.2). Its complete text treats fields, Boolean rings, chain rings and principal ideal rings; the new six-ideal example is nonprincipal, so those formulas neither state nor imply the field-value collision. The explicit counterexample and sharp all-length square-zero-local comparison were not found in the targeted mathematical-record, literature, or prior-claim comparisons. This establishes best-knowledge originality, not independently certified historical priority. A classical ideal-subspace observation could make the formula independently easy to rediscover; no claim of novelty is made for Gaussian-binomial counting alone.

## Mathematical value: PASS

The eight-element construction directly falsifies an explicitly published characterization conjecture, and minimality makes the boundary sharp. The family theorem supplies the full sequence for finite square-zero local rings, including mixed-characteristic cases, and explains why the field benchmark can be smaller, larger or identical depending on embedding dimension and number of factors. Thus the contribution resolves an identifiable mathematical gap beyond a one-off numerical example. It does not settle the original characterization for unrestricted rings at all lengths.
