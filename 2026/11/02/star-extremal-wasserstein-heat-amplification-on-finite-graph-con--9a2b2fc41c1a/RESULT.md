# Star-extremal Wasserstein heat amplification on finite graph cones

## Finding

Let \(H\) be any finite simple undirected graph on \(d\ge2\) vertices, with no connectivity assumption, and let \(G=K_1\vee H\) be its cone: adjoin a hub \(o\) connected to every vertex of \(H\). Give every edge length one and equip \(G\) with the shortest-path metric \(\operatorname{dist}_G\). Let \(Q_G f(x)=\sum_{z\sim_G x}(f(z)-f(x))\), with **unit rate in both directions on each edge**, and \(P_t=e^{tQ_G}\). Define the exact \(W_1\) amplification coefficient, for all \(t\ge0\), by

\[
\mathcal C_G(t)=\sup_{\mu\ne\nu}\frac{W_1^{G}(\mu P_t,\nu P_t)}{W_1^{G}(\mu,\nu)}.
\]

Then the following **universal, time-sharp comparison** holds for every such \(H\):

\[
\boxed{\quad\mathcal C_G(t)\le F_d(t):=\frac{2(d-1)}d e^{-t}-\frac{d-2}{d}e^{-(d+1)t}\quad(t\ge0).\quad}
\]

For **each** \(t>0\), equality holds if and only if \(H\) has an isolated vertex. When such a vertex \(y\) exists, \(\mu=\delta_o\), \(\nu=\delta_y\) attain equality at every time, so the whole all-time profile is exactly \(F_d\). When \(H\) has no isolated vertex, the inequality is strict for every \(t>0\).

For \(d\in\{2,3\}\), \(F_d\) is nonincreasing from one; hence every cone with at most four vertices has nonexpansive heat flow. For \(d\ge4\), the envelope has one positive maximizing time,

\[
t_d^*=\frac1d\log\left(\frac{(d-2)(d+1)}{2(d-1)}\right),
\]

and peak value

\[
F_d(t_d^*)=\frac{2(d-1)}{d+1}\left(\frac{2(d-1)}{(d-2)(d+1)}\right)^{1/d}>1.
\]

As \(d\to\infty\), \(t_d^*=\log(d/2)/d+O(d^{-2})\), and

\[
\max_{t\ge0}F_d(t)=2-\frac{2\log(d/2)+4}{d}+O\left(\frac{(\log d)^2}{d^2}\right).
\]

Thus star cones, and exactly cones retaining a pendant vertex, attain a universal diameter-two asymptotic peak. The assertion is a sharp universal envelope and its equality classification, **not** an exact formula for cones whose peripheral graph has no isolated vertex.

## Assumptions and scope

The graph \(H\) is arbitrary, simple, unweighted, and finite, with \(d\ge2\); isolated vertices are allowed. The cone \(G\) is connected even when \(H\) is not. The Markov generator is the **unnormalized combinatorial generator**, not the degree-normalized random-walk generator. All Wasserstein distances use the **same unweighted graph shortest-path metric**, independently of jump rates. The graph has \(d+1\) vertices. The statements concern probability measures and real Lipschitz functions; the dual expression extends by homogeneity to zero-total-mass signed measures.

## Proof

**1. Reduction to edges.** By finite Kantorovich duality, \(W_1\) is dual to the real Lipschitz seminorm. Thus \(\mathcal C_G(t)=\sup_{\operatorname{Lip}_G f\le1}\operatorname{Lip}_G(P_tf)\). Since all graph edges have length one and shortest-path distance is generated by them, this is

\[
\mathcal C_G(t)=\max_{xy\in E(G)}W_1^G(\delta_xP_t,\delta_yP_t).
\]

There are only two classes to bound: hub–periphery edges and the edges of \(H\).

**2. Exact cone semigroup decomposition.** Let \(Q_*\) be the unit-rate generator of the star on the same vertices, and let \(\widetilde Q_H\) be the generator of \(H\) acting on its vertices and acting as zero at the hub. Then \(Q_G=Q_*+\widetilde Q_H\). The operators commute: decompose functions into constants, the one-dimensional hub-versus-uniform-periphery space, and the mean-zero-periphery space. On the latter \(Q_*=-I\); on the former two spaces \(\widetilde Q_H=0\), using symmetry of \(Q_H\). Write \(R_t=e^{tQ_H}\) on the \(d\) peripheral vertices. The hub mode has eigenvalue \(-(d+1)\); each peripheral contrast is multiplied by \(e^{-t}R_t\).

**3. Hub edges.** Fix a peripheral vertex \(y\), let \(a=e^{-t}\), \(b=e^{-(d+1)t}\), and let \(\alpha=(a-b)/d\). The signed difference \(\rho=\delta_oP_t-\delta_yP_t\) has

\[
\rho(o)=b,\qquad \rho(v)=\alpha-a R_t(y,v)\quad (v\in V(H)).
\]

Replace \(G\) temporarily by the spanning star with the same unit radial edges, and write \(W_1^*\) for its transport metric. Since adding the edges of \(H\) cannot increase distances, \(W_1^G\le W_1^*\) on fixed measures. The edge-flow formula on a star gives \(W_1^*(\rho)=\sum_{v\in H}|\rho(v)|\). Let \(q_v=R_t(y,v)\), a probability vector. For \(t>0\) we have \(0<\alpha<a/d\), and

\[
\sum_v|\alpha-aq_v|=d\alpha+a-2\sum_v\min(\alpha,aq_v)\le a+(d-2)\alpha=F_d(t).
\]

The inequality \(\sum_v\min(\alpha,aq_v)\ge\alpha\) follows directly: either some \(aq_v\ge\alpha\), or all are smaller and their sum is \(a>\alpha\). Equality holds precisely when \(q\) is a point mass. For \(t>0\), the continuous-time \(H\)-walk from \(y\) remains a point mass precisely if \(y\) is isolated. If isolated, all transported negative mass is at \(y\), and \(\operatorname{dist}_G(y,o)=1\), \(\operatorname{dist}_G(y,v)=2\) for every other peripheral \(v\), so the star transport plan is also optimal on \(G\). Therefore the hub–isolate edge has **exactly** cost \(F_d(t)\). For nonisolated \(y\) its cost is strictly smaller. At \(t=0\), the inequality is equality, with both sides one.

**4. Internal peripheral edges.** Let \(u\sim_Hv\). The cone decomposition gives

\[
\delta_uP_t-\delta_vP_t=e^{-t}(\delta_uR_t-\delta_vR_t),
\]

with no hub mass. Uniformize the \(H\)-walk at rate \(d\): the stochastic matrix \(S=I+Q_H/d\) has diagonal entries at least \(1/d\), while \(S(u,v)=S(v,u)=1/d\). Therefore the rows \(S(u,\cdot)\) and \(S(v,\cdot)\) overlap in mass at least \(2/d\), yielding \(\operatorname{TV}(\delta_uS,\delta_vS)\le1-2/d\). Total variation cannot increase under a Markov kernel, so the same holds for \(S^k\), \(k\ge1\). All peripheral pairs are at graph distance at most two; hence \(W_1^G(\delta_uS^k,\delta_vS^k)\le2-4/d\), whereas at \(k=0\) the cost is one. Poisson uniformization and convexity of transport yield

\[
W_1^G(\delta_uP_t,\delta_vP_t)\le B_d(t)
:=e^{-t}\bigl[e^{-dt}+(1-e^{-dt})(2-4/d)\bigr].
\]

For \(d=2,3\), the bracket is at most one while \(e^tF_d(t)\ge1\), with strict separation for \(t>0\). For \(d\ge4\), subtracting gives

\[
e^t(F_d(t)-B_d(t))=\frac2d+\left(1-\frac4d\right)e^{-dt}-\left(1-\frac2d\right)e^{-(d+1)t}
\ge\frac2d(1-e^{-dt})>0\quad(t>0).
\]

Thus **every** internal edge is strictly below the universal bound at every positive time, independently of the structure or connectedness of \(H\). Combined with step 3 and the edge reduction, this proves the bound and the exact isolated-vertex equality criterion.

**5. Peak and asymptotics.** Direct differentiation of \(F_d\) gives the unique positive critical point when \(d\ge4\), and no positive critical point for \(d=2,3\). At that point \((d-2)(d+1)e^{-(d+1)t}=2(d-1)e^{-t}\), giving the explicit peak. Expanding \(\log((d-2)(d+1)/(2(d-1)))\) and the resulting peak yields the two claimed asymptotic expansions, with constants and error orders shown above.

## Verification

The proof is valid for every allowed finite \(H\), not by finite enumeration. As an independent supplementary test, `checker.py` reconstructs generators and Kantorovich dual problems from graphs, exhaustively enumerating **1,098** peripheral graphs on \(d=2,3,4,5\) vertices at seven times. It checks universal domination and, separately, equality for cones with an isolated peripheral vertex and strict inequality for cones without one. An additional 296-parameter peak test checks the calculus identities. Reproduction: run `python checker.py` with Python 3, NumPy and SciPy; the expected final message begins `CHECK_OK`. The checker enumerates extreme integral Lipschitz potentials, whose integrality follows from the total-unimodularity of graph difference constraints after fixing the hub potential to zero.

## Relationship to prior work

Münch and Wojciechowski, *Ollivier Ricci curvature for general graph Laplacians* (first arXiv version posted 2017-12-04, arXiv:1712.00875), prove a general relation between Lipschitz heat-semigroup behavior, Wasserstein transport and curvature; specifically their Theorem 3.8 concerns exponential bounds from a uniform curvature lower bound. Münch, *Non-negative Ollivier curvature on graphs* (arXiv:1907.13514, first posted 2019-07-31), studies Wasserstein/total-variation estimates under nonnegative curvature. Neither inspected paper states a sharp all-time star comparison for arbitrary cones or characterizes equality by peripheral isolated vertices. The formula specializes to stars and yields a comparison for symmetric windmills and other cone families, but such examples do not supply the universal arbitrary-\(H\) bound or its equality rigidity. The present proof uses a commuting cone-generator decomposition and a uniformized overlap comparison, rather than deducing its sharp envelope from a global curvature bound.

## Limitations

The result is a sharp **upper envelope**, not an evaluation of \(\mathcal C_G\) for cones with no peripheral isolates. It does not cover non-unit jump rates, weighted geometric edges, directed generators, non-cone graphs, infinite peripheral graphs, or \(W_p\) for \(p\ne1\). Historical priority is not independently certified; terminology such as universal-vertex graph, apex join, and cone may conceal equivalent earlier results despite the inspected searches.
