# Review: Stochastic persistence ordering by the number of domain walls

## Correctness
**PASS.** Each switch count chooses a uniformly random subset of the bonds. The two compositions of positive/negative runs give the signed conditional mass exactly; the even/odd identity follows from one binomial-ratio cancellation. The next odd law divided by the previous odd law is an explicit decreasing quadratic in the absolute displacement, which proves the likelihood-ratio and stochastic comparisons. A shared-uniform coupling of switch indicators gives the parameter order, and the positive-probability zero-vs-one switch event proves strictness at every nontrivial threshold. All boundary and normalization cases are stated. Separate exact enumerations up to length fourteen yield 4,906 assertions. Finite checks corroborate but do not establish the universal claim.

## Originality
**PASS, with residual historical-priority risk.** Pemmasani (arXiv:2610.06935v1, §§2,4) supplies the known combinatorial word counts and pointwise bin crossing order; it does not compare absolute total displacement simultaneously at every threshold and across all persistence values. Dekking–Kong (arXiv:1102.3613v1, §§2–5) classify marginal shapes and moments, not this parameter stochastic order; Zheng–Lin–Zhang (arXiv:2605.06691v1, §§3–5) compare fixed-energy densities and thermodynamic free energies, not the normalized finite-chain parameter order. The related 1981 study by Renshaw–Henderson was discoverable only through abstract/metadata, not its full content; an equivalent earlier observation cannot be excluded. Scientific comparison is by implication, not title matching. Current mathematical-record searches found no covering recorded result, which is not a proof of global novelty.

## Value
**PASS.** The parameter influences every spin jointly, and individual position-bin probabilities can be nonmonotone as the persistence changes; a simultaneous first-order order on the entire absolute-displacement law is substantially stronger than variance growth or a fixed-bin comparison. The finite-chain theorem works uniformly across the sign change of nearest-neighbor correlation, including the anti-persistent regime where usual ferromagnetic arguments cannot be invoked. The parity collapse and likelihood-ratio cascade exhibit a reusable structural mechanism that makes this full distributional comparison possible.

## Limits and antecedents
The existing exact coefficients are prior work, not an originality claim. The finding applies only to symmetric transitions with fair start and free endpoints. Historical priority remains qualified by inaccessible exact-law literature. The verification program is finite corroboration, not a substitute for proof.
