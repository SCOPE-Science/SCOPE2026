# Review: Stochastic persistence ordering by the number of domain walls

## Correctness
**PASS.** Each switch count chooses a uniformly random subset of the bonds. The two compositions of positive/negative runs give the signed conditional mass exactly; the even/odd identity follows from one binomial-ratio cancellation. The next odd law divided by the previous odd law is an explicit decreasing quadratic in the absolute displacement, which proves the likelihood-ratio and stochastic comparisons. A shared-uniform coupling gives the parameter order. At nontrivial thresholds the positive-probability zero-vs-one switch event gives strictness when the lower persistence is positive. At zero lower persistence the alternating displacement has the minimum absolute value, whereas any positive persistence gives a positive chance of no switches and maximal absolute displacement. The supplied checker exhausts lengths through fourteen; those finite checks do not establish the universal claim.

## Originality
Pemmasani (arXiv:2610.06935v1, Theorems 2.1 and 3.1) supplies the combinatorial word counts and pointwise bin crossing order. The conditional signed masses are encoded in those word counts and the parity equality follows by simplifying them. The distinct claim is the resulting ordered family of conditional laws and strict comparison simultaneously at every nontrivial threshold across all persistence values. Dekking–Kong (arXiv:1102.3613v1) classify marginal shapes and moments; Zheng–Lin–Zhang (arXiv:2605.06691v1) study monotonicity in magnetization of a Landau free energy. These do not give the normalized finite-chain comparison as the parameter varies. The 1981 Renshaw–Henderson article was compared at abstract level only; an equivalent earlier observation cannot be excluded.

## Value
**PASS.** The parameter influences every spin jointly, and individual position-bin probabilities can be nonmonotone as the persistence changes; a simultaneous first-order order on the entire absolute-displacement law is substantially stronger than variance growth or a fixed-bin comparison. The finite-chain theorem works uniformly across the sign change of nearest-neighbor correlation, including the anti-persistent regime where usual ferromagnetic arguments cannot be invoked. The parity collapse and likelihood-ratio cascade exhibit a reusable structural mechanism that makes this full distributional comparison possible.

## Limits and antecedents
The existing exact coefficients are prior work, not an originality claim. The finding applies only to symmetric transitions with fair start and free endpoints. Historical priority remains qualified by inaccessible exact-law literature. The verification program is finite corroboration, not a substitute for proof.
