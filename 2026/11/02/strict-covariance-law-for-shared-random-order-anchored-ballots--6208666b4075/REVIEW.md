# Scientific review: shared-order anchored ballot covariance

## Correctness — PASS

The fixed-ranking, uniformly shared-presentation model reduces ballot length to the count of lower records. The exact adjacent-swap identity follows from a measure-preserving transposition of two display clocks and integration of the changed record indicators. The intermediate-rank integrals telescope, leaving \(1/[r(r+1)]\) with \(r\ge1\), so no boundary or sign exception occurs. Adjacent inversion removal proves both uniqueness assertions; independent uniform insertion ranks yield the classical endpoint covariances. All relevant quantifiers, empty-predecessor conventions and the full-acceptability hypothesis are explicit. The extracted verifier reproduces complete small-size cases and independent moment formulas; it is not used as a substitute for the universal proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The closest 2026 paper by Fioravanti and Terzopoulou introduces the precise anchoring process but addresses voting-rule invariance under arbitrary separate display orders. That theorem does not imply a covariance formula under one *shared random* order. Gnedin's 2006 record-statistic paper covers the lower/upper endpoint type and makes those components classical, but does not yield the arbitrary fixed-ranking adjacent-inversion formula directly. Johnson, Leader and Long (2020) prove correlation facts for permutation up-sets, not this pair of display-record observables. Related published records on permutation inversion-indicator covariance and cycle-count/inversion dependence were also compared by their actual mathematical statements. There remains a residual risk of unindexed equivalent work on re-ranked permutation records; the review cannot establish absolute priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS

Shared voter-interface ordering is a natural constraint explicitly distinguished from independently chosen display orders. The theorem determines exactly how local differences in preference agreement change the induced covariance, with a nontrivial strict monotonicity law and unique global extrema for every number of alternatives. The lower endpoint is negative for every \(m\ge2\), whereas identical preferences maximize positive correlation; thus common randomization alone does not enforce positive cross-voter dependence. This identifies a concrete design-relevant distributional feature, not merely a restatement of classical record counts.

## Risks and limits

The analytic theorem is limited to fully acceptable alternatives, fixed strict rankings and shared uniform permutation. A literature search is not a novelty certificate; no independent scientific audit or empirical behavioral validation has been performed. The negative lower endpoint is a classical record-statistics consequence; the new claim is the full ranking-indexed exact adjacent-inversion and sharp extremal classification.
