# Strict negative covariance of disjoint multinomial absolute contrasts

## Finding
Let \(N\ge1\) and \((X_1,\ldots,X_k)\) be multinomial with \(k\ge4\) categories. For any four distinct categories with positive category masses, relabel them \(1,2,3,4\). Then
\[
\operatorname{Cov}\bigl(|X_1-X_2|,|X_3-X_4|\bigr)<0.
\]
The assertion holds without uniform category probabilities, without asymptotics, and without any restriction on the remaining category masses beyond nonnegativity and summing to one. If instead the sample total is Poisson with any positive mean, the corresponding covariance is exactly zero.

## Assumptions and scope
The count vector is generated by \(N\) independent categorical draws with probabilities \(p_1,\ldots,p_k\). Assume \(p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4>0\), and \(\sum_{i=1}^k p_i=1\). Other masses may vanish. Set \(a=p_1+p_2\), \(b=p_3+p_4\), \(\theta=p_1/a\), and \(\phi=p_3/b\). Thus \(a,b\in(0,1)\), \(a+b\le1\), and \(\theta,\phi\in(0,1)\). All covariance statements concern the same fixed sample size \(N\).

## Proof
Write \(M=X_1+X_2\), \(L=X_3+X_4\). Their joint law is \(\operatorname{Mult}(N;a,b,1-a-b)\). Conditional on \((M,L)\), the allocations inside the two blocks are independent. If \(S_m\) is a sum of \(m\) independent signs taking \(+1\) with probability \(\theta\) and \(-1\) otherwise, set \(f_\theta(m)=\mathbb E|S_m|\), with \(f_\theta(0)=0\). Consequently,
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(|X_1-X_2|,|X_3-X_4|)
=\operatorname{Cov}(f_\theta(M),f_\phi(L)).
\]
We first establish that \(f_\theta(m)\) is nondecreasing for every \(\theta\in[0,1]\) and integer \(m\ge0\). Put \(\mu=2\theta-1\). Conditioning on \(S_m\) gives the exact difference
\[
f_\theta(m+1)-f_\theta(m)
=\mu\bigl[\Pr(S_m>0)-\Pr(S_m<0)\bigr]+\Pr(S_m=0).
\]
When \(\theta\ge1/2\), coupling each sign with its reflected sign shows that \(S_m\) stochastically dominates \(-S_m\). Hence the bracket is nonnegative and so is the difference. When \(\theta<1/2\), reflecting every sign gives \(f_\theta=f_{1-\theta}\), reducing to the previous case. Also \(f_\theta(m)>0\) whenever \(m\ge1\): the random walk cannot be almost surely zero, and in the present positive-parameter case the all-positive sequence has positive probability.

Conditionally on \(L=\ell\), \(M\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N-\ell,r)\) with \(r=a/(1-b)\in(0,1]\). Define
\[
h(\ell)=\mathbb E f_\theta(\operatorname{Bin}(N-\ell,r)),\qquad 0\le\ell\le N.
\]
The standard nested-Bernoulli coupling and monotonicity of \(f_\theta\) imply that \(h\) is nonincreasing. Moreover \(h(N)=0\) and \(h(0)>0\), since \(r>0\) and \(N\ge1\). As \(L\sim\operatorname{Bin}(N,b)\), both events \(L=0\) and \(L=N\) have strictly positive probability. The function \(f_\phi\) is nondecreasing with \(f_\phi(0)=0<f_\phi(N)\).

By conditional expectation and the covariance identity for an independent copy \(L'\),
\[
2\operatorname{Cov}(f_\theta(M),f_\phi(L))
=\mathbb E\bigl[(h(L)-h(L'))(f_\phi(L)-f_\phi(L'))\bigr].
\]
All terms inside this expectation are nonpositive. On the positive-probability event \((L,L')=(0,N)\), the product is strictly negative. Thus the covariance is strictly negative, proving the claim for every permitted \(N\) and categorical law.

For the Poisson-total assertion, independently Poissonizing the sample count splits the category counts into independent Poisson variables. The two contrasts then depend on disjoint independent count collections and have zero covariance.

## Relationship to prior work
Banić and Elezović (2026), *Discrete total variation under the multinomial, Part 2: variance under the uniform null*, analyze the same disjoint-edge covariance under uniform cell masses. Their Section 4 gives a finite-sample expression and identifies its negative asymptotic limit as the sample size grows with category count fixed. The theorem here supplies a strict sign valid for **every** finite positive sample size and extends it to arbitrary positive masses on the four involved categories. The related Part 1 addresses the mean absolute difference. Neither previously established expression or asymptotic statement is claimed as new. Classical multinomial negative-dependence methods are a possible route to the same sign, but absolute count contrasts are not coordinatewise monotone in their individual count variables; this proof uses disjoint-block conditioning. Historical priority remains subject to independent review.

## Limitations
The theorem does not give a sharp nonzero numerical lower bound for the covariance. It does not concern contrasts involving overlapping categories, random non-Poisson totals, overdispersed sampling, or dependence among underlying draws. It establishes strict negativity for fixed \(N\), but not a universal asymptotic rate when the category masses themselves vary with \(N\).
