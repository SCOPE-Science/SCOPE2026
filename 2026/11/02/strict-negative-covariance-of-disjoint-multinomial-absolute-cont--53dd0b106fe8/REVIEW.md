# Scientific review

## Correctness: PASS
Conditioning on disjoint block totals makes the internal allocations independent. The mean absolute displacement of a biased sign walk is nondecreasing in the number of signs, as shown by its exact one-step increment identity and reflection coupling. Conditional binomial regression is oppositely monotone in the other block total; the two positive-probability endpoint configurations force the strict sign for all finite sample sizes. Poisson splitting proves the second statement. The 315 exact-rational checks are supporting evidence, not a replacement for the infinite-range argument.

## Originality: PASS, qualified
The closest primary paper, Banić–Elezović (arXiv:2610.17046v1, Section 4), analyzes disjoint-edge absolute-count covariance with equal category masses, obtaining its negative large-sample limit and finite-sample representation. This result proves a strict sign at every sample size and any four strictly positive category probabilities. Semantic database searches and accessible prior claims did not establish an equivalent statement. A more general unlocated theorem on multinomial negative dependence might imply the same sign; independent historical-priority verification is outstanding.

## Mathematical value: PASS
This determines the sign of a natural covariance contribution to multinomial discrete-total-variation variance without asymptotics or symmetry assumptions. The absolute contrast is not monotone in the underlying individual counts, so direct coordinatewise negative association does not settle this sign without another argument. The finding is limited to fixed-size multinomial sampling and disjoint contrasts; it supplies no universal magnitude bound or adjacent-edge sign.
