# Strict one-threshold phase diagram for a noisy harmonically trapped run-and-tumble particle

## Finding
For the stationary one-dimensional run-and-tumble particle with symmetric velocity reversals at rate \(\gamma\), confining drift \(-\mu X\), speed \(v\), and independent Brownian diffusivity \(D\), let \(h=\gamma/\mu\) and \(z=v^2/(2\mu D)\). For every \(0<h<1\) there is **exactly one** transition value \(z_*(h)>0\). The stationary position density has exactly one (central) maximum for \(z<z_*(h)\), exactly two symmetric maxima and one central minimum for \(z>z_*(h)\), and a unique quartically flat central maximum at equality. The transition curve \(z_*(h)\) is continuous and **strictly increasing** on \((0,1)\), with limits \(z_*(0+)=1/2\) and \(z_*(1-)=+\infty\). For \(h\ge1\) the density is strictly unimodal for every \(z>0\). Equivalently the critical Brownian diffusivity is strictly decreasing in the reversal rate and spans the full interval \((0,v^2/\mu)\), with no re-entrant or hidden additional-modal phases.

The implicit crossover equation itself is not new. With \(I_h(z)=\int_{-1}^1(1-u^2)^{h-1}e^{-zu^2}\,du\), that previously known equation is \(I_h(z)+2z I_h'(z)=0\). The finding concerns global uniqueness, strict parameter monotonicity, precise endpoint limits, and the rigorous absence of extra peaks.

## Assumptions and scope
Take \(\mu,v,D,\gamma>0\). Let \(S_t\in\{+1,-1\}\) change sign at rate \(\gamma\), independently of standard Brownian motion \(W_t\), and solve \(dX_t=(-\mu X_t+vS_t)\,dt+\sqrt{2D}\,dW_t\). The stationary distribution is unique and has a smooth strictly positive even density. Mode statements refer to the stationary position marginal, not to velocity or conditional internal-state densities. A local maximum is counted as one even when its second derivative vanishes.

Rescale \(x=\mu X/v\). In stationarity, \(x=U+G\), with independent \(G\sim N(0,1/(2z))\) and \(U\) on \((-1,1)\) having density proportional to \((1-u^2)^{h-1}\). Thus, ignoring a strictly positive normalization, the stationary density is \(p_{h,z}(x)=\int_{-1}^1(1-u^2)^{h-1}e^{-z(x-u)^2}\,du\).

## Proof
**Stationary decomposition.** In a two-state stationary forward equation without Brownian noise, zero net flux gives \((1-u)f_+(u)=(1+u)f_-(u)\). The remaining equation gives \(\partial_u[(1-u^2)f]=-2hu f\) for \(f=f_++f_-\), hence \(f(u)\propto(1-u^2)^{h-1}\). The Brownian component of the stable linear stochastic equation, integrated from the infinite past, is independent of the internal-state contribution and is Gaussian with variance \(D/\mu\). This proves the convolution representation and existence/uniqueness of the stationary density.

**A single central-curvature zero.** Set \(I(z)=I_h(z)>0\) and \(H(z)=I(z)+2zI'(z)\). Differentiating the convolution gives \(\operatorname{sign}p''_{h,z}(0)=-\operatorname{sign}H(z)\). The beta-integral representation gives \(I(z)=B(1/2,h)\,{}_1F_1(1/2;h+1/2;-z)\), so Kummer's differential equation becomes
\[zI''+(h+\tfrac12+z)I'+\tfrac12 I=0.\]
At every zero of \(H\), substitute \(I'=-I/(2z)\) into that equation to obtain
\[H'(z)=\frac{h-1}{z}I(z).\]
When \(0<h<1\), every zero crosses strictly from positive to negative. Also \(H(0)=I(0)>0\), and Laplace asymptotics around \(u=0\) give \(H(z)\sim-(1-h)\sqrt\pi z^{-3/2}<0\) as \(z\to\infty\). The intermediate value theorem and the common direction of all zero crossings therefore give exactly one simple zero \(z_*(h)\).

**No extra off-center modes.** Fix \(0<h<1\); then \(f(u)=(1-u^2)^{h-1}\) is strictly increasing for \(0<u<1\). Clip its boundary divergence by \(f_\varepsilon(u)=\min\{f(u),f(1-\varepsilon)\}\) for \(0\le u\le1\), extend evenly and set it to zero outside \([-1,1]\). Define \(K_z(x,u)=e^{-z(x-u)^2}-e^{-z(x+u)^2}>0\) for \(x,u>0\). Integration by parts for the Gaussian convolution shows
\[\frac{p'_{\varepsilon}(x)}{K_z(x,1)}=\int_0^{1-\varepsilon} f'(u)\frac{K_z(x,u)}{K_z(x,1)}\,du-f(1-\varepsilon),\qquad x>0.\]
For fixed \(0<u<1\), the ratio of kernels equals \(e^{z(1-u^2)}\sinh(2zxu)/\sinh(2zx)\) and is strictly decreasing in \(x>0\), since \(t\mapsto t\coth t\) is strictly increasing for positive \(t\). Consequently \(p'_\varepsilon(x)/K_z(x,1)\) is strictly decreasing. Passing to \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) gives a decreasing ratio \(p'_{h,z}(x)/K_z(x,1)\). Strictness survives because any fixed subinterval of \((0,1)\) has \(f'(u)>0\), contributing a uniform strictly positive difference between the ratios at any two distinct positive arguments. Thus \(p'\) has **at most one** zero for \(x>0\), crossing only positive-to-negative. For \(x>1\), direct differentiation of the Gaussian convolution gives \(p'(x)<0\). Combined with the sign of \(p''(0)\), this proves the stated entire-line mode classification, including the exact critical case. At criticality the center is quartically flat: the normalised density satisfies the heat equation \(\partial_{s^2}p=\tfrac12p''\) with \(s^2=1/(2z)\), and transverse crossing of the central curvature forces \(p''''(0)<0\).

For \(h\ge1\), the beta density is symmetric nonincreasing on \((0,1)\). Its layer-cake representation is a positive mixture of centered uniform densities on symmetric intervals; every convolution of one such density with a Gaussian is strictly decreasing on \(x>0\). Therefore \(p'_{h,z}(x)<0\) for every \(x>0\), establishing strict unimodality.

**Monotone transition and limits.** For fixed \(z>0\), let \(T=U^2\) have a probability density proportional to \(t^{-1/2}(1-t)^{h-1}e^{-zt}\) on \((0,1)\). Then \(H/I=1-2z\mathbb E_{h,z}[T]\). Whenever \(h\) increases, the likelihood ratio is a strictly decreasing function of \(t\), hence \(\mathbb E_{h,z}[T]\) strictly decreases; thus \(H/I\) strictly increases with \(h\). At the unique crossing this implies \(z_*(h)\) is strictly increasing. The implicit-function theorem applies because every zero is simple. As \(h\downarrow0\), the tilted beta law of \(T\) tends to the atom at \(1\), so \(H/I\to1-2z\) and \(z_*\to1/2\). As \(h\uparrow1\), the limiting uniform-density convolution is strictly centrally concave for every fixed \(z>0\), forcing \(z_*\to\infty\). This completes the proof.

It passed five root checks, ten global derivative-sign scans and one strict monotonicity check. This is a finite diagnostic, not a proof of the assertions for all real parameters. Run with `python check.py` using NumPy and SciPy. The proof above is independent of these packages.

## Relationship to prior work
The two-state RTP stationary beta distribution is standard, with a detailed review by Solon, Cates and Tailleur (2015). Sabhapandit and Majumdar (2024), arXiv:2404.02480, already derive the Gaussian-convolution stationary law, the confluent-hypergeometric equation for the active/passive phase boundary (their equations (95)–(96)), and plot a phase diagram; neither those formulas nor the qualitative existence of one- versus two-peak phases are asserted to be new. The claim here is the **rigorous global uniqueness, strict monotonicity, limiting range and no-additional-modes classification** absent from the corresponding examined passages. Guéneau, Majumdar and Schehr (2026), arXiv:2602.08436, derive broader stationary RTP structure and thermal-noise convolution, but the inspected discussion of this case does not establish the strengthened global statements. Structural implication, rather than title similarity, is the comparison basis.

## Limitations
This classification assumes a symmetric two-velocity Markovian reversal mechanism, linear confinement and additive independent Gaussian thermal noise. It does not cover asymmetric reversal, inertia, nonlinear trapping, several-dimensional radial-mode transitions, or time-dependent forcing. Publication-priority risk persists: an unexamined appendix or other equivalent analysis may have proven the stricter monotonicity statement. No independent scientific validation has been performed.
