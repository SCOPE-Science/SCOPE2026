# Scientific review

**Correctness — PASS.** Deletion of the marked leaves gives a measure-preserving bijection onto uniform Cayley trees on the remaining vertex labels. Prüfer-word vacancy indicators then yield the complete conditional leaf-count probability generating function and the covariance ratio. A strictly decreasing vacancy probability gives the sharp all-size hierarchy, with the terminal equality checked separately. An exact-rational independent enumeration through eight vertices corroborates the formula. The proof, rather than finite computation, establishes all \(n\ge4\).

**Originality — PASS, qualified by historical-priority uncertainty.** Searches for Cayley-tree leaf bouquets, sibling-leaf marked stars, Palm leaf counts, and related covariance found no stated equivalent or stronger exact hierarchy. Other published findings concerned root-degree versus leaves in random *recursive* trees or degree versus distance in Cayley trees, which do not imply this sibling-bouquet relation. Lavault [1] supplies classical Prüfer encoding; Bernardi–Morales [2] derive general multitype degree enumerators; Junk–Schmidt–Weber [3] study center-degree-three cherries and their symmetries. The closest inspected main results do not give the specific normalized covariance sequence. However older pendant-subtree count machinery or specialized multi-type enumeration may implicitly cover it; independent historical priority is unresolved.

**Value — PASS.** The precise sibling-bouquet events naturally quantify local pendant-leaf symmetry in a labeled tree. The theorem connects their entire scale of multiplicities to global leaf abundance through an exact size-biased conditional law, strict finite-size order, a nontrivial terminal equality, and an explicit positive linear asymptotic covariance. This is stronger than an isolated average count and supplies usable conditional distributions for motif studies.

**Limitations.** The statement is for unrooted uniform labeled Cayley trees. The normalized (rather than raw) covariance is ordered, with the sharp terminal plateau. Classical Prüfer encoding is prior art. Historical-priority comparison remains qualified, especially against broad generating-function literature.

Self-review: passed. Independent audit: not yet performed.
