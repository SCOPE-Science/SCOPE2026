# Review

## Correctness
PASS. The deletion/direct-sum bijection gives the exact finite joint-tail formula, including the overlapping boundary case. Strict Catalan log-convexity proves positive quadrant dependence and the monotone-transform consequence. A uniform log-ratio expansion with an explicit exponential tail cutoff justifies the sharp covariance limit. Independent brute-force enumeration at sizes 2–8 confirms 490 exact identities and inequalities; finite enumeration is not a substitute for the proof.

## Originality
PASS on a best-knowledge literature and published-record comparison, not a guarantee of historical priority. The 2022/2024 paper of Albert–Bouvel–Féray–Noy concerns first-order logical properties and explicitly excludes fixed-point statistics; the 2015 Hoffman–Rizzolo–Slivken theorem concerns scaled empirical fixed points rather than two extreme consecutive fixed blocks and a second-order covariance. Published records on unrestricted permutations or other Catalan structures do not logically imply the specific finite strict positive-quadrant statement. The exact marginal Catalan counts are straightforward consequences of known direct-sum closure. Implicit prior derivation of the covariance limit remains possible.

## Value
PASS. The two ends are natural, symmetric boundary observables of a classical random Catalan structure. A complete all-threshold, all-monotone-transform association law combined with a precise positive covariance that nevertheless decays as the inverse square of length describes a long-range dependence regime hidden by the limiting independence. The result is not presented as a claim about bulk fixed points or a general universality class.

## Limitations
No result for other avoidance classes or nonuniform distributions. No rate for full joint total variation is asserted. Priority and independent validation remain unresolved.
