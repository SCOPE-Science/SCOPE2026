# Scientific review

## Correctness — PASS

For a finite number-conserving hopping generator with unique product-weight sector stationary laws, the particle number is an exact binomial birth–death process under uniform weak exchange. Compactness identifies the unique zero-exchange stationary limit. Strict Newton inequalities for unequal positive site weights make every local Boolean-lattice cross-product strictly supermodular; the finite FKG inequality and MTP2 monotone-regression/marginal-closure properties imply positive covariance of every distinct site pair. Elementary symmetric polynomial log-concavity gives the strict opposite sign in every nontrivial canonical fixed-number sector. The explicitly specified reflecting/periodic strip has the required canonical measures by horizontal detailed balance and vertical telescoping. Finite-state continuity yields persistence at sufficiently small positive exchange. A standalone checker independently reconstructs the six-site generator and runs 240 random finite-size tests. These experiments corroborate, rather than substitute for, the proof.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)

The stationary binomial mixture is fully credited to Ishiguro–Sato (arXiv:2405.09261v1), and the conserved-number product weights to Ishiguro–Sato (arXiv:2403.01934v1). The one-dimensional weak-reaction precursor (Sato–Nishinari, arXiv:1711.06949) was inspected. Their scientific statements concern state construction, density profiles, currents and relaxation; they do not establish the strict sign reversal, MTP2 classification or pairwise finite-rate persistence. The FKG theorem and Newton inequalities are established classical mathematics and are used with credit. Searches for weighted Fisher/noncentral hypergeometric representations, normalized elementary-symmetric/log-supermodular Bernoulli count mixtures and nonequilibrium ASEP-Langmuir two-point covariances found no claim whose hypotheses imply the entire new conclusion. Publication priority is not independently certified; unindexed work on related mixture laws remains a real risk.

## Value — PASS

The classification resolves the sign of every two-site co-occupation covariance and indeed all increasing-event correlations in a concrete nonequilibrium stationary mixture, despite hardcore repulsion within each fixed-number sector. It gives a sharp homogeneous-weight equality case, an exact two-site amplitude and a rigorous small-positive-reaction implication, and continues to hold with persistent circulating currents in two-dimensional multi-lane exclusion. These are structural transport/statistical-mechanics facts rather than an arbitrary numerical slice. Only fixed finite systems are covered; no macroscopic lower bounds or arbitrary-reaction-rate conclusions are asserted.

## Closest literature and residual risks

The three primary arXiv works above, the classical FKG inequality and Newton's inequalities were compared by statement and implication. The stationary mixture is already published; only the dependence results are claimed as contributions. A known general theorem on biased fixed-size sampling and binomial cardinality mixing could subsume the MTP2 portion without using identical notation; the searches did not identify such a covering theorem, but completeness of literature discovery is not proved. The strict finite-rate consequence is a finite-system continuity statement and is not a claim of an all-rate transition.
