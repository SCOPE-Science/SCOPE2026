# Scientific assessment

**Correctness — PASS.** Unital subalgebras are split uniquely into those containing the one-dimensional socle and graphs of linear maps over totally isotropic subspaces. The exact count and dimension shift follow from a bijection. The isotropic-space products follow from counting orthogonal-quotient line extensions, with no spurious lift multiplicity. The Witt gap is an exact coefficient identity. Gaussian-binomial bounds establish the rare-socle probability exponent, and summable discrete-Gaussian tails justify both rank concentration and the centered limiting laws. Direct RREF enumeration of candidate subalgebras and multiplication confirms selected finite cases in `verify.py`. The general assertion is proved separately; the finite tests do not replace proof.

**Originality — PASS with residual priority risk.** The closest inspected motivating article, Bezerra et al. (arXiv:2206.05572), concerns Hilbert functions and Perazzo algebras rather than subalgebra rank counts and probability laws. Jelisiejew (arXiv:1511.08007) concerns local Gorenstein isomorphism classification. Classical isotropic-space formulas are prior mathematical tools and not claimed as new. An older paper by Strade on enumerating subalgebras in finite nilpotent algebras is especially relevant but inaccessible in complete text; it could subsume the exact formula, although no inspected statement establishes the combined finite-field Witt detector and rare-event asymptotics. Publication priority remains provisional. A relevant associative subalgebra enumeration in a free cube-zero algebra does not imply this multiplication-dependent orthogonal count. Search failure alone is not used as a novelty proof.

**Value — PASS.** The formula determines the full dimension distribution of embedded subalgebras for an infinite and natural Hilbert-vector \((1,d,1)\) family. The low-degree coefficient distinguishes the Witt form, and the rare-event law separates two asymptotically distinct subalgebra populations, one near half dimension and another near one-third. The result offers lattice-based detection of quadratic algebra structure and a quantified answer to the natural sampling question. These are intrinsic invariants, not arbitrary parameter substitutions. They are limited to odd-characteristic nonsingular quadratic socles.
