# Mathematical review

## Correctness — PASS
The exact original/duplicate neighborhood formula rules out omissions of both vertices at one position and yields the radius-two three-symbol condition. The independently computed \(81\)-state potential satisfies every one of \(178\) integer arc inequalities, proving the cyclic and path upper bounds. All \(90\) tight arcs have only three simple directed cycles, each length eight, so equality on lengths \(4\pmod8\) is impossible. The path end-state potentials yield a total boundary allowance of two. The six-position exceptional instance is verified by complete enumeration, and constructive base words with an eight-step insertion prove attainment for every order. Graph-level exhaustive verification agrees in twelve instances. No unproved extrapolation is used.

## Originality — PASS, with residual uncertainty
The principal 2022 paper explicitly leaves the neighborhood corona \(G\star K_1\) case to future research; its product theorem assumes a nontrivial second factor. The 2013 splitting-path/cycle article treats ordinary domination and domination integrity, not super domination. An earlier result about one-leaf **usual** coronas has a different adjacency pattern and cannot imply this statement. Exact formulas, the splitting-graph alias, both recurrences, semantic research records, and recoverable prior claims were compared; no equivalent or stronger covering assertion was found. Search incompleteness and possible unindexed work remain risks, not claims of absolute priority.

## Mathematical value — PASS
This resolves two infinite natural first-factor families singled out by an explicit published open direction, including a nonuniform length-six path and a genuine modulus-eight obstruction for cycles. The structural potential and tight-cycle classification explain why a simple scalar bound is insufficient; the result is not a routine substitution into a generic corona formula.

## Limitations
