# Self-review

## Correctness — PASS
The theorem is narrowly about a Haar central hyperplane of the unit cube in dimension \(d\ge3\). All \(2d\) ambient facets have independent opposite intersections; a pair is missed precisely when the corresponding absolute normal coordinate exceeds the sum of the other absolute coordinates. More than one such dominance event is impossible. Therefore the exceptional-probability event is exactly a disjoint union of \(d\) half-normal events. The Jacobian of the simplex-sum change of variables and the layer-cake identity normalize the rare-event probability to \(B_m=1/[\pi^{m/2}\Gamma(m/2+1)]\) times an exponential of a nonnegative Dirichlet-mixture variable. The product-mixture calculation gives its first four moments exactly. In particular the centered second, third, and fourth moments have orders \(m^{-1}\), \(m^{-2}\), \(m^{-2}\), respectively. Taylor's theorem with a globally valid one-sided remainder then yields the factor \(e^{-1}\) and first correction \(7/(2m)\), with a justified \(O(m^{-2})\) remainder. The stated Stirling equivalent follows. Verification uses the actual companion `verify.py` file and does not replace the proof. Boundary orientations have Haar measure zero.

## Originality — PASS, with residual historical uncertainty
The original contribution assessed is **not** the two-point cube-section law or the exact implicit finite-dimensional probability. Lonke's 1998 result gives an expected-facet asymptotic \(\mathbb E F_d\sim2d\), but no explicit superexponential deficit. Kabluchko–Seidel's 2020 Section 4 gives exact implicit expected-face expressions for cube sections; combining these with two-point support determines the finite-dimensional probability in principle, but the inspected Section 4 and relevant proof do not derive the two-term rare-event asymptotic. The published four-cube record gives \(p_4=1/3\), not a statement for growing dimension. Böröczky–Henk and Donoho–Tanner discuss different projection growth regimes. Semantic research-record searches and web searches for the equivalent Gaussian \(\ell_1\)-dominance event and the exact gamma normalization did not surface the same two-term result. Older or unindexed angle-asymptotic literature remains a genuine priority risk; negative search alone is not proof of novelty.

## Value — PASS
The probability that a random central section has fewer than all available cube facets is an invariant, natural failure event in high-dimensional convex geometry. The known expectation \(\mathbb E F_d\sim2d\) says only that the failure probability vanishes. The exact factor, gamma normalization, and first correction identify its **superexponential scale** and describe a rare geometric phase invisible to first-order average-face counts. The new calculation is a nontrivial sharp asymptotic of a canonical family, not a low-dimensional numerical enumeration or arbitrary parameter slice. It also makes transparent the concentration mechanism in the half-normal/Dirichlet conditioning.
