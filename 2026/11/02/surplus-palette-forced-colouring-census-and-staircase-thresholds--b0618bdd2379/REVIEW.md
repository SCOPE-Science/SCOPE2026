# Scientific assessment

## Correctness — PASS
The central necessity argument is quantitative: a part receiving \(s\ge2\) colours in any proper full completion has at most \(Q-s\le Q-2\) colours available among the neighbours of any of its vertices, so it cannot contribute a forced vertex. The first forced vertex sees \(Q-1\) colours outside its part, which compels that part to be monochromatic and compels the final colouring to use all \(Q\) colours. With two wholly unseeded monochromatic parts, no first force is available. Conversely, at most one empty monochromatic part is forced first, after which all remaining blanks have exactly \(Q-1\) coloured neighbour-colours. This proves the complete characterization for every finite vector of positive part sizes, not just tested sizes.

The Stirling-number formula counts each surjective proper completion via \(Q!\prod_i S(n_i,s_i)\) and each allowed restriction via the product-plus-single-omission polynomial. The general chromatic polynomial term includes all proper fully assigned initial colourings without overcounting. The balanced threshold follows from the exact palette-capacity inequality \(Q-r\le k(t-1)\) and an explicit construction with exactly \(k\) multicoloured parts. Independent direct enumerations and formula evaluations agreed in 18 graph/palette cases; a separate arithmetic test confirmed 16 balanced minimum/multiplicity cases. Verification code is included as `verify.py`. Finite checks corroborate, rather than prove, the infinite results.

## Originality — PASS (qualified)
Farr's 2026 full paper defines this precise local forcing rule, proves basic properties, and handles arbitrary bipartite graphs at palette size two; its Proposition 9 supplies only a necessary used-colour condition. The published October 2026 complete-multipartite result covers exactly the chromatic palette size \(Q=r\), and the separate complete-bipartite result covers three colours with two parts. Their statements do not imply the present enumeration for all \(r\ge3\) and \(Q>r\), nor the balanced sharp seed threshold. The classification is a new partwise completion criterion, rather than simply a restatement of the general used-colour condition.

published-finding corpus searches for multipartite forced-colouring formulas, surplus palettes, all-palette Stirling enumerators, and exact forced partial completions located these closest published findings but no covering result. The source comparisons are limited to accessible material; unpublished or differently indexed equivalent work remains a genuine historical-priority risk. The claims restricted to \(Q=r\), complete bipartite three-colour forcing, and standard proper-colouring counts are explicitly credited and not claimed as new.

## Mathematical value — PASS
The original framework identifies the intermediate palette regime as generally difficult. The theorem supplies a complete and computable generating polynomial throughout that regime on an infinite natural graph class, rather than one coefficient or a routine small parameter instance. Its structural content identifies precisely the possible stalled and recoverable seeds and proves a striking balanced-palette staircase, including an abrupt final loss of every non-total successful seed. The leading coefficient gives an exact census of extremizers at each step. This new information is mathematically motivated by the existing chromatic-palette result and by the forcing polynomial's sensitivity to extra colours.
