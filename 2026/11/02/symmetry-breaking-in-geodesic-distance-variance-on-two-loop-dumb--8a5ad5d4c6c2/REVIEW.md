# Research review of the two-loop dumbbell variance theorem

## Correctness
PASS. Distances are correctly decomposed by the three arclength components: same-circle geodesics, cross-circle routes through the unique bridge, circle-to-bridge routes, and within-bridge distances. The exact first and second moments follow from uniform radial distances on loops and uniform bridge coordinates. The variance derivative is affine in the symmetric length product, giving a complete minimization over every strictly positive length allocation and all positive bridge lengths. Both transition values and their endpoint inclusions follow from global polynomial root signs, not numeric root-finding. The executable checker confirms 343 exact rational moment identities and the critical brackets. Risk: the theorem excludes zero-length circles and other graph topologies, explicitly as stated.

## Originality
PASS within the inspected scientific record, with priority uncertainty. The 2013 Bose et al. paper concerns network farthest-point diagrams; the 2021/2023 Garijo–Márquez–Silveira source gives algorithms for the first moment including cactus graphs; the 2023/2024 Baptista–Kennedy–Mugnolo source gives first-moment graph surgery and extremals. Accessible nearby records and the indexed corpus produced no equivalent or stronger covering result, but an older 1982 Doyle–Graver article could only be assessed through the bibliographic record and later authors' synopsis; historical priority is therefore not conclusively proved.

## Mathematical value
PASS. This is a complete optimization of a natural dispersion invariant over the entire positive two-loop length simplex with bridge length fixed. It identifies a nontrivial intermediate interval of spontaneous symmetry breaking and nonattainment beyond a second threshold. The mean and variance have contrasting balance laws in the same interval; this is not a generic recomputation of the continuous Wiener index or a numerical special case.
