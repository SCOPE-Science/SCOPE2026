# Scientific review

## Correctness (C): PASS

The event excluding all incoming arrows to a designated set of vertices yields an exact \(n-s\)-vertex mapping conditional law. Substitution of the standard rooted-forest count proves every conditional falling-factorial moment and hence the full occupancy law. A simultaneous occupancy coupling proves the stochastic regression without relying on finite-size evidence. The covariance identity and sharp constant follow from exact cyclic-vertex tails and justified Gaussian-dominated Riemann sums. The independent enumeration/checker passed 241 assertions; it is corroboration, not a proof of all orders.

## Originality (O): PASS, historical-priority uncertainty

Direct comparisons considered cyclic-vertex marginal results of Mutafchiev–Finch and Tavaré, the preimage generating-function framework of Drmota–Soria, and the classical random-mapping framework of Flajolet–Odlyzko. The specific joint conditional law plus strict stochastic monotonicity and covariance/correlation asymptotic are not explicitly contained in the inspected passages, nor in the semantically closest published mathematical-result searches. This assessment is about statements and implications, not title matching: a broad generating function for preimage counts may contain joint information implicitly, but it does not itself prove the conditional antitone coupling. Full historical priority, particularly through uninspected older source sections, remains open.

## Value (V): PASS

Cyclic cores and zero-indegree peripheral vertices are canonical, statistically distinct structural features of functional graphs. A sharp all-\(n\) negative regression theorem is a distributional order law stronger than a moment identity. The occupancy coupling supplies a mechanism, while a growing-magnitude covariance with vanishing correlation distinguishes raw second-order dependence from normalized decorrelation, useful when assessing multivariate random-mapping statistics.
